Homogeneous hypersurfaces of the four-dimensional Thurston geometries Sol 1 4 \mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4} , Sol m , n 4 \mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4} and Nil 4 \mathrm{Nil}^{4} Thanks: X. Zhang is the corresponding author
Xiaoge Lu, Zeke Yao and Xi Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we focus on the four-dimensional Thurston geometries whose isometry groups are four-dimensional, namely Sol 1 4 \mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4} , Sol m , n 4 \mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4} and Nil 4 \mathrm{Nil}^{4} . We classify homogeneous hypersurfaces in the above three manifolds.

Primary 53C42; Secondary 53C40, 53C30

1.
Introduction

Homogeneous hypersurfaces serve as the fundamental models in isoparametric theory, providing the canonical examples for its complete classification. Here, a hypersurface
M
in Riemannian manifold
\widetilde{M}
is called
homogeneous
if there exists a closed subgroup
G\subset\operatorname{Iso}_{o}(\widetilde{M})
such that
M=G\cdot p=\{g\cdot p\mid g\in G\}
for some point
p\in\widetilde{M}
, where
\operatorname{Iso}_{o}(\widetilde{M})
is the connected component of the identity of isometry group of
\widetilde{M}
.

The classification of homogeneous hypersurfaces is a classical topic. Moreover,
classifying such hypersurfaces is equivalent to classifying cohomogeneity one actions
up to orbit equivalence.
For real space forms, the classification of homogeneous hypersurfaces can be found in references

[
4
,
23
,
27
,
29
]
. For nonflat complex space forms, the homogeneous hypersurfaces therein have been classified by Takagi
[
28
]
and Berndt-Tamaru
[
3
]
.
Kollross
[
24
]
classified cohomogeneity one actions on the irreducible symmetric spaces of compact type up to orbit equivalence. Later,
Díaz-Ramos, Domínguez-Vázquez and Otero
[
11
]
developed a structural result for cohomogeneity one actions on (not necessarily irreducible) symmetric spaces of noncompact type and arbitrary rank. Most recently, Sanmartín-López and Solonenko
[
26
]
completed the classification of isometric cohomogeneity one actions on such spaces up to orbit equivalence.

For Riemannian products of real space forms, which belong to reducible symmetric spaces, the study of homogeneous hypersurfaces and isoparametric hypersurfaces has achieved many interesting results.
Urbano
[
32
]
, Gao-Ma-Yao
[
20
]
, Domínguez-Vázquez and Manzano
[
15
]
, and de Lima and Pipoli
[
7
]
classified homogeneous hypersurfaces and isoparametric hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
,
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}
,
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
(
n\geq 2
), respectively.
Gao-Ma-Yao
[
19
]
classified isoparametric hypersurfaces in the product space
M_{\kappa_{1}}^{2}\times M_{\kappa_{2}}^{2}
of two-dimensional space forms for
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}\in\{-1,0,1\}
with
\kappa_{1}\neq\kappa_{2}
. Tan-Xie-Yan
[
30
]
established a complete classification of homogeneous hypersurfaces and isoparametric hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Subsequently,
de Lima and Pipoli
[
8
]
classified homogeneous hypersurfaces and isoparametric hypersurfaces in

M_{\kappa_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times M_{\kappa_{2}}^{n_{2}}
for
n_{1},n_{2}\geq 2
and
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}\in\{-1,0,1\}
with
|\kappa_{1}|+|\kappa_{2}|\neq 0
satisfying a one-point condition.

As far as the authors know, there has been little research on homogeneous hypersurfaces in homogeneous Riemannian manifolds that are non-symmetric spaces.
Notice that
\widetilde{{\rm SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})
,

{\rm Nil}^{3}
,
{\rm Sol}^{3}
and
{\rm Sol}_{0}^{4}
are homogeneous Riemannian manifolds but not symmetric spaces. The classification of homogeneous surfaces in
\widetilde{{\rm SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})
,

{\rm Nil}^{3}
and
{\rm Sol}^{3}
can be found in references
[
14
,
15
,
25
]
.
Very recently, D’haene-Wei-Yao-Zhang
[
10
]
established a complete classification for homogeneous hypersurfaces of the four-dimensional Thurston geometry
\mathrm{Sol}_{0}^{4}
. For more developments on the study of homogeneous hypersurfaces and isoparametric theory, we refer the readers to the review articles
[
2
,
5
,
6
,
12
,
21
]
and the references therein.

Based on the classification of three-dimensional model geometries (known as Thurston geometry), Thurston
[
31
]
proposed the well-known geometrization conjecture: Every compact three-manifold admits a canonical decomposition into pieces, each modeled on one of eight geometries:
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
\mathbb{H}^{3}
,
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
,
\widetilde{{\rm SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})
,

{\rm Nil}^{3}
and
{\rm Sol}^{3}
. While there is no analog of the geometrization conjecture for four-dimensional manifolds, there are 19 kinds of four-dimensional Thurston geometry (cf.
[
18
,
33
]
):

In this paper, we will focus on the study of homogeneous hypersurfaces in the four-dimensional Thurston geometry with four-dimensional isometry group. These are the manifolds
\text{Sol}_{1}^{4}
,
\text{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}
and
\text{Nil}^{4}
, and they are all homogeneous manifolds and non-symmetric spaces. As main results, we have the following three classification theorems.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a homogeneous hypersurface of
\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}
. Then up to isometries of
\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}
, one of the following two cases occurs:

(1)

M
is
M_{1,r}
for some
r\geq 0
, which is an orbit through

(\tanh r,0,0,-\ln(\cosh r))
of the subgroup

\{(0,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}\mid x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}\}
, see Example
3.1
;

(2)

M
is
M_{2,0}
, which is an orbit through the origin

(0,0,0,0)
of the subgroup

\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},0)\in\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}\mid x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}\}
, see Example
3.2
.

Before stating our next main result, we assume that
m
and
n
are positive integers for which the equation
X^{3}-mX^{2}+nX-1=0
admits three distinct real roots. These roots can be written as
e^{\alpha}
,
e^{\beta}
and
e^{\gamma}
with
\alpha<\beta<\gamma
and
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=0
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be a homogeneous hypersurface of
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}
. Then up to isometries of
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}
, one of the following five cases occurs:

(1)

M
is
M_{3,r}
for some
r\geq 0
, which is an orbit through

(0,\tfrac{1}{\beta}\tanh(|\beta|r),0,-\tfrac{1}{\beta}\ln(\cosh(|\beta|r)))
of the subgroup

\{(x_{1},0,x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n(m\neq n)}^{4}\mid x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}\}
, see Example
3.3
;

(2)

M
is
M_{4,d}
for some
0\leq d<1
, which is an orbit through the origin

(0,0,0,0)
of the subgroup

\{(x_{1},\tfrac{d}{\sqrt{1-d^{2}}}x_{2},x_{3},x_{2})\in\mathrm{Sol}_{m,m}^{4}\mid x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}\}
, see Example
3.4
;

(3)

M
is
M_{5,r}
for some
r\geq 0
, which is an orbit through

(\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\tanh(-\alpha r),0,0,-\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\ln(\cosh(-\alpha r)))
of the subgroup

\{(0,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}\mid x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}\}
, see Example
3.5
;

(4)

M
is
M_{6,r}
for some
r\geq 0
, which is an orbit through

(0,0,\tfrac{1}{\gamma}\tanh(\gamma r),-\tfrac{1}{\gamma}\ln(\cosh(\gamma r)))
of the subgroup

\{(x_{1},x_{2},0,x_{3})\in\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}\mid x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}\}
, see Example
3.6
;

(5)

M
is
M_{7,0}
, which is an orbit through the origin

(0,0,0,0)
of the subgroup

\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},0)\in\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}\mid x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}\}
,
see Example
3.7
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
be a homogeneous hypersurface of
\mathrm{Nil}^{4}
. Then up to isometries of
\mathrm{Nil}^{4}
, one of the following two cases occurs:

(1)

M
is
M_{8,d}
for some
0\leq d<1
, which is an orbit through the origin

(0,0,0,0)
of the subgroup

\{(x_{1},x_{2},\tfrac{d}{\sqrt{1-d^{2}}}x_{3},x_{3})\in\mathrm{Nil}^{4}\mid x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}\}
, see Example
3.8
;

(2)

M
is
M_{9,0}
, which is an orbit through the origin

(0,0,0,0)
of the subgroup

\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},0)\in\mathrm{Nil}^{4}\mid x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}\}
, see Example
3.9
.

Remark 1.1
.

The isometry group of each of the ambient spaces
\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}
,
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}
and
\mathrm{Nil}^{4}
is four-dimensional and explicitly known. Every homogeneous hypersurface in these spaces can be regarded as the orbit of some three-dimensional subgroup of the isometry group. Classifying homogeneous hypersurfaces is equivalent to classifying the three-dimensional subgroups of the isometry group. Therefore, in this paper we only need to consider the three left invariant metrics defined by the equations (
2.5
), (
2.10
) and (
2.16
) such that the corresponding isometry groups of
\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}
,
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}
and
\mathrm{Nil}^{4}
are four-dimensional, respectively.

Remark 1.2
.

A hypersurface is called
austere
if its multiset of principal curvatures is invariant under change of sign. This notion was introduced by Harvey-Lawson
[
22
]
for the construction of special Lagrangian submanifolds in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Consequently, every austere hypersurface is automatically minimal. Regarding the metrics considered in this paper defined by (
2.5
), (
2.10
) and (
2.16
), all hypersurfaces in the family of examples characterized here are minimal. Among these, the following hypersurfaces are austere:
M_{1,0}
,
M_{2,0}
,
M_{3,0}
,
\{M_{4,d},0\leq d<1\}
,
M_{5,0}
of
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}
,
\{M_{5,r},r>0\}
of
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,m}^{4}
,
M_{6,0}
of
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}
,
\{M_{6,r},r>0\}
of
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,m}^{4}
,
M_{7,0}
of
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,m}^{4}
,
\{M_{8,d},0\leq d<1\}
and
M_{9,0}
.

Remark 1.3
.

All homogeneous hypersurfaces characterized here have no focal manifolds.
For
i\in\{2,7,9\}
, all parallel hypersurfaces of
M_{i,0}
are congruent to
M_{i,0}
.
In contrast, for
j\in\{1,3,5,6\}
, any two distinct hypersurfaces in
\{M_{j,r},r\geq 0\}

are non-congruent.
For any fixed
d
, the parallel hypersurfaces of
M_{4,d}
(resp.
M_{8,d}
) are congruent to

M_{4,d}
(resp.
M_{8,d}
).
However, for
d_{1}\neq d_{2}
,
M_{4,d_{1}}
and
M_{4,d_{2}}
(resp.
M_{8,d_{1}}
and
M_{8,d_{2}}
) are non-congruent.
Besides, the hypersurfaces
M_{7,0}
and
M_{9,0}
are flat.

2.
Preliminaries

For convenience, we use the same symbol
g
to denote the Riemannian metric on each of the three ambient spaces
\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}
,
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}
and
\mathrm{Nil}^{4}
.
The specific one should be seen clearly from context.

2.1.
The geometric structure on
\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}

In this subsection, we review some basic materials about
\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}
from
[
16
,
17
]
.
The underlying manifold of the model space
\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}
is the following solvable Lie group

\left\{(x,y,z,t):=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&e^{-t}x&z\\
0&e^{t}&0&x\\
0&0&e^{-t}&y\\
0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}:x,y,z,t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

The group multiplication is given by

(2.1)

(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1},t_{1})(x_{2},y_{2},z_{2},t_{2})=(x_{1}+e^{t_{1}}x_{2},y_{1}+e^{-t_{1}}y_{2},z_{1}+z_{2}+e^{-t_{1}}x_{1}y_{2},t_{1}+t_{2}).

At a point
p=(x,y,z,t)\in\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}
, there are four left invariant vector fields:

(2.2)

E_{1}=e^{t}\partial_{x},\ \ E_{2}=e^{-t}(\partial_{y}+x\partial_{z}),\ \ E_{3}=\partial_{z},\ \ E_{4}=\partial_{t}.

Then one can check the following brackets:

(2.3)

\displaystyle[E_{1},E_{2}]=E_{3},

\displaystyle[E_{1},E_{3}]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle[E_{1},E_{4}]

\displaystyle=-E_{1},

\displaystyle[E_{2},E_{3}]=0,

\displaystyle[E_{2},E_{4}]

\displaystyle=E_{2},

\displaystyle[E_{3},E_{4}]

\displaystyle=0.

The group that acts on
\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}
making it a Thurston geometry is
\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}\rtimes D_{4}
(see
[
17
]
). Here,
\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}
acts on itself by left translations, and
D_{4}
denotes the dihedral group of 8 elements and is generated by the maps

\phi_{i}:\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}\rightarrow\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}
for
1\leq i\leq 4
, defined by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(x,y,z,t)=(-x,y,-z,t),\ \ \phi_{2}(x,y,z,t)=(-x,-y,z,t),

\displaystyle\phi_{3}(x,y,z,t)=(x,-y,-z,t),\ \ \phi_{4}(x,y,z,t)=(y,x,-z+xy,-t).

In this paper, we select the Riemannian metric
g
on
\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}
as defined in reference
[
16
]
:

(2.5)

g=e^{-2t}dx^{2}+e^{2t}dy^{2}+(dz-xdy)^{2}+dt^{2}.

Then the frame field
\{E_{i}\}^{4}_{i=1}
is orthonormal with respect to
g
.

Let
\tilde{\nabla}
be the Levi-Civita connection of
g
. Applying the equation (
2.3
) and the Koszul’s formula, we derive

(2.6)

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{1}

\displaystyle=E_{4},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}E_{3},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{3}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}E_{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{4}

\displaystyle=-E_{1},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{1}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}E_{3},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{2}

\displaystyle=-E_{4},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{3}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}E_{1},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{4}

\displaystyle=E_{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{1}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}E_{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}E_{1},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{3}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{4}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{2}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{3}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{4}

\displaystyle=0.

2.2.
The geometric structure on
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}

In this subsection, we collect some necessary materials about
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}
from
[
1
,
9
]
.
The underlying manifold of
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}
is a solvable Lie group consisting of the matrices

\begin{pmatrix}e^{\alpha t}&0&0&x\\
0&e^{\beta t}&0&y\\
0&0&e^{\gamma t}&z\\
0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix},

where
x,y,z,t,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha<\beta<\gamma
satisfying
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=0
. The values
e^{\alpha}
,
e^{\beta}
and
e^{\gamma}
are the roots of the equation
X^{3}-mX^{2}+nX-1=0
. Here,
m
and
n
are positive integers such that the roots are all real and distinct.
The group operation is given explicitly by

(2.7)

(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1},t_{1})(x_{2},y_{2},z_{2},t_{2})=(x_{1}+e^{\alpha t_{1}}x_{2},y_{1}+e^{\beta t_{1}}y_{2},z_{1}+e^{\gamma t_{1}}z_{2},t_{1}+t_{2}).

At a point
p=(x,y,z,t)\in\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}
, the left invariant vector fields are given by

(2.8)

\displaystyle E_{1}=e^{\alpha t}\partial_{x},\ \ E_{2}=e^{\beta t}\partial_{y},\ \ E_{3}=e^{\gamma t}\partial_{z},\ \ E_{4}=\partial_{t}.

Then we have the following commutation relations:

(2.9)

\displaystyle[E_{1},E_{2}]=0,

\displaystyle[E_{1},E_{3}]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle[E_{1},E_{4}]

\displaystyle=-\alpha E_{1},

\displaystyle[E_{2},E_{3}]=0,

\displaystyle[E_{2},E_{4}]

\displaystyle=-\beta E_{2},

\displaystyle[E_{3},E_{4}]

\displaystyle=-\gamma E_{3}.

When
m\neq n
, the group associated to this Thurston geometry is
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n(m\neq n)}^{4}\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{3}
, where
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n(m\neq n)}^{4}
acts on itself by left translations, and each
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
factor is generated by reflecting either the
x
-,
y
- or
z
-coordinate (see
[
9
]
). When
m=n
, we have
\beta=0
,
\alpha=-\gamma
, and
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,m}^{4}

can be identified with
\mathrm{Sol}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
.
The associated group is
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,m}^{4}\rtimes(D_{4}\times(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}))
, where
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is generated by reflecting the

y
-coordinate, and
D_{4}
is generated by the maps

\varphi_{i}:\mathrm{Sol}_{m,m}^{4}\rightarrow\mathrm{Sol}_{m,m}^{4}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
, defined by

\varphi_{1}(x,y,z,t)=(-x,y,z,t),\ \ \varphi_{2}(x,y,z,t)=(x,y,-z,t),\ \ \varphi_{3}(x,y,z,t)=(z,y,x,-t).

We choose the Riemannian metric
g
on
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}
as defined in reference
[
1
]
:

(2.10)

g=e^{-2\alpha t}dx^{2}+e^{-2\beta t}dy^{2}+e^{-2\gamma t}dz^{2}+dt^{2}.

Then the frame field
\{E_{i}\}^{4}_{i=1}
is orthonormal with respect to
g
.

Let
\tilde{\nabla}
be the Levi-Civita connection of
g
. By using (
2.9
) and Koszul’s formula, we obtain

(2.11)

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{1}

\displaystyle=\alpha E_{4},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{2}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{3}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{4}

\displaystyle=-\alpha E_{1},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{2}

\displaystyle=\beta E_{4},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{3}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{4}

\displaystyle=-\beta E_{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{2}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{3}

\displaystyle=\gamma E_{4},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{4}

\displaystyle=-\gamma E_{3},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{2}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{3}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{4}

\displaystyle=0.

2.3.
The geometric structure on
\mathrm{Nil}^{4}

In this subsection, we review some basic materials about
\mathrm{Nil}^{4}
from
[
9
,
13
]
.
The underlying manifold of
\mathrm{Nil}^{4}
is the following solvable Lie group:

\left\{(x,y,z,t):=\begin{pmatrix}1&t&\frac{t^{2}}{2}&x\\
0&1&t&y\\
0&0&1&z\\
0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}:x,y,z,t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

The group multiplication is

(2.12)

(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1},t_{1})(x_{2},y_{2},z_{2},t_{2})=(x_{1}+x_{2}+t_{1}y_{2}+\tfrac{t_{1}^{2}}{2}z_{2},y_{1}+y_{2}+t_{1}z_{2},z_{1}+z_{2},t_{1}+t_{2}).

At a point
p=(x,y,z,t)\in\mathrm{Nil}^{4}
, the left invariant vector fields are given by

(2.13)

\displaystyle E_{1}=\partial_{x},\ \ E_{2}=t\partial_{x}+\partial_{y},\ \ E_{3}=\tfrac{t^{2}}{2}\partial_{x}+t\partial_{y}+\partial_{z},\ \ E_{4}=\partial_{t}.

Then we obtain the following equations:

(2.14)

\displaystyle[E_{1},E_{2}]=0,

\displaystyle[E_{1},E_{3}]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle[E_{1},E_{4}]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle[E_{2},E_{3}]=0,

\displaystyle[E_{2},E_{4}]

\displaystyle=-E_{1},

\displaystyle[E_{3},E_{4}]

\displaystyle=-E_{2}.

According to
[
9
]
, the group associated to
\mathrm{\mathrm{Nil}}^{4}
is
\mathrm{Nil}^{4}\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}
, where
\mathrm{Nil}^{4}
acts on itself by left translations, and the copies of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is generated by the maps
\psi_{i}:\mathrm{Nil}^{4}\rightarrow\mathrm{Nil}^{4}
for
1\leq i\leq 2
, defined by

(2.15)

\psi_{1}(x,y,z,t)=(-x,-y,-z,t),\ \ \psi_{2}=(x,y,z,t)=(x,-y,z,-t).

We select the Riemannian metric
g
on
\mathrm{Nil}^{4}
as defined in reference
[
13
]
:

(2.16)

g=dx^{2}-2t\,dxdy+t^{2}\,dxdz+(1+t^{2})dy^{2}-2t(1+\tfrac{t^{2}}{2})dydz+(1+t^{2}+\tfrac{t^{4}}{4})dz^{2}+dt^{2}.

Then the frame field
\{E_{i}\}^{4}_{i=1}
is orthonormal with respect to
g
.

Let
\tilde{\nabla}
be the Levi-Civita connection of
g
. By using (
2.14
) and Koszul’s formula, we obtain

(2.17)

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}E_{4},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{3}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{1}}E_{4}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}E_{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{1}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}E_{4},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{2}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{3}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}E_{4},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{2}}E_{4}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}E_{1}-\tfrac{1}{2}E_{3},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}E_{4},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{3}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{3}}E_{4}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}E_{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{1}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}E_{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}E_{1}-\tfrac{1}{2}E_{3},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{3}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}E_{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{E_{4}}E_{4}

\displaystyle=0.

2.4.
Hypersurfaces of
\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}
,
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}
and
\mathrm{Nil}^{4}

In this subsection, we uniformly describe the basic theory of hypersurfaces in
\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}
,
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}
and
\mathrm{Nil}^{4}
.
Let
M
be an isometrically immersed hypersurface of any of
\mathrm{Sol}_{1}^{4}
,
\mathrm{Sol}_{m,n}^{4}
or
\mathrm{Nil}^{4}
with unit normal vector field
N
. We can assume that

N=aE_{1}+bE_{2}+cE_{3}+dE_{4},

where
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
is defined by (
2.2
)
(resp. (
2.8
), resp. (
2.13
)), and
a,b,c,d
are smooth functions on
M
,
called
angle functions
satisfying
a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2}=1
. Then the following tangent vector fields
are orthonormal on
M
:

(2.18)

\displaystyle T_{1}=bE_{1}-aE_{2}+dE_{3}-cE_{4},

\displaystyle T_{2}=cE_{1}-dE_{2}-aE_{3}+bE_{4},

\displaystyle T_{3}=dE_{1}+cE_{2}-bE_{3}-aE_{4}.

Let
\nabla
be the Levi-Civita connection of the induced metric
g

on
M
. Then the Gauss and Weingarten formulae are given respectively as below:

(2.19)

\tilde{\nabla}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+g(AX,Y)N,\ \ \tilde{\nabla}_{X}N=-AX,\ \hbox{for any}\ X,Y\in TM,

where
A
is the shape operator of
M
.

Let
\tilde{R}
and
R
be the Riemannian curvature tensors of the ambient space and
M
, respectively. Then, the Gauss and Codazzi equations are given by:

(2.20)

\displaystyle R(X,Y)Z

\displaystyle=[\tilde{R}(X,Y)Z]^{\top}+g(AY,Z)AX-g(AX,Z)AY,

(2.21)

(\nabla_{X}A)Y-(\nabla_{Y}A)X=-[\tilde{R}(X,Y)N]^{\top},

where
X,Y,Z\in TM
and
\cdot^{\top}
denotes the tangential component.

Proposition 3.1

The hypersurface

(1)

(2)

,
\tfrac{1-\tanh r}{2}

\tanh r

(3)

(2) Choose the orthonormal frame field

on

(3.1)

According to (
2.6
), (
3.1
) and the Gauss and Weingarten formulae, we get

(3.2)

\displaystyle\nabla_{W_{1}}W_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\nabla_{W_{1}}W_{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\tanh r}{2}W_{3},

\displaystyle\nabla_{W_{1}}W_{3}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\tanh r}{2}W_{2},

\displaystyle\nabla_{W_{2}}W_{1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{sech}rW_{2}-\tfrac{\tanh r}{2}W_{3},

\displaystyle\nabla_{W_{2}}W_{2}

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{sech}rW_{1},

\displaystyle\nabla_{W_{2}}W_{3}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\tanh r}{2}W_{1},

\displaystyle\nabla_{W_{3}}W_{1}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\tanh r}{2}W_{2},

\displaystyle\nabla_{W_{3}}W_{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\tanh r}{2}W_{1},

\displaystyle\nabla_{W_{3}}W_{3}

\displaystyle=0,

,
\tfrac{1-\tanh r}{2}

(3) It follows from (

∎
