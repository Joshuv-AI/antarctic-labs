Harmonic mappings, univalence criteria and a theorem of Lehtinen
Iason Efraimidis, Rodrigo Hernández

Abstract
. The harmonic inner radius σ H ​ ( Ω ) \sigma_{H}(\Omega) of a planar domain Ω \Omega is the largest constant with which a univalence criterion via the Schwarzian derivative holds for harmonic mappings. We show that σ H ​ ( Ω ) ≤ σ H ​ ( 𝔻 ) ≤ 3 / 2 \sigma_{H}(\Omega)\leq\sigma_{H}(\mathbb{D})\leq 3/2 for the unit disk 𝔻 \mathbb{D} and for every domain Ω \Omega that omits an open set. This is an analogue of a theorem of Lehtinen in the setting of holomorphic functions. We provide two related univalence criteria for harmonic mappings.

1. Introduction.
Let
\Omega
be a hyperbolic domain in
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}
,
i.e.
, a domain that has at least three boundary points, and let
\pi:{\mathbb{D}}\to\Omega
be a universal covering map, where
{\mathbb{D}}
is the unit disk. The density
\lambda_{\Omega}
of the hyperbolic (Poincaré) metric in
\Omega
is defined by

\lambda_{\Omega}\big(\pi(z)\big)|\pi^{\prime}(z)|\,=\,\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(z)\,=\,\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}},

which is independent of the choice of the covering map
\pi
. For a locally univalent analytic function
f
in
\Omega
the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of
f
are defined by

Pf=(\log f^{\prime})^{\prime}=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad Sf=(Pf)^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}(Pf)^{2},

respectively, while

\|Sf\|_{\Omega}\,=\,\sup_{z\in\Omega}\,\frac{|Sf(z)|}{\lambda_{\Omega}(z)^{2}}

(1)

denotes its Schwarzian norm. The inner radius of
\Omega
is defined as the number

\sigma(\Omega)\,=\,\sup\{\,c\geq 0\,:\,\,\|Sf\|_{\Omega}\leq c\,\Rightarrow\,f\,\text{univalent}\}.

This domain constant is Möbius invariant,
i.e.
,
\sigma(T\Omega)=\sigma(\Omega)
for every Möbius transformation
T
. Classical results of Nehari and Hille show that
\sigma({\mathbb{D}})=2
, while those of Ahlfors and Gehring show that for
\Omega
simply connected we have that
\sigma(\Omega)>0
if and only if
\Omega
is a quasidisk,
i.e.
, the image of
{\mathbb{D}}
under a quasiconformal self-map of
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}
. Lehtinen
[
15
]
showed that every (hyperbolic) simply connected domain
\Omega
satisfies
\sigma(\Omega)\leq 2
, with equality only in the case when
\Omega
is a disk or a half-plane. For finitely connected domains
\Omega
we have that
\sigma(\Omega)>0
if and only if all boundary components are either points or quasicircles (see
[
4
,
18
]
), while uniform domains
\Omega
satisfy
\sigma(\Omega)>0
(see
[
17
]
). For more information on the inner radius we direct the reader to Lehto’s book
[
16
, Ch.III, §5]
.

More generally, for a complex-valued harmonic mapping
f
in a planar domain
\Omega
we write
\omega=\overline{f_{\overline{z}}}/f_{z}
for its dilatation. According to Lewy’s theorem the mapping
f
is locally univalent if and only if its Jacobian
J_{f}=|f_{z}|^{2}-|f_{\overline{z}}|^{2}
does not vanish. Duren’s book
[
8
]
contains valuable information about the theory of planar harmonic mappings.

The pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of a locally univalent harmonic mapping
f
were defined in
[
13
]
as

P_{f}=(\log J_{f})_{z}\qquad\text{and}\qquad S_{f}\,=\,(P_{f})_{z}-\tfrac{1}{2}(P_{f})^{2}.

If
f=h+\overline{g}
, for
h
and
g
holomorphic, then these formulas are equivalent to

P_{f}\,=\,Ph-\frac{\overline{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}

(2)

and

S_{f}\,=\,Sh+\frac{\overline{\omega}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\left(\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{h^{\prime}}\omega^{\prime}-\omega^{\prime\prime}\right)-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\omega^{\prime}\overline{\omega}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right)^{2}.

(3)

When
f
is holomorphic these reduce to the classical pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives. Observe that the notations
Pf
and
Sf
are used when we know that
f
is holomorphic, while the notations
P_{f}
and
S_{f}
, with
f
as a subscript, are used in the more general context of harmonic mappings. The Schwarzian norm of a harmonic mapping is defined exactly as in (
1
).

Another definition for the Schwarzian derivative, introduced by Chuaqui, Duren and Osgood
[
5
]
, applies to harmonic mappings which admit a lift to a minimal surface via the Weierstrass-Enneper formulas. Focusing on the planar theory, in this article we adopt the definition from
[
13
]
given above.

The
harmonic inner radius
of a hyperbolic domain
\Omega
in
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}
is defined as the constant

\sigma_{H}(\Omega)\,=\,\sup\{\,c\geq 0\,:\,f\;\text{harmonic in}\;\Omega\;\text{with}\;\|S_{f}\|_{\Omega}\leq c\,\Rightarrow\,f\,\text{injective}\}.

Clearly, this is a Möbius invariant constant and it satisfies
\sigma_{H}(\Omega)\leq\sigma(\Omega)
since every holomorphic function is harmonic. In
[
14
]
it was shown that
\sigma_{H}({\mathbb{D}})>0
. For finitely connected domains
\Omega
we have that
\sigma_{H}(\Omega)>0
if and only if
\sigma(\Omega)>0
; see
[
9
]
. Moreover, it was shown in
[
10
]
that
\sigma_{H}(\Omega)>0
for uniform domains
\Omega
.

2. An analogue of Lehtinen’s theorem.
Our main result in this section is the following.

Theorem 1
.

For every domain
\Omega
in
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}
that omits an open set we have that

\sigma_{H}(\Omega)\leq\sigma_{H}({\mathbb{D}})\leq 3/2.

Proof.

We first prove the inequality
\sigma_{H}(\Omega)\leq\sigma_{H}({\mathbb{D}})
. Assume, in order to get a contradiction, that
\sigma_{H}(\Omega)>\sigma_{H}({\mathbb{D}})
. Then there exists a non-injective harmonic mapping
f
in
{\mathbb{D}}
for which

\sigma_{H}({\mathbb{D}})<\|S_{f}\|_{\mathbb{D}}<\sigma_{H}(\Omega).

Let
z_{1},z_{2}\in{\mathbb{D}}
be distinct points for which
f(z_{1})=f(z_{2})
. Let
\varphi
be a disk automorphism for which
\varphi(z_{1})=0
and
\varphi(z_{2})=x\in(0,1)
.

Since
\Omega
omits an open set there exists a circle that lies in its complement and touches its boundary. Applying an inversion with respect to this circle we may assume that
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{D}}
and that
1\in\partial\Omega
without changing its harmonic inner radius. Moreover, we may assume that
0\in\Omega
since, otherwise, we may apply the transformation

z\mapsto\frac{1-\overline{p}}{1-p}\,\frac{z-p}{1-\overline{p}z}

for any point
p\in\Omega
. Let
\varepsilon=\frac{1-x}{2}
and consider some point
z_{0}=r_{0}e^{i\theta_{0}}\in\Omega
for which
|1-z_{0}|<\varepsilon
. Since
1-r_{0}\leq|1-z_{0}|<\varepsilon
it is evident that

r_{0}>1-\varepsilon=\frac{1+x}{2}>x.

Hence, the function
\psi(z)=e^{-i\theta_{0}}\frac{x}{r_{0}}z
maps
{\mathbb{D}}
into
{\mathbb{D}}
, fixes the origin and sends
z_{0}
to
x
. Therefore, the domain

\widehat{\Omega}=\varphi^{-1}\circ\psi(\Omega)

contains the points
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
, so that
f
restricted on
\widehat{\Omega}
is non-injective. However, by the domain monotonicity of the hyperbolic metric we have that

\|S_{f}\|_{\widehat{\Omega}}\leq\|S_{f}\|_{\mathbb{D}}<\sigma_{H}(\Omega)=\sigma_{H}(\widehat{\Omega}),

which implies that
f
is injective in
\widehat{\Omega}
, a contradiction.

Next, we prove the inequality
\sigma_{H}({\mathbb{D}})\leq 3/2
. In
[
19
]
it was shown that the harmonic mapping
f_{\alpha}=h_{\alpha}+\overline{g_{\alpha}}
, defined by

h_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{1-(1-z)^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\qquad\text{and}\qquad g_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{1-(1+\alpha z)(1-z)^{\alpha}}{\alpha(1+\alpha)},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}},

is not injective for
\alpha>1
. The dilatation of
f_{\alpha}
is
\omega_{\alpha}(z)=z
. We compute
h_{\alpha}^{\prime}(z)=(1-z)^{\alpha-1}
, so that

Ph_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{1-\alpha}{1-z}\qquad\text{and}\qquad Sh_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{1-\alpha^{2}}{2(1-z)^{2}}.

Hence,

S_{f_{\alpha}}(z)=\frac{1-\alpha^{2}}{2(1-z)^{2}}+\frac{(1-\alpha)\overline{z}}{(1-|z|^{2})(1-z)}-\frac{3\overline{z}^{2}}{2(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}.

It is easy to see that

\|S_{f_{\alpha}}\|_{\mathbb{D}}\leq 2(\alpha^{2}-1)+2(\alpha-1)+\frac{3}{2},

which decreases to
3/2
when
\alpha\to 1^{+}
. Now, if
\sigma_{H}({\mathbb{D}})>3/2
were true, then there would exist
\alpha_{0}>1
for which the mapping
f_{\alpha_{0}}
would satisfy

\|S_{f_{\alpha_{0}}}\|_{\mathbb{D}}\leq 2(\alpha_{0}^{2}-1)+2(\alpha_{0}-1)+\frac{3}{2}=\sigma_{H}({\mathbb{D}}).

But this would imply that
f_{\alpha_{0}}
is injective, a contradiction.
∎

A few remarks are in order.

Remark 1
.

The method of proof of the first inequality in Theorem
1
can be applied to the holomorphic setting and give a new proof of Lehtinen’s inequality
\sigma(\Omega)\leq 2
that does not rely on any specific function; recall that Lehtinen’s proof makes use of the logarithm. On the other hand, an important drawback of our proof is that it fails to characterize the extremal functions, which is the essence of Lehtinen’s result.

Remark 2
.

If the domain
\Omega
is simply connected and does not omit an open set then it is not a Jordan domain, hence
\sigma(\Omega)=0
by a theorem of Gehring
[
11
]
and therefore
\sigma_{H}(\Omega)=0
, so that our inequality holds for
all
simply connected domains.

The situation is different if
\Omega
is finitely connected and does not omit an open set. In this case, if
\partial\Omega
has some component that is different than a point then
\sigma(\Omega)=0
by
[
4
]
. If, on the other hand,
\Omega
is the sphere
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}
with a finite number of punctures then it is not hard to see that filling in the punctures does not decrease the inner radius, so that
0<\sigma(\Omega)\leq\sigma({\mathbb{C}}_{0,1})
, where
{\mathbb{C}}_{0,1}={\mathbb{C}}\backslash\{0,1\}
is the largest hyperbolic domain. It seems to be an open problem to estimate the inner radius of
{\mathbb{C}}_{0,1}
or, at least, to show that it satisfies Lehtinen’s inequality.

3. Two conjectures.
We turn our attention to the unit disk. Hereafter, we simplify the notation for the Schwarzian norm and simply write
\|\cdot\|
instead of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{D}}
.

Motivated by the harmonic mapping
f_{\alpha}
, constructed in
[
19
]
and used in the proof of the second inequality in Theorem
1
, we state the following conjecture. Note that for
\alpha=1
, the injective mapping
f_{1}
is the horizontal shear of the function
z\mapsto z-z^{2}/2
with dilatation
\omega(z)=z
; its range
f_{1}({\mathbb{D}})
is shown in Figure
2
.

Conjecture 1
.

\sigma_{H}({\mathbb{D}})=3/2

According to a theorem of Gehring and Pommerenke
[
12
]
, if
f
is holomorphic with
\|Sf\|\leq 2
then we have that
f({\mathbb{D}})
is a Jordan domain and
f
admits a homeomorphic extension to
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}
, unless
f
is Möbius conjugate to the logarithm function
\ell(z)=\log\frac{1+z}{1-z}
, that is,
f=T\circ\ell\circ\tau
for Möbius transformations
T
and
\tau
with
\tau({\mathbb{D}})={\mathbb{D}}
. In short, at the threshold
\|Sf\|=2
the image is not, in general, a Jordan domain, so that univalence is lost above that threshold.

The proposed extremal harmonic mapping
f_{1}
for Conjecture
1
suggests an interesting distinction between the holomorphic and the harmonic settings: above the threshold
\|S_{f}\|=3/2
injectivity should, in general, be lost because a cusp in the image collapses. Indeed, we see this in the range of
f_{1,5}
in Figure
2
. One may then expect, in contrast to the holomorphic setting, to still have a Jordan domain at the threshold for harmonic mappings, without the need to exclude any specific mappings from consideration. We are thus led to the following statement, which is stronger than Conjecture
1
.

Conjecture 2
.

If
f
is a harmonic mapping in
{\mathbb{D}}
with
\|S_{f}\|\leq 3/2
then
f
admits a homeomorphic extension to
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}
.

In
[
9
]
it was shown that there exists a constant
c>0
for which the condition
\|S_{f}\|\leq c
implies the existence of a homeomorphic extension of
f
to
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}
.

4. Univalence criteria.
The fact that
\sigma_{H}({\mathbb{D}})>0
, namely, that there exists some
c>0
such that
\|S_{f}\|\leq c
implies that
f
is injective, raises the pressing question of finding an explicit positive lower bound for
\sigma_{H}({\mathbb{D}})
. Such a bound would be an interesting first step towards solving Conjecture
1
. In what follows, we provide a criterion of a different nature that, still, involves the Schwarzian derivative and, moreover, provides explicit constants. This makes use of the hyperbolic derivative of holomorphic self-maps
\omega
of
{\mathbb{D}}
, given by

\omega^{*}(z)=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}},

and the harmonic order operator given by

A_{f}(z)=\tfrac{1}{2}(1-|z|^{2})P_{f}(z)-\overline{z}.

(4)

It is known that
A_{f}
appears naturally as the second coefficient of the analytic part of the harmonic mapping that arises from
f
after an application of a Koebe transform followed by certain affine transform; see Section 3 in
[
3
]
.

We will be needing the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\omega
be a holomorphic self-mapping of
{\mathbb{D}}
. Then

\left|\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\omega^{\prime\prime}(z)}{2(1-|\omega(z)|^{2})}-\overline{z}\omega^{*}(z)+\overline{\omega(z)}\omega^{*}(z)^{2}\right|\leq 1-|\omega^{*}(z)|^{2},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Proof.

With the notation
\varphi_{a}(z)=\frac{a+z}{1+\overline{a}z},a\in{\mathbb{D}}
, for the automorphisms of
{\mathbb{D}}
, the lemma follows directly from considering the self-mapping

\varphi_{-\omega(a)}\circ\omega\circ\varphi_{a}(z)=\frac{\omega\!\left(\frac{a+z}{1+\overline{a}z}\right)-\omega(a)}{1-\overline{\omega(a)}\,\omega\!\left(\frac{a+z}{1+\overline{a}z}\right)}=a_{1}z+a_{2}z^{2}+\ldots

and applying to it the Schwarz-Pick inequality
|a_{2}|\leq 1-|a_{1}|^{2}
.
∎

Theorem 2
.

Let
f
be a locally univalent harmonic mapping in
{\mathbb{D}}
with dilatation
\omega:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{D}}
. If

(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|+2|\omega^{*}(z)A_{f}(z)|\leq\frac{1}{2}|\omega^{*}(z)|^{2},\qquad z\in{\mathbb{D}},

then
f
is injective.

Proof.

Writing
f=h+\overline{g}
, we will prove that
\|Sh\|\leq 2
, from which the univalence of
h
will follow. Thereafter, we observe that the hypothesis is invariant under affine transformations
F=f+a\overline{f},a\in{\mathbb{D}}
, since
P_{F}=P_{f}
and
S_{F}=S_{f}
(see
[
13
, Prop. 1]
), so that
A_{F}=A_{f}
and, moreover, the dilatation
\omega_{F}
of
F
satisfies

\omega_{F}^{*}=\frac{1+a\overline{\omega}}{1+\overline{a}\omega}\,\omega^{*},

therefore
|\omega_{F}^{*}|=|\omega^{*}|
. It follows that
h+ag
,
i.e.
, the analytic part of
F
, is univalent for all
a\in{\mathbb{D}}
. Letting
|a|\to 1^{-}
and using Hurwitz’ theorem we get that
h+ag
is univalent for all
a\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}
; the fact that
|\omega|<1
guarantees that this limit function is not constant. A standard rotational argument concludes the proof: Let
z_{1},z_{2}
be distinct points in
{\mathbb{D}}
for which
f(z_{1})=f(z_{2})
. Since
h
is injective, we have that
h(z_{1})\neq h(z_{2})
. Setting
\theta=\arg\big(h(z_{1})-h(z_{2})\big)
we see that

{\mathbb{R}}\ni e^{-i\theta}\big(h(z_{1})-h(z_{2})\big)=e^{-i\theta}\big(\overline{g(z_{2})}-\overline{g(z_{1})}\big)=e^{i\theta}\big(g(z_{2})-g(z_{1})\big),

from which we get that
h+e^{2i\theta}g
is not injective, a contradiction.

To show that
\|Sh\|\leq 2
, we rewrite (
3
), suppressing the variable
z
where it is possible, as follows

\displaystyle Sh=

\displaystyle\,S_{f}-\frac{\overline{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\left(Ph-\frac{\overline{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}-\frac{2\overline{z}}{1-|z|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\overline{\omega}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\left[\omega^{\prime\prime}-\frac{2\overline{z}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|z|^{2}}+\frac{2\overline{\omega}(\omega^{\prime})^{2}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right]-\tfrac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\overline{\omega}\omega^{\prime}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right)^{2}.

Multiplying both sides by
(1-|z|^{2})^{2}
, and in view of (
2
) and (
4
), we obtain

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}Sh(z)=

\displaystyle\,(1-|z|^{2})^{2}S_{f}(z)-2\overline{\omega(z)}\omega^{*}(z)A_{f}(z)-\tfrac{3}{2}\left[\overline{\omega(z)}\omega^{*}(z)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle+2\overline{\omega(z)}\left[\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\omega^{\prime\prime}(z)}{2(1-|\omega(z)|^{2})}-\overline{z}\omega^{*}(z)+\overline{\omega(z)}\omega^{*}(z)^{2}\right].

Finally, using the hypothesis and Lemma
1
we get that

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sh(z)|\leq

\displaystyle\,(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|S_{f}(z)|+2|\omega^{*}(z)A_{f}(z)|+\tfrac{3}{2}\left|\omega^{*}(z)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\left|\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\omega^{\prime\prime}(z)}{2(1-|\omega(z)|^{2})}-\overline{z}\omega^{*}(z)+\overline{\omega(z)}\omega^{*}(z)^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\tfrac{1}{2}\left|\omega^{*}(z)\right|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}\left|\omega^{*}(z)\right|^{2}+2\left(1-\left|\omega^{*}(z)\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,2,

as desired.
∎

Note that for the harmonic Möbius transformations,
i.e.
, post-compositions of a Möbius transformation
M
with an affine map:
f=M+c\overline{M},c\in{\mathbb{D}}
, it holds that
S_{f}=SM\equiv 0
and
\omega^{*}\equiv 0
, so that these mappings satisfy the hypotheses in Theorem
2
and are also, clearly, injective. We do not know if there are any other mappings that satisfy this criterion. It is interesting to ask for simple conditions that imply the inequality in this criterion (see
[
6
]
for such an analysis for a different criterion).

Finally, it is fairly simple to combine results from
[
2
]
and
[
7
]
in order to produce the following.

Theorem 3
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a locally univalent harmonic mapping in
{\mathbb{D}}
. If
|Sh(z)|\leq 2c^{2}
, where
c\approx 0,6533
is the first no negative solution of the equation
2x\tan x=1
, then
f
is injective.

Proof.

Let
a
and
b
be complex numbers for which
ah^{\prime\prime}(0)+b=0
, and set
f_{1}=af+b=h_{1}+\overline{g_{1}}
. Then
h_{1}^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
and
Sh_{1}=Sh
. In view of Theorem 5.2 in
[
2
]
, the normalization of
h_{1}
along with the the condition
|Sh_{1}(z)|\leq 2c^{2}
imply that
h_{1}
is convex. Hence,
f_{1}
is injective by
[
7
]
. It follows that
f
is injective.
∎
