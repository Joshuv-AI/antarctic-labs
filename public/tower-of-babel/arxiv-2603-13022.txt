The resolving completion of an exact category
Marianne Lawson, Janina C. Letz, Julia Sauter

Abstract
. For an exact category we provide two constructions of an ambient category in which the initial category is resolving: In the derived category and in the Gabriel–Quillen embedding. For the first construction we describe a pre-aisle and its right orthogonal using different acyclicty conditions. We provide necessary and sufficient conditions when this pair is a t-structure.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
18G10, 18G80, 16E35, 18E10

Introduction

Exact categories appear in various areas of mathematics; for
example, in representation theory, algebraic geometry, algebraic topology or functional analysis. It is a common objective to find derived equivalences between such categories; meaning their derived categories are triangle equivalent. For example, this is at the heart of tilting theory
[
Hap88
, Chapter III]
,
[
Ric89
]
,
[
AHHK07
, Chapter 5]
. Many of these triangle equivalences are based on the derived equivalence obtained for a resolving subcategory by
[
HvR20b
]
.

For an exact category
{\mathcal{E}}
we describe the
resolving completion

{\mathcal{R}}
of
{\mathcal{E}}
; that is
{\mathcal{E}}
can be identified with a resolving subcategory of
{\mathcal{R}}
and it is universal in the following sense: Whenever
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
is resolving, then
\mathcal{F}
can be identified with a resolving subcategory of
\mathcal{R}
. We give two constructions for ambient categories of
{\mathcal{E}}
:

•

We construct
{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})
as a full subcategory of
\operatorname{D}({\mathcal{E}})
, generalizing constructions of
[
Hub95
,
Sch99
,
HKvRW23
]
. It consists of complexes which are
{\mathcal{E}}
-acyclic in positive degrees and satisfy a weaker acyclicty condition, called left
\text{Ext}_{{\mathcal{E}}}
-acyclicity, in negative degrees. This notion is inspired by
[
Rum21
]
.

•

We construct
{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{E}})
as a fully exact subcategory of the localization of the functor category
\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{E}})
at the category of locally effaceable functors
\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{E}})
. That is, we extend
{\mathcal{E}}
in its Gabriel–Quillen embedding. This is inspired by
[
Rum20
,
HKvRW23
]
.

For these construction we show in
Propositions

6.2
,
6.3
and
6.4
the following result:

Theorem A
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a weakly idempotent complete small exact category. Then both
{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})
and
{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{E}})
are resolving completions of
{\mathcal{E}}
and there is an equivalence
{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})\to{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{E}})
.

Similar to the constructions of
{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})
and
{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{E}})
we also construct finite resolving completions
{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
and
{\mathcal{R}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
, which yield equivalences of the bounded derived categories.

In the construction
{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})
we obtain a pair
({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}})
, which is close to being a t-structure. In fact, when
{\mathcal{E}}
has kernels, this is the t-structure described in
[
Hub95
,
Sch99
]
. However, we do not expect the pair to be a t-structure in general, as the bounded derived category of an exact category need not support any t-structure by
[
Nee24
]
. Moreover, using this construction and its dual construction, we show in
Theorem

3.19
the following result:

Theorem B
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a weakly idempotent complete exact category. There exists a pair
({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}})
of full subcategories of
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
such that

(1)

{\mathcal{U}}
and
{\mathcal{V}}
are closed under direct summands, isomorphisms and extensions;

(2)

\Sigma{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathcal{U}}
and
{\mathcal{V}}\subseteq\Sigma{\mathcal{V}}
; and

(3)

(\Sigma{\mathcal{U}})^{\perp}={\mathcal{V}}
and
\Sigma{\mathcal{U}}={}^{\perp}{\mathcal{V}}
.

In fact, we construct two pairs with these properties which in general are not equal. Each pair
({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}})
yields a
maximally non-negative
extension
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{U}}\cap{\mathcal{V}}
of
{\mathcal{E}}
; this means
\Hom_{\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})}({\mathcal{H}},\Sigma^{<0}{\mathcal{H}})=0
and
{\mathcal{H}}
is maximal with the property.

The notion of left Ext-acyclicty is crucial in the constructions of
{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})
and
{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{E}})
. This is not surprising as it is preserved in a resolving extension. Left Ext-acyclicty encompasses two notions, namely left
{\mathcal{E}}
-acyclicity and Hom-acyclicity. The former is a slight generalization of
{\mathcal{E}}
-acyclicity, while the latter encodes acyclicity after application of the Yoneda functor.

In order to obtain equivalences between the unbounded derived categories, we construct the
n
-resolving completion
of
{\mathcal{E}}
for
n\geq 1
.
Moreover, this construction preserves finiteness of the global dimension.
The
1
-resolving completion is the full subcategory of
\operatorname{D}({\mathcal{E}})
consisting of two-term complexes represented by a monomorphism; this category has been studied by
[
Sch99
,
HKvRW23
]
.

1.
Acyclicity and resolving subcategories

In an abelian category there is a canonical notion of acyclicity of a sequence
L\xrightarrow{f}M\xrightarrow{g}N
; that is when
0\to\operatorname{ker}(g)\to M\to\coker(f)\to 0
is a short exact sequence. There are various equivalent characterizations of acyclicity in an abelian category. However, the characterizations can yield different notions in an exact category. The most common notion was introduced in
[
Nee90
, Section 1]
. Our interest lies in a different notion of acyclicity that is motivated by the Ext-kernels introduced in
[
Rum21
, Definition 3]
. The latter notion is closely connected to resolving subcategories.

1.1.
Exact categories and exact functors

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an additive category. A pair of morphisms
(i,p)
in
{\mathcal{A}}
is called a
kernel-cokernel pair
if
\operatorname{ker}(p)=i
and
\coker(i)=p
. An
exact structure
is a collection of kernel-cokernel pairs satisfying a certain set of axioms; this was introduced in
[
Qui73
]
. An
exact category

{\mathcal{E}}
is an additive category equipped with an exact structure. Generally, we use
{\mathcal{E}}
to refer to both the underlying additive category and the exact structure. We refer to those kernel-cokernel pairs in the exact structure of
{\mathcal{E}}
as
{\mathcal{E}}
-conflations
. If
(i,p)
is an
{\mathcal{E}}
-conflation, we call
i
an
{\mathcal{E}}
-inflation
and
p
an
{\mathcal{E}}
-deflation
. We represent
{\mathcal{E}}
-inflations by
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{E}}}
and
{\mathcal{E}}
-deflations by
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{E}}}
. In other sources inflations and deflations are called admissible monomorphisms and admissible epimorphisms, respectively. For an overview of exact categories see
[
Büh10
]
.

1.6.
Exact subcategories

We recall the terminology for subcategories in an exact category.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an exact category. An additive subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{E}}
is

(1)

extension-closed
, if for any
{\mathcal{E}}
-conflation
L\to M\to N
with
L,N\in{\mathcal{C}}
also
M\in{\mathcal{C}}
;

(2)

inflation-closed
, if for any
{\mathcal{E}}
-conflation
L\to M\to N
with
L,M\in{\mathcal{C}}
also
N\in{\mathcal{C}}
;

(3)

deflation-closed
, if for any
{\mathcal{E}}
-conflation
L\to M\to N
with
M,N\in{\mathcal{C}}
also
L\in{\mathcal{C}}
;

(4)

closed under summands
, if for any
M\oplus M^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}
also
M,M^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}
;

(5)

thick
, if it is extension-, inflation- and deflation-closed and closed under summands;

(6)

Serre
, if for any
{\mathcal{E}}
-conflation
L\to M\to N
one has
M\in{\mathcal{C}}
if and only if
L,M\in{\mathcal{C}}
.

Note that a subcategory is deflation-closed if and only if it is closed under kernels of
{\mathcal{E}}
-deflations.

If
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{E}}
is extension-closed, then there exists an induced exact structure on
{\mathcal{C}}
consisting of the
{\mathcal{E}}
-conflations
L\to M\to N
with
L,M,N\in{\mathcal{C}}
. We call this the
fully exact structure (coming from
{\mathcal{E}}
)
on
{\mathcal{C}}
.

A functor
\mathsf{F}\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{F}}
for exact categories
{\mathcal{E}}
and
{\mathcal{F}}
is

(1)

exact
, if it maps
{\mathcal{E}}
-conflations to
{\mathcal{F}}
-conflations;

(2)

an
exact embedding
, if it is a faithful exact functor which is injective on objects;

(3)

a
fully exact embedding
, if it is an exact embedding that is full and, whenever
\mathsf{F}(L)\to\tilde{M}\to\mathsf{F}(N)
is an
{\mathcal{F}}
-conflation with
L,N\in{\mathcal{E}}
, there exists
M\in{\mathcal{E}}
such that
\mathsf{F}(M)=\tilde{M}
and the
{\mathcal{F}}
-conflation is the image of an
{\mathcal{E}}
-conflation.

If
\mathsf{F}
is an exact embedding that is full, then we can identify
{\mathcal{E}}
with its image in
{\mathcal{F}}
and view
{\mathcal{E}}
as an exact subcategory of
{\mathcal{F}}
. Furthermore, when
\mathsf{F}
is a fully exact embedding, then the exact structure on
{\mathcal{E}}
consists of all
{\mathcal{F}}
-conflations with objects in
{\mathcal{E}}
.

An exact category
{\mathcal{E}}
is an
exact subcategory
of an exact category
{\mathcal{F}}
, if it is an additive subcategory and the inclusion functor is an exact embedding that is full. When the inclusion functor is a fully exact embedding, we say
{\mathcal{E}}
is a
fully exact subcategory
. In this case, the exact structure on
{\mathcal{E}}
coincides with the fully exact structure coming from
{\mathcal{F}}
.

1.7.
Resolving subcategories

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be an exact category. A fully exact subcategory

for any

{\mathcal{F}}
-deflation

{\mathcal{E}}
is deflation-closed in
{\mathcal{F}}
.

We say

{\mathcal{F}}

(R1’)

for any

with

; and

every epimorphism

in

.

If

is left abelian, then resolving and semi-resolving are equivalent. However, in general one has

and

\implies

.

Remark 1.8
.

, Definition 2.1]

.

2.
Ext resolutions

The sequence in

obtained from a resolving subcategory plays an important role in the sequel. In fact, one can think of

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an exact category. We denote the category of complexes over

-acyclic

-acyclic in each degree. A morphism of complexes is an

-acyclic.

Definition 2.1

Let

be an exact category. An

-resolution

-acyclic in negative degrees. We call an

-resolution

constructed in

3.
In the derived category

In the previous section we saw that left Ext-acyclicty behaves well with respect to many operations of complexes. In particular, we can consider left Ext-acyclicty in the setting of the homotopy and derived category. We use it to define an analogue of the standard t-structure in the derived category of an abelian category. While the resulting pair need not be a t-structure, their ‘heart’ is an interesting extension of the initial exact category.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an exact category with underlying additive category
{\mathcal{A}}
. We denote by
\operatorname{K}({\mathcal{A}})
the homotopy category of
{\mathcal{A}}
and by
\operatorname{D}({\mathcal{E}})
the derived category of
{\mathcal{E}}
; recall that the derived category
\operatorname{D}({\mathcal{E}})=\operatorname{K}({\mathcal{A}})/\operatorname{thick}(\operatorname{Ac}({\mathcal{E}}))
is the Verdier quotient of the homotopy category by the thick subcategory generated by the
{\mathcal{E}}
-acyclic complexes; see
[
Nee90
, Construction 1.5]
. When
{\mathcal{E}}
is (weakly) idempotent complete, then the
{\mathcal{E}}
-acyclic complexes already form a thick subcategory. We write
{\mathcal{A}}
to emphasize that the formation of the homotopy category depends only on the additive, and not the exact, structure.

We consider different boundedness conditions for the homotopy and derived category: We write
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
for the derived category of bounded complexes,
\operatorname{D}^{-}({\mathcal{E}})
the derived category of right bounded complexes and
\operatorname{D}^{+}({\mathcal{E}})
the derived category of left bounded complexes.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an exact category with underlying additive category
{\mathcal{A}}
. For
*\in\{\emptyset,b,+,-\}
the following holds:

(1)

The subcategory of
\operatorname{K}^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
of complexes that are left
\text{Ext}_{{\mathcal{E}}}
-acylic in degree
n
is closed under direct summands, isomorphisms and extensions.

(2)

The subcategory of
\operatorname{D}^{*}({\mathcal{E}})
of complexes that are left
\text{Ext}_{{\mathcal{E}}}
-acylic in degree
n
is closed under direct summands, isomorphisms and extensions.

Proof.

The category in
Item

1
is closed under direct summands and isomorphisms by
Lemma

2.4
, and it is extension-closed by
Lemma

2.3
. As
{\mathcal{E}}
-acyclicity implies left Ext
E
-acylicity by
Eq.

1.4.1
, the subcategory in
Item

1
contains all
{\mathcal{E}}
-acyclic complexes. Hence
Item

1
implies
Item

2
.
∎

3.2.
Candidate for a t-structure

The derived category of an abelian category has a standard t-structure whose heart is the initial abelian category. On the derived category of an exact category such a t-structure need not exist, as the heart of any t-structure is abelian. With additional assumptions on the exact category, it is possible to construct a t-structure on the derived category of an exact category; see
[
Hub95
, Section 2.1]
,
[
Sch99
, Section 1.2.2]
and
[
HKvRW23
, Proposition 3.5]
. These constructions all require the existence of kernels. Using left Ext-acyclicity, we define a pair of subcategories that generalizes these constructions. We will give necessary and sufficient conditions for this pair to be a t-structure.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an exact category and
*\in\{\emptyset,b,+,-\}
. We denote by
{\mathcal{U}}^{*}({\mathcal{E}})
and
{\mathcal{V}}^{*}({\mathcal{E}})
the full subcategories of
\operatorname{D}^{*}({\mathcal{E}})
containing the complexes that are
{\mathcal{E}}
-acyclic in positive and negative degrees, respectively. For an abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}
, the pair
({\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{A}}),{\mathcal{V}}({\mathcal{A}}))
is the standard t-structure. However, in general
{\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{E}})\cap{\mathcal{V}}({\mathcal{E}})
is not necessarily abelian.

We denote by
{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
and
{\mathcal{U}}^{*}_{r}({\mathcal{E}})
the full subcategories of
\operatorname{D}^{*}({\mathcal{E}})
containing the complexes that are left
\text{Ext}_{{\mathcal{E}}}
-acyclic in negative degrees and that are right
\text{Ext}_{{\mathcal{E}}}

3.6.
The heart

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an exact category. By
[
Dye05
]
, the triangulated structure of
\operatorname{D}^{*}({\mathcal{E}})
induces an exact structure on
{\mathcal{U}}^{*}({\mathcal{E}})\cap{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
. Explicitly, a sequence
L\to M\to N
is a conflation if and only if it fits into an exact triangle
L\to M\to N\to\Sigma L
in
\operatorname{D}^{*}({\mathcal{E}})
. We call this exact structure the
admissible exact structure (in
\operatorname{D}^{*}({\mathcal{E}})
)
.

We observe

{\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{E}})\cap{\mathcal{V}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})={\mathcal{U}}^{-}({\mathcal{E}})\cap{\mathcal{V}}^{-}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{U}}^{+}({\mathcal{E}})\cap{\mathcal{V}}^{+}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})={\mathcal{U}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})\cap{\mathcal{V}}^{b}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})

as the boundedness to the right has no effect on the intersection.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an exact category. We denote by

(1)

{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})
the exact category whose underlying additive category is
{\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{E}})\cap{\mathcal{V}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
and that is equipped with the admissible exact structure; this means
{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})
contains the complexes of
\operatorname{D}({\mathcal{E}})
that are left
\text{Ext}_{{\mathcal{E}}}
-acyclic in negative degrees, and
{\mathcal{E}}
-acyclic in positive degrees;

(2)

{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
the exact category whose underlying additive category is
{\mathcal{U}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})\cap{\mathcal{V}}^{b}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
and that is equipped with the admissible exact structure; this means
{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
contains the bounded complexes which are
{\mathcal{E}}
-acyclic in positive degrees and degrees
<n
for some integer
n\leq 0
, and left
\text{Ext}_{{\mathcal{E}}}
-acyclic in negative degrees.

We denote by
{\mathcal{RH}}({\mathcal{E}})
and
{\mathcal{RH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
the dual constructions.

We obtain a sequence of fully exact subcategories

{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})\subseteq{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})\,.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an exact category. Then

(1)

{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})
is a resolving subcategory;

(2)

{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
is a finitely resolving subcategory.

Proof.

We first show that
{\mathcal{E}}
is resolving in
{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})
. Let

. We may assume that

is concentrated in non-positive degrees. Brutal truncation yields the exact triangle

As

and

, this induces an
{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})
-conflation. Therefore
X^{0}\to X
is an
{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})
-deflation and
Item

R1
is satisfied.

Let

be an
{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})
-conflation with

. This means there is an exact triangle

in
\operatorname{D}({\mathcal{E}})
. Hence

where
M
and
N
lie in degrees 0 and 1, respectively. As the complex is
{\mathcal{E}}
-acyclic in degree 1, the morphism

is an
{\mathcal{E}}
-deflation. In particular,

has a kernel in
{\mathcal{E}}
and

As

is a fully exact subcategory of
{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})
,
{\mathcal{E}}
is a resolving subcategory of
{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
. By definition every object in
{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
is isomorphic to a complex

for some

that is left
\text{Ext}_{{\mathcal{E}}}
-acyclic in negative degrees. As

-conflation for every

-acyclic with

. So

∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be an exact category.

(1)

If
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
is a resolving subcategory, then there is an equivalence
{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})\to{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{F}})
of exact categories.

(2)

If
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
is a finitely resolving subcategory, then there is an equivalence
{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})\to{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{F}})
of exact categories.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
by resolving. By
[

, Theorem 3.11]
, the inclusion
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
lifts to an equivalence

of triangulated categories. By

, this equivalence restricts to a functor
{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})\to{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{F}})
. As the restriction is essentially surjective by

, it is an equivalence.

When

{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})\to{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{F}})
.
∎

From

we immediately obtain the following

.

.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an exact category. Then there are equivalences

.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an exact category and let

to show the claim for a complex

.

Definition 3.14
.

By construction, the subcategories

{\mathcal{E}}
-inflation and every epimorphism in
{\mathcal{E}}
is an
{\mathcal{E}}
-deflation.

The equivalence of

Lemma 3.16

{\mathcal{E}}
-inflation.

Proof.

The implication

We assume

{\mathcal{E}}
-inflation.

We assume

3
holds. Let

and

is not

-acyclic. Then

is quasi-isomorphic to a complex

with

. We assume

. Then

has to be a monomorphism, and by assumption an

-inflation. This is a contradiction, and hence

.

Proof of
Theorem

3.15
.

We assume
Item

1
holds. Since

is non-negative, equality holds. Analogously we get
{\mathcal{RH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})={\mathcal{E}}
.

We assume

3
holds. Let
{\mathcal{C}}\supseteq{\mathcal{E}}
be a non-negative subcategory of
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
. From

we obtain

{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq(\Sigma^{>0}{\mathcal{C}})^{\perp}\subseteq(\Sigma^{>0}{\mathcal{E}})^{\perp}=(\Sigma{\mathcal{U}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}}))^{\perp}={\mathcal{V}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})\,.

Using the dual of

we obtain

∎

Remark 3.17
.

It follows from
Theorem

3.15
: If
{\mathcal{E}}
is maximally non-negative in
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
, then the exact structure of
{\mathcal{E}}
is the maximal exact structure on its underlying additive category. It is easy to see that in this case
{\mathcal{E}}
is abelian if and only if it is pre-abelian.

In fact, if
{\mathcal{E}}
is the maximal exact structure on a quasi-abelian category which is not an abelian category, then it is not maximally non-negative in
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
.

An important example of maximally non-negative subcategories are the hearts of t-structures. More generally, we obtain non-negative subcategories through a weaker notion than t-structures:

Definition 3.18
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a triangulated category. We call a pair of two full subcategories
({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}})
of
{\mathcal{T}}
a
t-pair
if

(1)

{\mathcal{U}}
and
{\mathcal{V}}
are closed under direct summands, isomorphisms and extensions;

(2)

\Sigma{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathcal{U}}
and
{\mathcal{V}}\subseteq\Sigma{\mathcal{V}}
; and

(3)

\Hom_{\mathcal{T}}(\Sigma{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}})=0
.

We call
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{U}}\cap{\mathcal{V}}
the
heart
of the t-pair
({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}})
.

We say a t-pair
({\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{V}})
is
left maximal
if
(\Sigma{\mathcal{U}})^{\perp}={\mathcal{V}}
. It is
right maximal
if
\Sigma{\mathcal{U}}={}^{\perp}{\mathcal{V}}
. We say it is
maximal
if it is left and right maximal.

In
Proposition

3.3
we have shown that
({\mathcal{U}}^{*}({\mathcal{E}}),{\mathcal{V}}^{*}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}}))
is a left maximal t-pair in
\operatorname{D}^{*}({\mathcal{E}})
, and dually
({\mathcal{U}}^{*}_{r}({\mathcal{E}}),{\mathcal{V}}^{*}({\mathcal{E}}))
is a right maximal t-pair in
\operatorname{D}^{*}({\mathcal{E}})
. By applying these constructions after each other, we get a maximal t-pair:

Theorem 3.19
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a weakly idempotent complete exact category. Then
({\mathcal{U}}^{b}_{r}({\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})),{\mathcal{V}}^{b}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}}))
and
({\mathcal{U}}^{b}_{r}({\mathcal{E}}),{\mathcal{V}}^{b}_{\ell}({\mathcal{RH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})))
are maximal t-pairs in
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
.

Proof.

From
Proposition

3.3
applied to
({\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}}))^{\mathrm{op}}
, we get (1), (2) and the second part of (3), as well as
(\Sigma{\mathcal{U}}^{b}_{r}({\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})))^{\perp}\supseteq{\mathcal{V}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
using
Corollary

3.12
. Further, we get

\Sigma{\mathcal{U}}^{b}_{r}({\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}}))\supseteq\Sigma{\mathcal{U}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})\quad\implies\quad(\Sigma{\mathcal{U}}^{b}_{r}({\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})))^{\perp}\subseteq(\Sigma{\mathcal{U}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}}))^{\perp}={\mathcal{V}}^{b}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})\,;

the latter again holds by
Proposition

3.3
.
∎

The hearts of the t-pairs in
Theorem

3.19
are
{\mathcal{RH}}^{b}({\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}}))
and
{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{RH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}}))
. These do not need to coincide in general:

Example 3.20
.

Let
k
be a field and
\Lambda=k(1\leftarrow 2)
be the finite-dimensional
k
-algebra given by quiver representations of Dynkin type
A_{2}
. The category of finite-dimensional
\Lambda
-modules
\operatorname{mod}(\Lambda)
is Krull–Schmidt and has three indecomposable objects
P(1)
,
P(2)=I(1)
and
I(2)
.
The bounded derived category
\operatorname{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}(\Lambda))
is an Auslander–Reiten category; this means it is Krull–Schmidt and every indecomposable object fits into an almost split triangle. The Auslander–Reiten quiver is

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma^{-1}I(2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P(2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma P(1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma I(2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P(1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I(2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma P(2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}

where the dashed arrows point from the first to the third object of an exact triangle.

We consider the exact category
{\mathcal{E}}=\add(I(1)\oplus I(2))
. Then

\displaystyle{\mathcal{RH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle=\add(P(1)\oplus P(2)\oplus I(2))\cong\Lambda\text{-}{\rm mod}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle=\add(P(2)\oplus I(2)\oplus\Sigma P(1))\,.

Both are hearts of a t-structure and therefore maximally non-negative. In particular, we have

{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{RH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}}))={\mathcal{RH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})\neq{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})={\mathcal{RH}}^{b}({\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}}))\,.

The bounded derived equivalence between
{\mathcal{RH}}^{b}({\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}}))
and
{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{RH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}}))
can be seen as a generalization of derived equivalences for Artin algebras induced from tilting modules of projective dimension at most
n
and, more generally, of those induced by tilting objects in abelian categories, cf. the following example.

Example 3.21
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional algebra over a field

and

the category of finite-dimensional left
A
-modules. For

let

be an
n
-tilting module, that is

is self-orthogonal (

for all

),
\pdim_{A}T\leq n
and there exists an exact sequence

with

, cf.
[

, Chapter III]
. The category

is extension-closed and we let
{\mathcal{E}}
be

equipped with the fully exact structure. Then
{\mathcal{E}}
is coresolving in
A\text{-}{\rm mod}
. Moreover, for any

taking an injective resolution yields an exact sequence

are injective

\Ext^{i}_{A}(T,\Omega^{-n}M)=\Ext^{i+n}_{A}(T,M)=0
for all

as

. So
\Omega^{-n}M\in{\mathcal{E}}
and
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq A\text{-}{\rm mod}
is
n
-coresolving
; cf.

. In particular,

A\text{-}{\rm mod}={\mathcal{RH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})={\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{RH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})).

Let
B\coloneqq\End_{A}(T)^{\mathrm{op}}
. Then

, viewed as a left

-module, is
n
-tilting by
[
Miy86
, Theorem 1.5]
. Equivalently,

with

-cotilting module; see for example
[

, Section 2]
.
Dually to before,

is extension-closed and we let

be

{\mathcal{F}}
is
n
-resolving in

. In particular,

A classical result of Miyashita

, Theorem 1.16]
gives an equivalence of exact categories

\Hom_{A}(T,-)\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{F}}\,.

Therefore, the induced derived equivalence between

and

is implied by our construction:

induces triangle equivalences

\operatorname{D}^{*}(A\text{-}{\rm mod})\leftarrow\operatorname{D}^{*}({\mathcal{E}})\cong\operatorname{D}^{*}({\mathcal{F}})\to\operatorname{D}^{*}(B\text{-}{\rm mod})

for

using
[
HvR20a
]

Finitely generated modules over

-varieties in the sense of Auslander; see
[

]
. Using this we found the

-cotilting module

. In general, this is not possible.

An object
T
in an abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}
is
n
-tilting if

has enough projectives given by
\add(T)
and
{\mathcal{E}}
is
n
-coresolving.

-modules is abelian. Then

and we get a bounded derived equivalence between

Next we provide an example of maximally non-negative subcategories of
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
that are not abelian.

Example 3.22
.

Let
\Lambda=k[T]/(T^{2})
for any field
k
and
{\mathcal{E}}=\add(\Lambda)\subseteq\Lambda\text{-}{\rm mod}
. The only exact structure on

is the split exact structure and all epimorphisms and all monomorphisms are split. Hence, by

, the category

is maximally non-negative in

. Yet, the category
{\mathcal{E}}
is not abelian because there is a morphism, namely

, which has no epimorphism-monomorphism factorization.
On the other hand
{\mathcal{E}}
is not maximally non-negative in
\operatorname{D}^{-}({\mathcal{E}})=\operatorname{D}^{-}(\Lambda\text{-}{\rm mod})
as it is properly contained in
\Lambda\text{-}{\rm mod}
.

This

shows the t-pairs in

3.19
need not be t-structures in general. Moreover, since the t-pairs are maximal, they are not contained in a t-structure.

Question 3.23
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an exact category, that is not abelian and maximally non-negative in
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
. Does there exists a bounded t-structure on
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
? Or equivalently, does there exists an abelian maximally non-negative subcategory in
\operatorname{D}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
?

4.
Functorial view on Ext-resolutions

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a small additive category. The category of right
{\mathcal{A}}
-modules is the category of additive functors
\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{A}})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Add({\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{op}},{\mathcal{A}}b)
. By
[
Fre03
, Theorem 5.11]
, the category
\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{A}})
is abelian and the (co)kernels are precisely the pointwise (co)kernels. We denote by

\mathbb{Y}(-)\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{A}})\,,\quad M\mapsto\Hom_{{\mathcal{A}}}(-,M)

the
Yoneda embedding
. The functors
\Hom_{{\mathcal{A}}}(-,X)
are called

. In the functor category
\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{A}})
the representable functors are projective.

An object
\mathsf{F}\in\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{A}})
is called

, if there exists a morphism

in
{\mathcal{A}}
, such that

\mathbb{Y}(M)\xrightarrow{\mathbb{Y}(f)}\mathbb{Y}(N)\to\mathsf{F}\to 0

is exact in
\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{A}})
. The subcategory of finitely presented objects in
\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{A}})
is extension- and inflation-closed. We denote by
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{A}})
the exact category of the finitely presented objects in
\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{A}})
equipped with the fully exact structure.

4.1.

{\mathcal{E}}
be a small exact category. We are interested in subcategories of
\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{E}})
consisting of functors that have an Ext-resolution;
that is functors
\coker(\mathbb{Y}(d_{X}^{-1}))
where

is an
\text{Ext}_{{\mathcal{E}}}
-resolution. We say such a functor

\text{Ext}_{{\mathcal{E}}}
-resolution

4.6.
Localization by deflation-percolating subcategories

We recall the localization of exact categories as introduced in
[
HvR20a
]
. This construction yields an (inflation/deflation-)exact structure on the localization. However, we will not consider the induced exact structure, as it is not compatible with the exact structure on the localization of
\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{A}})
; see
Remark

4.12
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an exact category. A full subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq{\mathcal{E}}
is
deflation-percolating
, if it is a Serre subcategory and every morphism
L\to M
in
{\mathcal{E}}
with
M\in{\mathcal{C}}
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-admissible; that is, it factors as an
{\mathcal{E}}
-deflation followed by an
{\mathcal{E}}
-inflation.

Remark 4.7
.

This is the definition of deflation-percolating following
[
HKvR22
]
. In
[
HvR20a
, Definition 6.1]
this notion is called admissibly deflation-percolating, and deflation-percolating is a weaker notion; see
[
HvR20a
, Lemma 6.3]
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a deflation-percolating subcategory of an exact category
{\mathcal{E}}
. The class
S_{\mathcal{C}}
of finite compositions of
{\mathcal{E}}
-deflations with kernel in
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{E}}
-inflations with cokernel in
{\mathcal{C}}
is a right multiplicative system, also called a right calculus of fractions; see
[
HvR20a
, Theorem 6.14]
. Then

{\mathcal{E}}/{\mathcal{C}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathcal{E}}[S_{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}]

is the
localization of
{\mathcal{E}}
by
{\mathcal{C}}
.

and
N,C\in{\mathcal{E}}
, we also have
I\in{\mathcal{E}}
. As
{\mathcal{E}}
is extension-closed in
{\mathcal{F}}
and
K,I\in{\mathcal{E}}
, it follows that
M\in{\mathcal{E}}
. For
t=\id_{M}
, the composition
st\colon M\to N
lies in
S_{\mathcal{C}}
with
M,N\in{\mathcal{E}}
. Hence
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
is right cofinal in
S_{\mathcal{C}}
.
∎

4.9.
Effaceable functors

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a small exact category. An object
\mathsf{F}\in\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{E}})
is
effaceable
, if there exists an
{\mathcal{E}}
-deflation
d
such that
\mathsf{F}\cong\coker(\mathbb{Y}(d))
. We denote the strictly full subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{E}})
of effaceable objects by
\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})
. The effaceable functors are the finitely presented objects of a bigger class of functors, namely, the locally effaceable functors. An object
\mathsf{F}\in\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{E}})
is
locally effaceable
if for any
A\in{\mathcal{E}}
and
x\in\mathsf{F}(A)
, there exists an
{\mathcal{E}}
-deflation
p\colon B\to A
such that
\mathsf{F}(p)(x)=0
. We denote the strictly full subcategory of locally effaceable objects by
\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{E}})
. One has

(4.9.1)

\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})=\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{E}})\cap\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})\,;

see
[
Eno21
, Lemma 2.13]
and also
[
HKvR22
, Proposition 3.20]
.

Remark 4.10
.

The notation for (locally) effaceable functors is not consistent in the literature. In
[
Gro57
, 2.2]
and
[
Kel90
, Appendix A]
the locally effaceable functors are called effaceable, while in
[
HKvR22
, Definition 3.19]
the locally effaceable functors are called weakly effaceable. Our notation follows
[
Kra22
, Section 2.3]
.

By
[
Kel90
, Appendix A]
, the subcategory
\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{E}})\subseteq\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{E}})
is Serre, and, by
[
HKvR22
, Proposition 3.23, Corollary 3.24]
, the subcategory
\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})\subseteq\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})
is deflation-percolating. Hence the localizations exist, and

\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})\to\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{E}})

is a full embedding into an abelian category. Moreover,
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})
viewed as a subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{E}})
is extension-closed; this follows from
[
Nor24
, Theorem 3.4]
.

We denote by
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
the exact category with the underlying additive category
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})
equipped with the fully exact structure. This is the
left quotient category
introduced in
[
Rum20
, Section 3]
. By
[
Rum20
, Theorem 4]
,
{\mathcal{E}}
is semi-resolving in
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
.

Remark 4.11
.

By
[
Kel90
, Appendix A]
, the localization
\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{E}})
is equivalent to the subcategory of left exact functors of
\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{E}})
. By
[
Nor24
, Theorem 3.4]
, the subcategory
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})
can be identified with the subcategory of compact objects.

Remark 4.12
.

The fully exact structure on
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})
need not coincide with the exact structure induced by the localization. The exact structure on
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
is the maximal exact structure, it consists of all kernel-cokernel pairs in
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
. However, there exist kernel-cokernel pairs in
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
which are not induced from a conflation in
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})
. For example, for
\mathsf{F}\in{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{E}})
with an
\text{Ext}_{{\mathcal{E}}}
-resolution
X
the sequence

\coker(d_{X}^{-2})\to\mathbb{Y}(X^{0})\to\mathsf{F}

is a kernel-cokernel pair in
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})
by
Lemma

5.2
. However, it is not a kernel-cokernel pair in
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})
as the morphism
\coker(d_{X}^{-2})\to\operatorname{ker}(\mathbb{Y}(X^{0})\to\mathsf{F})
need not be an isomorphism in
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})
.

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a small exact category. Then
\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})
is a deflation-percolating subcategory of
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{E}})
and of
{\mathcal{M}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
.

Proof.

This follows from
Lemma

4.8
as
\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})\subseteq\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})
is deflation-percolating, and
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{E}})
and
{\mathcal{M}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
are deflation-closed, fully exact subcategories of
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})
by
Lemma

4.5
.
∎

5.
In the left quotient category

We have shown that
\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})
is deflation-percolating in the deflation-closed fully exact subcategories

{\mathcal{M}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{E}})\subseteq\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})\,.

By
Lemma

4.8
, we obtain a chain of subcategories

{\mathcal{M}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})\subseteq{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})\,.

We want to equip each localization in this chain with an exact structure.

First, we show that the Yoneda embedding sends left
\text{Ext}_{{\mathcal{E}}}
-acyclic sequences to acyclic sequences in the abelian category
\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})
. In particular,
\text{Ext}_{{\mathcal{E}}}
-resolutions become resolutions.

and
\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{F}})
are the pointwise pullbacks and pushouts, respectively, the functor
r
preserves pullbacks and pushouts.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a small exact category and
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
be a resolving subcategory.
For
\mathsf{F}\in\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{F}})
one has
\mathsf{F}\in\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{F}})
if and only if
r(\mathsf{F})\in\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{E}})
.

Proof.

For the forward direction assume
\mathsf{F}\in\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{F}})
. Let
N\in{\mathcal{E}}
and
x\in r(\mathsf{F})(N)
. As
x\in r(\mathsf{F})(N)=\mathsf{F}(N)
, there exists by assumption an
{\mathcal{F}}
-deflation
p\colon M\to N
with
M\in{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mathsf{F}(p)(x)=0
. As
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
is resolving, there exists an
{\mathcal{F}}
-deflation
q\colon L\to M
with
L\in{\mathcal{E}}
. Then the composition
pq
is an
{\mathcal{E}}
-deflation and
r(\mathsf{F})(pq)(x)=\mathsf{F}(pq)(x)=0
. Hence
r(\mathsf{F})\in\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{E}})
.

For the backward direction assume
r(\mathsf{F})\in\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{E}})
. Let
N\in{\mathcal{F}}
and
x\in\mathsf{F}(N)
. Since
{\mathcal{E}}
is a resolving subcategory there exists a
{\mathcal{F}}
-deflation
p\colon M\to N
with
M\in{\mathcal{E}}
. We consider
\mathsf{F}(p)(x)\in\mathsf{F}(M)=r(\mathsf{F})(M)
. By assumption, there exists an
{\mathcal{E}}
-deflation
q\colon L\to M
such that
0=r(\mathsf{F})(q)(\mathsf{F}(p)(x))=\mathsf{F}(pq)(x)
.
∎

As a consequence of this
Lemma
, there is an induced functor

\bar{r}\colon\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{F}})/\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{F}})\to\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{E}})\,,

that preserves pullbacks and pushouts.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a small exact category and
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}
be a resolving subcategory. Then
\bar{r}
restricts to an equivalence
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{F}})\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
.

Proof.

To avoid confusion, we write

\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(-)\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{E}})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(-)\colon{\mathcal{F}}\to\operatorname{Mod}({\mathcal{F}})

for the Yoneda functors on
{\mathcal{E}}
and
{\mathcal{F}}
, respectively. We observe that
r(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(M))=\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(M)
when
M\in{\mathcal{E}}
.

Step 1
.

Let
\alpha\colon\mathsf{F}\to\mathsf{G}
be a morphism in
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})
. Then there exists a morphism
\widehat{\alpha}\colon\widehat{\mathsf{F}}\to\widehat{\mathsf{G}}
in
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{F}})
such that
r(\widehat{\alpha})=\alpha
. Additionally, composition is preserved under this lift from
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})
to
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{F}})
.

Let
\mathsf{F}=\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(f))
and
\mathsf{G}=\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(g))
. We find the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(L)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(L^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(f))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(M^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(g))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(f)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(g)}

in
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})
. Using Yoneda’s lemma, we may lift the left square to a square in
{\mathcal{E}}
. We then embed it into
{\mathcal{F}}
under the inclusion functor and apply
\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(-)
. Composition is preserved, and we find an induced map
\widehat{\alpha}\colon\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(f))\to\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(g))
which satisfies the claim.

Step 2
.

For any morphism
f
in
{\mathcal{F}}
, there exists a morphism
g
in
{\mathcal{E}}
such that
\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(f))\cong\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(g))
in
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{F}})
.

Let
f\colon L\to M
be a morphism in
{\mathcal{F}}
. Since
{\mathcal{E}}
is resolving in
{\mathcal{F}}
, we find an
{\mathcal{F}}
-deflation
p\colon\widehat{M}\to M
with
\widehat{M}\in{\mathcal{E}}
. Form the pullback of
f
along
p
to obtain a morphism
\widehat{f}\colon P\to\widehat{M}
and an
{\mathcal{F}}
-deflation
P\to L
. Take another
{\mathcal{F}}
-deflation
q\colon\widehat{L}\to P
with
\widehat{L}\in{\mathcal{E}}
. We have a morphism
g\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\widehat{f}q\colon\widehat{L}\to\widehat{M}
in
{\mathcal{E}}
. As the Yoneda functor preserves pullbacks, we observe that
\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(f))=\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(\widehat{f}))
. Since
\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(q)
is a
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
-deflation, it follows that
\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(\widehat{f}))\cong\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(g))
.

Step 3
.

Let
f
and
g
be morphisms in
{\mathcal{E}}
. If
\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(f))=\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(g))
in
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})
then
\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(f))=\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(g))
in
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{F}})
.

By
1
, we can lift any morphism
\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(f))\to\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(g))
to a morphism of their presentations. The morphism on the presentation is unique up to homotopy. The claim follows as we can lift the homotopies to
{\mathcal{E}}
using Yoneda’s lemma.

Step 4
.

The functor
\bar{r}
restricts to a functor
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{F}})\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
; that is, if
\mathsf{F}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{F}})
, then
\bar{r}(\mathsf{F})\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
.

Using first
2
, let
\mathsf{F}=\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(g))
with
g
a morphism in
{\mathcal{E}}
. As
r(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(g))=\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(g)
and
\bar{r}
preserves cokernels, we have

(5.8.1)

\bar{r}(\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(g)))=\coker(\bar{r}(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(g)))=\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(g))\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}}).

Step 5
.

The restriction of
\bar{r}
to
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{F}})\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
is essentially surjective; that is, if
\mathsf{E}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
, then there exists
\mathsf{F}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{F}})
with
\mathsf{E}\cong\bar{r}(\mathsf{F})
.

Let
\mathsf{E}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
be of the form
\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{E}}}(g))
for some morphism
g\in{\mathcal{E}}
. Then by
Eq.

5.8.1
,
\mathsf{F}=\coker(\mathbb{Y}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(g))
satisfies the claim.

Step 6
.

The restriction of
\bar{r}
to
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{F}})\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
is full.

By
1
any morphism in
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})
lifts to a morphism in
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{F}})
. For a morphism
\alpha
in
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{F}})
we have the following equivalences

\displaystyle\alpha\in S_{\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{F}})}\text{ (that is invertible in ${\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{F}})$)}

\displaystyle\iff\operatorname{ker}(\alpha),\coker(\alpha)\in\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{F}})

\displaystyle\iff\operatorname{ker}(r(\alpha)),\coker(r(\alpha))\in\operatorname{Eff}({\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle\iff r(\alpha)\in S_{\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})}\text{ (that is invertible in ${\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})$)}\,.

For the first and third equivalence we use the localization of the ambient abelian category and the second equivalence holds by
Lemma

5.7
.

Step 7
.

The restriction of
\bar{r}
to
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{F}})\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
is faithful.

To see that
\bar{r}
is faithful, let
\eta\colon\mathsf{E}\to\mathsf{F}
be a morphism in
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{F}})
such that
\bar{r}(\eta)=0
in
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
. Suppose that
\eta
is represented by the zigzag
\mathsf{E}\xleftarrow{\alpha}\mathsf{G}\xrightarrow{\beta}\mathsf{F}
with
\alpha\in S_{\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})}
.
There is a morpshim
\mathsf{H}\xrightarrow{\gamma}\mathsf{G}
in
S_{\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})}
, and there is a homotopy witnessing
\beta\gamma=0
in
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})
. By a similar argument as in
1
, the homotopy lifts to
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{F}})
. By
Lemmas

5.7
and
4.9.1
the lift of
\gamma
to
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{F}})
lies in
S_{\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{F}})}
.
∎

6.
The resolving completion

Let
{\mathcal{E}}

and

Definition 6.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
be a fully exact embedding such that
{\mathcal{E}}
is (finitely) resolving in
{\mathcal{R}}

{\mathcal{F}}\to{\mathcal{R}}

. In particular, there is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{E}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{F}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{R}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\,.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists i}

It is straightforward to check that then the fully exact embedding
{\mathcal{F}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
is (finitely) resolving.

.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}

By

,

The uniqueness of this functor follows again from

.
∎

Theorem 6.4
.

{\mathcal{E}}

.

Let

.

For
{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})\neq{\mathcal{RH}}^{b}({\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{P}}))
it is enough to find an epimorphism in
{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
that is not an
{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
-deflation by the dual of
Lemma

3.16
. We use the identification
{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})={\mathcal{M}}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
. Let
a\colon P(2)\to P(1)
be the composition of the canonical morphisms
P(2)\to S(2)\to P(1)
. We show that
a
satisfies the desired conditions. In the category
\operatorname{mod}(\Lambda)
, we have
\coker(a)=S(1)
and
\operatorname{ker}(a)\cong S(3)^{\oplus\mathbb{N}}
. This implies that
S(1)
is not in
{\mathcal{M}}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
. Next, we observe that
S(1)
is an injective object, so every object
M
with a non-zero morphism
S(1)\to M
must have
S(1)
as a summand. But objects in
{\mathcal{M}}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
have no summand
S(1)
, therefore
a
is an epimorphism in
{\mathcal{M}}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
. It is not a
{\mathcal{M}}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
-deflation because it has no kernel in
{\mathcal{M}}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
.

6.9.
The n-resolving completion

The constructions of the finite resolving completion
{\mathcal{LH}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
and
{\mathcal{R}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
can be further restricted to those resolutions that have a universal bound.

We define

{\mathcal{V}}_{\ell}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})\coloneqq{\mathcal{V}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})\cap\Sigma^{n}{\mathcal{V}}({\mathcal{E}})\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{U}}_{r}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})\coloneqq{\mathcal{U}}_{r}({\mathcal{E}})\cap\Sigma^{-n}{\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{E}})\,.

We denote by
{\mathcal{LH}}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})
the category
{\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{E}})\cap{\mathcal{V}}_{\ell}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})
equipped with the admissible exact structure; and by
{\mathcal{RH}}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})
the category
{\mathcal{U}}_{r}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})\cap{\mathcal{V}}({\mathcal{E}})
equipped with the admissible exact structure.

For any
n\geq 0
, the category
{\mathcal{LH}}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})
contains
{\mathcal{E}}
as a finitely resolving subcategory. Moreover,
{\mathcal{E}}
is a
n
-resolving
subcategory: A fully exact subcategory
{\mathcal{E}}
of an exact category
{\mathcal{F}}
is called
n
-resolving in
{\mathcal{F}}
if
{\mathcal{E}}
is resolving in
{\mathcal{F}}
, and for any
M
in
{\mathcal{F}}
, the induced
{\mathcal{F}}
-acyclic sequence in
Eq.

1.8.1
is of length
n
; that is
X^{i}=0
for
i<n
. By
[
HvR20b
, Theorem 4.1]
, there is a derived equivalence

\operatorname{D}({\mathcal{E}})\cong\operatorname{D}({\mathcal{LH}}^{n}({\mathcal{E}}))

for all
n\geq 1
.

Similarly, we can also specialize the construction of
{\mathcal{R}}^{b}({\mathcal{E}})
: We define
{\mathcal{M}}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})
as the subcategory of
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{E}})
which consist of functors with
\text{Ext}_{{\mathcal{E}}}
-resolution
X
with
X^{i}=0
for
i<-n
. Analogously to
Corollaries

4.13
and
5.3
,
\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})
is a deflation-percolating subcategory of
{\mathcal{M}}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})
for
n\geq 2
and the localization
{\mathcal{M}}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})
is extension-closed in
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
. We therefore define categories
{\mathcal{R}}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})
as the category
{\mathcal{M}}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{eff}(E)
equipped with the fully exact structure in
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
for all
n\geq 2
.

For
n=1
, we cannot localize
{\mathcal{M}}^{1}({\mathcal{E}})
at the
{\mathcal{E}}
-effaceable functors, as the
{\mathcal{E}}
-effaceable functors are not contained in
{\mathcal{M}}^{1}({\mathcal{E}})
. To rectify this, we replace
{\mathcal{M}}^{1}({\mathcal{E}})
by the full subcategory
\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}^{1}({\mathcal{E}})
of
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})
consisting of functors of the form
\mathsf{F}=\coker{\mathbb{Y}(f)}
, where
f
is left
{\mathcal{E}}
-admissible; that is
f
factors as an
{\mathcal{E}}
-deflation followed by a monomorphism. One can show that
\operatorname{eff}(E)
is deflation-percolating in this larger category, and the localization is extension-closed in
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
. We denote by
{\mathcal{R}}^{1}({\mathcal{E}})
category
\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}^{1}({\mathcal{E}})/\operatorname{eff}({\mathcal{E}})
equipped with the fully exact structure in
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}({\mathcal{E}})
.

For all
n\geq 1
, it holds that
{\mathcal{E}}
is an
n
-resolving subcategory of
{\mathcal{R}}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})
. Moreover, if we define the
n
-resolving completion analogously to
Definition

6.1
, then the proofs of
Propositions

6.2
,
6.3
and
6.4
can be adjusted to show:

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a (small) exact category. Then
{\mathcal{LH}}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})
and
{\mathcal{R}}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})
are
n
-resolving completions for all
n\geq 1
.

Moreover, there is an equivalence
{\mathcal{LH}}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})\cong{\mathcal{R}}^{n}({\mathcal{E}})
of exact categories for all
n\geq 1
.

Previously, the 1-resolving and 2-resolving completions have been described in special cases when they provide t-structures; see
Proposition

3.5
.

Example 6.11
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a quasi-abelian category equipped with the maximal exact structure. Then
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})=\hat{{\mathcal{M}}}^{1}({\mathcal{E}})
. In particular, this yields

{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})\cong{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{E}})={\mathcal{R}}^{1}({\mathcal{E}})\cong{\mathcal{LH}}^{1}({\mathcal{E}})\,;

the latter is the full subcategory category of
\operatorname{D}({\mathcal{E}})
consisting of two-term complexes concentrated in degrees
0,-1
given by a monomorphism. This is the left heart as described in
[
Sch99
, Section 1.2.2]
.

Example 6.12
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an exact category with kernels. Then
\operatorname{mod}({\mathcal{E}})={\mathcal{M}}^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
. In particularly, this yields

{\mathcal{LH}}({\mathcal{E}})\cong{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{E}})={\mathcal{R}}^{2}({\mathcal{E}})\cong{\mathcal{LH}}^{2}({\mathcal{E}})\,;

the latter is the left heart as described in
[
HKvRW23
, Corollary 3.10]
. It is the full subcategory of
\operatorname{D}({\mathcal{E}})
consisting of three-term complexes of the form
\operatorname{ker}{f}\to L\xrightarrow{f}M
concentrated in degrees
0,-1,-2
.

There are plenty of exact categories that do not have kernels. For example, the category of finitely presented modules over a ring that is not left coherent. For other categories, like the category of complete
\mathsf{LB}
-spaces, it is not known whether kernels exist; see
[
Weg25
, Section 5]
.

Remark 6.13
.

We expect that most of our results can be generalized to one-sided exact categories as introduced in
[
Rum11
,
BC13
]
, similarly to
[
HKvRW23
]
.
This will be done in the PhD thesis of the first author.
