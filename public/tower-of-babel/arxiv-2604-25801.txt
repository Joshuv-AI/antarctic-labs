Hidden Hermiticity of the Jaynes–Cummings Projected Liouvillian
Kejun Liu

Abstract
The Jaynes–Cummings model has been studied for six decades, yet its Nakajima–Zwanzig projected Liouvillian Q ​ L ​ Q QLQ hides an unexpected structure: although manifestly non-Hermitian, its spectrum is purely real across all numerically accessible parameters. We resolve this anomaly by constructing a positive-definite metric η > 0 \eta>0 such that ( Q ​ L ​ Q ) † ​ η = η ⁡ ( Q ​ L ​ Q ) (QLQ)^{\dagger}\eta=\eta(QLQ) , proving that the generator of quantum memory is pseudo-Hermitian. The effect is genuine, not a block-diagonal artefact: the Δ ​ N = 0 \Delta N=0 and Δ ​ N = ± 2 \Delta N=\pm 2 sectors remain strongly non-Hermitian, yet the global spectrum is protected by η \eta . Building on Ng and Rabani’s identification of exceptional-point physics in Q ​ L ​ Q QLQ , we show that the Jaynes–Cummings point sits inside a symmetry-protected pseudo-Hermitian phase. Under continuous deformation toward the full Rabi model this protection breaks and re-enters, producing a U ⁡ ( 1 ) → U(1)\to complex → Z 2 \to Z_{2} phase structure bracketed by exceptional-point boundaries. The result supplies a structural reason for Hardy-space analyticity of the memory kernel in the canonical light–matter model and identifies a phase transition between oscillatory and genuinely dissipative quantum memory.



The Nakajima–Zwanzig (NZ) projection-operator formalism
nakajima1958
;
zwanzig1960
is foundational to non-Markovian open quantum systems. The projected Liouvillian

QLQ=Q\,\mathcal{L}\,Q,\qquad Q=\mathbb{I}-P,

(1)

where
\mathcal{L}=[H,\cdot]
and
P
is the stationary NZ projector
breuer2002
, generates the memory kernel
\mathcal{K}(t)\sim P\mathcal{L}Q\,e^{iQ\mathcal{L}Qt}\,Q\mathcal{L}P
. While
\mathcal{K}(t)
and the reduced state
\sigma(t)
have been studied extensively,
QLQ
itself has attracted little direct attention. This is a consequential gap: the recent Hardy-space programme
liu2026hardy
has shown that the analytic structure of the memory kernel is controlled by the spectrum of
QLQ
: a purely real spectrum guarantees the Cauchy spectral representation and hence Hardy-class membership, which in turn validates Kramers–Kronig (KK) dispersion relations.

The community default is that
QLQ
, being manifestly non-Hermitian whenever the bath reference state
\rho_{B}\neq\mathbb{I}/d_{B}
, has a generically complex spectrum. A numerical survey
liu2026hardy
confirmed this for the spin-boson model (
|\Imag\lambda|\sim 10^{-2}
–
10^{-1}
at moderate coupling). The anomaly is the Jaynes–Cummings (JC) model
jaynes1963
:
QLQ
has a
purely real spectrum
across all numerically accessible parameters (
N_{\rm max}=3
–
20
,
g=0.1
–
2.0
, vacuum and thermal baths), despite
\|QLQ-(QLQ)^{\dagger}\|_{F}\sim 10^{1}
–
10^{2}
. Below we resolve this anomaly.

Ng and Rabani identified the first exceptional-point physics of
QLQ
in a many-body-localized central-spin problem, where non-Hermiticity drives exponential growth of the memory kernel rather than spectral protection
ng2021
. Building on that observation, we show that the JC model realizes the opposite regime: a previously unknown positive-definite metric
\eta
renders
QLQ
pseudo-Hermitian and protects its real spectrum. Under counter-rotating deformation toward the full Rabi model this protection breaks and re-enters, producing a
U(1)\to
complex
\to Z_{2}
phase structure bracketed by exceptional-point boundaries. The point is not that
QLQ
can be pathological; it is that quantum memory generators themselves admit symmetry-protected phases.

Model.
—The single-mode JC Hamiltonian (
\hbar=1
) is
H_{\rm JC}=\frac{\omega_{0}}{2}\sigma_{z}+\omega_{c}\,a^{\dagger}a+g(\sigma_{+}a+\sigma_{-}a^{\dagger})
. The total Hilbert space has dimension
d=2(N_{\rm max}+1)
. The Liouvillian
L=\mathbb{I}\otimes H-H^{\mathsf{T}}\otimes\mathbb{I}
(column-stacking convention) is Hermitian. The NZ projector
P\rho=(\Tr_{B}\rho)\otimes\rho_{B}
with vacuum bath
\rho_{B}=|0\rangle\langle 0|
selects system operators tensored with the vacuum;
Q=\mathbb{I}-P
. The nontrivial spectrum of
QLQ
lives on
\operatorname{range}(Q)
, of dimension
d^{2}-4
.

Pseudo-Hermiticity.
—An operator
A
is
\eta
-pseudo-Hermitian
mostafazadeh2002a
if there exists
\eta>0
such that
A^{\dagger}\eta=\eta A
, implying
\eta^{1/2}A\eta^{-1/2}
is Hermitian. For a diagonalizable
A
with real eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}
and biorthonormal left/right eigenvectors
\langle l_{m}|r_{n}\rangle=\delta_{mn}
, any metric

\eta=\sum_{\lambda_{n}\neq 0}c_{n}|l_{n}\rangle\langle l_{n}|,\qquad c_{n}>0,

(2)

satisfies the intertwining relation. We adopt the canonical choice
c_{n}\equiv 1
.

We construct
QLQ
numerically, perform the biorthonormal diagonalisation, and verify: (i)
\max|\Imag\lambda_{n}|<10^{-14}
for all tested
N_{\rm max}
; (ii) the intertwining residual
\|(QLQ)^{\dagger}\eta-\eta QLQ\|_{F}<5\times 10^{-12}
; (iii)
\eta
is positive-definite on the nontrivial-eigenvalue subspace of
QLQ
; and (iv) the similarity-transformed operator is Hermitian to
<10^{-12}
. Table

Discussion.
—The real spectrum of
QLQ
in JC gives the Cauchy spectral representation required by the Hardy-membership theorem
liu2026hardy
, which is a structural reason for KK validity in the canonical light–matter model. The spin-boson model, which lacks this protection, provides the contrasting case: complex
QLQ
eigenvalues correspond to genuine failure of standard KK. The re-entrant phase realises, within a single model family, the “causality phase transition” conjectured in Ref.
liu2026hardy
, here in the spectral structure of
QLQ
rather than the reduced propagator.

Ng and Rabani showed that
QLQ
can sit at exceptional points where non-Hermiticity drives exponential memory-kernel growth
ng2021
. Our result exposes the protective side of the same spectral problem: in the JC model a positive-definite metric prevents this pathology and organises the spectrum into a symmetry-protected pseudo-Hermitian phase. Taken together, these two results suggest a classification programme for projected Liouvillians with at least three regimes: (i)
algebraic
pseudo-Hermiticity, where a metric exists without symmetry protection; (ii)
symmetry-protected
pseudo-Hermiticity, as demonstrated here for JC (
U(1)
) and the Rabi model (
Z_{2}
); and (iii)
generic
complex spectrum, where no protecting metric survives. The classification is controlled by the interplay of Hamiltonian symmetry and NZ projector structure. A pseudo-Hermitian
QLQ
produces purely oscillatory memory, whereas complex eigenvalues introduce genuine decay; the transition between them is in principle observable via non-Markovian process tomography
white2022
and in circuit-QED platforms with tunable counter-rotating couplings
casanova2010
.

Conclusion.
—We have shown that the NZ projected Liouvillian of the JC model is not merely non-Hermitian but secretly Hermitian under a positive-definite metric. The protection is genuine: strongly non-Hermitian sectors remain real-spectrum, and under counter-rotating deformation the protection breaks and re-enters in a
U(1)\to
complex
\to Z_{2}
phase structure. This identifies a phase transition between oscillatory and genuinely dissipative quantum memory and opens a classification programme for the spectral structure of open-system memory-kernel generators.

Reality of
QLQ

For the JC model with a real symmetric Hamiltonian and a real diagonal bath state,
QLQ
is a purely real matrix:
\|QLQ^{*}-QLQ\|_{F}=0
to machine precision. Consequently,
(QLQ)^{\dagger}=(QLQ)^{\mathsf{T}}
and the pseudo-Hermiticity condition simplifies to
(QLQ)^{\mathsf{T}}\eta=\eta(QLQ)
. The metric
\eta
constructed from the biorthonormal left eigenvectors is also purely real. For a real non-symmetric matrix with purely real eigenvalues, the existence of a positive-definite symmetrising metric is equivalent to the existence of a complete set of eigenvectors with no Jordan blocks—a condition verified by the diagonalisability of
QLQ
across all tested parameter regimes.
