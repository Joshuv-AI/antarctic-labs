Dual-Polarization Quasi-BIC Refractive Index Sensing via Dielectric Symmetry Breaking in TiO 2 -BeS Metasurfaces Thanks: Preprint : Submitted to Optics Express.
Shoumik Debnath, Sudipta Saha

Abstract
A dual-polarization dielectric metasurface sensor based on TiO 2 nanobar pairs with a 20 nm BeS gap insert is numerically investigated in the near-infrared. The BeS layer introduces dielectric symmetry breaking without requiring geometric asymmetry, enabling polarization-selective excitation of two distinct resonances. Under TE illumination, the structure supports a quasi-BIC resonance at 879.2 nm with Q = 128 Q=128 , whereas TM excitation produces a broader magnetic dipole resonance at 910.8 nm with Q = 36 Q=36 . The spectral separation between the two modes enables simultaneous tracking of both polarization channels within a single measurement window. For background refractive indices from 1.00 to 1.05, the TE and TM resonances exhibit sensitivities of 243.1 and 178.8 nm/RIU, respectively. The corresponding figures of merit reach 35 and 7 RIU -1 , with detection limits on the order of 10 − 5 10^{-5} RIU. Field distributions show strong confinement of the TE mode inside the gap region, leading to enhanced overlap with the surrounding analyte. Because the two resonances respond differently to refractive-index variations, the metasurface produces a polarization-dependent spectral fingerprint that may provide additional selectivity beyond conventional single-channel dielectric sensors. The proposed platform further shows good tolerance against dimensional variation and consistent resonance behavior across independent FDTD solvers.

Introduction

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) sensors remain widely used for
label-free refractive index sensing because of their strong surface
field confinement and high spectral sensitivity
[
19
]
.
However, Ohmic loss in the metal layer broadens the resonance
linewidth and limits the achievable quality factor

Q

[
53
,
44
]
.
Local heating effects can also become problematic for biological
analytes under certain operating conditions.

All-dielectric metasurfaces provide an alternative route for
low-loss refractive index sensing by supporting electric and magnetic
Mie resonances in high-index dielectric
nanostructures
[
13
,
29
,
12
,
9
,
51
,
26
]
.
Silicon and TiO
2
metasurfaces can support resonances with quality
factors ranging from
10^{2}
to
10^{4}
without the absorption losses
associated with metallic structures.
In particular, quasi-bound states in the continuum (quasi-BICs) have
attracted considerable attention because weak symmetry breaking can
introduce controlled radiative coupling into otherwise dark
modes
[
20
,
27
,
23
,
31
,
56
,
46
,
55
,
49
]
.
The resulting perturbation strength strongly influences both the
resonance linewidth and coupling efficiency, making it an important
design parameter in quasi-BIC metasurfaces.

Most dielectric refractive index sensors rely on a single spectral
resonance shift as the sensing
observable
[
28
,
7
,
46
,
45
]
.
As a result, different analytes can produce similar responses when
their effective refractive indices are close.
Additional sensing channels are therefore desirable, particularly for
surface-sensitive measurements and multidimensional optical detection.
Dual-resonance metasurfaces have been explored for this purpose
because different resonant modes can exhibit distinct spatial field
distributions and analyte overlap
[
46
,
45
]
.
Among the available approaches, polarization multiplexing provides a
relatively simple implementation in which TE and TM illumination
excite spectrally separated resonances within the same metasurface
geometry
[
3
]
.

TiO
2
is well suited to this operating regime.
Its refractive index
n\approx 2.3
–
2.5
near 900 nm supports
strong Mie resonances, its absorption edge near 350 nm keeps it
transparent through the visible and near-infrared, and ALD deposits
films with angstrom-level thickness control patternable by EBL to

\pm 5
nm
[
40
,
17
,
36
]
.
Reported TiO
2
metasurface sensors reach 82–185 nm/RIU at
Q

factors of several thousand
[
48
,
1
]
.
Silicon absorbs below
\sim
1100 nm and cannot be used at the
879–911 nm resonances of the present design, making TiO
2
the
practical choice for this wavelength range.

The symmetry-breaking element introduced here is a 20 nm BeS bar
placed inside the gap between each TiO
2
nanobar pair.
BeS is a II–VI semiconductor with an indirect bandgap near
5.4 eV
[
4
]
.
In thin-film form, the broken out-of-plane symmetry of the
zinc-blende lattice lifts the degeneracy of the principal dielectric
tensor components and introduces a birefringence

\Delta n=n_{x}-n_{z}\approx 0.10
–
0.12
at
900 nm
[
4
,
52
]
.
Both extinction coefficients are negligibly small across the
near-infrared, so the insert is effectively lossless.
The TE and TM fields therefore sample the gap region with different
effective permittivities, which lifts the resonance degeneracy of the
two bar-pair modes and produces two spectrally separated sensing
channels from a single geometry.
First-principles calculations further show that molecular adsorption
modifies the BeS optical constants along each crystallographic axis
in an analyte-specific way
[
52
]
, which points toward
chemical discrimination in a future experimental extension of this work.

We numerically show that a single 20 nm BeS bar inside the TiO
2

nanobar-pair gap activates a quasi-BIC resonance under TE
illumination and a magnetic dipole mode under TM illumination,
with no geometric asymmetry required.
FDTD simulations establish the sensing metrics of both channels.
Near-field maps connect the sensitivity difference between channels
to the spatial distribution of the modal energy.
A two-dimensional polarization fingerprint shows what a dual-channel
readout can resolve that a single-resonance sensor cannot.
Structural tolerance sweeps, BeS optical-constant perturbation tests,
and cross-platform validation against the Tidy3D solver confirm the
numerical robustness of the results.

All simulations used Lumerical FDTD

Transmission was recorded by a frequency-domain power monitor placed

33
,
34
]

T(\lambda)=T_{\mathrm{bg}}\,\frac{\bigl(q+\varepsilon\bigr)^{2}}{1+\varepsilon^{2}},\quad\varepsilon=\frac{2(\lambda-\lambda_{\mathrm{res}})}{\gamma},

\gamma

(FWHM).
The quality factor is
Q=\lambda_{\mathrm{res}}/\gamma
.
Both resonances exhibit asymmetric lineshapes arising from interference
between the narrow resonant mode and the broad radiation continuum, a
hallmark of quasi-BIC coupling that is well described by the Fano

[
24
,
27
,
23
]
.
Sensitivity was extracted from a linear fit to
\lambda_{\mathrm{res}}

over
n=1.00
–
1.05

[

and the limit of detection is LoD

41
]
.

3
Results

3.1
BeS crystal structure and optical constants

3.1.1
Crystal structure

Bulk BeS crystallizes in the zinc-blende (sphalerite) structure with
space group
F\bar{4}3m
(No. 216)
[
21
]
.
Each Be
2+
cation is tetrahedrally coordinated by four S
2-

anions with a Be–S bond length of 2.11 Å.
The cubic symmetry of the bulk phase means that the three principal
dielectric tensor components are degenerate in the unstrained crystal.
However, when BeS is grown as an ultrathin film or monolayer on a
planar substrate, the broken out-of-plane symmetry lifts this
degeneracy and introduces a measurable birefringence

\Delta n=n_{x}-n_{z}

[
22
,
52
]
.
It is this thin-film birefringence that is exploited in the present
design: the 20 nm BeS bar presents different permittivities to the
TE (
x
-polarized) and TM (
y
-polarized) fields, lifting the
resonance degeneracy of the two TiO
2
bar modes.

3.1.2
DFT origin of the optical constants

The directional optical constants used in this work come from a
comprehensive first-principles study of the BeS monolayer and its
interaction with volatile organic compounds
(VOCs)
[
39
]
.
DFT calculations used the GGA-PBE exchange-correlation
functional
[
35
]
together with the Grimme dispersion
correction
[
18
]
on a
3\times 3
BeS supercell with a
21 Å vacuum slab.
The relaxed pristine monolayer has a lattice constant of 3.459 Å,
a Be–S bond length of 1.993 Å, a bond angle of 120
∘
, and
a DFT-PBE band gap of 4.529 eV, consistent with earlier theoretical
reports
[
22
,
52
]
.

That same study found that adsorption of 4-hydroxyhexanal (4HHE),
isoprene, propanal, benzene, acetone, CO
2
, and H
2
O each
introduces mid-gap defect states on the BeS surface and alters the
in-plane and out-of-plane optical constants in a way that is distinct
for each analyte
[
39
]
.
The present work uses only the pristine-BeS constants, which establish
the baseline birefringence and the lossless character of the gap
insert.
The adsorbate-modified constants from Ref.
[
39
]

represent a ready, first-principles input dataset for extending the
metasurface to analyte-specific optical gas discrimination, and that
extension is left as a direct continuation of this work.

n
and
k

To our knowledge, no experimental near-infrared ellipsometry data

This uncertainty is addressed in Sec.

3.2
Effect of the BeS insert

Fig.
4
compares the 0
∘
transmission spectrum
of the bare TiO
2
bar pair (no BeS) with that of the
BeS-loaded structure at
n_{\mathrm{bg}}=1.0
.
Without the insert the bar pair supports a symmetric resonance at
876.9 nm with a relatively broad lineshape.
Adding the 20 nm BeS bar red-shifts the resonance by 2.3 nm to
879.2 nm and narrows the dip, as visible in the sharper descent on
the long-wavelength side.
The redshift originates from the increased effective index inside the
gap region, while the linewidth reduction indicates modified radiative
coupling introduced by the dielectric perturbation.
Without the BeS insert, the antisymmetric mode remains weakly coupled
to the radiation continuum because of the nearly symmetric bar-pair
geometry.
Introducing the gap insert breaks this symmetry and enables excitation
of the quasi-BIC resonance observed in the loaded structure.

The two resonances have distinct physical origins.
The 0

In a perfectly symmetric TiO

The BeS bar breaks this symmetry dielectrically by introducing
different permittivities along

This allows the dark mode to radiate, producing a quasi-BIC resonance
with strong gap field confinement.
The asymmetric Fano lineshape (

radiation continuum
[

standard lineshape reported for quasi-BIC sensors with weak geometric
or dielectric symmetry

[

23

The 90

resonance at 910.8 nm is a magnetic dipole (MD) mode
driven by the

The displacement current circulates inside the TiO
2
bar
cross-section, storing energy predominantly in the bar volume rather
than in the gap
[
14
,
42
]
.
The
E_{z}
map shows four lobes inside the bar, consistent with MD
excitation (Fig.
8
b).
Because the BeS insert occupies only a small fraction of the MD mode
volume, the analyte overlap integral is lower than for the gap
mode
[
38
,
37
]

This explains why

is 26% below
S^{(0^{\circ})}
.

3.6
Polarization fingerprint

Because the two resonances exhibit different refractive-index

maps to a distinct point
in the

(\Delta\lambda^{(0^{\circ})},\Delta\lambda^{(90^{\circ})})

As shown in Fig.

ratios
[
28
,
7
]
.
The isotropic response slope is determined by the metasurface
geometry and may additionally serve as an internal calibration

[

]
.

The

The moderate

), which produces only limited
radiative coupling of the quasi-BIC mode.
The extracted Fano asymmetry parameters (
q\approx 0.10

are consistent with this behavior.
The same perturbation-controlled
Q
trade-off is well documented in
geometric asymmetry designs: larger asymmetry gives lower

but
broader spectral coverage and greater fabrication

[

A recent permittivity-asymmetric qBIC design by Yang
et

[

takes a conceptually related approach

The key advantage of the present design is not the absolute
Q

but the dual-channel output and the NIR operating wavelength.
None of the high-

2
provide two independent polarization
channels.
Wang
et al.

[
46

achieve dual Fano

through geometric rather than dielectric symmetry breaking, and
without the analyte-specific detection pathway offered by the
adsorbate-responsive BeS insert.

The conceptually closest prior work is the TiO
2

state-of-polarization sensor of Kramadhati
et
al.

[
28
]
, which is experimentally demonstrated
but operates in the visible range.
The near-infrared is more relevant for biosensing applications because
many molecular absorption bands lie in the 700–1100 nm
window
[
6
,
32
]
.

The sensitivities of 243 and 179 nm/RIU sit within the range
reported for comparable TiO
2
and moderate-

silicon
sensors
[
48
,
1
]
.
TiO
2
is transparent from about 350 nm through the near-infrared,
which makes it practical at the 879 and 911 nm resonances here.
Silicon absorbs below about 1100 nm and cannot be used in this
range
[
40
,
17

.

The 20 nm BeS insert has two roles.
It sits at the gap field maximum and introduces directional
permittivity that breaks the polarization degeneracy of the bar modes,
playing the same role as the geometric
\delta

asymmetric designs
[
8
,
54
]
.
It also responds to molecular adsorption through changes in

and

39

this sensor can exploit for analyte-specific detection.
The adsorbate optical constants computed in

[
39
]
for seven lung-disease biomarkers

2
, H
2

BeS monolayer provide a ready, first-principles input dataset for

This study is entirely computational.
The TiO
2
and SiO
2
constants come from experimentally verified
datasets
[
40
,
25
]
, and mesh convergence
confirms numerical accuracy.
Sidewall roughness and BeS film non-uniformity are not included in
the model.
The tolerance sweeps cover systematic dimensional errors but not
stochastic roughness, which is left for future work.

5
Conclusion

A dual-polarization TiO
2
–BeS dielectric metasurface was
numerically investigated for refractive index sensing in the
near-infrared region.
By introducing a thin BeS insert inside the TiO
2
nanobar gap, the
structure supports two polarization-dependent resonances. This
eliminates the need for geometric asymmetry.
Under TE illumination, the quasi-BIC resonance at 879.2 nm achieves
a sensitivity of 243.1 nm/RIU and a quality factor of 128, while
the TM magnetic dipole mode at 910.8 nm yields 178.8 nm/RIU with

Q=36
.
The corresponding figures of merit are 35.4 and 7.1 RIU
-1
,
with detection limits of
4.1\times 10^{-5}
and

5.6\times 10^{-5}
RIU respectively.
The TE channel exhibits a quasi-BIC resonance with higher sensitivity,
while the TM channel supports a broader magnetic dipole mode with a
distinct spectral response.

The different refractive-index dependence of the two resonances
produces a polarization-dependent spectral fingerprint.
This can offer additional selectivity compared with conventional
single-channel dielectric sensors.
Because the two sensitivity values differ by 26%, isotropic analytes
trace a fixed slope in the dual-channel shift space, while analytes
with optical anisotropy or asymmetric surface interaction deviate
measurably from this reference line, providing a discrimination
capability unavailable from a single resonance.
The all-dielectric TiO
2
/SiO
2
platform is fully compatible with
ALD deposition and electron-beam lithography, placing the structure
within reach of current nanofabrication capabilities.
Cross-platform simulations and fabrication-tolerance studies further
reveal that the resonance behavior remains stable under moderate
numerical and structural variation.

The present study is fully computational and does not include
experimental uncertainties such as surface roughness or nonuniform
BeS film formation.
In addition, analyte-specific discrimination was not directly modeled
using adsorbate-modified optical constants. Future work may extend
the present platform toward experimentally realizable
polarization-resolved sensing using analyte-dependent BeS optical
responses and integrated microfluidic architectures.
First-principles optical constants for seven biomarker adsorbates on
BeS (4HHE, isoprene, propanal, benzene, acetone, CO
2
, and
H
2
O) are already available
[
39
]
, so this
extension does not require additional DFT calculations.
