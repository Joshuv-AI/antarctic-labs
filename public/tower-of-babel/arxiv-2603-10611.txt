RC-positivity, comparison theorems and prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors I
Mingwei Wang, Xiaokui Yang, Shing-Tung Yau

Abstract
. In this paper, we solve the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor problem. Let E E be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold ( M , ω g ) (M,\omega_{g}) . Suppose that there exists a smooth Hermitian metric h 0 h_{0} on E E such that the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor Λ ω g ​ − 1 ​ R h 0 \Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}} is positive definite. Then for any Hermitian positive definite tensor P ∈ Γ ⁡ ( M , E ∗ ⊗ E ¯ ∗ ) P\in\Gamma\left(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*}\right) , there exists a unique smooth Hermitian metric h h on E E such that Λ ω g ​ − 1 ​ R h = P . \Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=P. The proof is based on a new comparison theorem for Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors. Inspired by these results, we have also derived quantitative Chern number inequalities that apply to both holomorphic vector bundles and compact Kähler manifolds.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold. It is well-known that the class of Ricci curvature
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})
represents the first Chern class
c_{1}(M)
up to a factor of
2\pi
, i.e.,

\left[\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})\right]=2\pi c_{1}(M)\in H^{1,1}(M,{\mathbb{R}}).

The converse–given a cohomology class representative, can one find a Kähler metric whose Ricci form matches it–is the celebrated content of Yau’s solution to the Calabi conjecture
[
Cal57
,
Yau78
]
. Specifically, for any closed real
(1,1)
form
\Omega
representing
2\pi c_{1}(M)
, there exists a unique Kähler metric
\omega\in[\omega_{g}]
such that

(1.1)

\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)=\Omega.

By using
{\partial}{\overline{\partial}}
-lemma, this equation is reduced to a Monge-Ampère type equation

\left(\omega_{g}+\sqrt{-1}{\partial}{\overline{\partial}}\varphi\right)^{n}=e^{F}\omega_{g}^{n}.

The Calabi-Yau theorem has indeed been extended beyond Kähler manifolds to more general Hermitian manifolds, particularly through the work of G. Székelyhidi, V. Tosatti and B. Weinkove, among others. We refer to
[
TW10a
,
TW10b
,
STW17
]
and the references therein.

The Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem
[
UY86
]
(see also Donaldson
[
Don85
,
Don87
]
) asserts that for a stable holomorphic vector bundle
E
over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
, there exists a unique Hermitian-Einstein metric
h
on
E
up to scaling, satisfying

(1.2)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=\lambda_{0}\cdot h,

where
R^{h}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
is the Chern curvature tensor of
(E,h)
and

\lambda_{0}=\frac{n\int_{M}c_{1}(E)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1}}{\mathrm{rank}(E)\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}}.

In particular, the Chern number inequality holds

(1.3)

\int_{M}\left((r-1)c^{2}_{1}(E)-2rc_{2}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}\leq 0.

Together with results from
[
Kob82
,
Lub83
]
, this establishes a precise correspondence between the algebro-geometric notion of stability and the existence of Hermitian-Einstein metrics. This was generalized to the setting of Hermitian manifolds through the work
[
LY86
]
of Jun Li and S.-T. Yau.

The main result of this paper is the following analogue of the Calabi-Yau theorem, addressing the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor problem for holomorphic vector bundles on compact Kähler manifolds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that there exists a smooth Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
such that the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}
is positive definite. Then for any Hermitian positive definite tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, there exists a unique smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
such that

(1.4)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=P.

The positivity of
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}
can be weakened to the positivity of the integral of the minimum eigenvalue of

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}
over the manifold (Theorem
5.8
).
Moreover, by Serre duality, the corresponding conjugate equation of (
1.4
) admits a solution when
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}
is negative definite; see Remark
5.2
for further details. The method for solving equation (
1.4
) differs significantly from those for equations (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), and we provide a brief overview of the key ideas involved. The uniqueness part of Theorem
1.1
is established by the following comparison theorem for Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors of Hermitian vector bundles:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. If
h
and
h_{0}
are smooth Hermitian metrics on
E
satisfying
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}>0
and

(1.5)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}

as Hermitian tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, then
h\leq h_{0}
.

Remark 1.3
.

Note that the curvature constraint
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}>0
in Theorem
1.1
is closely related to the notions of RC-positivity and uniform RC-positivity introduced in
[
Yang18
,
Yang20
]
. A comparison theorem analogous to Theorem
1.2
for RC-positive vector bundles was also established in
[
XYY24+
]
. This curvature constraint should be replaced by RC-positivity, and this replacement is carried out in a separate project on general Hermitian manifolds.

To establish the existence result, we first introduce two key function spaces: let
\mathrm{Herm}(E)
denote the space of smooth Hermitian tensors on
E
, and
\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
be the space of smooth Hermitian metrics on
E
. We then define a Hermitian-Yang-Mills map

G:\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)\rightarrow\mathrm{Herm}(E)

by the formula

(1.6)

G(h)=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}.

We show that if there exists a smooth Hermitian metric
h_{0}
such that
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}>0
, then
G:G^{-1}\left(\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)\right)\rightarrow\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
is a bijection. The injectivity is given by Theorem
1.2
. For the surjectivity, we show that the image of
G

(1.7)

\mathscr{R}=\{P\in{\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)}\ |\ G(h)=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=P\text{ for some}\ h\in{\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)}\}

is both open and closed in
\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
with respect to the
C^{\infty}
-topology. The openness of
\mathscr{R}
(Theorem
5.6
) is established via the implicit function theorem, leveraging the bijectivity of the linearization of
G
(up to isomorphisms) at a reference metric
h_{1}
:

(1.8)

\mathscr{L}(\Psi)=\Delta_{\partial^{h_{1}}}\Psi+\Omega_{1}\cdot\Psi,

where the positive definiteness of the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor
\Omega_{1}
of
h_{1}
ensures that
\mathscr{L}
is an invertible self-adjoint elliptic operator. The closeness of
\mathscr{R}
(Theorem
5.3
) follows from two key analytic estimates: Theorem
1.2
, which provides a uniform
C^{0}
-bound on metrics
h
satisfying
G(h)=P
for
P\in\mathscr{R}
, and a higher-order regularity estimate (Theorem
4.1
), which controls the
C^{1}
-norm of such metrics. Together, these estimates imply that any sequence
\{P_{k}\}\subset\mathscr{R}
converging in the
C^{\infty}
-topology has a preimage sequence
\{h_{k}\}
that is precompact in the
C^{1}
-norm. By using
W^{k,p}
and
C^{k,\alpha}
-estimates for elliptic equations, we obtain a smooth limit metric
h
of some subsequence
\{h_{k_{s}}\}
with
G(h)=\lim P_{k_{s}}=P
. Hence
\mathscr{R}
is closed.

The Calabi-Yau theorem asserts that on a Fano manifold
M
, there exist Kähler metrics
\omega
in each Kähler class satisfying
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)>0
. In particular,
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{g}>0
. As an immediate application of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following result on the existence of Hermitian metrics with prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors on Fano manifolds:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
M
be a Fano manifold. Suppose that
P\in\Gamma(M,T^{*1,0}M\otimes T^{*0,1}M)
is a Hermitian positive definite section. Then for any Kähler class
[\omega]
, there exist a Kähler metric
\omega_{g}\in[\omega]
, and a unique Hermitian metric
h
on
T^{1,0}M
such that

(1.9)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=P.

The following special case is particularly worth highlighting:

Corollary 1.5
.

On a Fano manifold
M
, for any Kähler metric
\omega_{g}
with
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})>0
, there exists a unique Hermitian metric
h
on
T^{1,0}M
such that

(1.10)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=g.

Moreover, if
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})=c_{0}^{-1}\omega_{g}
for some
c_{0}>0
, then
h=c_{0}g
.

As inspired by Theorem
1.1
and the comparsion result described in Theorem
1.2
, we derive a quantitative version of Chern number inequalities, extending those associated with Hermitian-Einstein metrics.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold and
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
r
over
M
. Suppose that there exists a Hermitian metric
h
on
E
satisfying

(1.11)

a\cdot h\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\leq b\cdot h,

for some constants
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then the following Chern number inequality holds

(1.12)

\int_{M}\left((r-1)c^{2}_{1}(E)-2rc_{2}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}\leq\frac{r(r-1)\left(b-a\right)^{2}}{8\pi^{2}n^{2}}\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}.

Moreover, we establish the following Chern number inequalities extending those associated with Kähler-Einstein metrics (
[
Yau77
]
).

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold with
\dim M\geq 2
. If there exist two constants
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(1.13)

a\omega_{g}\leq\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})\leq b\omega_{g},

then the following Chern number inquality holds

(1.14)

\int_{M}\left(nc^{2}_{1}(M)-2(n+1)c_{2}(M)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}\leq\frac{(n^{2}-2)(b-a)^{2}}{8\pi^{2}n^{2}}\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}.

Remark 1.8
.

It is evident that Theorem
1.1
bears profound theoretical connections to the Calabi-Yau theory, the Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau theory, and the Kazdan-Warner theory. A number of related research initiatives are currently underway:

\bullet

A Higgs bundle-theoretic formulation of the main theorems will be presented in a separate work.

\bullet

A flow-based framework, constructed via variations of energy functionals, will be elaborated in subsequent sections.

\bullet

A refined notion of
\varepsilon
-stability, closely tied to the criterion in (
1.11
), is under development.

\bullet

Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
together provide an alternative proof of the Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem.

Background materials

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
and
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
M
of rank
r
. Fix a smooth Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
, the Chern connection of
(E,h_{0})
is denoted by
\nabla^{h_{0}}
. We shall use the natural decomposition

\nabla^{h_{0}}={\partial}^{h_{0}}+{\overline{\partial}},

where
{\partial}^{h_{0}}
is the
(1,0)
part and
{\overline{\partial}}
is the
(0,1)
part.
The formal adjoints of
{\partial}^{h_{0}}
and
{\overline{\partial}}
with respect to
\omega_{g}
and
h_{0}
are denoted by
{\partial}^{*,h_{0}}
and
\overline{{\partial}}{}^{*,h_{0}}
respectively. Let
R^{h_{0}}
be the Chern curvature tensor of
(E,h_{0})
. In local holomorphic coordinates
\{z^{i}\}
of
M
and local holomorphic basis
\{e_{\alpha}\}
of
E
, the Chern curvature tensor
R^{h_{0}}
can be written as

(2.1)

R^{h_{0}}=(R^{h_{0}})_{i\overline{j}\alpha}^{\beta}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes E).

(1)

The (first) Chern-Ricci curvature of
(E,h_{0})
is

(2.2)

\mathrm{Ric}(h_{0})=\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}_{i\overline{j}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}=\sqrt{-1}\sum_{\alpha}(R^{h_{0}})_{i\overline{j}\alpha}^{\alpha}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M).

It is well-known that the Chern-Ricci curvature represents the first Chern class
c_{1}(E)
up to a factor of
2\pi
.

(2)

The Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor
S^{h_{0}}=(S^{h_{0}})_{\alpha}^{\beta}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
of
(E,h_{0})
is defined as

(2.3)

S^{h_{0}}:=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}\right)=\left(g^{i\overline{j}}(R^{h_{0}})_{i\overline{j}\alpha}^{\beta}\right)e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}.

When no confusion arises, we also use the notation

(2.4)

S^{h_{0}}=(S^{h_{0}})_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}e^{\alpha}\otimes\overline{e}^{\beta}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})

where tensor components are

(2.5)

S^{h_{0}}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=(S^{h_{0}})_{\alpha}^{\gamma}h_{0,\gamma\overline{\beta}}=g^{i\overline{j}}R^{h_{0}}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}},\quad\mbox{and}\quad R^{h_{0}}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}=h_{0,\gamma\overline{\beta}}(R^{h_{0}})_{i\overline{j}\alpha}^{\gamma}.

We say
S^{h_{0}}>0
if the Hermitian matrix
\left(S^{h_{0}}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\right)
is positive definite over
M
.

(3)

The Chern scalar curvature of
(E,h_{0})
is

(2.6)

s_{h_{0}}=g^{i\overline{j}}R^{h_{0}}_{i\overline{j}}=\sum_{\alpha}(S^{h_{0}})_{\alpha}^{\alpha}=\sum h_{0}^{\alpha\overline{\beta}}S^{h_{0}}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=g^{i\overline{j}}h_{0}^{\alpha\overline{\beta}}R^{h_{0}}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}.

Let
h\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes{\overline{E}}^{*})
be another smooth Hermitian metric on
E
. Let
R^{h}
and
S^{h}
be the corresponding Chern curvature tensor and Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor of
(E,h)
respectively.
There is an induced smooth section
H\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
given by

(2.7)

H=h\cdot h_{0}^{-1}.

In local holomorphic basis
\{e_{\alpha}\}
of
E
,
H=H_{\alpha}^{\beta}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}
is represented by

(2.8)

H_{\alpha}^{\beta}=h_{\alpha\overline{\gamma}}h_{0}^{\beta\overline{\gamma}}.

It is clear that the inverse
H^{-1}=(H^{-1})_{\alpha}^{\beta}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
has components

(2.9)

(H^{-1})_{\alpha}^{\beta}=h_{0,\alpha\overline{\gamma}}h^{\beta\overline{\gamma}}.

For
P=P_{\alpha}^{\beta}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\in\Omega^{p}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
and
Q=Q_{\alpha}^{\beta}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\in\Omega^{q}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
, the product

(2.10)

P\cdot Q\in\Omega^{p+q}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)

is defined as

(2.11)

P\cdot Q=(P_{\alpha}^{\beta}\wedge Q_{\beta}^{\gamma})\;e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\gamma}.

In particular, one has

(2.12)

H\cdot H^{-1}=H^{-1}\cdot H=\mathrm{Id}_{E}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E).

The tensor
H
will play a central role in what follows; we now present some of its key linear algebraic properties.

Definition 2.1
.

A section
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
is called
h_{0}
-Hermitian if

(2.13)

h_{0}(Pv,w)=h_{0}(v,Pw),

for any
v,w\in\Gamma(M,E)
. It is easy to see that
P=P_{\alpha}^{\beta}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}
is
h_{0}
-Hermitian if and only if

(2.14)

h_{0,\alpha\overline{\delta}}h_{0}^{\beta\overline{\gamma}}\overline{P}{}_{\gamma}^{\delta}=P_{\alpha}^{\beta}.

An
h_{0}
-Hermitian section
P=P_{\alpha}^{\beta}e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
is called positive definite if

h_{0}(Pv,v)>0

for all
v\neq 0
. This is equivalent to the matrix

\left(P_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\right)=\left(h_{0,\gamma\overline{\beta}}P_{\alpha}^{\gamma}\right)
being Hermitian positive definite. We also write
P>0
. Similarly,
P\geq 0
is defined by requiring
h_{0}(Pv,v)\geq 0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(1)

H=h\cdot h_{0}^{-1}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
is
h_{0}
-Hermitian.

(2)

If
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
is
h_{0}
-Hermitian, for any
A,B\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
,

(2.15)

h_{0}(P\cdot A,B)=h_{0}(A,P\cdot B),\quad h_{0}(A\cdot P,B)=h_{0}(A,B\cdot P).

(3)

If
A,B\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
are
h_{0}
-Hermitian and
A\geq 0,B\geq 0
, then

(2.16)

h_{0}(A,B)\geq 0.

Moreover, for any section
C\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
, one has

(2.17)

h_{0}(A\cdot C\cdot B,C)\geq 0.

Proof.

(1) follows from a straightforward computation that

(2.18)

h_{0,\alpha\overline{\delta}}h_{0}^{\beta\overline{\gamma}}\overline{H}{}_{\gamma}^{\delta}=h_{0,\alpha\overline{\delta}}h_{0}^{\beta\overline{\gamma}}h_{0}^{p\overline{\delta}}h_{p\overline{\gamma}}=H_{\alpha}^{\beta}.

For (2), one has

\displaystyle h_{0}(A\cdot P,B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{0,\gamma\overline{q}}h_{0}^{\alpha\overline{p}}A_{\alpha}^{\beta}P_{\beta}^{\gamma}\overline{B}{}_{p}^{q}=h_{0,\gamma\overline{q}}h_{0}^{\alpha\overline{p}}A_{\alpha}^{\beta}\left(h_{0,\beta\overline{s}}h_{0}^{\gamma\overline{t}}\overline{P}{}_{t}^{s}\right)\overline{B}{}_{p}^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{0,\beta\overline{s}}h_{0}^{\alpha\overline{p}}A_{\alpha}^{\beta}\overline{B}{}_{p}^{q}\overline{P}{}_{q}^{s}=h_{0}(A,B\cdot P),

where the second identity holds since
P
is
h_{0}
-Hermitian. The relation
h_{0}(P\cdot A,B)=h_{0}(A,P\cdot B)
can be verified in a similar way.

For (3), since
A\geq 0
and
B\geq 0
,
A^{1/2}
and
B^{1/2}
are well-defined and it is easy to deduce that both of them are
h_{0}
-Hermitian. Hence,

(2.19)

h_{0}(A,B)=h_{0}\left(A^{1/2},A^{1/2}B\right)=h_{0}\left(A^{1/2}B^{1/2},A^{1/2}B^{1/2}\right)\geq 0.

A simple calculation shows that

h_{0}(A\cdot C\cdot B,C)=h_{0}(A^{1/2}\cdot C\cdot B^{1/2},A^{1/2}\cdot C\cdot B^{1/2})\geq 0
.

3.
A comparison theorem for Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors

In this section, we present a proof of Theorem
1.2
, which provides a comparison result for Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors of abstract vector bundles. We then explore its applications, including the uniqueness of solutions to the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor equation and a version of

C^{2}
-estimate for Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. If
h
and
h_{0}
are smooth Hermitian metrics on
E
satisfying
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}>0
and

(3.1)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}

as Hermitian tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, then
h\leq h_{0}
.

Remark 3.2
.

It is established via an analogous approach that: for an indecomposable vector bundle
E
, if the inequality

(3.2)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}

as Hermitian tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
with respect to
h_{0}
, then
h=ch_{0}
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

As an application, one has

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that
h
and
h_{0}
are smooth Hermitian metrics on
E
. If

(3.3)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}>0

as Hermitian tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, then
h=h_{0}
.

In particular, we establish the uniqueness in Theorem
1.1
:

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Then for any Hermitian positive definite section
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, there exists at most one smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
such that

(3.4)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=P.

By rescaling, we obtain the following result, which is analogous to the
C^{2}
-estimate in the proof of the Calabi-Yau Theorem.

Corollary 3.5
.

Suppose that
h
and
h_{0}
are two smooth Hermitian metrics on
E
. If
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}>0
and

(3.5)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\leq\lambda\cdot\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}

as Hermitian tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
, then
h\leq\lambda\cdot h_{0}
.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
starts with the following relation concerning Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that
h
and
h_{0}
are two smooth Hermitian metrics on
E
. Then

(3.6)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}={\partial}^{*,h_{0}}\left(({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)\cdot H^{-1}\right),

as tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
, where
H=h\cdot h_{0}^{-1}
and
{\partial}^{h_{0}}
is the
(1,0)
component of the Chern connection on
E^{*}\otimes E
induced by
(E,h_{0})
.

Proof.

The Chern connection
\nabla^{h_{0}}
is given by

(3.7)

\nabla_{i}^{h_{0}}e_{\beta}=(\Gamma_{0})_{i\beta}^{\mu}e_{\mu},\quad\nabla_{i}^{h_{0}}e^{\alpha}=-(\Gamma_{0})_{i\beta}^{\alpha}e^{\beta}.

where
(\Gamma_{0})_{i\alpha}^{\beta}
is the Christoffel symbol of the Chern connection on
(E,h_{0})
. Hence,

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}^{h_{0}}H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla^{h_{0}}_{i}\left(h_{0}^{\beta\overline{\gamma}}h_{\alpha\overline{\gamma}}\;e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=

where
\Omega=S^{h_{0}}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
. Moreover, the linearization map

(5.33)

\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}:\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{0})\rightarrow\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{0})

of
\widetilde{G}
at the point
\mathrm{Id}_{E}

is given by

(5.34)

\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}(\Psi)=\Delta_{{\partial}^{h_{0}}}\Psi+\Omega\cdot\Psi.

Proof.

For any
H\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E,h_{0})
, it induces a Hermitian metric

(5.35)

h=F^{-1}(H)=H\cdot h_{0}\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E).

By (
3.6
), one has the difference of the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors:

(5.36)

(S^{h})_{\alpha}^{\beta}-(S^{h_{0}})_{\alpha}^{\beta}=({\partial}^{*,h_{0}}({\partial}^{h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}))_{\alpha}^{\beta}.

Hence,

(5.37)

S^{h}_{\alpha\overline{\gamma}}=h_{\beta\overline{\gamma}}(S^{h_{0}})_{\alpha}^{\beta}+h_{\beta\overline{\gamma}}({\partial}^{*,h_{0}}({\partial}^{h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}))_{\alpha}^{\beta}.

That is

(5.38)

G(F^{-1}(H))=S^{h}_{\alpha\overline{\gamma}}\;e^{\alpha}\otimes\overline{e}{}^{\gamma}=\left(h_{\beta\overline{\gamma}}(S^{h_{0}})_{\alpha}^{\beta}+h_{\beta\overline{\gamma}}({\partial}^{*,h_{0}}({\partial}^{h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}))_{\alpha}^{\beta}\right)\;e^{\alpha}\otimes\overline{e}{}^{\gamma}.

Therefore, one has

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}(H)=F(G(F^{-1}(H)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{0}^{\delta\overline{\gamma}}h_{\beta\overline{\gamma}}\left((S^{h_{0}})_{\alpha}^{\beta}+({\partial}^{*}({\partial}^{h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}))_{\alpha}^{\beta}\right)\;e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\delta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\partial}^{*,h_{0}}({\partial}^{h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1})\cdot H+\Omega\cdot H.

We compute the tangent map of
\widetilde{G}
at
\mathrm{Id}_{E}\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E,h_{0})
. For any
\Psi\in\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{0})
, there is a curve
\gamma(t)=\mathrm{Id}_{E}+t\Psi
in
\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E,h_{0})
when
t
is small. It is easy to see that

(5.39)

(\mathrm{Id}_{E}+t\Psi)^{-1}=\mathrm{Id}_{E}-t\Psi+O(t^{2}).

Since
{\partial}^{h_{0}}(\mathrm{Id}_{E})=0
, one has

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}(\gamma(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t{\partial}^{*,h_{0}}(({\partial}^{h_{0}}\Psi)\cdot(\mathrm{Id}_{E}-t\Psi))+\Omega+t\Omega\cdot\Psi+O(t^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega+t\left(\Delta_{{\partial}^{h_{0}}}\Psi+\Omega\cdot\Psi\right)+O(t^{2}).

Hence, the linearization of
\widetilde{G}
at
\mathrm{Id}_{E}
is

(5.40)

\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}(\Psi)=\Delta_{{\partial}^{h_{0}}}\Psi+\Omega\cdot\Psi.

This completes the proof.
∎

The following result is useful in the
C^{k,\alpha}
and
W^{k,p}
estimates:

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
S\in\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{0})
and
\Omega\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E,h_{0})
. If
\Psi\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
satisfies

(5.41)

\Delta_{{\partial}^{h_{0}}}\Psi+\Omega\cdot\Psi=S,

then
\Psi\in\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{0})
.

Proof.

Let
T\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
. We show that

(5.42)

\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}T\right)^{*}={\overline{\partial}}T^{*},\quad\left({\overline{\partial}}T\right)^{*}={\partial}^{h_{0}}T^{*}.

Here and in the following, the Hermitian adjoint is taken with respect to
h_{0}
. Indeed,

(5.43)

{\partial}\left(h_{0}(Tv,w)\right)=h_{0}({\partial}^{h_{0}}T\cdot v+T\cdot{\partial}^{h_{0}}v,w)+h_{0}(Tv,{\overline{\partial}}w),

for any
v,w\in\Gamma(M,E)
.
Similarly, one has

(5.44)

{\partial}\left(h_{0}(v,T^{*}w)\right)=h_{0}({\partial}^{h_{0}}v,T^{*}w)+h_{0}(v,{\overline{\partial}}T^{*}\cdot w+T^{*}{\overline{\partial}}w).

Since
h_{0}(Tv,w)=h_{0}(v,T^{*}w)
, we deduce that

h_{0}({\partial}^{h_{0}}T\cdot v,w)=h_{0}(v,{\overline{\partial}}T^{*}\cdot w)

which implies
\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}T\right)^{*}={\overline{\partial}}T^{*}
. By using the expressions of

{\partial}{\overline{\partial}}(h_{0}(Tv,w))={\partial}{\overline{\partial}}(h_{0}(v,T^{*}w)),

we get

(5.45)

\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}{\overline{\partial}}T\right)^{*}={\overline{\partial}}{\partial}^{h_{0}}T^{*},\quad\left({\overline{\partial}}{\partial}^{h_{0}}T\right)^{*}={\partial}^{h_{0}}{\overline{\partial}}T^{*}.

In particular,

(5.46)

(\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}T)^{*}=\Delta_{{\partial}^{h_{0}}}T^{*},\quad\left(\Delta_{{\partial}^{h_{0}}}T\right)^{*}=\Delta_{{\overline{\partial}}}T^{*}.

Since
\Omega
is
h_{0}
-Hermitian,

(5.47)

(\Omega\cdot\Psi)^{*}=\Psi^{*}\cdot\Omega^{*}=\Psi^{*}\cdot\Omega.

By the Bochner-Kodaira identity on vector bundle
E^{*}\otimes E
, one can see

(5.48)

\Delta_{\overline{{\partial}}}\Psi^{*}-\Delta_{{\partial}^{h_{0}}}\Psi^{*}=\Omega\cdot\Psi^{*}-\Psi^{*}\cdot\Omega.

Therefore, by (
5.46
), (
5.47
) and (
5.48
), we obtain that

(5.49)

\Delta_{{\partial}^{h_{0}}}\Psi^{*}+\Omega\cdot\Psi^{*}=\Delta_{{\overline{\partial}}}\Psi^{*}+\Psi^{*}\cdot\Omega=(\Delta_{{\partial}^{h_{0}}}\Omega+\Omega\cdot\Psi)^{*}=S^{*}=S.

From (
5.41
) and (
5.49
), one has

(5.50)

\Delta_{{\partial}^{h_{0}}}\left(\Psi^{*}-\Psi\right)+\Omega\cdot(\Psi^{*}-\Psi)=0.

Since
\Omega
is
h_{0}
-Hermitian positive definite, by pairing with
(\Psi^{*}-\Psi)
in (
5.50
), one deduces that
\Psi^{*}-\Psi=0
and so
\Psi
is
h_{0}
-Hermitian.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
P\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
. If there exists a Hermitian metric
h_{1}\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
such that

(5.51)

P=G(h_{1})\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*}),

then there exist open neighborhoods
U
of
P\in\mathrm{Herm}(E)
and
V
of
h_{1}\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
such that
G:U\rightarrow V
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

We fix the center point
h_{1}\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
and so
F(h_{1})=\mathrm{Id}_{E}
.
Since
\widetilde{G}=F\circ G\circ F^{-1}
,

(5.52)

d\widetilde{G}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}=(dF)_{G(h_{1})}\circ(dG)_{h_{1}}\circ(dF^{-1})_{F(h_{1})}

Moreover, since
F
is an isomorphism, we only need to verify that

\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}=d\widetilde{G}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}:\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{1})\rightarrow\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{1})

is an isomorphism. By Lemma
5.4
, for any
\Psi\in\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{1})
,

(5.53)

\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}(\Psi)=\Delta_{{\partial}^{h_{1}}}\Psi+\Omega_{1}\cdot\Psi.

where
\Omega_{1}=P\cdot h_{1}^{-1}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
. Since both
\Omega_{1}
and
\Psi
are

h_{1}
-Hermitian,
\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}
is self-adjoint with respect to
\omega_{g}
and
h_{1}
. Since
\Omega_{1}>0
, one can see clearly that
\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}
is injective. Moreover, since
M
is compact, there exist
m_{1},m_{2}>0
such that

(5.54)

m_{1}\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{E}\leq\Omega_{1}\leq m_{2}\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{E}

with respect to
h_{1}
. Hence,

(5.55)

(\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}\Psi,\Psi)_{h_{1}}\geq(\Omega_{1}\cdot\Psi,\Psi)_{h_{1}}\geq m_{1}(\Psi,\Psi)_{h_{1}}.

By applying the Lax-Milgram Theorem framework together with standard elliptic regularity theory, we conclude that
\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}
is an isomorphism. For the convenience of readers, we outline key details of the proof below. For any positive integer
m
, let

(5.56)

W^{m,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)

be the space of
W^{m,2}
sections of
E^{*}\otimes E
with respect to the metric
\omega_{g}
and
h_{1}
.
Consider the pairing

(5.57)

\mathscr{B}:W^{1,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)\times W^{1,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

defined by

(5.58)

\mathscr{B}(\Psi,T)=(\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}(\Psi),T)_{h_{1}}

in the weak sense. It is clear that

(5.59)

\mathscr{B}(\Psi,T)=({\partial}^{h_{1}}\Psi,{\partial}^{h_{1}}T)+(\Omega_{1}\cdot\Psi,T)

and so

(5.60)

|\mathscr{B}(\Psi,T)|\leq C_{1}\left\|\Psi\right\|_{W^{1,2}}\left\|T\right\|_{W^{1,2}},\quad|\mathscr{B}(\Psi,\Psi)|\geq C_{2}\left\|\Psi\right\|_{W^{1,2}}^{2},

where
C_{1}=C_{1}(M,\omega_{g},h_{1})>0
and
C_{2}=C_{2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{1})>0
. Since
\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}
is self-adjoint, by the Lax-Milgram Theorem, one establishes an isomorphism

(5.61)

\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}:W^{1,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)\simeq\left(W^{1,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)\right)^{*}.

We claim that for all
k\geq 1
,

(5.62)

\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}:W^{k+2,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)\rightarrow W^{k,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)

are isomorphisms. We only need to show the injectivity and surjectivity.

(1)

(Injectivity) Suppose that there exists some
\Psi\in W^{k+2,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
such that

(5.63)

\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}(\Psi)=0\in W^{k,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E),

then by pairing with
\Psi
, one deduces
\Psi=0
.

(2)

(Surjectivity) There is a natural inclusion (e.g.
[
Bre11
, p. 291]
)

(5.64)

W^{k,2}(M,E\otimes E^{*})\subset W^{1,2}(M,E\otimes E^{*})\subset\left(W^{1,2}(M,E\otimes E^{*})\right)^{*}.

By (
5.61
),
for each
S\in W^{k,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
,
there exists some

(5.65)

\Psi\in W^{1,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)

such that

(5.66)

\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}(\Psi)=\Delta_{{\partial}^{h_{1}}}\Psi+\Omega_{1}\cdot\Psi=S

in the weak
W^{1,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
sense. By standard regularity theory for elliptic PDEs (e.g.
[
Eva10
, p. 329]
), one concludes that
\Psi\in W^{k+2,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
. Hence,
\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}
in (
5.62
) is surjective.

By the Sobolev embedding theorem and Lemma
5.5
, we deduce that the linearization map
\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}:\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{1})\rightarrow\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{1})
is an isomorphism and so Theorem
5.6
holds by the implicit function theorem.
∎

By refining the openness and closedness arguments in the proofs of Theorem
5.3
and Theorem
5.6
, we establish an existence result under the
C^{k,\alpha}
-norm. For any
k>1
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, let

C^{k,\alpha}\mathrm{Herm}(E)
and
C^{k,\alpha}\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
be
C^{k,\alpha}
Hermitian sections and
C^{k,\alpha}
Hermitian metrics on
E
respectively. Here, the
C^{k,\alpha}
-norm is taken with respect to
\omega_{g}
. For any
h_{1}\in C^{k,\alpha}\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
, one can also define
C^{\ell,\alpha}\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{1})
and
C^{\ell,\alpha}\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E,h_{1})
for any non-negative integer
\ell\leq k
. Moreover, we have the following diagram, which refines the construction in the proof of Theorem
5.6
:

\begin{CD}C^{k+2,\alpha}\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)@>{G_{k,\alpha}}>{}>C^{k,\alpha}\mathrm{Herm}(E)\\

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that there exists a smooth Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
such that the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor
S^{h_{0}}>0
. Then for any integer
k>1
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and any
P\in C^{k,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)}
, there exists a unique Hermitian metric
h\in C^{k+2,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)}
such that its Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor
S^{h}
coincides with
P
.

Proof.

The uniqueness of metric
h
is established in Corollary
3.3
since
h
is
C^{3}
. For the existence of such a metric, we define

(5.67)

\mathscr{R}=\{P\in C^{k,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)}|\ S^{h}=P\text{ for some}\ h\in C^{k+2,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)}\}.

By a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
5.6
, one deduces that
\mathscr{R}
is open in
C^{k,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)}
. Actually, for any
h_{1}\in C^{k+2,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)}
with
S^{h_{1}}>0
, one can show that the linearization map

(5.68)

\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}:C^{k+2,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{1})}\rightarrow C^{k,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{1})}

of
\widetilde{G}_{k,\alpha}
at point
\mathrm{Id}_{E}
is given by

(5.69)

\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{Id}_{E}}(\Psi)=\Delta_{{\partial}^{h_{1}}}\Psi+\Omega_{1}\cdot\Psi.

Moreover, it is an isomorphism. Indeed, there is an inclusion

(5.70)

C^{k,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{1})}\subset W^{1,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)\subset\left(W^{1,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)\right)^{*}.

By using similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
5.6
,
for any
S\in C^{k,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}(E,h_{1})}
,
by (
5.70
), there exists some

(5.71)

\Psi\in W^{1,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)

such that

(5.72)

\Delta_{{\partial}^{h_{1}}}\Psi+\Omega_{1}\cdot\Psi=S

in the weak
W^{1,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
sense. By standard regularity theory for elliptic PDEs,

(5.73)

\Psi\in W^{k+2,2}(M,E^{*}\otimes E).

By the Sobolev embedding theorem,
\Psi\in W^{3,p_{1}}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
with

(5.74)

\frac{1}{p_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{k-1}{2n}

By repeating the above process, we know

(5.75)

\Psi\in W^{k+2,p_{1}}(M,E^{*}\otimes E).

By induction, one can show that

(5.76)

\Psi\in W^{k+2,p_{m}}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)

where

(5.77)

\frac{1}{p_{m}}=\frac{1}{p_{m-1}}-\frac{k-1}{2n}.

By the induction formulation,

(5.78)

\frac{1}{p_{m}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{m(k-1)}{2n}.

Let
m_{0}=\lceil\frac{n}{k-1}\rceil-1
. Then

(5.79)

\frac{1}{p_{m_{0}}}\leq\frac{k-1}{2n}\leq\frac{k-\alpha}{2n}.

In particular, by the Sobolev embedding theorem, we obtain

(5.80)

W^{k+2,p_{m_{0}}}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)\subset W^{k+2,\frac{2n}{k-\alpha}}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)\subset C^{2,\alpha}(M,E^{*}\otimes E).

Hence,
\Psi\in C^{2,\alpha}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
. Since
S\in C^{k,\alpha}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
and
\Omega_{1}\in C^{k,\alpha}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
, by Schauder’s theory for the elliptic equation

\Delta_{{\partial}^{h_{1}}}\Psi+\Omega_{1}\cdot\Psi=S,

we conclude that
\Psi\in C^{k+2,\alpha}(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
. Hence by Lemma
5.5
, the map in (
5.68
) is an isomorphism and so
\mathscr{R}
is open in
C^{k,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)}
.
By using similar arguments as in the proof of Theorem
5.3
, one can show that:
if
\{h_{m}\}
is a sequence of
C^{k+2,\alpha}
-Hermitian metrics on
E
and

(5.81)

\lim_{m}\|S^{h_{m}}-P\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}=0

for some
P\in C^{k,\alpha}\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
, then there exists a unique
h\in C^{k+2,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)}
such that

S^{h}=P
. Hence,
\mathscr{R}
is closed in
C^{k,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)}
. Since
C^{k,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)}
is connected, one deduces that
\mathscr{R}=C^{k,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

For any
P\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
, by Theorem
5.7
, there exists a unique Hermitian metric
h_{k}\in C^{k+2,\alpha}{\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)}
such that

(5.82)

S^{h_{k}}=P.

Moreover, by Corollary
3.3
,
h_{k}
are the same for all
k>1
and we denote it by
h
. Hence,
h
is a smooth Hermitian metric on
E
and
S^{h}=P
.
∎

We demonstrate that Theorem
1.1
holds in a more general setting. Let
h
be a smooth Hermitian metric on
E
. The minimal eigenvalue function
\kappa_{h}:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor
S^{h}
is defined as

(5.83)

\kappa_{h}(x)=\inf_{0\neq v\in E_{x}}\frac{h(S^{h}v,v)}{h(v,v)}.

It is easy to see that
S^{h}>0
with respect to
h
if and only if
\kappa_{h}(x)>0
for any
x\in M
.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that there exists a Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
such that

(5.84)

\int_{M}\kappa_{h_{0}}(x)\omega_{g}^{n}>0.

Then for any Hermitian positive definite tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, there exists a unique smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
such that

(5.85)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=P.

Proof.

By using the condition (
5.84
), there exists some
\lambda_{0}>0
such that

(5.86)

\int_{M}\kappa_{h_{0}}(x)\omega_{g}^{n}=\lambda_{0}\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}.

Since
\kappa_{h_{0}}
is Lipschitz, by Hodge theory, there exists a function
f\in C^{2,\alpha}(M,\mathbb{R})
such that

(5.87)

\kappa_{h_{0}}+\Delta_{g}f=\lambda_{0}.

We define a new metric
\widetilde{h}=e^{-f}h
. By (
3.6
), one has

(5.88)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{\widetilde{h}}-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}={\partial}^{*,h_{0}}\left(({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)\cdot H^{-1}\right)

where
H=\widetilde{h}\cdot h_{0}^{-1}=e^{-f}\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{E}
. A straightforward computation shows

\displaystyle{\partial}^{*,h_{0}}\left(({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)\cdot H^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\partial}^{*,h_{0}}\left(-\left(e^{-f}{\partial}f\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right)\cdot H^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\overline{{\partial}}\left(-{\partial}f\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\overline{{\partial}}{\partial}f\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{E}=\Delta_{g}f\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{E}.

Moreover, one has

\displaystyle\kappa_{\widetilde{h}}(x)=\inf_{0\neq v\in E_{x}}\frac{{\widetilde{h}}(S^{\widetilde{h}}v,v)}{{\widetilde{h}}(v,v)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{0\neq v\in E_{x}}\frac{{\widetilde{h}}(S^{h_{0}}v,v)}{{\widetilde{h}}(v,v)}+\Delta_{g}f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{0\neq v\in E_{x}}\frac{h_{0}(S^{h_{0}}v,v)}{h_{0}(v,v)}+\Delta_{g}f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa_{h_{0}}(x)+\Delta_{g}f(x)=\lambda_{0}.

In particular, the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{\widetilde{h}}
is positive definite with respect to
\widetilde{h}
. By a perturbation trick, one can find a smooth Hermitian metric
h_{1}
in a small neighborhood of
\widetilde{h}
such that
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{1}}
is positive definite with respect to
h_{1}
. Now Theorem
5.8
follows by Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Chern number inequalities

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.6
and Theorem

.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold and
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
r
over
M
. Suppose that there exists a Hermitian metric
h
on
E
satisfying

(6.1)

a\cdot h\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\leq b\cdot h,

for some constants
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then the following Chern number inequality holds

(6.2)

\int_{M}\left((r-1)c^{2}_{1}(E)-2rc_{2}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}\leq\frac{r(r-1)\left(b-a\right)^{2}}{8\pi^{2}n^{2}}\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}.

Proof.

In the following, we work on the abstract Hermitian vector bundle
(E,h)
.
Let
\{z^{i}\}
be local holomorphic coordinates on
M
and
\{e_{\alpha}\}
be holomorphic frames of
E
. The Chern curvature tensor
R^{h}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
is given by

R^{h}=R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes\overline{e}^{\beta}.

We set

R^{(1)}_{i\overline{j}}=h^{\alpha\overline{\beta}}R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}},\quad R^{(2)}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=g^{i\overline{j}}R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad s_{h}=g^{i\overline{j}}R^{(1)}_{i\overline{j}}.

The following expressions are well-known:

(6.3)

\int_{M}c^{2}_{1}(E)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}\left(s_{h}^{2}-\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(1)}\right|_{g}^{2}\right)\omega_{g}^{n},

and

(6.4)

\int_{M}c_{2}(E)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}\left(s_{h}^{2}-\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(1)}\right|_{g}^{2}-\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(2)}\right|^{2}_{h}+|R^{h}|_{g,h}^{2}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

We define
T\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
:

(6.5)

T_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}=R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}-\frac{1}{n}g_{i\overline{j}}R^{(2)}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}-\frac{1}{r}R^{(1)}_{i\overline{j}}h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}+\frac{1}{nr}g_{i\overline{j}}h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}s_{h}.

A straightforward computation shows that

(6.6)

|T|^{2}=|R^{h}|^{2}-\frac{1}{n}\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(2)}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{r}\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(1)}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{nr}s_{h}^{2}.

By using
|T|^{2}
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(2rc_{2}(E)-(r-1)c^{2}_{1}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}\left(s_{h}^{2}-\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(1)}\right|^{2}-r\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(2)}\right|^{2}+r|R^{h}|^{2}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}\left(r|T|^{2}+\frac{n-1}{n}\left(s_{h}^{2}-r\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(2)}\right|^{2}\right)\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

Let
\{\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{r}\}
be the eigenvalues of
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}
with respect to
h
. Since
ah\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\leq bh
, it is obvious that

(6.7)

a\leq\lambda_{k}\leq b,

and so

(6.8)

s_{h}^{2}-r\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(2)}\right|^{2}=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_{k}\right)^{2}-r\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_{k}^{2}=-\sum_{i<j}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}\geq-\frac{r(r-1)}{2}(a-b)^{2}.

Finally, we establish the Chern number inequality

\int_{M}\left(2rc_{2}(E)-(r-1)c^{2}_{1}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}\geq-\frac{r(r-1)\left(b-a\right)^{2}}{8\pi^{2}n^{2}}\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}+\frac{r}{4\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}|T|^{2}\omega_{g}^{n}.

Hence, we obtain the result.
∎

The following result refines and extends Yau’s classical Chern number inequalities
[
Yau77
]
for Kähler-Einstein manifolds.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold with
\dim M\geq 2
. If there exist two constants
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(6.9)

a\omega_{g}\leq\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})\leq b\omega_{g},

then the following Chern number inquality holds

(6.10)

\int_{M}\left(nc^{2}_{1}(M)-2(n+1)c_{2}(M)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}\leq\frac{(a-b)^{2}(n^{2}-2)}{8\pi^{2}n^{2}}\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}.

Proof.

It is known that

(6.11)

\int_{M}c^{2}_{1}(M)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}\left(s^{2}-\left|\mathrm{Ric}\right|^{2}\right)\omega_{g}^{n},

and

(6.12)

\int_{M}c_{2}(M)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}\left(s^{2}-2\left|\mathrm{Ric}\right|^{2}+|R|^{2}\right)\omega_{g}^{n},

where
R
is the curvature tensor,
\mathrm{Ric}
is the Ricci curvature, and
s
is the scalar curvature of the Kähler metric
g
.
Let
T\in\Omega^{1,1}(M,T^{*1,0}M\otimes T^{*0,1}M)
be the tensor given by

(6.13)

T_{i\overline{j}k\overline{\ell}}=R_{i\overline{j}k\overline{\ell}}-\frac{1}{n}g_{i\overline{j}}R_{k\overline{\ell}}-\frac{1}{n}R_{i\overline{j}}g_{k\overline{\ell}}-\frac{1}{n(n+1)}g_{i\overline{\ell}}g_{k\overline{j}}s+\frac{n+2}{n^{2}(n+1)}g_{i\overline{j}}g_{k\overline{\ell}}s.

A straightforward computation shows that

(6.14)

|T|^{2}=|R|^{2}-\frac{2}{n}\left|\mathrm{Ric}\right|^{2}+\frac{2}{n^{2}(n+1)}s^{2}.

By (
6.11
), (
6.12
) and (
6.14
), one has

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(2(n+1)c_{2}(M)-nc_{1}(M)^{2}\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}\left(s^{2}-(n+2)\left|\mathrm{Ric}\right|^{2}+(n+1)|R|^{2}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}\left((n+1)|T|^{2}+\left(1-\frac{2}{n^{2}}\right)\left(s^{2}-n\left|\mathrm{Ric}\right|^{2}\right)\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

Let
\{\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}\}
be the eigenvalues of
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})
with respect to
g
. Since
a\omega_{g}\leq\mathrm{Ric}(\omega_{g})\leq b\omega_{g}
, it is obvious that
a\leq\lambda_{k}\leq b
,
and so

(6.15)

s^{2}-n\left|\mathrm{Ric}\right|^{2}=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\right)^{2}-n\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}^{2}=-\sum_{i<j}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}\geq-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}(a-b)^{2}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(2(n+1)c_{2}(M)-nc_{1}(M)^{2}\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}-2}{4\pi^{2}n^{3}(n-1)}\int_{M}\left(s^{2}-n\left|\mathrm{Ric}\right|^{2}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{(a-b)^{2}(n^{2}-2)}{8\pi^{2}n^{2}}\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

When
h
is a Kähler or balanced metric on a compact complex manifold– i.e.,
d\omega^{n-1}_{h}=0
– and satisfies the inequality

(6.16)

a\cdot h\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\leq b\cdot h,

for some constants
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then analogous Chern number inequalities to those in (
6.10
) hold.

7.
The prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor equation on line bundles

In this section, we investigate the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills equation on line bundles and clarify its connection with RC-positivity framework. Moreover, we show that the condition
P>0
in Theorem
1.1
is necessary:

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that there exists a Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
L
such that
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}>0
. If
P\in\Gamma(M,L^{*}\otimes\overline{L}^{*})
fails to be Hermitian positive definite, then the equation

(7.1)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=P

may admit two distinct smooth solutions.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Then for any Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
L
,

(7.2)

S^{h_{0}}=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}=s_{h_{0}}\cdot h_{0}\in\Gamma(M,L^{*}\otimes\overline{L}^{*})

where
s_{h_{0}}
is the Chern scalar curvature of
(L,h_{0})
.

Proof.

It is well-known for any Hermitian metrics
h_{1},h_{2}\in\Gamma(M,L^{*}\otimes\overline{L}^{*})
, there exists some smooth function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
such that

(7.3)

h_{2}=e^{-\varphi}h_{1}.

Moreover, if
P\in\Gamma(M,L^{*}\otimes\overline{L}^{*})
is a Hermitian tensor, then
P=fh_{1}
some
f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. Let
e
be a local holomorphic base of
L
and
h_{0}=h_{0}(e,e)
. By using the curvature formula, one has

(7.4)

S^{h_{0}}=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}=-g^{i\overline{j}}\frac{{\partial}^{2}\log h_{0}}{{\partial}z^{i}{\partial}\overline{z}^{j}}\cdot h_{0}e^{*}\otimes\overline{e}^{*}=-\left(\Delta_{g}\log h_{0}\right)h_{0}e^{*}\otimes\overline{e}^{*}.

The Chern scalar curvature function is

(7.5)

s_{h_{0}}=\mathrm{tr}_{h_{0}}S^{h_{0}}=-\Delta_{g}\log h_{0}.

Hence,
S^{h_{0}}=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}=s_{h_{0}}\cdot h_{0}

∎

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
(L,h_{0})
be a Hermitian holomorphic line bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
and
S^{h_{0}}=Fh_{0}
for some
F\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. Suppose that
P=Gh_{0}\in\Gamma(M,L^{*}\otimes\overline{L}^{*})
for some
G\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. Then there exists some Hermitian metric
h
on
L
satisfying

(7.6)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=P,

if and only if there exists some
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
such that

(7.7)

\left(\Delta_{g}\varphi+F\right)e^{-\varphi}=G.

In this correspondence, one has
h=e^{-\varphi}h_{0}
.

Proof.

It is known that
S^{h_{0}}=Fh_{0}
where

(7.8)

F=-\Delta_{g}\log h_{0}.

Hence, if
S^{h}=P=Gh_{0}
for some Hermitian metric
h
, then

(7.9)

G=-\Delta_{g}\log h\cdot\left(h\cdot h_{0}^{-1}\right).

On the other hand,
h=e^{-\varphi}h_{0}
for some
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. Therefore,

-\Delta_{g}\log h=-\Delta_{g}\log h_{0}+\Delta_{g}\varphi

and so
G=\left(\Delta_{g}\varphi+F\right)e^{-\varphi}
.
∎

The following result is a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
5.8
and Proposition
7.3
, which is a line bundle version of the Kazdan-Warner theorem (
[
KW74
]
).

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Suppose that there exists a Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
L
such that its Chern scalar curvature
F=s_{h_{0}}
satisfying

\int_{M}F\omega_{g}^{n}>0,

then for any positive smooth function
G
, the following equation has the unique smooth solution
\varphi
:

(7.10)

e^{-\varphi}(F+\Delta_{g}\varphi)=G.

For a detailed discussion on the existence of Hermitian metrics with positive Chern scalar curvature, positive total Chern scalar curvature and RC-positivity for line bundles, we refer to
[
Yang18
]
,
[
Yang19a
]
and
[
Yang19b
]
. This is also a motivation of this project for vector bundles. In this following, we show that the equation (
7.10
) may admit multiple solutions when
G
is not positive.

Proof of Proposition
7.1
.

Let
L
be an ample line bundle or a line bundle with

\int_{M}c_{1}(L)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1}>0

over a projective Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. There exists a Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
L
such that
S^{h_{0}}=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=Fh_{0}>0
(
[
Yang19a
]
). Recall that

(7.11)

F=-\Delta_{g}\log h_{0}=s_{h_{0}}.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
be a smooth function satisfying

(1)

f\geq 0
and there exists
p\in M
such that
f(p)=0
and
f(x)>0
if
x\neq p
.

(2)

There exists some small
\delta>0
and
q\in B(p,\delta)
such that

(7.12)

f(q)=\begin{cases}e^{-1/r^{n}}&\text{if }q\neq p\\
0&\text{if }q=p,\end{cases}

where
r=d_{g}(p,q)
is the distance function.

We consider the map
Q:(0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

(7.13)

Q(t)=\int_{M}\left(\Delta_{g}f\right)/(e^{f+t}-1)\cdot\omega_{g}^{n}.

We claim that

(7.14)

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}Q(t)=+\infty,\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}Q(t)=0.

Indeed, by Stokes’ theorem, one has

\displaystyle 2Q(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}\left(\Delta_{g}f\right)/(e^{f+t}-1)\cdot\omega_{g}^{n}=-\int_{M}g\left(\nabla f,\nabla(e^{f+t}-1)^{-1}\right)\cdot\omega_{g}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}\cdot(e^{f+t}-1)^{-2}\cdot e^{f+t}\cdot\omega_{g}^{n}=\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}\cdot\left(e^{(f+t)}+e^{-(f+t)}-2\right)^{-1}\cdot\omega_{g}^{n}.

Therefore,
Q
is a decreasing in
t
and by monotone convergence theorem, one has

(7.15)

\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}Q(t)=0.

On the other hand, by monotone convergence theorem

(7.16)

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}Q(t)=\int_{M\setminus\{p\}}|\nabla f|^{2}\cdot(e^{f}+e^{-f}-2)^{-1}\cdot\omega_{g}^{n}.

Moreover, there is a constant
A=A(M,\delta)>0
such that for any
q\in B(p,\delta)
, one has

(7.17)

e^{f}+e^{-f}-2\leq Af^{2},

and so

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}Q(t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle A^{-1}\int_{M\setminus\{p\}}|\nabla f|^{2}/f^{2}\cdot\omega_{g}^{n}=A^{-1}\int_{B(p,\delta)\setminus\{p\}}|\nabla\mathrm{log}(f)|^{2}\cdot\omega_{g}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{2}A^{-1}\int_{B(p,\delta)\setminus\{p\}}r^{-(2n+2)}\cdot\omega_{g}^{n}=+\infty.

By continuity of
Q(t)
, there exists some
t_{0}\in(0,+\infty)
such that

(7.18)

\int_{M}\left(\Delta_{\omega_{g}}f\right)/\left(e^{f+t_{0}}-1\right)\cdot\omega_{g}^{n}=n\int_{M}c_{1}(L)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1}>0.

Let
\Phi=f+t_{0}
. Then one has

(7.19)

\int_{M}\left(\Delta_{g}\Phi\right)/\left(e^{\Phi}-1\right)\cdot\omega_{g}^{n}=n\int_{M}c_{1}(L)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1}=\int_{M}s_{h_{0}}\cdot\omega_{g}^{n}=\int_{M}F\cdot\omega_{g}^{n}.

By Hodge theory, there exists a smooth function
\psi\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
such that

(7.20)

\frac{\Delta_{g}\Phi}{e^{\Phi}-1}=F-\Delta_{g}\psi.

If we define

(7.21)

G:=\frac{\Delta_{g}\Phi}{e^{\Phi}-1}\cdot e^{\psi}

then it is obvious that
the equation

(7.22)

e^{-\varphi}(F+\Delta_{g}\varphi)=G

has two different solutions
\varphi=-\psi
and
\varphi=\Phi-\psi
. In particular, the equation

(7.23)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=Gh_{0}

has two different solutions
h_{1}=e^{\psi}\cdot h_{0}
and
h_{2}=e^{\psi-\Phi}h_{0}
. ∎

Example 7.5
.

Let
(L,h_{0})
be a Hermitian holomorphic trivial line bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
.
Suppose
P=Gh_{0}\in\Gamma(M,L^{*}\otimes\overline{L}^{*})
for some
G\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
satisfying

\int_{M}G\omega_{g}^{n}<0

then the equation
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=P
has no solution.
Indeed, let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
and
h=e^{-\varphi}h_{0}
. One has

(7.24)

S^{h}=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=\left(e^{-\varphi}\Delta_{\omega_{g}}\varphi\right)\cdot h_{0}.

Moreover,

(7.25)

\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{h_{0}}S^{h}\cdot\omega_{g}^{n}=\int_{M}\left(e^{-\varphi}\Delta_{\omega_{g}}\varphi\right)\cdot\omega_{g}^{n}=\int_{M}e^{-\varphi}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\omega_{g}^{n}\geq 0.

In particular, if there exists some Hermitian metric
h
on
L
such that
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}R^{h}=P
, then
\int_{M}G\omega_{g}^{n}\geq 0
. This is a contradiction.
