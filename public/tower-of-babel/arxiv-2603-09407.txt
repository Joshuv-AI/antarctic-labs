On the topological complexity of non-simply connected spaces
Yuki Minowa

Abstract
. Topological complexity is a numerical homotopy invariant that measures the instability of motion planning in a space. To study the topological complexity of non-simply connected spaces, Costa and Farber introduced a cohomology class whose nilpotency gives a lower bound of topological complexity. Farber and Mescher constructed a spectral sequence that evaluates this nilpotency without direct computation. We extend these results with respect to a group homomorphism. As an application, we determine the topological complexity of some 3-manifolds with nonabelian fundamental group.



1.
Introduction

Topological complexity
is a numerical homotopy invariant introduced by Farber
[
11
]
to describe the instability in the motion planning problem. Let
\mathcal{P}X=\{\gamma\colon[0,1]\to X\}
denote the path space, and consider the evaluation map

\Pi\colon\mathcal{P}X\to X^{2},\quad\gamma\mapsto\left(\gamma(0),\gamma(1)\right).

The topological complexity of a space
X
, denoted by
\mathsf{TC}(X)
, is defined as the minimal integer such that
X^{2}
is covered by
k+1
open sets having local section of
\Pi
. If such an integer does not exist, then we set
\mathsf{TC}(X)=\infty
.

Since its introduction, topological complexity has been studied intensely in several contexts, and has been generalized in several directions. Among them, one of the core interests is a description of the topological complexity of a non-simply connected space, especially
K(\pi,1)
-space, in terms of its fundamental group. Costa and Farber
[
8
]
introduced a first cohomology class with local coefficients, and provided upper and lower bounds of topological complexity by the nilpotency of the class. This idea can be regarded as an analog of classical theory on the LS-category of
K(\pi,1)
-space by Berstein
[
3
]
, Švarc
[
22
]
, Dranishnikov and Rudyak
[
9
]
. However, it is quite hard to apply these bounds into practice, since the coefficients become significantly intricate as the nilpotency increases. There have been some computational results for spaces with abelian fundamental group, see
[
1
,
7
]
.

Farber and Mescher
[
15
]
considered a spectral sequence that evaluates the above nilpotency without direct computation. For a discrete group
G
, they constructed an exact couple

\cdots\xrightarrow{\delta}D_{r,s}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}E_{r,s}\to D_{r,s+1}\xrightarrow{\delta}D_{r+1,s}\to\cdots

such that the nontriviality of
\mathrm{Ker}(\varepsilon^{(n)})
in the
n
-th derived couple

\cdots\xrightarrow{\delta^{(n)}}D^{n}_{r,s}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon^{(n)}}E^{n}_{r-n+1,s+n-1}\to D^{n}_{r-n+1,s+n}\xrightarrow{\delta^{(n)}}D^{n}_{r-n+2,s+n-1}\to\cdots,

implies
\mathsf{TC}(BG)\geq n-1
. They also provided an explicit description of
E_{r,s}
in terms of group cohomology. As a result, they obtained a strong evaluation of
\mathsf{TC}(BG)
for a Gromov hyperbolic group
G
. Błaszczyk et al.
[
4
]
and Espinosa Baro et al.
[
10
]
generalized this spectral sequence to address variants of the motion planning problem. Nevertheless, this method has not been practically utilized for the computation of topological complexity. One possible reason is that the differentials of the spectral sequence were not precisely given, and so it is difficult to depict
E^{n}
for
n\geq 2
in general cases. Another explanation is that their construction is not functorial, which restrains us from applying abundant tools of group cohomology.

In this paper, we propose a new computational method grounded in these developments; we reconstruct their arguments using techniques from homological algebra. We investigate the property of a pair of adjoint functors associated with a group homomorphism, and the derived homomorphisms of
\mathrm{Ext}
groups. Our main purpose is to extend the above exact couple with respect to a group homomorphism, so as to facilitate the application of group cohomology. By utilizing our method, we determine the topological complexity of certain non-simply connected
3
-manifolds. As in
[
19
, Theorem 2]
, the
general quaternion group

Q_{8m}
acts on
S^{3}
without fixed point. Then the quotient
S^{3}/Q_{8m}
is a
3
-manifold with fundamental group
Q_{8m}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
m\geq 1
, the topological complexity of
S^{3}/Q_{8m}
is
6
.

Remark that Iwase and Miyata
[
18
]
computed
\mathsf{TC}(S^{3}/Q_{8})
by analysing a cell structure of
S^{3}/Q_{8}
.

This paper is structured as follows. Section
2
recalls preliminary results on the cohomological estimation of topological complexity. Section
3
prepares some terminology and lemmas on homological algebra. Section
4
provides an extension of the result of Farber and Mescher. Section
5
computes
\mathsf{TC}(S^{3}/Q_{8m})
by our method and proves Theorem
1.1
. Finally, Section
6
lists some remarks and problems that could lead to further investigation.

Topological preliminaries

2.1.
Sectional category and
\mathsf{TC}

We first recall the concept of sectional category. The

Let

denote the diagonal map. Then since there is a homotopy commutative diagram (cf.

, Theorem 7]

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Pi}

\displaystyle{X^{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\displaystyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Delta}
\displaystyle{X^{2},}

we get

.

We next recall a cohomological lower bound of topological complexity. Farber and Grant
[

]
introduced the
\mathsf{TC}
-weight of a cohomology class, which is an analog of the category weight for LS-category
[
13
]
. Let

be a space with fundamental group
G=\pi_{1}(X)
, and let

be a
G^{2}
-module. Then for a space

and a map

, we obtain a group homomorphism
\pi_{1}(Y)\to\pi_{1}(X^{2})=G^{2}
. We denote by
U_{f}(A)
the restriction of

by this homomorphism, which is a
\pi_{1}(Y)
-module; we will provide a precise definition in Section

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
15
, Definition 1]
)
The
\mathsf{TC}
-
weight
of a nontrivial cohomology class

, denoted by
\mathsf{wgt}(u)
, is the maximal integer

such that

f^{*}(u)=0\in H^{*}(Y;U_{f}(A))\text{ for any map }f\colon Y\to X^{2}\text{ with }\mathsf{secat}(f^{*}\Pi)<k.

We list some essential properties on this invariant. For proofs, see
[
12
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a space as above.

(1)

For any
u\in H^{*}(X^{2};A)
,
\mathsf{wgt}(u)\leq|u|
.

(2)

If
\mathsf{wgt}(u)=k
for some
u\in H^{*}(X^{2};A)
, then
\mathsf{TC}(X)\geq k
.

(3)

For
i=1,\cdots,m
, let
u_{i}\in H^{*}(X^{2};A_{i})
be cohomology classes with

u_{1}\smile\cdots\smile u_{i}\neq 0\in H^{*}(X^{2};A_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes A_{i}).

Then

\mathsf{wgt}(u_{1}\smile\cdots\smile u_{i})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathsf{wgt}(u_{i}).

(4)

Let
u\neq 0\in H^{*}(X^{2};A)
be a zero-divisor, i.e. a cohomology class satisfying
\Delta^{*}(u)=0\in H^{*}(X;U_{\Delta}A)
. Then
\mathsf{wgt}(u)\geq 1
.

2.2.
Canonical class and spectral sequence

In this section, we recall some previous results regarding the first cohomology class of Costa and Farber
[

. Let

Let

is free as a

(
[

The

is a cohomology class determined by a map

This class is canonical in the sense that, for the canonical map

associated with the universal covering, we have

. For this reason, we abbreviate

to

. Remark that, if

and so

. One may also check that

is a zero-divisor; therefore if

. In fact, they obtained a shaper estimation.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
8
, Theorem 7]
)
.

Let

be a CW-complex of dimension

\displaystyle\cdots\xrightarrow{\delta}\mathrm{Ext}^{r}_{G^{2}}(I_{G}^{\otimes s},A)\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}\mathrm{Ext}^{r}_{G^{2}}

Lemma 5.2

\displaystyle H^{*}(V)\cong A[x,y]\to A[u,v]/(u^{2})\cong H^{\ast}(C_{Q}(a)),x\mapsto iu,y\mapsto ju\,\text{ for }a=\pm\mathbf{x}^{i}\mathbf{y}^{j}.

Lemma 5.4

6.
Final remarks

We conclude this paper with some remarks on our results, and some open problems regarding the topological complexity of non-simply connected spaces. We hope that these problems precipitate further research.

First, we admit that we have not provided an explicit description of the differentials of
E^{n}
. While the statement of Lemma
5.3
implies triviality of the differentials, it is possible only because the group
G=\mathbb{Z}/2
and the set of tuples
(G\setminus\langle 1\rangle)^{s}
are enough simple. Błaszczyk et al.
[
4
]
mentioned the first differential
d^{1}
in more general setting, yet their description is not practical for computation. Therefore, it is essential to investigate the differentials and
E^{n}
-terms for
n\geq 2
. We believe that it concerns with Farrell cohomology theory, as a spectral sequence in
[
5
, X. (4.1)]
has some resemblance to (
4.5
).

Next, we remark on the determination of
\mathsf{TC}(S^{n}/G)
for each free action
G\curvearrowright S^{n}
. Finite groups that act freely on spheres are completely characterized by Madsen et al.
[
20
]
. In particular, Milnor
[
19
]
classified a finite group
G
acting freely on
S^{3}
.

Question 6.1
.

For each finite group
G
acting freely on
S^{3}
, determine
\mathsf{TC}(S^{3}/G)
. More generally, determine
\mathsf{TC}(S^{n}/G)
for each finite group
G
with free action on sphere.

The first question appears to be difficult to address, since the Poincaré sphere
S^{3}/(2I)
has the same integral cohomology as
S^{3}
. The second quection is more intractable; even the topological complexity of the lens space
L^{2n+1}_{p}=S^{2n+1}/(\mathbb{Z}/p)
is not completely determined. We expect that our method contributes to further studies on this problem.

Finally, we mention further generalizations to address various types of motion planning problem. There have been many analogs of topological complexity considered. The most significant one is the sequential (or higher) topological complexity introduced by Rudyak
[
21
]
, which arose from the robot motion planning passing through several intermediate points; for a space
X
and an integer
r\geq 2
, the invariant is restated as
\mathsf{TC}_{r}(X):=\mathsf{secat}(\Delta\colon X\to X^{r})
, where
\Delta
denotes the diagonal. Farber and Oprea
[
16
]
asked:

Question 6.2
.

For which space
X
, is the formal series
\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\mathsf{TC}_{r+1}(X)z^{r}
a rational function
P(z)/{(1-z)^{2}}
such that
P(z)
is a polynomial with
P(1)=\mathsf{cat}(X)
?

Notice that there exists a family of spaces not satisfying the above condition
[
14
]
. As a recent progress,
[
1
]
collected affirmative cases of spaces with finite cyclic fundamental group. We now state a conjecture:

Conjecture 6.3
.

For
m\geq 1
, the
r
-th sequential topological complexity of
S^{3}/Q_{8m}
is equal to
3r
, supporting Question
6.2
.

We also anticipate that our method will be applied to other variants, for instance, parametrized topological complexity introduced by Cohen et al.
[
6
]
.
