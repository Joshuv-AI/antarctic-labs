Thermalization with Gaussian Quantum Cellular Automata
Roman Geiko, Jake Gerenraich

Abstract
We study the long-time dynamics of many-body bosonic lattice systems under translation-invariant Gaussian quantum cellular automata. We formulate two sets of conditions on GQCAs which separately guarantee thermalization of any state on the local Weyl algebra to the infinite temperature state, whenever the state is locally normal and has uniformly bounded particle density. Our main intermediate result is a quantum many-body generalization of the classic Riemann-Lebesgue lemma which is a bound on expectation values of local Weyl operators involving their support and the state’s particle density.



keywords
Quantum lattice systems, quantum cellular automata, quantum dynamics, thermalization

Introduction and main results

The emergence of thermodynamic behavior from unitary evolution of quantum systems is central to justifying the formalism of equilibrium statistical mechanics from first principles. Despite decades of progress in the foundations of quantum statistical mechanics, a robust understanding of thermalization, or “approach to equilibrium”, has remained elusive. One path forward is to identify broad, physically motivated classes of dynamics for which thermalization can be rigorously established. Here we examine quantum lattice systems of infinite-dimensional degrees of freedom in infinite volume and formulate two sets of sufficient conditions on the dynamics to thermalize a certain class of initial states.

We consider discrete time dynamics which can be seen as the Floquet, or stroboscopic, picture of a continuous time evolution. In particular, we suppose the dynamics have a uniform bound on the spread of the support of local operators, known as Quantum Cellular Automata (QCAs)
8
. Thermalization with QCAs was studied in e.g.
6
;
2
;
3

4
. The authors of
2

3

4
, to arbitrary spatial dimension, number of qubits per site, and a much broader class of initial states. Here, we continue this research program by analyzing thermalization in quantum many-body systems with non-compact, continuous degrees of freedom under the action of Gaussian QCAs.

The local probes considered here are local Weyl operators, while the dynamics is given by translation-invariant Gaussian QCAs. Such automata have two useful features. First, they preserve locality: the support of a strictly local observable can grow only at finite speed. Second, they preserve the Weyl relations, so their action on Weyl operators is governed by a linear symplectic map on phase-space labels. The long-time problem for local Weyl observables can therefore be reduced to the spectral dynamics of a finite-spread symplectic convolution operator.

Remark 1.3

(Scope of the theorems)
.

The conclusion is deliberately formulated at the level of local Weyl observables, equivalently finite linear combinations of local Weyl operators. Within that class the assumptions on the initial state are weak: the state need not be translation invariant, quasi-free, or Gibbs. The proof uses no special normal form for the automaton beyond finite spread, translation invariance, and the stated hyperbolicity hypotheses.

The limitation is equally important. The theorem is not a statement about arbitrary bounded operators in
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma})
, nor about arbitrary unbounded local observables. The unbounded number operators enter only through the moment condition defining finite particle density.

Remark 1.4

(Multiple bosonic modes per unit cell)
.

Our results are formulated for a single bosonic mode per unit cell. Instead, we could have set the on-site Hilbert space as
{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{r})

1.1

The analytic input behind both theorems is a many-body version of the quantum Riemann–Lebesgue lemma of Werner
10

Theorem 1.5

(Many-body quantum Riemann–Lebesgue estimate)
.

Let
\omega
be a locally normal state satisfying (
1.2
). If
0\neq\xi\in V
and
\Gamma=\operatorname{supp}\xi
, then

\left\lvert\omega(W(\xi))\right\rvert\leqslant\frac{\pi\sqrt{2\nu\left\lvert\Gamma\right\rvert+1}}{\left\|\xi\right\|_{2}}.

(1.8)

The mechanism of the proof is then simple. Let
\xi_{k}=\Phi^{k}\xi
. The dynamical hypotheses imply
\left\|\xi_{k}\right\|_{2}\geqslant c_{\xi}e^{\gamma k}
for every nonzero local label
\xi
. On the other hand, finite spread implies
\left\lvert\operatorname{supp}\xi_{k}\right\rvert\leqslant C_{\xi}(1+k)^{d}
. Substituting these two bounds into (
1.8
) gives

\displaystyle\left\lvert\omega(W(\Phi^{k}\xi))\right\rvert\leqslant\frac{\pi\sqrt{2\nu C_{\xi}(1+k)^{d}+1}}{c_{\xi}e^{\gamma k}}\longrightarrow 0.

(1.9)

Thus exponential growth in phase-space frequency dominates the polynomial growth of spatial support, and local Weyl observables lose all memory of the initial state.

3
Many-body quantum Riemann-Lebesgue lemma

In this section we prove estimates on expectation values of local Weyl operators which are essential for the further discussion of thermalization. The classic Riemann-Lebesgue lemma states that the Fourier coefficients of an integrable function vanish at infinity. The quantum Riemann-Lebesgue lemma of R. Werner states that the “quantum Fourier coefficients” of a normal state vanish at infinity
10
. The main result of this section is a many-body generalization of the quantum Riemann-Lebesgue lemma where expectation values of local Weyl operators play the role of the Fourier coefficients.

Remark 3.1
.

Before treating the genuinely many-body situation, let us record the simplest case where the dynamics does not spread the support of a Weyl label but makes its phase-space norm diverge. Then one can use the ordinary finite-dimensional quantum Riemann–Lebesgue lemma of
10
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\omega
be a locally normal state on
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and let

\xi\in V
. Put
\xi_{k}=\Phi^{k}\xi
. Assume that there exists a finite region

\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
such that

\operatorname{supp}(\xi_{k})\subseteq\Gamma
for all
k\geqslant 0
and that

\|\xi_{k}\|_{2}\to\infty
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2|\Gamma|}
. Then,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\omega(W(\xi_{k}))=0.

(3.1)

Proof.

Since all
W(\xi_{k})
belong to the same finite-volume Weyl algebra

{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}
, local normality gives a density operator
\rho_{\Gamma}
on

{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma}
such that, in the Schrödinger representation,

\omega(W(\xi_{k}))={\operatorname{Tr}}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma}}(\rho_{\Gamma}W_{\Gamma}(\xi_{k})).

The right-hand side is the quantum Fourier transform of the trace-class operator

\rho_{\Gamma}
evaluated at
\xi_{k}
. Since
\|\xi_{k}\|\to\infty
, the quantum
Riemann–Lebesgue lemma
10
for finitely many oscillators
gives
\operatorname{Tr}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma}}(\rho_{\Gamma}W_{\Gamma}(\xi_{k}))\to 0
.
∎

The hypothesis of Lemma
3.2
is non-diffusive, and thus prevents full thermalization over the Weyl algebra. To amend this, one must impose diffusive properties onto the dynamics; consequently, the density operators live on larger and larger tensor factors, so the preceding finite-dimensional Riemann–Lebesgue argument is not uniform. The estimates beginning with Theorem
3.4
and the following corollaries replace this argument by a bound involving both
\|\Phi^{k}\xi\|_{2}
and
|\operatorname{supp}(\Phi^{k}\xi)|
.

This section is structured as follows. In §
3.1
, we introduce a quantity that bounds the expectation value of Weyl operators in locally normal states. In §
3.2
, we introduce convention for handling unbounded operators and prove some useful convergences and bounds on states and observables. In §
3.3
we connect local particle number to our bounds, followed by §
3.4
where we demonstrate that finite local particle number averages yield trace-class commutators between density operators and Weyl operators. Finally, in §
3.5
, we prove that, for bosonic many-body systems with bounded particle density, Weyl operator averages on locally normal states are bounded by the ratio of the QCA spread to the support of the Weyl label. That is to say, in the unstable sector of the QCA’s corresponding phase space map, the Weyl label norm grows exponentially, so long-time Weyl averages vanish.

Definition 3.5

Lemma 3.10

For the following proposition, we will use Bochner theory to bound trace norms, in particular, that Lebesgue theory passes through to measurable functions valued in Banach spaces. A standard introduction to the subject is Chapter V of

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
A
be a self-adjoint operator, and
\rho
a density operator. Assume that

. Then, the map

is continuously differentiable in trace norm, and

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(t)=ie^{iAt}[A,\rho]e^{-iAt}.

(3.29)

Equivalently,

\displaystyle e^{iAt}\rho e^{-iAt}-\rho=i\int_{0}^{t}e^{iAs}[A,\rho]e^{-iAs}ds,

(3.30)

a Bochner integral in
\mathcal{T}^{1}
. In particular,

\displaystyle\left\|e^{itA}\rho e^{-itA}-\rho\right\|_{1}\leqslant|t|\,\left\|[A,\rho]\right\|_{1}.

(3.31)

Proof.

Since
A\rho,\,\rho A\in\mathcal{T}^{1}
and
\mathbb{I}([-n,n])\rightarrow\mathbbm{1}
strongly, Lemma
3.9
gives

\displaystyle\left\|[A_{n},\rho]-[A,\rho]\right\|_{1}\to 0.

(3.32)

For each

, the generator

is bounded, so the usual bounded-operator calculus gives a trace norm

with derivative

\displaystyle F_{n}(t)-\rho=i\int_{0}^{t}e^{isA_{n}}[A_{n},\rho]e^{-isA_{n}}\,ds.

Let

. Since

strongly, uniformly for

\displaystyle\sup_{|t|\leqslant\tau}\left\|e^{itA_{n}}\rho e^{-itA_{n}}-e^{itA}\rho e^{-itA}\right\|_{1}\to 0,

(3.34)

\displaystyle\sup_{|t|\leqslant\tau}\left\|e^{itA_{n}}[A_{n},\rho]e^{-itA_{n}}-e^{itA}[A,\rho]e^{-itA}\right\|_{1}\to 0.

(3.35)

One then has that

\displaystyle e^{itA}\rho e^{-itA}-\rho=i\int_{0}^{t}e^{isA}[A,\rho]e^{-isA}\,ds,

3.3
Local number operators

To achieve our many-body Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, we control Weyl operator
averages by particle number in the finite-volume Schrödinger representations.
Although
{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}
is the algebraic Weyl
*
-algebra, its locally normal
representatives act on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma}
, where we have the usual unbounded ladder
operators

\displaystyle a_{x}:=\frac{\hat{q}_{x}+i\hat{p}_{x}}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad N_{\Gamma}:=\sum_{x\in\Gamma}a_{x}^{*}a_{x}.

(3.38)

The operator
N_{\Gamma}
is not an element of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\Gamma}
; it is an unbounded
operator affiliated with the Schrödinger representation and is used only to
impose the finite-density moment condition on locally normal states.

Definition 3.13
.

We define the core
\mathcal{D}_{fin,\Gamma}
of the local number operator
N_{\Gamma}
, as the finite span of number eigenvectors of the family
\{a^{*}_{x}a_{x}\}_{x\in\Gamma}
.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{fin,\Gamma}\coloneqq\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\Gamma}\,|\forall x\in\Gamma,\;\,a_{x}^{*}a_{x}\psi=n_{x}\psi\,\quad\mbox{for some}\,n_{x}\in\mathbb{N}\}\,.

(3.39)

It is a subset of
D(N_{\Gamma})
such that its graph closure is the graph over the domain, that is,
\overline{\{(x,N_{\Gamma}x)\,|\,x\in\mathcal{D}_{fin,\Gamma}\}}=\{(x,N_{\Gamma}x)\,|\,x\in D(N_{\Gamma})\}
. Working over the core allows one to avoid certain technical details with respect to the domains of unbounded operators in our next proposition, as it is closed under any finite string of ladder operators.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
be finite and let
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2|\Gamma|}
satisfy
\left\|\xi\right\|=1
. Then the following holds:

(i)

On
\mathcal{D}_{fin,\Gamma}
one has

\displaystyle R_{\Gamma}(\xi)^{2}\leqslant 2N_{\Gamma}+1

(3.40)

as quadratic forms.

(ii)

D(N_{\Gamma}^{1/2})\subseteq D(R_{\Gamma}(\xi))
and for every
\psi\in D(N_{\Gamma}^{1/2})
,

\displaystyle\left\|R_{\Gamma}(\xi)\psi\right\|^{2}\leqslant 2\langle\psi|N_{\Gamma}\psi\rangle+\left\|\psi\right\|^{2},

(3.41)

and so for
\rho_{\Gamma}
a density operator with
\omega_{\rho_{\Gamma}}(N_{\Gamma})<\infty
,

\displaystyle\omega_{\rho_{\Gamma}}(R_{\Gamma}(\xi)^{2})\leqslant 2\omega_{\rho_{\Gamma}}(N_{\Gamma})+1.

(3.42)

Proof.

(i)

Write
\xi=(u,v)
and define
\gamma_{x}:=v_{x}-iu_{x}
such that
\sum_{x\in\Gamma}|\gamma_{x}|^{2}=1
. On
\mathcal{D}_{fin,\Gamma}
we have

\displaystyle R_{\Gamma}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{x\in\Gamma}(\gamma_{x}a_{x}+\overline{\gamma}_{x}a_{x}^{*}).

(3.43)

Define the composite annihilation operator,

\displaystyle b=\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\gamma_{x}a_{x},

(3.44)

we have
[b,b^{*}]=1
and on
\mathcal{D}_{fin,\Gamma}
,

\displaystyle R_{\Gamma}(\xi)=\frac{b+b^{*}}{\sqrt{2}}.

(3.45)

For
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{fin,\Gamma}
,

\displaystyle\left\|b\psi\right\|^{2}=\Bigl\|\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\gamma_{x}a_{x}\psi\Bigr\|^{2}\leqslant\sum_{x\in\Gamma}|\gamma_{x}|^{2}\cdot\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\left\|a_{x}\psi\right\|^{2}=\langle\psi|N_{\Gamma}\psi\rangle,

(3.46)

so
b^{*}b\leqslant N_{\Gamma}
as quadratic forms on
\mathcal{D}_{fin,\Gamma}
. We have

\displaystyle 0\leqslant(b-

\displaystyle b^{*})^{*}(b-b^{*})=bb^{*}+b^{*}b-b^{2}-b^{*2}

\displaystyle\implies b^{2}+b^{*2}\leqslant bb^{*}+b^{*}b

(3.47)

which we can use to say that

\displaystyle(b+b^{*})^{2}

\displaystyle=b^{2}+b^{*2}+bb^{*}+b^{*}b\leqslant 2(bb^{*}+b^{*}b)=2(2b^{*}b+1)\leqslant 2(2N_{\Gamma}+1)

\displaystyle\implies\;\;\;\;\;\;

\displaystyle R_{\Gamma}(\xi)^{2}\leqslant 2N_{\Gamma}+1

(3.48)

as quadratic forms on
\mathcal{D}_{fin,\Gamma}
.

(ii)

For
\psi\in D(N_{\Gamma}^{1/2})
and
P_{N_{\Gamma},[0,n]}
the spectral projector onto
[0,n]
, we have that
P_{N_{\Gamma},[0,n]}\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{fin,\Gamma}
with

\displaystyle P_{N_{\Gamma},[0,n]}\psi\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\psi,\quad N_{\Gamma}^{1/2}P_{N_{\Gamma},[0,n]}\psi\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}N_{\Gamma}^{1/2}\psi.

(3.49)

Applying the quadratic form inequality from above,

\displaystyle\left\|R_{\Gamma}(\xi)\left(P_{N_{\Gamma},[0,n]}-P_{N_{\Gamma},[0,m]}\right)\psi\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left\langle\left(P_{N_{\Gamma},[0,n]}-P_{N_{\Gamma},[0,m]}\right)\psi,N_{\Gamma}\left(P_{N_{\Gamma},[0,n]}-P_{N_{\Gamma},[0,m]}\right)\psi\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\left(P_{N_{\Gamma},[0,n]}-P_{N_{\Gamma},[0,m]}\right)\psi\right\|^{2},

(3.50)

which goes to zero as

, so
R_{\Gamma}(\xi)P_{N_{\Gamma},[0,n]}\psi
is Cauchy in
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
. Since
R_{\Gamma}(\xi)
is self-adjoint, it is closed, and thus

with
R_{\Gamma}(\xi)P_{N_{\Gamma},[0,n]}\psi\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}R_{\Gamma}(\xi)\psi

\displaystyle\left\|R_{\Gamma}(\xi)\psi\right\|^{2}\leqslant 2\langle\psi|N_{\Gamma}\psi\rangle+\left\|\psi\right\|^{2}.

(3.51)

Lastly, we consider the spectral decomposition
\rho_{\Gamma}=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}\phi_{j}^{*}

3.6
implies that for every

\phi_{j}\in D(N_{\Gamma}^{1/2})
and thus

\displaystyle\omega_{\rho_{\Gamma}}(R_{\Gamma}(\xi)^{2})\leqslant 2\omega_{\rho_{\Gamma}}(N_{\Gamma})+1.

(3.52)

Finite second moments imply trace-class commutators

Proposition

Proposition 3.15
.

\rho
be a density operator and

(3.53)

\displaystyle\left\|[A,\rho]\right\|_{1}\leqslant 2\sqrt{\omega_{\rho}(A^{2})}.

(3.54)

Let

It follows from the spectral decomposition of

that

, and by the same argument as above,

\displaystyle\left\|\rho A\right\|_{1}\leqslant\sqrt{\omega_{\rho}(A^{2})}.

(3.57)

S
, such that

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{k}|\leqslant C(1+k)^{d}.

(3.70)

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\omega(W(\xi_{k}))|\leqslant\frac{\pi}{c\lambda^{k}}\sqrt{2\nu C(1+k)^{d}+1}\overset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0.

(3.71)

∎

4
Hyperbolicity and regularity of GQCA

In this section we introduce local and uniform hyperbolicity, and regularity of GQCAs. We discuss how combinations of these conditions lead to the dynamics thermalizing locally normal states on
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{loc}}
with finite particle density.

4.1
Uniform hyperbolicity

The complexified Hilbert space
P_{\mathbb{C}}\coloneqq P\otimes_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathbb{C}
is Fourier dual to
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{2})
. The dual to
a\in P_{\mathbb{C}}
is

\displaystyle\widehat{a}(\theta)=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}a_{x}e^{ix\cdot\theta},\qquad\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{d})\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\,.

(4.1)

Then,
\Phi
acts on
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{2})
by multiplication by the matrix-valued symbol

\displaystyle A(\theta):=\Phi(e^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,e^{i\theta_{d}})=\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\Phi_{x}e^{ix\cdot\theta}.

(4.2)

Definition 4.1
.

We call
\Phi\in Sp(R,2)

uniformly hyperbolic
if there exists
\delta>0
such that the spectrum of each fiber
A(\theta)
is uniformly separated from the unit circle:

\displaystyle\sigma(A(\theta))\cap\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:e^{-\delta}<|\zeta|<e^{\delta}\}=\varnothing,\qquad\forall\theta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}.

(4.3)

The uniform hyperbolicity condition is a direct analog of the condition satisfied by the Anosov diffeomorphisms
1
. Below we demonstrate that, similar to Anosov diffeomorphisms splitting the tangent bundle into stable/unstable subbundles, uniformly hyperbolic GQCAs split
P
into stable/unstable subspaces.

Assume from now on that
\Phi
is uniformly hyperbolic. Choose any radius
\rho
with
e^{-\delta}<\rho<e^{\delta}
where
\delta
is taken from the preceding definition. Since no eigenvalue of
A(\theta)
crosses the circle
|\zeta|=\rho
, the Riesz projection

\displaystyle\Pi_{-}(\theta):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|\zeta|=\rho}(\zeta I-A(\theta))^{-1}\,d\zeta

(4.4)

exists for every
\theta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
and is independent of the choice of
\rho
inside the spectral gap. It projects onto the generalized eigenspaces of
A(\theta)
with
|\lambda|<1
. We also set

\Pi_{+}(\theta):=I-\Pi_{-}(\theta),

which projects onto the generalized eigenspaces with
|\lambda|>1
. The maps
\theta\mapsto\Pi_{\pm}(\theta)
are continuous, and in fact real analytic on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
. Indeed, the symbol
A(\theta)

is real analytic on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
and extends holomorphically to a
complex neighborhood of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, since it is a Laurent polynomial
in
e^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,e^{i\theta_{d}}
, see
5
, Chapter II.

Note that the projections commute with the symbol:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\pm}(\theta)A(\theta)=A(\theta)\Pi_{\pm}(\theta).

(4.5)

Therefore they define bounded projections
\Pi_{\pm}
on
P_{\mathbb{C}}
by

\displaystyle(\Pi_{\pm}f)(\theta):=\Pi_{\pm}(\theta)f(\theta)\,.

(4.6)

Defining

P_{\mathbb{C},\pm}:=\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{\pm},

we obtain the
A(\theta)
-invariant splitting

\displaystyle P_{\mathbb{C}}=P_{\mathbb{C},-}\oplus P_{\mathbb{C},+}.

(4.7)

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Phi
be uniformly hyperbolic and let
\delta
be the constant from Definition
4.1
. For every
0<\gamma<\delta

there is
C_{\gamma}<\infty
such that, for all
k\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{k}f_{-}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\gamma}e^{-\gamma k}\|f_{-}\|_{2},\qquad f_{-}\in P_{\mathbb{C},-},

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{-k}f_{+}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\gamma}e^{-\gamma k}\|f_{+}\|_{2},\qquad f_{+}\in P_{\mathbb{C},+}.

(4.8)

Proof.

Choose
r_{\gamma}=e^{-\gamma}
. Since
\gamma<\delta
, the circle

|z|=r_{\gamma}
separates the stable spectrum of
A(\theta)
from
the unstable spectrum for every
\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. By compactness,

\displaystyle M_{\gamma}:=\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\ |z|=r_{\gamma}}\|(zI-A(\theta))^{-1}\|<\infty.

(4.9)

The holomorphic functional calculus gives

\displaystyle A(\theta)^{k}\Pi_{-}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=r_{\gamma}}z^{k}(zI-A(\theta))^{-1}\,dz.

(4.10)

Hence

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}\|A(\theta)^{k}\Pi_{-}(\theta)\|\leqslant C_{\gamma}e^{-\gamma k}.

(4.11)

Integrating the resulting pointwise bound over
\mathbb{T}^{d}
gives
the first estimate. The second estimate follows by applying the same
argument to
A(\theta)^{-1}
on
\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{+}(\theta)
,
whose spectrum is also contained in
\{|z|\leqslant e^{-\delta}\}
.

∎

Equivalently, nonzero vectors in
P_{\mathbb{C},+}
grow exponentially under positive powers of
\Phi
, while nonzero vectors in
P_{\mathbb{C},-}
grow exponentially under negative powers. Also note that the estimates need not hold with
C_{\gamma}=1
in the original
\ell^{2}
norm.

Having obtained a projection acting on the complex Hilbert space
P_{\mathbb{C}}
, we can pass to the real phase space. Since the coefficients of
\Phi
are real,

\displaystyle A(-\theta)=\overline{A(\theta)}.

(4.12)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\Pi_{\pm}(-\theta)=\overline{\Pi_{\pm}(\theta)}.

(4.13)

Thus
\Pi_{\pm}
preserve the real subspace
P\subset P_{\mathbb{C}}
, and we get an invariant splitting into real Hilbert subspaces

\displaystyle P=P_{-}\oplus P_{+},\qquad P_{\pm}:=P\cap P_{\mathbb{C},\pm}.

(4.14)

Remark 4.3
.

The Riesz projection
\Pi_{-}
is generally not a finite-range operator: its Fourier coefficients usually do not have finite support. The uniform spectral gap implies exponential off-diagonal decay of those coefficients, so the projection is quasi-local rather than strictly local.

Let

\displaystyle\Omega(a,b):=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}a_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}Jb_{n},\qquad a,b\in P,

(4.15)

be the real symplectic form, extended complex bilinearly to
P_{\mathbb{C}}
. In Fourier variables,

\displaystyle\Omega(f,g)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}f(-\theta)^{\mathsf{T}}Jg(\theta)\,\frac{d\theta}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,.

(4.16)

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\Phi
be a uniformly hyperbolic Gaussian QCA. Then there is a
\Phi
-invariant splitting

\displaystyle P=P_{-}\oplus P_{+}

(4.17)

into two Lagrangian subspaces. Moreover,
\Phi
is exponentially contracting on
P_{-}
, and
\Phi^{-1}
is exponentially contracting on
P_{+}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

The real subspaces
P_{-}
and
P_{+}
are Lagrangian subspaces of
(P,\Omega)
.

Proof.

Equation (
2.21
) implies that
\Phi
preserves
\Omega
. If
f,g\in P_{-}
, then

\Omega(f,g)=\Omega(\Phi^{k}f,\Phi^{k}g)

for every
k\geq 0
. By (
4.8
), the right-hand side tends to zero as
k\to\infty
, hence
\Omega(f,g)=0
. Thus
P_{-}
is isotropic. The same argument applied to
\Phi^{-1}
shows that
P_{+}
is isotropic. Since
P=P_{-}\oplus P_{+}
and
\Omega
is nondegenerate, each isotropic summand is maximal isotropic, hence Lagrangian.
∎

In summary, any uniformly hyperbolic GQCA splits
P
into two lagrangians containing stable and unstable local (as well as non-local) Weyl labels.

Local hyperbolicity

Definition 4.6
.

We call
\Phi\in Sp(R,2)

locally hyperbolic
if there exists
\theta_{0}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
such that
A(\theta_{0})
is hyperbolic, i.e., there exists
\delta>0
:

\displaystyle\sigma(A(\theta_{0}))\cap\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:e^{-\delta}<|\zeta|<e^{\delta}\}=\varnothing\,.

(4.18)

Since the hyperbolicity condition,
\det(zI-A(\theta_{0}))\neq 0
for all
z\in S^{1}
, is open, and
A
is continuous, there is an open neighborhood
U
of
\theta_{0}
such that
A(\theta)
is hyperbolic for
\theta\in U
. Then, for each
\theta\in U
we have Riesz spectral projection
\Pi_{-}(\theta)
which continuously depends on
\theta
. We denote the corresponding projection on
U
by
\Pi_{-}^{U}
. We obtain the same results about the splitting of
P
, now only for a range of frequencies.

4.2
Regularity

In this section we formulate two conditions on GQCAs. Their role is to guarantee that any local Weyl label contains an unstable component such that the appropriate dynamics expands their norm.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
\alpha
be a GQCA and
\Phi\in\operatorname{Sp}(R,2)
the corresponding symplectic transformation.

1.

We say that a GQCA
\Phi
is not regular if there exist
0\neq\xi\in V
,
c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}
, and
x\in{\Lambda}
such that

\displaystyle\alpha(W(\xi))=e^{i\chi(\xi)}\tau_{x}W(c\xi)\,.

(4.19)

Equivalently,

\displaystyle\Phi\xi=cT_{x}\xi\,.

(4.20)

Otherwise, we call
\alpha
(and
\Phi
)
regular
.

2.

Assume that
\Phi
is uniformly hyperbolic and let

P=P_{-}\oplus P_{+}
be the stable/unstable splitting defined by the
Riesz projection. We say that
\Phi
is
everyday
if

\displaystyle V\cap P_{-}=\{0\}.

(4.21)

Equivalently, every nonzero strictly local label
\xi\in V
has a
nonzero unstable component:

\displaystyle\Pi_{+}\xi\neq 0.

(4.22)

The regular GQCAs are discussed in
2
and in
3
. We are going to show that these two definitions are equivalent as long as we work with the on-site Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.

We define the field of rational functions
K:=\mathbb{C}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d})
and notice that
A
, the symbol of
\Phi
, as a Laurent polynomial matrix, can be viewed as an endomorphism of
K^{2}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

The uniformly hyperbolic GQCA
\alpha
(or
\Phi
) is everyday if and only if it is regular.

Proof.

Let
R_{\mathbb{C}}:=R\otimes_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}[u_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,u_{d}^{\pm 1}]

and identify
V_{\mathbb{C}}:=V\otimes_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathbb{C}
with
R_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. Under this
identification
T_{x}
is multiplication by the monomial
u^{x}
. Since the Riesz
projection defining
P_{-}
is real,

\displaystyle P_{-}\cap V=\{0\}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P_{\mathbb{C},-}\cap V_{\mathbb{C}}=\{0\}.

(4.23)

We prove the contrapositive in both directions.

First suppose that
\Phi
is not everyday. Choose

0\neq\xi\in P_{-}\cap V
and view
\xi
as an element of
R_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. Since

{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, the space
\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{-}(\theta)
is
one-dimensional for every
\theta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
. Hence, whenever

\widehat{\xi}(\theta)\neq 0
, the vectors
A(\theta)\widehat{\xi}(\theta)
and

\widehat{\xi}(\theta)
are proportional. Thus, in
K^{2}
,

\displaystyle A(u)\hat{\xi}(u)=\mu(u)\hat{\xi}(u)\,,\quad\mu\in K\,.

(4.24)

The eigenvalue
\mu(u)
is a root of characteristic polynomial
\chi_{A}(z,u)=\det(zI-A(u))=z^{2}-z{\operatorname{Tr}}[A(u)]+\mbox{Det}[A(u)]
. We observe that
\mu(u)\in R_{\mathbb{C}}
: we substitute
\mu=\frac{f}{g}
into the characteristic equation and find that
g|f^{2}
but
\operatorname{gcd}(f,g)=1
and
R_{\mathbb{C}}
is an UFD, which forces
g
be a unit. Then, indeed,
\mu(u)
is a Laurent polynomial.

The second root of
\chi_{A}(z,u)
is
\nu\coloneqq{\operatorname{Tr}}[A(u)]-\mu(u)
and
\mu(u)\nu(u)=\mbox{Det}[A(u)]\in R_{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
. Therefore,
\mu(u)
is invertible and there exist
c\in\mathbb{C}{{}^{\times}}
and
x\in{\Lambda}
such that

\displaystyle\mu(u)=cu^{x}\,.

(4.25)

Since
A\hat{\xi}
and

\hat{\xi}
have real coefficients, in fact
c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle A(u)\hat{\xi}(u)=cu^{x}\hat{\xi}(u)\,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Phi\xi=cT_{x}\xi,

(4.26)

with
0\neq\xi\in V
, so
\Phi
is not regular.

Conversely suppose that
\Phi
is not regular, so that

0\neq\xi\in V
,
c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}
, and
x\in{\Lambda}
satisfy (
4.26
). Uniform hyperbolicity excludes

|c|=1
. If
|c|<1
, then
\xi
belongs to the stable spectral subspace, so

0\neq\xi\in P_{-}\cap V
and
\Phi
is not everyday.

It remains to consider
|c|>1
. Put
\lambda(u)=cu^{x}
. Since
A
is symplectic,
\det[A(u^{-1})]\det[A(u)]=1
and since
A
is real,
|\det[A(u)]|=1
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
. Therefore, the other eigenvalue
\nu(u):=\frac{\det A(u)}{\lambda(u)}

has modulus
|c|^{-1}<1
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
. Choose a nonzero eigenvector

w\in K^{2}
for
\nu(u)
and clear denominators to obtain

0\neq\eta\in R_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
with
A(u)\eta=\nu(u)\eta
. Then

\eta\in P_{\mathbb{C},-}\cap V_{\mathbb{C}}
. By
(
4.23
),
P_{-}\cap V\neq\{0\}
, so
\Phi
is not
everyday. We have shown that non-everydayness and non-regularity are equivalent,
which proves the proposition.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

This proposition would fail if we considered the on-site Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{r})
with
r>1
. Namely, any uniformly hyperbolic everyday GQCA would be regular but not any uniformly hyperbolic regular GQCA would be everyday.

Remark 4.10

(Purely on-site GQCAs are not everyday)
.

Let
\Phi
be a translation-invariant Gaussian QCAs which is a tensor
product of identical on-site symplectic transformations. Equivalently,
its Laurent-polynomial symbol is constant:

\displaystyle A(\theta)=S,\qquad S\in\mathrm{Sp}(2,\mathbb{R}).

(4.27)

Assume that
\Phi
is uniformly hyperbolic, so that
S
has two eigenvalues
{\lambda}
and
1/{\lambda}
with
|{\lambda}|<1
. The stable Riesz projection for the
lattice system is then independent of
\theta
, and the global stable subspace is simply

\displaystyle P_{-}=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},E_{-})

(4.28)

where
E_{-}
is the eigenspace corresponding to eigenvalue
{\lambda}
. We have

\displaystyle V\cap P_{-}=c_{00}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d},E_{-})\neq\{0\}\,.

(4.29)

We find that a uniformly hyperbolic GQCAs which is merely a tensor product of
on-site symplectic transformations is never everyday.

5
Thermalization

In this section, we assemble the previous results into proofs of the main theorems.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Phi
be uniformly hyperbolic and everyday. Then,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\Phi^{k}\xi\|_{2}=\infty
exponentially fast for any nonzero
\xi\in V
.

Proof.

We decompose any
\xi\in V\subset P
as
\xi=\Pi_{-}\xi+\Pi_{+}\xi
. Suppose that there exists
0\neq\xi\in V
such that
\Pi_{+}\xi=0
. Then, we have that
V\ni\xi=\Pi_{-}\xi\in P_{-}
and
P_{-}\cap V\neq 0
. By negating this, we obtain that if
P_{-}\cap V=0
, then for any
0\neq\xi\in V
, we have
\Pi_{+}\xi\neq 0
.

Consider
\xi\in V
such that
\Pi_{+}\xi\neq 0
. Since
P_{+}
commutes with
\Phi
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi^{k}\Pi_{+}\xi\in P_{+}\qquad\text{for all }k\geqslant 0.

(5.1)

By uniform hyperbolicity, for every
0<\gamma<\delta
there exists

C_{\gamma}<\infty
such that

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{k}\Pi_{+}\xi\|_{2}\geqslant C_{\gamma}^{-1}e^{\gamma k}\|\Pi_{+}\xi\|_{2}\,.

(5.2)

Since the Riesz projection
\Pi_{+}
commutes with
\Phi
and it is a bounded operator,

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{k}\Pi_{+}\xi\|_{2}\leqslant\|\Pi_{+}\|\,\|\Phi^{k}\xi\|_{2}.

(5.3)

Combining the two inequalities gives

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{k}\xi\|_{2}\geqslant\frac{1}{\|\Pi_{+}\|}\|\Phi^{k}\Pi_{+}\xi\|_{2}\geqslant\frac{\|\Pi_{+}\xi\|_{2}}{C_{\gamma}\|\Pi_{+}\|}e^{\gamma k}.

(5.4)

∎

We conclude that when the stable subspace
P_{-}
of the Hilbert phase space
P
is maximally non-algebraic, i.e., when
P_{-}
contains no non-zero finitely supported labels, then
\Phi
drives the
\ell^{2}
-norm of any local label to infinity.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Phi
be locally hyperbolic and regular. Then,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\Phi^{k}\xi\|_{2}=\infty
exponentially fast for any nonzero
\xi\in V
.

Proof.

Let
0\neq\xi\in V
. Choose
\theta_{0}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
such that

A(\theta_{0})
is hyperbolic. After shrinking the neighborhood
U
of

\theta_{0}
, we may assume that
A(\theta)
is hyperbolic for all

\theta\in U
, and we denote the corresponding stable and unstable Riesz
projections by
\Pi_{-}^{U}(\theta)
and

\Pi_{+}^{U}(\theta)=I-\Pi_{-}^{U}(\theta)
.

We claim that
\Pi_{+}^{U}\widehat{\xi}
is not identically zero on
U
. Indeed, if

\Pi_{+}^{U}\widehat{\xi}\equiv 0
on
U
, then

\widehat{\xi}(\theta)\in\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{-}^{U}(\theta)
for

\theta\in U
. Since the fiber phase space is two-dimensional, this implies
that
A(\theta)\widehat{\xi}(\theta)
and
\widehat{\xi}(\theta)
are
proportional on
U
. Hence

\displaystyle\det\bigl(A(u)\widehat{\xi}(u),\widehat{\xi}(u)\bigr)

(5.5)

vanishes on a nonempty open subset of
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, and therefore vanishes
identically as a Laurent polynomial. Thus, over

K
, we have

\displaystyle A(u)\widehat{\xi}(u)=\mu(u)\widehat{\xi}(u)

(5.6)

for some
\mu\in K
. By the same algebraic argument as in the proof
of Proposition
4.8
,
\mu
is a unit of
R_{\mathbb{C}}
. Hence
\Phi\xi=cT_{x}\xi
for some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\times},x\in{\Lambda}
which contradicts regularity. This proves the claim.

Choose a compact set
B\subset U
of positive measure such that

\displaystyle g(\theta):=\Pi_{+}^{U}(\theta)\widehat{\xi}(\theta)

(5.7)

is nonzero in
L^{2}(B,\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Since
B
is compact and the fibers are
hyperbolic on
B
, the hyperbolicity over
B
is uniform. Therefore,
arguing as in Lemma
4.2
, for every sufficiently small

\gamma>0
there exists
C_{B,\gamma}<\infty
such that

\displaystyle\|A^{k}g\|_{L^{2}(B)}\geqslant C_{B,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\gamma k}\|g\|_{L^{2}(B)}.

(5.8)

Finally,
\Pi_{+}^{U}(\theta)
commutes with
A(\theta)
, and

\sup_{\theta\in B}\|\Pi_{+}^{U}(\theta)\|<\infty
. Hence

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{k}\xi\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|A^{k}\widehat{\xi}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}\geqslant\|A^{k}\widehat{\xi}\|_{L^{2}(B)}\geqslant\Bigl(\sup_{\theta\in B}\|\Pi_{+}^{U}(\theta)\|\Bigr)^{-1}\|\Pi_{+}^{U}A^{k}\widehat{\xi}\|_{L^{2}(B)}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sup_{\theta\in B}\|\Pi_{+}^{U}(\theta)\|\Bigr)^{-1}\|A^{k}g\|_{L^{2}(B)}\geqslant c_{\xi}e^{\gamma k},

(5.9)

with
c_{\xi}>0
. Thus
\|\Phi^{k}\xi\|_{2}\to\infty
exponentially fast.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
0\neq\xi\in V
, and set

\displaystyle\xi_{k}:=\Phi^{k}\xi,\qquad\Gamma_{k}:=\operatorname{supp}\xi_{k}.

(5.10)

The scalar phase in

\displaystyle\alpha^{k}(W(\xi))=e^{i\chi_{k}(\xi)}W(\xi_{k})

(5.11)

has modulus one, so it suffices to prove

\displaystyle\omega(W(\xi_{k}))\to 0.

(5.12)

By everydayness,
\Pi_{+}\xi\neq 0
. Choose
0<\gamma<\delta
.
Using the unstable estimate,

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{k}\Pi_{+}\xi\|_{2}\geqslant C_{\gamma}^{-1}e^{\gamma k}\|\Pi_{+}\xi\|_{2}.

(5.13)

Since
\Pi_{+}\Phi^{k}\xi=\Phi^{k}\Pi_{+}\xi
,

\displaystyle\|\xi_{k}\|_{2}\geqslant\frac{\|\Pi_{+}\xi\|_{2}}{C_{\gamma}\|\Pi_{+}\|}e^{\gamma k}=:c_{\xi}e^{\gamma k}.

(5.14)

Let
S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a finite spread set for
\Phi
. Then

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}\subset\operatorname{supp}\xi+kS,

(5.15)

so there is
C_{\xi}<\infty
such that

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{k}|\leqslant C_{\xi}(1+k)^{d}.

(5.16)

By Theorem
1.5
and Corollary
3.19
,

\displaystyle|\omega(W(\xi_{k}))|\leqslant\frac{\pi\sqrt{2\nu|\Gamma_{k}|+1}}{\|\xi_{k}\|_{2}}\leqslant\frac{\pi\sqrt{2\nu C_{\xi}(1+k)^{d}+1}}{c_{\xi}e^{\gamma k}}\longrightarrow 0.

(5.17)

This proves the theorem.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The proof is the same after we use
Lemma
5.2
.
∎

5.1
Examples

Let us consider a family of simple one-dimensional examples. Let
d=1
and
R=\mathbb{R}[u,u^{-1}]
and define a family of GQCAs by the following symplectic transformation

\displaystyle\Phi_{a}(u):=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
+1&t_{a}\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad t_{a}=a+u+u^{-1}\,.

(5.18)

We check that
\Phi_{a}(u^{-1})^{T}J\Phi_{a}(u)=J
,
so
\Phi\in\operatorname{Sp}(R,2)
.
The eigenvalues of
A(\theta)
are the roots of the characteristic polynomial
\chi_{a}(\theta,z)=z^{2}-z(a+2\cos\theta)+1
.
The fiber

A_{a}(\theta)
is hyperbolic exactly when

\displaystyle|t_{a}(\theta)|>2.

(5.19)

Then,

\displaystyle\Phi_{a}\text{ is uniformly hyperbolic}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad|a|>4.

(5.20)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\Phi_{a}\text{ is locally hyperbolic}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\neq 0.

(5.21)

We now check regularity. The characteristic polynomial is reducible over
K=\mathbb{C}(u)
if and only if its discriminant is a
square in
K
. The discriminant is

\displaystyle\Delta_{a}(u)

\displaystyle:=t_{a}(u)^{2}-4

(5.22)

\displaystyle=u^{-2}\bigl(u^{2}+(a-2)u+1\bigr)\bigl(u^{2}+(a+2)u+1\bigr).

(5.23)

The factor
u^{-2}
is a square in
K
. The two quadratic factors in
(
5.23
) are coprime, because their difference is

4u
and neither factor vanishes at
u=0
. Hence their product is a square
if and only if both quadratic factors are squares. Further, one checks

\displaystyle\chi_{a}(z,u)\text{ is reducible over }K\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a=0.

(5.24)

For
a=0
one has

\displaystyle\chi_{0}(z,u)=(z-u)(z-u^{-1})

(5.25)

and, for instance,

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}(u)\binom{1}{-u}=u\binom{1}{-u}\,,

(5.26)

so
\Phi_{0}
is not regular. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Phi_{a}\text{ is regular}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\neq 0.

(5.27)

By Proposition
4.8
, a uniformly hyperbolic
\Phi_{a}
is everyday iff it is regular. We conclude that
\Phi_{a}
is everyday for every
|a|>4
. In summary,
\Phi_{a}
is locally hyperbolic and regular for any
a\neq 0
and globally hyperbolic and everyday for any
a
such that
|a|>4
.

6
Discussion

We have proved thermalization for a class of translation-invariant Gaussian quantum cellular automata acting on bosonic lattice systems in infinite volume. The convergence is formulated on the algebraic local Weyl algebra: for every locally normal initial state with uniformly bounded particle density, the expectation of every nontrivial local Weyl operator converges
to zero under the dynamics.

We carried out the analysis of quantum many-body dynamical systems by transferring the problem to the classical phase space. In
3
this approach was dubbed QCA/CA (cellular automata) correspondence. In general, even classically, results about the dynamics on non-compact phase spaces are rare. In particular, the methods used in loc. cit. would not apply to our case. Nevertheless, the statement of our thermalization Theorem
1.2
is similar to Theorem 2.2 of
3
, and, in the classical case, to Theorem 3.1 of
4
.

Several natural questions remain. First, the everydayness condition is algebraic and checkable in examples, but a broader characterization of its prevalence in higher-dimensional parameter families of Laurent-polynomial symplectic maps would be desirable. Second, the proof yields no sharp thermalization rate beyond the bound obtained from the hyperbolicity gap and support growth; a tighter estimate would require more detailed information on the fiber dynamics and the initial state’s local number distribution. Third, it would be interesting to determine whether everydayness and hyperbolicity are stable under perturbations in the space of dynamics. Finally, the case when
\Phi
has eigenvalues on the unit circle presents a natural avenue toward understanding localization: in the absence of hyperbolicity, the space of Weyl operators may fragment in ways that obstruct thermalization, potentially giving rise to localized or partially ergodic behavior.
