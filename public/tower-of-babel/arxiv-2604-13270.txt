Constraints on Vector-Like Top Dipole Interactions from Top-Associated Photon Measurements at the LHC

Abstract

These results demonstrate that precision measurements of top-associated photon final states provide a powerful and complementary probe of vector-like quarks in scenarios where radiative decays dominate.

2
Theoretical and phenomenological framework

In this section the theoretical framework underlying the analysis is
presented. An effective field theory description of dipole interactions
between the vector-like top partner and SM fields is
introduced, and the corresponding decay widths of the heavy quark
T

are derived. Predictions for the signal rates in fiducial phase space
are then constructed, highlighting their dependence on the heavy-quark
mass and branching fractions.

2.1
Effective dipole interactions

This work investigates a simplified scenario where a vector-like top partner
T

(electric charge
+2/3
, color triplet,
SU(2)_{L}
singlet with hypercharge
Y=2/3
)
interacts with the SM primarily through effective dipole operators.
After integrating out heavier states, the leading interactions mediating radiative transitions between

T
and SM up-type quarks are
[
21
,
23
]

\mathcal{L}_{\rm EFT}\supset\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left[\frac{g^{\gamma}_{i}}{\Lambda}\,\bar{T}\,\sigma^{\mu\nu}u_{R,i}\,F_{\mu\nu}+\frac{g^{g}_{i}}{\Lambda}\,\bar{T}\,\sigma^{\mu\nu}T^{A}u_{R,i}\,G^{A}_{\mu\nu}+\text{h.c.}\right],

(2.1)

where
G^{A}_{\mu\nu}
and
F_{\mu\nu}
are the gluon and photon field-strength tensors, respectively.
The scale
\Lambda
denotes the characteristic mass of the UV degrees of freedom that generate these operators.

Flavor-changing neutral current processes, such as meson mixing, impose severe constraints on couplings to first- and
second-generation quarks
[
34
]
. We therefore follow the conventional approach in VLQ phenomenology
and retain only the third-generation couplings, defining
g_{t\gamma}\equiv g^{\gamma}_{i=3}
and
g_{tg}\equiv g^{g}_{i=3}
.
An analogous dipole operator for the
Z
boson is also possible after electroweak symmetry breaking, but we omit it here as we
focus on photonic and gluonic transitions. Phenomenologically, our setup is thus described by three independent
parameters: the Wilson coefficients
c_{t\gamma}\equiv g_{t\gamma}/\Lambda
and
c_{tg}\equiv g_{tg}/\Lambda
, and the top-partner mass
m_{T}
.

The operators in Eq. (
2.1
) are the lowest-dimension gauge-invariant interactions coupling the heavy quark
to SM field strengths. Their characteristic tensor structure,
\sigma^{\mu\nu}
, connects left- and right-handed chiralities.
Consequently, gauge-invariance before electroweak symmetry breaking requires the corresponding Wilson coefficients to
be proportional to a fermion mass, which in practice is supplied by
m_{T}
or
m_{t}
after symmetry breaking.
This explicit chirality flip is a sign of dipole moments and leads to amplitudes that
grow with energy, reflecting the higher-dimensional nature of the operator.
For decays, this translates into the characteristic
m_{T}^{3}
scaling of the partial widths presented below.

In this study, we focus on the radiative decays
T\to t\gamma
and
T\to tg
. These channels are probed indirectly
via precision measurements of top-associated photon production at the LHC. Specifically, single radiative decays
contribute to the
t\bar{t}\gamma
final state, whose differential cross sections have been measured by CMS.
The pair production of two radiatively-decaying top partners,
T\bar{T}\to t\bar{t}\gamma\gamma
, contributes to
the
t\bar{t}\gamma\gamma
final state, which is constrained by ATLAS measurements.
This framework allows us to interpret existing LHC data in terms of the parameters
c_{t\gamma}
,
c_{tg}
, and
m_{T}
.

2.2
Radiative decay widths from dipole operators

The dipole operators in Eq. (
2.1
) induce the two-body decays
T\to t\gamma
and
T\to tg
. At leading order, the partial widths are

\displaystyle\Gamma(T\to t\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{|c_{t\gamma}|^{2}}{8\pi}\,m_{T}^{3}\left(1-\frac{m_{t}^{2}}{m_{T}^{2}}\right)^{\!3},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Gamma(T\to tg)

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{F}|c_{tg}|^{2}}{8\pi}\,m_{T}^{3}\left(1-\frac{m_{t}^{2}}{m_{T}^{2}}\right)^{\!3},

(2.3)

where
C_{F}=4/3
is the quadratic Casimir invariant of the fundamental representation of
SU(3)
.
The strong dependence on
m_{T}^{3}
arises from the combination of the chirality-flipping operator,
which provides one power of the heavy mass, and the phase-space integral for a two-body decay,
which contributes two additional powers of momentum in the
m_{T}\gg m_{t}
limit.
In general, vector-like quarks can also decay through electroweak interactions to
Wb
,
Zt
, and
Ht
final states,
with rates governed by mixing angles with SM quarks
[
31
]
.
In our EFT scenario, we assume these mixing angles are sufficiently small such that dipole-induced decays dominate.
Under this dipole-dominance assumption, the total width is well approximated by the sum of the two radiative modes,
and the physical branching fractions are defined within the

\{T\to t\gamma,\,T\to tg\}
subspace as

\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}\equiv\text{BR}(T\to t\gamma)=\frac{\Gamma(T\to t\gamma)}{\Gamma(T\to t\gamma)+\Gamma(T\to tg)},\\
\mathcal{B}_{g}\equiv\text{BR}(T\to tg)=\frac{\Gamma(T\to tg)}{\Gamma(T\to t\gamma)+\Gamma(T\to tg)},

so that
\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}+\mathcal{B}_{g}=1
.
This defines the phenomenological regime we aim to probe: one where the heavy quark decays almost exclusively via radiative channels.
Using Eqs. (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), their ratio is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}}{\mathcal{B}_{g}}=\frac{1}{C_{F}}\frac{|c_{t\gamma}|^{2}}{|c_{tg}|^{2}}.

(2.4)

The color factor
C_{F}=4/3
enhances the gluonic channel; comparable branching fractions therefore
require
|c_{t\gamma}|\approx\sqrt{C_{F}}\,|c_{tg}|\approx 1.15\,|c_{tg}|
.
This relationship will be important when interpreting the experimental constraints on the two operators.

2.3
Signal rates and fiducial predictions

At hadron colliders, the dominant production mechanism for heavy
vector-like quarks is strong-interaction pair production,

pp\to T\bar{T},

which arises primarily from QCD interactions and depends mainly on the
heavy-quark mass and the partonic structure of the proton.
In the absence of additional interactions, the corresponding cross
section
\sigma_{\text{pair}}(m_{T})
is fully determined by QCD dynamics.
In the present EFT framework, however, the chromomagnetic dipole
operator introduces an additional interaction vertex connecting the
heavy quark, the top quark, and the gluon. As a result, pair production
also receives contributions from diagrams involving
t
-channel
top-quark exchange mediated by the dipole interaction, as well as
interference with the QCD amplitudes.
The total production rate can therefore depend weakly on the dipole
coupling
c_{tg}
.
The production and decay mechanisms of the vector-like top partner, including both QCD-driven processes and dipole-induced contributions, are illustrated in Fig.
1
.

Figure 1:

T
in the presence of dipole interactions.
(a) Pair production via gluon fusion,
gg\to T\bar{T}
, dominated by standard QCD interactions.
(b) Gluon-initiated production involving a
t
-channel exchange with insertions of the chromomagnetic dipole operator
c_{tg}
, leading to additional contributions beyond pure QCD.
(c) Pair production through quark–antiquark annihilation,
q\bar{q}\to T\bar{T}
.
(d) Dipole-induced decays of the heavy quark,
T\to tg
and
T\to t\gamma
, mediated by the chromomagnetic and electromagnetic operators, respectively.
While the production mechanisms arise from QCD interactions, diagram (b) highlights contributions from effective dipole operators as well.

In addition to the
s
-channel QCD-driven pair production shown in Fig.
1
(a,c), the process

gg\to T\bar{T}
receives contributions from
t
-channel diagrams, illustrated in Fig.
1
(b).
Two distinct
t
-channel topologies are present in our framework.
The first corresponds to the standard QCD diagram with heavy-
T

exchange, mediated by the usual
gT\bar{T}
vertex.
The second arises from the chromomagnetic dipole operator

c_{tg}\,\bar{T}\sigma^{\mu\nu}T^{A}t\,G^{A}_{\mu\nu}
and involves
the exchange of a top quark in the
t
-channel.
While the QCD amplitude is proportional to
g_{s}^{2}
and involves
vector interactions, the dipole-induced amplitude scales as

c_{tg}^{2}
and contains tensor structures of the form

\sigma^{\mu\nu}k_{\nu}
, leading to a stronger dependence on the
kinematic invariants.
In particular, at high energies the dipole contribution to the squared
matrix element exhibits the characteristic scaling

|\mathcal{M}|^{2}_{\rm dipole}\;\sim\;\frac{c_{tg}^{4}\,\hat{s}^{2}}{(t-m_{t}^{2})^{2}},

(2.5)

up to angular-dependent terms and mass corrections, reflecting the
momentum enhancement associated with the derivative nature of the
dipole operator.
The corresponding amplitudes can be written schematically as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{t}^{(T)}

\displaystyle\propto\frac{g_{s}^{2}}{t-m_{T}^{2}}\,\bar{u}(p_{1})\,\gamma^{\mu}(\not{q}_{t}+m_{T})\gamma^{\nu}\,v(p_{2})\,\epsilon_{\mu}(k_{1})\epsilon_{\nu}(k_{2}),

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{t}^{(t)}

\displaystyle\propto\frac{c_{tg}^{2}}{t-m_{t}^{2}}\,\bar{u}(p_{1})\,\sigma^{\mu\alpha}k_{1\alpha}(\not{q}_{t}+m_{t})\sigma^{\nu\beta}k_{2\beta}\,v(p_{2})\,\epsilon_{\mu}(k_{1})\epsilon_{\nu}(k_{2}),

(2.7)

where we define the momenta as

g(k_{1})\,g(k_{2})\to T(p_{1})\,\bar{T}(p_{2}),

with
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
the four-momenta of the incoming gluons, and

p_{1}
and
p_{2}
those of the outgoing heavy quark and antiquark,
respectively. The momentum flowing in the
t
-channel propagator is

q_{t}=p_{1}-k_{1}
, and the Mandelstam variable is

t=(k_{1}-p_{1})^{2}
.
Since both amplitudes share the same external state and color
structure, they interfere at tree level.
The total squared matrix element therefore contains three
contributions: the pure QCD term, the pure dipole-induced term,
and the interference term

2\,\mathrm{Re}\!\left(\mathcal{M}_{t}^{(T)}\,\mathcal{M}_{t}^{(t)\,*}\right),

(2.8)

which gives rise to a contribution proportional to

g_{s}^{2}\,c_{tg}^{2}
in the production cross section.
In this work,
these dipole-induced and interference contributions are fully
included in the event generation and can lead to non-negligible
modifications of both the total production rate and the kinematic
distributions.

Event generation is performed using the full matrix-element
structure, while the interpretation of signal rates retains an
approximately factorized description in terms of production,
branching fractions, and fiducial acceptance.
In this study, top-partner masses in the range

500\;\text{GeV}\leq m_{T}\leq 2\;\text{TeV}
are considered,
motivated by both the kinematic reach of current LHC measurements and
the mass scales at which dipole-induced decays become phenomenologically
accessible.
Once produced, each heavy quark decays via the dipole operators into a
top quark and a massless gauge boson,

T\to t\gamma\quad\text{or}\quad T\to tg,

with partial widths given in
Eqs. (
2.2
) and (
2.3
).
The two-body decay kinematics are governed by the mass hierarchy

m_{T}>m_{t}
, leading to a simple phase-space dependence encapsulated by
the factor
(1-m_{t}^{2}/m_{T}^{2})^{3}
.
For
m_{T}\gtrsim 500\;\text{GeV}
this factor is close to unity,
and the decay is essentially isotropic in the
T
rest frame.
The gluon or photon emitted in the decay is typically hard, carrying a
substantial fraction of the parent heavy-quark energy, which gives rise
to distinctive experimental signatures.
Depending on the decay channels of the two top partners, three distinct
final-state topologies arise from
T\bar{T}
production:

•

Diphoton channel:
Both heavy quarks decay radiatively,
T\bar{T}\to t\bar{t}\gamma\gamma
, yielding
two tops accompanied by two hard, isolated photons. This channel is characterized by the presence of two prompt photons
with large transverse momentum, arising from the electromagnetic dipole operator.

•

Single-photon channel:
One heavy quark decays to
t\gamma
and the other to
tg
, producing
t\bar{t}\gamma g

final states with a single hard photon and an additional hard jet originating from the gluon. The gluon fragments into a hadronic jet,
which can be distinguished from the photon through calorimetric and tracking information.

•

All-gluon channel:
Both heavy quarks decay to
tg
, giving
t\bar{t}gg
final states with no prompt photons.
This channel is not considered further in this analysis, as it is not constrained by the photon-based measurements we reinterpret.

The probabilities for each topology are determined solely by the dipole
branching fractions
\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}
and
\mathcal{B}_{g}
, which
satisfy
\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}+\mathcal{B}_{g}=1
under the assumption
that dipole decays dominate.
For a
T\bar{T}
event, the probability that exactly one of the two
heavy quarks decays radiatively is

f_{1\gamma}=2\,\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}\,\mathcal{B}_{g},

and the probability of two radiative decays is

f_{2\gamma}=(\mathcal{B}_{\gamma})^{2}.

The predicted fiducial signal cross section for a given final state can
be written approximately in factorized form as

\sigma_{\text{sig}}^{\rm fid}(m_{T};c_{t\gamma},c_{tg})=\sigma_{\text{prod}}(m_{T};c_{tg})\,f_{k\gamma}\,\times A^{\rm fid},

(2.9)

where
\sigma_{\text{prod}}
denotes the full pair-production cross
section including both QCD and dipole-induced contributions,
and
k=1,2
labels the single- or double-photon channel.
For the parameter range considered here, the dependence of

\sigma_{\text{prod}}
on
c_{tg}
is subleading but is retained in the
event generation.
The fiducial acceptance
A^{\rm fid}
denotes the probability that
a signal event satisfies the particle-level fiducial definition used in
the experimental analysis being reinterpreted.
For differential measurements, the predicted cross section in bin
i

of an observable
X
is

\sigma^{\rm fid}_{X,i}(m_{T};c_{t\gamma},c_{tg})=\sigma_{\text{prod}}(m_{T};c_{tg})\,f_{k\gamma}\,\times a^{\rm fid}_{X,i},

(2.10)

where
a^{\rm fid}_{X,i}
denotes the fraction of fiducial events
in bin
i
which varies with the mass of vector like top.

3
Experimental observables and statistical methodology

This section describes the reinterpretation of precision LHC measurements
used to constrain dipole interactions of a vector-like top partner.
Two complementary observables are considered: unfolded differential
cross sections for
t\bar{t}+\gamma
production
[
32
]
and the inclusive
fiducial cross section for
t\bar{t}+\gamma\gamma

[
33
]
, reported at
particle level by the CMS and ATLAS collaborations, respectively.
Rather than performing a dedicated resonance search, theoretical
predictions for the signal contribution are compared directly with
these measured fiducial cross sections.
Sensitivity arises both from kinematic distortions in differential
distributions and from rate modifications in inclusive measurements,
without requiring explicit reconstruction of the heavy
T
resonance.

3.1
Simulation and event generation

Signal events are generated using
MadGraph5_aMC@NLO

v3.5.5
[
35
]
, where the effective dipole operators
from
Equation

2.1
are implemented at the matrix-element
level via
FeynRules

[
36
]
and exported in UFO
format
[
37
]
.
The NNPDF23
[
38
]
parton distribution functions are used.
Pair production of the heavy quark,

pp\to T\bar{T}
, is simulated through QCD interactions at leading
order, followed by dipole-induced decays
T\to t\gamma
and
T\to tg
.

Parton-level events are interfaced to
Pythia 8.3

[
39
]
for parton showering, hadronization,
and particle decays.
Stable particles are defined according to the particle-level
definitions of the experimental measurement:
leptons are considered after final-state radiation, photons after QED
final-state radiation, and jets are clustered using the anti-
k_{T}

algorithm with radius parameter
R=0.4
implemented in

FastJet

[
40
]
.

3.2
Single-photon channel: differential distributions

The analysis employs unfolded differential fiducial cross sections
measured by the CMS Collaboration in Ref.
[
32
]
,
presented as functions of the photon transverse momentum
p_{T}^{\gamma}

and the azimuthal separation between the two leptons,

\Delta\phi_{\ell\ell}
.
These observables are particularly sensitive to heavy states that
decay radiatively through
T\to t\gamma
, which produce energetic
photons and modify dilepton kinematic correlations.

The fiducial region defined by the CMS
t\bar{t}+\gamma
measurement
is inclusive in jet multiplicity, requiring at least one
b
jet
but imposing no upper bound on the total number of jets.
The single-radiative signal topology

pp\to T\bar{T}\to(t\gamma)(\bar{t}g)

with dileptonic top decays produces final states containing
two
b
jets and at least one additional jet from the gluon,
and therefore populates a subset of this inclusive fiducial region.
Since the CMS measurement is integrated over all allowed jet
multiplicities, the signal contributes directly to the measured
fiducial cross section and differential distributions.

The unfolded measurements provide per-bin cross sections together with
full information on statistical and systematic uncertainties and
their correlations.
The particle-level fiducial phase space is defined by the following
requirements
[
32
]
:

•

Two oppositely charged leptons (
e
or
\mu
) with

p_{T,\ell_{1}}>25\;\text{GeV}
,
p_{T,\ell_{2}}>15\;\text{GeV}
,

|\eta_{\ell}|<2.4
, and dilepton invariant mass

m_{\ell\ell}>20\;\text{GeV}
;

•

At least one jet with
p_{T,j}>30\;\text{GeV}
,
|\eta_{j}|<2.4
,
matched to a
b
-hadron within
\Delta R<0.4
;

•

One isolated photon with
p_{T}^{\gamma}>20\;\text{GeV}
and
|\eta_{\gamma}|<1.44
, required to be separated from any other stable particle with
p_{T}>5\;\text{GeV}
(except neutrinos) by
\Delta R>0.1
;

•

Angular separation
\Delta R(\ell,\gamma)>0.4
,

\Delta R(j,\gamma)>0.1
,
\Delta R(\ell,j)>0.4
.

The photon transverse momentum distribution provides a direct probe of
the energy scale associated with the dipole interaction.
Photons produced in the decay
T\to t\gamma
, mediated by the
electromagnetic dipole operator

c_{t\gamma}\,\bar{T}\sigma^{\mu\nu}tF_{\mu\nu}
,
exhibit a characteristically harder spectrum than photons produced in
SM
t\bar{t}\gamma
production.

In the SM, photons arise predominantly from initial-state
radiation or final-state radiation off top quarks and their decay
products. These contributions are infrared-enhanced and populate
mainly the low-
p_{T}
region.
In contrast, the dipole-induced decay of a heavy resonance releases a
large fixed energy.
In the rest frame of the heavy quark, the two-body decay

T\to t\gamma
produces a monochromatic photon with energy

E_{\gamma}=\frac{m_{T}^{2}-m_{t}^{2}}{2m_{T}}\approx\frac{m_{T}}{2}\quad(m_{T}\gg m_{t}).

After boosting to the laboratory frame, this results in a photon
transverse-momentum spectrum extending to large values,
producing a characteristic enhancement at high
p_{T}^{\gamma}
relative
to the SM expectation.
The left panel of Fig.
2

illustrates the photon
p_{T}
distribution for signal events with

m_{T}=500~\text{GeV}
and
1000~\text{GeV}
, assuming a representative
coupling
c_{t\gamma}=1~\text{TeV}^{-1}
, chosen for illustration purposes.

The azimuthal separation between the two leptons,

\Delta\phi_{\ell\ell}
, provides complementary sensitivity to the
kinematic structure of the event.
In SM
t\bar{t}\gamma
production, the dilepton system
originates from the decays of a top–antitop pair produced close to
threshold, with moderate transverse momentum.
The decay products therefore retain strong angular correlations,
and the resulting
\Delta\phi_{\ell\ell}
distribution exhibits a
pronounced slope reflecting the underlying production and decay
kinematics.
In contrast, when the dilepton system originates from the decay of a
heavy vector-like top partner, the kinematics are substantially
different.
Pair-produced heavy quarks decay into highly energetic top quarks,
which are typically produced with significant Lorentz boost in the
laboratory frame.
The leptons from the subsequent top decays are therefore emitted in
collimated configurations aligned with the boosted top directions.
This boost reduces the sensitivity of the dilepton system to the
original production geometry and leads to a partial loss of angular
correlations.
As a result, the
\Delta\phi_{\ell\ell}
distribution becomes
progressively flatter compared to the SM prediction.
The degree of flattening increases with the heavy-quark mass.
Larger values of
m_{T}
produce more strongly boosted top quarks,
enhancing the kinematic decorrelation between the leptons and further
suppressing the slope of the
\Delta\phi_{\ell\ell}
spectrum.
This behaviour provides an additional handle to distinguish heavy
resonance decays from SM radiation processes.

3.2.1
Statistical procedure

Signal events are analyzed within the fiducial phase space defined in

Section

3.2
. For each mass point, the fiducial acceptance

A^{\text{fid}}(m_{T})
and the bin fractions
a^{\text{fid}}_{X,i}(m_{T})
are
determined from Monte Carlo simulated events. The acceptance exhibits a
mild increase with
m_{T}
, reflecting the harder kinematics of the
decay products at larger masses. Statistical uncertainties from
finite Monte Carlo samples are found to be negligible compared to
experimental uncertainties.
The comparison with data is performed using the full covariance
matrices provided by the experimental measurements.
The chi-square statistic is constructed by combining all bins of the

p_{T,\gamma}
and
\Delta\phi_{\ell\ell}
distributions.
For a given parameter point
(m_{T},c_{t\gamma},c_{tg})
, it is defined as

\chi^{2}(m_{T},c_{t\gamma},c_{tg})=\sum_{i,j}\left(\sigma_{i}^{\rm data}-\sigma_{i}^{\rm pred}\right)V^{-1}_{ij}\left(\sigma_{j}^{\rm data}-\sigma_{j}^{\rm pred}\right),

(3.1)

where the indices
i,j
run over all bins of both observables, and

V_{ij}
includes statistical and systematic uncertainties with their
bin-to-bin correlations.
Exclusion regions are obtained by scanning the parameter space and
identifying contours corresponding to the
95\%
CL,
defined by the appropriate
\Delta\chi^{2}
threshold for the number of
parameters of interest.

3.3
Diphoton Channel

While differential
t\bar{t}+\gamma
spectra probe configurations with a
single radiative decay, events in which both heavy quarks decay through

T\to t\gamma
produce final states containing two energetic photons.
These contribute directly to the inclusive
t\bar{t}\gamma\gamma

fiducial cross section and therefore provide an independent probe of
the electromagnetic dipole interaction.
Because the rate of such events scales with the square of the radiative
branching fraction, the diphoton channel is particularly sensitive in
scenarios where
T\to t\gamma
is sizable or dominant.

3.3.1
The
t\bar{t}\gamma\gamma
measurement

The ATLAS Collaboration has measured the fiducial cross section for

t\bar{t}\gamma\gamma
production
[
33
]
,

\sigma^{\text{fid}}(pp\to t\bar{t}\gamma\gamma)=2.42^{+0.58}_{-0.53}\;\text{fb},

(3.2)

in
pp
collisions at
\sqrt{s}=13\;\text{TeV}
with an integrated
luminosity of
140\;\text{fb}^{-1}
.
The measurement is characterized by a total relative uncertainty of
approximately
23\%
, dominated by statistical fluctuations in the
data at the level of about
17\%
. Systematic uncertainties contribute
around
15\%
and are driven primarily by experimental effects related
to photon and jet reconstruction, the estimation of fake-photon
backgrounds, and the normalization of additional
\gamma\gamma

background processes.
The measurement is performed at particle level in a fiducial phase
space defined by the ATLAS analysis
[
33
]
, which closely
follows the reconstruction-level event selection.
A detailed definition can be found in Ref.
[
33
]
; here we
summarize the main features relevant for the present reinterpretation.
Photons are required to be prompt (not originating from hadron decays),
with transverse momentum
p_{T}^{\gamma}>20~\text{GeV}
and pseudorapidity

|\eta_{\gamma}|<2.37
, and must satisfy an isolation requirement based on
the scalar sum of nearby charged-particle transverse momenta.
Electrons and muons are dressed with nearby photons and must be prompt.
Jets are reconstructed with the anti-
k_{T}
algorithm with radius
parameter
R=0.4
, and are identified as
b
-jets through ghost
matching to
b
-hadrons.
Selected leptons and jets are required to satisfy

p_{T}>25~\text{GeV}
and
|\eta|<2.5
, with overlap removal applied between
photons, leptons, and jets.
Angular separations of
\Delta R>0.4
are required between jets and leptons, jets and photons, and leptons and photons.
The fiducial event selection requires exactly two isolated photons,
exactly one charged lepton, and at least four jets, of which at least
one must be
b
-tagged. Events containing additional leptons with

p_{T}>7~\text{GeV}
are rejected.

The observed result is consistent with the SM prediction
based on the nominal
t\bar{t}\gamma\gamma
simulation obtained with

MadGraph5_aMC@NLO
interfaced to
Pythia 8
,
which yields
[
33
]
:

\sigma^{\text{fid}}_{\rm SM}=1.53^{+0.40}_{-0.52}\,(\text{scale})^{+0.05}_{-0.03}\,(\text{PDF})\;\text{fb}.

Given the experimental uncertainty, the measurement remains compatible
with this prediction and therefore allows room for additional
contributions from heavy-particle decays.
Because the diphoton rate depends quadratically on the radiative
branching fraction, this observable provides complementary sensitivity
to the single-photon channel.

3.3.2
Signal prediction and acceptance

In the present framework, the diphoton final state arises from pair
production of heavy top partners followed by radiative decays,

pp\to T\bar{T}\to(t\gamma)(\bar{t}\gamma).

As discussed previously, the corresponding fiducial signal cross section can be written as

\sigma^{\text{fid}}_{t\bar{t}\gamma\gamma}(m_{T};c_{t\gamma},c_{tg})=\sigma_{\rm prod}(m_{T};c_{tg})\times\,\big[\text{BR}(T\to t\gamma)\big]^{2}\times\,A^{\text{fid}}_{\gamma\gamma},

(3.3)

where
\sigma_{\rm prod}
denotes the full pair-production cross section,
including QCD and dipole-induced contributions as discussed in

Section

2.3
, and

A^{\text{fid}}_{\gamma\gamma}
is the particle-level fiducial acceptance.
The production rate can be expressed schematically as

\sigma_{\rm prod}(m_{T};c_{tg})=\sigma_{\rm QCD}(m_{T})+c_{tg}^{2}\,\sigma_{\rm int}(m_{T})+c_{tg}^{4}\,\sigma_{\rm dip}(m_{T}),

(3.4)

reflecting the pure QCD contribution, interference terms
(\sigma_{\rm int})
, and
dipole-induced
(\sigma_{\rm dip})
components.
The fiducial acceptance is evaluated using the simulation chain
described in
Section

3.1
, applying the ATLAS particle-level
selection.
It increases moderately with
m_{T}
, ranging from approximately

1.2\%
at
m_{T}=500\;\text{GeV}
to
9.6\%
at
m_{T}=2.0\;\text{TeV}
.
The smaller acceptance compared to the single-photon channel arises
from the requirement of two isolated photons.

3.3.3
Likelihood contribution

The measured fiducial cross section is incorporated as a constraint on the predicted total rate, accounting for both experimental and theoretical uncertainties. For a given parameter point, the predicted cross section is

\sigma^{\text{fid}}_{\rm pred}=\sigma^{\text{fid}}_{\rm SM}+\sigma^{\text{fid}}_{\rm signal}(m_{T};c_{t\gamma},c_{tg}).

The chi-square statistic is defined as

\chi^{2}_{\gamma\gamma}(m_{T};c_{t\gamma},c_{tg})=\frac{\big(\sigma^{\text{fid}}_{\rm pred}-\sigma^{\text{fid}}_{\rm obs}\big)^{2}}{(\Delta\sigma_{\rm exp})^{2}+(\Delta\sigma_{\rm SM})^{2}},

(3.5)

where
\sigma^{\text{fid}}_{\rm obs}=2.42\;\text{fb}
and

\Delta\sigma_{\rm exp}=0.58\;\text{fb}
is obtained from a symmetric
combination of the quoted experimental uncertainties.
The SM prediction and its theoretical uncertainty,

\sigma^{\text{fid}}_{\rm SM}=1.53^{+0.40}_{-0.52}\,(\text{scale})^{+0.05}_{-0.03}\,(\text{PDF})\;\text{fb}
,
are taken from Ref.
[
33
]
, and the scale and PDF
uncertainties are combined in quadrature to obtain

\Delta\sigma_{\rm SM}
.

4
Results

The exclusion limits obtained in the
(c_{t\gamma},c_{tg})
plane are
shown in Fig.
3
for representative masses

m_{T}=500,750,1000,1250,1500,1750,
and
2000~\text{GeV}
. The left panel displays the
constraints derived from the unfolded
t\bar{t}\gamma
differential
measurement, while the right panel shows the bounds obtained from the
inclusive
t\bar{t}\gamma\gamma
fiducial cross section. In both cases,
the dashed lines correspond to fixed values of

\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}
, providing a direct map
between the coupling plane and the physical decay pattern of the
vector-like top partner.

In the scenario considered here, where only the two dipole-induced
channels
T\to t\gamma
and
T\to tg
are present, the branching
fractions depend only on the ratio of effective couplings,

\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}=\frac{|c_{t\gamma}|^{2}}{|c_{t\gamma}|^{2}+C_{F}|c_{tg}|^{2}},\qquad\mathcal{B}_{g}=\frac{C_{F}|c_{tg}|^{2}}{|c_{t\gamma}|^{2}+C_{F}|c_{tg}|^{2}},

(4.1)

with
C_{F}=4/3
. As a result, the branching fractions depend only on ratios of effective couplings
and are therefore independent of both the heavy-quark mass
m_{T}
and
the EFT normalization scale
\Lambda
, and the
exclusion contours can be directly interpreted in terms of physical
decay scenarios.

A first important feature of Fig.
3
is the
interplay between the two measurements across the
(c_{t\gamma},c_{tg})

parameter space. The
t\bar{t}\gamma
channel exhibits the strongest
sensitivity over a broad range of couplings, particularly along directions
corresponding to mixed decay topologies, where one top partner decays via

T\to t\gamma
and the other via
T\to tg
. In this regime, the signal rate
scales as
2\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}\mathcal{B}_{g}
, and the sensitivity is driven
by the presence of a hard photon with characteristic energy

E_{\gamma}\simeq m_{T}/2
, which enhances the high-
p_{T}^{\gamma}
tail and
modifies angular observables such as
\Delta\phi_{\ell\ell}
.
In contrast, the
t\bar{t}\gamma\gamma
channel probes configurations where
both top partners decay radiatively, with a rate proportional to

\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}^{2}
. As a consequence, it is suppressed in the
gluon-dominated regime (
\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}\ll 1
) considered here, as
visible in the displacement of its exclusion contours toward larger values
of the effective couplings. Nevertheless, the diphoton channel provides a
complementary constraint, probing regions of parameter space characterized
by enhanced electromagnetic dipole interactions and distinct kinematic
features with reduced background contamination.

From a theoretical perspective, these limits constrain the hierarchy
between the electromagnetic and chromomagnetic dipole operators.
In the gluon-dominated benchmark considered here,

\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}=0.1
implies

|c_{t\gamma}|^{2}\ll C_{F}|c_{tg}|^{2}
, leading to a parameter space
where the chromomagnetic interaction governs both the production
rate and decay pattern of the vector-like quark.
The results therefore probe a regime in which the sensitivity to

c_{tg}
is driven indirectly through the fixed branching-fraction
relation, while
c_{t\gamma}
is tightly constrained by the

t\bar{t}\gamma
observable. In particular, the strong bounds on

c_{t\gamma}
at low masses reflect the high sensitivity of the
single-photon channel, even when the electromagnetic branching
fraction is suppressed.

Overall, the data disfavour scenarios with enhanced electromagnetic
dipole contributions relative to the gluonic interaction and impose
stringent constraints on the allowed hierarchy between the two
operators across the full mass range considered.
It is important to note that the limits presented in the

(c_{t\gamma},c_{tg})
plane are expressed in terms of the effective
couplings entering the low-energy description. While this
parametrization is directly constrained by the data, not all points
shown in the exclusion contours necessarily lie within the strict
domain of validity of the truncated effective field theory expansion
[
41
]
.
In particular, for large values of the couplings and high masses, the
combination
Q\,\sqrt{c_{tg}^{2}+c_{t\gamma}^{2}}
, with
Q=2m_{T}
, can
approach or exceed unity, indicating that higher-dimensional operators
may no longer be parametrically suppressed.
The outermost regions of the exclusion contours should therefore be
interpreted with caution from an EFT perspective. By contrast, the
branching-fraction interpretation remains robust, as it depends only
on ratios of effective couplings and is insensitive to the overall
normalization of the EFT expansion.

5
Conclusions

In this work, we have derived the first dedicated constraints on
electromagnetic and chromomagnetic dipole interactions of a
vector-like top partner using precision measurements of
top-associated photon production at the LHC. By reinterpreting
unfolded differential cross sections in
t\bar{t}\gamma
production
and the fiducial
t\bar{t}\gamma\gamma
cross section, we have
constrained the effective operators
c_{t\gamma}
and
c_{tg}
in a
model-independent way, without relying on explicit resonance
reconstruction.

The analysis exploits the complementarity between the single-photon
and diphoton final states. The
t\bar{t}\gamma
channel is primarily
sensitive to configurations with one radiative decay, scaling as

2\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}\mathcal{B}_{g}
, while the

t\bar{t}\gamma\gamma
channel probes double-radiative decays,
scaling as
\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}^{2}
. Their combination significantly reduces the
degeneracy between electromagnetic and chromomagnetic dipole
interactions and allows a direct interpretation in terms of physical
branching fractions.

Quantitatively, for
m_{T}=500~\text{GeV}
, the analysis reaches sensitivity
to effective couplings as small as

c_{t\gamma}\simeq 0.005~\text{TeV}^{-1}
in the gluon-dominated benchmark

\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}=0.1
, while the corresponding limit from the

t\bar{t}\gamma\gamma
channel is significantly weaker,

c_{t\gamma}\simeq 0.40~\text{TeV}^{-1}
. The sensitivity decreases with
increasing mass, but remains significant up to
m_{T}=2~\text{TeV}
, where
couplings of
\mathcal{O}(1)~\text{TeV}^{-1}
are still probed.
Across the full mass range, the
t\bar{t}\gamma
channel provides the
dominant sensitivity, driven by its larger event yield and stronger
kinematic reach, even in the regime where the electromagnetic branching
fraction is suppressed. The
t\bar{t}\gamma\gamma
channel remains less
sensitive due to the reduced rate of two-photon final states, although it
still offers a complementary probe of the underlying dipole interaction.

A key implication of this analysis is that the constraints probe
directly the relative strength of electromagnetic and chromomagnetic
dipole interactions, independently of the EFT scale. The results
therefore disfavour scenarios in which the electromagnetic dipole is
strongly enhanced with respect to the chromomagnetic one,
particularly at low masses, while configurations with comparable
dipole strengths remain viable over a wider region of parameter
space.

More broadly, this work demonstrates the power of precision
measurements to probe heavy new physics indirectly. Unlike
traditional searches for vector-like quarks, which rely on specific
decay modes such as
T\to Wb
,
Zt
, or
Ht
, the approach presented
here is sensitive to radiative decay channels that are typically not
targeted by dedicated searches. It therefore provides access to
complementary regions of parameter space in which dipole
interactions dominate.

The methodology developed in this work can be extended in several
directions, including the use of additional differential observables,
the incorporation of higher-order corrections, and the combination
with other precision measurements such as
t\bar{t}Z
or multi-top
production. With the increasing precision of LHC data and the
availability of unfolded spectra with full covariance information,
precision observables will continue to play an essential role in
probing the structure of possible ultraviolet completions.

In conclusion, top-associated photon measurements provide a powerful
and complementary probe of vector-like top partners with dipole
interactions, extending the sensitivity of the LHC beyond
conventional search strategies and offering new insight into the
structure of heavy new physics.
