A case of early diagnosis of alobar holoprosencephaly from Pakistan: importance of prompt prenatal imaging
Authors: Romesa Qazi, Ayesha Liaquat, Qurat Ul Ain Qazi, Bushra Tariq, Shajie Ur Rehman Usmani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHoloprosencephaly (HPE) is a rare congenital condition encompassing incomplete midline cleavage of the prosencephalon and associated craniofacial abnormalities.Case presentationWe report a rare case of a 34-year-old Memon woman, gravida 3 para 2, with no known comorbidities or exposure to teratogens, who presented for her 12-week scan which revealed a single alive fetus having an absent falx cerebri with fused thalami, a single large posterior ventricle, and facial anomalies suggestive of alobar HPE. A follow-up ultrasound showed abnormally fused portions of the brain and a high amniotic fluid index (AFI), indicating polyhydramnios. The fetus had intrauterine death at 7 months of gestation.ConclusionThis case highlights the importance of early prenatal diagnosis of alobar HPE, the most severe form, using transabdominal ultrasonography (USG) and transvaginal USG. Prompt prenatal imaging is crucial for early diagnosis of alobar HPE, enhancing antenatal care, and facilitating improved family planning strategies to alleviate potential psychological distress due to its poor prognosis. Our documentation of such a rare condition is essential to help create a comprehensive database of similar cases.

Introduction

Holoprosencephaly (HPE) is a rare congenital condition marked by the incomplete midline cleavage of the prosencephalon and associated craniofacial abnormalities [1]. It can be divided into four categories based on increasing cortical separation and decreasing severity: alobar, semi lobar, lobar, and middle interhemispheric variant (MIH) [2]. The etiology of HPE is varied, encompassing both genetic and environmental causes, with its incidence being approximately 1 in 250 fetuses and 1 in 16,000 live births [3]. Postnatal mortality is significantly high and the 1-year survival ranges from 15 to 54% for various forms of holoprosencephaly. Diagnostic modalities include ultrasound as the initial screening tool, with MRI used for more detailed evaluation when necessary [4]. Although studies have reported a high incidence of HPE among Pakistani individuals in US-based populations [5], there is limited medical literature on HPE in Pakistan. In this regard, we present a rare case of very early prenatal diagnosis of alobar HPE using transabdominal ultrasonography (USG) and transvaginal USG from Karachi, Pakistan. The purpose of this study is to highlight the important role of early ultrasonography in detection of fetal anomalies to allow better family planning, discuss the diagnostic challenges of differentiating HPE from other conditions and elucidate potential risk factors for the condition.

Case presentation

We report the case of a 12-week-old fetus of a 34-year-old gravida 3 para 2 Memon female that was identified on a routine prenatal check-up. The mother had no known comorbidities, addictions, or exposure to teratogens. On examination, she was underweight (BMI = 14.8 kg/m2) and anemic (Hb = 8.4 mg/dL). She took folic acid supplementation for the first 6 weeks of gestation. She denied any consanguinity or family history of congenital malformations and mental retardation. She tested negative for TORCH infections.

The initial scan was performed at 6 weeks of gestation, confirming a singleton pregnancy. The second scan was done at 11 + 2 weeks. Since the initial scan was performed using less advanced equipment, a repeat scan was done at 12 weeks. The repeat scan revealed a live singleton fetus having an absent falx cerebri with fused thalami and a single large posterior ventricle with no detectable lateral and third ventricles, midline structures, corpus callosum, interhemispheric fissure or cavum septum pellucidum (Figs. 1, 2). It also had facial anomalies suggestive of alobar HPE. The growth and development of the rest of the body appeared normal with satisfactory fetal movements. The parents were counseled accordingly. The fourth ultrasound done at 23 + 0 weeks showed abnormal fused portions of the brain with otherwise normal growth parameters. However, the amniotic fluid index (AFI) was high, indicating polyhydramnios. The fetus experienced intrauterine demise at 7 months of gestation. It was subsequently delivered via a cesarean section. On examination, fetal size was consistent with gestational age. The calvarium was fully developed, with continuous serous fluid discharging from an opening near the skull base. The parents declined further autopsy or genetic evaluation.

Discussion

HPE is a rare congenital craniofacial disorder marked by the partial or complete failure of prosencephalon cleavage. The neural tube divides into three primary brain vesicles by the fourth week of gestation, with further division of the prosencephalon into the diencephalon and telencephalon occurring in the fifth week. The prechordal mesoderm, located beneath the cephalic end of the notochord, contributes to the formation of midline facial structures; therefore, HPE is usually associated with mid-facial structural abnormalities, reiterating DeMeyer's well-known statement: 'The face predicts the brain [7, 8].' Anomalies associated with HPE range from severe forms such as anophthalmia, cyclopia, and proboscis to milder defects like cleft lip and hypotelorism [9]. The varying etiology of HPE encompasses both environmental and genetic factors. Trisomy 13, 18, and triploidy are the chromosomal aberrations associated with HPE [10] whereas its environmental causes include maternal diabetes and alcohol consumption, and prenatal exposure to retinoic acid, with approximately 1% of infants born to diabetic mothers affected by the disorder [11]. In our case, the mother did not drink or smoke; however, she did not take her folic acid supplements for the recommended period of 12 weeks, which has been described as a risk factor for alobar HPE.

Alobar HPE is the most severe of the three classic types and occurs due to the complete failure of prosencephalon cleavage leading to a posteriorly located mono-ventricle and fused thalami along with the absence of the falx, interhemispheric fissure, corpus callosum, optic tracts, olfactory bulbs, and the septum pellucidum [12]. Some of these findings such as absent septum pellucidum closely resemble that of hydrocephalus and hydranencephaly (HE). However, there are certain differentiating features between HPE and these two conditions, which can be identified through careful sonographic examination of the cranial anatomy. Hydrocephalus is characterized by the abnormal accumulation of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) in the brain, leading to ventricular dilation [13]. Unlike HPE, in hydrocephalus, thalami and anterior horns of ventricles are not fused, midline falx is complete, and the lateral ventricles are prominent [14]. HE is a rare congenital condition where the cerebral hemispheres are replaced by a membranous sac filled with CSF and necrotic debris [15]. In HE, unlike HPE, midline structures such as the thalami, falx cerebri, corpus callosum, and interhemispheric fissure are unaffected and are distinctly present [12]. Additionally, the microcephaly and facial malformations, which are seen in HPE, are absent in hydranencephaly [16]. Since HE follows fetal internal carotid arteries occlusion [17], presence of a rim of cortical tissue and intact internal carotid arteries and circle of Willis helps exclude HE [14].

Our case is noteworthy because it describes a rare case of alobar HPE from Pakistan where there exists a gap in the literature regarding the prevalence of HPE despite the extensive documentation of its high prevalence in Pakistanis based in the US. To the best of our knowledge, there is only a handful of other published Pakistani cases of alobar HPE [18, 19]. However, this does not mean that the incidence of the disease is low in the region. Low prenatal diagnosis and abortion rates—reasons for the high prevalence of HPE in US-based Pakistanis [5] and high consanguineous marriage rates make Pakistan a hotspot for genetic abnormalities [20]. This case is also remarkable as the diagnosis was made very early on in the pregnancy; that is, during the 12th week of gestation; to the best of our knowledge, the earliest diagnosis of alobar HPE was reported at the 9th week of gestation [21]. Early diagnosis, particularly before week 20 of gestation, is crucial as it opens the window for pregnancy termination and saves the family from the psychological distress of bearing a stillborn child with severe malformations [8]. The family, in our case, was counseled for early termination of pregnancy, but they refused on religious grounds. The result was a stillborn. Early differentiation of HPE from other intracranial abnormalities allows for a better and targeted approach toward its management as the prognosis, prenatal counseling, and treatment vary for HPE, hydrocephalus, and HE [22]. The prognosis for alobar HPE is generally poor, with neonates who have severe facial anomalies rarely surviving after birth. In some cases, those with milder facial malformations may live for a few months or years, though survival into adolescence is extremely rare [23]. The management is primarily supportive and requires a timely multidisciplinary approach to address the various medical challenges associated with the condition [24].

In addition, the family also refused autopsy and genetic evaluation of the child. Genetic evaluation is vital to check whether a chromosomal anomaly or aberration has led to a case of structural abnormality such as HPE. Because holoprosencephaly in most cases is associated with chromosomal and genetic abnormalities, karyotyping and microarrays are the most used methods used for genetic screening [25]. At least 13 genes associated with HPE exist [26], and a particular HPE mutation may confer up to a 20% recurrence risk in a seemingly healthy family [27]. Further, HPE may also be syndromic, associated with renowned syndromes such as Kallman syndrome and Smith–Lemli–Opitz syndrome [25]. Hence, genetic evaluation is vital for appropriate prenatal counseling and planning and management of future pregnancies. Similarly, the 18th–22nd week scan, which analyzes any structural development and growth defects and is widely known as a morphology or fetal anomaly scan [22], was also not performed due to financial constraints. Consequently, the status of the organs could not be assessed which could have led to greater information regarding the specific syndrome of the affected fetus based on any organ malformations [28]. This scan is also popularly used to identify fetal comorbidities associated with diabetes mellitus (DM), most common teratogen for HPE [25]. Since none of these assessments were done, the etiology of our case could not be specified.

Conclusion

Prenatal transvaginal ultrasonography plays a key role in early detection of HPE, allowing for informed family planning and better antenatal care. Our research is an incidence of correct differentiation of alobar HPE from hydrocephalus, 2 conditions which often pose a diagnostic dilemma. Records of such cases provide a useful database for future clinical practice.
