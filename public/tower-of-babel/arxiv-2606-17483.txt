Abstract
. We study the Dirichlet problem for the p p -Poisson equation in the metric measure space setting equipped with a doubling measure and supporting a ( p , p ) (p,p) -Poincaré inequality. We prove the existence of the solutions by using a variational approach. We prove the stability and uniqueness of the solutions, when the space is also geodesic.



Key words and phrases
:
p
-Poisson equation, Dirichlet problem, metric measure space, minimizer, existence, stability, Cheeger’s gradient, upper gradient.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 30L99, 35B35, 46E36.

1.
Introduction

In the Euclidean setting, for a function
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
defined in an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
p
-Laplacian is defined as

\Delta_{p}u:=\text{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u).

Given a function
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
the equation

-\Delta_{p}u=f\text{ in }\Omega

is called the
p
-Poisson equation with source term
f
.
The problem of our interest is the Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Poisson equation

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u

\displaystyle=f\text{ in }\Omega,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=g\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(1.1b)

where
g:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
describes the boundary conditions.
The case
f=0
, which corresponds to the
p
-Laplace equation, is relevant since it is the Euler equation of the integral
\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}
and such an integral is the simplest variational integral of non-quadratic growth.
So (
1.1a
) is the natural generalization.

A more general approach to partial differential equations is when the source term is a Radon measure

-\Delta_{p}u=\mu.

(1.2)

This approach is detailed more explicitly in
[
13
]
.
The equation (
1.1a
) is a particular case of (
1.2
), considering the measure induced by
f
.
However, not all partial differential equations of the type (
1.2
) can be stated as (
1.1a
).
The general issue in (
1.2
) is to represent the measure with a source function.
In
[
16
]
the Wolff potential is used to get pointwise approximations of a representation for the measure and it is shown that such approximations are appropriate to solve (
1.2
).
The equation (
1.2
) is also studied in
[
3
,
11
]
, but in this work we focus on the specific case (
1.1a
).

In
[
7
]
the
p
-Poisson equation is studied, and existence and regularity results are provided, when
\Omega
is convex.
Solutions to the
p
-Laplace equation are called
p
-harmonic.
The study of such functions is done abstractly considering
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic functions in
[
11
]
. This approach is also discussed and extended in
[
16
]
.

In our work we study the Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Poisson equation and the stability of solutions.
The
p
-Poisson equation is studied in
[
3
]
, but with Neumann conditions, and they make an analysis, where the parameter
p
tends to infinity, which is an aspect that is not discussed in our work.
The stability is also discussed in
[
14
]
in the Euclidean setting considering Hölder regularity.
Considering a suitable positive-valued weight function
w
one can define the weighted
p
-Laplacian

\Delta_{w,p}u=\text{div}(w|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u).

This weighted
p
-Laplacian is discussed in
[
9
]
.
It is worth to mention that in
[
9
]
the right-hand side of (
1.1a
) depends on the solution
u
.

The weak formulation of (
1.1a
) in the Euclidean setting is

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi=\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega).

(1.3)

This weak formulation makes sense even if
u
is not differentiable as long as
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
.
For the weak formulation of (
1.1b
), we require
g\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
u-g\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that on the boundary the value of this function is zero.
Thus the weak formulation of (
1.1b
) is

u-g\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega).

(1.4)

We aim to formulate the Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Poisson equation in the metric measure space setting.
From the weak formulations (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), it follows that to state the Dirichlet problem, we would need to find concepts to replace
\nabla u
,
|\nabla u|
and
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
in the metric measure space setting.
The upper gradients are one form of generalizing the magnitude of the derivative in metric measure spaces. However, since
\nabla u
is a vector, the replacement of this is more delicate. A way to do this is with Cheeger’s differential structure
[
6
]
.
The space
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is replaced by the Newtonian space with zero boundary values
N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

In the nonsmooth setting the proofs of potential theory as in
[
11
,
16
]
are done considering minimizers of the energy. In
[
10
,
17
]
the minimizing property is considered as the definition of
p
-harmonicity in metric measure spaces
and similarly we define solutions to the
p
-Poisson equation in the nonsmooth setting by using minimizers.
In the context of RCD spaces the
p
-Poisson equation is studied in
[
1
]
and it is shown that the solutions belong to
W^{2,2}(X)
, which is something that is not defined with Newtonian spaces.
The Neumann problem for the
p
-Laplace equation has been studied in the nonsmooth setting in
[
4
,
5
]
, where they obtain existence, global Hölder regularity and stability of solutions.

In the Cheeger’s setting we show that
u
is a solution in the sense of (
3.8
), which is a metric measure space version of (
1.3
), if and only if
u
is a minimizer of the functional

J_{C}(u)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|_{x}^{p}d\mu(x)-p\int_{\Omega}fud\mu.

Thus it is possible to define the solution as a minimizer of the functional
J_{C}
.
Note that
J_{C}
includes the function
u
and the magnitude of the Cheeger’s gradient
|\nabla u|_{x}
, but not the vector
\nabla u
itself. Thus this approach of minimizing
J_{C}
makes sense also if we use upper gradients instead of Cheeger’s gradients.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is to recall basic properties of metric measure spaces.
In Section
3
we discuss how to state the problems in the general metric measure space setting by using the functional
J
.
In Section
4
we discuss the existence of solutions.
In Section
5
we discuss the stability of the solutions in the Cheeger’s setting, where the space is geodesic.
As a corollary we obtain that the solution is unique.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper we let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space with
\mu
a Borel regular measure.
For
x\in X
and
r>0
the open ball is denoted by
B(x,r):=\{y\in X:d(x,y)<r\}
. For
a>0
we denote
aB(x,r)=B(x,ar)
.
For a
\mu
-measurable set
E\subseteq X
, with
0<\mu(E)<\infty
, and
f\in L^{1}(E)
we denote the
integral average
of
f
over
E
by

f_{E}:=\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptE}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{E}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{E}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{E}}fd\mu:=\frac{1}{\mu(E)}\int_{E}fd\mu.

For a function
u:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a Borel measurable function
g:X\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is an
upper gradient of
u
, if

|u(\gamma(a))-u(\gamma(b))|\leq\int_{\gamma}g(\cdot)ds

(2.1)

for all nonconstant compact rectifiable curves
\gamma:[a,b]\rightarrow X
.
If the inequality (
2.1
) is satisfied for all curves except for a curve family that has
p
-modulus zero, then
g
is a
p
-weak upper gradient of
u
.
If a function
u
has a
p
-weak upper gradient
g\in L^{p}(X)
, then there exists a unique
p
-weak upper gradient
g_{u}
of
u
with the smallest
L^{p}
norm among the
L^{p}
norms of all
p
-weak upper gradients of
u
. This function
g_{u}
is called the
minimal
p
-weak upper gradient of
u
.
See
[
12
]
for more on path integrals, the
p
-modulus of curve families and minimal
p
-weak upper gradients.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space and
p\geq 1
. We say that
u\in N^{1,p}(X)
, if
u\in L^{p}(X)
and there exists a
p
-weak upper gradient
g
of
u
such that
g\in L^{p}(X)
. The space
N^{1,p}(X)
is known as the
Newtonian space
and it is equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}:=\left(\int_{X}|u|^{p}+g_{u}^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space,
\Omega\subset X
and
p\geq 1
. The
Newtonian space with zero boundary values

N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
consists of functions
u\in N^{1,p}(X)
, restricted to
\Omega
, such that
u=0
in
X\setminus\Omega
.

See
[
2
,
17
]
for more information on the space
N_{0}^{1,p}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space and
p\geq 1
. The
capacity
of a set
E\subset X
is the number

C_{p}(E):=\inf\|u\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}^{p},

where the infimum is taken over all
u\in N^{1,p}(X)
such that
u\geq 1
on
E
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space such that
\mu(B)
is positive for every ball
B\subset X
.
The space
(X,d,\mu)

supports a
(q,p)
-Poincaré inequality
with
p\geq 1
and
q\geq 1
, if there exist constants
C_{P}>0
and
\lambda\geq 1
such that for every
u\in L^{1}(X)
,
x\in X
,
r>0
and
g
an upper gradient of
u
we have

\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x,r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,r)}}|u-u_{B(x,r)}|^{q}d\mu\right)^{1/q}\leq C_{P}r\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(x,\lambda r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,\lambda r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,\lambda r)}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(x,\lambda r)}}g^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}.

Definition 2.6
.

A Borel regular measure
\mu
on the metric space
(X,d)
is
doubling
, if there exists a constant
C_{\mu}\geq 1
such that

0<\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C_{\mu}\mu(B(x,r))<\infty

for every
x\in X
and
r>0
.

Now we introduce Maz’ya’s inequality, which plays an essential role in the estimations discussed in this paper.

Theorem 2.7
.

(Maz’ya’s inequality,
[
2
, Theorem 5.53]
)

Assume that the space
(X,d,\mu)
supports a
(q,p)
-Poincaré inequality for some
q\geq p
. For
u\in N_{loc}^{1,p}(X)
, let
S:=\{x\in X:u(x)=0\}
. Then for all balls
B=B(x,r)
,

\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt2B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{2B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{2B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{2B}}|u|^{q}d\mu\right)^{p/q}\leq\frac{C(r^{p}+1)}{C_{p}(B\cap S)}\int_{2\lambda B}g_{u}^{p}d\mu,

where
\lambda
is the dilation constant in the
(q,p)
-Poincaré inequality.
The constant
C
depends only on
p
and the constant
C_{P}
from the
(q,p)
-Poincaré inequality.

The next lemma follows from Maz’ya’s inequality and it is used many times in this paper.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space, where the measure
\mu
is doubling and the space supports a
(p,p)
-Poincaré inequality with
p\geq 1
.
Let
\Omega\subset X
be a nonempty bounded open connected set so that
\mu(X\setminus\Omega)>0
.
Let
u\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Then there exists a constant
c_{1}
that depends only on
p
,
C_{P}
,
\Omega
and
\mu
such that

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq c_{1}\|g_{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

We use Maz’ya’s inequality to prove this result. By considering the zero extension of
u
, we have
X\setminus\Omega\subset S
. We choose
x\in\Omega
and the radius
r
so big that
\Omega\subset 2B
and
\mu(B\setminus\Omega)>0
. Then
C_{p}(B\cap S)\geq C_{p}(B\setminus\Omega)\geq\mu(B\setminus\Omega)
and thus

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{2B}|u|^{p}d\mu\leq\frac{C(r^{p}+1)\mu(2B)}{C_{p}(B\cap S)}\int_{2\lambda B}g_{u}^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(r^{p}+1)\mu(2B)}{\mu(B\setminus\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}g_{u}^{p}d\mu.

Note that
g_{u}=0

\mu
-almost everywhere in the complement of
\Omega
, because
u=0
in the complement of
\Omega
, see
[
2
, Corollary 2.21]
.
Thus by denoting

c_{1}=\left(\frac{C(r^{p}+1)\mu(2B)}{\mu(B\setminus\Omega)}\right)^{1/p},

we get the result.

\square

General hypothesis

From now on in this paper
(X,d,\mu)
is a complete metric measure space, where the measure
\mu
is doubling and the space supports a
(p,p)
-Poincaré inequality with
1<p<\infty
.
We assume that
\Omega\subset X
is a nonempty bounded open connected set so that
\mu(X\setminus\Omega)>0
.

3.
Formulation of the Dirichlet problem in metric measure spaces

Here we will extend the
p
-Poisson equation into the nonsmooth metric measure setting. To do that we need to define the notion of Cheeger’s gradient.
The original idea appears in
[
6
]
. However, the explicit statement of the result that we use comes from
[
12
]
.
We will provide a summary of the construction of the theorem.
The idea is to construct a differentiable structure like a manifold but for metric measure spaces.
Consider the following definitions.

Definition 3.1
.

A function
u\in N_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,p}\left(X\right)
is
asymptotic
p
-harmonic at
x_{0}\in~X
, if

\lim_{r\to 0}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B\left(x_{0},r\right)}g_{u}^{p}\;d\mu-\inf_{\varphi\in N_{c}^{1,p}\left(B\left(x_{0},r\right)\right)}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B\left(x_{0},r\right)}g_{u+\varphi}^{p}\;d\mu\right)=0.

Here
N_{c}^{1,p}(B)
is the space of Newtonian functions that have compact support in
B
.

Definition 3.2
.

A function
u\in N_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,p}(X)
is
asymptotically generalized linear at
x_{0}\in X
,

if
u
is asymptotic
p
-harmonic at
x_{0}
and
x_{0}
is a Lebesgue point of
g_{u}^{p}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

(
[
12
, Theorem 13.4.4]
)

If
X
is geodesic, there exist a positive integer
N
and a countable collection of measurable sets
\left\{U_{\alpha}\right\}_{\alpha}
with
\mu\left(U_{\alpha}\right)>~0
satisfying
\mu\left(X\backslash\bigcup_{\alpha}U_{\alpha}\right)=0
, and, for each
U_{\alpha}
, there is a collection of 1-Lipschitz functions
f_{1}^{\alpha},\ldots,f_{N(\alpha)}^{\alpha}
on
X
, where
N(\alpha)\leq N
for each
\alpha
, such that

(1)

each
f_{j}^{\alpha},j=1,\ldots,N(\alpha)
, is asymptotically generalized linear at each point of
U_{\alpha}
,

(2)

for each
\vec{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{N(\alpha)}\backslash\{\overrightarrow{0}\}
and each
x_{0}\in U_{\alpha}
we have
g_{\vec{a}\cdot\overrightarrow{f^{\alpha}}}\left(x_{0}\right)>0
,

(3)

whenever
u:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is an L-Lipschitz function, there is a set
V_{\alpha}(u)\subset U_{\alpha}
, with
\mu\left(U_{\alpha}\backslash V_{\alpha}(u)\right)=0
, and Borel functions
b_{j}^{\alpha}(u):V_{\alpha}(u)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
j=1,\ldots,N(\alpha)
, such that for
x_{0}\in V_{\alpha}(u)
we have
g_{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{f}^{\alpha}-u}\left(x_{0}\right)=0
if and only if

\vec{a}=\vec{b}^{\alpha}(u)\left(x_{0}\right)=\left(b_{1}^{\alpha}(u)\left(x_{0}\right),\ldots,b_{N(\alpha)}^{\alpha}(u)\left(x_{0}\right)\right).

The function
\vec{b}^{\alpha}(u):V_{\alpha}(u)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N(\alpha)}
is called a derivative of
u
and is denoted
D^{\alpha}u
.
We may assume without loss of generality that the sets
U_{\alpha}
are pairwise disjoint and thus we define

D(u):=\sum_{\alpha}D^{\alpha}u\chi_{U_{\alpha}}.

(3.4)

The operator
D
is a linear operator for Lipschitz functions
[
12
]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

(
[
12
, Proposition 13.5.8]
)

Suppose that
X
is geodesic.
Then there is a positive integer
N
and a bounded linear differential operator

\nabla:N^{1,p}(X)\rightarrow L^{p}\left(X:\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)

such that
\nabla(uv)=u\nabla v+v\nabla u
whenever
u,v\in N^{1,p}(X)
are Lipschitz functions,

\left|\nabla u\left(x_{0}\right)\right|_{x_{0}}\leq g_{u}\left(x_{0}\right)\leq\sqrt{N}\left|\nabla u\left(x_{0}\right)\right|_{x_{0}}

for almost every
x_{0}\in X
, and
\nabla u
coincides, for Lipschitz functions
u
, with the operator defined in (
3.4
).

The existence of an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{x_{0}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, that induces the norm
|\cdot|_{x_{0}}
, is proved within the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 13.5.7]
.
These theorems provide the existence of the differentiable structure.
However the differentiable structure of Theorem
3.3
is not necessarily unique.
Theorem
3.5
shows us that if
u\in N^{1,p}(X)
, then
|\nabla u|_{x}\in L^{p}(X)
, where
\nabla u
is the Cheeger’s gradient, which is linear.
In particular

\|g_{u}\|_{L^{p}(X)}\leq\sqrt{N}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(X)}:=\sqrt{N}\left(\int_{X}(|\nabla u(x)|_{x})^{p}d\mu(x)\right)^{1/p}.

Therefore, if
u\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
X
is geodesic, we get from Lemma
2.8
that

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq c_{1}\sqrt{N}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

(3.6)

Now we can define the solution to the Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Poisson equation in the Cheeger’s setting.
Here we also replace the Sobolev space in (
1.4
) with the Newtonian space.

Definition 3.7
.

Suppose that
X
is geodesic and fix a differentiable structure of Theorem
3.3
.
A solution (in the Cheeger’s setting) for the
p
-Poisson equation with source term
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
is a function
u\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)=\int_{\Omega}f\varphi d\mu\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega).

(3.8)

Here
\nabla u
and
\nabla\varphi
denote Cheeger’s gradients.
A solution to the Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Poisson equation (in the Cheeger’s setting) with source term
f
and boundary data
g\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
is a function
u\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
u-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and (
3.8
) holds.

In the Cheeger’s setting
u
being a solution in the sense of the previous definition is actually equivalent to
u
being a minimizer of the functional

J_{C}(u):=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|_{x}^{p}d\mu(x)-p\int_{\Omega}fud\mu.

This is proved below by finding the critical points of
J_{C}
.
More precisely we fix a direction
\varphi\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and define the auxiliary function
L:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

L(\varepsilon):=J_{C}(u+\varepsilon\varphi)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi)|_{x}^{p}-pf(u+\varepsilon\varphi)d\mu(x).

(3.9)

We find critical points of
J_{C}
by checking the critical points of
L
.
In the next lemma we prove the differentiability of
L
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
X
be geodesic and fix a differentiable structure of Theorem
3.3
.
Let
u\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and
\varphi\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.
Then the function
L
is differentiable and furthermore

L^{\prime}(\varepsilon)=p\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi)|_{x}^{p-2}\langle\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi),\nabla\varphi\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)-p\int_{\Omega}f\varphi d\mu.

(3.11)

Proof.

The claim follows from
[
8
, Theorem 2.27]
, which allows us to move the derivative inside the integral.
To use this theorem we need to show that the partial derivative of the integrand in (
3.9
) with respect to
\varepsilon
is bounded by an integrable function in
\Omega
.
For the second term

\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon}pf(u+\varepsilon\varphi)=pf\varphi,

which is an integrable function by Hölder’s inequality.
Now we prove the same for the first term.
Notice that

\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon}|\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi)|_{x}^{p}=p|\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi)|_{x}^{p-2}\langle\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi),\nabla\varphi\rangle_{x}.

From the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequalities we get

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon}|\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi)|_{x}^{p}\right|\leq p|\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi)|_{x}^{p-1}|\nabla\varphi|_{x}\leq|\nabla\varphi|_{x}^{p}+(p-1)|\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi)|_{x}^{p}.

Then from the convexity of the function
t\to t^{p}
, we have

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon}|\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi)|_{x}^{p}\right|\leq|\nabla\varphi|_{x}^{p}+(p-1)2^{p-1}\left(|\nabla u|_{x}^{p}+|\varepsilon|^{p}|\nabla\varphi|_{x}^{p}\right).

Therefore
\frac{\partial}{\partial\varepsilon}|\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi)|_{x}^{p}
is bounded by an integrable function in
\Omega
. Thus we conclude that
L
is differentiable.
Finally (
3.11
) follows from the calculations above.

\square

To find the critical points of
J_{C}
we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
V
be an inner product space over the field
\mathbb{R}
with the induced norm
\|\cdot\|:=\sqrt{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
.
Then for all
a,b\in V
the following estimations hold:

\left\langle\|b\|^{p-2}b-\|a\|^{p-2}a,b-a\right\rangle\geq\begin{cases}2^{1-p}\|b-a\|^{p},&p\geq 2,\\
(p-1)(\|a\|+\|b\|)^{p-2}\|b-a\|^{2},&p<2.\end{cases}

Proof.

For the case
1<p<2
the following inequality is proved in
[
15
, Chapter 12]
:

\left\langle\|b\|^{p-2}b-\|a\|^{p-2}a,b-a\right\rangle\geq(p-1)\|b-a\|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\|a+t(b-a)\|^{p-2}dt.

From the above inequality and the triangle inequality

\|a+t(b-a)\|\leq(1-t)\|a\|+t\|b\|\leq\|a\|+\|b\|

together with the fact that
p-2<0
, we conclude that the claim holds.

The case
p\geq 2
is also explained in
[
15
, Chapter 12]
. There they obtain the larger coefficient
2^{2-p}
instead of
2^{1-p}
. However there is a mistake in the proof. Their proof works, if we have the coefficient
2^{1-p}
instead of
2^{2-p}
, which is why we have that here.
Technically in
[
15
]
the results are proven for vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the dot product, but the same proofs work also for a general inner product space.

\square

Now we prove the minimizing property of solutions.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
X
be geodesic and fix a differentiable structure of Theorem
3.3
.
Let
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and
u,g\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
u-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Then
u
satisfies (
3.8
) if and only if
J_{C}(u)\leq J_{C}(v)
for every
v\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
v-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

First assume that
u
satisfies (
3.8
) and let
v\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
v-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.
To prove that
J_{C}(u)\leq J_{C}(v)
we let
\varphi=v-u
.
Then we have
\varphi=(v-g)-(u-g)\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
L(1)=J_{C}(v)
.
For every positive
\varepsilon
we get from (
3.11
)

\displaystyle L^{\prime}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}p|\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi)|_{x}^{p-2}\langle\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi),\nabla\varphi\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)-p\int_{\Omega}f\varphi d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}p|\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi)|_{x}^{p-2}\langle\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi),\nabla\varphi\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle-p\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle|\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi)|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi)-|\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla u,\nabla(u+\varepsilon\varphi)-\nabla u\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where in the final step we used Lemma
3.12
.
In particular this implies that
L(1)\geq L(0)
, which is what we wanted to show.

Now suppose that
J_{C}(u)\leq J_{C}(v)
for every
v\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
v-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Let
\varphi\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Then by setting
v=u+\varepsilon\varphi
we have
v-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
for every
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
.
Thus
L(0)=J_{C}(u)\leq J_{C}(v)=L(\varepsilon)
for every
\varepsilon
, and
L
attains its minimum at 0. Therefore we must have
L^{\prime}(0)=0
and thus

L^{\prime}(0)=\int_{\Omega}p|\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)-p\int_{\Omega}f\varphi d\mu=0,

and (
3.8
) follows from this.

\square

This approach of minimizing the functional
J_{C}
has the benefit that
J_{C}
only includes the function
u
and the magnitude of the Cheeger’s gradient
|\nabla u|_{x}
, but not the vector
\nabla u
itself.
Thus this approach also makes sense even if we don’t have a Cheeger differentiable structure, so even if
X
is not geodesic. In this case we consider the minimal
p
-weak upper gradient instead of the Cheeger’s gradient. This motivates the following definition of solutions to the Dirichlet problem.

Definition 3.14
.

Let
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and define the functional
J:N^{1,p}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
by

J(u):=\int_{\Omega}g_{u}^{p}d\mu-p\int_{\Omega}fud\mu.

Here
g_{u}
is the minimal
p
-weak upper gradient of
u\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
.
A solution to the Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Poisson equation with source term
f
and boundary data
g\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
is defined as a function
u\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
u-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
u
minimizes
J
, i.e.
J(u)\leq J(v)
for every
v\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
v-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

4.
Existence of solutions

In this section we prove that a solution in the sense of Definition
3.14
exists. In particular we don’t need to assume that the space is geodesic.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and
g\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
.
There exists a solution
u_{0}
to the Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Poisson equation, that is to say
u_{0}\in~N^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
J(u_{0})=\inf\{J(u):u-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\}
and
u_{0}-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

The first step is to show that the functional
J
is bounded from below. Thus assume that
u-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. By using Hölder’s inequality and Minkowski’s inequality we get

\left|\int_{\Omega}fud\mu\right|\leq\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}(\|u-g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}).

Next we use Lemma
2.8
for the function
u-g
.
A useful property of upper gradients is that
g_{u}+g_{g}
is a
p
-weak upper gradient of
u-g
. Thus we have

\|u-g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq c_{1}\|g_{u-g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq c_{1}\|g_{u}+g_{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq c_{1}\|g_{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}\|g_{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

By putting these estimates into the functional
J(u)
, we get

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle=\|g_{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}-p\int_{\Omega}fud\mu

\displaystyle\geq\|g_{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}-p\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}(c_{1}\|g_{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}\|g_{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\geq(1-p)(c_{1}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)})^{\frac{p}{p-1}}-p\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}(c_{1}\|g_{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}).

The last inequality is obtained by finding the minimum of the function
[0,\infty)\ni t\to t^{p}-pc_{1}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}t
by using derivatives.
Thus we have found a lower bound for
J(u)
and therefore

I:=\inf\{J(u):u-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\}>-\infty.

Also clearly
I\leq J(g)<\infty
.

Now we will prove that there exists
u_{0}\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
so that
u_{0}-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
u_{0}
reaches the infimum.
Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence in
N^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n}-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
for every
n
and
J(u_{n})\to I
.

By passing to a subsequence if necessary, we can assume that
I\leq J(u_{n})\leq I+1
for every
n
. Then

\|g_{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq I+1+p\int_{\Omega}fu_{n}d\mu.

Since
u_{n}-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we get the same way as in the proof of the lower bound for
J(u)
that

\left|\int_{\Omega}fu_{n}d\mu\right|\leq\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}(\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}\|g_{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}\|g_{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}).

Therefore

\|g_{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq|I|+1+p\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}(\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}\|g_{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}\|g_{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}).

If
\|g_{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}>1
, we get by dividing both sides with
\|g_{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\|g_{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\leq|I|+1+p\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}(\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}+c_{1}\|g_{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}),

which is trivially true also if
\|g_{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq 1
.
For the
L^{p}
norm of
u_{n}
we get as in the proof of the lower bound for
J(u)
that

\|u_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}\|g_{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}\|g_{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

This shows that the sequence
u_{n}
is bounded in the
N^{1,p}(\Omega)
norm.

Since
g_{u_{n}}
is bounded in the
L^{p}
norm, by passing to a subsequence if necessary, we can assume that
\|g_{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
converges.
Then because
\|g_{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
converges and
J(u_{n})
converges,
\int_{\Omega}fu_{n}d\mu
also converges.

Since
p>1
, we have that
L^{p}(\Omega)
is reflexive
[
12
]
, and so we can apply Mazur’s lemma
[
12
, Theorem 7.3.8]
.
Then there exists a sequence of convex combinations of
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, denoted
\{v_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, and a sequence of convex combinations of
g_{u_{n}}
, denoted
\rho_{n}
, and functions
u_{0}\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
\rho\in L^{p}(\Omega)
so that
\rho
is a
p
-weak upper gradient of
u_{0}
,
\rho_{n}
is an upper gradient of
v_{n}
,
v_{n}
converges to
u_{0}
in the
L^{p}(\Omega)
norm and
\rho_{n}
converges to
\rho
in the
L^{p}(\Omega)
norm.

We will show that
J(u_{0})=I
, but first we show that
u_{0}-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.
Notice that
v_{n}-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
for every
n
. By extending
v_{n}-g
as zero in the complement of
\Omega
, we get
v_{n}-g\in N^{1,p}(X)
. Thus

\|v_{n}-g\|_{N^{1,p}(X)}=\|v_{n}-g\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq\|g\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}+C,

where
C
is the upper bound of
\|u_{n}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}
. Hence the sequence
(v_{n}-g)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in the
N^{1,p}(X)
norm.
Thus we get from
[
2
, Lemma 6.2]
that there exist
w\in N^{1,p}(X)
and a sequence of convex combinations of
(v_{n}-g)_{n=1}^{\infty}
, denoted
(w_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
, such that
w_{n}
converges to
w
in the
L^{p}(X)
norm and
w_{n}
converges to
w
quasieverywhere.
This means that there exists a set
A\subset X
such that
C_{p}(A)=0
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}w_{n}(x)=w(x)
for every
x\in X\setminus A
. As
w_{n}
is a convex combination of the functions
v_{n}-g
, we get that
w_{n}
is zero in
X\setminus\Omega
, and therefore for every
x\in(X\setminus\Omega)\setminus A
we have
w(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}w_{n}(x)=~0
.
In other words
w=0
quasieverywhere in
X\setminus\Omega
.
On the other hand
w\in~N^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
u_{0}-g\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\|w-(u_{0}-g)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|w_{n}-u_{0}+g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\sum_{i=n}^{m_{n}}\lambda_{i,n}(v_{i}-u_{0})\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=n}^{m_{n}}\lambda_{i,n}\|v_{i}-u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=0,

because
v_{i}
converges to
u_{0}
in the
L^{p}(\Omega)
norm.
Then
u_{0}-g=w

\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega
and
[
2
, Proposition 1.59]
implies that
u_{0}-g=w
quasieverywhere in
\Omega
.
Finally by extending
u_{0}-g
as zero in the complement of
\Omega
, we get that
u_{0}-g=w
quasieverywhere in
X
. Therefore by
[
2
, Proposition 1.61]
we have that
u_{0}-g\in N^{1,p}(X)
and therefore
u_{0}-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Now we show that
u_{0}
reaches the infimum
I
.
Let
\|g_{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
converge to
I_{1}
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N
so that
\|g_{u_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq I_{1}+\varepsilon
whenever
n\geq N
. Hence

\|\rho_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\left\|\sum_{i=n}^{m_{n}}\lambda_{i,n}g_{u_{i}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{i=n}^{m_{n}}\lambda_{i,n}\|g_{u_{i}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq I_{1}+\varepsilon.

This means that
\|\rho\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\rho_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq I_{1}
.

Let
\int_{\Omega}fu_{n}d\mu
converge to
I_{2}
.
Then for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N
so that
|\int_{\Omega}fu_{n}d\mu-I_{2}|<\varepsilon
whenever
n\geq N
. Hence

\left|\int_{\Omega}fv_{n}d\mu-I_{2}\right|=\left|\int_{\Omega}f\sum_{i=n}^{m_{n}}\lambda_{i,n}u_{i}d\mu-I_{2}\right|=\left|\sum_{i=n}^{m_{n}}\lambda_{i,n}\left(\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}d\mu-I_{2}\right)\right|<\varepsilon

and therefore
\int_{\Omega}fv_{n}d\mu
converges to
I_{2}
.
On the other hand
\int_{\Omega}fv_{n}d\mu
also converges to
\int_{\Omega}fu_{0}d\mu
, because by Hölder’s inequality

\left|\int_{\Omega}fv_{n}d\mu-\int_{\Omega}fu_{0}d\mu\right|\leq\int_{\Omega}|f||v_{n}-u_{0}|d\mu\leq\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|v_{n}-u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\to 0.

Thus
\int_{\Omega}fu_{0}d\mu=I_{2}
and finally

\displaystyle I\leq J(u_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}g_{u_{0}}^{p}d\mu-p\int_{\Omega}fu_{0}d\mu\leq\int_{\Omega}\rho^{p}d\mu-p\int_{\Omega}fu_{0}d\mu\leq I_{1}^{p}-pI_{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}g_{u_{n}}^{p}d\mu-p\int_{\Omega}fu_{n}d\mu=I.

This proves that
J
reaches the infimum at
u_{0}
.
Therefore
u_{0}
is a solution to the problem.

\square

5.
Stability of solutions

Now that we have proved the existence of solutions to the Dirichlet problem, we can ask about their stability.
In this section we prove the stability with respect to the source term and with respect to the boundary data.
We assume that
X
is geodesic and we fix a differentiable structure of Theorem
3.3
.
So in this section the gradients are interpreted as Cheeger’s gradients.
This is particularly useful, because the Cheeger’s gradient is linear and (
3.6
) holds.
In this section solutions are defined as in Definition
3.7
.
To prove the stability, we first need to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
u
be a solution to the Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Poisson equation (in the Cheeger’s setting) with source term
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and boundary data
g\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
.
Then

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq 1+\left(\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}\sqrt{N}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\left(1+\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\min\{1,p-1\}}}.

That is, there exists a uniform bound, that is independent of
u
, for the
p
-energy of
u
.
The constants
c_{1}
and
N
are the same as in (
3.6
).
The right-hand side of the previous inequality will be denoted by
K(f,g)
.

Proof.

Clearly the result holds, if
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq 1
.
Thus let
u
be a solution of the Dirichlet problem with
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}>1
.

Notice that
u-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Thus we can take
\varphi=u-g
in (
3.8
), so we have

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla g\rangle_{x}+f(u-g)d\mu(x).

By applying Hölder’s inequality to the above and then (
3.6
), we obtain

\begin{split}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}&\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|u-g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}\sqrt{N}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla(u-g)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}&\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}\sqrt{N}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right).\end{split}

(5.2)

If
p\geq 2
, we multiply both sides of (
5.2
) with
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{1-p}
to obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+c_{1}\sqrt{N}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{2-p}+\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{1-p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}\sqrt{N}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\left(1+\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right).

On the other hand if
1<p<2
, then by dividing both sides of (
5.2
) with
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
, we get

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-2}\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}\sqrt{N}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\left(1+\frac{\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}\sqrt{N}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\left(1+\|\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right).

This completes the proof.

\square

Theorem 5.3
.

The Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Poisson equation is stable with respect to perturbations of the source term, that is to say if
u
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Poisson equation (in the Cheeger’s setting) with source term
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and boundary data
g\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
, and
v
is a solution with source term
h\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and boundary data
g
, then

\|u-v\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

if
p\geq 2
, and

\|u-v\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\max\left\{\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}K(f,g)^{2-p},\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right\},

if
1<p<2
. Here
K(f,g)
is the same as in Lemma
5.1
and
C
is a constant that depends only on
c_{1}
,
N
and
p
, where
c_{1}
and
N
are the constants in (
3.6
).

Proof.

Considering (
3.8
) for both solutions
u
and
v
with the same
\varphi
and subtracting them, we have

\int_{\Omega}\langle|\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla v,\nabla\varphi\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)=\int_{\Omega}(f-h)\varphi d\mu\quad\forall\quad\varphi\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega).

Since
u
and
v
have the same boundary data, we have that
u-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
v-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and therefore
\varphi_{0}:=u-v=(u-g)-(v-g)\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Evaluating the previous equation with
\varphi_{0}
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\langle|\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla v,\nabla u-\nabla v\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)=\int_{\Omega}(f-h)(u-v)d\mu.

(5.4)

From Hölder’s inequality and (
3.6
) we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}(f-h)(u-v)d\mu&\leq\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|u-v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq c_{1}\sqrt{N}\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

(5.5)

From Theorem
3.5
and (
3.6
) we get that

\begin{split}\|u-v\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}&=\left(\|u-v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\|g_{u-v}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\\
&\leq\left(c_{1}^{p}\sqrt{N}^{p}\|\nabla(u-v)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\sqrt{N}^{p}\|\nabla(u-v)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\\
&=\left(c_{1}^{p}+1\right)^{1/p}\sqrt{N}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

(5.6)

Now assume that
p\geq 2
.
From (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) together with Lemma
3.12
it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u-\nabla v|_{x}^{p}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}2^{p-1}\langle|\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla v,\nabla u-\nabla v\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\sqrt{N}2^{p-1}\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\implies\|\nabla u-

\displaystyle\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\leq c_{1}\sqrt{N}2^{p-1}\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}.

From the above estimation and (
5.6
) we get that

\displaystyle\|u-v\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(c_{1}^{p}+1\right)^{1/p}\sqrt{N}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(c_{1}^{p}+1\right)^{1/p}\sqrt{N}^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\left(c_{1}\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

This proves the result for the case
p\geq 2
.

Next we assume that
1<p<2
.
Applying Hölder’s inequality with exponents
2/p
and
2/(2-p)
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-v)|_{x}^{p}d\mu(x)\\
=&\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-v)|_{x}^{p}(|\nabla u|_{x}+|\nabla v|_{x})^{p(p-2)/2}\cdot(|\nabla u|_{x}+|\nabla v|_{x})^{p(2-p)/2}d\mu(x)\\
\leq&\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-v)|_{x}^{2}(|\nabla u|_{x}+|\nabla v|_{x})^{p-2}d\mu(x)\right)^{p/2}\left\||\nabla u|_{x}+|\nabla v|_{x}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p(2-p)/2}\\
\leq&\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p-1}\langle|\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla v,\nabla u-\nabla v\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)\right)^{p/2}\\
&\cdot\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{p(2-p)/2}.\end{split}

(5.7)

In the last inequality we used Lemma
3.12
.
Using (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-v)|_{x}^{p}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{p-1}c_{1}\sqrt{N}\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{p(2-p)/2}

and therefore

\begin{split}\left\|\nabla u-\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}&\leq\frac{c_{1}\sqrt{N}}{p-1}\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{2-p}\\
\leq\frac{c_{1}\sqrt{N}}{p-1}&\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\left(2\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\left\|\nabla v-\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{2-p}.\end{split}

(5.8)

If
\|\nabla v-\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
, then we have

\left\|\nabla u-\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{c_{1}\sqrt{N}}{p-1}\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\left(3\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{2-p}.

By using Lemma
5.1
we obtain

\left\|\nabla u-\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{3^{2-p}c_{1}\sqrt{N}}{p-1}\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}K(f,g)^{2-p}.

On the other hand if
\|\nabla v-\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}>\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
, we can multiply both sides of (
5.8
) with
\|\nabla v-\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-2}
to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u-\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}\sqrt{N}}{p-1}\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{2\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}{\left\|\nabla v-\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}+1\right)^{2-p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3^{2-p}c_{1}\sqrt{N}}{p-1}\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}.

In conclusion by using (
5.6
)

\displaystyle\|u-v\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq(c_{1}^{p}+1)^{1/p}\sqrt{N}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\max\left\{\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}K(f,g)^{2-p},\|f-h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right\}.

This proves the result for the case
1<p<2
.

\square

Theorem 5.9
.

The Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Poisson equation is stable with respect to perturbations of the boundary data, that is to say if
u
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Poisson equation (in the Cheeger’s setting) with source term
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and boundary data
g\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
, and
v
is a solution with source term
f
and boundary data
\hat{g}\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
, then

\|u-v\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\max\left\{\|g-\hat{g}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)},\left\|g-\hat{g}\right\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}}K(f,g)^{1-\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}}\right\}.

Here
K(f,g)
is the same as in Lemma
5.1
and the constant
C
depends only on
c_{1}
,
N
and
p
, where
c_{1}
and
N
are the constants from (
3.6
).

Proof.

Note that
\varphi_{1}:=u-v+\hat{g}-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, because
u-g\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
v-\hat{g}\in N_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.
This means that we can apply (
3.6
) to the function
\varphi_{1}
.
Thus we get

\begin{split}&\|u-v\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\|u-v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\|g_{u-v}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\\
&\leq\|u-v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g_{u-v}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq\|u-v+\hat{g}-g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g-\hat{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\sqrt{N}\|\nabla(u-v)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq c_{1}\sqrt{N}\|\nabla(u-v+\hat{g}-g)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g-\hat{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\sqrt{N}\|\nabla(u-v)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq(c_{1}+1)\sqrt{N}\|\nabla(u-v)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+c_{1}\sqrt{N}\|\nabla(g-\hat{g})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g-\hat{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq(c_{1}+1)\sqrt{N}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+(c_{1}\sqrt{N}+1)\|g-\hat{g}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)},\end{split}

(5.10)

where we also used Theorem
3.5
.
This means that we have the stability, if we can bound
\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
with
\|g-\hat{g}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}
.
By substituting
\varphi_{1}
in (
3.8
) for both
u
and
v
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\varphi_{1}\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)=\int_{\Omega}f\varphi_{1}d\mu=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|_{x}^{p-2}\langle\nabla v,\nabla\varphi_{1}\rangle_{x}d\mu(x).

From Hölder’s inequality, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\langle|\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla v,\nabla u-\nabla v\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)\\
=&\int_{\Omega}\langle|\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla v,\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)\\
\leq&\int_{\Omega}\left||\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla v\right|_{x}|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}|_{x}d\mu(x)\\
\leq&\left\||\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
\leq&\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\left(\left\||\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}+\left\||\nabla v|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\right)\\
=&\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\left(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}+\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\right).\end{split}

(5.11)

Assume now that
p\geq 2
.
From Lemma
3.12
and (
5.11
) we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}&|\nabla u-\nabla v|_{x}^{p}d\mu(x)\leq\int_{\Omega}2^{p-1}\langle|\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla v,\nabla u-\nabla v\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)\\
&\leq 2^{p-1}\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\left(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}+\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\right)\\
&\leq 2^{p-1}\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\left(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{p-1}\\
&\leq 2^{p-1}\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\left(2\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\left\|\nabla u-\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{p-1}.\end{split}

(5.12)

If
\|\nabla v-\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
, then we have

\left\|\nabla u-\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq 6^{p-1}\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}.

By using Lemma
5.1
we obtain

\left\|\nabla u-\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq 6\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p}}K(f,g)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

On the other hand if
\|\nabla v-\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}>\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
, we can multiply both sides of (
5.12
) with
\|\nabla v-\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{1-p}
to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u-\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{2\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}{\|\nabla v-\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}+1\right)^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq 6^{p-1}\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

In conclusion by using Theorem
3.5
and (
5.10
) we get

\displaystyle\|u-v\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq(c_{1}+1)\sqrt{N}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+(c_{1}\sqrt{N}+1)\|g-\hat{g}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\max\left\{\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p}}K(f,g)^{1-\frac{1}{p}},\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right\}

\displaystyle+C\|g-\hat{g}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\max\left\{\|g-\hat{g}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)},\left\|g-\hat{g}\right\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p}}K(f,g)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\right\},

where the constant
C
depends only on
c_{1}
,
N
and
p
.
This proves the result for the case
p\geq 2
.

Now assume that
1<p<2
.
Notice that (
5.7
) holds.
Thus by using (
5.11
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-v)|_{x}^{p}d\mu(x)\\
&\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p-1}\langle|\nabla u|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|_{x}^{p-2}\nabla v,\nabla u-\nabla v\rangle_{x}d\mu(x)\right)^{p/2}\\
&\cdot\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{p(2-p)/2}\\
&\leq C\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p/2}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\right)^{p/2}\\
&\cdot\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{p(2-p)/2}\\
&\leq C\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p/2}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{p/2}\\
&\leq C\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p/2}\left(2\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{p/2},\end{split}

(5.13)

where the constant
C
depends only on
p
.
If
\|\nabla v-\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
, then we obtain by using Lemma
5.1

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u-\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p/2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p/2}K(f,g)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\implies\left\|\nabla u-\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{1/2}K(f,g)^{1/2}.

On the other hand if
\|\nabla v-\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}>\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
, we can multiply both sides of (
5.13
) with
\|\nabla v-\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{-p/2}
to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u-\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p/2}\left(\frac{2\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}{\|\nabla v-\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}+1\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p/2}

\displaystyle\implies\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

In conclusion by using Theorem
3.5
and (
5.10
) we get

\displaystyle\|u-v\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq(c_{1}+1)\sqrt{N}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+(c_{1}\sqrt{N}+1)\|g-\hat{g}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\max\left\{\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{1/2}K(f,g)^{1/2},\left\|\nabla g-\nabla\hat{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right\}+C\|g-\hat{g}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\max\left\{\|g-\hat{g}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)},\left\|g-\hat{g}\right\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{1/2}K(f,g)^{1/2}\right\},

where the constant
C
depends only on
c_{1}
,
N
and
p
.
This proves the result for the case
1<p<2
.

\square

Now we are ready to prove the general stability result.

Theorem 5.14
.

Let
(f_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
functions that converges to
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
in the
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
norm.
Similarly let
(g_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of
N^{1,p}(\Omega)
functions that converges to
g\in N^{1,p}(\Omega)
in the
N^{1,p}(\Omega)
norm.
For every
k\geq 1
let
u_{k}
be a solution to the Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Poisson equation (in the Cheeger’s setting) with source term
f_{k}
and boundary data
g_{k}
, and let
u
be a solution with source term
f
and boundary data
g
. Then
(u_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
converges to
u
in the
N^{1,p}(\Omega)
norm.

Proof.

For every
k\geq 1
let
v_{k}
be a solution to the Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Poisson equation (in the Cheeger’s setting) with source term
f_{k}
and boundary data
g
.
Notice that the solution
v_{k}
shares the source term with
u_{k}
, while
v_{k}
shares the boundary data with
u
.
Thus we can use the stability results when one of the parameters is fixed - the source term or the boundary data.
Then by using the triangle inequality we get

\displaystyle\|u-u_{k}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-v_{k}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}+\|v_{k}-u_{k}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f-f_{k}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle+C\max\left\{\|g-g_{k}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)},\left\|g-g_{k}\right\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p}}K(f_{k},g)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\right\},

if
p\geq 2
.
If
1<p<2
, we get

\displaystyle\|u-u_{k}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-v_{k}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}+\|v_{k}-u_{k}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\max\left\{\|f-f_{k}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}K(f,g)^{2-p},\|f-f_{k}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right\}

\displaystyle+C\max\left\{\|g-g_{k}\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)},\left\|g-g_{k}\right\|_{N^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}K(f_{k},g)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}.

In both cases we get the convergence, because
(K(f_{k},g))_{k=1}^{\infty}
also converges to
K(f,g)
, when
f_{k}\to f
in the
L^{p^{\prime}}
norm. This completes the proof.

\square

An immediate corollary of the stability results is the following.

Corollary 5.15
.

The solution to the Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Poisson equation (in the Cheeger’s setting) is unique.
