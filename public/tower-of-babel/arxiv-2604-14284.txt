A Busy Higgs Signal Preprint: UMN-TH-4524/26
Peiran Li, Zhen Liu, Lian-Tao Wang

Abstract
Higgs final states are prime targets in the search for physics beyond the Standard Model. In the conventional picture, S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) symmetry together with the Goldstone Equivalence Theorem correlates Higgs and gauge-boson final states, implying comparable sensitivity in channels such as h ​ h hh , Z ​ Z ZZ , and W ​ W WW in searches for heavy resonances. In this work, we identify a mechanism to parametrically violate this expectation. We show that higher-order Higgs couplings can induce an electroweak-symmetry-breaking enhancement that selectively amplifies Higgs-rich final states, allowing them to become the leading discovery channels of new resonances. For scalar resonances, this can make di-Higgs the dominant bosonic signal. For resonance masses higher than a couple of TeV, it also opens resonant tri-Higgs and four-Higgs channels as well-motivated search targets. The same underlying mechanism extends to heavy fermionic and vector resonances, where it can similarly enhance channels such as h ​ t ht , Z ​ h Zh , and γ ​ h \gamma h . We present this framework in effective field theory, demonstrate possible UV completions, and discuss its implications for collider searches.

II
The busy Higgs mechanism

Busy Scalar
: Contrary to this common lore, we show there is a new class of theories where Higgs final states will be the driving discovery mode! We first consider coupling
S(H^{\dagger}H)^{n}
, which is

\displaystyle{\cal L}

\displaystyle\supset S(H^{\dagger}H)^{n}

(5)

\displaystyle\to\frac{(v^{2})^{n-1}}{2^{n}}S\left(\Sigma_{i}a_{i}^{2}\binom{n}{1}+h^{2}\binom{n}{1}+4h^{2}\binom{n}{2}+\dots\right).

The first two terms still preserve the
SU(2)
relation between
a_{i}
and
h
. However, the third term gives rise to di-Higgs coupling proportional to
n^{2}
. This is an effect of Electroweak Symmetry Breaking (EWSB). What’s remarkable is that this can happen even with
m_{S}\gg v
. The partial width of these di-boson decay channels vanishes as
v\to 0
. However, the ratio

\displaystyle\frac{\text{BR}(S\to hh)}{\text{BR}(S\to a_{i}a_{i})}=(2n-1)^{2}

(6)

stays as a constant even with
v/m_{S}\to 0
. Therefore, unlike the usual expectation from
SU(2)
symmetry, the di-Higgs channel can remain parametrically enhanced relative to the di-gauge boson channels, and could become the dominant discovery mode.

Multi-Higgs final states can also become relevant at sufficiently large
m_{S}
. While three-body decays are phase-space suppressed relative to two-body decays,
this suppression becomes less severe as
m_{S}
increases, since the integrated
three-body phase space itself grows with
m_{S}^{2}
. Parametrically, the
n
-body phase-space factor

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}=\int\delta^{4}(P-\sum^{n}_{i}p_{i})\prod^{n}_{i}\frac{d^{3}p_{i}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{i}}

(7)

carries a positive mass dimension for
n>2
. In particular, the two-body factor scales as
1/(8\pi)
, whereas the three-body phase space scales as
m_{S}^{2}/(256\pi^{3})

44
. In addition, the
Sh^{3}
interaction from the busy operator receives a combinatorial enhancement, scaling as
\sim 2n(2n-1)(2n-2)/3!
. Taken together, these effects make a multi-Higgs-dominated decay pattern possible.

For example,
S(H^{\dagger}H)^{n}
will provide

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(S\to hhh)}{\Gamma(S\to hh)}\sim\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\frac{2(n-1)^{2}}{3}\frac{m_{S}^{2}}{v^{2}}.

(8)

As shown in

Higgs-infused Fermion
: This busy Higgs mechanism can be generalized in a straightforward manner to heavy fermionic resonances. Consider a heavy
SU(2)
singlet fermion
T
with coupling

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}\supset\bar{q}\tilde{H}T+\text{h.c.},

(9)

where
q=(t,b)_{L}
is a SM doublet fermion. From this interaction, we can derive the relations

\displaystyle\text{BR}(T\to Z_{L}t)=\text{BR}(T\to ht)=\frac{1}{2}\text{BR}(T\to W_{L}b).

(10)

However, if the coupling instead takes the form

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}\supset\frac{c_{n}}{\Lambda^{2n}}(H^{\dagger}H)^{n}\bar{q}\tilde{H}T+\text{h.c.},

(11)

we will have an
O(n^{2})
enhancement to the
T\to ht
channel.

Higgs-infused Vector
: The mechanism can be generalized to vector resonances as well.
A simple realization arises by introducing kinetic mixing with additional heavy vector field
Z^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle{\cal L}\supset\frac{1}{2}\frac{c_{n}}{\Lambda^{2n}}Z^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}(H^{\dagger}H)^{n},

(12)

where
B
is the SM Hypercharge field. After the EWSB and properly taking into account the kinetic mixing proportional to
\epsilon=c_{n}\left(\frac{v^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\right)^{n}
, this leads to

{\cal L}\supset\binom{n}{1}\epsilon Z^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}\frac{h}{v}+\epsilon Z^{\prime}_{\mu}J_{\text{SM}}^{\mu}+{\cal O}\left(\epsilon\frac{m_{Z}^{2}}{m_{Z}^{\prime 2}}\right),

(13)

where
B=c_{W}\gamma-s_{W}Z
and
J_{\text{SM}}
includes fermion current and Goldstone current. If only the second term were present, it would imply

\displaystyle\text{BR}(Z^{\prime}\to Zh)=\text{BR}(Z^{\prime}\to W^{+}W^{-}).

(14)

The first term, however, yields an
n^{2}
enhancement of the
Z^{\prime}\to Zh
and
Z^{\prime}\to\gamma h
amplitudes. Decays to a given SM fermion pair and to
WW
are parametrically suppressed relative to
Zh/\gamma h

Z^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}h
interaction is momentum enhanced, whereas the
Z^{\prime}_{\mu}J_{\text{SM}}^{\mu}
couplings are not. For example, for a
Z^{\prime}
with
m_{Z^{\prime}}=1\penalty\ \mathrm{TeV}
and
n=1

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(Z^{\prime}\to Zh\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \gamma h)}{\Gamma(Z^{\prime}\to WW)}\approx 30\penalty\ .

(15)

Therefore,
\text{BR}(Z^{\prime}\to Z/\gamma+h)
provides a significant bosonic discovery channel.

Higgs-infused Tensor
: There can be also generalizations of the busy Higgs to the spin-2 resonances. The (minimal) spin-2 coupling to the Higgs doublets can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal L}\supset h_{\mu\nu}D^{\mu}H^{\dagger}D^{\nu}H.

(16)

This preserves the
SU(2)
relation

\displaystyle\text{BR}(h_{\mu\nu}\to hh)

\displaystyle=\text{BR}(h_{\mu\nu}\to Z_{L}Z_{L})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\text{BR}(h_{\mu\nu}\to W_{L}W_{L}).

(17)

Multiplying the above interaction by
(H^{\dagger}H)^{n}
will generate enhanced multi-Higgs (
n_{h}>2
) decay channels, although this would not change the two-body BR relations since the minimal coupling already saturates the di-boson final state.

III
UV completions

To ensure the dominance of the busy Higgs mechanism, we want the operator with
n>1
to be the leading one. Generating such an operator at leading order is not difficult to imagine, in particular, through some notion of locality in theory space. For example, if we have the following interactions

\displaystyle\Phi N_{1}N^{\dagger}_{n+1},\ N_{1}N^{\dagger}_{2}H^{\dagger}H,\ ...\ ,\ N_{n}N^{\dagger}_{n+1}H^{\dagger}H,

(18)

where
\Phi,N_{i}
are additional scalars. Then, the coupling in
Eq.

5

can be generated at 1-loop with
S=\text{Re}(\Phi)
. However, since
H^{\dagger}H
is a singlet, there is no symmetry that forbids lower-dimensional operators at tree level, such as
SH^{\dagger}H
.
One possibility is to use the operator
H_{u}\epsilon H_{d}
in a two-Higgs doublet setup, with additional global charges assigned. Hence, we will be able to control the leading order at which this operator appears in the theory.
Parameterizing
H_{u}=(v_{u}+H_{u}^{0},\sqrt{2}H_{u}^{-})/\sqrt{2}
and
H_{d}=(\sqrt{2}H_{d}^{+},v_{d}+H_{d}^{0})/\sqrt{2}
, in the Higgs basis, we have

\displaystyle H_{u}\epsilon H_{d}

\displaystyle\supset\frac{1}{2}(-v\sin\beta\sin\alpha+v\cos\beta\cos\alpha)h-\frac{\sin 2\alpha}{4}h^{2}

\displaystyle\penalty\ \quad+\frac{\sin 2\beta}{4}a_{0}^{2}+\frac{\sin 2\beta}{2}a^{+}a^{-}+...,

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\sin 2\beta}{4}\left(2vh+(h^{2}+a_{0}^{2}+2a^{+}a^{-})\right)+...

(19)

where
\tan\beta=v_{u}/v_{d}
, and we have taken the decoupling limit,
\sin\alpha\simeq-\cos\beta
and
\cos\alpha\simeq\sin\beta
. The “
+...
” contains the heavy doublet without a VEV and misalignment corrections. If we look at the terms quadratic in fields, we again see a
SU(2)
symmetry between the light Higgs and the Goldstones. Starting with an operator
S(H_{u}\epsilon H_{d})^{n}
, we will obtain an effective operator
S(H^{\dagger}H)^{n}
after integrating out the heavy scalars.
In

C
, we explicitly show a couple of examples in which the busy Higgs operator can be generated.

Starting from an operator
S(H^{\dagger}H)^{n}

V
Summary and Outlook

In this work, we identified a class of models in which Higgs final states can play a qualitatively different role from the conventional expectation based on
SU(2)
symmetry and the Goldstone Equivalence Theorem. For heavy resonances with minimal couplings to the Higgs sector, Higgs and gauge-boson final states are typically correlated, leading to comparable sensitivities in channels such as
hh
,
ZZ
, and
WW
. We showed that this expectation can be violated once the resonance couples through higher-order Higgs structures. The key effect is that EWSB selectively amplifies Higgs-rich final states. In the scalar case, this can make
S\to hh
one of the leading decay modes, while for
n\geq 3
multi-Higgs final states from heavy resonances can become important as well.

We further showed that the same underlying mechanism extends beyond the scalar case. For heavy fermionic resonances, Higgs-infused couplings can enhance the
ht
channel relative to the corresponding gauge-boson modes. For vector resonances, analogous structures can make
Zh
and
\gamma h
especially prominent. Similar extensions also exist for spin-2 resonances, where higher-order Higgs operators naturally favor multi-Higgs final states. We also expect a similar lesson to hold for higher-spin resonances. Taken together, these examples indicate that Higgs-rich channels are not merely complementary to conventional diboson searches but can instead provide the primary discovery mode for new resonances.

These observations have direct implications for collider searches and future theory developments. In the scalar case, our estimates show that di-Higgs searches can probe the busy operators more effectively than diboson channels across an important part of the parameter space. Moreover, the same framework opens a broader search direction in high-multiplicity Higgs final states, providing explicit motivation for resonant tri-Higgs and four-Higgs searches. For fermionic and vector resonances, the mechanism likewise motivates dedicated searches in
ht
,
Zh
, and
\gamma h
final states. Therefore, our results point to Higgs final states as well-motivated and potentially leading discovery channels for new resonances.

A.2
Cross-check in Unitary Gauge

It is also useful to estimate the amplitudes directly in unitary gauge. In unitary gauge,

\displaystyle H=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
v+h\end{pmatrix},\qquad H^{\dagger}H=\frac{(v+h)^{2}}{2},

(29)

so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\supset\frac{c_{n}}{\Lambda^{2n-3}}S(H^{\dagger}H)^{n}=\frac{c_{n}}{2^{n}\Lambda^{2n-3}}S(v+h)^{2n}.

(30)

Expanding the operator gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\supset\frac{c_{n}}{2^{n}\Lambda^{2n-3}}\left[(2n)v^{2n-1}Sh+\binom{2n}{2}v^{2n-2}Sh^{2}+\binom{2n}{3}v^{2n-3}Sh^{3}+\cdots\right].

(31)

The term proportional to
Sv^{2n}
is a tadpole term. In principle, one can include a linear tadpole counterterm to make sure
\langle S\rangle=0
, so that
S
is defined around the true vacuum. This does not affect the branching-ratio pattern discussed here.

The channels involving electroweak gauge bosons arise through the
Sh
mixing term together with
the standard-model Higgs couplings

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm SM}\supset\frac{2m_{W}^{2}}{v}hW_{\mu}^{+}W^{-\mu}+\frac{m_{Z}^{2}}{v}hZ_{\mu}Z^{\mu}+\frac{m_{W}^{2}}{v^{2}}h^{2}W_{\mu}^{+}W^{-\mu}+\frac{m_{Z}^{2}}{2v^{2}}h^{2}Z_{\mu}Z^{\mu}+\cdots.

(32)

In the heavy-scalar limit
m_{S}\gg m_{h},m_{W},m_{Z}

longitudinal modes. Using

\displaystyle\epsilon_{L}(p_{1})\cdot\epsilon_{L}(p_{2})\simeq\frac{p_{1}\cdot p_{2}}{m_{V}^{2}},

(33)

one finds for the two-body channels

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(S\to W_{L}^{+}W_{L}^{-})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{c_{n}}{2^{n}\Lambda^{2n-3}}(2n)v^{2n-1}\frac{1}{m_{S}^{2}-m_{h}^{2}}\left(\frac{2m_{W}^{2}}{v}\right)\left(\frac{m_{S}^{2}}{2m_{W}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{c_{n}}{2^{n}\Lambda^{2n-3}}\,2n\,v^{2n-2},

(34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(S\to Z_{L}Z_{L})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{c_{n}}{2^{n}\Lambda^{2n-3}}(2n)v^{2n-1}\frac{1}{m_{S}^{2}-m_{h}^{2}}\left(\frac{2m_{Z}^{2}}{v}\right)\left(\frac{m_{S}^{2}}{2m_{Z}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{c_{n}}{2^{n}\Lambda^{2n-3}}\,2n\,v^{2n-2},

(35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(S\to hh)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{c_{n}}{2^{n}\Lambda^{2n-3}}\,(2n)(2n-1)\,v^{2n-2},

(36)

where the mixing-induced contribution to
S\to hh
through the SM Higgs cubic coupling is
suppressed by
m_{h}^{2}/m_{S}^{2}
and has been neglected.

For the three-body channels, the direct
Sh^{3}
interaction gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(S\to hhh)\approx\frac{c_{n}}{2^{n}\Lambda^{2n-3}}\,(2n)(2n-1)(2n-2)\,v^{2n-3}.

(37)

For

interaction followed
by

, together with the
Sh
mixing followed by the
hhVV
contact term. Their sum
scales as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(S\to hW_{L}^{+}W_{L}^{-})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{c_{n}}{2^{n}\Lambda^{2n-3}}\,2n(2n-2)\,v^{2n-3},

(38)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(S\to hZ_{L}Z_{L})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{c_{n}}{2^{n}\Lambda^{2n-3}}\,2n(2n-2)\,v^{2n-3}.

(39)

Likewise, the four-body amplitudes in unitary gauge will also reproduce the same combinatorial pattern as in the Goldstone-basis analysis, once the longitudinal electroweak gauge bosons are identified with the corresponding Goldstone modes in the high-energy limit. We therefore do not present the explicit expressions here. One can also check that the operators
\partial_{\mu}S\,\partial^{\mu}(H^{\dagger}H)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(S\to hh):\mathrm{Br}(S\to W^{+}W^{-}):\mathrm{Br}(S\to ZZ)=1:2:1\penalty\ .

Appendix B
Loop Effect from EFT Operator

Within the class of “busy” operators, a lower-dimensional interaction is generically induced by contracting one pair of
H^{\dagger}H
fields in the higher-dimensional operator
\frac{c_{n}}{\Lambda^{2n-3}}S(H^{\dagger}H)^{n}
. The corresponding loop integral is quadratically divergent and is naturally cut off at
\Lambda
,

\displaystyle I=\frac{n^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{16\pi^{2}},

(41)

where the factor
n^{2}
accounts for the number of ways to close one
H^{\dagger}H
pair. The induced correction to the lower-dimensional operator scales as

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{O}_{n-1}\sim\frac{n^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\frac{c_{n}}{\Lambda^{2n-3}}S(H^{\dagger}H)^{n-1}.

(42)

Parametrically, this modifies the decay amplitudes by
\sim\frac{n^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{v^{2}}
. For
n=2
, taking
\mathcal{O}_{n-1}
to be dominated by the loop-induced contribution, the amplitudes for
S\to hh/W_{L}W_{L}/Z_{L}Z_{L}
become

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(S\to hh)

\displaystyle=c_{2}\frac{v^{2}}{\Lambda}\left[3+\frac{4}{16\pi^{2}}\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{v^{2}}\right],

(43)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(S\to W_{L}W_{L})=\mathcal{M}(S\to Z_{L}Z_{L})

\displaystyle=c_{2}\frac{v^{2}}{\Lambda}\left[1+\frac{4}{16\pi^{2}}\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{v^{2}}\right].

(44)

This estimate is purely EFT-based, but it suggests that the correction is sensitive to the heavy degrees of freedom that generate the operator. As discussed in the next section, in many UV completions the loop-induced contribution is proportional to the heavy mass scale with logarithmic running.
