Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

The DeepFarm project, led by Principle Power Inc (PPI), focused on design, modeling and
development of innovative mooring and anchoring systems for semi-submersible floating wind
turbine platforms in a deep water, commercial-size wind farm. It explored the possibility of
sharing anchors amongst the floating platforms within the farm, and considers site conditions
representative of the West Coast and Hawaii, with a variety of water depths, as case studies to
ensure that the sensitivity to water depth and park size are well understood. NREL and PPI
worked together to develop and apply NREL’s computational tools for modeling floating wind
farm dynamics and costs.

NREL’s role in DeepFarm centered on developing and applying its computational tools related
to mooring system dynamics modeling and floating wind farm cost modeling. NREL added a
farm-level sea state capability to FAST.Farm to properly simulate wave propagation across a
wind farm. NREL enhanced its mooring dynamics model MoorDyn to support seabed friction
and non-uniform seabed bathymetry so that these factors can be represented in FAST.Farm
simulations. Lastly, NREL applied its ORBIT offshore balance-of-station model to perform cost
analyses of the developed floating wind farm designs.

Summary of Research Results:

Task N1: Spatially coherent sea state modeling in FAST.Farm/HydroDyn

To properly represent the timing of wave loads across the wind farm, NREL will add a farm-
level sea state capability to FAST.Farm. This capability will model wave propagation across the
farm, providing local, spatially coherent wave data for each platform. The hydrodynamic model
HydroDyn, within OpenFAST, will then calculate wave loadings on each platform with the
proper phasing, in turn providing accurate phasing of multidirectional mooring line loadings on
shared anchors.

Deliverable: NREL will provide PPI a draft report describing wave coherence improvements to
FAST.Farm/HydroDyn, including a demonstration comparing multi-line anchor loads from
spatially resolved versus random waves.

Estimated completion: Month 12

To properly represent the timing of hydrodynamic loads across the wind farm, NREL added a
farm-level sea state capability to FAST.Farm. These capability models wave propagation across
the farm, providing local, spatially coherent wave data for the loads on each platform and
mooring system. It also includes the capability to model user-specified current profiles to allow
representation of many ocean current conditions. These capabilities allow modeling of current
profiles and wave loadings on each platform with the proper phasing, in turn providing accurate
phasing of multidirectional mooring line loadings on shared anchors. These capabilities were
verified by comparing them with OrcaFlex results.

To properly represent the timing of wave loads across the floating turbines in an array, NREL
expanded the array-level wave propagation capability in FAST.Farm and HydroDyn (the module
responsible for modeling the hydrodynamics on each floating wind turbine) to work with user-
specified wave-elevation time series in OpenFAST (sometimes referred to as the “WaveMod 5”
option). This option historically allowed OpenFAST to precisely match the wave conditions of a
pre-existing scenario, whether from experiments or a different simulation. The expanded
capability applies phase shifts to represent how the user-specified waves propagate across the
array. It is now possible for FAST.Farm to match user-specified wave conditions on the full
array scale. This enables array-level verification and validation with other data sources, such as
OrcaFlex. Figure 1 shows a verification comparison between the new model results (labeled
“OpenFAST”) and results from OrcaFlex for the wave elevation at a point 1500 m behind the
reference point at which the wave elevations are originally supplied. As can be seen, after about
500 seconds, the agreement is almost identical. The disagreement near the beginning is an
unavoidable limitation when reconstructing the sea state based on a wave elevation time series at
a single point. At the simulation start time, no information is available about the wave elevation
beyond the reference point because those waves would have propagated from points that passed
the origin at an earlier point in time. As such, points that are further in the wave propagation
direction will not properly match the wave field until the waves have time to propagate from the
origin to that point. Notwithstanding this limitation, the figure confirms that the approach works
accurately.

 Figure 1. Wave elevation time series calculated for a location 1500 m behind the reference point

Fully accounting for wave loads on the mooring system of a floating wind array with shared
anchors requires calculating wave kinematics (wave-induced water motions) across the entire
area that the mooring lines cover. A capability to allow this has been added to MoorDyn in a way
that is compatible with the “WaveMod 5” approach described above. Just like the HydroDyn
module, the MoorDyn module can now load in a user-specified wave-elevation time series and
process it to compute a Fourier transform of the wave elevation of the origin. From there,
MoorDyn uses the same linear wave theory as HydroDyn to compute the wave-induced water
particle velocities and accelerations at grid points throughout the computational domain as
specified by the user. These fluid velocities and accelerations are then used when calculating the
hydrodynamic forces on the mooring lines.

Current loads can be significant on a floating wind array, including on the floating platform and
on the mooring lines and power cables. Current loads were already included in the HydroDyn
module with constant or predefined current profiles, but more flexible profile options were
needed. MoorDyn has been expanded to support user-specified current profiles that apply across
the entire mooring system as well as any dynamic power cables modeled in MoorDyn. Users can
now specify current velocities in the x and y directions as a function of water depth, allowing full
customization of the current profile. These current velocities are added to any wave-induced
velocities when computing the total drag loads on the mooring lines and dynamic cables.

Task N2: Seabed bathymetry and friction models in FAST.Farm/MoorDyn

To provide more complete seabed interaction detail, which is necessary for assessing the
complex multi-directional loads on multi-line anchors, NREL will enhance its mooring dynamics
model MoorDyn to support seabed friction and non-uniform bathymetry. Seabed friction models,
which have been tested in a stand-alone version of MoorDyn, will be completed and added
within OpenFAST/FAST.Farm. To account for bathymetric features that may be significant over
a wind farm’s total footprint, capabilities for seabed slope and depth changes will be added to
MoorDyn. These features will provide needed accuracy improvements, both in full-farm loads
analyses and in isolated mooring calculations within the optimization processes of Tasks N4 and
N5.

Deliverable: NREL will provide PPI a draft report describing seabed bathymetry and friction

improvements to FAST.Farm/MoorDyn, including a demonstration of multi-line anchor loads
with and without new seabed interaction features.

Estimated completion: Month 12

 4

A seabed bathymetry capability was developed that uses a rectangular grid to represent variation
in water depth. This bathymetry implementation allows for any form of seabed geometry and is
referenced by the MoorDyn model to determine the seabed contact at each point along a mooring
line. A seabed friction model was added to MoorDyn that represents the friction force as the
product of a kinetic friction coefficient and the seabed contact normal force. The friction is
calculated as a function of velocity with saturation to approximate a constant friction force. To
work with a sloped seabed, the calculation of the seabed contact normal force was modified to
reflect the local normal vector of whatever seabed panel the mooring line node is contacting.
Both axial and transverse components of the friction force on a mooring line are represented in
the model. With this approach, mooring lines on the seabed experience realistic three-
dimensional forces in response to contact with the seabed. Figure 2 gives an example from the
new seabed contact capability, with the mooring lines resting along a sloped seabed and taking a
curved shape along the seabed. See Housner et al. (2024) for more information.

Task N5: Cost modeling

NREL, with assistance from UMass, will conduct a comprehensive levelized cost of energy
(LCOE) study of the mooring system designs developed in DeepFarm Task 4 to estimate the cost
savings of the shared anchor designs relative to the conventional designs. The primary model
used for this analysis will be the Offshore Regional Balance-of-system Installation Tool
(ORBIT), an open source, bottom-up cost model developed by NREL to evaluate installation
costs, durations, and weather delays. NREL will also use its O&M model (currently under
development) to evaluate maintenance costs for the different mooring system designs. NREL
will determine annual energy production at the sites specified by PPI using FLORIS, a steady
state wake modeling tool. Combining these separate estimates for balance-of-system costs, O&M
costs, and Annual Energy Production (AEP), coupled with values for turbine costs and financing
rates tracked by NREL with feedback from PPI, will permit LCOE to be calculated for the
project sites.

As part of this task, NREL will update the model assumptions related to vessel characteristics
and charter rates, operational limits, and installation processes to accurately capture the
differences between the drag-embedment and suction anchor installation and capital costs.
Associated partners UMass, Aker, Scana, along with PPI, will provide detailed component cost
values and will consult on appropriate installation and maintenance methodologies that NREL
will use to inform the ORBIT and O&M models. PPI will provide proprietary, relative LCOE
values to NREL to cross-validate the LCOE calculations.

Deliverable: NREL will provide PPI a draft report on cost estimation of the developed open-
source mooring systems and farm configurations, including cost trade-offs of shared-anchor and
conventional mooring systems for case study sites.

Estimated completion: Month 28

NREL performed a levelized cost of energy (LCOE) study of the mooring system designs
developed in DeepFarm to estimate the cost savings of the shared anchor designs relative to the
conventional designs. The primary model used for this analysis was the Offshore Regional
Balance-of-system Installation Tool (ORBIT), an open source, bottom-up cost model developed
by NREL to evaluate installation costs, durations, and weather delays. NREL updated the model
assumptions related to vessel characteristics and charter rates, operational limits, and installation
processes to accurately capture the differences between the drag-embedment and suction anchor
installation and capital costs.

The study assumed a constant water depth of 850 meters (m) and a site near the Humboldt Bay
Wind Energy Area, centered on 40.13330 degrees north latitude, 124.73094 degrees west
longitude. The floating wind farms use 15-megawatt (MW) wind turbines and semisubmersible
platforms. Each farm had three mooring lines per platform, and either individual or shared
anchors. Two sizes of floating wind farms were considered: 6 turbines and 100 turbines. Two
layouts were compared in each case: one with individual anchors and one with shared anchors.

One offshore substation is included in the pilot-scale floating wind farms and two offshore
substations are included in the gigawatt-scale floating wind farms. In the shared anchor case,
each anchor is attached to either one, two, or three mooring lines depending on its position in the

shared anchors would make. The shared anchor failure and replacement rate was modified
relative to individual anchors by a factor of 2.82. Annual energy production of each farm was
calculated using FLORIS, a steady-state wake modeling tool, for the wind resource at the site.
