The GNB3 C825T Polymorphism Is Associated With Decreased Risk of Recurrence of Large‐Artery Atherosclerotic Acute Ischemic Stroke
Authors: Ran Yan, Xin Qiu, Yalun Dai, Kaixuan Yang, Hongqiu Gu, Yong Jiang, Si Cheng, Xia Meng, Yilong Wang, Xingquan Zhao, Hao Li, Yongjun Wang, Zixiao Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground GNB3 C825T polymorphism affects G protein–coupled receptor signaling, influencing platelet function, inflammation, and cardiovascular risk. This study aimed to investigate the unknown effects of the GNB3 C825T polymorphism on recurrent stroke risk in large‐artery atherosclerosis and its potential interaction with antiplatelet therapy.MethodsUsing the CNSR‐III (Third China National Stroke Registry) data, we analyzed 1233 patients with large‐artery atherosclerotic acute ischemic stroke enrolled within 72 hours of onset. Whole‐genome sequencing was performed to identify the GNB3 C825T polymorphism. Cox regression analysis was used to assess its association with 1‐year recurrent ischemic stroke after adjusting for vascular risk factors. Associations with antiplatelet regimens and mediating effects of inflammatory biomarkers in the overall cohort were also evaluated.ResultsCarriers of the GNB3 825T‐allele (CT/TT, 72.7%) exhibited a significantly lower risk of 1‐year recurrent ischemic stroke than noncarriers (adjusted hazard ratio, 0.67 [95% CI, 0.48–0.93]; P=0.02). In exploratory analyses, the association was observed in the aspirin monotherapy subgroup, but the interaction with aspirin use was not significant. Elevated baseline interleukin‐6 partially attenuated this genetic protection in the overall cohort (mediation proportion, –9.82%; P=0.02).ConclusionsThe GNB3 825T‐allele was associated with a reduced risk of recurrent stroke in patients with large‐artery atherosclerotic acute ischemic stroke. In exploratory analyses, this association was observed in the aspirin monotherapy subgroup; however, the formal interaction with aspirin use was not statistically significant. These findings support a potential prognostic role of the GNB3 C825T polymorphism and warrant further validation in independent cohorts.Keywords: acute ischemic stroke, aspirin, GNB3 C825T polymorphism, GNB3 gene, interleukin‐6, large‐artery atherosclerosisSubject Categories: Ischemic Stroke, Biomarkers

Large‐artery atherosclerotic (LAA) stroke is the most common subtype of acute ischemic stroke (AIS) in China 1 and is associated with a high risk of recurrence. 2 , 3 Atherosclerosis, the underlying cause of LAA stroke, is a chronic inflammatory process driven by dysregulated lipid metabolism. Inflammatory cytokines, such as interleukin‐6, are central in driving plaque instability and thrombosis. 4 , 5 , 6 The rupture of an atherosclerotic plaque exposes highly thrombogenic subendothelial matrices, triggering intense platelet activation and aggregation mediated by G proteins, a pivotal mechanism in atherothrombosis. Consequently, antiplatelet agents targeting platelet activation pathways, such as aspirin (a cyclooxygenase‐1 inhibitor) and clopidogrel (a P2Y12 receptor blocker), represent the cornerstone of secondary prevention in LAA stroke. 7 Nevertheless, the risk of residual recurrence persists despite performing guideline‐directed therapy. Genetic polymorphisms (eg, in PEAR1, PTGS1, and CYP2C19) can significantly contribute to this variability in treatment efficacy. 8 , 9

The GNB3 gene encodes the β3 subunit of heterotrimeric G proteins. The GNB3 C825T polymorphism produces a truncated, constitutively active Gβ3 protein (p.Trp65Ter) that enhances G protein–coupled receptor signaling. 10 , 11 Beyond platelet biology, GNB3 C825T polymorphism also significantly influences lipid metabolism and obesity, key risk factors for LAA stroke. Epidemiological studies have associated the GNB3 C825T T‐allele with incident hypertension, carotid intima‐media thickening, and subclinical atherosclerosis across diverse populations. However, its relationship with the first‐ever stroke remains inconsistent across ethnicities, showing increased risk in Europeans but controversial or context‐dependent effects in Asians. 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 Importantly, its role in recurrent stroke and potential relationship with antiplatelet therapy in patients with LAA remains unclear.

To address this gap, we analyzed data from the CNSR‐III (Third China National Stroke Registry) to investigate whether the GNB3 C825T polymorphism is associated with the risk of recurrent ischemic stroke (IS) in patients with LAA‐AIS and whether it is associated with differential outcomes according to commonly prescribed antiplatelet regimens in exploratory analyses.

Methods

Data Availability and Access

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Subjects

This study used data from CNSR‐III, a nationwide, prospective cohort consisting of 15 166 patients with AIS or transient ischemic attack across 201 Chinese hospitals between August 2015 and March 2018. For the present genetic analysis, we selected a subset of adult participants from CNSR‐III who met the following criteria: (1) index event confirmed as AIS due to LAA, as defined by the TOAST (Trial of Org 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment) classification system 16 (specifically >50% stenosis in the extracranial carotid artery, vertebral artery, or a major intracranial artery supplying the ischemic territory), given its relevance to platelet activation; (2) enrollment within 72 hours of symptom onset; and (3) availability of qualified whole‐genome sequencing data. Based on these criteria, 1233 patients with LAA‐AIS and evaluable genomic data were included in the final analysis. A participant selection flowchart is presented in Figure S1.

Individual‐level data encompassing clinical phenotypes, pathogenetic classifications, neuroimaging findings, biomarker levels, clinical outcomes, and whole‐genome sequencing results were prospectively collected according to the CNSR‐III protocol. The detailed methodology for data collection has been published previously. 17 The CNSR‐III study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Tiantan Hospital (Institutional Review Board Approval Number: KY2015‐001‐01) and adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legal representatives.

Laboratory Analyses

Fasting venous blood samples were collected, transported under cold‐chain conditions to the central laboratory at Beijing Tiantan Hospital, and stored at −80 °C. Standardized assays were used to measure MCP‐1 (monocyte chemoattractant protein‐1), hsCRP (high‐sensitivity C‐reactive protein), interleukin‐1 receptor antagonist, and interleukin‐6, as key mediators in atherosclerosis and thromboinflammation.

Genotyping

Total genomic DNA was isolated from white blood cells of each sample using a Magnetic Blood Genomic DNA Kit (DP329, TIANGEN Biotech Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) with a KingFisher Flex system (Thermo Scientific Co., Waltham, MA) for automated extraction. Whole‐genome sequencing was conducted on a BGISEQ‐500 platform (BGI Genomics, Shenzhen, China) with an average depth of >30× per subject. The allele and genotype distributions for GNB3 C825T conformed to Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium (P=0.864), indicating no significant population stratification or genotyping error bias.

Clinical Outcomes

At the 1‐year follow‐up, trained research coordinators conducted telephone interviews to assess the patient outcomes. The primary outcome was the presence of IS recurrence. The secondary outcomes were combined vascular events within 1 year, including transient ischemic attack, nonfatal stroke recurrence (IS, cerebral hemorrhage, or subarachnoid hemorrhage; excluding in‐hospital stroke progression), cerebrovascular intervention, nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal heart failure, cardiovascular intervention, and cardiovascular death. 18 Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage within 1 year was also assessed as a safety outcome.

For antiplatelet therapy subgroup analysis, patients were categorized into mutually exclusive groups based on discharge medications and 1‐year follow‐up status: (1) aspirin monotherapy: aspirin alone prescribed at discharge and maintained during follow‐up; (2) clopidogrel monotherapy: clopidogrel alone prescribed at discharge and maintained during follow‐up; and (3) dual antiplatelet therapy: both aspirin and clopidogrel prescribed at discharge and continued during the follow‐up.

Statistical Analysis

Quality control was performed using the PLINK version 1.9 software. Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium analysis for the GNB3 C825T polymorphism was conducted using the Haploview software (http://www.broadinstitute.org/haploview) and PLINK version 1.9, with deviation from Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium assessed using χ2 tests. Continuous variables were tested for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed variables were presented as mean±SD and compared using Student's t test, while nonnormally distributed variables were expressed as median (interquartile range) and analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies (percentages) and compared using χ2 or Fisher's exact tests, as appropriate.

Multivariable Cox proportional hazards models were used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% CIs for the association between the GNB3 C825T polymorphism (noncarrier [CC] versus carrier [CT/TT]) and recurrent IS and combined vascular events at 1 year. Covariates were selected a priori on the basis of literature review and clinical relevance, including age, sex, current smoking, heavy drinking, hypertension, dyslipidemia, coronary artery disease, admission National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, and onset‐to‐blood draw time. 19 , 20 Cumulative event rates were visualized using Kaplan–Meier curves (unadjusted) and adjusted survival curves, with group differences assessed using log‐rank tests and multivariable Cox models, respectively. Adjusted survival curves were generated from the multivariable Cox models by setting covariates to their mean values (for continuous variables) or reference categories (for categorical variables), providing covariate‐adjusted estimates of cumulative event rates. Additionally, an additive genetic model was applied to evaluate the potential gene–dose effect of the GNB3 C825T polymorphism on recurrent IS at 1 year, specifically among patients on aspirin monotherapy. Formal interaction analyses were performed to evaluate effect modification by aspirin use and clopidogrel use, with P values for interaction calculated using Cox proportional hazards models. Given the exploratory nature and lack of correction for multiple comparisons, these interaction tests should be interpreted as hypothesis‐generating.

For subgroup analyses of antiplatelet therapy, patient group definitions are provided in the Clinical Outcomes section. Within each subgroup, multivariable Cox regression was used to assess the association between GNB3 C825T and outcomes. In addition to the main model (model 1, adjusting for the core covariates listed above), a sensitivity analysis adjusting for additional covariates (model 2) was performed. Covariates for model 2 were selected on the basis of significant between‐group differences across different antiplatelet therapy subgroups in Table S1, including age, body mass index, hypertension, cerebral hemorrhage, National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale at admission, absolute lymphocyte count, fibrinogen, hsCRP, and interleukin‐6.

A mediation analysis was performed using the R package mediation to assess whether inflammatory biomarkers (interleukin‐6, interleukin‐1 receptor antagonist, hsCRP, and MCP‐1) mediated the association between GNB3 C825T and recurrent stroke. The causal assumptions underlying the mediation analysis are summarized in the directed acyclic graph (Figure S7). A Weibull model was used to estimate the total effect of the polymorphism, whereas linear models were applied to assess its effect on each biomarker, adjusting for model 1 covariates. Statistical significance was determined using 100 simulations; the average causal mediation effect (95% CI excluding 0) and the total effect (P<0.05) were considered significant. The mediated proportion was calculated as the average causal mediation effect/total effect.

All analyses were 2‐sided, and statistical significance was set at P<0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC).

Results

Baseline Characteristics

In total, 1233 patients with LAA‐AIS enrolled within 72 hours of symptom onset were included in this study. Stratification by GNB3 C825T polymorphism identified 336 noncarriers (CC, 27.3%) and 897 carriers (CT, n=612, 49.6%; TT, n=285, 23.1%). The baseline characteristics stratified by the GNB3 C825T polymorphism are presented in Table 1. The mean age of the overall cohort was 62.8±11.1 years, and 72.6% were men. Overall, demographic features, vascular risk factors, and most laboratory parameters were comparable between carriers and noncarriers. The only significant difference was a modestly higher baseline interleukin‐6 level in carriers than in noncarriers (5.3±5.0 versus 4.6±4.1 pg/mL, P=0.04).

Association of GNB3 C825T Polymorphism With 1‐Year Clinical Outcomes

At the 1‐year follow‐up, recurrent IS occurred in 53 of 336 noncarriers (15.8%) compared with 100 of 897 GNB3 C825T carriers (11.2%). Carriers exhibited a significantly reduced risk of IS recurrence (adjusted HR, 0.67 [95% CI, 0.48–0.93]; P=0.02) after adjusting for sex, age, smoking, heavy drinking, hypertension, dyslipidemia, coronary artery disease, National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score at admission, and onset‐to‐blood draw time (Table 2). This pattern was consistent in both unadjusted Kaplan–Meier analysis (log‐rank P=0.02; Figure 1A) and covariate‐adjusted survival curves (Figure 1B).

Exploratory Analyses According to Antiplatelet Regimen

To further examine whether the observed association between the GNB3 C825T polymorphism and recurrent stroke risk differed across antiplatelet treatment strata, we conducted exploratory subgroup analyses according to antiplatelet regimen. Baseline characteristics of carriers and noncarriers within each antiplatelet subgroup are shown in Tables S2 through S4. Among patients receiving aspirin monotherapy, carriers of the 825T allele had a lower risk of recurrent ischemic stroke at 1 year (model 1: adjusted HR, 0.45 [95% CI, 0.28–0.73]; P<0.01; model 2: adjusted HR, 0.56 [95% CI, 0.33–0.95]; P=0.03). No significant associations were observed with clopidogrel monotherapy or dual antiplatelet therapy (Figure 2). In an additive model restricted to aspirin monotherapy, each additional copy of the 825T allele was associated with a progressively lower risk of recurrence (adjusted HR per T allele, 0.52 [95% CI, 0.36–0.74]; P<0.01; Table 3). Mediating Effect of Inflammatory Biomarkers

Mediation analysis of the 4 biomarkers (interleukin‐6, interleukin‐1 receptor antagonist, hsCRP, and MCP‐1) revealed that only baseline interleukin‐6 was a potential mediator of the GNB3 C825T–protection relationship. For recurrent IS at 1 year, interleukin‐6 acted as a suppressor, masking 9.82% of the total protective effect (mediated proportion=−9.82%; P=0.02; Figure 4). No significant mediation was detected for interleukin‐1receptor antagonist, hsCRP, or MCP‐1 (all P>0.05; Figure S8).

Discussion

In this study, we observed that carriers of the GNB3 C825T polymorphism had a significantly reduced risk of IS recurrence and combined vascular events within 1 year among patients with LAA‐AIS. This protective association was evident only in the aspirin monotherapy subgroup and absent in those receiving dual antiplatelet therapy. Furthermore, interleukin‐6 partially mediated the association between GNB3 C825T and 1‐year stroke recurrence.

LAA‐AIS is the predominant IS subtype in Asian populations and carries a substantially higher recurrence risk compared with other pathogeneses. 3 In our cohort, the recurrence rate was 15.8% among GNB3 C825T noncarriers and 11.1% among GNB3 C825T carriers, slightly lower than previously reported rates in China, 21 underscoring the persistent need for improved secondary prevention. Dyslipidemia promotes plaque vulnerability through necrotic core expansion, fibrous cap thinning, and intraplaque hemorrhage. 22 Notably, residual lipid‐driven risk persists despite statin therapy, attributable to impaired cholesterol efflux, proinflammatory lipid derivatives, and genetic susceptibility. Inflammatory mediators such as interleukin‐6 further destabilize plaques. 6 , 23 Paradoxically, although 825T‐allele carriers exhibited modestly elevated baseline interleukin‐6 levels (5.3 versus 4.6 pg/mL, P=0.04), they demonstrated a significantly reduced recurrence risk (adjusted HR, 0.67). This finding suggests that the role of interleukin‐6 in stroke recurrence may be more complex than a straightforward proinflammatory effect and warrants further investigation into the specific immune context in which it operates. 24

The 825T‐allele encodes a truncated, constitutively active Gβ3s protein (p.Trp65Ter) that enhances G protein–coupled receptor signaling cascades. 25 Over time, this gain‐of‐function variant promotes several proatherogenic pathways: It drives aberrant adipogenesis leading to obesity, enhances α2‐adrenoceptor–mediated vasoconstriction contributing to hypertension, 26 and is associated with diabetes and metabolic syndrome. 27

Collectively, these mechanisms accelerate atherosclerosis development and progression. However, our findings are consistent with a protective role in the acute phase after LAA stroke, potentially involving direct effects on thrombosis and inflammation. Dusse et al demonstrated that the GNB3 825T allele attenuates ADP‐induced platelet aggregation in human whole blood. 28 This finding presents a mechanistic basis for our observation that the T allele may reduce thrombus formation after plaque rupture, a key event leading to recurrence. Notably, the effect of the GNB3 C825T polymorphism on platelet function is highly stimulus dependent and complex, as it has also been associated with enhanced reactivity to other agonists. 29 In addition, enhanced Gi signaling via Gβ3s may potentiate anti‐inflammatory G protein–coupled receptor pathways, such as those mediated by adenosine A2A receptor activation, thereby suppressing platelet–leukocyte aggregate formation and vascular inflammation. 30 , 31 , 32 These mechanisms may collectively contribute to the reduced risk of recurrence observed.

In subgroup analyses, the GNB3 C825T polymorphism was associated with lower recurrence risk only in the aspirin monotherapy subgroup. Formal interaction tests showed no significant interaction with aspirin use or clopidogrel use. However, when directly comparing aspirin monotherapy versus dual antiplatelet therapy, a significant interaction was observed, whereas no such interaction was found for clopidogrel monotherapy versus dual therapy. This pattern indicates that the association was not drug specific but regimen dependent; it manifests under aspirin monotherapy but is masked under dual therapy. Notably, the COMPRESS (Combination of Clopidogrel and Aspirin for Prevention of Early Recurrence in Acute Atherothrombotic Stroke) trial demonstrated that clopidogrel plus aspirin was not superior to aspirin alone in preventing new ischemic lesions in patients with LAA, 33 which is consistent with our observation that the association of the 825T allele was not evident in the dual‐therapy group. The study by Dusse et al demonstrated that the 825T allele attenuates ADP‐induced platelet aggregation. This implies that P2Y12‐mediated platelet activation might be intrinsically blunted in T‐allele carriers. Consequently, the additional platelet inhibition provided by clopidogrel may offer limited further benefit, thereby masking the genetic advantage that becomes apparent under aspirin monotherapy. 28 Thus, while these exploratory findings are hypothesis generating, they suggest that aspirin monotherapy allows the genetic advantage to emerge, whereas dual antiplatelet therapy overrides it.

Intriguingly, interleukin‐6 acted as a statistical suppressor, masking ≈9.82% of the GNB3 C825T T‐allele's true protective effect. This finding may suggest a temporally biphasic nature of the enhanced Gi signaling driven by the GNB3 C825T variant. G protein–coupled receptor –Gi pathways can exert context‐dependent dual effects in cerebrovascular disease. 34 In the acute phase, GNB3 C825T–enhanced Gi signaling may transiently contribute to interleukin‐6 release and neuroinflammation. Conversely, during subacute and recovery stages, this signaling axis may facilitate inflammation resolution and plaque stabilization, thereby conferring long‐term protection against recurrence. 32 , 34 Thus, the acute inflammatory burden could partially obscure the sustained vascular benefit observed over the subsequent year. A limitation of this study is the absence of longitudinal cytokine measurements to capture this dynamic shift.

Our findings suggest the potential use of GNB3 genotyping as a prognostic marker to identify patients at higher risk of recurrence, though its utility for guiding antiplatelet therapy remains hypothetical given the lack of significant interaction. Beyond its potential as a prognostic biomarker, our findings raise the intriguing possibility that modulating the Gβ3s‐mediated signaling pathway could represent a future therapeutic avenue for secondary stroke prevention, a hypothesis that warrants further investigation. Notably, the association between the GNB3 C825T T‐allele and reduced recurrence risk was observed in patients receiving aspirin monotherapy but not in those on dual antiplatelet therapy, raising the possibility that the genetic effect may be masked by more potent platelet inhibition. While formal interaction tests did not confirm a significant gene–drug interaction, this exploratory observation provides a biological rationale for further investigation. Future randomized clinical trials incorporating prospective genotyping strategies will be necessary to determine whether genotype‐guided antiplatelet approaches could improve secondary prevention in LAA stroke. Furthermore, the T allele was not associated with an increased risk of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage, supporting its safety profile across therapies.

Despite the significant findings uncovered above, this study had certain limitations. First, as an observational study, it carries an inherent risk of confounding bias, particularly confounding by indication given the nonrandomized choice of antiplatelet regimens, when assessing associations between genetic variants and treatment outcomes, with an evidence level lower than that of randomized controlled trials. However, the large sample size, multicenter design, and rigorous multivariable adjustments enhanced the reliability of our findings. Future randomized controlled trials are required to validate the clinical benefits of genotype‐guided therapies. Second, direct validation of Gβ3s‐mediated hyperreactivity via platelet function testing is lacking. Third, our cohort exclusively consisted of Chinese patients. Given that the 825T‐allele frequency exhibits significant ethnic variation (50% in Asian individuals, 30% in European individuals, and 80% in African individuals), 35 validation in multiethnic cohorts is imperative. Fourth, only a restricted panel of inflammatory biomarkers was measured (eg, interleukin‐1 receptor antagonist as a surrogate for unmeasured interleukin‐1β) at a single baseline time point. This limits causal inference and precludes definitive conclusions about a mediating role of interleukin‐6, although we addressed the main assumptions of mediation analysis. Residual or unmeasured confounding may still affect the mediator–outcome relationship. Future studies incorporating longitudinal cytokine measurements and more comprehensive inflammatory profiling will be necessary to validate and extend the present findings. Fifth, our study cohort comprised only patients with an index stroke. Thus, our findings are strictly applicable to the secondary prevention setting and require validation in appropriate cohorts for extrapolation to primary prevention.

In conclusion, the GNB3 C825T T‐allele was associated with a significantly reduced 1‐year risk of recurrent stroke in this cohort of patients with LAA‐AIS. This association was also observed in patients receiving aspirin monotherapy in exploratory analyses, but the interaction between the polymorphism and aspirin use was not statistically significant. Elevated baseline interleukin‐6 levels were associated with partial attenuation of this genetic association, suggesting a potential role of inflammatory pathways. Further randomized trials are needed to clarify the clinical utility of GNB3 genotyping for guiding antiplatelet therapy.
