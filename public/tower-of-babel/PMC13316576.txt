Experience in treating thrombosis after adjustment of a PICC catheter
Authors: Jin Zhang, Hao Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPeripheral central catheter related deep vein thrombosis (PICC-DVT) has been proven to be a significant cause of unplanned extubation. We reported a case of 78-year-old patient, whose PICC was found kinked. After the adjustment, the blood flowed smoothly again. On the next day, catheter was found to be occluded and Color Doppler Flow Imaging (CDFI) shown that it occurred multiple deep vein thrombosis. Based on this case and current literature, CDFI could be considered within the first 1-2 weeks after PICC placement and during follow-up in high-risk patients, particularly given that thrombosis may occur early and may be clinically silent.Keywords: Peripheral central catheter, Deep vein thrombosis, Vascular ultrasound, Color Doppler flow imaging

Introduction

Peripheral central catheter (PICC) is widely used for patients who require long-term intravenous infusion, especially chemotherapy drugs. Unplanned extubation is a serious adverse event, and PICC related deep vein thrombosis (PICC-DVT) has been shown to be a significant cause. Currently, in most regions, determining the presence of DVT often relies on the experience of specialized nurses during weekly maintenance, which is not reliable. Color Doppler flow imaging (CDFI) can effectively detect DVT early, but it is not a routine test. There is still controversy over the PICC-DVT monitoring project and frequency. We received a patient with a PICC, who first discovered the catheter was folded. After correction, she discovered DVT the next day. After investigating medical records and reviewing literature, we have summarized this report.

Case presentation

The patient, a 78-year-old female with a previous history of type 2 diabetes, was admitted to Nanjing Jiangbei Hospital because of “Follow-up for ovarian tumor with lymph node metastasis, diagnosed 3 months ago.” On October 10, 2024, she underwent PICC placement and received maintenance once a week (Fig. 1A). After receiving chemotherapy regimen with paclitaxel + cisplatin twice, on December 3, during routine maintenance operations, it was found that only a small amount of bright red blood during the pullback, nurse determined that the catheter may have kinked. Then the chest X-ray examination proved it (Fig. 1B). After the catheter adjustment by the interventional department, the blood flowed smoothly again. On the next day, the catheter was found to be occluded and CDFI shown that the right basilic vein, axillary vein, and subclavian vein thrombosis (Fig. 1C and D). Then, enoxaparin 1000U was subcutaneously injected, but no blood flow was observed after 24 hours, and patient’s body temperature rose to 38°C. Following the consultation of the interventional department, the PICC was successfully removed after the implantation of the superior vena cava filter (Fig. 2). The patient completed chemotherapy via an alternative central venous access without further thrombotic complications.

This report was approved and monitored by the ethics committee of Nanjing Jiangbei Hospital, and written informed consent was obtained from the patient.

Discussion

PICC is widely used in clinical practice for its safety, comfort, and economy. When DVT occurs, the risk of unplanned extubation will significantly increase. Many studies have shown that there are many factors that can cause blood clots, especially in cancer patients who already have a higher risk of DVT due to their disease [[1], [2], [3]].

At present, there are many studies on the risk factors of PICC-DVT in clinical practice, and the conclusions are not consistent. In summary, gender, age, education, partial thromboplastin activity, platelet count, white blood cell count, d-dimer level and other factors are generally considered important risk factors [[3], [4], [5]]. In this case, prior to the occurrence of DVT, the patient’s platelet and white blood cell counts remained at normal levels, but the level of d-dimer is 12.93 µg/mL before she accepted the PICC, indicating a high risk factor for DVT. But since current research indicates that different d-dimer diagnostic strategies cannot effectively and safely exclude the diagnosis of suspected PICC related upper limb DVT, they cannot serve as a basis for the use of prophylactic anticoagulant drugs [6]. Chen [7] indicated that when a catheter kinks inside a blood vessel, it may lead to thrombosis due to increased local pressure on the vessel wall and obstruction of blood flow velocity [7]. When blood flow in the kinked area of the catheter was obstructed and slowed down, it would upregulate the expression of prothrombotic proteins such as tissue factor and adhesion molecules such as P-selectin, E-selectin, and Von Willebrand factor that trapped white blood cells and platelets, leading to fibrinogen and other plasma proteins promoting the formation of bridges between cells. Subsequently, during the catheter correction, the frictional damage of the catheter to the vascular wall would lead to endothelial damage, caused the recruitment and activation of neutrophils, resulting in the release of various proteolytic enzymes and neutrophil extracellular traps and a complex cross-talk between the complement and contact coagulation pathways [8,9]. So mechanical manipulation of a kinked catheter may act as a trigger for thrombosis in high-risk patients, likely related to endothelial injury and altered flow dynamics.

At the same time, some DVT prediction models have been established and applied in daily work. According to the Khorana risk scoring model, the patient’s score is 1 point, shown that the risk of DVT is relatively low [10]. On the contrary, to the Padua, the patient meets the criteria of age >70 years and active tumor, totaling 4 points, indicating a high-risk situation [11]. So, using different prediction models, the risk of patients developing DVT also varies. Therefore, currently relying on nurse experience or related models cannot accurately reflect the probability of patients developing DVT.

CDFI can effectively detect DVT early. In recent years, there have been relevant reports that about 50% of patients who develop PICC-DVT can continue to use the catheter after using urokinase, but there are still many cases of unplanned extubation due to failed recanalization. This shows that the earlier the thrombus is detected and intervened, the better the benefits can be obtained.

Normally, during the process of PICC care and maintenance, nurses empirically judge whether there is DVT or internal tube rupture based on clinical manifestations such as blood draw speed. When it is determined that there is DVT or dislodgement, nurses will arrange chest X-ray or vascular ultrasound, which is not accurate and timely. Multiple studies have confirmed that early asymptomatic DVT can occur, especially in patients with solid tumors, after PICC placement [[12], [13], [14]]. The team led by Zhang and Xu [15] conducted CDFI testing on 173 tumor patients who underwent PICC placement every other day. They found that 113 patients were diagnosed with DVT within 3 days, and a total of 126 cases occurred within a week. Further research showed that only 31 patients with DVT had clinical manifestations, and regardless of whether they had symptoms or not, the diameter of the PICC lumen in patients with DVT was reduced compared to those who did not [15]. It follows that clinical manifestations cannot serve as a strong basis for determining the existence of DVT, the role of CDFI should be fully emphasized in routine maintenance procedures of PICC.

The patient we reported whose catheter was kinked and had developed multiple deep vein thrombosis. Although intervention correction was given, catheter blockage occurred again. CDFI examination confirmed the presence of DVT, and the catheter was removed ultimately. If CDFI examination could be performed early, DVT could be detected early and anticoagulant therapy would be started as soon as possible, and patients may not need to remove the catheter unplanned. Alternatively, if multiple deep vein thrombosis was detected before intervention, the catheter would be directly removed to alleviate the pain endured by the patient.

Conclusions

The occurrence of PICC-DVT will directly affect the implementation of clinical treatment, increase patients’ economic costs, additional pain, hospitalization time, etc. Early detection and intervention of DVT will effectively prolong the use of PICC. Compression ultrasound is standard for peripheral DVT and is not affected by subjective factors such as nurse experience or patients’ feelings. It should be used regularly in the daily maintenance of PICC. Based on this case and current literature, CDFI could be considered within the first 1-2 weeks after PICC placement and during follow-up in high-risk patients, particularly given that thrombosis may occur early and may be clinically silent. This article is a summary and inference of a single medical record with PICC-DVT related to catheter folded, since the mechanism to the generation of thrombosis is never a single mechanism, its process involves complex pathophysiology involving cellular collaboration and multiple biochemical pathways, so the specific mechanism of thrombus occurrence in such situations needs further research.
