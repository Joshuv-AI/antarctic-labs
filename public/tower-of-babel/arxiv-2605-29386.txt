Polynomial contractions of ℂ d \mathbb{C}^{d} and degree growth
Dmitrii Korshunov

Abstract
We give a simple example of a polynomial contraction automorphism of ℂ d \mathbb{C}^{d} , d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , with unbounded degree growth. Combined with Poincaré–Dulac theorem it provides an algebraic automorphism of ℂ d \mathbb{C}^{d} , d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , which is holomorphically but not algebraically linearizable.



1
Introduction

This note answers a question of Verbitsky and Ornea in connection with their work on GAGA-type theorems for complex manifolds equipped with a holomorphic contraction
[
7
]
.

Definition 1
.

An automorphism
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut\,}\mathbb{C}^{d}
is called a contraction if
0
is the unique fixed point and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma^{n}(x)=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Definition 2
.

Suppose a polynomial automorphism
\gamma
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is given by

(x_{1},\dots x_{d})\mapsto(P_{1}(x_{1},\dots x_{d}),\dots(P_{d}(x_{1},\dots x_{d}))
. We define the degree of
\gamma
to be the largest among degrees of
P_{i}
.

Question 1
.

Is there a polynomial contraction of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, such that degree of
\gamma^{n}
grows to infinity with
n\to\infty
?

For each
d\geq 3
we construct an example of such contraction, and show that for
d=2
they do not exist. The property of an algebraic automorphism to be of bounded degree growth is an algebraic conjugation invariant. On the other hand, Poincaré-Dulac theorem applied to our examples guarantees that they are holomorphically conjugate to a linear map. Thus we obtain examples of automorphisms that are holomorphically conjugate to a linear map but not algebraically conjugate to any linear map.

2
Algebraic groups of automorphisms and bounded degree

We will need the notion of strict algebraicity of a polynomial automorphism, which captures the property of having bounded degree growth.

Definition 3
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut\,}\mathbb{A}^{d}
. If there exists an algebraic action of an algebraic group
\Phi:G\times\mathbb{A}^{d}\to\mathbb{A}^{d}
, such that
\gamma(\cdot)=\Phi(g,\cdot)
for some
g\in G
, then
\gamma
is called strictly algebraic.

Proposition 1
.

A polynomial automorphism
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut\,}\mathbb{A}^{d}
is strictly algebraic if and only if
\sup_{n}\deg\gamma^{n}<+\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{Aut\,}_{\leq e}
be the (constructible) set of the space of all
d
-tuples of polynomials of degree
\leq e
, naturally identified with
\mathbb{A}^{d\binom{d+e}{e}}
. If
\deg\gamma^{n}\leq e
for all
n\geq 0
, then take as
G
the Zariski closure of the set
\{\gamma^{n}:n\geq 0\}
inside
\mathrm{Aut\,}_{\leq e}
. It is left to note that a constructible group over
\mathbb{C}
is actually algebraic.

Another way to see it, is to take the ind-Zariski closure of
\{\gamma^{n}:n\geq 0\}
in
\mathrm{Aut\,}\mathbb{A}^{d}
with its standard structure of an ind-group and see that when degree is bounded it lands to the finite dimensional ind-subgroup, that is an algebraic group. For a comprehensive treatment of groups of automorphisms as ind-groups see
[
5
]
.

To prove the converse, recall that an action of an algebraic group
G
on the coordinate ring of an affine variety is locally finite
[
2
, Proposition 1.9]
. That is, each polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots x_{d}]
belongs to a finite dimensional
G
-invariant subspace
G\cdot f
(indeed, its dimension is bounded from above by
\dim G
). Now choose the coordinate polynomials
x_{1},\dots x_{d}
, and take the smallest subspace of the filtration in which all
G\cdot x_{1},\dots G\cdot x_{d}
lie.

∎

Remark 1
.

If
\gamma
is strictly algebraic then
F\circ\gamma\circ F^{-1}
is also strictly algebraic for any polynomial automorphism
F\in\mathrm{Aut\,}\mathbb{A}^{d}
.

3
Polynomial contractions

Theorem 1
.

Any contraction automorphism
\gamma
of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
has bounded degree growth.

Proof.

By results of Milnor and Friedland
[
4
]
, any
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut\,}\mathbb{C}^{2}
is conjugate to either

1.

Affine, with constant degree-growth.

2.

Elementary, that is of the form
e(x,y)=(ax+P(y),by+c)
, with constant degree growth.

3.

Loxodromic, i.e. a finite composition of Hénon automorphisms
h_{i}(x,y)=(ay+P_{i}(x),x)
,
\deg P_{i}\geq 2
, with exponential degree growth .

On the other hand, by a theorem of Smillie
[
8
]
and Theorem 4.1 in
[
4
]
the topological entropy of a loxodromic automorphism equals the logarithm of its degree. But a contraction does not have periodic points except the origin, hence its topological entropy must be zero. Hence a loxodromic automorphism cannot be a contraction.
∎

Remark 2
.

Note that Cantat and Dujardin
[
3
]
prove that any two holomorphically conjugare loxodromic automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
are algebraically conjugate.

Contrary to the two-dimensional case, in dimension
d>2
there are polynomial contractions with unbounded growth of degree. This example is an affine-triangular automorphism in the sence of
[
1
]
.

Theorem 2
.

An automorphism of
\mathbb{A}^{3}
given by

\gamma:(x,y,z)\mapsto\left(\lambda_{1}(y+xz),\lambda_{2}x,\lambda_{3}z\right),0<\lambda_{i}<\frac{1}{2}

is a contraction and has linear degree growth.

Proof.

Let
(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})\in\mathbb{A}^{3}
be an arbitrary point.
Denote
x_{n}:=\gamma^{n}(x_{0})
. Expanding the recursive relations one obtains

1.

z_{n}=\lambda_{3}^{n}z_{0}

2.

y_{n}=\lambda_{2}x_{n-1}

3.

x_{n}=\lambda_{1}(y_{n-1}+x_{n-1}z_{n-1})=\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{2}(x_{n-2})+\lambda_{3}^{n-1}z_{0}x_{n-1})

For
\lambda_{3}^{n-1}z_{0}<\lambda_{2}
one has
|x_{n}|<\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}(|x_{n-2}|+|x_{n-1}|)
. Now note that
\lambda_{2}(|x_{n-2}|+|x_{n-1}|)<\max\{|x_{n-2}|,|x_{n-1}|\}
, hence
|x_{n}|<\lambda_{1}\max\{|x_{n-1}|,|x_{n-2}|\}
. This sequence converges to zero as
\lambda_{1}^{n-2}\max\{x_{1},x_{0}\}
. Thus
\lim_{n\to+\infty}|x_{n}|=\lim_{n\to+\infty}|y_{n}|=\lim_{n\to+\infty}|z_{n}|=0
, so we proved that
\gamma
is a contraction.

To compute the growth rate of degree, denote
\gamma^{n}:=(F_{n},G_{n},H_{n})
. Now

\deg\gamma^{n+1}=\max\{\deg F_{n+1},\deg G_{n+1},\deg H_{n+1}\}
, where
\deg H_{n+1}=1
,
\deg F_{n+1}=\max\{\deg F_{n}+1,\deg F_{n-1}\}
,
\deg G_{n+1}=\deg F_{n}
. Hence
\deg\gamma^{n+1}=\deg\gamma^{n}+1
, and finally
\deg\gamma^{n}=n+1
.

∎

Remark 3
.

The theorem still holds and its proof goes through for any norm on
\mathbb{A}^{3}
over a normed field.

Remark 4
.

This example can be used to produce a contraction with unbounded degree growth in any higher dimension. Indeed, define it as
\gamma
on the first three coordinates, and by scaling by
\lambda_{i}
,
i>3
, on the rest, with some
0<\lambda_{i}<1
.

Remark 5
.

The Jacobian matrix of this map is

D_{0}\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}z&\lambda_{1}&\lambda_{1}x\\
\lambda_{2}&0&0\\
0&0&\lambda_{3}\end{pmatrix}

Its determinant is constant and equals
-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}
. The eigenvalues of the differential in
0
are
\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}
and
\lambda_{3}
.

4
Holomorphically linearizable automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}^{n}

In this section we will recall the Poincaré–Dulac theorem
[
6
, Theorem 27.38]
.

Definition 4
.

A linear map with eigenvalues
\alpha_{1},\dots\alpha_{d}
is called resonant if for some
i
one has
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots\alpha_{d}^{m_{d}}
, where all
m_{j}\geq 0
and
m_{1}+\dots+m_{d}\geq 2
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\gamma
be an invertible holomorphic contraction of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
centered at
0
, with
n\geq 3
. Assume that the differential

D_{0}\gamma\in GL(T_{0}\mathbb{C}^{n})

is not resonant. Then there exists a holomorphic automorphism

U:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}

such that

U\circ\gamma\circ U^{-1}

is linear.

Recall that the eigenvalues of the differential at zero of the polynomial contractions constructed in the previous section are
\sqrt{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}},-\sqrt{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}},\lambda_{3},\dots\lambda_{d}
. If we chose all
\lambda_{i}
algebraically independent, then eigenvalues cannot satisfy the relations of Definition
4
. Thus we proved

Theorem 4
.

Let
\gamma_{d}
be an automorphism of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
d\geq 3

\gamma:(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dots x_{d})\mapsto\left(\lambda_{1}(x_{2}+x_{1}x_{3}),\lambda_{2}x_{1},\lambda_{3}x_{3},\dots\lambda_{d}x_{d}\right),0<\lambda_{i}<\frac{1}{2}

where
\lambda_{i}
are algebraically independent. Then

1.

There exists a holomorphic diffeomorphism
U
such that
U\circ\gamma_{d}\circ U^{-1}
is linear.

2.

There are no polynomial automorphisms with this property.
