Successful treatment of chronic retained bronchial foreign bodies and secondary bronchial stenosis with balloon bronchoplasty in young patients: A case series with review of literature
Authors: Deependra K. Rai, Dhruv Talwar, Niraj K. Singh, Jisha G. Panicker, Parul Sharma, Sreyashi Pal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTForeign body aspiration, though uncommon, remains a potentially fatal condition associated with significant morbidity and mortality. While children constitute majority of cases, a few occur in adults—particularly in the elderly. However, chronic retained foreign bodies in the young adult population are exceedingly rare, and when encountered, pose unique challenges due to chronic inflammatory changes, excessive granulation tissue, and resultant bronchial stenosis. These factors often complicate retrieval and may necessitate surgical intervention, including lobectomy. In this case series, we present two young patients with chronic retained bronchial foreign bodies (retained for 13 years and 15 years respectively) and associated bronchial stenosis, both of whom were successfully removed with balloon bronchoplasty to dilate the stenotic segment, enabling removal of the foreign body. Notably, both patients were spared lobectomy and its associated morbidity. Both the patients showed clinical and radiological improvement post procedure and are doing well on follow-up. Foreign bodies aspirated during childhood might present in the adulthood after being undetected for years due to neglected symptoms with associated chronic changes making foreign body removal complex. This case series highlights the importance of a complete trial of bronchoscopy guided foreign body removal strategy, including balloon bronchoplasty, in patients with chronic retained foreign bodies and associated bronchial stenosis before proceeding to surgical resection especially in young patients.

INTRODUCTION

Foreign body aspiration was first described in 1897 by Gustav Killian, who used esophagoscope to extract a bone from the right bronchus in a 63-year-old patient.[1]

With the discovery of rigid bronchoscopy, the high mortality rate associated with foreign body aspiration was reduced and it became the most common indication for the procedure until the discovery of flexible bronchoscopy that occurred in the 1970s, which enabled the access to the peripheral airways allowing retrieval of foreign bodies that are lodged in the distal airway with the help of various new specialized instruments.[2] Although majority of cases present in the childhood, adult cases of foreign body aspiration might be encountered with many of them misdiagnosed due to either the unavailability of flexible bronchoscopy at the grassroots level or the lack of suspicion of the clinician due to nonspecific history or the patient being unable to recall the aspiration episode.

Foreign body aspiration in adults accounts for about 25 percent of the cases.[3] Foreign body aspiration in adults may present with a history of a choking event which is often followed by vigorous coughing however in certain cases it may present with symptoms overlapping with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, asthma or an obstructive pneumonia often when the initial event of choking is neglected which is seen mostly in geriatric age group patients having altered mental status.[4]

When the diagnosis of foreign body aspiration is missed, retained foreign body may lead to recurrent incidence of pneumonias, bronchiectasis, haemoptysis, pneumothorax, lung abscesses, pneumomediastinum, or other complications.

The extraction of foreign body should be carried out on urgent basis to relieve the patient of symptoms and prevent permanent complications.

We present two cases of young patients one aged 25 years and one aged 18 years, who came with retained foreign bodies since 13 and 15 years, respectively with bronchial stenosis that was treated successfully with serial bronchial balloon dilation and foreign bodies were removed in both the cases thus saving the patients from a lobectomy which is usual treatment of such cases.

CASE 1

A 25-year-old male, presented in the emergency department with the chief complaint of cough (on and off) for 9 years, Haemoptysis (on and off) for 9 years, right sided chest pain for 9 years, Fever for 20 days and shortness of breath since 6 days.

On asking leading question, patient revealed a weight loss of 6 kgs in 6 months (52 kgs to 46 kgs-11.5%).

There was No history of Night sweats/hoarseness of voice/orthopnoea/PND/Pedal edema.

He had no history of any co morbidities or antitubercular therapy in the past.

He recalled accidental aspiration of a whistle 13 years ago, following that wheezing was audible to him on breathing and he visited a hospital for the same where he was treated with supportive care and endoscopy was conducted however, he was discharged on conservative management. Since the patient has visited multiple hospitals for recurrent haemoptysis however there was only interim relief by tranexamic acid. He was a non-smoker, welder by occupation and had no significant family history.

On examination, patient was conscious and oriented to time place and person, pallor was present.

He was maintaining saturation of 85% on room air with 95% on 4 L of oxygen, respiratory rate was 34/min and use of accessory muscles of respiration was present. On systemic examination there was dull note on percussion in right infrascupular area and right sided infrascapular breath sounds were reduced. Blood investigations of the case showed leukocytosis (White blood cells-15.21 thousand/Microliter), anaemia (haemoglobin 7.2 gm/dl) and raised procalcitonin (procalcitonin 1.99 ng/ml) however, sputum, blood and urine culture showed no growth.

Chest x ray was conducted showing right lower lobe collapse [Figure 1] following that a CECT thorax was conducted that was suggestive of cylindrical hyperdense structure (likely foreign body) in the right bronchus intermedius with lung abscess [Figure 2].

Chest x ray of case 1 suggestive of right lower lobe collapse

Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT) thorax of case 1 showing cylindrical hyperdense structure (likely foreign body) in the right bronchus intermedius with lung abscess

Patient was started on intravenous antibiotics along with oxygen support and was planned for fibreoptic bronchoscopy for an attempt to remove the foreign body after blood transfusion.

Fibreoptic bronchoscopy was conducted and a stricture of about 6 mm was visualized in the right bronchus intermedius [Figure 3], scope could not be negotiated through the same. Fogarty balloon was used to partly dilate the stricture and about 200 ml of pus was drained.

(a) Stricture visualized on the first bronchoscopy for case 1 (b) Foreign body with granulation tissue visualized in second bronchoscopy for case 1 (c) foreign body visualized after serial balloon dilations (up to 12 mm) in the third bronchoscopy for case 1 (d) Argon plasma coagulation (APC) being conducted to remove the extensive granulation tissue surrounding the stricture for case 1

Patient improved post procedure, total leucocyte counts normalized and was weaned off oxygen (maintaining spo2 of 99% on room air). Pus which was aspirated in the first bronchoscopy was negative for CBNAAT and routine cultures. A second bronchoscopy was conducted 10 days later and CRE Balloon was used to dilate the stricture serially up to 11 mm. A foreign body with granulation was visualized [Figure 3]. Attempt was made with forceps to extract the foreign body however it could not be removed. Another bronchoscopy was repeated 5 days later with CRE Balloon dilation up to 12 mm was conducted however due to extensive granulation tissue, the foreign body could not be extracted [Figure 3]. Finally, patient was planned for a final procedure under general anaesthesia 7 days later, APC coagulation was conducted to remove the extensive granulation tissue and serial balloon dilations were conducted up to 15 mm, foreign body was removed with the help of rat tooth forceps [Figures 3 and 4]. Post procedure the patient was stable and was extubated the next morning. He was discharged 4 days after the procedure (after 1 month of hospitalization) with normalization of blood parameters. Currently, the patient is doing well on follow up with no recurrence of symptoms.

CASE 2

An 18-year-old female presented to the outpatient department of pulmonary medicine with the complaint of shortness of breath and cough on and off for 10 years, she also had scanty haemoptysis for 4 months and fever for 2 months.

There was no history of orthopnoea/pedal edema/weight loss/night sweats.

She was being treated as a case of bronchial asthma and was on Long-acting beta 2 agonist and inhaled corticosteroids (medium dose) for the same for 1 year. There was no history of tuberculosis or anti tubercular therapy in the past and no known co morbidities were present.

An HRCT Thorax was conducted, which revealed a small hyperdense structure with central lucency seen in the left lower lobe bronchus measuring 6 × 6 × 8.5 mm with extensive mucoid plugging [Figure 5]. Blood investigations revealed raised total leukocyte count (13.27 thousand/Microliter) with procalcitonin 0.73 ng/ml, while rest of the investigations were within normal limit.

CECT Thorax of case 2 showing a small hyperdense structure with central lucency seen in the left lower lobe bronchus measuring 6 × 6 × 8.5 mm. CECT = Contrast-enhanced computed tomography

On examination she was febrile, maintaining spo2 of 98 on room air with normal general examination. On auscultation there were crept in the left infrascapular region.

Patient was admitted for further evaluation and a fibreoptic bronchoscopy was conducted. A stricture was seen in the left main bronchus with mucous secretions present distal to it [Figure 6]. Upon suctioning the mucus and secretions a foreign body was visualized [Figure 6].

(a) Stricture visualized in left main bronchus on bronchoscopy of case 2 (b) Foreign body visualized through stricture of case 2 after suctioning of mucus (c-e) CRE Balloon dilation conducted through stricture of left main bronchus in case 2 (f) Patent lumen visualized after controlled radial expansion (CRE) Balloon dilation and foreign body removal

An attempt was made to grasp the foreign body and remove it through the stricture that was unsuccessful due to the small size of the stricture and hence a decision of balloon bronchoplasty was taken. After a serial balloon dilation of 8 mm followed by 10 mm the foreign body was successfully extracted that was a whistle [Figures 6 and 7].

Foreign body removed in case 2

Upon a revisit of history with the parents of the patient it was revealed that she had aspirated a whistle when she was 3 years old (which the patient had no recollection of) and she was evaluated with a routine chest x ray at that time that was informed to be normal and hence no further evaluation was conducted at that time.

BAL culture showed growth of E. coli and antibiotics were given according to culture report. A repeat HRCT was conducted that showed clearing of secretions and some bronchiectasis in left lower lobe [Figure 8].

Repeat high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) Thorax of case 2 showing clearing of secretions and foreign body and left lower lobe bronchiectasis

Patient improved clinically during the hospital stay with no fever spikes, normalization of total leukocyte count, and procalcitonin. She was discharged in stable condition after 7 days of hospitalization and is doing well on follow-up.

DISCUSSION

The classical symptom of “penetration syndrome” which is described as a sensation of choking followed by coughing is more commonly seen in children than in adults with only half of the patients recollecting the aspiration episode.[5] The case 1 presented here had a patient presenting with a history of aspiration of foreign body 13 years ago however he underwent an endoscopy for the same due to lack of clear history taking therefore missing the diagnosis of foreign body aspiration. In case 2 the patient had no recollection of her aspiration however upon retrieval of the same, the parents could recollect aspiration of foreign body in her childhood which could have been an important clue for her diagnosis as the patient was having cough and shortness of breath for 10 years and was mis-diagnosed as asthma. This case series highlights the importance of careful history taking and keeping foreign body aspiration as an important differential, while treating a case of repeated pneumonias, shortness of breath and chronic cough.

The location of foreign body impaction may lead to different symptoms.

If the Impaction is in the trachea the physician might find inspiratory stridor with frequent coughing whereas if the impaction is in the lower bronchi such as in our cases patient may present with coughing, wheezing, dyspnoea, or haemoptysis and may be mistaken for an alternative diagnosis

Therefore, a good history taking is integral in management of foreign body aspiration as it might provide a clue about the type of foreign body which might be encountered in the airways.

Work up of a case of foreign body aspiration case includes obtaining a routine chest radiograph, which might be able to diagnose the case in 1/4th of the cases as only a minority of the foreign body might be radiopaque. Majority of the foreign bodies are radiolucent and are not seen on chest radiographs. Indirect findings on the chest radiograph such as atelectasis, hyperinflation, bronchiectasis, or lobar consolidation might be seen in cases of foreign body aspiration. Expiratory films might be of importance in such cases as they might show focal hyperinflation due to the ball valve phenomena seen because of foreign body leading to partial obstruction of airway during inhalation and complete obstruction during expiration. Computed tomography remains the standard and more sensitive than chest radiograph to identify foreign bodies with sensitivity of 100% and specificity of 75% in patients who were being evaluated for foreign body aspiration.

Flexible bronchoscope is the initial and the preferred method used to treat foreign body aspiration in the adults with rigid bronchoscope considered to be the safest in paediatric cases.

There have been a few reports of chronic retained foreign bodies in the airway in literature however only few could be managed conservatively. In a similar case of foreign body aspiration reported by Fola et al.,[6] an 18-year-old foreign body was visualized through bronchoscopy in a 29-year-old female that was complicated with lung abscess, the case was treated with thoracotomy and drainage of the lung abscess and removal of the foreign body which was later revealed to be a pen cap.

Dalvi et al.[7] reported a chronic case of retained foreign body in a 11-year-old male in the left bronchus that was treated with left lower lobectomy after multiple failed attempts of foreign body removal were made.

Yousef et al.[8] reported a case of 16-year-old male with 4-year-old foreign body aspiration leading to recurrent pneumonias that was treated ultimately with bilobectomy after unsuccessful visualisation of foreign body through bronchoscopy.

Reddy et al.[9] reported removal of a 12-year-old foreign body however this was conducted using a rigid bronchoscope in a 42-year-old male, who had retained piece of an aluminium beverage container in the left mainstem bronchus for 12 years.

Tewari et al.[10] reported two cases of 2 year and 9 months old foreign bodies that were removed with flexible bronchoscopies, this was the only case series after extensive literature search, which revealed removal of chronic foreign bodies using flexible bronchoscopy however the chronicity of the foreign body in both cases was much less than our case.

There have been a few reports of Fogarty balloon dilation used to retrieve foreign bodies[1112] however we could not find any report of CRE balloon being used to retrieve foreign body that was conducted in both of our cases.

The summary of these cases [Table 1] highlights that most of the chronic retained foreign bodies are difficult to diagnose due to lack of recall or non-specific signs and symptoms. These difficulties are further complicated with granulation tissue and other chronic changes associated with these chronic foreign bodies making them difficult to retrieve. While these chronic changes might lead to failure of foreign body removal thereby switching to lobectomy, there have been trials of argon plasma coagulation to remove the dense granulation tissue for retrieval of foreign body.

This case series highlights the importance of bronchoplasty done with CRE balloon in retrieving chronic foreign bodies, which might be deemed to be unextractable and referred for surgical management otherwise leading to a lobectomy. It is essential to put maximal efforts to treat such cases and retrieve the foreign bodies especially in young individuals to prevent further morbidity by subjecting the case for a lobectomy.

Due to the lack of rigid bronchoscopy at our centre, we did both the procedures with a fibreoptic bronchoscope successfully thereby demonstrating that fibreoptic bronchoscope is still an important and useful tool to extract foreign bodies even with secondary stenosis in centres that lack rigid bronchoscope and especially in foreign bodies which are lodged in the peripheral airways.

CONCLUSION

Endobronchial ablation, cryotherapy, or airway dilation techniques may be necessary in cases where foreign body retention has caused significant granulation tissue or airway stenosis. Chronic foreign body impaction management requires patience, determination, and team effort. While treating young patients, a lung sparing approach should be preferred saving them from invasive procedures and lifelong morbidity.

Our case series demonstrates that successful Fibreoptic bronchoscope guided removal may still be possible despite airway changes that occurred secondary to the aspirated foreign body over a period of 13–15 years with the help of balloon dilation and argon plasma coagulation making it a promising approach in such cases.
