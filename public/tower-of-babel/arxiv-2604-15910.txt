Asymptotic stability of Landau solutions to the MHD system and energy decay
Nicola De Nitti, Yun Wang, Shaoheng Zhang

Abstract
. We consider the three-dimensional incompressible MHD system. Any weak solution satisfying a strong energy inequality is L 2 L^{2} -asymptotically stable around a Landau solution. Under an additional integrability assumption on the initial perturbation, we also obtain an explicit algebraic decay rate for the L 2 L^{2} -norm of the velocity and magnetic perturbations.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The MHD and Navier–Stokes systems

The
magnetohydrodynamic
(MHD)
equations
describe the behavior of an electrically conducting, incompressible, viscous fluid and play a fundamental role in astrophysics, geophysics, plasma physics, and in certain industrial applications. An introduction to the topic can be found in
[
13
]
.

We consider the initial value problem for the MHD system in three space dimensions:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{u}}+{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}+\nabla p=\nu\,\Delta{\boldsymbol{u}}+{\boldsymbol{B}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{B}}+{\boldsymbol{f}},&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{B}}+{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{B}}={\boldsymbol{B}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}+\mu\,\Delta{\boldsymbol{B}},&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}=0,&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{B}}=0,&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
{\boldsymbol{u}}(\boldsymbol{x},0)={\boldsymbol{u}}_{0}(\boldsymbol{x}),&\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
{\boldsymbol{B}}(\boldsymbol{x},0)={\boldsymbol{B}}_{0}(\boldsymbol{x}),&\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{cases}

Here, the unknown functions
{\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{B}},p
denote the velocity, magnetic field, and pressure, respectively; the data
{\boldsymbol{u}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{B}}_{0}
and
{\boldsymbol{f}}
represent the given initial velocity, initial magnetic field, and external force.

The equations
Eq.

1.1
1,3
constitute the Navier–Stokes system with an external Lorentz force, while
Eq.

1.1
2,4
are derived from Maxwell’s equations for the magnetic field
\boldsymbol{B}
. In particular, in the absence of the magnetic field (i. e., when
\boldsymbol{B}\equiv\boldsymbol{0}
), the MHD system
Eq.

1.1
reduces to the
Navier–Stokes equations
, that is

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{u}}+{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}+\nabla p=\nu\Delta{\boldsymbol{u}}+{\boldsymbol{f}},&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}=0,&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
{\boldsymbol{u}}(\boldsymbol{x},0)={\boldsymbol{u}}_{0}(\boldsymbol{x}),&\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{cases}

The existence of weak solutions to the Navier–Stokes system with zero external force (
{\boldsymbol{f}}\equiv\mathbf{0}
) goes back to Leray
[
30
]
, for any divergence-free initial data
{\boldsymbol{u}}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
; an analogous result for nonzero
{\boldsymbol{f}}
can be found in
[
50
, Chapter III]
. Leray also raised the question whether the
L^{2}
-norm of a weak solution tends to zero as
t\to\infty
. This was answered positively by Kato
[
25
]
for strong solutions, and by Masuda
[
38
]
for weak solutions satisfying a strong energy inequality. Later, Schonbek
[
45
,
44
]
established explicit decay rates for the
L^{2}
-norm using the Fourier splitting technique.

For the MHD equations
Eq.

1.1
, several existence results have been established. In two spatial dimensions, when
\nu>0
and
\mu>0
, global well-posedness for the Cauchy
problem has been obtained in
[
47
,
14
]
for bounded domains and in
[
53
]
for the whole space. Using the vorticity-current formulation, local-in-time existence and regularity was more recently obtained in
[
41
]
for
L^{1}
initial data. In three dimensions, local-in-time well-posedness is contained as well in
[
47
,
14
,
53
]
, while global-in-time well-posedness
for small initial data was later obtained in
[
7
]
.

The time-asymptotic behavior of solutions to the MHD equations has also been widely studied. In
[

]
, lower and upper bounds are obtained for the decay rates of both the total and magnetic energies for solutions in

1.2.
Landau solutions for stationary Navier–Stokes and MHD equations

In this work, we focus on the long-time behavior of weak solutions to the three-dimensional incompressible MHD system
Eq.

1.1
near a stationary
Landau solution
.

Stationary solutions to the Navier–Stokes equations that are homogeneous of degree
-1
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
are the Landau solutions introduced in
[
29
]
; they are axisymmetric without swirl (see
Section

2.1
for the definition and explicit formulas, or
[
52
, Section 8.2]
).

Tian and Xin
[
51
]
, and Cannone and Karch
[
9
]
proved that all
(-1)
-homogeneous, axisymmetric solutions in
C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\{\mathbf{0}\}\right)
are Landau solutions. Later, in
[
48
]
, Šverák proved the assertion without the assumption of axisymmetry.

We stress that we focus on the three-dimensional case. Indeed, for
n=2
, the problem of finding
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions reduces to studying an ODE on the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, where a reasonably complete description of solution can be obtained in terms of elliptic functions; under the additional constraint that
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}=0
across the origin, there exists (modulo rotations) a countable family of
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions that are smooth away from the origin. On the other hand, for
n\geq 4
, there are no non-trivial
(-1)
-homogeneous solutions that are smooth away from the origin. We refer to
[
48
]
and the references therein for further details.

Landau solutions plays a fundamental role in stationary Navier–Stokes flows: Korolev and Šverák
[
26
]
showed that the asymptotic leading term of small solutions in exterior domains of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
must be a Landau solution; Miura and Tsai
[
39
]
proved that the leading term of a point singularity like
|\boldsymbol{x}|^{-1}
at
\boldsymbol{x}=\mathbf{0}
is also given by a Landau solution when it is sufficiently small.

For the stationary MHD system, the pair
({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{B}})
is a
(-1)
-homogeneous, axisymmetric solution whenever
{\boldsymbol{u}}
is a Landau solution and
{\boldsymbol{B}}\equiv\boldsymbol{0}
. The converse direction has been investigated under different structural assumptions. For the system
without magnetic diffusion
(i. e.,
\mu=0
), Zhang
[
57
]
showed that the converse holds provided the magnetic field
{\boldsymbol{B}}
has only the swirl component. In the case
with magnetic diffusion
(i. e.,
\mu>0
), Zhang, Wang, and Wang
[
56
]
proved that if
({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{B}})
is a smooth axisymmetric self-similar solution with
{\boldsymbol{B}}=B^{\theta}(\rho,\varphi)\,\boldsymbol{e}_{\theta}
and satisfies the pointwise decay condition
|{\boldsymbol{u}}(\boldsymbol{x})|\leq\varepsilon|\boldsymbol{x}|^{-1}
for some small
\varepsilon>0
, then
\boldsymbol{u}
must be a Landau solution and
\boldsymbol{B}
vanishes.
This condition was later refined by Zhang
[
58
]
, who showed that the conclusion holds under the weaker assumption that the cylindrical radial component
u^{r}\leq\beta
on the unit sphere for some constant
\beta<\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}
.

1.3.
Perturbations of the Landau solution

For simplicity, let us fix
\nu=\mu=1
. Consider initial data of the form
{\boldsymbol{u}}_{0}={\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0},\ {\boldsymbol{B}}_{0}
and
{\boldsymbol{f}}={\boldsymbol{b}}\delta_{\mathbf{0}}
, where
{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}
is the Landau solution corresponding to a vector field
{\boldsymbol{b}}
(as defined in
Section

2.1
) and
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{B}}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(cf.
Section

2.2
for the notation), provided
|{\boldsymbol{b}}|>0
is sufficiently small.
Throughout this paper,
\delta_{\mathbf{0}}
denotes the Dirac distribution, while
\delta,\tilde{\delta},\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\dots
are positive constants.

Denote by
({\boldsymbol{u}}(\boldsymbol{x},t),{\boldsymbol{B}}(\boldsymbol{x},t),p(\boldsymbol{x},t))
the solution to the MHD equations
Eq.

1.1
. Setting
{\boldsymbol{u}}(\boldsymbol{x},t)={\boldsymbol{U}}^{{\boldsymbol{b}}}(\boldsymbol{x})+{\boldsymbol{w}}(\boldsymbol{x},t)
and
p(\boldsymbol{x},t)=P^{\boldsymbol{b}}(\boldsymbol{x})+\pi(\boldsymbol{x},t)
, where
({\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}},P^{\boldsymbol{b}})
is the Landau solution defined in
Section

2.1
, we obtain the perturbed system

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{w}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{w}}+{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{w}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}+\nabla\pi=\Delta{\boldsymbol{w}}+{\boldsymbol{B}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{B}},&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{B}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{B}}+{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{B}}=\Delta{\boldsymbol{B}}+{\boldsymbol{B}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{w}}+{\boldsymbol{B}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}},&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{w}}=0,&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{B}}=0,&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
{\boldsymbol{w}}(\boldsymbol{x},0)={\boldsymbol{w}}_{0}(\boldsymbol{x}),&\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
{\boldsymbol{B}}(\boldsymbol{x},0)={\boldsymbol{B}}_{0}(\boldsymbol{x}),&\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{cases}

When
{\boldsymbol{B}}\equiv\mathbf{0}
, system
Eq.

1.3
reduces to the perturbed Navier–Stokes equations around the Landau solution:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{w}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{w}}+{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{w}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}+\nabla\pi=\Delta{\boldsymbol{w}},&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{w}}=0,&(\boldsymbol{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty),\\
{\boldsymbol{w}}(\boldsymbol{x},0)={\boldsymbol{w}}_{0}(\boldsymbol{x}),&\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{cases}

In
[
24
]
, Karch and Pilarczyk showed that if
|{\boldsymbol{b}}|
is sufficiently small and
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, there exists at least one weak solution
{\boldsymbol{w}}
to (
1.4
) satisfying a strong energy inequality, and that any such solution is
L^{2}
–asymptotically stable, i. e.,
\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. Under the additional assumption
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for some
\frac{6}{5}<q<2
, the solution also obeys the decay estimate
\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})}
.
Subsequently, Karch, Pilarczyk, and Schonbek
[
23
]
generalized the
L^{2}
-stability of the Landau solution to a broader class of global-in-time solutions, including singular and time-dependent flows, provided the background solution is small enough.
The energy decay rate established in
[
24
]
was further extended by Hishida and Schonbek
[
20
]
, who proved that for any
q\in[1,2)
, there exists a constant
\delta=\delta(q)>0
such that if
|{\boldsymbol{b}}|\leq\delta
, the typical decay rate
t^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})}
also holds, thus extending the admissible range of
q
.

Šverák
[
48
]
proved that every
(-1)
-homogeneous non-zero solution of the stationary Navier–Stokes equations in
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\})
is a Landau solution.
In
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
, the authors studied
(-1)
-homogeneous axisymmetric solutions that are smooth away from the symmetry axis
\{x_{1}=x_{2}=0\}
and may be singular along the entire axis. In this class, all no-swirl solutions were classified in
[
31
,
32
]
, while solutions with non-zero swirl were constructed in
[
31
,
33
]
. Among these solutions, the least singular ones (those with sufficiently mild singularities along the axis) were shown in
[
34
]
to be asymptotically stable under
L^{2}
-perturbations, thereby extending the stability result of
[
24
]
beyond the Landau case.

In
[
35
]
, the
L^{3}
-asymptotic stability of Landau solutions was studied: if
\boldsymbol{w}_{0}
and the background Landau solution are sufficiently small, then there exists a unique global solution with
\|\boldsymbol{w}(t)\|_{3}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. On the other hand, in
[
6
]
, the authors showed that the asymptotic stability around Landau solutions may fail for some initial perturbations in
L^{3,\infty}
(regardless of how small the Landau solution or the initial perturbations are in
L^{3,\infty}
).

Finally, in
[
8
]
, the authors studied the stability and asymptotic behavior of singular solutions to the 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes equations with singular external forces which are either singular finite measures or more general tempered distributions
with bounded Fourier transforms (including, in particular, the Landau solution).

We now define the notion of a weak solution to
Eq.

1.3
.
For initial data
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{B}}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}
,
({\boldsymbol{w}},{\boldsymbol{B}})=({\boldsymbol{w}}(\boldsymbol{x},t),{\boldsymbol{B}}(\boldsymbol{x},t))
is called a
weak solution
if
{\boldsymbol{w}},{\boldsymbol{B}}\in X
, where

X\coloneqq C_{w}\big([0,\infty);L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)\cap L^{2}\big([0,\infty);\dot{H}_{\sigma}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big),

and if for all
t\geq s\geq 0
and all test functions
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
belonging to

(1.5)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C\big([0,\infty);L^{2}_{\sigma}\cap L^{3}\big),\quad\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big([0,\infty);L^{2}\big),\quad\partial_{\tau}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big([0,\infty);(H^{1}_{\sigma})^{*}\big),

the following identities hold:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\langle{\boldsymbol{w}}(t),\boldsymbol{\varphi}(t)\rangle+\int_{s}^{t}\Big[\langle{\boldsymbol{w}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{w}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle-\langle{\boldsymbol{B}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{B}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle+a_{1}({\boldsymbol{w}},\boldsymbol{\varphi})\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\langle{\boldsymbol{w}}(s),\boldsymbol{\varphi}(s)\rangle+\int_{s}^{t}\langle{\boldsymbol{w}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\tau}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

\displaystyle\langle{\boldsymbol{B}}(t),\boldsymbol{\varphi}(t)\rangle+\int_{s}^{t}\Big[\langle{\boldsymbol{w}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{B}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle-\langle{\boldsymbol{B}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{w}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle+a_{2}({\boldsymbol{B}},\boldsymbol{\varphi})\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\langle{\boldsymbol{B}}(s),\boldsymbol{\varphi}(s)\rangle+\int_{s}^{t}\langle{\boldsymbol{B}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\tau}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Here,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the standard
L^{2}
-inner product, and
a_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
a_{2}(\cdot,\cdot)
are defined in
Eq.

3.5
.
The test function space is a special case of the more general framework introduced in
[
20
, eq. (2.16)]
.

1.4.
Main results

This work investigates the long-time behavior and decay rates of weak solutions to
Eq.

1.3
that obey a strong energy inequality of the form

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}+2\int_{s}^{t}\bigl(\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{w}}(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{B}}(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&\leq\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(s)\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(s)\|_{2}^{2}-2\int_{s}^{t}\bigl(\langle({\boldsymbol{w}}\cdot\nabla){\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}},{\boldsymbol{w}}\rangle-\langle({\boldsymbol{B}}\cdot\nabla){\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}},{\boldsymbol{B}}\rangle\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\end{aligned}

which holds for almost every
s\geq 0
(including
s=0
) and all
t\geq s
. We note that, as in
[
24
, Theorem 2.1]
, Galerkin’s method yields the existence of (at least) one weak solution to
Eq.

1.3
satisfying
Eq.

1.7
provided
{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}
is small.

Our first main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(Asymptotic stability)
.

There exists a constant
\delta>0
such that if
0<|{\boldsymbol{b}}|\leq\delta
, then every weak solution
({\boldsymbol{w}}(t),{\boldsymbol{B}}(t))
to
Eq.

1.3
with
Eq.

1.7
satisfies

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}=0,

provided
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{B}}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Under additional integrability assumptions on initial data, we also get the decay rate of
\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}
and
\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}
.

Theorem 1.2

(Energy decay rate)
.

Let
1\leq q<2
. There exists a constant
\tilde{\delta}=\tilde{\delta}(q)>0
such that if
0<|{\boldsymbol{b}}|\leq\tilde{\delta}
, then every weak solution
({\boldsymbol{w}}(t),{\boldsymbol{B}}(t))
to
Eq.

1.3
with
Eq.

1.7
satisfies

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}\leq C(t+1)^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})},\quad\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}\leq C(t+1)^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})},

provided
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{B}}_{0}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

These results extend the analysis of decay properties for weak solutions of
Eq.

1.4
satisfying a strong energy inequality analogous to
Eq.

1.7
, previously carried out in
[
20
]
. Unlike
[
20
]
, we do not use the Fourier splitting method to obtain the energy decay; instead, we present a streamlined argument that combines ideas from
[
20
]
,
[
24
]
, and
[
5
]
. In particular, inspired by
[
24
]
, we start from estimates of the type

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(s)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{-sL_{1}}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0}\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{s}\bigl\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{1}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{w}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{w}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{B}})\bigr\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(s)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{-sL_{2}}{\boldsymbol{B}}_{0}\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{s}\bigl\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{2}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{w}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{B}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{w}})\bigr\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

(cf.
Eqs.

4.4
–
4.5
below) and use them to derive the energy decay of solutions. The main difference from
[
24
]
lies in our treatment of the linear and nonlinear terms: we employ the more flexible
L^{q}
–
L^{2}
estimates from
[
20
]
(see,
Eqs.

3.10
–
3.11
), which yield a more direct proof.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Laudau solutions and Hardy-type inequality

This subsection is devoted to recalling the Landau solutions to the stationary Navier–Stokes equations and Hardy’s inequality.

We begin with some basic facts about the Landau solutions (see e. g.,
[
52
, Section 8.2]
). For each nonzero
{\boldsymbol{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, there exists a unique Landau solution
{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}
and an associated pressure
P^{\boldsymbol{b}}
, homogeneous of degrees
-1
and
-2
respectively, that solve the stationary Navier–Stokes equations

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}+\nabla P^{\boldsymbol{b}}=\Delta{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}},&\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\},\\
\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}=0,&\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\},\end{cases}

and are smooth in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
. Moreover, in the sense of distributions in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, they satisfy

\displaystyle-\Delta{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}+\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}})+\nabla P^{\boldsymbol{b}}={\boldsymbol{b}}\,\delta_{\mathbf{0}},\qquad\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}=0,

where
\delta_{\mathbf{0}}
denotes the Dirac distribution.

When
{\boldsymbol{b}}=(0,0,\beta)
with
\beta>0
, the Landau solution admits an explicit representation in spherical coordinates. Let
\boldsymbol{x}=(\rho\sin\varphi\cos\theta,\rho\sin\varphi\sin\theta,\rho\cos\varphi)
and define the orthonormal basis vectors

{\boldsymbol{e}}_{\rho}=\frac{\boldsymbol{x}}{\rho},\quad{\boldsymbol{e}}_{\theta}=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta,0),\quad{\boldsymbol{e}}_{\varphi}={\boldsymbol{e}}_{\theta}\times{\boldsymbol{e}}_{\rho}.

Then for any
A>1
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}=\frac{2}{\rho}\left[\left(\frac{A^{2}-1}{(A-\cos\varphi)^{2}}-1\right){\boldsymbol{e}}_{\rho}+\frac{-\sin\varphi}{A-\cos\varphi}{\boldsymbol{e}}_{\varphi}\right],\quad P^{\boldsymbol{b}}=\frac{4(A\cos\varphi-1)}{\rho^{2}(A-\cos\varphi)^{2}},

where the parameter
\beta
is related to
A
by

\beta=16\pi\left[A+\frac{1}{2}A^{2}\log\left(\frac{A-1}{A+1}\right)+\frac{4A}{3(A^{2}-1)}\right].

The corresponding Landau solution for a general
{\boldsymbol{b}}
is obtained by rotation.

There exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
C_{0}>0
such that for any
{\boldsymbol{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
0<|{\boldsymbol{b}}|<\varepsilon_{0}
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle|{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}(\boldsymbol{x})|\leq\frac{C_{0}|{\boldsymbol{b}}|}{|\boldsymbol{x}|},\quad|\nabla{\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}(\boldsymbol{x})|\leq\frac{C_{0}|{\boldsymbol{b}}|}{|\boldsymbol{x}|^{2}},\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}.

Notations and inequalities in weak

The following notation will be used throughout the paper. Let
L^{p}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
denote the completion of

in the
L^{p}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
, where

is the space of smooth, compactly supported, divergence-free vector fields.

For

, let

denote the

, i. e., Banach spaces of measurable functions on

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p,\infty}=\sup\left\{|E|^{-1+\frac{1}{p}}\int_{E}|f(s)|\,\mathrm{d}s:E\in\mathcal{B}\right\}.

Here

denotes the Lebesgue measure of a measurable set

, and
\mathcal{B}
is the collection of all Borel sets with finite positive measure.

One can verify that
|\cdot|^{-\frac{1}{p}}\in L^{p,\infty}(0,\infty)
for
p>1
. This follows from the Hardy–Littlewood inequality
\int_{E}s^{-\frac{1}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{0}^{|E|}s^{-\frac{1}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}s
together with
Eq.

2.5
.
Recall also the well-known embedding
L^{p}\subset L^{p,\infty}
, which is a consequence of Hölder’s inequality and
Eq.

2.5
.

The following lemma provides weak versions of Young’s and Hölder’s inequalities (see
[
5
, Lemma 2.2]
for a detailed proof).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\in L^{p,\infty}
and
g\in L^{q,\infty}
with
1<p,q<\infty
.

Weak Young’s inequality:

If
1+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
with
1<r<\infty
, then the convolution

belongs to
L^{r,\infty}
and satisfies

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|f\ast g\|_{r,\infty}\leq C_{p,q}\|f\|_{p,\infty}\|g\|_{q,\infty},

where
C_{p,q}>0
is a constant depending only on
p
and
q
.

Weak Hölder’s inequality:

If
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
with
1<r<\infty
, then the pointwise product
fg
belongs to
L^{r,\infty}
and satisfies

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{r,\infty}\leq C_{p,q}\|f\|_{p,\infty}\|g\|_{q,\infty},

where
C_{p,q}>0
is a constant depending only on
p
and
q
.

Linearized flow

Denote the adjoint linear operators by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} L_{1}^{*}{\boldsymbol{v}}&=\mathbb{P}\bigl[-\Delta{\boldsymbol{v}}-({\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot\nabla){\boldsymbol{v}}-\sum_{j=1}^{3}U_{j}^{\boldsymbol{b}}\nabla v_{j}\bigr],\\

\displaystyle a_{1}({\boldsymbol{w}},{\boldsymbol{v}})

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla{\boldsymbol{w}},\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\rangle+\langle({\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot\nabla){\boldsymbol{w}},{\boldsymbol{v}}\rangle+\langle({\boldsymbol{w}}\cdot\nabla){\boldsymbol{U}}^{\boldsymbol{b}},{\boldsymbol{v}}\rangle,

\displaystyle a_{2}({\boldsymbol{B}},{\boldsymbol{v}})

Let us recall a criterion due to Lions (see
[

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and let
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{H}
be a dense subspace. Assume that
\mathcal{V}
is a Hilbert space with the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathcal{V}}
and with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{V}}
such that for a constant

we have
\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant C\|x\|_{\mathcal{V}}
for all

. Let

be a bounded sesquilinear form on
\mathcal{V}
, which defines an operator
A:\mathcal{D}(A)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
as follows

\mathcal{D}(A)=\{z\in\mathcal{V}:\ |a(z,v)|\leqslant C\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}},v\in\mathcal{V}\},\quad(Az,v)_{\mathcal{H}}=a(z,v).

Suppose that for some
\alpha>0
and

we have

(3.6)

\alpha\|z\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\leqslant\text{\rm Re}\ a(z,z)+\lambda_{0}\|z\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Then

is the infinitesimal generator of a strongly continuous semigroup of linear operators on

which is holomorphic in a sector

for some

.

The argument follows that of

4.

In this section, we prove

and

.
Given a weak solution

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(s)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{-sL_{1}}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0}\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{s}\bigl\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{1}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{w}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{w}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{B}})\bigr\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

Starting from this consideration, the proofs of the main theorems rely on the strong energy inequality and on the following auxiliary proposition. Its first part provides an estimate for the linear terms, while the second part handles the nonlinear terms.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
L_{i}
, with

, be the linear operators defined in

. Then the following propositions hold.

(i)

There exists a constant
\delta_{3}>0
such that if

, then for every

,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{2}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\|e^{-sL_{i}}{\boldsymbol{f}}\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s=0.

(ii)

Let
\frac{6}{5}<r\leq 2
and let

be the constant from

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|e^{-sL_{i}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{u}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{v}})\|_{2}\leq Cs^{-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}}(\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{2}\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{2})^{\frac{3}{2r}-\frac{1}{2}}(\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{2}\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r})}.

Proof.

The two claims are consequences of

(i)

Let

r\in(\frac{6}{5},2]
. Consequently,

\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{r}\leq(\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{2}\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{2})^{\frac{3}{2r}-\frac{1}{2}}(\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{2}\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r})},

which completes the proof.

∎

We now turn to the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Step 1.

Duhamel-type representation.

As
({\boldsymbol{w}},{\boldsymbol{B}})
is a weak solution to
Eq.

1.3
, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\langle{\boldsymbol{w}}(s),\boldsymbol{\varphi}(s)\rangle+\int_{0}^{s}\Big[\langle{\boldsymbol{w}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{w}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle-\langle{\boldsymbol{B}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{B}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle+a_{1}({\boldsymbol{w}},\boldsymbol{\varphi})\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\langle{\boldsymbol{w}}(0),\boldsymbol{\varphi}(0)\rangle+\int_{0}^{s}\langle{\boldsymbol{w}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\tau}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\tau

for all
s\geq 0
and all
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
satisfying

\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C\big([0,\infty);L^{2}_{\sigma}\cap L^{3}\big),\quad\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big([0,\infty);L^{2}\big),\quad\partial_{\tau}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big([0,\infty);(H^{1}_{\sigma})^{*}\big).

There exists a constant
0<\delta_{4}\leq\min\{\varepsilon_{0},\frac{1}{6C_{0}}\}
such that for any
|{\boldsymbol{b}}|\leq\delta_{4}
and
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in C^{\infty}_{c,\sigma}
, the function
\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\tau)=e^{-(s-\tau)L_{i}^{*}}\boldsymbol{\psi}
belongs to the admissible test function space for
i=1,2
.
Indeed, by the analyticity of the semigroup
e^{-tL_{1}^{*}}
(see,
Theorem

3.2
and
[
35
, Theorem 3.1]
), we have
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C([0,\infty);L^{2}_{\sigma}\cap L^{3})
.
Moreover, using the commutativity
L_{1}^{*\frac{1}{2}}e^{-tL_{1}^{*}}=e^{-tL_{1}^{*}}L_{1}^{*\frac{1}{2}}
(see, e. g.,
[
19
, Proposition 3.1.1(f)]
and
[
40
, Theorem 1.2.4(c)]
) together with the estimates
Eq.

3.8
and
Eq.

3.9
, we obtain

\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\tau)\|_{2}\lesssim\|L_{1}^{*\frac{1}{2}}e^{-(s-\tau)L_{1}^{*}}\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{2}=\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{1}^{*}}L_{1}^{*\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{2}\leq\|L_{1}^{*\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{2}\lesssim\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{2},

which implies
\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty);L^{2})\subset L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty);L^{2})
.
For the time derivative, we note that
\partial_{\tau}\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\tau)=L_{1}^{*}\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\tau)
. Then for any
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in H^{1}_{\sigma}
,

|\langle\partial_{\tau}\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\tau),{\boldsymbol{v}}\rangle|=|a_{1}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\tau),{\boldsymbol{v}})|\leq C\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\tau)\|_{H^{1}}\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{H^{1}},

so that by duality
\|\partial_{\tau}\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\tau)\|_{(H^{1}_{\sigma})^{*}}\leq C(\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\tau)\|_{2}+\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\tau)\|_{2})
.
The boundedness of
\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\tau)\|_{2}
(from
Eq.

3.9
) and of
\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\tau)\|_{2}
then yields
\partial_{\tau}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty);(H^{1}_{\sigma})^{*})
.
Thus
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
satisfies the required regularity conditions.

Taking this
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
as a test function in (
4.3
) and using
\langle{\boldsymbol{w}},\partial_{\tau}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle=\langle{\boldsymbol{w}},L_{1}^{*}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle=a_{1}({\boldsymbol{w}},\boldsymbol{\varphi})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle{\boldsymbol{w}}(s),\boldsymbol{\psi}\rangle=\Big\langle e^{-sL_{1}}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0}-\int_{0}^{s}e^{-(s-\tau)L_{1}}\mathbb{P}({\boldsymbol{w}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{w}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}\cdot\nabla{\boldsymbol{B}})\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\boldsymbol{\psi}\Big\rangle.

By duality, this gives

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(s)\|_{2}\leq\|e^{-sL_{1}}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0}\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{s}\bigl\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{1}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{w}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{w}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{B}})\bigr\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Similarly, taking
\boldsymbol{\varphi}(\tau)=e^{-(s-\tau)L_{2}^{*}}\boldsymbol{\psi}
as a test function in
Eq.

1.6
2
yields

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(s)\|_{2}\leq\|e^{-sL_{2}}{\boldsymbol{B}}_{0}\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{s}\bigl\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{2}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{w}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{B}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{w}})\bigr\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Step 2.

Energy monotonicity and time averaging
. If
0<|{\boldsymbol{b}}|<\min\{\varepsilon_{0},\frac{1}{4C_{0}}\}
, Hardy’s inequality
Eq.

2.4
and the strong energy inequality
Eq.

1.7
imply

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}+2(1-4C_{0}|{\boldsymbol{b}}|)\int_{s}^{t}\left(\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{w}}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{B}}\|_{2}^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(s)\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(s)\|_{2}^{2},

which yields

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(s)\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(s)\|_{2}^{2}

for almost every
s\geq 0
(including
s=0
) and for all
t\geq s
.
Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\leq\sqrt{\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(s)\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(s)\|_{2}^{2}}\leq\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(s)\|_{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(s)\|_{2}.

Integrating the inequality over
s\in[\frac{t}{2},t]
gives

(4.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\leq\frac{2}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(s)\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{2}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(s)\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Step 3.

Combination of linear and nonlinear contributions.
Combining
Eq.

4.4
,
Eq.

4.5
and
Eq.

4.8
, we deduce

(4.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\bigl(\|e^{-sL_{1}}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0}\|_{2}+\|e^{-sL_{2}}{\boldsymbol{B}}_{0}\|_{2}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{s}\Bigl(\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{1}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{w}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{w}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{B}})\|_{2}

\displaystyle+\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{2}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{w}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{B}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{w}})\|_{2}\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigg]\mathrm{d}s.

Step 4.

Linear contribution.
If
|{\boldsymbol{b}}|\leq\delta_{3}
, where
\delta_{3}
is the constant in
4.1-(i)
,

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{2}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\bigl(\|e^{-sL_{1}}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0}\|_{2}+\|e^{-sL_{2}}{\boldsymbol{B}}_{0}\|_{2}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s=0.

Step 5.

Nonlinear contribution.
Inequality
Eq.

4.2
implies that

\displaystyle\frac{2}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\left[\int_{0}^{s}\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{1}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{w}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{w}})\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\left[\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{r_{1}}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(\tau)\|_{2}^{\frac{3}{r_{1}}-1}h^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r_{1}})}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right]\mathrm{d}s,

where
h(\tau)=\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{w}}(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}
and
r_{1}\in(\frac{6}{5},2]
is a constant to be determined.

Eq.

4.6
implies
h\in L^{1}(0,\infty)
; moreover, by (
4.7
),
\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(\tau)\|_{2}\leq\|{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0}\|_{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}_{0}\|_{2}
.
Therefore

\frac{C}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\left[\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{r_{1}}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(\tau)\|_{2}^{\frac{3}{r_{1}}-1}h^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r_{1}})}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right]\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{C}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}|\cdot|^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{r_{1}}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\ast h^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r_{1}})}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Using
Eq.

2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{C}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}|\cdot|^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\ast h^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r_{1}})}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}}\left\||\cdot|^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{r_{1}}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\ast h^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r_{1}})}\right\|_{\alpha_{1},\infty}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}}\left\||\cdot|^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{r_{1}}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{c_{1},\infty}\left\|h^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r_{1}})}\right\|_{c_{2}}.

Here we used
Eq.

2.6
and the embedding
L^{p}\subset L^{p,\infty}
in the second line, and
\alpha_{1},c_{1},c_{2}>1
are constants satisfying

\frac{1}{c_{1}}=\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2},\quad\frac{1}{c_{2}}=\frac{3}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{r_{1}}\right),

and

1+\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}=\frac{1}{c_{1}}+\frac{1}{c_{2}}.

Note that
\frac{1}{c_{1}}+\frac{1}{c_{2}}=\frac{5}{4}
, so
\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}=\frac{1}{4}
provided
c_{1},c_{2}\in(1,\infty)
. For instance, taking
r_{1}=\frac{3}{2}
gives
c_{1},c_{2}\in(1,\infty)
.
Therefore, if
|{\boldsymbol{b}}|\leq\delta_{2}(\frac{3}{2})
with
\delta_{2}(\frac{3}{2})
as in
4.1-(ii)
,

\frac{2}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\left[\int_{0}^{s}\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{1}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{w}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{w}})\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right]\mathrm{d}s\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\left[\int_{0}^{s}\Bigl(\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{1}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{w}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{w}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{B}})\|_{2}+\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{2}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{w}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{B}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{w}})\|_{2}\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{4}}

provided
|{\boldsymbol{b}}|\leq\delta_{2}(\frac{3}{2})
.

Step 6.

Conclusion.
In conclusion, if
0<|{\boldsymbol{b}}|\leq\delta
, where
\delta=\min\{\delta_{4},\delta_{3},\delta_{2}(\frac{3}{2})\}
,
Eq.

4.9
yields

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}=0.

∎

We now establish the decay estimate for the perturbed equation
Eq.

1.3
. The proof proceeds by iteration.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

Step 1.

Linear decay estimate.
By

3.4-(i)

\frac{2}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\|e^{-sL_{1}}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0}\|_{2}+\|e^{-sL_{2}}{\boldsymbol{B}}_{0}\|_{2}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s\leq Ct^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})}(\|{\boldsymbol{w}}_{0}\|_{q}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}_{0}\|_{q}).

Step 2.

Baseline decay bound.

From the estimates obtained in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\left[\int_{0}^{s}\Bigl(\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{1}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{w}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{w}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{B}})\|_{2}+\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{2}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{w}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{B}}-{\boldsymbol{B}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{w}})\|_{2}\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

Combining these estimates with
Eq.

4.9
, we have

\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})}+Ct^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

Step 3.

Decay regimes according to the integrability exponent
q
.
Thus
Theorem

1.2
holds whenever
-\frac{1}{4}\leq-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})
, that is,
\frac{3}{2}\leq q<2
. If
1\leq q<\frac{3}{2}
,
\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{4}}
.

Step 4.

Bootstrap improvement of decay for
1\leq q<\frac{3}{2}
.

Similarly,
Eq.

4.2
shows

\displaystyle\frac{2}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\left[\int_{0}^{s}\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{1}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{w}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{w}})\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\left[\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{r_{2}}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(\tau)\|_{2}^{\frac{3}{r_{2}}-1}h^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r_{2}})}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right]\mathrm{d}s.

Here
h(\tau)=\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}
, and
r_{2}\in(\frac{6}{5},2]
is a constant to be determined. Using
\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(\tau)\|_{2}\leq C\tau^{-\frac{1}{4}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{C}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\left[\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{r_{2}}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(\tau)\|_{2}^{\frac{3}{r_{2}}-1}h^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r_{2}})}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\left[\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{r_{2}}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{1}{4}(\frac{3}{r_{2}}-1)}h^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r_{2}})}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}|\cdot|^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{r_{2}}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\ast(|\cdot|^{-\frac{1}{4}(\frac{3}{r_{2}}-1)}\,h^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r_{2}})})\,\mathrm{d}s,

Recalling

Eq.

2.5
,
Eq.

2.6
, and
Eq.

2.7
, we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{C}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}|\cdot|^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{r_{2}}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\ast(|\cdot|^{-\frac{1}{4}(\frac{3}{r_{2}}-1)}\,h^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r_{2}})})\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}}}\left\||\cdot|^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{r_{2}}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\ast(|\cdot|^{-\frac{1}{4}(\frac{3}{r_{2}}-1)}\,h^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r_{2}})})\right\|_{\alpha_{2},\infty}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}}}\left\||\cdot|^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{r_{2}}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{c_{3},\infty}\left\||\cdot|^{-\frac{1}{4}(\frac{3}{r_{2}}-1)}\right\|_{c_{4},\infty}\left\|h^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r_{2}})}\right\|_{c_{5},\infty},

where
\alpha_{2},c_{3},c_{4},c_{5}>1
are constants satisfying

\frac{1}{c_{3}}=\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{1}{r_{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2},\quad\frac{1}{c_{4}}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{3}{r_{2}}-1\right),\quad\frac{1}{c_{5}}=\frac{3}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{r_{2}}\right),

and

0<\frac{1}{c_{4}}+\frac{1}{c_{5}}<1,\quad 1+\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}}=\frac{1}{c_{3}}+\frac{1}{c_{4}}+\frac{1}{c_{5}}.

Taking
r_{2}=\frac{15}{8}
gives
\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}}=\frac{2}{5}
, which yields

\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})}+Ct^{-\frac{2}{5}}.

Thus
Theorem

1.2
holds provided
-\frac{2}{5}\leq-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})
, i. e.,
\frac{30}{23}\leq q<\frac{3}{2}
.

Step
5.

Final bootstrap and completion of the proof.
If
1\leq q<\frac{30}{23}
,
\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-\frac{2}{5}}
. Analogously,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{t}\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}\left[\int_{0}^{s}\|e^{-(s-\tau)L_{1}}\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot({\boldsymbol{w}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{w}})\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{\alpha_{3}}}\left\||\cdot|^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{r_{3}}-\frac{1}{2})-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{c_{6},\infty}\left\||\cdot|^{-\frac{2}{5}(\frac{3}{r_{3}}-1)}\right\|_{c_{7},\infty}\left\|h^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r_{3}})}\right\|_{c_{8},\infty}.

Here
r_{3}\in(\frac{6}{5},2]
and
\alpha_{3},c_{6},c_{7},c_{8}>1
are constants satisfying

\frac{1}{c_{6}}=\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{1}{r_{3}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2},\quad\frac{1}{c_{7}}=\frac{2}{5}\left(\frac{3}{r_{3}}-1\right),\quad\frac{1}{c_{8}}=\frac{3}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{r_{3}}\right),

and

0<\frac{1}{c_{7}}+\frac{1}{c_{8}}<1,\quad 1+\frac{1}{\alpha_{3}}=\frac{1}{c_{6}}+\frac{1}{c_{7}}+\frac{1}{c_{8}}.

Choosing
r_{3}=\frac{4}{3}
gives
\frac{1}{\alpha_{3}}=\frac{3}{4}
, which yields

\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}\right)}+Ct^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

Thus the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 4.2

(Refinement of the decay rate)
.

In the proof of
Theorem

1.2
, the constants
r_{2}
and
r_{3}
can be chosen so that
r_{2}>3/2
is arbitrarily close to
3/2
and
r_{3}>\frac{6}{5}
is arbitrarily close to
\frac{6}{5}
. With such a choice, we have
\frac{1}{\alpha_{3}}=1-\varepsilon
for an arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
, which gives the decay estimate

\|{\boldsymbol{w}}(t)\|_{2}+\|{\boldsymbol{B}}(t)\|_{2}\leq Ct^{-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}\right)}+Ct^{-(1-\varepsilon)}.
