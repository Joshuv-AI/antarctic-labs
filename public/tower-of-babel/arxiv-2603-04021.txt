Explicit p p -adic Hodge theory for elliptic curves and non-split Cartan images
Matthew Bisatt, Lorenzo Furio, Davide Lombardo

Abstract
. Let E / ℚ p E/\mathbb{Q}_{p} be an elliptic curve whose mod p p Galois image is contained in the normaliser of a non-split Cartan. We classify the possible p p -adic images of E E using tools from p p -adic Hodge theory via a careful analysis of the local Galois structure of the p p -power torsion of E E . We pay special attention to the case where E E has potentially supersingular reduction, where we give an algorithm to determine the corresponding filtered ( φ , Gal ⁡ ( K / ℚ p ) ) (\varphi,\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}_{p})) -module from a Weierstrass model (which appears to be novel), and introduce alternative division polynomials that may be of independent interest. We deduce global consequences for elliptic curves E / ℚ E/\mathbb{Q} : when the mod p p representation of E E has non-split Cartan image and E E doesn’t have CM, the p p -adic image must be the full preimage of the normaliser of a mod p n p^{n} non-split Cartan for some n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 . As an application, we sharpen existing bounds on the adelic image in terms of the Weil height of the j j -invariant.



1.
Introduction

Galois representations attached to elliptic curves
E
over fields
F
of arithmetic significance are a cornerstone of modern number theory, connecting to many central themes, both theoretical and computational. Of particular interest are the mod
p^{n}
and
p
-adic representations
\rho_{E,p^{n}}
and
\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
, which encode the Galois action on the
p^{n}
-torsion subgroup
E[p^{n}]
and the Tate module
T_{p}E
respectively (after fixing bases, we always regard
\rho_{E,p^{n}}
and
\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
as taking values in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
and
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, respectively). In this paper, we develop tools to analyse the images of these representations over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and apply them to obtain global results for elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
.

When
F=\mathbb{Q}
, Serre proved the images of
\rho_{E,p}
and
\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
are intertwined: if
p>3
then one is surjective if and only if the other is
[
30
, IV-23, Lemma 3]
. In his landmark open image theorem
[
28
]
, he further showed that
\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
is surjective when
p
is sufficiently large so long as
E
does not have potential complex multiplication (CM). In the same paper, he then posed a natural uniformity question: does there exist a constant
N\geq 3
, such that
\rho_{E,p}
(and hence also
\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
) is surjective for every prime
p>N
, uniformly in non-CM curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
? This became known as
Serre’s uniformity question
. Despite substantial progress, the problem remains open, though it is conjectured to have an affirmative answer with optimal constant
N=37
.

By the work of many authors
[
29
,
24
,
3
,
2
,
38
,
18
,
9
]
, it is known that for a non-CM elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
and a prime
p>37
, the image of the representation
\rho_{E,p}
is either
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
or the normaliser of a non-split Cartan, which we henceforth denote by
C_{ns}^{+}(p)
(and
C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n})
for the mod
p^{n}
versions). Recent work has also led to significant progress towards the finer classification of possible
p
-adic images, which is an important first step in Mazur’s
Program B
. When
p>7
, Rouse–Sutherland–Zureick-Brown
[
25
, Theorem 1.1.6]
have classified the possible
p
-adic images unless
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
. In the non-split Cartan case, much less is known about the images of
\rho_{E,p^{n}}
for
n\geq 2
. Our first global result bridges this gap to describe the full
p
-adic tower:

Theorem 1.1

(=
Theorem

9.1
)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve without CM and let
p>7
be a prime. Suppose that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
. Then there exists
n\geq 1
such that

\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}=\pi_{n}^{-1}\left(C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n})\right),

where
\pi_{n}:\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
is the canonical projection.

We note that if Serre’s uniformity question has a positive answer, then the containment
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
only occurs for finitely many primes
p
, but this question remains wide open.
The refined classification of
Theorem

1.1
precludes the existence of images that had not previously been ruled out, thereby allowing us to strengthen previous adelic image bounds
[
21
,
11
]
.
We state here a simplified version of our result in terms of the
j
-invariant; a fully explicit version is given in
Theorem

9.3
. We note that the dependence on the height of
E
is essentially optimal for the available methods.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve without complex multiplication and let
\operatorname{h}(j(E))
be the absolute logarithmic Weil height of its
j
-invariant. For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists an effective constant
C_{\varepsilon}
such that the index of the adelic Galois representation of
E
is bounded by
C_{\varepsilon}\cdot\max\{1,\operatorname{h}(j(E))\}^{2+\varepsilon}
.

Theorem

1.1
is a consequence of a more explicit local one, where the hypotheses enable us to suppose that
E
has potentially supersingular reduction. Recall that the semistability defect
e
of an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is the minimal degree of an extension
L/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that the base change
E_{L}
is semistable; when
p>3
, one has
e\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
.

Theorem 1.3

(=
Lemma

2.20
+
Corollary

8.29
)
.

Let
p>7
be a prime and let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be an elliptic curve with semistability defect
e
, and suppose that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
. Then
E
has potentially good supersingular reduction and the following hold.

(i)

If
e\leq 2
then
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{n}}=C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n})
for all
n\geq 1
.

(ii)

Suppose
e\geq 3
. Let
j
be the
j
-invariant of
E
and let
n_{0}
be the positive integer

n_{0}:=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{lll}\!\left\lfloor\frac{1}{3}v(j)\right\rfloor&&\text{if $e\in\{3,6\}$}\\
\!\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2}v(j-1728)\right\rfloor&&\text{if $e=4$.}\end{array}\end{cases}

Let
\pi_{n_{0}}:\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n_{0}}\mathbb{Z})
be the canonical projection. If
p>\sqrt{n_{0}+1}
, then

\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{n_{0}}}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n_{0}})\text{ with index dividing $3$,}\qquad\qquad\text{and}\,\,\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}=\pi_{n_{0}}^{-1}(\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{n_{0}}}).

Moreover, if
e=4
or
p\not\equiv 2,5\bmod 9
, then
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{n_{0}}}=C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n_{0}})
.

Remark 1.4
.

As explained in
Remark

8.30
, even when
e\in\{3,6\}
and
p\equiv 2,5\bmod 9
one can easily decide whether the index
[C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n_{0}}):\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{n_{0}}}]
is
1
or
3
. In particular, for all such
e
and
p
there exist elliptic curves as in the statement for which the index is
3
.

The local classification problem has seen considerably less attention than the global version. If
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
has potentially good supersingular reduction and doesn’t have
potential formal CM
, a result of Serre
[
27
, Théorème 5]
shows that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
contains an open subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, but we are unaware of any bounds on the index of this subgroup. Our work doesn’t just bound this index, but precisely describes
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
in this setup.

1.1.
Explicit
p
-adic Hodge theory approach

We now give more details on our method for proving
Theorem

1.3
, where the novelty in our work lies in the systematic and computationally accessible utilisation of
p
-adic Hodge theory to study local Galois representations of
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
in the supersingular setting.

If
E
has good supersingular reduction over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
or a quadratic extension, then it is known that there are only two possible isomorphism classes for
T_{p}E
and the
p
-adic image is known (see
[
33
, top of p. 34]
and
Lemma

2.20
). We can then suppose that
E
has semistability defect
e\geq 3
and becomes semistable over
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt[e]{-p},\zeta_{e})
. For
p>7
, we show that in this context
T_{p}E
is irreducible as a Galois module and hence its Galois structure is uniquely determined by its rational counterpart
V_{p}E:=T_{p}E\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Known results in the literature, due in particular to work of Volkov, imply that
V_{p}E
(which is potentially crystalline) can be encoded by a single deformation parameter
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, corresponding to a filtered
(\varphi,\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}))
-module
D_{\alpha}
(
Definition

3.2
). We are able to extract from
D_{\alpha}
concrete arithmetic information about the torsion representations
E[p^{k}]
and thereby construct the following
alternative “division” polynomials
for
E
.

Theorem 1.5

(=
Theorem

4.2
+
Proposition

4.23
)
.

Let
p>7
and let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be an elliptic curve with potentially supersingular reduction, semistability defect
e\geq 3
and deformation parameter
\alpha
. For
k\geq 1
, define

g_{k}:=x^{p^{2k}}+\sum_{n=1}^{k}(-1)^{n}p^{n}(x^{p^{2k-2n}}+\alpha^{-1}(\sqrt[e]{-p})^{2}x^{p^{2k+1-2n}}).

If
v(\alpha^{-1})\geq 0
and
p>\max\{\sqrt{k},k-v(\alpha^{-1})-1\}
, then there is a
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-equivariant bijection between
E[p^{k}]
and the roots
\mathcal{R}_{k}
of
g_{k}
(for a suitable twisted Galois action on
\mathcal{R}_{k}
). Moreover, the fields
K(E[p^{k}])
and
K(\mathcal{R}_{k})
are equal.

The recursive structure of the polynomials
g_{k}
and their linear dependence on
\alpha^{-1}
emphasise their versatility in comparison to the classical (but considerably more complicated) division polynomials; see
Proposition

4.12
,
Remark

4.16
, and §
4.3
. We expect that these polynomials
g_{k}
will be useful in other problems involving local
p
-adic representations of elliptic curves, and note that they may also be used when the semistability defect is
1
or
2
(
Remark

4.24
).

We briefly remark on the hypothesis that
v(\alpha^{-1})\geq 0
. Replacing
E
by a ramified quadratic twist changes the parameter
\alpha
to
\pm p^{2}\alpha^{-1}
(
Remark

3.5
(i)). Thus, whenever
v(\alpha^{-1})\neq-1
, one can still construct a Galois-equivariant bijection between
\mathcal{R}_{k}
and
E[p^{k}]\otimes\chi
for a suitable quadratic character
\chi
. The case
v(\alpha^{-1})=-1
corresponds precisely to
E
having a canonical subgroup of order
p
(
Remark

3.5
(ii)), and will be excluded from our applications by the Cartan image assumption (
Corollary

2.10
); moreover, in this case the Tate module is no longer irreducible, so different methods are necessary.

The proof of
Theorem

1.3
is then based on two main pillars. On the one hand, we establish a dichotomy in the behaviour of the roots of
g_{k}
depending on the valuation of the deformation parameter
\alpha
: when
k
is sufficiently small compared to
v(\alpha^{-1})
, we show that the image of the Galois action on
\mathcal{R}_{k}
is contained in
C_{ns}^{+}(p^{k})
; conceptually, this happens because
E
is ‘sufficiently close’ to having complex multiplication. Conversely, for
k
large we see maximal growth, namely
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{k}}
is the full inverse image of
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{k-1}}
inside
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z})
. Proving this dichotomy gives a version of
Theorem

1.3
that may be expressed in terms of the deformation parameter
\alpha
(see
Theorem

5.4
).

The second pillar in the proof of
Theorem

1.3
is a connection between
\alpha
and the
j
-invariant of
E
, which we believe to be an interesting result in its own right.
Specifically, Volkov’s results give an abstract link between the set of
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with potentially good supersingular reduction and semistability defect
e
and the
(\varphi,\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}))
-modules
D_{\alpha}
, but the
\alpha
corresponding to such a curve is not immediately computable. We solve this problem by connecting Volkov’s description directly with Weierstrass models and formal logarithms.

Theorem 1.6

(=
Propositions

8.19
,
8.23
,
8.3
and
8.27
)
.

Let
p>3
be a prime. Suppose
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is an elliptic curve having potentially good supersingular reduction, with
j
-invariant
j
, minimal discriminant
\Delta
, and semistability defect
e\in\{3,4,6\}
satisfying
e<p-1
. Set
\pi_{e}=\sqrt[e]{-p}
and let
\log_{\hat{E}}(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}d_{n}t^{n}
be the formal logarithm attached to a short Weierstrass model of
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\pi_{e})
with good reduction. Define

\beta=\lim_{k\to\infty}-\frac{p}{\pi_{e}}\frac{d_{p^{2k+1}}}{d_{p^{2k}}},\qquad\text{which has valuation }v(\beta)=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{1}{3}v(j)-v(\pi_{e})&\,\text{ if $e\in\{3,6\}$},\\
\frac{1}{2}v(j-1728)-v(\pi_{e})&\,\text{ if $e=4$.}\end{array}\end{cases}

The deformation parameter
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
corresponding to
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is

\alpha=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{cc}-p\cdot(\beta/\pi_{e})^{-1}&\text{ if }v(\Delta)<6\\
-p\cdot\beta/\pi_{e}^{e-3}&\text{ if }v(\Delta)>6.\end{array}\end{cases}

Although
Theorem

1.6
gives only a limit formula for
\beta
, we discuss in
Section

8.4
how to make it computable, in the sense of being able to determine
\beta
to any given
p
-adic precision. We also note that the ratios
d_{p^{2k+1}}/d_{p^{2k}}
may be interpreted as higher analogues of the classical Hasse invariant of
E
, see
Remark

8.25
.

Having a formula for
\beta
then allows us to get access to the filtered
(\varphi,\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}))
-module of any given elliptic curve
E
as above, enabling the use of tools from
p
-adic Hodge theory which, while available in principle, were previously not accessible in practice. We also emphasise that
Theorem

1.6
has greater applicability than
Theorem

1.3
, since it holds for any potentially supersingular curve with semistability defect
e\geq 3
.

1.2.
Relation to existing literature

Previous classification of
p
-adic representations

Theorem

1.1
builds on previous work of the second author
[
11
, Theorem 1.6]
, who gave a similar classification, showing that either:
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
is the inverse image of
C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n})
for some
n\geq 1
; or
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}=\pi_{2}^{-1}(G_{ns}^{\#}(p^{2}))
, where
G_{ns}^{\#}(p^{2})
is an explicit group of level
p^{2}
which we describe in
Definition

2.5
.

One of the stepping stones towards
Theorem

1.3
– namely
Theorem

5.1
– removes this group-theoretic obstruction, namely it shows that
\rho_{E,p^{2}}(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}))
cannot be contained in
G_{ns}^{\#}(p^{2})
when
p>7
. This can be interpreted as a
p
-adic obstruction to the existence of rational points on the corresponding modular curve
X^{\#}_{ns}(p^{2})
, confirming the calculation of Rouse–Sutherland–Zureick-Brown when
p=11

[
25
, Section 8.7, label
121.605.41.1
]
.
When
p=7
, the situation is substantially different:
X_{ns}^{\#}(7^{2})
has a
\mathbb{Q}
-rational point, hence in particular (by smoothness) infinitely many
\mathbb{Q}_{7}
-rational points. The elliptic curves representing the points on
X_{ns}^{\#}(7^{2})
have potentially ordinary reduction, in contrast to the curves studied in this paper, for which the reduction is potentially supersingular. The case
p=7
is examined in detail in
[
10
]
.

We also remark that
Theorem

1.3
lies beyond the reach of purely group-theoretic arguments, since the image at level
p
does not determine the higher-level images. In particular, group theory does not provide information about the level at which the
p
-adic tower stabilises. The additional information given by the crystalline theory is therefore essential in our analysis.

Galois representations of elliptic curves in families

It is well-known that the mod
p^{n}
Galois representation of an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}:y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
is locally constant (see for example
[
16
, Theorem 5.1(1)]
for a much more general statement); i.e., there exists a (typically non-explicit)
p
-adic neighbourhood of the coefficients
A,B
where the Galois representation remains unchanged. The novel virtue of our approach is that we are able to determine the precise neighbourhood when
E
is as in
Theorem

1.3
via a delicate examination of division polynomials, coupled with Krasner’s lemma. In particular, the value
n_{0}
in
Theorem

1.3
is maximal such that the mod
p^{n_{0}}
representation of
E
coincides with the mod
p^{n_{0}}
representation of a certain CM curve
E_{\textrm{CM}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with
j
-invariant
0
(resp.
1728
) if
e=3,6
(resp.
e=4
), and is therefore contained in
C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n_{0}})
.

Our work is also related to the study of ramification in division fields for the family of elliptic curves with potentially supersingular reduction without a canonical subgroup of order
p
. Specifically
[
32
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also
[
22
]
) proves that the ramification degree of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E[p^{k}])
is divisible by
(p^{2}-1)p^{2k-2}
whereas our proof of
Theorem

7.1
crucially improves this to
(p^{2}-1)p^{2k}
(under appropriate assumptions). This additional factor is pivotal in proving the maximal growth condition
[\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E[p^{k}]):\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E[p^{k-1}])]=p^{4}
when
k
is sufficiently large.

Other versions of
p
-adic Hodge theory

We point out that – despite the fact that we are mainly interested in integral and torsion representations – we use a
rational
, rather than
integral
, version of
p
-adic Hodge theory. While several flavours of integral
p
-adic Hodge theory are now available (based for example on Breuil-Kisin modules
[
5
,
17
]
– see also
[
19
]
for the case of torsion representations – or Zink’s displays
[
36
]
), these theories currently do not seem to be amenable to fully explicit calculations. For example, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, the Breuil-Kisin machinery relies in part on approximation statements for Robba rings which do not appear to admit a systematic treatment in families. In our setting, we can use the rational version of the theory, as developed by Fontaine
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
and Volkov
[
33
]
, because of the easy but important fact that the rational
p
-adic Tate module determines the integral one, see
Lemma

2.18
.

A further comment is related to the restriction
p>\sqrt{n_{0}+1}
that appears in
Theorem

1.3
. This doesn’t come from the application of
p
-adic Hodge theory: the connection we establish between the
j
-invariant of
E
and the
p
-adic Hodge-theoretic invariants
\alpha
and
\beta
holds in much greater generality (in particular, for all
p>7
, see
Theorem

1.6
); the difficulty lies in the
p
-adic analysis that underpins the Galois-equivariant bijection between
E[p^{k}]
and the roots of
g_{k}(x)
. It would be interesting to remove this restriction from
Theorem

1.3
, which we believe also holds without it.

Other approaches to computing filtered
\varphi
-modules

Finally, we remark on another aspect which we believe to be important, namely, the use of formal logarithms to describe the deformation parameter
\beta
. In the case of elliptic curves with potentially ordinary reduction, the
p
-adic Hodge-theoretic invariants of
V_{p}E
can essentially be read off the trace of Frobenius, so there is no continuous family of deformations
[
33
, p. 111]
.
In the case of potentially multiplicative reduction, one does have continuous deformations, parameterised by Tate’s
q
-invariant, and the
p
-adic Hodge-theoretic invariants may be expressed in terms of suitable logarithms of
q

[
33
, p. 107]
. As already pointed out, a similar description was missing in the case of potentially supersingular reduction, although the connection of
\beta
with formal logarithms was predicted by Volkov
[
33
, Remarque on p. 133]
, who, however, did not suggest an explicit formula.

We explain why the use of formal logarithms bypasses a substantial issue. Recall that the filtration on the filtered
(\varphi,\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}))
-module corresponding to
E
comes from the comparison isomorphism between the crystalline and de Rham cohomologies of
E_{K}
(the former base-changed to
K
): the line giving the non-trivial piece of the filtration corresponds to the line of regular differentials in
H^{1}_{\operatorname{dR}}(E_{K}/K)
. Thus, computing the filtration based on the definitions would involve making explicit the comparison between crystalline and de Rham cohomology over the ramified extension
K
, a task which seems extremely difficult in practice. The recent preprint
[
4
]
takes an important first step in this direction, but only for curves having good reduction over an unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, which would not be sufficient for the applications in this paper.

1.3.
Layout

We begin in §
2
with background on Cartan subgroups. Working over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, we also show that if the mod
p
image is contained in
C_{ns}^{+}(p)
, then the curve has potentially supersingular reduction, and we establish a few basic properties that hold in this situation. We continue with a description and classification of possible lifts of
C_{ns}^{+}(p)
to
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, as well as analysing the special cases when
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
has CM or semistability defect at most
2
.

In §
3
, we review the results of Volkov’s PhD thesis which provides a description of
V_{p}E
via filtered
(\varphi,\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}))
-modules, most significantly providing a bijection between elements of
V_{p}E
and roots of an infinite series which depends on a parameter
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. The polynomials
g_{k}
of
Theorem

1.5
are defined in §
4
as truncations of this infinite series, with the roots of
g_{k}
serving as avatars for the
p^{k}
-torsion of
E
; the remainder of the section is dedicated to the polynomials
g_{k}
earning their description as
alternative “division” polynomials
by establishing a natural Galois-equivariant bijection between the roots of
g_{k}
and
E[p^{k}]
.

In §
5
we turn our focus to proving
Theorem

1.1
, which follows from
Theorem

1.3
after some preliminary reductions. In
Sections

6
and
7
we prove a version of
Theorem

1.3
in terms of the valuation of
\alpha
: we describe the image modulo
p^{k}
in terms of
\alpha
, with the split according to a suitable inequality between
k
and
v(\alpha^{-1})
.

Section

8
is then devoted to determining the deformation parameter
\alpha
associated to
E
culminating in an algorithm for computing
\alpha
. This is achieved via an analysis of the related invariant
\beta
for which we have a simple closed formula for
v(\beta)
in terms of the
j
-invariant as in
Theorem

1.6
; this completes the proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

We close in §
9
by giving the full statement and proof of our global results, namely
Theorem

1.1
and the refined version of
Theorem

1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Cartan subgroups and their normalisers

In this section, we introduce the non-split Cartan subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and review some of their basic properties. We will write
\operatorname{Id}
for the identity matrix.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a prime
p
and a subgroup
G\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, for every
n\geq 1
we define:

•

G(p^{n}):=G\bmod{p^{n}}\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}\left({\mathbb{Z}}/{p^{n}\mathbb{Z}}\right)
;

•

G_{n}:=\left\{A\in G\mid A\equiv\operatorname{Id}\bmod{\,p^{n}}\right\}
,

so that
G(p^{n})=G/G_{n}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and let
\varepsilon
be the reduction modulo
p
of the least positive integer which represents a quadratic non-residue in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
. We define the non-split Cartan subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
as

\displaystyle C_{ns}:=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&\varepsilon b\\
b&a\end{pmatrix}\,\middle|\,a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},\ (a,b)\not\equiv(0,0)\mod p\right\}.

The normaliser of
C_{ns}
in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is
C_{ns}^{+}:=C_{ns}\cup\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}C_{ns}
.

We list a few basic properties of the group
C_{ns}^{+}(p)
. The proofs are all straightforward.

Remark 2.3
.

The following hold:

(i)

The group
C_{ns}(p)
is cyclic of order
p^{2}-1
. It can be identified with
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}
: by writing
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}=\mathbb{F}_{p}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{p}\sqrt{\varepsilon}
as
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector spaces, the matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&\varepsilon b\\
b&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)
encodes the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\to\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
given by multiplication by
a+b\sqrt{\varepsilon}
.

(ii)

The square of any matrix in
C_{ns}^{+}(p)\setminus C_{ns}(p)
is a multiple of the identity. As a consequence, every element in
C_{ns}^{+}(p)\setminus C_{ns}(p)
has order dividing
2(p-1)
.

(iii)

The eigenvalues of a matrix
g
in
C_{ns}^{+}(p)
are either of the form
\lambda,\lambda^{p}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}
(if
g\in C_{ns}(p)
), or of the form
\lambda,-\lambda
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}
(if
g\in C_{ns}^{+}(p)\setminus C_{ns}(p)
).

Non-split Cartan groups have been studied extensively, especially in relation to Galois representations of elliptic curves. We recall some key group-theoretic properties they enjoy (for proofs see
[
37
, Lemma 2.4]
and
[
11
, §3.1]
).

Lemma 2.4
.

Writing
G=C_{ns}^{+}
we have:

(i)

each quotient
G_{n}/G_{n+1}
can be identified with an additive subgroup of
M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
via the map

G_{n}/G_{n+1}\to M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),\quad\qquad\operatorname{Id}+p^{n}A\mapsto A;

(ii)

the group
G(p)
acts by conjugation on
M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
and on each quotient
G_{n}/G_{n+1}
;

(iii)

the action of
G(p)
(or any index
3
subgroup thereof) on
M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is semisimple. For
p>7
, the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space
M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
decomposes as the sum of three irreducible and non-isomorphic representations of
G(p)
, given by

V_{1}=\mathbb{F}_{p}\cdot\operatorname{Id},\qquad V_{2}=\mathbb{F}_{p}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0&\varepsilon\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad V_{3}=\mathbb{F}_{p}\cdot\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}0&\varepsilon\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle.

Combining the last two properties in the lemma, it is easy to classify the subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})
whose projection to
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
is
C_{ns}^{+}(p)
. We’ll be especially interested in one of these groups, which we introduce in the following definition.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and let
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{3}\subseteq M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.
We define the group
G_{ns}^{\#}(p^{2})
as the unique subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})
of order
2p^{3}(p^{2}-1)
containing
\operatorname{Id}+pV
and whose projection modulo
p
equals
C_{ns}^{+}(p)
. In particular, we have
G_{ns}^{\#}(p^{2})\cong C_{ns}^{+}(p)\ltimes V
, where the semidirect product is defined by the conjugation action of
C_{ns}^{+}(p)
on
M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

2.2.
Supersingularity and canonical subgroups

Let
E/F
be an elliptic curve over a
p
-adic field. In this section, we show that if the image of the mod
p
representation is contained in
C_{ns}^{+}(p)
, then
E
has potentially good supersingular reduction and does not have a canonical subgroup at
p
, unless
p
is small relative to the absolute ramification index of
F
. We first consider general
p
-adic fields and then specialise to
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
Some of the statements that we present can be found in
[
11
, §4]
. However, these were proven for elliptic curves over number fields; here, we generalise them to elliptic curves over
p
-adic fields. We begin with introducing some notation that we shall use frequently throughout this section, including the semistability defect of
E/F
, which we remind the reader is defined as the minimal degree of an extension
L/F
such that
E/L
is semistable.

Remark 2.6
.

Since the exact image of
\rho_{E,p^{n}}
depends on the choice of a basis which we do not pick, we shall write
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{n}}\subseteq H\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
to mean that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{n}}
is contained in a conjugate subgroup of
H
, and similarly for
\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
.

The following simple lemma is well-known, but we provide some details.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
p>3
be a prime and let
E/F
be an elliptic curve with potentially good reduction. Let
\Delta
be the discriminant of any defining equation for
E
, and let
v_{F}
be a valuation of
F
, normalised so that a uniformiser of
F
has valuation
1
.

(i)

E
obtains good reduction over a totally tamely ramified extension of degree
e=\frac{12}{\gcd(12,v_{F}(\Delta))}\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
.

(ii)

There is a quadratic twist of
E
that acquires good reduction over an extension of
F
of degree at most 4.

Proof.

(i) is
[
28
, 5.6 a1)]
. For (ii), we only need to consider the case
e=6
: then
v_{F}(\Delta)\equiv\pm 2\bmod{12}
whereas the discriminant
\Delta^{\prime}
of a ramified quadratic twist
E^{\prime}
satisfies
v_{F}(\Delta^{\prime})\equiv v_{F}(\Delta)+6\equiv\pm 4\bmod{12}
, hence
E^{\prime}
has semistability defect
e^{\prime}=3
.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
E/F
be an elliptic curve such that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
. If
p-1\nmid 2e_{F}
, then
E
has potentially good reduction. Moreover, if
p-1\nmid 6e_{F}
and
p-1\nmid 8e_{F}
, then
E
has potentially supersingular reduction.

Proof.

The first statement is proved as in
[
11
, Proposition 4.1]
, noting that the argument given in
loc. cit.
is in fact carried out in
p
-adic fields.
Suppose now by contradiction that
E
has potentially ordinary reduction and let
K/F
be a totally ramified extension of degree
e\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
over which
E
acquires good reduction. Using
Lemma

2.7
we can assume
e\leq 4
(note that taking a quadratic twist does not change the potential reduction type).
Since the restriction of the mod
p
cyclotomic character to
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/K)
has order
\frac{p-1}{\gcd(e_{F}e,p-1)}
, by
[
11
, Lemma 4.3]
we know that there is an element of
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}
with eigenvalues
1
and
k
, where
k\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
has order
\frac{p-1}{\gcd(e_{F}e,p-1)}
.
Remark

2.3
(iii) gives
k=\pm 1
, and so
\frac{p-1}{\gcd(e_{F}e,p-1)}\mid 2
. This implies that
p-1\mid 2e_{F}e
, which contradicts the hypothesis.
∎

For applications, we need to know that
E
does not admit a canonical subgroup of order
p
in order to apply certain results in the literature. This is however the extent of our use of canonical subgroups, so we choose to omit the definition and instead refer the interested reader to e.g.
[
9
, Definition 3.5]
. The following result is a
p
-adic version of
[
11
, Theorem 4.5]
, which is in turn a generalisation of
[
9
, Theorem 3.11]
and proves the non-existence of canonical subgroups in the situation of interest.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
E/F
be an elliptic curve with potentially good reduction such that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
.

(i)

If
p>e_{F}e+1
and
p\neq 2e_{F}e+1
, then
E
does not have a canonical subgroup of order
p
.

(ii)

If
E
has potentially good supersingular reduction and
p>\max\{e_{F}e-2,2\}
, then
E
does not have a canonical subgroup of order
p
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as that of
[
11
, Theorem 4.5]
, noting that
[
32
, Theorem 4.6]
and
[
9
, Theorem 3.10]
are stated for number fields, but their proofs hold for
p
-adic fields.
∎

We now specialise to elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. The previous general results immediately imply:

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
p>7
be a prime number and
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be an elliptic curve. If
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
, then
E
has potentially good supersingular reduction and does not have a canonical subgroup of order
p
.

Proof.

Combine
Proposition

2.8
and
Theorem

2.9
(ii) in the case
e_{F}=1
.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
p>3
be a prime and
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be an elliptic curve with semistability defect
e\in\{3,4,6\}
.
Then
E
has potentially good reduction and
j(E)\equiv 0\bmod{p}
(resp.
j(E)\equiv 1728\bmod{p}
) if
e\in\{3,6\}
(resp.
e=4
).
Moreover, the reduction is potentially supersingular if and only if
e\mid p+1
. In particular, for every prime
p>7
and every elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
, the semistability defect
e
of
E
divides
p+1
.

Proof.

It is a classical result that
e\geq 3
implies that
E
has potentially good reduction (see e.g.
[
31
, Theorem C.14.1(d)]
, which shows that if the reduction is potentially multiplicative, then semistability is achieved over an extension of degree at most
2
). Let
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be an extension of degree
e
over which
E
acquires good reduction and let
\overline{E}
be the special fibre of a good reduction model of
E_{K}
.
It is well-known that
e
divides the order of the automorphism group of
\overline{E}
, so the following cases arise:

(i)

if
e=3
or
6
, then
3\mid\#\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{E})
, which implies
j(\overline{E})=0
. An elliptic curve with
j
-invariant
0
is supersingular in characteristic
p
if and only if
3\mid p+1
. Since
2\mid p+1
for every
p>2
, we conclude as desired that
e\mid p+1
if and only if
\overline{E}
is supersingular;

(ii)

if
e=4
, then
4\mid\#\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{E})
, which implies
j(\overline{E})=1728
. An elliptic curve with
j
-invariant
1728
is supersingular in characteristic
p
if and only if
4\mid p+1
.

To conclude, it suffices to notice that by
Corollary

2.10
the curve
E
has potentially good supersingular reduction whenever
p>7
and
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
p>7
be a prime number and
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be an elliptic curve such that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
. Then

\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\not\subseteq C_{ns}(p)
and
\rho_{E,p}(I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
is a cyclic group of order divisible by
\frac{p^{2}-1}{\gcd(e,3)}
. If
p\equiv 1\bmod 3
, then
\rho_{E,p}(I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
has order
p^{2}-1
.

Proof.

By
Corollary

2.10
,
E
has potentially good supersingular reduction. The Galois representation
E[p]
is described explicitly in
[
33
, §B.2.2]
for
e\in\{1,2\}
and in
[
33
, §B.3.2.3]
for
e\in\{3,4,6\}
. In the latter situation, there are several subcases depending on a parameter
v(\alpha)
. In the notation of
[
33
, p. 132]
, the case
v(\alpha)=1
cannot happen, because the image would not be contained in
C_{ns}^{+}(p)
. We then read from
[
33
]
that the representation is non-abelian – hence the image is not contained in
C_{ns}(p)
– and that the restriction of
E[p]\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
to
I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
factors via powers of the fundamental character of level
2
with exponents
\{1-\frac{p^{2}-1}{e},p+\frac{p^{2}-1}{e}\}
(if
e\in\{1,2\}
, or
e\in\{3,4,6\}
and
v(\alpha)\leq 0
) or
\{1+\frac{p^{2}-1}{e},p-\frac{p^{2}-1}{e}\}
(otherwise). Using
e\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
, one checks immediately that
\gcd\left(1\pm\frac{p^{2}-1}{e},p^{2}-1\right)=\gcd(1\pm\frac{p^{2}-1}{e},\mp e)\mid\gcd(e,3)
and
\gcd\left(p\pm\frac{p^{2}-1}{e},p^{2}-1\right)=\gcd(p\pm\frac{p^{2}-1}{e},\mp ep)\mid\gcd(e,3)
, so both characters have order divisible by
\frac{p^{2}-1}{\gcd(e,3)}
. When
p\equiv 1\bmod 3
, at least one of the exponents is also prime to
3
(their sum is
p+1\equiv 2\bmod 3
), so one of the two characters has order
p^{2}-1
.
∎

2.3.
N-Cartan lifts

We review some results on subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
that were proved in
[
11
]
. In particular, we focus on specific subgroups that we call
N-Cartan lifts
, which represent the possible images of the
p
-adic Galois representations attached to elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
in the non-split Cartan case.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and let
G\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
be a subgroup. Following
[
11
, Definition 3.6]
, we say that
G
is a
non-split N-Cartan lift
if it satisfies the following properties:

•

G
is closed;

•

\det(G)=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
;

•

G(p)

\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
, but
G(p)\not\subseteq C_{ns}(p)
;

•

there exists
g\in G(p)\cap C_{ns}(p)
which is not a scalar matrix.

We have the following classification result on non-split N-Cartan lifts (
[
11
, Theorem 3.14]
). Note that, given a subgroup
G
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and a positive integer
n
, the condition
G\supseteq\operatorname{Id}+p^{n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is equivalent to the fact that
G
is the inverse image in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
of its projection
G(p^{n})\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
G\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
be a non-split N-Cartan lift satisfying all of the following conditions:

•

|G_{1}/G_{2}|\geq p^{2}
;

•

there exists an element in
G(p)\cap C_{ns}(p)
whose image in
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
has order greater than
2
;

•

G\supset(1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p})\operatorname{Id}
.

Then one of the following holds:

•

G\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}
and
[C_{ns}^{+}:G]=[C_{ns}^{+}(p):G(p)]
;

•

There exists
n\geq 1
such that
G\supseteq\operatorname{Id}+p^{n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and
G(p^{n})\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n})
, with
[C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n}):G(p^{n})]=[C_{ns}^{+}(p):G(p)]
;

•

G\supseteq\operatorname{Id}+p^{2}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}),

G(p^{2})\subseteq G_{ns}^{\#}(p^{2})
, and
G(p^{2})\cong G(p)\ltimes V
, with
V
as in
Definition

2.5
.

It is shown in
[
11
]
that for a non-CM elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
for an odd prime
p
, the image
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
of the
p
-adic Galois representation is a non-split N-Cartan lift. We now extend this result to elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and show that the groups
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
satisfy the conditions of
Theorem

2.14
.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
p>7
be a prime and let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be an elliptic curve such that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
. The group
G:=\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
is a non-split N-Cartan lift.

Proof.

We have
\det(G)=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
since
\det\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
is the
p
-adic cyclotomic character.
By Lemma
2.12
we know that
G(p)\not\subseteq C_{ns}(p)
.
The group
G
is a continuous image of the profinite (hence compact) group
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, so it is compact. As the topology of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is Hausdorff, every compact subspace is closed, so
G
is closed.
Finally, we need to show that
G(p)\cap C_{ns}(p)
contains an element which is not a scalar matrix. By
Remark

2.3
(ii), every element in
C_{ns}^{+}(p)\setminus C_{ns}(p)
has order dividing
2(p-1)
, and the same holds for scalar matrices. Suppose by contradiction that
G(p)\cap C_{ns}(p)
consists of multiples of the identity. In particular, every element of
G(p)
has order dividing
2(p-1)
.
By
Lemma

2.12
, the group
G(p)
contains an element of order
\frac{p^{2}-1}{3}
. However, this is impossible because
\frac{p^{2}-1}{3}\nmid 2(p-1)
for
p>7
.
∎

Our next theorem classifies the possible Galois images subject to the group-theoretic constraints described above. We will show later that case (iii), although compatible with these abstract constraints, cannot occur for elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
p>7
be a prime and let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be an elliptic curve such that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
. Set
G:=\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
and let
e
be the semistability defect of
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. One of the following holds:

(i)

G\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}
and
[C_{ns}^{+}:G]\in\{1,3\}
, with
G=C_{ns}^{+}
if
p\equiv 1\bmod 3
or
e\in\{1,2,4\}
;

(ii)

there exists
n\geq 1
such that
G\supseteq\operatorname{Id}+p^{n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and
G(p^{n})\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n})
, with

[C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n}):G(p^{n})]=[C_{ns}^{+}(p):G(p)]\in\{1,3\}

and
G(p^{n})=C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n})
if
p\equiv 1\bmod 3
or
e\in\{1,2,4\}
;

(iii)

G\supseteq\operatorname{Id}+p^{2}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and
G(p^{2})\subseteq G_{ns}^{\#}(p^{2})
, with
[G_{ns}^{\#}(p^{2}):G(p^{2})]\in\{1,3\}
and
G(p^{2})=G_{ns}^{\#}(p^{2})
if
p\equiv 1\bmod 3
or
e\in\{1,2,4\}
.

Remark 2.17
.

With the notation of
Theorem

2.16
, note that

[G(p^{n+1}):G(p^{n})]=[\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E[p^{n+1}]):\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E[p^{n}])]=\begin{cases}p^{2}\,\,\text{ for all }n\geq 1&\quad\text{in case (i)},\\
p^{4}\,\,\text{ for }n\gg 0&\quad\text{in cases (ii) and (iii).}\end{cases}

As a consequence, if under the hypotheses of the theorem we assume furthermore that
[G(p^{n+1}):G(p^{n})]=p^{2}
for all sufficiently large
n
, or even just
\#G(p^{n})\leq Cp^{2n}
for some
C\in\mathbb{R}
, then
G
is a subgroup of
C_{ns}^{+}
of index
1
or
3
(and
G=C_{ns}^{+}
if
p\equiv 1\bmod 3
or
e\in\{1,2,4\}
). Similarly, if the
p
-adic valuation of
\#G(p^{2})
is
3
, we must be in case (iii) and
G(p^{2})
is a subgroup of
G_{ns}^{\#}(p^{2})
of index dividing
3
.

Proof of
Theorem

2.16
.

We show that
G
satisfies the hypotheses of
Theorem

2.14
.
First,
G
is a non-split N-Cartan lift by
Proposition

2.15
.
Next, we show that the image of the inertia subgroup
I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
via
\rho_{E,p^{2}}
has order divisible by
\frac{p^{2}(p^{2}-1)}{3}
, and by
p^{2}(p^{2}-1)
when
p\equiv 1\bmod 3
.
By
Theorem

2.9
we know that
E
does not have a canonical subgroup of order
p
. In particular, by
[
32
, Theorem 4.6]
– which is stated over number fields, but proved over
p
-adic fields – we know that the group
\rho_{E,p^{2}}(I_{K})
has order divisible by
\frac{p^{2}(p^{2}-1)}{e}
. On the other hand, by
Lemma

2.12
we know that
\rho_{E,p}(I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
, and hence a fortiori

\rho_{E,p^{2}}(I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
, contains an element of order
\frac{p^{2}-1}{3}
(or order
p^{2}-1
, in case
p\equiv 1\bmod 3
or
e\in\{1,2,4\}
), and so
\rho_{E,p^{2}}(I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
has order divisible by
\frac{p^{2}(p^{2}-1)}{3}
(or
p^{2}(p^{2}-1)
respectively).

Since
p\nmid\#\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}
, the image of
I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
via
\rho_{E,p}
is cyclic, hence it contains an element
g
of order
\frac{p^{2}-1}{3}
. Elements in
C_{ns}^{+}(p)\setminus C_{ns}(p)
have order
2
in
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
(they become scalar matrices when squared, see
Remark

2.3
(ii)), and so every element of order greater than
2
in
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
must be contained in
C_{ns}(p)
. However,
g
has order at least
\frac{p+1}{(3,p+1)}
in
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, which is greater than
2
for
p>7
. Moreover, since
p^{2}\mid\#\rho_{E,p^{2}}(I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
and
p\nmid\#\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}
, we know that
p^{2}
divides the cardinality of
\ker(G(p^{2})\to G(p))
. This implies
|G_{1}/G_{2}|\geq p^{2}
.

To check the last hypothesis of
Theorem

2.14
, it suffices to notice that
[
20
, Corollary 3.7]
shows that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\cdot\operatorname{Id}\subseteq G
, because
G(p)
contains
C_{ns}(p)^{3}
(or
C_{ns}(p)
, when
p\equiv 1\bmod 3
or
e\in\{1,2,4\}
) and an element of
C_{ns}^{+}(p)\setminus C_{ns}(p)
(since
G
is an N-Cartan lift).
∎

2.4.
Description of the Galois image in special cases

We assume again that
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is an elliptic curve such that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}
is contained in
C_{ns}^{+}(p)
. We describe the image of the full
p
-adic representation attached to
E
in two simple special cases: when
E
has potential complex multiplication and when it acquires good reduction over an at most quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. First, however, we show that when
E[p]
is an irreducible Galois module, the integral
p
-adic Tate module of
E
is determined by its rational counterpart.

In the next statement, by a
Galois-stable lattice
in
V_{p}E
we mean a (necessarily free)
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-submodule
T
of
V_{p}E
such that
\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}(\sigma)(T)\subseteq T
for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
T\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}=V_{p}E
.

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be an elliptic curve and suppose that
E[p]
is an irreducible
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-module. Then:

(i)

every Galois-stable lattice
T
in
V_{p}E
is homothetic to
T_{p}E
, and hence in particular isomorphic to
T_{p}E
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})]
-module;

(ii)

given any
T
as above,
T/p^{k}T
is isomorphic to
E[p^{k}]
as a
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-module for all
k>0
;

(iii)

if
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is another elliptic curve with
V_{p}E\cong V_{p}E^{\prime}
as Galois modules, then
T_{p}E\cong T_{p}E^{\prime}
as Galois modules.

Proof.

Let
T_{1}=T_{p}E\subseteq V_{p}E
be the Tate module and let
T_{2}
be any other Galois-stable lattice. All
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattices in
V_{p}E\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
are commensurable, so there exists
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
p^{m}T_{2}\subseteq T_{1}
. Choose
m\in\mathbb{Z}
minimal with this property, so that
p^{m}T_{2}\subseteq T_{1}
but
p^{m-1}T_{2}\not\subseteq T_{1}
. Consider the submodule
p^{m}T_{2}
of
T_{1}
and its image
p^{m}T_{2}/pT_{1}
inside
T_{1}/pT_{1}\cong E[p]
. Since
T_{2}
is Galois-stable, so is
p^{m}T_{2}/pT_{1}
, but
E[p]
is irreducible, so
p^{m}T_{2}/pT_{1}
is isomorphic to either
\{0\}
or
E[p]=T_{1}/pT_{1}
. In the former case, we have
p^{m}T_{2}\subseteq pT_{1}
, contradicting the choice of
m
. In the latter, we obtain
p^{m}T_{2}/pT_{1}=T_{1}/pT_{1}
, hence
p^{m}T_{2}+pT_{1}=T_{1}
. Nakayama’s lemma then implies
p^{m}T_{2}=T_{1}
, as desired.

Statements (i) and (ii) follow immediately. As for (iii), fix a Galois-equivariant isomorphism
\varphi:V_{p}E^{\prime}\to V_{p}E
and define
T:=\varphi(T_{p}E^{\prime})
. Then
T
is a Galois-stable lattice in
V_{p}E
, hence it is isomorphic to
T_{p}E
by (i).
∎

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
p>3
be a prime and
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be an elliptic curve with potential complex multiplication. Let
\mathcal{O}:=\operatorname{End}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(E)
be the ring of endomorphisms of
E_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
.

(i)

We have
\#\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E[p^{k}])/\mathbb{Q}_{p})\mid 2p^{2(k-1)}\cdot\#(\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. In particular, if
j(E)\in\{0,1728\}
, then the
p
-adic valuation of
\#\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E[p^{k}])/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is at most
2(k-1)
.

(ii)

If
p>7
and

\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
, then
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
is a subgroup of
C_{ns}^{+}
of index dividing
3
.

Proof.

Let
F
be a quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
containing the CM field
\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. Note that
F
is a field of definition for the endomorphisms of
E_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
. Since the action of
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)
must be compatible with the action of
\mathcal{O}
, we have that
\operatorname{Gal}(F(E[p^{k}])/F)\subseteq(\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}

[
26
, p. 502, Corollary 2]
, hence
\#\operatorname{Gal}(F(E[p^{k}])/F)\mid\#(\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}=\#(\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\cdot p^{2(k-1)}
and
\#\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E[p^{k}])/\mathbb{Q}_{p})\mid 2p^{2(k-1)}\cdot\#(\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. To complete the proof of (i), note that in the cases
j\in\{0,1728\}
and
p>3
we have
p\nmid\#(\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Now (ii) follows from
Remark

2.17
, which applies since
p>7
.
∎

Lemma 2.20
.

Assume
p>7
and let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be an elliptic curve such that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
. If
E
has good reduction over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
or acquires it over a quadratic extension, then
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
is equal to
C_{ns}^{+}
.

Proof.

First, we suppose
E
has good reduction. Let
\overline{E}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
be the reduction of
E
and let
\overline{j}
be its
j
-invariant. If
\overline{j}\neq 0,1728
,
[
12
, Lemma 3.3(i)]
shows that for all
k\geq 1
we have
[\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}(E[p^{k}]):\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}]=(p^{2}-1)p^{2(k-1)}
. Since
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
is a non-split N-Cartan lift by
Proposition

2.15
, it follows from the classification in
Theorem

2.16
that
G=C_{ns}^{+}
(see also
Remark

2.17
). If instead
\overline{j}=0
(resp. 1728), let
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be an elliptic curve with good reduction and
j
-invariant
0
, e.g.
y^{2}=x^{3}+1
(resp. with
j
-invariant
1728
, such as
y^{2}=x^{3}+x
). By
Corollary

2.10
we know that
E
has supersingular reduction, so
\overline{j}
is a supersingular
j
-invariant. We read from
[
34
, Proposition 2.7]
that there is a unique isomorphism class of rational Tate modules attached to supersingular elliptic curves with good reduction over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
: in the notation of
[
34
, §2.2.1]
, one has
D^{*}_{pst}(V_{p}E)\cong\mathbf{D}_{c}(\mathbf{1};\mathbf{0})
. It follows
V_{p}E\cong V_{p}E^{\prime}
as Galois modules, and moreover
T_{p}E\cong T_{p}E^{\prime}
by
[
33
, §B.2.2]
. The result then follows from the fact that in the potential CM case the Galois action on the
p
-adic Tate module has the stated property (
Lemma

2.19
; note that the index cannot be 3 by
Theorem

2.16
(i) since
e\in\{1,2\}
).

Finally, if
E
has bad reduction, then a quadratic twist
E^{\prime}
of
E
has good reduction over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. The claim holds for
E^{\prime}
by what we already proved. Note that the degrees
[\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E[p^{k+1}]):\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E[p^{k}])]
and
[\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E^{\prime}[p^{k+1}]):\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E^{\prime}[p^{k}])]
are equal for all
k\geq 1
, because they can differ at most by a factor of
2
, but they are all powers of
p
. As for
E^{\prime}
we have
[\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E^{\prime}[p^{k+1}]):\mathbb{Q}_{p}(E^{\prime}[p^{k}])]=p^{2}
for all
k
, the same holds for
E
and the claim follows from
Remark

2.17
.
∎

3.
Review of Volkov’s results

We review the results in Appendix B of Volkov’s PhD thesis
[
33
]
, which gives a description of the rational Tate modules of elliptic curves with semistability defect
e\in\{3,4,6\}
and potentially supersingular reduction. We will largely omit proofs; all the details can be found in
[
33
]
. The results we need from
[
33
]
all require
p>3
, so we make this assumption throughout this section.

Notation
.

For the remainder of the paper, we use
v
to denote the
p
-adic valuation on
\mathbb{C}_{p}
, normalised such that
v(p)=1
.

3.1.
The ring
R

We denote by
R
the perfection of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}/(p)
:

R=\varprojlim\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}}{(p)},

where
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}
is the valuation ring of
\mathbb{C}_{p}
and the transition maps are given by
x\mapsto x^{p}
.
By definition, an element
x\in R
is a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with each
x_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}/(p)
and
x_{n+1}^{p}=x_{n}
. Fix, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, an arbitrary lift
\hat{x}_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}
. For every
n\geq 0
, the sequence
\hat{x}_{n+m}^{p^{m}}
converges in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}
to an element
x^{(n)}
independent of the choice of lifts
\hat{x}_{n}
:

x^{(n)}:=\lim_{m\to\infty}\hat{x}_{n+m}^{p^{m}}.

The map
x\mapsto(x^{(0)},x^{(1)},\ldots)
is a bijection from
R
to sequences
(x^{(0)},x^{(1)},\ldots)
in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}_{p}}
satisfying
(x^{(i+1)})^{p}=x^{(i)}
for all
i\geq 0
; see
[
33
, p. 104]
for more details. We use this interpretation to define a valuation
v_{R}
on
R
given by

v_{R}(x)=v_{R}((x^{(0)},x^{(1)},\ldots))=v(x^{(0)}).

The elements of strictly positive valuation form the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{M}_{R}
of
R
.

3.2.
The ring of Witt bivectors
BW(R)

We let
BW(R)
denote the ring of Witt bivectors over
R
(see
[
33
, p. 121]
or
[
7
, §6.3]
). Elements of
BW(R)
are infinite sequences

\underline{a}=(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},\,\,a_{n}\in R\text{ such that }\exists\,n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z},\;\exists\,\varepsilon>0\text{ with }v_{R}(a_{n})\geq\varepsilon\text{ for }n\leq n_{0}.

The Witt bivectors are endowed with two operators,
\varphi
and
V
(Frobenius and Verschiebung), given by

\varphi((a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}})=(a_{n}^{p})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\quad\text{and}\quad\text{V}((a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}})=(a_{n-1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}.

These operators satisfy the relation
\varphi\text{V}=\text{V}\varphi=[p]
, multiplication by
p
in
BW(R)
. The ring
BW(R)
admits a natural embedding in
B_{\operatorname{dR}}
, from which it inherits a filtration
[
33
, top of p. 122]
.

We give some more details about addition in
BW(R)
. Let
\underline{a}=(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\underline{b}=(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be elements of
BW(R)
. We will be especially interested in the
0
-th component of the sum
\underline{d}:=\underline{a}+\underline{b}
. By definition, this is given by

d_{0}=\lim_{m\to\infty}S_{m}(a_{-m},\ldots,a_{0};b_{-m},\ldots,b_{0}),

where
S_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{0},\ldots;Y_{0},\ldots]
are the addition Witt polynomials. Recall that
S_{0}=X_{0}+Y_{0}
.
As in the proof of
[
33
, Lemme 2 on p. 123]
, we define the polynomials with integer coefficients

\displaystyle Q_{r}^{(m+1)}

\displaystyle=Q_{r}^{(m+1)}(X_{0},\ldots,X_{r};Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{r})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{p^{m+1-r}}(S_{r}(X_{0}^{p},\ldots,X_{r}^{p};Y_{0}^{p},\ldots,Y_{r}^{p})^{p^{m-r}}-(S_{r}(X_{0},\ldots,X_{r};Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{r})^{p})^{p^{m-r}})

and have the recurrence relation
S_{m+1}=X_{m+1}+Y_{m+1}+\sum_{0\leq r\leq m}Q_{r}^{(m+1)}.
For our purposes, we only need the following simple property of the addition polynomials, which will be applied later.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
m\geq 0
we have

S_{m}((-1)^{m}X_{m}^{p^{m}},\ldots,X_{m}^{p^{2}},-X_{m}^{p},X_{m};(-1)^{m+1}Y_{m}^{p^{m}},\ldots,-Y_{m}^{p^{2}},Y_{m}^{p},-Y_{m})=(X_{m}-Y_{m})(1+g_{m}(X_{m},Y_{m}))

for some polynomial
g_{m}
in the ideal
(X_{m},Y_{m})^{p}
of
\mathbb{Z}[X_{m},Y_{m}]
.

Proof.

Straightforward induction using the recurrence relation.
∎

3.3.
The filtered
(\varphi,G_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
-modules
D_{\alpha}

We consider elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with semistability defect
e\geq 3
, which necessarily have potentially good reduction. Fix
e\in\{3,4,6\}
and assume
p\equiv-1\bmod{e}
. We write
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\pi_{e},\zeta_{e})
for the splitting field of
x^{e}+p
, where
\pi_{e}=\sqrt[e]{-p}
is a fixed root of
x^{e}+p
and
\zeta_{e}
is a fixed primitive
e
-th root of unity. It is a standard fact that any
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with semistability defect
e
acquires semistable (hence good) reduction over
K
.

We denote the Galois group of
K
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
by
G_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
; it is generated by a Frobenius
\omega
and inertia element
\tau_{e}
with actions on
K
defined by

\omega(\zeta_{e})=\zeta_{e}^{-1},\quad\omega(\pi_{e})=\pi_{e}\qquad\quad\text{and}\qquad\quad\tau_{e}(\zeta_{e})=\zeta_{e},\quad\tau_{e}(\pi_{e})=\zeta_{e}\pi_{e}.

By results of Volkov that we now recall, every elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with semistability defect
e
corresponds to a filtered
(\varphi,G_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
-module, and the filtered
(\varphi,G_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
-modules that can arise in this way are parametrised by
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, as in the following definition. Throughout the paper, we use the convention that
\alpha^{-1}=0
when
\alpha=\infty
.

Definition 3.2

(
[
33
, p. 125]
)
.

Let
e\in\{3,4,6\}
and suppose
e\mid p+1
. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
we let
D_{\alpha}
be the filtered
(\varphi,G_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
-module given by a 2-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
-vector space with basis
b_{1},b_{2}
endowed with the following additional structures:

(i)

a Frobenius
\varphi
, defined by
\varphi(b_{1})=b_{2}
,
\varphi(b_{2})=-pb_{1}
;

(ii)

a filtration on
(D_{\alpha})_{K}:=D_{\alpha}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}}K
defined by

\operatorname{Fil}^{0}(D_{\alpha})_{K}=(D_{\alpha})_{K},\qquad\,\,\operatorname{Fil}^{1}(D_{\alpha})_{K}=(b_{1}\otimes\pi_{e}^{-1}+\alpha^{-1}\cdot b_{2}\otimes\pi_{e})K,\qquad\,\,\ \operatorname{Fil}^{2}(D_{\alpha})_{K}=\{0\},

where
\operatorname{Fil}^{1}(D_{0})_{K}=(b_{2}\otimes\pi_{e})K
by convention;

(iii)

a semi-linear action of
G_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
,
given on the basis by

\omega(b_{1})=b_{1},\quad\omega(b_{2})=b_{2}\quad\qquad\text{and}\quad\qquad\tau_{e}(b_{1})=\zeta_{e}\cdot b_{1},\quad\tau_{e}(b_{2})=\zeta_{e}^{-1}\cdot b_{2}.

Since
E
acquires good reduction over
K
, we can apply the functor
D_{\operatorname{cris},K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
(see
[
33
, p. 22]
and
[
8
, 5.3.7]
) to the
p
-adic Galois representation
V_{p}E
. Volkov shows
[
33
, p. 125]
that the resulting filtered
(\varphi,G_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
-module is of the form
D_{\alpha}
for some suitable
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Moreover, if
D_{\operatorname{cris},K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(V_{p}E)\cong D_{\alpha}
, we can recover the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})]
-module
V_{p}E
as

(3.1)

V_{p}E\cong V_{\alpha}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{(\varphi,G_{K})\operatorname{-mf}}(D_{\alpha},BW(R)),

where morphisms are in the category of
(\varphi,G_{K})
-filtered modules (that is,
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
-linear maps that respect Frobenius and the filtration, and are equivariant with respect to the action of
G_{K}:=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
). Note that
V_{\alpha}
is in an obvious way a
G_{K}
-module via the action

(g\cdot u)(d)=g\cdot(u(d))\quad\forall g\in G_{K},u\in V_{\alpha},d\in D_{\alpha}.

This
G_{K}
-action can be extended to an action of the full Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
as in
[
33
, p. 122]
, namely, by letting
g\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
act on
u\in V_{\alpha}
by

(3.2)

(g\cdot u)(d)=g\cdot(u([g^{-1}]\cdot d)),

where
[g^{-1}]
denotes the class of
g^{-1}
in
G_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
.

We summarise the above discussion in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
33
, Theorem 2.1 on p. 25 and p. 125]
)
.

Let
e\in\{3,4,6\}
and let
p>3
be a prime with
e\mid p+1
.

(i)

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be an elliptic curve with semistability defect
e
. Then there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
such that
V_{p}E
is isomorphic to
V_{\alpha}
as a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})]
-module, where
V_{\alpha}
is defined in
Equation

3.1
.

(ii)

Conversely, for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
there exists an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with semistability defect
e
, potentially supersingular reduction, and
V_{p}E\cong V_{\alpha}
as
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})]
-modules.

Remark 3.4
.

Note that, under the assumptions of the theorem,
E
has potentially good supersingular reduction by
Proposition

2.11
.

Remark 3.5
.

(i)

Taking a ramified quadratic twist of
E
replaces
\alpha
by
\pm p^{2}\alpha^{-1}
, with a different choice of
e
when
e\in\{3,6\}
(see
[
33
, top of p. 25]
).

(ii)

We have
v(\alpha)=1
precisely when the valuation of the Hasse invariant of
E
is strictly less than
\frac{p}{p+1}
(
[
33
, p. 133]
). This condition is in turn equivalent to the fact that
E
has a canonical subgroup of order
p
by
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
.

(iii)

The cases
\alpha=0,\infty
correspond to
j
-invariants in
\{0,1728\}

[
33
, p. 26]
.

We now make
V_{\alpha}
more concrete.

Note first that a
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
-linear map
u:D_{\alpha}\to BW(R)
is uniquely determined by the image of
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
. If it is furthermore
\varphi
-equivariant, then
u(b_{2})=u(\varphi(b_{1}))=\varphi(u(b_{1}))
, so
u
is determined by
u(b_{1})
alone.

Definition 3.6
.

Given
\underline{a}\in BW(R)
, we denote by
u_{\underline{a}}
the unique
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
-linear and
\varphi
-equivariant map
u:D_{\alpha}\to BW(R)
such that
u(b_{1})=\underline{a}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
u\in V_{\alpha}
.

(i)

u
is of the form
u_{\underline{a}}
for some
\underline{a}\in BW(R)
;

(ii)

If

Proof.

4.2.
Properties of

We establish some basic properties of the roots

of

(see

), namely their valuations and the valuations of their differences. To do this, we define a suitable shift of
g_{k}
and then compute its Newton polygon (which will be predominantly done in
Lemma

4.15
).

Proposition 4.12
.

Assume
v(\alpha^{-1})\geq 0
. For all roots
\gamma\in\mathcal{R}_{k},
there is a partition
\mathcal{R}_{k}\setminus\{\gamma\}=\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{k-1}\mathcal{R}_{k,\gamma}^{i}
which satisfies:

•

\#\mathcal{R}_{k,\gamma}^{i}=p^{2i}(p^{2}-1)
;

•

v(\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma)=\frac{1}{p^{2i}(p^{2}-1)}
for all
\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{k,\gamma}^{i}
.

In particular, all roots of
\mathcal{R}_{k}
are distinct and
\#\mathcal{R}_{k}=p^{2k}
.

Proof.

Let
h_{k}(t)=g_{k}(t+\gamma)
. The elements
\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma
for
\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{k}
are the roots of
h_{k}(t)
. The result will follow from a determination of the Newton polygon of
h_{k}
; namely, that it is given by the convex hull of the points

\{(0,\infty)\}\,\cup\,\{(p^{2i},k-i):0\leq i\leq k\}.

Indeed, this Newton polygon consists of segments of length
1
and
p^{2i+2}-p^{2i}
for
0\leq i\leq k-1
, with respective slopes
\infty
and
\frac{1}{(p^{2}-1)p^{2i}}
for
0\leq i\leq k-1
, which implies the statement. Observe that the coefficient
h_{k}^{(i)}
of
t^{i}
in
h_{k}
is given by

(4.4)

h_{k}^{(i)}=\binom{p^{2k}}{i}\gamma^{p^{2k}-i}+\sum_{n=1}^{k}(-1)^{n}p^{n}\left(\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}\binom{p^{2k+1-2n}}{i}\gamma^{p^{2k+1-2n}-i}+\binom{p^{2k-2n}}{i}\gamma^{p^{2k-2n}-i}\right),

where
\binom{a}{b}=0
if
b>a
. The Newton polygon of
h_{k}
now follows from
Lemma

4.15
.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

Note that for all
0\leq i\leq k-1
we have
\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{k,\gamma}^{i}}v(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})=1
.

Before we complete the computation of the Newton polygon, we first state a couple of straightforward combinatorial facts on the valuation of binomial coefficients.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
j
be a positive integer. Then
v\left(\binom{p^{j}}{a}\right)=j-v(a)
for all
1\leq a\leq p^{j}
. In particular:

(i)

v\left(\binom{p^{j}}{a}\right)\geq 1
whenever
a\neq 0,p^{j}
;

(ii)

If
1\leq a<p^{m}
for some
m\leq j
, then
v\left(\binom{p^{j}}{a}\right)\geq j-m+1
.

Proof.

We use Legendre’s formula
v(n!)=\sum_{i\geq 1}\left\lfloor\frac{n}{p^{i}}\right\rfloor
to compute
v\binom{p^{j}}{a}=\sum_{i\geq 1}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{p^{j}}{p^{i}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{a}{p^{i}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{p^{j}-a}{p^{i}}\right\rfloor\right)
. For each
1\leq i\leq j
, write
a=qp^{i}+r
with
0\leq r<p^{i}
, so
p^{j}-a=(p^{j-i}-q-1)p^{i}+(p^{i}-r)
. One then checks that
\left\lfloor\frac{p^{j}}{p^{i}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{a}{p^{i}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{p^{j}-a}{p^{i}}\right\rfloor\in\{0,1\}
and equals
0
if and only if
r=0
. It is now clear that the terms that contribute
1
are precisely the
i
such that
v(a)+1\leq i\leq j
, giving
v\binom{p^{j}}{a}=j-v(a)
, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 4.15
.

Assume
v(\alpha^{-1})\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
. Fix
\gamma\in\mathcal{R}_{k}
and let
h_{k}(t)=g_{k}(t+\gamma)=\sum_{i=0}^{p^{2k}}h_{k}^{(i)}t^{i}
. Then

(i)

h_{k}^{(p^{2k})}=1
and
h_{k}^{(0)}=0
;

(ii)

v(h_{k}^{(p^{2j})})=k-j
for
0\leq j\leq k-1
;

(iii)

if
p^{2m}<i<p^{2m+2}
for some
0\leq m\leq k-1
, then
v(h_{k}^{(i)})\geq v(h_{k}^{(p^{2m})})=k-m
.

Proof.

(i):
h_{k}^{(p^{2k})}
is the leading coefficient of
h_{k}
and hence equal to that of
g_{k}
. Note
h_{k}^{(0)}=g_{k}(\gamma)=0
by definition.

(ii): Let
0\leq j\leq k-1
and note that the binomomial coefficients in (
4.4
) (taking
i=p^{2j}
) are 0 for
n>k-j
, so we get the following expression for the
p^{2j}
-th coefficient of
h_{k}
:

h_{k}^{(p^{2j})}=\underbrace{\binom{p^{2k}}{p^{2j}}\gamma^{p^{2k}-p^{2j}}}_{>2k-2j}+\sum_{n=1}^{k-j}(-1)^{n}\left(\underbrace{\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}p^{n}\binom{p^{2k+1-2n}}{p^{2j}}\gamma^{p^{2k+1-2n}-p^{2j}}}_{>2k+1-n-2j}+\underbrace{p^{n}\binom{p^{2k-2n}}{p^{2j}}\gamma^{p^{2k-2n}-p^{2j}}}_{\geq 2k-n-2j}\right)

where in the underbraces we give bounds on the valuations of the terms, using
Lemma

4.14
and noting that
v(\alpha^{-1})\geq 0
and
v(\gamma),v(\pi_{e})>0
. It is clear that there is a unique term of minimal valuation: the rightmost term of the sum when

, in which case the exponent of
\gamma
is
0
and the valuation is
k-j
as claimed.

(iii) Let
p^{2m}<i<p^{2m+2}
. Using
Lemma

4.14
(ii) and considering only the powers of
p
and binomial coefficients, we get the following lower bounds on valuations:

h_{k}^{(i)}=\underbrace{\binom{p^{2k}}{i}\gamma^{p^{2k}-i}}_{{\geq 2k-2m-1}}+\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{n}\left(\underbrace{\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}p^{n}\binom{p^{2k+1-2n}}{i}\gamma^{p^{2k+1-2n}-i}}_{{\geq 2k-2m-n}}+\underbrace{p^{n}\binom{p^{2k-2n}}{i}\gamma^{p^{2k-2n}-i}}_{{\geq 2k-2m-n-1}}\right).

We now examine these terms more closely, showing that each of them has valuation at least
k-m
. Note
2k-2m-1=(k-m)+((k-1)-m)\geq k-m
since

, so

We now consider the valuation of

which is at least

. On the other hand, for this summand to occur, we must have

which implies that

, so this summand always has valuation at least
k-m
as claimed.
The final term of
p^{n}\binom{p^{2k-2n}}{i}
with valuation at least
2k-2m-n-1
is analogous: this is at least
k-m
when
n\leq k-m-1
and the appearance of the summand implies
n\leq k-m-1
as before.
∎

Remark 4.16
.

As we will prove formally in what follows, there is a natural bijection between the roots of
g_{k}(x)
and the points in
E[p^{k}]
.
Under this bijection, the root
0
corresponds to the identity element of
E[p^{k}]
and the
p^{2i}(p^{2}-1)
roots of valuation
\frac{1}{p^{2i}(p^{2}-1)}
correspond to the
p^{2i}(p^{2}-1)
points of order exactly
p^{i+1}
for
0\leq i\leq k-1
. The intuitive interpretation of the fact that the partition structure of
\mathcal{R}_{k}\setminus\{\gamma\}
is independent of the choice of
\gamma
is that for every choice of
P\in E[p^{k}]
, the number of elements
Q\in E[p^{k}]
such that
Q-P
has order
p^{i}
depends only on
i
and not on
P
.

4.3.
Construction of the map
\Phi_{k}

In this section, we construct the map
\Phi_{k}
whose existence is asserted by
Theorem

4.2
, assuming the notation and hypotheses of that theorem.

Lemma 4.17
.

Assume
v(\alpha^{-1})\geq 0
. Let
n\in\{0,\dots,k\}
and
a\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
. Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{R}_{k}
be a root of
g_{k}
that maximises
v(a-\gamma)
amongst the roots of
g_{k}
.
If
v(g_{k}(a))>k-n+\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
, then
v(a-\gamma)>\frac{1}{p^{2n}(p^{2}-1)}
. Moreover, if
v(g_{k}(a))\leq k-n+1+\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
, then
v(a-\gamma)=\frac{1}{p^{2n}}(v(g_{k}(a))-k+n)
.

Proof.

Write
g_{k}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{p^{2k}}(x-\gamma_{i})
where
\gamma=\gamma_{1}
. If
a=\gamma
, there is nothing to prove; hence, from now on we assume that
a\neq\gamma
.
Let
r
be the unique integer such that
\frac{1}{p^{2r}(p^{2}-1)}<v(a-\gamma)\leq\frac{1}{p^{2r-2}(p^{2}-1)}
and fix an ordering on the roots
\gamma_{i}
such that the sequence
(v(\gamma-\gamma_{i}))_{1\leq i\leq p^{2k}}
is (weakly) decreasing. We claim that:

v(a-\gamma_{i})=\begin{cases}v(a-\gamma)&\text{if }1\leq i\leq p^{2r},\\
v(\gamma-\gamma_{i})&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Indeed, if
i>p^{2r}
, then by
Proposition

4.12
,
v(\gamma-\gamma_{i})<v(a-\gamma)
so
v(a-\gamma_{i})=v((a-\gamma)+(\gamma-\gamma_{i}))=v(\gamma_{i}-\gamma)
. On the other hand, if
i\leq p^{2r},
then
v(\gamma_{i}-\gamma)\geq v(a-\gamma)
so
v(a-\gamma_{i})=v((a-\gamma)+(\gamma-\gamma_{i}))\geq v(a-\gamma)
, which must be an equality by maximality and hence proves the claim.
If
r\in\{0,\dots,k\}
, using
Remark

4.13
we obtain

(4.5)

\displaystyle v(g_{k}(a))

\displaystyle=v\left(\prod_{i=1}^{p^{2k}}(a-\gamma_{i})\right)=v\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p^{2r}}(a-\gamma_{i})\right)+v\left(\prod\limits_{i=p^{2r}+1}^{p^{2k}}(a-\gamma_{i})\right)=p^{2r}v(a-\gamma)+k-r.

For
r<0
or
r>k
, by a similar calculation we get

v(g_{k}(a))=v(a-\gamma)+k\quad\text{(if $r<0$)}\quad\quad\text{or}\quad\quad v(g_{k}(a))=p^{2k}v(a-\gamma)\quad\text{(if $r>k$)}.

Now note that, for
r<0
, by definition of
r
we have
v(a-\gamma)>\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}-1}
and the first part of the lemma follows. For the second part, the inequality
v(g_{k}(a))=v(a-\gamma)+k>1+\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}+k
implies that the additional assumption never holds. The case
r>k
cannot arise: by definition of
r
we would have
v(a-\gamma)\leq\frac{1}{p^{2k}(p^{2}-1)}
, but the inequality
v(g_{k}(a))=p^{2k}v(a-\gamma)\leq p^{2k}\frac{1}{p^{2k}(p^{2}-1)}=\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
then contradicts the assumption.

It follows that
0\leq r\leq k
.
Equation

4.5
, combined with the assumption
v(g_{k}(a))>k-n+\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
, then implies

r-n+\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}<v(g_{k}(a))+r-k=p^{2r}v(a-\gamma)\leq\frac{p^{2r}}{p^{2r-2}(p^{2}-1)}=1+\frac{1}{p^{2}-1},

and hence
n\geq r
. In particular, we have

v(a-\gamma)>\frac{1}{p^{2r}(p^{2}-1)}\geq\frac{1}{p^{2n}(p^{2}-1)},

which concludes the proof of the first part of the statement.
For the second half, it suffices to show that under the additional assumption we have
r=n
, since the conclusion then follows from
Equation

4.5
. To do so, notice that if we had
r\leq n-1
we would obtain

\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}+k-n+1<p^{2r}v(a-\gamma)+k-r=v(g_{k}(a))\leq k-n+1+\frac{1}{p^{2}-1},

giving a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4.18
.

Assume
v(\alpha^{-1})\geq 0
. Let
c_{k}=\frac{1}{p^{2k-2}(p^{2}-1)}
and let
a\in A_{c_{k}}
. Suppose
p>\max\{\sqrt{k},k-v(\alpha^{-1})-1\}
.
Then there exists a unique root
\gamma\in\mathcal{R}_{k}
such that
v(a^{(0)}-\gamma)>\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
.

Proof.

Note if
\gamma
exists, then it must be unique since if
\gamma^{\prime}
is any other such root, then
v(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})>\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
which contradicts
Proposition

4.12
.

For ease of notation set
\tilde{a}:=a^{(0)}
and
\varepsilon:=\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
. Recall that by definition
\tilde{a}
is a root of the series in
Equation

4.2
. Multiplying
Equation

4.2
by
\pi_{e}p^{k}
and rearranging gives

g_{k}(\tilde{a})=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n\neq-k,...,0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{n+k+1}p^{n+k}\left(\tilde{a}^{p^{-2n}}+\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}{\tilde{a}}^{p^{-2n+1}}\right)\right)-\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}\tilde{a}^{p^{2k+1}},

where we use the convention
\tilde{a}^{p^{-m}}:=a^{(m)}
for
m\geq 0
.

We claim that all the terms on the right-hand side have valuation strictly greater than
k+\varepsilon
. This is clear for the summands with
n>0
, and moreover

v(\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}\tilde{a}^{p^{2k+1}})=v(\alpha^{-1})+p^{2k+1}v(\tilde{a})+\frac{2}{e}\geq v(\alpha^{-1})+p^{2k+1}c_{k}+\varepsilon>v(\alpha^{-1})+p+\varepsilon

which is at least
k+\varepsilon
by assumption.
If
n:=-m\leq-k-1
, then

\displaystyle v\left(p^{n+k}\left(\tilde{a}^{p^{-2n}}+\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}\tilde{a}^{p^{-2n+1}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v\left(p^{k-m}\left(\tilde{a}^{p^{2m}}+\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}\tilde{a}^{p^{2m+1}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(k-m)+\min\{p^{2m}v(\tilde{a}),p^{2m+1}v(\tilde{a})\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(k-m)+p^{2m}c_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k-m)+p^{2m-2k}(1+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-1+p^{2}(1+\varepsilon)=p^{2}+\varepsilon>k+\varepsilon.

Hence we have
v(g_{k}(\tilde{a}))>k+\varepsilon
. The conclusion follows from
Lemma

4.17
applied with
n=0
.
∎

Lemma

4.18
justifies the following definition:

4.4.
Approximate addition formulae in
A_{c}

We now prove two technical lemmas which will allow us to replace the complicated subtraction formulae of the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector space structure on
A_{c}
with much simpler operations in
R
and in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}
. Note that given elements of
A_{c}\subset R
we can subtract them both using the vector space structure of §
4.1
(denoted
a\ominus b
) and using the ring structure of
R
(denoted
a-b
).

Lemma 4.20
.

Let
c>0
and
a,b\in A_{c}
. Then
v_{R}(a\ominus b)=v_{R}(a-b).

Proof.

By
Equation

4.3
and
Lemma

3.1
we have

\displaystyle a\ominus b

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}S_{m}((-1)^{m}a^{p^{m}},\ldots,a^{p^{2}},-a^{p},a;(-1)^{m+1}b^{p^{m}},\ldots,-b^{p^{2}},b^{p},-b)

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}(a-b)(1+g_{m}(a,b)).

In particular, since
v_{R}(a)>0
and
v_{R}(b)>0
, and
g_{m}
has no constant term, we have
v_{R}(g_{m}(a,b))>0
and therefore
v_{R}(1+g_{m}(a,b))=0
. Passing to the limit in the previous equation and using the fact that the valuation is continuous, we obtain as desired
v_{R}(a\ominus b)=v_{R}(a-b)
.
∎

Lemma 4.21
.

Let
c_{k}=\frac{1}{p^{2k-2}(p^{2}-1)}
and
a,b\in A_{c_{k}}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

a\equiv b\bmod{A_{p^{2k}c_{k}}}
;

(ii)

v_{R}(a-b)\geq p^{2k}c_{k}
;

(iii)

v(a^{(0)}-b^{(0)})>\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
.

Proof.

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii): This is immediate from
Lemma

4.20
since
a\equiv b\bmod{A_{p^{2k}c_{k}}}
is by definition equivalent to
a\ominus b\in A_{p^{2k}c_{k}}
, that is,
v_{R}(a\ominus b)\geq p^{2k}c_{k}
.

Before proving the remaining equivalences, we first make a remark. By definition we have

v_{R}(a-b)=\lim_{n\to\infty}v\left(a^{(n)}-b^{(n)}\right)^{p^{n}},

and since
p
is odd we can write

\displaystyle\left(a^{(n)}-b^{(n)}\right)^{p^{n}}

\displaystyle=\left(a^{(n)}\right)^{p^{n}}-\left(b^{(n)}\right)^{p^{n}}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq p^{n}-1}\binom{p^{n}}{i}\left(a^{(n)}\right)^{i}\left(-b^{(n)}\right)^{p^{n}-i}

\displaystyle=a^{(0)}-b^{(0)}+\left(\text{terms of valuation }\geq 1+c_{k}\right),

where we have used
Lemma

4.14
(i), and
v(a^{(n)})=\frac{1}{p^{n}}v(a^{(0)})\geq\frac{c_{k}}{p^{n}}
and similarly
v(b^{(n)})\geq\frac{c_{k}}{p^{n}}
.
In particular, passing to the limit in
n
we obtain

(4.6)

v_{R}(a-b)>\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}\Longleftrightarrow v(a^{(0)}-b^{(0)})>\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii): This follows directly from
Equation

4.6
since
v_{R}(a-b)\geq p^{2k}c_{k}=\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}-1}>\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Note that if
v(a^{(0)}-b^{(0)})>\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
then
v_{R}(a\ominus b)=v_{R}(a-b)>\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
by
Lemma

4.20
and

4.6
. Now the description of possible valuations for

given by
Lemma

4.9
shows that
v_{R}(a-b)=v_{R}(a\ominus b)\geq\frac{p^{2}}{p^{2}-1}=p^{2k}c_{k}
as desired.
∎

4.5.
Proof of
Theorem

4.2

We now show that the map
\Phi_{k}
of
Definition

4.19
factors via
A_{c_{k}}/p^{k}A_{c_{k}}
and induces a Galois-equivariant bijection
\Phi_{k}:A_{c_{k}}/p^{k}A_{c_{k}}\to\mathcal{R}_{k}
.

Theorem 4.22
.

\Phi_{k}
descends to an isomorphism
A_{c_{k}}/p^{k}A_{c_{k}}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{k}
of
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
-sets.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\Phi}_{k}:A_{c_{k}}/p^{k}A_{c_{k}}\to\mathcal{R}_{k}
be the induced map. We must show that it is well-defined, bijective and Galois equivariant. Recall from
Lemma

4.11
that
p^{k}A_{c_{k}}=A_{p^{2k}c_{k}}
.

Well-defined: Let
a_{1}\equiv a_{2}\bmod{A_{p^{2k}c_{k}}}
and let
\gamma_{1}=\Phi_{k}(a_{1}),\gamma_{2}=\Phi_{k}(a_{2})
. Then
v(a_{1}^{(0)}-a_{2}^{(0)})>\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
by
Lemma

4.21
and
v(\gamma_{1}-a_{1}^{(0)}),v(\gamma_{2}-a_{2}^{(0)})>\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
by definition of
\Phi_{k}
. Hence

v(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})=v((\gamma_{1}-a_{1}^{(0)})+(a_{1}^{(0)}-a_{2}^{(0)})-(\gamma_{2}-a_{2}^{(0)}))>\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}.

Therefore
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}
by
Proposition

4.12
and
\tilde{\Phi}_{k}
is well-defined.

Bijective: Note that both sides have order
p^{2k}
(see
Lemma

4.11
and
Proposition

4.12
), so it suffices to prove injectivity. Let
\gamma=\tilde{\Phi}_{k}(a_{1})=\tilde{\Phi}_{k}(a_{2})
so
v(\gamma-a_{1}^{(0)}),v(\gamma-a_{2}^{(0)})>\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
. Hence
v(a_{1}^{(0)}-a_{2}^{(0)})>\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
and therefore
a_{1}\equiv a_{2}\bmod{A_{p^{2k}c_{k}}}
by
Lemma

4.21
.

Galois equivariant: Let
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
. Since
\sigma
permutes the roots of
g_{k}
(which is defined over
K
) and is an isometry for valuations, we have that
v(\sigma(\Phi_{k}(a))-\sigma(a^{(0)}))=v(\Phi_{k}(a)-a^{(0)})>\frac{1}{p^{2}-1}
and hence
\sigma(\Phi_{k}(a))
satisfies the defining condition to be
\Phi_{k}(\sigma(a))
, so
\Phi_{k}
is Galois equivariant as required.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

4.2
.

This is an immediate corollary of
Theorem

4.22
, noting that
A_{c_{k}}/p^{k}A_{c_{k}}\cong E[p^{k}]
by
Lemma

4.11
; since
g_{k}
is defined over

, the equality of fields also follows.
∎

Proposition 4.23
.

Suppose the hypotheses of
Theorem

4.2
. Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{R}_{k}
and define

\sigma\bullet\gamma=\begin{cases}\Phi_{k}\!\big(\zeta_{e}^{\,m}\,\sigma(\Phi_{k}^{-1}(\gamma))\big),&\text{if }\sigma\bmod\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/K)=\omega\tau_{e}^{m},\\[4.0pt]

Then

(i)

\Phi_{k}
extends to an isomorphism

of
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-sets, with respect to the natural action on

\bullet
-action on

(ii)

(iii)

\sigma\bullet\gamma
is the unique root

of

which maximises

(resp.

(resp.

Proof.

First note that Volkov
[

, p. 126]
, starting from the action of Equation (

), defines a twisted action of

where
\sigma(a)
denotes the natural action of

on

becomes an isomorphism of

\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})

is equipped with the

-action and
\mathcal{R}_{k}
with the

(i): It is clear by definition that the bijection

for all

.

(ii): If

(iii): Suppose

; the other case is identical. It suffices to prove that

since the claim then follows from

4.12
, so it remains to compute this valuation. Write

\Phi_{k}(\zeta_{e}^{m}\sigma(\Phi_{k}^{-1}(\gamma)))-\zeta_{e}^{m}\sigma(\gamma)=\Phi_{k}(\zeta_{e}^{m}\sigma(\Phi_{k}^{-1}(\gamma)))-\left(\zeta_{e}^{m}\sigma(\Phi_{k}^{-1}(\gamma))\right)^{(0)}+\left(\zeta_{e}^{m}\sigma(\Phi_{k}^{-1}(\gamma))\right)^{(0)}-\zeta_{e}^{m}\sigma(\gamma).

). On the other hand, let

to

, we have

since

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose

. The splitting fields of
g_{k}
and

over

coincide.

We first show the following technical lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\gamma_{\infty}
be a root of

,
\gamma
a root of
g_{k}
, and
\zeta
a

•

•

v(g_{k}^{\infty}(\gamma))=v(g_{k}(\zeta\gamma))\geq v(\alpha^{-1})+1+\frac{2}{e}+\frac{p}{p^{2}-1}
.

Moreover equality holds when

(resp.

equals
\frac{1}{p^{2k-2}(p^{2}-1)}

Proof.

Set

and

. We have

(6.1)

We claim that for

, we have

with equality precisely when

and

and strict inequality otherwise. Indeed, this is clear if
n\geq 2
; the case
n=1
is a direct computation using that
v(x)\geq\frac{1}{p^{2k-2}(p^{2}-1)}
by
Proposition

4.12
. Combining this claim with (
6.1
) and the fact that
g(\gamma)=h(\gamma_{\infty})=0
then completes the proof for the valuations of
g(\gamma_{\infty})
and

.

Finally, consider

. Notice that
\zeta^{p^{2}}=\zeta
, and so
h(\zeta\gamma)=\zeta h(\gamma)
. Hence

\displaystyle g(\zeta\gamma)

\displaystyle=\zeta h(\gamma)+\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}\zeta^{p}\sum_{n=1}^{k}(-1)^{n}p^{n}\gamma^{p^{2k-2n+1}}=\zeta g(\gamma)+\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}(\zeta^{p}-\zeta)\sum_{n=1}^{k}(-1)^{n}p^{n}\gamma^{p^{2k-2n+1}}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}(\zeta^{p}-\zeta)\sum_{n=1}^{k}(-1)^{n}p^{n}\gamma^{p^{2k-2n+1}}.

Since

is a unit, we get
v(g(\zeta\gamma))=v\left(\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{k}(-1)^{n}p^{n}\gamma^{p^{2k-2n+1}}\right)=v(g(\gamma)-h(\gamma))=v(h(\gamma))
, where we have used (

) and the fact that

by assumption. This concludes the proof.

∎

7.1.
Preliminary estimates

We note the following version of the freshman’s dream:

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer and
a,b\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
with
v(a)\geq 0,v(b)\geq 0
. Then

(a+b)^{p^{n}}\equiv a^{p^{n}}+b^{p^{n}}\bmod p^{1+p^{n}\min\{v(a),v(b)\}}.

Proof.

We have
(a+b)^{p^{n}}-(a^{p^{n}}+b^{p^{n}})=\sum_{i=1}^{p^{n}-1}\binom{p^{n}}{i}a^{i}b^{p^{n}-i}
. Each binomial coefficient has valuation at least
1
by
Lemma

4.14
(i), while
v(a^{i}b^{p^{n}-i})=iv(a)+(p^{n}-i)v(b)\geq p^{n}\min\{v(a),v(b)\}
.
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

Suppose
p>3
and recall that
k=v(\alpha^{-1})+2
. Let
x,y\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
be elements such that
v(x)\geq\frac{1}{3p^{2}}
and
v(y)\geq 0
.
We have
g(x+y)\equiv g(x)+g(y)\mod p^{k+\min\{pv(x),1+v(x)\}}
.

Proof.

Set
h(x)=g_{k}^{\infty}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{k}(-1)^{n}p^{n}x^{p^{2k-2n}}
. By definition we have

g(x+y)=h(x+y)+\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{k}(-1)^{n}p^{n}(x+y)^{p^{2k-2n+1}}.

Letting
H_{n,i}:=(-1)^{n}p^{n}\binom{p^{2k-2n}}{i}x^{i}y^{p^{2k-2n}-i}
, every term in
h(x+y)
is of the form

(-1)^{n}p^{n}(x+y)^{p^{2k-2n}}=(-1)^{n}p^{n}x^{p^{2k-2n}}+(-1)^{n}p^{n}y^{p^{2k-2n}}+\sum_{i=1}^{p^{2k-2n}-1}H_{n,i}.

Consider a fixed
i\in\{1,\ldots,p^{2k-2n}-1\}
and let
r\in\mathbb{Z}
be the unique integer such that
p^{r-1}\leq i<p^{r}
. Note that
1\leq r\leq 2k-2n
. We show that for every
0\leq n\leq k-1
and
1\leq i\leq p^{2k-2n}-1
we have
v(H_{n,i})\geq k+\min\{pv(x),1+v(x)\}
. By
Lemma

4.14
(ii) we have

v(H_{n,i})\geq n+(2k-2n-r+1)+iv(x)=k+(k-n)-r+1+iv(x).

Since
k-n\geq\lceil\frac{r}{2}\rceil
and
i\geq p^{r-1}
by definition, we obtain
v(H_{n,i})\geq k-\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor+1+p^{r-1}v(x)
; this equals
k+1+v(x)
when
r=1
. If
r\geq 2
, we claim
v(H_{n,i})
is greater than
k+pv(x)
. To see this, note that
k-\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor+1+p^{r-1}v(x)=k+pv(x)+(p^{r-1}-p)v(x)-\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor+1\geq k+pv(x)+\frac{p^{r-2}-1}{3p}-\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor+1
, so it suffices to prove
\frac{p^{r-2}-1}{3p}\geq\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor-1
, which is easily done by induction.

This shows
h(x+y)\equiv h(x)+h(y)\mod p^{k+\min\{pv(x),1+v(x)\}}
. To conclude, it suffices to prove that

\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{k}(-1)^{n}p^{n}(x+y)^{p^{2k-2n+1}}\equiv\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{k}(-1)^{n}p^{n}(x^{p^{2k-2n+1}}+y^{p^{2k-2n+1}})\mod p^{k+\min\{pv(x),1+v(x)\}},

for which it suffices to show that
p^{n}(x+y)^{p^{2k-2n+1}}\equiv p^{n}(x^{p^{2k-2n+1}}+y^{p^{2k-2n+1}})\mod p^{2+\min\{pv(x),1+v(x)\}}
for
1\leq n\leq k
since
v(\alpha^{-1})=k-2.

Expanding the power on the left-hand side and cancelling like terms, we are left with showing

p^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{p^{2k-2n+1}-1}{p^{2k-2n+1}\choose i}x^{i}y^{p^{2k-2n-1}-i}\equiv 0\bmod{p^{2+\min\{pv(x),1+v(x)\}}}.

Note that every summand has valuation at least
1+v(x)
, since each binomial coefficient has valuation at least
1
by
Lemma

4.14
(i). The statement is now obvious for
n\geq 2
. For
n=1
, we consider separately
i<p
and
i\geq p
. If
i\geq p
, then the summand has valuation at least
1+iv(x)\geq 1+pv(x)
, whereas if
i<p
, then

4.14
(ii) gives
v(\binom{p^{2k-1}}{i}x^{i})\geq 2k-1+1-1+iv(x)\geq 3+v(x)
since
k\geq 2
.
∎

Lemma 7.6

We have
\gamma^{p^{2k-2}(p^{2}-1)}\equiv p(1+\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}\gamma^{p^{2k-2}(p-1)})\mod p^{2-\frac{1}{p^{2}}}

, so we have

of valuation at least

we obtain

with

, which gives the first part of the statement.

Now notice that the Taylor series for

has coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
since

, so it converges for any

. Raising the equality
\gamma^{p^{2k-2}(p^{2}-1)}=p\left(1+\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}\gamma^{p^{2k-2}(p-1)}+\delta^{\prime}\right)
to the power

\gamma^{p^{2k-2}\cdot\frac{2(p^{2}-1)}{e}}=\xi\pi_{e}^{2}\left(1+\frac{2}{e}\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}\gamma^{p^{2k-2}(p-1)}+\frac{2}{e}\delta^{\prime}+\delta^{\prime\prime}\right)
with
v(\pi_{e}^{2})=\frac{2}{e}
,
v(\delta^{\prime})\geq 1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}
, and
v(\delta^{\prime\prime})\geq 2\min\{v(\delta^{\prime}),v\left(\alpha^{-1}\pi_{e}^{2}\gamma^{p^{2k-2}(p-1)}\right)\}\geq\min\{2-\frac{2}{p^{2}},\frac{4}{e}+\frac{2}{p+1}\}
.
These inequalities easily imply the second congruence in the statement.
∎

Note that different values of

Definition 8.5

Definition 8.6
.

Let

be an algebraic extension of

and let

be an elliptic curve in short Weierstrass form. Let

The formal logarithm of

In particular,

.

Definition 8.7
.

Take

in the previous definition. We shall write

We collect here two results about formal logarithms that will be useful later.

Lemma 8.8

(Yasuda,
[

, Corollary 6(a)]
)
.

Let

be a

-algebra and

be given by

Let

\log_{\widehat{E}}(t)=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}{2m+3n\choose m+2n,m,n}\,A^{m}B^{n}\,\frac{t^{4m+6n+1}}{4m+6n+1}.

Lemma 8.9

Write

(ii)

(iii)

(ii) As

8.3.
The connection with formal logarithms

For a power series ring
R[[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]]
, we will write
R[[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]]_{0}
for the ideal of series with zero constant coefficient.

Definition 8.10
.

Following Fontaine
[
6
, p. 166]
and Kawachi
[
15
, p. 695]
, we set

MH_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\Gamma)=\left\{f\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}[[t]]_{0}:\ \frac{d}{dt}f\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[t]],\quad f(x)+f(y)-f(\Gamma(x,y))\in p\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[x,y]]_{0}\right\}/p\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[t]],

where
\Gamma(x,y)
is the formal group law of the formal group
\Gamma
. We will write
[f(t)]
for the class of

in

.

Remark 8.11
.

The prototypical example of an element of
MH_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\Gamma)
is

, where we take
t=-x/y
(cf.
Definition

8.6
); indeed
\log_{\Gamma}(x)+\log_{\Gamma}(y)-\log_{\Gamma}(\Gamma(x,y))=0
by definition.

Lemma 8.12
.

Let
M
be the Dieudonné module of the
p
-divisible group
\tilde{E}(p)
of
\tilde{E}
. The following hold.

(i)

There is an isomorphism of
\varphi
-modules

, where
\varphi
acts on
MH_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\Gamma)
by the formula

\varphi([f(t)])=[f(t^{p})].

(ii)

There exists a generator
e_{1}\in M
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\varphi]
-module such that
w(e_{1})=[\log_{\Gamma}(t)]
. The elements
e_{1},e_{2}:=\varphi(e_{1})
form a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis of
M
, and

w(e_{1})=[\log_{\Gamma}(t)],\quad w(e_{2})=\varphi([\log_{\Gamma}(t)])=[\log_{\Gamma}(t^{p})]

is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis of
MH_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\Gamma)
. Moreover,
e_{1}
is “adapted” to the automorphism group of
\tilde{E}
in the sense of
[
33
, p. 91]
, that is,
e_{1}\otimes 1\in M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
is an eigenvector for the action of the automorphisms of
(\tilde{E})_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}}
.

Proof.

Since
\tilde{E}
is supersingular,
[
34
, p. 86]
gives that not only is
M
free of rank
2
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, but it is also free of rank
1
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\varphi]
where
\varphi
satisfies
\varphi^{2}+p=0
and every

is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\varphi]
-generator. As recalled in
[
15
, p. 695]
,
[
6
, III, Proposition 6.5]
gives an isomorphism

of

-modules, where the Frobenius

acts by transport of structure. An easy calculation using the formulae in
[
15

yields that the
\varphi
-action on

is given by
\varphi([f(t)])=[f(t^{p})]
; this shows (i).

Fix an automorphism of order

of
\tilde{E}_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}}=(E_{\mathrm{CM}})_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}}
. It lifts uniquely to
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}}(E_{\mathrm{CM}})\cong\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{e}]^{\times}
, which (up to replacing it with its inverse) induces

on the completion of the local ring at

is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\varphi]
-generator, we choose the

-generator

Proposition 8.19
.

Let

be a lift of
\tilde{E}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
and write

\beta\equiv-\frac{p}{\pi_{e}}\frac{d_{p^{2k+1}}}{d_{p^{2k}}}\bmod\pi_{e}^{ke+1}.

In particular, its valuation is given by the formula

v(\beta)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(v(d_{p^{2k+1}})+(k+1)-\frac{1}{e}\right).

Proof.

Throughout the proof, a congruence

for

(not necessarily in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
) means as usual that the difference

By examining coefficients of (

d_{r}=ad_{\mathrm{CM},r}+b\tfrac{\pi_{e}}{p}d_{\mathrm{CM},r/p}\,\,\bmod{\pi_{e}\mathcal{O}_{L}},\qquad\text{where }d_{\mathrm{CM},r/p}=0\text{ if }p\nmid r.

Taking

to be a power of

and using

(i) and (ii), we find that for all
k\geq 0

d_{p^{2k}}\equiv ap^{-k}u_{p^{2k}}\bmod{\pi_{e}},\quad d_{p^{2k+1}}\equiv b\tfrac{\pi_{e}}{p}p^{-k}u_{p^{2k}}\bmod{\pi_{e}}

where
u_{p^{2k}}:=d_{\mathrm{CM},p^{2k}}\cdot p^{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
; equivalently

a\equiv d_{p^{2k}}p^{k}u_{p^{2k}}^{-1}\bmod{\pi_{e}^{ke+1}},\quad b\equiv d_{p^{2k+1}}\tfrac{p}{\pi_{e}}p^{k}u_{p^{2k}}^{-1}\bmod{\pi_{e}^{(k+1)e}}.

Since
(k+1)e\geq ke+1
and
a\in\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
(
Corollary

8.18
), the congruence on
\beta
follows. The congruence on
b
also shows that
v(b)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(v(d_{p^{2k+1}})+k+1-\frac{1}{e}\right)
; since
a
is a unit, the valuation formula in the statement is an immediate consequence.
∎

Remark 8.20
.

The congruence
d_{p^{2k}}\equiv ap^{-k}u_{p^{2k}}\bmod{\pi_{e}}
established in the course of the previous proof, combined with the fact that
a
is a unit, shows in particular that
v(d_{p^{2k}})=-k
for all
k\geq 0
. Similarly, since
v(b)\geq 0
we obtain
v(d_{p^{2k+1}})\geq\frac{1}{e}-(k+1)
.
We can then rewrite the congruence in the statement of
Proposition

8.19
as
\beta\equiv-\frac{b^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}}\bmod{\pi_{e}^{ke+1}}
, where the numerator
b^{\prime}=\frac{p^{k+1}}{\pi_{e}}\cdot d_{p^{2k+1}}
is in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
and the denominator
a^{\prime}=p^{k}d_{p^{2k}}
is in
\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
. Since we take a congruence modulo
\pi_{e}^{ke+1}
, it suffices to compute
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}
modulo
\pi_{e}^{ke+1}
. We discuss how to do this efficiently, starting with
b^{\prime}
. Using
Lemma

8.8
, we may write

b^{\prime}=\frac{p^{k+1}}{\pi_{e}}d_{p^{2k+1}}=\frac{1}{p^{k}\pi_{e}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geq 0\\
4m+6n+1=p^{2k+1}\end{subarray}}{2m+3n\choose m+2n,m,n}\,A^{m}B^{n}.

The factor in front has valuation
-(k+\frac{1}{e})
, while the
(m,n)
summand has valuation at least
m\,v(A)+n\,v(B)
. Since one of
A,B
has valuation at least
\frac{1}{e}
(using the model (
8.1
)), only summands with
m\leq 2ke+1
(if
v(A)>0
) or

n\leq 2ke+1
(if
v(B)>0
) can contribute modulo
\pi_{e}^{ke+1}
.
In particular, the number of relevant summands grows linearly, rather than exponentially, in
k
. A similar comment applies to
a^{\prime}
, which may be computed using the formula of
Lemma

8.8
, keeping only the
2ke
summands of lowest valuation.

Remark 8.21
.

Let
u\in 1+\pi_{e}\mathcal{O}_{L}
, and let
E^{\prime}
be obtained from
E
by the scaling
(x,y)\mapsto(u^{2}x,u^{3}y)
, so that

E^{\prime}:(y^{\prime})^{2}=(x^{\prime})^{3}+Au^{4}x^{\prime}+Bu^{6}
. It is easy to check that
[\log_{\widehat{E}^{\prime}}]=\frac{1}{u}[\log_{\widehat{E}}]
in
MH_{\mathcal{O}_{L}}(\widehat{E})
. By
Theorem

8.17
(iii), the invariant
\beta
depends only on the line generated by
[\log_{\widehat{E}}]
, and hence this equality shows that we obtain the same
\beta
using either model. In other words,
\beta
is invariant under strict isomorphisms, namely isomorphisms inducing the identity on the special fibre. This invariance is, of course, already implicit in Fontaine’s theory.

Remark 8.22
.

The statement of
[
15
, Proposition 2.2.1]
may suggest a different way to compute
\beta
, by looking only at the coefficient of
t^{p}
in
\log_{\widehat{E}}(t)
and using that
b_{p},\frac{\beta\pi_{e}}{p}
are
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-linearly independent. However, the proof of this proposition appears to be incorrect, since it deduces an exact equality of coefficients from an equality in
MH_{\mathcal{O}_{L}}(\widehat{E})
, where functions are only defined modulo
\pi_{e}\mathcal{O}_{L}[[t]]_{0}
. In particular, it is easy to check that
[
15
, Proposition 2.2.1]
leads to a formula for
\beta
that is not invariant under strict isomorphisms.

8.5.
Valuation of
\beta

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be an elliptic curve with semistability defect
e\geq 3
.
Our purpose in this section is to give explicit formulae expressing the valuations of the
\alpha
and
\beta
parameters of
E
in terms of its
j
-invariant. The following is the key result.

Proposition 8.23
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a potential
e
-lift of
\tilde{E}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
j(E)=j
. The valuation of the
\beta
-parameter of
E_{L}
(cf.
Proposition

8.13
) is given by

v(\beta)=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{1}{3}v(j)-\frac{1}{e}&&\text{ if $e\in\{3,6\}$}\\
\frac{1}{2}v(j-1728)-\frac{1}{e}&&\text{ if $e=4$.}\end{array}\end{cases}

In order to prove
Proposition

8.23
, we will need the following technical estimate on the coefficients of the formal logarithm of
E_{L}
corresponding to the choice of model (
8.1
).

Proposition 8.24
.

Let
\log_{\hat{E}_{L}}(t)=\sum_{r\geq 1}d_{r}t^{r}
be the formal logarithm associated with the good reduction model (
8.1
) of
E_{L}
. Suppose that
e\mid p+1
and
e<p-1
. Then for every
k\geq 0
we have

v\bigl(d_{p^{2k+1}}\bigr)=-(k+1)+v(A)\text{ for }e\in\{3,6\},\;\text{ respectively }\;v\bigl(d_{p^{2k+1}}\bigr)=-(k+1)+v(B)\text{ for }e=4.

Proof.

Recall that by
Lemma

8.8
we have

(8.3)

d_{p^{2k+1}}=p^{-(2k+1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geq 0\\
2m+3n=\frac{p^{2k+1}-1}{2}\end{subarray}}\binom{\frac{p^{2k+1}-1}{2}}{m,\;n,\;m+2n}\,A^{m}B^{n}.

Our strategy is to show that there is a unique term of minimal valuation – this occurs when
n=1
(resp.
m=1
) when
e=4
(resp.
e\in\{3,6\}
) – and then compute its valuation. We only treat the case
e=4
as the case
e\in\{3,6\}
is analogous.

Suppose then that
e=4
so
v(B)>v(A)=0
and set
N=\frac{p^{2k+1}-1}{2}
which is odd since
p\equiv-1\bmod{4}
. The equality
2m+3n=N
implies
n
is odd and the admissible options are
(m_{j},n_{j}):=(m_{0}-3j,1+2j)
for
0\leq j\leq\lfloor m_{0}/3\rfloor
, where
m_{0}=\frac{N-3}{2}
.
For
j
in this interval, write
T_{j}:=\binom{N}{m_{j},\;n_{j},\;m_{j}+2n_{j}}
; by definition we have

T_{j}=\frac{m_{0}!(m_{0}+2)!}{(m_{0}-3j)!(m_{0}+j+2)!}\cdot\frac{1}{(1+2j)!}T_{0}.

We claim that
v(T_{j})\geq v(\frac{1}{(1+2j)!}T_{0})
. We show this by induction. The base case
j=0
is trivial, and for the induction step it suffices to show
v(T_{j+1}\cdot(3+2j)!)\geq v(T_{j}\cdot(1+2j)!)
, which is implied by
v(m_{0}-3j-2)\geq v(m_{0}+j+3)
. The identity

3(m_{0}+j+3)+(m_{0}-3j-2)=p^{2k+1}

shows that, since both summands on the left-hand side are strictly between
0
and
p^{2k+1}
, they must have the same valuation, which completes the induction.

Hence, if
j>0
, then since
v(A)=0
, we have

v(T_{j}A^{m_{j}}B^{1+2j})\geq v(T_{0}A^{m_{0}}B)+2jv(B)-v((1+2j)!)>v(T_{0}A^{m_{0}}B^{n_{0}})

where the last inequality follows using the bounds
v(B)\geq\frac{1}{e}=\frac{1}{4}
and
v((1+2j)!)<\frac{1+2j}{p-1}
. This shows that (
8.3
) has a unique summand of minimal valuation, corresponding to
j=0
, that is,
(m,n)=(m_{0},1)
.

Therefore
v(d_{p^{2k+1}})=-(2k+1)+v(T_{0})+v(B)
so it remains to prove
v(T_{0})=k
. Now by expansion, we see
T_{0}=N\binom{N-1}{m_{0}}
with
v(N)=0
and so we compute the valuation of the binomial coefficient using Kummer’s theorem: letting
S(n)
be the sum of the
p
-adic digits of
n
, we have
v\left(\binom{N-1}{m_{0}}\right)=\frac{1}{p-1}(S(m_{0})+S(N+1-m_{0})-S(N-1))
.
One can check that the base
p
expansions are:

\displaystyle N-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{p-3}{2}\cdot p^{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{2k}\tfrac{p-1}{2}\cdot p^{i};

\displaystyle N-1-m_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{p+1}{4}\cdot p^{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tfrac{p-3}{4}\cdot p^{2i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(p-\tfrac{p+1}{4})\cdot p^{2i+1};

\displaystyle m_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\tfrac{p+1}{4}-2)\cdot p^{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tfrac{p-3}{4}\cdot p^{2i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(p-\tfrac{p+1}{4})\cdot p^{2i+1}.

From this, it is straightforward to compute the sum of the
p
-adic digits of
N-1,N-1-m_{0}
, and
m_{0}
, and Kummer’s theorem gives
v(T_{0})=k
as desired.
∎

Remark 8.25
.

Applying
Proposition

8.24
with
k=0
gives in particular

v(pd_{p})=\begin{cases}v(A),\qquad\text{if $e\in\{3,6\}$}\\
v(B),\qquad\text{if $e=4$}.\end{cases}

Combining

8.8
and the development of

using the multinomial theorem, one sees that the product

coincides with the coefficient of
x^{p^{n}-1}
in
\left(x^{3}+Ax+B\right)^{\frac{p^{n}-1}{2}}
. For

, the reduction modulo

of the coefficient of
x^{p-1}
in
\left(x^{3}+Ax+B\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}
, that is

, is known as the

of the elliptic curve

. This suggests that the ratios

should have an interpretation as higher analogues of the Hasse invariant. Note that by

8.19
these ratios converge

-adically to

.

Corollary 8.26
.

With the good reduction model of
E/L
given in (
8.1
), we have

v(\beta)=-\frac{1}{e}+\begin{cases}v(A),\qquad\text{ if }e\in\{3,6\}\\
v(B),\qquad\text{ if }e=4\end{cases}

Letting
H\in\mathcal{O}_{L}
be the coefficient of
x^{p-1}
in
(x^{3}+Ax+B)^{(p-1)/2}
, we can rewrite this as

v(\beta)=v(H)-\frac{1}{e}.

From
Proposition

8.19
we have
v(\beta)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(v(d_{p^{2k+1}})+(k+1)-\frac{1}{e}\right)
.

Proposition

8.24
shows that

is independent of

and equal to the formula given in the statement. The equality

and hence the formula involving

follow from
Remark

8.25
.
∎

We can now prove our formula for

.

Proof of
Proposition

8.23
.

Corollary

8.26
gives
v(\beta)=v(A)-\frac{1}{e}
(resp.
v(\beta)=v(B)-\frac{1}{e}
) when

(resp.
e=4
), so it remains to express the valuation of the coefficients via the

-invariant, noting that the discriminant

is a unit by assumption as the model of

given by (

The identities

∎

8.6.
Volkov’s
\alpha
and the
p
-adic image

With everything in place, we are finally in a position to completely describe
\alpha
itself for a potential
e
-lift. A priori, it appears that one should compute
\beta
in order to determine the sign
\varepsilon
, especially in the case
e=4
where knowing
v(\beta)
isn’t sufficient. We first show that this can be bypassed via the discriminant and conclude the proof of
Proposition

8.3
(iii).

Proposition 8.27
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a potential
e
-lift with minimal discriminant
\Delta
and write
E\cong E^{\varepsilon}_{\beta}
as in
Proposition

8.3
(iii), for some
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\pi_{e}\cup\mathbb{Z}_{p}\pi_{e}^{e-3}
and some
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
. Then
\varepsilon=1
if and only if
v(\Delta)<6
.
In particular, the Volkov parameter is
\alpha=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{cc}-p\cdot(\beta/\pi_{e})^{-1}&\text{ if }v(\Delta)<6\\
-p\cdot\beta/\pi_{e}^{e-3}&\text{ if }v(\Delta)>6.\end{array}\end{cases}

Proof.

We begin by recalling the action of
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}})
on the special fibre of a good reduction model (equivalently, Néron model) of
E_{K}
; see also
[
26
, p. 497]
.
The Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}})
acts on
E_{K}=E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}}\times_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}}K
via its action on
K
. By functoriality of the Néron model, it also acts on the Néron model
\mathcal{E}
of
E_{K}
, and the map
\mathcal{E}\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_{K}
is compatible with the Galois action on both schemes. Since
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}})
acts trivially on the residue field
\mathcal{O}_{K}/(\pi_{e})\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, it acts on the special fibre of
\mathcal{E}
, which may be canonically identified with
(\tilde{E})_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}}
.

By following Volkov’s argument
[
33
, p. 95]
, one sees that
\varepsilon
is determined as follows: taking
\tau_{e}\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}})
(where
\tau_{e}
is defined by
\tau_{e}(\pi_{e})=\zeta_{e}\pi_{e}
) in the above construction, we obtain an induced automorphism of
(\tilde{E})_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}}
, which in turn acts on its Dieudonné module. The sign
\varepsilon
is defined by the equality
\tau_{e}(e_{1}\otimes 1)=\zeta_{e}^{\varepsilon}(e_{1}\otimes 1)
, where
e_{1}
is the adapted generator of
Lemma

8.12
. Via the isomorphism of
Theorem

8.17
, it thus suffices to understand the action of
\tau_{e}
on
\log_{\Gamma}(t)
. We now do this in terms of a minimal model
y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
of
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with discriminant
\Delta
.

We give details in the case
e=4
and briefly sketch the differences for the remaining cases
e\in\{3,6\}
. Note that when
e=4
the valuation of
\Delta
can only be
3
or
9
(see
[
31
, Table 15.1]
), and that these two cases correspond to
v(A)=1
and
v(A)=3
respectively (with
v(B^{2})>v(A^{3})
in both cases).

Let
u:=\pi_{e}^{-v(A)}
. A good reduction model of
E_{K}
is given by
\mathcal{E}:z^{2}=w^{3}+Au^{4}w+Bu^{6}
, with the isomorphism given by

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\psi:&E_{K}&\to&\mathcal{E}\\
&(x,y)&\mapsto&(u^{2}x,u^{3}y).\end{array}

The action of
\tau_{e}
on the special fibre of
\mathcal{E}
may be computed by reducing modulo
\pi_{e}
the composition

\begin{array}[]{cccc}{}^{\tau_{e}}\psi\circ\psi^{-1}:&\mathcal{E}&\to&{}^{\tau_{e}}\mathcal{E}\\
&(w,z)&\mapsto&(\zeta_{e}^{-2v(A)}w,\zeta_{e}^{-3v(A)}z),\end{array}

where
{}^{\tau_{e}}\mathcal{E}:z^{2}=w^{3}+Au^{4}w+B\zeta_{e}^{-6}u^{6}
. Note that the special fibres of
\mathcal{E}
and
{}^{\tau_{e}}\mathcal{E}
coincide, because
v(Bu^{6})=v(B\zeta_{e}^{-6}u^{6})=v(B)-\frac{6}{4}v(A)>0
. Thus,
\tau_{e}
induces
(w,z)\mapsto(\zeta_{e}^{-2v(A)}w,\zeta_{e}^{-3v(A)}z)
on the special fibre
(\tilde{E})_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}}
, and hence sends the parameter

to
-\zeta_{e}^{-2v(A)}w/\zeta_{e}^{-3v(A)}z=\zeta_{e}^{v(A)}t
.

By
Lemma

8.9
(iii), we find that
\tau_{e}
acts on
\log_{\Gamma}(t)
(hence on
e_{1}\otimes 1
) as
\tau_{e}\log_{\Gamma}(t)=\zeta_{e}^{v(A)}\log_{\Gamma}(t)
, so we obtain
\varepsilon\equiv v(A)\bmod{4}
. This concludes the proof, because we have
v(A)=1
if and only if
v(\Delta)<6
, and similarly
v(A)=3\equiv-1\bmod{4}
if and only if
v(\Delta)>6
.

Finally, in the case

(resp.
e=3
) one takes

(resp.

), and by a similar calculation finds that
\tau_{e}
acts on

as multiplication by
\zeta_{e}^{v(B)}
(resp.
\zeta_{e}^{-v(B)}
). Since
v(\Delta)=2v(B)
can only be

or

(resp.

or

), we find in all cases that the eigenvalue of
\tau_{e}
acting on

is
\zeta_{e}
if
v(\Delta)<6
and
\zeta_{e}^{-1}
if
v(\Delta)>6
; these calculations agree with
[

, Remarque on p. 133]
which avoids the case
v(\alpha)=1
.
∎

We next determine the valuation of
\alpha
purely in terms of invariants that can be easily computed from a Weierstrass model.

Proposition 8.28
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a potential

-lift of

with

(resp.

).

Corollary 8.29

Let

\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)

Let

\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{n_{0}}}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n_{0}})\text{ with index dividing $3$,}\qquad\qquad\text{and}\,\,\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}=\pi_{n_{0}}^{-1}(\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{n_{0}}}).

\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{n_{0}}}=C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n_{0}})

Note that

and

by

8.28

.

It remains to verify the claim on the index
[C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n_{0}}):\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{n_{0}}}]=[C_{ns}^{+}(p):\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}]
. To do this, we consider the action of inertia on
E[p]\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
in terms of the level
2
fundamental character
\psi_{2}
. Volkov
[
33
, p. 129, p. 131]
shows the action is via
\{\psi_{2}^{1-\frac{p^{2}-1}{e}},\psi_{2}^{p+\frac{p^{2}-1}{e}}\}
if
v(\alpha)\leq 0
(resp.
\{\psi_{2}^{1+\frac{p^{2}-1}{e}},\psi_{2}^{p-\frac{p^{2}-1}{e}}\}
if
v(\alpha)\geq 2
). Now the index is
3
if only if both exponents
1\mp\frac{p^{2}-1}{e},p\pm\frac{p^{2}-1}{e}
are divisible by
3
; a quick check shows that if
e=4
or
p\not\equiv 2,5\bmod{9}
then
1\mp\frac{p^{2}-1}{e}\not\equiv 0\bmod 3
which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 8.30
.

The proof of the previous corollary also shows how to determine whether the index
[C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n_{0}}):\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{n_{0}}}]
is
1
or
3
in the missing cases when
e\in\{3,6\}
and
p\equiv 2,5\bmod{9}
via studying the exponents of the fundamental characters in the representation
E[p]\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

Let
\Delta
be a minimal discriminant of
E
. Using that
v(\alpha)\leq 0
if and only if
v(\Delta)<6
(
Proposition

8.28
(ii)), and recalling that
v(\Delta)\in\{\frac{12}{e},\frac{12(e-1)}{e}\}
(cf.
Lemma

2.7
), the index is
3
precisely in the following cases:

p\equiv 2\bmod{9}\,\,\,\text{ and }v(\Delta)\in\{4,10\};\qquad\qquad\text{or }\,\,\,p\equiv 5\bmod{9}\,\,\,\text{ and }v(\Delta)\in\{2,8\}.

Moreover, all four of these cases occur. For
p\equiv 2,5\bmod{9}
, consider the elliptic curves
E_{i}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}:y^{2}=x^{3}+p^{i}
for
i\in\{1,2,4,5\}
. These have potentially good supersingular reduction, minimal discriminant
p^{2i}
, and semistability defect
e=6/\gcd(2,i)
. Comparing with the conditions described above, we deduce that for every prime
p\equiv 2,5\bmod{9}
and each
e\in{3,6}
, the case
[C_{ns}^{+}(p):\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}]=3
does indeed occur.

We also remark that
C_{ns}^{+}(p)
has a unique index
3
subgroup up to conjugacy, so knowledge of the index suffices to describe the Galois image.

9.
Application to elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}

9.1.
Description of the
p
-adic Galois image

As a consequence of
Theorem

5.1
and
[
11
, Theorem 1.6]
we obtain the following structure theorem for the global image of the
p
-adic representations attached to non-CM elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
. Note that, unlike
Theorem

5.1
, this is a statement about representations of the absolute Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}
rather than of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a non-CM elliptic curve and
p>7
be a prime. Consider the mod
p
and
p
-adic Galois representations

\rho_{E,p}:\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}:\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Suppose that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p}\subseteq C_{ns}^{+}(p)
. Then there exists
n\geq 1
such that

\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}=\pi_{n}^{-1}\left(C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n})\right),

where
\pi_{n}:\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
is the canonical projection.

Proof.

We first apply
[
11
, Theorem 1.6]
. Since
p>7
, either:
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}=\pi_{n}^{-1}(C_{ns}^{+}(p^{n}))
; or
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{2}}\cong C_{ns}^{+}(p)\ltimes\{\operatorname{Id}+p\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\varepsilon\\
-b&c\end{smallmatrix}\right):a,b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}.
For contradiction, suppose the second option holds.
We now examine the local image
G:=\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}))\subseteq\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
, in particular the group
\ker(G(p^{2})\rightarrow G(p))
. Recall the definition and notation of the irreducible submodules of
M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
given in
Lemma

2.4
(iii). From the description above, one sees directly that
\ker(G(p^{2})\rightarrow G(p))\subseteq V_{1}\oplus V_{3}
.
On the other hand,
Theorem

5.1
states that either
\ker(G(p^{2})\to G(p))=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
(in case (i), and in case (ii) if
n>2
), or
\ker(G(p^{2})\to G(p))=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3}
(otherwise). In either case, we have a contradiction that completes the proof.
∎

Remark 9.2
.

Remark

5.2
shows that the image of the local Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
via
\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
is not necessarily open in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. This implies that for elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
, the value of
n
in
Theorem

9.1
cannot be bounded by purely
p
-adic arguments.

9.2.
Adelic bounds

We now give an improved version of
[
11
, Theorem 1.5]
. The key of the improvement consists in the fact that, thanks to
Theorem

9.1
, we are able to use a sharper bound on the index of the
p
-adic representations: if
n
is the smallest integer such that
\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}\supset\operatorname{Id}+p^{n}M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, then
[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}):\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}]\leq\frac{1}{2}p^{2n}
, while in
[
11
]
the bound
[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}):\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}]\leq\frac{1}{2}p^{3n}
was used.

Theorem 9.3
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a non-CM elliptic curve and let
\operatorname{h}(j(E))
be the logarithmic Weil height of its
j
-invariant. Let
\rho_{E}=\prod_{p}\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}:\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
. We have

[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}):\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E}]<1.6\cdot 10^{17}(\operatorname{h}(j(E))+480)^{3.11}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}):\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E}]<7\cdot 10^{17}(\operatorname{h}(j(E))+270)^{2+3.251\cdot\delta(12\operatorname{h}(j(E)))}.

where
\delta:(-0.75,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\delta(x):=\frac{1}{\log(\log(x+40)+7.6)-0.903}.

In particular, we have
[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}):\operatorname{Im}\rho_{E}]<\operatorname{h}(j(E))^{2+O\left(\frac{1}{\log\log\operatorname{h}(j(E))}\right)}
as
\operatorname{h}(j(E))
tends to
\infty
.

Before proving this theorem, we state and prove two lemmas. The first exploits
Theorem

9.1
to improve
[
11
, Corollary 6.8]
, whereas the second consolidates several of the results of
[
11
]
, which we will then apply in the proof of
Theorem

9.3
.

Let
