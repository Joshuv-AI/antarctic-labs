Uniform asymptotics for a multidimensional renewal risk model with random number of delayed claims and multivariate subexponentiality
Dimitrios G. Konstantinides, Charalampos D. Passalidis, Meng Yuan

Abstract
. In this paper we examine a multivariate risk model, with common renewal counting process, constant interest rate, and each claim vector is accompanied by a random number of delayed claim vectors. The interest is focused on the asymptotic behavior of the entrance probability of the discounted aggregate claims into some rare-sets, over a finite and an infinite time horizon. Our results study the the case where the main claims and the delayed claims have in some sense, asymptotic equivalent tails, but also the case where the delayed claims are negligible with comparisons with the main claims. More precisely, our estimations over finite time horizon are equipped with local uniformity, and are valid under the assumption of class 𝒮 A \mathcal{S}_{A} , of multivariate subexponential distributions for the claim distributions. On the case of infinite time horizon we need a mild restriction on the distribution class 𝒜 A ∗ \mathcal{A}^{*}_{A} of multivariate subexponential distributions with positive lower Karamata index. The asymptotic relations reflect completely as all the sources of randomness, under the concrete rare-sets A A , and the different dependence structures as well, without loosing elegance in spite of their generality. Further, we provide some more explicit formulas, together with relaxations of some assumptions, for the claim distributions from the multivariate regular variation. For the proof of the main results on infinite time case and for the construction of examples of multivariate distributions we need some closure properties of distribution classes 𝒜 ∗ \mathcal{A}^{*} and 𝒜 A ∗ \mathcal{A}^{*}_{A} . Especially, we present some necessary and sufficient conditions for the closure property with respect to convolution and some sufficient conditions for the closure property with respect to product convolution. Finally, we carry out some numerical studies to show the accuracy of our asymptotic estimations. The examples contain also cases with moderate heavy tails.



Keywords: Discounted aggregate claims; Uniformity;
Infinite time horizon; Lower Karamata index; Closure properties;
Multivariate linear single big jump principle

Introduction

1.1.
Model description

In modern insurance companies the claims are split into two types, the main and the delayed claims. The first type contains claims that are produced
directly by an event, for example in case of natural disaster, such type of claims describe the compensation of the property elements, for example
immobility insurance, or car insurance, or of the health complications, for example injure expenses, or instant death coverage. The second type of
claims includes the delayed claims, that were produced after some time delay, by the event, that causes the main claim. In case of natural disaster
such type of claims can include medical expenses, or death coverage after some curing period.

This way, we find an increasing number of papers focused on risk models with delayed claims, since any ignorance of them, eventually leads to
underestimation of the insurance risk, see relative discussions and literature overview on risk models with delayed claims in
subsection 1.2.

Further, the claim distributions with heavy tails and dependence among the portfolios, attracts the attention of an increasing number of researchers
in the area of multivariate risk models with heavy-tailed claims, see for example

2.1.
Heavy-tailed distribution classes

The multivariate heavy-tailed classes play important role in actuarial and financial mathematics, mostly in modeling the dependence effect, that appears
in real world applications. Special attention was gotten to the class of multivariate regularly varying distributions (
MRV
) that has a variety of applications
in mathematical statistics as well as in applied probability, see
[
30
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
31
]
among others.

During the last years we observed an attempt to study properties and applications of bigger (than

) classes, mostly motivated by seminal paper

[
32
]

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}:=\left\{A\subsetneq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\;:\;A\;\text{open,\,increasing},\;A^{c}\;\text{convex},\;{\bf 0}\notin\overline{A}\right\}\,.

(2.1)

The family

Remark 2.1
.

The set family (
2.1

\displaystyle A_{1}=\left\{{\bf y}\;:\;\sum_{j=1}^{d}l_{j}\,y_{j}>c\right\}\,,

(2.2)

with

,
\sum_{j=1}^{d}l_{j}=1
and
c>0
, and

\displaystyle A_{2}=\left\{{\bf y}\;:\;y_{j}>c_{j}\,,\;\exists\;j=1,\,\ldots,\,d\right\}\,,

(2.3)

with

, for all
j=1,\,\ldots,\,d
.
So, the probabilities in (

) with

, for

, play crucial role in estimation of the insurer’s solvency, in the sense of estimation
of the initial capital adequacy in dynamic framework. We also observe that for

, set

with

, is reduced to the set
(1,\,\infty)
and hence

This probability is well-studied in risk theory and further in the applied probability area. For more examples of sets from family

[
32

[

Hereafter, the distributions
F
have support from the non-negative orthant, and all the one-dimensional distributions have infinite

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
and
{\bf Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}V
, with

{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}

32

\displaystyle Z_{A}:=\sup\left\{u\;:\;{\bf Z}\in u\,A\right\}\,,

\displaystyle\overline{V}_{A}(x)=\mathbf{P}\left(\sup_{{\bf p}\in I_{A}}{\bf p}^{\top}\,{\bf Z}>x\right)=\mathbf{P}\left({\bf Z}\in x\,A\right)\,,

Thus, we have the definition of the multivariate subexponentiality as follows. We say that

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{V_{A}^{n*}}(x)}{\overline{V}_{A}(x)}=n\,,

)

\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}:=\bigcap_{A\in\mathscr{R}}\mathcal{S}_{A}

Through this way,

For an one-dimensional distribution

, with infinite right endpoint, its lower Karamata index is defined as

\displaystyle K_{B}^{-}=-\lim_{v\downarrow 1}\dfrac{\log\overline{B^{*}}(v)}{\log v}\,,

(2.6)

and its lower Matuszewska index is defined as

\displaystyle J_{B}^{-}=-\lim_{v\to\infty}\dfrac{\log\overline{B^{*}}(v)}{\log v}\,,

where

\displaystyle\overline{B^{*}}(v)=\limsup\dfrac{\overline{B}(v\,x)}{\overline{B}(x)}\,,

see in
[
1

V_{A}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
, and further
\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}=\bigcap_{A\in\mathscr{R}}\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}
.

We also denote by

the distribution class
\mathcal{L}
, with positive lower Karamata index, namely
B_{1}\in\mathcal{T}^{*}
, if
B_{1}\in\mathcal{L}
and

Here, we assume that the distributions
F
and
G
belong to class
\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}
to find the asymptotic expressions on infinite
time horizon as well as some uniform asymptotic expressions for all time horizons. For this purpose we need some necessary preliminary lemmas,
that are related with the closure properties with respect to convolution for distributions from classes
\mathcal{A}^{*}
and
\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}

and with respect to product convolution, see in Section 4.2. This kind of properties are also helpful in construction of examples in the classes

\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}
. We notice, that for all distributions
B
, it holds
K_{B}^{-}\leq J_{B}^{-}
, and that
J_{B}^{-}>0

if and only if

, namely if it holds

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{\overline{B}(v\,x)}{\overline{B}(x)}<1\,,

for all (or, equivalently, for some)

. Thus the class
\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}
, introduced in
[

]
, contains
\mathcal{A}^{*}
, although the difference is negligible. As before, we say
V\in\mathcal{A}_{A}
, if
V_{A}\in\mathcal{A}
, hence it holds
\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{A}_{A}
.

An one-dimensional distribution
B
, is called regularly varying with index
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, symbolically
B\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
,
if it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{B}(b\,x)}{\overline{B}(x)}=b^{-\alpha}\,,

for any
b>0
. From
[
1
, Theorem 1.5.2]
, we know that the above relation holds
uniformly in the following sense: for any fixed
\epsilon>0
it holds

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{b\in[\epsilon,\infty)}\left|\dfrac{\overline{B}(b\,x)}{b^{-\alpha}\overline{B}(x)}-1\right|=0\,.

(2.7)

Hence, we say that distribution
V
belongs to the class
MRV
, if there exists one-dimensional distribution
B\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, with
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
and some non-degenerate to zero Radon measure
\mu
such that

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\mathbf{P}({\bf Z}\in x\,{\mathbb{B}})}{\overline{B}(x)}=\mu({\mathbb{B}})\,,

(2.8)

for any Borel set
{\mathbb{B}}\in\overline{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}}
, bounded away from the
{\bf 0}
, which satisfies
\mu(\partial{\mathbb{B}})=0
.
Then we denote
V\in MRV(\alpha,\,B,\,\mu)
.

Let us notice that in the proof of
[
32
, Prop. 4.14]
was found that for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
we
have
\mu(\partial A)=0
,
\mu(A)\in(0,\,\infty)
, therefore if

, then

(the opposite does not holds in general) and hence by the one-dimensional inclusions
(see
[
24
, Ch. 2]
) we find that

\displaystyle MRV\subsetneq\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{\mathscr{R}}\,,

(2.9)

where
MRV
represents the union of all

distributions, see in

[
20
, Prop. 2.1, Rem. 2.2]
. Relation (
2.9
) is valid also for the
classes
\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}
,
\mathcal{S}_{A}
,
\mathcal{L}_{A}
, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
. We observe that the first
inclusion of (

) is not trivial, but contains a rich spectrum of distributions that have moderately heavy tails,
which does not contain class

. The second inclusion of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}

In next Section we construct some examples to show this type of inclusions. It is worth to mention that up to class

\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}
in relation (

linear single big jump principle, due to the property of insensitivity with respect to dimension (see also

[

3.
Local uniform asymptotic results

Now, we present the first main result and its proof, that provides local uniform, with respect to time, asymptotic expressions for
the first probability in (
1.2
), when the claim vectors follow a distribution from the class of multivariate subexponential
distributions. By the term local uniformity we mean that the results are valid uniformly for
t\in\Lambda_{T}
, where

\Lambda_{T}:=[0,\,T]\cap\Lambda
, for all fixed
T\in\Lambda
. Let us notice that hereafter the distribution of
the number of delayed claims
M
, has light tail, that means there exists some
\delta>0
, such that
\mathbf{E}\left[e^{\delta\,M}\right]<\infty
.

3.1.
Main result

The following theorem represents the first main result of the paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set, let consider the discounted aggregate claims of relation (
1.1
). We suppose
that Assumptions
1.1
and
1.2
are satisfied and there exists some
\delta>0
, such that

\mathbf{E}\left[e^{\delta\,M}\right]<\infty
. Let some fixed
T\in\Lambda
.

(i)

If
F,\,G\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
and
G(x\,A)\asymp F(x\,A)
, then it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}_{r}(t)\in x\,A\right)

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sim\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,e^{r\,s}\,A\right)\,\lambda(ds)+\mathbf{E}[M]\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t-s}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf Y}\in x\,e^{r\,(s+y)}\,A\right)\,H(dy)\,\lambda(ds)\,,

uniformly for
t\in\Lambda_{T}
.

(ii)

If
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
and
G(x\,A)=o\left[F(x\,A)\right]
, then it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}_{r}(t)\in x\,A\right)\sim\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,e^{r\,s}\,A\right)\,\lambda(ds)\,,

(3.2)

uniformly for
t\in\Lambda_{T}
.

Remark 3.1
.

If in Theorem
3.1
(i) [or (ii)] we consider that
F,\,G\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}
and
G(x\,A)\asymp F(x\,A)
, for
any
A\in\mathscr{R}
(or
F\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}
and
G(x\,A)=o\left[F(x\,A)\right]
for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, respectively),
then relation (
3.1
) (or, relation (
3.2
), respectively), holds uniformly for
t\in\Lambda_{T}
, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
.
This can be easily verified, by the arbitrariness in the choice of
A\in\mathscr{R}
, in Lemmas
3.1
-
3.4
below.
Theorem
3.1
remains new result, even in the one-dimensional subcase, with

, since contributes the local uniformity to

[
27
, Lem. 3.5]
. In the case where the premiums have bounded densities, then the results of Theorem
3.1
gives easily the finite-time ruin probability (over a ruin set connected with
A
) via some classical technics, see for example proof of
[
19
, Cor. 3.1]
for more details. Hence in the one-dimensional subcase we can equip local uniformity in the

[
27
, Th. 2.1]
, as also we can relax the condition of constant premiums density into bounded density. We omit such a section in order to safe space. Furthermore, Theorem
3.1
, when

almost surely, is reduced to
[
15

.

Remark 3.2
.

Relations (
3.1
) and (

discounted aggregate claims. In (

), where the delayed claims distribution has negligible tail, in comparison with the

Argumentation of Theorem

Before presentation of the argumentation of the previous result, we need some preliminary lemmas. The first lemma is inspired by

we need its non-randomly weighted multivariate version. The following lemma generalizes the
[
15
, Lem. 5.1]

Hereafter, we denote

\displaystyle Z_{A}^{(i)}:=\sup\left\{u\;:\;{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in u\,A\right\}\,,

for any

, and for two random variables

,

with

if for any

it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(Z_{A}^{(i)}>x)\leq\mathbf{P}(Z_{A}^{(j)}>x)\,.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set. We consider
n+m
non-negative, independent random vectors
{\bf Z}^{(1)},\,\ldots,\,{\bf Z}^{(n+m)}
, with
distributions
V_{1},\,\ldots,\,V_{n+m}
, respectively. Let
0<a\leq b<\infty
be some constants and
{\bf c}_{n}:=(c_{1},\,\ldots,\,c_{n})^{\top}\in[a,\,b]^{n}
,
while the
c_{n+1},\,\ldots,\,c_{n+m}
take any values from the
[0,\,b]^{m}
, with the assumption that for any
n+1\leq j\leq n+m
, there exists some

i_{j}\in\{1,\,\ldots,\,n\}
, such that
c_{j}\leq c_{i_{j}}
. If
V_{1},\,\ldots,\,V_{n}\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
and there exists
V\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, with

V_{i}(x\,A)\asymp V(x\,A)
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, and
V_{j}(x\,A)=o\left[V(x\,A)\right]
, for any
j=n+1,\,\ldots,\,n+m
, then it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\,,

(3.11)

uniformly for
{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}
, in the sense

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}}\left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)}-1\right|=0\,.

Proof.

For the lower bound for (
3.11
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\geq\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\,,

uniformly for
{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}
, where at the first step we used the increasing property of the set
A
, and that the summands
are non-negative, while at the second step we take into account
[
15
, Lem. 5.1]
.

It remains to show the upper bound for (
3.11
). At first, we observe that from
V_{1},\,\ldots,\,V_{n}\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
,
V\in\mathcal{S}_{A}

and
V_{i}(x\,A)\asymp V(x\,A)
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, is implied by
[
17
, Prop. 3.3]
that
V_{1},\,\ldots,\,V_{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
.

Further, since
V_{j}(x\,A)=o\left[V(x\,A)\right]
, for
j=n+1,\,\ldots,\,n+m
, hence for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\,1)
we can find some
x_{0}=x_{0}(\varepsilon)>0
,
such that it holds

\displaystyle V_{j}(x\,A)\leq\varepsilon\,V_{i_{j}}(x\,A)\,,

(3.12)

for any
x\geq x_{0}
, recall that
V_{i_{j}}(x\,A)\asymp V(x\,A)
for all
i_{j}\in\{1,\,\ldots,\,n\}
. We now construct the new random vectors
{\bf Z}^{(n+1)*},\,\ldots,\,{\bf Z}^{(n+m)*}

which are independent each other and independent of the rest sources of randomness, with distributions

\displaystyle V_{j}^{*}(x\,A)=\max\{\varepsilon\,V_{i_{j}}(x\,A),\,V_{j}(x\,A)\}\geq V_{j}(x\,A)\,,

(3.13)

respectively, for any
x\geq 0
. We see from relations (
3.12
), (
3.13
) that the
{\bf Z}^{(n+1)*},\,\ldots,\,{\bf Z}^{(n+m)*}

are also non-negative. Let
Z_{A}^{(i)*}:=\sup\{u\;:\;{\bf Z}^{(i)*}\in u\,A\}
,
for any
j=n+1,\,\ldots,\,n+m
. From relation (
3.13
) we obtain that

Z_{A}^{(i)}\leq_{st}Z_{A}^{(j)*}
. From relation (
3.13
) is implied

\displaystyle V_{j}^{*}(x\,A)\sim\varepsilon\,V_{i_{j}}(x\,A)\,,

(3.14)

and consequently
V_{j}^{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, by the closure property of
\mathcal{S}_{A}
with respect to strong tail equivalence, see

[
32
, Prop. 4.12(a)]
. Hence, from (
3.14
) we find
V_{j}^{*}(x\,A)\asymp V(x\,A)
. Therefore, we find

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\leq\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+m}c_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}+\sum_{j=n+1}^{n+m}c_{i_{j}}\,Z_{A}^{(j)*}>x\right)

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right)+\sum_{j=n+1}^{n+m}\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i_{j}}\,Z_{A}^{(j)*}>x\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)+\sum_{j=n+1}^{n+m}\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i_{j}}\,{\bf Z}^{(j)*}\in x\,A\right)\,,

(3.15)

uniformly for
{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}
, where at the first step we used
[
16
, Prop. 2.4]
, at the
second step we apply the inequalities
Z_{A}^{(j)}\leq_{st}Z_{A}^{(j)*}
,
c_{j}\leq c_{i_{j}}
and at the third step we use
[
38
, Lem. 1]

since the
Z_{A}^{(i)}
are subexponential with weak equivalent tails and all the coefficients belong to the interval

. By (
3.14
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=n+1}^{n+m}\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i_{j}}\,{\bf Z}^{(j)*}\in x\,A\right)\sim\varepsilon\,\sum_{j=n+1}^{n+m}\mathbf{P}\left(c_{i_{j}}\,{\bf Z}^{(i_{j})}\in x\,A\right)\,.

(3.16)

From relations (

) and (

∎

Remark 3.6

The next auxiliary assertion follows immediately by proof of

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

be some fixed set, and
\{{\bf Z}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
be i.i.d. copies of non-negative random vectors,
with

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{t\in\Lambda_{T}}\sup_{x\geq 0}\dfrac{\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf Z}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\,\tau_{i}}\in x\,A\,,\;N(t)=n\right)}{\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf Z}\in x\,e^{r\,s}\,A\right)\,\lambda(ds)}=0\,.

(3.19)

The last auxiliary result, is related with the closure properties of distribution class

with respect to convolution.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set, and
V_{1},\,V_{2}
be distributions with support in the non-negative orthant. We suppose that

V_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
and
V_{2}(x\,A)=o\left[V_{1}(x\,A)\right]
. Then it holds

\displaystyle V_{1}*V_{2}(x\,A)\sim V_{1}(x\,A)\,,

(3.20)

and further
V_{1}*V_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
.

Proof.

The proof could be an easy consequence of
[
16
, Cor. 3.1]
, and some one-dimensional closure properties
with respect to convolution of subexponential distributions. However, for sake of completeness, we provide an alternative proof.

Let
{\bf Z}^{(1)}\,,\;{\bf Z}^{(2)}
be non-negative random vectors with distributions
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, respectively. Then

\displaystyle V_{1}*V_{2}(x\,A)=\mathbf{P}\left({\bf Z}^{(1)}+{\bf Z}^{(2)}\in x\,A\right)\leq\mathbf{P}\left(Z_{A}^{(1)}+Z_{A}^{(2)}>x\right)\sim\mathbf{P}\left(Z_{A}^{(1)}>x\right)=V_{1}(x\,A)\,,

where at the second step we used
[
16
, Prop. 2.4]
, and at the third step we take advantage from

[
7
, Cor. 3.18]
, since
\overline{V}_{A}^{(2)}(x)=o\left[\overline{V}_{A}^{(1)}(x)\right]
, see also

[
24
, Prop. 3.13(v)]
. The last relation provides the upper bound for (
3.20
).

For the lower bound, since the set
xA
is increasing and the
{\bf Z}^{(1)}\,,\;{\bf Z}^{(2)}
are non-negative, via Bonferroni inequality we obtain

\displaystyle V_{1}*V_{2}(x\,A)\geq\mathbf{P}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{2}\left\{{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{P}\left({\bf Z}^{(1)}\in x\,A\right)+\mathbf{P}\left({\bf Z}^{(2)}\in x\,A\right)-\mathbf{P}\left({\bf Z}^{(1)}\in x\,A\right)\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf Z}^{(2)}\in x\,A\right)

\displaystyle\sim V_{1}(x\,A)+V_{2}(x\,A)=[1+o(1)]\,V_{1}(x\,A)\,.

Which completes the proof of relation (
3.20
).

From (
3.20
), keeping in mind that
V_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, from the closure property of
\mathcal{S}_{A}
with respect to
strong tail equivalence, see
[
32
, Prop. 4.12(a)]
, we obtain
V_{1}*V_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
.
∎

\mathbf{P}\left(\Theta_{1}\vee\Theta_{2}\leq x\right)=B_{1}\,B_{2}(x)
, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
B_{1},\,B_{2}
be distributions on
{\mathbb{R}}
, with
B_{1},\,B_{2}\in\mathcal{T}^{*}
. Then the following statements are equivalent

(i)

B_{1}*B_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
.

(ii)

B_{1}\,B_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
.

(iii)

p\,B_{1}+(1-p)\,B_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
, for any (or, equivalently, for some)
p\in(0,\,1)
.

Further, each of these statements implies that

\displaystyle\overline{B_{1}*B_{2}}(x)\sim\overline{B}_{1}(x)+\overline{B}_{2}(x)\,.

(4.7)

Proof.

At first, since
B_{1},\,B_{2}\in\mathcal{T}^{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{A}^{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}
, from
[
23
, Th. 1.1]
,
each one of the statements
(i)-(iii)
implies that (
4.7
) holds. Hence, for any
v>1
it holds,

\displaystyle\overline{(B_{1}*B_{2})^{*}}(v)

\displaystyle=\limsup\dfrac{\overline{B_{1}*B_{2}}(v\,x)}{\overline{B_{1}*B_{2}}(x)}\leq\limsup\dfrac{\overline{B}_{1}(v\,x)+\overline{B}_{2}(v\,x)}{\overline{B}_{1}(x)+\overline{B}_{2}(x)}\leq\limsup\left[\dfrac{\overline{B_{1}}(v\,x)}{\overline{B_{1}}(x)}\bigvee\dfrac{\overline{B_{2}}(v\,x)}{\overline{B_{2}}(x)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\limsup\dfrac{\overline{B_{1}}(v\,x)}{\overline{B_{1}}(x)}\,,\;\limsup\dfrac{\overline{B_{2}}(v\,x)}{\overline{B_{2}}(x)}\right\}\leq\max\{\overline{B_{1}^{*}}(v),\,\overline{B_{2}^{*}}(v)\}\,,

so we obtain

\displaystyle K_{B_{1}*B_{2}}^{-}=-\lim_{v\downarrow 1}\dfrac{\log\overline{(B_{1}*B_{2})^{*}}(v)}{\log v}\geq-\lim_{v\downarrow 1}\dfrac{\log\left(\max\left\{\overline{B_{1}^{*}}(v),\,\overline{B_{2}^{*}}(v)\right\}\right)}{\log v}>0\,,

(4.8)

where at the last step, we used that
B_{1},\,B_{2}\in\mathcal{T}^{*}
and thus
K_{B_{1}}^{-}\wedge K_{B_{2}}^{-}>0
.

Next, we shall show the equivalence of assertions
(i)-(iii)
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
. We assume that
B_{1}\,B_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
. Then from
[
23
, Th. 1.1]
follows
B_{1}*B_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
,
and further from (
4.8
) we obtain that
B_{1}*B_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. We assume that
B_{1}*B_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
. Then by
[
23
, Th. 1.1]
we find
B_{1}\,B_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
.
It remains to show that
K_{B_{1}\,B_{2}}^{-}>0
. At first we see that

\displaystyle\overline{B_{1}\,B_{2}}(x)=\mathbf{P}(\Theta_{1}\vee\Theta_{2}>x)=\overline{B}_{1}(x)+\overline{B}_{2}(x)-\overline{B}_{1}(x)\,\overline{B}_{2}(x)\sim\overline{B}_{1}(x)+\overline{B}_{2}(x)\,,

hence, it holds

\displaystyle\overline{(B_{1}\,B_{2})^{*}}(v)

\displaystyle=\limsup\dfrac{\overline{B_{1}\,B_{2}}(v\,x)}{\overline{B_{1}\,B_{2}}(x)}\leq\limsup\dfrac{\overline{B}_{1}(v\,x)+\overline{B}_{2}(v\,x)}{\overline{B}_{1}(x)+\overline{B}_{2}(x)}\leq\limsup\left[\dfrac{\overline{B_{1}}(v\,x)}{\overline{B_{1}}(x)}\bigvee\dfrac{\overline{B_{2}}(v\,x)}{\overline{B_{2}}(x)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\limsup\dfrac{\overline{B_{1}}(v\,x)}{\overline{B_{1}}(x)}\,,\;\limsup\dfrac{\overline{B_{2}}(v\,x)}{\overline{B_{2}}(x)}\right\}\leq\max\{\overline{B_{1}^{*}}(v),\,\overline{B_{2}^{*}}(v)\}\,,

for any
v>1
, and therefore we obtain

\displaystyle K_{B_{1}\,B_{2}}^{-}=-\lim_{v\downarrow 1}\dfrac{\log\overline{(B_{1}\,B_{2})^{*}}(v)}{\log v}\geq-\lim_{v\downarrow 1}\dfrac{\log\left[\max\left\{\overline{B_{1}^{*}}(v)\,,\;\overline{B_{2}^{*}}(v)\right\}\right]}{\log v}>0\,,

where at the last step we used the fact that
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{T}^{*}
. Hence, we conclude that
B_{1}\,B_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
.

(i)\Rightarrow(iii)
. Let
B_{1}*B_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
. Then, by
[
23
, Th. 1.1]
holds
p\,B_{1}+(1-p)\,B_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
,
for any (or, equivalently, for some)
p\in(0,\,1)
.

Further, it holds

\displaystyle\overline{\left(p\,B_{1}+(1-p)\,B_{2}\right)^{*}}(v)

\displaystyle=\limsup\dfrac{p\,\overline{B}_{1}(v\,x)+(1-p)\,\overline{B}_{2}(v\,x)}{p\,\overline{B}_{1}(x)+(1-p)\,\overline{B}_{2}(x)}\leq\limsup\left[\dfrac{\overline{B_{1}}(v\,x)}{\overline{B_{1}}(x)}\bigvee\dfrac{\overline{B_{2}}(v\,x)}{\overline{B_{2}}(x)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\limsup\dfrac{\overline{B}_{1}(v\,x)}{\overline{B}_{1}(x)}\,,\;\limsup\dfrac{\overline{B}_{2}(v\,x)}{\overline{B}_{2}(x)}\right\}\leq\max\left\{\overline{B_{1}^{*}}(v)\,,\;\overline{B_{2}^{*}}(v)\right\}\,,

for any (or, equivalently, for some)
p\in(0,\,1)
. Thus, it holds

\displaystyle K_{p\,B_{1}+(1-p)\,B_{2}}^{-}

\displaystyle=-\lim_{v\downarrow 1}\dfrac{\log\overline{\left(p\,B_{1}+(1-p)\,B_{2}\right)^{*}}(v)}{\log v}\geq-\lim_{v\downarrow 1}\dfrac{\log\left[\max\left\{\overline{B_{1}^{*}}(v)\,,\;\overline{B_{2}^{*}}(v)\right\}\right]}{\log v}>0\,,

for any (or, equivalently, for some)
p\in(0,\,1)
, which implies that
p\,B_{1}+(1-p)\,B_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
for any (or, equivalently, for some)
p\in(0,\,1)
.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Let for some
p\in(0,\,1)
, it holds
p\,B_{1}+(1-p)\,B_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
. Then for any
p\in(0,\,1)
, holds
p\,B_{1}+(1-p)\,B_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
, and further by
[
23
, Th. 1.1]
we obtain
B_{1}*B_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
. Since relation (
4.7
) is true, via (
4.8
) we conclude that
B_{1}*B_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
Z,\,W
two independent non-negative random variables with
Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}B\in\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}
and
\mathbf{P}(W=0)<1
,

\mathbf{P}(W>c\,x)=o\left[\overline{Q}(x)\right]
, for any
c>0
, where
W\,Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}Q
. Then
Q\in\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}
.

Proof.

At first, from
[
42
, Th. 1.3]
we obtain that
Q\in\mathcal{S}
. It remains to show
K_{Q}^{-}>0
.

For any fixed
v>1
and
\delta^{\prime}>0
, we can find some
x_{0}=x_{0}(\delta^{\prime})>0
, large enough, such that it holds

\displaystyle\overline{B}(v\,x)\leq(1+\delta^{\prime})\,\overline{B^{*}}(v)\,\overline{B}(x)\,,

for any
x\geq x_{0}
. Furthermore, for the concrete
x_{0}>0
, and by assumption on the heaviness of tails of
W
and
WZ
, we can find some
x_{1}>x_{0}
, such that
\mathbf{P}\left(W>\dfrac{x}{x_{0}}\right)\leq\delta^{\prime}\,\overline{Q}(x)
, for any
x_{1}\geq x_{0}
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{Q}(v\,x)\leq\int_{0}^{x/x_{0}}\overline{B}\left(\dfrac{v\,x}{y}\right)\,\mathbf{P}(W\in dy)+\mathbf{P}\left(W>\dfrac{x}{x_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\delta^{\prime})\,\overline{B^{*}}(v)\,\int_{0}^{x/x_{0}}\overline{B}\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right)\,\mathbf{P}(W\in dy)+\delta^{\prime}\,\overline{Q}(x)\leq\left[(1+\delta^{\prime})\,\overline{B^{*}}(v)+\delta^{\prime}\right]\,\overline{Q}(x)\,,

for any
x>x_{1}
. From the last relation, via the arbitrary choice of
\delta^{\prime}>0
, we find that

\displaystyle\overline{Q^{*}}(v)\leq\overline{B^{*}}(v)\,,

(4.12)

hence we have that

\displaystyle K_{Q}^{-}=-\lim_{v\downarrow 1}\dfrac{\log\overline{Q^{*}}(v)}{\log v}\geq-\lim_{v\downarrow 1}\dfrac{\log\overline{B^{*}}(v)}{\log v}=K_{B}^{-}>0\,,

that gives
Q\in\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}
.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

In fact, as we see from the first assertion of Theorem
4.1
(ii), the assumption
G\,\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, can be relaxed into

\displaystyle G_{\bf S}(x\,A)=o\left[F(x\,A)\right]\,,

(4.26)

that includes several cases of delayed claims, as for example the case when the distribution
G
has light tail. However, we keep the condition

G\,\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
on the Theorem, for the sake of simplicity of the assumption, but (
4.26
) contains many other cases.

The following lemma provides a sufficient condition for the validity of relation (
4.26
), and is inspired from
[
27
, Lem. 3.3]
.
Namely, this lemma permit the complete removal of the conditions
G\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, in the case of
F\in\mathcal{C}_{A}
, with
K_{F_{A}}^{-}>0
, where
we say
F\in\mathcal{C}_{A}
, if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{C}
, in the sense of

\displaystyle\lim_{b\uparrow 1}\limsup\dfrac{\overline{F}_{A}(b\,x)}{\overline{F}_{A}(x)}=1\,.

We recall that we say
F\in(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}})_{A}
, if
F_{A}\in(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}})
, and
\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}\subsetneq\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}}
.
In the following Lemma we need also the upper Matuszewska index of a distribution
B
, which defined as:

\displaystyle J_{B}^{+}=-\lim_{v\to\infty}\dfrac{\log\overline{B_{*}}(v)}{\log v}\,,

where

\displaystyle\overline{B_{*}}(v)=\liminf\dfrac{\overline{B}(v\,x)}{\overline{B}(x)}\,,

Recall also that if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{C}
then
J_{F_{A}}^{+}<\infty
.
Hence, the following lemma is helpful in the proof of Corollary
4.1
(ii).

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set and
\left\{{\bf Z}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
be a sequence of i.i.d. non-negative random vectors
with distribution
V
. We also assume that
F
is a distribution with support on the non-negative orthant, such that
F\in(\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{P_{D}})_{A}
,
and
V(x\,A)=o[F(x\,A)]
. Further, we suppose that
\left\{\Theta_{i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
represents a sequence of non-negative random
weights, independent of
\left\{{\bf Z}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
, and such that for some

it holds

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\Theta_{i}^{p_{1}}\right]\bigvee\mathbf{E}\left[\Theta_{i}^{p_{2}}\right]\right)^{1/p}<\infty\,,

(4.27)

where
p={\bf 1}_{\{0<J_{F_{A}}^{+}<1\}}+p_{2}\,{\bf 1}_{\{1\leq J_{F_{A}}^{+}<\infty\}}
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Theta_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)=o[F(x\,A)]\,.

(4.28)

Proof.

Firstly, for any
x>0
it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Theta_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(\sup_{{\bf p}\in I_{A}}{\bf p}^{\top}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Theta_{i}\,{\bf Z}^{(i)}\right]>xA\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Theta_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right)\,,

Further, following the line of the proof of
[
27
, Lem. 3.3]
, but now using
[
46
, Th. 2]
(due to (
4.27
)),
instead of Lemmma 3.2 in that paper, we can find that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Theta_{i}\,Z_{A}^{(i)}>x\right)=o[\overline{F}_{A}(x)]\,.

Hence, by these two last relations we conclude (
4.28
).
