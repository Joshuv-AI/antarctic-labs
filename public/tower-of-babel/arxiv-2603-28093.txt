Random Stability of Random Variables
Andrey Sarantsev

Abstract
. For a random variable N = 0 , 1 , 2 , … N=0,1,2,\ldots we study the following question: When does the sum of N N many independent and identically distributed copies of a random variable X X have the same law a a nontrivial rescaling of X X ? We show that such N N -stable random variable exists if and only 1 < 𝔼 ⁡ [ N ] < ∞ 1<\mathbb{E}[N]<\infty . Under an additional assumption 𝔼 ⁡ [ N ​ ln ⁡ N ] < ∞ \mathbb{E}[N\ln N]<\infty , we describe all N N -stable X X . We also study a converse problem: For a given X ≥ 0 X\geq 0 with 𝔼 ⁡ [ X ] = 1 \mathbb{E}[X]=1 , we study the set of all N N such that X X is N N -stable. Distributions of N N form a semigroup with respect to composition of probability generating functions. We show these probability generating functions need to commute with respect to composition. We present explicit families of composition semigroups. Equivalent formulations have appeared in difference forms, and this article aims to unify and extend them.

2.2.
Strictly stable distributions on the real line

Recall a well-known classification of stictly stable distributions

, see

Assume

2.6.
Geometric stability

The most classic example is for the geometric
N_{p}
for
p\in(0,1)
, with distribution

(11)

\mathbb{P}(N_{p}=n)=p(1-p)^{n-1},\quad n=1,2,\ldots;

and with expected value and the probability generating function

(12)

\mathbb{E}[s^{N}]=\frac{ps}{1-(1-p)s},\quad\mathbb{E}[N_{p}]=\frac{1}{p}.

Then the standard
N_{p}
-stable distribution of every
p\in(0,1)
is the standard exponential with mean
1
: It has density and the Laplace transform

(13)

p(x)=e^{-x},\quad x\geq 0;\quad L(u)=\mathbb{E}[e^{-uY}]=\frac{1}{1+u},\quad u\geq 0;\quad Y\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1).

A Gaussian
N_{p}
-stable distribution is also well-known: The
Laplace distribution
with density and the characteristic function

p(x)=0.5\beta e^{-\beta|x|},\quad x\in\mathbb{R};\quad f(u)=\frac{1}{1+\beta^{2}u^{2}}.

More generally, a symmetric
N_{p}
-stable distribution is known: This is the
Linnik distribution
with characteristic function

f(u)=\frac{1}{1+\beta|u|^{\alpha}}.

with
0<\alpha\leq 2
. See also
[
23
, Chapter 9]
, Subsection 9.4.2.1, Theorem 9.4.6. This distribution is absolutely continuous on the real line, but its density is explicitly known only for
\alpha=2
, when it becomes the Laplace distribution. The positive
N_{p}
-stable distribution has Laplace transform with
\beta>0
and

\alpha\in(0,1]
:

L(u)=\frac{1}{1+\beta u^{\alpha}}.

This is commonly known as the
Mittag-Leffler distribution
; see
[
23
, Chapter 9]
, Subsection 9.4.2.2, Theorem 9.4.9. Also, in
[
13
, Chapter 2]
, these were named
Kovalenko distributions
, see (3.2) or page 30. This distribution is also absolutely continuous, but its density is explicitly known only for

\alpha=1
or
\alpha=0.5
. In the former case, this distribution is exponential, as in (

). In the latter case, for
\beta=1
, it has density

f(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi x}}-\frac{2e^{x}}{\sqrt{x}}\int_{\sqrt{x}}^{\infty}e^{-z^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z.

These were the earliest and most studied examples. An entire chapter
[
23
, Chapter 9]
is devoted to these geometric stable distributions. See also a great survey
[

]
with large bibliography. Such geometric stable distributions were studied as far back as in 1984 in
[

]

A distribution

Lemma 5
.

Take infinitely divisible random variables

on the real line. Pick a

2.8.
Commuting distributions

The uniqueness result in Lemma
1
for the representation from Theorem
2
allows us to prove the following key statement. First, we consider the
composition operation

(16)

\varphi\circ\psi(s)\equiv\varphi(\psi(s))

for the two probability generating functions
\varphi
and
\psi
, or, equivalently, the corresponding distributions. This operation is clearly associative, so the set of all probabiltiy distributions on
\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
is a semigroup under this operation. In terms of the random variables
M
and
N
with probability generating functions
\varphi
and
\psi
, their composition
\varphi\circ\psi
is also a probability generating function of the random variable
N_{1}+\ldots+N_{M}
, where
N_{1},N_{2},\ldots
are independent (of each other and
M
) copies of
N
. The unit element for this composition operation is
\varphi(s)=s
, corresponding to the variable
M=1
.

Definition 6
.

We say that the probability generating functions
\varphi
and
\psi
, or their corresponding distributions, or random variables
M
and
N
,
commute
if

\varphi\circ\psi\equiv\psi\circ\varphi.

The key result is below. It was shown in other literature in particular cases, but not in this general form. For such results for or Gaussian
N
-stable random variables, see
[
13
, Chapter 4]
, Theorem 4.6.1 and Corollary 4.6.1;
[
25
, Theorem 1]
,
[
23
, Chapter 8]
, Theorem 8.3.4 and references therein;
[
42
, Theorem 2.1]
. The property of commutative semigroups was also shown in
[
29
, Remark 2.4]
. This survey
[
29
]
is very comprehensive for 1990s. However, they claim that geometric family is unique among such explicit commuting families. We discuss in Section 2 that this is not true.

Theorem 3
.

Assume
\mathbb{E}[M]<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}[N\ln N]<\infty
. If
X
is
N
-stable, then
M
and
N
commute if and only if
X
is
M
-stable. In this case,
\mathbb{E}[M\ln M]<\infty
. The standard
N
-stable and the standard
M
-stable distributions are the same. The index
\alpha
and the representation of
X
from Theorem
2
for both
M
and
N
are the same.

Remark 4
.

As a corollary of Theorem
3
, we get that
M
and
N
commute if and only if their standard random stable distributions coincide.

Lemma 6
.

Assume
\mathbb{E}[N\ln N]<\infty
. If
X
and
Y
are
N
-stable, and
X
is
M
-stable, then
Y
is also
M
-stable. In this case,
\mathbb{E}[M\ln M]<\infty
, and
\mathbb{E}[N]>1
, and
\mathbb{E}[M]>1
.

3.
Composition Semigroups

3.1.
Introduction to the problem

Fix a probability measure
Q

P
on the nonnegative integers such that
Q
is
P
-stable. Let
N\sim P
and assume
\mathbb{E}[N\ln N]<\infty
. Then
from Theorem

and Lemma

-stable for any such

. This allows to use the Laplace transform

of

and
\mathbb{E}[X]=1

However, we stress that not every

3.2.
Analytic description

Let us describe such

analytically. Let

. The Laplace transform

is a one-to-one strictly decreasing function

, and we can find its inverse

(denoted using the arrow to distinguish it from

(17)

L(L^{\leftarrow}(s))\equiv s,\quad s\in(\pi,1].

Recall that the functional equation (

) holds for the Laplace transform

of

as well as for its characteristic function

:

(18)

\varphi(L(u))=L(cu),\quad u\geq 0.

Using (

L^{\leftarrow}(\varphi(s))=cL^{\leftarrow}(s),\quad s\in(\pi,1].

In complex analysis, Schroder’s equation is well known, see
[

for a bibliography and a survey of results. We can also rewrite (

) using (

(19)

\varphi(s)=L(cL^{\leftarrow}(s)),\quad s\in(\pi,1].

Equation (

[

on random stability. It will play an important role in this article as well.

This defines the probability generating function on the interval
(\pi,1]\subseteq[0,1]
. Maybe this is not the entire
[0,1]
, but even if this interval is smaller, it is large enough to uniquely determine the function
\varphi
. Indeed, this function

. Therefore, the values of

on this interval uniquely determine it: This is a classic result in complex analysis.

3.3.
Semigroup definitions and properties

Take any probability measure

•

The set

•

The set

For a given

. Or

\mathcal{M}:c\mapsto L(cL^{\leftarrow}(\cdot))

Lemma 8
.

The set

is closed under composition and weak convergence. The set

is closed under multiplication and under the usual convergence of real numbers. The mapping

from (

Below we state an important result about finite moments for distributions in

Lemma 9
.

Pick a distribution

\mathbb{E}[N\ln N]<\infty

•

•

\mathbb{E}[N^{k}]<\infty
for a fixed

As a corollary, if one distribution

satisfies

3.4.
Parameterization

Here, we use
c=\mathbb{E}[N]\geq 1
(for the standard
X
, we have
\alpha=1
), and the semigroup operation on

is multiplication. This exactly matches the notation in
[

]
with

instead of

, and

. The corresponding semigroup operation here is addition. However, in other literature, a different parameterization is used:
p\in(0,1)
. Historically,

-stable

arose as weak limits of scaled random sums of random variables. Much like classic stable distributions are weak lmits of scaled and shifted sums of a deterministic nut growing number of independent identically distributed random variables. Therefore, the notation was used is
\nu_{p}
instead of

(21)

p\nu_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}Z\geq 0,\quad p\in\Xi\subseteq(0,1),\quad p\downarrow 0;

where

is, in fact, the standard
\nu_{p}
-stable random variable for any

; and
\Xi
is a subset of
(0,1)
with limit point
0
. They found it more convenient to impose this limit assumption, and not derive it from scratch. Often, another additional condition was imposed:
\mathbb{E}[\nu_{p}]=1/p
. Normalizing by convergence and expectation at the same time. But this might not be conistent, and we avoid this notation here. The reason is subtle: Match the discussion in Section 1, Remark
1
, with the
p
-notation, and recall the two cases, with
\mathbb{E}[N\ln N]<\infty
or
\mathbb{E}[N\ln N]=\infty
. In both cases, we do have
\mathbb{E}[N_{k}]=c^{k}
.

•

For the case
\mathbb{E}[N\ln N]<\infty
, or equivalently,
\mathbb{E}[Z]=1
, we can let

and
\nu_{p}=N_{k}
. Then

\mathbb{E}[\nu_{p}]=\frac{1}{p},\quad p\nu_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}Z,\quad p\downarrow 0.

•

If
\mathbb{E}[N\ln N]=\infty
, or equivalently,
\mathbb{E}[Z]=\infty
, then either let
p=c^{-k}
; then
\mathbb{E}[\nu_{p}]=1/p
but not
p\nu_{p}\to Z
; or let

, then
p\nu_{p}\to Z
but
\mathbb{E}[\nu_{p}]\neq 1/p
. We prefer parameterization

.

In the second case, we cannot have both expectation and convergence assumptions for
\nu_{p}
. The notation of
\nu_{p}
is used in the following literature (the list is not exhaustive):
[
42
, Section 2]
, (1) and (3), both expectation and convergence;
[
30
, Section 1]
, only convergence;
[
24
, page 304]
, they mentioned only expectation but clearly meant convergence as well, as seen from Theorem 2;
[
25
, Section 2]
, only expectation, but later convergence is introduced;
[
30
, Section 2]
(3) and (4), only convergence;
[
13
, Chapter 4]
, (6.32), only convergence. Sometimes, commutativity is required or derived in special cases.

The real reason behind the prevalence of this

-notation is that, historically, random stability study to a large extend (although not exclusively) was focused on geometric stability (that is, when
N=N_{p}
is geometric from (

)). In this case,

converges to the standard exponential distribution as

, and
\mathbb{E}[N_{p}]=1/p
. Approximations of sums of

many independent identically distributed random variables as

(similar in spirit to the Central Limit Theorem) were extensively studied. A good exposition is in
[

, Chapter 9]
.

Lemma 10
.

Pick a distribution
Q
on the half-line. Assume

is nontrivial. Then the random variable

with probability generating function (

) with

being the Laplace transform of

satisfies

\frac{N_{c}}{c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}Y,\quad c\to\infty,\quad c\in S(Q),

3.5.
Explicit-form semigroups

The geometric distribution has the
semigroup property:
The probability generating function
G_{p}
of
N_{p}
satisfies

(23)

G_{p}\circ G_{q}=G_{q}\circ G_{p}=G_{pq}.

Until recently, this geometric family (and its extensions, see below) were the only explicit families of probability generating functions
(G_{p},\,p\in(0,1])
, having finite mean
G^{\prime}_{p}(1)<\infty
, and this property (
23
). Extensions include the modifications of geometric distributions with atoms at zero. Their probability generating functions are general-form fractional-linear. Another extension includes the infinitely divisible case above. Then
X
is Gamma with shape
1/k
instead of an exponential.

Another example is the
Sibuya random variable

N_{p}
with probability generating function
G_{p}(s)=1-(1-s)^{p}
, introduced in
[
50
]
with
\beta=\gamma=1
in (2); see also a more recent article
[
31
]
with an extension with mass at zero. Such family of distributions satisfy (

) as well. But they have infinite mean. As follows from Theorem
1
, do not have any

-stable distributions, for any

.

Until recently, these were the only known exact-form commuting semigroups. However, recently there were breakthroughs on this topic, with several more explicit commuting families of probability generating functions, and with finite mean greater than
1
, including a generalization of the Sibuya distribution. This corresponds to continuous-time branching processes
N=(N(t),\,t\geq 0)
(See Section
6
for more details), with

(24)

G_{p}(s)=\mathbb{E}\left[s^{N(t)}\right],\quad p=e^{-t}.

From (

), the composition of probability generating functions of

and

is the probability generating function of
N(t+t^{\prime})
. Comparing (
21
), (

), (

) we have the following reparametrization rule:

(25)

p=\frac{1}{c}=e^{-t},\quad c=e^{t}=\frac{1}{p}.

4.
Equivalent Definitions of Random Stability

There is another definition of random stable variables in the literature, one that is based on weak limits of scaled random sums. Just as stable and strictly stable distributions (in the classic sense) are often defined as weka limits of scaled sums of
N
independent identically distributed random variables as
N\to\infty
, so can be done for random stable distributions. See
[
30
, Section 2]
, and
[
38
, Theorems 1, 2, 3]
.

Instead of
N\to\infty
, we need a semigroup of probability distributions on nonnegative integers; or, equivalently, a semigroup of probability generating functions. We might as well assume this semigroup is commutative. Although we think one does not really need this assumption, the proof is easy if we impose it.

Luckily, even when we start from one
N
such that
X
is
N
-stable, such semigroup comes naturally: This is the discrete semigroup generated by
N
; or, equivalently, the set of probability generating functions of
N_{k}
for each
k
, where
(N_{k})
is the discrete-time branching process with
N
offsprings.

The result below is simple but needs to be included separately. We could not find it explicitly written in the literature.

Lemma 11
.

Take a random variable
Y\geq 0
with
\mathbb{E}[Y]=1
. Assume its semigroup
G(Y)
is not trivial. Let
N_{c}
be the random variable with probability generating function as in (
19
), with
c\in S(Y)
. Then a random variable

X
is
N
-stable for at least one, and therefore for all,
N
with distribution in
G(Y)
, if and only if there exists a sequence of independent identically distributed random variables
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots
independent of all these
N
, and a function
a:S(Y)\to[0,\infty)
such that

(26)

\frac{U_{1}+\ldots+U_{N_{c}}}{a(c)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}X,\quad c\to\infty,\quad c\in S(Y).

If this is true, then for any such sequence
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots
the statement (
26
) is equivalent to (
27
):

(27)

\frac{U_{1}+\ldots+U_{[c]}}{a(c)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}Z,\quad c\to\infty,\quad c\in S(Y).

In this case, we have (
7
), with
Z
strictly
\alpha
-stable, and
Y
and
Z
independent.

We see that
U_{j}
belong to the strict domain of attraction: Recall our discussion above about defining strictly stable distributions as weak limits of scaled sums of independent identically distributed random variables. We stress this is different from classic

\alpha
-stable domains of attraction, where adding constants is allowed: Recall (
6
) and the discussion there. Strict domain of stability include scaling sums of independent indentically distributed random variables, but not adding constants. See also discussion in
[
30
, Section 2]
.

5.

Now we start the discussion of the structure of the semigroup

for a given nonnegative random variable

6.1.
Construction

Pick a probability measure
Q

[0,\infty)
with Laplace transform

This branching process is described as follows, as outlined in

with small

This continuous-time branching process contains

Discrete-time branching processes,

N_{k}=N(k\triangle t),\,k=0,1,2,\ldots

Their offspring distribution has probability generating function

for

. The converse question is

Whether a discrete-time branching process
(N_{k})
is really a skeleton of some continuous-time branching process
(N(t),\,t\geq 0)
, as in (

[

]
and
[

, Section 12.

A necessary and sufficient condition for existence and non-explosion (lack of

A particular case is

For such

, for any

. Using the alternative notation from (

for the discrete time (in their notation,

7.1.
Proof of Theorem

This is the longest proof, and we present a short overview. We split it in 8 steps. Step 1 is introductory and involves branching processes and random sums of copies of
X
. Step 2 is

. Steps 3–7 are for the case

and then use tightness arguments to arrive at a contradiction. Finally, Step 8 mentions the classic case

.

Step 1.
As before, let
\varphi
be the probability generating function of
N
. Consider a discrete-time branching process
(N_{k})
starting from

and each particle having the number of offsprings distributed as

. The probability generating function of

is the

th composition of

\varphi_{k}(s)=\mathbb{E}\left[s^{N_{k}}\right]=\varphi(\varphi(\ldots\varphi(s)\ldots)).

For background, we refer to the classic monographs
[
17
, Chapter 1]
and
[
1
, Chapter I]
. Applying the main equality
k
times, we get:

(34)

X_{1}+\ldots+X_{N_{k}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}c^{k}Z_{1}.

This identity is helpful for the proofs of lack of
N
-stable
X
for
\mathbb{E}[N]\geq 1
and
\mathbb{E}[N]=\infty
. The first case is simple, the second is much harder. We always assume
(N_{k})
is independent of
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots

Step 2.
Assume
\mathbb{E}[N]\leq 1
. From the classic theory of branching processes
[
17
, Chapter 1]
, Theorem 6.1 or
[
1
, Chapter I]
, Section 5, Theorem 1, the process
(N_{k})
becomes extinct with probability

. THat is.
N_{k}=0
for some
k
almost surely. This immediately implies that the left-hand side of (
34
) is equal to zero. Therefore, the right-hand side of (
34
) is also zero, which implies
X_{1}=0
almost surely. This contradiction completes the proof of lack of nontrivial
N
-stable
X
if
\mathbb{E}[N]\leq 1
.

Step 3.
Now assume
\mathbb{E}[N]=\infty
. Define
Y_{n}:=X_{n}-X^{\prime}_{n}
, where
X^{\prime}_{1},X^{\prime}_{2},\ldots
is yet another sequence of copies of
X
, independent of each other, of
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
and of the branching process
(N_{k})
. Then

(35)

Y_{1}+\ldots+Y_{N_{k}}=\left(X_{1}+\ldots+X_{N_{k}}\right)-\left(X^{\prime}_{1}+\ldots+X^{\prime}_{N_{k}}\right).

Dividing (
35
) by

, we get:

(36)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{k}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{c^{k}}\left(Y_{1}+\ldots+Y_{N_{k}}\right)=S_{k}-S^{\prime}_{k},

(37)

\displaystyle S_{k}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{c^{k}}\left(X_{1}+\ldots+X_{N_{k}}\right),\quad S^{\prime}_{k}=\frac{1}{c^{k}}\left(X^{\prime}_{1}+\ldots+X^{\prime}_{N_{k}}\right).

Note that
S_{k}
and
S^{\prime}_{k}
are dependent via
N_{k}
. Thus we
cannot
claim that
\Sigma_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}X-X^{\prime}
for an independent copy
X^{\prime}
of
X
. However, we can claim the sequence
(\Sigma_{k})
is tight (in other words, relatively compact, bounded in probability): For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a

large enough so that
\mathbb{P}(|\Sigma_{k}|\geq K)\leq\varepsilon
for all

. This follows from the tightness of sequences
(S_{k})
and
(S^{\prime}_{k})
, which all have the same distribution, the same as

.

Step 4.
Define the characteristic function of
\Sigma_{k}
:

(38)

g_{k}(u)=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\mathrm{i}u\Sigma_{k}}\right]

It is real-valued, since

. It turns out that this simplifies the proof in a critical way. See
[
36
, Chapter 3]
, Theorem 3.1.2. Applying
[

, Chapter 3]
, Lemma 3.1.3, we derive from tightness of the sequence

that

\sup\limits_{k\geq 1}|1-g_{k}(u)|\to 0,\quad u\to 0.

Since

is real-valued, and by

, Chapter 2]
, page 36,

, we can simply write this statement (

) without the absolute value. This characteristic function

from (

g_{k}(u)=\varphi_{k}(g(u/c^{k})),\quad g(u):=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\mathrm{i}uY_{n}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\mathrm{i}u(X_{n}-X^{\prime}_{n})}\right]=|f(u)|^{2}

is the characteristic function of

, see
[
36
, Chapter 3]
, Corollary 2 of Theorem 3.3.1.

Step 5.
It follows from scaling theory for discrete-time branching processes, see for example
[

, Theorem 4.4]
with infinite mean that for a fixed

has positive probability. For any

, we can rewrite

and note

almost surely. Applying the Fatou lemma, we get:

\varphi_{k}(s/a^{k})=\mathbb{E}\left[s^{N_{k}/a^{k}}\right]

\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\varphi_{k}(s/a^{k})\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\varlimsup\limits_{k\to\infty}s^{N_{k}/a^{k}}\right]\leq 0\cdot\mathbb{P}(A)+1\cdot\mathbb{P}(A^{c})<1.

Step 6.
Comparing (

(42)

g(u/c^{k})\geq s/a^{k}.

Take the logarithms in (

for

(43)

g(u/c^{k})-1\geq a^{k}\ln s.

Dividing (

and letting

(44)

\varliminf\limits_{k\to\infty}\left[a^{-k}(g(uc^{-k})-1)\right]\geq\ln s.

In the right-hand side of (

is arbitrary. Therefore, we can take

as close to

as we wish, to make

negative but as close to zero as we wish. Also, recall that by

36
, Chapter 2]
, page 36, we have:
g(uc^{-k})\leq 1
. Applying all this to (

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}a^{-k}(g(uc^{-k})-1)=0.

Step 7.
Rewrite as second-order discrete difference with step

2(g(uc^{-k})-1)=g(-uc^{-k})+g(uc^{-k})-2g(0)=\triangle^{t_{k}}_{2}g(0)

where we define

. Clearly,

, and letting

(since

is arbitrary), we rewrite (

\triangle_{2}^{t_{k}}g(0)/t_{k}^{2}\to 0
. Apply
[
36
, Chapter 2]
, Theorem 2.3.1 and conclude:

, thus

, and

almost surely, thus

. Comparing this with (

. Apply
[

, Chapter 2]
, Theorem 2.1.4, Corollary 1. We get:

and

. But

is

-stable. Plugging

The classic case

7.16.
Analytical Laplace transform

Take a random variable
X\geq 0
. Consider its Laplace transform
L(u)=\mathbb{E}[e^{-uX}]
.

Lemma 13
.

Assume
\mathbb{E}[X]<\infty
. We can define
L
as a complex-valued function on the half-plane
\mathbb{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\mathrm{Re}(z)>0\}
. This function is analytic on
\mathbb{H}
, and we have:

(54)

\frac{1-L(z)}{z}\to\mathbb{E}[X],\quad z\to 0,\quad\mathrm{Re}(z)>0.

Proof.

For
z=u+\mathrm{i}v\in\mathbb{H}
, we write
|e^{-zX}|=e^{-uX}
. Therefore,
\mathbb{E}|e^{-zX}|=\mathbb{E}[e^{-uX}]<\infty
, and
\mathbb{E}[e^{-zX}]
is well defined. It is complex analytic on
\mathbb{H}
. To this end, we need to prove in the complex analytic sense:
L^{\prime}(z)=\mathbb{E}[-Xe^{-zX}]
. But this, in turn, can be proved as follows. Take two points
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}
and draw a segment between them. It is parametrized as
w_{t}=zt+z^{\prime}(1-t)
. We need to show

(55)

L(w_{t})-L(z)=\int_{[z,w_{t}]}\mathbb{E}\left[-Xe^{-uX}\right]\,\mathrm{d}u.

This integral over the segment is understood in the complex analytic sense. This can be written using real-valued integration as

\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[-Xe^{-(zs+(1-s)z^{\prime})X}\right](z^{\prime}-z)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Remove expectations for a moment. From complex analysis, we get:

e^{-(zt+z^{\prime}(1-t))X}-e^{-ztX}=\int_{0}^{t}\left(-Xe^{-w_{s}X}\right)(z^{\prime}-z)\,\mathrm{d}s.

We need only to show that we need an interchange of integration and expectation. This requires us to use the Fubini theorem. Usually, this theorem is stated for real-vaued functions, but it works equally well for complex-valued functions. We need:

(56)

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\left|-Xe^{-w_{s}X}\right|(z^{\prime}-z)\,\mathrm{d}s<\infty.

Assuming
\mathrm{Re}(z)\leq\mathrm{Re}(z^{\prime})
without loss of generality,

\mathrm{Re}(z)\leq\mathrm{Re}(w_{s})
for all
s\in[0,1]
. Hence

\mathbb{E}|-Xe^{-w_{s}X}|=\mathbb{E}|Xe^{-\mathrm{Re}(z)X}|<\infty.

This proves (
56
), and with it proves (
55
). Thus
L
is analytic on
\mathbb{H}
. The property (
54
) can be shown similarly, with
z=0
, since
\mathbb{E}[Xe^{-zX}]=\mathbb{E}[X]
for
z=0
.
∎

8.
Conclusions and Open Problems

This article is an attempt to review and compare various streams of existing literature on branching processes and random stability. We added a few results of our own, which we could not find in the literature; therefore this article cannot really be considered merely a review, but a hybrid between the original research and literature survey.

A natural direction for further research is: For each probability measure
Q
on
[0,\infty)
with mean
1
, find the semigroup
G(Q)
, and describe the distributions in this semigroup.

Another research direction is subtle: Extend our results to the case
\mathbb{E}[N]<\infty
but
\mathbb{E}[N\ln N]=\infty
. In this article, we stressed the importance of the condition
\mathbb{E}[N\ln N]<\infty
, which is not emphasized in some literature.

Yet another possible extension is
smoothing transformation:
For other random variables
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots
independent of each other and of
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots

A_{1}X_{1}+\ldots+A_{N}X_{N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}X.

It reduces to random stability if
A_{j}=1/c
, and has other applications. We referred to this at the very start of this article:
[
35
]
. See also an earlier article
[
10
]
.
