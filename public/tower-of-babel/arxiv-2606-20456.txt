Lacunary Hyperbolic Groups with Fast Injectivity Radius Growth and Enough Loxodromic Elements are Selfless
Goulnara Arzhantseva, Martin Finn-Sell

Abstract
. We prove that a lacunary hyperbolic group G = lim → ⁡ G i G=\varinjlim G_{i} with sufficient generics is selfless in the sense of Amrutam–Gao–Kunnawalkam Elayavalli–Patchell [ 4 ] , provided the hyperbolicity constants δ i \delta_{i} and injectivity radii r i r_{i} satisfy δ i ​ ( log ⁡ r i ) 7 = o ⁡ ( r i ) \delta_{i}(\log r_{i})^{7}=o(r_{i}) . The proof replaces the acylindricity-based machinery of [ 4 ] with a direct geodesic n n -gon criterion due to Arzhantseva [ 5 ] , which applies in any δ \delta -hyperbolic space. As a consequence, combined with rapid decay, G G is C ∗ C^{*} -selfless. The condition is mild: torsion-free Tarski monsters, Jacobson’s mixed-identity-free elementary amenable groups and Gromov monster groups satisfy it for appropriate parameter choices. The amenable examples are selfless but cannot be C ∗ C^{*} -selfless, providing examples that separate these properties. Finally we remark that the special Gromov monster group examples provides a potential avenue to a non-exact C ∗ C^{*} -algebra that has strict comparison.

Introduction

A finitely generated group

[

\delta_{i}

This class is remarkably broad: it contains non-elementary amenable groups,

The

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Lacunary hyperbolic groups

The following notion, suggested by Gromov
[
11
]
, aims to capture the
asymptotic geometry of iterated small cancellation groups.
This can be traced back to Rips
[
18
]
, Bowditch
[
6
]
, and Thomas–Velickovic

[
23
]
, and has been systematically studied by
Ol’shanskii, Osin, and Sapir
[
24
]
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

A finitely generated group
G
is
lacunary hyperbolic
if there exists
a sequence of finitely generated
\delta_{i}
-hyperbolic groups
G_{i}
with
generating sets
S_{i}
and epimorphisms
\alpha_{i}\colon G_{i}\twoheadrightarrow G_{i+1}

with
\alpha_{i}(S_{i})=S_{i+1}
, such that
G=\varinjlim G_{i}
and

\delta_{i}=o(r_{i})
, where

r_{i}=r_{S_{i}}(\alpha_{i})\coloneqq\max\{\,r\geq 0:\alpha_{i}\text{ is injective on }B_{S_{i}}(r)\,\}

is the
injectivity radius
of
\alpha_{i}
.

By
[
24
, Remark 3.4]
, the sequence
r_{i}
may be taken non-decreasing.
The canonical map
\pi_{i}\colon G_{i}\to G
is then injective on
B_{S_{i}}(r_{i})
,
so the ball
B_{G_{i}}(r_{i})
is isometric to
B_{G}(r_{i})
.

We recall the following definition (and a related fact, c.f.
[
16
]
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group.
The
finite radical

W(G)
is the largest finite normal subgroup of
G
,
equivalently

W(G)=\bigcap_{\text{$g$ loxodromic}}E(g),

where
E(g)
denotes the unique maximal virtually cyclic subgroup containing
g
.
We say
G
has
trivial finite radical
if
W(G)=\{e\}
.

The intersection formula
W(G)=\bigcap E(g)
is
[
14
, Theorem 6.14]
. Non-elementary hyperbolic groups with trivial finite radical are
mixed-identity-free (MIF); see
[
17
, Lemma 3.3]
; the implication
W(G)=\{e\}\Rightarrow\text{MIF}
for acylindrically hyperbolic groups is
[
16
, Corollary 1.7]
, applied in
[
17
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Definition 2.3

(Lacunary hyperbolic with sufficient generics)
.

A lacunary hyperbolic group
G=\varinjlim G_{i}

has sufficient generics
if
for every
N\geq 1
there exist an index
i=i(N)
and a loxodromic element

g=g(N)\in G_{i(N)}
such that:

(i)

E(g)\cap B_{S_{i(N)}}(N)=\{e\}
;

(ii)

\pi_{i(N)}(g)
has infinite order in
G
.

Remark 2.4
.

Condition
(i)
(avoiding the elementary closure
E(g)
on a ball)
is related to the existence of elements avoiding quasiconvex subgroups in hyperbolic
groups. For a single torsion-free hyperbolic group
G
, this is supplied by

[
5
, Theorem 1]
: any quasiconvex subgroup of infinite index can be
“avoided” by a suitably chosen loxodromic element.
More precisely, given any loxodromic
g\in G
, the elementary closure
E(g)
is
virtually cyclic, hence quasiconvex, and has infinite index in the non-elementary
group
G
.
Applying
[
5
, Theorem 1]
with
H=E(g)
, we obtain a loxodromic
g^{\prime}
such
that
\langle E(g),g^{\prime}\rangle\cong E(g)*\langle g^{\prime}\rangle
is quasiconvex.
In particular
E(g^{\prime})\cap E(g)=\{e\}
, so
E(g^{\prime})\cap B(N)=\{e\}
for all

N
not exceeding the injectivity radius.
For the general case with hyperbolically embedded subgroups, see also

[
14
, Corollary 6.12]
.

Lemma 2.5

(Torsion-free approximants give sufficient generics)
.

Let
G=\varinjlim G_{i}
be lacunary hyperbolic. If each
G_{i}
is non-elementary
and torsion-free, then
G
has sufficient generics.

Proof.

Fix
N
. Since
W(G_{i})=\{e\}
for all
i
(torsion-free hyperbolic implies trivial
finite radical; see Definition
2.2
), and
B_{S_{i}}(N)
is finite, there exists a loxodromic

x\in G_{i}
with
E(x)\cap B_{S_{i}}(N)=\{e\}
.
Set
g=x^{k}
for
k
large enough that
|g|_{S_{i}}\geq N^{2}+C_{1}\delta_{i}
;
then
E(g)\subseteq E(x)
so
(i)
holds.
Since
G_{i}
is torsion-free,
g
has infinite order, and

\pi_{i}(g^{j})\neq e
for all
j<r_{i}/|g|_{S_{i}}
by injectivity of
\pi_{i}
on

B_{S_{i}}(r_{i})
, so
\pi_{i}(g)
has infinite order in
G
, giving
(ii)
.
∎

Lemma 2.6

(Torsion-free limit)
.

Let
G=\varinjlim G_{i}
be lacunary hyperbolic with each
G_{i}
torsion-free.
Then
G
is torsion-free.

Proof.

Suppose
g\in G
has finite order
p\geq 2
.
Choose
i
large enough that
r_{i}>p\cdot|g|_{G}
, and lift
g
to
\tilde{g}\in G_{i}

with
|\tilde{g}|_{S_{i}}=|g|_{G}
.
Then
|\tilde{g}^{p}|_{S_{i}}\leq p|g|_{G}<r_{i}
, so injectivity of
\pi_{i}
forces

\tilde{g}^{p}=e
in
G_{i}
. But
G_{i}
is torsion-free, so
\tilde{g}=e
, hence
g=e
.
∎

Lemma 2.7

(Trivial finite radical with surviving infinite-order elements gives sufficient generics)
.

Let
G=\varinjlim G_{i}
be lacunary hyperbolic with each
G_{i}
non-elementary.
Suppose
W(G_{i})=\{e\}
for all sufficiently large
i
, and that there exists an
infinite set
\mathcal{W}\subset G
of infinite-order elements such that for all
sufficiently large
i
the preimage
\pi_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{W})
is non-empty.
Then
G
has sufficient generics.

In particular, this holds whenever there is a surjection
G\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}

that factors as
G_{i}\twoheadrightarrow G
for each
i
: any loxodromic
x\in G_{i}
not
in the kernel of
G_{i}\to\mathbb{Z}
then maps to an element of infinite order in
G
.

Proof.

Fix
N
and take
i
large enough that
W(G_{i})=\{e\}
.
Since
W(G_{i})=\{e\}
, there exists a loxodromic
x\in G_{i}
with

E(x)\cap B_{S_{i}}(N)=\{e\}
(as the intersection of all
E(x)
over loxodromic

x
is trivial and
B_{S_{i}}(N)
is finite).
Loxodromic elements in
\delta
-hyperbolic groups have infinite order; by hypothesis
the image
\pi_{i}(x)
also has infinite order in
G
, so set
g=x
.
∎

Remark 2.8

(Groups that satisfy a law)
.

Condition
(ii)
of Definition
2.3
requires

\pi_{i}(g)
to have infinite order in
G
, which is impossible when
G
has
exponent
p
- this excludes the graded small cancellation constructions of Tarski Monsters with finite exponent due originally to Ol’shanskii
[
20
]
(presented, for example, in
[
24
, Theorem 4.26(2), Remark 4.27]
).
In particular, the condition of having sufficient generics is essentially designed to avoid this particular case.

Remark 2.9

(Comparison with stronger conditions)
.

One can formulate strictly stronger conditions on a lacunary hyperbolic system
that imply sufficient generics:

(a)

Primitive hyperbolically embedded relators.

If each kernel
K_{i}=\ker(G_{i}\to G_{i+1})
is normally generated by a set

R_{i}
of primitive loxodromic elements whose conjugation classes form a
hyperbolically embedded subgroup
\langle R_{i}\rangle\hookrightarrow_{h}G_{i}
,
then each
G_{i+1}
is again non-elementary torsion-free hyperbolic, so
Lemma
2.5
applies. This is the typical situation in graded small
cancellation constructions.

(b)

Infinite generic pullback.

If for all large
i
the preimage
\pi_{i}^{-1}(\mathcal{W})
is

exponentially generic
in
G_{i}
(positive spherical density or positive
hitting probability for the simple random walk), then in particular the
preimage is non-empty and consists of infinite-order elements, so
Lemma
2.7
applies. Exponential genericity is the quantitative
ingredient that Jacobson uses to propagate MIF witnesses to the direct limit
[
17
]
;
Definition
2.3
asks only for existence of a single
loxodromic element per level.

Neither condition is necessary for sufficient generics, and we do not assume any
of them in the statements that follow.

2.2.
Selfless groups

Definition 2.10

(
[
4
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

A finitely generated group
(G,X)
is
selfless
if there exists a
function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
with
\liminf_{n\to\infty}f(n)^{1/n}=1

such that for every
n\geq 1
there is an epimorphism

\varphi_{n}\colon G*\langle a\rangle\twoheadrightarrow G
satisfying:

(1)

\varphi_{n}|_{G}=\mathrm{id}_{G}
;

(2)

\varphi_{n}
is injective on
B_{X\cup\{a\}}(n)
;

(3)

\varphi_{n}\!\left(B_{X\cup\{a\}}(n)\right)\subseteq B_{X}(f(n))
.

2.3.
The Morse lemma

Theorem 2.11

(Morse lemma
[
8
, Chapter 11]
)
.

In a
\delta
-hyperbolic geodesic metric space, any
(\lambda,0)
-quasi-geodesic

\gamma
lies in the
H
-neighbourhood of a geodesic connecting its endpoints,
where

H=O(\lambda^{2}\delta).

3.
Quantitative
\delta
-tracking via Arzhantseva’s non-triviality lemma

The following lemma
[

provides the non-triviality
criterion we need. The proof is a geodesic
n
-gon argument in a

\delta
-hyperbolic
space
(not just a group), so no torsion-freeness

Lemma 3.1

(
[
5
, Lemma 13]
)
.

Let
G
be a group acting on a
\delta
-hyperbolic geodesic metric space
with generating set
S
.
Let
n\geq 1
,

, and let
y_{i},z_{i}\in G

(1\leq i\leq n)

(1)

|z_{i}|_{S}>4r+4\delta
for all

(2)

|y_{1}z_{1}|_{S}\geq|y_{1}|_{S}+|z_{1}|_{S}-2r

|z_{i-1}y_{i}z_{i}|_{S}\geq|z_{i-1}|_{S}+|y_{i}|_{S}+|z_{i}|_{S}-2r

Then
y_{1}z_{1}y_{2}z_{2}\cdots y_{n}z_{n}\neq e
in

.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a non-elementary group with finite generating set

, acting on its

\delta
-hyperbolic Cayley graph. Let

be a loxodromic element with

h_{1}g^{n_{1}}\cdots h_{m}g^{n_{m}}\neq e

|n_{j}|\geq C^{\prime}L^{2}\delta
for a universal constant

.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
3.1
with

and

.
We need to find

such that conditions
(1)

and
(2)
hold.

Setting

.

Since

is loxodromic,
|g^{n}|_{S}\geq|n|\cdot\tau(g)
, where

\tau(g)\geq c/L
for a universal

.
Set
r=2L+6\delta
.

Condition
(1)
.

We need
|g^{n_{i}}|_{S}>4r+4\delta=8L+28\delta
.
Since
|g^{n_{i}}|_{S}\geq|n_{i}|\cdot\tau(g)\geq|n_{i}|\cdot c/L
, the condition

, which exceeds

chosen sufficiently large (depending only on

).

Condition
(2)
, first inequality.

We need
|y_{1}z_{1}|_{S}\geq|y_{1}|_{S}+|z_{1}|_{S}-2r
.
The Gromov product satisfies

,

Condition

Since

Main theorem

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G=\varinjlim G_{i}
be a lacunary hyperbolic group with sufficient generics
(Definition
2.3
), satisfying

\delta_{i}(\log r_{i})^{7}=o(r_{i}).

Then

is selfless.

Proof.

We verify Definition
2.10
with

for a suitable choice of

and constant

.

Choice of

.

Since

, for each

to be the smallest

.

Choice of

By Definition

, where

is a universal constant.
Since

\varphi_{N}
is an epimorphism.

Since

and
\varphi_{N}
surjects onto all of

Verification of (2): injectivity on

Under

Lifting to

.

If

For

(replacing

if necessary, absorbing the factor of 2

).

With

,

,

•

Condition
(1)
:

,
which exceeds

for

).

•

Condition
(2)
first (

(s_{0},g^{Me_{1}})_{e}\leq|s_{0}|_{S_{i}}\leq L\leq r
,

•

Condition
(2)
second (

s_{j-1}\notin E(g)

(since

Lemma

Since

Length bound.

With

(for
N\geq C_{1}\delta_{i}
; the
complementary regime

is handled symmetrically with

),

2N\cdot(2N^{2})^{3}\delta_{i}=16\,N^{7}\delta_{i}\eqqcolon C_{0}N^{7}\delta_{i}.

By our choice of

, this is
<r_{i}
.

Verification of (3): image growth.

\varphi_{N}(B_{X\cup\{a\}}(N))\subseteq B_{X}(C_{0}N^{7}\delta_{i})
, so

f(N)=C_{0}N^{7}\delta_{i(N)}
.
We need
\liminf_{N\to\infty}f(N)^{1/N}=1
.

Pass to the subsequence of indices
k
at which
\delta_{k}
attains a new minimum,
i.e.
\delta_{k}<\delta_{j}
for all
j<k
.
This subsequence is cofinal (since
\delta_{i}\geq 0
, the infimum is approached),
so it still verifies
\liminf
.
Along this subsequence
(\delta_{k})
is strictly decreasing, and we define

N_{k}=\left\lfloor\Bigl(\frac{r_{k}}{2C_{0}\,\delta_{k}}\Bigr)^{1/7}\right\rfloor.

Since
\delta_{k}=o(r_{k})
, we have
N_{k}\to\infty
.
By definition of
N_{k}
,
2C_{0}N_{k}^{7}\delta_{k}\leq r_{k}
, so index
k
itself
satisfies the defining condition of
i(N_{k})
(namely
r_{k}>2C_{0}N_{k}^{7}\delta_{k}
),
giving
i(N_{k})\leq k
.
Since
(\delta_{j})
is decreasing along our subsequence and
i(N_{k})\leq k
, we have

\delta_{i(N_{k})}\leq\delta_{k}
, giving

f(N_{k})=C_{0}\,N_{k}^{7}\,\delta_{i(N_{k})}\leq C_{0}\,N_{k}^{7}\,\delta_{k}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}r_{k}.

Thus
f(N_{k})^{1/N_{k}}\leq r_{k}^{1/N_{k}}
, and

\log r_{k}^{1/N_{k}}=\frac{\log r_{k}}{N_{k}}\;\leq\;\log r_{k}\cdot\Bigl(\frac{2C_{0}\,\delta_{k}}{r_{k}}\Bigr)^{1/7}\;=\;(2C_{0})^{1/7}\,\frac{\delta_{k}^{1/7}\,(\log r_{k})}{r_{k}^{1/7}},

using
N_{k}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}(r_{k}/(2C_{0}\delta_{k}))^{1/7}
(from the floor).
The right side tends to
0
if and only if
\delta_{k}(\log r_{k})^{7}=o(r_{k})
,
which is exactly condition
(**)
.
Hence,

\liminf_{N\to\infty}f(N)^{1/N}\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}f(N_{k})^{1/N_{k}}=1,

and the reverse inequality
\geq 1
holds since

.
∎

Remark 4.2

(Condition
(**)
vs. the basic lacunary condition)
.

The standard lacunary condition requires only
\delta_{i}=o(r_{i})
.
Condition
(**)
requires
\delta_{i}(\log r_{i})^{7}=o(r_{i})
, which is a mild strengthening.
In particular, any sequence with

automatically satisfies

one gets

\delta_{i}(\log r_{i})^{7}\leq(\log r_{i})^{8}=o(r_{i})

(since

for any fixed

This covers all examples in Section

.

Remark 4.3

(Stability under finite index subgroups)
.

The hypotheses of Theorem
4.1
are stable under passing to finite index subgroups.
If

has finite index

, then
G^{\prime}
is lacunary hyperbolic with
\delta_{i}^{\prime}\sim\delta_{i}

and

, so condition
(**)
is preserved.
Sufficient generics passes to

: any loxodromic
g\in G_{i(N)}
with

E(g)\cap B(N)=\{e\}
and
\pi_{i}(g)
of infinite order in

has a power

whose image in

has infinite order (since

has finite index in
G
,
elements of infinite order in

have images of infinite order in

).

We obtain, as an immediate consequence, a variant of the result from
[

]
surrounding rapid decay and selflessness.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let

be a lacunary hyperbolic group satisfying the hypotheses of

[

.
Consequently,

-selfless

The obvious consequence here is that rapid decay implies

-simplicity for a broad class of lacunary hyperbolic groups.

4.1.
Ruling out sufficient generics and understanding

for lacunary hyperbolic groups

Proposition 4.5

(Trivial finite radical and MIF)
.

Let

be lacunary hyperbolic with each

The following are equivalent:

(a)

W(G_{i})=\{e\}
for all sufficiently large

(b)

Each

(c)

W(G)=\{e\}
.

Moreover, if
G
is mixed-identity-free then
(c)
holds.

(c)
does not imply that
G
is mixed-identity-free.

Proof.

(a)
\Leftrightarrow
(b)
: Non-elementary hyperbolic groups with trivial finite radical are MIF and vice versa
[
16
, Corollary 1.7]
.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(c)
: Suppose
F\trianglelefteq G
is a finite nontrivial normal subgroup.
Then
F\subset B_{G}(R)
for some
R
. For
i
large enough that
r_{i}>2R
and
W(G_{i})=\{e\}
,
the preimage
\tilde{F}=\pi_{i}^{-1}(F)\cap B_{G_{i}}(R)
is a finite subgroup of
G_{i}

(injectivity of
\pi_{i}
on

B_{G_{i}}(2R)\subset B_{G_{i}}(r_{i})

Since

is normal in
G
and
\pi_{i}
is
G
-equivariant,
\tilde{F}
is normal in
G_{i}
,
contradicting

.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(a)
: Suppose
W(G)=\{e\}
.
Since
\pi_{i}\colon G_{i}\twoheadrightarrow G
is surjective,
\pi_{i}(W(G_{i}))
is a finite normal subgroup of
G
,
hence
\pi_{i}(W(G_{i}))\subseteq W(G)=\{e\}
.
Thus
W(G_{i})\subseteq\ker(\pi_{i})
.

It remains to show
W(G_{i})=\{e\}
.
We claim
\diam_{S_{i}}(W(G_{i}))=O(\delta_{i})
.
Since
G_{i}
is non-elementary, loxodromic elements have limit points dense in
\partial G_{i}
.
Any
w\in W(G_{i})
lies in every maximal virtually cyclic subgroup

(for
g
loxodromic),
so
w
preserves all pairs of loxodromic fixed points in
\partial G_{i}
, hence fixes
\partial G_{i}

pointwise.
An isometry of a
\delta_{i}
-hyperbolic space fixing the boundary pointwise has displacement
O(\delta_{i})
:
for any loxodromic
g
, the quasi-axis
\ell
passes within

of

, and

since

fixes the two endpoints of
\ell

\partial G_{i}
.
Since

has finite order,

on

Hence

.
Hence

.

Since

, for all large

where

MIF implies

5.
Non-acylindrically hyperbolic examples of groups that are selfless

5.1.
Torsion-free Tarski monsters

Example 5.1

(Torsion-free Tarski monsters)
.

By
[
24
, Theorem 4.26(2)]
, starting from any non-elementary torsion-free
hyperbolic group
H
(e.g.
H=F_{2}
), one constructs a torsion-free group
Q

in which every proper subgroup is infinite cyclic, via a graded small cancellation
direct limit
H=G_{0}\twoheadrightarrow G_{1}\twoheadrightarrow\cdots
.
In particular,
Q
is an infinite simple group. See
[
19
,
21
]
for the original constructions of this group by Ol’shanskii.

Each approximating group
G_{i}
is a torsion-free non-elementary hyperbolic group

[
24
, Theorem 4.26(2), Lemma 4.13(a)]
.
Since each
G_{i}
is torsion-free, Lemma
2.5
gives sufficient generics.
The parameters
\rho_{i}
controlling the injectivity radius can be chosen to grow
as fast as desired
[
24
, Theorem 4.26]
, so condition
(**)
can be satisfied:
Theorem
4.1
therefore applies:
torsion-free Tarski monsters
satisfying
(**)
are selfless.

Remark 5.2
.

We note that Theorem 4.26(2) of
[
24
]
together with Remark 4.27 refers
to the construction in
[
21
]
, which covers the torsion-free case and the
case where proper subgroups have finite order (with possibly varying orders), but
does not explicitly state the finite-exponent case over an arbitrary
non-elementary torsion-free hyperbolic group.
We are confident the construction extends to that setting, but it is not
written explicitly in the literature cited here.

Since every proper subgroup of
Q
is abelian,
Q
is not acylindrically hyperbolic.
By
[
7
]
,
Q
is
C^{*}
-simple. Whether
Q
has rapid decay is open, so we cannot conclude
C^{*}
-selflessness via this method in this case. This seems like an ideal candidate for potentially separating the two properties.

Additionally, torsion-free Tarski monsters with the same
\forall\exists
-theory as
F_{2}
are constructed in
[
9
]
. This is significantly stronger than MIF: every
\forall\exists
-sentence true in
F_{2}
holds in these monsters. Since selflessness is
\forall\exists
-axiomatisable (it is a quantified form of the mixed-identity-free property), the results of
[
9
]
may directly imply selflessness for these Tarski monsters, independently of Theorem
4.1
. We note that using
[
9
]
to conclude
C^{*}
-selflessness would require continuous-logic considerations beyond
[
9
]
, which works in classical logic.

5.2.
Gromov monsters

Example 5.3

(Gromov monsters)
.

Gromov monster groups are groups obtained via geometric or graphical small cancellation
over sequences of logarithmic girth expander graphs
[
11
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
.
As explained in
[
1
]
, Gromov monsters are lacunary hyperbolic.

Geometric small cancellation Gromov monsters are
not
acylindrically hyperbolic
[
12
]
,
while certain special graphical small cancellation groups are
acylindrically hyperbolic
[
13
]
- so depending on the chosen small cancellation framework they straddle this boundary.

As these groups contain metrically embedded logarithmic girth expanders in their Cayley graphs, they are not
C^{*}
-exact.

By choosing the sequence of graphs to have girth growing sufficiently fast we can enforce condition
(**)
, which will enforce selflessness of Gromov monsters via Theorem
4.1
.

We note here directly that the geometric small cancellation examples explained in
[
1
]
can be made additionally to be property (T) groups, or by combining techniques to be Tarski monsters.

Given these examples, we are motivated to make the following conjecture:

Conjecture 5.4
.

There exists a finitely generated group whose reduced
C^{*}
-algebra is
C^{*}
-selfless but not
C^{*}
-exact.

What is missing to close this conjecture with the examples above is the rapid decay property, however an approach to the rapid decay property for infinitely presented small
cancellation groups was proposed in
[
2
]
, and we expect it to
apply in this setting (though this remains to be carried out in full). Such a group
G
would be
C^{*}
-selfless by Theorem
4.4
.

The interest of this example is that
C^{*}_{r}(G)
would then be a

non-exact

C^{*}
-algebra with strict comparison—a combination
not known to occur in the literature.

Finally, we remark that in the weaker case of classical small cancellation theory (i.e where the graphs are taken to be cycles and not expanders in the above framework), we can still obtain lacunary hyperbolic groups meeting condition
(**)
above. These groups do already have rapid decay by
[
2
]
assuming strong enough small cancellation parameters, and so would be
C^{*}
-selfless. However, these groups also have finite asymptotic dimension with linear control function
[
22
]
, so are exact (because they have asymptotic dimension 2).

5.3.
MIF elementary amenable groups

Example 5.5

(Jacobson’s MIF groups)
.

Jacobson
[
17
, Theorem 1.2]
constructs, for each prime
p
, a

2
-generated elementary amenable group
G(p,\mathbf{c})
that is
mixed-identity-free (MIF).
The construction is a modification of
[
24
]
: one chooses the sequence

\mathbf{c}=(c_{n})_{n\geq 1}
inductively so that witness elements for the
MIF property survive each bonding map.

By
[
17
, Lemma 3.5]
, each approximating group
G_{n}
is a non-elementary
hyperbolic group with
W(G_{n})=\{e\}
.
By
[
17
, Remark 3.6]
, the sequence
\mathbf{c}
can be chosen so that,
in addition,
G(p,\mathbf{c})
is lacunary hyperbolic. By choosing the sequence
\mathbf{c}
fast enough, we can ensure condition
(**)
is met.

To verify sufficient generics, we use the structure of
G_{n}
:
all finite-order elements lie in
A_{n}

[
17
, Proof of Lemma 3.5]
, while
loxodromic elements lie outside

.
The bonding maps and the quotient
G(p,\mathbf{c})\twoheadrightarrow G(p,\mathbf{c})/A(p,\mathbf{c})\cong\mathbb{Z}

preserve the

-exponent, so any loxodromic element of

has nonzero

-exponent
and maps to an element of infinite order in

.
This holds for
any
choice of
\mathbf{c}
, since the
t
-direction is untouched: Lemma
2.7
therefore applies.

We observe that the choices taken in Jacobson’s results for the sequence
\mathbf{c}
also meet condition
(**)
.

Concretely: Remark 3.6 of
[
17
]
requires only that
c_{k+1}
be chosen large
enough to ensure
r(\varepsilon_{k})\geq f(\delta_{k})
for some

with
n=o(f(n))
.
The minimal lacunary choice is
r_{k}\sim\delta_{k}^{m}
for any fixed

.
With

, condition
(**)

\delta_{k}(\log r_{k})^{7}=\delta_{k}\bigl(2\log\delta_{k}\bigr)^{7}\sim\delta_{k}(\log\delta_{k})^{7},

r_{k}=\delta_{k}^{2}

\delta_{k}(\log r_{k})^{7}/r_{k}\sim(\log\delta_{k})^{7}/\delta_{k}\to 0

Whether

is selfless, or even MIF for slowly-growing

or the results presented in
[

]
.

Finally, since

is amenable, it is not acylindrically hyperbolic (it can’t contain a free subgroup) - and since they are not virtually nilpotent, can’t satisfy the rapid decay property. These groups are also not

-simple, so certainly cannot be
C^{*}
-selfless.
