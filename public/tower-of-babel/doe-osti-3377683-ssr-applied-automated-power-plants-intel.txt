Public Executive Summary
The Stable Salt Reactor – Wasteburner (SSR-W) is a static-fuelled molten salt reactor, offering a
transformative reduction in overnight installed cost by simplification of nuclear power plant
systems and reliance upon inherent characteristics of molten salt fuel.
The SSR-W is a simpler MSR concept than the “classical MSR” design which circulates fuel.
Although this has development cost advantages, the SSR-W plant is constrained by similar
operation and maintenance (O&M) obligations as other solid-fuelled reactor designs that rely on
fuel assembly handling and management for refuelling. The SSR-W also has the added O&M costs
associated with high assay fuels, and the necessary protections and safeguards. The SSR-W is also
the first MSR plant design which claims to be operable for similar timeframes to GEN III designs
– 60 years – by using chemical control to inhibit corrosion. There is then a need to monitor and
detect aging in long-lived components to better predict remaining life duty and better plan
midlife refurbishments.
Technologies to automate operation and predictively schedule maintenance already exist. The
ability to incorporate these technologies into the baseline SSR-W design is limited by the ability
to validate their use in a nuclear safety licensing assessment. This project aimed to identify and
deliver evidence that is required to support licensing of these technologies. Moltex proposed 3
digital twin (DT) models to accomplish this. The purpose of the 3 DTs is to optimize online
refuelling strategy, simulate operating transients and optimize heat exchanger maintenance.
DT-1 provides methods for long-term fuel cycle modelling and optimization, to provide the
operator with an indication of which fuel assembly should be refueled and when this should
occur. It is based on the ANL code REBUS, with a Python wrapper program, ReloadPy, developed
as part of this project to provide various optimization strategies with user control of objectives
and constraints. Long term simulation has been demonstrated and many optimization strategies
have been tested, providing important insight into the achievable core performance through the
fuel cycle and the tradeoffs that must be considered. It has been demonstrated that 2D
simulation results in essentially the same fuel cycle strategy as 3D simulation, allowing a very
significant run time saving. It has also been demonstrated that an optimum strategy can be
obtained by optimizing the cycle only to the point that every position has been refueled once,
starting with a beginning of life core design that replicates the equilibrium core as closely as
possible. The same sequence can then be used in subsequent refueling.
DT-2 was originally envisaged to optimize plant performance during refuelling transients, by
adjusting secondary coolant flow rate to compensate for power and temperature changes that
could otherwise have a detrimental impact on core component lifetime. However, work with the
coupled PROTEUS-SAM model showed that the changes were acceptably small. The purpose of
this DT was therefore updated to provide a general transient predictive capability, by integrating
reactor physics and computational fluid dynamics codes into a system-level SAM model.
Methods have been developed for calibration of the integrated SAM model using operational
data from reactor instrumentation during commissioning and through operation. This calibration

improves the model and hence results in higher accuracy and lower uncertainties. Model
parameters are also updated using periodic recalculation of reactor kinetics data and reactivity
coefficients. A key aspect of this work was development of a model of the feedback due to axial
fuel expansion in SAM.
DT-3 models an innovative matrix heat exchanger and enables development of an
instrumentation and control strategy with fault detection capabilities, using machine learning
(ML) to aid detection and localization. The performance of seven ML algorithms has been
benchmarked for the detection and localization of channel plugging through binary classification.
In principle, all ML models could be used for real-time monitoring. Additionally, sensor noise has
been studied and introduced into COMSOL-simulated HX data to model the experimental
scenario with higher fidelity.
In addition to the DT models, a separate effects test loop is being constructed to provide thermal-
hydraulic validation data and demonstrate combined online chemistry monitoring and control in
a pilot-scaled, forced flow chloride system. This provides flowing salt data that has broader
impacts and benefits to the molten salt reactor, concentrating solar power, thermal energy
storage communities, in addition to validation of the pin to molten salt coolant heat transfer that
is unique to Stable Salt Reactors.

Project Activities
The focus of the project has been on the development of digital twins to de-risk licensing of
improved operating and maintenance practices. The operation of a bespoke flowing separate
effects molten salt loop at ANL, with realistic temperature gradients, will provide invaluable data
for computer codes validation.

Three digital twins of aspects of the SSR-W have been successfully developed using ANL expertise
and software. These digital twins have demonstrated optimization of the fuel cycle, the ability to
model transients using an integrated coupled neutronic – thermal-hydraulic model with a model
of the fuel expansion feedback so important to the inherent safety of the SSR-W. Advanced
machine learning techniques have been developed and demonstrated for optimization of heat
exchanger operation and maintenance.
