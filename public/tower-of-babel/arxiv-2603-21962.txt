Abstract
. We consider magnetic Schrödinger equations with sublinear magnetic potentials and subquadratic electric potentials on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , as well as generalizations thereof. We obtain new results on the global well-posedness of the Cauchy problem with initial data in magnetic modulation spaces M A p ​ ( ℝ d ) M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) . Our results are achieved by approximating the solution in phase space using the magnetic Hamiltonian flow. This method includes the potentials as part of the generalized Schrödinger operator instead of treating them as perturbations, and thereby allows us to deal with unbounded potentials. For A ≡ 0 A\equiv 0 , the space M A p ​ ( ℝ d ) M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) reduces to the usual modulation space M p ​ ( ℝ d ) M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) , for which relevant known results for the usual Schrödinger equation can be recovered.

1.
Introduction

Let
d\geq 2
. In this article, we study the global well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for the Schrödinger equation with unbounded electromagnetic potentials
A,V
in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(1)

i\partial_{t}u(t;y)=(H^{A}+V(t))u(t;y),\qquad u(0)=u_{0}

on magnetic modulation spaces
M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as defined below.
Here
H^{A}
denotes the magnetic Laplacian with the magnetic vector potential
A=(A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{d})
, that is

H^{A}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(-i\partial_{j}-A_{j}(y)\right)^{2},

and
V(t)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
denotes the time-dependent electric scalar potential. We consider unbounded potentials
A
with at most linear growth and
V(t)
with at most quadratic growth, that is for multi-indices
\alpha
and
\beta
, the potentials satisfy

|\partial^{\alpha}A(y)|\leq C_{A,\alpha},\qquad|\partial^{\beta}_{y}V(t;y)|\leq C_{V,\beta},\qquad|\alpha|\geq 1,\ |\beta|\geq 2.

We also define the magnetic field
B
to be a smooth, closed
2
-form

B(y):=\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq d}B_{jk}(y)dy_{j}\wedge dy_{k}

with the skew symmetry
B_{jk}=-B_{kj}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
. Then a magnetic vector potential
A
can be associated to
B
if it satisfies
dA=B
, that is
\partial_{k}A_{j}-\partial_{j}A_{k}=B_{jk}
.

For differential operators with long-range magnetic potentials, a pseudo-differential calculus adapted to the magnetic potential has been developed by Iftimie, Măntoiu, and Purice in
[
13
]
,
[
9
]
. The theory extends the usual Weyl calculus to the magnetic Weyl calculus
h\mapsto\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
, mapping a symbol
h\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
to a magnetic pseudo-differential operator (magnetic
\Psi
DO)
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
, acting on
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as

(2)

\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)u(y):=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i(y-y^{\prime})\cdot\eta}e^{-i\varphi^{A}(y^{\prime},y)}h(\tfrac{y+y^{\prime}}{2},\eta)u(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}d\eta.

The precise meaning of the above expression will be recalled in the next section. We note here that this adapted formulation differs from the usual Weyl calculus only by a magnetic-dependent phase term
e^{-i\varphi^{A}(y^{\prime},y)}
, which has the property

(3)

e^{-i\varphi^{A}(y^{\prime},y)}\big|_{A=0}\equiv 1.

We thus recover the usual Weyl calculus as the special case
A\equiv 0
. With this formulation, the magnetic Schrödinger operator in (
1
) can be expressed as

(4)

H^{A}+V=\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h),\qquad h(t;y,\eta)=|\eta|^{2}+V(t;y).

We show under suitable conditions, the Cauchy problems associated to a family of equations that contains (
1
)

(5)

i\partial_{t}u(t,y)=\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)u(t;y),\quad u(0)=u_{0}

is well-posed on the magnetic modulation space
M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Here the space
M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be seen as the
A
-adapted version of the usual modulation space, defined through the magnetic wavepacket transform
\mathcal{T}^{A}
for
u\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\mathcal{T}^{A}u(x,\xi):=\int e^{-i\xi\cdot(y-x)}e^{-i\varphi^{A}(y,x)}g(y-x)u(y)\ dy,\quad\|u\|_{M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\left\|\mathcal{T}^{A}u\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})},

where
g
denotes the normalized Gaussian function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(a window function). In particular, for
p=2
we recover the usual
L^{2}
space, that is
M^{2}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cong L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We also note, although the space
M^{p}_{A}
is formulated explicitly with the magnetic potential
A
, it in fact intrinsically depends only on the magnetic field
B
. In other words,
M^{p}_{A}
is gauge-independent, in the sense that for another magnetic potential
A^{\prime}
with
dA^{\prime}=dA=B
, we have
M^{p}_{A}\simeq M^{p}_{A^{\prime}}
isometrically.

This choice of space is inspired by the well-posedness of the usual Schrödinger equation in modulation spaces
M^{p,q}
, see
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
and the references therein. Also due to the phase term
e^{-i\varphi^{A}}
and its property (
3
), the magnetic modulation space
M^{p}_{A}
naturally extends the usual modulation space
M^{p,p}
, and is well-behaved interacting with the adapted magnetic
\Psi
DOs (
2
). One of the early ideas of defining magnetic versions of modulation spaces can be found in Măntoiu-Purice
[
14
]
, where they considered the case
p=2
. To the best of our knowledge, the definition and discussion of
M^{p}_{A}
and its weighted version
M^{m,p}_{A}
in this article is new, and so are its applications to magnetic Schrödinger equations.

Previously well-posedness results for the Cauchy problem of (
1
) with unbounded potentials are mostly obtained on
L^{2}
-based spaces. One foundational work is Yajima
[
18
]
, where unbounded, non-smooth potentials with
t
-dependence are considered. More generally, Doi in
[
7
]
showed under certain conditions the variable-coefficient version of (
1
) with unbounded potentials is also well-posed in
L^{2}
-based weighted Sobolev spaces. On the other hand, results for
L^{p}
-based spaces with
p\neq 2
seem out of reach, since the non-magnetic Schrödinger group
e^{-it\Delta}
is already unbounded on
L^{p}
for
p\neq 2
. For the non-magnetic
A\equiv 0
case, the introduction of the modulation space
M^{p,q}
as a substitute for
L^{p}
has been very successful, since the Schrödinger group
e^{-it\Delta}
has been shown to be bounded on the modulation spaces
M^{p,q}

[
1
]
. This suggests taking spaces of modulation type as candidates for wellposedness of magnetic Schrödinger equations. In this direction, Kato-Muramatsu
[
11
]
obtained a-priori estimates on
M^{p}
, but there the existence of the solution is still restricted to
L^{2}
.

By introducing the magnetic modulation space
M^{p}_{A}
and the weighted version
M^{m,p}_{A}
, we obtain a global well-posedness result for (
5
). Our assumptions on the potentials are the following.

Assumption 1.1
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
. We assume the magnetic field
B
has smooth components
\{B_{jk}\}_{1\leq j,k\leq d}
, which are all bounded with
\|B_{jk}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{B}
for some
C_{B}>0
. Furthermore, we assume the derivatives of
B_{jk}
satisfy a decay condition, that is for multi-indices
\alpha
, there exists
C_{\alpha,B}>0
such that

(6)

|\partial^{\alpha}B_{jk}(y)|\leq C_{\alpha,B}\langle y\rangle^{-1-\epsilon},\qquad|\alpha|\geq 1.

For such given magnetic field
B
, we define the magnetic vector potential
A
by the transversal gauge

(7)

A_{j}(y):=-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{1}sy_{k}B_{jk}(sy)\ ds,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For the electric scalar potential
V
, we allow
t
-dependence and assume that
V\in C(\mathbb{R},C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))
, with quadratic growth bounds that is uniform in
t
,

(8)

|\partial^{\alpha}_{y}V(t;y)|\leq C_{V,\alpha},\qquad|\alpha|\geq 2,\;t\in\mathbb{R}.

For the magnetic
\Psi
DO
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
generalizing the magnetic Schrödinger operator, we define the symbol class
S^{0,(k)}
, which aligns with the symbol class used in Tataru
[
16
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and multi-indices
\alpha,\beta
, a symbol
h\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is in the symbol class
S^{0,(k)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
if it satisfies

|\partial_{y}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta}h(y,\eta)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta},\qquad|\alpha|+|\beta|\geq k.

We make the following assumption on the symbols.

Assumption 1.3
.

We assume the time-dependent symbol
h\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}))
is real-valued, and
h(t),\dot{h}(t)\in S^{0,(2)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
uniformly in
t
. Furthermore, we assume
h
is of second-order in
\eta
, that is, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
there exists
C>0
such that
|h(t;0,\eta)|\geq C|\eta|^{2}
.

Remark 1.4
.

While the second-order part of the assumption guarantees the operator
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
to be of magnetic Schrödinger operator type, our result also holds free of this part of the assumption. In fact, for
h
that is first-order in
\eta
, the arguments will be simpler.

The above assumption gives a family of symbols which contains the symbol
h(t;y,\eta)=|\eta|^{2}+V(t;y)
in (
4
) with
V
satisfying Assumption
1.1
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
T>0
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. For a given magnetic field
B
satisfying Assumption
1.1
and a real-valued symbol
h
satisfying Assumption
1.3
, the Cauchy problem with initial value
u_{0}\in M^{2,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(9)

\begin{cases}\ i\partial_{t}u(t,y)=\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)u(t,y),\\
\ u(0,y)=u_{0}(y),\end{cases}

has a unique strong solution
u\in W^{1,1}([0,T],M_{A}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
with
u(t)\in M^{2,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Moreover, we have the bound

\|u(t)\|_{M^{2,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{T,h,B}\|u_{0}\|_{M^{2,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

This result gives the following simple corollary.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
T>0
and
1\leq p\leq\infty,s\geq 2
. For the same magnetic Schrödinger operator
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
as in Theorem
1.5
and
f\in L^{1}([0,T],M^{s,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, the Cauchy problem with initial value
u_{0}\in M^{2,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\begin{cases}\ i\partial_{t}u(t,y)=\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)u(t,y)+f(t,y),\\
\ u(0,y)=u_{0}(y)\end{cases}

has a unique strong solution
u\in W^{1,1}([0,T],M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
with
u(t)\in M^{2,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Our approach is also flexible enough to treat the case of time-dependent magnetic potentials
A(t)
, which we explain in Section 8.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
T>0
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Let
B(t)
be a time-dependent magnetic field with components
B_{jk}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. For some fixed
\epsilon>0
, assume for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
B_{jk}(t)
satisfies
\|B(t)\|_{\infty}+\|\dot{B}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq C_{B}
and the decay condition

(10)

|\partial^{\alpha}B_{jk}(t;y)|+|\partial^{\alpha}\dot{B}_{jk}(t;y)|\leq C_{\alpha,B}\langle y\rangle^{-1-\epsilon},\qquad|\alpha|\geq 1.

The Cauchy problem with initial value
u_{0}\in M^{2,p}_{A(0)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(11)

\begin{cases}\ i\partial_{t}u(t,y)=\operatorname{Op}^{A(t)}(h)u(t,y),\\
\ u(0,y)=u_{0}(y),\end{cases}

has a unique strong solution
u\in W^{1,1}([0,T],M_{A(t)}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
with
u(t)\in M^{2,p}_{A(t)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Moreover, we have the bound

\|u(t)\|_{M^{2,p}_{A(t)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{T,h,B}\|u_{0}\|_{M^{2,p}_{A(0)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

With the identification
M^{2}_{A(t)}\simeq L^{2}
, the above result can be seen as a generalization of the classical results in Yajima
[
18
]
.

Our strategy for the proof is inspired by the work of Tataru
[
16
]
, Smith
[
15
]
, Cordero-Gröchenig-Nicola-Rodino
[
3
]
, Cordero-Nicola-Rodino
[
4
]
and Cornean-Helffer-Purice
[
6
]
. The main idea of the strategy is to take a phase space perspective by using the wavepacket transform
\mathcal{T}^{A}
, so that the quantum evolution
u_{0}\mapsto u(t)
satisfying (
9
) can be related to a classical evolution in the phase space, that is a magnetic Hamiltonian flow with initial data
\tilde{u}_{0}:=\mathcal{T}^{A}u_{0}
. The flow property is then exploited through a generalized version of Yajima
[
18
, Lemma 2.1]
, which only requires the mild decay assumption on
B
as in (
6
). The
A
-adapted space
M^{p}_{A}
naturally arises from the use of the transform
\mathcal{T}^{A}
, and for
A\equiv 0
our result recovers the usual modulation space well-posedness results for the non-magnetic Schrödinger equation with unbounded electric potential
V
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

For the configuration space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the phase space is the cotangent bundle
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. For differential operators, we use the notation
D:=-i\partial
, and for a scalar function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we use
\partial_{k}f(x):=\partial_{x_{k}}f(x)
where
x:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},\dots,x_{d})
. If
f
is time-dependent, we also use the shorthand notation
\dot{f}:=df/dt
. For scalar products of
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we use
x\cdot y
or
\langle x,y\rangle
. For the
L^{2}
pairing we denote
\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}}:=\int\bar{f}g
, with the complex conjugation on the left.

We use the Japanese bracket
\langle x\rangle:=(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and note Peetre’s inequality, for
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\langle x+y\rangle^{s}\leq 2^{s}\langle x\rangle^{s}\langle y\rangle^{|s|},\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For inequalities, we use the notation
\lesssim
to hide absolute constants, and also the constants that only depend on invariant objects such as the Gaussian
g
. We use subscripts to denote the dependence of relevant objects or parameters, for example
C_{B}
denotes constants that can be determined from a given magnetic field
B
.

2.2.
Magnetic Potential and Flux

Let the magnetic field
B
with components
B_{jk}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
satisfy
\|\partial^{\alpha}B_{jk}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\alpha}
, and let
A(\cdot)
denote the magnetic vector potential obtained through the transversal gauge (
7
). For fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the vector potential
A(\cdot,x)
by

A_{j}(y,x):=-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{1}s(y_{k}-x_{k})B_{jk}(x+s(y-x))\ ds,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The above potential satisfies
dA(\cdot,x)=B
and also recovers
A(y)
by
A(y,0)=A(y)
. The vector-valued function
A(\cdot)-A(\cdot,x)
is related to the desired gauge-covariant property of the magnetic
\Psi
DOs. Notice we have
dA(\cdot)-dA(\cdot,x)=0
, so
A(\cdot)-A(\cdot,x)
is in fact a closed 1-form. We can thus find a function
\varphi^{A}
such that

(12)

\partial_{y_{j}}\varphi^{A}(y,x)=A_{j}(y)-A_{j}(y,x),\quad y,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ 1\leq j\leq d.

A straightforward choice is to integrate both sides above from
x
to
y
(cf.
[
5
]
equation (1.4)),

(13)

\varphi^{A}(y,x):=\int_{\Gamma_{x,y}}(A(\cdot)-A(\cdot,x))=\int_{\Gamma_{x,y}}A,\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\Gamma_{x,y}
denotes the line segment connecting
x
to
y
. Explicitly, we have

\int_{\Gamma_{x,y}}A:=\int_{\Gamma_{x,y}}A(\cdot,0)=(y-x)\cdot\int_{0}^{1}A((1-s)x+sy,0)\ ds,

and the
\int_{\Gamma_{x,y}}A(\cdot,x)
term in (
13
) vanishes, because
B
is skew-symmetric, so

\int_{\Gamma_{x,y}}A(\cdot,x)=\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}(y_{j}-x_{j})\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\theta s(y_{k}-x_{k})B_{jk}(x+\theta((1-s)x+sy))\ d\theta ds=0.

From this construction, we see that
\varphi^{A}
is anti-symmetric in its variables, i.e.

(14)

\varphi^{A}(y,x)=-\varphi^{A}(x,y),\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Because the value of
\varphi^{A}
equals integrating the vector field
A
along a line segment, we denote by
\Gamma^{B}(y,x,z)
the flux given by the magnetic field
B
through the oriented triangle with vertices
y,x,z
. Using Stokes’ theorem, we have

(15)

\Gamma^{B}(y,x,z)=\varphi^{A}(y,x)+\varphi^{A}(x,z)+\varphi^{A}(z,y).

By
[
10
, Lemma 2.10]
, we can formulate a matrix
C(y,x,z):=\left(c_{jk}(y,x,z)\right)_{1\leq j,k\leq d}
with

c_{jk}(y,x,z):=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}B_{jk}(ty+(s-t)x+(1-s)z)\ dtds,\quad(y,x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3d},

it then allows us to re-write
\Gamma^{B}
as

\Gamma^{B}(y,x,z)=\langle C(y,x,z)(y-x),(y-z)\rangle.

With the above formulation, the following estimates hold by direct calculation, which bounds the derivatives of the magnetic flux by the length of the line segments
|y-x|
and
|y-z|
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For a magnetic field
B
with
B_{jk}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
and
\|\partial^{\alpha}B_{jk}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\alpha}
, the associated magnetic flux
\Gamma^{B}
and phase function
e^{i\Gamma^{B}}
satisfy

\displaystyle|\Gamma^{B}(y,x,z)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{B}|y-x||y-z|,

(16)

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}e^{i\Gamma^{B}(y,x,z)}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha,B}(|y-x|+|y-z|)^{|\alpha|},\qquad|\alpha|\geq 1.

3.
Magnetic Pseudo-differential Operator

In this section, we recall the construction of magnetic pseudo-differential operators (
\Psi
DOs) with a given magnetic field
B
satisfying
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial^{\alpha}B_{jk}(x)|\leq C_{\alpha}
. With
\varphi^{A}
constructed in (
13
), we can now give a definition of magnetic
\Psi
DOs following the construction in
[
9
, Section 1]
), for general symbols
h
in the space of tempered distributions
\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Since tempered distributions can grow at most polynomially at infinity, the resulting magnetic
\Psi
DOs as integral operators are well-defined for Schwartz functions
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a symbol
h\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, the magnetic pseudo-differential operator
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d});\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is defined by

(17)

\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)u(y):=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{i(y-y^{\prime})\cdot\eta}e^{-i\varphi^{A}(y^{\prime},y)}h(\tfrac{y+y^{\prime}}{2},\eta)u(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}d\eta,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

This mapping of symbols to magnetic
\Psi
DOs
h\mapsto\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
is also called magnetic Weyl quantization. This formulation has the advantage that the magnetic potential
A
is fully decoupled from the symbol
h
. For comparison, the magnetic Schrödinger operator
H^{A}+V
in (
1
) can also be formulated with the usual
\Psi
DO theory, which requires a much complicated symbol

h_{A}(t;y,\eta):=|\eta-A(y)|^{2}+V(t;y)

such that

H^{A}+V=\operatorname{Op}(h_{A}):=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{i(y-y^{\prime})\cdot\eta}h_{A}(\tfrac{y+y^{\prime}}{2},\eta)u(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}d\eta.

Furthermore, the magnetic
\Psi
DO formulation enjoys a gauge-covariant property. That is, for
A^{\prime}=A+dv
with
v\in C_{\mathrm{pol}}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}\right)
a real-valued smooth function of at most polynomial growth,
A
and
A^{\prime}
are both associated to the magnetic field
B=dA^{\prime}=dA
. Then the magnetic
\Psi
DOs
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
and
\operatorname{Op}^{A^{\prime}}(h)
are unitarily equivalent in
L^{2}
(cf.
[
13
, Proposition 3.6]
). Thus this formulation depends more intrinsically on the object
B
, which allows us to formulate Assumption
1.1
in terms of the magnetic field
B
instead of the magnetic potentials
A
. In particular, the adapted magnetic modulation space
M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we formulate also depends intrinsically on
B
, which we show in the next section as Proposition
4.5
.

Another important feature of the above Weyl quantization is that for real symbols
h
, the operator
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
is symmetric on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, thanks to the balanced choice
\frac{y+y^{\prime}}{2}
in (
17
). But this can also make explicit calculations a bit awkward. To simplify calculations, a slightly different quantization is also useful, that is the magnetic Kohn-Nirenberg quantization
\operatorname{Op}_{KN}^{A}
(or the magnetic “left” quantization)

\operatorname{Op}_{KN}^{A}(h)u(y):=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{i(y-y^{\prime})\cdot\eta}e^{-i\varphi^{A}(y^{\prime},y)}h(y,\eta)u(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}d\eta,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The Weyl quantization can be converted to the Kohn-Nierenberg quantization by modifying the symbol
h
.
[
10
, Proposition 2.13]
provides such a conversion for a symbol class
S^{m}
, and the proof there can be used with obvious changes to give a conversion for our symbol class
S^{0,(2)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given
a\in S^{0,(2)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, there exists a symbol
b\in S^{0,(2)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
such that
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(a)=\operatorname{Op}_{KN}^{A}(b)
, with

b(y,\eta)=a(y,\eta)+a_{r}(y,\eta),\quad a_{r}\in S^{0,(0)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}).

By
[
10
, Proposition 2.13]
, we also have an explicit expression for the symbol
a_{r}
given by

a_{r}(y,\eta):=\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}\frac{1}{|\alpha|!}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{|\alpha|}\left(D_{y}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{\alpha}a\right)(y,\eta),\qquad(y,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

This implies that if the symbol
a
depends only on
y
or
\eta
, then the two quantizations
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(a)=\operatorname{Op}_{KN}^{A}(a)
coincide, because all cross-derivatives vanish. In particular, the symbols of the basic magnetic differentiation and multiplication operator

\operatorname{P}^{A}_{j}u(y):=(-i\partial_{y_{j}}-A_{j}(y))u(y),\quad\operatorname{Q}_{j}u(y):=y_{j}u(y)

coincide in the two quantizations. That is, for
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

(18)

\displaystyle\operatorname{P}^{A}_{j}u(y)=\operatorname{Op}^{A}(\eta_{j})u(y)=\operatorname{Op}_{KN}^{A}(\eta_{j})u(y);

\displaystyle\operatorname{Q}_{j}u(y)=\operatorname{Op}^{A}(y_{j})u(y)=\operatorname{Op}_{KN}^{A}(y_{j})u(y).

This can be shown with direct calculations using Fourier inversion. We showcase the calculation with
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(\eta_{j})u(y)
, and the other identity is similar. Denote by
\mathcal{F}_{y^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\eta}
the Fourier transform and inversion regarding the variables
y^{\prime}
and
\eta
. We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}^{A}(\eta_{j})u(y)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Op}_{KN}^{A}(\eta_{j})u(y)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{i(y-y^{\prime})\cdot\eta}e^{-i\varphi^{A}(y^{\prime},y)}\eta_{j}u(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}d\eta

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\eta}^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{y^{\prime}}\left(-i\partial_{y^{\prime}_{j}}(e^{-i\varphi^{A}(\cdot,y)}u)\right)(y)

\displaystyle=(-i\partial_{y_{j}}-A_{j}(y))u(y),

where the last equality is based on (
12
) and
\varphi^{A}(y,y)=0
, that is

-i\partial_{y^{\prime}_{j}}e^{-i\varphi^{A}(y^{\prime},y)}\big|_{y^{\prime}=y}=-(A_{j}(y^{\prime})-A_{j}(y^{\prime},y))e^{-i\varphi^{A}(y^{\prime},y)}\big|_{y^{\prime}=y}=A_{j}(y).

4.
The Magnetic Wavepacket Transform and Modulation Spaces

For a given magnetic field
B
and the associated magnetic potential
A
, we define a family of magnetic wavepackets using the magnetic phase function
\varphi^{A}
constructed in (
13
).

Definition 4.1
.

We denote the
L^{2}
-normalized Gaussian by

g(y):=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{d}{4}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}y^{2}},\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For fixed
\lambda\geq 1
, the family of
\lambda
-rescaled magnetic wavepackets
\{g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}\}_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
are Schwartz functions parameterized by
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, defined by

(19)

g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y):=\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}e^{i\xi\cdot(y-x)}g(\lambda(y-x))e^{i\varphi^{A}(y,x)},\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

By the above definition, for
A\equiv 0
and
\lambda=1
, we recover the standard Gaussian wavepackets
\{g_{x,\xi}\}_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
,

g_{x,\xi}(y):=e^{i\xi\cdot(y-x)}g(y-x)=\pi^{-\frac{d}{4}}e^{i\xi\cdot(y-x)}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(y-x)^{2}},\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

which are translated and modulated Gaussians, centered around
x
in the configuration space and around
\xi
in the frequency space. Thus, the magnetic wavepacket transform we define below can be seen as a generalization of the usual wavepacket transform (cf.
[
16
]
, equation (1)).

Definition 4.2
.

For a tempered distribution
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, its magnetic wavepacket transform
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A}u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is obtained by integrating against wavepackets
g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}
defined in (
19
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A}u(x,\xi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}}(y)u(y)\ dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}e^{-i\xi\cdot(y-x)}g(\lambda(y-x))e^{-i\varphi^{A}(y,x)}u(y)\ dy,\quad(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

This magnetic wavepacket transform
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A}
is an example of a phase space transform, since it takes a function (distribution) on the configuration space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to the phase space
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. We also define a formal adjoint
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A*}
acting on phase space distributions
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
by

\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A*}\tilde{u}(y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)\tilde{u}(x,\xi)\ dxd\xi.

Lemma 4.3
.

The magnetic wavepacket transform
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A}
and its formal adjoint
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A*}
have the following properties:

(i)

If
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda}u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. If
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
then
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A*}\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(ii)

\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A},\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A*}
are
L^{2}
isometries.

(iii)

For
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have the inversion formula
u=\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A*}\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A}u
.

The first two properties can be checked by direct calculation, or proved similarly as in the non-magnetic case, such as Proposition 1.82 in
[
2
]
(also cf. Proposition 3.3 in
[
14
]
). The inversion formula can be obtained by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A*}\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A}u(y)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3d}}e^{i\xi\cdot(y-y^{\prime})}g(\lambda(y-x))g(\lambda(y^{\prime}-x))e^{i\varphi^{A}(y,x)}e^{-i\varphi^{A}(y^{\prime},x)}u(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}dxd\xi

\displaystyle=\lambda^{d}(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\delta(y-y^{\prime})g(\lambda(y-x))g(\lambda(y^{\prime}-x))e^{i\Gamma^{B}(y,x,y^{\prime})}e^{-i\varphi^{A}(y^{\prime},y)}u(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}dx

\displaystyle=\lambda^{d}(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}u(y)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(\lambda(y-x))^{2}\ dx

\displaystyle=u(y),

where we used the evaluations

\Gamma^{B}(y,x,y^{\prime})\big|_{y^{\prime}=y}=\varphi^{A}(y,x)+\varphi^{A}(x,y)=0,\quad e^{-i\varphi^{A}(y^{\prime},y)}\big|_{y^{\prime}=y}=0.

From the above calculation we can also see the nice interaction between two wavepackets.

We then define the magnetic modulation space
M_{A}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
adapted to the magnetic potential
A
to be the space of functions whose
\mathcal{T}^{A}
transform has
L^{p}_{x,\xi}
integrability.

Definition 4.4
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, we define the magnetic modulation space
M_{A}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

M_{A}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|u\|_{M_{A}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A}u(x,\xi)|^{p}\ dxd\xi\right)^{1/p}<\infty\right\}.

For
p=\infty
, the integral should be replaced by
L^{\infty}
norm and the space
M_{A}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined as the closure of the Schwartz space with respect to the norm.

By this definition, when
A\equiv 0
we recover the usual modulation space
M^{p,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We note it is also possible to generalize by replacing
L^{p}_{x,\xi}
with
L^{p}_{x}L^{q}_{\xi}
integrability, which then align with the non-magnetic modulation space
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, but for this article we only focus on the case
p=q
. General information and properties of the non-magnetic
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
spaces can be found in
[
8
]
or Chapter 2 in
[
2
]
. Similar to the non-magnetic case, We can prove analogously the following properties for
M_{A}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. We have the following.

(i)

M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is independent of the choice
\lambda\geq 1
.

(ii)

M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Banach space.

(iii)

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(iv)

For
u\in M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have
u=\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A*}\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A}u
.

In particular, for
p=2
we have
M^{2}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\simeq L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
isometrically.

Up to now, we have used the magnetic vector potential
A
from the transversal gauge (
7
) to define the wavepacket transform and the modulation spaces. Here, we remark on the general magnetic potentials
A^{\prime}(y):=A(y)+\partial_{y}v(y)
. By replacing
A
with
A^{\prime}
, we can define accordingly
\mathcal{T}^{A^{\prime}}
and
M^{p}_{A^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which actually leads to the same space.

Proposition 4.6
.

For
v\in C_{\mathrm{pol}}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}\right)
and
A^{\prime}(y):=A(y)+\partial_{y}v(y)
, which satisfies
dA^{\prime}=dA
, we have
M^{p}_{A^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\simeq M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
isometrically.

Proof.

Notice that
A^{\prime}(y):=A(y)+\partial_{y}v(y)
gives

\varphi^{A^{\prime}}(y,x)=\int_{\Gamma_{x,y}}A^{\prime}=\int_{\Gamma_{x,y}}(A+\partial v)=\varphi^{A}(y,x)+v(y)-v(x).

Consider the injective map
u\mapsto e^{iv}u
for
u\in M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then for
w:=e^{iv}u
, we have

\mathcal{T}^{A^{\prime}}w(x,\xi)=\int e^{-i\xi\cdot(y-x)}g(y-x)e^{-i\varphi^{A^{\prime}}(y,x)}w(y)\ dy=e^{iv(x)}\mathcal{T}^{A}u(x,\xi),

so
w\in M^{p}_{A^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|w\|_{M^{p}_{A^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|u\|_{M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. The map
u\mapsto e^{iv}u
is also surjective, since for any
w^{\prime}\in M^{p}_{A^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we can take
u^{\prime}:=e^{-iv}w^{\prime}
, and by a similar calculation as above we have
u^{\prime}\in M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
∎

We also introduce the weighted version
M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
m\in\mathbb{R}
. We denote the standard weights
\nu_{m}(x,\xi):=(1+|x|^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{m/2}
and the re-scaled weights
\nu_{\lambda,m}(x,\xi):=(1+|\lambda^{-1}x|^{2}+|\lambda^{-1}\xi|^{2})^{m/2}
. The weighted magnetic modulation space
M_{A}^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by

M_{A}^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|u\|_{M_{A}^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\|\nu_{\lambda,m}\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}<\infty\right\},

where the case
p=\infty
is also understood as the closure of the Schwartz space with respect to the associated norm.

By the above definition,
M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is essentially a weighted
L^{p}
space from a phase space perspective, so it also enjoys properties analogous to the ones in Proposition
4.5
. For
m_{1}\geq m_{0}
, we have naturally
M^{m_{1},p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq M^{m_{0},p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For
p=2
, by the isomorphism
M^{0,2}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\simeq L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it is easy to see for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the space
M^{k,2}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
contains the
L^{2}
-based weighted Sobolev space
\Sigma(k)
used in Yajima
[
18
]

\Sigma(k)=\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\Sigma_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq k}\|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|u\|^{2}_{\Sigma(k)}<\infty\}.

Moreover, for our generalized magnetic Schrödinger operator
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
with symbol
h
satisfying Assumption
1.3
, the space
M^{2,p}_{A}
is a natural domain for
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
in
M^{p}_{A}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty,m\in\mathbb{R}
, and let symbol
h
satisfies the Assumption
1.3
. Then the magnetic pseudo-differential operator
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h):M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to M^{m-2,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is bounded.

This lemma can be seen as a consequence of the following more general lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
m,s\in\mathbb{R}
. If a linear operator
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies for all
N,N^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}

(20)

|\langle g^{A}_{z,\zeta},\mathcal{L}g^{A}_{x,\xi}\rangle_{L^{2}}|\lesssim\nu_{s}(z,\zeta)\langle z-x\rangle^{-N}\langle\zeta-\xi\rangle^{-N^{\prime}},

then
\mathcal{L}:M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to M^{m-s,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is bounded.

Proof.

By density of the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in
M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it suffices to consider
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Denote
G_{\mathcal{L}}(z,\zeta,x,\xi):=\langle g^{A}_{z,\zeta},\mathcal{L}g^{A}_{x,\xi}\rangle_{L^{2}}
.
By the reproducing formula
u=\mathcal{T}^{A*}\mathcal{T}^{A}u
we can write

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}u\|_{M^{m-s,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|\nu_{m-s}(z,\zeta)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{g^{A}_{z,\zeta}}(y)\mathcal{L}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}g^{A}_{x,\xi}(y)\mathcal{T}^{A}u(x,\xi)\ dxd\xi\right)\ dy\right|^{p}\ dzd\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nu_{m-s}(z,\zeta)G_{\mathcal{L}}(z,\zeta,x,\xi)\nu_{-m}(x,\xi)\nu_{m}(x,\xi)\mathcal{T}^{A}u(x,\xi)\ dxd\xi\right|^{p}\ dzd\zeta.

Notice that the inner integral on right hand side above can be seen as integrating a phase space function
\nu_{m}\mathcal{T}^{A}u
against an integral kernel involving
G_{\mathcal{L}}
. By the property (
20
), the integral kernel can be bounded for all
N,N^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
by

\displaystyle|\nu_{m-s}(z,\zeta)G_{\mathcal{L}}(z,\zeta,x,\xi)\nu_{-m}(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\nu_{m}(z,\zeta)\langle z-x\rangle^{-N}\langle\zeta-\xi\rangle^{-N^{\prime}}\nu_{-m}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\nu_{m}(z-x,\zeta-\xi)\nu_{m}(x,\xi)\langle z-x\rangle^{-N}\langle\zeta-\xi\rangle^{-N^{\prime}}\nu_{-m}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\langle z-x\rangle^{-N+m}\langle\zeta-\xi\rangle^{-N^{\prime}+m}.

With the sufficient off-diagonal decay of the kernel above, we obtain the boundedness of
\mathcal{L}
by Young’s inequality for integral operators

\|\mathcal{L}u\|_{M^{m-s,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|\nu_{m}\mathcal{T}^{A}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}=\|u\|_{M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

∎

5.
Magnetic Phase Space Flow

The magnetic Weyl quantization
h\mapsto\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
gives a correspondence between the classical Hamiltonian
h
and the quantum observable
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
. In the spirit of the correspondence principle, it is natural to relate the quantum evolution
u_{0}\mapsto u(t)
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to a classical phase flow on the phase space
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. Since our choice of quantization depends intrinsically on the magnetic field
B
, the corresponding flow should also depends on
B
instead of the vector potential
A
. This is achieved by equipping the phase space
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the twisted symplectic form
\omega_{B}
, given by

\omega_{B(x)}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}dx_{j}\wedge d\xi_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k}B_{jk}(x)dx_{j}\wedge dx_{k},

which then gives rise to the associated Poisson bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{B}

\{f,g\}_{B}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{\xi_{j}}f\partial_{x_{j}}g-\partial_{x_{j}}f\partial_{\xi_{j}}g\right)+\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}B_{jk}(\cdot)\partial_{\xi_{j}}f\partial_{\xi_{k}}g.

With the magnetic Poisson bracket above, we define the magnetic Hamiltonian flow induced by a time-dependent Hamiltonian
h
.

Definition 5.1
.

For a given magnetic field
B
, we define
\chi_{B}(h)(t,s):(x^{s},\xi^{s})\mapsto(x^{t},\xi^{t})
the magnetic Hamiltonian flow induced by the Hamiltonian
h\in C(\mathbb{R},C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}))
, satisfying the equations

(21)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{x^{t}}_{j}=\{h(t),x^{t}_{j}\}_{B},\\
\dot{\xi^{t}}_{j}=\{h(t),\xi_{j}^{t}\}_{B}.\end{cases}

By definition of the Poisson bracket, the flow
(x^{t},\xi^{t})
satisfies more explicitly

(22)

\dot{x^{t}_{j}}=\partial_{\xi_{j}}h(t;x^{t},\xi^{t}),\quad\dot{\xi^{t}_{j}}=-\partial_{x_{j}}h(t;x^{t},\xi^{t})+\sum_{k=1}^{d}B_{kj}(x^{t})\partial_{\xi_{k}}h(t;x^{t},\xi^{t}).

In the following, we focus on such magnetic flows associated to symbols
h
satisfying
h(t)\in S^{0,(2)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume
h\in C(\mathbb{R},C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}))
with
h(t)\in S^{0,(2)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
uniformly in
t
, and let the magnetic field
B
satisfy
\|B_{jk}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{B}
. Then the flow
\chi_{B}(h)
exists globally in time and
\chi_{B}(h)(t,s)
is volume-preserving for all
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

For this lemma, the
t
-dependence of
h(t)
does not play a role, because we only need the bounds for the
x,\xi
derivatives of
h
, which by our assumption are uniform in
t
. We thus suppress the notation and just take
h(x,\xi):=h(t;x,\xi)
. For the existence, we firstly note that the vector field in (
22
) associated to
h\in S^{0,(2)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})

\Phi_{h}:=\left(\partial_{\xi_{1}}h,\dots,\partial_{\xi_{d}}h,-\partial_{x_{1}}h+\sum_{k}B_{k1}(x)\partial_{\xi_{k}}h,\dots,-\partial_{x_{d}}h+\sum_{k}B_{kd}(x)\partial_{\xi_{k}}h\right)

is smooth and thus locally Lipschitz, so
\chi_{B}(h)(t,s)
exists locally in time. To extend it globally, we rule out finite time blow-up by showing, for
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
with
s<t
, we can take
T>0
such that
t-s<T
and bound

(23)

1+|x^{t}|+|\xi^{t}|\leq C_{T}(1+|x^{s}|+|\xi^{s}|).

By the boundedness of the second-order derivatives of
h\in S^{0,(2)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, we can bound its first-order derivatives by

|\partial_{x}h(x,\xi)|+|\partial_{\xi}h(x,\xi)|\leq C_{h}(1+|x|+|\xi|).

Take
C_{B}\geq 1
such that
|B(x)|\leq C_{B}
, and denote
C_{h,B}:=C_{h}C_{B}\geq C_{h}
. The above bounds for the derivatives of
h
and the expression (
22
) for
\dot{x^{t}}
and
\dot{\xi^{t}}
imply

(24)

|\dot{x^{t}}|\leq C_{h}(1+|x^{t}|+|\xi^{t}|),\qquad|\dot{\xi^{t}}|\leq C_{h,B}(1+|x^{t}|+|\xi^{t}|).

We then calculate

(25)

\begin{gathered}\dfrac{d|x^{t}|}{dt}=\dfrac{\dot{x^{t}}\cdot x^{t}}{|x^{t}|}\leq|\dot{x^{t}}|\leq C_{h}(1+|x^{t}|+|\xi^{t}|),\\
\dfrac{d|\xi^{t}|}{dt}=\dfrac{\dot{\xi^{t}}\cdot\xi^{t}}{|\xi^{t}|}\leq|\dot{\xi^{t}}|\leq C_{h,B}(1+|x^{t}|+|\xi^{t}|).\end{gathered}

Combining the above bounds, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{d(1+|x^{t}|+|\xi^{t}|)}{dt}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{h,B}(1+|x^{t}|+|\xi^{t}|).

By Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain the desired upper bound (
23
)

(26)

1+|x^{t}|+|\xi^{t}|\leq e^{2TC_{h,B}}(1+|x^{s}|+|\xi^{s}|).

Moreover, since the backward-in-time flow
(x^{t},\xi^{t})\mapsto(x^{s},\xi^{s})
can be seen as a flow driven by the vector field
-\Phi_{h}
, a similar argument as above also gives a reversed bound

(27)

1+|x^{s}|+|\xi^{s}|\leq e^{2TC_{h,B}}(1+|x^{t}|+|\xi^{t}|).

For the volume-preserving property, we use the fact that a flow generated by a divergence-free vector field is volume-preserving, see Proposition 16.33 in
[
12
]
. For the vector field
\Phi_{h}
we can compute

\operatorname{div}\Phi_{h}=\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{j}}\partial_{\xi_{k}}h-\partial_{\xi_{j}}\partial_{x_{k}}h+B_{kj}(x)\partial_{\xi_{j}}\partial_{\xi_{k}}h=0,

so the flow
\chi_{B}(h)
is volume-preserving.
∎

The following lemma shows that the magnitude of the position and momentum vectors of certain magnetic flows are comparable in a time-averaged sense. The lemma is inspired by
[
18
, Lemma 2.1]
, and is one of the crucial technical ingredients for dealing with unbounded magnetic potentials.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\chi_{B}(h)
be the magnetic Hamiltonian flow associated to a symbol
h
satisfying Assumption
1.3
and a magnetic field
B
satisfying
\|B_{jk}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{B}
. Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval and fix an arbitrary
\epsilon>0
. We have for
(x^{t},\xi^{t}),t\in I
, satisfying the flow
\chi_{B}(h)
in (
21
),

(28)

\int_{I}\langle x^{\tau}\rangle^{-1-\epsilon}|\xi^{\tau}|\ d\tau\leq C_{h,B,\epsilon}(1+|I|),\qquad(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

Proof.

We prove this in a similar way as
[
18
, Lemma 2.1]
. Again the
t
-dependence of
h(t)
only makes a difference at the very end of the proof, so we suppress the notation for now. By Assumption
1.3
,
h\in S^{0,(2)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and is second-order in
\xi
. We thus have a lower bound for its
\xi
-derivative, that is, there exist
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d},C\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(29)

\displaystyle|\partial_{\xi}h(x,\xi)|\geq\big||\xi|-|b\cdot x+C|\big|.

We denote a new constant
C_{h,B}
based on
C_{h}
,
C_{B}
and
|b|
by

C_{h,B}:=\max(|b|,C_{h}C_{B}).

Without loss of generality, we can assume the constant
C_{h,B}\geq 1
, since otherwise the arguments below hold by replacing
C_{h,B}
everywhere with
1
. Sub-divide the interval
I=\sum_{j}I_{j}
into compact intervals with length
|I_{j}|=T
, such that
T
is sufficiently small satisfying

(30)

200T^{2}C_{h,B}^{3}+60TC_{h,B}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}.

It suffices to prove (
28
) separately for each
I_{j}
. We fix
I_{j}
and suppress the index
j
, denoting it again by
I
. We also fix the starting point of the flow as
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Firstly, for the case of
TC_{h,B}|\xi^{t}|\leq 1+|x^{t}|
for all
t\in I
, the integral is bounded by

(31)

\int_{I}\langle x^{\tau}\rangle^{-1-\epsilon}|\xi^{\tau}|\ d\tau\leq(TC_{h,B})^{-1}\int_{I}\langle x^{\tau}\rangle^{-1-\epsilon}(1+|x^{\tau}|)\ d\tau\leq C_{h,B}^{-1}.

For the other case, there exists
r\in I
such that
TC_{h,B}|\xi^{r}|>1+|x^{r}|
. We claim the following bounds hold for all
t\in I
:

(32)

1+|x^{t}|<10TC_{h,B}|\xi^{r}|,\quad\tfrac{2}{3}|\xi^{r}|\leq|\xi^{t}|\leq\tfrac{3}{2}|\xi^{r}|,\quad t\in I.

We can in fact prove a stronger claim, that the above bounds are satisfied for all
t
with
|t-r|\leq T
. This claim can be verified by contradiction. Denote
T^{*}:=\sup\{\sigma\leq T:\eqref{eq:t growth}\text{ is satisfied for }|t-r|\leq\sigma\}
and suppose
T^{*}<T
. For
t
with
|t-r|\leq T^{*}
, we can bound
1+|x^{t}|
by

1+|x^{t}|\leq 1+|x^{r}|+T^{*}\sup_{\tau\in I}\left|\frac{d|x^{\tau}|}{d\tau}\right|.

Then by
TC_{h,B}|\xi^{r}|>1+|x^{r}|
, the bound for
\frac{d|x^{t}|}{dt}
in (
25
) and the bound (
26
), we obtain

\displaystyle 1+|x^{t}|

\displaystyle\leq 1+|x^{r}|+T^{*}C_{h,B}e^{2TC_{h,B}}(1+|x^{r}|+|\xi^{r}|)

\displaystyle<TC_{h,B}|\xi^{r}|+3TC_{h,B}(TC_{h,B}|\xi^{r}|+|\xi^{r}|)

\displaystyle=4TC_{h,B}|\xi^{r}|+3T^{2}C_{h,B}^{2}|\xi^{r}|

\displaystyle<9TC_{h,B}|\xi^{r}|,

where we used
e^{2TC_{h,B}}<3
because by the smallness assumption (
30
) of
T
we have
2TC_{h,B}<1
. Similarly by the bound for
\frac{d|\xi^{t}|}{dt}
in (
25
) and the above, we have

\displaystyle|\xi^{t}|

\displaystyle\leq|\xi^{r}|+T^{*}C_{h,B}e^{2TC_{h,B}}(1+|x^{r}|+|\xi^{r}|)

\displaystyle<|\xi^{r}|+(3T^{2}C_{h,B}^{2}+3TC_{h,B})|\xi^{r}|

\displaystyle<\tfrac{11}{10}|\xi^{r}|,

where the smallness of
T
as in (
30
) is also used to obtain the last inequality. The lower bound of
|\xi^{t}|
holds analogously, that is

|\xi^{t}|>|\xi^{r}|-(3T^{2}C_{h,B}^{2}+3TC_{h,B})|\xi^{r}|\geq\tfrac{9}{10}|\xi^{r}|.

Because the flow
(x^{t},\xi^{t})
is continuous in
t
, such
T^{*}
cannot be maximal, thus contradicting its definition.

Now for the constant
C
in (
29
), we distinguish two cases based on the size of
|\xi^{r}|
. Firstly for the case that
|\xi^{r}|\leq 10|C|
, we directly have a desired bound

\int_{I}\langle x^{\tau}\rangle^{-1-\epsilon}|\xi^{\tau}|\ d\tau\leq\int_{I}|\xi^{r}|\ d\tau\leq 10|C|T.

For the other case
|\xi^{r}|>10|C|
, using (
29
), (
25
) and (
32
) we can calculate a lower bound for the second-order
t
-derivative of
|x^{t}|
. Here we will be taking
t
-derivatives on
h(t)
, so the
t
-dependence makes a difference, but things are under control since by our Assumption
1.3
, we have
\dot{h}(t)\in S^{0,(2)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
uniformly in
t
. In particular, the assumption implies

|\partial_{\xi}\dot{h}(t;x^{t},\xi^{t})|\leq C_{h}(1+|x^{t}|+|\xi^{t}|).

Using bounds (
24
) and (
32
), we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{d^{2}|x^{t}|^{2}}{dt^{2}}

\displaystyle=2|\partial_{\xi}h(t;x^{t},\xi^{t})|^{2}+2\dfrac{d\partial_{\xi}h(t;x^{t},\xi^{t})}{dt}\cdot x^{t}

\displaystyle=2|\partial_{\xi}h(t;x^{t},\xi^{t})|^{2}+2\left(\partial_{\xi}\dot{h}(t;x^{t},\xi^{t})+\partial_{x}\partial_{\xi}h(t;x^{t},\xi^{t})\dot{x}^{t}+\partial_{\xi}^{2}h(t;x^{t},\xi^{t})\dot{\xi}^{t}\right)\cdot x^{t}

\displaystyle\geq 2(\tfrac{9}{10}|\xi^{t}|-|b\cdot x^{t}|)^{2}-2C_{h,B}(1+|x^{t}|+|\xi^{t}|)|x^{t}|

\displaystyle\geq 2(\tfrac{3}{5}|\xi^{r}|-10TC_{h,B}^{2}|\xi^{r}|)^{2}-2C_{h,B}(10TC_{h,B}|\xi^{r}|+\tfrac{3}{2}|\xi^{r}|)10TC_{h,B}|\xi^{r}|

\displaystyle\geq\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-(200T^{2}C_{h,B}^{3}+60TC_{h,B}^{2})\right)|\xi^{r}|^{2},

so by the smallness of
T
as in (
30
), we have
\frac{d^{2}|x^{t}|^{2}}{dt^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{4}|\xi^{r}|^{2}
. Then we show for
\tau\in I
,
|x^{\tau}|
can be bounded from below. Suppose at a time
\rho\in I
,

attains the minimum value
|x^{\rho}|=\inf_{t\in I}|x^{t}|
. Then by a Taylor expansion of
|x^{\tau}|^{2}
in the
t
-variable at

, we have

\displaystyle|x^{\tau}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|x^{\rho}|^{2}+\tfrac{d|x^{t}|^{2}}{dt}\Big|_{t=\rho}(\tau-\rho)+\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{d^{2}|x^{t}|^{2}}{dt^{2}}\Big|_{t=\theta}(\tau-\rho)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq|x^{\rho}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{8}|\xi^{r}|^{2}(\tau-\rho)^{2},

where in the last inequality we used the fact that
|x^{\rho}|^{2}
is a minimum, so
\tfrac{d|x^{t}|^{2}}{dt}\Big|_{t=\rho}(\tau-\rho)\geq 0
. Then we have the desired bound for the integral

\displaystyle\int_{I}\langle x^{\tau}\rangle^{-1-\epsilon}|\xi^{\tau}|\ d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I}(1+|x^{\tau}|)^{-1-\epsilon}|\xi^{\tau}|\ d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I}(1+|\xi^{r}||\tau-\rho|)^{-1-\epsilon}|\xi^{r}|\ d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\epsilon^{-1},

where we used the lower bound of
|x^{\tau}|
and the fact that
|\xi^{\tau}|,|\xi^{\rho}|
are comparable to

by (
32
). Combining this with the bound (

) for the other case, we obtain (
28
).
∎

6.
Phase space approximation

The evolution
u_{0}\mapsto u(t)
satisfying (
9
) can be seen as a quantum evolution associated to the operator
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
. Our goal in this section is to approximate such an evolution with a classical evolution associated to the Hamiltonian
h
in the phase space. This is in some sense a manifestation of the quantum-classical correspondence principle, and the relevant classical evolution turns out to follow a magnetic Hamiltonian flow
\chi_{B}(h)
in phase space. So by taking a phase space perspective using the wavepacket transform
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A}
, we can obtain an approximate evolution in phase space that is more transparent and tractable. This change of perspective reveals that to some extent, the quantum evolution
u_{0}\mapsto u(t)
preserves phase space localizations in the sense of magnetic wavepackets, which leads to the magnetic modulation space boundedness of
u_{0}\mapsto u(t)
.

6.1.
Conjugating magnetic
\Psi
DOs

The first step in approximating the generalized magnetic Schrödinger evolution in phase space is to approximate the effect of the operator
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
in phase space.
That is, we need to understand how the conjugated operator
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A}\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A*}
behaves on phase space functions. For real symbols
h
, the operator
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
is symmetric for the pairing of
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
g_{x,\xi}^{A,\lambda}
,

\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}^{A}\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)u(x,\xi)=\left\langle g_{x,\xi}^{A,\lambda},\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)u\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=\left\langle\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)g_{x,\xi}^{A,\lambda},u\right\rangle_{L^{2}},

so it boils down to determining an approximation of the term
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)g_{x,\xi}^{A,\lambda}
in phase space. The following proposition relates differentiation and multiplication on the configuration space and the phase space.

Lemma 6.1
.

For magnetic wavepackets
g_{x,\xi}^{A,\lambda}(y)
, multiplication and differentiation with respect to
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be approximated by multiplication and differentiation with respect to
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, that is,

(33)

\displaystyle-i\partial_{y_{j}}g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

\displaystyle=i\partial_{x_{j}}g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)-\sum_{k=1}^{d}B_{jk}(x)i\partial_{\xi_{k}}g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

\displaystyle+[A_{j}(y)-A_{j}(x)-r^{(1)}_{x}(A_{j}(y,x))+r^{(1)}_{x}(A_{j}(x,y))]g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y),

where
r^{(1)}_{x}
denotes the remainder of the first-order Taylor expansion of
y
at
x
.

Proof.

By property (
12
) and (
14
) of
\varphi^{A}(y,x)
, we have

\begin{gathered}\partial_{y_{j}}e^{i\varphi^{A}(y,x)}=i(A_{j}(y,0)-A_{j}(y,x))e^{i\varphi^{A}(y,x)},\\
\partial_{x_{j}}e^{i\varphi^{A}(y,x)}=i(-A_{j}(x,0)+A_{j}(x,y))e^{i\varphi^{A}(y,x)}.\end{gathered}

A direct computation gives

-i\partial_{y_{j}}g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)=i\partial_{x_{j}}g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)+[A_{j}(y)-A_{j}(x)-A_{j}(y,x)+A_{j}(x,y)]g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y).

Next, we use Taylor’s formula to expand the term
-A_{j}(y,x)+A_{j}(x,y)
with respect to the
y
variable at the value
x
. Recall the definition of the potential

A_{j}(y,x):=-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{1}s\left(y_{k}-x_{k}\right)B_{jk}(x+s(y-x))ds,

so evaluating
A_{j}
at
y=x
we have
A_{j}(x,x)=0
. The first-order derivatives of
A_{j}
are

\displaystyle\partial_{y_{l}}A_{j}(y,x)=-\int^{1}_{0}sB_{jl}(x+s(y-x))ds-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{1}s^{2}\left(y_{k}-x_{k}\right)\partial_{l}B_{jk}(x+s(y-x))ds.

The derivatives at
y=x
evaluate to

(34)

\partial_{y_{l}}A_{j}(x,x)=-\int^{1}_{0}sB_{jl}(x)ds=-\frac{1}{2}B_{jl}(x).

Further, the second-order derivatives are, for
1\leq m,l\leq d
,

(35)

\displaystyle\partial_{y_{m}}\partial_{y_{l}}A_{j}(y,x)

\displaystyle=-\int^{1}_{0}s^{2}\partial_{m}B_{jl}(x+s(y-x))ds-\int_{0}^{1}s^{2}\partial_{l}B_{jm}(x+s(y-x))ds

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{1}s^{3}\left(y_{k}-x_{k}\right)\partial_{m}\partial_{l}B_{jk}(x+s(y-x))ds,

so by (
34
) and (
35
), Taylor’s formula of
A_{j}
takes the form

A_{j}(y,x)=-\sum^{d}_{l=1}\frac{1}{2}B_{jl}(x)(y_{l}-x_{l})+r^{(1)}_{x}(A_{j}(y,x)),

where, for some
\theta\in(0,1)
, the remainder can be expressed by

(36)

\displaystyle r^{(1)}_{x}(A_{j}(y,x))

\displaystyle:=\sum_{m,l=1}^{d}(y_{m}-x_{m})(y_{l}-x_{l})\partial_{y_{m}}\partial_{y_{l}}A_{j}(x+\theta(y-x),x).

Expanding also
A_{j}(x,y)
and combining the terms, we obtain

-A_{j}(y,x)+A_{j}(x,y)=\sum_{l=1}^{d}B_{jl}(x)(y_{l}-x_{l})-r^{(1)}_{x}(A_{j}(y,x))+r^{(1)}_{x}(A_{j}(x,y)).

The lemma then follows from the identity
\partial_{\xi_{j}}g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)=i(y_{j}-x_{j})g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)
.
∎

We build upon the above lemma to show that the action of
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
on wavepackets
g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}
can be approximated, up to some error terms, as a phase space differentiation along the magnetic Hamiltonian vector field associated to
h
. The lemma below can hold for more general
h
, but for simplicity we formulate it for the most relevant symbol class
S^{0,(2)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
h\in S^{0,(2)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. The magnetic pseudo-differentiation
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
on wavepackets
g_{x,\xi}^{A,\lambda}
can be approximated by a phase space differentiation
\tilde{H}^{B}
. That is,

(37)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)g_{x,\xi}^{A,\lambda}(y)=\left(i\tilde{H}^{B}+\mathcal{R}\right)g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y),\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where the components of
\tilde{H}^{B}
are

\tilde{H}^{B}_{j}:=\left(\partial_{\xi_{j}}h\partial_{x_{j}},-\partial_{x_{j}}h\partial_{\xi_{j}}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}B_{kj}(x)\partial_{\xi_{k}}h\partial_{\xi_{j}}\right),\quad 1\leq j\leq d.

The approximation error
\mathcal{R}
term consists of the three parts

\mathcal{R}=m^{A}(h)+\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2},

as defined in (
43
), (
44
), and (
45
), where
m^{A}(h)
denotes a multiplier that only depends on the phase space variables
x,\xi
, while
\mathcal{R}_{1}
,
\mathcal{R}_{2}
denotes respectively a multiplicative operator and a magnetic pseudo-differential operator on the
y
variable.

Proof.

For convenience of later proofs, we first convert to the magnetic Kohn-Nirenberg quantization
\operatorname{Op}_{KN}^{A}(h^{*})=\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
. By Lemma
3.2
, the symbol
h^{*}\in S^{0,(2)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is given by

(38)

h^{*}(y,\eta):=h(y,\eta)+h_{r}(y,\eta),\quad h_{r}\in S^{0,(0)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),

and we can represent
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
using the Kohn-Nirenberg quantization

\operatorname{Op}^{A}\left(h\right)g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)=\left(\operatorname{Op}^{A}_{KN}\left(h\right)+\operatorname{Op}^{A}_{KN}\left(h_{r}\right)\right)g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y).

The
\operatorname{Op}^{A}_{KN}\left(h_{r}\right)
will be included in the pseudo-differential remainder
\mathcal{R}_{2}
, since its symbol
h_{r}
is nicely bounded. Our focus here is to approximate
\operatorname{Op}^{A}_{KN}\left(h\right)
, and we start with a Taylor expansion on
h
at the point
(x,\xi)
, that is,

(39)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}^{A}_{KN}(h)g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Op}_{KN}^{A}\left[h(x,\xi)+\partial_{y}h(x,\xi)\cdot(y-x)+\partial_{\eta}h(x,\xi)\cdot(\eta-\xi)+r^{(1)}_{x,\xi}(h)(y,\eta)\right]g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Op}_{KN}^{A}\left[\partial_{y}h(x,\xi)\cdot(y-x)+\partial_{\eta}h(x,\xi)\cdot\eta+r^{(1)}_{x,\xi}(h)(y,\eta)\right]g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

\displaystyle+(h(x,\xi)-\partial_{\eta}h(x,\xi)\cdot\xi)g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y).

Here
r^{(1)}_{x,\xi}(h)
denotes the remainder term from the first-order expansion:

(40)

\displaystyle r^{(1)}_{x,\xi}(h)(y,\eta)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\partial_{s}^{2}h(x+s(y-x),\xi+s(\eta-\xi))\ ds.

By the basic quantizations (
18
) and the identity (
33
) in Lemma
6.1
, we can calculate two magnetic
\Psi
DO terms on the right hand side of (
39
) as

(41)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}_{KN}^{A}\left[\partial_{y}h(x,\xi)\cdot(y-x)\right]g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

\displaystyle=\partial_{y}h(x,\xi)\cdot(y-x)g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

\displaystyle=-i\partial_{y}h(x,\xi)\cdot\partial_{\xi}g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y),

and

(42)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}_{KN}^{A}\left[\partial_{\eta}h(x,\xi)\cdot\eta\right]g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\eta}h(x,\xi)\cdot(-i\partial_{y}-A(y))g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{\eta_{j}}h(x,\xi)\left(i\partial_{x_{j}}g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)-\sum_{k=1}^{d}B_{jk}(x)i\partial_{\xi_{k}}g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)\right)+

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{\eta_{j}}h(x,\xi)\left(-A_{j}(x)-r^{(1)}_{x}(A_{j}(y,x))+r^{(1)}_{x}(A_{j}(x,y))\right)g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y).

Then substituting (
41
), (
42
) into (
39
) yields

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

\displaystyle=i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{\eta_{j}}h(x,\xi)\partial_{x_{j}}g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)-i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{y_{j}}h(x,\xi)-\sum_{k=1}^{d}B_{kj}(x)\partial_{\eta_{k}}h(x,\xi)\right)\partial_{\xi_{j}}g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)+

\displaystyle+\left(m^{A}(h)+\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2}\right)g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

with a multiplicative factor
m^{A}(h)
which only depends on the variable
x,\xi

(43)

\displaystyle m^{A}(h)(x,\xi):=h(x,\xi)-\partial_{\eta}h(x,\xi)\cdot(\xi+A(x)),

and two other error terms involving the
y
variable, including one multiplicative
\mathcal{R}_{1}
,

(44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

\displaystyle:=\partial_{\eta}h(x,\xi)\cdot\left(-r^{(1)}_{x}(A(y,x))+r^{(1)}_{x}(A(x,y))\right)g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

and one magnetic pseudo-differential
\mathcal{R}_{2}
,

(45)

\mathcal{R}_{2}g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y):=\operatorname{Op}_{KN}^{A}\left(r^{(1)}_{x,\xi}(h)+h_{r}\right)g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y).

∎

We observe that in the above lemma, the phase space vector field
\tilde{H}^{B}
coincides with the Hamiltonian vector field of the magnetic flow
\chi_{B}(h)
in (
22
). This makes it possible for us to relate the quantum evolution associated to
\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)
with the classical flow
\chi_{B}(h)
associated to
h
.

6.2.
Propagation along the phase space flow

Based on the representation (
37
) in Lemma
6.2
, we see that if a time-dependent wavepacket
w
satisfies

D_{t}w(t;x,\xi)+(i\tilde{H}^{B}+m^{A}(h)+\mathcal{R})w(t;x,\xi)=0,\quad w(t;x,\xi)=g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi},

on the phase space
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, then it will also satisfy

(D_{t}+\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h))w(t,x,\xi;y)=0

on the configuration space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
A solution
u(t)
to (
9
) can then be constructed as a superposition of such wavepackets
w
with the initial data
\tilde{u}_{0}:=\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda}u_{0}
, that is

u(t;y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}w(t,y;x,\xi)\tilde{u}_{0}(x,\xi)\ dxd\xi.

However, the existence of such
w
is unclear due to the complicated error term
\mathcal{R}w
. We instead first show the existence of a wavepacket solution to a simpler equation.

Lemma 6.3
.

For
h
satisfying Assumption
1.3
, with associated magnetic Hamiltonian vector field
\tilde{H}^{B}
and
m^{A}(h)
defined in (
43
), there exists a wavepacket
w\in C(\mathbb{R},C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}))
, which satisfies the equation

\begin{cases}D_{t}w(t;x,\xi)+(i\tilde{H}^{B}+m^{A}(h))w(t;x,\xi)=0,\\
w(0;x,\xi)=g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2
, the magnetic Hamiltonian flow
\chi_{B}(h)(t,0):(x,\xi)\mapsto(x^{t},\xi^{t})
associated to
h
exists globally, and we can construct a time-dependent wavepacket by setting

g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y):=\chi_{B}(h)(t,0)g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then this wavepacket
g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}
satisfies

\partial_{t}g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y)=\tilde{H}^{B}g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y),\quad g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}\big|_{t=0}(y)=g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y).

We can also construct a phase term
e^{i\psi(t)}
, with a phase function
\psi
based on
m^{A}(h)
, by

\psi(t;x,\xi):=\int_{0}^{t}-m^{A}(h)(\tau;x^{\tau},\xi^{\tau})\ d\tau,

so that
\psi(t)
satisfies

\partial_{t}\psi(t)=-m^{A}(h)(t;x^{t},\xi^{t}),\quad e^{i\psi(t)}\big|_{t=0}=1.

We modulate the wavepacket with the phase term
e^{i\psi(t)}
and define

g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}(t;y,x,\xi):=e^{i\psi(t)}g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y).

The modulated wavepacket
g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}
now satisfies

(46)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}(t;y,x,\xi)

\displaystyle=i\partial_{t}\psi(t)e^{i\psi(t)}g^{A}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y)+e^{i\psi(t)}\partial_{t}g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y)

\displaystyle=-im^{A}(h)e^{i\psi(t)}g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y)+e^{i\psi(t)}\tilde{H}^{B}g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y)

\displaystyle=\left(-im^{A}(h)+\tilde{H}^{B}\right)g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}(t;y,x,\xi),

with
g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}(0;y,x,\xi)=g^{A,\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)
as desired.
∎

Using the wavepacket solution
g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}(t)
from the above lemma, we can construct a parametrix to equation (
9
).

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
0\leq s,t\leq T
. We define the operator
\tilde{S}(t,s)
by

(47)

\tilde{S}(t,s)u_{0}(y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}(t;y,x,\xi)\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}}(s;y^{\prime},x,\xi)u_{0}(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}dxd\xi,

for
u_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We also define the operator
K(t,s)
by

(48)

K(t,s)u_{0}(y):=\int(\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2})g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}(t;y,x,\xi)\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}}(s;y^{\prime},x,\xi)u_{0}(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}dxd\xi,

for
u_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We then have

(49)

(D_{t}+\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h))\tilde{S}(t,s)u_{0}=K(t,s)u_{0},\quad u_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Moreover, for
1\leq p\leq\infty,m\in\mathbb{R}
and fixed
t,s
, the operator
\tilde{S}(t,s)
is bounded on
M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The operators
\tilde{S}(t,\cdot)
and
\tilde{S}(\cdot,s)
are also strongly continuous on
M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

By construction of
\tilde{S}
based on the wavepacket solution
g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}(t)
, we can calculate using Lemma
6.2
and (
46
)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h)g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}(t;y,x,\xi)

\displaystyle=e^{i\psi(t)}(i\tilde{H}^{B}+\mathcal{R})g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y)

\displaystyle=(i\tilde{H}^{B}+m^{A}(h)+\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2})g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}(t;y,x,\xi),

and

D_{t}(g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi})(t;y,x,\xi)=-(m^{A}(h)+i\tilde{H}^{B})g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}(t;y,x,\xi).

Then the equation (
49
) can be verified based on the above calculations

\displaystyle(D_{t}+\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h))\tilde{S}(t,s)u_{0}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(D_{t}+\operatorname{Op}^{A}(h))(g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi})(t;y,x,\xi)\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}}(s;y^{\prime},x,\xi)u_{0}(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}dxd\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2})(g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi})(t;y,x,\xi)\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}}(s;y^{\prime},x,\xi)u_{0}(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}dxd\xi

\displaystyle=K(t,s)u_{0}(y).

For the
M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
boundedness of
\tilde{S}(t,s)
, in view of Lemma
4.9
, it suffices to show the phase space kernel of
\tilde{S}(t,s)
has sufficient off-diagonal decay of the form
\langle\lambda(z-x^{t})\rangle^{-N}\langle\lambda^{-1}(\zeta-\xi^{t})\rangle^{-N^{\prime}}
. Note that by definition the
M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
norm of
\tilde{S}(t,s)u_{0}
equals the weighted
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
norm on the phase space

\displaystyle\|\tilde{S}(t,s)u_{0}\|_{M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\|\nu_{\lambda,m}\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}(t,s)u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}.

We fully write out the wavepacket transform
\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}(t,s)u_{0}
to see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}(t,s)u_{0}(z,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{z,\zeta}}(y)g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y)\ dy\left(e^{i\psi(t)-i\psi(s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{s},\xi^{s}}}(y^{\prime})u_{0}(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}\right)\ dxd\xi.

Denote by
G^{A,\lambda}
the integral kernel

(50)

G^{A,\lambda}(z,\zeta,x^{t},\xi^{t}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{z,\zeta}}(y)g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y)\ dy.

Then
\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}(t,s)u_{0}
can be seen as associated to the phase space kernel
G^{A,\lambda}

\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}(t,s)u_{0}(z,\zeta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}G^{A,\lambda}(z,\zeta,x^{t},\xi^{t})e^{i\psi(t)-i\psi(s)}\tilde{u}_{0}(x^{s},\xi^{s})\ dxd\xi,

We can then write out the weighted version as

\displaystyle\nu_{\lambda,m}(z,\zeta)\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}(t,s)u_{0}(z,\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nu_{\lambda,m}(z,\zeta)G^{A,\lambda}(z,\zeta,x^{t},\xi^{t})\nu_{\lambda,-m}(x^{s},\xi^{s})e^{i\psi(t)-i\psi(s)}\nu_{\lambda,m}(x^{s},\xi^{s})\tilde{u}_{0}(x^{s},\xi^{s})\ dxd\xi,

and note the weighted phase space kernel is related to the unweighted
G^{A,\lambda}
by

(51)

\displaystyle|\nu_{\lambda,m}(z,\zeta)G^{A,\lambda}(z,\zeta,x^{t},\xi^{t})\nu_{\lambda,-m}(x^{s},\xi^{s})|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\langle\lambda^{-1}(z-x^{t})\rangle^{m}\langle\lambda^{-1}(\zeta-\xi^{t})\rangle^{m}\nu_{\lambda,m}(x^{t},\xi^{t})\nu_{\lambda,-m}(x^{s},\xi^{s})|G^{A,\lambda}(z,\zeta,x^{t},\xi^{t})|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle C_{T,h,B}^{m}\langle\lambda^{-1}(z-x^{t})\rangle^{m}\langle\lambda^{-1}(\zeta-\xi^{t})\rangle^{m}|G^{A,\lambda}(z,\zeta,x^{t},\xi^{t})|,

where we used the flow property (
26
) to bound
\nu_{\lambda,m}(x^{t},\xi^{t})\nu_{\lambda,-m}(x^{s},\xi^{s})\lesssim C_{T,h,B}^{m}
.

By the volume-preservation property in Lemma
5.2
, we can replace
dxd\xi
with
dx^{s}d\xi^{s}
, so that

\displaystyle\|\nu_{\lambda,m}e^{i\psi(t)-i\psi(s)}\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\nu_{\lambda,m}(x^{s},\xi^{s})e^{i\psi(t)-i\psi(s)}\tilde{u}_{0}(x^{s},\xi^{s})|^{p}\ dx^{s}d\xi^{s}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\|u_{0}\|_{M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

So by Young’s inequality for integral operators and the relation (
51
), the
M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
boundedness of
\tilde{S}(t,s)
will be implied by the off-diagonal decay of the kernel

(52)

|G^{A,\lambda}(z,\zeta,x^{t},\xi^{t})|\lesssim\langle\lambda(z-x^{t})\rangle^{-N}\langle\lambda^{-1}(\zeta-\xi^{t})\rangle^{-N^{\prime}},

for sufficiently large
N,N^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
.
To show the above bound, we notice that based on (
15
), the product of the two wavepackets
\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{z,\zeta}}
and
g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}
can be complemented with a phase factor
e^{i\varphi^{A}(z,x^{t})}
to make a factor
e^{i\Gamma^{B}}
appear. That is,

\displaystyle e^{-i\varphi^{A}(z,x^{t})}e^{-i\zeta\cdot(z-x^{t})}\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{z,\zeta}}(y)g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{d}e^{i(\xi^{t}-\zeta)\cdot(y-x^{t})}e^{-i\varphi^{A}(y,z)}e^{i\varphi^{A}(y,x^{t})}e^{-i\varphi^{A}(z,x^{t})}g(\lambda(y-z))g(\lambda(y-x^{t}))

\displaystyle=\lambda^{d}e^{i(\xi^{t}-\zeta)\cdot(y-x^{t})}e^{i\Gamma^{B}(y,x^{t},z)}g(\lambda(y-z))g(\lambda(y-x^{t})).

We can then make use of the derivative bounds (
2.1
) of
e^{i\Gamma^{B}}
, which yield for
\lambda\geq 1

|\partial_{\lambda y}^{\alpha}e^{i\Gamma^{B}}(y,x^{t},z)|\leq C_{B,\alpha}(\langle\lambda(y-x^{t})\rangle+\langle\lambda(y-z)\rangle)^{|\alpha|},\qquad|\alpha|\geq 1.

Denote part of the wavepacket product by

(53)

W_{B,\lambda}(y,x^{t},z):=e^{i\Gamma^{B}(y,x^{t},z)}g(\lambda(y-z))g(\lambda(y-x^{t})).

By the derivative bounds of
e^{i\Gamma^{B}}
above and
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we see that the partial derivatives of
W_{B,\lambda}
with respect to
\lambda y
can be bounded for all
N,N^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
by

|\partial_{\lambda y}^{\alpha}W_{B,\lambda}(y,x^{t},z)|\leq C_{N,N^{\prime},\alpha,B}\langle\lambda(y-z)\rangle^{-N}\langle\lambda(y-x^{t})\rangle^{-N^{\prime}}.

Using
\langle\lambda(y-z)\rangle^{-N}\lesssim\langle\lambda(y-x^{t})\rangle^{N}\langle\lambda(z-x^{t})\rangle^{-N}
, we further obtain for all
N,N^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{N}

(54)

\displaystyle|\partial_{\lambda y}^{\alpha}W_{B,\lambda}(y,x^{t},z)|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{N,N^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime},\alpha,B}\langle\lambda(z-x^{t})\rangle^{-N}\langle\lambda(y-z)\rangle^{-N^{\prime}}\langle\lambda(y-x^{t})\rangle^{-N^{\prime\prime}}.

We now re-write
G^{A,\lambda}
with
W_{B,\lambda}
as

(55)

\displaystyle G^{A,\lambda}(z,\zeta,x^{t},\xi^{t})

\displaystyle=e^{i\varphi^{A}(z,x^{t})}e^{i\zeta\cdot(z-x^{t})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i(\xi^{t}-\zeta)\cdot(y-x^{t})}W_{B,\lambda}(y,x^{t},z)\ d\lambda y,

and we can obtain a decay factor
\langle\lambda^{-1}(\zeta-\xi^{t})\rangle^{-N^{\prime}}
by integration by parts repeatedly with respect to the
\lambda y
-integral, relying on the identity

\langle\lambda^{-1}(\zeta-\xi^{t})\rangle^{-2}(1-\Delta_{\lambda y})e^{i\lambda^{-1}(\xi^{t}-\zeta)\cdot\lambda y}=e^{i(\xi^{t}-\zeta)\cdot y}.

Now combined with the
W_{B,\lambda}
bounds (
54
), we see by integration by parts

\langle\lambda(z-x^{t})\rangle^{N}\langle\lambda^{-1}(\zeta-\xi^{t})\rangle^{N^{\prime}}|G^{A,\lambda}(z,\zeta,x^{t},\xi^{t})|\lesssim C_{B,N,N^{\prime}}

as desired.

For the strong continuity of
\tilde{S}
, by the fact that
\tilde{S}(t,s)
is continuous on
M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for fixed
t,s
, it suffices to show the strong continuity on the dense subspace
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of
M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Here we show the strong continuity for
\tilde{S}(\cdot,s)
, and the strong continuity of
\tilde{S}(t,\cdot)
is analogous. Since the flow
\chi_{B}(t,s)
is continuous in
t
, and the wavepackets
g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}
are dominated by the Gaussian
g
, that is

|g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}(\tau;y,x,\xi)|\leq|g(\lambda(y-x))|,

we can use the dominated convergence theorem to see at the limit
t\to s
, we recover the inversion formula in Proposition
4.5
. That is,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to s}\tilde{S}(t,s)u_{0}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lim_{t\to s}g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}(t;y,x,\xi)\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{\psi}}(s;y^{\prime},x,\xi)u_{0}(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}dxd\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{s},\xi^{s}}(y)\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{s},\xi^{s}}}(y^{\prime})u_{0}(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}dx^{s}d\xi^{s}

\displaystyle=u_{0}(y),

so the strong continuity of
\tilde{S}
holds for all
u_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and by continuity of
\tilde{S}(t,s)
and density of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in
M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
it holds on the whole space
M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
∎

7.
Modulation space well-posedness

We show the well-posedness of the generalized magnetic Schrödinger equation (
9
) by constructing its propagator, based on the parametrix construction in equation (
49
) and Duhamel’s principle. For this we need the following definition of a new space and a lemma for the Volterra equation involving the operator
K
defined in the Proposition
6.4
.

Definition 7.1
.

Define the time-dependent wavepacket transform
\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda,t}
on
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\tilde{u}(t;x^{t},\xi^{t}):=\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda,t}u(t;x^{t},\xi^{t}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}}(y)u(t;y)dy.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
m\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the Banach spaces
\mathcal{M}^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))
characterized by the mixed
L^{p}
-norm of the transform
\tilde{u}
on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. That is, we set

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{M}^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))}

\displaystyle:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\nu_{\lambda,m}(x,\xi)\int_{[0,T]}\left|\tilde{u}(t;x^{t},\xi^{t})\right|dt\right)^{p}\ dxd\xi\right)^{1/p},

\displaystyle\quad 1\leq p<\infty,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{M}^{m,\infty}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))}

\displaystyle:=\underset{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}}{\operatorname{ess\ sup}}\ \nu_{\lambda,m}(x,\xi)\int_{[0,T]}\left|\tilde{u}(t;x^{t},\xi^{t})\right|dt,

\displaystyle\quad p=\infty,

and define for
1\leq p\leq\infty

\mathcal{M}^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T])):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\bigg|\ \|u\|_{\mathcal{M}^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))}<\infty\right\}.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
T>0
be sufficiently small. For every
u_{0}\in M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there exists a unique solution
v\in\mathcal{M}^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))
to the Volterra equation

(56)

v(t;y)=-K(t,0)u_{0}(y)-i\int_{0}^{t}K(t,s)v(s;y)\ ds,\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},t>0.

Moreover, for the solution
v
we have

(57)

\|v\|_{\mathcal{M}^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))}\lesssim C_{T,h,B}\|u_{0}\|_{M^{m,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
4.9
and using arguments regarding the weights
\nu_{\lambda,m}
similar to the ones given in the proof of Proposition
6.4
, it suffices to prove the unweighted version with
m=0
as long as the relevant phase space kernel has sufficient off-diagonal decay. In the following we show the unweighted case with the spaces
M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{M}^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T])):=\mathcal{M}^{0,p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))
,
from which the desired off-diagonal decay of the kernels will become apparent. The necessary modifications for the weighted case are outlined at the end of the proof.

By the Banach fixed point theorem, to show the unique existence of a solution to (
56
), it suffices to show that the mapping
\Psi_{u_{0}}
defined by

(58)

\Psi_{u_{0}}(v)(y):=-K(t,0)u_{0}(y)-i\int_{0}^{t}K(t,s)v(s;y)\ ds,\quad v\in\mathcal{M}^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T])),

is a contraction mapping on
\mathcal{M}^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))
.

We first show that
K(t,0)u_{0}
is in the space
\mathcal{M}^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))
, by showing
K(t,0)
is a bounded linear operator between
M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{M}^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))
, that is

(59)

\|K(\cdot,0)u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))}\leq C_{K}\|u_{0}\|_{M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad u_{0}\in M^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

The above bound can be obtain by interpolating the following bounds (cf.
[
17
, Section 1.18.7, Theorem 1]
)

(60)

\begin{gathered}\|K(\cdot,0)u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{M}^{1}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))}\leq C_{K}^{\prime}\|u_{0}\|_{M^{1}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\\
\|K(\cdot,0)u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\infty}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))}\leq C_{K}^{\prime\prime}\|u_{0}\|_{M^{\infty}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{gathered}

We begin with the bound for
K(t,0):M^{1}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{M}^{1}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))
. By definition of
K
in (
48
), we have

(61)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda,t}K(t,0)u_{0}(z^{t},\zeta^{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{z^{t},\zeta^{t}}}(y)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2})g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y)e^{i\psi(t)}\tilde{u}_{0}(x,\xi)\ dxd\xi\right)\ dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{z^{t},\zeta^{t}}}(y)(\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2})g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y)\ dy\right)e^{i\psi(t)}\tilde{u}_{0}(x,\xi)\ dxd\xi.

Denote the integral kernel above as
G_{K}
, with

(62)

G_{K}(t;x,\xi,z,\zeta):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{z^{t},\zeta^{t}}}(y)(\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2})g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y)\ dy.

We claim that
G_{K}
satisfies the bound

(63)

\displaystyle|G_{K}(t;x,\xi,z,\zeta)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{N,h,B}(\lambda^{C_{N}}+\lambda^{-2}\langle x^{t}\rangle^{-1-\epsilon}|\xi^{t}|)\langle\lambda(z^{t}-x^{t})\rangle^{-N}\langle\lambda^{-1}(\zeta^{t}-\xi^{t})\rangle^{-N},\quad\forall N\in\mathbb{N},

which we will prove in the next section as part of Lemma
7.3
. From this, we obtain

\displaystyle\|K(t,0)u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{M}^{1}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\int_{[0,T]}\left|\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda,t}K(t,0)u_{0}(z^{t},\zeta^{t})\right|dt\ dzd\zeta

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\int_{[0,T]}\bigg[\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\langle\lambda(z^{t}-x^{t})\rangle^{-d-1}\langle\lambda^{-1}(\zeta^{t}-\xi^{t})\rangle^{-d-1}\ dz^{t}d\zeta^{t}\right)\times

\displaystyle\times C_{d,h,B}(\lambda^{C_{d}}+\lambda^{-2}\langle x^{t}\rangle^{-1-\epsilon}|\xi^{t}|)|\tilde{u}_{0}(x,\xi)|\bigg]\ dtdxd\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\int_{[0,T]}C_{d,h,B}(\lambda^{C_{d}}+\lambda^{-2}\langle x^{t}\rangle^{-1-\epsilon}|\xi^{t}|)\ dt\right)|\tilde{u}_{0}(x,\xi)|\ dxd\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{d,h,B}(T\lambda^{C_{d}}+\lambda^{-2}C_{\epsilon}(1+T))\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\tilde{u}_{0}(x,\xi)|\ dxd\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{d,h,B,\epsilon}(T\lambda^{C_{d}}+\lambda^{-2})\|u_{0}\|_{M^{1}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where for the
t
-integral we used Lemma
5.3
.

For the bound
K(t,0):M^{\infty}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{M}^{\infty}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))
, we proceed similarly as above, except that we use the other bound for
G_{K}
from Lemma
7.3
. That is,

(64)

\displaystyle|G_{K}(t;x,\xi,z,\zeta)|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{N,h,B}(\lambda^{C_{N}}+\lambda^{-2}\langle z^{t}\rangle^{-1-\epsilon}|\zeta^{t}|)\langle\lambda(z^{t}-x^{t})\rangle^{-N}\langle\lambda^{-1}(\zeta^{t}-\xi^{t})\rangle^{-N},\quad\forall N\in\mathbb{N}.

With the above bound and Lemma
5.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|K(t,0)u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\infty}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))}

\displaystyle=\underset{(z,\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}}{\operatorname{ess\ sup}}\int_{[0,T]}\left|\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda,t}K(t,0)u_{0}(z^{t},\zeta^{t})\right|dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\underset{(z,\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}}{\operatorname{ess\ sup}}\ \int_{[0,T]}\bigg[C_{d,h,B}(\lambda^{C_{d}}+\lambda^{-2}\langle z^{t}\rangle^{-1-\epsilon}|\zeta^{t}|)\times

\displaystyle\left.\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\langle\lambda(z^{t}-x^{t})\rangle^{-d-1}\langle\lambda^{-1}(\zeta^{t}-\xi^{t})\rangle^{-d-1}|\tilde{u}_{0}(x,\xi)|\ dxd\xi\right]dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\underset{(z,\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}}{\operatorname{ess\ sup}}\ \int_{[0,T]}C_{d,h,B}(\lambda^{C_{d}}+\lambda^{-2}\langle z^{t}\rangle^{-1-\epsilon}|\zeta^{t}|)\ dt\times

\displaystyle\times\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\operatorname{ess\ sup}}\left(\underset{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}}{\operatorname{ess\ sup}}|\tilde{u}_{0}(x,\xi)|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\langle\lambda(z^{t}-x^{t})\rangle^{-d-1}\langle\lambda^{-1}(\zeta^{t}-\xi^{t})\rangle^{-d-1}\ dx^{t}d\xi^{t}\right).

We then again use Lemma
5.3
for the
t
-integral and notice that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\langle\lambda(z^{t}-x^{t})\rangle^{-d-1}\langle\lambda^{-1}(\zeta^{t}-\xi^{t})\rangle^{-d-1}\ dx^{t}d\xi^{t}\leq C_{d}

uniformly in
t
, which yields

\|K(t,0)u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\infty}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))}\lesssim C_{d,h,B,\epsilon}(T\lambda^{C_{d}}+\lambda^{-2})\|u\|_{M^{\infty}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

With
K(t,0)u_{0}\in\mathcal{M}^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))
, to show
\Psi_{u_{0}}
defined in (
58
) is a contraction mapping on
\mathcal{M}^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))
, it suffices to show the map
F:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined as

F(v)(t;y):=-\int_{0}^{t}K(t,s)v(s;y)\ ds

is a contraction mapping on
\mathcal{M}^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))
. Similar to the bound (
59
) of
K(t,0)
, the contraction bound

\|F(v)\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))}\leq C_{F}\|v\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))},\quad C_{F}\in(0,1)

can be obtain by interpolating the bounds

(65)

\begin{gathered}\|F(v)\|_{\mathcal{M}^{1}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))}\leq C_{F}^{\prime}\|v\|_{\mathcal{M}^{1}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))},\quad C_{F}^{\prime}\in(0,1),\\
\|F(v)\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\infty}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))}\leq C_{F}^{\prime\prime}\|v\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\infty}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L^{1}([0,T]))},\quad C_{F}^{\prime\prime}\in(0,1).\end{gathered}

Notice that for the relevant wavepacket transform
\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda,t}F(v)(t;z^{t},\zeta^{t})
, we can re-write again with the integral kernel
G_{K}
defined in (
62
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{A}_{\lambda,t}F(v)(t;z^{t},\zeta^{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{z^{t},\zeta^{t}}}(y)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2})g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y)\left(e^{i\psi(t)-i\psi(s)}\tilde{v}(s;x^{s},\xi^{s})\right)\ dxd\xi\right)\ dsdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{z^{t},\zeta^{t}}}(y)(\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2})g^{A,\lambda}_{x^{t},\xi^{t}}(y)\ dy\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\psi(t)-i\psi(s)}\tilde{v}(s;x^{s},\xi^{s})\ ds\right)\ dxd\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}G_{K}(t;x,\xi,z,\zeta)\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\psi(t)-i\psi(s)}\tilde{v}(s;x^{s},\xi^{s})\ ds\right)\ dxd\xi.

The above representation is quite similar to (
61
), where the function
e^{i\psi(t)}\tilde{u}_{0}(x,\xi)
is replaced by the slightly more complicated
\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\psi(t)-i\psi(s)}\tilde{v}(s;x^{s},\xi^{s})\ ds
. Therefore, the proof of the bounds (
60
) for
K
can easily be modified to prove the bounds (
65
) for
F

-differentiation term by term inside the integral
I_{\lambda,B}(r^{*},g)
. For the phase term involving
y
, we see factors that can be dominated by
\langle\lambda^{-1}(\eta-\xi)\rangle
, since

(74)

|\partial_{\lambda y_{j}}e^{i\lambda(y-x)\cdot\lambda^{-1}(\eta-\xi)}|=\lambda^{-1}|\eta_{j}-\xi_{j}|\leq\langle\lambda^{-1}(\eta-\xi)\rangle.

For the
\lambda y
-differentiation on
r^{*}
, similar to (
72
), by
\lambda\geq 1
it can be bounded as

(75)

|\partial_{\lambda y}^{\alpha}\partial_{\lambda^{-1}\eta}^{\beta}r^{*}(y,\eta,x,\xi)|\lesssim C_{h,\alpha,\beta}\left(\lambda^{|\beta|}+\lambda^{|\beta|+2}\langle\lambda(y-x)\rangle^{2}\langle\lambda^{-1}(\eta-\xi)\rangle^{2}\right).

We note the extra
\lambda^{C}
factors on the right hand side above are due to the fact that the
\eta
-dependence of
r^{*}
is always in the form of
(\eta-\xi)
, and each
\partial_{\lambda^{-1}\eta}
produces a
\lambda
factor

\partial_{\lambda^{-1}\eta_{j}}(\eta_{j}-\xi_{j})\leq\lambda\partial_{\lambda^{-1}\eta_{j}}\lambda^{-1}(\eta_{j}-\xi_{j})\leq\lambda.

For the
\lambda y
-differentiation on
e^{i\Gamma^{B}(y^{\prime},x,y)}
, by the derivative bounds (
2.1
) of
e^{i\Gamma^{B}}
, for
\lambda\geq 1
and
|\alpha|\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}_{\lambda y}e^{i\Gamma^{B}}(y^{\prime},x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha,B}(\langle\lambda(y-y^{\prime})\rangle+\langle\lambda(y-x)\rangle)^{|\alpha|}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\alpha,B}(\langle\lambda(y-x)\rangle\langle\lambda(y^{\prime}-x)\rangle+\langle\lambda(y-x)\rangle)^{|\alpha|}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\alpha,B}[(1+\langle\lambda(y^{\prime}-x)\rangle)\langle\lambda(y-x)\rangle]^{|\alpha|}.

Because
g(\lambda(y^{\prime}-x))
is Schwartz in
\lambda(y^{\prime}-x)
, any factor of
\langle\lambda(y^{\prime}-x)\rangle
will be harmless. To show the bound (
73
), it remains to show that we can control factors of
\langle\lambda^{-1}(\eta-\xi)\rangle
and
\langle\lambda(y-x)\rangle
. Notice we can gain a decaying factor of
\langle\lambda(y-x)\rangle^{-2}
repeatedly by integration by parts with respect to the
\lambda^{-1}\eta
-integral using

(76)

\langle\lambda(y-x)\rangle^{-2}(1-\Delta_{\lambda^{-1}\eta})e^{i\lambda(y-x)\cdot\lambda^{-1}\eta}=e^{i\lambda(y-x)\cdot\lambda^{-1}\eta},

and gain a decaying factor
\langle\lambda^{-1}(\eta-\xi)\rangle^{-2}
factors by repeatedly integration by parts with respect to the
\lambda y^{\prime}
-integral using

(77)

\langle\lambda^{-1}(\eta-\xi)\rangle^{-2}(1-\Delta_{\lambda y^{\prime}})e^{i\lambda^{-1}(\eta-\xi)\cdot\lambda y^{\prime}}=e^{i\lambda^{-1}(\eta-\xi)\cdot\lambda y^{\prime}}.

For the integration by parts of the
\lambda^{-1}\eta
-integral, we need to check the
\partial_{\lambda^{-1}\eta}
derivatives of
r^{*}
and the
\partial_{\lambda^{-1}\eta}
derivatives of the
\lambda y^{\prime}
-integral. For
r^{*}
, we use again the derivative bounds (
75
). For the
\partial_{\lambda^{-1}\eta}
derivatives of the
\lambda y
-integral

I_{\lambda y^{\prime}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\lambda(y^{\prime}-x)\cdot\lambda^{-1}(\eta-\xi)}e^{i\Gamma^{B}(y^{\prime},x,y)}g(\lambda(y^{\prime}-x))\ d\lambda y^{\prime},

each
\lambda^{-1}\eta
-differentiation only produces a factor that can be dominated by the harmless factor
\langle\lambda(y^{\prime}-x)\rangle
, that is

(78)

|\partial_{\lambda^{-1}\eta_{j}}e^{-i\lambda(y^{\prime}-x)\cdot\lambda^{-1}(\eta-\xi)}|=\lambda|y^{\prime}_{j}-x_{j}|\leq\langle\lambda(y^{\prime}-x)\rangle.

So by repeated integration by parts relying on (
76
), we can obtain decay in
\langle\lambda(y-x)\rangle^{-N}
with the cost of some
\lambda^{C_{N}}
factor and a
\langle\lambda^{-1}(\eta-\xi)\rangle^{2}
factor from (
75
).
For the integration by parts (
77
) inside the
I_{\lambda y^{\prime}}
integral, we also note by the derivative bounds (
2.1
) of
e^{i\Gamma^{B}}
, for
\lambda\geq 1
and
|\alpha|\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}_{\lambda y^{\prime}}e^{i\Gamma^{B}}(y^{\prime},x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\alpha,B}[(1+\langle\lambda(y-x)\rangle)\langle\lambda(y^{\prime}-x)\rangle]^{|\alpha|}.

Combined with the fact that
g(\lambda(y^{\prime}-x))
is Schwartz in
\lambda(y^{\prime}-x)
, we obtain

(79)

\left|\partial_{\lambda y^{\prime}}^{\alpha}(e^{i\Gamma^{B}(y^{\prime},x,y)})g(\lambda(y^{\prime}-x))\right|\lesssim C_{N,\alpha,B}(1+\langle\lambda(y-x)\rangle^{|\alpha|})\langle\lambda(y^{\prime}-x)\rangle^{-N},\qquad N\in\mathbb{N}.

This means by integration by parts (
77
) for the
I_{\lambda y^{\prime}}
integral, we obtain sufficient decay of
\langle\lambda^{-1}(\eta-\xi)\rangle^{-N}
at the cost of some finite power of
\langle\lambda(y-x)\rangle
, which will be dominated by the sufficient decay of
\langle\lambda(y-x)\rangle^{-N}
we obtained from the integration by parts (
76
) in the
\lambda^{-1}\eta
-integral. Then the claimed bound (
73
) holds by aforementioned integration by parts and bounds (
74
), (
75
), (
78
) and (
79
).

With the decay property of
I_{\lambda,B}(r^{*},g)
in (
73
), we define
W^{\prime}_{B,\lambda}
similar to
W_{B,\lambda}
in (
53
)

W^{\prime}_{B,\lambda}(y,x^{t},\xi^{t},z^{t}):=e^{i\Gamma^{B}(y,x^{t},z^{t})}g(\lambda(y-z^{t}))I_{\lambda,B}(r^{*},g)(y,x^{t},\xi^{t}),

so that combining (
2.1
) and (
73
) we obtain a decay estimate similar to (
54
), that is for all
N,N^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(80)

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\lambda y}^{\alpha}W^{\prime}_{B,\lambda}(y,x^{t},\xi^{t},z^{t})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{N,N^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime},\alpha,B}\lambda^{C_{N^{\prime\prime}}}\langle\lambda(z^{t}-x^{t})\rangle^{-N}\langle\lambda(y-z^{t})\rangle^{-N^{\prime}}\langle\lambda(y-x^{t})\rangle^{-N^{\prime\prime}}.

Now we can re-write
G_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}
with
W^{\prime}_{B,\lambda}
as

\displaystyle G_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}(t;x,\xi,z,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{g^{A,\lambda}_{z^{t},\zeta^{t}}}(y)\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}e^{i\varphi^{A}(y,x^{t})}e^{i(y-x^{t})\cdot\xi^{t}}I_{\lambda,B}(r^{*},g)(y,x^{t},\xi^{t})\ dy

\displaystyle=e^{i\varphi^{A}(z^{t},x^{t})}e^{i\zeta^{t}\cdot(z^{t}-x^{t})}\times

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i(\xi^{t}-\zeta^{t})\cdot(y-x^{t})}e^{i\Gamma^{B}(y,x^{t},z^{t})}g(\lambda(y-z^{t}))I_{\lambda,B}(r^{*},g)(y,x^{t},\xi^{t})\ d\lambda y

\displaystyle=e^{i\varphi^{A}(z^{t},x^{t})}e^{i\zeta^{t}\cdot(z^{t}-x^{t})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i(\xi^{t}-\zeta^{t})\cdot(y-x^{t})}W^{\prime}_{B,\lambda}(y,x^{t},\xi^{t},z^{t})\ d\lambda y,

and integration by parts again based on

\langle\lambda^{-1}(\zeta^{t}-\xi^{t})\rangle^{-2}(1-\Delta_{\lambda y})e^{i\lambda^{-1}(\zeta-\xi)\cdot\lambda y}=e^{i(\zeta-\xi)\cdot\lambda y}.

Together with the decay bounds of
W^{\prime}_{B,\lambda}
in (
80
), we obtain for all
N\in\mathbb{N}

|G_{\mathcal{R}_{2}}(t;x,\xi,z,\zeta)|\lesssim C_{N,B}\lambda^{C_{N}}\langle\lambda(z^{t}-x^{t})\rangle^{-N}\langle\lambda^{-1}(\zeta^{t}-\xi^{t})\rangle^{-N}.

Combining the above bound with the
G_{\mathcal{R}_{1}}
bound in (
71
), we have the desired bound for
G_{K}
.
∎

8.
Extension to the case of time-dependent magnetic fields

In this section we consider a time-dependent magnetic field
B(t)
with components
B_{jk}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, satisfying the assumptions in Theorem
1.7
. In particular, we recall the decay condition (
10
) for
B_{jk}(t)
and
\dot{B}_{jk}(t)
, that is, for some
\epsilon>0

|\partial^{\alpha}B_{jk}(t;y)|+|\partial^{\alpha}\dot{B}_{jk}(t;y)|\leq C_{\alpha,B}\langle y\rangle^{-1-\epsilon},\qquad|\alpha|\geq 1.

In principal, by substituting
A(x)
with
A(t;x)
constructed from
B(t)
, we can adapt most of the proof for Theorem
1.5
to prove Theorem
1.7
. In the following, we only highlight the necessary changes needed beyond obvious substitution and straightforward calculations.

Recalling the construction for the time-independent case, we define analogously the time-dependent vector potentials
A(t)
and phase function
\varphi^{A(t)}
by

\displaystyle A_{j}(t;y,x)

\displaystyle:=-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{1}s(y_{k}-x_{k})B_{jk}(t;x+s(y-x))\ ds,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(81)

\displaystyle A_{j}(t;y)

\displaystyle:=-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{1}sy_{k}B_{jk}(t;sy)\ ds=A_{j}(t;y,0),

\displaystyle\varphi^{A(t)}(y,x)

\displaystyle:=(y-x)\cdot\int_{0}^{1}A(t;(1-s)x+sy,0)\ ds.

We can further construct time-dependent magnetic pseudo-differential operators

\operatorname{Op}^{A(t)}(h)u(y):=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{i(y-y^{\prime})\cdot\eta}e^{-i\varphi^{A(t)}(y^{\prime},y)}h(\tfrac{y+y^{\prime}}{2},\eta)u(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}d\eta,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and time-dependent magnetic wavepackets

g^{A(t),\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y):=\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}e^{i\xi\cdot(y-x)}g(\lambda(y-x))e^{i\varphi^{A(t)}(y,x)},\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and also accordingly the transform
\mathcal{T}^{A(t)}_{\lambda}
and the magnetic modulation spaces
M^{m,p}_{A(t)}
.

With the above constructions, Lemma
6.2
holds with obvious changes since the time dependence does not play a role there. It suffices to substitute the
A
and
B
in Lemma
6.2
with the time-dependent ones
A(t)
and
B(t)
, in particular (
43
), (
44
) and (
45
) should be replaced by

(82)

\displaystyle m^{A(t)}(h)(x,\xi)

\displaystyle:=h(x,\xi)-\partial_{\eta}h(x,\xi)\cdot(\xi+A(t;x))

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}(t)g^{A(t),\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

\displaystyle:=\partial_{\eta}h(x,\xi)\cdot\left(-r^{(1)}_{x}(A(t;y,x))+r^{(1)}_{x}(A(t;x,y))\right)g^{A(t),\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{2}(t)g^{A(t),\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y)

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{Op}_{KN}^{A(t)}\left(r^{(1)}_{x,\xi}(h)+h_{r}\right)g^{A(t),\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y).

On the other hand, some further modification is required for Lemma
6.3
and Proposition
6.4
, because it involves
t
-derivatives. We define a new vector field
\tilde{H}^{B(t)}

\tilde{H}^{B(t)}_{j}:=\left(\partial_{\xi_{j}}h(t)\partial_{x_{j}},-\partial_{x_{j}}h(t)\partial_{\xi_{j}}+\dot{A}_{j}(t;x)\partial_{\xi_{j}}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}B_{kj}(t;x)\partial_{\xi_{k}}h(t)\partial_{\xi_{j}}\right),\quad 1\leq j\leq d,

and the associated flow
\chi^{\prime}_{B}(h)(t,s):(x^{s},\xi^{s})\mapsto(x^{t},\xi^{t})
satisfying

\begin{cases}\dot{x^{t}_{j}}=\partial_{\xi_{j}}h(t;x^{t},\xi^{t}),\\
\dot{\xi^{t}_{j}}=-\partial_{x_{j}}h(t;x^{t},\xi^{t})+\dot{A}_{j}(t;x^{t})+\sum_{k=1}^{d}B_{kj}(t;x^{t})\partial_{\xi_{k}}h(t;x^{t},\xi^{t}).\end{cases}

Notice that
\tilde{H}^{B(t)}
is also divergence-free and by our assumptions of
B(t)
and construction of
A(t)
, we have

|\dot{A}_{j}(t;x^{t})|\lesssim 1+|x^{t}|,

so it is straightforward to verify that Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.3
also hold for
\chi^{\prime}_{B}(h)
.

In order to adapt Proposition
6.4
for the time-dependent magnetic potentials, we define

(83)

\begin{gathered}\psi^{\prime}(t;x,\xi):=\int_{0}^{t}-m^{A(\tau)}(h)(\tau;x^{\tau},\xi^{\tau})\ d\tau,\\
g^{A(t),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}(t;y,x,\xi):=e^{i\psi^{\prime}(t)}\chi^{\prime}_{B}(h)(t,0)g^{A(t),\lambda}_{x,\xi}(y).\end{gathered}

Lemma 8.1
.

The wavepacket
g^{A(t),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}
associated to the flow
\chi^{\prime}_{B}(h)
satisfies

(D_{t}+\operatorname{Op}^{A(t)}(h))g^{A(t),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}(t;y,x,\xi)=(\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2}+\mathcal{R}_{3})(t)g^{A(t),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}(t;y,x,\xi),

where
\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}
are as defined in (
82
), and for some
c\in(0,1)
,

(84)

\mathcal{R}_{3}(t;y,x^{t}):=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(y_{j}-x^{t}_{j})\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x}\dot{A}_{j}(t;x^{t}+cs(y-x^{t}))\cdot s(y-x^{t})\ ds,

which satisfies the bound

(85)

|\mathcal{R}_{3}(t;y,x^{t})|\leq C_{B}\langle y-x^{t}\rangle^{2},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

By the same calculation as in Lemma
6.2
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}^{A(t)}(h)g^{A(t),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}(t;y,x,\xi)

\displaystyle=i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{\xi_{j}}h\partial_{x_{j}}-(\partial_{x_{j}}h-\sum_{k=1}^{d}B_{kj}(t;x)\partial_{\xi_{k}}h)\partial_{\xi_{j}}\right)g^{A(t),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}(t;y,x,\xi)

\displaystyle+(m^{A(t)}(h)+\mathcal{R}_{1}(t)+\mathcal{R}_{2}(t))g^{A(t),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}(t;y,x,\xi)

with the terms defined in (
82
). Then it suffices to show that the
t
-derivative of
g^{A(t),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}
is of the form

(86)

\displaystyle D_{t}g^{A(t),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}(t;y,x,\xi)

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\tilde{H}^{B(t)}_{j}-\dot{A}_{j}(t;x)\partial_{\xi_{j}}\right)g^{A(t),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}(t;y,x,\xi)

\displaystyle-\left(m^{A(t)}(h)-\mathcal{R}_{3}(t)\right)g^{A(t),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}(t;y,x,\xi),

with
\mathcal{R}_{3}
satisfying the bound (
85
). We claim

D_{t}e^{i\varphi^{A(t)}(y,x)}=\left((y-x)\cdot\dot{A}(t;x)+\mathcal{R}_{3}(t;y,x)\right)e^{i\varphi^{A(t)}(y,x)},

which combined with the construction of
g^{A(t),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}
as in (
83
) gives (
86
). Firstly notice

\partial_{t}e^{i\varphi^{A(t)}(y,x)}=i\dot{\varphi^{A(t)}}(y,x)e^{i\varphi^{A(t)}(y,x)},\quad\dot{\varphi^{A(t)}}:=d\varphi^{A(t)}/dt.

Recall our construction in (
8
), from which we get

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi^{A(t)}}(y,x)

\displaystyle=(y-x)\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\dot{A}(t;(1-s)x+sy,0)\ ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(y_{j}-x_{j})\int_{0}^{1}\dot{A}_{j}(t;x+s(y-x),0)\ ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(y_{j}-x_{j})\int_{0}^{1}\dot{A}_{j}(t;x)\ ds

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{d}(y_{j}-x_{j})\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x}\dot{A}_{j}(t;x+cs(y-x))\cdot s(y-x)\ ds,

where we used the Taylor expansion of
\dot{A}_{j}
at
x
. With
\mathcal{R}_{3}
as defined in (
84
), we see that

\dot{\varphi^{A(t)}}(y,x)=(y-x)\cdot\dot{A}(t;x)+\mathcal{R}_{3}(t;y,x).

For the first term on the right hand side above we use the identity

[(y-x)\cdot\dot{A}(t;x)]g^{A(t),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}(t;y,x,\xi)=-i\dot{A}(t;x)\cdot\partial_{\xi}g^{A(t),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}(t;y,x,\xi).

For the
\mathcal{R}_{3}
term

\mathcal{R}_{3}(t;y,x):=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(y_{j}-x_{j})\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{x}\dot{A}_{j}(t;x+cs(y-x))\cdot s(y-x)\ ds,

we see explicitly for the integrand

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\dot{A}_{j}(t;x+cs(y-x))

\displaystyle=-\partial_{i}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{1}\theta(x_{k}+cs(y_{k}-x_{k}))\dot{B}_{jk}(t;\theta(x+cs(y-x)))\ d\theta\right)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}(\theta-cs)\dot{B}_{ji}(t;\theta(x+cs(y-x)))\ d\theta

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{1}\theta(x_{k}+cs(y_{k}-x_{k}))\partial_{i}\dot{B}_{jk}(t;\theta(x+cs(y-x)))\ d\theta.

By the boundedness of
B(t),\dot{B}(t)
and the decay assumption (
10
), we see that

\sup_{x,y}|\partial_{i}\dot{A}_{j}(t;x+cs(y-x))|\leq C_{B},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

By the above bound and our definition (
84
) of
\mathcal{R}_{3}
, we have the bound (
85
) as desired.
∎

With Lemma

, we can now adapt Proposition
6.4
to the case of

.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
0\leq s,t\leq T
. We define the operator

\tilde{S}(t,s)u_{0}(y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}g^{A(t),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}(t;y,x,\xi)\overline{g^{A(s),\lambda}_{\psi^{\prime}}}(s;y^{\prime},x,\xi)u_{0}(y^{\prime})\ dy^{\prime}dxd\xi,

Moreover, for

With the above proposition, we can prove adapted versions of Lemma
