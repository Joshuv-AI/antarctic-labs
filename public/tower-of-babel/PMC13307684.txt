Magnesium and gender in long COVID
Authors: Patrick William Chambers
Published: 2026

Bar-Tana (2025 JTRM) proposes mTORC as a central driver of long Covid [1]. I would like to hypothetically extend this by suggesting upstream regulation via sex hormone–dependent Th1/IFN-γ signaling and the impact of magnesium deficiency.

Low dose estrogen seen in reproductive age females, the most frequent targets of long Covid, enables a more robust production of IFN-γ and Th1 CD4 + response to viral infections compared to males.

For PASC patients this response is prolonged [2, 3]. Why does this immune reaction go awry in long haulers? The criticality of mTOR activity to the Th1 response [4] may provide some insight. Bar-Tana et al. have proposed hyperactivation of mTOR as integral to many chronic fatigue diseases, including long Covid. What might cause this hyper activation?

Energy sensitive AMPK and nutrient sensitive mTOR demonstrate a reciprocal relationship. Both are magnesium dependent kinases.

But magnesium deficiency also impacts the magnesium dependent ADP: AMP ratio, compromising sensing of intracellular energy by AMPK. Thus, it appears that on a net basis mechanistically a mild magnesium deficiency may exacerbate the down regulation of AMPK and the increase in mTOR: AMPK. This is consistent with the over activation of mTOR in chronic fatigue diseases, e.g., long Covid, reported by Bar-Tana. However, a more significant deficiency of magnesium, an extremely vital nutrient, may ironically downregulate mTOR and its suppression of AMPK. The IFN-γ induced increase in indoleamine dioxygenase (IDO) that degrades tryptophan, a vital amino acid, might further exacerbate this. An increased kynurenine to tryptophan ratio characterizes many of the long-term symptoms and diseases beyond fatigue linked to long Covid.

Severity of COVID-19 is reportedly directly related to the calcium to magnesium ratio (Ca: Mg). However, this same group in 2023 also showed that magnesium and vitamin D were both low in those with long Covid [5]. Both the storage and active forms of vitamin D (25(OH)D3 and 1,25(OH)2D3) are Mg dependent.

In summary, proposed is an extension of the Bar-Tana thesis that mechanistically incorporates estradiol, IFN-γ, and Mg (Ca: Mg ratio) and suggests that the fatigue linked to hyper activation of mTOR may represent the initial symptom of long Covid but that most of its symptoms and associated diseases, e.g., cognitive decline, autoimmune disease, cardiovascular disease, …, may develop, if Mg deficiency worsens. This extension of the Bar-Tana thesis suggests that modification of the mTOR: AMPK ratio, supplementation with Mg and vitamin D and rapamycin (in those with just fatigue, as it reportedly decreases mTOR: AMPK, clinical trials underway) might in combination prove beneficial in treating long Covid and associated long term diseases. Normalizing the Ca: Mg (1.7–2.5) might even extend the benefits of vitamin D to diminished risk for Covid-19, as calcium and magnesium compete for the same receptor/channel, calcium sensing receptor (CaSR) and transient receptor potential melastatin 7 channel (TRPM7). According to NHANES, the American mean for this ratio has remained well above 3.0 since 2000.
