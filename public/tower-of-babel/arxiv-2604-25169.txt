Nonlocal Cooper pairs in finite topological superconductors and their relation to Majorana nonlocality Preprint: APS/123-QED
Hiroto Mizoguchi, Yutaro Nagae, Yasuhiro Asano, Satoshi Ikegaya

Abstract
We identify two fundamental properties of the Gor’kov Green’s function of finite one-dimensional topological superconductors. In the low-frequency (low-energy) regime, the normal and anomalous Green’s functions, which describe single-particle and Cooper-pair correlations, respectively, become identical up to a phase factor. Moreover, they exhibit pronounced nonlocality: correlations between the two ends of the system grow exponentially with system length, whereas local correlations at either end vanish in the zero-frequency limit. These striking features signify the emergence of unconventional nonlocal Cooper pairs associated with a nonlocal fermionic mode composed of hybridized Majorana end modes. The nonlocal Cooper pairs are directly linked to fermion parity and to the nonlocal transport properties of finite topological superconductors. By focusing on pair correlations, our analysis advances the understanding of Majorana nonlocality, a key concept in topological quantum computation.



I
Introduction

In the mean-field theory of superconductivity, two complementary perspectives can be adopted.
One focuses on Bogoliubov quasiparticle excitations described by the Bogoliubov–de Gennes equations,
while the other focuses on Cooper-pair correlations described by the Gor’kov equations.
For example, the superconducting proximity effect can be interpreted
in the quasiparticle picture as the formation of correlated electron–hole pairs via Andreev reflection at junction interfaces
andreev_64
;
beenakker_97
;
courtois_00
.
In contrast, in the pair-correlation picture, it corresponds to the penetration of Cooper pairs into an adjacent material
gennes_64
;
eilenberger_68
;
usadel_70
.
The complementary use of these two viewpoints is essential for a comprehensive understanding of superconducting phenomena.

Over the past two decades, Majorana end modes in topological superconductors have been extensively studied within the quasiparticle framework
green_00
;
kitaev_01
;
kane_10
;
zhang_11
.
In parallel, odd-frequency Cooper pairs have attracted considerable attention from the perspective of pair correlations
berezinskii_74
;
efetov_05
;
tanaka_07
;
linder_19
.
It is now widely recognized that these two concepts are intimately connected:
odd-frequency pairing emerges wherever Majorana end modes exist
nagaosa_12
;
asano_13
;
cayao_20
,
as a consequence of bulk–boundary correspondence
gurarie_11
;
tamura_19
;
daido_19
.
However, this connection has been established for
semi-infinite
one-dimensional topological superconductors.

In
finite
one-dimensional topological superconductors,
Majorana end modes localized at opposite ends hybridize to form a single nonlocal fermionic mode.
The quantum nonlocality associated with this mode—referred to as Majorana nonlocality—has been a central issue in the field of topological superconductivity,
because the occupation of this nonlocal mode encodes a qubit for topological quantum computation
ivanov_01
;
kitaev_03
;
sarma_08
;
fisher_11
;
alicea_16
.
A variety of theoretical proposals have been put forward to detect Majorana nonlocality through multiterminal transport measurements
demler_07
;
tewari_08
;
beenakker_08
;
fu_10
;
egger_11
;
hu_13
;
law_13
;
rosenow_13
;
tewari_15
;
flensberg_20
;
xu_20
;
fujimoto_23
;
ikegaya_24
.
Most analyses, however, rely on a simplified low-energy effective Hamiltonian
kitaev_01
,

H_{M}=i(E_{M}/2)\gamma_{a}\gamma_{b}
, where
\gamma_{a}=\gamma_{a}^{\dagger}
and
\gamma_{b}=\gamma_{b}^{\dagger}

represent Majorana end modes localized at opposite ends, and
E_{M}
denotes the coupling energy
stanescu_12
.
Consequently, Majorana nonlocality has been investigated predominantly within the quasiparticle framework,
while its manifestation in terms of pair correlations remains largely unexplored.
In this work, we address this issue.

We analytically derive the Gor’kov Green’s function for finite one-dimensional topological superconductors.
In the low-frequency regime, the Green’s function exhibits two distinctive properties.
First, the normal and anomalous Green’s functions—describing single-particle and Cooper-pair correlations, respectively—are equivalent up to a phase factor.
Second, they display pronounced nonlocality: end-to-end correlations grow exponentially with the system length,
whereas local correlations at either edge vanish in the zero-frequency limit.
These striking features signify the emergence of unconventional nonlocal Cooper pairs in finite topological superconductors.
Such nonlocal Cooper pairs are directly linked to fermion parity and to nonlocal transport phenomena previously investigated within the quasiparticle framework.
Our results provide a foundation for revisiting Majorana nonlocality from the perspective of pair correlations.

II
Gor’kov Green’s function

II.1
Derivation of Green’s function

In this section, we derive the Gor’kov Green’s function for a one-dimensional topological superconductor.
Specifically, we employ the Kitaev chain model
kitaev_01
,
which captures the essential low-energy physics of a broad class of topological superconductors,
including superconductor–semiconductor hybrid nanowires
sarma_10
;
oreg_10
;
kouwenhoven_12
;
deng_12
,
magnetic atom chains on superconducting substrates
beenakker_11
;
yazdani_13
;
yazdani_14
;
yazdani_17
,
and planar topological Josephson junctions
flensberg_17
;
halperin_17
;
nichele_19
;
yacoby_19
;
ikegaya_22
.
The Bogoliubov–de Gennes Hamiltonian is given by
kitaev_01

\displaystyle H=\sum_{j}\Big[

\displaystyle-t\left(c_{j+1}^{\dagger}c_{j}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right)-\mu\left(c_{j}^{\dagger}c_{j}-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{i\Delta}{2}e^{i\theta}c_{j+1}^{\dagger}c_{j}^{\dagger}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right)\Big],

(1)

where
c_{j}
(
c_{j}^{\dagger}
) annihilates (creates) an electron at site
j
,

t
is the hopping amplitude, and
\mu
is the chemical potential.
The pair potential has amplitude
\Delta
and phase
\theta
.
In what follows, we set
\theta=0
for simplicity and take the lattice constant to be unity.
Introducing the Nambu basis, the Hamiltonian can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&H=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}\left(c_{j}^{\dagger},c_{j}\right)\hat{H}(j,j^{\prime})\begin{pmatrix}c_{j^{\prime}}\\
c_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix},\\
&\hat{H}(j,j^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\hat{T}&\text{for }j^{\prime}=j-1\\
\hat{T}^{\dagger}&\text{for }j^{\prime}=j+1\\
\hat{K}&\text{for }j^{\prime}=j\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},\\
&\hat{T}=-t\hat{\tau}_{3}+i\frac{\Delta}{2}\hat{\tau}_{1},\quad\hat{K}=-\mu\hat{\tau}_{3},\end{split}

(2)

where
\hat{\tau}_{1,2,3}
denote the Pauli matrices in Nambu space.

The Matsubara Gor’kov Green’s function
\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j,j^{\prime})
for the bulk system satisfies the Gor’kov equation

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1}}\left[i\omega\delta_{j,j_{1}}-\hat{H}(j,j_{1})\right]\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j_{1},j^{\prime})=\delta_{j,j^{\prime}},

(3)

where
\omega=(2n+1)\pi T
is the fermionic Matsubara frequency at temperature
T
.
In the regime

\displaystyle\Delta,\omega\ll t,

(4)

the Green’s function takes the form

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j,j^{\prime})=-\frac{i}{v_{F}\Omega_{k_{F}}}\left(a_{0}+a_{1}\hat{\tau}_{1}+a_{3}\hat{\tau}_{3}\right),

(5)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}&a_{0}=\omega\cos\!\big(k_{F}|j-j^{\prime}|\big)e^{-|j-j^{\prime}|/\xi},\\
&a_{1}=\Delta_{k_{F}}\sin\!\big(k_{F}(j-j^{\prime})\big)e^{-|j-j^{\prime}|/\xi},\\
&a_{3}=i\Omega_{k_{F}}\sin\!\big(k_{F}|j-j^{\prime}|\big)e^{-|j-j^{\prime}|/\xi},\\
&\Delta_{k}=\Delta\sin k,\quad\Omega_{k}=\sqrt{\omega^{2}+\Delta_{k}^{2}},\\
&k_{F}=\arccos\!\left(-\frac{\mu}{2t}\right),\quad v_{F}=2t\sin k_{F},\quad\xi=\frac{v_{F}}{\Omega_{k_{F}}}.\end{split}

(6)

A detailed derivation of Eq. (
5
) is provided in Appendix
A.1
.

To obtain the Green’s function for a finite system, we introduce boundary potentials at
j=0
and
j=L+1
and solve the Dyson equation

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{V}(j,j^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j,j^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}}\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j,j_{1})\hat{V}(j_{1},j_{2})\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{V}(j_{2},j^{\prime}),

(7)

with

\displaystyle\hat{V}(j,j^{\prime})=V\,\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}\bigl(\delta_{j,0}+\delta_{j,L+1}\bigr)\hat{\tau}_{3}.

(8)

Taking the hard-wall limit, we obtain the Green’s function of the finite topological superconductor for
1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq L
:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}(j,j^{\prime})=\lim_{V\to\infty}\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{V}(j,j^{\prime}).

(9)

The explicit expression of
\hat{\mathcal{G}}(j,j^{\prime})
is given in Eq. (
73
) of Appendix
A.2
.
We note that an equivalent Green’s function for a continuum model of a
p
-wave superconductor was derived using McMillan’s method in Ref. [
tanaka_24
];
however, a detailed analysis of its properties has not yet been carried out.
For later convenience, we decompose the Green’s function in Nambu space as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}(j,j^{\prime})=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{G}(j,j^{\prime})&\mathcal{F}(j,j^{\prime})\\
\underline{\mathcal{F}}(j,j^{\prime})&\underline{\mathcal{G}}(j,j^{\prime})\end{pmatrix},

(10)

where
\mathcal{G}
(
\underline{\mathcal{G}}
) denotes the normal Green’s function describing single-particle correlations of electrons (holes),
and
\mathcal{F}
and
\underline{\mathcal{F}}
denote the anomalous Green’s functions describing pair correlations.

II.2
Fundamental properties

In this section, we analyze the Green’s functions in the low-frequency regime,

\displaystyle\omega\ll\Delta_{k_{F}},

(11)

for a system of length
L
satisfying

\displaystyle 1\ll\xi_{0}\ll L,

(12)

where
\xi_{0}=v_{F}/\Delta_{k_{F}}
.
Under these conditions, we retain only the leading-order contributions in

\displaystyle\delta=\frac{\omega}{\Delta_{k_{F}}},\;\frac{1}{\xi_{0}},\;e^{-L/\xi_{0}}.

(13)

Accordingly, the Green’s functions reduce to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\mathcal{G}}(1,j^{\prime})=&-i\frac{\omega}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}}\frac{2}{\xi_{0}}\sin(k_F)\sin(k_F j')\left(1-\hat{\tau}_{2}\right)e^{-j^{\prime}/\xi_{0}}\\
&-i\frac{E_{M}}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}}\frac{2}{\xi_{0}}\sin(k_F)\sin(k_F (L+1-j'))\left(-\hat{\tau}_{1}-i\hat{\tau}_{3}\right)e^{-(L-j^{\prime})/\xi_{0}},\end{split}

(14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\mathcal{G}}(j,L)=&-i\frac{\omega}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}}\frac{2}{\xi_{0}}\sin(k_F (L+1-j))\sin(k_F)\left(1+\hat{\tau}_{2}\right)e^{-(L-j)/\xi_{0}}\\
&-i\frac{E_{M}}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}}\frac{2}{\xi_{0}}\sin(k_F j)\sin(k_F)\left(\hat{\tau}_{1}-i\hat{\tau}_{3}\right)e^{-j/\xi_{0}}.\end{split}

(15)

where

\displaystyle E_{M}=2\Delta_{k_{F}}\sin\!\big(k_{F}(L+1)\big)e^{-L/\xi_{0}}.

(16)

Detailed derivations of Eqs. (
14
) and (
15
) are provided in Appendix
A.2
.
Importantly, the second term in Eq. (
14
) [Eq. (
15
)]
increases
exponentially with increasing
j^{\prime}
[decreasing
j
],
whereas the first term decays exponentially with increasing
j^{\prime}
[decreasing
j
].
Both terms are suppressed near the center of the topological superconductor, i.e.,
j^{\prime}\sim L/2
[
j\sim L/2
].
To elucidate the properties of the Green’s functions, we focus on those associated with the outermost sites:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\hat{\mathcal{G}}(1,1)=-iC\frac{\omega}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}}\left(1-\hat{\tau}_{2}\right),\\
&\hat{\mathcal{G}}(L,L)=-iC\frac{\omega}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}}\left(1+\hat{\tau}_{2}\right),\end{split}

(17)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\hat{\mathcal{G}}(1,L)=-iC\frac{E_{M}}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}}\left(-\hat{\tau}_{1}-i\hat{\tau}_{3}\right),\\
&\hat{\mathcal{G}}(L,1)=-iC\frac{E_{M}}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{1}-i\hat{\tau}_{3}\right),\end{split}

(18)

where

\displaystyle C=\frac{2}{\xi_{0}}\sin^{2}k_{F}.

(19)

We now turn to the two key features of these Green’s functions.

First, for both the local and nonlocal components, the normal and anomalous Green’s functions differ only by a phase factor:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(j,j^{\prime})=\pm ie^{i\theta}\mathcal{F}(j,j^{\prime}),

(20)

where the superconducting phase
\theta
is included explicitly.
Recalling the relations between the Green’s functions and the field operators,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\langle c_{j}c_{j^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\rangle=-T\sum_{\omega}\mathcal{G}(j,j^{\prime}),\\
&\langle c_{j}c_{j^{\prime}}\rangle=-T\sum_{\omega}\mathcal{F}(j,j^{\prime}),\end{split}

(21)

we find that Eq. (
20
) implies that electrons and holes are equivalent up to a phase factor.
Thus, this equivalence between the normal and anomalous Green’s functions reflects the Majorana character of the low-energy physics.
We note that a similar property of the
local
Green’s functions in
semi-infinite
topological superconductors has been investigated previously
asano_13
.

Second, the Green’s functions exhibit pronounced nonlocality.
As shown in Eq. (
18
), the nonlocal Green’s functions,
\hat{\mathcal{G}}(1,L)
and
\hat{\mathcal{G}}(L,1)
, are proportional to

\displaystyle\frac{E_{M}}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}},

which represents a Lorentzian peak centered at zero frequency, with a width set by
E_{M}\propto e^{-L/\xi_{0}}
.
Accordingly, the nonlocal Green’s functions retain significant amplitudes for
\omega\ll E_{M}
.
In particular, at zero frequency, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\hat{\mathcal{G}}(1,L)\propto e^{L/\xi_{0}},\\
&\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\hat{\mathcal{G}}(L,1)\propto e^{L/\xi_{0}},\end{split}

(22)

indicating that the nonlocal correlations
grow
exponentially with the system length.
In contrast, as shown in Eq. (
17
), the local Green’s functions,
\hat{\mathcal{G}}(1,1)
and
\hat{\mathcal{G}}(L,L)
, are proportional to

\displaystyle\frac{\omega}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}},

exhibiting odd-frequency symmetry and therefore vanishing at zero frequency:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\hat{\mathcal{G}}(1,1)=0,\\
&\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\hat{\mathcal{G}}(L,L)=0.\end{split}

(23)

Consequently, the low-energy physics in the regime
\omega\ll E_{M}
is dominated by nonlocal correlations.
We note that in typical superconductors, nonlocal Green’s functions usually decay exponentially over a length scale
\xi_{0}
[see also Eq. (
5
)].
Thus, the present results clearly demonstrate the striking and intrinsic nonlocal character of the Green’s functions in finite topological superconductors.

Overall, we obtain anomalous Green’s functions satisfying

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(1,L)|=|\mathcal{G}(1,L)|,

(24)

together with

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(1,L)\propto e^{L/\xi_{0}}.

(25)

From the viewpoint of pair correlations, these properties signify the emergence of unconventional nonlocal Cooper pairs in finite topological superconductors.
To verify the robustness of these analytical findings, we present numerical results obtained without approximation,
where the Green’s functions are computed using the recursive Green’s function method
fisher_81
.
In Fig.
1
(a), we plot
|\mathcal{F}(1,L)|/|\mathcal{G}(1,L)|
at
\omega=0
as a function of the system length
L
.
The parameters are set to
\mu=-t
and
\Delta=0.001t
.
Over a wide range of
L
, we find
|\mathcal{F}(1,L)|/|\mathcal{G}(1,L)|=1
,
demonstrating the equivalence of the normal and anomalous Green’s functions, consistent with Eq. (
20
).
Figure
1
(b) shows
\log_{10}|\mathcal{F}(1,L)|
at
\omega=0
as a function of
L
.
We clearly observe the exponential growth of the nonlocal Green’s function predicted in Eq. (
22
).
Since
E_{M}\propto\sin(k_F L)
,
\mathcal{F}(1,L)
also exhibits rapid oscillations as a function of
L
.
Although our analytical results are derived under the assumption in Eq. (
12
),
we confirm that the key properties in Eqs. (
20
) and (
22
) remain valid over a broad range of
L
.

|\mathcal{F}(1,L)|/|\mathcal{G}(1,L)|

II.3
Relation to fermion parity

In this section, we examine the relationship between the nonlocal Green’s functions and the emergent nonlocal fermionic mode originating from Majorana end modes.
To this end, we focus on the end-to-end correlator

\displaystyle\langle c_{1}c_{L}^{\dagger}\rangle=-T\sum_{\omega}\mathcal{G}(1,L).

(26)

For sufficiently low temperatures (i.e.,
T\ll\Delta
) and for system lengths satisfying Eq. (
12
), this correlator reduces to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle c_{1}c_{L}^{\dagger}\rangle&\sim CT\sum_{\omega}\frac{E_{M}}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}}=\frac{C}{2}\left\{1-2f(E_{M})\right\},\end{split}

(27)

where
f(E)
denotes the Fermi distribution function.
Contributions from the continuum modes to the nonlocal correlator are exponentially suppressed and may therefore be neglected.
We next consider the low-energy effective Hamiltonian of the topological superconductor
kitaev_01
,

\displaystyle H_{M}=i\frac{E_{M}}{2}\gamma_{a}\gamma_{b},

(28)

where
\gamma_{a}
and
\gamma_{b}
represent Majorana end modes localized at
j=1
and
j=L
, respectively.
Introducing the nonlocal fermionic mode

\displaystyle f=\frac{\gamma_{a}+i\gamma_{b}}{2},

(29)

the effective Hamiltonian can be written in diagonal form as
kitaev_01

\displaystyle H_{M}=E_{M}\left(n_{f}-\frac{1}{2}\right),\quad n_{f}=f^{\dagger}f.

(30)

In thermal equilibrium, this yields
\langle n_{f}\rangle=f(E_{M})
.
Using this relation, the nonlocal correlator becomes

\displaystyle\langle c_{1}c_{L}^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle\sim\frac{C}{2}\left(1-2\langle n_{f}\rangle\right)=\frac{C}{2}\langle P_{f}\rangle,

(31)

where

\displaystyle P_{f}=-i\gamma_{a}\gamma_{b}=(-1)^{n_{f}},

(32)

denotes the fermion-parity operator associated with the occupation of the nonlocal fermionic mode
kitaev_01
.
We therefore arrive at

\displaystyle\langle P_{f}\rangle=-\frac{2T}{C}\sum_{\omega}\mathcal{G}(1,L).

(33)

On the basis of the equivalence in Eq. (
20
), the pair correlation is likewise directly related to the fermion parity:

\displaystyle\langle P_{f}\rangle=-\frac{2iT}{C}\sum_{\omega}\mathcal{F}(1,L),

(34)

which demonstrates a direct connection between unconventional nonlocal Cooper pairs and the fermion parity of the topological superconductor.

II.4
Role in charge transport

In this section, we examine the role of the nonlocal Green’s functions in charge transport.
To this end, we consider the multiterminal setup shown in Fig.
2
, where normal leads are attached to both ends of a topological superconductor.
A same bias voltage is applied to the normal leads, while the topological superconductor is grounded.
Charge transport in this system has previously been investigated within the quasiparticle framework.
We therefore begin by briefly reviewing the key results of the seminal work, Ref [
beenakker_08
], based on the effective low-energy Hamiltonian
H_{M}
,
and then clarify the motivation for reexamining the problem from the perspective of pair correlations.
Within the analysis based on
H_{M}
, the scattering coefficients at low energies
E\ll E_{M}
satisfy
beenakker_08

\displaystyle|s^{he}_{aa}|\ll|s^{he}_{ab}|=|s^{ee}_{ab}|,

(35)

where
s^{ee}_{\alpha\beta}
and
s^{he}_{\alpha\beta}
(
s^{eh}_{\alpha\beta}
and
s^{hh}_{\alpha\beta}
)
denote the scattering coefficients from an electron (hole) in lead
\beta
to an electron and a hole in lead
\alpha
, respectively.
Equation (
35
) highlights two important features.
First, inter-lead scattering dominates over local Andreev reflection.
Second, electron–electron tunneling (elastic cotunneling) and electron–hole tunneling (crossed Andreev reflection) occur with equal probability.
These properties give rise to an anomaly in the zero-frequency noise power, defined as

\displaystyle P_{\alpha\beta}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\overline{\delta I_{\alpha}(0)\delta I_{\beta}(t)}dt

(36)

where
\delta I_{\alpha}(t)=I_{\alpha}(t)-I_{\alpha}
denotes the current fluctuations at time
t
,
and
I_{\alpha}
is the time-averaged current in the lead
\alpha
.
In particular, for low bias voltages
eV\ll E_{M}
, the zero-frequency noise power satisfies
beenakker_08

\displaystyle 2P_{ab}=P_{aa}+P_{bb},

(37)

indicating a maximal positive cross correlation, which is widely regarded as an experimental signature of Majorana nonlocality.
It is important to note, however, that this anomaly arises in the regime
eV\ll E_{M}
, whereas the nonlocal fermionic mode appears at
E=E_{M}
.
This mismatch of energy scales suggests that the unusual transport properties of a finite topological superconductor
cannot be attributed solely to the presence of the nonlocal fermionic mode.
In light of this, we reexamine the transport properties from the perspective of pair correlations to gain further insight into the origin of the maximal positive cross correlation.

Schematic illustration of a multiterminal setup with normal leads attached to both ends of a topological superconductor.

To compute the scattering coefficients, we model the normal leads and the interfaces by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&H_{a}=-t\sum_{j<0}\left(a_{j+1}^{\dagger}a_{j}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right),\\
&H_{b}=-t\sum_{j>L}\left(a_{j+1}^{\dagger}a_{j}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right),\\
&H_{i}=-t_{i}\left(c_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right)-t_{i}\left(a_{L+1}^{\dagger}c_{L}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right),\end{split}

(38)

where the topological superconductor occupies the region
1\leq j\leq L
.
Here,
a_{j}
(
a_{j}^{\dagger}
) annihilates (creates) an electron in the normal leads,
and
t_{i}
denotes the hopping amplitude across the interfaces.
In the wide-band limit
E\ll t
, the scattering matrix is given by
ando_91

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\check{S}=1-\frac{2i}{t}\check{T}_{i}^{\dagger}\left(\check{G}^{-1}_{s}+\frac{i}{t}\check{T}_{i}\check{T}_{i}^{\dagger}\right)^{-1}\!\check{T}_{i},\\
&\check{G}_{s}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{G}(1,1)&\hat{G}(1,L)\\
\hat{G}(L,1)&\hat{G}(L,L)\end{array}\right),\\
&\check{T}_{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-t_{i}\hat{\tau}_{3}&0\\
0&-t_{i}\hat{\tau}_{3}\end{array}\right),\end{split}

(39)

where

\displaystyle\check{S}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{s}_{aa}&\hat{s}_{ab}\\
\hat{s}_{ba}&\hat{s}_{bb}\end{array}\right),\quad\hat{s}_{\alpha\beta}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}s^{ee}_{\alpha\beta}&s^{eh}_{\alpha\beta}\\
s^{he}_{\alpha\beta}&s^{hh}_{\alpha\beta}\end{array}\right).

The retarded Green’s function
\hat{G}(j,j^{\prime})
is obtained from the Matsubara Green’s function by analytic continuation,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}(j,j^{\prime})\xrightarrow{i\omega\to E+i0^{+}}\hat{G}(j,j^{\prime}).

(44)

A detailed derivation of Eq. (
39
) is provided in Appendix
B
.
In Nambu space, the retarded Green’s function is written as

\displaystyle\hat{G}(j,j^{\prime})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}G(j,j^{\prime})&F(j,j^{\prime})\\
\underline{F}(j,j^{\prime})&\underline{G}(j,j^{\prime})\end{array}\right).

We consider the low-energy and weak-coupling regime

\displaystyle E,t_{i}\ll E_{M}\ll\Delta_{k_{F}}\ll t,

(47)

for system lengths satisfying

\displaystyle 1\ll\xi_{0}\ll L.

(48)

In this regime, the scattering matrix reduces to

\displaystyle\check{S}\sim 1-\frac{2i}{t}\check{T}_{i}^{\dagger}\check{G}_{s}\check{T}_{i}.

(49)

The scattering coefficients are given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&s^{he}_{aa}=\frac{2it_{i}^{2}}{t}\underline{F}(1,1)=\Gamma\frac{E}{E^{2}-E_{M}^{2}}\sim 0,\\
&s^{he}_{ab}=\frac{2it_{i}^{2}}{t}\underline{F}(1,L)=\Gamma\frac{E_{M}}{E^{2}-E_{M}^{2}}\sim-\frac{\Gamma}{E_{M}},\\
&s^{ee}_{ab}=-\frac{2it_{i}^{2}}{t}G(1,L)=-i\Gamma\frac{E_{M}}{E^{2}-E_{M}^{2}}\sim i\frac{\Gamma}{E_{M}},\end{split}

(50)

where
\Gamma=2t_{i}^{2}C/t
.
The derivation of Eq. (
49
) is also provided in Appendix
B
.
These results show that the key features of the scattering coefficients in Eq. (
35
)
arise directly from the unusual properties of the Green’s functions discussed in Sec.
II.2
:

\displaystyle|\underline{F}(1,1)|\ll|\underline{F}(1,L)|=|G(1,L)|.

(51)

In particular, inter-lead scattering dominates over local Andreev reflection due to the pronounced nonlocality of the Green’s functions,
as demonstrated in Eqs. (
22
) and (
23
).
Moreover, the equal amplitudes of elastic cotunneling and crossed Andreev reflection follow directly from the equivalence of the normal and anomalous Green’s functions,
as shown in Eq. (
20
).
From the perspective of pair correlations, the maximal positive cross correlation in Eq. (
37
)
can thus be seen as a direct manifestation of the unconventional nonlocal Cooper pairs intrinsic to finite topological superconductors.

III
Discussion and Summary

In the present study, we focus on the Green’s function in the low-temperature regime,
T\ll E_{M}
.
For much longer topological superconductors, however, the opposite limit,
E_{M}\ll T
, may be realized.
In this regime, the nonlocal correlations vanish due to equal contributions from the
E=+E_{M}
and
E=-E_{M}
states,
i.e.,
\mathcal{G}(1,L)\propto\mathcal{F}(1,L)\propto\langle P_{f}\rangle=0
for
E_{M}/\omega\to 0
.
To preserve nonlocality in the limit
L/\xi_{0}\to\infty
, additional terms that fix the fermion parity—such as charging energy
fu_10
—may be required.
Analyzing the Green’s functions in the presence of such interactions remains an important direction for future work.
In addition, establishing a direct connection between the Green’s functions obtained here and experimentally accessible parity measurements is an important open problem
sarma_08
.
While we focus on systems hosting topologically protected Majorana end modes,
our theory can likely be extended to minimal Kitaev chains realized in quantum-dot–superconductor hybrid systems
hosting so-called poor man’s Majorana modes
dassarma_12
;
leijnse_12
.
Investigating the nonlocal Green’s functions in such systems would also be an intriguing direction for future work
cayao_24
.

In conclusion, we have examined the emergence of unconventional nonlocal Cooper pairs in finite topological superconductors,
characterized by two distinctive features of the Gor’kov Green’s functions:
the equivalence between single-particle and Cooper-pair correlations, and their pronounced nonlocality.
We have shown that these nonlocal Cooper pairs are intrinsically linked to fermion parity and to the nonlocal transport properties of finite topological superconductors.
On the basis of the pair-correlation picture, our results provide further insight into Majorana nonlocality, a key concept in topological quantum computation.

Appendix A
Detailed derivations of

Gor’kov Green’s functions

A.1
Bulk system

In this section, we compute the Green’s function for a bulk Kitaev chain.
Under periodic boundary conditions, the Bogoliubov–de Gennes Hamiltonian in Eq. (
2
) is diagonalized as

\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}}\hat{H}(j,j^{\prime})\hat{U}_{k}(j^{\prime})=\hat{U}_{k}(j^{\prime})\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}E_{k}&0\\
0&-E_{k}\end{array}\right),

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\hat{U}_{k}(j)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{k}&-v_{k}\\
v_{k}&u_{k}\end{array}\right)\frac{e^{ikj}}{\sqrt{L^{\prime}}},\\
&u_{k}=\frac{E_{k}+\xi_{k}}{\sqrt{2E_{k}(E_{k}+\xi_{k})}},\quad v_{k}=\frac{\Delta_{k}}{\sqrt{2E_{k}(E_{k}+\xi_{k})}},\\
&E_{k}=\sqrt{\xi_{k}^{2}+\Delta_{k}^{2}},\\
&\xi_{k}=-2t\cos k-\mu,\quad\Delta_{k}=\Delta\sin k,\end{split}

(54)

and
L^{\prime}
denotes the system length under periodic boundary conditions.
The Gor’kov Green’s function satisfying Eq. (
3
) can be then written as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j,j^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\hat{U}_{k}(j)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}i\omega-E_{k}&0\\
0&i\omega+E_{k}\end{array}\right)^{-1}\!\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{k}(j^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L^{\prime}}\sum_{k}\sum_{\eta=\pm}\frac{e^{ik(j-j^{\prime})}}{i\omega-\eta E_{k}}F_{\eta}(k)

(57)

where

\displaystyle F_{\pm}(k)=\frac{1}{2E_{k}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}E_{k}\pm\xi_{k}&\pm\Delta_{k}\\
\pm\Delta_{k}&E_{k}\mp\xi_{k}\end{array}\right).

Taking the limit
L^{\prime}\to\infty
, we obtain the bulk Green’s function,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j,j^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}dk\sum_{\eta=\pm}\frac{e^{ik(j-j^{\prime})}}{i\omega-\eta E_{k}}F_{\eta}(k).

(60)

For
\omega\geq 0
(
\omega<0
), the integrand with
\eta=+
(
-
) has poles at complex wave numbers satisfying

\displaystyle\cos k=\cos k_{F}-i\eta\frac{\Omega_{k}}{2t},

(61)

where

\displaystyle k_{F}=\arccos\left(-\frac{\mu}{2t}\right),\qquad\Omega_{k}=\sqrt{\omega^{2}+\Delta^{2}_{k}}.

(62)

In the following, we assume

\displaystyle\omega,\Delta\ll t,

(63)

and retain only the leading-order terms in
\delta=\omega/t,\Delta/t
.
In this regime, the pole positions reduce to

\displaystyle k=\pm\left(k_{F}+i\eta\kappa\right),

(64)

where

\displaystyle\kappa=\frac{1}{\xi}=\frac{\Omega_{k_{F}}}{v_{F}},\quad v_{F}=2t\sin k_{F}.

(65)

Evaluating the complex integral by computing the residues at these poles, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j,j^{\prime})=-\frac{i}{v_{F}\Omega_{k_{F}}}\left(a_{0}+a_{1}\hat{\tau}_{1}+a_{3}\hat{\tau}_{3}\right),\\
&a_{0}=\omega\cos\!\big(k_{F}|j-j^{\prime}|\big)e^{-\kappa|j-j^{\prime}|},\\
&a_{1}=\Delta_{k_{F}}\sin\!\big(k_{F}(j-j^{\prime})\big)e^{-\kappa|j-j^{\prime}|},\\
&a_{3}=i\Omega_{k_{F}}\sin\!\big(k_{F}|j-j^{\prime}|\big)e^{-\kappa|j-j^{\prime}|}.\end{split}

(66)

A.2
Finite system

In this section, we compute the Green’s function for a finite Kitaev chain.
We begin with the Dyson equation,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{V}(j,j^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j,j^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}}\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j,j_{1})\hat{V}(j_{1},j_{2})\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{V}(j_{2},j^{\prime}),

(67)

where

\displaystyle\hat{V}(j,j^{\prime})=V\,\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}\bigl(\delta_{j,0}+\delta_{j,L+1}\bigr)\hat{\tau}_{3}

(68)

describes boundary potentials at
j=0
and
j=L+1
.
Using the relation

\displaystyle\check{A}_{V}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{V}(0,j^{\prime})\\
\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{V}(\tilde{L},j^{\prime})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(0,j^{\prime})\\
\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(\tilde{L},j^{\prime})\end{pmatrix}

(69)

with

\displaystyle\check{A}_{V}=\begin{pmatrix}1-\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(0,0)V\hat{\tau}_{3}&-\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(0,\tilde{L})V\hat{\tau}_{3}\\
-\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(\tilde{L},0)V\hat{\tau}_{3}&1-\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(\tilde{L},\tilde{L})V\hat{\tau}_{3}\end{pmatrix},

(70)

and
\tilde{L}=L+1
,
we obtain the closed-form expression:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{V}(j,j^{\prime})=\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j,j^{\prime})+\left(\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j,0),\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j,\tilde{L})\right)\begin{pmatrix}V\hat{\tau}_{3}&0\\
0&V\hat{\tau}_{3}\end{pmatrix}A_{V}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(0,j^{\prime})\\
\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(\tilde{L},j^{\prime})\end{pmatrix}.

(71)

Taking the hard-wall limit, we obtain the Green’s function for the finite topological superconductor for
1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq L
:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}(j,j^{\prime})=\lim_{V\to\infty}\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{V}(j,j^{\prime})\;=\;\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j,j^{\prime})-\left(\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j,0),\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(j,\tilde{L})\right)\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(0,0)&\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(0,\tilde{L})\\
\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(\tilde{L},0)&\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(\tilde{L},\tilde{L})\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(0,j^{\prime})\\
\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(\tilde{L},j^{\prime})\end{pmatrix}.

(72)

After straightforward algebra, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}(j,j^{\prime})=-iD\left(b_{0}+b_{1}\hat{\tau}_{1}+b_{2}\hat{\tau}_{2}+b_{3}\hat{\tau}_{3}\right),

(73)

where

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{v_{F}\Omega_{k_{F}}}\frac{1}{\omega^{2}\bigl[\cosh^{2}(\kappa\tilde{L})-\cos^{2}(k_{F}\tilde{L})\bigr]+\Delta_{k_{F}}^{2}\sin^{2}(k_{F}\tilde{L})},

(74)

\displaystyle b_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\omega^{3}}{2}\Big[\cos\!\big(k_{F}(j+j^{\prime})\big)\sinh\!\big(\kappa(2\tilde{L}-j-j^{\prime})\big)+\cos\!\big(k_{F}(2\tilde{L}-j-j^{\prime})\big)\sinh\!\big(\kappa(j+j^{\prime})\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\omega^{3}\Big[\cos\!\big(k_{F}(j-j^{\prime})\big)\cosh(\kappa\tilde{L})\sinh\!\big(\kappa(\tilde{L}-|j-j^{\prime}|)\big)+\cos(k_F \tilde{L})\cos\!\big(k_{F}(\tilde{L}-|j-j^{\prime}|)\big)\sinh\!\big(\kappa|j-j^{\prime}|\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\omega\Delta_{k_{F}}^{2}\Big[\sin(k_F j)\sin(k_F j')\sinh\!\big(\kappa(2\tilde{L}-j-j^{\prime})\big)+\sin\!\big(k_{F}(\tilde{L}-j)\big)\sin\!\big(k_{F}(\tilde{L}-j^{\prime})\big)\sinh\!\big(\kappa(j+j^{\prime})\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\quad-\omega\Delta_{k_{F}}^{2}\sin(k_F \tilde{L})\sin\!\big(k_{F}(\tilde{L}-|j-j^{\prime}|)\big)\sinh\!\big(\kappa|j-j^{\prime}|\big),

(75)

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\omega^{2}\Delta_{k_{F}}}{2}\sin\!\big(k_{F}(j-j^{\prime})\big)\Big[\sinh\!\big(\kappa(2\tilde{L}-j-j^{\prime})\big)+\sinh\!\big(\kappa(j+j^{\prime})\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\omega^{2}\Delta_{k_{F}}\sin\!\big(k_{F}(j-j^{\prime})\big)\sinh(\kappa\tilde{L})\cosh\!\big(\kappa(\tilde{L}-|j-j^{\prime}|)\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\Delta_{k_{F}}\Omega_{k_{F}}^{2}\sin(k_F \tilde{L})\,f(j,j^{\prime})\sinh\!\big(\kappa(j-j^{\prime})\big),

(76)

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=\omega\Delta_{k_{F}}\Omega_{k_{F}}\Big[\sin\!\big(k_{F}(\tilde{L}-j)\big)\sin\!\big(k_{F}(\tilde{L}-j^{\prime})\big)\cosh\!\big(\kappa(j+j^{\prime})\big)-\sin(k_F j)\sin(k_F j')\cosh\!\big(\kappa(2\tilde{L}-j-j^{\prime})\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\omega\Delta_{k_{F}}\Omega_{k_{F}}}{2}\Big[\cos\!\big(k_{F}(2\tilde{L}-j-j^{\prime})\big)-\cos\!\big(k_{F}(j+j^{\prime})\big)\Big]\cosh\!\big(\kappa(j-j^{\prime})\big),

(77)

\displaystyle b_{3}

\displaystyle=-i\frac{\omega^{2}\Omega_{k_{F}}}{2}\Big[\sin\!\big(k_{F}(j+j^{\prime})\big)\cosh\!\big(\kappa(2\tilde{L}-j-j^{\prime})\big)+\sin\!\big(k_{F}(2\tilde{L}-j-j^{\prime})\big)\cosh\!\big(\kappa(j+j^{\prime})\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+i\omega\Omega_{k_{F}}\Big[\sin\!\big(k_{F}|j-j^{\prime}|\big)\cosh(\kappa\tilde{L})\cosh\!\big(\kappa(\tilde{L}-|j-j^{\prime}|)\big)+\cos(k_F \tilde{L})\sin\!\big(k_{F}(\tilde{L}-|j-j^{\prime}|)\big)\cosh\!\big(\kappa(j-j^{\prime})\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\quad-i\Delta_{k_{F}}^{2}\Omega_{k_{F}}\sin(k_F \tilde{L})\,f(j,j^{\prime})\cosh\!\big(\kappa(j-j^{\prime})\big).

(78)

and

\displaystyle f(j,j^{\prime})=\begin{cases}2\sin\bigl(k_{F}(\tilde{L}-j)\bigr)\sin(k_F j')&\text{for }j\geq j^{\prime}\\
2\sin(k_F j)\sin\bigl(k_{F}(\tilde{L}-j^{\prime})\bigr)&\text{for }j<j^{\prime}\end{cases}.

(79)

We next focus on the low-frequency regime,

\displaystyle\omega\ll\Delta_{k_{F}},

(80)

and consider a system length satisfying

\displaystyle 1\ll\xi_{0}\ll L,

(81)

where
\xi_{0}=v_{F}/\Delta_{k_{F}}
.
In what follows, we expand the Green’s function up to leading order in

\displaystyle\delta=\frac{\omega}{\Delta_{k_{F}}},\;\frac{1}{\xi_{0}},\;e^{-L/\xi_{0}}.

(82)

In this regime, the prefactor of the Green’s function can be approximated as

\displaystyle D\sim\frac{1}{v_{F}\Delta_{k_{F}}}\frac{4e^{-2L/\xi_{0}}}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}},

(83)

where

\displaystyle E_{M}=2\Delta_{k_{F}}\sin\!\big(k_{F}(L+1)\big)e^{-L/\xi_{0}}.

(84)

For
j=1
, the coefficients reduce to

\displaystyle\begin{split}b_{0}\sim{}&\frac{\omega\Delta_{k_{F}}^{2}}{2}\sin(k_F)\sin(k_Fj')e^{(2L-j^{\prime})/\xi_{0}},\\
b_{1}\sim{}&-\Delta_{k_{F}}^{3}\sin\!\big(k_{F}(L+1)\big)\sin(k_F)\sin(k_F (L+1-j'))e^{j^{\prime}/\xi_{0}},\\
b_{2}\sim{}&-\frac{\omega\Delta_{k_{F}}^{2}}{2}\sin(k_F)\sin(k_Fj')e^{(2L-j^{\prime})/\xi_{0}},\\
b_{3}\sim{}&-i\Delta_{k_{F}}^{3}\sin\!\big(k_{F}(L+1)\big)\sin(k_F)\sin(k_F (L+1-j'))e^{j^{\prime}/\xi_{0}}.\end{split}

(85)

Similarly, the coefficients for
j^{\prime}=L
reduce to

\displaystyle\begin{split}b_{0}\sim{}&\frac{\omega\Delta_{k_{F}}^{2}}{2}\sin(k_F (L+1-j))\sin(k_F)e^{(L+j)/\xi_{0}},\\
b_{1}\sim{}&\Delta_{k_{F}}^{3}\sin\!\big(k_{F}(L+1)\big)\sin(k_Fj)\sin(k_F)e^{(L-j)/\xi_{0}},\\
b_{2}\sim{}&\frac{\omega\Delta_{k_{F}}^{2}}{2}\sin(k_F (L+1-j))\sin(k_F)e^{(L+j)/\xi_{0}},\\
b_{3}\sim{}&-i\Delta_{k_{F}}^{3}\sin\!\big(k_{F}(L+1)\big)\sin(k_Fj)\sin(k_F)e^{(L-j)/\xi_{0}}.\end{split}

(86)

Using these coefficients, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\mathcal{G}}(1,j^{\prime})=&-i\frac{\omega}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}}\frac{2}{\xi_{0}}\sin(k_F)\sin(k_F j')\left(1-\hat{\tau}_{2}\right)e^{-j^{\prime}/\xi_{0}}\\
&-i\frac{E_{M}}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}}\frac{2}{\xi_{0}}\sin(k_F)\sin(k_F (L+1-j'))\left(-\hat{\tau}_{1}-i\hat{\tau}_{3}\right)e^{-(L-j^{\prime})/\xi_{0}},\end{split}

(87)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\mathcal{G}}(j,L)=&-i\frac{\omega}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}}\frac{2}{\xi_{0}}\sin(k_F (L+1-j))\sin(k_F)\left(1+\hat{\tau}_{2}\right)e^{-(L-j)/\xi_{0}}\\
&-i\frac{E_{M}}{\omega^{2}+E_{M}^{2}}\frac{2}{\xi_{0}}\sin(k_F j)\sin(k_F)\left(\hat{\tau}_{1}-i\hat{\tau}_{3}\right)e^{-j/\xi_{0}}.\end{split}

(88)

Appendix B
Detailed derivation of

the scattering matrix

In this section, we compute the scattering matrix of a topological superconductor connected to two normal leads.
The normal leads and the junction interfaces are described by the Hamiltonians given in Eq. (
38
).
We start from a general expression for the scattering matrix
ando_91
,

\displaystyle\check{S}=\check{G}\check{Q}-1,

(89)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\check{G}=\check{G}_{n}+\check{G}_{n}\check{T}_{i}^{\dagger}\left(\check{G}^{-1}_{s}-\check{T}_{i}\check{G}_{n}\check{T}_{i}^{\dagger}\right)^{-1}\!\check{T}_{i}\check{G}_{n},\\
&\check{G}_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{G}_{n}&0\\
0&\hat{G}_{n}\end{array}\right),\quad\hat{G}_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g_{n}&0\\
0&\underline{g}_{n}\end{array}\right),\\
&\check{G}_{s}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{G}(1,1)&\hat{G}(1,L)\\
\hat{G}(L,1)&\hat{G}(L,L)\end{array}\right),\\
&\check{T}_{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-t_{i}\hat{\tau}_{3}&0\\
0&-t_{i}\hat{\tau}_{3}\end{array}\right),\\
\end{split}

(90)

and

\displaystyle\check{Q}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{Q}&0\\
0&\hat{Q}\end{array}\right),\quad\hat{Q}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}q&0\\
0&\underline{q}\end{array}\right),

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}&g_{n}=(E+t\lambda_{+})^{-1},\quad q=t(\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}),\\
&\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{A\pm i\sqrt{4-A^{2}}}{2},\quad A=-\frac{E}{t},\end{split}

(95)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\underline{g}_{n}=(E-t\underline{\lambda}_{+})^{-1},\quad\underline{q}=-t(\underline{\lambda}_{+}-\underline{\lambda}_{-}),\\
&\underline{\lambda}_{\pm}=\frac{\underline{A}\mp i\sqrt{4-\underline{A}^{2}}}{2},\quad\underline{A}=\frac{E}{t}.\end{split}

(96)

The retarded Green’s function
\hat{G}(j,j^{\prime})
is obtained from the Matsubara Green’s function by analytic continuation,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}(j,j^{\prime})\xrightarrow{i\omega\to E+i0^{+}}\hat{G}(j,j^{\prime}).

(97)

For the wide-band limit
E/t\to 0
, the scattering matrix reduces to

\displaystyle\check{S}=1-\frac{2i}{t}\check{T}_{i}^{\dagger}\left(\check{G}^{-1}_{s}+\frac{i}{t}\check{T}_{i}\check{T}_{i}^{\dagger}\right)^{-1}\!\check{T}_{i}.

(98)

In the regime,

\displaystyle E\ll\Delta_{k_{F}},

(99)

and

\displaystyle 1\ll\xi_{0}\ll L,

(100)

the retarded Green’s functions are given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\hat{G}(1,1)=C\frac{E}{E^{2}-E_{M}^{2}}\left(1-\hat{\tau}_{2}\right),\\
&\hat{G}(L,L)=C\frac{E}{E^{2}-E_{M}^{2}}\left(1+\hat{\tau}_{2}\right),\end{split}

(101)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\hat{G}(1,L)=iC\frac{E_{M}}{E^{2}-E_{M}^{2}}\left(-\hat{\tau}_{1}-i\hat{\tau}_{3}\right),\\
&\hat{G}(L,1)=iC\frac{E_{M}}{E^{2}-E_{M}^{2}}\left(\hat{\tau}_{1}-i\hat{\tau}_{3}\right).\end{split}

(102)

Further assuming the low-energy limit,

\displaystyle E\ll E_{M}\ll\Delta_{k_{F}},

(103)

we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&G(1,1)\propto\frac{E}{E_{M}^{2}}\frac{\Delta_{k_{F}}}{t}\sim 0,\\
&G(L,L)\propto\frac{E}{E_{M}^{2}}\frac{\Delta_{k_{F}}}{t}\sim 0,\end{split}

(104)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&G(1,L)\propto\frac{1}{E_{M}}\frac{\Delta_{k_{F}}}{t},\\
&G(L,L)\propto\frac{1}{E_{M}}\frac{\Delta_{k_{F}}}{t}.\end{split}

(105)

Thus, in the tunneling regime satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{t_{i}^{2}}{t}\ll\left(\frac{1}{E_{M}}\frac{\Delta_{k_{F}}}{t}\right)^{-1}\;\Rightarrow\;\left(\frac{t_{i}}{t}\right)^{2}\ll\frac{E_{M}}{\Delta_{k_{F}}},

(106)

we find

\displaystyle\left(\check{G}^{-1}_{s}+\frac{i}{t}\check{T}_{i}\check{T}_{i}^{\dagger}\right)^{-1}\sim\check{G}_{s}.

(107)

Consequently, the scattering matrix in this regime reduces to

\displaystyle\check{S}\sim 1-\frac{2i}{t}\check{T}_{i}^{\dagger}\check{G}_{s}\check{T}_{i}.

(108)
