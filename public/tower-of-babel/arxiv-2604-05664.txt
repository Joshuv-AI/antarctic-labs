The Pandharipande–Thomas rationality conjecture for superpositive curve classes on projective complex 3-manifolds
Reginald Anderson, Dominic Joyce

Abstract
Let X X be a projective complex 3-manifold. An effective curve class β ∈ H 2 ​ ( X , ℤ ) \beta\in H_{2}(X,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}) is called positive if c 1 ​ ( X ) ⋅ β > 0 c_{1}(X)\cdot\beta>0 , and superpositive if all the effective summands of β \beta are positive. If X X is Fano then all curve classes are superpositive. The second author [ 28 ] developed a theory of enumerative invariants in abelian categories and wall-crossing formulae. We use this theory to prove conjectures by Pandharipande and Thomas [ 44 , 40 ] on the rationality and poles of generating functions of Pandharipande–Thomas invariants of X X with descendent insertions, for superpositive curve classes.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth, connected projective 3-fold over
{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}
. We will be interested in the homology classes of algebraic curves on

Let

\beta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}[C_{i}]

\gamma
a

\beta

\beta=\gamma+\delta

\beta

irreducible

\beta

We call an effective curve class
\beta

. We call

if every factor of
\beta

\beta
, this also implies that every factor of
\beta

If

is

The next definition follows Pandharipande–Thomas
[
43
]

Definition 1.2

Let

{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}

(1.1)

We combine these into a generating function

PT_{\beta}\bigl(\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\tau_{k_{i}}(\eta_{i}),q\bigr)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}}PT_{\beta,n}\bigl(\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\tau_{k_{i}}(\eta_{i})\bigr)q^{n}\quad\text{in ${\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}[[q]][q^{-1}]$.}

Definition 2.1
.

Let
V_{*}=\bigoplus_{n\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}}V_{n}
be a graded
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}
-vector space. Form the vector space
V_{*}[[z]][z^{-1}]
of
V_{*}
-valued Laurent series in a formal variable
z
, and make it
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}
-graded by declaring
\deg z=-2
. A
field
on
V_{*}
is a
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}
-linear map
V_{*}\rightarrow V_{*}[[z]][z^{-1}]
, graded of some degree. The set of all fields on
V_{*}
is denoted
{\mathbin{\cal F}}(V_{*})
and is considered as a graded
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}
-vector space by declaring
{\mathbin{\cal F}}(V_{*})_{n}
to be the set of degree
n
fields
V_{*}\rightarrow V_{*}[[z]][z^{-1}]
for
n\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}
.

A
graded vertex algebra

(V_{*},{\mathbin{\mathbbm{1}}},D,Y)
over
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}
is a
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}
-graded
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}
-vector space
V_{*}
with an identity element
{\mathbin{\mathbbm{1}}}\in V_{0}
, a
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}
-linear
translation operator

D:V_{*}\rightarrow V_{*+2}
of degree 2, and a grading-preserving
state-field correspondence

Y:V_{*}\rightarrow{\mathbin{\cal F}}(V_{*})_{*}
written
Y(u,z)v=\sum_{n\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}}u_{n}(v)z^{-n-1}
, where
u_{n}
maps
V_{*}\rightarrow V_{*+a-2n-2}
for
u\in V_{a}
, satisfying:

(i)

Y({\mathbin{\mathbbm{1}}},z)v=v
for all
v\in V_{*}
.

(ii)

Y(v,z){\mathbin{\mathbbm{1}}}=e^{zD}v
for all
v\in V_{*}
.

(iii)

For all
u\in V_{a}
and
v\in V_{b}
, there exists
N\gg 0
such that for all
w\in V_{*}

(z_{1}-z_{2})^{N}\bigl(Y(u,z_{1})Y(v,z_{2})w-(-1)^{ab}Y(v,z_{2})Y(u,z_{1})w\bigr)=0\;\>\text{in $V_{*}[[z_{1}^{\pm 1},z_{2}^{\pm 1}]]$.}

Definition 2.2
.

A
graded Lie algebra
over
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}
is a pair
(V_{*},[\,,\,])
, where
V_{*}=\bigoplus_{a\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}}V_{a}
is a graded
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}
-vector space, and
[\,,\,]:V_{*}\times V_{*}\rightarrow V_{*}
is a
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}
-bilinear map called the
Lie bracket
, which is graded (that is,
[\,,\,]
maps
V_{a}\times V_{b}\rightarrow V_{a+b}
for all
a,b\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}
), such that for all
a,b,c\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
u\in V_{a}
,
v\in V_{b}
and
w\in V_{c}
we have:

\displaystyle[v,u]=(-1)^{ab+1}[u,v],

\displaystyle(-1)^{ca}[[u,v],w]+(-1)^{ab}[[v,w],u]+(-1)^{bc}[[w,u],v]=0.

Remark 2.8
.

(a)

shows that except in

\mathop{\rm ch}\nolimits_{i}(\alpha)\cdot\eta\neq 0
and
\mathop{\rm ch}\nolimits_{i}(\beta)\cdot\eta=0,
and choose an arbitrary

with

I_{e_{\eta}=0}\bigl([u,v]\bigr)=\sum_{0\leqslant\penalty k\leqslant\penalty(a+b)/2}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}D^{k}\bigl[\bigl(I_{e_{\eta}=0}(u)\bigr)_{k}(v^{\prime})\bigr].

(2.13)

We can use this theorem to lift calculations in the Lie algebra

A useful case in Theorem

is

and

2.5
Equivariant (co)homology of stacks

Let
G
be a linear algebraic group over
{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}
and
S
be a projective complex manifold, or a
{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}
-scheme, or an Artin
{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}
-stack, or a higher
{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}
-stack, with a
G
-action. Then we can define the
G
-
equivariant cohomology groups

H_{G}^{k}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
for
k=0,1,2,\ldots,
and the
G
-
equivariant homology groups

H^{G}_{k}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
for
k\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}
.

Here we must work with a particular kind of equivariant (co)homology of stacks, explained in
[
27
]
and
[
28
]
. If
S
is a
{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}
-stack with an action of
G
then the equivariant cohomology
H_{G}^{*}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
is just
H^{*}([S/G],{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
, the ordinary cohomology of the quotient stack
[S/G]
.

However, equivariant homology
H^{G}_{*}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
is more complicated. We define
H^{G}_{k}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
for all
k\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}
, not just for
k\geqslant 0
. It is a module over
H_{G}^{-*}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
via the cap product
\cap:H^{G}_{k}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\times H_{G}^{l}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\rightarrow H^{G}_{l-k}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
. We write
H_{G}^{-*}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
here rather than
H_{G}^{*}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
to make clear that cohomology gradings are subtracted from homology gradings. Since the projection
\pi:S\rightarrow*
induces an algebra morphism
\pi^{*}:H_{G}^{*}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\rightarrow H_{G}^{*}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
,
H^{G}_{*}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
is also a module over
H_{G}^{-*}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
. If the
G
-action on
S
is trivial then

H^{G}_{k}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\cong\prod_{j\geqslant 0}H^{j}_{G}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\otimes_{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}H_{j+k}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}).

(2.15)

Thus,
H^{G}_{*}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
behaves like homology for
S
, but like cohomology for
G
.

We will study equivariant (co)homology using spectral sequences. See McCleary
[
37
]
for a good introduction to these. There is a first quadrant cohomology spectral sequence with
E_{2}
page
E_{2}^{p,q}=H_{G}^{p}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\otimes_{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}H^{q}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
converging to
H_{G}^{p+q}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
, coming from the fibration
[S/G]\rightarrow[*/G]
with fibre
S
. The differentials on the
k^{\rm th}
page act as
d_{k}^{p,q}:E_{k}^{p,q}\rightarrow E_{k}^{p+k,q+1-k}
. The
(k+1)^{\rm st}
page is obtained by taking cohomology on the
k^{\rm th}
page by

E_{k+1}^{p,q}=\frac{\mathop{\rm Ker}(d_{k}^{p,q}:E_{k}^{p,q}\longrightarrow E_{k}^{p+k,q+1-k})}{\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(d_{k}^{p-k,q-1+k}:E_{k}^{p-k,q-1+k}\longrightarrow E_{k}^{p,q})}\,.

(2.16)

They converge to the
\infty
-page
E_{\infty}^{p,q}
of the spectral sequence, that is,
E_{\infty}^{p,q}=E_{k}^{p,q}
for
k\gg 0
. Then
H^{k}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
has a filtration
H^{k}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})=F^{0}H^{k}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\supseteq F^{1}H^{k}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\supseteq\cdots
, with
\bigcap_{p\geqslant 0}F^{p}H^{k}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})=0
, and isomorphisms for
p\geqslant 0

\frac{F^{p}H^{k}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})}{F^{p+1}H^{k}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})}\cong E_{\infty}^{p,k-p}.

Similarly, there is a second quadrant homology spectral sequence with
E^{2}
page
E^{2}_{p,q}=H_{G}^{-p}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\otimes_{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}H_{q}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
converging to
H^{G}_{p+q}(S)
. The differentials on the
k^{\rm th}
page act as
d^{k}_{p,q}:E^{k}_{p,q}\rightarrow E^{k}_{p-k,q-1+k}
. The
(k+1)^{\rm st}
page is obtained by taking cohomology on the
k^{\rm th}
page by

E^{k+1}_{p,q}=\frac{\mathop{\rm Ker}(d^{k}_{p,q}:E^{k}_{p,q}\longrightarrow E^{k}_{p-k,q-1+k})}{\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(d^{k}_{p+k,q+1-k}:E^{k}_{p+k,q+1-k}\longrightarrow E^{k}_{p,q})}\,.

(2.17)

They converge to the
\infty
-page
E^{\infty}_{p,q}
of the spectral sequence, that is,
E^{\infty}_{p,q}=E^{k}_{p,q}
for
k\gg 0
. Then
H_{k}^{G}(S)
has a filtration
H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})=F^{0}H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\supseteq F^{-1}H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\supseteq\cdots
, with
\bigcap_{p\leqslant\penalty 0}F^{p}H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})=0
, and isomorphisms for
p\leqslant\penalty 0

\frac{F^{p}H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})}{F^{p-1}H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})}\cong E^{\infty}_{p,k-p}.

(2.18)

Since
E^{\infty}_{p,k-p}=0
if
k-p\leqslant\penalty 0
, we see that if
k<0
then

H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})=F^{0}H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})=F^{-1}H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})=\cdots=F^{k}H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}).

(2.19)

Both spectral sequences have graded actions of
H^{*}_{G}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
preserving all the structure. That is, there are
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}
-bilinear multiplication maps
H^{n}_{G}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\times E_{k}^{p,q}\rightarrow E_{k}^{p+n,q}
and
H^{n}_{G}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\times E^{k}_{p,q}\rightarrow E^{k}_{p-n,q}
for all
k=2,3,\ldots,\infty
and
n,p,q
, which when
k=2
come from multiplication
H^{n}_{G}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\times H^{\pm p}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\rightarrow H^{n\pm p}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
and the identity on
H^{q}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
or
H_{q}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
. The natural multiplication
H^{n}_{G}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\times H^{k}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\rightarrow H^{k+n}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
maps
F^{p}H^{k}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\rightarrow F^{p+n}H^{k+n}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
, and the natural multiplication
H^{n}_{G}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\times H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\rightarrow H_{k-n}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
maps
F^{p}H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\rightarrow F^{p-n}H_{k-n}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
. These multiplication maps commute with the
d_{k}^{p,q},d^{k}_{p,q}
and are compatible with the isomorphisms (
2.16
)–(
2.18
).

Many natural operations on
H^{*}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}),H^{G}_{*}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
are compatible with the filtrations
(F^{p}H^{k}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}))_{p\geqslant 0},(F^{p}H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}))_{p\leqslant\penalty 0}
. For example, if
\Phi:S\rightarrow T
is a
G
-equivariant morphism then
\Phi^{*}:H^{k}_{G}(T,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\rightarrow H^{k}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
maps
F^{p}H^{k}_{G}(T,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\rightarrow F^{p}H^{k}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
and
\Phi_{*}:H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\!\rightarrow\!H_{k}^{G}(T,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
maps
F^{p}H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\!\rightarrow\!F^{p}H_{k}^{G}(T,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
. The cap product
H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\times H^{l}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\rightarrow H_{k-l}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
maps
F^{p}H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\times F^{p^{\prime}}H^{l}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\rightarrow F^{p-p^{\prime}}H_{k-l}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
.

For
N\geqslant 0
, define the (
N
-)
truncated
G
-equivariant homology group

H_{k}^{G,\leqslant\penalty N}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})=\frac{H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})}{F^{-N-1}H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})}\,.

Then (
2.19
) implies that
H_{k}^{G,\leqslant\penalty N}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})=0
for
k<-N
. Operations on
H_{*}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
such as pushforwards and cap product with classes in
H^{*}_{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
descend to
H_{*}^{G,\leqslant\penalty N}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
. The definition of equivariant homology in
[
27
,
28
]
implies that
(F^{p}H_{k}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}))_{p\leqslant\penalty 0}
is a
complete
filtration, and
H^{G}_{*}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
is the inverse limit
\varprojlim_{N\rightarrow\infty}H^{G,\leqslant\penalty N}_{*}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
. In the action of
H^{*}_{G}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
on
H_{k}^{G,\leqslant\penalty N}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
, multiplication by
H^{>N}_{G}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
gives zero. Thus, we can roughly think of
H_{*}^{G,\leqslant\penalty N}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
as being the result of killing the action of
H^{>N}_{G}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
on
H_{*}^{G}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
. For example, if the
G
-action on
S
is trivial then modifying (
2.15
), we have

H^{G,\leqslant\penalty N}_{k}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{N}H^{j}_{G}(*,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})\otimes_{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}H_{j+k}(S,{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}).

2.6
Extension of §
2.2
–§
2.4
to equivariant (co)homology

In the situation of §
2.2
–§
2.4
, suppose
G
is a linear algebraic group over
{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}
which acts on the smooth projective
m
-fold
X
. Then
G
also acts on

and on the moduli stacks
{\mathbin{\cal M}},{\mathbin{\cal M}}^{\rm pl}
. In
[
27
]
the second author generalizes the vertex algebra and Lie algebra structures on

in §
2.2
–§
2.3
to

H_{*}^{G}({\mathbin{\cal M}}),H_{*}^{G}({\mathbin{\cal M}}^{\rm pl})
, and
H_{*}^{G,\leqslant\penalty N}({\mathbin{\cal M}}),H_{*}^{G,\leqslant\penalty N}({\mathbin{\cal M}}^{\rm pl})
, as in §
2.5
.

For each
N\geqslant 0
, Definition

for the vertex algebra structure on

extends without change to
H_{*}^{G,\leqslant\penalty N}({\mathbin{\cal M}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
, using truncated equivariant homology
H_{*}^{G,\leqslant\penalty N}(\cdots)
and equivariant cohomology

throughout.

To apply the same construction to the full equivariant homology

, there is a subtlety. Consider the definition (
2.4
) of the vertex algebra operation
Y(u,z)v
in
H_{*}^{G}({\mathbin{\cal M}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
. Suppose

and

. Then the coefficient of

in

is

\displaystyle[z^{k}]\bigl\{Y(u,z)v\bigr\}=

(2.20)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geqslant\max(0,\chi(\alpha,\beta)+\chi(\beta,\alpha)-k)\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!}\begin{aligned} &(-1)^{\chi(\alpha,\beta)}\bigl(\Phi_{\alpha,\beta}\circ(\Psi_{\alpha}\times{\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits}_{{\mathbin{\cal M}}_{\beta}})\bigr)_{*}\bigl(t^{i+k-\chi(\alpha,\beta)-\chi(\beta,\alpha)}\boxtimes{}\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad((u\boxtimes v)\cap c_{i}((\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}\mathit{xt}}\nolimits_{\alpha,\beta}^{\bullet})^{\vee}\oplus\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}^{*}(\mathop{{\mathcal{E}}\mathit{xt}}\nolimits^{\bullet}_{\beta,\alpha})))\bigr).\end{aligned}

Here the term
(u\boxtimes v)\cap c_{i}(\cdots)
in the final line lies in
H^{G}_{a+b-2i}({\mathbin{\cal M}}_{\alpha}\times{\mathbin{\cal M}}_{\beta},{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
. In non-equivariant homology this is zero if
i>(a+b)/2
, so that (
2.20
) is a finite sum. In truncated equivariant homology
H^{G,\leqslant\penalty N}_{a+b-2i}({\mathbin{\cal M}}_{\alpha}\times{\mathbin{\cal M}}_{\beta},{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}})
it is zero if

, so again (
2.20
) is a finite sum. But for

we may have

as

, so (

explained in §

Thus, the vertex algebra operations in Definition

are well defined for

we must replace the sum over

Definition 2.10
.

Let
{\mathbin{\cal A}}
be an abelian category, and
K_{0}({\mathbin{\cal A}})
its Grothendieck group. Suppose we are given a surjective quotient
K_{0}({\mathbin{\cal A}})\twoheadrightarrow K({\mathbin{\cal A}})

\llbracket E\rrbracket\in K({\mathbin{\cal A}})
for the class of
E\in{\mathbin{\cal A}}
. Suppose
0\in{\mathbin{\cal A}}
is the only object in class
0\in K({\mathbin{\cal A}})
. The
positive cone

C({\mathbin{\cal A}})\subset K({\mathbin{\cal A}})\setminus\{0\}
is
C({\mathbin{\cal A}})=\bigl\{\llbracket E\rrbracket:0\neq E\in{\mathbin{\cal A}}\bigr\}
.

Let
(T,\leq)
be a totally ordered set and
\tau:C({\mathbin{\cal A}})\rightarrow T
be a map. We call
(\tau,T,\leq)
a
weak stability condition
on
{\mathbin{\cal A}}
if for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in C({\mathbin{\cal A}})
with
\beta=\alpha+\gamma
, either

\tau(\alpha)\leq\tau(\beta)\leq\tau(\gamma)
, or
\tau(\alpha)\geq\tau(\beta)\geq\tau(\gamma)
.

We call
(\tau,T,\leq)
a
stability condition
if for all such
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
, either

\tau(\alpha)<\tau(\beta)<\tau(\gamma)
, or
\tau(\alpha)>\tau(\beta)>\tau(\gamma)
, or
\tau(\alpha)=\tau(\beta)=\tau(\gamma)
.

Let
(\tau,T,\leq)
be a weak stability condition. An object
E
of
{\mathbin{\cal A}}
is called:

(i)

\tau
-stable
if
\tau([E^{\prime}])<\tau([E/E^{\prime}])
for all subobjects
E^{\prime}\subset E
with
E^{\prime}\neq 0,E
.

(ii)

\tau
-semistable
if
\tau([E^{\prime}])\!\leq\!\tau([E/E^{\prime}])
for all
E^{\prime}\subset E
with
E^{\prime}\neq 0,E
.

(iii)

\tau
-unstable
if it is not
\tau
-semistable.

We now define universal combinatorial coefficients
S,U,\tilde{U}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n};\tau,\tilde{\tau})
which appear in wall-crossing formulae for enumerative invariants under change of stability condition. They first appeared in the second author’s series
[

,

on motivic invariants counting

[

]

[

]
and the second author
[

]
. The next definition comes from
[

, §4.1]
, but with notation changed as in
[

Definition 2.11
.

Let
{\mathbin{\cal A}}
be an abelian category, and choose
K_{0}({\mathbin{\cal A}})\twoheadrightarrow K({\mathbin{\cal A}})
as in Definition
2.10
. Let

(\tau,T,\leqslant\penalty),(\tilde{\tau},\tilde{T},\leqslant\penalty)
be weak stability conditions on

{\mathbin{\cal A}}
.

Let
n\geqslant 1
and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in C({\mathbin{\cal A}})
. If for all

i=1,\ldots,n-1
we have either

(a)

\tau(\alpha_{i})\leqslant\penalty\tau(\alpha_{i+1})
and

\tilde{\tau}(\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{i})>\tilde{\tau}(\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n})
, or

(b)

\tau(\alpha_{i})>\tau(\alpha_{i+1})
and
\tilde{\tau}(\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{i})\leqslant\penalty\tilde{\tau}(\alpha_{i+1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n})

then define
S(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n};\tau,\tilde{\tau})=(-1)^{r}
, where

is the number of

satisfying (a). Otherwise define

. Now
define

2.8
One-dimensional Donaldson–Thomas invariants

Donaldson–Thomas invariants
count moduli stacks
{\mathbin{\cal M}}_{\alpha}^{\rm ss}(\tau)
of
\tau
-semistable coherent sheaves
F
with
\llbracket F\rrbracket\!=\!\alpha\!\in\!K_{0}^{\text{\rm s-t}}(X)
on a smooth (quasi)projective 3-fold
X
. They are only defined under extra assumptions on
X,\alpha
. One problem in doing this is that the natural obstruction theory
\phi:{\mathbin{\cal E}}^{\bullet}\rightarrow{\mathbin{\mathbb{L}}}_{{\mathbin{\cal M}}_{\alpha}^{\rm ss}(\tau)}
on
{\mathbin{\cal M}}_{\alpha}^{\rm ss}(\tau)
is perfect in
[-2,1]
. To define a Behrend–Fantechi virtual class
[
5
]
we need it to be perfect in
[-1,1]
, and this happens only if
\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{3}(F,F)=0
for all
[F]\in{\mathbin{\cal M}}_{\alpha}^{\rm ss}(\tau)
.

There are actually (at least)
three different kinds
of Donaldson–Thomas invariants, which deal with the
\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{3}(F,F)
terms in different ways:

(i)

If
X
is a Calabi–Yau 3-fold then
\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{3}(F,F)\cong\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(F,F)^{*}
by Serre duality. If
{\mathbin{\cal M}}_{\alpha}^{\rm st}(\tau)={\mathbin{\cal M}}_{\alpha}^{\rm ss}(\tau)
this gives canonical isomorphisms
\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{3}(F,F)\cong{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}
for all
[F]\in{\mathbin{\cal M}}_{\alpha}^{\rm ss}(\tau)
, and the
\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{3}(F,F)
terms can be deleted from the obstruction theory. See Thomas
[
52
]
, Joyce–Song
[
29
]
, and Kontsevich–Soibelman
[
32
]
for more details.

(ii)

If
X
is any smooth projective 3-fold and we consider moduli stacks of rank 1 torsion-free sheaves
F
with fixed determinant
\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits F={\mathcal{O}}_{X}
, we can define the obstruction theory using trace-free Ext groups
\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{i}(F,F)_{0}
, and
\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{3}(F,F)_{0}=0
in this case. See Maulik–Nekrasov–Okounkov–Pandharipande
[
34
,
35
]
for more.

(iii)

If
X
is a Fano 3-fold and
\mathop{\rm dim}\nolimits\alpha>0
, or more generally if
X,\alpha
satisfy some positivity condition involving
c_{1}(X)
, then
\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{3}(F,F)=0
for all
[F]
in
{\mathbin{\cal M}}_{\alpha}^{\rm ss}(\tau)
, and we can define virtual classes using the natural obstruction theory. See Thomas
[
52
]
and the second author
[
28
]
for more.

We should think of these as
different theories
, not one theory. The dimensions of the virtual classes in (i)–(iii) are different, and for (i),(iii) the wall-crossing formulae under change of stability condition in
[
28
,
29
]
are different.

counting
one-dimensional

3.2
Piecewise quasi-polynomial functions

We develop some material we will need in §
3.3
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
V
be a
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}
-vector space. A function
f:{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}\rightarrow V
is a
quasi-polynomial
if there exist
d\geqslant 1
and polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{d}\in V[x]
such that

f(n)=P_{a}(n)\qquad\text{whenever }n\equiv a\mod d.

More generally, a function
f:{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{k}\rightarrow V
is a
quasi-polynomial
if there exist
d\geqslant 1
and
polynomials
P_{\boldsymbol{a}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in V[x_{1},\dots,x_{k}]
for
\boldsymbol{a}\in\{1,2,\ldots,d\}^{k}
, such that

f(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})=P_{\boldsymbol{a}}(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})\quad\text{whenever }(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})\equiv\boldsymbol{a}\mod d.

Quasi-polynomials
f,g:{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{k}\rightarrow V
are closed under addition, that is,
f+g
is also quasi-polynomial, taking lowest common multiples of periods.

A
chamber decomposition
of
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{k}
is a finite partition

{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{k}=A_{1}\amalg\cdots\amalg A_{N}

in which each chamber
A_{i}
is cut out by finitely many rational affine equalities and inequalities of the form

C_{1}n_{1}+\cdots+C_{k}n_{k}\ \square\ D,\quad\square\in\{<,\leqslant\penalty,=,\geqslant,>\},\quad C_{1},\dots,C_{k},D\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}.

(3.10)

A function
f:{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{k}\rightarrow V
is called
piecewise quasi-polynomial
if there is a chamber decomposition
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{k}=A_{1}\amalg\cdots\amalg A_{N}
and quasi-polynomials
g_{1},\ldots,g_{N}:{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{k}\rightarrow V
such that
f|_{A_{i}}=g_{i}|_{A_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,N
. Piecewise quasi-polynomial functions
f,g:{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{k}\rightarrow V
are closed under addition.

The next proposition is a standard result in Ehrhart theory. See, for example, Beck and Robins
[
4
, Chs. 3–4]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
Q\subset{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{k}
be a chamber cut out by finitely many rational equalities and inequalities of the form
(
3.10
)
. Suppose that
Q\cap\bigl\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{k}:x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}=n\bigr\}
is bounded for all
n\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}
. Let
V
be a
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}
-vector space and
w\in V[n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}]
. Then the function
g:{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}\rightarrow V
given by

g(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{l}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{k}:\\
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})\in Q,\;n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}=n\end{subarray}}w(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})

is piecewise quasi-polynomial in
n
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
V
be a
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Q}}}
-vector space and
F:{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{k}\rightarrow V
be piecewise quasi-polynomial. Assume that for each
n\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}
one has
F(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})=0
for all but finitely many
(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})
with
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}=n
. Define
H:{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}\rightarrow V
by

H(n)=\sum_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}=n}F(n_{1},\dots,n_{k}).

Then
H
is piecewise quasi-polynomial in
n
.

Proof.

Choose a chamber decomposition

{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{k}=A_{1}\amalg\cdots\amalg A_{N}

such that
F|_{A_{i}}=G_{i}|_{A_{i}}
for quasi-polynomial
G_{1},\ldots,G_{N}:{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{k}\rightarrow V
. Then

H(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}H_{i}(n),\qquad H_{i}(n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})\in A_{i}:\\
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}=n\end{subarray}}G_{i}(n_{1},\dots,n_{k}).

It therefore suffices to prove that each
H_{i}
is piecewise quasi-polynomial.

Fix
i=1,\ldots,N
. There exist
d\geqslant 1
and polynomials
P_{\boldsymbol{a}}\in V[n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}]
for
\boldsymbol{a}\in\{1,2,\ldots,d\}^{k}
with
G_{i}(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})=P_{\boldsymbol{a}}(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})
whenever
(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})\allowbreak\equiv\boldsymbol{a}\mod d
. Hence

\displaystyle H_{i}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})\in A_{i}:\\
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}=n\end{subarray}}G_{i}(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{l}\boldsymbol{a}\in\{1,2,\ldots,d\}^{k}:\\
n\equiv a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}\!\!\mod d\end{subarray}}\,\,\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{l}(m_{1},\ldots m_{k})\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{k}\cap\frac{1}{d}(A_{i}-\boldsymbol{a}):\\
m_{1}+\cdots+m_{k}=(n-a_{1}-\cdots-a_{k})/d\end{subarray}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!}P_{\boldsymbol{a}}(a_{1}+dm_{1},\ldots,a_{k}+dm_{k}).

By Proposition
3.4
, the final sum is piecewise quasi-polynomial in
(n-a_{1}-\cdots-a_{k})/d
, and so in
n
. Thus
H_{i}
is a finite sum of piecewise quasi-polynomials, and is piecewise quasi-polynomial.
∎
