Integrating machine learning and pathway modelling to explore factors associated with chronic post-surgical pain and quality of life: a secondary observational analysis of the ENIGMA-II trial
Authors: Mohammad Asghari-Jafarabadi, Paul S. Myles, Tim G. Coulson, Noam Winter, Tomás B. Corcoran, Matthew T.V. Chan, William K.K. Wu, Philip Peyton, Kate Leslie, Andrew Forbes
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChronic post-surgical pain (CPSP) is a common long-term complication with multifactorial contributors, and improved risk stratification remains a clinical priority. This study aimed to identify factors associated with CPSP and related outcomes and to explore hypothesised multivariable pathways using a hybrid analytical approach.MethodsWe performed a secondary observational analysis of 2999 participants from the Evaluation of Nitrous Oxide in the Gas Mixture for Anaesthesia (ENIGMA)-II CPSP substudy with 12-month postoperative assessment. Grouped lasso (GLASSO) was used for penalised feature selection across prespecified predictor groups, and partial least squares structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM) was applied within a theory-informed framework to explore associations among measured domains and outcomes. Analyses were exploratory and did not establish causal effects.ResultsGLASSO models showed moderate discrimination/explanatory power (CPSP area under the curve 0.69, 95% confidence interval 0.64–0.74; EQ-5D R2=0.34; pain severity R2=0.29; pain interference R2=0.24). In PLS-SEM for CPSP, presurgical pain showed the strongest association (B=0.348), with smaller associations for demographics (B=0.169) and surgical aspects (B=0.091) and lifestyle patterns showed a small inverse association (B=−0.051). For EQ-5D, lifestyle was negatively associated (B=−0.248), and NSAID-related treatment patterns showed a small association (B=0.079). No clear associations were observed between dexamethasone or NSAID treatment patterns and CPSP.ConclusionsA GLASSO–PLS-SEM workflow provided an exploratory approach to identifying key features and mapping hypothesised multivariable associations in CPSP-related outcomes. External validation and richer psychosocial/structural measures are needed before clinical translation.Trial registrationClinicalTrials.gov NCT00430989.

Chronic post-surgical pain (CPSP) is a frequent and challenging complication that arises after surgery.1,2 While the mechanisms underlying the transition from acute to chronic pain are not fully understood,3 evidence suggests that tissue damage during surgery, along with the resulting inflammatory and nociceptive responses, plays a central role in this process.3,4

CPSP's multifactorial nature requires sophisticated modelling techniques to capture complex relationships between clinical, psychological, and socioeconomic factors.5,6 Clinical prediction models can identify patients at high-risk and guide preventive strategies,7,8 yet many are susceptible to bias and lack generalisability.9,10 Advanced statistical techniques are crucial to enhance clinical utility.11,12 Integrating feature selection methods with causal modelling has gained increasing attention for its potential to improve model accuracy and generalisability.13, 14, 15

Machine learning methods such as the (group) least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (GLASSO) are penalised regression approaches that add a tuning (penalty) parameter to shrink coefficients, allowing a slightly less perfect fit in the training data to improve prediction and reduce overfitting in new data. Our study extends this idea by selecting or shrinking groups of related predictors together, helping identify key variables associated with CPSP (see Appendix). Pathway modelling can then be used to explore how selected variables may relate to each other and to outcomes,16,17 complementing machine learning by examining multivariable patterns in complex clinical data.18 Partial least squares structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM) is a regression-based structural modelling approach that estimates hypothesised relationships between observed variables and latent constructs and can be used to evaluate theory-informed pathway structures linking predictors to patient-reported outcomes.19,20

Methodologically, this study applies a hybrid approach integrating GLASSO and PLS-SEM to support exploratory assessment of complex relationships among multiple predictors and patient-reported outcomes. This framework is intended to aid modelling in high-dimensional clinical datasets, particularly where interrelated domains and latent constructs may be relevant.21,22

Our literature review identified limited work examining hypothesised pathway structures underlying CPSP and related outcomes (pain severity, pain interference, and quality of life). Accordingly, this study aimed to: (1) use GLASSO for feature selection to identify key predictors associated with CPSP outcomes, (2) specify a theory-informed conceptual pathway model, and (3) apply PLS-SEM to explore relationships between observed indicators, latent constructs, and outcomes within this dataset.

Methods

This study is a secondary, observational analysis of data from the Evaluation of Nitrous Oxide in the Gas Mixture for Anaesthesia (ENIGMA)-II CPSP substudy data. We assessed CPSP, pain severity, pain interference, and EQ-5D at 12 months and applied a two-stage exploratory modelling workflow: (1) GLASSO for outcome-specific feature selection with cross-validated penalty tuning, followed by (2) PLS-SEM within a theory-informed pathway framework to explore multivariable associations among latent constructs and outcomes. Despite the use of pathway modelling techniques, this analysis does not establish causal effects of interventions.

The original ENIGMA-II randomised trial NCT00430989 was conducted to assess the effects of nitrous oxide in patients with known or suspected coronary artery disease undergoing major surgery.4,23 The ENIGMA-II nested CPSP study examined the relationship between nitrous oxide exposure and CPSP,23 including 19 hospitals across five countries, with pain outcomes and general health status assessed 12 months after surgery.23 Ethics approval was obtained from all participating centres, and written informed consent was provided by all patients. Patients or members of the public were not involved in the conceptualisation, design, conduct, or dissemination plans of this research. No additional ethics approval was required for this secondary analysis of de-identified ENIGMA-II data under the original approvals and participant consent. This analysis followed a prespecified analytic plan developed by the study team before the current modelling, with GLASSO tuning and PLS-SEM specification conducted within an exploratory framework.

This study was reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guideline and, where applicable, the Transparent Reporting of a multivariable prediction model for Individual Prognosis Or Diagnosis (TRIPOD) guideline.

Primary outcome: CPSP

At 12 months post-surgery, patients were contacted by telephone and asked whether they had experienced wound pain in the previous 3 months that had not been present before surgery. If yes, pain intensity was rated on a 0–10 numeric rating scale. Patients completed the modified Brief Pain Inventory (mBPI) and Neuropathic Pain Questionnaire.24 CPSP was defined according to the International Association for the Study of Pain criteria as pain at the surgical site developing or worsening after surgery and persisting for at least 3 months. Neuropathic pain was classified according to the Neuropathic Pain Questionnaire criteria.

Secondary outcome: pain severity and pain interference

The mBPI is a nine-item questionnaire assessing pain severity and functional impact, with scores ranging from 0 to 100, where higher scores indicate greater severity or interference. Preoperative chronic pain and opioid therapy were recorded to allow adjustment for pre-existing pain conditions in the analyses.

Secondary outcome: quality of life

General health status was assessed using the EQ-5D questionnaire, which covers mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression. The EQ-5D utility score ranges from 0 to 1.0, with 1.0 representing full health.25

Dexamethasone was administered to patients as a single intravenous dose of 8 mg, given immediately before surgical incision. The primary indication for administration was prophylaxis against postoperative nausea and vomiting. This regimen was also associated with reductions in acute postoperative pain. Follow-up assessments used standardised, uniform procedures across centres, with interviewer standardisation to support consistent outcome ascertainment.

Main predictors

All candidate predictors were prespecified based on clinical relevance and prior ENIGMA-II CPSP reporting and are summarised in Supplementary Table S1. Age was analysed as a categorical variable (Age_cat: <55, 55–64, 65–74, 75–84, ≥85 years) to align with the previously published ENIGMA-II CPSP substudy and support clinically interpretable risk stratification. Categorical predictors (e.g. sex, race/ethnicity, procedure group, ASA physical status, smoking, exercise) were modelled as indicator variables. Surgical cleanliness was defined using standard wound classification: clean (no inflammation encountered and respiratory, alimentary, or genitourinary tracts not entered, with no break in aseptic technique), clean-contaminated (entry into these tracts without significant spillage), and dirty (acute inflammation or visible wound contamination, including gross spillage or recent compound/open injuries). Latent constructs in the PLS-SEM (e.g. lifestyle, chronic medical illness, surgical aspects, and NSAIDs) were specified a priori as formative composites to summarise correlated clinical domains and reduce multicollinearity; their scores therefore represent patterns of indicators rather than single exposures. For medication-related constructs (e.g. NSAIDs), “non-use” contributed to the construct definition because treatment was not randomly assigned and may reflect clinical decision-making or contraindications; accordingly, these constructs are interpreted as perioperative treatment patterns rather than causal effects. These predictors include both non-modifiable risk markers and potentially modifiable factors; clinical implications are discussed cautiously, given the exploratory, observational design.

GLASSO and PLS-SEM

We applied a hybrid approach combining GLASSO and PLS-SEM to the ENIGMA-II CPSP dataset to identify key factors associated with CPSP incidence following major noncardiac surgery and to evaluate associations with patient-reported outcomes, as measured by the mBPI and EQ-5D. The primary outcome was CPSP incidence, defined according to established guidelines.26,27 Secondary outcomes included pain characteristics and overall health status. Racialised identity and ethnicity were treated as social and cultural constructs, while sex assigned at birth (biological classification) was recorded; gender identity was not collected.

Bias

Potential sources of bias were considered a priori. To mitigate selection bias arising from non-participation or incomplete 12-month CPSP assessments, we compared included participants with those who were missing and applied inverse probability weighting under a missing-at-random assumption. Measurement bias was possible because pain and quality-of-life outcomes were self-reported, which may be influenced by recall, reporting, and cultural factors. Confounding was addressed through comprehensive covariate adjustment; however, residual and unmeasured confounding may remain. Modelling-related bias was also considered, as data-driven feature selection (GLASSO) and subsequent SEM specification can be sample- and model-dependent; therefore, the hybrid GLASSO–PLS-SEM analyses were conducted as exploratory and interpreted cautiously.

Statistical analysis

The sample size was determined by available data.23 Of 3518 eligible patients in the ENIGMA-II chronic pain study, 2924 were interviewed at 12 months post-surgery. With a 16.5% CPSP incidence, this provided 91% power to detect a 20% difference in relative risk (α=0.05).

Internal consistency was assessed using Cronbach's alpha (>0.7 considered fair reliability). Scores were computed by averaging items. Missing pain assessments were handled using inverse probability weighting, estimated via logistic regression, adjusting for baseline characteristics.

For feature selection, GLASSO was used to identify relevant variables for each outcome using the grpreg package in R. Model selection employed 10-fold cross-validation across regularisation parameter grids. A binomial family was used for CPSP (binary outcome), whereas a Gaussian family was used for pain severity and EQ-5D (continuous outcomes). Variable importance was assessed by plotting absolute model coefficients.

A conceptual model for PLS-SEM was specified a priori as an exploratory, theory-informed framework to examine complex associations among candidate determinants and CPSP-related outcomes. The model was developed with expert input from methodology, anaesthesiology, and pain medicine specialists, and adapted from Rabbitts and colleagues28 with iterative refinement grounded in existing theory and clinical plausibility. Given the absence of a widely accepted alternative CPSP pathway framework suitable for operationalisation with the variables available in this dataset, this model was selected as the most appropriate structure for hypothesis-generating analysis. The measurement model linked observed indicators to latent constructs formatively, followed by structural relationships defined by the conceptual model. PLS-SEM was fitted using the SEMinR package in R.

Model adequacy was evaluated using Cronbach's alpha, composite reliability (rhoC), average variance extracted (AVE), Dillon-Goldstein's rho (rhoA), and R2 for endogenous constructs.29 Path coefficient significance was assessed using bootstrapping (1000 samples). Final models with path coefficients were presented as path diagrams for each outcome. Analyses used R 4.4.1 (https://cran.r-project.org/) with RStudio (Version 2024.09.0 Build 375) at α=0.05.

Results

We included 2999 patients from the ENIGMA-II trial in the CPSP analysis Fig. 1. Of 3518 eligible participants, 193 died before the 12-month follow-up and were excluded, and a further 401 (12.1%) could not be contacted despite multiple attempts, leaving 2924 participants with available 12-month follow-up data. An additional 75 participants with complete 12-month outcome data were available for the present analysis but were not included in the earlier publication, resulting in a final analytic sample of 2999. The cohort was 64.2% male, and age distribution was as follows: 8.4% were <55 years, 21.9% were 55–64 years, 37.7% were 65–74 years, 27.7% were 75–84 years, and 4.3% were ≥85 years. Most participants identified as White (65.1%) or Asian (30.9%). Regarding lifestyle, 78.2% reported regular exercise, 15.4% were current smokers, and 1.3% followed vegetarian/vegan diets. One-third received dexamethasone,23 and 58.2% were ASA physical status II.

Common comorbidities included diabetes (36.7%), prior myocardial infarction (18.8%), and a history of coronary artery bypass graft surgery (CABG) or percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) (20.6%). Prevalent medications were statins (61.2%), angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers (54.3%), and β-blockers (37.9%). Aspirin was used by 40.3% in the preceding 5 days.

Most frequent surgeries were vascular (32.7%), gastroenterological (11.6%), orthopaedic (11.2%), and urological (11.0%). Clean surgeries comprised 72.8% of cases, with prophylactic antibiotics used in 96.6% and regional/local blocks in 25.4% of procedures (Supplementary Table S1).

CPSP occurred in 16.5% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 15.2%–17.9%). EQ-5D scores showed considerable variability (mean=0.206; standard deviation [sd]=0.4; median=0.073). Pain interference averaged 28.0 (sd=26.4; median=20), while pain severity averaged 32.0 (sd=22.1; median=30).

Optimal lambda selection

Supplementary Figs S1–S4 present the cross-validation curves used to select the optimal lambda values for each outcome. The minimum cross-validation errors corresponded to lambda values of 0.0038 for CPSP, 0.0096 for EQ-5D, 0.5469 for pain severity, and 1.0106 for pain interference, indicating the optimal degree of model sparsity for each network.

PLS-SEM model adequacy metrics

PLS-SEM model adequacy metrics are summarised in Supplementary Table S2. Internal consistency was generally acceptable across models (Cronbach’s α 0.65–0.72; rhoC 0.70–0.75), with slightly lower reliability for the pain interference model. Convergent validity was supported across outcomes (AVE 0.51–0.58). These indices are reported to support transparent interpretation of the exploratory pathway models (Supplementary Table S2).

For CPSP, GLASSO retained demographic and clinical features including sex, age category, ASA physical status, procedure group, preoperative pain, and selected comorbidities and medications. Variables such as race/ethnicity, diet, smoking, and exercise were not highlighted as key CPSP predictors because their coefficients were shrunk to zero or were comparatively small at the optimal lambda (i.e. they contributed less to prediction once other correlated features were accounted for). For EQ-5D, retained predictors included similar demographic/clinical features and additionally heart failure, diabetes, and cyclooxygenase (COX)-2 inhibitor use. For pain severity, retained predictors included sex, age, heart failure, prior myocardial infarction, and medications such as statins and β-blockers. For pain interference, retained predictors included sex, age, smoking status, and selected medication features (Fig. 2).

In the cross-validation plots (Supplementary Figs S1–S4), the “groups selected” axis refers to the number of predefined predictor groups retained by GLASSO. In our implementation, predictors were organised into 32 groups, corresponding to the categorical domains/factors represented in Supplementary Table S1 (e.g. procedure category, ASA physical status, medication groups, comorbidity groups, and lifestyle factors). Thus, “0–32” indicates the number of grouped predictors retained at each lambda, with larger lambdas yielding sparser models (fewer retained groups).

Model performance for GLASSO-only feature selection models was moderate. The CPSP model showed an area under the curve of 0.69 (95% CI 0.64–0.74). For continuous outcomes, the explained variance (R2) was 0.34 for EQ-5D, 0.29 for pain severity, and 0.24 for pain interference.

In the next step, we developed exploratory conceptual models for the outcome measures—CPSP, EQ-5D, pain severity, and pain interference—based on a general framework proposed by Rabbitts and colleagues (2020),28 incorporating the features selected through GLASSO.

Discussion

In this secondary analysis of 2999 ENIGMA-II participants, CPSP occurred in 16.5% at 12 months. The hybrid GLASSO–PLS-SEM approach showed moderate discriminatory power (AUC 0.69) and identified presurgical pain as the strongest predictor of CPSP (B=0.348), with additional associations for demographic factors (B=0.169) and surgical aspects (B=0.091). Lifestyle patterns showed a small inverse association with CPSP, while no clear associations were found for dexamethasone or NSAIDs.

Our findings highlight the multifactorial nature of CPSP. Demographic factors—particularly sex, age, and ethnicity—were consistently associated with CPSP and related outcomes. Older females and individuals of Asian ethnicity showed higher CPSP levels and distinct quality-of-life patterns, suggesting the value of demographically and culturally responsive perioperative assessment and follow-up.23 Ethnicity should be interpreted as a contextual marker rather than a biologically deterministic factor. Similarly, observed differences by sex should not be assumed to be purely biological, as they may also reflect gendered differences in comorbidity profiles, health-seeking, communication, analgesic prescribing, and pain reporting.

Any observed associations may reflect unmeasured structural and social determinants (e.g. socioeconomic factors, access to care, communication, and cultural differences in pain reporting) and residual confounding; therefore, ethnicity-related findings should not be interpreted causally. However, without external validation, reproducibility and transportability beyond this dataset are unknown, and statistically significant—especially small or indirect—paths should be viewed as hypothesis-generating rather than clinically actionable.

Lifestyle and baseline health status were associated with outcomes. Healthier behavioural patterns (non-smoking and physical activity) tended to align with better recovery, while greater illness burden (comorbidities, polypharmacy, higher ASA physical status) aligned with poorer outcomes. Presurgical pain remained the strongest correlate, underscoring the value of early risk identification. The PLS-SEM models highlight interrelationships between domains; however, some constructs—particularly medication-related factors—should be interpreted as perioperative treatment patterns (including non-use) rather than causal effects. Consistent with this, dexamethasone and NSAID constructs showed no clear association with CPSP, and these findings should be interpreted cautiously.

We employed a hybrid GLASSO–PLS-SEM approach to identify key features associated with CPSP, EQ-5D, pain severity, and pain interference within an exploratory, theory-informed framework. To our knowledge, this combined application is novel in this context and offers the practical advantages of data-driven feature selection alongside modelling of complex multivariable pathways, while remaining hypothesis-generating rather than definitive evidence of causality.21,22

The integration of GLASSO and PLS-SEM provides a pragmatic, exploratory framework for analysing complex health data, supporting feature selection and modelling multivariable relationships in the presence of collinearity. GLASSO facilitates data-driven selection of correlated predictors without extensive prespecification, which is useful in higher-dimensional settings.30,31 Within a theory-informed conceptual model, PLS-SEM can represent relationships among latent constructs and outcomes, which is particularly useful for hypothesis-generating analyses in observational data where residual confounding may remain.19,20,29,32, 33, 34

By combining these methodologies, the hybrid GLASSO–PLS-SEM approach retains the strengths of both while improving interpretability for clinical research, particularly for exploratory feature selection and mapping complex multivariable associations. Within our theory-informed conceptual framework, the models help characterise how measured domains relate to CPSP, EQ-5D, pain severity, and pain interference, but they should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive evidence of causality or treatment effects.21,22 Overall, the findings underscore the complex and multidimensional nature of these outcomes (see Appendix A for further details).

This approach aligns with a growing literature using advanced analytical techniques—such as deep learning and neural networks—to improve risk modelling and generate hypotheses about factors associated with CPSP.35,36 These methods may support the development of more accurate predictive tools for individualised perioperative risk stratification. With appropriate prospective design and external validation, such models could also help prioritise and evaluate candidate interventions aimed at reducing CPSP, moving toward more personalised surgical and pain management.

Across all outcomes—CPSP, EQ-5D, pain severity, and pain interference—demographic and lifestyle factors were consistently associated with variation in outcomes. Female sex, older age, and Asian ethnicity were associated with higher CPSP and pain severity, consistent with reported demographic disparities in persistent postoperative pain13,37 (however, ethnicity should be interpreted as a contextual marker rather than a causal or biologically deterministic factor). Regular physical activity and non-smoking were associated with better quality of life and lower pain interference, aligning with their roles in recovery.38,39 Preoperative pain showed the strongest association with CPSP, reinforcing that higher baseline pain identifies patients at greater risk of persistent postoperative pain.3,40

Clinically, several variables in our models function primarily as risk stratifiers rather than intervention targets, including age, sex, ethnicity (as a contextual marker), comorbidity burden/ASA physical status, and procedure type. Potentially modifiable elements are more limited and include optimisation of preoperative pain (and perioperative analgesic planning) and support for healthy behaviours such as smoking cessation and physical activity, noting these were only partially captured, and associations are not causal. Clinicians should not change perioperative prescribing (e.g. NSAIDs or dexamethasone) based on these results alone, as medication constructs reflect treatment patterns (including non-use), may be confounded by indication/contraindications, and the analyses are exploratory without external validation.

Chronic medical illness and surgical characteristics showed outcome-specific associations. Surgical cleanliness and procedure type were associated with CPSP and EQ-5D, but were less consistently associated with pain severity or interference. The association with clean surgery should be interpreted cautiously. In this dataset, surgical cleanliness reflects wound class (rather than operative quality) and may primarily capture case-mix/procedure extent and the high prevalence of elective operations within this category, which increases statistical power and may leave residual confounding. Accordingly, we are reluctant to propose a causal mechanism and interpret this as a weak, hypothesis-generating signal requiring further study. The absence of clear associations between CPSP and the NSAIDs or dexamethasone constructs—best interpreted as perioperative treatment patterns (including non-use) rather than causal drug effects—is consistent with meta-analyses suggesting limited long-term benefit of these medications for preventing chronic pain.41 Overall, the findings support a multidimensional, patient-centred approach emphasising early risk identification, lifestyle support, and tailored pain prevention strategies.

Limitations

This study was restricted to patients aged ≥45 years with known or suspected coronary artery disease, potentially limiting generalisability. The nested CPSP study included 2999 of 3518 eligible patients (85.2%); inverse probability weighting was applied to account for missing assessments, assuming data were missing at random. The cross-sectional 12-month design precluded assessment of temporal causality, and the observational nature limits causal inference despite advanced modelling. Unmeasured confounding may persist despite comprehensive covariate adjustment. Ethnicity and racialised identity were self-reported categorical variables, potentially not fully capturing their complexity. In addition, our findings have not been externally validated in an independent cohort; therefore, reproducibility and transportability beyond the current dataset remain unknown and require confirmation in future studies.

Although the study was framed within a biopsychosocial model, our analyses were constrained by the available variables and did not include several core psychological and psychosocial constructs with established relevance to chronic post-surgical pain, such as anxiety, depression, pain catastrophising, posttraumatic stress disorder symptoms, coping strategies, perceived social support, and socioeconomic context. Lifestyle factors were also only partially captured (primarily exercise and smoking). This limitation may reduce the explanatory scope of the models and means that the findings should be interpreted as reflecting associations within the measured clinical, demographic, and behavioural domains rather than a comprehensive biopsychosocial account.

Generalisability may be further limited because ENIGMA-II enrolled older surgical patients (≥45 years) with known or suspected coronary artery disease undergoing major noncardiac surgery; findings may not apply to younger patients, lower-risk elective populations, emergency surgery, cardiac surgery, or settings with different perioperative pathways and health care systems. In addition, lifestyle and contextual variables were measured in a limited and simplified way (e.g. exercise and smoking only) and do not capture intensity, duration, or broader social determinants; therefore, any lifestyle-related associations should be interpreted cautiously and should not be taken as evidence that modifying these factors would causally improve CPSP outcomes.

Conclusion

This study illustrates the potential of combining GLASSO and PLS-SEM as an exploratory, hypothesis-generating approach for analysing CPSP and related outcomes in high-dimensional observational data. Rather than asserting definitive causal effects, the models map model-dependent patterns of association and inter-relationships among demographic, lifestyle, medical, and surgical domains. GLASSO supported systematic feature selection, and PLS-SEM provided a structured way to examine how selected factors may relate through direct and indirect pathways.

The latent constructs should be interpreted as composite indicators (and, for medication-related constructs, as perioperative treatment patterns that may include non-use) rather than single clinically actionable exposures. The observed indirect lifestyle pathways and differing patterns across subgroups generate testable hypotheses and may help guide future risk stratification and tailored perioperative care. However, in the absence of external validation, reproducibility and transportability beyond this dataset remain unknown, and statistically significant—particularly small or indirect—paths should be viewed as hypothesis-generating signals rather than clinically actionable causal effects. Future studies should validate these models in independent cohorts and incorporate richer psychosocial and structural determinants to strengthen clinical interpretability and generalisability.
