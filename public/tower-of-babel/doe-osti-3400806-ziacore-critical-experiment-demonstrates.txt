 ZiaCore Critical Experiment Demonstrates Key Technologies for
 Nuclear Energy Systems
 By Nicholas Thompson, Los Alamos National Laboratory, LA-UR-XX-XXXXX
ZiaCore is a LANL Laboratory Directed Research and Development (LDRD) project
focused on developing and demonstrating key technologies for future nuclear energy
systems. The project itself was split into three tasks:
 1) Design of the ZiaCore Reactor, a UO2 fueled, graphite and zirconium-hydride
 (ZrH) moderated, heat pipe cooled micro-reactor
 2) Development of the ZrH and heat pipes components
 3) Performance of a critical experiment with a representative portion of the ZiaCore
 reactor incorporating the ZrH and heat pipes developed and made at LANL
The experiment used the Deimos testbed, replacing the inner platen region with a
vacuum chamber surrounded by graphite. The vacuum chamber contained graphite
core blocks, a sodium heat pipe, three quartz-encapsulated ZrH rods, 42 UO2 fuel pins,
electric heaters and thermocouples surrounded by multi-layer insulation. The vacuum
chamber both prevented high temperature oxidation of the graphite within the chamber
and, in combination with multi-layer insulation, minimized heating of the assembly
outside of the chamber. Figure 1 shows the vacuum chamber being installed.
The central core was surrounded by the Deimos outer region containing CNPS HALEU
TRISO fuel cups, polyethylene rods, and stainless-steel rods. Approach to critical was
performed on number of CNPS fuel cups and insertion distance. The final high
temperature configuration had 272 CNPS fuel cups, 42 UO2 fuel pins, ten layers of top
reflector graphite, and a 1 inch spacer.

Figure 1: Chandler De Leon with the ZiaCore vacuum chamber mounted on the Comet Critical Assembly.

The critical experiment took place over five weeks between April and June 2026,
reaching criticality on 5/7/2026. The experiment culminated on 5/20/2026 with a critical
measurement at a temperature of 826.1°C with an operating heat pipe.
The four weeks of operations included two extended workdays to allow slow, methodical
heating of the test region. During the experiment, temperature data was recorded along
the length of the heat pipe (including in the heat rejection region above the core) and
throughout the graphite core blocks.
This experiment was a cross-division collaboration between MST-8, SIGMA, NEN-2,
and other groups at LANL. The project was led by Principal Investigator (PI) Topher
Matthews along with Co-PI’s Samantha Lawrence and Nicholas Thompson. Theresa
Cutler was the Crew Chief for the NCERC critical experiment. There were also many
other people at LANL and NNSS who enabled the experiment, from getting the fuel
delivered to NNSS, to moving components into the facility, to the RCTs and other DAF
facility staff – thank you to all who helped make the experiment a success!
