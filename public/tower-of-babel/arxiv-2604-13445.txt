Sub-nm range momentum-dependent exciton transfer from a 2D semiconductor to graphene
Aditi Raman Moghe, Delphine Lagarde, Sotirios Papadopoulos, Etienne Lorchat, Luis E. Parra López, Loïc Moczko, Kenji Watanabe, Takashi Taniguchi, Michelangelo Romeo, Maxime Mauguet, Xavier Marie, Arnaud Gloppe, Cédric Robert, Stéphane Berciaud

Abstract
Van der Waals heterostructures made from atomically thin transition metal dichalcogenides (TMD) and graphene have emerged as a building block for optoelectronic devices. Such systems are also uniquely poised to investigate interfacial coupling as well as photoinduced charge and energy transfer in the 2D limit. Recent works have revealed efficient photoluminescence quenching and picosecond transfer in TMD/graphene heterostructures. However, key questions regarding the transfer mechanisms remain. Here, employing time-resolved photoluminescence spectroscopy with 1 ps resolution in MoSe 2 monolayer directly coupled to a few-layer “staircase-like” graphene flake, we consistently observe an exciton transfer time of ∼ 2.5 ​ ps \sim 2.5~\mathrm{ps} at cryogenic temperature that is marginally affected by the number of graphene layers. Remarkably, exciton transfer vanishes in samples consisting in an MoSe 2 monolayer separated from graphene by a thin dielectric spacer of hexagonal boron nitride, as soon as the spacer thickness reaches 1 nm. These results suggest that charge tunnelling processes govern exciton dynamics. Other mechanisms mediated the dipolar interactions (Förster-type energy transfer) have no measurable impact on bright excitons (with near-zero center of mass momentum) but may accelerate the relaxation of finite momentum “hot” excitons, leading to larger photoluminescence quenching than anticipated based on the measurements of the photoluminescence decay rates. Our work provides important insights into charge and energy transfer in van der Waals materials with direct implications for energy harvesting and funneling.



Introduction

A wealth of low-dimensional physical phenomena and related models can be accurately tested using atomically-thin layers of van der Waals materials. These materials offer unique opportunities to investigate truly two-dimensional physics, without being hindered by issues associated with dangling bonds, buffer layers and surface roughness
Novoselov et al. 2016
. In particular, it is now clear that interfacial coupling may strongly alter the electronic, vibrational and optical response of individual two-dimensional layers
Hong et al. 2014
;
Rivera et al. 2015
;
Pospischil et al. 2013
;
Raja et al. 2017
;
Moczko et al. 2025
. Interlayer (spatially-indirect) excited states may form on ultrafast timescales upon photoexcitation in heterobilayers of transition dichalcogenides (TMD)
Hong et al. 2014
;
Rivera et al. 2015
;
Kozawa et al. 2016
. Correlated electronic phases emerge in twisted layers of graphene
Cao et al. 2018
, as well as in twisted TMD homo- or hetero-bilayers
Wilson et al. 2021
. Coupled layers also offer a model system to investigate the role of dielectric screening
Raja et al. 2017
;
Raja et al. 2019
;
Moczko et al. 2025
;
Gerber and Marie 2018
as well as charge and energy transfer in a layered donor-acceptor system separated by a sub-nm van der Waals gap. Several experimental observations of ultrafast interlayer charge and/or energy transfer have been reported using a variety of time-resolved (TR) experimental techniques such as transient absorption spectroscopy
He et al. 2014
;
Zhou et al. 2021
, TR photocurrent spectroscopy
Massicotte et al. 2016
, TR photoluminescence (TRPL) spectroscopy
Kozawa et al. 2016
;
Froehlicher et al. 2018
;
Lorchat et al. 2020
, TR Raman spectroscopy
Ferrante et al. 2022
TR angle-resolved photo-emission spectroscopy (TR-ARPES)
Aeschlimann et al. 2020
;
Krause et al. 2021
;
Dong et al. 2023
, key questions remain open regarding the microscopic transfer mechanisms at play, specifically the range of the interactions, the associated timescales and the different behaviors between bright excitons (with near-zero center of mass momentum), hot excitons (optically inactive, with finite center of mass momentum) and charge carriers.

On the theoretical side, it becomes challenging and often speculative to address quantitatively the charge and energy transfer rates in the limit of sub-nanometer separation between coupled layers, where orbital overlap prevail
Koppens et al. 2011
;
Selig et al. 2019
;
Dong et al. 2023
. It is thus important to scrutinize the specific case of tightly coupled TMD and graphene layers in order to get an accurate estimation of the transfer time as well as clear insights into the transfer mechanism.

In this work, we study the low-temperature excitonic dynamics in two distinct types of MoSe
2
/graphene heterostructures. First, in a monolayer of MoSe
2
directly coupled to a graphene flake comprising several domains with thicknesses ranging from
N=1
to
N=6
layers, we observe two effects: i) sizeable PL quenching and ii) reduction of the bright exciton lifetime. Remarkably, the latter is marginally dependent on
N
, whereas PL quenching shows an appreciable increase with
N
. Second, the investigation of MoSe
2
monolayers decoupled from a graphene monolayer by thin hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) spacers reveals that a 3 layer-thick (1.0 nm) hBN spacer suffices to fully decouple MoSe
2
from graphene. These results strongly indicate that the dominant transfer mechanism for bright excitons is very short range (sub-nm) and implies charge tunnelling. In addition, by confronting an electrodynamic model
SMn
to our experimental results, we argue that even if dipole-dipole mediated (Förster-type) energy transfer has no sizeable impact on bright excitons, this mechanism contributes to PL quenching by accelerating the relaxation of hot excitons through efficient transfer to graphene layers, with a rate that appreciably depends on
N
. Our work sheds new light on charge and energy transfer mechanisms at ultrashort (sub-nm) distances and is a strong impetus to further develop existing theoretical frameworks to address charge and energy transfer in the charge tunnelling regime.

Discussion

Cold (
\rm X^{0}
) excitons –
We now comment on the microscopic mechanisms leading to a reduction of the
\rm X^{0}
lifetime and subsequent
\rm X^{0}
PL quenching. As discussed previously, both short-range charge tunelling and dipole-dipole mediated Förster-type resonant transfer (FRET)
Förster 1948
may contribute. FRET from TMD excitons to graphene has recently been considered theoretically and experimentally, following numerous studies in 2D-2D hybrid systems, including organic/inorganic semiconductor heterostructures
Basko et al. 1999
and semiconductor nanoplatelet/graphene heterostructures
Federspiel et al. 2015
. In contrast to the case of point-like systems or 0D-2D heterostructures
Chen et al. 2010
;
Gaudreau et al. 2013
;
Federspiel et al. 2015
;
Prins et al. 2014
, conservation of the exciton center of mass momentum in a 2D-2D system has drastic consequences on the FRET rate. Indeed, since the momentum-dependent FRET efficiency scales as
q_{\rm X}^{2}\exp{\left(-2q_{\rm X}d\right)}

Basko et al. 1999
;
Selig et al. 2019
, where
d
the TMD-graphene distance, no FRET is expected for excitons at
q_{\rm X}=0
and more broadly for
\rm X^{0}
excitons in the light cone (such that
q_{\rm X}\lesssim 10^{7}\rm m^{-1}
), i.e.,that is
q_{\rm X}\ll E_{\rm X^{0}}/(\hbar v_{\rm F})
, with
v_{\rm F}
the Fermi velocity of graphene).

Importantly, in the limit where the donor and acceptor are separated by a sub-nanometer distance (arguably as is the case for Sample 1), the FRET rate is marginally dependent on the donor-acceptor distance and decays exponentially with the donor-acceptor distance
d
with a characteristic length in the nanometer range at cryogenic temperatures
Selig et al. 2019
. Beyond
\sim 10~\rm{nm}
, a well-known
1/d^{4}
scaling is expected. These theoretical predictions contrast with our observations that a 1 nm-thin hBN spacer is sufficient to recover PL intensities and dynamics that are very similar to bare MoSe
2
(Fig.
3
and
SMn
). Further studies at room temperature, where more efficient FRET is expected, have revealed that the distance-dependence of the transfer rate deviates strongly from theoretical predictions based solely on FRET, when the TMD-graphene distance is within the tunnelling range (that is, with no spacer between TMD and graphene or just one atomic layer of hBN)
Tebbe et al. 2024
.

We thus conclude that another microscopic mechanism must be at play in order to explain the reduction of the
\rm X^{0}
lifetime that we consistently observe in MoSe
{}_{2}/N
LG. Considering that the
\rm X^{0}
dynamics in MoSe
2
/Gr i) is extremely sensitive to the interlayer distance for a sub-nm interlayer gap but then marginally depends on the distance outside the tunnelling range, and ii) depends very weakly on
N
(also in contrast with a FRET-based scenario
SMn
as further discussed below), it is sensible to invoke charge tunnelling. Dexter energy transfer (also known as electron exchange)
Dexter 1953
may in principle contribute but the Dexter energy transfer rate has been predicted is orders of magnitude smaller than the FRET rate, chiefly due to limited overlap between TMD and graphene electronic orbitals
Dong et al. 2023
. The only plausible mechanism that may account for the shortening of
\rm X^{0}
lifetime and its dependence on
d
and on
N
is direct charge tunnelling of electrons and holes that compose
\rm X^{0}
. Since our previous experiments did not reveal any fingerprint of a long-lived charge separated state
Aeschlimann et al. 2020
on timescales on the order of the measured exciton lifetime
Ferrante et al. 2022
, we suggest that sequential (balanced) tunnelling of electrons and holes is responsible for the shortening of the exciton lifetime.

Conclusion and outlook

In conclusion, we demonstrate that the low temperature dynamics (typically
T\leq 16~\rm K
) of the lower lying, bright excitons in MoSe
{}_{2}/N
-layer graphene is largely independent on
N
and determined by sub-nm range picosecond non-radiative transfer processes to graphene. In contrast, the photoluminescence quenching factor increases appreciably in the small
N
limit before saturating for
N\gtrsim 3
. A slower exciton dynamics typical from a bare MoSe
2
monolayer is recovered for separations in excess of
1~\rm{nm}
between MoSe
2
and a graphene monolayer. Together, these results establish that Förster-type resonant energy transfer is inefficient for bright excitons at low temperatures, as the center of mass momentum of these excitons is very close to zero, but may contribute significantly to the transfer of hot, finite momentum excitons. This hypothesis is supported by the increase of the quenching factor with
N
and supported by an electrodynamical model.
Altogether, charge tunelling phenomena are chiefly responsible for the luminescence quenching and accelerated decay of bright excitons, whereas hot excitons likely experience an additional decay channel arising from Förster-type energy transfer.

Exciton dynamics is known to be complex in bare MoSe
2
due to a subtle interplay between bright exciton formation and recombination kinetics as well as a sizeable contribution from trions and, more broadly spatial heterogeneities in the dielectric and electrostatic landscape. This situation leads to challenging assignments of the rise and decay times in TRPL traces of MoSe
2
. In contrast, we observe a particularly robust and reproducible bright exciton photoluminescence dynamics in MoSe
{}_{2}/N
LG, nearly independent of the subtleties of the exciton dynamics in the reference, with a sub-ps rise time and a decay time in the range 2.0 - 2.5 ps.

Our work invites further experimental and theoretical investigations of charge tunneling phenomena in optically excited TMD/Graphene heterostructures and of the impact of charge and energy transfer on other excitonic species such as spin- or momentum-dark excitons, as well as interlayer excitons, including when the latter are trapped in a moiré superpotential.
