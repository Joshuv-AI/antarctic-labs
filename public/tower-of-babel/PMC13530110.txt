Background

Lung cancer (LC) represents a significant global health burden, accounting for the highest mortality and incidence rates among all malignancies, with an estimated 1.6 million deaths annually [1]. Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), a dominant subtype accounting for roughly 40% of lung cancers [2], can appear anywhere within the population. While smoking remains the primary risk factor, LUAD can also occur in never-smokers, contributing substantially to worldwide cancer-related mortality [3]. In addition, most cases of LUAD are typically diagnosed at an advanced or metastatic stage, which significantly hinders successful treatment and exacerbates already high LC mortality rates [4]. In the past few decades, there have been significant advances in the treatment of LC. The advent of surgical resection, cytotoxic therapy, immunotherapy, and targeted therapy have revolutionized physicians’ approach to treating LUAD [5]. Despite these strides, the 5-year survival rate for LUAD remains concerningly low, maintaining around 15% [6]. A critical challenge hindering successful treatment is the perplexing phenomenon of tumor heterogeneity. Tumor heterogeneity, a term encompassing genetic and molecular variations within a tumor, is increasingly recognized as a critical obstacle leading to treatment resistance and tumor recurrence [7]. As a result, deciphering the cellular and molecular underpinnings of LUAD is paramount to identifying novel molecular biomarkers for improved diagnosis, targeted therapy, and patient prognosis.

Tumor heterogeneity inevitably leads to heterogeneity of the tumor microenvironment (TME), which in turn leads to heterogeneity of immunity and differences in response to immunotherapy. Most tumors develop under intense pressure from nutrition, metabolism, immunity and therapy, and the relative influence of these factors on tumor development varies over time and space. The emergence and maintenance of cancer hallmarks depend to varying degrees on the contribution of the TME [8, 9], which is far more therapeutically advantageous than directly targeting cancer cells. The inherent genetic instability of cancer cells renders them more susceptible to developing resistance to therapies [10]. Conversely, the TME, composed of non-tumor cells with greater genetic stability, presents a more attractive therapeutic target [11]. However, the TME’s inherent heterogeneity, arising from its diverse cell populations with distinct phenotypes and functions, remains the primary obstacle to deciphering its complexities [12]. The TME consists mainly of immune cells surrounding tumor cells, fibroblasts, nearby stroma and various chemokines. The basement membrane (BM) is an important component of the TME. It is crucial for the growth and proper functioning of several tissues [13], separating epithelial cells, endothelial cells, nerves and fat from underlying tissues [14] and providing surface cells with a wealth of biochemical and physical cues [15]. The BM in tumors has fewer junctions than in normal tissue and is, therefore, more susceptible to proteolysis and remodeling when tumor cells breach the BM. Tumor cells can travel through the circulatory or lymphatic system to distant organs and begin to metastasize, which is an important cause of increased LUAD mortality [16, 17].

Due to the significant role of the BM in tumor progression, the exploration of basement membrane-related biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets for inhibiting tumor progression has garnered research attention. For example, epithelial-mesenchymal transition of the BM can convert epithelial cells into mesenchymal-type cells, aiding tumor metastasis from the primary site to distant organs. Collagen, laminin, and integrins are major components of the basement membrane and are crucial for tumor cell metastasis from the bone marrow. They are regarded as significant targets for anticancer therapies [18]. Furthermore, cell migration may be tightly regulated by the basement membrane, and its degradation might also be a key step in tumor progression. Currently, several studies are investigating the combination of tumor-associated basement membrane-targeted therapies with immune checkpoint inhibitors in clinical trials. For instance, pegvorhyaluronidase alfa has been shown to successfully degrade hyaluronic acid in tumors and remodel the tumor stroma, thereby improving perfusion and drug delivery [19]. Placental growth factor-2 heparin-binding domain-conjugated immune checkpoint inhibitors exhibit high affinity for various basement membrane proteins, and intratumoral injection of placental growth factor-2 anti-PD-L1 can increase retention within the tumor tissue [20].

The advent of single-cell sequencing technology provides an exceptional chance to unravel the complexity of the TME in an unbiased manner, facilitating single-cell high-throughput sequencing of transcriptomes, genomes, and epigenomes [21]. Its appearance can also shed light on the gene structure and gene expression transition of a single cell, as well as analysing the process underlying intratumoral heterogeneity. This approach offers a novel lens into intratumoral heterogeneity, potentially informing mechanisms of tumorigenesis, development, diagnosis, and treatment. This study collected single-cell sequencing datasets of LUAD and applied integrative bioinformatics methods to analyze the transcriptional profiles of heterogeneous cell types in LUAD. Marker genes for each cell type were identified, revealing their crucial roles in the LUAD microenvironment. GO/KEGG enrichment analysis, and GSEA were performed to clarify the biological functions and related signaling pathways of these marker genes. Subsequently, intercellular communication and trajectory analyses were conducted, and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was used to identify prognostic effects of marker genes in modules closely related to clinical features. This research aims to expand molecular insights into the microenvironment of LUAD malignancy and metastasis, providing new perspectives on the TME of LUAD patients under various pathological conditions, improving patient prognosis, and guiding clinical LUAD treatment decisions.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition and preprocessing

GeneCards was used to extract the BM-related genes with correlation scores ≥ 3. The expression and survival information from TCGA were downloaded from University of California Santa Cruz (UCSC) Xena. One scRNA sequencing data set (GSE117570) and two RNA sequencing data sets (GSE72094, GSE50081)from the Gene-Expression Omnibus(GEO) databases. Data on tumor groups were included, and samples with missing survival and outcomes were excluded. Of these, GSE50081 served as the validation set, while expression data from TCGA-LUAD and GSE72094, together with their clinical features, were used as the training set.

Annotation of cell type

Three LUAD samples were selected from GSE117570. Cells expressing fewer than fifty genes and cells expressing fewer than three genes were eliminated. More than 5% of cells with mitochondrial genes were filtered by “Seurat” [22] packets.CCA was used to complete the integration of multiple samples, and after removing unwanted cells from the dataset, global data standardization was used to calculate subsets of features that showed high variability in the dataset. 1500 genes were used per dataset. These are used for dimensionality reduction and principal component analysis, and the Jackstrawberry plot function provides a visualization tool to compare the p-value distribution for each PC with the uniform distribution.The first 11 principal components were subjected to dimensional reduction clustering analysis using the T-distribution (tSNE) to obtain the primary clusters and to search for marker genes in the different clusters. The marker genes are used to easily assign clusters to known unit types. The " CellChat " [23] packages annotate cell clusters based on the gene foundation.

Cell differentiation trajectory analysis and cell communication analysis

Using the ‘Monocle’ method based on LUAD data from scRNA-seq, single-cell pseudotime trajectories were generated. The assumption was made that cells on the same branch were at similar stages of development, providing a better understanding of the differentiation trajectories of each individual cell. CellChat accurately identifies and analyses intercellular communication signals, aiding in the discovery of new intercellular communications and the creation of cell-cell communication maps across different tissues.

Enrichment analysis

Analyzed Gene Ontology (GO) biological processes and KEGG pathways in relation to gene risk profiles associated with cell trajectorie. Enrichment pathways for each branch were then determined by assigning a score to each sample’s KEGG pathway and GO biological processes.

Classification based on basement membrane related differentiation genes

To clarify the patterns and associations of Basement Membrane -related genes (BMRGs) in LUAD, among the differentiation genes found above, we used BM-related genes from GeneCards to perform unsupervised consensus clustering on the GSE72094 dataset. ConsensusCluster is an unsupervised clustering method. By using the consensus clustering algorithm to perform 1,000 iterations, 80% of the data is extracted in each iteration, and the K-means algorithm divides each subsample into several groups. The Consensus Matrix (CM) and Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) graphs are visualized to find the ideal number of clusters, this method was used to evaluate the variability and stability of clustering of BM locus genes and BM locus subtypes. Finally, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was used to evaluate the survival outcomes of the different sub cohorts.

Immune purity prediction and immune cell count

The stromal and immune scores may be estimated by the ESTIMATE method [24] using expression data. In a tumor sample, which indicates the existence of stromal and immune cells. The sum of the two scores gives an ESTIMATE score, which can be used to estimate the purity of the tumor. CIBERSORT [25] is a tool designed to estimate the abundance of cell clusters in bulk RNA-seq data by deconvolution of immune cell subtype expression matrices based on the concept of linear support vector regression.

WGCNA analysis

Based on LUAD cell differentiation genes, the co-expression network was constructed using the “WGCNA” R package [26]. In addition, the Pearson correlation coefficient was calculated and the similarity matrix was obtained. After transforming the adjacency matrix into a topological overlap matrix, the degree of gene connection is evaluated, appropriate weights and soft thresholds are chosen for the computation, and the network is then made to conform to the scale-free network distribution. Furthermore, the matrices were hierarchically clustered using the R tool “WGCNA”. The gene clustering tree is chopped using the dynamic tree-cutting approach in the generated clustering tree aspect. Each branch represents a co-expression module, and genes with comparable expression patterns are assigned to it. The clinical features and gene importance of each module were used to establish correlations between the two.

Development and verification of a risk model

An initial univariate Cox analysis was performed on the identified BMRGs to assess their connection with survival outcomes and evaluate the prognostic significance of relevant genes. Six prognostic genes were identified for further analysis. To reduce the number of prognosis-related genes, the least absolute contraction and selection operator (LASSO) regression was used. Multivariate Cox Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) regression analysis was then performed using survival packages to create an optimal BMRGs-associated risk signature with linear integrals based on the level of expression of specific BMRGs and the regression coefficients of the multivariate Cox regression analysis. This formula is used to determine the risk score:

Risk score = coefficient1 * gene1 expression + …+ coefficientN * geneN expression.

where expression is the expression value of the BMRGs and coefficient is the matching regression coefficient found using multivariate Cox regression analysis. The training queue consisted of TCGA data, while the validation queue consisted of GSE50081 data.

Patients were then categorized into low-risk and high-risk categories based on their median risk score. The study employed Kaplan-Meier estimators to investigate variations in survival rates between the high-risk and low-risk cohorts.To assess the predictive power of the gene signature, we constructed univariate Cox risk models and ROC receiver operating characteristics (ROC curves) in the training and validation cohorts based on patient risk score data.

The construction of a nomograph

Univariate and multivariate Cox analyses were used including patient age, sex, tumour grade, and BMRGs risk score. The BMRGs risk scores were used to construct predictive nomograms. The prognostic effect was evaluated using a c-index chart and correction chart.

Evaluation of immune infiltration and therapeutic effect

The Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) approach uses a gene list sorted by phenotypic significance to evaluate the trend of gene distribution in a preset gene set. This facilitates the identification of highly enriched pathways between high- and low-risk groups, as well as the contribution of each gene to the phenotype. The Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) algorithm (http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/) was used to predict the immunotherapy response for both high and low risk groups.

Statistical analysis

R software (V. 4.3.2) was used for all bioinformatics analyses. A P-value of less than 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

In addition, the total intracellular sequence and sequencing depth showed a strong correlation (r = 0.88)(Fig. 1B). According to the results of differential expression analysis performed using the R package DESeq2, there were 1662 associated genes and 1500 highly variant genes (Fig. 1C). LUAD cells are not significantly different according to principal component analysis (PCA) (Fig. 1D). Based on the marker genes, eleven clusters were identified as follows: Clusters 0, 4 and 8 consisted of monocytes, cluster 3 of dendritic cells, clusters 6, 7 and 10 of B cells, cluster 5 of erythroid cells, clusters 1 and 2 of CD4 + T cells and clusters 9 and 10 of HSCs (Fig. 2G, H).

Cell differentiation trajectory analysis and cell communication analysis

Using the R package “Monocle”, differentiation trajectories and pseudo-temporal analyses based on marker genes found in each cluster were examined to ascertain the connection between these cell clusters and the state. It was discovered that cells with different differentiation patterns can be distinguished using trajectory and pseudo-time analysis. The cells of the tumor mass were all pushed into three branches (branches I, II and III) in three dimensions (Fig. 2A). Branch-specific signature genes were identified as progressively up- or down-regulated genes from the root to each branch (Supplementary Figure S1). DRGs were identified as up-regulated genes of each branch. Based on the pseudo-temporal sequence, it appears that the cells originate in clusters 0, 3, 4, and then move to clusters 5, 7, 9, and 1, 2, 6, 7 (Fig. 2B, C). Furthermore, we found that the simulated cell development process moves from left to right and branches into different states (Fig. 2D).

There may be a strong correlation between cell-to-cell communication and the types of cells that make up the TME. Cellchat divides receptor-ligand pairings into four categories by combining the KEGG database with the literature: Non-protein signalling, cell-to-cell contact, ECM receptor and secreted signalling (Fig. 2E). The number (Fig. 2F) and strength (Fig. 2G) of interactions between different cell populations within the LUAD sample were then inferred. Larger nodes indicate a greater number of cells, while thicker connecting lines indicate better relationships between cells within that type.The greatest cell-to-cell contacts within and between cell subpopulations are seen in the monocyte, haematopoietic stem cell (HSC), dendritic cell, erythroid cell and macrophage populations (Fig. 2H).

Signaling pathways in cellular communication

We modelled the ligand-receptor interactions of several cell types in the lung cancer microenvironment to further determine if there were significant associations between cell subsets, and the results show the presence of the MIF pathway in a variety of cell-ligand-receptor interactions (Fig. 3A). The heatmap of cell communication reveals that monocytes, erythroid cells, dendritic cells, CMPs, and HSCs may all act as ligand cells, transmitting signals to other cells. Furthermore, the likelihood of interaction appears to be higher between closely related cell types (Fig. 3B). This was followed by the identification of the key senders, recipients, mediators and influencers of the MIF signalling pathway (Fig. 3C). Specifically, the majority of cells in the LUAD tumour microenvironment express high levels of MIF (Fig. 3D). The L-R couples MIF - (CD74 + CXCR4) and MIF - (CD74 + CD44) facilitated most of the communication between these cells, with MIF - (CD74 + CXCR4) making the largest contribution (Fig. 3E–G).

Identifying important genes and building a model for risk prediction

To avoid over-fitting, lasso regression was performed on 8 genes and 6 genes associated with prognosis were identified by determining the lambda values corresponding to the combination of variables when the mean square error was minimal, cox models were constructed using the 6 genes selected by AIC-based stepwise regression (Fig. 9A). CXCR4, FCGRT, CTSH are the protective genes(HR<1).KRT8, S100A9, MT2A are at high risk(HR>1) genes(Fig. 9B). The LUAD data from the TCGA were used to create risk profiles. The risk score was calculated as follows: risk score = (-0.1710 × CXCR4 expression) + (0.233 × KRT8 expression) + (0.0199 × S100A9 expression) + (-0.0891 × FCGRT expression) + (0.1268 × MT2A expression) + (-0.1211 × CTSH expression). To assess the predictive accuracy of the risk scores, we calculated the AUC values for one-year, three-year, and five-year overall survival using ROC curves on TCGA data. Additionally, we validated these risk scores on GEO datasets, confirming their utility across different cohorts.Time-dependent ROC curves further underscored the robust predictive performance of our model, with AUC values consistently exceeding 0.5 at 1, 3, and 5 years in both TCGA and GSE50081 datasets. In the GSE50081 dataset, the AUC for predicting 1, 3 and 5-year OS were 0.67, 0.64 and 0.70, respectively(Fig. 9E). The KM analysis demonstrated that the low-risk group exhibited a better prognosis compared to the high-risk group. This stratification was achieved by dividing the risk scores into high-risk and low-risk categories based on the median risk score. (Fig. 9F). Figure 9C shows the distribution of survival-related prediction models in the TCGA and GSE50081 datasets, with the GSE50081 dataset serving as the validation cohort (Fig. 9D). Individuals in the high-risk category had a shorter survival period and higher mortality risk. Significant changes were seen in the expression levels of BMRGs in both groups.

Discussion

The basement membrane (BM) is increasingly recognized as an important component of the tumor microenvironment in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD). It not only plays a crucial role in tumor initiation, progression, and metastasis but also in the interaction between tumor cells and the surrounding stroma. In recent years, bioinformatics approaches have provided new perspectives on understanding the role of BM in LUAD. Through high-throughput data analysis, researchers can explore the molecular mechanisms of BM in LUAD by analyzing transcriptomic, genomic, and proteomic data. For example, components of the basement membrane, such as collagen and laminin, exhibit different expression patterns in the development of LUAD, which may be closely associated with the migration, invasion, and metastatic potential of tumor cells [27, 28]. Through bioinformatics analysis, scholars have identified differentially expressed BM-related genes, further revealing their potential regulatory mechanisms in the tumor microenvironment. Moreover, network pharmacology and biomarker discovery approaches contribute to the identification of possible therapeutic targets. The application of bioinformatics methods allows us to systematically understand the role of the basement membrane in LUAD at the genome-wide level, providing new theoretical foundations and strategies for the precise treatment of LUAD.

Of the several LC types, LUAD is the most often diagnosed and the primary cause of mortality from cancer, despite some advancements in patient prognosis and longevity attributed to the development of targeted molecular and immunotherapy [29]. Nevertheless, the therapeutic efficacy of LUAD remains notably inadequate. Tumor heterogeneity is a major challenge in cancer treatment and is a confounding factor [30, 31].

In a subsequent study of intercellular communication, we found that MIF ligand-receptor pairs in the TME were significantly activated. Macrophage migration inhibitory factor (MIF) is a pleiotropic cytokine [32]expressedat almost all stages of the development of various human cancers. In addition, it promotes cell proliferation, development, progression and immune escape of tumours by exerting auto and paracrine effects on cancer cells, thus playing a multifunctional role in human cancer.

E-cadherin is responsible for the formation of focal adhesion complexes in malignant tumours, which maintain BM and cell-cell contact, and E-cadherin expression is reduced by MIF overexpression. The function of the focal adhesion complex is compromised by a decrease in E-cadherin expression, leading to epithelial-mesenchymal transition [33]. MIF and other angiogenic factors that are highly expressed cause matrix metalloproteinase proteins (MMPs) to be expressed more often.When the right homing factors are given to tumor cells, these enzymes break down the BM, allowing the tumor cells to enter the bloodstream and grow into secondary tumors in other organs [34].

In addition, the distribution and migration of CD8 T cells is controlled by the orientation, spacing and density of collagen fibers in the basement membrane matrix [35].Culture of A549 LC cells on highly rigid BM stroma induced expression of the receptor tyrosine kinases EGFR and c-Met, leading to increased tumor cell proliferation [36].TME has attracted increasing attention in research and drug development because it is an important component of evolution and ecology in the development and treatment of cancer. However, many clinical trials involving TME have not produced encouraging results in cancer patients.The exception is immunotherapy, which includes immune checkpoint blockade therapy [11, 37, 38]. Existing research has confirmed that biomarkers play a key role in regulating the tumor microenvironment [39]. Therefore, discovering reliable BM-associated biomarkers to guide targeted therapeutic strategies for TME is crucial for achieving successful cancer treatment [40].

From the standpoint of cell differentiation trajectories, we examined genetic variability in the LUAD tumor microenvironment. The development of scRNA-seq technology provides a new way to study the types of cells and their state of differentiation within tumors, as well as genes that are differentially expressed, particularly in resistant cancers [41]. The WGCNA identified 21 key DRGs affecting OS in LUAD patients, eight of which were related to BM. Cox regression analysis was used to construct BMRGs and nomograms including six genes (CXCR4, KRT8, S100A9, FCGRT, MT2A and CTSH).In addition, ROC analysis showed that the BMRGs had AUC values greater than 0.60 in predicting the annual survival of LUAD patients in both the TCGA and Geo cohorts. BMRG is a useful prognostic biomarker, as evidenced by the fact that the survival rate of LUAD patients in the low-risk group was greater than that of those in the high-risk group.

Six essential genes were found in this study and used to build the prognostic model. The reduced prognostic risk was associated with negative coefficients of CXCR4, FCGRT and CTSH. However, it has been found that LUAD progression and poor survival are associated with CXCR4 overexpression [42], which differs from these findings. It is mainly expressed in hematopoietic cell types and activates multiple signaling pathways to promote tumor cell invasion and distant metastasis, resulting in a poor prognosis [43, 44].On the other hand, the role of CXCR4 in LUAD has not been extensively studied and its value in LUAD is not clear. FCGRT influences cancer metabolism by modulating metabolic signaling pathways in both malignant and non-malignant cells within the tumor microenvironment, with the lung being a key organ expressing FcRn, a member of the IgG FC receptor family encoded by FCGRT. It is important for anti-tumour immunity [45]. A deficiency in FCGRT may hinder the growth and performance of innate immune cells, which would be detrimental to their prognosis. There is growing evidence that MT2A plays an important role in the development of several malignancies and is directly linked to the risk of developing several types of solid tumors [46]. It has a strong correlation with chemotherapy resistance, malignant biological behavior and tumor differentiation [47]. Its abnormal expression affects tumour development and prognosis and is a molecular marker for early diagnosis and targeted therapy. Cathepsins play an important role in protein catabolism. Extracellular cathepsins are thought to play a central role in cancer progression by regulating the structure of the extracellular matrix [48]. However, cathepsin H (CTSH) has received comparatively less attention in oncology research, overexpression of CTSH has been associated with more aggressive disease in breast cancer [49] and prostate cancer [50]. In LUAD, the knockdown of CTSH increases the ability of cells to migrate and invade, while reducing apoptosis. This is consistent with our conclusion that CTSH may have a protective effect by preventing LUAD cell proliferation, EMT, migration and invasion, while promoting LUAD cell death [51].

In contrast, positive coefficients for KRT8, S100A9 and MT2A were associated with an increased prognostic risk. KRT8 is a key component of the intermediate filament cytoskeleton [52]. Although its role in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is unclear, KRT8 is associated with poor prognosis in renal cell carcinoma and gastric cancer [53, 54]. It is also useful for measuring treatment response, monitoring tumor recurrence and guiding clinical decision-making.S100A9 is a calcium-binding protein that belongs to the S100 family of proteins [55]. Macrophages expressing S100A8 are recruited to sites of inflammation in many cancers [56]. In NSCLC, patients with early-stage LC who overexpressed S100A9 in their cancer cells had significantly worse 5-year overall survival [57]. These hub genes identified in our study warrant further investigation to validate their biological roles and prognostic significance.

Previous studies have shown that tumor mutations vary in different immune subtypes.To examine the mutation characteristics of the BMRGs subgroup, the largest mutation difference was TP53 mutation and the highest mutation rate was C5. The TP53 tumour suppressor gene produces the p53 protein. MIF interacts with p53 in a redox state-dependent manner, stabilising the link between p53 and MDM2, but also causing p53 production to decrease [58]. A major disruption of DNA repair pathways caused by inactivation of P53 function can lead to the accumulation of mutations and genomic instability, increasing the likelihood that cells will become malignant [59].

The studies showed that CD4 T cells need to recognize antigen-presenting cells in the immune response to regulate the activity of CD8 T cells [60], and a positive prognosis is predicted by strong CD8 T cell infiltration of the TME [61]. Tumour growth is aided by neutrophil polarisation caused by cytokine signaling in the microenvironment [62]. Our research confirms their finding that the low-risk group had a better prognosis, as they showed that the high-risk group had less anti-tumour immunity than the low-risk group. Erlotinib, gefitinib, crizotinib, cisplatin, trametinib, gemcitabine and savolitinib were also beneficial for people in the low-risk category.

However, several limitations remain in the methodology of this study. First, the data we utilized were obtained from a public database, and our cohort data have not been further verified. Moreover, the scRNA-seq analysis was conducted on only three LUAD samples, which may be insufficient to draw definitive conclusions regarding tumor heterogeneity or differentiation trajectories. Additionally, although our single-cell sequencing revealed gene expression at the cellular level and bioinformatics methods were used to predict and infer gene trajectories, these findings have not yet been experimentally validated. Therefore, future studies with larger sample sizes and experimental validation are necessary to confirm and expand upon these results.

Conclusion

To sum up, a single-cell sequencing data technique was applied in this investigation. Several techniques were used to assess the heterogeneity of BM in LUAD between the cell differentiation locus and functional enrichment. In addition, prognostic indicators were shown to accurately predict OS and provide survival estimates for patients with LUAD when combined with total RNA-seq data. Furthermore, our research contributes to a better understanding of how tumor infiltration is regulated in LUAD. However, further research is needed to confirm the current findings in both clinical and experimental settings.
