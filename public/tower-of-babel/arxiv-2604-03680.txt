Separating zeros of polynomials using an added interlacing point
Kerstin Jordaan, Vikash Kumar

Abstract
Following a systematic analysis of existing results, we investigate when complete interlacing between the zeros of distinct polynomial sequences, { 𝒫 n } \{\mathcal{P}_{n}\} and { 𝒢 n } \{\mathcal{G}_{n}\} can be achieved by using a naturally arising extra point. Specifically, we analyse several general mixed recurrence relations that ensure the n + 1 n+1 zeros of the polynomial ( x − E ) ​ 𝒫 n ​ ( x ) (x-E)\mathcal{P}_{n}(x) interlace with the k k zeros of 𝒢 k \mathcal{G}_{k} , where k = n k=n or n + 1 n+1 . In addition, we show that imposing specific conditions on the extra point E E yields full interlacing between the zeros of 𝒫 n \mathcal{P}_{n} and 𝒢 k \mathcal{G}_{k} for a suitable choice of n n . The approach provides a consolidated framework broadly applicable to both orthogonal and non-orthogonal polynomials and we illustrate this with new interlacing results for zeros of Krawtchouk, Meixner, and Narayana polynomials. We also illustrate that this general approach can be used to recover and refine existing results regarding the complete interlacing of zeros for classical Jacobi and Laguerre polynomials.

3.1
Krawtchouk polynomials

Monic Krawtchouk polynomials, denoted by
K_{n}(x;p,N)
, are defined in terms of the Gauss hypergeometric function as

K_{n}(x;p,N)=(-N)_{n}p^{n}\,{{}_{2}F_{1}}\left(\begin{matrix}-n,-x\\
-N\end{matrix};\frac{1}{p}\right).

(3.1)

These polynomials are orthogonal with respect to the discrete weight function supported on the set
\{0,1,\dots,N\}
, which is given by the binomial distribution

w(x;p,N)=\binom{N}{x}p^{x}(1-p)^{N-x},\quad x\in\{0,1,\dots,N\},

(3.2)

with parameters
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<p<1
, and
0\leq n\leq N

These monic polynomials satisfy the following three-term recurrence relation (cf.
[
16
, page 237]

\displaystyle K_{n+2}(x;p,N+1)

\displaystyle=(x-(n+1)(1-p)-(N-n)p)K_{n+1}(x;p,N+1)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-(n+1)p(1-p)(N+1-n)K_{n}(x;p,N+1)

(3.3)

Next, we derive a mixed recurrence relation for monic Krawtchouk polynomials with parameters
N
and
N+1
. This relation explicitly identifies a linear factor governing the interlacing of zeros.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

denote monic Krawtchouk polynomials of degree
n
with parameters
0<p<1,N\in\mathbb{N}
and

. Then the following mixed recurrence relation holds

p(1-p)(n+1)(N+1-n)K_{n}(x;p,N+1)\\
=(N+1-x)K_{n+1}(x;p,N)+\Big(x-(N+1)+p(n+1)\Big)K_{n+1}(x;p,N+1).

(3.4)

Proof.

We use the Christoffel transformation
[
4
, page 35]
connecting Krawtchouk measures with parameters
N
and
N+1

w(x;p,N)=\frac{N+1-x}{(N+1)(1-p)}w(x;p,N+1),

the monic polynomial of degree

evaluated at

, orthogonal with respect to

, is expressed as
\displaystyle K_{n+1}(x;p,N)

=\frac{1}{N+1-x}\left(-K_{n+2}(x;p,N+1)+\frac{K_{n+2}(N+1;p,N+1)}{K_{n+1}(N+1;p,N+1)}K_{n+1}(x;p,N+1)\right).

(3.5)

Employing the evaluation

K_{k}(N+1;p,N+1)=k!\binom{N+1}{k}(1-p)^{k},

this simplifies (
3.5
) and we may write the relation as

(N+1-x)K_{n+1}(x;p,N)=(N-n)(1-p)K_{n+1}(x;p,N+1)-K_{n+2}(x;p,N+1).

(3.6)

Substituting (
3.1
) into (
3.6
) to eliminate
K_{n+2}
and grouping the terms for
K_{n+1}(x;p,N+1)
and

gives

\displaystyle(N+1-x)K_{n+1}(x;p,N)=(n+1)p(1-p)(N+1-n)K_{n}(x;p,N+1)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\Big[(N-n)(1-p)-x+(n+1)(1-p)+(N-n)p)\Big]K_{n+1}(x;p,N+1).

Finally, we simplify the bracketed term
[\cdot]
and rearrange to obtain (
3.4
).
∎

We define monic Meixner polynomials, denoted by

\displaystyle M_{n+2}(x;t,w)

\displaystyle=\left(x+\frac{n+1+w(n+t+1)}{w-1}\right)M_{n+1}(x;t,w)-\frac{w(n+1)(n+t)}{(w-1)^{2}}M_{n}(x;t,w).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
M_{n}(x;t,w)
denote the monic Meixner polynomial of degree
n
with parameters

and
0<w<1
. These polynomials satisfy the following mixed recurrence relation

\displaystyle\frac{w(n+1)(n+t)}{(w-1)^{2}}M_{n}(x;t,w)

\displaystyle=-(x+t)M_{n+1}(x;t+1,w)+\left(x+t+\frac{w(n+1)}{w-1}\right)M_{n+1}(x;t,w)

(3.12)

Proof.

The Christoffel transformation
[
4
, page 35]
applied to the weight (
3.10
) at the point

\displaystyle\rho(x;t+1,w)=\frac{x+t}{t}\rho(x;t,w).

(3.13)

The corresponding Christoffel-transformed polynomials are given by

\displaystyle M_{n+1}(x;t+1,w)=\frac{1}{x+t}\left[M_{n+2}(x;t,w)-\frac{n+t+1}{w-1}M_{n+1}(x;t,w)\right].

Substituting the three-term recurrence (

The polynomial

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)=x^{n-1}\mathcal{N}_{n}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right).

Furthermore, the zeros of
\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)

(-\infty,0)

[
17
, Corollary 7]
.

We define the Christoffel-transformed polynomial related to reduced Narayana polynomials as

\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\tilde{c}_{n,j}x^{j}=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{n+1}(x)-\rho_{n}\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)}{x-1},\quad\text{with }\rho_{n}=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{n+1}(1)}{\mathcal{N}_{n}(1)}.

(3.19)

This example is of particular interest because reduced Narayana polynomials fall outside the classical orthogonal framework considered in the previous subsections.

Subsequently, we demonstrate that the interlacing property is preserved under the perturbation in the coefficients of reduced Narayana polynomials defined in (
3.19
). Specifically, we show that the zeros of reduced Narayana polynomials interlace with the zeros of the polynomials generated by these perturbed coefficients.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)
be the monic reduced Narayana polynomial of degree
n-1
, and let
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(x)
be the polynomial associated with the perturbed coefficients defined in (
3.19
). Then,
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(x)\prec\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)
.

Proof.

Recall the three-term recurrence relation for standard Narayana polynomials
N_{n}(x)
given in
[
19
, Eq. 3.3]

(n+2)N_{n+1}(x)=(2n+1)(x+1)N_{n}(x)-(n-1)(x-1)^{2}N_{n-1}(x).

(3.20)

Substituting
N_{k}(x)=x\mathcal{N}_{k}(x)
for
k=n-1,n,n+1
, we obtain the recurrence for the reduced polynomials

(n+2)\mathcal{N}_{n+1}(x)=(2n+1)(x+1)\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)-(n-1)(x-1)^{2}\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(x).

(3.21)

Evaluating (
3.21
) at
x=1
yields

\rho_{n}=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{n+1}(1)}{\mathcal{N}_{n}(1)}=\frac{2(2n+1)}{n+2}.

Substituting this value of
\rho_{n}
into (
3.19
), we have

(x-1)\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(x)=\mathcal{N}_{n+1}(x)-\frac{2(2n+1)}{n+2}\mathcal{N}_{n}(x).

(3.22)

Next, we substitute the expression for
\mathcal{N}_{n+1}(x)
from (
3.21
) into (
3.22
)

\displaystyle(x-1)\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{2n+1}{n+2}(x+1)\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)-\frac{n-1}{n+2}(x-1)^{2}\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(x)\right]-\frac{2(2n+1)}{n+2}\mathcal{N}_{n}(x).

Grouping the terms containing
\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)
and simplifying, we obtain

\frac{n+2}{n-1}\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(x)=\frac{2n+1}{n-1}\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)-(x-1)\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(x).

(3.23)

It is known that
\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)\prec\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(x)

[
17
, Corollary 7]
. Setting
\mathcal{G}_{n}(x):=\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)
,
\mathcal{Q}_{n-1}(x):=\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(x)
, and
\mathcal{P}_{n}(x):=\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(x)
, equation (
3.23
) satisfies the conditions required by Theorem
2.2
. Hence,
(x-1)\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(x)\prec\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)
and the result follows.
∎

Using the relation given in (
3.23
), we can derive a compact form for the coefficients
\tilde{c}_{n,j}
of the polynomial
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(x)
expressed in terms of the Narayana numbers.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n}(x)
be the polynomial of degree
n-1
defined in (
3.19
). The coefficients
\tilde{c}_{n,j}
satisfy the following relation

\tilde{c}_{n,j}=\frac{3n-2j}{n+2}c_{n,j+1}.

(3.24)

Proof.

By comparing the coefficients of
x^{j}
on both sides of (
3.23
) and applying the boundary conditions
c_{n-1,0}=0
and
c_{n-1,n}=0
, we obtain the equation

\frac{n+2}{n-1}\tilde{c}_{n,j}=\frac{2n+1}{n-1}c_{n,j+1}-(c_{n-1,j}-c_{n-1,j+1}).

(3.25)

We observe the following identities relating the coefficients
c_{n-1,k}
and
c_{n,j+1}

\displaystyle\frac{c_{n-1,j+1}}{c_{n,j+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j+1}\binom{n-1}{j}}{\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{j+1}\binom{n}{j}}=\frac{(n-j-1)(n-j)}{n(n-1)}

\displaystyle\implies c_{n-1,j+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-j)(n-j-1)}{n(n-1)}c_{n,j+1},

(3.26)

and similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{c_{n-1,j}}{c_{n,j+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}\binom{n-1}{j-1}}{\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{j+1}\binom{n}{j}}=\frac{n}{n-1}\cdot\frac{j+1}{n}\cdot\frac{j}{n}

\displaystyle\implies c_{n-1,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{j(j+1)}{n(n-1)}c_{n,j+1}.

(3.27)

Substituting these expressions into the coefficient equation (
3.25
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{n+2}{n-1}\tilde{c}_{n,j}

\displaystyle=c_{n,j+1}\left[\frac{2n+1}{n-1}-\frac{j(j+1)}{n(n-1)}+\frac{(n-j)(n-j-1)}{n(n-1)}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{n,j+1}}{n(n-1)}\left[n(2n+1)-j(j+1)+(n-j)(n-j-1)\right].

Simplifying the above equation leads to the desired expression (
3.24
).
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)
be the reduced Narayana polynomial of degree
n-1
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}d_{n,j}x^{j}
be the polynomial defined by the coefficients

\displaystyle d_{n,j}=\binom{n-1}{j}^{2}+\binom{n-1}{j+1}\binom{n-1}{j-1}

(3.28)

Then, for any odd integer
n\geq 3
,
(x+1)\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)\prec\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)
, while for every even integer
n\geq 2
,
\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)\prec\frac{\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)}{x+1}
.

Proof.

We first establish the polynomial identity that relates
\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)
,
\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(x)
, and
\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)
.
Let
c_{n,j+1}=\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{j+1}\binom{n}{j}
be the coefficient of
x^{j}
in
\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)
.

We apply Pascal’s Identity,
\binom{n}{r}=\binom{n-1}{r}+\binom{n-1}{r-1}
, to both binomial terms in the product

\displaystyle nc_{n,j+1}=\left[\binom{n-1}{j+1}+\binom{n-1}{j}\right]\cdot\left[\binom{n-1}{j}+\binom{n-1}{j-1}\right].

(3.29)

Expanding this product yields

\displaystyle nc_{n,j+1}=\underbrace{\left[\binom{n-1}{j}^{2}+\binom{n-1}{j+1}\binom{n-1}{j-1}\right]}_{d_{n,j}}+\left[\binom{n-1}{j}\binom{n-1}{j-1}+\binom{n-1}{j+1}\binom{n-1}{j}\right].

(3.30)

We now reconstruct the polynomials by multiplying the above equation by
x^{j}
and summing over
j=0
to
n-1

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}nc_{n,j+1}x^{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}d_{n,j}x^{j}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j+1}\binom{n-1}{j}x^{j}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}\binom{n-1}{j-1}x^{j}.

(3.31)

The second term in the right-hand side of (
3.31
) can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j+1}\binom{n-1}{j}x^{j}=(n-1)\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(x).

(3.32)

Similarly, the third term in the right-hand side of (
3.31
) can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}\binom{n-1}{j-1}x^{j}

\displaystyle=x\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\binom{n-1}{j+1}\binom{n-1}{j}x^{j}=x(n-1)\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(x).

(3.33)

Substituting (
3.32
) and (
3.33
) in (
3.31
), we obtain

\displaystyle n\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)=\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)+(n-1)\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(x)+x(n-1)\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(x).

Rearranging the term, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-1}\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)=\frac{n}{n-1}\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)-(x+1)\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(x).

(3.34)

It is known that
\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)\prec\mathcal{N}_{n-1}(x)

[
17
, Corollary 7]
. By the reciprocal property (
3.18
) of reduced Narayana polynomials, we see that
x=-1
is a zero of polynomials
\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)
for any even integer
n\geq 2
. Hence, for any odd integer
n\geq 3
,
\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
\mathcal{N}_{n}
share no common zero. Consequently, the identity (
3.34
) satisfies the conditions of Theorem
2.2
, and the result for odd degree follows. Moreover, for every even integer
n\geq 2
, the relation (
3.34
) implies that
\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)
and
\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)
share a common zero at
x=-1
. Consequently, for every even integer
n\geq 2
, the zeros of
\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)
, defined in (
3.28
), interlace the zeros of the quotient polynomial
\frac{\mathcal{N}_{n}(x)}{x+1}
.
∎

3.4
Jacobi polynomials

Monic Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
form an orthogonal sequence on the interval
(-1,1)
with respect to the measure
d\mu(x)=(1-x)^{\alpha}(1+x)^{\beta}\,dx,
where
\alpha>-1
and
\beta>-1

[
4
]
. These polynomials satisfy the following three-term recurrence relation

\displaystyle P_{n+1}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)

\displaystyle=(x-c^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n+1})P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)-\lambda^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n+1}P_{n-1}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x),

(3.35)

with the initial conditions
P_{0}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)=1,\quad P_{-1}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)=0.
The recurrence coefficients
c_{n+1}
and
\lambda_{n+1}
are given by

\displaystyle\lambda^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{4n(n+\alpha)(n+\beta)(n+\alpha+\beta)}{(2n+\alpha+\beta)^{2}(2n+\alpha+\beta+1)(2n+\alpha+\beta-1)},

\displaystyle c^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{2}-\alpha^{2}}{(2n+\alpha+\beta)(2n+\alpha+\beta+2)}.

(3.36)

Corollary 3.5
.

[
2
, Theorem 1]
.
Let
P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)
be a monic Jacobi polynomial of degree
n
and denote the zeros of the polynomial
P^{(\alpha,\beta+1)}_{n+1}(x)
, in ascending order, by
\{x_{k,n+1}\}_{k=1}^{n+1}
. Then,
x_{n+1,n+1}>E_{n,\alpha,\beta}:=-1+\frac{2(n+1)(n+\alpha+1)}{(2n+\alpha+\beta+2)(2n+\alpha+\beta+3)}
and
(x-E_{n,\alpha,\beta})P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)\prec P^{(\alpha,\beta+1)}_{n+1}(x)
for any
\alpha>-1,\beta>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover,
P^{(\alpha,\beta+1)}_{n+1}(x)\prec P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)
for suitable choice of
n\in\mathbb{N},\alpha>-1,\beta>0
if and only if the smallest zero of
P^{(\alpha,\beta+1)}_{n+1}(x)
is greater than the point
E_{n,\alpha,\beta}
(i.e.
x_{1,n+1}>E_{n,\alpha,\beta}
).

Proof.

This result was proved in
[
2
, Theorem 1]
and can be shown to be a straightforward consequence of Theorem
2.1
:
Let
\{y_{k,n+1}\}_{k=1}^{n+1}
denote the zeros of the polynomial
P^{(\alpha,\beta-1)}_{n+1}(x)
. Using (cf.
[
2
, eq. (4)]
), the monic Jacobi polynomial
P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)
satisfies the following mixed three-term recurrence relation

\displaystyle M_{n,\alpha,\beta}\left(x+1+\frac{2\beta}{2n+\alpha+\beta+3}\right)P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)=-(n+\alpha+\beta+1)_{2}(x+1)P^{(\alpha,\beta+1)}_{n+1}(x)

\displaystyle+\left(x+1-\frac{2(n+1)(n+\alpha+1)}{(2n+\alpha+\beta+2)(2n+\alpha+\beta+3)}\right)P^{(\alpha,\beta-1)}_{n+1}(x),

(3.37)

where
M_{n,\alpha,\beta}=\frac{2(n+1)(n+\alpha+\beta)_{2}}{(2n+\alpha+\beta+1)_{2}}
for
n\in\mathbb{N},\alpha>-1,\beta>0
. Denoting
\mathcal{G}_{n+1}(x):=P^{(\alpha,\beta+1)}_{n+1}(x)
,
\mathcal{Q}_{n+1}(x):=P^{(\alpha,\beta-1)}_{n+1}(x)
, and
\mathcal{P}_{n}(x):=P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)
, the zeros of
\mathcal{G}_{n+1}(x)
and
\mathcal{Q}_{n+1}(x)
satisfy the interlacing property (
4.1
) using (cf.
[
7
, Theorem 2.4]
) for
\alpha>-1,\beta>0
. Hence, the result directly follows by applying Theorem
2.1
(1).∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)
be a monic Jacobi polynomial of degree
n
. Let
\{x_{k,n}\}_{k=1}^{n}
denote the zeros of the polynomial
P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n}(x)

and define

\displaystyle E_{n,\alpha,\beta}:=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2n+\alpha+\beta+2},

(3.38)

for any
\alpha>-1,\beta>-1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
(x-E_{n,\alpha,\beta})P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)\prec P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n}(x)
. In particular,
P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n}(x)\prec P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)
, whenever
E_{n,\alpha,\beta}<x_{1,n}
while
P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)\prec P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n}(x)
, whenever
E_{n,\alpha,\beta}>x_{n,n}
for a suitable choice of
n\in\mathbb{N},\alpha>-1,\beta>-1
.

Proof.

It is known
[
10
, eq. (8)]
that the monic Jacobi polynomial satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{n+\alpha+\beta+1}{n}\,P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2n+\alpha+\beta+1}{n}\,P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n}(x)-\left(x-\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2n+\alpha+\beta+2}\right)P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n-1}(x).

(3.39)

Setting

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle:=P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n}(x),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle:=P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n-1}(x),

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle:=P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)

and

\displaystyle E:=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2n+\alpha+\beta+2},

the relation rewrites as

\displaystyle A(x)\,\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)=B(x)\,\mathcal{G}_{n}(x)-(x-E)\,\mathcal{Q}_{n-1}(x)

with positive constants

\displaystyle A(x)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{n+\alpha+\beta+1}{n}>0,

\displaystyle B(x)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{2n+\alpha+\beta+1}{n}>0.

Since
P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n}(x)\prec P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n-1}(x)
,
B(E)>0\neq 0
, and
P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)
and
P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n}(x)
share no common zeros, all assumptions of Theorem
2.2
are satisfied. The asserted interlacing properties follow immediately.
∎

3.5
Laguerre polynomials

Monic Laguerre polynomials
\{L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
form an orthogonal sequence on the interval
(0,\infty)
with respect to the measure
d\mu(x)=x^{\alpha}e^{-x}\,dx,
where
\alpha>-1

[
4
]
. This sequence satisfies a three-term recurrence relation

L_{n+1}^{(\alpha)}(x)=(x-c_{n+1}^{(\alpha)})L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)-\lambda_{n+1}^{(\alpha)}L_{n-1}^{(\alpha)}(x),

(3.40)

with initial conditions
L_{0}^{(\alpha)}(x)=1
and
L_{-1}^{(\alpha)}(x)=0
. The recurrence coefficients
c_{n+1}^{(\alpha)}
and
\lambda_{n+1}^{(\alpha)}
are explicitly given by
c_{n+1}^{(\alpha)}=2n+\alpha+1
and
\lambda_{n+1}^{(\alpha)}=n(n+\alpha)
.

Corollary 3.7
.

[
1
, Theorem 2.1]

Let
L^{(\alpha)}_{n}(x)
be a monic Laguerre polynomial of degree
n
with
\alpha>-1
. Let
\{z_{k,n}\}_{k=1}^{n}
, and
\{x_{k,n+1}\}_{k=1}^{n+1}
denote the zeros of the polynomials
L^{(\alpha)}_{n}(x)
and
L^{(\alpha+1)}_{n+1}(x)
, respectively, in ascending order. Then,
x_{n+1,n+1}>n+1
and
(x-n-1)L^{(\alpha)}_{n}(x)\prec L^{(\alpha+1)}_{n+1}(x)
for any
\alpha>-1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover,
L^{(\alpha+1)}_{n+1}(x)\prec L^{(\alpha)}_{n}(x)
for suitable choice of
n\in\mathbb{N},\alpha>-1
if and only if the smallest zero of
L^{(\alpha+1)}_{n+1}(x)
is greater than the point
n+1
(i.e.
x_{1,n+1}>n+1
).

Proof.

This result was proved in
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
and is a direct consequence of Theorem
2.1
: Let
\{y_{k,n+1}\}_{k=1}^{n+1}
denote the zeros of the polynomial
L^{(\alpha)}_{n+1}(x)
. Using (cf.
[
8
, eq. (1)]
), the monic Laguerre polynomial
L^{(\alpha)}_{n}(x)
satisfies the following mixed three-term recurrence relation

\displaystyle(n+1)(n+\alpha+1)L^{(\alpha)}_{n}(x)=-xL^{(\alpha+1)}_{n+1}(x)+(x-n-1)L^{(\alpha)}_{n+1}(x),

(3.41)

for
n\in\mathbb{N},\alpha>-1
. Denoting
\mathcal{G}_{n+1}(x):=L^{(\alpha+1)}_{n+1}(x)
,
\mathcal{Q}_{n+1}(x):=L^{(\alpha)}_{n+1}(x)
, and
\mathcal{P}_{n}(x):=L^{(\alpha)}_{n}(x)
, the zeros of
\mathcal{G}_{n+1}(x)
and
\mathcal{Q}_{n+1}(x)
satisfy (
4.1
) by
[
8
, Theorem 2.3]
. Hence, the result follows immediately from Theorem
2.1
(1).
∎

Note that the lower bound
n+1
of the largest zero
x_{n+1,n+1}
of
L^{(\alpha+1)}_{n+1}(x)
is explicitly proved in Corollary
3.7
whereas the lower bound is stated in
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
without a clear proof.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.
Suppose that the zeros of
\mathcal{G}_{n+1}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{n+1}
, denoted by

and
\{y_{k,n+1}\}_{k=1}^{n+1}
, respectively, satisfy the alternating property

\displaystyle y_{1,n+1}<x_{1,n+1}<y_{2,n+1}<x_{2,n+1}<\cdots<x_{n,n+1}<y_{n+1,n+1}<x_{n+1,n+1}

(4.1)

or

\displaystyle x_{1,n+1}<y_{1,n+1}<x_{2,n+1}<y_{2,n+1}<\cdots<y_{n,n+1}<x_{n+1,n+1}<y_{n+1,n+1},

(4.2)

on the (finite or infinite) interval
(a,b)
.
Note that
E\neq x_{k,n+1}

, since if
E=x_{k,n+1}
for some

, which is zero of
\mathcal{G}_{n+1}
then by (

),

5
Conclusion

The results obtained in this study provide a systematic analysis for completing the interlacing of zeros of distinct polynomial sequences by involving an additional interlacing point,
E
, that appears naturally in the general mixed recurrence relations used to prove the results.

The general mixed recurrence relations introduced here serve as a versatile tool for obtaining new interlacing results. Because the main findings of this work are independent of orthogonality constraints, the framework is equally effective for both orthogonal and non-orthogonal sequences. This is demonstrated through novel interlacing results derived for Krawtchouk, Meixner, and Narayana polynomials. We anticipate that the generality of our framework will facilitate the discovery of new interlacing properties across a much wider family of polynomials.

Beyond new examples, the analysis also reinforces existing literature for Laguerre and Jacobi polynomials. Our general approach provides straightforward proofs for the interlacing properties of classical Jacobi (see Corollaries
3.5
and
3.6
) and Laguerre polynomials (see Corollary
3.7
), effectively recovering the results presented in
[
2
, Theorem 1]
,
[
2
, Theorem 3]
, and
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
. More importantly, by precisely evaluating the conditions imposed on
E
, our framework improves the interlacing results established in
[
2
, Theorem 3]
(see Corollary
3.6
and Remark
3.1
and
3.2
). We also prove full interlacing of zeros of these polynomials under assumption of appropriate position(s) for the extra point,
E
.
