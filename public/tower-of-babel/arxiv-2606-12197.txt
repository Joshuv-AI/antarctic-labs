On Brouwer’s Laplacian conjecture
Pravesh K. Kothari, Stefan Tudose

Abstract
. Brouwer’s Laplacian conjecture states that the sum of the largest k k eigenvalues of a graph’s Laplacian is less than or equal to the number of edges plus ( k + 1 2 ) \binom{k+1}{2} . We give a proof of this conjecture. Our proof relies on the Grone–Merris–Bai theorem for split graphs. We also show the converse implication, giving an equivalence between Brouwer’s conjecture and the Grone–Merris–Bai theorem.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a simple graph on
n
vertices and
m
edges. Let
A
be its
n\times n
adjacency matrix and let
D
be the diagonal matrix with
D(i,i)=d_{i}
, the degree of vertex
i
. The Laplacian of
G
is the positive semidefinite matrix
L=D-A
. In the following, we write
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{n}=0
, for the eigenvalues of the Laplacian of a graph
G
.

Brouwer conjectured
[
4
,
16
]
the following bound on the sum of top
k

G
:

Conjecture 1

(Brouwer’s Laplacian conjecture)
.

The following inequality holds:

\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{k}\leq m+\dbinom{k+1}{2},

for any
1\leq k\leq n
.

Brouwer’s conjecture is a basic problem in spectral graph theory and has attracted substantial attention since its formulation by Brouwer and Haemers
[
4
,
16
]
. There are two main kinds of results known about the problem. The first kind proves the conjecture for special classes of graphs
[
16
,
11
,
9
,
26
,
22
,
18
,
25
]
or under additional structural assumptions such as bounds on girth, vertex cover, diameter and other such graph parameters
[
12
,
6
,
7
,
13
,
8
]
. The second line of work proves
approximate
results that hold for every graph
[
8
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
. Recent work has also studied closely related Kikuchi-graph and token-graph viewpoints, with applications to Brouwer-type eigenvalue bounds and bounds on the ground state energy of certain quantum Hamiltonians
[
21
,
3
]
.

Brouwer was motivated by a related conjecture of Grone and Merris about the relationship between the Laplacian spectrum of a graph and its degree sequence. The main observation is that for
threshold
graphs, the Laplacian eigenvalues, written in decreasing order, are exactly equal to the
conjugate degree sequence

[
14
,
23
,
17
]
.

Definition 1.1

(Conjugate Degree Sequence)
.

For a graph
G
, let
d_{k}^{\prime}
be the number of vertices in
G
of degree at least
k
. Then,
(d_{1}^{\prime},d_{2}^{\prime},\cdots,d_{n}^{\prime})
is called the
conjugate
degree sequence of
G
.

Grone and Merris conjectured that the threshold-graph comparison is an extreme case and that in fact for any graph the Laplacian eigenvalue sequence is
majorized
by the conjugate degree sequence
[
15
]
. Recall that a sequence
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
is majorized by a sequence
b=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
if the inequality
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{j}a_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{j}b_{i}
is true for any
1\leq j\leq n-1
and
a_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}=b_{1}+\ldots+b_{n}
.

Theorem 1.2

(Grone-Merris-Bai)
.

The following inequality holds

\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{k}\leq d_{1}^{\prime}+\ldots+d_{k}^{\prime},

for any
1\leq k\leq n
.

The Grone-Merris conjecture was proved in full by Bai
[
2
]
. Before Bai’s proof, Stephen proved several special cases
[
24
]
, including all trees, and gave an equivalent formulation in terms of Dirichlet Laplacians. Later work of Abiad, Fiol, Haemers, and Perarnau
[
1
]
developed related eigenvalue interlacing bounds for sums of the largest Laplacian eigenvalues. We note that the Grone-Merris conjecture has also inspired analogs for simplicial complexes, notably, the Duval–Reiner conjecture
[
10
]
.

In this paper, we prove Brouwer’s conjecture using the Grone-Merris-Bai theorem. We also show a simple proof of the Grone-Merris-Bai theorem from Brouwer’s conjecture showing that the two statements are in a sense equivalent. Curiously, our proof of Brouwer’s theorem invokes the Grone-Merris bound for “split graphs” that also arise as the main technical case in the proof of
[
2
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Our proof of Brouwer’s conjecture is based on a nuclear norm bound on “centered” Laplacian of
split graphs
. Let us recall some relevant definitions and set up some basic notation.

Definition 2.1

(Split Graph)
.

A graph
G
on vertex set
[n]
is a
split graph
if there is a subset
K\subseteq[n]
such that the induced subgraph on
K
is a clique and the induced subgraph on
S=[n]\setminus K
is an independent set.

Notation:
Let
J=11^{T}
be the all ones matrix and
C=I_{n}-\frac{1}{n}J
be the centering matrix. We denote by
\left\|A\right\|_{*}
the nuclear norm of the matrix
A
. For a graph
G
and disjoint vertex sets
K,S
, we write
E(K,S)
to denote the number of “cut” edges with one end point in
K
and
S
each.

3.
Results and Proof Plan

We give a proof of Brouwer’s conjecture in this work.

Theorem 3.1

(Brouwer’s Conjecture)
.

For any graph
G
with Laplacian eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\lambda_{n}\geq 0
, we have:

\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{k}\leq m+\dbinom{k+1}{2},

for any
1\leq k\leq n
.

Our proof relies on a bound on the nuclear norm of the “centered” Laplacians of split graphs formulated below.

Lemma 3.2

(Centered Split Graph Nuclear Norm Bound)
.

Let
H
be a split graph which has a clique
K
and an independent set
S
. The following inequality holds

\left\|L_{H}-|K|C\right\|_{*}\leq|K|\cdot|S|.

We will prove Lemma
3.2
using the Grone-Merris-Bai theorem. We need the Grone-Merris-Bai theorem only for the case of split graphs but note that split graphs are the main “hard” case in Bai’s proof of the Grone-Merris conjecture.

We next show that Brouwer’s conjecture implies the following trace inequality for all split graphs.

\mathrm{\text{tr}}\left(Q(L_{H}-|K|I_{n})\right)\leq E(K,S)

4.
All but Brouwer

We defer the proof that Lemma
3.2
implies Brouwer’s conjecture to the next section and present simple proofs of the remaining implications in this section.

Grone-Merris-Bai implies Lemma
3.2
.

Let
r=|K|,\ s=|S|,\ n=r+s
and let
\lambda_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{n}
be the eigenvalues of the Laplacian
L_{H}
of
H
.
By Grone-Merris-Bai, for any
1\leq k\leq n
we have the inequality

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}^{\prime}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\min(d_{i},k)=\sum\limits_{i\in K}\min(d_{i},k)+\sum\limits_{j\in S}\min(d_{j},k)\leq kr+E(K,S).

The identity
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}^{\prime}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\min(d_{i},k)
follows by double counting the pairs
(i,j)
with
d_{i}\geq j,\ 1\leq j\leq k
.

Let
t=\#\{i\leq n-1:\lambda_{i}>r\}
. If
t=0
, then

\sum_{\lambda_{i}>r}(\lambda_{i}-r)=0
. If
t\geq 1
, the preceding estimate
with
k=t
gives

\sum_{\lambda_{i}>r}(\lambda_{i}-r)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\lambda_{i}-tr\leq E(K,S).

Since
1
is an eigenvector with eigenvalue
0
for both
L_{H}
and
C
, we obtain

\left\|L_{H}-rC\right\|_{*}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}|\lambda_{i}-r|=2\sum\limits_{\lambda_{i}>r}(\lambda_{i}-r)+rs-2E(K,S)\leq rs.

∎

Brouwer’s Laplacian conjecture implies Lemma
3.3
.

Let
r=|K|,\ s=|S|,\ n=r+s
and
q=\text{rank}\ Q
. Define
\overline{Q}=C-Q
and let
\overline{H}
be the complement graph of
H
. We use the complement to reduce to the case
\operatorname{rank}Q<r
.

Since
Q1=0
, we have
Q\preceq C
, so
\overline{Q}=C-Q
is again an
orthogonal projection and
\overline{Q}1=0
. Moreover
\operatorname{rank}\overline{Q}=n-1-q
.
If
q\geq r
, then

\operatorname{rank}\overline{Q}=n-1-q\leq n-1-r=s-1<s
,
so the complementary split graph has clique size
s
and falls into the
case
\operatorname{rank}\overline{Q}<s
.

Now, observe that
\text{tr}\left(\overline{Q}(L_{\overline{H}}-sI_{n})\right)=\text{tr}\left(Q(L_{H}-rI_{n})\right)+rs-2E(K,S)
. Thus,

\text{tr}\left(Q(L_{H}-rI_{n})\right)\leq E(K,S)\Leftrightarrow\text{tr}\left(\overline{Q}(L_{\overline{H}}-sI_{n})\right)\leq rs-E(K,S).

We can therefore assume without loss of generality that
\text{rank}\ Q=q<r
by working with
(\overline{Q},\overline{H})
instead of
(Q,H)
if necessary.

Define the subspace
\mathcal{K}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ \langle x,1_{K}\rangle=0,\ x|_{S}=0\}
. This is an
r-1
dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be a
r-1-q
dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{K}\cap(\text{Im}\ Q)^{\perp}
, and let
Q_{\mathcal{L}}
be the orthogonal projection onto this subspace.
For
v\in\mathcal{K}
we have

\langle v,L_{H}v\rangle=\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in E(H)}\left\|v_{i}-v_{j}\right\|^{2}\geq\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in E(K)}\left\|v_{i}-v_{j}\right\|^{2}=r\sum\limits_{i\in K}v_{i}^{2}-\left\|\sum\limits_{i\in K}v_{i}\right\|^{2}=r\left\|v\right\|^{2}.

This in turn implies

\text{tr}(Q_{\mathcal{L}}L_{H})\geq r\text{dim}\ \mathcal{L}=r\left(r-1-q\right).

Since
Q+Q_{\mathcal{L}}
is a projection of rank
r-1
, the trace of
L_{H}
on its image is at most the sum of the largest
r-1
eigenvalues of
L_{H}
. Thus, by Brouwer’s inequality (Theorem
3.1
), we obtain

\text{tr}\left((Q+Q_{\mathcal{L}})L_{H}\right)\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}\lambda_{i}(H)\leq r(r-1)+E(K,S),

and the desired inequality follows.
∎

Lemma
3.3
implies Grone-Merris-Bai.

Order the vertices so that
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\ldots\geq d_{n}
. We will show that

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda_{i}-r)_{+}\leq\sum\limits_{i=r+1}^{n}d_{i},

for every
0\leq r\leq n
. The case
r=0
follows from
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=2m=\sum_{i}d_{i}
. Let
1\leq r\leq n
.
Let
Q
be the projection on the eigenspace corresponding to the set of
>0
eigenvalues of
L_{G}-rI_{n}
so that

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda_{i}-r)_{+}=\text{tr}(Q(L_{G}-rI_{n}))
.

Let
H
be the split graph obtained from
G
by taking the set
K
of the vertices with the largest
r
degrees and making it a clique, taking the set
S
of the vertices with the smallest
s=n-r
degrees and making it an independent set, and keeping any edges in-between
K
and
S
. We have

\text{tr}(QL_{G})=\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in E(G)}\left\|Qe_{i}-Qe_{j}\right\|^{2}\leq 2e+\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in E(H)}\left\|Qe_{i}-Qe_{j}\right\|^{2}=\text{tr}(QL_{H})+2e,

where
e
is the number of edges removed, i.e. the edges between the vertices with the smallest
s
degrees. By Lemma
3.3
we obtain

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda_{i}-r)_{+}=\text{tr}(Q(L_{G}-rI_{n}))\leq\text{tr}(Q(L_{H}-rI_{n}))+2e\leq E(K,S)+2e=\sum\limits_{i=r+1}^{n}d_{i}.

We now use two elementary facts. First,
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\leq rk+\sum_{i}(\lambda_{i}-r)_{+}
for every
r
. Next, since the degrees are in the descending order,
\min_{0\leq r\leq n}\bigl(rk+\sum_{i=r+1}^{n}d_{i}\bigr)=\sum_{i}\min(d_{i},k)
. We thus obtain the Grone-Merris-Bai inequality:

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\leq\min_{0\leq r\leq n}\left(rk+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda_{i}-r)_{+}\right)\leq\min_{0\leq r\leq n}\left(rk+\sum\limits_{i=r+1}^{n}d_{i}\right)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\min(d_{i},k)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}^{\prime}.

∎

5.
Brouwer’s Laplacian conjecture

In this section, we will show that Lemma
3.2
implies Brouwer’s Laplacian conjecture.

Lemma 5.1

(Eigenvalue Sums Reduce to Projections)
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices and
m
edges which has Laplacian
L
. Fix
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Suppose that every orthogonal projection
P
of rank
k
satisfying
P1=0
obeys

\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in E(G)}(P_{ii}+P_{jj}-2P_{ij}-1)\leq\dbinom{k+1}{2}.

Then

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(L)\leq m+\dbinom{k+1}{2}.

Proof.

Since
L1=0
, the subspace
1^{\perp}
is invariant under
L
and the eigenvalues of the restriction of
L
to
1^{\perp}
are
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}
. Let
P
be the projection matrix to the subspace spanned by the top
k
eigenvectors of
L
. Then,
P
is rank
k
with
P1=0
such that

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(L)=\text{tr}(PL).

Using the decomposition

L=\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in E(G)}(e_{i}-e_{j})(e_{i}-e_{j})^{T},

we obtain

\text{tr}(PL)=\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in E(G)}(e_{i}-e_{j})^{T}P(e_{i}-e_{j})=\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in E(G)}(P_{ii}+P_{jj}-2P_{ij}).

The assumed projection estimate therefore gives

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(L)\leq m+\dbinom{k+1}{2}.

∎

Lemma 5.2

(A Projection Identity)
.

For any orthogonal projection
P
of rank
k
which satisfies
P1=0
, the following holds

(5.1)

\dfrac{1}{4}\sum\limits_{i\neq j}\left[(P_{ii}+P_{jj}-2P_{ij})^{2}-(P_{ii}-P_{jj})^{2}\right]=k(k+1).

Proof.

Let
a_{ij}=P_{ii}+P_{jj}-2P_{ij}
. Since
P^{2}=P
,
\text{tr}(P)=k
, and
P1=0
, we have

\sum\limits_{i,j}a_{ij}^{2}=2n\sum\limits_{i}P_{ii}^{2}+2k^{2}+4k.

Indeed, the mixed term

\sum\limits_{i,j}(P_{ii}+P_{jj})P_{ij}

vanishes because every row and column sum of
P
is zero, while
\sum_{i,j}P_{ij}^{2}=\text{tr}(P^{2})=k
. Also,

\sum\limits_{i,j}(P_{ii}-P_{jj})^{2}=2n\sum\limits_{i}P_{ii}^{2}-2k^{2}.

Subtracting gives

\sum\limits_{i,j}\left[a_{ij}^{2}-(P_{ii}-P_{jj})^{2}\right]=4k(k+1).

The terms with
i=j
vanish, so the desired identity follows.
∎

The following lemma is the key technical ingredient in our proof.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
P
be an orthogonal projection of rank
k
which satisfies
P1=0
. Define the matrix
M
with diagonal entries
M_{ii}=0
and off-diagonal entries
M_{ij}=P_{ii}+P_{jj}-2P_{ij}-1
. Let
v=CM1
. Then

(5.2)

\sum\limits_{i\neq j}(1-|M_{ij}|)^{2}\geq\dfrac{2}{n}\left\|v\right\|^{2}.

Remark 5.4

(The cut/routing viewpoint)
.

Observe that if the left hand side has no absolute value on
M_{ij}
, then the lower bound above is an easy “row-sum” estimate. Indeed,
W_{ij}=1-M_{ij}\quad(i\neq j),\qquad W_{ii}=0
,
we have

CW\mathbf{1}=-CM\mathbf{1}=-v
.
Thus,

\sum_{i\neq j}(1-M_{ij})^{2}=2\sum_{i<j}W_{ij}^{2}\geq\frac{2}{n}\|CW\mathbf{1}\|^{2}=\frac{2}{n}\|v\|^{2}.

The lemma shows that the
same
lower bound remains true even after
replacing
1-M_{ij}
by the smaller quantity
1-|M_{ij}|
. Our final proof is short but let us describe the intuition behind.

First, observe that it is enough to prove (the formally stronger) claim that for every
x
,
|\langle x,v\rangle|\leq\sum_{i<j}(1-|M_{ij}|)|x_{i}-x_{j}|
. The inequality we want then follows by plugging in
x=v
. This inequality has a pleasing and familiar looking form of “capacitated routing” with capacities
c_{ij}=(1-|M_{ij}|)
on the complete graph (see, e.g.,
[
5
, Theorem 5.19]
). To verify the inequality for a given
x
, one need only check the “level” cuts: sort the vertices of
x
and take all cuts formed by prefixes
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{n-1}
in this ordering. We then observe that for any
r
, our proof reduces this problem to the nuclear norm estimate from Lemma
3.2
for the split graph with a clique on
U_{r}
. This relies on the simple but key identity in (
5.3
).

Proof of Lemma
5.3
via Lemma
5.5
below.

If
v=0
, the result is immediate. Let
v\neq 0
in the following.
Then, (
5.2
) follows from Lemma
5.5
by Cauchy-Schwarz:

n\left\|v\right\|^{2}\cdot\sum\limits_{i<j}(1-|M_{ij}|)^{2}=\sum\limits_{i<j}(1-|M_{ij}|)^{2}\cdot\sum\limits_{i<j}|v_{i}-v_{j}|^{2}\geq\left(\sum\limits_{i<j}(1-|M_{ij}|)\cdot|v_{i}-v_{j}|\right)^{2}\geq\left\|v\right\|^{4}.

Now, observe that
\sum_{i<j}(v_{i}-v_{j})^{2}=n\left\|v\right\|^{2}
. Dividing by
n\left\|v\right\|^{2}
gives

\sum_{i<j}(1-|M_{ij}|)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n}\left\|v\right\|^{2},

which is equivalent to (
5.2
).
∎

Lemma 5.5

(A Routing Inequality for
M
)
.

Let
P
be an orthogonal projection of rank
k
which satisfies
P1=0
. Define the matrix
M
with diagonal entries
M_{ii}=0
and off-diagonal entries
M_{ij}=P_{ii}+P_{jj}-2P_{ij}-1
. Let
N=CMC
and
v=CM1
.
For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the following inequality holds

|\langle x,v\rangle|\leq\sum\limits_{i<j}(1-|M_{ij}|)\cdot|x_{i}-x_{j}|.

In particular,

\sum\limits_{i<j}(1-|M_{ij}|)|v_{i}-v_{j}|\geq\left\|v\right\|^{2}.

Proof.

For
i\neq j
, we have

P_{ii}+P_{jj}-2P_{ij}=\left\|P(e_{i}-e_{j})\right\|^{2}\in[0,2],

and hence
1-|M_{ij}|\geq 0
.
We now compute the norm of
N
. Let
d=(P_{11},\ldots,P_{nn})^{T}
. Since

M=d1^{T}+1d^{T}-2P-J+I,

and
C1=0
,
CP=PC=P
, we get

N=CMC=C-2P.

Thus
N
has eigenvalue
-1
on
\operatorname{Im}P
, eigenvalue
1
on

1^{\perp}\cap(\operatorname{Im}P)^{\perp}
, and eigenvalue
0
on

\operatorname{span}\{1\}
. In particular,
\left\|N\right\|=1
.

It is enough to prove the one-sided estimate

\langle x,v\rangle\leq\sum\limits_{i<j}(1-|M_{ij}|)\cdot|x_{i}-x_{j}|.

By relabeling the coordinates, we may assume without loss of generality that
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\ldots\geq x_{n}
.

For each
1\leq r\leq n-1
, let
G_{r}
be the split graph on
[n]
obtained by making
\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
a clique,
\{r+1,\ldots,n\}
an independent set, and adding an edge across the cut wherever
M_{ij}\geq 0
. We first prove the identity

(5.3)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\sum\limits_{j=r+1}^{n}|M_{ij}|=-\text{tr}\left((L_{G_{r}}-rC)N\right).

Since
L_{G_{r}}1=C1=0
and
N=CMC
, the right hand side is

-\text{tr}\left((L_{G_{r}}-rC)M\right).

The diagonal entries of
M
are zero, and therefore

-\text{tr}\left((L_{G_{r}}-rC)M\right)=2\sum\limits_{ij\in E(G_{r})}M_{ij}+r\text{tr}(CM).

Writing
K_{r}=\{1,\ldots,r\}
, the last expression is

2\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
i,j\in K_{r}\end{subarray}}M_{ij}+2\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\leq r<j\\
M_{ij}\geq 0\end{subarray}}M_{ij}-\dfrac{r}{n}1^{T}M1.

On the other hand,

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}=2\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
i,j\in K_{r}\end{subarray}}M_{ij}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\sum\limits_{j=r+1}^{n}M_{ij}-\dfrac{r}{n}1^{T}M1.

Adding
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=r+1}^{n}|M_{ij}|
and using

M_{ij}+|M_{ij}|=2(M_{ij})_{+}
proves (
5.3
).

By Lemma
3.2
and
\left\|N\right\|=1
, (
5.3
) gives

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\sum\limits_{j=r+1}^{n}|M_{ij}|\leq\left\|L_{G_{r}}-rC\right\|_{*}\cdot\left\|N\right\|\leq r(n-r).

Hence

(5.4)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\sum\limits_{j=r+1}^{n}(1-|M_{ij}|).

Since
\sum_{i}v_{i}=0
, telescoping gives

\langle x,v\rangle=\sum\limits_{r=1}^{n-1}(x_{r}-x_{r+1})\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}v_{i}.

Combining this with (
5.4
), and using
x_{r}-x_{r+1}\geq 0
, we obtain

\langle x,v\rangle\leq\sum\limits_{r=1}^{n-1}(x_{r}-x_{r+1})\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\sum\limits_{j=r+1}^{n}(1-|M_{ij}|)=\sum\limits_{i<j}(1-|M_{ij}|)(x_{i}-x_{j}).

This is the desired one-sided estimate. Applying it to
-x
gives the absolute-value form.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

For any orthogonal projection
P
of rank
k
which satisfies
P1=0
, the following holds

(5.5)

\sum\limits_{i\neq j}(P_{ii}+P_{jj}-2P_{ij}-1)_{+}\leq k(k+1).

In particular, Lemma
3.2
implies Brouwer’s Laplacian conjecture.

Remark 5.7
.

Observe that for any rank
k
projection matrix
P
, the graph
G
of pairs
i,j
such that
i\neq j
and
(P_{ii}+P_{jj}-2P_{ij}-1)>0
gives the “worst-case” graph, i.e., maximizes the projection expression in Lemma
5.1
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
5.2
and the fact that
(x-1)_{+}=\frac{1}{4}\left(x^{2}-(1-|x-1|)^{2}\right)
, inequality (
5.5
) is equivalent to

(5.6)

\sum\limits_{i\neq j}\left(1-|P_{ii}+P_{jj}-2P_{ij}-1|\right)^{2}\geq\sum\limits_{i\neq j}(P_{ii}-P_{jj})^{2}.

Define the matrix
M
with diagonal entries
M_{ii}=0
and off-diagonal entries
M_{ij}=P_{ii}+P_{jj}-2P_{ij}-1
. Then,
M\mathbf{1}=n(P_{11},\ldots,P_{nn})^{T}+(k-n+1)\mathbf{1}
,
and hence
v=CM\mathbf{1}=nC(P_{11},\ldots,P_{nn})^{T}
.
Thus,
v_{i}-v_{j}=n(P_{ii}-P_{jj})
.

Finally,
v\perp\mathbf{1}
,

\sum_{i\neq j}(P_{ii}-P_{jj})^{2}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i\neq j}(v_{i}-v_{j})^{2}=\frac{2}{n}\left\|v\right\|^{2}.

Thus the (
5.6
) is exactly (
5.2
), which holds by Lemma
5.3
.

The case
k=n
is immediate from
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=2m
and
m\leq\binom{n}{2}\leq\binom{n+1}{2}
.

Now, let
1\leq k\leq n-1
. For any orthogonal projection
P
of rank
k
satisfying
P1=0
, (
5.5
) gives

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in E(G)}(P_{ii}+P_{jj}-2P_{ij}-1)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in E(G)}(P_{ii}+P_{jj}-2P_{ij}-1)_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i\neq j}(P_{ii}+P_{jj}-2P_{ij}-1)_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\dbinom{k+1}{2}.

∎

Brouwer’s Laplacian conjecture follows by combining Lemma
5.1
with Lemma
5.6
.
