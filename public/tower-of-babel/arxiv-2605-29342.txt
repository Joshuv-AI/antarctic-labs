Inducing Whittaker Functions from Higher Ranks
Vishal Muthuvel

Abstract
. We construct a family of Whittaker functions for SL ⁡ ( m , ℤ ) \SL(m,\mathbb{Z}) induced directly from Whittaker functions for SL ⁡ ( n , ℤ ) \SL(n,\mathbb{Z}) , for any 2 ≤ m < n 2\leq m<n . Given Jacquet’s Whittaker function W α , N ( n ) W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)} on the generalized upper half-plane 𝔥 n \mathfrak{h}^{n} , we show that the function V α , N ( m ) : 𝔥 m → ℂ V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}:\mathfrak{h}^{m}\to\mathbb{C} defined by restricting W α , N ( n ) W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)} to the block-diagonal embedding 𝔥 m ↪ 𝔥 n \mathfrak{h}^{m}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{h}^{n} is a Whittaker function for SL ⁡ ( m , ℤ ) \SL(m,\mathbb{Z}) , provided the Langlands parameters α = ( α i ) 1 ≤ i ≤ n \alpha=(\alpha_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n} satisfy ∑ i = 1 m α i = m ⁡ ( m − n ) / 2 \sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}=m(m-n)/2 . Under this condition, the induced function carries Langlands parameters ( α i + n − m 2 ) 1 ≤ i ≤ m \bigl(\alpha_{i}+\frac{n-m}{2}\bigr)_{1\leq i\leq m} and inherits the first m − 1 m-1 entries of the character tuple of W α , N ( n ) W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)} . This result complements the propagation formulas of Ishii and Stade, which relate Whittaker functions on GL ⁡ ( n , ℝ ) \GL(n,\mathbb{R}) to those on GL ⁡ ( n − 1 , ℝ ) \GL(n-1,\mathbb{R}) and GL ⁡ ( n − 2 , ℝ ) \GL(n-2,\mathbb{R}) . In contrast, our construction passes directly from GL ⁡ ( n , ℝ ) \GL(n,\mathbb{R}) to GL ⁡ ( m , ℝ ) \GL(m,\mathbb{R}) for any m < n m<n in a single step.



1.
Introduction

Whittaker functions are a cornerstone of the theory of automorphic forms
and
L
-functions. Jacquet
[
Jac67
]
initiated the general study of
Whittaker functions on Chevalley groups over local fields, but in this
paper, we focus on the case of
\GL(n,\mathbb{R})
. Ishii and Stade derived
propagation formulas for Whittaker functions on
\GL(n,\mathbb{R})
, expressing
them in terms of Whittaker functions on
\GL(n-2,\mathbb{R})

[
Ish05
]
and

\GL(n-1,\mathbb{R})

[
IS07
]
. Our
main result here is a backpropagation formula: a construction giving
Whittaker functions on
\GL(m,\mathbb{R})
from those on
\GL(n,\mathbb{R})
for any

m<n
.

To place Whittaker functions in their broader context, let
\mathbb{A}
denote
the ring of adeles over
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
\pi
be a unitary cuspidal automorphic
representation of
\GL(n,\mathbb{A})
with trivial central character. By
Flath’s tensor product theorem,
\pi
decomposes as a restricted tensor product

\displaystyle\pi\ \cong\ \bigotimes_{v}^{\prime}\pi_{v}

over all places
v
of
\mathbb{Q}
,
where
\pi_{v}
is an irreducible admissible representation of

\GL(n,\mathbb{Q}_{v})
(where
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}=\mathbb{R}
). When
\pi
is unramified at every finite prime
p
, such a representation corresponds classically to a Hecke–Maass cusp form for
\SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
: i.e., a smooth,
square-integrable eigenfunction of the ring of
invariant differential operators on the generalized upper half-plane

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}^{n}=\GL(n,\mathbb{R})/(\mathrm{O}(n,\mathbb{R})\cdot\mathbb{R}^{\times}).

The archimedean
component
\pi_{\infty}
is parametrized by a list of
n
complex numbers

\displaystyle\alpha\ =\ (\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\ \in\ \mathbb{C}^{n},

called the Langlands parameters, which satisfies
\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\alpha_{\ell}=0
. The Whittaker function
W_{\alpha}:\GL(n,\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{C}
is then the smooth function corresponding to the
spherical vector in
\pi_{\infty}
: it is characterized as the

\mathrm{O}(n,\mathbb{R})
-invariant, moderate-growth eigenfunction of the ring of invariant differential operators that transforms by a non-degenerate unitary character
\psi
under the
unipotent radical
U(n,\mathbb{R})
. The significance of
W_{\alpha}
is made concrete
through the Fourier–Whittaker expansion, which expresses any Hecke–Maass
cusp form as a sum over Fourier coefficients weighted by translates of

W_{\alpha}
, through which the analytic properties of the form, including
the associated
L
-function, become accessible (see
[
Gol06
, Theorem 9.3.11]
). It is in this classical
framework that our
results are stated and proved.

In this classical setting, a Whittaker function for
\SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
is
specified by two pieces of data: the Langlands parameters
\alpha=(\alpha_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}=0
, and a character

\psi_{N}
of the unipotent radical
U(n,\mathbb{R})
, indexed by a tuple

N=(N_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n-1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
. The multiplicity one theorem for

\GL(n,\mathbb{R})
, proved by Shalika
[
Sha74
]
, guarantees that any
Whittaker function is a scalar multiple of Jacquet’s Whittaker function

W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}:\mathfrak{h}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
with the same associated data, so that

W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}
serves as the canonical representative in its
Whittaker model. The propagation formulas of Ishii and Stade show that
Whittaker functions satisfy recursive structure across the tower

\GL(2,\mathbb{R}),\GL(3,\mathbb{R}),\dots,\GL(n,\mathbb{R})
; specifically, a Whittaker function on

\GL(n,\mathbb{R})
can be expressed in terms of Whittaker functions on

\GL(n-2,\mathbb{R})
and
\GL(n-1,\mathbb{R})
via explicit integral identities. These
formulas propagate downward one step at a time and are fundamental tools
in the analytic study of Whittaker functions (and their Mellin transforms).

Definition 1.1
.

A
Whittaker function
for

\SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
with Langlands parameters
\alpha=(\alpha_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
and character tuple
N=(N_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n-1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
is a
smooth function
W:\mathfrak{h}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying the following three conditions:

(1)

(Equivariance)

W(ug)=\psi_{N}(u)\,W(g)
for all

u\in U(n,\mathbb{R})
and
g\in\mathfrak{h}^{n}
, where
\psi_{N}:U(n,\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}

is the unitary character given by

\psi_{N}\!\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&u_{1,2}&\cdots&u_{1,n}\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&1&u_{n-1,n}\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right):=e^{2\pi i(N_{1}u_{1,2}+\cdots+N_{n-1}u_{n-1,n})};

(2)

(Eigenfunction)

D_{\ell}^{(n)}W=\lambda_{\ell}^{(n)}(\alpha)\,W

for every Casimir operator
D_{\ell}^{(n)}
of order
\ell
,

1\leq\ell\leq n
(see Definition
2.5
), where

\lambda_{\ell}^{(n)}(\alpha)
denotes the eigenvalue of the shifted
power function
|\cdot|_{B}^{\alpha+\rho^{(n)}}
under
D_{\ell}^{(n)}

(see (
2.3
));

(3)

(Moderate growth)

W
is of moderate growth on
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
.

By the multiplicity one theorem of Shalika
[
Sha74
]
, any
W
satisfying
Definition
1.1
is a scalar multiple of Jacquet’s
Whittaker function
W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}
, the canonical element of the
Whittaker model introduced in
[
Jac67
]
and given explicitly by the
integral formula (
3.1
).

Here we establish a complementary result to those of Ishii and
Stade: a direct backpropagation formula that jumps from
\GL(n,\mathbb{R})
to

\GL(m,\mathbb{R})
for any
2\leq m<n
in a single step. The construction is
natural: given Jacquet’s Whittaker function
W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}
on

\mathfrak{h}^{n}
, we define a function
V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}:\mathfrak{h}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}
by
embedding
\mathfrak{h}^{m}
into
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
via the block-diagonal inclusion

xy\;\longmapsto\;\begin{pmatrix}xy&0\\
0&I_{n-m}\end{pmatrix},

and restricting
W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}
to this subspace. The central
question is whether this restriction is itself a Whittaker function for

\SL(m,\mathbb{Z})
, and if so, what its associated Langlands parameters and
character are. Our main theorem answers this affirmatively, under a single
linear condition on the Langlands parameters.

Theorem 1.2
.

Consider any
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
2\leq m<n
. Let

W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}:\mathfrak{h}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be Jacquet’s Whittaker function for

\SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
with Langlands parameters
\alpha=(\alpha_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}

and character tuple
N=(N_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n-1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
. If

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\;=\;\frac{m(m-n)}{2},

then the function
V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}:\mathfrak{h}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}(xy):=W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}\!\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}xy&0\\
0&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)
is a Whittaker function for

\SL(m,\mathbb{Z})
with Langlands parameters
\bigl(\alpha_{i}+\frac{n-m}{2}\bigr)_{1\leq i\leq m}
and character tuple
(N_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m-1}
.

Several features of this result merit comment. The Langlands parameter
condition
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}=m(m-n)/2
is not merely a technical
hypothesis but has a representation-theoretic interpretation: it is
precisely the condition ensuring that the shifted parameters

\bigl(\alpha_{i}+\frac{n-m}{2}\bigr)_{1\leq i\leq m}
satisfy the
normalization
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bigl(\alpha_{i}+\frac{n-m}{2}\bigr)=0
required
for a valid Langlands parameter tuple on
\GL(m,\mathbb{R})
. The uniform shift
by
(n-m)/2
in the output parameters reflects the change in the

\rho
-shift between
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathfrak{gl}(m,\mathbb{R})
, and is consistent with
the behavior seen in the Ishii–Stade formulas in the special cases

m=n-1
and
m=n-2
. The character tuple, by contrast, truncates cleanly:
only the first
m-1
entries of
N
are inherited, corresponding to the
restriction of
\psi_{N}
to the unipotent radical of the standard Borel in

\GL(m,\mathbb{R})
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we
recall the necessary background on the generalized upper half-plane

\mathfrak{h}^{n}
, Jacquet’s Whittaker function, and the Casimir operators following

[
Gol06
]
. In Section
3
we state the main theorem and
give an overview of the proof. In Section
4
we prove
Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

We largely follow the notational conventions of
[
Gol06
]
, and write

[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.1.
The generalized upper half-plane

The
Iwasawa decomposition
of
\GL(n,\mathbb{R})
states that every element
can be written uniquely as a product of an upper-triangular unipotent
matrix, a positive diagonal matrix, and an orthogonal matrix. As observed
by Gelfand, Graev, and Piatetski-Shapiro
[
GGP69
]
, this yields a
concrete realization of the generalized upper half-plane.

Definition 2.1
.

The
unipotent group

U(n,\mathbb{R})
is the group of
n\times n

upper-triangular real matrices with ones on the diagonal:

U(n,\mathbb{R}):=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&u_{1,2}&\cdots&u_{1,n}\\
&1&\cdots&u_{2,n}\\
&&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&&1\end{pmatrix}\;\middle|\;u_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}\;\forall\;1\leq i<j\leq n\right\}.

Definition 2.2
.

The
generalized upper half-plane

\mathfrak{h}^{n}
is the coset space

\mathfrak{h}^{n}\;:=\;\GL(n,\mathbb{R})\big/\bigl(\mathrm{O}(n,\mathbb{R})\cdot\mathbb{R}^{\times}\bigr),

identified via the Iwasawa decomposition with

\mathfrak{h}^{n}\;=\;\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&x_{1,2}&\cdots&x_{1,n}\\
&1&\cdots&x_{2,n}\\
&&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}\cdots y_{n-1}&&&\\
&\ddots&&\\
&&y_{1}&\\
&&&1\end{pmatrix}\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{l}x_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R},\;y_{i}>0\\
\forall\;1\leq i<j\leq n\end{array}\right\}.

A typical element of
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
is denoted
xy
, where
x
denotes the
unipotent factor and
y
the diagonal factor.

The group
\SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
of
n\times n
integer matrices of determinant
1

acts on
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
by left multiplication. The fundamental domain of this
action is well approximated by the Siegel set
\mathcal{P}_{\sqrt{3}/2,1/2}
:

Definition 2.3
.

For
a,b\geq 0
, the
Siegel set

\mathcal{P}_{a,b}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{n}

consists of all
xy\in\mathfrak{h}^{n}
with
|x_{i,j}|\leq b
and
y_{i}>a
for all

1\leq i<j\leq n
.

There is an
\SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
-invariant measure on
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
, given by

(2.1)

d^{*}z\;:=\;\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}dx_{i,j}\;\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}y_{i}^{-i(n-i)-1}\,dy_{i}.

2.2.
Invariant differential operators and the power function

Let
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{R})
denote the Lie algebra of
n\times n
real matrices with
bracket
[a,b]=ab-ba
, and let
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{R}))
be its universal
enveloping algebra. The center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{R})))

coincides with the algebra of
\SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
-invariant differential operators
on
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
. Denoting by
E_{i,j}
the matrix with
1
in position
(i,j)

and
0
elsewhere, we define:

Definition 2.4
.

For
\alpha\in\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{R})
, the differential operator
D_{\alpha}^{(n)}
acts
on smooth
F:\GL(n,\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{C}
by

D_{\alpha}^{(n)}F(g)\;:=\;\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F\bigl(g\cdot(I+t\alpha)\bigr)\Big|_{t=0}.

We write
D_{i,j}^{(n)}
for
D_{E_{i,j}}^{(n)}
.

Definition 2.5
.

The
Casimir operator of order
\ell
for
\GL(n,\mathbb{R})
, where

1\leq\ell\leq n
, is

(2.2)

D_{\ell}^{(n)}\;:=\;\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell})\in[n]^{\ell}}D_{i_{1},i_{2}}^{(n)}\circ\cdots\circ D_{i_{\ell-1},i_{\ell}}^{(n)}\circ D_{i_{\ell},i_{1}}^{(n)}.

Capelli
[
Cap87
]
proved that
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{R})))

is generated by
D_{1}^{(n)},\ldots,D_{n}^{(n)}
. The quintessential
simultaneous eigenfunction of these operators is the power function.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}=0
. The
power function

|\cdot|_{B}^{\alpha}:\mathfrak{h}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
with
Langlands parameters
\alpha
is defined for all
xy\in\mathfrak{h}^{n}
by

|xy|_{B}^{\alpha}\;:=\;\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{n-i}y_{j}^{\alpha_{i}}.

The
\rho
-shift

\rho^{(n)}:=\bigl(\frac{n+1}{2}-i\bigr)_{1\leq i\leq n}
arises from the theory of root systems. The shifted power
function
|\cdot|_{B}^{\alpha+\rho^{(n)}}
is a simultaneous eigenfunction
of all Casimir operators: for all
g\in\mathfrak{h}^{n}
and
1\leq\ell\leq n
,

(2.3)

D_{\ell}^{(n)}\,|g|_{B}^{\alpha+\rho^{(n)}}\;=\;\lambda_{\ell,\alpha}^{(n)}\,|g|_{B}^{\alpha+\rho^{(n)}},

where
\lambda_{\ell,\alpha}^{(n)}\in\mathbb{C}
are eigenvalues computed by
Terras
[
Ter88
]
and Miller
[
Mil97
]
for
n=2
and by Muthuvel
[
Mut26
]
in general.

Whittaker functions for
\SL(n,\mathbb{Z})

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}=0
and

1

Every

\psi_{N}(u)\;:=\;e^{2\pi i(N_{1}u_{1,2}+\cdots+N_{n-1}u_{n-1,n})}.

A

(1)

comprise the Fourier

3.
Objective and Overview

Consider any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
2\leq m<n
. Given a Whittaker
function
W:\mathfrak{h}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
for
\SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
, we would like to induce a
Whittaker function for
\SL(m,\mathbb{Z})
from it.

By definition,
W
has associated:

•

Langlands parameters
\alpha=(\alpha_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}=0
and

\Re(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{i+1})>0
for all
1\leq i<n
; and

•

a character
\psi_{N}:U(n,\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
identified with

N=(N_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n-1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
.

The multiplicity one theorem for
\GL(n,\mathbb{R})
, proved by
Shalika
[
Sha74
]
, implies that
W
is a scalar multiple of Jacquet’s
Whittaker function for
\SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
with the same Langlands parameters and
character. Introduced by Jacquet in 1967
[
Jac67
]
, it is given for all

g\in\mathfrak{h}^{n}
by

(3.1)

W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}(z)\;:=\;\int_{U(n,\mathbb{R})}\bigl|w_{n}uz\bigr|_{B}^{\alpha+\rho^{(n)}}\,\overline{\psi_{N}(u)}\,du,

where

w_{n}\;:=\;\begin{pmatrix}&&(-1)^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\\
&\iddots&\\
1&&\end{pmatrix}

is the long element in the Weyl group of
\GL(n,\mathbb{R})
. The condition

\Re(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{i+1})>0
for all
1\leq i<n
ensures the absolute
convergence of the integral.

We attend to the function
V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}:\mathfrak{h}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}
, defined for all

xy\in\mathfrak{h}^{m}
by

(3.2)

\displaystyle V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}(xy)

\displaystyle:=W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}xy&0\\
0&I_{n-m}\end{pmatrix}\right),

decomposing the
n\times n
matrix in the argument of
W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}

into its
m\times m
,
m\times(n-m)
,
(n-m)\times m
, and

(n-m)\times(n-m)
blocks. Our main objective is to show that this
function on
\mathfrak{h}^{m}
, induced from an
\SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
-Whittaker function on

\mathfrak{h}^{n}
, is itself an
\SL(m,\mathbb{Z})
-Whittaker function.

Theorem 3.1
.

Consider any
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
2\leq m<n
. Let

W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}:\mathfrak{h}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be Jacquet’s Whittaker function for

\SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
with Langlands parameters
\alpha=(\alpha_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}

and character tuple
N=(N_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n-1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
, given
in (
3.1
). If

(3.3)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\;=\;\frac{m(m-n)}{2},

then the function
V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}:\mathfrak{h}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}
defined
in (
3.2
) is a Whittaker function for
\SL(m,\mathbb{Z})

with Langlands parameters
\bigl(\alpha_{i}+\frac{n-m}{2}\bigr)_{1\leq i\leq m}
and character tuple
(N_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m-1}
.

The condition (
3.3
) ensures that the shifted
parameters
\bigl(\alpha_{i}+\frac{n-m}{2}\bigr)_{1\leq i\leq m}
satisfy

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bigl(\alpha_{i}+\frac{n-m}{2}\bigr)=0
, which is required for
a valid Langlands parameter tuple on
\GL(m,\mathbb{R})
and guarantees that

V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}
is invariant under multiplication by the center

Z(\GL(m,\mathbb{R}))
. Moreover,
V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}
is invariant under right
multiplication by every
u\in\mathrm{O}(m,\mathbb{R})
, since
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}u&0\\
0&I\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\in\mathrm{O}(n,\mathbb{R})
. Together,
these observations confirm that
V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}
is well defined on

\mathfrak{h}^{m}
.

We prove Theorem
3.1
in Section
4
by
verifying that
V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}
satisfies the three defining properties
of Definition
2.7
. Specifically, we show that

V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}
:

(1)

associates with the character
\psi_{(N_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m-1}}

for
U(m,\mathbb{R})
(Lemma
4.1
);

(2)

is an eigenfunction of all Casimir operators
D_{\ell}^{(m)}
for

\GL(m,\mathbb{R})
, hence of
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{gl}(m,\mathbb{R})))

(Lemma
4.2
); and

(3)

is square-integrable on
\mathcal{P}_{\!\sqrt{3}/2,\,1/2}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{m}

(Lemma
4.3
).

While the proofs of properties (1) and (2) follow from elementary
observations, the proof of property (3) appeals to a nontrivial bound for

\SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
-Whittaker functions due to
[
GMW21
]
.

4.
Proof of the Main Theorem

Theorem
3.1
follows immediately from the three lemmas
below. Throughout,
V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}
is the function induced from
Jacquet’s Whittaker function
W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}
for
\SL(n,\mathbb{Z})
with
Langlands parameters
(\alpha_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
and character tuple

(N_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n-1}
as in (
3.2
). The guiding
observation throughout is that matrices of the block form

\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}A&0\\
0&I\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\in\GL(n,\mathbb{R})
,
with
A\in\GL(m,\mathbb{R})
and
I\in\GL(n-m,\mathbb{R})
the identity, are closed under
multiplication, and that their product reduces to multiplication of the

m\times m
top-left blocks.

Lemma 4.1
.

For all
u\in U(m,\mathbb{R})
and
g\in\mathfrak{h}^{m}
,

V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}(ug)\;=\;\psi_{(N_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m-1}}(u)\,V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}(g).

Proof.

For
u=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&u_{1,2}&\cdots&u_{1,m}\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&1&u_{m-1,m}\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\in U(m,\mathbb{R})

and
xy\in\mathfrak{h}^{m}
,

\displaystyle V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}(uxy)

\displaystyle=W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}u&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}xy&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{N}\!\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&u_{1,2}&\cdots&u_{1,m}&&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots&&\\
&&1&u_{m-1,m}&&\\
&&&1&&\\
&&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}xy&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi i(N_{1}u_{1,2}+\cdots+N_{m-1}u_{m-1,m})}\,V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}(xy)

\displaystyle=\psi_{(N_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m-1}}(u)\,V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}(xy).\qed

Lemma 4.2
.

For all positive integers
\ell\leq m
,

D_{\ell}^{(m)}\,V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}(xy)\;=\;\lambda_{\ell,\beta}^{(m)}\,V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}(xy),\quad\text{where }\beta_{i}:=\alpha_{i}+\tfrac{n-m}{2}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq m.

Proof.

Let
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell})\in[m]^{\ell}
be arbitrary. Unraveling
Definition
2.4
and applying the block-multiplication
observation,

\displaystyle D_{i_{1},i_{2}}^{(m)}\circ\cdots\circ D_{i_{\ell},i_{1}}^{(m)}\,V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}(xy)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{\ell}}{\partial t_{1}\cdots\partial t_{\ell}}V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}\!\left(xy\,(I+t_{\ell}E_{i_{\ell},i_{1}}^{(m)})\cdots(I+t_{1}E_{i_{1},i_{2}}^{(m)})\right)\Bigg|_{\forall t_{j}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{\ell}}{\partial t_{1}\cdots\partial t_{\ell}}W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}xy&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I+t_{\ell}E_{i_{\ell},i_{1}}^{(m)}&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\cdots\right)\Bigg|_{\forall t_{j}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{\ell}}{\partial t_{1}\cdots\partial t_{\ell}}W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}xy&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}(I+t_{\ell}E_{i_{\ell},i_{1}}^{(n)})\cdots(I+t_{1}E_{i_{1},i_{2}}^{(n)})\right)\Bigg|_{\forall t_{j}=0}

\displaystyle=D_{i_{1},i_{2}}^{(n)}\circ\cdots\circ D_{i_{\ell},i_{1}}^{(n)}\,W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}xy&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\right),

where the third equality uses that
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}I+t_{j}E_{i_{j},i_{j+1}}^{(m)}&0\\
0&I\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)=I+t_{j}E_{i_{j},i_{j+1}}^{(n)}
since
i_{j}\leq m
. Summing over
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell})\in[m]^{\ell}
gives

(4.1)

\displaystyle D_{\ell}^{(m)}\,V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}(xy)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell})\in[m]^{\ell}}D_{i_{1},i_{2}}^{(n)}\circ\cdots\circ D_{i_{\ell},i_{1}}^{(n)}\,W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}xy&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\right).

Since
|\cdot|_{B}^{\alpha+\rho^{(n)}}
is an eigenfunction of

D_{i_{1},i_{2}}^{(n)}\circ\cdots\circ D_{i_{\ell},i_{1}}^{(n)}
for all

(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell})\in[m]^{\ell}\subseteq[n]^{\ell}
, we may pass the
differential operators under the integral in (
3.1
),
which shows that
V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}
is an eigenfunction of
D_{\ell}^{(m)}
.
Letting
\beta=(\beta_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}
denote the Langlands parameters
of
V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}
, matching eigenvalues on both sides
of (
4.1
) via
[
Mut26
, Theorem 1.5]
gives

\lambda_{\ell,\beta}^{(m)}=\lambda_{\ell,\alpha}^{(n)}
for all
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell})\in[m]^{\ell}
,
from which
\beta_{i}=\alpha_{i}+\rho_{i}^{(n)}-\rho_{i}^{(m)}=\alpha_{i}+\frac{n-m}{2}

for all
1\leq i\leq m
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

The function
V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}
is square-integrable on

\mathcal{P}_{\!\sqrt{3}/2,\,1/2}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{m}
:

\int_{\mathcal{P}_{\!\sqrt{3}/2,\,1/2}}\bigl|V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}(z)\bigr|^{2}\,d^{*}z\;<\;\infty.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{1}^{\prime}=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
. By

[
Gol06
, Proposition 5.5.2]
, for all
xy\in\mathfrak{h}^{n}
,

(4.2)

W_{\alpha,N}^{(n)}(xy)\;=\;a\cdot\psi_{N}(x)\cdot W_{\alpha,\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}^{(n)}(y),

for some
a\in\mathbb{C}
depending on
\alpha
and
N
. We use the bound
from
[
GMW21
, p. 76]
: there exists
M>0
such that whenever

y_{i}\geq 1
for all
1\leq i<n
,

(4.3)

W_{\alpha,\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}^{(n)}(y)\;\ll_{\alpha}\;\frac{1}{(y_{1}\cdots y_{n-1})^{M}}.

Since
W_{\alpha,\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}^{(n)}
is smooth on the compact set

[\sqrt{3}/2,1]^{n-1}
, the extreme value theorem implies it attains a
finite maximum there. Hence (
4.3
) holds (with a possibly
different
M>0
) whenever
y_{i}\geq\sqrt{3}/2
for all
1\leq i<n
.

Expanding the integral over
\mathcal{P}_{\!\sqrt{3}/2,\,1/2}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{m}
,
applying the definition (
3.2
), then using the
factorization (
4.2
) and
|\psi_{N}|=1
gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{P}_{\!\sqrt{3}/2,\,1/2}}\bigl|V_{\alpha,N}^{(m)}(z)\bigr|^{2}\,d^{*}z\;\ll_{\alpha,N}\;\int_{\sqrt{3}/2}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{\sqrt{3}/2}^{\infty}\left|W_{\alpha,\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}^{(n)}\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}y&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\right)\right|^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}y_{i}^{-i(m-i)-1}\,dy_{i},

where the
x_{i,j}
-integrals over
[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]
contribute
a finite constant. Applying the bound (
4.3
) then yields

\ll_{\alpha,N}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\int_{\sqrt{3}/2}^{\infty}y_{i}^{-2M-i(m-i)-1}\,dy_{i}.

Each factor converges since
-2M-i(m-i)-1<-1
, establishing
square-integrability.
∎
