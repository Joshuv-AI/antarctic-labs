Gromov-Hausdorff Convergence of Spectral Truncations for Quantum GroupS
Xintao Peng , Qin Wang

Abstract
. We study the quantum Gromov–Hausdorff convergence of spectral truncations for compact quantum groups. Using a proper length function, we define a Dirac operator and the associated spectral truncations. This work extends the previous convergence results for tori (Leimbach–van Suijlekom) to a broad class of quantum groups, and provides the first Gromov–Hausdorff convergence result for spectral truncations on quantum groups, encompassing both compact and discrete quantum groups. Our results are applicable to S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) , S ​ O ​ ( N ) SO(N) and discrete quantum groups with strong polynomial growth.



1. Introduction

Rieffel
[

]
introduced compact quantum metric spaces, extending the Gromov–Hausdorff distance to the noncommutative setting.
Connes and van Suijlekom
[
1

considered spectral truncations of noncommutative geometry and operator systems, further studied by van Suijlekom
[

and Leimbach–van Suijlekom

, who proved convergence for tori.
Rieffel
[

later considered Fourier truncations for compact quantum groups and finitely generated groups.
However, a systematic treatment of spectral truncations for quantum groups has been lacking.

Recently, Austad and Kyed
[
16
]
provided a crucial foundation.
In the quantum group framework, a length function is a map
\ell
on the set
\Irred(\mathbb{G})
of equivalence classes of irreducible corepresentations.
For any compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
equipped with a proper length function
\ell:\Irred(\mathbb{G})\to[0,\infty)
, one can construct (by
[
23
]
) a spectral triple

\bigl(\Pol(\mathbb{G}),L^{2}(\mathbb{G}),D_{\ell}\bigr),

where
\Pol(\mathbb{G})
is the Hopf
*
-algebra of polynomial functions on
\mathbb{G}
, and
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
is the GNS Hilbert space obtained from the Haar state.
The unbounded operator
D_{\ell}:\pi(\Pol(\mathbb{G}))\to L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
is defined by

D_{\ell}\bigl(\pi(u_{i,j}^{\alpha})\bigr):=\ell(\alpha)\cdot\pi(u_{i,j}^{\alpha}).

Since
\frac{1}{\|\pi(u_{i,j}^{\alpha})\|_{2}}\pi(u_{i,j}^{\alpha})
form an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
,
D_{\ell}
is self-adjoint. (
\pi
is the canonical map from the GNS construction.)
However, this spectral triple does not automatically give a compact quantum metric space; for it to be one, the associated Lipschitz seminorm
L(f)=\|[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\pi(f)]\|
must induce the weak
∗
topology on the state space.

Austad and Kyed proved that for a coamenable compact quantum group of Kac type, with a proper length function
\ell:\Irred(\mathbb{G})\to[0,\infty)
, the following are equivalent:

•

the algebra of central functions
(C_{z}(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}^{z})
(with the Lipschitz seminorm inherited from the Dirac operator on
L_{z}^{2}(\mathbb{G})
) is a compact quantum metric space;

•

the full algebra
(C(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell})
is a compact quantum metric space.

Here
L_{\ell}
denotes the Lip-norm associated with
\ell
, and
L_{\ell}^{z}
is its restriction to
L_{z}^{2}(\mathbb{G})
.
Define a map
\chi:c_{c}(\Irred(\mathbb{G}))\to L_{z}^{2}(\mathbb{G})
on basis elements by

\chi(\alpha)=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\alpha}}\pi(u_{ii}^{\alpha}),\qquad\alpha\in\Irred(\mathbb{G}),

where
\pi:\Pol(\mathbb{G})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
is the canonical inclusion.
One can conclude that
(F(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell})
is a compact quantum metric space iff
(C_{z}(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}^{z})
is.

These equivalences guarantee that all three algebras share the same metric properties, allowing us to study their spectral truncations meaningfully.

Now we give an overview of our work. First we recall the definition of the
quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance
between two compact quantum metric spaces.

Definition 1.1

(Quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance)
.

Let
(A,L_{A})
and
(B,L_{B})
be compact quantum metric spaces.
Let
\mathcal{M}(L_{A},L_{B})

and

The

\operatorname{dist}_{q}(A,B)=\inf\Bigl\{\operatorname{dist}_{H}^{d^{L}}\bigl(S(A),S(B)\bigr):L\in\mathcal{M}(L_{A},L_{B})\Bigr\}.

We often consider a spectral triple
(A,\mathcal{H},D)
consisting of a
C^{*}
-algebra

acting as bounded operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, together with a self-adjoint operator
D
such that the resolvent of

extends to a bounded operator for

*
-subalgebra of

A spectral truncation for the spectral triple is given by a spectral projection

of

onto the eigenspaces with eigenvalues of modulus
\leq\Lambda
.
The

-algebra

is replaced by
P_{\Lambda}AP_{\Lambda}
, acting on

, and we set

If

is the Lipschitz seminorm associated with

If this distance tends to zero as

Now we consider the spectral truncation of a
C^{*}
-algebra associated with a compact quantum group, and its convergence in quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance.
Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact quantum group and

F(\mathbb{G})=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\alpha\mid\alpha\in\Irred(\mathbb{G})\}

its fusion algebra.
We endow
F(\mathbb{G})
with the inner product for which the elements of

form an orthogonal basis, and denote the completion by
\ell^{2}(\Irred(\mathbb{G}))

Definition 1.2

(Rapid decay for fusion algebras)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact matrix quantum group.
Let

be the fusion algebra with irreducible basis

is said to have the

if there exist constants

such that

.

Definition 1.3

(Polynomial growth)
.

Let

be a compact quantum group.

By Proposition

By Proposition

forms a Følner sequence (i.e. it satisfies
\sum_{\xi\in\partial_{S}(A_{\Lambda})}d(\xi)^{2}<\epsilon\sum_{\xi\in A_{\Lambda}}d(\xi)^{2}
for every finite
S

).

Now we introduce Lip-seminorms on
F(\mathbb{G})
.
Set
L_{\ell}^{k}(\cdot)=\|\delta^{k}(\cdot)\|
, where
\delta^{k}(\cdot)=[\tilde{D}_{\ell},[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\dots[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\pi(\cdot)]\dots]]
(

Theorem 1.4
.

If
\mathbb{G}
is a compact quantum group with polynomial growth of order s, then for every integer
k>s

together with the seminorm

is a compact quantum metric space.

Consequently, connected compact Lie groups equipped with certain Lip-norms yield compact quantum metric spaces; for example, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
SU(N)
and
SO(N)
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact quantum group equipped with a proper length function. If
\bigl(C(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}^{k}\bigr)
is a compact quantum metric space, then there exists
\Lambda
such that quantum Gromov–Hausdorff convergence holds:

\operatorname{dist}_{q}\bigl((C(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}),\,(P_{\Lambda}C(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda},L_{\ell,\Lambda})\bigr)\to 0.

Concretely, we construct states on the truncated algebras that approximate those on the full algebra, and we verify the bi-invariance of the Lipschitz norm.
Then, by appealing to ergodic theory, the proof is completed.

We now consider another algebra closely related to the fusion algebra, the

,

C_{z}(\mathbb{G}):=\{a\in C(\mathbb{G})\mid\sigma\Delta(a)=\Delta(a)\},

(1.1)

where
\sigma
is the flip automorphism on
C(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\mathbb{G})
.

Theorem 1.6
.

If
\mathbb{G}
is a compact, coamenable quantum group of Kac type and
\ell:\Irred(\mathbb{G})\to[0,\infty)
is a proper length function, then
(C(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}^{k})
is a compact quantum metric space if and only if
(C_{z}(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}^{k})

In
[
16

, Austad and Kyed treated the case

Based on their proof, we generalize to arbitrary

In
[
23

, Bhowmick, Voigt, and Zacharias studied compact quantum metric spaces arising from quantum groups of rapid decay.
This naturally leads us to investigate whether their spectral truncations admit quantum Gromov–Hausdorff convergence.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a discrete quantum group of Kac type, equipped with a proper length function and having strong polynomial growth . Then there exists
\Lambda
such that quantum Gromov–Hausdorff convergence holds:

\operatorname{dist}_{q}\bigl((C(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}),\,(P_{\Lambda}C(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda},L_{\ell,\Lambda})\bigr)\to 0.

The proof relies on a net of states

Our results apply to a wide range of examples, including the compact quantum groups

These examples illustrate the breadth of our convergence theorem.

2. Preliminaries

2.1. Quantum Gromov-Hausdorff Distance

This subsection reviews the concept of compact quantum metric spaces
and the quantum analogue of Gromov–Hausdorff convergence introduced
by Marc A. Rieffel in
[
9
]
. The relevant definitions and key
properties are recalled below.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a complete operator system. A seminorm

L\colon A\to[0,\infty]
is called a
Lipschitz seminorm

if
L
satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

L
is
densely defined
, meaning that the domain

\operatorname{Dom}(L):=\{a\in A:L(a)<\infty\}
is a
norm-dense subspace of
A
;

(2)

L(a^{*})=L(a)
for all
a\in A
, and the kernel of
L

is exactly the scalar multiples of the identity, i.e.
L(a)=0\iff a\in\mathbb{C}\cdot e_{A}
.

Definition 2.2

(Compact Quantum Metric Space)
.

Let
A
be an operator system equipped with a Lipschitz seminorm

L\colon A\to[0,+\infty]
. We define a metric
d^{L}
on the state
space
S(A)
by

d^{L}(\phi,\psi):=\sup\bigl\{|\phi(a)-\psi(a)|:a\in A,\ L(a)\leq 1\bigr\},\qquad\phi,\psi\in S(A).

(1)

We require that the topology induced by
d^{L}
on
S(A)
coincides with
the weak
∗
topology. The pair
(A,L)
is then called a

compact quantum metric space
. In this case,
L
is referred to
as a
Lip-norm
.

Remark 2.3
.

The metric
d^{L}
defined in (1) is referred to as the
Monge–Kantorovič metric. The condition that
d^{L}
metrises
the weak
∗
topology ensures that
S(A)
is compact, hence the
terminology.

Next, we introduce the
quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance

between two compact quantum metric spaces.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(A,L_{A})
and
(B,L_{B})
be compact quantum metric spaces.
Let
\mathcal{M}(L_{A},L_{B})
be the set of Lip-norms on
A\oplus B

which induce
L_{A}
and
L_{B}
. The
quantum Gromov–Hausdorff
distance
between them, denoted
\operatorname{dist}_{q}(A,B)
, is
defined by

\operatorname{dist}_{q}(A,B)=\inf\bigl\{\operatorname{dist}_{H}^{d^{L}}(S(A),S(B)):L\in\mathcal{M}(L_{A},L_{B})\bigr\}.

We now present a proposition that gives an upper bound for the
quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
9
]
, Proposition 8.5)
.

Let
(A,L_{A})
be a compact quantum metric space, and let
B
be a
subspace of
A
containing
e_{A}
. Let
L_{B}
be the restriction of

L_{A}
to
B
, so that
(B,L_{B})
is a compact quantum metric space.
Let
P:A\to B
be a map
for which there exists a
\delta>0
such that

(1)

L_{B}(P(a))\leq L_{A}(a)
for all
a\in A
;

(2)

\|a-P(a)\|\leq\delta\,L_{A}(a)
for all
a\in A
.

Then
\operatorname{dist}_{q}(A,B)\leq\delta
.

Now we focus on spectral triples and the compact quantum metric spaces
arising from them.

Definition 2.6

(Operator system spectral triple)
.

An

is a triple

(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{H},D)
where
\mathcal{E}
is a dense subspace
of a (concrete) operator system
E
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,

\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space, and

is a self-adjoint operator
in
\mathcal{H}
with compact resolvent such that
[D,T]
is a bounded
operator for all
T\in\mathcal{E}
.

We now consider sequences of operator system spectral triples and their
limits via spectral truncation. The limit structure is captured by the following notion of an approximate order isomorphism.

Let
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{H},D)
be a spectral triple as above:

\mathcal{E}
acts on
\mathcal{H}
,
D
is self-adjoint with compact
resolvent, and
[D,a]
extends to a bounded operator for all
a
in a
dense
*

\mathcal{E}
.
A spectral truncation is given by a spectral projection
P_{\Lambda}
of

.
The space
\mathcal{E}
is then truncated to

\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}:=P_{\Lambda}\mathcal{E}P_{\Lambda}

\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}:=P_{\Lambda}\mathcal{H}
, and

We define the associated Lipschitz seminorms: for

,

and

, set

\displaystyle L^{k}(a):=\|\delta^{k}(a)\|=\bigl\|[D,[D,\dots[D,a]\dots]]\bigr\|,

If these Lipschitz seminorms are actually Lipschitz norms , then we can consider the quantum
Gromov–Hausdorff distance between the corresponding compact quantum

2.2. Compact Quantum Groups

Quantum groups emerged in the 1980s as a framework for studying symmetries
of noncommutative spaces and exactly solvable models in statistical mechanics
and quantum field theory. Among them, compact quantum groups, introduced by
Woronowicz
[
12
]
in the late 1980s, provide a natural generalization of
compact topological groups to the noncommutative setting. They play a central
role in noncommutative geometry, representation theory, and the theory of
operator algebras. In this subsection we recall the basic definition and some
well‑known facts that will be needed later.

Definition 2.7
.

A
compact quantum group
is a pair
(\mathcal{A},\Delta)
where

\mathcal{A}
is a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and

\Delta:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}
is a unital

*
-homomorphism, called the
comultiplication
, satisfying

(1)

(\Delta\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{A}})\Delta=(\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\Delta)\Delta

as homomorphisms
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}
(coassociativity);

(2)

the spaces

(\mathcal{A}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{A}})\Delta(\mathcal{A}):=\operatorname{span}\{(a\otimes 1_{\mathcal{A}})\Delta(b):a,b\in\mathcal{A}\}
and

(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathcal{A})\Delta(\mathcal{A})

are dense in
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}

(the cancellation property).

Here the tensor product is the minimal
C^{*}
-tensor product.

A fundamental fact about compact quantum groups is the existence and
uniqueness of a
Haar state

h:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
.
It is a state satisfying the left and right invariance conditions

(\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes h)\circ\Delta=h(\cdot)1_{\mathcal{A}}=(h\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{A}})\circ\Delta.

In general, the Haar state may not be a trace, and it may not be faithful
on
\mathcal{A}
.

Two important classes of compact quantum groups are distinguished by
additional properties of the Haar state. A compact quantum group

(\mathcal{A},\Delta)
is said to be of
Kac type
if its Haar state
is a trace, i.e.
h(ab)=h(ba)
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
.
Equivalently, there exists a bounded antipode
S
on a dense

*
-subalgebra satisfying
S^{2}=\operatorname{id}
.

A compact quantum group is called
coamenable
if its dual discrete
quantum group is amenable. For a discrete group
\Gamma
, the compact
quantum group
C^{*}(\Gamma)
is coamenable precisely when
\Gamma
is an
amenable group. In the general theory, coamenability can be characterised
by faithfulness of the Haar state on the reduced
C^{*}
-algebra, or by the
existence of a bounded approximate invariant mean for the dual action.

Example 2.8

(Discrete group duals)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group. Denote by
C^{*}(\Gamma)
the full group C
∗
-algebra. There is a natural comultiplication
\Delta:C^{*}(\Gamma)\to C^{*}(\Gamma)\otimes C^{*}(\Gamma)
determined by
\Delta(\lambda_{g})=\lambda_{g}\otimes\lambda_{g}
for the canonical unitaries
\lambda_{g}
(
g\in\Gamma
). Together with the usual involution and unit, this makes
(C^{*}(\Gamma),\Delta)
a compact quantum group. Its Haar state is given by the trivial character
h(\lambda_{g})=1
for all
g
, i.e. the
*
-homomorphism
\varepsilon:C^{*}(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{C}
. This compact quantum group is commutative, and it can be regarded as the dual of the discrete group
\Gamma
.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\pi)
denote the GNS representation of
C(\mathbb{G})

associated with the Haar state, where
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
and

\pi:C(\mathbb{G})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{G})

2.2.1. The fundamental unitaries

On the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\hat{\otimes}L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
,
the
left fundamental unitary

W
and the
right fundamental

are defined on the dense subspace

\pi\otimes\pi\bigl(C(\mathbb{G})\odot C(\mathbb{G})\bigr)

\displaystyle W^{*}\bigl(\pi(x)\otimes\pi(y)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\pi\otimes\pi\bigl(\Delta(y)(x\otimes 1)\bigr),

\displaystyle V\bigl(\pi(x)\otimes\pi(y)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\pi\otimes\pi\bigl(\Delta(x)(1\otimes y)\bigr),

for all

. Both unitaries implement the comultiplication
of

via the fundamental relations

\Delta(x)=W^{*}(1\otimes x)W=V(x\otimes 1)V^{*}\qquad(x\in C(\mathbb{G})).

(2.3)

Consequently,
\Delta
extends uniquely to a normal
*
-homomorphism

\Delta\colon L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\longrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\,\hat{\otimes}\,L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}),

turning

into a compact von Neumann algebraic quantum

26
]

2.2.2. Flip operators and leg-numbering notation

The Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\hat{\otimes}L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
carries
a natural
flip unitary

\Sigma
, defined on elementary tensors by

\Sigma\bigl(\pi(x)\otimes\pi(y)\bigr)=\pi(y)\otimes\pi(x),\qquad x,y\in C(\mathbb{G}).

Conjugation by
\Sigma
implements the
flip automorphism

\sigma

at the
C^{*}
-algebraic level:

\sigma(a\otimes b)\;:=\;b\otimes a\;=\;\Sigma\,(a\otimes b)\,\Sigma,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{B}\bigl(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\bigr).

We shall consistently use the following conventions:

(1)

Leg‑numbering.
For an operator

T\in\mathbb{B}\bigl(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\hat{\otimes}L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\bigr)

we set

T_{12}=T\otimes 1,\qquad T_{13}=(1\otimes\sigma)(T\otimes 1),\qquad T_{23}=1\otimes T,

and analogously for more legs. The same notation is employed for operators
on tensor products of more than two copies of
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
.

(2)

Sweedler notation.
Whenever an element
a\in C(\mathbb{G})

satisfies
\Delta(a)\in C(\mathbb{G})\odot C(\mathbb{G})
, we write

\Delta(a)=a_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)}.

The same convention is used for more general coactions of
\mathbb{G}
;
sums are understood in the usual Sweedler fashion.

Since
W
and
\Sigma V\Sigma
are unitary corepresentations and
W_{23}

and
(\Sigma V\Sigma)_{13}
commute,

Z=W\Sigma V\Sigma\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B}\bigl(L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\bigr)
is also a unitary corepresentation.

2.2.3. Corepresentation theory

A
unitary corepresentation
of a compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}

on a Hilbert space
H
is a unitary element

U\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\,\bar{\otimes}\,\mathcal{B}(H)
satisfying

(\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id})(U)=U_{13}U_{23}.

(2.4)

Both fundamental unitaries
W
and
V
satisfy the pentagon identity
(for
W
it reads
W_{12}W_{13}W_{23}=W_{23}W_{12}
); together with (2.4)
this implies that
W
and
\Sigma V\Sigma
are unitary corepresentations
of
\mathbb{G}
.

The corepresentation theory of
\mathbb{G}
closely resembles the
representation theory of a classical compact group. One has natural
notions of direct sums, tensor products, equivalence and irreducibility.
Moreover, every finite‑dimensional unitary corepresentation decomposes as
a direct sum of irreducible ones. The unit element
1\in C(\mathbb{G})

gives a one‑dimensional (irreducible) corepresentation, whose class is
denoted by
e
.

For each
\alpha\in\Irred(\mathbb{G})
the element
u^{\alpha}
belongs to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\,\bar{\otimes}\,\mathcal{B}(H_{\alpha})
for a
finite dimensional Hilbert space
H_{\alpha}
; write
d_{\alpha}:=\dim H_{\alpha}
.
Choosing an orthonormal basis of
H_{\alpha}
we identify

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\,\bar{\otimes}\,\mathcal{B}(H_{\alpha})\cong M_{d_{\alpha}}\bigl(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\bigr)
and obtain the
matrix coefficients
u_{ij}^{\alpha}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})

(
1\leq i,j\leq d_{\alpha}
). Actually one has
u_{ij}^{\alpha}\in C(\mathbb{G})
,
and the linear span

\Pol(\mathbb{G}):=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\bigl\{\,u_{ij}^{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Irred(\mathbb{G}),\;1\leq i,j\leq d_{\alpha}\,\bigr\}

forms a dense Hopf
*
‑subalgebra of
C(\mathbb{G})
, called the

regular Hopf algebra
of
\mathbb{G}
. The comultiplication restricts
to a map
\Delta:\Pol(\mathbb{G})\to\Pol(\mathbb{G})\odot\Pol(\mathbb{G})

which on matrix coefficients reads

\Delta(u_{ij}^{\alpha})=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{\alpha}}u_{ik}^{\alpha}\otimes u_{kj}^{\alpha}.

(2.5)

The algebra
\Pol(\mathbb{G})
also carries an antipode
S
and a counit

\varepsilon
(see, e.g.,
[
26
, Theorem 5.4.1]
). Because

\Pol(\mathbb{G})
is a Hopf
*
‑algebra, the operators
W^{*}
and
V
map

\Pol(\mathbb{G})\odot\Pol(\mathbb{G})
bijectively onto itself

[
26
, Theorem 1.3.18]
; the same is true for
W
and
V^{*}
.

2.3. Fusion algebras

Now we turn to the fusion algebra, a structure closely tied to the
representation theory of quantum groups.
Fusion algebras, also known as based rings, provide an algebraic framework
that unifies the representation theory of compact groups and the combinatorial
structure of discrete groups. These algebras first emerged systematically in
the study of conformal field theory and have since found profound applications
in subfactor theory, quantum groups, and tensor categories.

Definition 2.9
.

An algebra
R=\bigoplus_{\xi\in I}\mathbb{C}\xi
over
\mathbb{C}
with a
basis
I
and an identity
e\in I
is called a
fusion algebra
if it
satisfies the following axioms:

(i)

Structure constants.
There exist non-negative integers

N_{\xi,\eta}^{\zeta}
for
\xi,\eta,\zeta\in I
such that

\xi\eta=\sum_{\zeta\in I}N_{\xi,\eta}^{\zeta}\,\zeta.

These integers
N_{\xi,\eta}^{\zeta}
are called the

structural constants
of
R
.

(ii)

Conjugation.
There exists an involutive map

\xi\mapsto\bar{\xi}
on
I
, called
conjugation
, whose

\mathbb{Z}
-linear extension to
R
satisfies

\overline{\xi\eta}=\bar{\eta}\bar{\xi},\quad\xi,\eta\in I.

(iii)

Frobenius reciprocity.
The structural constants obey
the symmetry relations

N_{\xi,\eta}^{\zeta}=N_{\xi,\zeta}^{\bar{\eta}}=N_{\xi,\bar{\eta}}^{\bar{\zeta}},\quad\xi,\eta,\zeta\in I.

(iv)

Dimension function.
There exists a function

d:I\to[1,\infty)
, called the
dimension function
(or

Frobenius–Perron dimension
), such that

d(\xi)=d(\bar{\xi})
for all
\xi\in I
and

d(\xi)d(\eta)=\sum_{\zeta\in I}N_{\xi,\eta}^{\zeta}\,d(\zeta),\quad\xi,\eta\in I.

Remark 2.10
.

The dimension function
d
is uniquely determined by the above conditions
and satisfies
d(e)=1
. Moreover, the Frobenius reciprocity (iii) ensures
that the algebra
R
is semisimple when equipped with the bilinear form

\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=\delta_{\xi,\bar{\eta}}
.

We now present two classical examples of fusion algebras: group algebras of
discrete groups and representation rings of compact quantum groups.

Example 2.11

(Group algebra)
.

Let
G
be a discrete group. The group algebra

R=\mathbb{C}[G]=\bigoplus_{g\in G}\mathbb{C}g
forms a fusion algebra
with the following data:

•

Basis:
\Xi_{0}=G
with identity
e
being the group identity;

•

Multiplication:
N_{g_{1},g_{2}}^{h}=\delta_{h,g_{1}g_{2}}
for

g_{1},g_{2},h\in G
;

•

Conjugation:
\bar{g}=g^{-1}
;

•

Dimension:
d(g)=1
for all
g\in G
.

This example represents the simplest class of fusion algebras, where all
basis elements have trivial dimension and the structural constants are given
by the group multiplication.

Example 2.12

(Representation ring)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact matrix quantum group and let

\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
denote the set of equivalence classes of irreducible
unitary corepresentations of
\mathbb{G}
. The representation ring

\Xi=R(\mathbb{G})=\bigoplus_{\xi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}}\mathbb{C}\xi

carries a fusion algebra structure characterized by:

•

Basis:
I=\Irred(\mathbb{G})
, where
\Irred(\mathbb{G})
is a
complete family of representatives for the equivalence classes of
irreducible unitary corepresentations of
\mathbb{G}
;

•

Multiplication:
N_{\xi,\eta}^{\zeta}
equals the multiplicity of

\zeta
in the irreducible decomposition of the tensor product

\xi\otimes\eta
;

•

Conjugation:
\bar{\xi}
is the conjugate corepresentation of
\xi
;

•

Dimension:
d(\xi)=\dim\xi
, the quantum dimension.

The dimension condition (iv) in Definition
2.9
reflects
the well-known identity
\dim(\xi\otimes\eta)=(\dim\xi)(\dim\eta)
in
representation theory.

The notion of amenability for finitely generated fusion algebras was
introduced in
[
14
]
. A finitely generated fusion algebra is called

amenable
if there exist
1<p<\infty
and a finitely supported,
symmetric, non‑degenerate probability measure
\mu
on
I
such that

\|\lambda_{p,\mu}\|=1.

If
\mathbb{G}
is a compact matrix quantum group, its corepresentation ring

R(\mathbb{G})
is finitely generated. Hence amenability of
\mathbb{G}
can
be defined naturally through the amenability of
R(\mathbb{G})
.

In
[
3
]
, David Kyed proposed a more general definition that applies to
quantum groups that do not necessarily possess a fundamental corepresentation.
We shall introduce this definition in detail below.

Definition 2.13

(Amenable Fusion Algebra)
.

A fusion algebra
R=\mathbb{C}[I]
is called
amenable
if

1\in\sigma(\lambda_{2,\mu})
for every finitely supported, symmetric
probability measure
\mu
on
I
.

Here
\sigma(\lambda_{2,\mu})
denotes the spectrum of the operator

\lambda_{2,\mu}
. From Proposition 1.3 and Corollary 4.4 in
[
14
]

it follows that the definition agrees with the one in
[
14
]
on the
class of finitely generated fusion algebras.

Definition 2.14

(Boundary)
.

Let
R=\mathbb{C}[I]
be a fusion algebra. For two finite subsets

S,F\subseteq I
we define the
boundary of
F
relative to
S
as
the set

\displaystyle\partial_{S}(F)

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in F\mid\exists\,\xi\in S:\operatorname{supp}(\alpha\xi)\nsubseteq F\}

\displaystyle\quad\cup\{\alpha\in F^{c}\mid\exists\,\xi\in S:\operatorname{supp}(\alpha\xi)\nsubseteq F^{c}\}.

Here, and in what follows,
F^{c}
denotes the set
I\setminus F
.

Definition 2.15

(Følner condition)
.

Let
R=\mathbb{C}[I]
be a fusion algebra with dimension function
d
.
For every finite, non-empty subset
S\subseteq I
and every

\varepsilon>0
there exists a finite subset
F\subseteq I
such that

\sum_{\xi\in\partial_{S}(F)}d(\xi)^{2}<\varepsilon\sum_{\xi\in F}d(\xi)^{2}.

Remark 2.16
.

By
[
3
]
, a fusion algebra
R
is amenable provided that it satisfies the
Følner condition.

3. Compact Quantum Metric Space from Fusion Algebras

Consider a compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
and its fusion algebra

F(\mathbb{G})=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\alpha\mid\alpha\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})\}.

We endow
F(\mathbb{G})
with the inner product for which the elements in

\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
form an orthogonal basis, and denote
the completion by
\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))
.
Let
\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))
be the Hilbert space with
orthogonal basis
\{\alpha:\alpha\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})\}

and
\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle=d(\alpha)^{2}
. Here
d(\alpha)
is the
dimension function from
\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})\to[1,\infty)

such that
d(\alpha)=d(\bar{\alpha})
and

d(\alpha)d(\beta)=\sum_{\gamma}N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}d(\gamma)
,

\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
. We denote by

N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}
the multiplicity of

\gamma\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
in the decomposition of

\alpha\otimes\beta
into irreducibles; i.e.

\alpha\otimes\beta\simeq\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})}\gamma^{\oplus N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}}.

(3.1)

Let
c_{c}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))
denote the dense subspace
spanned by the orthogonal basis.

For each
f\in F(\mathbb{G})
, we obtain a linear map

\pi_{0}(f)\colon c_{c}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))\longrightarrow c_{c}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))
defined by left multiplication.

From
[
16
, Lemma 3.3]
, we know that
\pi_{0}(f)
extends to a bounded
operator
\pi(f)
on
\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact quantum group. A
length function
on

\mathbb{G}
is a function

\ell\colon\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})\to[0,\infty)
such that

(i)

\ell(e)=0
;

(ii)

\ell(\bar{\alpha})=\ell(\alpha)
for all

\alpha\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
;

(iii)

\ell(\gamma)\leq\ell(\alpha)+\ell(\beta)
for all

\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
such that

\gamma
is equivalent to a sub-corepresentation of

\alpha\otimes\beta
.

A length function
\ell
is called
proper
if

\ell^{-1}([0,N])
is finite for all
N\geq 0
and

\ell(\alpha)=0
only when
\alpha=e
.
The length function also defines an unbounded operator

\tilde{D}_{\ell}:c_{c}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))\longrightarrow\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})),\qquad\tilde{D}_{\ell}(\alpha)=\ell(\alpha)\,\alpha,

and the range of
\ell
is
\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
We now additionally assume that
\mathbb{G}
is equipped with a proper
length function
\ell\colon\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Setting

A_{n}:=\operatorname{span}\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})\mid\ell(\alpha)\leqslant n\},

then
A_{m}\subset A_{n}
if
m<n
,

F(\mathbb{G})=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}
,
A_{n}^{*}=A_{n}
,

A_{m}A_{n}\subseteq A_{m+n}
, and
A_{0}=\mathbb{C}1_{A}
. Consequently,
the fusion algebra
F(\mathbb{G})
belongs to the class of filtered

*
-algebras studied by Ozawa and Rieffel in
[
14
]
.

We can view each
A_{n}
as a finite-dimensional, hence closed, subspace
of
\mathcal{H}
. Let
P_{n}
denote the orthogonal projection of

\mathcal{H}
onto
A_{n}
. We set
Q_{n}
for
n\geq 1
and

P_{0}=Q_{0}
such that for each
a\in F(\mathbb{G})
and each
n
we set

a_{n}=Q_{n}(a)
with
\tilde{D}_{\ell}(a_{n})=na_{n}
. If there is no element

\alpha\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
with
\ell(\alpha)=n
,
then
a_{n}=0
. The
Q_{n}
are mutually orthogonal, and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Q_{n}=I_{\mathcal{H}}
in the strong operator
topology. Then
a_{n}\in A_{n}
, but
a_{n}\notin A_{n-1}
unless

a_{n}=0
. Moreover,
a=\sum_{n}a_{n}
, with at most
p
non‑zero terms
if
a\in A_{p}
.

Actually,
\tilde{D}_{\ell}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nQ_{n}
. We now consider
whether it can form a spectral triple for the fusion algebra.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact quantum group equipped with a proper
length function
\ell
. Then the triple

\bigl(F(\mathbb{G}),\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})),\tilde{D}_{\ell}\bigr)
is a spectral triple.

Proof.

For all
x,y\in\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))
,

\langle\tilde{D}_{\ell}x,y\rangle=\sum_{\alpha}\overline{\ell(\alpha)}\,\overline{x_{\alpha}}\,y_{\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha}\overline{x_{\alpha}}\,\ell(\alpha)\,y_{\alpha}=\langle x,\tilde{D}_{\ell}y\rangle,

so
\tilde{D}_{\ell}
is self‑adjoint.

We show that
\tilde{D}_{\ell}
has compact resolvent. If

\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
is finite this is trivial. Assume

\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
is infinite and take

\lambda\notin\sigma(\tilde{D}_{\ell})
. The resolvent

(\tilde{D}_{\ell}-\lambda)^{-1}
is the diagonal operator with
eigenvalues
(\ell(\alpha)-\lambda)^{-1}
. Since
\ell
is proper,
the set

\ell^{-1}([0,R])=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}):\ell(\alpha)\leq R\}

is finite for every
R\geq 0
. Consequently, the eigenvalues of

(\tilde{D}_{\ell}-\lambda)^{-1}
tend to zero, i.e. the resolvent
is compact.

Now suppose
a\in A_{p}
. For any
m,n\geq 0
, if

Q_{m}aQ_{n}\neq 0
then there exists
\xi\in A_{n}
with

a\xi\in A_{m}
. Because
A_{p}A_{n}\subseteq A_{p+n}
, we have

p+n\geq m
. Taking adjoints,
Q_{m}a^{*}Q_{n}\neq 0
implies

p+m\geq n
. Hence
|m-n|\leq p
. Therefore

a=\sum_{|m-n|\leq p}Q_{m}aQ_{n},

converging in the strong operator topology. For each
j
with

|j|\leq p
set

T_{j}=\sum_{m}Q_{m}aQ_{m-j}.

For fixed
j
, the ranges of the terms
Q_{m}aQ_{m-j}
are mutually
orthogonal, as are their domains, so

\|T_{j}\|=\sup_{m}\|Q_{m}aQ_{m-j}\|\leq\|a\|.

For any
m,n
we have

[\tilde{D}_{\ell},Q_{m}aQ_{n}]=(m-n)Q_{m}aQ_{n}
. In particular,

[\tilde{D}_{\ell},Q_{m}aQ_{m-j}]=j\,Q_{m}aQ_{m-j},

and thus
[\tilde{D}_{\ell},T_{j}]=jT_{j}
. Since

a=\sum_{|j|\leq p}T_{j}
, we obtain

[\tilde{D}_{\ell},a]=\sum_{|j|\leq p}jT_{j},

which is a bounded operator. Therefore

\bigl(F(\mathbb{G}),\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})),\tilde{D}_{\ell}\bigr)
is a spectral triple.
∎

When considering a discrete group equipped with a length function, one often studies the properties of rapid decay and polynomial growth. In
[
20
]
, Vergnioux introduced a notion of rapid decay for discrete quantum groups. We will apply this property of rapid decay to fusion algebras constructed from the irreducible corepresentations of a compact quantum group. Since every
f\in F(\mathbb{G})
has finite support, we can directly give a definition on the fusion algebra.

Definition 3.3

(Rapid Decay for Fusion Algebras)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact matric quantum group.Let
F(\mathbb{G})
be a fusion algebra with irreducible basis
\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
, and for each
\alpha\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
let
d(\alpha)
denote its dimension. Fix a length function
\ell:\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})\to[0,\infty)
(e.g., induced by a set of generators). For
f\in F(\mathbb{G})
define the weighted
\ell^{2}
-norm

\|f\|_{2,s}:=\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})}|f(\alpha)|^{2}\,d(\alpha)^{2}\,(1+\ell(\alpha))^{2s}\right)^{1/2}\qquad s>0.

The fusion algebra
F(\mathbb{G})
is said to have the
rapid decay property
if there exist constants
C>0
and
s>0
such that

\|\pi(f)\|\leq C\|f\|_{2,s}

for every
f\in F(\mathbb{G})
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
G
be a compact quantum group. We say that
G
has

polynomial growth of order s with respect to length function
\ell
on
G

if there exist
c,s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell(\alpha)\in(n-1,n]\end{subarray}}d(\alpha)^{2}\leq cn^{s}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We say that
G
has
strong polynomial growth with respect to
length function
\ell
on
G
if there exist

c_{1},c_{2},s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

c_{2}n^{s}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell(\alpha)\in(n-1,n]\end{subarray}}d(\alpha)^{2}\leq c_{1}n^{s}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We say that
G
has
(strong) polynomial growth
if it has
(strong) polynomial growth with respect to some length function on
G
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
G
be a compact matrix quantum group with strong polynomial growth.
Then the sequence
(A_{\Lambda})_{\Lambda\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Følner
sequence in
F(G)
.

Proof.

For every irreducible representation
\pi\in\operatorname{Irred}(G)
,
define the
\pi
-boundary of
A_{\Lambda}
by

\partial_{\pi}A_{\Lambda}=\bigl\{\alpha\in A_{\Lambda}:\exists\beta\in A_{\Lambda}^{c},\ \beta\in\alpha\otimes\pi\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{\beta\in A_{\Lambda}^{c}:\exists\alpha\in A_{\Lambda},\ \alpha\in\beta\otimes\pi\bigr\}.

Then we estimate the weighted cardinality of
\partial_{\pi}(A_{\Lambda})
:

\sum_{\xi\in\partial_{\pi}(A_{\Lambda})}d(\xi)^{2}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha:N_{\alpha,\pi}^{\beta}\neq 0,\ \ell(\alpha)\leq\Lambda,\ \ell(\beta)\geq\Lambda\end{subarray}}d(\alpha)^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta:N_{\alpha,\pi}^{\beta}\neq 0,\ \ell(\alpha)\leq\Lambda,\ \ell(\beta)\geq\Lambda\end{subarray}}d(\beta)^{2}.

From the definition of the length function, for any non‑zero multiplicity

N_{\alpha,\pi}^{\beta}
we have

\Lambda\leq\ell(\beta)\leq\ell(\alpha)+\ell(\pi),\qquad\Lambda-\ell(\pi)\leq\ell(\alpha)\leq\Lambda.

Consequently every
\alpha
appearing in the first sum satisfies

\Lambda-\ell(\pi)\leq\ell(\alpha)\leq\Lambda
and every
\beta

appearing in the second sum satisfies

\Lambda\leq\ell(\beta)\leq\Lambda+\ell(\pi)
. Hence

\sum_{\xi\in\partial_{\pi}(A_{\Lambda})}d(\xi)^{2}\leq\sum_{\alpha:\ \Lambda-\ell(\pi)\leq\ell(\alpha)\leq\Lambda+\ell(\pi)}d(\alpha)^{2}.

Since
G
has strong polynomial growth, there exists a constant

\varepsilon_{\Lambda}
(depending only on
\Lambda
and
\pi
) such that

\sum_{\xi\in\partial_{\pi}(A_{\Lambda})}d(\xi)^{2}\leq\varepsilon_{\Lambda}\sum_{\xi\in A_{\Lambda}}d(\xi)^{2},

and
\varepsilon_{\Lambda}\to 0
as
\Lambda\to\infty
. This shows that

(A_{\Lambda})
is a Følner sequence in
F(G)
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
G
be a compact matrix quantum group with polynomial growth.
Then
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{G})
is rapid decay.

Proof.

For any unit vector

\xi=\sum_{\beta\in A_{\Lambda}}\xi_{\beta}\beta
in

\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))
,

f\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{G})
we have

\displaystyle\|f\xi\|

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{\beta}f(\alpha)\xi(\beta)\sum_{\gamma}N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}\gamma\Bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha}|f(\alpha)|\Bigl\|\sum_{\gamma}\sum_{\beta}N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}\xi_{\beta}\gamma\Bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha}|f(\alpha)|\sup_{\xi}\Bigl(\sum_{\gamma}\Bigl(\sum_{\beta}N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}\xi_{\beta}\Bigr)^{2}d(\gamma)^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha}|f(\alpha)|\sup_{\xi}\Bigl(\sum_{\gamma}\sum_{\beta}(N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}\xi_{\beta})^{2}d(\alpha)^{2}d(\gamma)^{2}d(\alpha)^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha}|f(\alpha)|\sup_{\xi}\Bigl(\sum_{\beta}(\xi_{\beta})^{2}\Bigl(\sum_{\gamma}N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}d(\gamma)\Bigr)^{2}d(\alpha)^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}|f(\alpha)|\sup_{\xi}\Bigl(\sum_{\beta}(\xi_{\beta})^{2}d(\beta)^{2}d(\alpha)^{4}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}|f(\alpha)|d(\alpha)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\sum_{\alpha}\bigl(|f(\alpha)|^{2}d(\alpha)^{2}(1+\ell(\alpha))^{2s}\bigr)\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{\alpha}\frac{d(\alpha)^{2}}{(1+\ell(\alpha))^{2s}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
G
has polynomial growth,

C=\bigl(\sum_{\alpha}\frac{d(\alpha)^{2}}{(1+\ell(\alpha))^{2s}}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}
is a constant. We complete the proof.
∎

Now we begin to consider Lip-seminorm on fusion algebra.
If we define
\delta(f):=[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\pi(f)]
, then

\delta^{k}(f)=[\tilde{D}_{\ell},[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\dots[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\pi(f)]\dots]]
,
where the commutator is taken
k
times.

Lemma 3.7
.

For the fusion algebra
F(\mathbb{G})
and for every positive integer

k
,
\|\delta^{k}\|
is a Lip-seminorm.

Proof.

For any
f=\sum_{\alpha}f(\alpha)\,\alpha\in F(\mathbb{G})
, let

\pi(f)
denote its left regular representation on

\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))
:

\pi(f)\beta=\sum_{\alpha,\gamma\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})}f(\alpha)N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}\,\gamma.

The adjoint
[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\pi(f)]^{*}
satisfies

\langle\beta,[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\pi(f)]^{*}\gamma\rangle=\overline{\langle\gamma,[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\pi(f)]\beta\rangle}=\sum_{\alpha}(\ell(\gamma)-\ell(\beta))\overline{f(\alpha)}N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}.

And

\displaystyle\langle\beta,[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\pi(f^{*})]\gamma\rangle

\displaystyle=(\ell(\beta)-\ell(\gamma))\langle\beta,\pi(f^{*})\gamma\rangle

\displaystyle=(\ell(\beta)-\ell(\gamma))\sum_{\alpha}f^{*}(\alpha)N_{\bar{\alpha},\gamma}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}(\ell(\beta)-\ell(\gamma))\overline{f(\alpha)}N_{\bar{\alpha},\gamma}^{\beta}.

For a compact quantum group, the fusion coefficients satisfy

N_{\bar{\alpha},\gamma}^{\beta}=N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}

(the representation category is spherical). Using this,

\langle\beta,[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\pi(f^{*})]\gamma\rangle=\sum_{\alpha}(\ell(\beta)-\ell(\gamma))\overline{f(\alpha)}N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}=-\sum_{\alpha}(\ell(\gamma)-\ell(\beta))\overline{f(\alpha)}N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}.

Comparing with the matrix elements of
[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\pi(f)]^{*}
we obtain

[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\pi(f)]^{*}=-\,[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\pi(f^{*})].

If we define
\delta(f):=[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\pi(f)]
, then

\delta^{k}(f)=[\tilde{D}_{\ell},[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\dots[\tilde{D}_{\ell},\pi(f)]\dots]]
.
Now, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in F(\mathbb{G})
, consider

\delta^{k}(f)^{*}=(-1)^{k}\delta^{k}(f^{*}).

Then
\|\delta^{k}(f)^{*}\|=\|\delta^{k}(f^{*})\|
.
Note that if
e\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
is the identity of

F(\mathbb{G})
,

\delta^{k}(f)e=\sum_{\alpha\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})}\ell(\alpha)^{k}f(\alpha)\alpha
.
Then for
f\in F(\mathbb{G})
, we have
\|\delta^{k}(f)\|=0
iff

f\in\mathbb{C}e
.
So the Lipschitz semi‑norm (Rieffel’s framework) can be defined as the
operator norm of this commutator:

L_{k}(\cdot):=\|\delta^{k}(\cdot)\|.

∎

Remark 3.8
.

In the truncated picture, for
\Lambda>0
let
P_{\Lambda}
be the
orthogonal projection onto

A_{\Lambda}=\operatorname{span}\{\alpha:\ell(\alpha)\leq\Lambda\}
,
set
\tilde{D}_{\ell\Lambda}=P_{\Lambda}\tilde{D}_{\ell}P_{\Lambda}
, and for

f_{\Lambda}\in P_{\Lambda}F(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda}
(which corresponds to a
function
f
on
\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
via

f_{\Lambda}=P_{\Lambda}\pi(f)P_{\Lambda}
) we have

[\tilde{D}_{\ell\Lambda},f_{\Lambda}]\beta=\sum_{\gamma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha:\ \exists\beta,\gamma\in A_{\Lambda}\\
\text{s.t. }N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}\neq 0\end{subarray}}(\ell(\gamma)-\ell(\beta))f(\alpha)N_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}\,\gamma,\qquad\gamma,\beta\in A_{\Lambda}.

Note that for
f\in F(\mathbb{G})
, we have

[\tilde{D}_{\ell\Lambda},f_{\Lambda}]=0
iff
f_{\Lambda}\in\mathbb{C}e
.
and

[\tilde{D}_{\ell,\Lambda},\pi(f_{\Lambda})]^{*}=-\,[\tilde{D}_{\ell,\Lambda},\pi(f_{\Lambda}^{*})].

Consequently,

\|\delta_{\Lambda}^{k}(\cdot)\|=\bigl\|[\tilde{D}_{\ell\Lambda},[\tilde{D}_{\ell\Lambda},\dots[\tilde{D}_{\ell\Lambda},\pi(\cdot)]\dots]]\bigr\|

is a
\operatorname{Lip}
-seminorm for
A_{\Lambda}
. Since
A_{\Lambda}

is finite dimensional,
\|\delta_{\Lambda}^{k}(\cdot)\|
is a Lip-norm.

For any Lip seminorm
\|\cdot\|
on
F(\mathbb{G})
,
f\in F(\mathbb{G})

and
r\geqslant 0
we denote the corresponding closed balls as follows:

\mathbb{B}_{r}^{\|\cdot\|}(f):=\{g\in F(\mathbb{G})\mid\|f-g\|\leqslant r\}.

Here we let
[\cdot]:F(\mathbb{G})\to F(\mathbb{G})/\mathbb{C}
denote the
quotient map and
\|\cdot\|_{F(\mathbb{G})/\mathbb{C}}
denote the quotient
norm on
F(\mathbb{G})/\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 3.9
.

If
\mathbb{G}
is a compact quantum group with polynomial growth of order s, then
for any positive integer
k>s
, the algebra
F(\mathbb{G})
together
with the seminorm
L_{\ell}^{k}
is a compact quantum metric space.

Proof.

By Rieffel
[
9
]
, we know
(F(\mathbb{G}),L_{k})
is a compact quantum
metric space if and only if the subset

\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{1}^{L_{k}}(0)\subseteq F(\mathbb{G})/\mathbb{C}
is
totally bounded with respect to the quotient norm

\|\cdot\|_{F(\mathbb{G})/\mathbb{C}}
on
F(\mathbb{G})/\mathbb{C}
.
Let
B=\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{1}^{L_{k}}(0)=B_{1}+B_{2}
where

B_{1}=\Bigl\{f\in B\Bigm|f=\sum_{\alpha\,|\,\ell(\alpha)\leq n}f(\alpha)\alpha\Bigr\}

and

B_{2}=\Bigl\{f\in B\Bigm|f=\sum_{\alpha\,|\,\ell(\alpha)\geq n}f(\alpha)\alpha\Bigr\}.

For all
f\in B_{1}
,

\displaystyle\|f\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell(\alpha)\leq n}|f(\alpha)|d(\alpha)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\biggl(\sum_{\ell(\alpha)\leq n}|f(\alpha)|^{2}d(\alpha)^{2}(1+\ell(\alpha)^{2s})\biggr)^{1/2}\biggl(\sum_{\ell(\alpha)\leq n}\frac{d(\alpha)^{2}}{(1+\ell(\alpha))^{2s}}\biggr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}.

where

C_{n}=\Bigl(\sum_{\ell(\alpha)\leq n}\frac{d(\alpha)^{2}}{(1+\ell(\alpha))^{2s}}\Bigr)^{1/2}
.
For all
f\in B_{2}
, fix a positive integer
k\geq s
. By the rapid
decay property we have

\displaystyle\|f\|

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigl(\sum_{\ell(\alpha)\geq n}|f(\alpha)|^{2}d(\alpha)^{2}(1+\ell(\alpha)^{2s})\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{s}n^{s-k}\Bigl(\sum_{\ell(\alpha)\geq n}|f(\alpha)|^{2}d(\alpha)^{2}(\ell(\alpha))^{2k}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Then
B_{1}
is a bounded subset of a finite‑dimensional normed space, and
thus totally bounded. Moreover, by our choice of
n
, the set
B_{2}
is
contained in the
\epsilon
-ball around
0
. Therefore, for any positive
integer
k\geq s
, the algebra
F(\mathbb{G})
together with the
seminorm
L_{k}
is a compact quantum metric space.
∎

Example 3.10

(Connected compact Lie groups)
.

Let
G
be a connected compact Lie group and equip the commutative
compact quantum group
C(G)
with a length function

\ell(\alpha)=\|\lambda_{\alpha}\|
, where
\lambda_{\alpha}
is the highest
weight of an irreducible representation
\alpha
.
Then
d(\alpha)
is the ordinary dimension of the representation.
The Weyl dimension formula yields

\sum_{\ell(\alpha)\in(n-1,n]}d(\alpha)^{2}\asymp n^{D-1},

where

is the dimension of

as a smooth manifold.
Thus

has strong polynomial growth of order

.
For instance, for all

,
\mathrm{SU}(N)
and

\mathrm{SO}(N)
have strong polynomial growth.

4. Gromov–Hausdorff Convergence of Spectral
Truncation for Compact Quantum Groups

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact quantum group with Hopf algebra of polynomial
functions
\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})
. Let
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
be the
set of equivalence classes of irreducible unitary representations.
Let
\{u_{ij}^{\alpha}\}
be the matrix coefficients of

\alpha\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
, and let
(\mathcal{H},\pi)
denote the
GNS representation of
C(\mathbb{G})
associated with the Haar state. Then

\{\pi(u_{ij}^{\alpha})\}
forms an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
.
According to Definition
3.1
, for a proper length function, an
unbounded Dirac operator

D_{\ell}:\pi(\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G}))\to\mathcal{H}
can be defined by

D_{\ell}\bigl(\pi(u_{i,j}^{\alpha})\bigr)=\ell(\alpha)\,\pi(u_{i,j}^{\alpha}).

By
[
23
, Lemma 5.4]
, we know that

(\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G}),\mathcal{H},D_{\ell})
is a spectral triple.

A spectral truncation for the spectral triple is given by a spectral
projection
P_{\Lambda}
of
D_{\ell}
onto the eigenspaces with eigenvalues of
modulus
\leq\Lambda
. The
C^{*}
-algebra
C(\mathbb{G})
is replaced by

P_{\Lambda}C(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda}
, acting on the Hilbert space

P_{\Lambda}\mathcal{H}
, and we set

D_{\ell,\Lambda}:=D_{\ell}|_{P_{\Lambda}\mathcal{H}}
.
If
L_{C(\mathbb{G})}
is the Lipschitz seminorm associated with
D_{\ell}
, we
consider the quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance between
C(\mathbb{G})
and
its truncation. If this distance tends to zero as
\Lambda\to\infty
, we say
that the spectral truncation of
C(\mathbb{G})
converges in quantum
Gromov–Hausdorff distance.

When we consider the spectral truncation of this Dirac operator, since the
length function is proper the resulting spectral truncation is analogous to
the classical Peter–Weyl truncation.
The commutator seminorm

L_{\ell}:\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})\to[0,\infty)
is defined by

L_{\ell}(a):=\|[D_{\ell},a]\|,

where
[D_{\ell},a]=D_{\ell}a-aD_{\ell}
and
\|\cdot\|
is the operator norm
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
.

We now prove that
L_{\ell}
is a Lip-seminorm in the sense of Rieffel.

Lemma 4.1
.

The functional
L_{\ell}
is a Lip-seminorm on
\mathbb{G}
, and its
truncation
L_{\ell,\Lambda}
is a Lip-norm on the finite-dimensional
space
P_{\Lambda}C(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda}
.

Proof.

Let
a=\sum_{\alpha,i,j}a_{ij}^{\alpha}\,\pi(u_{ij}^{\alpha})
.
Since
(\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G}),L^{2}(\mathbb{G}),D_{\ell})
is a
spectral triple,
L_{\ell}
is densely defined.
Observe that for every element

u_{i,j}^{\alpha}\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})
,

D_{\ell}\bigl(\pi(u_{i,j}^{\alpha})\bigr)=\ell(\alpha)\,\pi(u_{i,j}^{\alpha}).

First, if
a=c\cdot e
for some
c\in\mathbb{C}
, then

[D_{\ell},a]=0
, and consequently
L_{\ell}(a)=0
.

Conversely, assume
L_{\ell}(a)=0
; that is,
[D_{\ell},a]=0
. Then

[D_{\ell},a]=\sum_{\alpha,i,j}a_{ij}^{\alpha}\,\ell(\alpha)\,\pi(u_{ij}^{\alpha}).

Since the set
\{\pi(u_{ij}^{\alpha})\}
forms an orthonormal basis, the
condition
\|[D_{\ell},a]\|=0
forces

a_{ij}^{\alpha}\,\ell(\alpha)=0\qquad\text{for all }\alpha,i,j.

Since the length function
\ell
is proper,
\ell(\alpha)=0
only when

\alpha=e
. Hence the kernel of
L_{\ell}
consists exactly of the scalar
multiples of the identity.
Moreover, for every
\alpha\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
we have

\ell(\alpha)=\ell(\bar{\alpha})
, which implies that
D_{\ell}
is
self‑adjoint. It follows that
L_{\ell}(a)=L_{\ell}(a^{*})
.
Hence
L_{\ell}
satisfies the axioms of a Lipschitz seminorm.

Because
P_{\Lambda}C(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda}
is finite‑dimensional, the
restriction
L_{\ell,\Lambda}
is in fact a Lip‑norm, as required.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
a\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})
,
define the iterated commutator

\delta^{k}(a)=[D_{\ell},[D_{\ell},\dots[D_{\ell},a]\dots]]\quad(k\text{ times}),

and set

L_{\ell}^{k}(a)=\|\delta^{k}(a)\|.

Analogously, for
b\in P_{\Lambda}\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda}
let

\delta_{\Lambda}^{k}(b)=[D_{\ell,\Lambda},[D_{\ell,\Lambda},\dots[D_{\ell,\Lambda},b]\dots]]

and

L_{\ell,\Lambda}^{k}(b)=\|\delta_{\Lambda}^{k}(b)\|.

The same reasoning as in the proof of Lemma
4.1
shows
that
L_{\ell}^{k}
is a Lip‑seminorm on
\mathbb{G}
, while its truncation

L_{\ell,\Lambda}^{k}
is a Lip‑norm on the finite‑dimensional subspace

P_{\Lambda}C(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda}
.

Definition 4.3
.

A Lip-seminorm
L
on a compact quantum group
(\mathcal{A},\Delta)
is
said to be
left-invariant
(or
right-invariant
) if for all

a\in\mathcal{A}
with
a^{*}=a
and all
\mu\in S(\mathcal{A})
we have

L(a*\mu)\leq L(a)\quad(\text{or }L(\mu*a)\leq L(a)),

where
a*\mu=(\mu\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta(a)
and similarly for

\mu*a
. Then
L
is said to be
bi-invariant
if it is both left
and right invariant.

Now we show that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
L_{\ell}^{k}
is left-invariant.
First, the comultiplication extends to a normal
*
-homomorphism

\Delta\colon L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
, turning

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
into a compact von Neumann algebraic quantum
group
[
22
]
. On
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\hat{\otimes}L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
we have the
left fundamental unitary
W
defined on the dense subspace

\pi\otimes\pi(C(\mathbb{G})\odot C(\mathbb{G}))
by the relation

W^{*}(\pi(x)\otimes\pi(y))=\pi\otimes\pi\bigl(\Delta(y)(x\otimes 1)\bigr).

It implements the comultiplication on
C(\mathbb{G})
by means of

\Delta(x)=W^{*}(1\otimes x)W.

Lemma 4.4
.

L_{\ell}^{k}
is left-invariant, i.e. the seminorm
L_{\ell}^{k}
satisfies

L_{\ell}^{k}\bigl((\varphi\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta(a)\bigr)\leq\|\varphi\|\cdot L_{\ell}^{k}(a)

for all
a\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})
and

\varphi\in S(C(\mathbb{G}))
.

Proof.

Computing with matrix coefficients we obtain

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}\otimes D_{\ell})W^{*}\bigl(\pi(u_{ij}^{\alpha})\otimes\pi(u_{pq}^{\beta})\bigr)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}\otimes D_{\ell})(\pi\otimes\pi)\bigl(\Delta(u_{pq}^{\beta})(u_{ij}^{\alpha}\otimes 1)\bigr)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}\otimes D_{\ell})\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{d_{\beta}}\pi(u_{pk}^{\beta}u_{ij}^{\alpha})\otimes\pi(u_{kq}^{\beta})\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\ell(\beta)\sum_{k=1}^{d_{\beta}}\pi(u_{pk}^{\beta}u_{ij}^{\alpha})\otimes\pi(u_{kq}^{\beta})

\displaystyle=W^{*}(\mathrm{id}\otimes D_{\ell})\bigl(\pi(u_{ij}^{\alpha})\otimes\pi(u_{pq}^{\beta})\bigr).

For
a\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})
, we compute as follows, where all
equalities are understood to hold on the dense subspace

\pi(\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G}))\odot\pi(\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G}))\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\hat{\otimes}L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
:

\displaystyle[\operatorname{id}\otimes D_{\ell},\,[\operatorname{id}\otimes D_{\ell},\,\dots,\,[\operatorname{id}\otimes D_{\ell},\Delta(a)]\dots]]

\displaystyle=[\operatorname{id}\otimes D_{\ell},\,[\operatorname{id}\otimes D_{\ell},\,\dots,\,[\operatorname{id}\otimes D_{\ell},W^{*}(1\otimes a)W]\dots]]

\displaystyle=W^{*}\,[\operatorname{id}\otimes D_{\ell},\,[\operatorname{id}\otimes D_{\ell},\,\dots,\,[\operatorname{id}\otimes D_{\ell},1\otimes a]\dots]]\,W.

We therefore obtain the following identities for operators defined on the
dense subspace
\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
:

\displaystyle L_{\ell}^{k}\bigl((\varphi\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta(a)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\bigl\|[D_{\ell},[D_{\ell},\dots,[D_{\ell},(\varphi\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta(a)]\dots]]\bigr\|

\displaystyle=\bigl\|\varphi(a_{(1)})\,[D_{\ell},[D_{\ell},\dots,[D_{\ell},a_{(2)}]\dots]]\bigr\|

\displaystyle=\bigl\|(\varphi\otimes\operatorname{id})[\operatorname{id}\otimes D_{\ell},\,[\operatorname{id}\otimes D_{\ell},\,\dots,\,[\operatorname{id}\otimes D_{\ell},\Delta(a)]\dots]]\bigr\|

\displaystyle=\bigl\|(\varphi\otimes\operatorname{id})\bigl(W^{*}(1\otimes[D_{\ell},[D_{\ell},\dots,[D_{\ell},a]\dots]])W\bigr)\bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\otimes\operatorname{id}\|\cdot\bigl\|W^{*}(1\otimes[D_{\ell},[D_{\ell},\dots,[D_{\ell},a]\dots]])W\bigr\|

\displaystyle=\|\varphi\|\cdot L_{\ell}^{k}(a).

Thus the desired inequality holds.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

As the same argument as in Lemma
4.4
shows, we can similarly
conclude on the truncated space that the seminorm
L_{\ell,\Lambda}^{k}
is
left-invariant. Specifically, for all
b\in P_{\Lambda}\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda}

and
\varphi\in S(P_{\Lambda}C(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda})
, we have

L_{\ell,\Lambda}^{k}\bigl((\varphi\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta(b)\bigr)\leq\|\varphi\|\cdot L_{\ell,\Lambda}^{k}(b).

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\bigl(C(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}^{k}\bigr)
be a compact quantum metric space.
Then for any state
\mu\in S(C(\mathbb{G}))
and any
\epsilon>0
, there
exists a state
\nu\in S(C(\mathbb{G}))
satisfying

|\mu(a)-\nu(a)|\;\leq\;\epsilon\,L_{\ell}^{k}(a),\qquad\forall a\in C(\mathbb{G}),

such that
\nu(b)=0
whenever

b\in\bigl(P_{\Lambda}C(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda}\bigr)^{c}
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\pi)
denote the GNS representation of
C(\mathbb{G})

associated with the Haar state. Since
(C(\mathbb{G}),\Delta)
is in reduced
form, the representation
\pi
is faithful. Define

\mathcal{K}\;:=\;\pi\bigl(\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})\bigr)\,\mathcal{H},

where
\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})
is the dense
*
-subalgebra of

C(\mathbb{G})
consisting of matrix coefficients. Then
\mathcal{K}
is
a dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}
.

Fix a state
\mu\in S(C(\mathbb{G}))
and
\epsilon>0
. By

[
11
, Proposition 4.7]
, there exists a finite convex combination

\nu
of vector states, where the vectors are taken from
\mathcal{K}
,
such that

|\mu(a)-\nu(a)|\;\leq\;\epsilon\,L_{\ell}^{k}(a),\qquad\forall a\in C(\mathbb{G}).

We now construct the required support condition for
\nu
. Let

a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})
be the length-one elements
in
\mathcal{K}
that determine the vector states comprising
\nu
. For
each
j=1,\dots,m
, define
F_{a_{j}}
as in
[
11
, Proposition 2.2]
.
Set

F\;:=\;\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}F_{a_{j}}.

By
[
11
, Proposition 2.2]
, the set
F
consists of finitely many
irreducible representations of
\mathbb{G}
, and the vector states
corresponding to
a_{j}
are supported on the finite-dimensional subspaces

P_{F}\mathcal{H}
, where
P_{F}
denotes the projection onto the isotypic
component of
F
. Consequently, the state
\nu
is supported on

P_{F}\mathcal{H}
, which implies

\nu(b)=0\quad\text{for all }b\in\bigl(P_{F}C(\mathbb{G})P_{F}\bigr)^{c}.

Since
F
is finite, we have
P_{F}\leq P_{\Lambda}
for some
\Lambda
.
Consequently,
P_{F}C(\mathbb{G})P_{F}\subseteq P_{\Lambda}C(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda}
,
which yields the desired property.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact quantum group equipped with a proper length
function. If
\bigl(C(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}^{k}\bigr)
is a compact quantum
metric space, then there exists
\Lambda
such that we have quantum
Gromov–Hausdorff convergence

\operatorname{dist}_{q}\bigl((C(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}),\,(P_{\Lambda}C(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda},L_{\ell,\Lambda})\bigr)\to 0.

Proof.

Let
I^{\mathbb{G}}
be the coidentity of
\mathbb{G}
, viewed as an element
of
S(C(\mathbb{G}))
. By Lemma
4.6
, there exists a
sequence
\chi_{\Lambda}
converging to
I^{\mathbb{G}}
in the weak
∗

topology on
S(C(\mathbb{G}))
.

Define an operator
P_{\chi_{\Lambda}}
on
P_{\Lambda}C(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda}
by

P_{\chi_{\Lambda}}(u)=\chi_{\Lambda}*u=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\chi_{\Lambda})\Delta(u),\qquad u\in C(\mathbb{G}).

The range of
P_{\chi_{\Lambda}}
is contained in
P_{\Lambda}C(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda}
,
and
L_{\ell,\Lambda}^{k}(P_{\chi_{\Lambda}}(u))\leq L_{\ell}^{k}(u)
follows directly
from the left‑invariance property and the definition of
P_{\chi_{\Lambda}}(u)
.

Now for every self‑adjoint
u\in C(\mathbb{G})
(i.e.
u=u^{*}
) we have

\displaystyle\|u-P_{\chi_{\Lambda}}(u)\|

\displaystyle=\|I^{\mathbb{G}}*(u-P_{\chi_{\Lambda}}(u))\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi\in S(\mathbb{G})}\bigl|\phi\bigl(I^{\mathbb{G}}*(u-\chi_{\Lambda}*u)\bigr)\bigr|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi\in S(\mathbb{G})}\bigl|I^{\mathbb{G}}(u*\phi)-\chi_{\Lambda}(u*\phi)\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\phi\in S(\mathbb{G})}\varepsilon\,L_{\ell}^{k}(u*\phi)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\,L_{\ell}^{k}(u),

where the last inequality uses the left‑invariance of
L_{\ell}^{k}
.

Applying Proposition 8.5 of
[
9
]
immediately yields that for every

\varepsilon>0
,

\operatorname{dist}_{q}\bigl((C(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}),\,(P_{\Lambda}C(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda},L_{\ell,\Lambda})\bigr)\to 0\quad\text{for all }\Lambda\geq N.

Thus the required convergence holds.
∎

5. Gromov–Hausdorff Convergence of Spectral Truncation for
Coamenable Compact Quantum Group of Kac Type and Discrete Quantum Groups

In this section, we discuss two examples: coamenable compact quantum groups
of Kac type and discrete quantum groups.

For coamenable compact quantum groups of Kac type, it follows from
[
16
]

that
(C(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell})
is a compact quantum metric space if and only
if
(F(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell})
is a compact quantum metric space. Later in this
section, we will show that
(C(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}^{k})
is a compact quantum
metric space if and only if
(F(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}^{k})
is a compact quantum
metric space. And, by the results in Theorem
5.3
,
if
\mathbb{G}
has polynomial growth, then
(C(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}^{k})
is a
compact quantum metric space.

On the other hand, for discrete quantum groups, it is known from
[
23
]

that a discrete quantum group of rapid decay can also be equipped with a
compact quantum metric space structure.

We first recall the definition of the algebra of central functions,

C_{z}(\mathbb{G}):=\{a\in C(\mathbb{G})\mid\sigma\Delta(a)=\Delta(a)\},

(5.1)

where
\sigma
denotes the flip automorphism on
C(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\mathbb{G})
.
This algebra characterizes precisely the conditions under which

\bigl(C(\mathbb{G}),\,L_{\ell}\bigr)
, where
L_{\ell}
denotes the Lip-seminorm
induced by the length function
\ell
, forms a
compact quantum metric space

in the sense of Rieffel.

Indeed, by
[
16
, Theorem A]
, if
\mathbb{G}
is a compact, coamenable
quantum group of Kac type and
\ell:\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})\to[0,\infty)

is a proper length function, then
(C(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell})
is a compact quantum
metric space if and only if
(C_{z}(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell})
is.

Let
\pi
be the GNS representation of
C(\mathbb{G})
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
.
By construction, the unbounded operator
D_{\ell}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
(defined
in Section 3.1) leaves the subspace
\pi(\operatorname{Pol}_{z}(\mathbb{G}))

invariant. Its restriction to this subspace is essentially self‑adjoint; we
denote its self‑adjoint closure (still written as
D_{\ell}^{z}
) by the same symbol.
Consequently
(\operatorname{Pol}_{z}(\mathbb{G}),L^{2}_{z}(\mathbb{G}),D_{\ell}^{z})

becomes a spectral triple, where

L^{2}_{z}(\mathbb{G}):=\overline{\pi(\operatorname{Pol}_{z}(\mathbb{G}))}
.

Definition 5.1
.

A state
\varphi\in S(C(\mathbb{G}))
is called
central
if

(\varphi\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\varphi)\Delta
.

Remark 5.2
.

In
[
16
]
, Austad and Kyed defined a normal conditional expectation

E:\mathbb{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))\to\mathbb{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
given by

E(a)=(h\otimes\mathrm{id})\partial(a)
, where

\partial(\cdot):=Z^{*}(1\otimes\cdot)Z
. And they prove that a state

\varphi\in S(C(\mathbb{G}))
is central if and only if

\varphi=\varphi\circ E
and there exists a sequence of central states

\{\chi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to
\epsilon
in the weak
∗

topology on
S(C(\mathbb{G}))
.

Theorem 5.3
.

If
\mathbb{G}
is a compact, coamenable quantum group of Kac type and

\ell:\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})\to[0,\infty)
is a proper length
function, then
(C(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}^{k})
is a compact quantum metric space
if and only if
(C_{z}(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}^{k})
is.

Proof.

Similarly to
W
, the operator
\mathrm{id}\otimes D_{\ell}
commutes with

\Sigma V\Sigma
,
\Sigma V^{*}\Sigma
,
Z
and
Z^{*}
, as operators on the
dense subspace

\pi(\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G}))\odot\pi(\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G}))\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{G})\hat{\otimes}L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
.

For
a,x,y\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})
, consider the relation between

L_{\ell}^{k}(E(a))
and
L_{\ell}^{k}(a)
:

\displaystyle\langle\pi(x),\delta^{k}(E(a))\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\pi(x),[D_{\ell},[D_{\ell},\dots,[D_{\ell},E(a)]\dots]]\pi(y)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-l}\langle\pi(x),D_{\ell}^{l}\bigl((h\otimes\mathrm{id})\partial(a)\bigr)D_{\ell}^{k-l}\pi(y)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-l}\langle\pi(1)\otimes\pi(x),\,(h\otimes\mathrm{id})Z^{*}(1\otimes D_{\ell}^{l}(a)D_{\ell}^{k-l})Z\,(\pi(1)\otimes\pi(y))\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\pi(x),\,(h\otimes\mathrm{id})[D_{\ell},[D_{\ell},\dots,[D_{\ell},E(a)]\dots]]\,\pi(y)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\pi(x),E(\delta^{k}(a))\pi(y)\rangle.

Then
\delta^{k}(E(a))=E(\delta^{k}(a))
and by
[
16
, Proposition 4.2]
we
know the map defines a normal conditional expectation, hence

L_{\ell}^{k}(E(a))=\|\delta^{k}(E(a))\|=\|E(\delta^{k}(a))\|\leq L_{\ell}^{k}(a)
.

Since for any two central states
\varphi
and
\psi
on
C(\mathbb{G})
,

\varphi=\varphi\circ E
and
\psi=\psi\circ E
. When
\mathbb{G}
is
of Kac type, by
[
16
, Proposition 4.2]
we know
E
maps

\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})
to
\operatorname{Pol}_{z}(\mathbb{G})
. Then

\displaystyle d_{\ell}(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle:=\sup\bigl\{|\varphi(a)-\psi(a)|\;;\;a\in\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G}),\;L_{\ell}^{k}(a)\leq 1\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\sup\bigl\{|\varphi(a)-\psi(a)|\;;\;a\in\operatorname{Pol}_{z}(\mathbb{G}),\;L_{\ell}^{k}(a)\leq 1\bigr\}

\displaystyle=:d_{\ell}^{z}\bigl(\varphi|_{C_{z}(\mathbb{G})},\;\psi|_{C_{z}(\mathbb{G})}\bigr).

For any
\chi\in S(C(\mathbb{G}))
and for any
a\in C(\mathbb{G})
, we denote
by
\alpha_{\chi}:C(\mathbb{G})\to C(\mathbb{G})
the ucp map defined by

\alpha_{\chi}(a)=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\chi)\Delta(a)
.

\displaystyle\|a-\alpha_{\chi}(a)\|

\displaystyle=\sup\{\,|\varphi(a-\alpha_{\chi}(a))|\mid\|\varphi\|\leq 1\,\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\,|\varphi(\mathrm{id}\otimes(\epsilon-\chi))\Delta a|\mid\|\varphi\|\leq 1\,\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\{\,d_{\ell}(\epsilon,\chi)\cdot L_{\ell}^{k}((\varphi\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta a)\mid\|\varphi\|\leq 1\,\}

\displaystyle\leq d_{\ell}(\epsilon,\chi)\cdot L_{\ell}^{k}(a).

By Remark 5.2 (from the paper), there exists a sequence of central states

\chi_{n}
converging to
\epsilon
, hence we obtain:

\displaystyle\|a-\alpha_{\chi_{n}}(a)\|

\displaystyle=\|\alpha_{\epsilon}(a)-\alpha_{\chi_{n}}(a)\|

\displaystyle\leq d_{\ell}(\epsilon,\chi_{n})\cdot L_{\ell}^{k}(a)=d_{\ell}\bigl(\epsilon|_{C_{z}(\mathbb{G})},\chi_{n}|_{C_{z}(\mathbb{G})}\bigr)\cdot L_{\ell}^{k}(a).

Since each
\alpha_{\chi_{n}}
is ucp with finite-dimensional image and

\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{\ell}^{z}\bigl(\epsilon|_{C_{z}(\mathbb{G})},\chi_{n}|_{C_{z}(\mathbb{G})}\bigr)=0
,
by
[
27
, Theorem 3.1]
the proof is complete.
∎

In
[
26
]
, Thomas Timmerman introduced the Schur orthogonality relations
between the fusion algebra and the central function algebra. Furthermore, in

[
16
]
, Austad and Kyed established several lemmas regarding these relations.
Summarizing these results, we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 5.4
.

If
\mathbb{G}
is a compact quantum group, then the spectral triples

\bigl(F(\mathbb{G}),\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})),\tilde{D}_{\ell}\bigr)

and

\bigl(\operatorname{Pol}_{z}(\mathbb{G}),L^{2}_{z}(\mathbb{G}),D_{\ell}^{z}\bigr)

are unitarily equivalent via the unitary
\tilde{\chi}
.

Proof.

We construct the unitary
\tilde{\chi}
explicitly and then verify that it
intertwines the representations and the Dirac operators.

Step 1: Definition and unitary extension of
\tilde{\chi}
.

Define a map
\chi:c_{c}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))\to L^{2}_{z}(\mathbb{G})

on the basis elements by

\chi(\alpha)=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\alpha}}\pi(u_{ii}^{\alpha}),\qquad\alpha\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}),

where
\pi:\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
is
the canonical inclusion. By construction, the image of
\chi
is dense in

L^{2}_{z}(\mathbb{G})
.

The Schur orthogonality relations
[
15
, Proposition 5.3.8 (iii)]
yield

\langle\chi(\alpha),\chi(\beta)\rangle_{L^{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\alpha}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\beta}}h\bigl((u_{ii}^{\alpha})^{*}u_{jj}^{\beta}\bigr)=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\alpha}}\frac{1}{d_{\alpha}}=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}.

Hence
\chi
sends the orthonormal basis
\{\delta_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})}

of
\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))
onto an orthonormal basis of

L^{2}_{z}(\mathbb{G})
, and therefore extends uniquely to a unitary operator

\tilde{\chi}:\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))\longrightarrow L^{2}_{z}(\mathbb{G}).

Step 2: Intertwining of the representations.

Recall that
F(\mathbb{G})
acts on
\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))
via
the left regular representation
\pi_{0}
of the fusion algebra, and that
\lambda

denotes the left multiplication action of
\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})
on

L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
. For any
\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
we have

\tilde{\chi}\pi_{0}(\beta)(\delta_{\alpha})=\tilde{\chi}(\beta\cdot\alpha)=\chi(\beta\cdot\alpha)=\lambda\bigl(\chi(\beta)\bigr)\chi(\alpha)=\lambda\bigl(\chi(\beta)\bigr)\tilde{\chi}(\delta_{\alpha}),

where we used that
\chi
is a unital
*
-isomorphism from
F(\mathbb{G})
onto

\operatorname{Pol}_{z}(\mathbb{G})
and that
\lambda
is a faithful representation.
By linearity this identity extends to all finitely supported functions, so for
every
x\in F(\mathbb{G})
we obtain

\pi_{0}(x)=\tilde{\chi}^{*}\lambda(\chi(x))\tilde{\chi}

as operators on the dense subspace
c_{c}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))
.
Consequently
\pi_{0}(x)
extends to a bounded operator on

\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G}))
, and this extension satisfies

\pi(x)=\tilde{\chi}^{*}\,\lambda(\chi(x))\,\tilde{\chi}
.

Step 3: Intertwining of the Dirac operators.

For any
\alpha\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
we compute

\tilde{\chi}\tilde{D}_{\ell}(\delta_{\alpha})=\ell(\alpha)\,\tilde{\chi}(\delta_{\alpha})=\ell(\alpha)\,\chi(\alpha)=D_{\ell}^{z}\,\tilde{\chi}(\delta_{\alpha}),

where
\ell(\alpha)
denotes the length of
\alpha
(i.e., the eigenvalue of the
Dirac operator on the basis vector
\delta_{\alpha}
). Thus
\tilde{\chi}
maps the
core
\operatorname{span}\{\delta_{\alpha}\}
into
\operatorname{Dom}(D_{\ell}^{z})

and satisfies
\tilde{\chi}\tilde{D}_{\ell}=D_{\ell}^{z}\tilde{\chi}
on this core.
It follows immediately that
\tilde{\chi}(\operatorname{Dom}(\tilde{D}_{\ell}))\subset\operatorname{Dom}(D_{\ell}^{z})
and the equality holds on the whole domain.
Hence
\tilde{\chi}
is a unitary equivalence between the two spectral triples.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact quantum group equipped with a proper length
function. If
\bigl(\operatorname{Pol}_{z}(\mathbb{G}),L_{z\ell}^{k}\bigr)

is a compact quantum metric space and the spectral truncations of

\bigl(\operatorname{Pol}_{z}(\mathbb{G}),L_{z\ell}^{k}\bigr)
exhibit
Gromov–Hausdorff convergence, then there exists
\Lambda\geq 0
such
that the quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance

\operatorname{dist}_{q}\bigl(F(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}^{k}),\,(P_{\Lambda}F(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda},L_{\ell\Lambda}^{K})\bigr)\to 0.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.4
, we know

\bigl(\operatorname{Pol}_{z}(\mathbb{G}),L_{z\ell}^{k}\bigr)
is a compact
quantum metric space if and only if
(F(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}^{k})
is a compact
quantum metric space. Let

\tilde{\chi}_{\Lambda}^{*}\colon P_{\Lambda}(L^{2}_{z}(\mathbb{G}))\to P_{\Lambda}(\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})))
be the unitary operator
obtained by restricting
\tilde{\chi}^{*}
to the truncated subspaces; it is
therefore an isometry (in fact, unitary).

By hypothesis, the spectral truncations of

\bigl(\operatorname{Pol}_{z}(\mathbb{G}),L_{z\ell}\bigr)
converge in quantum
Gromov–Hausdorff distance.

From Proposition
5.4
,
\tilde{\chi}
is a unitary equivalence
between the above spectral triples:

\tilde{\chi}^{*}\bigl(\operatorname{Pol}_{z}(\mathbb{G}),L^{2}_{z}(\mathbb{G}),D_{\ell}^{z}\bigr)\tilde{\chi}=\bigl(F(\mathbb{G}),\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})),\tilde{D}_{\ell}\bigr).

Similarly,
\tilde{\chi}_{\Lambda}
provides a unitary equivalence between the
truncated versions:

\tilde{\chi}_{\Lambda}^{*}\bigl(P_{\Lambda}\operatorname{Pol}_{z}(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda},\,P_{\Lambda}L^{2}_{z}(\mathbb{G}),\,D_{z,\ell,\Lambda}\bigr)\tilde{\chi}_{\Lambda}=\bigl(P_{\Lambda}F(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda},\,P_{\Lambda}\ell^{2}(\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})),\,\tilde{D}_{\ell,\Lambda}\bigr).

Since unitary equivalence preserves the quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance,
we obtain

\operatorname{dist}_{q}\bigl(F(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}\bigr),\,(P_{\Lambda}F(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda},L_{\ell\Lambda})\bigr)\to 0.

∎

Hence we can give some examples whose spectral truncations exhibit
Gromov–Hausdorff convergence.

Example 5.6
.

By Corollary C in
[
16
]
, we know that when equipped with the length
function
\ell
arising from the standard fundamental corepresentation,
the pairs

(C(SU(2)),L_{\ell}),\quad(C(O_{2}^{+}),L_{\ell}),\quad(C(SO(3)),L_{\ell})\quad\text{and}\quad(C(S_{4}^{+}),L_{\ell})

are compact quantum metric spaces.
By Theorem
4.7
, we know that the spectral truncations of
these quantum compact metric spaces (and of their central function forms)
converge in the Gromov–Hausdorff sense. Moreover, by
Theorem
5.5
, the same holds for their fusion algebra forms.

Now we consider another important class of examples: discrete quantum groups
with the property of rapid decay. The study of this property for discrete
quantum groups was initiated by Vergnioux
[
28
]
. Building on this work,
Bhowmick, Voigt, and Zacharias
[
20
]
demonstrated that for quantum groups
possessing the rapid decay property, one can naturally construct compact
quantum metric spaces. In what follows, we briefly outline this construction.

Let
G
be a discrete quantum group. We say that
G
has
property RD

with respect to a length function
\ell
on
G
if there exist constants

C,s>0
such that

\|\mathcal{F}(f)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C\|f\|_{2,s}

for all
f\in C_{c}(G)
. We say that
G
has property RD if it has property RD
with respect to some length function.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and define
T=C^{1/(2k)}
. For any

a\in\mathbb{C}[G]
, set

\delta(a)=[D_{\ell},a],

and define

\delta_{T}(a)=[D_{\ell},TaT],\qquad L_{T}^{k}(a)=\|\delta_{T}^{k}(a)\|.

If
\mathbb{G}
is a discrete quantum group of rapid decay, by
[
20
]
we
know that
(C(\mathbb{G}),L_{T}^{k})
is a compact quantum metric space.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a discrete quantum group of Kac type that has strong
polynomial growth. Then for any state
\mu\in S(C(\mathbb{G}))
and any

\epsilon>0
, there exists a state
\nu\in S(C(\mathbb{G}))
satisfying

|\mu(a)-\nu(a)|\;\leq\;\epsilon\,L_{T}^{k}(a),\qquad\forall a\in C(\mathbb{G}),

such that
\nu(b)=0
whenever

b\in\bigl(P_{\Lambda}C(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda}\bigr)^{c}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{G}
has strong polynomial growth,

A_{\Lambda}\subset\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
is a Følner sequence
for
\mathbb{G}
. We define a sequence of functions

\phi_{\Lambda}(\pi)=\frac{\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in A_{\Lambda}}N_{\bar{\alpha},\beta}^{\pi}\,d(\alpha)d(\beta)}{d(\pi)\bigl(\sum_{\xi\in A_{\Lambda}}d(\xi)^{2}\bigr)},

where
N_{\bar{\alpha},\beta}^{\pi}
are the fusion coefficients. By

[
2
, Lemma 5.5]
, we have
\lim_{\Lambda}\phi_{\Lambda}(\pi)=1
for all

\pi\in\operatorname{Irred}(\mathbb{G})
, and the associated multipliers

T_{\phi_{\Lambda}}\colon\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})\to\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{G})

defined by
T_{\phi_{\Lambda}}(u_{i,j}^{\alpha}):=\phi_{\Lambda}(\alpha)u_{i,j}^{\alpha}

extend to unital completely positive (ucp) maps on
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
.
Let
\chi_{\Lambda}:=\epsilon\circ T_{\phi_{\Lambda}}\colon C(\mathbb{G})\to\mathbb{C}
.
Then
\{\chi_{\Lambda}\}_{\Lambda}
is a sequence of states satisfying

\chi_{\Lambda}(u_{i,j}^{\alpha})=\epsilon(\phi_{\Lambda}(\alpha)u_{i,j}^{\alpha})=\phi_{\Lambda}(\alpha)\delta_{i,j}\xrightarrow[\Lambda]{}\delta_{i,j}=\epsilon(u_{i,j}^{\alpha}).

Since the support of
\phi_{\Lambda}
is contained in
A_{\Lambda}
, the states

\chi_{\Lambda}
have the desired properties, which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a discrete quantum group of Kac type, equipped with a
proper length function and having strong polynomial growth. Then there
exists
\Lambda
such that we have quantum Gromov–Hausdorff convergence

\operatorname{dist}_{q}\bigl((C(\mathbb{G}),L_{\ell}),\,(P_{\Lambda}C(\mathbb{G})P_{\Lambda},L_{\ell,\Lambda})\bigr)\to 0.

Proof.

The result follows by repeating the argument in the proof of
Theorem
4.7
.
∎
