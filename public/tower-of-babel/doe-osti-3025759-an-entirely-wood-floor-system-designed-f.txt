Public Executive Summary
This project focused on the development and demonstration of an innovative floor system for
buildings designed to extend the technical capabilities and market advantages of mass timber.
The system was conceived to address the whole building lifecycle—from design and
construction through occupancy and operation to end-of-life and future reuse of materials. A
hypothetical three-story office building in Atlanta, GA, was taken as the case study for
developing the floor system.

Technical viability was assessed and refined through laboratory testing and analytical
evaluation, including structural performance, acoustic performance, fire requirements (code
compliance), and de/reconstruction potential. Environmental performance was evaluated
through a life cycle assessment (LCA) of the case study building. Economic and market viability
were assessed through technoeconomic analysis (TEA) and structured feedback from an
industry advisory panel.

The proposed system provides multiple benefits, including storage of biogenic carbon,
increased structural span lengths, dedicated access for plumbing and other building services,
and construction detailing that facilitates deconstruction, reconstruction, and reuse.
Additionally, by facilitating the use of mass timber products, the knowledge created in the
project can enhance market potential for the domestic timber industry.

Accomplishments and Objectives
The floor system developed in this project opens new possibilities for the use of mass timber
structures in office buildings. The ARPA-E award enabled Clemson University to achieve the
following key objectives:
 • Demonstrate the structural viability of the floor system for spans and loads typical of
 office buildings.
 • Demonstrate that the floor system’s acoustic performance complies with code
 requirements for office and residential buildings.
 • Identify multiple paths for the floor system to comply with building code requirements
 related to fire safety.
 • Disassemble the floor system specimens and reuse the mass timber components in
 subsequent construction.
 • Calculate the potential of the mass timber system to store biogenic carbon.

Tasks and milestones were laid out in Attachment 3, Technical Milestones and Deliverables, at
the beginning of the project. The actual performance against the stated milestones is
summarized in Table 1. Completion dates are based on the associated quarterly report dates.

Project Activities
This project focused on the development and demonstration of an innovative floor system for
buildings designed to extend the technical capabilities and market advantages of mass timber.
The system addresses the whole building lifecycle—from design and construction through
occupancy and operation to end-of-life and future reuse of materials.
Technical viability was assessed and refined through laboratory testing and analytical
evaluation, including structural performance, acoustic performance, fire requirements (code

compliance), and de/reconstruction potential. Environmental performance was evaluated
through a life cycle assessment (LCA) of the case study building. Economic and market viability
were assessed through technoeconomic analysis (TEA) and structured feedback from an
industry advisory panel. The proposed system provides multiple benefits, including storage of
biogenic carbon, increased structural span lengths, dedicated access for plumbing and other
building services, and construction detailing that facilitates deconstruction, reconstruction, and
reuse.

The project closely followed the SOPO, with three minor changes. First, off-the-shelf GLT-GLT
connections were selected instead of bespoke designs. The presence of existing technology
allowed the team to refocus efforts to higher-priority tasks. Second, the acoustic testing was
conducted and evaluated under alternative test standards (NNIC and NSIR). The alternative
standards are accepted by building codes. The change allowed the team to test multiple
configurations of the floor system and finish. Third, work extended beyond 9/12/25. This
allowed the team to consider additional variables in the system-level vibration tests. A no-cost
extension request was filed but held up by a federal shutdown. The extension did not result in
additional cost but did result in additional benefits.
