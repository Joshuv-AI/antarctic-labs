Weakly nonlinear analysis of Hopf bifurcations in the elastohydrodynamics of Cosserat rods
Mohamed Warda

Abstract
We study the weakly nonlinear saturation of the flutter instability of a planar Cosserat rod in a viscous fluid driven by a terminal follower force. This instability, established in our preceding work as a Hopf bifurcation of a non-self-adjoint linear operator, produces stable limit-cycle oscillations in the fully nonlinear overdamped dynamics. Here we derive an analytical description of the emergence of this limit cycle near threshold. Working close to the critical follower force, we perform a multiple-scale expansion about the compressed straight base state and systematically remove secular growth at higher orders. Solvability at cubic order, enforced using the adjoint eigenmode of the non-Hermitian operator, yields a Stuart-Landau amplitude equation for the critical oscillatory mode. The Landau coefficients are expressed as explicit inner products involving the critical eigenmode, its adjoint, and quadratic corrections. The resulting reduced theory predicts a supercritical Hopf bifurcation with a steady-state tip oscillation amplitude scaling as the square root of the distance from threshold. These predictions rationalize the near-threshold scaling observed in nonlinear simulations and provide an analytical normal form for the onset of self-sustained beating in pressure-driven soft robotic arms at low Reynolds number.

Introduction

Soft slender structures interacting with viscous fluids arise in a broad range of biological and engineering settings, including active cytoskeletal filaments, synthetic micro-swimmers, underwater soft robotic manipulators, and minimally invasive continuum devices

Nevertheless, nonconservative forcing can continuously inject energy into the structure and generate self-excited oscillations, enabling autonomous beating, propulsion, pumping, and mixing

A canonical mechanism for such behavior is the follower-force instability

Because the load direction depends on the instantaneous configuration of the rod, the resulting linearized dynamics is non-self-adjoint. Unlike conservative Euler buckling, such systems can lose stability through a Hopf bifurcation even in the overdamped limit, as demonstrated in active filament and follower-force models
(

;

, follower-force models have re-emerged in soft matter and active filament contexts, where they provide effective descriptions of tip-directed motor forces or localized active stresses

;

;

;

;

.

While linear stability theory determines the threshold of oscillatory instability, it does not describe the finite-amplitude state selected beyond onset. Near the threshold, weakly nonlinear analysis provides the natural asymptotic framework for deriving reduced amplitude equations that capture the slow modulation and saturation of unstable modes. Such methods provide a universal framework for describing pattern-forming and oscillatory instabilities across fluid mechanics, active matter, and elasticity
(

;

;

;
22
;

)
. In particular, Stuart-Landau equations determine whether a Hopf bifurcation is supercritical or subcritical and predict amplitude scaling laws and nonlinear frequency shifts, as described in the classical theory of Hopf bifurcations and amplitude equations
(

These ideas have recently been applied to the follower-force model for overdamped filaments. Schnitzer
(
21
)
analyzed the three-dimensional follower-force model for inextensible, unshearable filaments near the onset of instability, showing that the transition is governed by a symmetry-induced double Hopf bifurcation. The resulting reduced amplitude equations generalize the Stuart-Landau normal form and explain the emergence and selection of non-planar whirling states from planar modes. Related nonlinear studies of active or axially driven filaments have also demonstrated spontaneous oscillation, morphological transitions, and self-organized beating states

(

;

;

;
25
)
.

The present work addresses a distinct but closely related problem motivated by soft robotics. In our preceding paper
(
25
)
, we introduced a geometrically exact planar Cosserat rod model of a pressure-driven soft robotic arm immersed in a viscous fluid, including stretch and shear degrees of freedom as well as anisotropic local drag. Linearization about the compressed straight base state yielded a non-Hermitian differential operator whose spectrum loses stability through a Hopf bifurcation at a critical value of the follower force. Fully nonlinear simulations further showed the emergence of stable limit cycle oscillations
(
25
)
.

Here, we derive the weakly nonlinear dynamics governing this transition. Working near the onset of instability, we employ the method of multiple scales
(
13
)
about the compressed base state and eliminate resonant or secular growth order by order. This procedure yields a Stuart-Landau amplitude equation for the critical oscillatory mode, with Landau coefficients that we determine explicitly. The incorporation of Cosserat degrees of freedom into the model introduces longitudinal degrees of freedom and additional quadratic couplings absent in the Kirchhoff-filament formulation, modifying the hierarchy of near threshold corrections and the explicit form of the Landau coefficients. The reduced theory predicts a supercritical Hopf bifurcation with a saturated tip amplitude scaling as the square root of the distance from the instability threshold. These predictions are shown to agree quantitatively with direct simulations of the fully nonlinear Cosserat rod dynamics in
(
25
)
.

Beyond the present application, the analysis provides a general framework for reducing nonconservative elastohydrodynamic instabilities in geometrically exact rod models to low-dimensional normal forms, with potential relevance to the yields of soft robotic control, active filaments, and self-oscillating structures at low Reynolds number.

IV
Weakly Nonlinear Analysis

To determine the nonlinear saturation of the flutter instability, we consider values of the follower force close to the Hopf threshold and perform a weakly nonlinear analysis using the method of multiple scales
(
13
)
. We write the control parameter as

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}=\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{c}+\epsilon^{2}\chi

(20)

where
0<\epsilon\ll 1
is a small perturbation parameter and
\chi=\mathcal{O}(1)
measures the signed distance from threshold.

The distinguished scaling above reflects the standard balance for a Hopf bifurcation: at the critical follower force
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}=\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{c}
, the linearized dynamics admits neutrally stable oscillations at frequency
\omega_{c}
, whose leading-order contribution may be written as a critical eigenmode with complex amplitude
\tilde{A}
, as in (
18
). In the full nonlinear problem, cubic terms generically generate resonant forcing at the same frequency, which produces secular corrections growing in time like
t|\tilde{A}|^{3}
. Such corrections become comparable to the leading-order oscillation over long times
t=\mathcal{O}(|\tilde{A}|^{-2})
, indicating that nonlinear effects modulate the amplitude on a slow time scale. For
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
near
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{c}
, the real part of the leading eigenvalue
\lambda
satisfies
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\sim\tilde{\mathcal{F}}-\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{c}
, so that linear growth or decay occurs over the long time scale
\mathcal{O}(|\tilde{\mathcal{F}}-\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{c}|^{-1})
. Balancing this linear time scale with the nonlinear modulation time scale yields the distinguished scaling
|\tilde{A}|=\mathcal{O}(|\tilde{\mathcal{F}}-\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{c}|^{1/2})
, which underlies the multiple-scale expansion and the emergence of a Stuart-Landau amplitude equation.

Accordingly, we introduce a fast time
\tau=t
, resolving oscillations at the Hopf frequency
\omega_{c}
, together with a slow modulation time
T=\epsilon^{2}t
. All rod variables are expanded in powers of
\epsilon
about the compressed straight base state corresponding to
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}=\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{c}
. Substitution into the full nonlinear Cosserat rod equations yields a hierarchy of boundary value problems at successive orders in
\epsilon
. Schematically, the hierarchy of boundary value problems at
n
th order takes the form

\partial_{\tau}\xi^{(n)}=\mathcal{L}\xi^{(n)}+\Gamma^{-1}f^{(n)}

(21)

along with the appropriate boundary conditions, where
\xi^{(n)}
is a vector of
n
th order corrections of the fields
(x,y,\theta)
associated with the multiple-scale expansion and
f^{(n)}
is an
n
th order generalized forcing term composed of lower order contributions. The complete expansions and order-by-order equations and boundary conditions are given in Appendix
A
.

At leading order, one recovers the critical oscillatory eigenmode of the linearized problem. At second order, quadratic interactions generate non-resonant corrections consisting of a mean deformation and a second-harmonic response, encoded in
f^{(2)}
. In particular, in the perturbative expansions

\displaystyle x(u,\tau,T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^{(n)}(u,\tau,T)\epsilon^{n},

(22)

\displaystyle F_{1}(u,\tau,T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{1}^{(n)}(u,\tau,T)\epsilon^{n},

we find that the second-order contributions
x^{(2)}
and
F_{1}^{(2)}
take the form

\displaystyle x^{(2)}

\displaystyle=A^{2}X_{2}e^{2i\omega_{c}\tau}+|A|^{2}X_{0}-\frac{\chi}{2\kappa}u+\mathrm{c.c.},

(23)

\displaystyle F_{1}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=A^{2}P_{2}e^{2i\omega_{c}\tau}+|A|^{2}P_{0}-\chi+\mathrm{c.c.},

where
X_{0}
,
X_{2}
,
P_{0}
and
P_{2}
are functions of
u
, given explicitly in Appendix
B
. These corrections do not alter the leading-order oscillation directly, but they enter the cubic forcing at the next order.

At third order, resonant terms proportional to the critical frequency appear in
f^{(3)}
as well as in the boundary conditions of the problem. The validity of the perturbative expansion necessitates the removal of these resonant terms. This yields a solvability condition
(
14
)
, which determines the slow evolution of the modal amplitude and provides the Stuart-Landau normal form of the instability. Thus, the weakly nonlinear problem reduces to determining the amplitude dynamics of the critical Hopf mode, which is presented in the next section.

V
The Stuart–Landau Equation

At leading order, the rod executes neutral oscillations at the Hopf frequency
\omega_{c}
with spatial structure given by the critical eigenmode
(0,Y(u),\Theta(u))
. We therefore write the first order solution as

\displaystyle x^{(1)}(u,\tau,T)

\displaystyle=0,

(24)

\displaystyle y^{(1)}(u,\tau,T)

\displaystyle=A(T)Y(u)e^{i\omega_{c}\tau}+\mathrm{c.c.},

\displaystyle\theta^{(1)}(u,\tau,T)

\displaystyle=A(T)\Theta(u)e^{i\omega_{c}\tau}+\mathrm{c.c.},

where
A(T)
is a complex amplitude varying only on the slow time scale.

The follower-force boundary condition renders the linearized operator non-self-adjoint. Consequently, the cubic-order solvability condition must be imposed using the adjoint eigenmode under the drag-weighted inner product (
17
). Carrying out this projection (Appendix
B
) removes resonant forcing at frequency
\omega_{c}
and yields the amplitude equation

\frac{\mathrm{dA}}{\mathrm{d}T}=\alpha|A|^{2}A+\beta\chi A.

(25)

This is the Stuart-Landau normal form governing the onset of beating in the planar Cosserat rod. The complex coefficients
\alpha
and
\beta
depend on the direct and adjoint critical eigenfunctions together with the quadratic corrections generated at second order; explicit expressions are derived and presented in Appendix
B
.

VI
Comparisons with Numerical Simulations

In this section we compare the weakly nonlinear predictions of the
Stuart-Landau equation with direct numerical simulations of the full
nonlinear Cosserat-rod dynamics. We focus on the shear-stretch isotropic case
\tilde{k}_{1}=\tilde{k}_{2}=\kappa
and present results for
\kappa=10^{4}
. Near the Hopf threshold, the slow-time
amplitude
A(T)
satisfies

\frac{\mathrm{dA}}{\mathrm{d}T}=\alpha|A|^{2}A+\beta\chi A,

(26)

where
\chi
is the detuning from threshold. In the asymptotic scaling of
Sec.
A
we have

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}=\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{c}+\epsilon^{2}\chi
with

\chi=O(1)
. For the purpose of comparison with simulations, it is
convenient to identify

\epsilon^{2}=\tilde{\mathcal{F}}-\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{c},\qquad\chi=1,

(27)

so that the small parameter
\epsilon
directly measures the distance
from threshold.
Seeking a uniform-amplitude oscillation on the slow time scale, we set

A(T)=\rho\,e^{i\sigma T},

(28)

where
\rho
is a complex constant and
\sigma
is a real nonlinear
frequency correction on the slow time scale. Writing

\rho=|\rho|e^{i\phi},

(29)

and substituting (
28
) into (
26
) gives

\alpha|\rho|^{2}+\beta\chi-i\sigma=0.

(30)

Separating real and imaginary parts yields the predicted steady-state
amplitude

|\rho|=\sqrt{-\frac{\mathrm{Re}\,\beta}{\mathrm{Re}\,\alpha}\,\chi},

(31)

which exists for
\chi>0
when
\mathrm{Re}(\alpha)<0
and

\mathrm{Re}(\beta)>0
, corresponding to a supercritical Hopf
bifurcation. The corresponding slow-time frequency correction is

\sigma=-\mathrm{Im}\,\alpha\,\frac{\mathrm{Re}\,\beta}{\mathrm{Re}\,\alpha}\,\chi+\mathrm{Im}\,\beta\,\chi.

(32)

Using the leading-order eigenmode normalization
Y(1)=1
, the transverse
tip displacement is, to leading order in
\epsilon
,

y(1,t)\approx 2\epsilon|\rho|\cos\!\left(\omega_{c}t+\sigma\epsilon^{2}t+\phi\right).

(33)

With the identification
\epsilon^{2}=\tilde{\mathcal{F}}-\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{c}
,
this becomes

y(1,t)\approx 2|\rho|\,\sqrt{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}-\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{c}}\,\cos\!\left[\left(\omega_{c}+(\tilde{\mathcal{F}}-\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{c})\sigma\right)t+\phi\right].

(34)

Equations (
31
)–(
34
) provide explicit
predictions for the scaling of the saturated oscillation amplitude and
the shift in oscillation frequency as functions of the distance from
threshold.

VII
Discussion and Conclusion

In this work we derived the weakly nonlinear dynamics governing the onset and saturation of self-sustained oscillations in a planar pressure-driven soft robotic arm modeled as an overdamped Cosserat rod in a viscous fluid. Building on the linear stability analysis established in our preceding paper, we considered follower forces near the critical value at which a complex-conjugate eigenvalue pair crosses the imaginary axis. The resulting Hopf bifurcation produces a transverse flutter instability and persistent beating in the fully nonlinear dynamics.

To determine the nonlinear saturation mechanism, we carried out a multiple-scale expansion about the compressed straight base state and introduced a slow time scale associated with the near-threshold growth rate. At leading order the dynamics is governed by the critical eigenmode of a non-self-adjoint linear operator arising from the follower-force boundary condition. At second order, quadratic nonlinearities generate longitudinal corrections at zero and twice the critical frequency, which feed into the third-order transverse dynamics. At cubic order, resonant forcing at the critical frequency appears. Because the linear operator is non-Hermitian, elimination of secular growth requires projection onto the adjoint eigenmode. Enforcing the resulting Fredholm solvability condition yields a Stuart-Landau equation governing the slow evolution of the complex amplitude of the critical mode.

The resulting amplitude equation provides a reduced normal form description of the flutter instability in the planar Cosserat-rod setting. It predicts a supercritical Hopf bifurcation in the parameter regime of interest, a steady-state oscillation amplitude proportional to the square root of the distance from threshold, and a frequency correction linear in the distance from threshold. These predictions rationalize the near-threshold scaling observed in nonlinear simulations of the full Cosserat rod equations. Beyond its asymptotic value, the reduced model provides a compact description suitable for parameter sweeps and control-oriented modeling of pressure-driven soft robotic arms in viscous environments.

It is instructive to place these results in the context of recent weakly nonlinear analyses of the classical follower-force filament problem. In particular, Schnitzer derived a higher-dimensional amplitude equation associated with the symmetry-induced double-Hopf bifurcation of an inextensible, unshearable filament in three dimensions, providing a mechanism for the emergence and selection of whirling states near onset
(
21
)
. The present work complements that analysis by treating a robotics-motivated Cosserat-rod model with extensibility and shear, restricted to planar motion. In this setting the Hopf bifurcation is non-degenerate and the leading-order reduced dynamics is captured by a Stuart-Landau normal form, while the additional Cosserat degrees of freedom enter through the structure of the quadratic corrections and the resulting Landau coefficients.

Several extensions are natural. The present analysis assumed shear-stretch isotropy in the main text; the anisotropic case may be treated similarly and is summarised in Appendix
C
. More broadly, the same methodology can be extended to three-dimensional Cosserat rods including twist, where symmetry may again lead to degenerate Hopf structure and higher-dimensional amplitude equations analogous to those obtained for Kirchhoff filaments. Incorporating nonlocal hydrodynamic interactions beyond local Stokes drag is another natural direction. Such generalizations would further strengthen the connection between geometrically exact rod theory, nonconservative stability, and reduced-order descriptions of soft robotic actuation at low Reynolds number.

\mathrm{d}A/\mathrm{d}T
,
(ii) detuning from threshold through
\chi
, and
(iii) cubic interactions among the lower-order fields.

Because the Hopf bifurcation occurs at the critical eigenvalue

i\omega_{c}
, only the component of the forcing proportional to

e^{i\omega_{c}\tau}
can resonate with the neutral mode and generate
secular growth. We therefore decompose the cubic forcing as

\Gamma^{-1}f^{(3)}=\mathcal{G}\,e^{i\omega_{c}\tau}+\mathrm{c.c.}+\mathrm{n.s.t.}.

(80)

A key feature of the follower-force problem is that the linear operator

\mathcal{L}
is non-self-adjoint. As a result, the solvability
condition at
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})
must be imposed using the adjoint
eigenmode rather than the primal eigenmode. Moreover, because the
overdamped Cosserat-rod dynamics takes the weighted form

\Gamma\,\partial_{\tau}\xi=\mathcal{M}\xi,

(81)

the natural solvability condition involves the
\Gamma
–weighted inner
product

(\Psi,\Phi)_{\Gamma}=\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}u\,\Psi^{\dagger}(u)\Gamma\Phi(u).

(82)

We denote by
\xi_{c}=(0,Y,\Theta)
the critical eigenfunction of the
primal problem at the Hopf threshold,

\mathcal{L}\,\xi_{c}=i\omega_{c}\xi_{c},

(83)

satisfying the primal boundary conditions

Y(0)=\Theta(0)=0,\qquad Y^{\prime}(1)-\nu\Theta(1)=\Theta^{\prime}(1)=0.

(84)

We denote by
\Psi=(0,\Psi_{Y},\Psi_{\Theta})
the corresponding adjoint
eigenfunction satisfying

\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}\Psi=-i\omega_{c}\Psi,

(85)

together with the adjoint boundary conditions

\displaystyle\Psi_{Y}(0)=\Psi_{\Theta}(0)=0,

(86)

\displaystyle\tilde{k}_{2}\Psi_{Y}^{\prime}(1)-(\tilde{k}_{2}\nu+\mathcal{F}_{c})\Psi_{\Theta}(1)=\Psi_{\Theta}^{\prime}(1)+\mathcal{F}_{c}\Psi_{Y}(1)=0,

which we have derived in
(
25
)
. We also refer to
(
25
)
for the details of the semi-analytical method for computing the eigenvalues, eigenfunctions, and adjoint eigenfunctions.

We normalize the adjoint mode using the
\Gamma
–weighted inner product
as

\mathcal{N}:=(\Psi,\xi_{c})_{\Gamma}=\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}u\,\left[\tilde{\gamma}_{2}\Psi_{Y}^{*}(u)Y(u)+\tilde{\gamma}_{3}\Psi_{\Theta}^{*}(u)\Theta(u)\right].

(87)

Since the longitudinal component of the critical eigenfunction vanishes,
only the transverse components enter
\mathcal{N}
.

We remark that the appearance of the drag weights
\gamma_{2}
and

\gamma_{3}
in (
87
) differs from the inner
product used by Schnitzer
21
, who considers an
inextensible Kirchhoff filament in resistive-force theory. In the
present extensible/shearable Cosserat formulation, the overdamped
dynamics is naturally expressed in the weighted form
(
81
), and the Fredholm solvability
condition is correspondingly imposed using the
\Gamma
–weighted adjoint
eigenmode.

Substituting the
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
and
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})

solutions into the forcing terms in the
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})

equations yields the resonant forcing vector
\mathcal{G}
in
(
80
). Retaining only the contributions
proportional to
e^{i\omega_{c}\tau}
, we may write

\mathcal{G}=|A|^{2}A\begin{bmatrix}0\\[2.0pt]
i\omega_{c}\left(1-\tilde{\gamma}_{1}\right)\left(2X_{2}\Theta^{*}-Y^{*}\Theta^{2}+2Y|\Theta|^{2}\right)\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{\tilde{\gamma}_{3}}\left[\Theta^{*}\bigl((\Theta^{\prime})^{2}-P_{2}\bigr)+2\Theta\bigl(|\Theta^{\prime}|^{2}-\mathrm{Re}\,P_{0}\bigr)\right]\end{bmatrix}\\
+\chi A\begin{bmatrix}0\\[2.0pt]
\dfrac{i\omega_{c}}{\kappa}\Theta(1)u\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{\tilde{\gamma}_{3}}\bigl[\Theta-\Theta(1)\bigr]\end{bmatrix}-\frac{\mathrm{dA}}{\mathrm{d}T}\begin{bmatrix}0\\
Y\\
\Theta\end{bmatrix}.

(88)

Since the homogeneous problem admits a solution at frequency

e^{i\omega_{c}\tau}
, solvability of the inhomogeneous system requires
that the resonant forcing be orthogonal to the adjoint eigenmode under
the
\Gamma
–weighted inner product. This is the Fredholm solvability
condition,

(\Psi,\mathcal{G})_{\Gamma}=0.

(89)

Substituting (
88
) into (
89
) and
using the normalization (
87
) yields the
Stuart–Landau amplitude equation governing the slow evolution of the
Hopf mode,

\frac{\mathrm{dA}}{\mathrm{d}T}=\alpha|A|^{2}A+\beta\chi A.

(90)

The coefficients
\alpha
and
\beta
are given explicitly by

\alpha=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}u\,i\omega_{c}(1-\tilde{\gamma}_{1})\Psi_{Y}^{*}\left(2X_{2}\Theta^{*}-Y^{*}\Theta^{2}+2Y|\Theta|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}u\,\Psi_{\Theta}^{*}\left[\Theta^{*}\bigl((\Theta^{\prime})^{2}-P_{2}\bigr)+2\Theta\bigl(|\Theta^{\prime}|^{2}-\mathrm{Re}\,P_{0}\bigr)\right],

(91)

and

\beta=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}u\,\left(\frac{i\omega_{c}}{\kappa}\Psi_{Y}^{*}\Theta(1)u+\bigl[\Theta-\Theta(1)\bigr]\Psi_{\Theta}^{*}\right).

(92)

The primal and adjoint eigenfunctions were computed numerically using the semi-analytical procedure described in
25
, after which the integrals defining
\alpha
and
\beta
were evaluated numerically in Mathematica
1
.

Equation (
90
) provides a reduced normal form for the
Hopf bifurcation in the follower-force Cosserat-rod problem. In
particular, the real part of
\beta
determines the linear growth rate
near threshold, while the real part of
\alpha
determines whether the
Hopf bifurcation is supercritical or subcritical. In the parameter
regime studied here we find
\mathrm{Re}(\alpha)<0
, corresponding to a
supercritical Hopf bifurcation and the emergence of a stable limit-cycle
oscillation for
\chi>0
.
