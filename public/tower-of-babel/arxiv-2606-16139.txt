The Computational Complexity of Team Zero-Sum Games
Ioannis Anagnostides, Ioannis Panageas, Tuomas Sandholm, Jingming Yan

Abstract
A celebrated consequence of the minimax theorem is that two-player zero-sum games admit a tractable equilibrium characterization. In many central applications, however, each side comprises multiple independent agents who share a common objective but cannot perfectly coordinate their actions. Such settings can be modeled as team zero-sum games , a natural generalization of both two-player zero-sum games and potential games—the two most well-studied classes of games in algorithmic game theory. In this paper, we settle the complexity of team zero-sum games by establishing that computing Nash equilibria is 𝖯𝖯𝖠𝖣 \mathsf{PPAD} -complete. As a result, despite the global adversarial structure, team zero-sum games are as hard as general-sum games. Our hardness result holds even when i) the precision is inverse polynomial, thereby ruling out a fully polynomial-time approximation scheme (unless 𝖯 = 𝖯𝖯𝖠𝖣 {\mathsf{P}}={\mathsf{PPAD}} ); ii) each team consists of only two players; and iii) the underlying class of games is polymatrix. As a byproduct, we resolve the complexity of group-wise zero-sum polymatrix games, a class introduced and examined in the seminal work of Cai and Daskalakis (SODA ’11), and more recently highlighted by Hollender, Maystre, and Nagarajan (ICLR ’25). Moreover, we show that computing a first-order stationary point in min-max optimization is 𝖯𝖯𝖠𝖣 \mathsf{PPAD} -complete even for quadratic (multilinear) objectives. From a technical standpoint, we develop a series of team zero-sum game gadgets that allow us to simulate the breakthrough reduction of Bernasconi and Castiglioni (STOC ’26). Moreover, to obtain hardness results for quadratic objectives, we make use of a general technique based on linear local approximation, which is of independent interest.

Introduction

Two-player zero-sum games constitute one of the central models of strategic interaction, going back to the foundational work of
52
and the inception of game theory. The minimax theorem endows two-player zero-sum games with a well-defined value and an unambiguous prescription of optimal play. Moreover, a minimax equilibrium—equivalently, a

[

However, many strategic interactions involve multiple agents who share the same objective and compete against an opposing team of agents. Canonical examples include competition between firms
[

[

potential games

[

In this paper, we examine the complexity of computing a Nash equilibrium—the standard solution concept in noncooperative games—in team zero-sum games. A celebrated series of works established that computing a Nash equilibrium even in two-player general-sum games is
\mathsf{PPAD}
-complete
[
20
,
16
]
. However, those results do not apply to team zero-sum games since they are significantly more structured: there are only two diametrically opposing objectives, one for each team. That is, unlike general-sum games, team zero-sum games maintain a global adversarial structure. From a technical standpoint, this fundamentally precludes the use of existing techniques and reductions developed for general-sum games, which explains why the complexity of computing Nash equilibria in team zero-sum games has eluded prior research. A central question is to understand whether the tractability of two-player zero-sum games extends in the multiagent setting, or whether it is subject to the complexity barriers circumscribing general equilibrium computation.

A team zero-sum game can be viewed as a min-max optimization problem over a product of probability simplices. The key challenge is that once a team contains multiple players, the objective of the team becomes nonconvex/nonconcave in terms of their joint strategy. In this context, a recent breakthrough result by
9
established that computing a
first-order stationary point
—the counterpart of Nash equilibrium beyond finite games—in min-max optimization is
\mathsf{PPAD}
-hard even when the precision is inverse polynomial in the dimension. This establishes a stark separation between constrained optimization of a single function—wherein gradient descent efficiently converges to first-order stationary points—and min-max optimization. However, team zero-sum games are significantly more structured than general min-max optimization problems in terms of both the underlying objective and the constraint sets. This leaves open the tantalizing question of characterizing the complexity of team zero-sum games.

The complexity landscape of computing Nash equilibria is by now well-understood in the special case where one of the two teams contains a
single

The three gadgets.

The construction uses three main gadgets. The first is the

Reducing to a quadratic polynomial

Preliminaries

Team zero-sum games

Our primary focus in this paper is on
team zero-sum games
. We work in the usual normal-form representation. We assume that the game contains
[n+m]\coloneqq\{1,\dots,n+m\}
players. Each player
i\in[n+m]
selects as strategy a probability distribution over a finite set of available actions
\mathcal{A}_{i}
. Each player
i
has a utility function
u_{i}:\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}_{n+m}\to\mathbb{R}
. In an
n

m
team zero-sum game, there exists a utility function
u:\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\dots\times\mathcal{A}_{n+m}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for any joint action profile
\bm{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n+m})
, we have
u_{i}(\bm{a})=u(\bm{a})
for any
i\in[n]
and
u_{i}(\bm{a})=-u(\bm{a})
for any
i\in[n+1,n+m]\coloneqq\{n+1,\dots,n+m\}
. When we are dealing with a game with a constant number of players, the utility function
u
will be given explicitly as part of the input. More generally,
u
will be described succinctly as a (multilinear) polynomial of constant degree. In particular, when
u
is a quadratic (multilinear) polynomial, the game is referred to as
polymatrix

[
12
,
11
]
.

(

{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q}\simeq{\bm{x}}_{q}
and
{\bm{y}}^{\prime}_{q}\simeq{\bm{y}}_{q}
, the value
V_{q}
approximates
\left\|{\bm{x}}_{q}-{\bm{y}}_{q}\right\|^{2}.
Furthermore, for every node
q
, we define the linking function
H_{q}
as

\displaystyle H_{q}\coloneqq\sum_{r\in[m]}\langle\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}},{\bm{x}}_{q,r}-{\bm{y}}_{q,r}\rangle.

For any
{\mathsf{NOR}}
gate with input nodes
u,v
and output node
w
, we introduce a maximizing variable
\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{u,v,w}\in\Delta_{3}
and define the
{\mathsf{NOR}}
gate gadget to be

\displaystyle{\mathsf{NOR}}_{u,v,w}\coloneqq

\displaystyle K\left(\alpha^{u,v,w}_{1}(V_{u}-3n-\frac{1}{2})+\alpha^{u,v,w}_{2}(V_{v}-3n-\frac{1}{2})\right)+\alpha_{3}^{u,v,w}H_{w},

where
K=10^{4}\kappa^{2}n^{9}\log(m)
is a scaling parameter independent of
\epsilon
, and
\kappa
denotes the maximum out-degree of
PureCircuit
. Intuitively, the value of
\alpha^{u,v,w}_{3}
serves as the output value of node
w
from the
{\mathsf{NOR}}
gate.

For any
{\mathsf{PURIFY}}
gate with input node
u
and output nodes
v,w
, we introduce two maximizing variables
\boldsymbol{\beta}^{u,v,w},\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{u,v,w}\in\Delta_{2}
and define the gadget to be

\displaystyle{\mathsf{PURIFY}}_{u,v,w}\coloneqq

\displaystyle K\beta^{u,v,w}_{1}\left(V_{u}-3n-\frac{1}{4}\right)+\beta_{1}^{u,v,w}H_{v}+K\gamma^{u,v,w}_{1}\left(V_{u}-3n-\frac{3}{4}\right)+\gamma_{1}^{u,v,w}H_{w}.

The intended values for output nodes
v,w
are
\beta_{1}^{u,v,w}
and
\gamma_{1}^{u,v,w}
respectively. Since for each node
q\in V
, it is the input of at most
\kappa
gates and the output of exactly one gate, the value for node
q
is uniquely determined, and we denote it as
s_{q}
. Specifically, we define

\displaystyle s_{q}\coloneqq\sum_{(u,v,q)\in G_{{\mathsf{NOR}}}}\alpha_{3}^{u,v,q}+\sum_{(u,q,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{PURIFY}}}}\beta_{1}^{u,q,w}+\sum_{(u,v,q)\in G_{{\mathsf{PURIFY}}}}\gamma_{1}^{u,v,q}.

Exactly one term on the right side of the equation above is non-zero and
0\leq s_{q}\leq 1
. Finally, for every node
q\in V
, for every copy
r\in[m],
let
\delta=\kappa^{-2}n^{-6}
, we define

\displaystyle M_{r}\coloneqq\delta\left(-\frac{m}{2}+r\right),

(2)

and the regularization

\displaystyle\Phi=\sum_{q\in V}\sum_{r\in[m]}M_{r}\sum_{i\in[n]}(x_{q,r,i}-y_{q,r,i})(x^{\prime}_{q,r,i}-y^{\prime}_{q,r,i}).

The final objective in our team zero-sum game is

\displaystyle F\coloneqq\sum_{(u,v,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{NOR}}}}{\mathsf{NOR}}_{u,v,w}+\sum_{(u,v,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{PURIFY}}}}{\mathsf{PURIFY}}_{u,v,w}+\Phi+K_{\mathrm{copy}}\sum_{q\in V}{\mathsf{COPY}}_{q},

(3)

where we set the parameter
K_{\mathrm{copy}}=4mn\kappa(K+n+m\delta)/\epsilon
. Furthermore, for each node
q\in V
, we define the following function

\displaystyle F_{q,\text{in}}=\sum_{(q,v,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{NOR}}}}{\mathsf{NOR}}_{q,v,w}+\sum_{(u,q,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{NOR}}}}{\mathsf{NOR}}_{u,q,w}+\sum_{(q,v,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{PURIFY}}}}{\mathsf{PURIFY}}_{q,v,w}

to be the total sum of all gate gadgets where
q
is the input of the gate. Since each node
q
is the input to at most
\kappa
gates in
PureCircuit
,
F_{q,in}
has at most
\kappa
non-zero terms. Similarly, we define

\displaystyle F_{q,\text{out}}=\sum_{(u,v,q)\in G_{{\mathsf{NOR}}}}{\mathsf{NOR}}_{u,v,q}+\sum_{(u,q,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{PURIFY}}}}{\mathsf{PURIFY}}_{u,q,w}+\sum_{(u,v,q)\in G_{{\mathsf{PURIFY}}}}{\mathsf{PURIFY}}_{u,v,q}

to be the sum of gate gadgets where
q
is the output of the gate. Since each node
q
is exactly the output of one gate, only one term in
F_{q,out}
is non-zero.

3.2
Copy consistency

Next, we prove a lemma concerning the
{\mathsf{COPY}}
gadget that will help us to establish the correctness of each gadget we implement.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
\epsilon
-first-order stationary point of the objective defined in (
3
) and for any node
q\in V
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{4\epsilon}{K_{\mathrm{copy}}},\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|{\bm{y}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{y}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{4\epsilon}{K_{\mathrm{copy}}}.

Proof.

Observe that for the maximizer
{\bm{z}}_{q}^{\prime}
, by putting all probability mass on the coordinate
z^{\prime}_{q,r,i,+}
or
z^{\prime}_{q,r,i,-}
where
|x^{*}_{q,r,i}-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}|=\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{r\in[m],i\in[n]}\left(z^{\prime}_{q,r,i,+}(x^{*}_{q,r,i}-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i})+z^{\prime}_{q,r,i,-}(x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-x^{*}_{q,r,i})\right)+z^{\prime}_{0}\cdot 0=\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}.

Thus, from the
\epsilon
-first-order stationary condition of
{\bm{z}}^{\prime*}_{q}
, it holds that

\displaystyle K_{\mathrm{copy}}\left(\sum_{r\in[m],i\in[n]}\left(z^{\prime*}_{q,r,i,+}(x^{*}_{q,r,i}-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i})+z^{\prime*}_{q,r,i,-}(x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-x^{*}_{q,r,i})\right)+z^{\prime*}_{0}\cdot 0\right)\geq K_{\mathrm{copy}}\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}-\epsilon.

(4)

Now consider the deviation of
{\bm{x}}_{q}^{*}
to
{\bm{x}}_{q}^{\prime*}.
Since
{\bm{x}}_{q}^{*}
is part of an
\epsilon
-first-order stationary point, it holds that

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{x}}_{q}^{\prime*}-{\bm{x}}_{q}^{*},\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}F_{q,\text{out}}+\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}\Phi+K_{\mathrm{copy}}\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}{\mathsf{COPY}}_{q}\rangle\geq-\epsilon.

(5)

We aim to bound each term individually. First,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}F_{q,in}\right\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{(q,v,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{NOR}}}}K\alpha^{q,v,w,*}_{1}\left\|{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}-{\bm{y}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}+\sum_{(u,q,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{NOR}}}}K\alpha^{u,q,w,*}_{2}\left\|{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}-{\bm{y}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\left\|\sum_{(q,v,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{PURIFY}}}}K(\beta^{q,v,w,*}_{1}+\gamma^{q,v,w,*}_{1})\left\|{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}-{\bm{y}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\kappa K,

where in the last step we use the fact that
q
is the input to at most
\kappa
gates,
|\beta^{q,v,w,*}_{1}+\gamma^{q,v,w,*}_{1}|\leq 2
, and
\left\|{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}-{\bm{y}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1.
Similarly, for
F_{q,out},
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q,r}}F_{q,out}\right\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{u,v,q\in G_{{\mathsf{NOR}}}}\alpha^{u,v,q,*}_{3}\left(\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}}\right)\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\sum_{u,q,w\in G_{{\mathsf{PURIFY}}}}\beta^{u,q,w,*}_{1}\left(\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}}\right)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\left\|\sum_{u,v,q\in G_{{\mathsf{PURIFY}}}}\gamma^{u,v,q,*}_{1}\left(\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}}\right)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2n,

where in the last step we use the fact that
\left\|\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq n+1\leq 2n
and
q
is the output for exactly one gate. For the regularization term
\Phi,
it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}\Phi\right\|_{\infty}\leq m\delta\left\|{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}-{\bm{y}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}\leq m\delta.

From (
4
) and the fact that after the deviation of
{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}
to
{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}
, the value of the term involving
{\bm{x}}_{q}
and
{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q}
in the
{\mathsf{COPY}}
gadget becomes
0.
Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}-{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q},K_{\mathrm{copy}}\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}{\mathsf{COPY}}_{q}\rangle\leq-K_{\mathrm{copy}}\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}+\epsilon.

Thus

\displaystyle\langle{\bm{x}}_{q}^{\prime*}-{\bm{x}}_{q}^{*},\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}F_{q,\text{out}}+\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}\Phi+K_{\mathrm{copy}}\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}{\mathsf{COPY}}_{q}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle{\bm{x}}_{q}^{\prime*}-{\bm{x}}_{q}^{*},\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}F_{q,\text{out}}+\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}\Phi\rangle-K_{\mathrm{copy}}\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq mn\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}(\left\|\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}F_{q,\text{in}}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}F_{q,\text{out}}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}}\Phi\right\|_{\infty})-K_{\mathrm{copy}}\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}+\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\left(mn(2\kappa K+2n+m\delta)-K_{\mathrm{copy}}\right)\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}+\epsilon.

Combining with (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\left(mn(2\kappa K+2n+m\delta)-K_{\mathrm{copy}}\right)\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}+\epsilon\geq-\epsilon.

The inequality above gives

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{2\epsilon}{K_{\mathrm{copy}}-mn(2\kappa K+2n+m\delta)}\leq\frac{4\epsilon}{K_{\mathrm{copy}}},

where in the last step we use the fact that
K_{\mathrm{copy}}=4mn\kappa(K+n+m\delta)/\epsilon\geq 2mn(2\kappa K+2n+m\delta)
for any
\epsilon\leq 1
. The proof for
\left\|{\bm{y}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{y}}^{\prime*}_{q}\right\|_{\infty}
follows similarly.
∎

We have the following corollary from the previous lemma.

Corollary 3.2
.

For every
\epsilon
-first-order stationary point and for any node
q\in V,
it is the case that

\displaystyle\left|V_{q}-\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{y}}^{*}_{q}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right|\leq\frac{8mn\epsilon}{K_{\mathrm{copy}}}.

Proof.

From
Lemma

3.1
, for every
r\in[m]
and
i\in[n],
we have

\displaystyle\left|(x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i})-(x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i})\right|\leq\frac{8\epsilon}{K_{\mathrm{copy}}}.

(6)

Thus, from the definition of
V_{q}
in (
1
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\left|V_{q}-\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{y}}^{*}_{q}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right|\leq\frac{8mn\epsilon}{K_{\mathrm{copy}}}.

∎

3.3
Well-guessed copies

For every node
q\in V,

\nabla_{{\bm{x}}_{q}}F
consists of four parts:
\nabla_{{\bm{x}}_{q}}F_{q,\text{in}}
,
\nabla_{{\bm{x}}_{q}}F_{q,\text{out}}
,
\nabla_{{\bm{x}}_{q}}\Phi
, and the gradient from the
{\mathsf{COPY}}
gadget. Our next goal is to bound the second and third part.

For any node
q\in V,
we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\bm{x}}_{q}}F_{q,\text{in}}=K\left(\sum_{(q,v,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{NOR}}}}\alpha_{1}^{q,v,w}+\sum_{(u,q,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{NOR}}}}\alpha^{u,q,w}_{2}+\sum_{(q,v,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{PURIFY}}}}(\beta^{q,v,w}_{1}+\gamma^{q,v,w}_{1})\right)({\bm{x}}_{q}^{\prime}-{\bm{y}}_{q}^{\prime}).

For the regularization term, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\bm{x}}_{q,r}}\Phi=M_{r}({\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}-{\bm{y}}^{\prime}_{q,r}).

We define

\displaystyle\Delta_{q}\coloneqq K\left(\sum_{(q,v,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{NOR}}}}\alpha_{1}^{q,v,w}+\sum_{(u,q,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{NOR}}}}\alpha^{u,q,w}_{2}+\sum_{(q,v,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{PURIFY}}}}(\beta^{q,v,w}_{1}+\gamma^{q,v,w}_{1})\right).

Thus, for every
q\in V
,
r\in[m]
,
i\in[n]
,
\nabla_{{\bm{x}}_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{{\bm{x}}_{q,r,i}}\Phi
=
(\Delta_{q}+M_{r})(x^{\prime}_{q,r,i}-y^{\prime}_{q,r,i}).
Moreover, due to the symmetry between
{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q}-{\bm{y}}^{\prime}_{q}
and
{\bm{x}}_{q}-{\bm{y}}_{q}
, we also have
\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}\Phi
=
(\Delta_{q}+M_{r})(x_{q,r,i}-y_{q,r,i}).

We proceed to show the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
\epsilon
-first-order stationary point, for any
q\in V,r\in[m]
, and
i\in[n]
, if
\Delta_{q}+M_{r}\neq 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left|x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}\right|\leq\frac{2ns_{q}}{\left|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}\right|}+3\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon}{|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|}}.

Proof.

We consider two cases.

•

For the case where
\Delta_{q}+M_{r}>0,
we consider the deviation from
x^{*}_{q,r,i}
to
y^{*}_{q,r,i}.
From the definition of
\epsilon
-first-order stationary point, we have

\displaystyle\langle y^{*}_{q,r,i}-x^{*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{x_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{x_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{out}}+\nabla_{x_{q,r,i}}\Phi+K_{\mathrm{copy}}(z^{\prime*}_{q,r,i,+}-z^{\prime*}_{q,r,i,-})\rangle\geq-\epsilon.

(7)

Similarly, if we consider the deviation from
x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}
to
y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{x^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{x^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{out}}+\nabla_{x^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}\Phi+K_{\mathrm{copy}}(-z^{\prime*}_{q,r,i,+}+z^{\prime*}_{q,r,i,-})\rangle\geq-\epsilon.

(8)

We sum up (
7
) and (
8
) and analyze each term individually. First,

\displaystyle\langle y^{*}_{q,r,i}-x^{*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{x_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{out}}\rangle+\langle y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{x^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{out}}\rangle

\displaystyle=(y^{*}_{q,r,i}-x^{*}_{q,r,i})\left(s_{q}\nabla_{x_{q,r,i}}H_{q}\right)+(y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i})\left(s_{q}\nabla_{x^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}H_{q}\right)

\displaystyle=s_{q}\left((y^{*}_{q,r,i}-x^{*}_{q,r,i})\left(\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}}\right)_{i}+(y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i})\left(\sum_{j\in[n]}\mathbf{D}_{j,i}(x_{q,r,j}-y_{q,r,j})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq s_{q}\left(2n|x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}|+n|x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}|\right),

where in the last step we use the fact that
\left\|\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}_{q,r}^{\prime}+\bm{c}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 2n
and
\left\|\mathbf{D}\right\|_{1}\leq 1.
From (
6
), we have

\displaystyle s_{q}\left(2n|x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}|+n|x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq s_{q}(2n|x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}|+n|x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}|+\frac{8n\epsilon}{K_{\mathrm{copy}}})

\displaystyle\leq 3ns_{q}|x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}|+\epsilon,

where in the last step we use the fact that
0\leq s_{q}\leq 1
and we set
\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}
so that
K_{\mathrm{copy}}>8n.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left\langle y^{*}_{q,r,i}-x^{*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{x_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{x_{q,r,i}}\Phi\right\rangle+\left\langle y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{x^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{x^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}\Phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle y^{*}_{q,r,i}-x^{*}_{q,r,i},(\Delta_{q}+M_{r})(x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i})\right\rangle+\left\langle y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i},(\Delta_{q}+M_{r})(x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\left(\Delta_{q}+M_{r}\right)\left(x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}\right)\left(x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-2\left(\Delta_{q}+M_{r}\right)\left(x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}\right)^{2}+\left(\Delta_{q}+M_{r}\right)\frac{8\epsilon}{K_{\mathrm{copy}}},

where the last step follows from (
6
).

We have
0\leq\Delta_{q}\leq\kappa K
and
|M_{r}|\leq m\delta
. Since we set
K_{\mathrm{copy}}=4mn\kappa(K+n+m\delta)/\epsilon
, by choosing any
\epsilon<\frac{1}{2},
we also have
(\Delta_{q}+M_{r})\frac{8\epsilon}{K_{\mathrm{copy}}}\leq\epsilon.
Thus, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\langle y^{*}_{q,r,i}-x^{*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{x_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{x_{q,r,i}}\Phi\right\rangle+\left\langle y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{x^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{x^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}\Phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-2\left(\Delta_{q}+M_{r}\right)\left(x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}\right)^{2}+\epsilon.

Finally, for the
{\mathsf{COPY}}
gadget, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle y^{*}_{q,r,i}-x^{*}_{q,r,i},K_{\mathrm{copy}}\left(z^{\prime*}_{q,r,i,+}-z^{\prime*}_{q,r,i,-}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i},K_{\mathrm{copy}}\left(-z^{\prime*}_{q,r,i,+}+z^{\prime*}_{q,r,i,-}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=K_{\mathrm{copy}}\left(z^{\prime*}_{q,r,i,+}\left(x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-x^{*}_{q,r,i}+y^{*}_{q,r,i}\right)+z^{\prime*}_{q,r,i,-}\left(x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}+y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 8\epsilon,

where the last step follows from (
6
) and the fact that
\left|z^{\prime*}_{q,r,i,+}-z^{\prime*}_{q,r,i,-}\right|\leq 1
.
Thus, summing up (
7
) and (
8
), we get

\displaystyle-2\left(\Delta_{q}+M_{r}\right)\left(x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}\right)^{2}+3ns_{q}\left|x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}\right|+10\epsilon\geq-2\epsilon.

Using the assumption that
\Delta_{q}+M_{r}>0
and solving for the above quadratic, we get

\displaystyle\left|x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3ns_{q}+\sqrt{9n^{2}s_{q}^{2}+96(\Delta_{q}+M_{r})\epsilon}}{4\left(\Delta_{q}+M_{r}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3ns_{q}+\sqrt{9n^{2}s_{q}^{2}}+\sqrt{96(\Delta_{q}+M_{r})\epsilon}}{4\left(\Delta_{q}+M_{r}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2ns_{q}}{\Delta_{q}+M_{r}}+3\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon}{\Delta_{q}+M_{r}}}.

•

For the case where
\Delta_{q}+M_{r}<0,
consider a deviation from
y^{*}_{q,r,i}
to
x^{*}_{q,r,i}
and from
y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}
to
x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i},
we get

\displaystyle\langle x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{y_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{y_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{out}}+\nabla_{y_{q,r,i}}\Phi+K_{\mathrm{copy}}(z^{*}_{q,r,i,+}-z^{*}_{q,r,i,-})\rangle\leq\epsilon

(9)

and

\displaystyle\langle x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{y^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{y^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{out}}+\nabla_{y^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}\Phi+K_{\mathrm{copy}}(-z^{*}_{q,r,i,+}+z^{*}_{q,r,i,-})\rangle\leq\epsilon.

(10)

Summing up (
9
) and (
10
), all other terms follow similarly as the previous case except for
F_{q,\text{out}}
. For
F_{q,\text{out}}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{y_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{out}}\rangle+\langle x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{y^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{out}}\rangle

\displaystyle=s_{q}(x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i})\left(-\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}-{\bm{c}}\right)_{i}

\displaystyle\geq-2ns_{q}\left|x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}\right|.

Thus, combining (
9
) and (
10
), we get

\displaystyle 2\left|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}\right|(x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i})^{2}-2ns_{q}\left|x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}\right|-9\epsilon\leq 2\epsilon.

(11)

Solving for the quadratic,

\displaystyle\left|x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}\right|\leq\frac{ns_{q}}{\left|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}\right|}+3\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon}{|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|}}\leq\frac{2ns_{q}}{\left|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}\right|}+3\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon}{|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|}}.

Thus, we conclude that if
\Delta_{q}+M_{r}\neq 0
, regardless of the sign of
\Delta_{q}+M_{r},
we have
\left|x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i}\right|\leq\frac{2ns_{q}}{\left|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}\right|}+3\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon}{|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

We proceed to show the following technical lemma concerning well-guessed copies.

Lemma 3.4
.

For every node
q\in V
and for any constant
\rho<1,
there are at least
\frac{\rho}{2\delta}
copies
r\in[m]
that satisfy
|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|\leq\rho
. Moreover, there are at most two copies that satisfy
|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|<\delta.

Proof.

We set
h_{q}=\frac{m}{2}-\frac{\Delta_{q}}{\delta}
. From the definition of
M_{r}
in (
2
), we can write
\Delta_{q}+M_{r}=\delta(r-h_{q}).
Since
K=10^{4}\kappa^{2}n^{9}\log(m)
and
\delta=\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}n^{6}}
, for sufficiently large
n
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{m\delta}{4}\geq\frac{n^{10}}{4\kappa^{2}}\geq\kappa K\geq\Delta_{q},

where we use the fact that
m\geq n^{16}
and
0\leq\Delta_{q}\leq\kappa K.
Thus, we have
0\leq\Delta_{q}\leq\frac{m\delta}{4}
which implies
\frac{m}{4}\leq h_{q}\leq\frac{m}{2}.

Since
h_{q}\in[\frac{m}{4},\frac{m}{2}]
and
1<\frac{\rho}{\delta}=\rho\kappa^{2}n^{6}<m
, there are at least
\lfloor\frac{\rho}{\delta}\rfloor\geq\frac{\rho}{2\delta}
copies
r\in[m]
such that
|r-h_{q}|\leq\frac{\rho}{\delta}.
Given that
|r-h_{q}|\leq\frac{\rho}{\delta}
implies
|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|\leq\rho,
this proves the first part of the lemma.

To prove the second part, since having copies
r\in[m]
such that
|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|<\delta
is equivalent to having copies satisfying
|r-h_{q}|<1.
Because
r
is an integer, there are at most two integers that satisfy this condition.
∎

From
Lemma

3.4
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.5
.

For any node
q\in V,
consider all the copies
r\in[m]
such that
|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|\geq\delta
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{r:|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|\geq\delta}\frac{1}{|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|}\leq\frac{4\log(m)}{\delta}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{r:|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|\geq\delta}\frac{1}{|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|^{2}}\leq\frac{4}{\delta^{2}}.

Proof.

From
Lemma

3.4
, we can group all the copies
r\in[m]
such that
k\leq|r-h_{q}|<k+1
for any integer
k\in[1,m].
For each interval
[k,k+1),
there are at most 2 copies satisfying this condition. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{r:|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|\geq\delta}\frac{1}{|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|}\leq\frac{2}{\delta}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{k}\leq\frac{4\log(m)}{\delta}.

Similarly, we can show that

\displaystyle\sum_{r:|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|\geq\delta}\frac{1}{|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{\delta^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\leq\frac{4}{\delta^{2}}.

∎

3.4
Gadget consistency

Having established the characterization of well-guessed copies in the previous subsection, we move on to show the consistency of the
{\mathsf{NOR}}
and
{\mathsf{PURIFY}}
gadgets we design.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\epsilon<n^{-8}
. Given any
\epsilon
-first-order stationary point, for any node
q\in V,
if
s_{q}\leq\epsilon
, then it is the case that
V_{q}\leq 3n.

Proof.

From
Lemma

3.3
, we have

\displaystyle(x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i})^{2}\leq\frac{8n^{2}\epsilon^{2}}{|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|^{2}}+\frac{18\epsilon}{|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|}.

From
Lemma

3.4
and
Corollary

3.5
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q}-{\bm{y}}^{*}_{q}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r:|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|<\delta}\left\|{\bm{x}}_{q,r}^{*}-{\bm{y}}^{*}_{q,r}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{r:|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|\geq\delta}\left\|{\bm{x}}_{q,r}^{*}-{\bm{y}}^{*}_{q,r}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2n+n\left(\frac{32n^{2}\epsilon^{2}}{\delta^{2}}+\frac{72\log(m)\epsilon}{\delta}\right)\leq 3n,

where in the last step we use the fact that
\epsilon<n^{-8}
so
\frac{32n^{3}\epsilon^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\leq\frac{n}{2}
and
\frac{72\log(m)\epsilon}{\delta}<\frac{n}{2}
for sufficiently large
n
. The proof is complete.
∎

We complement
Lemma

3.6
with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

Given any
\epsilon
-first-order stationary point, for any node
q\in V,
if
s_{q}\geq 1-\epsilon
, and there is no copy
r\in[m]
such that
{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q,r}
is a
\rho
-solution to
LinVI
, then
V_{q}\geq 3n+1
.

Proof.

Let
r
be a well-guessed copy such that
|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|\leq\rho^{\prime}=\frac{\rho}{24}.
We aim to show that
\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q,r}-{\bm{y}}^{*}_{q,r}\right\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{\rho}{6n}.

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that
\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q,r}-{\bm{y}}^{*}_{q,r}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\rho}{6n}.
For any
i\in[n]
and any
x\in[0,1]
, we consider the deviations from
y^{*}_{q,r,i}
to
x
and from
y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}
to
x.
Since
y^{*}_{q,r,i}
and
y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}
are part of an
\epsilon

\displaystyle\langle x-y^{*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{y_{q,r,i}}F\rangle\leq\epsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\langle x-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{y^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}F\rangle\leq\epsilon.

Summing up these two inequalities, we get

\displaystyle\langle x-y^{*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{y_{q,r,i}}F\rangle+\langle x-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{y^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}F\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x-y^{*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{y_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{y_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{out}}+\nabla_{y_{q,r,i}}\Phi+K_{\mathrm{copy}}(z^{*}_{q,r,i,+}-z^{*}_{q,r,i,-})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\langle x-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{y^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{y^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{out}}+\nabla_{y^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}\Phi+K_{\mathrm{copy}}(-z^{*}_{q,r,i,+}+z^{*}_{q,r,i,-})\rangle

We analyze each term individually. First, for the terms involving
F_{q,\text{in}}
and
\Phi
,

\displaystyle\langle x-y^{*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{y_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{y_{q,r,i}}\Phi\rangle+\langle x-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{y^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{in}}+\nabla_{y^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=-(x-y^{*}_{q,r,i})(\Delta_{q}+M_{r})(x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i})-(x-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i})(\Delta_{q}+M_{r})(x^{*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i})

\displaystyle\geq-2|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|\geq-2\rho^{\prime},

where in the last step we use the fact that
r
is a well-guessed copy. For the
{\mathsf{COPY}}
gadget term, we have

\displaystyle\langle x-y^{*}_{q,r,i},K_{\mathrm{copy}}(z^{*}_{q,r,i,+}-z^{*}_{q,r,i,-})+\langle x-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i},K_{\mathrm{copy}}(-z^{*}_{q,r,i,+}+z^{*}_{q,r,i,-})\rangle

\displaystyle=(y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i})K_{\mathrm{copy}}(z^{*}_{q,r,i,+}-z^{*}_{q,r,i,-})\geq-4\epsilon,

where the last step follows from
Lemma

3.1
.
Finally, for the term involving
F_{q,\text{out}},
we have

\displaystyle\langle x-y^{*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{y_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{out}}\rangle+\langle x-y^{\prime*}_{q,r,i},\nabla_{y^{\prime}_{q,r,i}}F_{q,\text{out}}\rangle

\displaystyle=-s_{q}(x-y^{*}_{q,r,i})(\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}})_{i}

\displaystyle\geq-s_{q}(x-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i})(\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}})_{i}-s_{q}(x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i}-y^{*}_{q,r,i})(\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}})_{i}

\displaystyle\geq-s_{q}(x-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i})(\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}})_{i}-2n\left\|{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q,r}-{\bm{y}}^{*}_{q,r}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\geq-s_{q}(x-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i})(\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}})_{i}-2n\left\|{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q,r}-{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q,r}\right\|_{\infty}-2n\frac{4\epsilon}{K_{\mathrm{copy}}},

where the last step holds because of
Lemma

3.1
.
Thus, for all
x\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle-2\rho^{\prime}-4\epsilon-s_{q}(x-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i})(\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}})_{i}-2n\left\|{\bm{x}}^{\prime*}_{q,r}-{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q,r}\right\|_{\infty}-2n\frac{4\epsilon}{K_{\mathrm{copy}}}\leq 2\epsilon.

This further gives

\displaystyle s_{q}(x-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i})(\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}})_{i}\geq-2\rho^{\prime}-\frac{\rho}{3}-7\epsilon.

Using
s_{q}\geq 1-\epsilon>\frac{1}{2}
and set
\rho^{\prime}=\frac{\rho}{24},\epsilon<0.01\rho
, for all
x\in[0,1],
we have

\displaystyle(x-x^{\prime*}_{q,r,i})(\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}})_{i}\geq-4\rho^{\prime}-\frac{2\rho}{3}-14\epsilon>-\rho.

This contradicts the fact that
{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}
is not a
\rho
-solution to
LinVI
. Thus, for all well-guessed copies
r\in[m],
we have
\left\|{\bm{x}}^{*}_{q,r}-{\bm{y}}^{*}_{q,r}\right\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{\rho}{6n}.
From
Lemma

3.4
, there are at least
\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{2\delta}
well-guessed copies. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{x}}_{q}-{\bm{y}}_{q}\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{2\delta}\cdot\frac{\rho^{2}}{36n^{2}}.

Since we set the parameter
\delta=\kappa^{-2}n^{-6}
and
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
are constants, we have
\left\|{\bm{x}}_{q}-{\bm{y}}_{q}\right\|_{2}^{2}\geq 3n+2.
Finally, from
Corollary

3.2
and the fact that
K_{\mathrm{copy}}>8mn\epsilon,
we have

\displaystyle V_{q}\geq 3n+2-\frac{8mn\epsilon}{K_{\mathrm{copy}}}\geq 3n+1.

The proof is complete.
∎

3.5
Gate consistency

So far, we have shown that
V_{q}
satisfies the threshold property given the value of each node
s_{q}.
We now prove the other direction. Namely, given the input values, we show that the value
s_{q}
correctly captures the output value. We start with the following technical lemma on bounding the linking term
H_{q}=\sum_{r\in[m]}\langle\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}},{\bm{x}}_{q,r}-{\bm{y}}_{q,r}\rangle.

Lemma 3.8
.

For any
\epsilon
-first-order stationary point, for every node
q\in V
, it holds that

|H_{q}|\leq\frac{K}{100}.

Proof.

From
Lemma

3.3
and
Corollary

3.5
, for any
\epsilon
-first-order stationary point, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{x}}_{q}-{\bm{y}}_{q}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2n+\sum_{r:|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|\geq\delta}\sum_{i\in[n]}\left(\frac{2ns_{q}}{|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|}+3\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon}{|\Delta_{q}+M_{r}|}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2n+n\left(\frac{8n\log(m)}{\delta}+3m\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon}{\delta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2n+8\kappa^{2}n^{8}\log(m)+3\leq 10\kappa^{2}n^{8}\log(m),

where we use the fact that
\delta=\kappa^{-2}n^{-6}
and
\epsilon<n^{-39}\leq\kappa^{-2}n^{-6}m^{-2}.

Since
\left\|\mathbf{D}{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}+{\bm{c}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 2n
, we have

\displaystyle|H_{q}|\leq 2n\left\|{\bm{x}}_{q}-{\bm{y}}_{q}\right\|_{1}\leq 20\kappa^{2}n^{9}\log(m)\leq\frac{K}{100},

where in the last step we use the fact that
K=10^{4}\kappa^{2}n^{9}\log(m)
.
∎

The preceding lemma, together with our choice of
K
, plays an important role in proving gate consistency. We first establish the correctness of the
{\mathsf{NOR}}
gate, where

\displaystyle{\mathsf{NOR}}_{u,v,w}\coloneqq

\displaystyle K\left(\alpha^{u,v,w}_{1}\left(V_{u}-3n-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\alpha^{u,v,w}_{2}\left(V_{v}-3n-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)+\alpha_{3}^{u,v,w}H_{w}.

(12)

In particular, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.9
.

For any
\epsilon
-first-order stationary point, consider a
{\mathsf{NOR}}
gate with
(u,v,w)
where
u,v
are the input nodes and
w
is the output node. We have

•

If
V_{u}\leq 3n
and
V_{v}\leq 3n,
then
s_{w}\geq 1-\epsilon.

•

If
V_{u}\geq 3n+1
or
V_{v}\geq 3n+1,
then
s_{w}<\epsilon.

Proof.

If
V_{u}\leq 3n
and
V_{v}\leq 3n
, the payoffs of actions
\alpha_{1}^{u,v,w}
and
\alpha_{2}^{u,v,w}
in (
12
) are
K(V_{u}-3n-\frac{1}{2})\leq-\frac{K}{2}
and
K(V_{v}-3n-\frac{1}{2})\leq-\frac{K}{2}
respectively. Meanwhile, from
Lemma

3.8
, the value of (
12
) when
\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{u,v,w}
chooses
\alpha^{u,v,w}_{3}
is bounded below by
-\frac{K}{100}.
Let
U(\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{u,v,w})
denote the payoff for
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{u,v,w}}
in the
{\mathsf{NOR}}
gadget, from the
\epsilon
-first-order stability condition of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{u,v,w,*}
, we have

\displaystyle U(\alpha^{u,v,w}_{3})-U(\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{u,v,w,*})\leq\epsilon.

Substituting the lower bound for
U(\alpha_{3}^{u,v,w}),
we get

\displaystyle-\frac{K}{100}-U(\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{u,v,w,*})\leq\epsilon.

Since
U(\alpha_{1}^{u,v,w,*})\leq-\frac{K}{2},
it holds that

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{K}{100}+\frac{K}{2}\right)\alpha^{u,v,w,*}_{1}\leq\epsilon.

Thus, we conclude that
\alpha^{u,v,w,*}_{1}\leq\frac{4\epsilon}{K}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}
. Similarly, we can show
\alpha_{2}^{u,v,w,*}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}
and therefore
s_{w}=\alpha_{3}^{u,v,w,*}\geq 1-\epsilon.

On the other hand, without loss of generality, assume that
V_{u}\geq 3n+1
,

\displaystyle U(\alpha_{1}^{u,v,w})-U(\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{u,v,w,*})\leq\epsilon.

Substituting the bounds for
U(\alpha_{1}^{u,v,w})
and
U(\alpha_{3}^{u,v,w,*}),
and by following similar analysis from the case above, we get

\displaystyle\left(\frac{K}{2}-\frac{K}{100}\right)\alpha_{3}^{u,v,w,*}\leq\epsilon.

We conclude that in this case,
s_{w}=\alpha^{u,v,w,*}_{3}\leq\frac{3\epsilon}{K}\leq\epsilon
.
∎

We continue with the correctness of
{\mathsf{PURIFY}}
gate, where

\displaystyle{\mathsf{PURIFY}}_{u,v,w}\coloneqq

\displaystyle K\beta^{u,v,w}_{1}\left(V_{u}-3n-\frac{1}{4}\right)+\beta_{1}^{u,v,w}H_{v}+K\gamma^{u,v,w}_{1}\left(V_{u}-3n-\frac{3}{4}\right)+\gamma_{1}^{u,v,w}H_{w}.

(13)

Lemma 3.10
.

For any
\epsilon
-first-order stationary point, consider a
{\mathsf{PURIFY}}
gate with
(u,v,w)
where
u
is the input node and
v,w
are the output nodes. It holds that

•

If
V_{u}\leq 3n,
then
s_{v}\leq\epsilon
and
s_{w}\leq\epsilon.

•

If
V_{u}\geq 3n+1,
then
s_{v}\geq 1-\epsilon
and
s_{w}\geq 1-\epsilon.

•

If
3n<V_{u}<3n+1,
then
s_{v}\geq 1-\epsilon
or
s_{w}\leq\epsilon.

Proof.

Let
U(\boldsymbol{\beta}^{u,v,w})
and
U(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{u,v,w})
be the payoffs of (
13
) given
\boldsymbol{\beta}
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
respectively. If

then

, whereas

Therefore, following a similar argument as in

3.9
, we have

\displaystyle 0-U(\boldsymbol{\beta}^{u,v,w,*})\leq\epsilon\quad\Rightarrow\quad\left(0+\frac{K}{5}\right)\beta_{1}^{u,v,w,*}\leq\epsilon.

We have shown the consistency of the
{\mathsf{NOR}}
and
{\mathsf{PURIFY}}
gadgets we design in both directions. Now we are ready to state the main theorem for this section.

Theorem 3.11
.

For any
\epsilon<n^{-39}
, finding an
\epsilon
-first-order stationary point for min-max optimization with degree-

multilinear objective is
\mathsf{PPAD}
-complete.

Proof.

The membership follows, for example, from
22
. To prove the hardness, we construct a degree-

) and find an
\epsilon
-first-order stationary point of this objective. For every

, and

{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}

\rho
-solution of

. There are only
{\mathsf{poly}}(n)

{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r}

\mathsf{PPAD}
-hardness follows from the
\mathsf{PPAD}
-hardness of
LinVI
(
Theorem

2.4
). Suppose, on the other hand, that no such
{\bm{x}}^{\prime}_{q,r,i}
exists. For each node

we define the following assignment function
\lambda(q)
.

\displaystyle\lambda(q)\coloneqq\begin{cases}0&V_{q}\leq 3n,\\
1&V_{q}\geq 3n+1,\\
\bot&3n<V_{q}<3n+1.\end{cases}

From
Lemmas

3.6
,
3.7
and
3.9
, if there is no

which is a
\rho
-solution to

, then for any
{\mathsf{NOR}}

(u,v,w)
in

, the

, and

. Furthermore, from

3.6
,
3.7

, if

\mathsf{PPAD}

2.3

Although the objective function in
Theorem

3.11
is multilinear, it contains degree-3 terms such as
\alpha^{u,v,w}_{1}y_{q,r,i}y^{\prime}_{q,r,i}
which capture interactions among three independent maximizers. These terms prevent us from establishing
{\mathsf{PPAD}}
-hardness for 2 vs. 2 team zero-sum games. To overcome this technical difficulty, in the next section we introduce a gadget with a quadratic multilinear objective that simulates the degree-3 objective.

4
Reducing to a quadratic multilinear function

In this section, we show that we can approximate the degree-
3
multilinear objective in
Theorem

3.11
with a degree-
2
multilinear objective. In the objective function, the regularization term
\Phi
and the
{\mathsf{COPY}}
gadget have degree two. Therefore, we focus on approximating all degree-3 terms in the objective

\displaystyle\sum_{(u,v,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{NOR}}}}{\mathsf{NOR}}_{u,v,w}+\sum_{(u,v,w)\in G_{{\mathsf{PURIFY}}}}{\mathsf{PURIFY}}_{u,v,w}

(14)

with a degree-2 multilinear function.

Since the function in (
14
) is multilinear, we can rewrite all the degree-3 terms as
P=\sum c_{k}\cdot\tilde{x}_{k}\tilde{y}_{k}\tilde{z}_{k}
,
where
\tilde{x}_{k},

\tilde{y}_{k},

\tilde{z}_{k}\in[0,1]
are coordinates of the original strategy variables in (
14
) and
c_{k}
is the corresponding coefficient of the
k^{th}
term. We write
\tilde{x}_{k}\tilde{y}_{k}\tilde{z}_{k}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{k}\tilde{y}_{k}\tilde{z}_{k}=\frac{1}{6}\left(27\left(\frac{\tilde{x}_{k}+\tilde{y}_{k}+\tilde{z}_{k}}{3}\right)^{3}-8\left(\frac{\tilde{x}_{k}+\tilde{y}_{k}}{2}\right)^{3}-8\left(\frac{\tilde{x}_{k}+\tilde{z}_{k}}{2}\right)^{3}-8\left(\frac{\tilde{y}_{k}+\tilde{z}_{k}}{2}\right)^{3}+\tilde{x}_{k}^{3}+\tilde{y}_{k}^{3}+\tilde{z}_{k}^{3}\right).

Thus, in order to simulate the term
\tilde{x}_{k}\tilde{y}_{k}\tilde{z}_{k},
all we need is to construct functions of the form
\phi_{k}(\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{y}_{k},\tilde{z}_{k})^{3}
where
\phi_{k}(\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{y}_{k},\tilde{z}_{k}):[0,1]^{3}\to[0,1]
takes one of the following forms:

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{x}_{k}+\tilde{y}_{k}+\tilde{z}_{k}}{3},\quad\frac{\tilde{x}_{k}+\tilde{y}_{k}}{2},\quad\frac{\tilde{x}_{k}+\tilde{z}_{k}}{2},\quad\frac{\tilde{y}_{k}+\tilde{z}_{k}}{2},\quad\tilde{x}_{k},\quad\tilde{y}_{k},\quad\tilde{z}_{k}.

(15)

Note that
\phi_{k}(\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{y}_{k},\tilde{z}_{k})
is always a linear combination of coordinates
\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{y}_{k},\tilde{z}_{k}
from the original variables in (
14
). We continue with the following definition.

Definition 4.1

(Tangent affine function)
.

Let
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be a differentiable function, for any point
p\in[0,1],
the tangent affine function of
f
at
p
is

\displaystyle T^{f}_{p}(t)=f(p)+(t-p)f^{\prime}(p).

When
f(t)=ct^{3}
, we have
T^{f}_{p}(t)=cp^{3}+3c(t-p)p^{2}.
We proceed with the following technical lemma.

Lemma 4.2

(Linear local approximation)
.

Let
f(t):[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
f(t)=ct^{3}
with
c>0
and let
\mathcal{G}\subset[0,1]
be a finite grid with mesh
\delta^{\prime}
which scales inverse-polynomially in the size of the input. Suppose for any
t\in[0,1]
, there exists a variable
\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\Delta_{|\mathcal{G}|}
satisfying

\displaystyle\max_{p\in\mathcal{G}}T^{f}_{p}(t)-\sum_{p\in\mathcal{G}}\zeta_{p}T^{f}_{p}(t)\leq\epsilon^{\prime},

then we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{p\in\mathcal{G}}\zeta_{p}f^{\prime}(p)-f^{\prime}(t)\right|\leq\sqrt{18c(c\delta^{\prime 2}+\epsilon^{\prime})}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle f(t)-T^{f}_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=ct^{3}-cp^{3}-3c(t-p)p^{2}=c(t-p)^{2}(t+2p).

(16)

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\left|f^{\prime}(p)-f^{\prime}(t)\right|=3c|p-t|(p+t).

By using the fact that
0\leq p+t\leq 2,
it holds that

\displaystyle\left|f^{\prime}(p)-f^{\prime}(t)\right|^{2}\leq 18c\left(f(t)-T^{f}_{p}(t)\right).

Since
\mathcal{G}\subset[0,1]
is a finite grid with mesh
\delta^{\prime},
for any point
t\in[0,1],
we can find a point
p_{t}\in\mathcal{G}
such that
\left|p_{t}-t\right|\leq\delta^{\prime}/2.
From (
16
), this implies
f(t)-T^{f}_{p_{t}}(t)\leq c\delta^{\prime 2}.
Therefore,

\displaystyle f(t)-\sum_{p\in\mathcal{G}}\zeta_{p}T^{f}_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=f(t)-\max_{p\in\mathcal{G}}T^{f}_{p}(t)+\max_{p\in\mathcal{G}}T^{f}_{p}(t)-\sum_{p\in\mathcal{G}}\zeta_{p}T^{f}_{p}(t)

\displaystyle\leq f(t)-T^{f}_{p_{t}}(t)+\epsilon^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq c\delta^{\prime 2}+\epsilon^{\prime}.

Thus, by Jensen’s inequality, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{p\in\mathcal{G}}\zeta_{p}f^{\prime}(p)-f^{\prime}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p\in\mathcal{G}}\zeta_{p}|f^{\prime}(p)-f^{\prime}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal{G}}\zeta_{p}|f^{\prime}(p)-f^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{18c\left(\sum_{p\in\mathcal{G}}\zeta_{p}\left(f(t)-T^{f}_{p}(t)\right)\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{18c\left(f(t)-\sum_{p\in\mathcal{G}}\zeta_{p}T^{f}_{p}(t)\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{18c(c\delta^{\prime 2}+\epsilon^{\prime})}.

The proof is complete.
∎

For the case where
c<0,
we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
f(t):[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
f(t)=ct^{3}
with
c<0
. Suppose for any
t\in[0,1]
, there exists a variable
\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\Delta_{|\mathcal{G}|}
satisfying

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathcal{G}}\zeta_{p}T^{f}_{p}(t)-\min_{p\in\mathcal{G}}T^{f}_{p}(t)\leq\epsilon^{\prime},

then we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{p\in\mathcal{G}}\zeta_{p}f^{\prime}(p)-f^{\prime}(t)\right|\leq\sqrt{18|c|(|c|\delta^{\prime 2}+\epsilon^{\prime})}.

Proof.

The proof of this corollary follows similarly from the proof of
Lemma

4.2
with reversed signs.
∎

Let
\boldsymbol{\tau}=(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\gamma},{\bm{x}},{\bm{x}}^{\prime},{\bm{y}},{\bm{y}}^{\prime},{\bm{z}},{\bm{z}}^{\prime})
be the concatenation of the original variables in the degree-3 multilinear objective. For every
k
and every
\phi_{k}(\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{y}_{k},\tilde{z}_{k})
that takes one value in (
15
), we define
\psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau})=\phi_{k}(\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{y}_{k},\tilde{z}_{k})
. Since
\phi_{k}(\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{y}_{k},\tilde{z}_{k})
is always a linear combination of coordinates
\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{y}_{k},\tilde{z}_{k}
,
\psi_{k}(\cdot)
is an affine function of
\boldsymbol{\tau}
.
Lemma

4.2
suggests that if one can find
\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\Delta_{|\mathcal{G}|}
such that
\sum_{p}\zeta_{p}T^{f}_{p}(t)
is close to
\max_{p}T^{f}_{p}(t),
then
\sum_{p}\zeta_{p}f^{\prime}(p)
is a good approximation of
f^{\prime}(t).
For each coordinate combination
c_{k}\phi_{k}(\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{y}_{k},\tilde{z}_{k})
with positive coefficient
c_{k}>0,
this allows us to introduce a maximizer variable
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k}^{+}\in\Delta_{|\mathcal{G}|}
as in
Lemma

4.2
and define the approximation gadget

\displaystyle G_{k,\psi_{k}}^{+}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+}_{k})\coloneqq\sum_{p\in\mathcal{G}}\zeta_{k,p}^{+}T^{f}_{p}(\psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau})),

(17)

where
f(t):[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is given as
f(t)=c_{k}t^{3}.
On the other hand, with negative coefficient
c_{k}<0
, we introduce a minimizer variable
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k}^{-}\in\Delta_{|\mathcal{G}|}
and the following approximation gadget

\displaystyle G_{k,\psi_{k}}^{-}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k}^{-})\coloneqq\sum_{p\in\mathcal{G}}\zeta_{k,p}^{-}T^{f}_{p}(\psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau})).

(18)

We show the following lemma with the above approximation gadget.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let the objective function be the approximation gadgets defined in (
17
) and (
18
), at any
\epsilon^{\prime}
-first-order stationary point, if
c_{k}>0,
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}G_{k,\psi_{k}}^{+}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k}^{+,*})-\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\left(c_{k}\psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*})^{3}\right)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{18c_{k}(c_{k}\delta^{\prime 2}+\epsilon^{\prime})}.

If
c_{k}<0,
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}G_{k,\psi_{k}}^{-}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k}^{-,*})-\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\left(c_{k}\psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*})^{3}\right)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{18|c_{k}|(|c_{k}|\delta^{\prime 2}+\epsilon^{\prime})}.

Proof.

We prove the case with positive coefficient
c_{k}>0
; the other case follows similarly. Since the maximizer
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k}^{+}
only appears in the approximation gadget term, the
\epsilon^{\prime}
-first-order stationarity gives

\displaystyle\max_{p\in\mathcal{G}}T^{f}_{p}(\psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*}))-\sum_{p\in\mathcal{G}}\zeta^{+,*}_{k,p}T^{f}_{p}(\psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*}))\leq\epsilon^{\prime}.

Since
f=c_{k}t^{3}
, from
Lemma

4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{p}\zeta^{+,*}_{k,p}f^{\prime}(p)-f^{\prime}(\psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*}))\right|\leq\sqrt{18c_{k}(c_{k}\delta^{\prime 2}+\epsilon^{\prime})}.

On the other hand, the chain rule yields

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}G_{k,\psi_{k}}^{+}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k}^{+,*})-\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\left(c_{k}\psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*})^{3}\right)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{p}\zeta^{+,*}_{k,p}f^{\prime}(p)-f^{\prime}(\psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*}))\right|\cdot\left\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*})\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{18c_{k}(c_{k}\delta^{\prime 2}+\epsilon^{\prime})},

where the last step holds because of the choice of the value of
\phi(\cdot)
in (
15
), which implies

\left\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*})\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\nabla\phi_{k}(\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{y}_{k},\tilde{z}_{k})\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1.

The proof for
c_{k}<0
follows similarly.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Since each
\psi_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau})
is an affine function of
\boldsymbol{\tau},
the approximation gadget
G_{k,\psi_{k}}^{+}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k}^{+})
and
G_{k,\psi_{k}}^{-}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k}^{-})
are degree-2 multilinear functions.
Lemma

4.4
effectively shows that, up to an error that scales polynomially in the size of the input, we can approximate the gradient of degree-3 terms
\psi(\boldsymbol{\tau})^{3}
through the approximation gadget
G_{k,\psi_{k}}^{+}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k}^{+})
and
G_{k,\psi_{k}}^{-}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k}^{-})
.

For any degree-3 term
\tilde{x}_{k}\tilde{y}_{k}\tilde{z}_{k}
with coefficient
c_{k}
in (
14
), we construct
7
independent functions
\phi_{k,l}(\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{y}_{k},\tilde{z}_{k})
where each
\phi_{k,l}
takes one of the forms in (
15
). Moreover, the coefficients
c_{k,l}
take the following values

\frac{27}{6}c_{k},\quad-\frac{8}{6}c_{k},\quad-\frac{8}{6}c_{k},\quad-\frac{8}{6}c_{k},\quad\frac{1}{6}c_{k},\quad\frac{1}{6}c_{k},\quad\frac{1}{6}c_{k}.

Then each degree-3 term
\tilde{x}_{k}\tilde{y}_{k}\tilde{z}
can be written as

c_{k}\tilde{x}_{k}\tilde{y}_{k}\tilde{z}_{k}=\sum_{l=1}^{7}c_{k,l}\phi_{k,l}^{3}(\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{y}_{k},\tilde{z}_{k})=\sum_{l=1}^{7}c_{k,l}\psi_{k,l}^{3}(\boldsymbol{\tau}).

Based on the sign of the coefficients
c_{k,l}
, we introduce approximation gadgets
G_{k,l,\psi_{k,l}}^{+}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+}_{k,l})
if
c_{k,l}>0
and
G_{k,l,\psi_{k,l}}^{-}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{-}_{k,l})
if
c_{k,l}<0.

We define the final approximation to be

\displaystyle G_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+}_{k},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{-}_{k})\coloneqq\sum_{l:c_{k,l}>0}G_{k,l,\psi_{k,l}}^{+}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+}_{k,l})+\sum_{l:c_{k,l}<0}G_{k,l,\psi_{k,l}}^{-}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{-}_{k,l}).

(19)

We have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let the objective function be the approximation gadget
G_{k}
for the term
c_{k}\tilde{x}_{k}\tilde{y}_{k}\tilde{z}_{k}
. For any
\epsilon^{\prime}
-first-order stationary point, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}G_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+*}_{k},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{-*}_{k})-\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(c_{k}\tilde{x}_{k}^{*}\tilde{y}_{k}^{*}\tilde{z}_{k}^{*})\right\|_{\infty}\leq 70\sqrt{|c_{k}|(5|c_{k}|\delta^{\prime 2}+\epsilon^{\prime})}.

Proof.

The proof follows by observing that
G_{k}
is a sum of
7
terms as in (
19
). Combining
Lemma

4.4
with the fact that
|c_{k,l}|\leq 5|c_{k}|
yields the desired upper bound.
∎

Finally, let
F(\boldsymbol{\tau})
denote the original objective function in (
3
). Since
F(\boldsymbol{\tau})
is a degree-
3
multilinear function, we can write it as

\displaystyle F(\boldsymbol{\tau})=F_{\leq 2}(\boldsymbol{\tau})+\sum_{k}c_{k}\tilde{x}_{k}\tilde{y}_{k}\tilde{z}_{k},

where
F_{\leq 2}(\boldsymbol{\tau})
denotes the sum of all terms in
F(\boldsymbol{\tau})
with degree less than or equal to
2
. We have at most
|V|(4mn+2mn^{2})\leq 3|V|mn^{2}
different degree-3 terms in (
14
). We define the approximation function of
F
using approximation gadget
G_{k}
defined in (
19
) as

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{-})\coloneqq F_{\leq 2}(\boldsymbol{\tau})+\sum_{k}G_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k}^{+},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k}^{-}).

Since
G_{k}
is a degree-2 multilinear function, the approximation
\hat{F}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{-})
is a degree-2 multilinear function. We proceed with the following lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

For every
\epsilon^{\prime}
-first-order stationary point of the degree-2 multilinear approximation function
\hat{F}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{-})
,
\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*}
is an
\left(\epsilon^{\prime}+600|V|^{2}m^{2}n^{3}K\sqrt{\delta^{\prime 2}+\epsilon^{\prime}}\right)
-first-order stationary point of the original function (
3
).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\hat{F}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+*},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{-*})

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}F_{\leq 2}(\tau^{*})+\sum_{k}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}G_{k}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+*}_{k},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{-*}_{k}).

From
Lemma

4.6
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*}}\hat{F}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+*},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{-*})-\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}F(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*})\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}70\sqrt{|c_{k}|(5|c_{k}|\delta^{\prime 2}+\epsilon^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq 600|V|mn^{2}K\sqrt{\delta^{\prime 2}+\epsilon^{\prime}},

where we use the fact that
k\leq 3|V|mn^{2}
and
|c_{k}|\leq K.

Therefore, for any minimizing variable
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\min}
(i.e.
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\min}\in\left\{{\bm{x}},{\bm{x}}^{\prime},{\bm{z}}\right\}
),
let
D_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\min}}
denote the diameter of
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\min},
we have
D_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\min}}\leq D_{{\bm{x}}}\leq|V|mn.
Therefore, let
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\min}^{*}
be an arbitrary minimizer variable in
\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*}
. From the
\epsilon^{\prime}
-first-order stationarity condition, considering any deviation from
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\min}^{*}
to
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\min}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\min}-\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*}_{\min},\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\min}}\hat{F}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+*},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{-*})\right\rangle\geq-\epsilon^{\prime}.

This implies

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\min}-\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*}_{\min},\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\min}}F(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*})\right\rangle\geq-\epsilon^{\prime}-\left\|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\min}^{*}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\min}\right\|_{1}\left\|\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\hat{F}(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+*},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{-*})-\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}F(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*})\right\|_{\infty}.

Plugging in the bound, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\min}-\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*}_{\min},\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\min}}F(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*})\right\rangle\geq-\epsilon^{\prime}-600|V|^{2}m^{2}n^{3}K\sqrt{\delta^{\prime 2}+\epsilon^{\prime}}.

The proof for maximizer variables
\left\{{\bm{y}}_{q},{\bm{y}}^{\prime}_{q},{\bm{z}}^{\prime}_{q},\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{u,v,w},\boldsymbol{\beta}^{u,v,w},\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{u,v,w}\right\}
follows similarly.
∎

-first-order stationary point

\mathsf{PPAD}

The
{\mathsf{PPAD}}
membership follows again from

. To prove the hardness, by the choice of

and

4.7
implies that
\boldsymbol{\tau}^{*}
is an
\epsilon
-first-order stationary point of the original objective defined in (

.

5
Reducing to a 2 vs. 2 team zero-sum game

Theorem

4.8
shows that for some sufficiently small
\epsilon
that is inversely polynomial in the size of the input, it is
\mathsf{PPAD}
-complete to find an
\epsilon
-first-order stationary point of the min-max optimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{{\bm{X}}\in\mathcal{X}}\max_{{\bm{Y}}\in\mathcal{Y}}\hat{F}({\bm{X}},{\bm{Y}}),

(20)

where
\hat{F}
is a degree-2 multilinear function. Moreover,
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are products of multiple simplex domains such that
{\bm{X}}
is the vector concatenation of all minimizers
({\bm{x}},{\bm{x}}^{\prime},{\bm{z}},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+})
and
{\bm{Y}}
is the vector concatenation of all maximizers
({\bm{y}},{\bm{y}}^{\prime},{\bm{z}}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{-}).
Some coordinates of the original variables have domain
[0,1]
. For each of these coordinates, we represent it by a 2-dimensional vector
(t,1-t)\in\Delta_{2}
. In this section, we extend the
\mathsf{PPAD}
-hardness result to
2
vs.
2
team zero-sum games.

We construct a team zero-sum game from (
20
). Specifically, we introduce two minimizers
{\bm{X}},{\bm{X}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
and two maximizers
{\bm{Y}},{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}
and define the objective function
\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}},{\bm{X}}^{\prime},{\bm{Y}},{\bm{Y}}^{\prime})
as follows.

•

For every degree-
2
term
X_{i}X_{j}
in the objective
\hat{F},
we include
\frac{1}{2}(X_{i}X^{\prime}_{j}+X_{j}X^{\prime}_{i})
in
\hat{F}^{\prime};

•

for every degree-
2
term
Y_{i}Y_{j}
in the objective
\hat{F},
we include
\frac{1}{2}(Y_{i}Y^{\prime}_{j}+Y_{j}Y^{\prime}_{i})
in
\hat{F}^{\prime};

•

for every degree-
2
term
X_{i}Y_{j}
in the objective
\hat{F},
we include
\frac{1}{4}(X_{i}Y_{j}+X_{i}Y^{\prime}_{j}+X_{i}^{\prime}Y_{j}+X_{i}^{\prime}Y_{j}^{\prime})
in
\hat{F}^{\prime};

•

for every degree-
1
term
X_{i}
in the objective
\hat{F},
we include
\frac{1}{2}(X_{i}+X^{\prime}_{i})
in
\hat{F}^{\prime};

•

for every degree-
1
term
Y_{i}
in the objective
\hat{F},
we include
\frac{1}{2}(Y_{i}+Y^{\prime}_{i})
in
\hat{F}^{\prime}.

By the construction of
\hat{F}^{\prime},
if
{\bm{X}}={\bm{X}}^{\prime}
and
{\bm{Y}}={\bm{Y}}^{\prime},
then it holds that
\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}},{\bm{X}},{\bm{Y}},{\bm{Y}})=\hat{F}({\bm{X}},{\bm{Y}})
and
\nabla_{{\bm{X}}}\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}},{\bm{X}},{\bm{Y}},{\bm{Y}})=\nabla_{{\bm{X}}^{\prime}}\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}},{\bm{X}},{\bm{Y}},{\bm{Y}})=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{{\bm{X}}}\hat{F}({\bm{X}},{\bm{Y}}).

However, there are two challenges for us to extend the
\mathsf{PPAD}
-hardness result to 2 vs. 2 team zero-sum games. The first challenge is that we need to design a gadget that enforces
{\bm{X}}\simeq{\bm{X}}^{\prime}
and
{\bm{Y}}\simeq{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}
at equilibrium. The second challenge is that in order to establish hardness in 2 vs. 2 team zero-sum games, we need to encode
{\bm{X}}
and
{\bm{Y}}
to a single simplex domain. Since the domain
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are products of multiple simplices, we need to introduce a gadget that ensures
{\bm{X}}
and
{\bm{Y}}
assign nearly equal probability mass to each of the original simplex blocks.

Below we introduce a gadget that addresses both challenges. Let
L_{{\bm{X}}}
and
L_{{\bm{Y}}}
be the number of simplices concatenated in
{\bm{X}}\in\mathcal{X}
and
{\bm{Y}}\in\mathcal{Y}
respectively. Moreover, let
N_{{\bm{X}}}
and
N_{{\bm{Y}}}
denote the number of coordinates (i.e., number of pure actions) in
{\bm{X}}
and
{\bm{Y}}
respectively. Since
{\bm{Y}}
contains more players and more strategies than
{\bm{X}}
, we have
L_{{\bm{Y}}}\geq L_{{\bm{X}}}
and
N_{{\bm{Y}}}\geq N_{{\bm{X}}}
. We introduce two minimizers
\tilde{{\bm{X}}}\in\Delta_{2N_{{\bm{X}}}N_{{\bm{Y}}}},

\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime}\in\Delta_{N_{{\bm{X}}}L_{{\bm{Y}}}}
and two maximizers

\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}\in\Delta_{2N_{{\bm{X}}}N_{{\bm{Y}}}},

\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime}\in\Delta_{N_{{\bm{Y}}}L_{{\bm{X}}}}.
One can think of each pure action in
\tilde{{\bm{X}}}
of the form
(i,j),
where
i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]
and
j\in\{k^{+},k^{-}\}
for
k\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}].
Similarly, each pure action in
\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}
takes the form
(i,j)
, where
i\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]
and
j\in\{k^{+},k^{-}\}
for
k\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}].
Intuitively, for each
i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}],
one can think of player
\tilde{{\bm{X}}}
assigning
\sum_{k\in N_{{\bm{Y}}}}\left(\tilde{X}_{i,k^{+}}+\tilde{X}_{i,k^{-}}\right)
probability mass to action
i
.

Each pure action of
\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime}
takes the form
(i,j),
where
i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]
and
j\in[L_{{\bm{Y}}}].
For
i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}],
the probability mass
\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime}
assigns to the
i^{th}
action is
\sum_{j\in[L_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{X}^{\prime}_{i,j}
. Similarly, each pure action in
\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime}
takes the form
(i,j)
where
i\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]
and
j\in[L_{{\bm{X}}}],
and
\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime}
assigns
\sum_{j\in[L_{\bm{X}}]}\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{i,j}
probability mass to action
i.

We define functions

{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}})_{i}=L_{{\bm{X}}}\left(\sum_{k\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\left(\tilde{X}_{i,k^{+}}+\tilde{X}_{i,k^{-}}\right)\right)

and

{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}})_{i}=L_{{\bm{Y}}}\left(\sum_{k\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\left(\tilde{Y}_{i,k^{+}}+\tilde{Y}_{i,k^{-}}\right)\right).

Additionally, we let
{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}})
be the concatenation of all
{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}})_{i}
and similarly for
{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}).
Therefore
{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}})
and
{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}})
map
\tilde{{\bm{X}}}
and
\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}
to the same dimensions induced by domains
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}.
Similarly, we introduce
{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime})_{i}=L_{{\bm{X}}}\left(\sum_{k\in[L_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{X}^{\prime}_{i,k}\right)
and
{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime})_{i}=L_{{\bm{Y}}}\left(\sum_{k\in[L_{{\bm{X}}}]}\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{i,k}\right)
.
{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime})
and
{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime})
are defined accordingly.

For any
i\in[L_{\bm{X}}]
, we also let
\mu_{i}^{{\bm{X}}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}})
denote the probability mass that player
\tilde{{\bm{X}}}
assigns to the
i^{th}
simplex block. Specifically,

\displaystyle\mu_{i}^{{\bm{X}}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}})=\sum_{j:\text{action $j$ is in $i^{th}$ simplex block}}\sum_{k\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\left(\tilde{X}_{j,k^{+}}+\tilde{X}_{j,k^{-}}\right).

Similarly, we define

\displaystyle\mu_{i}^{{\bm{Y}}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}})=\sum_{j:\text{action $j$ is in $i^{th}$ simplex block}}\sum_{k\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\left(\tilde{Y}_{j,k^{+}}+\tilde{Y}_{j,k^{-}}\right).

To ensure that
{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}})
and
{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime})
assign similar probability mass to each action
i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]
(similarly for
{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}})
and
{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime})
), we introduce the following objective

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{copy}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime})=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\left(\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\tilde{X}_{j,i^{+}}\left({\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime})_{i}-{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}})_{i}\right)+\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\tilde{X}_{j,i^{-}}\left({\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}})_{i}-{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime})_{i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\left(\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}_{j,i^{+}}\left({\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime})_{i}-{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}})_{i}\right)+\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}_{j,i^{-}}\left({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}})_{i}-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime})_{i}\right)\right).

At a high level, we can think of
\tilde{X}_{j,i^{+}},\tilde{X}_{j,i^{-}}
and
\tilde{Y}_{j,i^{+}},\tilde{Y}_{j,i^{-}}
as playing roles similar to
{\bm{z}}_{+},{\bm{z}}_{-},{\bm{z}}^{\prime}_{+},{\bm{z}}^{\prime}_{-}
in our design of the
{\mathsf{COPY}}
gadget in
Section

3
.

Furthermore, to ensure that
{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}})
and
{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}})
assign roughly equal probability mass to each simplex block, we design the following gadget following the same spirit as the generalized matching-pennies game
[
20
]
:

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{block}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime})=\sum_{i\in L_{{\bm{X}}}}\mu_{i}^{{\bm{X}}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}})\cdot\left(\sum_{j\in N_{{\bm{Y}}}}\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime}_{j,i}\right)-\sum_{i\in L_{{\bm{Y}}}}\mu_{i}^{{\bm{Y}}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}})\cdot\left(\sum_{j\in N_{{\bm{X}}}}\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime}_{j,i}\right).

Effectively, one can think of
(\tilde{{\bm{X}}},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime})
playing a generalized matching-pennies game and
(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime})
playing another generalized matching-pennies game.
We define the objective function to be

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle+K_{1}\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{copy}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime})+K_{2}\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{block}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime}).

Since
{\bm{X}}(\cdot),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\cdot),{\bm{Y}}(\cdot),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\cdot)
are all linear functions and
\hat{F}^{\prime},\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{copy}},\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{block}}
are degree-2 multilinear functions,
\tilde{F}
is a degree-2 multilinear function.

Remark 5.1
.

Since each of
{\bm{X}}(\tilde{X}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{X^{\prime}}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{Y}),
and
{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{Y}^{\prime})
may not lie in a product of simplices, we slightly modify the domain of
\hat{F}^{\prime}.
Specifically, we define its domain to be the Euclidean space with the same dimension as
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{Y}.
Nevertheless, later in the proof of
Theorem

5.4
, we will construct
\overline{{\bm{X}}}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\overline{{\bm{Y}}}\in\mathcal{Y}
to ensure the reduction goes through.

We continue with the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
L_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}
be the Lipschitz constant for
\hat{F}^{\prime}.
Then
L_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}
scales polynomially in the size of the input. Let the objective function be
\tilde{F}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime})
with

. At any
\epsilon
-approximate Nash equilibrium, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{4\epsilon}{K_{1}},\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*})-{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{4\epsilon}{K_{1}}.

Proof.

Let
i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]
such that
|{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})_{i}-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})_{i}|

{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})_{i}-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})_{i}<0,
the proof for the other case follows symmetrically. Consider the deviation of
\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}
to
\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}

\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}_{j,i^{-}}=\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}_{j,k}=0
for

We have
{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*})={\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}).
Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*}))=\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})),

and

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{block}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})=\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{block}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*}).

Moreover, the
\epsilon
-approximate Nash equilibrium condition of
\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\left(\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}^{*}_{j,i^{+}}\left({\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})_{i}-{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})_{i}\right)+\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}^{*}_{j,i^{-}}\left({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})_{i}-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})_{i}\right)\right)

(21)

\displaystyle\geq\left\|{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})\right\|_{\infty}-\frac{\epsilon}{K_{1}}.

(22)

On the other hand, consider the deviation of
\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}
to some
\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime}
such that
{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime})={\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{block}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})=\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{block}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*}).

Therefore, since
\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}
is part of an
\epsilon
-approximate Nash equilibrium, we have

\displaystyle\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*}))+K_{1}\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{copy}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})

\displaystyle K_{1}\left(\sum_{i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\left(\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}^{*}_{j,i^{+}}\left({\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})_{i}-{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})_{i}\right)+\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}^{*}_{j,i^{-}}\left({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})_{i}-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})_{i}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*}))-\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*}))\leq\epsilon.

Taking the fact that

is

-Lipschitz continuous for each coordinate of

we have

\displaystyle K_{1}\left(\sum_{i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\left(\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}^{*}_{j,i^{+}}\left({\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})_{i}-{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})_{i}\right)+\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}^{*}_{j,i^{-}}\left({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})_{i}-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})_{i}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle-L_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}N_{{\bm{X}}}\left\|{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})\right\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon.

Combining with (

\displaystyle(K_{1}-L_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}N_{{\bm{X}}})\left\|{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})\right\|_{\infty}\leq 2\epsilon.

By setting

Lemma 5.3

Let the objective function be

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{copy}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})=\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{copy}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime}).

we have

\displaystyle\max_{i\in[L_{{\bm{X}}}]}\mu_{i}^{{\bm{X}}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})\leq\sum_{i\in L_{{\bm{X}}}}\mu_{i}^{{\bm{X}}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})\cdot\left(\sum_{j\in N_{{\bm{Y}}}}\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*}_{j,i}\right)+\frac{\epsilon}{K_{2}}.

(23)

On the other hand, let
j\in[L_{{\bm{X}}}]
be the simplex block where

\displaystyle\min_{j}\sum_{k\in N_{{\bm{Y}}}}\tilde{Y}^{\prime*}_{k,j}\leq\frac{1}{L_{{\bm{X}}}}.

i
in simplex
j
, and consider the deviation of the minimizer
\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}
to
\tilde{{\bm{X}}}
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{i,k^{+}}=\sum_{l\in N_{{\bm{X}}}}\tilde{X}^{*}_{l,k^{+}},\quad\tilde{X}_{i,k^{-}}=\sum_{l\in N_{{\bm{X}}}}\tilde{X}^{*}_{l,k^{-}},

and

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{l,k^{+}}=\tilde{X}_{l,k^{-}}=0\quad\forall l\neq i.

From the definition of
\epsilon
-approximate Nash equilibrium, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in L_{{\bm{X}}}}\mu_{i}^{{\bm{X}}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})\cdot\left(\sum_{j\in N_{{\bm{Y}}}}\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*}_{j,i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\min_{j}\sum_{k\in N_{{\bm{Y}}}}\tilde{Y}^{\prime*}_{k,j}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{K_{2}}\left(\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*}))-\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*}))\right)

\tilde{X}^{\dagger}_{j,k^{-}}

such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\tilde{X}^{\dagger}_{j,k^{+}}=\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\tilde{X}^{*}_{j,k^{+}},\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\tilde{X}^{\dagger}_{j,k^{-}}=\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\tilde{X}^{*}_{j,k^{-}}.

(29)

Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{block}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\dagger},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})=\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{block}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*}).

(30)

Additionally, we construct
\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime\dagger}\in\Delta_{N_{{\bm{X}}}L_{{\bm{Y}}}}
such that for action
j
in simplex
i
,

\displaystyle\tilde{X}^{\prime\dagger}_{j,k}=\mu_{i}^{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})X_{j}^{\dagger}\left(\sum_{l\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\tilde{X}^{\prime*}_{l,k}\right).

This ensures that
{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\dagger})={\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime\dagger})
and also

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\tilde{X}^{\prime\dagger}_{i,j}=\sum_{i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\tilde{X}^{\prime*}_{i,j}\quad\forall j\in[L_{{\bm{Y}}}].

Therefore, we also have

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{block}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime\dagger},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})=\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{block}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*}).

(31)

Since
\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}
is part of an
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}
-approximate Nash equilibrium, it holds that

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\dagger},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})+\tilde{F}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime\dagger},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})-2\tilde{F}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})\geq-2\epsilon^{\prime\prime}.

(32)

From (
30
) and (
31
), we know that
\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{block}}
parts are canceling out. For
\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{copy}}
parts, using the fact from (
29
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{copy}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\dagger},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})+\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{copy}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime\dagger},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})-2\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{copy}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg|\sum_{i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\left(\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}^{*}_{j,i^{+}}\left({\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})_{i}-{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\dagger})_{i}\right)+\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}^{*}_{j,i^{-}}\left({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\dagger})_{i}-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})_{i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\left(\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}^{*}_{j,i^{+}}\left({\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime\dagger})_{i}-{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})_{i}\right)+\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}^{*}_{j,i^{-}}\left({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})_{i}-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime\dagger})_{i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle-2\left(\sum_{i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\left(\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}^{*}_{j,i^{+}}\left({\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})_{i}-{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})_{i}\right)+\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}^{*}_{j,i^{-}}\left({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})_{i}-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})_{i}\right)\right)\right)\Bigg|

Since
{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\dagger})={\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime\dagger}),
the equality above is

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i\in[N_{{\bm{X}}}]}\left(\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}^{*}_{j,i^{+}}\left({\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})_{i}-{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})_{i}\right)+\sum_{j\in[N_{{\bm{Y}}}]}\tilde{Y}^{*}_{j,i^{-}}\left({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})_{i}-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})_{i}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}^{\prime}}^{*})\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{4\epsilon^{\prime\prime}}{K_{1}},

where the last step follows from (
24
). Therefore, from (
32
), it holds that

\displaystyle\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\dagger}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*}))+\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime\dagger}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*}))

\displaystyle-2\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*}))

\displaystyle\geq-2\epsilon^{\prime\prime}-K_{1}\frac{4\epsilon^{\prime\prime}}{K_{1}}=-6\epsilon^{\prime\prime}.

Since we have
{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\dagger})={\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime\dagger}),
use (
28
)
along with the fact that
\hat{F}^{\prime}({\bm{X}},{\bm{X}}^{\prime},{\bm{Y}},{\bm{Y}}^{\prime})
is linear with respect to
{\bm{X}}
and
{\bm{X}}^{\prime}
individually, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle{\bm{X}}^{\dagger}-{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),\nabla_{{\bm{X}}}\hat{F}^{\prime}\left({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle{\bm{X}}^{\dagger}-{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}),\nabla_{{\bm{X}}^{\prime}}\hat{F}^{\prime}\left({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})\right)\right\rangle\geq-6\epsilon^{\prime\prime}-2L_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}N_{{\bm{X}}}L_{{\bm{X}}}\epsilon^{\prime\prime}.

Using (
24
) and the fact that
\hat{F}^{\prime}
is
L_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}
-Lipschitz continuous, we have

\displaystyle\Big\langle{\bm{X}}^{\dagger}-{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),\nabla_{{\bm{X}}}\hat{F}^{\prime}\left({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\nabla_{{\bm{X}}^{\prime}}\hat{F}^{\prime}\left({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})\right)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-6\epsilon^{\prime\prime}-2L_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}N_{{\bm{X}}}L_{{\bm{X}}}\epsilon^{\prime\prime}-\frac{4L_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}N_{{\bm{X}}}\epsilon^{\prime\prime}}{K_{1}}

\displaystyle\geq-10\epsilon^{\prime\prime}-2L_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}N_{{\bm{X}}}L_{{\bm{X}}}\epsilon^{\prime\prime},

where the last step follows from our choice of
K_{1}
such that
K_{1}\geq L_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}N_{{\bm{X}}}.
Now from our construction of
\hat{F}^{\prime},
let
\ell_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}
be the smoothness coefficient of
\hat{F}^{\prime}
, which is polynomial in the size of the input. We have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{{\bm{X}}}\hat{F}^{\prime}\left({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})\right)-\nabla_{{\bm{X}}}\hat{F}^{\prime}\left(\overline{{\bm{X}}},\overline{{\bm{X}}},\overline{{\bm{Y}}},\overline{{\bm{Y}}}\right)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}\left(\left\|{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})-\overline{{\bm{X}}}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*})-\overline{{\bm{X}}}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*})-\overline{{\bm{Y}}}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})-\overline{{\bm{Y}}}\right\|_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{16\epsilon^{\prime\prime}}{K_{1}}+2(L_{{\bm{X}}}+L_{{\bm{Y}}})\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\right),

where the last step follows from (
24
) and (
27
). Similarly,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{{\bm{X}}^{\prime}}\hat{F}^{\prime}\left({\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),{\bm{X}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime*}),{\bm{Y}}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{*}),{\bm{Y}}^{\prime}(\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime*})\right)-\nabla_{{\bm{X}}}\hat{F}^{\prime}\left(\overline{{\bm{X}}},\overline{{\bm{X}}},\overline{{\bm{Y}}},\overline{{\bm{Y}}}\right)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{16\epsilon^{\prime\prime}}{K_{1}}+2(L_{{\bm{X}}}+L_{{\bm{Y}}})\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Big\langle{\bm{X}}^{\dagger}-{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}),2\nabla_{{\bm{X}}}\hat{F}^{\prime}\left(\overline{{\bm{X}}},\overline{{\bm{X}}},\overline{{\bm{Y}}},\overline{{\bm{Y}}}\right)\Big\rangle\geq-10\epsilon^{\prime\prime}-2L_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}N_{{\bm{X}}}L_{{\bm{X}}}\epsilon^{\prime\prime}-N_{{\bm{X}}}\ell_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{32\epsilon^{\prime\prime}}{K_{1}}+4(L_{{\bm{X}}}+L_{{\bm{Y}}})\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\right).

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle{\bm{X}}^{\dagger}-{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*})={\bm{X}}^{\dagger}-\overline{{\bm{X}}}+\overline{{\bm{X}}}-{\bm{X}}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{*}).

From (
26
), we have

\displaystyle\Big\langle{\bm{X}}^{\dagger}-\overline{{\bm{X}}},2\nabla_{{\bm{X}}}\hat{F}^{\prime}\left(\overline{{\bm{X}}},\overline{{\bm{X}}},\overline{{\bm{Y}}},\overline{{\bm{Y}}}\right)\Big\rangle\geq-10\epsilon^{\prime\prime}-3L_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}N_{{\bm{X}}}L_{{\bm{X}}}\epsilon^{\prime\prime}-N_{{\bm{X}}}\ell_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{32\epsilon^{\prime\prime}}{K_{1}}+4(L_{{\bm{X}}}+L_{{\bm{Y}}})\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\right).

Finally,
2\nabla_{{\bm{X}}}\hat{F}^{\prime}\left(\overline{{\bm{X}}},\overline{{\bm{X}}},\overline{{\bm{Y}}},\overline{{\bm{Y}}}\right)=\nabla_{{\bm{X}}}\hat{F}(\overline{{\bm{X}}},\overline{{\bm{Y}}})
. By setting
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}
to be inversely polynomial to the size of the input such that
10\epsilon^{\prime\prime}+3L_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}N_{{\bm{X}}}L_{{\bm{X}}}\epsilon^{\prime\prime}+N_{{\bm{X}}}\ell_{\hat{F}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{32\epsilon^{\prime\prime}}{K_{1}}+4(L_{{\bm{X}}}+L_{{\bm{Y}}})\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\right)\leq\epsilon^{\prime}
, for
\epsilon^{\prime}
in
Theorem

4.8
, we show that for all
{\bm{X}}^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{X},

\displaystyle\Big\langle{\bm{X}}^{\dagger}-\overline{{\bm{X}}},\nabla_{{\bm{X}}}\hat{F}\left(\overline{{\bm{X}}},\overline{{\bm{Y}}}\right)\Big\rangle\geq-\epsilon^{\prime}.

Similarly, one can show for all
{\bm{Y}}^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{Y},

\displaystyle\Big\langle{\bm{Y}}^{\dagger}-\overline{{\bm{Y}}},\nabla_{{\bm{Y}}}\hat{F}\left(\overline{{\bm{X}}},\overline{{\bm{Y}}}\right)\Big\rangle\leq\epsilon^{\prime}.

Therefore, from an
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}
-approximate Nash equilibrium of the 2 vs. 2 polymatrix team zero-sum game with objective function
\tilde{F}(\tilde{{\bm{X}}},\tilde{{\bm{X}}}^{\prime},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}},\tilde{{\bm{Y}}}^{\prime}),
one can recover an
\epsilon^{\prime}
-first-order stationary point of the min-max optimization problem defined in (
20
). The
\mathsf{PPAD}
-hardness then follows from
Theorem

4.8
.
∎

6
Conclusions and future research

We established that computing approximate Nash equilibria in team zero-sum games is
\mathsf{PPAD}
-complete even in the polymatrix setting with a
2
vs.
2
structure. As a result, team zero-sum games are computationally as hard as general-sum games, despite maintaining a global adversarial structure. As a byproduct, we also showed that computing first-order stationary points in min-max optimization is
\mathsf{PPAD}
-complete even for quadratic objectives, strengthening the recent breakthrough result of
9
.

Our results leave open an important question. Specifically, the hardness established here applies under an inverse polynomial precision, ruling out a
fully
polynomial-time approximation scheme (
{\mathsf{FPTAS}}
)—unless
{\mathsf{P}}={\mathsf{PPAD}}
. It remains unclear whether a
{\mathsf{PTAS}}
exists. Can one adapt the hardness result of
49
(or even
3
) in team zero-sum games?
