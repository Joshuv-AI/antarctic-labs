Shining light on short-range atomic ordering in semiconductors alloys
Anis Attiaoui, Shunda Chen, J. C. Woicik, J. Zach Lentz, Liliane M. Vogl, Jarod E. Meyer, Kunal Mukherjee, Andrew Minor, Tianshu Li, Paul C. McIntyre

Abstract
The functional properties of semiconductors are typically controlled by tuning their average chemical composition and their state of strain, and by controlling their long-range structural order, including the presence of extended defects such as dislocations and grain boundaries. In addition to these approaches, theoretical predictions suggest that short-range ordering (SRO) of atoms in group-IV semiconductor alloys can modify the bandgap, a defining property of any semiconductor. Herein, a new machine learning enabled, computation-guided methodology for extended X-ray absorption fine structure (EXAFS) analysis of SRO is used to quantify the effects of local atomic order on the band gap of germanium-tin ( Ge Sn ) alloy single crystal nanostructures with well-controlled strain and composition. Correlative analysis of EXAFS and photoluminescence (PL) measured from the nanostructures establishes the relationship between bandgap and the Warren-Cowley short-range order (WC-SRO) parameter of the Ge Sn alloys. It is further demonstrated that, for a given average as-deposited composition, SRO can be tuned readily and over a large range by post-deposition annealing of the alloy crystals. This work indicates that control of SRO can be an important design degree of freedom, along with factors such as average composition and strain, for band engineering, and suggests the potential for atomistic determination and tuning of SRO in other semiconductor alloy systems.

Conclusions

An integrated theoretical and experimental analysis establishes that modulation of chemical
short-range ordering (SRO)
in group-IV
Ge
Sn
alloys provides a promising and novel route for bandgap engineering at fixed average composition and strain. By integrating advanced Bayesian modeling of
extended X-ray absorption fine structure (EXAFS)
data with first-principles supercell calculations, the Warren–Cowley
SRO
parameter,
\alpha
, of
Ge
Sn
is quantitatively extracted from synchrotron spectra collected across a sample set that investigates the effects of post-growth annealing on photoluminescence. More specifically, three key findings emerge. First, post-growth thermal annealing controllably enhances
SRO
, quantified by the Warren-Cowley
SRO
parameter, from
0.20\pm 0.05
to
0.52\pm 0.05
without detectably altering either average composition or strain state. This indicates that the local atomic configuration in metastable group-IV alloys can be tuned independently from conventional structural parameters. Second,
SRO
-driven bandgap modulation (
79\pm$10\text{\,}\mathrm{meV}$/\alpha
) rivals shifts achievable through typical average composition or strain engineering. Achieving the measured
25\text{\,}\mathrm{meV}
blueshift via compositional tuning, for example, would require reducing
Sn
content by approximately
1.5\pm 0.5
at.%.
SRO
tuning, by contrast, is observed to occur at a fixed nominal composition in a strain relaxed configuration. Third, quantitative agreement between measured and predicted sensitivities (
S_{\text{expt}}=79\pm$10\text{\,}\mathrm{meV}$/\alpha
vs.
S_{\text{theo}}=65\pm$6\text{\,}\mathrm{meV}$/\alpha
) validates atomic-scale understanding of
SRO
in the
Ge
Sn
alloy system and establishes predictive design rules: a target bandgap shift
\Delta E_{g}
directly maps to a required
SRO
change
\Delta\alpha\approx\Delta E_{g}/S
, translatable into specific thermal protocols. By demonstrating that local atomic arrangements constitute a significant and largely overlooked degree of freedom, this work establishes control of short-range order as a new approach to semiconductor alloy engineering.
