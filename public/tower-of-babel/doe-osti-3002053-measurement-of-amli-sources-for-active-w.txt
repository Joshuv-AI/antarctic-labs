 SUMMARY

To support the use of the Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) large-volume active well coincidence
counter (LV-AWCC) in material control and accountancy (MC&A) measurements, a set of AmLi neutron
sources was acquired from Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL). These sources were measured
to verify their suitability for use in the ORNL LV-AWCC. The sources produce neutrons at a rate that is
within 4% of the rate for one set of AmLi sources already in use and 40% higher than the rate for the
other set. These measurements indicate that the SRNL sources are suitable for use in the LV-AWCC
because their sensitivity and measurement precision will be similar to those of the sets already in use.
However, the SRNL sources still need to be evaluated for MC&A use.

 1. BACKGROUND

Neutron coincidence counting is a standard technique used for quantitative measurements of special
nuclear material (SNM) for MC&A inventory purposes. The neutron coincidence counting rate can be
calibrated with respect to the fissile mass of SNM items. To measure uranium-bearing items, active
measurements must be performed, in which a neutron source induces fissions to be measured. At ORNL,
the instrument for uranium coincidence counting measurements is the large-volume active well
coincidence counter (LV-AWCC), which holds two sources that provide neutrons. The sources are AmLi;
the alpha particles emitted by the radioactive decay of 241Am collide with lithium and release neutrons.
The AmLi sources are no longer manufactured, and so acquiring existing sources and determining their
continued suitability for use with neutron counters is of great value. Accordingly, a set of AmLi sources
was transferred from SRNL to ORNL, and its neutron emission rates were compared to the two sets of
AmLi sources in use at ORNL to determine if the new sources would be appropriate for use in the LV-
AWCC.

 2. MEASUREMENTS

2.1 MEASUREMENT SETUP

To compare the AmLi sources, they were measured using an Antech Neutron Coincidence Collar neutron
detector, Model 2071-17 (Figure 1). This detector is composed of four high-density polyethylene slabs,
each of which encloses six 3He-filled neutron detectors. The system also has four cadmium sheets,
sandwiched between layers of aluminum to assist with handling, that were arranged on the outside of the
collar to reduce background neutron counts and potential room return.

The detectors were connected in series and operated with a Canberra JSR-15 shift register. The JSR-15
supplies the high-voltage for the 3He detectors and the 5 V connection for the electronics and also collects
and records the neutron count rate.

 Figure 1. The Antech Neutron Coincidence Collar with the JSR-15 shift register.

The sources were placed on a stand such that the AmLi source would be near the center of the detector
volume (Figure 2). The high-voltage was set at 1,680 V, and the count time was set to 100 s. Each AmLi
source was counted 20 times. Background was counted each day measurements were performed. The total
neutron count rate was recorded for comparison between the AmLi sources.

The new sources produce a count rate that is within 4% of the rate for one of the existing ORNL sets and
approximately 40% higher than the rate for the other set.

 3. CONCLUSIONS

The sources acquired from SRNL are, like the ORNL sources, AmLi neutron sources; thus, the neutron
energy spectrum of the SRNL set is the same as that of the two sets of ORNL AmLi sources.

The neutron count rate measured from the SRNL sources was within 4% of the count rate from one
corresponding ORNL source and 40% higher than the count rate of the other. These results mean that the
newly acquired sources produce sufficient neutrons to have similar operational characteristics when used
in the LV-AWCC. Specifically, the new sources will lead to comparable counting times when measuring
uranium-bearing items with the LV-AWCC and may lead to reduced counting times relative to one of the
existing source sets.

Finally, although the sources are comparable in terms of neutron emission rate, in an active system the
strength of the source is a primary factor in determining the minimum sensitivity and measurement
precision that can be achieved. Further measurements and analysis are required to determine the total
measurement uncertainty of LV-AWCC measurements using the newly acquired sources.
