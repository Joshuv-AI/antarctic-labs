ABSTRACT
This document details the resulting velocity of Mk279 Mod1 projectiles when fired from a RD24
barrel with between 15 and 110 grains of smokeless propellant to achieve velocities between 163
and 651m/s.

1. LOAD DATA FOR MK279 PROJECTILES

1.1. Introduction

The Mk279 Mod1 is a projectile designed to be fired from the RD24 disruptor barrel. The objective
of this work was to determine the velocity of the MK279 when loaded with different amount of
propellant. The Mk279 Mod1 has a 12 gauge diameter, 0.73 inches (18.5mm), with a steel core
and copper plating 0.006 inches (0.15mm) thick and is shown in Figure 1-1. It has a mass of 1.8oz
(51g). The maximum propellant load for this series was 110 grains of powder. The propellant was
a dual powder load made up of approximately 20 grains of Alliant Unique and approximately 90
grains of ST Marks OBP502.

1.2. Experimental Setup

The following experiments were conducted at Thunder Range one of Sandia National Laboratories’
explosive test sites. The experimental setup consisted of a RD24 barrel aimed perpendicular to
two high speed cameras. The two cameras were setup to be redundant data collect and the cameras
were placed perpendicular to each other. The cameras were calibrated for distance by recording
a known distance, ruler, along the flight path to determine a distance calibration for each camera.
The RD24 was electrically fired and was mounted in a recoil absorbing stand, see Figure 1-2.
The experimental procedure involved loading a primed shotshell hull with the designated amount
of powder followed by a Mk279 Mod1 projectile. The loaded shell was fired, and the resulting
projectile velocity was determined using high speed imaging data. All powder loads were achieved
by first adding the Unique powder up to 20 grains and then adding OBP502 to reach the final
propellant mass. For propellant masses below 20 grains, only Unique powder was used. Cardboard

High speed video was captured of the projectile motion and the velocity of the projectiles was
determined using the high speed images. The velocity for each shot was determined using an
custom MATLAB script. The script first generated a "synthetic" streak image by extracting a single
row of pixels from every image in a data set (black line in Figures 1-3) and stacking them vertically
downward such that time is on the y axis and distance is on the x-axis. As such, the inverse of the
slope of the projectile path is the projectiles velocity. A line was then fit to the streak image using
the polyfit MATLAB function, Figure 1-4, and the slope of the fitted line used to determine the
projectile velocity.

1.3. Results

Table 1.3 shows the load data gathered in the present work. The first column is the powder load
in grains, second is the shot ID for internal reference, the third column is the velocity of the shot
determined using the automated process outlined in Figures 1-3 and 1-4. The last column lists the
average velocity for that powder load determined as the arithmetic mean rounded to the nearest
whole number. The uncertainty in the mean is the difference between the mean and maximally
different velocity. The velocity values are plotted in Figure 1-5 along with a polynomial curve fit
with polynomial values and R2 value.

1.4. Conclusion

This report collects ongoing load development data for Mk279 Mod1 projectiles when fired from a
RD24 barrel. These data are collected to facilitate future work using the Mk279 Mod1 projectiles
as a characterized ballistic impactor by acting as a reference for future test planning. The number
of shots at each powder load was not large enough for the average velocities to be considered
statistically significant. Additional shots at each powder load are required to achieve a high level
of confidence in the reported average velocities.
