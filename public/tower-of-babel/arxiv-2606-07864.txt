On 3 3 -manifolds with small mass and L 2 L^{2} -curvature
Conghan Dong, Antoine Song

Abstract
. One of S.T. Yau’s problems asks the following: given a 3 3 -dimensional asymptotically flat manifold M M with non-negative scalar curvature and L 2 L^{2} -norm of the curvature tensor at most 1 1 , if the mass of M M is small, is there a bilipschitz diffeomorphism from M M to the flat Euclidean space ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} ? We provide a strong positive answer to this problem by using our previous work [ 20 ] .



1.
Introduction

The famous Positive Mass Theorem, first proved by Schoen and Yau
[
38
]
, states that if
(M,g)
is an asymptotically flat 3-manifold with non-negative scalar curvature, then the ADM mass of any given end is non-negative, and it is zero if and only if
(M,g)
is isometric to the Euclidean 3-space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. For related work, see for instance the references in
[
20
]
.
In an attempt to better understand the “almost-rigidity” or “stability” properties of this rigidity theorem when the mass is close to zero, S.T. Yau asked the following problem in
[
47
]
:

Problem 1.1
.

(
[
47
, Problem 17]
)
Let
M
be a complete three-dimensional asymptotically flat manifold with non-negative scalar curvature. If the total mass is small when we normalize the
L^{2}
-norm of the curvature tensor to be one, is
M
diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
so that the metric is also uniformly equivalent to the flat metric?

In other words, Problem
1.1
asks whether imposing a small upper bound on the scale invariant quantity
m(g)\int_{M}|\mathrm{Rm}_{g}|^{2}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}
implies that
(M,g)
is bilipschitz to the Euclidean 3-space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Later, G. Huisken and T. Ilmanen formulated a related stability conjecture
[
31
, Section 9]
, where only a small upper bound on
m(g)
is assumed. Building on the harmonic map method
[
11
]
, we confirmed Huisken-Ilmanen’s conjecture in
[
20
]
, where we also resolved the related Bartnik’s strict positivity conjecture
[
8
]
.
Several other versions of the stability problem have been explored, for instance from a differential geometric perspective
[
10
,
24
,
19
,
25
,
34
,
32
,
1
,
21
]
or from a more geometric measure theoretic viewpoint
[
43
,
33
,
42
,
14
,
30
,
29
,
28
,
2
]
.
Closely related stability problems for tori with almost nonnegative scalar curvature in a pointwise or integral sense were investigated in

[
35
,
3
,
1
,
18
,
13
]
. See also other references in
[
20
,
41
,
22
]
.

Problem
1.1
is guided by the general phenomenon of
\epsilon
-regularity in geometric analysis.
\epsilon
-regularity theorems and more generally regularity improvement phenomena involving integral curvature bounds play a central role in geometric analysis, see
[
6
,
4
,
26
,
37
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
for results with a small
L^{p}
-curvature assumption. In spite of their apparent simplicity,
L^{p}
-curvatures
[\int_{M}|\mathrm{Rm}_{g}|^{p}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}]^{\frac{1}{p}}
are not well-understood quantities, see questions in M. Berger’s book
[
9
, Section 11.3]
(in particular the discussion after
[
9
, Lemma 268]
), and the conjecture in
[
40
, Introduction]
for Einstein 4-manifolds.

Our goal is to prove the following theorem, which provides a positive and quantitative answer to Problem
1.1
:

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
\epsilon_{0}(\epsilon)>0
depending only on
\epsilon
such that the following holds.
Let
(M,g)
be a complete
3
-dimensional asymptotically flat manifold with non-negative scalar curvature, whose
L^{2}
-norm of curvature satisfies
\int_{M}|\mathrm{Rm}_{g}|^{2}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}\leq 1
.
If the ADM mass of one end of
M
satisfies
m(g)\leq\epsilon_{0}(\epsilon)
, then
(M,g)
is
\epsilon
-bilipschitz to the flat Euclidean 3-space
(\mathbb{R}^{3},g_{\mathrm{Eucl}})
, namely there exists a diffeomorphism
\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that

\displaystyle(1-\epsilon)g\leq\phi^{*}g_{\mathrm{Eucl}}\leq(1+\epsilon)g.

Remark 1.1
.

Actually, our proof implies that the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
holds under a substantially more general assumption: for any
\epsilon>0
and any
p>\frac{3}{2}
, if
the scaling invariant quantity
m(g)^{2p-3}\int_{M}|\mathrm{Rm}_{g}|^{p}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}
is smaller than some positive constant
\epsilon_{0}(\epsilon,p)
only depending on
\epsilon
and
p
, then
(M,g)
is
\epsilon
-bilipschitz to the flat Euclidean 3-space
(\mathbb{R}^{3},g_{\mathrm{Eucl}})
. Note that
\frac{3}{2}
is a natural threshold here, since
\int_{M}|\mathrm{Rm}_{g}|^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}
is scaling invariant and does not involve the mass.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on our recent work
[
20
]
, where we proved an almost rigidity result for the positive mass theorem without assuming an
L^{2}
-bound on the curvature.
The idea can be summarized as follows.
Let
0<\epsilon_{0}\ll 1
be a fixed small constant. By a simple rescaling argument, it is enough to show the theorem when
(M,g)
satisfies

\displaystyle m(g)\leq\epsilon_{0},\ \int_{M}|\mathrm{Rm}_{g}|^{2}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}\leq\epsilon_{0}.

For a uniformly small
\epsilon_{0}
, by the work of D. Yang
[
44
,
45
,
46
]
on
3
-manifolds with small
L^{2}
-curvature, we know that if the volume is locally uniformly non-collapsed, then there is a uniform lower bound on the
W^{2,2}
-harmonic radius and thus on the
C^{\alpha}
-harmonic radius for some
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. On the other hand, by applying
ideas from our proof of Bartnik’s strict positivity conjecture
[
20
, Theorem 1.4]
,
we prove that if the mass is small enough and if the metric on a ball
B
in
(M,g)
is uniformly controlled in the
C^{0}
-topology, then
B
has an almost Euclidean volume. In other words, an a priori coarse volume lower bound can be improved to an almost Euclidean volume bound. This way, using the asymptotic flatness of
(M,g)
and a continuity argument, we deduce that the almost Euclidean volume bound holds everywhere, which already yields local
C^{\alpha}
-closeness of the metric
g
to a Euclidean metric. For the last step, we apply
[
20
]
again to construct a global bilipschitz map from
(M,g)
to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Metrics with bounded
L^{p}
-curvature

We collect some results about the regularity of metrics under bounded
L^{p}
-curvature assumptions that we will use later. Let us first recall the definition of harmonic radius.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Given
p>\frac{n}{2}
, the
W^{2,p}
-harmonic radius at
x\in M
is the largest number
r_{H}(x)=r_{H}(p,x)
such that on the geodesic ball
B=B_{g}(x,r_{H}(x))
, there is a harmonic coordinate chart
(u^{1},\ldots,u^{n}):B\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that for
g_{ij}=g\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{j}}\right)
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{ij}\right)\leq\left(g_{ij}\right)\leq 2\left(\delta_{ij}\right),

\displaystyle r_{H}^{1-\frac{n}{p}}\|\partial g_{ij}\|_{L^{p}}+r_{H}^{2-\frac{n}{p}}\|\partial^{2}g_{ij}\|_{L^{p}}\leq 1.

Remark 2.1
.

By the Sobolev embedding theorem, a lower bound
r_{H}(x)\geq r_{0}>0
on the
W^{2,p}
-harmonic radius at
x\in(M,g)
yields a lower bound
r_{H}^{\prime}(x)\geq C(r_{0})>0
on the analogously defined
C^{\alpha}
-harmonic radius
r_{H}^{\prime}(x)
for
\alpha\in(0,2-\frac{n}{p})
, where
C(r_{0})
only depends on
r_{0}
. That in turn gives a bound depending only on
r_{0}
on the (relative) isoperimetric constant and the Sobolev constant of the ball
B_{g}(x,C(r_{0}))
(see e.g.
[
44
]
for definitions).

Let
\omega_{n}
be the volume of the Euclidean unit
n
-ball.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
44
,
45
]
)
.

Let
n\geq 3,p>\frac{n}{2}
,
0<\eta<1
, and
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold. There exists a constant
\epsilon(n,p,\eta)
such that if a geodesic ball
B=B_{g}(x,1)
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B)

\displaystyle\geq\eta^{n}\omega_{n},

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{Rm}_{g}\|_{L^{p}(B)}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon(n,p,\eta),

then there exists a uniform
r_{0}>0
, such that the
W^{2,p}
-harmonic radius at
x
satisfies
r_{H}(x)\geq r_{0}
.

Proof.

Thanks to
[
44
, Theorem 7.4]
, we know that the isoperimetric constant on
B
is uniformly controlled from below (see
[
44
, Section 4]
for definitions). It is well-known that this constant is directly related to the Sobolev constant

[
44
, (4.1)]
so the Sobolev constant on
B
is uniformly bounded from above. By

[
45
, Theorem 7.1]
, we conclude the proof.
∎

Under integral control of the negative part of the Ricci curvature, the following relative volume comparison was shown in
[
37
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
37
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
p>\frac{n}{2}
, and
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold. There is a constant
C(n,p,R)
which is nondecreasing in
R
such that for any
x\in M
, when
r<R
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,R))}{\omega_{n}R^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}-\left(\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,r))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq C(n,p,R)\cdot\left(\int_{M}|(\mathrm{Ric}_{g})_{-}|^{p}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}.

In particular, for
0<r\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,r))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq 1+C(n,p)\left(\int_{M}|(\mathrm{Ric}_{g})_{-}|^{p}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}.

We recall several definitions from
[
46
]
for later use. Let
C_{S}(\Omega)
denote the Sobolev constant, that is, the smallest
A>0
such that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|f|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq A\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f|^{2},\quad\forall f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)

holds. We say that an
(A,B)
-Sobolev inequality holds on
(\Omega,g)
if

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|f|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-2}}\leq A\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f|^{2}+B\int_{\Omega}f^{2},\ \forall f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega).

Given
0<\delta_{0}<1
, the weak injectivity radius
\rho(\delta_{0},x)
at
x
is defined by the largest radius
\rho
such that

\displaystyle C_{S}(B_{g}(x,\rho))\leq\delta_{0}^{-2}C_{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

and for any
B_{g}(y,r)\subset B_{g}(x,\rho)
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(y,r))\leq\delta_{0}^{-n}\omega_{n}r^{n}.

Define

\displaystyle\rho(\delta_{0},M)=\inf_{x\in M}\rho(\delta_{0},x).

Remark 2.2
.

From the definition of harmonic radius and Remark
2.1
, a lower bound
r_{H}(x)\geq r_{0}>0
on the
W^{2,p}
-harmonic radius at
x
with
p>\frac{n}{2}
implies a lower bound
\rho(\delta_{0},x)\geq C(r_{0})>0
on the weak injectivity radius
\rho(\delta_{0},x)
for some
\delta_{0}>C^{\prime}(r_{0})>0
where the constants
C(r_{0}),C^{\prime}(r_{0})
only depend on
r_{0}
.

Remark 2.3
.

By
[
46
, Lemma 3.1]
, a uniform lower bound on the weak injectivity radius
\rho(\delta_{0},M)
implies a uniform
(A,B)
-Sobolev inequality, with
A,B
depending only on
n,\delta_{0}
and
\rho(\delta_{0},M).

We also recall a result related to smoothings of metrics by local Ricci flows
[
44
,
46
]
. Consider
a smooth
n
-dimensional manifold
M
with a complete Riemannian metric
g_{0}
and
\Omega
an open subset of
M
. Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
be a compactly supported nonnegative smooth function on
\Omega
. The local Ricci flow is defined by the following evolution equation:

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g(t)}{\partial t}=-2\chi^{2}\mathrm{Ric}_{g(t)},\ g(0)=g_{0}.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
44
, Theorems 9.8, B.1, 9.9]
,
[
46
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
q>n
. Assume that a uniform
(A_{0},B_{0})
-Sobolev inequality holds on
(\Omega,g_{0})
and that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|\mathrm{Rm}_{g_{0}}|^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathrm{dvol}_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}\leq\mu_{0}.

Then the local Ricci flow (
1
)
has a unique smooth solution for
0\leq t\leq T_{0}
, where

T_{0}^{-1}\leq C(n,q)(\|\nabla\chi\|^{2}_{\infty}+A_{0}^{-1}B_{0}+A_{0}^{n/(q-n)}\mu_{0}^{q/(q-n)}).

Moreover, for
0<t\leq T_{0}
, we have the following uniform estimates

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{Rm}_{g(t)}\|_{L^{q/2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\mathrm{Rm}_{g_{0}}\|_{L^{q/2}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\chi(x)|\mathrm{Rm}_{g(t)}|(x)

\displaystyle\leq C(n,q)A_{0}^{n/q}t^{-n/q}\|\chi^{2(1-n/q)}\mathrm{Rm}_{g_{0}}\|_{L^{q/2}(\Omega)}.

If the stronger assumption
\|\mathrm{Rm}_{g_{0}}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\leq K
holds, then for some
0<T_{1}\leq T_{0}
with
T_{1}^{-1}\leq C(n)(\|\nabla\chi\|_{\infty}+K)
, we have
\|\mathrm{Rm}_{g(t)}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\leq 2K
for
0\leq t\leq T_{1}
.

2.2.
Small ADM mass and harmonic maps

A smooth orientable connected complete Riemannian
3
-manifold
(M,g)
is called asymptotically flat if there exists a compact subset
K\subset M
such that
M\setminus K=\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{N}M_{\mathrm{end}}^{k}
consists of finite pairwise disjoint ends, and for each
1\leq k\leq N
, there exist
B>0,\sigma>\frac{1}{2}
, and a
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphism
\Phi_{k}:M_{\mathrm{end}}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{\mathrm{Eucl}}(0,1)
such that under this identification,

|\partial^{l}(g_{ij}-\delta_{ij})(x)|\leq B|x|^{-\sigma-|l|},

for all multi-indices
|l|=0,1,2
and any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{\mathrm{Eucl}}(0,1)
, where
B_{\mathrm{Eucl}}(0,1)
is the standard Euclidean ball with center
0
and radius
1
. Furthermore, we always assume the scalar curvature
R_{g}
is integrable over
(M,g)
. For a given end, its ADM mass from general relativity is then well-defined (see
[
5
,
7
]
) and given by

m(g):=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{S_{r}}\sum_{i,j}(g_{ij,i}-g_{ii,j})v^{j}dA

where
v
is the unit outer normal to the coordinate sphere
S_{r}
of radius
|x|=r
in the given end, and
dA
is its area element.

Assume that
(M^{3},g)
is a complete asymptotically flat
3
-manifold with non-negative scalar curvature, which satisfies for a given end:

m(g)\leq\epsilon_{0}.

Recall that for that end,
there is an associated “exterior region”
M_{ext}
, which contains that end, is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
minus finitely many disjoint balls, and has a (possibly empty) minimal boundary, but contains no other closed minimal surface in this region (see e.g.
[
31
]
).
Given any Riemannian metric
h
, let
d_{h}
denote the induced Riemannian distance.

Building on the harmonic map method of
[
11
]
, the following was shown in
[
20
, Proposition 4.13 and (27)]
:

Theorem 2.4
.

Given a fixed
0<\epsilon<1
, for all small enough
\epsilon_{0}
, if
m(g)\leq\epsilon_{0}
then there exists an unbounded connected good region
E\subset M_{ext}
which is closed and has smooth boundary, so that
E
contains the end of
M
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Area}_{g}(\partial E)\leq C\cdot\epsilon_{0}^{2-\epsilon},

and for any base point
q\in E
, there is a harmonic map
\mathbf{u}:M_{ext}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying

(1)

\mathbf{u}|_{E}
is a diffeomorphism onto its image and
\mathbf{u}(q)=0\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
;

(2)

the Jacobian matrix of
\mathbf{u}
satisfies
|\mathrm{Jac}(\mathbf{u})-\mathrm{Id}|\leq\epsilon
on
E
;

(3)

for any
D>0
, for any
x,y\in\hat{B}_{g,E}(q,D)
,

\displaystyle|\hat{d}_{g,E}(x,y)-d_{g_{\mathrm{Eucl}}}(\mathbf{u}(x),\mathbf{u}(y))|\leq C(D)\cdot\epsilon,

where the constant
C(D)
only depends on
D
;

(4)

(\mathbf{u})_{\sharp}\left(\mathrm{dvol}_{g}|_{\hat{B}_{g,E}(q,D)}\right)
converges to
\mathrm{dvol}_{g_{\mathrm{Eucl}}}|_{B_{g_{\mathrm{Eucl}}}(0,D)}
weakly as measures as
\epsilon_{0}\to 0
.

Here,
{g_{\mathrm{Eucl}}}
is the standard flat metric on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and the geometric quantities with the hat
\,\hat{}\,
notation are taken with respect to the induced length metric in
E
(i.e. the distance between two points is measured using the paths inside
E
).

3.
Local non-collapsing

In this section, we will consider small enough
\epsilon_{0}>0
and assume that
(M,g)
is a complete asymptotically flat
3
-manifold with non-negative scalar curvature, which satisfies for a given end:

(2)

\displaystyle m(g)\leq\epsilon_{0},\quad\int_{M}|\mathrm{Rm}_{g}|^{2}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}\leq\epsilon_{0}.

Let

(3)

\displaystyle E\subset M

be the good region inside
M
whose existence and properties are guaranteed by Theorem
2.4
. We will use the notation
C
to denote some universal constant (which may change from line to line).

First we observe the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
v_{0}\in(0,1)
, there exists uniform
\epsilon_{0}(v_{0})>0
such that for all
0<\epsilon_{0}\leq\epsilon_{0}(v_{0})
, for any
x\in M
with
\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,1))\geq v_{0}
, there exists a uniform
r_{0}=r_{0}(v_{0})>0
so that the
W^{2,2}
-harmonic radius satisfies

\displaystyle r_{H}(y)\geq r_{0},\quad\forall y\in B_{g}(x,2).

Proof.

By the volume comparison result Theorem
2.2
, for any
y\in B_{g}(x,3)
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(y,1))}{\omega_{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(y,4))}{\omega_{3}4^{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}-C\cdot\epsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle=\left(64^{-1}\omega_{3}^{-1}v_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}-C\cdot\epsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(10^{-3}v_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}},

where in the last inequality, we used
\epsilon_{0}\leq\epsilon(v_{0})\ll v_{0}
.
Applying Theorem
2.1
together with the small
L^{2}
-curvature assumption (
2
), the conclusion follows if
\epsilon_{0}
is uniformly small.

∎

Next, we prove the following key proposition:

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\epsilon_{0}(\epsilon)>0
such that for all
\epsilon_{0}\leq\epsilon_{0}(\epsilon)
, for any
x\in M
, if
\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(E\cap B_{g}(x,1))\geq(1-\epsilon)\omega_{3}
, then
\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(E\cap B_{g}(x,1))\geq(1-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon)\omega_{3}
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Assuming that the conclusion does not hold, there exists a fixed
\epsilon>0
and a sequence of pointed asymptotically flat
3
-manifolds
(M_{i},g_{i},p_{i})
satisfying (
2
) with
\epsilon_{i}\to 0
and for the corresponding good regions
E_{i}
in
M_{i}
given by Theorem
2.4
, we have

(4)

\displaystyle(1-\epsilon)\omega_{3}\leq\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}(E_{i}\cap B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},1))<(1-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon)\omega_{3}.

By Lemma
3.1
, we know
r_{H}(y)\geq 2r_{0}
for a uniform
0<r_{0}\ll 1
and for all
y\in B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},2)
. By the Sobolev embedding theorem, up to a subsequence, we can assume that for some
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, for some
C^{\alpha}
-Riemannian metric
g_{0}
on a manifold and a geodesic ball
B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},2)
with respect to
g_{0}
,

\displaystyle(B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},2),g_{i})\to(B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},2),g_{0})

in the
C^{\alpha}
-topology. In other words, there exist
C^{1,\alpha}
-embeddings

\psi_{i}:B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},2)\to B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},2+r_{0})

with

\quad\psi_{i}(p_{0})=p_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},2)\subset\psi_{i}(B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},2))\subset B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},2+\delta_{i})

for some positive
\delta_{i}\to 0
, and
\psi_{i}^{*}g_{i}\to g_{0}
as tensors in the
C^{\alpha}
-topology. In particular
\psi_{i}^{*}g_{i}\to g_{0}
uniformly, so finite perimeter subsets of
B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},2)
satisfy a relative isoperimetric inequality with a uniform constant (cf.
[
23
, Theorem 5.11 (ii)]
).

Set
D_{i}:=B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},2)\cap E_{i}
. If
\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}(D_{i})<\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}(B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},2))
, by the relative isoperimetric inequality, we know that for all
i
large enough,

\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}(D_{i})\leq C\cdot\mathrm{Area}_{g_{i}}(\partial E_{i}\cap B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},2))^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq C\cdot\epsilon_{i}^{2}\to 0\quad\text{as $i\to\infty$},

where in the last inequality, we used the area bound given in Theorem
2.4
. This
contradicts our noncollapsing assumption (
4
). So by
\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}(D_{i})\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Vol}(B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},2))
, and by the relative isoperimetric inequality again, we have in fact

(5)

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}(B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},2)\setminus E_{i})=0.

By the uniform convergence of the metric tensors, together with the contradiction assumption (
4
),

(6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},1))=\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}(B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},1)\cap E_{i})\leq(1-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon)\omega_{3}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\hat{d}_{g_{i},E_{i}}
be the induced length metric given by the restriction of
g_{i}
to
E_{i}
.
For any
x\in B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},1)
, up to a subsequence, the maps

\displaystyle\psi_{i}^{-1}:\left(E_{i}\cap B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},r_{0}),\hat{d}_{g_{i},E_{i}}\right)\to\left(B_{g_{0}}(x,r_{0}),d_{g_{0}}\right)

are
\delta^{\prime}_{i}
-Gromov-Hausdorff approximation for
x_{i}:=\psi_{i}(x)
and some positive
\delta^{\prime}_{i}\to 0
. Moreover,
(\psi_{i}^{-1})_{\sharp}(\mathrm{dvol}_{g_{i}}|_{E_{i}\cap B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},r_{0})})
weakly converges to
\mathrm{dvol}_{g_{0}}|_{B_{g_{0}}(x,r_{0})}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
20
, Lemma 5.4]
. The map
\psi_{i}^{-1}
restricted to
E_{i}\cap B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},r_{0})
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
-diffeomorphism onto its image, so we can view
E_{i}\cap B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},r_{0})
as a subset of
B_{g_{0}}(x,r_{0})
equipped with the metric
g_{i}
, and we will discard the notation
\psi_{i}
in what follows. Earlier, we saw that
g_{i}\to g_{0}
in the
C^{\alpha}
-topology, so we have

(7)

\displaystyle(1-\delta_{i})g_{0}\leq g_{i}\leq(1+\delta_{i})g_{0}

for some positive
\delta_{i}\to 0
.
Recall the notation
\hat{B}_{g_{i},E_{i}}(y,r)

for the region in
E_{i}
equal to the metric
r
-ball centered at
y
in
E_{i}
with respect to the induced length metric on
E_{i}
.
By the smallness of the volume of the bad region (
5
) and the uniform convergence (
7
), we can choose basepoints
x_{i}^{\prime}\in E_{i}\cap B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},r_{0})
so that for any fixed
r>0
and any
\hat{B}_{g_{i},E_{i}}(y_{i},r)\subset\hat{B}_{g_{i},E_{i}}(x_{i}^{\prime},\frac{3}{2}r_{0})
with
y_{i}\to y\in B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},2)
, the volume of good regions converges:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}}(\hat{B}_{g_{i},E_{i}}(y_{i},r))\to\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(y,r)).

Notice that
\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(y,r))\geq c_{g_{0}}r^{3}

for some constant
c_{g_{0}}>0
depending only on
g_{0}
. This coarse volume lower bound and the same argument as in
[
20
, Lemma 5.4]
then implies the following properties for any fixed small
r>0
and some sequence
\delta_{i}^{\prime}\to 0
:

(i)

B_{g_{0}}(x,r_{0})
is contained in the
\delta_{i}^{\prime}
-neighborhood of
E_{i}\cap B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},r_{0})
in terms of the metric
g_{0}
;

(ii)

For any
y,z\in E_{i}\cap B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},r_{0})
with
d_{g_{0}}(y,z)\geq r
,

\displaystyle|\hat{d}_{g_{i},E_{i}}(y,z)-d_{g_{0}}(y,z)|\leq\delta_{i}^{\prime};

(iii)

\mathrm{dvol}_{g_{i}}|_{E_{i}\cap B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},r_{0})}\to\mathrm{dvol}_{g_{0}}|_{B_{g_{0}}(x,r_{0})}
weakly as measures.

Finally, by taking a sequence
r\to 0
and choosing a subsequence if necessary, we conclude.

∎

Let
\mathbf{u}_{i}:E_{i}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the harmonic maps given by Theorem
2.4
restricted to
E_{i}
. We can now apply the same Arzelà-Ascoli type argument as in the proof of
[
20
, Theorem 5.1]
to the domains
\Omega_{i}:=B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},1)
, with (
5
) and Lemma
3.3
replacing respectively
[
20
, Lemma 5.3]
and
[
20
, Lemma 5.4]
(while in the context of
[
20
, Theorem 5.1]
, we considered a fixed domain
\Omega
with a fixed metric, in our present case the metrics
g_{i}
in
\Omega_{i}
converge uniformly to a
C^{\alpha}
-metric
g_{0}
).
We conclude that
\mathbf{u}_{i}|_{B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},1)\cap E_{i}}
converges in the uniform topology to an isometric embedding
\mathbf{u}_{\infty}:(B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},1),g_{0})\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
. This implies that
\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}(B_{g_{0}}(p_{0},1))=\omega_{3}
, which is the desired contradiction with (
6
).

∎

By a continuity argument, we obtain the following uniform noncollapsing result.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\epsilon_{0}(\epsilon)
such that whenever
\epsilon_{0}\leq\epsilon_{0}(\epsilon)
, we have
\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(E\cap B_{g}(x,1))\geq(1-\epsilon)\omega_{3}
for any
x\in M
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,1)\setminus E)\leq 2\epsilon\cdot\omega_{3}.

Proof.

Consider the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=\{x\in M:\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(E\cap B_{g}(x,1))\geq(1-\epsilon)\omega_{3}\}.

By the asymptotic flatness condition on
M
,
\mathcal{A}\neq\emptyset
.
Since
E
is a smooth domain,
\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(E\cap B_{g}(x,1))
is continuous with respect to
x
, so
\mathcal{A}
is a closed subset. By Proposition
3.2
, for
\epsilon_{0}(\epsilon)
small enough,
\mathcal{A}
is also open. Thus
\mathcal{A}=M
. The upper bound for
\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,1)\setminus E)
follows from (
5
). Alternatively, one can also prove it by combining the lower volume bound with the volume comparison Theorem
2.2
: for any
\epsilon_{0}
smaller than a uniform constant,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,1)\setminus E)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,1))-\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(E\cap B_{g}(x,1))

\displaystyle\leq(1+C\cdot\epsilon_{0}^{1/4})^{4}\omega_{3}-(1-\epsilon)\omega_{3}

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon\omega_{3}.

∎

3.1
, we immediately get

Corollary 3.5
.

There exists a uniform
0<r_{1}\leq 1
such that for any complete asymptotically flat
3
-manifold
(M,g)
with non-negative scalar curvature and (
2
) satisfied for a uniformly small
\epsilon_{0}
, for any
x\in M
,
the

-harmonic radius at

.

4.
Proof of the main theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

If
(M_{1},g_{1})
is a complete asymptotically flat
3
-manifold with non-negative scalar curvature, such that for a chosen end,

\displaystyle m(g_{1})\leq\epsilon_{0},\quad\int_{M}|\mathrm{Rm}_{g_{1}}|^{2}\mathrm{dvol}_{g_{1}}\leq 1

for some constant
\epsilon_{0}>0
,
then the rescaled metric
\tilde{g}=\epsilon_{0}^{-1}g_{1}
satisfies

(8)

\displaystyle m(\tilde{g})\leq\sqrt{\epsilon_{0}},\quad\int_{M}|\mathrm{Rm}_{\tilde{g}}|^{2}\mathrm{dvol}_{\tilde{g}}\leq\sqrt{\epsilon_{0}}.

Hence, to show the main theorem, it is clearly enough to prove that for any
\epsilon>0
, the desired statement holds if the metric satisfies the upper bounds (
8
) for some
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(\epsilon)
small enough.

Thus, fix
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, let
\epsilon_{0}>0
which will be fixed in the proof and which will only depend on
\epsilon
, and assume that
(M,g)
is a complete asymptotically flat
3
-manifold with non-negative scalar curvature, which satisfies for some chosen end:

(9)

\displaystyle m(g)\leq\epsilon_{0},\quad\int_{M}|\mathrm{Rm}_{g}|^{2}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}\leq\epsilon_{0}.

In the following, we denote by
C
a universal positive constant, which may change from line to line.

Let us explain why we can assume without loss of generality that the metric
g
is uniformly bounded in the
C^{l}
-topology for any integer
l\geq 0
.
We choose an exhaustion
\Omega_{i}
of
(M,g)
and choose smooth functions
\chi_{i}
taking values in
[0,1]
and supported in
\Omega_{i}
such that
\chi_{i}\to 1
and
\|\nabla\chi_{i}\|_{\infty}\to 0
locally uniformly. By the uniform lower bound on the harmonic radius in Corollary
3.5
and Remark
2.2
, we know that the weak injectivity radius satisfies a uniform lower bound
\rho(\delta_{0},M)\geq\rho_{0}
for some uniform
0<\delta_{0}<1
and
\rho_{0}>0
. By Remark
2.3
, an
(A_{0},B_{0})
-Sobolev inequality holds on
M
for uniform
A_{0},B_{0}>0
. By Theorem
2.3
, we have a sequence of local Ricci flows
g_{i}(t)
satisfying
\frac{\partial g_{i}(t)}{\partial t}=-2\chi_{i}^{2}\mathrm{Ric}_{g_{i}(t)}
and
g_{i}(0)=g
, all of which exist for a uniform time
T_{0}>0
. Since
(M,g)
is asymptotically flat, in particular having bounded geometry depending on the metric
g
, by the curvature estimate in Theorem
2.3
, we know that
g_{i}(t)
has bounded curvature for a short time, depending on the initial metric
g
while independent of
i
. By Hamilton’s compactness theorem
[
27
]
, up to a subsequence,
g_{i}(t)\to g(t)
locally smoothly for a Ricci flow
g(t)
,
0\leq t\leq T_{0}
on
M
with
g(0)=g
. By the standard uniqueness result for Ricci flow with bounded curvature,
g(t)
agrees with the classical Ricci flow starting from
g
.
By the uniform estimates in Theorem
2.3
, we have for any
t\in[0,T_{0}]
:

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\mathrm{Rm}_{g(t)}|^{2}(t)\mathrm{dvol}_{g(t)}

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\int_{M}|\mathrm{Rm}_{g}|^{2}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}\leq C\cdot\epsilon_{0},

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{Rm}_{g(t)}\|_{C^{0}(M)}

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot t^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|\mathrm{Rm}_{g}\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

Note that
\int_{0}^{T_{0}}t^{-\frac{3}{4}}dt<\infty
so using the curvature estimate above and the Ricci flow equation, we also have
e^{-C\sqrt{\epsilon_{0}}t^{\beta}}g\leq g(t)\leq e^{C\sqrt{\epsilon_{0}}t^{\beta}}g
for some
\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
.
Set
h:=g(T_{0})
. Then

(10)

\displaystyle e^{-C\sqrt{\epsilon_{0}}}g\leq h\leq e^{C\sqrt{\epsilon_{0}}}g,\ \ \int_{M}|\mathrm{Rm}_{h}|^{2}\leq C\cdot\epsilon_{0}.

By Shi’s estimates
[
39
]
, we have for any integer
l\geq 0
:

\displaystyle|\nabla^{l}\mathrm{Rm}_{h}|\leq C(l).

Moreover, it is well-known that
h
is asymptotically flat with the same mass
m(h)=m(g)\leq\epsilon_{0}
and
R_{h}>0
(cf.
[
36
, Section 2]
).
Since
g
and
h
are
\epsilon
-bilipschitz by (
10
) if
\epsilon_{0}
is small enough, we only need to prove the theorem for
h
instead of
g
.
This finishes the explanation of why
g
can assumed to be uniformly
C^{l}
-bounded for our purpose.

In the remaining of the proof, we will thus assume that
(M,g)
is as in (
9
), and additionally that the metric
g
is uniformly bounded in the
C^{l}
-topology for any integer
l\geq 0
.
Our goal is to show that
g
is
\epsilon
-bilipschitz to the flat
\mathbb{R}^{3}
if
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(\epsilon)
is small enough.

We first notice that
M=M_{ext}
. Otherwise,
\partial M_{ext}\neq\emptyset
consists of minimal
2
-spheres
\Sigma
. By the Gauss-Codazzi equation and the uniform curvature bound,

(11)

\displaystyle K_{\Sigma}=\frac{1}{2}R_{g}-\mathrm{Ric}_{g}(\nu,\nu)-\frac{1}{2}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\leq C.

By the famous Penrose inequality
[
31
,
12
]
, we know that

\mathrm{Area}_{g}(\Sigma)\leq 16\pi\cdot m(g)^{2}\leq C\cdot\epsilon_{0}^{2}.

By the Gauss-Bonnet theorem, and the fact that
\Sigma
consists of
2
-spheres, together with (
11
), we obtain

\displaystyle 4\pi\leq\int_{\Sigma}K_{\Sigma}\leq C\cdot\mathrm{Area}_{g}(\Sigma)\leq C\cdot\epsilon_{0}^{2},

a contradiction for all small enough
\epsilon_{0}
.

Recall by Theorem
2.4
that
we have a good region

E\subset M

and a
globally well-defined harmonic map

\mathbf{u}=(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3}):M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Let
k\in\{1,2,3\}
.
By the mass inequality (
[
11
, Theorem 1.2]
), we have

(12)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla^{2}u^{k}|^{2}}{|\nabla u^{k}|}\leq C\cdot\epsilon_{0}.

Let
2r_{0}\in[0,1]
be equal to the minimum of
1
and the infimum of the
W^{2,2}
-harmonic radius on
(M,g)
(see Definition
2.1
). By Corollary
3.5
,
2r_{0}
is lower bounded by a uniform strictly positive number independent of
(M,g)
as long as
\epsilon_{0}
is small enough.
For any
x\in M
, set
\tilde{u}^{k}:=u^{k}-\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}u^{k}
. Then using the uniform lower bound on
r_{0}
, we have in some harmonic coordinates in
B_{g}(x,2r_{0})
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}\tilde{u}^{k}=g^{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{u}^{k}=0,

and

C^{-1}\delta_{ij}\leq g^{ij}\leq C\delta_{ij}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\partial^{l}g^{ij}\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq C(l),

where the constant
C(l)
depends only on the integer
l\geq 0
. By the Poincaré inequality,

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\tilde{u}^{k}|\leq Cr_{0}\cdot\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla\tilde{u}^{k}|=C\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|,

where we used in the last inequality that
r_{0}\leq 1
. By Moser’s iteration argument, we have

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{g}(x,\frac{7}{4}r_{0}))}\leq C\cdot\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|.

By Schauder’s estimate, we have

(13)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}^{k}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(B_{g}(x,\frac{3}{2}r_{0}))}\leq C\cdot\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|.

Since the Ricci curvature of
g
is uniformly bounded thanks to the uniform
C^{l}
-bound on
g
, the Bishop inequality yields a uniform upper bound on the relative volume ratio. Together with the Poincaré inequality and the mass inequality (
12
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\fint_{B_{g}(x,r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|-\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|\right|

\displaystyle\ \ \leq\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,2r_{0}))}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,r_{0}))}\cdot\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}\left||\nabla u^{k}|-\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|\right|

\displaystyle\ \ \leq C\cdot\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla|\nabla u^{k}||

\displaystyle\ \ \leq C\left(\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}\frac{|\nabla|\nabla u^{k}||^{2}}{|\nabla u^{k}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\ \ \leq C\cdot\epsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which implies the inequality

(14)

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\fint_{B_{g}(x,r_{0})}(|\nabla u^{k}|+1).

Indeed, if
\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|\leq 1
then (
14
) is clearly true.
If
\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|\geq 1
, then
\left(\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|
, which together with the above inequality implies that

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|\leq\fint_{B_{g}(x,r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|+C\epsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|,

thus by taking
\epsilon_{0}\ll 1
so that
C\epsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
,

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{g}(x,2r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|\leq 2\fint_{B_{g}(x,r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|,

which gives (
14
) too.

Next, combining (
13
) and (
14
), we have

(15)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}^{k}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(B_{g}(x,\frac{3}{2}r_{0}))}\leq C\cdot\fint_{B_{g}(x,r_{0})}(|\nabla u^{k}|+1).

We claim that there exists a small enough
\epsilon_{0}(\epsilon)
depending only on
\epsilon
such that for all
\epsilon_{0}\leq\epsilon_{0}(\epsilon)
, the following holds:

(16)

\displaystyle\text{if $\sup_{B_{g}(x,r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|\leq 1+\epsilon$, then $\sup_{B_{g}(x,r_{0})}|\nabla u^{k}|\leq 1+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon$}.

To check this, we notice that the assumption in (
16
) together with (
15
) implies that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}^{k}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(B_{g}(x,\frac{3}{2}r_{0}))}\leq C.

In particular,

(17)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}u^{k}\|_{C^{0}(B_{g}(x,\frac{3}{2}r_{0}))}=\|\nabla^{2}\tilde{u}^{k}\|_{C^{0}(B_{g}(x,\frac{3}{2}r_{0}))}\leq C.

Choose
0<\delta_{2}\ll\delta_{1}\ll\epsilon<1
and
\delta_{1}<\frac{1}{2}r_{0}
. Recall that by Lemma
3.4
, for all small enough
\epsilon_{0}
, and all
x\in M
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,2r_{0})\setminus E)\leq C\cdot\delta_{2},

where
E
is the good region in
M
.

Consider any point
z\in B_{g}(x,r_{0})
. Then
B_{g}(z,\delta_{1})\subset B_{g}(x,\frac{3}{2}r_{0})
. Using the assumption in (
16
), (
17
) and bullet (2) in Theorem
2.4
, we have for
\epsilon_{0}
small enough:

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{g}(z,\delta_{1})}|\nabla u^{k}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(z,\delta_{1}))}\int_{B_{g}(z,\delta_{1})\cap E}|\nabla u^{k}|+C\delta_{1}^{-3}\cdot\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(z,\delta_{1})\setminus E)

\displaystyle\leq 1+\delta_{2}+C\cdot\delta_{1}^{-3}\delta_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\delta_{1}.

So there exists
z^{\prime}\in B_{g}(z,\delta_{1})
such that

|\nabla u^{k}|(z^{\prime})\leq 1+\delta_{1}
. By the uniform Hessian estimate (
17
), we have
|\nabla u^{k}|(z)\leq|\nabla u^{k}|(z^{\prime})+C\cdot d(z,z^{\prime})\leq 1+\delta_{1}+C\delta_{1}\leq 1+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon
, which implies the conclusion of the claim (
16
).

By this improvement property (
16
) and a continuity argument, we obtain that
|\nabla u^{k}|(x)\leq 1+\epsilon
holds for all
x\in M
. Besides, (
17
) shows that
|\nabla^{2}u^{k}|(x)\leq C
for all
x\in M
. Similarly, if
\epsilon_{0}
is small enough, for all
x\in M
, using bullet (2) in Theorem
2.4
again, we have

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{g}(x,\delta_{1})}|\langle\nabla u^{i},\nabla u^{j}\rangle_{g}-\delta_{ij}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,\delta_{1}))}\int_{B_{g}(x,\delta_{1})\cap E}|\langle\nabla u^{i},\nabla u^{j}\rangle_{g}-\delta_{ij}|

\displaystyle\ \ +C\delta_{1}^{-3}\cdot\mathrm{Vol}_{g}(B_{g}(x,\delta_{1})\setminus E)

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{1},

which together with the uniform Hessian estimate implies that for all
x\in M
,

(18)

\displaystyle|\langle\nabla u^{i},\nabla u^{j}\rangle_{g}(x)-\delta_{ij}|\leq C\delta_{1}\ll 1.

Thus
\mathbf{u}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is globally nondegenerate.

Since
\mathbf{u}
is one-to-one from the end of
M
to the end of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, by a degree argument,
\mathbf{u}
must be a diffeomorphism. Moreover, by (
18
),
\mathbf{u}
is
\epsilon
-bilipschitz whenever
\delta_{1}
is small enough depending only on
\epsilon
.
We have thus proved that for any
\epsilon>0
, for all small enough
\epsilon_{0}\leq\epsilon_{0}(\epsilon)
, any
(M,g)
as in (
9
) is
\epsilon
-bilipschitz to the flat
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

∎
