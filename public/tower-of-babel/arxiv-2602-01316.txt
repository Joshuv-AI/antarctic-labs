Ordnung muss sein
Henning Krause

Abstract
. For any length category, we establish a set of rules (necessary and sufficient) that ensure a partial order on the isomorphism classes of simple objects such that the category is equivalent to the category of finite dimensional representations of this partially ordered set.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
16G20 (primary); 18E10 (secondary)

1.
Introduction

A
length category
is an abelian category such that any chain of
subobjects for a given object is finite and the isomorphism classes of
objects form a set
[
8
]
. There are two basic invariants: the

centre
, which is the ring of natural transformations from the
identity functor to itself, and the
ext-quiver
, for which the
vertices are given by the isomorphism classes of simple objects, and
there is an arrow
[S]\to[T]
if
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(T,S)\neq 0
. The following
result describes a class of length categories that are completely
determined by these invariants.
1
1

1

Following the title of the
paper, we may refer to conditions (1)–(3) as ‘Hausordnung’, cf.
https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausmeister_Krause_-_Ordnung_muss_sein
.

For an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
we write
Z(\mathcal{A})
for its centre and say
that an endomorphism of an object
X
is
central
if it lies in
the image of the canonical map
Z(\mathcal{A})\to\operatorname{End}(X)
. The category
\mathcal{A}
is

connected
if
0
and
1
are the only idempotents in

Z(\mathcal{A})
.

Theorem 1
.

A connected length category is equivalent to the category of finite dimensional
representations of a finite poset over a field if and only if the following
hold:

(1)

The ext-quiver has no oriented cycle.

(2)

For each simple object all its endomorphisms are central.

(3)

There exists an object
M
with a distributive lattice of
subobjects such that each object is a subquotient of a finite
direct sum of copies of
M
.

In this case the field equals the centre of the category and the poset is
given by the isomorphism classes of simple objects, with
[S]\leq[T]

if there is a path from
[S]
to
[T]
in the ext-quiver.

There is a long tradition of studying linear representations of posets, and
we refer to the monograph of Simson for a detailed account

[
11
]
. Intimately related is the study of incidence algebras
and their modules. In fact, the question when a finite dimensional
algebra is an incidence algebra has been investigated by several
authors
[
2
,
6
,
10
]
. In particular, the relevance of
distributive modules has been noticed, but the more general context of
length categories seems to be new.

Categories of linear representations of finite posets over a field are
nothing but categories of sheaves on finite
T_{0}
-spaces with values
in the category of vector spaces; see Remark
14
. This
yields another perspective on the class of length categories arising
in Theorem
1
, which is reminiscent of Gabriel’s
reconstruction of a noetherian scheme from its category of
quasi-coherent sheaves
[
7
, VI]
.

2.
The proof

Distributive objects

Fix a length category. For any object
M
and a simple object

S
let
[M:S]
denote the multiplicity of
S
in a composition series
of
M
. The object
M
is
multiplicity free
if
[M:S]\leq 1

for every simple object
S
.

Recall that an object has a distributive lattice of subobjects if and
only if there is no simple object
S
such that
S\oplus S
arises as
a subquotient; see for instance the first exercise in

[
3
]
. Objects with this property are called

distributive
.

Lemma 2
.

Every multiplicity free object is distributive. The converse holds
when the ext-quiver has no oriented cycles.

Proof.

The first assertion is clear, given the characterisation of
distributive objects. Now fix an object
M
and set

M^{i}:=\operatorname{rad}^{i}M
for
i\geq 0
. Then
M^{r}=0
for some
r\geq 0
, and
this yields a filtration

0=M^{r}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq M^{1}\subseteq M^{0}=M

with
semisimple factors. For any pair of simple objects

S\subseteq M^{i}/M^{i+1}
and
T\subseteq M^{i-1}/M^{i}
there exists
an arrow
[S]\to[T]
in the ext-quiver, which comes from the
extension

0\longrightarrow M^{i}/M^{i+1}\longrightarrow M^{i-1}/M^{i+1}\longrightarrow M^{i-1}/M^{i}\longrightarrow 0.

If
[M:S]>1
for a simple object
S
, then
S
arises in

M^{i}/M^{i+1}
and
M^{j}/M^{j+1}
for some pair
i,j
. When
M

is distributive, then
i\neq j
. Thus there exists a cycle in the ext-quiver.
∎

Module categories

We fix a length category
\mathcal{A}
and recall from
[
4
]
a simple
criterion for
\mathcal{A}
to be a category of finite length modules. Let
\mathbb{K}
be a
commutative ring and suppose that
\mathcal{A}
is
\mathbb{K}
-linear
, so the
morphism sets are
\mathbb{K}
-modules and the composition maps are
bilinear. Then
\mathcal{A}
is called
hom-finite
if
\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)
is a

\mathbb{K}
-module of finite length for each pair of objects
X,Y
.

For any ring
A
let
\operatorname{mod}A
denote the category of
finitely
presented right
A
-modules
.

Proposition 3
.

A hom-finite length category is equivalent to
\operatorname{mod}A
for some Artin
algebra
A
if and only if there exists an object
M
such that each
object is a subquotient of a finite direct sum of copies of

M
.

Proof.

See Proposition 2.14 in
[
4
]
.
∎

Example 4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a length category and view it as
\mathbb{K}
-linear category
for
\mathbb{K}=Z(\mathcal{A})
. Suppose that for each simple object all its
endomorphisms are central. Then
\mathcal{A}
is hom-finite. More precisely,
for each pair of objects
X,Y
in
\mathcal{A}
we have

\ell_{\mathbb{K}}(\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y))\leq\ell(X)\cdot\ell(Y).

Representations of posets

Fix a finite poset
P
and a field
\mathbb{K}
. It is
convenient to view
P
as a category: the objects are the elements of

P
and there is a unique morphism
x\to y
if and only if
x\leq y
. We write

\operatorname{rep}(P,\mathbb{K})
for the category of
contravariant
representations
, so

\operatorname{rep}(P,\mathbb{K}):=\operatorname{Fun}(P^{\mathrm{op}},\operatorname{mod}\mathbb{K}).

Let
\mathbb{K}P
denote the
incidence algebra
which is a

\mathbb{K}
-linear space with basis given by elements
a_{xy}
for any pair

x\leq y
in
P
and multiplication induced by

(1)

a_{xy}a_{vw}:=\delta_{wx}a_{vy}.

For
M
in
\operatorname{rep}(P,\mathbb{K})
there is a canonical right
action of
\mathbb{K}P
on
\bigoplus_{x\in P}M(x)
.

Lemma 5
.

The assignment
M\mapsto\bigoplus_{x\in P}M(x)
induces an
equivalence

\operatorname{rep}(P,\mathbb{K})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{mod}\mathbb{K}P.

Proof.

Taking a
\mathbb{K}P
-module
N
to the representation of
P
given by

x\mapsto N(x):=Na_{xx}

and for
x\leq y
the linear map
N(y)\to N(x)
given by
right multiplication with
a_{xy}
yields a quasi-inverse.
∎

Representations of algebras

We recall a few basic facts about modules over finite dimensional algebras. Let

A
be a right Artinian ring and fix a decomposition
1=\sum_{x\in P}e_{x}
into
pairwise orthogonal and primitive idempotents. We may assume that
A

is basic and connected. Thus the
indecomposable projective modules are of the form
e_{x}A
for some
index
x
, and

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(e_{x}A,M)=Me_{x}

for any
A
-module
M
.
Let
J=J(A)
denote the Jacobson radical of
A
. Then the
simple modules are of the form
S_{x}:=e_{x}(A/J)
for some index

x
. Set

E_{x}:=\operatorname{End}_{A}(S_{x})\cong e_{x}(A/J)e_{x}.

The following lemma is the basic tool that relates the ext-quiver of

\operatorname{mod}A
to the structure of the algebra
A
.

Lemma 6
.

There is an an isomorphism of
E_{y}
-
E_{x}
-bimodules

\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{1}(S_{x},S_{y})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(e_{x}(J/J^{2}),S_{y})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{E_{y}}(e_{x}(J/J^{2})e_{y},E_{y}).

Proof.

See for example Proposition 2.4.3 in
[
3
]
.
∎

Now assume that the ext-quiver of
\operatorname{mod}A
is acyclic. Then

\operatorname{End}_{A}(e_{x}A)\cong E_{x}
for each
x
by the above lemma. For any

A
-module
M
of finite length, an induction on its length shows that
the multiplicity
of
S_{x}
in a composition series is given by

[M:S_{x}]=\operatorname{dim}_{E_{x}}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(e_{x}A,M).

Lemma 7
.

Let
A
be a right artinian ring such that the ext-quiver of

\operatorname{mod}A
is connected and acyclic. Then the following are
equivalent.

(1)

For each simple
A
-module all its endomorphisms are central.

(2)

The centre
Z(A)
of
A
is a field and isomorphic to the
endomorphism ring of each simple
A
-module.

Proof.

One direction is clear. Thus assume that (1) holds. We may identify

Z(A)=Z(\operatorname{mod}A)
. For any index
x
let
I_{x}
denote the kernel of
the canonical homomorphism
Z(A)\to E_{x}
. Then
Z(A)/I\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}E_{x}

since we assume that all elements of
E_{x}
are central. For each
pair
x,y
the action of
Z(A)
on

e_{y}Ae_{x}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(e_{x}A,e_{y}A)

factors through
E_{x}
and
through
E_{y}
, since the ext-quiver is acyclic. Thus
I_{x}=I_{y}
when

e_{y}Ae_{x}\neq 0
. Because
A
is connected it follows that

I_{x}=I_{y}
for all
x,y
. Finally,
I_{x}=0
for all
x
since
I_{x}

annihilates all elements of
A
.
∎

From now on assume that for each simple
A
-module all its
endomorphisms are central and set
\mathbb{K}:=Z(A)
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
M
be an
A
-module such that
[M:S_{x}]=1
and
e_{x}A
is a
subquotient of
M^{r}
for some
r\geq 1
. Then every non-zero morphism

e_{x}A\to M
is a monomorphism.

Proof.

The assumption yields a monomorphism
\phi\colon e_{x}A\to M^{r}
for
some
r\geq 1
. Choose a non-zero morphism
\psi\colon e_{x}A\to M
. Then there are
\lambda_{i}\in E_{x}
such that
\phi_{i}=\psi\lambda_{i}

for
1\leq i\leq r
. We have

\operatorname{Ker}\psi=\bigcap_{i}\operatorname{Ker}\psi\lambda_{i}=\operatorname{Ker}\phi=0

where the first
equality uses that the
\lambda_{i}
are central.
∎

Now fix an
A
-module
M
such that
[M:S_{x}]=1
for all
x
, and we
assume that every non-zero morphism
e_{x}A\to M
is a
monomorphism. Then
e_{x}A
is multiplicity free for all
x

and there is a partial order on the
set
P
given by

x\leq y\quad:\iff\quad e_{y}Ae_{x}\neq 0.

Choose a
\mathbb{K}
-basis
of
M
that consists of elements
m_{x}\in Me_{x}
.
This choice determines a
\mathbb{K}
-basis of
A
consisting of elements

e_{xy}\in e_{y}Ae_{x}
such that for all
x\leq y

m_{y}e_{xy}=m_{x}.

For
x\leq y\leq z
the equality

m_{z}(e_{yz}e_{xy})=(m_{z}e_{yz})e_{xy}

implies

(2)

e_{yz}e_{xy}=e_{xz}.

We are ready to compare
A
with the incidence algebra
\mathbb{K}P
that
is given by the poset coming from the ext-quiver of
\operatorname{mod}A
. I am
grateful to Dieter Vossieck for suggesting a simplification of this argument.

Lemma 9
.

The
\mathbb{K}
-linear map
A\to\mathbb{K}P
given by the assignment

e_{xy}\mapsto a_{xy}
for any
x\leq y
is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{K}
-algebras.

Proof.

The map is an isomorphism of vector spaces; it is multiplicative
because of the identities (
1
) and (
2
).
∎

Proof of the main theorem

Recall that the
Hasse diagram
of a poset
P
is the quiver
with vertex set
P
and an arrow
x\to y
if
x<y
and there is no
z

with
x<z<y
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a length category. Suppose first that
\mathcal{A}
is equivalent
to
\operatorname{rep}(P,\mathbb{K})
for some finite poset
P
and a field
\mathbb{K}
. The
simple representations identify with the elements of

P
. Specifically, for
x\in P
the simple representation
S_{x}
is
given by
S_{x}(y)=\delta_{xy}\mathbb{K}
for
y\in P
. In particular, it
satisfies
\operatorname{End}(S_{x})\cong\mathbb{K}
. The ext-quiver identifies with the
Hasse diagram of
P
. Thus there are no oriented cycles. Now
consider the representation
M
given by
M(x)=\mathbb{K}
and

M(y)\to M(x)
the identity map for all
x\leq y
. Then
[M:S_{x}]=1

for all
x
, and therefore the lattice of subobjects is
distributive. The subrepresentation
M_{x}\subseteq M
satisfying

M_{x}(y)\neq 0
if and only if
y\leq x
yields a projective cover of

S_{x}
. Thus every indecomposable projective representation embeds
into
M
, and each object is a subquotient of
M^{r}
for some

r\geq 1
.

Now suppose that conditions (1)–(3) hold for
\mathcal{A}
. It follows from
Proposition
3
that
\mathcal{A}
is equivalent to

\operatorname{mod}A
for some Artin algebra
A
. More precisely, condition (2)
implies that
\mathcal{A}
is hom-finite; see Example
4
.
Then condition (3) implies that
\mathcal{A}
admits a projective generator

Q
such that
\operatorname{Hom}(Q,-)
induces an equivalence
\mathcal{A}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{mod}A
for

A:=\operatorname{End}(Q)
. Next observe that
\mathbb{K}:=Z(\mathcal{A})
is a field by
Lemma
7
. Here we need to assume that
\mathcal{A}
is
connected. Using Lemmas
2
and
8
, the
existence of a distributive object implies that Lemma
9

can be applied. Thus
A\cong\mathbb{K}P
for the poset
P
given by the
isomorphism classes of simple
A
-modules, and the equivalence with

\operatorname{rep}(P,\mathbb{K})
follows from Lemma
5
.
∎

3.
Remarks

We conclude with several comments on the main theorem.

Remark 10
.

Consider a length category
\mathcal{A}=\operatorname{rep}(P,\mathbb{K})
as in
Theorem
1
. The distributive object
M
arising in
condition (3) is not necessarily unique. In fact, such objects are
parameterised by
2
-cocycles
\mu=(\mu_{xy})
in

H^{*}(\Sigma(P),\mathbb{K}^{\times})
, where
\Sigma(P)
denotes the
simplicial complex associated with
P
and for
x\leq y
the map

M(y)\to M(x)
sends the basis element
m_{y}
to
m_{x}\mu_{xy}
; see

[
9
,
10
]
.

Remark 11
.

In Theorem
1
condition (1) is needed. Let

\mathcal{A}=\operatorname{mod}\mathbb{K}[\varepsilon]
where
\mathbb{K}[\varepsilon]
denotes the algebra of duals
numbers over a field. This is not a category of poset
representations, though conditions (2) and (3) hold. Take for
M

the indecomposable projective module.

The following example is interesting for several reasons, and I am
grateful to Claus Michael Ringel for suggesting it. The example shows
that in Theorem
1
condition (2) is needed. Also, it yields
infinitely many objects
M
of length two such that all objects are a
subquotient of a finite direct sum of copies of
M
.

Example 12
.

Consider the matrix algebra

A=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{C}&\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}_{\sigma}\\
0&\mathbb{C}\end{bmatrix}

where the action of
\mathbb{C}
on
\mathbb{C}_{\sigma}
from the
right is twisted by conjugation; it is a finite dimensional

\mathbb{R}
-algebra. There are infinitely many isomorphism classes of
length two
A
-modules, and all but two are faithful; see the Addendum
of
[
5
]
.

Remark 13
.

In Theorem
1
the assumption on the length category
\mathcal{A}

to be connected can be removed. Then one obtains a finite product

\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{A}_{n}
of connected length categories and
possibly different fields
Z(\mathcal{A}_{1}),\ldots,Z(\mathcal{A}_{n})
.

Remark 14
.

Any partially ordered set
P
carries the structure of a topological
space, by taking as open subsets the downward closed subsets of

P
. This assignment identifies finite posets with finite

T_{0}
-spaces
[
1
, §1]
. Moreover, for any abelian category
\mathcal{C}
the category
of sheaves on
P
with values in
\mathcal{C}
identifies with the category
of representations
P^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{C}
, by taking a sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on

P
to the representation

x\longmapsto\mathscr{F}_{x}=\mathscr{F}(\downarrow\!x)\qquad(x\in P)

where

\downarrow\!x:=\{y\in P\mid y\leq x\}.

Thus Theorem
1
characterises the categories of sheaves on
finite
T_{0}
-spaces with values in the category of finite dimensional
vector spaces over a field. One may compare this with Gabriel’s
reconstruction of a noetherian scheme from its category of
quasi-coherent sheaves
[
7
, VI]
.

Remark 15
.

It would be interesting to have a version of Theorem
1

characterising categories of the form
\operatorname{rep}(P,\mathbb{K})
when
\mathbb{K}
is
a skew field.
