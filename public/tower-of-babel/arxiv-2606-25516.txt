Total Positivity and Symmetric Spaces
Huanchen Bao

Abstract
. We define a notion of total positivity for the symmetric space G / K G/K by taking the Hausdorff closure of the image of Lusztig’s totally positive part G > 0 G_{>0} in G / K G/K . We introduce double Bruhat cells for the symmetric space and define their totally positive pieces. We prove a cell decomposition of the totally nonnegative symmetric space, give explicit positive parametrizations of all cells, establish closure relations, and show that the transition maps between the two natural families of parametrizations are subtraction-free.



1.
Introduction

1.1.

Total positivity began with the classical study of matrices whose minors are all nonnegative or positive. Lusztig
[
LuTotalPositivity
]
extended this theory from matrices to arbitrary split connected reductive groups, using deep positivity properties coming from his theory of canonical bases. One of the key features of Lusztig’s theory is that total positivity is not merely a subset of real points; it comes with a rich cell structure. For
G
itself, the relevant cells are closely related to double Bruhat cells. Lusztig also defined totally nonnegative flag varieties and partial flag varieties. Marsh and Rietsch
[
MarshRietschParametrizationsFlagVarieties
]
gave explicit positive parametrizations of the cells in the full flag variety using positive subexpressions and generalized Chamber Ansatz coordinates, while Rietsch
[
RietschClosureRelationsPartialFlag
]
proved closure relations for totally nonnegative cells in partial flag varieties. The resulting theory of total positivity has become a central object connecting algebraic groups, canonical bases, cluster structures, Poisson geometry, and combinatorics.

1.2.

The purpose of this paper is to develop the theory of total positivity for symmetric spaces.

Let
G
be a split connected reductive group over
\mathbb{R}
, let
\theta
be an involution of
G
, and let
K=G^{\theta}
be the fixed-point subgroup. We study the symmetric space

\mathcal{S}=G/K.

In the Lusztig theory of total positivity, one needs to fix a pinning for the reductive group
G
, including Borel subgroups
B^{\pm}
. We further require
(B^{+},T)
to be a
\theta
-split pair in the sense of Springer
[
SpringerAlgebraicGroupsWithInvolutions
]
. In particular,
\theta(B^{+})=\dot{w}^{\bullet}B^{+}\dot{w}^{\bullet,-1}
for some Weyl group element
w^{\bullet}
. The involution
\theta
is further normalized using the
\imath
-root datum attached to the symmetric pair; the precise convention is recalled in §
2.1
. One should take this as the pinning data for the symmetric space.

Throughout this paper we restrict to the class of symmetric pairs for which this normalized involution is compatible with the diagram involution, equivalently the parameters in §
2.1
may be chosen so that
\overline{\zeta}_{i}=-1
. This condition excludes a few cases (see §
2.1
for the detailed
list), but it is the natural setting in which the involution is compatible with the positivity structure used below.

1.3.

The central geometric idea of the paper is to introduce the analogue of double Bruhat cells for the symmetric space. We use the embedding

\iota:G/K\longrightarrow G,\qquad gK\longmapsto g\theta(g)^{-1},

which realizes
G/K
as a closed subvariety of
G
. Then, for
u\leq w^{\bullet}
in the Weyl group of
G
, we define

\mathring{\mathcal{S}}_{u}=\iota^{-1}(B^{+}\dot{u}B^{-}\dot{w}^{\bullet})=\iota^{-1}(B^{+}\dot{u}\dot{w}^{\bullet}\theta(B^{-})).

We call the strata
\mathring{\mathcal{S}}_{u}
the double Bruhat cells of the symmetric space. Note that
\mathring{\mathcal{S}}_{u}=\emptyset
if
u\not\leq w^{\bullet}
.

This terminology is not only formal. These strata are motivated by Poisson geometry studied by Evens and Lu
[
EvensLu2006
]
: they are the
T
-leaves for the natural Poisson structure on the symmetric space. Thus they play for
G/K
the role played by double Bruhat cells in
G
and by projected Richardson strata in partial flag varieties. In the special case where the symmetric space is the group itself, this construction recovers the usual double Bruhat cells in
G
. This Poisson-geometric interpretation is one of the main reasons these strata are the correct pieces on which to study total positivity. The relevant Frobenius splitting and cluster structure on the double Bruhat cell shall appear in forthcoming works.

We now define the totally nonnegative symmetric space. Let

\mathcal{S}_{\geq 0}=(G/K)_{\geq 0}:=\overline{G_{>0}K/K},

where the closure is taken in the Hausdorff topology on the real locus of
G/K
. For each
u\leq w^{\bullet}
, define

\mathcal{S}_{u}^{>0}:=\mathcal{S}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{S}}_{u},\qquad\mathcal{S}_{u}^{\geq 0}:=\overline{\mathcal{S}_{u}^{>0}}.

We also define the totally positive symmetric space by

\mathcal{S}_{>0}:=\mathcal{S}_{e}^{>0}.

The indexing set for these cells can be described in two equivalent ways. From the symmetric space side, the strata are indexed by

Q=\{u\in W\mid u\leq w^{\bullet}\}.

The second indexing set is motivated by partial flag varieties and the projected Richardson strata that appear there:

P=\{(v,w)\mid v\in W_{I_{\bullet}},\ w\in W^{I_{\bullet}},\ v\leq w\}.

The two descriptions are related by the map

\phi:P\longrightarrow Q^{\mathrm{opp}},\qquad(v,w)\longmapsto v\theta(w^{-1})w^{\bullet}.

The main combinatorial result of the paper is that this map is an isomorphism of posets; see Proposition
3.2
. Thus the symmetric space stratification is controlled by the same combinatorics that governs projected Richardson strata. The poset
Q
has a natural involution via the inverse map. We denote the induced involution on
P
by
\sigma
.

1.4.

We can now state the main results. Let
(v,w)\in P
, choose a reduced expression
\mathbf{w}
of
w
, and let
\mathbf{v}_{+}
be the positive subexpression of
v
in
\mathbf{w}
in terms of Marsh and Rietsch
[
MarshRietschParametrizationsFlagVarieties
]
. Let
G^{>0}_{\mathbf{v}_{+},\mathbf{w}}
denote the corresponding Marsh–Rietsch positive parameter set. We further define the positive set
H^{>0}_{\mathbf{v}_{+},\mathbf{w}}
as the positive-root analogue of the Marsh–Rietsch set
G^{>0}_{\mathbf{v}_{+},\mathbf{w}}
; it is defined precisely in §2.4 by applying the Chevalley involution to the Marsh–Rietsch parameter set. Let
\overline{T}=T/T^{\theta}
be the quotient torus
and let
\overline{T}_{>0}
be its totally positive part.

Theorem 1

(Theorem
3.5
)
.

(1)

The space
\mathcal{S}_{\geq 0}
is entirely contained in both the open
B^{+}
-orbit and the open
B^{-}
-orbit of
G/K
, that is,

\mathcal{S}_{\geq 0}\subset(B^{-}K/K)\cap(B^{+}K/K).

(2)

For every
(v,w)\in P
, there is an isomorphism of semi-algebraic varieties

G^{>0}_{\mathbf{v}_{+},\mathbf{w}}\times\overline{T}_{>0}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{S}_{\phi(v,w)}^{>0}.

(3)

For every
(v,w)\in P
, there is an isomorphism of semi-algebraic varieties

H^{>0}_{\mathbf{v}_{+},\mathbf{w}}\times\overline{T}_{>0}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{S}_{\phi(v,w)^{-1}}^{>0}.

(4)

The space
\mathcal{S}_{\geq 0}
admits the cell decomposition

\mathcal{S}_{\geq 0}=\bigsqcup_{u\in Q}\mathcal{S}_{u}^{>0}.

(5)

We have the closure relations

\overline{\mathcal{S}_{u}^{>0}}=\bigsqcup_{u\leq u^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}_{u^{\prime}}^{>0}.

(6)

Let
u\in Q
. The cell
\mathcal{S}_{u}^{>0}
is a connected component of
\mathring{\mathcal{S}}_{u}(\mathbb{R})
.

(7)

Let
(v,w)\in P
and write
\sigma(v,w)=(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})
. After identifying the quotient torus
\overline{T}_{>0}
with positive real coordinates, the transition map

\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\dim\overline{T}+\ell(w)-\ell(v)}\rightarrow G^{>0}_{\mathbf{v}_{+},\mathbf{w}}\times\overline{T}_{>0}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{\phi(v,w)}^{>0}\rightarrow H^{>0}_{\mathbf{v}^{\prime}_{+},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}\times\overline{T}_{>0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\dim\overline{T}+\ell(w)-\ell(v)}

is subtraction-free.

(8)

The total nonnegative symmetric space
\mathcal{S}_{\geq 0}
is contractible.

1.5.

Lusztig
[
LusztigTotalPositivitySymmetricSpaces
]
has already introduced a notion of total positivity for symmetric spaces. Our approach is different. First, our pinning is based on a split pair
(B^{+},T)
, which is opposite to Lusztig’s choice of a fundamental pair. This leads to different definitions of totally nonnegative symmetric spaces. Second, we use an entirely different stratification: the double Bruhat cell stratification motivated by Poisson geometry. This leads to a particularly simple cell decomposition, closure order, and subtraction-free transition functions. In particular, after passing to the quotient torus, the transition functions are subtraction-free without taking square roots.

1.6.

The paper is organized as follows. In §2 we recall the necessary background on symmetric spaces, the embedding
G/K\hookrightarrow G
, Lusztig total positivity, and Marsh–Rietsch parametrizations. In §3.1 we prove the poset isomorphism between the symmetric space indexing set and the projected Richardson indexing set. In §3.2 we define the totally nonnegative symmetric space and state the main theorem. In §3.3 we carry out the rank-one calculations. In §3.4 we introduce a special regular function used to prove that
\mathcal{S}_{\geq 0}
lies in the open
B^{\pm}K/K
charts. This also leads to a new proof of the core geometric structure of
G_{\geq 0}
bypassing Lusztig’s theory of canonical bases. Finally, §3.5 proves the main theorem.

Algebraic groups, involutions,
\imath

Let

(Y,X,\{\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\}_{i\in I},\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in I},\theta)\text{ of type $(I=I_{\bullet}\sqcup I_{\circ},\tau)$.}

[

*§2.4.2, we have

We record automorphisms that will appear repeatedly. First, we write

for the Chevalley involution attached to the chosen pinning; it is characterized by

\omega(x_{i}(a))=y_{i}(a),\qquad\omega(y_{i}(a))=x_{i}(a),\qquad\omega(t)=t^{-1}\quad(t\in T).

Second, after choosing representatives
\dot{w}_{0},\dot{w}_{\bullet}\in N_{G}(T)
, we have
\Int(\dot{w}_{0}):G\to G

\overline{\zeta}_{i}\overline{\zeta}_{\tau(i)}=(-1)^{\langle 2\rho_{\bullet}^{\vee},\alpha_{i}\rangle}.

Here

.
In particular, we define the involution on

\theta=\Int(\dot{w}_{\bullet})\circ\tau\circ\omega\circ\Xi(\overline{\zeta}),

2.3.
Lusztig’s total positivity

We recall Lusztig’s theory of total positivity for a split reductive group with the fixed pinning above
[
LuTotalPositivity
]
*§§2.2–2.12. Let
T_{>0}:=\langle\chi(a)\mid\chi\in Y,\ a\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\rangle\subset T(\mathbb{R})
be the submonoid (with
1
) generated by
\chi(a)
for various
\chi\in Y,\ a\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
For a reduced expression
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{r}}
, set

U^{+}_{w,>0}=\{x_{i_{1}}(a_{1})\cdots x_{i_{r}}(a_{r})\mid a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\}

and

U^{-}_{w,>0}=\{y_{i_{1}}(a_{1})\cdots y_{i_{r}}(a_{r})\mid a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\}.

These subsets are independent of the chosen reduced expression. We write
U^{+}_{>0}:=U^{+}_{w_{0},>0}
and
U^{-}_{>0}:=U^{-}_{w_{0},>0}
. We also write
B^{-}_{>0}=U^{-}_{>0}T_{>0}
and
B^{+}_{>0}=U^{+}_{>0}T_{>0}
.

The totally positive part of
G
is

G_{>0}:=U^{+}_{>0}T_{>0}U^{-}_{>0}=U^{-}_{>0}T_{>0}U^{+}_{>0},

and the totally nonnegative part
G_{\geq 0}
is the submonoid of
G(\mathbb{R})
generated by
T_{>0}
together with all
x_{i}(a)
and
y_{i}(a)
for
i\in I
and
a\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

We shall frequently use the following elementary exchange relations associated with the pinning
[
LuTotalPositivity
]
*§§1.1–1.3. For
t\in T
, one has

tx_{i}(a)=x_{i}(\alpha_{i}(t)a)t,\qquad ty_{i}(a)=y_{i}(\alpha_{i}(t)^{-1}a)t.

For distinct simple roots
i\neq j
, the simple opposite root subgroups commute:

y_{i}(a)x_{j}(c)=x_{j}(c)y_{i}(a).

Finally, in rank one one has the relation

(2.1)

y_{i}(a)\alpha_{i}^{\vee}(b^{-1})x_{i}(c)=x_{i}\!\left(\frac{c}{ac+b^{2}}\right)\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\!\left(\frac{b}{ac+b^{2}}\right)y_{i}\!\left(\frac{a}{ac+b^{2}}\right),

whenever the displayed expressions are defined. In particular, if
a,b,c>0
, then
ac+b^{2}>0
, and all three transformed parameters are again positive.

Recall the Demazure product
\ast

[
BaoHe2022TwistedProduct
]
*§2.6 on the set
W
, determined by the following two rules:

•

x\ast y=xy
if
x,y\in W
such that
\ell(xy)=\ell(x)+\ell(y)
;

•

s_{i}\ast w=w
if

3.
Total Positivity in Symmetric Spaces

3.1.
Two posets attached to the symmetric space

We recall the following proposition from
[

]
*Proposition 4.6. Since
W_{I_{\bullet}}
is finite, the statements are simplified by
[
BaoHe2021
]
*Remark 2.2.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
x,x^{\prime}\in W_{I_{\bullet}}
and
y,y^{\prime}\in W^{I_{\bullet}}
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

w_{\bullet}x^{\prime}(y^{\prime})^{-1}\leq w_{\bullet}xy^{-1}
;

(2)

There exists
u\in W_{I_{\bullet}}
such that
x\leq x^{\prime}u
and
y^{\prime}u\leq y
.

Let
Q=\{u\in W\mid u\leq w_{0}w_{\bullet}\}
, equipped with the Bruhat order inherited from
W
. Let
Q^{\mathrm{opp}}
be the opposite poset of
Q
. Recall the poset
P
in §
2.5
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The map

\phi:P\longrightarrow Q^{\mathrm{opp}},\qquad(v,w)\longmapsto v\theta(w^{-1})w^{\bullet}=vw_{\bullet}\tau(w^{-1})w_{0}=w_{\bullet}\tau(v)\tau(w^{-1})w_{0}

is an isomorphism of posets.

Proof.

We first show the map is well-defined, that is,
v\theta(w^{-1})w^{\bullet}\in Q
. It suffices to show
w_{\bullet}\leq w_{\bullet}\tau(v)\tau(w^{-1})
. Since
v\leq w
and
\tau
is induced from a diagram automorphism, we have
\tau(v)\leq\tau(w)
. Since
\tau(I_{\bullet})=I_{\bullet}
, we have
\tau(v)\in W_{I_{\bullet}}
and
\tau(w)\in W^{I_{\bullet}}
. Now by Proposition
3.1
, we see that
w_{\bullet}\leq w_{\bullet}\tau(v)\tau(w^{-1})
.

We next show the map
P\to Q
is bijective. The injectivity is clear by the parabolic factorization. We show surjectivity here. Let
u\leq w^{\bullet}
. Then
w_{\bullet}\leq uw_{0}=w_{\bullet}(w_{\bullet}uw_{0})
. We have the unique factorization
w_{\bullet}uw_{0}=\tau(u^{\prime\prime})\tau(u^{\prime})^{-1}
for
u^{\prime}\in W^{I_{\bullet}}
and
u^{\prime\prime}\in W_{I_{\bullet}}
. It follows by Proposition
3.1
again that
u^{\prime\prime}\leq u^{\prime}
. In particular,
u=\phi((u^{\prime\prime},u^{\prime}))
with
(u^{\prime\prime},u^{\prime})\in P
. This finishes the surjectivity.

Finally, the map
\phi
respects the partial orderings by Proposition
3.1
. This completes the proof.
∎

The poset
Q
has a natural involution via the inverse map. Under the isomorphism of posets, we have the induced involution

\sigma:P\to P,\qquad(t,s)\mapsto(v,w),\quad\text{where }wv^{-1}=\theta(st^{-1})^{-1}.

3.2.
Totally nonnegative symmetric spaces

Definition 3.3
.

We define the totally nonnegative symmetric space by

\mathcal{S}_{\geq 0}:=\overline{G_{>0}K/K},

where the closure is taken in the Hausdorff topology.

By definition,
\mathcal{S}_{\geq 0}
is contained in the real locus
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
of the symmetric space. We also have the natural action of
G_{\geq 0}
on
\mathcal{S}_{\geq 0}

\mathring{\mathcal{S}}_{u}
from §

\mathcal{S}_{u}^{>0}:=\mathcal{S}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{S}}_{u},\qquad\mathcal{S}_{u}^{\geq 0}:=\overline{\mathcal{S}_{u}^{>0}}.

We also define the totally positive symmetric space to be

\mathcal{S}_{>0}:=\mathcal{S}_{e}^{>0}.

Recall the quotient torus
\overline{T}=T/T^{\theta}
. For any

, we denote by
\overline{t}
its image in
\overline{T}
. We have
\overline{T}\cong TK/K
. Note that the induced map
T(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\overline{T}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}
-points is generally not surjective. We define
\overline{T}_{>0}=\langle\chi(a)|\chi\in\breve{Y},a\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\rangle

Lemma 3.4
.

The quotient map
T_{>0}\rightarrow\overline{T}_{>0}
is surjective.

Proof.

The image of the cocharacter lattice
Y
has finite index in
\breve{Y}
via the quotient map (at most

in our case). On the other hand, for any

∎

We now state the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 3.5
.

(1)

The totally nonnegative symmetric space satisfies

\mathcal{S}_{\geq 0}\subset(B^{-}K/K)\cap(B^{+}K/K).

(2)

Let
(v,w)\in P
. We have an isomorphism of semi-algebraic varieties

G^{>0}_{\mathbf{v}_{+},\mathbf{w}}\times\overline{T}_{>0}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{S}^{>0}_{\phi(v,w)},\quad(g,\overline{t})\mapsto gtK.

(3)

Let
(v,w)\in P
. We have an isomorphism of semi-algebraic varieties

H^{>0}_{\mathbf{v}_{+},\mathbf{w}}\times\overline{T}_{>0}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{S}^{>0}_{\phi(v,w)^{-1}},\quad(g,\overline{t})\mapsto gtK.

(4)

The stratification
\mathcal{S}_{\geq 0}=\sqcup_{u\in Q}\mathcal{S}^{>0}_{u}
is a cell decomposition. In particular, the stratum
\mathcal{S}^{>0}_{u}
is non-empty, and
\mathcal{S}^{>0}_{u}\cong\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\ell(w^{\bullet})-\ell(u)+r}
.

(5)

Let

is given by

\overline{\mathcal{S}_{u}^{>0}}=\bigsqcup_{u\leq u^{\prime}}\mathcal{S}_{u^{\prime}}^{>0}.

In particular, the totally positive part

\mathcal{S}_{\geq 0}

(6)

Let

\mathcal{S}_{u}^{>0}
is a connected component of
\mathring{\mathcal{S}}_{u}(\mathbb{R})
.

(7)

Let
(v,w)\in P
