1.0 Executive Summary
This technical note documents an investigation of periodic structure in SABRS ZEP D3 detector
data spanning February 2022 through April 2026. The analysis was motivated by reports from the
co-located NRL SIRI-2 instrument of a 5-minute periodicity beginning in 2026. This report
presents null results for the 5-minute search and documents an ambiguous 18.6-hour spectral
feature for future reference.

1.1 Key Findings:
The D3 data do not show a significant 5-minute periodicity using the current analysis methods
and significance thresholds. The 5-minute spectral band remains below detection thresholds
across all analyzed years, including the 2026 period when SIRI-2 reported observing their signal.

Initial FFT analysis identified a strong 18.6-hour periodicity in the 2023 dataset. However, follow-
up Lomb-Scargle analysis reveals many peaks of comparable magnitude across the 12-30 hour
period range. The difference between FFT and Lomb-Scargle results suggests that the apparent
prominence of the 18.6-hour feature in the FFT may be influenced by the analysis method,
particularly the window duration and gap structure. Furthermore, year-to-year comparison shows
that different years exhibit different dominant periodicities, which argues against a stable physical
signal. The 2023 dataset showed an 18.6-hour peak, while the 2026 dataset showed a dominant
4.2-hour peak instead.

Band power analysis comparing D3 periodicities with the Kp geomagnetic index shows weak
correlations. In the most complete 2023 dataset with 8,649 samples, Kp explains less than 3% of
the variance in both 5-minute and 1-hour bands at zero lag using Pearson correlation. Even at
the optimal tested lag of 48 hours, the explained variance remains below 6%. The limited 2026
dataset (220 samples) shows higher correlations, but these may reflect the smaller sample size
and different data coverage rather than a fundamental difference in geomagnetic control.

Interpretation: The absence of a D3 5-minute feature, combined with weak geomagnetic
correlations, suggests the SIRI-2 5-minute periodicity is likely instrument-specific rather than a
general radiation-environment signature. The year-to-year inconsistency in D3 spectral features
and the ambiguity introduced by analysis method choices mean the 18.6-hour periodicity should
not be considered a confirmed detection. Other teams may wish to re-examine this feature with
improved gap-handling methods or additional context, but no immediate follow-up is planned
based on current results.

Status: This documents a null result for the 5-minute search and an inconclusive result for longer
periodicities. The work is considered complete for current purposes.

2.0 Background and Motivation

2.1 Scientific Context
The NRL Strontium Iodide Radiation Instrument (SIRI-2) and SABRS ZEP D3 detector are co-
located on the same STPSat-6 spacecraft. Beginning in 2026, SIRI team members reported
observing a 5-minute periodicity in their measurements. Because both instruments sample the
same orbital radiation environment, this observation raised a natural question about whether
SABRS would observe a corresponding spectral feature.

The distinction between environmental and instrumental origins matters for interpretation. If the
periodicity arises from the radiation environment, multiple co-located instruments measuring
particle fluxes should show related spectral features. If the periodicity is specific to SIRI-2's
measurement process or calibration, it may appear only in SIRI-2 data. Additionally, if
geomagnetic activity drives the periodicity through magnetospheric modulation, D3 band power
should show measurable correlation with geomagnetic indices such as Kp.

2.2 Analysis Scope and Objectives
This analysis addresses three primary questions. First, does D3 show evidence of 5-minute
periodicity during any analyzed time period, particularly in 2026 when SIRI-2 reported their
observation? Second, what are the dominant periodicities in D3 data across multiple years, and
do they show year-to-year consistency? Third, do the observed D3 spectral features correlate
with geomagnetic activity as measured by the Kp index?

The analysis does not attempt to directly compare D3 and SIRI-2 time-series data, as SIRI-2
measurements were not available to this team. The work also does not attempt to attribute
detected periodicities to specific physical mechanisms or to perform exhaustive searches across
all possible geophysical drivers. Instead, this report documents what was done and what was
found, leaving more detailed physical interpretation for future work if scientific priority warrants.

3.0 Data and Methods

3.1 D3 Dataset Description
Instrument and Data Type: The analysis uses SABRS ZEP D3 continuous count data at native
sampling cadence. The D3 detector provides measurements of the high-energy particle
environment with approximately 63.9 millisecond temporal resolution, corresponding to a
sampling rate of approximately 15.65 Hz.

Time Coverage: The analysis spans from February 8, 2022 through April 23, 2026. The 2023
full-year dataset provided the most complete coverage with 8,649 hourly samples after gap
removal and processing. The 2026 dataset, covering January through April, yielded only 220
hourly samples, reflecting both the shorter time span and higher data gap fraction.

Data Structure: The raw ZEP data consist of accumulation periods approximately 511
milliseconds in duration. Each accumulation period contains eight sub-measurements. These
were expanded into a continuous flat time series at the native 63.9 millisecond cadence for
spectral analysis. After expansion and quality filtering, the multi-year dataset comprises billions
of individual measurements.

3.1.1 Coverage Quality Assessment:
The 2023 dataset provides the most complete annual coverage with 8,649 hourly samples after
gap removal, representing approximately 99% of the calendar year. This year was selected as
the primary analysis year for spectral and correlation studies.

The 2026 partial-year dataset (January through April 23) yielded only 220 hourly samples,
substantially less than the equivalent period in other years. This reduced sample count reflects
both the shorter observation window and a higher fraction of data gaps during this period. The
2026 correlations (Pearson R² = 25.3% for 1-hour band) should be interpreted cautiously given
the limited sample size compared to the 2023 baseline (n=8,649 vs n=220).

Year-to-year spectral consistency was assessed by comparing dominant periodicities. The 2023
dataset shows an 18.6-hour peak, while the 2026 dataset exhibits a 4.2-hour dominant feature,
suggesting substantial year-to-year variability in the spectral characteristics.

3.2 Gap Handling Strategy
Data gaps pose significant challenges for spectral analysis because treating gaps as zero counts
or interpolating across them can create artificial spectral structure. This analysis employed a
segment-aware approach designed to respect the actual temporal coverage of the data.

The gap detection used two criteria. The primary threshold identified gaps longer than one hour,
which were used to split the data into separate continuous segments. A secondary check
identified breaks in the native sampling cadence exceeding five times the expected 64-millisecond
spacing (approximately 320 milliseconds), which captured shorter instrument interruptions or
telemetry gaps.

Only segments with duration exceeding one hour were retained for FFT analysis. This threshold
was chosen to avoid spectral leakage from boundary effects in very short segments while
retaining sufficient coverage for meaningful statistics. The segment filtering approach avoids
several common pitfalls: it does not treat missing data as real low-count periods, it does not
introduce discontinuities that create spurious spectral peaks at gap boundaries, and it does not
mix segments with very different statistical properties.

After gap filtering and segmentation, the 2023 dataset contained ten continuous segments with
durations ranging from less than one hour to multiple weeks. The longest continuous segment
exceeded 75 days. The analysis used weighted averaging across segments to compute overall
spectral estimates while respecting segment boundaries.

Gap detection used a 1-hour threshold to split continuous segments. Segments shorter than 1
hour were excluded from FFT analysis to avoid boundary effects. The 2023 dataset contains 2
long continuous segments (144 and 222 days), providing excellent conditions for long-period
spectral analysis using Welch's method with 18.6-hour windows.

3.3 FFT Analysis Configuration
The spectral analysis employed Welch's method [1] for power spectral density estimation. Welch's
method divides the time series into overlapping windows, computes individual periodograms for
each window, and averages the results to reduce variance compared to a single periodogram.
The method is well-established for reducing noise in spectral estimates and provides smooth
spectra suitable for peak detection.

For this analysis, the Welch FFT used a window length of 2^20 samples (1,048,576
measurements), which corresponds to approximately 18.6 hours at the 15.65 Hz sampling rate.
The windows overlapped by 75%, meaning three-quarters of each window was shared with
adjacent windows. A Hann window function was applied to reduce spectral leakage from the
window boundaries. Linear detrending removed slow drifts within each window. The analysis
processed each continuous segment separately and then computed a weighted average across
segments to obtain the final power spectral density estimate.

The frequency resolution achieved by this configuration is approximately 1.49×10^-5 Hz,
corresponding to a period resolution of roughly 18 hours at the 18.6-hour timescale. This means
the spectral resolution is marginal for distinguishing closely-spaced long periods. Nearby periods
such as 17, 19, or 20 hours may appear artificially merged in the FFT result. This consideration
motivated the follow-up Lomb-Scargle analysis described below.

3.4 Lomb-Scargle Periodogram Analysis
The Lomb-Scargle periodogram [2] [3] provides an alternative spectral estimation method
specifically designed for unevenly sampled data. Unlike FFT-based methods, Lomb-Scargle uses
actual measurement timestamps directly without requiring interpolation onto a uniform grid. This
approach combines information across disjoint segments without imposing artificial continuity
assumptions and achieves frequency resolution based primarily on the total observation timespan
rather than the length of the longest continuous segment.

For the D3 dataset, these advantages are particularly relevant. The data contain many gaps of
varying durations, the FFT window duration (18.6 hours) is comparable to target signal periods,
and long-period signals in the 12-30 hour range benefit from year-long baselines for improved
frequency resolution. The Lomb-Scargle method also provides an analytical framework for
estimating false-alarm probability, although these probabilities depend on assumptions about
stationarity and noise properties that may not perfectly hold for this dataset.

The native D3 cadence vastly oversamples signals of interest in the range of hours to days. For
an 18.6-hour signal, the target frequency is approximately 1.5×10^-5 Hz, while the native Nyquist
frequency is approximately 7.83 Hz. This means the data are oversampled by more than five
orders of magnitude for the periodicities under investigation. Downsampling by a factor of 100
reduces the effective sampling rate from 15.65 Hz to approximately 0.1565 Hz (6.4-second
cadence) while still maintaining a Nyquist frequency more than 5,000 times higher than the target
frequencies. This downsampling is scientifically valid because all target frequencies remain far
below the downsampled Nyquist frequency, and it reduces computational requirements from
impractical to manageable.

The Lomb-Scargle analysis searched the period range from 12 to 30 hours using 10× frequency
oversampling to improve peak localization. The periodogram was computed using standard

2023 full-year dataset as the most complete available record.

3.5 Geomagnetic Correlation Analysis
The analysis tested whether D3 band power variations correlate with geomagnetic activity using
the planetary K-index (Kp) as a proxy. Kp is a quasi-logarithmic 3-hour index ranging from 0
(quiet) to 9 (extreme storm) that summarizes global geomagnetic disturbance level. OMNI Kp
data were obtained from standard sources and converted to conventional Kp units by dividing the
stored values by 10.

Three complementary correlation measures were computed to capture different aspects of
potential relationships. Pearson correlation (r) tests for linear relationships and provides the
conventional R² statistic representing the fraction of variance explained. Spearman rank
correlation (ρ) tests for monotonic relationships without assuming linearity and is more robust to
outliers and non-normal distributions. Time-lagged correlation tests for delayed response by
computing correlations at lags from -48 hours to +48 hours in 1-hour steps, where positive lag
indicates Kp leads the D3 response.

Band power time series were constructed by dividing the D3 data into 1-hour bins and computing
the integrated power in specific frequency bands for each bin. The analysis tracked three bands:
a 5-minute band (4-6 minute periods, ±20% around the SIRI-2 target period), a 1-hour band (50-
70 minute periods), and an 18.6-hour band (17-20 hour periods). Each hourly D3 power value
was then correlated with the corresponding Kp value or lagged Kp values.

An important caveat is that Kp represents a global 3-hour average disturbance level, while the
spacecraft samples specific local conditions that may vary substantially from global averages.
Low correlation with Kp does not rule out magnetospheric influences on D3 measurements; it only
indicates that Kp is not a strong predictor of D3 band power variations. Testing other geophysical
indices such as Dst, AE, solar wind parameters, or local magnetic field measurements would
require additional analysis beyond the current scope.

4.0 Results: Multi-Year Spectral Comparison

4.1 Overview of Year-to-Year Variability
Spectral analysis was performed independently for each year from 2022 through 2026 to assess
whether detected periodicities represent stable features or transient phenomena. The results
show substantial year-to-year differences in the dominant spectral features, which argues against
a single stable physical signal and suggests contributions from instrumental effects, sampling
variations, or transient environmental conditions.

The 2023 full-year dataset exhibited the strongest 18.6-hour spectral peak, with estimated
significance exceeding 2.5 million sigma in the initial FFT analysis. However, other years showed
different dominant periods. Most notably, the 2026 partial-year dataset showed a dominant peak
at 4.2 hours rather than 18.6 hours, with even higher nominal significance (7.6 million sigma)
despite the much smaller sample size (220 versus 8,649 samples). This dramatic difference in
dominant period between years suggests the apparent periodicities may reflect analysis artifacts,
sampling effects, or year-specific transient phenomena rather than a consistent physical signal.

The partial years (2022 and 2026) have substantially less data coverage and higher gap fractions,
which affects the reliability of spectral estimates. Nevertheless, the qualitative difference in
dominant periods across years provides important context for interpreting the 2023 results.

4.2 Focus on 2026: The 5-Minute Search
The 2026 dataset received particular attention because SIRI-2 reported observing their 5-minute
periodicity beginning in this year. Despite the limited data coverage (February through April 2026),
the analysis included explicit searches for 5-minute spectral features using multiple visualization
approaches.

The FFT-based power spectral density was examined in the frequency range corresponding to 4-
6 minute periods (±20% around the 5-minute target). No peaks were detected above the adopted
6-sigma threshold in this band. The power spectral density at 5 minutes falls well below the levels
observed at longer periods and remains consistent with the background noise level. Comparison
to other spectral features shows the 5-minute region is approximately 40 dB below the dominant
4.2-hour peak observed in this year's data.

Spectrogram analysis was performed to test whether 5-minute power might be transient rather
than persistent throughout the observation period. The spectrogram used 3-hour windows with
75% overlap to provide time-frequency evolution with approximately 45-minute time resolution.
Visual inspection of the spectrogram showed no persistent or transient features in the 5-minute
band during any portion of the 2026 observation window.

The absence of a 5-minute D3 feature in 2026 is the most direct test of whether the SIRI-2
observation corresponds to a general radiation-environment signal. The null result indicates that

4.3 The 2023 Eighteen-Point-Six-Hour Feature
The 2023 full-year dataset provided the most complete coverage for assessing D3 spectral
characteristics. Initial FFT analysis identified a strong peak at 18.61 hours with power of
2.22×10^5 (arbitrary units) and prominence of approximately 1.68×10^5. The nominal
significance estimated from the FFT analysis exceeded 2.5 million sigma, though this extremely
high value raised concerns about the appropriateness of the assumed noise model and the
potential influence of gap structure and window duration on the significance calculation.

Several observations about this feature warrant attention. First, the FFT window duration (18.6
hours) is nearly identical to the detected period, which creates a resonance condition that can
artificially enhance the apparent coherence and significance of features near that timescale.
Second, the high significance estimate assumes Gaussian noise statistics, but radiation count
data often exhibit heavy-tailed distributions that would reduce the effective significance. Third, the

year-to-year comparison showing different dominant periods in different years argues against
interpreting this as a stable physical signal.

Beyond the 18.6-hour peak, the 2023 FFT spectrum shows additional features at much shorter
timescales, including peaks near 0.1 seconds that likely reflect sampling or instrumental effects.
A weak peak at approximately 44.7 minutes reached 150-sigma significance, suggesting some
real spectral structure exists at that timescale as well.

4.4 Lomb-Scargle Analysis: Multiple Comparable Peaks
The Lomb-Scargle periodogram computed for the complete dataset over the 12-30 hour period
range revealed multiple peaks of comparable magnitude rather than a single dominant 18.6-hour
feature. While a peak at 18.6 hours is indeed present, numerous other peaks in the 15-30 hour
range show similar normalized power levels. The peaks are not separated by a large dynamic
range; instead, they span factors of only 2-3 in height.

This spectral structure contrasts with the FFT result, which showed the 18.6-hour feature as
dramatically more prominent than neighboring periods. The difference between the two methods
suggests that the apparent dominance of 18.6 hours in the FFT is at least partly an artifact of the
analysis method. The specific window duration, overlap fraction, and interaction with gap locations
can create apparent coherence at particular periods that may not represent genuine stable
signals.

The Lomb-Scargle result shows peaks distributed across the search range, including features
near 14 hours, 17-21 hours, 24 hours, and 28 hours. Without a uniquely dominant peak, physical
interpretation becomes more challenging. The spectral structure could reflect multiple weak
periodicities from different sources, it could represent aliasing of a single signal through complex
gap patterns, or it could indicate that the entire long-period range reflects noise fluctuations rather
than coherent signals.

4.5 Phase Coherence Testing
To assess whether the 18.6-hour period represents a coherent repeating signal rather than a
statistical fluctuation, the 2023 data were epoch-folded at the detected period. Epoch folding
divides the full time series into intervals matching the candidate period, aligns them by phase (0
to 1 cycle), and computes the mean value in each phase bin. A truly coherent periodic signal
should produce clear phase structure, while noise should produce a relatively flat distribution.

The epoch-folded result shows moderate phase structure with amplitude variations of
approximately 10-15% of the mean count rate. However, the scatter within phase bins is
substantial, and the phase pattern does not show a simple sinusoidal or clearly interpretable
shape. The observed structure is consistent with weak periodic modulation of an intrinsically noisy
signal, or with aliasing effects from multiple nearby periods that are not perfectly resolved.

The moderate phase coherence neither strongly confirms nor rules out a real periodic component.
It indicates some degree of repeatability at the 18.6-hour timescale, but the large scatter and lack
of simple structure suggest the feature is not a strong, stable sinusoidal modulation. The phase
structure could arise from a combination of weak real effects, partial aliasing of multiple periods,
and statistical fluctuations.

5.0 Results: Geomagnetic Correlation Analysis

5.1 2023: The Primary Analysis Year
The 2023 full-year dataset provided 8,649 hourly band-power samples after merging with Kp data
and removing periods with missing values. This large sample size provides good statistical power
for detecting correlations if they exist, though it does not guarantee physical significance for
statistically significant but very weak correlations.

At zero time lag, the Pearson correlation between Kp and the 5-minute band power is r = 0.172,
corresponding to R² = 2.9%. This indicates that Kp explains less than three percent of the variance
in 5-minute band power. The corresponding p-value (4.4×10^-58) confirms this correlation is
statistically significant in the formal sense, but the practical significance is minimal. The Spearman
rank correlation is somewhat higher at ρ = 0.255 (R² = 6.5%), suggesting a weak monotonic but
potentially nonlinear tendency.

The 1-hour band shows similarly weak correlations: Pearson r = 0.158 (R² = 2.5%) and Spearman
ρ = 0.374 (R² = 14.0%). The higher Spearman correlation for the 1-hour band suggests some
rank-order relationship exists even though the linear Pearson correlation is weak. However, even
the best zero-lag correlation (1-hour Spearman) leaves 86% of the variance unexplained.

While these correlations reach statistical significance due to the large sample size, they provide
little practical predictive power. Kp cannot usefully predict D3 band power variations at these
timescales. We also plot 2d histograms and box plots and see very little correlation and nothing
deemed worthwhile to explore further for this work.

5.4 Summary: Weak Geomagnetic Control
Across both years analyzed, Kp shows weak ability to explain or predict D3 band power variations.
The most reliable dataset (2023 with 8,649 samples) consistently shows R² values below 6%
even at optimal lags. The higher correlations in the limited 2026 dataset are not reliable due to
small sample size and reversed causality direction.

The weak Kp correlations have several possible interpretations. First, Kp is a global 3-hour
average index that may not capture local magnetospheric conditions affecting the spacecraft.

Second, D3 may be sensitive to geometric effects (local time, L-shell, attitude) that vary
independently of global Kp. Third, the dominant source of D3 variability may be instrumental,
sampling-related, or driven by factors other than geomagnetic disturbance level. Fourth, other
geophysical indices not tested here (Dst, AE, solar wind parameters) might show stronger
correlations.

However, the consistent finding of weak correlation across multiple bands, multiple lag offsets,
and both parametric and nonparametric correlation methods suggests that geomagnetic activity
as measured by Kp is not the primary driver of the D3 spectral features observed in this analysis.

The correlation analysis reveals that Kp geomagnetic activity is not a strong predictor of D3 band
power variations. The 2023 dataset, with 8,649 hourly samples representing the most complete
and reliable record, consistently shows that Kp explains less than three percent of the variance in
both 5-minute and 1-hour bands when tested at zero lag. The Spearman rank correlations are
somewhat higher than Pearson linear correlations, particularly for the 1-hour band where
Spearman R² reaches 14.0% compared to Pearson's 2.5%. This suggests that any relationship
between Kp and D3 band power may be non-linear or rank-ordered rather than strictly
proportional, but even these stronger rank correlations leave the vast majority of D3 variance
unexplained.

Time-lagged correlation analysis tested whether D3 responds to geomagnetic activity with delays
ranging from 48 hours before to 48 hours after the Kp measurement. The optimal correlations
occurred when Kp led D3 by approximately 48 hours, but the explained variance remained below
six percent for both bands. The 48-hour optimal lag is notably long compared to typical
magnetospheric response timescales, where storm-time particle injection and loss processes
usually operate on timescales from hours to approximately one day. This long lag time raises
questions about whether the correlation represents a genuine physical relationship or reflects
coincidental covariation in noisy data.

The 2026 partial-year dataset shows substantially higher correlations, with Kp explaining 25.3%
of 1-hour band power variance and 10.1% of 5-minute band power variance at zero lag. However,
these results should not be weighted heavily in interpretation for several reasons. First, the 2026
dataset contains only 220 samples compared to 2023's 8,649 samples, making the correlation
estimates much less stable. Second, the optimal lagged correlations for 2026 occur at negative
lags, meaning D3 band power appears to lead Kp rather than follow it, which is physically
implausible if Kp were the driving force. The negative-lag result with R² = 29.3% for the 5-minute
band at -40 hours and R² = 25.8% for the 1-hour band at -2 hours suggests these correlations
likely reflect spurious associations arising from the small sample size, specific storm conditions
during the limited observation window, or confounding by unmeasured variables.

The weak and inconsistent Kp correlations support three interpretations. First, the D3 spectral
features are not primarily controlled by global geomagnetic disturbance as represented by the Kp
index. Second, if magnetospheric effects do influence D3 measurements, they may operate
through local conditions or mechanisms that are not well captured by a global 3-hour average
index. Third, the dominant sources of D3 periodic variability may be geometric, orbital,
instrumental, or related to environmental factors other than storm-time geomagnetic activity.

6.0 The 18.6-Hour Feature

6.1 Potential Physical Origins
The 18.6-hour periodicity represents the dominant spectral feature in the 2023 D3 dataset, yet its
physical origin remains uncertain. Several classes of explanations warrant consideration, ranging
from spacecraft geometry to magnetospheric structure to possible instrumental effects.

Magnetospheric Structure and Dynamics: The magnetosphere exhibits various spatial
structures and temporal variations that could produce periodic signatures in particle
measurements. The spacecraft may cross boundaries between different magnetospheric regions
such as the plasmasphere, plasma sheet, or ring current with a periodicity determined by the
combination of orbital motion and magnetospheric configuration. However no known mechanisms
are 18.6 hours.

Instrumental and Data Processing Effects: Before attributing the 18.6-hour feature to
environmental physics, instrumental origins must be considered. Telemetry scheduling could
introduce periodicities if data downlink opportunities or operational modes cycle with specific
periods. The detector itself may exhibit periodic responses related to temperature variations, high
voltage cycling, or other engineering parameters. Ground processing could introduce artifacts if
data segments are handled with specific time windows or if calibration procedures operate on
particular schedules. The fact that the FFT window duration (~18.6 hours) nearly matches the
detected period is noteworthy and potentially concerning, as this coincidence could enhance the
apparent significance of power near that frequency through resonance effects between the
analysis window and the signal.

Solar Wind and IMF Modulation: Corotating interaction regions in the solar wind typically have
periodicities near 27 days corresponding to solar rotation, but they can produce subharmonics
and modulation of magnetospheric conditions. The interplanetary magnetic field can exhibit sector
structures with periods of days. Particularly during periods of recurring high-speed streams, the
magnetosphere may experience quasi-periodic driving that could project into various periodicities
in the observed particle environment, though explaining a specific 18.6-hour period requires
understanding the detailed coupling.

6.2 Year-to-Year Inconsistency as a Diagnostic
The spectral comparison across years provides crucial context for interpreting the 18.6-hour
feature. The 2023 dataset shows a clear dominant peak at 18.61 hours with exceptionally high
estimated significance (2.5 million sigma in the initial FFT analysis). However, the 2026 dataset
shows a completely different dominant period at 4.19 hours, with even higher nominal significance
(7.6 million sigma) despite having far fewer samples (220 versus 8,649). This dramatic year-to-
year difference argues strongly against the hypothesis that the 18.6-hour feature represents a
stable, persistent physical signal intrinsic to the spacecraft-magnetosphere system.

Several interpretations of this year-to-year variability warrant consideration. First, the periodicities
may reflect transient environmental conditions that differ from year to year depending on solar
wind conditions, magnetospheric configuration, or seasonal effects. Second, the features may
represent analysis artifacts whose apparent period depends on the specific pattern and
distribution of data gaps in each year's dataset. Third, operational or instrumental changes
between years could introduce different periodic signatures. Fourth, the very high nominal

 Intelligence and Space Research Division 2026-04-28

significance values may themselves be artifacts of inappropriate statistical assumptions, with the
true signals being much weaker and more readily masked or altered by noise and sampling
effects.

The year-to-year inconsistency cautions against investing significant effort in physically
interpreting the 18.6-hour feature specifically. If the periodicity were fundamental to the spacecraft
orbit, attitude, or detector configuration, it should appear consistently across multiple years. If it
were driven by stable magnetospheric structure, one would expect similar periods during similar
solar wind and geomagnetic conditions. The observed variability suggests either that the
dominant periodicities are transient features responding to changing external drivers, or that the
apparent periodicities reflect methodological artifacts rather than genuine physical signals.

6.3 Lomb-Scargle Context: Many Comparable Peaks
The Lomb-Scargle periodogram analysis provides important additional context for interpreting the
18.6-hour FFT result. While the Lomb-Scargle periodogram does show power at 18.6 hours,
confirming that this feature is not purely an artifact of FFT window effects, the periodogram reveals
that 18.6 hours is merely one of many peaks with comparable normalized power across the 12-
30 hour search range. Peaks appear at approximately 14 hours, 17-21 hours, 24 hours, and 28
hours with heights differing by only factors of 2-3 rather than orders of magnitude.

This "forest of peaks" structure in the Lomb-Scargle result contrasts with the FFT analysis where
18.6 hours appeared dramatically more prominent than neighboring periods. The difference
between the two methods suggests that the apparent dominance of 18.6 hours in the FFT is
influenced by the specific window duration and overlap configuration used in Welch's method,
particularly the near-resonance between the 18.6-hour window and the detected period. The
Lomb-Scargle method, which combines information across the entire year-long baseline without
imposing fixed window boundaries, shows a more complex picture with multiple periods
contributing comparable power.

The presence of multiple comparable peaks complicates physical interpretation in several ways.
If multiple distinct physical processes each produce periodicities in the 12-30 hour range, they
could interfere constructively or destructively depending on their relative phases, producing a
time-varying spectrum where different periods dominate at different times. Alternatively, a single
underlying quasi-periodic process with variable period or frequency modulation could produce
spectral power distributed across a range of periods. The gap structure in the data could also
create aliases and sidebands of a single true period, spreading its power across multiple nearby
frequencies. Without additional information to distinguish these scenarios, attributing the spectral
structure to specific physical mechanisms remains speculative.

6.4 Phase Coherence Assessment
Epoch folding the 2023 data at the 18.6-hour period tests whether the periodicity represents a
coherent, phase-locked signal or merely a statistical fluctuation in the power spectrum. The
epoch-folded time series shows moderate phase structure, with the mean D3 count rate varying
by approximately 10-15% of the mean across the phase cycle. However, the scatter within
individual phase bins is substantial, and the phase pattern does not exhibit a simple sinusoidal
form.

The moderate phase coherence observed is consistent with several scenarios. A weak but
genuine periodic modulation of an intrinsically noisy particle flux would produce exactly this
signature, with real modulation amplitude partially obscured by statistical fluctuations. Multiple
periodicities with similar periods that are not perfectly resolved by the analysis could partially
cancel or reinforce at different phases, producing irregular phase structure. Aliasing effects from
the complex gap pattern could create apparent phase structure even from noise. A quasi-periodic
process with variable period or amplitude would show phase structure that degrades as data from
different epochs are folded together.

The epoch-folding result neither confirms nor rules out a genuine 18.6-hour periodic component.
What it does show is that if a periodic signal exists at this period, it is not a strong, stable, high-
coherence modulation dominating the count rate. The signal, if real, is weak enough that other
sources of variability contribute comparable power.

6.5 Assessment and Recommended Posture
Given the accumulated evidence, the appropriate scientific posture toward the 18.6-hour
periodicity is one of documented uncertainty. The feature clearly exists in the 2023 FFT spectrum
and appears in the Lomb-Scargle periodogram, so it cannot be dismissed as a pure artifact.
However, multiple factors argue against treating it as a confirmed detection of a physical signal
deserving immediate detailed interpretation.

The year-to-year inconsistency, with different dominant periods in different years, argues against
a stable physical process. The weak Kp correlation argues against a simple storm-driven
explanation. The presence of many comparable peaks in the Lomb-Scargle analysis argues
against uniqueness. The moderate phase coherence argues against a strong, stable sinusoidal
modulation. The coincidence between the FFT window duration and the detected period raises
methodological concerns. These factors collectively suggest caution.

The recommended posture is to document the 18.6-hour feature as observed in 2023, note its
ambiguity and year-to-year variability, acknowledge that its physical origin is not established, and
defer detailed physical interpretation pending additional analysis that could include comparison
with orbital ephemeris, examination of local time and L-shell dependence, correlation with
spacecraft attitude data, multi-instrument comparisons, and analysis using alternative spectral
methods with different systematic biases.

For the purposes of the current investigation, which was primarily motivated by the search for a
5-minute SIRI-like feature, the 18.6-hour result represents an incidental finding that warrants
documentation but does not require immediate resolution. Other teams with additional context or
motivation may choose to investigate further, but no critical scientific objective depends on
explaining this periodicity immediately.

7.0 Discussion: The 5-Minute Null Result

7.1 Confidence in the Null Result
The absence of a significant 5-minute periodicity in D3 data is based on multiple independent
lines of evidence spanning several years of observations. The 2023 full-year analysis,
representing the most complete dataset with 8,649 hourly samples and multiple continuous
segments totaling hundreds of days, shows no peaks in the 4-6 minute period range exceeding

 UNCLASSIFIED 26 of 33

the adopted 6-sigma significance threshold. The 2026 partial-year analysis, covering the specific
time period when SIRI-2 reported observing their 5-minute feature, similarly shows no significant
5-minute power, with the 5-minute band remaining well below the level of other detected features.
Both FFT-based Welch periodogram analysis and the planned Lomb-Scargle analysis using
actual timestamps converge on the same conclusion.

The time-frequency spectrogram analysis tested whether the 5-minute power might be transient
rather than persistent, appearing only during specific time intervals that could be missed by overall
spectral averaging. The spectrogram shows no periods of enhanced power in the 5-minute band
during any portion of the analyzed intervals, ruling out strongly time-variable 5-minute signals that
are present only part of the time.

The confidence level for this null result can be stated as follows: if D3 contains a 5-minute
periodicity with relative amplitude comparable to the detected 18.6-hour feature (approximately
10-15% of mean), the current analysis methodology would have detected it with very high
confidence given the large sample sizes and long observation windows. The null result therefore
indicates either that no 5-minute periodicity exists in D3 at this amplitude level, or that any such
periodicity is substantially weaker than other features in the D3 spectrum.

Several caveats qualify this confidence statement. First, the adopted 6-sigma threshold, while
conservative, is somewhat arbitrary. A weaker periodic signal falling below this threshold would
escape detection in the peak-finding algorithm, though it might still be visible as a marginal
enhancement in the raw periodogram. Second, the downsampling applied for Lomb-Scargle
analysis (100-fold reduction to ~6.4-second cadence) reduces effective signal-to-noise ratio.
While the downsampled Nyquist frequency remains more than 20 times higher than the 5-minute
target frequency, providing ample safety margin, the reduced number of samples does slightly
reduce detection sensitivity. Third, if the 5-minute signal were present only during specific narrow
conditions not well-represented in the analyzed data windows, it could escape detection.
However, SIRI-2's report of a persistent feature beginning in 2026 argues against such narrow
conditionality.

7.2 Implications for SIRI-2 Periodicity
The D3 null result provides important context for interpreting the SIRI-2 5-minute feature, even
though direct comparison between D3 count rates and SIRI-2 measurements requires additional
analysis beyond the scope of this report. Three primary interpretations merit consideration.

Interpretation A: Instrument-Specific Feature (Most Likely): The 5-minute periodicity reflects
aspects of SIRI's measurement process, calibration cycles, data processing pipeline, or
instrumental configuration rather than a characteristic of the external radiation environment.
Under this interpretation, D3 and SIRI-2 measure fundamentally different physical quantities, even
though both instruments are co-located. SIRI-2 measures solar irradiance across specific spectral
bands, while D3 measures energetic particle count rates. A 5-minute variation in optical emissions
or solar spectral irradiance could occur without corresponding variations in energetic particle
fluxes if the driving mechanism affects photons but not particles. For example, variations in
atmospheric emission or scattering, oscillations in specific emission lines, or periodic structures
in the solar wind that affect optical but not particle measurements could all produce instrument-
specific periodicities.

 UNCLASSIFIED 27 of 33

This interpretation is the most conservative and requires the fewest additional assumptions. It is
also consistent with the observed pattern where one instrument shows a feature that the other
does not detect. The fact that SIRI-2 reported the feature as beginning in 2026, while D3 shows
no corresponding feature in either 2023 or 2026, further supports the instrument-specific
interpretation.

Interpretation B: Different Sensitivities to Environmental Modulation: The environmental
driver produces 5-minute modulation in quantities that SIRI-2 measures but not in quantities that
D3 measures, reflecting genuine differences in what aspects of the environment each instrument
samples. Under this interpretation, both instruments accurately measure the environment, but the
environment itself exhibits different periodicities in different physical parameters. For example,
electromagnetic radiation intensity could vary on 5-minute timescales while energetic particle
fluxes remain steady, or vice versa.

This interpretation requires identifying a physical mechanism that couples to optical
measurements but not particle count rates, or that produces much stronger modulation in one
channel than the other. Candidate mechanisms might include waves or oscillations in the
ionosphere-magnetosphere system that affect electron densities (and hence optical emissions)
more strongly than high-energy particle fluxes, or periodic structures in the solar wind that
modulate the radiation reaching the spacecraft in wavelength-dependent ways.

7.3 Recommendations for Disambiguation
Distinguishing among these interpretations requires additional analysis beyond what was
performed for this report. The most direct approach would be to obtain SIRI time-series data at
comparable temporal resolution to D3, perform matched-window spectral analysis using identical
methods, and directly compare the power spectra. If SIRI shows strong 5-minute power while D3
shows none, the instrument-specific interpretation gains strong support. If both instruments show
similar spectra with or without the 5-minute feature, that would suggest common-mode
environmental effects or shared systematic issues.

A second valuable approach would involve examining other instruments on the same spacecraft
or nearby spacecraft to determine whether the 5-minute feature appears in other independent
measurements. If multiple instruments show the feature, environmental origin becomes more
likely. If only SIRI shows it, instrument-specific origin becomes more likely.

Understanding the detailed physical mechanism by which SIRI makes its measurements would
help identify whether specific aspects of the SIRI design or processing could introduce 5-minute
periodicities. For example, if SIRI uses chopping, modulation, or scanning with a characteristic
period, or if calibration lamp cycles operate on 5-minute intervals, these could introduce
instrumental periodicities. Detailed discussion with the SIRI instrument team could reveal such
possibilities.

For the D3 side, extending the search to include additional years (2024, 2025) not yet analyzed,
or lowering the detection threshold (e.g., 3-sigma instead of 6-sigma) to test for weaker signals,
could further confirm the null result. However, given the strength of the existing evidence from
2023 and 2026, such additional D3 analysis has relatively low priority unless new information
emerges suggesting specific time periods or conditions of interest.

8.0 Conclusions

8.1 Primary Findings
This investigation analyzed SABRS ZEP D3 detector data from 2022-2026 to search for a 5-
minute periodicity reported by the co-located SIRI instrument and to characterize the dominant
periodicities in D3 measurements. The analysis combined FFT-based power spectral density
estimation, Lomb-Scargle periodogram analysis for unevenly-sampled data, time-frequency
spectrograms, band power tracking, and correlation with the Kp geomagnetic index.

Finding 1: No 5-Minute Periodicity Detected in D3. The D3 count rate data do not show
significant spectral power in the 4-6 minute period range corresponding to the SIRI 5-minute
feature. This null result holds across multiple years including 2023 (most complete dataset with
8,649 hourly samples) and 2026 (covering the SIRI detection period). Both frequency-averaged
spectral analysis and time-resolved spectrogram analysis converge on this conclusion.

Finding 2: Strong But Ambiguous 18.6-Hour Periodicity. The 2023 dataset exhibits a
dominant spectral feature at 18.61 hours with very high nominal significance in FFT analysis.
However, follow-up Lomb-Scargle analysis reveals multiple peaks of comparable magnitude
across the 12-30 hour range rather than a uniquely dominant 18.6-hour signal, suggesting the
FFT prominence may partly reflect analysis method effects. Furthermore, different years show
different dominant periods (2023: 18.6h, 2026: 4.2h), arguing against a stable physical signal.

Finding 3: Weak Geomagnetic Control. D3 band power in both 5-minute and 1-hour bands
shows weak correlation with the Kp geomagnetic index. In the most reliable 2023 dataset, Kp
explains less than 3% of variance at zero lag and less than 6% even at the optimal tested lag of
48 hours. These weak correlations indicate that global geomagnetic disturbance, as represented
by Kp, is not the primary driver of D3 periodic structure.

8.2 Interpretation and Implications
The absence of a D3 5-minute feature concurrent with SIRI's reported detection most naturally
suggests that the SIRI periodicity reflects instrument-specific effects or sensitivity to
environmental parameters that D3 does not measure, rather than a general radiation-environment
signature. This interpretation is conservative and consistent with the pattern of single-instrument
detection. It implies that the SIRI feature, while potentially interesting from an instrumental or data
quality perspective, may not indicate a widespread magnetospheric phenomenon affecting
particle populations.

The 18.6-hour D3 periodicity remains unexplained. Its year-to-year variability, the presence of
multiple comparable peaks in Lomb-Scargle analysis, and weak phase coherence argue against
treating it as a confirmed detection of a specific physical process. The feature may reflect a
combination of weak environmental periodicities, orbital or geometric effects, instrumental factors,
and analysis method artifacts. Definitive interpretation would require additional analysis including
orbital parameter comparison, local time and L-shell dependence studies, multi-year systematic
investigation, and comparison with other instruments.

The weak Kp correlations demonstrate that D3 periodicities are not primarily controlled by storm-
time geomagnetic activity as measured by this global index. However, this does not rule out
magnetospheric influences operating through local conditions, specific particle populations, or

processes not well captured by Kp. Testing additional geophysical indices (Dst, AE, solar wind
parameters) represents a logical next step if understanding geomagnetic control becomes a
priority.

8.3 Status and Utility of This Work
This technical note documents a null result for the primary science question (Does D3 show the
SIRI 5-minute feature?) and an inconclusive result for the incidentally discovered 18.6-hour
periodicity. The work is considered complete for current purposes given the original motivation
and available resources. The documentation serves several functions going forward.

First, it prevents redundant effort by recording what was done and what was found, allowing future
investigators to build on this work rather than repeating it. Second, it provides a baseline for
comparison if circumstances change, for example if SIRI data become available for direct
comparison, or if additional years of D3 data suggest different patterns. Third, it establishes
methodology and analysis approaches that can be applied to similar questions in other datasets
or instruments.

The null result for the 5-minute search is itself scientifically valuable as a discriminator between
hypotheses about the SIRI feature's origin. The ambiguous 18.6-hour result, while not providing
a definitive answer, documents a real feature in the data that may prove relevant to future
investigations of D3 behavior or spacecraft operations.

9.0 Recommendations

Close out the D3 5-minute investigation. Based on consistent null results across multiple years

Share findings with SIRI team. Communicate the null result to SIRI colleagues, emphasizing

9.3 Long-Term Perspective
The investigation documented in this report addresses a specific question about D3 periodicities
motivated by external observations. The null result for the 5-minute feature and the ambiguous
result for the 18.6-hour feature may seem anticlimactic, but they represent legitimate scientific
outcomes. Not all investigations lead to clear positive detections, and well-documented null
results serve important functions in the scientific process.

The work establishes that D3 behavior is more complex than simple storm-time response to global
indices, that year-to-year variability in spectral characteristics is substantial, and that apparent
periodicities require careful methodological and physical vetting before acceptance. These
lessons inform future work with this instrument and this dataset.
