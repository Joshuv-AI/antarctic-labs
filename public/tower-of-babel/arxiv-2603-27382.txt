Dynamic Constrained Stabilization on the n n -sphere
Mayur Sawant, Abdelhamid Tayebi

Abstract
We consider the constrained stabilization problem of second-order systems evolving on the n n -sphere. We propose a control strategy with a constraint proximity-based dynamic damping mechanism that ensures safe and almost global asymptotic stabilization of the target point in the presence of star-shaped constraints on the n n -sphere. It is also shown that the proposed approach can be used to deal with the constrained rigid-body attitude stabilization. The effectiveness of the proposed approach is demonstrated through simulation results on the 2 2 -sphere in the presence of star-shaped constraint sets.

Various mechanical systems have states that evolve on the

In many practical scenarios, the attitude stabilization problem can also be recast as a stabilization on the

The stabilization problem on the

[

]

[
3
]
,

In
[
9
]
, a logarithmic barrier function is used to design a quaternion-based feedback controller for rigid body attitude stabilization in the presence of multiple attitude-constrained zones, and a single attitude-mandatory zone, characterized by quadratic inequalities.
In
[
11
]
, the authors employ an explicit reference governor to ensure trajectory tracking under conic constraints and actuator saturation limits.
In
[
5
]
, an invariant set motion planner is proposed to generate a sequence of quaternion waypoints that steer the spacecraft attitude to a desired configuration while enforcing conic constraints on a body-fixed direction evolving on the
2
-sphere.
In
[
1
]
, the authors addressed the stabilization problem on the
n
-sphere under conic constraints by leveraging the stereographic projection to transform the problem into a classical navigation problem in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with spherical obstacles, enabling the use of existing navigation function-based obstacle avoidance methods.

Although existing approaches address constrained stabilization for dynamical systems, the constraint representations are often limited to conic sets.
Conic constraints are conservative and exclude a significant portion of the free space from the feasible region for stabilization compared to star-shaped constraints (which include conic constraints as a special case).
In our earlier work
[
14
]
, we introduced a kinematic-level constrained stabilization framework on the
n
-sphere that accommodates star-shaped constraint sets.

In this work, we propose a control strategy for second-order systems on the
n
-sphere with star-shaped obstacles, relying on constraint-proximity-based dynamic damping, guaranteeing safety over the feasible state space and almost global asymptotic stabilization of the target location.

Unit-quaternion-based constrained attitude stabilization

The attitude of a rigid body with respect to the inertial frame can be described by a four-parameter representation, namely unit-quaternion.
To denote the unit-quaternion, we use
\mathbf{x}=[\eta,\mathbf{q}^{\top}]\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
, where
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
The unit-quaternion-based dynamic attitude model is given by

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x})\boldsymbol{\omega},

(18)

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{m}\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}

\displaystyle=-\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{J}_{m}\boldsymbol{\omega}+\boldsymbol{\tau},

where
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the angular velocity and
\mathbf{J}_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
is a constant positive definite inertia matrix.
The matrix
\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x})
is defined as

\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x})=\begin{bmatrix}-\mathbf{q}^{\top}\\
\eta\mathbf{I}_{3}+\mathbf{q}^{\times}\end{bmatrix},

(19)

where
\mathbf{q}^{\times}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
is a skew symmetric matrix such that
\mathbf{q}^{\times}\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{q}\times\mathbf{p}
for any
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
\times
being the vector cross product.

Given any initial condition
(\mathbf{x}(0),\boldsymbol{\omega}(0))\in\mathcal{M}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the objective is to design the torque control input
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
in (
18
) that ensures
\mathbf{x}(t)\in\mathcal{M},\;\forall t\geq 0
, and renders
(\mathbf{x}_{d},\mathbf{0}_{3})
almost globally asymptotically stable over
\mathcal{M}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

We make use of the transformation
\mathbf{v}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x})\boldsymbol{\omega}
to obtain
\boldsymbol{\tau}
.
Substituting
\mathbf{v}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x})\boldsymbol{\omega}
into (
4
), and invoking the identities

Differentiating

VIII
Conclusion

In this work, we propose a feedback control design for the constrained stabilization problem of second-order systems evolving on the
n
-sphere.
Unlike the majority of the existing literature, where the unsafe regions are typically represented by conic sets, our approach is able to handle a more general class of obstacles represented star-shaped sets on the
n
-sphere which offers a more flexible characterization of the unsafe regions, potentially enabling a larger safe region for stabilization purposes.
The proposed feedback control input guarantees safety and almost global asymptotic stability of the equilibrium
(\mathbf{x}_{d},\mathbf{0}_{n+1})
over the state space
\mathcal{M}\times\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

\mathbb{S}^{n}
at
\mathbf{x}^{*}
is defined as in Section
II
.
Therefore, any non-zero
\lambda
satisfying
\left(\mathbf{J}_{d}(\mathbf{x}^{*})-\lambda\mathbf{I}_{n+1}\right)\mathbf{n}_{2}=\mathbf{0}_{n+1}
ensures that the equality (
48
) holds for any
\mathbf{n}_{2}\in\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{x}^{*}}\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
Consequently, any non-zero eigenvalue
\lambda_{g}
of
\mathbf{J}_{d}(\mathbf{x}^{*})
with associated eigenvector
\mathbf{n}_{2}
in
\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{x}^{*}}\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n+1}\}
is an eigenvalue of
\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{0}_{n+1})
.
By (
47
), the associated eigenvector is given by

\mathbf{n}=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{g}}\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x}^{*})\mathbf{n}_{2}\\
\mathbf{n}_{2}\end{bmatrix},

where
\mathbf{n}_{2}\in\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{x}^{*}}\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{n+1}\}
.

To obtain the remaining eigenvalue of
\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{0}_{n+1})
, we require the following fact:

Fact 1
.

For a vector field
\mathbf{v}_{d}(\cdot)
that satisfies Properties
V1
-
V6
, one has
\mathbf{J}_{d}(\mathbf{x}^{*})\mathbf{x}^{*}=\mathbf{0}_{n+1}
for any
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in\mathcal{E}\cup\{\mathbf{x}_{d}\}
, where
\mathbf{J}_{d}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\nu}_{d}(\mathbf{x}^{*})}{\partial\mathbf{x}}
, and
\boldsymbol{\nu}_{d}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{v}_{d}(\mathbf{x})
as defined in (
6
).

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}_{n+1}
for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, one has

\boldsymbol{\nu}_{d}(\mathbf{x})^{\top}\mathbf{x}=0

for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
Differentiating with respect to
\mathbf{x}
on both sides and setting
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}^{*}
, one obtains,

\mathbf{J}_{d}(\mathbf{x}^{*})\mathbf{x}^{*}+\boldsymbol{\nu}_{d}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\mathbf{0}_{n+1}.

Since according to Property
V2
,
\boldsymbol{\nu}_{d}(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\mathbf{0}_{n+1}
for all
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in\mathcal{E}\cup\{\mathbf{x}_{d}\}
, it holds that
\mathbf{J}_{d}(\mathbf{x}^{*})\mathbf{x}^{*}=\mathbf{0}_{n+1}
.
This completes the proof of Fact
1
.
\blacksquare

Finally, by setting
\mathbf{n}_{1}=\mathbf{x}^{*}
,
\mathbf{n}_{2}=\mathbf{0}_{n+1}
and using Fact
1
, one can confirm that

\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{0}_{n+1})\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{n}_{1}\\
\mathbf{n}_{2}\end{bmatrix}=\lambda\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{n}_{1}\\
\mathbf{n}_{2}\end{bmatrix},

with
\lambda=0
.
Consequently,
\lambda=0
is an eigenvalue of
\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{0}_{n+1})
, and the eigenvector associated with it is given by
\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}^{*}\\
\mathbf{0}_{n+1}\end{bmatrix}
.

Even though the velocity
\mathbf{v}
evolves in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the orthogonal projection operator
\mathbf{P}
in (
4
) ensures that if
\mathbf{x}(0)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, then
\mathbf{x}(t)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
for all
t\geq 0
.
Consequently, the manifold
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is forward invariant under the closed-loop system (
4
)-(
7
).
Therefore, to determine stability of the equilibrium points
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{0}_{n+1})
in
\mathcal{S}\cup\{(\mathbf{x}_{d},\mathbf{0}_{n+1})\}
, it is sufficient to analyze eigenvalues of the Jacobian
\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{0}_{n+1})
such that associated eigenvectors belong to
\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{x}^{*}}\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
As a result, we ignore the eigenvalue
\lambda=0
because its corresponding eigenvector
\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}^{*}\\
\mathbf{0}_{n+1}\end{bmatrix}
does not belong to
\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{x}^{*}}\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

By Property
V2
,
\left(\mathcal{E}\cup\{\mathbf{x}_{d}\}\right)\subset\mathcal{M}
and therefore, by (
8
),
\beta(d_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbf{x}^{*}))>0
for all
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in\mathcal{E}\cup\{\mathbf{x}_{d}\}
.
Because
k_{d}>0
, one has
-k_{d}\beta(d_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbf{x}^{*}))<0
.
Consequently, the stability of the equilibrium points
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{0}_{n+1})
in
\mathcal{S}\cup\{(\mathbf{x}_{d},\mathbf{0}_{n+1})\}
can be inferred by analyzing the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{J}_{d}(\mathbf{x}^{*})
such that the associated eigenvectors belong to the tangent space
\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{x}^{*}}\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

According to Property
V4a
, every eigenvalue of
\mathbf{J}_{d}(\mathbf{x}_{d})
whose associated eigenvector belongs to
\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{x}_{d}}\mathbb{S}^{n}
has negative real parts.
Consequently,
(\mathbf{x}_{d},\mathbf{0}_{n+1})
is asymptotically stable for the closed-loop system (
4
)-(
7
) on
\mathcal{M}\times\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Furthermore, according to Property
V4b
, for each
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in\mathcal{E}
, at least one eigenvalue whose associated eigenvector belongs to
\mathsf{T}_{\mathbf{x}^{*}}\mathbb{S}^{n}
has a positive real part.
Consequently, by center manifold theorem
[
13
, Section 2.7, Pg 116]
, every undesired equilibrium point
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{0}_{n+1})
in
\mathcal{S}
has stable manifold of zero Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Additionally, as proved earlier,
\mathcal{S}\cup\{(\mathbf{x}_{d},\mathbf{0}_{n+1})\}
is globally attractive for the closed-loop system (
4
)-(
7
) over
\mathcal{M}\times\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Consequently, the desired equilibrium point
(\mathbf{x}_{d},\mathbf{0}_{n+1})
is almost globally asymptotically stable for the closed-loop system (
4
)-(
7
) over
\mathcal{M}\times\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
This completes the proof of Claim
4
of Theorem
1
.
