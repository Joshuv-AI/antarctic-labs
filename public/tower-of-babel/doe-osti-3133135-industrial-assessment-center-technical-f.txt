Executive Summary:
The work to be performed involves managing Industrial Assessment Centers through a
portfolio of activities. The core activities to be performed are in the general areas of;
quality assurance of IAC operations, enhancement of the student IAC experience,
program development for improving assessments and implementing smart
manufacturing projects, program outreach to carry assessment successes beyond the
program, and program integration with other DOE/manufacturing assistance programs.

with DOE and provides technical management for the IAC program working under

Background
The US DOE Industrial Assessment Centers (IACs) can help small and medium sized
US manufacturers save energy, improve productivity, and reduce waste by providing
no-cost technical assessments conducted by university-based teams of engineering
students and faculty.
The IAC (formerly called the Energy Analysis and Diagnostic Centers) were created by
the Department of Commerce in 1976 in response to the oil embargo and rising energy
costs. The program was specifically focused on helping small and medium-sized
manufacturing facilities cut back on unnecessary costs from inefficient energy use.
The IAC program was moved to the Department of Energy just after it was formed in
1978 and was later expanded to include evaluations of ineffective production
procedures, excess waste production, and other production-related problems. More
recent additions to the IAC program scope include improving cybersecurity awareness,
exploring smart manufacturing technologies, and implementing comprehensive energy
management systems. Currently, the IAC program is administered through the Office of
Manufacturing and Energy Supply Chains (MESC) at the Department of Energy.
After an on-site visit, the IAC team provides a comprehensive report with specific details
on all opportunities for improving competitiveness identified, including applicable
rebates and incentives. This is where the Technical Field Manger comes in. As the Field
Mangers, it is our role to organize and oversee the IAC program while also
accomplishing specific tasks assigned by DOE per contract period as detailed in this
report.

Project Objectives
The primary objectives of the Industrial Assessment Center (IAC) Technical Field
Manager are to sustain and continuously improve the quality and value of assessments,
as well as enhance the student experience, database, and associated informational
resources. This role also involves providing an environment for students and faculty that
promotes excellence, creativity, and responsibility, while managing operations with a
strong emphasis on flexibility and adaptability to accommodate new changes and
opportunities within these contract years.
Additionally, the Technical Field Manager is responsible for offering leadership in team
building across the IACs, the Department of Energy (DOE), industry groups, and other
program stakeholders. Finally, the role includes promoting DOE priority areas such as
energy management, water efficiency, smart technologies, and cybersecurity, along
with advancing DOE tools, industrial energy efficiency best practices, and leading-edge
technologies from both DOE and other relevant sources.

Project Results and Discussion
Task 1. Coordinating and monitoring center operations, assessments, and
outreach activities
Task summary: Ensure that the Centers are able and equipped to perform their
required assessments; provide guidance on follow-through activities in order to promote
greater implementation of recommendations through proactive follow-up with industries;
assist the Centers in developing marketing and outreach strategies; and develop and
maintain IAC communication pieces for both outreach/promotion and results reporting.
All outreach and promotion pieces will be coordinated with the DOE Project Officer
 Subtask 1.1 Conduct periodic on-site surveillances of 30% of Centers for FY2020.
 Subtask Summary: Surveillances are monitoring activities which ensure that the
 Centers are able, equipped, and performing their required assessments and following
 all required methodologies on the IAC assessment process, assessment
 requirements, assessment guidelines, appropriate training/tools, and assessment
 results reporting.
 Milestone 1.1: The Rutgers FMO staff has completed nineteen on-site
 surveillances as of the end of Q2 FY2023. No on-site
 surveillances were performed in FY 2020 due to the ongoing COVID-19
 pandemic, which limited our ability to travel. Since restrictions have been lifted, a
 few visits have resumed including one visit to UNL during Q1 of FY2022. In Q2, a
 second visit was made to UNL as well as a visit to Arizona State University and
 Texas A&M University. Both UNL and AS visits in Q2 had associated research
 initiatives in addition to the traditional site visit. In Q3 a visit was made to Lehigh
 University for an associated research initiative. In Q1 FY2023, a visit was made
 to Tennessee Tech IAC and in Q2 a visit was made to Colorado School of
 Mines. The FMO shadowed a site visit to a local New Jersey plant with Lehigh
 IAC in Q4. In Q1 FY2024, representatives from the FMO attended a site visit with
 University of Delaware as well as a second site visit with Oregon State University
 IAC.
 Subtask 1.2 Establish a consistent online IAC reporting format for client review.
 Subtask Summary: Solicit and receive feedback from centers during and
 after Annual Director’s meeting, establish a working draft page which will accept
 uploaded report data in a format which will be easy to follow by plant personnel, use
 test centers to evaluate the reporting, launch the new reporting format and evaluate
 and update.
 Milestone 1.2: The Rutgers FMO team has created a mock online IAC reporting
 format for DOE to review. The draft online reporting portal was prepared for
 review at the 2019 Annual Director’s meeting. Per DOE program
 managers’ input, the initiative was put on hold in exchange for other competing
 priorities.

Task 2. Provide technical reviews of all IAC assessment reports
Task Summary: Reviews of all IAC assessment reports will be conducted by
Rutgers FMO staff and include a review of the required information that should be in
every report. Report critiques are comprised of an evaluation of the recommendations
made by the Centers on technical grounds and a written summary of the review which is
returned to the center. All critiques which have identified a weakness will require a
response from the center. Assessment reports are reviewed and critiqued, and
summaries are provided to the Centers within 30 days of receipt.

Subtask 3.4 Smart Manufacturing (SMART)
Subtask Summary: Devise a process and protocol for identifying and recommending
“SMART” projects to manufacturers including questions to ask during the intake
interview, observations to make during a plant tour, and measurements to make
throughout the day. Also, develop categories for “SMART” recommendations, which
indicate the difficulty and sophistication of implementation. Activities also include:
 • Develop initial case studies; issue a request to centers for self-reporting of
 SMART case studies
 • In collaboration with DOE, articulate goals, timing on implementation and
 evaluation metrics
 Milestone 3.4: Rutgers FMO has been working on a “Smart Assessment”
 protocol for the IACs to use.
 The FMO team evaluated different “SMART” sensors such as an ABB Ability
 Sensor and Proteus sensors and Centenelle’s ePM motor driven system
 monitor on the Rutgers campus to help inform the “Smart Assessment”
 protocol. Rutgers purchased and evaluated IIoT-based assessment data
 collection tools and is developing improved tools to demonstrate innovative
 functionality.
 Rutgers has a compressed air IIoT test facility to evaluate air flow sensor
 accuracy and demonstrate related IIoT features. Recent
 results indicate significant increasing air flow rate measurement error as function
 of pressure. The compressed air team toured a manufacturing plant in MD
 including a demonstration of a commercialized compressed air IIoT
 platform. Rutgers partnered with UNL on an assessment including compressed
 air where IIoT sensors were installed.
 Value proposition of SMART retrofit solutions is being investigated. A
 paper was presented at SmartGreens 2022 on this work.
 SMART mixing was investigated in the lab to see if data from controlling
 mixers with VFDs can be used to assess product quality. Mixing results from
 glycerin and water shows motor power is a good indicator of torque.
 The use of 15-minute data in assessments, especially for solar PV, is being
 investigated with ASU.

Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions
There are a few new strategic priorities that were developed by DOE and FMO as part
of the IAC Annual Director’s Meeting that are highlighted in this section:
 1. Non-Traditional Assessments: Standards for two-day assessments have
 been finalized and recorded in the official IAC guidelines. The guidelines state:
 The typical IAC industrial assessment is expected to be conducted in one day.
 Two-day assessments can be requested for pre-approval from the Technical
 Field Managers as long as both of the following criteria are met:
 • Plant area is greater than 500,000 ft2
 • Plant energy bill is greater than $1 million
 Other assessment exceptions are approved by FMO and DOE on a case-by-
 case basis. These assessments require special approval because they do not
 meet the minimum assessment criteria outlined in the IAC program guidelines,
 i.e. a facility with annual utility expenses over $3.5M or annual sales over
 $100M. The maximum annual energy bill increased from $2.5M to $3.5M with
 the start of the new 5-year contract for centers.
 The Rutgers FMO is currently tracking these exceptions as they are requested

Path Forward

been established. Rutgers Center for Advanced Energy Systems is currently
established as a recipient of an Industrial Assessment Center (IAC) Field Manager for
Institutions of Higher Education (IHE)- Based IACs opportunity to provide services for
the Department of Energy (DOE) to increase the likelihood of success in the conduct of
cooperative or joint activities typically with small business firms and educational
institutions.

based at IHEs. In this role, the Field Manager provides technical assistance and
outreach to individual centers and is the primary mechanism for ensuring the IACs and
their associated satellite centers perform at the highest possible level and that their
activities support the achievement of MESC goals.
