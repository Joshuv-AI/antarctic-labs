Abstract
. The paper deals with Ulrich wildness of decomposable threefold scrolls X X over Hirzebruch surfaces 𝔽 a \mathbb{F}_{a} , for any a ⩾ 0 a\geqslant 0 . Our Main Theorem enstablishes that for a = 0 a=0 , the moduli space of rank- r r Ulrich bundles, for any r ⩾ 2 r\geqslant 2 and of given Chern classes, contains a generically smooth , unirational component ℳ ⁡ ( r ) \mathcal{M}(r) of computed dimension whose general point corresponds to a slope-stable Ulrich bundle; in particular X X turns out to be Ulrich wild . When a ⩾ 1 a\geqslant 1 and in presence of modular obstructions , X X is nevertheless shown to be Ulrich wild .

Introduction

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a
n
-dimensional smooth projective variety with a hyperplane section divisor
H
. A vector bundle
\mathcal{U}
on
X
is called a
\mathcal{O}_{X}(H)
-Ulrich bundle
if

H^{*}(\mathcal{U}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-iH))=0,\;\;1\leqslant i\leqslant n.

The
Ulrich complexity
of
X
is defined as

uc_{H}(X):=\min\{r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\mid\exists\,\mathcal{U}\text{ indecomposable Ulrich bundle, }\text{rk}(\mathcal{U})=r\}.

Such bundles exhibit several key structural properties: they are closed under extensions; any such
\mathcal{U}
has an
Ulrich dual
bundle,
\mathcal{U}^{U}:=\mathcal{U}^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}((n+1)H)
, of identical rank; moreover they are always (
Gieseker/slope
)
semistable
, and also
stable
if they are not extensions of lower-rank Ulrich bundles (cf. e.g.
[
2
,
3
]
), so they fill up subsets of moduli spaces of semistable bundles.

While well-characterized for curves and certain classes of surfaces, existence and rank classification remain largely open in dimension
n\geqslant 3
(cf. e.g.
[
4
]
for an overview).

The paper investigates Ulrich bundles on threefold scrolls over Hirzebruch surfaces
\mathbb{F}_{a}
,
a\geqslant 0
,
f
denoting fiber class of
\mathbb{F}_{a}
whereas
C_{-}
either the unique section of
\mathbb{F}_{a}
with
C_{-}^{2}=-a
, if
a>0
, or the fiber of the other ruling, if
a=0
; so we denote line bundles on
\mathbb{F}_{a}
as
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{a}}(\alpha,\beta):=\alpha C_{-}+\beta f
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In this set-up, taking the projective bundle
\varphi:\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{a}}(\mathcal{E}_{a}^{b})\to\mathbb{F}_{a}
, with
a,b\geqslant 0
and
\mathcal{E}_{a}^{b}:=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{a}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{a}}(0,-b)
, let
\xi
be the
tautological class
,
C_{0}:=\varphi^{*}(C_{-})
and
F:=\varphi^{*}(f)
, so that the
Chow ring
of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{a}}(\mathcal{E}_{a}^{b})
is determined by

\xi^{2}=-b\xi F,\;C_{0}^{2}=-aC_{0}F,\;F^{2}=0\;{\rm and}\;\xi C_{0}F=1.

To ease notation, we also set:

\mathcal{O}_{a}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}):=\alpha_{1}\xi+\alpha_{2}C_{0}+\alpha_{3}F\quad\text{and}\quad[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}]:=\alpha_{1}\xi C_{0}+\alpha_{2}\xi F+\alpha_{3}C_{0}F.

For any
c\in\mathbb{Z}
, we let
h=h_{a}:=\mathcal{O}_{a}(1,1,c)
which turns out to be very ample if and only if
c\geqslant a+b+1
(cf.
[
9
, Remark 2.1]
). Under this condition, one has the embedding:

(0.1)

\Phi_{|h|}:\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{a}}(\mathcal{E}_{a}^{b})\hookrightarrow X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}

so that
X
is a
threefold scroll
over
\mathbb{F}_{a}
of degree
d=3(2c-a-b)
,

canonical bundle

\omega_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{a}(-2,-2,-(a+b+2))
,
sectional genus

g=2c-a-b-1
, where
N:=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{a}}(\mathcal{E}^{b}_{a}),h)-1=4c-2a-2b+3
.

Previous paper
[
9
]
established a
multiple scroll structure
on
X
and classified
h
-Ulrich line bundles on it, as follows.

Theorem A
(cf.
[
9
, Prop. 2.2, Cor. 2.4, Thm. 3.1]
)
(1) For any integers
a,b\geqslant 0
one has

\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{a}}(\mathcal{E}_{a}^{b}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{a}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{a}}(0,-b))\cong\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{b}}(\mathcal{E}_{b}^{a}:=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{b}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{b}}(0,-a)).

In particular, for any
c\geqslant a+b+1
, threefold scrolls
X
as in (
0.1
) are endowed with a
multiple scroll structure over both Hirzebruch surfaces
\mathbb{F}_{a}
and
\mathbb{F}_{b}
for which

(0.2)

\mathcal{O}_{a}(1,1,c)=h_{a}=h=h_{b}=\mathcal{O}_{b}(1,1,c).

(2) For any
a,b\geqslant 0
and
c\geqslant a+b+1
, the Ulrich complexity of
X
is
uc_{h}(X)=1
. More precisely, isomorphism in (1) and simmetry in (
0.2
) permit to assume
b\geqslant a\geqslant 0
and to classify
h
-Ulrich line bundles on
X
as follows:

(i) for any
b\geqslant a>0,\;c\geqslant a+b+1
,
X
supports exactly two
h
-Ulrich line bundles, namely
N=\mathcal{O}_{a}(2,0,2c-a-1)
and its Ulrich dual

;

(ii) for any
b>0=a,\;c\geqslant b+1
,
X
supports exactly four
h
-Ulrich line bundles, namely
N
and
N^{U}
as in (i), together with the two extra line bundles
L=\mathcal{O}_{0}(1,0,3c-b-1)
and its Ulrich dual
L^{U}=\mathcal{O}_{0}(1,2,c-1)
;

(iii) if
a=b=0
then, for any
c\geqslant 1
,
X
supports exactly six
h
-Ulrich line bundles, namely
N,\;N^{U},\;L,\;L^{U}
as in (ii), together with the two extra line bundles
M=\mathcal{O}_{0}(2,1,c-1)
and its Ulrich dual
M^{U}=\mathcal{O}_{0}(0,1,3c-1)
.

Inspired by representation theory of ACM-graded modules (cf. e.g.
[
5
]
), a smooth, projective variety
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is said to be
(geometrically)

\mathcal{O}_{Y}(H)
-Ulrich wild
if it supports
p
-dimensional families of pairwise non-isomorphic, indecomposable
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(H)
-Ulrich bundles for arbitrarily large
p
; this property suggests a rich internal geometry of
Y
(in particular the
Ulrich set

Ur(Y)
is large). In this circle of ideas, in
[
9
]
the authors focused on threefold scrolls
(X,h)
as above and considered modular components of
h
-Ulrich bundles arising from
iterated extensions
of the pair
(N,N^{U})
, under
0\leqslant a\leqslant b\leqslant 1
.

In fact, building on
Theorem A
-(2),
[
9
]
first identified
h
-Ulrich line bundles
L
and
L^{U}
as those obtained via
twisted pullbacks
from the base, namely of the form
h\otimes\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{F}_{a}})
for some
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{F}_{a}}
which, up to a twist, is Ulrich on
\mathbb{F}_{a}
w.r.t. a natural very ample polarization on
\mathbb{F}_{a}
(cf.
[
9
, Thm. 1.12, Rem. 3.3]
). Instead, iterated extensions involving
(M,M^{U})
or even
mixed pairs
have been excluded in
[
9
]
, as they have been proved to yield only strictly semistable bundles, i.e. failing to produce positive-dimensional modular components (cf. Theorem 4.1-
Cases k
, with
k=3,4,8
in
[
9
]
).

This explains why
[
9
, Thm. 5.6]
focused on (
deformations
of)
iterated extensions
involving
(N,N^{U})
, as these line bundles were proved to be not
twisted pullbacks
from either
\mathbb{F}_{a}
or
\mathbb{F}_{b}
, dealing therefore with
h
-Ulrichness
on
X
not coming from base-surfaces. Nevertheless, in
[
9
, Thm. 5.6]
, the authors were forced to use
0\leqslant a\leqslant b\leqslant 1
to avoid
obstructions
to the existence of generically smooth modular components for Ulrich bundles arising from them (cf.
[
9
, Thm. 4.1-(
1_{b}
),  Thm. 4.2-(jj), (jjj)]
).

The aim of this paper is to provide description of what occurs in the remaining cases, distinguishing between two primary regimes based on the nature of the
h
-Ulrich line bundles and the modular behavior of their higher-rank iterated (deformed) extensions:

\bullet

The Inherited Case

(L,L^{U})
: for
b\geqslant 0=a,\;c\geqslant b+1
, we consider here deformations of iterated extensions of
(L,L^{U})
, both
twisted pullbacks
from the base
\mathbb{F}_{0}
.

\bullet

The Obstructed Case

(N,N^{U})
: outside the range
0\leqslant a\leqslant b\leqslant 1
, the pair
(N,N^{U})
presents
cohomological obstructions
to the existence of smooth modular families (cf.
[
9
, Thm. 4.1-(
1_{b}
),  Thm. 4.2-(jj) and (jjj)]
). However,
h
-Ulrich wildness
can still be proven without existence of smooth modular components.

Main Theorem

(1) Let
b\geqslant 0=a
and
c\geqslant b+1
be integers and let
(X,h)
be a threefold scroll over
\mathbb{F}_{0}
as in (
0.1
). Then, for any integer
r\geqslant 1
, the moduli space of rank-
r
vector bundles
\mathcal{U}_{r}
on
X
, which are
h
-Ulrich and with Chern classes

(0.3)

\displaystyle c_{1}(\mathcal{U}_{r}):=\begin{cases}\mathcal{O}_{0}\left(r,\,r+1,\frac{(r-1)(4c-b-2)+2(c-1)}{2}\right),&\mbox{if $r$ is odd},\\
&\\
\mathcal{O}_{0}\left(r,\,r,\frac{r}{2}(4c-b-2)\right),&\mbox{if $r$ is even},\end{cases}

\displaystyle{\small c_{2}(\mathcal{U}_{r})=\begin{cases}\scriptstyle\left[r^{2}-1,\;r^{2}(2c-b-1)+r-(3r-1)c+b\frac{(3r-1)}{2},\;(r^{2}-1)(2c-1)-b\frac{(r^{2}-1)}{2}\right],&\mbox{if $r\geqslant 3$ odd},\\
&\\
\scriptstyle\left[r(r-1),\;r(r-1)(2c-b-1),\;\frac{r^{2}}{2}(4c-b-2)+(1-c)r\right],&\mbox{if $r$ even},\end{cases}}

\displaystyle{\small c_{3}(\mathcal{U}_{r})=\begin{cases}(2c-b-1)r^{3}+(2b-4c+2)r^{2}-(2c-b-1)r-2b+4c-2,&\mbox{if $r\geqslant 3$ odd},\\
&\\
(2c-b-1)r^{3}+5b\frac{r^{2}}{2}-(5c-3)r^{2}+(2c-b-2)r,&\mbox{if $r\geqslant 4$ even},\end{cases}}

is not empty and it contains a generically smooth component, denoted by
\mathcal{M}(r)
, which is moreover unirational for
r\geqslant 3
and rational for
r=2
, of dimension

(0.4)

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(\mathcal{M}(r))=\begin{cases}(r^{2}-1)(2c-b-1),&\mbox{if $r$ is odd},\\
r^{2}(2c-b-1)+1,&\mbox{if $r$ is even},\end{cases}

whose general point
[\mathcal{U}_{r}]\in\mathcal{M}(r)

corresponds to a slope-stable vector bundle, of
h
-slope

\mu(\mathcal{U}_{r})=4(2c-b-a)-2
. In particular,
X
is (geometrically)
h
-Ulrich wild with
Ur(X)=\mathbb{N}^{*}
, more precisely with no slope-stable-
h
-Ulrich-rank gaps.

(2) Let either
0\leqslant a\leqslant 1
,
b\geqslant 2
or
b\geqslant a\geqslant 1,\;(a,b)\neq(1,1)
be integers and let
(X,h)
be a threefold scroll over
\mathbb{F}_{a}
as in (
0.1
). Then, for any
c\geqslant a+b+1
,
X
is (geometrically)
h
-Ulrich wild with
Ur(X)=\mathbb{N}^{*}
, i.e. with no indecomposable-
h
-Ulrich-rank gaps.

{\rm Hom}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1},N_{\epsilon_{r}})=H^{0}(\mathcal{G}^{\vee}_{r-1}\otimes N_{\epsilon_{r-2}})\cong H^{0}(N^{\vee}_{\epsilon_{r-1}}\otimes N_{\epsilon_{r-2}})=(0)
, the latter equality following by different parity, proving the second equality in (
1.9
).

Now
(g_{r})
tensored by
\mathcal{G}^{\vee}_{r-1}
gives

0\to{\rm End}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1})\to{\rm Hom}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1},\mathcal{G}_{r})\to{\rm Hom}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1},N_{\epsilon_{r}})
so, from the vanishing in (
1.9
), we get

(1.10)

{\rm End}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1})\cong{\rm Hom}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1},\mathcal{G}_{r})

Taking now the dual sequence of
(g_{r})
tensored by
\mathcal{G}_{r}
and passing to cohomology gives

0\to{\rm Hom}(N_{\epsilon_{r}},\mathcal{G}_{r})\to{\rm End}(\mathcal{G}_{r})\to{\rm Hom}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1},\mathcal{G}_{r})\cong{\rm End}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1})\to\cdots,

the isomorphism on the right-side following from (
1.10
). In other words, for any
r\geqslant 3
, one has that

(1.11)

1\leqslant\operatorname{dim}({\rm End}(\mathcal{G}_{r}))\leqslant\operatorname{dim}({\rm Hom}(N_{\epsilon_{r}},\mathcal{G}_{r}))+\operatorname{dim}({\rm End}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1})).

We therefore need to compute
\operatorname{dim}({\rm Hom}(N_{\epsilon_{r}},\mathcal{G}_{r}))
. Observe that, for any
r\geqslant 2
, one has also

(1.12)

{\rm Hom}(N_{\epsilon_{r}},\mathcal{G}_{r})\cong{\rm Hom}(N_{\epsilon_{r}},\mathcal{G}_{r-1});

indeed, considering
(g_{r})
then, for any
f\in{\rm Hom}(N_{\epsilon_{r}},\mathcal{G}_{r})
, one must have
p_{r}\circ f=0
otherwise
(g_{r})
would be a splitting sequence, against assumptions. In other words,
Im(f)\subseteq Ker(p_{r})=\mathcal{G}_{r-1})
, i.e.
f\in{\rm Hom}(N_{\epsilon_{r}},\mathcal{G}_{r-1})
. Thus, from (
1.12
), we are reduced to compute
\operatorname{dim}({\rm Hom}(N_{\epsilon_{r}},\mathcal{G}_{r-1}))
for any
r\geqslant 2
. By recursively applying the first isomorphism in (
1.9
), if
r=2k+1
is odd, then
N_{\epsilon_{r}}=N_{1}
so

(1.13)

{\rm Hom}(N_{\epsilon_{r}},\mathcal{G}_{r-1})={\rm Hom}(N_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2k})\cong{\rm Hom}(N_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2k-1})\cong\cdots\cong{\rm Hom}(N_{1},N_{1})\cong\mathbb{C}.

When otherwise
r=2k
is even, then
N_{\epsilon_{r}}=N_{2}
and, from recursive application of the first isomorphism in (
1.9
), we get

(1.14)

{\rm Hom}(N_{\epsilon_{r}},\mathcal{G}_{r-1})={\rm Hom}(N_{2},\mathcal{G}_{2k-1})\cong{\rm Hom}(N_{2},\mathcal{G}_{2k-2})\cong\cdots\cong{\rm Hom}(N_{2},N_{1})=(0).

Putting together (
1.12

1.13
) and
(
1.14

\operatorname{dim}({\rm Hom}(N_{\epsilon_{r}},\mathcal{G}_{r}))=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $r$ is odd},\\
0&\mbox{if $r$ is even}\end{cases}

which, together with (
1.11
), gives

(1.15)

1\leqslant\operatorname{dim}({\rm End}(\mathcal{G}_{r}))\leqslant\begin{cases}\operatorname{dim}({\rm End}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1}))+1&\mbox{if $r\geqslant 3$ is odd},\\
\operatorname{dim}({\rm End}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1}))&\mbox{if $r$ is even}.\end{cases}

Since, for
r=2
,
\operatorname{dim}({\rm End}(\mathcal{G}_{2}))=1
because
\mathcal{G}_{2}
is simple, one deduces from (
1.15
) that, for any
r\geqslant 2
, one has
1\leqslant\operatorname{dim}({\rm End}(\mathcal{G}_{r}))=\operatorname{dim}({\rm Aut}(\mathcal{G}_{r}))\leqslant\lceil\frac{r}{2}\rceil
.

We deduce therefore that

\operatorname{dim}\left({\rm Aut}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1})\times{\rm Aut}(N_{\epsilon_{r}})\right)\leqslant\lceil\frac{r-1}{2}\rceil+1.

Hence, if
r=2k\geqslant 4
is even,

\operatorname{dim}\left({\rm Aut}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1})\times{\rm Aut}(N_{\epsilon_{r}})\right)\leqslant(k-1)+1=k

1.1

\operatorname{dim}({\rm Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1},N_{\epsilon_{r}}))\geqslant 2k+1
.

When otherwise

\operatorname{dim}\left({\rm Aut}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1})\times{\rm Aut}(N_{\epsilon_{r}})\right)\leqslant k+1

\operatorname{dim}({\rm Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{r-1},N_{\epsilon_{r}}))\geqslant 2k

k=1

r=3

k+1
. We are left with the case

1.1

1.4

\operatorname{dim}({\rm Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{2},N_{1}))=h^{1}(\mathcal{G}_{2}\otimes N_{1}^{\vee})=h^{0}(\mathcal{G}_{2}\otimes N_{1}^{\vee})+h^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{2}\otimes N_{1}^{\vee})-\chi(\mathcal{G}_{2}\otimes N_{1}^{\vee})

-\chi(\mathcal{G}_{2}\otimes N_{1}^{\vee})\geqslant 2

1.1
but also

(g_{2})

, i.e.

, whereas
\operatorname{dim}\left({\rm Aut}(\mathcal{G}_{2})\times{\rm Aut}(N_{1})\right)=2
, completely proving the claim.
