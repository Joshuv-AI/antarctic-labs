Counteranion-mediated dynamic kinetic asymmetric fluorination to access sulfur-stereogenic center
Authors: Zhihuang Chen, Wen-Yan Tong, Jiye Shu, Junliang Zhang, Xiaodong Xiong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSulfonimidoyl fluorides serve as versatile click linkers and have demonstrated significant utility in the construction of S-stereogenic centers for drug discovery and organic synthesis. Although several established methods enable stereoselective access to these aza−sulfur compounds, achieving direct enantiocontrol in the formation of S(VI)–F bonds remains challenging. Herein, we present a highly efficient strategy to synthesize sulfonimidoyl fluorides through the dynamic kinetic asymmetric fluorination at mild reaction conditions. This one-pot process, involving sequential direct fluorination, hydrolysis and asymmetric fluorination, delivers a wide range of enantioenriched S(VI) compounds with high enantioselectivities. This protocol establishes an ideal platform for concise synthesis of structurally diverse array of S−chirogenic motifs via stereospecific transformations. Mechanistic studies suggest that the steric hindrance and hydrogen-bonding between substrate and phosphate anion are responsible for enantiocontrol.Subject terms: Synthetic chemistry methodology, Stereochemistry

Introduction

Sulfur fluoride exchange (SuFEx) reaction was widely applied for molecular linkage in diverse fields, such as organic synthesis1–6 chemical biology7–11 drug discovery12–16 and polymer chemistry17–21, owing to the high stability, excellent dynamic reactivity and selectivity of the S(VI)–F bond. The SuFEx reaction is utilized to produce S–O, S–N and S–C linkages via the substitution of nitrogen-, oxygen-, and carbon-based nucleophiles6,22–28. Thus, the development of diverse SuFEx linkage agents with high molecular linkage efficiency shows considerable promise for SuFEx chemistry29–35. As the first generation of SuFEx linkage reagent, sulfonyl fluoride functions as a versatile synthon to produce high-value S(VI) compounds36–43. Sulfonimidoyl fluoride, the aza-isostere of sulfonyl fluoride with one substitution of S=N for S=O, serve as a stable precursor for rapid assembly of sulfonimidoyl containing molecular skeletons with sulfur-centered chirality, typically facilitating SuFEx click chemistry44–51. The introduction of nitrogen in sulfonimidoyl fluoride provide additional handles for increased flexibility, which variable electronic and steric properties were considered for precise adjustment of stability and reactivity, and functional optimization52–54. Such modification provides a new point for further functionalization with the potential to modulate the pharmacological potency of these molecules, which could be explored for the study of structure-activity relationships55–59. These favorable properties render sulfonimidoyl fluoride a valuable candidate for bioconjugation10, polymer synthesis21 and drug discovery16,53 (Fig. 1a). Moreover, chiral sulfondiimidoyl fluoride could demonstrate stereodependent biomolecular recognition, ascribed to the chiral lysine pocket in BSA60.

However, unlike the readily accessible sulfonyl fluorides, synthetic approaches to enantioenriched sulfonimidoyl fluorides still remain underexplored, significantly limiting their widespread applications. Traditionally, chiral sulfonimidoyl fluorides were prepared through the electrophilic fluorination of chiral sulfinamides under basic conditions24. The great breakthrough for catalytic synthesis of sulfonimidoyl fluoride was achieved via the chiral bidentate nitrogen ligands induced desymmetrization of iminosulfur oxidifluorides using organolithium as nucleophiles (Fig. 1b)61. In 2025, Jiang and co-workers demonstrated the enantioselective synthesis of sulfonimidoyl fluoride, involving F/Cl exchange at S(VI) centers62. Recent seminal work from Guo’s group developed a dynamitic kinetic asymmetric transformation process to prepare sulfonimidoyl fluoride by enantioselective F/Cl exchange reaction using chiral ArPNO as catalyst63. Despite great advances in the past few years, the development of efficient catalytic system for enantioselective synthesis of sulfonimidoyl fluoride is still highly appealing, facilitating the in-depth studies of this linkage agent with tremendous potential in SuFEx chemistry.

Ammonium ion-pairing catalysis has emerged as one of the most powerful strategy for asymmetric induction on charged species. Its successful application in the design of novel catalytic cycles has garnered sustained attention from synthetic chemists64–69. Ion-pairing interactions are primarily electrostatic in nature and lack the well-defined geometric constraints of covalent bonds or hydrogen bonds. The inherent flexibility makes it challenging to achieve precise stereocontrol in asymmetric catalysis, as the relative orientation of the cation and counteranion is not strictly enforced65. The successful implementation of counteranion-mediated ammonium ion-paring catalysis provides a platform for absolute stereocontrol in the development of highly enantioselective reactions for constructing C-based stereocenters and axial chirality that utilize positively charged reagents or proceed via cationic intermediates70–75. However, there is still no general method for enantioselective synthesis of sulfur-stereogenic compounds through the ammonium ion-pairing catalysis, thus limiting their applications in the areas of organocatalysis.

Inspired by our previous studies on asymmetric halogenation with hydrogen-bonded ion-pairing catalysis76–78 and enantioselective synthesis of sulfondiimidoyl fluoride79,80, here we exhibit a dynamic kinetic resolution (DKR) strategy for the direct construction of S-stereogenic centers. The sulfenamides undergo oxidative fluorination and subsequent hydrolysis to generate racemic sulfinamides, which were further converted to enantioenriched sulfonimidoyl fluorides through the fluorinative DKR utilizing ammonium ion-paring catalysis in one-pot (Fig. 1c). The obtained sulfonimidoyl fluoride serve as ideal platform compound, facilitating access to enantioenriched sulfoximine, sulfonimidate ester, sulfonimidamide and other S(VI) species. These chiral aza−sulfur compounds have gained considerable attention in modern medicinal chemistry and organic chemistry due to their unique and appealing properties45,59.

Results

Reaction optimization

We initiated our studies on the asymmetric fluorination of sulfenamide by choosing readily available N-(phenylthio)benzamide 1a and chiral phosphoric acid (CPA) C1 as the primary starting material and catalyst (Table 1). To our delight, target product 2a was produced in 40% yield when the reaction was performed in toluene at 25 °C for 48 h, albeit with 66:34 e.r. (entry 1). Based on this promising result, a variety of inorganic bases were investigated in the reaction, and the best enantioselectivity of product 2a was obtained in good enantioselectivity with NaHCO3 as the base (entries 1–4). Several BINOL-, H8-BINOL-, SPINOL-derived catalysts were evaluated and displayed remarkable effects on the outcome of this reaction. Other phosphoric acids failed to result in further improvement of stereocontrol (entries 5–10). Of the solvents tested for the reaction catalyzed by C1, toluene proved optimal with respect to enantioselectivity (entries 11–13). Almost no enantioselectivity of product 2a was obtained with hexane as the solvent, most likely because the catalyst C1 had less interaction with reactants. During the preparation of sulfonimidoyl fluoride from chiral sulfinamide, it was found that fluoride ions could cause recemization of the sulfonimidoyl fluorides through a degenerate exchange, which is avoided by fluoride trapping24. To improve the catalytic efficiency, the effect of additives was also examined in the presence of catalyst C1 (entries 14–17). Satisfyingly, we found that reactions conducted with LiI and PS (Proton-sponge) afforded the desired product 2a in improved enantioselectivity (entries 2, 16–17). Finally, by employment of the combination of LiI and PS, 2a was furnished in an acceptable yield and high enantioselectivity (entry 18). The reaction concentration has a fair influence on the enantioselectivity (entry 19). When less concentrated solution was employed, the enantioselectivity of the product slightly increased. Higher catalyst loading has a positive effect on the result, the desired improvement in enantioselectivity was observed (entry 20).

Exploration of the substrate scope

Under the optimized reaction conditions, we then investigated the scope of this ammonium ion-paring catalysis with readily available sulfenamides (Fig. 2). A wide range of aryl sulfenamides bearing electron-donating or electronwithdrawing groups at the para, meta, or ortho-positions all participated smoothly in this catalytic system, yielding the sulfonimidoyl fluoride products in high yields and excellent enantioselectivity (2a–2o), suggesting that the electron properties of sulfenamides have a negligible effect on the reactivity and stereocontrol of the desired transformation. Notably, halide substituents including chloride and bromide (2d–2e, 2h–2i, 2k, 2n and 2o) were compatible with the reaction conditions, allowing for potential downstream functionalization. Moreover, substrates with a substituent at ortho-positions were evaluated and found to deliver desired products 2g–2k with excellent enantioselectivities (up to 95.5:4.5 e.r.). These excellent results indicated that the steric interactions had less influence on asymmetric induction. Next, the scope of sulfenamides with various N-acyl groups was evaluated. It was found that the sulfenamides 1p–1s bear an electron-donating or electron-withdrawing substituent at the 4-position of the aryl group were compatible with the reaction, giving 2p–2s with excellent e.r. values under the optimized conditions. The reaction underwent smoothly when 2-substituted sulfenamides 1t–1v were subjected, offering enantioenriched products 2t–2v in excellent enantioselectivities. Sulfenamides with meta-substituted were well tolerated under the reaction conditions, giving the desired enantiomerically enriched products 2w−2aa smoothly. Furthermore, substrate with 2-naphthyl amide was also amenable to afford chiral sulfonimidoyl fluoride 2ab in moderate yield and high enantioselectivity. The amide with t-butyl group was also tolerated, leading to the corresponding product 2ac in moderate yield and enantioselectivity.

To further demonstrate the broad applicability of sulfonimidoyl fluorides, we explored their functionalization reactions, enabling the incorporation of carbon, oxygen and nitrogen fragments to yield the corresponding enantioenriched sulfoximines, sulfonimidate esters and sulfonimidamides (Fig. 3). Remarkably, a scale-up reaction (1 mmol) was performed, affording sulfonimidoyl fluoride 2a in 74% yield with 96.5:3.5 e.r., a result consistent with that obtained on the model reaction scale. The nucleophilic displacement of sulfonimidoyl fluoride using various organometallic reagents to generate a library of S–C coupling products 3 was studied. Following treatment of sulfonimidoyl fluoride 2a with methylmagnesium bromide produced the corresponding sulfoximine product 3a in high yield and enantioselectivity. Other alkyl and aryl Grignard reagents could also be employed as building blocks to provide the desired sulfoximine 3b–3d in excellent results. In addition, the N-Bz group of 3a could be readily cleaved to deliver the sulfonimine 3aa with a transferable N–H handle for subsequent elaboration in 85% yield, which is the key chiral intermediate for bioactive sulfoximine molecules, including BET inhibitor81, gambogenic acid derivatives82 and FEN1 inhibitor83. Then, the SuFEx reaction scope of sulfonimidoyl fluoride and phenolate to afford chiral sulfonimidate ester was also explored. A series of phenol as O-centered nucleophiles with either electron-donating groups or electron-withdrawing groups were smoothly converted to the desired sulfonimidate ester 4 in good efficiency. Notably, the naturally occurring estrone (4f) was readily modified at this mild reaction condition, which provided a versatile stereoselective aza-sulfonyl functionalization platform for biologically relevant molecules. Finally, primary and secondary amine nucleophiles reacted with sulfonimidoyl fluoride via SuFEx reaction to give the desired sulfonimidamides 5 with high stereocontrol. Encouraged by the broad applicability of the catalytic approach, the post-modification in biological compounds and commercial pharmaceuticals by introducing the chiral sulfonimidamide motif as a unique pharmaciphore was further investigated. The structurally complex molecules, including lomerizine (5c), perospirone and ziprasidone (5d), clopidogrel (5e), buspirone and piribedil (5f), paroxetine (5g) and desloratadine (5h) were subjected to this conversion, affording the corresponding enriched sulfonimidamide analogs with good results. The low stereoselectivity observed for 5g may be attributed to the formation of the S–N chiral axis, induced by the steric hindrance of the bulky 3-((benzo[d][1,3]dioxol-5-yloxy)methyl) group.

Control experiments were performed to get a better understanding of the catalytic stereoselective synthesis of chiral sulfonimidoyl fluoride (Fig. 4). The model reaction worked for 8 h to deliver the sulfinamide 6 in 90% yield with 50:50 er and only a trace amount of 2a, implying the enantioselectivity was not controlled by the oxidative fluorination and hydrolysis. However, when the reaction was conducted in the absence of NaHCO3, only a trace amount of 2a was observed, and sulfenamide 1a was recovered almost completely. To gain insight into the configurational stability of 6, the enantiomerization studies by use of enantioenriched sulfinamide (R)-6 were investigated. Direct stirring of sulfinamide (R)-6 in toluene for 48 h did not change the enantioselectivity, whereas a decrease in the er values of 6 was observed in the presence of base, indicating that base could promote racemization. Additionally, the presence of LiI and PS significantly accelerated the racemization of (R)-6. As evidenced by the time-course study of enantioselectivity of (R)-6, lower er of 6 was obtained when the racemization occurred for 48 h. Moreover, the racemic 6 was subjected to the optimized reaction condition, 2a was also obtained in moderate yield and excellent enantioselectivity (95.5:4.5 e.r.). When (R)-6 was employed, the reaction proceeded smoothly to furnish 2a in 76% yield and high enantioselectivity (92:8 e.r.), whereas (S)-6 gave comparable yield, albeit in high enantioselectivity (87:13 e.r.). The higher enantioselectivity obtained from racemic 6 (95.5:4.5 e.r.) compared to (R)-6 alone (92:8 e.r.) is attributed to the concentration-dependent background racemization of product 2a under the reaction conditions. Meanwhile, the 18O-labeled sulfinamide 6 was detected when the control reaction was performed in the presence of H218O, suggesting that a nucleophilic substitution process involving H218O is operative in the racemization. In addition, time-course kinetics further revealed that (R)-6 reacted nearly twice as fast as (S)-6 (kR/ks = 2.7), consistent with a matched/mismatched interaction between substrate and chiral catalyst. Taken together with the racemization and matched/mismatched data, these results support a DKR cycle in which (S)-6 undergoes continuous racemization driven by base, thereby regenerating (R)-6, and catalyst C1-matched (R)-enantiomer then undergoes the oxidative fluorination to deliver the enantioenriched product 2a. A slight negative nonlinear relationship between the er of C1 and the er of product 2a was observed, which implies that the exchange of both tetrafluoroborate anions with chiral phosphates was involved in the pathway before the reaction with substrate24.

Based on the observations above and previous reports, a plausible catalytic cycle is proposed in Fig. 570. The sulfenamide 1a underwent oxidative fluorination and subsequent hydrolysis smoothly to afford racemic sulfinamide 684. Catalyst C1 was readily transferred to phosphate A in the presence of base, which undergo salt metathesis with poorly soluble dicationic Selectfluor to produce chiral ion pair B. This ion pair is soluble in the nonpolar reaction medium, which is available to mediate the oxidative fluorination of sulfinamide (R)-6, generating the key intermediate C. The hydrogen-bonding interaction between the substrate and the phosphate anion fixes the position of ammonium cation in this intermediate, which is responsible for the asymmetric induction with high enantioselectivity. Upon reaction, fluoride is then delivered to the sulfinamide (R)-6 to afford chiral sulfonimidoyl fluoride 2a, and release catalyst C1 and the defluorinated monocationic ion pair D. The phosphate A can be readily regenerated by deprotonation and ion exchange, respectively. (R)-6 underwent the DKR fluorination to give 2a with high enantioselectivity, whereas (S)-6 isomerized to generate the sulfinamide sodium E85. The reactive sulfinate hypofluorite ester F is then formed via nucleophilic attack of the oxygen atom of E on Selectfluor. Subsequently, the intermediate F undergoes H2O-driven racemization through the nucleophilic substitution process, thereby regenerating (R)-6.

To gain insight into the origin of the observed enantioselectivity, preliminary density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed on the possible enantiodetermining transition states (Fig. 6, see SI). Based on previous reports that Selectfluor forms a stable cation-anion assembly with two phosphate anions, we optimized this ion pair and designated it as intermediate B70. In the calculated transition states (TSs) corresponding to the two stereochemical pathways (TS-major and TS-minor), the congested steric environment of B preferentially stabilizes the binding mode leading to TS-major, thereby favoring formation of the (S)-product 2a via the energetically more accessible transition state. A further comparison of the steric environment of B in the two TSs reveals that the %Vf of the pocket in the energetically favorable TS-major (SW quadrant: 18.7%, SE quadrant: 14.7 %) is notably larger than that in TS-minor (SW quadrant: 16.5%, SE quadrant: 11.8 %)86. This observation is also consistent with the results of the geometric analysis of sulfinamide 6: the key bond angle of stable 6 is 104.5°, which widens to 110.9° in TS-major (ΔEdist((R)-6) = 5.9 kcal/mol) while contracting drastically to 93.6° in TS-minor (ΔEdist((S)-6) = 8.8 kcal/mol)87. Furthermore, the sulfinamide 6 forms a shorter N–H···O hydrogen bond with the phosphate in TS-major (1.70 Å) than in TS-minor (2.11 Å). Consistent with this structural difference, the overall interaction energy is more stabilizing in TS-major (ΔEint = −9.7 kcal/mol) than in TS-minor (ΔEint = −7.0 kcal/mol). Thus, the reduced steric hindrance and stronger intermolecular interactions cause the energy of TS-major to be significantly lower than that of TS-minor.

Discussion

In summary, we have developed a highly enantioselective method for synthesizing enantioenriched sulfonimidoyl fluoride via counterion-mediated fluorinative dynamic kinetic resolution at mild condition. This protocol demonstrates general compatibility with a broad range of sulfenamide substrates, affording chiral sulfonimidoyl fluoride with excellent efficiencies and enantioselectivities. The resulting chiral sulfonimidoyl fluorides serve as versatile platform linkers for the assembly of diversified chiral aza−sulfur(VI) compounds through SuFEx reaction with various nucleophilic reagents, which offers an attractive strategy for the late-stage incorporation of the sulfonimidoyl moiety of natural products and drugs. Mechanistic studies obtained experimentally and computationally suggest that the oxidative fluorination of sulfinamides is the enantio-determining step. Notably, the steric hindrance and hydrogen-bonding interaction between sulfenamide and phosphate anion is key to governing the stereoselective formation of the chiral S–F bond. The efficient catalytic synthesis and transformation of chiral sulfonimidoyl fluoride furnishes an alternative strategy to address challenging enantioselectivity issues in S-stereogenic center construction.

Methods

General procedure for preparation of chiral sulfonimidoyl fluoride 2

To a stirred solution of sulfenamide 1 (0.1 mmol, 1.0 equiv.), C1 (10 mol%), NaHCO3 (0.3 mmol, 3 equiv.), PS (0.2 mmol, 2 equiv.) and LiI (0.2 mmol, 2 equiv.) in toluene (4 mL) was added Selectfluor (0.3 mmol, 3 equiv.). The reaction mixture was stirred at room temperature until completion (judged by TLC, 48 h). Upon completion, the reaction was quenched with saturated aqueous NH4Cl (5 mL). The organic layer was separated, and the aqueous layer was extracted with CH2Cl2 (10 mL × 3). The combined organic layers were dried over anhydrous Na2SO4, filtered, and concentrated under reduced pressure. The residue was purified by silica gel column chromatography (hexane/EtOAc = 10:1) to yield 2.

General procedure for synthesis of sulfoximine 3

Sulfonimidoyl fluoride 2a (1.0 equiv.) was dissolved in anhydrous THF (0.05 M) in an oven-dried round-bottom flask and was purged with nitrogen gas. The mixture was cooled to −78 °C before Grignard reagent RMgBr (2.0 equiv.) was added. After being stirred at −78 °C for overnight, the reaction was quenched with saturated aqueous NH4Cl (10 mL) and extracted with EtOAc (10 mL × 3), the combined organic layers were dried over anhydrous Na2SO4, filtered and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude product was purified by flash column chromatography to afford the desired sulfoximine 3.

General procedure for synthesis of sulfonimidate ester 4

Sulfonimidoyl fluoride 2a (1.0 equiv.) was added to an oven-dried round-bottom flask and dissolved in dry ClCH2CH2Cl (0.025 M), then ArONa (3.0 equiv.) was added. The reaction was stirred for another 20 h at 25 °C, then diluted with CH2Cl2 and quenched with sat. aq. NaCl solution. The organic layer was separated and the aqueous layer was extracted with CH2Cl2 twice. The combined organic layers were dried over anhydrous Na2SO4, filtered and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude product was purified by flash column chromatography to afford the desired sulfonimidate ester 4.

General procedure for synthesis of sulfonimidamide 5

Sulfonimidoyl fluoride 2a (1.0 equiv.) was dissolved in ClCH2CH2Cl (0.025 M) in an oven-dried round-bottom flask. Amine R1R2NH2 (2.0 equiv.) and Ca(NTf2)2 (1.5 equiv.) were added and the reaction was stirred at 25 °C until completion (judged by TLC, 12 h). The reaction mixture was concentrated. The crude product was purified by flash column chromatography to afford the desired sulfonimidamide 5.
