A Universal Magnetoelectric Limit for Chiral and Tellegen Bi-Isotropic Scatterers
Jorge Olmos-Trigo

Abstract
We reveal the existence of a universal upper bound on the magnetoelectric coupling of any bi-isotropic nanoparticle. The bound arises solely from energy conservation, making it independent of the specific material properties of the nanoparticle and illumination conditions. Moreover, the bound does not rely on reciprocity, being identical for reciprocal (chiral) and non-reciprocal (Tellegen) nanoparticles. We further show that the chiral Mie coefficient of spherical particles of arbitrary optical size obeys the same bound across all multipolar scattering channels. Our results introduce a universal metric on the magnetoelectric coupling of bi-isotropic objects, setting identical limits on chiral and Tellegen light-matter interactions at the single particle level.

A Universal Bound for Any Bi-Isotropic NP.–—
For any dipolar NP, bi-anisotropic or not, the energy conservation law can be written in terms of
\tilde{\alpha}
as
(
7
, Eq. (10))
:

\frac{\tilde{\alpha}-\tilde{\alpha}^{\dagger}}{2\mathrm{i}}\geq\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\tilde{\alpha}^{\dagger}\tilde{\alpha},

(3)

where
``\dagger"
denotes Hermitian conjugation.
The left-hand side of Eq. (
3
) is proportional to extinction, whereas the right-hand side is proportional to scattering.
For any lossless NP, extinction equals scattering, yielding zero absorption.

At this point, we express
Eq. (
3
) in terms of

\alpha_{\rm ee}
,
\alpha_{\rm mm}
,
\alpha_{\rm em}
, and
\alpha_{\rm me}
. This can be done by introducing the Hermitian positive semidefinite (HPSD) absorbance matrix
\tilde{\mathscr{A}}

2
;
26
;
55
:

\tilde{\mathscr{A}}=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}-\tilde{\alpha}^{\dagger}}{2{\rm i}}-\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\tilde{\alpha}^{\dagger}\tilde{\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathscr{A}_{11}&\mathscr{A}_{12}\\
\mathscr{A}_{12}^{*}&\mathscr{A}_{22}\end{pmatrix}.

(4)

The matrix elements of
\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
can be calculated using the
2\times 2
bi-isotropic polarizability tensor given in Eq. (
2
), yielding:

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{11}

\displaystyle=\Im\{\alpha_{\rm ee}\}-\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\left(|\alpha_{\rm ee}|^{2}+|\alpha_{\rm em}|^{2}\right),

(5)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{22}

\displaystyle=\Im\{\alpha_{\rm mm}\}-\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\left(|\alpha_{\rm mm}|^{2}+|\alpha_{\rm me}|^{2}\right),

(6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{12}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}-\alpha_{\rm me}^{*}}{2{\rm i}}-\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\left(\alpha_{\rm ee}^{*}\alpha_{\rm em}+\alpha_{\rm me}^{*}\alpha_{\rm mm}\right).

(7)

At this point, we use the fact that
\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
is, by construction a HPSD matrix. In particular, we use Sylvester’s criterion
25
, which, as a reminder to the reader, imposes that the principal minors of any HSPD matrix must be nonnegative. Applying Sylvester’s criterion to
\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
given in Eq. (
4
) yields

\mathscr{A}_{11}\geq 0,\qquad\mathscr{A}_{22}\geq 0,\qquad\mathscr{A}_{11}\mathscr{A}_{22}\geq|\mathscr{A}_{12}|^{2}.

(8)

It is important to note that any physically admissible polarizability model of a bi-isotropic NP must satisfy Eq. (
8
).
As a sanity check, we note that for
\alpha_{\rm{em}}=\alpha_{\rm{me}}=0
, one recovers the well-known relations for isotropic NPs

;
51
in the dipolar regime, namely,

\displaystyle\Im\{\alpha_{\rm{ee}}\}\geq\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}|\alpha_{\rm{ee}}|^{2},

\displaystyle\Im\{\alpha_{\rm{mm}}\}\geq\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}|\alpha_{\rm{mm}}|^{2}.

Moreover, if the NP is lossless, then we have
\mathscr{A}_{11}=\mathscr{A}_{22}=\mathscr{A}_{12}=0
(and the converse statement also holds).
In this case, the following relations are satisfied:

\displaystyle\Im\{\alpha_{\rm ee}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\left(|\alpha_{\rm ee}|^{2}+|\alpha_{\rm em}|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\Im\{\alpha_{\rm mm}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\left(|\alpha_{\rm mm}|^{2}+|\alpha_{\rm me}|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{\rm em}-\alpha_{\rm me}^{*}}{2{\rm i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\left(\alpha_{\rm ee}^{*}\alpha_{\rm em}+\alpha_{\rm me}^{*}\alpha_{\rm mm}\right).

We stress that any bi-isotropic NP must fulfill the previous identities in the absence of losses.

Next, we turn our attention to the first two Sylvester minors given by Eq. (
8

\displaystyle\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}|\alpha_{\rm em}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Im\{\alpha_{\rm ee}\}-\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}|\alpha_{\rm ee}|^{2},

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}|\alpha_{\rm me}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Im\{\alpha_{\rm mm}\}-\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}|\alpha_{\rm mm}|^{2}.

(10)

Equations (
9
)-(
10

\alpha_{\rm{ee}}=0
(
\alpha_{\rm{mm}}=0
), then
\alpha_{\rm{em}}=0

(\alpha_{\rm{me}}=0
), in close agreement with previous findings
3
. Having noted these well-known limits, we now maximize the
right-hand side of Eqs. (
9
)-(
10
) to determine the overlooked upper bounds of
|\alpha_{\rm{em}}|^{2}
and
|\alpha_{\rm{me}}|^{2}
.

Let
z
denote either
\alpha_{\rm ee}
or
\alpha_{\rm mm}
, and write

z^{\prime}=\Re\{z\}
and
z^{\prime\prime}=\Im\{z\}
. The relevant function to be maximized in both electric and magnetic dipolar channels is then:

f(z)=\Im\{z\}-\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}|z|^{2}=z^{\prime\prime}-\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\left(z^{\prime 2}+z^{\prime\prime 2}\right).

The function
f(z)
attains its global maximum, given by
3\pi/(2k^{3})
, at

z^{\prime}=0
and
z^{\prime\prime}=3\pi/k^{3}
. We next show that this complex-valued point (
z^{\prime}=0,z^{\prime\prime}=3\pi/k^{3}
) lies within the admissible domain of both

\alpha_{\rm ee}
and
\alpha_{\rm mm}
and is physically realizable. To show this, we write
\mathscr{A}_{11}\geq 0
and
\mathscr{A}_{22}\geq 0

\displaystyle\Im\{\alpha_{\rm ee}\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\left(|\alpha_{\rm ee}|^{2}+|\alpha_{\rm em}|^{2}\right)\geq\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}|\alpha_{\rm ee}|^{2},

(11)

\displaystyle\Im\{\alpha_{\rm mm}\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}\left(|\alpha_{\rm mm}|^{2}+|\alpha_{\rm me}|^{2}\right)\geq\frac{k^{3}}{6\pi}|\alpha_{\rm mm}|^{2}.

(12)

From these inequalities, it follows that the allowed values of

\alpha_{\rm ee}

\alpha_{\rm mm}

\alpha_{\rm me}

z^{\prime 2}+\left(z^{\prime\prime}-\frac{3\pi}{k^{3}}\right)^{2}\leq\left(\frac{3\pi}{k^{3}}\right)^{2}.

(13)

It can be noted that the complex-valued point maximizing

z^{\prime}=0

13

\displaystyle|\alpha_{\rm em}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\pi}{k^{3}},

\displaystyle\quad|\alpha_{\rm em}|=\frac{3\pi}{k^{3}}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\alpha_{\rm ee}={\rm i}\frac{3\pi}{k^{3}},

(14)

\displaystyle|\alpha_{\rm me}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\pi}{k^{3}},

\displaystyle\quad|\alpha_{\rm me}|=\frac{3\pi}{k^{3}}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\alpha_{\rm mm}={\rm i}\frac{3\pi}{k^{3}}.

Equations (
14

\alpha_{\rm me}

Importantly, the saturation of these upper bounds is not an independent condition on the cross-couplings alone. Instead, it reflects a fundamental constraint of the full dipolar response: when

|\alpha_{\rm me}|

Conclusions
\&
Outlook.—–
We have found a novel upper bound in light–matter interactions: the cross-polarizabilities
\alpha_{\rm em}
and
\alpha_{\rm me}
of any bi-isotropic NP cannot exceed one half of the maximum value of the polarizability of a resonant isotropic point electric dipole.
Importantly, we have demonstrated that chiral or Tellegen NPs can reach the upper bound only if they are lossless.

We have further generalized our findings to spherical chiral objects of arbitrary optical size. Specifically, we have shown that the chiral Mie coefficient
c_{\ell}
possesses an ultimate upper bound of
0.5
, which—just as in bi-isotropic NPs—can only be attained in the absence of losses.
Importantly, because the upper bound stems from energy conservation, it holds under general illumination conditions and for any specific choice of material parameters.

Ultimately, this work introduces a universal metric for the magnetoelectric coupling of bi-isotropic objects, quantifying how closely these can approach their ultimate upper bound.
