Mismatch Capacity under Stochastic Decoding
Francesc Molina and Albert Guillén i Fàbregas

Abstract
This manuscript investigates channel capacity under mismatched stochastic likelihood decoding. We derive Feinstein- and Verdú-Han-style bounds on the error probability coded communication. These are used to obtain a general information-spectrum formula for the channel capacity under mismatched stochastic decoding. The mismatch capacity formula is expressed as the supremum over all input distribution sequences of the limit inferior in probability of the sequence of normalized mismatched information densities. The resulting capacity formula is the mismatched analog of the channel capacity formula for the matched case by Verdú and Han. We also show that when the sequence of normalized mismatched information densities is uniformly integrable, the capacity formula admits an upper-bound as the limit of the corresponding sequence of expectations. This upper-bound is shown to be achievable for discrete-memoryless channels and product decoding metrics, showing that the Csiszár-Narayan conjecture is tight for mismatched stochastic decoders.



I
Introduction

Most coding theorems in information theory are often established under the assumption that the channel transition law is known at the decoder. In this setting, the maximum likelihood decoder is the optimal decoder in the sense that it minimizes error probability.
Other decoders that exploit the channel knowledge have also been studied in the literature, such as threshold, typical-set or stochastic likelihood decoders, and have been used to characterize channel capacity.

In situations where the channel law is unknown or too costly to compute, one naturally adopts sub-optimal or mismatched decoding metrics. Mismatched decoding is a variant of the reliable communication problem where the decoder employs a fixed, not necessarily optimal, decoding metric
[
12
,
5
,
11
,
14
]
. Examples of mismatched decoding are surveyed in
[
14
]
: channel uncertainty where the decoder only has an imperfect channel estimate available; finite-precision decoder implementations in which the likelihoods are computed using quantized or approximate arithmetic; nearest neighbor decoding in additive non-Gaussian noise channels;
zero-error and zero-undetected error communication; bit-interleaved coded modulation where independent bit-wise metrics are employed in place of the corresponding symbol metric; or computing achievable rates for channels with memory using simplified metrics.

To be specific, we consider a standard point-to-point setting in which
M_{n}
messages are to be reliably transmitted over a channel with transition law
W^{n}(y^{n}|x^{n})
with input-output alphabets
\mathcal{X}^{n}
and
\mathcal{Y}^{n}
, respectively.
We consider a communication system with an encoder and a decoder, where the encoder maps each equiprobable message
m=1,\dotsc,M_{n}
to an
n
-length codeword
x^{n}{(m)}=(x_{1}{(m)},\dots,x_{n}{(m)})\in\mathcal{X}^{n}
.
We refer to the codebook as the set of all codewords
\mathcal{C}_{n}=\{x^{n}{(1)},\dots,x^{n}{(M_{n})}\}
, with rate
R_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}
. In its simplest instance, the mismatched decoding problem is formulated as a variant of maximum likelihood decoding, where the channel likelihood
W^{n}(y^{n}|x^{n})
is replaced with a non-negative decoding metric
q^{n}(x^{n},y^{n})
. Upon observing
y^{n}
, the maximum metric decoder outputs the message whose codeword maximizes the decoding metric, i.e.,

\displaystyle\hat{m}=\argmax_{1\leq m\leq M_{n}}q^{n}(x^{n}(m),y^{n}).

(1)

The channel capacity
C
is defined as the supremum of all the code rates for which the error probability can be made arbitrarily small. Instead, the mismatch capacity
C_{M}
is defined as the supremum of all the code rates for which the error probability can be made arbitrarily small for a fixed channel-metric pair
(W^{n},q^{n})
. Even in the discrete-memoryless case, a single-letter expression of the mismatch capacity with the maximum metric decoder (
1
) remains elusive, apart from a small number cases. The literature is rich in lower bounds (see e.g.
[
14
]
and references therein) and only recently a few single-letter upper-bounds have been proposed
[
2
,
18
,
1
,
19
]
. The most common achievable rates are the generalized mutual information (GMI)
[
10
]
and the LM rate
[
9
,
4
]
, attained by i.i.d. and constant-composition random coding, respectively. Csiszár and Narayan in
[
5
]
conjectured that the mismatch capacity under decoder (
1
) for discrete-memoryless channels and product decoding metrics was given by the limiting multi-letter extension of the optimized LM rate. The conjecture remains open at the time of writing this article.

Given the difficulty of analyzing the mismatch capacity with decoder (
1
), a number of alternative mismatched decoders have been considered in the literature alongside a variety of techniques. Inspired by Shannon
[
16
]
, Verdú and Han used in
[
7
,
20
]
information-spectrum techniques to lower-bound the error probability as the tail probability of the information density, and derive a capacity formula for general channels in terms of the limit inferior in probability of the sequence of normalized information densities.
Based on this information-spectrum framework, Somekh-Baruch derived a general formula for the mismatch capacity for a broad family of threshold-type decoders, including maximum metric, margin and constant-threshold decoders
[
17
]
. Although very general, the capacity expression is multi-letter in nature and does not lend itself to simple analysis even in the discrete-memoryless case. For some specific decoders, excepting the decoder in (
1
), the expression allows to prove that the Csiszár-Narayan conjecture is tight
[
17
]
.

In this work, we instead consider mismatched stochastic likelihood decoders and characterize average error probability and mismatch capacity.
The main appeal of stochastic decoders lies in the fact that they enable markedly simpler derivations while, in the matched case, achieving the same rate and error exponent as maximum likelihood decoding.
Stochastic decoders were first considered in quantum information theory (see e.g.
[
8
]
) within the framework of positive operator-valued measures, the mathematical framework for describing general quantum measurements. Yassaee
et al.
employed the stochastic likelihood decoder to provide general one-shot achievability derivations in single- and multi-user scenarios
[
22
]
. Building on the ideas introduced in
[
15
]
for mismatched stochastic decoders,
we derive Feinstein- and Verdú-Han-type bounds on the average error probability for general multi-letter channels and decoding metrics.
Using the derived bounds, we find an information-spectrum formula for the mismatch capacity with stochastic decoders given as the supremum over all input distribution sequences of the limit inferior in probability of the sequence of normalized mismatched information densities, establishing a direct parallel with Verdú and Han’s general formula for channel capacity
[
7
,
20
]
.
An interesting consequence of our analysis is that the Csiszár-Narayan conjecture is tight for mismatched stochastic likelihood decoders.

II
Preliminaries and Contributions

This section introduces the main definitions relevant to this work. We consider a point-to-point reliable communication setting in which
M_{n}
equiprobable messages are transmitted over a channel with transition law
W^{n}
and input-output alphabets
\mathcal{X}^{n}
and
\mathcal{Y}^{n}
.
We begin by introducing the notion of an
(n,M_{n},P_{X^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}_{n}})
-code.

Definition 1
.

An
(n,M_{n},P_{X^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}_{n}})
-code is a set of
M_{n}
equiprobable
n
-length codewords
\mathcal{C}_{n}=\{x^{n}{(1)},\dots,x^{n}{(M_{n})}\}
, where
x^{n}{(m)}=(x_{1}{(m)},\dots,x_{n}{(m)})\in\mathcal{X}^{n}
for
m=1,\dots,M_{n}
. The rate of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is
R_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}
. The induced (equiprobable) codebook distribution is
P_{X^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}_{n}}(x^{n})\triangleq\frac{1}{M_{n}}\mathbbm{1}\{x^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{n}\}
.

Inspired by the work in
[
15
]
, we adopt a mismatched stochastic decoder driven by a general posterior distribution of correct message decoding, as defined below.

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{n}
be a
(n,M_{n},P_{X^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}_{n}})
-code and
q^{n}\colon\mathcal{X}^{n}\times\mathcal{Y}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
be the stochastic likelihood decoding metric.
Given the channel output sequence
y^{n}
, the stochastic decoder randomly outputs message
m
with probability

\displaystyle P_{\hat{X}^{n}|Y^{n}}(x^{n}(m)|y^{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{n}(x^{n}{(m)},y^{n})}{\sum_{\bar{m}=1}^{M_{n}}q^{n}(x^{n}{(\bar{m})},y^{n})}.

(2)

We next define the sequences of distributions and decoding metrics used throughout the paper and introduce the operators on sequences of random variables that serve the main tools of our information-spectrum analysis.

Definition 3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{X}=\{X^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be the input process and
\boldsymbol{Y}=\{Y^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be the output process induced by the channel. A sequence of:

i)

probability distributions for input process
\boldsymbol{X}
is defined as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}\triangleq\{P_{X^{n}}(x^{n})\colon\ x^{n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}.

(3)

ii)

channels with
n
-letter conditional distributions
W^{n}
is defined by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{W}\triangleq\{W^{n}(y^{n}|x^{n})\colon\ (x^{n},y^{n})\in\mathcal{X}^{n}{\times}\mathcal{Y}^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}.

(4)

iii)

stochastic likelihood decoding metrics with non-negative
n
-letter metrics
q^{n}
is defined by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{q}\triangleq\{q^{n}(x^{n},y^{n})\colon\ (x^{n},y^{n})\in\mathcal{X}^{n}{\times}\mathcal{Y}^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}.

(5)

Definition 4
.

The limit inferior in probability of a sequence of random variables
\{X_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is
[
7
, pp. 14]

\displaystyle\mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}X_{n}

\displaystyle\triangleq\sup\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\colon\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[X_{n}\leq\alpha]=0\},

(6)

the largest
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
up to which the limiting cumulative distribution is zero. Similarly, the limit superior in probability of a sequence of random variables
\{X_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{p}\limsup_{n\to\infty}X_{n}

\displaystyle\triangleq\inf\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\colon\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[X_{n}\geq\alpha]=0\},

(7)

the smallest
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
above which the limiting tail distribution is zero.

We next define the relevant magnitudes in this work: the channel capacity and the mismatch capacity under stochastic likelihood decoding.

Definition 5
.

The capacity of the sequence of channels
\boldsymbol{W}
, denoted by
C(\boldsymbol{W})
, is the supremum of all rates for which there exists a sequence of
n
-length codes achieving arbitrarily small error probability as the block length
n
grows to infinity.

Definition 6
.

The mismatch capacity of the sequence of channels
\boldsymbol{W}
with sequence of decoding metrics
\boldsymbol{q}
, denoted by
\widetilde{C}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{W})
, is the supremum of all rates for which there exists a sequence of
n
-length codes achieving arbitrarily small error probability as the block length
n
grows to infinity with the stochastic likelihood decoder (
2
).

Verdú and Han
[
20
]
developed the information-spectrum framework to characterize the capacity of general sequences of channels
\boldsymbol{W}
in terms of the spectral inf- and sup-information rates

\displaystyle\underline{I}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\imath(X^{n},Y^{n})

(8)

\displaystyle\overline{I}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathrm{p}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\imath(X^{n},Y^{n}),

(9)

defined for input and output processes
\boldsymbol{X}
and
\boldsymbol{Y}
, and where

\displaystyle\imath(x^{n},y^{n})\triangleq\log\frac{W^{n}(y^{n}|x^{n})}{P_{Y^{n}}(y^{n})}

(10)

is the information density, i.e., the
n
-letter information random variable whose expectation is the
n
-letter mutual information
\mathbb{E}[\imath(X^{n},Y^{n})]=I(X^{n};Y^{n})
.
The general channel capacity formula is given by
[
20
]

\displaystyle C(\boldsymbol{W})=\sup_{\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}}\underline{I}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})

(11)

where the supremum is taken over all
\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}
according to Definition
3
.

For the purposes of this work, we generalize the aforementioned information-spectrum framework by defining the spectral inf- and sup- mismatched information rates

\displaystyle\underline{I}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})

(12)

\displaystyle\overline{I}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathrm{p}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})

(13)

where

\displaystyle\imath_{q^{n}}(x^{n},y^{n})

\displaystyle\triangleq\log\frac{q^{n}(x^{n},y^{n})}{\sum_{\bar{x}^{n}}P_{X^{n}}(\bar{x}^{n})q^{n}(\bar{x}^{n},y^{n})}

(14)

is the mismatched information density.
The information density in (
14
) naturally generalizes to arbitrary alphabets by replacing summations with integrals under the corresponding measure.

.

The following relationships hold

\displaystyle\underline{I}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})

\displaystyle\leq\underline{I}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})

(15)

\displaystyle\overline{I}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})

\displaystyle\leq\overline{I}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y}).

(16)

Proof.

See Appendix
A
.
∎

In contrast to the matched case where
\underline{I}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})\geq 0

[

, Eq. (3.2.3)]
, in the mismatched case

II-A

Technical Contributions

Our main contribution to the study of stochastic decoders driven by mismatched posterior distributions is threefold:

1)=0em

Probability of error
. We derive Feinstein- and Verdú-Han-type bounds on the probability of error for general channels and decoding metrics.
Specifically, we show that there exists a codebook
\mathcal{C}_{n}
with
M_{n}
codewords, such that

\displaystyle P_{\!\rm{e}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}+\gamma\right]+e^{-n\gamma}

(17)

for
\gamma>0
, and where probability is taken under the joint law
P_{X^{n}}W^{n}
. In addition, for every codebook
\mathcal{C}_{n}
with
M_{n}
codewords the error probability satisfies

\displaystyle P_{\!\rm{e}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}-\gamma\right]-e^{-n\gamma}

(18)

for
\gamma>0
, and where probability is taken under
P_{X^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}_{n}}W^{n}
.

2)=0em

Mismatch capacity formula
. We provide the following general formula for the channel capacity under mismatched stochastic decoding

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{W})=\sup_{\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}}\underline{I}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y}).

(19)

The result extends the general formula for channel capacity in
[
20
]
, given in (
11
), by replacing the channel law with the decoding metric.

3)=0em

Mismatch capacity of discrete-memoryless channels and product metrics
. We prove that the Csiszár-Narayan conjecture holds for stochastic likelihood decoders. Specifically, we show that

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{q}(W)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{P_{\!X^{k}}}\frac{1}{k}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\log\frac{q^{k}(X^{k},Y^{k})}{\mathbb{E}[q^{k}(\bar{X}^{k},Y^{k})|Y^{k}]}\right]

(20)

can be achieved via the product-space improvement of either random i.i.d. or constant-composition codes.

III
Probability of Error and Capacity

In this section, we analyze the stochastic likelihood decoder given in (
2
). The corresponding
average probability of correct decoding for an
(n,M_{n},P_{X^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}_{n}})
-code
\mathcal{C}_{n}
is given by

\displaystyle P_{\!\rm{c}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M_{n}}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{n}}\sum_{y^{n}\in\mathcal{Y}^{n}}W^{n}(y^{n}|x^{n}(m))P_{\hat{X}^{n}|Y^{n}}(x^{n}(m)|y^{n})

(21)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x^{n}\in\mathcal{X}^{n}}\sum_{y^{n}\in\mathcal{Y}^{n}}P_{X^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}_{n}}(x^{n})W^{n}(y^{n}|x^{n})\cdot\frac{q^{n}(x^{n},y^{n})}{\sum_{\bar{m}=1}^{M_{n}}q^{n}(x^{n}{(\bar{m})},y^{n})}

(22)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{q^{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})}{\sum_{\bar{m}=1}^{M_{n}}q^{n}(x^{(\bar{m})},Y^{n})}\right]

(23)

where the expectation is taken under the joint law
P_{X^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}_{n}}W^{n}
. The average error probability is thus
P_{\!\rm{e}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})=1-P_{\!\rm{c}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})
.

We first examine the random-coding error probability. Building on the analysis in
[
15
]
, we derive an equivalent Feinstein-type lemma for mismatched stochastic decoders.

Theorem 1
.

Consider sequences of channels
\boldsymbol{W}
and decoding metrics
\boldsymbol{q}
.
For every
n\geq 1
, there exists an
(n,M_{n},P_{X^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}_{n}})
-code whose error probability satisfies

\displaystyle P_{\!\rm{e}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}+\gamma\right]+e^{-n\gamma}

(24)

where
(X^{n},Y^{n})\thicksim P_{X^{n}}W^{n}
,
\gamma>0
is arbitrary, and
\imath_{q^{n}}
is the mismatched information density defined in (
14
).

Proof.

The proof builds on the random-coding union bound in
[
15
, Th. 1]
showing the existence of a code whose error probability is upper-bounded by

\displaystyle P_{\!\rm{e}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\min\left\{1,\frac{M_{n}{-}1}{s}\frac{\mathbb{E}[q^{n}(\bar{X}^{n},Y^{n})^{s}|Y^{n}]}{q^{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})^{s}}\right\}\right]

(25)

for
s\in(0,1]
. This can be easily turned into a Feinstein-type bound by following the steps in
[
14
, Sec. 2.6.4]
: first using the upper-bounds
M_{n}-1\leq M_{n}
and
\min\{1,z\}\leq\mathbbm{1}\{z\geq\delta\}+\delta
for any
\delta>0
, and then taking logarithms on both sides. After some algebraic manipulations, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{\!\rm{e}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}\!\left\{\frac{M_{n}}{s}\frac{\mathbb{E}[q^{n}(\bar{X}^{n},Y^{n})^{s}|Y^{n}]}{q^{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})^{s}}\geq\delta\right\}\right]+\delta

(26)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{q^{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})^{s}}{\mathbb{E}[q^{n}(\bar{X}^{n},Y^{n})^{s}|Y^{n}]}\leq R_{n}-\frac{1}{n}\log s-\frac{1}{n}\log\delta\right]+\delta.

(27)

The desired result follows by setting
\delta=e^{-n\gamma}
,
s=1
, and using the mismatched information density (
14
).
∎

We next derive, for a fixed
(n,M_{n},P_{X^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}_{n}})
-code, a lower bound to the error probability that is reminiscent of the Verdú-Han lemma
[
20
, Th. 4]
.

Theorem 2
.

Consider sequences of channels
\boldsymbol{W}
, decoding metrics
\boldsymbol{q}
, and of
(n,M_{n},P_{X^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}_{n}})
-codes
\{\mathcal{C}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
. For every
n\geq 1
, the error probability of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
satisfies

\displaystyle P_{\!\rm{e}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}-\gamma\right]-e^{-n\gamma}

(28)

where
(X^{n},Y^{n})\thicksim P_{X^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}_{n}}W^{n}
,
\gamma>0
is arbitrary, and
\imath_{q^{n}}
is the mismatched information density defined in (
14
).

Proof.

We proceed by rewriting the probability of correct decoding (
23
) in terms of
Z_{n}\triangleq\tfrac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})
as

\displaystyle P_{\!\rm{c}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{q^{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})}{\sum_{\bar{m}=1}^{M_{n}}q^{n}(x^{n}(\bar{m}),Y^{n})}\right]

(29)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{q^{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})}{M_{n}\sum_{\bar{x}^{n}}P_{X^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}_{n}}(\bar{x}^{n})q^{n}(\bar{x}^{n},Y^{n})}\right]

(30)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[e^{n(Z_{n}-R_{n})}]

(31)

where
(X^{n},Y^{n})\thicksim P_{X^{n}}^{\mathcal{C}_{n}}W^{n}
. We first apply a decomposition over the disjoint subsets
\{Z_{n}\leq\xi\}
and
\{Z_{n}>\xi\}
for any
\xi>0
, as

\displaystyle P_{\!\rm{c}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[e^{n(Z_{n}-R_{n})}\mathbbm{1}\{Z_{n}\leq\xi\}]+\mathbb{E}[e^{n(Z_{n}-R_{n})}\mathbbm{1}\{Z_{n}>\xi\}],

(32)

and then upper-bound the two resulting terms separately:

i)

The first term is upper-bounded as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{n(Z_{n}-R_{n})}\mathbbm{1}\{Z_{n}\leq\xi\}]

\displaystyle\leq e^{n(\xi-R_{n})}\,\mathbb{P}[Z_{n}\leq\xi]

(33)

\displaystyle\leq e^{n(\xi-R_{n})}.

(34)

ii)

As for the second term, we use that from (
31
)

\displaystyle e^{n(Z_{n}-R_{n})}=\frac{q^{n}(x^{n},y^{n})}{\sum_{\bar{m}=1}^{M_{n}}q^{n}(x^{n}(\bar{m}),Y^{n})}\leq 1

(35)

for all
x^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
and all
y^{n}\in\mathcal{Y}^{n}
, and consequently

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{n(Z_{n}-R_{n})}\mathbbm{1}\{Z_{n}>\xi\}]\leq\mathbb{P}[Z_{n}>\xi].

(36)

Finally, by choosing
\xi=R_{n}-\gamma
we get

\displaystyle P_{\!\rm{c}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})>R_{n}-\gamma\right]+e^{-n\gamma}.

(37)

The claim follows from
P_{\!\rm{e}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})=1-P_{\!\rm{c}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})
.
∎

\boldsymbol{X}

\boldsymbol{Y}

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}\leq\sup_{\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}}\underline{I}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}>\sup_{\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}}\underline{I}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})

\limsup_{n\to\infty}P_{\!\rm{e}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})>0

Proof.

(Achievability)

From Theorem

, there exists a code
\mathcal{C}_{n}
for which the error probability is bounded for every

as

\displaystyle P_{\!\rm{e}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}+\gamma\right]+e^{-n\gamma}.

(41)

Setting

we get

\displaystyle P_{\!\rm{e}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})\leq\underline{I}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})-\gamma\right]+e^{-n\gamma}.

(42)

Thus, for any probability distribution sequence
\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}
, from the definition of limit inferior in probability in (
6
) we have that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}P_{\!\rm{e}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})\leq\underline{I}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})-\gamma\right]

(43)

\displaystyle=0.

Since this holds for every
\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}
, it also holds for the
\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}
achieving the supremum in (
39
).

(Converse)

Fix
\gamma>0
and suppose we can construct a sequence of codes
\{\mathcal{C}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
such that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}>\sup_{\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}}\underline{I}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})+3\gamma

(44)

and
\limsup_{n\to\infty}P_{\!\rm{e}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})=0
.
From the definition of limit, there exists an

such that for

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}>\sup_{\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}}\underline{I}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})+2\gamma.

(45)

Then, from Theorem
2
we have that

\displaystyle P_{\!\rm{e}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}\log M_{n}-\gamma\right]-e^{-n\gamma}

(46)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})\leq\sup_{\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}}\underline{I}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})+\gamma\right]-e^{-n\gamma}

(47)

which, by taking the limit as

and using the definition of

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}P_{\!\rm{e}}(\mathcal{C}_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})\leq\sup_{\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}}\underline{I}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y})+\gamma\right]

\displaystyle>0

.

We conclude this section by showing an upper-bound to the mismatch capacity formula via the uniform integrability of the sequence of normalized mismatched information densities, as shown below.

Proposition 1
.

If the sequence of normalized mismatched information densities
\big\{\tfrac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})\big\}_{n\geq 1}
is uniformly integrable, then

\displaystyle\mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}[\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})]

(58)

where the expectation is taken under the joint law
P_{X^{n}}W^{n}
.

Proof.

We use the shorthand notation:
Z_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})
and
\alpha\triangleq\mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}Z_{n}-\delta
for
\delta>0
. The proof extends that in
[
17
, Cor. 2]
by considering
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
uniformly integrable.
We first decompose
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}\mathbbm{1}\{Z_{n}>\alpha\}]+\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}\mathbbm{1}\{Z_{n}\leq\alpha\}]

(59)

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\mathbb{P}[Z_{n}>\alpha]-\mathbb{E}[|Z_{n}|\mathbbm{1}\{Z_{n}\leq\alpha\}].

(60)

Taking
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
on both sides,
\mathbb{P}[Z_{n}>\alpha]\to 1
and

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]

\displaystyle\geq\alpha-\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[|Z_{n}|\mathbbm{1}\{Z_{n}\leq\alpha\}].

(61)

We show that the second term can be made arbitrarily small by uniform integrability of
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
.
Specifically, by splitting the term over
\{|Z_{n}|<K\}
and
\{|Z_{n}|\geq K\}

[
21
, Ch. 13]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|Z_{n}|\mathbbm{1}\{Z_{n}\leq\alpha\}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[|Z_{n}|\mathbbm{1}\{Z_{n}\leq\alpha,|Z_{n}|<K\}]+\mathbb{E}[|Z_{n}|\mathbbm{1}\{Z_{n}\leq\alpha,|Z_{n}|\geq K\}]

(62)

\displaystyle\leq K\mathbb{P}[Z_{n}\leq\alpha]+\mathbb{E}[|Z_{n}|\mathbbm{1}\{|Z_{n}|\geq K\}].

(63)

Taking
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
on both sides, using
\mathbb{P}[Z_{n}\leq\alpha]\to 0
and the uniform integrability of
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[|Z_{n}|\mathbbm{1}\{Z_{n}\leq\alpha\}]

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon

(64)

for any
\epsilon>0
. Hence, substituting it into (
61
), recovering the definition of
\alpha
, and noting that both
\delta>0
and
\epsilon>0
are arbitrary, we conclude that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}Z_{n}.

(65)

∎

A direct consequence of Proposition
1
is the result below.

Corollary 1.1
.

The general mismatch capacity formula is upper-bounded as

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{W})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}}\ \mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})

(66)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}}\ \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}[\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})]

(67)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{P_{X^{n}}}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\log\frac{q^{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})}{\mathbb{E}[q^{n}(\bar{X}^{n},Y^{n})|Y^{n}]}\right]\!.

(68)

Proof.

The first inequality follows from Proposition
1
. The second inequality follows from upper-bounding each term of the sequence by the supremum over the input distribution set, followed by replacing the limit inferior with the limit as the involved sequence is non-decreasing in
n
and bounded by the matched capacity.
∎

IV
Special Cases

IV-A

Maximum Metric Decoding

We first consider the connection with the mismatched maximum metric decoder. As shown in
[
17
, Th. 2]
, the general capacity formula under mismatched maximum metric decoding is

\displaystyle C_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\text{mm}}(\boldsymbol{W})=\sup_{\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}}\ \mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{\Phi_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})}

(69)

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{q^{n}}(x^{n},y^{n})\triangleq\mathbb{P}[q^{n}(\overline{X}^{n},y^{n})\geq q^{n}(x^{n},y^{n})]

(70)

is the pairwise error probability for input
x^{n}
when
y^{n}
is received from the channel output.
From Markov’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{q^{n}}(x^{n},y^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[q^{n}(\overline{X}^{n},y^{n})]}{q^{n}(x^{n},y^{n})}

(71)

\displaystyle=e^{-\imath_{q^{n}}(x^{n},y^{n})}.

(72)

By taking logarithms and
\mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}
on both sides, the following holds for every
\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{\Phi_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})}\geq\mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n}).

(73)

Therefore, it follows that the capacity under mismatched maximum metric decoding is no less than that under mismatched stochastic likelihood decoding:

\displaystyle C_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\text{mm}}(\boldsymbol{W})

\displaystyle\triangleq\sup_{\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}}\ \mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{1}{\Phi_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})}

(74)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\boldsymbol{P}_{\!\boldsymbol{X}}}\ \mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})

(75)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{C}_{\boldsymbol{q}}(\boldsymbol{W}).

(76)

This is in line with the results of
[
15
]
for discrete-memoryless channels and product metrics. The bound in (
75
) also appears in
[
17
, Eq. (40)]
as a lower bound on
C_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\text{mm}}(\boldsymbol{W})
. Our results reveal that, in fact, it corresponds to the mismatch capacity under stochastic likelihood decoding.

The previous analysis can be easily extended to the generalized stochastic decoding metric
f(q^{n})
, with
f(\cdot)
a non-decreasing scalar function.
Since Markov’s inequality also holds when applying non-decreasing functions of
q^{n}
giving

\displaystyle\Phi_{q^{n}}(x^{n},y^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[f(q^{n}(\overline{X}^{n},y^{n}))]}{f(q^{n}(x^{n},y^{n}))}

(77)

we get

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{f(\boldsymbol{q})}(\boldsymbol{W})\leq C_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\text{mm}}(\boldsymbol{W}).

(78)

Thus, the resulting stochastic decoder after applying a non-decreasing transformation of the decoding metric cannot outperform the maximum metric decoder.

The following observations from Theorem

with block length

and

.
Consider the case of

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{W}(W)=\sup_{P_{X}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\log\frac{W(Y|X)}{\sum_{\bar{x}}P_{X}(\bar{x})W(Y|\bar{x})}\right]\!.

(100)

ii)

Erasures-only metric
q(x,y)=\mathbbm{1}[W(y|x)>0]

[
3
]
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{\rm{eo}}(W)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{P_{\!X^{k}}}\frac{1}{k}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\log\frac{1}{\sum_{\bar{x}^{k}:W^{k}(Y^{k}|\bar{x}^{k})>0}P_{X^{k}}(\bar{x}^{k})}\right]\!.

(101)

V
Conclusion

We have investigated mismatched stochastic likelihood decoders with general decoding metrics. We derived bounds on the probability of error similar to those of Feinstein and Verdú and Han. We derived the corresponding information-spectrum formula for channel capacity, which is the direct analog of the capacity formula of Verdú and Han for deterministic matched decoding.
We also derive an upper-bound on the mismatch capacity formula when the sequence of normalized mismatched information densities is uniformly integrable.
For discrete-memoryless channels and product metrics, we have shown that the aforementioned upper-bound is achievable, implying that the Csiszár-Narayan conjecture via the product-space improvements of both random i.i.d. and constant-composition codes holds.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
1

This appendix proves the following relationship for every input distribution sequence:

\displaystyle\mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})\leq\mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\imath(X^{n},Y^{n}).

(102)

The proof is relatively straightforward from writing

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\imath(X^{n},Y^{n})+\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{q^{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})}{W^{n}(Y^{n}|X^{n})}\frac{\mathbb{E}[W^{n}(Y^{n}|\bar{X}^{n})]}{\mathbb{E}[q^{n}(\bar{X}^{n},Y^{n})]}.

(103)

After taking
\mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}
on both sides of (
103
) and using Property 4 in
[
7
, pp. 14]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{p}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\imath(X^{n},Y^{n})+\mathrm{p}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{q^{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})}{W^{n}(Y^{n}|X^{n})}\frac{\mathbb{E}[W^{n}(\bar{Y}^{n}|\bar{X}^{n})]}{\mathbb{E}[q^{n}(\bar{X}^{n},\bar{Y}^{n})]}.

(104)

The final result follows from the fact that the latter term is shown to be non-positive. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{q^{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})}{W^{n}(Y^{n}|X^{n})}\frac{\mathbb{E}[W^{n}(Y^{n}|\bar{X}^{n})]}{\mathbb{E}[q^{n}(\bar{X}^{n},Y^{n})]}\geq\epsilon\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{q^{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})}{W^{n}(Y^{n}|X^{n})}\frac{\mathbb{E}[W^{n}(\bar{Y}^{n}|\bar{X}^{n})]}{\mathbb{E}[q^{n}(\bar{X}^{n},\bar{Y}^{n})]}\geq e^{n\epsilon}\right]

(105)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-n\epsilon}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{q^{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})}{W^{n}(Y^{n}|X^{n})}\frac{\mathbb{E}[W^{n}(Y^{n}|\bar{X}^{n})]}{\mathbb{E}[q^{n}(\bar{X}^{n},Y^{n})]}\right]

(106)

\displaystyle=e^{-n\epsilon}

(107)

where (
106
) is obtained from Markov’s inequality. Hence, the above probability vanishes as
n\to\infty
for every
\epsilon>0
, and, according to the operator
\mathrm{p}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
defined in (
7
), the infimum is upper-bounded by zero.

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
2

This appendix proves the uniform integrability of
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
with
Z_{n}\triangleq\tfrac{1}{n}\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})
for a product metric with a bounded single-letter decoding metric
q(x,y)
that satisfies

\displaystyle q_{\star}=\min_{(x,y)\colon W(y|x)>0}q(x,y)>0.

(108)

All we need to prove is that for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
K>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}[|Z_{n}|\mathbbm{1}\{|Z_{n}|\geq K\}]\leq\epsilon.

(109)

Uniform integrability is immediate under bounded and strictly positive decoding metrics. Our proof is more involved since we want to encompass the case where
q(x,y)=0
when
W(y|x)>0
, like in the zero-undetected error case.
Following
[
21
, Sec. 13.3]
, we show the above from a uniform bound on the second moments of
Z_{n}
. Specifically,

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}[|Z_{n}|\mathbbm{1}\{|Z_{n}|\geq K\}]\leq\sup_{n\geq 1}\frac{\mathbb{E}(Z_{n}^{2})}{K}

(110)

for every
K>0
. Hence, if
\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{2}]<\infty
, we can always choose
K
sufficiently large so that the right hand side is smaller than
\epsilon
, therefore proving that
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is uniformly integrable.

We now verify the required second moment bound:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\mathbb{E}[\imath_{q^{n}}(X^{n},Y^{n})^{2}]

(111)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}[(\log q^{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})-\log\mathbb{E}[q^{n}(\bar{X}^{n},Y^{n})|Y^{n}])^{2}]}{n^{2}}

(112)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\mathbb{E}[\log^{2}q^{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})]}{n^{2}}+\frac{2\mathbb{E}[\log^{2}\mathbb{E}[q^{n}(\bar{X}^{n},Y^{n})|Y^{n}]]}{n^{2}}

(113)

where we have used
(a-b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
.

We now bound each term separately, assuming that the single-letter decoding metric
q(x,y)
satisfies

\displaystyle 0<q_{\star}\leq q(x,y)\leq 1

(114)

for every
(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
with
W(y|x)>0
.
If
q(x,y)>1
, the above can always be enforced by scaling
q
by a positive constant, which does not affect the decoder. The bounds on each term rely on the fact that
\log^{2}(x)
is decreasing in
(0,1]
and thus
\log^{2}f(x)\leq\log^{2}\min_{x\in(0,1]}f(x)
. Specifically:

i)

For the first term,
q^{n}(x^{n},y^{n})\geq q^{n}_{\star}\cdot\mathbbm{1}\{W^{n}(y^{n}|x^{n})>0\}
and thus

\displaystyle\frac{2\mathbb{E}[\log^{2}q^{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})]}{n^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\log^{2}q_{\star}.

(115)

ii)

As for the second term, we use that
\mathbb{E}[q^{n}(\bar{X}^{n},y^{n})]\geq q^{n}_{\star}\cdot\mathbb{P}[W^{n}(y^{n}|\bar{X}^{n})>0]\geq q^{n}_{\star}/|\mathcal{X}|^{n}
and thus

\displaystyle\frac{2\mathbb{E}[\log^{2}\mathbb{E}[q^{n}(\bar{X}^{n},Y^{n})|Y^{n}]]}{n^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\log^{2}\frac{q_{\star}}{|\mathcal{X}|}.

(116)

By combining both results,
\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{2}]\leq 2\log^{2}q_{\star}+2\log^{2}q_{\star}/|\mathcal{X}|
. Hence, the statement follows since
q_{\star}>0
and the input alphabet is discrete.
