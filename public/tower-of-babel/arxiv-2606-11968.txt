Efficient Multinomial Logistic Bandit via Frequent Directions

Abstract
This paper studies efficient online algorithms for multinomial logistic bandits (MLogB), where the feedback distribution over K + 1 K+1 outcomes follows a multinomial logistic model of d d -dimensional action vectors. A representative UCB-type algorithm, OFUL-MLogB, achieves a regret bound of 𝒪 ~ ​ ( K ​ d ​ T ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(Kd\sqrt{T}) , but still requires 𝒪 ⁡ ( K 3 ​ d 3 ) \mathcal{O}(K^{3}d^{3}) time and 𝒪 ⁡ ( K 2 ​ d 2 ) \mathcal{O}(K^{2}d^{2}) space per round due to parameter estimation and optimistic reward construction, which is prohibitive in high-dimensional settings. To address this limitation, we propose EOFD-MLogB, which integrates frequent directions matrix sketching into OFUL-MLogB. By maintaining a low-rank SVD sketch of the accumulated Hessian, constrained online Newton updates in parameter estimation and K ​ d × K Kd\times K spectral-norm computations in the reward bonus are reduced to one-dimensional root-finding tasks and K × K K\times K eigenvalue computations, respectively. This yields dominant per-round time complexity 𝒪 ⁡ ( K ​ d ​ ( m + K ) 2 ) \mathcal{O}(Kd(m+K)^{2}) and space complexity 𝒪 ⁡ ( K ​ d ​ ( m + K ) ) \mathcal{O}(Kd(m+K)) , where m ≪ d m\ll d is the sketch size. We further prove a regret bound of 𝒪 ~ ​ ( Δ T ​ ( K ​ d ​ ln ⁡ Δ T + m ) ​ T ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\Delta_{T}(Kd\ln\Delta_{T}+m)\sqrt{T}) , where the sketching error factor Δ T \Delta_{T} is controlled by the m m -truncated spectral tail of the Hessian. Thus, when the Hessian is approximately low-rank, the regret is close to that of OFUL-MLogB. Experiments validate the computational efficiency and competitive performance.



1
Introduction

Stochastic multinomial logistic bandits (MLogB)
(
2
)
model a class of sequential decision-making problems with categorical feedback.
At each round
t\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, an agent selects an action
{\bm{x}}_{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and observes environment feedback
y_{t}\in[K]\cup\{0\}
,
where each outcome is associated with a reward
r_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
The feedback distribution is parameterized by an unknown environment parameter
W_{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
through
\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}}\left[y_{t}=k\mid{\bm{x}}_{t}\right]=[{\bm{\sigma}}(W_{*}{\bm{x}}_{t})]_{k}
, where
{\bm{\sigma}}(\cdot)
is the multinomial logistic function:

[{\bm{\sigma}}({\bm{z}})]_{0}=\frac{1}{1+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\exp(z_{k})},\quad[{\bm{\sigma}}({\bm{z}})]_{i}=\frac{\exp(z_{i})}{1+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\exp(z_{k})},\quad i\geq 1.

(1)

This framework captures a wide range of applications such as recommendation systems
(
18
;
19
)
,
where different user responses to a recommended item are mapped to different rewards.
The goal is to minimize the cumulative regret

\mathop{\mathrm{Regret}}(T)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\max_{{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}{\bm{r}}^{\top}{\bm{\sigma}}(W_{*}{\bm{x}})-{\bm{r}}^{\top}{\bm{\sigma}}(W_{*}{\bm{x}}_{t}).

(2)

A standard approach is the upper confidence bound (UCB),
which maintains confidence sets for
W_{*}
and selects actions using optimistic rewards.
A prior UCB-type algorithm, MNL-UCB
(
2
)
, combines batch maximum likelihood estimation with a first-order Taylor-based optimistic reward approximation,
achieving provable regret guarantees.
Its improved variant
(
2
, Appendix C)
further employs second-order Taylor-based optimistic reward approximation to construct a tighter optimistic rewards for the non-convex multinomial logistic reward,
but still relies on batch parameter estimation and hence full-history storage.
OFUL-MLogB
(
27
)
avoids this storage cost by using online Newton step (ONS)
(
12
)
for parameter estimation,
while retaining the second-order optimistic reward design.
However, it still maintains and manipulates a
Kd\times Kd
Hessian matrix,
leading to per-round time complexity
\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{A}_{t}|K^{3}d^{2}+K^{3}d^{3})
and space complexity
\mathcal{O}(K^{2}d^{2})
,
which are prohibitive in high-dimensional settings.

The bottleneck comes from the role of a
Kd\times Kd
accumulated Hessian in both parameter estimation and action selection.
The ONS parameter update requires solving a quadratic-constrained quadratic program (QCQP),
while the optimistic reward construction involves high-dimensional spectral norm evaluations;
both scale poorly with the Hessian dimension.
Although matrix sketching has been successfully used to accelerate stochastic linear bandits
(
14
)
, the nonlinear multinomial logistic model prevents analogous closed-form reductions in the associated QCQP and spectral norm computations.
Consequently, existing sketching techniques do not directly remove these MLogB bottlenecks.
Similar Hessian-induced bottlenecks also arise in ONS-based generalized linear bandits
(
28
)
and other second-order MLogB algorithms
(
17
;
4
)
.

We propose EOFD-MLogB, an efficient variant of OFUL-MLogB based on frequent directions (FD) matrix sketching
(
11
)
.
By maintaining a rank-
m
SVD sketch of the accumulated Hessian, EOFD-MLogB reduces the space complexity from
\mathcal{O}(K^{2}d^{2})
to
\mathcal{O}(Kd(m+K))
.
Moreover, the sketched spectral structure reduces the
Kd
-dimensional QCQP in ONS to a one-dimensional root-finding problem,
and reduces the
Kd\times K
spectral norm computations in optimistic reward construction to
K\times K
computations.
As a result, the per-round computational cost becomes
\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{A}_{t}|(K^{3}+mK^{2}+dmK)+dK(m+K)^{2})
.

On the theoretical side, we prove a regret bound
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\Delta_{T}(Kd\ln\Delta_{T}+m)\sqrt{T})
, where
\Delta_{T}=1+\rho_{1:T}/\lambda
is the sketching factor, and
\rho_{1:T}
is the sketching error term bounded by the
m
-truncated spectral tail of the Hessian.
Thus, when the accumulated Hessian is approximately low-rank, EOFD-MLogB achieves regret close to the
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(Kd\sqrt{T})
regret of OFUL-MLogB while substantially improving computational efficiency.
Finally, experiments on
MNIST
, synthetic data, and additional real datasets demonstrate significant speedups with competitive regret performance.

Notations.

For
W\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
, let
\overrightarrow{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{Kd}
denote its row-wise vectorization.
We use
\|\cdot\|_{F}
and
\|\cdot\|_{2}
for Frobenius and spectral norms, respectively,

\otimes
for the Kronecker product, and
\|{\bm{x}}\|_{Z}=\sqrt{{\bm{x}}^{\top}Z{\bm{x}}}
for the norm induced by a positive semidefinite matrix
Z
.
We use
\mathcal{O}(\cdot)
to hide constants, highlighting the dependency on
m,d,K,\kappa,T
,
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\cdot)
to further hide polylogarithmic factors.

2
Related Work

In this section, we review prior work related to MLogB and frequent directions.

2.1
MLogB and OFUL-MLogB

MLogB extends logistic bandits (
K=1
), a canonical instance of generalized linear bandits (GLB)
(
10
;
19
;
7
)
.
For logistic bandits, a UCB-type algorithm OL
2
M
(
26
)
employed ONS to estimate the parameter;
subsequent works
(
1
;
9
)
improved the regret bound to the optimal order.
The multinomial case (
K>1
) is more challenging, because maximizing the expected multinomial logistic reward is generally a non-convex problem.
The improved variant of MNL-UCB
(
2
, Appendix C)
and subsequent UCB-type algorithms
(
17
)
handle this non-convexity using second-order optimistic reward approximations,
but rely on batch MLE and storage of the full interaction history.
The state-of-the-art ONS-based OFUL-MLogB algorithm
(
27
)
combines second-order Taylor expansion of optimistic rewards with curvature-aware online Newton step (ONS) updates,
eliminating full-history storage but requiring the maintenance and manipulation of a
Kd\times Kd
Hessian matrix.

We next summarize the components of OFUL-MLogB that are relevant to our method.
After selecting
{\bm{x}}_{t}
and observing
y_{t}
, we define the logistic loss
\ell_{t}(W)=-\ln[{\bm{\sigma}}(W{\bm{x}}_{t})]_{y_{t}}
.
OFUL-MLogB maintains an estimate
W_{t}
of
W_{*}
, updated by a proximal ONS step each round:

W_{t+1}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{W\in\mathcal{W}}\underbrace{\left<\nabla\ell_{t}(W_{t}),\overrightarrow{W}-\overrightarrow{W}_{t}\right>+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\overrightarrow{W}-\overrightarrow{W}_{t}\right\|_{\nabla^{2}\ell_{t}(W_{t})}^{2}}_{\widetilde{\ell}_{t}(W)}+\frac{1}{2\eta}\left\|\overrightarrow{W}-\overrightarrow{W}_{t}\right\|_{H_{t}}^{2},

(3)

where
\widetilde{\ell}_{t}(W)
is a second-order surrogate of
\ell_{t}
at
W_{t}
up to an additive constant.
The Hessian is updated as

H_{t+1}=H_{t}+\nabla^{2}\ell_{t}(W_{t+1})
, with
H_{1}=\lambda I_{Kd}
.

For action selection, OFUL-MLogB chooses
{\bm{x}}_{t}
according to the UCB principle under probability at least
1-\delta
:

{\bm{x}}_{t}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}R_{t}({\bm{x}},\delta),

(4)

where

R_{t}({\bm{x}},\delta)=\begin{cases}\max_{{\bm{w}}\in\mathcal{C}_{t}}\sigma({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}),&K=1\\
{\bm{r}}^{\top}{\bm{\sigma}}(W_{t}{\bm{x}})+b_{t}({\bm{x}},\delta),&K\geq 2\end{cases}.

(5)

Here
\mathcal{C}_{t}=\{W\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}\mid\|\overrightarrow{W}-\overrightarrow{W}_{t}\|_{H_{t}}^{2}\leq\beta_{t}(\delta)\}
is the confidence region, and
b_{t}({\bm{x}},\delta)
is the optimistic bonus whose definition is deferred to (
10
) in Section
3.1
.
When
K=1
, we assume
(r_{0},r_{1})=(0,1)
, so the expected reward reduces to the scalar logistic reward
\sigma({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})
, and
\mathcal{C}_{t}
reduces to the corresponding vector confidence set; see Appendix
B.1
.
These optimistic constructions ensure, with probability at least
1-\delta
, that
{\bm{r}}^{\top}{\bm{\sigma}}(W_{*}{\bm{x}})
is upper-bounded by
R_{t}({\bm{x}},\delta)
for all candidate actions
{\bm{x}}
.
We refer readers to Theorem
1
and Lemma
7
for the corresponding guarantee.

Although OFUL-MLogB achieves a near-optimal regret bound
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(Kd\sqrt{T})
in the multinomial case
(
27
, Appendix C.5)
, its dependence on the full
Kd\times Kd
Hessian leads to costly
\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{A}_{t}|K^{3}d^{2}+K^{3}d^{3})
time and
\mathcal{O}(K^{2}d^{2})
space per round in high-dimensional settings.
Section
3.1
details these computational obstacles.

2.2
Matrix Sketching and Frequent Directions

Matrix sketching is a standard tool for reducing the cost of maintaining high-dimensional covariance matrices.
For a sketch size
m\ll d
and a stream of vectors
{\bm{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\bm{x}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a sketch maintains
S_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
such that
S_{t}^{\top}S_{t}\approx\sum_{s=1}^{t}{\bm{x}}_{s}{\bm{x}}_{s}^{\top}
.
Unlike randomized methods such as random projection
(
3
)
or column selection
(
8
)
, Frequent Directions (FD)
(
11
)
is deterministic and operates efficiently in a streaming fashion.

At step
t
, FD updates the sketch by appending
{\bm{x}}_{t}
to
S_{t-1}
,
forming
\widehat{S}_{t}=[S_{t-1}^{\top},{\bm{x}}_{t}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m+1)\times d}
, and computes its SVD

U\Sigma V^{\top}=\widehat{S}_{t}.

(6)

Since
\widehat{S}_{t}^{\top}\widehat{S}_{t}=V\Sigma^{2}V^{\top}=S_{t-1}^{\top}S_{t-1}+{\bm{x}}_{t}{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\top}
,
FD keeps the top
m
right singular directions and shrinks the singular values:

S_{t}=\sqrt{\Sigma_{1:m}^{2}-\sigma_{m+1}^{2}I_{m}}\,V_{:,1:m}^{\top},

(7)

where
\sigma_{m+1}
is the
(m+1)
-th singular value,

\Sigma_{1:m}=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m})
contains the top
m
singular values,
and
V_{:,1:m}
contains the corresponding right singular vectors.

FD has been used in stochastic linear bandits
(
14
;
6
;
24
)
and online convex
optimization
(
20
;
21
;
23
;
25
)
.
In stochastic linear bandits (SLB), sketching is effective because both the parameter update and the UCB bonus admit closed-form reductions via the Woodbury identity
(
14
)
.
In contrast, the nonlinear multinomial logistic model in MLogB does not admit the same closed-form sketching reductions, so new spectral reductions are needed.
We detail the resulting computational obstacles in Section
3.1
.

3
Efficient Algorithms for MLogB

We operate under the following standard assumptions
(
2
;
27
)
:

Assumption 1
.

The action set
\mathcal{A}_{t}
, the true parameter
W_{\star}
, and the reward vector
{\bm{r}}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}_{+}
are bounded:

\sup_{{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\|{\bm{x}}\|_{2}\leq D
,
\|W_{\star}\|_{F}\leq S
, and
\|{\bm{r}}\|_{2}\leq R
.
Without loss of generality, we set
r_{0}=0
as a dummy outcome,
and ignore
0
-th dimension in the subsequent analysis since it does not contribute to the reward and the regret.
See Appendix
B.1
for more details.

Assumption 2
.

The multinomial logistic mapping
{\bm{\sigma}}(\cdot)
satisfies strong convexity condition:
for any
W\in\mathcal{W}
and
{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{A}_{t}
, there exist constants
\kappa>0
and
L\in[0,1]
such that
\frac{1}{\kappa}I_{K}\preceq\nabla{\bm{\sigma}}(W{\bm{x}})\preceq LI_{K}
.

The constant
\kappa
typically depends exponentially on the domain diameter
SD

(
2
, Section 3)
.

3.1
Computational Obstacles

We analyze the two Hessian-induced bottlenecks that hinder the scalability of OFUL-MLogB.

Constrained optimization for parameter estimation.

The proximal ONS update (
3
) can be decomposed into an unconstrained gradient step followed by an ellipsoidal projection:

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{W}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\overrightarrow{W}_{t}-\eta\widetilde{H}_{t}^{-1}\nabla\ell_{t}(W_{t}),

(8)

\displaystyle W_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\|W\|_{F}\leq S}\left\|\overrightarrow{W}-\overrightarrow{W}^{\prime}\right\|_{\widetilde{H}_{t}}^{2},

(9)

where
\widetilde{H}_{t}=H_{t}+\eta\nabla^{2}\ell_{t}(W_{t})
.
The gradient step requires solving a linear system involving the
Kd\times Kd
matrix
\widetilde{H}_{t}
,
and the projection is a QCQP without a closed-form solution for a general Hessian.
Standard exact methods require a full eigen-decomposition
(
12
)
, leading to
\mathcal{O}(K^{3}d^{3})
time per round.
Moreover, simply replacing
H_{t}
by a sketched matrix
S_{t}^{\top}S_{t}+\lambda I
does not by itself yield an efficient projection,
which motivates our explicit use of the sketch’s spectral form.

High-dimensional spectral norms in optimistic rewards.

For
K\geq 2
, evaluating the optimistic reward
R_{t}({\bm{x}},\delta)
in (
5
) requires the bonus

b_{t}({\bm{x}},\delta)=\sqrt{\beta_{t}(\delta)}\left\|(\nabla{\bm{\sigma}}(W_{t}{\bm{x}})\otimes{\bm{x}}){\bm{r}}\right\|_{H_{t}^{-1}}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\bm{r}}\|_{1}\beta_{t}(\delta)\underbrace{\left\|H_{t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(I_{K}\otimes{\bm{x}})\right\|_{2}^{2}}_{\text{spectral norm term}}.

(10)

The dominant cost lies in the spectral norm term.
Specifically, computing the inverse square root
H_{t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
incurs
\Omega(K^{2}d^{2})
time (
\mathcal{O}(K^{3}d^{3})
via eigen-decomposition) and
\mathcal{O}(K^{2}d^{2})
space overhead.
Even after
H_{t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is formed, computing
\|H_{t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(I_{K}\otimes{\bm{x}})\|_{2}
still demands
\mathcal{O}(K^{3}d^{2})
time.
The eigenvalue-based implementation of OFUL-MLogB costs
\mathcal{O}(K^{3}d^{3})
time for each action
(
27
, C.3.4)
.
Thus, action selection becomes impractical in high-dimensional settings.
The same type of bottleneck appears in other MLogB algorithms using second-order optimistic bonuses
(
16
;
4
)
.

Contrast with SLB.

In stochastic linear bandits,
sketching is straightforward because the least-squares parameter update
\widehat{\theta}=H_{t}^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{t}y_{t}{\bm{x}}_{t}
and the UCB bonus
\|{\bm{x}}\|_{H_{t}^{-1}}
both admit closed-form Woodbury reductions to matrix-vector products
(
14
)
.
MLogB lacks this structure:
the nonlinear model leads to a constrained second-order projection (
9
) for parameter estimation and high-dimensional spectral norms (
10
) in the optimistic bonus.
These two obstacles preclude a direct application of standard sketching techniques and motivate the spectral reductions developed below.

We propose EOFD-MLogB (
E
fficient
O
ptimism via
F
requent
D
irections),
whose time and space complexities scale linearly with the dimension
d

3.2.1
Spectral Maintenance via Adapted FD

Unlike prior works that maintain the sketch matrix directly
(
14
;
20
)
,
EOFD-MLogB maintains a low-rank spectral decomposition of the sketched Hessian.
We approximate
H_{t}=\lambda I_{Kd}+\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\nabla^{2}\ell_{s}(W_{s+1})
by

Z_{t}=\lambda I_{Kd}+V_{t}\Lambda_{t}V_{t}^{\top},

(11)

where
\Lambda_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
is diagonal and positive semidefinite,
and
V_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{Kd\times m}
has orthonormal columns.
This spectral form is crucial for the efficient parameter update and optimistic reward computation below.

Standard FD is designed for vector streams, while each Hessian update in MLogB is a rank-
K
matrix:
\nabla^{2}\ell_{t}(W_{t+1})=\nabla{\bm{\sigma}}(W_{t+1}{\bm{x}}_{t})\otimes{\bm{x}}_{t}{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\top}
(cf. Appendix
B.1
).
To apply FD, we perform eigen-decomposition

PDP^{\top}=\mathop{\mathrm{EIG}}(\nabla{\bm{\sigma}}(W_{t+1}{\bm{x}}_{t})).

(12)

Then the Hessian update admits the block factorization

\nabla^{2}\ell_{t}(W_{t+1})=B_{t}B_{t}^{\top},\qquad B_{t}=PD^{1/2}\otimes{\bm{x}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{Kd\times K}.

(13)

EOFD-MLogB performs a single block FD update by applying FD to
[V_{t}\Lambda_{t}^{1/2},B_{t}]\in\mathbb{R}^{Kd\times(m+K)}
.
Specifically, compute

\bar{U}\bar{\Sigma}\bar{V}^{\top}=\mathop{\mathrm{SVD}}\!\left([V_{t}\Lambda_{t}^{1/2},B_{t}]^{\top}\right),

(14)

and update

\Lambda_{t+1}=\bar{\Sigma}_{1:m}^{2}-\bar{\sigma}_{m+1}^{2}I_{m},\qquad V_{t+1}=\bar{V}_{:,1:m}.

(15)

where
\bar{\sigma}_{m+1}
is the
(m+1)
-th singular value,

\bar{\Sigma}_{1:m}=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m})
contains the top
m
singular values,
and
\bar{V}_{:,1:m}
contains the corresponding right singular vectors.
The discarded mass
\bar{\sigma}_{m+1}^{2}
is accumulated into the sketching error
\rho_{1:t}=\rho_{1:t-1}+\bar{\sigma}_{m+1}^{2}
.
The procedure is summarized in Algorithm
1
.

, Hessian update
\nabla^{2}\ell_{t}(W_{t+1})=\nabla{\bm{\sigma}}(W_{t+1}{\bm{x}}_{t})\otimes{\bm{x}}_{t}{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\top}
, current sketch
(V_{t},\Lambda_{t},\rho_{1:t-1})

3.2.2
Optimistic Reward Construction and Parameter Update

EOFD-MLogB replaces the full Hessian
H_{t}=\lambda I_{Kd}+\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\nabla^{2}\ell_{s}(W_{s+1})
by the sketched Hessian
Z_{t}
in both parameter estimation (
3
) and optimistic reward construction (
5
).

In each iteration
t
, given the current estimator
W_{t}
, the algorithm selects an action
{\bm{x}}_{t}
according to the optimistic principle.
In the binary case (
K=1
), the decision rule simplifies to a linear estimation term plus a bonus vector norm term
(
26
)
due to the monotonicity of
\sigma(\cdot)\in(0,1)
,

{\bm{x}}_{t}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}\max_{{\bm{w}}\in\mathcal{C}_{t}}\sigma({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})\iff{\bm{x}}_{t}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{A}_{t}}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}{\bm{x}}+\sqrt{\beta_{t}(\delta)}\|{\bm{x}}\|_{Z_{t}^{-1}}.

(16)

The exploration bonus
\left\|{\bm{x}}\right\|_{Z_{t}^{-1}}
can be computed efficiently using the Woodbury identity:

Z_{t}^{-1}=\frac{1}{\lambda}(I_{d}-V_{t}\widehat{\Lambda}_{t}V_{t}^{\top})
, where
\widehat{\Lambda}_{t}=\Lambda_{t}(\lambda I_{m}+\Lambda_{t})^{-1}
.
In the multinomial case (
K\geq 2
),
the first norm term in (
10
) is computed using the same Woodbury identity for
Z_{t}^{-1}
,
and the second term requires evaluating the spectral norm
\|Z_{t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(I_{K}\otimes{\bm{x}})\|_{2}^{2}
.
The following lemma exploits the spectral form
Z_{t}=\lambda I+V_{t}\Lambda_{t}V_{t}^{\top}
to reduce this term to the spectral norm of a
K\times K
matrix.

Lemma 1
.

For any
{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{A}_{t}
, let
Z_{t}=\lambda I_{Kd}+V_{t}\Lambda_{t}V_{t}^{\top}
.
The spectral norm satisfies

\left\|Z_{t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(I_{K}\otimes{\bm{x}})\right\|_{2}^{2}=\|A({\bm{x}})\|_{2},

(17)

where
A({\bm{x}})\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times K}
is a symmetric matrix with entries given by

A({\bm{x}})_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{k}+\lambda}-\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)\left<{\bm{x}},{\bm{u}}_{ki}\right>\left<{\bm{x}},{\bm{u}}_{kj}\right>+\frac{\|{\bm{x}}\|_{2}^{2}}{\lambda}\delta_{ij}.

(18)

Here,
\mu_{k}
is the
k
-th diagonal element of
\Lambda_{t}
.

{\bm{u}}_{ki}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denotes the sub-vector extracted from indices
(i-1)d+1
to
id
of
V_{t}
’s
k
-th column,
and
\delta_{ij}
denotes the Kronecker delta.

Based on Lemma
1
, the optimistic reward becomes

R_{t}({\bm{x}},\delta)={\bm{r}}^{\top}{\bm{\sigma}}(W_{t}{\bm{x}})+\sqrt{\beta_{t}(\delta)}\left\|(\nabla{\bm{\sigma}}(W_{t}{\bm{x}})\otimes{\bm{x}}){\bm{r}}\right\|_{Z_{t}^{-1}}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\bm{r}}\|_{1}\beta_{t}(\delta)\|A({\bm{x}})\|_{2}.

(19)

After submitting
{\bm{x}}_{t}
and receiving feedback
y_{t}
, the algorithm updates the parameter estimate.
Since the parameter update in (
8
) and (
9
) involves
\widetilde{H}_{t}=H_{t}+\eta\nabla^{2}\ell_{t}(W_{t})
,
the algorithm maintains a surrogate Hessian
\widetilde{Z}_{t}=Z_{t}+\eta\nabla^{2}\ell_{t}(W_{t})
and decomposes it into spectral form.
Similar to Algorithm
1
, we compute the eigen-decomposition of
\nabla{\bm{\sigma}}(W_{t}{\bm{x}}_{t})
and then perform an SVD on the concatenated matrix:

\displaystyle\nabla{\bm{\sigma}}(W_{t}{\bm{x}}_{t})

3.3
Regret Analysis

In this section, we establish theoretical guarantees for EOFD-MLogB.
Let
\Delta_{t}=1+\rho_{1:t}/\lambda
, where

is the cumulative FD sketching error associated with

, defined in line 7 of Algorithm

We first establish a confidence set for the unknown parameter

Theorem 1

(Confidence Region)
.

Let
\alpha=2(1+SD)+\ln(K+1)
, and
\Delta_{t}=1+\rho_{1:t}/\lambda
.
Set
\lambda\geq\max\{2LD^{2},\alpha\max\{2\sqrt{6}SLD^{2},24Kd\}\}
and
\eta=\alpha/2

For any

W_{\star}\in\mathcal{C}_{t}=\left\{\,W\in\mathcal{W}\,\middle|\,\left\|\overrightarrow{W}-\overrightarrow{W}_{t}\right\|_{Z_{t}}^{2}\leq\beta_{t}(\delta)\,\right\},\quad\forall t\geq 1,

(26)

where the radius

\beta_{t}(\delta)=\mathcal{O}\left(\Delta_{t}\ln^{2}K\ln t(m\ln t+Kd\ln\Delta_{t}+\ln\frac{1}{\delta})\right).

(27)

The precise definition of
\beta_{t}(\delta)
is given in (
36
) in appendix,
and the detailed proof is provided in Appendix
B.2
.
The confidence region is defined with respect to the sketched matrix
Z_{t}
instead of the full Hessian
H_{t}
.
Compared with the OFUL-MLogB confidence radius
\widetilde{\beta}_{t}(\delta)=\mathcal{O}(\ln^{2}K\ln t(Kd\ln t+\ln\frac{1}{\delta}))

(
27
, Theorem 3)
,
the sketched radius replaces

by
Kd\ln\Delta_{t}+m\ln t
and introduces the multiplicative factor
\Delta_{t}
.
These terms quantify the cumulative approximation error introduced by FD.

Remark 1

(Independence of
\kappa
)
.

Theorem
1
preserves the
\kappa
-independence of OFUL-MLogB.
This follows because EOFD-MLogB keeps the same second-order surrogate in (

) and index-shifted Hessian

as OFUL-MLogB,
while the singular values discarded by FD are controlled by the upper bound

on Hessian updates rather than the strong-convexity lower bound

We next derive the regret bound for the multinomial case based on Theorem

Theorem 2

(Multinomial Regret)
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
1

1-\delta

(

and the replacement of

When

The sketching error is controlled by the spectral tail of the cumulative Hessian.

If the Hessian has slow spectral decay, the sketching error may grow and the regret bound correspondingly deteriorates.

.

The proposed spectral reductions are not specific to OFUL-MLogB.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed EOFD-MLogB to address the prohibitive full-Hessian time and space costs of OFUL-MLogB in high-dimensional settings.
By maintaining a low-rank FD sketch of the accumulated Hessian, EOFD-MLogB reduces parameter estimation and optimistic reward construction to efficient spectral subroutines.
It achieves dominant per-round time complexity
\mathcal{O}(Kd(m+K)^{2})
and space complexity
\mathcal{O}(Kd(m+K))
, where
m\ll d
is the sketch size.
Theoretically, we established a regret bound
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\Delta_{T}(Kd\ln\Delta_{T}+m)\sqrt{T})
, where
\Delta_{T}=1+\rho_{1:T}/\lambda
and
\rho_{1:T}
is controlled by the spectral tail of the cumulative Hessian.
This characterizes the FD approximation error:
when the Hessian is approximately low-rank, EOFD-MLogB maintains regret close to OFUL-MLogB while substantially reducing computation.
Experiments on real and synthetic data confirm that EOFD-MLogB delivers significant speedups with competitive regret.

The regret guarantee depends on the spectral decay of the accumulated Hessian;
for slower spectral decay, the sketching factor
\Delta_{T}
may become large.
Future work includes adaptive selection of the sketch size
m
and developing problem-dependent theoretical conditions or adaptive algorithms that directly control
\Delta_{T}
.

M_{t}:=\frac{\lambda}{2}I+\sum_{s=1}^{t}\nabla^{2}\ell_{s}(W_{s+1})
,
and by
\nabla^{2}\ell_{t}(W_{t+1})\preceq\frac{\lambda}{2}I
,
we have

H_{t}=\frac{\lambda}{2}I+\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\nabla^{2}\ell_{s}(W_{s+1})+\frac{\lambda}{2}I\succeq\frac{\lambda}{2}I+\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\nabla^{2}\ell_{s}(W_{s+1})+\nabla^{2}\ell_{t}(W_{t+1})=M_{t}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{t}\tr(\nabla^{2}\ell_{s}(W_{s+1})H_{s}^{-1})\leq

\displaystyle~\sum_{s=1}^{t}\tr(\nabla^{2}\ell_{s}(W_{s+1})M_{s}^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{s=1}^{t}\tr((M_{s}-M_{s-1})M_{s}^{-1})

(by the concavity of
y=\ln|X|
)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\sum_{s=1}^{t}\ln\frac{\left|M_{s}\right|}{\left|M_{s-1}\right|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\sum_{s=1}^{t}\ln\frac{\left|M_{s}\right|}{\left|M_{s-1}\right|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\ln\frac{\left|M_{t}\right|}{\left|\frac{\lambda}{2}I\right|}

By the FD Lemma
6
,
we have

\sum_{s=1}^{t}\tr(\nabla^{2}\ell_{s}(W_{s+1})H_{s}^{-1})\leq Kd\ln\Delta_{t}^{\lambda/2}+m\ln(1+2\frac{D^{2}}{m\lambda}t).

∎

Lemma 14
.

\lambda\geq 2LD^{2}
,
then for any
t>0
, we have

\sum_{s=1}^{t}\left\|I\otimes{\bm{x}}_{s}\right\|_{Z_{s}^{-1}}^{2}\leq\kappa\Delta_{t}^{\lambda}\left(Kd\ln\Delta_{t}^{\lambda/2}+m\ln(1+2\frac{D^{2}}{m\lambda}t)\right).

Proof.

H_{t}
be the original version of
Z_{t}
,

M_{t}:=\frac{\lambda}{2}I+\frac{1}{\kappa}\sum_{s=1}^{t}I\otimes{\bm{x}}_{s}{\bm{x}}_{s}^{\top}
,

\frac{1}{\kappa}(I\otimes{\bm{x}}_{s}{\bm{x}}_{s}^{\top})\preceq LD^{2}I\preceq\frac{\lambda}{2}I
,

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{t}\left\|I\otimes{\bm{x}}_{s}\right\|_{Z_{s}^{-1}}^{2}=

\displaystyle~\sum_{s=1}^{t}\tr((I\otimes{\bm{x}}_{s}{\bm{x}}_{s}^{\top})Z_{s}^{-1})

(by FD Lemma
5
)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\sum_{s=1}^{t}\Delta_{s}^{\lambda}\tr((I\otimes{\bm{x}}_{s}{\bm{x}}_{s}^{\top})H_{s}^{-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\sum_{s=1}^{t}\Delta_{s}^{\lambda}\kappa\tr(\frac{1}{\kappa}(I\otimes{\bm{x}}_{s}{\bm{x}}_{s}^{\top})M_{s}^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\Delta_{t}^{\lambda}\kappa\sum_{s=1}^{t}\tr((M_{s}-M_{s-1})M_{s}^{-1})

(by the concavity of
y=\ln|X|
)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\Delta_{t}^{\lambda}\kappa\sum_{s=1}^{t}\ln\frac{\left|M_{s}\right|}{\left|M_{s-1}\right|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\Delta_{t}^{\lambda}\kappa\sum_{s=1}^{t}\ln\frac{\left|M_{s}\right|}{\left|M_{s-1}\right|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\Delta_{t}^{\lambda}\kappa\ln\frac{\left|M_{t}\right|}{\left|\frac{\lambda}{2}I\right|}

By the FD Lemma
6
,
we have

\sum_{s=1}^{t}\left\|I\otimes{\bm{x}}_{s}\right\|_{Z_{s}^{-1}}^{2}\leq\kappa\Delta_{t}^{\lambda}\left(Kd\ln\Delta_{t}^{\lambda/2}+m\ln(1+2\frac{D^{2}}{m\lambda}t)\right).

\displaystyle~\sum_{s=1}^{t}\tr((I\otimes{\bm{x}}_{s}{\bm{x}}_{s}^{\top})Z_{s+1}^{-1})
