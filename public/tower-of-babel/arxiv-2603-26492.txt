The motivic tt-geometry of real quadrics
Jean Paul Schemeil

Abstract
. We study the tensor-triangular geometry of the category of Voevodsky motives generated by real quadrics. At the prime 2, we determine its Balmer spectrum, and find that it is a countably infinite, non-Noetherian space of Krull dimension 2. We detail the relationship between this space, the real Artin-Tate spectrum computed by Balmer–Gallauer, and Vishik’s isotropic points. We conclude by combining our computation with Balmer–Gallauer’s results on Artin-Tate motives to obtain a full description of the spectrum of integral motives of quadrics over real algebraic numbers.



1.
Introduction

Tensor-triangular geometry (
tt-geometry
for short) has found fruitful applications in many settings
[
balmer2010tensor
]
. A particularly active direction is
motivic tt-geometry
, whose long-term goal is to classify motives up to the tensor-triangulated structure of their ambient category

\DM(k):=\DM(k;\Z),

introduced by Voevodsky
[
voevodsky2000triangulated
]
. For
geometric motives
(the compact objects of
\DM(k)
), this amounts to describing the Balmer spectrum
[
balmer2005spectrum
]
of the tt-category
\DMgm(k)
. Although the current status of the problem remains largely open, recent work of Vishik
[
vishik2024isotropic
,
vishik2025balmer
]
has provided profound insights into the complexity of this space. When the characteristic of the base field
k
is zero, Vishik defined for any prime number
p
the category of
isotropic motives

\DM(k/k;\F_{p})
by localising
\DM(k;\F_{p})
at motives of
p
-anisotropic varieties (those whose closed points have degree divisible by
p
). Using this framework, Vishik constructed a family of
isotropic realisation
functors

\psi_{E,p}:\DM(k)\to\DM(E(\PP^{\infty})/E(\PP^{\infty});\F_{p})

indexed over all primes
p
and field extensions
E/k
. When restricted to geometric motives, the kernels of these
functors detect the largest currently known family of closed points of the spectrum of
\DMgm(k)
. As a striking consequence, over the real numbers
k=\R
, these realisations allowed Vishik to establish that
\operatorname{Spc}(\DMgm(\R))
has cardinality
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
. This bound provides a first measure of the complexity of this space, anticipating the challenge involved in achieving a complete topological classification.

A sensible approach to navigate this complexity is to study the spectrum of the motivic category "from below": rather than confronting
\DMgm(k)
all at once, one considers the tt-geometry of carefully chosen full tt
-
subcategories of the motivic category. The relevance of this strategy relies on a general theorem of Balmer
[
balmer2018surjectivity
]
who showed that the inclusion of a full tt-subcategory

\mathscr{L}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{K}

induces a continuous surjective restriction map on spectra

\operatorname{Spc}(\mathscr{K})\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Spc}(\mathscr{L}).

This surjection effectively provides a lower bound on the complexity of the ambient spectrum. In the motivic setting, manifestations of this idea are exemplified by the work of Peter
[
peter2013prime
]
and Gallauer
[
gallauer2019tt
]
who initiated the study of the spectrum of
Tate motives

\DTMgm(k)
; Balmer-Gallauer
[
balmer2022three
,
balmer2025spectrum
]
who introduced the study of the spectrum of
Artin motives

\DAMgm(k)
and
Artin-Tate motives

\DATMgm(k)
; and Sparks
[
sparks2025tensor
]
who studied the tt-geometry of these categories in isotropic contexts. The relevance of restricting to such full tt-subcategories is that their tt-geometry can often be described explicitly in terms of specific algebraic invariants of the base field. For example, over algebraically closed and real closed fields, Gallauer
[
gallauer2019tt
]
and Balmer-Gallauer
[
balmer2022three
]
demonstrated that the spectrum of Tate motives admits a description in terms of the
motivic cohomology
of the base field; for Artin motives, the recent breakthrough of Balmer-Gallauer
[
balmer2025spectrum
]
has shown that, over any field, the spectrum can be expressed in terms of the
absolute Galois group
of the base field. Furthermore, Artin-Tate motives over real closed fields, Balmer-Gallauer
[
balmer2022three
]
have obtained a corresponding description involving a combination of the above invariants. Finally, in isotropic contexts, Sparks
[
sparks2025tensor
]
has shown that at the prime
2
, the tt-geometry of
isotropic (Artin-)Tate motives over flexible fields
can be described in terms of the
isotropic motivic cohomology
of the base field.

Following the themes outlined above, in this article we propose to initiate the study of the Balmer spectrum of the full tt-subcategory generated by
motives of smooth projective quadrics

\DQMgm(k;R).

Originally introduced by Bachmann
[
bachmann2017invertibility
]
to study the
Picard group
of the motivic category, this subcategory provides a natural first candidate to incorporate motives of higher dimensional varieties into the landscape of motivic tt-geometry. To set the stage, we briefly address the case when the base field
k
is algebraically closed. Because all quadratic forms over such a field are isotropic, the motives of quadrics completely decompose into Tate motives; consequently,
\DQMgm(k,R)=\DTMgm(k,R)
. Thanks to the work of Peter
[
peter2013prime
]
and Gallauer
[
gallauer2019tt
]
, the tt-geometry of this category is already well understood. However, if
k
is not algebraically closed,
\DTMgm(k;R)
forms a proper tt-subcategory of
\DQMgm(k;R)
. In general, it is expected that the tt-geometry of
\DQMgm(k;R)
reflects the complexity of the theory of quadratic forms over
k
: this is because
\DQMgm(k;R)
is generated by Tate motives and by
motives of anisotropic quadrics
, which correspond to anisotropic quadratic forms over
k
. This naturally identifies the case
k=\R
(or more generally, any real closed field) as the natural first step beyond the algebraically closed fields, and it will represent the primary focus of this article. Indeed, we have very good control over the theory of quadratic forms over
\R
; moreover, if
R=\F_{p}
for
p
an odd prime (or more generally,
2
is invertible in
R
), then motives of quadrics decompose into Tate motives and motives of quadratic extensions (i.e.
\M(\C)
), in particular we have an equivalence

\DATMgm(\R;\F_{p})\simeq\DQMgm(\R;\F_{p}).

By
[
balmer2022three
, Corollary 11.2]
, this spectrum is completely understood. However, for
R=\F_{2}
(or more generally,
2
is not a unit of
R
), the inclusion

\DATMgm(\R;\F_{2})\hookrightarrow\DQMgm(\R;\F_{2})

is far from being an equivalence, since motives of arbitrary dimensional
2
-anisotropic quadrics enter the picture, making the tt-category
\DQMgm(\R;\F_{2})
substantially richer. The main result of this article is the computation of the Balmer spectrum of this tt-category.

We construct a family of geometric functors out of the big tt-category

Computing

[
sparks2025tensor
]

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we briefly recall the tt-geometric and motivic background used throughout the paper, and we fix notation and terminology.

2.1.
tt-geometric recollections

Throughout, let
\mathscr{K}
be a
rigidly-compactly generated big tt-category
, and let
\mathscr{K}^{c}
denote its essentially small rigid tt-subcategory of compact objects. Following
[
balmer2005spectrum
]
, the
Balmer spectrum
of
\mathscr{K}^{c}
is the topological space

\operatorname{Spc}(\mathscr{K}^{c}),

whose underlying set consists of prime tt-ideals of
\mathscr{K}^{c}
, endowed with the topology for which a basis of closed sets is given by the supports

\operatorname{supp}(X):=\{\,\p\in\operatorname{Spc}(\mathscr{K}^{c})\mid X\notin\p\,\},

where
X
ranges over all objects of
\mathscr{K}^{c}
.

In these settings, the assignment

\mathscr{J}\longmapsto\bigcup_{X\in\mathscr{J}}\operatorname{supp}(X)

induces an inclusion-preserving bijection between (radical) tt-ideals
\mathscr{J}\subseteq\mathscr{K}^{c}
and Thomason subsets of
\operatorname{Spc}(\mathscr{K}^{c})
.

Jointly conservative families of geometric functors

Let
F:\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{L}
be a tt-functor between rigidly-compactly generated big tt-categories. We say that
F
is
geometric
if it preserves coproducts. Any geometric tt-functor preserves compact objects, hence restricts to a tt-functor

F^{c}:\mathscr{K}^{c}\to\mathscr{L}^{c}.

By the functoriality of the Balmer spectrum,
F^{c}
induces a continuous spectral map

\operatorname{Spc}(F^{c})\colon\operatorname{Spc}(\mathscr{L}^{c})\to\operatorname{Spc}(\mathscr{K}^{c}),

given by the pull-back of prime tt-ideals.

A family of geometric functors
\{F_{i}\colon\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{K}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is called
jointly conservative
if
F_{i}(X)=0
for all
i\in I
implies
X=0
.

The relevance of this notion lies in the following fundamental result in tt-geometry.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
barthel2024surjectivity
]
)
.

If a family of geometric functors as above is jointly conservative, then the induced map on spectra

\bigsqcup_{i\in I}\operatorname{Spc}(F^{c}_{i}):\bigsqcup_{i\in I}\operatorname{Spc}(\mathscr{K}_{i}^{c})\to\operatorname{Spc}(\mathscr{K}^{c})

is surjective.

Smashing localisations

(
[
balmer2011generalized
]
)
A full triangulated subcategory
\mathscr{J}\subseteq\mathscr{K}
is called a
localising tensor-ideal
if it is closed under arbitrary coproducts and satisfies
\mathscr{K}\otimes\mathscr{J}\subseteq\mathscr{J}
. For a collection of objects
S\subseteq\mathscr{K}
, we write
\Loc_{\otimes}(S)
for the smallest localising tensor-ideal containing
S
.

Remark 2.2
.

(
[
greenlees2019balmer
, Theorem 8.1]
,
[
stevenson2013support
]
)
If
S
is a set of compact objects, then the tt-ideal
\langle S\rangle\subseteq\mathscr{K}^{c}
identifies as

\langle S\rangle\;=\;\Loc_{\otimes}(S)\cap\mathscr{K}^{c}.

A
localisation
of
\mathscr{K}
is a pair
(L,\lambda)
, where
L:\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{K}
is an exact functor and
\lambda
is a natural transformation
\lambda:\mathrm{Id}_{\mathscr{K}}\to L
such that
L\lambda=\lambda L
and
L\lambda:L\to L^{2}
is an isomorphism. A colocalisation of
\mathscr{K}
is a pair
(\Gamma,\gamma)
such that
(\Gamma^{op},\gamma^{op})
is a localisation of
\mathscr{K}^{op}
.

A localisation
(L,\lambda)
of
\mathscr{K}
is called
smashing
if
L
preserves coproducts. A localising tensor-ideal
\mathscr{S}\subseteq\mathscr{K}
is called
smashing
if there exists a smashing localisation
(L_{\mathscr{S}},\lambda)
of
\mathscr{K}
such that
\ker(L_{\mathscr{S}})=\mathscr{S}
. In this case, there is an associated colocalisation functor
(\Gamma_{\mathscr{S}},\gamma)
such that
\im(\Gamma_{\mathscr{S}})=\mathscr{S}
. Moreover, there is an idempotent triangle

\Gamma_{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbbm{1})\longrightarrow\mathbbm{1}\longrightarrow L_{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbbm{1})\longrightarrow\Gamma_{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbbm{1})[1].

Crucially, in these settings, smashing localisations are in one-to-one correspondence with idempotent triangles
[
balmer2011generalized
, Theorem 3.5]
. In particular, there are natural isomorphisms

L_{\mathscr{S}}\simeq L_{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbbm{1})\otimes-,\qquad\Gamma_{\mathscr{S}}\simeq\Gamma_{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbbm{1})\otimes-.

Conversely, any idempotent triangle in
\mathscr{K}

X\to\mathbbm{1}\to Y\to X[1]

gives rise to a smashing localisation
L_{Y}=Y\otimes-:\mathscr{K}\to\mathscr{K}
.

Weight structures

(
[
bondarko2010weight
]
)
A
weight structure
on a triangulated category
\mathscr{T}
is a pair of full subcategories
\mathscr{T}^{\leq 0},\mathscr{T}^{\geq 0}\subseteq\mathscr{T}
satisfying the following axioms:

(1)

\mathscr{T}^{\leq 0}
and
\mathscr{T}^{\geq 0}
are idempotent-complete.

(2)

\mathscr{T}^{\leq 0}\subseteq\mathscr{T}^{\leq 0}[1]
and
\mathscr{T}^{\geq 0}[1]\subseteq\mathscr{T}^{\geq 0}
.

(3)

Orthogonality
: For every
X\in\mathscr{T}^{\leq 0}
and
Y\in\mathscr{T}^{\geq 0}
, we have
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathscr{T}}(X,Y[1])=0
.

(4)

Weight decomposition
: For every object
X\in\mathscr{T}
, there exists a distinguished triangle

B\to X\to A\to B,

with
B\in\mathscr{T}^{\leq 0}
and
A\in\mathscr{T}^{\geq 0}[1]
.

We denote the shifted subcategories and their intersection by

\mathscr{T}^{\leq n}:=\mathscr{T}^{\leq 0}[n],\quad\mathscr{T}^{\geq n}:=\mathscr{T}^{\geq 0}[n],\quad\mathscr{T}^{=0}:=\mathscr{T}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathscr{T}^{\geq 0},

and we call
\mathscr{T}^{=0}
the
heart
of the weight structure. A weight structure is
bounded
if it exhausts the category:

\bigcup_{n\in\Z}\mathscr{T}^{\leq n}=\mathscr{T}=\bigcup_{n\in\Z}\mathscr{T}^{\geq n}.

\mathscr{T}
is equipped with a bounded weight structure, any object
X\in\mathscr{T}
can be expressed as an iterative extension of finitely many graded pieces
X_{i}=C_{i}[i]
, where
C_{i}\in\mathscr{T}^{=0}
. These pieces may be arranged into a finite
weight complex
for

, which we denote as follows:

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[

, Theorem 4.3.2]
)

Suppose that
\mathscr{T}
is idempotent-complete and let

be a collection of objects in
\mathscr{T}
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

Negativity:
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathscr{T}}(X,Y[n])=0
for all

and

.

(2)

is all of

.

Then there exists a unique weight structure on
\mathscr{T}
whose heart

is the idempotent-completion of the additive subcategory generated by

.

\mathscr{K}^{c}
that admit a stable symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categorical enhancement. When such a bounded weight structure is compatible with the tensor structure–meaning its heart
\mathscr{H}=(\mathscr{K}^{c})^{=0}
contains the tensor unit
\mathbbm{1}
and it is closed under tensor products–then by

]
and
[

]
, we may define a

which is a conservative tt-functor.

.

A useful consequence of the above result is that if
\K^{b}(\mathscr{H})
is a

tt-category (that is,

is a prime tt-ideal)–for instance, if
\mathscr{H}
is semisimple–then the conservativity of the weight complex functor implies that

is also local.

Motives of Čech simplicial schemes

(

For a smooth variety

(1)

(2)

and

are connected smooth projective varieties, then

\mathscr{X}_{P}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\geq}}\mathscr{X}_{Q}\iff k(P)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\geq}}k(Q).

(4)

The structure map
\mathscr{X}_{P}\to\T
factors through the structure map
\mathscr{X}_{Q}\to\T
.

3.
Mixed motives of quadrics

We introduce the main categories of interest of this article.

Definition 3.1
.

The big tt-category of
mixed motives of quadrics

\DQM(k;R)

is defined as the full localising subcategory of
\DM(k,R)
generated by all twists of the motives of smooth projective quadrics over
k
. We denote by

\DQMgm(k;R)

its full tt-subcategory of compact objects.

From now on, we restrict our attention to the case of primary interest,
R=\F_{2}
. Nevertheless, it is clear that the following definitions readily generalise to more general coefficient rings.

Definition 3.2
.

For any non-zero pure symbol
\alpha\in\K^{M}_{*}(k)/2
, we denote by

\DQM_{\tilde{\mathscr{X}}_{\alpha}}(k;\F_{2})

the full localising subcategory of
\DQM(k;\F_{2})
consisting of objects that are annihilated upon tensoring with
\mathscr{X}_{\alpha}
. Furthermore, we define

\DTM_{\tilde{\mathscr{X}}_{\alpha}}(k;\F_{2})

to be the full localising subcategory of
\DQM_{\tilde{\mathscr{X}}_{\alpha}}(k;\F_{2})
generated by twists of
\tilde{\mathscr{X}}_{\alpha}
.

Observe that
\tilde{\mathscr{X}}_{\alpha}
is a right idempotent, appearing in the idempotent triangle

\mathscr{X}_{\alpha}\to\T\to\tilde{\mathscr{X}}_{\alpha}\to\mathscr{X}_{\alpha}[1].

In particular
\DQM_{\tilde{\mathscr{X}}_{\alpha}}(k;\F_{2})=\tilde{\mathscr{X}}_{\alpha}\otimes\DQM(k;\F_{2})
is a smashing localisation, whose kernel is the localising tensor ideal generated by the Rost motive
\M_{\alpha}
. This implies that
\DQM_{\tilde{\mathscr{X}}_{\alpha}}(k;\F_{2})
is a rigidly-compactly generated tt-category, and its compact generators are twists of
\tilde{\mathscr{X}}_{\alpha}\otimes\M(Q)
, where
Q
is a smooth projective quadric over
k
. We denote by

\DQM^{\geom}_{\tilde{\mathscr{X}}_{\alpha}}(k;\F_{2})

the full tt-subcategory of compact objects, and

\DTM^{\geom}_{\tilde{\mathscr{X}}_{\alpha}}(k;\F_{2})

the full tt-subcategory generated by twists of
\tilde{\mathscr{X}}_{\alpha}
.

3.2.
Function fields of real anisotropic quadrics

A key feature of anisotropic quadrics over real closed fields is that the Milnor K-theory (modulo 2) of their function fields vanishes in degrees exceeding their dimension.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
Q
be an anisotropic quadric over
F
of dimension
m
. Then
\K^{M}_{n}(F(Q))/2=0
for all
n\geq m+1
.

Proof.

Let
I\subseteq W(F(Q))
denote the fundamental ideal of the Witt ring of
F(Q)
. By the Milnor conjecture on quadratic forms
[
orlov2007exact
]
, it suffices to show that
I^{n}=0
for all
n\geq m+1
. Since
F(Q)
is an extension of
F
of transcendence degree
m
, then by the Tsen-Lang theorem
[
lam2005introduction
, Chapter XI]
any quadratic form over
F(Q)(\sqrt{-1})
of dimension strictly greater than
2^{m}
is isotropic. By the Elman-Lam-Krüskemper theorem
[
becher2011elman
, Theorem 1.3]
, this bounds the torsion part of the fundamental powers, forcing
(I^{n})^{tor}=0
for all
n\geq m+1
. However,
-1
is a sum of squares in
F(Q)
. As a result, every quadratic form over
F(Q)
is torsion in the Witt ring; in particular,
I^{n}=(I^{n})^{tor}
for all
n
. Combining these facts, we conclude that
I^{n}=(I^{n})^{tor}=0
for all
n\geq m+1
, as required.
∎
