Multi-omics analyses reveal the regulatory mechanisms of riboflavin to improve meat quality in Pekin ducks
Authors: Qingyi Wu, Qing Shao, Wei Zhou, Lei Zhuang, Yongyan Jin, Zhengkui Zhou, Ming Xie, Shuisheng Hou, Jing Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study explored the effects of dietary riboflavin supplementation on breast meat quality, muscle fiber characteristics, metabonomics, transcriptomics, and phosphoproteomics of Pekin ducks. A total of 96 14-day-old ducks were randomly allotted to two treatment groups, each with 8 replicates of 6 birds, and were fed diets supplemented with 0 or 10 mg/kg riboflavin for 28 days. Compared to the control group without riboflavin supplementation, dietary riboflavin supplementation significantly increased breast muscle shear force, myofiber diameter, perimeter, and cross-sectional area, as well as myofibril diameter. Dietary riboflavin supplementation increased gene expression involved in myogenic differentiation of breast muscle in Pekin ducks, including MYOD, MRF4, and MYF5. Breast muscle metabolomics revealed that riboflavin stimulated fatty acid beta oxidation, as most carnitine-related metabolites were upregulated. Transcriptomics analysis revealed that riboflavin upregulated seven genes involved in muscle contraction (MYO3A, MYO5B, MYH11, MYBPC1, TNNI1, TNNT2, A2M), and ten genes involved in fatty acid synthesis, oxidation, and transport processes, which may contribute to enhanced intramuscular fat content. Furthermore, phosphoproteomics analysis indicated dietary riboflavin supplementation altered the phosphorylation levels of proteins involved in muscle contraction and glycolysis (PGM1, ENO1, and TPI1), which may lead to an increase in pH. In conclusion, riboflavin supplementation in the diet improved the breast meat quality and fiber development of ducks probably by activating fatty acid beta oxidation, synthesis, and transport, as well as muscle contraction, while simultaneously depressing glycolysis process.Keywords: Riboflavin, Meat quality, Metabonomics, Transcriptomics, Phosphoproteomics

Introduction

Duck meat has desirable nutritional characteristics, such as high protein and low fat (Jin et al., 2021). In recent years, duck meat production has experienced significant growth worldwide (Shin et al., 2023). At present, consumers pay more attention to the meat quality compared to the meat quantity. Therefore, the approaches to improve the duck meat quality are always needed to be investigated, such as dietary intervention. For example, it has been shown that dietary supplementation of additives could improve the meat quality of ducks, such as resveratrol (Jin et al., 2021), 25‑hydroxy-cholecalciferol (Zhuang et al., 2026), and riboflavin (Tang et al., 2023).

As a precursor of flavin mononucleotide (FMN) and flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD), riboflavin participates in a series of metabolic processes, including antioxidant systems, fatty acid oxidation, tricarboxylic acid cycle, and electron transport chain (Olfat et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2025b). Due to its important roles in antioxidant and mitochondrial function, riboflavin is essential for muscle development and meat quality of animals. Riboflavin improves meat quality, antioxidant capacity, and lipid composition of breast muscle in pigeons (Zhang et al., 2025a). Dietary riboflavin supplementation improved the quality of fish flesh, which was attributed to increased antioxidant capacity (Jiang et al., 2019). We also previously found that dietary riboflavin supplementation improved breast meat yield and meat quality of ducks, as indicated by increased pH24h, color, intramuscular fat (IMF) content, polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA) content, which may be attributed to enhanced antioxidant capacity and mitochondrial functions (Tang et al., 2023). Furthermore, riboflavin is essential for animal muscle fiber development, and riboflavin supplementation has been shown to increase breast muscle fiber diameter and cross-sectional area in pigeons (Zhang et al., 2025a). In vitro, riboflavin administration improved myogenic differentiation (e.g., increased differentiation rate and myotube width) by enhancing differentiation-related genes in C2C12 cells (Lee et al., 2025). It has been shown that muscle fiber characteristics affect meat traits, such as color, pH, and tenderness (Listrat et al., 2016; Ertbjerg and Puolanne, 2017). However, whether riboflavin supplementation promotes muscle fiber development in ducks, and the underlying mechanisms remain unclear, which are needed to be investigated.

The multi-omics technologies, such as metabonomics, transcriptomics, and phosphoproteomics, have become powerful tools for identifying key regulatory metabolites, genes, and phosphoproteins. These technologies have been widely used to uncover the molecular mechanisms of meat quality and muscle fiber development in poultry (Liu et al., 2023; Zhuang et al., 2026; Weng et al., 2022, 2026). Phosphorylation is regarded as the most common posttranslational modification of proteins that primarily regulates muscle fiber development and meat quality traits (Li et al., 2021). A series of studies revealed that phosphorylation of myofibrillar proteins involved in muscle contraction and glycolytic enzymes influence meat quality traits such as pH, color, and tenderness in broilers (Zhang et al., 2024), pigs (Zou et al., 2020), and lambs (Bai et al., 2020). Therefore, the objectives of the present study were to determine the effects of dietary riboflavin supplementation on breast muscle fiber characteristics of Pekin ducks, and multi-omics (metabonomics, transcriptomics, and phosphoproteomics) were performed to reveal the regulatory molecular mechanisms.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

The animal trial protocols were approved by the Animal Welfare Committee of Institute of Animal Science, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences (approval number: IAS2020-41).

Experimental design, animals, and diets

A total of 96 14-day-old female White Pekin ducks from the Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences Pekin duck breeding farm were randomly assigned to two treatment groups, each with eight replicate pens of six birds per pen. The control (CON) and riboflavin (RF) groups were fed basal diets supplemented with 0 or 10 mg/kg riboflavin (purity, 99%; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA), respectively. The experiment lasted for 28 days. The basal diet was formulated to meet the nutrient requirements of meat-type duck (NY/T, 2122-2012) as shown previously (Tang et al., 2023), containing 1.31 mg riboflavin/kg. The supplemented riboflavin dosage of RF group (10 mg/kg) was according to the nutrient requirements of meat-type duck (NY/T, 2122-2012). From days 15 to 42, the ducks were kept in a temperature-controlled room, with free access to water and feed.

Data and sample collection

At 42 days of age, the ducks were fasted for 12 hours, and two ducks from each pen were randomly selected and euthanized by CO2 inhalation. Subsequently, breast muscle from the duck carcasses was collected. The left breast muscle was stored at 4°C to determine meat quality trait (shear force). A portion of the right breast was fixed in the solution for histological sectioning. The remaining right breast sample was snap frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80°C for subsequent analysis.

Meat quality trait

For shear force measurement, after storage at 4°C for 24 h, the breast muscle was packaged in a cooking bag, heated at 80°C in a water bath to an internal temperature of 75°C, cut into three strips (1 × 1 × 3 cm) parallel to the muscle fiber direction. Then, the strip was measured for shear force using a digital tenderness meter (TA.XTplusC, Stable Micro Systems, Britain).

Breast muscle fiber characteristics

Fresh breast muscle samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and glutaraldehyde fixatives. After being embedded in paraffin blocks, hematoxylin and eosin staining was performed for the muscle sections. Stained slices were imaged under a light microscope (BH2, Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) to determine the fiber diameter, perimeter, and cross-sectional area by Image-Pro Plus 6.1 software (Media Cybernetics, Silver Spring, USA) as described previously (Liu et al., 2021). The fresh breast muscle samples were also fixed in 2% glutaraldehyde at 4°C. The microstructural details of samples were observed by transmission electron microscopy (HT7700, Hitachi, Japan) according to the method described previously (Tang et al., 2023).

RT-qPCR

Breast muscle RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and real-time PCR were performed as described previously (Zhang et al., 2025a). The target genes included myogenic differentiation factor (MYOD), muscle regulatory factor (MRF4), and myogenic factor 5 (MYF5). The primer sequences are listed in Table 1. Gene expression was normalized to glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH).

Metabonomics

Eight breast muscle samples from each group were used to conduct metabonomics analysis. Metabonomics analysis was performed with an LC‒ESI‒MS/MS system (HPLC, Shim-pack UFLC Shimadzu CBM30A system, MS, Applied Biosystems 6500 QTRAP) equipped with an ESI Turbo Ionspray interface controlled by Analyst 1.6.3 software (ABSciex) as described previously (Liu et al., 2023). The differential metabolites were screened using P < 0.05 and | fold change (FC) | ≥ 1.5.

Transcriptomics

Total RNA was extracted from 50 mg of frozen breast muscle using TRIzol reagent (Takara, Japan) following the manufacturer’s instructions. After RNA quality assessment (RNA integrity number > 7.5), 5 RNA samples of breast muscle from each group were used to perform transcriptomics analysis as previously described (Fan et al., 2020). Ten libraries were produced for RNA-seq experiment and sequenced on an Illumina X ten machine using the 150-bp paired-end sequencing module. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the two groups (RF vs. CON) were identified with the DESeq2 R package using a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 and | FC | ≥ 2. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses of the DEGs were performed.

Phosphoproteomics

Each group contained three biological replicates. LC-MS/MS based quantitative phosphoproteomics analysis mainly included protein extraction, trypsin digestion, tandem mass tag (TMT) labelling, phosphopeptide enrichment, LC-MS/MS analysis, database search, and bioinformatics analysis, according to the method described previously (Weng et al., 2022). The Uniprot- Anas platyrhynchos platyrhynchos - 8840 - database was employed for this analysis. | FC | ≥ 1.5 and P < 0.05 between RF group and CON group were used to determine whether identified proteins were upregulated (FC > 1.5) or downregulated (FC < - 1.5). GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of the differentially expressed phosphoproteins were performed.

Statistical analyses

Comparisons between two groups were done with Student’s t-test (SAS 9.3; SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). For all analyses, P < 0.05 was considered significant and data were reported as means and their standard deviation.

Results

Breast meat quality and muscle fiber characteristics

Compared to the control group, dietary riboflavin supplementation increased breast meat shear force (P < 0.05; Figure 1A). Compared to the control group, dietary riboflavin supplementation increased myofiber diameter, perimeter, and cross-sectional area of breast muscle in Pekin ducks (P < 0.05; Figure 1B, C). Furthermore, electron microscopy section showed dietary riboflavin supplementation increased myofibril diameter of breast muscle in Pekin ducks (P < 0.05; Figure 1D).

Phosphoprotein identification and differentially expressed phosphoprotein analysis

A total of 5060 phosphosites (phosphorylation sites) corresponding to 1406 phosphoproteins were identified in breast muscle of Pekin ducks. Statistical analysis of these identified phosphosites determined that 76.09%, 20.81%, and 3.1% of the phosphosites were located on serine (Ser), threonine (Thr), and tyrosine (Tyr) residues, respectively. Principal component analysis (PCA) of these phosphoproteins clearly distinguished between the RF and CON groups. The differentially expressed phosphoproteins (DEPs) between the RF group and CON group were identified using the screening criteria P < 0.05 and | FC | ≥ 1.5. A total of 218 DEPs were found, of which 87 were upregulated and 131 were downregulated in the RF group compared to the CON group. To further understand the biological functions of phosphoproteins in regulation of duck meat quality, GO and KEGG analyses were performed on the DEPs (Fig. 3C, D). Among the BP terms, the DEPs were primarily enriched in sarcomere organization, myofibril assembly, actomyosin structure organization, cellular component assembly involved in morphogenesis, muscle cell development, striated muscle contraction, hexose catabolic process, actin-myosin filament sliding, and muscle filament sliding. The CC terms enriched were myofibril, contractile fiber, sarcomere, Z disc, I band, M band, A band, contractile actin filament bundle, stress fiber, actin filament bundle, actomyosin, myosin filament, striated muscle thin filament, myofilament, and costamere. The MF terms enriched were muscle alpha-actinin binding, alpha-actinin binding, actinin binding, titin binding, calmodulin-dependent protein kinase activity, ankyrin binding, and cell-cell adhesion mediator activity. The KEGG pathway enrichment analysis showed that the DEPs between the RF and CON groups were involved in computational model of aerobic glycolysis, striated muscle contraction pathway, glycolysis and gluconeogenesis, pathways in clear cell renal cell carcinoma, metabolic reprogramming in colon cancer, Cori cycle, glycogen synthesis and degradation, apoptosis-related network due to altered Notch3 in ovarian cancer.

Specifically, in glycolysis process, riboflavin downregulated the phosphorylation levels of phosphoglucomutase 1 (PGM1), alpha-enolase (ENO1), triosephosphate isomerase (TPI1), and upregulated the phosphorylation levels of glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH), pyruvate kinase (PKM), phosphoglycerate kinase (PGK1). In muscle contraction pathway, riboflavin downregulated the phosphorylation levels of myosin binding protein C, slow type (MYBPC1), myosin light chain 1 (MYL1), tropomyosin alpha-1 chain (TPM1), vimentin (VIM), myomesin 2 (MYOM2), myopalladin (MYPN), myozenin 1 (MYOZ1), spectrin beta chain, brain 1 (SPTBN1), obscurin (OBSCN), synaptopodin 2 like (SYNPO2), titin (TTN), and upregulated the phosphorylation levels of calcium/calmodulin dependent protein kinase II delta (CAMK2D), calponin, myosin heavy chain 7B (MYH7B), myomesin 1 (MYOM1), and myosin heavy chain 2.

Discussion

In our previous study, dietary riboflavin supplementation improved breast muscle yield and meat quality of ducks indicated by increased IMF content, PUFA content, redness, and pH24h, and decreased lightness and yellowness (Tang et al., 2023). To investigate the underlying molecular mechanisms, breast muscle fiber characteristics, metabonomics, transcriptomics, and phosphoproteomics were performed in this study.

Meat quality is commonly assessed by physical indicators such as color, pH, and shear force. In previous studies, dietary riboflavin supplementation improved breast meat quality indicated by color and pH in ducks (Tang et al., 2023) and pigeons (Zhang et al., 2025a). Consumers generally prefer firmer and darker red meat, and lighter and softer poultry meat is known as PSE (pale, soft, exudative) meat. In the present study, dietary riboflavin supplementation enhanced breast meat shear force, thereby improved the chewiness quality of the meat, which is consistent with the findings in fish (Jiang et al., 2019) and pigeons (Zhang et al., 2025a).

In the present study, dietary riboflavin supplementation promoted myofiber development of breast muscle in Pekin ducks indicated by enhanced myofiber diameter, perimeter, and cross-sectional area, as well as myofibril diameter. The finding is consistent with a recent study in pigeons that riboflavin deficiency led to thin muscle fibers and elongated muscle sarcomeres (Zhang et al., 2025a). It has been proved that muscle shear force value is significantly correlated with fiber diameter and cross-sectional area (Huo et al., 2021). In the present study, riboflavin administration increased breast meat shear force by promoting the development of muscle fibers.

Tissue riboflavin content is a sensitive indicator of riboflavin status in animals. In the present study, breast muscle metabonomics showed that dietary riboflavin supplementation increased riboflavin concentration in Pekin ducks. This result confirmed our previous HPLC findings (Tang et al., 2023) and is consistent with the previous studies in pigeons (Zhang et al., 2025a) and ducks (Tang et al., 2015, 2017). Furthermore, breast muscle metabonomics revealed that most carnitine-related metabolites were upregulated by riboflavin, including carnitine C9:0 isomer, carnitine C10:1 isomer, carnitine C10:1, carnitine C7:0, carnitine C7-OH, carnitine C8:1, carnitine C8:1 isomer, carnitine C8:0, carnitine C8:0 isomer, octanoylcarnitine, carnitine C6:0, carnitine C5:DC, carnitine C10:0 isomer, indicating fatty acid oxidation process was activated. In support of energy metabolism, carnitine transports long-chain fatty acids from the cytosol into mitochondria to be oxidized for free energy production. This finding is consistent with the previous results showing that riboflavin stimulated fatty acid beta oxidation process in breast muscle, as indicated by the upregulation of seven related proteins, leading to increased energy generation (Tang et al., 2023). Thus, riboflavin improved energy generation such as fatty acid oxidation of breast muscle in ducks, which would promote myofiber development observed. On the other hand, riboflavin increased breast muscle aerobic respiration, and may suppress glycolysis process as a feedback mechanism indicated by increased intermediate products, such as d-fructose 6-phosphate, mannose 1-phosphate, d-mannose 6-phosphate, d-fructose 6-phosphate-disodium salt, d-glucose 6-phosphate.

It has been shown that riboflavin administration improved myogenic differentiation by enhancing gene expression of key muscle differentiation markers (such as MYOD, MYOG, MRF4) in C2C12 cells, indicated by an increased differentiation rate and myotube width (Lee et al., 2025). Consistent with the previous study in vitro, dietary riboflavin supplementation enhanced the gene expression in muscle myogenic differentiation of ducks in the present study, including MYOD, MRF4, and MYF5. Thus, riboflavin promotes muscle fiber development by myogenic differentiation.

In this study, transcriptomics analysis revealed that riboflavin upregulated six genes (MYO3A, MYO5B, MYH11, MYBPC1, TNNI1, TNNT2) involved in muscle contraction. MYO3A, MYO5B, MYH11, MYBPC1 are subunits of myosin, which is responsible for actin-based motility in muscle contraction (Berg et al., 2001). Troponin and tropomyosin play important roles in regulating striated muscle contraction (Filatov et al., 1999). The increased expression of above genes due to riboflavin may imply stronger muscle contraction. As muscle contraction is an ATP-dependent process, this result may be caused by greater ATP production in riboflavin-supplemented ducks. And this finding is consistent with the better fiber morphology caused by riboflavin supplementation.

Interestingly, breast muscle phosphoproteomics analysis also showed that the DEPs caused by riboflavin supplementation are mainly enriched in muscle contraction in the present study, most of which are categorized as myofibrillar proteins. These results indicated that riboflavin regulates muscle contraction via multiple approaches, such as gene transcription and protein phosphorylation modification. Riboflavin upregulated the phosphorylation levels of CAMK2D, calponin, MYH7B, MYOM1, and myosin heavy chain 2, and downregulated the phosphorylation levels of MYBPC1, MYL1, TPM1, VIM, MYOM2, MYPN, MYOZ1, SPTBN1, OBSCN, SYNPO2, TTN. CAMK2D, one isoform of calcium (Ca2+)/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII), as a mediator of Ca2+signals, regulates muscle contraction (Martinez-Canton et al., 2024) and muscle cell proliferation positively (House et al., 2007). CaMKII Thr287 phosphorylation is upregulated after exercise training (Thomassen et al., 2016). Calponin is involved in regulating actin-myosin interaction and therefore muscle contraction, and calponin function in turn is regulated by Ca2+-dependent phosphorylation (Winder and Walsh, 1990). MYBPC1 is involved in muscle development, growth, and maintenance, and a conditional knockout of MYBPC1 in mice disrupted actomyosin interaction and sarcomere structural integrity, and impaired muscle growth and function (Song et al., 2023). MYBPC1 exhibits increased levels of phosphorylation in mouse dystrophic muscles compared to normal tissues (Ackermann and Kontrogianni-Konstantopoulos, 2011). TPM1 and TTN are actomyosin dissociation-related phosphoproteins. TPM1 controls binding of actin to calcium in the thin filament to cause muscle contraction. Titin is a central mediator for hypertrophic signaling, exercise-induced mechanosignaling and skeletal muscle remodeling (Krüger and Kötter, 2016). Higher phosphorylation of TTN and TPM1 contributes to increased muscle contraction (Zhang et al., 2024). VIM phosphorylation at Ser-56 regulates the intercellular force transmission and muscle contraction (Li et al., 2016). Riboflavin regulated breast muscle contraction of Pekin ducks probably by modulating the phosphorylation levels of myofibrillar proteins above.

Furthermore, in the present study, another enriched pathway of the DEPs caused by riboflavin supplementation is glycolysis based on phosphoproteomics analysis. Of these, riboflavin downregulated the phosphorylation levels of PGM1, ENO1, and TPI1. PGM1 catalyzes the conversion of glucose-1-phosphate to glucose-6-phosphate, which is regulated by phosphorylation. ENO1 catalyzes the conversion of 2-phosphoglycerate to phosphoenolpyruvate. TPI1 catalyzes the isomerization of glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate and dihydroxyacetone phosphate. Higher phosphorylation levels of the glycolytic enzymes (ENO1, PGM1) correlate with lower post-mortem pH of broiler breast muscle (Xing et al., 2020; Weng et al., 2022). Similar results were also found in beef, where early postmortem phosphorylation of PGM1 accelerated the pH decline rate, leading to greater final sarcomere length (Silva et al., 2019). Pre-slaughter stress accelerated postmortem muscle glycolysis by upregulating the phosphorylation levels of glycometabolic enzymes (such as PGM1, ENO1, TPI1), thereby leading to rapid pH decline (Xing et al., 2020). A recent study showed that dietary chlorogenic acid supplementation improved meat quality traits (such as water holding capacity and post-mortem pH) of immunologically stressed broilers by downregulating glycolytic enzyme protein phosphorylation, including ENO1 and PGM1 (Zhang et al., 2024). Consistent with the previous studies, dietary riboflavin supplementation probably suppressed breast muscle glycolysis rate by downregulating PGM1, ENO1, and TPI1 phosphorylation of ducks in the present study, which may contribute to the increased pH observed previously.

A novel and important finding of the present study is that riboflavin mainly upregulated ten genes involved in lipid metabolism based on breast muscle transcriptomics, such as APOA4, APOB, APOC3, ELOVL6, and SCD. Apolipoproteins play important roles in lipid transport, binding lipids (such as fats and cholesterol) to form lipoproteins. In the present study, riboflavin upregulated breast muscle apolipoproteins, including APOA4, APOB, APOC3, indicating that lipid transport was activated. It has been reported that flavoproteins are involved in the oxidative folding of secretory proteins in the endoplasmic reticulum and riboflavin deficiency impaired oxidative folding and secretion of APOB in hepatocytes (Manthey et al., 2005). ELOVL6 is involved in the formation of long-chain fatty acids, which catalyzes the first and rate-limiting reaction of the four reactions that constitute the long-chain fatty acids elongation cycle (Matsuzaka and Shimano, 2009). SCD is required for the biosynthesis of monounsaturated fatty acids, which catalyzes the first double bond into saturated fatty acyl-CoA substrates (Paton and Ntambi, 2009). In the present study, riboflavin enhanced the expression of ELOVL6 and SCD in breast muscle, indicating unsaturated fatty acid synthesis process was stimulated. Taken together, riboflavin improved lipid metabolism of breast muscle by activating fatty acid synthesis, oxidation, and transport processes in ducks, which may provide an explanation for the enhanced unsaturated fatty acid, PUFA, and IMF contents.

In the present study, several phosphorylation sites of proteins involved in glycolysis and muscle contraction regulated by riboflavin were identified based on phosphoproteomics analysis. However, the functional validation of the key protein phosphorylation sites is still lacking. Furthermore, the underlying mechanisms of riboflavin promoting myogenic differentiation in vitro require further investigation.

Conclusion

Compared to the CON group, dietary riboflavin supplementation increased breast meat shear force and promoted muscle fiber development indicated by enhanced fiber diameter and cross-section area, as well as myofibril diameter. Furthermore, breast muscle multi-omics (metabonomic, transcriptomic, and phosphoproteomic) revealed riboflavin supplementation activated fatty acid beta oxidation process, whereas suppressed glycolysis process by PGM1, ENO1, and TPI1 phosphorylation. Riboflavin enhanced breast IMF content probably by upregulating the genes involved in fatty acid synthesis, oxidation, and transport processes. These findings provided a practical approach to improve meat quality of ducks.
