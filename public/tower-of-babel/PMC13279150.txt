Scalp Lesions in a Neonate
Authors: Maxwell Rowley, Dharshana Krishnaprasadh
Published: 2026

Patient Presentation

A 2-day-old boy presented to the emergency department (ED) with a scalp rash. Multiple demarcated vesicles were noted over the left parieto-occipital region, where a scalp electrode had been placed during labor. The vesicles measured 0.5 to 1 cm in diameter with crusting and underlying erythema. Prenatal care began late, at 24 weeks’ gestation. Maternal history was notable for treated chorioamnionitis associated with trichomonas and chlamydia. At the initial newborn outpatient follow-up visit, the baby was referred to the ED for worsening scalp lesions (Fig). Diagnosis: Neonatal Herpes Simplex Virus Skin-Eye-Mouth Disease

Neonatal herpes simplex virus (HSV) skin-eye-mouth (SEM) disease is typically diagnosed between 10 and 19 days of age.1,2 SEM disease is localized to the skin and mucosal surfaces and accounts for about 45% of neonatal HSV cases.1,2 Most transmission occurs perinatally (85%) through exposure to HSV-1 or HSV-2 in the birth canal.3 Postnatal transmission accounts for ∼10% via direct contact with infected lesions, whereas in utero exposure accounts for ∼5%.

Neonatal HSV can cause severe complications, particularly with central nervous system involvement. A full sepsis evaluation, including lumbar puncture, was performed. Empiric intravenous ampicillin, ceftazidime, and acyclovir were initiated. Pustule cultures grew Streptococcus agalactiae, and skin polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and serology were positive for HSV-2.

Consistent prenatal care can help identify maternal HSV infection. If active infection is present, suppressive acyclovir is initiated at 36 weeks of gestation. Cesarean delivery is recommended when maternal viral load exceeds 1000 copies/mL prior to birth.4
