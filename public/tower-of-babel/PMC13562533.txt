Circulating matrix metalloproteinase profile in early-stage primary biliary cholangitis
Authors: Magdalena Rogalska, Sławomir Ławicki, Agnieszka Błachnio-Zabielska, Piotr Zabielski, Kamila Roszczyc-Owsiejczuk, Jacek Janica, Aleksandra Andrzejuk, Justyna Kryńska, Maksymilian Lech, Andrzej Dąbrowski, Robert Flisiak, Paweł Rogalski
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPrimary biliary cholangitis (PBC) is a chronic cholestatic liver disease with progressive bile duct injury, inflammation, and fibrosis. Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) contribute to extracellular matrix remodeling but their circulating profile and clinical relevance in early-stage PBC remain incompletely defined. We characterized circulating MMPs in early-stage PBC and related them to non-invasive fibrosis and portal hemodynamics. Forty-six patients with early-stage PBC and 31 healthy controls were studied. Plasma MMP-2, -3, -7, -9, -10, and -26 were quantified by ELISA; liver stiffness was measured by point shear-wave elastography and portal flow indices by Doppler ultrasound. Compared with controls, PBC patients showed higher MMP-7 (p < 0.0001), MMP-2 (p = 0.0007), and MMP-10 (p = 0.046). MMP-7 correlated with liver stiffness (R = 0.68, p < 0.001) and demonstrated the highest discriminatory performance for significant fibrosis defined as LSM > 5.56 kPa (AUC = 0.800, p < 0.001). In exploratory regression analyses, higher MMP-7 concentrations remained associated with significant fibrosis after adjustment for age, whereas APRI and FIB-4 did not show significant discriminatory performance in ROC analysis. Introduction

Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) is a chronic cholestatic liver disease, characterized by the progressive destruction of the intrahepatic bile ducts1. The resulting chronic cholestasis leads to the retention of toxic bile acids, hepatocellular injury, and persistent inflammation2. These pathological processes activate hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) and promote their differentiation into myofibroblasts, leading to excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition and subsequent fibrosis with cirrhotic remodeling3.

ECM turnover is strictly regulated by matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), a family of zinc-dependent endopeptidases4. Based on substrate specificity and structural domains, MMPs are classified into major subgroups, including collagenases, gelatinases, stromelysins, and matrilysins5. Under physiological conditions, these enzymes mediate continuous ECM remodeling maintaining a dynamic balance between matrix synthesis and degradation6,7. In pathological states, however, this balance is disrupted. Importantly, the consequences of this dysregulation extend beyond altered ECM turnover to include modulation of inflammatory cytokines and regulation of immune cells migration6,8.

While specific enzymatic patterns have been described in other cholestatic conditions, the comprehensive MMP profile in early-stage PBC remains poorly defined9–12. Clinically, the early phase of PBC represents a critical therapeutic window: it is characterized by active inflammation and initiation of matrix remodeling rather than established organ failure, and may therefore offer greater potential for disease modification13. Accordingly, identifying sensitive markers of early fibrosis progression is fundamental, particularly for risk stratification in patients who do not respond adequately to standard ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) therapy and may require second-line treatment14.

The aim of this study was to evaluate plasma concentrations of selected MMPs representing different subfamilies (MMP-2, MMP-3, MMP-7, MMP-9, MMP-10, and MMP-26) in patients with early-stage PBC. We further investigated associations between these biomarkers and liver stiffness assessed by elastography, ultrasonographic signs of portal hypertension and biochemical markers of cholestasis, hepatocellular injury, and systemic inflammation to assess their potential utility for non-invasive risk stratification.

Materials and methods

Study population

The study included 46 patients diagnosed with early-stage PBC and 31 age- and sex-matched healthy controls. PBC was diagnosed according to the European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL) recommendations and treated with ursodeoxycholic acid at a dose of 13–15 mg/kg/day. All patients fulfilled Paris-II biochemical response criteria after at least 12 months of UDCA therapy15. The cohort consisted of ambulatory patients with compensated PBC, without previous hepatic decompensation or clinical evidence of cirrhosis. The characterization of the cohort as representing an early/compensated disease spectrum was based on the EASL risk-stratification framework, in which early versus advanced-stage PBC is distinguished using biochemical parameters and liver stiffness measurement by transient elastography at a threshold of approximately 10 kPa16. Because liver stiffness in the present study was assessed using point shear-wave elastography (ElastPQ), this threshold was used only as a supportive framework for cohort characterization. Significant fibrosis within this compensated cohort was defined as ElastPQ > 5.56 kPa, based on a PBC-specific pSWE study evaluating histological fibrosis ≥F217. Histological staging was not available in the present cohort. All participants underwent clinical assessment and laboratory evaluation. Exclusion criteria included a history of hepatic decompensation, coexisting liver diseases, active infection, known active malignancy, use of immunosuppressive agents, or systemic inflammatory or autoimmune disorders other than PBC. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment and the study protocol was approved by the Institutional Bioethics Committee (approval no. APK.002.108.2023). The patient cohort in this study was part of a larger clinical project. While some biochemical and metabolic characteristics of these individuals have been previously reported - specifically regarding acylcarnitines, sphingolipids, and BDNF levels - the present analysis of matrix metalloproteinase profiles and their correlation with portal hemodynamics is entirely original and has not been published elsewhere18–20.

Measurement of matrix metalloproteinases

Plasma concentrations of MMPs were measured using commercially available ELISA kits according to the manufacturers’ protocols. MMP-2 and MMP-9 were quantified using DuoSet ELISA Development Kits (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA; DY902 and DY911, respectively), with absorbance read at 450 nm and correction at 570 nm. MMP-3 and MMP-7 were assessed using Quantikine ELISA kits (R&D Systems; DMP300 and DMP700, respectively), with plasma samples diluted 1:10 (MMP-3) or 1:2 (MMP-7). MMP-10 and MMP-26 levels were measured using sandwich ELISA kits from MyBioSource (San Diego, CA, USA; MBS2544092 and MBS2514092). For all assays, samples were analyzed in duplicate, and concentrations were calculated from standard curves using either four-parameter logistic or log-transformed fits. Plasma samples were stored at − 80 °C and analyzed after a single freeze–thaw cycle.

Measurement of interleukin -6 (IL-6)

Plasma levels of IL-6 were measured using a commercially available sandwich ELISA kit (Sigma-Aldrich, Saint Louis, MO, USA; RAB0306), following the manufacturer’s instructions. The assay range was 1.37–1000 pg/mL with a sensitivity of < 3 pg/mL. Plasma samples were stored at − 80 °C until analysis. Absorbance was read at 450 nm, and cytokine concentrations were calculated from standard curves.

Liver ultrasound and elastography

LSM was assessed by point shear wave elastography (ElastPQ, Philips iU22, C5-1 probe), with ten valid measurements obtained from the right liver lobe. The median value, expressed in kilopascals (kPa), was used for analysis. Measurements with an interquartile-to-median ratio (IQR/M) > 30% were excluded. Significant fibrosis was defined as LSM > 5.56 kPa based on the ElastPQ/pSWE study by Park et al., in which this threshold was proposed for detecting histological fibrosis ≥F2 in patients with PBC. This cut-off was used for fibrosis stratification within the present cohort and should not be interpreted as a criterion for advanced fibrosis17. Doppler ultrasound was performed to evaluate portal vein diameter, flow velocity, and signs of portal hypertension, including collateral circulation. Standard liver morphology parameters were also recorded.

Non-invasive markers of liver fibrosis

APRI and FIB-4 were calculated as supportive non-invasive fibrosis indices. The cut-offs APRI > 0.7 and FIB-4 > 3.25, derived from studies in viral hepatitis, were used only for exploratory subgroup analyses, as universally accepted PBC-specific thresholds for these scores are lacking21,22. Fibrosis classification in this study was primarily based on liver stiffness measurement.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using Statistica 14.1.0.4 (Cloud Software Group, Inc.). The normality of data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed data or as median with interquartile range (IQR) for non-normally distributed data.

Differences in continuous variables between groups were evaluated using the Student’s t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test, depending on the data distribution. The strength of associations between plasma metalloproteinase concentrations and clinical or elastographic parameters was assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficients.

Subgroup analyses were performed using an available-case approach. Patients were included in each analysis only when the corresponding clinical, biochemical, or elastographic variable was available and technically valid. No imputation of missing data was performed. For LSM-based analyses, only valid elastography measurements were included; measurements with an IQR/M > 30% were excluded. For analyses based on ALT, ALP, and IL-6, only the lowest and highest tertiles were compared, while the middle tertile was intentionally omitted.

Logistic regression analyses were performed to identify factors associated with the disease presence and severity. For the identification of independent predictors of PBC diagnosis, a multivariate logistic regression model with stepwise backward elimination was applied to address potential multicollinearity among the analyzed metalloproteinases and to select the strongest independent predictors. Conversely, for the prediction of significant liver fibrosis, a standard multivariate logistic regression analysis (enter method) was conducted to strictly control for established clinical confounders, such as age. Results were presented as Odds Ratios (OR) with 95% Confidence Intervals (95% CI).

The diagnostic performance of the studied markers was evaluated using Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The Area Under the Curve (AUC) with its 95% CI was calculated for each parameter. The optimal cut-off values were determined using the Youden Index. The diagnostic accuracy of the novel markers (MMPs) was statistically compared with standard non-invasive indices (APRI, FIB-4) using the DeLong test.

Results

Study cohort characteristics

The study group comprised 46 patients with early-stage primary biliary cholangitis and 31 healthy controls matched for age and sex. There were no statistically significant differences in demographic characteristics between the two groups (p > 0.05). As expected, patients with PBC presented with significantly higher activities of cholestatic enzymes (ALP, GGT) and aminotransferases (ALT, AST), as well as elevated levels of inflammatory markers (CRP) compared to the control group (p < 0.0001 for all). Furthermore, non-invasive markers of liver fibrosis (APRI, FIB-4) and liver stiffness measurements (LSM) were significantly increased in the PBC group. Despite these statistical differences, clinical and biochemical parameters in the PBC cohort largely reflected an early, non-cirrhotic stage of the disease. Plasma concentrations of matrix metalloproteinases

Plasma concentrations of the analyzed matrix metalloproteinases differed significantly between patients with PBC and healthy controls. The most pronounced alterations were observed for MMP-7 and MMP-26. Specifically, MMP-7 levels were significantly higher in the PBC group (p < 0.0001), while MMP-26 concentrations were markedly reduced compared to controls (p < 0.0001). Additionally, patients with PBC presented with elevated levels of MMP-2 (p = 0.0007) and MMP-10 (p = 0.0462). The concentration of MMP-9 was significantly lower in patients with PBC than in the control group (p = 0.0007). No statistically significant difference was found for MMP-3 (p = 0.74). Diagnostic potential of MMPs for PBC identification

To evaluate the clinical utility of the observed alterations in MMP profiles, logistic regression and ROC curve analyses were performed to distinguish patients with early-stage PBC from healthy controls. In the univariate logistic regression, MMP-7 (p < 0.001), MMP-26 (p = 0.004), MMP-9 (p = 0.005), and MMP-2 (p = 0.005) were identified as significant predictors of disease presence, whereas MMP-10 showed no significant association (p = 0.53). However, multivariate stepwise backward regression analysis revealed that MMP-7 was the sole independent predictor of PBC (p < 0.001, OR 1.017, 95% CI 1.008–1.027), displacing other metalloproteinases from the model. This points to the central role of MMP-7 elevation in the biochemical profile of the disease.

ROC curve analysis (Fig. 1) confirmed the superior diagnostic value of MMP-7, which showed an AUC of 0.893 (95% CI 0.824–0.963, p < 0.001). At the optimal cut-off of > 336.9 ng/mL (Youden Index = 0.64), MMP-7 identified PBC with 93.5% sensitivity, 71.0% specificity, and 84.4% accuracy. High PPV (82.7%) and NPV (88.0%), along with a low negative likelihood ratio (LR- = 0.09), demonstrate its reliability as a diagnostic marker in early-stage disease. Although excluded from the final multivariate model, MMP-26 demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy as a specific negative marker (AUC 0.823, 95% CI 0.729–0.916, p < 0.001), with depleted concentrations (< 1.85 µg/mL) indicative of the disease. Given the inverse plasma profiles of these two matrilysins - marked elevation of MMP-7 and significant depletion of MMP-26—we hypothesized that their combined assessment could optimize diagnostic balance. To investigate the potential synergistic diagnostic value of the most altered matrilysins, a composite index based on the MMP-7/MMP-26 ratio was evaluated. The MMP-7/MMP-26 ratio demonstrated an AUC of 0.896 (95% CI 0.825–0.967), which did not differ significantly from the AUC of MMP-7 alone (p = 0.94, DeLong test). At the optimal cut-off value of > 201.94 (Youden Index = 0.70), the ratio showed a more balanced diagnostic profile, with a specificity of 87.1% and a sensitivity of 82.6%. However, because the ratio did not significantly outperform MMP-7 alone in AUC analysis, it should be interpreted as a preliminary, hypothesis-generating composite marker requiring validation in independent cohorts. MMP-2 (AUC 0.696, 95% CI 0.579–0.814, p = 0.001) and MMP-9 (AUC 0.692, 95% CI 0.573–0.811, p = 0.002) showed moderate diagnostic utility.

Association of MMPs with liver stiffness and fibrosis markers

Plasma levels of MMPs showed significant correlations with parameters reflecting liver fibrogenesis and fibrosis severity. MMP-7 demonstrated the strongest positive association with LSM (R = 0.68, p < 0.001), as well as with non-invasive fibrosis indices (APRI, FIB-4). Similarly, MMP-2 levels correlated positively with LSM (R = 0.51, p = 0.002), APRI, and FIB-4. Stratification analysis confirmed that concentrations of both MMP-7 (p = 0.007) and MMP-2 (p = 0.053) tended to be higher in patients with significant fibrosis (LSM > 5.56 kPa), although for MMP-2 this difference was borderline significant. In contrast, MMP-9 exhibited an inverse relationship with fibrogenesis, showing negative correlations with liver stiffness (R = − 0.36, p = 0.040), APRI, and FIB-4.

Correlation with hepatocellular injury, cholestasis, and systemic inflammation

Markers of active hepatocellular injury and cholestasis were primarily associated with MMP-2. Its concentrations were significantly higher in patients stratified to the high-ALT subgroup (p = 0.008) and high-ALP subgroup (p = 0.021). MMP-2 also showed positive correlations with continuous values of ALT (R = 0.51, p < 0.001) and ALP (R = 0.50, p = 0.001).

In terms of inflammatory markers, a distinct pattern was observed. MMP-2 levels displayed an inverse relationship with interleukin-6 (IL-6), being significantly lower in the high-IL-6 subgroup (p = 0.032) and showing a negative correlation with serum IL-6 concentrations (R = − 0.35, p = 0.016). Conversely, MMP-9 exhibited a positive correlation with white blood cell count (WBC; R = 0.46, p = 0.002). Furthermore, MMP-10 was the only metalloproteinase to show a significant positive correlation with C-reactive protein (CRP; R = 0.37, p = 0.014).

Association with ultrasonographic signs of portal hypertension

Analysis of splenoportal hemodynamics revealed significant associations, particularly for MMP-7. This enzyme showed strong positive correlations with spleen dimensions, including spleen length (R = 0.64, p < 0.001) and splenic index (R = 0.59, p < 0.001). Furthermore, it demonstrated the most extensive associations with Doppler parameters of portal hypertension. MMP-7 was the only metalloproteinase significantly correlated with the portal vein congestion index (CI) (R = 0.46, p < 0.001). It also showed a strong positive correlation with hepatic artery flow velocity (R = 0.59, p < 0.001). Conversely, MMP-9 levels correlated negatively with spleen length (R = − 0.36, p = 0.021) and hepatic artery flow. MMP-3, MMP-10, and MMP-26 showed only isolated correlations with single hemodynamic or ultrasonographic parameters.

Predictors of significant liver fibrosis

Univariate logistic regression analysis was performed to identify potential predictors of significant liver fibrosis (defined as LSM > 5.56 kPa). Among the analyzed parameters, MMP-7 was identified as a significant predictor (OR 1.006, 95% CI 1.002–1.010, p = 0.0031), alongside MMP-2 (p = 0.0353). Age (p = 0.1440), platelet count (p = 0.2429), and bilirubin levels (p = 0.3708) were not significantly associated with fibrosis in the univariate analysis. To assess the independence of MMP-7 as a predictor, a multivariate logistic regression model was constructed, adjusted for age. In this final model, MMP-7 remained a significant, independent predictor of significant fibrosis (p = 0.041), with an odds ratio of 1.006 per 1 ng/mL increase in plasma concentration. This indicates that higher levels of MMP-7 are associated with an increased likelihood of fibrosis, regardless of the patient’s age. Diagnostic performance of MMPs for significant fibrosis detection

ROC curve analysis was performed to evaluate the diagnostic value of MMP-7 and MMP-2 in detecting significant liver fibrosis (LSM > 5.56 kPa) compared to standard non-invasive indices (APRI, FIB-4). MMP-7 demonstrated the highest diagnostic accuracy among all analyzed parameters, yielding an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.800 (95% CI 0.648–0.952, p < 0.001) (Fig. 2). At the optimal cut-off value of > 503.6 ng/mL, MMP-7 offered a sensitivity of 69.6% and a specificity of 80.0%. Although the exploratory MMP-7/MMP-26 ratio showed promising discriminatory performance for distinguishing PBC patients from healthy controls, its performance for significant fibrosis detection was lower than that of MMP-7 alone. While the direct comparison of ROC curves via the DeLong test showed only a trend towards significance for MMP-7 vs. APRI (p = 0.088), MMP-7 was the marker that achieved a statistically significant AUC in this cohort. Discussion

Liver fibrosis is a dynamic and potentially reversible wound-healing response to chronic injury, determined by the balance between ECM deposition and degradation. Under physiological conditions, ECM homeostasis is maintained through continuous turnover regulated by MMPs and their inhibitors - TIMPs, thereby maintaining a tightly controlled MMP/TIMP balance23.

In this study involving patients with early-stage primary biliary cholangitis, we observed a specific profile of plasma matrix metalloproteinases compared to healthy controls. The most significant alterations involved MMP-7 and MMP-26, followed by MMP-9 and MMP-2. Specifically, MMP-7 concentrations were significantly elevated, whereas MMP-26 and MMP-9 levels were markedly decreased in the PBC cohort. MMP-7 emerged as the strongest independent predictor of significant fibrosis among all analyzed metalloproteinases.

Several groups have been distinguished based on structural and functional characteristics of MMPs: gelatinases, stromelysins, matrilysins, collagenases, membrane-type MMPs, and other MMPs4. In this study, we focused on the first three groups because of their central roles in ECM remodeling during fibrogenesis. Accordingly, we interpret our results within the context of these MMP classes and relate the observed changes to their specific functional characteristics.

Regarding the matrilysin subfamily, our data revealed distinct profiles for MMP-7 (matrilysin-1) and MMP-26 (matrilysin-2). MMP-7 demonstrated the highest diagnostic accuracy for identifying PBC (AUC = 0.893) and was the only metalloproteinase that independently predicted disease presence in multivariate analysis. Consistently, MMP-7 also showed the strongest positive correlations with liver stiffness measurements, and with ultrasonographic parameters of portal hypertension, including splenic dimensions and the portal vein congestion index, linking this enzyme to both fibrosis and hemodynamic consequences of the disease. MMP-7 is one of the smallest MMPs and acts on a broad range of substrates8,24. Through these actions, MMP-7 can modulate ECM remodeling and inflammatory/apoptotic pathways, resulting in context-dependent pro- or anti-apoptotic effects24. Importantly, MMP-7 is mainly secreted by bile ductular epithelial cells, Kupffer cells, and periportal hepatocytes, and is markedly increased in cholestatic fibrotic conditions such as biliary atresia, where it correlates with histological fibrosis stage25,26. MMP-7 expression has also been reported in liver progenitor (oval) cells and implicated in progenitor-dependent regeneration during liver injury27. In the study by Irvine et al., among a panel of 44 candidate fibrosis biomarkers, circulating MMP-7 was proposed as a valuable, novel indicator of advanced fibrosis with potential added value over established panels28.

In the gelatinase subfamily, we observed opposing trends between MMP-2 (gelatinase A) and MMP-9 (gelatinase B). Gelatinases are structurally similar to other MMPs but are distinguished by their capacity to bind gelatin (denatured collagen) as well as native collagen and laminin, placing them at a central intersection of ECM remodeling and tissue injury29.

In our cohort, MMP-2 levels were significantly higher in the PBC group and correlated positively with liver stiffness, as well as with APRI, and FIB-4 indices, supporting the view that MMP-2 tracks with disease severity and the pro-fibrogenic environment. This is in line with reports that both MMP-2 and MMP-9 contribute to hepatic fibrogenesis and may be elevated in advanced cirrhosis (stage C); however, our data suggest that in PBC, MMP-2 is the dominant gelatinase linked to fibrosis burden3,30. MMP-2 is expressed by multiple hepatic cell types, predominantly HSCs and Kupffer cells and is secreted as a pro-enzyme that is activated by MMP-1 and MMP-1431,32. MMP-2 is localized to vascular regions where it contributes to vascular homeostasis, consistent with a role in the remodeling processes accompanying progressive portal–parenchymal restructuring. Moreover, high MMP-2 expression has been reported in myofibroblasts4,33. Multiple studies have also linked hepatic MMP-2 expression to fibrosis progression (with no detectable expression in normal liver), supporting its largely profibrogenic properties31,34,35.

In addition, MMP-2 showed significant positive associations with biochemical markers of hepatocellular injury (ALT, AST) and cholestasis (ALP) in our cohort. As previously reported, MMP-2 can also degrade non-ECM substrates, including IL-1β and other MMPs (-1, -9, and -13), and it has been proposed to modulate intracellular inflammatory pathways and lipid metabolism through cleavage of metabolic regulators. These properties offer possible links to the combined fibrosis, injury and cholestasis correlations observed here36,37.

Conversely, we found MMP-9 concentrations to be significantly lower in patients with PBC than in controls, which aligns with available data indicating that MMP-2 activity is inversely proportional to MMP-9 activity25,38. MMP-9 exhibited a negative correlation with liver stiffness and fibrosis scores, as well as with spleen length. It also showed a positive correlation with platelet count, suggesting an association with portal pressure – related features rather than active inflammation.

These findings fit with the broader biology of MMP-9 as a multifunctional gelatinase whose effects are highly context dependent38. Moreover, MMP-9 can promote inflammation during IRI through targeted ECM degradation, increasing matrix permeability and generating chemotactic ECM fragments that enhance leukocyte influx and impair liver function39. In contrast, in chronic liver disease of different etiology, MMP-9 has been reported to increase with advanced injury; for example, Prystupa et al. observed markedly elevated MMP-9 levels in alcoholic cirrhosis40. Notably, our cohort consisted predominantly of patients with early-stage PBC without advanced fibrosis, which may partly explain the lower MMP-9 levels observed.

Finally, the stromelysin subfamily - MMP-3 (stromelysin 1) and MMP-10 (stromelysin 2) showed the least clinical relevance in our cohort. MMP-3 concentrations did not differ significantly between PBC patients and controls and showed no correlation with fibrosis severity or cholestatic parameters. MMP-3 is released as pro-MMP-3 and becomes activated by serine proteases; once active, it can cleave a broad spectrum of ECM substrates and can also contribute to the activation of other MMPs, including MMP-1 and MMP-941,42. Increased hepatic MMP-3 expression has been reported in inflammatory liver injury, ischemia-reperfusion injury, and hepatocellular carcinoma, whereas expression by stellate cells and macrophages decreases during fibrosis resolution41–43. Notably, one study in a PBC population reported higher serum MMP-3 levels in PBC than in healthy controls and found positive associations with clinical and immunological parameters and with advanced fibrosis, which differs from our observations44. The absence of group differences and correlations in our cohort may be explained by the predominance of early-stage PBC without advanced fibrosis, in which MMP-3-driven inflammatory and remodeling pathways may be less pronounced.

In our cohort, circulating MMP-10 levels were only marginally elevated and showed no association with liver stiffness or established non-invasive fibrosis markers, suggesting limited utility as a marker of fibrotic burden in this clinical context. MMP-10 expression has been reported to be markedly upregulated in settings of acute liver injury, liver cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma and experimental data indicate that MMP-10 supports hepatic repair and regeneration following partial hepatectomy and bile duct ligation33,45,46. Taken together, the absence of correlations with stiffness and fibrosis indices in our study may reflect the predominance of non-advanced liver disease, where a strong induction of MMP-10 is not expected; similarly to our observations for MMP-3, relatively low MMP-10 levels in this cohort may therefore be a consequence of limited disease severity rather than a lack of biological relevance.

This study has several limitations. First, it was conducted in a relatively small, selected cohort of patients with early-stage PBC which limits statistical power and generalizability and increases the risk of statistical overfitting, particularly in multivariable and stepwise regression analyses. Therefore, the regression models should be regarded as exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory. This study should be considered a pilot investigation, and the observed associations, especially those involving MMP-7 and the MMP-7/MMP-26 ratio, require validation in larger, independent PBC cohorts. Second, the design was cross-sectional with single time-point measurements, so prognostic value for fibrosis progression or clinical outcomes cannot be established and longitudinal studies are needed. Third, fibrosis severity was assessed non-invasively rather than histology, which may introduce misclassification, however, given the overall low fibrosis burden in our cohort, this is unlikely to have materially affected the results.

Conclusions

Patients with early-stage primary biliary cholangitis display a distinct plasma metalloproteinase profile compared with healthy controls, characterized primarily by elevated MMP-7 and MMP-2 levels and reduced MMP-26 and MMP-9 levels. Among the analyzed enzymes, MMP-7 showed the highest diagnostic accuracy for distinguishing PBC from healthy controls and was associated with significant fibrosis in exploratory analyses. Its correlations with liver stiffness and ultrasonographic parameters related to portal hemodynamics support further evaluation of MMP-7 as a candidate non-invasive biomarker of fibrosis burden and portal-hemodynamic alterations in PBC.
