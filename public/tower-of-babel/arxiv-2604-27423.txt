Hirzebruch χ y \chi_{y} -genus of compact almost Kähler manifold with negative sectional curvature
Teng Huang, Pan Zhang

Abstract
Let ( X , J , ω ) (X,J,\omega) be a closed 2 ​ n 2n -dimensional almost Kähler manifold with negative sectional curvature. We prove that if the Nijenhuis tensor of the almost complex structure is sufficiently small, then the components of the Hirzebruch χ y \chi_{y} -genus satisfy the inequality ( − 1 ) n − p ​ χ p ​ ( X ) ≥ 1 (-1)^{n-p}\chi_{p}(X)\geq 1 for all p = 0 , 1 , ⋯ , n p=0,1,\cdots,n . In particular, this result implies the Hopf conjecture in this setting, namely that the Euler number satisfies ( − 1 ) n ​ χ ​ ( X ) ≥ n + 1 (-1)^{n}\chi(X)\geq n+1 . The proof is based on new L 2 L^{2} -estimates for harmonic forms on the universal covering, combined with a refined vanishing theorem for the operator ∂ ¯ + ∂ ¯ ∗ \bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{*} and Atiyah’s L 2 L^{2} -index theorem. This work extends the classical result of Gromov [J. Differential Geom., 1991] from the Kähler to the almost Kähler setting under the stated smallness condition.

Introduction

In complex geometry, Dolbeault cohomology plays a fundamental role in the study of complex manifolds. A classical method for computing these cohomology groups on closed complex manifolds relies on the associated spaces of harmonic forms.
More precisely, if
X
is a complex manifold, the exterior derivative
d
splits as
\partial+\bar{\partial}
, and these operators satisfy
\bar{\partial}^{2}=\partial^{2}=[\partial,\bar{\partial}]=0
. Consequently, one can define the Dolbeault cohomology groups as

H^{p,q}_{\bar{\partial}}:=\frac{\ker\bar{\partial}}{{\rm{Im}}\bar{\partial}},\ H^{p,q}_{\partial}:=\frac{\ker\partial}{{\rm{Im}}\partial}.

If
X
is closed, then it is a fundamental result that these cohomology spaces are isomorphic to the kernels of the two elliptic operators, namely the
\bar{\partial}
-Laplacian
\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}
and
\partial
-Laplacian
\Delta_{\partial}
.

However, on a non-complex Hermitian manifold
X
–that is,
a manifold where the almost complex structure
J
is non-integrable–the exterior derivative splits as
\partial+\mu+\bar{\partial}+\bar{\mu}
.
In particular,
\bar{\partial}^{2}\neq 0
, and hence the standard Dolbeault cohomology and its conjugate are not well-defined. Recently, Cirici and Wilson
[
4
]
introduced a definition for Dolbeault cohomology in the non-integrable setting
by considering the operator
\bar{\mu}
together with
\bar{\partial}
.

Similar to the integrable case, one can develop a Hodge theory for harmonic forms on
(X,J,\omega)
even without a cohomological counterpart (see
[
5
,
21
,
22
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
). We define two elliptic, self-adjoint differential operators:

\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}=\bar{\partial}\bar{\partial}^{\ast}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}\bar{\partial},\ \Delta_{\partial}=\partial\partial^{\ast}+\partial^{\ast}\partial.

Cirici and Wilson recently established a generalized Lefschetz decomposition theorem for closed almost Kähler manifolds. Denoting the Hodge Laplacian by
\Delta_{d}=dd^{\ast}+d^{\ast}d
, the space of harmonic
(p,q)
-forms
\ker\Delta_{d}\cap\Omega^{p,q}
is given by
\mathcal{H}^{p,q}_{d}
. In
[
3
, Corollary 5.4]
), they showed that if
(X,J,\omega)
is a closed almost Kähler manifold, then

\mathcal{H}^{p,q}_{d}=\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}L^{r}(\mathcal{H}^{p-r,q-r}_{d}\cap\mathrm{ker}(\Lambda)),

where
L
is the Lefschetz operator and
\Lambda
is its adjoint.

In this article, we will consider the spaces of (
\bar{\partial}
,
\partial
)-harmonic forms given by the intersections

\mathcal{H}^{p,q}_{(2);\bar{\partial}}\cap\mathcal{H}^{p,q}_{(2);\partial}.

These are identified with the kernel of the self-adjoint elliptic operator
\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}
acting on the
L^{2}
-space
\Omega^{p,q}_{(2)}(X)
. We denote by

l^{p,q}_{(2)}:=\dim(\mathcal{H}^{p,q}_{(2);\bar{\partial}}\cap\mathcal{H}^{p,q}_{(2);\partial})=\dim\ker(\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}+\Delta_{\partial})\cap\Omega^{p,q}_{(2)}

the dimensions of these spaces. In the integrable case, since
\Delta_{\partial}=\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{d}
, these dimensions coincide with the Hodge numbers of a closed Kähler manifold. As
\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}:\Omega^{p,q}\rightarrow\Omega^{p,q}
, there is an orthogonal direct sum decomposition

\ker(\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}})\cap\Omega^{k}_{(2)}=\bigoplus_{p+q=k}\ker(\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}})\cap\Omega^{p,q}_{(2)}.

We will investigate the elliptic self-adjoint operator
\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}
and study its
L^{2}
-estimates on complete almost Kähler manifolds. The following theorem provides a fundamental norm inequality for compactly supported smooth
(p,q)
-forms under the assumption that there exists a bounded 1-form
\theta
such that
\omega=d\theta
. This estimate is a key tool in our subsequent discussion of vanishing theorems for harmonic forms and the topological rigidity of almost Kähler manifolds. For simplicity, we denote by
\Omega^{p,q}_{0}(X)
the space of smooth
(p,q)
-forms with compact support on
X
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem
3.3
+Corollary
3.5
)
Let
(X,J,\omega)
be a complete
2n
-dimensional almost Kähler manifold. Suppose that there exists a bounded
1
-form
\theta
such that

\omega=d\theta.

Then for any
\alpha\in\Omega^{p,q}_{0}(X)
with
k:=p+q\neq n
, the following estimates hold:

\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\leq c_{2}(n,k)\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\Big((\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}})\alpha,\alpha\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}\Big(1-c_{2}(n,k)^{2}\|\theta\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}\sup|N_{J}|^{2}\Big)\leq 2c_{2}(n,k)^{2}\|\theta\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}(\Delta_{\bullet}\alpha,\alpha),

where
\bullet=\partial,\bar{\partial}
.

In the remainder of this article, we will study the topology and geometry of closed almost Kähler manifolds with negative sectional curvature. We begin by recalling a well-known conjecture related to the negativity of Riemannian sectional curvature (cf.
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
18
]
).

Conjecture 1

(Hopf Conjecture)
.

Let
X
be a closed
2n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with negative sectional curvature
\mathrm{sec}<0
. Then the Euler number of
X
satisfies

(-1)^{n}\chi(X)>0.

This conjecture holds in dimensions 2 and 4 (i.e.
n=1
and
2
), as the Gauss–Bonnet integrands in these two low-dimensional cases possess the desired sign. In higher dimensions, however, the sign of the sectional curvature does not determine the sign of the Gauss–Bonnet–Chern integrand.

Let
(X,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold and
\pi:(\tilde{X},\tilde{g})\rightarrow(X,g)
be its universal covering with
\tilde{g}=\pi^{\ast}g
. A differential form
\alpha
on
X
is called
\tilde{d}
(bounded) if its lift
\pi^{\ast}\alpha
is a
d
(bounded) form on
(\tilde{X},\tilde{g})
. Gromov observed that if
(X,g)
is a complete simply-connected manifold with strictly negative sectional curvature, then every smooth bounded closed form of degree
k\geq 2
is
d
(bounded). He subsequently proved the Hopf conjecture in the Kähler case by exploiting Kähler identities. A fundamental obstruction to extending Gromov’s argument beyond the integrable setting lies in the failure of the standard Kähler identities. In the non-integrable case, the crucial commutation relations that underpin Gromov’s
L^{2}
-estimates no longer hold. Our approach overcomes these difficulties by establishing a new family of
L^{2}
-estimates that quantitatively absorb the non-integrability of the almost complex structure.

In the symplectic case, inspired by Kähler geometry, one can analogously define symplectic hyperbolic manifolds. A closed almost Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
is called symplectic hyperbolic if the lift
\tilde{\omega}
of
\omega
to the universal covering
(\tilde{X},\tilde{\omega})\rightarrow(X,\omega)
is
d
(bounded) on
(\tilde{X},\tilde{\omega})

[
15
,
16
]
. Hind and Tomassini
[
14
]
constructed a
d
(bounded) complete almost Kähler manifold
X
satisfying
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{(2)}(X)\neq\{0\}
by employing methods from contact geometry.

The primary application of our analytic estimates is to extend Gromov’s result in
[
11
]
to the almost Kähler setting. We establish that the Hopf conjecture remains valid for almost Kähler manifolds provided the Nijenhuis tensor is bounded by a constant depending on the sectional curvature. In particular, we prove that the components of the Hirzebruch
\chi_{y}
-genus satisfy the inequality
(-1)^{n-p}\chi_{p}(X)\geq 1
for all
p=0,1,\dots,n
. This not only confirms the Hopf conjecture for the Euler number but also yields stronger topological constraints on all
\chi_{p}
. Our approach combines the
L^{2}
-estimates established in Theorem
1.1
with a refined vanishing theorem for the operator
\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}
and Atiyah’s
L^{2}
-index theorem
[
1
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(X,J,\omega)
be a closed
2n
-dimensional almost Kähler manifold with negative sectional curvature,
i.e. there exists a constant
K>0
such that

\mathrm{sec}\leq-K.

If the Nijenhuis tensor of
X
satisfies

|N_{J}|^{2}\leq C(n)K,

where
C(n)
is a uniform positive constant, then

(-1)^{n-p}\chi_{p}(X)\geq 1,\ \forall p=0,\cdots,n.

In particular, the Euler numbes of
X
obeys

(-1)^{n}\chi(X)\geq n+1.

Remark 1.3
.

(1) According to the identity for the Nijenhuis tensor provided in Proposition
2.2
, the hypothesis of Theorem
1.2
is equivalent to the curvature condition

\hat{W}(\omega^{\sharp},\omega^{\sharp})-\frac{n-1}{2n-1}s_{g}\leq\frac{C(n)}{16}K.

(2) If for all
i,j
, the complex sectional curvature satisfies

\bigl(\mathfrak{R}^{\mathbb{C}}(Z_{i}\wedge Z_{j}),\overline{Z_{i}\wedge Z_{j}}\bigr)\geq-\frac{C(n)}{32(n^{2}-n)}K,

then

|N_{J}|^{2}\leq C(n)K.

Note that any smooth Kähler metric satisfies
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathbb{C}}(Z_{i}\wedge Z_{j}),\overline{Z_{i}\wedge Z_{j}})=0
for all
i,j
.

Following Theorem
1.2
and Lemma
2.4
, we can immediately obtain the following conclusion.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
(X,J,\omega)
be a closed
2n
-dimensional almost Kähler manifold with negative sectional curvature,
i.e. there exists a constant
K>0
such that

\mathrm{sec}\leq-K.

If the Nijenhuis tensor of
X
satisfies

|N_{J}|^{2}\leq C(n)K,

then for any
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and for any
0\leq i\leq n
, the following inequality holds:

(-1)^{n-i}K_{i}(t)\geq\sum_{k=i}^{n}\binom{k}{i}t^{k-i},

where
K_{i}(t)
denote the coefficients in the Taylor expansion of
\chi_{y}(X)
centered at
y=-t
.

Preliminaries

2.1
Differential operators on almost Hermitian manifolds

We begin by recalling some basic definitions and results concerning differential forms on almost complex and almost Hermitian manifolds.

Let
X
be a
2n
-dimensional manifold (without boundary) equipped with a smooth almost complex structure
J
.
The natural action of
J
on the space of complex-valued differential forms
\Omega^{k}(X,\mathbb{C}):=\Omega^{k}(X)\otimes\mathbb{C}
induces a type decomposition:

\Omega^{k}(X,\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{p+q=k}\Omega^{p,q}(X),

where
\Omega^{p,q}(X)
denotes the space of complex forms of type
(p,q)
with respect to
J

[
13
]
.

The exterior derivative
d
acts on these spaces as follows:

d:\Omega^{p,q}\rightarrow\Omega^{p+2,q-1}\oplus\Omega^{p+1,q}\oplus\Omega^{p,q+1}\oplus\Omega^{p-1,q+2},

and consequently
d
splits into four components:

d=\mu+\partial+\bar{\partial}+\bar{\mu},

where each component is a graded algebra derivation. The operators
\mu
and
\bar{\mu}
are
0
-order differential operators.
The bidegrees of the components are given by

|\mu|=(2,-1),\ |\partial|=(1,0),\ |\bar{\partial}|=(0,1),\ |\bar{\mu}|=(-1,2).

Recall that the Nijenhuis tensor

N_{J}:TX\otimes TX\rightarrow TX,

is defined by

N_{J}(X,Y):=[X,Y]+J[X,JY]+J[JX,Y]-[JX,JY].

One can show that
\mu+\bar{\mu}
is, up to a scalar, equal to the dual of the Nijenhuis tensor (cf.
[
3
]
).
More precisely,

\mu+\bar{\mu}=-\frac{1}{4}(N_{J}\otimes{\rm{id}_{\mathbb{C}}})^{\ast},

where the right-hand side has been extended to all forms as a derivation.
The Newlander–Nirenberg integrability theorem asserts that the almost complex structure
J
is integrable if and only if
its Nijenhuis tensor
N_{J}
vanishes, which is equivalent to
\mu=0

[
3
,
4
]
.

For any almost Hermitian manifold
(X,J,g)
of dimension
2n
, there is an
associated Hodge-star operator

\star:\Omega^{p,q}\rightarrow\Omega^{n-q,n-p}

defined by

\alpha\wedge\star\beta=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\mathrm{dVol}_{g},

where
\mathrm{dVol}_{g}
is the volume form determined by the metric
g
.

The Lefschetz operator
L:\Omega^{p,q}\rightarrow\Omega^{p+1,q+1}
defined by

L(\alpha)=\omega\wedge\alpha.

The dual Lefschetz operator
\Lambda
is the adjoint of
L
, given by
\Lambda=\star^{-1}L\star

[
17
]
.
The operators
\bar{\mu},\bar{\partial},\partial,\mu
(each generically denoted by
\delta
) each possess an
L^{2}
-adjoint;
we denote the adjoint of
\delta
by
\delta^{*}
.
When
X
is closed, one may verify the following relations:

\bar{\mu}^{\ast}=-\star\mu\star\hskip 10.00002ptand\hskip 10.00002pt\bar{\partial}^{\ast}=-\star\partial\star.

For each such operator
\delta
, the associated Laplacian is defined by

\Delta_{\delta}:=\delta\delta^{\ast}+\delta^{\ast}\delta.

It satisfies

\star\Delta_{\bar{\delta}}=\Delta_{\delta}\star,

and consequently, the operator
\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}+\Delta_{\partial}
commute with the Hodge star operator:

\star(\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}+\Delta_{\partial})=(\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}+\Delta_{\partial})\star.

On an almost Kähler manifold, Cirici and Wilson established almost Kähler identities involving the differential operators
\partial
and
\bar{\partial}
,
the operators
\mu
and
\bar{\mu}
, the Lefschetz operator
L
, and their complex conjugates and adjoints.
These identities play a crucial role in the analysis of harmonic forms. We recall them here for later use.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[
3
]
)
For any almost Kähler manifold, the following identities hold:

(1)
[\partial,\bar{\partial}^{\ast}]=[\bar{\mu}^{\ast},\bar{\partial}]+[\mu,\partial^{\ast}]
and
[\bar{\partial},\partial^{\ast}]=[\mu^{\ast},\partial]+[\bar{\mu},\bar{\partial}^{\ast}]
.

(2)
[L,\bar{\mu}^{\ast}]=\sqrt{-1}\mu
,
[L,\mu^{\ast}]=-\sqrt{-1}\bar{\mu}
and
[\Lambda,\bar{\mu}]=\sqrt{-1}\mu^{\ast}
,
[\Lambda,\mu]=-\sqrt{-1}\bar{\mu}^{\ast}
.

(3)
[L,\bar{\partial}^{\ast}]=-\sqrt{-1}\partial
,
[L,\partial^{\ast}]=\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}
and
[\Lambda,\bar{\partial}]=-\sqrt{-1}\partial^{\ast}
,
[\Lambda,\partial]=\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}^{\ast}
.

Let
(X,J,g)
be an almost Hermitian manifold of real dimension
2n
, and let
\nabla
be the Levi-Civita connection of
(X,g)
.
The curvature of
(X,g)
is defined by

R(e_{i},e_{j})e_{k}=[\nabla_{e_{i}},\nabla_{e_{j}}]e_{k}-\nabla_{[e_{i},e_{j}]}e_{k},

and the Riemannian curvature tensor is given by
R(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l}):=g(R(e_{i},e_{j})e_{k},e_{l})
.
The associated scalar curvature
s_{g}
and
\ast
-scalar curvature
s_{g}^{*}
are given by

s_{g}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}R(e_{i},e_{j},e_{i},e_{j})\hskip 10.00002ptand\hskip 10.00002pts_{g}^{\ast}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}R(e_{i},e_{j},Je_{i},Je_{j}).

Let
W
be the Weyl tensor of
(X,g)
. The following useful formula was proved in
[
7
]
:

(2n-1)s_{g}^{\ast}-s_{g}=2(2n-1)\hat{W}(\omega^{\sharp},\omega^{\sharp}),

where
\hat{W}
is defined by
\hat{W}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j},e_{k}\wedge e_{l}):=W(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{l})

and
\omega^{\sharp}
denotes the dual tensor of the fundamental form
\omega
.

A fundamental relation between these objects in the almost Kähler setting is given by the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

(
[
24
, Lemma 2.4]
)
Let
(X,J,\omega)
be a closed
2n
-dimension almost Kähler manifold. Then the following identities hold:

|\nabla{J}|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}|N_{J}|^{2}=2(s_{g}^{\ast}-s_{g})=4\big(\hat{W}(\omega^{\sharp},\omega^{\sharp})-\frac{n-1}{2n-1}s_{g}\big).

We extended the metric to a complex bilinear form; we now extend the curvature operator at
x\in X
,
\mathfrak{R}:\Lambda^{2}T_{x}X\rightarrow\Lambda^{2}T_{x}X
, to a complex linear transformation
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathbb{C}}:\Lambda_{2}T_{x}X\otimes\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\Lambda_{2}T_{x}X\otimes\mathbb{C}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
12
, Lemma 3.3]
)
Let
Z_{j}
be any orthonormal basis of
T_{x}^{1,0}
. Then

s^{\ast}_{g}-s_{g}=-4\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(\mathfrak{R}^{\mathbb{C}}(Z_{i}\wedge Z_{j}),\overline{Z_{i}\wedge Z_{j}}).

2.2
Hirzebruch
\chi_{y}
-genus

We now turn to the definition of the Hirzebruch
\chi_{y}
-genus and its relation to the index theory of elliptic operators.

Let
(X,J)
be a closed almost complex manifold of real dimension
2n
with an almost complex structure
J
.
The choice of an almost Hermitian metric on
X
enables us to define the Hodge-star operator
\star

and the formal adjoint
\bar{\partial}^{\ast}
the
\bar{\partial}
-operator. For each
0\leq p\leq n
,
we define an elliptic differential operator

\mathcal{D}_{p}:=\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}:\Omega^{p,+}(X)\rightarrow\Omega^{p,-}(X),

where

\Omega^{p,+}(X)=\bigoplus_{q=even}\Omega^{p,q}(X)\hskip 10.00002ptand\hskip 10.00002pt\Omega^{p,-}(X)=\bigoplus_{q=odd}\Omega^{p,q}(X).

The index of this operator is denoted, following Hirzebruch’s notation, by

\chi_{p}(X):=\dim\ker(\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast})-\dim{\rm{coker}}(\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}).

Applying the general Riemann–Roch–Hirzebruch theorem (first proved by Hirzebruch for projective manifolds and later extended to the general case by Atiyah and Singer),
we have the formula

\chi_{p}(X)=\int_{X}\mathrm{ch}(\Omega^{p,0}(X))\mathrm{Td}(TX),

where
\mathrm{ch}(\cdot)
is the Chern character and
\mathrm{Td}(\cdot)
is the Todd class.
The Hirzebruch
\chi_{y}
-genus
\chi_{y}(X)
is then defined as the polynomial

\chi_{y}(X)=\sum_{p=0}^{n}\chi_{p}(X)y^{p}.

Let
\gamma_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, denote the formal Chern roots of the tangent bundle; that is, the
i
-th elementary symmetric polynomial of
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}
represents the
i
-th Chern class
c_{i}(X)
of

TX
, then the Atiyah–Singer Index Theorem yields the following expression:

\chi_{y}(X)=\int_{X}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+ye^{-\gamma_{i}})\frac{\gamma_{i}}{1-e^{-\gamma_{i}}}.

The Hirzebruch
\chi_{y}
-genus
\chi_{y}(X)
interpolates between three fundamental topological invariants of
X
:

•

the Euler number (when
y=-1
),

•

the Todd genus (when
y=0
),

•

the signature (when
y=1
).

In an almost complex manifold, following the idea introduced by Salamon
[
23
]
,
we can define
K_{i}(X)
for
0\leq i\leq n
via the Hirzebruch
\chi_{y}
-genus

\chi_{y}(X):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}K_{i}(X)(y+1)^{i}.

Based on
[
23
]
, the primary role of the quantity
K_{i}(X)
is
to extract finer topological invariants with good multiplicative properties from the Hirzebruch
\chi_{y}
-genus.
A direct inversion yields the explicit expression (cf.
[
23
, Corollary 3.3]
):

K_{i}(X)=\sum_{k=i}^{n}(-1)^{k-i}\binom{k}{i}\chi_{k}(X).

More generically, we can consider a shifted expansion by introducing a parameter
t
.
Specifically, let
K_{i}(t)
denote the coefficients in the Taylor expansion of
\chi_{y}(X)
centered at
y=-t
;
that is,

\chi_{y}(X):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}K_{i}(t)(y+t)^{i}.

The relation between these shifted coefficients and the numbers
\chi_{k}(X)
is given by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any integer
0\leq i\leq n
, the shifted coefficient
K_{i}(t)
satisfies the identity

(-1)^{n-i}K_{i}(t)=\sum_{k=i}^{n}\binom{k}{i}(-1)^{n-k}\chi_{k}(X)t^{k-i}.

Proof.

By the standard formula for the coefficients of a Taylor expansion, we have

\begin{split}K_{i}(t)&=\frac{1}{i!}\big(\chi_{y}(X)\big)^{(i)}|_{y=-t}\\
&=\frac{1}{i!}\big(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\chi_{k}(X)y^{k}\big)^{(i)}|_{y=-t}\\
&=\sum_{k=i}^{n}\binom{k}{i}(-t)^{k-i}\chi_{k}(X).\\
\end{split}

Multiplying both sides by
(-1)^{n-i}
and simplifying the powers of
-1
yields the desired identity.
∎

2.3
Atiyah’s
L^{2}
-index theorem

Let
\pi:(\tilde{X},\tilde{J})\rightarrow(X,J)
be the universal covering with
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(X)
as an isometry group of deck transformations.
The Dolbeault-type operators
\mathcal{D}_{p}=\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}
lift naturally to operators on
(\tilde{X},\tilde{J})
:

\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{p}=\widetilde{\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}}:\Omega^{p,+}_{(2)}(\tilde{X})\rightarrow\Omega^{p,-}_{(2)}(\tilde{X}).

We denote by
\dim_{\Gamma}\ker(\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{p})
and
\dim_{\Gamma}\mathrm{coker}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{p})

the von Neumann dimensions of
\ker(\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{p})
and
\mathrm{coker}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{p})

with respect to
\Gamma
, which are nonnegative real numbers
[
1
,
2
,
19
]
.
The following fundamental property of the von Neumann dimension is essential (see
[
19
, Theorem 1.12]
):

\dim_{\Gamma}\ker(\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{p})=0\Leftrightarrow\ker(\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{p})=0,

\dim_{\Gamma}\mathrm{coker}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{p})=0\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{coker}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{p})=0.

The
L^{2}
-index of the lifted operators
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{p}
is then defined as

L^{2}\mathrm{Index}_{\Gamma}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{p})=\dim_{\Gamma}(\ker\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{p})-\dim_{\Gamma}({\rm{coker}}\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{p}).

We now recall the following Atiyah’s
L^{2}
index theorem
[
1
,
20
]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
20
, Theorem 6.1]

Let
X
be a closed Riemannian manifold and let
P
be a determined elliptic operator on sections of certain bundles over
X
.
Denote by
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
its lift of
\mathcal{D}
to the universal convering space
\tilde{X}
, and let
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)
.
Then the
L^{2}
kernel of
\tilde{P}
has a finite
\Gamma
-dimension, and

L^{2}\mathrm{Index}_{\Gamma}(\tilde{P})=\mathrm{Index}(P).

As an immediate consequence, we obtain the following relation between the
\chi_{p}
-genus of
X
and the
L^{2}
-index of the lifted operator.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(X,J)
be a closed almost complex manifold. Then the following equality holds:

\chi_{p}(X)=L^{2}\mathrm{Index}_{\Gamma}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{p}).

Remark 2.7
.

When
J
is integrable, i.e.
\bar{\partial}^{2}=0
,
\chi_{p}(X)
equals the index of the following well-known Dolbeault complex

\cdots\Omega^{p,q-1}(X)\xrightarrow{\bar{\partial}}\Omega^{p,q}(X)\xrightarrow{\bar{\partial}}\Omega^{p,q+1}(X)\rightarrow\cdots

and consequently

\chi_{p}(X)=\sum_{q=0}^{n}(-1)^{q}h^{p,q}(X),

where
h^{p,q}(X)
denote the Hodge numbers of
X
.

3
Vanishing theorem of
L^{2}
-harmonic forms

Throughout this section, we employ the notation
\alpha\lesssim\beta
to indicate that
\alpha\leq C\beta
for some positive constant
C

that is independent of certain parameters on which
\alpha
and
\beta
depend. The precise parameters with respect to
which
C
is independent will be either clear from the context or specified at each occurrence.

Throughout this section,
(X,J,\omega)
denotes a complete almost Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
. The global
L^{2}
-inner product is defined as

(u,v)=\int_{X}\langle u,v\rangle dV=\int_{X}u\wedge\star\bar{v},

where
dV=\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}
is the volume form determined by
\omega
. We also write

|u|^{2}=\langle u,u\rangle,\hskip 10.00002pt\|u\|^{2}=\int_{X}|u|^{2}dV.

In addition to the
L^{2}
norm, we will also need the notion of pointwise boundedness.
A differential form
\alpha
is called bounded with respect to the metric
g
if its
L^{\infty}
-norm is finite, namely,

\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}=\sup_{x\in X}|\alpha(x)|<\infty.

Furthermore, a
k
-form
\alpha
is said to be
d
(bounded) if
\alpha=d\beta
for some bounded
(k-1)
-form
\beta
. It is obvious that if
X
is closed, then every exact form is
d
(bounded).

3.1
The
L^{2}
-estimates

We begin with a useful decomposition result for exact
(1,1)
-forms on a complete almost Kähler manifold.

Proposition 3.1
.

If the
(1,1)
-form
\tilde{\omega}
on a complete almost Kähler manifold
X
is exact, then there exists a
1
-form
\theta
such that

\tilde{\omega}=\partial\theta^{0,1}+\bar{\partial}\theta^{1,0},

where
\theta^{1,0}
(resp.
\theta^{0,1}
) is the
(1,0)
(resp.
(0,1)
) part of
\theta
.

Proof.

By the hypothesis, we have
\tilde{\omega}=d\theta
. Decomposing
d
and
\theta
according to type yields

\begin{split}d\theta&=(\partial+\mu+\bar{\partial}+\bar{\mu})(\theta^{1,0}+\theta^{0,1})\\
&=(\partial\theta^{1,0}+\mu\theta^{0,1})+(\partial\theta^{0,1}+\bar{\partial}\theta^{1,0})+(\bar{\partial}\theta^{0,1}+\bar{\mu}\theta^{1,0}).\\
\end{split}

Since
\tilde{\omega}
is a
(1,1)
-form, the components of types
(2,0)
and
(0,2)
must vanish. Hence

\partial\theta^{1,0}+\mu\theta^{0,1}=\bar{\partial}\theta^{0,1}+\bar{\mu}\theta^{1,0}=0

and consequently

\tilde{\omega}=\partial\theta^{0,1}+\bar{\partial}\theta^{1,0}.

This completes the proof.
∎

The next proposition provides a key estimate involving a bounded
1
-form
\theta
and the
(\partial
,
\bar{\partial})
-Laplacian.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(X,J,\omega)
be a complete
2n
-dimensional almost Kähler manifold. Suppose that
\theta
is a bounded
1
-form.
Then for any
\alpha\in\Omega^{p,q}_{0}(X)
with
k:=p+q<n
, the following estimate holds:

\Big|\Big((d\theta)^{1,1}\wedge\alpha,\omega\wedge\alpha\Big)\Big|\leq c_{1}(n,k)\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\Big((\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}})\alpha,\alpha\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(3.1)

where
c_{1}(n,k)
is a positive constant which depends only
n
and
k
.

Proof.

Using integration by parts, the decomposition from Proposition
3.1
, and the almost Kähler identities, we compute:

\begin{split}\Big((d\theta)^{1,1}\wedge\alpha,\omega\wedge\alpha\Big)&=\Big((\partial\theta^{0,1}+\bar{\partial}\theta^{1,0})\wedge\alpha,\omega\wedge\alpha\Big)\\
&=\Big(\partial(\theta^{0,1}\wedge\alpha)+\theta^{0,1}\wedge\partial\alpha,\omega\wedge\alpha\Big)+\Big(\bar{\partial}(\theta^{1,0}\wedge\alpha)+\theta^{1,0}\wedge\bar{\partial}\alpha,\omega\wedge\alpha\Big)\\
&=(\theta^{0,1}\wedge\alpha,[\partial^{\ast},L]\alpha)+(\theta^{0,1}\wedge\alpha,L(\partial^{\ast}\alpha))+(\theta^{0,1}\wedge\partial\alpha,\omega\wedge\alpha)\\
&\quad+(\theta^{1,0}\wedge\alpha,[\bar{\partial}^{\ast},L]\alpha)+(\theta^{1,0}\wedge\alpha,L(\bar{\partial}^{\ast}\alpha))+(\theta^{1,0}\wedge\bar{\partial}\alpha,\omega\wedge\alpha)\\
&=(\theta^{0,1}\wedge\alpha,-\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}\alpha)+(\theta^{0,1}\wedge\alpha,L(\partial^{\ast}\alpha))+(\theta^{0,1}\wedge\partial\alpha,\omega\wedge\alpha)\\
&\quad+(\theta^{1,0}\wedge\alpha,\sqrt{-1}\partial\alpha)+(\theta^{1,0}\wedge\alpha,L(\bar{\partial}^{\ast}\alpha))+(\theta^{1,0}\wedge\bar{\partial}\alpha,\omega\wedge\alpha).\\
\end{split}

In the last equality, we employed the almost Kähler identities from Proposition
2.1
:

[\partial^{\ast},L]=-\sqrt{-1}\bar{\partial}\hskip 10.00002ptand\hskip 10.00002pt[\bar{\partial}^{\ast},L]=\sqrt{-1}\partial.

Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to each term, we obtain

\begin{split}&\quad\Big|\Big((d\theta)^{1,1}\wedge\alpha,\omega\wedge\alpha\Big)\Big|\\
&\lesssim\|\theta^{0,1}\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\|\bar{\partial}\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\theta^{0,1}\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\|\partial^{\ast}\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\theta^{0,1}\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\|\partial\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\\
&\quad+\|\theta^{1,0}\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\|\partial\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\theta^{1,0}\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\|\bar{\partial}^{\ast}\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\theta^{1,0}\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\|\bar{\partial}\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\\
&\leq c_{1}(n,k)\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\Big((\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}})\alpha,\alpha\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

With the above preparations, we now state the first main vanishing theorem.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(X,J,\omega)
be a complete
2n
-dimensional almost Kähler manifold. Suppose that there exists a bounded
1
-form
\theta
such that

\omega=d\theta.

Then for any
\alpha\in\Omega^{p,q}_{0}(X)
with
k:=p+q\neq n
, the following estimate holds:

\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\leq c_{2}(n,k)\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\Big((\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}})\alpha,\alpha\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(3.2)

where
c_{2}(n,k)
is a positive constant which depends only
n
and
k
. Furthermore, for any
p+q\neq n
,

\ker(\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}})\cap\Omega^{p,q}_{(2)}(X)=\{0\}.

Proof.

Assume first that
k:=p+q<n
and
\alpha\in\Omega^{p,q}_{0}(X)
. From the estimate (
3.1
), we obtain

\begin{split}\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}&\lesssim\|\omega\wedge\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\Big(\omega\wedge\alpha,(d\theta)^{1,1}\wedge\alpha\Big)\\
&\leq c_{2}(n,k)\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\Big((\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}})\alpha,\alpha\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

The case
k:=p+q>n
follows by Poincaré duality.
The Hodge star operator
\star:\Omega^{p,q}(X)\rightarrow\Omega^{n-q,n-p}(X)
maps a form of degree
k>n
to a form of degree
2n-k<n
.
Since it commutes with the Laplacian, that is,

\star(\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}+\Delta_{\partial})=(\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}+\Delta_{\partial})\star,

for any
\alpha\in\Omega^{p,q}_{0}(X)
with
p+q>n
we have

\begin{split}\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}=\|\star\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}&\leq c_{2}(n,k)\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\Big((\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}})(\star\alpha),\star\alpha\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&=c_{2}(n,k)\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\Big(\star(\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}})\alpha,\star\alpha\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&=c_{2}(n,k)\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\Big((\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}})\alpha,\alpha\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

The vanishing statement follows directly from the estimate (
3.2
).
∎

3.2
L^{2}
-harmonic forms

We now introduce the spaces of
L^{2}
-harmonic forms with respect to the Laplacians
\Delta_{\partial}
and
\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}
:

\mathcal{H}^{p,q}_{(2);\bullet}(X):=\{\alpha\in\Omega^{p,q}_{(2)}(X):\Delta_{\bullet}\alpha=0\},

where
\bullet=\partial,\bar{\partial}
.
The following standard fact characterizes harmonicity in terms of the vanishing of first-order derivatives.

Lemma 3.4
.

(
[
15
, Lemma3.3]
)
If an
L^{2}

(p,q)
-form
\alpha
on
X
is
\Delta_{\partial}
- (resp.
\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}
-) harmonic, then

\partial\alpha=0,\ \partial^{\ast}\alpha=0\hskip 10.00002pt(resp.\ \bar{\partial}\alpha=0,\ \bar{\partial}^{\ast}\alpha=0).

Recall from
[
3
]
(cf. also
[
25
]
) that on a complete almost Kähler manifold, the following operator identity holds:

\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}+\Delta_{\mu}=\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\mu}}.

This identity plays a crucial role in relating the various Laplacians. Combining it with Theorem
3.3
yields the following refined estimate.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
(X,J,\omega)
be a complete
2n
-dimensional almost Kähler manifold. Suppose that there exists a bounded
1
-form
\theta
such that

\omega=d\theta.

Then for any
\alpha\in\Omega^{p,q}_{0}(X)
with
k:=p+q\neq n
, the following estimate holds:

\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}\Big(1-c_{2}(n,k)^{2}\|\theta\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}\sup|N_{J}|^{2}\Big)\leq 2c_{2}(n,k)^{2}\|\theta\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}(\Delta_{\bullet}\alpha,\alpha),

(3.3)

where
\bullet=\partial,\bar{\partial}
. Furthermore, if the smallness condition

c_{2}(n,k)\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\sup|N_{J}|<1

holds, then for any
k\neq n
,

\mathcal{H}^{p,q}_{(2);\bullet}(X)=\{0\}.

Proof.

For any
\alpha\in\Omega^{p,q}_{0}(X)
, we have

\begin{split}\Big((\Delta_{\mu}-\Delta_{\bar{\mu}})\alpha,\alpha\Big)&=\|\mu\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}+\|\bar{\mu}\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}+\|\mu^{\ast}\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}+\|\bar{\mu}^{\ast}\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}\\
&\leq\sup|N_{J}|^{2}\cdot\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}.\\
\end{split}

Observe the following identities relating the various Laplacians:

\begin{split}&\Delta_{\partial}=\frac{1}{2}(\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}+\Delta_{\mu}-\Delta_{\bar{\mu}}),\\
&\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}=\frac{1}{2}(\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}-\Delta_{\mu}+\Delta_{\bar{\mu}}).\\
\end{split}

Combining these with estimate (
3.2
), we obtain for
\bullet=\bar{\partial},\partial
:

\begin{split}(\Delta_{\bullet}\alpha,\alpha)&=\frac{1}{2}\Big((\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}})\alpha,\alpha\Big)\pm\frac{1}{2}\Big((\Delta_{\mu}-\Delta_{\bar{\mu}})\alpha,\alpha\Big)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big((\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\partial}})\alpha,\alpha\Big)-\frac{1}{2}\sup|N_{J}|^{2}\cdot\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}\\
&\geq\Big(\frac{1}{2}c_{2}(n,k)^{-2}\|\theta\|^{-2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}-\frac{1}{2}\sup|N_{J}|^{2}\Big)\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}.\end{split}

Rearrangement this inequality yields (
3.3
). The vanishing statement follows directly from (
3.3
) under the stated smallness condition.
∎

Next, we establish an
L^{2}
-estimate for the operator
\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{*}
under the same hypotheses.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(X,J,\omega)
be a complete
2n
-dimensional almost Kähler manifold. Suppose that there exists a bounded
1
-form
\theta
such that

\omega=d\theta.

Then there exist constants
c_{3}(n),c_{4}(n)
depending only on
n
such that the following holds:
for any
\alpha\in\Omega_{0}^{p,+}(X)
when
n-p
is odd (resp.
\alpha\in\Omega_{0}^{p,-}(X)
when
n-p
is even),

\|\theta\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}\|(\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast})\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}\geq\Big(c_{3}(n)-c_{4}(n)\|\theta\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}\sup|N_{J}|^{2}\Big)\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}.

Proof.

We give the proof for the case when
n-p
is odd; the even case is entirely analogous.

First, observe that

({\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}})^{2}=\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}+(\bar{\partial})^{2}+(\bar{\partial}^{\ast})^{2}.

For any
\alpha\in\Omega_{0}^{p,+}(X)
, we decompose
\alpha:=\sum\alpha_{2i}
with
\alpha_{2i}\in\Omega^{p,2i}
. Then

\begin{split}\|({\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}})\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}&=(\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}\alpha,\alpha)+\Big((\bar{\partial})^{2}\alpha,\alpha\Big)+\Big((\bar{\partial}^{\ast})^{2}\alpha,\alpha\Big)\\
&=(\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}\alpha,\alpha)+2\mathrm{Re}\Big((\bar{\partial})^{2}\alpha,\alpha\Big)\\
&=\sum(\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i})+2\sum\mathrm{Re}\Big((\bar{\partial})^{2}\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i+2}\Big)\\
&=\sum(\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i})-2\sum\mathrm{Re}\Big([\partial,\bar{\mu}]\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i+2}\Big),\end{split}

where we used the identity

\bar{\partial}^{2}=-[\partial,\bar{\mu}].

For each term in the sum, we estimate:

\begin{split}|([\partial,\bar{\mu}]\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i+2})|&=|(\bar{\mu}\alpha_{2i},\partial^{\ast}\alpha_{2i+2})+(\partial\alpha_{2i},\bar{\mu}^{\ast}\alpha_{2i+2})|\\
&\leq\|\bar{\mu}\alpha_{2i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial^{\ast}\alpha_{2i+2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial\alpha_{2i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\bar{\mu}^{\ast}\alpha_{2i+2}\|_{L^{2}}.\\
\end{split}

Summing over
i
and applying the elementary inequality
2ab\leq c_{0}a^{2}+\frac{1}{c_{0}}b^{2}

for all
c_{0}>0
, we obtain

\begin{split}|2\sum\mathrm{Re}([\partial,\bar{\mu}]\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i+2})|&\leq 2\sum(\|\bar{\mu}\alpha_{2i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial^{\ast}\alpha_{2i+2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial\alpha_{2i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\bar{\mu}^{\ast}\alpha_{2i+2}\|_{L^{2}})\\
&\leq\sum c_{0}(\|\partial\alpha_{2i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\partial^{\ast}\alpha_{2i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}})+\sum\frac{1}{c_{0}}(\|\bar{\mu}\alpha_{2i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\bar{\mu}^{\ast}\alpha_{2i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}})\\
&=\sum c_{0}(\Delta_{\partial}\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i})+\sum\frac{1}{c_{0}}(\Delta_{\bar{\mu}}\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i}).\\
\end{split}

Combining this with the earlier expression for
\|({\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}})\alpha\|^{2}
and using the identity

\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}=\Delta_{\partial}+\Delta_{\bar{\mu}}-\Delta_{\mu},

we find

\begin{split}\|({\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}})\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}&\geq\sum\big((\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i})-c_{0}(\Delta_{\partial}\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i})-\frac{1}{c_{0}}(\Delta_{\bar{\mu}}\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i})\big)\\
&=\sum\big((1-c_{0})(\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i})-c_{0}(\Delta_{\mu}\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i})-(\frac{1}{c_{0}}-c_{0})(\Delta_{\bar{\mu}}\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i})\big).\\
\end{split}

Choosing
c_{0}=\frac{1}{2}
and applying Corollary
3.5
, we obtain

\begin{split}\|({\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}})\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}&\geq\sum\Big(\frac{1}{2}(\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i})-\frac{1}{2}(\Delta_{\mu}\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i})-\frac{3}{2}(\Delta_{\bar{\mu}}\alpha_{2i},\alpha_{2i})\Big)\\
&\geq\sum\|\theta\|^{-2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}\Big(c_{3}(n)-c_{4}(n)\|\theta\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}\sup|N_{J}|^{2}\Big)\|\alpha_{2i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}\\
&=\|\theta\|^{-2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}\Big(c_{3}(n)-c_{4}(n)\|\theta\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(X)}\sup|N_{J}|^{2}\Big)\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

3.3
The case of small Nijenhuis tensor

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be
C^{\infty}
-vector bundles over a smooth manifold
X
,
and let
\mathcal{D}:C^{\infty}(E_{1})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(E_{2})
be a differential operator between
C^{\infty}
-sections of these bundle.
Suppose furthermore that
X
is a smooth Riemannian manifold and that
\Gamma
is a discrete group of isometrics of
X
such that
the differential operator
\mathcal{D}
commutes with the action of
\Gamma
. Let
(\mathcal{L},\nabla)
be a
\Gamma
-invariant Hermitian line bundle on
X
,
and assume that
X/\Gamma
is closed. Under these hypotheses, we recall Atiyah’s
L^{2}
-index theorem for the twisted operator
\mathcal{D}\otimes\nabla
.

Theorem 3.7
.

[
11
, Theorem 2.3.A]

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a first-order elliptic operator. Then there exists a closed nonhomogeneous form

I_{D}=I^{0}+I^{1}+\cdots+I^{n}\in\Omega^{\ast}(X)=\Omega^{0}\oplus\Omega^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\Omega^{n}

invariant under
\Gamma
, such that the
L^{2}
-index of the twisted operator
\mathcal{D}\otimes\nabla
is given by

L^{2}\mathrm{Index}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{D}\otimes\nabla)=\int_{X/\Gamma}I_{\mathcal{D}}\wedge\exp{[\omega]},

where
[\omega]
is the Chern form of
\nabla
, and

\exp{[\omega]}=1+[\omega]+\frac{[\omega]\wedge[\omega]}{2!}+\frac{[\omega]\wedge[\omega]\wedge[\omega]}{3!}+\cdots.

Remark 3.8
.

(1) An non-zero
L^{2}\mathrm{Index}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{D}\otimes\nabla)
implies that
either
\mathcal{D}\otimes\nabla
or its adjoint admits a non-trivial
L^{2}
-kernel.

(2) In the present article, the operator
\mathcal{D}
is taken to be
\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}
.
In this case, the
I^{0}
-component of
I_{\mathcal{D}}
is nonzero.
Consequently, the integral
\int_{X/\Gamma}I_{\mathcal{D}}\wedge\exp{t[\omega]}\neq 0
, for almost all
t
,
provided the curvature form
\omega
is homologically nonsingular
\int_{X/\Gamma}\omega^{n}\neq 0
, for
n=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X
.

Gromov defined the lower spectral bound
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(\mathcal{D})\geq 0
as the upper bound of the negative numbers
\lambda
, such that

\|\mathcal{D}s\|_{L^{2}}\geq\lambda\|s\|_{L^{2}}

for those sections
e
of
E
where
\mathcal{D}s
in
L^{2}
.
Now let
\mathcal{D}
be a
\Gamma
-invariant, first-order elliptic operator on
X
,
and let
I_{D}=I^{0}+I^{1}+\cdots+I^{n}\in\Omega^{\ast}(X)
be the corresponding index form.
Let
\omega
be a closed
\Gamma
-invariant
2
-form on
X
and denote by
I_{t}^{n}
the top component of product
I_{\mathcal{D}}\wedge\exp{t\omega}
,
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence
I_{t}^{n}
is a
\Gamma
-invariant
n
-form on
X
,
\dim X=n
depending on parameter
t
.
The following theorem provides a criterion for the vanishing of the lower spectral bound.

Theorem 3.9
.

(
[
11
, 2.4.A. Theorem]
)
Assume that
H^{1}_{dR}(X)=0
, that
X/\Gamma
is closed, and that
\int_{X/\Gamma}I_{t}^{n}\neq 0
, for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
If the form
\omega
is
d
(bounded), then either
\lambda_{0}(\mathcal{D})=0
or
\lambda_{0}(\mathcal{D}^{\ast})=0
,
where
\mathcal{D}^{\ast}
is the adjoint operator.

We also recall the following classical result due to Gromov, for which Chen and Yang provided a detailed proof.

Lemma 3.10
.

(
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
)
Let
(X,g)
be a complete simply-connected manifold with negative sectional curvature bounded from above by a negative constant, i.e.

\mathrm{sec}\leq-K

for some
K>0
. Then for any bounded and closed
k
-form
\alpha
on
X
, where
k\geq 2
, there exists a bounded
(k-1)
-form
\beta
such that

\alpha=d\beta

and

\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}}.

We now apply the above general theory to the specific setting of almost Kähler manifolds with negative sectional curvature.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
(X,J,\omega)
be a closed
2n
-dimensional almost Kähler manifold with negative sectional curvature,
i.e. there exists a constant
K>0
such that

\mathrm{sec}\leq-K.

Let
\pi:(\tilde{X},\tilde{J},\tilde{\omega})\rightarrow(X,J,\omega)
be the universal covering map of
X
.
Then for any
\alpha\in\Omega_{0}^{p,+}(\tilde{X})
when
n-p
is odd (resp.
\alpha\in\Omega_{0}^{p,-}(\tilde{X})
when
n-p
is even),
the following estimate holds:

\|(\widetilde{\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}})\alpha\|_{L^{2}(\tilde{X})}\geq\Big(\frac{c_{3}(n)}{n}K-c_{4}(n)\sup|N_{J}|^{2}\Big)\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\tilde{X})}.

Proof.

Note that the pointwise norm of the Kähler form satisfies
|\tilde{\omega}|^{2}=n
. By Lemma
3.10
, the negative curvature condition implies the existence of a
1
-form
\theta
such that

\tilde{\omega}=d\theta\hskip 10.00002ptand\hskip 10.00002pt\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{X})}\leq K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{n}.

Applying Theorem
3.6
on the universal cover
\tilde{X}
with this
1
-form
\theta
, we obtain

\begin{split}K^{-1}\|(\widetilde{\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}})\alpha\|_{L^{2}(\tilde{X})}&\geq\|\theta\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{X})}\|(\widetilde{\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}})\alpha\|_{L^{2}(\tilde{X})}\\
&\geq\Big(c_{3}(n)-c_{4}(n)\|\theta\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{X})}\sup|N_{J}|^{2}\Big)\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\tilde{X})}\\
&\geq\Big(c_{3}(n)-c_{4}(n)K^{-1}n\sup|N_{J}|^{2}\Big)\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\tilde{X})}.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

We are now in a position to prove the main theorem of this section.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Assume that the Nijenhuis tensor satisfies the smallness condition

\sup|N_{J}|^{2}\leq\frac{c_{3}(n)K}{2nc_{4}(n)}:=C(n)K.

Substituting this bound into the estimate from Corollary
3.11
, we find that when
n-p
is odd, for any
\alpha\in\Omega^{p,+}_{0}(\tilde{X})
,

\|(\widetilde{\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}})\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\tilde{X})}\geq\Big(\frac{c_{3}(n)K}{2n}\Big)\|\alpha\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\tilde{X})}.

This strictly positive lower bound implies that the lower spectral bound

\lambda_{0}
of the operator
\widetilde{\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}}:\Omega_{(2)}^{p,+}(\tilde{X})\rightarrow\Omega_{(2)}^{p,-}(\tilde{X})

is strictly positive.

On the other hand, since
H^{1}_{dR}(\tilde{X})=0
for a simply-connected manifold,
X=\tilde{X}/\Gamma
is closed,
the negative sectional curvature implies that the Kähler form
\tilde{\omega}
is
d
(bounded),
and the integral
\int_{X}I^{n}_{t}\neq 0
for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
, (see Remark
3.8
), we can apply Theorem
3.9
. The positivity of
\lambda_{0}
forces the kernel of the operator itself to be trivial, which, by the Theorem
3.9
, forces the kernel of the adjoint operator to be non-trivial; specifically,

{\rm{coker}}(\widetilde{\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{\ast}})\cap\Omega^{p,odd}_{(2)}(\tilde{X})\neq\{0\}.

Applying Proposition
2.6
, we conclude that

\chi_{p}(X)=-\dim_{\Gamma}\mathrm{coker}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{p})<0.

When
n-p
is even, an analogous argument (using the spaces
\Omega^{p,-}_{(2)}(\tilde{X})
) shows that
\chi_{p}(M)>0
.
Combining both cases, we obtain the uniform inequality

(-1)^{n-p}\chi_{p}(X)\geq 1

for all
p=0,1,\cdots,n
. The statement for the Euler number follows by evaluating the Hirzebruch genus at
y=-1
, where

\chi_{y}(M)|_{y=-1}=(-1)^{n}\chi(M)=\sum_{p=0}^{n}(-1)^{p}\chi_{p}(X).

Since each term
(-1)^{n-p}\chi_{p}(X)\geq 1
, their sum is at least
n+1
, giving
(-1)^{n}\chi(X)\geq n+1
.
∎
