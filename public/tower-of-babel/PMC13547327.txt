Circulating IGF2BP3 enables risk stratification and predicts treatment response in Ewing sarcoma
Authors: Caterina Mancarella, Alessandra De Feo, Irene Dirignani, Elisa Simonetti, Margherita Maioli, Maria Cristina Manara, Cristina Ferrari, Federica Costantino, Marilena Cesari, Alberto Bazzocchi, Toni Ibrahim, Giuseppe Bianchi, Marco Gambarotti, Katia Scotlandi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Ewing sarcoma (EWS), the second most common pediatric bone tumor, presents with a markedly heterogeneous clinical spectrum and optimal risk stratification is therefore crucial for improving treatment outcomes. The RNA-binding protein IGF2BP3 is a critical oncogenic driver of EWS malignancy. This study evaluates the clinical utility of circulating IGF2BP3 as a biomarker to predict treatment response and risk of disease progression in patients with EWS. Plasma samples from 60 patients with EWS diagnosed and treated at the IRCCS Rizzoli Orthopedic Institute (Bologna, Italy) were collected at diagnosis before treatment initiation and/or after induction chemotherapy. For 51 of these patients, blood was collected at diagnosis, prior to any treatments. For 25 patients, blood samples were available at diagnosis and before surgical intervention, allowing longitudinal analysis in the same patient. For 9 patients, blood was collected only after preoperative chemotherapy, before surgical intervention. Circulating IGF2BP3 levels were quantified using a highly specific and sensitive ELISA assay. Plasma samples from healthy donors served as controls. IGF2BP3 plasma levels were correlated with IGF2BP3 tumor tissue expression, established clinical risk factors, and cumulative incidence of relapse using univariable and multivariable analyses. Plasma IGF2BP3 levels were significantly elevated in patients with EWS compared with healthy controls, with a subset of patients (22/51, 43.2%) exhibiting clinically relevant concentrations. Circulating IGF2BP3 levels reflected tumor expression of the molecule and provided additional prognostic information beyond standard clinicopathologic features. The prognostic impact of circulating IGF2BP3 was primarily observed in patients with localized disease, in whom elevated levels were identified as a significant adverse prognostic factor for disease-specific survival (hazard ratio, 10.63; 95% CI, 1.27–88.62; P = 0.029). Longitudinal monitoring demonstrated that persistence of IGF2BP3 in plasma after induction chemotherapy was a strong predictor of poor clinical outcomes. Circulating IGF2BP3 represents a valuable biomarker for early risk stratification in EWS, particularly in patients with localized disease. Although this is single-marker assay, the expression of the molecule may impact on the fate of many mRNAs. We present an accurate, simple, cost-effective and easy clinical applicable tool to support risk-adapted therapeutic interventions. Introduction

Ewing sarcoma (EWS) is an aggressive primary malignancy of bone and soft tissue, with a peak incidence in children and adolescents, driven by oncogenic FET–ETS gene fusions, most commonly EWS::FLI11. At diagnosis, EWS presents as localized disease in approximately 70–75% of patients, whereas the remaining 25–30% present with metastatic disease2. Patients are treated with intensive, multidrug neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by consolidation therapy, while local control relies on surgery and/or radiotherapy3,4. This multidisciplinary approach achieves long-term survival rates of approximately 70–75% for patients with localized disease. In contrast, outcomes for patients with metastatic or relapsed disease remain poor, with overall 5-year survival estimates of 30–50% across unselected cohorts5.

Survivors frequently experience late toxic effects, including anthracycline-related cardiomyopathies, physical disabilities and infertility, as well as an increased risk of secondary malignancies6,7. As therapeutic advances have increased survival rates, survivorship care has become an increasingly important component of EWS management. In addition to mitigating treatment-related toxicities through preventive strategies8–10, there is a pressing need to identify robust biomarkers for patient stratification. Such biomarkers would guide treatment intensity, improve clinical trial design, enable early prediction of disease progression, and support the implementation of more personalized therapeutic strategies11,12. In EWS, the presence of metastases at diagnosis remains the strongest predictor of poor prognosis13. Other clinicopathological factors—including age, tumor site and volume, and histologic response to induction chemotherapy—also influence event-free and overall survival14,15. However, variability in surgical and radiotherapeutic practices across centers complicates the use of these parameters for robust risk stratification. Consequently, there is a growing need to integrate genetic and molecular biomarkers into risk-classification strategies for newly diagnosed EWS.

Genomic analyses of EWS tumor samples have shown that pathogenic alterations in TP53 or STAG2, as well as recurrent copy number alterations (CNAs) such as 1q gain, 16q loss, and chromosome 8 gains, are associated with adverse outcomes16–19. Additional candidate tissue biomarkers linked to prognosis include STEAP1, CXCR4, EZH2, galectin 3 binding protein, miRNA-34a, LGALS3BP, DKK2, and IGF2BP320–24. However, for most of these markers, prognostic value has not yet been validated in large, independent cohorts. Moreover, tissue biopsies do not allow longitudinal monitoring of treatment response or early detection of relapses and cannot be repeatedly obtained to capture the spatial and temporal evolution of the disease25. Liquid biopsy has, therefore, emerged as a minimally invasive alternative to surgical tissue sampling and is rapidly becoming part of standard cancer care for disease monitoring and therapy guidance. In EWS, several studies have demonstrated the clinical utility of circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA). Detection of circulating EWS::FLI1 ctDNA has been shown to predict prognosis and treatment response26. However, breakpoint-specific assays such as droplet digital PCR (ddPCR) require prior knowledge of fusion junctions and may suffer from limited sensitivity. More recently, whole-genome sequencing of ctDNA combined with computational approaches successfully distinguished EWS patients from healthy controls and patients with other sarcomas27, although such approaches remain costly and technically demanding for routine clinical use. In addition to genetic alterations, EWS is characterized by marked epigenetic heterogeneity, and several epigenetic or post-transcriptional regulators have been proposed as circulating biomarkers, including circulating miRNAs such as miRNA-34a, and extracellular vesicles (EVs) and their RNA or protein cargo28–31. Among potential biomarkers in EWS, the RNA-binding protein (RBP) insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding protein 3 (IGF2BP3) represents a particularly attractive candidate.

IGF2BP3 is highly expressed during fetal development, largely absent in adult tissues, and re-expressed in approximately 15–20% of human cancers32. It regulates post-transcriptional gene expression by recognizing N⁶-methyladenosine (m⁶A) modifications on mRNA and preventing the decay of m⁶A-modified transcripts33. In EWS, IGF2BP3 acts as a critical oncogenic co-driver, cooperating with EWS::FLI1 to sustain tumor growth and malignancy23,34–36. Elevated IGF2BP3 expression in tumor tissue is associated with poor survival, increased metastatic potential, and chemoresistance23,35,37. Notably, IGF2BP3 is also released extracellularly within EVs, where it can modulate the tumor microenvironment and promote cell migration36. While tissue IGF2BP3 has been consistently linked to adverse outcomes across multiple cancer types, its potential role as a circulating biomarker in EWS has not yet been explored. In this study, we analyzed circulating IGF2BP3 levels at diagnosis and after induction therapy in a cohort of EWS patients treated at the IRCCS Rizzoli Orthopedic Institute. We demonstrate that quantification of circulating IGF2BP3 enables early risk stratification and provides an initial assessment of treatment response in patients with EWS, particularly in localized disease.

Methods

Study population

Patients with a diagnosis of EWS and treated at the IRCCS Rizzoli Orthopedic Institute (Bologna, Italy) were included in the study. All patients were enrolled in clinical studies previously reported in detail4,38,39. Local treatment following induction chemotherapy consisted of radiotherapy, surgery, or surgery followed by radiotherapy. In surgically treated patients, histologic response to chemotherapy was assessed by the degree of necrosis (necrosis cut-off < 90%)40. For all patients, clinical data were collected, and tumor volume was assessed using magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Tumor tissue was identified as regions showing contrast enhancement relative to the surrounding normal tissues on gadolinium-enhanced sequences41. The maximum tumor diameter was measured along three orthogonal planes, and the tumor volume was calculated using formulas adapted to either an ellipsoidal or cylindrical tumor shape, applying a correction factor (F) of F = π/6 = 0.52 or F = π/4 = 0.785, respectively42.

A total of 60 EWS patients (39 with localized primary EWS; 21 with metastasis at diagnosis) were included in this study. Clinico-pathological features of the tumors are shown in Table 1. For 51 patients, blood was collected at diagnosis, prior to any treatments; for 25 patients, blood samples were available at diagnosis and after preoperative chemotherapy, before surgical intervention, allowing longitudinal analyses in the same patient. For 9 patients, blood was collected only after preoperative chemotherapy, before surgical intervention. Clinical and follow-up data were updated to April 2025. The median follow-up of the population was 34 months (2-123) for relapse-free survival (RFS) and 54 months (range 10–123 months) for disease-specific survival (DSS). The rate of 5-year RFS and DSS was 46.7% and 60%, respectively. Major metrics availability for each sample is summarized in Supplementary Fig. 1. The study was approved by the Comitato Etico di Area Vasta Emilia Centro Ethics Committee (Codice CE AVEC 505/2019/Sper/IOR).

Peripheral blood collection and plasma preparation

Peripheral blood samples from patients with EWS were obtained during routine clinical procedures, either at diagnostic biopsy or before surgery after completion of neoadjuvant chemotherapy. Blood was collected into K₂EDTA tubes and processed within 1 h. Plasma was separated by centrifugation at 2000 rpm for 10 min and stored at − 80 °C until use. Control human plasma (K₂EDTA) was obtained from healthy donors aged 18–40 years (NeoBiotech, catalog no. CUST-BB-29112023-4B). All healthy donors were confirmed to be free of any known malignancy at the time of donation.

ELISA assay

Circulating IGF2BP3 levels were quantified in 250 µL of plasma using a commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA; Novus Biologicals, catalog no. NBP2-82216) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (GloMax Multi Detection System, Promega). The assay demonstrated a sensitivity of 0.19 ng/mL and a measurable range of 0.31–20 ng/mL.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Serial 4-µm sections were prepared from formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor specimens and processed according to standardized immunohistochemical procedures. Sections were immunostained with an anti-IGF2BP3 antibody (clone EPR12021-114; #ab179807, rabbit monoclonal; Abcam). Single staining was performed using an avidin–biotin–HRP detection system (VECTASTAIN ABC Kit, PK-4001; Vector Laboratories). Slides were counterstained with hematoxylin and eosin for morphologic evaluation. IGF2BP3 protein levels were semiquantitatively evaluated using the H-score, calculated with the following formula: [0 x (% cells 0) + 1 × (% cells 1+) + 2 × (% cells 2+) + 3 × (% cells 3+)]16. Samples with H-scores of 0 were categorized as negative, whereas were considered positive when any tumor cell positivity was detected with scores > 0.

Statistical analysis

Comparisons between two groups were performed with the unpaired Student’s t-test or the nonparametric Mann-Whitney rank-sum test, while differences among group means were assessed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Correlations were analysed using Spearman rank or Pearson correlation tests, as appropriate. Fisher’s exact test or Chi square test were employed to assess association between categorized variables. Paired differences in patients before and after chemotherapy were assessed with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Coefficient of variation (CV) was calculated as the ratio between standard deviation and mean. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to evaluate the discriminatory potential of plasma IGF2BP3 and to establish cut-off value of circulating IGF2BP3. Survival curves were generated by the Kaplan–Meier method, and differences between groups were compared using the log-rank test. RFS was defined as the time from diagnosis to recurrence or metastasis at any site, and DSS as the time from diagnosis to cancer-related death. Patients alive or lost to follow-up were censored at the date of last contact. Multivariate analyses were conducted using stepwise Cox proportional hazards modelling, and hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software, version 22.0 (IBM Corp.), and GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Software).

Results

Circulating IGF2BP3 levels in EWS patients at diagnosis

Circulating IGF2BP3 levels were measured in plasma samples collected at diagnosis from 51 patients with EWS, including 33 patients with localized primary tumors and 18 patients with metastatic disease at diagnosis, and were compared with samples from 27 healthy donors (HDs). Detectable levels of circulating IGF2BP3 were observed in all EWS patients (Fig. 1A). Median plasma IGF2BP3 levels were significantly higher in EWS patients than in HDs (1096 pg/mL vs. 848 pg/mL, respectively; p = 0.011, Student’s t-test). Among EWS patients, no significant difference in circulating IGF2BP3 levels was detected between patients with localized or metastatic tumors at diagnosis (median plasma IGF2BP3 of 1105 pg/mL vs. 1086 pg/mL, respectively; Supplementary Fig. 2).

Circulating IGF2BP3 levels in EWS patients showed a positive correlation with IGF2BP3 expression in matched tumor tissues analyzed by IHC (n = 35; r = 0.57, p < 0.0001, Spearman correlation; Fig. 1B), supporting the notion that IGF2BP3 expressed in tumor tissue is released into the bloodstream, as previously described in experimental models36. Samples negative for expression of IGF2BP3 in tumor tissue (H-score = 0; in green) are also negative or weakly positive for circulating IGF2BP3, except for one case. The group of patients with H-score > 0 includes samples with both high tumor H-scores and high circulating IGF2BP3 levels, as well as samples with low tumor H-scores but high circulating IGF2BP3 levels, likely reflecting cases in which a minority of positive EWS cells produce very high amounts of IGF2BP3. This scenario is consistent with the heterogeneous nature of EWS43. Circulating IGF2BP3 levels were only weakly associated with initial tumor volume (r = 0.34, p = 0.022, Pearson correlation; n = 45; Fig. 1C), with some patients harboring small tumors (< 200 mL) nevertheless exhibiting high circulating IGF2BP3 levels (> 5000 pg/mL; Fig. 1C).

EWS patients displayed pronounced inter-individual heterogeneity in circulating IGF2BP3 compared with HDs (range: 263.5–11752 pg/mL vs. 0–2003 pg/mL, respectively), as reflected by higher coefficients of variation (124% vs. 51.4%, respectively). Notably, a substantial proportion of EWS patients exhibited circulating IGF2BP3 levels comparable to those observed in HDs. To identify clinically meaningful elevations, ROC curve analysis was performed, identifying 1326 pg/mL as the optimal cut-off value, with a specificity of 77.8% and a sensitivity of 43.1% for discriminating EWS patients from HDs (Fig. 1D). Using this cut-off, 22 of 51 patients (43.2%) were classified as high-expressors, while the remaining 29 patients (56.8%) were classified as low-expressors. High-expressors exhibited significantly higher circulating IGF2BP3 levels than both HDs and low-expressors (Fig. 1E).

To investigate the source of this heterogeneity, IGF2BP3 tissue levels analyzed by IHC (n = 35) were categorized based on H-score value as positive (H-score > 0) or negative (H-score = 0). IGF2BP3 positivity in tumor tissues (H-score > 0) was observed in 18 of 35 cases (51.4%), while 17 cases (48.6%) were negative (H-score = 0). A strong concordance was observed between circulating and tissue IGF2BP3 expression: 14 of 17 patients with high expression of circulating IGF2BP3 (high-expressors) were also positive for IGF2BP3 in tumor tissues by IHC, whereas 14 of 18 low-expressors were negative in tumor tissue (Fisher’s exact test, p < 0.001). Accordingly, high-expressors displayed significantly higher tumor IGF2BP3 H-scores than low-xpressors (p = 0.0006, Mann–Whitney test; Supplementary Fig. 3A). In contrast, initial tumor volume did not differ between high- and low-expressors (Supplementary Fig. 3B), further supporting the limited contribution of tumor size to circulating IGF2BP3 levels.

Association between circulating IGF2BP3 and risk stratification

The clinical relevance of circulating IGF2BP3 was assessed by evaluating associations with clinicopathological features and survival outcomes. No significant associations were observed between high circulating IGF2BP3 levels at diagnosis (≥ 1326 pg/mL) and gender, age, metastatic status at diagnosis, tumor localization, local treatment, initial tumor volume, or histological response (Supplementary Table 1).

A follow-up of 5 years was considered. The median follow-up was 40 months (range: 2–60 months) for RFS and 52 months (range: 10–60 months) for DSS. Patients with an expression of IGF2BP3 ≥ 1326 pg/mL (high-expressors) showed a directionally increased risk of adverse events compared with low-expressors (56.7% vs. 46.2%, respectively, Fisher’s exact test, p = 0.577), as well as a lower DSS (41.7% vs. 69.6%, respectively, Fisher’s exact test, p = 0.082). Kaplan–Meier analysis confirmed that elevated circulating IGF2BP3 levels were significantly associated with poor DSS (p = 0.048; Fig. 2A). However, multivariable analysis including all variables associated with survival in univariate analysis (Supplementary Table 2) identified metastatic disease at diagnosis as the only independent prognostic factor, consistent with its established role in EWS outcome13. To better define the prognostic role of circulating IGF2BP3, subsequent analyses were separately performed in patients with localized disease (n = 33) versus patients with metastatic disease (n = 18). In the subgroup of patients with localized disease, Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated that elevated circulating IGF2BP3 was the only variable significantly associated with DSS (Supplementary Table 3). High-expressors exhibited significantly worse DSS compared with low expressors (5-year DSS: 55% vs. 94.4%, respectively; p = 0.007; Fig. 2B). For RFS, a trend toward increased risk of relapse was observed in high -expressors (44.4% vs. 27.3% at 5 years), although this difference did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.187). Importantly, no association was observed between circulating IGF2BP3 levels and clinical characteristics (Supplementary Table 4) and circulating IGF2BP3 remained an independent prognostic factor for DSS in multivariable analysis (Supplementary Table 3), with high-expressors showing an increased risk of death (HR = 10.63; 95% CI: 1.27–88.62; p = 0.029). On the contrary, Kaplan–Meier analysis performed in the subgroup of patients with metastatic disease (n = 18) demonstrated that circulating IGF2BP3 levels were not significantly associated with patient survival (Supplementary Fig. 4, Supplementary Table 5).

Longitudinal assessment of circulating IGF2BP3 after induction chemotherapy

To evaluate the potential utility of IGF2BP3 as a dynamic biomarker of treatment response, circulating IGF2BP3 levels were measured after induction chemotherapy and compared with levels at diagnosis. Post-chemotherapy IGF2BP3 levels (n = 34) were significantly reduced compared with baseline values (n = 51): median: 909.6 pg/mL (range: 0–4776 pg/mL) vs. 1096 pg/mL (range: 263.5–11752 pg/mL), respectively; p = 0.003, Student t-test (Fig. 3A).

Longitudinal paired analyses of circulating IGF2BP3 were performed in the 25 patients (Supplementary Table 6) with available plasma samples both before and after chemotherapy (16 patients with localized disease, 9 with metastatic tumors). These analyses confirmed a significant overall reduction in circulating IGF2BP3 following treatment (Wilcoxon matched-pairs test, p = 0.01; Fig. 3B). However, responses were heterogeneous, and not all patients exhibited a decline in IGF2BP3 levels. Given that clinically relevant IGF2BP3 levels were defined as ≥ 1326 pg/mL, paired analyses were stratified by baseline expression status. Significant reductions after chemotherapy were observed exclusively in the high-expressor group (≥ 1326 pg/mL at diagnosis; n = 13), whereas no significant changes were detected among low-expressors (n = 12) (Fig. 3C). Accordingly, in high-expressors, the median reduction in circulating IGF2BP3 levels following chemotherapy was approximately 31% whereas in low-expressors the median reduction was approximately 1%. Based on these findings, patients were stratified into two groups: the first group includes patients with low circulating IGF2BP3 levels at diagnosis (< 1326 pg/mL) plus patients with high circulating IGF2BP3 at diagnosis (≥ 1326 pg/mL) who exhibited a reduction of > 31% in circulating IGF2BP3 following chemotherapy; the second group includes patients with high circulating IGF2BP3 levels at diagnosis (≥ 1326 pg/mL) and persistently elevated levels after chemotherapy.

Kaplan–Meier analysis indicated that patients with persistent IGF2BP3 plasma levels after chemotherapy displayed a significantly reduced RFS and DSS compared to patients exhibiting IGF2BP3 reduction and patients with low circulating IGF2BP3 levels at diagnosis (5-year RFS: 28.6% vs. 60.6%, respectively; p = 0.045 and 5-year DSS: 28.6% vs. 68.9%, respectively; p = 0.01; Fig. 4A). Multivariate analysis indicated that the dynamic changes of circulating IGF2BP3 after chemotherapy was an independent factor for DSS (HR = 6.36; 95% CI: 1.33–30; p = 0.02; Supplementary Table 6).

When the subgroup of cases with localized disease was considered (n = 16; Supplementary Table 7), patients showing persistent levels of IGF2BP3 after chemotherapy displayed a significantly reduced RFS and DSS compared to patients exhibiting IGF2BP3 reduction and patients with low circulating IGF2BP3 levels at diagnosis (5-year RFS: 16.7% vs. 100%, respectively; p = 0.001; 5-year DSS: 16.7% vs. 100%, respectively; p < 0.0001; Fig. 4B). Case report

As proof of principle, two representative cases are presented to illustrate the use of circulating IGF2BP3 for monitoring treatment response and predicting adverse outcomes.

Case 1

A 14-year-old boy was diagnosed with localized EWS of extremities. He received neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by surgical resection and adjuvant chemotherapy. After 120 months of follow-up, the patient remains alive with no evidence of disease.

At diagnosis, IGF2BP3 was positive in both tumor tissue and plasma (tumor H-score: 170; plasma level: 8153.97 pg/mL). Following neoadjuvant chemotherapy, circulating IGF2BP3 levels decreased by 62%, accompanied by a 50% reduction in tumor volume while histopathological evaluation revealed loss of IGF2BP3 expression in tumor tissue by immunohistochemistry and a poor chemotherapy-induced tumor necrosis (65%).

Case 2

A 22-year-old woman was diagnosed with localized EWS of the femur and received the same multimodal treatment regimen. Despite initial therapy, she developed bone metastases 9 months after diagnosis and died of disease progression 21 months after diagnosis.

At baseline, both tumor tissue and plasma were positive for IGF2BP3, although at lower levels compared with Case 1 (tumor H-score: 40; plasma level: 1493.39 pg/mL). Following chemotherapy, no robust reduction in circulating IGF2BP3 was observed, with a decrease of only 25%. This was paralleled by the absence of tumor volume reduction and the persistence of IGF2BP3 expression in tumor tissue (post-treatment H-score: 20). Histopathological analysis revealed a 45% chemotherapy-induced tumor necrosis.

Discussion

This study provides initial evidence supporting the measurement of circulating IGF2BP3 as a novel blood-based prognostic biomarker in patients with EWS, particularly in those with localized disease. Elevated circulating levels of IGF2BP3 at diagnosis identified a subgroup of patients with adverse outcomes. Furthermore, longitudinal assessment of circulating IGF2BP3 provided insight into differential responses to treatment.

Previous studies have demonstrated that IGF2BP3 is physiologically detectable in human plasma, consistent with the observation that a minority of healthy adult tissues—including the testes, lymph nodes, and tonsils—retain low levels of IGF2BP3 expression44. However, IGF2BP3 circulating levels increase in pathological conditions, and its quantification can serve as a predictor of cancer-specific survival in several tumors45–47. In particular, retrospective analyses of patients with prostate and renal cell carcinomas showed that IGF2BP3 plasma levels were significantly higher in patients with tumors compared with healthy controls, and even further elevated in those with metastatic disease46,47. Elevated circulating IGF2BP3 concentrations were also associated with poor prognosis and emerged as an independent risk factor relative to other clinicopathologic features46,47.

We previously demonstrated that elevated tissue expression of IGF2BP3 is associated with poor outcomes in patients with EWS23,35,37. In the present study, we extend these findings by showing that the circulating form of IGF2BP3 retains prognostic significance in patients with localized EWS. No prognostic value was observed when the expression of circulating IGF2BP3 was detected at diagnosis in the group of patients with metastasis at diagnosis. This might simply reflect the small cohort of metastatic patients (n = 18), but, more likely, it is due to the fact that the presence of metastases at diagnosis has a stronger prognostic relevance compared to the levels of circulating IGF2BP3. Thus, when measured at diagnosis, the prognostic value of circulating IGF2BP3 applies particularly to patients with localized tumors and showed strongest association with DSS, rather than RFS. However, circulating levels of IGF2BP3 have also been demonstrated to serve as a longitudinal biomarker both in patients with localized EWS and those with metastatic disease. In this study, we showed that persistence of elevated plasma IGF2BP3 levels after chemotherapy was associated with rapid disease progression, independently from the presence of metastasis at diagnosis. In contrast, patients whose IGF2BP3 levels decreased following chemotherapy showed significantly improved survival, indicating a higher likelihood of cure.

To date, only a limited number of circulating biomarkers have been evaluated longitudinally in EWS: persistent ctDNA following chemotherapy correlated with poor survival in patients enrolled in the EWING2008 trial26, and longitudinal ctDNA measurements reflected changes in disease burden in relapsed EWS48. ctDNA is highly sensitive for detecting minimal residual disease through tumor-specific genomic alteration and its analysis is the current predominant liquid biopsy strategy in EWS. The detection of patient-specific fusion sequences requires identification of individual EWS::ETS breakpoints using next-generation sequencing–based methods, followed by validation with PCR-based techniques26. While such approaches are routinely applied in therapy monitoring for other malignancies, such as lymphoblastic leukemia49, they are time- and cost-intensive and require prior knowledge of tumor-specific genomic alterations. Additional evidence supports the utility of longitudinal monitoring of circulating miRNA-34a, as a marker of therapeutic efficacy in EWS28,29. While ctDNA is highly sensitive for detecting minimal residual disease through tumor-specific genomic alterations, miRNA-34a reflects broader regulatory networks and further studies are needed to explain how its increased levels after chemotherapy favor a better outcome. Of note, IGF2BP3 is a target of miRNA-34a50, and the relationship between these two molecules should be explored in larger cohort of samples. In this context, circulating IGF2BP3 provides complementary information to ctDNA tests, as it reflects tumor protein expression and captures oncogenic activity at the post-transcriptional level, and it can be measured using a standard ELISA assay. Despite technical challenges, including limited sensitivity for low-abundance analytes, and a restricted concentration window which may lead to undetectable levels at low concentrations and saturation at high concentrations51, ELISA assay offers several advantages, including cost-effectiveness, minimal plasma requirements, and straightforward integration into existing laboratory workflows52,53.

One limitation of this study is its retrospective, mono-institutional design, and small sample size, which may limit the generalizability of the observed associations and their translation to real-world clinical practice54. Studying rare tumors significantly impacts the ability to collect large datasets. Small-sample studies may yield spurious results in terms of estimated means, medians, correlations, and survival analysis, particularly when subgroups of patients are considered55. However, the mono-institutional nature of this study also confers important strengths, including a high degree of homogeneity in clinico-pathological characteristics, such as chemotherapy regimens, surgical approaches, and local treatments, thereby reducing treatment-related variability. Prospective validation in large, clinically annotated cohorts will however be required to confirm the prognostic impact of circulating IGF2BP3.

A second limitation is the evaluation of a single circulating biomarker. Single-analyte approaches may not fully capture the biological heterogeneity of cancer. Nevertheless, this limitation must be considered in the context of the biological functions of IGF2BP3, which being an RBP affects the fate of hundreds of mRNAs33. IGF2BP3 is released within EVs36. Further studies incorporating the isolation of IGF2BP3-positive EVs and characterization of IGF2BP3 mRNA targets, such as IGF1R, CD164, and ABCF1, which we have previously shown to positively or negatively influence the prognostic value of IGF2BP3, may provide more precise information and improve the accuracy of risk-based patient stratification. However, this combinatorial approach may introduce greater variability and more complex data interpretation, potentially limiting its applicability in a rare tumor setting.

Conclusions

In summary, our data demonstrates that circulating IGF2BP3 represents a potentially valuable biomarker for early risk stratification and monitoring of treatment response in patients with EWS. Notably, IGF2BP3 was informative both at diagnosis and when dynamic changes before and after chemotherapy were considered, with this association being particularly significant in patients with localized disease. Consequently, integrating IGF2BP3 quantification with conventional imaging and histopathological assessments may enable more precise monitoring of disease progression and therapeutic efficacy. This minimally invasive approach has the potential to support earlier, personalized, and risk-adapted therapeutic interventions for patients with EWS but it needs to be validated in larger cohorts of patients.
