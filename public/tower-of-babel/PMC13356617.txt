Economic evaluation of added neoadjuvant cemiplimab to upfront wide local excision for advanced cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck
Authors: Christian L. Bailey-Burke, David M. Brizel, Peter A. Ubel, Shelby D. Reed, Meenal K. Kheterpal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma occurs with increasing incidence in the aging United States population and, depending on the tumor stage, can require multimodal treatment. Neoadjuvant cemiplimab has shown promise in improving pathological response and event-free survival, but its cost effectiveness compared with starting with upfront wide local excision has not been explored. This health economics evaluation aims to determine whether adding neoadjuvant cemiplimab may be a more economically valuable intervention in the management of head and neck locoregionally advanced cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma using a decision tree combined with a state-transition Markov model over a 10-year horizon. In the model’s base-case analysis, the expected long-term cost per patient was $19,021 lower with the addition of neoadjuvant immunotherapy than with upfront wide local excision alone and yielded 0.11 more quality-adjusted life years, with adjusted time horizon and health utilities leading to the greatest differences in mean costs and quality-adjusted life years, respectively. In this model, neoadjuvant immunotherapy followed by wide local excision was associated with lower expected costs and modestly higher quality-adjusted life years over a 10-year horizon, although the results were sensitive to modeling parameters and assumptions. Future research is needed to clarify long-term costs, outcomes, and corresponding health state utilities.

Introduction

Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cSCC) is the second most common form of skin cancer in the United States (U.S.) after basal cell carcinoma and continues to increase in frequency with the aging population (Jiang et al, 2024). The most common risk factors for cSCC include UV exposure, immunosuppression, having fairer skin, and increasing age (Thompson et al, 2016). The lifetime risk of cSCC in the U.S. is estimated to be 9–14% for men and 4–9% for women (Jiang et al, 2024). The median age at which cSCC is diagnosed is between 71 and 80 years, which implies that most of the cost of treatment will fall on the Medicare system rather than private insurers (Udkoff et al, 2022). In fact, the overall estimated annual costs for skin cancer treatment are $8.9 billion, comprising approximately 10% of treatment expenditures across all forms of cancer (Kao et al, 2023).

Most cases of cSCC are manageable with surgery (either Mohs micrographic surgery [MMS] or wide local excision [WLE]) (Udkoff et al, 2022). However, high-risk, locoregionally advanced tumors often require multimodal approaches, typically consisting of surgery, radiation therapy, and/or chemotherapy or immunotherapy, because of the higher risk of recurrence and progression (Alberti and Bossi, 2022; Brancaccio et al, 2021; Migden et al, 2018; Phillips et al, 2019). In cases of progression, especially for larger tumors, perineural invasion and regional lymph node involvement can occur, resulting in increased morbidity and mortality (Phillips et al, 2019).

One therapeutic approach that can potentially decrease this risk is neoadjuvant immunotherapy with cemiplimab (Ferrarotto et al, 2022; Gross et al, 2022). The use of neoadjuvant cemiplimab (a PD-1 inhibitor) followed by surgery demonstrates high rates of pathological response and event-free survival (EFS) in high-risk, resectable locoregionally advanced cSCC (NCT04154943) (Ferrarotto et al, 2022; Gross et al, 2023, 2022). Although previous cost-effectiveness analyses have evaluated cemiplimab against other immunotherapies and WLE against MMS, there is limited evidence on the economic value of initiating treatment with neoadjuvant cemiplimab followed by WLE versus upfront WLE for locoregionally advanced-stage tumors (Konidaris et al, 2021; Paul et al, 2021; Udkoff et al, 2022). This study aims to evaluate the economic utility of neoadjuvant immunotherapy versus upfront WLE for locally advanced cSCC from the perspective of the U.S. Medicare system to help inform clinical decisions and resource allocation. Because available trial data have a limited follow-up duration, decision-analytic modeling was employed to project long-term costs and health outcomes, an approach commonly used to evaluate oncology therapies with high upfront costs (McManus et al, 2019).

Sensitivity analyses

Differences in mean total costs and QALYs resulting from adjustments to key model parameters in a deterministic sensitivity analysis are presented in Table 1. The results demonstrate that changes to the time horizon generated a greater mean difference in costs, whereas adjustments to health state utilities had the most significant impact on the mean QALY difference. Specifically, the 2-year time horizon generated the largest impact from a cost perspective because neoadjuvant immunotherapy is more costly than upfront WLE by $19,844; the lower bound of the 95% confidence interest associated with the health state utilities (disease-free survival [DFS]/EFS, locoregional recurrence [LRR]) generated the largest difference in QALYs (of 0.20) (Table 1).

The probabilistic sensitivity analysis demonstrated mean incremental cost differences of −$19,770 (95% credible interval [CRI] = −$58,672 to $21,520) and mean incremental QALY differences of +0.11 (95% CRI = −0.10 to 0.33) for neoadjuvant cemiplimab compared with upfront WLE (Table 1). However, CRIs crossing zero for incremental QALYs reflect uncertainty in the modeled estimates. The cost-effectiveness plan shows dispersion of incremental costs and QALYs across 4 quadrants, reflecting uncertainty in both economic and effectiveness outcomes. Although simulations span each quadrant, majority lie within the southeast quadrant (lower cost, greater effectiveness), implying a dominated strategy (Figure 1). The cost-effectiveness acceptability curve summarizes this uncertainty by estimating the probability that each strategy achieves the highest net monetary benefit at varying willingness-to-pay (WTP) thresholds (Figure 2). Scenario analysis

A scenario analysis, in which costs, transition probabilities, and health utilities were adjusted simultaneously across 4 scenarios, revealed the following, as highlighted in Table 2: in scenario 1, when relevant costs increased by 25%, LRR and disease-specific death transition probabilities increased (upper bound of set range), and health utilities (DFS/EFS and LRR) increased; this resulted in a situation where the neoadjuvant cemiplimab strategy had higher incremental costs than the upfront WLE strategy by $14,537, despite the increased benefit of 0.23 in QALYs. In scenario 2, when keeping costs increased at 25%, decreasing the transition state probabilities and increasing the health utilities, the neoadjuvant cemiplimab arm demonstrated cost savings of $21,558 with a generated QALY difference of 0.04 compared with upfront WLE. For scenario 3, when costs were decreased by 25%, and the transition probabilities and health utilities were adjusted to the level of scenario 1, the neoadjuvant cemiplimab strategy cost $8,568 more than WLE, with a QALY benefit that is 0.23 more. Finally, in scenario 4, keeping costs decreased at 25% and decreasing the transition probabilities and health state utilities as in scenario 2 resulted in lower estimated costs of $12,906 in the neoadjuvant arm with a slight QALY increase of 0.04. Discussion

Patients with locoregionally advanced head and neck cSCC have limited treatment options. Upfront WLE, often with or without radiation therapy, is a first-line intervention, but it risks disfigurement or loss of function (Gross et al, 2023, 2022). Neoadjuvant immunotherapy, specifically cemiplimab, has emerged as an alternative initial treatment, given the achievable 51% pathological complete response in patients with resectable advanced tumors (Gross et al, 2023, 2022). However, this treatment strategy is costly, prompting an evaluation of its economic viability from the U.S. healthcare payer perspective, specifically Medicare.

Per the model in this study, neoadjuvant immunotherapy before WLE may lead to cost savings (mean = −$19,780; CRI = −$58,672 to $21,520) for U.S.-based patients with head and neck locoregionally advanced cSCC and could result in increased QALYs compared with upfront WLE alone in the long term. At a $100,000 per QALY WTP threshold, neoadjuvant cemiplimab demonstrated the highest net monetary benefit than upfront WLE. Improved pathological response and EFS in clinical trials may be contributing factors to the QALY outcome in the neoadjuvant immunotherapy arm. The small mean difference (mean = 0.11; CRI = −0.10 to 0.33) reflects conservative modeling assumptions. Both arms had different baseline utility values for disease-free states (0.78 for upfront WLE; 0.82 for neoadjuvant cemiplimab) and LRR (0.78), but immunotherapy patients incurred additional disutility penalties for treatment-related adverse events (AEs) (−0.0001) and adjuvant therapy (−0.07), which were not counterbalanced by QOL improvements. These benefits may be disproportionately meaningful for certain subgroups, including those with cosmetically or functionally sensitive head and neck tumors, those with higher surgical risk, and younger patients with longer life expectancy and greater preserved function and reduced recurrence risk. The QALY difference in older Medicare-aged patients may not translate to younger individuals, who are less affected by background mortality. Thus, the marginal QALY difference of 0.11 in this model is likely within the uncertainty, especially given the lack of consensus on utility weights for nonmelanoma skin cancers.

To our knowledge, this study reports a previously unreported economic evaluation comparing added neoadjuvant cemiplimab with WLE for head and neck locoregionally advanced cSCC. Under modeled assumptions, this intervention was associated with lower expected costs and marginally increased QALYs compared with upfront WLE. However, its marginal benefit should be weighed by a multidisciplinary team, including payers, oncologists, and dermatologists, considering the potential financial toxicity of most oncologic therapies to ensure equitable access (Hussaini et al, 2022; Rand and Kesselheim, 2021; Schaft et al, 2023; Supapannachart et al, 2023; Tran and Zafar, 2018). Given that most U.S. patients with advanced cSCC are on Medicare, the clinical benefits of novel treatments must justify their costs, especially because reimbursement policies change. Recent shifts in Medicare drug pricing negotiations, particularly regarding part B reform, may influence the adoption of this strategy (Wu et al, 2025).

This study has several limitations. First, long-term outcomes data from previous investigations using neoadjuvant cemiplimab for locally advanced tumors are limited, with most survival data accounting for only the first 2.5 years (Gross et al, 2023, 2022). Second, the outcome data for each strategy arm are from different studies that may not be comparable. Third, there is a lack of consensus and limited data regarding the health utilities for nonmelanoma skin cancers, limiting the ability to detect clinically meaningful advantages among subpopulations (Alam et al, 2024; So et al, 2021). Next, reliance on Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services data for cost estimates poses uncertainty, because reimbursement rates may fluctuate. In addition, focusing on 1 population (Medicare patients aged ≥73 years) may not capture the full spectrum of cSCC tumor accrual patterns observed in younger or immunosuppressed populations, where disease behavior and progression rates differ substantially (Granger et al, 2024). Finally, given that in the neoadjuvant cemiplimab study, patients could undergo WLE or MMS, there could be a different outcome if solely MMS is considered as the treatment arm. Although not modeled in this analysis, Mohs surgery demonstrates superior oncologic outcomes for primary cSCC, with local recurrence rates of 1.2–4.1% compared with approximately 5–10% for traditional excision (Wysong, 2023). In this context, the incremental value of neoadjuvant cemiplimab may differ substantially: although overall costs might be lower owing to reduced recurrence-related expenses, the QOL benefit from tumor downstaging and potential avoidance of morbid resections could become the dominant economic consideration. Future studies should examine the economics of Mohs surgery with neoadjuvant cemiplimab.

This study demonstrated that neoadjuvant immunotherapy yields lower long-term costs and improved QOL for patients with head and neck locoregionally advanced cSCC; however, results remain sensitive to assumptions and evolving clinical trial data. Further studies should build consensus around the values placed on QOL (health utilities), because a lack of consensus makes it difficult to generate the necessary incremental cost-effectiveness ratio to fully evaluate its economic viability. Nonetheless, healthcare payers, oncologists, otolaryngologists, and dermatologists should utilize these insights when determining treatment decisions and resource allocation.

Materials and Methods

This manuscript adhered to the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards guidelines (Husereau et al, 2022).

Study population

The hypothetical target study population is based on the primary group that benefited in the clinical study NCT04154943 (Ferrarotto et al, 2022; Gross et al, 2023, 2022): patients aged ≥73 years with high-risk, resectable stages III–IV (M0) or T2B-T3 cSCC of the head and neck, per the American Joint Committee on Cancer and Brigham and Women’s Hospital staging systems, with M0 indicating patients without metastasis (Hughley and Schmalbach, 2018; Patel et al, 2022; NCCN Squamous Cell Cancer, 2025; Ruiz et al, 2019).

Intervention and comparator

The intervention for this analysis is intravenous neoadjuvant cemiplimab (4 cycles, 350 mg), followed by curative-intent WLE with postoperative margins (Ferrarotto et al, 2022; Gross et al, 2023, 2022). Both WLE and MMS are guideline-recommended surgical options for locally advanced cSCC (NCCN Squamous Cell Cancer, 2025). However, this analysis focused on WLE as the comparator, given the availability of long-term transition data and costs specific to the population in this study.

Model overview

A decision tree was created in Microsoft Excel to represent possible treatment and early outcome pathways (Figure 3). Values for each path's probabilities were assigned as outlined in relevant studies and based on expert opinion (Table 3). A state-transition Markov model was also constructed in Microsoft Excel to assess the long-term costs and QALYs for each path in the decision tree (Figure 3). Consistent with other models used in cost-effectiveness analyses of treatments for skin cancers, the state-transition model included 3 health states: DFS after WLE or EFS (neoadjuvant immunotherapy), LRR, and death (Paul et al, 2021; Konidaris et al, 2021; Udkoff et al, 2022). The Markov model cycle length was quarterly for the first 2 years and annually for the remaining 8 years (for a total 10-year time horizon [ie, from age 73 years to age 83 years]) to best capture cSCC’s clinical course and management course (Briggs et al, 2006; Martin et al, 2024). Patients entered the model in the DFS/EFS state and either remained in this health state or transitioned to a different state within each cycle. The parameters and assumptions for this model are documented in Table 3. The outcomes of interest, including mean total discounted costs and QALYs, 95% CRIs, and the probability of cost-savings at various WTP thresholds, were calculated. Model assumptions

Per the neoadjuvant cemiplimab study protocol NCT04154943, patients receive 4 cycles of treatment followed by curative-intent surgery over an average period of 4 months, excluding diagnosis. Similarly, the rate of grade 3 AEs or more severe AEs is approximately 18% (Gross et al, 2022). AE costs are captured as an average weighted cost per most common severe AEs associated with cemiplimab (Zheng et al, 2021; Regeneron Pharmaceuticals, 2025). Given the timeframe and clinical practice assumptions, the average time from diagnosis to curative-intent tumor removal is assumed to be within 1 year on average for both treatment strategies. Moreover, it is assumed that a LRR can occur within the first year (year 0) after the initial treatment intervention. This model assumes that the health utility for EFS is greater than DFS because a less morbid surgical procedure is required owing to tumor shrinkage with neoadjuvant treatment (Meregaglia and Cairns, 2017). The patient demographic in this study likely has multiple comorbidities, but they are not individually accounted for in the model (Stenholm et al, 2015). It is also assumed that, because of the age demographic, Medicare payment rates are used to estimate costs in the model (U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, 2022).

Clinical data and transition probabilities

Quarterly and annual transition probabilities representing progression to LRR and from LRR to disease-specific death are estimated using study arm-specific Kaplan–Meier survival curves and modeling assumptions (Ferrarotto et al, 2022; Gidwani and Russell, 2020; Gross et al, 2022; Ruiz et al, 2022; Win et al, 2023). Where 5-year cumulative incidence estimates were reported, annual transition probabilities were derived using exponential transformation methods, assuming a constant hazard over the specified time (Briggs et al, 2006). To reflect the observed rate of cSCC recurrence, the recurrence risk was weighted more heavily within the first 2 years after treatment in the model, with quarterly cycles used during this period and annual cycles thereafter. To account for all-cause mortality, age-specific annual background death probabilities are taken from U.S. life actuarial tables (Social Security, 2020).

Cost inputs

In this model, direct medical costs represent payments from the perspective of U.S. fee-for-service Medicare (Table 3). Immunotherapy costs are based on U.S. Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Average Sales Price data (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, 2025). A disutility value is assigned to account for the impact of AEs (grade 3 or higher) on health-related QOL, consistent with previous cost-effectiveness analyses (Table 3). For the upfront WLE strategy, the associated cost was adopted from a previous evaluation study that examined the value of neoadjuvant atezolizumab in upfront WLE for advanced cSCC (Wu et al, 2025).

Adjuvant treatment costs included in this model are radiation therapy, which patients receive after initial WLE or an LRR, and immunotherapy (Table 3). For adjuvant radiation therapy, an institution-based estimate is used for a 5-fraction course, with patients receiving on average about a 30-fraction course of radiation (NCCN Squamous Cell Cancer, 2025). Regarding adjuvant immunotherapy, the average dosing and number of cycles that a patient receives are similar to those in study NCT04154943: 350 mg cemiplimab every 3 weeks for 48 weeks (16 cycles). The model does not account for AE costs from adjuvant radiation therapy, but immunotherapy-related AE costs are captured (Gross et al, 2023; Regeneron Pharmaceuticals, 2025; Tripathi et al, 2020; Zheng et al, 2021).

Other costs that this model includes relate to the long-term health state outcomes in the Markov model. Estimates for DFS/EFS costs depend on the recommended protocol for surveillance (NCCN Squamous Cell Cancer, 2025). For the LRR state, costs for subsequent treatment are a weighted cost (eg, probability of a patient receiving adjuvant immunotherapy vs adjuvant radiation therapy vs surveillance) that is derived according to the next clinical decision made in the neoadjuvant cemiplimab study (Gross et al, 2023). Finally, this model estimates death costs, specifically disease-specific death per oncology end-of-life care costs, with costs for the last month of life serving as the input (Hoverman et al, 2020).

Utility inputs

No consensus has been reached regarding utility values for nonmelanoma skin cancers (So et al, 2021; Alam et al, 2024). Moreover, the study NCT04154943 did not report QOL data. Therefore, utility weights for the 3 health states were assigned using values consistent with previous cost-effectiveness analyses involving cemiplimab and other studies analyzing the QOL impact of surgery (Gross et al, 2023; Konidaris et al, 2021; Udkoff et al, 2022). To account for uncertainty, beta distributions were applied to the health utilities in this model (bounded between 0 and 1). Disutility weights representing temporary decrements were applied to health state utilities to capture the impact of adjuvant treatments as well as severe AEs (grade 3 or higher), but they were not independently beta distributed. The AE disutility values were applied as described in Table 3.

Outcome measures and sensitivity analyses

Estimates for total costs and QALYs are calculated for all possible pathways, and weighted averages are calculated for both treatment strategies. A deterministic sensitivity analysis assesses the model’s robustness to individual changes in parameters and assumptions, including WLE and neoadjuvant (and thus adjuvant immunotherapy) costs (±25%), time horizon (2 years vs 5 years), utility weights (adjusted from past cost-effectiveness analysis), and transition state probabilities (Table 1). A probabilistic sensitivity analysis was also completed over 1000 iterations to capture the mean difference in total costs and total QALYs, 95% CRIs, and the highest net monetary benefit at various U.S. WTP thresholds (Table 1 and Figures 1 and 2). Finally, scenario analyses were conducted to better characterize the effects of changing key parameters simultaneously (Table 2).
