The category of Probabilistic metric spaces
E. Colebunders, R. Lowen

Abstract




Abstract

The paper is devoted to a categorical study of the category
\mathsf{ProbMet}
of probabilistic metric spaces.
The study is based on an isomorphic description of the category of probabilistic metric spaces.
The isomorphic description was obtained in
[
3
]
and is in terms of objects that are sets endowed with a collection of distances, where
the distances involved do not satisfy the triangle inequality but fulfil a mixed triangle condition instead. The morphisms are levelwise non-expansive maps.
We show that the category of probabilistic metric spaces is a monotopological category over
\mathsf{Set}
. We describe the regular closure on a space
X
in
\mathsf{ProbMet}
and prove that it coincides with the closure in the underlying strong topology. This enables us to characterize the class
\mathcal{E}
of all epimorphisms as the dense maps and the class
\mathcal{M}
of all regular monomorphisms as the closed embeddings in terms of the closure operator.
We prove that the category
\mathsf{Met^{\infty}}
of extended metric spaces with non-expansive maps is both coreflectively and reflectively embedded in
\mathsf{ProbMet}.

Keywords: Probabilistic metric space in terms of collections of distances; Mixed triangle inequality; Categorical properties of probabilistic metric spaces and non-expansive maps; Regular closure; Epimorphisms; Cowell-poweredness; Embedding of extended metric spaces.

Mathematics Subject Classification: 54E70; 54E35; 54A05.

1.
Introduction

The paper is devoted to a categorical study of the category
\mathsf{ProbMet}
of probabilistic metric spaces and its subcategory
\mathsf{Met^{\infty}}
of extended metric spaces.
The study is based on an isomorphic description of the category of probabilistic metric spaces in terms of objects that are sets endowed with a collection of distances labeled by the unit interval
]0,1]
and with morphisms that are levelwise non-expansive. The distances involved do not satisfy the triangle inequality but fulfil a mixed triangle condition instead. This isomorphic description was given in
[
3
]
, where it was derived from the study of probabilistic metrizability of uniform approach spaces.

We show that
\mathsf{ProbMet}
is a monotopological category over
\mathsf{Set}
. We describe the regular closure on a space
X
in
\mathsf{ProbMet}
and prove that it coincides with the closure in the strong topology underlying
X.
This enables us to characterize the class
\mathcal{E}
of all epimorphisms as the dense maps and the class
\mathcal{M}
of all regular monomorphisms as the closed embeddings in terms of the closure operator. It follows that
\mathsf{ProbMet}
is an
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M})
-category, with
\mathcal{E}
the dense maps and
\mathcal{M}
the closed embeddings that is cowell-powered. We prove that the category
\mathsf{Met^{\infty}}
of extended metric spaces with non-expansive maps is both coreflectively and reflectively embedded in
\mathsf{ProbMet}.

First we fix some notations.
A function
d:X\times X\rightarrow[0,\infty]
that is zero on the diagonal is called a
distance.

An
extended pseudo metric
on a set
X
is a map
d:X\times X\rightarrow[0,\infty]
which is zero on the diagonal, satisfies the triangle inequality and symmetry. If moreover
d
satisfies separation, meaning it is only zero on the diagonal, it is called an extended metric and the category of extended metric spaces with non-expansive maps is denoted
\mathsf{Met}^{\infty}
.

We recall some terminology from
[
12
]
and
[
7
]
.

A function
\varphi:[0,\infty]\rightarrow[0,1]
is called a
distance distribution
if
\varphi
is monotone,
\varphi(0)=0,\varphi(\infty)=1
and
\varphi
is left-continuous on
]0,\infty[
.

A binary operation
*
on the interval
[0,1]
is a
continuous t-norm
if
([0,1],*,1)
is a commutative monoid, which is continuous as a function on
[0,1]^{2}
to
[0,1]
with respect to the usual topologies and satisfies
p*q\leq p^{\prime}*q^{\prime}
whenever
p\leq p^{\prime}
and
q\leq q^{\prime}
in
[0,1].

A
probabilistic metric space
is a set
X
endowed with a map

(1)

\alpha:X\times X\times[0,\infty]\rightarrow[0,1]

and a continuous t-norm
*
such that

(P1)

\alpha(x,y,-):[0,\infty]\rightarrow[0,1]
is a distance distribution,

(P2)

\alpha(x,x,-)=\varepsilon_{0},
the largest distance distribution,

(P3)

\alpha(x,y,r)=\alpha(y,x,r),

(P4)

\alpha(x,y,-)=\varepsilon_{0}\Rightarrow x=y,

(P5)

\alpha(y,z,r)*\alpha(x,y,s)\leq\alpha(x,z,r+s),

for all
x,y,z\in X,r,s\in[0,\infty].

A map
f:(X,\alpha)\rightarrow(Y,\beta)
between probabilistic metric spaces is
non-expansive
if

(2)

\alpha(x,x^{\prime},t)\leq\beta(f(x),f(x^{\prime}),t),

for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X,t\in[0,\infty].

In this paper we will always assume that the probabilistic metric spaces are defined with respect to some continuous t-norm which we denote by
*
.

The category of probabilistic metric spaces with non-expansive maps is denoted by
\mathsf{ProbMet}.

3.
An isomorphic description of the category of Probabilistic Metric Spaces

Several attempts have been made to describe the category of probabilistic metric spaces with non-expansive maps isomorphically in terms of collections of extended metrics with levelwise non-expansive maps. However in order to obtain an isomorphism based on extended metrics, one had to make restrictions regarding the axiom (P5),
[
12
]
,
[
10
]
.

Here we stay with the axiom (P5) formulated for an arbitrary continuous t-norm as in (
1
) and we work with collections of distances where the triangle condition of the members of the collection is replaced by a mixed triangle condition of the
collection itself.
We start from the isomorphic description of
\mathsf{ProbMet}
as in
[
3
]
where the result was deduced from the characterization of probabilistic metrizability of (uniform) approach spaces. Since explicit proofs for collections of distances were omitted in
[
3
]
, for clarity we will include explicit proofs here.

3.1 Definition
.

Consider the category which we temporarily denote
\mathfrak{Z}
with objects,
(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}),
sets endowed with a collection of distances numbered by
0<\lambda\leq 1,
where
\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}

satisfies the following conditions:
(US)
,
(UD)
,
(UT)
for some continuous t-norm
*
and
(UH)
.

Symmetry: For every
0<\lambda\leq 1
and for
x,y\in X
, we have

d_{\lambda}(x,y)=d_{\lambda}(y,x).

Density: For every
0<\lambda\leq 1

d_{\lambda}=\myinf_{\rho<\lambda}d_{\rho}.

t-Norm: For
0<\varepsilon\leq 1,

0<\lambda\leq 1
and
0<\lambda^{\prime}\leq 1
with

(1-\lambda^{\prime})*(1-\lambda)>1-\varepsilon,

and for
x,y,z\in X
we have

d_{\varepsilon}(x,z)\leq d_{\lambda}(x,y)+d_{\lambda^{\prime}}(y,z).

Separation:

x\not=y\ \text{then}\ \exists\lambda,\ 0<\lambda\leq 1,\ d_{\lambda}(x,y)>0.

Using the terminology of
[
3
]
a morphism
f:(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})\rightarrow(Y,\{d^{\prime}_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})
is called
levelwise non-expansive
if

(3)

\forall\lambda,\ d^{\prime}_{\lambda}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq d_{\lambda}(x,x^{\prime}),\ \text{whenever}\ x,x^{\prime}\in X.

Let
(X,\alpha,*)
be a probabilistic metric space. As in
[
10
]

(4)

(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})

is associated with
(X,\alpha,*)
by

d_{\lambda}(x,y)=\myinf\{0\leq\gamma<\infty\ |\ \alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda\}.

3.2 Proposition
.

We have the following equivalence,
for
0\leq\gamma<\infty
and for
0<\lambda\leq 1

(5)

d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma\Leftrightarrow\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda.

Proof.

Suppose
d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma,
then there exists
\mu<\gamma
satisfying
\alpha(x,y,\mu)>1-\lambda.

This implies
\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda
since
\alpha
is nondecreasing.

To prove the other implication, assume
\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda.
Left continuity of
\alpha
implies that there exists
\mu<\gamma
with
\alpha(x,y,\mu)>1-\lambda.
This implies

d_{\lambda}(x,y)\leq\mu<\gamma.

∎

3.3 Proposition
.

The transition in (
4
) defines a concrete functor

\Delta:\mathsf{ProbMet}\rightarrow\mathfrak{Z}

which maps an object
(X,\alpha,*)
to
(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})
satisfying the conditions

(US)
,
(UD)
,
(UT)
and
(UH)
.

Proof.

(US)
Follows from (P3), since
\alpha(x,y,\gamma)=\alpha(y,x,\gamma)
for all
x,y,\gamma.

(UD)
Suppose
0<\lambda^{\prime}\leq\lambda\leq 1
and
x\in X.
First observe that in the infinite case, when
d_{\lambda}(x,y)=\infty
then
\{\gamma\ |\ \alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda\}=\emptyset
and then clearly also
\{\gamma\ |\ \alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda^{\prime}\}=\emptyset
and
d_{\lambda^{\prime}}(x,y)=\infty.

In the finite case it is sufficient to apply (
5
) in order to conclude that
d_{\lambda}\leq d_{\lambda^{\prime}}.

From the previous observation we already have
d_{\lambda}\leq\myinf_{\rho<\lambda}d_{\rho}.

We show the reverse inequality. Let
x,y\in X
. In the infinite case with
d_{\lambda}(x,y)=\infty,
there is nothing to show.
Next assume
d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma<\infty.
Applying (
5
) we have
\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda.
Then we can choose
\rho<\lambda
with

\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\rho.
Clearly
d_{\rho}(x,y)<\gamma
which implies
\myinf_{\rho<\lambda}d_{\rho}(x,y)<\gamma.

(UT)

Let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1,

0<\lambda\leq 1
and
0<\lambda^{\prime}\leq 1
satisfy

(1-\varepsilon)<(1-\lambda^{\prime})*(1-\lambda).

For
x,y,z\in X
arbitrary,
if the righthandside of the inequality
d_{\varepsilon}(x,z)\leq d_{\lambda}(x,y)+d_{\lambda^{\prime}}(y,z)
is infinite, there is nothing to prove. Next assume
d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma
and
d_{\lambda^{\prime}}(y,z)<\gamma^{\prime}
. Then we have
\alpha(y,z,\gamma^{\prime})>1-\lambda^{\prime}
and
\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda.

Now apply (P5)

\alpha(x,z,\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})\geq\alpha(y,z,\gamma^{\prime})*\alpha(x,y,\gamma)\geq(1-\lambda^{\prime})*(1-\lambda)>1-\varepsilon.

Finally we get
d_{\varepsilon}(x,z)<\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}.

(UH)

Suppose
x\not=y.
That there exists
0<\lambda\leq 1
with
d_{\lambda}(x,y)>0
follows from the fact that by (P4)
\exists\gamma>0
with
\alpha(x,y,\gamma)\not=1
and hence
\exists\lambda
with
\alpha(x,y,\gamma)\not>1-\lambda.

Next suppose
f:(X,\alpha)\rightarrow(Y,\alpha^{\prime})
is a non-expansive map.
First observe that in case
d^{\prime}_{\lambda}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))=\infty
then for every
\gamma<\infty,

\alpha(f(x),f(x^{\prime}),\gamma)\not>1-\lambda.

This implies that for every
\gamma<\infty,\ \alpha(x,x^{\prime},\gamma)\not>1-\lambda.
Hence we can conclude that
d_{\lambda}(x,x^{\prime})=\infty.

In the finite case, applying (
5
) we obtain the following equivalences:

\displaystyle f:(X,\alpha)\rightarrow(Y,\alpha^{\prime})\ \text{is non-expansive}\

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall t,\ \alpha(x,x^{\prime},t)\leq\alpha^{\prime}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}),t)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall t,\forall\lambda:\ \ \alpha(x,x^{\prime},t)>1-\lambda\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}),t)>1-\lambda

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall t,\forall\lambda:\ d_{\lambda}(x,x^{\prime})<t\Rightarrow d^{\prime}_{\lambda}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))<t

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall\lambda,\ d^{\prime}_{\lambda}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq d_{\lambda}(x,x^{\prime}).

It follows that
\Delta(f):(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})\rightarrow(Y,\{d^{\prime}_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})
is levelwise non-expansive.
∎

Next we investigate the other direction.
Let
(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})
be an object as defined in
3.1
. We associate a probabilistic metric space
(X,\beta,*)

with it as follows:

\beta(x,y,\infty)=1
and further for
\gamma<\infty
:

(6)

\beta(x,y,\gamma)=\sup\{1-\lambda\ |\ d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma\}=1-\myinf\{\lambda\ |\ d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma\}.

3.4 Proposition
.

For
\gamma<\infty
we have

(7)

\beta(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda\Leftrightarrow d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma.

Proof.

Let
\sup\{1-\rho\ |\ d_{\rho}(x,y)<\gamma\}>1-\lambda.
Then
\exists\rho<\lambda,\ d_{\rho}(x,y)<\gamma
and applying
(UD)
we have

d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma.

For the other implication, assume
d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma.
Then again applying
(UD)

\exists\rho<\lambda,\ \ d_{\rho}(x,y)<\gamma.

Hence
1-\lambda<1-\rho\leq\beta(x,y,\gamma).

∎

Before we show that the transition defines a concrete functor, we recall a lemma from

3.5 Lemma
.

Let
([0,1],*)
be a continuous t-norm. For all
a,b,d\in]0,1],
the following are equivalent:

(1)

d\geq a*b

(2)

\forall\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in]0,1],
if

then
d\geq(1-\lambda^{\prime})*(1-\lambda)

(3)

\forall\rho\in]0,1],
if

then

3.6 Proposition
.

The transition in (
6
) defines a concrete functor

\Phi:\mathfrak{Z}\rightarrow\mathsf{ProbMet}

which maps an object
(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})
to
(X,\beta,*)

Proof.

We first show that due to the properties of

listed above,

as in (

\beta(x,y,0)=0
as

and

\beta(x,y,-)

\beta(x,y,-)

\sup_{s<\gamma}\beta(x,y,s)\leq\beta(x,y,\gamma)
is clear from the previous property. For the reverse inequality assume
\beta(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda
. By (
7
) this implies that
d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma
and hence
\exists s<\gamma,\ \ d_{\lambda}(x,y)<s.
This implies that
\exists s<\gamma,\ \beta(x,y,s)>1-\lambda.
So we have that
\sup_{s<\gamma}\beta(x,y,s)>1-\lambda.

From the previous claims we can conclude that (P1) holds.

To see that (P2) holds, observe that for
\gamma>0
we have

\beta(x,x,\gamma)=\sup\{1-\lambda\ |\ \varphi_{\lambda,x}(x)<\gamma\}=1.

Clearly (P3) follows immediately from
(US)
.

Next we check (P4). Suppose
x\not=y,

(UH)

\exists\lambda,\ d_{\lambda}(x,y)\not=0.

Choosing
\gamma

from (

) we obtain
\beta(x,y,\gamma)\not=1.

Next we check that
\beta
satisfies (P5). Let

and assume

and
\beta(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda.

For every

satisfying

1-\varepsilon<(1-\lambda^{\prime})*(1-\lambda),

applying

,
we have

d_{\varepsilon}(x,z)\leq d_{\lambda}(x,y)+d_{\lambda^{\prime}}(y,z)<\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma.

Hence

\beta(x,z,\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})>1-\varepsilon.

This implies

\beta(x,z,\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})\geq(1-\lambda^{\prime})*(1-\lambda).

Applying

we can conclude that
\beta(x,z,\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})\geq\beta(y,z,\gamma^{\prime})*\beta(x,y,\gamma).

Let

First observe that in case
\gamma=\infty,

In the finite case, applying (

) we obtain the following equivalences:

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall\lambda,\ d^{\prime}_{\lambda}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq d_{\lambda}(x,x^{\prime})

.

The category
\mathsf{ProbMet}

.

\Phi\circ\Delta=\mathsf{id}_{\mathsf{ProbMet}}

The category

Proof.

(f_{i}:X\rightarrow(X_{i},(d^{i}_{\lambda})_{\lambda}))_{i\in I}
