Local and global well-posedness for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with nonhomogeneous boundary conditions Journal: J. Math. Pure Appl.
Engui Fan ∗,, Yuan Li, Xinhan Liu

Abstract
In this paper, we study the initial-boundary value problem for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation in ℝ + n \mathbb{R}^{n}_{+} i ​ ∂ t u + Δ ​ u + λ ​ | u | p ​ u = 0 , ( x , t ) ∈ ℝ + n × ℝ + , p ∈ ℝ + i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+\lambda|u|^{p}u=0,\qquad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\ \ p\in\mathbb{R}_{+} with nonhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. For the corresponding linear problem, endpoint Strichartz estimates are derived. For the nonlinear problem, we prove local well-posedness in H s ​ ( ℝ + n ) H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}) with s ∈ [ 0 , 5 2 ) s\in[0,\frac{5}{2}) and p < 4 n − 2 ​ s p<\frac{4}{n-2s} . Moreover, global well-posedness is established in the same regularity range. For s ∈ [ 1 , 5 2 ) s\in[1,\frac{5}{2}) , the one-dimensional global theory of [ 4 ] in H s ​ ( ℝ + ) H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}) is extended to H s ​ ( ℝ + n ) H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}) . Additionally, we obtain global solutions in the lower regularity setting s ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) s\in[0,1) for the first time. It is noteworthy that for s = 0 s=0 , we overcome the lack of mass conservation resulting from the nonzero boundary data and derive the pivotal L 2 ​ ( ℝ + n ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}) a priori estimates.

Introduction

We consider the nonhomogeneous initial-boundary value problem (IBVP) for the nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, formulated as:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+\lambda|u|^{p}u=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
&u(x^{\prime},0,t)=h_{0}(x^{\prime},t),\quad(x^{\prime},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
n\geq 2
and
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{n})=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, with parameters
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
p\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. The cases
\lambda<0
and
\lambda>0

correspond to defocusing and focusing situations, respectively.
Our main purpose is to establish local and global well-posedness for the IBVP (
1.1
) in the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

The NLS equation arises as a model for various physical phenomena, it has been widely used in fields such as condensed matter physics, water wave dynamics, nonlinear optics and plasma physics
[
23
,
36
,
11
,
12
,
44
,
45
]
. Specifically, this equation describes the Bose-Einstein condensate, a state of matter occurring near absolute zero where particles macroscopically manifest quantum mechanical behavior
[
23
,
36
]
. It also models the nonlinear waves on the surface of an infinitely deep fluid
[
44
]
and the collapse of Langmuir waves in a hot plasma
[
45
]
.

The IBVP for the NLS equation under different boundary conditions has been widely studied. The first class is homogeneous IBVP in the exterior of a compact set, an unbounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+\lambda|u|^{p}u=0,\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\Omega,\\
&u(x,t)|_{x\in\partial\Omega}=0,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

Brezis and Gallouet
[
7
]
first considered local and global existence of (
1.2
) in the Sobolev space
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
for
n=2
. In the context of
n>2
, higher regularity assumptions on the initial data were required
[
40
,
41
]
. Lower regularity results in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
L^{2}(\Omega)
were provided by Burq, Gérard, and Tzvetkov
[
8
]
. Additional related studies of (
1.2
) can be found in
[
3
,
27
,
32
]
.

The second class is nonhomogeneous IBVP in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
with
n\geq 1
. This includes the high-dimensional setting (
1.1
) addressed in this paper and the one-dimensional NLS equation posed on the half line:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u+\lambda|u|^{p}u=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&u(0,t)=h_{0}(t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

The foundational work was established by Holmer
[
26
]
, who proved the local well-posedness of (
1.3
) in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for
s\in[0,\frac{3}{2})\setminus\{\frac{1}{2}\}
, with
p
satisfying
\left\{\begin{aligned} &1\leq p<\tfrac{4}{1-2s},\ s\in[0,\tfrac{1}{2}),\\
&\quad p\geq 1,\quad\quad\;s\in(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{3}{2}).\end{aligned}\right.
Here, the boundary data
h_{0}(t)
was selected in
H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, this stems from the Kato smoothing estimate
[
30
]

\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\dot{H}_{t}^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}(\mathbb{R}))}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})}.

(1.4)

Bona, Sun, and Zhang
[
4
]
extended Holmer’s local well-posedness result
[
26
]
to
s\in[0,\frac{5}{2})\setminus\{\frac{1}{2}\}
. More significantly, they established global well-posedness of (
1.3
) in Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for
s\in[1,\frac{5}{2})
, including both defocusing case with
p>0
and focusing case with
0<p\leq 2
. In addition, Fokas, Himonas, and Mantzavinos
[
21
]
were the first to employ the unified transform method (Fokas method) to establish local well-posedness for the cubic NLS equation ((
1.3
) with
p=2
). Further analysis of this case was conducted by Erdogan and Tzirakis, who investigated several regularity properties and proved that in the defocusing setting, the
H^{s}
norm (for
s\in[1,\frac{5}{2})
) of global solutions grows at most polynomially in time
[
17
]
.

Compared to the one-dimensional case (
1.3
), the
n
-dimensional IBVP (
1.1
) is more challenging, in which the boundary data
h_{0}(x^{\prime},t)
depends not only on time variable
t
but also on the partial space variables
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})
. For
n=2
, Ran, Sun and Zhang
[
37
]
introduced an optimal boundary function space
\mathcal{H}^{s}
(given by (
1.5
) with
n=2
) and derived the global well-posedness of the IBVP (
1.1
) in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2})
for the defocusing case with
p\geq 1
and the focusing case with
1\leq p\leq\frac{4}{3}
. Subsequently, Audiard
[
2
]
generalized the space
\mathcal{H}^{s}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 2
, defined by

\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\widehat{f}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\left(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+|\xi_{n}|\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\left||\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+\xi_{n}\right|^{\frac{1}{4}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\in L_{\xi}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right\},

(1.5)

where
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\xi=(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
, and equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+|\xi_{n}|\right)^{s}\left||\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+\xi_{n}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(1.6)

Correspondingly, the trace estimate for the
n
-dimensional Schrödinger group reads

\|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(1.7)

The properties of the space
\mathcal{H}^{s}
are thoroughly studied in
[
2
]
, and based on this analysis, the author established Strichartz estimates without endpoint for the linear problem corresponding to (
1.1
). Moreover, the work in
[
2
]
obtained the existence of local solutions (for
0<p<\frac{4}{n-2}
) and global solutions for small data (for
\frac{4}{n}\leq p<\frac{4}{n-2}
) to the IBVP (
1.1
) in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
.

The aim of this paper is to establish the local and global well-posedness of the IBVP (
1.1
) for the
n
-dimensional NLS equation in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, with
s\in[0,\frac{5}{2})
. To our knowledge, the nonlinear well-posedness results for the one-dimensional IBVP (
1.3
) established in
[
26
,
4
]
have not been fully extended to the
n
-dimensional setting (
1.1
). Here, local well-posedness is established under the condition
p<\frac{4}{n-2s}
, which parallels the one-dimensional condition
p<\frac{4}{1-2s}
. Furthermore, we remove the small-norm assumption in
[
2
]
and prove global well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
for the range
s\in[1,\frac{5}{2})
, thereby generalizing the one-dimensional global result of
[
4
]
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Moreover, global solutions are constructed for the first time in the lower regularity range
s\in[0,1)
, based on the key derivation of the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
a priori estimates. Our results are formally stated in the next section.

1.1
Statement of results

In this paper, we consider initial data
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and boundary data
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, and focus on the distributional solutions with strong traces of the IBVP (
1.1
) in the sense defined below. The counterpart of this definition for the one-dimensional nonhomogeneous IBVP (
1.3
) was provided in
[
26
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

We say
u:\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\times[0,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
0<T\leq\infty
is a distributional solution of the IBVP (
1.1
) with strong traces if

(i)

u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times(0,T))
and
|u|^{p}u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times(0,T))
;

(ii)

u
satisfies equation (
1.1
) in the sense of distributions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times(0,T)
;

(iii)

space trace:
t\mapsto u(\cdot,t)\in C([0,T);H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}))
and
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}
;

(iv)

time trace:
x_{n}\mapsto u(\cdot,x_{n},\cdot)\in C\left(\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}};\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,T))\right)
and
u(\cdot,0,\cdot)=h_{0}
.

Moreover, we always assume that the initial data
u_{0}
and boundary data
h_{0}
satisfy the following compatibility conditions

u_{0}(x^{\prime},0)=h_{0}(x^{\prime},0),\quad\text{for}\ \ \tfrac{1}{2}<s<\tfrac{5}{2},

(1.8)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{|u_{0}(x^{\prime},t)-e^{-it^{2}\Delta^{\prime}}h_{0}(x^{\prime},t^{2})|^{2}}{t}dt\,dx^{\prime}<\infty,\quad\text{for}\ \ s=\tfrac{1}{2},

(1.9)

where (
1.9
) is given by
[
2
]
.

Definition 1.2

(See
[
29
]
)
.

We say that the exponent pair
(q,r)
is an admissible pair if
q,r\geq 2
, and

\frac{2}{q}=n\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}\right),

(1.10)

with the exception of the case
(q,r)=(2,\infty)
when
n=2
.
In particular, for
n>2
,
(q,r)=(2,\frac{2n}{n-2})
is called the endpoint.

We now state the main results of this paper. Theorem
1.3
provides the local well-posedness theory, and Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
establish the global well-posedness results.

Theorem 1.3

(Local well-posedness)
.

Let
s\in[0,\frac{5}{2})
,
p\geq\lceil s\rceil-1
and
(n-2s)\,p<4
. For initial data
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and boundary data
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
satisfying the compatibility condition, there exists a maximal solution

u\in C\left(\left[0,T_{\max}\right);H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\right)\cap C\left(\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}\mkern-4.0mu}\,;\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,T_{\max}))\right)

(1.11)

to the IBVP (
1.1
). Moreover, the solution
u
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

When
s<1
and
p\leq\frac{2+2s}{n-2s}
(with
p<\frac{2+2s}{n-2s}
when
n=2
) or
s\geq 1
, the solution is unique. In other cases, uniqueness requires an additional condition
u\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{q}\left(\left[0,T_{\max}\right);L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\right)
, for some admissible pair
(q,r)
.

(ii)

If
T_{\max}<\infty
, then
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow T_{\max}}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\infty
.

(iii)

u\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{q}\left(\left[0,T_{\max}\right);W^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\right)
for each admissible pair
(q,r)
.

(iv)

If
p\geq\lceil s\rceil
or
p\in 2\mathbb{Z}
, the solution map
\left(u_{0},h_{0}\right)\mapsto u
is continuous.

Theorem
1.3
concerns two distinct cases: low regularity solutions for
s\leq\frac{n}{2}
and high regularity solutions for
s>\frac{n}{2}
. Accordingly, the condition on the exponent
p
splits into two forms:

(n-2s)p<4\quad\text{implies}\quad\left\{\begin{aligned} &p<\tfrac{4}{n-2s},\quad s\leq\tfrac{n}{2},\\
&p<\infty,\quad s>\tfrac{n}{2},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.12)

where the inequality
p<\frac{4}{n-2s}
is
interpreted as
p<\infty
when
s=\frac{n}{2}
. The main challenge in proving Theorem
1.3
resides in obtaining the low-regularity solutions for
s\leq\frac{n}{2}
under the condition
p<\frac{4}{n-2s}
.

The upper bound
\frac{4}{n-2s}
arises from the scaling

u(x,t)\mapsto u_{\nu}(x,t):=\nu^{\frac{2}{p}}u(\nu x,\nu^{2}t),\quad\nu>0,

which leaves the equation invariant. This scaling yields

\|u_{\nu}(\cdot,0)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\nu^{\frac{2}{p}-\frac{n}{2}+s}\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where the case
\frac{2}{p}-\frac{n}{2}+s=0
is called critical, corresponding to
p=\frac{4}{n-2s}
, and the case
\frac{2}{p}-\frac{n}{2}+s>0
is called subcritical, that is,
p<\frac{4}{n-2s}

[
34
]
. The case
p<\frac{4}{n-2s}
in the initial value problem (IVP) for the
n
-dimensional NLS equation has been widely studied. For instance, Ginibre and Velo
[
22
]
established the existence and uniqueness in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p<\frac{4}{n-2}
, a result later extended by Tsutsumi
[
42
]
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p<\frac{4}{n}
. Cazenave and Weissler
[
9
]
generalized the local existence theory to
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
s\in\left[0,\frac{n}{2}\right)
with
p\leq\frac{4}{n-2s}
. In addition, the IVP has also been investigated extensively in the critical cases: the energy-critical problem (
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
p=\frac{4}{n-2}
)
[
6
,
13
,
38
,
39
,
43
]
and the mass-critical problem (
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
p=\frac{4}{n}
)
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

In Theorem

(i)

For

(ii)

For

(defined by (

Theorem 1.5

(Global well-posedness for
s\in[1,\frac{5}{2})
)
.

Let
s\in[1,\frac{5}{2})
and
p\geq\lceil s\rceil-1
. Given initial and boundary data:

•

For
s\in[1,2)
,
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
;

•

For
s\in[2,\frac{5}{2})
,
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
,

satisfying the compatibility condition, and assume the parameters satisfy

\lambda<0:\,\begin{cases}p<\frac{4}{n-2},&s=1\ \text{or}\ s\in[2,\frac{5}{2}),\\
p<\frac{8}{3(n-2)},&s\in(1,2),\end{cases}\;\quad\ \lambda>0:\ p\leq\tfrac{4}{n+1}.

(1.13)

Then the IBVP (
1.1
) admits a unique global solution
u\in C\left([0,\infty);H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\right)
.

Remark 1.6
.

For
s=1
, the proof of Theorem
1.5
under the condition
0<p<\frac{4}{n-2}
(
\lambda<0
) or
0<p\leq\frac{4}{n+1}
(
\lambda>0
) relies on the
H^{1}
a priori estimate (
4.3
), which requires solutions in
H^{2}
.
For
p\geq 1
, Theorem
1.3
establishes the existence of such solutions. However, in the low-power case where

, Theorem
1.3
fails to provide

solutions, as it requires the solution to satisfy the boundary data in the
\mathcal{H}^{2}
sense. To address this, we establish weaker solutions (in Proposition
3.9
) satisfying the boundary data in the
\mathcal{W}^{2}
sense (
2.90
). This is sufficient to obtain global

solutions through approximation.

In addition, for
s\in(1,\frac{5}{2})
, the analytical approach
[
4
]
used to establish global well-posedness for the one-dimensional IBVP (

) cannot be directly applied, as the key embedding

is not valid for

. Therefore, new analytical strategies are required. Specifically, for
s\in(1,\frac{3}{2}]
, we first derive a priori bound for
\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}
on a small interval

with
\varepsilon>0
, and then develop a decomposition-recursion technique (

)–(

) to extend the bound up to

. To handle the range

, the core idea of our argument is to first obtain a difference estimate (

) via Lemma

, and subsequently, through the

-mean continuity to convert it into a pointwise estimate of the time derivative (
4.30
).

Theorem 1.7

(Global well-posedness for
s\in[0,1)
)
.

Let
s\in[0,1)
. The following global well-posedness results hold:

(i)

For
n\geq 2
, given initial data
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and boundary data
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
satisfying the compatibility condition, and with the parameters satisfying

\displaystyle s=0:\ p\leq\tfrac{2}{n}\ \text{with}\ (p,n)\neq(1,2),

\displaystyle s\in(0,1):\ p\leq\min\{1,\tfrac{2(s+1)}{n}\}\ \text{with}\ \begin{cases}p<\frac{2}{n-2},&n<6,\\
p\leq\frac{6-4s}{3(n-2)},&n\geq 6,\end{cases}

then the IBVP (
1.1
) admits a unique global solution

u\in C\left([0,\infty);H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\right).

(ii)

For

, assume

. Then for any
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
h_{0}\in H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
satisfying the compatibility condition,
the IBVP (
1.3
) admits a unique global solution
u\in C\left([0,\infty);H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\right).

Remark 1.8
.

Unlike the initial value problem and the homogeneous IBVP, the nonhomogeneous IBVP (
1.1
) lacks mass conservation. The standard
L^{2}
a priori estimate (
4.7
) produces the trace of the normal derivative
\partial_{n}u|_{x_{n}=0}
. To eliminate this negative effect, we consider the solution
v
of a unforced linear IBVP (
4.48
) with the same initial and boundary data as the nonlinear IBVP (
1.1
). By establishing an
L^{2}
a priori estimate for the difference
u-v
, we prove, for the first time, the global well-posedness of the IBVP (
1.1
) in the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
. As a corollary, we also obtain the first global well-posedness result in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for the one-dimensional IBVP (
1.3
).

Our results for the nonlinear IBVP (
1.1
) rely on the Strichartz estimates for the corresponding linear problem (
2.1
), which serve as the foundation for the further nonlinear estimates of (
1.1
). Notably, in contrast to the linear results provided in
[
2
]
, we establish the endpoint Strichartz estimates for the first time; this is guaranteed by Propositions
2.3
and
2.11
. The core idea behind these propositions is to construct multiplier-type Fourier integral operators that meet the conditions of the Keel-Tao theorem
[
29
]
. These operators are designed to reformulate the components of the solution formulae that cannot be represented by the Schrödinger group, thereby enabling the application of the Keel-Tao theorem
[
29
]
to complete the proofs.

An outline of this paper is as follows. Section
2
is devoted to deriving Strichartz estimates including the endpoint for the linear IBVP (
2.1
). This linear problem is decomposed into three components, each of which is estimated separately. Section
3
develops the local theory for the nonlinear IBVP (
1.1
) and proves Theorem
1.3
through a fixed-point argument in several settings. In Section
4
, we establish
H^{1}

and
L^{2}
a priori estimates for the nonlinear IBVP (
1.1
) and perform associated regularity analyses, which subsequently yields the global results stated in Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
.

1.2
Notation

–

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1<p<\infty
,
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the standard Sobolev space, and
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For an open set
\Omega
,
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is defined as the restriction of
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
\Omega
.

–

For
s\in(0,1)
,
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, a Banach space
X
, and an open interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, the vector-valued Besov space
B^{s}_{p,q}(I;X)
is defined by the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}(I;X)}

\displaystyle:=\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(I;X)}+\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(I;X)}

(1.14)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{I}\|f(t)\|_{X}^{p}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|f(t+h)-f(t)\|_{L^{p}(I_{h};X)}^{q}\frac{dh}{|h|^{1+sq}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
I_{h}=\{t:t+h\in I\}
, with the standard modifications when
p=\infty
or
q=\infty
.

–

\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(a,b))
is defined as the restriction space of
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to the domain
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(a,b)
, with norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(a,b))}:=\inf\left\{\|F\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:F\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),F|_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(a,b)}=f\right\}.

(1.15)

–

For function spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,T)
, the subscript “
\mathrm{loc}
” refers only to the time variable:
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,T))
if and only if
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,T^{\prime}))
for any
T^{\prime}\in(0,T)
. The same convention also applies to
H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{s}
.

–

For
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we set
\langle\xi\rangle:=\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}
.

–

We write
A\lesssim B
if
A\leq CB
for some constant
C>0
, and
A\approx B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

–

Throughout,
(q,r)
denotes an admissible pair as in Definition
1.2
, with
q^{\prime}
and
r^{\prime}
being their respective conjugate exponents. The pair
(\gamma,\rho)
denotes another admissible pair, with conjugates
\gamma^{\prime}
and
\rho^{\prime}
.

2
Linear estimates

In this section, we focus on the following linear IBVP

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=f,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
&u(x^{\prime},0,t)=h_{0}(x^{\prime},t),\quad(x^{\prime},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

Our primary objective is to establish Strichartz-type estimates for solution
u
, as these estimates serve as fundamental tools in establishing both local and global well-posedness for the nonlinear problem. By the linear superposition principle, we decompose the linear IBVP (
2.1
) into three components: the unforced initial value problem (
2.2
), the forced initial value problem (
2.9
), and the reduced initial-boundary value problem (
2.62
).

2.1
Unforced initial value problem

First, we consider the IVP

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}v+\Delta v=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},\\
&v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.2)

The solution to (
2.2
) can be represented as
v=e^{it\Delta}v_{0}
, where the corresponding estimates are established in the following proposition. In particular, the
(x^{\prime},t)
-estimate (
2.4
) of
v
provides a feasible function space
\mathcal{H}^{s}
for boundary data.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and
0<l<1
, the solution
v
to initial value problem (
2.2
) satisfies the following estimates:

\|v\|_{C_{b}^{j}(\mathbb{R};H^{s-2j}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\|v\|_{W^{j,q}(\mathbb{R};W^{s-2j,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\|v\|_{B^{l}_{q,2}(\mathbb{R};W^{s-2l,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(2.3)

Moreover, for
s\geq 0
, we have

\|v\|_{C_{b}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(2.4)

and

\|v\|_{C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(2.5)

Proof.

The estimates for
\|v\|_{C_{b}(\mathbb{R};H^{s})}
and
\|v\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R};W^{s,r})}
can be found in Chapter 2 of
[
10
]
. From
v_{t}=i\Delta v
, we derive the bounds for
\|v\|_{C_{b}^{j}(\mathbb{R};H^{s-2j})}
and
\|v\|_{W^{j,q}(\mathbb{R};W^{s-2j,r})}
. The third term in (
2.3
) is then obtained by interpolation. For the estimate (
2.4
), note that the solution
v
to (
2.2
) can be expressed as

v(x,t)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{-i|\xi|^{2}t}\widehat{v}_{0}({\xi})d\xi.

(2.6)

Applying the variable substitution to (
2.6
): For
\xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, set
\xi_{n}=\sqrt{|\eta+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}|}
; For
\xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{-}
, set
\xi_{n}=-\sqrt{|\eta+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}|}
, where
\eta\in(-\infty,-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2})
. This yields
v=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(v_{+}+v_{-}\right)
with

v_{\pm}(x,t)=\pm\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{-\infty}^{-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}e^{ix^{\prime}\cdot\xi^{\prime}\pm ix_{n}\sqrt{\left|\eta+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}\right|}}\,e^{i\eta t}\,\tfrac{\widehat{v}_{0}\left(\xi^{\prime},\pm\sqrt{\left|\eta+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}\right|}\right)}{2\sqrt{\left|\eta+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}\right|}}d\eta\,d\xi^{\prime}.

(2.7)

We only estimate
v_{+}
since
v_{-}
can be treated analogously. By the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{s}
and the substitution
\eta=-\xi_{n}^{2}-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}
with
\xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|v_{+}(\cdot,x_{n},\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{-\infty}^{-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}\left(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+|\eta|\right)^{s}\tfrac{\left|\widehat{v}_{0}\left(\xi^{\prime},\sqrt{\left|\eta+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}\right|}\right)\right|^{2}}{\sqrt{\left|\eta+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}\right|}}d\eta\,d\xi^{\prime}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+\xi_{n}^{2}\right)^{s}|\widehat{v}_{0}(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n})|^{2}d\xi_{n}\,d\xi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.

The continuity follows from the dominated convergence theorem, and hence (
2.4
) holds. For (
2.5
), differentiating (
2.7
) with respect to
x_{n}
yields

\partial_{n}v_{+}(x,t)=\frac{i}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{-\infty}^{-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}e^{ix^{\prime}\cdot\xi^{\prime}+ix_{n}\sqrt{|\eta+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}|}}\,e^{i\eta t}\,\widehat{v}_{0}(\xi^{\prime},\sqrt{\left|\eta+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}\right|})d\eta\,d\xi^{\prime}.

Similarly, by the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{s}
and substitution, we have

\left\|\partial_{n}v_{+}(\cdot,x_{n},\cdot)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left(1+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}+\xi_{n}^{2}\right)^{s-1}\xi_{n}^{2}\left|\widehat{v}_{0}(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n})\right|^{2}d\xi_{n}\,d\xi^{\prime}\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.

∎

2.2
Forced initial value problem

Next, we study the forced IVP

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}w+\Delta w=f,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},\\
&w(x,0)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.9)

By Duhamel’s principle, the solution admits the representation

w(t)=-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}f(\tau)d\tau.

(2.10)

We now give the Strichartz estimates for
w
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the
w
given by (
2.10
) satisfies
the following estimates

\|w\|_{C_{b}(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\|w\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R};W^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};W^{s,\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))},

(2.11)

\|w\|_{C^{1}([0,T];H^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\|w\|_{W^{1,q}(0,T;W^{s-2,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|f(0)\|_{H^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|f\|_{W^{1,\gamma^{\prime}}(0,T;W^{s-2,\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

(2.12)

For
s\in(0,2)
,

\|w\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R};W^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\|w\|_{B^{\frac{s}{2}}_{q,2}(\mathbb{R};L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};W^{s,\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\|f\|_{B^{\frac{s}{2}}_{\gamma^{\prime},2}(\mathbb{R};L^{\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

(2.13)

Proof.

The estimate (
2.11
) can be found in Chapter 2 of
[
10
]
. For (
2.12
), starting from

\partial_{t}w(t)=-ie^{it\Delta}f(0)-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}\partial_{t}f(\tau)d\tau,

we apply (
2.3
) and (
2.11
) to get

\|\partial_{t}w\|_{C([0,T];H^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{q}(0,T;W^{s-2,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|f(0)\|_{H^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\partial_{t}f\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(0,T;W^{s-2,\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

(2.14)

Thus (
2.12
) follows by combining (
2.11
) with (
2.14
). The estimate (
2.13
) is presented in Section 3 of
[
2
]
.
∎

The remainder of this section is devoted to the
\mathcal{H}^{s}
estimates of
w
.

Proposition 2.3
.

The
w
given by (
2.10
) satisfies

\|w\|_{C_{b}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

(2.15)

We emphasize that the right-hand side of the inequality holds for arbitrary admissible pair
(\gamma,\rho)
. Significantly, the framework successfully extends to the endpoint case
(\gamma,\rho)=\left(2,\frac{2n}{n-2}\right)
,
n>2
, which is established for the first time. The proof of this proposition makes use of the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4

(See
[
29
]
)
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space. Suppose that for all
g\in H
,
h\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the operator
U(s):H\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies the following estimates:

\|U(s)g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|g\right\|_{H}\quad\text{and}\quad\|U(s)U(\tau)^{*}h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim|s-\tau|^{-\frac{n}{2}}\|h\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(2.16)

where
s\neq\tau
, and
U(s)^{*}
denotes the adjoint operator of
U(s)
. Then the estimates

\|U(s)g\|_{L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R};L^{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\left\|g\right\|_{H},

(2.17)

\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}U(\tau)^{*}f(\tau)d\tau\right\|_{H}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

(2.18)

hold for all admissible pairs
(\gamma,\rho)
, and all
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

Following the methodology in
[
31
,
26
]
, the solution formula can be reformulated as

\displaystyle w(x,t)=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\text{sgn}(\tau)}{2}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}f(\tau)d\tau

(2.19)

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mu+|k_{n}|^{2}\geq\varepsilon}e^{ik\cdot x}\frac{e^{it\mu}}{\mu+|k|^{2}}\widehat{f}(k,\mu)dk_{n}\right]\,d\mu\,dk^{\prime}\equiv I_{1}+I_{2}.

From estimate (
2.4
) and the Strichartz estimates listed in Chapter 2 of
[
10
]
, we obtain

\|I_{1}\|_{C_{b}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\text{sgn}(\tau)e^{-i\tau\Delta}f(\tau)d\tau\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

(2.20)

By applying properties of the Fourier transform, we rewrite
I_{2}
in convolution form:

I_{2}(x,t)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}e^{it\mu}e^{ik^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime}}\left[\widehat{f}^{\,y^{\prime}}(k^{\prime},\cdot,\tau)\ast M(k^{\prime},\cdot,\tau,\mu)\right](x_{n})\,dk^{\prime}\,d\mu\,d\tau,

(2.21)

where the integral kernel

M(k^{\prime},\delta,\tau,\mu)=\frac{e^{-i\mu\tau}}{2\sqrt{\left|\mu+|k^{\prime}|^{2}\right|}}\left(-\raisebox{1.93748pt}{$\chi$}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(\mu+|k^{\prime}|^{2})e^{-|\delta|\sqrt{\left|\mu+|k^{\prime}|^{2}\right|}}+\raisebox{1.93748pt}{$\chi$}_{\mathbb{R}_{-}}(\mu+|k^{\prime}|^{2})\sin(|\delta|\sqrt{|\mu+|k^{\prime}|^{2}|})\right).

Here, we have used
\left(\tfrac{1}{(\cdot)^{2}+\zeta}\right)^{\vee}=\tfrac{e^{-|\cdot|\sqrt{|\zeta|}}}{2\sqrt{|\zeta|}}
when
\zeta>0
, and
\left(\text{P.V.}\tfrac{1}{(\cdot)^{2}+\zeta}\right)^{\vee}=\tfrac{\sin\left(|\cdot|\sqrt{|\zeta|}\right)}{2\sqrt{|\zeta|}}
when
\zeta<0
. The former is a classical conclusion regarding the Poisson kernel, and the latter follows from the decomposition:

\displaystyle\left(\text{P.V.}\tfrac{1}{(\cdot)^{2}+\zeta}\right)^{\vee}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt{|\zeta|}}\left(\text{P.V.}\tfrac{1}{(\cdot)-\sqrt{|\zeta|}}-\text{P.V.}\tfrac{1}{(\cdot)+\sqrt{|\zeta|}}\right)^{\vee}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{i\text{ sgn}\left(\cdot\right)}{4\sqrt{|\zeta|}}\left(e^{i\sqrt{|\zeta|}(\cdot)}-e^{-i\sqrt{|\zeta|}(\cdot)}\right)=\tfrac{\sin\left(|\cdot|\sqrt{|\zeta|}\right)}{2\sqrt{|\zeta|}}.

For all
x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we define

\left[U_{x_{n}}(s)g\right](z):=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{g}(k^{\prime},\mu)\overline{M(k^{\prime},x_{n}-z_{n},s,\mu)}\sqrt{\left|\mu+|k^{\prime}|^{2}\right|}\,d\mu\right)^{\vee_{k^{\prime}}}(z^{\prime}),

(2.22)

where
z=(z^{\prime},z_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
. By Plancherel’s identity combined with the variable substitution
\mu=\eta|\eta|-|k^{\prime}|^{2}
, we derive

\displaystyle\left\|U_{x_{n}}(s)g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\left\|\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{g}(k^{\prime},\mu)\overline{M(k^{\prime},x_{n}-z_{n},s,\mu)}\sqrt{\left|\mu+|k^{\prime}|^{2}\right|}\,d\mu\right\|_{L^{2}_{z_{n}}}\right\|_{L_{k^{\prime}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\left\|\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{g}(k^{\prime},\eta|\eta|-|k^{\prime}|^{2})e^{i\left(\eta|\eta|-|k^{\prime}|^{2}\right)s}\left(-\raisebox{1.72221pt}{$\chi$}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(\eta)e^{-|x_{n}-z_{n}||\eta|}+\raisebox{1.72221pt}{$\chi$}_{\mathbb{R}_{-}}(\eta)\sin\left(\left|x_{n}-z_{n}\right|\left|\eta\right|\right)\right)|\eta|\,d\eta\right\|_{L^{2}_{z_{n}}}\right\|_{L_{k^{\prime}}^{2}}.

Applying the
L^{2}
-boundedness of the Laplace transform (see
[
5
]
) yields

\|U_{x_{n}}(s)g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|\widehat{g}\left(k^{\prime},\eta|\eta|-|k^{\prime}|^{2}\right)\left|\eta\right|\right\|_{L^{2}_{k^{\prime},\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(2.23)

Then, we can define a bounded operator
U_{x_{n}}(s):\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, as
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Furthermore, the adjoint operator
U_{x_{n}}(s)^{*}
can be expressed explicitly as

U_{x_{n}}(s)^{*}h=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{h}^{\,y^{\prime}}(k^{\prime},y_{n})M(k^{\prime},x_{n}-y_{n},s,\mu)\,dy_{n}\right)^{\vee_{(k^{\prime},\mu)}},

(2.24)

where
h\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We can then reformulate
I_{2}
in (
2.21
) as

I_{2}(x,t)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[U_{x_{n}}(\tau)^{*}f(\tau)\right](x^{\prime},t)d\tau.

(2.25)

From (
2.22
), (
2.24
) and the variable substitution
\mu=\eta|\eta|-|k^{\prime}|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left[U_{x_{n}}(s)U_{x_{n}}(\tau)^{*}h\right](z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{h}^{y^{\prime}}(k^{\prime},y_{n})M(k^{\prime},x_{n}-y_{n},\tau,\mu)\,\overline{M(k^{\prime},x_{n}-z_{n},s,\mu)}\sqrt{\left|\mu+|k^{\prime}|^{2}\right|}\,dy_{n}\,d\mu\right)^{\vee_{k^{\prime}}}(z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}h(y)e^{i(\eta|\eta|-|k^{\prime}|^{2})(s-\tau)}e^{ik^{\prime}\cdot(z^{\prime}-y^{\prime})}\Big(-\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\chi$}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(\eta)e^{-|\eta|\left(|x_{n}-y_{n}|+|x_{n}-z_{n}|\right)}

\displaystyle+\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\chi$}_{\mathbb{R}_{-}}(\eta)\sin(|x_{n}-y_{n}||\eta|)\sin\left(|x_{n}-z_{n}||\eta|\right)\Big)dy^{\prime}\,dy_{n}\,d\eta\,dk^{\prime}.

Applying Fubini’s theorem and van der Corput’s lemma
[
35
]
gives the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|[U_{x_{n}}(s)U_{x_{n}}(\tau)^{*}h]\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\varlimsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|h(y)|\Big|\int_{[-m,m]^{n-1}}e^{-i|k^{\prime}|^{2}(s-\tau)}e^{ik^{\prime}\cdot(z^{\prime}-y^{\prime})}dk^{\prime}\Big|

(2.26)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(\,\Big|\int_{0}^{m}e^{i\eta^{2}(s-\tau)}e^{-\eta\left(|x_{n}-y_{n}|+|x_{n}-z_{n}|\right)}d\eta\Big|+\Big|\int_{-m}^{0}e^{-i\eta^{2}(s-\tau)}\sin(|x_{n}-y_{n}|\eta)\sin(|x_{n}-z_{n}|\eta)d\eta\Big|\,\right)dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|s-\tau|^{\frac{n}{2}}}\|h\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Combining the estimates (
2.23
), (
2.26
) with a density argument, we verify that the hypotheses of Lemma
2.4
are fulfilled. Consequently, for any
x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
, we derive

\|I_{2}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

(2.27)

∎

Building on Proposition
2.3
, we establish the following result for
s\in(0,2)
, which is also valid for arbitrary admissible pairs
(\gamma,\rho)
. The norm equivalence

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\partial_{t}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\Delta^{\prime}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad s\in\mathbb{R}

(2.28)

is needed, which follows immediately from the standard properties of the Fourier transform.

Proposition 2.5
.

For
s\in(0,2)
, the
w
given by (
2.10
) satisfies

\|w\|_{C_{b}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};W^{s,\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\|f\|_{B^{\frac{s}{2}}_{\gamma^{\prime},2}(\mathbb{R};L^{\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

(2.29)

Proof.

We first prove the following inequality

\|w\|_{C_{b}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};W^{2,\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\|f\|_{W^{1,\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

(2.30)

By a density argument, it suffices to consider
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
. Clearly, there exists
a>0
such that
f(\cdot,t)=0
for
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus(-a,a)
. We decompose (
2.10
) as

\displaystyle w(t)

\displaystyle=i\int_{-a}^{0}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}f(\tau)d\tau-i\int_{-a}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}f(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle\equiv w_{1}(t)-w_{2}(t).

From (
2.4
) and (
2.18
), we derive

\|w_{1}\|_{C_{b}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\left\|\int_{-a}^{0}e^{-i\tau\Delta}f(\tau)d\tau\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};W^{2,\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))},

where we have used the fact that the adjoint operator of
e^{i\tau\Delta}
is
e^{-i\tau\Delta}
. Since
f(-a)=0
, we have
\partial_{t}w_{2}(t)=i\int_{-a}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}\partial_{t}f(\tau)d\tau
. By (
2.15
), it follows that

\|w_{2}\|_{C_{b}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+\|\partial_{t}w_{2}\|_{C_{b}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{1,\gamma^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};L^{\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))},

and similarly,

Proposition 2.7

For

Proof.

We define

Note that for any

is a zero extension of

to the domain

a similar result holds for

\|G(\cdot,x_{n},\cdot,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|\left[e^{i(\cdot)\Delta}f(\tau)\right](\cdot,x_{n})\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(2.34)

By using (
2.33

2.34

2.4

\displaystyle\|w(\cdot,x_{n},\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|\left[e^{i(\cdot)\Delta}f(\tau)\right](\cdot,x_{n})\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}d\tau\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

(2.35)

Proof of Proposition
2.9

2.3
Reduced initial-boundary value problem

Finally, we study the reduced IBVP

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}\phi+\Delta\phi=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&\phi(x,0)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
&\phi(x^{\prime},0,t)=g_{0}(x^{\prime},t),\quad(x^{\prime},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.62)

In the following, we employ the Fokas method to derive the solution formula for (
2.62
) and establish the Strichartz estimates. The Fokas method is a novel approach for solving IBVP for linear and integrable nonlinear PDEs, it was discovered by Fokas in 1997
[
18
]
in his request to generalize inverse scattering method and was further developed by
[
19
,
20
]
. This method has been successfully applied to establish well-posedness for both the one-dimensional and two-dimensional NLS equations
[
21
,
25
]
. As noted in
[
2
]
, Strichartz estimates for the
n
-dimensional problem have not been obtained via this method; in this work, we provide these estimates.

For
\phi
sufficiently smooth and sufficiently decaying as
x\rightarrow\infty
, the half space Fourier transform is defined by

\mathscr{F}[\phi](\zeta,k,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\phi(x^{\prime},x_{n},t)e^{-i\zeta\cdot x^{\prime}-ikx_{n}}dx_{n}\,dx^{\prime},\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\ \text{Im}k\leq 0,

(2.63)

where
\mathscr{F}[\phi]\,(\zeta,\cdot,t)
is analytic in
\mathbb{C}^{-}
and continuous up to
\mathbb{R}
. Apply the half space Fourier transform to the equality in (
2.62
). After integrating by parts twice with respect to
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and integrating in time over
(0,t)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\|\varphi\|_{W^{s,r}}^{p}+\|\psi\|_{W^{s,r}}^{p}\right)\|\varphi-\psi\|_{W^{s-2,r}}.

Here, the embedding

is used. Since

, Lemma
3.3
implies that for any

and

holds. Then from estimate (

), we have

\displaystyle\|F(u)(\cdot+h)-F(u)(\cdot)\|_{L^{\frac{q}{p+1}}(V;H^{s-2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{q}(0,T;W^{s,r})}^{p}\|u(\cdot+h)-u(\cdot)\|_{L^{q}(V;W^{s-2,r})}

(3.47)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{q}(0,T;W^{s,r})}^{p}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{q}(0,T;W^{s-2,r})}|h|.

Since

is reflexive, by (

(3.48)

An application of Lemma

with

and
\psi=u_{0}
gives

\|F(u)(t)-F(u_{0})\|_{H^{s-2}}\lesssim\left(\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{p+1-\theta}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{p+1-\theta}\right)\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{H^{s-2}}^{\theta},

where

. Consequently,

implies

. Moreover, we have

(3.49)

which follows analogously by setting
\varphi=0
and
\psi=u_{0}
. Substituting (
3.45
), (
3.48
) and (
3.49
) into (

\|\Gamma u\|_{X_{T}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{p+1}+T^{\frac{q-(p+1)}{q}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{p+1},

(3.50)

where

. Similarly, from (

) and (
2.94

For any

(ii)

such that

is an admissible pair. Then, from

) and Hölder’s inequality, we have for any

on

.

\sup_{t\in[0,T)}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq\alpha_{T}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}+\|h_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,T))}\right).

(4.3)

Proof.

We employ the approach of Bona et al.
[
4
]
to establish our result. For simplicity, we write
L^{p}_{t}=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,t))
and
H^{1}_{t}=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,t))
. We start with the following three identities for a solution
u
to (
1.1
):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|u|^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\,\text{Im}\nabla\cdot(\overline{u}\,\nabla u),

(4.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{n}\left(|\partial_{n}u|^{2}-|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2}+\frac{2\lambda}{p+2}|u|^{p+2}\right)

\displaystyle=-2\,\text{Re}\nabla^{\prime}\cdot(\overline{\partial_{n}u}\,\nabla^{\prime}u)-i\partial_{t}(u\overline{\partial_{n}u})+i\partial_{n}(u\overline{\partial_{t}u}),

(4.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{2\lambda}{p+2}|u|^{p+2}\right)

\displaystyle=2\,\text{Re}\nabla\cdot(\overline{\partial_{t}u}\nabla u),

(4.6)

where
\nabla^{\prime}
is the gradient on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Identity (
4.4
) can be derived by multiplying both sides of the equation in (
1.1
) by
2\overline{u}
, then taking imaginary parts. Identities (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) follow from multiplying by
2\overline{\partial_{n}u}
and
2\overline{\partial_{t}u}
, respectively, then taking the real part. Integrating both sides of the identity (
4.4
) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times[0,t]
for
t\in[0,T)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|u(x,t)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|u(x,0)|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{t}2\,\text{Im}\,[\overline{u}\partial_{n}u](x^{\prime},0,\tau)d\tau dx^{\prime}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\|h_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{t}}\|\partial_{n}u(\cdot,0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{t}}.

To estimate
\|\partial_{n}u(\cdot,0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{t}}
, we integrate (
4.5
) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times[0,t]
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{t}(|\partial_{n}u|^{2}-|\nabla^{\prime}u|^{2})(x^{\prime},0,\tau)d\tau dx^{\prime}+\frac{2\lambda}{p+2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{t}\left|u(x^{\prime},0,\tau)\right|^{p+2}d\tau dx^{\prime}

(4.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}(u\overline{\partial_{n}u})(x,t)-(u\overline{\partial_{n}u})(x,0)dx-i\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{t}(u\overline{\partial_{t}u})(x^{\prime},0,\tau)d\tau dx^{\prime}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality to (
4.8
) yields

\displaystyle\|\partial_{n}u(\cdot,0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{t}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{n}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{n}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

(4.9)

\displaystyle+\|h_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{t}}\|\partial_{t}h_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{t}}+\|h_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{t}}^{2}+\frac{2|\lambda|}{p+2}\|h_{0}\|_{L^{p+2}_{t}}^{p+2}.

Substituting (
4.9
) into (
4.7
) and using Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|h_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{t}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{n}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\|\partial_{n}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|h_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{t}}^{\frac{4}{3}}+C_{1}(t),

where
C_{1}(t)=2\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+3\|h_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{t}}+\frac{2|\lambda|}{p+2}\|h_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{p+2}}^{p+2}
. Therefore, we have

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\|\partial_{n}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|h_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{t}}^{\frac{4}{3}}+\frac{4}{3}C_{1}(t).

(4.10)

By integrating (
4.6
) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times[0,t]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla u|^{2}(x,t)-|\nabla u|^{2}(x,0)dx-\frac{2\lambda}{p+2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|u|^{p+2}(x,t)-|u|^{p+2}(x,0)dx

(4.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{t}-2\,\text{Re}[\overline{\partial_{t}u}\partial_{n}u](x^{\prime},0,\tau)d\tau dx^{\prime}.

We note that the validity of both the differential equations (
4.4
)–(
4.6
) and the integral equations in (
4.7
)–(
4.8
), (
4.11
) is guaranteed by the regularity assumptions (
4.1
)–(
4.2
).

For
\lambda<0
, from (
4.11
) we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq\|\partial_{n}u(\cdot,0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{t}}^{2}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\frac{2|\lambda|}{p+2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p+2}}^{p+2}+\|\partial_{t}h_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{t}}^{2}.

(4.12)

By substituting (
4.9
) into (
4.12
) and applying (
4.10
) together with the Young’s inequality, we derive

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{n}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+C_{1}(t)+C_{2}(t)

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{n}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\right)\left(2^{\frac{3}{2}}\|h_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{t}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\right)+\sqrt{\frac{4}{3}C_{1}(t)}\|\partial_{n}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+C_{1}(t)+C_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3}\|\partial_{n}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{2^{\frac{9}{2}}}{3}\|h_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{t}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{n}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{5}{3}C_{1}(t)+C_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{6}\|\partial_{n}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{3}(t),

where
C_{2}(t)=\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\frac{2|\lambda|}{p+2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p+2}}^{p+2}+\|h_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{t}}^{2},

C_{3}(t)=10\left(C_{1}(t)+C_{2}(t)\right)
. The Sobolev embedding
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{p+2}
(valid for the given range of
p
) guarantees the estimate for
\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
. Combined with the estimate (
4.10
), this completes the proof for the case
\lambda<0
.

For
\lambda>0
, (
4.11
) implies that we need to estimate
\|u(t)\|_{L^{p+2}}
. By using Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and (
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{p+2}}^{p+2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\frac{np}{2}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2+p-\frac{np}{2}}

(4.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\frac{np}{2}}\left(\|\partial_{n}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2+p}{3}-\frac{np}{6}}\|h_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{t}}^{\frac{4+2p-np}{3}}+C_{1}(t)^{\frac{4+2p-np}{4}}\right).

Recalling (
4.13
), then from (
4.14
) we have

\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq\frac{5}{6}\|\partial_{n}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\frac{2+p+np}{3}}\|h_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{t}}^{\frac{4+2p-np}{3}}+\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\frac{np}{2}}C_{1}(t)^{\frac{4+2p-np}{4}}\right)+C_{3}(t),

(4.15)

where
C>0
is a constant. When
0<p<\frac{4}{n+1}
, we have
\frac{2+p+np}{3}
,
\frac{np}{2}<2
, then an application of Young’s inequality gives

\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{5}{6}+\varepsilon\right)\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+C({\varepsilon})\left(C_{1}(t)^{\beta_{1}}+C_{1}(t)^{\beta_{2}}\right)+C_{3}(t),

(4.16)

where
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}>0
.

When
p=\frac{4}{n+1}
, we have
\frac{2+p+np}{3}=2
and
\frac{np}{2}<2
. Applying the Young’s inequality to (
4.15
), we obtain

\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{5}{6}+C\|h_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{t}}^{\nu_{1}}\right)\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+C(\varepsilon)C_{1}(t)^{\nu_{2}}+C_{3}(t),

where
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}>0
. Choosing
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{12}
yields

\left(\frac{1}{12}-C\|h_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{t}}^{\nu_{1}}\right)\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\lesssim C_{1}(t)^{\nu_{2}}+C_{3}(t).

On the other hand, we can choose
\delta>0
such that for all
\rho,\tau\in(0,T)
with
|\rho-\tau|<\delta
,
\|h_{0}\|^{\nu_{1}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(\rho,\tau))}\leq\tfrac{1}{24C}
. Set
t_{j}=\frac{jT}{\lfloor\frac{T}{\delta}\rfloor+1}
(
j=0,1,\dots,\lfloor\frac{T}{\delta}\rfloor+1
), then
\|h_{0}\|^{\nu_{1}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(t_{j},t_{j+1}))}\leq\frac{1}{24C}
. First estimate
\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}
on
[0,t_{1}]
, then take
u(t_{1})
as new initial data to extend the estimate to
[t_{1},t_{2}]
. Iterating this process yields the bound for
\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}
over all
t\in[0,T)
.
∎

4.2
Regularity analysis for
s\in[1,\frac{5}{2})

Building upon the solution properties established in Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
3.10
, we introduce the function space
\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T}
to facilitate the subsequent analysis.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
s\in\left[0,\frac{5}{2}\right)
,
p\geq\lceil s\rceil-1
with
(n-2s)p<4
, and
T\in(0,\infty]
.
The space
\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T}
consists of all functions

u\in C\left([0,T);H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\right)\cap C\left(\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}\mkern-4.0mu}\,;\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,T))\right)

that solve (
1.1
) and additionally satisfy:

(1)

For all
s\in\left[1,\frac{5}{2}\right)
,

u\in\bigcap_{(q,r)\,\text{admissible}}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\left[0,T\right);W^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\right)
;

(2)

For
s\in\left(\frac{3}{2},\frac{5}{2}\right)
,
u\in C^{1}\left([0,T);H^{s-2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\right)
;

(3)

For
s=1
,
u\in C^{1}\left(\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}\mkern-4.0mu}\,;\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,T))\right)
.

We note that the above definition of
\mathscr{X}^{2,p}_{T}
does not cover the case
0<p<1
, since in this range a solution satisfying the boundary data in
\mathcal{H}^{2}
is absent. The requirement can, however, be weakened by replacing
\mathcal{H}^{2}
with
\mathcal{W}^{2}
. In view of Proposition
3.9
and Corollary
3.11
, we now define the corresponding space:

Definition 4.3
.

For
0<p<1
with
(n-4)p<4
and
T\in(0,\infty]
, the space
\mathscr{X}^{2,p}_{T}
consists of all functions

u\in C\left([0,T);H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\right)\cap C\left(\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}\mkern-4.0mu}\,;\mathcal{W}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,T))\right)\cap C^{1}\left([0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\right)

that solve (
1.1
).

Building upon Proposition
2.17
, the following Proposition
4.4
establishes regularity properties in the space
\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T}
for
s\in(1,2)
. For the one-dimensional case (see
[
4
]
), the regularity properties for
s\in(1,2)
is derived by nonlinear interpolation, which relies on the key embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. However, for
n\geq 2
the embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
fails, thus this technique cannot be generalized to higher dimensions.
For
s\in\left(1,\frac{3}{2}\right]
, we resolve the difficulties through the development a decomposition-recursive technique (
4.23
)–(
4.25
). For
s\in\left(\frac{3}{2},2\right)
, the focus is on obtaining pointwise estimates for the time derivative of the nonlinear term. Lemma
3.8
provides the difference estimate (
4.29
), and the
L^{p}
-mean continuity transforms them into pointwise one (
4.30
).

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
s\in(1,2)
with
1\leq p<\frac{8}{3(n-2)}
,

u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
satisfy the compatibility condition. For any
T\in(0,\infty]
, if
u\in\mathscr{X}^{1,p}_{T}
, then
u\in\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
3.10
, there exist
T_{\max}\in(0,\infty]
and a solution belonging to
\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T_{\max}}
. By uniqueness, to establish
u\in\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T}
, it suffices to prove
T_{\max}\geq T
. We proceed by contradiction, assuming
T_{\max}<T
.

We first analyze the case
s\in\left(1,\frac{3}{2}\right]
. When
n=2
, estimate (
2.100
) with
\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=\left(\frac{3}{3-s},2\right)
yields that, for
t\in[0,T_{\max})
, we have

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|u\|_{C([0,t];H^{s})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}+\langle t\rangle^{\frac{s}{3}}\|F(u)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{3-s}}(0,t;H^{s})}.

(4.17)

Since
u\in\mathscr{X}^{1,p}_{T}
and
T_{\max}<T
, Definition
4.2
implies
u\in L^{q}\left(0,T_{\max};W^{1,r}\right)
for all admissible pairs
(q,r)
. We choose
(q,r)=\left(3p,\frac{6}{3p-2}\right)
, which satisfies
r>2
. By Lemma
3.2
and the embedding
W^{1,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
, we obtain

\left\|F(u)(t)\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,r}}^{p}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}.

(4.18)

Substituting (
4.18
) into (
4.17
), we have

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{3}{3-s}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{3}{3-s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{\frac{3}{3-s}}+\langle t\rangle^{\frac{s}{3-s}}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{W^{1,r}}^{\frac{3p}{3-s}}\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{3}{3-s}}d\tau.

(4.19)

An application of Gronwall’s inequality gives

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{3}{3-s}}\lesssim\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{3}{3-s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{\frac{3}{3-s}}\right)\exp\left(C\langle t\rangle^{\frac{s}{3-s}}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{W^{1,r}}^{\frac{3p}{3-s}}d\tau\right),

(4.20)

where
C>0
is a constant. Since
\frac{3p}{3-s}<3p=q
,
\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}
is bounded on
[0,T_{\max})
. This contradicts the blow-up criterion, proving
T_{\max}\geq T
.

When
3\leq n\leq 4
, applying (
2.100
) with
(\alpha,\beta)=(2,\frac{6n}{3n+6-4s})
and Lemma
3.2
with
\frac{3n+6-4s}{6n}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3-2s}{3n}
, we obtain

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{2}+\langle t\rangle^{\frac{2s}{3}}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\frac{3np}{3-2s}}}^{2p}\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}d\tau.

(4.21)

We choose the admissible pair
(q,r)=\left(2p,\frac{2np}{np-2}\right)
to estimate
\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\frac{3np}{3-2s}}}^{2p}
. The embedding
W^{1,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{3np}{3-2s}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
holds provided that

\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{np}-\frac{1}{n}<\frac{3-2s}{3np}\leq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{np},

which is equivalent to

\frac{12-4s}{3n}\leq p<\frac{4(3-s)}{3(n-2)}.

(4.22)

For any
1\leq p<\frac{8}{3(n-2)}
, there exists
s^{\prime}\in\left(1,\frac{3}{2}\right)
such that for all
s\in(1,s^{\prime}]
, the inequality (
4.22
) is satisfied. Applying this embedding and Gronwall’s inequality to (
4.21
), we conclude
u\in\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T}
for
s\in(1,s^{\prime}]
.
While our current result holds only for certain small ranges of
s
(particularly as
p
approaches
\frac{8}{3(n-2)}
), the technique developed below will ultimately extend this to the full range
s\in\left(1,\frac{3}{2}\right]
.

In order to establish
u\in\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T}
for
s\in\left(1,\frac{3}{2}\right]
, we decompose the interval via
\left(1,\frac{3}{2}\right]=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(s_{k+1},s_{k}\right]
, where
s_{k}=1+\frac{1}{2^{k}}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
The proof reduces to showing that for all
k\geq 1
, if
u\in\mathscr{X}^{s_{k+1},p}_{T}
, then
u\in\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T}
for
s\in(s_{k+1},s_{k}]
. The key embedding
W^{s_{k+1},r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{3np}{3-2s}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
holds for
s\in(s_{k+1},s_{k}]
provided that

\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{np}-\frac{s_{k+1}}{n}<\frac{3-2s}{3np}\leq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{np},

(4.23)

which is equivalent to

\frac{12-4s}{3n}\leq p<\frac{2^{k+2}(3-s)}{3(2^{k}(n-2)-1)}.

(4.24)

For
s\in(s_{k+1},s_{k}]
, we verify:

\displaystyle\frac{12-4s}{3n}\leq 1\leq p

\displaystyle<\frac{8}{3(n-2)}=\frac{4\cdot 2^{k+1}}{3\cdot 2^{k}(n-2)}

\displaystyle<\frac{4(2^{k+1}-1)}{3(2^{k}(n-2)-1)}=\frac{2^{k+2}(3-s_{k})}{3(2^{k}(n-2)-1)}

\displaystyle<\frac{2^{k+2}(3-s)}{3(2^{k}(n-2)-1)},

confirming that (
4.24
) always holds for
k\geq 1
. Combining (
4.21
) with this embedding yields, for
s\in(s_{k+1},s_{k}]
,

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{2}+\langle t\rangle^{\frac{2s}{3}}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{W^{s_{k+1},r}}^{2p}\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}d\tau.

(4.25)

This completes the proof for
s\in\left(1,\frac{3}{2}\right]
.

For
s\in\left(\frac{3}{2},2\right)
, using (
2.101
) and Hölder’s inequality in time variable yields:

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}+\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{H^{s-2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\,\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}+\|F(u)(0)\|_{H^{s-2}}

(4.26)

\displaystyle+\left(\langle t\rangle^{\frac{3}{4}}+t^{1-\frac{s}{2}}\right)\|F(u)\|_{L^{4}(0,t;H^{s})}+t^{\frac{1}{4}}\|F(u)\|_{\dot{W}^{1,4}(0,t;H^{s-2})}.

From Lemmas
3.7
and
3.8
, we have

\|F(u)(0)\|_{H^{s-2}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{p+1}.

(4.27)

When
2\leq n\leq 3
, we choose
(q,r)=\left(5p,\frac{10np}{5np-4}\right)
and note that
u\in L^{q}(0,T_{\max};W^{\frac{3}{2},r})
. Then the embedding

W^{\frac{3}{2},r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\cap W^{2-s,\frac{n}{2-s}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})

holds since
2<r<\frac{n}{2-s}
.
From Lemma
3.2
and
W^{\frac{3}{2},r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
, we have

\left\|F(u)(t)\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{W^{\frac{3}{2},r}}^{p}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}.

(4.28)

Using (
3.11
), Lemma
3.8
and
W^{\frac{3}{2},r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\hookrightarrow W^{2-s,\frac{n}{2-s}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
, we obtain

\|F(u)(t+h)-F(u)(t)\|_{H^{s-2}}\lesssim\left(\|u(t+h)\|_{W^{\frac{3}{2},r}}^{p}+\|u(t)\|_{W^{\frac{3}{2},r}}^{p}\right)\|u(t+h)-u(t)\|_{H^{s-2}}.

(4.29)

From Definition
4.2
, we have
u\in C^{1}\left([0,T_{\max});H^{s-2}\right)
.
Then we can conclude that

F(u)\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,\frac{q}{p}}(0,T_{\max};H^{s-2})

by using (
4.29
) and Lemma
3.3
. Therefore,
F(u)
is differentiable almost everywhere with respect to
t
. On the other hand, according to
L^{p}
-mean continuity. and diagonal rule,
u\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;W^{\frac{3}{2},r})
implies that there exists a sequence
\{h_{n}\}
with
h_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, such that for almost every
t\in(0,T)
,

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u(t+h_{n})\|_{W^{\frac{3}{2},r}}=\|u(t)\|_{W^{\frac{3}{2},r}}.

Replacing
h
in (
4.29
) with
h_{n}
, dividing both sides of the inequality by
h_{n}
, and letting
n\to\infty
yields for a.e.
t\in(0,T_{\max})
:

\left\|\partial_{t}F(u)(t)\right\|_{H^{s-2}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{W^{\frac{3}{2},r}}^{p}\left\|\partial_{t}u(t)\right\|_{H^{s-2}}.

(4.30)

Substituting (
4.27
), (
4.28
) and (
4.30
) into (
4.26
), we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{4}+\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{H^{s-2}}^{4}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{4}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{4}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{4(p+1)}

(4.31)

\displaystyle+\langle t\rangle^{3}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{W^{\frac{3}{2},r}}^{4p}\left(\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{s}}^{4}+\|\partial_{t}u(\tau)\|_{H^{s-2}}^{4}\right)d\tau.

An application of Gronwall’s inequality implies

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{4}+\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{H^{s-2}}^{4}\lesssim\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{4}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{4}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{4(p+1)}\right)\exp\left(C\langle t\rangle^{3}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{W^{\frac{3}{2},r}}^{4p}d\tau\right),

(4.32)

where
C>0
is a constant. The condition
4p<5p=q
ensures that
\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}
is bounded on
[0,T_{\max})
.

When
n=4
, the required embedding fails, so the analysis splits into two cases based on the range of
s
. For
s\in\left(\frac{3}{2},\frac{7}{4}\right]
, from (
2.101
) and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}+\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{H^{s-2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}+\|F(u)(0)\|_{H^{s-2}}

(4.33)

\displaystyle+\left(\langle t\rangle^{\frac{11}{8}}+t^{\frac{7}{8}-\frac{s}{2}}\right)\|F(u)\|_{L^{\frac{8}{3}}(0,t;H^{s})}+t^{\frac{1}{8}}\|F(u)\|_{\dot{W}^{1,\frac{8}{3}}(0,t;H^{s-2})}.

The special admissible pair
(q,r)=\left(\frac{11}{3},\frac{11}{4}\right)
ensures the embedding

W^{\frac{3}{2},r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\cap\,W^{2-s,\frac{n}{2-s}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}),

thus (
4.27
), (
4.28
) and (
4.30
) remain valid. Substituting these estimates into (
4.33
) and applying Gronwall’s inequality yields

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{8}{3}}\lesssim\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{8}{3}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{\frac{8}{3}}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{8}{3}(p+1)}\right)\exp\left(C\langle t\rangle^{\frac{11}{3}}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{W^{\frac{3}{2},r}}^{\frac{8}{3}p}d\tau\right).

Since
\frac{8}{3}p<\frac{11}{3}=q
, we conclude
u\in\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T}
for
s\in\left(\frac{3}{2},\frac{7}{4}\right]
. For
s\in\left(\frac{7}{4},2\right)
, taking
(q,r)=\left(\frac{16}{3},\frac{32}{13}\right)
provides the embedding

W^{\frac{7}{4},r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\cap W^{2-s,\frac{n}{2-s}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})

and analogous arguments yield
u\in\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T}
.
∎

Finally, we give the regularity result for
s\in\left[2,\frac{5}{2}\right)
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
satisfy the compatibility condition. For any
T\in(0,\infty]
, the following regularity properties hold:

(i)

For
s=2
with
0<p<\frac{4}{n-2}
,
u\in\mathscr{X}^{1,p}_{T}\implies u\in\mathscr{X}^{2,p}_{T}
.

(ii)

For
s\in\left(2,\frac{5}{2}\right)
with
2\leq p<\frac{4}{n-2}
,
u\in\mathscr{X}^{2,p}_{T}\implies u\in\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T}
.

Proof.

For
s=2
, by Theorem
1.3
with Corollary
3.10
–
3.11
and Proposition
3.9
, there exist
T_{\max}\in(0,\infty]
and a solution belonging to
\mathscr{X}^{2,p}_{T_{\max}}
. As in Proposition
4.4
, arguing by contradiction, we assume
0<T_{\max}<T
and bound
\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}
on
[0,T_{\max})
. The estimate (
2.102
) gives for all
t\in[0,T_{\max})
:

\|u\|_{C([0,t];H^{2})}+\|u\|_{W^{1,q}(0,t;L^{r})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+\|F(u)\|_{C([0,t];L^{2})}+\|F(u)\|_{W^{1,\gamma^{\prime}}(0,t;L^{\rho^{\prime}})}.

(4.34)

For
0<p<\frac{4}{n-2}
, we now determine the exponents appearing in (
4.34
). We choose
2<\sigma,r,\rho<\frac{2n}{n-2}
, such that the relation

\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}=\frac{p}{\sigma}+\frac{1}{r}
holds. Indeed, consider the function
f_{p}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
f_{p}(x,y,z)=x+py+z-1
, which satisfies
f_{p}(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})>0
and
f_{p}(\frac{n-2}{2n},\frac{n-2}{2n},\frac{n-2}{2n})<0
. The exponents
q
and gamma are chosen so that
(q,r)
and
(\gamma,\rho)
are admissible pairs; clearly we have
q>\gamma^{\prime}
.

From Sobolev embeddings and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we derive

\|F(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|F(u)(t)\|_{W^{1,\rho^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{L^{\sigma}}^{p}\|u(t)\|_{W^{1,r}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{p+1-\theta}\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{\theta},

(4.35)

where
\theta\in(0,1)
. Substituting (
4.35
) into (
4.34
), and applying Young’s inequality yields

\|u\|_{C([0,t];H^{2})}+\|u\|_{W^{1,q}(0,t;L^{r})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+\|u\|_{C([0,t];H^{1})}^{\frac{p+1-\theta}{1-\theta}}+\|F(u)\|_{W^{1,\gamma^{\prime}}(0,t;L^{\rho^{\prime}})}.

(4.36)

We also have the following inequalities

\|F(u)(\tau)\|_{L^{\rho^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{1}}^{p}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{r}},

(4.37)

\|F(u)(\tau+h)-F(u)(\tau)\|_{L^{\rho^{\prime}}}\lesssim\left(\|u(\tau+h)\|_{H^{1}}^{p}+\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{1}}^{p}\right)\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{r}},

(4.38)

where
\tau
,
\tau+h\in(0,t)
. Then from Lemma
3.3
, we deduce that

\|F(u)\|_{W^{1,\gamma}(0,t;L^{\rho^{\prime}})}\lesssim\|u\|_{C([0,t];H^{1})}^{p}\|u\|_{W^{1,\gamma^{\prime}}(0,t;L^{r})}.

(4.39)

Substituting (
4.39
) into (
4.36
), we make use of
q>\gamma^{\prime}
to apply Lemma
3.4
. Combining this with assumptions
u\in\mathscr{X}^{1,p}_{T}
and
T_{\max}<T
, we derive
\|u\|_{W^{1,q}(0,t;L^{r})}\leq M(t)
, where
M\in C_{b}[0,T_{\max})
. Using
q>\gamma^{\prime}
once more, we see that
\|u\|_{W^{1,\gamma^{\prime}}(0,t;L^{r})}
is controlled. Together with (
4.36
), we finally obtain the bound for
\|u\|_{C([0,t];H^{2})}
.

For
s\in\left(2,\frac{5}{2}\right)
by using
u\in L^{q}\left(0,T_{\max};W^{2,r}\right)
where
(q,r)=\left(5p,\frac{10np}{5np-4}\right)
to ensure the embedding

W^{2,r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\cap W^{s-2,\frac{n}{s-2}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}).

From (
2.97
) and an analysis analogous to that in (
4.27
)–(
4.32
), we derive the desired result.
∎

4.3
Proof of Theorem

In this section, we mainly use the
H^{1}
a priori estimate in Section
4.1
and the regularity analysis in Section
4.2
to prove Theorem
1.5
.

Before proving Theorem
1.5
, we first establish the existence of the solution
u\in\mathscr{X}^{2,p}_{\infty}
for the case
s=2
under the broader parameter range (
4.40
) in Proposition
4.6
. This preliminary result serves two key purposes:
(i) To handle the case
s=1
through an approximation argument;
(ii) To directly construct global solutions for

as stated in Theorem

, within the framework of Definition

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
and
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})

satisfy the compatibility condition. Under the following parameter constraints:

\text{when $\lambda<0$, $p<\tfrac{4}{n-2}$}\;\text{or when $\lambda>0$, $p\leq\tfrac{4}{n+1}$},

(4.40)

there exists a unique solution
u\in\mathscr{X}^{2,p}_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Since
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
and
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\subset\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
, Theorem
1.3
guarantees the existence of a maximal existence time
T_{\max}\in(0,\infty]
and a solution
u\in~\mathscr{X}^{1,p}_{T_{\max}}
. By the blow-up criterion, if
T_{\max}<\infty
, then

\lim_{t\to T_{\max}}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\infty.

(4.41)

From Proposition
4.5
, we conclude that
u\in\mathscr{X}^{2,p}_{T_{\max}}
, which implies
u
satisfies the regularity condition (
4.1
). Building upon the definitions of
\mathscr{X}^{2,p}_{T_{\max}}
(see Definitions
4.2
–
4.3
) and
\mathcal{W}^{2}
(see (
2.90
)), together with (
3.9
), we obtain

\partial_{t}u\in C\left(\,\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}\mkern-4.0mu}\,;L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,T_{\max}))\right).

The same result also holds for
\partial_{x_{i}}u
(
1\leq i\leq n-1
) by using the definition of
\mathscr{X}^{1,p}_{T_{\max}}
. Finally, Definition
4.2
(3) with (
3.9
) implies

u\in C^{1}\left(\,\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}\mkern-4.0mu}\,;L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,T_{\max}))\right).

Consequently,
u
satisfies the regularity assumptions required by Proposition
4.1
.

Using the a priori estimate (
4.3
) and the embedding (
3.10
), we derive that
\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}
is bounded on
[0,T_{\max})
. This contradicts (
4.41
); therefore, we conclude that
T_{\max}=\infty
and
u\in\mathscr{X}^{1,p}_{\infty}
. Applying Proposition
4.5
directly gives
u\in\mathscr{X}^{2,p}_{\infty}
.
∎

Combining results from the above we can now prove Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

For
s=1
, given
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
and
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
3.10
establish the existence of a maximal solution
u\in\mathscr{X}^{1,p}_{T_{\max}}
.
Assume
T_{\max}<~\infty
.
We construct approximating sequences:

(i)

\{u_{0m}\}\subset H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
with
\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}u_{0m}=u_{0}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
;

(ii)

\{h_{0m}\}\subset\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
with
\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}h_{0m}=h_{0}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
,

where
u_{0m}
and
h_{0m}
satisfy the compatibility condition. These sequences are justified as follows: We first choose
u_{0m}
satisfying (i). Since
u_{0}-h_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
, we can select
g_{0m}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
such that
g_{0m}\rightarrow u_{0}-h_{0}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
, and finally define
h_{0m}=u_{0m}-g_{0m}
. For these initial and boundary data, it follows from Proposition
4.6
that there exists

u_{m}\in\mathscr{X}^{1,p}_{\infty}\cap\mathscr{X}^{2,p}_{\infty}
. Applying Proposition
4.1
, we obtain

\|u_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{\max};H^{1})}\lesssim\alpha_{T_{\max}}\left(\|u_{0m}\|_{H^{1}}+\|h_{0m}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,T_{\max}))}\right).

(4.42)

Thus, there exists a subsequence
\{u_{m_{j}}\}
such that
u_{m_{j}}\rightarrow\tilde{u}
weakly star in
L^{\infty}(0,T_{\max};H^{1})
.

Since
0<p<\frac{4}{n-2}
, as in the proof of Proposition
4.5
, we choose
2<\sigma,r,\rho<\frac{2n}{n-2}
, such that
\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}=\frac{p}{\sigma}+\frac{1}{r}
, and take
q
and
\gamma
such that
(q,r)
and
(\gamma,\rho)
are admissible pairs.

From (
2.94
),
for any
T\in(0,T_{\max})
and
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\|u_{m}-u\|_{L^{q}(0,t;L^{r})}\lesssim\|u_{0m}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h_{0m}-h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}+\|F(u_{m})-F(u)\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(0,t;L^{\rho^{\prime}})}.

Using (
3.12
), Hölder’s inequality, and the embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, we obtain

\|F(u_{m})-F(u)\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(0,t;L^{\rho^{\prime}})}\lesssim\left(\|u_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;H^{1})}^{p}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;H^{1})}^{p}\right)\|u_{m}-u\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(0,t;L^{r})}.

Combining these estimates yields

\|u_{m}-u\|_{L^{q}(0,t;L^{r})}\leq c_{T}\left(\|u_{0m}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h_{0m}-h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}\right)+c_{T}\|u_{m}-u\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}(0,t;L^{r})}.

(4.43)

By applying Lemma
3.4
to (
4.43
), we derive

\|u_{m}-u\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{r})}\lesssim C_{T}\left(\|u_{0m}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h_{0m}-h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}\right).

(4.44)

The embedding (
2.57
) implies
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. From (
4.44
), it follows that
u_{m}\rightarrow u
in
L^{q}(0,T;L^{r})
, hence
u=\tilde{u}
almost everywhere.

By the properties of weak-star convergence and the continuity of
\alpha_{T_{\max}}
, the estimate (
4.42
) yields

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{\max};H^{1})}\leq\varliminf_{m_{j}\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{m_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{\max};H^{1})}\lesssim\alpha_{T_{\max}}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+\|h_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,T_{\max}))}\right),

which contradicts the blow-up criterion, proving
T_{\max}=\infty
.
Consequently, we conclude that the solution
u\in\mathscr{X}^{1,p}_{\infty}
, which completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
for the case
s=1
. An application of Proposition
4.4
yields the result for
s\in(1,2)
.

For the case
s=2
, the global solution stated in Theorem
1.5
can be obtained by Proposition
4.6
. For higher regularity
s\in\left(2,\frac{5}{2}\right)
, the result follows immediately from Proposition
4.5
.
∎

4.4
L^{2}
a priori estimates

This section focuses on the establishment of the
L^{2}
estimates.

Proposition 4.7
.

Suppose that
p\leq\frac{2}{n}
and
(p,n)\neq(1,2)
. Let
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
satisfy the compatibility condition. For any
T>0
, every solution
u
to IBVP (
1.1
) possessing the regularity

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto u(\cdot,t)\in C\left([0,T);H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\right)\cap C^{1}\left([0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\right),

(4.45)

\displaystyle x_{n}

\displaystyle\mapsto u(\cdot,x_{n},\cdot)\in C^{1}\left(\,\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}\mkern-4.0mu}\,;L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,T))\right),

(4.46)

satisfies the estimate

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(1+t)A\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}\right)e^{tA\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}\right)},

(4.47)

for all
t\in[0,T)
. Here,
A:\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is a continuous, non-decreasing function with
A(0)=0
.

Proof.

We define
v
as the solution to the following linear IBVP for the unforced Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}v+\Delta v=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&v(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
&v(x^{\prime},0,t)=h_{0}(x^{\prime},t),\quad(x^{\prime},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.48)

Then the difference
w:=u-v
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}w+\Delta w+\lambda|v+w|^{p}(v+w)=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&w(x,0)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
&w(x^{\prime},0,t)=0,\quad(x^{\prime},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.49)

Multilplying
\bar{w}
to both sides of the equation in (
4.49
), and then taking the imaginary part, we obtain

\partial_{t}|w|^{2}+2\text{Im}[\Delta w\bar{w}]+2\lambda\text{Im}[|v+w|^{p}v\bar{w}]=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

(4.50)

Integrating both sides of (
4.50
) over
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\times(0,t)
, and utilizing integration by parts we derive

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\|w(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\text{Im}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}(w\partial_{n}w)(x^{\prime},0,\tau)dx^{\prime}d\tau

(4.51)

\displaystyle+2\text{Im}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla w(x,\tau)|^{2}dxd\tau+2\lambda\text{Im}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}(|v+w|^{p}v\bar{w})(x,\tau)dxd\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\lambda\text{Im}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}(|v+w|^{p}v\bar{w})(x,\tau)dxd\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|w|^{p+1}|v|(x,\tau)+|v|^{p+1}|w|(x,\tau)dxd\tau,

an application of Hölder’s inequality leads to

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\frac{4}{np}}(0,t;L^{\frac{2}{1-p}})}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|w(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4(p+1)}{4-np}}d\tau\right)^{\frac{4-np}{4}}

(4.52)

\displaystyle+\|v\|_{L^{\frac{4(p+1)}{np}}(0,t;L^{2(p+1)})}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|w(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{4-np}}d\tau\right)^{\frac{4-np}{4}}.

Given the range of
p
, both
\frac{2}{1-p}
and
2(p+1)
lie in
[2,\infty)
when
n=2
, and in
\left[2,\frac{2n}{n-2}\right]
, when
n>2
. This guarantees that both

\big(\frac{4}{np},\frac{2}{1-p}\big)
and
\big(\frac{4(p+1)}{np},2(p+1)\big)
are admissible pairs. Hence, applying the Strichartz estimate (
2.94
) with
f=0
yields

\max\Bigl\{\|v\|_{L^{\frac{4(p+1)}{np}}(0,t;L^{2(p+1)})},\|v\|_{L^{\frac{4}{np}}(0,t;L^{\frac{2}{1-p}})}\Bigr\}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}.

(4.53)

Using the fact that
\frac{4(p+1)}{4-np}\leq\frac{8}{4-np}
, we combine estimate (
4.52
) with Young’s inequality, to obtain

\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{8}{4-np}}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}})\left(t+\int_{0}^{t}\|w(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{8}{4-np}}d\tau\right),

(4.54)

where
C(x)=ax^{b}
with
a,b>0
. Then Gronwall’s inequality applied to (
4.54
) gives

\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{8}{4-np}}\leq tC(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}})e^{tC(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}})}.

(4.55)

Recall that
u=v+w
. Together with the linear estimate
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{2})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}
(which also provided by (
2.94
)), this yields the final estimate for
u
.
∎

The method developed above is completely adapt to the case
n=1
, providing a new estimate for the one-dimensional IBVP (
1.3
).

Corollary 4.8
.

p\leq 1
,
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
h_{0}\in H^{\frac{5}{4}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
satisfy the compatibility condition. For any

, every solution

to IBVP (

) possessing the regularity

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto u(\cdot,t)\in C\left([0,T);H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\right)\cap C^{1}\left([0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\right),

(4.56)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto u(x,\cdot)\in C^{1}\big(\,\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}\mkern-4.0mu}\,;H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\frac{1}{4}}(0,T)\big),

(4.57)

satisfies the estimate

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(1+t)A(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{4}}})\;e^{tA\big(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|h_{0}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{4}}}\big)},

for all

. Here,
A:\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}

Proof.

Let
v
be the solution to the linear problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}v+\partial_{x}^{2}v=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&v(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad v(0,t)=h_{0}(t).\end{aligned}\right.

(4.59)

For
v
, the Strichartz estimate (see
[
26
,
4
]
) states that

\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,t;L^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|h_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{4}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

(4.60)

In the range
p\leq 1
, both the pairs
\big(\frac{4}{p},\frac{2}{1-p}\big)
and
\big(\frac{4(p+1)}{p},2(p+1)\big)
are admissible. The estimate for
w=u-v
follows by setting
n=1
in the proof of Proposition
4.7
, this completes the proof.
∎

Proposition
4.7
will be applied to the case
s=0
,
n\geq 2
in Theorem
1.7
. We now establish the following proposition to prepare for proving the case
s\in(0,1)
,
n\geq 2
in Theorem
1.7
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Suppose
s\in(0,1)
and
p\leq\min\{1,\frac{2(s+1)}{n}\}
. Let
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
satisfy the compatibility condition. Then for any solution
u
to IBVP (
1.1
) that satisfies (
4.45
)–(
4.46
), the estimate

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(1+t)A\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}\right)e^{tA\left(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}\right)}

(4.61)

holds for all
t\in[0,T)
. Here,
A:\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is a continuous, non-decreasing function with
A(0)=0
.

Proof.

We define
v
as the solution of (
4.48
) and
w:=u-v
. Since the estimate for
v
is known, it suffices to bound
w
. Applying Hölder’s inequality with respect to
x
in (
4.51
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|w(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{p+1}\|v(\tau)\|_{L^{\frac{2}{1-p}}}+\|v(\tau)\|_{L^{2(p+1)}}^{p+1}\|w(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau.

(4.62)

We first treat the case
n=2
, for which only
p=1
remains, since
p\in(0,1)
is already proved in Proposition
4.7
. For any
s>0
, choose
r\in(2,\infty)
such that
W^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, and let
q\in(2,\infty)
be such that
(q,r)
is an admissible pair. Combining this with (
4.62
) leads to

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{2})}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,t;W^{s,r})}\|w\|_{L^{2q^{\prime}}(0,t;L^{2})}^{2}+t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{L^{4}(0,t;L^{4})}^{2}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{2})}.

(4.63)

Note that
(4,4)
is an admissible pair for
n=2
. Applying Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,t;W^{s,r})}\|w\|_{L^{2q^{\prime}}(0,t;L^{2})}^{2}+t\|v\|_{L^{4}(0,t;L^{4})}^{4}

(4.64)

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}})\left(1+t+\|w\|_{L^{2q^{\prime}}(0,t;L^{2})}^{2}\right),

where
C(x)=a_{1}x^{b_{1}}+a_{2}x^{b_{2}}
with
a_{i},b_{i}>0
for
i=1,2
. From Gronwall’s inequality, we bound
w
as follows:

\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{2})}\lesssim(1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}})e^{tC(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}})}.

(4.65)

For
n>2
, the estimate in the range
p\leq\frac{2}{n}
has been proved in Proposition
4.7
; we therefore consider the remaining range
\frac{2}{n}<p\leq\frac{2(s+1)}{n}
. A direct computation gives

\frac{n-2}{2n}-\frac{s}{n}\leq\frac{1-p}{2}<\frac{n-2}{2n},

(4.66)

which implies
W^{s,\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{2}{1-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. On the other hand, from computation we can show for any
p
, there exists
r\in[2,\frac{2}{n-2}]
, such that
W^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{2(p+1)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Indeed:

•

when
\frac{2}{n}<p\leq\frac{2}{n-2}
, we have
2<2(p+1)\leq\frac{2n}{n-2}
,

•

when
\frac{2}{n-2}\leq p\leq\frac{2(s+1)}{n}
, we have
W^{s,\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\hookrightarrow L^{2(p+1)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Hence, we can choose
q\in[2,\infty]
such that
(q,r)
is an admissible pair. Applying these two embeddings to (
4.62
), and then using the estimate (
2.95
) for
v
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,t;W^{s,\frac{2n}{n-2}})}\|w\|_{L^{2(p+1)}(0,t;L^{2})}^{p+1}+t^{1-\frac{p+1}{q}}\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,t;W^{s,r})}^{p+1}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{2})}

(4.67)

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}})\left(\|w\|_{L^{2(p+1)}(0,t;L^{2})}^{p+1}+(1+t)\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{2})}\right).

A use of Young’s inequality yields

\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{2})}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}})(1+t+\|w\|_{L^{2(p+1)}(0,t;L^{2})}).

(4.68)

Applying Gronwall’s inequality to this estimate leads to

\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{2})}\lesssim(1+t)C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}})e^{tC(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}})}.

(4.69)

∎

4.5
Regularity analysis for
s\in[0,1)

This section provides the regularity analysis for
s\in[0,1)
. We still rely on the space
\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T}
given by Definitions
4.2
and
4.3
, and benefit from techniques similar to those in Proposition
4.4
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Suppose
s\in(0,1)
and
p\leq 1
. Further assume that
p\leq\frac{6-4s}{3(n-2)}
when
n\geq 6
, and
p<\frac{2}{n-2}
when
n<6
. Let
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
satisfy the compatibility condition. For any
0<T\leq\infty
, if
u\in\mathscr{X}^{0,p}_{T}
, then
u\in\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T}
.

Proof.

Following the contradiction scheme of Proposition
4.4
, we assume
0<T_{\max}<T
and proceed to bound
\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}
on
[0,T_{\max})
. For
n=2
, select
\beta\in\left(\frac{3n}{3n+6-4s},2\right)
satisfying
\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{p}{2}
, and take
\alpha\in\left(\frac{3}{3-s},2\right)
such that
(\alpha,\beta)
satisfy the condition in (
2.100
). Define
\sigma
by
\frac{1}{\sigma}=\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}
. Applying (
2.100
) together with (
3.3
) yields

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{\alpha}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{\alpha}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{\alpha}+\langle t\rangle^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\sigma p}}^{\alpha p}\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{s}}^{\alpha}d\tau.

(4.70)

Note that
\sigma p\in[2,\infty)
and
\alpha p<2
, and
u\in\mathscr{X}^{0,p}_{T}
implies that

u\in L^{\alpha p}(0,T_{\max};L^{\sigma p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})).

The bound for
\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}
then follows from Gronwall’s inequality.

We now consider
n>2
with
p\leq\frac{6-4s}{3(n-2)}
. Define
\sigma=\frac{2n}{p(n-2)}
, then take
\beta
such that
\frac{1}{\beta}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\sigma}
, and the range of
p
implies
\beta\in\left[\frac{3n}{3n+6-4s},2\right)
. Then we can choose
\alpha\in\left(\frac{3}{3-s},2\right]
such that
(\alpha,\beta)
satisfy the condition of the estimate (
2.100
). Combining (
2.100
) with (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|F(u)(\tau)\|_{W^{s,\beta}}d\tau

(4.71)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}+\langle t\rangle^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{p\sigma}}^{\alpha p}\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{s}}d\tau.

Note that
p\sigma=\frac{2n}{n-2}
and
u\in\mathscr{X}^{0,p}_{T}
implies that
u\in L^{\alpha p}\big(0,T_{\max};L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\big)
. Thus we obtain the bound for
\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}
. This means that we have completed the proof of the case
n\geq 6
with
p\leq\frac{6-4s}{3(n-2)}
.

Next, we prove the case
n<6
with
p<\frac{2}{n-2}
. Observe that
\frac{6-4s}{3(n-2)}<\frac{2}{n-2}
for
s\in(0,1)
. The proof above shows that the proposition holds for
n<6
with
p<\frac{6-4s}{3(n-2)}
. Therefore, we only need to prove the remaining range
n<6
with
\frac{6-4s}{3(n-2)}<p<\frac{2}{n-2}
. The above argument shows that for any
p<\frac{2}{n-2}
, there exists some
\varepsilon>0
such that
u\in\mathscr{X}^{0,p}_{T}
implies
u\in\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T}
for
s\in(0,\varepsilon]
. We now extend this result to the full range
s\in(0,1)
. Taking
(\alpha,\beta)=\left(2,\frac{6n}{3n+6-4s}\right)
in estimate (
2.100
) yields

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{2}+\langle t\rangle^{\frac{2s}{3}}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\frac{3np}{3-2s}}}^{2p}\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}d\tau.

(4.72)

Following the procedure used in Proposition
4.4
, we define
s_{k}=r^{k}
with
r\in(0,1)
. The proof reduces to verifying that for any
k\geq 0
, if
u\in\mathscr{X}^{s_{k+1},p}_{T}
, then
u\in\mathscr{X}^{s,p}_{T}
for
s\in(s_{k+1},s_{k}]
. Since
u\in\mathscr{X}^{s_{k+1},p}_{T}
implies
u\in L^{2}(0,T_{\max};W^{s_{k+1},\frac{2n}{n-2}})
, it
this is then converted to deriving the embedding
W^{s_{k+1},\frac{2n}{n-2}}\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{3np}{2-2s}}
for
s\in(s_{k+1},s_{k}]
, provided that

\frac{n-2}{2n}-\frac{s_{k+1}}{n}<\frac{3-2s}{3np}\leq\frac{n-2}{2n}.

(4.73)

The inequality
\frac{3-2s}{3np}\leq\frac{n-2}{2n}
follows from the assumption
p>\frac{6-4s}{3(n-2)}
. Thus, only the left-hand inequality needs to be satisfied, which requires

p<f_{r}(k)=\frac{2(3-2r^{k})}{3(n-2-2r^{k+1})}\quad\text{for}\quad k\geq 0.

(4.74)

By computation, for
n=3,4
, we have
p<\frac{2}{n-2}\leq f_{\frac{3}{4}}(k)
. For
n=5
, there exists
r\in(0,1)
such that
p<f_{r}(0)\leq\frac{2}{n-2}
; and note that
f_{r}(k)\rightarrow\frac{2}{n-2}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, then
p<f_{r}(k)
holds. Therefore, inequality (
4.73
) holds for all
k
, which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.11
.

Suppose
s\in(0,1)
,
p\leq 1
,
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
h_{0}\in H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
satisfy the compatibility condition. For any
0<T\leq\infty
, if

u\in C([0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))\cap C\big(\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}};H^{\frac{1}{4}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T)\big)

satisfies (
1.3
) and
u\in\bigcap_{(q,r)\,\text{admissible}}L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\left[0,T\right);L^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\right)
, then
u\in C([0,T);H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
.

Proof.

Within the same framework as Proposition
4.10
, we proceed to obtain an
H^{s}
bound. According to the linear estimate in
[
4
]
, we can obtain a unified linear estimate

\|u\|_{C([0,t];H^{s})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|h_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}}+\langle t\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\|F(u)\|_{L^{2}(0,t;H^{s})}.

Applying it to (
1.3
) gives

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|h_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}}^{2}+\langle t\rangle\|F(u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,t;H^{s})}.

(4.75)

Using
\|F(u)(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}
, and the fact that
(q,r)=(4,\infty)
is an admissible pair in the one-dimensional setting, together with (
4.75
), we obtain

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|h_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}}^{2}+\langle t\rangle\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2p}\|u(\tau)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}d\tau,

(4.76)

thereby establishing the desired
H^{s}
bound.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

Propositions
4.9
–
4.10
and Corollary
4.11
will be used to prove Theorem
1.7
in the next section. Indeed, they also hold for
s=1
, thus the results for
s\in(0,1)
in Theorem
1.7
hold for
s=1
. This yields two improvements for the global result in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. First, for
n=2,3
and
p\leq 1
, the additional
H^{1}
condition on the boundary data can be removed in Theorem
1.5
. For
n=1
and
p\leq 1
the one-dimensional result in
[
4
]
is also improved, where the requirement on the boundary data can be weakened from
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
to
H^{\frac{3}{4}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Second, for the focusing Schrödinger equation, the global
H^{1}
solutions for the IVP are known to exist for
p<\frac{4}{n}

[
22
]
. In contrast, for the nonhomogeneous IBVP, Theorem
1.5
establishes global solutions for
p\leq\frac{4}{n+1}
, due to the use of Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality to the nonhomogeneous boundary setting in the proof of Proposition
4.1
. It is unknown if the upper bound
\frac{4}{n+1}
can be improved to
\frac{4}{n}
; here, in the four-dimensional setting, we achieve
p\in(0,1)
, offering a possibility to narrow this gap.

.

(i)

It then follows that for

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq(1+t)A\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}\right)e^{CtA\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}\right)}.

(4.78)

with

,
