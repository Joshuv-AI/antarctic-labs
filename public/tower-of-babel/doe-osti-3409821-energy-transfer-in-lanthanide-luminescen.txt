1. Summary of Project Objectives

The primary objective of this project was to investigate the mechanisms of energy transfer in
lanthanide luminescent complexes, focusing on elucidating the role of ligand-to-metal charge
transfer processes and the efficiency of sensitization mechanisms. Specifically, this work aimed to
determine the mechanistic details of energy transfer pathways in systems incorporating lanthanide
ions such as terbium and europium, with a focus on advancing the application of these complexes
in molecular imaging and photonic technologies.

2. Methods

The project employed a combination of synthetic chemistry, optical spectroscopy, and advanced
X-ray transient absorption (XTA) techniques. The main experimental approach involved:

 • Multistep synthesis of lanthanide-binding ligands and their corresponding metal
 complexes.
 • Preparation of samples for synchrotron-based XTA studies to directly probe lanthanide
 excited states.
 • Use of optical transient absorption spectroscopy to monitor ligand state dynamics.
 • Use of synchrotron facilities at the Advanced Photon Source (APS) and SLAC National
 Accelerator Laboratory for XTA experiments.

3. Results

Over the course of the project, key progress was made in characterizing ligand-to-metal energy
transfer processes in lanthanide complexes. The project achieved two principal scientific
outcomes:

 1. A peer-reviewed publication in Journal of the American Chemical Society (2019)
 established that Dexter charge exchange is the dominant ligand-to-metal energy transfer
 mechanism in Eu(III) complexes, with near-unity efficiency.

 2. Successful collection and analysis of X-ray Transient Absorption (XTA) data for the H22-
 IAM-MOE Eu(III) and Tb(III) complexes at the Stanford Synchrotron Radiation
 Lightsource (SSRL).

Key findings from the XTA measurements include:

 • Clear negative-positive difference features at the L₃ edges of both Eu and Tb complexes,
 consistent with edge shifts due to ligand-to-metal energy transfer.
 • Kinetic analyses of the H22-IAM-MOE complex revealed a dominant rise time of ~1.6 ns,
 attributed to the decay of the ligand triplet state followed by energy transfer to the
 lanthanide center.
 • These results support the extension of mechanistic understanding from Eu(III) to Tb(III)
 systems and validate the combined OTA/XTA approach developed in this project.

The XTA experiments confirmed energy transfer to the Tb(III) 5D4 excited state, consistent with
the proposed sensitization mechanism. These measurements represent the culmination of the
synthetic and preparative efforts undertaken within the grant's active timeline.

Collectively, these outcomes confirm the project’s success in advancing the mechanistic
understanding of energy transfer in lanthanide complexes through synthesis and cutting-edge
spectroscopy.

5. Concluding Remarks

The project successfully advanced the understanding of energy transfer mechanisms in lanthanide
luminescent complexes. The combined use of synthetic chemistry, optical spectroscopy, and
synchrotron-based XTA represents a significant methodological contribution to the field. The
project delivered critical insights into the efficiency of Dexter-type energy transfer and laid the
groundwork for future investigations.

This report reflects the collaborative efforts across multiple national laboratories and universities,
emphasizing the importance of interdisciplinary and multi-institutional research in addressing
complex scientific questions.
