Quasiautomorphic forms are isomorphic to vector-valued automorphic forms
Michael Andrew Henry

Abstract
We utilize the structure of quasiautomorphic forms over a Hecke triangle group to define a mapping from a quasiautomorphic form to a vector-valued automorphic form (vvaf). This kind of vvaf we call a Hecke vector-form. First we supply a proof of the functional equations that hold for Hecke vector-forms modulo the group generators. Then, utilizing the multiplier system for these Hecke vector-forms, we prove the opposite direction and complete the bijection. Since the modular group is a special instance of the Hecke triangle groups, our results hold for quasimodular forms.



Keywords: Automorphic form; Quasiautomorphic form; extremal quasiautomorphic form; Hecke triangle group; Modular form; Quasimodular form; Hecke vector-form

A

A lot of the interest in modular forms has come by studying the arithmetic of the coefficients of series expansions of particular modular forms — some of the astonishing work of Ramanujan is a prime example — where the integrality and combinatorial richness of those coefficients has proven decisive.

\displaystyle{g\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right)}={(cz+d)^{2k}}g(z),\quad\text{whenever }\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\

The situation is past due for a reappraisal, however, since recent mathematical developments are legitimizing non-integer counting functions. Specifically, in some very challenging areas of mathematics, such as string theory and enumerative geometry, the landscape of enumerative combinatorics is being developed in unexpected diretions: more and more studies are finding ways of attaching discrete combinatorial meaning to rational numbers. The most famous examples are probably the so-called Gromov-Witten invariants. Originating in string theory, these numerically rational invariants, while hard to define simply, are often calculated by determining coefficients in infinite series, just as with so many examples from classical combinatorics

The techniques appear to the author (who is not an expert) to descend from the counting revolution that had already begun taking place in physics. These advances compel us to return to automorphic forms on other groups such as the non-arithmetic
\mathfrak{t}_{\mu}
as potentially rich sources of combinatorial information that have been overlooked. The present article is progress in this direction.

Having provided some context, we arrive at the main question motivating this article: what exactly is a quasiautomorphic form? We will answer this completely by constructing an isomorphism between quasiautomorphic forms and some much better understood objects. Precisely, for
U
a quasiautomorphic form, we determine a bijective mapping
U\,\leftrightarrow\,\mathbf{F}_{U}
such that
\mathbf{F}_{U}(z)
is a vector-valued automorphic form. In our terminology, this function
\mathbf{F}_{U}(z)
is a called a

. Now we quote the main result of the article:

Theorem 1

(Isomorphism theorem)
.

The function
U_{\mathfrak{t}_{\mu},w,r}(z)=U
is a quasiautomorphic form (with respect to
\mathfrak{t}_{\mu}
) of weight
w=2k
and depth
0\leq r\leq\frac{w}{2}
if and only if there is a vector-valued automorphic form
\mathbf{F}_{U}(z)
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{U}(Tz)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{r}(T)\,\mathbf{F}_{U}(z),

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{F}_{U}(Sz)}{z^{w-r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{r}(S)\,\mathbf{F}_{U}(z)

for generators
T_{\mathfrak{t}_{\mu}}=T
and
S_{\mathfrak{t}_{\mu}}=S
of
\mathfrak{t}_{\mu}
, where the multiplier system
\varepsilon_{r}
is given by natural, simple, and integral
(r+1)\times(r+1)
matrices.

It turns out that our multiplier system
\varepsilon_{r}
is generic in that it is independent of
U
; only
\varepsilon_{r}(T)
takes the parameter value
\varpi_{\mu}=2\cos\frac{\pi}{\mu}
which appears in the generator
T
.

Our Theorem now allows quasiautomorphic forms to fall under the general methods of Fuchsian
n
-th order linear differential equations whose solutions are invariant with respect to
T
and
S
. Conforming closely to existing convention, these might be called
automorphic linear differential equations
(or ALDEs), solutions of which are
vector-valued automorphic forms
(or vvafs) (we prefer the term vector-form). Our Theorem is then a natural and substantial strengthening of the existing, more familiar theory of
modular linear differential equations
(or MLDEs), solutions being
vector-valued modular forms
(or vvmfs). What is promising in our approach is that MLDEs already have a very large literature and thus a body of facts and techniques that predates quasiautomorphic forms considerably
[
10
]
, including already known connections to vertex operator algebras and conformal field theory (CFT). All of this is now available, via our bijection, as a toolkit for the study of quasiautomorphic forms.

Proposition 1

The relation

\displaystyle\frac{g_{\ell}(Sz)}{z^{w-r-\ell}}=\sum_{m=0}^{r-\ell}\binom{r-\ell}{m}g_{\ell+m}z^{r-\ell-m}

Proof.

Starting with

as defined at (

\displaystyle g_{\ell}(Sz)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{\ell}\sum_{m=0}^{r-\ell}\{r,\ell\}_{m}B_{U,\ell+m}(Sz)\,E_{2}^{m}(Sz)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{\ell}\sum_{m=0}^{r-\ell}\{r,\ell\}_{m}z^{w-2(\ell+m)}B_{U,\ell+m}(z)\,(z^{2}E_{2}(z)+Cz)^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{\ell}\sum_{m=0}^{r-\ell}\{r,\ell\}_{m}z^{w-2\ell}B_{U,\ell+m}(z)\,\left(E_{2}(z)+\frac{C}{z}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{\ell}\sum_{m=0}^{r-\ell}\{r,\ell\}_{m}z^{w-2\ell}B_{U,\ell+m}(z)\,\left(\sum^{m}_{k=0}\binom{m}{k}E_{2}^{m-k}(z)\left(\frac{C}{z}\right)^{k}\right)

hold. Apply properties of sums to get something more orderly, namely

\displaystyle C^{\ell}\sum_{m=0}^{r-\ell}\{r,\ell\}_{m}z^{w-2\ell}B_{U,\ell+m}(z)\,\left(\sum^{m}_{k=0}\binom{m}{k}E_{2}^{m-k}(z)\left(\frac{C}{z}\right)^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{\ell}\sum_{m=0}^{r-\ell}\sum^{m}_{k=0}\binom{m}{k}\{r,\ell\}_{m}z^{w-2\ell}B_{U,\ell+m}(z)E_{2}^{m-k}(z)\left(\frac{C}{z}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z^{w-2\ell}{C^{\ell}}\sum_{m=0}^{r-\ell}\sum^{m}_{k=0}\binom{m}{k}\{r,\ell\}_{m}B_{U,\ell+m}(z)E_{2}^{m-k}(z)\left(\frac{C}{z}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{r-\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{m}a_{k,m}=\sum_{k=0}^{r-\ell}\sum_{m=0}^{r-\ell-k}a_{k,m+k}

to get (after dropping arguments from notation of

\displaystyle z^{w-2\ell}C^{\ell}\sum_{p=0}^{r-\ell}\sum^{r-\ell-p}_{m=0}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+1}{p}\{r,\ell\}_{m+1}B_{U,\ell+m+p}E_{2}^{m}\left(\frac{C_{\mathfrak{t}_{\mu}}}{z}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle z^{w-\ell}\sum_{p=0}^{r-\ell}\binom{r-\ell}{p}g_{\ell+p}z^{-\ell-p}

Theorem 4

Let

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{r}\cong\Sym^{r}\varepsilon_{1}

Utilizing

When

Now set

\varepsilon_{1}(R)=\varepsilon_{1}(S^{-1})\varepsilon_{1}(T)=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\

is satisfied. We see that the characteristic polynomial of

\displaystyle X^{2}-(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})X+1

\zeta^{-1}

Now for

such that

\varepsilon_{r}(S)
and
\varepsilon_{r}(T)

\displaystyle e:=\left(\begin{matrix}1\\

\displaystyle\left(\Sym^{r}\varepsilon_{1}(S)\right)\cdot w_{k}=\frac{f^{k}\cdot(-e)^{r-k}}{k!\,(r-k)!}=(-1)^{r-k}\,w_{r-k}.

\left(\Sym^{r}\varepsilon_{1}(T)\right)\cdot w_{k}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{\ell!\,(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})^{k-\ell}}{(k-\ell)!}\,w_{\ell}.

Thus, in the basis
(w_{k})
, the
(\ell,k)

\Sym^{r}\varepsilon_{1}(T)
(for
\ell\leq k

\frac{\ell!\,(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})^{k-\ell}}{(k-\ell)!}.

On the other hand, since
(A_{r})_{i,j}=i\,\delta_{i,j+1}

(A_{r}^{m})_{i,j}=\frac{i!}{(i-m)!}\,\delta_{i,j+m}

\bigl[\exp\!\left((\zeta+\zeta^{-1})A_{r}\right)\bigr]_{\ell,k}=\frac{(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})^{\ell-k}}{(\ell-k)!}\cdot\frac{\ell!}{k!}\Bigg|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{lower}\\

Thus,

(\ell,k)

Renaming

\frac{\ell!\,(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})^{k-\ell}}{(k-\ell)!\,}=\frac{(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})^{k-\ell}}{(k-\ell)!}\cdot\ell!,

This shows that in the
