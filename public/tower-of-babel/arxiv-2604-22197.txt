Trace estimates and improved pointwise bounds for joint Eigenfunctions
Xianchao Wu and Xiao Xiao

Abstract
. For L 2 L^{2} -normalized joint eigenfunctions in a quantum integrable system, [ 8 ] gave polynomial improvements over the standard Hömander bounds for typical points. In this paper, we improve their result by establishing a sharp bound of h − n + k + 1 2 h^{\frac{-n+k+1}{2}} for the points satisfying a rank k k non-degeneracy condition.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary. By basic functional analysis, the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{g}:=\textit{div}_{g}\circ\nabla_{g}
has discrete spectrum
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=0,1,...}
. The corresponding eigenfunctions are
u_{j}
satisfying

-\Delta_{g}u_{j}=\lambda_{j}u_{j}.

We opt to use the semiclassical notation. Let
h_{j}:=\lambda_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, and omitting the subscript
j
, the above equation becomes

(-h^{2}\Delta_{g}-1)u_{h}=0.

(1.1)

The principal symbol of the operator
P(h)=-h^{2}\Delta_{g}-1
is

p(x,\xi)=|\xi|_{g(x)}^{2}-1.

(1.2)

The symbol
p(x,\xi)
is a smooth function on
T^{*}M
and can be viewed as a Hamiltonian. The associated Hamiltonian flow is exactly the lifted geodesic flow on
T^{*}M
. (Under the standard projection, this flow is the geodesic flow on
M
.) By the quantum-classical correspondence, (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) should influence each other in the following sense: The dynamical properties of the geodesic flow (
1.2
) of
(M,g)
influence the asymptotic properties of
u_{h}
in (
1.1
) as
h\to 0^{+}
.

There has been an perennial endeavor to understand the concentration of eigenfunctions. A compelling question is how spiky can a sequence of eigenfunction be: Seeking for an
L^{\infty}
bound of
L^{2}
normalized
u_{h}
in terms of
h
.

Beginning in the 1950’s, in a series of works by Levitan
[
13
]
, Avakumović
[
1
]
, and Hörmander
[
10
]
, the following estimate is obtained (known as the Hörmander bound):

\|u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}=O(h^{\frac{1-n}{2}}),

(1.3)

as
h\to 0
and (
1.3
) is saturated on the round sphere. This bound was improved to
o(h^{\frac{1-n}{2}})
by Safarov, Sogge, Toth, Zelditch and Galkowski
[
15
,
17
,
16
,
19
,
18
,
7
,
9
]
under various dynamical assumptions at
x
. When
(M,g)
has no conjugate points, a quantitative improvement

\|u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}=O(h^{\frac{1-n}{2}}/\sqrt{|\ln h|}),

(1.4)

as
h\to 0
, has been known since the classical work of Bérard
[
2
,
3
,
14
]
. In recent times, Canzani and Galkowski
[
4
,
5
]
developed the tool of geodesic beams to study the quantitative improvements without global geometric assumptions on
(M,g)
.

1.1.
Quantum completely integrable (QCI) system

A semiclassical pseudodifferential operator
P_{1}(h)
is called quantum completely integrable (QCI) if there exist functionally independent semiclassical pseudodifferential operators
P_{2}(h),\cdots,P_{n}(h)
such that

[P_{i}(h),P_{j}(h)]=0,\quad i,j=1,\cdots,n.

Given real numbers
E_{1}(h),\cdots,E_{n}(h)
, we want to study the
L^{2}
normalised functions
u_{h}
that solves
P_{j}(h)u_{h}=E_{j}(h)u_{h}
for all
j=1,\cdots,n
. We call them joint eigenfunctions corresponding to the joint eigenvalues
E(h):=(E_{1}(h),\cdots,E_{n}(h))\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of the system
\hat{\mathcal{P}}:=(P_{1}(h),\cdots,P_{n}(h))
.

The principal symbols
p_{j}\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}M)
of each operator
P_{j}(h)
give rise to a moment map that defines an associated classical integrable system. The moment map is denoted by

\mathcal{P}:=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}):T^{*}M\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

The regular set of
\mathcal{P}
, denoted by
(T^{*}M)_{reg}
, consists of points
(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M
such that

\dim span\{dp_{1}(x,\xi),\cdots,dp_{n}(x,\xi)\}=n.

As a global assumption, the classical system
\mathcal{P}
is
Liouville integrable
, i.e. the set
(T^{*}M)_{reg}
is an open dense subset of
T^{*}M
.

The above construction induces a Lagrangian torus fibration of
T^{*}M
. If
E=(E_{1},\cdots,E_{n})
is a regular value of
\mathcal{P}
, then by the Arnold-Liouville theorem,
\mathcal{P}^{-1}(E)
consists of a finite disjoint union of Lagrangian tori:

\mathcal{P}^{-1}(E)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}\Lambda_{j}(E).

Each
\Lambda_{j}(E)
is a Lagrangian submanifold of
T^{*}M
and is fixed by the torus action of
\mathcal{P}
. In fact, the joint flow

G_{t}(x,\xi):=\exp(t_{1}H_{p_{1}})\circ\cdots\circ\exp(t_{n}H_{p_{n}})(x,\xi)

is defined for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and the Hamiltonian flow
\exp(t_{j}H_{p_{j}})
preserves the level set
\{p_{j}=E_{j}\}
. Furthermore, the definition does not depend on the order of composition since we assumed
[P_{i},P_{j}]=0
, which implies
\{p_{i},p_{j}\}=0
.

The singular set
T^{*}M\setminus(T^{*}M)_{reg}
can be view as the degenerate Lagrangian tori.

Galkowski-Toth
[
8
]
investigated the pointwise bounds of
u_{h}
for the case
P_{1}(h)
being a Schrödinger operator. More explicitly, when
E_{1}
is a regular value of
p_{1}
and the system is of
Morse type
at point
x
, they established the following bound:

|u_{h}(x)|=O(I_{n}(h)),

(1.5)

here
I_{2}(h)=h^{-\frac{1}{4}}
,
I_{3}(h)=h^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\ln h|^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
I_{n\geq 4}(h)=h^{\frac{2-n}{2}}
.

1.2.
Main results

One purpose of this paper is to obtain a little
o
improvement of the pointwise bounds (
1.5
) under certain non-degeneracy condition. We achieve this in Theorem
2
. A useful tool is developed along the way (Theorem
1
), which we call a
quantitative trace estimate
. It is of independent interest.

We say that
p_{1}
is of real principal type on the hypersurface
p_{1}^{-1}(E_{1})
: if
E_{1}>0
is a regular value of
p_{1}
, for any
(x,\xi)\in p_{1}^{-1}(E_{1})
, the following inequality holds,

\partial_{\xi}p_{1}(x,\xi)\neq 0.

(1.6)

One sets

\mathcal{C}_{x}:=\{\xi\in T_{x}^{*}M:\,p_{1}(x,\xi)=E_{1}\}.

(1.7)

and define the
loop set
to be

\mathcal{L}_{x}:=\{\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{x}:\exists t<+\infty,\,\Phi_{t,p_{1}}(x,\xi)\in\mathcal{C}_{x}\},

(1.8)

where
\Phi_{t,p_{1}}
is the Hamiltonian flow of
p_{1}
. If furthermore, a covector loops back in a direction infinitly close to it original direction infinitly many times in the future and the past, we call it a
recurrent direction
. Define the
recurrent set
to be

\mathcal{R}_{x}:=\{\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{x}:\Big(\bigcap_{T>0}\overline{\bigcup_{t>T}\Phi_{t,p_{1}}(x,\xi)|_{\mathcal{C}_{x}}}\Big)\bigcap\Big(\bigcap_{T>0}\overline{\bigcup_{t>T}\Phi_{-t,p_{1}}(x,\xi)|_{\mathcal{C}_{x}}}\Big)\}.

(1.9)

The closure is under the subspace topology from
\mathcal{C}_{x}
.

It is the omega-limit of the following dynamical system: Define the return-time function
T_{x}:\mathcal{C}_{x}\to{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
,

T_{x}(\xi)=\inf_{t>0}\{t:\Phi_{t,p_{1}}(x,\xi)\in\mathcal{C}_{x}\}.

Then the return map
\widetilde{\Phi}_{x}:=\Phi_{T_{x},p_{1}}:\mathcal{L}_{x}\to\mathcal{C}_{x}
restricted to
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{x}:=\bigcap_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\widetilde{\Phi}^{k}(\mathcal{L}_{x})
is a dynamical system. By (
1.9
),
\xi\in\mathcal{R}_{x}
if and only if
\xi
is a limit point of the orbit of
\widetilde{\Phi}_{x}(\xi)
. See
[
16
]
and
[
9
]
.

Note that we have the following inclusion:

\mathcal{R}_{x}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{x}\subset\mathcal{L}_{x}\subset\mathcal{C}_{x}.

Definition
1
.

Suppose that
1\leq k\leq n-1
and
\Gamma
is a subset of
\mathcal{C}_{x}
. We say that
\mathcal{P}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
satisfies
rank
k
condition
at
x\in M
for
\Gamma
if

\text{ for any }\xi\in\Gamma,\quad\text{dim span}\{\partial_{\xi}p_{2},\,\partial_{\xi}p_{3},\cdots,\partial_{\xi}p_{n}\}=k

(1.10)

as a subspace in
T_{\xi}\mathcal{C}_{x}
, where
\partial_{\xi}
denotes the full gradient along
\mathcal{C}_{x}
.

For brevity, we say
\mathcal{P}|_{\Gamma}
is rank
k
at
x
.

Remark:

It is noteworthy that similar conditions were used in
[
6
, Def. 1.2]
, under the name “fiber rank
k
condition”, and in
[
21
, Thm 0.1 (2)]
, under the name “admissibility condition”. Our formulation is slightly more general in the sense that it specifies the energy level and the subset
\Gamma
of energy surface of
p_{1}
. Geometrically, condition (
1.10
) means that
\mathcal{P}^{-1}(E)\cap\Gamma
is a
k
dimensional submanifold which can be parameterized by
(\xi_{i_{1}},\dots,\xi_{i_{k}})
.

From now on, we always suppose that
\hat{\mathcal{P}}=\{\sqrt{-h^{2}\Delta},P_{2}(h),\dots,P_{n}(h)\}
is a QCI system on a smooth, compact manifold without boundary,
(M,g)
. Let
\{u_{h}\}
be the
L^{2}
-normalized joint eigenfunctions.
As a key ingredient of our paper, we can state our first theorem,

Theorem
1
.

Assume
A(x,hD)\in\Psi^{0}(M)
with symbol
a=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}h^{j}
. If
\mathcal{P}|_{\Gamma}
is rank

at
x
with
\Gamma=\mathcal{C}_{x}\cap{\operatorname{supp\,}}a
, then there exists a positive constant

so that for

,

|A(x,hD)u_{h}|^{2}=O(h^{-n+k+1}).

(1.11)

Remark:

Section
2
shows that Theorem
1
relies on the trace estimates, which also play a critical role in proving the local Weyl law (see
[
20
, Section 5]
).

Definition
2
.

We say that
\mathcal{P}=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n})
is
Morse
at
x\in M
if there exists a function
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\mathbb{R}})
, such that the principal symbol
q
of the operator
Q:=f(P_{1},\cdots,P_{n})
satisfies

q\big|_{\mathcal{C}_{x}}(\xi)\text{ is a Morse function in }\xi.

(1.12)

Remark:

For surfaces of revolution, let
\hat{\mathcal{P}}
be the standard QCI system (see Section 4). Then
\mathcal{P}
is
not
Morse at north and south poles, because
P_{2}
vanishes there and
f=f(P_{1})
, the symbol of whom, restricted to the energy surface of
p_{1}
, is a constant function.

Also for the triaxial ellipsoid, the standard QCI system is
not
Morse at the four umbilical points
[
8
]
.

Our main theorem is

Theorem
2
.

If
\mathcal{P}|_{\Gamma}
is rank
k

(

if

) at

with
\Gamma=\mathcal{R}_{x}\cap\bigcap\limits_{2\leq j\leq n}\{|p_{j}(x,\xi)-E_{j}|\leq\varepsilon\}

such that for any

(1)

Regarding the first conclusion (

1.3.
Example and superintegrability

Let
M=\mathbb{T}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, then
\mathcal{C}_{x}
is the round cosphere. Let
p_{2}=\xi_{2},\cdots,p_{n}=\xi_{n}
. On any pair of poles, at most one of
p_{j}
’s is degenerate. Therefore,
\mathcal{P}|_{\mathcal{C}_{x}}
only satisfies a rank
n-2
condition at any
x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
. As a consequence, (
1.14
) gives
|u_{h}(x)|=O(h^{-1/2})
, a bound that is not optimal. However, we can use a simple trick as follows:

Let
u_{h}
be the joint eigenfunction of

\hat{\mathcal{P}}=\{\sqrt{-h^{2}\Delta},-ih\partial_{x_{2}},-ih\partial_{x_{3}},\cdots,-ih\partial_{x_{n}}\}

with eigenvalue
E=(1,1,0,\cdots,0)
, then
\mathcal{P}
degenerates at
(|\xi|,\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{n})=(1,1,0,\cdots,0)
. But the same function
u_{h}
can be viewed as a joint function

\hat{\mathcal{Q}}=\{\sqrt{-h^{2}\Delta},-ih\partial_{x_{1}},-ih\partial_{x_{3}},\cdots,-ih\partial_{x_{n}}\}

with eigenvalue
F=(1,0,0,\cdots,0)
.
\mathcal{Q}
satisfies the rank
n-1
condition on
\Gamma^{\prime}
. As a consequence, (
1.14
) gives
|u_{h}(x)|=O(1)
which is sharp.

Figure 1.
On the left: At
\xi
, the system
\mathcal{P}
only satisfies a rank
n-2
condition; On the right: At
\xi
, the system
\mathcal{Q}
satisfies the rank
n-1
condition. The same function

is a joint eigenfunction of both
\hat{\mathcal{P}}
and
\hat{\mathcal{Q}}
.

This method is possible only if there are at least
n
independent, commuting operators that also commute with the Laplacian. We will not delve deeper into this issue in this paper.

1.4.
Plan of the paper

Section
2
proves Theorem
1
by a Fourier inversion of the operator and computing the on-diagonal Schwartz kernel, which we call the trace estimate (à la Sogge). It is an important technical ingredient of this paper. In section
3

by taking a closer look at the recurrent set and building test symbols accordingly. We conclude by a study of examples on surfaces of revolutions in section

4.1.
Surface of revolutions

We study the surface of revolution as an example to demonstrate several points mentioned in the Introduction. Proposition
4.1
shows the geometric meaning of the rank
1
condition in this simple case; Proposition
4.2
and
4.3
clarifies the point made in Remark (1) of Theorem
2
. We believe that some constructions can be generalized to Riemannian warped products.

In general, a surface of revolution embedded in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is given by parametrization

\begin{cases}x=f(t)\cos\varphi\\
y=f(t)\sin\varphi\\
z=g(t),\end{cases}

(4.1)

for
t\in[t_{-},t_{+}]
and
\varphi\in[0,2\pi]
. Its first fundamental form is

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=e_{t}\cdot e_{t}=f^{\prime}(t)^{2}+g^{\prime}(t)^{2}

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=e_{t}\cdot e_{\varphi}=0

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=e_{\varphi}\cdot e_{\varphi}=f^{2}(t)

and we want to arrange for
f^{\prime}(t)^{2}+g^{\prime}(t)^{2}=1
, so that the metric is
ds^{2}=dt^{2}+f^{2}(t)d\varphi^{2}
. We call this metric
g
, not to be confused with the above function
g
. One can compute the Laplacian in the coordinate system
(t,\varphi)
,

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\sum\partial_{j}(\sqrt{|g|}\,g^{jk}\partial_{k})

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}^{2}+f^{-2}(t)\partial_{\varphi}^{2}+\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}\partial_{t}.

(4.2)

The principal symbol is then
|\xi|^{2}_{g}=\xi_{t}^{2}+f^{-2}(t)\xi_{\varphi}^{2}
. The quantum integrable system is defined by
\mathcal{P}=(p_{1},p_{2})
, where
p_{1}=|\xi|^{2}_{g}
,
p_{2}=\xi_{\varphi}
.

We will make extra assumptions in order to make the problem simpler. We choose
f
such that
f(t_{-})=f(t_{+})=0
and assume that
f
makes the metric smooth at the poles. We also assume that
f^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})\neq 0
for the
t_{0}
where
f^{\prime}(t_{0})=0
. The assumption guarantees that the set
\{(t,\varphi)\in M:f^{\prime}(t)=0\}
consists of disjoint union of horizontal circles. For simplicity, we may assume there is only one such circle, which we refer to as the
equator
.

There is a simple characterization of the rank
1
condition in this case.

Proposition
4.1
.

Let
M
be a surface of revolution as above, and
x\in M
not a pole nor on the equator. Then
\mathcal{P}
satisfies the rank
1
condition on
\Gamma=\{(\xi_{t},\xi_{\varphi})\in S^{*}_{x}M:\xi_{t}\neq 0\}
at
x
.

Proof.

At
x=(t,\varphi)
, the coshpere is an ellipse
\mathcal{C}_{x}=\{\xi:\xi_{t}^{2}+\frac{\xi_{\varphi}^{2}}{f^{2}(t)}=1\}
. Then,
{p_{2}}|_{\mathcal{C}_{x}}=\xi_{\varphi}|_{\mathcal{C}_{x}}
is critical only if
\xi_{t}=0
, that is, when the covector is vertical. Then,
\mathcal{P}
satisfies the rank
1
condition on
\{\xi_{t}\neq 0\}
at
x
.
∎

Remark:

Note that the trick in Section
1.3
cannot be used here.

The next proposition characterizes the recurrent set of certain surfaces of revolution.

Proposition
4.2
.

Let
M
be a surface of revolution as above with a real-analytic profile function
f
, and that
g
is not a Zoll metric. Let
x\in M
be a point on the equator, then the recurrent set
\mathcal{R}_{x}
is a dense subset in
S^{*}_{x}M
and
\mu(\mathcal{R}_{x})=0
, where
\mu
is the induced Liouville measure on
S^{*}_{x}M
.

Remark:

In this case,
\mathcal{R}_{x}=\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{x}=\mathcal{L}_{x}
. Also, the conclusion holds actually for any
x
that is not a pole. To keep the proof short, we assume that it lies on the equator.

We can almost produce a one-line proof following
[
11
, Thm 3.5.24]
, which claims that the geodesic flow on a surface of revolution is ”equivalent” to certain linear torus action, the slope of which is explicit. However, we opt for an elementary proof as below.

Figure 2.
The relation of the equator (red) and the geodesic with initial angle
\psi
(blue) in the proof.
Left: trajectories on the surface. Middle: canonical coordinates
(t,\varphi)
. Right: geodesic normal coordinates
(s,\psi)
.

Proof.

We invoke the Clairaut relation that holds on every surface of revolution: For a geodesic
l
, the quantity
\gamma=f(t)\sin(\psi(t))
is a constant along
l
, where
\psi(t)
is the angle between the geodesic
l
and the meridian it intersects with at
(t,\varphi)
. Write
\psi=\psi(t_{0})
for the initial angle. It is known that the geodesic oscillates within a tubular region near the equator (
[
11
]
).

Fix
x
on the equator. Without loss of generality, let
\varphi(x)=0
. For a geodesic
l
starting at
x
with angle
\psi
to the meridian, it is parametrized by arc length so that
l(s)=(t(s),\varphi(s))
,
t^{\prime}(s)^{2}+f^{\prime}(t(s))^{2}\varphi^{\prime}(s)^{2}=1
. It satisfies the geodesic equations

\begin{cases}\frac{d^{2}t}{ds^{2}}=f(t(s))\,\frac{df}{ds}\left(\frac{d\varphi}{ds}\right)^{2}\\
\frac{d}{ds}\left(f^{2}(t(s))\frac{d\varphi}{ds}\right)=0\end{cases}

(4.3)

subject to the initial conditions

\displaystyle\varphi(0)=0,\quad t(0)=0,\quad\frac{d\varphi}{ds}(0)=\frac{\sin\psi}{f(t_{0})},\quad\frac{dt}{ds}(0)=\cos\psi.

(4.4)

The only free variable in the initial conditions is
\psi
. Using the fundamental theorem of ODE, one can write a real-analytic function
L:(\psi,s)\mapsto(t(\psi,s),\varphi(\psi,s))=(L_{1},L_{2})
, which serve as a
C^{\omega}
change-of-coordinates into the geodesic normal coordinates
(\psi,s)
. Then, the equator as a curve
\{(t,\varphi):t=0\}
can be expressed as
\{(\psi,s):s=S(\psi)\}
, a graph of some real-analytic function
S
. Therefore, the function
\Phi(\psi)=L_{2}(\psi,S(\psi))
is real-analytic, possibly multi-valued function, which denotes the change of longitude when the geodesic
l
returns to the equator. We take the smallest positive value as a well-defined branch, and still denote by
\Phi
. Its inverse
\Phi^{-1}
is also real-analytic in a suitable domain.

The geodesic is recurrent if and only if
\Phi(\psi)
is a rational multiple of
2\pi
. Let
B:=\{\text{rational multiples of }2\pi\}
. Suppose
\exists A\subset S^{1},\,\mu(A)>0
such that
\Phi(A)=B
. Then
\exists A^{\prime}\subset A
,
\mu(A^{\prime})>0
,
\Phi(A^{\prime})=\{c\}
. By analyticity,
\Phi=const.
Since
M
possess rotational symmetry, we get the same constant for every geodesic. It forces the manifold to be a Zoll surface.

By assumtion, the surface of revolution is not Zoll. Then, the inverse image
\Phi^{-1}(B)
must have zero measure. Denseness follows from the fact that
\Phi^{-1}
is
C^{\omega}
and that
B
is dense.

∎

We mention a method for constructing a sequence of joint quasimodes from a sequence of eigenfunctions of its horizontal factor. We believe that such a construction also exists in certain Riemannian warped products.

Proposition
4.3
.

Let
M
be a surface of revolution as above with profile function
f
such that
f\leq 1
, and
\frac{|f^{\prime}|}{|1-f^{2}|}
is bounded where
f=1
. Then there exists an
O(h)
-quasimode
u_{h}
such that
|u_{h}(t_{0},\varphi)|\simeq h^{-1/4}
and
|u_{h}(t,\varphi)|\simeq e^{-C(t,\varphi)/h}
for
f(t)\neq 1
.

Proof.

Separation of variables: Let
u_{\lambda}(t,\varphi)=T(t)e^{i\lambda\varphi}
. We call the following
highest-weight quasimode
:

u_{\lambda}(t,\varphi)=\lambda^{1/4}\exp\{\lambda\int\sqrt{\frac{1-f^{2}}{f^{2}}}dt\}\,e^{i\lambda\varphi}.

(4.5)

One can check by direct computation that

\displaystyle\Delta u_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\Big(\partial_{t}^{2}+f^{-2}(t)\partial_{\varphi}^{2}+\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}\partial_{t}\Big)u_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\Big(-\lambda^{2}-\lambda\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)^{2}\sqrt{1-f(t)^{2}}}+\lambda\frac{f^{\prime}(t)\sqrt{1-f(t)^{2}}}{f(t)^{2}}\Big)u_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\Big(-\lambda^{2}-\frac{\lambda f^{\prime}(t)}{\sqrt{1-f(t)^{2}}}\Big)u_{\lambda}

(4.6)

At points where
f(t_{0})=1
, we expect that on the equator
f^{\prime}(t_{0})=0
at correct order s.t. the second term does not blow up. It is guaranteed by our assumption.

∎

Remark:

Back to the round sphere, we let
f=\cos t
, then

\exp\{\lambda\int\sqrt{\frac{|1-f^{2}|}{f^{2}}}dt\}=\exp\{\lambda\int|\tan t|dt\}=\exp\{\lambda\ln|\cos t|\}=(\cos t)^{\lambda}.

(in this convention,
t\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
, so
\cos t>0
.)

Combining the Proposition
4.2

, we exemplified Remark (1) of Theorem
