A third-order multi-moment cell-centered Lagrangian scheme for hydrodynamics with an accurate 2D nodal solver
Xiaoteng Zhang 1,2, Xun Wang 3, Zhijun Shen 4,5, Chao Yang 1,2

Abstract
This paper presents a novel high-order cell-centered Lagrangian scheme for 2D compressible hydrodynamics by bridging the multi-moment constrained finite volume method (MCV) [ 16 , 51 , 52 ] with a nodal Riemann solver. This scheme (denoted by LMCV) not only maintains high-order accuracy as MCV but also inherits the conservation and robust properties of the nodal Riemann solver. On the one hand, the MCV employs and evolves both the point-values (PV) at cell vertexes and the volume-integrated averages (VIA) on computational mesh, which ensures the rigorous numerical conservation and establishes an adequate foundation for the computation of Lagrangian fluxes with high accuracy. On the other hand, we developed a 2D Riemann solver based on EUCCLHYD [ 24 ] , it takes fully advantage of numerical formulations from high-order scheme and accomplishes the compatibility between the mesh movement and numerical fluxes. The main new features of the solver are the introduction of a new set of jump and balance conditions. The jump condition provides a high-accurate formulation linking the surface pressure of each cell to its nodal velocity, while the balance condition ensures nodal conservation and stabilizes the velocity field without losing accuracy. More intriguing is that our nodal solver can be regarded as a natural high-order extension of the HLLC and the HLLC-2D [ 41 ] solvers. The comparison between these solvers better demonstrates our innovative approach in addressing the difficulties encountered in constructing 2D high-order Lagrangian schemes. A variety of numerical experiments are carried out to illustrate the accuracy and robustness of the algorithm.

Introduction

The hypersonic flows problems are frequently found in the two main applications: design research on hypersonic flight vehicles and assessment studies of their aerodynamic and aerothermal characteristics. These problems are often too complicated for analytical investigation and present significant challenges for the experimental observation or measurement of physical variables. Numerical simulation provides effective methods and insights for the interpretation of compressible flow phenomena across virtually all scales, ranging from laboratory experiments to astrophysical environments. Since its inception in the late 1950s

Representing by essentially non-oscillatory scheme (ENO)

Despite improvements and encouraging results in recent years, a number of issues still remain. Most of high-order schemes are formulated in Euler framework for single-material, which remains a primary challenge: capturing of contact discontinuities in multi-material fluid flows.

Many interface-capturing methods, such as marker particle and front-tracking approaches, are developed based on Lagrangian techniques

Based on these first-order schemes, high-order Lagrangian schemes are developed. Take two-dimension for example, in order to track fluid motion in a velocity field with high-accuracy, most of existing schemes introduce curvilinear meshes, which require extra shape-control points on each edge. As a result, velocities and fluxes need be solved at both cell corners and edges. Several strategies are developed in this case.

In work by Vilar et al.
[
47
]
,
the multidimensional approximate Riemann solver by Maire et al.
[
24
,
25
]
is used to solve the Riemann problem at the corner, while a 1D Riemann solver is used at the middle of each edge. Morgan et al. used this same approach for quadratic triangular cells
[
30
]
with two different Riemann solvers. A weakness of this approach is that the number of inputs to the Riemann problem varies along each edge. The inconsistency between the Riemann solvers may give rise to some spurious mesh motion or even self intersection. The above issues have been mentioned in various articles, some of which use post-processing techniques to cure the ill-conditioned velocity field. In work by Cheng and Shu
[
4
]
, a velocity limitation is introduced to prevent cell degeneration without destroying the third-order accuracy of the numerical scheme. Similarly, a velocity filter is proposed by Morgan et al.
[
31
]
to dissipate spurious mesh motion by modifying the velocity reconstructions. The reconstructed velocities are adjusted as a function of the difference between the resulting vertex Riemann velocities and a linear fit to these velocities. The strategy works well for many problems with strong shocks.
In an alternative perspective, several articles are investigating the treatment of the underlying causes of spurious motion through pre-processing techniques. In work by Xiaodong Liu et al.
[
22
]
, each quadratic quadrilateral cell is carefully reconstructed into four quadrilateral subcells by subcell mesh stabilization (SMS). The middle point of each edge is surrounded by four subcells so that it is similar to the vertex at the cell corner, which makes the multidimensional Riemann solver consistently applicable at every vertices. This SMS scheme enables stable mesh motion and accurate solutions in the context of a Lagrangian high-order DG method that is up to third-order with quadratic cells.

In this paper, we develop a novel high-order cell-centered Lagrangian scheme for 2D compressible hydrodynamics based on MCV, which achieves third-order accuracy using an augmented 2D nodal Riemann solver. The MCV discretizes the governing equations by both PVs and VIAs, which are evolved separately by Lax-Friedrichs method and our nodal solver. The main features of this solver are the introduction of a new set of jump and balance conditions, which not only provide a formulation between surface pressure and nodal velocity with high-order accuracy but also stabilizes the nodal velocity field without losing accuracy or breaking nodal conservation. Furthermore, 3rd order TVD Runge-Kutta method and several limiting strategies are employed for accuracy and stability. The combination of MCV and our nodal solver, enables the cell-centered Lagrangian method, for the first time, to acquire third-order accuracy without introducing curvilinear meshes.

6
Procedures

This section introduces the additional procedures required to complete this scheme.

6.1
Limiting procedure

In order to suppress the numerical oscillations, limiting procedure is defined as follows.

Firstly, a smoothness indicator is introduced for each cell
\omega^{i}
to measure the discontinuities around the cell. To that end, we use the total boundary variations (TBV) across cell boundaries

\displaystyle\mathrm{TBV}^{i}(\varphi)=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}^{i}}\left[\frac{1}{|e_{ij}|}\int_{e_{ij}}(\varphi^{i}-\varphi^{j})\,\mathrm{d}s\right]^{\beta}}{\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}^{i}}\left(\bar{\varphi}^{i}-\bar{\varphi}^{j}\right)^{\beta}},

(62)

where
\mathcal{N}^{i}
is the index set of neighbor cells of
\omega^{i}
,
e_{ij}=\omega^{i}\cap\omega^{j}
,
\varphi
is a scalar physical field used to detect discontinuities, and power
\beta
is used to control the indicator sensitivty. In our test, we choose
\varphi=\rho
and
\beta=4
for better numerical preformance. Similar to
[
3
,
17
]
, the smoothness indicator is defined as

\displaystyle S^{i}=\frac{1-\mathrm{TBV}^{i}(\varphi)}{\max\left\{\mathrm{TBV}^{i}(\varphi),\epsilon\right\}},

(63)

with
\epsilon=10^{-16}
to prevent zero-division. It is noted that
S^{i}
increases to infinite for
\mathrm{TBV}^{i}(\varphi)\to 0
in smooth region, and remains small for discontinuities occured. As a result, a problem-dependent cutoff number
S_{c}
is used as threshold and the cell
\omega^{i}
with
S^{i}<S_{c}
is considered as non-smooth cell, where the linear reconstruction is applied.

Secondly, for each non-smooth cell
\omega^{i}
, a local linear reconstruction is applied as

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}^{i}(\boldsymbol{x})\leftarrow\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{avg}}^{i}+(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathbf{U})^{i}\cdot(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{b}),

(64)

where
\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{b}
is centroid of
\omega^{i}
, so the linear reconstruction is conserved. The gradient
(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathbf{U})^{i}
is acquired by solving

\displaystyle\underset{(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathbf{U})^{i}}{\arg\min}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}^{i}}\left|\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{avg}}^{i}-\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{avg}}^{j}+(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\mathbf{U})^{i}\cdot(\boldsymbol{x}^{j}_{b}-\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{b})\right|^{2}.

(65)

Thirdly, MLP
[
34
]
reconstruction is used to suppress numerical oscillations causing by false local extremes. For each cell
\omega^{i}
and conserved scalar
\varphi
, we update
\varphi^{i}(\boldsymbol{x})
by

\displaystyle\varphi^{i}(\boldsymbol{x})\leftarrow\bar{\varphi}^{i}+\lambda_{\mathrm{MLP}}\left(\varphi^{i}(\boldsymbol{x})-\bar{\varphi}^{i}\right),

(66)

with
\lambda_{\mathrm{MLP}}=\min_{r=1}^{4}\lambda_{r}
and

\displaystyle\lambda_{r}=\begin{cases}f_{\lim}\left(\frac{\varphi_{\max,r}-\bar{\varphi}^{i}}{\varphi^{i}_{r}-\bar{\varphi}^{i}}\right),&\varphi^{i}_{r}-\bar{\varphi}^{i}>0,\\
f_{\lim}\left(\frac{\varphi_{\min,r}-\bar{\varphi}^{i}}{\varphi^{i}_{r}-\bar{\varphi}^{i}}\right),&\varphi^{i}_{r}-\bar{\varphi}^{i}<0,\\
1,&\varphi^{i}_{r}-\bar{\varphi}^{i}=0,\end{cases}

(67)

where
\varphi_{\max,r}
and
\varphi_{\min,r}
are the maximum and minimum of
\bar{\varphi}^{j}
among the adacent cells of
\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r}
. In this work, we use the Michalak-Gooch limiter
[
28
]
as

\displaystyle f_{\lim}(x)=\begin{cases}-\frac{4}{27}x^{3}+x,&x<\frac{3}{2},\\
1,&x\geq\frac{3}{2}.\end{cases}

(68)

It should be noticed that the MLP reconstruction is related to the selection of conserved scalars
\varphi
.
In order to maintain symmetry, we choose
\varphi\in\{\rho,\rho{\bf v}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{n},\rho{\bf v}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{t},\rho E\}
for
\omega^{i}
with
\{\mathbf{e}_{n},\mathbf{e}_{t}\}
as orthonormal basis, where
\mathbf{e}_{n}\mathrel{/\negmedspace/}\bar{{\bf v}}^{i}
.

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{\textsc{pv}}}^{(1)}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{\textsc{pv}}}^{n}+\Delta t^{n}L_{\mathrm{\textsc{pv}}}(\mathbf{U}^{n},\boldsymbol{x}^{n}),

Stage 2)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}^{(2)}=\frac{3}{4}\boldsymbol{x}^{n}+\frac{1}{4}\left[\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)}+\Delta t^{n}\tilde{\bf v}(\mathbf{U}^{(1)},\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)})\right],

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{\textsc{via}}}^{(2)}=\frac{3}{4}\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{\textsc{via}}}^{n}+\frac{1}{4}\left[\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{\textsc{via}}}^{(1)}+\Delta t^{n}L_{\mathrm{\textsc{via}}}(\mathbf{U}^{(1)},\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)})\right],

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{\textsc{pv}}}^{(2)}=\frac{3}{4}\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{\textsc{pv}}}^{n}+\frac{1}{4}\left[\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{\textsc{pv}}}^{(1)}+\Delta t^{n}L_{\mathrm{\textsc{pv}}}(\mathbf{U}^{(1)},\boldsymbol{x}^{(1)})\right],

Stage 3)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}^{n+1}=\frac{1}{3}\boldsymbol{x}^{n}+\frac{2}{3}\left[\boldsymbol{x}^{(2)}+\Delta t^{n}\tilde{\bf v}(\mathbf{U}^{(2)},\boldsymbol{x}^{(2)})\right],

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{\textsc{via}}}^{n+1}=\frac{1}{3}\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{\textsc{via}}}^{n}+\frac{2}{3}\left[\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{\textsc{via}}}^{(2)}+\Delta t^{n}L_{\mathrm{\textsc{via}}}(\mathbf{U}^{(2)},\boldsymbol{x}^{(2)})\right],

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{\textsc{pv}}}^{n+1}=\frac{1}{3}\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{\textsc{pv}}}^{n}+\frac{2}{3}\left[\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{\textsc{pv}}}^{(2)}+\Delta t^{n}L_{\mathrm{\textsc{pv}}}(\mathbf{U}^{(2)},\boldsymbol{x}^{(2)})\right],

where time step
\Delta t^{n}
satisfies CFL condition

\displaystyle\Delta t^{n}\leq\sigma_{e}\dfrac{m^{i}}{\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{4}L^{i}_{r,r+1}\left({\frac{1}{6}}\alpha^{i}_{r}+\frac{2}{3}\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}+{\frac{1}{6}}\alpha^{i}_{r+1}\right)},\;\forall i=1,2,3,\cdots,I,

(69)

with CFL conffcient
\sigma_{e}=0.2
.

6.3
Summary of solution procedure

The whole solution procedure at
n
-th time step could be summarized as follows:

1.

According to Section
3.2
and
6.1
, physical field
\mathbf{U}^{n}(\boldsymbol{x})
is reconstructed and limited with VIA and PV values
\mathbf{U}^{n}=(\mathbf{U}^{n}_{\mathrm{avg}},\mathbf{U}^{n}_{\mathrm{\textsc{pv}}})
and mesh
\boldsymbol{x}^{n}
at time
t^{n}
.

2.

With physical field
\mathbf{U}^{n}(\boldsymbol{x})
on mesh
\boldsymbol{x}^{n}
, time step
\Delta t^{n}
is determined by (
69
).

3.

Let
k=0,\mathbf{U}^{(0)}=\mathbf{U}^{n},\boldsymbol{x}^{(0)}=\boldsymbol{x}^{n}
, the Runge-Kutta stages are performed successively as follows:

(a)

Physical field
\mathbf{U}^{(k)}(\boldsymbol{x})
is reconstructed and limited based on
\mathbf{U}^{(k)},\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}
;

(b)

L_{\mathrm{avg}}(\mathbf{U}^{(k)},\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}),L_{\mathrm{\textsc{pv}}}(\mathbf{U}^{(k)},\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)})
and
\tilde{\bf v}(\mathbf{U}^{(k)},\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)})
are computed based on Section
3.3
and
3.4
.

(c)

\mathbf{U}^{(k+1)}
and
\boldsymbol{x}^{(k+1)}
are updated by third-order SSP RK method as Section
6.2
shows, let
k=k+1
.

4.

The result of SSP RK method is denoted as
\mathbf{U}^{n+1}=\mathbf{U}^{(k)},\boldsymbol{x}^{n+1}=\boldsymbol{x}^{(k)}
, and the time is updated by
t^{n+1}=t^{n}+\Delta t^{n}
.

8
Conclusion

We presented a novel third-order cell-centered Lagrangian scheme for hydrodynamics called LMCV on quadrilateral meshes using an augmented fourth-order 2D nodal solver. In our method,
the physical field is discretized into moments of two kind (i.e. VIA and PV) and approximated by polynomial reconstruction on reference cells. Based on augmented jump condition and balance condition which take the physical field on cell boundaries into full consideration, our new 2D nodal solver is capable to deliver conservative and accurate fluxes and consistent nodal velocities, lays a solid foundation for the robustness and high accuracy of our scheme.

Further more, a set of challenging test cases are calculated to verify the capacity of LMCV. Firstly, three various smooth tests are performed, the results confirm the three-order accuracy of LMCV across various flow fields and initial mesh configurations. Secondly, four flows with strong shocks are used to demonstrate the robustness of our scheme without losing accuracy facing discontinuities. To the best of our knowledge, LMCV is the first cell-centered Lagrangian scheme which achieves third-order accuracy on quadrilateral meshes without introducing curved edges.

Appendix A
. Proof of specific volume accuracy

This appendix primarily illustrates the specific volume error resulting from straight edges on quadrilateral mesh.

For a set of uniformly refined quadrilateral mesh
\left\{M_{h}\right\}_{h\in\mathbb{R}}
and a smooth field
\mathbf{U}
defined on physical domain, we assume that every vertices in
M_{h}
flow EXACTLY according the velocity field
{\bf v}
given by
\mathbf{U}
, that is
\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}={\bf v}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})={\bf v}^{i}_{r}
. Consider a quadrilateral cell
\omega^{i}
, let

(\tau^{i}_{\mathrm{ref}})_{t}=\frac{1}{m_{i}}\int_{\partial\omega^{i}}{\bf v}\cdot{\bf n}\mathrm{d}l,

be the exact change rate of specific volume according to Eqn. (
33
), while the actual change rate of specific volume is

(\tau^{i})_{t}=\frac{1}{m_{i}}\sum_{r=1}^{4}L^{i}_{r,r+1}\left({\frac{1}{2}}{\bf v}^{i}_{r}+{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\right)\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1},

since the edge stay straight. Our goal is to prove

(\tau^{i}_{\mathrm{ref}})_{t}-(\tau^{i})_{t}=\mathcal{O}(h^{2}).

To be more specific, mesh
M_{h}
in
\left\{M_{h}\right\}_{h\in\mathbb{R}}
is defined by its nodes
\boldsymbol{x}_{k,l}
with
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
, and we assumed that the refinement follows
\boldsymbol{x}_{k,l}=\boldsymbol{\phi}(kh,lh)
with
\boldsymbol{\phi}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a smooth homeomorphism.

Firstly, the error at each edge
e=\overline{\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r}\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r+1}}
can be quantified as follows. Let
u(\boldsymbol{x})={\bf v}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}
,
u^{i}_{r}=u(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})
and
u^{i}_{r+1}=u(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r+1})
, then the error is computed based on trapezoidal rule as

\displaystyle\int_{e}{\bf v}\cdot{\bf n}\mathrm{d}l-L^{i}_{r,r+1}\left({\frac{1}{2}}{\bf v}^{i}_{r}+{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\right)\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{e}u\mathrm{d}l-L^{i}_{r,r+1}\left({\frac{1}{2}}u^{i}_{r}+{\frac{1}{2}}u^{i}_{r+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

Appendix B
. Proof of flux accuracy

This appendix primarily illustrates the relationship between flux accuracy and the movement of the grid.

For a set of uniformly refined quadrilateral mesh
\left\{M_{h}\right\}_{h\in\mathbb{R}}
and a smooth field
\mathbf{U}
, Simpson’s rule provides a 4th-order accurate approximation for the Lagrangian flux across the edge
[r,r+1]
of cell
\omega^{i}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{S}=\begin{bmatrix}-\left({\frac{1}{6}}{\bf v}^{i}_{r}+{\frac{2}{3}}{\bf v}^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}+{\frac{1}{6}}{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\right)\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}\\
\left({\frac{1}{6}}P^{i}_{r}+{\frac{2}{3}}P^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}+{\frac{1}{6}}P^{i}_{r+1}\right){\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}\\
\left({\frac{1}{6}}P^{i}_{r}{\bf v}^{i}_{r}+{\frac{2}{3}}P^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}{\bf v}^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}+{\frac{1}{6}}P^{i}_{r+1}{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\right)\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}\end{bmatrix}

(75)

Based on the augmented nodal solver, the Lagrangian flux across the edge
[r,r+1]
of cell
\omega^{i}
is:

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{M}=\begin{bmatrix}-{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}+\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\right)\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}\\
{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\widetilde{P}^{i}_{r,r+{\frac{1}{2}}}+\widetilde{P}^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}},r+1}\right){\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}\\
{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\widetilde{P}^{i}_{r,r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}+\widetilde{P}^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}},r+1}\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\right)\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}\end{bmatrix}.

(76)

Our goal is to prove

\mathbf{F}_{M}-\mathbf{F}_{S}=\mathcal{O}(h^{4}).

To be more specific, mesh
M_{h}
in
\left\{M_{h}\right\}_{h\in\mathbb{R}}
is defined by its nodes
\boldsymbol{x}_{k,l}
with
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
, and we assumed that the refinement follows
\boldsymbol{x}_{k,l}=\boldsymbol{\phi}(kh,lh)
with
\boldsymbol{\phi}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a smooth homeomorphism.

The key step to prove is to explore the properties of
\delta\tilde{\bf v}
as below.

Lemma 2

(Properties of
\delta\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}=\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}-\mathbf{v}
)
.

For a set of uniformly refined quadrilateral mesh
\left\{M_{h}\right\}_{h\in\mathbb{R}}
and a smooth field
\mathbf{U}
, the nodal velocity correction
\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}
satisfies:

1.

\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}=\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
.

2.

\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}-\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}=\mathcal{O}(h^{3})
.

3.

{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}\cdot\left(\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}+\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\right)=2w^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}+\mathcal{O}(h^{4})
.

Proof of Lemma
2
.

In order to fully utilize the advantages of quadrilateral meshes, a new set of notation is introduced for the proof of this lemma. Let
\boldsymbol{x}_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l},\boldsymbol{x}_{k,l+{\frac{1}{2}}}
be the middle points of edge
\overline{\boldsymbol{x}_{k,l}\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1,l}}
and
\overline{\boldsymbol{x}_{k,l}\boldsymbol{x}_{k,l+1}}
. For any smooth physical field
\varphi
,
\varphi_{k,l}
,
\varphi_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}
and
\varphi_{k,l+{\frac{1}{2}}}
are the field
\varphi
at point
\boldsymbol{x}_{k,l}
,
\boldsymbol{x}_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}
and
\boldsymbol{x}_{k,l+{\frac{1}{2}}}
separately. The unit normal vector and length of
\overline{\boldsymbol{x}_{k,l}\boldsymbol{x}_{k+1,l}}
is denoted as
{\bf n}_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}
and
L_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}
.
{\bf n}_{k,l+{\frac{1}{2}}}
and
L_{k,l+{\frac{1}{2}}}
to
\overline{\boldsymbol{x}_{k,l}\boldsymbol{x}_{k,l+1}}
is defined similarly (see Fig.
21
).

Subsequently, reconsider Eqn. (
44
) for node
\boldsymbol{x}_{k,l}
with the smoothness of fields, one can get

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathcal{N}(k,l)}L_{m}\left[\left(\frac{1}{3}\alpha_{k,l}+\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{m}\right)(\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}\cdot{\bf n}_{m})-\alpha_{m}w_{m}\right]{\bf n}_{m}=\boldsymbol{0},\quad\mathcal{N}(k,l)=\left\{(k\pm\tfrac{1}{2},l),\ (k,l\pm\tfrac{1}{2})\right\},

(77)

where
w_{k\pm{\frac{1}{2}},l}\coloneqq{\frac{2}{3}}\left({\bf v}_{k\pm{\frac{1}{2}},l}-{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf v}_{k,l}-{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf v}_{k\pm 1,l}\right)\cdot{\bf n}_{k\pm{\frac{1}{2}},l}
and
w_{k,l\pm{\frac{1}{2}}}\coloneqq{\frac{2}{3}}\left({\bf v}_{k,l\pm{\frac{1}{2}}}-{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf v}_{k,l}-{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf v}_{k,l\pm 1}\right)\cdot{\bf n}_{k,l\pm{\frac{1}{2}}}
.
Rewriting (
77
) into a linear system as

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{k,l}\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}-\boldsymbol{b}_{k,l}=\boldsymbol{0},

where

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{M}_{k,l}=\sum_{m\in\mathcal{N}(k,l)}L_{m}\left(\frac{1}{3}\alpha_{k,l}+\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{m}\right){\bf n}_{m}{\bf n}_{m}^{\top}=\alpha_{k,l}\left\{L_{kk}\left[{\bf n}^{0}_{k,l}{\bf n}^{0,\top}_{k,l}+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\right]+L_{ll}\left[{\bf n}^{1}_{k,l}{\bf n}^{1,\top}_{k,l}+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\right]\right\},\\
&\boldsymbol{b}_{k,l}=\sum_{m\in\mathcal{N}(k,l)}L_{m}\alpha_{m}w_{m}{\bf n}_{m}=\frac{\alpha_{k,l}}{2}\left\{L_{kk}\left(w_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}+w_{k-{\frac{1}{2}},l}\right)\left[{\bf n}^{0}_{k,l}+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\right]+L_{ll}\left(w_{k,l+{\frac{1}{2}}}+w_{k,l-{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\left[{\bf n}^{1}_{k,l}+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\right]\right\},\end{aligned}\right.

with

\displaystyle L_{kk}=L_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}+L_{k-{\frac{1}{2}},l},\quad L_{ll}=L_{k,l+{\frac{1}{2}}}+L_{k,l-{\frac{1}{2}}},\quad{\bf n}^{0}_{k,l}=\frac{{\bf n}_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}+{\bf n}_{k-{\frac{1}{2}},l}}{|{\bf n}_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}+{\bf n}_{k-{\frac{1}{2}},l}|},\quad{\bf n}^{1}_{k,l}=\frac{{\bf n}_{k,l+{\frac{1}{2}}}+{\bf n}_{k,l-{\frac{1}{2}}}}{|{\bf n}_{k,l+{\frac{1}{2}}}+{\bf n}_{k,l-{\frac{1}{2}}}|}.

It can be observed that
\mathbb{M}_{k,l}
and
\boldsymbol{b}_{k,l}
consist two independent parts related to
{\bf n}^{0}_{k,l}
and
{\bf n}^{1}_{k,l}
.
Comparing the two components separately, one can get

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\bf n}_{k,l}^{0}\cdot\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}={\frac{1}{2}}\left(w_{k-{\frac{1}{2}},l}+w_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}\right)\left[1+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\right],\\
&{\bf n}_{k,l}^{1}\cdot\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}={\frac{1}{2}}\left(w_{k,l-{\frac{1}{2}}}+w_{k,l+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\left[1+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\right],\end{aligned}\right.

(78)

With this essential relations, three properties of
\delta\tilde{\bf v}
can be found.
Firstly, noticing that
w_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l},w_{k,l+{\frac{1}{2}}}=\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
, it is obvious that

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}=\mathcal{O}(h^{2}),

Then, taking the smoothness into consideration,

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k+1,l}-\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}=\mathcal{O}(h^{3}),\quad\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l+1}-\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}=\mathcal{O}(h^{3})

At last, adding Eqs. (
78
) from two adjacent nodes,
M_{k,l}
and
M_{k+1,l}
for instance, we get

\displaystyle{\bf n}^{0}_{k,l}\cdot\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}+{\bf n}^{0}_{k+1,l}\cdot\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k+1,l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}\left(w_{k-{\frac{1}{2}},l}+2w_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}+w_{k+\frac{3}{2},l}\right)\left[1+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2w_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}\left[1+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\right].

Meanwhile, the left formula is

\displaystyle{\bf n}^{0}_{k,l}\cdot\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}+{\bf n}^{0}_{k+1,l}\cdot\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k+1,l}={\bf n}_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}\cdot\left(\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}+\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k+1,l}\right)\left[1+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\right].

The final property is obtained from the above two equations as

\displaystyle{\bf n}_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}\cdot\left(\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}+\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k+1,l}\right)=2w_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}\left[1+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\right]=2w_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}+\mathcal{O}(h^{4}).

Similar result can be acquired in another direction as

\displaystyle{\bf n}_{k,l+{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot\left(\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}+\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l+1}\right)=2w_{k,l+{\frac{1}{2}}}+\mathcal{O}(h^{4}).

In summary, three proved properties of
\delta\tilde{\bf v}
are listed as

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}=\mathcal{O}(h^{2}),

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k+1,l}-\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}=\mathcal{O}(h^{3})\\
\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l+1}-\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}=\mathcal{O}(h^{3})\end{aligned}\right.\,,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} {\bf n}_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}\cdot\left(\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}+\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k+1,l}\right)=2w_{k+{\frac{1}{2}},l}+\mathcal{O}(h^{4})\\
{\bf n}_{k,l+{\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot\left(\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l}+\delta\tilde{\bf v}_{k,l+1}\right)=2w_{k,l+{\frac{1}{2}}}+\mathcal{O}(h^{4})\end{aligned}\right.\,.

which play an essential role in following lemma.
∎

Lemma 3

(Flux accuracy from
\delta\tilde{\bf v}
)
.

For a set of uniformly refined mesh
\left\{M_{h}\right\}_{h\in\mathbb{R}}
and a smooth field
\mathbf{U}
, if the nodal velocity correction
\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}
satisfies:

1.

\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}=\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
,

2.

\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}-\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}=\mathcal{O}(h^{3})
,

3.

{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}\cdot\left(\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}+\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\right)=2w^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}+\mathcal{O}(h^{4})
,

then the flux accuracy satisfies

\mathbf{F}_{M}-\mathbf{F}_{S}=\mathcal{O}(h^{4}).

Proof of Lemma 2.

For start, the difference between first component is computed as

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}+\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\right)\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}-\left({\frac{1}{6}}{\bf v}^{i}_{r}+{\frac{2}{3}}{\bf v}^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}+{\frac{1}{6}}{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\right)\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}+\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\right)\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}-{\frac{2}{3}}\left({\bf v}^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}-{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf v}^{i}_{r}-{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\right)\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}+\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\right)\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}-w^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}=\mathcal{O}(h^{4}),

where
\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}=\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}-{\bf v}^{i}_{r}
.

Recall the jump condition Eqs. (
41
) for surface
[r,r+1]
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}^{i}_{r,r+{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{3}P^{i}_{r}+\frac{2}{3}P^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)-\left[\left(\frac{1}{3}\alpha^{i}_{r}+\frac{2}{3}\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}-\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}w^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right],

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}},r+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{3}P^{i}_{r+1}+\frac{2}{3}P^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)-\left[\left(\frac{1}{3}\alpha^{i}_{r+1}+\frac{2}{3}\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}-\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}w^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right],

then the pressure difference can be simplified as

\displaystyle\left({\frac{1}{6}}P^{i}_{r}+{\frac{2}{3}}P^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}+{\frac{1}{6}}P^{i}_{r+1}\right){\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}-{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\widetilde{P}^{i}_{r,r+{\frac{1}{2}}}+\widetilde{P}^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}},r+1}\right){\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{3}\alpha^{i}_{r}+\frac{2}{3}\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}+\left(\frac{1}{3}\alpha^{i}_{r+1}+\frac{2}{3}\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}\right]-\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}w^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}+\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\right)\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}\left[1+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\right]-\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}w^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}w^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\right]-\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}w^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}=\mathcal{O}(h^{4}).

At last, the energy term of
\mathbf{F}_{M}
is

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\widetilde{P}^{i}_{r,r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}+\widetilde{P}^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}},r+1}\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\right)\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{3}P^{i}_{r}+\frac{2}{3}P^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)-\left(\frac{1}{3}\alpha^{i}_{r}+\frac{2}{3}\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}+\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}w^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right]\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{3}P^{i}_{r+1}+\frac{2}{3}P^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)-\left(\frac{1}{3}\alpha^{i}_{r+1}+\frac{2}{3}\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\delta\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}+\alpha^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}w^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right]\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{6}P^{i}_{r}+\frac{1}{3}P^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r}\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}+\left(\frac{1}{6}P^{i}_{r+1}+\frac{1}{3}P^{i}_{r+{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\tilde{\bf v}^{i}_{r+1}\cdot{\bf n}^{i}_{r,r+1}}_{\mathrm{I}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}

Appendix C
. Derivates reconstruction

In order to reconstruct derivates
\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{x},\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{y}
and one-sided derivates
\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{x,L},\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{x,R},\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{y,L},\hat{\mathbf{U}}_{y,R}
used in Section
3.4
, it is sufficient to reconstruct for each scalar component
\varphi
. Let
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})
be the index set of cells adjacent to
\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r}
, then a least squares problem is introduced as

\displaystyle\min_{\nabla\varphi,\nabla^{2}\varphi}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})}|D\varphi(\boldsymbol{x}^{j}_{c})-\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}\varphi^{j}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})|^{2}

where
\varphi^{j}
is the high order reconstruction function of cell
\omega^{j}
defined in Section
3.2
, and

\displaystyle D\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})=\nabla\varphi+\nabla^{2}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r}),

where
\nabla\varphi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\nabla^{2}\varphi\in M_{2}(\mathbb{R})
are undetermined coefficients of the least squares problem, which gives
(\hat{\varphi}_{x},\hat{\varphi}_{y})^{\top}=\nabla\varphi
after solved.

One-sided derivates are obtained by a much straightforward way, since they only participate in numerical viscosity. Taking
\hat{\varphi}_{x,L},\hat{\varphi}_{x,R}
for example, weight averages on both sides are considered as

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{x,L}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{x,L}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})}\frac{\partial\varphi^{j}}{\partial x}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\,(\boldsymbol{x}^{j}_{c}-\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{0}}{\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{x,L}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})}(\boldsymbol{x}^{j}_{c}-\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{0}},

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{x,R}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{x,R}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})}\frac{\partial\varphi^{j}}{\partial x}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\,(\boldsymbol{x}^{j}_{c}-\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{0}}{\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{x,R}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})}(\boldsymbol{x}^{j}_{c}-\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{0}},

where
\mathbf{e}_{0}=(1,0)^{\top}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{x,L}=\left\{j,\;(\boldsymbol{x}^{j}_{c}-\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{0}<0\right\}\cap\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{x,R}=\left\{j,\;(\boldsymbol{x}^{j}_{c}-\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{0}>0\right\}\cap\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r}),

which indicate the left and right neighborhood of
\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r}
.

In the same way,
\hat{\varphi}_{y,L}
and
\hat{\varphi}_{y,R}
are defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{y,L}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{N}_{y,L}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})}\frac{\partial\varphi^{j}}{\partial y}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\,(\boldsymbol{x}^{j}_{c}-\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}}{\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{N}_{y,L}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})}(\boldsymbol{x}^{j}_{c}-\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}},

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{y,R}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{N}_{y,R}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})}\frac{\partial\varphi^{j}}{\partial y}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\,(\boldsymbol{x}^{j}_{c}-\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}}{\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{N}_{y,R}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})}(\boldsymbol{x}^{j}_{c}-\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}}

with
\mathbf{e}_{1}=(0,1)^{\top}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{y,L}=\left\{j,\;(\boldsymbol{x}^{j}_{c}-\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}<0\right\}\cap\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{y,R}=\left\{j,\;(\boldsymbol{x}^{j}_{c}-\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}>0\right\}\cap\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{i}_{r}).
