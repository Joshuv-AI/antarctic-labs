Max-tree for d d -permutations and pattern avoidance
Thomas Muller

Abstract
Higher dimensional permutations are tuples of d − 1 d-1 permutations that can be identified with a point set in a d − d- dimensional grid. In N. Bonichon and P.-J. Morel, J. Integer Sequences 25 (2022), several conjectures regarding the enumeration of pattern avoiding d − d- permutations were stated. In this paper, we consider a mapping from d − d- permutations to 2 d − 1 − 2^{d-1}- ary trees that naturally generalizes the classical max-tree construction for permutations. We then show that, when restricted to d − d- permutations avoiding ( 21 , 12 ) ({\color[rgb]{0.832,0.168,0.043}21},{\color[rgb]{0.0781,0.3164,0.8008}12}) and 231 231 , this mapping yields a bijection with d-ary trees. This result resolves one of the conjectures of Bonichon and Morel.

Introduction

Permutations are bijections from the set
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
to itself that represent rearrangements of distinguishable elements. They are central objects in combinatorics that can be studied from many different perspectives. In particular, the notion of pattern avoidance in permutations has led to numerous connections with other combinatorial structures (see for instance
[
8
]
and
[
2
]
). One celebrated example of such a connection is the enumeration of permutations avoiding a single pattern of size three which is given by the Catalan numbers
[
9
]
.

In this work, we focus on a classical mapping from permutations to binary trees that we recall here: the
max-tree
of a permutation. This tree is obtained through a recursive decomposition of the permutation around its maximal element. A permutation
\pi
is viewed as a set of points
(i,\pi(i))
in the plane and the root of the tree is defined as the point with maximal value. Removing this point partitions the permutation into two subpermutations: the points lying to its left and those lying to its right. Then, applying the same construction recursively to these two subpermutations yields the left and right subtrees of the root, respectively.

Figure 1:
A permutation and its max-tree

It is well-known that the max-tree construction induces a bijection between
231
-avoiding permutations and binary trees. Moreover, considering the dual construction, in which the minimal point is used instead of the maximal one, yields a second binary tree called the
min-tree
. The pairs formed by the min-tree and the max-tree are
twin binary trees
, and it is also known that the set of such pairs is in bijection with Baxter permutations
[
6
]
.

In this article, we generalize the notion of max-tree to the higher-dimensional setting of
d-
permutations. A
d-
permutation
is a tuple of
d-1
permutations that can be geometrically represented as a set of points in a
d-
dimensional grid. While, in the two-dimensional case, the recursive decomposition of the max-tree is based on the left and right regions determined by a point, in the higher-dimensional setting it relies on the hyperoctants lying below a point with respect to the last axis of the grid. Consequently, the associated recursive structure is no longer a binary tree but a
2^{d-1}-
ary tree.

Moreover, if one considers the sequence of points of a
d-
permutation
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
ordered by decreasing last coordinate and then projects these points onto the first
d-1
coordinates, the
d-1
dimensional hyperoctree (also called
2^{d-1}-
tree)
[
13
]
associated with this set of points corresponds to the max-tree of the
d-
permutation
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
. The max-tree of a
3-
permutation thus corresponds to the quadtree
[
7
]
of a set of two-dimensional points, while the max-tree of a
4-
permutations corresponds to the octree
[
11
]
of a set of three-dimensional points.

Additionally, as in the two dimensional case, the mapping from
d-
permutations to
2^{d-1}-
ary tree defined by the max-tree is surjective. This leads to the definition of certain
admissible classes
of
d-
permutations, for which this mapping becomes bijective. The definition of these classes rely on total orderings of the hyperoctants lying below a point of a
d-
permutation.

The second contribution of this paper focuses on the specialization of this mapping to pattern avoiding
d-
permutations. Following the notion of pattern avoidance considered in
[
3
]
, we show that the set
S^{d-1}_{n}\left(({\color[rgb]{0.832,0.168,0.043}21},{\color[rgb]{0.0781,0.3164,0.8008}12}),231\right)
of
d-
permutations avoiding
({\color[rgb]{0.832,0.168,0.043}21},{\color[rgb]{0.0781,0.3164,0.8008}12})
and
231
, forms a subset of one of the admissible classes of
d-
permutations, for which the associated max-trees correspond to
d-
ary trees. This yields a bijection between
S^{d-1}_{n}\left(({\color[rgb]{0.832,0.168,0.043}21},{\color[rgb]{0.0781,0.3164,0.8008}12}),231\right)
and the set of
d-
ary trees with
n
internal nodes.

This result is of twofold interest. First, it naturally generalizes the classical bijection between
231-
avoiding permutations and binary trees to arbitrary dimension
d
. Second, it provides an enumeration of the
d-
permutations in
S^{d-1}_{n}\left(({\color[rgb]{0.832,0.168,0.043}21},{\color[rgb]{0.0781,0.3164,0.8008}12}),231\right)
:

|S^{d-1}_{n}\left(({\color[rgb]{0.832,0.168,0.043}21},{\color[rgb]{0.0781,0.3164,0.8008}12}),231\right)|=\frac{1}{dn+1}\binom{dn+1}{n}\;.

(1)

This answers a conjecture originally formulated in
[
3
]
and extended in
[
10
]
. It is also worth mentioning that this bijection adds to other bijective results involving pattern-avoiding
d
-permutations and combinatorial structures such as triangle solitaire
[
12
]
and higher-dimensional floorplans
[
4
]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
3

d
-permutations and
d
-ary trees. We then introduce the max-tree associated with a
d
-permutation, together with admissible classes of
d
-permutations under this max-tree. We show that, restricted to these classes, the max-tree construction yields a bijection with
2^{d-1}
-ary trees. In Section
4

S^{d-1}_{n}\left(({\color[rgb]{0.832,0.168,0.043}21},{\color[rgb]{0.0781,0.3164,0.8008}12}),231\right)
and the set of

A

and denote by
\pi_{i}(p)
the coordinate

of this position vector.

Directions of dimension

A

{\bf dir}
is an element of

-1
. In a

2^{d}

), we denote this set (in dimension

.

Let also
{\bf dir}
be a direction, the

, denoted
(-{\bf dir})
, is the direction such that
(-{\bf dir})=(-1)\times{\bf dir}
.

Definition 1
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
be a
d
-permutation with
n

be two points in

. The

.

In a

associated with the direction

. Note that if
{\bf dir}(p_{a},p_{b})={\bf f}
, one also has that

. In order to characterize the directions between the points of a

3.1
From permutations to trees

Let
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
be a
d-
permutation of size
n
, the maximal point of
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
with respect to the coordinate
x_{d-1}
, denoted by
p_{max,d-1}
, is the point for which
\pi_{k}(p_{max,d-1})=n
. We also denote by
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}^{\bf f}
the
d-
permutation defined by the set of points of
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
for which the direction with respect to
p_{max,d-1}
is
{\bf f}
(i.e all the points
p^{\prime}
of
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
for which
{\bf dir}(p_{max,d-1},p^{\prime})={\bf f}
). This splits
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
in
2^{d-1}
subpermutations, each associated to a direction in
\mathbb{F}^{d}
.

Definition 2
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
be a
d
-permutation, the
max-tree
of
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
with respect to the axis
d-1
, denoted
{\mathcal{T}}_{max}({\boldsymbol{\pi}})
, is the
2^{d-1}
-ary tree obtained recursively such that:

•

The maximal point
p_{max,d-1}
of
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
is the root of the tree.

•

It has
2^{d-1}
subtrees: each such subtree is associated to a direction
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{F}^{d}
and is given by
{\mathcal{T}}_{max}({\boldsymbol{\pi}}^{\bf f})
.

•

The recursion ends with leaves associated to the direction
{\bf dir}\in\mathbb{F}^{d}
when the corresponding subpermutation associated with
dir
is empty.

The max-tree of a permutation with respect to another axis
k
is defined similarly by considering the maximal point with respect to that axis and recursively applying the construction using the corresponding coordinate. Dually, one can define the min-tree of a d-permutation. In this case, the construction proceeds by choosing the minimal point with respect to a given axis and recursing upward rather than downward.

3.2
Compatible total orders and main theorem

Let
C
be a total order on a set of directions
F
. We say that
C
is
compatible
with respect to the axis
k
if, in
C
, there is no direction for which the
k^{th}
coordinate is a
+
that precedes a direction for which the
k^{th}
coordinate is a
-
. Similarly, let
{\bf C}=(C_{0},\ldots,C_{d-1})
be a tuple of
d
total orders on
F
, we say that
C
is a compatible set of total orders if, for
i=1,\ldots,d
,
C_{i}
is compatible with respect to the axis
i
.

Definition 3
.

Let
{\bf C}=C_{0},\ldots,C_{d-1}
be a compatible set of total orders on
\mathbb{F}^{d}
. Let
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
be a
d
-permutation and let
{\mathcal{T}}=\gamma^{d}({\boldsymbol{\pi}})
be its max-tree with respect to the axis
d-1
.

We say that
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
is
admissible
with respect to
{\bf C}
if, for every internal node
r
of
{\mathcal{T}}
, the subpermutations of
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
corresponding to the subtrees of
{\mathcal{T}}
attached to
r
form ordered blocks on each coordinate axis
l
, according to the order prescribed by
C_{l}
.

More precisely, for every coordinate
l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
and for any two directions
{\bf f}_{1},{\bf f}_{2}\in\mathbb{F}^{d}
such that
{\bf f}_{1}
precedes
{\bf f}_{2}
in
C_{l}
, every point corresponding to a node of the subtree
{\mathcal{T}}_{{\bf f}_{1}}(r)
has smaller
l

{\mathcal{T}}_{{\bf f}_{2}}(r)
.

We denote by
S^{d-1}_{n,{\bf C}}
the set of

permutations of size
n
that is admissible with respect to
{\bf C}
. We also denote the restriction of
\gamma^{d}
to

.

3.3
Proof of the main theorem

Let us now prove Theorem
3.2
in this subsection. We first prove that
\gamma^{d}_{\bf C}
is surjective on
\mathbb{T}^{2^{d-1}}_{n}
(which implies that
\gamma^{d}
is also surjective on
\mathbb{T}^{2^{d-1}}_{n}
). Then, we prove that
\gamma^{d}_{\bf C}
is injective.

Lemma 3.4
.

The mapping
\gamma^{d}_{\bf C}
is surjective.

Proof.

We show this by induction on the size of the
d-
permutations. At size 0, the only element of
\mathbb{T}^{2^{d-1}}_{0}
is a single leaf which is the max tree of the empty
d-
permutation in
S^{d-1}_{0,{\bf C}}
. Similarly at size
1
, the only element of
\mathbb{T}^{2^{d-1}}_{1}
is a single internal node with
2^{d-1}
leaves, which is also the max-tree of the
d-
permutation with one point in
S^{d-1}_{1,{\bf C}}
.

Let us now consider a tree in
{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathbb{T}^{2^{d-1}}_{k}
. It consists of a root node together with
s
subtrees
{\mathcal{T}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{T}}_{s}
attached to this root. Without loss of generality, assume that each subtree
{\mathcal{T}}_{i}
is associated to a direction
{\bf dir}_{i}\in\mathbb{F}^{d}
and contains
k_{i}
internal nodes with
k_{i}<k
.

By the induction hypothesis, each subtree
{\mathcal{T}}_{i}
is the max-tree of a
d-
permutation
{{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}}^{{\bf dir}_{i}}
. It follows that
{\mathcal{T}}
is the max-tree of any
d-
permutation
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
whose subpermutations with respect to the maximal point
p_{max,d-1}
satisfy, for every
{\bf f}\in\mathbb{F}^{d}
,
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}^{\bf f}={{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}}^{\bf f}
. Moreover, for such a permutation to belong to
S^{d-1}_{k,{\bf C}}
it suffices that the subpermutations
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}^{\bf f}
appear as ordered blocks according to the total orders prescribed by
C
on each coordinate axis, as described in Definition
3
. Since
C
is a compatible set of total orders such an arrangement is always possible. Therefore, there always exists at least one
d-
permutation
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}\in S^{d-1}_{k,{\bf C}}
such that
\gamma^{d}_{\bf C}({\boldsymbol{\pi}})={\mathcal{T}}
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For any tree
{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathbb{T}^{2^{d-1}}_{n}

Conclusion

In this paper, we generalized the notion of max-tree of a permutation to
d-
permutations. This construction induces a mapping from
d-
permutations to
2^{d-1}-
ary trees. We then showed that, given a compatible set of total orders on
\mathbb{F}^{d}
, one can define an
admissible
permutation class for which this mapping is bijective. This yields a bijection between these admissible classes and
2^{d-1}-
ary trees.

By restricting this general bijection, we answered a conjecture formulated in
[
3
]
and
[
10
]
. More precisely, we proved that the
d-
permutation avoiding
({\color[rgb]{0.832,0.168,0.043}21},{\color[rgb]{0.0781,0.3164,0.8008}12})
and
231
are in bijection with
d-
ary trees which also solves the enumeration problem for this class of
d-
permutations.

As follow-up of this work, several open problems appear interesting to us.

Question 1:

The bijection between
S^{d-1}_{n}\left(({\color[rgb]{0.832,0.168,0.043}21},{\color[rgb]{0.0781,0.3164,0.8008}12}),231\right)
and the set of
d-
ary trees is obtained by constructing the max-trees with respect to the last axis of the permutation in
S^{d-1}_{n}\left(({\color[rgb]{0.832,0.168,0.043}21},{\color[rgb]{0.0781,0.3164,0.8008}12}),231\right)
. As mentioned in Section
3
, a similar construction can be defined using any other axis of a
d-
permutation. For each such axis, one can similarly define admissible classes of
d-
permutations by considering total orderings of the corresponding negative directions.

In this context, a natural open question is whether one can characterize, in terms of forbidden patterns, other classes of
d-
permutations that are in bijection with
d-
ary trees through a construction based on an axis different from the last axis ?

Question 2:

The bijection between
S^{d-1}_{n}\left(({\color[rgb]{0.832,0.168,0.043}21},{\color[rgb]{0.0781,0.3164,0.8008}12}),231\right)
and the set of
d-
ary trees arises as a restriction of the general mapping
\gamma^{d}
to
d-
permutations whose max-trees avoid certain directions. This restriction allows the arity of the trees to match the dimension of the permutations. A second open question is whether there exists a more general class of
d-
permutations, in bijection with
2^{d-1}-
ary trees that can be characterized in terms of forbidden patterns.

Question 3:

In the construction of the max-trees of a
d-
permutation, one considers the maximal point of the permutation with respect to the last axis. One may instead consider the dual construction based on the minimal point of a
d-
permutation, leading to the notion of min-tree. In the classical case of permutations, the pair (min-tree, max-tree) is known to be a pair of twin binary trees. Moreover, such pairs of trees are known to be in bijection with Baxter permutations.

This raises the following question: can analogous phenomena be identified in the higher-dimensional setting ? In particular, can we identify structural conditions on the pair (min-tree, max-tree) of a
d-
permutation and can we identify permutations classes that are in bijection with these pairs of trees ?
