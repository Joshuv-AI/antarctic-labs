Abstract
. For α ∈ ( 0 , n ) \alpha\in(0,n) and a growth function φ : [ 0 , ∞ ) → [ 0 , ∞ ) \varphi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty) , it is proved that the commutator [ b , I α ] [b,I_{\alpha}] generated by fractional integral operator I α I_{\alpha} and Orlicz BMO \mathrm{BMO} function b b is bounded from Orlicz-Hardy space H b φ ​ ( ℝ n ) H_{b}^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) to Lebesgue space L n n − α ​ ( ℝ n ) L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) , where H b φ ​ ( ℝ n ) H_{b}^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) is a suitable Orlicz-Hardy space. 1.
Introduction

For
0<\alpha<n
, the fractional integral operator
I_{\alpha}
is defined as follows:

I_{\alpha}(g)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{g(z)}{\left|x-z\right|^{n-\alpha}}\,dz\quad\text{for all}\,x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.

Two classical results (see
[
10
, Theorem 1.2.3]
and
[
12
, Theorem 4]
) asserted that there exists constants
C
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\|I_{\alpha}g\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha},\,{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

and

\displaystyle\|I_{\alpha}g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

with
1<p<q<\infty
and
1/q-1/p=\alpha/n
.
In the framework of Hardy spaces, the boundedness of
I_{\alpha}
is considered by Stein and Weiss
[
26
, Theorem G]
in their pioneering work on multi-variable harmonic functions, they established that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|I_{\alpha}g\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|g\|_{H^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})},

which reveals the compatibility between the regularity of fractional integral operator and the atomic structure of Hardy spaces.

Given a locally integrable function
b
and a sublinear operator
T
,
the commutator is denoted by
[b,T]
, it is given by

[b,T]g:=bTg-T(bg),

where
g
is a continuous function whose support is compact.
A famous theorem of Chanillo
[
4
]
characterizes the
\mathrm{BMO}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
space via the boundedness of
[b,I_{\alpha}]
, that is,

b\in\mathrm{BMO}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
if and only if the commutator
[b,I_{\alpha}]
is bounded from
L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
L^{q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, where
0<\alpha<n
and
1<p<\frac{n}{\alpha}
with

1/q=1/p-\alpha/n
.
Paluszyński
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
proved that
[b,I_{\alpha}]
does not map from
H^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
into
L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
continuously.
Later,
Pérez
[
23
]
introduced a subspace
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{b}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\subset H^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
in the study of commutator of Calderón-Zygmund operator.
Prompted by the bilinear splitting technique applied to the pair of Hardy space
H^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
\mathrm{BMO}
space, which is
originally introduced by Bonami and Grellier
[
2
]
,
an important work is that Ky
[
14
]
found a suitable subspace
H_{b}^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
of
H^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
established that
[b,I_{\alpha}]
have boundedness from
H_{b}^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.
Moreover, Ky
[
14
]
also proved that the
[b,I_{\alpha}]
maps
H^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
continuously into the weak Lebesgue space
L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha},\,\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.
For more related results,
we refer to the interested reader to
[
1
,
5
,
11
,
13
,
16
,
21
,
24
,
25
]
.

Motivated by the above works, the present paper studies the
boundedness of commutator generated by Orlicz-
\mathrm{BMO}
function and fractional integral operator within the framework of Orlicz-type spaces. To formulate our main results precisely, it is necessary to establish some definitions and notation.

A non-decreasing function
\varphi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is termed Orlicz function if it satisfies:
\varphi(0)=0
,
\varphi(s)>0
for all
s>0
and
lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(s)=\infty
. For Orlicz function
\varphi
, it is of lower type
p_{1}
(with
p_{1}\in[0,\infty)
) whenever one can find a constant
C_{p_{1}}>1
such that

\displaystyle\varphi(st)\leq C_{p_{1}}s^{p_{1}}\varphi(t)\qquad\forall\,t\geq 0,\;s\in(0,1].

The symbol

\displaystyle i(\varphi):=\sup\{p_{1}\geq 0:\varphi\,\text{is of lower type}\,p_{1}\}

represents the critical lower type index of
\varphi
.
For Orlicz function
\varphi
, it is of upper type
p_{2}
(with
p_{2}\in[0,\infty)
) whenever one can find a constant
C_{p_{2}}>1
such that

\displaystyle\varphi(st)\leq C_{p_{2}}s^{p_{2}}\varphi(t)\qquad\forall\,t\geq 0,\,\;s\in[1,\infty).

The symbol

\displaystyle I(\varphi):=\inf\{p_{2}\geq 0:\varphi\,\text{is of upper type}\,p_{2}\}

represents the critical upper type index of
\varphi
.

Definition 1.1
.

We term
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
a growth function if it is of upper type
1
and lower type
p
with
p\in(0,1]
.

Definition 1.2

(
[
15
]
)
.

For a given Orlicz function
\varphi
, the space
\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
stands for the set of all locally integrable functions

f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\int_{B}|g(x)-g_{B}|\,dx<\infty,

where the supremum ranges over all balls
B
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

g_{B}:=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}g(x)\,dx.

If
\varphi(t)=t
,
\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
For more research on
\mathrm{BMO}
type spaces, see
[
1
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
27
]
.

The Orlicz-Lebesgue space
L^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
contains all locally integrable functions
g
for which

\|g\|_{L^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\,\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\varphi(|g(x)|/\lambda)\,dx\leq 1\right\}<\infty.

We denote by
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the Schwartz class of rapidly decreasing smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

its topological dual
(space of tempered distributions). Define

\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\,\|\psi\|=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
|\alpha|\leq 1\end{subarray}}(1+|x|)^{2(n+1)}\,|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi(x)|\leq 1\right\}.

Let
g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\psi_{t}(\cdot)=t^{-n}\psi(\cdot/t)
.
The nontangential grand maximal function
\mathfrak{M}g
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}(g)(x):=\sup_{\psi\in S_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\;\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
|y-x|<t\end{subarray}}|(g*\psi_{t})(y)|\quad\text{for all}\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Definition 1.3
.

The Orlicz-Hardy space
H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the collection of all tempered distributions

g
such that
\mathfrak{M}g
belongs to
L^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Its (quasi-)norm is

\displaystyle\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}}:=\|\mathfrak{M}g\|_{L^{\varphi}}.

Definition 1.4
.

Suppose that
\varphi
is a growth function satisfying

\frac{n}{n+1}<i(\varphi)\leq 1.

Given
b\in\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
,
we denote by
H_{b}^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
the collection of all functions
g\in H^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
for which the function

[b,\mathfrak{M}]g:=\mathfrak{M}\left((b-b(\cdot))g(\cdot)\right)

is an element of
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For any
g\in H_{b}^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, define

(1.3)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{H_{b}^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|[b,\mathfrak{M}]\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The subsequent primary results offer a generalisation of the preceding results on the boundedness in Ky
[
14
]
to the Orlicz setting.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
0<\alpha<n
and
\varphi
is growth function with

(1.4)

\displaystyle n/(n+1)<i(\varphi)\leq I(\varphi)<1

and

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1/I(\varphi)>1/i(\varphi)-1\quad&\mbox{if}\ n=1;\\
1/I(\varphi)>(\lfloor n(1/i(\varphi)-1)\rfloor+n-1)/n\quad&\mbox{if}\ n\geq 2.\end{cases}

If
b\in\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
g\in H_{b}^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, then there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\|[b,I_{\alpha}]g\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C\|g\|_{H_{b}^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

Theorem 1.6
.

Under the same assumptions on
\varphi
,
\alpha
, and
b
as in Theorem
1.5
, if
g\in H^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
,
then there exists constant
C>0
such that

\|[b,I_{\alpha}]g\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha},\,\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq C\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

.

Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
are a further extension for the results of Ky

2.
Class
\mathcal{K}_{\varphi,\,\alpha}
and typical example

In order to give the definition of
\mathcal{K}_{\varphi,\,\alpha}
,
we start by recalling from
[
15
]
the atomic description of Orlicz-Hardy spaces.
Subsequently, a typical example is presented in
\mathcal{K}_{\varphi,\,\alpha}
.

For ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
q\in[1,\infty]
, the symbol
L^{q}_{B}
means the space of all measurable functions
g
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which
g
satisfies
\operatorname{supp}g\subset B
, the norm of
L^{q}_{B}
is defined by

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{q}_{B}}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|g(x)|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\!1/q}<\infty,&1\leq q<\infty;\\[10.76385pt]
\displaystyle\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{x\in B}|g(x)|<\infty,&q=\infty.\end{cases}

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose that
\varphi
is growth function with the critical lower type exponent
i(\varphi)
.
A triple
(\varphi,q,s)
is termed admissible if
q\in(1,\infty]

and the positive integer
s
satisfies

s\geq m(\varphi):=\lfloor n(1/i(\varphi)-1)\rfloor.

A locally integrable function
a
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is termed
(\varphi,q,s)
-atom if the next conditions are valid:

(i)

\operatorname{supp}a\subset B
for some ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

(ii)

\left\|a\right\|_{L^{q}_{B}}\leq\left\|{\mathbf{1}}_{B}\right\|^{-1}_{L^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}
;

(iii)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\gamma}a(x)\,dx=0
for every multi-indices
\gamma\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}
with
|\gamma|\leq s
.

Definition 2.2
.

For
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
s\geq m(\varphi):=\lfloor n(1/i(\varphi)-1)\rfloor
, suppose that
\{b_{j}\}_{j}
is a sequence of multiples of
(\varphi,q,s)
-atom, each of which is supported in ball
B_{j}
. Define

\Lambda_{q}\left(\{b_{j}\}\right):=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\sum_{j}\varphi\left(\frac{\|b_{j}\|_{L_{B_{j}}^{q}}}{\lambda}\right)|B_{j}|\leq 1\right\}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
0<\alpha<n
and
\varphi
be growth function such that

n/(n+1)<i(\varphi)\leq 1
.
The
\mathcal{K}_{\varphi,\,\alpha}
stands for the set consisting of all sublinear operator
R
that fulfills the following three conditions below:

(i)

R
is bounded from
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
;

(ii)

R
is bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to

L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha},\,\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
;

(iii)

For every
b\in\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and every
(\varphi,q,0)
-atom
a
whose support is contained in a ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
q\in(1,n/\alpha)
, there exists constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|(b-b_{B})Ra\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|a\|_{L^{q}_{B}}\|{\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}}.

To facilitate subsequent proof, it is proposed that the result in
[
6
, (2.3)]
be employed as a lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is of upper type
p_{1}\in(0,1]
and lower type
p_{2}\in(0,1]
. Then, for ball
B(x_{0},r)
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{1}}_{B(x_{0},\,tr)}\|_{L^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\leq\begin{cases}C^{+}t^{n/p_{1}}\|{\mathbf{1}}_{B(x_{0},\,r)}\|_{L^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}&\text{as}\ t\in(0,1];\\
C^{-}t^{n/p_{2}}\|{\mathbf{1}}_{B(x_{0},\,r)}\|_{L^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}&\text{as}\ t\in[1,\infty),\end{cases}

where
C^{+}
and
C^{-}
are positive constants.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
0<\alpha<n
and
\varphi
be growth function with

n/(n+1)<i(\varphi)\leq 1
. Then
I_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{K_{\varphi,\,\alpha}}
.

Proof.

Accoording to
[
19
, Theorem 3.1]
and
[
10
, Theorem 1.2.3]
,
I_{\alpha}
has boundedness from
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, and
I_{\alpha}
also bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha},\,\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.
In order to prove this lemma, it suffices to show that
I_{\alpha}
satisfies Definition
2.3
(iii).

Now, we begin verify that
I_{\alpha}
satisfies Definition
2.3
(iii).
From the condition
n/(n+1)<i(\varphi)
,
we should choose
\sigma
such that
\varphi
is of lower type
\sigma
with
n/(n+1)<\sigma<i(\varphi)
. So,

(2.7)

\displaystyle n\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}-1\right)<1.

Suppose
b\in\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
a
is
(\varphi,q,0)
atom
with
\operatorname{supp}a\subset B
.
Denote

p_{\alpha}=\frac{n}{n-\alpha}.

Write

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|(b-b_{B})I_{\alpha}a\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(b-b_{B})\mathbf{1}_{2B}I_{\alpha}a\|_{L^{p_{\alpha}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}+\|(b-b_{B})\mathbf{1}_{(2B)^{c}}I_{\alpha}a\|_{L^{p_{\alpha}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle=:{\rm I}+{\rm II}.

To estimate
{\rm I}
, if
q\in(1,n/\alpha)
and
1/q_{0}=1/q-\alpha/n
,
then we see that
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Taking
r>1
with
1/r+1/q_{0}=1/p_{\alpha}
,
by Hölder inequality, Lemma
2.4

and
[
6
, Lemma 3.4 and Lemma 5.5]
, it follows that

(2.9)

\displaystyle{\rm I}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left(\int_{2B}\left|b(x)-b_{B}\right|^{p_{\alpha}\cdot\frac{r}{p_{\alpha}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p_{\alpha}}{r}}\cdot\left(\int_{2B}\left|I_{\alpha}a(x)\right|^{p_{\alpha}\cdot\frac{q_{0}}{p_{\alpha}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p_{\alpha}}{q_{0}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{2B}|b(x)-b_{B}|^{r}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\int_{2B}|I_{\alpha}a(x)|^{q_{0}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left(\left(\int_{2B}|b(x)-b_{2B}|^{r}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}+\left(\int_{2B}|b_{2B}-b_{B}|^{r}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left(|2B|^{\frac{1}{r}-1}\|{\mathbf{1}}_{2B}\|_{L^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+|2B|^{\frac{1}{r}}2^{n/\sigma}|2B|^{-1}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}}\|{\mathbf{1}}_{B}\|_{L^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\ |B|^{\frac{1}{r}-1}\|{\mathbf{1}}_{B}\|_{L^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|a\|_{L^{q}_{B}}|B|^{\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}-1}\|\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|a\|_{L^{q}_{B}}\|\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where the penultimate step used
\|a\|_{L^{q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}=\|a\|_{L^{q}_{B}}|B|^{1/q}
(see (
2.6
)) and the last step used

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}-1=\frac{1}{q_{0}}+\frac{\alpha}{n}+\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{q_{0}}-1=\frac{\alpha}{n}+\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}-1=0.

Below, we consider
{\rm II}
.
Denote
B=B(x_{0},r)
. Due to the fact that
a
is a
(\varphi,q,0)
-atom supported on a ball
B\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, we have
\operatorname{supp}a\subset B
and
\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}a(x)\,dx=0
.
For any
z\in B
and
x\in(2^{j+1}B)\setminus(2^{j}B)
with
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
we find a point, which is labeled by
\xi_{z}
, lying on the straight segment joining
z
to
x_{0}
, satisfying that

(2.10)

\displaystyle|I_{\alpha}a(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{B}a(z)\left(\frac{1}{|x-z|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n-\alpha}}\right)\,dz\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B}\left|\frac{1}{|x-z|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{n-\alpha}}\right||a(z)|\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B}|z-x_{0}|\left|(n-\alpha)|x-\xi_{z}|^{-(n-\alpha+1)}\right||a(z)|\,dz

\displaystyle\leq(n-\alpha)r\cdot\left(\frac{2^{j}r}{2}\right)^{-(n-\alpha+1)}\int_{B}|a(z)|\,dz,

where the last step utilized

|x-\xi_{z}|\geq|x-x_{0}|-|x_{0}-\xi_{z}|\geq|x-x_{0}|-\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{2}=\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{2}\geq\frac{2^{j}r}{2}.

In addition, by
\|a\|_{L^{q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}=\|a\|_{L^{q}_{B}}|B|^{1-1/q^{\prime}}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B}|a(z)|\,dz\leq|B|^{1/q^{\prime}}\|a\|_{L^{q}}=|B|\|a\|_{L^{q}_{B}},

which and (
2.10
) imply

\displaystyle|I_{\alpha}a(x)|\lesssim r\cdot r^{n}\cdot r^{-(n-\alpha+1)}\cdot 2^{-j(n-\alpha+1)}\|a\|_{L^{q}_{B}}=r^{\alpha}\cdot 2^{-j(n-\alpha+1)}\|a\|_{L^{q}_{B}}.

This, along with lemma
2.4
and
[
6
, Lemma 3.4 and Lemma 5.5]
, yields

\displaystyle{\rm II}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{(2B)^{c}}\left|I_{\alpha}a(x)\left(b(x)-b_{B}\right)\right|^{p_{\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{(2^{j+1}B)\setminus(2^{j}B)}\left|I_{\alpha}a(x)\left(b(x)-b_{B}\right)\right|^{p_{\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{\alpha}\|a\|_{L_{B}^{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j(n-\alpha+1)}\Bigg[\left(\int_{2^{j+1}B}\left|b(x)-b_{2^{j+1}B}\right|^{p_{\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\int_{2^{j+1}B}\left|b_{B}-b_{2^{j+1}B}\right|^{p_{\alpha}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{\alpha}\|a\|_{L_{B}^{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j(n-\alpha+1)}\Big(|2^{j+1}B|^{\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}-1}\|{\mathbf{1}}_{2^{j+1}B}\|_{L^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle\qquad+|2^{j+1}B|^{\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}}2^{\frac{(j+1)n}{\sigma}}|2^{j+1}B|^{-1}\|\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{\alpha}\|a\|_{L^{q}_{B}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j(n-\alpha+1)}|2^{j+1}B|^{\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}-1}2^{\frac{(j+1)n}{\sigma}}\|\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle\approx r^{\alpha}r^{n\left(\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}-1\right)}\|a\|_{L^{q}_{B}}\|\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j(1+n-\alpha)+n(j+1)(\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}-1)+\frac{(j+1)n}{\sigma}}\right).

Further, by
\alpha=-n\left(1/p_{\alpha}-1\right)
and

-j(1+n-\alpha)+n(j+1)\left(\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}}-1\right)+\frac{(j+1)n}{\sigma}=-j\left(1-n\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}-1\right)\right)+\frac{n}{\sigma}-\alpha,

we use (
2.7
) to obtain that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}\lesssim\mathit{\|a\|_{L^{q}_{B}}\|\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j\left(1-n\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}-1\right)\right)}\lesssim\mathit{\|a\|_{L^{q}_{B}}\|\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}}.

Substituting (
2.9
) and (
2.11
) into (
2.8
) yields that
I_{\alpha}
satisfies Definition
2.3
(iii).
∎

3.
Decomposition of commutator

This section is devoted to find a wavelet decomposition of commutator,
where the wavelet basis is introduced in
[
20
]
.

Denote
F=\{0,1\}^{n}\setminus\{(0,\dots,0)\}
, the function of all dyadic cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is written as
\mathcal{D}
. Let
\{\psi_{I}^{\lambda}\}_{I\in\mathcal{D},\lambda\in F}
be a normalized and orthogonal wavelet basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with compact support and sufficient smoothness (see
[
20
]
).

Any
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be expanded as

g=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{\lambda\in F}\langle g,\psi_{I}^{\lambda}\rangle\psi_{I}^{\lambda}\ \quad\text{for all}\ g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

According to
[
2
]
, we define the bilinear operators
\Pi_{i}\ (i=1,2,3,4)
by

(3.12)

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}(g_{1},g_{2})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}\\
|I|=|I^{\prime}|\end{subarray}}\sum_{\lambda\in F}\langle g_{1},\varphi_{I}\rangle\langle g_{2},\psi_{I^{\prime}}^{\lambda}\rangle\varphi_{I}\psi_{I^{\prime}}^{\lambda},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\Pi_{2}(g_{1},g_{2})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}\\
|I|=|I^{\prime}|\end{subarray}}\sum_{\lambda\in F}\langle g_{1},\psi_{I}^{\lambda}\rangle\langle g_{2},\varphi_{I^{\prime}}\rangle\psi_{I}^{\lambda}\varphi_{I^{\prime}},

(3.14)

\displaystyle\Pi_{3}(g_{1},g_{2})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}\\
|I|=|I^{\prime}|\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in F\\
(I,\lambda)\neq(I^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})\end{subarray}}\langle g_{1},\psi_{I}^{\lambda}\rangle\langle g_{2},\psi_{I^{\prime}}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\rangle\psi_{I}^{\lambda}\psi_{I^{\prime}}^{\lambda^{\prime}}

and

(3.15)

\displaystyle\Pi_{4}(g_{1},g_{2})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{\lambda\in F}\langle g_{1},\psi_{I}^{\lambda}\rangle\langle g_{2},\psi_{I}^{\lambda}\rangle(\psi_{I}^{\lambda})^{2}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
\varphi
is a growth function, its lower and upper critical indices satisfy
(
1.4
).
Respectively, each
\Pi_{i}
(
i=1,2,3,4
) corresponds to the expression in (
3.12
), (
3.13
), (
3.14
) and (
3.15
).
Consequently, the statements below are true.

(i)

Both
\Pi_{1}
and
\Pi_{3}
are bounded from

H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
;

(ii)

\Pi_{4}
is bounded from
H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
;

(iii)

Suppose that
I(\varphi)\in\left(n/(n+1),1\right)
and (
1.5
) holds for
\varphi
, a sequence of dyadic cubes are denoted by
R_{j}
. Given
b\in\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}
and a finite atomic decomposition

f=\sum_{j=1}^{M}a_{j}
with
f\in H^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, where each
a_{j}
can be written as a constant times a
(\varphi,\infty,s)
atom, and
\operatorname{supp}a_{j}\subset R_{j}
.
Then

\sum_{j=1}^{M}\big\|\Pi_{2}(a_{j},b-b_{B_{j}})\big\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\;\lesssim\;\|f\|_{H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
b_{B_{j}}
is the average of
b
over ball
B_{j}
and
B_{j}\supseteq R_{j}
is the smallest ball.

Proof.

Note that (i) and (ii) follow from
[
6
, Propositions 3.14, 3.16 and 3.17]
.
By
[
6
, (5.17)]
, we obtain that (iii) holds.
∎

Next we will establish a decomposition for the commutator
[b,T]
. The proof relies on a wavelet orthonormal basis
\{\psi_{l}^{\lambda}\}
(see
[
20
]
) and the associated bilinear operators
\Pi_{i}
introduced in
[
3
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given a growth function
\varphi
and its lower and upper critical indices satisfy (
1.4
) and (
1.5
).
Let
\Pi_{4}
be defined as (
3.15
)
and
T\in\mathcal{K}_{\varphi,\,\alpha}
with
0<\alpha<n
.
For any
b\in\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and any
g\in H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists an operator

\mathfrak{T}:H^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\times\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\to L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

such that

(3.16)

\displaystyle[b,T]g=\mathfrak{T}(g,\,b)-T(\Pi_{4}(g,\,b)).

Proof.

Based on the argument of
[
18
, Proposition 6.7]
,
the following three steps will be used to prove the desired result.

Step 1: Verifying (
3.16
) under the case that
g
has a finite linear combination of wavelets.

We unitize
[
6
, Lemma 3.3, (c) and (a)]
to obtain
that
g
has finite atomic decomposition

g=\sum^{M}_{k=1}a_{k}

with

a_{k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{D}\\
I\subset R_{k}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\lambda\in F}\langle g,\psi^{\lambda}_{I}\rangle\psi^{\lambda}_{I}\qquad(k=1,\dots,M)

and

\Lambda_{\infty}\bigl(\{a_{k}\}_{k=1}^{M}\bigr)\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
\operatorname{supp}a_{k}\subseteq R_{k}

and
\langle g,\psi^{\lambda}_{I}\rangle
is the coefficients of
a_{k}
.
Further, let
B_{k}
be the smallest ball containing dyadic cube
R_{k}
.
According to
[
6
, Proposition 2.5]
, we have

(3.17)

\sum_{k=1}^{M}\|a_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{B_{k}}}\|\mathbf{1}_{B_{k}}\|_{L^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\Lambda_{\infty}\bigl(\{a_{k}\}_{k=1}^{M}\bigr)\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Denote

\mathcal{W}(g,\,b):=T\left(\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left[(b-b_{B_{k}})a_{k}-\Pi_{2}\left(a_{k},b-b_{B_{k}}\right)\right]\right).

For almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, using a standard algebraic identity (see
[
18
, (6.9)]
), it holds

\displaystyle b(x)g(\cdot)-b(\cdot)g(\cdot)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{M}\Bigl[\bigl(b(x)-b_{B_{k}}\bigr)a_{k}(\cdot)-\Pi_{2}\bigl(a_{k},b-b_{B_{k}}\bigr)(\cdot)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad-\Pi_{1}(g,b)(\cdot)-\Pi_{3}(g,b)(\cdot)-\Pi_{4}(g,b)(\cdot).

This implies

(3.18)

\displaystyle[b,T]g=\mathcal{W}(g,\,b)-T(\Pi_{1}(g,\,b))-T(\Pi_{3}(g,\,b))-T(\Pi_{4}(g,\,b)).

Now, if we define

\mathfrak{T}(g,\,b)=\mathcal{W}(g,\,b)-T(\Pi_{1}(g,\,b))-T(\Pi_{3}(g,\,b)),

then immediately obtain

[b,T]g=\mathfrak{T}(g,\,b)-T(\Pi_{4}(g,\,b)),

which is the desired equality.

Step 2:
For
b\in\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
g\in H^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with finite linear combination of wavelets, it follows that

\|\mathfrak{T}(g,\,b)\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}.

Using Proposition
3.1
,
for arbitrary
g\in H^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
b\in\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
,

\|\Pi_{i}(g,\,b)\|_{H^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})},\quad i=1,3.

By this,
T\in\mathcal{K}_{\varphi,\,\alpha}
, Proposition
3.1
(iii) , (
3.17
) and
\|a_{k}\|_{L^{q}_{B_{k}}}\leq\|a_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{B_{k}}}
with
q\in(1,n/\alpha)
, we see that

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{T}(g,b)\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{U}(g,b)\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|T(\Pi_{1}(g,b))\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|T(\Pi_{3}(g,b))\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\|(b(\cdot)-b_{B_{k}})Ta_{k}\right\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\left\|T\left(\sum_{k=1}^{M}\Pi_{2}(a_{k},b-b_{B_{k}})\right)\right\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\Pi_{1}(g,b)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\Pi_{3}(g,b)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\|a_{k}\|_{L^{q}_{B_{k}}}\|\mathbf{1}_{B_{k}}\|_{L^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\|\Pi_{2}(a_{k},b-b_{B_{k}})\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

as desired.

Step 3: The proof of (
3.16
).

Based on the facts obtained in
Steps 1
and
2
, we repeat the argument of
[
6
, (5.18)]
to obtain (
3.16
).
This implies Lemma
3.2
.
∎

4.
Proofs of Theorems
1.5
and
1.6

The primary objective of this section is to complete the proofs of Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
by using Lemma
4.1
.
We first recall the following lemma from
[
6
, Theorem 5.10]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
\varphi
is growth function and it satisfies (
1.4
) and (
1.5
).
If
b\in\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, then

g\in H^{\varphi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is equivalent to
\Pi_{4}(g,\,b)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\displaystyle\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}+\left\|\Pi_{4}(g,\,b)\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Under the assumptions that
b\in\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
g\in H^{\varphi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by Lemma
4.1
,
we have
\Pi_{4}(g,\,b)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

(4.19)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}+\|\Pi_{4}(g,\,b)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Based on Lemma
2.5
, we use Lemma
3.2
to obtain that there exists operator

\mathfrak{T}:H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\to L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

such that

[b,I_{\alpha}]g=\mathfrak{T}(g,\,b)-{I_{\alpha}}(\Pi_{4}(g,\,b)).

From this and (
1.2
), it follows that

\displaystyle\|[b,I_{\alpha}]g\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle=\|\mathfrak{T}(g,\,b)-I_{\alpha}(\Pi_{4}(g,\,b))\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathfrak{T}(g,\,b)\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}+\|I_{\alpha}(\Pi_{4}(g,\,b))\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}+\|\Pi_{4}(g,\,b)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\approx\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

which proves Theorem
1.5
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

By Lemma
3.2
, we find that there exists operator

\mathfrak{T}:H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\to L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

such that for any
b\in\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
g\in H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

[b,I_{\alpha}]g(y)=\mathfrak{T}(g,\,b)(y)-{I_{\alpha}}(\Pi_{4}(g,\,b))(y)\ \quad\text{for a. e.}\,y\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.

So, using (
1.1
) and Proposition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|[b,I_{\alpha}]g\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha},\,{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathfrak{T}(g,\,b)\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha},\,{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|I_{\alpha}(\Pi_{4}(g,\,b))\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha},\,{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathfrak{T}(g,\,b)\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\Pi_{4}(g,\,b)\|_{L^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|b\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{\varphi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

This implies Theorem
1.5
.
∎
