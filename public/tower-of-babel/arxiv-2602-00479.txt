Abstract
. In this paper, we give two new characterizations of the bounded lower oscillation(BLO) space by using the Gaussian heat semigroup. By the new characterizations, we prove the regularity property of the solutions to the heat equation with BLO boundary value. Also, we reprove the BMO-BLO boundedness of the Littlewood-Paley g g -function by using the semigroup method.



1.
Introduction

The theory of Hardy spaces and BMO spaces on the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
plays an important role in various fields of analysis and partial differential equations. It is well known that
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and BMO(
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
) are, respectively, suitable substitutes of
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. For example, it is known that the classical Riesz transforms are not bounded on
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, but bounded on
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and BMO(
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
). In 1980, in order to study the relationship between
A_{p}
weights and
BMO
functions, R. Coifman and R. Rochberg in
[
3
]
introduced BLO(
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
) space which is a subset of BMO(
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
), and discussed the
relationship between BMO functions and BLO functions. We recall the definition of BLO space as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

The space
{\rm BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
consists of locally integrable functions
f
such that

\left\|f\right\|_{{\rm BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:=\sup_{B}{1\over|B|}\int_{B}[f(x)-\underset{B}{\essinf}\ f]dx<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

By the definition of
\rm{BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
space, we know that it is not a linear space. For example,
f(x)=-\ln|x|\cdot\chi_{\{|x|>0\}}(x)\in\rm{BLO}({\mathbb{R}})
, but
-f\notin{\rm BLO}(\mathbb{R}).
In fact, we will give an exact proof of this in Section
4
.
In
[
2
]
, C. Bennett established another characterization of BLO(
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
) space with a variant Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. In recent years, there exists an increasing interest on the study of BLO type spaces. Y. Jiang
[
6
]
introduced RBLO space for non-doubling measures
\mu
and established boundedness of a kind of maximal singular integral operator and the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator from
L^{\infty}(\mu)
to RBLO(
\mu)
. L. Tang
[
14
]
introduced BLO spaces associated with the sections and studied the BLO type estimates for a kind of maximal singular integral operator and the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator. D. Yang et al
[
15
]
studied the space of BLO type associated with Schrödinger operators. In
[
1
]
, Ameida et al introduced the BLO spaces in the rational Dunkel setting. The authors in
[
4
]
considered the BMO-BLO boundedness of the differential transforms. Karagulyan proved the boundedness of the maximal operator on spaces BMO and BLO on abstract measure space equipped with a ball-basis in
[
7
]
. For more information and recent development about the BLO spaces, see, for example,
[
5
,
9
,
16
]
.

The heat semigroup associated with Laplacian is a basic tool in harmonic analysis. We denote the classical heat kernel by

{W}_{t}(x,y)=(4\pi t)^{-{n\over 2}}e^{-{{|x-y|^{2}}\over 4t}}

and

{W}_{t}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{W}_{t}(x,y)f(y)dy

for suitable function
f
. Using the heat semigroup and Poisson semigroup to characterize function spaces can be tracked to the work of E. Stein and M. Taibleson; see
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
. In this paper, we will give a new characterization of
{\rm BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
by using the heat semigroup in the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

A locally integrable function

f\in{\rm BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

if and only if

\sup_{t>0}\left\|W_{t}f(\cdot)-\underset{z\in B(\cdot,\sqrt{t})}{\inf}W_{t}f(z)\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\infty.

Moreover, if
f\in{\rm BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, then

\left\|f\right\|_{\rm BLO({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sim\sup_{t>0}\left\|W_{t}f(\cdot)-\underset{z\in B(\cdot,\sqrt{t})}{\inf}W_{t}f(z)\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

As we have mentioned before, the space
{\rm BLO({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
is also related with the
A_{1}
Muckenhoupt weights. We recall the definition of the
A_{1}
Muckenhoupt weights as follows.

Definition 1.3
.

(
A_{1}
Muckenhoupt Weights)
A non-negative, locally integrable function
w:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be an
A_{1}
weight if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(1.1)

Mw(x)\leq Cw(x)\quad\text{for almost every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
M
denotes the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator. The smallest constant
C
, such that the above inequality holds, is called the
A_{1}
constant of
w
.

In
[
3
]
, R. Coifman and R. Rochberg proved a characterization of BLO space as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

A function
f
is in
\mathrm{BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
if and only if
e^{\varepsilon f}\in A_{1}
for some positive constant
\varepsilon
.

Based on the theorem above and the heat characterization of BLO space as in Theorem
1.2
, we can get another new weight characterization of BLO space by using the heat semigroup.

Theorem 1.5

(Heat Characterization via
A_{1}
Weights)
.

For a locally integrable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, the following three statements are equivalent:

(i)

f\in\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(ii)

There exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
e^{\varepsilon f}\in A_{1}
.

(iii)

There exists
\varepsilon>0
and
C>0
such that for all
t>0
and almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

W_{t}(e^{\varepsilon f})(x)\leq Ce^{\varepsilon f(x)}.

Moreover, the constants in these equivalences depend only on the dimension
n
.

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give the proof of Theorem
1.2
. We also prove the regularity of the solutions of heat equation with BLO-boudary value and the BMO–BLO boundedeness of the Littlewood-Paley
g
-function by using the heat semigroup characterization in Theorem
1.2
. In Section
3
, we give the proof of Theorem
1.5
. At last, in Section
4
we give an example to show that,
\rm{BLO}
space is not a linear space, but it still have some stability property under
L^{\infty}
perturbation.

Throughout this article, the letters
C,c
will denote positive
constants which may change from one instance to another and depend on
the parameters involved. We will make a frequent use, without
mentioning it in relevant places, of the fact that for a positive

A
and a non-negative constant
a,

\sup\limits_{t>0}t^{a}e^{-At}=C_{a,A}<\infty.

2.
Proof of the heat semigroup characterization and its applications

In this section, we will give the proof of Theorem
1.2
. And, based on the heat semigroup characterization, we prove the regularity property of the solution to the heat equation and the BMO-BLO boundedness of the Littlewood-Paley
g
-function.

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Now, we are in a position to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We prove the necessity first. Assume that
f\in\text{BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. We want to show that there exists
C>0
such that

\left|W_{t}f(x)-\underset{z\in B(x,\sqrt{t})}{\inf}W_{t}f(z)\right|\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{{\rm BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
t>0
.
The heat kernel
{W}_{t}(x,y)
is rapidly decreasing and acts as a local average. For a fixed
x
and
t
, the values
{W}_{t}f(z)
for
z
near
x
are primarily influenced by the values of
f
on a ball roughly of radius
r
with
r\sim\sqrt{t}
.
Take two points
x,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|x-z|\leq\sqrt{t}
. Let us estimate the difference
{W}_{t}f(x)-{W}_{t}f(z)
. Observe that

\displaystyle{W}_{t}f(x)-{W}_{t}f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}({W}_{t}(x,y)-{W}_{t}(z,y))f(y)dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}({W}_{t}(x,y)-{W}_{t}(z,y))(f(y)-m)dy,

for any constant
m
.
The natural choice is to take
m
to be the essential infimum of
f
on a ball containing the support of the effective difference of the kernels. Note that
|{W}_{t}(x,y)-{W}_{t}(z,y)|
is significant only when
y
is in a ball of radius
C\sqrt{t}
around
x
(and hence around
z
). Let
B=B(x,\sqrt{t})
and choose
m=\underset{B}{\text{essinf}}\ f
. Then,

\displaystyle|{W}_{t}f(x)-{W}_{t}f(z)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B}|{W}_{t}(x,y)-{W}_{t}(z,y)||f(y)-\underset{B}{{\essinf}}\ f|dy+\int_{B^{c}}|{W}_{t}(x,y)-{W}_{t}(z,y)||f(y)-\underset{B}{{\essinf}}\ f|dy

\displaystyle=:I+II.

For
I
, since
\left|\nabla_{x}W_{t}(x,y)\right|\leq Ct^{-1/2}W_{t}(x,y)
and
|x-z|\leq\sqrt{t},
by the mean value theorem, there exists
\xi
between
x
and
z
such that

I\leq C{|x-z|\over\sqrt{t}}\int_{B}W_{t}(\xi,y)|f(y)-\underset{B}{{\essinf}}\ f|dy\leq{C\over t^{n/2}}\int_{B}|f(y)-\underset{B}{{\essinf}}\ f|dy\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{{\rm BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

For
II
, it follows from the definition of
W_{t}(x,y)
that

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\leq C{|x-z|\over\sqrt{t}}\int_{B^{c}}W_{t}(\xi,y)|f(y)-\underset{B}{{\essinf}}\ f|dy

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\int_{2^{k+1}B\backslash 2^{k}B}W_{t}(\xi,y)|f(y)-\underset{B}{{\essinf}}\ f|dy

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-n/2}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}e^{-c4^{k}}\int_{2^{k+1}B}|f(y)-\underset{2^{k+1}B}{{\essinf}}\ f|dy

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-n/2}\left\|f\right\|_{{\rm BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}e^{-c4^{k}}\cdot(k+1)\cdot\left|2^{k+1}B\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{{\rm BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}e^{-c4^{k-1}}k2^{kn}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{{\rm BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Combining the estimates of
I
and
II
, we prove that

|{W}_{t}f(x)-{W}_{t}f(z)|\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{{\rm BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Since the inequality above holds for any
z\in B(x,\sqrt{t})
, it holds in particular for the
z
that minimizes
{W}_{t}f(z)
in the ball. Therefore,

W_{t}f(x)-\underset{z\in B(x,\sqrt{t})}{{\inf}}W_{t}f(z)\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{{\rm BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},

which is the desired result. Taking the supremum over
x
and
t
finishes this direction.

Now, let us prove the sufficiency.
Assume that
\underset{t>0}{\sup}\left\|{{W}_{t}f(\cdot)-\underset{y\in B(\cdot,\sqrt{t})}{\inf}{W}_{t}f(y)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq K
. We want to show that
f\in\text{BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
Since
\underset{t>0}{\sup}\left\|{{W}_{t}f(\cdot)-\underset{y\in B(\cdot,\sqrt{t})}{\inf}{W}_{t}f(y)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq K
, then, for any
t>0
and a.e.
z,w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
\left|z-w\right|\leq\sqrt{t}
, we have

\left|W_{t}f(z)-W_{t}f(w)\right|\leq K.

Let
B
be a ball of radius
R
, and set
t=R^{2}.
For any two points
y,y^{\prime}\in B
, the distance
\left|y-y^{\prime}\right|\leq 2R=2\sqrt{t}.
Let
z
be the midpoint of
y
and
y^{\prime}
. Then
\left|y-z\right|\leq R=\sqrt{t}
and
\left|z-y^{\prime}\right|\leq R=\sqrt{t}
. So

\left|W_{t}f(y)-W_{t}f(z)\right|\leq K

and

\left|W_{t}f(z)-W_{t}f(y^{\prime})\right|\leq K.

Hence,

\left|W_{t}f(y)-W_{t}f(y^{\prime})\right|\leq\left|W_{t}f(y)-W_{t}f(z)\right|+\left|W_{t}f(z)-W_{t}f(y^{\prime})\right|\leq 2K.

Therefore, the oscillation of
W_{t}f
over
B
is bounded by
2K
, i.e.,

\sup_{y\in B}W_{t}f(y)-\inf_{y\in B}W_{t}f(y)\leq 2K.

Let
m_{t}=\underset{y\in B}{\inf}W_{t}f(y).
Then for any
y\in B,

W_{t}f(y)\leq m_{t}+2K.
Thus,

{1\over|B|}\int_{B}W_{t}f(y)dy\leq m_{t}+2K,

which implies

(2.1)

{1\over|B|}\int_{B}W_{t}f(y)dy-m_{t}\leq 2K.

Since the heat kernel is an approximation of the identity, we have that
W_{t}f\rightarrow f
in
L^{1}(B)
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}.
Let
m=\underset{B}{\text{essinf}}\ f.

Fix
\epsilon>0
, by definition of the essential infimum, there exists a measurable set
E\subset B
with
|E|>0
such that

f(y)<m+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\quad\text{for all }y\in E.

Since the heat semigroup is an approximation of the identity, we have
W_{t}f\to f
in
L^{1}(B)
as
t\to 0^{+}
. Hence, there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
0<t<\delta
,

\int_{B}|W_{t}f(y)-f(y)|\,dy<\frac{\epsilon|E|}{2}.

Now, for any
0<t<\delta
,

\displaystyle m_{t}

\displaystyle=\inf_{y\in B}W_{t}f(y)\leq\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}W_{t}f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}f(y)\,dy+\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}|W_{t}f(y)-f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}\left(m+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)dy+\frac{1}{|E|}\cdot\frac{\epsilon|E|}{2}

\displaystyle=m+\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}=m+\epsilon.

Then, we get that

-m\leq-\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}m_{t}.

By (
2.1
), we deduce that

\displaystyle f_{B}-m

\displaystyle\leq f_{B}-\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}m_{t}=f_{B}+\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}(f_{m}-m_{t})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}(f_{B}-m_{t})=\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}[(W_{t}f)_{B}-m_{t}]

\displaystyle\leq 2K.

So, we have

{1\over|B|}\int_{B}[f(y)-m]dy=f_{B}-m\leq 2K.

And this inequality holds for every ball
B.
Thus

\left\|f\right\|_{{\rm BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\underset{B}{\sup}{1\over|B|}\int_{B}[f(y)-\underset{B}{\essinf}\ f]dy\leq 2K.

We complete the proof.
∎

2.2.
Regularity property of the solutions to the heat equation

We can use the heat semigroup characterization of BLO space as in Theorem
1.2
to prove a regularity property of the solutions to the heat equation with BLO boundary values.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Consider the solution
u(x,t)
to the heat equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=\Delta u,&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\\
u(x,0)=f(x),&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

Then
u(x,t)
satisfies the following regularity estimate

u(x,t)\leq\inf_{z\in B(x,\sqrt{t})}u(z,t)+C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ t>0,

where
C>0
depends only on the dimension
n
.

Proof.

The solution is given by the heat semigroup

u(x,t)=W_{t}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(4\pi t)^{-n/2}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}f(y)\,dy.

Since
f\in\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by Theorem
1.2
we have

\sup_{t>0}\left\|W_{t}f(\cdot)-\underset{z\in B(\cdot,\sqrt{t})}{{\inf}}\,W_{t}f(z)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

In particular, for any fixed
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

W_{t}f(x)-\inf_{z\in B(x,\sqrt{t})}W_{t}f(z)\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Rewriting this in terms of
u(x,t)
, we obtain

u(x,t)\leq\inf_{z\in B(x,\sqrt{t})}u(z,t)+C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},

which is the desired estimate.
∎

By the theorem above, we can get an oscillation estimate of solutions to the heat equation with
\mathrm{BLO}
boundary values in the following.

Theorem 2.2
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
2.1
, for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t>0
, we have

\sup_{x\in B(x_{0},\sqrt{t})}u(x,t)-\inf_{x\in B(x_{0},\sqrt{t})}u(x,t)\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

For any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t>0
, denote
B=B(x_{0},\sqrt{t})
. For any
x,y\in B
, let
z
be the midpoint of
x
and
y
. Therefore,
x\in B(z,\sqrt{t})
and
y\in B(z,\sqrt{t})
.

By Theorem
2.1
, we have

u(z,t)\leq\inf_{w\in B(z,\sqrt{t})}u(w,t)+C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Since
x\in B(z,\sqrt{t})
, we have
\displaystyle\inf_{w\in B(z,\sqrt{t})}u(w,t)\leq u(x,t)
. So

(2.2)

u(z,t)\leq u(x,t)+C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Moreover, we apply Theorem
2.1
to the point
x
and get

u(x,t)\leq\inf_{w\in B(x,\sqrt{t})}u(w,t)+C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Since
z\in B(x,\sqrt{t})
, we have
\underset{w\in B(x,\sqrt{t})}{\inf}u(w,t)\leq u(z,t)
. So

(2.3)

u(x,t)\leq u(z,t)+C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

From inequalities (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we obtain

|u(x,t)-u(z,t)|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Similarly, by applying Theorem
2.1
to
y
and
z
, we get:

|u(y,t)-u(z,t)|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Therefore,

|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|\leq|u(x,t)-u(z,t)|+|u(z,t)-u(y,t)|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Since this inequality holds for all
x,y\in B
, we conclude:

\sup_{x,y\in B}|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

which implies:

\sup_{B}u(x,t)-\inf_{B}u(x,t)\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

This completes the proof of the oscillation estimate.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

(1)

The results above can be extended to more general parabolic equations of the form

\partial_{t}u=\text{div}(A(x)\nabla u),

where
A(x)
is a real matrix which is uniformly elliptic and bounded.

(2)

The
BLO
condition on initial data provides a natural framework for studying the local behavior of solutions to diffusion equations. It ensures that the solution exhibits controlled local behavior.
These estimates are particularly useful in the study of long-time behavior and regularity theory for nonlinear parabolic equations.

2.3.
BMO–BLO boundedness of the Littlewood-Paley
g
-function

In
[
8
]
, Meng and Yang proved that the Littlewood-Paley
g
-function is bounded from BMO(
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
) to BLO(
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
). At here, as an application of Theorem
1.2
, we reprove it by using the semigroup method.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
g
be the Littlewood-Paley
g
-function defined by

g(f)(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|t\partial_{t}W_{t}f(x)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/2}.

Then, for any
f\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
g(f)(x)<\infty
a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the function
[g(f)]^{2}
belongs to
\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the norm estimate

\|[g(f)]^{2}\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},

where
C>0
depends only on the dimension
n
.

Proof.

We prove the result by using the heat characterization of BLO space. We will show that

\sup_{t>0}\left\|W_{t}([g(f)]^{2})-\underset{B(\cdot,\sqrt{t})}{\inf}W_{t}([g(f)]^{2})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.

Let
h(x)=[g(f)(x)]^{2}
. For fixed
t_{0}>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, set
r=\sqrt{t_{0}}
. We need to estimate

W_{t_{0}}h(x)-\inf_{z\in B(x,r)}W_{t_{0}}h(z).

Firstly, let us consider
W_{t_{0}}h(x)
.
We write

\displaystyle W_{t_{0}}h(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}W_{t_{0}}(x,y)[g(f)(y)]^{2}dy=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}W_{t_{0}}(x,y)|s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(y)|^{2}dy\right]\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{4r^{2}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}W_{t_{0}}(x,y)|s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(y)|^{2}dy\right]\frac{ds}{s}+\int_{4r^{2}}^{\infty}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}W_{t_{0}}(x,y)|s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(y)|^{2}dy\right]\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle=:I_{\text{near}}(x)+I_{\text{far}}(x).

Let us estimate the near part
I_{\text{near}}(x)
first.
For any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
r>0,
let
B=B(x,r)
and decompose

f=f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{8B},

where
f_{1}=(f-f_{8B})\chi_{8B}
and
f_{2}=(f-f_{8B})\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 8B}.

Then, by the property of the heat semigroup and Jensen’s inequality,

(2.4)

I_{\text{near}}(x)=W_{t_{0}}\left([g_{4r^{2}}(f)]^{2}\right)(x)\leq CM\left([g_{4r^{2}}(f)]^{2}\right)(x),

where

\displaystyle g_{4r^{2}}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{r^{2}}|t\partial_{t}W_{t}f(x)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/2}.

Since
g_{4r^{2}}(f_{8B})=0
, we have

(2.5)

\int_{B}[g_{4r^{2}}(f)(y)]^{2}dy\leq 2\int_{B}[g_{4r^{2}}(f_{1})(y)]^{2}dy+2\int_{B}[g_{4r^{2}}(f_{2})(y)]^{2}dy.

Since
f_{1}
is supported in
8B
and
\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C|8B|^{1/2}\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
by the John-Nirenberg inequality, the
L^{2}
-boundedness of the
g
-function gives

\|g_{4r^{2}}(f_{1})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C|8B|^{1/2}\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Thus,

(2.6)

\int_{B}[g_{4r^{2}}(f_{1})(y)]^{2}dy\leq\|g_{4r^{2}}(f_{1})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}\leq C|B|\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}.

And for the part related to
g_{4r^{2}}(f_{2})
, decompose
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 8B
into annuli

A_{k}=\{z:2^{k}\cdot 8r\leq|y-z|<2^{k+1}\cdot 8r\},\ \ k=0,1,2,\cdots.

For
z\in A_{k}
, we have
|y-z|\sim 2^{k}r
, and by the John-Nirenberg inequality

\int_{A_{k}}|f_{2}(z)|\,dz\leq C(2^{k}r)^{n}(1+k)\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

So,

\displaystyle|s\partial_{s}W_{s}f_{2}(y)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus 8B}s\partial_{s}W_{s}(y,z)f_{2}(z)dz\right|=\left|\sum_{k\geq 0}\int_{A_{k}}s\partial_{s}W_{s}(y,z)f_{2}(z)dz\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k\geq 0}\int_{A_{k}}s^{-{n\over 2}}e^{-c{|y-z|^{2}\over s}}{|y-z|^{2}\over s}|f_{2}(z)|dz

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sum_{k\geq 0}s^{-1-n/2}e^{-c(2^{k}r)^{2}/s}(2^{k}r)^{n+2}(1+k).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle[g_{4r^{2}}(f_{2})(y)]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}\sum_{k,l\geq 0}(1+k)(1+l)(2^{k}r)^{n+2}(2^{l}r)^{n+2}\int_{0}^{4r^{2}}s^{-n-2}e^{-c{\left((2^{k}r)^{2}+(2^{l}r)^{2}\right)\over s}}\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}\left(C_{1}+\sum_{k\neq l\geq 0}C_{2}(1+k)(1+l)e^{-c^{\prime}|k-l|}\right)\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}.

Hence,

(2.7)

\int_{B}[g_{4r^{2}}(f_{2})(y)]^{2}dy\leq C|B|\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}.

Combining (
2.4
)–(
2.7
) and taking the supremum over balls with
x
being its center, we get that

(2.8)

I_{\text{near}}(x)\leq C\sup_{B\ni x}{1\over|B|}\int_{B}[g_{4r^{2}}(f)]^{2}(y)dy\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}.

For the far part, in this case
s>4r^{2}
, we use the smoothness and decay properties of the heat kernel.
For
z\in B(x,r)
,

|I_{\text{far}}(x)-I_{\text{far}}(z)|\leq\int_{4r^{2}}^{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}W_{t_{0}}(y)\left(|s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(x-y)|^{2}-|s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(z-y)|^{2}\right)dy\right|\frac{ds}{s}.

In order to estimate
|I_{\text{far}}(x)-I_{\text{far}}(z)|
, we need a lemma in the following.

Lemma 2.5
.

(1)

For
f\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
s>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
n
such that:

|s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(y)|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad\text{for all }y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2)

For
f\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
s>0
, and
x,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\left||s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(x)|^{2}-|s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(z)|^{2}\right|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\frac{|x-z|}{\sqrt{s}}.

Proof.

{\it(1)}
We provide a complete proof using the kernel representation and careful estimates.
The time derivative has the kernel representation

s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{s}(y,z)f(z)dz,

where

K_{s}(y,z)=(4\pi s)^{-n/2}e^{-\frac{|y-z|^{2}}{4s}}\left[-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{|y-z|^{2}}{4s}\right].

We know that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{s}(y,z)dz=0.

Let
B=B(y,\sqrt{s})
. Then

s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{s}(y,z)[f(z)-f_{B}]dz.

Decompose the integral into two parts

s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(y)=\tilde{I}_{\text{near}}(y)+\tilde{I}_{\text{far}}(y),

where

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{\text{near}}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{2B}K_{s}(y,z)[f(z)-f_{B}]dz,

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{\text{far}}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 2B}K_{s}(y,z)[f(z)-f_{B}]dz.

We estimate the near part first.
For
z\in 2B
, we have
|y-z|\leq 2\sqrt{s}
. And

|K_{s}(y,z)|\leq Cs^{-n/2}\left(1+\frac{|y-z|^{2}}{s}\right)e^{-\frac{|y-z|^{2}}{4s}}\leq Cs^{-n/2}.

Therefore,

|\tilde{I}_{\text{near}}(y)|\leq Cs^{-n/2}\int_{2B}|f(z)-f_{B}|dz.

By the John-Nirenberg inequality, we get

\int_{2B}|f(z)-f_{B}|dz\leq C|2B|\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=Cs^{n/2}\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Thus,

|\tilde{I}_{\text{near}}(y)|\leq Cs^{-n/2}\cdot Cs^{n/2}\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

For
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 2B
, we have
|y-z|\geq 2\sqrt{s}
. The kernel has rapid decay. Then, we have

|K_{s}(y,z)|\leq Cs^{-n/2}\left(\frac{|y-z|}{\sqrt{s}}\right)^{2}e^{-\frac{|y-z|^{2}}{4s}}\leq Cs^{-n/2}e^{-c\frac{|y-z|^{2}}{s}}.

Decompose the far region
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus 2B
into annuli

A_{k}=\{z:2^{k}\cdot 2\sqrt{s}\leq|y-z|<2^{k+1}\cdot 2\sqrt{s}\},\quad k\geq 0.

For
z\in A_{k}
, we have
|y-z|\sim 2^{k}\sqrt{s}
. So,

|K_{s}(y,z)|\leq Cs^{-n/2}e^{-c4^{k}}.

Then, we have

\left|\int_{A_{k}}K_{s}(y,z)[f(z)-f_{B}]dz\right|\leq Cs^{-n/2}e^{-c4^{k}}\int_{A_{k}}|f(z)-f_{B}|dz\leq Ce^{-c4^{k}}2^{kn}(1+k)\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Summing over
k\geq 0
, we get that

|\tilde{I}_{\text{far}}(y)|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-c4^{k}}2^{kn}(1+k)\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Combining the near and far estimates, we have

|s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(y)|\leq|\tilde{I}_{\text{near}}(y)|+|\tilde{I}_{\text{far}}(y)|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

{\it(2)}

By the estimation in
{\it(1)}
, we have

\displaystyle\left||s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(x)|^{2}-|s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(z)|^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(x)-s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(z)|\cdot\left(|s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(x)|+|s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(z)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}|s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(x)-s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(z)|.

Now, we need estimate the difference

s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(x)-s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[K_{s}(x,w)-K_{s}(z,w)]f(w)dw.

By the mean value theorem and heat kernel gradient estimates, we have

|K_{s}(x,w)-K_{s}(z,w)|\leq|x-z|\cdot\sup_{\xi\in[x,z]}|\nabla_{\xi}K_{s}(\xi,w)|\leq C|x-z|s^{-(n+1)/2}\sup_{\xi\in[x,z]}e^{-c|\xi-w|^{2}/s}.

A refined analysis using the BMO condition yields

|s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(x)-s\partial_{s}W_{s}f(z)|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\frac{|x-z|}{\sqrt{s}}.

Combining these estimates, we get the desired result.
∎

Now, we continue the proof of Theorem
2.4
.
By Lemma
2.5
, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
z\in B(x,r),
we have

|\tilde{I}_{\text{far}}(x)-\tilde{I}_{\text{far}}(z)|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}\int_{4r^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{|x-z|}{\sqrt{s}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}W_{t_{0}}(y)dy\frac{ds}{s}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}.

Combining the near and far estimates, we get that

|W_{t_{0}}h(x)-W_{t_{0}}h(z)|\leq|\tilde{I}_{\text{near}}(x)-\tilde{I}_{\text{near}}(z)|+|\tilde{I}_{\text{far}}(x)-\tilde{I}_{\text{far}}(z)|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}^{2}.

Taking the infimum over
z\in B(x,\sqrt{t_{0}})
:

W_{t_{0}}h(x)-\inf_{z\in B(x,\sqrt{t_{0}})}W_{t_{0}}h(z)\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}^{2}.

This holds uniformly in
x
and
t_{0}
, which proves that
[g(f)]^{2}\in\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the claimed norm bound.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
2.4
,
g(f)\in\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\|g(f)\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

For any ball
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we have

{1\over|B|}\int_{B}\left(g(f)(x)-\inf_{B}g(f)\right)dx\leq{1\over|B|}\int_{B}\left([g(f)(x)]^{2}-\inf_{B}[g(f)]^{2}\right)^{1/2}dx\\
\leq\left(C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}=C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Taking supremum over balls
B
, we get the result.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

In this section, we will give the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Proof.

(
i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii).
This is the classical result in
[
3
]
which was developed by Coifman and Rochberg.

(
ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii).
Assume that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
e^{\varepsilon f}\in A_{1}
with constant
0<C_{1}
as in (
1.1
). The heat kernel is an approximation of the identity, and there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
such that for any
t>0
,

W_{t}(e^{\varepsilon f})(x)\leq C_{2}M(e^{\varepsilon f})(x)\quad\text{for any }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Combining with the
A_{1}
condition, we get

W_{t}(e^{\varepsilon f})(x)\leq C_{2}M(e^{\varepsilon f})(x)\leq C_{2}C_{1}e^{\varepsilon f(x)}.

Thus,
(iii)
holds with
C=C_{2}C_{1}
.

(
iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii).

Assume
(iii)
: there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
C>0
such that for all
t>0
and almost every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

W_{t}(e^{\varepsilon f})(x)\leq Ce^{\varepsilon f(x)}.

We will show that this implies
e^{\varepsilon f}\in A_{1}
.
Recall that the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function
M
satisfies the pointwise inequality

M(g)(x)\leq C_{n}\sup_{t>0}W_{t}(g)(x)\qquad\text{for any nonnegative function }g,

where
C_{n}
is a dimensional constant. This follows because the heat kernel is radial, decreasing, and integrable, and such kernels dominate the characteristic function of balls up to a constant.
Applying this to
g=e^{\varepsilon f}
, we obtain

M(e^{\varepsilon f})(x)\leq C_{n}\sup_{t>0}W_{t}(e^{\varepsilon f})(x)\leq C_{n}\cdot Ce^{\varepsilon f(x)}.

Thus
e^{\varepsilon f}\in A_{1}
with constant at most
C_{n}\cdot C
. This proves
(ii).

This completes the proof of of Theorem
1.5
.
∎

With the Theorem
1.5
and its proof, we can get a quantitative version inequality as follows.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exist constants
c,C>0
depending only on the dimension
n
such that for any
f\in\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

c\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq N(f)\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where

N(f):=\inf\left\{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log C_{0}:\varepsilon>0,\;C_{0}>1,\;\sup_{t>0}W_{t}(e^{\varepsilon f})\leq C_{0}e^{\varepsilon f}\text{ a.e.}\right\}.

Proof.

Let us prove the upper bound
N(f)\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
first.
Let
f\in\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By Bennett’s lemma (Lemma 2 in [2]), there exist dimensional constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|\widetilde{M}f-f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{2}\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
\displaystyle\widetilde{M}f(x)=\sup_{B\ni x}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}f(y)\,dy
.
Set
K:=\|\widetilde{M}f-f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. Then for any ball
B=B(x,r)
,

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}f(y)\,dy\leq f(x)+K.

Since
\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have
f\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Indeed, for any ball
B
,

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f-f_{B}|\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f-\underset{B}{\essinf}f|+|\underset{B}{\essinf}f-f_{B}|\\
\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}(f-\underset{B}{\essinf}f)\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

By the John–Nirenberg inequality for
\mathrm{BMO}
functions, there exist constants
c_{n},C_{n}>0
depending only on
n
such that for any ball
B
,

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\exp\left(\frac{c_{n}|f-f_{B}|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)\leq C_{n}.

Choose
\varepsilon=\min\left(\frac{c_{n}}{2\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}},\,\frac{1}{K}\right)
.
Because
\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, we have

\frac{c_{n}}{2\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\leq\frac{c_{n}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}.

So
\varepsilon\leq\frac{c_{n}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}
, and
\varepsilon K\leq 1
.
Now fix a ball
B=B(x,r)
. Since
f_{B}\leq f(x)+K
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}e^{\varepsilon f(y)}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}e^{\varepsilon(f(y)-f_{B})}e^{\varepsilon f_{B}}\,dy\leq e^{\varepsilon f_{B}}\cdot\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}e^{\varepsilon|f(y)-f_{B}|}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq e^{\varepsilon f_{B}}\cdot\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\exp\left(\frac{c_{n}|f(y)-f_{B}|}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}}\right)dy\leq e^{\varepsilon f_{B}}\,C_{n}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}e^{\varepsilon(f(x)+K)}=C_{n}e^{\varepsilon K}e^{\varepsilon f(x)}\leq C_{n}e\,e^{\varepsilon f(x)}.

Taking the supremum over all balls containing
x
yields

M(e^{\varepsilon f})(x)\leq C_{n}e\,e^{\varepsilon f(x)}.

Hence,
e^{\varepsilon f}\in A_{1}
with
A_{1}
constant at most
C_{n}e
.
A standard comparison gives a constant
C^{\prime}_{n}>0
(depending only on
n
) such that for any nonnegative function
g
,

W_{t}g(x)\leq C^{\prime}_{n}Mg(x)\quad\text{for all }t>0,\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Applying this to
g=e^{\varepsilon f}
, we get

\sup_{t>0}W_{t}(e^{\varepsilon f})(x)\leq C^{\prime}_{n}M(e^{\varepsilon f})(x)\leq C^{\prime}_{n}C_{n}e\,e^{\varepsilon f(x)}.

Thus, we have found
\varepsilon>0
and
C_{0}=C^{\prime}_{n}C_{n}e
such that

\sup_{t>0}W_{t}(e^{\varepsilon f})(x)\leq C_{0}e^{\varepsilon f}(x)\quad\text{a.e.}\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Note that
\varepsilon\geq\min\left(\frac{c_{n}}{2\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}},\,\frac{1}{c_{2}\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\right)
because
K\leq c_{2}\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.
Hence, there exists a constant
c^{\prime}>0
(depending only on
n
) such that
\displaystyle\varepsilon\geq\frac{c^{\prime}}{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}
. Consequently,

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log C_{0}\leq\frac{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{c^{\prime}}\log(C^{\prime}_{n}C_{n}e)=C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Therefore, we get that

N(f)\leq C\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Now, we prove the lower bound
c\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq N(f)
.
Assume that for some
\varepsilon>0
and
C_{0}>1
we have

(3.1)

\sup_{t>0}W_{t}(e^{\varepsilon f})(x)\leq C_{0}e^{\varepsilon f(x)}\qquad\text{for a.e. }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Let
B
be a ball of radius
r
. Set
t=r^{2}
. For a.e.
x\in B
, there exists
0<c_{n}<1
such that for all
y\in B
,

(4\pi t)^{-n/2}e^{-|x-y|^{2}/(4t)}\geq c_{n}|B|^{-1},

since
|x-y|\leq 2r=2\sqrt{t}
.
Then for any
x\in B
,

(3.2)

W_{t}(e^{\varepsilon f})(x)\geq\int_{B}(4\pi t)^{-n/2}e^{-|x-y|^{2}/(4t)}e^{\varepsilon f(y)}dy\\
\geq c_{n}|B|^{-1}\int_{B}e^{\varepsilon f(y)}dy.

Combining (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) gives

(3.3)

\displaystyle c_{n}|B|^{-1}\int_{B}e^{\varepsilon f(y)}dy\leq C_{0}e^{\varepsilon f(x)}\quad\text{a.e.}\ \ x\in B.

Let
a_{B}=\essinf_{B}f
. Then (
3.3
) implies

(3.4)

\displaystyle c_{n}|B|^{-1}\int_{B}e^{\varepsilon(f(y)-a_{B})}dy\leq C_{0}e^{\varepsilon(f(x)-a_{B})}\quad\text{a.e.}\ \ x\in B.

Since
a_{B}
is the essential infimum of
f
over
B
, for any
\delta>0
there exists
B_{\delta}\subset B
such that
|B_{\delta}|>0
, and for any
x_{\delta}\in B_{\delta}
we have
f(x_{\delta})-a_{B}\leq\delta
. Applying (
3.4
) to
x_{\delta}
,

c_{n}|B|^{-1}\int_{B}e^{\varepsilon(f(y)-a)}dy\leq C_{0}e^{\varepsilon\delta}.

Letting
\delta\to 0^{+}
yields

|B|^{-1}\int_{B}e^{\varepsilon(f(y)-a)}dy\leq\frac{C_{0}}{c_{n}}.

By Jensen’s inequality,

\exp\left(\varepsilon|B|^{-1}\int_{B}(f(y)-a)dy\right)\leq|B|^{-1}\int_{B}e^{\varepsilon(f(y)-a)}dy\leq\frac{C_{0}}{c_{n}}.

Taking logarithms,

\varepsilon|B|^{-1}\int_{B}(f(y)-a)dy\leq\log\left(\frac{C_{0}}{c_{n}}\right).

Hence,

|B|^{-1}\int_{B}(f(y)-a)dy\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log\left(\frac{C_{0}}{c_{n}}\right).

Since this holds for every ball
B
,

\|f\|_{\text{\rm BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\log\left(\frac{C_{0}}{c_{n}}\right).

The pair
(\varepsilon,C_{0})
is arbitrary satisfying (
3.1
). Taking the infimum over all such pairs, we obtain

\|f\|_{\text{\rm BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\frac{1}{c}N(f),

where
c>0
depends only on
n
. More precisely,
N(f)\geq c_{n}^{\prime}\|f\|_{\text{\rm BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
with
c_{n}^{\prime}=c_{n}/\log(C_{0})
.
∎

4.
Non-linearity of
{\rm BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

In this section, we give a simple example of functions in
{\rm BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
n=1
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
f(x)=-\ln|x|\cdot\chi_{\{|x|>0\}}(x),x\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
f\in{\rm BLO}(\mathbb{R})

while
-f\notin{\rm BLO}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

For any
I=(a,b)
with
-\infty<a<0<b<\infty
,

\underset{I}{\essinf}\ (-f)=-\infty.

Thus
-f\notin{\rm BLO}(\mathbb{R})
. Now we prove
f\in{\rm BLO}(\mathbb{R})
. To this end, it suffices to show that
there exists a positive constant
C
such that for any interval
I
,

(4.1)

f_{I}-\underset{I}{\essinf}\ f\leq C.

For any given interval
I=(a,b)
, we consider the following three cases.

(i)

0\leq a<b<\infty
. In this case,

f_{I}=\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{a}^{b}-\ln xdx=\frac{-1}{b-a}[b\ln b-a\ln a-(b-a)]=1-\frac{b\ln b-a\ln a}{b-a}.

Since
\underset{I}{\essinf}\ f=f(b)=-\ln b
, observe that if
a=0
, then

f_{I}-\underset{I}{\essinf}\ f=1.

On the other hand, for
a>0
, by
b/a>1
, we conclude that

\displaystyle f_{I}-\underset{I}{\essinf}\ f

\displaystyle=1-\frac{b\ln b-a\ln a-(b-a)\ln b}{b-a}=1-\frac{a(\ln b-\ln a)}{b-a}=1-\frac{\ln\frac{b}{a}}{b/a-1}<1.

(ii)

a<b\leq 0
. In this case, by similarity we also have that

f_{I}-\underset{I}{\essinf}\ f\leq 1.

(iii)

a<0<b
. Observe that

\displaystyle f_{I}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b-a}\left[\int_{a}^{0}+\int_{0}^{b}\right](-\ln|x|)dx=\frac{1}{b-a}[a\ln(-a)-a-b\ln b+b]=1-\frac{b\ln b-a\ln(-a)}{b-a}.

If
|a|>b
, then
\underset{I}{\essinf}\ f=f(a)=-\ln(-a)
, which implies

\displaystyle f_{I}-\underset{I}{\essinf}\ f

\displaystyle=1-\frac{b\ln b-a\ln(-a)-(b-a)\ln(-a)}{b-a}=1-\frac{b\ln\frac{b}{-a}}{b-a}=1+\frac{\ln\frac{-a}{b}}{1+\frac{-a}{b}}\leq 2.

Similarly, if
|a|\leq b
, then
\underset{I}{\essinf}\ f=f(b)=-\ln b.
This yields

\displaystyle f_{I}-\underset{I}{\essinf}\ f

\displaystyle=1-\frac{b\ln b-a\ln(-a)-(b-a)\ln b}{b-a}=1+\frac{\ln\frac{b}{-a}}{\frac{b}{-a}+1}\leq 2.

By summarizing the three cases above, we conclude that (
4.1
) holds, which completes the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

The above example in the proposition shows that, the
\mathrm{BLO}
space is not a linear space. Especially, when
f,g\in\mathrm{BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
-f
and
f-g
maybe not in
\mathrm{BLO}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
But, we still have the following stability property under
L^{\infty}
perturbation.

Theorem 4.2
.

If
f\in\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
f-g\in\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,

\|f-g\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{BLO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+2\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The proof of the theorem above is easy by using the definition of
\rm{BLO}
space. We leave it to the interested readers.
