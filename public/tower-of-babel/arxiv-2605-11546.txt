The Entropy of Floating-Point Numbers
Sultan Daniels * , Samuel H. D’Ambrosia * , Michael R. DeWeese, and Anant Sahai

Abstract
Here we present an analytic approximation for the entropy of floating-point numbers, along with bounds on the error of this approximation. It is well-known that the differential entropy is tightly linked to the discrete entropy of a uniformly quantized random variable. Our approximation uncovers a different quantity that provides this link for floating-point quantization. Additionally, we prove that the entropy of a floating-point quantized random variable is approximately unchanged under scaling. Closed-form expressions for the floating-point entropy of common distributions are provided and compared to exact results. * SMD and SHD contributed equally to this work. This work was supported in part by the H2H8 Nonprofit Organization.

I
Introduction

Floating point numbers are a common choice for the discrete representation of continuous variables
[
1
,
2
]
.
This representation uses a finite register of binary bits to represent a real value in binary scientific notation.
Today, floating-point numbers play a central role in modern deep learning algorithms and hardware
[
3
,
4
]
,
and there has been renewed interest in developing novel floating-point formats that find the right tradeoffs between precision, dynamic range, memory, computational speed and numerical stability
[
5
,
6
]
.

In the design of variable-rate quantizers,
the objective is to find the best quantizer that minimizes entropy and distortion
[
7
]
. It was shown by
[
8
]
, that in the high-resolution limit, as the number of quantization levels are taken to infinity, uniform quantizers achieve this optimality.

Here, we consider a fixed non-uniform quantizer: a floating-point number, and ask the basic question, what is its output entropy?
We present a predictive analytic approximation for this entropy, through extending the exact relation between differential entropy and the discrete entropy of uniform quantizers with finite levels given in
[
9
]
to the setting of nonuniform quantization (Section
III
), and applying a smoothing approximation for the floating-point bin size (Section
IV
). This allows for closed-form expressions for the entropy of common distributions represented on floating-point numbers (Table
I
), which can be compared to the numerically evaluated exact discrete entropy (Figure
1(d)
). Bounds on the error of these approximations are provided in Sections
III-B
and
IV
.
Finally, we prove the scale-invariance property of the analog of the differential entropy for floating-point quantization (Section
V
), which predicts that the output entropy of a floating point representation is left unchanged when the underlying continuous random variable is scaled by a constant.
This contrasts the scale-dependence of differential entropy.

II
The Structure of a Floating-point Number

A normalized floating-point number stores a real value
x\in\mathbb{R}
in the form

\displaystyle x_{fp}(x)=s_{fp}(x)\times 2^{e_{fp}(x)}\times(1+m_{fp}(x)),

(1)

where
s_{fp}(x)
corresponds to the sign of the number
x
encoded by a single bit,
e_{fp}(x)
is the exponent of the number
x
encoded using
E
bits, and

is the mantissa or significand specifying the fractional significant digits of

encoded using
p-1
bits, where
p
is referred to as the precision of the floating-point number
[
1
,
2
]

s_{fp}(x),e_{fp}(x),
and
m_{fp}(x)
are given in Appendix
D

The floating-point format can be thought of as a nonuniform quantizer for a continuous random variable
X
.
The quantization scheme is a map from the real line to
K
representable values

\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{K}\}
, where

u_{1}<u_{2}<\dots<u_{K}
.
We will refer to the resulting quantized discrete random variable as
X_{fp}
. In this paper, we will consider a simplified floating-point format, with rounding to the nearest representable value, that does not use subnormal numbers (as defined in
[
1
]
). The structure of a floating-point number is shown in Fig.
1(a)
. See
[
1
,
2
]
for further details on the floating-point representation.

For
E

is an integer between
e_{min}=-(2^{E-1}-1)
and
e_{max}=2^{E-1}

Primarily controlled by

\mathbb{U}\triangleq\left[-2^{e_{max}+1}+2^{e_{max}-p},\,\,2^{e_{max}+1}-2^{e_{max}-p}\right]^{d}

[
7
]

Let
\Delta:\mathbb{U}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

encode the bin sizes of the quantization scheme for a single floating-point number.
Within each exponent interval
[2^{e_{fp}},2^{e_{fp}+1})

2^{p-1}
uniformly spaced values. This allows us to define the quantization bin size within an exponent interval as,

\Delta(x)=\frac{2^{e_{fp}+1}-2^{e_{fp}}}{2^{p-1}}=2^{e_{fp}-(p-1)}\text{ if }2^{e_{fp}}\leq x<2^{e_{fp}+1}.

(2)

Bins at the edges of the granular region and at the boundaries of exponent intervals are handled in Appendix
C

We can generalize this to the case of vector quantization, where we assume each component of the vector is independently quantized. Let
\mathbf{X}\sim f_{\mathbf{X}}
be a
d

\bm{\Delta}(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\prod_{j=1}^{d}\Delta(x_{j}),\qquad\log\bm{\Delta}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\log\Delta(x_{j}).

(3)

In this paper,

is assumed to be base 2. Let

index the

-dimensional quantization bins, where

\frac{u_{i}+u_{i-1}}{2}&2\leq i\leq K,\end{cases}

(4)

\qquad b_{i}=\begin{cases}\frac{u_{i+1}+u_{i}}{2}&1\leq i\leq K-1\\[2.0pt]

(5)

where for each

B_{\mathbf{i}}\triangleq\prod_{j=1}^{d}[a_{i_{j}},b_{i_{j}}].

(6)

Let

(7)

where

III
Relation of differential entropy to discrete floating-point entropy

We can extend the analysis of
[
9
]
, to the case of quantization with nonuniform bins, to derive an exact identity relating the discrete entropy in (
8
) to the differential entropy of
\mathbf{X}
. Define the piecewise-uniform density

g(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\sum_{\mathbf{i}}\frac{p_{\mathbf{i}}}{\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{i}}\right\}},

(9)

where
\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|=\bm{\Delta}(\mathbf{x})
for
\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{i}}
is the volume of each rectangular bin. By construction,
g
is constant on each bin and integrates to one across all bins.

By the definition of Kullback-Leibler divergence,

\displaystyle D\left(f_{\mathbf{X}}||g\right)

\displaystyle=-h(\mathbf{X})-\int_{\mathbb{U}^{d}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\log g(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}.

(10)

Since
g(\mathbf{x})=p_{\mathbf{i}}/\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|
for
\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{i}}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{U}^{d}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\log g(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{i}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\log p_{\mathbf{i}}-\sum_{\mathbf{i}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\log\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|.

(11)

Substituting Eq. (
11
) into Eq. (
10
) yields

\displaystyle D\left(f_{\mathbf{X}}||g\right)

\displaystyle=-h(\mathbf{X})-\sum_{\mathbf{i}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\log p_{\mathbf{i}}+\sum_{\mathbf{i}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\log\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|

\displaystyle=-h(\mathbf{X})+H(\mathbf{X}_{fp})+\mathbb{E}[\log\bm{\Delta}(\mathbf{X})],

(12)

or equivalently,

\boxed{H(\mathbf{X}_{fp})=h(\mathbf{X})-\mathbb{E}[\log\bm{\Delta}(\mathbf{X})]+D\left(f_{\mathbf{X}}||g\right).}

(13)

A first approximation

The first approximation made corresponds to neglecting the nonnegative correction term
D\left(f_{\mathbf{X}}||g\right)
:

\boxed{\tilde{H}(\mathbf{X}_{fp})\triangleq h(\mathbf{X})-\mathbb{E}[\log\bm{\Delta}(\mathbf{X})].}

(14)

III-B

Distributions that lead to small approximation error

Since (
14
) neglects the KL divergence term,
two questions arise: what properties of
f_{\mathbf{X}}
ensure that this correction term is small, and what properties make this term large?
Since
D\left(f_{\mathbf{X}}||g\right)
captures how far the density
f_{\mathbf{X}}
is from uniform on each bin, it will be shown that “spikiness” is the key property that determines this term, with spikes in small bins less harmful than spikes in large bins. Theorem
1
makes this precise and Remark
1
provides more geometric intuition.

For
\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{i}}
with
p_{\mathbf{i}}>0
, let
g_{\mathbf{i}}\triangleq g(\mathbf{x})
and define the relative density ratio by

R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\frac{f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})}{g_{\mathbf{i}}}.

For
\lambda>0
, define

\displaystyle L_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda)\triangleq\frac{1}{\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|}\left|\left\{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{i}}:R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\geq\lambda\right\}\right|,

(15)

which encodes the normalized width of
R_{\mathbf{i}}
’s
\lambda
-superlevel set.

As the average over a bin
g_{\mathbf{i}}
follows the absolute peak density in that bin, if
g_{\mathbf{i}}
is held constant, a peak of
R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})
of a certain height, then constrains the peak’s width. The following proposition proves that a relative peak of height
\lambda\geq 1
can occupy at most
1/\lambda
of the bin width
|B_{\mathbf{i}}|
.

Proposition 1
(Peak height-width feasibility bound)

For every bin
B_{\mathbf{i}}
where
p_{\mathbf{i}}>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{Unif}(B_{\mathbf{i}})}\left[R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{X})\right]=1.

(16)

If
\lambda\geq 1
, this induces the peak height-width feasibility bound

\displaystyle L_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}.

(17)

Proof:

First, observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{Unif}(B_{\mathbf{i}})}\left[R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{X})\right]=\frac{1}{\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|}\int_{B_{\mathbf{i}}}R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}

(18)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|}\int_{B_{\mathbf{i}}}\frac{f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})}{g_{\mathbf{i}}}\,d\mathbf{x}=\frac{1}{\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|g_{\mathbf{i}}}\int_{B_{\mathbf{i}}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}

(19)

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{\mathbf{i}}}{\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|g_{\mathbf{i}}}=1.

(20)

Let
\pi_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda)\triangleq\left\{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{i}}:R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\geq\lambda\right\}
. For
\lambda\geq 1
, by the definition of
L_{\mathbf{i}}
,

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|}\int_{B_{\mathbf{i}}}R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}\geq\frac{1}{\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|}\int_{\pi_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda)}R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}

(21)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\frac{1}{\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|}\left|\pi_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda)\right|=\lambda L_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda).

(22)

Therefore

L_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}.

∎

Now, intuitively, we bound the KL divergence by changing variables and integrating over relative peak heights
\lambda

Theorem 1
(Bounds on the KL divergence)

Under the convention
0\log 0=0

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathbf{i}}\triangleq\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{i}}}R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{g_{\mathbf{i}}}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{i}}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})<\infty.

(23)

Then, for all

,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\left[t\log t-(t-1)\log e\right]L_{\mathbf{i}}(t)

(24)

\displaystyle\leq D\left(f_{\mathbf{X}}||g\right)\leq\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\int_{1}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{i}}}\left(\log\lambda+\log e\right)L_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda.

(25)

See Appendix
A
for the proof. Applying the peak-width feasibility bound in Proposition
1
leads to the looser worst-case upper bound
D\left(f_{\mathbf{X}}||g\right)\leq\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\int_{1}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{i}}}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\log\lambda+\log e\right)\,d\lambda

\lambda
until the maximum relative height
\Lambda_{\mathbf{i}}
over each bin.
We can provide more geometric intuition by assuming the density only has one peak in each bin.

Remark 1
(The one peak per bin height-width upper bound)

Suppose the above-average region in

B_{\mathbf{i}}
is contained in a measurable set
P_{\mathbf{i}}\subset B_{\mathbf{i}}
with width

\left|P_{\mathbf{i}}\right|=w_{\mathbf{i}}.

Let
H_{\mathbf{i}}\geq g_{\mathbf{i}}
be the max peak height of the density, so
f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\leq H_{\mathbf{i}}
for all
\mathbf{x}\in P_{\mathbf{i}}
,

Then, for every
\lambda\geq 1
, the superlevel set
\left\{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{i}}:R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\geq\lambda\right\}
is contained in
P_{\mathbf{i}}
, so by the definition of
L_{i}
,

L_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda)\leq\frac{w_{\mathbf{i}}}{\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|}.
Then, the upper bound in Theorem
1
gives

\displaystyle D\left(f_{\mathbf{X}}||g\right)=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}}\int_{B_{\mathbf{i}}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\log{\frac{f_{\mathbf{X}}}{g_{\mathbf{i}}}}\,d\mathbf{x}

(26)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\frac{w_{\mathbf{i}}}{\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|}\int_{1}^{H_{\mathbf{i}}/g_{\mathbf{i}}}\left(\log\lambda+\log e\right)\,d\lambda

(27)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\frac{w_{\mathbf{i}}}{\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|}\frac{H_{\mathbf{i}}}{g_{\mathbf{i}}}\log\frac{H_{\mathbf{i}}}{g_{\mathbf{i}}}=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}}w_{\mathbf{i}}H_{\mathbf{i}}\log\frac{H_{\mathbf{i}}\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|}{p_{\mathbf{i}}}.

(28)

This is the one peak per bin height-width bound. The ratio
\frac{H_{\mathbf{i}}\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|}{p_{\mathbf{i}}}\geq 1
is the area of the rectangle with the height of the peak and the width of the entire bin, divided by the probability mass in that bin.
For smooth densities and small bins,
\frac{H_{\mathbf{i}}\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|}{p_{\mathbf{i}}}\approx 1
.
The KL divergence bound increases only logarithmically with this ratio, while for
w_{\mathbf{i}}H_{\mathbf{i}}
, the maximum area under the peak, the KL divergence bound increases linearly. In the setting of floating-point representations, this shows the absolute height
H_{\mathbf{i}}
of a peak is not the only important quantity for controlling the KL divergence term. The KL divergence term can still be small for peaked distributions if the peaks have very small width, or if they are near the origin where the bins are very small.

IV
Smoothing and Extending the Bin Size Function

Our second approximation will alter the bin size function given in (
2
). First, we can smooth the steps by introducing a best-fit linear approximation as shown as the dashed red line in Figure
1(a)
, which uses
e_{fp}(x)\approx e_{s}(x)\triangleq\log(|x|/\sqrt{2})
. This allows us to define a smoothed bin size function
\Delta_{s}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
by

\Delta_{s}(x)\triangleq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|x|\cdot 2^{1-p}\approx\Delta(x).

(29)

Extending the domain of the bin size function from the granular region
\mathbb{U}
to
\mathbb{R}
allows for initially truncated integrals to then be given in concise closed form expressions. Again, we can generalize to
d
-dimensional vector quantization by defining
\bm{\Delta}_{s}(\mathbf{X})\triangleq\prod_{j=1}^{d}\Delta_{s}(X_{j})
. Building upon the first approximation
\tilde{H}(\mathbf{X}_{fp})
given by (
14
), we define the smoothed and extended approximation

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \tilde{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X}_{fp})&\triangleq h(\mathbf{X})-\mathbb{E}[\log(\bm{\Delta}_{s}(\mathbf{X}))]\\
&=d\left(p-\frac{1}{2}\right)+h(\mathbf{X})-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\log|X_{j}|\right],\end{aligned}}

(30)

where we assume the integrals defining the differential entropy and first log-moment of the folded (absolute value) continuous distribution are given by integrals over the whole real line.

For a
d
-dimensional quantization, we prove that the error caused by introducing this smooth approximation is bounded by
d/2
, while the extension error is given simply by the magnitude of the contribution of overflow and underflow values to the entropy integral. For many distributions, the smoothing error will be much smaller than
d/2
due to cancellation in the overestimation and underestimation of the bin size.

Theorem 2
(Error bound for smoothing and extending the bin-size function)

Let
\mathbf{X}\sim f_{\mathbf{X}}
be a
d
-dimensional random vector with the probability density
f_{\mathbf{X}}
. Let
\mathbb{O}^{d}\triangleq\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathbb{U}^{d}
be the overflow region, and the underflow region be
\mathbb{S}^{d}\triangleq[-2^{e_{min}},2^{e_{min}}]^{d}

\left|\tilde{H}(\mathbf{X}_{fp})-\tilde{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X}_{fp})\right|\leq\frac{d}{2}+\epsilon,

(31)

where

\epsilon=\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\cup\mathbb{O}^{d}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\log\left[f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\bm{\Delta}_{s}(\mathbf{x})\right]d\mathbf{x}\right|.

(32)

Proof:

The bound can be shown by considering the ratio between bin sizes. There are three types of bins for which to bound this ratio: bins on the interior of an exponent block, bins on the outer edges, and bins on the boundary between exponent blocks. Here, the bound is given for the interior bins, the other two cases proceed similarly and are given in Appendix
C
. The bin size within an exponent block

is defined by Eq. (
2
),

\Delta(x_{j})=2^{e-(p-1)}\quad\text{for }2^{e}\leq|x_{j}|<2^{e+1},

(33)

and within this range
\Delta_{s}(x_{j})=\frac{|x_{j}|}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot 2^{1-p}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{2^{e}}{\sqrt{2}}2^{1-p}\leq\Delta_{s}(x_{j})\leq\frac{2^{e+1}}{\sqrt{2}}2^{1-p}

(34)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\leq\frac{\Delta_{s}(x_{j})}{\Delta(x_{j})}<\sqrt{2}.

From (
14
), (
30
), and the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\big|\tilde{H}(\mathbf{X}_{fp})-

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X}_{fp})\big|\leq\left|\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{U}\setminus\mathbb{S}}f_{x_{j}}\log\left[\frac{\Delta_{s}(x_{j})}{\Delta(x_{j})}\right]dx_{j}\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\cup\mathbb{O}^{d}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\log\left[f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\bm{\Delta}_{s}(\mathbf{x})\right]d\mathbf{x}\right|.

(35)

Applying the bound in (
34
) to the integral over
\mathbb{U}\setminus\mathbb{S}
term in
35
completes the proof.
∎

This implies that the smoothing and extending approximation is strictly bound by
d/2
plus the contribution of the overflow and underflow parts of the continuous distribution to be quantized.

Here, we provide a simple proof that the approximate floating-point entropy does not change when the underlying continuous random variable is scaled by a constant. This predicts that the exact floating point entropy is approximately scale-invariant as long as the probability of overflow and underflow is low. Figure

Discussion

For a sufficiently smooth distribution and sufficiently high range as set by
E
, the entropy of a floating-point quantized random variable can be approximated by

\boxed{H(X_{fp})\approx\tilde{H}_{s}(X_{fp})\triangleq p-\frac{1}{2}-\mathbb{E}[\log(|X|f_{X}(X))],}

(41)

where the differential floating-point entropy,
-\mathbb{E}[\log(|X|f_{X}(X))]
, is the differential entropy
h(X)
minus the first log-moment of its corresponding folded distribution
\mathbb{E}[\log|X|]
. The error on this approximation is bounded by

\boxed{|H(X_{fp})-\tilde{H}_{s}(X_{fp})|\leq\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon+D\left(f_{X}||g\right)},

(42)

where
\epsilon
is the contribution of the underflow and overflow regions to the entropy, and
D\left(f_{X}||g\right)
is the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the true distribution and its piecewise constant approximation.

Appendix A
Bounds on the KL divergence term

Now to prove Theorem
1
. Its statement is copied here for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem
1
(Bounds on the KL divergence)

Under the convention
0\log 0=0
, assume

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}\triangleq\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{i}}}R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{g_{\mathbf{i}}}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{i}}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})<\infty.

(43)

Then, for all
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\left[t\log t-(t-1)\log e\right]L_{\mathbf{i}}(t)\leq D\left(f_{\mathbf{X}}||g\right)\leq\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}}p_{\mathbf{i}}\int_{1}^{\Lambda_{i}}\left(\log\lambda+\log e\right)L_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda.

(44)

Proof:

The KL divergence can be written as

\displaystyle D\left(f_{\mathbf{X}}||g\right)=\int f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\log\frac{f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})}{g(\mathbf{x})}\,d\mathbf{x}=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{i}}\int_{B_{\mathbf{i}}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\log\frac{f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})}{g_{\mathbf{i}}}\,d\mathbf{x}.

(45)

To prove the upper bound, since for all
\mathbf{i}
, when
\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{i}}
,

\displaystyle f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\log\frac{f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})}{g_{\mathbf{i}}}\leq 0\quad\text{whenever }f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\leq g_{\mathbf{i}},

(46)

we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\mathbf{i}}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\log\frac{f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})}{g_{\mathbf{i}}}\,d\mathbf{x}\leq\int_{\left\{R_{\mathbf{i}}\geq 1\right\}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\log R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}=g_{\mathbf{i}}\int_{B_{\mathbf{i}}}R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\log R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\geq 1\right\}}\,d\mathbf{x}.

(47)

For
r\geq 1
,

\displaystyle r\log r=\int_{1}^{r}\log\lambda\,d\lambda+\log e(r-1).

(48)

Thus, for every
\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{i}}
,

\displaystyle R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\log R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\geq 1\right\}}=\int_{1}^{R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})}\left(\log\lambda+\log e\right)\,d\lambda\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\geq 1\right\}}=\int_{1}^{\Lambda_{i}}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\lambda\leq R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\right\}}\left(\log\lambda+\log e\right)\,d\lambda.

(49)

Since for
\lambda\geq 1
,
\log\lambda\geq 0
, Tonelli’s theorem can be applied to obtain the bound,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\mathbf{i}}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\log\frac{f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})}{g_{\mathbf{i}}}\,d\mathbf{x}\leq g_{\mathbf{i}}\int_{B_{\mathbf{i}}}\int_{1}^{\Lambda_{i}}\left(\log\lambda+\log e\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\geq\lambda\right\}}\,d\lambda\,d\mathbf{x}

(50)

\displaystyle=g_{\mathbf{i}}\int_{1}^{\Lambda_{i}}\left(\log\lambda+\log e\right)\int_{B_{\mathbf{i}}}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\geq\lambda\right\}}\,d\mathbf{x}\,d\lambda=g_{\mathbf{i}}\int_{1}^{\Lambda_{i}}\left(\log\lambda+\log e\right)\left|\left\{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{i}}:R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\geq\lambda\right\}\right|\,d\lambda

(51)

\displaystyle=g_{\mathbf{i}}\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|\int_{1}^{\Lambda_{i}}\left(\log\lambda+\log e\right)L_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda=p_{\mathbf{i}}\int_{1}^{\Lambda_{i}}\left(\log\lambda+\log e\right)L_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda.

(52)

Summing over
\mathbf{i}
yields the upper bound on
D\left(f_{\mathbf{X}}||g\right)
.

Now to prove the lower bound, let
\phi(r)\triangleq r\log r-\log e(r-1)
. Taking its first and second derivatives,

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=\log r,\qquad\phi^{\prime\prime}(r)=\frac{\log e}{r},

(53)

so
\phi
is decreasing on
\left(0,1\right)
, increasing on

\left(1,\infty\right)
, and
\phi(1)=0.

Thus

\phi(r)\geq 0

for all
r\geq 0.

Moreover, for

\lambda\geq 1
, monotonicity of
\phi
on
[1,\infty)
gives

\phi(r)\geq\phi(\lambda)
whenever
r\geq\lambda
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\phi\left(R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\phi(\lambda)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\geq\lambda\right\}}.

(54)

By the monotonicity of expectation, we have

\displaystyle p_{\mathbf{i}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{Unif}(B_{\mathbf{i}})}\left[\phi\left(R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{X})\right)\right]\geq p_{\mathbf{i}}\phi(\lambda)\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{Unif}(B_{\mathbf{i}})}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{X})\geq\lambda\right\}}\right]=p_{\mathbf{i}}\phi(\lambda)\frac{1}{\left|B_{\mathbf{i}}\right|}\left|\left\{\mathbf{x}\in B_{\mathbf{i}}:R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{x})\geq\lambda\right\}\right|=p_{\mathbf{i}}\phi(\lambda)L_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda).

(55)

Finally, substituting the definition of
\phi
and using Lemma
1
(which gives
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{Unif}(B_{\mathbf{i}})}\left[R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{X})\right]=1
) yields

\displaystyle p_{\mathbf{i}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{Unif}(B_{\mathbf{i}})}\left[R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{X})\log R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{X})-\log e\left(R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{X})-1\right)\right]=p_{\mathbf{i}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{Unif}(B_{\mathbf{i}})}\left[R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{X})\log R_{\mathbf{i}}(\mathbf{X})\right]\geq p_{\mathbf{i}}\left[\lambda\log\lambda-(\lambda-1)\log e\right]L_{\mathbf{i}}(\lambda).

(56)

This holds for every
\lambda\geq 1
; summing over
\mathbf{i}
and renaming
\lambda
to
t
gives the lower bound.
∎

Appendix B
The Entropy for Commonly Distributed Random Variables

We will proceed to closed form formulas for the discrete entropy of commonly distributed random variables represented in floating-point, applying Eq. (
41
). Throughout, let
p
be the precision of the floating point number, the differential entropy be

h(X)\triangleq-\int f_{X}(x)\log[f_{X}(x)]dx,

(57)

and the approximate bin size be

\Delta_{s}(x)\triangleq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|x|\cdot 2^{1-p}.

(58)

B-A

Centered Univariate Gaussian Distribution

Let
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{x}^{2})
. The approximate entropy of the floating point quantized discrete random variable
X_{fp}
is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{s}(X_{fp})

\displaystyle\triangleq h(X)-\mathbb{E}_{f_{X}}\left[\log\left(\Delta_{s}(X)\right)\right]

(59)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log[2\pi e\sigma^{2}_{x}]-\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{\exp(-\frac{x^{2}}{2\sigma_{x}^{2}})}{\sigma_{x}\sqrt{2\pi}}\log[\Delta_{s}(x)]\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log[2\pi e\sigma^{2}_{x}]-\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\frac{\exp(-\frac{x^{2}}{2\sigma_{x}^{2}})}{\sigma_{x}\sqrt{2\pi}}(\log[|x|/\sqrt{2}]-(p-1))\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log[2\pi e\sigma^{2}_{x}]+(p-1)-2\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{\exp(-\frac{x^{2}}{2\sigma_{x}^{2}})}{\sigma_{x}\sqrt{2\pi}}\log[x/\sqrt{2}]\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log[2\pi e\sigma^{2}_{x}]+(p-1)+\bigg(1+\frac{\gamma_{e}}{2\ln[2]}-\frac{1}{2}\log[\sigma^{2}_{x}]\bigg)

\displaystyle=(p-1)+\frac{1}{2}\log[2\pi e]+1+\frac{\gamma_{e}}{2\ln[2]}

\displaystyle=p+\frac{1}{2}\log[2\pi e]+\frac{\gamma_{e}}{2\ln[2]}.

where
\gamma_{e}
is Euler’s constant.

B-B

Uniform Distribution

Let
X\sim\mathrm{Unif}\left(a,b\right)
and
b>a\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{s}(X)

\displaystyle=h(X)-\mathbb{E}_{f_{X}}\left[\log\left(\Delta_{s}(X)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\log(b-a)-\frac{1}{b-a}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\log\left(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{2}}\right)+(1-p)dx

\displaystyle=\log(b-a)+p-1+\log(e)

\displaystyle+\log\sqrt{2}+\frac{a\log|a|-b\log|b|}{b-a}.

\displaystyle=p-1+\log(\sqrt{2}e(b-a))+\frac{a\log|a|-b\log|b|}{b-a}.

This implies that for a centered uniform distribution, where
a=-b
and
b>0

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{s}(X)

\displaystyle=p-1+\log(2\sqrt{2}eb)+\frac{-b\log b-b\log b}{2b}

\displaystyle=p-1+\log(2\sqrt{2}eb)-\log b

\displaystyle=p+\frac{1}{2}+\log(e)

\displaystyle=h(\mathbf{X})-\mathbb{E}_{f_{\mathbf{X}}}\left[\log\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}\Delta_{s}(x_{i})\right)\right]

(94)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\left((2\pi e)^{d}|\Sigma|\right)-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}_{f_{\mathbf{X}}}\left[\log\left(\Delta_{s}(X_{i})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\left((2\pi e)^{d}|\Sigma|\right)-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}_{f_{X_{i}}}\left[\log\left(\Delta_{s}(X_{i})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\left((2\pi e)^{d}|\Sigma|\right)

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\left[-(p-1)-\left(1+\frac{\gamma_{e}}{2\ln[2]}-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\Sigma_{ii}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\left((2\pi e)^{d}|\Sigma|\right)+d\left(p+\frac{\gamma_{e}}{2\ln[2]}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\log\left(\Sigma_{ii}\right)

\displaystyle=d\left(\frac{1}{2}\log\left(2\pi e\right)+\left(p+\frac{\gamma_{e}}{2\ln[2]}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}(\Sigma)}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}\Sigma_{ii}}\right).

Notice that the last term is zero when
\Sigma
is diagonal.

Appendix C
Bound on error from outer clipping bins and exponent boundary bins

Here we provide the full proof to Theorem
2
. Its statement is copied here for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem
2
(Error bound for smoothing and extending the bin-size function)

Let
\mathbf{X}\sim f_{\mathbf{X}}
be a
d
-dimensional random vector with the probability density
f_{\mathbf{X}}
. Let
\mathbb{O}^{d}\triangleq\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathbb{U}^{d}
be the overflow region, and the underflow region be
\mathbb{S}^{d}\triangleq[-2^{e_{min}},2^{e_{min}}]^{d}
. Then

\left|\tilde{H}(\mathbf{X}_{fp})-\tilde{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X}_{fp})\right|\leq\frac{d}{2}+\epsilon,

(95)

where

\epsilon=\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\cup\mathbb{O}^{d}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\log\left[f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\bm{\Delta}_{s}(\mathbf{x})\right]d\mathbf{x}\right|.

(96)

Proof:

There are 3 types of bins for which we will bound the error: bins on the interior of an exponent block, bins on the outer edges, and bins on the boundary between exponent blocks (regions for which a single exponent value applies). The bound can be shown by considering the ratio between bin sizes, case by case.

Interior Bins

For interior bins strictly within each exponent block
e
, by Eq. (
2
),

\Delta(x_{j})=2^{e-(p-1)}\quad\text{for }2^{e}\leq|x_{j}|<2^{e+1},

(97)

and within this range
\Delta_{s}(x_{j})=\frac{|x_{j}|}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot 2^{1-p}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{2^{e}}{\sqrt{2}}2^{1-p}\leq\Delta_{s}(x_{j})\leq\frac{2^{e+1}}{\sqrt{2}}2^{1-p}

(98)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\leq\frac{\Delta_{s}(x_{j})}{\Delta(x_{j})}<\sqrt{2}.

Outer clipping bins

For the outer clipping bins, by symmetry it suffices to analyze
[\frac{u_{K}+u_{K-1}}{2},2^{e_{\max}+1}-2^{e_{\max}-p}]
which is the bin on positive side of the real line. When
p\geq 2
, we know that
u_{K}=2^{e_{max}+1}(1-2^{-p})
, and
u_{K-1}=2^{e_{max}+1}(1-2^{1-p})
, so the outer clipping bin size is

\Delta(x_{j})=2^{e_{max}+1}-2^{e_{max}-p}-\frac{u_{K}+u_{K-1}}{2}=2^{e_{max}}\left(2^{1-p}\right)=2^{e_{max}-p+1}

(99)

Now, we have

\displaystyle\frac{(u_{K}+u_{K-1})}{2\sqrt{2}}2^{1-p}\leq\Delta_{s}(x_{j})\leq\frac{2^{e_{max}+1}-2^{e_{max}-p}}{\sqrt{2}}2^{1-p}

(100)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\frac{2^{e_{max}}(1-2^{-1-p}-2^{-p})}{2^{e_{max}-p}\sqrt{2}}2^{1-p}\leq\frac{\Delta_{s}(x_{j})}{\Delta(x_{j})}\leq\frac{2^{e_{max}}(1-2^{-1-p})}{2^{e_{max}-p}\sqrt{2}}2^{1-p}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\frac{2(1-2^{-1-p}-2^{-p})}{\sqrt{2}}\leq\frac{\Delta_{s}(x_{j})}{\Delta(x_{j})}\leq\frac{2(1-2^{-1-p})}{\sqrt{2}}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}(1-2^{-1-p}-2^{-p})\leq\frac{\Delta_{s}(x_{j})}{\Delta(x_{j})}\leq\sqrt{2}(1-2^{-1-p}).

When
p=1
,
u_{K}=2^{e_{max}}
and
u_{K-1}=2^{e_{max}-1}
. The true bin width is

\Delta(x_{j})=2^{e_{max}+1}-2^{e_{max}-1}-\frac{1}{2}(2^{e_{max}}+2^{e_{max}-1})=3\cdot 2^{e_{max}-2}.

(101)

Using the same bounding technique, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2^{e_{max}}+2^{e_{max}-1}}{2\sqrt{2}}\leq\Delta_{s}(x_{j})\leq\frac{2^{e_{max}+1}-2^{e_{max}-1}}{\sqrt{2}}

(102)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\frac{3\cdot 2^{e_{max}-2}}{3\cdot 2^{e_{max}-2}\sqrt{2}}\leq\frac{\Delta_{s}(x_{j})}{\Delta(x_{j})}\leq\frac{3\cdot 2^{e_{max}-1}}{3\cdot 2^{e_{max}-2}\sqrt{2}}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\leq\frac{\Delta_{s}(x_{j})}{\Delta(x_{j})}\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}.

For the outer clipping bins, we see that for all
p\geq 1
,
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\leq\frac{\Delta_{s}(x_{j})}{\Delta(x_{j})}\leq\sqrt{2}
.

Exponent boundary bins

At exponent boundaries, let the last representable value of the
e
-th exponent block be
u_{A}=2^{e+1}-2^{e-(p-1)}
. the first representable value of the
e+1
-th exponent block be
u_{B}=2^{e+1}
, and the second representable value of the
e+1
-th exponent block be
u_{C}=2^{e+1}+2^{e+1-(p-1)}
. The left boundary of the midpoint-quantization bin for
u_{B}
is
m_{L}=\frac{u_{A}+u_{B}}{2}=2^{e+1}(1-2^{-p-1})
while the right boundary is
m_{R}=\frac{u_{B}+u_{C}}{2}=2^{e+1}(1+2^{-p})
. When
x_{j}\in[m_{L},m_{R})
, the bin width is

\Delta(x_{j})=m_{R}-m_{L}=3\cdot 2^{e-p},

(103)

and the ratio
\Delta_{s}(x_{j})/\Delta(x_{j})
is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{2^{e+1}(1-2^{-p-1})}{\sqrt{2}}2^{1-p}\leq\Delta_{s}(x_{j})\leq\frac{2^{e+1}(1+2^{-p})}{\sqrt{2}}2^{1-p}

(104)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\frac{2^{e+1}(1-2^{-p-1})}{3\cdot 2^{e-p}\sqrt{2}}2^{1-p}\leq\frac{\Delta_{s}(x_{j})}{\Delta(x_{j})}\leq\frac{2^{e+1}(1+2^{-p})}{3\cdot 2^{e-p}\sqrt{2}}2^{1-p}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{2}(1-2^{-p-1})}{3}\leq\frac{\Delta_{s}(x_{j})}{\Delta(x_{j})}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{2}(1+2^{-p})}{3}.

When
p=1
the lower bound is equal to
1/\sqrt{2}
and the upper bound is equal to
\sqrt{2}
. The lower bound is monotonically increasing in
p
while the upper bound is monotonically decreasing in
p
, and both converge to
2\sqrt{2}/3
as
p\rightarrow\infty
, which is between
1/\sqrt{2}
and
\sqrt{2}
. This means both bounds lie within
[1/\sqrt{2},\,\sqrt{2}]
, so for within exponent block bins and exponent boundary bins,

-\frac{1}{2}\leq\log\left[\frac{\Delta_{s}(x_{j})}{\Delta(x_{j})}\right]<\frac{1}{2}.

(105)

Bound on entropy difference from bin ratios

Hence, for every
x_{j}\in\mathbb{U}\setminus(\mathbb{S}\cup\mathbb{O})
,

\left|\log\left[\frac{\Delta_{s}(x_{j})}{\Delta(x_{j})}\right]\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}.

(106)

The remaining points are exactly the overflow and underflow bins collected in
\mathbb{S}\cup\mathbb{O}
, where we do not claim a uniform pointwise bound and instead keep their contribution explicitly. By the triangle inequality and (
105
):

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{H}(\mathbf{X}_{fp})-\tilde{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X}_{fp})\right|=\Bigg|-\int_{\mathbb{U}^{d}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\,\log\left[f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\prod_{j=1}^{d}\Delta(x_{j})\right]d\mathbf{x}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\,\log\left[f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\prod_{j=1}^{d}\Delta_{s}(x_{j})\right]d\mathbf{x}\Bigg|

(107)

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{U}^{d}\setminus\mathbb{S}^{d}}f_{X_{j}}(x_{j})\,\log\left|\frac{\Delta_{s}(x_{j})}{\Delta(x_{j})}\right|dx_{j}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\cup\mathbb{O}^{d}}f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\,\log\left[f_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\prod_{j=1}^{d}\Delta_{s}(x_{j})\right]d\mathbf{x}\Bigg|\leq\frac{d}{2}+\epsilon.

∎

Appendix D
Computing the exact entropy of quantized random variables

Here we compute the exact discrete entropy of quantized random variables, such as normally distributed random variables held on floating point numbers.

In this paper, we will use a simple normalized floating-point format with midpoint rounding that does not use subnormal numbers (as defined in
[
1
]
) and where zero is not in the representable set.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
E\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. The following map is defined on
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
; since all distributions used in this paper are absolutely continuous, the event that the value zero is realized is probability-zero. Define

\mathrm{round}_{p}(\alpha)\triangleq\left(\argmin\limits_{i\in\{0,\dots,2^{p-1}\}}\left|\alpha-i2^{-(p-1)}\right|\right)2^{-(p-1)},

(108)

where ties in the
\argmin
function are broken towards the higher index
i
. Assuming rounding to the nearest representable value, the value stored on a floating-point number can be written as

x_{fp}(x)\triangleq s_{fp}(x)\times 2^{e_{fp}(x)}\times\left(1+m_{fp}(x)\right),

(109)

where
1
1

must be introduced to handle cases where mantissa value rounds up to the next exponent level. This occurs when

\displaystyle s_{fp}(x)

\displaystyle\triangleq(-1)^{\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x<0\right\}}},

(110)

\displaystyle\tilde{e}_{fp}(x)

\displaystyle\triangleq-\left(2^{E-1}-1\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\log|x|<-\left(2^{E-1}-1\right)\right\}}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2^{E}-1}\left[i-\left(2^{E-1}-1\right)\right]\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{i-\left(2^{E-1}-1\right)\leq\log|x|<i+1-\left(2^{E-1}-1\right)\right\}}+2^{E-1}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\log|x|\geq 2^{E-1}+1\right\}}

\displaystyle m_{fp}(x)

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathrm{round}_{p}\left(|x|2^{-\tilde{e}_{fp}(x)}-1\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\log|x|<2^{E-1}+1\right\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\mathrm{round}_{p}\left(|x|2^{-\tilde{e}_{fp}(x)}-1\right)\neq 1\right\}}

Theorem 4
(Entropy of a Clipped and Arbitrarily Midpoint Quantized Random Variable)

X
be an absolutely continuous random variable with cumulative distribution function
F

representable values in the quantization scheme and denote the set of these values as
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{K}\}

is the resulting clipped and midpoint quantized representation of

is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} H(X_{Q})&=-F\left(\frac{u_{1}+u_{2}}{2}\right)\log\left[F\left(\frac{u_{1}+u_{2}}{2}\right)\right]\\
&-\sum_{i=2}^{K-1}\left[F\left(\frac{u_{i+1}+u_{i}}{2}\right)-F\left(\frac{u_{i}+u_{i-1}}{2}\right)\right]\log\left[F\left(\frac{u_{i+1}+u_{i}}{2}\right)-F\left(\frac{u_{i}+u_{i-1}}{2}\right)\right]\\

(111)

when

K=2

K=1

Since

u_{1}\text{ if }K=1.\end{cases}

(112)

H(X_{Q})=-F\left(\frac{u_{1}+u_{2}}{2}\right)\log\left[F\left(\frac{u_{1}+u_{2}}{2}\right)\right]-\left[1-F\left(\frac{u_{1}+u_{2}}{2}\right)\right]\log\left[1-F\left(\frac{u_{1}+u_{2}}{2}\right)\right].

(113)

\displaystyle P\{X_{Q}=u_{i}\}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\int\limits_{\frac{u_{i}+u_{i-1}}{2}}^{\frac{u_{i+1}+u_{i}}{2}}f_{X}(x)dx\text{ if }i\in[2,K-1]\\

Corollary 2
(Entropy of a Random Variable Quantized to a Floating-point Number)

Let
X
be an absolutely continuous random variable with cumulative distribution function
F
. Let
X_{fp}
be the clipped and midpoint quantized floating-point representation of
X
with an
E
-bit exponent and a
(p-1)
-bit significand. The discrete entropy of
X_{fp}
is given by Theorem
4
where
K=2^{E+p}
,

\displaystyle u_{i}=\begin{cases}u^{\prime}_{i-2^{E+p-1}}\text{ if }i>2^{E+p-1}\\
-u^{\prime}_{2^{E+p-1}-(i-1)}\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

(116)

and

\displaystyle u^{\prime}_{i}=2^{\left[\left\lfloor(i-1)2^{-(p-1)}\right\rfloor-\left(2^{E-1}-1\right)\right]}\left(1+\left[(i-1)2^{-(p-1)}\bmod 1\right]\right).

(117)

Proof:

We will explicitly construct the ordered sequence of representable values
\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{K}\}
from the structure of the floating-point format given in Eq. (
110
), then apply Theorem
4
is. There are
2^{p-1}
distinct mantissa values for each exponent and
2^{E}
exponent values, giving
2^{E+p-1}
positive floating-point values in total. Including the negative values by symmetry, there are
K=2^{E+p}
representable values altogether.
The smallest value of the exponent
e_{\min}=-(2^{E-1}-1)
while the largest value
e_{\max}=2^{E-1}
.

We first enumerate the
2^{E+p-1}
positive floating-point values
u^{\prime}_{i}
in increasing order, indexed by
i=1,2,\ldots,2^{E+p-1}
. For each fixed exponent value, we exhaust all
2^{p-1}
mantissa values before incrementing the exponent.
Concretely, for index
i
, the exponent index is
\lfloor(i-1)/2^{p-1}\rfloor
, which starts at zero steps up by one exactly every
2^{p-1}
values of
i
. The mantissa index within that exponent block is
(i-1)\bmod 2^{p-1}
, which cycles through
0,1,\ldots,2^{p-1}-1
repeatedly. Translating the exponent index into the true exponent by subtracting
e_{\min}
, and the mantissa index into its fractional value by multiplying by
2^{-(p-1)}
, gives

\displaystyle u^{\prime}_{i}=2^{\left\lfloor(i-1)2^{-(p-1)}\right\rfloor-(2^{E-1}-1)}\Bigl(1+\left[(i-1)2^{-(p-1)}\bmod 1\right]\Bigr).

(118)

The full sequence
u_{1}<u_{2}<\cdots<u_{K}
must enumerate the negative and positive floating-point values in increasing order. Since the negative floating-point values are the mirror image of the positive ones, the most negative value corresponds to
-u^{\prime}_{2^{E+p-1}}
and the least negative to
-u^{\prime}_{1}
. Therefore, for
i\leq 2^{E+p-1}
, we set

\displaystyle u_{i}=-u^{\prime}_{2^{E+p-1}-(i-1)},

(119)

which enumerates the negative values in increasing order. For
i>2^{E+p-1}
, we set

\displaystyle u_{i}=u^{\prime}_{i-2^{E+p-1}},

(120)

which enumerates the positive values in increasing order. With these
u_{i}
and
K=2^{E+p}
, Theorem
4
can be used to compute
H(X_{fp})
, completing the proof.
