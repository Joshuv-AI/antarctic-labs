Characterizing entanglement dynamics in QED scattering processes
M. Blasone, S. De Siena, G. Lambiase, B. Micciola, K. Simonov

Abstract
We study entanglement dynamics among helicity degrees of freedom in quantum electrodynamics (QED) scattering processes. For generic initial states, we consider scattering at fixed momentum, corresponding to a generalized measurement described by a positive operator-valued measure , resulting in a post-measurement state. Such processes are modeled in terms of quantum maps, whose spectral structure fully determines the associated entanglement dynamics. For scattering involving only fermions, maximal entanglement present in the initial state is always preserved. Moreover, iterating the corresponding quantum maps on arbitrary initial states, we obtain the fixed points of the maps, which, in the largest number of cases, are asymptotic (pure) maximally entangled states. The structure of the maps also accounts for the entanglement dynamics in processes involving both fermions and photons. The defining properties of these maps originate from discrete symmetries of the QED interaction.

. We briefly summarize the formalism and assumptions adopted in this work, following Refs.

We assume sharp momentum filtering of the outgoing particles, without resolving their helicity degrees of freedom, corresponding to right- (
R
) and left-handed (
L
) states. This corresponds to performing a generalized measurement described by a POVM on the state
\rho^{f}
resulting from the unitary evolution and provides the post-measurement helicity state

where

denotes a fixed outgoing momentum and

The matrix elements

are expressed in terms of the

S^{(qp)}_{ij;kl}=\bra{q,ij}S\ket{p,kl}

\mathcal{M}_{ij;kl}

S^{(qp)}_{ij;kl}=i(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(p-q)\mathcal{M}_{ij;kl}.

(2)

The scattering amplitudes

For a given process, nonlocal helicity correlations can then be analyzed by computing the scattering amplitudes in the COM frame at a given perturbative order. These amplitudes depend on the scattering angle

\mu=|\vec{p}|/m

Scattering quantum maps
.
We introduce real matrices
\mathbf{M}
, defined in terms of the scattering amplitudes as

\hskip-14.22636pt\textbf{M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathcal{M}_{RR;RR}&\mathcal{M}_{RL;RR}&\mathcal{M}_{LR;RR}&\mathcal{M}_{LL;RR}\\
\mathcal{M}_{RR;RL}&\mathcal{M}_{RL;RL}&\mathcal{M}_{LR;RL}&\mathcal{M}_{LL;RL}\\
\mathcal{M}_{RR;LR}&\mathcal{M}_{RL;LR}&\mathcal{M}_{LR;LR}&\mathcal{M}_{LL;LR}\\
\mathcal{M}_{RR;LL}&\mathcal{M}_{RL;LL}&\mathcal{M}_{LR;LL}&\mathcal{M}_{LL;LL}\end{array}\right),

(3)

from which the quantum maps describing QED scattering processes can be constructed. The initial state is written as

\displaystyle\rho_{-\infty}

\displaystyle=\rho^{\mathrm{in}}=\sum_{i,j,k,l}\,\rho^{\mathrm{in}}_{ij;kl}\,\ket{p;ij}\bra{p;kl},

(4)

where
\rho^{\mathrm{in}}_{ij;kl}=\bra{p;kl}\rho^{\mathrm{in}}\ket{p;ij}
.

Momentum filtering on the final state
\rho^{f}
is implemented by the projector
\Pi_{q}=\sum_{r,s\in\{R,L\}}|q;rs\rangle\langle q;rs|
, yielding the post-selected state

\tilde{\rho}^{f}=\Pi_{q}\rho^{f}\Pi_{q}=\sum_{r,s,r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}\tilde{\rho}^{f}_{rs;r^{\prime}s^{\prime}}\ket{q;rs}\bra{q;r^{\prime}s^{\prime}},

(5)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}^{f}_{rs;r^{\prime}s^{\prime}}=\mathcal{N}^{-1}\!\!\sum_{i,j,k,l}\rho^{\mathrm{in}}_{ij;kl}\bra{q;rs}S\ket{p;ij}\bra{p;kl}S^{\dagger}\ket{q;r^{\prime}s^{\prime}},

(6)

and
\mathcal{N}=\operatorname{Tr}[\sum_{i,j,k,l}\,\rho^{\mathrm{in}}_{ij;kl}\,S\ket{p;ij}\bra{p;kl}S^{\dagger}]
. This procedure corresponds to a projective measurement onto a fixed outgoing momentum. Upon restricting to the helicity degrees of freedom, assuming an initial state with definite momentum, it induces a generalized measurement described by a POVM. The measurement outcome is associated with a positive operator
F
, which determines the corresponding probability via the Born rule,
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho^{\mathrm{in}}F)
.

The corresponding state transformation is yielded by the completely positive
trace-nonincreasing map associated with this outcome, i.e., a single element of the underlying quantum instrument
4
4

4

A quantum channel is a completely positive, trace-preserving (CPTP) map describing the most general physical evolution of a quantum system. A quantum operation is a completely positive, trace-nonincreasing map associated with a given measurement outcome. A quantum instrument is a collection of such operations, indexed by the possible outcomes, whose sum yields a CPTP map. These maps admit a unitary representation in an extended Hilbert space, where the system is coupled to an auxiliary degree of freedom (in our case, the momentum), and one either traces over it, obtaining a quantum channel, or performs a selective measurement (filtering) on it, obtaining a quantum operation as part of a quantum instrument
51
;
60
.
. To make the structure of the resulting map explicit, we express Eq. (
6
) in terms of the scattering amplitudes, obtaining

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}^{f}_{rs;r^{\prime}s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{-1}\!\sum_{i,j,k,l}\mathcal{M}_{ij;rs}\rho^{\mathrm{in}}_{ij;kl}\mathcal{M}_{r^{\prime}s^{\prime};kl}.

(7)

This can be written in compact form as

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}^{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\textbf{M}\rho^{\rm in}\textbf{M}^{T}}{\operatorname{Tr}[\textbf{M}\rho^{\rm in}\textbf{M}^{T}]},

(8)

thereby defining a (nonlinear) map, where
\mathbf{M}
plays the role of the corresponding single Kraus operator of the quantum operation
\textbf{M}\rho^{\rm in}\textbf{M}^{T}
. In this representation, the associated POVM element is given by
F=\mathbf{M}^{T}\mathbf{M}
.

The
n
-th iteration of the transformation (

\tilde{\rho}_{n}=\mathcal{N}_{n}^{-1}\textbf{M}^{n}\rho^{\mathrm{in}}(\textbf{M}^{T})^{n},

(9)

with
\mathcal{N}_{n}
denoting the normalization factor at step
n

If the initial state
|i\rangle
is pure, the post-measurement state remains pure and is given by

\ket{f}=\frac{\textbf{M}\ket{i}}{\sqrt{\bra{i}\textbf{M}^{T}\textbf{M}\ket{i}}},

(10)

while the
n
-th iteration reads

\ket{f_{n}}=\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{n}\textbf{M}^{n}\ket{i}.

(11)

15

Conclusions
. We have investigated entanglement dynamics in the helicity degrees of freedom in tree-level QED scattering processes at fixed momenta. We introduced an effective framework for characterizing the dynamics of quantum correlations by modeling scattering processes as quantum maps. Within this approach, the spectral properties of the matrices implementing the maps from the initial to the post-measurement state completely characterize the entanglement dynamics. In particular, these properties guarantee conservation of maximal entanglement in fermion-fermion scattering processes.

Iterating the maps on arbitrary initial states leads to asymptotic fixed points, corresponding to states invariant under further scatterings. For scattering processes involving fermions only, the convergence to maximally entangled states is achieved for almost all values of the kinematical parameters
(\theta,\mu)
, and guaranteed in the ultra-relativistic case into the relevant Hilbert subspace. When photons are involved in the scattering, the presence of particles obeying different statistics modifies the structure and spectral properties of the corresponding maps, giving rise to qualitatively different entanglement dynamics.

Our results highlight a strong connection between QED interaction and maximal entanglement. This relation can be traced back mainly to the invariance of the theory under parity (
\mathcal{P}
) transformations, which enforces specific relations among scattering amplitudes
34
and, in turn, governs the entanglement dynamics. Maximal entanglement conservation appears thus to be sensitive to the symmetry content of the interaction, and could be used in other contexts as a probe for new physics.

A natural extension of the present work is to investigate whether similar methods provide equally robust insights for other fundamental interactions. Moreover, going beyond the tree-level approximation by including one-loop contributions
36
would enable an assessment of whether higher-order corrections do affect the above results.
