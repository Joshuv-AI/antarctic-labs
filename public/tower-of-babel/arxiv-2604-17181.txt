Pairing Mechanism in Bilayer Nickelate La 3 Ni 2 O 7 Superconductors
Xianxin Wu, Tao Xiang, Jiangping Hu

Abstract
The recent discovery of superconductivity with T c ≈ 80 T_{c}\approx 80 K in bilayer nickelate La 3 Ni 2 O 7 provides a new setting in which to test the organizing principles of unconventional high-temperature superconductivity. We show that the gene principle and the collaborative Fermi-surface rule which were previously proposed to unify unconventional high temperature superconductors, extend naturally to this bilayer, multi-orbital system. We identify that there are two antiferromagnetic exchange channels that can provide the dominant pairing force: an interlayer intra-orbital nearest-neighbour exchange J ⟂ J_{\perp} between d z 2 d_{z^{2}} orbitals mediated by the inner apical oxygen, and an intralayer inter-orbital nearest-neighbour exchange J x ​ z J_{xz} between d z 2 d_{z^{2}} and d x 2 − y 2 d_{x^{2}-y^{2}} orbitals mediated by the in-plane oxygen. Owing to the bilayer bonding–antibonding splitting and the B 1 ​ g B_{1g} symmetry of the d x 2 − y 2 d_{x^{2}-y^{2}} orbital, these two channels cooperate to produce a robust s ± s^{\pm} superconducting state with an internal sign reversal between mirror-even and mirror-odd Fermi-surface pockets in momentum space. Both pairing channels maximize the superconducting gap on the β \beta pocket with a form factor ( c ​ o ​ s ​ k x − c ​ o ​ s ​ k y ) 2 (cosk_{x}-cosk_{y})^{2} in momentum space. The result places La 3 Ni 2 O 7 within a unified framework for unconventional superconductivity while revealing a distinct electronic environment for high- T c T_{c} pairing.

The second is the

These two ideas account naturally for both cuprates and iron-based superconductors. In cuprates, nearest-neighbour (NN) Cu–O–Cu superexchange

III
Two Leading AFM Exchange Interactions

We seek the leading AFM exchange interaction in the strong electron-electron correlation limit. Before the derivation, it is important to address the filling on orbitals. The orbital filling distribution is highly nonuniform. The
d_{z^{2}}
bonding state
(
\gamma
pocket) is near half-filling—the strongly correlated sector. The

d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}
orbital contributes approximately
1/4
filling across
\alpha
and
\beta
,
far from half-filling. Consequently, the NN
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}
–O–
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}
superexchange responsible
for
d
-wave pairing in cuprates is strongly suppressed in Ni327. The dominant
magnetic exchange must instead involve the near-half-filled
d_{z^{2}}
orbital, through
both the interlayer and intralayer pathways analyzed in the next section.

The dominant interlayer exchange is the
d_{z^{2}}
–
d_{z^{2}}
superexchange through the
inner apical oxygen, generated by the large interlayer hopping
t^{\perp}_{zz}
:

H_{\perp}=J_{\perp}\sum_{\langle i\rangle}\mathbf{S}_{i_{1},z}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{i_{2},z},\qquad J_{\perp}=\frac{4(t^{\perp}_{zz})^{2}}{U},

(1)

where
U
is the onsite Hubbard interaction between the same orbitals. This is an AFM exchange between
d_{z^{2}}
spins on opposite layers on the same Ni site, generated through the apical oxygens between the two NiO
2

layers.

The second exchange interaction is the intralayer inter-orbital AFM exchange, which is mediated by the inter-orbital NN hopping
t_{xz}
. In strong correlation limit, at the second order in perturbation theory, an intralayer inter-orbital AFM
exchange:

H_{J}=J_{xz}\sum_{\langle ij,\alpha\rangle}\mathbf{S}_{i_{\alpha},z}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{j_{\alpha},x},\qquad J_{xz}=\frac{2t_{xz}^{2}}{U+\Delta_{xz}}+\frac{2t_{xz}^{2}}{U-\Delta_{xz}},

(2)

where
\Delta_{xz}=\varepsilon_{x}-\varepsilon_{z}>0
is the crystal field splitting.
This is an AFM exchange between the localized
d_{z^{2}}
spin on
site
i
and the
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}
spin on NN site
j
, mediated by the in-plane oxygen.
The two exchanges
J_{\perp}
and
J_{xz}
are generated by fundamentally different
anion-mediated pathways.
J_{\perp}
is on vertical c-axis bond between same orbitals (
d_{z^{2}}
–
d_{z^{2}}

orbital of the apical O.

is on horizontal ab-plane NN bond between different orbitals (
d_{z^{2}}

d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}

p_{x,y}
orbital of the in-plane O.

Pairing Symmetry: The
s^{\pm}
State

The interlayer exchange
J_{\perp}
acts between two
d_{z^{2}}
orbitals on opposite
layers. Decoupling
J_{\perp}
in the particle-particle channel accordingly generates only
an
s-wave
interlayer singlet pairing, namely,
\langle d_{z^{2}\uparrow,1}(\bm{k})d_{z^{2}\downarrow,2}(-\bm{k})-d_{z^{2}\downarrow,1}(\bm{k})d_{z^{2}\uparrow,2}(-\bm{k})\rangle=\Delta_{\perp}
. The key consequence of this s-wave interlayer pairing is a sign change between
Fermi-surface pockets of even and odd mirror symmetry under
M_{z}
. Since
J_{\perp}

couples the two layers antiferromagnetically, the resulting interlayer Cooper pairs
have even (bonding) and odd (antibonding) combinations across the bilayer. In the
band basis, the mirror-even pockets
\alpha
and
\gamma
(L
+
, eigenvalue
+1
)
acquire gap
+\Delta_{\perp}
, while the mirror-odd pocket
\beta
(L
-
, eigenvalue
-1
)
acquires gap
-\Delta_{\perp}
. This sign change is the direct bilayer analogue of the

s^{\pm}
sign reversal between
\Gamma
and
M
pockets in iron pnictides, here
encoded in the mirror quantum number of the bilayer rather than in the in-plane
Fermi-surface topology. It is important to notice that this interlayer exchange would not contribute to pairing in a
d
-wave state. It is straightforward to show that it provides
the superconducting orders in each pocket as

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\perp}^{\alpha}\,(\cos k_{x}-\cos k_{y})^{2},

(3)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\beta}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{\perp}^{\beta}\,(\cos k_{x}-\cos k_{y})^{2},

(5)

The intralayer inter-orbital exchange

\langle d_{z^{2}\uparrow}(\bm{k})d_{x^{2}-y^{2}\downarrow}(-\bm{k})-d_{z^{2}\downarrow}(\bm{k})d_{x^{2}-y^{2}\uparrow}(-\bm{k})\rangle=\Delta_{s}f(\bm{k})

However, there is an important difference. In the intermediate-coupling FRG approach,

We can argue that the on-site same-orbital pairing appearing in the weak-coupling

has

\mathbf{k}=0

weak-coupling approach and the inter-orbital pairing in the strong-coupling approach
are two complementary descriptions of the same physical process, linked by the
orbital hybridization that connects
d_{z^{2}}
and
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}
through
t_{xz}

\gamma

\gamma
