Three loop QCD corrections to electroweak radiative parameters
Tanmoy Pati, Narayan Rana, Alessandro Vicini

Abstract
We reevaluate the vacuum polarization functions for electroweak gauge bosons at three loops in QCD, employing state-of-the-art perturbative techniques. We apply these results to determine the 𝒪 ⁡ ( α ​ α s 2 ) {\mathcal{O}}(\alpha\alpha_{s}^{2}) corrections to the electroweak radiative parameters Δ ​ ρ \Delta\rho , Δ ​ r \Delta r and Δ ​ κ \Delta\kappa . We improve the accuracy of the calculation at this perturbative order, compared to the existing literature, and present some phenomenological implications of these results. We find a shift in the prediction of the W W boson mass, significant in view of the FCC precision targets. We improve the prediction of the MS ¯ {\overline{\mathrm{MS}}} electric charge at q 2 = m Z 2 q^{2}=m_{Z}^{2} with the inclusion of these 𝒪 ⁡ ( α ​ α s 2 ) {\mathcal{O}}(\alpha\alpha_{s}^{2}) corrections.

Introduction

The discovery of the Higgs boson at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) marked the completion of the Standard Model (SM) particle spectrum. However, the subsequent absence of direct signals for New Physics has pivoted the frontier of particle physics towards precision phenomenology.
As experimental uncertainties continue to shrink, driven by the High-Luminosity LHC
[

[

[

A precision test program requires high-precision theoretical predictions for the cross sections of key scattering processes, where any discrepancy with the data may serve as a signature of physics beyond the SM. At both the LHC and future lepton colliders, this necessitates the simultaneous inclusion of higher-order Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) and electroweak (EW) corrections,
accounting for their non-trivial interplay.
The Drell-Yan process plays a central role in this program. It has been calculated to next-to-next-to-next-to-leading order (N

2
Theoretical Framework

2.1
The
{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}
-renormalized electric charge

The electric charge
e
is defined via Thomson scattering in the limit of zero momentum transfer. Its on-shell (OS) renormalization is achieved by imposing that the counterterm
\delta e=e_{0}-e
, with
e_{0}
the bare charge, preserves to all orders the value of the renormalized charge, given by the fine structure constant
\alpha(0)=e^{2}/(4\pi)=1/137.035999084(21)

[
66
]
.
The electric charge renormalization at two-loop EW level has been presented in ref.
[
33
]
. The proof of the all-order structure of the counterterms has been discussed in ref.
[
36
]
.
Due to the validity of the QED Ward identities, the OS electric charge counterterm depends only on the photon vacuum polarization,
making manifest the universality of the electric charge.
Within the SM, the validity of QED-like Ward identities can be maintained to all orders by adopting a gauge fixing chosen according to the Background Field Method
[
34
,
33
,
36
]
.
This approach allows the renormalization counterterm to be defined through the photon self-energy only.

The transverse part of the photon vacuum polarization can be written as

\Pi^{T}_{\gamma\gamma}(s)=e_{0}^{2}\,s\,\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}(s)
, and the function
\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}(s)

can be decomposed into several distinct contributions according to the particles coupling to the external photons: leptons (
\ell
), bosons (
b
), light quarks (
5
), and perturbative contributions from the top quark (
p
).
To address the non-perturbative nature of the light-quark contributions at
s=0
,

\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(5)}(0)
is replaced by an experimental quantity,

\Delta\alpha_{had}^{(5)}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})
,
linked to the photon vacuum polarization and its perturbative remainder via a dispersion relation.

\displaystyle\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}(0)

\displaystyle=\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(\ell)}(0)+\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(b)}(0)+\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(p)}(0)+\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(5)}(0)

\displaystyle=\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(\ell)}(0)+\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(b)}(0)+\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(p)}(0)+\mathrm{Re}\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(5)}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})

\displaystyle~~~~+\mathrm{Re}\left[\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(5)}(0)-\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(5)}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})\right],

(1)

where
\Delta\alpha_{had}^{(5)}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})=4\pi\alpha\,\mathrm{Re}[\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(5)}(0)-\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(5)}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})]

includes all the non-perturbative corrections due to the strong interaction at low momenta.
The term
\mathrm{Re}\,\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(5)}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})
can be safely evaluated with a perturbative approach.

The electromagnetic coupling can be defined within the
{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}
renormalization scheme, as

\alpha_{{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})=\frac{\alpha(0)}{1-\Delta\alpha_{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})}\,.

(2)

\Delta\alpha_{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})
is given by the finite part of the counterterm, subtracted of its ultraviolet (UV) pole according to the
{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}
prescription and is evaluated with the renormalization scale
\mu_{R}=m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}
.

\displaystyle\Delta\alpha_{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})

\displaystyle=-4\pi\alpha\left[\hat{\Pi}_{\gamma\gamma}^{(\ell)}(0)+\hat{\Pi}_{\gamma\gamma}^{(b)}(0)+\hat{\Pi}_{\gamma\gamma}^{(p)}(0)\right]

\displaystyle~~~-4\pi\alpha\,\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(5)}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})-\Delta\alpha_{had}^{(5)}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})\,.

(3)

The symbols
\hat{\Pi}_{\gamma\gamma}(s)
represent the finite part of the photon vacuum polarization, after removing the UV poles according to the
{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}
prescription.
In this paper we compute the
{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha\alpha_{s}^{2})
contribution
to
\Delta\alpha_{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})
.
Only
\hat{\Pi}_{\gamma\gamma}^{(p)}(0)
and
\Pi_{\gamma\gamma}^{(5)}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})
contribute at
{\cal O}(\alpha\alpha_{s})
and
{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha\alpha_{s}^{2})
.
We obtain an accurate prediction of
\alpha_{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})
, which can be used in turn as boundary condition for the evaluation of the coupling at arbitrary scales
\mu
, solving the Renormalization Group Equation.

2.2
The electroweak parameters
\Delta\rho,\Delta r,\Delta\kappa

The strength of the weak charged-current interaction is parameterized by the Fermi constant,

G_{\mu}
, which is determined with high precision from measurements of the muon lifetime within the Fermi theory. The current experimental value is

G_{\mu}=1.11663788(6)\times 10^{-5}
GeV
-2

[
66
]
.
By equating the muon-decay amplitudes at zero momentum transfer in both the Fermi theory and the SM,
one establishes a fundamental relation between
G_{\mu}
,
\alpha(0)
,
m_{\scriptscriptstyle W}
and
m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}
.

G_{\mu}=\frac{\pi\,\alpha(0)}{\sqrt{2}(1-m_{\scriptscriptstyle W}^{2}/m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})~m_{\scriptscriptstyle W}^{2}}(1+\Delta r)\,.

(4)

The parameter
\Delta r

[
75
]
is a finite physical quantity accounting for radiative corrections to the muon-decay amplitude,
after subtracting the QED components that are identical in both the Fermi theory and the SM.
This relation defines a renormalized coupling within the SM that characterizes the strength of the weak charged-current interaction.
Solving Eq. (
4
) for
m_{\scriptscriptstyle W}
provides a theoretical prediction for
the
W
boson mass. The impact of higher-order radiative corrections on
\Delta r
and
the resulting
m_{\scriptscriptstyle W}
prediction have been discussed extensively
in refs.
[
48
,
6
,
30
,
50
]
.

At
{\cal O}(\alpha)
the radiative corrections to the muon-decay amplitude include
self-energy contributions (
\Pi_{WW}
) to the
W
propagator,
vertex (
V_{W}
) and box (
B_{W}
) corrections and the renormalization of
the tree-level amplitude parameters.
Working within the OS scheme, we define the gauge-boson mass counterterms from the transverse part of the corresponding self-energies:
\delta m_{V}^{2}=\mathrm{Re}\left(\Pi^{T}_{VV}(m_{V}^{2})\right)
.
In this framework,
\Delta r
can be explicitly expressed as:

\displaystyle\Delta r

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{W}^{2}}\left[\Pi^{T}_{WW}(0)-\text{Re}(\Pi^{T}_{WW}(m_{W}^{2}))\right]+V_{W}+B_{W}

\displaystyle+2\frac{\delta e}{e}+\frac{c_{w}^{2}}{s_{w}^{2}}\text{Re}\left(\frac{\Pi^{T}_{WW}(m_{W}^{2})}{m_{\scriptscriptstyle W}^{2}}-\frac{\Pi^{T}_{ZZ}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})}{m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2}}\right)\,,

(5)

where
s_{w}^{2}=1-m_{\scriptscriptstyle W}^{2}/m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2}
is the squared sine of the weak mixing angle and
c_{w}^{2}=1-s_{w}^{2}
.
Higher-order QCD corrections, e.g. at
{\cal O}(\alpha\alpha_{s})
,  and
{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha\alpha_{s}^{2})
, enter
\Delta r
exclusively through
the quark loop contributions to the gauge-boson self-energies.
The vertex (
V_{W}
) and box (
B_{W}
) terms do not receive corrections at these perturbative orders.

The
\rho
parameter quantifies the relative strength of the neutral-current weak coupling
compared to the charged-current coupling. At the tree level, this quantity is exactly one, because of the accidental global custodial symmetry of the SM. The radiative corrections break the symmetry, via the mass splitting in the fermion doublets and via hypercharge corrections, yielding
\Delta\rho=\rho-1\neq 0
. The largest effects stem from the third family, because of the large difference between the top and bottom quark masses.
The universal, i.e. process independent, contributions to
\Delta\rho
can be identified in the comparison of neutral- and charged-current elastic lepton-neutrino scattering
[
62
]
:

\Delta\rho(0)=\frac{\Pi_{ZZ}^{T}(0)}{m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2}}-\frac{\Pi_{WW}^{T}(0)}{m_{\scriptscriptstyle W}^{2}}\,.

(6)

As a remnant of the underlying custodial symmetry,
\Delta\rho(0)
is UV-finite at the one-loop level.
It does not require independent renormalization at its calculation order, as it only depends on the renormalization of lower-order amplitudes. This makes
\Delta\rho(0)
a highly sensitive and robust prediction for testing the SM at the precision frontier.
The QCD corrections to
\Delta\rho(0)
are relevant only for the top-bottom doublet case and have been computed up to the four-loop level

[
38
,
53
,
55
,
54
,
37
,
5
,
24
,
25
,
78
,
46
,
40
,
73
,
23
,
17
]
.
If we assume that the light quarks of the first two families are all massless, then their contribution to
\Delta\rho(0)
vanishes.
It is possible to recast the formula for
\Delta r
as:

\Delta r=\Delta\alpha(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})-\frac{c_{w}^{2}}{s_{w}^{2}}\Delta\rho(0)+\Delta r_{rem}.

(7)

The first term is responsible for the large (
\sim
7%) running of the electric charge.

\Delta\rho(0)
contains the large heavy fermion effects (
\sim
-3.5%, due to the enhancement by the factor
c_{w}^{2}/s_{w}^{2}\simeq 3.5
)
of the
\rho
parameter, while the last term is a finite contribution specific of the muon-decay process.

A third set of EW radiative corrections is responsible for the redefinition of the weak mixing angle at the quantum level. Such effects can be studied from the analysis of the form factor describing the coupling of a
Z
boson to a fermion
f
.
The strength of the V–A component of the neutral-current interaction, expressed in units of
G_{\mu}
,
is parameterized by a factor
\rho^{f}(q^{2})

[
32
]
.
This factor includes fermion-specific and
q^{2}
-dependent corrections beyond those present in
\Delta\rho(0)
.
Any residual deviation between this coupling and the electromagnetic component of the
Z
current
is parameterized by the factor
\kappa(q^{2})\equiv 1+\Delta\kappa(q^{2})
.
Under this construction, the
Zf{\bar{f}}
vertex function reads

\displaystyle\Gamma_{Zf\bar{f}}^{\mu}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=i\bar{z}_{f}\gamma^{\mu}\bigg(T_{3}^{f}\frac{1-\gamma_{5}}{2}-\kappa(q^{2})\sin^{2}\theta_{W}\,Q_{f}\bigg),

(8)

where
\bar{z}_{f}=2m_{Z}(\sqrt{2}G_{\mu})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\rho^{f}(q^{2})
.
In Eq. (
8
),
the product
\kappa(q^{2})\sin^{2}\theta_{W}
can be interpreted as the square of the sine of a renormalized mixing angle that absorbs the radiative corrections
\Delta\kappa
.
At the
Z
resonance, this parameterization is particularly relevant, because the form factor is gauge invariant. This allows for the definition of an effective, fermion species dependent,
weak mixing angle
\sin^{2}\theta_{eff}^{f}\,
, which reabsorbs the effect of all those radiative corrections
that differently affect the left- and the right-handed components of the fermionic current.
The effective weak mixing angle can be determined experimentally
(cfr. the extraction in terms of pseudo-observables
[
72
]
or with
a direct approach relevant at hadron colliders
[
27
]
) and it can be compared with its theoretical prediction in the SM
[
32
,
31
]
,
which can be computed starting either from an OS or from an
{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}
-renormalized weak mixing angle,
and evaluating the respective contributions to the
Z
form factor.

\sin^{2}\theta_{eff}^{f}=\kappa^{f}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})\sin^{2}\theta_{OS}=\hat{\kappa}^{f}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})\sin^{2}\theta_{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}\,.

(9)

The corrections to
\Delta\kappa
stem from the
\gamma-Z
self-energy contributions, the renormalization of the tree-level mixing angle present in the vector coupling of the
Zf\bar{f}
vertex function, and the vertex corrections.
The oblique corrections to
\Delta\kappa
at
{\cal O}(\alpha\alpha_{s})
and
{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha\alpha_{s}^{2})
enter via the quark self-energies as follows:

\displaystyle\Delta\kappa^{oblique}_{QCD}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{w}^{2}}{s_{w}^{2}}\text{Re}\bigg(\frac{\Pi_{ZZ}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})}{m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2}}-\frac{\Pi_{WW}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle W}^{2})}{m_{\scriptscriptstyle W}^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{w}}{s_{w}}\frac{\Pi_{\gamma Z}(m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2})}{m_{\scriptscriptstyle Z}^{2}}\,.

(10)

2.3
The finite renormalization combinations
\delta c_{V}

The one-loop EW corrections to the production of vector bosons at hadron colliders necessitate the renormalization of tree-level parameters and the wave-functions (WFs) of external OS particles.
A significant simplification occurs within the framework of the BFG, because the gauge fixing of the background fields is chosen in a way to preserve QED-like Ward Identities (WIs). In the case of
W
and
Z
bosons, other WIs relate the WF renormalization constants to the charge counterterms.
The QCD corrections to first and second order in
\alpha_{s}
, on top of an
{\cal O}(\alpha)
kernel, preserve these relations
[
14
,
15
,
69
]
, while the same statement does not hold for purely EW corrections, starting from
{\cal O}(\alpha^{2})
.
We thus find it convenient to identify specific combinations of charge and WF counterterms which turn out to be UV finite and are building blocks which systematically appear in any higher-order calculation.

The definition of
\delta c_{V}
(for
V=A,Z,W
) depends on the input scheme.
As such, with
(\alpha(0),m_{W},m_{Z})
in input,
they are defined as:

\displaystyle\delta c_{A}

\displaystyle=\delta\,Z_{AZ}-\frac{2c_{w}s_{w}}{s_{w}^{2}}\frac{\delta c_{w}^{2}}{c_{w}^{2}}=\frac{2s_{w}}{c_{w}}\Delta\kappa_{QCD}^{oblique}\,.

\displaystyle\delta c_{Z}

\displaystyle=\delta\,Z_{ZZ}+2\,\frac{\delta e}{e}+\frac{c_{w}^{2}-s_{w}^{2}}{s_{w}^{2}}\frac{\delta c_{w}^{2}}{c_{w}^{2}}\,.

\displaystyle\delta c_{W}

\displaystyle=\delta\,Z_{WW}+2\,\frac{\delta e}{e}+\frac{c_{w}^{2}}{s_{w}^{2}}\frac{\delta c_{w}^{2}}{c_{w}^{2}}\,.

(11)

Here
Z_{VV}=1+\delta\,Z_{VV}
is the WF renormalization constants of the OS vector boson
V
.

3
Computational procedure

We follow an automated computational workflow to evaluate the vacuum polarization
functions for both charged and neutral gauge bosons.
Corresponding Feynman diagrams were generated with
QGRAF

[
67
]
.
For the third generation of quarks (
t,b
), we identify 70 diagrams contributing to
\Pi_{ZZ}
and 31 diagrams for
\Pi_{WW}
.
The raw output from
QGRAF
was subsequently processed by in-house
FORM

[
76
]
routines,
which translates the diagrams into Feynman amplitudes and performs the Dirac, Lorentz, and color algebra.
The resulting expressions consist of
\sim{\mathcal{O}(10^{4})}
scalar Feynman integrals.
We employ the standard method of Integration-By-Parts (IBP) reduction
[
77
,
26
,
57
]

to reduce the scalar integrals to 94 Master Integrals (MIs).
We have utilized the public codes
Kira

[
61
,
52
,
56
]

and
LiteRed

[
58
,
59
]
to perform the IBP reduction.

The primary task lies in the evaluation of the three-loop two-point MIs at the
kinematic scales
q^{2}=0
and
q^{2}=m_{V}^{2}
(where
V\in\{Z,W\}
).
From two-loop onward, the analytic structure of the MIs becomes complex,
frequently involving elliptic multiple polylogarithms.
Hence, we solve the MIs using a system of differential equations evaluated via a series expansion.
Given that the ratio
z_{t}\equiv m_{Z}^{2}/m_{t}^{2}\simeq 0.28
resides well within the radius of convergence
for a series centered at zero, the expansion provides sufficient accuracy,
eliminating the need for full closed-form analytic solutions at
q^{2}=m_{V}^{2}
.
We determine these series solutions using the generalized Frobenius method, implemented through an in-house
Mathematica
routine.
The boundary conditions at
q^{2}\to 0
are determined through a combination of different techniques: the
OS-reduction method, the request of regularity of the solution at the pseudo-thresholds, and, for a few cases,
high-precision PSLQ fits
[
47
]
via
PolyLogTools

[
43
]
based on numerical evaluations using
AMFlow

[
60
]
.
In addition to multiple zeta values (MZVs),
\ln(2)
and
\ln(3)
,
our results involve the following set of transcendental constants
[
19
,
51
,
64
,
74
]

and their combinations

\left\{\begin{gathered}\sqrt{3}\pi\zeta(2),\sqrt{3}\pi\zeta(2)\ln(3),\sqrt{3}\pi\zeta(3),\sqrt{3}\,\text{Cl}_{2}(\psi),\\
\sqrt{3}\,\text{Cl}_{2}(\psi)\ln(3),\sqrt{3}\,\text{Cl}_{2}(\psi)\ln^{2}(3),\sqrt{3}\,\text{Cl}_{2}(\psi)\zeta_{2},\\
\text{Cl}^{2}_{2}(\psi),\text{Li}_{4}(1/2),\sqrt{3}\,\text{Ls}_{3}(2\psi),\\
\sqrt{3}\,\text{Ls}_{3}(2\psi)\ln(3),\sqrt{3}\,\text{Ls}_{4}(\psi),\sqrt{3}\,\text{Ls}_{4}(2\psi)\end{gathered}\right\}

where
\psi=\pi/3
.
Clausen functions and log-sine integrals are defined as

\displaystyle\text{Cl}_{2}\left(\phi\right)=\text{Im}\left[\text{Li}_{2}\left(e^{i\phi}\right)\right]\,.

(12)

\displaystyle\text{Ls}_{n}\left(\phi\right)=\int_{0}^{\phi}\ln^{n-1}\left|2\sin\frac{x}{2}\right|dx\,.

(13)

While all these constant appear in the intermediate stages, they cancel out in the final expressions, except for MZVs and
\text{Cl}_{2}(\psi)
.

We have performed the UV renormalization using a mixed scheme: the heavy quark mass is
renormalized within the OS framework, while
\alpha_{s}
is treated in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme.
The required mass-renormalization constants are adopted from refs.
[
18
,
65
,
63
]
.

4
Results

In this section, we present the
{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha\alpha_{s}^{2})
corrections to
the electric charge renormalization,

\Delta\rho(0)
,
\Delta r
and
\Delta\kappa
.
We also discuss
{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha\alpha_{s}^{2})
contributions to the particular
UV finite renormalization combinations
\delta c_{V}
.
Results for all these parameters discussed in this work are provided in an ancillary file
attached to the
arXiv
submission.
At
{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha\alpha_{s}^{2})
we provide a fully independent re-evaluation and validation of the existing three-loop results for
\Delta\rho(0)
,
\Delta r
and
\Delta\kappa

[
25
]
. We further improve
\Delta r
and
\Delta\kappa
by adding the three-loop light-quark contributions. In the evaluation of the self-energy functions by series expansion, we include a larger number of terms compared to those considered in the seminal work
[
25
]
, and check the corresponding numerical impact.
We observe that this improved theoretical accuracy has an impact on the predictions, not negligible in view of the precision target at the FCC.

5
Conclusion

Precision physics stands at the forefront of the modern particle physics program.
Precision measurements of EW radiative parameters are essential for probing the limits of the SM. To meet the requirements of future electron-positron colliders, the theoretical accuracy must reach a comparable level.

In this work, we utilized advanced perturbative techniques to reevaluate the three-loop QCD vacuum polarization functions for EW gauge bosons.
We have reported, for the first time, the
{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha\alpha_{s}^{2})
contributions to the running of the

{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}
-renormalized electric charge.
We further applied the results of the vacuum polarization functions to determine
the corresponding corrections to
\Delta\rho
,
\Delta r
and
\Delta\kappa
.
We have provided a compact analytic expression for
\Delta\rho^{(2)}
and found full agreement with existing literature.
We have presented
\Delta r^{(2)}
and
\Delta\kappa^{(2)}
, as a Taylor series in
z_{t}
up to
{\mathcal{O}}(z_{t}^{15})
, and we have included novel
{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha\alpha_{s}^{2})
contributions from massless quarks.
We show that these contributions are numerically significant and indispensable for the high-precision determination of
m_{\scriptscriptstyle W}
and
\sin^{2}\theta_{eff}^{f}\,
at the future lepton colliders.
Furthermore, we have presented new results for the UV-finite renormalization combinations

\delta c_{Z}^{(2)}
and
\delta c_{W}^{(2)}
.

To facilitate the use of these results in future phenomenological studies,
we have provided the expressions in an accompanying ancillary file.
These results represent a necessary step toward the sub-permille theoretical
accuracy required for the next generation of precision experiments.
