Étale cohomology of Stein algebras
Olivier Benoist

Abstract
. We prove that the singular cohomology with finite coefficients of a finite-dimensional Stein space S S is isomorphic to the étale cohomology of the Stein algebra 𝒪 ⁡ ( S ) \mathcal{O}(S) . We deduce that any class in H k ​ ( S , ℤ ) H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}) comes from an algebraic variety by pullback by a holomorphic map (if k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 ), and vanishes on the complement of a nowhere dense closed analytic subset of S S (if k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 ).



1.
Introduction

1.1.
A comparison theorem in Stein geometry

Let
V
be a complex algebraic variety and let
V^{\mathrm{an}}=V(\mathbb{C})
be its analytification. Artin’s comparison theorem (see
[
SGA

4
III

, XVI, Théorème 4.1]
) states that the natural morphism

(1.1)

H^{k}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(V,\mathbb{L})\to H^{k}(V^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}})

from the étale cohomology of
V
to the singular cohomology of
V^{\mathrm{an}}
is an isomorphism for all
k\geq 0
and all constructible étale sheaves
\mathbb{L}
on
V
(e.g. when
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Z}/m
for some
m\geq 1
). By providing a purely algebraic description of singular cohomology groups (with finite coefficients) of complex algebraic varieties, Artin’s theorem offers insights into both their algebraic and topological properties.

The main goal of this article is to prove an analogue of Artin’s theorem in (complex-analytic) Stein geometry. Recall that a complex space
S
is
Stein
if the restriction map
\mathcal{O}(S)\to\mathcal{O}(T)
is surjective for all discrete subsets
T
of
S
. Examples include the affine spaces
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
n\geq 0
, as well as all their closed complex subspaces.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.7
)
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. Let
\mathbb{L}
be a constructible étale sheaf on a proper
\mathcal{O}(S)
-scheme of finite presentation
X
. Then

(1.2)

H^{k}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{L})\to H^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}})

is an isomorphism for all
k\geq 0
.

In Theorem
1.1
, the complex space
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is the analytification of
X
in the sense of Bingener
[
Bin76
]
, which is naturally in bijection with
X(\mathbb{C})
where
X
is viewed as a
\mathbb{C}
-scheme (see §
4.1
), and the comparison morphism (
1.2
) is defined in (
4.3
).

The hypotheses that
S
be finite-dimensional, that
X
be proper, that
X
be of finite presentation (rather than merely of finite type), and that
\mathbb{L}
be constructible are all essential for the validity of Theorem
1.1
(see Remarks
4.8
).

A dévissage based on the relative comparison theorem
[
Ben25
, Theorem 4.9]
reduces Theorem
1.1
to the following key particular case, which computes the étale cohomology of a Stein algebra (a ring of holomorphic functions on a Stein space).

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.5
)
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. Then

H^{k}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{Z}/m)\to H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)

is an isomorphism for all
k\geq 0
and
m\geq 1
.

We highlight the no less difficult particular case of Theorem
1.2
where
S=\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Corollary 1.3
.

One has
H^{k}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{n})),\mathbb{Z}/m)=0
for all
k,m\geq 1
and
{n\geq 0}
.

Since a Stein space of dimension
n
has the homotopy type of a CW complex of dimension
\leq n
(see
[
Ham86
, Korollar]
), Theorem
1.2
implies the next corollary.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
S
be a Stein space of dimension
n
. Then
{H^{k}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{Z}/m)}
vanishes for all
k>n
and
m\geq 1
.

1.2.
Applications to the singular cohomology of Stein spaces

Artin’s comparison theorem (
1.1
) can be applied in two distinct ways. By computing the étale cohomology of complex algebraic varieties in topological terms, it can be used to give topological proofs of algebraic statements. Conversely, leveraging distinctive features of étale cohomology,
it can help study the topological properties of algebraic varieties.
Comparison theorems in Stein geometry can also be exploited in both directions. Our initial motivation to study them was to deduce, from our excellent understanding of the topology of Stein spaces, algebraic results on rings of holomorphic or meromorphic functions. Applications along these lines (of other comparison theorems discussed in §
1.3
) appear in
[
Ben25
,
Ben24
]
(e.g. quantitative results on the analytic version of Hilbert’s 17th problem
[
Ben25
, Theorem 7.5]
, or a period-index theorem in Stein geometry
[
Ben24
, Theorem 8.3]
). In this article, we illustrate Theorem
1.1
by presenting two applications of the second kind.

First, we prove that integral cohomology classes of degree
\geq 2
on finite-dimensional Stein spaces are supported on nowhere dense closed analytic subsets.

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem
5.9
)
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. Fix a class
\alpha\in H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z})
for some
k\geq 2
. There exists a nowhere dense closed analytic subset
Z\subset S
such that
\alpha|_{S\setminus Z}=0
in
H^{k}(S\setminus Z,\mathbb{Z})
.

One can also require
Z
to avoid a prescribed countable subset of
S
(see Theorem
5.9
). Theorem
1.5
is easy for
k=2
(then
\alpha
is the first Chern class of a holomorphic line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
S
and one can let
Z
be the zero locus of a section of
\mathcal{L}
), but nontrivial for
k\geq 3
.
We refer the reader to §
1.5
for a sketch of its proof.

In §
5.5
, we define
unramified cohomology groups

H^{k}_{\mathrm{nr}}(S,\mathbb{Z})
in Stein geometry, by analogy with the corresponding groups studied in algebraic geometry (see e.g.
[
BO74
,
CTV12
]
). We deduce from Theorem
1.5
that the natural morphism

H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z})\to H^{k}_{\mathrm{nr}}(S,\mathbb{Z})

vanishes identically for all finite-dimensional Stein spaces and all
k\geq 2
(see Theorem
5.11
). This shows that unramified cohomology groups behave very differently in the Stein setting compared to the algebraic one.

Second, we prove that integral cohomology classes of degree
\geq 1
on finite-dimensional Stein spaces come from algebraic varieties.

Theorem 1.6

(Corollary
6.4
)
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. Fix a class
\alpha\in H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z})
for some
k\geq 1
. There exist an affine algebraic variety
V
over
\mathbb{C}
, a holomorphic map
f:S\to V^{\mathrm{an}}
, and a class
\beta\in H^{k}(V^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})
such that
\alpha=f^{*}\beta
.

The theorem we prove is more precise (see Theorem
6.3
), and has a counterpart with finite coefficients (see Theorem
6.1
). We use the latter to endow
H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
with a canonical exhaustive increasing filtration which is functorial for holomorphic maps of Stein spaces, and which we call the
Stein weight filtration
(see §
6.3
).

1.3.
Previous works

Theorem
1.2
was already known for
k\leq 1
(see
[
Ben24
, Remark 6.7 (v)]
) and for
k=2
(see
[
BH26
, Theorem 4.2]
), but is new for
k\geq 3
.

Prior to this article, two other comparison theorems in Stein geometry had appeared in
[
Ben25
,
Ben24
]
. To compare them with Theorem
1.1
, we state them in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) below. Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space, let
X
be an
\mathcal{O}(S)
-scheme of finite presentation, and let
\mathbb{L}
be a constructible étale sheaf on
X
.

Let
K\subset S
be a compact subset that is
Stein
in the sense that it admits a basis of Stein open neighborhoods in
S
(for instance,
K
could be the closed unit ball in
S=\mathbb{C}^{n}
). Then the comparison morphisms

(1.3)

\underset{U}{\mathrm{colim}}\,H^{k}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\mathcal{O}(U)},\mathbb{L})\to\underset{U}{\mathrm{colim}}\,H^{k}((X_{\mathcal{O}(U)})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}),

where
U
runs over all Stein open neighborhoods of
K
in
S
, are isomorphisms for all
k\geq 0
(see
[
Ben25
, Theorem 6.1]
).

Assume that
S
is reduced of dimension
\leq 2
. Then the comparison morphisms

(1.4)

\underset{a}{\mathrm{colim}}\,H^{k}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\mathcal{O}(S)[\frac{1}{a}]},\mathbb{L})\to\underset{a}{\mathrm{colim}}\,H^{k}((X_{\mathcal{O}(S)[\frac{1}{a}]})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}),

where
a\in\mathcal{O}(S)
runs over all nonzerodivisors, are isomorphisms for all
k\geq 0
(see
[
Ben24
, Theorem 6.6]
). It is not known if the restrictive hypothesis on the dimension of
S
can be removed.

Applied to
X=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
, the comparison isomorphisms (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) compute in topological terms the étale cohomology groups of the ring
\mathcal{O}(K)
of germs of holomorphic functions on a Stein compact set
K\subset S
(
resp.
of the ring
\mathcal{M}(S)
of meromorphic functions on
S
, assuming that
S
is reduced of dimension
\leq 2
). In contrast, applying Theorem
1.1
with
X=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
computes the étale cohomology groups of the Stein algebra
\mathcal{O}(S)
itself (see Theorem
1.2
).

A crucial difference between the isomorphisms (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) on the one hand and Theorem
1.1
on the other hand is that the former hold for all finitely presented
\mathcal{O}(S)
-schemes
X
, whereas the latter fails in general for nonproper
X
(see Remark
4.8
(ii)). As we now explain, this complicates the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The approach to the comparison theorems (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) taken in
[
Ben25
,
Ben24
]
originates from Artin’s work
[
SGA

4
III

, XI, §4]
. Its idea is to let
\varepsilon:X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\to(X^{\mathrm{an}})_{\mathrm{cl}}
be the morphism from the small étale site of
X
to the site of local isomorphisms of
X^{\mathrm{an}}
(so
\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}=\varepsilon^{*}\mathbb{L}
and the comparison morphism is given by pullback by
\varepsilon
), and to consider its Leray spectral sequence

(1.5)

E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathrm{R}^{q}\varepsilon_{*}\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}})\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}).

If
\mathbb{L}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\varepsilon_{*}\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}
and
\mathrm{R}^{q}\varepsilon_{*}\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}=0
for
q>0
, then (
1.5
) degenerates and yields isomorphisms
H^{k}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{L})\to H^{k}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}})
for
k\geq 0
. To show that (
1.3
) (
resp.
(
1.4
)) are isomorphisms, we prove these assertions in the limit where
U
gets smaller and smaller (
resp.
where
a
becomes more and more divisible). By design, if this strategy of proof applies to
X
, it also applies to all its Zariski-open subsets. Consequently, it cannot be used to prove a theorem that fails for nonproper
X
, such as Theorem
1.1
.

1.4.
Killing cohomology classes with finite coefficients on finite flat covers

We therefore had to devise a different, more global approach to Theorem
1.1
. We henceforth focus on Theorem
1.2
to which Theorem
1.1
can be reduced (as we explained in §
1.1
).
To prove it in §
4.3
, we compute both sides of

(1.6)

H^{k}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{Z}/m)\to H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)

à la Čech using finite hypercoverings (this technique is referred to as
cohomological descent
and goes back to
[
SGA

4
II

, Vbis]
). The categories of finite hypercoverings of
S
and
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
satisfying appropriate finiteness conditions are equivalent (this follows from
[
Ben24
, Proposition 2.7]
). Refining these hypercoverings ad libitum, one can deduce that (
1.6
) is an isomorphism for all
k\geq 0
from the easy case
k=0
, if one knows how to kill classes in
H^{k}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{Z}/m)
and
H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
with
k>0
on appropriate finite coverings. For
H^{k}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{Z}/m)
, this is possible by a theorem of Bhatt
[
Bha12
, Theorem 1.1]
. That one can also achieve this for
H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
results from
the following theorem, which is the heart of the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.7

(Theorem
3.1
)
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. For all
k,m\geq 1
and all
\alpha\in H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
, there exist
d\geq 1
and a holomorphic map
{p:\widetilde{S}\to S}
that is finite flat of degree
d
such that
p^{*}\alpha=0
in
H^{k}(\widetilde{S},\mathbb{Z}/m)
.

A variant of Theorem
1.7
, restricted to a Stein compact subset
K\subset S
, and only requiring that
p
be finite surjective (instead of finite flat of degree
d\geq 1
), was established in
[
Ben25
, Proposition 3.4]
as a key step towards the comparison theorem (
1.3
). However, the argument in
[
Ben25
]
does not ensure the flatness of
p
, which is essential in the application to Theorem
1.2
.
More importantly, its mechanism of proof (induction on the cohomological degree based on Mayer–Vietoris exact sequences) cannot work without a compactness hypothesis (the degree of the map
p
constructed in
[
Ben25
]
depends on the cardinality of a well-chosen open covering of
K
; this number can be chosen finite by compactness of
K
but grows with
K
).

We must therefore rely on an entirely different argument. Assume that there exists a complex manifold
\Omega
with the homotopy type of (a sufficiently big skeleton of) the Eilenberg–MacLane space
K(\mathbb{Z}/m,k)
. Then
\alpha\in H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
comes from the tautological class
\alpha_{\Omega}\in H^{k}(\Omega,\mathbb{Z}/m)
by pullback by a continuous map
{f:S\to\Omega}
. Assume moreover that
\Omega
is
Oka
(in Forstnerič’s sense
[
For09
, Definition 1.2]
recalled in §
2.1
), so any continuous map from a Stein space to
\Omega
is homotopic to a holomorphic map. This allows us to choose
f
holomorphic, which reduces the problem of killing
\alpha
on a finite flat cover to the same problem for
\alpha_{\Omega}
. Given a concrete description of
\Omega
, one can hope to solve this problem in a direct manner.

Showing the existence of Oka manifolds with a prescribed homotopy type is hard, and we do not know how to construct the required Oka approximations of Eilenberg–MacLane spaces. Instead, we apply the above strategy to an Oka manifold
\Omega_{k,m}
of the homotopy type of the Moore space
M(\mathbb{Z}/m,k)
(see §
2.3
). In this case, the class
\alpha
may not come from the tautological class
{\alpha_{k,m}\in H^{k}(\Omega_{k,m},\mathbb{Z}/m)}
by pullback by a continuous map
f:S\to\Omega_{k,m}
. However, the obstructions to the existence of
f
, which lie in higher degree cohomology groups of
S
, can be killed using
downward
induction on the cohomological degree, allowing the proof to proceed.

To construct the
\Omega_{k,m}
, we rely on a theorem of Kusakabe
[
Kus24
, Corollary 1.3]
(see also the proof of Forstnerič and Wold
[
FW20
, Theorem 1.2]
) stating that
\mathbb{C}^{N}\setminus K
is Oka if
K
is a polynomially convex compact subset of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
for some
{N\geq 2}
. When
{k\geq 3}
, for appropriate choices of
N
and
K
, the Oka manifold
{\Omega_{k,m}:=\mathbb{C}^{N}\setminus K}
has the homotopy type of
M(\mathbb{Z}/m,k)
(see §
2.4
). In addition, if
k\geq 5
, a careful choice of the embedding of
K
as a polynomially convex subset of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
ensures that
{\alpha_{k,m}\in H^{k}(\Omega_{k,m},\mathbb{Z}/m)}
can be killed on a finite flat cover of
\Omega_{k,m}
(see §
2.5
). This completes the proof of Theorem
1.7
for
k\geq 5
(see §
3.3
).

When
k\in\{3,4\}
, we do not know how to kill
\alpha_{k,m}
on a finite flat cover of
\Omega_{k,m}
. The best we can do, by means of a delicate choice of the embedding of
K
as a polynomially convex subset of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, is to ensure that
\alpha_{k,m}
lifts to an integral cohomology class, after pullback by a finite flat cover of
\Omega_{k,m}
. This allows us to reduce Theorem
1.7
for
k\in\{3,4\}
to the case where
\alpha
is the reduction modulo
m
of an integral cohomology class, which can be dealt with by further applications of Oka theory (see §
3.4
). When
k\in\{1,2\}
, we cannot rely on the above strategy. Instead, we give direct proofs of Theorem
1.7
(the case
k=1
is trivial and, when
k=2
, we exploit the study of Brauer groups of Stein algebras carried out in
[
BH26
]
; see §
3.2
).

1.5.
Killing integral cohomology classes on Zariski-open subsets

We conclude this introduction by sketching the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

A crucial input is a consequence of the Bloch–Kato conjecture (proved by Rost and Voevodsky
[
Voe11
]
) according to which, if
V
is an algebraic variety over
\mathbb{C}
and
{\alpha\in H^{k}(V^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})}
is torsion, then
V
is covered by Zariski-open subsets
{V^{\prime}\subset V}
with
{\alpha|_{(V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}}}=0}
(Colliot-Thélène–Voisin
[
CTV12
, Théorème 3.1]
; in the smooth case, the argument is due to Bloch, see
[
Blo80
, End of Lecture 5]
and
[
BS83
, Proof of Theorem 1 (ii)]
). We actually need a slight refinement of
[
CTV12
, Théorème 3.1]
where
(V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}}
is required to contain a prescribed countable subset
\Xi
of
V^{\mathrm{an}}
(see §
5.1
); this is essential for the application to unramified cohomology).

As a first step towards Theorem
1.5
, we find an affine dense open subset
U
of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
such that
\alpha|_{U^{\mathrm{an}}}
is
m
-torsion for some
m
(see §
5.2
; the argument uses Oka theory in the spirit of the strategy explained in §
1.4
). Then
{\alpha|_{U^{\mathrm{an}}}\in H^{k}(U^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})}
is the image of a class
{\delta\in H^{k-1}(U^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z}/m)}
by the Bockstein morphism. If one could lift
\delta
to a class
{\tilde{\delta}\in H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,\mathbb{Z}/m)}
along the comparison morphism

(1.7)

H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,\mathbb{Z}/m)\to H^{k-1}(U^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z}/m),

then one could further lift
\tilde{\delta}
to a class in the étale cohomology of some affine algebraic variety
V
over
\mathbb{C}
, where one could conclude by exploiting the above-mentioned
[
CTV12
, Théorème 3.1]
(see §
5.4
). Unfortunately, the morphism (
1.7
) may not be surjective (Theorem
1.1
does not apply because the inclusion
U\subset\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
is not proper). To overcome this obstacle, we ensure, after reducing to the case where
S
is a connected Stein manifold and through a careful choice of
U
, that
\tilde{\delta}
lifts to a class in the cohomology of a constructible complex of étale sheaves on
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
(see §
5.3
). There, our comparison theorem (Theorem
1.1
) is applicable.

1.6.
Structure of the article

We construct and study some Oka manifolds with prescribed homotopy type in Section
2
, and we use them in Section
3
to kill cohomology classes of Stein spaces with finite coefficients on finite flat covers, thereby proving Theorem
1.7
. This result is applied in Section
4
to prove our main comparison theorem (Theorem
1.1
), as well as a
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})
-equivariant extension. Applications of the comparison theorem to the singular cohomology of Stein spaces appear in Section
5
(where we prove Theorems
1.5
and study unramified cohomology) and Section
6
(where we prove Theorem
1.6
and define the Stein weight filtration).

1.7.
Conventions

We refer to
[
GR84
, 1, §1.5]
for the definition of
complex spaces
. We require them to be second-countable and Hausdorff, but not necessarily reduced or finite-dimensional. By a continuous map from a complex space
S
to a topological space, we always mean a map from the underlying topological space of
S
, thus disregarding nilpotent elements of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
. A complex space
S
is
Stein
if
H^{k}(S,\mathcal{F})=0
for all
k\geq 1
and all coherent sheaves
\mathcal{F}
on
S
(see
[
GR79
]
).

An algebraic variety over
\mathbb{C}
is a separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
.

2.
Oka models of Moore spaces

In this section, we construct the Oka manifolds
\Omega_{k,m}
(see Proposition
2.5
) and study the behavior of their tautological cohomology classes
\alpha_{k,m}\in H^{k}(\Omega_{k,m},\mathbb{Z}/m)
on finite flat covers (see Propositions
2.7
and
2.8
).

2.1.
Preliminaries on Oka theory

Theorem
2.1
is (a particular case of) a result of Forstnerič
[
For09
]
, which builds on earlier work of Gromov
[
Gro89
]
. Our goal in §
2.1
is to dispel any doubts regarding its validity when the Stein space
S
is possibly not reduced, for lack of an appropriate reference. Following Forstnerič
[
For09
, Definition 1.2]
, we say that a complex manifold
\Omega
is
Oka
if for all convex compact subsets
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
and all open neighborhoods
U
of
K
in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, any holomorphic map
U\to\Omega
can be approximated uniformly on
K
by holomorphic maps
\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\Omega
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
S
be a Stein space. Let
p:T\to S
be a locally trivial holomorphic fibration with Oka fibers. Then any continuous section of
p
is homotopic to a holomorphic section of
p
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3
below, we may assume that
S
is reduced. One can then apply the more general
[
For09
, Theorem 1.1]
.
∎

We could not find a reference for the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
p:T\to S
be a submersion between complex spaces. Let
S^{\prime}\subset S
be a closed subspace defined by a coherent ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}_{S}
with
\mathcal{I}^{2}=0
. Let
{f^{\prime}:S^{\prime}\to T}
be a holomorphic section of
p
over
S^{\prime}
. If
H^{1}(S^{\prime},(f^{\prime})^{*}T_{T/S}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S^{\prime}}}\mathcal{I})=0
,
then
f^{\prime}
can be extended to a holomorphic section
f:S\to T
of
p
.

Proof.

Let
(U_{i})_{i\in I}
be a Stein open cover of
S
. Let
U_{i}^{\prime}\subset U_{i}
be the closed subspace defined by
\mathcal{I}|_{U_{i}}
. After refining the cover
(U_{i})_{i\in I}
, we may assume that
f^{\prime}(U^{\prime}_{i})
is included in a chart
V_{i}\subset T
with
V_{i}\simeq U_{i}\times W_{i}
, where
W_{i}
is an open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
with coordinates
(z_{1}^{(i)},\dots,z_{N}^{(i)})
, and where
p|_{V_{i}}:V_{i}\to U_{i}
is given by the first projection
{V_{i}\simeq U_{i}\times W_{i}\to U_{i}}
. Since
U_{i}
is Stein, the map
\mathcal{O}(U_{i})\to\mathcal{O}(U_{i}^{\prime})
is onto. We may thus lift the holomorphic section
f^{\prime}|_{U_{i}^{\prime}}:U_{i}^{\prime}\to U^{\prime}_{i}\times W_{i}
of
p
over
U^{\prime}_{i}
, one coordinate of
W_{i}
at a time, to a holomorphic section
\tilde{f}_{i}:U_{i}\to U_{i}\times W_{i}
of
p
over
U_{i}
.

On
U^{\prime}_{i,j}:=U^{\prime}_{i}\cap U^{\prime}_{j}
, the assignment
a(f^{\prime})^{*}db\mapsto a(\tilde{f}_{i}^{*}b-\tilde{f}_{j}^{*}b)
(where

(f^{\prime})^{*}\Omega^{1}_{T/S}|_{U^{\prime}_{i,j}}\to\mathcal{I}|_{U^{\prime}_{i,j}}

{\alpha_{i,j}\in H^{0}(U^{\prime}_{i,j},(f^{\prime})^{*}T_{T/S}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S^{\prime}}}\mathcal{I})}
. The family
(\alpha_{i,j})
forms a cocycle. By vanishing of

, there exist
{\beta_{i}\in H^{0}(U^{\prime}_{i},(f^{\prime})^{*}T_{T/S}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S^{\prime}}}\mathcal{I})}
with
{\alpha_{i,j}=\beta_{i}-\beta_{j}}
. View
\beta_{i}
as a morphism of sheaves
(f^{\prime})^{*}\Omega^{1}_{T/S}|_{U^{\prime}_{i}}\to\mathcal{I}|_{U^{\prime}_{i}}
. Let
{f_{i}:U_{i}\to V_{i}\simeq U_{i}\times W_{i}}
be the holomorphic section of
p
over
U_{i}
such that
(f_{i})^{*}z_{k}^{(i)}=(\tilde{f}_{i})^{*}z_{k}^{(i)}-\beta_{i}((f^{\prime})^{*}dz_{k}^{(i)})
for

. Our choices imply that the
f_{i}
glue to a holomorphic section
f:S\to T
of

.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
p:T\to S
be a submersion of complex spaces with
S
Stein. Any holomorphic section

of

over

can be extended to a holomorphic section

.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{O}_{S}
be the nilradical of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
. Let

be the subspace defined by the coherent ideal sheaf
\mathcal{N}^{k+1}\subset\mathcal{O}_{S}
, so
S_{0}=S^{\mathrm{red}}
. Set
f_{0}:=f^{\prime}
. Apply Lemma
2.2
inductively to construct holomorphic sections
(f_{k}:S_{k}\to T)_{k\geq 1}
of

over
S_{k}
such that
{(f_{k})|_{S_{k-1}}=f_{k-1}}
. Locally on

, there exists

of

.
∎

2.2.
Polynomially convex simplicial complexes

A compact subset
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is said to be
polynomially convex
if
K
is equal to its
polynomial hull

\widehat{K}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid|P(z)|\leq\sup_{K}|P|\textrm{ for all }P\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]\}.

If
\Sigma
is a simplicial complex, we let
\Sigma_{\leq d}
denote the
d
-skeleton of
\Sigma
. The next proposition is a consequence of a theorem of Vodozov and Zaidenberg
[
VZ71
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite simplicial complex of dimension
\leq n
. Then there exists an injective continuous map
f:\Sigma\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
such that:

(i)

f
is piecewise linear on the first barycentric subdivision of
\Sigma
;

(ii)

f(\Sigma)\cap\mathbb{C}^{d}=f(\Sigma_{\leq d-1})
in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
for
0\leq d\leq n+1
;

(iii)

f(\Sigma)
is a polynomially convex subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.

Proof.

Let
C(\Sigma,\mathbb{C})
be the Banach algebra of continuous complex-valued functions on
\Sigma
. By
[
VZ71
, Theorem 1]
, one can find
f_{1},\dots,f_{n+1}\in C(\Sigma,\mathbb{C})
such that the sub-
\mathbb{C}
-algebra of
C(\Sigma,\mathbb{C})
generated by the
f_{i}
is dense in
C(\Sigma,\mathbb{C})
. The proof given in
[
VZ71
, p. 747]
is constructive and the choices made there ensure that the
f_{i}
are piecewise linear on the first barycentric subdivision of
\Sigma
and that the zero locus of
f_{d+1}
on
\Sigma_{\leq d}
is
\Sigma_{\leq d-1}
. Set
f:=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n+1})
, so
(i)
and
(ii)
hold. The map
f
is injective because
C(\Sigma,\mathbb{C})
separates the points of
\Sigma
. The compact subset
f(\Sigma)
of
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is polynomially convex by
[
Sto07
, Theorem 1.2.10]
, so
(iii)
also holds.
∎

2.3.
Moore spaces

Fix
k,m\geq 1
. Consider a CW complex with one cell of dimension
0
, one cell of dimension
k
(yielding a
k
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{k}
) and one cell of dimension
k+1
with attaching map
\mathbb{S}^{k}\to\mathbb{S}^{k}
of degree
m
. Let
M(\mathbb{Z}/m,k)
be a finite simplicial complex of dimension
k+1
that has the homotopy type of this CW complex (see
[
Hat02
, Theorem 2C.5]
). The space
M(\mathbb{Z}/m,k)
is called a
Moore space
(see
[
Hat02
, Example 2.40]
). Using cellular (co)homology, one checks that

(2.1)

\widetilde{H}^{i}(M(\mathbb{Z}/m,k),\mathbb{Z})=0\textrm{ for }i\neq k+1\textrm{ \,\,and\,\, }\widetilde{H}^{k+1}(M(\mathbb{Z}/m,k),\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}/m,

that
\widetilde{H}_{i}(M(\mathbb{Z}/m,k),\mathbb{Z})=0
for
i\neq k
and
\widetilde{H}_{k}(M(\mathbb{Z}/m,k),\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}/m
, and that
H^{k}(M(\mathbb{Z}/m,k),\mathbb{Z}/m)=\mathbb{Z}/m
. We also note that
\pi_{i}(M(\mathbb{Z}/m,k))=0
for
0<i<k
.

2.4.
The Oka manifolds
\Omega_{k,m}

Fix
k\geq 3
and
m\geq 1
. Set
l:=\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor
, so
k=2l
or
k=2l+1
. By Proposition
2.4
, there exist injective continuous maps

f_{k,m}:M(\mathbb{Z}/m,1)\to\mathbb{C}^{l+2}\textrm{ \,\,and\,\, }g_{k,m}:M(\mathbb{Z}/m,2)\to\mathbb{C}^{l+2},

piecewise linear on the first barycentric subdivision of
M(\mathbb{Z}/m,1)
and
M(\mathbb{Z}/m,2)
respectively, such that the equalities
f_{k,m}(M(\mathbb{Z}/m,1))\cap\mathbb{C}^{l+1}=f_{k,m}(M(\mathbb{Z}/m,1)_{\leq l})
and
g_{k,m}(M(\mathbb{Z}/m,2))\cap\mathbb{C}^{l+1}=g_{k,m}(M(\mathbb{Z}/m,2)_{\leq l})
hold, and whose images are polynomially convex. We define
K_{k,m}:=f_{k,m}(M(\mathbb{Z}/m,1))
when
k
is odd and
{K_{k,m}:=g_{k,m}(M(\mathbb{Z}/m,2))}
when
k
is even. Set
\Omega_{k,m}:=\mathbb{C}^{l+2}\setminus K_{k,m}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

For
k\geq 3
and
m\geq 1
, the complex manifold
\Omega_{k,m}
is Oka (in the sense recalled in §
2.1
) and has the homotopy type of
M(\mathbb{Z}/m,k)
.

Proof.

The complex manifold
\Omega_{k,m}
is Oka by a theorem of Kusakabe
[
Kus24
, Corollary 1.3]
(see also
[
FW20
, Theorem 1.2]
), because
K_{k,m}
is polynomially convex.

The manifold
\Omega_{k,m}
is connected (
resp.
simply connected). To see this, fix a path in
\mathbb{C}^{l+2}
between two points of
\Omega_{k,m}
(
resp.
a based homotopy in
\mathbb{C}^{l+2}
from a loop in
\Omega_{k,m}
to the constant loop). As
f_{k,m}
and
g_{k,m}
are piecewise linear, one can perturb this path (
resp.
this homotopy) to ensure it avoids
K_{k,m}
(by transversality).

By a form of Alexander duality, for all
i\geq 1
, one has

(2.2)

H^{2l+3-i}(K_{k,m},\mathbb{Z}){\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}H_{i+1}(\mathbb{C}^{l+2},\Omega_{k,m},\mathbb{Z}){\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}H_{i}(\Omega_{k,m},\mathbb{Z})

(apply
[
Hat02
, Proposition 3.4.6]
). We deduce from (

) and (

) that

\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Omega_{k,m},\mathbb{Z})=0\textrm{ for }i\neq k\textrm{ \,\,and\,\, }\widetilde{H}_{k}(\Omega_{k,m},\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}/m.

It therefore follows from the characterization
[
Hat02
, Example 4.34]
of Moore spaces that
\Omega_{k,m}
has the homotopy type of
M(\mathbb{Z}/m,k)
.
∎

By Proposition
2.5
and (
2.1
), one has

. We let

be a generator of

Finite flat covers of the

Keep the notation of §

Lemma 2.6
.

For

and

If

-skeleton, and with

∎

3.

The main goal of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. For all
k,m\geq 1
and all
\alpha\in H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
, there exist

and a holomorphic map

that is finite flat of degree

such that

in

.

Remark 3.2
.

It follows from the proof of Theorem
3.1
that the integer
d
only depends on
m
and on the dimension of
S
. It also follows from this proof that the finite map
p
can be chosen to be locally of complete intersection, in addition to being flat.

After gathering general facts about Severi–Brauer spaces in §
3.1
, we prove Theorem
3.1
in §
3.2
when
k\in\{1,2\}
, in §
3.3
when
k\geq 5
(this is the heart of the proof), and in §
3.4
for the slightly more involved cases where
{k\in\{3,4\}}
.

3.1.
Severi–Brauer spaces

Let
S
be a complex space. A
Severi–Brauer space
of relative dimension
N
over
S
is a locally trivial holomorphic fibration
\pi:X\to S
whose fibers are isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
. As the group of holomorphic automorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
is
\mathrm{PGL}_{N+1}(\mathbb{C})
, Severi–Brauer spaces of relative dimension
N
over
S
are classified by
H^{1}(S,\mathrm{PGL}_{N+1}(\mathcal{O}_{S}))
. The following two lemmas are classical in algebraic geometry (in particular, Lemma
3.3
is a complex-analytic version of Châtelet’s theorem
[
Châ44
, p. 35]
), and we include their proofs for completeness.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\pi:P\to S
be a Severi–Brauer space over a complex space
S
. If
\pi
has a holomorphic section, then
\pi
is the projectivization of a holomorphic vector bundle on
S
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{O}_{S}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{P}
, one has
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{P}^{\times}
. The Leray spectral sequence

E^{p,q}_{2}=H^{p}(S,\mathrm{R}^{q}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{P}^{\times})\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(P,\mathcal{O}_{P}^{\times})

therefore gives rise to an exact sequence

(3.1)

H^{1}(P,\mathcal{O}_{P}^{\times})\to H^{0}(S,\mathrm{R}^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{P}^{\times})\to H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times})\to H^{2}(P,\mathcal{O}_{P}^{\times}).

That
\pi
has a holomorphic section shows that
H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times})\to H^{2}(P,\mathcal{O}_{P}^{\times})
is injective.
If
U
is a contractible Stein open subset of
S
that trivializes
\pi:P\to S
, then

H^{1}(\pi^{-1}(U),\mathcal{O}_{\pi^{-1}(U)}^{\times})=H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})\times U,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})\times U}^{\times})=\mathbb{Z}

(use the exponential short exact sequence
[
GR79
, Lemma p. 142]
on
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})\times U
). We deduce an isomorphism
\mathrm{R}^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{P}^{\times}\to\mathbb{Z}
given by the degree of line bundles on the fibers of
\pi
. It now follows from (
3.1
) that there is a holomorphic line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
P
restricting to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})}(1)
on the fibers of
\pi
. One then checks locally (over open subsets
U
of
S
as above) that
\pi_{*}\mathcal{L}
is locally free and that the natural holomorphic map
{P\to\mathbb{P}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{L})}
over
S
is an isomorphism (use cohomology and base change in its form
[
BS76
, III, Corollary 3.9]
).
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. Let
\pi:P\to S
be a Severi–Brauer space of relative dimension
N
. There exist
d\geq 1
and a closed complex subspace
T\subset P
such that
\pi|_{T}:T\to S
is finite flat of degree
d
.

Proof.

Fix
e\geq 1
. Let
\mathcal{L}:=\mathcal{O}_{P}(-K_{P/S})
be the relative anticanonical line bundle of
\pi
, which restricts to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})}(N+1)
on the fibers of
\pi
. By cohomology and base change (see e.g.
[
BS76
, III, Corollary 3.9]
), the sheaf
\mathcal{E}:=\pi_{*}(({\mathcal{L}^{\otimes e})^{\oplus N}})
is locally free and, letting
f:E\to S
be the geometric vector bundle associated with
\mathcal{E}
, the fiber
E_{s}
of
f
over any
s\in S
can be identified with
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C}),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})}(e(N+1)))^{\oplus N}
(this identification is well defined up to the natural action of
\mathrm{GL}_{N+1}(\mathbb{C})
).

Consider the Zariski-open subset
\Theta
of
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C}),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})}(e(N+1)))^{\oplus N}
consisting of
N
-tuples
(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{N})
such that
{\{\sigma_{1}=\dots=\sigma_{N}=0\}}
is a complete intersection in
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
. As
\Theta
is
\mathrm{GL}_{N+1}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant, there exists an open subset
E^{0}\subset E
such that
E^{0}\cap E_{s}
is sent to
\Theta
by the above identifications, for all
s\in S
. The restriction
f^{0}:E^{0}\to S
of
f
is a locally trivial holomorphic fibration with fiber
\Theta
.

Let
n
be the dimension of
S
. By
[
BD18
, Corollary 3.2]
, if
e
has been chosen big enough, then the complement of
\Theta
in
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C}),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})}(e(N+1)))^{\oplus N}
has complex codimension
>\frac{n}{2}
, and it follows that
\pi_{i}(\Theta)=0
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. As
S
has the homotopy type of a CW complex of dimension
\leq n
(see
[
Ham86
, Korollar]
), one can therefore use obstruction theory to find a continuous section
g:S\to E^{0}
of
f^{0}
(apply
[
Ste51
, Theorem 34.2]
).
As
\Theta
is an Oka manifold (see
[
For17
, Proposition 5.6.10]
), Theorem
2.1
shows that
g
can be chosen to be holomorphic.

The section
g
corresponds to a section
(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{N})\in H^{0}(S,\mathcal{E})=H^{0}(P,\mathcal{L}^{\otimes e})^{\oplus N}
. Define
T:=\{\sigma_{1}=\dots=\sigma_{N}=0\}\subset P
. By construction, the complex subspace
T
of
P
is a relative complete intersection of codimension
N
over
S
and hence
{\pi|_{T}:T\to S}
is finite flat of degree
d:=(e(N+1))^{N}
.
∎

Lemma 3.5

Let

Let

(3.2)

\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{an}}(S){\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{top}}(S){\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{tors}}

on

whose class in

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. Fix
\gamma\in H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})
. For all
{m\geq 1}
, there exist
d\geq 1
and a holomorphic map
p:\widetilde{S}\to S
that is finite flat of degree
d
such that
p^{*}\gamma
is divisible by
m
in
H^{2}(\widetilde{S},\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

The cohomology long exact sequence associated with the exponential short exact sequence
0\to\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{2\pi i}\mathcal{O}_{S}\xrightarrow{\exp}\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}\to 0
(for which see
[
GR79
, Lemma p. 142]
) and the vanishing of
H^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})
(because
S
is Stein) show that
\gamma
is the first Chern class of a holomorphic line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
S
.

Let
n
be the dimension of
S
. By
[
Kri69
, Theorem 1]
, the line bundle
\mathcal{L}
(which is globally generated since
S
is Stein) is generated by
n+1
global sections
\sigma_{0},\dots,\sigma_{n}
. Let
{\sigma:S\to\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})}
be the holomorphic map defined by
{\sigma(s):=[\sigma_{0}(s):\dots:\sigma_{n}(s)]}
, so
\sigma^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})}(1)\simeq\mathcal{L}
. The degree
m^{n}
finite flat holomorphic map
r:\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
given by
{r([x_{0}:\dots:x_{n}])=[x_{0}^{m}:\dots:x_{n}^{m}]}
is such that
r^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})}(1)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})}(m)
. Define
p:\widetilde{S}\to S
to be the base change of

by
\sigma
. Then
p^{*}\mathcal{L}
is the
m
-th tensor power of some holomorphic line bundle on
\widetilde{S}
, and the lemma is proved.
∎

\geq 5

Recall that
\alpha_{k,m}
was defined in §

to be a generator of
{H^{k}(\Omega_{k,m},\mathbb{Z}/m)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/m}

.

Fix
k\geq 3
and

. Then

for

Proposition 3.9
.

Theorem
3.1
holds for
k\geq 5
.

Proof.

Let
n
be the dimension of
S
. We henceforth fix
n
and argue by downward induction on
k
. If
k>n
, then
H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)=0
because
S
has the homotopy type of a CW complex of dimension
\leq n
(see
[
Ham86
, Korollar]
), so the result is proven in this case.
We assume from now on that
5\leq k\leq n
.

Let
\varphi:S\to K(\mathbb{Z}/m,k)
be a continuous map classifying
\alpha\in H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
. Consider the problem of finding a continuous map
\psi:S\to\Omega_{k,m}
such that
\varphi_{k,m}\circ\psi
is homotopic to
\varphi
. Obstruction theory provides successive obstructions for this problem, which live in

(see
[
Ste51
, Theorem 34.2]
). These obstructions vanish for
{i\leq k}
by Lemma

and for

has the homotopy type of a CW complex of dimension
\leq n
(see
[
Ham86
, Korollar]
). As

is finite for
{k<i<n}
(see Lemma

), one can kill them after replacing

with a finite flat cover of positive degree, by repeated applications of the induction hypothesis.

We may therefore assume that there exists a continuous map
\psi:S\to\Omega_{k,m}
such that

. As
\Omega_{k,m}
is Oka by Proposition

, it follows from Theorem
2.1
that there exists a holomorphic map
\chi:S\to\Omega_{k,m}
that is homotopic to

, and hence such that

4.1.
Analytification in Stein geometry

Let
S
be a Stein space and let
X
be a separated

\mathcal{O}(S)
-scheme locally of finite presentation. In
[
Bin76
, Satz 1.1]
, Bingener functorially associates with
X
its
analytification
: a complex space
X^{\mathrm{an}}
over
S
endowed with a morphism
{i_{X}:X^{\mathrm{an}}\to X}
. Concretely, if
{X=\{f_{1}=\dots=f_{M}=0\}\subset\mathbb{A}^{N}_{\mathcal{O}(S)}}
is affine, then
X^{\mathrm{an}}=\{f_{1}=\dots=f_{M}=0\}\subset S\times\mathbb{C}^{N}
. In general, the construction proceeds by gluing analytifications of affine charts. The space
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is naturally in bijection with
X(\mathbb{C})
, where
X
is viewed as a
\mathbb{C}
-scheme (see
[
Ben24
, Lemma 2.3]
).
Analytification is compatible with fiber products (see
[
Bin76
, p. 3]
) and restriction of scalars (see
[
Ben24
, Lemma 2.2]
). The
analytification
of a finitely presented quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
is the coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{an}}:=i_{X}^{*}\mathcal{F}
on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
(see
[
Bin76
, p. 3]
).

Let
S
be a Stein space. A coherent sheaf
\mathcal{G}
on
S
is said to be
globally finitely presented
if it admits a presentation of the form
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\oplus N^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\oplus N}\to\mathcal{G}\to 0
for some
N,N^{\prime}\geq 0
(we warn the reader that the reference
[
Ben24
, §2.3]
calls such sheaves
finitely presented
). A finite holomorphic map
p:T\to S
is said to be
globally finitely presented
if the coherent sheaf
p_{*}\mathcal{O}_{T}
is globally finitely presented.

The next proposition, in the spirit of
[
For67
]
, follows from
[
Ben24
, Proposition 2.5 and Remarks 2.6]
(see
[
BH26
, (2.2)]
). It will be used in the proof of Proposition
5.6
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
S
be a Stein space. There is an exact monoidal adjoint equivalence of categories

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\textrm{finitely presented quasi-coherent}\\
\hskip 20.00003pt\textrm{sheaves on }\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))\end{array}\right\}\mathrel{{\mathop{\rightleftarrows}\limits}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\textrm{globally finitely presented}\\
\hskip 6.99997pt\textrm{coherent sheaves on }S\end{array}\right\}

and right-to-left functor

.
If

is finite-dimensional, then (

\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
and holomorphic vector bundles on

.

The following proposition is used in the proof of Theorem

.

Proposition 4.2
.

If
S
is a Stein space, then the two functors
X\mapsto X^{\mathrm{an}}
and
{T\mapsto\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(T))}
induce an adjoint equivalence

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\textrm{finite }\mathcal{O}(S)\textrm{-schemes }X\\
\hskip 3.00003pt\textrm{of finite presentation}\end{array}\right\}\rightleftarrows\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\hskip 20.00003pt\textrm{globally finitely presented}\\
\textrm{finite holomorphic maps }p:T\to S\end{array}\right\}.

If the
\mathcal{O}(S)
-scheme
X
is flat (
resp.
surjective) then so is
p
.

If
p
is flat (
resp.
flat and surjective) then so is the
\mathcal{O}(S)
-scheme
X
.

Proof.

This is proved in
[

, Proposition 2.7]
, except for the parenthesized assertions. If

is a surjective morphism of finite presentation, then
f^{\mathrm{an}}:X^{\mathrm{an}}\to S
is surjective by
[

, Satz 3.1 (2)]
. Now let
p:T\to S
be a surjective globally finitely presented finite flat holomorphic map with induced finite flat morphism of finite presentation
f:\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(T))\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
. The image
Z
of

is a closed and open subscheme of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
. As the surjective map

factorizes through the open subspace

of

, one has
Z^{\mathrm{an}}=S
. It therefore follows from the equivalence (

∎

Remark 4.3
.

In the setting of Proposition
4.2
, if
p
is not assumed to be flat, the surjectivity of

does not necessarily imply the surjectivity of
f
. Here is an example. For
n\geq 1
, let
S_{n}
be the zero-dimensional Stein space with
{\mathcal{O}(S_{n})=\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^{n})}
. Let
S
be the disjoint union of the
(S_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. Let
p:T:=S^{\mathrm{red}}\hookrightarrow S
be the reduction of
S
. As
T
is the zero locus of
\varepsilon
in
S
, the surjective finite holomorphic map
p
is globally finitely presented. Since the element
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{O}(S)=\prod_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^{n})
is not nilpotent, it does not vanish on all points of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))

f:\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(T))=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)/\varepsilon)\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
is not surjective.

Let
S
be a Stein space. To address the difficulty mentioned in Remark
4.3
, we say that a finite holomorphic map

is
algebraically surjective
if the induced scheme morphism
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(T))\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
is surjective.

Finally, the next lemma gives criteria for a finite holomorphic map between Stein spaces to be globally finitely presented.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let

be a Stein space.

(i)

If two finite holomorphic maps

and

are globally finitely presented, then so is their fiber product
(p,p^{\prime}):T\times_{S}T^{\prime}\to S
.

(ii)

If two finite holomorphic maps
p:T\to S
and

are globally finitely presented, then so is their composition
p\circ q
.

(iii)

If
S
is finite-dimensional, then any holomorphic map
p:T\to S
that is finite flat of some degree

is globally finitely presented.

Proof.

Assertions
(i)
and
(ii)
follow from Proposition
4.2
(since they become clear on the left side of the equivalence (
4.2
)). Assertion
(iii)
holds because

is locally free of degree

4.1
.
∎

4.3.
The étale cohomology of a Stein algebra

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. The morphisms

H^{k}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{Z}/m)\to H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)

are isomorphisms for all
k\geq 0
and all
m\geq 1
.

Proof.

We refer to
[
SP
, Definition
01G5
]
for the definition of a hypercovering of an object of a site. By (
4.2
), there is an equivalence of categories between hypercoverings
X_{\bullet}\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
in
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))_{\mathrm{f}}
and hypercoverings
T_{\bullet}\to S
in
S_{\mathrm{f}}
(given by
T_{\bullet}=X_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{an}}
and
X_{\bullet}=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S_{\bullet}))
). Note that
X_{\bullet}\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
and
T_{\bullet}\to S
are hypercoverings for the h and qc topology respectively (see (
4.4
)).

Take the colimits over all such hypercoverings (the transition maps being given by refinements of hypercoverings) of Čech-to-derived spectral sequences (for which see
[
SP
, Lemma
01GY
]
) for the h and qc topologies. This yields spectral sequences

(4.5)

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}=\mathrm{colim}_{X_{\bullet}}\check{H}^{p}(X_{\bullet},\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{h}}^{q}(\mathbb{Z}/m))

\displaystyle\Rightarrow H^{p+q}_{\mathrm{h}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{Z}/m)\textrm{ \hskip 5.0ptand }

(4.6)

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}=\mathrm{colim}_{T_{\bullet}}\check{H}^{p}(T_{\bullet},\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{qc}}^{q}(\mathbb{Z}/m))

\displaystyle\Rightarrow H^{p+q}_{\mathrm{qc}}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m),

where
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{h}}^{q}(\mathbb{Z}/m)
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{qc}}^{q}(\mathbb{Z}/m)
respectively are the presheaves

and

.
Using
[
SP
, Lemma
0EWH
]
(which, in the excellent noetherian case, is due to Suslin and Voevodsky

, Corollary 10.10]
) and
[

]
, one can rewrite the spectral sequences (

) and (

) as

(4.7)

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}=\mathrm{colim}_{X_{\bullet}}\check{H}^{p}(X_{\bullet},\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{q}(\mathbb{Z}/m))

\displaystyle\Rightarrow H^{p+q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathbb{Z}/m)\textrm{ \hskip 5.0ptand }

(4.8)

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}=\mathrm{colim}_{T_{\bullet}}\check{H}^{p}(T_{\bullet},\mathcal{H}^{q}(\mathbb{Z}/m))

\displaystyle\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m),

where
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{q}(\mathbb{Z}/m)
and
\mathcal{H}^{q}(\mathbb{Z}/m)
are the presheaves given by

and

. In addition, there are natural morphisms from (

) to (

), and hence from (

) to (

), induced by vertical arrows in (

).

Fix

and

. For any morphism

]

The

-equivariant morphism of sites
\varepsilon:(X^{\mathrm{an}})_{\mathrm{cl}}\to(X_{\mathcal{O}(S)})_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
induces a morphism between the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequences

(4.15)

E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}(G,H^{q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{\mathcal{O}(S)},\mathbb{L}))\Rightarrow H^{p+q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{L})

and

(4.16)

E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}(G,H^{q}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}}))\Rightarrow H_{G}^{p+q}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{L}^{\mathrm{an}})

(see
[
Sch94
, Remark 10.9]
). It induces an isomorphism between the second pages of (
4.15
) and (
4.16
) by Theorem
4.7
, hence also between their abutments.
∎

5.
Application to the coniveau

Theorem
5.9
below is an application of our main comparison theorem (Theorem
4.7
) to the coniveau of integral cohomology classes on Stein spaces.

5.1.
Coniveau of torsion cohomology classes, in algebraic geometry

By way of preparation for the proof of Theorem
5.9
, we include the following refinement of Colliot-Thélène and Voisin’s
[
CTV12
, Théorème 3.1]
. In its proof, the reduction to the smooth case uses Hoobler’s trick
[
Hoo06
]
(see also
[
Ker09
, Proof of Theorem 7.8]
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
V
be a quasi-projective algebraic variety over
\mathbb{C}
. Fix
k\geq 0
and a torsion class
{\alpha\in H^{k}(V^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})}
. Let
\Xi\subset V^{\mathrm{an}}
be a countable subset. There exists a dense open subset
V^{\prime}\subset V
such that
\Xi\subset(V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}}
and
\alpha|_{(V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}}}=0
in
H^{k}((V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

Let
\overline{V}
be a projective compactification of
V
. Let
D\subset\overline{V}
be an ample divisor containing
\overline{V}\setminus V
and such that
D^{\mathrm{an}}\cap\Xi=\varnothing
. After replacing
V
with
\overline{V}\setminus D
and
\alpha
with
\alpha|_{(\overline{V}\setminus D)^{\mathrm{an}}}
, we may assume that
V
is affine.

Let
m\geq 1
be such that
m\alpha=0
. Denote by
\partial_{m}
the boundary maps induced by the short exact sequence
0\to\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{m}\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/m\to 0
. Choose a class
\beta\in H^{k-1}(V^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z}/m)
such that
\partial_{m}(\beta)=\alpha
. Let
\tilde{\beta}\in H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(V,\mathbb{Z}/m)
be the inverse image of
\beta
by Artin’s comparison isomorphism
H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(V,\mathbb{Z}/m){\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}H^{k-1}(V^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z}/m)
.

Fix
N\geq 0
and a closed embedding
V\subset\mathbb{A}^{N}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
i:V\hookrightarrow\widehat{V}
be the henselization of
\mathbb{A}^{N}_{\mathbb{C}}
along
V
(see
[
SP
, Lemma
0A02
]
). By Gabber’s affine base change theorem (see
[
Gab94
, Theorem 1]
), there exists
\tilde{\gamma}\in H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\widehat{V},\mathbb{Z}/m)
with
i^{*}\tilde{\gamma}=\tilde{\beta}
. The scheme
\widehat{V}
is a directed limit of affine étale
\mathbb{A}^{N}_{\mathbb{C}}
-schemes (see the proof of
[
SP
, Lemma
0A02
]
). By
[
SP
, Theorem
09YQ
]
, there exist a smooth affine algebraic variety
W
over
\mathbb{C}
, a morphism of
\mathbb{C}
-schemes
{f:\widehat{V}\to W}
, and a class
{\tilde{\delta}\in H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(W,\mathbb{Z}/m)}
such that
f^{*}\tilde{\delta}=\tilde{\gamma}
. Let
\delta\in H^{k}(W^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z}/m)
be the image of
\tilde{\delta}
by the comparison morphism
H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(W,\mathbb{Z}/m)\to H^{k-1}(W^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z}/m)
. Replacing
V
with
W
, the class
\alpha
with
\partial_{m}(\delta)
and
\Xi
with
(f\circ i)^{\mathrm{an}}(\Xi)
, we reduce to the case where
V
is smooth.

By
[
CTV12
, Théorème 3.1]
, there exists a dense open subset
V^{+}\subset V
such that
\alpha|_{(V^{+})^{\mathrm{an}}}=0
. Set
Z^{+}:=V\setminus V^{+}
, so
\alpha
lifts to a class
\alpha^{+}\in H^{k}_{Z^{+}}(V,\mathbb{Z})
. By the Bloch–Ogus–Gabber effacement theorem (see
[
BO74
, (4.2.3)]
,
[
CTHK97
, Theorem 2.2.7]
or
[
Sch24
, Theorem 1.1]
, and Remark
5.2
below) applied over
k=\mathbb{C}
to the cohomology theory
H^{*}=H^{*}((-)^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})
, there exists a dense open subset
{V^{\prime}\subset V}
with
{\Xi\subset(V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}}}
such that
\alpha^{+}
(hence also
\alpha
) vanishes in
H^{k}((V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Keep the notation of the proof of Theorem
5.1
. The Bloch–Ogus–Gabber effacement theorem as stated in
[
BO74
,
CTHK97
,

]
applies when

is a single point (in
[

]
) or a finite set (in
[
CTHK97
,
Sch24
]
). However, in the situation we consider (over

for

), making very general choices in the proofs of
[

,
CTHK97
,

]
allows them to go through when

is countable. It is easier to check this for the proof of
[

, Theorem 2.2.7]
given in
[

(in

5.2.
Coniveau of integral cohomology classes, up to a multiple

As a first step towards Theorem
5.9
, we show that any integral cohomology class of degree
\geq\penalty 2
on a finite-dimensional Stein space has a multiple of coniveau
\geq\penalty 1
, in a strong sense (see Propositions
5.5
and
5.6
). Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
are used to prove Proposition
5.5
for odd and even degree classes respectively. Lemma
5.4
appears in
[
BV24
, Proposition 3.1]
but the proof there only applies to finite CW complexes.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
T
be a finite-dimensional CW complex. Fix
{\alpha\in H^{k}(T,\mathbb{Z})}
for some
k\geq 1
odd. Then there exist
m\geq 1
and a continuous map
f:T\to\mathbb{S}^{k}
such that
m\alpha=f^{*}\omega
(where
\omega\in H^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{k},\mathbb{Z})
denotes the canonical generator).

Proof.

Let
K(\mathbb{Z},k)
be an Eilenberg–MacLane CW complex with tautological class
\beta\in H^{k}(K(\mathbb{Z},k),\mathbb{Z})
. Let
K(\mathbb{Z},k)_{\leq d}
be the
d
-skeleton of
K(\mathbb{Z},k)
. For
m\geq 1
, we let
{\mu_{m}:K(\mathbb{Z},k)\to K(\mathbb{Z},k)}
be a cellular map (well defined up to homotopy) induced by multiplication by
m
on
\mathbb{Z}
, so that
(\mu_{m})^{*}\beta=m\beta
.

Let
\nu:\mathbb{S}^{k}\to K(\mathbb{Z},k)
be a continuous map classifying
\omega
(so
\nu^{*}\beta=\omega
) and let
F_{\nu}
be the homotopy fiber of
\nu
. Since
k
is odd, Serre’s results on the homotopy groups of spheres
[
Ser51
, Proposition 3 p. 494]
and the long exact sequence of homotopy groups in a fibration show that
\pi_{i}(F_{\nu})
is finite for all
i
, and vanishes for
1\leq i\leq k
.

We claim that, for all
d\geq 0
, there exist an integer
m_{d}\geq 1
and a continuous map
g_{d}:K(\mathbb{Z},k)_{\leq d}\to\mathbb{S}^{k}
such that
\nu\circ g_{d}=(\mu_{m_{d}})|_{K(\mathbb{Z},k)_{\leq d}}
. The proof is by induction on
d
. Assuming that
m_{d-1}
and
g_{d-1}
have been constructed, we need to extend the lift
g_{d-1}
of
\nu
to a lift
g_{d}:K(\mathbb{Z},k)_{\leq d}\to\mathbb{S}^{k}
, after maybe replacing
g_{d-1}
with
g_{d-1}\circ\mu_{m^{\prime}}
for some
m^{\prime}\geq 1
(then set
m_{d}:=m^{\prime}m_{d-1}
). The obstruction for doing so lives in
H^{d}(K(\mathbb{Z},k),\pi_{d-1}(F_{\nu}))
(see
[
Ste51
, Theorem 34.2]
). As
\pi_{d-1}(F_{\nu})
is finite, this obstruction class can be killed after pullback by
\mu_{m^{\prime}}
for
m^{\prime}
sufficiently divisible (the argument appears at the end of
[
BV24
, Proof of Lemma 2.2]
).

If
d
is the dimension of
T
, one can find a continuous map
\varphi:T\to K(\mathbb{Z},k)_{\leq d}
classifying
\alpha
, so
\varphi^{*}(\beta|_{K(\mathbb{Z},k)_{\leq d}})=\alpha
. Set
f:=g_{d}\circ\varphi
and
m:=m_{d}
. To conclude the proof, one computes that

∎

f^{*}\omega=f^{*}\nu^{*}\beta=\varphi^{*}g_{d}^{*}\nu^{*}\beta=\varphi^{*}(\mu_{m_{d}}|_{K(\mathbb{Z},k)_{\leq d}})^{*}\beta=m\varphi^{*}(\beta|_{K(\mathbb{Z},k)_{\leq d}})=m\alpha.

Let
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
be the Grassmannian of quotients of dimension
r
of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, and let
\mathcal{E}_{r,N}
denote the tautological rank
r
vector bundle on
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
T
be a finite-dimensional CW complex. Fix
r\geq 1
and classes
\alpha_{i}\in H^{2i}(T,\mathbb{Z})
for
1\leq i\leq r
. Then there exist integers
m\geq 1
and
N\geq r
, and a continuous map
f:T\to\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
such that
m\alpha_{i}=f^{*}c_{i}(\mathcal{E}_{r,N})
for
1\leq i\leq r
.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{BGL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
be the classifying space of complex vector bundles of rank
r
, constructed as the colimit of the
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
for
N\geq r
. Let
\mathcal{E}_{r}
be the universal vector bundle on
\mathrm{BGL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
(so
\mathcal{E}_{r}|_{\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)}=\mathcal{E}_{r,N}
). Let
\lambda:\mathrm{BGL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})\to\prod_{1\leq i\leq r}K(\mathbb{Z},2i)
be a continuous map classifying the Chern classes of
\mathcal{E}_{r}
.

As
H^{*}(\mathrm{BGL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}[c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{r}),\dots,c_{r}(\mathcal{E}_{r})]
, and in view of the computation of
H^{*}(\prod_{1\leq i\leq r}K(\mathbb{Z},2i),\mathbb{Q})
in
[
Ser51
, Proposition 4 p. 501]
, the map
\lambda
induces an isomorphism on
H^{*}(-,\mathbb{Q})
, hence on
H_{*}(-,\mathbb{Q})
, hence on
\pi_{*}(-)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
by
[
Ser53
, Théorème 3]
. It also induces an isomorphism on
\pi_{i}(-)
for
i\leq 2
, by the Hurewicz theorem. Letting
F_{\lambda}
be the homotopy fiber of
\lambda
, the long exact sequence of homotopy groups of a fibration shows that
\pi_{i}(F_{\lambda})
is finite, and vanishes for
{i\in\{1,2\}}
.

Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
5.3
(using the map
\lambda
instead of
\nu
), one finds an integer
m\geq 1
and a continuous map
f:T\to\mathrm{BGL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
such that
m\alpha_{i}=f^{*}c_{i}(\mathcal{E}_{r})
for
1\leq i\leq r
. Since
T
is finite-dimensional and the inclusion
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)\subset\mathrm{BGL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
is a
(2N-2r+1)
-equivalence, the map
f
factorizes through
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
for
N
big enough, which concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. Fix
{\alpha\in H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z})}
for some
k\geq 1
. Set
c:=\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor
. There exist
m\geq 1
, an algebraic variety
V
over
\mathbb{C}
that is homogeneous under a linear algebraic group
G
, a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
of rank
c
on
V
, a section
{\sigma\in H^{0}(V,\mathcal{E})}
transverse to the zero section with zero locus
j:V^{\prime}\hookrightarrow V
, a class
\zeta\in H^{k-2c}((V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})
, and a continuous map
{f:S\to V^{\mathrm{an}}}
with
m\alpha=f^{*}(j^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\zeta
.

Proof.

Recall that
S
has the homotopy type of a finite-dimensional CW complex (see
[
Ham86
, Korollar]
), so the use of Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
below is legitimate.

Assume first that
k=2c+1\geq 1
is odd. Endow
V:=\mathbb{A}^{c+1}_{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{(0,\dots,0)\}
with its natural action of
G:=\mathrm{GL}_{c+1,\mathbb{C}}
. Set
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{O}_{V}^{\oplus c}
and
{\sigma:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{c})\in\mathcal{O}(V)^{\oplus c}}
(so
{V^{\prime}=\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}}
). The manifolds
(V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}}
and
V^{\mathrm{an}}
have the homotopy type of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
\mathbb{S}^{k}
respectively, and we let
\zeta\in H^{1}((V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})
and
\omega\in H^{k}(V^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})
be the canonical generators (so
\omega=(j^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\zeta
). Lemma
5.3
shows that there exists a continuous map
f:S\to V^{\mathrm{an}}
such that
m\alpha=f^{*}\omega=f^{*}(j^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\zeta
for some
{m\geq 1}
.

Assume now that
k=2c\geq 2
is even. By Lemma
5.4
(applied with
{r=c}
and
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r})=(0,\dots,0,\alpha)
), there exist
m\geq 1
and
N\geq r
, as well as a continuous map
{f:S\to\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)}
, such that
m\alpha=f^{*}c_{r}(\mathcal{E}_{r,N})
. Let
V
and
\mathcal{E}
be the algebraic variety and the algebraic vector bundle whose analytifications are
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
and
\mathcal{E}_{r,N}
. Note that
V
is homogeneous under the natural action of
G:=\mathrm{GL}_{N,\mathbb{C}}
. As
\mathcal{E}
is globally generated, the Bertini theorem shows that a general section
{\sigma\in H^{0}(V,\mathcal{E})}
is transverse to the zero section. Let
{j:V^{\prime}\hookrightarrow V}
be its zero locus. Set
{\zeta:=1\in H^{0}((V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})}
, so
{(j^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\zeta=c_{r}(\mathcal{E}_{r,N})}
and
{m\alpha=f^{*}(j^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\zeta}
.
∎

We define
Koszul-regular immersions
of schemes as in
[
SP
, Definition
063J
]
. A Koszul-regular immersion has
codimension

c
if its conormal sheaf (see
[
SP
, Definition
01R2
]
) is locally free of rank
c
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
S
be a connected Stein manifold. Let
\Xi\subset S
be a countable subset. Fix
{\alpha\in H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z})}
for some
k\geq 2
. Set
c:=\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor
. There exist
m\geq 1
, a Koszul-regular closed immersion
\iota:X\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
of codimension
c
, and a cohomology class
{\beta\in H^{k-2c}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})}
, such that
X^{\mathrm{an}}\cap\Xi=\varnothing
, such that
\iota^{\mathrm{an}}:X^{\mathrm{an}}\hookrightarrow S
is the inclusion of a submanifold of codimension
c
, and such that
m\alpha=(\iota^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\beta
.

Proof.

Let
(m,V,G,\mathcal{E},\sigma,j,V^{\prime},\zeta,f)
be as in Proposition
5.5
. Since
V^{\mathrm{an}}
is Oka (see
[
For17
, Proposition 5.6.1]
), we may choose
f
holomorphic (see Theorem
2.1
). As
V^{\mathrm{an}}
is
G^{\mathrm{an}}
-homogeneous, the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
map
S\times G^{\mathrm{an}}\to V^{\mathrm{an}}
given by
(s,g)\mapsto g\cdot f(s)
is a submersion. By the transversality theorem
[
GP74
, p. 68]
, the map
{f_{g}:s\mapsto g\cdot f(s)}
is transverse to
j^{\mathrm{an}}
for almost all
g\in G^{\mathrm{an}}
. Replacing
f
with
f_{g}
for a well-chosen
{g\in G^{\mathrm{an}}}
, we may assume that
f
is transverse to
j^{\mathrm{an}}
and that

.

Set
\mathcal{F}:=f^{*}\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{an}}
and
{\tau:=f^{*}\sigma^{\mathrm{an}}\in H^{0}(S,\mathcal{F})}
. By transversality of
f
and
j^{\mathrm{an}}
, the section
\tau
is transverse to the zero section and its zero locus
h:T\hookrightarrow S
is a codimension
c
submanifold of
S
. Define
{\beta:=(f|_{T})^{*}\zeta\in H^{k-2c}(T,\mathbb{Z})}
. Again by transversality of

and

, one has
{m\alpha=f^{*}(j^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\zeta=h_{*}(f|_{T})^{*}\zeta=h_{*}\beta}
.

Applying the equivalence of categories of Proposition
4.1
to
\mathcal{F}
and
\tau
yields a vector bundle
\mathcal{G}
of rank
c
on
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
and a section
\upsilon\in H^{0}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathcal{G})
. Let
\iota:X\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
be the zero locus of
\upsilon
. The Koszul complex

(5.1)

0\to\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!c}}\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\tau}\dots\xrightarrow{\tau}\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\tau}\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\tau}\mathcal{O}_{S}\to\mathcal{O}_{T}\to 0

associated with

is exact (use
[
SP
, Lemma
062F
]
in the local rings of

).
Applying the above equivalence of categories to (
5.1
) gives rise to the Koszul complex

(5.2)

0\to\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!c}}\mathcal{G}^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\upsilon}\dots\xrightarrow{\upsilon}\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}\mathcal{G}^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\upsilon}\mathcal{G}^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\upsilon}\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to 0

associated with
\upsilon
. Since this equivalence is exact (see Proposition
4.1
), the complex (

) is a resolution of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. This means that

is a Koszul-regular immersion, of codimension

because the conormal sheaf of

is
\mathcal{G}^{\vee}|_{X}
.

It follows from Proposition

that (

) is the analytification of (

), hence that

and

. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

5.3.
Applying the comparison theorem

Recall that we denote by
\partial_{m}
the boundary maps induced by the short exact sequence
0\to\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{m}\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/m\to 0
. The proof of the next proposition is complicated by the fact that the comparison morphism
H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,\mathbb{Z}/m)\to H^{k-1}(U^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z}/m)
that appears in its statement may not be an isomorphism.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
S
be a connected Stein manifold. Let
\Xi\subset S
be a countable subset. Fix
k\geq 2
and
\alpha\in H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z})
. There exist
m\geq 1
, a dense open subset
U
of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
, and a class
\tilde{\delta}\in H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,\mathbb{Z}/m)
with image
\delta\in H^{k-1}(U^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z}/m)
by the comparison morphism
H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,\mathbb{Z}/m)\to H^{k-1}(U^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z}/m)
, such that
\Xi\subset U^{\mathrm{an}}
and
{\partial_{m}(\delta)=\alpha|_{U^{\mathrm{an}}}}
in
H^{k}(U^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

Set
c:=\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor
. Let
m
, as well as
\iota:X\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
and
\beta\in H^{k-2c}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})
be as in Proposition
5.6
. Set
U:=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))\setminus X
, so
U^{\mathrm{an}}=S\setminus X^{\mathrm{an}}
.

Let
\mathrm{Cl}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\iota^{\mathrm{an}}}:\mathbb{Z}[-2c]{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}(\iota^{\mathrm{an}})^{!}\mathbb{Z}
be the Thom isomorphism in
D^{+}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})
. After pushforward by
\iota^{\mathrm{an}}
and composition with the natural morphism
{(\iota^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}(\iota^{\mathrm{an}})^{!}\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}}
, it gives rise to the Gysin morphism
\mathrm{Gys}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\iota^{\mathrm{an}}}:(\iota^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{Z}[-2c]\to\mathbb{Z}
in
D^{+}(S,\mathbb{Z})
. Let
{\mathbb{K}\in D^{+}(S,\mathbb{Z})}
be defined by the distinguished triangle

(5.3)

\mathbb{Z}\oplus(\iota^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{Z}[-2c]\xrightarrow{(m,\mathrm{Gys}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\iota^{\mathrm{an}}})}\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{K}\xrightarrow{+1}.

As
m\alpha=(\iota^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\beta
(see Proposition
5.6
), the class
(\alpha,-\beta)\in H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}\oplus(\iota^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{Z}[-2c])
lifts, in the long exact sequence of cohomology of (
5.3
), to a class
\gamma\in H^{k-1}(S,\mathbb{K})
. Since the restriction of (
5.3
) to
U^{\mathrm{an}}
reads

(5.4)

\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{m}\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/m\xrightarrow{+1},

one has
\mathbb{K}|_{U^{\mathrm{an}}}=\mathbb{Z}/m
, and
\delta:=\gamma|_{U^{\mathrm{an}}}\in H^{k-1}(U^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z}/m)
satisfies
{\partial_{m}(\delta)=\alpha|_{U^{\mathrm{an}}}}
.
It remains to show that
\delta
lifts to a class
\tilde{\delta}\in H^{k-1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,\mathbb{Z}/m)
.

Let
\mathrm{Cl}_{\iota^{\mathrm{an}}}:\mathbb{Z}/m[-2c]{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}(\iota^{\mathrm{an}})^{!}\mathbb{Z}/m
be the modulo
m
Thom isomorphism in
D^{+}(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z}/m)
, and denote by
\mathrm{Gys}_{\iota^{\mathrm{an}}}:(\iota^{\mathrm{an}})_{*}\mathbb{Z}/m[-2c]\to\mathbb{Z}/m
the induced Gysin morphism in
D^{+}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
.

U^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
, but that is nonzero at all points of
\Xi
. Define
Z:=\{a=0\}\subset S
. As
S\setminus Z\subset(U^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}}
, the class
\alpha|_{S\setminus Z}\in H^{k}(S\setminus Z,\mathbb{Z})
vanishes, which concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

The analogue of Theorem
5.9
with finite coefficients does not hold in general, even for
k=2
. Indeed, if
S
is a Stein manifold and
\alpha\in H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
vanishes on the complement of a nowhere dense closed analytic subset
Z
of
S
, then
\alpha
is the class of a divisor of
S
supported on
Z
, and hence is the reduction modulo
m
of a class in
H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})
. As a consequence, any
\alpha
that does not lift to an integral cohomology class cannot vanish on the complement of such a
Z
.

5.5.
Cohomology and unramified cohomology of Stein spaces

Let
S
be a Stein space. For
k\geq 0
, define
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{S}(\mathbb{Z})
to be the Zariski sheaf on
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
associated with the presheaf
U\mapsto H^{k}(U^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})
. Equivalently, one has
{\mathcal{H}^{k}_{S}(\mathbb{Z})=\mathrm{R}^{k}i_{*}\mathbb{Z}}
, where
{i:S\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))}
is the canonical continuous morphism from
S
(with its usual topology) to
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
(with the Zariski topology). We define the
unramified cohomology groups
of
S
with integral coefficients to be

H^{k}_{\mathrm{nr}}(S,\mathbb{Z}):=H^{0}(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)),\mathcal{H}^{k}_{S}(\mathbb{Z})).

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. For all
k\geq 2
, the natural morphism
H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z})\to H^{k}_{\mathrm{nr}}(S,\mathbb{Z})
vanishes identically.

Proof.

Fix
\alpha\in H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z})
and
x\in\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
. Let
{\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}(S)}
be the prime ideal associated with
x
. By Lemma
5.12
below, there exists a discrete (hence countable) subset
\Xi\subset S
such that any element of
\mathfrak{p}
vanishes at some point of
\Xi
. By Theorem
5.9
, there exists a closed analytic subset
Z\subset S
such that
Z\cap\Xi=\varnothing
and
\alpha|_{S\setminus Z}=0
in
H^{k}(S\setminus Z,\mathbb{Z})
. Choose
a\in\mathcal{O}(S)
vanishing on
Z
but not on any point of
\Xi
(so
a\notin\mathfrak{p}
). Define
U:=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S)[\frac{1}{a}])\subset\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}(S))
, so
U^{\mathrm{an}}=S\setminus\{a=0\}
. One has
x\in U
(because
a\notin\mathfrak{p}
) and
\alpha|_{U^{\mathrm{an}}}=0
(because
U^{\mathrm{an}}\subset S\setminus Z
). Since
x
was arbitrary, this shows that the image of
\alpha
in
H^{k}_{\mathrm{nr}}(S,\mathbb{Z})
vanishes.
∎

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. Let
I\subsetneq\mathcal{O}(S)
be an ideal. There exists a discrete subset
\Xi\subset S
such that any
a\in I
vanishes at a point of
\Xi
.

Proof.

Choose a finite collection
(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
of elements of
I
such that the dimension of
T:=\{a_{1}=\dots=a_{r}=0\}\subset S
is minimal. Since
S
is Stein, the exact sequence
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\oplus r}\xrightarrow{(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})}\mathcal{O}_{S}\to\mathcal{O}_{T}\to 0
remains exact after taking global sections. As
I\neq\mathcal{O}(S)
, we deduce that
T
is nonempty.

Let
\Xi\subset S
be a discrete subset containing at least one point from each irreducible component of
T
. Suppose for contradiction that
a\in I
does not vanish at any point of
\Xi
. Then
T^{\prime}:=\{a=a_{1}=\dots=a_{r}=0\}\subset T
contains no irreducible component of
T
, so
\dim(T^{\prime})<\dim(T)
. This contradicts our choice of
T
.
∎

Remarks 5.13
.

(i)
Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space and fix
k\geq 2
. We do not know whether
H^{k}_{\mathrm{nr}}(S,\mathbb{Z})
vanishes (this would strengthen Theorem
5.11
).

(ii)
We do not know whether
\mathcal{H}_{S}^{k}(\mathbb{Z})
is torsion-free for all finite-dimensional Stein spaces and all

. This would be a Stein analogue of
[

.

(iii) The Leray spectral sequence of the morphism

reads

[

CTV12
]
. Theorem
5.11
suggests that the behaviour of (

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. Fix
\alpha\in H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z})
for some
k\geq 1
. There exist an affine algebraic variety
V
over
\mathbb{C}
, a holomorphic map
f:S\to V^{\mathrm{an}}
, and a class
\beta\in H^{k}(V^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z})
such that
\alpha=f^{*}\beta
.

Remarks 6.5
.

(i) The morphism (
6.3
) is not surjective for
k=0
in general (a class
\alpha\in H^{0}(S,\mathbb{Z})
taking infinitely many distinct values is not in its image).

(ii) Let us show that (
6.3
) is not injective in general. Serre constructed two quasi-projective algebraic varieties
V
and
V^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{C}
such that
V^{\mathrm{an}}
and
(V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}}
are both biholomorphic to
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
, but such that
H^{1}(V^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Q})
and
H^{1}((V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Q})
are pure of weights
1
and
2
in the sense of Deligne
[
Del71
,
Del74
]
(see
[
Har70
, VI, Example 3.2]
for the construction of
V
and
V^{\prime}
). As morphisms of rational Hodge structures are strict with respect to the weight filtration (see
[
Del71
, Théorème 2.3.5 (iii)]
), there cannot exist an algebraic variety
W
over
\mathbb{C}
and morphisms
h:W\to V
and
h^{\prime}:W\to V^{\prime}
such that
h^{*}(H^{1}(V^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Q}))
and
(h^{\prime})^{*}(H^{1}((V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Q}))
are equal and nonzero. This implies that (
6.3
) is not injective for
S=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
.

6.3.
The Stein weight filtration

Inspired by Deligne’s mixed Hodge theory, Gillet and Soulé defined a weight filtration
W_{\bullet}
on the compactly supported singular cohomology of algebraic varieties over
\mathbb{C}
, with arbitrary coefficients (see
[
GS96
, §3.1.2]
). Their construction was extended to cohomology without support by Cirici and Guillén
[
CG14
, §6]
(this is attributed to
[
GNA02
]
in the last lines of
[
GS96
, §3.1.2]
).

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. Fix
k\geq 0
and
m\geq 1
. For
{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
, we define
W^{\mathrm{St}}_{j}H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
to be the subgroup of
H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
spanned by the
f^{*}\alpha
, where
{f:S\to V^{\mathrm{an}}}
is a holomorphic map to the analytification of some quasi-projective algebraic variety
V
over
\mathbb{C}
, and where
\alpha\in W_{j}H^{k}(V^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z}/m)
. In view of Theorem
6.1
, one can restrict to those
V
that are affine in the above definition.

We call the increasing filtration
W_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{St}}
on
H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
the
Stein weight filtration
. The following proposition gathers its basic properties.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
S
be a finite-dimensional Stein space. Fix
k\geq 0
and
m\geq 1
.

(i)

Let
p:T\to S
be a holomorphic map of finite-dimensional Stein spaces. For
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, one has
p^{*}(W^{\mathrm{St}}_{j}H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m))\subset W^{\mathrm{St}}_{j}H^{k}(T,\mathbb{Z}/m)
.

(ii)

One has
W_{-1}^{\mathrm{St}}H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)=0
and
W_{2k}^{\mathrm{St}}H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)=H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
.

(iii)

Let
V
be a quasi-projective algebraic variety over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
f:S\to V^{\mathrm{an}}
be holomorphic. If
\alpha\in H^{k}(V^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z}/m)
satisfies
f^{*}\alpha\in W_{j}^{\mathrm{St}}H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
, then there exists a morphism
g:V^{\prime}\to V
of quasi-projective algebraic varieties over
\mathbb{C}
such that
f
lifts to
f^{\prime}:S\to(V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}}
and
(g^{\mathrm{an}})^{*}\alpha\in W_{j}H^{k}((V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb{Z}/m)
.

Proof.

Assertion
(i)
is immediate from the definition. Assertion
(ii)
follows from the corresponding properties of the weight filtration (see
[
CG14
, Corollary 6.3]
) and from the surjectivity of (
6.1
). Assertion
(iii)
follows from the injectivity of (
6.1
).
∎

Remarks 6.7
.

(i)
In
[
Tot02
, §2]
, Totaro extended the construction of the weight filtration on the compactly supported cohomology of algebraic varieties given in
[
GS96
]
to the case of complex spaces endowed with an equivalence class of compactifications. The weight filtration on cohomology without support is also defined in this more general setting (see
[
CG14
, §6]
). The Stein weight filtration defined above is not a special case of this construction. Indeed, Stein spaces are not compactifiable in general. In addition, when a Stein space is compactifiable, the weight filtration may depend on the choice of compactification (as in Serre’s example described in Remark
6.5
(i)), whereas the Stein weight filtration is entirely canonical.

(ii)
As an example, let us compute
W_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{St}}H^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{\times},\mathbb{Z}/m)
. For any elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{C}
, there exists a holomorphic map
f:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to E^{\mathrm{an}}
that is a topological covering of group
\mathbb{Z}
. This shows that
W_{1}^{\mathrm{St}}H^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{\times},\mathbb{Z}/m)=H^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{\times},\mathbb{Z}/m)
. In addition, if
V
is an algebraic variety over
\mathbb{C}
and
f:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to V^{\mathrm{an}}
is holomorphic, we let
V^{\prime}
be a desingularization of the Zariski closure of the image of
f
. As
f
lifts to a holomorphic map
f^{\prime}:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to(V^{\prime})^{\mathrm{an}}
and
V^{\prime}
is smooth, this shows that
W_{0}^{\mathrm{St}}H^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{\times},\mathbb{Z}/m)=0
.

(iii)
We do not know whether
W_{k}^{\mathrm{St}}H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)=H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
holds for all finite-dimensional Stein spaces. If one could restrict, in (
6.1
), to those
f:S\to V^{\mathrm{an}}
with
V
projective, this equality could be deduced from
[
CG14
, Corollary 6.3 2)]
.

(iv)
Let
S
be a Stein manifold. We do not know whether
W_{k-1}^{\mathrm{St}}H^{k}(S,\mathbb{Z}/m)
always vanishes. If one could restrict, in (
6.1
), to those
f:S\to V^{\mathrm{an}}
with
V
smooth, this could be deduced from
[
CG14
, Corollary 6.3 1)]
. In turn, this fact would follow from the (unproven) assertion that
\mathcal{O}(S)
is a filtered colimit of smooth
\mathbb{C}
-algebras (a Stein analogue of the Néron–Popescu desingularization theorem
[
Swa98
]
).
