A scale-free density bound for Gaussian maxima
Suhas Vijaykumar

Abstract
. We derive a scale-free bound on the density of the maximum of a centered Gaussian vector. The basic bound is non-uniform, depends logarithmically on the dimension, and allows any covariance matrix. When the largest marginal variance is separated from zero, it implies that the density of the maximum is uniformly controlled at all quantiles above 2 / 3 \nicefrac{{2}}{{3}} , which is sufficient for many hypothesis testing applications; it yields validity of Gaussian and bootstrap approximations for maxima of high-dimensional sums at test levels α < 1 / 3 \alpha<\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}} without further restricting the covariance. The result also implies uniform anti-concentration bounds and control of the variance of the maximum with optimal dimension dependence, in terms of the expectation of the maximum and the largest marginal variance. We discuss implications for high-dimensional correlation testing, time-uniform sequential testing, and non-parametric inference under latent, low-dimensional structure.



1.
Introduction

Gaussian and bootstrap approximations for maxima of sums have become an essential component of modern statistics, providing practical methods for inference in problems of high-dimensional selection, multiple testing, and non-parametric estimation. These tools are especially useful when the distribution being approximated has a complex form and its covariance is difficult to characterize, yet simulation-based inference remains effective
[
7
]
.

A certain limitation of the theory, however, concerns
degeneracy
: settings where coordinates have vanishing variance, a high degree of correlation, and where standardization is not viable. In these cases, it becomes difficult to verify that the approximating law has a bounded density (or is
anti-concentrated
), which is needed to justify conventional statistical inference. This setting arises in a wide range of modern statistical problems, from multiple testing with genomic or gene-expression data, to anytime-valid sequential testing, to the construction of uniform confidence bands when data have latent, low-dimensional structure.

In each of these cases, the standard remedy of dividing by coordinate-wise standard deviations can fail. This happens for two primary reasons: as
\sigma(x)
approaches zero, uniform control of
|\hat{\sigma}(x)/\sigma(x)-1|
becomes challenging, and, moreover, the standardized process may not be tight. In the context of high-dimensional correlation testing, the relevant “coordinates” are sparse linear combinations
\left\langle\beta,\,X\right\rangle
, and estimating the variance of each such combination is non-trivial; indeed, certifying that the smallest restricted eigenvalue exceeds any fixed threshold is NP-hard
[
2
,
16
]
. In anytime-valid sequential testing, standardizing the time-dependent variance results in an approximating Gaussian law that is not tight. In non-parametric inference on data with latent low-dimensional structure, pointwise variances depend strongly on the unknown intrinsic dimension and local geometry, causing the two problems to compound.

1.1.
Primary contributions

This paper studies anti-concentration and Gaussian approximation under degeneracy. We state density bounds for the maximum which depend logarithmically on the dimension and, importantly, place minimal restrictions on the covariance. This leads to Gaussian and bootstrap approximations under similarly permissive conditions.

The most general bound covers the upper quantiles of the distribution, including the most relevant range for hypothesis testing. As a consequence, we demonstrate validity of standard Gaussian and bootstrap critical values for maxima of high-dimensional sums at levels
\alpha<\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}
under the simple requirement of a single non-degenerate coordinate.

These arguments further imply variance bounds and global anti-concentration bounds (i.e., ones that hold at all quantiles of the distribution), which have optimal dependence on the ambient dimension and which remain sharp under degeneracy. The following summarizes the main results.

Example 3: confidence bands under latent structure

1.2.
Related work

Densities of maxima of Gaussian vectors are classical objects of study, and
8
demonstrate their central role in high-dimensional and non-parametric statistics. They showed that controlling the density of the Gaussian maximum—together with an appropriate high-dimensional coupling—suffices to establish distributional approximation in several problems of practical and theoretical interest. A general anti-concentration bound was obtained for centered vectors by
9
, yielding a density bound of order
(\min_{i}\sigma_{i})^{-1}\sqrt{\log p}
, which is proved to be essentially optimal when all marginal variances are of constant order. Further work extended this result to the non-centered case
[
10
,
11
]
via Nazarov’s inequality on the Gaussian surface area of polytopes
[
31
,
25
]
.

Subsequent work has sought to relax the dependence on
\min_{i}\sigma_{i}
.
30
, in establishing improved bootstrap approximations under weak variance decay, also obtain improved density bounds by separating the contributions of large and small variances.
14
, who provide novel non-Gaussian bootstrap couplings, further improve anti-concentration estimates in certain regimes including for non-centered Gaussian vectors (see also
[
10
]
). More recently,
20
studies general order statistics and relates the problem to control of the variance. With the exception of
30
, who exploit structured variance decay and control of correlations, dependence on the minimum variance persists in each of these works.

This paper eliminates the dependence on
\min_{i}\sigma_{i}
for quantiles above
\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}
, and provides uniform anti-concentration bounds of optimal order which remain valid under degeneracy.
Our approach exploits the geometry of centered maxima—specifically, that facets of the max polytope corresponding to small variance coordinates are moved far from the origin by standardization.

Organization

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
contains the main technical density bounds for Gaussian maxima: a non-uniform bound that requires essentially no condition on the covariance, location-uniform bounds and variance control under mild conditions on
\mathbb{E}[\max_{i}Z_{i}]
, a characterization of the density under weak variance decay, and a bound at extremal quantiles. Section
3
works out the implications for Gaussian and multiplier-bootstrap approximation of maxima of high-dimensional sums under weakened variance restrictions. Section
5

A

2.
Anti-concentration of degenerate maxima

We begin with a density bound for arbitrary finite-dimensional Gaussian vectors. The novelty of the bound is that it is non-uniform in the location

\Sigma\succeq 0

2.1.
A non-uniform, scale-free density bound

. Recall the definition
\coth(x)=(e^{2x}+1)/(e^{2x}-1)
. Then

\mathrm{Var}(X)\geq\mathbb{E}[X]^{2}\left\{\frac{1}{2B}\coth\left(\frac{1}{2B}\right)-1\right\}\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}[X]^{2}}{13B^{2}},

where the first inequality is tight, and the last inequality holds whenever
B\geq 1
.

Lemma
2.2
follows from a short tilting argument (Lemma
5.1
), after ruling out mass on
(-\infty,0]
. One can show that the minimum variance is attained by a density of the form
g_{*}(t)=(B/t)\mathbbm{1}\{a\leq t\leq b\}
, and then optimize over
b>a>0
subject to
\int g_{*}(t)\,dt=1
. The full proof of the lemma is postponed to Section
5.2
. Proposition
5
is then proved by applying Lemma
2.2
with
X=M
and
B=4\log(p)
.
∎

Variance-density bounds

One may consider lifting Proposition
5
into a uniform density bound using a statement of the following form (cf.
[
20
]
).

Assumption A
.

It holds that
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}f(t)\leq\sqrt{\kappa/\mathrm{Var}(M)}
.

A variance-density principle of this form was recently proposed
[
20
, Theorem 2]
. However, as stated, it requires additional
qualification for any (even dimension-dependent)
\kappa
: take

Z\sim N(0,\Sigma)
with
\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\delta,1,\ldots,1)
in any dimension
p\geq 2
.
As
\delta\downarrow 0
, both mean and variance of
\max_{i}Z_{i}
converge to positive constants, while the maximum density diverges as
\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
4
4

4

By our Proposition
3
, such a density spike must occur in a neighborhood of
0
.

Nevertheless, we record some interesting implications of Assumption
A
below. In particular, under Assumption
A
, we recover a general principle for lifting non-uniform bounds into uniform bounds by evaluating them at
\mu
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that Assumption
A
holds. Then, in the setting of Proposition
5
,

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}f(t)\leq\frac{15\sqrt{\kappa}\log p}{\mu}.

Moreover, under Assumption
A
, the density of the maximum of any non-trivial, not necessarily centered Gaussian vector, satisfies the relation

(10)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}f(t)\lesssim\kappa^{2}f(\mu).

Proof.

The first claim is an immediate consequence of Proposition
5
and Assumption
A
. For the second, we combine Assumption
A
with Ehrhard-concavity of the CDF of
M
(Lemma
2.5
below) and a variational argument; a full proof is given in Appendix
A
.
∎

Remark 3

(Unsigned maxima)
.

For unsigned maxima,
M^{*}=\max_{i}|Z_{i}|
, one may take
Z\sim N(0,\Sigma)
for

\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}\{4,1/(\log p),\ldots,1/(\log p)\}
to obtain
\sup_{t}f(t)\asymp\log p
with
\mathrm{Var}(M^{*})
of constant order. This leaves open the question of whether a uniform density bound of order
\log p
, and a variance-density principle with
\kappa
depending on the dimension, may hold for unsigned maxima.

2.4.
Improved bounds under pointwise decay

We now consider the behavior of our bounds under further structural assumptions, particularly the weak variance decay considered by
30
.
Without loss of generality, suppose that the standard deviations
\{\sigma_{q}\}_{1\leq q\leq p}
are given in decreasing order.
The bounds of Section
2.1
admit the following extension, implying a dimension-independent bound when
\sigma_{q}=o(1/\sqrt{\log q})
.

Proposition 6
.

For
u>0
, let
S_{u}=\{q:\sigma_{q}\geq u\}
be the set of coordinate indices whose standard deviations exceed
u
and
S_{u}^{c}
be the complementary set. We then have

(11)

f(t)\lesssim\inf_{u>0}\left\{\frac{\sqrt{\log(\#S_{u}\vee 2)}}{u}+\sum_{q\in S_{u}^{c}}\frac{1}{\sigma_{q}}\exp\left(\frac{-t^{2}}{2\sigma_{q}^{2}}\right)\right\}.

Proof.

See Section
5.1
.

Proposition
6
recovers the following sharp boundary in the behavior of the density under weak variance decay. The result is based upon convergence of the sum appearing in (
11
).

Example

(Boundedness under weak variance decay)
.

Suppose that for some
\alpha>0
, the vector
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
satisfies (after a possible rearrangement of the coordinates):

(12)

\sigma_{k}\leq\frac{t}{\sqrt{2\alpha\log k}}.

If
\alpha>1
, then
f(t)
is bounded by an envelope which depends only upon
\alpha
(and not
p
).

On the other hand, for any
p
and any
t>0
, we may take
Z
to be a Gaussian with variance
t^{2}(2\log p)^{-1}I_{p}
. This construction satisfies (
12
) with the precise constant
\alpha=1
, and it has
f(t)\asymp\sqrt{\log p}/t
for each
p
, so that the density is unbounded as
p
grows. In particular, this construction has
\mu\sim 1
as
p\uparrow\infty
, and
M-\mu=O_{p}(\log^{-1}p)

[
5
, Appendix A]
.

Thus, (
11
) captures the threshold for uniform boundedness of the density under this variance profile, up to the sharp constant
\alpha=1
in the regime
\sigma_{k}\asymp(2\alpha\log k)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.

2.5.
Density at extremal quantiles

Arguments based on concavity of the Gaussian law can provide a more complete picture of the density at extremal points. As usual, we let
f
denote the density of
M=\max_{i}Z_{i}
on
(0,\infty)
and we let
F(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}f(s)\,ds
be its cumulative distribution function.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
Z\sim N(0,\Sigma)
be a centered Gaussian vector in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
, let
M=\max_{1\leq i\leq p}Z_{i}
with density
f
on
(0,\infty)
, and let
m
be a median of
M
. Then, whenever
m>0
,
f
is non-increasing on
[m,\infty)
, and for all
h\geq 0
,

(13)

f(m+h\sigma_{\mathrm{max}})\leq\sqrt{2\pi}\,f(m)\,\varphi(h)=f(m)\,e^{-h^{2}/2},

where
\varphi
is the standard Gaussian density. Thus, the non-uniform bound (
3
) gives the envelope

\frac{4\log p}{m}\,e^{-h^{2}/2}\quad(h\geq 0).

Lemma
2.4
provides a differential analog of the Gaussian concentration inequality, which states that
1-F(m+h\sigma_{\mathrm{max}})\leq 1-\Phi(h)
. It follows from the celebrated inequality of
17
, which implies the following.

Lemma 2.5

(Ehrhard concavity)
.

The map
G(t)\!=\!\Phi^{-1}\{F(t)\}
is concave on the support of
F
.

4
showed, moreover, that
G(t)
is concave if and only if
F
is the cumulative distribution function of the supremum of a separable Gaussian process.

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

Let
G(s)=\Phi^{-1}\{F(s)\}
, so that
F(s)=\Phi(G(s))
. By Lemma
2.5
,
G
is concave, hence differentiable almost everywhere with
G^{\prime}
non-increasing; at these points,

f(s)=G^{\prime}(s)\,\varphi(G(s)).

We restrict to points of differentiability and extend to all
h\geq 0
by continuity. This immediately gives

(14)

G^{\prime}(m)=f(m)/\varphi(0)=\sqrt{2\pi}\,f(m).

Gaussian concentration gives the slope-one lower bound

G(m+h\sigma_{\mathrm{max}})\;\geq\;h,\qquad h\geq 0.

Combining this with
G^{\prime}\downarrow
and
G(m+h\sigma_{\mathrm{max}})\geq h\geq 0
, which implies
\varphi(G(m+h\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}))\leq\varphi(h)
, we obtain

f(m+h\sigma_{\mathrm{max}})\;=\;G^{\prime}(m+h\sigma_{\mathrm{max}})\,\varphi(G(m+h\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}))\;\leq\;G^{\prime}(m)\,\varphi(h)\;=\;\sqrt{2\pi}\,f(m)\,\varphi(h),

which is (
13
). For the envelope, note that if
m>0
the non-uniform bound (
3
) applies at
t=m
to give
f(m)\leq 4\log p/m
; combined with the monotonicity of
f
on
[m,\infty)
established above, this yields
\sup_{t\geq m}f(t)=f(m)\leq 4\log p/m
and the displayed Gaussian envelope.
∎

3.
Some implications

In this section, we work out the implications of the bounds of Section
2
for Gaussian and bootstrap approximation of maxima of high-dimensional sums. The novelty is that these approximations place weaker restrictions on the pointwise variances, and thus they remain applicable under degeneracy.

We rely on the high-dimensional couplings of
26
, which build on the seminal Gaussian and bootstrap approximation bounds of
11
using a randomized Lindeberg construction. The coupling is the substantive ingredient; our density bounds enter only as a replacement for Koike’s application of Nazarov’s surface area inequality, and the only work is subsequent optimization of the bound. In other words, the corollaries below follow by combining the two results with little additional effort. We focus on this particular coupling because its proof needs only anti-concentration of
centered
Gaussian maxima—the regime in which our results apply. Subsequent improvements to the high-dimensional CLT
[
6
, e.g.,]
achieve tighter dimension dependence but rely internally on anti-concentration of non-centered Gaussian vectors, which we do not address.

Throughout this section,
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
are independent, centered random vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
whose entries satisfy the sub-exponential bound
\|X_{ij}\|_{\psi_{1}}\leq K
for some constant
K\geq 1
. We write
S_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
for the normalized sum and
\Sigma=\mathbb{E}[S_{n}S_{n}^{\top}]
for its covariance, with largest marginal variance

(15)

\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}=\max_{1\leq j\leq p}\Sigma_{jj}=\max_{1\leq j\leq p}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[X_{ij}^{2}].

We let
Z\sim N(0,\Sigma)
denote the Gaussian analog of
S_{n}
, and
t_{q}^{\Sigma}
the
q^{\mathrm{th}}
quantile of
\max_{1\leq j\leq p}Z_{j}
. For the bootstrap results we additionally fix multipliers
(w_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, i.i.d. and independent of
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
, with
\mathbb{E}[w_{i}]=0
,
\mathbb{E}[w_{i}^{2}]=1
, and
|w_{i}|\leq b
almost surely for some constant
b\geq 1
, and form the multiplier (wild) bootstrap statistic

S_{n}^{\mathrm{WB}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}(X_{i}-\bar{X}_{n}),\qquad\bar{X}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}.

We additionally impose the following mild large-sample condition, which ensures that the lower-order remainder term in the coupling of
26
is dominated.

Assumption B
.

The quantities above satisfy

b\,K^{2}(\log p)^{1/2}(\log n)^{2}\;\leq\;\sqrt{n},

with
b=1
understood for the Gaussian results of Section
3.1
(which involve no multipliers).

Assumption
B
can be dropped whenever
K,b=O(1)
, as a non-trivial rate already requires
\log^{7}p\lesssim n
, and then
(\log p)^{1/2}(\log n)^{2}=o(\sqrt{n})
. It plays no role beyond ensuring the second remainder term of the coupling is of lower order; see the proof of Corollaries
7
–
10
in Appendix
B
.

3.1.
High-dimensional CLT for degenerate vectors

The utility of the approximations below is their immediate ability to handle vectors
X_{i}
, which may have many coordinates with very small variance. In fact, the main requirement is that
\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}=\max_{j}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}X_{ij}^{2}
is not too small, which is enforced by a simple, global rescaling; note that (
16
) is scale-invariant.

Corollary 7

(Non-uniform CLT)
.

Under Assumption
B
, we have

(16)

\sup_{q\geq\frac{2}{3}}\left|\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq p}Z_{j}\leq t_{q}^{\Sigma}\right\}-\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq p}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{ij}\leq t_{q}^{\Sigma}\right\}\right|\lesssim{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{-2/3}}\left(\frac{K^{4}\log^{7}p}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}.

Our results also imply a high-dimensional CLT in Kolmogorov’s distance analogous to
11
, though restricted to centered maxima, in the regime
\mu\gg\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}
.

Corollary 8

(Uniform CLT)
.

Put
\mu=\mathbb{E}[\max_{1\leq j\leq p}Z_{j}]>0
. Under Assumption
B
, we have

(17)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq p}Z_{j}\leq t\right\}-\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq p}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{ij}\leq t\right\}\right|\lesssim e^{\frac{-\mu^{2}}{8\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}}}+\mu^{-2/3}\left(\frac{K^{4}\log^{7}p}{n\,}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}.

3.2.
Bootstrap approximation

A parallel result holds for the multiplier (or wild) bootstrap statistic
S_{n}^{\mathrm{WB}}
introduced above, analyzed by
26
, whose conditional law given the data
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
approximates that of
\max_{1\leq j\leq p}Z_{j}
.

Corollary 9

(Non-uniform bootstrap)
.

Under Assumption
B
,

(18)

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sup_{q\geq\frac{2}{3}}\,\left|\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq p}\,(S_{n}^{\mathrm{WB}})_{j}\leq t_{q}^{\Sigma}\,\big|\,X\right\}-\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq p}Z_{j}\leq t_{q}^{\Sigma}\right\}\right|\right]\lesssim\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{-2/3}\left(\frac{b^{2}K^{4}\log^{7}p}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}.

Corollary 10

(Uniform bootstrap)
.

With
\mu
as in Corollary
8
, under Assumption
B
,

(19)

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,\left|\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq p}\,(S_{n}^{\mathrm{WB}})_{j}\leq t\,\big|\,X\right\}-\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq p}Z_{j}\leq t\right\}\right|\lesssim e^{\frac{-\mu^{2}}{8\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}}}+\mu^{-2/3}\left(\frac{b^{2}K^{4}\log^{7}p}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}.

3.3.
Approximation of unsigned maxima

The term
e^{-\mu^{2}/8\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}}
in Corollaries
8
and
10
is informative only when
\mu\gg\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}
. For the
unsigned
maximum it disappears: the small-ball estimate of Lemma
2.1
replaces Gaussian concentration and yields the unrestricted bound (
6
). In what follows, we write
\mu^{*}=\mathbb{E}\{\max_{1\leq j\leq p}|Z_{j}|\}
.

Corollary 11

(Uniform CLT, unsigned maxima)
.

Under Assumption
B
,

(20)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbb{P}\Big\{\max_{1\leq j\leq p}|(S_{n})_{j}|\leq t\Big\}-\mathbb{P}\Big\{\max_{1\leq j\leq p}|Z_{j}|\leq t\Big\}\right|\;\lesssim\;(\mu^{*})^{-2/5}\left(\frac{K^{4}\log^{7}p}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{10}}.

Corollary 12

(Uniform bootstrap, unsigned maxima)
.

Under Assumption
B
,

(21)

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbb{P}\Big\{\max_{1\leq j\leq p}|(S_{n}^{\mathrm{WB}})_{j}|\leq t\,\big|\,X\Big\}-\mathbb{P}\Big\{\max_{1\leq j\leq p}|Z_{j}|\leq t\Big\}\right|\;\lesssim\;(\mu^{*})^{-2/5}\left(\frac{b^{2}K^{4}\log^{7}p}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{10}}.

Remark 4
.

Unlike Corollaries
8
and
10
, the bounds (
20
)–(
21
) apply even when
\mu^{*}\asymp\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}
; a single non-degenerate coordinate (implying
\mu^{*}>0
) suffices. The price is the exponent
\nicefrac{{1}}{{10}}
in place of
\nicefrac{{1}}{{6}}
, driven entirely by
t
near zero—for
t
bounded away from
0
the density envelope (
3
) alone applies and the rate exponent
\nicefrac{{1}}{{6}}
is recovered.

Corollaries
7
-
12
require no lower bound on the minimum variance
\min_{j}\sigma_{j}
, in contrast to similar results which depend explicitly on
(\min_{j}\sigma_{j})^{-1}

[
8
,
11
,
26
]
.

4.
Conclusion

This paper established a scale-free density bound for the maximum of a centered Gaussian vector: on
(0,\infty)
the density satisfies
f(t)\leq 4\log p/t
, with no restriction on the covariance. From this we derived uniform anti-concentration and variance bounds for the maximum, control of its density at extremal quantiles, and Gaussian and bootstrap approximation guarantees for high-dimensional maxima that remain valid under degeneracy.

Two questions are left open. First, the corresponding theory for unsigned maxima
\max_{i}|Z_{i}|
is unresolved: as noted in Remark
3
, the signed counterexamples do not fully transfer, and it is possible that a uniform
density
bound of order
\log p
and a variance–density principle with a dimension-dependent constant can be verified. Second, our density bounds enter the approximation results of Section
3
only as a drop-in replacement for Nazarov’s inequality within the coupling of
26
; it would be of interest to incorporate our anti-concentration results more intrinsically into Gaussian comparison and coupling arguments, as was done by
[
6
]
in the non-degenerate case.

5.
Proofs of main results

5.1.
The master bound

We begin by stating a more general result, from which the bounds of Sections
2.1
and
2.4
follow quite easily. Its proof will be given in Section
5.3
.

Proposition 13
.

Let
Z
be a centered Gaussian random vector in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
with covariance
\Sigma
.
Let
\varphi
be the standard normal density.
Then, for any
u,t>0
, it holds that

(22)

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\left(\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq p}Z_{i}\leq t+\delta\right\}-\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq p}Z_{i}\leq t\right\}\right)\leq\left(\frac{u+t}{u^{2}}\right)+\sum_{0<\sigma_{i}<u}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\varphi\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{i}}\right).

In particular,
M=\max_{1\leq i\leq p}Z_{i}
admits a density on
(0,\infty)
.

Proof of Proposition
3
(scale-free bound)

We begin by explaining how Proposition
13
may be used to recover the main results. Note that the function
xe^{-x^{2}}
is decreasing for
x\geq 1/\sqrt{2}
as its derivative is
(1-2x^{2})e^{-x^{2}}
. Put
x=x(\sigma)=t/(\sigma\sqrt{2})
, which is a decreasing function of
\sigma
. It follows that if
\sigma\leq t
, so that
x(\sigma)\geq 1/\sqrt{2}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma}\exp\left(\frac{-t^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)=\sqrt{2}t^{-1}xe^{-x^{2}}

is decreasing in
x
, hence the left-hand side is an increasing function of
\sigma
. Thus, whenever
u\leq t
the bound (
22
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\left(\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq p}Z_{i}\leq t+\delta\right\}-\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq p}Z_{i}\leq t\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{u+t}{u^{2}}\right)+\frac{p}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{1}{u}\exp\left(\frac{-t^{2}}{2u^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\log p}{t}+\left(\sqrt{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\right)\frac{\sqrt{\log p}}{t}\leq\frac{4\log p}{t},

where the last line follows from taking
u=(2\log p)^{-\frac{1}{2}}t
and simplifying. This proves one of the two claimed bounds.

Next, put

u^{\prime}=t(2\log p+2\log\log p)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

and note that for

\sigma\leq u^{\prime},

we have

\sigma^{-1}\varphi(t/\sigma)\leq(\sqrt{2\pi}\,p\,t)^{-1}.

Thus, our upper bound (
22
) may be analyzed as

(23)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{u+t}{u^{2}}\right)+\sum_{\sigma_{i}<u}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\varphi\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{u+t}{u^{2}}\right)+\sum_{u>\sigma_{i}>u^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\varphi\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{i}}\right)+\sum_{u^{\prime}\geq\sigma_{i}}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\varphi\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{u+t}{u^{2}}\right)+\sum_{u>\sigma_{i}>u^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\varphi\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{i}}\right)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}t}.

Note that optimizing the bound given by the first two summands is analogous to the computation performed in the preceding paragraph, where we now take
u=\{2\log p^{*}(t)\}^{-\frac{1}{2}}t
. Here,
p^{*}(t)
is the number of
\sigma_{i}
that exceed
u^{\prime}
. This proves the second claim of Proposition
3
.

Proof of Proposition
6
(weak variance decay)

We adapt the argument used to prove Proposition
3
. Fix the outer threshold
u>0
appearing in (
11
) and set

k:=\#S_{u}\vee 2=\#\{i:\sigma_{i}\geq u\}\vee 2,

so that every coordinate of
S_{u}
has
\sigma_{i}\geq u
. The master bound (Proposition
13
) holds at an arbitrary inner threshold
v>0
,

(24)

f(t)\leq\frac{v+t}{v^{2}}+\sum_{\sigma_{i}<v}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\,\varphi\!\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{i}}\right);

we bound the contribution of the first term and the summands
\sigma_{i}\geq u
in two ways.

First, take
v=u_{*}:=t/\sqrt{2\log k}
. The first term of (
24
) satisfies
(u_{*}+t)/u_{*}^{2}\leq 2t/u_{*}^{2}=4\log k/t
, since
u_{*}\leq t
for
k\geq 2
. We split the remaining sum at
u
, the second sum below being empty when
u\geq u_{*}
:

(25)

\sum_{\sigma_{i}<u_{*}}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\,\varphi\!\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\;\leq\;\sum_{u\leq\sigma_{i}<u_{*}}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\,\varphi\!\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\;+\;\sum_{\sigma_{i}<u}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\,\varphi\!\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{i}}\right).

The function
\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{-1}\varphi(t/\sigma)
is increasing on
(0,t]
, so each of the at most
k
summands in the first sum is bounded by
u^{-1}\varphi(t/u_{*})=u^{-1}/(\sqrt{2\pi}\,k)
by the definition of
u_{*}
; hence that sum is at most
1/(\sqrt{2\pi}\,u)
. Thus the contribution is at most
4\log k/t+1/(\sqrt{2\pi}\,u)
.

Second, we may optimize the inner threshold in
t
. If
t\leq u\sqrt{2\log k}
, take
v=u
: no coordinate of
S_{u}
enters the sum in (
24
), and the first term obeys
(u+t)/u^{2}\leq(1+\sqrt{2\log k})/u\lesssim\sqrt{\log k}/u
, using
k\geq 2
. If instead
t>u\sqrt{2\log k}
, take
v=u_{*}>u
; then
(u_{*}+t)/u_{*}^{2}\leq 4\log k/t<2\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{\log k}/u
, while the at most
k
leftover coordinates in
[u,u_{*})
contribute, exactly as above, at most
1/(\sqrt{2\pi}\,u)\leq\sqrt{\log k}/u
. Either way the contribution is
\lesssim\sqrt{\log k}/u
.

Taking the smaller of the two, and recalling
k=\#S_{u}\vee 2
, we obtain (
11
) up to absolute constants. Taking the infimum over
u>0
completes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Proofs from Section
2.3
(variance bounds).

In this subsection we prove Lemma
2.2
, the variational bound underlying Proposition
5
. Lower-bounding
\mathrm{Var}(M)
amounts to a variational problem over densities under the pointwise restriction
f(t)\leq B/t
supplied by Proposition
3
(with
B=4\log p
), which is solved exactly by the tilting argument stated next.

Lemma 5.1

(Bathtub principle)
.

Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be measurable,
a:I\to[0,\infty]
, and
c,w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}:I\to\mathbb{R}
measurable. Suppose
f_{*}
satisfies
0\leq f_{*}\leq a
and, for some multipliers
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
, the tilted integrand
P:=c-\sum_{m=1}^{k}\lambda_{m}w_{m}
obeys

f_{*}(t)=a(t)\ \text{ where }P(t)<0,\qquad f_{*}(t)=0\ \text{ where }P(t)>0.

Then
f_{*}
minimizes
\int_{I}c\,f
among all measurable
f
with
0\leq f\leq a
and
\int_{I}w_{m}f=\int_{I}w_{m}f_{*}
for every
m
. For
k=1

w_{1}\equiv 1

[cf.
29

.

Proof.

Let

be any competitor. Pointwise
P\,(f-f_{*})\geq 0

f_{*}=a\geq f
, and where

f_{*}=0\leq f

Proof of Lemma
2.2

Since any feasible distribution can be transported to a feasible distribution on the nonnegative half-line while reducing the cost
\mathbb{E}X^{2}
and preserving the mean, via
X\mapsto X_{+}\,\mathbb{E}[X]/\mathbb{E}[X_{+}]
with
X_{+}=X\mathbf{1}\{X\geq 0\}
, it suffices to consider the problem over distributions on the nonnegative half-line carrying mass
q
on
(0,\infty)
and an atom of mass
1-q
at the origin, which affects neither the mean nor the second moment.

The extremal distribution must place its mass as close as possible to the mean while respecting the upper bound
f(t)\leq B/t
. We apply Lemma
5.1
on
I=(0,\infty)
with cost
c(t)=t^{2}
, box
a(t)=B/t
, and the two constraints
w_{1}\equiv 1
(mass
q
) and
w_{2}(t)=t
(mean
A
). The tilt
P(t)=t^{2}-\lambda_{2}t-\lambda_{1}
is an upward parabola, so
\{P<0\}
is an interval
(a,b)
and the minimizer saturates the box there:

f_{*}(t)=\frac{B}{t}\mathbf{1}\{a\leq t\leq b\}.

The constants
a,b
are fixed by the two constraints,

\int_{a}^{b}\frac{B}{t}\,dt=B\log(b/a)=q,\qquad\int_{a}^{b}t\,\frac{B}{t}\,dt=B(b-a)=A,

that is,

a=\frac{A}{B(e^{q/B}-1)},\qquad b=a\,e^{q/B}=\frac{Ae^{q/B}}{B(e^{q/B}-1)}.

The second moment of the extremal distribution is

\mathbb{E}X_{*}^{2}=\int_{a}^{b}t^{2}\frac{B}{t}\,dt=\frac{B}{2}(b^{2}-a^{2}).

After plugging in the values of
a
and
b
, a further manipulation yields

\mathbb{E}X_{*}^{2}=\frac{A^{2}}{2B}\coth\!\left(\frac{q}{2B}\right),

and hence any such distribution satisfies

\operatorname{Var}(X)\geq\operatorname{Var}(X_{*})=\mathbb{E}X_{*}^{2}-A^{2}=A^{2}\left[\frac{1}{2B}\coth\!\left(\frac{q}{2B}\right)-1\right]\geq A^{2}\left[\frac{1}{2B}\coth\!\left(\frac{1}{2B}\right)-1\right],

the final inequality because
q\leq 1
and
\coth
is decreasing. If
B\geq 1
, the lower bound
1/(13B^{2})
then follows by monotonicity of
u^{-2}(u\coth u-1)
.
∎

5.3.
Proof of Proposition

(master bound)

Let
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
be a Gaussian random vector with covariance
\Sigma
of rank

. Then, we may write

for

a standard Gaussian vector in
\mathbb{R}^{s}
, where

is a

real matrix such that

.
Note that the

row of

, and we write its normalization as

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq p}Z_{i}\leq t+\delta\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq p}\left\langle a_{i},\,g\right\rangle\leq t+\delta\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq p}\left\langle\frac{a_{i}}{\|a_{i}\|_{2}},\,g\right\rangle\leq\frac{t}{\|a_{i}\|_{2}}+\frac{\delta}{\|a_{i}\|_{2}}\right\}

(26)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq p}\left\langle\nu_{i},\,g\right\rangle\leq\sigma_{i}^{-1}t+\sigma_{i}^{-1}\delta\right\}.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that all
\sigma_{i}
are strictly positive, as the remaining coordinates can be dropped from the event in (
26
); since the final bound increases in the number of inequalities,
p
, this is harmless. We use the following Lemma
5.2
, mirroring the standard reduction to Nazarov’s inequality in
[
10

. For completeness, its proof is given in the appendix.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{s}
denote the polytope defined by the inequalities
\left\langle\nu_{i},\,x\right\rangle\leq t/\sigma_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq p
, and let
F_{i}\subset\partial K
be the facet contained in the hyperplane
H_{i}
defined by
\left\langle\nu_{i},\,x\right\rangle=t/\sigma_{i}
. Then

(27)

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\left(\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq p}Z_{i}\leq t+\delta\right\}-\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq i\leq p}Z_{i}\leq t\right\}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\int_{F_{i}}\varphi_{s}(x)\mathscr{H}^{s-1}(dx),

where
\mathscr{H}^{s-1}
is the
(s-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\partial K\subset\mathbb{R}^{s}
.

Proof.

See Appendix
A
.
∎

The crucial observation in the proof is that the facets
F_{i}
in (
27
) are far away from the origin precisely when the corresponding
\sigma_{i}
are small. Accounting for this correspondence drastically improves the final bound.

Following
[
12
]
, for a set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{s}
, we may define
d(x,S)=\inf_{y\in S}\|x-y\|
and, for any facet
F_{i}
as defined in Lemma
5.2
, we define its outward normal column as

N_{i}=\left\{x+y\nu_{i}\,\middle|\,x\in\mathrm{relint}(F_{i}),y>0\right\}.

We also have the following estimates, which are taken from the proof of Nazarov’s inequality given by
[
12
]
. Proofs are provided in Appendix
A
.

Lemma 5.3
.

The sets
N_{i}=\left\{x+y\nu_{i}\,\middle|\,x\in\mathrm{relint}(F_{i}),y>0\right\}
are disjoint, so
\sum_{i}\gamma_{s}(N_{i})\leq 1
.

Lemma 5.4
.

For any facet
F_{i}\subset\partial K
as defined in Lemma
5.2
, we have

(28)

\displaystyle\int_{F_{i}}\varphi_{s}(x)\mathscr{H}^{s-1}(dx)\leq[1+d(0,H_{i})]\gamma_{s}(N_{i})\,\text{ and }

(29)

\displaystyle\int_{F_{i}}\varphi_{s}(x)\mathscr{H}^{s-1}(dx)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left\{-\frac{d(0,H_{i})^{2}}{2}\right\}.

Finally, we apply the main novel idea in the proof: we show that facets

F_{i}
for which
\sigma_{i}
is small (corresponding to
i\in S_{1}
below) cannot contribute much to the sum on the right hand side of inequality (
27
).
To do so, we partition
\sigma_{i}
into two complementary subsets
S_{1}(u)
and
S_{2}(u)
defined by:

\displaystyle S_{1}(u)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{i\in[p]:\sigma_{i}\leq u\right\};

\displaystyle S_{2}(u)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{i\in[p]:\sigma_{i}>u\right\}.

Crucially, note that the distance between the hyperplane
H_{i}
and the origin satisfies

d(0,H_{i})=t/{\sigma_{i}}

by construction. For
i\in S_{1}(u)
, inequality (

) implies that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\int_{F_{i}}\varphi_{s}(x)\mathscr{H}^{s-1}(dx)\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left\{-\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma_{i}^{2}}\right\}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\varphi\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{i}}\right),

so that

\sum_{i\in S_{1}(u)}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\int_{F_{i}}\varphi_{s}(x)\mathscr{H}^{s-1}(dx)\leq\sum_{i\in S_{1}(u)}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\varphi\left(\frac{t}{\sigma_{i}}\right).

Meanwhile, for

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in S_{2}(u)}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\int_{F_{i}}\varphi_{s}(x)\mathscr{H}^{s-1}(dx)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in S_{2}(u)}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}[1+d(0,H_{i})]\gamma_{s}(N_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{u+t}{u^{2}}\sum_{i\in S_{2}(u)}\gamma_{s}(N_{i})\leq\frac{u+t}{u^{2}}.

This completes the proof.

Appendix A

A.1.
Proof of Lemma
2.1

Corollary
4
uses the following linear small-ball bound, a standard consequence of the Gaussian S-inequality of
28
: among symmetric convex sets of a given Gaussian measure, the symmetric strip is extremal under dilation. The argument applies to any seminorm of a Gaussian vector; we state it for
M^{*}=\max_{1\leq i\leq p}|Z_{i}|=\|Z\|_{\infty}
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
Z\sim N(0,\Sigma)
be non-trivial and put
M^{*}=\max_{1\leq i\leq p}|Z_{i}|
and
\mu^{*}=\mathbb{E}[M^{*}]>0
. There is a universal constant
C
such that

\mathbb{P}\{M^{*}\leq r\mu^{*}\}\leq Cr,\qquad 0<r\leq 1.

Proof.

Let
g
be standard Gaussian with
Z=\Sigma^{1/2}g
in distribution (restricting to the support of
Z
if
\Sigma
is singular), and write
\gamma
for the law of
g
. Let
m
be a median of
M^{*}
. Then
K=\{x:\|\Sigma^{1/2}x\|_{\infty}\leq m\}
is a symmetric convex set with
\gamma(K)=\mathbb{P}\{M^{*}\leq m\}=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
and
\mathbb{P}\{M^{*}\leq sm\}=\gamma(sK)
for
s\geq 0
. The S-inequality
[
28
, Corollary 1]
compares the dilates of
K
with those of a symmetric strip
P=\{x:|x_{1}|\leq w\}
of equal measure
\gamma(P)=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
, that is
w=\Phi^{-1}(\nicefrac{{3}}{{4}})
:

\gamma(tK)\leq\gamma(tP)\ \ (0\leq t\leq 1),\qquad\gamma(tK)\geq\gamma(tP)\ \ (t\geq 1).

For
0<t\leq 1
the small-dilation side gives

\mathbb{P}\{M^{*}\leq tm\}=\gamma(tK)\leq\gamma(tP)=\mathbb{P}\{|g_{1}|\leq tw\}\leq\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi}}\,w\,t=:C_{1}t,

using that the density of
|g_{1}|
is at most
\sqrt{2/\pi}
. For
t\geq 1
the large-dilation side gives
\mathbb{P}\{M^{*}>tm\}\leq\mathbb{P}\{|g_{1}|>tw\}
, so that

\mu^{*}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\{M^{*}>s\}\,ds=m\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\{M^{*}>tm\}\,dt\leq m\Big(1+\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\{|g_{1}|>tw\}\,dt\Big)=:C_{0}\,m.

Hence for
0<r\leq 1/C_{0}
,
\mathbb{P}\{M^{*}\leq r\mu^{*}\}\leq\mathbb{P}\{M^{*}\leq C_{0}r\,m\}\leq C_{1}C_{0}\,r
, while for
1/C_{0}<r\leq 1
the claim is trivial since
1\leq C_{0}r
. Enlarging the constant completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Lemma

It remains to establish the lifting relation (

. The relation follows from the result below, applied with

Lemma A.2
.

Let
X
have density
f
, CDF
F
, mean

\mu

and variance

\sigma^{2}.

Assume (i) that

is Ehrhard-concave, i.e.

, and (ii) that

\|f\|_{\infty}\sigma\leq C

for some

.
Then

Proof.

By translating and rescaling, we may assume that

\mu=0
and
\|f\|_{\infty}=1.

Then (ii) gives
\sigma^{2}\leq C^{2}
, and it suffices to prove that
f(0)\gtrsim 1
.

Step 1:
f(t)\leq 2f(0)
for

We first note that Ehrhard-concavity gives a one-sided median bound. In particular, since

is increasing and concave,

is increasing and

.

Step 2:

\varepsilon\geq C^{\prime}bK^{2}n^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\log n)(\log p)

\rho(X)
, depending on
X
but not on the choice of
A
or
y
, with

(32)

\mathbb{E}[\rho(X)]\leq C^{\prime}\varepsilon^{-2}\!\left(\frac{b\,K^{2}(\log p)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{b^{2}K^{4}(\log p)^{2}(\log n)^{2}}{n}\right),

such that, on
\Omega_{0}
, for every Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
and every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,

(33)

\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq p}(S_{n}^{\mathrm{WB}})_{j}-y_{j}\in A\,\middle|\,X\right\}\leq\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq p}Z_{j}-y_{j}\in A^{6\varepsilon}\right\}+\rho(X).

Proof.

The statement is the one-sided, pre-optimization form of
26
; since we use the intermediate coupling rather than its optimized conclusion, we recall the steps from the proof in
26
. We abbreviate the remainders

\delta_{n,1}=\frac{B_{n}^{2}(\log p)^{3}}{n},\qquad\delta_{n,2}=\frac{B_{n}^{2}(\log p)^{2}(\log n)^{2}}{n},

and use
B_{n}\asymp K^{2}
(Remark
5
); thus
\sqrt{\delta_{n,1}}\asymp K^{2}(\log p)^{3/2}/\sqrt{n}
and
\delta_{n,2}\asymp K^{4}(\log p)^{2}(\log n)^{2}/n
are the two terms in (
32
). Set
\kappa_{n}=2K^{2}\log n
and, exactly as in the truncation underlying Lemma
B.1

[
26
, Lemma 5.7]
, put
\tilde{X}_{ij}=X_{ij}\mathbbm{1}\{|X_{ij}|\leq\kappa_{n}\}-\mathbb{E}[X_{ij}\mathbbm{1}\{|X_{ij}|\leq\kappa_{n}\}]
and
\tilde{Y}_{i}=\tilde{X}_{i}-\bar{\tilde{X}}_{n}
, so that
\max_{i,j}|w_{i}\tilde{Y}_{ij}|\leq 4b\kappa_{n}
almost surely.

Truncation split.

Writing
Y_{i}=X_{i}-\bar{X}_{n}
(so that
S_{n}^{wY}:=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i}w_{i}Y_{i}=S_{n}^{\mathrm{WB}}
), for every Borel
A
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,

(34)

\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{j}(S_{n}^{\mathrm{WB}})_{j}-y_{j}\in A\,\middle|\,X\right\}\leq\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{j}(S_{n}^{w\tilde{Y}})_{j}-y_{j}\in A^{\varepsilon}\,\middle|\,X\right\}+\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\|S_{n}^{w(Y-\tilde{Y})}\|_{\infty}>\varepsilon\,\middle|\,X\right\}.

Conditional coupling.

Conditionally on
X
, the vectors
(w_{i}\tilde{Y}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
are independent and centered, with conditional covariance
n^{-1}\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}[w_{i}^{2}]\,\tilde{Y}_{i}\tilde{Y}_{i}^{\top}=n^{-1}\sum_{i}\tilde{Y}_{i}\tilde{Y}_{i}^{\top}
since
\mathbb{E}[w_{i}^{2}]=1
. Applying the coupling of
26
—proved through the third-moment-matching randomized Lindeberg interpolation of
26
together with a Stein-kernel estimate—to
(w_{i}\tilde{Y}_{i})
given
X
, with Gaussian target
Z\sim N(0,\Sigma)
,
\Sigma=\mathbb{E}[S_{n}S_{n}^{\top}]
, yields a probability-one event
\Omega_{0}
on which, for every
\varepsilon
above the stated threshold, every Borel
A
and every
y
,

(35)

\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{j}(S_{n}^{w\tilde{Y}})_{j}-y_{j}\in A^{\varepsilon}\,\middle|\,X\right\}\leq\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{j}Z_{j}-y_{j}\in A^{6\varepsilon}\right\}+C\varepsilon^{-2}\!\left(\Delta^{*}_{n,0}\log p+\Delta^{*}_{n,1}\sqrt{\tfrac{(\log p)^{3}}{n}}\right),

where the
5\varepsilon
enlargement of
26
turns
A^{\varepsilon}
into
A^{6\varepsilon}
, and the conditional remainder quantities are

\Delta^{*}_{n,0}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\max_{1\leq j,k\leq p}\Big|\tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_{i}w_{i}^{2}\tilde{Y}_{ij}\tilde{Y}_{ik}-\Sigma_{jk}\Big|\,\middle|\,X\right],\qquad\Delta^{*}_{n,1}=\left(\tfrac{1}{n}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\max_{j}\textstyle\sum_{i}w_{i}^{4}\tilde{Y}_{ij}^{4}\,\middle|\,X\right]\right)^{1/2}.

Crucially,
\Delta^{*}_{n,0}
and
\Delta^{*}_{n,1}
depend on
X
but not on
(A,y)
, so (
35
) holds for all
(A,y)
simultaneously on
\Omega_{0}
with an
A
-independent error; this is what later permits taking a supremum over
t
inside the conditional probability.

Truncation error.

Since
|w_{i}|\leq b
and
\sqrt{n}(\bar{X}_{n}-\bar{\tilde{X}}_{n})=S_{n}^{X-\tilde{X}}
, the second term of (
34
) is bounded exactly as the truncation estimate underlying Lemma
B.1

[
26
, Eq. (25) and Sect. 6.1]
:

\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\|S_{n}^{w(Y-\tilde{Y})}\|_{\infty}>\varepsilon\,\middle|\,X\right\}\leq C\varepsilon^{-2}b^{2}\delta_{n,2}.

Combining the three displays gives (
33
) on
\Omega_{0}
with the nonnegative,
A
-independent remainder

\rho(X)=\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\|S_{n}^{w(Y-\tilde{Y})}\|_{\infty}>\varepsilon\,\middle|\,X\right\}+C\varepsilon^{-2}\!\left(\Delta^{*}_{n,0}\log p+\Delta^{*}_{n,1}\sqrt{\tfrac{(\log p)^{3}}{n}}\right).

The threshold
\varepsilon\geq C^{\prime}bK^{2}n^{-1/2}(\log n)(\log p)
corresponds to the requirement
\varepsilon\geq 12\,b\kappa_{n}(\log p)/\sqrt{n}
, ensuring the truncation level is compatible with the coupling.

Remainder in expectation.

Taking expectations over
X
bounds each conditional term. As
\mathbb{E}[w_{i}^{2}]=1
, the conditional mean of
n^{-1}\sum_{i}w_{i}^{2}\tilde{Y}_{ij}\tilde{Y}_{ik}
is the truncated sample covariance, whose deviation from
\Sigma
is controlled by the fourth-moment concentration of
26
and a maximal inequality for
\|\bar{X}_{n}\|_{\infty}
; the computation of
26
gives
\mathbb{E}[\Delta^{*}_{n,0}]\log p\lesssim b\sqrt{\delta_{n,1}}+b^{2}\delta_{n,2}
. For
\Delta^{*}_{n,1}
, the bound
\mathbb{E}[w_{i}^{4}]\leq b^{2}\mathbb{E}[w_{i}^{2}]=b^{2}
together with the Jensen and Lyapunov inequalities reduces it to the Gaussian fourth-moment term, giving
\mathbb{E}[\Delta^{*}_{n,1}]\sqrt{(\log p)^{3}/n}\lesssim b\sqrt{\delta_{n,1}}+b^{2}\delta_{n,2}
. Collecting the three contributions yields
\mathbb{E}[\rho(X)]\leq C^{\prime}\varepsilon^{-2}(b\sqrt{\delta_{n,1}}+b^{2}\delta_{n,2})
, which is (
32
).
∎

B.2.
Proof of Corollaries
7
–
12

The signed corollaries are proved by combining the one-sided couplings of Lemmas
B.1
–
B.2
with the density bounds of Section
2
; the unsigned corollaries are treated at the end. We write the argument for the Gaussian case; the bootstrap argument is identical after replacing Lemma
B.1
with Lemma
B.2
and tracking the additional factor of
b
.

Step 1: a two-sided Prokhorov bound.

Lemma
B.1
provides a one-sided bound; to derive a two-sided bound on a CDF difference, we apply it twice with
y=0
. First, taking
A=(-\infty,t]
in (
31
), we obtain

(36)

\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{j}(S_{n})_{j}\leq t\right\}-\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{j}Z_{j}\leq t\right\}\leq\mathbb{P}\!\left\{t<\max_{j}Z_{j}\leq t+6\varepsilon\right\}+R_{n}(\varepsilon),

where
R_{n}(\varepsilon):=C\varepsilon^{-2}\!\left(K^{2}(\log p)^{3/2}/\sqrt{n}+K^{4}(\log p)^{2}(\log n)^{2}/n\right)
. Second, taking
A=(t,\infty)
, we get

(37)

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{j}Z_{j}\leq t-6\varepsilon\right\}-\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{j}(S_{n})_{j}\leq t\right\}&\leq R_{n}(\varepsilon).\end{split}

Combining (
36
) and (
37
) yields

(38)

\left|\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{j}(S_{n})_{j}\leq t\right\}-\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{j}Z_{j}\leq t\right\}\right|\leq\mathbb{P}\!\left\{t-6\varepsilon<\max_{j}Z_{j}\leq t+6\varepsilon\right\}+R_{n}(\varepsilon).

Step 2: anti-concentration of
\max_{j}Z_{j}
.

The first term on the right-hand side of (
38
) is controlled by the density of
\max_{j}Z_{j}
on
[t-6\varepsilon,t+6\varepsilon]
. Throughout,
C
denotes a universal constant whose value may change between occurrences. We distinguish two cases.

Case I (Corollaries
7
and
9
,
t=t_{q}^{\Sigma}
for
q\geq\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}
).

By stochastic dominance,
t_{q}^{\Sigma}\geq q^{M}_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}\geq\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}\Phi^{-1}(\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}})\geq\nicefrac{{2\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}}}{{5}}
for every
q\geq\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}
. Hence, provided
\varepsilon\leq\nicefrac{{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}}}{{40}}
, the interval
[t-6\varepsilon,t+6\varepsilon]
lies above
\nicefrac{{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}}}{{4}}
, and Proposition
3
gives
f(s)\leq C\log p/\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}
there, so

\mathbb{P}\!\left\{t-6\varepsilon<\max_{j}Z_{j}\leq t+6\varepsilon\right\}\leq\frac{C\,\varepsilon\log p}{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}}.

Case II (Corollaries
8
and
10
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
arbitrary).

Splitting
[t-6\varepsilon,t+6\varepsilon]
at the fixed cut
a=\mu/2
, as in the proof of Corollary
4
but without tying the cut to the window width, the mass below
a
is at most
e^{-\mu^{2}/8\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}}
by (
8
), while above
a
the density is at most
C\log p/\mu
by (
3
). Hence

\mathbb{P}\!\left\{t-6\varepsilon<\max_{j}Z_{j}\leq t+6\varepsilon\right\}\leq e^{-\mu^{2}/8\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}}+\frac{C\,\varepsilon\log p}{\mu}.

The additive Gaussian-tail term does not shrink with the coupling scale
\varepsilon
; it is independent of
n
and depends only on the ratio
\mu/\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}
.

Step 3: optimization in
\varepsilon
.

Write
\nu=\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}
,
P=0
in Case I, and
\nu=\mu
,
P=e^{-\mu^{2}/8\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}}
in Case II. Combining (
38
) with Step 2, for every admissible
\varepsilon
,

(39)

\left|\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{j}(S_{n})_{j}\leq t\right\}-\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{j}Z_{j}\leq t\right\}\right|\leq P+C\Big(\frac{\varepsilon\log p}{\nu}+\varepsilon^{-2}\Delta_{1}+\varepsilon^{-2}\Delta_{2}\Big),

where
\Delta_{1}=K^{2}(\log p)^{3/2}/\sqrt{n}
and
\Delta_{2}=K^{4}(\log p)^{2}(\log n)^{2}/n
are the two terms of
R_{n}(\varepsilon)
. Balancing the first two non-penalty terms gives the optimal scale

\varepsilon^{*}\asymp\nu^{1/3}K^{2/3}(\log p)^{1/6}n^{-1/6},\qquad\frac{\varepsilon^{*}\log p}{\nu}+\varepsilon^{*-2}\Delta_{1}\asymp R:=\nu^{-2/3}\Big(\frac{K^{4}\log^{7}p}{n}\Big)^{1/6}.

Two observations complete the proof. First, the remaining term obeys

\varepsilon^{*-2}\Delta_{2}=R\cdot K^{2}(\log p)^{1/2}(\log n)^{2}n^{-1/2}\leq R

by Assumption
B
(with
b=1
). Second, the optimal
\varepsilon^{*}
may be inadmissible—either
\varepsilon^{*}>\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}/40
(violating the requirement of Case I), or
\varepsilon^{*}
below the lower threshold
\asymp\sqrt{\Delta_{2}}
of Lemma
B.1
—but in either case
R
exceeds a universal positive constant (in the first,
R\geq(\log p)/40
; in the second, Assumption
B
forces
R\gtrsim 1
), so the asserted bound holds trivially because the left-hand side is at most
1
. In all cases,

\left|\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{j}(S_{n})_{j}\leq t\right\}-\mathbb{P}\!\left\{\max_{j}Z_{j}\leq t\right\}\right|\lesssim P+R.

Taking the supremum over
t
—over
t\geq q^{M}_{\nicefrac{{2}}{{3}}}
in Case I (
P=0
), and over
t\in\mathbb{R}
in Case II—gives Corollaries
7
and
8
; in Case II the displayed rate dominates
P
precisely when
\mu\gtrsim\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}\sqrt{\log n}
.

For the bootstrap corollaries we argue from the coupling of Lemma
B.2
. On the probability-one event
\Omega_{0}
, the two-sided bound (
38
) holds for every
t
simultaneously with
R_{n}(\varepsilon)
replaced by the
A
-independent remainder
\rho(X)
. Since
\rho(X)
does not depend on
t
, we may take the supremum over
t
on
\Omega_{0}
inside the expectation, after which
\mathbb{E}[\rho(X)]
obeys (
32
). As the remainder carries an extra factor
b
in
\Delta_{1}
and
b^{2}
in
\Delta_{2}
, the same optimization yields
\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{-2/3}(b^{2}K^{4}\log^{7}p/n)^{1/6}
, and the analogue with
\mu
, giving Corollaries
9
and
10
.

Unsigned maxima (Corollaries
11
and
12
).

Apply the one-sided couplings to the reflected vectors
(X_{i},-X_{i})
(and
w_{i}(X_{i}-\bar{X}_{n})
likewise) in dimension
2p
, whose coordinate maxima are
\max_{j}|(S_{n})_{j}|
and
\max_{j}|(S_{n}^{\mathrm{WB}})_{j}|
with Gaussian analog
\max_{j}|Z_{j}|
; this only replaces
\log p
by
\log 2p
in the remainder. In Step 2, in place of the cut at
\mu/2
we invoke the unsigned window bound (

\varepsilon\lesssim\mu^{*}/\log p
and every

,

\mathbb{P}\Big\{t-6\varepsilon<\max_{j}|Z_{j}|\leq t+6\varepsilon\Big\}\;\lesssim\;\Big(\frac{\varepsilon\log p}{\mu^{*}}\Big)^{1/2},

with no additive penalty. Substituting into (

gives

\varepsilon^{*}\asymp\Big(\frac{\mu^{*}K^{4}\log^{2}p}{n}\Big)^{1/5},\qquad\Big(\frac{\varepsilon^{*}\log p}{\mu^{*}}\Big)^{1/2}+\varepsilon^{*-2}\Delta_{1}\asymp(\mu^{*})^{-2/5}\Big(\frac{K^{4}\log^{7}p}{n}\Big)^{1/10}=:R^{*}.

As before

under Assumption

—then

exactly as for Corollary
