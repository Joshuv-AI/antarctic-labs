L ∞ L^{\infty} -metrics on tori and Schoen’s conjecture Thanks: The second author is partially supported by NSFC 12501169.
Jian Wang, Jinmin Wang, Zhizhang Xie

Abstract
. We prove Schoen’s conjecture on L ∞ L^{\infty} -metrics for tori under an additional assumption on the fundamental group of the singular set. More precisely, we consider an L ∞ L^{\infty} -metric on a torus that is smooth and has non-negative scalar curvature away from a singular set of Minkowski dimension at most n − 3 + ( n − 1 ) − 1 n-3+(n-1)^{-1} . We show that if the induced homomorphism from the fundamental group of the singular set to the fundamental group of the torus is not surjective, then this metric extends to a smooth flat metric on the torus. Our proof uses weighted scalar curvature and the relative index theorem.



1.
Introduction

The study of scalar curvature on Riemannian manifolds has played a central role in differential geometry and geometric topology for several decades. A landmark result of Schoen–Yau
[
19
,
20
]
, using minimal hypersurface methods, and Gromov–Lawson
[
10
,
11
]
, using index theory, asserts that the torus does not admit any Riemannian metric of positive scalar curvature, solving the Geroch conjecture. In this paper, we investigate positive scalar curvature bounds for
L^{\infty}
-metrics on the torus, as formalized in the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
M
be a closed smooth manifold, and let
g_{0}
be a reference smooth Riemannian metric on
M
. We say that a measurable section
g
over
M
taking values in the bundle of positive-definite symmetric
2
-tensors is an
L^{\infty}
-metric if there exists a constant
\Lambda>0
such that
\Lambda^{-1}g_{0}\leq g\leq\Lambda g_{0}
holds almost everywhere.

For
L^{\infty}
-metrics that are smooth outside a singular subset of sufficiently high codimension, it is natural to study the implications of lower scalar curvature bounds on the regular locus. The first general results in this direction were obtained by Li–Mantoulidis
[
17
]
, who popularized the following conjecture due to Schoen.

Conjecture 1.2

(Schoen)
.

Suppose
g
is an
L^{\infty}
-metric on a closed manifold
M
that is smooth away from a closed embedded submanifold
S
of codimension at least
3
. If
\mathrm{Sc}(g)\geq 0
on
M\setminus S
and
M
has nonnegative Yamabe invariant, then
g
extends to a smooth flat metric on
M
.

As stated in this level of generality, the conjecture is false, as shown by counterexamples of Cecchini–Frenck–Zeidler
[
3
]
. Nevertheless, it remains a very interesting problem to determine under what additional assumptions the conjecture does hold, possibly subject to further topological constraints on the underlying manifold
M
. Indeed, Conjecture
1.2
is known to hold when
\dim(M)=3
, by work of Li–Mantoulidis
[
17
]
, and for tolerable singular sets
S
when
\dim(M)=4
, by Kazaras
[
16
]
. Llarull-type theorems for
L^{\infty}
-metrics have also been established for certain singular sets by Chu–Lee–Zhu
[
4
]
and Wang–Xie
[
22
]
. In the special case of conical metrics, scalar curvature rigidity has been verified by Dai–Sun–Wang
[
5
,
6
]
and Jovanovic–Wang
[
15
]
.

Given the central role of tori in the development of scalar curvature problems, it is particularly natural to investigate whether Schoen’s conjecture holds on tori. Wang–Xie
[
22
]
partially confirmed Conjecture
1.2
for
\mathbb{T}^{n}
by proving that if
\operatorname{codim}(S)\geq\frac{n}{2}+1
and the induced map

i_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\pi_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})

is trivial, then any
L^{\infty}
-metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus S
must be Ricci-flat. This result was subsequently strengthened by Dai–Wang–Wang–Wei
[
7
]
, who showed that if

\operatorname{codim}(S)\geq\max\left\{4,\frac{n}{2}+1\right\}
,
then the resulting Ricci-flat metric extends smoothly to a flat metric on the entire torus.

In this paper, we improve the results of
[
22
]
and
[
7
]
, confirming Conjecture
1.2
for the torus, under an additional assumption on the fundamental group of the singular set.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathbb{T}^{n}
be the
n
-torus. Assume that
S\subset\mathbb{T}^{n}
has Minkowski dimension at most
n-3+(n-1)^{-1}
. Assume that

i_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\pi_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\textup{ is not surjective.}

Then, any
L^{\infty}
-metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
that is smooth with non-negative scalar curvature on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
extends to a smooth flat metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

For clarity, the Minkowski dimension is defined as follows:

Definition 1.4
.

Let
M
be a smooth closed manifold and
S\subset M
. Let
N(S,r)
be the smallest number of balls of radius
r
needed to cover
S
. We say that
S
has Minkowski dimension at most
m
, if

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\log N(S,r)}{\log(1/r)}\leq m.

In fact, Theorem
1.3
is a direct consequence of the following stronger result, which distinguishes the dimensional constraints required for Ricci-flatness and flatness.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathbb{T}^{n}
be the
n
-torus and
S\subset\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Assume that

i_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\pi_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\textup{ is not surjective.}

Let
g
be an
L^{\infty}
-metric that is smooth on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
with non-negative scalar curvature.

(1)

If the Hausdorff dimension of
S
is
<n-3+2n^{-1}
, then
g
is Ricci-flat on the regular set
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
.

(2)

If furthermore the Minkowski dimension of
S
is
\leq n-3+(n-1)^{-1}
, then
g
extends to a smooth flat metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

As an immediate corollary, Schoen’s conjecture for
\mathbb{T}^{n}
holds whenever the subset
S
in Conjecture
1.2
is contained in a ball in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
\mathbb{T}^{n}
be the
n
-torus, and let
S
be a closed submanifold of codimension
\geq 3
in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Assume that
S
is contained in a ball of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.
Then any
L^{\infty}
-metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
with non-negative scalar curvature extends to a smooth flat metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

The assumptions in Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.5
are essential. For instance, there exists a smooth metric on the torus whose scalar curvature is positive outside a small disk. By removing this disk, one can view this metric as a singular metric on the torus with a point singularity, which violates the
L^{\infty}
assumption. The dimension assumption in Corollary
1.6
is also sharp: codimension-two singularities
may carry conical angle defects, and such cone-type singularities can contribute negative scalar curvature concentrated along the singular set.
The theorems show that any sharp Minkowski-codimension threshold for the validity of Schoen’s conjecture for torus must lie between codimensions two and
three. Determining the precise threshold remains an interesting open problem.

It also remains an open question whether the nonsurjectivity of

\pi_{1}(S)\to\pi_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})

is strictly necessary. This should be compared with Theorem
1.7
below, where the corresponding non-surjectivity condition is provably necessary.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
proceeds in two main stages.

First, we employ the conformal blow-up method introduced in
[
4
,
22
]
. Starting with an
L^{\infty}
-metric
g
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
, we construct a complete conformal metric
g_{\times}
on the regular set. In the previous work
[
22
]
, the conformal metric
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
has positive scalar curvature and is complete under a stronger codimension restriction, which leads to Ricci-flatness (see also
[
21
,
25
]
). To completely remove the codimension restrictions, we use a refined blow-up procedure to construct a complete metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
with strictly positive
(\mu,f)
-weighted scalar curvature:

\mathrm{Sc}_{\mu,f}(g_{\times})\coloneqq\mathrm{Sc}(g_{\times})+2\Delta_{g_{\times}}f-\mu|\nabla^{g_{\times}}f|^{2}

for a constant
\mu>\frac{n-1}{n}
and a smooth function
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S)
.

When
\mu=1
, this notion agrees with the
P
-scalar curvature introduced by Perelman (see also
[
9
]
). Deng
[
8
]
and Zhou–Zhu
[
28
]
established Llarull- and Listing-type theorems under
P
-scalar curvature lower bounds. In this paper, we extend these techniques from
\mu=1
to
\mu>\frac{n-1}{n}
, establishing the following result, which can be viewed as a generalization of the Geroch conjecture.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\mathbb{T}^{n}
be the
n
-torus and
S
a Lipschitz embedded simplicial complex such that

i_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\pi_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\textup{ is not surjective.}

Then
M\coloneqq\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
does not admit any complete metric with positive
(\mu,f)
-weighted scalar curvature for any
\mu>\frac{n-1}{n}
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

To prove Theorem
1.7
, we adapt the relative index theorem
[
26
]
and the constructions of
[
21
]
to define a non-local Callias-type operator on a cover of
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
, which has non-zero
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-equivariant index. We construct a modified Dirac operator whose Lichnerowicz formula witnesses the
weighted scalar curvature for
\mu>\frac{n-1}{n}
.

We emphasize that the fundamental group condition on
S
is necessary for Theorem
1.7
. As demonstrated in Proposition
2.6
, the
n
-torus with its
k
-skeleton removed admits a complete metric of uniformly positive scalar curvature whenever
n\geq 3
and
k\geq 2
. While the full resolution of Conjecture
1.2
for torus with arbitrary
S
remains open, the condition that
i_{*}(\pi_{1}(S))\neq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is likely optimal within the framework of this blow-up method.

Second, we show that any Ricci-flat
L^{\infty}
-metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
is identically flat and extends smoothly across
S
. Dai–Wang–Wang–Wei
[
7
]
established this for
\textup{codim}(S)\geq 4
by showing that, when
g
is Ricci-flat,
(\mathbb{T}^{n},g)
is an
RCD(0,n)
space. We resolve the remaining case by constructing
n
independent harmonic maps from
\mathbb{T}^{n}
to
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. We use De Giorgi–Nash–Moser estimates to derive a priori bounds for the gradients of these harmonic maps near the singular set. By applying the Bochner formula and the refined Kato inequality, we conclude that these harmonic maps have vanishing Hessians, thus providing a global smooth flat chart.

Remark 1.8
.

During the final stages of preparing this manuscript, Bi and Zhu posted a related result concerning
L^{\infty}
-metrics on the torus
[
27
, Remark 6.9]
. These two works were developed independently. Although both approaches make use of weighted scalar curvature, the underlying methodologies are entirely distinct. Specifically, their proof relies on minimal surface techniques, whereas ours employs relative index theory and Dirac operators. Furthermore, Theorem
1.7
is unique to our framework and does not follow directly from minimal surface methods.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we define the
(\mu,f)
-weighted scalar curvature and prove Theorem
1.7
. In Section
3
, we first apply this theorem to establish Ricci-flatness and prove Theorem
3.3
, and we use the harmonic map method to upgrade Ricci-flatness to global flatness in Section
3.4
.

(\mu,f)
-Weighted scalar curvature

In this section, we prove that a torus with a small set removed does not admit any complete metric with positive weighted scalar curvature, with an improved constant.

A_{1}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})}{\lambda^{\prime}(n+\lambda)+n+\lambda^{\prime}},\quad A_{2}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(n+\lambda)}{4(\lambda^{\prime}(n+\lambda)+n+\lambda^{\prime})}.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in H_{0}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
anti-commutes with Clifford multiplication, we have

(2.21)

\|B\sigma\|^{2}\geq\|D\sigma\|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\chi_{\rho}\sigma\|^{2}-\varepsilon L_{\rho}\|\chi_{d\rho}\sigma\|^{2},

where
L_{\rho}
is the Lipschitz constant of
\rho
with respect to
g
.

By the standard Lichnerowicz formula, we have

(2.22)

D^{2}=\nabla^{*}\nabla+\frac{\mathrm{Sc}}{4}.

Thus

(2.23)

\|D\sigma\|^{2}\geq\|\nabla\sigma\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{M}}\mathrm{Sc}(g)|\sigma|^{2}.

Recall that

(2.24)

\nabla^{f}_{X}=\nabla_{X}+\frac{1}{2}X(f).

Consequently,

\begin{split}\|\nabla^{f}\sigma\|^{2}=&\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{M}}|\nabla_{e_{i}}\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\langle e_{i},\nabla f\rangle\sigma|^{2}\\
=&\|\nabla\sigma\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{M}}|\nabla f|^{2}|\sigma|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{M}}\big(\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}\sigma,\langle e_{i},\nabla f\rangle\sigma\rangle+\langle\langle e_{i},\nabla f\rangle\sigma,\nabla_{e_{i}}\sigma\rangle\big)\\
=&\|\nabla\sigma\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{M}}|\nabla f|^{2}|\sigma|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{M}}\langle\nabla f,\nabla(|\sigma|^{2})\rangle\\
=&\|\nabla\sigma\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{M}}\Big(|\nabla f|^{2}-2\Delta f\Big)|\sigma|^{2}.\end{split}

Therefore,

(2.25)

\begin{split}\|D\sigma\|^{2}\geq&\|\nabla^{f}\sigma\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{M}}\Big(\mathrm{Sc}(g)+2\Delta f-|\nabla f|^{2}\Big)|\sigma|^{2}.\end{split}

Note that

(2.26)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}c(e_{i})\nabla^{f}_{e_{i}}=D+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}c(e_{i})\langle e_{i},\nabla f\rangle=D+\frac{1}{2}c(\nabla f).

Therefore, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have pointwise

(2.27)

\left(|D\sigma|^{2}+\lambda^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big|-c(e_{i})\nabla^{f}_{e_{i}}\sigma\big|^{2}\right)(1+\frac{n}{\lambda^{\prime}})\geq\frac{1}{4}|df|^{2}|\sigma|^{2}.

Hence,

(2.28)

\frac{1}{\lambda^{\prime}}|D\sigma|^{2}+|\nabla^{f}\sigma|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n+\lambda^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{1}{4}|df|^{2}|\sigma|^{2}.

It follows that

(2.29)

\frac{1}{\lambda^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{n+\lambda^{\prime}}{n+\lambda}|D\sigma|^{2}+\frac{n+\lambda^{\prime}}{n+\lambda}|\nabla^{f}\sigma|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n+\lambda}\cdot\frac{1}{4}|df|^{2}|\sigma|^{2}.

Consequently, we obtain

(2.30)

\begin{split}\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{n+\lambda^{\prime}}{n+\lambda}\right)\|D\sigma\|^{2}\geq&~\frac{\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}}{n+\lambda}\|\nabla^{f}\sigma\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{M}}\mathrm{Sc}_{\mu,f}(g)|\sigma|^{2}.\end{split}

This implies that

(2.31)

\begin{split}\|B\sigma\|^{2}\geq&\|D\sigma\|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\chi_{\rho}\sigma\|^{2}-\varepsilon L_{\rho}\|\chi_{1-\rho}\sigma\|^{2}\\
\geq&\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})}{\lambda^{\prime}(n+\lambda)+n+\lambda^{\prime}}\|\nabla^{f}\sigma\|^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(n+\lambda)}{4(\lambda^{\prime}(n+\lambda)+n+\lambda^{\prime})}\int_{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{M}}\mathrm{Sc}_{\mu,f}(g)\cdot|\sigma|^{2}\\
&+\varepsilon^{2}\|\chi_{\rho}\sigma\|^{2}-\varepsilon L_{\rho}\|\chi_{1-\rho}\sigma\|^{2}.\end{split}

This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Following the same arguments as in
[
21
,
24
]
, one can show that if the
(\mu,f)
-weighted scalar curvature of
(M,g)
is non-negative, then
g
is Ricci-flat and
f
is constant. We will revisit this argument in the proof of Theorem
3.3
.

The topological assumption

i_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\pi_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\textup{ is not surjective }

in Theorem
1.7
is necessary as shown in the following example.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{T}^{n}
be the
n
torus, and
\Sigma_{k}
the standard
k
-skeleton of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Then
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash\Sigma_{k}
admit a complete metric with uniform positive scalar curvature if
n\geq 3
and
k\geq 2
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{T}^{n}=(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{n}
, and we fix a base point
*\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Then the
k
-skeleton of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is given by

\Sigma_{k}=\bigcup_{\binom{n}{k}}(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{k}\times\{*\}^{n-k}.

Let
N(\Sigma_{k})
be a small open neighborhood of
\Sigma_{k}
, and define
W_{n-k}\coloneqq\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash N(\Sigma_{k})
as the resulting manifold with boundary. We claim that
W_{j}
, where
j=n-k-1
, admits a positive scalar curvature metric with a product structure near
\partial W_{j}
, provided
n\geq 3
and
k=n-j-1\geq 2
. We construct this metric by induction.

For the base case
j=0
,
W_{0}
is diffeomorphic to the
n
-ball
B^{n}
. It admits a positive scalar curvature metric with a product structure near the boundary because
\partial B^{n}=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
n-1\geq 2
.

Inductively,
W_{j}
is obtained from
W_{j-1}
by performing
\binom{n}{j}
surgeries. Each surgery attaches a
B^{n-j}\times B^{j}
to a
B^{n-j}\times\mathbb{S}^{j-1}\subset\partial W_{j-1}
. On the boundary, this process removes a
B^{n-j}\times\mathbb{S}^{j-1}
and attaches an
\mathbb{S}^{n-1-j}\times B^{j}
. By the Gromov–Lawson surgery construction, to ensure
W_{j}
carries a positive scalar curvature metric with a product structure near the boundary, it suffices to require
n-1-j=k\geq 2
.

Observe that
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash\Sigma_{k}
is diffeomorphic to
W_{n-k}\cup(\partial W_{n-k}\times[0,+\infty))
. Thus, extending the metric on
W_{n-k}
via the product structure on
\partial W_{n-k}
yields the desired complete metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash\Sigma_{k}
.
∎

L^{\infty}

In this section, we prove Theorem

3.1.
Laplace-Beltrami operators for
L^{\infty}
-metrics

Let
g
be an
L^{\infty}
-metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with respect to a fixed smooth metric
g_{0}
and let
S
denote its singular set, assumed to be have codimension at least
k
. Choose open neighborhoods
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
of
S
such that

S\subset\overline{U}_{1}\subset U_{2}.

Let
\rho
be a smooth cut-off function on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
satisfying that

(3.1)

0\leq\rho\leq 1,\quad\rho=1\text{ on }U_{1}\quad\overline{\mathrm{supp}(\rho)}\subset U_{2}.

Since
g
is an
L^{\infty}
-metric, the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}
of
(\mathbb{T}^{n},g)
is well-defined and coincides with the standard Sobolev space of
(\mathbb{T}^{n},g_{0})
. Consequently, the Laplace-Beltrami operator is well-defined with the domain

dom(\Delta)=\{f\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}):\exists C>0,~\forall\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),~\left|\int\langle\nabla f,\nabla\varphi\rangle\right|\leq C\cdot\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\}.

Moreover,
\Delta
is self-adjoint and has a discrete spectrum. Using Theorem 1.1 of
[
12
]
(also see
[
4
,
18
]
) obtains the Green function on
U_{2}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(U_{2},g|_{U_{2}})
and
S
be as above. Then there is a Green function

G:U_{2}\times U_{2}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}

associated to the Dirichlet problem on
(U_{2},g|_{U_{2}})
. Moreover, for any
x\in S
, the function
y\mapsto G(x,y)
is smooth, positive, and harmonic on
U_{2}\backslash S
and there is a constant
C>0
such that for for any
x\in S
and
y\in\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\setminus S

C^{-1}d_{g}(x,y)^{2-n}<G(x,y)<Cd_{g}(x,y)^{2-n}.

Motivated by the construction in
[
22
, Lemma 2.3]
, one may use such Green functions to blow up the singular set with prescribed rates. Combining this idea with the approach of
[
1
]
leads to a stronger blow-up result.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that the Hausdorff dimension of the singular set
S
is strictly smaller than
n-2-c^{-1}
, for
c>0
. Then there exists a smooth positive harmonic function
G_{S}
on
U_{2}\setminus S
. Moreover, for any
\varepsilon>0
, the conformal metric

g_{\times}\coloneqq(1+\varepsilon\rho G_{S})^{2c}g

is complete.

Proof.

Following the argument of
[
1
, Lemma 2.19]
, we first construct a finite measure
\nu_{S}
supported on
S
whose associated Wolff potential (see (
3.5
)) diverges on
S
.

Choose
s\in(0,n-2-c^{-1})
strictly larger than the Hausdorff dimension of
S
. Thus, for any integer
\ell\geq 1
, there exists a countable cover
\{B_{r_{\ell,j}}(x_{\ell,j})\}_{j}
of
S
with
x_{\ell,j}\in S
satisfying

(3.2)

r_{\ell,j}\leq 2^{-\ell}\qquad\textup{and}\qquad\sum_{j}r_{\ell,j}^{s}\leq 1.

Define the atomic measure on
S
by

(3.3)

\nu_{S}\coloneqq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j}r_{\ell,j}^{n-2-c^{-1}}\delta_{x_{\ell,j}},

where
\delta_{x_{\ell,j}}
is the Dirac measure at
x_{\ell,j}
. A direct computation yields that for any measurable set
X
,

(3.4)

\begin{split}\nu_{S}(X)&\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j}r_{\ell,j}^{n-2-c^{-1}-s}r_{\ell,j}^{s}\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j}2^{-\ell(n-2-c^{-1}-s)}r_{\ell,j}^{s}\\
&\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}2^{-\ell(n-2-c^{-1}-s)}<\infty.\end{split}

Next, define the Wolff potential

(3.5)

W(x)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\left(\nu_{S}(B_{r}(x))r^{2-n}\right)^{c}dr

Observe that for any
x\in S
and
\ell\geq 1
, there exists an index
j^{\prime}=j(\ell)
such that
x\in B_{r_{\ell,j^{\prime}}}(x_{\ell,j^{\prime}})
. Then, for any
r\in[r_{\ell,j^{\prime}},2r_{\ell,j^{\prime}}]
, the inclusion
x_{\ell,j^{\prime}}\in B_{r_{\ell,j^{\prime}}}(x)\subset B_{r}(x)
holds, which implies that

(3.6)

\nu_{S}(B_{r}(x))\geq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}r_{\ell,j^{\prime}}^{n-2-c^{-1}}\cdot\chi_{[r_{\ell,j^{\prime}},2r_{\ell,j^{\prime}}]}(r).

Consequently, for
x\in S
,

(3.7)

W(x)\geq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\int_{r_{\ell,j^{\prime}}}^{2r_{\ell,j^{\prime}}}\left(r_{\ell,j^{\prime}}^{n-2-c^{-1}}\cdot r^{2-n}\right)^{c}dr\geq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\int_{r_{\ell,j^{\prime}}}^{2r_{\ell,j^{\prime}}}r_{\ell,j^{\prime}}^{-1}dr=+\infty.

We now define

(3.8)

G_{S}(y)\coloneqq\int_{S}G(x,y)d\nu_{S}(x).

By the Harnack inequality,
G_{S}
is a smooth, positive harmonic function on
U_{2}\setminus S
.

Finally, let
\gamma:[0,s)\rightarrow U_{2}
be unit-speed curve with
\lim_{t\to s-}\gamma(s)\in S
. Arguing exactly as in
[
1
, Lemma 2.20]
, one obtains

(3.9)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{s}G_{S}(\gamma(t))^{c}dt&=\int_{0}^{s}\left(\int_{S}G(x,\gamma(t))d\nu_{S}(x)\right)^{c}dt\\
&\geq\int_{0}^{s}\left(\nu_{S}(B_{s-t}(\gamma(s)))\cdot C^{-1}(s-t)^{2-n}\right)^{c}dt\\
&=C^{-c}\big(W(\gamma(s))-c^{\prime}\big)=+\infty\end{split}

for some constant
c^{\prime}>0
. It follows that the conformal metric
g_{\times}
is complete,thereby completing the proof.
∎

3.2.
Scalar curvature extremality

In this subsection and the next, we establish the first part of Theorem
1.5
, restated here for clarity:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathbb{T}^{n}
be the
n
-torus and
S\subset\mathbb{T}^{n}

i_{*}\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\pi_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\textup{ is not surjective.}

Let

-metric that is smooth on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
with non-negative scalar curvature. If
S
has Hausdorff dimension strictly smaller than

is Ricci-flat on the regular set

As a first step, we prove in this subsection a preliminary, weaker version of Theorem
3.3
:

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume the same hypotheses as in Theorem
3.3
. In particular, the Hausdorff dimension of
S
is at most
n-k

Then there is no
L^{\infty}

Suppose
g
is an
L^{\infty}
-metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S

(3.10)

g_{\times}=w^{2c}g\quad w=1+\varepsilon\rho G_{S},

on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S

(3.11)

c>\frac{1}{k-2},

so that

is complete.

We define the weight function

(3.12)

f=b\log w.

Lemma 3.5
.

If

k>3-\frac{2}{n},

then there exist constants
b\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mu>\frac{n-1}{n}
, and
c>\frac{1}{k-2}
such that

\mathrm{Sc}_{\mu,f}(g_{\times})=w^{-2c}\mathrm{Sc}(g)+w^{-2c-1}\left(2(n-1)c+2b\right)\Delta w.

Furthermore, there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that

|\mathrm{Sc}_{\mu,f}(g_{\times})-w^{-2c}\mathrm{Sc}(g)|\leq C_{0}\varepsilon\chi_{d\rho},

where
\chi_{d\rho}
denotes the characteristic function of the support of
d\rho
, which is contained in
\overline{U_{2}}\backslash U_{1}

Proof.

Recall the definition of the weighted scalar curvature:

(3.13)

\mathrm{Sc}_{\mu,f}(g_{\times})=\mathrm{Sc}(g_{\times})+2\Delta_{g_{\times}}f-\mu|d_{g_{\times}}f|^{2}_{g_{\times}}.

Expressing these terms with respect to the original metric
g
, we have

\displaystyle w^{2c}\mathrm{Sc}(g_{\times})=

\displaystyle~\mathrm{Sc}(g)+2(n-1)c(-\Delta\log w)-(n-1)(n-2)c^{2}|d(\log w)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\mathrm{Sc}(g)+2(n-1)c\frac{-\Delta w}{w}+\big(2(n-1)c-(n-1)(n-2)c^{2}\big)|d(\log w)|^{2}.

\displaystyle w^{-2c}\Delta_{g_{\times}}f=

\displaystyle~\Delta f+(n-2)c\langle d(\log w),\nabla f\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~b\Delta(\log w)+(n-2)cb|d(\log w)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~b\frac{\Delta w}{w}+\big((n-2)cb-b\big)|d(\log w)|^{2}

\displaystyle w^{-2c}|d_{g_{\times}}f|_{g_{\times}}^{2}=

\displaystyle~b^{2}|d(\log w)|^{2}.

(3.14)

This equation for

(3.15)

\left((n-2)c-1\right)^{2}+\mu\big(2(n-1)c-(n-1)(n-2)c^{2}\big)\geq 0.

In the limiting case as

, inequality (

which implies that

Therefore line (

) admits a solution

and

if

for the completeness of

. Therefore, if these bounds are compatible, meaning

\frac{1}{k-2}<\frac{n}{n-2},

or equivalently,

k>2+\frac{n-2}{n}=3-\frac{2}{n},

then we can find some

and
\mu>\frac{n-1}{n}
such that

(3.16)

\mathrm{Sc}(g_{\times})+2\Delta_{g_{\times}}f-\mu|d_{g_{\times}}f|_{g_{\times}}^{2}=w^{-2c}\mathrm{Sc}(g)+w^{-2c-1}\left(2(n-1)c+2r\right)\Delta w

holds for some

. The remainder of the lemma follows directly from the fact that

(3.17)

w^{-2c-1}\Delta w=\begin{cases}0,&\textup{on }U_{1}\\
O(\varepsilon),&\textup{on }U_{2}\backslash U_{1}\\
0,&\textup{on }\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash U_{2}.\end{cases}

3.3.
From non-negative scalar curvature to Ricci-flat

Building on the previous subsection, we now move a step further to prove Theorem
3.3
.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Let
g
be an
L^{\infty}
-metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
with non-negative scalar curvature. Suppose that
g
is not Ricci-flat. Then there exists a point
p\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
and a small radius
\delta>0
such that

|\textup{Ric}_{g}|>\delta,~\textup{ on }B_{\delta}(p)\subset\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S,

where
B_{\delta}(p)
denotes the closed
\delta
-ball centered at
p
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
B_{\delta}(p)
is completely contained within the support of
(1-\rho)
.

We use the same notation as in Section
2
. Let
\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
be a local orthonormal basis near
p
. Then we may assume that

|\mathrm{Ric}_{g}(e_{1})|\geq\delta>0\textup{ on }B_{\delta}(p).

We apply the same argument in
[
21
, Section 4]
. Let
\psi
be a smooth cut-off function supported on
B_{\delta}(p)
with
\psi\equiv 1
on
B_{\delta/2}(p)
. Then for any smooth compactly supported spinor
\sigma
,

\sum_{j=1}^{n}c(e_{1})(\nabla_{e_{1}}\nabla_{e_{j}}-\nabla_{e_{j}}\nabla_{e_{1}}-\nabla_{[e_{1},e_{j}]})\sigma=-\frac{1}{2}c(\mathrm{Ric}_{g}(e_{1}))\cdot\sigma.

Hence,

\Big\langle\sum_{j=1}^{n}c(e_{1})(\nabla_{e_{1}}\nabla_{e_{j}}-\nabla_{e_{j}}\nabla_{e_{1}}-\nabla_{[e_{1},e_{j}]})\sigma,-\frac{1}{2}c(\mathrm{Ric}_{g}(e_{1}))\cdot\psi\sigma\Big\rangle=\frac{1}{4}\psi|\mathrm{Ric}_{g}(e_{1})|^{2}|\sigma|^{2}.

Integrating over
B_{\delta}(p)
and applying Stokes’ theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\delta^{2}\int_{B_{\delta/2}(p)}|\sigma|^{2}\leq\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}\frac{1}{4}\psi|\mathrm{Ric}_{g}(e_{1})|^{2}|\sigma|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}\big\langle\nabla_{e_{j}}\sigma,-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{e_{1}}\left(c(e_{1})c(\mathrm{Ric}_{g}(e_{1}))\psi\sigma\right)\big\rangle-\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}\big\langle\nabla_{e_{1}}\sigma,-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{e_{j}}\left(c(e_{1})c(\mathrm{Ric}_{g}(e_{1}))\psi\sigma\right)\big\rangle

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}\big\langle\nabla_{[e_{1},e_{j}]}\sigma,\frac{1}{2}c(\mathrm{Ric}_{g}(e_{1}))\psi\sigma\big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~C_{1}\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}\big(|\nabla\sigma|^{2}+2|\langle\nabla\sigma,\sigma\rangle|\big).

for some
C_{1}>0
depending only on
n
and
g
on
B_{\delta}(p)
.

By the Poincaré inequality
[
23
, Lemma 2.7]
, there exists
C_{2}>0
such that

\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}|\sigma|^{2}\leq C_{2}\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}|\nabla\sigma|^{2}+C_{2}\int_{B_{\delta/2}(p)}|\sigma|^{2}.

Thus for any
\alpha>0
, we have

(3.18)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{4}\delta^{2}\cdot\int_{B_{\delta/2}(p)}|\sigma|^{2}\leq~C_{1}\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}\left(|\nabla\sigma|^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}|\nabla\sigma|^{2}+\alpha|\sigma|^{2}\right)\\
\leq&\left(C_{1}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}|\nabla\sigma|^{2}+\alpha C_{1}C_{2}\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}|\nabla\sigma|^{2}+\alpha C_{1}C_{2}\int_{B_{\delta/2}(p)}|\sigma|^{2}\end{split}

Let us pick
\alpha=\delta^{2}/(8C_{1}C_{2})
and set

C_{3}=\frac{4}{\delta^{2}}\left(C_{1}+\frac{1}{\alpha}+\alpha C_{1}C_{2}\right).

We then obtain that

(3.19)

\int_{B_{\delta/2}(p)}|\sigma|^{2}\leq C_{3}\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}|\nabla\sigma|^{2}.

Now we consider the metric
g_{\times}
on
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{M}
as in as in Section
2
. Note that on the compact set
B_{\delta}(p)
, the metrics satisfy
g_{\times}\to g
uniformly in the
C^{\infty}
-topology as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Consequently, the constant
C_{3}
above can be chosen independently of
\varepsilon
. Therefore, we obtain that for some
c_{1}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
,

(3.20)

\int_{B_{\delta}(\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{p})}|\nabla\sigma|^{2}\geq c_{1}\int_{B_{\delta/2}(\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{p})}|\sigma|^{2}

for any compactly supported spinor over
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{M}
,
where
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{p}
denotes the collection of lifts of
p
to the cover
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{M}
.

Similarly, we have the Poincaré inequality
[
23
, Lemma 2.7]

(3.21)

\|\chi_{1-\rho}\sigma\|^{2}\leq c_{2}\|\chi_{1-\rho}\nabla\sigma\|^{2}+c_{3}\int_{B_{\delta/2}(\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{p})}|\sigma|^{2}

for some constants
c_{2},c_{3}>0
that are also independent of
\varepsilon
. Thus, we obtain

(3.22)

\|\chi_{1-\rho}\sigma\|^{2}\leq\left(c_{2}+\frac{c_{3}}{c_{1}}\right)\|\chi_{1-\rho}\nabla\sigma\|^{2}.

Again, on the support of
(1-\rho)
,
f\to 1
uniformly in the
C^{\infty}
-norm as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Therefore, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, we have

(3.23)

\|\chi_{1-\rho}\sigma\|^{2}\leq 2\left(c_{2}+\frac{c_{3}}{c_{1}}\right)\|\chi_{1-\rho}\nabla^{f}\sigma\|^{2}.

We also note that
\rho
is uniformly
2L_{\rho}
-Lipschitz with respect to
g_{\times}
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, as
d\rho
is compactly supported. Therefore, applying Lemma
2.4
yields

\displaystyle\|B\sigma\|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle A_{1}\|\nabla^{f}\sigma\|^{2}+A_{2}\int_{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{M}}\mathrm{Sc}_{\mu,f}(g_{\times})\cdot|\sigma|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\chi_{\rho}\sigma\|^{2}-2\varepsilon L_{\rho}\|\chi_{1-\rho}\sigma\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle A_{2}\int_{\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{M}}w^{-2c}\mathrm{Sc}(g)\cdot|\sigma|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\chi_{\rho}\sigma\|^{2}+\left(\frac{A_{1}}{2c_{2}+\frac{2c_{3}}{c_{1}}}-A_{2}C_{0}\varepsilon-2\varepsilon L_{\rho}\right)\|\chi_{1-\rho}\sigma\|^{2}.

By choosing
\varepsilon
small enough, the strictly positive dominant terms guarantee that
B
is uniformly bounded from below, making it invertible. However, this directly contradicts Lemma
2.3
, completing the proof.
∎

3.4.
From Ricci-flat to flat

In this subsection, we finish the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Since the metric
g
is an
L^{\infty}
-metric, the
W^{1,2}
-norms defined with respect to
g
and the smooth background metric
g_{0}
are equivalent. We view the
n
-torus as the quotient
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and equip
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the standard coordinate basis
\{e_{\ell}\}_{1\leq\ell\leq n}
. We first construct
n
harmonic functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

There exist
n
functions
\{u_{\ell}\}_{1\leq\ell\leq n}
such that

(1)

u_{\ell}\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(2)

u_{\ell}(p+e_{j})=u_{\ell}(p)+\delta_{\ell j}
, and

(3)

for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0})
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\nabla u_{\ell},\nabla\varphi\rangle=0

with respect to the metric
g
.

Proof.

Let
x_{\ell}
be the coordinate function in the
\ell
-th direction. In particular,
x_{\ell}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and satisfies

(3.24)

x_{\ell}(p+e_{j})=x_{\ell}(p)+\delta_{\ell j}

for any
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Therefore, the gradient
\nabla x_{\ell}
is invariant under the
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-action, descends to
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, and lies in
L^{2}(T(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
. Consider the space

W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})_{*}=\left\{f\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}):\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}f=0\right\}.

Then there exists a constant
\lambda_{1}>0
such that

\|\nabla f\|^{2}\leq\|f\|_{W^{1,2}}^{2}=\|f\|^{2}+\|\nabla f\|^{2}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\right)\|\nabla f\|^{2}.

Thus, the map

\nabla\colon W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})_{*}\to L^{2}(T(\mathbb{T}^{n})),~f\mapsto\nabla f

is injective and has closed range. Let
P
denote the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(T(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
onto the image
\nabla(W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})_{*})
. We set

(3.25)

f_{\ell}=\nabla^{-1}(P(\nabla x_{\ell})),

and define

(3.26)

u_{\ell}=x_{\ell}-\widetilde{f}_{\ell},

where
\widetilde{f}_{\ell}
is the lift of
f_{\ell}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Clearly,
u_{\ell}
satisfies conditions (1) and (2). For item (3), by construction, the projection ensures that
(\nabla x_{\ell}-\nabla f_{\ell})\perp\nabla(W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})_{*})
. For any
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n},g_{0})
, the function
\psi-\langle\psi,1\rangle\cdot 1
belongs to
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})_{*}
. Thus,

0=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\langle\nabla x_{\ell}-\nabla f_{\ell},\nabla(\psi-\langle\psi,1\rangle\cdot 1)\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\langle\nabla u_{\ell},\nabla\psi\rangle.

The general case follows by decomposing any test function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0})
into a finite sum of lifts of functions from
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

The functions
\{u_{\ell}\}_{1\leq\ell\leq n}
constructed in Lemma
3.6
satisfy:

(1)

u_{\ell}
is smooth away from
\widetilde{S}
,

(2)

u_{\ell}\in C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and

(3)

|\nabla u_{\ell}|\leq Cr^{-1+\alpha}
, where
r=\mathrm{dist}(~\cdot~,\widetilde{S})
.

Proof.

Recall that
g_{0}
is a smooth metric on
T^{n}
. By abuse of the notation, we also denote by
g_{0}
its lift to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The operator
\Delta_{g}
may be written,
with respect to
g_{0}
, as a uniformly elliptic divergence-form operator with bounded measurable coefficients.
Standard elliptic regularity for divergence-form equations, together with
[
13
, Theorem 8.9]
, implies that there exist constant
C
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that, for every geodesic ball
B(x,r)
with
B(x,2r)
contained in the coordinate domain,

(3.27)

\operatorname{osc}_{B(x,r)}u_{\ell}\leq Cr^{\alpha}\sup_{B(x,2r)}|u_{\ell}|.

In particular, each
u_{\ell}
is locally Hölder continuous across the singular set
\widetilde{S}
. From the periodic property of
u_{\ell}
, we have that
u_{\ell}\in C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, since the coefficients of
\Delta_{g}
are smooth away from
\widetilde{S}
, standard elliptic bootstrapping shows that
u_{\ell}
is smooth away
\widetilde{S}
.

We now prove the desired gradient estimate. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\widetilde{S}
be any point and
r_{x}=\text{dist}(x,\widetilde{S})
. Hence,
B(x,\frac{1}{2}r_{x})
is smooth and contained in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\widetilde{S}
. Consider the positive harmonic function on
B(x,\frac{1}{2}r_{x})

(3.28)

u_{\ell}-\inf_{B(x,\frac{1}{2}r_{x})}u_{\ell}+\epsilon

for any positive constant
\epsilon>0
. Since the Ricci curvature vanishes on this ball, we apply Cheng-Yau’s gradient estimate to obtain that

(3.29)

\begin{split}|\nabla u_{\ell}|(x)&\leq Cr_{x}^{-1}\sup_{B(x,\frac{1}{2}r_{x})}|u_{\ell}-\inf_{B(x,\frac{1}{2}r_{x})}u_{\ell}+\epsilon|\\
&\leq Cr_{x}^{-1}(\operatorname{osc}_{B(x,\frac{1}{2}r_{x})}u_{\ell}+\epsilon)\\
&\leq Cr^{-1}_{x}(r^{\alpha}_{x}\sup_{B(x,r_{x})}|u_{\ell}|+\epsilon)\end{split}

where we have used (
3.27
) in the last inequality and
C
only relies on the dimension
n
.
Taking
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
and using the periodic property of
u_{\ell}
produces that

(3.30)

|\nabla u_{\ell}|(x)\leq Cr^{\alpha-1}.

∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Assume that
S\subset\mathbb{T}^{n}
has Minkowski dimension at most
n-k
. Let
\varepsilon_{1}>0
be any small positive number. Then for any small
\delta>0
, there exists a cut-off function
\chi_{\delta}
such that

0\leq\chi_{\delta}\leq 1,\qquad\chi_{\delta}=1\quad\text{on }N_{\delta}(S),\qquad\mathrm{supp}\,\chi_{\delta}\subset N_{2\delta}(S),

and

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\nabla\chi_{\delta}|^{2}dV_{g}\leq C_{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdot\delta^{k-2-\varepsilon_{1}}.

Proof.

Let
g_{0}
be the background metric. Since the Minkowski dimension of
S

\leq n-k
, for every small
\varepsilon_{1}>0
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{\prime}
such that, for all
sufficiently small
\delta>0
,

(3.31)

\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{0}}\bigl(N_{\delta}(S)\bigr)\leq{C_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{\prime}}\delta^{k-\varepsilon_{1}}.

Here
N_{\delta}({S})
denotes the
\delta
-neighborhood of
{S}
with respect to
g_{0}
.

Choose smooth cutoff functions
\chi_{\delta}
satisfying

(3.32)

0\leq\chi_{\delta}\leq 1,\qquad\chi_{\delta}=1\quad\text{on }N_{\delta}({S}),\qquad\mathrm{supp}\,\chi_{\delta}\subset N_{2\delta}({S}),

and

(3.33)

|\nabla\chi_{\delta}|_{g_{0}}\leq\frac{2}{\delta}.

Since
g
and
g_{0}
are uniformly equivalent, there is a constant
C
, independent of
\delta,\varepsilon_{1}
, such that

(3.34)

|\nabla\chi_{\delta}|_{g}^{2}\,dV_{g}\leq C|\nabla\chi_{\delta}|_{g_{0}}^{2}\,dV_{g_{0}}.

Therefore

(3.35)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\nabla\chi_{\delta}|_{g}^{2}\,dV_{g}\leq\frac{4C}{r^{2}}\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{0}}\bigl(N_{2\delta}({S})\bigr)\leq C_{\varepsilon_{1}}\delta^{k-2-\varepsilon_{1}}.

∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Assume that
\textup{Ric}_{g}\geq 0
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
, and that
S
has Minkowski dimension at most
n-3+(n-1)^{-1}
.
Then the functions
u_{\ell}
above have vanishing Hessians and are therefore Lipschitz. Furthermore, there exists a smooth flat metric
\overline{g}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the induced map

\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{U}=(\bar{u}_{1},\ldots,\bar{u}_{n})\colon\mathbb{T}^{n}\to(\mathbb{T}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n},\overline{g})

is a homeomorphism and satisfies
g={\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{U}}^{*}\overline{g}
.

Proof.

For simplicity of notation, we omit the subscript
\ell
. On
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\widetilde{S}
, using the metric
g
, we have the Bochner formula:

(3.36)

\frac{1}{2}\Delta(|\nabla u|^{2})=\langle\nabla(\Delta u),\nabla u\rangle+|\textup{Hess}(u)|^{2}+\textup{Ric}_{g}(\nabla u,\nabla u)\geq|\textup{Hess}(u)|^{2}.

Since
\nabla u
descends to
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, inequality (
3.36
) also holds on
(\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S,g)
. We define

v=(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p}

for some exponent
p\in(0,1)
. Taking the gradient yields

\nabla v=p(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}\nabla(|\nabla u|^{2})=2p(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}|\nabla u|\cdot\nabla(|\nabla u|).

Computing the Laplacian, we find

\displaystyle\Delta v=

\displaystyle~p(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}\Delta(|\nabla u|^{2})+p(p-1)(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-2}|\nabla(|\nabla u|^{2})|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~p(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}\Delta(|\nabla u|^{2})+4p(p-1)(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-2}|\nabla u|^{2}\cdot\big|\nabla|\nabla u|\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~2p(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\Delta(|\nabla u|^{2})+2(p-1)\big|\nabla|\nabla u|\big|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~2p(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}\left(|\textup{Hess}(u)|^{2}+2(p-1)\big|\nabla|\nabla u|\big|^{2}\right).

By the refined Kato inequality, we have

(3.37)

|\textup{Hess}(u)|^{2}\geq\frac{n}{n-1}\big|\nabla|\nabla u|\big|^{2}.

Thus

(3.38)

\Delta v\geq 2p\left(1+2(p-1)\frac{n-1}{n}\right)(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}|\textup{Hess}(u)|^{2}.

We require that the coefficient remains positive:

(3.39)

1+2(p-1)\frac{n-1}{n}>0,\quad\textup{namely}\quad 2p>\frac{n-2}{n-1}.

Let
\chi_{\delta}
be the cut-off function as in Lemma
3.8
, and
\psi_{\delta}\coloneqq 1-\chi_{\delta}
. Therefore

(3.40)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\nabla\psi_{\delta}|^{2}\lesssim\delta^{k-2-\varepsilon_{1}}.

Because
\psi_{\delta}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and vanishes near
S
, integration by parts yields

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\psi_{\delta}^{2}\Delta v=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\langle\nabla(\psi_{\delta}^{2}),\nabla v\rangle\geq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\psi_{\delta}^{2}\cdot 2p\left(1+2(p-1)\frac{n-1}{n}\right)(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}|\textup{Hess}(u)|^{2}.

Rearranging and applying the chain rule, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\psi_{\delta}^{2}\cdot 2p\left(1+2(p-1)\frac{n-1}{n}\right)(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}|\textup{Hess}(u)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\nabla(\psi_{\delta}^{2})|\cdot|\nabla v|=4p\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}\cdot\psi_{\delta}\big|\nabla|\nabla u|\big|\cdot|\nabla u|\cdot|\nabla\psi_{\delta}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4p\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}\psi_{\delta}^{2}|\textup{Hess}(u)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}|\nabla u|^{2}\cdot|\nabla\psi_{\delta}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the last inequality follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the Kato inequality. Set

\alpha=1+2(p-1)\frac{n-1}{n}.

We obtain that

\displaystyle~\alpha\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\psi_{\delta}^{2}\cdot(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}|\textup{Hess}(u)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\psi_{\delta}^{2}\cdot(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}|\textup{Hess}(u)|^{2}+\frac{2}{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}|\nabla u|^{2}\cdot|\nabla\psi_{\delta}|^{2},

Canceling the common factor, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\psi_{\delta}^{2}\cdot(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}|\textup{Hess}(u)|^{2}\leq\frac{4}{\alpha^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p}\cdot|\nabla\psi_{\delta}|^{2}.

Applying Lemma
3.7
and Lemma
3.8
, we can bound the right-hand side by

(3.41)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p}|\nabla\psi_{\delta}|^{2}\lesssim\delta^{(-2+2\alpha)p}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|\nabla\psi_{\delta}|^{2}\lesssim\delta^{(-2+2\alpha)p+k-2-\varepsilon_{1}}.

We require that the exponent of
\delta
is strictly positive for some small
\varepsilon_{1}>0
, meaning

(3.42)

k-2-2p+2\alpha p>0,\quad\textup{or equivalently,}\quad 2p<\frac{k-2}{1-\alpha}.

A valid exponent
p
satisfying both (
3.39
) and (
3.42
) exists if and only if

(3.43)

\frac{n-2}{n-1}<\frac{k-2}{1-\alpha}.

This inequality holds provided that

(3.44)

k\geq 2+\frac{n-2}{n-1}=3-\frac{1}{n-1}.

Assuming this dimensional constraint, we can fix a choice of
\varepsilon_{1}>0
and take the limit as
\delta\to 0
to conclude that

(3.45)

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\psi_{\delta}^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+1)^{p-1}|\textup{Hess}(u)|^{2}=0.

Consequently,
\textup{Hess}(u)\equiv 0
. It immediately follows that
\nabla(\nabla u)=0
identically on
(\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S,g)
.

Now, let

(3.46)

G=\left(\langle\nabla u_{\ell},\nabla u_{\ell^{\prime}}\rangle\right)_{1\leq\ell,\ell^{\prime}\leq n}

be the Gram matrix associated with the parallel vector fields
\{\nabla u_{\ell}\}_{1\leq\ell\leq n}
. Because the fields are parallel,
G
is a constant positive semi-definite matrix on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
. If
\det(G)=0
, then there exist constants
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
, not all zero, such that

(3.47)

\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}a_{\ell}\nabla u_{\ell}=0.

This relation lifts to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, implying that

(3.48)

\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}a_{\ell}u_{\ell}=C

for some global constant
C
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. However, evaluating this sum translated by any basis vector
e_{j}
yields

(3.49)

C=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}a_{\ell}u_{\ell}(p+e_{j})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}a_{\ell}(u_{\ell}(p)+\delta_{\ell j})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}a_{\ell}u_{\ell}(p)+a_{j}=C+a_{j}.

This implies that all
a_{j}=0
, which contradicts our linear dependence assumption. Hence,
G
is positive definite (
\det(G)>0
). We can therefore define a flat metric
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{g}=G
, and the functions
\{u_{\ell}\}_{1\leq\ell\leq n}
descend to define a local isometry

\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{U}=(\bar{u}_{1},\ldots,\bar{u}_{n})\colon\mathbb{T}^{n}\to(\mathbb{T}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n},\overline{g}).

Recall from Lemma
3.6
that
u_{\ell}=x_{\ell}-\widetilde{f}_{\ell}
, where
\widetilde{f}_{\ell}
is a periodic function. Thus,
u_{\ell}
is linearly homotopic to the standard coordinate function
x_{\ell}
. As a result,
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{U}
is an orientation-preserving covering map of degree one, making it a homeomorphism.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Suppose
g
is an
L^{\infty}
-metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
with non-negative scalar curvature. By Theorem
3.3
,
g
must be Ricci-flat on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\backslash S
, provided that the Hausdorff dimension of
S
is strictly smaller than
n-3+2n^{-1}
. If futhermore the Minkowski dimension of
S
is at most
n-3+(n-1)^{-1}
, Proposition
3.9
implies that
g
is identically flat, and the map
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{U}
defines a smooth structure on the entire torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.
∎
