Spectral stability in the modified Camassa-Holm equation
Lili Fan, Hongjun Gao, Ji Li*

Abstract
. We investigate the spectral stability of small-amplitude, periodic, traveling-wave solutions of the modified Camassa-Holm equation with cubic nonlinearities. More precisely, we analyze the L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}) -spectrum of the associated linearized operator in a neighborhood of the origin in the spectral plane. Inspired by a recently novel method based on Kato’s perturbation theory [Berti et al, Full description of Benjamin-Feir instability of Stokes waves in deep water, Invent. Math. , 230 (2022), 651-711.], we provide a complete description of the spectrum near the origin of the linearized operator–an integro-differential operator with periodic coefficients–and thus prove that such waves are not subject to modulational instability. Moreover, a spectral analysis reveals a remarkable threshold phenomenon: such waves with wave number k 2 ≤ 3 k^{2}\leq 3 exhibit spectral stability, while instability emerges when k 2 > 3 k^{2}>3 .



Keywords
: Modified Camassa-Holm equation, periodic traveling waves, spectral stability

(2010): 35B35, 35C07, 37K45.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the spectral stability of periodic traveling waves of the following
modified Camassa–Holm (mCH) equation

\left(1-\partial_{x}^{2}\right)u_{t}+\left(u^{3}-u^{2}u_{xx}-uu^{2}_{x}+u^{2}_{x}u_{xx}\right)_{x}=0.

(1.1)

This equation has been proposed in the modelling of nonlinear water waves by Fokas
[
15
]
, and recently been shown to govern weakly nonlinear shallow water waves of moderate amplitude propagating over a flat bottom
[
12
]
, where the function
u(t,x)
is related to the horizontal velocity in certain level of fluid. Structurally, the mCH equation (
1.1
) is formally integrable in the sense that it admits a bi-Hamiltonian structure
[
31
]
and was later shown to admit a Lax formulation
[
32
]
. Moreover, it is worth mentioning that the scaling limit case of equation (
1.1
), when combined with the first-order term
\gamma u_{x}
, is the
short-pulse
equation for
v=u_{x}

v_{t}=\frac{1}{3}\left(v^{3}\right)_{xx}+\gamma v,

which is a model for the propagation of ultra-short light pulses in silica optical fibers
[
34
]
.

Traveling wave solutions are fundamental for understanding the dynamics of nonlinear PDEs. These solutions not only reveal intrinsic pattern formation mechanisms but also provide bridges between theoretical models and observed phenomena in fields such as hydrodynamic turbulence, nonlinear optics, and quantum field theory. Among classical traveling wave types, solitary waves and periodic traveling waves have received particular attention. Research on the mCH equation, especially concerning the stability of its solitary wave solutions, has developed into such an extensive research domain that a complete survey lies beyond the scope of this work. For foundational aspects of this field, we refer readers to
[
24
,
33
]
, with recent advances documented in
[
11
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
. Notably, while stability of solitary waves in the mCH equation has been extensively studied, to our knowledge, the stability of periodic traveling waves has not been studied in the literature.

In this paper, we aim at understanding how a periodic traveling wave behaves under smooth localized perturbations. Specifically, we will study the existence of small-amplitude periodic traveling waves of the mCH equation (
1.1
), as well as determine the modulational stability/instability of the said waves. Here, modulational instability, also known as Benjamin-Feir instability, refers to spectral instability of the underlying wave to perturbations in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
in a sufficiently small ball about the origin in the spectral plane (see Definition
3.1
, Section
3
). The investigation of this problem dates back to the pioneering work of Benjamin and Feir
[
2
,
3
]
in the 1960s, who discovered that small-amplitude spatially periodic Stokes waves exhibit instability under long-wavelength perturbations. Subsequent three decades saw this phenomenon corroborated by both experimental and numerical evidence. While rigorous mathematical proof emerged later: Bridges and Mielke
[
8
]
first established modulational instability for finite-depth waves (1995), and Nguyen and Strauss
[
29
]
resolved the infinite-depth case (2020). Most recently, Berti et al.
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
fully described the figure-8 structure of unstable eigenvalues, covering deep, finite, and critical water depths.

Beyond the classical Stokes wave, modulational instability has been widely observed in various other nonlinear waves, including: quasiperiodic solutions of the nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation
[
35
]
, the periodic standing waves of the NLS equation
[
10
]
, the periodic standing wave solutions of the generalized Korteweg–de Vries (KdV) equation
[
9
]
, and the wave trains of the Camassa-Holm equation
[
21
]
. For the small-amplitude periodic traveling waves under consideration, significant results exist for a variety of dispersive models including the fractional KdV
[
20
]
, the Whitham equation
[
17
]
, the general Benjamin-Bona-Mahony
[
18
,
16
]
, the full-dispersion Camassa-Holm equations
[
19
]
, the generalized KdV equation
[
1
]
, the Ostrovsky equation
[
7
]
, the Novikov equation
[
13
]
, and the
b
-family equation
[
14
]
. Particularly notable is recent work on generalized KdV equations
[
28
]
, revealing figure-8 configuration of unstable eigenvalues near the origin in the spectral plane. This paper aims at extending spectral stability/instability analysis in
[
4
,
28
]
to the mCH model (
1.1
), addressing its nonlocal and strongly nonlinear nature.

1.1.
Main results

Although the basic idea of the approach stems from the significant works
[
4
,
28
]
, the nonlocal dispersion and strong nonlinearity inherent to the mCH equation (
1.1
) makes the spectral computation a lot more involved. On the one hand, the presence of an integro-differential operator requires the development of a new symplectic basis, distinct from those employed in
[
4
,
28
]
. On the other hand, the complexity of the linearized operator leads to computational difficulties in determining the entries of the matrix
\mathrm{A}_{\xi,a}
, where we use Mathematica to carry out the arduous calculations. Despite these obstacles, we establish the following main result concerning spectral stability of small-amplitude periodic traveling waves in the mCH equation.

Theorem 1.1

(Modulational Stability vs. Spectral Stability/Instability)
.

A sufficiently small
2\pi/k
-periodic traveling wave solution of the modified Camassa-Holm equation (
1.1
), as constructed in Lemma
2.1
, is (i) modulationally stable for any
k>0
; (ii) spectrally stable if
k^{2}\leq 3
; (iii) spectrally unstable if
k^{2}>3
.

The above theorem demonstrates that the mCH equation does not exhibit the modulational instability for small-amplitude periodic traveling waves. This result deviates from the typical scenario in which a critical wave number threshold
k^{*}>0
exists such that periodic traveling waves of period
2\pi/k
are modulationally stable when
k\in(0,k^{*})
and modulationally unstable when
k>k^{*}
. However, this threshold behavior is not universal. Contrary examples cover from the shallow water equations of the KdV equation
[
20
]
, the Camassa-Holm equation and Degasperis-Procesi
[
14
]
, to the fluid system of electronic Euler-Poisson
[
30
]
, where all small-amplitude periodic traveling waves were proven to be modulationally stable. These findings suggest that modulational instability arises either higher-order dispersive or strong nonlinear effects are considered. This also implies that modulational instability analyses must a priori consider the potential absence of a critical wave number threshold. Moreover, our findings align well with the spectral stability observed in the context of mCH solitary waves (see
[
24
]
for details) for
k\rightarrow 0
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we construct the existence of a family of the one-dimensional small-amplitude periodic traveling waves of the mCH equation by employing implicit function theorem and Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction, and give a parameterization of these waves. In Section
3
, we formulate the spectral stability problem based on the linearization of (
1.1
) about the obtained periodic traveling wave, and present the complete spectral Theorem
3.1
. In Section
4
, we focus on the introduction of Kato’s perturbed basis and the reduction to the study of the eigenvalues of a
3\times 3
matrix
\mathrm{A}_{\xi,a}
. In Section
5
, we compute the matrix
\mathrm{A}_{\xi,a}
for
|\xi|,|a|
sufficiently small. In Section
6
, we carry out a non-perturbative block-diagonalization that conjugates the matrix
\mathrm{A}_{\xi,a}
to a new matrix with two decoupled blocks: a purely imaginary number, and a

block, whose eigenvalues can be computed explicitly. Finally, in Section
7
, we give the spectral instability analysis far from origin.

3.1.
Spectrum of

The spectral analysis for the operators

(3.9b)

Then the constant solutions of (
1.1
) is spectrally stable to square integrable perturbations. Additionally, for sufficiently small
|a|
, Lemma
3.2
implies that multiple eigenvalues
colliding on imaginary axis may bifurcate to leave the imaginary axis and induce instabilities though
simple eigenvalues remain purely imaginary for small
|a|
. Therefore, it is now important to determine the more precise locations of these eigenvalues
i\omega_{n,\xi}
, with particular emphasis on their multiplicities.

Given the Hamiltonian nature of
\mathcal{A}_{\xi,a}
, we utilize the Krein signature in standard linear Hamiltonian theory to detect the possible collisions (a detailed discussion appears in
[
22
, Proposition 7.1.14]
). Specifically, the Krein signature
K_{n}
of an eigenvalue

of
\mathcal{A}_{\xi,0}
is defined as

K_{n}:=\operatorname{sgn}\left(\left(\mathcal{L}_{\xi,0}\mathrm{e}^{inz},\mathrm{e}^{inz}\right)\right)=\operatorname{sgn}\left((n+\xi)^{2}-1\right).

(3.10)

A necessary condition for a pair of eigenvalues to leave imaginary axis after collision is that they carry opposite Krein signatures. In the case
\xi=0
, it is obvious that the Krein signatures of all eigenvalues maintain positive with the sole exception of the zero eigenvalue at
n=\pm 1
, where the Krein signature calculation becomes inconclusive. For
\xi\neq 0
, a straightforward analysis reveals that the eigenvalues
i\omega_{-1,\xi}
and
i\omega_{0,\xi}
possess negative Krein signatures, whereas all remaining eigenvalues exhibit positive Krein signatures. We summarize in the following lemma all the possible collisions of the eigenvalues
i\omega_{n,\xi}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
\xi\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
, the eigenvalues
i\omega_{n,\xi}
have the following properties:

(i)

For
\xi=0
, the collision occurs only at
\omega_{0,0}=\omega_{-1,0}=\omega_{1,0}=0
;

(ii)

For some
\xi_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, there exist possible collisions of eigenvalues
i\omega_{-1,\xi_{0}}
and
i\omega_{n,\xi_{0}},n\geq 1
, and of
i\omega_{0,\xi_{0}}
and
i\omega_{-n,\xi_{0}},n\geq 2
. Furthermore, if
\xi_{0}\in(0,1-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}]
,
i\omega_{-1,\xi_{0}}
collides only possibly with
i\omega_{1,\xi_{0}}
.

(iii)

For
\xi\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, there holds

•

for
k^{2}\leq 3
,

\cdots<\omega_{-3,\xi}<\omega_{-2,\xi}<\omega_{0,\xi}<0<\omega_{-1,\xi}<\omega_{1,\xi}<\omega_{2,\xi}<\omega_{3,\xi}<\cdots;

(3.11)

•

for
3<k^{2}\leq 4
,
\omega_{-1,\xi_{0}}=\omega_{1,\xi_{0}}
with
\xi_{0}=\sqrt{1-\frac{3}{k^{2}}}
;

•

for
k^{2}\geq 4
,
\omega_{-2,\xi_{0}}=\omega_{0,\xi_{0}}
with
\xi_{0}=1-\sqrt{1-\frac{3}{k^{2}}}
.

Proof.

At
\xi=0
, we find that

\cdots<\omega_{-3,0}<\omega_{-2,0}<\omega_{0,0}=\omega_{-1,0}=\omega_{1,0}=0<\omega_{2,0}<\omega_{3,0}<\cdots.

For
\xi\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, it is obvious

•

\omega_{-1,\xi}=\frac{2k^{2}w_{0}^{2}}{1+k^{2}}\frac{\xi-1}{1+k^{2}(\xi-1)^{2}}\left((\xi-1)^{2}-1\right)>0
and
\omega_{0,\xi}=\frac{2k^{2}w_{0}^{2}}{1+k^{2}}\frac{\xi}{1+k^{2}\xi^{2}}\left(\xi^{2}-1\right)
<0.

•

\omega_{-n,\xi}<0,n\geq 2
and
\omega_{n,\xi}>0,n\geq 1
.

Consequently, two distinct types of eigenvalue collisions may occur: of
i\omega_{0,\xi}
and
i\omega_{-n,\xi}
,
n\geq 2
, and of
\omega_{-1,\xi}
and
\omega_{n,\xi}>0
,
n\geq 1
. On the other hand, the monotonic characteristic of the function
f(x)=\frac{x(x^{2}-1)}{1+k^{2}x^{2}}
for
x^{2}>\frac{1}{3}
permits

\cdots<\omega_{-3,\xi}<\omega_{-2,\xi}<0<\omega_{1,\xi}<\omega_{2,\xi}<\omega_{3,\xi}<\cdots.

Additionally, rigorous calculation establishes that when
(\xi-1)^{2}\in[\frac{1}{3},1)

0<\omega_{-1,\xi}\leq\frac{2k^{2}w_{0}^{2}}{1+k^{2}}\cdot\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{9+3k^{2}}<\frac{2k^{2}w_{0}^{2}}{1+k^{2}}\cdot\frac{6}{1+4k^{2}}<\omega_{2,\xi}=\frac{2k^{2}w_{0}^{2}}{1+k^{2}}\frac{(\xi+2)\left((\xi+2)^{2}-1\right)}{1+k^{2}(\xi+2)^{2}}.

Thus
i\omega_{-1,\xi}
collides possibly only with
i\omega_{1,\xi}
if
\xi_{0}\in(0,1-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}]
. Besides, if
k^{2}\leq 3
, then for
\xi\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]

\omega_{-2,\xi}-\omega_{0,\xi}=-\frac{2k^{2}w_{0}^{2}}{1+k^{2}}\cdot\frac{2(\xi-1)^{2}\left(k^{2}\xi^{2}-2k^{2}\xi+3\right)}{\left(k^{2}(\xi-2)^{2}+1\right)\left(k^{2}\xi^{2}+1\right)}<0,

and

\omega_{-1,\xi}-\omega_{1,\xi}=-\frac{2k^{2}w_{0}^{2}}{1+k^{2}}\cdot\frac{2\xi^{2}\left(k^{2}\xi^{2}-k^{2}+3\right)}{k^{4}\left(\xi^{2}-1\right)^{2}+2k^{2}\left(\xi^{2}+1\right)+1}<0,

which, when combined with the foregoing arguments, gives the conclusions (iii).
∎

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\mathcal{A}_{0,0}-\lambda\right)^{-1}\mathcal{A}_{0,0}^{\prime}\left(\mathcal{A}_{0,0}-\lambda\right)^{-1}\dot{\mathcal{A}}_{0,0}1\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}\left(\mathcal{A}_{0,0}-\lambda\right)^{-1}\mathcal{A}_{0,0}^{\prime}\left(i\frac{2k^{2}w_{0}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}(1+k^{2})}\right)\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}\left(-i\frac{2k^{2}w_{0}^{3}(6+2k^{2})}{\lambda^{3}(1+k^{2})^{2}}\sin z\right)\mathrm{d}\lambda=0,

Moreover since
\dot{\mathcal{A}}_{0,0}^{\prime}1=i\hat{\alpha}\cos z
for some constant
\hat{\alpha}
, by (
B.11
) we find also

\widehat{\mathrm{III}}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\mathcal{A}_{0,0}-\lambda\right)^{-1}\dot{\mathcal{A}}_{0,0}^{\prime}1\mathrm{d}\lambda=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{i\hat{\alpha}}{\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{d}\lambda=0.

The qualities in (
B.21
) follow immediately from the observation
P_{0,0}\dot{P}_{0,0}^{\prime}=0
as a result of
\dot{P}_{0,0}^{\prime}\mathcal{V}_{0,0}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{L^{2}}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

It follows from the expansion in equation (
B.9
), Lemma
B.5
and Lemma
B.7
. Using that

\left(f_{1}^{+}(0,a),1\right)=\left(U_{0,a}f_{1}^{+},1\right)=\left(U_{0,a}\mathcal{J}_{0}f_{1}^{-},1\right)=\left(\mathcal{J}_{0}U_{0,a}^{-*}f_{1}^{-},1\right)=0,

one can prove that the term of order
a^{2}
of
f_{1}^{+}(\xi,a)
has zero average. Moreover, by the Taylor expansion, we get that

\displaystyle h(\xi,a):=

\displaystyle f_{m}^{\sigma}(\xi,a)-f_{m}^{\sigma}-\xi\dot{U}_{0,0}f_{m}^{\sigma}-aU_{0,0}^{\prime}f_{m}^{\sigma}-\xi a\dot{U}_{0,0}^{\prime}f_{m}^{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}U_{0,0}^{\prime\prime}f_{m}^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi^{3}\varphi_{0}(\xi,a)+\xi^{2}a\varphi_{1}(\xi,a)+\xi a^{2}\varphi_{2}(\xi,a)+a^{3}\varphi_{3}(\xi,a)

for some
\mathcal{C}^{r-2}
functions
\varphi_{j},j=0\ldots 3
and
r\geq 3
. By Lemma
B.2
,
h(\xi,0)\equiv 0
, and thus
\varphi_{0}(\xi,0)\equiv 0
. Then
\varphi_{0}(\xi,a)=a\tilde{\varphi}_{0}(\xi,a)

\mathcal{C}^{r-3}

\tilde{\varphi}_{0}(\xi,a)
(
[

), and in conclusion
