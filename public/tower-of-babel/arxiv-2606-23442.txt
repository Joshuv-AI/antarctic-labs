Sort-Stratified Semantics for Temporal Conflict Detection in ODRL Policies
Daham M. Mustafa, Diego Collarana, Sabrina Kirrane, Christoph Lange, Christoph Quix, Sandra Geisler, Stefan Decker, Rafiqul Haque

Abstract
In the Open Digital Rights Language (ODRL), temporal constraints range over two sorts, instants and durations, but the comparison operators do not distinguish them. The same operator thus means ‘‘earlier instant’’ or ‘‘shorter duration,’’ leaving conflict detection between two policies unsound. We resolve this by sort stratification: each temporal operand is typed to one of two ordered domains, points in time or amounts of time. Each constraint then denotes an interval, and conflict reduces to interval comparison under a three-valued verdict (Conflict, Compatible, Unknown). We characterise the check’s decidability across a static and a runtime fragment, prove it sound, and evaluate it on a benchmark of policy problems compiled to TPTP and SMT-LIB, available as an artefact. 1 1 1 https://github.com/Daham-Mustaf/odrl-temporal-benchmark

Introduction

Data spaces
[
20
,
12

let organisations exchange digital assets under
explicit usage policies, which commonly carry temporal conditions, e.g., a policy may expire on a fixed
date, impose an embargo before first use, limit the total usage time, or grant access only on a
recurring schedule. These policies are written in the Open Digital Rights Language (ODRL) 2.2
[

[

[

This check precedes any access;

constraints
over dedicated operands: the instant
dateTime
; the duration-based

delayPeriod
,
elapsedTime
, and
meteredTime
; and the recurrence

timeInterval

[
16
]

[
24
,
23
]

and Bonatti et al.
[
5
]
already compare two policies, they do so over

alone, as a sortless attribute. We give these operands semantics and check
compatibility between an offer and a request over the full temporal operand set.

Comparison operators do not distinguish between instants and durations sorts, so the same
lt
operator has different interpretations: on an instant it means “earlier than”, as in
(\textsf{dateTime},\textsf{lt},\textsf{2026-12-31})
; on a duration it means “shorter than”, as in
(\textsf{elapsedTime},\textsf{lt},\textsf{P30D})
, where
P30D
denotes thirty days in ISO 8601. Because of this ambiguity, the operator alone does not determine the intended interpretation, nor does it prevent comparisons between incomparable sorts. Introducing a sort for each operand resolves this ambiguity by fixing the interpretation of operators and excluding comparisons between instants and durations.

Additionally, the operands are not independent. Metered usage, the time actually spent using an asset, can never exceed the elapsed time since access began
[
16
]
; and under
andSequence
, which requires constraints to be satisfied in order, a
delayPeriod
is counted from the moment the preceding constraint is first satisfied, not from a fixed date. Thus

\textsf{andSequence}(\textsf{dateTime}~\textsf{gteq}~\textsf{2026-01-01},\ \textsf{delayPeriod}~\textsf{gteq}~\textsf{P1D})

permits the action only at least one day after the
2026-01-01
constraint is first satisfied. Checking each operand against its own constraint in isolation, therefore, misses conflicts that arise from these relations: an offer’s
meteredTime

eq

P30D
and a request’s
elapsedTime

lteq

P10D
are each satisfiable alone but jointly unsatisfiable, since thirty days of metered usage necessarily require at least thirty elapsed days, which the request’s limit of ten forbids.

In this paper, we propose a sort-stratified semantics for ODRL’s temporal constraints, and a conflict
detector built on it. The semantics gives each operand a precise
meaning, separating time points from durations, so that constraints the current operators read ambiguously gain a single interpretation. The detector decides whether an offer and a request are
compatible over their temporal terms, returning one of three answers, “compatible”, “in conflict”, or
“undetermined” when only one side constrains a condition; it catches conflicts spanning two
operands, such as metered use never exceeding elapsed time, handles the recurrence and ordered

andSequence
operators, and classifies each conflict by the
reasoning it needs. The same semantics covers conflict detection at both negotiation time and runtime, so a conflict
found before agreement provably holds at runtime. The benchmark encodes
conflict-detection problems in TPTP and SMT-LIB and decides them with automated reasoners. The remainder of the paper covers preliminaries (
Section

0.2
), the sort-stratified
denotational semantics (
Section

0.3
), the conflict-detection framework (
Section

0.4
), the evaluation (
Section

0.5
),
related work (
Section

0.6
) and the conclusion
(
Section

0.7
).

Definition 0.3.1

(Temporal structures)
.

The
timeline

\mathbb{T}=(T,\leq_{\mathbb{T}})
is a linear order that is

affine
over
(\mathbb{Q},+)
: fixing any origin identifies

(T,\leq_{\mathbb{T}})
order-preservingly with
(\mathbb{Q},\leq)
. The
duration domain

\mathbb{D}=(D,+,0,\leq_{\mathbb{D}})
is a totally ordered
cancellative commutative monoid embedding order-preservingly into

(\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0},+,\leq)
and closed under the non-negative differences of instants. For an instant
s\in T
and
d\in\mathbb{Q}
, the

shift

s+d\in T
is the instant
d
after
s
; the
difference

t-s\in\mathbb{Q}
is its inverse, and the
elapsed duration

\delta(s,t)=t-s
lies in

exactly when
s\leq_{\mathbb{T}}t
.

We exclude
xsd:yearMonthDuration
because month- and year-based durations do not denote fixed-length intervals. Consequently the full
xsd:duration
datatype is only partially ordered, since values such as
P1M
and
P30D
are not comparable. We build the semantics on intervals, so we first consider only the operators whose constraints denote intervals.
A comparison such as
before a value
,
at least a value
, or

exactly a value
selects an order-convex subset of the operand’s domain
(i.e., whenever it contains two points, it also contains all intermediate points).

Definition 0.3.2

(Interval operators)
.

On a totally ordered set
(D,\leq)
, an operator
\bowtie
is an

if for every

the solution set

\{x\in D\mid x\bowtie v\}
is order-convex. Among the ODRL operators the
interval operators are exactly

\mathcal{O}_{I}=\{\textsf{lt},\textsf{lteq},\textsf{gt},\textsf{gteq},\textsf{eq}\}
.

Each interval operator is interpreted on the ordered domain fixed by its operand’s sort; hence, every temporal operand must be assigned a sort to fix the meaning of its constraints.

Definition 0.3.3

(Temporal sorts)
.

A

is
\sigma\in\{\mathsf{Inst},\mathsf{Dur}\}
: the instant sort
\mathsf{Inst}
,

\mathbb{T}
, and the duration sort
\mathsf{Dur}

and

The ODRL vocabulary recommends a restricted set of operators for each temporal operand

16
]

Lemma 0.3.7

(Recurrence conflict)
.

For periodic constraints with anchors
a_{1},a_{2}
and periods
p_{1},p_{2}
,

\mathsf{Per}(a_{1},p_{1})\cap\mathsf{Per}(a_{2},p_{2})\neq\emptyset\iff\gcd(p_{1},p_{2})\mid(a_{2}-a_{1}),

where
\gcd(p_{1},p_{2})
is the positive generator of the subgroup

p_{1}\mathbb{Z}+p_{2}\mathbb{Z}
of
(\mathbb{Q},+)
, and
g\mid\Delta
means

\Delta/g\in\mathbb{Z}
.
When the intersection is nonempty, it is itself periodic,

\mathsf{Per}(a_{1},p_{1})\cap\mathsf{Per}(a_{2},p_{2})=\mathsf{Per}(a^{\ast},\operatorname{lcm}(p_{1},p_{2}))
, where

a^{\ast}
is any common occurrence, obtained from the extended Euclidean algorithm
applied to
p_{1},p_{2}
and
a_{2}-a_{1}
.

Proof.

The intersection is nonempty iff
a_{2}-a_{1}=kp_{1}-jp_{2}
for some

k,j\in\mathbb{Z}
, that is
a_{2}-a_{1}\in p_{1}\mathbb{Z}+p_{2}\mathbb{Z}
. This
subgroup of
(\mathbb{Q},+)
is finitely generated, hence cyclic with positive
generator
\gcd(p_{1},p_{2})
, so membership is exactly

\gcd(p_{1},p_{2})\mid(a_{2}-a_{1})
.
∎

Lemma 0.3.8

(Windowed recurrence)
.

Let
I
be an interval of
\mathbb{T}
with ends
\ell\in\mathbb{T}\cup\{-\infty\}
,

u\in\mathbb{T}\cup\{+\infty\}
, and let
\mathsf{Per}(a,p)
have
p>0
. Then

I\cap\mathsf{Per}(a,p)\neq\emptyset
iff some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
has
a+kp\in I
. For bounded
I

this is
\lceil(\ell-a)/p\rceil\leq\lfloor(u-a)/p\rfloor
(strict where the matching end of

I
is open); for
u=+\infty
it always holds, dually for
\ell=-\infty
. The condition is
Presburger-definable.

Proof.

a+kp\in[\ell,u]
iff
(\ell-a)/p\leq k\leq(u-a)/p
for
p>0
; an integer in that range
exists iff
\lceil(\ell-a)/p\rceil\leq\lfloor(u-a)/p\rfloor
, and if
u=+\infty
any

k\geq\lceil(\ell-a)/p\rceil
works. For an open end the bound is strict: an open left end at
\ell
gives
k\geq\lfloor(\ell-a)/p\rfloor+1
and an open right end at
u
gives
k\leq\lceil(u-a)/p\rceil-1
, in place of
\lceil(\ell-a)/p\rceil
and
\lfloor(u-a)/p\rfloor
. Floor and divisibility over a fixed period are
Presburger-definable.
∎

0.3.2
Conflict Criterion and the Product Denotation

When multiple constraints apply to one operand, its denotation is the set of values
satisfying all of them, i.e., the intersection of their intervals. Among temporal
operands, only
dateTime
is bounded from both sides; the duration operands
take a single upper or lower bound.

Lemma 0.3.9

(Per-operand normalisation)
.

For an interval-valued operand
\ell
and a finite set
C
of admissible constraints
conjoined under
and
, the
normalised interval

I_{\ell}(C)=\bigcap_{c\in C,\ \operatorname{operand}(c)=\ell}\llbracket c\rrbracket

(taken as
D_{\ell}
when no constraint of
C
has operand
\ell
) is either an
interval over
D_{\ell}
or empty.

Proof.

A finite intersection of intervals of a totally ordered set is an interval or empty.
∎

Two intervals on one operand are disjoint when one lies entirely below the other,
which we check by comparing the upper bound of one against the lower bound of the
other.

Definition 0.3.10

(Bound order)
.

For an upper bound
u
and a lower bound
l
, we write
u\prec l
iff
u<l
, or

u=l
and at least one of the corresponding intervals excludes the boundary point.

The second case,
u=l
covers boundary-touching intervals, which are disjoint if at least one excludes the shared boundary point.

Theorem 0.3.11

(Conflict criterion)
.

For admissible
c_{1},c_{2}
on the same operand, with lower bounds
l_{1},l_{2}
and upper
bounds
u_{1},u_{2}
,

\llbracket c_{1}\rrbracket\cap\llbracket c_{2}\rrbracket=\emptyset\iff u_{1}\prec l_{2}\ \vee\ u_{2}\prec l_{1}.

Proof.

Each denotation is a nonempty interval of a totally ordered domain (
Section

0.3

under admissibility), and two such intervals are disjoint iff one lies wholly below the
other, that is, an upper bound falls before the other lower bound under
\prec
.
∎

A policy bounds several operands at once. Because distinct operands range over
independent domains, the region satisfying all constraints is the product of the
per-operand intervals.

Definition 0.3.12

(Instant-axis denotation)
.

dateTime
and
timeInterval
both constrain
\rho.\mathit{now}
, so they share the
instant axis. The
instant-axis denotation
of a policy
C
is
N(C)=W(C)\cap S(C)
,
where
W(C)=I_{\textsf{dateTime}}(C)
is the normalised
dateTime
interval
(
Section

0.3.2
; the full timeline if absent) and
S(C)
is the periodic set of

C
’s
timeInterval
(
Section

0.3.1
; the full timeline if absent).

The date and recurrence conditions have already been combined into one instant axis, so every
remaining condition concerns a duration, and durations vary independently of one another. A
policy’s denotation is then the instant axis together with the range allowed for each duration,
satisfied only when all of these conditions hold together.

Definition 0.3.13

(Product denotation)
.

For a finite conjunction
C
, its denotation is the product of the shared instant
axis with the duration operands,

\llbracket C\rrbracket=N(C)\times I_{\textsf{elapsedTime}}(C)\times I_{\textsf{meteredTime}}(C)\times I_{\textsf{delayPeriod}}(C),

where
N(C)
is the instant-axis denotation of
Section

0.3.2
. A value
vector lies in
\llbracket C\rrbracket
exactly when its instant component lies in
N(C)
and each
duration component lies in its operand’s normalised interval.
dateTime
and

timeInterval
are not separate product axes: both constrain
\rho.\mathit{now}
and
enter only through
N(C)
.

In
Section

0.2
the offer’s denotation is

N(\mathcal{P}_{\text{offer}})\times[0,\textsf{P30D}]\times\{\textsf{P30D}\}\times[\textsf{P1D},\top)
on the
instant,
elapsedTime
,
meteredTime
, and
delayPeriod
axes, where

N(\mathcal{P}_{\text{offer}})=(\bot,\textsf{2026-12-31})\cap\mathsf{Per}(\textsf{2026-01-01},\textsf{P30D})
folds the

dateTime
window and the
timeInterval
recurrence onto one axis. Conversely, every product of realizable intervals comes from some
conjunction, so the denotation expresses exactly these product regions and no others (
Theorem

0.3.14
).

Theorem 0.3.14

(Product expressibility)
.

Let
I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{n}
have each
I_{k}
a nonempty interval of
D_{\ell_{k}}

realizable by admissible constraints on
\ell_{k}
(
Section

0.3
). Then some
conjunction
C
of admissible constraints has
\llbracket C\rrbracket=I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{n}
.

0.3.3
Background Constraints and Tiered Decision

A policy’s constraints permit certain executions, but some properties hold in every execution: a duration is never negative, and metered usage never exceeds elapsed time. We collect these properties into a background theory, so conflict detection asks not just whether two policies overlap but whether their overlap is consistent with the background theory.

Definition 0.3.15

(Background theory and conflict)
.

Write
\llbracket\ell\rrbracket_{\rho}\in D_{\ell}
for the value operand
\ell
takes in a context

\rho
; let
C_{i}
be the constraint set of policy
P_{i}
and
a_{0}
the instant access
is granted, shared by the two policies. The
background theory
is

\displaystyle\Phi={}

\displaystyle\{0\leq_{\mathbb{D}}\llbracket op\rrbracket_{\rho}\mid op\text{ a duration operand}\}\cup\{\,\llbracket\textsf{meteredTime}\rrbracket_{\rho}\leq_{\mathbb{D}}\llbracket\textsf{elapsedTime}\rrbracket_{\rho}\,\}

\displaystyle\cup\{\,\llbracket\textsf{delayPeriod}\rrbracket_{\rho}\leq_{\mathbb{D}}\llbracket\textsf{elapsedTime}\rrbracket_{\rho}\,\}\cup\{\,a_{0}\leq_{\mathbb{T}}\rho.\mathit{trig}\leq_{\mathbb{T}}\rho.\mathit{now}\,\}.

Constraint sets
C_{1},C_{2}

conflict
when
C_{1}\wedge C_{2}\wedge\Phi
is
unsatisfiable, equivalently
\llbracket C_{1}\rrbracket\cap\llbracket C_{2}\rrbracket\cap\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket=\emptyset
, where

\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
is the set of value assignments satisfying
\Phi
.

The product denotation
\llbracket C\rrbracket
captures only per-operand restrictions. It ignores
cross-operand relations from the background theory
\Phi
. Therefore, some conflicts
are invisible at the product level and become apparent only after intersecting with

\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket
. Writing
m
and
e
for the metered and elapsed values,

meteredTime

eq

P30D
and

elapsedTime

lteq

P10D
are consistent on each axis, yet

\llbracket\textsf{meteredTime}\rrbracket_{\rho}\leq\llbracket\textsf{elapsedTime}\rrbracket_{\rho}
forces

30\leq m\leq e\leq 10
, which is inconsistent.

The complexity of conflict detection depends on whether the constraints require simple ordering,
arithmetic between values, or reasoning about periodic structure. These give three tiers. Order
alone (
\mathsf{Ord}
) suffices when each atom bounds one operand by a literal. A difference (
\mathsf{Diff}
) is
needed when an operand is bounded relative to a constrained instant, or when two operands are
related by
\Phi
. Modular reasoning (
\mathsf{Mod}
) is needed when an operand is periodic.

Definition 0.3.16

(Tier)
.

Let
S=C_{1}\wedge C_{2}\wedge\Phi
be the combined system of
Section

0.3.3
. A
cross-operand relation of
\Phi
is
active
when both operands it relates are
constrained by
C_{1}
or
C_{2}
. Then

\operatorname{tier}(S)=\begin{cases}\mathsf{Mod}&\text{some operand of }S\text{ is periodic (\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{def:ti-rec})},\\
\mathsf{Diff}&\text{otherwise, if }S\text{ contains a difference }\delta(s,t)\text{ over a}\\
&\text{constrained instant or an active cross-operand relation},\\
\mathsf{Ord}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Each tier has its own decision procedure, each a special case of the next. The order tier
uses a bound comparison, the difference tier a negative-cycle check on a difference graph, and the
modular tier a divisibility test.

Theorem 0.3.17

(Tiered conflict decision)
.

Let
S=C_{1}\wedge C_{2}\wedge\Phi
. If
\operatorname{tier}(S)=\mathsf{Ord}
, then
S
is unsatisfiable iff some shared
operand has disjoint bounds (
Theorem

0.3.11
), an EPR problem. If
\operatorname{tier}(S)=\mathsf{Diff}
, then
S

is unsatisfiable iff its difference graph has a negative cycle, in PTIME, which subsumes
\mathsf{Ord}

since
\mathsf{Ord}\subseteq\mathsf{Diff}
. If
\operatorname{tier}(S)=\mathsf{Mod}
, decide the instant axis and the duration axes and
conjoin:
S_{1}\cap S_{2}
is
\mathsf{Per}(a^{\ast},\operatorname{lcm}(p_{1},p_{2}))
when

\gcd(p_{1},p_{2})\mid(a_{2}-a_{1})
and
\emptyset
otherwise (
Section

0.3.1
); with
W
the feasible

\mathit{now}
-interval cut out by the
dateTime
bounds and the difference system, the instant axis
conflicts iff
S_{1}\cap S_{2}=\emptyset
,
W=\emptyset
, or
W\cap(S_{1}\cap S_{2})=\emptyset

(
Section

0.3.1
), and
S
is unsatisfiable iff the instant axis conflicts or the duration
difference system has a negative cycle. Each sub-check is in Presburger arithmetic. The decision is
sound and complete relative to
\Phi
: every conflict entailed by
C_{1}\wedge C_{2}\wedge\Phi
is
detected;
\Phi
(
Section

0.3.3
) fixes the domain relations (durations nonnegative, metered at most elapsed, and delay at most elapsed), so a conflict resting on a relation outside
\Phi
is not guaranteed. The proof is in
Section

.8
.

Corollary 0.3.18

(Product exactness)
.

If
\operatorname{tier}(S)=\mathsf{Ord}
, the difference graph of
Theorem

0.3.17
has no cross-operand edge, so it
decomposes operand by operand and conflict is decided by the per-operand criterion
(
Theorem

0.3.11
). The product denotation is then exact, while in
\mathsf{Diff}
and
\mathsf{Mod}
it
over-approximates.

In
Section

0.2
the
dateTime
and
elapsedTime
pairs are
\mathsf{Ord}
, the

timeInterval
pair is
\mathsf{Mod}
, and the metered-against-elapsed extension of
Section

0.3.3

is
\mathsf{Diff}
, where
30\leq m\leq e\leq 10
is a negative cycle.
Full metric temporal logic over dense time is undecidable
[
21
]
, so we stay in decidable fragments:
\mathsf{Diff}
in difference logic and
\mathsf{Mod}
in Presburger arithmetic over integer period indices, with the time model dense throughout.

0.4
Conflict Detection, Composition, and Runtime

The three verdicts form a totally ordered algebra in which conjunction and disjunction are the
strong Kleene meet and join.

Definition 0.4.1

(Verdict algebra)
.

\mathbb{V}=\{\textsc{Conflict},\textsc{Unknown},\textsc{Compatible}\}
is totally ordered by

\textsc{Conflict}\sqsubset\textsc{Unknown}\sqsubset\textsc{Compatible}
. Conjunction
\wedge_{K}
is
\min
and disjunction

\vee_{K}
is
\max
under
\sqsubset
.

Conflict
and
Compatible
are determinate;
Unknown
is epistemic.

Definition 0.4.2

(Per-operand verdict)
.

For an operand
\ell
constrained by at least one of
C_{1},C_{2}
,

V_{\ell}=\begin{cases}\textsc{Conflict}&\text{both constrain }\ell\text{ and their denotations on }\ell\text{ are disjoint},\\
\textsc{Compatible}&\text{both constrain }\ell\text{ and their denotations on }\ell\text{ intersect},\\
\textsc{Unknown}&\text{exactly one of }C_{1},C_{2}\text{ constrains }\ell.\end{cases}

Here
\ell
ranges over the duration operands
elapsedTime
,
meteredTime
,

delayPeriod
; the denotations on
\ell
are the normalised intervals

I_{\ell}(C_{1}),I_{\ell}(C_{2})
and disjointness is decided by
Theorem

0.3.11
. The
instant operands
dateTime
and
timeInterval
are handled jointly by the
instant-axis verdict (
Section

0.4
).

Definition 0.4.3

(Instant-axis verdict)
.

Say a policy
constrains the instant axis
when it has a
dateTime
or a

timeInterval
constraint. With
N(\cdot)
as in
Section

0.3.2
,

V_{\mathsf{Inst}}=\begin{cases}\textsc{Conflict}&\text{both }C_{1},C_{2}\text{ constrain the instant axis and }N(C_{1})\cap N(C_{2})=\emptyset,\\
\textsc{Compatible}&\text{both constrain the instant axis and }N(C_{1})\cap N(C_{2})\neq\emptyset,\\
\textsc{Unknown}&\text{exactly one of }C_{1},C_{2}\text{ constrains the instant axis.}\end{cases}

Definition 0.4.4

(Product (policy) verdict)
.

For
C_{1},C_{2}
, the
policy verdict
is

V_{\mathrm{prod}}(C_{1},C_{2})=\min\bigl(V_{\mathsf{Inst}},\ \min_{\ell}V_{\ell}\bigr),

the minimum of the instant-axis verdict (
Section

0.4
) and the
per-operand verdicts (
Section

0.4
) over the duration operands
constrained by at least one of
C_{1},C_{2}
.
dateTime
and
timeInterval

contribute only through
V_{\mathsf{Inst}}
, not as separate product axes.

On operands both policies constrain, the verdict is determinate and, in the
\mathsf{Ord}

tier, agrees with the conflict of
Section

0.3.3
. On an operand only one policy
constrains it is
Unknown
, a case
Section

0.3.3
does not separate from
compatibility. The
\mathsf{Diff}
and
\mathsf{Mod}
tiers use
Theorem

0.3.17
.
In
Section

0.2
,
V_{\mathsf{Inst}}=\textsc{Conflict}
(the offer’s window-and-recurrence set
and the request’s share no instant),
V_{\textsf{elapsedTime}}=\textsc{Compatible}
,

V_{\textsf{delayPeriod}}=\textsc{Conflict}
, and
V_{\textsf{meteredTime}}=\textsc{Unknown}
(only the offer
constrains it), so
V_{\mathrm{prod}}=\textsc{Conflict}
.

Proposition 0.4.5

(Monotonicity)
.

If
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
both constrain
\ell
,
I_{\ell}(C_{1}^{\prime})\subseteq I_{\ell}(C_{1})
, and
the other operands are unchanged, then
V_{\mathrm{prod}}(C_{1}^{\prime},C_{2})\sqsubseteq V_{\mathrm{prod}}(C_{1},C_{2})
.

Proof.

On
\ell
, shrinking to
I_{\ell}(C_{1}^{\prime})\subseteq I_{\ell}(C_{1})
keeps a disjoint pair
disjoint and can only turn an intersecting pair disjoint, so
V_{\ell}^{\prime}\sqsubseteq V_{\ell}
. The other operands are unchanged, so

V_{\mathrm{prod}}(C_{1}^{\prime},C_{2})=\min_{\ell}V_{\ell}^{\prime}\sqsubseteq\min_{\ell}V_{\ell}=V_{\mathrm{prod}}(C_{1},C_{2})
.
∎

For instance, an offer permitting
dateTime
until
2026-12-31
is

Compatible
with a request fixing
2026-06-01
; tightening the deadline to

2026-03-01
excludes that date, giving
Conflict
.
An
Unknown
verdict is undetermined: completing the silent policy, that is, adding a
constraint for the operand it leaves open, can make the pair
Compatible
or
Conflict
.

Definition 0.4.6

(Completion)
.

A
completion
of
C_{1},C_{2}
is a pair
(\hat{C}_{1},\hat{C}_{2})
with

C_{i}\subseteq\hat{C}_{i}
that adds, for every operand
\ell
unconstrained by
C_{i}
,
exactly one admissible constraint targeting
\ell
.

Theorem 0.4.7

(Unknown soundness)
.

V_{\mathrm{prod}}(C_{1},C_{2})=\textsc{Unknown}
iff no operand constrained by both policies yields

Conflict
and some operand is constrained by exactly one of them. In that case a
completion with
V_{\mathrm{prod}}=\textsc{Compatible}
exists, and, provided some operand
\ell

constrained by exactly one policy
C_{j}
has
I_{\ell}(C_{j})\neq D_{\ell}
, a completion
with
V_{\mathrm{prod}}=\textsc{Conflict}
exists.

The proof is in
Section

.8
. For instance, when only one policy bounds an operand, completing
the other with an overlapping bound on it yields
Compatible
and with a disjoint bound
Conflict
.

0.4.1
Logical Composition

The logical operands
and
,
or
, and
xone
combine constraints evaluated at a
single exercise, by intersection, union, and exactly-one-of on their denotations.
andSequence

sequences constraints over a trace rather than at a single exercise, and is treated separately.

Definition 0.4.8

(Logical composition)
.

Over one exercise,

\llbracket\textsf{and}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})\rrbracket=\bigcap_{i}\llbracket C_{i}\rrbracket
,

\llbracket\textsf{or}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})\rrbracket=\bigcup_{i}\llbracket C_{i}\rrbracket
, and

\llbracket\textsf{xone}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})\rrbracket=\bigcup_{i}\big(\llbracket C_{i}\rrbracket\setminus\bigcup_{j\neq i}\llbracket C_{j}\rrbracket\big)
.

A disjunctive policy denotes a union of products, so its verdict against another
policy is determined by the verdicts of all branch pairs.

Definition 0.4.9

(Branch)
.

A
branch
is a disjunct of a policy’s
or
or
xone
constraint, a
constraint or conjunction, whose product verdict against another branch is given by

Section

0.4
. A
branch pair

(B_{i},B_{j})
takes one branch from
each policy.

Definition 0.4.10

(Disjunction and exclusive-disjunction verdicts)
.

\operatorname{verdict}_{\textsf{or}}
is
Compatible
if
V_{\mathrm{prod}}(B_{i},B_{j})=\textsc{Compatible}
for some
pair,
Conflict
if every pair is
Conflict
, and
Unknown
otherwise. For

xone
the pair verdicts give only the sound rule
\operatorname{verdict}_{\textsf{xone}}=\textsc{Conflict}
when every pair is
Conflict
; otherwise the verdict is decided on the

xone
denotation (
Section

0.4.1
):
Compatible
when

\llbracket\textsf{xone}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})\rrbracket\cap\llbracket\textsf{xone}(C_{1}^{\prime},\dots,C_{m}^{\prime})\rrbracket\neq\emptyset
, and
Conflict
when it is empty. Counting
Compatible
pairs decides neither
direction, since a satisfying point must lie in exactly one branch of each side.

For each combinator a
Conflict
verdict reduces to disjointness of the combined
denotations, since intersection distributes over the unions that
or
and

xone
introduce.

Theorem 0.4.11

(Composition soundness)
.

A
Conflict
verdict under
and
,
or
, or
xone
implies that the
two policies’ denotations are disjoint.

The proof is in
Section

.8
.
The product verdict (
Section

0.4
) is sound but not complete: it ranges
over operands independently and so omits the cross-operand relations of
\Phi
, and a
conflict arising only through such a relation is missed. The
\mathsf{Diff}
tier of
Theorem

0.3.17
catches exactly the cross-operand conflicts, and the
\mathsf{Mod}
tier the periodic ones.

0.4.2
Sequences

Sequencing is evaluated over a trace, the timestamped states of a single execution.

Definition 0.4.12

(Trace)
.

A
trace
is a sequence
\mathsf{Tr}=\langle S_{1},\dots,S_{z}\rangle
with strictly
increasing timestamps
t_{S_{1}}<\cdots<t_{S_{z}}
, each
S_{i}
inducing a context

\rho_{i}
with
\rho_{i}.\mathit{now}=t_{S_{i}}
.

andSequence
requires its constraints to be satisfied in order along the trace,
with each delay measured from the previous satisfaction. It is the only logical operand in which a later constraint depends on an earlier instant.

Definition 0.4.13

(
andSequence
)
.

\textsf{andSequence}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})
holds on
\mathsf{Tr}
iff there are indices

1\leq j_{1}\leq\cdots\leq j_{n}\leq z
such that each
C_{k}
is satisfied at
S_{j_{k}}
in
the context
\rho_{j_{k}}
whose trigger is bound to the preceding satisfaction instant,

\rho_{j_{k}}.\mathit{trig}=t_{S_{j_{k-1}}}
, with
\mathit{trig}
free for
k=1
. It is

unresolvable
on
\mathsf{Tr}
when no such non-decreasing tuple exists.

The index order
j_{1}\leq\cdots\leq j_{n}
is non-strict, so two consecutive constraints may be
satisfied at the same state. A step forces strict separation
j_{k-1}<j_{k}
exactly when it
imposes a positive delay.

Lemma 0.4.14

(Delay-induced separation)
.

If
C_{k}
depends on the trigger
\rho_{j_{k}}.\mathit{trig}=t_{S_{j_{k-1}}}
and forces

\delta(\rho_{j_{k}}.\mathit{trig},\rho_{j_{k}}.\mathit{now})>0
(e.g.
\textsf{delayPeriod}~\textsf{gt}~r

with
r\geq 0
,
\textsf{delayPeriod}~\textsf{gteq}~r
with
r>0
, or

\textsf{delayPeriod}~\textsf{eq}~r
with
r>0
), then every satisfying tuple has

t_{S_{j_{k}}}>t_{S_{j_{k-1}}}
, hence
j_{k-1}<j_{k}
.

Proof.

At such a step
\delta(\rho_{j_{k}}.\mathit{trig},\rho_{j_{k}}.\mathit{now})=t_{S_{j_{k}}}-t_{S_{j_{k-1}}}>0
,
so
t_{S_{j_{k}}}>t_{S_{j_{k-1}}}
; since
\mathsf{Tr}
has strictly increasing timestamps,

j_{k-1}<j_{k}
.
∎

Strict separation thus holds exactly at steps carrying a positive delay, and all other steps
order non-strictly. For instance,

\textsf{andSequence}(\textsf{dateTime}~\textsf{gteq}~\textsf{2026-01-01},\ \textsf{delayPeriod}~\textsf{gteq}~\textsf{P1D})
requires the second constraint to be satisfied at
least
P1D
after the first.
Section

0.4.2
gives the single-state case; otherwise a sequence is decided over the trace as a difference system.

Proposition 0.4.15

(Collapse to
and
)
.

If no
C_{k}
depends on
\mathit{trig}
, then for every state
S
,
\textsf{andSequence}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})

holds on the single-state trace
\langle S\rangle
iff
\textsf{and}(C_{1},\dots,C_{n})
holds at
S
,
equivalently when the two have the same per-state denotation
\bigcap_{k}\llbracket C_{k}\rrbracket
.

Proof.

With no constraint depending on
\mathit{trig}
, each
\llbracket C_{k}\rrbracket_{\rho}
is determined by the state. On

\langle S\rangle
the constant tuple
j_{1}=\cdots=j_{n}=1
is the only non-decreasing choice and is
admissible, so the sequence holds iff every
C_{k}
holds at
S
, that is iff

S\in\bigcap_{k}\llbracket C_{k}\rrbracket
.
∎

The only operand whose value depends on
\rho.\mathit{trig}
is
delayPeriod
. Under

andSequence
its value
\delta(\rho.\mathit{trig},\rho.\mathit{now})
is the duration from the trigger to

\rho.\mathit{now}
, the one case in which positional order and metric delay are decided together.
Encoding the trigger as a timepoint and each delay as a difference classifies this system in the

\mathsf{Diff}
tier of
Theorem

0.3.17
, where the sequence is unresolvable exactly when the difference
system is infeasible, a negative cycle. The other duration operands take their values from
context-fixed instants and introduce no cross-operand difference.

0.4.3
Static and Runtime Conflict

Static and runtime conflict are the same question under different quantification. A policy
pair is in
static conflict
when the two are jointly unsatisfiable in the sense of

Section

0.3.3
, the context left symbolic: the negotiation-time check, before any access,
decided by
Theorem

0.3.17
. In
Section

0.2
the
dateTime
disjointness is a static

Conflict
for every exercise. A
runtime conflict
holds when a concrete ground
context, or a trace under
andSequence
, fails a policy at enforcement, the duration
operands computed from actual timestamps and the metered total rather than searched; it
explores no executions, only checking observed usage against the constraints.

Definition 0.4.16

(Ground evaluation)
.

A
ground context

\hat{\rho}
fixes concrete
\mathit{now},\mathit{trig}\in\mathbb{T}
and the access-begin
a_{0}
of
Section

0.3.3
. Over usage segments

[s_{1},e_{1}],\dots,[s_{z},e_{z}]
, the duration operands are computed as

\llbracket\textsf{elapsedTime}\rrbracket_{\hat{\rho}}=\delta(a_{0},\mathit{now})
,

\llbracket\textsf{delayPeriod}\rrbracket_{\hat{\rho}}=\delta(\mathit{trig},\mathit{now})
, and

\llbracket\textsf{meteredTime}\rrbracket_{\hat{\rho}}=\sum_{i}\delta(s_{i},e_{i})
, and
\hat{\rho}\models C
holds iff

(\llbracket\ell\rrbracket_{\hat{\rho}})_{\ell}\in\llbracket C\rrbracket
.

This evaluation is a ground computation. Its only arithmetic is
+
over
\mathbb{D}
, and it performs
no search. For instance, with
a_{0}=\textsf{2027-06-01}
and
\mathit{now}=\textsf{2027-06-09}
,

\llbracket\textsf{elapsedTime}\rrbracket_{\hat{\rho}}=\textsf{P8D}
, satisfying

elapsedTime

lteq

Entailment and Policy Comparison

One constraint entails another when every execution satisfying the first also satisfies the
second. Entailment orders constraints by restrictiveness and is finer than compatibility, which
only checks whether two policies are jointly satisfiable.

0.7
Conclusions and Future Work

We gave a sort-stratified semantics for ODRL’s temporal constraints that types each operand as an
instant or a duration and reads the comparison operators per sort, removing the ambiguity that made
conflict detection unsound. Conflict between an offer and a request then reduces to interval
comparison under a background theory and a three-valued verdict, covering duration arithmetic,
recurrence, cross-operand relations, and
andSequence
. Each conflict falls into one of three
decidable tiers, negotiation-time and runtime conflict are tied by a soundness bridge, and a
benchmark on which four reasoners agree confirms the semantics is faithfully mechanised.
Future work.
The sort discipline extends to ODRL’s other temporal operands,
positions within a media stream; the static semantics could drive an enforcement
engine that computes the duration and recurrence operands current ODRL engines omit; and a
machine-checked development(Isabelle/HOL) of the soundness results is in progress.
