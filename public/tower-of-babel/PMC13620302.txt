Analysis of the Mediating Role of Cytokines in the Relationship Between Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease and Cognitive Impairment After Ischemic Stroke

Xin Du, Jingwei Li

Abstract

AbstractObjectiveTo explore whether nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is associated with poststroke cognitive impairment (PSCI) and to investigate the mediating role of cytokine levels.Research methodsThis study included 247 patients with ischemic stroke. The subjects were divided into no‐to‐mild and moderate‐to‐severe groups according to the severity of NAFLD. The levels of peripheral blood cytokines and the scores of the Montreal cognitive assessment scale (MoCA) were measured for all subjects. The scale scores and cytokine levels were compared between the two groups, and the correlations among NAFLD, cytokines, and cognitive function were analyzed.ResultsCompared with those in the no‐to‐mild group, the levels of IL‐1β, IL‐6, IL‐10, and TNF‐α in the moderate‐to‐severe group were significantly higher, while the MoCA score was significantly lower. Multivariate linear regression analysis indicated that the NAFLD grade could significantly affect the MoCA score and the levels of IL‐1β and TNF‐α. Mediation analysis revealed that the level of TNF‐α significantly mediated the relationship between NAFLD and cognitive function, with a mediation effect size of 0.36.ConclusionElevated TNF‐α levels may be a key intermediate factor in the development of PSCI in patients with NAFLD. The prevention and treatment of NAFLD and the inhibition of the peripheral inflammatory response may lead to new targets for early screening and intervention in PSCI.Keywords: cytokines, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, poststroke cognitive impairment
This article reveals the mediating role of cytokine levels between non‐alcoholic fatty liver disease and post‐stroke cognitive impairment, providing new therapeutic targets for post‐stroke cognitive dysfunction.

Introduction

Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is a globally prevalent chronic condition and has become an increasingly serious public health issue (Wang et al. ). NAFLD is closely related to inflammation. To date, many experiments have demonstrated that the levels of proinflammatory cytokines in the peripheral blood of patients with NAFLD are significantly elevated (Mirea et al. ; Wong et al. ). Because of the accumulation of lipids in NAFLD hepatocytes, the resulting lipotoxicity and oxidative stress induce the massive release of proinflammatory cytokines, which in turn promote the progression of NAFLD, forming a vicious cycle (El Amrousy and El‐Afify ).

In clinical practice, we have reported that NAFLD shares the same risk factors as those associated with cognitive dysfunction, such as diabetes, obesity, and hyperlipidemia (Bertolotti et al. ; Colognesi et al. ); therefore, we believe that NAFLD may have a negative effect on cognitive function. Recent studies have reported that patients with NAFLD perform significantly worse in terms of cognitive domains such as memory, mental speed, attention, psychomotor speed, and visual‐spatial perception (George et al. ). A cross‐sectional study in Japan also revealed that the scores of patients with NAFLD in a verbal fluency task (VFL) were significantly lower (Takahashi et al. ). Although the above studies have shown that NAFLD is associated with cognitive decline to some extent, there are still no relevant reports on whether NAFLD is involved in the pathogenesis of poststroke cognitive impairment (PSCI).

The levels of proinflammatory cytokines in the peripheral blood of patients with NAFLD are elevated (El Amrousy and El‐Afify ), and the levels of proinflammatory cytokines have proven to be significantly correlated with PSCI. Therefore, we speculate that proinflammatory cytokines may, to some extent, mediate the association between NAFLD and PSCI. In this study, we investigated the relationships among NAFLD, peripheral blood cytokine levels, and cognitive dysfunction after ischemic stroke. We further explored the mediating role of cytokine levels in this process, with the aim of identifying new targets for early screening and intervention in PSCI.

Materials and Methods

Research Subjects

A total of 247 patients with acute ischemic stroke who visited Xuzhou First People's Hospital from October 2022 to January 2025 were selected as the experimental group. They were divided into a no‐to‐mild group (146 cases) and a moderate‐to‐severe group (101 cases) according to the severity of NAFLD.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

The inclusion criteria for patients were as follows: (1) a clear diagnosis of acute ischemic cerebral infarction based on the diagnostic criteria for acute ischemic stroke in the guidelines (Chinese Neurology Society, Neurology Group of Cerebrovascular Disease of Chinese Medical Association ); (2) a first‐onset stroke; (3) the capability to undergo a cognitive function scale examination; and (4) the provision of informed consent for this study.

Patients were excluded if they met the following criteria: (1) undergoing thrombolytic or thrombectomy treatment; (2) liver diseases caused by long‐term alcohol abuse; (3) viral hepatitis, immune‐related liver diseases, and so on; (4) taking drugs that may affect liver metabolism within the last month, such as thiazides or glucocorticoids; (5) suffering from other autoimmune, tumor, or inflammatory diseases; (6) history of dementia, mild cognitive impairment, or other chronic conditions significantly affecting cognitive function; (7) having other organic diseases, such as neurological or mental disorders in the brain, apart from cerebral infarction; (8) having a history of substance abuse involving psychoactive substances; (9) unable to cooperate with the examination of cognitive function scales; and (10) patients with congenital or other cause‐induced intellectual disability.

Research Methodology

A detailed medical history was obtained for all patients, including name, sex, age, educational level, NIHSS score, infarction site, number of infarction lesions, classification of stroke causes, infarction area (with a boundary value set at the maximum diameter of 3.0 cm for acute infarction lesions), atrial fibrillation, hypertension, diabetes, smoking, alcohol consumption, coronary heart disease, hyperhomocysteinemia, cholesterol, triglycerides, low‐density lipoprotein, ALT, AST, and abdominal ultrasound examination findings. The levels of peripheral blood cytokines were measured in patients 3 months after stroke, and their cognitive function was evaluated. The clinical data, cognitive function scale scores, and cytokine levels of the no‐to‐mild group and the moderate‐to‐severe group were compared, and the correlations between the severity of NAFLD and cytokines, as well as the scale scores, were analyzed. The role of cytokines in the relationship between NAFLD and cognitive function was explored.

Assessment of Cognitive Function Scale

The Montreal cognitive assessment scale (MoCA) was used to evaluate the cognitive function of patients.

Cytokine Detection Methods

Venous blood (5 mL) was collected in the morning while the research subjects were fasting. The levels of IL‐1β, IL‐2, IL‐6, IL‐8, IL‐10, IL‐12, and TNF‐α were detected using the multiplex microsphere flow cytometry immunofluorescence luminescence method.

Diagnostic Criteria for Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease

According to the “Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines for Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (Revised Edition 2010)”, (1) the following clinical diagnostic criteria were met: (a) there was no history of alcohol consumption, or the amount of alcohol consumed was less than 70 g per week for women and less than 140 g per week for men; and (b) other causes were excluded, and the liver imaging diagnosis was consistent with diffuse fatty liver. (2) Ultrasound imaging diagnosis: (a) the near‐field echo of the liver was diffusely enhanced, and the echo was stronger than that of the kidneys; (b) the internal duct structures of the liver were unclear; and (c) the echo intensity in the far field of the liver area was decreased. If any two of these three criteria were met, a diagnosis of diffuse fatty liver was made.

The severity of NAFLD was classified by the examiners as none, mild, moderate, or severe. In the present study, we classified NAFLD into no‐to‐mild and moderate‐to‐severe NAFLD on the basis of previous studies (Seo et al. ).

Statistical Methods

Statistical analysis of the data was conducted using SPSS 26.0. The measurement data were subjected to normality tests. Data that met the normal distribution criteria are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (x ̄± s). Intergroup comparisons were conducted using two independent samples t tests. Data that did not follow a normal distribution are expressed as M (Q1, Q3), and comparisons between the two groups were conducted using the Mann‒Whitney U test. The categorical count data are expressed as the number of cases (percentage), and comparisons between groups were conducted using the χ2 test. For the factors with statistical significance in the univariate analysis, multiple linear regression analysis was performed. The Process plugin was used to test the effects of peripheral blood cytokine levels on the relationship between NAFLD and cognitive function. A p value < 0.05 indicated a statistically significant difference.

Discussion

Numerous studies have shown that patients with NAFLD have a persistent low‐level inflammatory response in their peripheral blood (He et al. ; Duan et al. ). Some scholars have reported that in the adipose tissue of patients with NAFLD, the levels of proinflammatory immune cells are significantly elevated, and these cells continuously secrete inflammatory cytokines into the bloodstream (Binvignat et al. ). In this study, the levels of cytokines in the no‐to‐mild group and the moderate‐to‐severe group were compared, and the levels of IL‐1β, IL‐6, IL‐10, and TNF‐α in the moderate‐to‐severe group were significantly higher than those in the no‐to‐mild group. In addition, a multiple linear regression model revealed that the severity of NAFLD significantly affected the levels of proinflammatory cytokines in the peripheral blood, and as the severity increased, the levels of proinflammatory cytokines also increased. These findings further confirmed the close relationship between NAFLD and the inflammatory response. Fuchs et al. () reported similar findings. Comparisons of the levels of subcutaneous adipose tissue and plasma inflammatory factors between patients with NAFLD and healthy controls revealed that the levels of these inflammatory markers, including total macrophages and proinflammatory (M1‐like) macrophages, activated and memory T‐cell subsets, and proinflammatory cytokines, were often significantly higher in the NAFLD group than in the healthy control group (Fuchs et al. ). Fontes‐Cal et al. () reported that cytokines may be involved in the immune pathogenesis of NAFLD. According to the “double hit” hypothesis, insulin resistance, oxidative stress, and immune cell activation are the key pathogenic factors involved in the progression from simple steatosis to steatohepatitis. This theory suggests that lipid accumulation leads to liver steatosis, followed by multiple types of damage, including the secretion of adipokines, inflammatory responses, lipotoxicity, glucose and lipid metabolism disorders, and so on, ultimately triggering NASH and cirrhosis (Auguet et al. ). Proinflammatory cytokines, such as IL‐6, IL‐12, TNF, and IL‐10, are considered to play a central role in these processes (Stojsavljević et al. ). Some studies have shown that the plasma concentration of IL‐6 may be positively correlated with systemic insulin resistance, liver IL‐6 expression, liver inflammation, and the degree of liver fibrosis in patients with NAFLD (Fontes‐Cal et al. ). IL‐17 has also been shown to promote the progression of NASH and liver fibrosis (He et al. ). Certain cytokines, such as IL‐6, IL‐8, and adiponectin, may also be associated with the severity of NAFLD (Auguet et al. ; Damavandi and Zeinali ).

Fifty million people worldwide suffer from dementia (Fontes‐Cal et al. ). As the prevalence of cognitive impairment and dementia continues to increase, it is estimated that by 2050, the prevalence of cognitive impairment and dementia among older adults (aged 60 and above) will increase to approximately 2 billion, accounting for 22% of the global population (Takahashi et al. ). With the aging population and the lack of effective preventive intervention measures, the social and economic burden of these conditions has increased worldwide (Pérez Palmer et al. ). Early assessment and prevention of related risk factors are crucial for optimizing the management of cognitive dysfunction. In recent years, numerous studies have shown that NAFLD is closely associated with the onset of cognitive impairment (Hadjihambi et al. ; Wernberg et al. ). A cross‐sectional study involving 320 patients who underwent a liver biopsy revealed that compared with control patients, patients with NAFLD had poorer immediate and delayed memory. Severe hepatic steatosis is associated with increased memory impairment (Miao et al. ). A meta‐analysis revealed that compared with that in healthy controls, the risk of cognitive impairment in patients with NAFLD increased by a factor of 1.44 overall (Wang et al. ). In our study, compared with those in the no‐to‐mild group, the MoCA scores in the moderate‐to‐severe group were significantly lower. We then further conducted a multiple linear regression analysis using the MoCA score as the dependent variable and the severity of NAFLD as the independent variable. The results revealed that a higher severity of NAFLD was associated with a lower MoCA score. NAFLD had a significant negative effect on cognitive function, suggesting that NAFLD might also play an indispensable role in the pathogenesis of PSCI.

Proinflammatory cytokines may be involved in the pathogenesis of PSCI. In a mouse model of left unilateral common carotid artery occlusion (UCCAO) cerebral ischemia model, it was found that the levels of the proinflammatory cytokines TNF‐α and IL‐1β in the central nervous system significantly increased after acute ischemic stroke. This may lead to the expansion of brain damage and neuronal death and result in significant impairment of cognitive performance in mice (Silva et al. ). In clinical studies, Kulesh et al. () reported significant differences in cytokine expression levels between patients with normal cognition and those with cognitive impairment after acute ischemic stroke. Serum IL‐6 levels and the levels of IL‐1β and IL‐10 in the cerebrospinal fluid were significantly higher in patients with executive dysfunction than in patients without cognitive impairment (Kulesh et al. ). Accumulating evidence suggests that cytokines may be related to neurogenesis, synaptic plasticity, long‐term potentiation (LTP), and the formation and consolidation of memory. This might be an important mechanism through which cytokines affect cognitive function (Silva et al. ). The levels of proinflammatory cytokines in the peripheral blood of patients with NAFLD are elevated, and these proinflammatory cytokines may also be involved in the pathogenesis of PSCI; thus, we reasonably speculate that cytokines may play a crucial role in the process through which NAFLD can damage cognitive function. Therefore, through mediation effect analysis, we found that TNF‐α plays a significant mediating role between NAFLD and MoCA scores. It is worth noting that the mediation analysis conducted in the present study is purely exploratory. Since cytokine levels and the MOCA scale were measured and assessed concurrently, there is currently a lack of longitudinal time‐series evidence to verify the causal relationships among the exposure variables, the mediating variables, and the outcome variables. Accordingly, the significant mediation effect identified in this study only confirms the statistical association between NAFLD, elevated TNF‐α, and cognitive decline, but does not establish a clear biological causal pathway. Inferring a robust biological causal pathway requires further validation via prospective longitudinal studies and mechanistic experiments. Notwithstanding this limitation, this exploratory mediation analysis still offers a novel perspective for elucidating the relationship between NAFLD and PSCI. Preventing and treating NAFLD and inhibiting peripheral inflammatory responses may lead to new targets for early screening and intervention in PSCI.

Recent animal studies have shown that TNF‐α antagonists can have beneficial effects on cognitive function (Ignatowski et al. ). Etanercept is a biological TNF‐α antagonist. It has been reported to reduce microglial cell activation in experimental models and can also reduce inflammation and vascular dysfunction, thereby improving cognitive deficits in rats (Gocmez et al. ). In some studies using animal models, researchers reported that mice treated with etanercept showed significant improvement in cognitive function (Tuttolomondo et al. ). However, at present, there is a lack of relevant clinical studies. In the future, more clinical experiments are needed to further verify the clinical therapeutic value of TNF‐α antagonists for PSCI.

Conclusion

This study confirmed that elevated TNF‐α levels may be a key intermediate factor in the development of PSCI in patients with NAFLD. Preventing and treating NAFLD and inhibiting the peripheral inflammatory response may lead to new targets for early screening and intervention in PSCI. The limitations of this study include the relatively small sample size and the single‐center design, which may not represent the general population of patients with ischemic stroke. In the future, multicenter studies should be conducted to expand the sample size and further verify the conclusions of this study. The measurement of cytokine levels and the assessment using the MoCA scale were conducted at the same time point; subsequent studies at different time points can be carried out to verify the causal relationship. In addition, the follow‐up time for assessing the cognitive function of patients with ischemic stroke in this study was relatively short. Future studies should extend the follow‐up time to explore whether these conclusions still hold.
