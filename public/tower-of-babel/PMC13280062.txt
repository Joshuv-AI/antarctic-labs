Impact of different PSMA PET-based eligibility criteria on outcome of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA radioligand therapy using [18F]rhPSMA-7/[18F]rhPSMA-7.3 (Flotufolastat)
Authors: Sonia Grigorascu, Thomas Langbein, Isabel Rauscher, Kimberley Hansen, Theo Lorenzini, Wolfgang A. Weber, Türkay Hekimsoy, Lena Unterrainer, Matthias Eiber
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background[177Lu]Lu-PSMA radioligand therapy (RLT) is key in treating metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC). Various PSMA agents and eligibility criteria for [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT have been described. We retrospectively assessed applicability and outcomes using Blue Earth Therapeutics’ (BET) phase I study criteria with [18F]rhPSMA-7 and Flotufolastat ([18F]rhPSMA-7.3), comparing them to VISION trial-based PSMA PET/CT criteria. Patients eligible for [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT, as assessed by an interdisciplinary board, were retrospectively stratified into four groups based on whether their PSMA PET scans with Flotufolastat met BET (B + /B-) and/or VISION (V + /V-) criteria. Outcome parameters like PSA response, PSA progression-free survival (PSA-PFS), and overall survival (OS) were compared, and reasons for (in-)eligibility by BET or VISION were analyzed.ResultsIn total, 191 mCRPC patients from our institutional database were included, of which 138 underwent [18F]rhPSMA-7.3 and 53 [18F]rhPSMA-7. 161 (84.3%) met both criteria (V + /B +), 13 (6.8%) were ineligible (V-/B-), and 17 (8.9%) met VISION but not BET criteria (V + /B-). No patient was BET-eligible without also fulfilling VISION criteria. Of the 30 patients excluded by BET criteria, 17 (56.7%) lacked the “majority” tumor volume criterion; 10/13 (76.9%) excluded by VISION had visceral metastases with lower PSMA expression than the liver. In the subgroups V + /B + , V + /B-, and V-/B-, a PSA response > 50% was present in 62/157 (39.5%), 2/17 (11.8%), and 0/13 (0%) patients, respectively. V + /B + patients had the significantly longest PSA-PFS compared to V + /B- and V-/B- (PSA-PFS: 4.3 months (95% CI 3.4 – 5.2) vs. 1.5 months (95% CI 1.4 – 1.6) vs. 2.8 months (95% CI 0.0 – 6.5), p = 0.002) and a trend towards longer OS (14.9 vs. 13.1 vs. 7.7 months, p = 0.065).ConclusionMultiple similarities between BET and VISION eligibility criteria (V + /B +) prior to [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT were observed, with the majority of patients fulfilling both. Positivity in both criteria was associated with the best outcome. Data from our pilot cohort suggest that the criteria for BET’s phase I trial select patients more strictly for [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT than VISION criteria, while OS did not significantly differ between the three different groups.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13550-026-01459-z.

Introduction

[177Lu]Lu-PSMA radioligand therapy (RLT) has become a cornerstone for patients with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) [1–3]. [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT is highly effective with low toxicity [2, 4, 5] and was also recently FDA-approved in mCRPC prior to first-line chemotherapy [6]. Patients who receive [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT usually undergo prior PET imaging to determine target expression for treatment suitability and agents such as [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11, [18F]rhPSMA-7.3 (POSLUMA®), and [18F]DCFPyL (Pylarify®) are commonly used to screen for sufficient PSMA-ligand uptake [7]. However, according to the SNMMI/EANM guidelines, all PSMA agents are suitable for selecting patients for [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT [8]. To date, the PET-based criteria used to select patients are frequently inconsistent in clinical routine and often even differ between prospective clinical trials.

In 2021, the VISION trial, a multicenter randomized phase III study involving 831 patients with mCRPC, assessed the effectiveness of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 plus standard of care compared to standard of care alone. Before treatment, patients underwent [68Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT imaging to confirm PSMA-positive status and were only admitted to therapy if they fulfilled a set of PSMA PET-based criteria. The trial findings demonstrated significantly longer overall survival (OS) and radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS) for patients receiving [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617 than the standard of care alone [2].

[177Lu]Lu-rhPSMA-10.1 is another therapeutic PSMA-targeting agent that has shown promising results for treating mCRPC [9–11]. It has already been demonstrated in patients treated with up to 6 cycles that [177Lu]Lu-rhPSMA-10.1 displays moderately low toxicity and encouraging declines in PSA levels [10, 11]. This agent is currently being explored in a prospective, multicenter, non-randomized trial by Blue Earth Therapeutics (NCT05413850, Registration Date: 20/05/2022). Patients in this trial undergo a Flotufolastat PET scan prior to therapy to assess treatment eligibility, and are required to present with at least one PET/CT-visible lesion with a PSMA expression score higher than liver uptake. They are excluded from therapy if they show any of the following: lymph node > 10 mm short axis and uptake ≤ blood pool, bone metastasis with soft tissue component with uptake ≤ blood pool, visceral metastasis > 10 mm long axis and uptake ≤ liver, or majority of all metastases (based on a visual assessment) ≤ liver.

Considering the frequently high cost of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT and the sometimes limited benefit, careful patient selection is important to optimize the average benefit per patient. It has been demonstrated that PSMA PET/CT-related findings, such as specific types of metastases (i.e., visceral metastases), low SUVs in PSMA PET, or PSMA-negative tumor lesions, are predictive factors linked to unfavorable treatment outcomes and responses to [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT [12–15]. For that reason, the 2021 TheraP trial, based on previous findings [16], excluded patients with FDG-positive/PSMA-negative (discordant) lesions from RLT to optimize response rates [17]. However, it is also known that patients with lower PSMA expression, which would not have been eligible for the VISION trial, can respond to [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT [18]. Based on advice from the FDA to broaden access to novel [177Lu]Lu-PSMA agents for patients, including those with low PSMA-expressing tumors but also excluding patients with negative prognostic PET features, a new set of criteria has been developed for this [177Lu]Lu-rhPSMA-10.1-based phase I trial (BET-PSMA-121).

In this study, we retrospectively assessed how the BET-PSMA-121 eligibility criteria align with those of the VISION trial, and whether these differences affect patient outcomes.

Material and methods

Patients

We reviewed data from our institutional database and included all patients who received [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT with [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-I&T from October 2017 to August 2021 and underwent imaging via ([18F]rhPSMA-7 or Flotufolastat PET/CT with a median interval of 21 days prior to treatment (IQR: 12–34.5; range, 0–219). Patients treated at our institution were deemed eligible for therapy and discussed in an interdisciplinary tumor board. From this group, 191 consecutive patients were selected, all of whom had previously received second-line hormonal therapy and taxane-based chemotherapy. The retrospective analysis was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Technical University of Munich (permit number 2018–115) and is in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The requirement to obtain informed consent for publication of the data was waived.

PET imaging

([18F]rhPSMA-7 and Flotufolastat were synthesized according to Wurzer et al. [19]. The substance was administered to patients via an intravenous bolus, followed by an intravenous injection of diuretics (furosemide). The PET acquisition started approximately 60 min after the injection. All patients underwent examination on a Biograph mCT or a Biograph Vision scanner (Siemens Healthineers). A diagnostic CT scan was initially performed during the portal venous phase, 80 s after the intravenous injection of an iodinated contrast agent, followed by the PET scan. Each patient received a diluted oral contrast agent (300 mg of Telebrix; Guerbet). The PET scans were conducted in 3-dimensional mode, with an acquisition time of 3–4 min per bed position, or 1.1–1.5 mm/s using the flow technique. Emission data were adjusted for randoms, dead time, scatter, and attenuation and were iteratively reconstructed using ordered-subsets expectation maximization (4 iterations, 8 subsets), followed by a post-reconstruction smoothing Gaussian filter (5 mm full width at half maximum).

Image analysis and definition of PET eligibility

Metastatic patterns were assessed according to the miTNM classification [20, 21] (N1/M1a: presence of lymph node metastases; M1b: presence of bone metastases; and M1c: presence of visceral metastases), derived from ([18F]rhPSMA-7 and Flotufolastat PET imaging, for every patient.

All patients were assessed by a board-certified nuclear medicine physician (with > 5 years of experience) to determine if they met (showcased by a “ + ”) the imaging-based eligibility criteria for the BET (B) and the VISION trial (V), and stratified into four groups depending on their eligibility: 1) V + /B + , 2) V + /B-, 3) V-/B + , and 4) V-/B-. The criteria utilized for this analysis from BET and VISION are shown in Table 1. In short, VISION criteria exclude any patient who fulfills at least one of the following abnormalities with uptake ≤ liver (miPSMA score 0 or 1) in the baseline PET scan: lymph nodes ≥ 2,5 cm, solid organ/parenchymal metastases ≥ 1 cm, and/or bone metastases with soft tissue component ≥ 1 cm (Table 1).

BET criteria similarly exclude any patient with at least one of the following: lymph nodes > 1 cm with miPSMA score 0, bone metastases with negative (miPSMA score 0) soft tissue components, as well as visceral metastases > 1 cm and miPSMA score 0 or 1 (Table 1). Additionally, BET criteria exclude a patient when, based on a visual assessment by the assessing physician, the majority of lymph nodes and/or bone lesions exhibit PSMA levels ≤ liver (miPSMA score 0 or 1) (“majority criterion”) (Table 1).

[177Lu]Lu-PSMA-I&T RLT

[177Lu]Lu-PSMA-I&T was synthesized and radiolabeled as reported by Weineisen et al. [22]. The preparation of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-I&T complied with good manufacturing practices and the German Medicinal Products Act (Arzneimittelgesetz §13 2b). In total, we administered 750 cycles of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT, with a median of 4 cycles per patient (ranging from 1 to 10 cycles). Treatment was discontinued in cases of radiographic and/or clinical signs of progression and/or severe toxicity. Patients received an intravenous treat ment with an average standard activity of 7.4 GBq of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-I&T every 5 to 16 weeks (median of 6 weeks), which could be slightly adjusted based on laboratory test results and tumor burden.

Outcome measures

PSA response and PSA progression

PSA levels and their relative change between cycles were assessed. According to the Prostate Cancer Clinical Trials Working Group 3 [23], a PSA response was defined as a PSA decline of at least 50% from baseline. PSA progression was defined as either a PSA increase of at least 25% and at least 2 ng/mL above the nadir following an initial PSA decline or a PSA increase of at least 25% and at least 2 ng/mL from baseline in cases with no PSA decline.

PSA progression-free survival

PSA-PFS was defined as the time from the first cycle of RLT to the time of PSA progression or until the time of death if progression had not been observed.

Overall survival

OS was defined as the time from initiation of RLT until death from any cause. Patients who were alive or lost to follow-up were censored at the last date they were known to be alive.

Statistical analysis

Kaplan–Meier graphs and t-tests were generated using SPSS Statistics, version 29.0.2.0 (IBM Corp.) for Mac. Other statistical calculations and waterfall plots were performed and generated via Microsoft Excel for Mac, version 16.92. All statistical tests were conducted for the entire cohort and after stratification by eligibility criteria. Kaplan–Meier tests were calculated for survival analyses. A p-value < 0.05 was rated as statistically significant. Cohen’s κ calculations were performed with Microsoft Excel for Max, version 16.92, and agreement between sets of eligibility criteria was rated according to the benchmarks established by Landis & Koch, with κ < 0.0 signifying poor strength, 0.0–0.2 slight, 0.21–0.4 fair, 0.41–0.6 moderate, 0.61–0.8 substantial, and 0.81–1.0 almost perfect agreement [24].

Clinical outcomes

PSA response: PSA response and PSA-PFS could be assessed for 186/191 patients (97.4%). Out of the 186 patients, 107 (57.5%) exhibited an initial PSA decline, with 64 (34.4%) achieving a 50% reduction in PSA levels. In 77 cases (41.4%), PSA values increased under PSMA RLT; 74 patients (39.8%) exhibited 25% PSA progression. In two cases (1.0%), PSA values were stable after PSMA RLT.

In the subgroups V + /B + , V + /B-, and V-/B-, a > 50% PSA response was present in 62/157 (39.5%, 95% CI 31.8–47.6%), 2/17 (11.8%, 95% CI 1.5–36.4), and 0/13 (0%, 95% CI 0–24.7%) patients, respectively. The median time from baseline to assessment of PSA response was 73 days (IQR: 42–133; range: 0–1243 days). The median PSA change from baseline to final assessment (best response or progression) was -16% (IQR: -77.1% to 51.0%, range: -100 to + 5660%). Discussion

Certain (bio-)markers, including imaging-based ones such as PSMA-negative metastases, can predict a poorer response to [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT [25]. To provide well-suited mCRPC patients with [177Lu]Lu-PSMA therapy, there have been multiple attempts to develop ideal selection criteria prior to therapy [13, 14, 26–28], such as those from the VISION and BET trials. Considering the mixed responses to [177Lu]Lu-PSMA therapy reported in some patients, establishing optimal selection criteria is crucial to identify and offer RLT to those most likely to benefit.

The VISION trial excluded patients with metastases (visceral, bone with a soft tissue component, lymph node) expressing low PSMA levels (miPSMA score 0 or 1) from [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT [2]. The BET trial introduced more detailed criteria, such as the majority criterion, which excludes patients in whom the majority of tumor volume shows less uptake than the liver, and is somewhat more permissive regarding lymph node metastases.

In this retrospective analysis, we applied both sets of election criteria to the same group of mCRPC patients to determine whether different eligibility thresholds identified different cohorts and influenced responses to therapy with [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT.

Under VISION criteria, 174/191 patients (91.1%) were eligible, compared to 161/191 patients (84.3%) under BET. No patient in our cohort was included in BET but excluded from VISION (V-/B +). Most patients were eligible under both, which correlated with better OS and PFS outcomes, indicating that V + /B + effectively stratifies optimal candidates for [177Lu]Lu-PSMA therapy.

The VISION exclusion criterion for bone metastases specifies “bone metastasis with PSMA-negative soft tissue component (uptake ≤ liver) ≥ 1 cm”. BET’s equivalent is similar but without the size limit, thus including VISION’s criterion. For lymph nodes, BET defines them as “ > 10 mm short axis with uptake ≤ blood pool,” whereas VISION excludes lymph nodes only when “ ≥ 2.5 cm short axis with PSMA ligand uptake ≤ liver.” Despite VISION’s stricter uptake criterion, it allows patients with low-expressing lymph nodes < 2.5 cm, as their PSMA-expression is not a selection factor. BET’s “majority criterion” was designed to include patients with low-expressing lesions in principle, but restrict against a predominant pattern of low-expressing disease.

We hypothesized a case in which a lymph node metastasis would meet BET but not VISION criteria (V-/B +), concluding that it would need to be > 2.5 cm in size with low but not negative tracer uptake (> blood pool and < liver). While possible, we did not observe this in our cohort. BET’s criterion regarding visceral metastases is identical to VISION’s. Overall, both trials exclude a similar number of patients (13 for VISION, 15 for BET) from therapy (V-/B-).

Of the 13 V-/B- patients, 7/13 were excluded solely due to PSMA-negative (PSMAscore ≤ 1) visceral metastases. Considering that V-/B- had the worst survival data of the three sub-groups, the criteria that led to V-/B- might be successful for the prediction of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT-ineligibility.

The remaining differences mostly involve the “majority criterion” in BET, which excludes 15/17 V + /B- patients; notably, two such patients responded with over 50% PSA decrease (92% and 95%), respectively. The remaining two V + /B- patients had PSMA-negative lymph nodes.

Since VISION does not use the majority criterion, the 15 aforementioned patients were classified as V + , reducing the total eligible population under BET’s stricter rule. The V + /B- group showed the worst PSA-PFS but no significantly decreased OS; however, the small sample size limits the ability to draw definitive conclusions. Future prospective studies should assess whether the application of both criteria sets enhances prognostic accuracy in mCRPC patients.

In summary, BET’s criteria, particularly due to the majority criterion, are more restrictive than VISION’s within this cohort. Including the majority criterion and BET’s lymph node criterion appears to exclude patients who would not profit from RLT. The requirements regarding visceral metastases are identical between the two sets, and the absence of size restrictions within BET’s bone metastasis criterion had a limited impact compared to VISION’s.

There are several limitations to our study. It was retrospective, leading to some uncertain data, such as missing follow-up PSA values. Treatment decisions were made clinically, so minor deviations from recommended eligibility criteria were at the discretion of the treating nuclear medicine physician. Since follow-up treatments after [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT are unknown to us, they may also have impacted PSA-PFS. Additionally, the disproportionately small sizes of the V-/B- and V + /B- cohorts relative to V + /B + reduce our study’s statistical power.

Differences in tracer uptake with various diagnostic tracers should also be noted. While 72% of our patients, like those in the BET patient cohort, underwent Flotufolastat PET scans, the VISION trial used [68 Ga]Ga-PSMA-11 for eligibility assessments. Although these tracers differ slightly in aspects such as half-life and imaging resolution, they are all considered appropriate for detecting PSMA-expressing prostate cancer as per guidelines. Furthermore, differences in outcomes may be attributable to our use of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-I&T, as opposed to VISION’s use of [177Lu]Lu-PSMA-617, and BET’s use of [177Lu]Lu-rhPSMA-10.1.

Lastly, the assessment of the PET/CT scans was conducted by a single experienced nuclear medicine physician, which represents a methodological limitation as interobserver variability could not be assessed. This absence of an interobserver reliability assessment may have introduced bias in the PET eligibility classification. To reduce subjectivity and improve reproducibility, future studies should ideally employ multiple independent readers for image analysis.

Conclusion

Most patients fulfilled both BET and VISION eligibility criteria prior to [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT (V + /B +) and were associated with the best patient outcomes. Our data suggest that the criteria for BET’s phase I trial select patients more strictly for [177Lu]Lu-PSMA RLT than VISION criteria, with a mismatch in 8.9% of cases between VISION and BET (V + /B-) criteria, while overall survival did not differ significantly between the three groups. The implementation of both criteria in prospective trials will further compare BET and VISION PET-based eligibility criteria.
