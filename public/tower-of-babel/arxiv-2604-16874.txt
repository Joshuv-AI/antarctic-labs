ultracontact Algebras and Stack Systems
Luca Carai, Ivo Düntsch, Rafał Gruszczyński, Anna Laura Suarez

Abstract
. We study the class of structures that, in a way, generalize various approaches to the contact relation on Boolean algebras. Keywords : contact relation, ultracontact, Boolean algebras, Boolean contact algebras, region-based topology, spatial logic MSC: Primary 06E25, Secondary 03G05



1.
Introduction

Boolean contact algebras (BCAs) are structures meant to study the phenomenon of nearness of regions in an abstract setting
[
12
,
5
,
4
]
. As it is normally done, the standard language of Boolean algebras is expanded with a binary predicate ‘
\con
’ of
contact
and we interpret
x\con y
as the situation in which—in some sense—region
x
is in contact with region
y
. In a point-based setting, we would say that
x
and
y
share at least one point. The standard BCAs are subalgebras of the complete algebras of regular closed subsets of topological spaces. In this context,
x\con y
if
x\cap y
(the set-theoretical intersection) is non-empty. If
X
is a topological space,
\RC(X)
is its complete Boolean algebra of regular closed sets,
S
is a subalgebra of
\RC(X)
, and
x,y\in S
, then
\cap
usually is not an operation of
S
, but the operation of the power set algebra of
\RC(X)
. So the nearness of
x
and
y
is expressed by means of an operation from beyond
S
. In the abstract setting,
\con
is assumed as primitive, and the standard axioms for it are listed in Section
6
.

In this work, we aim to study an expansion of the notion of contact relations from pairs of regions to arbitrary collections. That is, we can easily imagine a ternary relation
\con_{3}
that holds among
x
,
y
, and
z
if all three regions are in contact in the sense that there is a location in space common to all three. Transferring the intuition to the point-based environment again, it reduces to the existence of at least one point that is in
x\cap y\cap z
.
The ternary relation
\con_{3}
allows one to distinguish between the two configurations shown in Figure
1
, which are indistinguishable from the point of view of binary contact relations, since each pair of regions among
x,y,z
shares a common point

x
y
z
x
y
z

Configurations that are indistinguishable by binary contact relations

This idea can be further generalized to arbitrary
n
-ary contact relations, as it was done in
[
10
]
. However, we aim at something more far-reaching, and in a way we want to study in one swoop all those possibilities, including infinite ones, by considering a family
\mathscr{K}
of subsets of a Boolean algebra
B
, such that every set
M\in\mathscr{K}
consists of regions sharing a common location, or having <<a mutual point of contact>>.
Allowing
\mathscr{K}
to contain infinite subsets of
B
makes it possible, for example, to express that the family
\{(0,\frac{1}{n}]\mid n\in\omega\}
of subsets of the real interval
(0,1]
lacks a common point of contact; a property that cannot be captured by finitary contact relations.
Thus, we propose to study structures
\mathfrak{B}\coloneqq\left\langle B,\mathscr{K}\right\rangle
where
\mathscr{K}
is a family of subsets of
B
, which we are going to call
ultracontact algebras
. In Section
9
we point to possible ways to continue our work.

Throughout the paper,
\left\langle B,+,\cdot,-,\mathbf{0},\mathbf{1}\right\rangle
will denote a non-trivial Boolean algebra, with the operations—respectively—of join, meet, and complement. On
B
we define, in the standard way, the binary order relation
\leq
.
B^{+}
is the set of all non-zero elementts of
B
. If
M\subseteq B
, then
x\in B
is a
lower bound
of
M
if for all
y\in M
,
x\leq y
. To simplify things, we write
x\leq M
in this case. Further, if
x\in B
, then

\upop x\coloneqq\{y\in B\mid x\leq y\},

and for
M\subseteq B

\upop M\coloneqq\bigcup_{x\in M}\upop x.

If
B
and
S
are Boolean algebras, then we write
S\leq B
in the case
S
is a Boolean subalgebra of
B
.
\Atom(B)
is the collection of all atoms of the algebra
B
, i.e., all its non-zero elements minimal with respect to the Boolean order
\leq
.

For any set
X
,
2^{X}
is the power set of
X
, and
2^{X}_{+}
are all non-empty subsets of
X
;
\Fin(X)
is the collection of all finite subsets of
X
.

For any unexplained notion we refer the reader to
[
9
]
for Boolean algebras and
[
1
]
for lattice theory.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the properties of stacks, filters, and grills. In Section
3
we introduce and study the relation of
support
that plays a key role in the presentation of the central concept of our work,

, which is introduced in Section
4

5

is definitionally equivalent to the purely order-theoretic notion of a
stack system
.

2.
Stacks, filters, grills

This section is a refresher on various order-related subsets of Boolean algebras.

Definition 2.1
.

A
stack
of
B
is an upward closed subset
U
of
B
w.r.t. the Boolean order. The family of stacks of
B
is denoted by
\Stack(B)
.

Proposition 2.2

(
8
, Theorem 8.2
)
.

\left\langle\Stack(B),\mathord{\cup},\mathord{\cap},\emptyset,B\right\rangle
is a complete lattice, with arbitrary unions and intersections as lattice operations and
\{\mathbf{1}\}
as its sole atom.

Definition 2.3
.

A subset
F
of
B
is called a
filter
if it satisfies

(Fil0)

\mathbf{1}\in F
.

(Fil1)

If
a\in F
and
a\leq b
, then
b\in F
.

(Fil2)

If
a,b\in F
, then
a\cdot b\in F
.

F
is called
proper
, if
\mathbf{0}\not\in F
. The collection of filters is denoted by
\Fil(B)
, and the collection of proper filters by
\Filp(B)
. A maximal element of
\Filp(B)
with respect to set inclusion is called an
ultrafilter
. We denote the collection of ultrafilters by
\Ult(B)
. The following description of ultrafilters is well known, see e.g.
[
9
, Proposition 2.15]
:

Theorem 2.4
.

For every
F\in\Filp(B)
the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

F\in\Ult(B)
.

(2)

F
is prime, i.e., for any
a,b\in B
, if
a+b\in F
, then
a\in F
or
b\in F
.

The dual notion of a filter is that of an ideal:

Definition 2.5
.

A subset
I
of
B
is an
ideal
if:

(Id0)

\mathbf{0}\in I
.

(Id1)

If
a\in I
and
b\leq a
, then
b\in I
.

(Id2)

If
a\in I
and
b\in I
, then
a+b\in I
.

An ideal is called
proper
if
\mathbf{1}\notin I
. We let
\Id(B)
be the set of all ideals of
B
, and
\Id^{\star}(B)
be the family of all proper ideals of
B
.

Theorem 2.6

(
1
, Corollary II.9.4
)
.

The structure
\left\langle\Id(B),\bigwedge,\bigvee,\{\mathbf{0}\},B\right\rangle
is a complete lattice where infimum is intersection and for
\{I_{n}:n\in J\}
,
\bigvee\{I_{n}:n\in J\}
is the intersection of all ideals containing
\bigcup\{I_{n}:n\in J\}
.

Note that the smallest ideal containing
\emptyset\neq M\subseteq B
is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\{b\in B\,\middle|\,b\leq\bigvee T\text{ for some finite }T\subseteq M\right\}.

The following concept is of major importance in the sequel:

Definition 2.7

(
3
)
.

G\subseteq B
is called a
grill
, if it is a non-empty stack and
a+b\in G
implies
a\in G
or
b\in G
. More concretely,
G
is a grill if it satisfies

(Gr0)

\mathbf{0}\notin G
.

(Gr1)

If
a\in G
and
a\leq b
, then
b\in G
.

(Gr2)

If
a+b\in G
, then either
a\in G
or
b\in G
.

The family of grills of
B
is denoted by
\Grill(B)
. There is an intimate connection between grills and ultrafilters:

Theorem 2.8

(
13
)
.

Every grill of
B
is a union of ultrafilters and every union of ultrafilters is a grill.

The following extension result is sometimes called the
Grill Lemma
:

Lemma 2.9

(Grill Lemma,
14
, Theorem 1.1
)
.

If
G
is a grill and
F
is a filter of
B
such that
F\subseteq G
, then there is an ultrafilter
U\subseteq G
such that
F\subseteq U
.

Let

F+G\coloneqq\{f+g\mid f\in F\text{ and }g\in G\}.

In other words,
F+G
is the image of the Cartesian product of
F
and
G
w.r.t. the Boolean join operation
+
. In consequence,
F+G
is non-empty iff both
F
and
G
are non-empty.

The following characterization of grills will be used further in the paper:

Proposition 2.10
.

For every
G\in 2^{B}
,
G\in\Grill(B)
iff for all
F,H\in 2^{B}
, if
F+H\subseteq G
, then
F\subseteq G
or
H\subseteq G
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose
F\nsubseteq G
and
H\nsubseteq G
, and let
f\in F\setminus G
and
h\in H\setminus G
. Then,
f+h\in F+H
by the definition, yet
f+h\notin G
, by (
Gr2
).

(
\Leftarrow
) If
x+y\in G
, then
\{x\}+\{y\}\subseteq G
, so
\{x\}\subseteq G
or
\{y\}\subseteq G
.
∎

Definition 2.11
.

x\neq\mathbf{1}
is a
meet-prime
element of a lattice, if
a\cdot b\leq x
implies that
a\leq x
or
b\leq x
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Grills are precisely the meet-prime elements of the lattice
\Stack(B)
.

Proof.

By the definition, any grill is different from
B
.

Suppose that
G
is a grill, and that
U
and
V
are stacks such that
U\nsubseteq G
and
V\nsubseteq G
. Let
u\in U
and
v\in V
be such that
u,v\notin G
. As
G
is a grill,
u+v\notin G
. But
u+v\in U\cap V
, and so
U\cap V\nsubseteq G
.

For the converse, suppose that
G\in\Stack(B)
is a meet-prime element and that
u,v\notin G
. Then,
\mathop{\uparrow}u,\mathop{\uparrow}v\nsubseteq G
. By primality,
\mathop{\uparrow}u\cap\mathop{\uparrow}v\nsubseteq G
. As
\mathop{\uparrow}u\cap\mathop{\uparrow}v=\mathop{\uparrow}(u+v)
and
G
is a stack, it must be the case that
u+v\notin G
.
∎

Grills are closely related to ideals:

Lemma 2.13

(
13
)
.

G\in\Grill(B)
if and only if
B\setminus G\in\Idp(B)
, so there is a one-to-one correspondence between grills and proper ideals.

The bijective correspondence between grills and proper ideals from the lemma above is an anti-isomorphism between
\Grill(B)
and
\Id^{*}(B)
. Then, the meet of a family of grills
\{G_{i}\mid i\in I\}
in
\Grill(B)
is the complement of the join of
\{B\setminus G_{i}\mid i\in I\}
in
\Id^{*}(B)
. However, such a meet exists only for only for those families
\{G_{i}\mid i\in I\}
, for which there exists an ultrafilter
U\subseteq\bigcap_{i\in I}G_{i}
. This implies that the family of grills can be made into a semi-lattice with arbitrary unions as suprema and partial infima given by

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{i\in I}G_{i}\coloneqq B\setminus\bigcup\left\{\mathop{\downarrow}(g_{0}+\ldots+g_{n})\,\middle|\,n\in\m@thbbch@rN\text{ and }g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}\notin\bigcap_{i\in I}G_{i}\right\}

or, equivalently, by

\bigwedge_{i\in I}G_{i}\coloneqq\left\{a\in B\,\middle|\,(\forall b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\in B)\,(a\leq b_{1}+\ldots+b_{n}\Rightarrow(\exists i\in I)\,b_{i}\in\bigcap G_{i})\right\},

which is a consequence of the correspondence we pointed to above and the fact that for ideals, the supremum is computed as

\bigvee_{i\in I}J_{i}\coloneqq\left\{a\in B\,\middle|\,(\exists b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\in\bigcup_{i\in I}J_{i})\,a\leq b_{1}+\ldots+b_{n}\right\}.

3.
The support relation

Before we formulate the axioms, we define an auxiliary relation of
support
between subsets of a Boolean algebra, and we investigate its elementary properties.

Definition 3.1
.

For
F,G\in 2^{B}
, we define
F\preceq G
iff for every
g\in G
there is
f\in F
such that
f\leq g
. In the case
F\preceq G
we say that
F

supports

G
or that
F

is a support
of
G
.
\dashv

For
B
a Boolean algebra, and
F\subseteq B
, we define

\Upop F\coloneqq\{G\subseteq B\mid F\preceq G\text{ and }G\neq\emptyset\}.

We have that for any
a\in B
and any
M\subseteq B^{+}

M\in\Upop\{a\}\iff a\leq M,

and

\bigcup_{m\in M}\Upop\{m\}\subseteq\Upop M,

but the reverse inclusion does not hold in general, as the example below shows.

Example 3.2
.

Let
x,y\in B^{+}
be such that
x\cdot y=\mathbf{0}
. Then for
M\coloneqq\{x,y\}
we have that
M\in\Upop M
, but
M\notin\Upop\{x\}\cup\Upop\{y\}
.
\dashv

Proposition 3.3
.

If
F,G,H,M
are subsets of
B
, then:

(1)

F\preceq F+G
.

(2)

F+G\preceq H
implies that
F\preceq H
and
G\preceq H
.

(3)

F\preceq H
and
G\preceq M
imply that
F+G\preceq H+M
.

(4)

F\preceq G
and
G\preceq H
imply that
F\preceq H
.

(5)

(F+G)+H=F+(G+H)
.

(6)

F\preceq G
iff
G\subseteq\upop F
.

(7)

If
F\subseteq G
, then
G\preceq F
.

(8)

F\sim G
iff
\upop F=\upop G
.

(9)

F\sim\upop F
.

(10)

\Upop F=2^{\upop F}_{+}
.

Proof.

Routine.
∎

The support relation is reflexive and transitive but not antisymmetric.

Example 3.4
.

Consider the interval algebra
B
over the reals, that is,
B
consists of
\emptyset,[0,\infty)
and those subsets of
[0,\infty)
that are finite unions of left-closed, right-open intervals
[x,y)

[
9
, Example 1.11]
. Let
F
be the set of elements of
B
with rational endpoints, and
G
the set of elements with irrational endpoints. Then,
F\cap G=\emptyset
,
F\preceq G
and
G\preceq F
.
\dashv

Example 3.5
.

For a more topological example, consider
\RC(\m@thbbch@rR)
, the Boolean algebra of regular closed subsets of the reals with the standard topology. The sets
F\coloneqq\{[-\nicefrac{{1}}{{n^{2}}},\nicefrac{{1}}{{n^{2}}}]\mid n\in\m@thbbch@rN^{+}\}
and
G\coloneqq\{[-\nicefrac{{1}}{{n^{3}}},\nicefrac{{1}}{{n^{3}}}]\mid n\in\m@thbbch@rN^{+}\}
mutually support each other, but they are clearly different sets.
\dashv

As the support relation is a pre-order, its symmetric closure

(
\mathtt{df}\,{\sim}
)

F\sim G\;\mathrel{\mathord{:}\mathord{\longleftrightarrow}}\;F\preceq G\text{ and }G\preceq F.

is an equivalence relation. If
F\sim G
, then we say that
F
and
G
are
similar
. For a subset
F
of an algebra
B
, let
[F]
be the equivalence class of
F
with respect to the similarity relation

[F]\coloneqq F/_{\sim}.

We can now extend the support relation to the equivalence classes in the obvious way

[F]\preceq[G]\;\mathrel{\mathord{:}\mathord{\longleftrightarrow}}\;F\preceq G.

The relation is well-defined, that is, if
F^{\prime}\in[F],G^{\prime}\in[G]
and
[F]\preceq[G]
, then
[F^{\prime}]\preceq[G^{\prime}]
, which is a consequence of the transitivity of the support relation.

Proposition 3.6
.

\left\langle 2^{B}/_{\sim},\preceq\right\rangle
is a lattice. The join of
[F]
and
[G]
is given by
[F+G]
, and the meet by
[F\cup G]
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a Boolean algebra, and let
F,G\subseteq B
. Every element of
F+G
is of the form
f+g
for some
f\in F
,
g\in G
, and so
f\in F
and
g\in G
are such that
f,g\leq f+g
. Then,
F,G\preceq F+G
. Suppose that
F,G\preceq H
for
H\subseteq B
. Let
h\in H
, and let
f\in F
and
g\in G
be such that
f,g\leq h
. Then,
f+g\leq h
. So,
F+G\preceq H
. Thus
F+G
is the join of
F
and
G
.

For the meet, it is clear that
F\cup G\preceq F,G
. Suppose
H\preceq F,G
. We show that
H\preceq F\cup G
. W.l.o.g., let
x
be an element of
F
. Since by assumption
H\preceq F
, in
H
there is an
h
such that
h\leq x
. So
H\preceq F\cup G
. It follows that
F\cup G
is the meet of
F
and
G
.
∎

If
x\in B
, then

\{x\}\sim\mathop{\uparrow}x

Indeed,
\{x\}\preceq\mathop{\uparrow}x
since
x\leq y
for all
y\in\mathop{\uparrow}x
. Conversely,
\mathop{\uparrow}x\preceq\{x\}
since
x\in\mathop{\uparrow}x
.

Let
[x]
be the equivalence class of
\{x\}
with respect to the similarity relation

[x]\coloneqq\{x\}/_{\mathord{\sim}}.

Lemma 3.7
.

If
x\in B
, then

[x]=\{M\subseteq B\mid x\in M\text{ and }x\leq M\}.

Proof.

(
\subseteq
) Suppose
M\preceq\{x\}
and
\{x\}\preceq M
. From the first condition, we have that there is an
m_{0}\in M
such that
m_{0}\leq x
. From the second,
x\leq M
, so in particular
x\leq m_{0}
. Therefore, by the antisymmetry of
\leq
we obtain
x=m_{0}
, i.e.,
x\in M
.

(
\supseteq
) If
x\in M
, then clearly
M\preceq\{x\}
, and if
x
is a lower bound of
M
, then
\{x\}\preceq M
. Thus
\{x\}
and
M
are similar, as desired.
∎

4.
Ultracontact Algebras

We introduce the central concept of our work.

Definition 4.1
.

An
ultracontact algebra
(UCA for short) is a pair
\left\langle B,\mathscr{K}\right\rangle
such that
B
is a Boolean algebra and
\mathscr{K}\subseteq 2^{B}
is a family—which we call
ultracontact
or, briefly, UC—that satisfies the following axioms for all
F,G\subseteq B
:

(K0)

\emptyset\notin\mathscr{K}
.

(K1)

If
\mathbf{0}\in F
, then
F\notin\mathscr{K}
.

(K2)

If
x\neq\mathbf{0}
, then
\{x\}\in\mathscr{K}
.

(K3)

If
F\preceq G\neq\emptyset
and
F\in\mathscr{K}
, then
G\in\mathscr{K}
.

(K4)

If
F+G\in\mathscr{K}
, then
F\in\mathscr{K}
or
G\in\mathscr{K}
.
\dashv

A straightforward induction on
n
shows that (
K4
) can be generalized to

(K4
′
)

\text{If }F_{1}+\ldots+F_{n}\in\mathscr{K},\text{ then }(\exists i\leqslant n)\,F_{i}\in\mathscr{K},

where

F_{1}+\ldots+F_{n}\coloneqq\{f_{1}+\ldots+f_{n}\mid(\forall i\leqslant n)\,f_{i}\in F_{i}\}.

Observe that (
K2
) may be equivalently formulated as

(K2
′
)

\Atom\left(2^{B^{+}}\right)\subseteq\mathscr{K},

and (
K3
) as

(K3
′
)

If
F\in\mathscr{K}
, then
\Upop F\subseteq\mathscr{K}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For any ultracontact algebra
\left\langle B,\mathscr{K}\right\rangle
, and any non-empty
F,G\in 2^{B}
, if
F\in\mathscr{K}
or
G\in\mathscr{K}
, then
F+G\in\mathscr{K}
.

Proof.

Indeed, if
F\neq\emptyset\neq G
, then
F+G\neq\emptyset
. Moreover,
F\preceq F+G
. So, if
F\in\mathscr{K}
, then
F+G\in\mathscr{K}
. Similarly,
G\in\mathscr{K}
implies
F+G\in\mathscr{K}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Any ultracontact
\mathscr{K}
is closed under non-empty subsets of elements
of
\mathscr{K}
. In particular,
\mathscr{K}
must be closed under non-empty intersections.

Proof.

Let
M
be an element of
\mathscr{K}
and
N
be a non-empty subset of
M
. Then
M\preceq N
, by Proposition
3.3
(7). So
N\in\mathscr{K}
by (
K3
).
∎

We show that every Boolean algebra carries at least two ultracontacts: the smallest
{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}
and the largest
{\mathscr{K}_{\max}}
. Let:

\begin{split}{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}\coloneqq{}&\{M\in 2^{B}_{+}\mid(\exists x\in B^{+})\,x\leq M\}\\
={}&\textstyle\bigcup\{\Upop\{x\}\mid x\in B^{+}\}\end{split}

and

{\mathscr{K}_{\max}}\coloneqq\{M\in 2^{B}_{+}\mid\mathbf{0}\notin M\}.

Theorem 4.4
.

\left\langle B,{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}\right\rangle
is a UCA, and for every ultracontact
\mathscr{K}
on
B
,
{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}\subseteq\mathscr{K}
.

Proof.

The axioms (
K0
)–(
K2
) are obvious.

For (
K3
), if
F
supports
G\neq\emptyset
and
F\in{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}
, then
F
has a non-zero lower bound
x
. But
x\leq G
as well, as any lower bound of
F
must be a lower bound of
G
.

For (
K4
), if neither
F
nor
G
has a non-zero lower bound, so
\bigwedge F=\mathbf{0}=\bigwedge G
. By
[
9
, Lemma 1.33]
it must be the case that
\bigwedge(F+G)=\mathbf{0}
.

For the second part, let
\mathscr{K}
be an arbitrary ultracontact on
B
and let
M\in{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}
. So,
M\neq\emptyset
and
M
has a lower bound
x\in B^{+}
. Then
\{x\}
supports
M
. Further, by (

For the second part, let

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\left\langle B,\mathscr{K}\right\rangle
be an ultracontact algebra and

. Then,

Proof.

The right-to-left implication is a special case of Theorem

. For the other direction, assume that

is an ultracontact but

is not a grill. Since

, from Lemma

we obtain that

is a stack. As

and

\bigwedge M=\mathbf{0}
. Therefore, by Theorem

there are ultracontacts

and
\mathscr{K}_{2}

Adding

for a grill

. Trivially,

. But there are more interesting cases.

Example 5.8
.

Consider the algebra
\FC(\omega)
of all finite and co-finite subsets of the set of natural numbers and its only free ultrafilter

, the Fréchet filter. Let
\mathscr{Z}\coloneqq\{Z\subseteq U\mid\bigwedge Z=\mathbf{0}\}

and thus

In general, if

Generalizing a bit further, for any grill

Proposition 5.9
.

If

It is enough to observe that for every

contains only such sets that have a non-zero lower bound, because every such

Lemma 5.10

If

Theorem 5.12
.

In general, the set of UCs on a Boolean algebra

Proof by example.

Consider the sixteen-element algebra

whose atoms are

6.
Contact relations from UCs

A
contact relation

\con
on a Boolean algebra
\left\langle B,\cdot,+,\mathbf{0},\mathbf{1}\right\rangle
is a binary relation
\mathord{\con}\subseteq B\times B
that satisfies the following axioms (
\mathrel{\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 3.19444pt\hskip-3.19444pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\con$}\hskip-3.19444pt\hskip-2.62497pt\raisebox{0.0pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{{/}}$}}\hskip-2.62497pt\hskip 3.19444pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 3.19444pt\hskip-3.19444pt\hbox{$\textstyle\con$}\hskip-3.19444pt\hskip-2.62497pt\raisebox{0.0pt}{\hbox{$\textstyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{{/}}$}}\hskip-2.62497pt\hskip 3.19444pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 2.23611pt\hskip-2.23611pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\con$}\hskip-2.23611pt\hskip-1.83748pt\raisebox{0.0pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{{/}}$}}\hskip-1.83748pt\hskip 2.23611pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 1.59721pt\hskip-1.59721pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\con$}\hskip-1.59721pt\hskip-1.31248pt\raisebox{0.0pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{{/}}$}}\hskip-1.31248pt\hskip 1.59721pt}}}
is the set-theoretical complement of
\con
):

(C0)

\displaystyle(\forall x\in B)\,\mathbf{0}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 3.19444pt\hskip-3.19444pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\con$}\hskip-3.19444pt\hskip-2.62497pt\raisebox{0.0pt}{\hbox{$\displaystyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{{/}}$}}\hskip-2.62497pt\hskip 3.19444pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 3.19444pt\hskip-3.19444pt\hbox{$\textstyle\con$}\hskip-3.19444pt\hskip-2.62497pt\raisebox{0.0pt}{\hbox{$\textstyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{{/}}$}}\hskip-2.62497pt\hskip 3.19444pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 2.23611pt\hskip-2.23611pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\con$}\hskip-2.23611pt\hskip-1.83748pt\raisebox{0.0pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{{/}}$}}\hskip-1.83748pt\hskip 2.23611pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 1.59721pt\hskip-1.59721pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\con$}\hskip-1.59721pt\hskip-1.31248pt\raisebox{0.0pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\raisebox{0.4pt}{{/}}$}}\hskip-1.31248pt\hskip 1.59721pt}}}x,

(C1)

\displaystyle(\forall x\in B)\,(x\neq\mathbf{0}\rightarrow x\con x),

(C2)

\displaystyle(\forall x,y\in B)\,(x\con y\rightarrow y\con x),

(C3)

\displaystyle(\forall x,y,z\in B)\,(x\con y\wedge y\leq z\rightarrow x\con z),

(C4)

\displaystyle(\forall x,y,z\in B)\,(x\con y+z\rightarrow x\con y\vee x\con z).

A
Boolean contact algebra
is a pair
\left\langle B,\con\right\rangle
such that
B
is a BA, and
\con
is a contact relation. In the case
x\con y
we say that
x

is in contact with

y
. Since the axioms (
C0
)–(
C4
) are universal, each restriction of
\con
to a subalgebra of
B
is also a contact relation.

Each UCA
\left\langle B,\mathscr{K}\right\rangle
gives rise to the contact relation determined by
\mathscr{K}
in a natural way, by taking the two-element subsets of
\mathscr{K}

(
\mathtt{df}\,{\con_{\mathscr{K}}}
)

x\con_{\mathscr{K}}y\;\mathrel{\mathord{:}\mathord{\longleftrightarrow}}\;\{x,y\}\in\mathscr{K}.

Theorem 6.1
.

If
B
is a Boolean algebra and
\mathscr{K}
is an ultracontact, then
\con_{\mathscr{K}}
is a contact relation.

Proof.

We check all the axioms for contact.

(
C0
) This holds as any pair of the form
\{\mathbf{0},x\}
cannot be in
\mathscr{K}
by (
K1
).

(
C1
) By (
K2
), if
x\neq\mathbf{0}
, then
\{x\}\in\mathscr{K}
, so
x\con_{\mathscr{K}}x
.

(
C2
) Immediate.

(
C3
) Suppose that
x\con_{\mathscr{K}}y
and
y\leq z
. Clearly,
\{x,y\}\preceq\{x,z\}
, and so
\{x,z\}\in\mathscr{K}
, as desired.

(
C4
) Suppose that
\{x,x_{1}+x_{2}\}\in\mathscr{K}
. We have

\{x,x_{1}+x_{2}\}\preceq\{x,x+x_{1},x+x_{2},x_{1}+x_{2}\}.

Then, by (
K3
),
\{x,x+x_{1},x+x_{2},x_{1}+x_{2}\}\in\mathscr{K}
. But

\{x,x+x_{1},x+x_{2},x_{1}+x_{2}\}=\{x,x_{1}\}+\{x,x_{2}\},

so, by (

\{x,x_{1}\}\in\mathscr{K}
or
\{x,x_{2}\}\in\mathscr{K}
.
∎

Theorem
4.4
can be interpreted as the counterpart of the fact that the overlap relation is the smallest contact relation, i.e., the smallest relation that satisfies axioms (

)–(

in the sense of (

For the smallest UC on B we have

Example 6.3
.

Let us show that there are different ultracontacts that induce the same contact relation. To this end, we proceed with the 8-element algebra
B
with atoms
\{a,b,c\}
. We start with an observation that the minimal contact of
B
is

{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}=2^{B}_{+}\setminus\{\{a,b\},\{a,c\},\{b,c\},\{a,b,c\},\{a,b+c\},\{b,a+c\},\{c,a+b\}\}.

The algebra has three ultrafilters:
\upop a
,
\upop b
, and
\upop c

\Grill(B)=\{\upop a,\upop b,\upop c,\upop a\cup\upop b,\upop a\cup\upop c,\upop b\cup\upop c,\upop a\cup\upop b\cup\upop c\}.

We have

non-empty subsets of the set of grills, but by Proposition
5.9
not all of them give rise to ultracontacts that differ from
{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}
. By the same corollary, the two sets below (and others that contain ultrafilters of

\{\upop a\cup\upop b\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{\upop a,\upop a\cup\upop b\}

will result in the same
\mathscr{K}
. It is easy to see that the following are the minimal UCs on
B
extending
{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}_{a,b}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}\cup 2^{\upop a\cup\upop b}_{+},

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}_{a,c}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}\cup 2^{\upop a\cup\upop c}_{+},

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}_{b,c}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}\cup 2^{\upop b\cup\upop c}_{+}.

In the case of
\mathscr{K}_{a,b}
, since

Similar observations can be made about

and their corresponding contact relations. We can see that these are three minimal UCs on

{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}

Different UCs on

that generate the same contact relation are

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}\cup 2^{\upop a\cup\upop b}_{+}\cup 2^{\upop a\cup\upop c}_{+}\cup 2^{\upop b\cup\upop c}_{+}\quad\text{and}\quad

Both generate the full contact relation on

all triples of regions <<share a point>>, in particular there is a <<point>> of contact of

, and

(which is not the case for

).

Let us encapsulate the above in the following theorem.

.

There exists a Boolean algebra

and different UCs

Let us make one more observation that emphasizes the specific nature of the approach to nearness of regions presented in this paper. Given a BCA
\left\langle B,\con\right\rangle
by a

we understand a non-empty set

such that

. Let

be the family of all such cliques. Then,

) by definition, (

The bottom line is that ultracontact does not axiomatize cliques in an abstract setting, but a different phenomenon, that may be compared to a situation in which we have a set of “spokes” that all meet in a common locus (rather than a set of spokes each of which connects a pair of loci in a space).

.

\left\langle B,\con\right\rangle

6.1.
Hypercontact in the framework of UCA

The motivation somewhat similar to ours is a driving factor behind the work of Lipparini
[
10
]
, where the author generalizes contact relations to finite collections of elements sharing <<a point>> of contact. Lipparini introduces the notion of a hypercontact lattice, but we limit ourselves to considering his concept in the setting of Boolean algebras.

Definition 6.7
.

A pair
\left\langle B,\Delta\right\rangle
is a
hypercontact
algebra if
B
is a Boolean algebra, and
\Delta
is a collection of finite subsets of
B
such that

(H1)

If
\mathbf{0}\in F
, then
F\notin\Delta
.

(H2)

If
x\neq\mathbf{0}
, then
\{x\}\in\Delta
.

(H3)

If
F\in\Delta
and
G\subseteq F
, then
G\in\Delta
.

(H4)

If
F\in\Delta
and there is an
x\in F
such
x\leq y
, then
F\cup\{y\}\in\Delta
.

(H5)

If
F\cup\{x+y\}\in\Delta
, then
F\cup\{x\}\in\Delta
or
F\cup\{y\}\in\Delta
.

\dashv

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
\left\langle B,\mathscr{K}\right\rangle
be an ultracontact algebra. For

\Delta_{\mathscr{K}}\coloneqq\{F\in\mathscr{K}\mid F\text{ is finite}\}\cup\{\emptyset\},

the pair
\left\langle B,\Delta_{\mathscr{K}}\right\rangle
is a hypercontact algebra.

Proof.

Axioms (
H1
) and (
H2
) are obvious.

For (
H3
), suppose that
F\in\Delta_{\mathscr{K}}
, and that
G\subseteq F
. If
F
is empty, then
G
is empty, and so
G\in\Delta_{\mathscr{K}}
. If
F\neq\emptyset
, then for any non-empty
G\subseteq F
, we have that
F\preceq G
and so
G\in\mathscr{K}
by (
K3
). Since
G
is finite,
G\in\Delta_{\mathscr{K}}
.

For (
H4
), suppose that
F\in\Delta_{\mathscr{K}}
and there is an
x\in F
such
x\leq y
. Then,
F\preceq F\cup\{y\}
. Thus,
F\cup\{y\}\in\mathscr{K}
by (
K3
), and as
F\cup\{y\}
is finite
F\cup\{y\}\in\Delta_{\mathscr{K}}
.

For (
H5
), assume that
F\cup\{x+y\}\in\Delta_{\mathscr{K}}
. Observe that
F\cup\{x+y\}\preceq(F\cup\{x\})+(F\cup\{y\})
. Then, by (
K3
), the right-hand side is in
\mathscr{K}
. By (
K4
),
F\cup\{x\}\in\mathscr{K}
or
F\cup\{y\}\in\mathscr{K}
. As both sets are finite, one of them is in
\Delta_{\mathscr{K}}
.
∎

To conclude, the results from this section imply that the approach put forward by us generalizes (
mutatis mutandis
) various approaches to the nearness of regions known in the literature.

7.
Finite ultracontact algebras and simplicial complexes

We call an ultracontact algebra
\left\langle B,\mathscr{K}\right\rangle

finite
when
B
is finite. Below, we study the properties of such algebras, thanks to which we may put our work in a larger perspective by revealing a connection between ultracontacts and abstract simplicial complexes.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\left\langle B,\mathscr{K}\right\rangle
be a finite ultracontact algebra and
F\subseteq B
be non-empty. Then
F\in\mathscr{K}
if and only if there exists
G\subseteq\Atom(B)
such that
G\preceq F
and
G\in\mathscr{K}
.

Proof.

The implication from left to right is an immediate consequence of (
K3
). To establish the other one, consider
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}\in\mathscr{K}
. We need to prove that there exist
b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\in\Atom(B)
such that
\{b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\}\in\mathscr{K}
and
b_{i}\leq a_{i}
for every
i\leq n
. To this end, it is sufficient to show that for every
i\leqslant n
there is a
b_{i}\in\Atom(B)
such that
b_{i}\leq a_{i}
and
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{i-1},b_{i},a_{i+1},\dots,a_{n}\}\in\mathscr{K}
. Without loss of generality, we prove it for
i=1
. Since
B
is finite, there exist
c_{1},\dots,c_{m}\in\Atom(B)
for which
a_{1}=c_{1}+\ldots+c_{m}
. Observe that

(7.1)

\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}\preceq\{c_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\}+\ldots+\{c_{m},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\}.

Indeed, every element
d
in the set of joins above either is the join of a collection that includes
a_{i}
for some
i\leq n
, or not, in which case
d=c_{1}+\ldots+c_{m}
, and then
a_{1}\leqslant d
.
This establishes (
7.1
). As
\mathscr{K}
is an ultracontact, (
7.1
) together with (
K3
) and (
K4′
) yield that there exists
c_{j}
for some
j\leqslant m
such that
\{c_{j},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\}\in\mathscr{K}
. It is then sufficient to pick
b_{1}\coloneqq c_{j}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

The lemma lets us point to a connection of the central concept of this paper to the concept of
simplicial complex
known from algebraic topology. Let us recall the definition.

Definition 7.2

(
11
, p. 108
)
.

A
abstract simplicial complex
is a pair
\left\langle V,\Sigma\right\rangle
such that
V
is a set whose elements are called
vertices
and
\Sigma
is a family of finite non-empty subsets of
V
, the elements of
\Sigma
are called
simplexes
and satisfy the following two conditions:

(SC1)

every singleton subset of
V
is in
\Sigma
(for every
v\in V
,
\{v\}
is a simplex),

(SC2)

If
S\in\Sigma
and
\emptyset\neq T\subseteq S
, then
T\in\Sigma
(
\Sigma
is closed under non-empty subsets, i.e., every non-empty subset of a simplex is a simplex).

Given a set of vertices
V
, by
\SC(V)
we mean the collection of all simplexes on
V
, i.e., all those
\Sigma\subseteq V
for which
\left\langle V,\Sigma\right\rangle
is an abstract simplicial complex.
\dashv

Lemma 7.3
.

If
B
is a finite algebra, then for every
\mathscr{K}\in\UC(B)
,
\mathscr{K}\cap 2^{\Atom(B)}_{+}\in\SC(\Atom(B))
.

Proof.

(SC1) Let
a\in\Atom(B)
. Then
a\neq\mathbf{0}
, and so
\{a\}\in\mathscr{K}
by (
K2
). Therefore,
\mathscr{K}\cap 2^{\Atom(B)}_{+}
contains all the singletons.

(SC2) Consider
F,G\in 2^{\Atom(B)}_{+}
such that
F\subseteq G
and
G\in\mathscr{K}
. Then
G\preceq F
by Proposition
3.3
(7) and (
K3
) we obtain that
F\in\mathscr{K}
. So,
\mathscr{K}\cap 2^{\Atom(B)}_{+}
is closed for non-empty subsets.
∎

Theorem 7.4
.

If
B
is a finite algebra, then the mapping

\sigma\colon\UC(B)\to\SC(\Atom(B))\quad\text{such that}\quad\sigma(\mathscr{K})\coloneqq\mathscr{K}\cap 2^{\Atom(B)}_{+}

is an order isomorphism (w.r.t. the set-theoretical inclusion).

Proof.

Thanks to Lemma
7.3
,
\sigma
is well-defined, and it is obvious that it preserves inclusions. In order to show that it also reflects inclusions, suppose that
\mathscr{K}
and
\mathscr{K}^{\prime}
are two ultracontacts on
B
such that
\mathscr{K}\cap 2^{\Atom(B)}_{+}\subseteq\mathscr{K}^{\prime}\cap 2^{\Atom(B)}_{+}
. Consider
F\in\mathscr{K}
. Then Lemma
7.1
yields
G\subseteq\Atom(B)
such that
G\preceq F
and
G\in\mathscr{K}
. By (
K0
),
F\neq\emptyset
, and hence
G\preceq F
implies that
G\neq\emptyset
as well. So,
G\in\mathscr{K}\cap 2^{\Atom(B)}_{+}
. From
\mathscr{K}\cap 2^{\Atom(B)}_{+}\subseteq\mathscr{K}^{\prime}\cap 2^{\Atom(B)}_{+}
it follows that
G\in\mathscr{K}^{\prime}
. Since
\mathscr{K}^{\prime}
is an ultracontact and
G\preceq F
, we obtain that
F\in\mathscr{K}^{\prime}
by (
K3
). This shows that
\mathscr{K}\subseteq\mathscr{K}^{\prime}
.

Since the assignment reflects inclusions, it is injective. It remains to show that it is also surjective. Let
\left\langle\Atom(B),\Sigma\right\rangle
be an abstract simplicial complex. Define
\mathscr{K}\subseteq 2^{B}_{+}
by setting

\mathscr{K}\coloneqq\{F\in 2^{B}_{+}\mid\text{there exists }H\in\Sigma\text{ with }H\preceq F\}.

We need to prove that
\mathscr{K}
is an ultracontact and
\sigma(\mathscr{K})=\Sigma
. Let
F,G\subseteq B
.

(
K0
) holds for
\mathscr{K}
by the definition.

(
K1
) For the sake of contradiction, suppose that
F\in\mathscr{K}
and
\mathbf{0}\in F
. Then there exists
H\in\Sigma
with
H\preceq F
. So,
\mathbf{0}\in H
, which is not possible because
H\subseteq\Atom(B)
.

(
K2
) Let
x\in B
. Since
B
is finite, there exists
a\in\Atom(B)
such that
a\leq x
. So,
\{a\}\preceq\{x\}
and
\{a\}\in\Sigma
because
\Sigma
contains all singletons of
\Atom(B)
. The definition of
\mathscr{K}
implies that
\{x\}\in\mathscr{K}
.

(
K3
) Assume that
F\preceq G\neq\emptyset
and
F\in\mathscr{K}
. Since
F\in\mathscr{K}
, there exists
H\in\Sigma
with
H\preceq F
. Since
H\preceq F
and
F\preceq G
, Proposition
3.3
(4) yields that
H\preceq G
.

(
K4
) We prove this axiom by contraposition assuming that
F,G\notin\mathscr{K}
. Let
H\in\Sigma
. Since
F,G\notin\mathscr{K}
, the definition of
\mathscr{K}
implies that
H\npreceq F
and
H\npreceq G
. Therefore, there exist
f\in F
and
g\in G
such that
a\nleq f
and
a\nleq g
for every
a\in H
. Since
H\subseteq\Atom(B)
, we obtain that
a\nleq f+g
for every
a\in H
. We conclude that
H\npreceq F+G
. Since
H
is an arbitrary element of
\Sigma
, it follows that
F+G\notin\mathscr{K}
.

Thus, we know that
\mathscr{K}
is an ultracontact on
B
. It remains to prove that
\sigma(\mathscr{K})=\Sigma
. As
H\preceq H
for every
H\in\Sigma
, the inclusion from right to left is immediate. To prove the other inclusion, consider
F\in\sigma(\mathscr{K})\coloneqq\mathscr{K}\cap 2^{\Atom(B)}_{+}
. Then
F\in 2^{\Atom(B)}_{+}
and there exists
H\in\Sigma
such that
H\preceq F
. So, for every
f\in F
there exists
h\in H
such that
h\leq f
. Since
H,F\subseteq\Atom(B)
, we have that
h\leq f
implies
h=f
for every
h\in H
and
f\in F
. Thus,
F\subseteq H
. As
\Sigma
is closed under non-empty subsets, we obtain that
F\in\Sigma
. This establishes the desired inclusion. Hence,
\sigma(\mathscr{K})=\Sigma
.

This ends the proof of Theorem
7.4
.
∎

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
B
be a finite Boolean algebra. If two ultracontacts
\mathscr{K}_{1}
and
\mathscr{K}_{2}
on
B
coincide on
2^{\Atom(B)}
, then
\mathscr{K}_{1}=\mathscr{K}_{2}
.

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
\left\langle B,\con\right\rangle
be a finite Boolean contact algebra and
x,y\in B
. Then
x\con y
iff there exist
a,b\in\Atom(B)
such that
\{a,b\}\preceq\{x,y\}
and
a\con b
.

Proof.

Since the algebra is finite and
x,y\in B
, there are atoms
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
such that
x=a_{1}+\ldots+a_{k}
and
y=b_{1}+\ldots+b_{n}
. If
x\con y
, then
a_{1}+\ldots a_{k}\con b_{1}+\ldots+b_{n}
. By (
C2
) and (
C4
) we may reduce the left-hand side to an
a_{i}
with
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
, and the right-hand side to
b_{j}
with
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
such that
a_{i}\con b_{j}
. Clearly,
\{a_{i},b_{j}\}\preceq\{x,y\}
. The other direction is a direct consequence of (
C2
) and (
C3
).
∎

Proposition 7.7
.

If
\left\langle B,\mathscr{K}\right\rangle
is a finite ultracontact algebra, then:

(1)

x\con_{\mathscr{K}}y
iff there exist
a,b\in\Atom(B)
such that
\{a,b\}\preceq\{x,y\}
and
\{a,b\}\in\sigma(\mathscr{K})
.

(2)

Let
\con
be a contact on
B
. Then
\con=\con_{\mathscr{K}}
iff for all
a,b\in\Atom(B)
we have

a\con b\iff\{a,b\}\in\sigma(\mathscr{K}).

(3)

For all
\mathscr{K}^{\prime}\in\UC(B)
,
\con_{\mathscr{K}}=\con_{\mathscr{K}^{\prime}}
iff
\sigma(\mathscr{K})
and
\sigma(\mathscr{K}^{\prime})
contain the same
2
-element sets.

Proof.

(
1
) Since
\con_{\mathscr{K}}
is a contact, Lemma
7.6
implies that
x\con_{\mathscr{K}}y
iff there exist
a,b\in\Atom(B)
such that
\{a,b\}\preceq\{x,y\}
and
a\con_{\mathscr{K}}b
. By the definitions of
\con_{\mathscr{K}}
and
\sigma(\mathscr{K})
, the latter condition is equivalent to the existence of
a,b\in\Atom(B)
such that
\{a,b\}\preceq\{x,y\}
and
\{a,b\}\in\sigma(\mathscr{K})
.

(
2
) Suppose that
\con=\con_{\mathscr{K}}
and let
a,b\in\Atom(B)
. Then
a\con b
iff
a\con_{\mathscr{K}}b
. The definitions of
\con_{\mathscr{K}}
and
\sigma(\mathscr{K})
imply that
a\con_{\mathscr{K}}b
iff
\{a,b\}\in\sigma(\mathscr{K})
. So,
a\con b
iff
\{a,b\}\in\sigma(\mathscr{K})
.
The converse implication is an immediate consequence of (
1
) and Lemma
7.6
.

(
3
) It follows from (
1
) that
\con_{\mathscr{K}}=\con_{\mathscr{K}^{\prime}}
iff
\sigma(\mathscr{K})
and
\sigma(\mathscr{K}^{\prime})
contain the same sets of cardinality at most

\sigma(\mathscr{K})

contain all the singletons in

3
) holds.
∎

8.
Stack systems

In this section, we introduce the notion of a
stack system
and a
stack algebra
that are definitionally equivalent to ultracontact and ultracontact algebras, and offer a different perspective on the general phenomenon we study.

Definition 8.1
.

We say that
\mathscr{S}\subseteq\Stack(B)
is a
stack system
if the following axioms hold for all
U,V\in\Stack(B)
and all
x\in B
:

(SS0)

\emptyset\notin\mathscr{S}
.

(SS1)

B\notin\mathscr{S}
.

(SS2)

If
x\neq\mathbf{0}
, then
\upop x\in\mathscr{S}
.

(SS3)

If
\emptyset\neq U\subseteq V
and
V\in\mathscr{S}
, then
U\in\mathscr{S}
.

(SS4)

If
U\cap V\in\mathscr{S}
, then
U\in\mathscr{S}
or
V\in\mathscr{S}
.

A
stack algebra
is a pair
\left\langle B,\mathscr{S}\right\rangle
such that
B
is a Boolean algebra and
\mathscr{S}
is a stack system on
B
.
\dashv

We aim to show that ultracontact algebras and stack algebras are definitionally equivalent structures.

Lemma 8.2
.

For any UCA
\left\langle B,\mathscr{K}\right\rangle
, if
F\in\mathscr{K}
, then
\mathop{\uparrow}F\in\mathscr{K}
.

Proof.

Let
F\in\mathscr{K}
, which in particular means that
F\neq\emptyset
. Then
\upop F\neq\emptyset
, and since
\upop F\subseteq\upop F
, by Proposition
3.3
(6),
F\preceq\upop F
. Then, by (
K3
) we obtain that
\upop F\in\mathscr{K}
.
∎

For an ultracontact
\mathscr{K}
, define

(
\mathrm{df}\,\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
)

\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}\coloneqq\{\upop F\mid F\in\mathscr{K}\},

and for a stack system
\mathscr{S}
we put

(
\mathrm{df}\,\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
)

\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}\coloneqq\{F\subseteq B\mid\upop F\in\mathscr{S}\}.

Proposition 8.3
.

For any Boolean algebra
B
,

(1)

\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}\subseteq\mathscr{K}
for every ultracontact
\mathscr{K}
.

(2)

\mathscr{S}\subseteq\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
for every stack system
\mathscr{S}
.

Proof.

(1) If
\mathscr{K}
is an ultracontact, and
\mathop{\uparrow}F\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
, for some
F\in\mathscr{K}
, then
\upop F\in\mathscr{K}
by Lemma
8.2
(2).

(2) Let
F\in\mathscr{S}
. Then,
F=\mathop{\uparrow}F
, since
F
is a stack. So
F\in\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
.
∎

Lemma 8.4
.

For any
F,G\in 2^{B}
,
\upop(F+G)=\upop F\cap\upop G
.

Proof.

(\subseteq)
If
x\in\upop(F+G)
, then there are
f\in F
and
g\in G
such that
f+g\leq x
. Thus
f\leq x
and
g\leq x
, and so
x\in\upop F\cap\upop G
.

(\supseteq)
If
x\in\upop F\cap\upop G
, then there are
f\in F
and
g\in G
such that
f\leq x
and
g\leq x
, and so
f+g\leq x
. Thus
x\in\upop(F+G)
.
∎

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
B
be a Boolean algebra.

(1)

\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
is a stack system for every ultracontact
\mathscr{K}
.

(2)

\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
is an ultracontact for every stack system
\mathscr{S}
.

Proof.

(1) Suppose that
\mathscr{K}
is an ultracontact. We will show that
\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
is a stack system.

(
SS0
) If
\emptyset\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
, then for some
F\in\mathscr{K}
,
\emptyset=\upop F
. But this means that
F=\emptyset
, which contradicts (
K0
).

(
SS1
) If
\mathop{\uparrow}F=B
for some
F\in\mathscr{K}
, then
\mathbf{0}\in F
, contradicting (
K1
).

(
SS2
) If
x\neq\mathbf{0}
, then
\{x\}\in\mathscr{K}
by (
K2
), and thus
\mathop{\uparrow}x\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
.

(
SS3
) Let
U,V\in\Stack(B),V\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
, and
\emptyset\neq U\subseteq V
. By definition of
\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
there is some
F\in\mathscr{K}
such that
\mathop{\uparrow}F=V
. By Proposition
3.3
(6)
F\preceq U
, and (
K3
) implies
U\in\mathscr{K}
. Now,
U=\mathop{\uparrow}U
shows that
U\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
.

(
SS4
) Let
U,V\in\Stack(B)
and
U\cap V\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
. By Lemma
5.3
,
U+V\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
, so there is an
M\in\mathscr{K}
such that
U+V=\upop M
, and in consequence
M\preceq U+V
by Proposition
3.3
(6). By (
K3
),
U+V\in\mathscr{K}
, and by (
K4
), either
U
or
V
is in
\mathscr{K}
. If it is
U
, then
\mathop{\uparrow}U\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
, and since
U=\mathop{\uparrow}U
, we obtain
U\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
. If it is
V
, then similarly,
V\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
.

(2) Suppose that
\mathscr{S}
is stack system. We will show that
\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
is an ultracontact.

(
K0
) Since
\emptyset\not\in\mathscr{S}
by (
SS0
), we also have
\emptyset\notin\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
.

(
K1
) Suppose that
\mathbf{0}\in F\subseteq B
. Then
\mathop{\uparrow}F=B\not\in\mathscr{S}
which implies
F\not\in\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
.

(
K2
) If
x\neq\mathbf{0}
, then
\mathop{\uparrow}x\in\mathscr{S}
by (
SS2
) which implies
\{x\}\in\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
.

(
K3
) Let
F\in\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}},G\neq\emptyset
, and
F\preceq G
. By Proposition
3.3
(6) we have
G\subseteq\mathop{\uparrow}F
. Then,
\mathop{\uparrow}G\subseteq\mathop{\uparrow}F
, and
\mathop{\uparrow}F\in\mathscr{S}
since
F\in\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
. Now, (
SS3
) implies that
\mathop{\uparrow}G\in\mathscr{S}
, and therefore,
G\in\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
.

(
K4
) Let
F+G\in\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
. Then,
\mathop{\uparrow}(F+G)\in\mathscr{S}
by definition of
\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
, and therefore,
\mathop{\uparrow}F\cap\mathop{\uparrow}G\in\mathscr{S}
by Lemma
8.4
. It follows from (
SS4
) that either
\mathop{\uparrow}F\in\mathscr{S}
which implies
F\in\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
, or
\mathop{\uparrow}G\in\mathscr{S}
which implies
G\in\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}

∎

Theorem 8.6
.

The mappings
\mathscr{K}\mapsto\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
and
\mathscr{S}\mapsto\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
are mutual inverses, that is
\mathscr{K}=\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}}
and
\mathscr{S}=\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}}
, so for any Boolean algebra
B
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between ultracontacts and stack systems on
B
.

Proof.

First, let us check
\mathscr{K}=\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}}
for every ultracontact
\mathscr{K}
. The inclusion
\mathscr{K}\subseteq\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}}
is clear, since if
F\in\mathscr{K}
then
\mathop{\uparrow}F\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
, by definition, hence
F\in\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}}
. For the other inclusion, suppose that
F\subseteq B
is such that
\mathop{\uparrow}F\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}}
. Note that
F\neq\emptyset
as
\mathop{\uparrow}\emptyset=\emptyset
and
\emptyset\notin\Stack(B)
. This means that there is
G\in\mathscr{K}
with
\mathop{\uparrow}F=\mathop{\uparrow}G
. In particular,
F\subseteq\mathop{\uparrow}G
, that is
G\preceq F
by Proposition
3.3
(6), and so
F\in\mathscr{K}
by (
K3
).

Let us now check that
\mathscr{S}=\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}}
, for all stack systems
\mathscr{S}
. If
U\in\mathscr{S}
then
U\in\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
by Proposition
8.3
(2), and it follows that
\mathop{\uparrow}U=U\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}}
. Conversely, let
U\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}}
. Then
U=\mathop{\uparrow}F
for some
F\in\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
. As
F\in\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}}
, it follows that
\mathop{\uparrow}F=U\in\mathscr{S}
.
∎

Given a Boolean algebra
B
, let
\STS(B)
be the poset of all stack systems on
B
ordered by the set-theoretical inclusion. From the definitions, for any ultracontacts
\mathscr{K}_{1}
,
\mathscr{K}_{2}
, and any stack systems
\mathscr{S}_{1}
,
\mathscr{S}_{2}
the following equivalences hold

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}_{1}\subseteq\mathscr{K}_{2}\iff\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}_{1}}\subseteq\mathscr{S}_{\mathscr{K}_{2}},

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{1}\subseteq\mathscr{S}_{2}\iff\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}_{1}}\subseteq\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{S}_{2}}.

Moreover, by Theorem
8.6
, we know that there is a bijective correspondence between ultracontacts and stack systems on
B
. This implies that
\STS(B)
is a complete lattice. In particular,
\mathscr{S}_{{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}}
and
\mathscr{S}_{{\mathscr{K}_{\max}}}
are the zero
\mathscr{S}_{\min}
and the unity
\mathscr{S}_{\max}
of
\STS(B)
. We can describe precisely what they are.

Proposition 8.7
.

For every Boolean algebra
B

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{\min}

\displaystyle{}=\{M\in\Stack(B)\mid M\subseteq\mathop{\uparrow}x,\text{ for some }x\in B^{+}\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{\max}

\displaystyle{}=\Stack(B)\setminus\{\emptyset\}

are—respectively—the zero and the unity of
\STS(B)
.

Proof.

We have that

{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}=\{M\subseteq B\mid\emptyset\neq M\subseteq\upop x,\text{ for some }x\in B^{+}\}.

By the definition,
\mathscr{S}_{{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}}\coloneqq\{\upop F\mid F\in{\mathscr{K}_{\min}}\}
. So, the first equality follows by the order isomorphisms of the lattices of stack systems and ultracontacts. For the second one, recall that
{\mathscr{K}_{\max}}=2^{B}_{+}
, so by the definition of
\mathscr{S}_{{\mathscr{K}_{\max}}}
we obtain that it consists of all non-empty stacks, and again, the second equality from the proposition follows from the order isomorphism of the two lattices.
∎
