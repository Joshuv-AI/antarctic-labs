Risk and mediation analysis of early β-blocker use on survival outcomes in sepsis patients and new-onset atrial fibrillation: an analysis of the MIMIC-IV database
Authors: Tingting Xie, Yi Yu, Linbao Sheng, Li Li, Jian Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNew-onset atrial fibrillation (NOAF) frequently occurs in patients with sepsis and is associated with poor prognosis, but evidence regarding the prognostic impact and potential mechanisms of early β-blocker therapy remains limited. This study explores the relation of early β-blocker administration to adverse survival outcomes in sepsis patients complicated by NOAF and the mediating role of the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study included 2008–2022 Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care IV (MIMIC-IV) patients satisfying the Sepsis-3 criteria and developing NOAF after the first intensive care unit (ICU) entry. Exposure was β-blocker use within 24 hours after entering the ICU. The primary and secondary outcomes were 30- and 90-day all-cause mortality (ACM). Baseline characteristics were balanced via propensity score matching (PSM) (1:1). Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival analysis and Cox proportional hazards models were utilized to assess associations. The mediating effect of the SOFA score was quantified via mediation analysis.ResultsAfter PSM, 177 patients receiving early β-blocker therapy were matched with 177 patients who did not, achieving adequate covariate balance. Cox proportional hazards modeling demonstrated a significant association between early β-blocker use and a lower 30-day ACM risk [hazard ratio (HR) =0.663;95% confidence interval (CI): 0.468–0.939; P=0.02], with a similar trend for 90-day ACM. Subgroup and sensitivity analyses confirmed the robustness of the results. Mediation analysis indicated that the SOFA score significantly mediated the association with 30-day ACM, accounting for 34.228% of the total effect.ConclusionsAmong critically ill sepsis patients complicated by NOAF, early β-blocker administration was associated with reduced 30- and 90-day ACM, partially mediated by a reduction in SOFA scores. These findings warrant further verification in future research.

Highlight box

Key findings

• After 1:1 propensity score matching (PSM) (177 patients per group), early β-blocker use within 24 hours of first intensive care unit (ICU) admission in adult sepsis patients with new-onset atrial fibrillation (NOAF) was associated with lower 30-day all-cause mortality (ACM) (hazard ratio: 0.663, 95% confidence interval: 0.468–0.939) and showed a similar trend for 90-day ACM.

• Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) partially mediated the association between early β-blocker use and ACM (30-day mediation proportion 34.228%; 90-day mediation proportion 55.290%).

What is known and what is new?

• NOAF in sepsis is linked to worse short- and longer-term outcomes, but optimal management (rate vs. rhythm control; choice of agents) remains controversial and high-quality randomized controlled trial (RCT) evidence is lacking; β-blockers may cause hypotension and impaired perfusion in some patients.

• This study evaluates early β-blocker exposure in a defined sepsis + NOAF cohort and quantifies organ dysfunction (SOFA) as a mechanistic pathway via mediation analysis.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Early β-blocker administration may be considered a candidate strategy for sepsis patients with NOAF, but the findings are observational and should be confirmed in prospective multicenter studies/RCTs; future work should also clarify optimal timing/dosing and whether SOFA improvement is reproducibly on the causal pathway.

Introduction

Sepsis, a life-threatening organ dysfunction, results from a dysregulated host immune response to infection (1). Cardiovascular dysfunction frequently complicates the clinical course of sepsis, and atrial fibrillation (AF) is one of the most common arrhythmias associated with adverse outcomes (2). Prior studies have reported the possible AF incidence in the sepsis population as high as 13.5% (3). A systematic review has further demonstrated that new-onset atrial fibrillation (NOAF) significantly increases both in-hospital mortality (IHM) and post-discharge mortality risk (4). Most prior large-scale epidemiological studies have analyzed pre-existing AF and NOAF as a combined entity (5,6). Unlike pre-existing AF, NOAF may reflect acute sepsis-related pathophysiological changes, including hemodynamic instability, excessive inflammation, sympathetic overactivation, coagulation abnormalities, and progressive organ dysfunction (7,8).

Existing evidence suggests that β-blockers may confer potential benefits in selected patients with sepsis-related NOAF. By reducing heart rate, myocardial oxygen consumption, and sympathetic overactivity, β₁-selective blockade may improve cardiovascular efficiency, microcirculatory perfusion, inflammatory response, and organ function (9,10). Observational studies also suggest that intravenous β-blockers achieve faster heart-rate control and may be associated with lower IHM in sepsis-related NOAF (11). In addition, the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score remains a core measure of sepsis-related organ dysfunction and a robust predictor of mortality (12,13).

Despite these findings, optimal management strategies for sepsis complicated by NOAF remain unclear. High-quality randomized evidence is still limited, and concerns persist regarding potential risks of hypotension and impaired tissue perfusion following β-blocker use in critically ill patients (11,14). Moreover, although organ dysfunction is central to sepsis prognosis, previous studies have largely treated the SOFA score as a confounder or severity adjustment variable rather than a potential mechanistic pathway linking β-blocker therapy to survival outcomes (15). Therefore, it remains uncertain whether early β-blocker therapy improves outcomes partly through attenuation of organ dysfunction.

This study aimed to evaluate the association between early intravenous β-blocker use during ICU stay after NOAF onset and 30- and 90-day all-cause mortality (ACM) in sepsis patients. Mediation analysis was further performed using the SOFA score to assess whether improvement in organ function partially explains the association between β-blocker therapy and survival outcomes. It was hypothesized that early β-blocker treatment is associated with lower mortality and that this association is at least partially mediated by improvement in sepsis-related organ dysfunction. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1-0259/rc).

Methods

Data came from the Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care IV (MIMIC-IV 3.1) (16). MIMIC-IV is an open database containing detailed clinical records for over 65,000 intensive care unit (ICU) entries at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center in Boston, Massachusetts. The data, collected between 2008 and 2022, encompasses a wide range of information like demographics, vital signs, lab tests, and diagnoses. To access it, Author T Xie obtained the required certification and subsequently extracted pertinent variables (Certification No: 62698550). This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Study population selection criteria and definitions

A total of 775 MIMIC-IV patients from 2008 to 2022 were encompassed. Inclusion criteria were: (I) age ≥18 years; (II) first ICU entry; (III) sepsis diagnosis as per Sepsis-3.0 criteria (17); (IV) documented baseline status of early β-blocker use (yes or no); and (V) occurrence of AF after the first 24 h of ICU entry. Exclusion criteria were: (I) ICU length of stay <24 h; (II) survival time <24 h; (III) presence of implausible or extreme laboratory values; (IV) a prior history of AF. The initial laboratory measurements were taken within the first 24 hours after admission to the ICU. NOAF was the absence of a prior history of AF or atrial flutter, with AF subsequently documented in bedside nursing rhythm records more than 24 h after ICU admission (18).

The exposure of interest was early β-blocker use, defined as administration of any sort or dose of a β-blocker within the first 24 h after entering the ICU. β-blockers encompassed labetalol, metoprolol, and esmolol (19).

Study outcomes

The primary and the secondary outcomes were 30-day and 90-day ACM, respectively.

Data collection

Data was extracted via structured query language (SQL) in Navicat Premium 16, with specific retrieval based on patients’ identifier for different ICU stays during a single hospital admission (stay_id) and unique identifier for a hospital admission record (hadm_id) (20). Extracted demographic variables were age, sex, and race. Clinical data included demographic characteristics, vital signs, comorbidities, initial lab results within 24h after ICU entry, clinical scoring systems, and therapeutic interventions. Information on the use of any form or dose of β-blockers within the first 24h after entering the ICU was also collected. All information was independently extracted by two investigators utilizing identical SQL queries, and records were double-matched by unique patient identifiers (stay_id and hadm_id) to ensure data uniqueness and completeness. After extraction, key variables (e.g., age, sex, admission time, and discharge outcomes) were subjected to logical consistency and completeness checks to exclude duplicate or conflicting records. For continuous variables (e.g., vital signs and laboratory measurements), outlier detection was conducted before analysis based on clinically plausible ranges and prior literature, and physiologically impossible or clearly erroneous values were excluded. For missing data, variables with a low proportion of missingness (<20%) were imputed using multiple imputation based on a random-forest algorithm implemented in the R package mice, with five imputations performed; variables with a high proportion of missingness (>20%) were removed.

Statistical analysis

Our statistical analyses were enabled by R 4.4.3. According to early β-blocker use, patients were categorized into an early β-blocker group and a non-use group for between-group comparisons. Continuous variables not in normal distribution were shown in medians with interquartile ranges [M (IQR)] and compared via the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Categorical variables were in counts (percentages) and compared utilizing the Pearson chi-square or Fisher’s exact test. To reduce the influence of confounding, variables that differed significantly at baseline or were considered clinically relevant were encompassed as covariates in a propensity score matching (PSM) model. One-to-one matching was performed with a caliper width of 0.2. Post-matching covariate balance was evaluated using standardized mean differences (SMDs), with an SMD below 0.2 denoting acceptable balance. Survival outcomes were visualized through Kaplan-Meier (KM) curves, and differences in 30- and 90-day survival between groups were assessed with the log-rank test. The relation of early β-blocker administration to 30- and 90-day ACM was further quantified using Cox proportional hazards models to account for time-to-event information and censoring during follow-up. Logistic regression analyses were additionally conducted as a sensitivity analysis for the fixed-time mortality outcomes,and the results are presented in Table S1. To examine the consistency of the results, subgroup analyses were carried out across predefined patient strata based on age, sex, comorbidities (diabetes, hypertension, heart failure (HF), acute myocardial infarction (AMI), chronic kidney disease (CKD), liver disease, cerebrovascular disease), and treatments (use of vasoactive agents or non-invasive ventilation). Additionally, the robustness of our findings was rated via sensitivity analyses on the pre-matched cohort. All analyses employed two-sided tests, with P<0.05 signifying statistical significance.

Results

Baseline characteristics of the study population

65,366 records of MIMIC-IV patients with a first ICU entry were extracted, of whom 30,882 satisfied the Sepsis-3.0 diagnostic criteria. As per exclusion criteria, 775 sepsis patients complicated by NOAF were ultimately included in the analysis. Of these, 186 patients were assigned to the early β-blocker cohort and 589 to the non-early β-blocker group (Figure 1). In the original group, significant differences were observed across the two with respect to age, respiratory rate, hypertension, diabetes, AMI, CKD, liver disease, cerebrovascular disease, use of vasoactive agents, hemoglobin levels, platelet counts, bicarbonate levels, and SOFA scores (all P<0.05). The overall 30-day ACM was 39.35%, and the overall 90-day ACM was 48.13%. The 30-day ACM was lower in the early β-blocker group than in the non-early β-blocker group (31.2% vs. 41.9%, P=0.01), and a similar trend was observed for 90-day ACM (P=0.04). A detailed comparison of baseline characteristics across cohorts is provided in Table 1. Following PSM, 177 individuals in the early β-blocker group were successfully matched to 177 patients in the non-early β-blocker cohort, and all covariates achieved adequate balance, with SMDs <0.2 for all variables. The detailed SMD values before and after PSM are presented in Tables S2,S3.

KM analysis of cumulative 30- and 90-day ACM risk

Following PSM, KM survival curve analysis demonstrated notably lower 30-day (log-rank P=0.02) and 90-day (log-rank P=0.04) ACM in the early β-blocker group than the non-early β-blocker cohort (Figure 2A,2B). Before matching, the former also displayed significantly lowered 30-day (log-rank P=0.007) and 90-day (log-rank P=0.02) ACM in comparison to the latter (Figure 2C,2D).

Cox proportional hazards models

Using a rigorous approach incorporating 1:1 PSM, Cox proportional hazards regression analysis showed that early β-blocker use was linked to a 33.7% drop in 30-day ACM [hazard ratio (HR) =0.663; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.468–0.939; P=0.02] and a 27.8% reduction in 90-day ACM (HR =0.722; 95% CI: 0.530–0.983; P=0.04) (Table 2). Before matching, Cox proportional hazards models similarly indicated the significant relation of early β-blocker use to a lower ACM risk at 30 days (HR =0.677; 95% CI: 0.509–0.902; P=0.008) and 90 days (HR =0.740; 95% CI: 0.576–0.951; P=0.02).

Subgroup analysis

The effects of early β-blocker use across different clinical subpopulations were examined via subgroup analyses, with stratified hazard analyses performed for 30-day and 90-day ACM (Figure 3A,3B). The prespecified subgroups included age, sex, diabetes, hypertension, HF, AMI, CKD, liver disease, history of cerebrovascular disease, use of vasoactive agents, and non-invasive mechanical ventilation use. The relation of early β-blocker use to ACM among the sepsis population complicated by NOAF remained directionally consistent across most subgroups, with early β-blocker use generally related to a trend toward reduced ACM risk. Interaction analysis showed a significant interaction between sex and early β-blocker use only in the 90-day ACM analysis (P for interaction =0.034). Specifically, early β-blocker use was associated with a reduced risk of 90-day ACM in female patients (HR =0.53, 95% CI: 0.35–0.81), whereas no significant association was observed in male patients (HR =1.04, 95% CI: 0.66–1.66). Although stratified analysis showed that patients aged <75 had lower ACM at both 30 days (HR =0.49, 95% CI: 0.28–0.86) and 90 days (HR =0.56, 95% CI: 0.35–0.91), no significant association was observed in those aged ≥75 years. However, the interaction by age was not statistically significant. No significant interactions were found for the remaining subgroups, including age, diabetes, hypertension, HF, AMI, CKD, liver disease, or vasoactive agent use.

Mediation analysis

To further elucidate the potential mechanisms by which early β-blocker use lowers ACM in sepsis individuals complicated by NOAF, a mediation analysis was carried out with the SOFA score as the mediator. Early β-blocker use was notably related to lower SOFA scores, and reductions in SOFA scores were, in turn, related to decreased short-term and intermediate-term ACM. As shown in Figure 4A, for the 30-day ACM analysis, the indirect effect was statistically significant (IE =−0.035, 95% CI: −0.064 to −0.011, P<0.001), whereas the direct effect was statistically insignificant (DE =−0.068, 95% CI: −0.175 to 0.019, P=0.13), with a mediation proportion of 34.228%. As illustrated in Figure 4B, in the 90-day ACM analysis, the indirect effect remained significant (IE =−0.050, 95% CI: −0.081 to −0.021, P<0.001), and the proportion mediated rose to 55.290%. These findings suggest that β-blockers may partially mediate their protective effects on short-term and mid-term ACM through improvement in organ function.

Discussion

Based on the MIMIC-IV database, the present study included 775 sepsis patients complicated by NOAF and systematically evaluated the relation of early β-blocker use to short-term as well as intermediate-term survival outcomes. After PSM, early β-blocker therapy was notably linked to reduced 30- and 90-day ACM. These findings were consistently supported by KM survival curves and Cox proportional hazards models, and the association remained robust in analyses conducted before PSM. Further subgroup analyses suggested that this protective effect was directionally consistent across most clinical subgroups, although a prominent interaction was noted in the sex-stratified analysis for 90-day ACM. In addition, mediation analysis demonstrated that the relation of early β-blocker use to survival outcomes was partially mediated by the SOFA score, indicating that improvement in organ function possibly represents one of the principal pathways through which ACM risk is reduced.

Early β-blocker use was related to a significant reduction in ACM among the sepsis-related NOAF population. The robustness of these findings was maintained after rigorous adjustment for confounding through PSM and multivariable Cox proportional hazards modeling. These observations are consistent with prior large-scale real-world studies, like that by Walkey et al. (21), which also reported a relation of β-blocker use to lower ACM. Potential mechanisms underlying this benefit include attenuation of systemic inflammatory responses, correction of sympathetic-parasympathetic imbalance, modulation of myocardial metabolic dysregulation, and prevention of adverse structural remodeling. By antagonizing β-adrenergic receptors, β-blockers effectively suppress excessive sympathetic activation in sepsis-related NOAF, thereby mitigating catecholamine-mediated myocardial toxicity (22), reducing heart rate and myocardial oxygen consumption (23), and improving diastolic function and coronary perfusion (24). Moreover, heart rate reduction prolongs diastolic filling time, enhances cardiac mechanical efficiency, improves ventricular filling and stroke volume, and enables the cardiovascular system to maintain effective perfusion more stably (25). This possibly prevents tachycardia-induced organ hypoperfusion and lactate accumulation, thereby optimizing tissue oxygenation and metabolic homeostasis (26). In addition, β-blockers exert immunomodulatory effects by downregulating the release of proinflammatory cytokines like TNF-α and IL-6 while upregulating the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10, thereby alleviating secondary myocardial and microcirculatory injury caused by the “cytokine storm” in sepsis-related NOAF (27). Their ability to improve endothelial function, reduce capillary permeability (28), and attenuate microcirculatory flow heterogeneity (29) further contributes to the restoration of tissue oxygenation and organ function (28).

Subgroup analysis showed that, after age stratification, early β-blocker use was associated with reduced 30- and 90-day ACM in patients aged <75 years, whereas no significant association was observed in those aged ≥75 years.

This finding may be biologically plausible. Sepsis-related NOAF is often accompanied by sympathetic overactivation, rapid ventricular response, elevated catecholamine levels, increased myocardial oxygen consumption, and impaired diastolic filling (10). Compared with older patients, individuals aged <75 years generally have better cardiovascular reserve, autonomic regulatory capacity, and organ compensatory function. They may therefore better tolerate the negative chronotropic effects of β-blockers and derive greater benefit from heart rate control, sympathetic inhibition, reduced myocardial oxygen consumption, and hemodynamic stabilization (30). In contrast, patients aged ≥75 years often present with greater frailty, more comorbidities, reduced organ reserve, and increased susceptibility to hypotension and tissue hypoperfusion, which may attenuate the potential benefits of β-blocker therapy (31). However, in this study, the age interaction did not reach statistical significance, and the limited sample size after PSM further reduced the number of patients and events in each age-stratified subgroup. Therefore, it remains unclear whether age is a true effect modifier of the association between early β-blocker use and ACM risk. Accordingly, the protective association observed in patients aged <75 years should be interpreted as exploratory rather than definitive evidence of age-related treatment heterogeneity.

Sex-related differences were also observed. In male patients, early β-blocker use was associated with reduced 30-day ACM, but this protective effect did not persist at 90 days. This may be because the 30-day ACM more closely reflects acute-phase hemodynamic instability and sympathetic overactivation in sepsis. Male patients may experience more pronounced cardiovascular stress and sympathetic activation during the acute phase (32). Early β-blocker therapy may confer short-term benefits by rapidly controlling heart rate, attenuating catecholamine excess, reducing myocardial oxygen demand, improving diastolic filling, and stabilizing hemodynamics(21). However, as follow-up extends to 90 days, ACM is less driven by acute hemodynamic factors and more influenced by persistent organ dysfunction, secondary infections, comorbidity burden, nutritional status, and post-ICU recovery (33). In contrast, in female patients, early β-blocker use was not significantly associated with reduced 30-day ACM but showed a significant protective association at 90 days, with a significant sex interaction. This suggests that the benefit of β-blockers in females may be less related to acute heart rate control and more associated with mid- to long-term processes, including immunomodulation, endothelial protection, microcirculatory improvement, and recovery of organ function after sepsis (34). A multicenter retrospective study by Zangrillo et al. reported that in septic shock patients aged 18–55 years, young females had lower 28-day, ICU, and IHM than age-matched males, whereas no significant sex differences were observed in the overall population. The authors suggested that inconsistent findings across previous studies may be related to the lack of stratification by age and female reproductive potential, and proposed that sex hormones may exert protective effects by modulating host immune responses, reducing microvascular injury, and alleviating organ dysfunction (35). In addition, sex differences in β-blocker pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics, such as higher drug exposure or reduced clearance in females, may further enhance therapeutic effects (36). These mechanisms may partly explain the protective association observed in females at 90 days.

The mediation analysis further revealed that the SOFA score partially mediated the relation of β-blocker use to ACM, suggesting that β-blockers may indirectly improve survival by ameliorating multi-organ dysfunction. Mechanistically, β-blockade possibly initially lowers heart rate and improves diastolic perfusion and oxygen supply-demand matching (37), enhances microcirculatory perfusion efficiency (29), and attenuates sympathetic-inflammatory coupling by downregulating TNF-α and IL-6 while upregulating IL-10. At the cellular level, these effects may alleviate mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress (27). Collectively, these interconnected improvements possibly benefit cardiovascular, respiratory, hepatic, and renal systems, leading to parallel improvements across SOFA components. The resultant reduction in SOFA scores thus appears to serve as a key intermediate mechanism translating β-blocker therapy into improved survival outcomes.

The present study has several strengths. First, it was based on a large, open critical care dataset with a substantial sample size and comprehensive clinical information. The database encompasses critically ill patients from different types of ICUs within a single large academic medical center, enabling evaluation of the relation of early β-blocker use to survival outcomes in the sepsis population complicated by NOAF in a real-world setting. As such, the findings possess a degree of external generalizability. Second, NOAF was rigorously distinguished from pre-existing AF, thereby minimizing bias arising from the mixing of different disease stages. Baseline imbalances were addressed through PSM, complemented by KM survival analyses at multiple time points and Cox proportional hazards models, which collectively enhanced the robustness of the results. Third, this study introduced mediation analysis into this research field to unveil how the SOFA score mediates the relationship between early β-blocker use and ACM, providing a novel statistical perspective for understanding the clinical benefits of β-blocker therapy.

Nevertheless, there are limitations. First, owing to its retrospective observational design, causal inference remains limited. Although PSM was employed to control for known confounders, residual confounding and unmeasured bias cannot be entirely excluded. In particular, patients who received β-blockers later during hospitalization may represent a clinically distinct population from those who never received β-blockers, as late administration may be influenced by disease stabilization, survival duration, and clinicians’ treatment decisions. Because the primary focus of this study was early β-blocker exposure within 24 hours of ICU admission, we did not perform an in-depth analysis of late β-blocker users. This issue warrants further investigation in future studies. Second, several important variables, including detailed time-stamped hemodynamic parameters, vasopressor exposure, and lactate levels, were unavailable or not incorporated into the current analytical framework. As a result, it was impossible to reliably identify septic shock according to Sepsis-3 criteria within the same time window as NOAF onset and early β-blocker exposure. Although septic shock could be identified using ICD codes, these codes may reflect diagnoses recorded during the overall hospitalization rather than the patient’s precise clinical status at ICU admission or at the time of β-blocker initiation. Consequently, septic shock subgroup analysis was not performed, and future studies with more precise time-resolved clinical data are needed to clarify the effects of β-blockers in this important subgroup. Third, the sample size was relatively limited after PSM, and subsequent subgroup stratification further reduced the number of patients and events within each subgroup. Therefore, subgroup findings, particularly those related to age and sex, should be interpreted cautiously and considered exploratory. Finally, as MIMIC-IV is a single-center database, the findings lack external validation. Future studies conducted in multicenter settings, including prospective cohorts and randomized controlled trials (RCTs), are necessary to corroborate the robustness and clinical applicability of these conclusions.

Conclusions

Among sepsis patients complicated by NOAF, early β-blocker use was related to a reduction in ACM. Further prospective trials are needed for high-level evidence regarding the optimal timing and dosing strategies for β-blocker administration in this population.
