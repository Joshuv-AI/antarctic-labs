Abstract
. Let G G be a finite connected simple graph, and let 𝒥 K m , G \mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G} denote its generalized binomial edge ideal. By investigating the colon ideals of 𝒥 K m , G \mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G} , we derive a formula for the local v \operatorname{v} -number of 𝒥 K m , G \mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G} with respect to the empty cut set. Furthermore, we classify graphs for which this generalized binomial edge ideal has v \operatorname{v} -numbers 1 or 2. When G G is a connected closed graph, we compute the local v \operatorname{v} -number of 𝒥 K 2 , G \mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G} by generalizing the work of Dey et al. Additionally, under the condition that G G is Cohen–Macaulay, we derive formulas for the v \operatorname{v} -number of 𝒥 K m , G \mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G} and 𝒥 K 2 , G k \mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k} , and show that the v \operatorname{v} -number of 𝒥 K 2 , G k \mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k} is a linear function of k k .



1.
Introduction

Let
R={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]=\bigoplus\limits_{d\geq 0}R_{d}
be the standard graded polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
.
If
I\subset R
is a graded ideal, then we denote the set of all associated primes of
I
by
\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. For any
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
,
the
local
\operatorname{v}
-number
of
I
at
\mathfrak{p}

is defined to be

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\coloneqq\min\{d\geq 0\mid\text{\ there exists an\ }f\in R_{d}\text{\ such that\ }(I:f)=\mathfrak{p}\}.

The
\operatorname{v}
-number

of
I
, denoted by
\operatorname{v}(I)
, is defined as

\operatorname{v}(I)\coloneqq\min\{\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)\}.

The
\operatorname{v}
-number, named after Wolmer Vasconcelos, was introduced in
[
6
]
to study the asymptotic behavior of the minimum distance of projective Reed-Muller type codes. This invariant has since
been the subject of extensive study from algebraic and combinatorial perspectives.

A significant body of work has focused on the
\operatorname{v}
-number of monomial ideals (see
[
13
,
2
,
18
,
25
]
).
Squarefree monomial ideals have an intrinsic combinatorial nature and their algebraic properties often carry information on their underlying combinatorial structures, and vice versa. In
[
16
]
the authors consider these ideals as edge ideals of clutters and find a combinatorial expression for their
\operatorname{v}
-numbers. This combinatorial description has been
exploited to classify
W_{2}
graphs (see
[
16
, Theorem 4.2]
) and to study the combinatorial structure of graphs whose edge ideals have a Cohen–Macaulay second symbolic power.
In
[
26
]
, Saha studied the relation between the
\operatorname{v}
-number and the regularity of cover ideals of graphs. He showed that
\operatorname{v}(J(G))\leq\operatorname{reg}(J(G))+1
, where
J(G)
is the cover ideal of a graph

G
, and the equality holds if
G
is a complete multipartite graph.
In
[
18
]
, the authors express the
\operatorname{v}
-number of the Stanley–Reisner ideal of a simplicial complex in terms of its Alexander dual complex and prove that the
\operatorname{v}
-number of
a cover ideal is just two less than the initial degree of its syzygy module.

Let
I\subset R
be a graded ideal. Independently, Kodiyalam

[
19
]
and Cutkosky, Herzog, and Trung
[
7
]
proved that

\operatorname{reg}(I^{k})
is a linear function in
k
for all sufficiently large
k
.
Motivated by these results, Conca
[
5
]
established that the
function
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
is eventually linear in
k
; more precisely, there exist
constants
a
and
b
such that
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=ak+b
for all
k\gg 0
.
For the case where
R
is a polynomial ring over a field, Ficarra and Sgroi
independently obtained the same result (see
[
13
, Theorem
3.1]
). They conjectured that if
I
has linear powers, then

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\alpha(I)k-1
for all integers
k\geq 1
, where
\alpha(I)

stands for the initial degree of
I
. Within the same paper, they gave an
affirmative resolution to this conjecture for three classes of ideals: edge
ideals with linear resolutions, polymatroidal ideals, and Hibi ideals.

In this paper, we focus on the
\operatorname{v}
-number of powers of generalized binomial edge ideals. First, we recall the definition of a generalized binomial edge ideal.

Let
m
and
n
be positive integers with
m,n\geq 2
. Following standard convention, the notation
[m]
denotes the set
\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
. Let
S={\mathbb{K}}[\mathbf{X}]\coloneqq{\mathbb{K}}[x_{i,j}:i\in[m],j\in[n]]
be the polynomial ring over the field
{\mathbb{K}}
in
m\times n
variables.

In
[
9
]
, Ene et al. introduced the binomial edge ideal of a pair of graphs. Specifically, let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be simple graphs on vertex sets
[m]
and
[n]
, respectively. Let
\varepsilon_{1}=\{i,j\}\in E(G_{1})
and
\varepsilon_{2}=\{k,l\}\in E(G_{2})
be edges with
i<j
and
k<l
. One can then assign a
2
-minor
p_{(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})}=[i,j\mid k,l]\coloneqq x_{i,k}x_{j,l}-x_{i,l}x_{j,k}
to the pair
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})
. The
binomial edge ideal of the pair
(G_{1},G_{2})
is defined as

\mathcal{J}_{G_{1},G_{2}}\coloneqq\left(p_{(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})}\mid\varepsilon_{1}\in E(G_{1}),\varepsilon_{2}\in E(G_{2})\right)

in
S
. This ideal generalizes the classical
binomial edge ideals
introduced in
[
15
,
23
]
, which are recovered when one of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
is the complete graph
K_{2}
.

Let
K_{m}
be the complete graph on the vertex set
[m]
and
G
be a simple graph on the vertex set
[n]
; then
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is the
generalized binomial edge ideal
associated with
G
, first introduced by Rauh in
[
24
]
for the study of conditional independence ideals.
Note that the ideal
\mathfrak{P}_{\emptyset}\coloneqq(p_{(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})}\mid\varepsilon_{1}\in E(K_{m}),\varepsilon_{2}\in E(K_{n}))
is always a minimal prime ideal of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
.

The (local)
\operatorname{v}
-numbers of binomial edge ideals have recently been discussed
in
[
1
,
8
,
17
,
20
]
. In
[
1
]
, the
authors studied the properties and bounds of the
\operatorname{v}
-number of binomial edge
ideals. They characterized all connected graphs
G
with
\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})=1
,
and showed that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{P}_{\emptyset}}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})=\textup{min-comp}(G)
,
where
\textup{min-comp}(G)
is the minimum completion number of the simple
graph
G
. In addition, when
G
is a connected non-complete graph,
Jaramillo-Velez and Seccia in
[
17
]
proved that

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{P}_{\emptyset}}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})=\gamma_{c}(G)
, where
\gamma_{c}(G)
is
the connected domination number of
G
. In
[
8
]
, amongst many
beautiful results, Dey et al. characterized all connected graphs
G
with

\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})=2
, as well as computing the
\operatorname{v}
-number of the binomial
edge ideal of a Cohen–Macaulay closed graph. Building on the work of

[
8
,
21
]
, Kumar et al. in
[
20
]
proved
that
\operatorname{v}(J_{C_{n}})=\lceil\frac{2n}{3}\rceil
for all
n\geq 6
, where
C_{n}
is a cycle with
n
vertices.
Nevertheless, to date, no literature has examined the
\operatorname{v}
-number of generalized binomial edge ideals.

The main focus of this paper is to generalize the work of Dey et al. on the
\operatorname{v}
-number of the binomial edge ideal of a connected Cohen–Macaulay closed graph.
This generalization is achieved in three non-trivial aspects. Specifically, we explicitly determine three key quantities: the local
\operatorname{v}
-number of the binomial edge ideal of a connected closed graph, the
\operatorname{v}
-number of the generalized binomial edge ideal of a connected Cohen–Macaulay closed graph, and the
\operatorname{v}
-number of powers of the binomial edge ideal of a connected Cohen–Macaulay closed graph. We address each of these objectives in Sections
4
through
6
.
In particular, the
\operatorname{v}
-number of powers of the generalized binomial edge ideal of a connected Cohen–Macaulay closed graph is a linear function of the power.

To achieve these goals, we will first review several necessary definitions and terms in the next section. Subsequently, in Section
3
, we focus on the colon ideals of generalized binomial edge ideals. As a result, we derive a formula for

\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{P}_{\emptyset}}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})

in terms of the minimum completion number and the connected domination number. Furthermore, we classify graphs for which the generalized binomial edge ideals have
\operatorname{v}
-numbers
1
or
2
. Influenced by the results in Section
3
, it is natural to conjecture that the
\operatorname{v}
-number of the generalized binomial edge ideal
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is independent of the number
m
. However, as our result in Section
5
shows, this is not the case, and the computation of
\operatorname{v}
-numbers of generalized binomial edge ideals is undoubtedly more involved.

2.
Preliminary

Let
G
be a simple graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
.
In what follows, we assume that
V(G)=[n]\coloneqq\{1,2,\dots,n\}
for some positive integer
n
.
More generally, for non-negative integers
n_{1}\leq n_{2}
, we write
[n_{1},n_{2}]\coloneqq\{n_{1},n_{1}+1,\dots,n_{2}\}
.

For any subset
A
of
V(G)
, let
G[A]
denote the
induced subgraph
of
G
on the vertex set
A
; that is, for
i,j\in A
,
\{i,j\}\in E(G[A])
if and only if
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
. We denote the induced subgraph of
G
on
V(G)\setminus A
by
G\setminus A
. For simplicity, if
A=\{v\}
is a singleton, we write this induced subgraph as
G\setminus v
.

Complete graphs, path graphs, and cones are common simple graphs.

Definition 2.1
.

(1)

A graph
G
is called a
complete graph
if there is an edge between every pair of its vertices. If
G
has
n
vertices, we often denote it by
K_{n}
.

(2)

A
path graph
with
n
vertices, denoted by
P_{n}
, is a graph whose vertex set can be ordered as
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
such that
E(P_{n})=\{\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}\mid 1\leq i\leq n-1\}
. The
length
of
P_{n}
is the number of edges in
P_{n}
, which is
n-1
.

(3)

A graph
G
is called a
cone
if there is
subgraph
H
and a vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus V(H)
such that
V(G)=V(H)\cup\{v\}
and
E(G)=E(H)\cup\{\{u,v\}\mid u\in V(H)\}
. In this case, we often denote it by
\operatorname{cone}(v,H)
.

As introduced earlier, given a simple graph
G
with
n
vertices and an integer
m\geq 2
, we can consider the generalized binomial edge ideal
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
in
S={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1,1},\dots,x_{m,n}]
. Throughout this paper, we always adopt the lexicographic order induced by

(1)

x_{1,1}>x_{1,2}>\cdots>x_{1,n}>x_{2,1}>x_{2,2}>\cdots>x_{2,n}>\cdots>x_{m,1}>\cdots>x_{m,n}.

It is well-known that
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is a radical ideal. Its minimal prime ideals can be described explicitly as follows:
Let
c(G)
denote the number of connected components of the graph
G
. A vertex
v\in V(G)
is called a
cut vertex
of
G
if
c(G)<c(G\setminus v)
. For a subset
T\subseteq V(G)
, we abuse notation by letting
c(T)
denote the number of connected components of
G\setminus T
.
We say that a subset
T
of
V(G)
is a
cut set
of
G
if, for every
v\in T
,
v
is a cut vertex of the induced subgraph
G\setminus(T\setminus\{v\})
. We define
\mathcal{C}(G)
to be the
collection of all cut sets of
G
. Note that
\emptyset\in\mathcal{C}(G)
.

For each subset
T\subseteq[n]=V(G)
, we define the ideal

\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{m},G)\coloneqq(x_{i,j}:(i,j)\in[m]\times T)+\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G_{1}}}+\cdots+\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G_{c(T)}}}

in
S
, where
G_{1},\ldots,G_{c(T)}
are the connected components of
G\setminus T
, and
\widetilde{G_{i}}
is the complete graph over the vertices of
G_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,c(T)
.
It is well-known that
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is a radical ideal with
\operatorname{Ass}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=\{\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{m},G)\mid T\in\mathcal{C}(G)\}
.
For simplicity, the local
\operatorname{v}
-number of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
with respect to
\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{m},G)
will be denoted by
\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})
.

The main focus of this paper is to study the (local)
\operatorname{v}
-numbers associated to the generalized binomial edge ideal of a connected closed graph.
Closed graphs have many interesting characterizations and beautiful properties. For instance, we can choose the following as its definition:

Definition 2.2

(
[
10
, Theorem 2.2]
and
[
9
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on the vertex set
[n]
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The minimal generators
\{p_{(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})}\mid\varepsilon_{1}\in E(K_{m}),\varepsilon_{2}\in E(G)\}
of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
form a quadratic Gröbner basis with respect to the lexicographic order defined in (
1
);

(2)

For all integers
1\leq i<j<k\leq n
, if
\{i,k\}\in E(G)
then
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
and
\{j,k\}\in E(G)
;

(3)

All facets of the clique complex
\Delta(G)
of
G
are intervals of the form
[a,b]\subset[n]
.

A graph
G
is
closed
if there exists a vertex labeling for which one of the above equivalent conditions holds.

In this paper, we say that
G
is
Cohen–Macaulay
if
S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}
is Cohen–Macaulay. By
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
, a connected Cohen–Macaulay closed graph
G
with
n
vertices has a vertex labeling and integers
1=a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{t}<a_{t+1}=n
for which the maximal cliques
F_{1},\ldots,F_{t+1}
of
G
satisfy
F_{i}=[a_{i},a_{i+1}]
for all
i=1,\dots,t
.

Our investigation of the
\operatorname{v}
-number of a connected closed graph depends heavily on the knowledge of the local
\operatorname{v}
-number with respect to
\mathfrak{P}_{\emptyset}=\mathfrak{P}_{\emptyset}(K_{m},G)
. When
m=2
, there are two equivalent approaches for handling this.

For a connected graph
G
, the notion of connected domination number is widely used; see, for instance,
[
17
]
. For the simplicity of the subsequent study, we introduce some variance of this notion. A
reduced connected dominating set
is a subset
D
of its vertices such that the induced subgraph
G[D]
is connected, and every two vertices
u,v
in
V(G)\setminus D
can be connected with a path
u=u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{s},u_{s+1}=v
such that
u_{1},\dots,u_{s}\in D
. The
reduced connected domination number
of
G
, denoted by
\gamma_{c}^{*}(G)
, is the minimum cardinality of all such sets. In this language, if
G
is a complete graph, then the empty set
\emptyset
is a reduced connected dominating set, and
\gamma_{c}^{*}(G)=0
. On the other hand, if
G
is not complete, then
D
is a reduced connected dominating set of
G
if and only if
D
is a connected dominating set of
G
. Whence,
\gamma_{c}^{*}(G)
is precisely the connected domination number
\gamma_{c}(G)
of
G
. One of the main results of
[
17
]
shows that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{P}_{\emptyset}}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})
is given by
\gamma_{c}^{*}(G)
.

Relatedly, for a vertex
v
of
G
, its
neighborhood
is defined as
N_{G}(v)\coloneqq\{u\in V(G)\mid\{u,v\}\in E(G)\}
.
Furthermore, the
completion graph
of
G
with respect to the vertex
v
is the graph
G_{v}
, which is defined by

V(G_{v})=V(G)\quad\text{and}\quad E(G_{v})=E(G)\cup\{\{u,w\}\mid u,w\in N_{G}(v),u\neq w\}.

More generally, the
completion graph
of
G
with respect to a subset
V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}\subseteq V(G)
is the graph
G_{V}
defined iteratively as
G_{V}=G_{v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{k}}\coloneqq(\ldots((G_{v_{1}})_{v_{2}})\ldots)_{v_{k}}
.
[
1
, Proposition 3.2]
shows that the definition of
G_{V}
is well-defined. A subset
W\subseteq V(G)
is called a
completion set
if
G_{W}
is a disjoint union of complete graphs. A
minimal completion set
is a completion set
W
such that
G_{U}
is not a disjoint union of complete graphs for any proper subset
U
of
W
. The
minimum completion number
, denoted
\textup{min-comp}(G)
, is the minimum cardinality of all minimal completion sets of
G
. It is shown in
[
1
]
that
\operatorname{v}_{\mathfrak{P}_{\emptyset}}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})
is also equal to
\textup{min-comp}(G)
.

3.
\operatorname{v}
-number of generalized binomial edge ideals

As is evident from the work in
[
1
]
, colon ideals of elements associated to vertices are fundamental to the study of the
\operatorname{v}
-number of binomial edge ideals. Therefore, we start by studying those colon ideals for generalized binomial edge ideals.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on the vertex set
[n]
. Then for any
i\in[m]
and
j\in[n]
, we have
(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}:x_{i,j})=\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{j}}
, where
G_{j}
is the completion graph of
G
with respect to the vertex
j
.

Proof.

Firstly, we show that
(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}:x_{i,j})\supseteq\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{j}}
. Since

\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{j}}=\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}+(x_{k,p}x_{l,q}-x_{k,q}x_{l,p}\mid\text{$p,q\in N_{G}(j)$ and $1\leq k<l\leq m$}),

it suffices to show that
x_{i,j}(x_{k,p}x_{l,q}-x_{k,q}x_{l,p})\in\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
for any
p,q\in N_{G}(j)
and
1\leq k<l\leq m
. But this can be checked straightforwardly. Observe that

\displaystyle x_{i,j}(x_{k,p}x_{l,q}-x_{k,q}x_{l,p})

\displaystyle=x_{k,p}(x_{i,j}x_{l,q}-x_{i,q}x_{l,j})+x_{i,q}(x_{k,p}x_{l,j}-x_{k,j}x_{l,p})

\displaystyle\qquad+x_{l,p}(x_{i,q}x_{k,j}-x_{i,j}x_{k,q}).

Since
\{p,j\},\{q,j\}\in E(G)
, we have

x_{i,j}x_{l,q}-x_{i,q}x_{l,j},x_{k,p}x_{l,j}-x_{k,j}x_{l,p},x_{i,q}x_{k,j}-x_{i,j}x_{k,q}\in\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}.

Hence,
x_{i,j}(x_{k,p}x_{l,q}-x_{k,q}x_{l,p})\in\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
, as expected.

It remains to show that
(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}:x_{i,j})\subseteq\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{j}}
. For this purpose, let us take arbitrary
f\in(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}:x_{i,j})
. Whence,

x_{i,j}f\in\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}\subseteq\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{j}}\subseteq\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{m},G_{j}),

for every
T\in\mathcal{C}(G_{j})
. By
[
3
, Lemma 4.5 (1)]
,
this implies that
T\in\mathcal{C}(G)
and
j\notin T
. Since
x_{i,j}\notin\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{m},G_{j})
, we have
f\in\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{m},G_{j})
for every
T\in\mathcal{C}(G_{j})
. In other words,
f\in\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{j}}
. From this, we conclude that
(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}:x_{i,j})\subseteq\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{j}}
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on the vertex set
[n]
. Suppose that
\varepsilon_{1}\in E(K_{m})
and
\varepsilon_{2}=\{k,l\}\in E(\widetilde{G})\setminus E(G)
, such that
P:k,k_{1},\ldots,k_{s},l
is a path from
k
to
l
in
G
. Then

x_{i_{1},k_{1}}x_{i_{2},k_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{s},k_{s}}p_{(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})}\in\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G},

where
i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\in[m]
.

Proof.

Let
G^{\prime}=(\cdots(G_{k_{1}})_{k_{2}}\cdots)_{k_{s}}
. It follows from
3.1
that

(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}:x_{i_{1},k_{1}}x_{i_{2},k_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{s},k_{s}})=\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G^{\prime}}.

Since
\varepsilon_{2}\in E(G^{\prime})
, the expected containment holds.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on the set
[n]
and
m\geq 2
. Then

\operatorname{v}_{\emptyset}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=\operatorname{v}_{\emptyset}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})=\textup{min-comp}(G)=\gamma_{c}^{*}(G),

where
\textup{min-comp}(G)
is the minimum completion number of
G
and
\gamma_{c}^{*}(G)
is the reduced connected domination number of
G
.

Proof.

It follows from
[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
that
\operatorname{v}_{\emptyset}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})=\gamma_{c}^{*}(G)
. At the same time, it follows from
[
1
, Theorem 3.6]
that
\operatorname{v}_{\emptyset}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})=\textup{min-comp}(G)
. Notice that the current
3.1
is a generalization of
[
1
, Proposition 3.1]
to the generalized binomial edge ideal case. One can also easily generalize
[
1
, Lemma 3.5]
to the generalized binomial edge ideal case using exactly the same argument. Therefore, we can do the same trick as in
[
1
, Theorem 3.6]
to get that
\operatorname{v}_{\emptyset}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=\textup{min-comp}(G)
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on the vertex set
[n]
. Then
\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=1
if and only if
G=\operatorname{cone}(v,H)
is a cone graph for some non-complete graph
H
.

Proof.

(1)

Suppose that
\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=1
. Then
G
is not a complete graph and it follows from the definition that there is a homogeneous polynomial

. Since

is a prime ideal, we have either

or

. Furthermore, for the given

Since

\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
,
P_{\emptyset}(K_{m},G)=(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}:f)=\bigcap_{T\in\mathcal{C}(G)}(\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{m},G):f)

for every non-empty

in
\mathcal{C}(G)
. Since
\operatorname{deg}(f)=1

be a variable appearing in the expression of

for some non-complete graph

.

(2)

Conversely, suppose that

for some non-complete graph

.

.

, we may assume without loss of generality that
\operatorname{in}(f)\notin\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})
.
Two cases contribute to the factors of
\operatorname{in}(f)
.

(1)

Let
\tau
be an arbitrary isolated vertex of
L(T)
. It is readily verified that
\tau=b_{l}
for some
1\leq l\leq t-1
. Furthermore,
b_{l-1}
and
b_{l+1}
lie in the same connected component of
G\setminus T
, which we denote by
G^{\prime}
. In particular,
b_{l}-1
and
b_{l}+1
belong to
G^{\prime}
.

Thus, for every
1\leq k_{1}<k_{2}\leq m
, the binomial
[k_{1},k_{2}|b_{l}-1,b_{l}+1]
is contained in the prime ideal
\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{m},G)
. Since
(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}:f)=\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{m},G)
, this implies
f
is in the colon ideal

\displaystyle(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}:[k_{1},k_{2}|b_{l}-1,b_{l}+1])=(x_{k,b_{l}}\mid k\in[m])+\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus b_{l}},

by
4.6
. Therefore,

\operatorname{in}(f)\in(x_{k,b_{l}}\mid k\in[m])+\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus b_{l}}).

Since
\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus b_{l}})\subseteq\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})
but
\operatorname{in}(f)\notin\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})
, there exists some
\gamma_{l}\in[m]
such that
x_{\gamma_{l},b_{l}}
divides
\operatorname{in}(f)
.

(2)

Let
\tau
be an arbitrary element of
T
; then
\tau=b_{j_{i}}
for some
i\in[s]
.
For every
k\in[m]
, we have
x_{k,b_{j_{i}}}\in\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{m},G)
, and thus
f\in(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}:x_{k,b_{j_{i}}})
.
As shown in
3.1
, this colon ideal is equal to
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{b_{j_{i}}}}
.
Recall that
G_{b_{j_{i}}}
is a Cohen–Macaulay closed graph with
E(G_{b_{j_{i}}})=E(G)\cup\{\{u,v\}\mid u,v\in N_{G}(b_{j_{i}})\}
.
Since
\operatorname{in}(f)\notin\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})
, we conclude there exists a monomial
x_{k_{i}^{\prime},w_{i}^{\prime}}x_{k_{i}^{\prime\prime},w_{i}^{\prime\prime}}
dividing
\operatorname{in}(f)
such that
1\leq k_{i}^{\prime}<k_{i}^{\prime\prime}\leq m
and
b_{j_{i}-1}\leq w_{i}^{\prime}<b_{j_{i}}<w_{i}^{\prime\prime}\leq b_{j_{i}+1}
.
Notably, neither
w_{i}^{\prime}
nor
w_{i}^{\prime\prime}
is an isolated vertex in
L(T)
.
Furthermore, we have
b_{j_{i}-1}\leq\alpha_{i}<b_{j_{i}}<\beta_{i}\leq b_{j_{i}+1}
.

Therefore, the squarefree monomial

M\coloneqq\operatorname{lcm}\bigl(\{x_{\gamma_{l},b_{l}}\mid b_{l}\text{ is an isolated vertex of }L(T)\}\cup\{x_{k_{i}^{\prime},w_{i}^{\prime}}x_{k_{i}^{\prime\prime},w_{i}^{\prime\prime}}\mid b_{j_{i}}\in T\}\bigr)

divides
\operatorname{in}(f)
. We next consider the “flattened” monomial

\widetilde{M}\coloneqq\operatorname{lcm}\bigl(\{x_{\gamma_{l},b_{l}}\mid b_{l}\text{ is an isolated vertex of }L(T)\}\cup\{x_{k_{i}^{\prime},\alpha_{i}}x_{k_{i}^{\prime\prime},\beta_{i}}\mid b_{j_{i}}\in T\}\bigr).

It is straightforward to see that
\operatorname{deg}(\widetilde{M})\leq\operatorname{deg}(M)
. Furthermore, if the variable
x_{i,v}
divides
\widetilde{M}
, then
v\in V(L(T))
.

Consider an equivalence relation (partition)
\mathcal{P}
on
E(L(T))
generated by the following rule: if
x_{k_{i}^{\prime\prime},\beta_{i}}=x_{k_{i+1}^{\prime},\alpha_{i+1}}
, then
\{\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\}\sim\{\alpha_{i+1},\beta_{i+1}\}
. Suppose that there are
q
equivalence classes, and the vertex sets of the respective equivalence classes are

\{c_{i_{p},1}<c_{i_{p},2}<\dots<c_{i_{p},\ell_{p}}\}

for
p\in[q]
and
2\leq\ell_{p}
.

For each
p\in[q]
, suppose that
c_{i_{p},r}=\alpha_{j_{r}}
for
r\in[\ell_{p}-1]
. Observe that

1\leq k_{j_{1}}^{\prime}<k_{j_{1}}^{\prime\prime}=k_{j_{2}}^{\prime}<\cdots<k_{j_{\ell_{p}-2}}^{\prime\prime}=k_{j_{\ell_{p}-1}}^{\prime}<k_{j_{\ell_{p}-1}}^{\prime\prime}\leq m.

Hence
\ell_{p}\leq m
, so in particular
\mathcal{P}
is an
m
-compatible partition of
E(L(T))
. Furthermore, the squarefree monomial

\operatorname{lcm}\bigl\{x_{k_{j}^{\prime},\alpha_{j}}x_{k_{j}^{\prime\prime},\beta_{j}}\mid j=j_{r}\text{ for }r\in[\ell_{p}-1]\bigr\}

equals

x_{k_{j_{1}}^{\prime},\alpha_{j_{1}}}x_{k_{j_{2}}^{\prime},\alpha_{j_{2}}}\cdots x_{k_{j_{\ell_{p}-1}}^{\prime},\alpha_{j_{\ell_{p}-1}}}x_{k_{j_{\ell_{p}-1}}^{\prime\prime},\beta_{j_{\ell_{p}-1}}}.

We denote this monomial by
\widetilde{f}_{p}
.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}=\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{l}\text{ is an isolated}\\
\text{vertex of }L(T)\end{subarray}}x_{\gamma_{l},b_{l}}\right)\cdot\prod_{p=1}^{q}\widetilde{f}_{p}.

Since
\operatorname{deg}(M)\geq\operatorname{deg}(\widetilde{M})=\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}})
and
M
divides
\operatorname{in}(f)
, we obtain
\operatorname{deg}(f)\geq\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}})
, as desired.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

Note that the graph
L(T)
in
4.7
is defined specifically for non-empty cut sets of
G
. Indeed, if
T
is the empty set, we may take
L(T)
to be the graph consisting of
\gamma_{c}^{*}(G)
isolated vertices. Thus the proof of
5.4
still carries over without difficulty, which further confirms that the local
\operatorname{v}
-number equals
\gamma_{c}^{*}(G)
, as predicted in
4.4
.

5.2.
Realization of
\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})

It follows from
5.4
that there exists an
m
-compatible partition
\mathcal{P}
of
E(L(T))
such that
T\in\mathcal{C}(G)
and
\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}})
attains the desired minimum value, that is,

\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}})=\min\{\operatorname{v}_{W}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})\mid W\in\mathcal{C}(G)\}.

We now make the following observations concerning this optimal cut set
T
and the optimal partition
\mathcal{P}
.

Observations 5.6
.

(1)

If
1=b_{0}\in V(L(T))
, then
b_{1}=b_{j_{1}}\in T
and
b_{0}
is not an isolated vertex. In other words,
b_{0}
is the left endpoint of some slice from the optimal partition
\mathcal{P}
. We remove the edge
\{b_{0}=\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\}
from
E(L(T))
but retain the vertex
b_{0}
in
V(L(T))
. This yields a graph
L^{\prime}(T^{\prime})
that is closely related to
L(T^{\prime})
for the cut set
T^{\prime}\coloneqq T\setminus\{b_{1}\}\in\mathcal{C}(G)
. For the resulting partition
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
on
E(L(T^{\prime}))
, we obtain the new polynomial
f_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}
.

(a)

If
b_{2}=b_{j_{2}}\in T
, then
b_{0}
is an isolated vertex of
L^{\prime}(T^{\prime})
. Removing
b_{0}
yields a graph isomorphic to
L(T^{\prime})
. In this case,
\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}})>\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}})
, which contradicts the minimality of
\mathcal{P}
.

(b)

Otherwise,
b_{2}\notin T
and the single edge
\{b_{0},b_{2}\}
is a slice of length
1
in
\mathcal{P}
. It is easy to see that
L^{\prime}(T^{\prime})
is isomorphic to
L(T^{\prime})
, with the isolated vertex
b_{0}
replaced by
b_{1}
. In this case,
\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}})\geq\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}})
. The strict inequality
\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}})>\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}})
holds if and only if
b_{2}
is also the endpoint of another adjacent slice in
\mathcal{P}
.

Thus, for simplicity, we may assume
b_{0}\notin V(L(T))
. By symmetry, we may also assume
n=b_{t}\notin V(L(T))
.

(2)

Recall that
\widetilde{C}(G)\coloneqq\{b_{1},\dots,b_{t-1}\}
denotes the set of cut vertices of
G
. We currently have
\widetilde{C}(G)\subseteq V(L(T))\sqcup T
. If this containment is strict, then by the assumption in Item
1
above, there exists some
\beta_{i}=\alpha_{i+1}\notin\widetilde{C}(G)
. Let
i
be the minimal index for which this holds. Note that
\beta_{i}=\alpha_{i+1}
is adjacent to both
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i+1}
. We remove the edge
\{\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\}
from the graph
L(T)
. As in Item
1
above, the resulting graph is isomorphic to
L(T^{\prime})
for the cut set
T^{\prime}\coloneqq T\setminus\{b_{j_{i}}\}\in\mathcal{C}(G)
. Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
be the induced partition on
E(L(T^{\prime}))
; one can verify that
\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}})\geq\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}})
.

Thus, for simplicity, we may assume
\widetilde{C}(G)=V(L(T))\sqcup T
.
As a result,
j_{i}+2\leq j_{i+1}
for all
i\in[s-1]
.

(3)

Without loss of generality, we may assume all isolated vertices lie at the right (larger) end of
\widetilde{C}(G)
. We claim
L(T)
contains at most two isolated vertices. To see this, suppose for contradiction that
L(T)
has at least three isolated vertices. We may take such vertices to be
b_{t-3},b_{t-2},b_{t-1}
. Adding
b_{t-2}
to
T
gives a new cut set
T^{\prime}
satisfying

E(L(T^{\prime}))=E(L(T))\cup\{\{b_{t-3},b_{t-1}\}\}.

For the corresponding partition
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of
E(L(T^{\prime}))
where the single edge
\{b_{t-3},b_{t-1}\}
is a slice, we have
\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}})=\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}})-1
, contradicting the minimality of
\mathcal{P}
.

Thus, we may assume
L(T)
contains at most two isolated vertices.

(4)

Suppose that there exist two adjacent slices with lengths
(n_{1}-1)
and
(n_{2}-1)
respectively, where
2\leq n_{1},n_{2}\leq m
, and that they are connected by a common vertex
b_{j_{v}}
. Due to the assumption in the previous Item
2
, the leftmost edge of the right (second) slice must be
\{b_{j_{v}},b_{j_{v+2}}\}
, with
b_{j_{v+1}}\in T
. Removing the edge
\{b_{j_{v}},b_{j_{v+2}}\}
from
L(T)
and supplementing it with an isolated vertex
b_{j_{v+1}}
yields
L(T^{\prime})
for the cut set
T^{\prime}\coloneqq T\setminus\{b_{j_{v+1}}\}\in\mathcal{C}(G)
. Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
be the resulting partition on
E(L(T^{\prime}))
. Notice that
\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}})\geq\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}})
.

Therefore, for simplicity, we may assume that all the slices resulting from the partition
\mathcal{P}
are vertex-disjoint.

(5)

Suppose there exist two adjacent but non-connected slices of lengths
n_{1}-1
and
n_{2}-1
, respectively, with
2\leq n_{1}<m
and
2\leq n_{2}\leq m
. We move the left end-edge of the right slice to the left slice, creating two new slices of lengths
(n_{1}+1)-1
and
(n_{2}-1)-1
, respectively. If
n_{2}=2
, we regard the resulting right slice, an isolated vertex, as a degenerate slice. After suitable relabeling, the resulting graph corresponds to another
m
-compatible partition
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of
E(L(T^{\prime}))
for some cut set
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}(G)
. Note that
\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}})=\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}})
.

Thus, by the assumptions in Items
3
and
4
and the aforementioned movement, we may assume for simplicity that all slices have length
m-1
, except the rightmost slice.

(6)

Suppose the vertex set of the rightmost slice of
L(T)
is
\{c_{i_{q},1}<c_{i_{q},2}<\dots<c_{i_{q},\ell_{q}}\}
with
2\leq\ell_{q}\leq m
. We claim that
\ell_{q}=m
if
L(T)
has two isolated vertices.

To verify this, suppose
c_{i_{q},\ell_{q}}=b_{j_{v}}
. It follows that the two isolated vertices are
b_{j_{v}+1}
and
b_{j_{v}+2}
, which are indeed
b_{t-2}
and
b_{t-1}
, respectively. Now assume for contradiction that
\ell_{q}<m
. We may replace
T
with
T^{\prime}\coloneqq T\cup\{b_{j_{v}+1}\}\in\mathcal{C}(G)
. For such
T^{\prime}
, we have

V(L(T^{\prime}))=V(L(T))\setminus\{b_{j_{v}+1}\}\quad\text{and}\quad E(L(T^{\prime}))=E(L(T))\cup\bigl\{\{b_{j_{v}},b_{j_{v}+2}\}\bigr\}.

Extending the rightmost slice of
\mathcal{P}
by the newly added edge yields an
m
-compatible partition
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of
E(L(T^{\prime}))
. Since
\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}})<\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}})
, this contradicts the minimality of
\mathcal{P}
.

We therefore conclude that
\ell_{p}=m
whenever
L(T)
contains two isolated vertices.

To summarize, for the optimal cut set
T
and the optimal partition
\mathcal{P}
of
E(L(T))
satisfying
\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=\operatorname{deg}(f_{\mathcal{P}})
, we may impose the following assumptions:

•

The vertex set
V(L(T))
of
L(T)
satisfies
V(L(T))\sqcup T=\widetilde{C}(G)
;

•

The slices are pairwise vertex-disjoint;

•

Every slice has length
m-1
, except possibly the rightmost slice;

•

The number of isolated vertices is at most two, and these vertices lie near the right end of the graph;

•

If there are two isolated vertices, the rightmost slice also has length
m-1
.

Under these assumptions, both the optimal cut set
T
and the optimal partition
\mathcal{P}
are uniquely determined.

Construction 5.7
.

We describe the construction of the optimal cut set

and the optimal partition

as follows. Let
\widetilde{C}(G)=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{t-1}\}
denote the base vertex set.

We may write

for some integer

with

. Using this decomposition, for each

\left\lfloor\frac{t-1}{2m-1}\right\rfloor

for

.

When

, we have

. Let

If

For this construction, the optimal cut set

\mathcal{P}
of

.

6.
\operatorname{v}
-number of powers of binomial edge ideals of connected Cohen–Macaulay closed graphs

In this section, we keep
G
as a connected Cohen–Macaulay closed graph. Our aim is to determine the
\operatorname{v}
-number of powers of the binomial edge ideal
\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}
.

Accordingly, we assume there exist integers
1=b_{0}<b_{1}<\cdots<b_{t-1}<b_{t}=n
such that the maximal cliques of
G
are given by
F_{i}=[b_{i-1},b_{i}]
for
i\in[t]
. Let
P
be the spine of
G
; that is,
P
is the path graph with vertex set
V(P)=\{b_{0},b_{1},\dots,b_{t}\}
.

We first establish two basic lemmas concerning initial ideals and associated primes of powers of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}
.
These results will be used repeatedly throughout the section.

Lemma 6.1
.

For every positive integer
k
, we have
\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})=(\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}))^{k}
and
\operatorname{Ass}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})
.

Proof.

It follows from
[
11
, Corollary 3.4]
that
\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k}=\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{(k)}
, where
\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{(k)}
denotes the
k
-th symbolic power of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}
. Since
\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}
is radical, we have
\operatorname{Ass}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{(k)})=\operatorname{Ass}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})
. Moreover, the proof of
[
11
, Corollary 3.4]
implies that
[
11
, Lemma 3.1]
is applicable. In particular, the proof of
[
11
, Lemma 3.1]
yields
\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})=(\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}))^{k}
.
∎

In what follows, for an edge
\varepsilon=\{k,l\}\in E(G)
with
1\leq k<l\leq n
, we set
f_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq x_{1,k}x_{2,l}-x_{1,l}x_{2,k}\in S={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1,1},\dots,x_{2,n}]
.

We next prove a key colon ideal identity that controls the behavior of powers with respect to the extremal edge
\{1,2\}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

For every positive integer
k
, we have
(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k}:g)=\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k-1}
, where
g\coloneqq f_{\{1,2\}}
.

Proof.

Since
\{1,2\}\in E(G)
, the case
k=1
is trivial; we therefore assume
k\geq 2
. It is clear that
\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k-1}\subseteq(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k}:g)
. Thus, it suffices to show
\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k}:g)\subseteq\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k-1})
.

To establish this, let
f\in(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k}:g)
. By
6.1
, we have
\operatorname{in}(f)\operatorname{in}(g)\in\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})=(\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}))^{k}
. Note that
\operatorname{in}(g)=x_{1,1}x_{2,2}
, and
x_{1,1}x_{2,2}
is the unique minimal monomial generator of
\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})
divisible by
x_{2,2}
. It follows immediately that
((\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}))^{k}:\operatorname{in}(g))=(\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}))^{k-1}
. This implies
\operatorname{in}(f)\in(\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}))^{k-1}
, and hence
\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k}:g)\subseteq\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k-1})
, as desired.
∎

To understand the local
\operatorname{v}
-numbers of powers, we first analyze the case where
G
is a path graph, which serves as the building block for all connected Cohen–Macaulay closed graphs.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
G=P
be a path graph and let
T
be a cut set of
G
. For any monomial

h\in(\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}):\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G)))\setminus\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}),

we have
\operatorname{deg}(h)\geq\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})
.

Proof.

Recall that for the cut set
T
, we may construct the graph
L(T)
. Since
m=2
, there exists a unique
2
-compatible partition of
E(L(T))
, which is trivial in the sense that each equivalence class contains exactly one edge. Let
\mathcal{P}_{0}
denote this unique
2
-compatible partition. By abuse of notation, we write
f_{T}
for the polynomial
f_{\mathcal{P}_{0}}
. It then follows from
4.12
that
\operatorname{deg}(f_{T})=\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})
.

For the given monomial
h
, we make the following observations:

(1)

For each vertex
v\in T
, since
x_{1,v},x_{2,v}\in\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G))
and

h\,\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G))\subseteq\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})=(x_{1,i}x_{2,i+1}\mid i\in[n-1]),

we conclude that both
x_{2,v+1}
and
x_{1,v-1}
divide
h
. In other words,
h
is divisible by
x_{1,v-1}x_{2,v+1}
. Note that
\{v-1,v+1\}
is an edge of
L(T)
. Neither
v-1
nor
v+1
is an isolated vertex of
L(T)
.

(2)

For each isolated vertex
u
of
L(T)
, the vertices
u-1
and
u+1
lie in the same connected component of
G\setminus T
. Hence
x_{1,u-1}x_{2,u+1}\in\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G))
. Since
h\,\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G))\subseteq\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})
, it follows that
h
is divisible by either
x_{1,u}
or
x_{2,u}
. For simplicity, we denote this element by
z_{u}
.

From the above reasoning, we deduce that
h
is divisible by

(7)

h^{\prime}=\operatorname{lcm}\bigl(\{\uline{x_{1,v-1}x_{2,v+1}}\mid v\in T\}\cup\{\uline{z_{u}}\mid\text{$u$ is an isolated vertex of $L(T)$}\}\bigr).

It is straightforward to check that
h^{\prime}
is exactly the product of the underlined monomials in (
7
) and that
\operatorname{deg}(h^{\prime})=\operatorname{deg}(f_{T})
. Accordingly, we obtain
\operatorname{deg}(h)\geq\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})
.
∎

Using the preceding lemma, we can now determine the local
\operatorname{v}
-numbers for powers of binomial edge ideals over path graphs.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
G=P
be a path graph on vertex set
[n]
with
n\geq 3
, and let
T
be a cut set of
G
. Then for any integer
k\geq 1
,

\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})=\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})+2(k-1).

Proof.

Let
f
be a homogeneous polynomial in
S
such that
(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}:f)=\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G)
and
\operatorname{deg}(f)=\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})
. By
6.2
, it follows that
(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k}:g^{k-1}f)=\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G)
, where
g=f_{\{1,2\}}
. Since
\operatorname{Ass}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})
, this implies
\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})\leq\operatorname{deg}(g^{k-1}f)=\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})+2(k-1)
. It remains to show that
\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})\geq\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})+2(k-1)
, which we establish below.

Let
h
be a homogeneous polynomial such that
(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k}:h)=\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G)
. We will show that

(8)

\operatorname{deg}(h)\geq\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})+2(k-1).

By
3.9
, we may assume
\operatorname{in}(h)\notin\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})
. Note that
\operatorname{in}(h)\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G))\subseteq\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})=(\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}))^{k}
, where the last equality holds by
6.1
.

If
T\neq\emptyset
, take an arbitrary
v\in T
. Since
x_{1,v}\in\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G))
and
\operatorname{in}(h)\notin\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})
, it follows that
\operatorname{in}(h)=x_{2,v+1}\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{1}})\cdots\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{k-1}})h^{\prime}
for some edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{k-1}\in E(G)
and some monomial
h^{\prime}\in S
. Since
\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})
is a complete intersection,

\left((\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}))^{k}:\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{1}})\cdots\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{k-1}})\right)=\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})

by
[
4
, Exercise 1.1.15]
. This implies

x_{2,v+1}h^{\prime}\in\left((\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}))^{k}:\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{1}})\cdots\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{k-1}})\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G))\right)=\left(\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}):\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G))\right).

As a consequence,
6.3
yields

\operatorname{deg}(h)-2(k-1)=\operatorname{deg}(x_{2,v+1}h^{\prime})\geq\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}).

Thus, the inequality (
8
) holds in this case.

If
T=\emptyset
, then
\operatorname{v}_{\emptyset}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})=n-2
by Corollary
3.3
. For each
v\in[n-2]
, since
x_{1,v}x_{2,v+2}\in\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{\emptyset}(K_{2},G))
and
\operatorname{in}(h)\notin\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})
, either

x_{2,v+2}\operatorname{in}(h)=x_{2,v+1}\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{1}})\cdots\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{k-1}})h^{\prime}

or

x_{1,v}\operatorname{in}(h)=x_{1,v+1}\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{1}})\cdots\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{k-1}})h^{\prime}

for some edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{k-1}\in E(G)
and some monomial
h^{\prime}\in S
.

(1)

Suppose
x_{2,v+2}\operatorname{in}(h)=x_{2,v+1}\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{1}})\cdots\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{k-1}})h^{\prime}
. If
x_{2,v+2}
divides
h^{\prime}
, then
\operatorname{in}(h)
is divisible by
\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{1}})\cdots\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{k-1}})
. Using the complete intersection property, the remainder of the proof proceeds similarly to the
T\neq\emptyset
case. Otherwise, we may assume
x_{2,v+2}
divides
\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{k-1}})
. Since
\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{k-1}})
must equal
x_{1,v+1}x_{2.v+2}
, this implies
x_{1,v+1}x_{2,v+1}\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{1}})\cdots\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{k-2}})
divides
\operatorname{in}(h)
.

(2)

Suppose
x_{1,v}\operatorname{in}(h)=x_{1,v+1}\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{1}})\cdots\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{k-1}})h^{\prime}
. This case is analogous to the previous one.

Thus, we only need to consider the case where, for each
v\in[n-2]
, there exists a minimal monomial generator
F_{v}
of
\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k-2})
such that
F_{v}x_{1,v+1}x_{2,v+1}
divides
\operatorname{in}(h)
.

(1)

Suppose there exist
v,v^{\prime}\in[n-2]
such that
F_{v}\neq F_{v^{\prime}}
. Since both
F_{v}
and
F_{v^{\prime}}
divide
\operatorname{in}(h)
, Then
\operatorname{lcm}(F_{v},F_{v^{\prime}})
divides
\operatorname{in}(h)
, and
\operatorname{deg}(\operatorname{lcm}(F_{v},F_{v^{\prime}}))\geq 2(k-1)
. This implies
\operatorname{in}(h)
is divisible by
\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{1}})\cdots\operatorname{in}(f_{e_{k-1}})
for some edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{k-1}\in E(G)
. Using the complete intersection property, the remainder of the proof proceeds similarly to the
T\neq\emptyset
case.

(2)

Otherwise,
F_{1}=\cdots=F_{n-2}
. Let
F
denote this common monomial. Then
\operatorname{in}(h)
is divisible by
Fx_{1,2}x_{2,2}x_{1,3}x_{2,3}\cdots x_{1,n-1}x_{2,n-1}
.

When
n\geq 4
, this implies

\operatorname{deg}(h)\geq 2(k-2)+2(n-2)\geq 2(k-1)+(n-2)=2(k-1)+\operatorname{v}_{\emptyset}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}),

as desired.

When
n=3
, using the complete intersection property, we have

\operatorname{in}(h)/F\in(\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k}):F\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{\emptyset}(K_{2},G)))=(\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{2}):\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{\emptyset}(K_{2},G))).

It is straightforward to verify that

\displaystyle(\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{2}):\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{\emptyset}(K_{2},G)))

\displaystyle=(x_{1,1}x_{2,2},x_{1,2}x_{2,3})^{2}:(x_{1,1}x_{2,2},x_{1,2}x_{2,3},x_{1,1}x_{2,3})

\displaystyle=(x_{1,2}x_{2,2}x_{2,3},x_{1,2}^{2}x_{2,3},x_{1,1}x_{2,2}^{2},x_{1,1}x_{1,2}x_{2,2}).

Thus,
\operatorname{deg}(h)-2(k-2)\geq 3
, i.e.,
\operatorname{deg}(h)\geq 2(k-1)+\operatorname{v}_{\emptyset}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})
, as desired. ∎

Having established the formula for path graphs, we now return to the general case of connected Cohen–Macaulay closed graphs.
The spine subgraph allows us to reduce the general case to the path graph case.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
G
be a connected Cohen–Macaulay closed graph. For every positive integer
k
, we have

\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})=\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})+2(k-1).

Proof.

Let
f
be a homogeneous polynomial in
S
such that
(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}:f)=\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G)
for some
T\in\mathcal{C}(G)
and
\operatorname{deg}(f)=\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})
. By
6.2
, we have
(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k}:g^{k-1}f)=\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G)
for
g\coloneqq f_{\{1,2\}}
.
Since
\operatorname{Ass}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})
, it follows that
\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})\leq\operatorname{deg}(g^{k-1}f)=\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})+2(k-1)
. In particular,
\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})\leq\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})+2(k-1)
.

It remains to show that
\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})\geq\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})+2(k-1)
. To this end, let
\varphi
be the
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra homomorphism from
S={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1,1},\dots,x_{1,n},x_{2,1},\dots,x_{2,n}]
onto its subring

R={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1,b_{0}},x_{1,b_{1}},\dots,x_{1,b_{t}},x_{2,b_{0}},x_{2,b_{1}},\dots,x_{2,b_{t}}],

defined by

•

the restriction of
\varphi
to
R
is the identity map;

•

if
b_{j}<v<b_{j+1}
, then
\varphi(x_{1,v})=x_{1,b_{j}}
and
\varphi(x_{2,v})=x_{2,b_{j+1}}
.

It is straightforward to check that
\varphi(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})=\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},P}
, where
P
is the spine of
G
with vertex set
V(P)=\{b_{0},b_{1},\dots,b_{t}\}
. Note that
\mathcal{C}(P)\subseteq\mathcal{C}(G)\subseteq\{b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{t-1}\}
. Since
T\in\mathcal{C}(G)
, then
\varphi(\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G))=\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},P)
. Notice that if
T\in\mathcal{C}(G)\setminus\mathcal{C}(P)
, then
\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},P)
is a non-minimal prime ideal containing
\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},P}
.

Now let
h
be a homogeneous polynomial in
S
such that
(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k}:h)=\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G)
. By
3.9
, we may assume that
\operatorname{in}(h)\notin\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})
. Since
\operatorname{in}(h)\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G))\subseteq\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})
, we obtain

\varphi(\operatorname{in}(h))\varphi(\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},G)))\subseteq\varphi(\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})),

that is,

\varphi(\operatorname{in}(h))\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{2},P))\subseteq\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},P}^{k}).

Let
\mathfrak{P}_{T^{\prime}}(K_{2},P)
be a minimal prime of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},P}
contained in

\mathfrak{P}_{T}(P)
. Then

\varphi(\operatorname{in}(h))\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{T^{\prime}}(K_{2},P))\subseteq\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},P}^{k}).

By the proof of
6.4
, this implies that

\displaystyle\operatorname{deg}(h)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{deg}(\operatorname{in}(h))=\operatorname{deg}(\varphi(\operatorname{in}(h)))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\eqref{eqn:deg_h}}}}{{\geq}}\operatorname{v}_{T^{\prime}}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},P})+2(k-1)

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},P})+2(k-1)=\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})+2(k-1),

where the last equality holds because the
\operatorname{v}
-numbers of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},P}
and
\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}
depend only on the number of their maximal cliques by
5.11
.
∎

The following observation clarifies the behavior of local
\operatorname{v}
-numbers under the reduction to the spine subgraph.

Remark 6.6
.

If
T\in\mathcal{C}(P)\subseteq\mathcal{C}(G)
, then
T^{\prime}=T
holds in the proof of
6.5
.
Consequently, for every positive integer
k
,

\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})=\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})+2(k-1).

As an immediate consequence, we obtain an explicit formula for path graphs, which are the simplest examples of Cohen–Macaulay closed graphs.

Corollary 6.7
.

If
G=P_{n}
is a path graph with
n
vertices, then for any integer
k\geq 1
,

\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G}^{k})=\left\lceil\frac{2(n-2)}{3}\right\rceil+2(k-1).

Proof.

Note that
G
is a connected Cohen–Macaulay closed graph with precisely
n-1
maximal cliques,
\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{2},G})=\left\lceil\frac{2(n-2)}{3}\right\rceil
by Corollary
5.11
. The result follows from Proposition
6.5
.
∎

We conclude this section with a remark that indicates possible extensions of our methods to complete graphs
K_{m}
with
m\geq 3
.

Remark 6.8
.

Let
G
be a path graph and let
T
be a cut set of
G
.

(1)

For any integer
m\geq 2
, the method used in the proof of
6.2
can be adapted to show that

\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}^{k})\leq\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})+2(k-1).

However, equality need not hold in general. For instance, if
G=P_{5}
and
T=\{3\}
, a direct computation yields

\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{3},G})=\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{3},G})=2

and

\operatorname{v}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{3},G}^{2})=\operatorname{v}_{T}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{3},G}^{2})=3.

(2)

Nevertheless, the modules

(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}^{k}:\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{m},G))\big/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}^{k}

and

\bigl((\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}))^{k}:\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{P}_{T}(K_{m},G))\bigr)\big/(\operatorname{in}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}))^{k}

seem to be closely related. It would be worthwhile to investigate further consequences of this relation.
