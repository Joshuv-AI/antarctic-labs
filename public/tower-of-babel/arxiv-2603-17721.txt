Limiting behavior of principal eigenvalues for a class of mixed boundary value problems as the measure of the support domain goes to zero
Julián López-Gómez, Alejandro Sahuquillo

Abstract
In this paper we characterize the limiting behavior of the principal eigenvalue, σ 1 ​ [ − Δ , β , Ω ] \sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega] , of the boundary value problem ( 1.1 ) as the Lebesgue measure of the underlying domain, Ω \Omega , tends to zero. Naturally, the domains Ω \Omega are assumed to be included on a fixed open set D D such that β ∈ 𝒞 ⁡ ( D ) \beta\in\mathcal{C}(D) , and they satisfy Ω ¯ ⊂ D \bar{\Omega}\subset D . Our main result establishes that, in the classical case when inf D β > 0 \inf_{D}\beta>0 , lim | Ω | ↓ 0 σ 1 ​ [ − Δ , β , Ω ] = + ∞ , \lim_{|\Omega|\downarrow 0}\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]=+\infty, whereas lim | Ω | ↓ 0 σ 1 [ − Δ , β , Ω ] = − ∞ if sup D β < 0 , \lim_{|\Omega|\downarrow 0}\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]=-\infty\;\;\hbox{if}\;\;\sup_{D}\beta<0, which is a surprising result at the light of the classical existing theorems for Dirichlet boundary conditions. Furthermore, in the special case when β > 0 \beta>0 is a constant, we can prove that lim R ↓ 0 ( R ​ σ 1 ​ [ − Δ , β , B R ] ) = β ​ Area ⁡ ( ∂ B 1 ) | B 1 | , \lim_{R\downarrow 0}\left(R\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,B_{R}]\right)=\beta\frac{\mathrm{Area}(\partial B_{1})}{|B_{1}|}, where, we are denoting B ϱ := { x ∈ ℝ N : | x | < ϱ } B_{\varrho}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\;:\;|x|<\varrho\} for all ϱ > 0 \varrho>0 .

1
Introduction

This paper analyzes the limiting behavior of the principal eigenvalue,
\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]
, of the linear eigenvalue problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u=\sigma u&\hbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}+\beta(x)u=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

as
|\Omega|\downarrow 0
, where
\Omega
is a bounded domain of class
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
) for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
|\Omega|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
\Omega
,
n
stands for the outward unit normal vector field along
\partial\Omega
. Throughout this paper,
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an open set such that
\bar{\Omega}\subset D
and
\beta\in\mathcal{C}(D)
is an arbitrary continuous function.

According to, e.g., Theorem 5.1 of
[
7
]
, or Proposition 8.6 of
[
9
]
, which are based on the celebrated inequality of Faber
[
4
]
and Krahn
[
6
]
, it is already known that the principal eigenvalue of the associated
Dirichlet
problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u=\sigma u&\hbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
u=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

denoted by
\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\mathcal{D},\Omega]
, satisfies

\lim_{|\Omega|\downarrow 0}\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\mathcal{D},\Omega]=+\infty.

(1.2)

Naturally, this property fails to be true for the
Neumann
problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u=\sigma u&\hbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

as, in such a case, the underlying principal eigenvalue, denoted by
\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\mathcal{N},\Omega]
in this paper, equals zero regardless the size of
\Omega
. Up to the best of our knowledge, the behavior of the principal eigenvalue of (
1.1
) as
|\Omega|\downarrow 0
remains an open problem even in the case when
\beta
is assumed to be constant on
\partial\Omega
.
Our first result provides us with that limiting behavior in the simplest one-dimensional prototype model

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-u^{\prime\prime}=\sigma u\qquad\hbox{in}\;\;[0,L],\\[4.30554pt]
\mathcal{B}_{0}u(0)=0,\quad\mathcal{B}_{L}u(L)=0,\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

as
L\downarrow 0
, where
\mathcal{B}_{0},\;\mathcal{B}_{L}\in\{\mathcal{D},\mathcal{N},\mathcal{R}_{\beta}\}
are boundary operators of some of the following types: either
\mathcal{D}u=u
, or
\mathcal{N}u=u^{\prime}
, or

\mathcal{R}_{\beta}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}+\beta u:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-u^{\prime}(0)+\beta_{0}u(0)&\quad\hbox{at}\;\;x=0,\\[4.30554pt]
u^{\prime}(L)+\beta_{\omega}u(L)&\quad\hbox{at}\;\;x=L,\end{array}\right.

where
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{\omega}
are two arbitrary constants, not necessarily positive as in the classical setting of the Sturm–Liouville theory. Note that
\mathcal{R}_{\beta}=\mathcal{N}
at
x=0
(resp.

x=L
) if
\beta_{0}=0
(resp.
\beta_{\omega}=0
). Actually, we can always assume that
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\mathcal{R}_{\beta}
at
x=0
and

\mathcal{B}_{L}=\mathcal{R}_{\beta}
at
x=L
by adopting the convention that

\beta_{0}=+\infty\;\;\hbox{if}\;\;\mathcal{B}_{0}=\mathcal{D},\;\;\hbox{and}\;\;\beta_{\omega}=+\infty\;\;\hbox{if}\;\;\mathcal{B}_{L}=\mathcal{D}.

With these conventions in mind, throughout this paper, the principal eigenvalue of (
1.3
) will be denoted by

\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0},\beta_{\omega},(0,L)]
. Note that problem (
1.3
) cannot be thought as a particular case of (
1.1
), for some choice of
\beta(x)
, because we are not imposing that
\beta_{0}=\beta_{\omega}
. Indeed, in the one-dimensional setting of problem (
1.1
), if
\Omega=(0,L)
, the continuity of
\beta
entails that

\lim_{L\downarrow 0}\beta(L)=\beta(0),

whereas in the setting of (
1.3
), in general,
\beta_{0}\neq\beta_{\omega}
.

The main result in the one-dimensional case can be stated as follows. It characterizes
whether, or not, the condition (
1.2
) holds true for the model (
1.3
) under general boundary conditions
of mixed type, not necessarily of Sturm–Liouville type.

Theorem 1.1
.

The following conditions are satisfied.

(a)

If
\beta_{0}+\beta_{\omega}>0
, then

\lim_{L\downarrow 0}\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0},\beta_{\omega},(0,L)]=+\infty.

(b)

If
\beta_{0}+\beta_{\omega}=0
, then

\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0},\beta_{\omega},(0,L)]=-\beta_{0}^{2}\leq 0\;\;\hbox{for all}\;\;L>0.

(c)

If
\beta_{0}+\beta_{\omega}<0
, then

\lim_{L\downarrow 0}\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0},\beta_{\omega},(0,L)]=-\infty.

The behavior of Part (c) has not been previously described in the literature. Possibly, because
in the setting of the Sturm–Liouville theory
\beta_{0}\geq 0
and
\beta_{\omega}\geq 0
.

The main result of this paper in the multidimensional case can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]
be the principal eigenvalue of (
1.1
). Then,

\lim_{|\Omega|\downarrow 0}\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]=+\infty\quad\hbox{if}\;\;\inf_{D}\beta>0.

(1.4)

Moreover,

\lim_{|\Omega|\downarrow 0}\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]=-\infty\quad\hbox{if}\;\;\sup_{D}\beta<0.

(1.5)

The distribution of this paper is the following. Section 2 delivers some preliminaries, among them, the monotonicity properties of the principal eigenvalues used throughout this paper. Section 3 is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, which relies on some elementary integration techniques. In Section 4 we deliver the proof of Theorem
1.2
, which is based on some sophisticated comparison techniques combined with some technical devices introduced in Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 2.1 of
[
9
]
. Finally, in Section 5 we find out the asymptotic expansion of

\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,B_{R}]
as
R\downarrow 0
in the special case when
\beta>0
is a constant. It turns out that

\lim_{R\downarrow 0}\left(R\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,B_{R}]\right)=\beta\frac{\mathrm{Area}(\partial B_{1})}{|B_{1}|}.

2
Preliminaries

This section collects some preliminary results, mainly based on comparison principles, that will be used
throughout this article. We begin by recalling some general properties of principal eigenvalues and their associated eigenfunctions.

Subsequently, we will denote by
\varphi_{1}
any principal eigenfunction associated to
\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]
, and by
\psi_{1}
any principal eigenfunction of (
1.3
) associated with
\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0},\beta_{\omega},(0,L)]
. According to Sections 7 and 8 of
[
9
]
, we already know that:

•

\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]
and
\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0},\beta_{\omega},(0,L)]
are the only eigenvalues of (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) that admit some positive eigenfunction;

•

\varphi_{1}
and
\psi_{1}
are unique, up to positive multiplicative constants, and they satisfy
\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\bar{\Omega})\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}(\Omega)
,
\varphi_{1}(x)>0
for all
x\in\bar{\Omega}
,

\psi_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}[0,L]
,
\psi_{1}(x)>0
for all
x\in(0,L)
,
\psi_{1}(0)>0
if
\mathcal{B}_{0}\in\{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{R}_{\beta}:\beta\in\mathbb{R}\}
,
\psi_{1}^{\prime}(0)>0
if
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\mathcal{D}
, and

\psi_{1}(L)>0
if
\mathcal{B}_{L}\in\{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{R}_{\beta}:\beta\in\mathbb{R}\}
and
\psi_{1}^{\prime}(L)<0
if
\mathcal{B}_{L}=\mathcal{D}
. These properties can be summarized by saying that
\varphi_{1}
and
\psi_{1}
are strongly positive (
\varphi_{1},\psi_{1}\gg 0
);

•

any other eigenvalues of (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) are real and greater than their respective principal eigenvalues
\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]
and
\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0},\beta_{\omega},(0,L)]
. Thus, they are strictly dominant.

Most of these properties go back to Cano-Casanova and López-Gómez
[
2
]
for general boundary conditions.

Throughout this paper, a
supersolution
of the problem (
1.1
) is any function
\overline{u}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\bar{\Omega})\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\overline{u}\geq\sigma\overline{u}&\hbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\partial\overline{u}}{\partial n}+\beta(x)\overline{u}\geq 0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

In such a case,
\overline{u}
is said to be a
strict supersolution
if some of these inequalities is strict. Similarly, a
supersolution
of (
1.3
) is any function
\overline{u}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}[0,L]
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\overline{u}^{\prime\prime}\geq\sigma\overline{u}\qquad\hbox{in}\;\;[0,L],\\[4.30554pt]
\mathcal{B}_{0}\overline{u}(0)\geq 0,\quad\mathcal{B}_{L}\overline{u}(L)\geq 0,\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

and
\overline{u}
is said to be a
strict supersolution
if some of these inequalities is strict. The following results establish the main monotonicity properties of
\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0},\beta_{\omega},(0,L)]
and
\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The principal eigenvalue of (
1.3
) is increasing with respect to
\beta_{0}\in(-\infty,+\infty]
and

\beta_{\omega}\in(-\infty,+\infty]
, in the sense that

\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0}^{2},\beta_{\omega}^{2},(0,L)]>\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0}^{1},\beta_{\omega}^{1},(0,L)]\quad\hbox{if}\;\;\beta_{0}^{2}\geq\beta_{0}^{1},\;\;\beta_{\omega}^{2}\geq\beta_{\omega}^{1},\;\;(\beta_{0}^{2},\beta_{\omega}^{2})\neq(\beta_{0}^{1},\beta_{\omega}^{1}).

(2.3)

Proof.

Assume that
u_{1}\gg 0
and
u_{2}\gg 0
are principal eigenfunctions associated to the eigenvalue problems

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-u_{1}^{\prime\prime}=\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0}^{1},\beta_{\omega}^{1},(0,L)]u_{1}\qquad\hbox{in}\;\;[0,L],\\[4.30554pt]
-u_{1}^{\prime}(0)+\beta_{0}^{1}u_{1}(0)=0,\quad u_{1}^{\prime}(L)+\beta_{\omega}^{1}u_{1}(L)=0,\end{array}\right.

(2.4)

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-u_{2}^{\prime\prime}=\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0}^{2},\beta_{\omega}^{2},(0,L)]u_{2}\qquad\hbox{in}\;\;[0,L],\\[4.30554pt]
-u_{2}^{\prime}(0)+\beta_{0}^{2}u_{2}(0)=0,\quad u_{2}^{\prime}(L)+\beta_{\omega}^{2}u_{2}(L)=0,\end{array}\right.

(2.5)

respectively. In the proof we will differentiate among several cases.

Case 1:
\beta_{0}^{1},\beta_{\omega}^{1}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then, by a direct calculation, we find that

-u_{1}^{\prime}(0)+\beta_{0}^{2}u_{1}(0)=\left(\beta_{0}^{2}-\beta_{0}^{1}\right)u_{1}(0)\geq 0\quad\hbox{and}\quad u_{1}^{\prime}(L)+\beta_{\omega}^{2}u_{1}(L)=\left(\beta_{\omega}^{2}-\beta_{\omega}^{1}\right)u_{1}(L)\geq 0,

even in the case when
\beta_{0}^{2}
, or
\beta_{\omega}^{2}
, equals
+\infty
. Since
\beta_{0}^{1},\beta_{\omega}^{1}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that
u_{1}(0)>0
and
u_{1}(L)>0
. Hence, one of the previous inequalities is strict because
(\beta_{0}^{2},\beta_{\omega}^{2})\neq(\beta_{0}^{1},\beta_{\omega}^{1})
. Thus,

u_{1}
is a positive strict supersolution of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-u^{\prime\prime}=\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0}^{1},\beta_{\omega}^{1},(0,L)]u\qquad\hbox{in}\;\;[0,L],\\[4.30554pt]
-u^{\prime}(0)+\beta_{0}^{2}u(0)=0,\quad u^{\prime}(L)+\beta_{\omega}^{2}u(L)=0.\end{array}\right.

(2.6)

By the notational conventions in Section 1,
-u^{\prime}(0)+\beta_{0}^{2}u(0)=0
becomes into
u(0)=0
if
\beta_{0}^{2}=+\infty
. Similarly,
u^{\prime}(L)+\beta_{\omega}^{2}u(L)=0
becomes into
u(L)=0
if
\beta_{\omega}^{2}=+\infty
. Consequently, it follows from
[
9
, Th. 7.10]
(going back to López-Gómez and
Molina-Meyer
[
10
]
and Amann and López-Gómez
[
1
]
) that

\sigma_{1}[-D^{2}-\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0}^{1},\beta_{\omega}^{1},(0,L)],\beta_{0}^{2},\beta_{\omega}^{2},(0,L)]>0.

Therefore, (
2.3
) holds. This ends the proof in this case.

Case 2:
\beta_{0}^{1}=+\infty
and
\beta_{\omega}^{1}\in\mathbb{R}
.
This entails
\beta_{0}^{2}=+\infty
. Thus, by the convention adopted in Section 1,
u_{1}(0)=u_{2}(0)=0
. Moreover, since
\beta_{\omega}^{1}\in\mathbb{R}
, we find that
u_{1}(L)>0
and

u_{1}^{\prime}(L)+\beta_{\omega}^{2}u_{1}(L)=\left(\beta_{\omega}^{2}-\beta_{\omega}^{1}\right)u_{1}(L)\geq 0.

On the other hand, since
\beta_{0}^{1}=\beta_{0}^{2}=+\infty
and
(\beta_{0}^{2},\beta_{\omega}^{2})\neq(\beta_{0}^{1},\beta_{\omega}^{1})
, necessarily

\beta_{\omega}^{2}>\beta_{\omega}^{1}
. Hence,

u_{1}^{\prime}(L)+\beta_{\omega}^{2}u_{1}(L)=\left(\beta_{\omega}^{2}-\beta_{\omega}^{1}\right)u_{1}(L)>0.

Consequently,
u_{1}
is a positive strict supersolution of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-u^{\prime\prime}=\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},+\infty,\beta_{\omega}^{1},(0,L)]u\qquad\hbox{in}\;\;[0,L],\\[4.30554pt]
u(0)=0,\quad u^{\prime}(L)+\beta_{\omega}^{2}u(L)=0.\end{array}\right.

(2.7)

Note that
u^{\prime}(L)+\beta_{\omega}^{2}u(L)=0
becomes into
u(L)=0
if
\beta_{\omega}^{2}=+\infty
. Therefore, in any circumstances,
it follows from
[
9
, Th. 7.10]
that

\sigma_{1}[-D^{2}-\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},+\infty,\beta_{\omega}^{1},(0,L)],+\infty,\beta_{\omega}^{2},(0,L)]>0,

which implies (
2.3
).

Case 3:
\beta_{0}^{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta_{\omega}^{1}=+\infty
.
This entails
\beta_{\omega}^{2}=+\infty
and
\beta_{0}^{2}>\beta_{0}^{1}
.
Thus,
u_{1}(0)>0
and
u_{1}(L)=u_{2}(L)=0
. Hence,

-u_{1}^{\prime}(0)+\beta_{0}^{2}u_{1}(0)=\left(\beta_{0}^{2}-\beta_{0}^{1}\right)u_{1}(0)>0.

So,
u_{1}
is a positive strict supersolution of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-u^{\prime\prime}=\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0}^{1},+\infty,(0,L)]u\qquad\hbox{in}\;\;[0,L],\\[4.30554pt]
-u^{\prime}(0)+\beta_{0}^{2}u(0)=0,\quad u(L)=0.\end{array}\right.

(2.8)

Observe that
-u^{\prime}(0)+\beta_{0}^{2}u(0)=0
becomes into
u(0)=0
if
\beta_{0}^{2}=+\infty
. Therefore,
it follows from
[
9
, Th. 7.10]
that

\sigma_{1}[-D^{2}-\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0}^{1},+\infty,(0,L)],\beta_{0}^{2},+\infty,(0,L)]>0,

which implies (
2.3
). The proof is complete.
∎

Similarly, the next result holds.

Proposition 2.2
.

The principal eigenvalue of the problem (
1.1
) is increasing with respect to
\beta
, in the sense that

\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{2},\Omega]>\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{1},\Omega]\quad\hbox{if}\;\;\beta_{2}\gneq\beta_{1}\;\;\hbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega.

(2.9)

Proof.

Let
\beta_{2}\gneq\beta_{1}
, and
u_{1}\gg 0
and
u_{2}\gg 0
be principal eigenfunctions of
the eigenvalue problems

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u_{1}=\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{1},\Omega]u_{1}&\hbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial n}+\beta_{1}u_{1}=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(2.10)

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u_{2}=\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{2},\Omega]u_{2}&\hbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial n}+\beta_{2}u_{2}=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(2.11)

respectively. Since
u_{1}(x)>0
for all
x\in\bar{\Omega}
and
\beta_{2}\gneq\beta_{1}
, we have that

\tfrac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial n}+\beta_{2}u_{1}\gneq\tfrac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial n}+\beta_{1}u_{1}=0.

Thus,
u_{1}
is a positive strict supersolution of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u=\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{1},\Omega]u&\hbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}+\beta_{2}u=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(2.12)

Therefore, again by
[
9
, Th. 7.10]
, we obtain that

\sigma_{1}[-\Delta-\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{1},\Omega],\beta_{2},\Omega]>0,

which concludes the proof.
∎

The next result establishes a useful symmetry property of
\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0},\beta_{\omega},(0,L)]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

The principal eigenvalue of (
1.3
) is symmetric with respect to
\beta_{0}\in(-\infty,+\infty]
and
\beta_{\omega}\in(-\infty,+\infty]
, in the sense that

\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0},\beta_{\omega},(0,L)]=\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{\omega},\beta_{0},(0,L)].

Proof.

Let
u_{1}\gg 0
be a principal eigenfunction of (
1.3
). Then, the change of variable

v_{1}(y):=u_{1}(x)\quad\hbox{with}\;\;y:=L-x,\;\;y\in[0,L],

(2.13)

yields
u_{1}(0)=v_{1}(L)
and
u_{1}(L)=v_{1}(0)
. Moreover,
u_{1}^{\prime}(x)=-v_{1}^{\prime}(y)
and
u_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x)=v_{1}^{\prime\prime}(y)
for all
x\in[0,L]
. In particular,

u_{1}^{\prime}(0)=-v_{1}^{\prime}(L),\quad u_{1}^{\prime}(L)=-v_{1}^{\prime}(0).

Thus, the problem (
1.3
) becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-v_{1}^{\prime\prime}=\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0},\beta_{\omega},(0,L)]v_{1}\qquad\hbox{in}\;\;[0,L],\\[4.30554pt]
-v_{1}^{\prime}(0)+\beta_{\omega}v_{1}(0)=0,\quad v_{1}^{\prime}(L)+\beta_{0}v_{1}(L)=0.\end{array}\right.

(2.14)

Since
u_{1}\gg 0
, (
2.13
) implies that
v_{1}\gg 0
. Hence,
v_{1}
is a principal eigenfunction of (
2.14
) and, consequently, the uniqueness of the principal eigenvalue entails that

\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{\omega},\beta_{0},(0,L)]=\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},\beta_{0},\beta_{\omega},(0,L)].

The proof is complete.
∎

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The proof relies on the fact that the principal eigenvalue can be estimated explicitly. Note that, for every

\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, the general solution of the differential equation

-u^{\prime\prime}=\sigma u

(3.1)

is given by

u(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A\cos\left(\sqrt{\sigma}x\right)+B\sin\left(\sqrt{\sigma}x\right)&\hbox{if}\;\;\sigma>0,\\[4.30554pt]
Ax+B&\hbox{if}\;\;\sigma=0,\\[4.30554pt]
A\cosh\left(\sqrt{-\sigma}x\right)+B\sinh\left(\sqrt{-\sigma}x\right)&\hbox{if}\;\;\sigma<0,\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

where
A,B\in\mathbb{R}
are arbitrary constants. Moreover, multiplying by
u
and integrating by parts (
3.1
) in
[0,L]
yields to

-u(L)u^{\prime}(L)+u(0)u^{\prime}(0)+\int_{0}^{L}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dx=\sigma\int_{0}^{L}u^{2}\,dx.

(3.3)

The proof is divided into 6 different cases.

3.1
Case 1:
\mathcal{B}_{0},\mathcal{B}_{L}\in\{\mathcal{D},\mathcal{N}\}

Suppose
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\mathcal{B}_{L}=\mathcal{N}
, i.e.,
\beta_{0}=\beta_{\omega}=0
. Then,
\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},0,0,(0,L)]=0
, regardless the size of
L>0
, and, hence, in this case, Part (b) holds true. Thus, throughout the remaining of this case we will assume that

\{\mathcal{B}_{0},\mathcal{B}_{L}\}\neq\{\mathcal{N}\}
. Hence,
\beta_{0},\beta_{\omega}\in\{0,+\infty\}
with
(\beta_{0},\beta_{\omega})\neq(0,0)
.
Since
\mathcal{B}_{0},\mathcal{B}_{L}\in\{\mathcal{D},\mathcal{N}\}
,

-u(L)u^{\prime}(L)+u(0)u^{\prime}(0)=0.

Consequently, it follows from (
3.3
) that any eigenvalue
\sigma
of (
1.3
) must satisfy
\sigma\geq 0
.
Suppose
\sigma=0
. Then, by (
3.3
),
u^{\prime}=0
in
(0,L)
and, hence, there is some constant
C\in\mathbb{R}
such that
u(x)=C
for all
x\in[0,L]
. In particular,
u(0)=u(L)=C
. Consequently, as we are assuming that
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\mathcal{D}
, or
\mathcal{B}_{L}=\mathcal{D}
, we find that
C=0
. Therefore, any eigenvalue of (
1.3

4
Proof of Theorem
1.2

4.1
Proof of (
1.4
)

The proof of (
1.4
) relies primarily on the Faber–Krahn inequality established by Daners in
[
3
]
, and it uses a number of
comparisons and auxiliary results of a rather technical nature. The main goal is analyzing the limiting behavior
of
\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]
as
|\Omega|\downarrow 0
in the special case when

\beta_{\ell}:=\inf_{D}\beta>0.

(4.1)

This entails that, for every bounded domain
\Omega\subset D
with
\bar{\Omega}\subset D
,

\min_{\partial\Omega}\beta\geq\beta_{\ell}>0.

Note that
\min_{\partial\Omega}\beta
is attaint because
\partial\Omega\subset D
is compact.
Let
\varphi_{1}\gg 0
be a principal eigenfunction associated to
\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]
. Then,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\varphi_{1}=\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]\varphi_{1}&\hbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\partial\varphi_{1}}{\partial n}+\beta\varphi_{1}=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

Thanks to (
4.1
), it follows from Proposition
2.2
that

\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]\geq\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{\ell},\Omega]>\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,0,\Omega]=0.

(4.3)

Moreover, by Theorem 1.1 of Daners
[
3
]
, the ball of radius
R>0
,
B_{R}
, has the smallest principal eigenvalue within all the bounded domains
\Omega
such that
|\Omega|=|B_{R}|
. In other words,

\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{\ell},\Omega]\geq\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{\ell},B_{R}]\quad\hbox{if}\;\;|\Omega|=|B_{R}|,

(4.4)

where
\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{\ell},B_{R}]>0
is the principal eigenvalue of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\theta=\sigma\theta&\hbox{in}\;\;B_{R},\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial n}+\beta_{\ell}\theta=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial B_{R}.\end{array}\right.

(4.5)

Consequently, owing to (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), in order to prove (
1.4
) it suffices to show that

\lim_{R\downarrow 0}\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{\ell},B_{R}]=+\infty

(4.6)

because, since
|\Omega|=|B_{R}|
,
R\downarrow 0
entails
|\Omega|\downarrow 0
.

To prove (
4.6
) we can proceed as follows. Let
\theta\gg 0
be a principal eigenfunction associated to
\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{\ell},B_{R}]
. As the support domain is a ball,
\theta(x)
must be radially symmetric. Thus, there exists a
function
\xi(r)
, with
\xi^{\prime}(0)=0
, such that

\theta(x)=\xi(r),\quad\hbox{with}\;\;r:=|x|=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{N}^{2}}.

So, for every
r\in(0,R]
and
j\in\{1,...,N\}
, we have that

\tfrac{\partial\theta}{\partial x_{j}}(x)=\xi^{\prime}(r)\tfrac{\partial r}{\partial x_{j}}=\xi^{\prime}(r)\tfrac{x_{j}}{r},\quad\tfrac{\partial^{2}\theta}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}(x)=\xi^{\prime\prime}(r)\left(\tfrac{x_{j}}{r}\right)^{2}+\xi^{\prime}(r)\frac{r-\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{r}}{r^{2}},

(4.7)

and, hence, it is apparent that

\nabla_{x}\theta(x)=\xi^{\prime}(r)\tfrac{x}{r},\qquad\Delta_{x}\theta(x)=\xi^{\prime\prime}(r)+\tfrac{N-1}{r}\xi^{\prime}(r).

Furthermore,
r=R
if

Consequently, (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\xi^{\prime\prime}(r)-\tfrac{N-1}{r}\xi^{\prime}(r)=\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{\ell},B_{R}]\xi(r)\qquad\hbox{in}\;\;(0,R],\\[4.30554pt]
\xi^{\prime}(0)=0,\quad\xi^{\prime}(R)+\beta_{\ell}\xi(R)=0.\end{array}\right.

(4.8)

Next, we consider the associated one-dimensional eigenvalue problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\zeta^{\prime\prime}=\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},0,\beta_{\ell},(0,R)]\zeta\qquad\hbox{in}\;\;[0,R],\\[4.30554pt]

(4.9)

where the convection term in (

\zeta\gg 0
be the principal eigenfunction of
(
4.9

\zeta(0)=1

\zeta^{\prime}(r)<0\quad\hbox{for all}\;\;r\in(0,R].

Indeed, since

\sigma_{1}(R):=\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},0,\beta_{\ell},(0,R)]>\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},0,0,(0,R)]=0.

Thus,

for some constants

Therefore, since

Thanks to (

-\zeta^{\prime\prime}(r)-\tfrac{N-1}{r}\zeta^{\prime}(r)-\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},0,\beta_{\ell},(0,R)]\zeta(r)>-\zeta^{\prime\prime}(r)-\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},0,\beta_{\ell},(0,R)]\zeta(r)=0

for all

is a positive strict supersolution of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-u^{\prime\prime}-\tfrac{N-1}{r}u^{\prime}=\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},0,\beta_{\ell},(0,R)]u\qquad\hbox{in}\;\;(0,R],\\[4.30554pt]
u^{\prime}(0)=0,\quad u^{\prime}(R)+\beta_{\ell}u(R)=0.\end{array}\right.

(4.11)

Thus, it follows from Theorem 7.10 of
[
9
]
that

\sigma_{1}\left[-D^{2}-\tfrac{N-1}{r}D-\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},0,\beta_{\ell},(0,R)],0,\beta_{\ell},(0,R)\right]>0.

Hence, due to (
4.8

\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{\ell},B_{R}]>\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},0,\beta_{\ell},(0,R)].

(4.12)

Finally, since
\beta_{\ell}>0
, Theorem
1.1
guarantees that

\lim_{R\downarrow 0}\sigma_{1}[-D^{2},0,\beta_{\ell},(0,R)]=+\infty.

Therefore, (
4.6
) holds from (

4.2
Proof of (

Throughout this section,

\beta_{m}:=\sup_{D}\beta<0.

This implies that, for every bounded domain
\Omega
of
D

\max_{\partial\Omega}\beta\leq\sup_{D}\beta=\beta_{m}<0.

(4.14)

Let
\varphi_{1}\gg 0
be a principal eigenfunction associated to
\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\varphi_{1}=\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]\varphi_{1}&\hbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]

(4.15)

Thanks to Proposition
2.2

\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta,\Omega]\leq\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{m},\Omega]<\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,0,\Omega]=0.

Thus, to prove (

\lim_{|\Omega|\downarrow 0}\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{m},\Omega]=-\infty.

(4.16)

According to Theorems 3.1 and 4.1 of Nardi

, the boundary value problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\phi=-1&\hbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]

(4.17)

where
\phi_{0}
is a constant, possesses a solution
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})
, unique up to an additive constant, if and only if

\phi_{0}=\frac{|\Omega|}{{\rm Area}(\partial\Omega)}.

(4.18)

Suppose (
4.18
), let
\phi
be a solution of (
4.17
), and consider the function

\vartheta_{1}:=\frac{\Phi_{1}}{h}\quad\hbox{with}\;\;h:=e^{M\phi},

(4.19)

\Phi_{1}\gg 0

\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{m},\Omega]
and

is an arbitrary constant
to be chosen later. Then,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\Phi_{1}=\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{m},\Omega]\Phi_{1}&\hbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\partial\Phi_{1}}{\partial n}+\beta_{m}\Phi_{1}=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(4.20)

and differentiating (

\nabla h=Mh\nabla\phi
. Thus,

\Delta h={\rm div}\left(Mh\nabla\phi\right)=M\langle\nabla h,\nabla\phi\rangle+Mh\Delta\phi=M^{2}h|\nabla\phi|^{2}+Mh\Delta\phi.

(4.21)

On the other hand,

\begin{split}\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{m},\Omega]\vartheta_{1}h&=\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{m},\Omega]\Phi_{1}=-\Delta\Phi_{1}=-\Delta(h\vartheta_{1})\\
&=-h\Delta\vartheta_{1}-2\langle\nabla h,\nabla\vartheta_{1}\rangle-\vartheta_{1}\Delta h\end{split}

and, hence, since

-\Delta\vartheta_{1}-2M\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\vartheta_{1}\rangle-M\left(M|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\Delta\phi\right)\vartheta_{1}=\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{m},\Omega]\vartheta_{1}.

(4.23)

Moreover, on
\partial\Omega

\displaystyle\tfrac{\partial\Phi_{1}}{\partial n}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla\Phi_{1},n\rangle=\langle\nabla(\vartheta_{1}h),n\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\vartheta_{1}Mh\nabla\phi+h\nabla\vartheta_{1},n\rangle=\vartheta_{1}Mh\tfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}+h\tfrac{\partial\vartheta_{1}}{\partial n}.

Consequently, since
h>0
on
\partial\Omega
, it follows from the boundary condition
of (
4.20
) that

\tfrac{\partial\vartheta_{1}}{\partial n}+\left(M\;\tfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}+\beta_{m}\right)\vartheta_{1}=0.

(4.24)

Therefore, thanks to (
4.23

4.24

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\vartheta_{1}-2M\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\vartheta_{1}\rangle-M\left(M|\nabla\phi^{2}|+\Delta\phi\right)\vartheta_{1}=\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{m},\Omega]\vartheta_{1}&\hbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\[8.61108pt]
\tfrac{\partial\vartheta_{1}}{\partial n}+\left(M\;\tfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}+\beta_{m}\right)\vartheta_{1}=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(4.25)

Since
\phi

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\vartheta_{1}+2\tfrac{\beta_{m}}{\phi_{0}}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\vartheta_{1}\rangle+\tfrac{\beta_{m}}{\phi_{0}}\left(-\tfrac{\beta_{m}}{\phi_{0}}|\nabla\phi^{2}|+1\right)\vartheta_{1}=\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{m},\Omega]\vartheta_{1}&\hbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
\tfrac{\partial\vartheta_{1}}{\partial n}=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(4.26)

Let denote by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+2\tfrac{\beta_{m}}{\phi_{0}}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla u\rangle+\tfrac{\beta_{m}}{\phi_{0}}u=\nu u&\hbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
\tfrac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(4.27)

As
\tfrac{\beta_{m}}{\phi_{0}}
is a constant,

\nu_{1}[\beta_{m},\Omega]=\tfrac{\beta_{m}}{\phi_{0}}

and the constant function

\tfrac{\beta_{m}}{\phi_{0}}>\tfrac{\beta_{m}}{\phi_{0}}\left(-\tfrac{\beta_{m}}{\phi_{0}}|\nabla\phi^{2}|+1\right),

we can infer from (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(-\Delta+2\tfrac{\beta_{m}}{\phi_{0}}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\rangle+\tfrac{\beta_{m}}{\phi_{0}}-\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{m},\Omega]\right)\vartheta_{1}>0&\hbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
\tfrac{\partial\vartheta_{1}}{\partial n}=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(4.28)

Consequently, thanks to Theorem 7.10 of
[
9
]

\nu_{1}[\beta_{m},\Omega]=\tfrac{\beta_{m}}{\phi_{0}}=\beta_{m}\tfrac{{\rm Area}(\partial\Omega)}{|\Omega|}>\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{m},\Omega].

(4.29)

\lim_{|\Omega|\downarrow 0}\;\frac{{\rm Area}(\partial\Omega)}{|\Omega|}=+\infty.

5
Asymptotic expansion of
\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{\ell},B_{R}]
as
R\downarrow 0
in case
\beta_{\ell}>0

Let
\Phi_{1}\gg 0
be a principal eigenfunction associated to
\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{\ell},B_{R}]
. Then,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\Phi_{1}=\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{\ell},B_{R}]\Phi_{1}&\hbox{in}\;\;B_{R},\\[4.30554pt]
\tfrac{\partial\Phi_{1}}{\partial n}+\beta_{\ell}\Phi_{1}=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial B_{R}.\end{array}\right.

(5.1)

Moreover, the change of variable

\Psi_{1}(y):=\Phi_{1}(x),\qquad y:=\tfrac{x}{R},

(5.2)

transforms (
5.1

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\Psi_{1}=R^{2}\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{\ell},B_{R}]\Psi_{1}&\hbox{in}\;\;B_{1},\\[4.30554pt]
\tfrac{\partial\Psi_{1}}{\partial n}+\beta_{\ell}R\Psi_{1}=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial B_{1}.\end{array}\right.

(5.3)

Since
\Psi_{1}\gg 0
, by the uniqueness of the principal eigenvalue, it becomes apparent that

\Sigma(R):=\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{\ell}R,B_{1}]=R^{2}\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{\ell},B_{R}]>0.

(5.4)

The main result of this section can be stated as follows.

Theorem 5.1
.

The mapping

is real analytic. Moreover,

\Sigma(R)=\beta_{\ell}\frac{{\rm Area}(\partial B_{1})}{|B_{1}|}R+O(R^{2})\quad\hbox{as}\;\;R\downarrow 0.

(5.5)

Thus,

\lim_{R\downarrow 0}\frac{\Sigma(R)}{R}=\beta_{\ell}\frac{{\rm Area}(\partial B_{1})}{|B_{1}|},

and, thanks to (

\sigma_{1}[-\Delta,\beta_{\ell},B_{R}]=\frac{\Sigma(R)}{R^{2}}=\beta_{\ell}\frac{{\rm Area}(\partial B_{1})}{|B_{1}|}\frac{1}{R}+O(1)\quad\hbox{as}\;\;R\downarrow 0.

Proof.

To show the analyticity of
\Sigma(R)
we can argue as follows. The function

\phi(x):=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{1-|x|^{2}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

is analytic and it satisfies

\tfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=-1\quad\hbox{in}\;\;\partial B_{1},

where
n
stands for the outward unit
normal at
B_{1}

M>0
to be chosen later, we consider the function

\vartheta_{1}:=\frac{\Psi_{1}}{h}\quad\hbox{with}\;\;h:=e^{M\phi}.

(5.6)

Then, arguing as in Section 4.2, it is apparent that (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\vartheta_{1}-2M\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\vartheta_{1}\rangle-\left(M^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+M\Delta\phi\right)\vartheta_{1}=\Sigma(R)\vartheta_{1}&\hbox{in}\;\;B_{1},\\[4.30554pt]
\tfrac{\partial\vartheta_{1}}{\partial n}+\left(-M+\beta_{\ell}R\right)\vartheta_{1}=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial B_{1}.\end{array}\right.

(5.7)

Thus, choosing

M:=\beta_{\ell}R>0,

the problem (
5.7
) reduces to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\vartheta_{1}-2\beta_{\ell}R\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\vartheta_{1}\rangle-\beta_{\ell}\left(\beta_{\ell}R^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+R\Delta\phi\right)\vartheta_{1}=\Sigma(R)\vartheta_{1}&\hbox{in}\;\;B_{1},\\[4.30554pt]
\tfrac{\partial\vartheta_{1}}{\partial n}=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial B_{1}.\end{array}\right.

(5.8)

Consequently, since the dependence of the operator in
R
is analytic and
\Sigma(R)
is an algebraically simple eigenvalue, by Lemma 2.1.1 of
[
8
]

\Sigma(R)
must be analytic in

(see Kato
[
5
]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\Psi_{1}=\Sigma(R)\Psi_{1}&\hbox{in}\;\;B_{1},\\[4.30554pt]
\tfrac{\partial\Psi_{1}}{\partial n}+\beta_{\ell}R\Psi_{1}=0&\hbox{on}\;\;\partial B_{1}.\end{array}\right.

(5.9)

Only when
R>0
, we are going to have (
5.4
). So,
\Sigma(R)
stands for the principal eigenvalue of the problem (
5.8
) (or (
5.9

\Sigma(R)=\Sigma(0)+\dot{\Sigma}(0)R+O(R^{2})\quad\hbox{as}\;\;R\to 0,

where   “

Moreover, since
\Sigma(0)=0

\Sigma(R)=\dot{\Sigma}(0)R+O(R^{2})\quad\hbox{as}\;\;R\to 0.

(5.10)

(5.11)

According to Lemma 2.1.1 of
[
8
]

\vartheta_{1}\equiv\vartheta_{1}(R)
can be also chosen to be
analytic in
R
. Moreover, differentiating (
5.8

R

\dot{\vartheta}_{1}(R)
satisfies
\frac{\partial\dot{\vartheta}_{1}}{\partial n}(R)=0
on
\partial B_{1}
, and

\begin{split}-\Delta\dot{\vartheta}_{1}-2\beta_{\ell}\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\vartheta_{1}\rangle&-2\beta_{\ell}R\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla\dot{\vartheta}_{1}\rangle-\beta_{\ell}\left(\beta_{\ell}2R|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\Delta\phi\right)\vartheta_{1}\\
&-\beta_{\ell}\left(\beta_{\ell}R^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+R\Delta\phi\right)\dot{\vartheta}_{1}=\dot{\Sigma}(R)\vartheta_{1}+\Sigma(R)\dot{\vartheta}_{1}\end{split}

(5.12)

in

5.6
