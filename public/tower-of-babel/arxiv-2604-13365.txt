Representation of Ramanujan’s tau function by twisted divisor functions
Tianyu Ni

Abstract
. We present an infinite family of identities that represent Ramanujan’s tau function in terms of convolution sums of twisted divisor functions. Our method involves explicitly constructing non-vanishing level 1 1 cusp forms from modular forms of higher levels.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
11F11, 11A25

1.
Introduction

The Ramanujan’s tau function
\tau
is defined by the following expression:

\displaystyle\Delta(z):=q\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})^{24}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\tau(n)q^{n},

where
q=e^{2\pi iz}
with
\im(z)>0
, and the function

\Delta(z)
is a cusp form of weight
12
and level
1
.
There are various works on finding a formula for
\tau(n)
which involve convolution sums of the divisor functions, see
[
15
]
and the references therein. In this paper, we prove an infinite family of identities that represent
\tau(n)
in terms of convolution sums of twisted divisor functions that are defined below. Throughout the paper, let
D\geq 1
denote an odd square-free integer unless specified.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
l\geq 3
be an integer, and let
\phi
and
\psi
be respectively Dirichlet characters
modulo
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
such that
D=D_{1}D_{2}
and

\phi(-1)\psi(-1)=(-1)^{l}
. We define

\displaystyle\sigma_{l-1,\phi,\psi}(n):

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-L(1-l,\phi)L(0,\psi)&n=0,\\
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}>0\\
d_{1}d_{2}=n\end{subarray}}\phi(d_{1})\psi(d_{2})d_{1}^{l-1}&n\geq 1.\end{cases}

Note that
\sigma_{l-1,\phi,\psi}(n)
appear as the Fourier coefficients of some Eisenstein series (see e.g.,
[
4
, p.129]
).
To state our results concisely, we also introduce the following notation, which arises from the Fourier coefficients of certain cusp forms of level
1
; see Proposition
4.3
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo
D
, and let
\ell,k
be integers with the same parity such that
3\leq\ell\leq k
and
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{\ell}
. For
n\geq 1
, we define

\displaystyle a_{D,\ell,k,e}(n;\chi):=\sum_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}\overline{\chi}_{2}(-1)D_{2}^{-e}\hskip-5.0pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=nD_{2}\end{subarray}}\hskip-5.0pt\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}(a_{1})\sigma_{k-1,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}(a_{2})\cdot c_{e,a_{1},a_{2}},

\displaystyle c_{e,a_{1},a_{2}}:=\sum_{r=0}^{e}(-1)^{r}a_{1}^{r}a_{2}^{e-r}\binom{e+\ell-1}{e-r}\binom{e+k-1}{r},

where the summation
\sum_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}
is over all factorizations of
D=D_{1}D_{2}
as a product of two positive integers, and
\chi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
is the corresponding factorization into primitive characters modulo
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
. We write
a_{D,k,e}(n)
for
a_{D,k,k,e}(n,\chi_{D})
, where
\chi_{D}
denotes the quadratic character modulo
D
.

We can now state the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo
D
, and let

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{D,\ell,k,e}(n;\chi):=\frac{a_{D,\ell,k,e}(n;\chi)}{a_{D,\ell,k,e}(1;\chi)}.

(1)

If
\chi(-1)=-1
, then

(a.1)

\displaystyle\tau(n)

\displaystyle=\tilde{a}_{D,3,5,2}(n;\chi),

(a.2)

\displaystyle\tau(n)

\displaystyle=\tilde{a}_{D,3,7,1}(n;\chi).

(2)

If
\chi(-1)=1
, then

(b)

\displaystyle\tau(n)

\displaystyle=\tilde{a}_{D,4,6,1}(n;\chi).

(3)

If
D=1
or a prime congruent to
5\pmod{8}
, then

(c)

\displaystyle\tau(n)

\displaystyle=\tilde{a}_{D,4,2}(n).

Before going on to sketch the idea of the proof, we look at several examples.

Example 1.1
.

First, we consider the case
D=1
, where
\sigma_{l-1,\phi,\psi}(n)
becomes the divisor function
\sigma_{l-1}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d^{l-1}
for
n\geq 1
and
\sigma_{l-1}(0)=\zeta(1-l)/2
. Let
\mathbf{1}
denote the trivial character. Note that

\displaystyle a_{1,4,6,1}(n;\mathbf{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sigma_{3}(m)\sigma_{5}(n-m)\sum_{0\leq r\leq 1}(-1)^{r}m^{r}(n-m)^{1-r}\binom{4}{1-r}\binom{6}{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta(-3)}{2}\sigma_{5}(n)4n-\frac{\zeta(-5)}{2}\sigma_{3}(n)6n+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n-1}(4n-10m)\sigma_{3}(m)\sigma_{5}(n-m)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{60}\sigma_{5}(n)n+\frac{1}{84}\sigma_{3}(n)n+\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}(4n-10m)\sigma_{3}(m)\sigma_{5}(n-m).

Now, (
b
) becomes

(i)

\displaystyle\tau(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{12}n\sigma_{3}(n)+\frac{7}{12}n\sigma_{5}(n)+70\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}(2n-5m)\sigma_{3}(m)\sigma_{5}(n-m),

which also appeared in
[
15

\displaystyle a_{1,4,2}(n)=\frac{1}{12}n^{2}\sigma_{3}(n)+5\sum\limits^{n-1}_{m=1}(2n-3m)(n-3m)\sigma_{3}(m)\sigma_{3}(n-m).

Hence (
c
) becomes

(ii)

\displaystyle\tau(n)=n^{2}\sigma_{3}(n)+60\sum\limits^{n-1}_{m=1}(2n-3m)(n-3m)\sigma_{3}(m)\sigma_{3}(n-m),

as given in
[
17
, p.344]
and
[

) and (

) are examples of the van der Pol type identities for Ramanujan’s tau function
[

[

or Rankin-Cohen brackets
[

]
on Eisenstein series of level 1, see
[

[

for details.

Example 1.2
.

\displaystyle a_{p,3,7,1}(n,\chi)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{m=0}^{np}(3n-\frac{10m}{p})\sigma_{2,\mathbf{1},\overline{\chi}}(m)\sigma_{6,\mathbf{1},\chi}(np-m)

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=0}^{n}(3n-10m)\sigma_{2,\chi,\mathbf{1}}(m)\sigma_{6,\overline{\chi},\mathbf{1}}(n-m).

Here,

unless

\displaystyle a_{p,3,7,1}(n,\chi)=

\displaystyle-\sum_{m=1}^{np-1}m^{2}(3n-\frac{10m}{p})(np-m)^{6}\sigma_{-2,\overline{\chi}}(m)\sigma_{-6,\chi}(np-m)

\displaystyle+\frac{L(-2,\chi)}{2}\sigma_{6,\overline{\chi}}(n)3n-\frac{L(-6,\overline{\chi})}{2}\sigma_{2,\chi}(n)7n

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}(3n-10m)\sigma_{2,\chi}(m)\sigma_{6,\overline{\chi}}(n-m),

and for

\displaystyle a_{p,3,7,1}(1,\chi)=

\displaystyle-\sum_{m=1}^{p-1}m^{2}(3-\frac{10m}{p})(p-m)^{6}\sigma_{-2,\overline{\chi}}(m)\sigma_{-6,\chi}(p-m)

(1.1)

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{2}{L(-2,\chi)}-\frac{7}{2}L(-6,\overline{\chi}).

Example 1.3
.

It is perhaps worth mentioning one application of Theorem
1.1
to evaluate the value of the Dirichlet
L
-function. Recall the following classical result (see e.g.,
[
21
, Theorem 4.2]
). Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character
\chi
modulo
q
, and let
m
be a positive integer. Then

\displaystyle L(1-m,\chi)=-\frac{q^{m-1}}{m}\sum_{a=1}^{q}\chi(a)B_{m}(\frac{a}{q}),

where
B_{m}(x)
is the Bernoulli polynomial defined by

\displaystyle\frac{te^{xt}}{e^{t}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

For a prime
p\equiv 5\pmod{8}
and
\chi_{p}(n):=\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{n}{p}\hskip-1.0pt\right),
we use Theorem
1.1
(
c
) to give an alternative formula for
L(-3,\chi_{p})
, which does not involve Bernoulli polynomials. One can easily verify that

\displaystyle a_{p,4,2}(n)=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=D_{1}D_{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{nD_{2}}(\frac{45m^{2}}{D_{2}^{2}}-\frac{45mn}{D_{2}}+10n^{2})\sigma_{3,\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}(m)\sigma_{3,\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}(nD_{2}-m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{np-1}(\frac{45m^{2}}{p^{2}}-\frac{45mn}{p}+10n^{2})\sigma_{3,\mathbf{1},\chi_{p}}(m)\sigma_{3,\mathbf{1},\chi_{p}}(np-m)

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}(45m^{2}-45mn+10n^{2})\sigma_{3,\chi_{p}}(m)\sigma_{3,\chi_{p}}(n-m)

\displaystyle+2\cdot\frac{L(-3,\chi_{p})}{2}\cdot 10n^{2}\sigma_{3,\chi_{p}}(n).

In particular, we have

\displaystyle a_{p,4,2}(1)=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{p-1}(\frac{45m^{2}}{p^{2}}-\frac{45m}{p}+10)\sigma_{3,\mathbf{1},\chi_{p}}(m)\sigma_{3,\mathbf{1},\chi_{p}}(p-m)+10L(-3,\chi_{p}).

Note that
\sigma_{3,\chi_{p}}(2)=1+2^{3}\left(\hskip-1.0pt\frac{2}{p}\hskip-1.0pt\right)=-7

\displaystyle-5-280L(-3,\chi_{p})+\sum_{m=1}^{2p-1}(\frac{45m^{2}}{p^{2}}-\frac{90m}{p}+40)\sigma_{3,\mathbf{1},\chi_{p}}(m)\sigma_{3,\mathbf{1},\chi_{p}}(2p-m).

, (

\displaystyle L(-3,\chi_{p})=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{40}\sum_{m=1}^{2p-1}(\frac{45m^{2}}{p^{2}}-\frac{90m}{p}+40)\sigma_{3,\mathbf{1},\chi_{p}}(m)\sigma_{3,\mathbf{1},\chi_{p}}(2p-m)

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{5}\sum_{m=1}^{p-1}(\frac{45m^{2}}{p^{2}}-\frac{45m}{p}+10)\sigma_{3,\mathbf{1},\chi_{p}}(m)\sigma_{3,\mathbf{1},\chi_{p}}(p-m).

We now sketch the idea of the proof. Our method involves constructing a family of cusp forms of level
1
from forms of higher levels. To do so, we take the Rankin-Cohen brackets of Eisenstein series of higher levels and then take the trace of them, as seen in Section
2
. However, unlike the case of level
1
, where one can easily show that the construction is non-vanishing by checking that at least one Fourier coefficient is non-zero, our construction involves an additional parameter
D
, for which we are unable to show that it is non-vanishing directly. In Section
3
, we show that our construction is non-vanishing by the Rankin-Selberg method. In Section
4
, we prove our result by explicitly computing the Fourier coefficients. Finally, we conclude the paper by proposing two conjectures.

2.
Constructing level one cusp forms from higher level forms

In this section, we construct a family of cusp forms of level 1 from modular forms of higher levels. Note that this type of construction has already appeared in
[
9
]
, which was generalized in
[
8
]
and
[
14
]
.
For
N\geq 1
and a Dirichlet character
\chi
modulo
N
, let
M_{k}(N,\chi)
and
S_{k}(N,\chi)
be the space of modular forms and cusp forms of weight
k
, level
N
and nebentypus
\chi
, respectively. We write
M_{k}(N)
and
S_{k}(N)
if
\chi
is trivial, and use
M_{k}
and
S_{k}
to denote
M_{k}(1)
and
S_{k}(1)
when
N=1
.

The Rankin–Cohen bracket of two modular forms
[
16
,
2
]
, generalizing the product of two modular forms, produces a cusp form.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f(z)\in M_{a}(N,\nu_{1})
and
g(z)\in M_{b}(N,\nu_{2})
be modular forms of level
N
, weights
a
and
b
and nebentypus
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
, respectively. For an integer
e\geq 1
, the
e
-th Rankin-Cohen bracket is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle[f(z),g(z)]_{e}:=\sum_{r=0}^{e}(-1)^{r}\binom{e+a-1}{e-r}\binom{e+b-1}{r}f(z)^{(r)}g(z)^{(e-r)},

where
f(z)^{(r)}
is the
r
-th normalized derivative
f(z)^{(r)}:=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{r}}\frac{d^{r}f(z)}{dz^{r}}
of
f
. Moreover,
[f,g]_{e}\in S_{a+b+2e}(N,\nu_{1}\nu_{2})
; see
[
2
, Theorem 7.1]
.
Note that the definition in Zagier
[
22
, (73)]
is related to (
2.1
) through
F_{e}^{(a,b)}(f(z),g(z))=(-2\pi i)^{e}e![f(z),g(z)]_{e}
.

We introduce a type of Eisenstein series; see also
[
9
, pp. 185]
and
[
13
, pp. 269-270]
.
Assume
k\geq 3
. Let
D\geq 1
be an odd square-free integer and
\chi
be a primitive character mod
D
such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. Define

\displaystyle G_{k,\chi}(z):

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sigma_{k-1,\chi}(n)q^{n}\in M_{k}(D,\chi),

\displaystyle\sigma_{k-1,\chi}(n):

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{L(1-k,\chi)}{2}&n=0,\\
\sum\limits_{d\mid n}\chi(d)d^{k-1}&n\geq 1.\end{cases}

Here,
L(s,\chi)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\chi(n)n^{-s}
(
\re(s)\gg 0
) is the Dirichlet series.

Now, we construct a family of cusp forms of level one by taking the trace of Rankin-Cohen brackets of the aforementioned Eisenstein series.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\chi
be a primitive character mod
D
, and let
\chi_{D}
denote the quadratic character mod
D
. For
e\geq 1
and integers
\ell,k
with the same parity, such that
3\leq\ell\leq k
and
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{\ell}
, we define

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}(z;\chi):=\begin{cases}\Tr^{D}_{1}[G_{\ell,\chi}(z),G_{k,\overline{\chi}}(z)]_{e}&\ell<k,\\
\Tr^{D}_{1}[G_{k,\chi_{D}}(z),G_{k,\chi_{D}}(z)]_{e}&\ell=k,\end{cases}

where
\Tr_{1}^{D}
is the trace map

\displaystyle\Tr_{1}^{D}:M_{m}(D)\rightarrow M_{m}(1),\quad g\mapsto\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(D)\backslash\Gamma_{0}(1)}g|_{m}\gamma,

and for
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\Gl_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
the slash operator
[
2
, Theorem 7.1]
is

(2.3)

\displaystyle g(z)|_{m}\gamma=\det(\gamma)^{m/2}(cz+d)^{-m}g\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right).

We write
\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}(z)
for
\mathcal{F}_{D,k,k,e}(z;\chi_{D})
.

3.
Non-vanishing of the construction

In this section, we show that
\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}
is not identically zero in several cases. The idea is to use the Rankin-Selberg method (see e.g.,
[
1
]
for an exposition) to transform the problem into the non-vanishing of twisted
L
-values.
For
f,g\in M_{k}(N,\chi)
such that
fg
is a cusp form, the Petersson inner product is given by

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{N}=\int_{\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}y^{k}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}.

We write
\Gamma_{\infty}:=\{\pm\begin{pmatrix}1&n\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Let
E_{k,N}^{\ast}(z;\chi)
be the Eisenstein series for the cusp at infinity of level
N

[
13
, p. 272]
:

\displaystyle E_{k,N}^{\ast}(z;\chi):=\sum_{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(N)}\frac{\chi(d)}{(cz+d)^{k}}.

We first consider the case
\ell<k
.
Recall the classical result on the Rankin-Selberg method below, which was reformulated and generalized in Zagier
[
22
]
, keeping in mind the difference between (
2.1
) and the one used therein.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
22
, Proposition 6]
)
.

Let
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
be real numbers with
k_{2}\geq k_{1}+2>2
. Let
f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a(n)q^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}b(n)q^{n}
be modular forms in
S_{k}(N,\chi)
and
M_{k_{1}}(N,\chi_{1})
, where
k=k_{1}+k_{2}+2e,e\geq 0
and
\chi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
.
Then

(3.1)

\displaystyle\langle f,[g,E^{*}_{k_{2},N}(\cdot;\chi_{2})]_{e}\rangle_{N}=\frac{(-1)^{e}}{e!}\frac{\Gamma(k-1)\Gamma(k_{2}+e)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}\Gamma(k_{2})}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)\overline{b(n)}}{n^{k_{1}+k_{2}+e-1}}.

For a normalized Hecke eigenform
f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n)q^{n}
in
S_{K}
, its twisted Hecke
L
-series is given by

L(f,\chi,s):=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n)\chi(n)n^{-s},

which is holomorphic in the reigion
\re(s)>1+\frac{K-1}{2}
(due to Deligne’s bound). A standard computation gives the following result; see also
[
22
, (72)]
.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
14
, Lemma 2.5]
)
.

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n)q^{n}\in S_{K}
be a normalized Hecke eigenform. Then for a Dirichlet character
\chi
, an integer
\ell\geq 3
and a complex number
s
with
\re(s)>\ell+\frac{K-1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{f}(n)\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi}(n)}{n^{s}}=\frac{L(f,s)L(f,\chi,s-\ell+1)}{L(2s-\ell+2-K,\chi)}.

The following proposition is likely well-known to experts, but we include a proof here due to the lack of an exact reference.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose
\ell<k
. If
f\in S_{k+\ell+2e}
is a normalized Hecke eigenform, then

\displaystyle\langle f,\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}\rangle_{1}=

\displaystyle\tilde{c}\cdot L(f,k+\ell+e-1)L(f,\overline{\chi},k+e),

where
\tilde{c}
is some non-zero constant depending on
k,\ell,e
and
\chi
.

Proof.

Since
\langle f,g\rangle_{N}=\langle f,\Tr^{D}_{1}g\rangle_{1}
for
f\in S_{K}
and
g\in M_{K}(D)
(see e.g.,
[
7
, p.271]
), we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\langle f,\mathcal{F}_{D,k,\ell,e}\rangle_{1}=\langle f,[G_{\ell,\chi}(z),G_{k,\overline{\chi}}(z)]_{e}\rangle_{D}.

Note that (see e.g.,
[
14
, Lemma 2.2]
):

(3.3)

\displaystyle G_{k,\overline{\chi}}(z)=\frac{(k-1)!D^{k}L(k,\chi)}{(-2\pi i)^{k}\tau(\chi)}E_{k,D}^{\ast}(z;\chi),

where
\tau(\chi)
is the Gauss sum. By Lemma
3.1
, (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we get

(3.4)

\displaystyle\langle f,\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}\rangle_{1}=c\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{f}(n)\sigma_{\ell-1,\overline{\chi}}(n)}{n^{k+\ell+e-1}}.

Here,
c
is some non-zero constant depending only on
\ell,k,e
and
\chi
. Note that for
K=k+\ell+2e
and
s=k+\ell+e-1
, we have

\re(s)-(\ell+\frac{K-1}{2})=\frac{k-\ell-1}{2}>0
since
k-\ell\geq 2
. Hence, from Lemma
3.2
we deduce that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{f}(n)\sigma_{\ell-1,\overline{\chi}}(n)}{n^{k+\ell+e-1}}=\frac{L(f,k+\ell+e-1)L(f,\overline{\chi},k+e)}{L(k,\overline{\chi})}.

Now, the result follows from (
3.4
) and (
3.5
).
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose
\ell<k
. If
\dim S_{k+\ell+2e}\geq 1
then
\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}
is not identically zero.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}\equiv 0
. Then
\langle f,\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}\rangle_{1}=0
for all normalized Hecke eigenforms
f
in
S_{k+\ell+2e}
.
On the other hand, by Proposition
3.3
and the fact that
k+\ell+e-1
and
k+e
are respectively within the regions of absolute convergence of
L(f,s)
and
L(f,\overline{\chi},s)
, we get
\langle f,\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}\rangle_{1}\neq 0
, leading to a contradiction.
∎

For
n\geq 1
, let
a_{D,\ell,k,e}(n;\chi)
denote the
q^{n}
-coefficient of
\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}(z;\chi)
, and let

(3.6)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{D,\ell,k,e}(z;\chi):=\frac{\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}(z;\chi)}{a_{D,\ell,k,e}(1;\chi)},

where we understand that
a_{D,\ell,k,e}(1,\chi)
has to be nonzero to validate the definition of
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{D,\ell,k,e}(z;\chi)
. See Conjecture
5.1
for the non-vanishing of
a_{D,\ell,k,e}(1,\chi)
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character mod
D
. Then

\displaystyle\Delta(z)=\begin{cases}\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{D,3,5,2}(z;\chi)=\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{D,3,7,1}(z;\chi)&\chi(-1)=-1,\\
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{D,4,6,1}(z;\chi)&\chi(-1)=1.\end{cases}

Proof.

It is immediate from Proposition
3.4
and the fact that
\Delta(z)
is the unique normalized Hecke eigenform in
S_{12}
.
∎

We now consider the case
\ell=k
, where we can not use Lemma
3.1
to compute
\langle f,\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}\rangle_{1}
. To tackle this difficulty, we can follow Shimura
[
19
]
and Lanphier
[
11
]
; see
[
8
, pp. 21-23]
for complete details. Here we only state the result.

Proposition 3.6

(
[
8
, Proposition 5.6]
)
.

Suppose
e\equiv 0\pmod{2}
,
k\geq 4
and
\chi_{D}(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. If
f\in S_{2k+2e}
is a normalized Hecke eigenform, then

\displaystyle\langle f,\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}\rangle_{1}=\frac{\Gamma(2k+2e-1)\Gamma(k+e)L(1-k,\chi_{D})}{2e!(4\pi)^{2k+2e-1}\Gamma(k)L(k,\chi_{D})}L(f,2k+e-1)L(f,\chi_{D},k+e).

Now, the non-vanishing of
\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}
comes from the following result on the non-vanishing of the twisted central
L
-value due to Kohnen-Zagier
[
9
]
.

Proposition 3.7

(
[
9
, Corollary 2]
)
.

For
k\equiv 2\pmod{4}
and
D
a prime congruent to
5\pmod{8}
, there is at least one Hecke eigenform
f\in S_{2k}
for which both
L(f,k)
and
L(f,\chi_{D},k)
are different from zero.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
e>0
be integers such that
e\equiv 0\pmod{2}
and
k+e\equiv 2\pmod{4}
, and
D
be a prime congruent to
5\pmod{8}
. Then
\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}
is not identically zero.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}\equiv 0
. Then
\langle f,\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}\rangle_{1}=0
for all normalized Hecke eigenforms
f
in
S_{2k+2e}
, implying that
L(f,\chi_{D},k+e)=0
by Proposition
3.6
, which contradicts Proposition
3.7
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
D=1
or a prime congruent to
5\pmod{8}
. Then

\displaystyle\Delta(z)=\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{D,4,2}(z;\chi_{D}).

Proof.

The case
D=1
is trivial. The other case is immediate from Proposition
3.8
and the fact that
\Delta(z)
is the unique normalized Hecke eigenform in
S_{12}
.
∎

4.
Computation of the Fourier coefficients

In this section, we compute the Fourier coefficients
a_{D,\ell,k,e}(n;\chi)
. We introduce another type of Eisenstein series; see also
[
9
, p.193]
and
[
13
, pp. 269-270]
. Let
D=D_{1}D_{2}
such that
D_{1}>0
, and
\chi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
, where
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
are primitive Dirichlet characters mod
D_{1}
and mod
D_{2}
, respectively. Define

\displaystyle G_{k,\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}(z):

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sigma_{k-1,\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}(n)q^{n},

\displaystyle\sigma_{k-1,\chi_{1},\chi_{2}}(n):

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-L(1-k,\chi_{1})L(0,\chi_{2})&n=0,\\
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}>0\\
d_{1}d_{2}=n\end{subarray}}\chi_{1}(d_{1})\chi_{2}(d_{2})d_{1}^{k-1}&n\geq 1.\end{cases}

The following lemma is useful for computing the trace; see also
[
7
, pp.273-275]
.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
14
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Let
D=D_{1}D_{2}
with
D_{1}>0

and
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character mod
D
such that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}
. If
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
is a matrix in
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
\gcd(c,D)=D_{1}
then

(4.1)

\displaystyle G_{k,\chi}(z)\bigg|_{k}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\chi_{2}(c)\chi_{1}(d)\overline{\chi}_{1}(D_{2})\overline{\chi}_{2}(D_{1})\frac{G(\overline{\chi}_{1})}{G(\overline{\chi})}G_{k,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}\left(\frac{z+c^{\ast}d}{D_{2}}\right),

where
\chi=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}
is the product of primitive characters
\chi_{1}
mod
D_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
mod
D_{2}
, and
c^{\ast}
is an integer with
cc^{\ast}\equiv 1\pmod{D_{2}}
and
D_{1}\mid c^{\ast}
.

For
m\geq 1
and
f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{f}(n)q^{n}\in M_{k}(N,\chi)
we define the
U
-operator

(4.2)

\displaystyle U_{m}f(z)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{v~{\rm mod}~m}f\left(\frac{z+v}{m}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{f}(mn)q^{n}.

Equivalently, we may write

\displaystyle U_{m}f(z)=m^{k/2-1}\sum_{v\text{ mod }m}f(z)\bigg|_{k}\begin{pmatrix}1&v\\
0&m\end{pmatrix}.

We are ready to compute
a_{D,\ell,k,e}(n;\chi)
. The special case
e=0
and
k=\ell
was computed by Kohnen-Zagier
[
22
, p.193]
, and some other special cases have been calculated in
[
8
,
14
]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}(z;\chi)
be defined as in (
2.2
). Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}(z;\chi)=\sum_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}\overline{\chi}_{2}(-1)D_{2}^{-e}U_{D_{2}}\left([G_{\ell,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}(z),G_{k,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}(z)]_{e}\right).

Proof.

Consider the following system of representatives of
\Gamma_{0}(D)\backslash\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
(see
[
7
, p. 276]
and
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
):

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
D_{1}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\mu\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}~:~D=D_{1}D_{2},~\mu\text{ mod }D_{2}\right\}.

By Lemma
4.1
and the fact that the slash operator (
2.3
) commutes with the Rankin-Cohen bracket, we have
\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}(z;\chi)=

\displaystyle\sum_{D_{1}D_{2}=D}\sum_{\mu\text{ mod }D_{2}}\left[G_{\ell,\chi}(z),G_{k,\overline{\chi}}(z)\right]_{e}\bigg|_{k+\ell+2e}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
D_{1}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\mu\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{D_{1}D_{2}=D}\sum_{\mu\text{ mod }D_{2}}\left[G_{\ell,\chi}(z)\bigg|_{\ell}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
D_{1}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\mu\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},G_{k,\overline{\chi}}(z)\bigg|_{k}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
D_{1}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\mu\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right]_{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{D_{1}D_{2}=D}\sum_{\mu\text{ mod }D_{2}}\hskip-5.0pt\overline{\chi}_{2}(-1)D_{2}^{-1}\left[G_{\ell,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}\left(\frac{z+\mu+D_{1}^{*}}{D_{2}}\right),G_{k,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}\left(\frac{z+\mu+D_{1}^{*}}{D_{2}}\right)\right]_{e},

where we used
G(\chi_{1})G(\overline{\chi}_{1})=\chi_{1}(-1)D_{1}
in the last equality (see e.g.,
[
3
, Corollary 2.1.47]
). On the other hand,
U_{D_{2}}([G_{\ell,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}(z),G_{k,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}(z)]_{e})=

\displaystyle\sum_{v~\text{mod}~D_{2}}D_{2}^{(k+\ell+2e)/2-1}\left[G_{\ell,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}(z)\bigg|_{\ell}\begin{pmatrix}1&v\\
0&D_{2}\end{pmatrix},G_{k,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}(z)\bigg|_{k}\begin{pmatrix}1&v\\
0&D_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right]_{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v~\text{mod}~D_{2}}D_{2}^{(k+\ell+2e)/2-1}\left[D_{2}^{-\ell/2}G_{\ell,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}\left(\frac{z+v}{D_{2}}\right),D_{2}^{-k/2}G_{k,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}\left(\frac{z+v}{D_{2}}\right)\right]_{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v~\text{mod}~D_{2}}D_{2}^{e}D_{2}^{-1}\left[G_{\ell,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}\left(\frac{z+v}{D_{2}}\right),G_{k,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}\left(\frac{z+v}{D_{2}}\right)\right]_{e},

which gives the desired result.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

For
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle a_{D,\ell,k,e}(n;\chi)=\hskip-5.0pt\sum_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}\overline{\chi}_{2}(-1)D_{2}^{-e}\hskip-5.0pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=nD_{2}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}(a_{1})\sigma_{k-1,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}(a_{2})\cdot c_{e,a_{1},a_{2}},

\displaystyle c_{e,a_{1},a_{2}}:=\sum_{r=0}^{e}(-1)^{r}a_{1}^{r}a_{2}^{e-r}\binom{e+\ell-1}{e-r}\binom{e+k-1}{r}.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.2
and (
4.2
), we have

\displaystyle g(n)=\sum_{D=D_{1}D_{2}}\overline{\chi}_{2}(-1)D_{2}^{-e}h(nD_{2}),

where
h(n)
denotes the
q^{n}
-coefficient of
[G_{\ell,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}(z),G_{k,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}(z)]_{e}
. Note that

\displaystyle G_{\ell,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}(z)^{(r)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n^{r}\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}(n)q^{n}.

Thus, by (
2.1
), we get
h(nD_{2})=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{e}(-1)^{r}\binom{e+\ell-1}{e-r}\binom{e+k-1}{r}\hskip-7.5pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=nD_{2}\end{subarray}}\hskip-7.5pta_{1}^{r}\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}(a_{1})\cdot a_{2}^{e-r}\sigma_{\ell-1,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}(a_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0\\
a_{1}+a_{2}=nD_{2}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{\ell-1,\chi_{1},\overline{\chi}_{2}}(a_{1})\sigma_{\ell-1,\overline{\chi}_{1},\chi_{2}}(a_{2})\cdot c_{e,a_{1},a_{2}},

giving the desired result.
∎

Now, Theorem
1.1
follows immediately from Propositions
3.5
,
3.9
and
4.3
.

5.
Discussion

First, we would like to mention that one can construct more identities that involve
\tau(n)
by applying Propositions
3.4
and
3.8
to the cases when
S_{k+\ell+2e}
has dimension
1
, where
\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}
must be a Hecke eigenform. It is also possible to obtain more identities by considering Eisenstein series of weight
2
of higher levels.

We make some remarks about the construction of
\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}
. A natural question is whether we can show that
\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}
is not identically zero without using the Rankin-Selberg method. For example, one can try to show that at least one Fourier coefficient of
\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}
is non-zero. Unfortunately, even the non-vanishing of
a_{p,3,7,1}(1;\chi)
(see (
1.1
)) doesn’t seem obvious to the author. However, one can approach this question by considering the scenario when the weight
K=k+\ell+2e
is large. In
[
14
]
, the authors considered the case where
e=0
and
k=\ell
(the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{D,k,\ell,e}
needs to be modified), and showed that if
K\geq 10D+2
, then its first Fourier coefficient is non-zero; see
[
14
, Proposition 5.6]
. It is therefore reasonable to propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 5.1
.

Let
K=k+\ell+2e
. Then
a_{D,\ell,k,e}(1;\chi)
is not zero for
K\gg_{D}1
.

Finally, note that
\mathcal{F}_{D,\ell,k,e}
is not a Hecke eigenform in general when
S_{k+\ell+2e}
has dimension greater than
1
, and the case
\ell<k
can be proved by
using the non-vanishing of twisted
L
-values as in Proposition
3.4
.
The case
\ell=k
is trickier since we don’t know the non-vanishing of twisted central
L
-value
L(f,\chi_{D},k+e)
. One special case,
D=1
and
e=0
(where we need to modify the definition to the cuspidal projection of Eisenstein series), has been considered in
[
6
]
; see
[
6
, Theorem 4.2]
. We propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 5.2
.

Suppose
\dim S_{2k+2e}>1
. Then
\mathcal{F}_{D,k,e}
is not a Hecke eigenform.
