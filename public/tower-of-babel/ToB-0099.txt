## What Is This?

George Lincoln Burr's *The Literature of Witchcraft* (1903) is the foundational scholarly bibliography and historical essay on the subject. Originally published as an academic reference work, it surveys the entire corpus of witchcraft literature from the late medieval period through the 19th century — covering everything from the *Malleus Maleficarum* through Bodin, Weyer, James, the Salem trials, and beyond.

## Who Was George Lincoln Burr?

Burr (1857–1938) was an American historian and professor at Cornell University. He was one of the first academics to approach witchcraft history with genuine scholarly rigor, applying source criticism and historical methodology to the study of witch trials. He also edited the influential *Narratives of the Witch Trials* (1896).

## What the Book Covers

Burr's essay is organized thematically and chronologically, covering:

- **The medieval background** — How witchcraft beliefs developed before the great witch panics
- **The Reformation connection** — Why witch hunting intensified in Protestant and Catholic regions simultaneously
- **The key treatises** — In-depth analysis of Bodin, Weyer, James, Delrio, and dozens of others
- **The legal literature** — How legal scholars debated witchcraft in courts
- **Confession literature** — The genre of published witch confessions
- **The decline** — How Enlightenment skepticism ended the witch trials

## Why It Matters

No serious study of witchcraft history is complete without Burr. His essay provides the essential framework for understanding how the literature of witchcraft developed, what the major texts said, and how they influenced each other. For researchers building a witchcraft library, Burr is the map that shows you how everything connects.

## How to Use This Book## How to Use This Book

Think of Burr as the index and annotated bibliography for the entire field of witchcraft studies. Read the essay to understand the landscape, then use his citations to find primary sources. Burr's own collection of original documents is available separately as *Narratives of the Witch Trials* (ToB: not yet acquired — future target).
