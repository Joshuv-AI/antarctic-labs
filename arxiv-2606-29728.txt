Ideal n n -cotorsion pairs in Frobenius extriangulated categories 0 0 footnotetext: Panyue Zhou is supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant No. 12371034) and by the Scientific Research Fund of Hunan Provincial Education Department (Grant No. 24A0221).
Yixia Zhang, Panyue Zhou

Abstract
Motivated by the correspondence between ideal cotorsion pairs in Frobenius exact categories and those in their stable categories, we introduce the notion of an ideal n n -cotorsion pair in an extriangulated category. We study the relationship between ideal n n -cotorsion pairs in a Frobenius extriangulated category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} and those in its stable category 𝒞 ¯ = 𝒞 / ω \underline{\mathcal{C}}=\mathcal{C}/\omega . Our main result shows that ( ℐ , 𝒥 ) (\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}) is an ideal n n -cotorsion pair in 𝒞 \mathcal{C} if and only if ( ℐ / ω , 𝒥 / ω ) (\mathcal{I}/\omega,\mathcal{J}/\omega) is an ideal n n -cotorsion pair in 𝒞 ¯ \underline{\mathcal{C}} . This provides a bridge between higher ideal approximation theory in Frobenius extriangulated categories and its counterpart in their stable categories. Additionally, in Krull–Schmidt exact categories, we establish a bijective correspondence between complete cotorsion pairs and complete ideal cotorsion pairs, answering a question of Fu, Guil Asensio, Herzog and Torrecillas. Keywords: extriangulated category; approximation theory; ideal cotorsion pair; n n -cotorsion pair; stable category 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 18G15; 18G25; 18G80; 18E40



1
Introduction

Approximation theory is a fundamental tool in homological algebra and representation theory. Its basic idea is to study complicated objects by means of relatively simple ones. It has been widely used to construct resolutions, define derived functors and investigate homological dimensions in module categories, abelian categories and exact categories. The notion of cotorsion pairs, introduced by Salce
[
19
]
in the category of abelian groups, provides a central framework for classical approximation theory. It was later developed in various settings, including sheaf categories
[
6
]
, Grothendieck categories
[
8
,
16
]
and exact categories
[
20
]
. In particular, complete cotorsion pairs connect precovers, preenvelopes and special approximations in a unified way.

Classical approximation theory is mainly formulated in terms of objects and subcategories. However, many approximation phenomena are naturally governed by morphisms rather than by objects alone. To address this point, Fu, Guil Asensio, Herzog and Torrecillas
[
7
]
introduced ideal approximation theory, replacing subcategories by ideals of morphisms and developing the notion of ideal cotorsion pairs. This framework extends classical approximation theory to the morphism level and establishes a close relation between special precovering ideals and complete ideal cotorsion pairs in exact categories. Breaz and Modoi
[
1
]
further developed ideal approximation theory in triangulated categories. One striking feature of the triangulated setting is that every precovering or preenveloping ideal is automatically special, while this is no longer true in general exact categories. This contrast indicates that ideal approximation behaves differently in exact and triangulated contexts, and therefore motivates the study of intermediate and unifying settings.

Frobenius exact categories provide such a bridge. They are exact categories with enough projective and enough injective objects, and the projective objects coincide with the injective objects. If
\omega
denotes the full subcategory of projective-injective objects, then the stable category
\mathcal{C}/\omega
carries a canonical triangulated structure. Thus it is natural to compare ideal approximation theory in a Frobenius category with that in its stable category. Recently, Sun, Tan, Wang and Zhu
[
21
]
proved that, under suitable assumptions on ideals containing projective objects, the precovering property, the special precovering property and the completeness of ideal cotorsion pairs in a Frobenius category are equivalent to the corresponding properties in its stable category.
On the other hand, higher homological methods have led to higher versions of cotorsion theory. Mao and Ding
[
17
]
studied cotorsion dimension and higher-order orthogonality via the functor
{\rm Ext}^{n+1}(-,-)
. Further developments include Gorenstein
n
-cotorsion modules, Gorenstein
n
-flat covers and
n
-cotorsion envelopes over
n
-coherent rings
[
10
]
.
The notion of
n
-cotorsion pairs was introduced in
[
10
]
as a higher analogue of complete cotorsion pairs, motivated by approximation phenomena in Gorenstein homological algebra. Its basic idea is to replace the usual first-extension orthogonality by higher extension vanishing up to degree
n
, together with suitable finite resolution or coresolution conditions. This framework recovers complete cotorsion pairs when
n=1
, and connects higher approximation theory with Gorenstein modules, chain complexes, and cluster tilting theory
[
10
]
. It was later extended to extriangulated categories, where
n
-cotorsion pairs of the form
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X})
correspond to
(n+1)
-cluster tilting subcategories
[
11
]
. More recent developments study mutation of
n
-cotorsion pairs in triangulated and extriangulated settings, showing that such pairs are stable under mutation and admit geometric interpretations in higher cluster categories
[
3
,
4
]
.

Extriangulated categories, introduced as a common generalization of exact categories and triangulated categories, provide a natural framework in which both ideal approximation theory and higher cotorsion theory can be considered simultaneously. Motivated by the above developments, we introduce the notion of an ideal
n
-cotorsion pair in an extriangulated category. This notion combines the morphism-level viewpoint of ideal cotorsion pairs with the higher orthogonality underlying
n
-cotorsion pairs.
The main purpose of this paper is to compare ideal
n
-cotorsion pairs in a Frobenius extriangulated category with those in its stable category. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Frobenius extriangulated category, and let
\omega
be the full subcategory of projective-injective objects. We write
\underline{\mathcal{C}}=\mathcal{C}/\omega
for the stable category. For ideals
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
of
\mathcal{C}
, we denote by
\mathcal{I}/\omega
and
\mathcal{J}/\omega
their induced ideals in
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
. Our main result states that
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})
is an ideal
n
-cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
(\mathcal{I}/\omega,\mathcal{J}/\omega)
is an ideal
n
-cotorsion pair in
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
.
This theorem shows that the higher ideal cotorsion structure of a Frobenius extriangulated category can be detected after passing to its stable category. In particular, when
\mathcal{C}
is a Frobenius exact category and
n=1
, our result recovers the correspondence studied in
[
21
]
. Thus the present work extends the relation between ideal approximation theory in Frobenius categories and stable categories to the higher ideal setting.

Inspired by the work of Fu, Guil Asensio, Herzog and Torrecillas
[
7
]
, we study the relationship between complete cotorsion pairs and complete ideal cotorsion pairs in extriangulated categories. In
[
7
, Question 28]
, the authors asked whether a complete ideal cotorsion pair whose two ideals are object ideals necessarily arises from a complete cotorsion pair. We provide a positive answer to this question in the Krull-Schmidt setting. Specifically, we prove two main results: Theorem
3.5
shows that every complete cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
in an extriangulated category gives rise to a complete ideal cotorsion pair
([\mathcal{X}],[\mathcal{Y}])
; conversely, Theorem
3.11
establishes that under the Krull-Schmidt assumption, every complete ideal cotorsion pair of the form
([\mathcal{X}],[\mathcal{Y}])
comes from a complete cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
. As a consequence, in Krull–Schmidt exact categories, we obtain an affirmative answer to Question 28 of
[
7
]
. A related result for Frobenius exact categories was given in
[
21
, Corollary 1.3]
; however, our approach is direct and does not rely on stable category techniques, thus applying to arbitrary Krull–Schmidt exact categories.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2,
we recall the definition of extriangulated categories and some basic facts.
In Section 3, we answer a question of Fu, Guil Asensio, Herzog and Torrecillas
[
7
, Question 28]
by establishing, in the Krull-Schmidt setting, a bijective correspondence between complete cotorsion pairs and complete ideal cotorsion pairs (see Theorems
3.5
and
3.11
).
In Section 4, we introduce ideal
n
-cotorsion pairs in extriangulated categories, establish their elementary properties, prove the main correspondence theorem (see Theorem
4.7
), and give some applications.

2.3
Orthogonality of ideals and ideal approximations

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an extriangulated category,
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}
be ideals of
\mathcal{C}
,
k
any positive integer. An ideal
\mathcal{I}
in
\mathcal{C}
is an additive two-sided ideal of its morphism category, i.e., a collection of subgroups
\mathcal{I}(X,Y)\subseteq\mbox{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
closed under composition with arbitrary morphisms on both sides for any objects
X,\ Y
in
\mathcal{C}
.

Denote
{\rm Ob}(\mathcal{I})=\{X\in\mathcal{C}\ |\ 1_{X}\in\mathcal{I}\}
. It is easy to see that
{\rm Ob}(\mathcal{I})
is closed under direct sums and direct summands.

Two morphisms

are called
Hom
-orthogonal if
\mbox{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(f,g)=0
.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be a collection of morphisms of
\mathcal{C}
, define left
Hom
-orthogonal ideal and right
Hom
-orthogonal ideal as follows:

{}^{\bot}\mathcal{S}=\{f\ |\ \mbox{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(f,s)=0\ \text{for any morphism}\ s\in\mathcal{S}\},

\mathcal{S}^{\bot}=\{g\ |\ \mbox{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(s,g)=0\ \text{for any morphism}\ s\in\mathcal{S}\}.

Two morphisms
f\colon X\rightarrow Y,\ g\colon A\rightarrow B
are called Ext-orthogonal if
\mathbb{E}(f,g)=0
.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be a collection of morphisms of
\mathcal{C}
, define left
k
-th Ext-orthogonal ideal and right
k
-th Ext-orthogonal ideal as follows:

{}^{\bot_{k}}\mathcal{S}=\{f\ |\ \mathbb{E}^{k}(f,s)=0\ \text{for any morphism}\ s\in\mathcal{S}\},

\mathcal{S}^{\bot_{k}}=\{g\ |\ \mathbb{E}^{k}(s,g)=0\ \text{for any morphism}\ s\in\mathcal{S}\}.

A morphism
f\colon X\rightarrow M
in
\mathcal{I}
is called an
\mathcal{I}
-precover, if for any morphism

in
\mathcal{I}
, there exists a morphism

such that

. A morphism

\mathcal{J}
is called a
\mathcal{J}
-preenvelope, if for any morphism

in
\mathcal{J}
, there exists a morphism
h\colon Y\rightarrow Y^{\prime}
such that
g=hf
.

The ideal
\mathcal{I}
is precovering, if for any object

, there exists an
\mathcal{I}
-precover of
M
. The ideal
\mathcal{J}
is preenveloping, if for any object

, there exists a
\mathcal{J}
-preenvelope of
M
.

\mathcal{I}

\mathcal{I}
-precover, if

-triangles

-preenvelope, if

3
Comparison between complete cotorsion pairs and complete ideal cotorsion pairs

We begin by recalling the definitions of complete cotorsion pairs and complete ideal cotorsion pairs.

Definition 3.1
.

[
18
, Definition 4.1]

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an extriangulated category,
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
subcategories closed under isomorphisms and direct summands. The pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is called a complete cotorsion pair, if it satisfies the following conditions.

(1)

\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})=0
.

(2)

For any
C\in\mathcal{C}
, there exists an
\mathbb{E}
-triangle

Y^{C}\longrightarrow X^{C}\longrightarrow C\dashrightarrow,

where
X^{C}\in\mathcal{X},Y^{C}\in\mathcal{Y}
.

(3)

For any
C\in\mathcal{C}
, there exists an
\mathbb{E}
-triangle

C\longrightarrow Y_{C}\longrightarrow X_{C}\dashrightarrow,

where
X_{C}\in\mathcal{X},Y_{C}\in\mathcal{Y}
.

Definition 3.2
.

[
22
, Definition 3.8]

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an extriangulated category. An ideal pair
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})
is called an ideal cotorsion pair, if
\mathcal{I}^{\bot_{1}}=\mathcal{J},\ \mathcal{I}={{}^{\bot_{1}}\mathcal{J}}
. Moreover, an ideal cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})
is complete, if
\mathcal{I}
is a special precovering ideal and
\mathcal{J}
is a special preenveloping ideal.

Remark 3.3
.

(1)
[
1
, Theorem 5.3.4]
In a triangulated category,
\mathcal{I}
is special precovering if and only if
\mathcal{I}
is precovering. When
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})
is an ideal cotorsion pair,
\mathcal{I}
is special precovering if and only if
\mathcal{J}
is special preenveloping.

(2)
[
22
, Lemma 4.5]
In an extriangulated category with enough projective and enough injective objects, when
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})
is an ideal cotorsion pair,
\mathcal{I}
is special precovering if and only if
\mathcal{J}
is special preenveloping.

We collect some examples of complete ideal cotorsion pairs.

Example 3.4
.

(1)
[
9
, Corollary 4.19]

Let
R
be a Gorenstein ring and let
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}
denote the class of right
R
-modules of finite projective dimension.
Recall that a morphism
f\colon A\to X
is
\mathcal{G}
-phantom if the pullback of every short exact sequence with end term
X
along
f
is
{\rm Hom}_{R}(-,\mathcal{P}^{<\infty})
-exact.
A morphism
g\colon M\to N
is
\mathcal{G}
-injective if the pushout of every
{\rm Hom}_{R}(-,\mathcal{P}^{<\infty})
-exact sequence with initial term
M
along
g
is split.
Let
\varphi(\mathcal{G}\text{-xt})
denote the class of
\mathcal{G}
-phantom morphisms, and let
\mathcal{G}\text{-inj}
denote the class of
\mathcal{G}
-injective morphisms.
Then
(\varphi(\mathcal{G}\text{-xt}),\mathcal{G}\text{-inj})
is a complete ideal cotorsion pair in
{\rm Mod}R
.

(2)
[
1
, Proposition 6.1.1]

Let
\mathcal{T}

Since every exact category can be viewed as an extriangulated category, Theorem

Corollary 3.6
.

[
7

Let
\mathcal{B}

\mathcal{B}

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})

, then
([\mathcal{X}],[\mathcal{Y}])

We have just established that every complete cotorsion pair gives rise to a complete ideal cotorsion pair via the ideal generation construction
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\mapsto([\mathcal{X}],[\mathcal{Y}])

([\mathcal{X}],[\mathcal{Y}])
generated by subcategories
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, can we conclude that
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is a complete cotorsion pair?
To prove this, we need the following some preparations.

We recall some notions. Let
\mathcal{A}
be an additive category. A morphism
e\colon A\to A
is called idempotent if
e^{2}=e
. The category
\mathcal{A}
is said to be idempotent complete (or to have split idempotents) if every idempotent morphism in
\mathcal{A}
has a kernel.

An additive category
\mathcal{A}
is weakly idempotent complete if every retraction has a kernel.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an additive category. If
\mathcal{A}
is idempotent complete,
then
\mathcal{A}
is weakly idempotent complete.

Proof.

Let
r:A\to B
be a retraction. Then there exists
s:B\to A
such that

rs=\mathrm{id}_{B}
.
Set
p:=sr:A\to A
. Then we have

p^{2}=(sr)(sr)=s(rs)r=s\mathrm{id}_{B}r=sr=p.

Thus
p
is an idempotent morphism.
Since
\mathcal{A}
is idempotent complete,
p
has a kernel. Let

k:K\to A

be a kernel of
p
. Then
p\circ k=0
.

We claim that
k:K\to A
is also a kernel of
r
.

We first show that
rk=0
.
Since
pk=0
and
p=sr
, we have
srk=0
. Then

rk=\mathrm{id}_{B}(rk)=(rs)(rk)=r(srk)=r0=0.

Now let
f:X\to A
be any morphism such that
r\circ f=0
.
Then

pf=srf=s0=0.

Since
k:K\to A
is a kernel of
p
, the universal property of kernels gives a unique morphism
g:X\to K
such that

f=k\circ g
.
This shows that
k:K\to A
is a kernel of
r
. Hence
\mathcal{A}
is weakly idempotent complete.
∎

Recall that an additive category is called Krull-Schmidt if every object decomposes as a finite direct sum of indecomposable objects with local endomorphism rings, and this decomposition is unique up to isomorphism and permutation of summands.

By
[

, Corollary 4.4]
(see also
[
2

is semiperfect for every object

. Consequently, by Proposition

, every Krull-Schmidt additive category is weakly idempotent complete.

We now turn to the converse of Theorem
3.5
. In full generality, this converse remains open. The difficulty lies in the fact that, for an arbitrary extriangulated category, a special
[\mathcal{X}]
-precover need not restrict to a special
\mathcal{X}
-precover on the object level, since the domain of the precover may not decompose into an
\mathcal{X}
-part and a
[\mathcal{X}]^{\perp_{1}}
-part.
However, if
\mathcal{C}
is Krull-Schmidt, every object admits a unique decomposition into indecomposable summands, and every morphism admits a right minimal decomposition. This allows us to extract an
\mathcal{X}
-precover from a given
[\mathcal{X}]
-precover. We thus obtain the following positive result.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be a Krull–Schmidt extriangulated category, and let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
. If
([\mathcal{X}],[\mathcal{Y}])
is a complete ideal cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}
, then
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is a complete cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

(1) The condition
\mathbb{E}([\mathcal{X}],[\mathcal{Y}])=0
immediately implies
\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})=0
.

Before proving (2) and (3), we establish the equalities

\mathcal{X}={}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{Y}~~\text{and}~~\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{1}}.

The inclusion
\mathcal{X}\subseteq{}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{Y}
is immediate. For the reverse inclusion, let
M\in{}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{Y}
. Then
\mathbb{E}(M,\mathcal{Y})=0
, which gives
\mathbb{E}(1_{M},[\mathcal{Y}])=0
. Hence
1_{M}\in{}^{\perp_{1}}[\mathcal{Y}]=[\mathcal{X}]
, so
M\in\mathcal{X}
. Thus
\mathcal{X}={}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{Y}
. The dual argument yields
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{1}}
.

Since
\mathcal{X}={}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{1}}
, both
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are automatically closed under direct summands. Indeed, if
X=X_{1}\oplus X_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
, then for any
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
,

\mathbb{E}(X_{1},Y)\oplus\mathbb{E}(X_{2},Y)\cong\mathbb{E}(X,Y)=0,

so
X_{1}\in{}^{\perp_{1}}\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{X}
. The proof for
\mathcal{Y}
is dual.

(2) Let
M\in\mathcal{C}
. Since
[\mathcal{X}]
is special precovering, there exists an
\mathbb{E}
-triangle

A\longrightarrow B\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}M\dashrightarrow,

where
f:B\to M
is a special
[\mathcal{X}]
-precover. As
f\in[\mathcal{X}]
, it factors through some object
X\in\mathcal{X}
, say
f=f_{1}f_{2}
with
f_{1}:X\to M
and
f_{2}:B\to X
. Then
f_{1}
is an
\mathcal{X}
-precover of
M
.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is Krull-Schmidt, the morphism
f_{1}
admits a right minimal decomposition

f_{1}=(f_{1}^{\prime},0):X=X_{1}\oplus X_{2}\longrightarrow M,

where
f_{1}^{\prime}:X_{1}\to M
is right minimal (see Lemma
3.10
). Moreover,
f_{1}^{\prime}
is a deflation because
f_{1}=(f_{1}^{\prime},0)=f_{1}^{\prime}(1,0)
(see Lemma
3.9
). Hence there exists an
\mathbb{E}
-triangle

K\longrightarrow X_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{1}^{\prime}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M\dashrightarrow.

We claim that
f_{1}^{\prime}
is still an
\mathcal{X}
-precover. Indeed, for any morphism
g:X^{\prime}\to M
, since
f_{1}
is an
\mathcal{X}
-precover, there exists
h=\binom{h_{1}}{h_{2}}:X^{\prime}\to X_{1}\oplus X_{2}
such that
f_{1}h=g
, i.e.,

(f_{1}^{\prime},0)\binom{h_{1}}{h_{2}}=f_{1}^{\prime}h_{1}=g.

Thus
g
factors through
f_{1}^{\prime}
, so
f_{1}^{\prime}
is an
\mathcal{X}
-precover of
M
. Therefore
f_{1}^{\prime}
is a right minimal
\mathcal{X}
-precover. By
[
5
, Lemma 3.3]
, we have
K\in\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{1}}=\mathcal{Y}
.

Since
X=X_{1}\oplus X_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{X}
is closed under direct summands, we have
X_{1}\in\mathcal{X}
. Therefore, the
\mathbb{E}
-triangle

K\longrightarrow X_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{1}^{\prime}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M\dashrightarrow

is the desired one, with
X_{1}\in\mathcal{X}
and
K\in\mathcal{Y}
.

(3) This follows dually to (2).

Hence
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is a complete cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}
.
∎

Corollary

[

[

for Krull–Schmidt Frobenius exact categories. However, our approach is entirely different from that of

4
Ideal
n
-cotorsion pairs in Frobenius extriangulated categories

Motivated by the corresponding notions of cotorsion pairs in triangulated categories, exact categories and extriangulated categories, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an extriangulated category.
An ideal pair
(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})
is called an ideal
n
-cotorsion pair if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\mathcal{I}=\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot_{k}}\mathcal{J}}
;

(2)

\mathcal{J}=\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{I}^{\bot_{k}}
;

(3)

\mathcal{I}
is precovering and
\mathcal{J}
is preenveloping.

Remark 4.2
.

It follows directly from the definitions that an ideal
1
-cotorsion pair is precisely a complete ideal cotorsion pair in the sense of
[
22
, Definition 3.8]
for extriangulated categories with enough projectives and injectives. In particular, when
\mathcal{C}
is an exact category with enough projectives and injectives, an ideal
1
-cotorsion pair coincides with a complete ideal cotorsion pair in the sense of
[
7
, Page 771]
.

Now we give some examples of ideal
n
-cotorsion pairs.

Example 4.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an extriangulated category with enough projective objects and enough injective objects.
Let
[\mathcal{P}]
,
[\mathcal{I}]
, and
{\rm Mor}(\mathcal{C})
denote the ideal of morphisms factoring through projective objects, the ideal of morphisms factoring through injective objects, and the class of all morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
, respectively.
Then
([\mathcal{P}],{\rm Mor}(\mathcal{C}))
and
({\rm Mor}(\mathcal{C}),[\mathcal{I}])
are ideal
n
-cotorsion pairs.

Proof.

We only prove the former assertion, since the proof of the latter is similar.

(1) On the one hand, for any morphism
i\colon A\rightarrow B\in[\mathcal{P}],\ j\colon C\rightarrow D\in{\rm Mor}(\mathcal{C})
,
\mathbb{E}^{k}(i,j)=0
for any integer
1\leq k\leq n
implies
[\mathcal{P}]\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot_{k}}{\rm Mor}(\mathcal{C})}
. On the other hand, let
i\colon A\rightarrow B
be a morphism in
\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot_{k}}{\rm Mor}(\mathcal{C})}
.
Since
\mathcal{C}
has enough projective objects, then there exists an
\mathbb{E}
-triangle

K\longrightarrow P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}B\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\dashrightarrow}},

where
P
is a projective object. Applying the functor
\mbox{Hom}(A,-)
to the above
\mathbb{E}
-triangle, we obtain the following exact sequence

\mbox{Hom}(A,P)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mbox{Hom}(A,B)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta_{\sharp}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{E}(A,K).

It follows that
\mathbb{E}(i,1_{K})=0
implies
i\in\mathsf{Ker}\hskip 0.72229pt(\delta_{\sharp})=\mathrm{Im}\hskip 0.72229pt(p_{*})
. Thus we have
i\in[\mathcal{P}]
and then
\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot_{k}}{\rm Mor}(\mathcal{C})}\subseteq[\mathcal{P}].

(2)
{\rm Mor}(\mathcal{C})=\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{[\mathcal{P}]^{\bot_{k}}}
is obvious.

(3) Since
\mathcal{C}
has enough projectives, we have that
[\mathcal{P}]
is precovering.
It is obvious that
{\rm Mor}(\mathcal{C})
is preenveloping.

This shows that
([\mathcal{P}],{\rm Mor}(\mathcal{C}))
is an ideal
n
-cotorsion pair.
∎

In what follows, unless otherwise stated, we assume that
(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
is a Frobenius extriangulated category. Let
\omega
denote the full subcategory of projective-injective objects, and let
\underline{\mathcal{C}}=\mathcal{C}/[\omega]
be the stable category associated with
\mathcal{C}
, where
[\omega]
is the ideal of morphisms factoring through objects in
\omega
. Moreover,
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
, and
\mathcal{I}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{C}
satisfying
\omega\subseteq{\rm Ob}(\mathcal{I})
.

To prove the main result of this section, we first establish several preliminary facts concerning ideals under the canonical projection from a Frobenius extriangulated category to its stable category. These facts allow us to compare ideal orthogonality in
\mathcal{C}
with that in
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
, and to relate the precovering and preenveloping properties of ideals in the two categories. Our main result shows that ideal

-cotorsion pairs are both preserved and reflected when passing to the stable category.

Lemma 4.4
.

(1)

\mathcal{I}
is precovering in
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\mathcal{I}/\omega
is precovering in
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
.

(2)

\mathcal{I}
is preenveloping in
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\mathcal{I}/\omega
is preenveloping in
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

We just prove that (1) since (2) is the dual of (1).

Necessity.
For any object M in
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
, there exists an
\mathcal{I}
-precover
f\colon X\rightarrow M
in
\mathcal{C}
. We shall show that
\underline{f}\colon X\rightarrow M
is an
\mathcal{I}/\omega
-precover of M in
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
. In fact, for any morphism
\underline{g}\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow M
in
\mathcal{I}/\omega
, there exists
g^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow M\in\mathcal{I}
such that
\underline{g}=\underline{g^{\prime}}
. It follows that
g-g^{\prime}
factors through some object
W
in
\omega
. Assume that
g-g^{\prime}=w_{1}w_{2}
with
w_{1}\colon W\rightarrow M,\ w_{2}\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow W
in
\mathcal{I}
. Then we obtain
g=g^{\prime}+w_{1}w_{2}\in\mathcal{I}
. Therefore there exists
h\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
such that

. Obviously,
\underline{g}=\underline{fh}
.

Sufficiency.
For any object
M
in
\mathcal{C}
, there exists an
\mathcal{I}/\omega
-precover
\underline{f}\colon X\rightarrow M
in
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
and a deflation

, where
f,\ p\in\mathcal{I}
. We claim that
f^{\prime}=(f\ p)\colon X\oplus P\rightarrow M
is an
\mathcal{I}
-precover of
M
. In fact,
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}
since
f^{\prime}=f(1\ 0)+p(0\ 1)
. For any morphism
g\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow M\in\mathcal{I}
, there is a morphism
\underline{h}\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
such that
\underline{g}=\underline{fh}
. Since
X=X\oplus P,\ \underline{f}=\underline{f^{\prime}}
in
\underline{\mathcal{C}}
, there is a morphism
\underline{h^{\prime}}\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X\oplus P
such that
\underline{g}=\underline{f^{\prime}h^{\prime}}
. Then
g-f^{\prime}h^{\prime}
factors through some object

, say
g-f^{\prime}h^{\prime}=w_{1}w_{2}
with
w_{1}\colon W\rightarrow M,\ w_{2}\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow W
. By Proposition

,

is a deflation, thus there is a morphism

such that

g=f^{\prime}h^{\prime}+w_{1}w_{2}=f^{\prime}h^{\prime}+f^{\prime}lw_{2}=f^{\prime}(h^{\prime}+lw_{2})

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}
be ideals of
\mathcal{C}
,
\omega

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}
be ideals of
\mathcal{C}
,
\omega
the subcategory consisting of projective-injective objects. If
\mathbb{E}^{k}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})=0
if and only if
\underline{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{I}/\omega,\Sigma^{k}\mathcal{J}/\omega)=0
for any positive integer

, then the following statements are true.

(1)

\mathcal{I}=\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot_{k}}\mathcal{J}}
if and only if
\mathcal{I}/\omega=\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot}}(\Sigma^{k}\mathcal{J}/\omega)
,

(2)

\mathcal{J}=\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{\mathcal{I}^{\bot_{k}}}
if and only if
\mathcal{J}/\omega=\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{(\Omega^{k}\mathcal{I}/\omega)^{\bot}}
.

Proof.

We just need prove that (1) since (2) is dual.

Necessity.

\underline{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{I}/\omega,\Sigma^{k}\mathcal{J}/\omega)=0
directly implies
\mathcal{I}/\omega\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot}}(\Sigma^{k}\mathcal{J}/\omega)
. For any morphism
\underline{i}\in\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot}}(\Sigma^{k}\mathcal{J}/\omega),\ \underline{\mathcal{C}}(\underline{i},\Sigma^{k}\mathcal{J}/\omega)=0
shows that
\mathbb{E}^{k}(i,\mathcal{J})=0
. So we have
i\in\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot_{k}}\mathcal{J}}=\mathcal{I}
and then
\underline{i}\in\mathcal{I}/\omega
. That is
\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot}}(\Sigma^{k}\mathcal{J}/\omega)\subseteq\mathcal{I}/\omega
.

Sufficiency.

\underline{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{I}/\omega,\Sigma^{k}\mathcal{J}/\omega)=0
implies that
\mathbb{E}^{k}(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J})=0
which directly shows that
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot_{k}}\mathcal{J}}
. For any morphism
i\in\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot_{k}}\mathcal{J}}
,
\mathbb{E}^{k}(i,\mathcal{J})=0
implies that
\underline{\mathcal{C}}(\underline{i},\Sigma^{k}\mathcal{J}/\omega)=0
. Therefore
\underline{i}\in\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot}}(\Sigma^{k}\mathcal{J}/\omega)=\mathcal{I}/\omega
. Then
i\in\mathcal{I}
and
\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}{{}^{\bot_{k}}\mathcal{J}}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
.
∎
