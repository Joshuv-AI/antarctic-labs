Composition operators between de Branges-Rovnyak and Hardy spaces
Evgueni Doubtsov, Andrei V. Vasin

Abstract
. Let d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 and φ : B d → 𝔻 \varphi:B_{d}\to\mathbb{D} be a holomorphic function, where B d B_{d} denotes the open unit ball of ℂ d \mathbb{C}^{d} and 𝔻 = B 1 \mathbb{D}=B_{1} . Let b : 𝔻 → 𝔻 b:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D} be a holomorphic function and ℋ ⁡ ( b ) \mathcal{H}(b) denote the corresponding de Branges–Rovnyak space. We show that compactness of the composition operator C φ C_{\varphi} from ℋ ⁡ ( b ) \mathcal{H}(b) to the Hardy space H 2 ​ ( B d ) H^{2}(B_{d}) is related to natural restrictions on the Nevanlinna counting functions of the slice-functions φ ζ \varphi_{\zeta} , ζ ∈ ∂ B d \zeta\in\partial B_{d} .



1.
Introduction

For
d\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{H}ol({B_{d}})
denote the space of holomorphic functions in the unit ball

{B_{d}}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:|z|<1\}
.
Let
\mathbb{D}=B_{1}
and
\mathbb{T}=\partial\mathbb{D}
.
Let
\sigma_{d}
denote the normalized Lebesgue measure on the unit sphere
\partial B_{d}
.

For
0<p<\infty
, the classical Hardy space
H^{p}=H^{p}({B_{d}})
consists of

f\in\mathcal{H}ol({B_{d}})
such that

\|f\|_{H^{p}}^{p}=\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\partial B_{d}}|f(r\zeta)|^{p}\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)<\infty.

As usual, the Hardy space
H^{p}({B_{d}})
,
p>0
, is identified with the space

H^{p}(\partial B_{d})
of the corresponding boundary values.

1.1.
Composition operators

Consider a holomorphic mapping
\varphi:{B_{d}}\to\mathbb{D}
,
d\geq 1
.
It is well known that the composition operator
C_{\varphi}:f\mapsto f\circ\varphi
maps
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})

into
H^{p}({B_{d}})
,
p>0
.
Therefore, it is natural to ask about characterizations
of those symbols
\varphi
for which

C_{\varphi}:H^{p}(\mathbb{D})\to H^{p}({B_{d}})
is a compact operator.
For
d=1
, an explicit approach based on the Nevanlinna counting function
was developed in
[
9
]
.
A different answer, in terms of the Clark measures, was obtained in
[
2
]
.

1.2.
The space
\mathcal{H}(b)

Each function
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})

generates the Toeplitz operator
T_{\psi}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})

defined by

T_{\psi}(f)=P_{+}(\psi f),\quad f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}),

where
P_{+}
is the Riesz orthogonal projector from
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
onto
H^{2}(\mathbb{T})
.
To each holomorphic function
b:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
, we associate the de Branges–Rovnyak space

\mathcal{H}(b)=(I-T_{b}T_{\overline{b}})^{1/2}H^{2},

equipped with the inner product

(1.1)

\langle(I-T_{b}T_{\overline{b}})^{1/2}f,(I-T_{b}T_{\overline{b}})^{1/2}g\rangle_{b}=\langle f,g\rangle_{H^{2}},

making
\mathcal{H}(b)
a Hilbert space. In formula (
1.1
),
f
and
g
are taken in
H^{2}
such
that
f,g\bot\mathrm{ker}(I-T_{b}T_{\overline{b}})^{1/2}
.

For further results about the spaces
\mathcal{H}(b)
,
see
[
5
]
and
[
8
]
.

1.3.
Composition operators on de Branges–Rovnyak spaces
\mathcal{H}(b)

In the present work,
we use the Nevanlinna counting function
to obtain necessary conditions and sufficient conditions
for the compactness of the operator
C_{\varphi}:\mathcal{H}(b)\to H^{2}({B_{d}})
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function.
Recall that the Nevanlinna counting function
N_{\phi}
is defined by

(1.2)

N_{\phi}(w)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{D}:\,\phi(z)=w}\log\frac{1}{|z|},\quad w\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{\phi(0)\},

where each pre-image is counted according to its multiplicity.

For
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
and a holomorphic mapping
\varphi:{B_{d}}\to\mathbb{C}
,
d\geq 1
, the slice-function
\varphi_{\zeta}

is defined as
\varphi_{\zeta}(z)=\varphi(z\zeta)
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

The following theorem is the main result of the present paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\varphi:{B_{d}}\to\mathbb{D}
,
d\geq 1
, be a holomorphic function,
\varphi(0)=0
.
Let
b:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that
\mathcal{H}(b)
is infinite dimensional.
Consider the following properties:

(i)

for some
\gamma\in(0,1/3)
,

(1.3)

\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(z)\left(\frac{(1-|b(z)|)^{\gamma}}{1-|z|^{2}}\right)^{2}\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)\to 0\quad\textrm{as}\ |z|\to 1-,

(ii)

the operator
C_{\varphi}:\mathcal{H}(b)\to H^{2}({B_{d}})
is compact,

(iii)

(1.4)

\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(z)\frac{1-|b(z)|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)\to 0\quad\textrm{as}\ |z|\to 1-.

Then
\mathrm{(i)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(ii)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(iii)}
.

For
d=1
, Theorem
1.1
was obtained in
[
4
]
.
If
b
is an inner function, then
\mathcal{H}(b)
coincides with the model space
K_{b}=H^{2}\ominus bH^{2}
.
Characterizations of the compact operators
C_{\varphi}:K_{b}\to H^{2}({B_{d}})
were earlier obtained in
[
7
]
for
d=1
.
See also
[
3
]
for the case
d\geq 1
.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we adapt methods from
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
9
]

to the case of several variables.

Organization of the paper

Auxiliary results are collected in Section
2
.
A necessary condition for the compactness of

C_{\varphi}:\mathcal{H}(b)\to H^{2}({B_{d}})
is given in Section
3
.
The corresponding sufficient conditions are obtained in Section
4
.

2.
Auxiliary results

2.1.
Littlewood–Paley identity and its generalizations

For
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, the Littlewood–Paley identity states that

(2.1)

\|f\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}=|f(0)|^{2}+2\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(w)|^{2}\log\frac{1}{|w|}\,dA(w),

where
A
denotes the area measure on the disk
\mathbb{D}
.

2.1.1.
Stanton’s formula

To study the composition operator generated by a holomorphic symbol
\phi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
,
J. H. Shapiro
[
9
]
used for
f\circ\phi
an analog of the identity (
2.1
).
This analog is based on the Nevanlinna counting function
N_{\phi}
defined by (
1.2
).
The following Stanton formula is the principal technical tool in Shapiro’s argument.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function.
Then

(2.2)

\|f\circ\phi\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}=|f(\phi(0))|^{2}+2\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(w)|^{2}N_{\phi}(w)\,dA(w).

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\varphi:{B_{d}}\to\mathbb{D}
,
d\geq 1
, be a holomorphic function.
Then

(2.3)

\|f\circ\varphi\|^{2}_{H^{2}({B_{d}})}=|f(\varphi(0))|^{2}+2\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(w)|^{2}\left(\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(w)\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)\right)\,dA(w).

Proof.

Let
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
.
Applying Theorem
2.1
with
\phi=\varphi_{\zeta}
and integrating with respect to the normalized Lebesgue measure

\sigma_{d}
on
\partial B_{d}
, we obtain the required equality (
2.3
).
∎

2.2.
Subharmonic property for the Nevanlinna counting function

Proposition 2.3

(
[
9
, Section 4.6]
)
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{D}
and
\phi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function.
Let
\Delta\subset\mathbb{D}
be a disk centered at
w
and such that
\phi(0)\notin\Delta
.
Then

(2.4)

N_{\phi}(w)\leq\frac{1}{A(\Delta)}\int_{\Delta}N_{\phi}(z)\,dA(z).

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{D}
and
\varphi:{B_{d}}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function.
Let
\Delta\subset\mathbb{D}
be a disk centered at
w
and such that
\varphi(0)\notin\Delta
.
Then

(2.5)

\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(w)\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)\leq\frac{1}{A(\Delta)}\int_{\Delta}\left(\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(z)\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)\right)\,dA(z).

Proof.

Let
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
.
Using Proposition
2.3
with
\phi=\varphi_{\zeta}
,
integrating with respect to
\sigma_{d}
and applying Fubini’s theorem, we obtain the required
inequality (
2.5
).
∎

2.3.
Spaces boundedly contained in
H^{2}

Recall
that a Hilbert space
E
is said to be boundedly contained in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
if

E\subset H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
\|f\|_{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{E}

for all
f\in E
.

Lemma 2.5

(see, for example,
[
4
, Lemma 10]
)
.

Let
E
be boundedly contained in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Then the subspace

E_{n}=\{f\in E:f\ \textrm{has zero of order $n$ at the origin}\}

is closed in
E
. Moreover, the subspace
E_{n}^{\bot}
is finite dimensional.

2.4.
Reproducing kernels for
\mathcal{H}(b)

It is well known that
\mathcal{H}(b)
is contractively contained in
H^{2}
and thus it is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space. The reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{H}(b)
at
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
is given by the following formula

k^{b}_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{1-b(z)\overline{b(\lambda)}}{1-z\overline{\lambda}},\quad z,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}.

It is known that

\|k^{b}_{\lambda}\|_{b}=\left(\frac{1-|b(\lambda)|^{2}}{1-|\lambda|^{2}}\right)^{1/2},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{D}.

Hence, the normalized reproducing kernel is

\widetilde{k}^{b}_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{1-b(z)\overline{b(\lambda)}}{1-z\overline{\lambda}}\left(\frac{1-|\lambda|^{2}}{1-|b(\lambda)|^{2}}\right)^{1/2},\quad z,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}.

Let
D_{\varepsilon}(w)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z-w|<\varepsilon|1-z\overline{w}|\}
, that is,
let
D_{\varepsilon}(w)
denote the pseudo-hyperbolic
\varepsilon
-disk centered at
w\in\mathbb{D}
.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
7
, Lemma 1]
and
[
4
, Lemma 8]
)
.

Let
b:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function.
Let
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|w_{n}|\to 1
as
n\to\infty
and

(2.6)

|b(w_{n})|<a

for some parameter
a\in(0,1)
. Then

(i)

\widetilde{k}_{w_{n}}^{b}\overset{w^{\ast}}{\longrightarrow}0
as
n\to\infty
;

(ii)

there exist
\varepsilon>0
,
C>0
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

|(k_{w_{n}}^{b})^{\prime}(z)|\geq\frac{C}{(1-|w_{n}|^{2})^{2}},\quad z\in D_{\varepsilon}(w_{n}),

for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

2.5.
Bernstein inequalities

Let
1<p<2
and let
q
be the conjugate exponent of
p
. Also, let

\rho(\zeta)=1-|b(\zeta)|^{2}
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
, and

\mathfrak{K}^{\rho}_{\lambda}(z)=\overline{b(\lambda)}\frac{2b(z)\overline{b(\lambda)}-b^{\prime}(z)\overline{b^{\prime}(\lambda)}}{1-z\overline{\lambda}},\quad z,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}.

Finally, put

\omega_{p}(z)=\min\left\{\|(k^{b}_{z})^{2}\|^{-p/(p+1)}_{q},\|\rho^{1/q}\mathfrak{K}^{\rho}_{z}\|^{-p/(p+1)}_{q}\right\},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Recall that a finite positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
is called a Carleson measure
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\mu(S(\zeta,h))\leq Ch

for all
h>0
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
, where
S(\zeta,h)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z-\zeta|<h\}
.

Theorem 2.7

(
[
1
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a Carleson measure on
\mathbb{D}
, let
1<p<2
and let
b:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(\mu,p)
such that

\|f^{\prime}\omega_{p}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq C\|f\|_{b},\quad f\in\mathcal{H}(b).

Also, by Lemma 3.5 from
[
1
]
, there exists a constant
A>0
such that

(2.7)

\omega_{p}(z)\geq A\frac{1-|z|}{\left(1-|b(z)|\right)^{\frac{p}{q(p+1)}}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

3.
A necessary condition

In this section, we prove that (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) in Theorem
1.1
.

Assume that
C_{\varphi}:\mathcal{H}(b)\to H^{2}({B_{d}})
is compact but (iii) does not hold.
Then there exists a sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{D}
such that
|w_{n}|\to 1
and

(3.1)

\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(w_{n})\frac{1-|b(w_{n})|^{2}}{1-|w_{n}|^{2}}\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)\geq c>0.

By the Littlewood subordination principle
[
6
]
,
N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(w_{n})\leq\log\frac{1}{|w_{n}|}
,
hence, (
3.1
) implies (
2.6
) for some
a\in(0,1)
.
Therefore, Lemma
2.6
is applicable.

Sequentially applying (
2.3
), Lemma
2.6
(ii) and Corollary
2.4
, we obtain

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|C_{\varphi}\widetilde{k}_{w_{n}}^{b}\|^{2}_{H^{2}({B_{d}})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\|k_{w_{n}}\|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|k^{\prime}_{w_{n}}(z)|^{2}\left(\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(z)\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)\right)\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{D(w_{n},\varepsilon)}\frac{C}{(1-|w_{n}|^{2})^{3}}\left(\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(z)\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)\right)\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{1-|w_{n}|^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(w_{n})\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta).

Now, recall that
C_{\varphi}
is a compact operator,
thus,
\|C_{\varphi}\widetilde{k}_{w_{n}}^{b}(z)\|\to 0
by Lemma
2.6
(i).
Therefore, (
3.2
) contradicts (
3.1
).
The proof of the implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) is finished.

4.
Sufficient conditions

In this section, we prove that (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) in Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\varphi:{B_{d}}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function,
\varphi(0)=0
.
Let
b:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that
\mathcal{H}(b)
is infinite dimensional.
Assume that there exists
p\in(1,2)
such that

(4.1)

\omega_{p}^{-2}(z)\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(z)\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)\to 0\quad\textrm{as}\ |z|\to 1-.

Then the operator
C_{\varphi}:\mathcal{H}(b)\to H^{2}({B_{d}})
is compact.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{n}(b)=\{f\in\mathcal{H}(b):f\textrm{\ has zero of order $n$ at the origin}\}
.
Recall that
\mathcal{H}(b)
is contractively contained in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, thus,
by Lemma
2.5
, we can define the corresponding orthogonal projector

P_{n}:\mathcal{H}(b)\to\mathcal{H}_{n}(b).

By Lemma
2.5
, the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{H}_{n}(b)
is finite dimensional.
We claim that

(4.2)

\|C_{\varphi}P_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}(b)\to H^{2}({B_{d}})}\to 0\quad\textrm{as\ }n\to\infty.

Then the operator
C_{\varphi}
is compact, since it is approximable
by finite rank operators
C_{\varphi}(I-P_{n})
.

To verify property (
4.2
), fix
\varepsilon>0
and
f\in\mathcal{H}(b)
,
\|f\|_{b}=1
.
Put
g_{n}=P_{n}f
, then we have
\|g_{n}\|_{b}\leq 1
.
Also, given
R<1
, there exists
n(R,\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
independent of
f
and such that

(4.3)

|g_{n}(w)|<\varepsilon,\ |g^{\prime}(w)|<\varepsilon\quad\textrm{for}\ |w|<R\ \textrm{and for all}\ n>n(R,\varepsilon).

Indeed, we have
g_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{n}(b)
.
Thus, the Taylor series of
g_{n}
at the origin has the following form:

g_{n}(w)=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{g_{n}(0)}{k!}w^{k}.

Observe that

\left|\frac{g_{n}(0)}{k!}\right|=|\langle g_{n},w^{k}\rangle_{2}|\leq\|g_{n}\|_{2}\|w^{k}\|_{2}=\|g_{n}\|_{2}\leq\|g_{n}\|_{b}\leq\|f\|_{b}=1.

Thus, for
|w|\leq R
, we obtain

|g_{n}(w)|\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}|w|^{k}=\frac{|w|^{n}}{1-|w|}\leq\frac{R^{n}}{1-R}.

Therefore, there exists
n_{1}(R,\varepsilon)
such that
|g_{n}|<\varepsilon

for all
n>n_{1}(R,\varepsilon)
.

Also, we have

g_{n}^{\prime}(w)=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{g_{n}(0)}{k!}kw^{k-1}.

Thus,

|g_{n}^{\prime}(w)|\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}k|w|^{k-1}\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}kR^{k-1}.

Therefore, there exists
n_{2}(R,\varepsilon)
such that
|g_{n}^{\prime}|<\varepsilon

for all
n>n_{2}(R,\varepsilon)
.
Hence, (
4.3
) holds.

Now, using Stanton’s formula (
2.3
), we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\|C_{\varphi}P_{n}f\|_{2}^{2}&=\int_{|z|<R}|g_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(z)\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)dA(z)\\
&\quad+\int_{R\leq|z|<1}|g_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(z)\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)dA(z)\\
&\leq\sup_{|z|<R}|g_{n}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\cdot\int_{|z|<R}\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(z)\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)dA(z)\\
&\quad+\sup_{R\leq|z|<1}\left(\omega_{p}^{-2}(z)\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(z)\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)\right)\cdot\int_{R\leq|z|<1}|g_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}\omega_{p}^{2}(z)\\
&=I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

Below we estimate
I_{2}
and
I_{1}
.

Applying (
4.1
), select
R<1
such that

\sup_{R\leq|z|<1}\omega_{p}^{-2}(z)\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(z)\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)<\varepsilon.

Next, the area measure on
\mathbb{D}
is a Carleson measure and
\|g_{n}\|_{b}\leq 1
,
hence, by Theorem
2.7
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(4.4)

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g_{n}^{\prime}|^{2}\omega_{p}^{2}(z)\,dA(z)\leq C

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus,

I_{2}\leq C\varepsilon

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now, we consider
I_{1}
. We have

\sup_{|z|<R}|g_{n}^{\prime}(z)|^{2}<\varepsilon

for all
n>n(R,\varepsilon)
.
Also, by Littlewood’s inequality,

|N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(z)|\leq\log\frac{1}{|z|},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\},

since
\varphi_{\zeta}(0)=0
for all
\zeta\in\partial B_{d}
.
Hence,

\int_{\partial B_{d}}\int_{|z|<R}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(z)\,dA(z)\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)\leq C.

Therefore,

I_{1}\leq C\varepsilon

for all sufficiently large
n
.

In sum, we obtain
I_{1}+I_{2}\leq C\varepsilon
for all sufficiently large
n
.
Thus, for all
f\in\mathcal{H}(b)
with
\|f\|_{b}=1
, we have
\|C_{\varphi}P_{n}f\|_{2}^{2}\leq C\varepsilon

with an absolute constant
C>0
and for all sufficiently large
n
.
Hence, (
4.2
) holds. The proof of the proposition is finished.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\varphi:{B_{d}}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function,
\varphi(0)=0
.
Let
b:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that
\mathcal{H}(b)
is infinite dimensional.
Assume that there exists
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{3})
such that

(4.5)

\left(\frac{(1-|b(z)|)^{\gamma}}{1-|z|^{2}}\right)^{2}\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(z)\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)\to 0\quad\textrm{as}\ |z|\to 1-.

Then the operator
C_{\varphi}:\mathcal{H}(b)\to H^{2}({B_{d}})
is compact.

Proof.

Put
p=\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}
. Observe that
p\in(1,2)
.
Let
q
denote the conjugate exponent of
p
.
Then

\gamma=\frac{p-1}{p+1}=\frac{p}{q(p+1)}.

By (
2.7
), we have

\omega_{p}(z)\geq A\frac{1-|z|}{(1-|b(z)|)^{\gamma}}.

Therefore, (
4.5
) implies that

\omega_{p}^{-2}(z)\int_{\partial B_{d}}N_{\varphi_{\zeta}}(z)\,d\sigma_{d}(\zeta)\to 0

as
|z|\to 1
. Applying Proposition
4.1
, we conclude that the operator

C_{\varphi}:\mathcal{H}(b)\to H^{2}({B_{d}})
is compact.
∎

Finally, observe that Corollary
4.2
coincides with the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii).
Thus, the proof of Theorem
1.1
is finished.
