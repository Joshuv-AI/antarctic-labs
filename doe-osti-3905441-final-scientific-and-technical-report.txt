Final Scientific and Technical Report
Quantum Phenomena in Two-Dimensional Ferroelectrics
DOE award DE-SC0023140
Principal investigator Evgeny Tsymbal, University of Nebraska-Lincoln
Project period: August 1, 2022–July 31, 2027

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This project investigated how polar layer stacking and ferroelectric switching generate and
control quantum phenomena in two-dimensional materials. Combining first-principles
electronic-structure calculations, symmetry analysis, atomistic spin dynamics, and models of
spin-dependent transport, we established several routes by which interlayer displacement or
polarization changes anomalous Hall response, spin splitting, topological magnetic textures, and
electron tunneling. In polar-stacked MnBi2Te4 we predicted a polarization-reversible anomalous
Hall response and switching between trivial and Chern-insulating states. Polar stacking of CrI3
generated anisotropic Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interactions and electrically reconfigurable
antiskyrmions. We demonstrated a nonvolatile valley spin valve in ferroelectric 1T″-MoS2 and
polarization-controlled nonrelativistic spin splitting in antiferromagnetic bilayers. Collaborations
extended the project to twisted CrSBr tunnel junctions and all-van der Waals multiferroic tunnel
junctions. The results were disseminated in the peer-reviewed papers listed below. These
advances show how an electrical degree of freedom can control magnetic and transport
properties of ultrathin materials.

Original objectives and overall outcome
The project set six related objectives: (1) engineer ferroelectricity by layer stacking and examine
emergent spin textures; (2) investigate polarization-controlled spin textures and a nonvolatile
valley spin valve; (3) study spin-dependent transport in junctions made from two-dimensional
ferroelectrics and magnets; (4) investigate polarization and magnetism in stacked insulating
magnets, including CrX3; (5) predict electrically controlled spin polarization and anomalous Hall
phenomena in antiferromagnetic two-dimensional systems; and (6) determine how polarization
controls Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction and skyrmions or antiskyrmions. The work below
addresses these objectives through related material platforms. A proposed investigation of
Cr2Ge2Te6 was redirected to CrI3 when reliable DMI calculations proved difficult for the more
complex structure.

SCIENTIFIC ACCOMPLISHMENTS

Polar stacked antiferromagnets and Hall transport
We used density-functional calculations and symmetry analysis to study MnBi2Te4 layers
assembled into polar bilayers and trilayers [1]. Relative in-plane translation of the layers

 1

produces two structures with opposite out-of-plane polarizations. In the antiferromagnetic
bilayer, polar stacking removes the combined inversion and time-reversal symmetry that
otherwise constrains the Berry curvature. The resulting anomalous Hall conductivity reverses
with polarization. In a trilayer, the relative polarization of the interfaces controls whether the
electronic state is a trivial insulator or a Chern insulator with a quantized anomalous Hall
response. This result provides a route to changing a topological electronic state by rearranging a
van der Waals interface.

Ferroelectric control of topological magnetic textures
We calculated exchange, anisotropy, and Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interactions in polar-stacked
CrI3. Polar stacking creates anisotropic DMI; Mn substitution reduces the magnetic anisotropy
enough to favor antiskyrmions. Atomistic spin-dynamics calculations predicted elliptical
antiskyrmions in a Cr0.88Mn0.12I3 bilayer and a rotation of their spin texture when polarization
reverses [2]. An allied study of CrI2 found a ferroelectric orthorhombic phase and showed that Pt
intercalation enhances polarization and DMI, producing Néel skyrmions whose handedness
follows polarization [6]. The latter study involved project-supported theoretical analysis of DMI
at Nebraska, alongside support from another award for the collaborating group.
Although polar Cr2Ge2Te6 stacking was energetically feasible, the DMI extracted from the
required large spin-spiral calculations could not be established with sufficient reliability. We
therefore focused on CrI3 for the quantitative prediction of magnetic textures. This change
preserved the scientific objective while using a material whose interaction parameters could be
tested more convincingly.

Valley spin transport and electrically reversible spin splitting

We demonstrated a nonvolatile valley spin valve based on ferroelectric 1T″-MoS2 [4]. Reversing
its polarization reverses the spin polarization of the electronic valleys. Quantum transport
calculations showed that a uniformly polarized channel conducts, whereas a domain wall
between oppositely polarized regions strongly suppresses transmission through spin mismatch.
The predicted ON/OFF ratio reaches approximately 10⁷ for electrons and 10⁶ for holes.

We also investigated how electric polarization controls nonrelativistic spin splitting in polar
antiferromagnetic bilayers [7]. Symmetry analysis and first-principles calculations showed that
polarization reversal does not switch the splitting in MnPSe3 but does reverse it in MnPS3 and
polar-stacked 1T-CrTe2, without requiring reversal of the antiferromagnetic order. These results
identify the conditions under which ferroelectric switching can electrically control spin-polarized
electronic bands.

Magnetic and multiferroic tunnel junctions
In collaboration with experimental groups, we modeled transport in CrSBr junctions formed
from twisted atomic layers. Momentum matching between states in the magnetic electrodes and
the vacuum barrier helped explain the twist-dependent tunneling magnetoresistance observed in

 2

the all-antiferromagnetic junction [3]. Follow-up work on asymmetric twisted CrSBr structures
demonstrated multiple nonvolatile magnetic resistance states, supported by Nebraska
calculations of tunneling magnetoresistance [9]. Calculations also contributed to understanding
twist-sensitive pseudotunnel magnetoresistance in Fe3GeTe2 homojunctions [5].
We further contributed to studies of van der Waals multiferroic tunnel junctions using
ferromagnetic electrodes and ferroelectric barriers. Modeling of interfacial electronic structure
and band alignment helped interpret resistance changes driven by electrode magnetization and
barrier polarization; the experiments demonstrated distinct nonvolatile states and tunability
through material selection [8]. Related collaborative work demonstrated room-temperature
magnetoelectric coupling in Fe3GaTe2/CuInP2S6 heterostructures [10]. The award is explicitly
acknowledged for the PI’s contribution to these collaborative papers.

TRAINING AND PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT
The project provided research training through regular individual meetings with the PI, group
discussion, collaborative calculations, manuscript preparation, and presentation of results.
• Tengfei Cao, a postdoctoral researcher, led the MnBi2Te4 calculations, including the
 anomalous Hall analysis, and prepared the initial manuscript. He received frequent research
 mentoring and presented the work at the 2023 APS March Meeting.
• Kai Huang developed expertise in density-functional calculations, magnetic interaction
 parameters, DMI, and atomistic spin dynamics through the CrI₃ project. He defended his PhD
 in December 2023 and subsequently continued in the group as a postdoctoral researcher. He
 presented at the 2023 APS March Meeting and the 2023 Magnetism and Magnetic Materials
 conference, received a poster prize at a 2023 Lincoln conference and a Holland Computing
 Center travel award, and received guided experience in peer review. His following research
 contributed to the valley spin valve and electrically reversible spin-splitting papers [4,7].
• Naafis A. Shahed, a graduate student, learned electron-tunneling theory and advanced
 computational methods through the twisted CrSBr work with postdoctoral researcher Kartik
 Samanta. He attended the 2023 summer school on Principles and Applications of Symmetry
 in Magnetism, took part in research discussions with collaborators in Germany, and
 presented research at the 2024 APS March Meeting and the 2024 EQUATE conference. He
 contributed to the modelling of twisted van der Waals heterostructures and tunnel junctions
 based on CrSBr [3,9].
• Himanshu Mavani, a PhD student, contributed to the symmetry analysis and first-principles
 study of electrically switchable nonrelativistic spin splitting in two-dimensional
 antiferromagnets published in [7]. His research developed expertise in spin-space-group
 symmetry, altermagnetism, polar stacking, and the connection between ferroelectric
 switching and electronic spin polarization. He attended the 2026 Principles and Applications
 of Symmetry in Magnetism Summer School, where he presented a poster on ferroelectrically
 switchable wurtzite altermagnets and discussed his work with students and faculty.
