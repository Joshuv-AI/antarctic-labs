Synergistic spatial-sequential modeling for enhanced Rosa roxburghii disease detection

Xude Zhang, Houbing Tang, Xiaoping Wu, Jianghai Wang

ABSTRACT
The rapid advancement of smart agricultural technologies has enabled deep learning–based approaches to markedly enhance the efficiency of crop disease detection and quality monitoring. To overcome the challenges of low detection efficiency and high false-positive rates in large-scale cultivation of the characteristic economic crop Rosa roxburghii, this study introduces a synergistic spatial–sequential modeling framework. This synergy is achieved via a gated fusion mechanism that bidirectionally enhances CNN-extracted local spatial features and Mamba-derived global sequential features through adaptive re-weighting, forming a mutual refinement loop instead of a simple cascade. Built upon YOLOv8, the model integrates the Mamba module from state space models (SSMs) to develop a hybrid feature extraction framework, jointly optimizing detection accuracy and inference efficiency. A selective feature enhancement strategy further strengthens the network’s ability to characterize subtle lesions on the surface of Rosa roxburghii by adaptively amplifying informative responses through channel-wise recalibration and spatial refinement. The backbone network also incorporates the selective scan for 2D data (SS2D) module to capture long-range dependencies beyond conventional convolution. Experimental validation conducted on our self-constructed dataset, which consists of field-captured images of Rosa roxburghii plants manually collected and annotated by the research team, demonstrates that the proposed model delivers notable performance gains, achieving mAP@50 improvements of 6%, 2.87%, 8.38%, 7.08%, 16.27%, and 6.55% over YOLOv5-N, YOLOv8-N, YOLOv10-N, YOLOv11-N, YOLOv12-N, and MambaYOLO-T, respectively. Concerning mean average precision (mAP)@ 50–95, the model attains corresponding gains of 6.85%, 0.27%, 6.99%, 5.19%, 13.66%, and 2.57%, all while maintaining comparable FLOPs (G) and parameter counts. These results highlight the model’s excellent performance, confirming its effectiveness for agricultural visual inspection and suggesting a promising approach for intelligent disease monitoring in mountainous specialty crop production.

Related work Traditional method-based image detection of Rosa roxburghii As a high-value specialty crop, Rosa roxburghii Tratt requires accurate detection of surface lesions to ensure optimal yield and quality. Traditional image processing methods primarily rely on extracting and classifying lesion features in Rosa roxburghii fruit images, employing detection methods based on color space analysis, texture feature extraction, and morphological operations. In particular, color space analysis reveals distinct differences in color distribution between lesion regions and healthy tissues on the Rosa roxburghii fruit surfaces [ 18 ]. To mitigate the effects of uneven natural environments, research employs the Top-Hat Transform for shadow removal with median filtering to suppress noise interference [ 19 ]. Images are converted to the HSV color space, and threshold segmentation is applied to the H channel to isolate lesion regions under consistent lighting. For overlapping fruits, the watershed algorithm combined with the color difference analysis enables accurate detection and localization, even under occlusion or crowding conditions, enhancing segmentation accuracy [ 20 ]. Additionally, texture feature extraction plays a crucial role in differentiating lesions from healthy tissue. The Gray-Level Co-occurrence Matrix quantifies image texture by calculating the gray-level relationships of pixel pairs across various orientations and distances, capturing features, such as roughness and contrast within lesion regions. To mitigate textural blurring caused by mildew on Rosa roxburghii fruit surfaces, studies extract GLCM feature parameters, including energy and entropy, combined with a support vector machine for classification, yielding high accuracy on test datasets [ 21 ]. Within the test set containing images, this method achieves favorable classification accuracy. Furthermore, Local binary patterns and their variants enhance the detection sensitivity to subtle lesions by comparing the gray-level relationships between central pixels and their neighbors [ 22 ]. Deep learning-based image detection of Rosa roxburghii Recent advancements in deep learning have significantly improved Rosa roxburghii disease detection, addressing the limitations of traditional image processing techniques in complex field environments. CNNs are widely applied in Rosa roxburghii disease identification due to their powerful local feature extraction capabilities. Enhanced models, such as an improved YOLOv3 integrated with residual modules, have shown promising results in real-time and accurate Rosa roxburghii fruit recognition in natural environments [ 23 ]. However, traditional CNNs are limited by fixed receptive fields, making them less effective in capturing the long-range and cross-regional spread of Rosa roxburghii diseases [ 24 ]. To overcome this issue, the Transformer architectures have been introduced, leveraging self-attention mechanisms to model global lesion correlations. Nevertheless, its high computational complexity and reliance on large-scale data training pose challenges for deployment on edge devices [ 25 ]. Recently, SSMs, especially the Mamba mechanism, have emerged as efficient alternatives for Rosa roxburghii disease detection, offering linear computational complexity and selective state updates. The VMUnet-MSADI model, which integrates visual state space modules, multi-scale attention mechanisms, and detail injection blocks [ 26 ], demonstrates superior accuracy in agricultural image segmentation tasks compared to traditional U-Net [ 27 ]. InsectMamba integrates SSMs, CNNs, and self-attention mechanisms to effectively fuse multimodal features through hybrid SSM blocks, achieving strong results in pest classification and demonstrating the broad applicability of SSMs in agricultural vision [ 28 ]. By leveraging CNNs for local feature extraction and Mamba for long-range dependencies, this approach enhances the accuracy and efficiency of Rosa roxburghii disease detection, providing reliable technical support for smart agriculture [ 29 , 30 ]. However, these prior methods do not fully exploit bidirectional feature enhancement. In contrast, our YOLO-HMC introduces three key innovations beyond the VSSBlock: a synergistic spatial-sequential fusion mechanism that bidirectionally enhances local and global features, a selective feature enhancement strategy that adaptively amplifies lesion responses, and a gated fusion mechanism for iterative feature refinement. These components collectively address the multi-scale and complex background challenges inherent in agricultural disease detection. Method Preliminaries This section introduces the foundational concepts of SSMs [ 31 ], which form the foundation of YOLO-HMC. SSMs, such as S4 and Mamba Dare, are based on discretizing continuous systems to model sequential data. These models construct an implicit latent state space to progressively map input sequences x ( t ) ∈ R to output sequences y ( t ) . The system first maps discrete input sequences into a continuous latent space h ′ ( t ) ∈ R N using a parameterized state transition matrix A, and models dynamics through the state and observation equations. The S4 model enables efficient training via fast convolution kernel computation, while Mamba enhances expressiveness by introducing input-dependent dynamic parameters. This discretized continuous-system approach effectively captures long-range dependencies and improves computational efficiency through structured parameterization. The mathematical definition of the system can be summarized as follows: h ′ ( t ) = A h ( t ) + B x ( t ) (1) y ( t ) = C h ( t ) (2) The Mamba model introduces a novel time scale parameter 𝛥 to discretize continuous state-space models for improved integration with deep learning frameworks. It uses a zero-order hold technique to transform the continuous parameters A and B into their discrete counterparts, A ― and B ― , following a precise discretization rule: A ― = exp ( Δ A ) (3) B ― = ( Δ A ) − 1 ( exp ( Δ A ) − I ) Δ B (4) where A ― and B ― denote the discrete-time equivalents of the continuous parameters A and B, respectively, capturing their dynamics across the predetermined sampling period 𝛥 . The identity matrix I ensures dimensional consistency throughout the conversion process. After discretization, the model operates via a linear recurrence given by: h ′ ( t ) = A ― h t − 1 + B ― x t (5) Alternatively, the model’s sequential computation can be expressed as a global convolution of the input sequence with a structured kernel determined by the SSM parameters. This convolutional representation is mathematically expressed as: y = x * K ― (6) where K ― ∈ R L indicates the SSM convolution kernel, and L signifies the length of the input sequence x . C2f Block Fig 2 illustrates the C2f Block, which consists of an input convolutional layer, feature splitting, multiple Bottleneck blocks, and a final convolutional layer. Its key innovation employs a cross-stage partial fusion strategy: the feature maps are split along the channel dimension into two pathways. One pathway processes deep semantic features through stacked bottleneck blocks, while the other retains shallow spatial details. These multi-scale features are fused by concatenation. This design shortens gradient propagation paths and boosts feature reuse rates, reducing parameters and computation without sacrificing accuracy. Synergistic spatial-sequential fusion mechanism The synergy is defined as bidirectional enhancement between spatial and sequential features rather than a simple cascade. The YOLOv8 backbone and C2f blocks extract spatial features F C N N ,while the VSSBlock with SS2D captures sequential features T ′ .The synergy occurs via gated fusion at the C2f-VSSBlock interface: W = σ ( Linear ( T ′ ) ) , F f u s e d = W ⊙ F C N N (8) where σ is sigmoid, ⊙ is element-wise multiplication, and W are importance weights.This fusion executes after each VSSBlock stage and creates a bidirectional loop where F f u s e d feeds to subsequent VSSBlock stages, iteratively refining both representations. This mutual enhancement constitutes the synergy in our title. VSSBlock Fig 3 highlights the VSSBlock as the core component of the YOLO-HMC model, playing a critical role in enhancing the model’s overall feature extraction capabilities while maintaining high processing efficiency and throughput. It introduces the scanning selection in the two dimensions (SS2D) module, which uses a novel four-directional scanning strategy to overcome the limitations in traditional feature extraction methods [ 32 ]. As shown in Fig 4 , the SS2D module scans the feature map concurrently from the top-left, top-right, bottom-left, and bottom-right corners. This parallelized scanning allows each feature element to gather information from all other positions via multiple paths, effectively capturing multi-scale features. During scanning, the SS2D module preserves the image’s original spatial structure and enhances its ability to capture image details through independent directional scans. Scanning paths from multiple orientations capture features across varying scales, ranging from fine local details to broad global structures, thereby forming a hierarchical feature representation. The SS2D module then cleverly merges one-dimensional vectors obtained from these directions into a compact two-dimensional feature output using a specialized fusion method. This process reduces feature redundancy, improves the compactness of feature representation, and boosts feature quality, leading to more precise target identification in later stages. 10.1371/journal.pone.0358868.g003 Fig 3 Overview of the VSS block. 10.1371/journal.pone.0358868.g004 Fig 4 Scanning process of the SS2D module. Selective feature enhancement strategy To strengthen the network’s ability to characterize subtle lesions, we introduce a selective feature enhancement strategy that adaptively amplifies informative responses while suppressing irrelevant background activations. This strategy operates on the fused feature maps F f u s e d produced by the synergistic fusion mechanism. The enhancement process consists of three steps: First, a global attention vector is computed by applying global average pooling to F f u s e d , followed by two fully connected layers with a reduction ratio r to capture channel-wise dependencies: g = σ ( W 2 δ ( W 1 GAP ( F f u s e d ) ) ) (11) where GAP denotes global average pooling, W 1 and W 2 are learnable weights, δ is ReLU, and σ is sigmoid. Second, the attention vector g is used to recalibrate the feature map through channel-wise multiplication: F e n h a n c e d = g ⊙ F f u s e d (12) Third, a spatial refinement module further emphasizes lesion-relevant regions by applying a lightweight convolutional layer with kernel size 3x3 to generate spatial attention weights: A s p a t i a l = Conv 3 × 3 ( F e n h a n c e d ) , F f i n a l = A s p a t i a l ⊙ F e n h a n c e d (13) This selective enhancement strategy enables the network to focus on subtle lesion features such as mildew spots and scabs while suppressing irrelevant background noise, thereby improving detection accuracy for fine-grained disease patterns. Discussion The seven disease categories in our dataset reflect distinct biological processes: leaf lesions indicate fungal infections, fruit pest infestations reflect direct yield damage, and fruit disorders signal physiological stress. The superior detection performance across these categories suggests strong potential for differentiating biotic and abiotic stresses in real-world cultivation. For practical deployment, our framework can be integrated with mobile platforms to provide real-time diagnostic feedback, enabling targeted interventions and reducing pesticide overuse in mountainous specialty crop production. Conclusions This study introduces YOLO-HMC, an enhanced version of YOLOv8 that combines spatial and sequential modeling to substantially improve accuracy and efficiency in Rosa roxburghii image disease detection. The core innovation is the synergistic fusion mechanism where CNN-extracted local spatial features and Mamba-derived global sequential features are bidirectionally enhanced via gated re-weighting, enabling mutual refinement instead of simple aggregation. YOLO-HMC overcomes the shortcomings of conventional CNNs in modeling long-range dependencies and mitigates the computational demands typical of pure Vision Transformers by incorporating advanced features from the Mamba model. The introduction of VSSBlock integrates state space representations with linear-time update mechanisms, enhancing the model’s ability to capture complex disease features in Rosa roxburghii images while maintaining computational efficiency on large-scale datasets. Experimental results confirm that YOLO-HMC delivers superior detection performance across seven Rosa roxburghii disease categories, including leaf lesions, fruit pest infestations, and fruit disorders—outperforming existing approaches. Ablation studies validate the essential contribution of the VSSBlock in improving model accuracy, expediting convergence, and minimizing computational demands. This work introduces novel technical advancements for intelligent pest and disease monitoring in agriculture and expands the scope of deep learning applications within the sector. For future studies, we will further investigate the capabilities of Mamba models in agricultural image analysis and refine the YOLO-HMC framework to enhance efficiency and precision across diverse crop disease detection tasks. We will also prioritize external validation on independent datasets and cross-domain evaluation to rigorously assess the generalizability and practical applicability of our approach across different agricultural environments and crop types. These efforts will support the intelligent and sustainable advancement of agriculture and related industries.
