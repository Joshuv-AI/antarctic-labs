Probing Strange-Quark Hadronization via (Multi-)Strange Hadron Multiplicity Distributions in Small Collision Systems with ALICE
Sara Pucillo 1 for the ALICE Collaboration

Abstract
Strangeness enhancement is defined as the increased relative production of strange hadrons in heavy-ion collisions compared to proton–proton (pp) interactions. It was originally proposed as one of the signatures of quark–gluon plasma (QGP) formation. At the LHC, the ALICE experiment observed that strange-hadron-to-pion yield ratios rise with increasing charged-particle multiplicity at midrapidity, independently of collision energy ( s \sqrt{s} ) and system size, from pp to p–Pb and up to Pb–Pb collisions. To gain deeper insight into the mechanisms of strangeness production, the ALICE collaboration has measured the probability distribution of producing a given number of strange particles ( K S 0 {\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}} , Λ \Lambda ( Λ ¯ \overline{\Lambda} ), Ξ − \Xi^{-} ( Ξ ¯ + \overline{\Xi}^{+} ), and Ω − \Omega^{-} ( Ω ¯ + \overline{\Omega}^{+} )) of the same species per event in pp collisions at s = 5.02 \sqrt{s}~=~5.02 TeV [ 1 ] . This measurement extends the study of strangeness production beyond the mean particle yield by employing, for the first time, a technique based on event-by-event particle counting. It provides a new test bench for production mechanisms, probing events with large imbalances between strange and non-strange content. The results are compared with state-of-the-art phenomenological models implemented in commonly used Monte Carlo event generators, offering enhanced sensitivity to the underlying dynamics of strangeness production.

2
Measurement of (multi-)strange particle multiplicity distribution

To better understand how strangeness is produced, the ALICE collaboration has measured the probability distribution for producing a given number of strange particles,
{\rm P}(n_{\rm S})
, of the same species in a single event in pp collisions at
\sqrt{s}~=~5.02
TeV, using a new technique based on event-by-event strange-particle counting
[
1
]
.

After topological selections on the weak decay and particle identification of the decay daughters, signal and combinatorial background contributions are separated through fits to the invariant-mass distributions in intervals of
p_{\rm T}
and event multiplicity. The signal shape is described by a double-sided Crystal Ball (dsCB) function
[
33
]
, while the residual background is parameterized with a first-degree polynomial (Gaussian for the
\Omega
). This allows one to assign to each candidate a signal probability weight,

w_{\it S}~(p_{\rm T}{\rm,{\it m}_{inv};V0M})=\frac{{\rm dsCB}~(p_{\rm T}{\rm,{\it m}_{inv};V0M})}{{\rm Total}~(p_{\rm T}{\rm,{\it m}_{inv};V0M})}~\{~\cdot~w_{FD}(p_{\rm T}{\rm;V0M})~\}~~\quad,~~w_{\it B}~=~1-w_{\rm S},

(1)

with an additional feed-down correction factor
w_{FD}
applied in the
\Lambda
(
\overline{\Lambda}
) case to account for contributions from
\Xi^{-}
(
\overline{\Xi}^{+}
) and
\Xi^{0}
decays.

Each of the
N
candidates in a given event is considered together with its corresponding signal weight
w_{\it S}
. The probabilities of all possible signal–background configurations are then evaluated and summed, from the case in which all candidates are background to the one in which all are signal. In this way, an event-by-event probability distribution for the true strange-particle multiplicity, ranging from 0 to
N
, is reconstructed. The final
{\rm P}(n_{\rm S})
distribution is obtained by summing these event-level probabilities over the full data sample and normalizing to the total number of analyzed events.

Detector effects are corrected through a one-dimensional Bayesian unfolding procedure
[
34
]
, based on dedicated simulations including realistic strange-particle
p_{\rm T}
spectra and detector conditions. In addition, trigger inefficiency is accounted for both at the event level, through the factor
\varepsilon_{trig}
reported in Ref.
[
35
]
, and at the particle level, through a correction factor
\varepsilon_{part}
estimated from MC simulations for candidates lost in non-triggered events. The corrected multiplicity distribution is therefore written as

{\rm P}(n_{\rm S})=\frac{\varepsilon_{trig}}{\varepsilon_{part}^{n}}\cdot\left\{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{tot}}R_{i}[n]}{N_{tot}}\right\}_{Unfolded},

(2)

where
R_{i}[n]
is the reconstructed distribution and
N_{tot}
is the total number of analyzed events.

distributions are shown in Fig.

The measurement of multiple strange-hadron yields makes it possible to investigate particle-yield ratios with either balanced or strongly unbalanced strange-quark content between numerator and denominator. Ratios with
{\rm\Delta S}
= 0 are especially useful to isolate effects that are not trivially driven by net strangeness content.
As shown in Fig.
2
, even perfectly balanced ratios (
{\rm\Delta S}
= 0) exhibit a non-trivial multiplicity dependence. In particular,
\rm\langle Y_{n\Lambda}\rangle
/
\rm\langle Y_{n{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}}}\rangle
increases with
{\langle\mathrm{d}N_{\mathrm{ch}}/\mathrm{d}\eta\rangle}_{\mbox{$\left|\eta\right|<0.5$}}
(V0M class) for
n>1
, demonstrating for the first time that the evolution of multi-particle yield ratios with multiplicity cannot be attributed solely to the strangeness difference between numerator and denominator.
Additional
{\rm\Delta S}
= 0 ratios, shown in the second line of Fig.
2
, display the opposite behavior, but help clarify the picture. In particular,
\rm\langle Y_{1\Xi^{-}}\rangle
/
\rm\langle Y_{2{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}}}\rangle
,
\rm\langle Y_{1\Omega^{-}}\rangle
/
\rm\langle Y_{3{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}}}\rangle
, and
\rm\langle Y_{2\Xi^{-}}\rangle
/
\rm\langle Y_{4{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}}}\rangle
decrease with multiplicity despite the heavier and more baryon-rich numerator. This suggests that neither mass nor baryon number alone determines the observed evolution. A qualitatively consistent interpretation is provided by a naïve quark-coalescence picture. At fixed strange-quark content, these ratios probe how the available strange quarks compete with the available light quarks in forming hadrons with different valence-quark structure. For example, forming an
\Omega^{-}
requires only strange quarks, while forming three
{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}}
requires, in addition, three light quarks. In low-multiplicity events, the scarcity of light quarks increases the likelihood that the three strange quarks will form an
\Omega^{-}
rather than three
{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}}
. Conversely, at higher multiplicity, the increasing abundance of light quarks makes kaon production more likely, leading to a suppression of ratios such as
\rm\langle Y_{1\Omega^{-}}\rangle
/
\rm\langle Y_{3{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}}}\rangle
. A similar argument also accounts for the stronger decrease observed when baryons appear in the denominator, as in the ratios to
\Lambda
, since a
\Lambda
requires two light quarks whereas a
{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}}
requires only one.

Overall, these measurements show that the multiplicity evolution of multi-strange yield ratios at
{\rm\Delta S}
= 0 contains non-trivial information beyond conventional strangeness enhancement and that the interplay between strange- and light-quark availability may provide a coherent qualitative explanation of the observed patterns. Model comparisons provide further insight into the underlying production mechanisms. PYTHIA 8 Monash 2013 does not reproduce either the absolute values or the multiplicity evolution of the measured ratios. EPOS LHC gives a reasonable description for ratios to
{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}}
, but systematically overestimates those to
\Lambda
. PYTHIA 8 QCD-CR + Ropes provides the best overall agreement, reproducing both the absolute scale of the observables and their evolution. This suggests that, while the production rate of strange quarks remains an open issue, as indicated by the failure to describe the strange-hadron multiplet yields in Fig.
1
(right), the color-reconnection mechanism with light quarks captures the main trends observed in the data once strangeness is produced.

4
Conclusions

In summary, the production of (multi-)strange hadrons in pp collisions at
\sqrt{s}~=~5.02
TeV has been investigated using a novel event-by-event counting technique, extending the study of strangeness beyond average yields
[
1
]
. The measurement of
{\rm P}(n_{\rm S})
and the subsequent extraction of multiplet yields
\rm\langle Y_{nS}\rangle
provide a new, stringent test for hadronization models, probing events with large imbalances between strange and non-strange content.
The results presented in this work, focusing on yield ratios with balanced strangeness content (
{\rm\Delta S}
= 0), reveal a non-trivial multiplicity dependence that cannot be attributed to the strangeness content alone. The observed increase of
\rm\langle Y_{n\Lambda}\rangle
/
\rm\langle Y_{n{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}}}\rangle
with multiplicity for
n>1
, contrasted with the decreasing trends in ratios involving multi-strange baryons (such as
\rm\langle Y_{1\Omega^{-}}\rangle
/
\rm\langle Y_{3{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}}}\rangle
and
\rm\langle Y_{mbaryon}\rangle
/
\rm\langle Y_{n\Lambda}\rangle
), suggests that light-quark availability plays a significant role in the hadronization process. These patterns are qualitatively consistent with a quark-coalescence picture, where the competition between strange and light quarks for hadron formation evolves with event activity. Among the tested models, PYTHIA 8 QCD-CR + Ropes fornisce the most accurate description of these
{\rm\Delta S}
= 0 trends, highlighting the importance of color reconnection mechanisms.
Comprehensive results reported in Ref.
[
1
]
, which include unbalanced
{\rm\Delta S}
ratios, allow for the study of strangeness enhancement at its extremes, reaching
{\rm\Delta S}
up to 5 and an enhancement of two orders of magnitude from low to high multiplicity. Taken together, these measurements establish a new experimental approach that significantly constrains the dynamics of strange-particle production in small collision systems.
