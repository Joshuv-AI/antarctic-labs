On the Asymptotic Inadmissibility of Double Machine Learning Estimators Under Structure-Agnostic Models
Lin Liu, Rajarshi Mukherjee, James M. Robins

Abstract
Structure-agnostic (SA) models introduced by Balakrishnan et al. 2026 aim to reflect the general lack of knowledge of structural assumptions on data-generating laws such as smoothness or sparsity in practice. Roughly speaking, SA models restrict the observed-data generating law to be in some r n r_{n} -neighborhood of (black-box machine learning) estimates, treated as given and fixed, where r n r_{n} encodes the convergence rates of the estimates to the truth. Under SA models, Balakrishnan et al. 2026 show that the popular Double Machine Learning (DML) estimators for three functionals, the quadratic functional in the Gaussian sequence model, the quadratic density integral functional and the expected conditional covariance, are minimax. However, minimax estimators may be inadmissible. In this paper, we show that, for the first two of the three functionals, the DML estimator is asymptotically inadmissible under the SA model. In particular, we show that these two functionals fall into a class of functionals, which we refer to as the monotone bias class . For this class, we exhibit second-order ( U U -statistic) estimators, which asymptotically dominate DML estimators, under the SA model. These second-order estimators are empirical higher-order influence function (HOIF) estimators introduced in Liu et al. 2017 . Furthermore, the empirical HOIF estimator, like the DML estimator, is minimax for the third functional (the expected conditional covariance), although neither asymptotically dominates the other. Finally, we compare the SA model with the assumption-lean model of Liu et al. 2020a ; Liu et al. 2024 that imposes no assumptions beyond the trivial and empirically untestable hypothesis that the bias of any estimator, including DML estimators and empirical HOIF estimators, may be of order 1 1 . As a consequence, under our assumption-lean model, a Wald confidence interval centered at a DML estimator may under-cover. Liu et al. 2024 introduced a class of valid tests that can falsify, for functionals in the monotone bias class , the hypothesis that a DML-estimator-centered confidence interval covers the truth at its nominal level or greater. However, our tests are not consistent under the assumption-lean model, because no consistent tests exist ( Robins and Ritov 1997 ) . Furthermore, for any functional with the mixed bias property of Rotnitzky et al. 2021 , such as the expected conditional covariance or the average treatment effect ( Jin and Syrgkanis 2025b ) , the above falsification tests can falsify the hypothesis of rate-double-robustness .

Foundations of Statistics, Higher-Order Influence Functions, Structure-Agnostic Models, Assumption-Lean Inference, Minimaxity, (In)admissibility

In scientific disciplines such as epidemiology, clinical medicine and economics, one of the most important statistical tasks is to infer from the observed data a low-dimensional, smooth functional

Our Contribution

Our main technical contribution of this paper is related to the
decision-theoretic properties of estimators, tracing back to the classical
work of Abraham Wald

(
Wald 1941
;
Wald 1945
;
Wald 1947
)
. Wald is
renowned as the inventor of the minimax principle
(
Wald 1945
)
,
arguably the most popular theoretical paradigm used to measure the quality of
an estimator by modern (bio)statisticians and econometricians

(
Brown 1994
;
Andrews and Shapiro 2021
;
Adusumilli 2026
)
and
adopted in
Balakrishnan et al. 2026
.

However, certain minimax estimators may be inadmissible. One celebrated
example of the difference between minimaxity and (in)admissibility is Stein’s
paradox, asserting that the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE), although
minimax, is everywhere dominated in mean squared error (MSE) for every sample
size
n
by the James-Stein (JS) estimator in the many-normal-means model of
dimension at least three

(
Stein 1956
;
James and Stein 1961
;
Brown 1971
)
.
Hence, the MLE is inadmissible. In this paper, we will show that, under

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})
, there exists a class of
parameters
\psi
, which we refer to as the
monotone bias class
, for
which higher-order influence function (HOIF) estimators

(
Robins et al. 2008
;
Robins et al. 2016
;
Liu et al. 2017
)
dominate the first-order DML
estimator
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
in the large-
n
limit whenever
\prod_{j=1}^{J}r_{n,j}\gtrsim n^{-1}
(see Theorem
0
for the
actual statement). That is, we show that in the
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})
model, the mimimax DML estimator is asymptotically
inadmissible, when estimating a parameter in the
monotone bias class
.
To make the above claims precise, we next define:
asymptotic
(in)admissibility
and the
monotone bias class
of functionals. We shall
see that two out of the three functionals studied in

Balakrishnan et al. 2026
are in the
monotone bias class
.

Definition 1

(Asymptotic (in)admissibility)
.

An estimator sequence

\psi_{n}
of
\psi(\theta)
indexed by
n
is said to be
asymptotically
inadmissible
in scaled MSE loss under model
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})
if there exists another estimator sequence
\psi_{n}^{\prime}
of
\psi(\theta)
also indexed by
n
such that

\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathsf{mse}(\psi_{n}^{\prime})-\mathsf{mse}(\psi_{n})}{\mathsf{mse}(\psi_{n})}\leq 0\text{ and }\inf_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathsf{mse}(\psi_{n}^{\prime})-\mathsf{mse}(\psi_{n})}{\mathsf{mse}(\psi_{n})}<0,

where
\mathsf{mse}(\cdot)\equiv\mathsf{mse}_{\theta}(\cdot)\coloneqq\mathsf{E}_{\theta}(\cdot-\psi(\theta))^{2}
. If otherwise, we say
that
\psi_{n}
is
asymptotically admissible
.

In the above definition, the difference in MSEs is scaled. Because the MSE of
a reasonable estimator should decay to zero under the large
n
limit,
obtaining nontrivial results requires an appropriate scaling. Here, we choose
the MSE of
\psi_{n}
as a natural scaling factor. We refer the interested
readers to Appendix
B
for a more elaborate discussion on the
choice of the denominator.

The following definition of the
monotone bias class
is different from,
but as explained below, is essentially equivalent to that in our previous work
(
Liu et al. 2020a
)
.

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})
and
\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})
be, respectively, the bias and variance of the
first-order DML estimator
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
of
\psi
.
\psi
is said to be
in the
monotone bias class
if the following hold:

(1)

For any
r_{n}
,
|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})|\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{J}r_{n,j}
, there always exists another estimator,
denoted by
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
, such that
|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})|/|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})|-1\leq 0
, and the inequality becomes strict (asymptotically) at some law in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})
;

(2)

The variances of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
and
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
satisfy the
following condition: there exists a constant
v>0
such that
\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})=v/n
and

\begin{split}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})}{\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})}=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}1&\text{if }\dfrac{|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})|-|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})|}{v}=o(1),\\
\delta&\text{if }\dfrac{|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})|-|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})|}{v}\gtrsim 1,\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(2)

for some constant
\delta\neq 1
but possibly
\delta>1
.

Here the constants
v
and
\delta
can depend on the data generating
distribution
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}
.

We are now ready to state the following general theorem regarding the
asymptotic inadmissibility of the DML estimator
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
. The proof
is given following a few remarks.

Theorem 0
.

For
\psi
belonging to the
monotone bias class
,
under the SA model
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})
, there
exists another estimator
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
such that (i)
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}

is asymptotically not greater in scaled MSE than
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
over

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})
for any
r_{n}
, and (ii)

\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
is asymptotically strictly smaller than
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}

in scaled MSE at some law in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})

whenever
\prod_{j=1}^{J}r_{n,j}\gtrsim n^{-1}
. Hence,
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
is
asymptotically inadmissible if and only if
\prod_{j=1}^{J}r_{n,j}\gtrsim n^{-1}
.

It should be noted that Theorem
0
does not discuss
minimaxity of either estimator. As we shall see, for two examples in the

monotone bias class
– the quadratic functional in the Gaussian
sequence model in Section
2
and the quadratic density integral
functional in Section
3
, the minimaxity of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
or

\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
requires an additional condition
\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{J}r_{n,j}}\gtrsim n^{-1}
. As explained in

Balakrishnan et al. 2026
, when
\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{J}r_{n,j}}\ll n^{-1}
, the so-called plug-in estimators dominate both
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
and
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
in these two examples, because the plug-in estimators have zero variance and squared bias
\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{J}r_{n,j}}\ll n^{-1}
, while both
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
and
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
have variances of order
n^{-1}
. However, as also noted by
Balakrishnan et al. 2026
,
\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{J}r_{n,j}}\ll n^{-1}
generally does not hold if the sample size used to estimate
\widehat{\theta}
is of the same order as
n
. Therefore, there is essentially no loss of generality if we exclude the case where
\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{J}r_{n,j}}\ll n^{-1}
holds, as we do in Sections
2
and
3
, in which case both
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
and
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
are minimax rate-optimal.

Remark 1
.

In all the examples covered in this paper and in

Balakrishnan et al. 2026
,
|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})|^{2}

is upper bounded by
\prod_{j=1}^{J}r_{n,j}
times a constant. In view of
Theorem
0
, when
\psi
is in the
monotone bias
class
,
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
is
asymptotically inadmissible
and is
asymptotically dominated by
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
when
|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})|\gtrsim n^{-1/2}
. When
|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})|\ll n^{-1/2}
,
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
is asymptotically never worse than
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
and both are minimax. In particular, when DML estimators
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
are deployed in practice, it is often implicitly assumed that

\prod_{j=1}^{J}r_{n,j}\ll n^{-1}
holds, a condition often referred to
as the
rate-double-robustness
when
J=2
. Under this condition,
neither estimator dominates the other; further,
|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})|\ll n^{-1/2}
automatically holds, and thus both
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
and
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
can be used to construct an asymptotically valid
Wald confidence interval (CI) of length
O(n^{-1/2})
, which is common
practice, although other non-Wald CI constructions are also under rapid
development
(
Zheng et al. 2025
)
.

Since in practice
r_{n}
is unknown and the possibility that it is of order
1
cannot be empirically excluded
(
Robins and Ritov 1997
;
Ritov et al. 2014
)
, we consider the following assumption-lean model.

Definition 3
.

Given a constant
R^{\ast}>0
, we define the model
\mathcal{P}_{\rm AL}(\widehat{\theta})\equiv\mathcal{P}_{\rm SA}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n}=(R^{\ast},\cdots,R^{\ast}))

Under the assumption-lean model, for parameters in the

, it follows from Theorem

\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}

dominates

In fact, as discussed in Section

below, we can say more. Given a

\psi
in the

, we can construct an

\mathcal{H}_{0}
:

that, when

and thus also empirical evidence that the Wald CI centered on

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{AL}}(\widehat{\theta})

Remark 2
.

It will be clear in later sections that all examples studied by
Balakrishnan et al. 2026
are in the
monotone bias class
defined in Definition
2
, except for the expected conditional covariance (see Section

). The two parts of conditions in Definition

need further elaboration. Part (1) states that the bias of
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
is never greater than but sometimes strictly smaller than that of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
. Part (2) says that the difference between the variances of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
and
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
is negligible (of order
o(1/n)
) if the bias reduction of
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
is negligible (of order
o(1)
). The original definition of the
monotone bias class
in
Liu et al. 2020a
contains only part (1) but part (2) was implicit. As described later, the alternative estimator
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
is a second-order
U
-statistic (heretofore referred to as the second-order estimators for simplicity), constructed via the theory of HOIFs
(
Robins et al. 2008
;
Robins et al. 2016
;
Liu et al. 2017
)
. Such second-order estimators satisfy both Parts (1) and (2). The difference between the variances of
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
and
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
can be bounded as follows, as is the case in all our examples in later sections:

\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})-\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})\lesssim\frac{k}{n^{2}}+\frac{|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})|-|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})|}{n}+\frac{v^{1/2}\{|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})|-|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})|\}^{1/2}}{n},

where
k
is a tuning parameter chosen so that
k=o(n)
. This will be
demonstrated in the proofs of Theorem
1
–Theorem
4
in Appendix
A
. It is then not difficult
to verify that Part (2) of Definition
2
holds. In terms of Part
(1), the second-order estimator
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
can be viewed as debiasing

\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
by estimating a part of
\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})
;
also see comments after Lemma
2
,
4
, and

6
, and Theorem
4
. For functionals outside the

monotone bias class
such as the expected conditional covariance
functional covered in Section
4
,
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
is still rate-optimal but may have a bias exceed that of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
under certain laws in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})
.

With these ingredients, the proof of Theorem

is almost

Proof of Theorem
0
.

The result follows directly from Definition
2
. To see this, we first decompose the scaled MSE difference as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})-\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})}{\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})}=-\frac{\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})^{2}-\mathsf{bias}^{2}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})}{\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})^{2}+\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})}+\frac{\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})-\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})}{\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})^{2}+\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})}\coloneqq T_{1,n}+T_{2,n}.

When
\dfrac{|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})|-|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})|}{n\cdot\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})}=o(1)
holds, by (
2
), we always have:

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}T_{2,n}=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})/\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})-1}{\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})^{2}/\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})+1}=0.

Since
T_{1,n}
is always non-positive, we have
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}T_{1,n}+T_{2,n}\leq 0
. In words, when the bias reduction is sufficiently small, by (2) of Definition
2
, the asymptotic variance of the second-order estimator is not different from that of the first-order DML estimator. On the contrary, we now suppose that
|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})|-|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})|\gtrsim n\cdot\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})
, and hence also
|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})|\gtrsim n\cdot\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})
. We must also have

\displaystyle\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})^{2}-\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})^{2}\gg\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})-\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}),

and by the non-positivity of
T_{1,n}
, we have
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}T_{1,n}+T_{2,n}\leq 0
. When
|\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})|^{2}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{J}r_{n,j}\ll n^{-1}
, the denominator in the scaled MSE difference is dominated by
\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})=v/n
. The numerator can only be of smaller order than the denominator, rendering
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}T_{1,n}+T_{2,n}=0
. When
\prod_{j=1}^{J}r_{n,j}\gtrsim n^{-1}
, there must exist a law such that
\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})\gtrsim n^{-1/2}
. By Definition
2
, there must exist a distribution for which
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}T_{1,n}<0
, which completes the proof.

L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
-norm. If
\mathbb{P}
is the Lebesgue measure, we omit the subscript and write
\mathsf{\Pi}(\cdot\mid\bar{f}_{k})
and
\|\cdot\|_{2}
for short.
\|\cdot\|_{2}
also denotes the

\ell^{2}
-norm of a vector. We denote the population Gram matrix of
\bar{f}_{k}
under the distribution
\mathbb{P}
as
\Sigma_{\mathbb{P},\bar{f}_{k}}\coloneqq\mathsf{E}[\bar{f}_{k}(X)\bar{f}_{k}(X)^{\top}]
. When
it is clear from the context, we omit the dependence in the subscript on

\mathbb{P}
or
\bar{f}_{k}
or both.

The remainder of this paper makes Theorem
0
concrete.
Sections
2
–
4
cover four examples of
\psi
, one of
which does not belong to the
monotone bias class
.
Theorem
0
will then be specialized for the three examples
in the
monotone bias class
. Section
5
concludes the paper by
making some additional comments on the relevance of the SA model to
practitioners who are more interested in uncertainty quantification or
statistical inference. Proofs are deferred to the Appendix.

2
Quadratic Functional in the Gaussian Sequence Model

As in
Balakrishnan et al. 2026
, we observe data drawn from the
infinite Gaussian sequence model:

\displaystyle Y_{i}=\theta_{i}+\varepsilon_{i},i=1,2,\cdots

(3)

where
\{\varepsilon_{i},i=1,2,\cdots\}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{N}(0,n^{-1})
. Let
\theta\coloneqq\{\theta_{i},i=1,2,\cdots\}
and
Y\coloneqq\{Y_{i},i=1,2,\cdots\}
. We are interested in
learning about the quadratic functional

\displaystyle Q(\theta)\coloneqq\|\theta\|_{2}^{2}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\theta_{i}^{2}.

(4)

The SA model corresponding to
\psi(\theta)
is defined by

Balakrishnan et al. 2026
as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}((\widehat{\theta},\widehat{\theta}),(r_{n},r_{n}))\coloneqq\left\{\theta:\|\widehat{\theta}-\theta\|_{2}^{2}\leq r_{n}\right\},

(5)

where
\widehat{\theta}
is some initial estimator of
\theta
. It is noteworthy
that we deliberately write
\widehat{\theta}
and
r_{n}
twice in the notation

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}((\widehat{\theta},\widehat{\theta}),(r_{n},r_{n}))
to
emphasize that we take
J=2
and
\prod_{j=1}^{J}r_{n,j}=r_{n}^{2}
in
this case. In the sequel, however, we write
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})
instead in the text to simplify the notation. We adopt
a similar convention for the quadratic density integral functional in
Section
3
and the expected conditional variance in
Section
4
.

Balakrishnan et al. 2026
obtained the following results.

Lemma 1
.

The following hold. When
r_{n}\gtrsim n^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{n}(Q;\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n}))\coloneqq\inf_{\widehat{Q}}\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})}\mathsf{E}_{\theta}[(\widehat{Q}-Q(\theta))^{2}]\gtrsim r_{n}^{2}+\frac{\|\widehat{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}}{n}.

This lower bound is attained by the first-order estimator
\widehat{Q}_{1,n}
,
defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{Q}_{1,n}\coloneqq 2\langle Y,\widehat{\theta}\rangle-\|\widehat{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}.

The bias, variance, and mean squared error (MSE) of
\widehat{Q}_{1,n}
have the
following forms:

\displaystyle\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{Q}_{1,n})=-\|\widehat{\theta}-\theta\|_{2}^{2},\mathsf{var}(\widehat{Q}_{1,n})=\frac{4}{n}\|\widehat{\theta}\|_{2}^{2},\text{ and }\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{Q}_{1,n})=\|\widehat{\theta}-\theta\|_{2}^{4}+\frac{4}{n}\|\widehat{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}.

The lower and upper bounds can be found in Theorem 1, Part 1 and Theorem 2,
Part 1 of
Balakrishnan et al. 2026
, respectively. These results,
taken together, prove the rate optimality of
\widehat{Q}_{1,n}
in the minimax sense.

To show the asymptotic inadmissibility of the minimax estimator
\widehat{Q}_{1,n}
, we need to exhibit a different estimator that improves upon
\widehat{Q}_{1,n}
. To this end, we adopt the following second-order estimator
appeared in
Robins and van der Vaart 2006
:

\displaystyle\widehat{Q}_{2,n}(k)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i,1}Y_{i,2}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\left(2Y_{i}\widehat{\theta}_{i}-\widehat{\theta}_{i}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\widehat{Q}_{1,n}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(Y_{i,1}Y_{i,2}-2Y_{i}\widehat{\theta}_{i}+\widehat{\theta}_{i}^{2}\right),

where
Y_{i,1}\coloneqq Y_{i}+\Phi^{-1}(U_{i})/\sqrt{n}
,
Y_{i,2}\coloneqq Y_{i}-\Phi^{-1}(U_{i})/\sqrt{n}
,
\Phi
is the standard normal
cumulative distribution function and
U_{i}
’s are independent uniform random
variables over
[0,1]
. Here
Y_{i,1}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\displaystyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\textstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}}Y_{i,2}
. The difference between

\widehat{Q}_{2,n}(k)
and
\widehat{Q}_{1,n}
is an unbiased estimator of

\|\mathsf{\Pi}_{k}(\theta-\widehat{\theta})\|_{2}^{2}
, where
\mathsf{\Pi}_{k}(\cdot)
denotes the projection onto the first
k
coordinates of the
input infinite-dimensional vector, with
\mathsf{\Pi}_{k}^{\perp}(\cdot)

naturally meaning the projection onto the
(k+1)
-th coordinate and onward.

The following lemma characterizes the bias, variance, and mean squared error
of
\widehat{Q}_{2,n}(k)
. The proof can be found in
Appendix
C.1
.

Lemma 2
.

The bias and variance of
\widehat{Q}_{2,n}(k)
read as:

\displaystyle\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{Q}_{2,n}(k))

\displaystyle=-\|\mathsf{\Pi}_{k}^{\perp}(\widehat{\theta}-\theta)\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim r_{n},

\displaystyle\mathsf{var}(\widehat{Q}_{2,n}(k))

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{n}\|\widehat{\theta}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{4k}{n^{2}}+\frac{4}{n}\|\mathsf{\Pi}_{k}(\widehat{\theta}-\theta)\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{4}{n}\langle\mathsf{\Pi}_{k}\widehat{\theta},\mathsf{\Pi}_{k}(\widehat{\theta}-\theta)\rangle\lesssim\frac{1}{n},

where the inequalities hold for
\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})
.

We note that
\|\mathsf{\Pi}_{k}(\widehat{\theta}-\theta)\|_{2}^{2}\equiv\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{Q}_{1,n})-\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{Q}_{2,n}(k))
so

\widehat{Q}_{2,n}(k)
corrects the bias of
\widehat{Q}_{1,n}
by estimating a
lower bound of
\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{Q}_{1,n})\lesssim r_{n}
. By
Lemma
2
,
Q(\theta)
belongs to the
monotone bias
class
. Comparing
\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{Q}_{2,n}(k))
and
\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{Q}_{1,n})
in the asymptotic sense, we obtain the first main
statistical result of this paper. There always exists a distribution in

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})
such that
Definition
2
(1) holds. To see this, consider the case where

\theta=\widehat{\theta}+r_{n}^{1/2}\upsilon
, where
\|\upsilon\|_{2}=1
and the coordinates of
\upsilon
from
k+1
onward are all zeros. With
this choice,
\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{Q}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k}))=0
. The rest
of the proof can be found in Appendix
A.1
.

Theorem 1
.

Under Model
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})
with
r_{n}\gtrsim n^{-1}
,
\widehat{Q}_{2,n}(k)
is

asymptotically minimax
and the following hold as long as
k
is chosen
such that
k=o(n\|\widehat{\theta}\|_{2}^{2})
.

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{Q}_{2,n}(k))-\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{Q}_{1,n})}{\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{Q}_{1,n})}\leq 0,\text{ and when $r_{n}^{2}\gtrsim n^{-1}$}

\displaystyle\inf_{\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{Q}_{2,n}(k))-\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{Q}_{1,n})}{\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{Q}_{1,n})}<0.

Thus, by Definition
1
, the first-order DML estimator

\widehat{Q}_{1,n}
is
asymptotically inadmissible
when
r_{n}^{2}\gtrsim n^{-1}
. The same conclusions hold when we replace the SA model
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{\theta},r_{n})
with the assumption-lean model

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{AL}}(\widehat{\theta})
and drop the assumptions on
r_{n}
.

Echoing the comment right after Theorem
0
, for the
minimaxity of
\widehat{Q}_{1,n}
or
\widehat{Q}_{2,n}(k)
, we need
r_{n}\gtrsim n^{-1}
. When
r_{n}\ll n^{-1}
, the so-called plug-in estimator
\widehat{Q}_{\mathrm{pi}}\coloneqq\|\widehat{\theta}\|^{2}
has zero variance and
squared bias of order
r_{n}
, thus dominating both
\widehat{Q}_{1,n}
and

\widehat{Q}_{2,n}(k)
when
\|\widehat{\theta}\|^{2}
is of order 1.
However,
r_{n}\ll n^{-1}
, or equivalently
\|\widehat{\theta}-\theta\|\ll n^{-1/2}
, is generally difficult to hold if the sample used to
compute
\widehat{\theta}
is of size similar to
n
, as such a condition says
that we can estimate the possibly infinite-dimensional
\theta
at a rate much
faster than the parametric rate. A similar discussion also applies to the
quadratic density integral functional to be discussed next.

3
Quadratic Density Integral Functional

The second example is about estimating the quadratic density integral
functional of the probability density function
f
of
X
based on
n
i.i.d.
observations
\{X_{i}\in[0,1]^{d}\}_{i=1}^{n}\sim f
:

\psi(f)\coloneqq\int f(x)^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

(6)

The SA model corresponding to
\psi(f)
is defined by

Balakrishnan et al. 2026
as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}((\widehat{f},\widehat{f}),(r_{n},r_{n}))\coloneqq\left\{f:\|\widehat{f}-f\|_{2}^{2}\leq r_{n},\int f(x)\mathrm{d}x=1,f\geq 0,\|\widehat{f}\|_{\infty}\leq C,\|f\|_{\infty}\leq C\right\},

(7)

where
\widehat{f}
is some initial estimator of
f
computed from a separate
independent sample treated as fixed. Similar to the case in
Section
2
, we take
J=2
and
\prod_{j=1}^{J}r_{n,j}=r_{n}^{2}
, and write
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{f},r_{n})
instead.

Remark 3
.

As mentioned in footnote 1, we use the same estimator
\widehat{f}\equiv\widehat{f}_{1}\equiv\widehat{f}_{2}
to compute
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
, which is
the standard DML estimator commonly employed in the literature

(
Chernozhukov et al. 2018
)
but excludes more refined estimators with
f

estimated by separate samples studied in
Newey and Robins 2018
;
McGrath and Mukherjee 2026
;
McClean et al. 2026
.

The following lemma, paraphrasing the results of

Balakrishnan et al. 2026
, summarizes the lower and upper bounds of
the error rate of estimating
\psi(f)
under
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{f},r_{n})
.

Lemma 3
.

The following hold. When
r_{n}\gtrsim n^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{n}(\psi;\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{f},r_{n}))\coloneqq\inf_{\widehat{\psi}}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{f},r_{n})}\mathsf{E}_{f}[(\widehat{\psi}-\psi(f))^{2}]\gtrsim r_{n}^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\left(\|\widehat{f}\|_{3}^{3}-\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}^{4}\right).

This lower bound is attained by the first-order estimator
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
,
defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}\coloneqq\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{f}(X_{i})-\int\widehat{f}(x)^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

The bias, variance and MSE of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
have the following forms:

\displaystyle\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})

\displaystyle=-\int(\widehat{f}(x)-f(x))^{2}\mathrm{d}x\equiv-\|\widehat{f}-f\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{n}\mathsf{var}(\widehat{f}(X))\equiv\frac{4}{n}\left\{\int\widehat{f}(x)^{2}f(x)\mathrm{d}x-\left(\int\widehat{f}(x)f(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}\right\},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})

\displaystyle=\|\widehat{f}-f\|_{2}^{4}+\frac{4}{n}\mathsf{var}(\widehat{f}(X)).

The lower and upper bounds can be found in Theorem 1, Part 2 and Theorem 2,
Part 2 of
Balakrishnan et al. 2026
, respectively. These results,
taken together, prove the optimality of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
in the minimax sense.

To show the asymptotic inadmissibility of the minimax estimator
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
, when
r_{n}^{2}\gtrsim n^{-1}
or equivalently
r_{n}^{1/2}\gtrsim n^{-1/4}
, we exhibit a different estimator that improves on

\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
. To this end, let
\bar{\phi}_{k}\coloneqq(\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{k})^{\top}
be a
k
-dimensional orthonormal basis with respect
to the Lebesgue measure
(
Chen 2007
)
. We then construct the following
second-order
U
-statistic estimator:

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}+\mathbb{U}_{n,2}\left[\left(\bar{\phi}_{k}(X_{1})-\int\bar{\phi}_{k}(x)\widehat{f}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\top}\left(\bar{\phi}_{k}(X_{2})-\int\bar{\phi}_{k}(x)\widehat{f}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\right]

\displaystyle\equiv\mathbb{U}_{n,1}[2(\widehat{f}(X)-\mathsf{\Pi}[\widehat{f}\mid\bar{\phi}_{k}](X))]+\mathbb{U}_{n,2}[\bar{\phi}_{k}(X_{1})^{\top}\bar{\phi}_{k}(X_{2})]-\int\left(\widehat{f}(x)^{2}-\mathsf{\Pi}[\widehat{f}\mid\bar{\phi}_{k}](x)^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x.

Remark 4
.

Expert readers shall realize that
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k})
debiases
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
by subtracting an unbiased
estimator of a part of its bias, based on HOIFs. The part of the bias of

\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
to be estimated is determined by the choice of
\bar{\phi}_{k}
. We mention in passing that the falsification test of

Liu et al. 2020a
mentioned earlier is essentially based on the statistic

\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k})-\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
. Similar tests or
estimators have also been considered in instrumental variable or proximal
causal inference settings
(
Breunig and Chen 2024
;
Liu et al. 2024
)
.

Let
\eta\coloneqq\int\bar{\phi}_{k}(x)f(x)\mathrm{d}x
and
\widehat{\eta}\coloneqq\int\bar{\phi}_{k}(x)\widehat{f}(x)\mathrm{d}x
. We also make the
following assumption on
\Sigma
.

Assumption 1
.

\Sigma
is assumed to have bounded spectra.

We now state the following lemma. The proof can be found in
Appendix
C.2
.

Lemma 4
.

The bias and variance of
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k})
read as:

\displaystyle\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k}))=

\displaystyle-\int(\widehat{f}(x)-f(x))^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int\mathsf{\Pi}[\widehat{f}-f\mid\bar{\phi}_{k}](x)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\equiv-\|\widehat{f}-f\|_{2}^{2}+\|\mathsf{\Pi}[\widehat{f}-f\mid\bar{\phi}_{k}]\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\|\widehat{f}-f\|_{2}^{2}+\|\widehat{\eta}-\eta\|_{2}^{2}\equiv-\|\mathsf{\Pi}^{\perp}[\widehat{f}-f\mid\bar{\phi}_{k}]\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim r_{n},

\displaystyle\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k}))=

\displaystyle\ \frac{4}{n}\mathsf{var}[\widehat{f}(X)]+\frac{8}{n}\left(\int\bar{\phi}_{k}(x)f(x)\widehat{f}(x)\mathrm{d}x-\widehat{\eta}\right)^{\top}(\widehat{\eta}-\eta)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\left\{\begin{array}[c]{c}\mathsf{Tr}(\Sigma^{2})-4\widehat{\eta}^{\top}\Sigma\eta+2\widehat{\eta}^{\top}\Sigma\widehat{\eta}+2\widehat{\eta}^{\top}\widehat{\eta}\cdot\eta^{\top}\eta\\
+\,2(\widehat{\eta}^{\top}\eta)^{2}-4\widehat{\eta}^{\top}\widehat{\eta}\cdot\widehat{\eta}^{\top}\eta+(\widehat{\eta}^{\top}\widehat{\eta})^{2}\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{4}{n}\mathsf{var}[\widehat{f}(X)]+\frac{C}{n}\|\mathsf{\Pi}[\widehat{f}-f\mid\bar{\phi}_{k}]\|_{2}+\frac{Ck}{n^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{n},

where the inequalities hold for
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{f},r_{n})
and under Assumption
1
.

We note that
\|\mathsf{\Pi}[\widehat{f}-f\mid\bar{\phi}_{k}]\|_{2}^{2}\equiv\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})-\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k}))
so
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k})
corrects the bias
of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
by estimating a lower bound of
\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})\lesssim r_{n}
. By Lemma
1
,
\psi(f)

belongs to the
monotone bias class
. The following theorem therefore
instantiates Theorem
0
for the quadratic density integral
functional
\psi(f)
. There always exists a distribution in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{f},r_{n})
such that Definition
2
(1) holds.
To see this, consider the case where
f=\widehat{f}+r_{n}^{1/2}\beta^{\top}\bar{\phi}_{k}
with
\|\beta\|_{2}=1
, for which
\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k}))=0
. The rest of the proof can be found
in Appendix
A.2
.

Theorem 2
.

Under model
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{f},r_{n})
and
Assumption
1
,
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k})
is

asymptotically minimax
and the following hold as long as
k
is chosen
such that
k=o(n\mathsf{var}[\widehat{f}(X)])
:

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{f},r_{n})}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k}))-\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})}{\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})}\leq 0,\text{ and when
$r_{n}^{2}\gtrsim n^{-1}$}

\displaystyle\inf_{f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{f},r_{n})}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k}))-\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})}{\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})}<0.

Thus, by Definition
1
, the first-order DML estimator

\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
is
asymptotically inadmissible
when
r_{n}^{2}\gtrsim n^{-1}
. The same conclusions hold when we replace the SA model

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}(\widehat{f},r_{n})
with the assumption-lean model

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{AL}}(\widehat{f})
and drop the assumptions on
r_{n}
.

4
Expected Conditional Covariance

All the functionals that we have analyzed so far fall within the

monotone bias class
.
In this section, we turn to the Expected Conditional Covariance (ECC)
functional, defined as

\displaystyle\psi(a,b)\coloneqq\mathsf{E}[\mathsf{cov}(A,Y\mid X)]\equiv\mathsf{E}[(A-a(X))(Y-b(X))],

where
X\in[0,1]^{d}
denotes the baseline covariates,
A,Y\in\mathbb{R}

are two types of responses,
a(\cdot)\coloneqq\mathsf{E}(A\mid X=\cdot)

and
b(\cdot)\coloneqq\mathsf{E}(Y\mid X=\cdot)
. The ECC functional, as
extensively discussed in
Liu et al. 2020a
, is not in the
monotone
bias class
. Therefore, not surprisingly, we can no longer conclude the
asymptotic inadmissibility of the first-order DML estimator
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
for
\psi(a,b)
. Specifically, based on
n
i.i.d. observations
\{X_{i},A_{i},Y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
,
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
reads as:

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(A_{i}-\widehat{a}(X_{i}))(Y_{i}-\widehat{b}(X_{i})).

As usual, before presenting our new results, we first summarize the
statistical properties and minimaxity of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
obtained in

Balakrishnan et al. 2026
under the SA model defined by

Balakrishnan et al. 2026
for
\psi(a,b)
:

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}((\widehat{a},\widehat{b}),(r_{n},s_{n}))\coloneqq\left\{(a,b):\|a-\widehat{a}\|_{2,\mathbb{P}}^{2}\lesssim r_{n},\|b-\widehat{b}\|_{2,\mathbb{P}}^{2}\lesssim s_{n}\right\}.

(8)

We let
p
denote the marginal density of
X
, which, for simplicity, is
assumed to be
\mathrm{Unif}([0,1]^{d})
.

Lemma 5
.

The following hold.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{n}(\psi;\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}((\widehat{a},\widehat{b}),(r_{n},s_{n})))\coloneqq\inf_{\widehat{\psi}}\sup_{(a,b)\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}((\widehat{a},\widehat{b}),(r_{n},s_{n}))}\mathsf{E}_{a,b}[(\widehat{\psi}-\psi(a,b))^{2}]\gtrsim r_{n}\cdot s_{n}+\frac{1}{n}.

This lower bound is attained by the first-order estimator
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
.
The bias, variance, and MSE of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
have the following forms:

\displaystyle\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})

\displaystyle=-\langle a-\widehat{a},b-\widehat{b}\rangle_{\mathbb{P}},

\displaystyle\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left\{\mathsf{E}[(A-\widehat{a}(X))^{2}(Y-\widehat{b}(X))^{2}]-\mathsf{E}^{2}[(A-\widehat{a}(X))(Y-\widehat{b}(X))]\right\},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})

\displaystyle=\langle a-\widehat{a},b-\widehat{b}\rangle_{\mathbb{P}}^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\left\{\mathsf{E}[(A-\widehat{a}(X))^{2}(Y-\widehat{b}(X))^{2}]-\mathsf{E}^{2}[(A-\widehat{a}(X))(Y-\widehat{b}(X))]\right\}.

To construct the second-order estimator, we similarly find a
k
-dimensional
dictionary
\bar{\phi}_{k}
and denote
\Sigma\coloneqq\mathsf{E}[\bar{\phi}_{k}(X)^{\otimes 2}]
. In practice, one needs to estimate
\Sigma
from data.
We make the following assumptions on
\Sigma
and its estimator.

Assumption 2
.

\Sigma
is assumed to have bounded spectra and there exists
an estimator
\widehat{\Sigma}
of
\Sigma
such that
\widehat{\Sigma}
also has
bounded spectra and
\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o(1)
,
where
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}
denotes the matrix operator norm.
Without loss of generality, we take
\Sigma=\Sigma^{-1}=\mathrm{I}
.

We then construct the following second-order estimator for
\psi(a,b)
.

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k};\widehat{\Sigma})\coloneqq\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}+\widehat{U}_{n,2}(\bar{\phi}_{k};\widehat{\Sigma}),\text{ where }

\displaystyle\widehat{U}_{n,2}(\bar{\phi}_{k};\widehat{\Sigma})\coloneqq\mathbb{U}_{n,2}\left[(A_{1}-\widehat{a}(X_{1}))\bar{\phi}_{k}(X_{1})^{\top}\widehat{\Sigma}^{-1}\bar{\phi}_{k}(X_{2})(Y_{2}-\widehat{b}(X_{2}))\right].

We further introduce some short-hand notation for ease of exposition:

\displaystyle\widehat{\varepsilon}_{a}\coloneqq A-\widehat{a}(X),\widehat{\varepsilon}_{b}\coloneqq Y-\widehat{b}(X),

\displaystyle\alpha\coloneqq\mathsf{E}[(a(X)-\widehat{a}(X))\bar{\phi}_{k}(X)],\beta\coloneqq[(b(X)-\widehat{b}(X))\bar{\phi}_{k}(X)],

\displaystyle\Sigma_{a,a}\coloneqq\mathsf{E}[(A-\widehat{a}(X))^{2}\bar{\phi}_{k}(X)\bar{\phi}_{k}(X)^{\top}],\Sigma_{b,b}\coloneqq\mathsf{E}[(Y-\widehat{b}(X))^{2}\bar{\phi}_{k}(X)\bar{\phi}_{k}(X)^{\top}],

\displaystyle\text{and }\Sigma_{a,b}\coloneqq\mathsf{E}[(A-\widehat{a}(X))(Y-\widehat{b}(X))\bar{\phi}_{k}(X)\bar{\phi}_{k}(X)^{\top}].

We are now ready to state the following lemma. The proof is by direct
calculations and can be found in Appendix

Lemma 6
.

The bias and variance of
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k};\widehat{\Sigma})
read as:

\displaystyle\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k};\widehat{\Sigma}))

\displaystyle=-\langle\mathsf{\Pi}^{\perp}[\widehat{a}-a\mid\bar{\phi}_{k}],\mathsf{\Pi}^{\perp}[\widehat{b}-b\mid\bar{\phi}_{k}]\rangle_{\mathbb{P}}+\alpha^{\top}(\widehat{\Sigma}^{-1}-\mathrm{I})\beta\lesssim r_{n}^{1/2}\cdot s_{n}^{1/2},

\displaystyle\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k};\widehat{\Sigma}))

\displaystyle=\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})+\mathsf{var}(\widehat{U}_{n,2}(\bar{\phi}_{k};\widehat{\Sigma}))+2\mathsf{cov}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n},\widehat{U}_{n,2}(\bar{\phi}_{k};\widehat{\Sigma}))\lesssim\frac{1}{n},

where

\displaystyle\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{n}\left\{\mathsf{E}[\widehat{\varepsilon}_{a}^{2}\widehat{\varepsilon}_{b}^{2}]-\mathsf{E}^{2}[\widehat{\varepsilon}_{a}\widehat{\varepsilon}_{b}]\right\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{var}(\widehat{U}_{n,2}(\bar{\phi}_{k};\widehat{\Sigma}))=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{n(n-1)}\mathsf{Tr}\left\{\Sigma_{a,a}\widehat{\Sigma}^{-1}\Sigma_{b,b}\widehat{\Sigma}^{-1}+(\Sigma_{a,b}\widehat{\Sigma}^{-1})^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle+\frac{n-2}{n(n-1)}\left(\alpha^{\top}\widehat{\Sigma}^{-1}\Sigma_{b,b}\widehat{\Sigma}^{-1}\alpha+\beta^{\top}\widehat{\Sigma}^{-1}\Sigma_{a,a}\widehat{\Sigma}^{-1}\beta+2\alpha^{\top}\widehat{\Sigma}^{-1}\Sigma_{a,b}\widehat{\Sigma}^{-1}\beta\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{2(2n-3)}{n(n-1)}(\alpha^{\top}\widehat{\Sigma}^{-1}\beta)^{2},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{Ck}{n^{2}}+\frac{C}{n}\left\{\|\mathsf{\Pi}[\widehat{a}-a\mid\bar{\phi}_{k}]\|_{2,\mathbb{P}}^{2}+\|\mathsf{\Pi}[\widehat{b}-b|\bar{\phi}_{k}]\|_{2,\mathbb{P}}^{2}\right\}\lesssim\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle 2\mathsf{cov}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n},\widehat{U}_{n,2}(\bar{\phi}_{k};\widehat{\Sigma}))=

\displaystyle\ \frac{2}{n}\left\{\mathsf{E}[\widehat{\varepsilon}_{a}\widehat{\varepsilon}_{b}^{2}\bar{\phi}_{k}(X)^{\top}]\widehat{\Sigma}^{-1}\alpha+\mathsf{E}[\widehat{\varepsilon}_{a}^{2}\widehat{\varepsilon}_{b}\bar{\phi}_{k}(X)^{\top}]\widehat{\Sigma}^{-1}\beta-2\mathsf{E}[\widehat{\varepsilon}_{a}\widehat{\varepsilon}_{b}]\alpha^{\top}\widehat{\Sigma}^{-1}\beta\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{C}{n}\left\{\|\mathsf{\Pi}[\widehat{a}-a\mid\bar{\phi}_{k}]\|_{2,\mathbb{P}}+\|\mathsf{\Pi}[\widehat{b}-b\mid\bar{\phi}_{k}]\|_{2,\mathbb{P}}\right\}\lesssim\frac{1}{n}.

The inequalities hold for
(a,b)\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SA}}((\widehat{a},\widehat{b}),(r_{n},s_{n}))
under Assumption
2
.

It is not difficult to also see that
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}(\bar{\phi}_{k};\widehat{\Sigma})
corrects the bias of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
by estimating a lower

.

5
Concluding Remarks

The SA model introduced in
Balakrishnan et al. 2026
is a mathematically appealing construct that has inspired follow-up work
(
Jin and Syrgkanis 2025b
;
Bonvini et al. 2024
;
Jin et al. 2025
;
Jin and Syrgkanis 2025a
;
Gu et al. 2026
;
Gu 2025
)
, including our current paper. The assumption-lean model
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{AL}}(\widehat{\theta})
defined in Definition
3
, is aligned with the goal of understanding what can be learned from a model that makes almost no assumptions. As discussed earlier, in terms of point estimation, both the first-order DML estimator
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
and our second-order estimator
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
remain minimax with rate
O(1)
in the assumption-lean model
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{AL}}(\widehat{\theta})
for all the parameters studied in
Balakrishnan et al. 2026
; for
\psi
in
monotone bias class
, our
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
continues to asymptotically dominate
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
in the scaled MSE.

However, statisticians care about uncertainty quantification or inference as much as or even more than point estimation. Neither the (asymptotic) minimaxity/inadmissibility of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
nor the minimaxity of
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
in the assumption-lean model
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{AL}}(\widehat{\theta})
offer any guidance on how to quantify uncertainty, absent further knowledge of
\widehat{\theta}
or
\Theta
. The above argument is not new. Before
Balakrishnan et al. 2026
, we considered inference on
\psi
under the assumption-lean model
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{AL}}(\widehat{\theta})
in
Liu et al. 2020a
and
Liu et al. 2024
. The former paper was discussed by the authors of

Balakrishnan et al. 2026
; see
Kennedy et al. 2020
and
Liu et al. 2020b
.
Since no uniformly consistent estimators of
\psi
exist in model

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{AL}}(\widehat{\theta})

(
Ritov and Bickel 1990
;
Robins and Ritov 1997
;
Ritov et al. 2014
)
, we, instead,
developed valid falsification tests of the following null hypothesis for

\psi
in the
monotone bias class
:

\mathcal{H}_{0}
:
The bias of the first-order DML estimator
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
of
\psi
is sufficiently small such that a standard Wald CI
centered at
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
has nominal coverage asymptotically.

The proposed tests are only falsification tests because, although valid under
\mathcal{H}_{0}
, they will have no power under many alternatives to
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. However, when a test rejects the null, it provides empirical evidence that the bias of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
is too large for the Wald CI to deliver valid inference. The test statistics used are based on the same second-order estimators that we have analyzed in this paper or their higher-order extensions
(
Robins et al. 2008
;
Robins et al. 2016
;
Liu et al. 2017
)
. For
\psi
belonging to the so-called mixed-bias classes (which includes the expected conditional covariance analyzed above)
(
Rotnitzky et al. 2021
)
, in
Liu et al. 2020a
and
Liu et al. 2024
, we showed that these tests are no longer valid under
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. However, these tests remain valid falsification tests of the so-called rate-double-robustness property, as defined in Remark
1
or Remark
7
. We note that the rate-double-robustness implies that
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is true. For this reason, complexity-reducing assumptions strong enough to imply rate-double-robustness are often made by investigators to justify the validity of their Wald CIs centering
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
. In our view, unlike the minimaxity of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
or of
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
, these falsification tests provide further empirical information even in the assumption-lean model
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{AL}}(\widehat{\theta})
, whenever they reject and thus can be of value to domain scientists for whom inference is important.

Appendix B
Discussion on the Scaled MSE Difference

In this section, we discuss why we choose the scaled MSE difference as the
criterion in Definition
1
and discuss the consequence of
using other scaling factors instead of
\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})
in
all the examples analyzed in the main text. To simplify the presentation, we
use
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
to denote a generic second-order estimator. Scaling the
MSE difference by the minimax rate of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
is similar to
scaling the difference by the MSE of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
. Below we consider
several other natural scaling choices.

First, we consider the unscaled MSE difference:
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})-\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})
. Without
any scaling, it is not difficult to see that for all the examples in the

monotone bias class
(Definition
2
), we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})-\mathsf{mse}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})\leq-\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})^{2}-\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})^{2}\right)\leq 0.

This is because all the terms related to the difference in variances vanish as

n\rightarrow\infty
and only the difference in biases may possibly remain.
We find such a comparison overly simplifying the difference between
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
and
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
because it does not take into account
the possible variance inflation when using
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
, a disadvantage
of
\widehat{\psi}_{2,n}
in particular in finite samples.

Second, we consider the MSE difference scaled by
\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})\equiv v/n
. Then by the same argument as in the proof of
Theorem
0
,
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}T_{1,n}+T_{2,n}\leq 0
. However, because the MSE difference is not further scaled by

\mathsf{bias}^{2}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})
,
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}T_{1,n}
can diverge to
-\infty
.

Finally, we consider the difference in MSEs, scaled by only
\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})^{2}
but not the variance. In this case, when

\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})^{2}=o(1/n)
, since
\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{2,n})-\mathsf{var}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})
often has a term of
order
k/n^{2}=o(1/n)
by the choice of
k=o(n)
, then the ratio
between the variance difference and
\mathsf{bias}(\widehat{\psi}_{1,n})^{2}

may diverge to
+\infty
. However, we want to emphasize that this will only
happen when the first-order DML estimator
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
has bias faster
than parametric rates. If this were true, bias alone cannot reflect the
statistical behavior of
\widehat{\psi}_{1,n}
due to the
O(1/n)
variance,
and thus this scaling choice is not very meaningful statistically.
