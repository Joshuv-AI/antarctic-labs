Adaptive fuzzy super-twisting sliding mode control with nonlinear active disturbance rejection for electromechanical stabilizer bars to improve vehicle roll stability

Tuan Anh Nguyen, Jinhao Liang

Abstract

Electromechanical stabilizer bars are commonly equipped in luxury vehicles to enhance rollover stability during aggressive steering maneuvers at high speeds. However, most existing studies simplify actuator and vehicle dynamics, while parametric uncertainties, model nonlinearities, and external disturbances are often insufficiently addressed. This paper proposes a robust adaptive control framework for an active anti-roll system with three main contributions. First, a comprehensive coupled dynamic model is established to explicitly capture the interaction between the electromechanical stabilizer-bar actuator and vehicle dynamics. Second, an integrated Super-Twisting Sliding Mode Control and Nonlinear Active Disturbance Rejection Control scheme is developed, in which the nonlinear extended state observer estimates and compensates for lumped disturbances and uncertainties, while the super-twisting mechanism provides robust motor current tracking. Third, an adaptive fuzzy gain-scheduling mechanism adjusts the control gain online according to vehicle operating conditions. The novelty therefore lies in the coordinated integration of coupled actuator–vehicle modeling, disturbance compensation, robust current tracking, and operating-condition-dependent gain adaptation within an electromechanical stabilizer-bar control framework. Numerical simulations demonstrate superior performance to the benchmark controllers in reducing vertical tire-force variations and improving vehicle dynamics. Under a J-turn maneuver at 100 km/h, the maximum motor-current tracking error is limited to 0.11 A, with an overshoot of 3.59% and a settling time of 0.17 s. These results demonstrate the effectiveness and potential of the proposed framework for automotive active control systems.

1 Introduction

Vehicle rollover accidents occur relatively infrequently; however, they often result in severe consequences, particularly for heavy-duty vehicles []. The causes of rollover are multifaceted, involving driving conditions, vehicle geometric parameters, and steering maneuvers []. Alrejjal et al. [] identified that weather conditions, road characteristics, and vehicle speed have a significant influence on rollover instability. Hassan et al. [] emphasized that tire–road adhesion and suspension system parameters play a crucial role in maintaining rollover stability during steering. A study conducted by Alrejjal and Ksaibati further demonstrated that crosswinds have a considerable impact on vehicle rollover behavior []. Xu et al. [] reported that steering maneuvers are a key factor in ensuring vehicle stability during high-speed cornering. Similarly, Xin et al. [] confirmed that aggressive steering at high speeds substantially increases the risk of rollover. Moreover, Tan et al. [] verified that vehicles with a high center of gravity, such as Sport Utility Vehicles (SUVs), are more prone to rollover under high-speed steering conditions.

In recent years, various controllable active safety systems have been introduced in modern vehicles to enhance stability during steering. Chen et al. [] investigated several advanced anti-roll strategies aimed at improving vehicle roll dynamics. Tan et al. [] reported that uneven load distribution can significantly increase the risk of vehicle rollover and subsequently proposed advanced control solutions for motor-in-wheel systems to mitigate this issue. Arslan and Sever [] designed a constrained Model Predictive Control (MPC) scheme based on quadratic programming to control an active steering system, thereby improving yaw rate response and vertical load transfer. A rollover prevention strategy based on steer-by-wire control using MPC with a barrier cost function was presented by Ataei et al. in []. Yao et al. [] proposed an H∞ control approach for active suspension systems to compensate rollover-induced moments, and simulation results demonstrated a significant reduction in roll angle compared with passive suspension systems. Qian et al. [] developed an integrated control framework combining MPC (to reduce steering angle) and Sliding Mode Control (SMC) (to redistribute yaw moment during braking) based on a simplified vehicle dynamic model, to enhance rollover stability. Furthermore, Wu et al. [] developed a Nash game-based control strategy grounded in Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) theory to coordinate steering and braking systems for improved rollover stability. Zhou et al. [] introduced a Kalman Filter (KF) integrated with a sliding-mode observer to estimate roll angle variations and designed a hierarchical MPC scheme with time-varying weights and constraints to enhance the control effectiveness of engine torque, differential braking, and active steering. Although the aforementioned approaches can improve rollover stability under certain operating conditions, they are often associated with high implementation costs, and rollover risks cannot be eliminated.

Equipping vehicles with stabilizer bars is an effective solution for enhancing rollover resistance. Nguyen et al. [] introduced two types of active stabilizer bars: hydraulic stabilizer bars and electromechanical stabilizer bars, both of which exhibit superior performance compared to conventional passive stabilizer bars. Muniandy et al. [] reported that active stabilizer bars are considerably more cost-effective than fully active suspension systems while still providing substantial performance improvements. In recent years, several studies have focused on the control of hydraulic stabilizer bars. Huang et al. [] presented a virtual functional testing framework for the hydraulic actuator of an active stabilizer bar. Although the dynamics of the hydraulic valve system were considered in their study, the structure of the control strategy was not explicitly addressed. An application of Proportional–Integral–Derivative (PID) control for controlling the hydraulic valve system within a stabilizer bar actuator was introduced in []. However, the proposed control scheme is relatively simple, lacks optimal design considerations, and does not rigorously address system stability. Dawei et al. [] conducted experimental validation of a fuzzy PID controller designed for hydraulic stabilizer bar control. Nevertheless, the proposed fuzzy algorithm employs fixed membership functions and rule bases, resulting in limited adaptability. Nemeth and Varga [] proposed a hierarchical Linear Parameter-Varying (LPV) control approach to regulate the performance of a hydraulic stabilizer bar under actuator current saturation. In addition, several fuzzy control strategies have recently been developed to enhance rollover stability during high-speed steering by considering both road holding and ride comfort [,]. Despite their effectiveness under certain conditions, these approaches often suffer from performance degradation due to non-optimized or non-adaptive control structures.

The structure of hydraulic stabilizer bar systems is relatively bulky and complex, and the influence of time delay remains unavoidable. To overcome these limitations, electromechanical stabilizer bars have been increasingly adopted in high-end vehicles as an alternative to hydraulic stabilizer bars. A representative configuration of an electromechanical stabilizer bar system implemented in the Audi AQ7 was introduced by Sagewka et al. []. Buma et al. [] developed an electrical actuator model capable of delivering a maximum torque of up to 665 Nm; however, the corresponding control system architecture was not fully described, which limits its reproducibility and practical accessibility. Kong et al. [] proposed a hierarchical control approach consisting of an SMC algorithm for computing the target active anti-roll torque at the upper layer, a fuzzy logic algorithm for torque distribution, and a PI controller for regulating the actuator output torque. Dawei et al. [] developed a coordinated control strategy for integrated active steering and electromechanical stabilizer bar systems based on fuzzy PID and SMC techniques. Nevertheless, the proposed fuzzy control structure is relatively simple and lacks adaptability, while the actuator dynamics are not discussed in sufficient detail. Anafjeh et al. [] compared the performance of three controllers, including Linear Quadratic Gaussian (LQG), PI, and MPC combined with a KF, for controlling an electromechanical stabilizer bar system applied to railway vehicles. Simulation results indicated that the PI controller produced unstable lateral acceleration responses, whereas the LQG controller suffered from excessive overshoot. More recently, Nguyen [] proposed an SMC-based approach with a sliding surface constructed using an expanded sigmoid function tuned by a dynamic fuzzy mechanism. Although the output of the fuzzy algorithm was optimized through a scaling gain based on a performance index function, its adaptability was still not rigorously guaranteed.

Several extended applications of SMC have recently been developed to enhance vehicle rollover stability. Liu et al. [] proposed a fixed-time adaptive SMC approach based on an improved super-twisting observer to enhance yaw and roll stability. Huang et al. [] introduced an adaptive SMC scheme that incorporates adjustable barrier functions to enhance vehicle rollover stability during steering. Another application of Super-Twisting Sliding Mode Control (STSMC) for active steering systems was reported by Chen et al. []. In addition, several hybrid control frameworks combining Active Disturbance Rejection Control (ADRC) with nonlinear MPC [], as well as ADRC integrated with SMC [,], have demonstrated high effectiveness in controlling electromechanical systems subjected to disturbances and uncertainties.

Some similarity exists in the mathematical modeling of vehicle steering dynamics because the same fundamental vehicle dynamics are required to describe steering maneuvers. However, the control objectives and methodologies are substantially different. Nguyen [] developed a fuzzy control strategy for a hydraulic stabilizer bar, demonstrating the effectiveness of fuzzy control in improving vehicle rollover stability. More recently [], proposed a nonlinear ADRC-based sliding-mode controller for an Electric Power Assisted Steering (EPAS) system to enhance steering performance by estimating disturbances. Although these studies provide useful insights, they focus on different actuators, mathematical models, and control objectives. In contrast, the present work addresses the control of an electromechanical stabilizer bar by explicitly considering the electromechanical actuator dynamics and their coupling with the full vehicle dynamics. Furthermore, an Adaptive Fuzzy STSMC integrated with Nonlinear ADRC is developed to improve robustness, disturbance rejection, and adaptability under uncertain operating conditions.

Recent studies have also investigated coordinated chassis control for improving the dynamic performance of electric vehicles. In [], a decentralized multi-agent framework integrating active front steering and active suspension was developed using distributed model predictive control, achieving improved lateral and vertical stability while considering ride comfort and roll safety. More recently [], integrated torque vectoring and active suspension for distributed-drive electric vehicles using a multi-agent framework that combines distributed model predictive control and game theory, demonstrating improved vehicle dynamic performance while maintaining ride comfort. These studies demonstrate the potential of coordinated chassis-control systems; however, robust roll control using electromechanical stabilizer bars, while explicitly accounting for actuator electromechanical dynamics and uncertainties, remains comparatively underexplored.

Recent developments in super-twisting-based control have demonstrated its effectiveness in addressing robustness and chattering problems across nonlinear and uncertain systems. Rsetam et al. [] applied super-twisting integral SMC to a flexible-joint robot, combining the robustness of integral sliding mode with the continuous super-twisting action to improve trajectory tracking and reduce chattering. More recently, a model-based smooth STSMC was developed for nonlinear cancer chemotherapy models subject to parametric uncertainties and external disturbances, achieving smoother control action and improved disturbance rejection compared with conventional STSMC []. For permanent magnet synchronous motors, Zheng et al. [] integrated super-twisting integral terminal SMC with an extreme learning machine, where the latter compensates for lumped disturbances and relaxes the disturbance-bound requirement of the controller. These studies illustrate the evolution of super-twisting techniques toward smoother control, faster convergence, and enhanced disturbance compensation in nonlinear systems.

Observer-based disturbance rejection has also evolved toward greater robustness to increasingly complex uncertainties. Rsetam et al. [] combined a cascaded ESO with SMC for an underactuated flexible-joint robot, using the observer to estimate unmeasured states and lumped disturbances while reducing noise amplification and peaking effects. Subsequently, an adaptive ESO with error-dependent observer gains was incorporated into the ADRC for an electric furnace to improve disturbance estimation and alleviate high-gain and noise-sensitivity problems []. This adaptive observer concept was further applied to a nonlinear steer-by-wire system with communication delays, in which the observer estimates unmeasured states and compound disturbances, including parameter uncertainties, nonlinearities, external disturbances, and delay effects []. More recently, a generalized proportional-integral observer was integrated with continuous SMC for networked servo systems to estimate unmatched disturbances arising from communication delays, and its feasibility was further demonstrated experimentally []. These developments show a clear progression from state and disturbance estimation toward adaptive and delay-aware observer structures integrated with robust control.

Recent studies have brought these robust-control and disturbance-rejection concepts closer to electromechanical stabilizer bar applications. A detailed coupled vehicle–actuator model with Fixed-time SMC (FxTSMC) was developed in [], demonstrating the benefit of explicitly accounting for electric anti-roll-bar dynamics rather than assuming ideal force generation. A disturbance-rejection-enhanced smooth SMC framework was subsequently proposed in [], where an LESO and a fuzzy-tuned sigmoid switching function were employed to improve disturbance rejection, control smoothness, and adaptability. In [], nonlinear ADRC was integrated with fuzzy-enhanced finite-time SMC, using an NESO to estimate uncertainties and external disturbances while adaptively adjusting the switching gain. More recently, FxTSMC and ADRC were combined within a comprehensive nonlinear electromechanical stabilizer bar model to exploit the complementary advantages of rapid convergence and active disturbance compensation []. Collectively, these studies indicate a transition from standalone robust control toward integrated observer-based and adaptive control architectures. Nevertheless, the coordinated integration of nonlinear disturbance estimation, super-twisting robust tracking, and online adaptive gain scheduling within a coupled electric stabilizer bar–vehicle model remains insufficiently investigated.

1.1 Research gaps and key contributions

Overall, research on the control of electromechanical stabilizer bar systems remains relatively limited. Although notable progress has been achieved in rollover prevention through the application of electromechanical stabilizer bars, several critical issues remain unresolved. First, many existing studies neglect the coupling effects of vehicle dynamics, while the actuator dynamics are often described in an oversimplified manner. Second, the influence of adverse factors, including model nonlinearities, external disturbances, and parametric uncertainties, has not been sufficiently addressed during system modeling and control design. Third, commonly used control strategies, such as PID, LQR, and ADRC, still suffer from overshoot issues, whereas SMC–based approaches are prone to chattering phenomena. Finally, the mathematical structure of fuzzy-based tuning mechanisms is typically fixed, resulting in limited adaptability under varying operating conditions. These limitations constitute the remaining research gaps in the current literature.

These research gaps motivate the development of a control framework that can simultaneously account for the coupled vehicle–actuator dynamics, compensate for adverse effects, and maintain robust and adaptive performance under varying operating conditions. Accordingly, this paper proposes a robust control framework that integrates STSMC and Nonlinear ADRC (NADRC). The effects of adverse factors are estimated using a Nonlinear Extended State Observer (NESO) and compensated through the NADRC-based control law. At the same time, STSMC is employed to ensure robust tracking performance and mitigate overshoot and chattering. Additionally, the control parameters are adaptively updated via an adaptive fuzzy gain-scheduling mechanism, thereby enhancing system adaptability under varying operating conditions. Moreover, the interaction between vehicle and actuator dynamics is considered comprehensively, explicitly accounting for adverse effects. These aspects constitute the main contributions of this paper and distinguish the proposed approach from existing studies.

The coupled vehicle–actuator formulation plays an important role in the subsequent control design. Unlike a simplified model in which the commanded anti-roll force or torque is assumed to be generated instantaneously, the actual actuator force in the present model is determined by the motor’s electrical dynamics, the transmission dynamics, and the mechanical interactions among the output shaft, stabilizer bar, and vehicle suspension. Consequently, the required anti-roll action at the vehicle level must be achieved through accurate motor current regulation while accounting for the actuator’s dynamic response and uncertainties. This coupling establishes a physical interaction between the control voltage and motor current and the generated actuator force, and ultimately the vehicle roll response. Neglecting these intermediate dynamics may lead to an overestimation of achievable tracking and roll-stabilization performance because actuator lag, electromechanical dynamics, and parameter variations are not accounted for. Therefore, the coupled model provides a more appropriate basis for designing and evaluating the proposed robust controller under non-ideal operating conditions.

The fuzzy fine-tuning parameter (kf) is added to the control law (36) to improve the system’s adaptability under different operating conditions. An adaptive fuzzy gain-scheduling mechanism is developed based on a continuous-time Takagi–Sugeno fuzzy inference structure. The fuzzy system employs two inputs, denoted by xf1 (roll angle) and xf2 (vertical tire force change), which represent selected vehicle dynamic variables used to modulate the control gain. The first input is partitioned into five linguistic terms, while the second input is partitioned into seven linguistic terms, leading to a total of 35 fuzzy rules. Each fuzzy rule is assigned to one of four output groups indexed by k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4}, according to a predefined rule table.

The membership functions and fuzzy rules are designed to provide adequate coverage of the expected operating ranges while maintaining smooth transitions between adjacent fuzzy regions. For xf1, the five membership functions cover approximately −20–20° and consist of trapezoidal functions at the two outer regions, triangular functions in the intermediate regions, and a Gaussian function around the origin. For xf2, the seven membership functions cover approximately −5000–5000 N, with trapezoidal functions at the outer regions, triangular functions in the intermediate regions, and Gaussian functions around the central operating region. This arrangement provides broad coverage near the limits and finer resolution around the central operating region. The 35 input combinations are assigned to the four consequent groups according to a symmetric rule matrix that accounts for the signs and relative magnitudes of the two inputs. Thus, different combinations of roll response and vertical tire force variation activate distinct groups of consequences, while the overlapping membership functions provide smooth transitions between adjacent operating regions.

Remark 2. The STSMC, NADRC, and adaptive fuzzy techniques employed in this study are established control concepts; therefore, the contribution of the proposed framework does not arise from these individual techniques alone, but from their coordinated integration for the coupled electromechanical stabilizer bar–vehicle system. Specifically, the NADRC component employs the NESO to estimate and compensate for the lumped effects of external disturbances, model nonlinearities, and parameter uncertainties. The STSMC component provides robust motor-current tracking in the presence of residual uncertainties while alleviating the chattering associated with conventional first-order SMC. Meanwhile, the adaptive fuzzy mechanism adjusts the control gain online based on the vehicle roll angle and vertical tire force variation, allowing the control action to respond to changes in vehicle dynamic conditions rather than relying solely on fixed control parameters. Thus, rather than addressing disturbance estimation, robust tracking, and gain adaptation as separate control functions, the proposed framework coordinates these complementary functions within the coupled actuator–vehicle dynamics. This integration enables the simultaneous incorporation of disturbance compensation, robust actuator tracking, and operating-condition-dependent gain adaptation into the electromechanical stabilizer bar control framework, which constitutes the main methodological contribution of the proposed approach.

4 Simulation and discussion

A numerical simulation study is conducted to validate the performance of the proposed control scheme. The physical parameters in are selected to represent a realistic passenger-vehicle and electromechanical stabilizer bar system. The parameter selection considers physical plausibility, consistency among the vehicle, suspension, tire, and actuator subsystems, and suitability for evaluating the coupled vehicle–actuator dynamics under the investigated steering maneuvers.

Two investigation scenarios are considered (), corresponding to two steering maneuvers: a single lane-change steering maneuver (Case 1) and a J-turn steering maneuver (Case 2). The first case is conducted at a moderate vehicle speed of v1 = 70 km/h, whereas the second case is evaluated at a significantly higher speed of v2 = 100 km/h. Model nonlinearities and parametric uncertainties are considered in both scenarios. However, the influence of external disturbances (Td) differs between the two cases, as illustrated in .

For each scenario, the results obtained using the proposed control strategy (AFSTSMC) are compared with those achieved by several commonly used controllers, including ADRC, sSMC, and PID. It is worth noting that although the observer gains employed in ADRC are identical to those used in the proposed control, the observer structure and the corresponding control law are substantially different. Additionally, the control law designed for sSMC does not account for the effects of adverse factors.

4.3 Discussion

The simulation results obtained from both scenarios demonstrate that the proposed control strategy delivers superior performance compared with conventional controllers, effectively reducing observation errors, tracking errors, and variations in vertical tire forces. As a result, vehicle dynamic stability is significantly enhanced, thereby mitigating the risk of rollover. In addition, the influence of adverse factors (including parametric uncertainties, model nonlinearities, and external disturbances) is effectively compensated when the proposed approach controls the electromechanical stabilizer bars. Furthermore, chattering and phase lag are completely eliminated, while key transient performance indices (such as overshoot, settling time, and time to band) are significantly reduced.

Accurate current and actuator-force tracking obtained with the coupled model is particularly important because the commanded anti-roll action is not applied directly to the vehicle body. Instead, it is transmitted through the actuator’s electrical and mechanical dynamics before affecting the vehicle’s response. Therefore, the improvements in roll response and vertical tire forces demonstrate the controller’s effectiveness while accounting for non-ideal actuator dynamics, rather than assuming ideal force generation.

Despite the promising performance of the AFSTSMC approach, several limitations remain. First, the parameters of the TD and NESO are selected to ensure system stability but are not strictly optimized. Second, although the output of the fuzzy logic algorithm is adaptively updated, the membership function structure remains fixed, which may limit the achievable performance under a broader range of operating conditions. In addition, the proposed framework requires more online computational effort than conventional PID and ADRC controllers due to the additional NESO, nonlinear STSMC operations, and adaptive fuzzy gain-scheduling mechanism. Nevertheless, the online implementation mainly involves algebraic calculations, observer-state updates, nonlinear functions, and low-dimensional fuzzy inference, without requiring online numerical optimization or iterative solution procedures. Therefore, the computational burden is expected to remain moderate. However, since the present study is limited to numerical simulations, the actual execution time, feasible sampling frequency, and computational delay on automotive embedded hardware have not yet been experimentally quantified. Practical implementation may also be affected by sensor noise, sampling and communication delays, actuator saturation, and uncertainties beyond those considered in the simulations. Under severe operating conditions, these factors may reduce the accuracy of disturbance estimation and degrade tracking and roll-control performance. Finally, the assumptions adopted in the theoretical analysis, together with the simplifications introduced in the numerical model, may lead to discrepancies between the simulation results and actual system behavior. These issues are regarded as existing limitations and will be addressed through real-time implementation and experimental validation in future work.

5 Conclusion

This paper presents the design of a robust control strategy aimed at enhancing the performance of active stabilizer bar systems. The proposed control framework is constructed by integrating STSMC with NADRC to effectively compensate for external disturbances, model nonlinearities, and parametric uncertainties, while ensuring system stability and robustness. The control parameters are continuously updated through an adaptive fuzzy gain scheduling mechanism to enhance the system’s adaptability under varying operating conditions.

The numerical results demonstrate that the proposed control approach achieves superior performance compared to the benchmark controllers, resulting in significant reductions in tracking error, overshoot, settling time, and time to band. Moreover, vehicle stability is markedly improved by mitigating variations in vertical tire forces and enhancing overall vehicle dynamic behaviors. Nevertheless, the proposed control method still faces challenges related to optimizing control parameters and refining membership function structures. Future work will focus on experimental validation to further demonstrate the effectiveness and practicality of the proposed approach.
