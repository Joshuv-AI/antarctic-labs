Topology and Euler characteristics of tropical varieties
Scott Hiatt, Connor Simpson, Botong Wang, Chenxi Wu

Abstract
. We study Euler characteristics of tropical subvarieties of tropical abelian varieties. We prove that every H-regular subvariety, locally modeled on tropicalizations of sufficiently well-behaved very affine varieties, has nonnegative signed Euler characteristic. This gives a tropical analogue of a theorem of Green–Lazarsfeld for subvarieties of complex abelian varieties. The main input is a local vanishing theorem for H-regular tropical fans, which also yields a Lefschetz-type theorem for affine H-regular tropical varieties. We further show that the signed Euler characteristic inequality fails for general tropical subvarieties of tropical abelian varieties, and we construct a 3-dimensional tropical fan whose link is not homotopy equivalent to a bouquet of 2-spheres.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth subvariety of a complex abelian variety. A theorem of Green and Lazarsfeld (
[
19
]
) states that the Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
satisfies

(-1)^{\dim X}\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\geq 0.

In this paper, we seek an analog of the above inequality for subvarieties of tropical abelian varieties.
A tropical abelian variety is a real torus with a positive definite quadratic form. Our main result concerns H-regular subvarieties, which are locally isomorphic to tropicalizations of certain nice subvarieties of the affine torus (see Definitions
2.1
and
2.3
).
We prove that the analog of the theorem of Green–Lazarsfeld holds for such tropical subvarieties.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a tropical abelian variety (see Definition
2.9
) and let
\mathcal{X}
be a subvariety of
\mathcal{A}
of pure dimension
d
. If
\mathcal{X}
is H-regular, then

(-1)^{d}\chi(\mathcal{X})\geq 0.

By
[
21
, Example 17]
,
\chi(\mathcal{X})=\sum_{q}(-1)^{q}h_{0,q}(\mathcal{X})
, where
h_{0,q}(\mathcal{X})
are the tropical homology Hodge numbers. When
\mathcal{X}
is defined by a nice degeneration of subvarieties of abelian varieties, our theorem is a consequence of
[
19
]
and
[
21
, Theorem 1]
.

The proof of the above theorem (Section
7
) is motivated by the approach in
[
22
]
and uses Morse theory.
The Morse-theoretic argument rests upon the following local property of H-regular tropical varieties.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Theorem
2.8
)
Let
\mathcal{T}
be a
d
-dimensional H-regular tropical fan in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\ell:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a linear function that does not vanish on any of the rays of
\mathcal{T}
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{\leq 0}=\mathcal{T}\cap\{\ell\leq 0\}
be the nonpositive part of
\mathcal{T}
and let
L(\mathcal{T}_{\leq 0})
be its link. Then the reduced homology of
L(\mathcal{T}_{\leq 0})
satisfies

\widetilde{H}_{k}(L(\mathcal{T}_{\leq 0});\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for all }k\neq d-1.

To prove the above theorem, we generalize the argument of
[
20
]
(see also
[
27
]
). Instead of studying the mixed Hodge structures on the cohomology groups of an affine variety, we study a quasi-projective variety that admits a proper birational map to an affine variety.

As explained in Example
2.6
, the Bergman fan and the augmented Bergman fan of a matroid realizable over
\mathbb{C}
are both H-regular. Hence we obtain a different proof of a slightly weaker version of
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
, and thereby answer a question of Mikhalkin–Ziegler for matroids realizable over
\mathbb{C}
(see
[
25
]
).

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the Bergman fan of a rank
d+1
matroid realizable over
\mathbb{C}
, or the augmented Bergman fan of a rank
d
matroid realizable over
\mathbb{C}
. As in Theorem
2.8
, we have

\widetilde{H}_{k}(L(\mathcal{T}_{\leq 0});\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for all }k\neq d-1.

Another consequence of Theorem
2.8
is the following Lefschetz-type result, comparable to
[
1
, Theorem 7.4]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an affine H-regular tropical variety of dimension
d
. Let
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a general affine hyperplane, that is, an affine hyperplane that is not parallel to any 1-dimensional face of
\mathcal{X}
. Then

H_{k}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}\cap H;\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for all }k\neq d.

Finally, in Example
2.11
, we demonstrate that the inequality of Theorem
1.1
does not hold for arbitrary pure-dimensional subvarieties. In Section
8
, using similar ideas, we construct an irreducible subvariety
X\subset(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{6}
such that the link of its tropicalization is not homotopy equivalent to a bouquet of spheres, addressing a question in
[
20
, Remark 2.11]
.

Outline of the paper

In Section
2
, we introduce H-regular tropical varieties. Section
3
develops a notion of transversality in toric varieties and establishes its stability under toric modifications. In Section
4
, we prove a vanishing theorem for the top weights of cohomology using mixed Hodge modules and the decomposition theorem. Section
5
relates the cohomology of complements of divisors to the homology of their dual complexes.

In Section
6
, we prove the local vanishing theorem for H-regular tropical varieties.
In Section
7
, we deduce the main theorem on the signed Euler characteristic of tropical subvarieties of tropical abelian varieties via a Morse-theoretic argument. In Section
8
, we construct an explicit example of a non-H-regular affine tropical variety and show that it does not exhibit a vanishing property proved by Hacking for H-regular affine tropical varieties. Finally, in Section
9
, we discuss further questions and possible generalizations of our results.

2.
H-regular tropical varieties

The following condition was introduced by Hacking
[
20
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
U\subset(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
be a closed irreducible subvariety. We say that
U
is
H-regular
if there exists a smooth projective toric variety
X_{\Delta}
such that the closure
\overline{U}
of
U
in
X_{\Delta}
is smooth and intersects every torus orbit
O\subset X_{\Delta}
transversely. Moreover, for every torus orbit
O
, the intersection
\overline{U}\cap O
is either empty or connected.

If such an
X_{\Delta}
is fixed, we say that
U
is H-regular with respect to
X_{\Delta}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Condition (
*
) in
[
20
, Theorem 2.5]
only requires connectedness when the intersection
\overline{U}\cap O
is positive dimensional. We need the stronger condition because we will pass to new compactifications by performing weighted blowups along toric boundary strata.

Definition 2.3
.

By a
tropical fan
, we mean a weighted rational polyhedral fan satisfying the balancing condition. In particular, tropicalizations of subvarieties of complex affine tori are tropical fans.

A tropical fan
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
H-regular
if
\mathcal{T}=\mathrm{trop}(U)
for some H-regular subvariety
U\subset(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
. More generally, a tropical variety
\mathcal{X}
is called
H-regular
if, for every vertex
P
of
\mathcal{X}
, there is an integral-affine isomorphism from a small neighborhood of
P
in
\mathcal{X}
to a neighborhood of the origin in some H-regular tropical fan.

Remark 2.4
.

If
U
is an H-regular subvariety of
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
with respect to a smooth projective toric variety
X_{\Delta}
, then
\mathrm{trop}(U)
is the union of all cones
\sigma\in\Delta
such that
O_{\sigma}\cap\overline{U}\neq\emptyset

[
23
, Proposition 6.4.7]
. Moreover, if
\overline{U}
meets a torus orbit
O_{\sigma}\subset X_{\Delta}
of complementary dimension, then H-regularity implies that
\overline{U}\cap O_{\sigma}
consists of a single point and that the intersection is transverse. It follows that
\sigma
is a top-dimensional cone of
\mathrm{trop}(U)
with weight one. Therefore, every top-dimensional cone of an H-regular tropical fan has weight one
[
23
, Proposition 6.7.2]
.

Remark 2.5
.

Because of the connectedness assumption, H-regularity is stronger than being Schön. Hacking’s result is sometimes cited as if it implied that, for every Schön subvariety
U
,

\widetilde{H}_{k}(L(\mathrm{trop}(U)),\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for all }k\neq\dim U-1.

However, we are not aware of any proof of this claim in this generality.

Let

Example 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a one-dimensional tropical fan in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and assume that every ray of
\mathcal{T}
has weight one. If
\mathcal{T}
is not contained in a one-dimensional linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then
\mathcal{T}
is H-regular.

Indeed, choose a complete unimodular fan
\Delta

\mathbb{R}^{2}
that contains
\mathcal{T}
as a subfan. The tropical fan
\mathcal{T}
determines a divisor class

on the smooth projective toric surface

. Since the cone of effective divisors of

is generated by the torus-invariant divisors, and since

has nonnegative intersection with every torus-invariant divisor, the divisor class

is nef. Hence

is base-point-free.

Moreover, the self-intersection number

is equal to the stable self-intersection number of
\mathcal{T}
, which is strictly positive by the assumption that
\mathcal{T}
is not contained in a one-dimensional linear subspace. Hence

Thus, if

denotes the restriction of such a general member to the dense torus

is H-regular with respect to
X_{\Delta}
, and

\mathcal{T}=\mathrm{trop}(U).

\mathcal{T}

3.
Transversality in toric varieties

Let
\sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone of codimension
m=n-\dim\sigma
. Then
X_{\sigma}
is an affine normal toric variety with dense torus
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
. The minimal-dimensional
T
-orbit of
X_{\sigma}
, denoted
O
, is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m}
. After choosing a noncanonical splitting of the ambient lattice, we may write

X_{\sigma}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m}\times X_{\sigma^{\prime}},

where
\sigma^{\prime}
denotes the same cone
\sigma
, viewed as a
full-dimensional cone in
\operatorname{span}(\sigma)
. The minimal torus orbit of
X_{\sigma^{\prime}}
is a point, which we denote by
P
.

Definition 3.1
.

With the notation above, let
Y\subset X_{\sigma}
be a locally closed analytic subvariety of pure codimension
k
. We say that
Y
intersects
O

transversely
if, for any point
x\in Y\cap O
, there exist an analytic neighborhood
U_{x}
of
x
in
X_{\sigma}
, an analytic neighborhood
U_{P}
of
P
in
X_{\sigma^{\prime}}
, and an isomorphism of analytic varieties

U_{x}\simeq B^{m}\times U_{P}

(1)

such that:

(1)

B^{m}
is a complex ball of dimension
m
;

(2)

under the isomorphism (
1
), we have

Y\cap U_{x}\simeq B^{m-k}\times U_{P}

for a closed
(m-k)
-dimensional complex ball
B^{m-k}\subset B^{m}
;

(3)

the product stratification induced by the torus-orbit stratification
of
X_{\sigma^{\prime}}
agrees, via (
1
), with the restriction of the torus-orbit
stratification of
X_{\sigma}
to
U_{x}
.

Remark 3.2
.

By definition, if
Y
intersects
O
transversely and

Y\cap O\neq\emptyset
, then
k\leq m
. Equivalently, if

Y\subset X_{\sigma}
is a locally closed analytic subvariety of codimension larger than
m
, then
Y
intersects
O
transversely if and only if
Y\cap O=\emptyset
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
X
be a normal toric variety with dense torus
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
. For every torus orbit
O\subset X
, let
X_{\geq O}
be the unique affine open toric subvariety of
X
whose minimal orbit is
O
. Let

Y\subset X
be a closed analytic subvariety. We say that
Y
intersects all torus orbits of
X

transversely
if, for every torus orbit
O\subset X
, the subvariety
Y\cap X_{\geq O}
intersects
O
transversely in
X_{\geq O}
in the sense of Definition
3.1
.

Remark 3.4
.

When
O
is the dense torus orbit, the above transversality condition requires that
Y\cap O
is smooth. More generally, if
Y
intersects all torus orbits of
X
transversely, then
Y\cap O
is a closed complex submanifold of
O
for every torus orbit
O\subset X
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth toric variety. Then an equidimensional closed analytic subvariety
Y\subset X
intersects all torus orbits transversely if and only if
Y
is smooth and intersects every torus orbit
O\subset X
transversely in the usual sense, that is, for every
x\in Y\cap O
,

T_{x}Y+T_{x}O=T_{x}X.

Proof.

Since both conditions are local on
X
, we may assume that
X
is a smooth affine toric variety,

X=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-m}.

It suffices to check the equivalence near a point
x\in Y\cap O
, where

O=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m}\times\{0\}\subset(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-m}.

In this case both
U_{x}
and
U_{P}
are smooth. Hence the condition that
Y\cap U_{x}
is equal to
B^{m-k}\times U_{P}
under the product decomposition
U_{x}\simeq B^{m}\times U_{P}
implies that
Y
intersects
O
transversely in the usual sense.

Conversely, assume that
Y
intersects
O
transversely in the usual sense. We need to show that
Y
intersects
O
transversely in the sense of Definition
3.1
. Let
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-m}
be the coordinates of
\mathbb{C}^{n-m}
, so that the divisors
z_{i}=0
are the toric divisors of
\mathbb{C}^{n-m}
. We regard the
z_{i}
as holomorphic functions on
X=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-m}
. Since
Y
is smooth, for every point
x\in Y\cap O
, there exist local holomorphic functions
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}
near
x
such that, schematically,

Y=\{y_{1}=\cdots=y_{k}=0\}

near
x
. Since
Y
intersects
O
transversely in the usual sense, the cotangent vectors

dz_{1},\ldots,dz_{n-m},dy_{1},\ldots,dy_{k}

are linearly independent at
x
. Therefore, by the implicit function theorem, we can find local holomorphic functions

y_{k+1},\ldots,y_{m}
near
x
such that

y_{1},\ldots,y_{m},z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-m}

form local coordinates of
X
at
x
. These coordinates define a product decomposition

U_{x}\simeq B^{m}\times U_{P}.

In
B^{m}
, choose

B^{m-k}=\{y_{1}=\cdots=y_{k}=0\}.

Then the two conditions in Definition
3.1
are satisfied. Therefore
Y
intersects
O
transversely in the sense of Definition
3.1
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
X
be a normal toric variety, and let
Y\subset X
be an irreducible closed analytic subvariety that intersects every torus orbit transversely in the sense of Definition
3.3
. Let

\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X

be a weighted blowup along a toric subvariety of
X
. Then

\pi^{-1}(Y)
is irreducible
and intersects every torus orbit of
\widetilde{X}
transversely.

Proof.

The statement is local on
X
. To prove the claim near a point

\widetilde{x}\in\pi^{-1}(Y)
, we may replace
X
by the smallest affine open toric subvariety containing
x\coloneqq\pi(\widetilde{x})
. Thus, we may assume that
X
is affine and that
x
lies in the minimal torus orbit of
X
. With the notation of Definition
3.1
, we have

X=X_{\sigma}\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m}\times X_{\sigma^{\prime}},

where
X_{\sigma^{\prime}}
is an affine toric variety whose minimal torus orbit is a point
P
.

By Definition
3.1
, there exists a neighborhood
U_{x}
of
x
in
X
such that

U_{x}\simeq B^{m}\times U_{P},

where
U_{P}
is a neighborhood of
P
in
X_{\sigma^{\prime}}
, and such that this isomorphism restricts to

Y\cap U_{x}\simeq B^{m-k}\times U_{P}.

Moreover, for any closed toric subvariety
Z\subset X
, the same product decomposition gives

Z\cap U_{x}\simeq B^{m}\times(U_{P}\cap Z^{\prime}),

where
Z^{\prime}\subset X_{\sigma^{\prime}}
is a closed toric subvariety.

Both claims now follow from the fact that toric weighted blowups commute with products with a smooth factor. More precisely, the isomorphism
U_{x}\simeq B^{m}\times U_{P}
induces

\Blowup_{Z}X\cap\pi^{-1}(U_{x})\simeq\Blowup_{Z\cap U_{x}}U_{x}\simeq B^{m}\times\Blowup_{Z^{\prime}\cap U_{P}}U_{P},

where the weighted blowups are taken with the corresponding toric weights.
Thus,

\pi^{-1}(Y\cap U_{x})\simeq B^{m-k}\times\Blowup_{Z^{\prime}\cap U_{P}}U_{P}.

This shows that
\pi^{-1}(Y)
is locally irreducible. Since
\pi
is an isomorphism over the dense torus
T
, we regard
Y\cap T
as an open subset of
\pi^{-1}(Y)
. The local product description shows that every neighborhood of a point of
\pi^{-1}(Y)
meets
Y\cap T
. Hence

Y\cap T
is dense in
\pi^{-1}(Y)
. Since
Y\cap T
is irreducible and
\pi^{-1}(Y)
is locally irreducible, it follows that
\pi^{-1}(Y)
is irreducible.

Finally, the same local product description gives the product structures
required in Definition
3.1
for the torus orbits of
\widetilde{X}
. Hence

\pi^{-1}(Y)
intersects every torus orbit of
\widetilde{X}
transversely.
∎

Lemma 3.7

X_{\widetilde{\Delta}}
intersects every torus orbit transversely. Moreover, the intersection of
\overline{U}^{\widetilde{\Delta}}
with any toric subvariety of
X_{\widetilde{\Delta}}
is rationally smooth.

Proof.

Since each star subdivision corresponds locally to a toric weighted blowup, the first statement follows from Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
.

For the second statement, denote
\overline{U}^{\widetilde{\Delta}}
by
Y
and let
Z\subset X_{\widetilde{\Delta}}
be a toric subvariety.
Since
Y\subset X_{\widetilde{\Delta}}
intersects every torus orbit transversely, for any
x\in Y\cap Z
, there exists a neighborhood
U_{x}
of
x
in
X_{\widetilde{\Delta}}
such that

U_{x}\simeq B^{m}\times U_{P},

where
U_{P}
is an open neighborhood in a simplicial affine toric variety
X_{\sigma^{\prime}}
. Furthermore, this isomorphism restricts to

Y\cap U_{x}\simeq B^{m-k}\times U_{P}

and

Z\cap U_{x}\simeq B^{m}\times(U_{P}\cap Z^{\prime})

where
Z^{\prime}\subset X_{\sigma^{\prime}}
is a toric subvariety. Hence

(Y\cap Z)\cap U_{x}\simeq B_{m-k}\times(U_{P}\cap Z^{\prime}).

Since
X_{\sigma^{\prime}}
is simplicial, the toric variety
Z^{\prime}
is also simplicial, hence rationally smooth. It follows that
B^{m-k}\times(U_{P}\cap Z^{\prime})
is rationally smooth. Therefore
Y\cap Z
is rationally smooth.
∎

4.
Vanishing in the weight filtration

In this section, we prove the following vanishing theorem. Although our main interest is the case where

X
is rationally smooth and the vanishing of the top weight piece
Gr_{2d}^{W}
, we state the result in greater generality for independent interest.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X
be an irreducible complex algebraic variety of dimension
d
. Assume there exists a proper birational map
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
where
Y
is an affine variety. Then

Gr_{2d}^{W}H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{and}\quad Gr_{2d-1}^{W}H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})=0

(2)

for any
i\neq d
.

Proof.

The proof relies on Saito’s theory of mixed Hodge modules
[
28
]
. An overview of the main definitions and results can be found in
[
29
]
. First, for
\mathcal{M}\in D^{b}\MHM(X)
, we define

p(\mathcal{M})=\min\{p:Gr^{F}_{p}DR(\mathcal{M})\not\simeq 0\}.

Since the Hodge filtration on
H^{i}(X,DR(\mathcal{M}))
is dependent on the Hodge filtration on the de Rham complex
DR(\mathcal{M})
, we have

Gr^{F}_{p}H^{i}(X,DR(\mathcal{M}))=0\quad\text{for $p<p(\mathcal{M})$ and $i\in\mathbb{Z}.$}

Note that we are using the Hodge filtration as an increasing filtration, in line with Saito’s convention, rather than a decreasing filtration.
Thus, the
(p,q)
-graded component of the mixed Hodge structure

H^{i}(X,DR(\mathcal{M}))\cong H^{i}(X,\rat(\mathcal{M}))\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}

is trivial whenever
p<p(\mathcal{M})
or
q<p(\mathcal{M})
. In particular, the
(p,q)
-graded component is trivial if
p+q<2p(\mathcal{M})
. With Saito’s convention that the Hodge filtration is increasing, the
(p,q)
-graded component has weight
-(p+q)
. Thus, for
j\geq-2p(\mathcal{M})+1
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,

Gr^{W}_{j}H^{i}(X,\rat(\mathcal{M}))=0.

(3)

Since
\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d]\in D^{b}\MHM(X)
and
IC^{H}_{X}\in\HM_{X}(X,d)
, by
[
31
, Corollary 0.3]
we have

p(\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d])=p(IC^{H}_{X})=-d=-\dim X,

and

Gr^{F}_{-d}DR(\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d])\simeq Gr^{F}_{-d}DR(IC^{H}_{X}).

By
[
28
, Proposition 1.14]
, we have the identity

Gr^{W}_{d}H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d])=IC^{H}_{X}.

Therefore, there exists
\mathcal{N}\in D^{b}\MHM(X)
such that we have the following distinguished triangle in
D^{b}\MHM(X),

\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d]\longrightarrow\displaystyle IC^{H}_{X}\xrightarrow{\;+1\;}.

This triangle induces a long exact sequence of mixed Hodge structures

\cdots\rightarrow H^{i-d}(X,\rat(\mathcal{N}))\rightarrow H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow IH^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{i+1-d}(X,\rat(\mathcal{N}))\rightarrow\cdots,

where the intersection cohomology groups
IH^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})=H^{i-d}(X,IC_{X}(\mathbb{Q}))
. Because the map

Gr^{F}_{-d}DR(\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d])\rightarrow Gr^{F}_{-d}DR(IC^{H}_{X})

is a quasi-isomorphism, we must have

Gr^{F}_{p}DR(\mathcal{N})\simeq 0\quad\text{for $p<-d+1$.}

This implies

Gr^{W}_{j}H^{i-d}(X,\rat(\mathcal{N}))=0\quad\text{for $j\geq 2d-1$ and $i\in\mathbb{Z}.$}

Thus by the long exact sequence, we have

Gr^{W}_{j}H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})\cong Gr^{W}_{j}IH^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q}),

(4)

for
j\geq 2d-1
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Therefore, it suffices to prove that

Gr_{2d}^{W}IH^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{and}\quad Gr_{2d-1}^{W}IH^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for $i\neq d$.}

Since
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
is proper and birational, by the decomposition theorem (
[
28
, Theorem 1.12]
), we have

\pi_{+}IC^{H}_{X}\simeq IC_{X}^{Y}\oplus\bigoplus_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{M}_{\ell}[-\ell],

where each
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}
is a pure Hodge module whose support is a proper closed subset of
Y
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{M}_{\ell}=0
for all but finitely many
\ell
.
Taking cohomology, we have

\displaystyle IH^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle\cong IH^{i}(Y,\mathbb{Q})\oplus\bigoplus_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{i-d}\big(Y,\rat(\mathcal{M}_{\ell}[-\ell])\big)

\displaystyle\cong IH^{i}(Y,\mathbb{Q})\oplus\bigoplus_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{i-d-\ell}\big(Y,\rat(\mathcal{M}_{\ell})\big).

By
[
30
, Proposition 2.6]
, we have

Gr^{F}_{p}DR(\mathcal{M}_{\ell})=0\quad\text{for $p<-d+1$ and $\ell\in\mathbb{Z}$.}

Thus,

p(\mathcal{M}_{\ell})\geq-d+1\quad\text{for $\ell\in\mathbb{Z}$.}

By (
3
),

Gr^{W}_{j}H^{i}(Y,\rat(\mathcal{M}_{\ell}))=0\quad\text{for $j\geq 2d-1$ and $i,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}$.}

Therefore,

Gr^{W}_{j}IH^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})\cong Gr^{W}_{j}IH^{i}(Y,\mathbb{Q}),\quad\text{for $j\geq 2d-1$ and $i\in\mathbb{Z}$.}

(5)

Since
Y
is affine, by Artin’s vanishing theorem (see
[
7
, Théorème 4.1.1]
), we have

IH^{i}(Y,\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for $i>d$.}

By the following lemma, we have

Gr^{W}_{j}IH^{i}(Y,\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for $i<d$ and $j\geq 2d-1$.}

Therefore, we have

Gr_{2d}^{W}IH^{i}(Y,\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{and}\quad Gr_{2d-1}^{W}IH^{i}(Y,\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for $i\neq d$.}

Thus, by (
4
) and (
5
), we obtain the desired vanishing result (
2
).
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
Y
be an irreducible complex quasi-projective variety. Then,

Gr^{W}_{j}IH^{i}(Y,\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for $j>2i$.}

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{Y}\to Y
be a resolution of singularities. Then, by the decomposition theorem, the Hodge structure
IH^{i}(Y,\mathbb{Q})
is a direct summand of
H^{i}(\widetilde{Y},\mathbb{Q})
. Since
\widetilde{Y}
is a smooth quasi-projective variety, it follows from
[
12
, Corollaire 3.2.15]
that

Gr^{W}_{j}H^{i}(\widetilde{Y},\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for $j>2i$.}

Since
IH^{i}(Y,\mathbb{Q})
is a direct summand of
H^{i}(\widetilde{Y},\mathbb{Q})
as Hodge structures, the same vanishing holds for the weight graded components of
IH^{i}(Y,\mathbb{Q})
.
∎

5.
The cohomology of the dual complex of divisors

The following theorem is a generalization of
[
20
, Theorem 3.1]
, where the projective variety is smooth and the divisors have simple normal crossings.
In the rationally smooth setting, instead of using differential forms as in loc. cit., we use constructible sheaves to deduce that the Gysin sequence computes the cohomology of a rationally smooth quasi-projective variety. For more details about the Gysin sequence, see
[
15
, Example 5.3]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a rationally smooth projective variety of dimension
d
. Let
D_{1},\dots,D_{l}
be a collection of divisors on
X
such that for any
I\subset\{1,\dots,l\}
,
D_{I}\coloneqq\bigcap_{j\in I}D_{j}
is connected, rationally smooth and of codimension
|I|
(when
|I|>d
,
D_{I}
is empty). Let
K
be the dual complex of
D=D_{1}\cup\cdots\cup D_{l}
. Denote
X\setminus D
by
U
. Then,

\widetilde{H}_{i}(K,\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathrm{Gr}_{2d}^{W}H^{2d-(i+1)}(U,\mathbb{Q}).

Proof.

For
1\leq k\leq d
, let
D^{(k)}=\bigcup_{|I|=k}D_{I}
, and let
\widetilde{D}^{(k)}
be the normalization of
D^{(k)}
. Denote by
\iota_{k}:\widetilde{D}^{(k)}\to X
the composition of the normalization map
\widetilde{D}^{(k)}\to{D}^{(k)}
and the closed embedding
D^{(k)}\to X
.
Let
j:U\to X
be the open embedding. Then, we have a Čech-type exact sequence

0\to j_{!}\mathbb{Q}_{U}\to\mathbb{Q}_{X}\to\iota_{1*}(\mathbb{Q}_{\widetilde{D}^{(1)}})\to\iota_{2*}(\mathbb{Q}_{\widetilde{D}^{(2)}})\to\cdots\to\iota_{d*}(\mathbb{Q}_{\widetilde{D}^{(d)}})\to 0.

Then the hypercohomology spectral sequence satisfies

E_{1}^{pq}=H^{q}\big(X,\iota_{p*}(\mathbb{Q}_{\widetilde{D}^{(p)}})\big)\cong\bigoplus_{|I|=p}H^{q}(D_{I},\mathbb{Q})\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X,j_{!}\mathbb{Q}_{U})\cong H^{p+q}_{c}(U,\mathbb{Q}),

where we set
\widetilde{D}^{(0)}=D_{\emptyset}=X
, and
\iota_{0}:X\to X
is the identity map. Moreover, all the arrows in the spectral sequence are maps of Hodge structures. Hence, it degenerates at the
E_{2}
-page (see
[
12
, Lemme (3.2.10)]
). Denote the
p
-th cohomology of the complex

0\to H^{q}(X,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{q}(\widetilde{D}^{(1)},\mathbb{Q})\to\cdots\to H^{q}(\widetilde{D}^{(d-1)})\to H^{q}(\widetilde{D}^{(d)},\mathbb{Q})\to 0

by
N^{p,q}
, where
H^{q}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is in degree 0. Then, the degeneration of the above spectral sequence at the
E_{2}
-page implies that

H_{c}^{k}(U,\mathbb{Q})\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=k}N^{p,q}.

Since
H^{q}(D_{I},\mathbb{Q})
is a pure Hodge structure of weight
q
and

H^{q}(\widetilde{D}^{(i)},\mathbb{Q})\cong\bigoplus_{|I|=i}H^{q}({D}_{I},\mathbb{Q}),

N^{p,q}
is a pure Hodge structure of weight
q
. Hence,

\mathrm{Gr}_{2d-m}^{W}H_{c}^{2d-k}(U,\mathbb{Q})\cong N^{m-k,2d-m}.

By Poincaré duality,

H^{2d-k}(U,\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{k}_{c}(U,\mathbb{Q})^{\vee}.

Since the Poincaré duality is compatible with mixed Hodge structures, we have

\mathrm{Gr}_{2d-m}^{W}H^{2d-k}(U,\mathbb{Q})\cong\big(\mathrm{Gr}_{m}^{W}H^{k}_{c}(U,\mathbb{Q})\big)^{\vee}.

Therefore,

\mathrm{Gr}_{2d}^{W}H^{2d-(i+1)}(U,\mathbb{Q})\cong\big(\mathrm{Gr}_{0}^{W}H^{(i+1)}_{c}(U,\mathbb{Q})\big)^{\vee}\cong\big(N^{i+1,0}\big)^{\vee}.

By definition,
\big(N^{i+1,0}\big)^{\vee}
is isomorphic to the
(i+1)
-th homology of the complex

0\to H^{0}(\widetilde{D}^{(d)},\mathbb{Q})^{\vee}\to H^{0}(\widetilde{D}^{(d-1)},\mathbb{Q})^{\vee}\to\cdots\to H^{0}(\widetilde{D}^{(1)},\mathbb{Q})^{\vee}\to H^{0}(X,\mathbb{Q})^{\vee}\to 0

where
H^{0}(X,\mathbb{Q})^{\vee}
is in degree 0. The above complex is isomorphic to

0\to\bigoplus_{|I|=d}H^{0}({D}_{I},\mathbb{Q})^{\vee}\to\bigoplus_{|I|=d-1}H^{0}({D}_{I},\mathbb{Q})^{\vee}\to\cdots\\
\to\bigoplus_{|I|=1}H^{0}({D}_{I},\mathbb{Q})^{\vee}\to H^{0}(X,\mathbb{Q})^{\vee}\to 0.

Since
H^{0}({D}_{I},\mathbb{Q})^{\vee}\cong\mathbb{Q}
for all
I
with
|I|\leq d
, the
(i+1)
-th homology of the above complex is equal to the
i
-th reduced homology of the dual complex
K
.
∎

Combining Propositions
4.1
and
5.1
, we immediately have the following corollary.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
X
,
D
and
U
be as in Proposition
5.1
. Assume further that
U
admits a proper birational map to an affine variety. Then, the dual complex
K
of
D
satisfies

\widetilde{H}_{i}(K,\mathbb{Q})=0

unless
i=d-1
.

6.
Vanishing result for
H
-regular tropical varieties

In this section, we prove Theorem

. Given an H-regular tropical variety
\mathcal{T}
, by definition it is the tropicalization of an H-regular subvariety
U\subset(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}

where
\overline{U}
is projective, and

admits a proper birational map to an affine variety.
We then show that
L(\mathcal{T}_{\leq 0})
is homotopy equivalent to the dual complex of the divisor

2.8
then follows from Corollary
5.2
.

The following result will help us to construct appropriate (partial) compactifications of

.

.

Let
\Delta
be a complete simplicial fan in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
\ell:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}

, and let

be the subfan of

of

The proof of Proposition

\displaystyle C(\mathcal{S}\setminus\sigma_{\mathcal{S}})

C(\Sigma^{\leq})

\Sigma

Let
\sigma\in\Sigma

If

\sigma

Let

Proposition 6.4
.

Under the above notations, we have a homotopy equivalence

where
\sigma_{\mathcal{T}}
is a rational simplicial cone associated to
\mathcal{T}
as in Proposition

.

Proof.

Since our construction satisfies Proposition
6.1
, the inclusion

L(\mathcal{T}_{\leq 0})\subset L(\mathcal{T}\setminus\sigma_{\mathcal{T}})

is a homotopy equivalence. Hence, we have proved the second homotopy equivalence.

Let
\Sigma^{\prime}
be the subfan of
\Delta^{\prime}
whose support is equal to
\mathcal{T}
. Then,
\Sigma^{\prime}
is a refinement of
\Sigma
. Denote by
\Sigma^{c}
the subfan of
\Sigma^{\prime}
generated by all the rays not contained in
\sigma_{\mathcal{T}}
. First, we notice that the rays of
\Sigma^{c}
correspond to the irreducible components of the boundary divisor
\overline{U}\setminus U^{+}
. It follows from
[
32
, Proposition 3.9]
that as simplicial complexes
K\cong L(|\Sigma^{c}|)

where as the link of a simplicial fan,
L(|\Sigma^{c}|)
is naturally a simplicial complex.

Furthermore, notice that there is a strong deformation retract from
L(|\Sigma^{\prime}|\setminus\sigma_{\mathcal{T}})
to
L(|\Sigma^{c}|)
. In fact, both
L(|\Sigma^{\prime}|\cap\sigma_{\mathcal{T}})
and
L(\Sigma^{c})
are simplicial subcomplexes of
L(|\Sigma^{\prime}|)
, and they are generated by vertices that form a partition of the vertices of
L(\Delta)
. Given any simplex
\sigma
and a face
\tau
of
\sigma
, there is a standard linear retract of
\sigma
to
\tau
. Using such a retract, we can construct a deformation retract from

Thus, the first homotopy equivalence follows.
∎

Finally, we can deduce Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
2.8
.

The desired statement follows from Proposition
6.4
and the vanishing property (

).

Morse theory arguments

We now prove Theorem

via an argument similar to the one in
[

]
(see also
[

]
).

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a
d
-dimensional tropical subvariety of an
n
-dimensional tropical abelian variety
\mathcal{A}
. For every vertex
v
in
\mathcal{X}
, we say that a linear function
\ell
defined on some neighborhood of
v
is
Morse
if it is not constant on any
1
-simplex containing
v
. Given any Morse linear function
\ell
near a vertex
v
, let

L_{v}
be the link of
\mathcal{X}
at
v
, and let

L^{\leq}_{\ell,v}=L_{v}\cap\{x\in U_{v}\mid\ell(x)\leq\ell(v)\},

where
U_{v}
is a neighborhood of
v
which contains
L_{v}
and on which
\ell
is defined. Theorem
1.1
will be deduced from the following theorem.

Theorem 7.1
.

Under the above notations, suppose that for all vertices
v
of
\mathcal{X}
, and any local Morse linear function
\ell
near
v
,

\widetilde{H}^{i}\big(L^{\leq}_{\ell,v},\mathbb{Q}\big)=0\quad\text{for all}\;\;i\neq d-1,

(7)

or more generally,

(-1)^{d-1}\big(\chi(L^{\leq}_{\ell,v})-1\big)\geq 0.

Then we have

(-1)^{d}\chi(\mathcal{X})\geq 0.

Remark 7.2
.

When
d=1
, the assumption of Theorem
7.1
is trivially true, and the conclusion is valid as well because
if
G
is a finite graph with positive number of edges and without leaf, then
\chi(G)\leq 0
.

Let
f:X\to S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
be a circle-valued function from a topological space
X
. Given any
a\in\mathbb{R}
and interval
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
with
a\leq b<a+1
, by abusing notation, we also use them to denote their images in
S^{1}
, and we denote
f^{-1}(a)
and
f^{-1}([a,b])
by
X_{a}
and
X_{[a,b]}
, respectively. The following is a basic fact in circle-valued Morse theory. We include the proof for reader’s convenience.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
X
be a finite CW complex. Let
f:X\to S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
be a circle-valued function such that the preimage of any point or interval has finite-dimensional cohomology over
\mathbb{Q}
. Assume that away from a finite set
W\subset S^{1}
,
f
is a fiber bundle.
Then,

\chi(X)=\sum_{c\in W}\chi\big(X_{[c-\epsilon,c+\epsilon]},X_{c-\epsilon}\big)

for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, where the relative Euler characteristic is defined by

\chi\big(X_{[c-\epsilon,c+\epsilon]},X_{c-\epsilon}\big)=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}\dim H^{i}\big(X_{[c-\epsilon,c+\epsilon]},X_{c-\epsilon};\mathbb{Q}\big).

Proof.

If
W=\emptyset
, then
X
is a fiber bundle over
S^{1}
. The Euler characteristic of a fiber bundle is equal to the product of the Euler characteristics of the fiber and the base. Hence,
\chi(X)=0
, and the desired equation holds. For the rest of the proof, we assume that
W\neq\emptyset
.

Fix
b\in S^{1}\setminus W
, and sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
. Then, by the long exact sequence of relative cohomology, we have

\chi(X)=\chi\big(X,X_{[b-\epsilon,b+\epsilon]}\big)+\chi\big(X_{[b-\epsilon,b+\epsilon]}\big).

(8)

By excision and the long exact sequence of relative cohomology, we have

\displaystyle\chi\big(X,X_{[b-\epsilon,b+\epsilon]}\big)

\displaystyle=\chi\big(X_{[b+\epsilon,b+1-\epsilon]},X_{b+\epsilon}\cup X_{b+1-\epsilon}\big)

(9)

\displaystyle=\chi\big(X_{[b+\epsilon,b+1-\epsilon]},X_{b+\epsilon}\big)-\chi\big(X_{b+1-\epsilon}\big).

Since
f
is a fiber bundle over
[b-\epsilon,b+\epsilon]
, we have

X_{[b-\epsilon,b+\epsilon]}\simeq X_{b-\epsilon}=X_{b+1-\epsilon}.

Thus, combining equations (
8
) and (
9
), we have

\chi(X)=\chi\big(X_{[b+\epsilon,b+1-\epsilon]},X_{b+\epsilon}\big).

(10)

By excision and the long exact sequences of relative cohomology, for any
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
with
a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}<a_{1}+1
, we have

\displaystyle\chi\big(X_{[a_{1},a_{3}]},X_{a_{1}}\big)

\displaystyle=\chi\big(X_{[a_{1},a_{3}]},X_{[a_{1},a_{2}]}\big)+\chi\big(X_{[a_{1},a_{2}]},X_{a_{1}}\big)

\displaystyle=\chi\big(X_{[a_{2},a_{3}]},X_{a_{2}}\big)+\chi\big(X_{[a_{1},a_{2}]},X_{a_{1}}\big).

Therefore, breaking
[b+\epsilon,b+1-\epsilon]
into smaller closed intervals containing exactly one point of
W
each, and using the fact that
f
is a fiber bundle away from
W
, we have

\chi\big(X_{[b+\epsilon,b+1-\epsilon]},X_{b+\epsilon}\big)=\sum_{c\in W}\chi\big(X_{[c-\epsilon,c+\epsilon]},X_{c-\epsilon}\big).

(11)

Now, the desired equation follows from (
10
) and (
11
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

Since the statement is topological, we may identify the tropical abelian variety
\mathcal{A}
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Given a
d
-dimensional tropical cycle
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{A}
, we may choose a general real Lie group map
f_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathcal{A}\to S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
such that all vertices of
\mathcal{X}
have distinct images. In particular, any local lift of
f_{\mathcal{A}}
is a Morse function. Denote the restriction by
f=f_{\mathcal{A}}|_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathcal{X}\to S^{1}
.

Let
V\subset\mathcal{X}
be the set of vertices, and let
W=f(V)
. Then,
f
is obviously a fiber bundle over
S^{1}\setminus W
. Given
c\in W
, we consider the pair
(\mathcal{X}_{[c-\epsilon,c+\epsilon]},\mathcal{X}_{c-\epsilon})
for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
. By our assumption, there is a unique
v\in V
such that

. Notice that for
1\gg\delta\gg\epsilon>0
, the inclusions

\mathcal{X}_{c-\epsilon}\subset\big(\mathcal{X}_{[c-\epsilon,c+\epsilon]}\setminus B^{\circ}_{\delta}(v)\big)\cup\mathcal{X}_{c-\epsilon}

and

\mathcal{X}_{[c-\epsilon,c+\epsilon]}\cap B_{\delta}(v)\subset\mathcal{X}\cap B_{\delta}(v)

are both deformation retracts, where
B_{\delta}(v)\subset\mathcal{A}
is the ball centered at
v
with radius
\delta
and
B_{\delta}^{\circ}(v)
is its interior. Therefore, using excision we can deduce that for any
i\geq 0
,

\displaystyle H^{i}\big(\mathcal{X}_{[c-\epsilon,c+\epsilon]},\mathcal{X}_{c-\epsilon};\mathbb{Q}\big)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle H^{i}\big(\mathcal{X}_{[c-\epsilon,c+\epsilon]},\big(\mathcal{X}_{[c-\epsilon,c+\epsilon]}\setminus B_{\delta}^{\circ}(v)\big)\cup\mathcal{X}_{c-\epsilon};\mathbb{Q}\big)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle H^{i}\big(\mathcal{X}_{[c-\epsilon,c+\epsilon]}\cap B_{\delta}(v),\big(\mathcal{X}_{[c-\epsilon,c+\epsilon]}\big)\cap\partial B_{\delta}(v)\big)\cup\big(\mathcal{X}_{c-\epsilon}\cap B_{\delta}(v)\big);\mathbb{Q}\big)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle H^{i}\big(\mathcal{X}\cap B_{\delta}(v),\mathcal{X}_{[c-0.5,c+\epsilon]}\cap\partial B_{\delta}(v);\mathbb{Q}\big)

where
\partial B_{\delta}(v)
is the boundary of
B_{\delta}(v)
. Therefore, by the long exact sequence of relative cohomology,

\displaystyle\chi\big(\mathcal{X}_{[c-\epsilon,c+\epsilon]},\mathcal{X}_{c-\epsilon}\big)

\displaystyle=\chi\big(\mathcal{X}\cap B_{\delta}(v),\mathcal{X}_{[c-0.5,c+\epsilon]}\cap\partial B_{\delta}(v)\big)

\displaystyle=\chi(\mathcal{X}\cap B_{\delta}(v))-\chi\big(\mathcal{X}_{[c-0.5,c+\epsilon]}\cap\partial B_{\delta}(v)\big).

Notice that
\mathcal{X}\cap B_{\delta}(v)
is contractible and
\mathcal{X}_{[c-0.5,c+\epsilon]}\cap\partial B_{\delta}(v)
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathcal{X}_{[c-0.5,c]}\cap\partial B_{\delta}(v)
, which can be regarded as the half link

. Therefore,

\chi\big(\mathcal{X}_{[c-\epsilon,c+\epsilon]},\mathcal{X}_{c-\epsilon}\big)=1-\chi(L^{\leq}_{\ell,v}).

Thus, by (
7
), we have

(-1)^{d}\chi\big(\mathcal{X}_{[c-\epsilon,c+\epsilon]},\mathcal{X}_{c-\epsilon}\big)\geq 0

for any
c\in W
. Now, we can apply Lemma

and conclude that

(-1)^{d}\chi(\mathcal{X})\geq 0.\qed

.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
\ell:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a linear function such that the restriction of
\ell
to any one-dimensional face of
\mathcal{X}
is non-constant, and assume that
H=\{\ell=c\}
for some constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
. For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and any closed interval
[a,b]
, set

\mathcal{X}_{t}=\mathcal{X}\cap\{\ell=t\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{X}_{[a,b]}=\mathcal{X}\cap\{a\leq\ell\leq b\}.

Assume that
\{\ell=b\}
contains exactly one vertex
v
of
\mathcal{X}
. Denote the link of
\mathcal{X}
at
v
by
L_{v}
and let

L^{\leq}_{\ell,v}=L_{v}\cap\{x\in U_{v}\mid\ell(x)\leq\ell(v)\}.

Then, for any
a<b
and
1\gg\delta\gg\epsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[a,\,b]},\mathcal{X}_{[a,\,b-\epsilon]};\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle\cong H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[a,\,b]}\cap B_{\delta}(v),\mathcal{X}_{[a,\,b-\epsilon]}\cap B_{\delta}(v);\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle\cong H_{k}(\mathcal{X}\cap B_{\delta}(v),\mathcal{X}_{[a,\,b-\epsilon]}\cap\partial B_{\delta}(v);\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle\cong\widetilde{H}_{k-1}(\mathcal{X}_{[a,\,b-\epsilon]}\cap\partial B_{\delta}(v);\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle\cong\widetilde{H}_{k-1}(L^{\leq}_{\ell,v_{i}};\mathbb{Q}),

where the first isomorphism follows from the same arguments as the proof of Theorem
7.1
, the third isomorphism follows from
\mathcal{X}\cap B_{\delta}(v)
being contractible.

In general, if
\{\ell=b\}
contains more than one vertex of
\mathcal{X}
, then the above formula can be replaced by

H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[a,\,b]},\mathcal{X}_{[a,\,b-\epsilon]};\mathbb{Q})\cong\bigoplus_{i}\widetilde{H}_{k-1}(L^{\leq}_{\ell,v_{i}};\mathbb{Q}),

where the sum is over all vertices
v_{i}
of
\mathcal{X}
in
\{\ell=b\}
.

In any case, it follows from the H-regular assumption of
\mathcal{X}
and Theorem
2.8
that

\widetilde{H}_{k-1}(L^{\leq}_{\ell,v_{i}};\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for all }k\neq d.

Hence for any
a<b
and sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
,

H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[a,\,b]},\mathcal{X}_{[a,\,b-\epsilon]};\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for all }k\neq d.

Now, let
a_{1},\dots,a_{\mu},b_{1},\dots,b_{\nu}
be the values of
\ell
on the set of vertices of
\mathcal{X}
that are different from
c
such that

a_{\mu}<\cdots a_{1}<c<b_{1}<\cdots<b_{\nu}.

Then, for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, the inclusion
\mathcal{X}_{c}\subset\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{1}-\epsilon]}
is a homotopy equivalence. In particular,

H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{1}]},\mathcal{X}_{c};\mathbb{Q})\cong H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{1}]},\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{1}-\epsilon]};\mathbb{Q})

which vanishes except when
k=d
.

Similarly, for any
1\leq i\leq\nu-1
, the inclusion
\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{i}]}\subset\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{i+1}-\epsilon]}
is also a homotopy equivalence. Hence,

H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{i+1}]},\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{i}]};\mathbb{Q})\cong H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{i+1}]},\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{i+1}-\epsilon]};\mathbb{Q})

which also vanishes except when
k=d
.

Consider the long exact sequence on relative homology groups

\cdots\to H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{i}]},\mathcal{X}_{c};\mathbb{Q})\to H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{i+1}]},\mathcal{X}_{c};\mathbb{Q})\\
\to H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{i+1}]},\mathcal{X}_{[c,b_{i}]};\mathbb{Q})\to H_{k-1}(\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{i}]},\mathcal{X}_{c};\mathbb{Q})\to\cdots.

We have proved that both
H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{1}]},\mathcal{X}_{c};\mathbb{Q})
and
H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{i+1}]},\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{i}]};\mathbb{Q})
vanish except when

. Thus, using induction on

, we can conclude that for any

H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{i}]},\mathcal{X}_{c};\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for all }k\neq d.

In particular,

H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[c,\,b_{\nu}]},\mathcal{X}_{c};\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for all }k\neq d.

(12)

Replacing
\ell
by

and applying similar arguments, we can inductively show that

H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[a_{i},\,b_{\nu}]},\mathcal{X}_{[c,b_{\nu}]};\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for all }k\neq d.

In particular,

H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[a_{\mu},\,b_{\nu}]},\mathcal{X}_{[c,b_{\nu}]};\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for all }k\neq d.

(13)

By (

) and (

), and the long exact sequence of relative homology groups, we conclude that

H_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{[a_{\mu},\,b_{\nu}]},\mathcal{X}_{c};\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{for all }k\neq d.

Since

has no vertex in

, the inclusion

8.
An example of a non-H-regular tropical variety

In this section, we give an example of a tropical fan which is not H-regular. By
[
20
, Theorem 2.5]
, if
\mathcal{T}
is an H-regular tropical fan of dimension
d
, then the reduced homology of its link satisfies

\widetilde{H}^{i}(L(\mathcal{T}),\mathbb{Q})=0\quad\text{when $i\neq d-1$}.

In
[
20
, Remark 2.11]
, Hacking suggested that there should exist tropical fans violating the above vanishing property, but noted that he was not aware of any such example. Here we provide one, motivated by our earlier Example
2.11
.

Let
V
be a 3-dimensional affine subspace in
\mathbb{C}^{6}
in general position. Let
U=V\cap(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{6}
. Then

\mathrm{trop}(U)=\bigcup_{1\leq i<j<k\leq 7}\Cone(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k})

where
e_{1}=(-1,0,\dots,0),\dots,e_{6}=(0,\dots,0,-1),e_{7}=(1,\dots,1)
are vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{6}
and
\Cone(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k})
denotes the cone generated by the vectors
e_{i},e_{j},e_{k}
.

Consider the projection

\displaystyle\pi:(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{6}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{5}

\displaystyle(z_{1},\dots,z_{6})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\left(z_{1}z_{4},\,z_{2}z_{5},\,z_{3}z_{6},\,\frac{z_{5}}{z_{2}^{2}},\,\frac{z_{3}}{z_{1}^{3}}\right).

Then the tropicalization of
\pi
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{trop}(\pi):\mathbb{R}^{6}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{5}

\displaystyle(x_{1},\dots,x_{6})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\bigl(x_{1}+x_{4},\,x_{2}+x_{5},\,x_{3}+x_{6},\,x_{2}-2x_{1},\,x_{3}-3x_{1}\bigr).

By
[
23
, Corollary 3.2.13]
, we have

\mathrm{trop}\big(\overline{\pi(U)}\big)=\mathrm{trop}(\pi)\big(\mathrm{trop}(U)\big)

where
\overline{\pi(U)}
denotes the closure of
\pi(U)
in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{5}
.

Notice that
v_{1}=(1,2,3,0,0,0)
and
v_{2}=(0,0,0,1,2,3)
are vectors in
\mathrm{trop}(U)
. Moreover,
\mathrm{trop}(\pi)
maps
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
into the same vector

\overline{v}\coloneqq(1,2,3,0,0).

We can also check that away from the two rays generated by
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
, respectively,

\mathrm{trop}(\pi):\mathrm{trop}(U)\to\mathrm{trop}(\pi)\big(\mathrm{trop}(U)\big)

is a bijection. More precisely, we have a bijection

\mathrm{trop}(U)\setminus\big(\Cone(v_{1})\cup\Cone(v_{2})\big)\cong\mathrm{trop}(\pi)\big(\mathrm{trop}(U)\big)\setminus\Cone(\overline{v}).

Lemma 8.1
.

The link of
\mathrm{trop}(U)
at the origin is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of 10 2-spheres. In particular,

H_{i}\big(L(\mathrm{trop}(U)),\mathbb{Z}\big)\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&i=0;\\
\mathbb{Z}^{10}&i=3;\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The link of
\mathrm{trop}(U)
is homeomorphic to the 2-skeleton of a 6-simplex. Hence, its homology is isomorphic to the homology of the simplicial chain complex of the 6-simplex truncated at degree 2, which implies the description of the homology groups as in the lemma.

We can also deduce the description in the lemma from the well-known fact that the
k
-skeleton of an
n
-simplex is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of
\binom{n}{k+1}

k
-spheres.
∎

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
L
be the link of
\mathrm{trop}\big(\overline{\pi(U)}\big)
at the origin. Then

H_{i}(L,\mathbb{Z})\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&i=0,1;\\
\mathbb{Z}^{10}&i=2;\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

By definition,
\mathrm{trop}\big(\overline{\pi(U)}\big)
is homeomorphic to
\mathrm{trop}(U)
with two distinct points identified. Gluing two distinct points in a connected finite CW-complex is homotopy equivalent to taking wedge sum with a circle. Hence, it increases the first Betti number by 1, while leaving all other Betti numbers unchanged.
∎

Remark 8.3
.

Both the example in this section and Example
2.11
are not locally connected in codimension 1, in particular, not
\mathbb{Q}
-normal as defined in
[
4
]
. We do not expect Theorem
1.1
or
[
20
, Theorem 2.5]
to generalize to tropical varieties that are locally connected in codimension 1. However, we do not have any counterexamples.

9.
Further discussion and questions

Generalizations to arbitrary matroids

A geometric proof of a statement about realizable matroids often suggests a purely combinatorial proof, which would extend the statement to arbitrary matroids. This leads to the following question.

Question 9.1
.

Can the conclusion of Corollary
1.3
be proved for arbitrary matroids using combinatorial Hodge theory?

In the current proof of Corollary
1.3
, we use refinements of (augmented) Bergman fans and the decomposition theorem. Both of these ingredients have combinatorial counterparts, namely
[
6
]
and
[
3
]
. However, it is not clear to us how to formulate a combinatorial analogue of Artin vanishing for perverse sheaves on an affine variety.

Euler characteristics of
\Omega^{p}

As discussed after Theorem
1.1
, the Euler characteristic of a tropical variety
\mathcal{X}
can be expressed in terms of the tropical homology
Hodge numbers introduced in
[
21
]
,

\chi(\mathcal{X})=\sum_{q}(-1)^{q}h_{0,q}(\mathcal{X}).

On the other hand, the generic vanishing theorem (
[
19
, Theorem 2]
) implies that for a
d
-dimensional smooth subvariety
X
of an abelian variety,

(-1)^{d-p}\chi(X,\Omega^{p})=\sum_{q}(-1)^{d-p-q}h^{p,q}(X)\geq 0.

This suggests the following tropical analogue.

Question 9.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a
d
-dimensional H-regular subvariety of a tropical
abelian variety. Do the tropical homology Hodge numbers of
\mathcal{X}
satisfy

(-1)^{d-p}\,\chi_{p}(\mathcal{X})\coloneqq\sum_{q}(-1)^{d-p-q}h_{p,q}(\mathcal{X})\geq 0

for all
p
?

Fourier–Mukai transformations

Recently, Ghosh and Shokrieh
[
18
]
constructed a cohomological Fourier–Mukai transform for tropical abelian varieties. Since the theorem of Green–Lazarsfeld that

(-1)^{\dim X}\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\geq 0

for smooth closed subvarieties
X
of complex abelian varieties can be proved using Fourier–Mukai transformations
[
26
]
, it is natural to ask whether there is a tropical counterpart of this approach.

Question 9.3
.

Can the cohomological Fourier–Mukai transform of
[
18
]
be used to give an alternative proof of Theorem
1.1
? More generally, can it be used to identify classes of cycles in tropical abelian varieties with nonnegative signed Euler characteristic?

Non-abelian analogues

Some non-abelian analogues of the Green–Lazarsfeld theorem, and of its topological counterpart, the Franecki–Kapranov theorem
[
17
]
, have recently been established (see
[
14
,
5
,
13
]
). In the classical setting, non-abelian analogues of subvarieties of abelian varieties include
smooth projective varieties with negative sectional curvature, and more generally smooth projective varieties with large fundamental group, such as compact smooth Shimura varieties. It would be interesting to develop a corresponding theory in tropical geometry.

Question 9.4
.

Is there a natural class of “negatively curved” or “hyperbolic” tropical varieties, or of tropical varieties with large fundamental group, that is closed under Cartesian products and passage to smooth subvarieties? Moreover, does every H-regular tropical variety in such a class have nonnegative signed Euler characteristic?

Chern classes

For a compact complex manifold, the topological Euler characteristic is equal to the integral of the top Chern class, or equivalently to the degree of the zero-dimensional homology Chern class. Thus the other Chern classes may be viewed as refinements of the Euler characteristic.

The Chern classes of smooth subvarieties of abelian varieties satisfy signed positivity properties. For example, if
X
is a smooth subvariety of a complex abelian variety, then

\Omega_{X}^{1}
is globally generated.
Consequently, the signed Chern classes of
X
are effective. More precisely, if
c_{k}(X)
denotes the
k
-th cohomological Chern class of
T_{X}
, then
(-1)^{k}c_{k}(X)
is effective.
