 Foreword
This standard presents a set of optional metadata fields for managed digital objects within the Nuclear
Security Enterprise (NSE) and provides a deeper look at data representation in metadata by looking at
the representation of 1) Records Management required metadata, and 2) common representations of
technical/scientific data. Metadata standardization is a critical enabler for effectively sharing data,
documents, and other digital objects between NSE sites, and for tracing the digital thread at the object
level. Standardization is necessary for both schemas and vocabularies, meaning that both field standards
and value standards must be specified. This document serves as a complementary field standard,
recommending an optional set of fields that should be uniformly built for all managed digital objects
within the NSE. This document specifically focuses on extending the shared discovery layer defined in
the first white paper by introducing additional descriptive and data representation fields that improve
cross-site search and interpretation.

1.0 Purpose & Scope
The purpose of metadata is to enhance the discoverability, usability, management, and archiving of the
resources it describes. High-quality descriptive metadata not only improves a user’s search and retrieval
experience but also aids in maintaining long-term access to and understanding of resources. Our first
white paper, Metadata Standards for the NSE: Core Fields, provided a recommendation for the
establishment of several fields which should be considered “core” and required for every NSE-managed
digital object, regardless of the nature of the digital object. This document builds on that work by
recommending a metadata field standard for general use within the Nuclear Security Enterprise (NSE)
and its affiliated laboratories to capture non-core, optional metadata. While this standard can apply to
any managed digital objects that advance the NSE’s mission or are stored in repositories intended to
collect resources created by or on behalf of the NSE, these fields are not considered core or required. A
shared standard is nonetheless recommended, as having these optional fields compliant with a shared
NSE standard would significantly increase the interoperability of the data in a cross-site context.

The primary objective of this work is to improve cross-repository discoverability of NSE-managed digital
objects by defining a shared set of extended metadata fields that complement the core discovery layer
defined in the previous white paper.

In addition to optional fields, this recommendation white paper also discusses data representation
fields. These topics are combined in this white paper for two reasons: 1) neither type of field (optional or
data representation) is considered to be a core or required field type, and 2) the standardization of both
optional and data representation fields would benefit the enterprise, but may reach a level of granularity
that would be neither necessary or practical to recommend with the same focus as the core fields
presented in Metadata Standards for the NSE: Core Fields.

The core fields defined in the first white paper establish the minimum metadata required to enable
discovery of any NSE-managed digital object across repositories. The extended fields recommended in
this document build upon that foundation by providing additional descriptive context that improves
users’ ability to evaluate and interpret resources once they have been discovered. This work intentionally
focuses on metadata required to support discovery and interoperability across repositories and does not
attempt to define institution-specific governance, policy, or implementation requirements.

1.2 Defining Extended & Data Representation Fields
This white paper addresses two distinct categories of metadata fields that extend beyond the core fields
established in our first white paper:

Extended (Descriptive) Fields provide additional context about a digital object’s content, provenance,
and characteristics. While the core fields are required for every NSE-managed digital object, extended
fields are optional but highly recommended when applicable. These fields enhance discoverability across
all digital object types – whether documents, datasets, images, software, or multimedia – support more

Page 10 of 33

nuanced search and retrieval and provide richer context for users working with diverse research outputs.
Examples include detailed descriptions, subject keywords, format specifications, and relationship
information. Standardizing these optional fields across the NSE ensures that when sites choose to
capture this information, they do so in a consistent, interoperable manner.

Data Representation Fields serve a fundamentally different purpose from descriptive metadata. Rather
than describing a dataset as a whole, they capture specific data elements contained within it. In scientific
and technical contexts, these fields represent variables such as experimental parameters, simulation
inputs, engineering measurements, or material properties. Users in these domains often need to
discover datasets based on the data they contain, not only on descriptive metadata. While NSE sites
currently maintain independent repositories, the consistent representation of these data elements
within local systems can support more advanced discovery capabilities over time. Establishing shared
approaches to representing scientific and technical data helps lay the groundwork for future cross-
repository search and supports advanced digital engineering workflows.

In addition to scientific and technical data, locally implemented data representation fields may also
include elements required for records management, though these are not always relevant for cross-site
discovery and may remain local to institutional systems. These requirements are context dependent and
are typically enforced at the institutional or system level. Although this information must be captured to
meet compliance obligations, it is not necessarily shared across sites. Institutions may choose whether
and how to include these elements in their local repositories based on needs and system capabilities.
The focus of this white paper remains on metadata that supports cross-site discovery and
interoperability, rather than on metadata required solely for local governance or compliance.

Together, the core, extended, and data representation fields form a layered metadata model in which
each layer builds on the previous to support progressively richer discovery and interpretation.

Unlike the core fields recommended in the first white paper, which may be considered required, both
extended and data representation fields should be considered optional. However, when sites choose to
implement these fields, aligning with the standards outlined in this document will significantly enhance
interoperability and support future cross-site capabilities within the NSE.

1.2.1 Extended Fields
Extended metadata fields provide an extension mechanism to be used when the core terms are not
sufficient. The MWG conceptualized these fields based on Qualified Dublin Core, which uses additional
metadata fields to broaden the usefulness of the original Dublin Core schema consisting of only fifteen
metadata terms. While the core fields recommended in the previous white paper were designed as a
fundamental set of metadata fields that should be included with every digital object managed by the
NSE, the purpose of extended fields is to provide additional descriptive metadata that sites can choose
to use if applicable.

Via both white papers, the MWG is recommending the use of both core and extended fields to provide
descriptive metadata for sharing data that is useful to scientists and engineers across the NSE. This
specific type of metadata provides context about the resource’s content. This helps facilitate discovery
within a repository and allows end users to make informed decisions about digital objects of all types.

1.2.2 Data Representation Fields
The challenge with data representation fields is their inherent domain specificity. While extended
descriptive fields like “Description” or “Subject” apply broadly across content types, data representation
fields often require deep domain knowledge to define and implement. A neutronics dataset requires
different data representation fields than a materials science dataset or a computational fluid dynamics
simulation output. However, where domain-specific standards already exist, such as the NeXus format
for neutron and x-ray science, or established manufacturing terminology, these should be adopted to
maximize interoperability both within the NSE and with the broader scientific community.

Section 4.0 of this white paper provides more detailed recommendations for standardizing data
representation fields, recognizing that this remains an evolving area where NSE-wide coordination can
significantly enhance cross-site collaboration and data sharing.

1.3 Implementation Considerations
This document is intentionally presented as a white paper describing recommended metadata field
standards for the exchange of data. The following considerations outline possible governance
approaches that could support the adoption and coordination of these recommendations across the

Page 12 of 33

METADATA STANDARDS METADATA WORKING GROUP MAY 2026

NSE. These considerations are intended to illustrate how the recommendations described in this paper
could be supported and coordinated across sites, rather than to prescribe specific policy requirements.

Site-specific control boards: An extended field standard would be supported at the site level by a local
control mechanism. Such a control board would exist to ensure or advocate for the presence of field
standards in each site’s managed repositories. It would also be responsible for concurring mappings
between local repository fields and the extended field standard, should a mapping approach be selected.
Although not formally part of this white paper, such a control board could eventually manage semantic
resources like ontologies and controlled vocabularies, including creating, merging, and expanding
semantic models.

NSE-wide metadata control group: With a control board at each site, the NSE-wide metadata control
group would facilitate cross-site collaboration and communication, providing implementation-level
support to sites. This group should comprise representatives from each site-specific control board,
enabling them to confer on challenging implementation issues and make decisions that ensure a
consistent approach across the NSE as new challenges arise.

Funder requirements: Offices within the National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) that fund
work at various NSE sites play a crucial role in setting priorities for the work they support. To ensure the
adoption of a field standard, adoption of the standard could be encouraged through programmatic
expectations passed down from the NNSA offices to the sites. This would empower site-specific control
boards to prioritize compliance with the standard and ensure its appropriate implementation in each
managed repository.

2.0 Prior and Current Efforts
Efforts to standardize metadata within the NSE have been ongoing for decades, driven by the need to
support national security through effective data and information management. The unique nature of this
subject matter and the sensitivity of the content have posed challenges to standardization. Initial efforts
began in the 1990s with the Nuclear Weapons Information Group (NWIG), which developed a set of
mandatory and recommended metadata elements in the Nuclear Weapons Information Group Metadata
Exchange Guide. Although the timeline for NWIG’s efforts is unclear, recent initiatives have continued to
build on this legacy.

2.1 LLNL Current Work
Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) has undertaken metadata standardization efforts across
multiple repositories and systems, with particular focus within the Strategic Deterrence (SD) directorate.
The primary repository for text-based documents is DSpace, which implements a custom metadata
element set based largely on qualified Dublin Core. This foundation aligns well with the extended field
standards proposed in this white paper, as both approaches build upon Dublin Core’s proven framework
for descriptive metadata.

For datasets, LLNL maintains the Digital Archive (Darc), which houses scientific and technical datasets,
including those related to stockpile systems maintained and developed by the Laboratory. Additional
enterprise systems include the Enterprise Lifecycle Management (ELM) system, used for collaboration

Page 13 of 33

between design agencies like LLNL and production facilities across the NSE, housing design-related
documentation and technical specifications. Beyond these managed repositories, Livermore Computing
(LC) and the National Ignition Facility (NIF) generate and store substantial volumes of simulation and
experimental data, respectively.

LLNL has established an internal data governance working group focused on metadata standardization to
support an institutional data catalog initiative. This framework relies primarily on Dublin Core and DCAT
US 3.0 (the U.S. standard application profile for data catalogs), with extensions developed as necessary
for specific use cases. This approach mirrors the philosophy of this white paper: establish a core set of
standardized fields applicable across all digital object types, then extend judiciously when domain-
specific requirements demand it.

The adoption of NSE-wide metadata standards as proposed in this white paper series would significantly
benefit LLNL’s ongoing efforts by providing a consistent framework that extends beyond institutional
boundaries. Standardized extended fields would enable more robust cross-repository search within
LLNL’s institutional data catalog, while also supporting federated search capabilities across NSE sites.
When repositories at all three national laboratories implement the same extended field standards – even
as optional fields – the potential for meaningful cross-site discovery and data sharing increases
dramatically. This alignment would reduce the burden on individual sites to independently develop
metadata standards while ensuring that local implementations remain interoperable with enterprise-
wide initiatives.

2.2 LANL Current Work
The Online Vault/National Security Data Solution (OLV/NSDS) is the centralized institutional repository
for archival and research data at Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL). Built on a MarkLogic back-end
with a custom front-end, NSDS offers robust search capabilities and a user-friendly interface. Its
metadata creation process is highly customizable, allowing for flexibility that is essential for capturing
and researching raw data and reference materials unique to nuclear weapons design and engineering.
This versatility supports the alignment of extended metadata, facilitating easy standardization and
adaptation to the unique domains within the NSE.

To better support this functionality, the NSRC’s metadata standard was developed based on the NARA
Bulletin 2015-04 Metadata Guidance for the Transfer of Permanent Electronic Records, Appendix A, with
customization for local tracking and specialized requirements for data representation fields. The NSRC
further utilizes Resource Description Framework (RDF) to export terse triples (TTL) files for preservation
metadata and anticipated use of linked data applications. These triples are validated against a Shapes
Constraint Language (SHACL) schema accompanying the preservation metadata to ensure structural and
semantic consistency.

2.3 Sandia Current Work
Sandia National Laboratories (SNL) has a variety of approved record repositories with no current
standardized metadata standards applied broadly across them in any lab-wide effort. Metadata
standardization efforts have been made across Sandia’s permanent record digitization projects, to
include digitization of Corporate Archive records, and nuclear deterrent records digitized through the
Archive Data and Management (ADAM) project. Both digitization efforts have standardized descriptive

Page 14 of 33

metadata fields for entry based on the National Archives and Records Administration’s (NARA)
permanent record standards and store both digitized files and their associated metadata in Sandia’s
internal repository, Enterprise Information Management Services (EIMS) Filenet. Though SNL’s
digitization efforts have standardized metadata fields for entry, controlled vocabularies are not currently
being used, nor is the formatting consistent across field entries with free-text fields such as Author.
Legacy digitized records from the Corporate Archives collections were also not consistent with field
entries and formatting, and there is currently no effort to standardize these backlog digitized records.

Additionally, Sandia’s Corporate Archive was successfully able to stand-up an internal-only version of the
ArchivesSpace application in 2024. Collection information for both physical and digital collections
maintained in the Corporate Archive is in the process of being input into ArchivesSpace. Sandia is
currently working on a metadata standardization document as it applies to ArchivesSpace, also in
accordance with NARA’s descriptive metadata requirements for permanent records, but to be applied at
collection and series-level rather than as item-level descriptive metadata. Collection information being
loaded into ArchivesSpace is also successful in the implementation of broadly used controlled
vocabularies when feasible, such as the Library of Congress Subject Headings (LCSH) and the Library of
Congress Name Authority File (LCNAF). SNL has also used local sources such as phonebooks and
directories to create personalized controlled vocabularies relevant to the Lab. While Sandia has seen
early success in metadata standardization within the ArchivesSpace platform, it should be reiterated that
this applies only to a small set of records held by the Corporate Archive. Given that ArchivesSpace is not
a digital asset management system where files are stored, the metadata and information is created at
higher levels of organization and stored separately from the actual records.

In addition to these repository-specific practices, Sandia has also developed approaches for representing
structured scientific and technical data. The following describes one example of how scientific and
technical metadata and data representation may be implemented within the NSE.

Sandia’s current approach to metadata and data representation is implemented within its records
management framework, where both administrative records and historical or research materials are
managed in approved records repositories. Metadata practices are aligned with NARA requirements and
integrated with records management systems, including the use of Dublin Core-based elements to
support compliance, access control, and long-term records management requirements.

In this context, structured data elements are also used to describe technical and scientific information,
including attributes such as data element name, definition, data type, permissible values, units of
measure, and stewardship information, consistent with data dictionary practices reflected in standards
such as ISO/IEC 11179 and related data cataloging approaches. These approaches emphasize clearly
defined data elements, traceability, and documentation to support interpretation and reuse of
information. Such representations are maintained within local systems and reflect Sandia’s existing
metadata and data management practices.

3.0 Recommended Additional & Data Representation
Fields
The following extended metadata fields are recommended for implementation across NSE systems to
enhance data sharing and retrieval. They are designed to be applied to digital objects on both open and
closed networks, with two additional fields recommended specifically for closed networks.

While not required for all records, these fields are recommended when applicable to improve
discoverability and usability. They extend the core discovery layer by providing additional descriptive
context that improves search precision, filtering, and user understanding across repositories.

For systems on open networks, the recommended extended fields include:

 • Description: A textual account of the resource, providing additional context, summary, or
 explanation.
 • Subject: A topic, keyword, or phrase that describes the subject matter or thematic content of the
 resource.
 • Type: The nature or genre of the resource, indicating its fundamental form, function, or content
 category.
 • Format: The file format, physical medium, or dimensions of the resource, indicating how the
 resource is stored, delivered, or rendered.

3.1 Description

Intended use The Description field is intended to supply supplementary information about the
 resource, enhancing understanding, discoverability, and usability. It may include
 abstracts, summaries, or content descriptions that clarify the nature or scope of
 the resource. This field is especially valuable when the title alone does not
 convey sufficient detail for effective identification or retrieval.
Value format Free text entry. Contributors should aim for clear, concise, and informative
 descriptions, using complete sentences or well-structured summaries. Length
 and detail may vary depending on resource type and repository practices, but

4.0 Data Representation Considerations
The extended fields described in Section 3 focus on improving the discoverability and contextual
understanding of digital objects across NSE repositories. In some cases, users may also need to identify
resources based on information contained within those objects, such as experimental parameters,
simulation conditions, or material properties. This type of information is typically represented through
structured data elements maintained within the resource or associated systems, rather than through
descriptive metadata fields alone.

Unlike descriptive metadata, which describes a digital object at a general level, these representations
capture specific data elements within the resource. Because scientific and technical domains vary widely,
approaches to representing such information are typically defined within domain-specific systems and
data management practices. While organizations may reference established standards or frameworks to
structure these elements, implementation remains local. Consistent descriptive metadata, as defined in
this and the companion core fields document, remains the primary mechanism for cross-repository
discovery.

4.1 Records Management Context
Records management requirements for federal entities and contractors are defined by the National
Archives and Records Administration (NARA), including guidance in 36 CFR Chapter XII Subchapter B and
associated bulletins. NARA guidance incorporates Dublin Core-based elements to describe records
materials and includes additional requirements for access controls and classification where applicable.
These frameworks support interoperability with federal records systems and ensure compliance with
retention and access requirements.

4.2 Scientific and Technical Context
Scientific and technical data are commonly described using structured data elements maintained in data
dictionaries or similar systems. These may include attributes such as data element name, definition, data
type, permissible values, units of measure, identifier, and stewardship information. Approaches to
defining and managing these elements often reference established practices such as ISO/IEC 11179, FAIR
principles, or related data catalog and provenance standards.

5.0 Conclusion
In conclusion, this document has concentrated on presenting an extended set of standardized metadata
fields designed to enhance the electronic sharing of information within the NSE and its affiliated
laboratories. By focusing on extended metadata standards, we have continued the foundation
established in MWG’s first white paper, Metadata Standards for the NSE: Core Fields. This layered
approach reflects the reality that metadata standardization at the NSE scale must prioritize
interoperability and shared discovery, while allowing governance, policy, and implementation details to
remain locally defined. Recognizing the need for policy recommendations to foster cross-laboratory
cohesion, we proposed the establishment of control boards and a potential NSE-wide metadata control
group to facilitate collaboration and communication across sites.

The successes of librarians and archivists at LANL, LLNL, and SNL in information management and
metadata standardization underscore the importance of this initiative. Their work lays the groundwork
for a potential NSE-wide adoption of metadata standardization, highlighting the significant benefit of a
unified approach. Additionally, our inclusion of data representation fields aims to broaden the scope of
traditional metadata to researcher-requested experimental data while continuing to preserve our
distinct metadata dialect within the broader NSE framework.

While acknowledging that further work remains, we are optimistic that this paper provides a solid
groundwork for advancing the implementation of the FAIR principles—Findability, Accessibility,
Interoperability, and Reuse—within the NSE. By doing so, we aim to bolster national security efforts
through enhanced data sharing and digital engineering capabilities. As we move forward, continued
collaboration and innovation will be essential to fully realize the potential of standardized metadata in
supporting the NSE’s mission. Ultimately, the value of these recommendations lies in enabling consistent
discovery of digital assets across heterogeneous systems without requiring uniform governance or
implementation across the NSE.
