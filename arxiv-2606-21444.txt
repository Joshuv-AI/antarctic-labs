Verification of Parametric Markov Automata under Time-bounded Reachability
Kevin van de Glind, Matthias Volk, Tim A.C. Willemse

Abstract
Analysis of Markov models is of high importance for formal verification. Until now, analysis of Markov Automata required them to be fully specified, which is a considerable restriction as rates may be unknown or influenced by uncertainty of the environment. We introduce parametric Markov Automata (pMA) to capture this uncertainty with parametric transition functions. On these parametrized models, two different synthesis problems for time-bounded reachability properties are considered: I) Does there exist a valuation in the parameter space such that the instantiated model satisfies/violates the property, and II) given the parameter space, how can it be partitioned into satisfying and violating regions? Our approach comprises two steps: I) The pMA is discretized to a parametric Markov decision process (pMDP), and II) through analysis of the pMDP, bounds are obtained for the reachability probability in the parametric MA. This approach solves the above problems up to a specified precision, as the accumulated error terms can be made arbitrarily small. We implemented the approach using the Storm model checker. Our experimental evaluation shows that the main performance bottlenecks originate from the discretization of the pMA.

Introduction

Markov Automata (MA)

[
13
]
are a compositional model for nondeterministic, continuous stochastically timed systems. They are able, as motivated in
[
4
]
, to serve as semantics for other reliability models such as Generalized Stochastic Petri Nets
[
32
]
and Dynamic Fault Trees
[
12
]
. Analysis of MA is supported by various tools, such as
Storm

[
27
]
,
Modest

[
23
]
and
IMCA

[
16
]
.

Until now, all algorithms for model checking MA required the model to be completely specified. In particular, each probabilistic and rate transition had to be precisely known prior to analysis. This can be an unrealistic assumption for real-world systems, as rates or probabilities may be subject to environmental changes or not precisely known.
In order to reason about such uncertainty, we introduce
parametric Markov Automata (pMA)
, where transitions can be parametrized.
This parametric extension of MA follows similar extensions of both discrete-time models
[
29
]
and continuous-time Markov chains
[
22
,
8
]
.

Given a pMA and a region of parameter valuations, we solve two problems for time-bounded reachability properties. Firstly,
satisfaction verification
checks whether the valuations in the region satisfy the property. Secondly,
synthesis

[
29
]
partitions the region into satisfying and violating subregions.
We solve both problems by a two-step approach. First, we discretize the pMA and obtain a
parametric Markov decision process (pMDP)
. Second, after some transformations on the pMDP to make it suitable for further analysis, we perform
parameter lifting (PL)

[
36
]
on this discretized model. PL encodes the choice of parameter valuation as non-deterministic choices, yielding an MDP that allows analysis via off-the-shelf methods.
The proposed approach yields upper and lower bounds for the true reachability probability on the pMA, governed by three different error terms.
These bounds are then used for a classical threshold synthesis scheme
[
29
,
8
]
.

Related work.

Similar models to MA include
Continuous-Time MDPs (CTMDPs)

[

[

]
. The former allows for states with a combination of nondeterminism and timing, the latter already assumes a clear separation between these two types of states. In the literature, these became known as probabilistic and Markovian states. Markov Automata generalize both and introduced the possibility of immediate choice with a successor distribution in probabilistic states
[
26
]
. Several approaches have been proposed to analyze time-bounded reachability properties on MA: some operate directly on the continuous model
[
4
,
1
]
, while others first discretize the model and then carry out the analysis using step-bounded queries
[
17
,
26
,
35
]
.

Parametric extensions are natural for Markov models.
Model checking algorithms exist for discrete-time models
[
11
,
20
,
29
]
supported through e.g.,
Storm

[
27
]
and
iscasMC

[
21
]
, probabilistic timed automata
[
24
]
and continuous-time Markov chains
[
22
,
8
]
through
PRISM-PSY

[
10
]
.
A subset of parametric models are interval models, where each transition is assigned an interval of possible values instead.
These are supported by e.g.,
PRISM

[

or

[

]
.

Contributions.

This paper introduces parametric Markov automata.
Our main contribution is an algorithm for time-bounded reachability on pMA.
To perform analysis, we generalize the discretization result of
[
17
]
to pMA.
Furthermore, under common mild assumptions—non-zenoness of the pMA, multi-affine parametric functions and a weak global condition
—we transform the discretized pMA to an MDP, and solve both satisfaction verification and synthesis up to arbitrary precision.
We implemented our approach using
Storm
and demonstrate it on a variety of pMA models.

Preliminaries

We briefly introduce Markov Automata and the discretization analysis technique
[
17
]
on which our approach is based.

Definition 1
(Markov Automaton)

A
Markov Automaton (MA)
is a tuple
\mathcal{M}=(S,A,s_{0},\rightarrow,\Rightarrow)
, where:

•

S
is a finite set of
states
.

•

A
is a finite set of
actions
.

•

s_{0}\in S
is the
initial state
.

•

\rightarrow\subseteq S\times A\times D(S)
is the
probabilistic
transition relation, where
D(S)
is the set of discrete distributions over
S
.

•

\Rightarrow\subseteq S\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times S
is the
rate
transition relation. The
rate
of a transition is given by
R(s,s^{\prime})=\sum_{(s,r,s^{\prime})\in\Rightarrow}r
.

Under the
maximal progress assumption
—a common assumption for verification of MA
[
5
]
—we assume that probabilistic transitions happen without delay and therefore take precedence over rate transitions which happen instantaneously with probability zero. We can therefore partition the state space into
probabilistic
and
Markovian
states
S=\textit{PS}\sqcup\textit{MS}
.
The two transition relations can then be restricted to probabilistic and Markovian states, respectively:
\rightarrow\subseteq\textit{PS}\times A\times D(S)
and
\Rightarrow\subseteq\textit{MS}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times S
.
Figure
1(b)
depicts an example MA.

For a Markovian state
s\in\textit{MS}
the
sojourn time
of the state is governed by an
exponential distribution
with rate
\lambda(s)=\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}R(s,s^{\prime})
. Thus, the probability of leaving a state
s
within
t
units is given by
1-e^{-\lambda(s)t}
. Afterwards, we transition to successor state
s^{\prime}\in S
with probability
p(s,s^{\prime})=\frac{R(s,s^{\prime})}{\lambda(s)}
.
A probabilistic state
s\in\textit{PS}
is left immediately by choosing an available action
\alpha\in A
with
(s,\alpha,\mu)\in\rightarrow
, and transitions to state
s^{\prime}
with probability
p(s,s^{\prime})=\mu(s^{\prime})
.

An MA can therefore alternatively be defined as a tuple
\mathcal{M}=(S,A,s_{0},\lambda,p)
, where we set
\lambda(s)=+\infty
for all probabilistic states
s\in\textit{PS}
, similar to
[
17
]
.

A
scheduler

\sigma
resolves non-determinism on an MA
\mathcal{M}
by choosing available actions in probabilistic states, resulting in a deterministic model
\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}
. Schedulers
\sigma:\textit{PS}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow A
base this choice on the current state and the total time spent in the system. These belong to the class of total-time positional deterministic schedulers, denoted by
TP(\mathcal{M})
. Under a scheduler, one can define a probability measure on the MA; we refer to
[
34
]
for the details.

MA are a superclass of
Markov decision processes (MDPs)
and
continuous-time Markov chains (CTMCs)
.
An MDP is a tuple
(S,A,s_{0},p)
and can be seen as an MA where
\textit{MS}=\emptyset
. Similarly, a CTMC can be viewed as an MA where
\textit{PS}=\emptyset
. For a more detailed description of both models, we refer to
[
30
]
.

Time-bounded reachability.

In this paper, we focus the analysis on
time-bounded reachability properties
of the form
\mathbb{P}[\diamond^{\leq t}G]\sim\theta
, computing: “Is the maximal/minimal probability to reach a set of goal states
G\subseteq S
within
t
time units greater/less than a given threshold
\theta
?”. For a given MA
\mathcal{M}
and scheduler
\sigma
, the
time-bounded reachability probability
is denoted by
Pr^{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}}[\diamond^{\leq t}G]
. Comparing this value with a user-specified threshold
\theta\in~[0,1]
yields a time-bounded reachability property. This can be formulated as a CSL model checking problem
[
2
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}\models\mathbb{P}[\diamond^{\leq t}G]\sim\theta\quad\textit{iff}\quad Pr^{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}}[\diamond^{\leq t}G]\sim\theta,\text{ for}\sim\in\{<,\leq,\geq,>\},\theta\in[0,1].

For time-bounded reachability probabilities, total-time positional deterministic schedulers
TP(\mathcal{M})
suffice
[
35
]
and we define the maximal/minimal probability:

\displaystyle\textit{Pmax}[\mathcal{M},\diamond^{\leq t}G]=\sup_{\sigma\in TP(\mathcal{M})}Pr^{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}}[\diamond^{\leq t}G]

\displaystyle\textit{Pmin}[\mathcal{M},\diamond^{\leq t}G]=\inf_{\sigma\in TP(\mathcal{M})}Pr^{\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}}[\diamond^{\leq t}G]

Similarly to before, one can use
Pm[\mathcal{M},\diamond^{\leq t}G]\leq\theta
for
m\in\{min,max\}
to define time-bounded reachability properties.

Discretization.

Time-bounded reachability properties can be analysed using the
discretization method

[
17
,
6
,
35
]
, also called digitization
[
26
]
.
This transformation allows one to reason about the approximate behaviour of the MA by analyzing the time-discretized model, yielding a (discrete-time) MDP.

Definition 2
(Discretized MA)

Let MA
\mathcal{M}=(S,A,s_{0},\rightarrow,\Rightarrow)
and discretization step
\delta>0
. The
discretized MA
is an MDP
\mathcal{M}^{\delta}=(S,A,s_{0},P^{\prime})
, where:

\displaystyle P^{\prime}(s,s^{\prime})=\begin{cases}(1-e^{-\lambda(s)\delta})p(s,s^{\prime}),&\text{if }s\neq s^{\prime}\\
(1-e^{-\lambda(s)\delta})p(s,s)+e^{-\lambda(s)\delta},&\text{if }s=s^{\prime}\end{cases}

An example of this transformation is shown in Figure
1(b)
-
1(c)

The central idea relies on the fact that, for small

, the probability of taking more than one Markovian step within time
\delta
is small.
The error made by this discretization can be bounded
[

]
.

Theorem 2.1

[

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an MA,

,

.

.

3
Parametric Markov Automata

In this section, we define
parametric MA
which extend MA with parametric rate- and probabilistic functions.
We lift the discretization result of Theorem
2.1
to this parametric model in Lemma
1
. Last, we provide the formal problem definition, defining the satisfaction verification and synthesis problems.

Parametric Markov automata.

We first introduce the necessary notation for parameters.
Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{1},...,x_{N}\}
be a finite set of
parameters
. A
(parameter) instantiation
is a mapping
\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. A parametrized successor distribution is denoted by
D^{\mathcal{X}}:S\rightarrow[0,1]
. A
parameter region

\mathcal{R}
is a closed and bounded subset, in the topological sense as described in for example
[
14
]
,
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{|\mathcal{X}|}
with
any
v\in\mathcal{R}
called a
valuation
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{R}
is a polytope, we write
V_{\mathcal{R}}
to denote its vertices. A polynomial over
\mathcal{X}
is a weighted sum over monomials
x_{1}^{a_{1}}...x_{n}^{a_{n}}
for
a_{1},...,a_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
and it is called
multi-affine
if for each monomial
a_{1},...,a_{n}\leq 1
.
The set of polynomials over
\mathcal{X}
with real coefficients is denoted by
\mathbb{R}[\mathcal{X}]
. A polynomial
f\in\mathbb{R}[\mathcal{X}]
is
monotone increasing/decreasing
on
\mathcal{R}
with respect to
x\in\mathcal{X}
if, respectively,
\frac{d}{dx}f|_{\mathcal{R}}\geq 0
or
\frac{d}{dx}f|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq 0
.
A polynomial
f
is called
monotone
on
\mathcal{R}
if it is monotone with respect to each parameter. Multi-affine functions are monotone on any region
\mathcal{R}
.

Definition 3
(Parametric Markov Automaton)

A
parametric Markov Automaton (pMA)
over
\mathcal{X}
is a tuple
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{X}}=(S,A,s_{0},\rightarrow,\Rightarrow)
, where states
S
, actions
A
and initial state
s_{0}
are as in Definition
1
, and the two transition relations
\rightarrow\subseteq PS\times A\times D^{\mathcal{X}}(S)
and
\Rightarrow\subseteq MS\times\mathbb{R}[\mathcal{X}]\times S
are parametrized over
\mathcal{X}
.

Similarly to pMDPs and pCTMCs
[
22
,
29
]
, pMAs can be instantiated. An example of a pMA and an instantiation can be seen in Figure
1(a)
-
1(b)
.

Definition 4
(Instantiated MA)

For any pMA
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{X}}
and valuation
v\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{X}|}
, we obtain the
instantiated MA

\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{X}}[v]
by applying the instantiation
x_{i}\mapsto v_{i}
.

We use the same notation for an instantiation of a single rate or successor distribution with valuation
v
as for instantiating a pMA.
A valuation
v
is called
well-defined
if the resulting instantiated MA
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{X}}[v]
satisfies Definition
1
.
A well-defined valuation is
graph-preserving
if the following conditions are met:

\displaystyle\forall

\displaystyle s\in\textit{MS},s^{\prime}\in S,v\in\mathcal{R}:

\displaystyle\quad R(s,s^{\prime})\not\equiv 0\rightarrow R[v](s,s^{\prime})\neq 0

\displaystyle\forall

\displaystyle s\in\textit{PS},s^{\prime}\in S,\alpha\in A,v\in\mathcal{R}:

\displaystyle\quad P(s,\alpha,s^{\prime})\not\equiv 0\rightarrow P[v](s,\alpha,s^{\prime})\neq 0

In the remainder we assume each valuation is well-defined and graph-preserving.

A pMA is called
non-zeno
if, for every valuation, the instantiated MA does not have a
zeno cycle
, which is a cycle of probabilistic states one does not exit almost surely. For further details on zenoness of MA, we refer to
[
26
,
17
]
.
We assume each pMA to be non-zeno, a common assumption on MA in the literature
[
7
,
26
]
. Moreover, in practice, non-zenoness, and the stronger occurrence of having any cycles of probabilistic states at all, is extremely rare
[
7
]
and existing MA models, found for example in the
QCOMP
benchmark suite
[
25
]
, do not have, to our knowledge, cycles of probabilistic states.

In order to determine the exact extremal values of parametric functions, these are restricted to multi-affine polynomials. This is in line with the restriction on parametric rate functions in
[
8
]
. For the sake of presentation, we restrict attention to hyper-rectangular parameter spaces, a standard assumption in literature
[
8
,
29
]
. However, our methods extend to finite unions of hyper-rectangles.

Lifting discretization.

In order to solve time-bounded reachability queries on pMA, we generalize the discretization result of Theorem
2.1
.
The discretization of pMA is analogous to Definition
2
, but instead yields a parametric MDP. The generalization is shown in Lemma
1
, where, for any two parametric models
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
over the same parameters, we write
Pmax[\mathcal{M},\phi]\leq Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\phi]
if for each valuation
v\in\mathcal{R}
we have
Pmax[\mathcal{M}[v],\phi]\leq Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\prime}[v],\phi]
.

Lemma 1

Let
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{X}}
be a pMA over a region
\mathcal{R}
and
t>0
. Fix
\delta>0
as discretization step such that
t/\delta\in\mathbb{N}
.
We get the following:

\displaystyle Pmax[{\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{X}}}^{\delta},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]\leq Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{X}},\diamond^{\leq t}G]\leq

\displaystyle Pmax[{\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{X}}}^{\delta},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]

\displaystyle+1-\inf_{v\in\mathcal{R}}[e^{-\lambda[v]t}(1+\lambda[v]\delta)^{t/\delta}]

The same results hold for
Pmin[\cdot]
.

Proof

Consider any valuation
v\in\mathcal{R}
. By Theorem
2.1
the following bound is obtained:
Pmax[{\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{X}}}^{\delta}[v],\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]\leq Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{X}}[v],\diamond^{\leq t}G]
.
Therefore, we immediately prove our lower bound. Similarly, we get:

\displaystyle Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{X}}[v],\diamond^{\leq t}G]

\displaystyle\leq Pmax[{\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{X}}}^{\delta}[v],\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]+1-e^{-\lambda[v]t}(1+\lambda[v]\delta)^{t/\delta}

\displaystyle\leq Pmax[{\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{X}}}^{\delta}[v],\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]+1-\inf_{w\in R}[e^{-\lambda[w]t}(1+\lambda[w]\delta)^{t/\delta}]

The proof for
Pmin[\cdot]
is analogous. ∎

The infimum in the error bound requires a maximization of the maximum sojourn rate. From this point onward, for readability, we will denote pMA and its corresponding discretization by
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\delta}
, respectively, whenever it is clear from the context that we are referring to the parametric model. In such cases, the discretization error will be written as
\epsilon_{\delta}=1-\inf_{v\in\mathcal{R}}[e^{-\lambda[v]t}(1+\lambda[v]\delta)^{t/\delta}]
.

Problem statement.

Now that pMA are formally defined, we provide a formal problem definition.
In contrast to MA, the analysis of pMA also needs to reason about the parameter valuations.
We solve two main problems for time-bounded reachability properties: (1)
satisfaction verification
checks whether there exists a valuation in the region which satisfies/violates the given property, and (2)
synthesis
partitions the parameter space into satisfying and violating subregions.

Four different satisfaction relations are considered, distinguishing cases where the
player
—making the non-deterministic choices—and
nature
—assigning the parameter values—can either be cooperative or adversarial, similar to Definition 14 in
[
29
]
for discrete-time models.

Definition 5
(Satisfaction relations)

For a pMA
\mathcal{M}
, region
\mathcal{R}
and time-bounded reachability property
\phi=\mathbb{P}[\diamond^{\leq t}G]~\sim\theta
, we define the following satisfaction relations:

(Feasibility)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},\mathcal{R}\models_{f}\phi\quad\text{iff}\quad\exists\sigma\in TP(\mathcal{M})~\exists v\in\mathcal{R}:\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}[v]\models\phi

(Angelic-Aid)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},\mathcal{R}\models_{a}\phi\quad\text{iff}\quad\forall\sigma\in TP(\mathcal{M})~\exists v\in\mathcal{R}:\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}[v]\models\phi

(Robust)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},\mathcal{R}\models_{r}\phi\quad\text{iff}\quad\exists\sigma\in TP(\mathcal{M})~\forall v\in\mathcal{R}:\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}[v]\models\phi

(Demonic)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},\mathcal{R}\models_{d}\phi\quad\text{iff}\quad\forall\sigma\in TP(\mathcal{M})~\forall v\in\mathcal{R}:\mathcal{M}_{\sigma}[v]\models\phi

We now define the two main problems.
Given a pMA
\mathcal{M}
, region
\mathcal{R}
, time-bounded reachability property
\phi
and satisfaction relation
\clubsuit\in\{f,a,r,d\}
, the
satisfaction verification
problem is to decide whether
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{R}\models_{\clubsuit}\phi
. The verification problems for discrete-time models considered in
[
29
]
are the robust and demonic cases of this problem. The
synthesis
problem is to find a partitioning
\mathcal{R}=T\sqcup F
such that
\forall{T^{\prime}}\subseteq{T}:~\mathcal{M},T^{\prime}\models_{\clubsuit}~\phi
and
\forall{F^{\prime}}\subseteq{F}:~\mathcal{M},F^{\prime}\not\models_{\clubsuit}\phi
. This definition is equivalent to the approximate synthesis problem in
[
29
]
. As for each valuation in
T,F
the property must be satisfied/violated, feasibility and robust satisfaction relations yield the same result, and so do angelic-aid and demonic.

4
Model transformations

Using Lemma
1
, one could try to naively perform parameter synthesis on the discretized pMA. However, the discretized pMA requires an adapted value-iteration scheme
[
26
]
, which combines unbounded reachability between PS states with time-bounded reachability in MS.
Instead we transform the discretized pMA as outlined in Figure
3
, which allows us to apply existing analysis techniques for step-bounded reachability on pMDP.
In the remainder of this section, we discuss these transformations in more detail.

Section
4.1
focuses on transforming and discretizing the pMA into a collapsed discretized pMA, where the instantaneous transitions from PS states are collapsed into Markovian states, using state-elimination approaches such as
[
19
]
.
The adapted reachability scheme of the original model then yields the same result as step-bounded reachability on the transformed model.
We could then perform parameter synthesis on the resulting pMDP.
However, discretizing the pMA is expensive and would need to be performed for each subregion.
Instead, Section
4.2
introduces a method to obtain approximate discretizations
\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}
which include the discretization as a parameter in the discretized pMA, allowing the transformations covered in this section to be a preprocessing step.

After the preprocessing, we instantiate the discretization for each parameter region and perform the verification.
The verification either terminates with a conclusive result, or we need to further partition the region and check each subregion individually.
This process, including how to take approximation errors into account, is described in Section
5
.

4.1
Collapse of Probabilistic states

The original adapted VI scheme
[
26
]
only counts steps in the MDP if they are taken in states corresponding to Markovian states in the MA. Therefore, we propose a method to eliminate
PS
using state elimination
[
19
]
, such that all steps in the discretized model will be taken in
MS
and the alternative value-iteration scheme reduces to a step-bounded reachability query.

As time does not pass in a chain of probabilistic states and we assume non-zenoness, one can fix the policy in the first state of the chain and immediately get the distribution over Markovian states by eliminating the intermediate probabilistic states. Thus, a chain of
PS
can be reduced to a single state. In essence, this approach follows a similar idea to the MA to SAMA transformation in
[
15
]
. This transformation naturally comes at the cost of an exponential blow-up in the number of available actions for the given chain. However, in practice, chains of PS tend to be short, which controls this growth of available actions.

To eliminate the remaining
PS
, the pMA needs to be discretized, as pMA have a clear distinction between states with stochastic timing and non-deterministic choice. After discretization, we can collapse the PS into MS using well known elimination procedures
[
20
]
.
Appendix
0.C
provides the details of this algorithm.
Correctness of this approach immediately follows from the adapted value-iteration scheme in
[
26
]
.
The cost of this transformation is again an exponential increase in the total number of actions with respect to the outdegree of Markovian states, which tends to be small in practice.

4.2
Approximate discretization

Throughout the discretization of the pMA, in accordance with Definition
2
, the parametric functions on the resulting pMDP will contain an exponential term. However, current methods
[
29
,
18
]
only allow for rational functions.
We therefore need to represent the exponential via rational functions. We briefly describe two methods: I)
Minimizing/Maximizing the sojourn rates
to get the respective minimum/maximum reachability results, or II)
approximating the exponential
by a partial Taylor expansion
[
37
]
. Both of these result in over- and underapproximations of the final reachability probabilities.

Minimizing/Maximizing sojourn rates.

The first method to obtain an over- and underapproximation of the reachability probability is to maximize/minimize the sojourn rates for each state independently from the probabilistic transitions of the same state. Clearly, maximizing these rates results in a higher probability of reaching the goal state within the set time bound, as states are left faster and the goal state can be reached earlier. In practice, this means that we set the sojourn rates of each state to
\max_{v\in V_{\mathcal{R}}}\lambda[v](s)
or
\min_{v\in V_{\mathcal{R}}}\lambda[v](s)
.
We use the assumption of multi-affine parametric rate functions here to guarantee that the extrema are attained at the vertices of the region. The main drawback of this approach is that intra-state dependencies between sojourn rate and successor distribution are lost, potentially leading to severe approximation errors.

Approximating the exponential function.

To maintain intra-state dependencies, we approximate the exponential function by a partial Taylor expansion:

\displaystyle\forall N\in\mathbb{N}:\quad\sum_{k=0}^{2N+1}\frac{(-x)^{k}}{k!}\leq e^{-x}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{2N}\frac{(-x)^{k}}{k!}

(1)

The bounds in Equation
1
are directly implied by the alternating series remainder theorem
[
37
]
.
Based on this approximation, we introduce the
approximate discretized Markov Automaton
.

Definition 6
(Approximate discretized pMA)

Given discretization step
{\delta>0}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, and pMA
\mathcal{M}=(S,A,s_{0},\rightarrow,\Rightarrow)
.
The
approximate discretized parametric Markov Automaton
is given by
\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{N}=(S,A,s_{0},P^{\prime})
, where:

\displaystyle P^{\prime}(s,s^{\prime})=\begin{cases}(1-\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{(-\lambda(s))^{k}}{k!})p(s,s^{\prime}),&\textit{ if }s\neq s^{\prime}\\
(1-\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{(-\lambda(s))^{k}}{k!})p(s,s^{\prime})+\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{(-\lambda(s))^{k}}{k!},&\textit{ otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

Using Theorem
4.1
, given below, the approximate discretization is guaranteed to bound the reachability probability of the pMA, and the error of this additional approximation is also bounded in terms of the discretized pMA.

Given pMA
\mathcal{M}=(S,A,s_{0},\rightarrow,\Rightarrow),G\subseteq S,t>0

so

and pick

and
\lambda=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{R}}\lambda[v]

\displaystyle Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]-\epsilon^{2K}_{t/\delta}\leq Pmax[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Pmax[\mathcal{M},\diamond^{\leq t}G]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Pmax[\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]+\epsilon_{\delta}\leq Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]+\epsilon^{2K+1}_{t/\delta}+\epsilon_{\delta},

where

5
Solving satisfaction verification and synthesis problems

Section
4
focused on the necessary transformations to make step-bounded reachability on the discretized MA possible.
This section focuses on the verification of regions on the resulting MDP using parameter lifting
[
36
]
.

5.1
Parameter lifting

Parameter Lifting (PL)
is a technique which abstracts inter-state dependencies away with a transformation from pMDP to parameter-free
stochastic games (SG)

[
36
]
. The extremal values over the parametric transition functions are precomputed, and nature, as a second player, chooses an extremal parameter value which, depending on the satisfaction relation, minimizes/maximizes the time-bounded reachability probability
[
29
]
.
This relaxation introduces another error which becomes smaller when the absolute difference between extrema decreases.

As PL assumes that extrema are attained at the vertices of the region
[
36
]
, we must ensure that this holds throughout the transformations from Section
4
. Therefore, we assume that no two states in a chain of probabilistic states share a parametric function defined over the same variable. Moreover, to cover the collapse of PS in MS, we assume that the sets of parameters over rate- and probabilistic transition functions are disjoint. These assumptions can be enforced, if needed, by introducing auxiliary parameters. We show in Appendix
0.E

that I) by adding auxiliary parameters, one does not lose the guarantee of reasoning up to arbitrary precision over the original parameter space and II) these assumptions suffice to guarantee correctness of PL.
None of the models we have come across from the literature are negatively impacted by these assumptions.
Moreover, the same assumptions are used for pCTMC
[
8
]
, motivating that these conditions are weak enough to model realistic scenarios.

5.2
Solving the safisfaction verification and synthesis problems

The problems can now be solved by comparing the bounds obtained through PL with the threshold
\theta
, using a well-known refinement scheme
[
8
,
29
,
9
,
3
]
:

\displaystyle M,\mathcal{R}\models_{\clubsuit}\mathbb{P}[\diamond^{\leq t}G]\leq\theta=\begin{cases}\text{Satisfied, if }upper_{\clubsuit}\leq\theta,\\
\text{Violated, if }lower_{\clubsuit}>\theta,\\
\text{Inconclusive and needs further splitting},\textit{Otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

The bounds
upper_{\clubsuit}
/
lower_{\clubsuit}
differ per satisfaction relation and are affected by the accumulated errors. Recall the three error terms: i) discretization error
\epsilon_{\delta}
, ii) the error of the approximate discretization, and iii) the PL error. We denote
Pr_{p,n}[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]
with
p,n\in\{\max,\min\}
to indicate whether player and nature are trying to maximize/minimize the reachability probability in the SG, which results from applying PL on either pMDP
\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}
or
\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}
.

The satisfaction verification for different satisfaction relations can now be derived using the bounds below.
For feasibility and angelic-aid, due to the existential quantification over the valuations, one witness valuation
v\in\mathcal{R}
as upper bound suffices to conclude that the entire region is satisfying.
Similarly, for demonic and robustness, one counterexample suffices to conclude that the region is violating. Thus, the scheme can be optimized by terminating in such cases.

\displaystyle[lower_{\clubsuit},upper_{\clubsuit}]\in\begin{cases}[Pr_{\min,\min}[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G],Pmin[\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}[v],\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]+\epsilon_{\delta}]\text{, if }\clubsuit=f,\\
[Pr_{\max,\min}[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G],Pmax[\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}[v],\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]+\epsilon_{\delta}]\text{, if }\clubsuit=a,\\
[Pmin[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}[v],\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G],Pr_{\min,\max}[\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]+\epsilon_{\delta}]\text{, if }\clubsuit=r,\\
[Pmax[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}[v],\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G],Pr_{\max,\max}[\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]+\epsilon_{\delta}]\text{, if }\clubsuit=d.\end{cases}

Bounds for the synthesis problem are derived similarly to the satisfaction verification.
However, the early termination criterion does not apply anymore, as we aim to classify each valuation as violating or satisfying.

\displaystyle[lower_{\clubsuit},upper_{\clubsuit}]{\in}\begin{cases}[Pr_{\min,\min}[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G],Pr_{\min,\max}[\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]{+}\epsilon_{\delta}]\text{, if }\clubsuit{\in}\{f,r\},\\
[Pr_{\max,\min}[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G],Pr_{\max,\max}[\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]{+}\epsilon_{\delta}]\text{, if }\clubsuit{\in}\{a,d\}.\end{cases}

In practice, the synthesis is sample-guided, similar to synthesis methods for other models
[
29
,
8
]
. First, a relatively fast check is performed on the instantiated MA
\mathcal{M}[v]
, for an
v\in\mathcal{R}
, whose value then determines which inequality to check.

The bounds for both problems are affected by the discretization error
\epsilon_{\delta}
. While the parameter lifting error approaches zero as the region becomes smaller, the discretization error remains constant.
We may therefore encounter cases where the outcome remains inconclusive for every subregion. To solve problems up to arbitrary precision, one must detect this situation and decrease the discretization step
\delta
accordingly. A conservative method is presented in Section
6
.

In summary, using the methodologies described in Sections
4
-
5
, we cover the entire process as depicted in Figure

.

Implementation.

We implemented our approach, supporting both pCTMCs and pMAs, using the
Stormpy
Python interface of the
Storm

[
27
]
.
The implementation is publicly available on GitLab.

1

The current implementation relies on explicit labels on goal-states and is able to check time-bounded reachability properties. Moreover, it can be easily extended to time-bounded until queries
\textit{safe}\;U^{\leq t}\;G
, where
\textit{safe},G\subseteq S

[
3
]
.

One can configure in which order to verify regions and when to decrease the discretization error, as it does not affect the theoretical guarantees. However, as it impacts performance, we briefly cover our choices. As the discretization error is the main component in performance degradation, we implemented a conservative method to decrease the discretization error. Intuitively, our approach uses that the difference between
Pr_{p,\max}[\cdot]
and
Pr_{p,\min}[\cdot]
tends to zero as the volume of the region decreases. Thus, if
Pr_{p,\min}[\cdot]+\epsilon_{\delta}>\theta
whilst
Pr_{p,\min}[\cdot]\leq\theta
, no subregion will satisfy
Pr_{p,\max}[\cdot]+\epsilon_{\delta}\leq\theta
. Therefore, if
Pr_{p,\max}[\cdot]\leq\theta
as well, splitting the region will not help and the discretization error should be reduced instead. The subregions that need to be checked after splitting are handled as a FIFO queue, ensuring that larger regions are handled first.
We implemented an additional heuristic which tracks a frontier of the already decided parameter space, allowing to prioritize regions bordering decided regions.

7
Conclusion

We introduced the notion of parametric Markov Automata, extending Markov Automata with parametric rate- and transition functions. We presented methods to perform satisfaction verification and parameter synthesis on these models. Our approach consists of two steps. First, we transform the pMA to an approximate discretized pMA. Second, we analyze the approximate behaviour of the pMA by applying existing parameter lifting techniques.
The experimental evaluation indicated that both the predominant source of error and computational demand arise from the discretization method.

Future work.

Given our findings above, future research could investigate approaches which do not use the discretization method. This could for instance require the analysis of continuous-time stochastic games without discretization in order to perform parameter lifting. Alternatively, one could opt for an approach using uniformization, such as in
[
6
]
, instead. However, this would most likely require many costly calls on the SG due to the prophetic scheduler.

A better choice of heuristics may optimize the total decided area for the parameter synthesis problem. That is, one may investigate how to use previous information to decide when to reduce the discretization error. Less conservative conditions on when to decrease the discretization error could potentially save expensive model checking queries on the discretized MA. However, it may lead to, potentially, checking regions with a lower discretization error than necessary.

We believe our method can also be extended to time-interval bounded queries. Here, the main difficulty lies in finding an appropriate approximate discretization such that lower and upper bounds are guaranteed.

\delta

\epsilon_{\delta}

\epsilon_{\delta}

1

\mathcal{R}

2^{|\mathcal{X}|}

Appendix 0.C
Probabilistic State collapse algorithm

After discretizing the pMA, the remaining
PS
states must be collapsed into Markovian states in order to compute the step-bounded reachability probability.
For this collapse, we must be able to distinguish which states originally belonged to
MS
and
PS
. This is done by adding a reward structure
R
to the discretization such that:

\displaystyle R(s)=\begin{cases}1,\textit{ if }s\in MS\\
0,\textit{ if }s\in PS\end{cases}

Definition 7
(Binary reward MDP)

We call an MDP
M=\{S,A,s_{0},\rightarrow,R\}
with reward structure
R
a
binary reward MDP
if
R(s)\in\{0,1\}
for all
s\in S
.
Moreover, we partition
S=S_{1}\sqcup S_{0}
into sets of states with reward
1
and
0
, respectively.

It is easy to see that cost-bounded reachability with respect to
R
in this adapted discretized pMA yields the same result as the adapted VI-scheme. Algorithm
1
ensures that, given a binary reward MDP without
0
-reward loops, it returns an MDP for which the step-bounded reachability is equivalent to the cost-bounded reachability in the former. In particular, for our use case, it means that Algorithm
1
collapses the remaining zero-reward states of the discretized model into states with reward one.
The algorithm follows state-elimination ideas such as in
[
19
]
.
Note that our discretized pMA is guaranteed to be zero-loop free due to preprocessing as covered in Section
4.1
. Therefore, the following result is obtained:

\mathcal{M}^{\delta}
with the reward structure defined above,
G\subseteq\textit{MS}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{1}
be the model returned from Algorithm
1
. Then we have the following:

\displaystyle Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta},\diamond^{c_{R}\leq N}G]=Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{1},\diamond^{\leq N}G]

Moreover, the same holds for
Pmin[\cdot]
.

Proof
(Sketch)

For any valuation
v\in R
, the equality holds due to the reasoning above. The inequality then holds for the parametric models as well. ∎

Appendix 0.D
Approximate discretization

In this section, we will prove Theorem
4.1
in two steps. Lemma
3
guarantees lower and upper bounds with respect to the true reachability probability and Lemma
4
guarantees that this additional error tends to zero as the discretization step goes to zero.

Lemma 3

Given pMA
\mathcal{M}{=}(S,A,s_{0},\rightarrow,\Rightarrow)
and discretization step
\delta{>}0
, we have:

Pmax[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]\leq Pmax[\mathcal{M},\diamond^{\leq t}G]\leq Pmax[\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]+\epsilon_{\delta},

where
\epsilon_{\delta}
is the discretization error as per Lemma
1
.
Moreover, the same result holds for
Pmin[\cdot]
.

Proof

For
\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}
, the exponential function is over- and under-approximated, as per Equation
1
. This exponential is the probability of a self-loop in the approximate discretization of
\mathcal{M}
. Therefore, the probability mass of taking a self loop is, respectively, over- and under-approximated. As higher self-loop probabilities lead to smaller probabilities for time-bounded reachability, we get the following chain of inequalities.

\displaystyle Pmax[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]\leq Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]

\displaystyle\leq_{\text{Lemma}~\ref{lemma:parametricMADiscretization}}Pmax[\mathcal{M},\diamond^{\leq t}G]

\displaystyle\leq_{\text{Lemma}~\ref{lemma:parametricMADiscretization}}Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]+\epsilon_{\delta}\leq Pmax[\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]+\epsilon_{\delta}

The case
Pmin[\cdot]
is analogous; thus we conclude the proof.∎

Lemma 4

Given pMA
\mathcal{M}=(S,A,s_{0},\rightarrow,\Rightarrow)
, discretization step
\delta>0
and
N>0
. Let
\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}=\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{2N},\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}=\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{2N+1}
. We have that:

\displaystyle Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]-\epsilon^{2N}_{t/\delta}

\displaystyle\leq Pmax[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]

\displaystyle Pmax[\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]

\displaystyle\leq Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta},\diamond^{\leq t/\delta}G]+\epsilon^{2N+1}_{t/\delta},

where
\epsilon^{M}_{t/\delta}=(1+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{M}}{M!})^{t/\delta}-1
with
\lambda=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{R}}\lambda[v]
.
Moreover, the same result holds for
Pmin(\cdot)
.

Proof

Let
Sched(M)
denote the set of all positional schedulers on model
M
. We first show that:
Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s},\diamond^{\leq k}G]-\epsilon^{2N}_{k}\leq Pmax[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}_{s},\diamond^{\leq k}G]
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in S
. Here one should not forget that we should only count steps if taken in Markovian states.

For case
k=0
, equality holds, because no steps are taken. Therefore, we continue our proof with induction, using base case
k=1
:

•

Suppose that
s\in\textit{MS}
, then:

\displaystyle Pmax[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}_{s},\diamond^{\leq 1}G]=(1-\sum_{j=0}^{2N}\frac{(-\lambda(s)\delta)^{j}}{j!})\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}p(s,s^{\prime})Pmax[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}_{s^{\prime}},\diamond^{\leq 0}G]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\sum_{j=0}^{2N}\frac{(-\lambda(s)\delta)^{j}}{j!})\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}p(s,s^{\prime})Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s^{\prime}},\diamond^{\leq 0}G]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-e^{-\lambda(s)\delta}-\frac{(\lambda(s)\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}p(s,s^{\prime})Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s^{\prime}},\diamond^{\leq 0}G]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s},\diamond^{\leq 1}G]-\frac{(\lambda(s)\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s},\diamond^{\leq 1}G]-\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!}=Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s},\diamond^{\leq 1}G]-\epsilon_{1}^{2N}

•

Suppose that
s\in PS
, then:

\displaystyle Pmax[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}_{s},\diamond^{\leq 1}G]=\sup_{\pi\in Sched(\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}})}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\textit{MS}}p_{\pi}(s,s^{\prime})Pmax[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}_{s^{\prime}},\diamond^{\leq 1}G]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sup_{\pi\in Sched(\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}})}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\textit{MS}}p_{\pi}(s,s^{\prime})(Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s^{\prime}},\diamond^{\leq 1}G]-\epsilon_{1}^{2N})

\displaystyle=

{}_{\textit{Non-zenoness assumption}}\sup_{\pi\in Sched(\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}})}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\textit{MS}}p_{\pi}(s,s^{\prime})Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s^{\prime}},\diamond^{\leq 1}G]-\epsilon_{1}^{2N}

\displaystyle=

{}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textit{available actions \&}\\
\textit{graph structure are equal}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\pi\in Sched(\mathcal{M}^{\delta})}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\textit{MS}}p_{\pi}(s,s^{\prime})Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s^{\prime}},\diamond^{\leq 1}G]-\epsilon_{1}^{2N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s},\diamond^{\leq 1}G]-\epsilon_{1}^{2N}

Now for the step case, we consider
k+1
steps:

•

Suppose that
s\in MS
:

\displaystyle Pmax[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}_{s},\diamond^{\leq k+1}G]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\sum_{j=0}^{2N}\frac{(-\lambda(s)\delta)^{j}}{j!})\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}p(s,s^{\prime})Pmax[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}_{s^{\prime}},\diamond^{\leq k}G]

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt+\sum_{j=0}^{2N}\frac{(-\lambda(s)\delta)^{j}}{j!}Pmax[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}_{s},\diamond^{\leq k}G]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-e^{-\lambda(s)\delta}-\frac{(\lambda(s)\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}p(s,s^{\prime})Pmax[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}_{s^{\prime}},\diamond^{\leq k}G]

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt+e^{-\lambda(s)\delta}Pmax[\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}_{s},\diamond^{\leq k}G]

\displaystyle\geq

{}_{\textit{IH}}(1-e^{-\lambda(s)\delta}-\frac{(\lambda(s)\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}p(s,s^{\prime})(Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s^{\prime}},\diamond^{\leq k}G]-\epsilon_{k}^{2N})

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt+e^{-\lambda(s)\delta}(Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s},\diamond^{\leq k}G]-\epsilon_{k}^{2N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-e^{-\lambda(s)\delta})\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}p(s,s^{\prime})(Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s^{\prime}},\diamond^{\leq k}G])+e^{-\lambda(s)\delta}(Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s},\diamond^{\leq k}G])

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt-\frac{(\lambda(s)\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}p(s,s^{\prime})Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s^{\prime}},\diamond^{\leq k}G]

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt-(1-e^{-\lambda(s)\delta}-\frac{(\lambda(s)\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}p(s,s^{\prime})\epsilon_{k}^{2N}-e^{-\lambda(s)\delta}\epsilon_{k}^{2N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Pmax(\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s},\diamond^{k+1}G)-(1-\frac{(\lambda(s)\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})\epsilon_{k}^{2N}-\frac{(\lambda(s)\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}p(s,s^{\prime})Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s^{\prime}},\diamond^{\leq k}G]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle Pmax(\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s},\diamond^{k+1}G)-(1-\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})\epsilon_{k}^{2N}-\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle Pmax(\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s},\diamond^{k+1}G)-(1+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})\epsilon_{k}^{2N}-\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!}

Here, the last inequality could be left out to obtain slightly sharper bounds. However, keeping this inequality yields the same bounds for both
\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}
and
\underline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}
, so for sake of presentation, we keep them as is.
We then have

\displaystyle Pmax(\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s},\diamond^{k+1}G)-(1+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})\epsilon_{k}^{2N}-\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Pmax(\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s},\diamond^{k+1}G)-(1+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})((1+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})^{k}-1)-\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Pmax(\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s},\diamond^{k+1}G)-((1+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})^{k+1}-(1+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!}))-\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Pmax(\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s},\diamond^{k+1}G)-((1+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})^{k+1}-1)+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!}-\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Pmax(\mathcal{M}^{\delta}_{s},\diamond^{k+1}G)-\epsilon_{k}^{2N}

•

If
s\in PS
, the reasoning is analogous to the base case.

This concludes this part of the proof, a similar methodology can be applied for
\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\delta}}
, where instead we get a positive sign for
\epsilon_{k}^{2N+1}
to obtain an upper bound. Finally, the following recurrence relation has been found:

\displaystyle\epsilon_{n+1}^{2N}

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})\epsilon_{n}^{2N}+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!}

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}^{2N}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N!)}

Solving this yields
\epsilon_{n}^{2N}=\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N!)}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(1+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})^{j}
. For
n=t/\delta
we obtain:

\displaystyle\epsilon_{t/\delta}^{2N}=

\displaystyle\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N!)}\sum_{j=0}^{t/\delta-1}(1+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((1+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})^{t/\delta-1}+\frac{(2N)!}{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}((1+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})^{t/\delta-1}-1))\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((1+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})^{t/\delta-1}(1+\frac{(2N)!}{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}})-\frac{(2N)!}{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}})\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\frac{(\lambda\delta)^{2N}}{(2N)!})^{t/\delta}-1

Therefore, we obtained the error bounds as claimed. The case for
Pmin(\cdot)
is analogous.
∎

Theorem
4.1
then follows as a direct corollary of Lemmas
3
and
4
.

Appendix 0.E
Details on correct use of Parameter Lifting

As covered in Section
5.1
, we need additional global assumptions to ensure that the transformations throughout Section
4
yield parametric formulas such that the extrema are attained at one of the vertices of the region. To reiterate, over
PS
transitions, we assumed the following:

“I) To ensure that the elimination of
PS
chains yields monotonic parametric functions, we assume that no two states of such a chain have a parametric function over the same variable and II) to cover the collapse of
PS
in
MS
, we assume that the sets of parameters over rate- and probabilistic transition functions are disjoint.”

Therefore, we have to show two things: I) by adding additional auxiliary variables, we increase the number of dimensions. Due to this, it could be the case that one partitions the region up to arbitrary precision, whilst the relevant hyperplane remains undecided. However, we show that this is not the case and one can still decide this hyperplane up to arbitrary precision. II) If we can show that the probabilistic transitions of the approximate discretization are indeed monotone, we have shown that extrema are attained at the vertices of the region, due to the structure of the solution function of the relaxed MDP as given in
[
29
]
. In Lemma
5
, we show that this property is maintained throughout discretization.

First of all, we start with showing I). In particular, we show that the solution function of the pMA is continuous over each parameter, by using a known result on continuity of graph-preserving parametric Markov chains
[
29
]
. Thus, if one can decide whether
\mathcal{M}[v]
is accepting or not by considering an interval on the hyperplane, one can find a small enough subregion on the higher dimensional model, containing
v
, such that the same conclusion can be drawn.

Theorem 0.E.1

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a pMA over parameters
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\}
, region
\mathcal{R}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
G\subseteq S
. Consider the property
Pmax[\mathcal{M}[v],\diamond^{\leq t}G]
and an oracle which, for every
D\subseteq\mathcal{R}
, gives lower and upper bounds for this probability with non-zero error
\epsilon
, where
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
as
|D|\rightarrow 0
.

Now, consider pMA
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
over parameters
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=\{x_{1},...,x_{n},x_{i}^{\prime}\}
, obtained by replacing some occurrences of parameter
x_{i}
by auxiliary parameter
x^{\prime}_{i}
, and region
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=\mathcal{R}\times\mathcal{R}|_{x_{i}}
. If one can decide a valuation
(v_{1},...,v_{n})\in\mathcal{R}
with this oracle, there exists an
\eta
-ball in
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
such that the valuation can be decided by our approach on a hyperrectangle
D^{\prime}=\bigotimes_{i\in[|\mathcal{X}^{\prime}|]}[a_{i},b_{i}],a_{i}<b_{i}
contained in this
\eta
-ball and
(v_{1},...,v_{n},v_{i})\in D^{\prime}
.

Proof

Let
D\subseteq\mathcal{R}
with
v\in D
and let
lb,ub
be the bounds obtained for checking region
D
with this oracle. Suppose that one can decide satisfaction or violation of the property by the model induced by valuation
v
from these bounds. Then, per our non-zero assumption of the oracle,
\epsilon\leq\min(Pmax[\mathcal{M}[v],\diamond^{\leq t}G]-lb,ub-Pmax[\mathcal{M}[v],\diamond^{\leq t}G])
. Now we will show that there exists an
\eta>0
such that for any
w
in the
\eta
-ball around
v^{\prime}=(v_{1},...,v_{n},v_{i})
, we have that
|Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\prime}[v^{\prime}],\diamond^{\leq t}G]-Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\prime}[w],\diamond^{\leq t}G]|<\epsilon
. That is, the solution function is continuous with respect to the parameters of the pMA, from which we can conclude that there exists some hyperrectangular region with which to decide acceptance/violation of
v^{\prime}
. Thus, to show continuity:

\displaystyle|Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\prime}[v^{\prime}],\diamond^{\leq t}G]-Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\prime}[w],\diamond^{\leq t}G]|

\displaystyle\leq

{}_{\textit{for small enough $\delta$}}|Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}[v^{\prime}],\diamond^{\leq t}G]-Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}[w],\diamond^{\leq t}G]|+\frac{2}{3}\epsilon

It is known that graph preserving pMC give continuous solution functions
[
29
]
. Thus, as we can rewrite
Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}[v^{\prime}],\diamond^{\leq t}G]
as the maximum of all reachability probabilities of a DTMC induced by a non-randomized scheduler, of which there are finitely many, and the maximum of finitely many continuous functions is continuous as well, we get that
Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta},\diamond^{\leq t}G]
admits a continuous solution function with respect to its parameters. Thus there exists an
\eta>0
such that:

\displaystyle|v-w|<\eta\rightarrow|Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}[v],\diamond^{\leq t}G]-Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\delta}[w],\diamond^{\leq t}G]|<\frac{\epsilon}{3}

Thus, we conclude that, for any valuation
w
in the
\eta
-ball centered around
v^{\prime}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\prime}[v^{\prime}],\diamond^{\leq t}G]-Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\prime}[w],\diamond^{\leq t}G]|<\epsilon

In particular, this means that there exists a hyperrectangle
D^{\prime}
, contained in this ball, for which our approach yields less strict bounds than obtained by the oracle. Therefore, we can decide
v^{\prime}
using our approach and the proof is concluded as
Pmax[\mathcal{M}^{\prime}[v^{\prime}],\diamond^{\leq t}G]=Pmax[\mathcal{M}[v],\diamond^{\leq t}G]
per construction of
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
.∎

Note that the theorem above does not require our approach to work on the model without auxiliary variables, but our approach can decide at least as much as any oracle yielding results with non-zero error bounds. As many variables may be substituted, iterative application of Theorem
0.E.1
yields the same claim of our result.

Secondly, we show monotonicity of the discretized model. Here, it is important to note that the collapse of states does not impact the result, as they can safely assumed to be over different parameters.

Lemma 5

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a pMA,
R\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{X}|}
a hyperrectangular region,
s\in\textit{MS}
and
\delta>0
a discretization step according to Theorem
2.1
. Suppose that
R(s,s^{\prime})
is multi-affine for all
s^{\prime}\in S
, then the extrema of the following function is attained at one of the vertices of
R
:

\displaystyle f_{1}(x)=(1-e^{-\lambda(s)(x)\delta})p(s,s^{\prime})(x)

Proof

As
R(s,s^{\prime})(x)
is multi-affine for all
s^{\prime}\in S
, so is
\lambda(s)=\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}R(s,s^{\prime})
. Moreover, as mentioned in Section
2
, multi-affine functions are monotone in each variable. It suffices to show that
f_{1}(x)
is monotone. Therefore, consider
\frac{d}{dx}f_{1}
:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}f_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dx}\left((1-e^{-\lambda(s)(x)\delta})\frac{R(s,s^{\prime})(x)}{\lambda(s)(x)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda(s)(x)\frac{d}{dx}R(s,s^{\prime})(x)-R(s,s^{\prime})(x)\frac{d}{dx}\lambda(s)(x)}{(\lambda(s)(x))^{2}}(1-e^{-\delta\lambda(s)(x)})

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt+\delta\frac{d}{dx}[\lambda(s)(x)]e^{-\delta\lambda(s)(x)}(\frac{R(s,s^{\prime})(x)}{\lambda(s)(x)})

Now we denote
\phi(x)=\frac{\lambda(s)(x)\frac{d}{dx}R(s,s^{\prime})(x)-R(s,s^{\prime})(x)\frac{d}{dx}\lambda(s)(x)}{(\lambda(s)(x))^{2}}
. From now on, assume that
\lambda
is monotone increasing in
x
, where the decreasing case is analogous.

In case that
\phi(x)\geq 0
, we get:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}f_{1}(x)\geq\phi(x)(1-e^{-\delta\lambda(s)(x)})\geq\phi(x)(1-e^{-\delta\inf_{z\in R}\lambda(s)(z)})\geq 0

Inequality
\inf_{z\in R}\lambda(s)(z)>0
holds due to the assumption that region valuations are well-defined, as made in Section
2
.

Similarly, for
\phi\leq 0
:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}f_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\phi(x)(1-e^{-\delta\lambda(s)(x)})+\delta\frac{d}{dx}[\lambda(s)(x)]e^{-\delta\lambda(s)(x)}(\frac{R(s,s^{\prime})(x)}{\lambda(s)(x)})

\displaystyle=e^{-\delta\lambda(s)(x)}\left(\phi(x)e^{\delta\lambda(s)(x)}-\phi(x)+\delta\frac{d}{dx}[\lambda(s)(x)](\frac{R(s,s^{\prime})(x)}{\lambda(s)(x)})\right)\geq 0

This holds, as for small enough
\delta
, we have that:

\displaystyle\delta\frac{d}{dx}[\lambda(s)(x)](\frac{R(s,s^{\prime})(x)}{\lambda(s)(x)})\geq\phi(x)(1-e^{\delta\lambda(s)(x)})

Thereby concluding the proof. ∎

In this Lemma
5
, we do not cover the case of discretizing a self-loop, as each MA can be transformed to an equivalent MA without self-loops in Markovian states. Therefore, the self-loop probability of a state
s
in the discretized model is monotone increasing/decreasing if
\lambda(s)
is respectively decreasing/increasing.
