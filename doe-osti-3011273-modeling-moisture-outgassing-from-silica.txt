INTRODUCTION

Even though a generalized moisture outgassing modeling has been established and published in a
peer-reviewed venue for as-received silica-filled silicones, 1 it is still not yet intuitive for use in
engineering/scientific applications. A simplification of the mathematics down to a practical level
and illustration of how to do it would be beneficial to the practitioners in the field. In this report,
simplified and more intuitive demonstrations of how to use the generalized outgassing kinetics for
the prediction of H2O release from as-received and previously vacuum-baked silicones will be
illustrated.

In equation (1), MODVS, MOTPD, and MO are moisture outgassing from dynamic vapor sorption
(DVS) experiments, moisture outgassing from temperature programmed desorption (TPD)
experiments, and total moisture outgassing, respectively. W H2O by DVS is the total moisture content
(in ppm or wt.%) by DVS which assesses moisture diffusion out of the silicone matrix prior to the
start of TPD experiments. WH2O by DVS depends on the temperature and relative humidity (RH) of
the storage environment. WH2O by DVS relates to the potential for the diffusive release of spooled
water (i.e., multilayers of H2O molecules that are hydrogen bonded to each other, except for the

spectrometer chamber to achieve high vacuum equilibrium before actual TPD experiments. W H2O

the release of chemisorbed water by the condensation of OH groups on SiO 2 surfaces. In the last

The derivation of the kinetic parameters (i.e., E, , xi) for as-received M9787 and molded LL50
has been provided in reference 1 and will not be repeated here. The tightly bound physisorbed
water releases from P2 and P1 are likely from the first layer of H2O molecules hydrogen-bonded
to SiOH groups on SiO2 surfaces and the first one or two monolayers of H2O molecules above it,
respectively. Note that chemisorbed H2O species with very high activation energy barriers like P5
and P6 originate from heat induced diffusion and subsequent condensation of distant OH bonds
and contribute very little to the outgassing process. On the contrary, chemisorbed H 2O species
with lower activation energy barriers like P3 and P4 arise from condensation of H 2O out of higher
concentration of SiOH groups, which are responsible for lower temperature outgassing.
The kinetic predictions of potential moisture outgassing from as-received M9787 and molded
LL50 at a constant temperature of 303 K can be made with the help of Equations (1) – (4) and
Table 1 and are presented in Figure 2.

Figure 2. Total H2O outgassing in ppm (contributed by both DVS and TPD experiments) from as-
received M9787 and LL50 for the first 100 hours (left) and up to 10 years (right) at 303 K.

The total moisture outgassing potential (DVS + TPD) from as-received M9787 and LL50 at 303
K are ~ 5146 ppm and ~ 900 ppm, respectively. Thanks to the use of more hydrophobic silica
fillers, the total moisture outgassing from as-received LL50 is reduced roughly by a factor of ~
5.7 in comparison with as-received M9787. It is seen that the loose physisorbed moisture (i.e.,
multilayers of H2O molecules hydrogen-bonded to each other from the pooling effect during
moisture absorption) is completely removed at 303 K in less than 6 minutes in vacuum (see the
left plots in Figure 2). Therefore, if there is a pulling of vacuum even only at room temperature
over silica-filled silicones for 10 – 15 minutes after long term storage in laboratory environment
before the start of sealed applications, all pooled water (i.e., loosely bound physisorbed water or
multilayers of H2O molecules hydrogen-bonded to each other) can be removed, and the long-
term outgassing potential can be solely estimated from tightly bound physisorbed and
chemisorbed water from TPD experiment. Even though the outgassing from the TPD experiment
contributes only ~ 10% and ~ 7% of the total moisture potentials for M9787 and LL50, the
tightly bound physisorbed and chemisorbed water released from TPD are the main contributors
to long term outgassing in sealed applications. For sealed applications after 10 - 15 minutes of
room temperature vacuum pump, the total outgassing over 100 years at 303 K is only ~ 67 ppm
for LL50 and 570 ppm for M9787 (a significant reduction from 900 ppm for LL50 and 5146
ppm for M9787 without the pre-pump)..

The results of similar outgassing modeling as those presented in Figure 2 (without 10 – 15 minutes
of vacuum pump prior to application) but with a time-varying temperature profile for as-received
M9787 and molded LL50 are presented in Figure 3. By comparison with isothermal outgassing at
303 K in Figure 2, the outgassing curves with a time-temperature profile in Figure 3 are not

Developing H2O outgassing model for previously vacuum-baked silica-filled silicones with
subsequent 30 ppm moisture re-exposure

Values for WH2O by DVS can be looked up from the work on DVS for as-received M9787 and for as-
received LL50. In the absence of DVS data on moisture absorption of previously vacuum-baked
silicones, WH2O by DVS values for as-received silicones can be reasonably taken as the upper limit
approximation for moisture uptake from previously vacuum-baked silicones which undergo
subsequent limited moisture re-exposure. Unfortunately, the lowest ranges of DVS absorption
experiments only reached down to ~ 0.3 % RH at 313 K for M9787 (~ 218 molar volume ppm in
glovebox filled with inert gas at atmospheric condition) and to ~ 0.8% RH at 303 K for molded
LL50 (~335 molar volume ppm in glovebox condition). However, most glovebox moisture levels
are roughly ~ 30 ppm. So, extrapolation of experimental DVS absorption experiments to 0% RH
was performed and shown for M9787 at 313 K and molded LL50 at 303 K in Figure 4. But as
mentioned in the previous section on as-received M9787 and as-received LL50, the physisorbed
water portion resulted from DVS experimentation is completely removed after 10 - 15 minutes of
vacuum pump at room temperature.

Figure 4. Extrapolation of moisture uptake for M9787 at 313 K and for molded LL50 at 303 K
(taken from references 3 & 4) down to 0% RH.

Table 2 list the rate limiting mechanisms (n = 1 for 1 st order desorption kinetic and n = 2 for 2nd
order desorption kinetics), activation energy barriers, pre-exponential factors, and fractional
contributions for all the TPD deconvoluted peaks of previously vacuum-baked M9787 and molded
LL50 silicones with subsequent low moisture exposure (~ 30 ppm). 5-6 The fits provided by these
generalized kinetic parameters with 0.025 K/s TPD spectra of previously vacuum-baked M9787
and molded LL50 after subsequent 30 ppm moisture exposure are shown in Fig. 5. Note that in
order to get the best fits for the total areas under the M9787 and molded LL50 TPD spectra, the
overall simulation curves add up to 113% and 108% (instead of 100%) of the areas under the TPD
curves. So, the experimental error bars in the TPD portions are on the order of 8 - 13%. For
comparison, the moisture outgassing from TPD curves (i.e., excluding pooled water from DVS
which can be vacuum-pumped out in 10 - 15 minutes) at 303 K over 100 years for as-received
M9787 and previously vacuum-baked M9787 with subsequent 30 ppm H 2O re-exposure are ~ 570
ppm and ~ 118 ppm, respectively. Similarly, the moisture outgassing under TPD curves (again,
excluding pooled water from DVS) at 303 K over 100 years for as-received molded LL50 and
previously vacuum-baked molded LL50 with subsequent 30 ppm H 2O re-exposure are ~ 67 ppm
and ~ 35 ppm, respectively. The net results of the vacuum baking (even with subsequent limited
moisture exposure) are both a reduction and rearrangement of OH bonds on silica fillers’ surfaces
with a significant decrease in potential H2O outgassing.

The kinetic prediction of moisture outgassing from previously vacuum-baked M9787 and molded
LL50 after subsequent 30 ppm moisture exposure can be done analogously to those from as-
received samples in Figures 2 & 3. The results of such outgassing modeling (without 10 - 15
minutes of vacuum pump prior to application) are presented in Figure 6 for the same time varying
temperature profile as that in Figure 3. Compared to as-received silicones in Figure 3, the vacuum
bake even with subsequent 30 ppm H2O re-exposure reduces the outgassing from M9787 and LL50
by ~ 20 times. For the same reason explained for Figures 2 & 3, even with moisture re-exposure
following the vacuum baking, a pulling of vacuum at room temperature over these silica-filled
silicones for 10 – 15 minutes is enough to remove all pooled water measured/estimated from DVS
experiment. With such a short vacuum pump prior to the start of sealed applications, the long-term
outgassing potential results essentially from tightly bound physisorbed and chemisorbed water and
are determined solely from TPD experiments, which yield ~ 118 ppm (vs. 217 ppm without the
pre-pump) and ~ 35 ppm (vs. 43 ppm without the pre-pump) for pre-baked M9787 and LL50,
respectively.

CONCLUSION

We have successfully simplified mathematics as well as provided illustrating examples so that the
generalized outgassing modeling of H2O release from silica-filled silicones become more intuitive
and user-friendly to scientists/engineers in the field. It is noted that due to OH bond rearrangement
upon prolonged vacuum baking, previously heat-treated silica-filled silicones with subsequent
H2O re-exposure do not share the same set of generalized outgassing kinetic parameters with as-
received silicones. In addition, compared to as-received silicones, even with 30 ppm H 2O re-
exposure after the vacuum baking steps, previously vacuum-baked M9787 and previously
vacuum-baked LL50 outgas by ~ 20 times less than their counterparts in the as-received states.
Therefore, vacuum baking of silica-filled silicones is a very effective technique to significantly
reduce H2O outgassing during application lifetime. In addition, 10 - 15 minutes of vacuum pump
at room temperature are enough to remove loose physisorbed water (AKA pooled water) estimated
from DVS experiment and the long-term outgassing potentials can be tracked just by TPD
experimentation/modeling alone. Without vacuum pump prior to sealed applications, the potential
outgassing from as-received M9787 and LL50 are ~ 5146 ppm and ~ 900 ppm, respectively. With
10 - 15 minutes vacuum pump prior to sealed application, the outgassing potential reduces to ~
570 ppm and 67 ppm for as-received M9787 and LL50, respectively. For previously vacuum-
baked silicones with subsequent limited moisture re-exposure, the outgassing potentials for M9787
and LL50 after 10-15 minutes of vacuum pump prior to the start of sealed applications are only ~
118 ppm and ~ 35 ppm, respectively. Without the pre-pump, the moisture potentials of previously
vacuum-baked silicones with subsequent limited moisture re-exposure are ~ 217 ppm and ~ 43
ppm for M9787 and LL50, respectively. For lowest possible outgassing potential in service life,
we recommend the use of previously vacuum baked silicones containing silica fillers with
hydrophobic treatment and a vacuum pump of 10 -15 minutes (or more if possible) prior to sealed
applications.
