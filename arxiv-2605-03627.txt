Stein Variational Gradient Descent dynamics for highly concentrated kernels
José A. Carrillo, Jakub Skrzeczkowski, Jethro Warnett

Abstract
. Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) is a widely used in practice algorithm for scalable sampling with deterministic particle updates. We study its behavior in the singular limit where the kernel bandwidth tends to zero. In this regime, we show that the nonlocal SVGD dynamics converge to a local evolution equation that can be formally interpreted as a Wasserstein gradient flow with quadratic mobility. We analyze this singular limit in two settings: integrable kernels and weighted kernels. In the weighted case, the proof is supported by recently established Stein-log-Sobolev inequalities, which provide the necessary functional control. Overall, our results clarify how SVGD collapses from a nonlocal interacting particle system to a local gradient-flow dynamics as the kernel concentrates.

1.
Introduction

Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) is a deterministic particle method for approximating a target distribution
\rho_{\infty}\,\propto\,e^{-V}
by transporting a collection of particles so as to decrease the Kullback–Leibler divergence to
\rho_{\infty}
(also known as the relative entropy) most rapidly within a reproducing-kernel Hilbert space (RKHS)
[
28
]
. Each discrete SVGD update is obtained by projecting the functional gradient of the
\mathrm{KL}
divergence onto a Stein-admissible vector field in an RKHS. Specifically, the update uses a positive-definite interaction kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
which both regularizes the particle interaction and defines the RKHS geometry used for descent. The SVGD algorithm is an effective deterministic sampler and has motivated a variety of applications including amortized sampling and generator training
[
20
]
, energy-based reinforcement learning
[
21
]
, model-predictive control
[
26
]
, constrained or trust-aware sampling
[
29
]
, and interacting-particle methods for quantization and measure approximation
[
33
]
.

Taken to the infinite-particle limit, the empirical particle system of SVGD formally converges to a nonlinear mean-field PDE. Writing
\rho_{t}
for the time density of the continuum limit, the mean-field SVGD dynamics can be written as the continuity equation

\partial_{t}\rho_{t}(x)=-\mathrm{div}\big(\rho_{t}(x)\,v[\rho_{t}](x)\big),

(1.1)

with the kernelized velocity field

\displaystyle v[\rho](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(K(x,y)\nabla\log\rho_{\infty}(y)+\nabla_{y}K(x,y)\Big)\rho(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,

so that the particle interpretation
\dot{X}_{t}=v[\rho_{t}](X_{t})
matches the empirical SVGD update in the large-population limit. This mean-field description has been developed and rigorously justified in several works, including the gradient-flow / KSD-decay viewpoint in
[
27
]
, the scaling-limit and well-posedness results in
[
30
]
, non-asymptotic population-limit analyses
[
25
]
, kernelized Wasserstein-gradient-flow interpretations
[
11
]
, and further convergence and stability improvements and geometric perspectives in
[
31
,
32
,
17
,
9
,
24
]
. Recently, there was some work done for the finite particle regime in
[
3
,
23
]
.

This paper investigates the behavior of the mean-field PDE (
1.1
) when the interaction kernel is translation invariant and becomes increasingly concentrated near the origin. This problem was motivated in
[
30
]
. Concretely, let

K_{\sigma}(x,y)=w(x)\,k_{\sigma}(x-y)\,w(y),\qquad k_{\sigma}(z)=\sigma^{-d}k(z/\sigma),

(1.2)

denote a family of translation-invariant kernels with
k_{\sigma}
concentrating to a Dirac mass as
\sigma\to 0
(here,
k
is a fixed, integrable function). Our goal is to understand whether, as
\sigma\to 0
, the nonlocal mean-field PDEs (
1.1
) with
K:=K_{\sigma}
converge in a suitable topology to a well-defined local limit. Furthermore, we want to identify the limiting equation and study its analytic properties.

To be more precise, we will consider two type of PDEs

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\varrho&=\mathrm{div}\Big(\varrho\;(k_{\sigma}\ast(\nabla\varrho+\varrho\,\nabla V))\Big)\\
\varrho(0,x)&=\varrho_{0}(x)\end{split}\begin{split}&\mbox{ on }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
&\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{split}

(1.3)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\rho&=\mathrm{div}\Big(\rho\;e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\;\big(k_{\sigma}\ast(\nabla\left(\rho\,e^{V}\right)e^{-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}})\big)\Big)\\
\rho(0,x)&=\rho_{0}(x)\end{split}\begin{split}&\mbox{ on }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
&\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{split}

(1.4)

Both equations (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) use the kernel in (
1.2
), where the first uses the weight
w=1
, and the second uses the weight

\displaystyle w(x)=e^{V(x)-\frac{\mathbb{V}(x)}{2}},

(1.5)

where
\mathbb{V}
is a strictly convex quadratic polynomial such that
V\geq\mathbb{V}
. In both cases,
k_{\sigma}
is always assumed to be of the form

\displaystyle k_{\sigma}=\omega_{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma},

with some further assumptions to be specified later. The first equation is a standard formulation of the continuous SVGD method
[
30
,
17
,
27
]
while the second is motivated by the recent paper
[
10
]
which has proven for the very first time the existence of a kernel that satisfies the so-called Stein-log-Sobolev inequality, conjectured in one space dimention in
[
17
]
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda\,\mathrm{KL}(\rho\,||\,\rho_{\infty})&\leq\mathbb{D}^{2}(\rho\,||\,\rho_{\infty})\\
&:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\rho}{\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{\infty}}\right)(x)\cdot K_{\sigma}(x,y)\nabla\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\rho}{\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{\infty}}\right)(y)\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{\infty}(y)\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{\infty}(x),\end{split}

(SLSI)

where
K_{\sigma}
is defined in (
1.2
) with the weight

given by (
1.5
) and
k\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})+L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with its Fourier transform satisfying the growth bounds

\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}_{0}}\,\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\leq\widehat{k}(\xi)\leq\mathcal{D}_{1}\,\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}},

(1.6)

and
\lambda=\lambda_{0}/\mathcal{D}_{0}
is independent of
\sigma\in(0,1)
(see Remark
2.8
). The inequality (
SLSI
) implies exponential rate of convergence of
\mathrm{KL}(\rho_{t}\,||\,\rho_{\infty})\to 0
whenever
\rho_{t}
solves (
1.4
).

In the limit
\sigma\to 0
, we expect to obtain for (
1.3
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\varrho&=\mathrm{div}\Big(\varrho^{2}\;\nabla\big(\ln(\varrho)+V\big)\Big)\\
\varrho(0,x)&=\varrho_{0}(x)\end{split}\begin{split}&\mbox{ on }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
&\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{split}

(1.7)

and for (
1.4
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\rho&=\mathrm{div}\Big(\rho^{2}\;e^{2V-\mathbb{V}}\;\nabla\big(\ln(\rho)+V\big)\Big)\\
\rho(0,x)&=\rho_{0}(x)\end{split}\begin{split}&\mbox{ on }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
&\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{split}

(1.8)

The target of the paper is to justify the limit
\sigma\to 0
rigorously. More precisely, we show that the weak solutions
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
,
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
of both equations (
1.3
) and
(
1.4
) converge to the weak solutions
\rho
,
\varrho
of both equations (
1.7
) and
(
1.8
)
respectively on bounded time intervals as
\sigma\to 0
in a suitable weak topology. However, we are only able to show
exponentially fast convergence of weak solutions
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
with respect to the Kullback-Leibler divergence to
\rho_{\infty}
as
t\to\infty
, uniformly with respect to
\sigma
. The same exponential rate holds for the solution
\rho
of the limit equation (
1.8
), which converges to
\rho_{\infty}
as
t\to\infty
. This is mainly due to an uniform in
\sigma
estimate on the Stein-log-Sobolev inequality (
SLSI
). The situation is different for the weak solutions
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
of (
1.3
) due to the lack of the corresponding Stein-log-Sobolev inequality (
SLSI
). As a consequence, we cannot prove asymptotic convergence of the weak solution
\varrho
of (
1.7
) to
\rho_{\infty}
in time. This remains as of now an open problem.

The problem is important for the mathematical analysis of the SVGD method. Actually, it was conjectured in
[
30
, Equation 9]
that if the kernel is scaled proportionally to the number of particles
N^{\beta}K(N^{\beta}\,\cdot)
, one expects the behavior of the particle system to resemble (
1.7
), and not (
1.3
). In the current work we address this question partially by studying the effect of the concentration of the kernels at the continuous level (
1.3
) and (
1.4
). The general problem of simultaneously studying concentration of the kernels and the mean-field limit from particles remains open and appears challenging. The main difficulty is that, for the interacting particle system, one lacks uniform estimates on the dissipation
\mathbb{D}^{2}(\rho_{t}\,||\,\rho_{\infty})
, which are essential to our approach. Additionally, for the equations (
1.3
) we can apply a Dobrushin type result to justify the joint-limit
N\rightarrow\infty
and
\sigma\to 0
of the particle system to the mean-field limit, see for instance
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
. This is not possible for (
1.4
) because of the lack of regularity for the kernel
k
implied by the growth estimates of its Fourier transform in (
1.6
).

Our work also addresses new challenges in the study of nonlocal-to-local limits for partial differential equations, where one rigorously justifies the passage to the limit in nonlocal models. Such limits, among others, connect different equations in mathematical physics, such as the Cahn–Hilliard equation
[
14
,
19
]
, and provide a rigorous foundation for numerical methods like the blob method
[
4
,
5
,
13
]
. The challenge in the analysis of nonlocal-to-local limits is to control the quantities related to
\rho^{\sigma}
or
\rho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}
, uniformly in
\sigma
. Our approach consists of two new novel ideas. First, in Lemma
3.6
we are able to deduce a uniform estimate on
\nabla\rho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}
by exploiting the uniform bound on dissipation
\mathbb{D}^{2}(\rho^{\sigma}\,||\,\rho_{\infty})
and viewing
(\rho^{\sigma}\,\nabla V)\ast\omega_{\sigma}
as its small perturbation. This estimate is only local in space which poses additional challenges with kernels which are globally supported. Thus, we have to carefully control the tails.

The second idea addresses so-called commutator estimates
[
12
,
16
,
18
]
. When passing to the limit in the distributional formulation of (
1.3
)–(
1.4
), we have to prove strong convergence of the term
(\nabla\varphi\,\rho^{\sigma})\ast\omega_{\sigma}
where
\varphi
is smooth and compactly supported. A priori, this is difficult because there is no compactness information available on
\rho^{\sigma}
itself while
\rho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}
is compact only locally in space. In Lemma
4.2
we propose a new idea based on expanding test function
\nabla\varphi(x-y)
into a Taylor’s series:

\displaystyle|(\nabla\varphi\,\rho^{\sigma})\ast\omega_{\sigma}-\nabla\varphi\,(\rho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=2}^{r}C_{k}\,|\nabla^{k}\varphi(x)|\,(\rho^{\sigma}\ast(\omega_{\sigma}\,|y|^{k-1}))+C_{r+1}\,\|\nabla^{r+1}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\rho^{\sigma}\ast(\omega_{\sigma}\,|y|^{r}).

The second term converges strongly to 0 because
r
is large. The first term can be handled by the estimate
|y|^{k-1}\leq|y|^{r}+1
and the Vitali convergence theorem since the test function localizes the reasoning to a compact set. We also introduce a new approach to estimating the commutator
(f\chi)\ast\omega_{\sigma}-\chi\,(f\ast\omega_{\sigma})
for a test function
\chi
, where
f
has only
H^{-1}
regularity in space, by working in Fourier variables, see Lemma
3.1
. This plays an important role in the compactness argument.

Moreover, the limiting PDE (
1.7
) can be viewed as the 2-Wasserstein gradient flow of the functional
\mathcal{F}[\rho]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\rho\,\log\rho+\rho\,V)\,\mathrm{d}x
with a convex mobility
m(\rho)=\rho^{2}
. However, a rigorous understanding of PDEs with nonlinear mobilities is still missing. In particular, PDEs of the form
\partial_{t}\rho=\mathrm{div}(m(\rho)\,\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta\rho})
have rigorous gradient flow interpretation only for concave mobilities
m

[
8
]
with additional results available for
m(\rho)=\rho^{\alpha}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)

[
15
,
6
]
. For convex mobilities like
m(\rho)=\rho^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>1
the available results deal only with
\mathcal{F}[\rho]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho\,(\mathcal{N}\ast\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x
, where
\mathcal{N}
is a Newtonian potential
[
15
,
7
]
. Therefore, we are able to expand on this literature by adding a new class of functionals.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce relevant notation and present the main results of the paper. Sections
3
,
4
and
5
are devoted to establishing crucial a priori estimates, uniform with respect to the parameter

. Specifically, in Section

we establish some basic estimates, in Section

. Finally, in Section

we combine all the previous results to prove Theorems
2.5
and
2.9
. Additionally, in Appendix
A
we present the Bessel potential as an example of a kernel that satisfies Assumption
2.1
. Appendix
B
proves the existence of weak solutions to (
1.3
) and Appendix

contains necessary auxiliary results that do not fit the rest of the paper.

\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(T,x)\,\varrho_{T}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(0,x)\,\varrho_{0}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{t}\psi\;\varrho\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varrho\,\nabla\psi\cdot(\nabla\varrho+\varrho\nabla V)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(2.15)

The curve is absolutely continuous with respect to the Wasserstein metric
W_{1}
and it enjoys the following regularity, for all
T,R>0
,

\displaystyle\varrho+|\varrho\ln(\varrho)|+|\varrho V|\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\,L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),

(2.16)

\displaystyle|\nabla\varrho+\varrho\nabla V|\in L^{2}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(2.17)

\displaystyle\varrho\in L^{2}(0,T;\,H^{1}(B_{R})).

(2.18)

Additionally,
\varrho
satisfies an energy dissipation type inequality

\mathrm{KL}(\varrho_{t}\,||\,\rho_{\infty})+\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla\varrho_{s}+\varrho_{s}\nabla V|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\mathrm{KL}(\varrho_{0}\,||\,\rho_{\infty}).

(2.19)

2.1.
Notation

Let us first solidify some conventions and notation used throughout the paper. All objects live on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with variables
x
and
(t,x)
respectively. For
R>0
we denote the open ball by
B_{R}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ |x|<R\}
. The Schwartz space is written
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with dual
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The space
\mathrm{Lip}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
consists of
L
–Lipschitz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the space of Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, endowed with the Kantorovich–Rubinstein distance, which coincides with the
1
–Wasserstein metric:

W_{1}(\mu,\nu):=\sup_{f\in\mathrm{Lip}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right|.

A curve
[0,T]\ni t\mapsto\mu_{t}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is called absolutely continuous in
(\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),W_{1})
if

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}<\infty

and if there exists
f\in L^{1}(0,T)
such that for all
0\leq t\leq s\leq T
,

W_{1}(\mu_{t},\mu_{s})\leq\int_{t}^{s}f(r)\,\mathrm{d}r.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
we use the standard Sobolev space

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}\widehat{u}(\xi)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}.

If
X
is a Banach space of functions or distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the Bochner space
L^{p}(0,T;X)
carries the norm

\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T;X)}=\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|_{X}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}.

We abbreviate these norms using subscripts on
t
and
x
, e.g.

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{L_{t}^{p}H_{x}^{1}}:=\|\,\cdot\,\|_{L^{p}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))},\qquad\|\,\cdot\,\|_{H^{1}}:=\|\,\cdot\,\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

The same conventions apply to all other function spaces.
For any Banach space
X
, we write
X^{\ast}
for its dual.
Convergences are denoted by
f_{n}\to f
(strong) and
f_{n}\rightharpoonup f
(weak). For
p,q,r\in[1,\infty]
with
1+\tfrac{1}{r}=\tfrac{1}{p}+\tfrac{1}{q}
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the Young inequality yields

(f\ast g)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x-y)\,g(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\qquad\|f\ast g\|_{L^{r}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}}.

Given measurable functions
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define temporal and spatial mollification by

(f\ast\varphi)(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s,x)\,\varphi(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad(f\ast\omega)(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,y)\,\omega(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

The context will always make clear which variable is being mollified.
For measurable sets
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote the indicator function by
\mathds{1}_{E}
. For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})+L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we use the Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)=\,\widehat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\,f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

For a multiindex
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and a test function
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\partial^{\alpha}\psi=\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{x_{d}}^{\alpha_{d}}\psi,\qquad(y^{\alpha}\psi)(x)=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}\psi(x),

and we write

\alpha!:=\alpha_{1}!\cdots\alpha_{d}!,\qquad|\alpha|:=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{d}.

Throughout the paper we use the following interval notation:

\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}d&\mathcal{I}_{d}&\mathcal{J}_{d}&\mathcal{K}_{d}\\
\hline\cr 1&[1,\infty]&[1,3]&[6,\infty)\\
2&[1,\infty)&[1,3)&(6,\infty)\\
\geq 3&[1,\tfrac{2d}{\,d-2\,}]&[1,2+\tfrac{2}{d}]&[2d+2,\infty)\end{array}

Given two values
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
we use as shorthand
a\vee b=\max\{a,b\}
. We fix a smooth cutoff
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\chi=1
on
B_{1}
,
\chi=0
outside
B_{2}
, and
0\leq\chi\leq 1
.
For
R>0
we then define the rescaled cutoff

\chi_{R}(x):=\chi(x/R).

(2.20)

3.
Uniform estimates

In this section we establish estimates that will be used later. Reader should note that some estimates listed here are only proved for one of the equations (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).
Throughout this paper we assume that
\sigma\in(0,1)
and we define
\omega_{\sigma}(x):=\sigma^{-d}(x/\sigma)
. The following technical lemma will be a crucial part for proving the a priori estimates and the commutator arguments for Theorem
2.5
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let (
2.1
) hold. Let
f\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, we have that
f\ast\omega_{\sigma}\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\sigma
and
f
(but dependent on
\chi
) such that

\displaystyle\|(f\chi)\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\leq C\|f\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

(3.1)

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\;\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\\
\|f\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\|(f\chi)\ast\omega_{\sigma}-\chi\,(f\ast\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}=0.

(3.2)

Remark 3.2
.

The convergence statement (
3.2
) is very delicate. One might try to obtain this result by writing

(f\chi)\ast\omega_{\sigma}-\chi\,(f\ast\omega_{\sigma})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(y)\,(\chi(y)-\chi(x))\,\omega_{\sigma}(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

However, in order to further estimate this term, one would need to impose additional sign assumptions on
f
and
\omega_{\sigma}
. This does not seem reasonable as
f
is only a distribution.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

By using the Plancherel Theorem and the convolution formula, it is sufficient to prove (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) in the Fourier space. This means we must show

\displaystyle\|(\hat{f}\ast\hat{\chi})\,\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,\xi}^{2}}\leq C\,\|\hat{f}\,\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\;\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\\
\|\hat{f}\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,\xi}^{2}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\|(\hat{f}\ast\hat{\chi})\,\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}-(\hat{f}\hat{\omega}_{\sigma})\ast\hat{\chi}\|_{L_{t,\xi}^{2}}=0.

We may substitute
\hat{f}\,\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}
by
g\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, as
\hat{f}\,\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
by our assumptions. Thus, we can rephrase the statements (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) as

\displaystyle\|((g/\hat{\omega_{\sigma}})\ast\hat{\chi})\,\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,\xi}^{2}}\leq C\,\|g\|_{L_{t,\xi}^{2}},

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\;\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\\
\|g\|_{L_{t,\xi}^{2}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\|((g/\hat{\omega}_{\sigma})\ast\hat{\chi})\,\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}-g\ast\hat{\chi}\|_{L_{t,\xi}^{2}}=0,

or equivalently in terms of linear operators

\displaystyle\|T_{\sigma}g\|_{L_{t,\xi}^{2}}\leq C\,\|g\|_{L_{t,\xi}^{2}},

\displaystyle\|T_{\sigma}-T_{0}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }\sigma\rightarrow 0\text{ as a linear operator on }L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where we define the operators on
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle T_{\sigma}g(\xi):=\big(((g/\hat{\omega}_{\sigma})\ast\hat{\chi})\,\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}\big)(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K_{\sigma}(\xi,\eta)\,g(\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta,\quad T_{0}g:=g\ast\hat{\chi},

with the kernel defined by

\displaystyle K_{\sigma}(\xi,\eta):=\frac{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\xi)}{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\eta)}\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta).

Step 1:
T_{\sigma}
is a bounded operator.

Using
|\eta|^{2}\leq 2|\xi-\eta|^{2}+2|\xi|^{2}
we find

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1+\sigma^{2}|\eta|^{2}}{1+\sigma^{2}|\xi|^{2}}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1+2\sigma^{2}|\xi|^{2}+2\sigma^{2}|\xi-\eta|^{2}}{1+\sigma^{2}|\xi|^{2}}}\leq\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}|\xi-\eta|^{2}}.

(3.3)

Hence, we discover the uniform upper bound

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\xi)}{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\eta)}\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta)\right|\leq\mathcal{D}_{0}\mathcal{D}_{1}\sqrt{\frac{1+\sigma^{2}|\eta|^{2}}{1+\sigma^{2}|\xi|^{2}}}\,|\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta)|\leq\sqrt{2}\,\mathcal{D}_{0}\mathcal{D}_{1}\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}|\xi-\eta|^{2}}\,|\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta)|.

We now estimate
T_{\sigma}g
by using the same idea as in the Schur’s test (see for instance
[
22
, Theorem 5.2]
). We estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K_{\sigma}(\xi,\eta)|\,|g(\eta)|\,\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K_{\sigma}(\xi,\eta)|\,\mathrm{d}\eta\right)^{1/2}\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K_{\sigma}(\xi,\eta)|\,|g(\eta)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\right)^{1/2}.

(3.4)

Then, by using Fubini’s Theorem, and the fact that
\mathcal{\chi}
is a Schwartz function, the
L^{2}
norm is controlled by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|T_{\sigma}g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\xi}}&\leq\left(\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K_{\sigma}(\xi,\eta)|\,\mathrm{d}\eta\right)\left(\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K_{\sigma}(\xi,\eta)|\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\\
&\leq 2(\mathcal{D}_{0}\mathcal{D}_{1})^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}|\xi|^{2}}\,|\hat{\chi}(\xi)|\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{2}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\xi}}.\end{split}

(3.5)

Integrating in time yields the desired boundedness (
3.1
).

Step 2:
T_{\sigma}-T_{0}
converges to the null-operator.
We use again the same boundedness idea in (
3.4
) and (
3.5
). This means it suffices to prove the convergence

\limsup_{\sigma\rightarrow 0}\;\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K_{\sigma}(\xi,\eta)-\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta)|\,\mathrm{d}\eta=0,\quad\limsup_{\sigma\rightarrow 0}\;\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K_{\sigma}(\xi,\eta)-\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta)|\,\mathrm{d}\xi=0.

(3.6)

To that end, fix any
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\hat{\chi}
is Schwartz, choose
R>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{|z|>R}\sqrt{1+|z|^{2}}\,|\hat{\chi}(z)|\,\mathrm{d}z<\varepsilon.

Step 2.1: Far from the diagonal.
We use the same estimate (
3.3
), then we discover the uniform upper bound

\displaystyle\left|\left(\frac{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\xi)}{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\eta)}-1\right)\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta)\right|\leq\left(1+\frac{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\xi)}{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\eta)}\right)\,|\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\mathcal{D}_{0}\mathcal{D}_{1}\sqrt{\frac{1+\sigma^{2}|\eta|^{2}}{1+\sigma^{2}|\xi|^{2}}}\right)\,|\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta)|\leq C\sqrt{1+|\xi-\eta|^{2}}\,|\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta)|,

for some constant
C>0
independent of
\sigma
and
\varepsilon
(recalling that
\sigma<1
). Taking the integral now yields

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi-\eta|>R}\left|\left(\frac{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\xi)}{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\eta)}-1\right)\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta)\right|\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leq C\int_{|\xi-\eta|>R}\sqrt{1+|\xi-\eta|^{2}}\,|\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta)|\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{|z|>R}\sqrt{1+|z|^{2}}\,|\hat{\chi}(z)|\,\mathrm{d}z\leq C\varepsilon.

The same bound holds if we integrate in
\eta
instead.

Step 2.2: Near the diagonal.
Take any
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
|\xi-\eta|\leq R
. Let
\theta
be a modulus of continuity for
\zeta
, this means
|\zeta(\xi)-\zeta(\eta)|\leq\theta(|\xi-\eta|)
,
\theta
is non-decreasing and
\theta(t)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow 0
. Then, we estimate

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\zeta(\sigma\xi)}{\zeta(\sigma\eta)}-1\right|=\frac{|\zeta(\sigma\xi)-\zeta(\sigma\eta)|}{\zeta(\sigma\eta)}\leq\mathcal{D}_{0}\,\theta(\sigma|\xi-\eta|)\leq\mathcal{D}_{0}\,\theta(\sigma R).

Define the function
\phi(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\ln(1+|t|^{2})
. Since
|\phi^{\prime}(t)|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
, we estimate

\displaystyle\left|\ln\frac{\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}|\eta|^{2}}}{\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}|\xi|^{2}}}\right|=|\phi(\sigma\eta)-\phi(\sigma\xi)|\leq\frac{\sigma}{2}|\xi-\eta|\leq\frac{\sigma R}{2}.

We use this to derive two bounds (recalling that
\sigma<1
and
e^{x}
is
e^{\alpha}
-Lipschitz on
(-\infty,\alpha]
)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}|\eta|^{2}}}{\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}|\xi|^{2}}}-1\right|\leq\left|e^{\ln\frac{\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}|\eta|^{2}}}{\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}|\xi|^{2}}}}-e^{0}\right|\leq\frac{\sigma R}{2}\,e^{\frac{R}{2}},\qquad\left|\frac{\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}|\eta|^{2}}}{\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}|\xi|^{2}}}\right|\leq e^{\frac{\sigma R}{2}}\leq e^{\frac{R}{2}}.

Thus we can bound the error close to the diagonal by

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\xi)}{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\eta)}-1\right|\,|\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta)|=\left|\left(\frac{\zeta(\sigma\xi)}{\zeta(\sigma\eta)}-1\right)\frac{\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}|\eta|^{2}}}{\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}|\xi|^{2}}}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}|\eta|^{2}}}{\sqrt{1+\sigma^{2}|\xi|^{2}}}-1\right)\right|\,\|\hat{\chi}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathcal{D}_{0}\,\theta(\sigma R)+\frac{\sigma R}{2}\right)e^{\frac{R}{2}}\,\|\hat{\chi}\|_{\infty}.

Step 3: Final estimate.
By combining the bound away from the diagonal and near the diagonal, we compute the integral

\displaystyle\limsup_{\sigma\rightarrow 0}\;\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\left(\frac{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\xi)}{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\eta)}-1\right)\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta)\right|\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\sigma\rightarrow 0}\;\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|\xi-\eta|>R}\left|\left(\frac{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\xi)}{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\eta)}-1\right)\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta)\right|\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\int_{|\xi-\eta|\leq R}\left|\left(\frac{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\xi)}{\hat{\omega}_{\sigma}(\eta)}-1\right)\hat{\chi}(\xi-\eta)\right|\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\sigma\rightarrow 0}\;C\varepsilon+C\,R^{d}\left(\mathcal{D}_{0}\,\theta(\sigma R)+\frac{\sigma R}{2}\right)e^{\frac{R}{2}}\,\|\hat{\chi}\|_{\infty}=C\varepsilon,

for some constant
C>0
independent of
\sigma
and
\varepsilon
. The same bound holds if we integrate in
\eta
instead of
\xi
. As
\varepsilon
can be chosen as small as desired, we have proven (
3.6
).
∎

Lemma 3.3

(Basic gradient flow estimates for (

) and (

))
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold. Let
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
be the weak solutions to (

the following assertions hold:

(GF1)

\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
,

,

Proof.

We focus on the proof of

(3.7)

) follows by multiplying (

In the following lemma we derive estimates specifically for the weak solutions

Lemma 3.4

(Estimates for solutions of (
1.4
))
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold and let
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
be the weak solution to (
1.4
). Then, for any fixed
T,R>0
the following assertions are true:

(S1)

\{\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

(S2)

\{(\nabla\rho^{\sigma}+\rho^{\sigma}\nabla V)e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;\,H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
;

(S3)

\{(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;\,H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, where
\chi_{R}
is defined in (
2.20
), with the bound depending on
R>0
.

Proof.

We first observe that, by assumption (
2.1
), we have for
\sigma\in(0,1)

\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{1+\sigma^{2}\,|\xi|^{2}}\leq\mathcal{D}_{0}^{2}\,|\widehat{\omega}_{\sigma}|^{2}.

(3.8)

To see
(S2)
, we let
f^{\sigma}=\nabla(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V})e^{-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}
, change to Fourier variables and apply (
3.8
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|f^{\sigma}\|_{H^{-1}_{x}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\widehat{f^{\sigma}}(\xi)|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leq\mathcal{D}_{0}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widehat{f^{\sigma}}(\xi)|^{2}\,|\widehat{\omega}_{\sigma}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

which is bounded in
L^{2}(0,T)
by
(GF2)
. To see
(S1)
, we note that
[
10
, p. 19-20]
gives a constant
C
such that for all probability measures
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
g\,e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\nabla(ge^{V})e^{-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\in H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

\|g\,e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\mathcal{F}(\nabla(g\,e^{V})e^{-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}})(\xi)|^{2}}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right).

Applying this inequality with
g=\rho^{\sigma}
we arrive at
(S1)
by applying
(S2)
. Next, for the bound
(S3)
we first observe that the sequence
\{(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
by
(S1)
and the Young convolution inequality. Next, for the gradient we compute the identity

\displaystyle\nabla((\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma})=((\nabla\rho^{\sigma}+\rho^{\sigma}\nabla V)e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma}+(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\nabla(\chi_{R}e^{-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}})e^{\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}})\ast\omega_{\sigma}.

(3.9)

For the first term on the RHS of (
3.9
), there exists by Lemma
3.1
a constant
C>0
independent of
\sigma
such that the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\|((\nabla\rho^{\sigma}+\rho^{\sigma}\nabla V)e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\leq C\|((\nabla\rho^{\sigma}+\rho^{\sigma}\nabla V)e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

We observe that the RHS is uniformly bounded by
(GF2)
of Lemma
3.3
. For the second term on the RHS of (
3.9
), we observe that
\nabla(\chi_{R}e^{-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}})e^{\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
so by using the Young convolution inequality and
(S1)
, we discover that
\{(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\nabla(\chi_{R}\,e^{-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}})e^{\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This concludes the proof of
(S3)
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We have used Lemma
3.1
in order to derive a

bound on the sequence
\{(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
so that we can obtain a strongly convergent subsequence in Lemma
6.2
.

Lemma 3.6

(Estimates for solutions of (
1.3
))
.

Let Assumption
2.3
hold and let
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
be the weak solution to (
1.3
). Then, for any fixed
T,R>0
, the sequence
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(B_{R}))
.

Proof.

We will employ the estimate in
(GF2)
of Lemma
3.3
and control the sequence
\{(\varrho^{\sigma}\,\nabla V)\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
. Our reasoning is divided into three steps.

Step 1: Pointwise estimate on
\{(\varrho^{\sigma}\,\nabla V)\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}
.

We first show that there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\sigma
such that

\displaystyle|(\varrho^{\sigma}\,\nabla V)\ast\omega_{\sigma}|\leq C(1+|x|^{p_{0}^{\ast}})\,(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})+C\,(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(|y|^{p_{0}^{\ast}\vee m_{0}}\omega_{\sigma})).

(3.10)

To that end, we first employ (
2.5
) from Assumption
2.3
to derive the bound

\displaystyle|(\varrho^{\sigma}\,\nabla V)\ast\omega_{\sigma}|\leq(\varrho^{\sigma}\,[1+|\nabla V|^{p_{0}}])\ast\omega_{\sigma}\leq(1+C_{V})\,(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})+C_{V}\,(\varrho^{\sigma}V)\ast\omega_{\sigma}.

Next, by Remark
2.4
and Assumption
2.3
, we use the polynomial upper bound (
2.8
) to further estimate the second term on the RHS by

\displaystyle(\varrho^{\sigma}V)\ast\omega_{\sigma}\leq C_{0}\,(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})+C_{0}\,(\varrho^{\sigma}|x|^{p_{0}^{\ast}})\ast\omega_{\sigma}.

We use the bound
|x|^{p_{0}^{\ast}}\leq 2^{p_{0}^{\ast}-1}|x-y|^{p_{0}^{\ast}}+2^{p_{0}^{\ast}-1}|y|^{p_{0}^{\ast}}
to further bound the second term on the RHS by

\displaystyle(\varrho^{\sigma}|x|^{p_{0}^{\ast}})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\leq 2^{p_{0}^{\ast}-1}\,|x|^{p_{0}^{\ast}}\,(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})+2^{p_{0}^{\ast}-1}\,\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(|y|^{p_{0}^{\ast}}\omega_{\sigma}).

In the last step we need to bound the second term of the RHS again by

\displaystyle\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(|y|^{p_{0}^{\ast}}\omega_{\sigma})\leq\varrho^{\sigma}\ast((1+|y|^{p_{0}^{\ast}\vee m_{0}})\,\omega_{\sigma}).

By combining this chain of inequalities, we discover that (
3.10
) holds true. We remark that all the above steps require
\omega_{\sigma}\geq 0
.

Step 2: Application of pointwise estimate.
We combine the triangle inequality and (
3.10
) to derive the estimate

\displaystyle\|\nabla\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times B_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\nabla\varrho^{\sigma}+\varrho^{\sigma}\,\nabla V)\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times B_{R})}+C\,\|\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{p_{0}^{\ast}\vee m_{0}}\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times B_{R})}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq}+C\,\|(1+|x|^{p_{0}^{\ast}})\,(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times B_{R})}.

The first two terms on the RHS are bounded by
(GF2)
of Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
C.3
together with assumption (
2.3
). For the last one we apply Lemma
C.2
to find, for some
\delta>0
to be chosen later, constants
C,C_{\delta}>0
independent of
\sigma
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times B_{R})}\leq C+C\,\|\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times B_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C+C\delta\,\|\nabla\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times B_{R})}+C\,C_{\delta}\,\|\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{1}(B_{R}))}.

Observe that by
(GF1)
of Lemma
3.3
we know that
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{1}(B_{R}))
. Then, choosing
\delta<1/C
, we conclude the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.7

(A posteriori estimates for (
1.3
) and (
1.4
))
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold and let
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
be the weak solutions to (
1.4
). Then, for all fixed
q\in\mathcal{I}_{d}
and
r\in\mathcal{J}_{d}
the following assertions hold:

(AP1)

\{(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;\,L^{1}(B_{R}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\,L^{q}(B_{R}))
,

(AP2)

\{(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{r}((0,T)\times B_{R})
,

where
\chi_{R}
is defined in (
2.20
). Analogue assertions hold under Assumption
2.3
for
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
the weak solutions of (
1.3
), if we replace
\{(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
with
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
.

Proof.

The proofs for
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
and
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
are analogue so we focus on the former and briefly point out the differences for the latter. First, we prove
(AP1)
. We use
(S3)
of Lemma
3.4
(respectively we use Lemma
3.6
) and observe that the Sobolev embedding theorem guarantees the continuous embedding
H^{1}(B_{R})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(B_{R})
so that
\{(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;\,L^{q}(B_{R}))
. Then, as
\chi_{R}
is compactly supported we can use the Young convolution inequality and a change of variables to derive

\displaystyle\|(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{1}}\leq\|e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\|\omega\|_{L^{1}}\,\|\rho^{\sigma}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{1}},

which concludes the proof of
(AP1)
. In order to prove
(AP2)
, we perform the interpolation between the bounds in
L^{\infty}(0,T;\,L^{1}(B_{R}))
and
L^{2}(0,T;\,L^{q}(B_{R}))
, obtained in
(AP1)
. We obtain that
\{(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{p_{\theta}}((0,T)\times B_{R})
whenever

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{1-\theta}{\infty}=\frac{\theta}{q}+\frac{1-\theta}{1}\text{ and }\theta\in[0,1].

By solving this equation, we obtain
p_{\theta}=3-\frac{2}{q}
, yielding that the set of admissible exponents
p_{\theta}
is
\mathcal{J}_{d}
. With this we conclude the proof.
∎

4.
Commutator arguments

In this section, we develop several commutator arguments. In the weak formulations of (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), the test function
\varphi
appears inside the convolution with the kernel
\omega_{\sigma}
. This prevents us from applying estimates that are local in space, since such estimates require the test function to be outside the convolution. To overcome this difficulty, we employ a commutator argument that allows us to move the test function outside the convolution with
\omega_{\sigma}
. We first carry out this argument for the weak solutions
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
of (
1.4
), and then for the weak solutions
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
of (
1.3
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold, let
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
be the weak solutions to (
1.4
), let
q=\frac{p_{0}}{p_{0}-1}
with
p_{0}
defined in
(A2)
, and let
T,R>0
. Then,

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\;\;\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in L^{q}(0,T;\,W_{0}^{1,\infty}(B_{R}))\\
\|\varphi\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{1,\infty}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\;\;\|(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\varphi)\ast\omega_{\sigma}-\varphi\,((\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}=0.

(4.1)

Proof.

First, we observe that as
\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\subseteq[0,T]\times B_{R}
, we have that
\varphi=\varphi\,\chi_{R}
. We estimate pointwisely neglecting the variable
t
for simplicity:

\displaystyle|((\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\varphi)\ast\omega_{\sigma})(x)-\varphi(x)\,((\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma})(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{\sigma}(x-y)\,e^{V(x-y)-\frac{\mathbb{V}(x-y)}{2}}\chi_{R}(x-y)\;|\varphi(x-y)-\varphi(x)|\;|\omega_{\sigma}(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\;((\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast|y\,\omega_{\sigma}|)(x)\;\|\varphi\|_{W_{x}^{1,\infty}}.

This means it suffices to show

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\;\;\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in L^{q}(0,T;\,W_{0}^{1,\infty}(B_{R}))\\
\|\varphi\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{1,\infty}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\;\;\|((\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast|y\,\omega_{\sigma}|)\;\|\varphi\|_{W_{x}^{1,\infty}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}=0.

(4.2)

We know that
\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\,L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\,L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
by
(S1)
from Lemma
3.4
and as
\chi_{R}
is compactly supported. Thus, we can use the interpolation theorem to get the inclusion

\displaystyle\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R}\in L^{r}(0,T;L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),

(4.3)

where
s\in(1,2)
and
r=\frac{s}{s-1}
. We choose
s
such that

\displaystyle 1+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{p_{0}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\frac{1}{s}=\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{0}},

(4.4)

which is possible as
p_{0}\in(1,2)
. We use (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and apply the Young convolution inequality to get

\displaystyle\|(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast|y\,\omega_{\sigma}|\|_{L^{r}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\leq\|\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R}\|_{L^{r}_{t}L^{s}_{x}}\,\|y\,\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{p_{0}}_{x}}.

Next, we wish to apply Hölder in time so that

\displaystyle\|((\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast|y\,\omega_{\sigma}|)\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\leq\|(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast|y\,\omega_{\sigma}|\|_{L^{r}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}_{t}W_{x}^{1,\infty}}

whenever the temporal exponents satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{q}.

But we already have
1/r=1-1/s
, so combining with (
4.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{2}-\left(1-\frac{1}{s}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}+\left(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{0}}\right)=1-\frac{1}{p_{0}}=\frac{1}{q},

by the choice
q=\frac{p_{0}}{p_{0}-1}
. Thus in order to prove that (
4.2
) holds, it suffices to show
y\,\omega_{\sigma}\to 0
in
L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. To that end, let us compute the
L^{p_{0}}
–norm of
y\,\omega_{\sigma}
explicitly. We use the change of variables
y=\sigma z
to get

\displaystyle\|y\,\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{p_{0}}}^{p_{0}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|y|^{p_{0}}\,|\omega(y/\sigma)|^{p_{0}}\,\sigma^{-dp_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}y=\sigma^{p_{0}-dp_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{p_{0}}\,|\omega(z)|^{p_{0}}\,\sigma^{d}\,\mathrm{d}z=\sigma^{p_{0}-dp_{0}+d}\,\|y\,\omega\|_{L^{p_{0}}}^{p_{0}}.

The claim now immediately follows from (
2.2
) of Assumption
2.1
and the fact that

\displaystyle 1-d\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{0}}\right)>0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad p_{0}<\frac{d}{d-1},

where we treat
\infty=\frac{1}{0}
(note also that
\frac{2(d+1)}{2d+1}<\frac{d}{d-1}
). As this estimate holds uniformly for all
\varphi
with
\|\varphi\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{1,\infty}}\leq 1
, we conclude the proof by duality.
∎

The following lemma is concerned with solutions
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
to (
1.3
) for which we do not have a priori estimates for
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
, but only for the mollified quantities
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
. Therefore, we need to establish a stronger commutator result in which the localization is completely removed from the convolution.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let Assumption
2.3
hold, let
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
be the weak solutions to (
1.3
), let
T,R>0
and
q\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
. Then, with
m_{0}
as in Assumption
2.3
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\;\;\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in L^{q}(0,T;\,W_{0}^{m_{0},\infty}(B_{R}))\\
\|\varphi\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{m_{0},\infty}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\;\;\|(\varrho^{\sigma}\varphi)\ast\omega_{\sigma}-\varphi\,(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}=0.

(4.5)

Proof.

For this proof it is important to observe that there exists
p\in\mathcal{J}_{d}
such that
p>2
with
\tfrac{2}{p}+\tfrac{2}{q}=1
. This follows by checking the value of
p=\frac{2q}{q-2}
for the boundary values of
q\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
. We will work with a fixed function
\varphi\in L^{q}(0,T;\,W_{0}^{m_{0},\infty}(B_{R}))
with
\|\varphi\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{m_{0},\infty}}\leq 1
. Let us give the proof strategy. We carry out the argument in four steps. First, we approximate
\varphi
with a smooth compactly supported function, so that in Step 2 we can apply a Taylor’s approximation argument. Finally, Steps 3 and 4 are devoted to showing that error terms converge to zero.

Step 1: Approximation of
\varphi
.
We choose a smooth compactly supported function
\gamma~\in~C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\gamma\geq 0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\gamma\,\mathrm{d}x=1
. We define the mollification kernel
\gamma_{\varepsilon}(x):=\varepsilon^{-d}\gamma(x/\varepsilon)
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then, we smooth the function
\varphi
in the spatial variable by
\varphi_{\varepsilon}:=\varphi\ast\gamma_{\varepsilon}
. For any multiindex
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
|\alpha|\leq m
, we note that

\displaystyle\|\varphi-\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{\infty}}\leq\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{\infty}}\|y\,\gamma_{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\leq\varepsilon\|\varphi\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{m_{0}+1,\infty}}\|y\,\gamma\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\leq C\,\varepsilon,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{\infty}}\leq\|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{\infty}}\|\gamma\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\leq 1,

(4.7)

where
C\geq 1
is a constant independent of
\varphi
and
\varepsilon
. Then, we use the Young convolution inequality, the Hölder inequality,
(GF1)
of Lemma
3.3
and the change of variables formula to estimate

\displaystyle\|(\varrho^{\sigma}\varphi)\ast\omega_{\sigma}-(\varrho^{\sigma}\varphi^{\varepsilon})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\leq\|\varrho^{\sigma}(\varphi-\varphi^{\varepsilon})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{1}}\|\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi-\varphi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{\infty}}\|\varrho^{\sigma}\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{1}}\|\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C\,\sigma^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|\varphi-\varphi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{\infty}}.

Furthermore, by the Hölder inequality, the fact that
\varphi-\varphi^{\varepsilon}
is supported on
[0,T]\times\overline{B_{R+1}}
and
(AP2)
of Lemma
3.7
we can estimate

\displaystyle\|\varphi(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})-\varphi^{\varepsilon}(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\leq\|\varphi-\varphi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{\infty}}\|\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\,L^{2}(B_{R+1}))}

\displaystyle\leq|B_{R+1}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\|\varphi-\varphi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{\infty}}\|\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}((0,T)\times B_{R+1})}\leq C\|\varphi-\varphi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{\infty}},

where
C>0
is some constant independent of
\sigma
and
\varepsilon
. Hence, we deduce the following error estimate

\|(\varrho^{\sigma}\varphi)\ast\omega_{\sigma}-\varphi\,(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\leq\|(\varrho^{\sigma}\varphi^{\varepsilon})\ast\omega_{\sigma}-\varphi^{\varepsilon}(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\!+\!2C\,\sigma^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,\|\varphi-\varphi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{\infty}}.

(4.8)

Now, by using (
4.6
) we will choose
\varepsilon=\sigma^{d}
so that
\sigma^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,\|\varphi-\varphi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{\infty}}\to 0
.

Step 2: Taylor’s expansion.
For a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, we use the Taylor’s expansion and derive the pointwise estimate

\begin{split}&((\varrho_{t}^{\sigma}\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon})\ast\omega_{\sigma})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varrho_{t}^{\sigma}(x-y)\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x-y)\omega_{\sigma}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x)(\varrho_{t}^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})(x)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\\
1\leq|\alpha|<m_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}\partial^{\alpha}\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x)\;(\varrho_{t}^{\sigma}\ast(y^{\alpha}\omega_{\sigma}))(x)+R_{t}^{\sigma,\varepsilon}(x),\end{split}

(4.9)

where we define the remainder by

\displaystyle R_{t}^{\sigma,\varepsilon}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\\
|\alpha|=m_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{m_{0}}{\alpha!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varrho_{t}^{\sigma}(x-y)\,y^{\alpha}\omega_{\sigma}(y)\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{m_{0}-1}\partial^{\alpha}\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x-sy)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y.

Step 3: Convergence of an individual summand
\alpha
.
We will prove that for each
|\alpha|<m_{0}

\|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi^{\varepsilon}\;(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(y^{\alpha}\omega_{\sigma}))\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\to 0\mbox{ as }\sigma\to 0.

(4.10)

We first estimate using the Hölder inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi^{\varepsilon}\;(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(y^{\alpha}\omega_{\sigma}))\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\leq\|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{\infty}}\;\|\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(y^{\alpha}\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2}(B_{R}))}\\
&\leq C\,\|\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(y^{\alpha}\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2}(B_{R}))}\leq C\,\|\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(|y|^{k}\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{2}(B_{R}))},\end{split}

(4.11)

where
C>0
is some constant independent of
\varepsilon
and
\sigma
and
k:=|\alpha|
. This estimate removes the dependence on
\varepsilon
. Next, we observe that

\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{k}\,\omega_{\sigma})\to 0\mbox{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;\,L^{1}(B_{R})).

(4.12)

Indeed, this is a consequence of
(GF1)
of Lemma
3.3
and
(B2)
of Assumption
2.3
in conjunction with the Young convolution inequality, and then employing change of variables:

\displaystyle\|\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{k}\,\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varrho^{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\,\||x|^{k}\,\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{k}\,\|\varrho_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\||x|^{k}\,\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to 0.

As
|x|^{k}\leq 1+|x|^{m_{0}}
we estimate

0\leq\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{k}\,\omega_{\sigma})\leq\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}+\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{m_{0}}\,\omega_{\sigma}).

(4.13)

This estimate inspires the following partition

\displaystyle A_{\sigma}

\displaystyle:=\{\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\geq\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{m_{0}}\,\omega_{\sigma})\}\cap[0,T]\times B_{R},

\displaystyle B_{\sigma}

\displaystyle:=\{\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}<\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{m_{0}}\,\omega_{\sigma})\}\cap[0,T]\times B_{R}.

Then on the set
A_{\sigma}
we have the estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|(\rho^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{k}\omega_{\sigma}))\mathds{1}_{A_{\sigma}}\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{2}}^{p}=\int_{0}^{T}\|(\rho_{t}^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{k}\omega_{\sigma}))\mathds{1}_{A_{\sigma}}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|(\rho_{t}^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{k}\omega_{\sigma}))\mathds{1}_{A_{\sigma}}\|_{L_{x}^{1}}^{\theta p}\|(\rho_{t}^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{k}\omega_{\sigma}))\mathds{1}_{A_{\sigma}}\|_{L_{x}^{p}}^{(1-\theta)p}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\|(\rho^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{k}\omega_{\sigma}))\mathds{1}_{A_{\sigma}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{1}}^{\theta p}\;\int_{0}^{T}\|(\rho_{t}^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{k}\omega_{\sigma}))\mathds{1}_{A_{\sigma}}\|_{L_{x}^{p}}^{(1-\theta)p}\,\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

(4.14)

where we have used interpolation with
\theta=\frac{1}{2}\frac{p-2}{p-1}
. Using (
4.13
), the inequality determining the set
A_{\sigma}
, the fact that
(1-\theta)<1
, and the Hölder inequality we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|(\rho^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{k}\omega_{\sigma}))\mathds{1}_{A_{\sigma}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{1}}^{\theta p}\int_{0}^{T}\|(\rho_{t}^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{k}\omega_{\sigma}))\mathds{1}_{A_{\sigma}}\|_{L_{x}^{p}}^{(1-\theta)p}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq 2\,T^{\theta}\,\|(\rho^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{k}\omega_{\sigma}))\mathds{1}_{A_{\sigma}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{1}}^{\theta p}\|\rho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{p}(B_{R}))}^{(1-\theta)p}.\end{split}

(4.15)

Now by
(AP2)
from Lemma
3.7
,
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{p}((0,T)\times B_{R})
, thus using (
4.12
) we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\|(\rho^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{k}\omega_{\sigma}))\mathds{1}_{A_{\sigma}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{1}}^{\theta p}\|\rho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{p}(B_{R}))}^{(1-\theta)p}=0.\end{split}

(4.16)

Let us now focus on the set
B_{\sigma}
. We have
\varrho^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{m_{0}}\omega_{\sigma})\to 0
as
\sigma\to 0
in
L^{p}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
by Lemma
C.3
, since
m_{0}>\frac{d}{2}
. Thus, using (
4.13
) and the inequality determining
B_{\sigma}
we get the convergence

\displaystyle\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\|(\rho^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{k}\omega_{\sigma}))\mathds{1}_{B_{\sigma}}\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{2}}^{p}\leq\lim_{\sigma\to 0}2\|(\rho^{\sigma}\ast(|x|^{m_{0}}\omega_{\sigma}))\mathds{1}_{B_{\sigma}}\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{2}}^{p}=0.

(4.17)

Thus by combining (
4.14
)–(
4.17
) we have shown that the RHS in (
4.11
) converges to zero, thus proving (
4.10
).

Step 4: Convergence of remainder.

We estimate

\displaystyle|R_{t}^{\sigma,\varepsilon}(x)|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\\
|\alpha|=m_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{m_{0}}{\alpha!}\|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}|(\varrho_{t}^{\sigma}\ast(|y^{\alpha}|\,\omega_{\sigma}))(x)|\leq C\,\|\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}\|_{W_{x}^{m_{0},\infty}}(\varrho_{t}^{\sigma}\ast(|y|^{m_{0}}\omega_{\sigma})(x)),

for some constant
C>0
independent of
\sigma
and
\varepsilon
. From Lemma
C.3
and
m_{0}>\frac{d}{2}
we deduce

\displaystyle\|R_{t}^{\sigma,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t}W_{x}^{m_{0},\infty}}\|\varrho_{t}^{\sigma}\ast(|y|^{m_{0}}\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}_{t}W_{x}^{m_{0},\infty}}\,\|\varrho_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\,\||y|^{m_{0}}\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C\,\||y|^{m_{0}}\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\to 0\mbox{ as }\sigma\to 0,

since
q>2
taking into account (
4.7
). Thus, we deduce
R_{t}^{\sigma,\varepsilon}\to 0
strongly in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Combining with (
4.10
), we deduce that every summand in the Taylor’s expansion (
4.9
) converges strongly to 0 in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence, the first term on the RHS of (
4.8
) converges to 0 while the second converges by the choice of
\varepsilon
in terms of
\sigma
. As these estimates hold uniformly for all
\varphi
with
\|\varphi\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{m_{0},\infty}}\leq 1
, we conclude the proof by duality.
∎

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
be the weak solution to (

hold. Then, for any fixed

,
k=2

.

, the weak solution of (

), whenever Assumption
2.3
holds,

,

.

Proof.

We concentrate on the bound for
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
the weak solution of (

the weak solution of (

(5.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|(\rho_{t}^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\nabla\psi)\ast\omega_{\sigma}|\\

(5.2)

where

Corollary 5.2
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold and let
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
be the weak solution to (
1.4
). Then for any fixed
T,R>0
and
q=\frac{p_{0}}{p_{0}-1}
, where
p_{0}
is determined by
(A2)
, the sequence
\{\partial_{t}((\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma})\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in the space
(L^{q}(0,T;\,W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})))^{\ast}
.

Proof.

We employ a duality argument. Take any test function
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and compute

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{t}\,((\rho_{t}^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma})\;\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right|=\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{t}\rho_{t}^{\sigma}\;(\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma})\,e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right|.

Then, by Lemma
5.1
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\sigma
such that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{2R}}\partial_{t}\rho_{t}^{\sigma}\;(\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma})\,e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\leq C\;\|(\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma})\,e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R}\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{2,\infty}}.

Since
e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R}
is supported on
B_{2R}
and
\omega_{\sigma}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we find with Young’s convolution inequality,
(A3)
and the change of variables formula

\displaystyle\|(\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma})e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R}\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{2,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq 4\,\|\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{2,\infty}}\|e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq 4\,\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{2,\infty}}\|\omega\|_{L^{1}}\|e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}.

Taking the supremum over all
\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{2,\infty}}\leq 1
proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let Assumption
2.3
hold and let
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
be the weak solution to (
1.3
). Then, for all fixed
T,R>0
and
q\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
, the sequence
\{\partial_{t}(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in the space
(L^{q}(0,T;\,W_{0}^{m_{0}+1,\infty}(B_{R})))^{\ast}
, where
m_{0}
is as in Assumption
2.3
.

Proof.

Let us give a proof strategy. In Step 1, we reduce the problem to showing that a specific function is uniformly bounded. Then, in Step 2 we find a decomposition of the function via the Taylor approximation. Finally, in Step 3 we employ this decomposition and we close the proof by showing the resulting terms in the decomposition are bounded.

Step 1: Problem reduction.
Take any test function
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T]\times B_{R})
. We use the weak formulation of (
1.3
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to compute

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R}}\partial_{t}\,(\varrho_{t}^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})\;\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right|=\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{t}\varrho_{t}^{\sigma}\;(\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla\varrho^{\sigma}+\varrho^{\sigma}\nabla V)\ast\omega_{\sigma}\cdot((\nabla\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma})\varrho^{\sigma})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|(\nabla\varrho^{\sigma}+\varrho^{\sigma}\nabla V)\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|((\nabla\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma})\varrho^{\sigma})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

The first term of the RHS is uniformly bounded by
(GF2)
of Lemma
3.3
. Thus, we only need to estimate the remaining term.

Step 2: Convolution decomposition.
We would like to use a commutator argument for the function
\nabla\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma}
as described in Lemma
4.2
. However, we observe that in general the function
\nabla\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma}
is not compactly supported and thus the lemma is not directly applicable.

Hence, we derive a decomposition as in Lemma
4.2
. We use the Taylor’s expansion to find a constant
C>0
independent of
\sigma
such that the following pointwise bound holds

\displaystyle|(\nabla\psi_{t}\ast\omega_{\sigma})(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\||y|^{m_{0}}\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}}\|\nabla^{m_{0}+1}\psi_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\sum_{k=0}^{m_{0}-1}\||y|^{k}\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}}|\nabla^{k+1}\psi_{t}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|(1+|y|^{m_{0}})\,\omega\|_{L^{1}}\left(\sigma^{m_{0}}\|\nabla^{m_{0}+1}\psi_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sum_{k=0}^{m_{0}-1}\sigma^{k}\|\nabla^{k+1}\psi_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\chi_{R}(x)\right),

where we used the change of variables formula to derive
\||y|^{k}\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}}=\sigma^{k}\||y|^{k}\omega\|_{L^{1}}
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,m_{0}\}
. Thus, there exists non-negative functions
\varphi_{0},\ldots,\varphi_{m_{0}-1}\in C^{0}([0,T];\,C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{2R}))
and non-negative function
\Phi_{m_{0}}\in C^{0}([0,T];\,C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
such that

\displaystyle|\nabla\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma}|\leq\sigma^{m_{0}}\Phi_{m_{0}}+\sum_{k=0}^{m_{0}-1}\sigma^{k}\varphi_{k},

(5.4)

\displaystyle\max\{\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{m_{0}+1,\infty}},\,\ldots,\,\|\varphi_{m_{0}-1}\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{m_{0}+1,\infty}},\,\|\Phi_{m_{0}}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{\infty}}\}\leq\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{m_{0}+1,\infty}}.

(5.5)

Step 3: Derivation of the estimate and conclusion.

By Step 1, we only need to focus on estimating
\|((\nabla\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma})\varrho^{\sigma})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}
. By using the decomposition (
5.4
), we derive the bound

\displaystyle\|((\nabla\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma})\varrho^{\sigma})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\leq\sigma^{m_{0}}\|(\Phi_{m_{0}}\varrho^{\sigma})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}+\sum_{k=0}^{m_{0}-1}\sigma^{k}\|(\varphi_{k}\varrho^{\sigma})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

To bound an individual summand for
0\leq k\leq m-1
, we first use the triangle inequality

\displaystyle\|(\varphi_{k}\varrho^{\sigma})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\leq\|(\varphi_{k}\varrho^{\sigma})\ast\omega_{\sigma}-\varphi_{k}(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}+\|\varphi_{k}(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

We see by the commutator argument (
4.5
) of Lemma
4.2
, it is sufficient to bound the second term on the RHS. We then compute by the Hölder inequality,
(AP2)
of Lemma
3.7
and the inequality (
5.5
)

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{k}(\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\leq\|\varphi_{k}\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{m_{0}+1,\infty}}\|\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\,L^{2}(B_{2R}))}

\displaystyle\leq|B_{2R}|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{m_{0}+1,\infty}}\|\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}((0,T)\times B_{2R})}\leq C\,\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{m_{0}+1,\infty}},

for some constant
C>0
, independent of
\sigma
, and with
p:=\frac{2q}{q-2}
, so that
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{2}{q}=1
and
p\in\mathcal{J}_{d}
whenever
q\in\mathcal{K}_{d}
(see the beginning of the proof of Lemma
4.2
). To bound the remainder, we use the Hölder inequality, then the Young convolution inequality and finally Lemma
C.3
to get

\displaystyle\sigma^{m_{0}}\|(\Phi_{m_{0}}\varrho^{\sigma})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\leq\sigma^{m_{0}}\|\Phi_{m_{0}}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{\infty}}\|\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{2}}\leq\sigma^{m_{0}-\frac{d}{2}}\|\Phi_{m_{0}}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{\infty}}\|\varrho^{\sigma}\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{1}}\|\omega\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

As
m_{0}>\frac{d}{2}
, we see that the remainder term is uniformly bounded for all
\sigma\in(0,1)
. By taking the supremum over all
\|\psi\|_{L_{t}^{q}W_{x}^{m_{0}+1,\infty}}\leq 1
in the obtained estimate for
\|((\nabla\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma})\varrho^{\sigma})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}
we conclude the proof.
∎

We show that there exist curves
\rho,\varrho\in C^{0}([0,\infty);\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
to (

) and

to (

Lemma 6.1

(Basic convergence results)
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold and let
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
be the weak solution to (
1.4
). Then, for all fixed

and

the following convergences hold (up to passing to some subsequence):

(BC1)

\rho^{\sigma}\to\rho
as
\sigma\rightarrow 0
in
C^{0}([0,T];H^{-M}(B_{R}))
;

(BC2)

\rho^{\sigma}_{t}\rightharpoonup\rho_{t}
as
\sigma\rightarrow 0
weakly in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and narrowly for a.e.

;

(BC3)

\rho^{\sigma}\rightharpoonup\rho
as

in

.

Analogue convergences hold for the weak solutions
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
to (
1.3
) under the Assumption
2.3
and assuming
M>\frac{d}{2}+m_{0}+1
, where
m_{0}
is as in Assumption
2.3
.

Proof.

We focus on the weak solutions of (
1.4
) and point out minor differences for the weak solutions of (
1.3
). The convergence
(BC1)
follows because by Lemma
5.1
,
\{\partial_{t}\rho^{\sigma}_{t}\}
is bounded in
(L^{q}(0,T;\,W_{0}^{2,\infty}(B_{R})))^{\ast}
(respectively
(L^{q}(0,T;\,W_{0}^{m_{0}+1,\infty}(B_{R})))^{\ast}
), which by the Sobolev embedding
H_{0}^{M}(B_{R})\hookrightarrow W_{0}^{2,\infty}(B_{R})
(respectively

) gives a bound in
L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(0,T;\,H^{-M}(B_{R})))
. Hence, by a direct integration in time,
\{\rho^{\sigma}_{t}\}
is bounded in
C^{\beta}([0,T];H^{-M}(B_{R}))
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
and we can apply classical Arzela-Ascoli argument, see
[
10

for an example. For the proof of
(BC2)

(GF1)
from Lemma
3.3
and the classical Dunford-Pettis Theorem together with a subsequence argument and identification of the limit for all

obtained in

[

for an example. We observe that by assumption
(A3)
(respectively
(B3)
), the uniform bound

\displaystyle\sup_{\sigma\in(0,1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{t}^{\sigma}(x)|\ln(\rho_{t}^{\sigma}(x))|\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{t}^{\sigma}(x)V(x)\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty

implies again that
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
is equi-integrable and tight on

. Thus, by the Dunford-Pettis Theorem, the sequence is weakly precompact in

. We take any weakly convergence subsequence (not relabeled)
\rho^{\sigma}\to\mu
in

and identify the limit. To that end, for any

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{\sigma}\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\lim_{\sigma\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\rho_{t}^{\sigma},\psi_{t}\rangle_{H^{-M},H^{M}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\rho_{t},\psi_{t}\rangle_{H^{-M},H^{M}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho\,\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

As this limit holds for any converging subsequence we choose, it follows that
\rho^{\sigma}\rightharpoonup\rho
. Similarly, we show narrow convergence

) we have that

∎

Lemma 6.2

(Strong convergence)
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold, let
T,R>0
and let
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
be the weak solutions to (
1.4
) and let
\rho
be the corresponding curve of probability measures in Lemma
6.1
. Then, we have the strong convergence
(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\rightarrow\rho e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R}
as
\sigma\rightarrow 0
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times B_{R})
.

Analogously, under the Assumption
2.3
,
\varrho^{\sigma}\ast\omega_{\sigma}\to\varrho
strongly in
L^{2}([0,T]\times B_{R})
.

Proof.

The proofs of
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
and
\{\varrho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
are the exact same. We only show
\{\rho^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
and point out differences for the other. First, we observe by
(S3)
in Lemma
3.4
(respectively Lemma
3.6
) that
\{(\rho_{t}^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;\,H^{1}(B_{R}))
, and by Corollary
5.2
(respectively Lemma
5.3
) we have for
q=\frac{p_{0}}{p_{0}-1}
, with
p_{0}
defined in
(A2)
, (respectively for any

) and

(respectively

) that
\{\partial_{t}\,(\rho_{t}^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{q}(0,T;H_{0}^{-M}(B_{R}))
, where we used the embedding
H_{0}^{M}(B_{R})\hookrightarrow W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(respectively
H_{0}^{M}(B_{R})\hookrightarrow W_{0}^{m_{0}+1,\infty}(B_{R})
). The Aubin-Lions lemma yields (up to relabelling) a strongly convergent subsequence
(\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma}\rightarrow\mu
for some
\mu~\in~L^{2}([0,T]\times B_{R})
. In order to identify the limit, we take a test function
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T]~\times~B_{R})
and note that we have strong convergence
\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma}\rightarrow\psi
in
L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Thus, by using
(BC3)
of Lemma
6.1
we have the weak convergence
\rho^{\sigma}\rightharpoonup\rho
in

. Then, we compute the limit

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R}}\mu\,\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\lim_{\sigma\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R}}((\rho^{\sigma}e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R})\ast\omega_{\sigma})\;\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\lim_{\sigma\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{2R}}\rho^{\sigma}\;(\psi\ast\omega_{\sigma})e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{R}}\rho\,e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R}\;\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

This proves that
\mu=\rho\,e^{V-\frac{\mathbb{V}}{2}}\chi_{R}
. Since, every subsequence we could have chosen yields the same limit, the whole sequence must strongly converge and the proof is concluded.
∎

Lemma 6.3

(Weak convergence)
.

as

in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

Appendix A
The Bessel potential of order
\alpha

Let
d\geq 1
and
\alpha>0
. We use the standard definition
[
2
, Equation (4, 1)]

\displaystyle G_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{2^{\frac{d+\alpha-2}{2}}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)}\,K_{\frac{d-\alpha}{2}}\!(|x|)\,|x|^{\frac{\alpha-d}{2}},

with
K_{\nu}
the modified Bessel function of the second kind. Its Fourier transform and semigroup property
[
2
, Equation (4, 6) and (4, 7)]
are

\displaystyle\widehat{G_{\alpha}}(\xi)=\frac{1}{(1+4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2})^{\alpha/2}},\qquad G_{\alpha+\beta}=G_{\alpha}\ast G_{\beta}\quad\text{for}\quad\alpha,\beta>0.

Hence the fundamental solution
k
of the screened Poisson equation
(-\Delta)k+k=\delta_{0}
satisfies
\hat{k}(\xi)=(1+4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2})^{-1}
and therefore
k=G_{2}=G_{1}\ast G_{1}
. Throughout we set
\omega:=G_{1}
.

The basic asymptotics of
\omega
are up to multiplicative constants (depending only on
d
)
[
2
, Equation (4.2)]

\displaystyle\omega(x)\sim\begin{cases}-\log|x|,&d=1,\\
|x|^{\,1-d},&d>1,\end{cases}\quad(|x|\to 0),\qquad\qquad\omega(x)\sim|x|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,e^{-\frac{|x|}{2\pi}}\quad(|x|\to\infty).

We derive an exact radial formula for
\nabla\omega
. Writing
r=|x|
and using the classic identity
K_{\nu}^{\prime}(z)=-K_{\nu+1}(z)+\frac{\nu}{z}K_{\nu}(z)
,

\displaystyle\nabla\omega(x)=\frac{x}{r}\,\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(\frac{1}{2^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}\,r^{\frac{1-d}{2}}K_{\frac{d-1}{2}}(r)\right)=-\frac{1}{2^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}\,r^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\,K_{\frac{d+1}{2}}(r)\;x.

The asymptotic properties of the the gradient thus follow from those of
K_{\frac{d+1}{2}}

[
2
, Equation (3, 4) and (3, 5)]
. The leading orders (again up to multiplicative constants) are

\displaystyle|\nabla\omega(x)|\sim|x|^{-d}\quad(|x|\to 0),\qquad\qquad|\nabla\omega(x)|\sim|x|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,e^{-|x|}\quad(|x|\to\infty).

These two asymptotic behaviors immediately give the
L^{p}
information. For
\omega
,

\displaystyle\omega\in\begin{cases}L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ \text{for all }p\in[1,\infty),&d=1,\\[2.84526pt]
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ \text{iff }p\in\left[1,\frac{d}{d-1}\right),&d>1,\end{cases}

since the only restriction comes from the singularity at the origin, while the tail is exponentially integrable. For the gradient, the near–
0
behaviour
|\nabla\omega(x)|\sim|x|^{-d}
shows that
\nabla\omega\notin L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
p\geq 1
(and is exponentially integrable at infinity for all
p
). In particular, we have that
\omega\notin W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Theorem B.1
.

Lemma B.2

(Existence of classical solutions to (

Let Assumption

Proof.

The goal is to prove that the mollified data satisfies the conditions of

•

We have

•

We use (

Secondly, we observe by the Young convolution inequality that

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{|x|\geq R}\frac{|\nabla V(x)|}{V(x)}\leq\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{|x|\geq R}C_{V}^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\left(V(x)^{-p_{0}}+V(x)^{1-p_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}=0.

This means that
\big\|\frac{|\nabla V|}{V}\,|\psi|\,\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}}\big\|_{L_{t,x}^{\infty}}\to 0
as
R\to\infty
, so using the bounds on
\rho\,V
and
\rho_{\varepsilon}\,V
in
L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho)\,\nabla V\cdot\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\leq C\,\left\|\frac{|\nabla V|}{V}\,|\psi|\,\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}}\right\|_{L_{t,x}^{\infty}}.

As
R
is arbitrary, the proof is concluded.
∎

Lemma C.5
.

Let
f\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
D\geq 1
be such that
f(0)=1
and

\displaystyle f(x)\geq\frac{1}{D}\frac{1}{1+|x|^{2}}.

For all
\sigma\in(0,1)
define
f_{\sigma}(x):=f(\sigma x)
. Then for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
\sigma_{\ast}\in(0,1)
such that for all
\sigma\in(0,\sigma_{\ast})
we have

\displaystyle f_{\sigma}(x)\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+|x|^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
c:=D(1-\varepsilon)
. Since
f
is continuous at
0
and
f(0)=1
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\inf_{|y|<\delta}f(y)\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Now, choose
\sigma_{\ast}\in(0,1)
sufficiently small such that for all
\sigma\in(0,\sigma_{\ast})

\displaystyle c\sigma^{2}<1\qquad\text{and}\qquad 1+\frac{\delta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\geq c(1+\delta^{2}).

(C.6)

Next we split into two cases. The first case is when
|x|<\delta/\sigma
. Then
|\sigma x|<\delta
, so

\displaystyle f_{\sigma}(x)=f(\sigma x)\geq 1-\varepsilon\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+|x|^{2}}.

It remains to consider the other case
|x|\geq\delta/\sigma
. Using the assumed bound on
f
, we have

\displaystyle f(\sigma x)\geq\frac{1}{D(1+\sigma^{2}|x|^{2})}.

Thus, it suffices to show

\displaystyle\frac{1}{D(1+\sigma^{2}|x|^{2})}\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+|x|^{2}}\iff(1-c)+(1-c\,\sigma^{2})\,|x|^{2}\geq 0.

Note that
(1-c\,\sigma^{2})\geq 0
by (
C.6
) so we can estimate using
|x|^{2}\geq\delta^{2}/\sigma^{2}

(1-c)+(1-c\,\sigma^{2})\,|x|^{2}\geq(1-c)+(1-c\,\sigma^{2})\,\frac{\delta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}=1+\frac{\delta^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}-c\,(1+\delta^{2})\geq 0

by the second item in (
C.6
). The proof is concluded.
∎
