On the Emergence of Pendular Structure in Multi-Contact Locomotion
Lingxue Lyu, Zihui Liu

Abstract
LIPM is everywhere in legged-locomotion control, but almost always as a modeling choice rather than as something the controller’s cost actually prefers. This note tries to make that link more explicit. Working from a small centroidal OCP that penalizes the rate of angular momentum, we look at what its optimum tends to look like. Three things come out. With full-rank stance, the optimum drifts toward a pendular force pattern at a rate determined by the SVD of the moment Jacobian; the constant is set by foot-span geometry and matches the experiments to within ∼ 16 % {\sim}16\% . With N = 2 N{=}2 stance, as in trot, the friction cone introduces a lower bound on ‖ 𝑯 ˙ G ‖ \|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}\| that no amount of weight tuning fixes; we also see a non-smooth feasibility kink at a critical horizontal acceleration that we can write in closed form. Adding a task term that asks for a nonzero 𝑯 ˙ G \dot{\bm{H}}_{G} moves the optimum off the pendular set in a predictable way. None of this is far from the classical ZMP/DCM picture. We test these claims on a point-mass quadruped and on the Unitree Go1 in MuJoCo (open-loop QP and a torque-level closed-loop controller), and we note where the asymptotic story stops being a good description of what the closed loop actually does.

II
PROBLEM FORMULATION

Let
\bm{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the CoM,
m>0
the total mass,
\bm{g}=[0,0,-g]^{\top}
.
With
N_{c}\geq 1
contact points
\bm{r}_{i}
exerting forces
\bm{f}_{i}
, the
centroidal dynamics
[
17
]
read

\displaystyle m\ddot{\bm{c}}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum_{i}\bm{f}_{i}+m\bm{g},

(1)

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\sum_{i}(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{c})\times\bm{f}_{i},

(2)

so the full-body dynamics affect the CoM only through the net wrench.
Each contact obeys friction

\bm{f}_{i}\in\mathcal{K}_{i}=\{\bm{f}:f_{n,i}\geq 0,\,\|\bm{f}_{t,i}\|\leq\mu_{i}f_{n,i}\}
,
and the ZMP is
\bm{p}_{\mathrm{ZMP}}=\bm{c}_{xy}-(h/g)\ddot{\bm{c}}_{xy}

[
21
]
.

Definition 1

(Net-Wrench OCP)
.

For
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\geq 0
,
\lambda\geq 0
, target

\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{\mathrm{task}}(t)
, and support normal
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{c}(\cdot),\,\{\bm{f}_{i}(\cdot)\}}\;\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\Bigl[

\displaystyle\alpha\left\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}\right\|^{2}+\lambda\left\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}-\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{\mathrm{task}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\beta(\bm{n}^{\top}\ddot{\bm{c}})^{2}+\gamma\textstyle\sum_{i}\left\|\bm{f}_{i}\right\|^{2}\Bigr]dt

(3)

subject to (
1
),
\bm{f}_{i}\in\mathcal{K}_{i}
, and boundary conditions.
The
\lambda=0
case is the
balance-dominated
OCP;
\lambda>0
is

task-dominated
.

Definition 2

(Pendular Manifold)
.

\mathcal{P}(\bm{n},h)=\{(\bm{c},\bm{F}):\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}=\bm{0},\;\bm{n}^{\top}(\bm{c}-\bm{p})=h,\;\bm{F}=(mg/h)(\bm{c}-\bm{p}),\;\bm{p}\in\Pi\}
.

Assumption 1

(Feasibility/Regularity)
.

The OCP is feasible with bounded cost, friction cones are non-empty and
convex, and
h^{*}=\bm{n}^{\top}(\bm{c}^{*}-\bm{p}^{*})\geq h_{\min}>0
.

III
ASYMPTOTIC STRUCTURE WITH FULL-RANK STANCE

Set
\lambda=0
for now.
A simple cross-product calculation shows that the pendular force

\bm{F}_{\mathrm{pend}}=(mg/h)(\bm{c}-\bm{p})
achieves
\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}=\bm{0}
pointwise.
Theorem
1
below combines this pointwise observation with the
asymptotic implications of the cost.

Theorem 1
.

Let Assumption
1
hold and let

(\bm{c}^{*}_{\alpha\beta},\{\bm{f}^{*}_{i,\alpha\beta}\})
minimize (
3
)
with
\lambda=0
.

(i) Pointwise certificate.
Among all net forces
\bm{F}
achieving a prescribed horizontal acceleration,
the pendular force
\bm{F}_{\mathrm{pend}}
uniquely minimizes
\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}\|^{2}
.

(ii) Asymptotic collapse.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\left\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{*}\right\|^{2}dt\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\alpha},\quad\int_{0}^{T}\!(\bm{n}^{\top}\ddot{\bm{c}}^{*})^{2}dt\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\beta},

(4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\left\|\bm{F}^{*}-\tfrac{mg}{h^{*}}(\bm{c}^{*}-\bm{p}^{*})\right\|^{2}dt\leq\tfrac{C_{1}}{\alpha}+\tfrac{C_{2}}{\beta}.

(5)

(iii) Tightness.
Any trajectory with pendular deviation
\varepsilon_{P}
incurs

\int\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}\|^{2}dt\geq(h_{\min}^{2}/K^{2})T\varepsilon_{P}
,
matching (
5
) up to constants.

Proof.

(i) Pointwise certificate.

For prescribed horizontal acceleration
\ddot{\bm{c}}_{xy}
, Newton’s
law (
1
) fixes
\bm{F}_{xy}
.
Decompose
\bm{F}=\bm{F}_{\parallel}+\bm{F}_{\perp}
with

\bm{F}_{\parallel}\parallel(\bm{c}-\bm{p})
.
Substituting in (
2
) gives

\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}=(\bm{c}-\bm{p})\times\bm{F}_{\perp}
, hence

\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}\|^{2}=\|\bm{c}-\bm{p}\|^{2}\|\bm{F}_{\perp}\|^{2}
, which equals zero iff

\bm{F}_{\perp}=\bm{0}
, i.e.
\bm{F}
is co-linear with
\bm{c}-\bm{p}
.
The pendular force
\bm{F}_{\mathrm{pend}}=(mg/h)(\bm{c}-\bm{p})
is the unique
co-linear choice that also satisfies Newton’s law for the prescribed
acceleration.

(ii) Asymptotic collapse.

The cost upper bound
J^{*}_{\alpha\beta}\leq C_{0}
gives

\alpha\int\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{*}\|^{2}dt\leq C_{0}
and

\beta\int(\bm{n}^{\top}\ddot{\bm{c}}^{*})^{2}dt\leq C_{0}
directly.
Using
\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{*}=(\bm{c}^{*}-\bm{p}^{*})\times\bm{F}^{*}=\|\bm{c}^{*}-\bm{p}^{*}\|\|\bm{F}^{*}\|\sin\theta^{*}
where
\theta^{*}
is the angle between

\bm{F}^{*}
and
\bm{c}^{*}-\bm{p}^{*}
:

\int\|\bm{c}^{*}-\bm{p}^{*}\|^{2}\|\bm{F}^{*}\|^{2}\sin^{2}\theta^{*}dt\leq C_{0}/\alpha
.
By Assumption
1

\|\bm{c}^{*}-\bm{p}^{*}\|\geq h_{\min}
, and gravity
support gives
\|\bm{F}^{*}\|\geq mg
, so

\int\sin^{2}\theta^{*}dt\leq C_{0}/(\alpha h_{\min}^{2}m^{2}g^{2})
, i.e.
\bm{F}^{*}

becomes co-linear with
\bm{c}^{*}-\bm{p}^{*}
at rate
O(1/\sqrt{\alpha})
in
L^{2}
.
Projecting (
1
) onto
\bm{n}
:

m\bm{n}^{\top}\ddot{\bm{c}}^{*}=\lambda^{*}h^{*}-mg
, so

\lambda^{*}=mg/h^{*}+O(1/\sqrt{\beta})
.
Combining the two estimates and squaring yields (
5
).

(iii) Tightness.

For an arbitrary feasible trajectory write

\bm{F}=\lambda(\bm{c}-\bm{p})+\bm{F}_{\perp}
with
\bm{F}_{\perp}\perp(\bm{c}-\bm{p})
.
Then
\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}=(\bm{c}-\bm{p})\times\bm{F}_{\perp}
and

\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}\|^{2}\geq h_{\min}^{2}\|\bm{F}_{\perp}\|^{2}
.
The pendular deviation expands as

\|\bm{F}-(mg/h)(\bm{c}-\bm{p})\|^{2}=\|\bm{F}_{\perp}\|^{2}+(\lambda-mg/h)^{2}\|\bm{c}-\bm{p}\|^{2}\leq K^{2}(\|\bm{F}_{\perp}\|^{2}+(\lambda-mg/h)^{2})
for a geometric constant
K

bounding
\|\bm{c}-\bm{p}\|
.
Integrating gives the claimed lower bound.
∎

The rate is sharp; the next proposition makes the constant explicit.

Proposition 2
.

Stack the equilibrium-null-space forces into the moment Jacobian

\bm{J}_{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3(N-1)}
with SVD
\bm{J}_{H}=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^{\top}
and

\sigma_{1}\!\geq\!\sigma_{2}\!\geq\!\sigma_{3}\!>\!0
.
Let
h_{k}(t)=(\bm{U}^{\top}\dot{\bm{H}}_{G,0}(t))_{k}
be the excitation projection from
gravity and inertia.
For
\alpha\sigma_{k}^{2}\!\gg\!\gamma
,

\tfrac{\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{*}\|}{m}\;\approx\;\tfrac{\gamma}{\alpha}\,K,\quad K=\sqrt{\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{3}\langle h_{k}^{2}\rangle_{t}/\sigma_{k}^{4}}.

(6)

For a rectangular four-foot polygon with half-spans
L_{x},L_{y}
, the singular
values are exactly the geometric foot spans:

\sigma_{1}=2\sqrt{L_{x}^{2}+L_{y}^{2}}
,
\sigma_{2}=2L_{x}
,
\sigma_{3}=2L_{y}
.

Proof.

The KKT stationarity for the unconstrained QP gives

\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{*}=\sum_{k}\frac{\gamma}{\gamma+\alpha\sigma_{k}^{2}}h_{k}\bm{u}_{k}
,
which scales as
\gamma/(\alpha\sigma_{k}^{2})
in each mode for large
\alpha
.
Time-averaging in
L^{2}
produces (
6
).
The singular-value identity follows from direct computation of

\bm{J}_{H}\bm{J}_{H}^{\top}
for the symmetric four-foot rectangle.
∎

Remark 1

(Weight selection)
.

Theorem
1
immediately yields the design rule

\alpha^{*}=K/(\varepsilon^{*})^{2}
for a target residual
\varepsilon^{*}
:
one calibration sweep estimates

, and any larger

III-A

ZMP and DCM Bridges

On the pendular manifold, two classical objects emerge for free.

Lemma 3

(ZMP equals virtual pivot)
.

On
\mathcal{P}(\bm{n},h)
,
\bm{p}_{\mathrm{ZMP}}=\bm{p}_{xy}
.

Proof.

\bm{F}=(mg/h)(\bm{c}-\bm{p})
gives
\ddot{\bm{c}}_{xy}=(g/h)(\bm{c}_{xy}-\bm{p}_{xy})
;
substituting into the ZMP definition cancels the
h/g
factor.
∎

Corollary 4

(DCM orbital equation)
.

For
\bm{\xi}=\bm{c}_{xy}+\dot{\bm{c}}_{xy}/\omega
and
\omega=\sqrt{g/h}
:

\dot{\bm{\xi}}=\omega(\bm{\xi}-\bm{p}_{xy})
on
\mathcal{P}(\bm{n},h)
.

ZMP-based controllers are thus solving an instance of (
3
) at high

\alpha
, and DCM capturability
[
8
,
15
]

inherits its orbital structure from the same source.

IV
LOWER BOUND UNDER
N=2
STANCE

When the moment Jacobian has full row rank, Theorem
1
drives

\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}\|\to 0
as
\alpha\to\infty
.

N=2
stance is qualitatively different: the moment operator is rank-
1
, and
a residual along
\hat{\bm{D}}
remains for any choice of contact-force
distribution.

Theorem 5
.

For
N=2
feet at
\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2}
with
\bm{D}=\bm{p}_{1}-\bm{p}_{2}
,
midpoint
\bar{\bm{p}}
, and
\bm{r}=\bar{\bm{p}}-\bm{c}

\tfrac{\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}\|}{m}\;\geq\;\tfrac{|(\bm{r}\times\bm{F}_{\mathrm{net}})\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}|}{m}\quad\text{for all }\alpha\geq 0,

(7)

and equality is attainable iff the friction cones admit the canceller

\bm{\delta}^{*}
defined in Theorem
6
.

Proof.

Write
\bm{f}_{i}=\bm{F}_{\mathrm{net}}/2+(-1)^{i+1}\delta\bm{f}
.
Then
\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}=\bm{r}\times\bm{F}_{\mathrm{net}}+\bm{D}\times\delta\bm{f}
.
The map
\delta\bm{f}\!\mapsto\!\bm{D}\times\delta\bm{f}
has range
\bm{D}^{\perp}
, so
the
\hat{\bm{D}}
-component of
\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}
equals

(\bm{r}\times\bm{F}_{\mathrm{net}})\cdot\hat{\bm{D}}
for every choice of
\delta\bm{f}
.
Taking norms gives (
7
).
∎

Remark 2

(Why diagonal trot hits the floor)
.

For Go1 in diagonal trot (FR
+
RL stance),

\hat{\bm{D}}=[\cos 34^{\circ},-\sin 34^{\circ},0]^{\top}
is nearly horizontal and
aligns with the horizontal excitation

\dot{\bm{H}}_{G,0}/m=z_{c}[a_{y},-a_{x},0]^{\top}
, so the projection
captures
55
–
98\%
of
\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G,0}\|/m
across typical accelerations.
The maximum cancellation factor is therefore
1.0
–
1.8\times
, in stark
contrast to the
\sim\!70\times
of full-rank stance.
The mechanism: the pendular direction tilts
40.6^{\circ}
from vertical, while
the friction cone half-angle is only
\arctan(\mu)=31^{\circ}
at
\mu=0.6
.
The QP can push forces to the cone boundary but no further.

The geometric floor of Theorem
5
is the moment along
\hat{\bm{D}}

that the QP cannot cancel for
any
force redistribution.
A second, qualitatively distinct obstruction appears in the

\hat{\bm{D}}^{\perp}
channel: the unconstrained min-norm canceller of the
perpendicular moment may fall outside the friction cone, in which case the

The transition is non-smooth.

Theorem 6
.

Parametrize
\bm{f}_{i}=\bm{F}_{\mathrm{net}}/2+(-1)^{i+1}\bm{\delta}
and let

\bm{H}_{0}=\bm{r}\times\bm{F}_{\mathrm{net}}

\bm{H}_{0}^{\perp}=\bm{H}_{0}-(\bm{H}_{0}\!\cdot\!\hat{\bm{D}})\hat{\bm{D}}

Define the unconstrained min-norm canceller

\bm{\delta}^{*}\;=\;\frac{\bm{D}\times\bm{H}_{0}^{\perp}}{\|\bm{D}\|^{2}}.

(8)

Then
\inf_{\alpha}\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}\|/m
equals the geometric floor of
Theorem
5

if and only if

\bm{F}_{\mathrm{net}}/2\pm\bm{\delta}^{*}\in\mathcal{K}_{i}
for

.
When this fails, the achievable infimum strictly exceeds the geometric floor,
and the excess depends only on how far
\bm{\delta}^{*}
violates the cone.

For
N=2
Go1 trot stance, body at
\bm{c}=(0,0,h)
, and fore–aft excitation

\bm{F}_{\mathrm{net}}=m(a_{x},0,g)

.

The decomposition
\bm{f}_{i}=\bm{F}_{\mathrm{net}}/2+(-1)^{i+1}\bm{\delta}

ADDING A TASK-TRACKING TERM

Many tasks are not balance-dominated.
Fast turns, push-recovery, swings, and agile leaps demand a nonzero

\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}
, and multi-contact recovery

[
23
,
24
,
25
]
as well as
angular-momentum-aware whole-body
control
[
18
,
26
,
16
,
6
]
make this explicit.
A natural way to model the requirement is an additional tracking penalty,
which interacts with the pendular regularization in a transparent way.

Theorem 7
.

With
\lambda>0
and full-rank moment Jacobian (so the local wrench subspace
spans
\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
), the pointwise minimizer of (
3
) satisfies

\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{*}(t)\;=\;\frac{\lambda}{\alpha+\lambda}\,\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{\mathrm{task}}(t).

(10)

Hence
\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{*}\neq\bm{0}
wherever
\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{\mathrm{task}}\neq\bm{0}
, and the
trajectory leaves
\mathcal{P}(\bm{n},h)
.
In the limit
\lambda/\alpha\!\to\!\infty
,
\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{*}\to\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{\mathrm{task}}
.

Proof.

The cost in
\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}
is the strictly convex quadratic

(\alpha+\lambda)\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}\|^{2}-2\lambda\langle\dot{\bm{H}}_{G},\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{\mathrm{task}}\rangle+\lambda\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{\mathrm{task}}\|^{2}
;
its unique minimizer is (
10
).
∎

Corollary 8
.

If the task imposes

\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}-\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{\mathrm{task}}\|\leq\varepsilon
, then

\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}\|\geq\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{\mathrm{task}}\|-\varepsilon
.
Whenever
\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}^{\mathrm{task}}\|>\varepsilon
, no feasible trajectory lies on

\mathcal{P}(\bm{n},h)
.

VII
DISCUSSION

A few comments on what the analysis does and does not say.

The story is cleanest when the moment Jacobian has full row rank.
There the optimum sits near the pendular set, the rate is set by the SVD of
that Jacobian, and ZMP
[
21
]
/DCM
[
8
,
15
]

drop out of Lemma
3
and Corollary
4
as
consequences.
This is also where multi-contact OCPs that don’t ask for any particular
angular-momentum behavior keep producing pendular-looking solutions—the
cost structure, not the model class, is doing the work.

For trot-style stances the picture is different.
The rank-
1
obstruction is unavoidable, and the friction cone adds its own
constraint on top—the kink of Theorem
6
.
This has been useful as an intuition pump: when a closed-loop controller
refuses to lower
\|\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}\|
no matter how high
\alpha
is, our first
guess is now that we crossed
a_{x}^{\star}
.
The Test D
\alpha=300
outlier is consistent with the opposite failure
mode—a high-
\alpha
tracker fighting the floor and losing on height—
but we haven’t isolated that, so it’s a guess.

The “add a task term” regime (Theorem
7
) is where most
of the literature on aggressive multi-contact behavior already lives—
hand-contact recovery
[
23
,
24
]
,
contact-transition trees
[
25
]
,
underactuated balance
[
20
]
, whole-body trajectory
optimization
[
6
]
, DDP-based agile
synthesis
[
9
,
11
,
28
]
.
The
\lambda/(\alpha+\lambda)
factor isn’t deep, but it does make explicit
what is otherwise a fuzzy switch between “balance” and “task” modes;
whether that’s a useful runtime signal, possibly combined with online
calibration
[
22
]
or state
estimation
[
3
]
, we haven’t checked.

The main caveat is that the analysis is quasi-static.
It takes
\dot{\bm{H}}_{G}
as the controlled quantity and ignores the coupling
between contact transitions, swing-leg inertia, and finite control rate.
On hardware—and in our closed-loop test, which is the closest thing
here to it—compliance, foot-slip, and friction heterogeneity each look
big enough to overwrite the static residuals; the 1.71-vs-0.28 Test D gap
is consistent.
The
N=2
analysis assumes ideal point contacts; finite patches or ankle
torque may relax the floor.
Theorem
7
is pointwise; horizon trade-offs are open.
Flight and large-
\Delta h/h
regimes are outside the model
entirely
[
10
,
12
]
, and we don’t have a closed-loop
counterpart to Theorem
1
—which is what would actually
bound the Test D gap.

VIII
CONCLUSIONS

We tried to write down, for a small centroidal OCP, when the LIPM-style
pendular pattern is what the optimum actually wants.
Roughly: yes under full-rank stance, at
O(1/\alpha)
with a foot-span
constant; no under
N\!=\!2
stance, where a friction-bound residual and a
non-smooth kink at
a_{x}^{\star}
appear; and a simple ratio shift if a task
term is added.
The numerics line up in the static tests, partly in the closed-loop one.
Where they don’t, the gap is bigger than the static analysis can
account for.
