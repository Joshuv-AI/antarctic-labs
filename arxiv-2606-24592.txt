A characterization of virtually free actions via arc spaces and its application to the lower semi-continuity conjecture
Yusuke Nakamura, Kohsuke Shibata

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the precise inversion of adjunction (PIA) conjecture and the lower semi-continuity (LSC) conjecture for hyperquotient singularities. Previously known results for these conjectures in this setting required the singularity to be klt, and without this assumption, a counterexample to the PIA conjecture is known to exist. To resolve this obstacle, we introduce a localized notion of virtually free actions and characterize it via the arc spaces of quotient varieties. Utilizing this characterization, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition for the PIA conjecture to hold for arbitrary hyperquotient singularities, thereby clarifying the mechanism of the counterexample. Furthermore, as an application of this insight, we unconditionally establish the LSC conjecture for arbitrary hyperquotient singularities.

1.
Introduction

The minimal log discrepancy is a fundamental invariant of singularities in birational geometry.
In
[
1
]
, Ambro proposed the lower semi-continuity (LSC) conjecture for minimal log discrepancies.
As shown by Shokurov
[
17
]
, the LSC conjecture, together with the ascending chain condition (ACC) conjecture, implies the termination of flips.
In this paper, we focus on the LSC conjecture and the related precise inversion of adjunction (PIA) conjecture.
Throughout this paper, we work over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero.

The LSC conjecture asserts the lower semi-continuity of the minimal log discrepancy as the closed point varies:

Conjecture 1.1

(LSC conjecture,
[
1
]
)
.

Let
(X,\mathfrak{a})
be a log pair, and let
|X|_{\rm cl}
denote the set of all closed points of
X
with the Zariski topology.
Then the function

|X|_{\rm cl}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{-\infty\};\quad x\mapsto\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,\mathfrak{a})

is lower semi-continuous.

The LSC conjecture has been confirmed in the following settings:

(
1.1
.1)

Ambro
[
1
]
proved the conjecture for
\dim X=3
.

(
1.1
.2)

Ein, Mustaţă, and Yasuda
[
4
]
proved it when
X
is smooth.

(
1.1
.3)

Ein and Mustaţă
[
5
]
generalized the result (
1.1
.2) to normal local complete intersection varieties.

(
1.1
.4)

The first author
[
15
]
proved the conjecture when
X
has quotient singularities.

(
1.1
.5)

In
[
12
,
13
]
, the authors generalized the results (
1.1
.3) and (
1.1
.4) to hyperquotient klt singularities.
More generally, they proved the conjecture for a finite group quotient of a complete intersection variety defined by invariant equations, provided that the variety
X
is klt
(the case of semi-invariant equations is treated in
[
14
]
).

The following PIA conjecture is useful for replacing the variety under consideration with a better ambient space when studying minimal log discrepancies.

Conjecture 1.2

(PIA conjecture,
[
7
]
*17.3.1)
.

Let
(X,\mathfrak{a})
be a log pair, and let
D
be a normal prime Cartier divisor on
X
.
Let
x\in D
be a closed point. Suppose that
D
is not contained in the cosupport of the
\mathbb{R}
-ideal sheaf
\mathfrak{a}
.
Then, we have

\operatorname{mld}_{x}\bigl(X,\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\bigr)=\operatorname{mld}_{x}(D,\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{D}).

The PIA conjecture has been confirmed in the following settings:

(
1.2
.1)

The conjecture is known to hold for
\dim X=3
(cf.
[
11
]
*Proposition 6.1).

(
1.2
.2)

Ein, Mustaţă, and Yasuda
[
4
]
proved the conjecture when
X
is smooth.

(
1.2
.3)

Ein and Mustaţă
[
5
]
generalized the result (
1.2
.2) to normal local complete intersection varieties.

(
1.2
.4)

In
[
12
,
13
]
, the authors proved the conjecture when
X
has quotient singularities and
D
is klt at
x
.
More generally, they established it for
X
a finite group quotient of a complete intersection variety defined by invariant equations, provided that both

are klt at

(the case of semi-invariant equations is treated in
[

]
).

Despite these affirmative results, the conjecture is not true in full generality:

(
1.2
.5)

In
[

]
, the authors provided a counterexample
(X,D)
to the conjecture, where
X
is a
5

is log canonical but not klt.

This counterexample (
1.2
.5) demonstrates that the klt assumption in the result (
1.2
.4) is indeed indispensable.

The LSC conjecture and the PIA conjecture are intertwined as follows.
In general, assuming the PIA conjecture for
X
and
D
, the LSC conjecture for an ambient space
X
implies the LSC conjecture for
D
.
Indeed, the result (
1.1
.3) in
[
5
]
for the LSC conjecture was proved by reducing it to the smooth case (
1.1
.2) via the result (
1.2
.3) for the PIA conjecture.
Similarly, the result (
1.1
.5) in
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
was established by reducing it to the case of quotient singularities (
1.1
.4) using the result (
1.2
.4).
The klt assumption in the result (
1.1
.5) was indispensable because it was required to enable this reduction.

However, as demonstrated by the counterexample (
1.2
.5), the PIA conjecture fails for hyperquotient singularities that are not klt, meaning we can no longer rely on this naive reduction strategy.
Furthermore, the authors have shown in
[
11
]
*Example 5.1 that there also exist counterexamples to the LSC conjecture for families in the setting of hyperquotient singularities without the klt assumption.
Consequently, it is highly natural to suspect that non-klt hyperquotient singularities could serve as counterexamples to the original LSC conjecture (Conjecture
1.1
) as well.

The primary objective of this paper is to uncover the mechanism behind the counterexample to the PIA conjecture for hyperquotient singularities, thereby clarifying the fundamental role of the klt condition.
Furthermore, contrary to the pessimistic expectation mentioned above, we utilize this insight to unconditionally prove the LSC conjecture for hyperquotient singularities, completely removing the previously required klt assumption from the result (
1.1
.5).

Theorem 1.3

(
=
Theorem
10.1
)
.

Suppose that a finite subgroup
G
acts on a smooth variety
A
.
Let
A^{\prime}:=A/G
be the quotient variety.
Let
X
be a normal subvariety of
A^{\prime}
of codimension
c

such that
X
is locally defined by
c
equations in
A^{\prime}
.
Then, the LSC conjecture (Conjecture
1.1
) is true for
X
.

To establish Theorem
1.3
without relying on the reduction via the PIA conjecture, a new approach is required. Specifically, we achieve this by characterizing virtually free actions in terms of their arc spaces.

The concept of virtually free actions was recently introduced by the authors in
[
11
]
to construct the counterexample to the PIA conjecture.
Let
G
be a finite group acting on a normal variety
X
.
The
G
-action on
X
is said to be
virtually free
if, for every
G
-equivariant proper birational morphism
Y\to X
from a normal variety
Y
, the
G
-action on
Y
is free in codimension one (see Definition
2.10
).
As noted in Remark
2.12
, this concept is an intermediate notion between a free action and an action that is free in codimension one.
Furthermore, when
X
is klt, the property of being virtually free is equivalent to being free (see Theorem
2.14
).

In this paper, we also introduce a localized version of this notion.
For a closed point
x\in X
and an element
\gamma\in G
, we say that the
\gamma
-action on
X
is
virtually free at
x
if, for every
\gamma
-equivariant proper birational morphism
f\colon Y\to X
from a normal variety
Y
, the
\gamma
-fixed locus on
Y
intersects the fiber
f^{-1}(x)
in a subset of codimension at least two in
Y
(see Definition
6.1
).
Let
\operatorname{NVF}(X,x)
denote the set of elements
\gamma\in G
such that the
\gamma
-action on
X
is not virtually free at
x
.

A key consequence of this localized notion is that the elements
\gamma\in G
whose action is virtually free at
x
do not contribute to the drop in the minimal log discrepancy.

Proposition 1.4

(
=
Proposition
6.9
)
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a normal
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein variety
X
, and that the action is free in codimension one.
Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a non-zero
\mathbb{R}
-ideal sheaf on
X/G
.
Let
x\in X
be a closed point, and let
x^{\prime}\in X/G
be its image.
Then, we have

\operatorname{mld}_{x^{\prime}}(X/G,\mathfrak{a})=\min_{\gamma\in\operatorname{NVF}(X,x)}\operatorname{mld}_{x^{\prime}_{\gamma}}\bigl(X/\langle\gamma\rangle,\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{X/\langle\gamma\rangle}\bigr),

where
x^{\prime}_{\gamma}
denotes the image of
x
in
X/\langle\gamma\rangle
.

In
[
12
]
*Remark 4.13, it is proved that

\operatorname{mld}_{x^{\prime}}(X/G,\mathfrak{a})=\min_{\gamma\in G}\operatorname{mld}_{x^{\prime}_{\gamma}}\bigl(X/\langle\gamma\rangle,\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{X/\langle\gamma\rangle}\bigr).

Proposition
1.4
asserts the stronger fact that this minimum is always attained at some element
\gamma\in\operatorname{NVF}(X,x)
(see Remark
6.10
).

To present our main result on the PIA conjecture, we introduce the following simplified setting of quotient singularities (see Setup
8.5
for the general setting).

Setup 1.5
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a smooth variety
X
, and that the action is free in codimension one.
Let
X^{\prime}:=X/G
be the quotient variety.
Let
x\in X
be a
G
-fixed closed point, and let
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
be its image.
Let
D^{\prime}
be a normal prime Cartier divisor on
X^{\prime}
passing through
x^{\prime}
, and let
D
be its inverse image in
X
.

Under this setup, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition for the PIA conjecture to hold, thereby clarifying the mechanism of the counterexample.

Theorem 1.6

(
\subset
Theorem
8.10
)
.

In the setting of Setup
1.5
, let
\mathfrak{b}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-ideal sheaf on
X^{\prime}
.
Suppose that
D^{\prime}
is not contained in the cosupport of
\mathfrak{b}
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

The minimum

\min_{\gamma\in\operatorname{NVF}(X,x)}\operatorname{mld}_{x^{\prime}_{\gamma}}\bigl(X/\langle\gamma\rangle,\mathfrak{b}\mathcal{O}_{X/\langle\gamma\rangle}(-D_{\gamma})\bigr)

is attained at some
\gamma\in\operatorname{NVF}(D,x)
, where
x^{\prime}_{\gamma}\in X/\langle\gamma\rangle
denotes the image of
x
, and
D_{\gamma}:=D/\langle\gamma\rangle
.

(ii)

The PIA conjecture holds; that is,

\operatorname{mld}_{x^{\prime}}\bigl(X^{\prime},\mathfrak{b}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-D^{\prime})\bigr)=\operatorname{mld}_{x^{\prime}}(D^{\prime},\mathfrak{b}\mathcal{O}_{D^{\prime}}).

As a direct consequence of this characterization, we obtain the following practical sufficient condition for the PIA conjecture. This occurs exactly when condition (i) is automatically satisfied.

Corollary 1.7

(
\subset
Corollary
8.11
)
.

In the setting of Setup
1.5
, let
\mathfrak{b}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-ideal sheaf on
X^{\prime}
.
Suppose that
D^{\prime}
is not contained in the cosupport of
\mathfrak{b}
.
If
\operatorname{NVF}(D,x)=G
, then it follows that

\operatorname{mld}_{x^{\prime}}\bigl(X^{\prime},\mathfrak{b}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-D^{\prime})\bigr)=\operatorname{mld}_{x^{\prime}}(D^{\prime},\mathfrak{b}\mathcal{O}_{D^{\prime}}).

The LSC conjecture for
D^{\prime}
under Setup
1.5
, which is a special case of Theorem
1.3
, then follows from Proposition
1.4
and Corollary
1.7
, together with the semi-continuity of the set
\operatorname{NVF}(D,x)
(see Lemma
7.2
(2)).

The proof of Theorem
1.6
relies on the theory of arc spaces for quotient varieties, originally developed by Denef and Loeser
[
3
]
and later generalized by the authors in
[
12
]
.
In Denef and Loeser’s framework, the arc space
X^{\prime}_{\infty}
of the quotient variety
X^{\prime}=X/G
is investigated by decomposing it into subsets
{X^{\prime}}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}\subset X^{\prime}_{\infty}
associated with each element
\gamma\in G
(see Definition
4.1
).
However, when dealing with non-klt varieties, a critical bottleneck arises: as pointed out in
[
12
]
*Introduction, the subset
{X^{\prime}}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
can become a
thin set
of
X^{\prime}_{\infty}
.
In such cases, it becomes too small to carry meaningful geometric information.
This phenomenon is precisely the reason why the klt assumption was indispensable in the previous result (
1.2
.4), and why the counterexample (
1.2
.5) occurs without it.

To overcome this obstacle, we establish a connection between this arc-theoretic behavior and our localized notion of virtually free actions.

Theorem 1.8

(
\subset
Theorem
6.6
)
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a normal variety
X
.
Let
X^{\prime}=X/G
be the quotient variety.
Let
x\in X
be a
G
-fixed closed point, and let
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
be its image.
For an element
\gamma\in G
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The
\gamma
-action on
X
is not virtually free at
x
(i.e.,
\gamma\in\operatorname{NVF}(X,x)
).

(2)

The local arc space
{X^{\prime}}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty,x^{\prime}}
is not a thin set of
X^{\prime}_{\infty}
(see Remark
4.4
).

Here,
{X^{\prime}}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty,x^{\prime}}
denotes the set of arcs in
{X^{\prime}}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
centered at
x^{\prime}
(see Definition
6.4
).

This theorem is utilized in Theorem
7.1
, which is the key to the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

In the approach of
[
3
]
, the subset
{X^{\prime}}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
is investigated by defining a certain
k[t]
-scheme
X^{(\gamma)}
and considering its arc space (Greenberg scheme)
X^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
as a
k[t]
-scheme (see Section
5
).
In this paper, we introduce a new
k[t]
-scheme, denoted by
X^{[\gamma]}
, to prove the above theorem.
By definition,
X^{[\gamma]}
is the quotient of
X\times\operatorname{Spec}k[t^{1/d^{\prime}}]
by the
\langle\gamma\rangle
-action, where
d^{\prime}
is the order of
\gamma
(see Definition
3.1
).
Both
X^{(\gamma)}
and
X^{[\gamma]}
can be viewed as “branched versions” of the twisted variety associated with the
G
-action on
X
.
Indeed, they are isomorphic over the open subset
t\neq 0
(see Remarks
3.2
and
5.1
), and there is a natural bijection between their sets of
K
-arcs (Proposition
5.3
).

The primary advantage of
X^{[\gamma]}
lies in its functorial construction.
This functoriality makes
X^{[\gamma]}
highly compatible with the concept of virtually free actions, which is defined via
G
-equivariant resolutions.
On the other hand, the classical construction
X^{(\gamma)}
is defined only for linear actions and lacks this functoriality.
However, it is important to note that
X^{(\gamma)}
cannot be entirely replaced by
X^{[\gamma]}
in the arguments of
[
3
]
and
[
12
]
.
The crucial advantage of
X^{(\gamma)}
is that the number of its defining equations exactly coincides with that of
X
(see Example
5.5
).
This property is well-suited for the computation of the canonical divisors and the estimation of codimensions of arc spaces.
In fact, the proof of the previous result (
1.2
.4) in
[
12
]
heavily relied on this advantage of
X^{(\gamma)}
.
In this paper, by bridging the geometric functoriality of
X^{[\gamma]}
and the algebraic tractability of
X^{(\gamma)}
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.6
, as carried out in Theorems
1.8
,
7.1
, and
8.2
.

We emphasize here that Corollary
1.7
(and consequently Theorem
1.6
) can be seen as a generalization of the previous result (
1.2
.4), which strictly required the klt assumption.
This is because
\operatorname{NVF}(D,x)=G
holds if
D
is klt at
x
(see Theorem
6.7
).
It is worth noting that the proof of Theorem
6.7
relies on the following deep geometric facts:

•

Takayama’s theorem
[
18
]
*Theorem 1.1 on the simple connectedness of the fibers of a resolution of a klt point; or

•

Hacon and McKernan’s result
[
9
]
on the rational chain connectedness of such fibers, combined with the theorem of Graber, Harris, and Starr
[
8
]
asserting the existence of a section for morphisms with rationally connected fibers.

Notation

•

k
denotes an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

•

A variety over
k
is an integral separated scheme of finite type over
k
.

•

An action of a finite group
G
on a variety
X
is called
free in codimension one

if it is free on the set of closed points,
|X|_{\rm cl}
, away from a closed subset of codimension at least two.

•

When considering the quotient
X/G
of a variety
X
by a finite group

, we always assume that

is a variety.
Note that this assumption is satisfied, for instance, if
X
is quasi-projective over

.

2.
Preliminaries

Following
[
12
]
, we review some definitions and facts regarding log pairs and arc spaces.

2.1.
Log pairs

A
log pair

(X,\mathfrak{a})
consists of a normal
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein variety
X
over
k
and
an
\mathbb{R}
-ideal sheaf
\mathfrak{a}
on
X
.
Here, an
\mathbb{R}
-
ideal sheaf

\mathfrak{a}
on
X
is a formal product

\mathfrak{a}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{\tau_{i}}
, where
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{s}
are
non-zero coherent ideal sheaves on
X

and
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{s}
are positive real numbers.
For a morphism
Y\to X
and an
\mathbb{R}
-ideal sheaf
\mathfrak{a}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{\tau_{i}}
on
X
,
we denote by
\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
the
\mathbb{R}
-ideal sheaf
\prod_{i=1}^{s}(\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathcal{O}_{Y})^{\tau_{i}}
on
Y
.

Let
\bigl(X,\mathfrak{a}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathfrak{a}_{i}^{\tau_{i}}\bigr)
be a log pair.
Let
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a proper birational morphism from a normal variety
X^{\prime}
, and let
E
be a prime divisor on
X^{\prime}
.
We denote the relative canonical divisor by
K_{X^{\prime}/X}:=K_{X^{\prime}}-f^{*}K_{X}
.
Then the
log discrepancy
of
(X,\mathfrak{a})
at
E
is defined as

a_{E}(X,\mathfrak{a}):=1+\operatorname{ord}_{E}(K_{X^{\prime}/X})-\operatorname{ord}_{E}\mathfrak{a},

where we define
\operatorname{ord}_{E}\mathfrak{a}:=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\tau_{i}\operatorname{ord}_{E}\mathfrak{a}_{i}
.
The image
f(E)
is called the
center
of
E
on
X
, and we denote it by
c_{X}(E)
.
For a closed point
x\in X
, we define the
minimal log discrepancy
at
x
as

\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,\mathfrak{a}):=\inf_{c_{X}(E)=\{x\}}a_{E}(X,\mathfrak{a})

if
\dim X\geq 2
, where the infimum is taken over all prime divisors
E
over
X
with center
c_{X}(E)=\{x\}
.
It is known that
\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,\mathfrak{a})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{-\infty\}
in this case (cf.
[
10
]
*Corollary 2.31).
When
\dim X=1
, we define
\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,\mathfrak{a}):=\inf_{c_{X}(E)=\{x\}}a_{E}(X,\mathfrak{a})

if the infimum is non-negative, and we define
\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,\mathfrak{a}):=-\infty
otherwise.

Let
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X
.
Take
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\ell D
is Cartier.
Then we define

a_{E}(X,D):=a_{E}\bigl(X,(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\ell D))^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\bigr),\quad\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,D):=\operatorname{mld}_{x}\bigl(X,(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\ell D))^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\bigr).

Note that these values do not depend on the choice of
\ell
.

When
\mathfrak{a}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
, we write
a_{E}(X):=a_{E}(X,\mathfrak{a})
and
\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X):=\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,\mathfrak{a})
for simplicity.

2.2.
Arc spaces of
k
-schemes

In this subsection, we briefly review the definitions and some properties of jet schemes and arc spaces. The reader is referred to
[
6
]
for details.

Let
X
be a scheme over
k
.
Let
({\sf Sch}/k)
be the category of
k
-schemes and
({\sf Sets})
the category of sets.
Define a contravariant functor
F_{m}\colon({\sf Sch}/k)\to({\sf Sets})
by

F_{m}(Y)=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}\left(Y\times_{\operatorname{Spec}k}\operatorname{Spec}k[t]/(t^{m+1}),X\right).

This functor
F_{m}
is represented by a scheme
X_{m}
over
k
, and
the scheme
X_{m}
is called the
m
-th
jet scheme
of
X
.
For
m\geq n\geq 0
, the canonical surjective homomorphism
k[t]/(t^{m+1})\to k[t]/(t^{n+1})
induces a morphism
\pi_{mn}\colon X_{m}\to X_{n}
.
The projective limit

X_{\infty}:=\varprojlim_{m}X_{m}

exists, together with natural projections
\psi_{m}\colon X_{\infty}\to X_{m}
. The scheme
X_{\infty}
is called the
arc space
of
X
.
For any field extension
K
of
k
, there is a natural bijection

\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(\operatorname{Spec}K,X_{\infty})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(\operatorname{Spec}K[[t]],X).

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
and a morphism
f\colon Y\to X
of schemes of finite type over
k
,
we denote by
f_{m}\colon Y_{m}\to X_{m}
the morphism induced by
f
.

A subset
C\subset X_{\infty}
is called a
cylinder
if
C=\psi_{m}^{-1}(S)
holds for some
m\geq 0
and
a constructible subset
S\subset X_{m}
. A typical example of a cylinder appearing in this paper is the
contact locus

\operatorname{Cont}^{\geq m}(\mathfrak{a})
, which is defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

(1)

For an arc
\alpha\in X_{\infty}
represented by a morphism
\alpha\colon\operatorname{Spec}K[[t]]\to X
(where
K
is a field extension of
k
) and an ideal sheaf
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
,
the
order
of
\mathfrak{a}
measured by
\alpha
is defined as

\operatorname{ord}_{\alpha}(\mathfrak{a})=\sup\{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid\alpha^{*}(\mathfrak{a})\subset(t^{r})\}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\},

where
\alpha^{*}(\mathfrak{a})
denotes the ideal of
K[[t]]
generated by the image of
\mathfrak{a}
.

(2)

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the
contact locus

\operatorname{Cont}^{\geq m}(\mathfrak{a})\subset X_{\infty}
is defined as

\operatorname{Cont}^{\geq m}(\mathfrak{a})=\{\alpha\in X_{\infty}\mid\operatorname{ord}_{\alpha}(\mathfrak{a})\geq m\}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a variety over
k
. A subset
A\subset X_{\infty}
is said to be
thin
if
A\subset Z_{\infty}
for some proper closed subscheme
Z
of
X
.

Remark 2.3
.

For a cylinder
A\subset X_{\infty}
,
A
is thin if and only if
A\subset(X_{\rm sing})_{\infty}
, where
X_{\rm sing}
denotes the singular locus of
X
(see
[
6
]
*Lemma 5.1).

2.3.
Arc spaces of
k[t]
-schemes

In this subsection, we briefly discuss arc spaces of
k[t]
-schemes.
We refer the reader to
[
3
]
and
[
12
]
for further details.

Let
X
be a scheme over
k[t]
.
For a non-negative integer

, we define a contravariant functor
F^{X}_{m}\colon({\sf Sch}/k)\to({\sf Sets})
by

F^{X}_{m}(Y)=\operatorname{Hom}_{k[t]}\left(Y\times_{\operatorname{Spec}k}\operatorname{Spec}k[t]/(t^{m+1}),X\right).

This functor
F^{X}_{m}
is represented by a scheme
X_{m}
over
k
.
We retain the notation
X_{\infty}
,
\psi_{m}
, and
\pi_{mn}
used in the previous setting:

X_{\infty}:=\mathop{\varprojlim}\limits_{m}X_{m},\qquad\psi_{m}\colon X_{\infty}\to X_{m},\qquad\pi_{mn}\colon X_{m}\to X_{n}.

A subset

is called a

if it is of the form

for some integer
m\geq 0
and a constructible subset
S\subset X_{m}
.
The
contact locus

\operatorname{Cont}^{\geq m}(\mathfrak{a})
is defined in the same way as in Definition
2.1
.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
X
be a scheme of finite type over
k
, and let
X^{\prime}=X\times_{\operatorname{Spec}k}\operatorname{Spec}k[t]
be its base change to
k[t]
.
Then there is a natural isomorphism
X_{m}\simeq X^{\prime}_{m}
for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
.
Here,
X_{m}
denotes the jet or arc scheme over
k
as defined in Subsection
2.2
, while
X^{\prime}_{m}
denotes the corresponding scheme over
k[t]
as defined in this subsection.
Therefore, the theory of arc spaces of
k[t]
-schemes can be viewed as a generalization of that for
k
-schemes.

Remark 2.5
.

The schemes
X_{m}
and
X_{\infty}
coincide with the Greenberg schemes
\operatorname{Gr}_{m}(\mathfrak{X})
and
\operatorname{Gr}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{X})
(as defined in
[
16
]
and
[
2
]
) of the formal scheme
\mathfrak{X}
over
k[[t]]
associated to the
k[t]
-scheme
X
. We refer the reader to
[
12
]
*Remark 2.14 for details.

Remark 2.6
.

Let
X
be a scheme of finite type over
k[t]
.
Let
X^{(1)},\ldots,X^{(\ell)}
be the irreducible components of
X
that dominate
\operatorname{Spec}k[t]
.
Then the arc space
X_{\infty}
decomposes into the union of the arc spaces of these components:
X_{\infty}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}X^{(i)}_{\infty}
.

Remark 2.7
.

In
[
12
]
, the authors introduce the condition
(\star)_{n}
below, and they study the arc spaces of such
k[t]
-schemes

.

(\star)_{n}
:
X
is a scheme of finite type over
\operatorname{Spec}k[t]
.
Any irreducible component of

has dimension at least

.
Furthermore, any irreducible component dominating
\operatorname{Spec}k[t]
is exactly
(n+1)
-dimensional.

Definition 2.8
.

Let

be a non-negative integer, and
let
X
be a scheme of finite type over

satisfying
(\star)_{n}
.
A subset

is called

if
A\subset Z_{\infty}
holds for some closed subscheme

of

with
\dim Z\leq n
.

Remark 2.9
.

(1)

Suppose that
X
is a variety over
k[t]
and
A
is a cylinder in Definition
2.8
.
Then
A
is thin if and only if
A\subset W_{\infty}
,
where
W\subset X
is the locus defined by the relative Jacobian ideal
\operatorname{Jac}_{X/k[t]}
(see
[
12
]
*Lemma 2.23).

(2)

By Remark
2.4
, for a variety
X
over
k
, its arc space

is naturally identified with the arc space of

viewed as a

-scheme.
Under this identification, a cylinder

is thin in the sense of Definition

if and only if the corresponding cylinder is thin in the sense of Definition

.
This equivalence follows from Remark

and the property explained in (1) above.

(3)

If we consider a general subset

of

that is not necessarily a cylinder, the equivalence in (2) no longer holds.
While any subset that is thin in the sense of Definition

is always thin in the sense of Definition

, the converse is not true in general.

2.4.
Virtually free actions

In this subsection, following
[
11
]
, we define the concept of virtually free actions and summarize their main properties established therein.

Definition 2.10
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a normal variety
X
.
We say that the
G
-action on
X
is
virtually free
if the following condition holds:
for every
G
-equivariant proper birational morphism
Y\to X
from a normal variety
Y
, the
G
-action on
Y
is free in codimension one.

This definition admits the following equivalent characterizations.

Proposition 2.11

(
[
11
]
*Proposition 3.8)
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a normal variety
X
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The
G
-action on
X
is virtually free.

(2)

There exists a
G
-equivariant proper birational morphism
Y\to X
from a normal variety
Y
such that the
G
-action on
Y
is free.

(3)

For every
G
-equivariant proper birational morphism
Y\to X
from a smooth variety
Y
, the
G
-action on
Y
is free.

Remark 2.12
.

By definition, we have the following implications for a
G
-action on
X
:

\text{free}\implies\text{virtually free}\implies\text{free in codimension one}.

Theorem
2.14
below shows that the converse “virtually free
\implies
free” holds when
X
is klt.
In Example
2.13
, we provide an example of an action that is virtually free but not free.

Example 2.13
.

Let
\xi\in k
be a primitive cube root of unity.
Let
H\subset\mathbb{A}_{k}^{3}
be the hypersurface defined by
x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3}=0
.
Let
\gamma:=\operatorname{diag}(1,\xi,\xi^{2})\in\operatorname{GL}_{3}(k)
.
Then, the
\langle\gamma\rangle
-action on
H
is virtually free.
Indeed, one can see that the induced
\langle\gamma\rangle
-action on the blow-up
\operatorname{Bl}_{0}H
of
H
at the origin
(0,0,0)
is free (see
[
11
]
*Lemma 4.1 for more details).

An important property of virtual freeness is that, as noted in Remark
2.12
, the converse implication
“
\text{virtually free}\implies\text{free}
” holds true provided that
X
is klt.

Theorem 2.14

(
[
11
]
*Theorem 3.10)
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a klt variety
X
.
If the
G
-action on
X
is virtually free, then this action is free.

The following proposition is also an important property of virtually free actions: it asserts that the minimal log discrepancy is preserved under the quotient, precisely as if the action were free.

Proposition 2.15

(
[
11
]
*Proposition 3.5)
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a normal
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein variety
X
.
Suppose that the
G
-action on
X
is virtually free.
Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a non-zero
\mathbb{R}
-ideal sheaf on
X/G
.
Then, we have

\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{X})=\operatorname{mld}_{x^{\prime}}(X/G,\mathfrak{a})

for any closed point
x\in X
and its image
x^{\prime}
on the quotient variety
X/G
.

3.
Virtually free actions and their characterizations

In this section, we fix a variety
X
and a finite group
G
of order
d
.
Suppose that
G
acts on
X
.
We also fix a primitive
d
-th root of unity
\xi\in k
.
For
d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we define

T:=\operatorname{Spec}k[t],\qquad T_{1/d^{\prime}}:=\operatorname{Spec}k[t^{1/d^{\prime}}].

Definition 3.1
.

(1)

For an element
\gamma\in G
of order
d^{\prime}
, we define

X^{[\gamma]}:=(X\times T_{1/d^{\prime}})/\langle\gamma\rangle,

where
\langle\gamma\rangle
acts diagonally on
X\times T_{1/d^{\prime}}
such that the action of
\gamma
on the coordinate ring of
T_{1/d^{\prime}}
is given by
t^{1/d^{\prime}}\mapsto\xi^{d/d^{\prime}}t^{1/d^{\prime}}
.

(2)

Since the second projection
X\times T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to T_{1/d^{\prime}}
is
\gamma
-equivariant and we have a natural identification
T_{1/d^{\prime}}/\langle\gamma\rangle\cong T
,
it induces a morphism

X^{[\gamma]}\to T.

Therefore,
X^{[\gamma]}
naturally has the structure of a
k[t]
-scheme.
We define
X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
as the arc space of the
k[t]
-scheme
X^{[\gamma]}
in the sense of Subsection
2.3
.

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

The fiber of
X^{[\gamma]}\to T
over any non-zero closed point
t=c\neq 0
is isomorphic to
X
.
The central fiber over
t=0
is generally a non-reduced scheme, and its reduced structure is isomorphic to the quotient variety
X/\langle\gamma\rangle
.

(2)

In particular,
X^{[\gamma]}
satisfies the condition
(\star)_{n}
in Remark
2.7
with
n=\dim X
.

(3)

Over
T\setminus\{0\}
, the base change
T_{1/d^{\prime}}\setminus\{0\}\to T\setminus\{0\}
is a Galois cover with Galois group
\langle\gamma\rangle
.
Hence, the restriction of
X^{[\gamma]}
over
T\setminus\{0\}
coincides with the classical twist of
X
by the
\langle\gamma\rangle
-torsor
T_{1/d^{\prime}}\setminus\{0\}\to T\setminus\{0\}
.
The scheme
X^{[\gamma]}
can be viewed as a “branched version” of the twisted variety associated with the
G
-action on
X
.

We now discuss the basic properties of
X^{[\gamma]}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For an element
\gamma\in G
of order
d^{\prime}
, the following assertions hold.

(1)

There exists a natural finite surjective morphism

X\times T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to X^{[\gamma]}\times_{T}T_{1/d^{\prime}},

which induces an isomorphism

X\times(T_{1/d^{\prime}}\setminus\{0\})\xrightarrow{\sim}\bigl(X^{[\gamma]}|_{t\neq 0}\bigr)\times_{T\setminus\{0\}}(T_{1/d^{\prime}}\setminus\{0\})

over
T_{1/d^{\prime}}\setminus\{0\}
.

(2)

There exists a one-to-one correspondence between
the set of sections of
X^{[\gamma]}\to T
and the set of
\gamma
-equivariant morphisms
T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to X
.

(3)

Let
X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
be the arc space of the
k[t]
-scheme
X^{[\gamma]}
.
For any field extension
K
of
k
, there is a natural bijection

X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}(K)\simeq\left\{\operatorname{Spec}K[[t^{1/d^{\prime}}]]\to X\;\middle|\;\text{\rm$\gamma$-equivariant}\right\}.

Proof.

(1) The following two compositions coincide:

X\times T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to X^{[\gamma]}=(X\times T_{1/d^{\prime}})/\langle\gamma\rangle\to T\quad\text{and}\quad X\times T_{1/d^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{p_{2}}T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to T.

Therefore, by the universal property of fiber products, we obtain a morphism

X\times T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to X^{[\gamma]}\times_{T}T_{1/d^{\prime}},\quad(x,s)\mapsto\bigl(\overline{(x,s)},s\bigr).

Since
X^{[\gamma]}
is the quotient by a finite group, this induced morphism is finite and surjective. The fact that it is an isomorphism outside
t=0
follows because the
\langle\gamma\rangle
-action on
T_{1/d^{\prime}}
is free away from the origin.

(2) For a
\gamma
-equivariant morphism
f\colon T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to X
, the map

T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to X\times T_{1/d^{\prime}},\quad s\mapsto(f(s),s)

is
\gamma
-equivariant, and hence it descends to a morphism
T\to X^{[\gamma]}
, which is a section of
X^{[\gamma]}\to T
.

Conversely, given a section
\sigma\colon T\to X^{[\gamma]}
of
X^{[\gamma]}\to T
, the base change induces a section

T_{1/d^{\prime}}\cong T\times_{T}T_{1/d^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{\sigma^{\prime}}X^{[\gamma]}\times_{T}T_{1/d^{\prime}}

of the projection
X^{[\gamma]}\times_{T}T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to T_{1/d^{\prime}}
.
Since the morphism
q\colon X\times T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to X^{[\gamma]}\times_{T}T_{1/d^{\prime}}
is finite (in particular, proper) and an isomorphism over
T_{1/d^{\prime}}\setminus\{0\}
, the valuative criterion of properness guarantees that this section lifts uniquely to a section

\widetilde{f}\colon T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to X\times T_{1/d^{\prime}}

of the projection
X\times T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to T_{1/d^{\prime}}
.

\textstyle{X\times T_{1/d^{\prime}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\textstyle{T_{1/d^{\prime}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\widetilde{f}}
\scriptstyle{\sigma^{\prime}}
\textstyle{X^{[\gamma]}\times_{T}T_{1/d^{\prime}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma}
\textstyle{X^{[\gamma]}}

This
\widetilde{f}
is necessarily a
\gamma
-equivariant map.
Indeed, since
\sigma^{\prime}
and
q
are
\gamma
-equivariant, the conjugated morphism
\gamma^{-1}\circ\widetilde{f}\circ\gamma
is also a lift of the section
\sigma^{\prime}
, which must coincide with
\widetilde{f}
by uniqueness.
Therefore,
\widetilde{f}
yields a
\gamma
-equivariant morphism
T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to X
.

It is straightforward to check that these two correspondences are mutually inverse.

(3) The assertion follows from the exact same argument as in the proof of (2), simply by replacing
T
and
T_{1/d^{\prime}}
with
\operatorname{Spec}K[[t]]
and
\operatorname{Spec}K[[t^{1/d^{\prime}}]]
, respectively.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

For an element
\gamma\in G
of order
d^{\prime}
,
the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

The morphism
X^{[\gamma]}\to T
has a section.

(2)

There exists a
\gamma
-equivariant morphism
T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to X
.

(3)

X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}\neq\emptyset
.

(4)

X
has a
\gamma
-fixed closed point.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.3
(2), (1) and (2) are equivalent.

Next, we prove the equivalence (2)
\Leftrightarrow
(4).
If
f\colon T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to X
is a
\gamma
-equivariant morphism, the point
f(0)
is necessarily a
\gamma
-fixed point.
Conversely, for a
\gamma
-fixed point
x\in X
, the constant morphism
f\colon T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to X
factoring through
x
is naturally a
\gamma
-equivariant morphism.

By Proposition
3.3
(3), the equivalence (3)
\Leftrightarrow
(4) is proved in the same way as (2)
\Leftrightarrow
(4) by replacing
T_{1/d^{\prime}}
with
\operatorname{Spec}k[[t^{1/d^{\prime}}]]
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a normal variety
X
.
Then, the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The
G
-action on
X
is virtually free.

(2)

For any
\gamma\in G\setminus\{1_{G}\}
,
X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
is a thin set of
X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
itself.

Proof.

We shall prove the implication (1)
\Rightarrow
(2).
Suppose that there exists
\gamma\in G\setminus\{1_{G}\}
such that
X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
is not a thin set of
X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
itself.
Let
f\colon Y\to X
be an arbitrary
G
-equivariant resolution,
and let
Z\subset X
be a
G
-invariant closed subset such that
f
is an isomorphism outside
Z
.
Then, we can identify
Z^{[\gamma]}:=(Z\times T_{1/d^{\prime}})/\langle\gamma\rangle
with a closed subset of
X^{[\gamma]}
,
and hence,
Z^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
with a closed subset of
X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
.
Since
Z^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
is a thin set by definition, but
X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
is not a thin set of itself, there exists an arc
\alpha\in X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}\setminus Z^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
.
Since the induced proper morphism
Y^{[\gamma]}\to X^{[\gamma]}
is an isomorphism over
X^{[\gamma]}\setminus Z^{[\gamma]}
, the valuative criterion of properness guarantees that the arc
\alpha
lifts to an arc in
Y^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
.
Consequently, it follows that
Y^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}\neq\emptyset
.
By Corollary
3.4
,
Y
has a
\gamma
-fixed closed point.
Since
f\colon Y\to X
was an arbitrary resolution, the
G
-action on
X
is not virtually free by the equivalent condition for a virtually free action in Proposition
2.11
.

We shall prove the implication (2)
\Rightarrow
(1).
Suppose that the
G
-action on
X
is not virtually free.
Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a
G
-equivariant resolution.
By the equivalent condition for a virtually free action in Proposition
2.11
,
there exist
\gamma\in G\setminus\{1_{G}\}
and a closed point
y\in Y
such that
y
is a
\gamma
-fixed point.
Thus, by Corollary
3.4
,
Y^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}\neq\emptyset
.
Finally, by applying Lemma
3.6
below to the
k[t]
-morphism
Y^{[\gamma]}\to X^{[\gamma]}
, we conclude that
X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
is not a thin set of
X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
itself.
∎

Lemma 3.6

(cf.
[
12
]
*Lemma 2.27)
.

Let

be a proper birational

-morphism of

-varieties

.
Suppose that

is smooth over

.
If

Although

4.
Arc spaces of quotient varieties: general actions

In this section, we discuss the relationship between the arc space
X^{\prime}_{\infty}
of the quotient variety
X^{\prime}=X/G
and
the arc space
X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
introduced in Section

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a variety over

, and let
G
be a finite group of order

acting on

Let

be the quotient variety.
Let
\xi
be a primitive

(1)

For

{X^{\prime}}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}(K):=\left\{\varphi\in X^{\prime}_{\infty}(K)\;\middle|\;\begin{aligned} &\text{$\varphi$ lifts to a $K$-arc $\varphi^{\prime}\in X_{\infty}^{1/d}(K)$}\\

(2)

Let
\gamma\in G
be an element of order

.
Then, the
\gamma
-equivariant morphism

X\times T_{1/d^{\prime}}\to X^{\prime}\times T_{1/d^{\prime}};\quad(x,s)\mapsto(\overline{x},s)

induces a

which in turn induces a morphism on the arc spaces

h_{\infty}\colon X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}\to X^{\prime}_{\infty}

(cf. Remark

).

Remark 4.2
.

The subset
{X^{\prime}}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
was introduced by Denef and Loeser in
[

]
to study

via

, which is introduced in Section

.
By
[

]
*Section 2.1 (cf.
[

]
*Section 3,

]
*Proposition 3.4), we have

X^{\prime}_{\infty}\setminus Z^{\prime}_{\infty}=\bigsqcup_{[\gamma]\in\operatorname{Conj}(G)}\bigl({X^{\prime}}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}\setminus Z^{\prime}_{\infty}\bigr),

where
Z^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}
is the minimal closed subset such that the quotient map

is étale outside
Z^{\prime}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

In the setting of Definition

, the image of
h_{\infty}\colon X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}\to X^{\prime}_{\infty}
coincides with
{X^{\prime}}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Let

be a field extension.
Let
\varphi\in X^{\prime}_{\infty}(K)
be a

-arc.
By Proposition

(3),
\varphi\in\operatorname{Im}(h_{\infty})
if and only if there exists a

-equivariant morphism
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon\operatorname{Spec}K[[t^{1/d^{\prime}}]]\to X
making the following diagram commute:

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\textstyle{X^{\prime}}

5.
Arc spaces of quotient varieties: linear actions

In this section, we focus on the case where a finite group
G
acts linearly on an affine space.
In this setting, Denef and Loeser
[
3
]
introduced a certain
k[t]
-scheme, denoted by
X^{(\gamma)}
, to investigate the arc space of the quotient variety (cf.
[
12
]
).
The goal of this section is to recall the construction of
X^{(\gamma)}
and to compare it with the scheme
X^{[\gamma]}
introduced in Section
3
.
In particular, we will establish a natural bijection between their sets of
K
-arcs for any field extension
K
(Proposition
5.3
).

Let
N
be a positive integer, and let
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]
.
Let
G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{N}(k)
be a finite subgroup of order
d
.
Suppose that
G
linearly acts on
\mathbb{A}^{N}=\operatorname{Spec}R
.
Let
\xi
be a primitive
d
-th root of unity.

Let
\gamma\in G
be an element of order
d^{\prime}
.
Since
G
is a finite group,
\gamma
can be diagonalized with respect to a new basis
x_{1}^{(\gamma)},\ldots,x_{N}^{(\gamma)}
consisting of
k
-linear combinations of the original basis
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}
.
We may assume that the action of
\gamma
is represented by the diagonal matrix
\operatorname{diag}\left(\xi^{e_{1}},\ldots,\xi^{e_{N}}\right)
with respect to this basis, where
0\leq e_{i}\leq d-1
.
We define a
k
-algebra homomorphism
{\lambda}^{*}_{\gamma}\colon R\to R[t^{1/d}]
as follows:

{\lambda}^{*}_{\gamma}\colon R\to R[t^{1/d}];\quad x_{i}^{(\gamma)}\mapsto t^{e_{i}/d}x_{i}^{(\gamma)}.

We note that the ring homomorphism
{\lambda}^{*}_{\gamma}
does not depend on the choice of the basis
x_{1}^{(\gamma)},\ldots,x_{N}^{(\gamma)}
as long as
\gamma
is diagonalized with respect to it.

By trivially extending
{\lambda}^{*}_{\gamma}
to
R[t^{1/d^{\prime}}]
so that
{\lambda}^{*}_{\gamma}\bigl(t^{1/d^{\prime}}\bigr)=t^{1/d^{\prime}}
, it induces a
k[t]
-algebra homomorphism

{\lambda}^{*}_{\gamma}\colon\bigl(R[t^{1/d^{\prime}}]\bigr)^{\langle\gamma\rangle}\to R[t],

where the
\gamma
-action on
t^{1/d^{\prime}}
is defined by
\gamma\bigl(t^{1/d^{\prime}}\bigr)=\xi^{d/d^{\prime}}t^{1/d^{\prime}}
.
We note that the image of this homomorphism indeed falls into
R[t]
.
In fact, an arbitrary monomial
\bigl(t^{1/d^{\prime}}\bigr)^{m}\prod\bigl(x_{j}^{(\gamma)}\bigr)^{m_{j}}
belongs to the invariant subring
\bigl(R[t^{1/d^{\prime}}]\bigr)^{\langle\gamma\rangle}
if and only if
\xi^{m(d/d^{\prime})+\sum m_{j}e_{j}}=1
, which means
m(d/d^{\prime})+\sum m_{j}e_{j}
is a multiple of
d
.
Since its image under
\lambda^{*}_{\gamma}
is given by
t^{m/d^{\prime}+\sum m_{j}e_{j}/d}\prod\bigl(x_{j}^{(\gamma)}\bigr)^{m_{j}}
, the exponent of
t
is exactly
\frac{1}{d}\bigl(m(d/d^{\prime})+\sum m_{j}e_{j}\bigr)
, which is an integer.
Geometrically,
{\lambda}^{*}_{\gamma}
induces a morphism of schemes over
T
:

\lambda_{\gamma}\colon\mathbb{A}^{N}\times T\to(\mathbb{A}^{N})^{[\gamma]}.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{c}\in R^{G}
be a regular sequence which is contained in the maximal ideal
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})^{G}
.
We define

X:=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(R/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{c})\bigr)\subset\mathbb{A}^{N},\quad X^{\prime}:=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(R^{G}/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{c})\bigr).

Then, we have
X^{\prime}\cong X/G
.
Suppose that
X
is normal.

Since the polynomials
f_{i}
are
G
-invariant (in particular,
\gamma
-invariant), we have

F_{i}:=\lambda^{*}_{\gamma}(f_{i})\in R[t].

We define a
k[t]
-scheme

X^{(\gamma)}:=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(R[t]/(F_{1},\ldots,F_{c})\bigr)\subset\mathbb{A}^{N}\times T.

Then,
\lambda_{\gamma}\colon\mathbb{A}^{N}\times T\to(\mathbb{A}^{N})^{[\gamma]}
induces a morphism

\lambda_{\gamma}\colon X^{(\gamma)}\to X^{[\gamma]}.

Let
X^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
denote its arc space as a
k[t]
-scheme.

Remark 5.1
.

The morphism
\lambda_{\gamma}\colon X^{(\gamma)}\to X^{[\gamma]}
is an isomorphism over
T\setminus\{0\}
.
Indeed, over the open subset where
t\neq 0
, the ring homomorphism
\lambda_{\gamma}^{*}\colon\bigl(R[t^{1/d^{\prime}}]\bigr)^{\langle\gamma\rangle}\to R[t]
has an inverse defined by

x_{i}^{(\gamma)}\mapsto t^{-e_{i}/d}x_{i}^{(\gamma)}.

Note here that the element
t^{-e_{i}/d}x_{i}^{(\gamma)}
is
\gamma
-invariant.
Therefore,
\lambda_{\gamma}
gives an isomorphism outside
t=0
.

Remark 5.2
.

In particular,
X^{(\gamma)}
satisfies the condition
(\star)_{n}
in Remark
2.7
with
n:=\dim X=N-c
.

Proposition 5.3
.

The morphism
\lambda_{\gamma}\colon X^{(\gamma)}\to X^{[\gamma]}
induces a bijection
\lambda_{\gamma,\infty}\colon X^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}(K)\xrightarrow{\sim}X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}(K)
for any field extension
K
of
k
.
Furthermore, this bijection is a homeomorphism with respect to the induced Zariski topology.

Proof.

Since the inverse image of
X^{[\gamma]}
by the morphism
\lambda_{\gamma}\colon\mathbb{A}^{N}\times T\to(\mathbb{A}^{N})^{[\gamma]}
coincides with
X^{(\gamma)}
by definition, it is sufficient to show the assertions for the case
X=\mathbb{A}^{N}=\operatorname{Spec}R
.

A
K
-arc in
(\mathbb{A}^{N}\times T)_{\infty}
corresponds to a
k[t]
-algebra homomorphism
R[t]\to K[[t]]
, which is uniquely determined by assigning
x_{i}^{(\gamma)}\mapsto\alpha_{i}(t)\in K[[t]]
for each
i
.
On the other hand, a
K
-arc in
(\mathbb{A}^{N})^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
corresponds to a
\gamma
-equivariant
k
-algebra homomorphism
\varphi^{*}\colon R\to K[[t^{1/d^{\prime}}]]
.
Since
\gamma
acts on
x_{i}^{(\gamma)}
by
\xi^{e_{i}}
and on
t^{1/d^{\prime}}
by
\xi^{d/d^{\prime}}
, this equivariance condition means

\xi^{e_{i}}\varphi^{*}\bigl(x_{i}^{(\gamma)}\bigr)\bigl(t^{1/d^{\prime}}\bigr)=\varphi^{*}\bigl(x_{i}^{(\gamma)}\bigr)\bigl(\xi^{d/d^{\prime}}t^{1/d^{\prime}}\bigr).

This equality forces all non-zero terms in the power series
\varphi^{*}\bigl(x_{i}^{(\gamma)}\bigr)=\sum c_{m}\bigl(t^{1/d^{\prime}}\bigr)^{m}\in K[[t^{1/d^{\prime}}]]
to satisfy
\xi^{m(d/d^{\prime})}=\xi^{e_{i}}
.
Since
\xi
is a primitive
d
-th root of unity, this is equivalent to
m(d/d^{\prime})\equiv e_{i}\pmod{d}
, which means
m/d^{\prime}\equiv e_{i}/d\pmod{1}
.
Therefore,
\varphi^{*}\bigl(x_{i}^{(\gamma)}\bigr)
can be uniquely factored as

\varphi^{*}\bigl(x_{i}^{(\gamma)}\bigr)=t^{e_{i}/d}\alpha_{i}(t)

for some power series
\alpha_{i}(t)\in K[[t]]
.
Note that since
\gamma^{d^{\prime}}=1
, we have
\xi^{e_{i}d^{\prime}}=1
, implying
e_{i}
is a multiple of
d/d^{\prime}
. Thus
t^{e_{i}/d}
is an integer power of
t^{1/d^{\prime}}
.

Since the morphism
\lambda_{\gamma}
is defined by the ring homomorphism
\lambda_{\gamma}^{*}\bigl(x_{i}^{(\gamma)}\bigr)=t^{e_{i}/d}x_{i}^{(\gamma)}
, the induced map on the arc spaces exactly sends the arc
x_{i}^{(\gamma)}\mapsto\alpha_{i}(t)
to the
\gamma
-equivariant arc
\varphi^{*}\bigl(x_{i}^{(\gamma)}\bigr)=t^{e_{i}/d}\alpha_{i}(t)
.
This correspondence gives a natural bijection between the sets of
K
-arcs.

Finally, we observe that this bijection is a homeomorphism.
As shown above, a
K
-arc in either
X^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}(K)
or
X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}(K)
is determined by the exact same set of coefficients of the power series
\alpha_{i}(t)\in K[[t]]
for
1\leq i\leq N
.
Both spaces of
K
-arcs are naturally identified with the inverse limit of affine spaces
\varprojlim_{m}\mathbb{A}^{N(m+1)}_{K}
parameterized by these coefficients.
Since the bijection
\lambda_{\gamma,\infty}
simply acts as the identity map under this natural identification of coordinates, it is a homeomorphism.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

For a subset
C\subset X^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

C
is a thin set of
X^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
.

(2)

\lambda_{\gamma,\infty}(C)
is a thin set of
X^{[\gamma]}_{\infty}
.

Proof.

The assertion follows from Remarks
2.6
and
5.1
.
Indeed, by Remark

, the arc spaces
X^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
and

only depend on the irreducible components dominating

.
Since
\lambda_{\gamma}
is an isomorphism over the open subset

by Remark

, it induces a natural bijection between the proper closed subsets of
X^{(\gamma)}
dominating
\operatorname{Spec}k[t]
and those of
X^{[\gamma]}
.
Therefore,
C
is contained in the arc space of a proper closed subset if and only if
\lambda_{\gamma,\infty}(C)
is, which completes the proof.
∎

We begin by introducing a localized version of a virtually free action for a fixed element
\gamma\in G
at a closed point

.

Definition 6.1
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a normal variety

.

(1)

For a closed point

(2)

For a closed point

at

\gamma
-action on

for all
\gamma\in G\setminus\{1_{G}\}
.

Similar to Proposition

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a normal variety
X
.
For a closed point
x\in X
and an element
\gamma\in G

(1)

The
\gamma
-action on

.

(2)

There exists a
\gamma
-equivariant proper birational morphism

\gamma
-fixed closed point.

For every
\gamma
-equivariant proper birational morphism

\gamma
-fixed closed point.

Proof.

The equivalence follows from the same argument as in

]
*Proposition 3.8.
∎

.

.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{mld}_{x^{\prime}}(Y^{\prime},\mathfrak{a})

\displaystyle=\min_{\gamma\in S}\operatorname{mld}_{x^{\prime}_{\gamma}}\bigl(X/\langle\gamma\rangle,(f_{1}\cdots f_{c})\mathfrak{b}\mathcal{O}_{X/\langle\gamma\rangle}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in G;\\
x^{\prime}\in Z^{\prime}_{\gamma}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{mld}_{x^{\prime}_{\gamma}}\bigl(X/\langle\gamma\rangle,(f_{1}\cdots f_{c})\mathfrak{b}\mathcal{O}_{X/\langle\gamma\rangle}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\min_{\gamma\in G}g_{\gamma}(x^{\prime}).

Since each
g_{\gamma}
is lower semi-continuous, their finite minimum
x^{\prime}\mapsto\operatorname{mld}_{x^{\prime}}(Y^{\prime},\mathfrak{a})
is also lower semi-continuous on
Y^{\prime}
.
This completes the proof.
∎
