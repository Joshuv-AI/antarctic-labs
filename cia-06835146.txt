Although the UN-backed arms embargo and West African sanctions are

, increasingly affecting rebel leader Taylor’s operations, he has a well
Stocked arsenal, and sanctions are not likely to force him to accept a
political settlement.

At the regional summit in Abuja last month, West African countries
decided to enforce sanctions more aggressively in an effort to bring
Taylor back to the negotiating table. The UN Security Council
decision last month to impose an arms embargo _on Liberia’s warring
factions gave the sanctions additional impetus.

Enforcement Having Some Impact

Since carly last month, the regional security force in Liberia
(ECOMOG) has blockaded rebel-controled ports, attacked key rebel
facilities, and disrupted their air and logistic operations. Airstrikes
and naval bombardments of port facilities by ECOMOG have
seriously disrupted commercial shipping. The West Africans have
repeatedly bombed Roberts International Airfield, a key arms transit
pent, and are planning a major ground operation to seize the airfield.

The West African countries, led by Nigeria, last month launched a
diplomatic offensive to press Ivory Coast and Burkina, two longtime
Taylor supporters, to adhere to the sanctions. Ivorian President
Houphouet-Boigny last month decided to close his border with
Liberia because of growing diplomatic pressure and doubts about
Taylor's commitment to negotiations. Ivory Coast has seized at least
