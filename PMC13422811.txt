The DECIDE-T protocol: adoption and enhancement of a care pathway for children with medical complexity – an implementation evaluation project for paediatric tracheostomy

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMedical advancements have led to increased survival for critically ill children, many of whom require tracheostomy. Standardised protocols in paediatric tracheostomy have been associated with reduced hospital length of stay, lower readmission rates and reduced acute healthcare costs, without compromising safety. DECIDE-T (Digital hEalth pathway for ChIldren with meDical complExity requiring Tracheostomy) is a novel initiative that moves beyond single-site quality-improvement efforts to deliver a provincially coordinated, multicentre digital care pathway integrated into the electronic health record system. The initiative spans the full care continuum—from decision-making and acute care to home transition and decannulation—with the goal of improving clinical outcomes, efficiency and caregiver experience.Methods and analysisDECIDE-T is a multicentre single-arm pre–post implementation study that applies multiple theory-based frameworks for implementation and evaluation. It incorporates both quantitative and qualitative research methods to assess the implementation and impact of the intervention. Guided by the Quality Implementation Framework and the Interactive Systems Framework, the study includes four phases: (1) Site assessments to identify implementation barriers and facilitators using the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR); (2) Developing a comprehensive plan and implementation strategies using the CFIR-Expert Recommendations for Implementing Change Matching Tool; (3) Pathway roll-out and evaluation of clinical, process and economic outcomes guided by the RE-AIM framework (reach, effectiveness, adoption, implementation and maintenance); and (4) Dissemination of lessons learnt from the implementation experience. Data collection will involve surveys, interviews, focus groups, care pathway audits, patients’ clinical and administrative data, analysed using thematic content analysis and interrupted time series analysis. The economic evaluation will be conducted from the perspective of the public healthcare system through a net-monetary benefit analysis.Ethics and disseminationThe study was reviewed and approved by the University of Alberta Health Research Ethics Board. The results will be disseminated to DECIDE-T care pathway end users, partners and collaborators. Background

Medical advancements have increased survival rates among critically ill children, many of whom require tracheostomy (a breathing tube in the neck) with or without long-term ventilation (tracheostomy/LTV).1–3 Although a relatively small population, with an annual median incidence of 2.4 per 100 000 children in Alberta, Canada,4 these children represent one of the medically complex paediatric patients,5–7 requiring prolonged hospitalisation (median 107 days) and incurring healthcare costs of approximately $C349 500 per patient annually.4

Evidence supporting standardised digital-based care pathways has been well documented in the literature. Implementation of such standardised protocols in paediatric tracheostomy has been associated with reduced hospital length of stay (LOS) and re-admission rates, contributing to a lower acute healthcare cost.8–11 Moreover, other outcomes, including mortality and tracheostomy-related adverse events, have trended down or remained unchanged after the implementation of standardised care pathways, suggesting no negative impact on patient safety.8 11–15 Additionally, instituting such interventions has the potential to contribute positively to caregivers’ knowledge and confidence in hospital and outpatient tracheostomy care, reduce caregivers’ burden, promote coordinated care, improve care efficiency and reduce critical airway emergencies,10 16 17 and ultimately, improve parental trust in the healthcare systems.

While prior studies have shown that focused, single-site interventions such as standardised discharge processes, multidisciplinary airway teams, or ‘go-bag’ standardisation can reduce LOS and improve safety, these typically address one phase of care at one centre (eg, a team-based discharge protocol or local airway team quality improvement project).8 9 14

The DECIDE-T (Digital hEalth pathway for ChIldren with meDical complExity requiring Tracheostomy) initiative is novel because it moves beyond single-site, single-solution quality-improvement efforts to deliver a provincially coordinated, multicentre digital care pathway integrated into the provincial electronic health record system. The care pathway spans the full paediatric tracheostomy care continuum, from shared decision-making to the tracheostomy procedure, inpatient stabilisation, hospital-to-home transition, telemedicine follow-up and eventual decannulation.18 We hypothesise that this model of care will reduce hospital LOS and associated costs without adversely impacting patient outcomes while also improving care satisfaction for both healthcare providers (HCPs) and family caregivers.

Methods and analysis

Study design

DECIDE-T is a multicentre single-arm pre–post implementation study that applies multiple theory-based frameworks for implementation and evaluation (figure 1). It incorporates both quantitative and qualitative research methods to assess the implementation and impact of the intervention. This protocol conforms to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials guidelines (online supplemental table 1).19 20 The before-and-after implementation trial to evaluate the clinical effectiveness and cost-effectiveness of the intervention is registered on clinicaltrials.gov (NCT06375369; 19 April 2024). The economic evaluation will be conducted alongside the trial and will be reported in accordance with the Consolidated Health Economics Evaluation Reporting Standards checklist.21

Study setting

Participation sites include the two paediatric tertiary care centres in the province of Alberta, Canada: the Stollery Children’s Hospital (SCH) and the Alberta Children’s Hospital (ACH). These hospitals serve paediatric populations (ages 0–17 years) in the province of Alberta and those referred from surrounding provinces and the Northwest Territories of Canada with specialised needs. The SCH performs approximately 20–30 tracheostomies per year, while the ACH performs about 5–10 tracheostomies annually.4 In both hospitals, multiple units will implement the DECIDE-T care pathway, including two neonatal intensive care units (ICUs), three paediatric ICUs, two operating units/surgical services involved in tracheostomy placement, eight inpatient paediatric wards and two outpatient multidisciplinary clinics. Implementation of the care pathway will occur simultaneously at both hospitals after the care pathway has been tailored to the local context of each hospital through engagement with HCPs and family caregivers.

System engagement and project governance

The key interested partners engaged in the design and implementation of this study include family caregivers, front-line HCPs, hospital administrators, senior operational management, homecare programmes and organisations involved in the care of children with a tracheostomy. Following the Canadian Institutes of Health Research recommendations for patient-oriented research, we engage HCPs and family caregivers as equal partners in all stages of this project and ensure shared decision-making between the research team, HCPs and families.22 We also engaged with implementation experts and academic colleagues who provided expertise in implementation methods and others such as indigenous organisations. A steering committee with representation from the different study partners was created with an advisory role to provide feedback on the content and implementation strategies. The steering committee provides guidance on implementation priorities, feasibility, workflow integration, evaluation metrics, dissemination and sustainability. Decisions are made through consensus. We continue to engage with these individuals and other collaborators to achieve quality implementation, applying the Interactive Systems Framework (ISF) to identify partners and collaborators’ roles within the clinical and research context.23 The ISF consists of three systems: (1) The delivery (implementation) system, which consists of those responsible for carrying out the activities needed to integrate the care pathway into clinical practice; (2) The support system, which builds capacity and supports implementation efforts at the hospitals throughout all stages of implementation; and (3) The synthesis and translation system, which is responsible for data collection and synthesis to support monitoring and evaluation of the care pathway and translates it into user-friendly formats for dissemination (figure 2).

Adoption and enhancement of the care pathway

The DECIDE-T care pathway builds on a previous quality improvement initiative at one of the participating hospitals (ACH: 2015–2018), resulting in the development and implementation of a road map aimed at improving decision-making and care coordination.24 Following this initiative, process mapping with project partners was completed at the provincial level through a series of site visits (2020–2022) to better understand the local contexts and explore the possibility of a provincial shared care pathway. Focus groups were conducted with family caregivers with lived experiences caring for a child with tracheostomy,25 and a survey was administered to front-line HCPs across care environments—from ICU to homecare programmes to analyse care gaps, discharge barriers and potential facilitators of care. This was followed by evidence reviews and syntheses26 27 to describe an ideal pathway, including the identification of standard practices, guidelines and evidence-based tools. This information informed a proposal for an integrated provincial care pathway and highlighted the need for digital health solutions that addressed some of the identified gaps and barriers.

Four provincial working groups were established to create and prioritise the components of the care pathway. Using a survey and purposive sampling strategy, we invited HCPs and homecare staff who provide care for children with tracheostomy and education for caregivers, hospital administrators who oversee hospital operations with key decision-making roles, and family caregivers with lived experience caring for a child with a tracheostomy or deciding for or against tracheostomy for their child to participate in the activities of the working groups. Individuals had the opportunity to select which working groups they wished to belong to. To obtain maximum variation in professional roles and practice settings, some individuals were directed to particular working groups by the study investigators. The four working groups were: (1) Conversations and decision-making; (2) Training and education; (3) Continuity of care; and (4) Community connections. The working groups are part of the support system from the ISF (see figure 2). The working groups used a nominal technique to develop the content and digital tools of each component of the care pathway and prioritised them based on feasibility and clinical applicability.

Research phases

The DECIDE-T care pathway will be implemented using the Quality Implementation Framework (QIF),37 a process model designed to facilitate the effective implementation of interventions. The phases of the QIF include site assessments, implementation, evaluation and sharing lessons learnt (figure 4).

Phase I: Site assessments

This phase aims at identifying perceived barriers and facilitators to the implementation of the DECIDE-T care pathway. We will create and share a preimplementation survey for completion by HCPs to explore readiness to change and potential barriers and facilitators to the implementation of the care pathway. Using a purposive sampling strategy, the survey will be distributed to HCPs by managers of all participating units of the two participating hospitals, respiratory departments, outpatient clinics and homecare programmes involved in the care of children with tracheostomy. In addition to demographic and profession-related questions, we will ask open-ended questions on barriers and enablers of implementing the care pathway. Participants will also be asked to provide their contact information for a follow-up interview to discuss potential barriers and facilitators to implementation of the care pathway in more depth. We anticipate approximately 50 completed surveys and 15 interviews based on previous participation of HCPs in the prestudy phase of this project. We will use REDCap electronic data capture tools hosted and supported by the Women and Children’s Health Research Institute at the University of Alberta38 39 to administer the survey. HCPs who provide their contact information will be contacted for an interview using a semistructured interview guide which will include questions related to intervention fit with existing workflow, available resources and support, culture of improvement and learning, communication and collaboration, leadership engagement, and sustainability. Interviews will be conducted virtually via Zoom and audio recorded. For verbatim transcription, we will use the transcription software Otter.ai (University of Alberta approved online data transcription service) followed by a human check of the transcript, and identifying information will be removed. We will perform content analysis using a deductive coding approach,40 guided by the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR).41 Two researchers will independently code the survey and interview data. They will then compare their coding, resolve discrepancies through discussion and iteratively discuss findings with the study team. They will focus the analysis on the most salient categories of the five CFIR domains41: (1) Intervention characteristics, (2) Outer setting, (3) Inner setting, (3) Characteristics of individuals, and (5) Process to identify the facilitators and barriers to the implementation of DECIDE-T. The results from this analysis will inform our implementation plan and strategies.

Phase II: Implementation plan and strategies

The goal of this phase is to create a comprehensive implementation plan that outlines the strategies for implementation, details how to operationalise these strategies and identifies the necessary supports required to deliver the care pathway. To address identified barriers to the implementation of the care pathway, we will use the CFIR-Expert Recommendations for Implementing Change (ERIC) matching tool42 to generate recommended implementation strategies based on perceived barriers from the preimplementation survey and interviews. Because the CFIR-ERIC matching tool is based on the original CFIR framework, we used the original rather than the updated CFIR, for which no validated ERIC mapping currently exists. This ensures consistency between identifying implementation determinants and selecting implementation strategies. The CFIR-ERIC tool was developed to close the gap between identifying theoretical barriers to implementation and strategies appropriate to overcome specific barriers.43 We will describe and operationalise the implementation strategies following Proctor’s guidelines for defining and reporting these strategies.44 This involves clearly naming the strategy, defining it conceptually and specifying its core components, including who delivers it, the target audience, the actions taken and the intended outcomes. Operationalisation requires describing the dose, duration and intensity of the strategy, as well as the context in which it is delivered, to ensure transparency and facilitate effective implementation in other settings. The support system of the ISF will provide supports such as tools, training, technical assistance and quality assurance to address needs and challenges that may arise during the implementation period.23

Phase III: Monitoring and evaluation

This phase will examine implementation quality using the RE-AIM framework, which includes reach, effectiveness, adoption, implementation and maintenance. The RE-AIM framework serves as an evaluation tool to measure the success of an intervention within a specific context.45 Adoption/uptake refers to the proportion and representativeness of settings, institutions and individuals (eg, HCPs, units) who initiate or deliver the care pathway. Reach focuses on the absolute number, proportion and representativeness of individuals who participate in or are affected by the care pathway. Implementation includes fidelity, consistency, adaptations and quality with which the care pathway is delivered. The evaluation dimension assesses the positive and negative impacts of the care pathway on important outcomes, including child-level outcomes, family caregiver outcomes, HCP outcomes and system-level outcomes. Maintenance is the extent to which the care pathway is sustained over time at both the individual and organisational levels.

We will use a multimethod approach to monitor the use and feasibility of the DECIDE-T care pathway. This will include interviews with HCPs and family caregivers to gather their perspectives and experiences, online surveys to assess satisfaction with the tools, and pathway audits/analytics to capture usage patterns and user characteristics. We will conduct a before-and-after implementation trial with interrupted time series analysis46 to assess the clinical outcomes and cost-effectiveness of the DECIDE-T care pathway (online supplemental table 2). We have developed monitoring and evaluation metrics to assess the various components of the RE-AIM framework (online supplemental table 3). These metrics were informed by the Evaluating QUality and ImPlementation tool,47 evidence synthesis on the assessment of the quality in care pathways48 49 and previous literature evaluating similar tracheostomy care pathways in paediatrics.8 9 11 12 16 50 51

Economic analysis

A net-monetary benefit analysis will be conducted to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of the DECIDE-T intervention from the perspective of a publicly funded healthcare system. Costs will be compared between preimplementation and postimplementation cohorts using a quasi-experimental design. Total healthcare costs will be estimated at the patient level and will include index hospitalisation, outpatient visits, physician billings, re-admissions within 12 months of discharge and prescription medications. Resource utilisation will be derived from linked administrative and clinical data sets, and unit costs will be obtained from standardised provincial sources, including the Canadian Institute for Health Information cost of a standard hospital stay, the Alberta Schedule of Medical Benefits and the Alberta Drug Benefit List. Mean costs will be estimated for each cohort, and incremental cost-effectiveness will be assessed by calculating the net monetary benefit. Healthcare cost avoidance attributable to DECIDE-T will be estimated as the difference in mean per-patient costs between preimplementation and postimplementation periods. The economic analysis will support policy makers and health system leaders in resource allocation, budget planning, prioritisation of digital care pathways and decision about sustaining the intervention.

Phase IV: Lessons from the implementation experience

This phase aims to share insights from our research findings. Our goal is to enhance future implementation efforts of patient-focused digital health solutions in similar settings. Additionally, we expect to strengthen interorganisational relationships through co-planning, developing, implementation and evaluation of the care pathway. We will collaborate with relevant partners to ensure that evaluation becomes an ongoing process within their organisations. For sustainability, we will create a provincial paediatric tracheostomy dashboard for long-term monitoring and evaluation, and set up a provincial tracheostomy committee that will own the outputs of this project and ensure continuous evaluation and updates of tools, resources and processes. The provincial tracheostomy committee will be supported by the child health programme within Acute Care Alberta and the participating health system partners.

Ethics and dissemination

The study was approved by the University of Alberta Health Research Ethics Board (HREB) (Pro00127121; 3 February 2023) following a comprehensive review of the study’s methodological, ethical and legal considerations, as well as its risk–benefit balance. The HREB waived the requirement for individual consent for the retrospective component, while caregivers of participants involved in the prospective component will provide written informed consent prior to inclusion.

The results will be disseminated to DECIDE-T care pathway end users, partners and collaborators including patients and families, HCPs, researchers and decision makers at provincial, national and international levels. We will use public engagement forums, webinars, peer-reviewed publications and presentations at national and international scientific conferences.

Patient and public involvement statement

Four caregivers with lived experience of caring for a child with a tracheostomy were involved from the earliest stages of the DECIDE-T care pathway study through consultations and co-design activities. Their priorities and experiences informed the overall study design, ensuring a family centred and feasible approach. Caregivers contributed to development of the DECIDE-T care pathway, refining recruitment strategies and study procedures, and assessed the anticipated burden of participation, including time and emotional demands. They will remain actively involved in implementing, evaluating and interpreting study findings, as well as in dissemination. The four caregivers are coauthors of this protocol.

Discussion

The DECIDE-T initiative is a comprehensive, evidence-based approach to addressing critical care gaps for children requiring tracheostomy/LTV by creating digital tools that support clinical decision-making and care coordination across multiple phases of care. Although it requires significant and sustained efforts and resources, the implementation of standardised tracheostomy care pathways in paediatric settings has previously demonstrated significant health and economic benefits.8 9 11 13 14

DECIDE-T extends beyond single-site, single-solution quality-improvement efforts to deliver a provincially coordinated, multicentre digital care pathway integrated into the provincial electronic health record system. It also extends beyond acute care delivery by incorporating family centred and digitally accessible supports for caregivers. These features have been shown to reduce caregiver burden and enhance confidence in home tracheostomy care and the overall parental trust in health systems.17 28 The integration of standardised simulation training, web-based educational modules and telemedicine follow-up aligns with recent innovations in paediatric home ventilation that emphasise personalised, remote and scalable models of care.18 34 36

One of the strengths of the DECIDE-T care pathway implementation is the use of a multisystem strategy informed by QIF, ISF, CFIR and ERIC. This ensures a structured approach to making changes, continuous interested partners engagement, adaptive delivery and sustained evaluation. Additionally, the use of the RE-AIM framework facilitates a robust, multidimensional evaluation of the care pathway’s adoption, reach, implementation, effectiveness and maintenance.45

We employ patient-oriented and family oriented research to prioritise the needs of families and co-design resources with them, ensuring that resources are accessible and family centred. This approach has become standard practice and is central to advancing child health.22 Family caregivers are involved as research partners with multidisciplinary research team members along a continuum (from being consulted to being engaged) in addressing patient priorities and planning/conducting research.22 52 This framework empowers families as equal partners alongside clinicians, researchers and hospital administrators in both research and care decisions22 53 and improves the child’s health outcomes.54 55

The DECIDE-T care pathway is expected to generate significant impacts across multiple levels of the paediatric healthcare system. For children with a tracheostomy, the implementation of a standardised, province-wide care pathway is expected to improve clinical outcomes by reducing variation in tracheostomy practices, discharge delays and inconsistent caregiver support. For family caregivers, the care pathway will offer tailored digital tools and structured education that can improve preparedness, reduce stress and enhance confidence in managing tracheostomy care at home.17 28 HCPs will benefit from integrated clinical tools within the EMR that support evidence-informed practice and reduce unwarranted variation in care, and therefore has the potential to reduce moral distress associated with suboptimal care experiences. These tools are also expected to enhance interdisciplinary coordination and streamline documentation, increasing HCP efficiency and satisfaction.11 At the system level, the DECIDE-T care pathway will standardise care delivery across institutions, thereby promoting equitable outcomes regardless of location or socioeconomic status. The implementation of the DECIDE-T care pathway has the potential to reduce hospitalisation costs by decreasing LOS and hospital re-admission. Additionally, the pathway’s design supports scalability and adaptability, providing a foundation for broader application to other medically complex paediatric populations.16 34 Finally, insights from the implementation of the DECIDE-T care pathway will contribute to the broader scientific understanding of how to implement digital, family engaged care pathways using robust implementation science frameworks. This project will demonstrate how digital innovation, informed by knowledge users, can enhance paediatric complex care and promote sustainable improvements in clinical practice and family well-being.

Potential challenges include ensuring implementation and fidelity across diverse clinical settings, achieving equitable engagement of rural, remote and Indigenous families, embedding the pathway in the EMR system, integrating the care pathway into existing workflows and sustaining long-term adoption. These challenges will be mitigated through context-specific implementation strategies, early broad partner engagement, clinical champions, standardised training, ongoing audit and feedback, and provincial governance to support sustainability.

The DECIDE-T initiative is an innovative, multicomponent, knowledge user-informed approach to implementing a multicentre digital care pathway for medically complex children with a tracheostomy. Grounded in evidence-based implementation science and digital health design, this project is positioned to improve clinical outcomes, reduce caregiver stress and strengthen their trust in the health system, enhance provider satisfaction, and improve system-level efficiency. By combining (1) A regional, multisite governance and implementation structure, (2) Co-development with families and clinical teams, and (3) Multiple interoperable digital solutions across all stages of care, the DECIDE-T care pathway offers a scalable model that addresses gaps left by single-centre protocols and discrete training initiatives, enabling consistent best practice, smoother transitions and data capture for ongoing monitoring and evaluation.
