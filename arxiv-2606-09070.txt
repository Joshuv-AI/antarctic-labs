Beyond Fixed Local Updates: An Adaptive Decentralized Quasi-Newton Method Free of Stepsize Degradation Thanks: This work was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant Number 11971231).
Hao Wu, Liping Wang

Abstract
This paper proposes a novel Adaptive Decentralized Quasi-Newton (AdaDQN) method for solving smooth nonconvex optimization problems over undirected networks. While existing decentralized algorithms with multiple local updates suffer from a pessimistic theoretical bottleneck, where the convergence stepsize must be inversely proportional to the number of local updates, our work overcomes this limitation. We revisit local update methods through a Majorization-Minimization (MM) lens and establish a Robust Inexact Algorithm (RIA) framework. Inspired by this framework, AdaDQN integrates a safeguarded consensus-aware termination criterion to dynamically balance local computational gains against consensus error, complemented by a scalable memoryless BFGS update and an event-triggered communication protocol to significantly reduce communication overhead. We establish the global convergence of AdaDQN to a first-order stationary point. Crucially, we prove that the convergence stepsize bound is decoupled from the reciprocal of maximum number of local updates. Numerical experiments demonstrate that AdaDQN achieves a superior computation-communication tradeoff, outperforming state-of-the-art decentralized methods across various performance metrics.

Introduction

Decentralized optimization involves a network of interconnected nodes aiming to collaboratively solve a global optimization problem. The network is modeled as a graph
\mathcal{G}=\left(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}\right)

\mathcal{V}=\{1,\ldots,n\}

\mathop{\min}\limits_{{\bf{z}}\in{\mathbb{R}^{p}}}\;F({\bf{z}}):=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{{f_{i}}({\bf{z}})},

Our main contributions can be summarized as follows.

1.

We revisit decentralized algorithms with multiple local updates through the lens of Majorization-Minimization (MM) and establish a Robust Inexact Algorithm (RIA) framework, demonstrating that existing methods with fixed local updates are special cases of this general framework. Our analysis demystifies why these methods work by demonstrating that their convergence is driven fundamentally by two regularity conditions, specifically sufficient descent and bounded deviation. It also substantiates that under worst-case analysis, to control the accumulated deviation induced by multi-step local updates, the stepsize is inevitably required to be inversely proportional to the number of local steps.

2.

We propose an Adaptive Decentralized Quasi-Newton (AdaDQN) method, which achieves double-level adaptivity, dynamically adjusting both the number of local updates and communication rounds based on real-time feedback. This design effectively handles the significant heterogeneity in computation and communication capabilities across nodes. Specifically, we introduce a safeguarded consensus-aware termination criterion for the local update loop, which applies the bounded deviation condition as a safeguard and automatically halts local update when the consensus error dominates the local optimization gain. On the other hand, we employ an event-triggered communication protocol that broadcasts variables only when the local state evolves significantly, thereby reducing redundant transmissions.

3.

We establish the global convergence of the AdaDQN algorithm for nonconvex smooth optimization problems with appropriate stepsize choices, given standard assumptions. More importantly, we prove that the convergence stepsize is completely decoupled from the reciprocal of the maximum number of local updates, which fundamentally distinguishes our method from existing approaches. Numerical results on various datasets confirm the efficacy of AdaDQN in saving both communication and computation, and demonstrate that our method achieves superior performance in terms of iteration count, communication cost, and gradient evaluations compared to state-of-the-art relative decentralized algorithms.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 formally introduces the problem setting, outlining the necessary assumptions on the objective functions and the network topology. Section 3 presents a novel MM perspective for decentralized methods with multiple local updates, establishes the RIA framework, and provides its convergence analysis. In Section 4, we detail the development of the proposed AdaDQN method and establish its global convergence. Numerical experiments of comparing our AdaDQN method with other well-established methods for solving decentralized nonconvex optimization are presented in Section 5.
We finally draw some conclusions in Section 6.

3.1
Majorization-minimization interpretation

A vast class of gradient-tracking-based methods with single local update can be written in a general form as

\displaystyle{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t+1}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\tilde{W}_{ij}\left({\bf{x}}_{j}^{t}-\alpha{\bf{H}}_{j}^{t}{\bf{v}}_{j}^{t}\right),

(4)

\displaystyle{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\tilde{W}_{ij}\left({\bf{v}}_{j}^{t}+{\bf{g}}_{j}^{t+1}-{\bf{g}}_{j}^{t}\right),

(5)

{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}

if

We provide an interpretation of the above updates (

To address this, we employ a MM strategy and implicitly construct a surrogate for the global objective

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{i}({\bf{z}};{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t})=f_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t})+\langle\nabla f_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}),{\bf{z}}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\alpha}({\bf{z}}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}({\bf{H}}_{i}^{t})^{-1}({\bf{z}}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}),

\displaystyle\tilde{F}({\bf{z}},{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}):=\tilde{f}_{i}({\bf{z}};{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t})+\langle{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}-\nabla f_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}),{\bf{z}}\rangle.

Should we take an exact minimization for the local surrogate function

We give a negative answer.

Remark 3.2

(Why Exact Minimization Fails)
.

Solving the subproblem (
6
) exactly is not merely computationally expensive but theoretically detrimental in the decentralized nonconvex setting. The failure of exact minimization stems from the following conflicts: Suppose
{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t+1/2}
is the exact solution of
\phi_{i}^{t}
.
Exact minimization of nonconvex
\phi_{i}^{t}
drives nodes toward disparate local minima. The heterogeneity across all
\phi_{i}^{t}
s usually leads to a massive discrepancy between local minima
{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t+1/2}
. The dynamic consensus mechanism for
{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}
relies on the smooth evolution of iterates to cancel out gradient tracking errors
[
10
]
. Note

\|\nabla f({\bf{x}}^{t+1})-\nabla f({\bf{x}}^{t})\|\leq L\|{\bf{W}}^{K_{c}}{\bf{x}}^{t+1/2}-{\bf{x}}^{t+1/2}\|+\|\nabla f({\bf{x}}^{t+1/2})-\nabla f({\bf{x}}^{t})\|.

The gradient difference is unbounded and thus causes the gradient tracker
{\bf{v}}^{t}
becomes unstable, failing to point in the correct descent direction. As a result, the algorithm iterates may diverge. On the other hand, performing a limited number of iteration steps acts as an implicit regularization, ensuring that nodes do not drift so far apart that consensus is hindered and stay within the region where the gradient estimate is valid.

3.2
Robust inexact algorithm framework

Motivated by the limitations of fixed local updates and the risks of exact minimization, we propose the Robust Inexact Algorithm (RIA) framework. This framework formalizes the local update as an adaptive procedure that terminates based on solution quality rather than a fixed iteration count
K_{g}
.
The RIA framework generalizes the update formulas as follows:

\displaystyle{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t+1/2}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\text{InexactSolver}\left(\min_{{\bf{z}}}\phi_{i}^{t}({\bf{z}})\right),

(7)

\displaystyle{\bf{x}}^{t+1}

\displaystyle={\bf{W}}^{K_{c}}{\bf{x}}^{t+1/2},

(8)

\displaystyle{\bf{v}}^{t+1}

\displaystyle={\bf{W}}^{K_{c}}\left({\bf{v}}^{t}+{\bf{g}}^{t+1}-{\bf{g}}^{t}\right).

(9)

Critically,
{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t+1/2}
is not required to be a stationary point. Instead, to guarantee convergence, the inexact solution should satisfy two specific regularity conditions stated in the following assumption.

Assumption 3
.

The approximate solution
{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t+1/2}
should satisfy:

1.

Sufficient Descent:

\displaystyle\phi_{i}^{t}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t+1/2})\leq\phi_{i}^{t}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t})-\gamma_{1}\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}

(10)

for some
\gamma_{1}>0
.

2.

Bounded Deviation:

\displaystyle\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t+1/2}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\leq\gamma_{2}\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}

(11)

for some
\gamma_{2}>0
.

The sufficient descent condition ensures that each inexact minimization makes sufficient progress in reducing the local surrogate function. On the other hand, the bounded deviation condition limits movement from the current iterate, preventing excessive deviations that could disrupt consensus.

3.3
Convergence analysis for RIA

We now establish the global convergence for the proposed RIA applied to problem (

{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t,k}
becomes

\displaystyle{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t,k}={\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}+\nabla f_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k})-\nabla f_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}).

(30)

Using the above relation and the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla f_{i}
, we have for
k\geq 1
that

\displaystyle\quad\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\|=\left\|\alpha\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t,j}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t,j}\right\|\leq\alpha\Psi\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t,j}\|

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\Psi\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|+\|\nabla f_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,j})-\nabla f_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t})\|\right)\leq k\alpha\Psi\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|+\alpha L\Psi\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,j}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\|.

We proceed by induction. For
k=1
,
\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,1}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\|=\alpha\|{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t,0}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|\leq\alpha\Psi\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|
, which satisfies the bound. Assume the hypothesis holds for all
j<k
. Then,

\displaystyle\quad\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\|\leq k\alpha\Psi\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|+\alpha L\Psi\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(2\alpha\Psi j\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|)

\displaystyle=\alpha\Psi k\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|+2\alpha^{2}L\Psi^{2}\frac{k(k-1)}{2}\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|=\alpha\Psi k\left[1+\alpha L\Psi(k-1)\right]\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|.

Provided that
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{L\Psi K_{g}}
, we have
1+\alpha L\Psi(k-1)\leq 2
. Thus,

\|\mathbf{x}_{i}^{t,k}-\mathbf{x}_{i}^{t}\|\leq 2\alpha\Psi k\|\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t}\|.

(31)

Taking
k=K_{g}
and squaring both sides of (
31
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{x}_{i}^{t,K_{g}}-\mathbf{x}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\leq 4\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}K_{g}^{2}\|\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}.

Thus, the bounded deviation condition holds with

\gamma_{2}=4\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}K_{g}^{2}.

(32)

Then, we verify the sufficient descent condition that
\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{t,K_{g}})\leq\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{t})-\gamma_{1}\|\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}
for some constant
\gamma_{1}>0
. Since
\nabla f_{i}
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous, the surrogate function
\phi_{i}^{t}
is
L
-smooth. Applying the descent lemma for each inner step
k=0,\dots,K_{g}-1
with the update rule
\mathbf{x}_{i}^{t,k+1}=\mathbf{x}_{i}^{t,k}-\alpha\mathbf{H}_{i}^{t,k}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t,k}
yields

\displaystyle\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{t,k+1})

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{t,k})-\alpha\langle\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t,k},\mathbf{H}_{i}^{t,k}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t,k}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\alpha^{2}\|\mathbf{H}_{i}^{t,k}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t,k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{t,k})-\left(\alpha\psi-\frac{L}{2}\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}\right)\|\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t,k}\|^{2},

where we utilize the uniform eigenvalue bounds
\psi\mathbf{I}\preceq\mathbf{H}_{i}^{t,k}\preceq\Psi\mathbf{I}
. Summing the above inequality over
k=0
to
K_{g}-1
, we obtain

\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{t,K_{g}})\leq\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{t})-c_{\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^{K_{g}-1}\|\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t,k}\|^{2},

(33)

where
c_{\alpha}=\alpha\psi-\frac{L}{2}\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}>0
for
\alpha<\frac{2\psi}{L\Psi^{2}}
.
Using (
30
), Lemma
13
, and the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of each
\nabla f_{i}
, we have

\|\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t,k}\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}-L^{2}\|\mathbf{x}_{i}^{t,k}-\mathbf{x}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}.

Summing over
k=0
to
K_{g}-1
and substituting (
31
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{K_{g}-1}\|\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t,k}\|^{2}\geq\frac{K_{g}}{2}\|\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}-L^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{K_{g}-1}4\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}k^{2}\|\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\geq\left(\frac{K_{g}}{2}-\frac{4}{3}\alpha^{2}L^{2}\Psi^{2}K_{g}^{3}\right)\|\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t}\|^{2},

where the last inequality uses the bound
\sum_{k=0}^{K_{g}-1}k^{2}\leq K_{g}^{3}/3
. By restricting the stepsize such that
\frac{4}{3}\alpha^{2}L^{2}\Psi^{2}K_{g}^{3}\leq\frac{K_{g}}{4}
, which mandates
\alpha\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4L\Psi K_{g}}
, then we obtain
\sum_{k=0}^{K_{g}-1}\|\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t,k}\|^{2}\geq\frac{K_{g}}{4}\|\mathbf{v}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}
. Substituting this back into (
33
) establishes the sufficient descent condition with

\gamma_{1}=\frac{K_{g}}{4}\left(\alpha\psi-\frac{L}{2}\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}\right).

(34)

As presented in Theorem
5
, the RIA framework should satisfy the condition (
24
) for convergence. Substituting (
34
) and (
32
) back yields

\displaystyle\frac{K_{g}}{4}\left(\alpha\psi-\frac{L}{2}\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}\right)-4\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}K_{g}^{2}\bigg(\frac{L}{2}+\frac{2L^{2}}{n(1-\sigma^{2K_{c}})}

\displaystyle+\frac{64\sigma^{2K_{c}}L^{2}}{n(1-\sigma^{2K_{c}})^{3}}+\frac{3n(1-\sigma^{2K_{c}})}{16}\bigg)>0,

(35)

which implies that the convergence stepsize bound evaluates to

\alpha<\frac{\psi}{\frac{L\Psi^{2}}{2}+16\Psi^{2}K_{g}\big(\frac{L}{2}+\frac{2L^{2}}{n(1-\sigma^{2K_{c}})}+\frac{64\sigma^{2K_{c}}L^{2}}{n(1-\sigma^{2K_{c}})^{3}}+\frac{3n(1-\sigma^{2K_{c}})}{16}\big)}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{K_{g}}\right).

(36)

This derivation verifies the theoretical validity of existing methods
[
43
,
26
,
25
,
42
]
under the RIA framework. On the other hand, it exposes their theoretical bottleneck: despite utilizing advanced techniques to establish a tight bound on accumulated descent over the entire local trajectory, the stepsize must shrink proportional to
\mathcal{O}(1/K_{g})
to counteract the node deviation.

This phenomenon is closely related to the limitations observed in federated learning under heterogeneous (non-IID) settings. Specifically, as demonstrated in the discussion of the SCAFFOLD algorithm
[
51
]
, traditional local update methods (e.g., FedAvg
[
52
]
) are forced to use much smaller stepsizes to counteract the client-drift caused by data heterogeneity. More importantly, they established through a strongly convex counterexample that the resulting error lower bound is an inherent structural flaw of fixed-step local updates, which cannot be circumvented by mere analytical techniques. Inspired by their counterexample, we present the following proposition to formally prove that the
\mathcal{O}(1/K_{g})
stepsize bound is strictly tight for local update methods in decentralized optimization.

Proposition 6

(Tightness of the
\mathcal{O}(1/K_{g})
Bound)
.

For any decentralized gradient tracking algorithm with
K_{g}
local updates (e.g., the RIA framework with
{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t,k}={\bf{I}}
), there exists a
L
-smooth decentralized optimization problem such that the algorithm diverges geometrically unless the stepsize satisfies
\alpha\leq\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{K_{g}})
.

Proof.

We construct a worst-case instance over a fully connected two-node network (
n=2,W_{ij}=1/2
). Consider minimizing a one-dimensional objective
F(x)=\frac{1}{2}(f_{1}(x)+f_{2}(x))
, where the local functions are

f_{1}(x)=\frac{L}{2}(x-c)^{2},\quad f_{2}(x)=0,

for a constant
c>0
. The maximum smoothness constant is
L
, and the unique global optimal solution is
x^{*}=c
.

Suppose at iteration
t
, both nodes reach a consensus state
x_{1}^{t}=x_{2}^{t}=x^{t}
. The gradient trackers realize that
v_{1}^{t}=v_{2}^{t}=v^{t}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla f_{1}(x^{t})+\nabla f_{2}(x^{t}))=\frac{L}{2}(x^{t}-c)
. During the local update phase, the nodes minimize their respective MM surrogate functions
\phi_{i}^{t}(z)=f_{i}(z)+\langle v^{t}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t}),z\rangle
,
i=1,2
.

We look at local trajectory of the node one. The surrogate gradient is
\nabla\phi_{1}^{t}(z)=L(z-c)-L(x^{t}-c)+v^{t}=Lz-\frac{L}{2}(x^{t}+c)
. The local update rule is
x_{1}^{t,k+1}=x_{1}^{t,k}-\alpha\nabla\phi_{1}^{t}(x_{1}^{t,k})
. The state after
K_{g}
steps with the initial condition
x_{1}^{t,0}=x^{t}
is presented as

x_{1}^{t,K_{g}}=\frac{x^{t}+c}{2}+(1-\alpha L)^{K_{g}}\left(\frac{x^{t}-c}{2}\right).

(37)

Furthermore, we consider the local trajectory of the node two, whose surrogate gradient is constant, i.e.,
\nabla\phi_{2}^{t}(z)=v^{t}=\frac{L}{2}(x^{t}-c)
. Thus, after
K_{g}
steps, its state becomes

x_{2}^{t,K_{g}}=x^{t}-K_{g}\alpha\frac{L}{2}(x^{t}-c).

(38)

The next global state is generated by averaging the local states,
x^{t+1}=\frac{1}{2}(x_{1}^{t,K_{g}}+x_{2}^{t,K_{g}})
. Let
e^{t}=x^{t}-c
denote the optimal error. Then, we obtain the error dynamics

\displaystyle e^{t+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{e^{t}}{2}+(1-\alpha L)^{K_{g}}\frac{e^{t}}{2}+e^{t}-K_{g}\alpha\frac{L}{2}e^{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{1}{4}\left[3+(1-\alpha L)^{K_{g}}-K_{g}\alpha L\right]}_{\mathbf{M}(\alpha,K_{g})}e^{t}.

(39)

The error sequence
e^{t}
converges asymptotically to 0 if and only if the spectral radius of the transition matrix
\mathbf{M}(\alpha,K_{g})
is strictly less than 1, i.e.,
\rho[\mathbf{M}(\alpha,K_{g})]<1
.
To prevent geometric divergence, it requires

-1<\frac{1}{4}\left[3+(1-\alpha L)^{K_{g}}-K_{g}\alpha L\right]\implies K_{g}\alpha L-(1-\alpha L)^{K_{g}}<7.

(40)

For local gradient descent to be stable,
\alpha L\leq 2
, implying
(1-\alpha L)^{K_{g}}\geq-1
. Applying this to (
40
) yields

K_{g}\alpha L<7+(1-\alpha L)^{K_{g}}\leq 8\implies\alpha<\frac{8}{LK_{g}}.

(41)

This confirms that for local update methods, the convergence stepsize must shrink proportional to
\mathcal{O}(1/K_{g})
.
∎

4
A new method with adaptive computation and communication

The RIA framework in Section
3.2
establishes the theoretical validity for a broad class of decentralized optimization algorithms in the worst case relying on the
\mathcal{O}(1/K_{g})
stepsize. In practice, multiplying
K_{g}
by a certain factor does not actually require a corresponding proportional reduction in the stepsize to ensure convergence.
Moreover, it is important to recognize that employing fixed numbers for both local computation and communication steps may lead to inefficient utilization of computation and communication resources. Choosing proper
K_{c}
(communication steps) and
K_{g}
(computation steps) presents significant challenges because of the inherent heterogeneity in computation and communication capabilities across decentralized nodes. To address these limitations, we try to develop an aggressive method beyond the RIA framework with enhanced convergence results.

Memoryless BFGS update.

In decentralized optimization, storing and updating a full
p\times p
inverse Hessian approximation at each node incurs prohibitive
\mathcal{O}(p^{2})
memory and computational costs, especially for high-dimensional problems. To alleviate this burden, we propose a scalable memoryless BFGS update strategy (outlined in Algorithm
3
) that constructs the approximation using only the most recent curvature information.

We first define the base memoryless update function
H_{i}^{t}:\Omega_{i}^{t}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
as

\displaystyle H_{i}^{t}({\bf{y}})=\tau_{i}^{t}\left({\bf{I}}_{p}-\frac{{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{y}})^{\sf T}+{\bf{y}}({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}}\right)+\left(1+\frac{\tau_{i}^{t}\|{\bf{y}}\|^{2}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}}\right)\frac{{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}},

(42)

where
\Omega_{i}^{t}=\left\{{\bf{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}>0\right\}
.
To theoretically guarantee global convergence, the approximate matrix must have strictly bounded eigenvalues. Thus, we introduce a safeguarded curvature vector
{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}
:

{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t},&\text{ if }[\lambda_{\min}(H_{i}^{t}(\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})),\lambda_{\max}(H_{i}^{t}(\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}))]\subset[l,u]\text{ and }({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}>0;\\
\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t},&\text{ otherwise, }\end{array}\right.

(43)

where the damped difference is given by
\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}={\bf{g}}_{i}^{t+1}-{\bf{g}}_{i}^{t}+h_{i}^{t}{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}
, with the damping parameter
h_{i}^{t}=\varrho+\max\left\{-\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}({\bf{g}}_{i}^{t+1}-{\bf{g}}_{i}^{t})}{\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}},0\right\}
for a sufficiently small constant
\varrho>0
.

Using this safeguarded vector, our proposed memoryless BFGS matrix is constructed as

\displaystyle{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}

\displaystyle=H_{i}^{t}({\bf{y}}_{i}^{t})=\tau_{i}^{t}\left({\bf{I}}_{p}-\frac{{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{y}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}+{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}\right)+\left(1+\frac{\tau_{i}^{t}\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}\right)\frac{{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}{\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{\bf{I}}_{p}-\frac{{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{y}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}+{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}}{\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}+2\frac{{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}},

(44)

where the scaling parameter is chosen as
\tau_{i}^{t}=\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}{\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}
.
It is straightforward to verify the spectrum of
{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}
. Provided that
\tau_{i}^{t}>0
,
{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}
possesses
p-2
eigenvalues equal to
\tau_{i}^{t}
, while the remaining two eigenvalues, denoted as
0<\lambda_{i}^{t+1}\leq\Lambda_{i}^{t+1}
, are analytically evaluated as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\lambda_{i}^{t+1}=\lambda_{\min}(H_{i}^{t}({\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}))=\frac{\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}}\left(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{(({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})^{2}}{\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\|{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}}\right),\\
\\
\Lambda_{i}^{t+1}=\lambda_{\max}(H_{i}^{t}({\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}))=\frac{\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}}\left(1+\sqrt{1-\frac{(({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})^{2}}{\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\|{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}}\right).\end{array}\right.

The formulation in (
44
) ensures that
{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}
is strictly positive definite and uniformly bounded. Specifically, when
{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}=\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}
, the condition in (
43
) inherently guarantees
l{\bf{I}}_{p}\preceq{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}\preceq u{\bf{I}}_{p}
. Alternatively, when
{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}=\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}
, (
4.1
) yields

\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}}{2\|\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{\bf{I}}_{p}\preceq{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}\preceq\frac{2\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}}{\bf{I}}_{p}.

By invoking the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla f_{i}
, we can bound
\|\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\|=\|{\bf{g}}_{i}^{t+1}-{\bf{g}}_{i}^{t}+h_{i}^{t}{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|\leq(2L+\varrho)\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|
. This implies
({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\geq\varrho\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\geq\hat{\varrho}\|\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}
with
\hat{\varrho}=\frac{\varrho}{(2L+\varrho)^{2}}
. Consequently, the eigenvalues remain strictly bounded in both cases:

\psi=\min\left\{l,\frac{\hat{\varrho}}{2}\right\}{\bf{I}}\preceq{\bf{H}}^{t+1}\preceq\max\left\{u,\frac{2}{\varrho}\right\}{\bf{I}}=\Psi.

(48)

Finally, a critical advantage of this memoryless design is that the dense
p\times p
matrix
{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}
never needs to be explicitly formed or stored. The quasi-Newton search direction
{\bf{d}}_{i}^{t+1}=-{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}
can be computed efficiently via a sequence of vector inner products, significantly reducing the computational complexity to
\mathcal{O}(p)
:

{\bf{d}}_{i}^{t+1}=-\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}{\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}+\frac{(({\bf{y}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}){\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}+(({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}){\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}{\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}-2\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}.

(49)

Safeguarded consensus-aware adaptive computation.

To avoid the over-fitting (i.e., client drift) caused by fixed multiple local updates and to dynamically balance local gains against the consensus error, we introduce a hybrid dynamic termination mechanism for the local computation loop (Steps 5-12 in Algorithm
2
).

At the
k
-th inner iteration of the outer iteration
t
, node
i
evaluates a dual-criterion condition. It will terminate the inner loop early if either of the following criteria is met.

\displaystyle\alpha\|{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t,k}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t,k}\|

\displaystyle<\max\left\{\varepsilon\left\|{\bf{x}}^{t}_{i}-\sum\nolimits_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\tilde{W}_{ij}{\bf{x}}^{t}_{j}\right\|,\tilde{\varepsilon}\right\},

(50)

\displaystyle\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\|

\displaystyle\geq\gamma\alpha\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|.

(51)

These two criteria serve complementary purposes in practice and theory. On the one hand, the condition (
50
) monitors the magnitude of the single quasi-Newton step relative to the distance between the node with its neighborhood average. If met, it indicates that the local stationary gap has become negligible compared to the consensus error. Continuing local update would only exacerbate the consensus error without yielding meaningful objective descent. Thus, terminating the loop effectively saves computational resources.
On the other hand, while (
50
) restricts the step-wise increment, (
51
) places a restriction on the cumulative drift
\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\|
. It guarantees that the total deviation of the local iterates from the anchor point
{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}
never exceeds a margin proportional to the initial tracker’s norm
\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|
. As we will demonstrate in the subsequent analysis, this bound on cumulative drift
\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\|
is the linchpin that decouples the convergence stepsize
\alpha
from the reciprocal of the maximum local steps
1/K_{g}
.

Event-triggered adaptive communication.

To reduce communication overhead, we employ an event-triggered mechanism, as presented in Algorithm
4
. Instead of transmitting variables at every inner communication step
s
, node
i
broadcasts its state only when it has significantly evolved.
Let
\hat{{\bf{z}}}_{i}^{t,s}
denote the latest variable transmitted by node
i
to its neighbors up to step
s
. The update rule for the consensus variable
{\bf{z}}
becomes

{\bf{z}}_{i}^{t,s+1}={\bf{z}}_{i}^{t,s}-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\tilde{W}_{ij}\left(\hat{{\bf{z}}}_{i}^{t,s}-\hat{{\bf{z}}}_{j}^{t,s}\right).

(52)

Node
i
broadcasts its new state
{\bf{z}}_{i}^{t,s+1}
(updating
\hat{{\bf{z}}}_{i}^{t,s+1}={\bf{z}}_{i}^{t,s+1}
) only if the innovation exceeds a time-varying threshold:

\|{\bf{z}}_{i}^{t,s+1}-\hat{{\bf{z}}}_{i}^{t,s}\|\geq\eta^{t}\cdot\tau^{s+1},

(53)

where
\{\eta^{t}\}
and
\{\tau^{s}\}
are non-increasing sequences governing the decay of the threshold. Otherwise, node
i
remains silent (maintaining
\hat{{\bf{z}}}_{i}^{t,s+1}=\hat{{\bf{z}}}_{i}^{t,s}
), significantly saving communication when the system is near consensus. The same logic is applied to the gradient tracking variable
{\bf{q}}
.

Remark 4.1

(Relationship between existing event-triggered mechanisms)
.

It is important to emphasize that we do not propose a novel event-triggered mechanism in this paper; instead, we adopt a well-established approach. Similar event-triggered mechanisms have been utilized in
[
35
,
36
,
29
,
37
,
38
]
. Differently,
[
31
]
employs a threshold based on the difference between two consecutive updates, whereas
[
30
]
utilizes a threshold related to the discrepancy on iterates between a node and its neighbors.

4.2
Convergence analysis for AdaDQN

Now turn our attention to the global convergence of the AdaDQN algorithm.
To demonstrate that AdaDQN generates a sequence satisfying (
13
), we construct the following potential function:

P({\bf{x}}^{t},{\bf{v}}^{t})=F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})+\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1-(1+\eta){\sigma}^{2K_{c}}}{144(1+1/\tilde{\eta})L^{2}}\|{\bf{v}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2},

(54)

where
\tilde{\eta}
and
\eta
are some positive constants introduced by Young’s inequality. The following lemma provides several key inequalities.

Lemma 7
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
2
hold. Let
\{{\bf{x}}^{t}\}
be the sequence generated by the AdaDQN algorithm. If the stepsize satisfies
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{\gamma L}
, then for all

and

\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\|\leq\Gamma\alpha\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|,

(55)

\displaystyle\|{\bf{x}}^{t,k}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t,k}\|^{2}\leq 2\Gamma^{2}\alpha^{2}\|{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}+2\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2},

(56)

where
\Gamma=\gamma+2\Psi
is a constant.

Proof.

Proof of (
55
):
Let
k_{i}^{*}\leq K_{g}
denote the step index where node
i
triggers either of the termination conditions (
50
) and (
51
) in Algorithm
2
. For any
k\leq k_{i}^{*}

did not trigger the safeguard condition (

), which guarantees

\displaystyle\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k-1}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\|<\gamma\alpha\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|.

(57)

By the recursion
{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t,k-1}={\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}+{\bf{g}}_{i}^{t,k-1}-{\bf{g}}_{i}^{t}
and the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla f_{i}
, the tracking variable is bounded by

\displaystyle\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t,k-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|+L\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k-1}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\|

\displaystyle<(1+\gamma L\alpha)\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|\leq 2\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|,

(58)

where the last inequality applies the stepsize condition
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{\gamma L}
.
According to the update rule
{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k}={\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k-1}-\alpha{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t,k-1}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t,k-1}
and the bounded approximation
{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t,k-1}\preceq\Psi{\bf{I}}
, it holds at step

that

\displaystyle\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k-1}\|\leq\alpha\Psi\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t,k-1}\|<2\alpha\Psi\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|.

(59)

Applying the triangle inequality combining (
57
) and (
59
) yields

\displaystyle\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\|\leq\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k-1}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\|+\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k-1}\|<(\gamma+2\Psi)\alpha\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|=\Gamma\alpha\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|.

For
k>k_{i}^{*}
, the iterates remain stationary (
{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k}={\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,k_{i}^{*}}
). Thus, the bound (
55
) holds for all
0\leq k\leq K_{g}
.

Proof of (
56
):
Using the property
\|{\bf{I}}-{\bf{M}}\|\leq 1
and (
55
), we derive

\displaystyle\|{\bf{x}}^{t,k}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t,k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|({\bf{I}}-{\bf{M}})({\bf{x}}^{t,k}-{\bf{x}}^{t})+({\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|{\bf{x}}^{t,k}-{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}+2\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Gamma^{2}\alpha^{2}\|{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}+2\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

The following Lemma establishes the one-step descent inequality of the global objective function

Lemma 8
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
2
hold. Let
\{{\bf{x}}^{t}\}
be the sequence generated by the AdaDQN algorithm. If the stepsize satisfies

\displaystyle F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t+1})\leq

\displaystyle F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})-\left(\frac{K_{g}\alpha\psi}{6n}-\frac{K_{g}L\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}}{12n}-\frac{L\Gamma^{2}\alpha^{2}(2+d+b)}{2n}\right)\|{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}

(60)

where

are arbitrary positive constants introduced by Young’s inequality, and

Note that the event-triggered mechanism preserves the average over the network:

Applying to the

-Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla F

\displaystyle F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t+1})\leq

\displaystyle F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})+\underbrace{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})-\overline{\nabla}f({{\bf{x}}}^{t}),{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t,K_{g}}-{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t}\Big\rangle}_{\text{Term I}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\overline{\nabla}f({{\bf{x}}}^{t})-{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t},{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t,K_{g}}-{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t}\Big\rangle}_{\text{Term II}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t},{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t,K_{g}}-{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t}\Big\rangle}_{\text{Term III}}

For Term I and Term II, utilizing (

Term I

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L}{2bn}\|{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{Lb\Gamma^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2n}\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2},

(63)

Term II

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2Ldn}\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{Ld\Gamma^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2n}\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2},

(64)

where

are two positive constants introduced by Young’s inequality.

For Term III, we use the definition of the surrogate function from the Majorization-Minimization framework, i.e.,

\displaystyle\phi_{i}^{t}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,K_{g}})\leq

\displaystyle\phi_{i}^{t}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t})-\left(\alpha\psi-\frac{L}{2}\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}\right)\left(\frac{K_{g}}{2}-\frac{K_{g}}{3}\alpha^{2}L^{2}\Gamma^{2}\right)\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\phi_{i}^{t}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t})-\frac{K_{g}}{6}\left(\alpha\psi-\frac{L}{2}\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}\right)\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}.

(65)

Substituting the definition of
\phi_{i}^{t}
into (
4.2
) and rearranging the terms yields

\displaystyle\langle{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t},{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,K_{g}}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\rangle\leq

\displaystyle f_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t})-f_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,K_{g}})+\langle\nabla f_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}),{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,K_{g}}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{K_{g}}{6}\left(\alpha\psi-\frac{L}{2}\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}\right)\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}.

(66)

By the
L
-smoothness of each

, we have

f_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t})-f_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,K_{g}})+\langle\nabla f_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}),{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,K_{g}}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\rangle\leq\frac{L}{2}\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,K_{g}}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}.

Therefore, (

\langle{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t},{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,K_{g}}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\rangle\leq\frac{L}{2}\|{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t,K_{g}}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}-\frac{K_{g}}{6}\left(\alpha\psi-\frac{L}{2}\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}\right)\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}.

Averaging this inequality over all
n

55

Term III

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t},{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t,K_{g}}-{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{L\Gamma^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2}\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}-\frac{K_{g}}{6}\left(\alpha\psi-\frac{L}{2}\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}\right)\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{K_{g}\alpha\psi}{6n}-\frac{K_{g}L\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}}{12n}-\frac{L\Gamma^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2n}\right)\|{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}.

(67)

The quadratic term in (
4.2

55

(68)

Substituting (
4.2

63
), (
64
), and (

∎

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we investigate the computation-communication tradeoff in decentralized nonconvex optimization. We first introduce the Robust Inexact Algorithm (RIA) framework from a Majorization-Minimization perspective, which not only unifies existing methods with multiple local updates but also mathematically exposes their theoretical bottleneck: the worst-case stepsize must shrink proportionally to
\mathcal{O}(1/K_{g})
to counteract accumulated node deviation. To circumvent this limitation, we propose the Adaptive Decentralized Quasi-Newton (AdaDQN) method. By integrating a safeguarded consensus-aware termination criterion, an event-triggered communication protocol, and a scalable memoryless BFGS update, AdaDQN achieves double-level adaptivity that eliminates unnecessary communication and computation steps.

Assuming the objective function is bounded from below and has Lipschitz gradients, we establish the global convergence of AdaDQN to a first-order stationary point and prove that the theoretically required stepsize is not inversely proportional to the maximum number of local updates. Ablation experiments and comparison experiments
on nonconvex machine learning tasks with various datasets demonstrate that AdaDQN achieves a superior computation-communication tradeoff and significantly outperforms state-of-the-art decentralized optimization methods, including ET-DOGT
[
31
]
, DETNO
[
32
]
, LSGT
[
25
]
, and GTA
[
43
]
.

Appendix A
Analytical tools

Two analytical tools are provided below.

Lemma 12
.

(Young’s inequality) For any two vectors
{\bf{v}}_{1},{\bf{v}}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, and
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle 2{\bf{v}}_{1}^{\sf T}{\bf{v}}_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\eta\|{\bf{v}}_{1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\eta}\|{\bf{v}}_{2}\|^{2},

\displaystyle\|{\bf{v}}_{1}+{\bf{v}}_{2}\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\|{\bf{v}}_{1}\|^{2}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta}\right)\|{\bf{v}}_{2}\|^{2}.

Lemma 13
.

(Jensen’s inequality) For a set of vectors
\{{\bf{v}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
,

\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf{v}}_{i}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|{\bf{v}}_{i}\|^{2}.
