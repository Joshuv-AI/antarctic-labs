Interaction and correlation functions for π ​ f 1 ​ ( 1285 ) \pi f_{1}(1285) , η ​ f 1 ​ ( 1285 ) \eta f_{1}(1285)
Wen-Hao Jia, Hai-Peng Li, Wei-Hong Liang, Jing Song, Eulogio Oset

Abstract
We have studied the interaction of π 0 ​ ( η ) ​ f 1 ​ ( 1285 ) \pi^{0}(\eta)f_{1}(1285) assuming the f 1 ​ ( 1285 ) f_{1}(1285) to be a molecular state of K ∗ ​ K ¯ − K ¯ ∗ ​ K K^{*}\bar{K}-\bar{K}^{*}K . We use a framework in which a π 0 ​ ( η ) ​ f 1 ​ ( 1285 ) \pi^{0}(\eta)f_{1}(1285) optical potential is obtained, which is later used as the kernel of the Lippmann-Schwinger equation, following the standard method for the interaction of particles with nuclei. The optical potential is obtained using the fixed center approximation to the Faddeev equations, where a cluster, here the f 1 ​ ( 1285 ) f_{1}(1285) , remains unchanged during the interaction, appropriate to the situation that one has here. We have obtained the scattering matrix for this system, the scattering length and effective range, plus the correlation functions. The framework used has been previously tested in the study of the p ​ f 1 ​ ( 1285 ) pf_{1}(1285) interaction and has been shown to give results in agreement with the recent experimental measurement of the p ​ f 1 ​ ( 1285 ) pf_{1}(1285) correlation function. On the other hand, from this interaction we do not obtain clear signals for the π 1 ​ ( 1400 ) \pi_{1}(1400) or π 1 ​ ( 1600 ) \pi_{1}(1600) , nor for the η 1 ​ ( 1855 ) \eta_{1}(1855) resonances, which in other approaches have been claimed to arise from the same dynamics. We, however, obtain a structure in the π 0 ​ f 1 ​ ( 1285 ) \pi^{0}f_{1}(1285) amplitude around 1500 − 1600 ​ MeV 1500-1600\textrm{ MeV} and a strong cusp at the η ​ f 1 ​ ( 1285 ) \eta f_{1}(1285) threshold of 1833 ​ MeV 1833\textrm{ MeV} .



I
Introduction

The BESIII Collaboration reported the observation of a state branded as
\eta_{1}(1855)
, with quantum numbers
I^{G}(J^{PC})=0^{+}(1^{-+})

Ablikim et al. 2022
, which make this state exotic from the
q\bar{q}
point of view.
The state was observed in the
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{1}(1855);\;\eta_{1}(1855)\to\eta\eta^{\prime}
decay, and is already tabulated in the Particle Data Group (PDG)
Navas et al. 2024
with only the BESIII entry.
This state would be related to the
\pi_{1}(1600)
with
1^{-}(1^{-+})
, which, according to the PDG, could also be the
\pi_{1}(1400)
that also tabulated there.
Theoretical interest in these states is great, since they challenge the conventional
q\bar{q}
structure of the mesons and require at least four quarks.
Even then, there can be different structures like compact tetraquarks or meson-meson molecules.
In Ref.
Zhang et al. 2002
, the possibility that the
\pi_{1}(1400)
and
\pi_{1}(1600)
could correspond to molecular states of
\pi\eta(1295)
and
\pi\eta(1440)
, respectively, was studied, with negative conclusions.
In Ref.
Bernard et al. 2003
, a tetraquark nature is advocated with different SU(3) classifications.
A hybrid nature for the
\pi_{1}(1600)
is advocated in Refs.
Close and Dudek 2004
;
Eshraim et al. 2020
;
Li et al. 2022
, but different conclusions are obtained in the work of Ref.
General et al. 2007
.
Lattice QCD calculations have been able to reproduce a
(1^{-+})
state compatible with the
\pi_{1}(1600)

Hedditch et al. 2005
.
QCD sum rules have also been applied to obtain a tetraquark compatible with the
\pi_{1}(1600)
in Refs.
Chen et al. 2008
;
Narison 2009
;
Zhang et al. 2014
.
A very different proposal is made in Refs.
Zhang et al. 2017
, where the
\pi_{1}(1600)
is assumed to be a three-body molecular state of
\pi K^{*}\bar{K}
.
Review papers on this issue can be found in Refs.
Meyer and Swanson 2015
;
Chen et al. 2023a
.

The discovery of the
\eta_{1}(1855)
state has also raised interest in the theoretical community.
In Refs.
Dong et al. 2022
;
Yang et al. 2023
;
Huang and Zhu 2023
;
Liu et al. 2025
, it is obtained as a
K\bar{K}_{1}(1400)
molecule.
Once again the hybrid picture gets support in Refs.
Shastry et al. 2022
;
Chen et al. 2023b
;
Tan et al. 2024
;
Qiu and Zhao 2022
, but in Ref.
Zhang et al. 2026
this structure is shown to lead to widths that are too large compared with experiment.
A tetraquark structure using QCD sum rules is advocated in Ref.
Wan et al. 2022
.

A different structure for the
\pi_{1}(1600)
and
\eta_{1}(1855)
states is advocated in Ref.
Yan et al. 2023
, where the interaction of
\pi
with
f_{1}(1285)
, together with other coupled channels of a pseudoscalar meson and an axial vector meson, gives rise to the
\pi_{1}(1600)
, while the
\eta f_{1}(1285)
together with other coupled channels gives rise to the
\eta_{1}(1855)
.
The kernel of the interaction is obtained by assuming the axial-vector mesons as matter fields, which provides the Weinberg-Tomozawa interaction of order
O(1/f^{2})
, with
f
being the pion decay constant.
This work shares some overlap with the one we present here.
The difference is that we do not consider the axial vector mesons as matter fields but rather as molecular states composed of a pseudoscalar meson and a vector meson
Lutz and Kolomeitsev 2004
;
Roca et al. 2005
;
Zhou et al. 2014
.
We then we consider the interaction of a pseudoscalar meson with a molecular state, analogous to the interaction of a particle with a nucleus composed of two particles, where the interaction goes beyond the
O(1/f^{2})
approximation, which anticipates that we should obtain different results.

The interaction of a particle with a nucleus is most commonly addressed by defining an optical potential, and then solving the Schroedinger equation (or the Lippmann-Schwinger equation)
Ericson and Weise 1988
;
Seki and Masutani 1983
;
Nieves et al. 1993
;
Brown and Weise 1975
.
For the case of a particle interacting with a bound state of two particles, this would involve the construction of an optical potential and the posterior solution of the Lippmann-Schwinger equation.
This can be done by using the Fixed Center Approximation (FCA) to the Faddeev equations for the three-body system, in which the molecular state is considered as a cluster and the external particle collides with it without breaking it.
This is the basic assumption in the FCA, which is suited to the present case, where the cluster, the molecule, is identified in both the initial and final states.

The interaction of a particle with an axial-vector meson has recently received a boost with the measure of the correlation function for the interaction of a proton with the
f_{1}(1285)
state in Ref.
Šerkšnytė and Kundu
.
In anticipation of this experiment, a calculation was performed in Ref.
Encarnación et al. 2025
to obtain the correlation function for this system using the ordinary FCA, in which the last step mentioned above of the solution of the Lippmann-Schwinger equation was not done. Even then, the predicted results for the correlation function are in qualitative agreement with the recently measured magnitude.

The work of Ref.
Encarnación et al. 2025
spotted a problem in the FCA method, showing that it did not fulfill elastic unitarity at the particle-cluster threshold.
Although an empirical solution was proposed in Ref.
Encarnación et al. 2025
, a formal resolution was later provided by
Ref.
Ikeno and Oset 2025
in the study of the
n\bar{D}_{s0}(2317)
interaction, where the FCA results were understood as a particle-cluster optical potential, analogous to that in particle-nucleus interaction, which was unitarized in the particle-cluster system by means of the solution of the Lippmann-Schwinger equation.
Simplified formulas for the general solution provided in Ref.
Ikeno and Oset 2025
were found in Ref.
Agatão et al. 2025
, in the study of the correlation functions for the
n\,\bar{D}_{s1}(2460)
and
n\,\bar{D}_{s1}(2536)
systems, which allowed to find an analytical proof of the elastic unitarity.
Further work was done in the study of the super exotic three-body system
K^{*+}D^{*+}K^{*+}
, implementing some off-shell dependence in the formalism.
This is the final status of the evolution of the method, which has been also applied to evaluate correlation functions of the
Kf_{1}(1285)
in Ref.
Jia et al. 2026a
and
KD_{s0}(2317)
in Ref.
Jia et al. 2026b
.

The reliability of the method to study the interaction of these systems has been corroborated by the experimental results in Ref.
Šerkšnytė and Kundu
for the correlation function of
pf_{1}(1285)
, which show good agreement with the updated results of Ref.
Encarnación et al. 2025
in Ref.
Encarnación et al. 2026
, in light of the recent developments implementing elastic unitarity, which were obtained prior to the experimental results of Ref.
Šerkšnytė and Kundu
.

IV
Discussion and conclusions

We have studied the interaction of a
\pi^{0}
(or
\eta
) with the
f_{1}(1285)
resonance, assuming that the
f_{1}(1285)
is a
K^{*}\bar{K}-\bar{K^{*}}K
bound state.
We have used a framework to calculate it as one does in calculations of an external particle with a nucleus, defining an optical potential and later on constructing the Lippmann-Schwinger equation from this optical potential.
In our case, the role of the nucleus is played by the two-particle cluster, the
f_{1}(1285)
, while the external particle is either a
\pi^{0}
or an
\eta
.
The optical potential is obtained with the formalism of the fixed center approximation (FCA) to the three-body Faddeev equations, where the main assumption is that the two-particle cluster remains intact during the interaction, a situation perfectly suited to the present case, where we have the
f_{1}(1285)
particle at both the beginning and the end of the interaction.

The study of this interaction becomes more relevant when compared to other calculations where the kernel of the
\pi^{0}(\eta)\,f_{1}(1285)
interaction is obtained by assuming the
f_{1}(1285)
as a matter source and using the Weinberg-Tomozawa interaction, which happens to vanish in these two cases.
We have seen that in our case we go beyond the
O(1/f^{2})
level of the Weinberg-Tomozawa interaction, because our total amplitudes use as input individual
t_{i}
matrices that contain higher order terms than
O(1/f^{2})
, obtained from coupled-channel calculations implying unitarity in coupled channels, and these amplitudes do not vanish.

We have calculated the total amplitudes for the
\pi^{0}(\eta)\,f_{1}(1285)
interaction and find that we do not obtain clear signals for the
\pi_{1}(1400)
or
\pi_{1}(1600)
, nor for the
\eta_{1}(1855)
resonances from our approach.
However, we note the appearance of a weak structure around
1500-1600\textrm{ MeV}
in the
\pi^{0}f_{1}(1285)
amplitude, and a strong cusp structure in the
\eta f_{1}(1285)
amplitude at the
\eta f_{1}(1285)
threshold around
1830\textrm{ MeV}
.

We should mention that in Ref.
Yan et al. 2023
, in spite of having the
\pi^{0}(\eta)\,f_{1}(1285)
interaction null, the use of coupled channels involving other pseudoscalar meson-axial vector meson channels leads finally to the
\pi_{1}(1600)
and
\eta_{1}(1855)
states.
In our case one could in principle do that; however, rather than dealing with the overlap of two particles in the framework of Ref.
Yan et al. 2023
, we would have to deal with the overlap of three particles in this hypothetical calculation, since the axial-vector mesons are molecular states in our approach, and we deem this three-body overlap to be more difficult than in the two-body case, for what we do not anticipate much of an effect from this hypothetical calculation.
This renders the theoretical explanation of the
\pi_{1}(1600)
and
\eta_{1}(1855)
states a bit further than previously assumed.

As to the work of Ref.
Zhang et al. 2017
, the authors claim to get the
\pi_{1}(1600)
from the
\pi f_{1}(1285)
interaction, using the FCA.
It is unclear whether the structure that we find for the
\pi^{0}f_{1}(1285)
amplitude around
1500-1600\textrm{ MeV}
is related to the state found in Ref.
Zhang et al. 2017
.
There are some differences between our approach and that of Ref.
Zhang et al. 2017
, since we use the new formalism implementing elastic unitarity in the FCA amplitude.
As previously discussed, the standard FCA amplitude does not satisfy unitarity at threshold; hence cannot be reliably used near threshold, and becomes more uncertain when one goes further above it.
This problem was already anticipated, when comparing the FCA results with Faddeev calculation for a state appearing above threshold, the
\phi(2170)
, where one could see the failure of the FCA in that case
Martinez Torres et al. 2011
.

In the present work we have evaluated the scattering length and effective range for the
\pi^{0}(\eta)\,f_{1}(1285)
interaction, as well as the corresponding correlation functions.
With the advent of the first experimental measurement of the correlation function for a proton and the
f_{1}(1285)
, a door has been opened for future studies of the interaction of other particles with the
f_{1}(1285)
and with other resonances.
It will be interesting to compare these predictions with the experimental results.
The good agreement between the predictions obtained using the present formalism for the interaction of a proton and the
f_{1}(1285)
in Ref.
Encarnación et al. 2026
and posterior experimental results
Šerkšnytė and Kundu
makes us optimistic about the result of these comparisons.
