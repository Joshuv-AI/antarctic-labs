Speech enhancement method integrating squeeze and excitation channel attention mechanism and improved CGAN

Yecai Guo, Meiyu Liang, Tianyue Jiang

ABSTRACT
To avoid the additional computational cost and algorithmic latency introduced by short-time Fourier transformation and waveform reconstruction, we propose CGAN-SECA, which incorporates a squeeze-and-excitation channel attention mechanism to enhance noisy speech directly in the time domain. The generator employs a symmetric encoder-decoder backbone with skip connections to reconstruct waveform samples and reduce information loss during down-sampling and up-sampling. SECA adaptively recalibrates channel-wise feature responses, enabling the network to emphasize speech-relevant representations under complex noise. In the discriminator, Virtual Batch Normalization (VBN) and Dropout are introduced to regularize adversarial training. Least-squares adversarial objectives replace cross-entropy-based objectives, and an L1 reconstruction term encourages sample-level fidelity. The method is evaluated on VoiceBank-DEMAND, which contains environmental and babble noise, and on the THCHS30-DNS Challenge dataset under multiple Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) conditions. On VoiceBank-DEMAND, CGAN-SECA achieved PESQ and STOI scores of 3.018 and 0.954, respectively, ranking second only to CMGAN and outperforming the remaining comparison methods. CGAN-SECA also maintained fast inference, with a real-time factor (RTF) of 0.00423. On the THCHS30-DNS Challenge dataset, CGAN-SECA achieved the highest average PESQ and STOI scores among all evaluated methods, reaching 2.274 and 0.838, respectively, across the −5, 0, and 5 dB conditions. In particular, it produced the best results for both metrics at 0 and 5 dB, demonstrating effective speech enhancement and good generalization across different noise levels and datasets.

Introduction Speech enhancement refers to the process of using various signal processing and improvement technologies to heighten the quality, clarity and understandability of speech signals. As the key front-end technology of speech processing, it is a challenging task. Researchers have tried to implement various speech enhancement algorithms. Among them, traditional single-channel speech enhancement algorithms, that is, non-neural network speech enhancement algorithms, such as spectral subtraction method [ 1 ], subspace method [ 2 ], etc., belong to unsupervised learning algorithms. By assuming a stationary noise signal, these unsupervised learning algorithms are used to eliminate noises from noisy speech, thereby achieving the purpose of the speech enhancement. However, in practical applications, the noise signals are not always stable. Therefore, based on the assumption that the noise is stationary, it is very difficult for these algorithms to be used to eliminate the non-stationary noises from the noisy speech. Since their denoising effect is very poor, they cannot effectively materialize the purpose of the speech enhancement. Therefore, Speech enhancement tasks need to be tackled urgently. In recent years, in order to deal with the non-stationary noises better, deep learning models have been introduced into speech enhancement tasks, which include two main methods. One is Mask, whose speech spectrum can fully display the time and frequency domain information. So, the Mask method starts from the speech spectrum and realizes speech enhancement via processing the time-frequency units on the spectrum [ 3 ]. Common Mask methods include: Ideal Binary Mask (IBM) [ 4 ], Ideal Ratio Mask(IRM) [ 5 ], Ideal Amplitude Mask(IAM) [ 6 ], Phase Sensitive Mask(PSM) [ 7 ], complex Ideal Ratio Mask(cIRM) [ 8 ], Accurate Ratio Masking(ARM) [ 9 ], etc. Although a good enhancement effect of the speech can be achieved by using Mask, the speech spectrum cannot be directly converted into speech signal, and it needs additional operations to convert into speech signal. The other is Mapping, which uses the deep learning models to learn the mapping relationship between the noisy speech and pure speech and may be divided into spectrum mapping [ 10 ] and waveform mapping [ 11 ]. Common deep learning models are as follows: Deep Neural Network (DNN) [ 12 ]. The DNN can be used to build nonlinear functions, but too long input data will lead to too many parameters, too short input data will result in that the model has limited ability to capture contextual dependencies, and it is difficult to capture the long-term dependence and global structural characteristics of the speech signals. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) can solve the problem of limited input data, but cannot make good use of the context information of speech [ 13 ]. In order to make better use of the speech information, Long-Short Term Memory (LSTM) model [ 14 , 15 ] is employed to realize speech enhancement. In the LSTM model, the output of the current moment depends on the information of the previous moment, and the model can make full use of the fore and aft correlation of the data. However, there are some problems in training, such as the difficulty in capturing the long-term dependence among the speech signals, gradient disappearance or gradient explosion. In order to effectively establish a long-term dependency relationship, Gated Linear Unit (GLU) is introduced into the convolutional recurrent network to establish Gated Convolutional Recurrent Networks (GCRN), but it cannot understand the context relationship between speech data very well. In order to make full use of the speech information in the speech enhancement task, Researchers [ 16 ] designed some speech enhancement algorithms based on Gate-Driven Recurrent Convolutional Neural Network (GDRCNN), Fully Convolutional Neural Networks(FCNN) [ 17 ], or Convolutional-Recurrent Neural Network (CRN) [ 18 ], etc. Many time-frequency-domain speech enhancement methods first convert speech waveforms into time-frequency representations and reconstruct the waveforms after enhancement. These operations require magnitude and phase processing and introduce additional computation and algorithmic delay. In contrast, direct waveform mapping learns the relationship between noisy and clean speech directly in the time domain, avoiding the intermediate transformation and reconstruction steps. Based on this time-domain formulation, we propose an improved Conditional Generative Adversarial Network integrating a Squeeze-and-Excitation Channel Attention mechanism (CGAN-SECA). In the proposed model, the generator adopts a symmetric encoder–decoder architecture. Both the encoder and decoder incorporate SECA modules and use PReLU activation functions. Skip connections transfer features from each encoder layer to the corresponding decoder layer, thereby reducing information loss during downsampling and supporting detailed waveform reconstruction. Unlike the generator, the discriminator is an encoder-only network that processes channel-wise concatenated waveform pairs through one-dimensional convolutional blocks. The discriminator consists of the convolutional layers, SECAs, and Virtual Batch Normalization (VBN) modules, Dropout layers that are added to prevent overfitting, an LReLU function as the activation function, and the output layer including a one-dimensional convolutional layer, a fully connected layer, and a Sigmoid function. In the CGAN, SECA directs the model toward informative speech features and adjusts the channel weights during training. This channel weighting suppresses noise-related features while preserving speech content, thereby improving clarity and intelligibility. In summary, the main contributions of our work are as follows: 1 We propose CGAN-SECA, a waveform-domain conditional generative adversarial network for direct speech enhancement. Its generator adopts a symmetric encoder–decoder architecture with skip connections to transfer multiscale features, reduce information loss during downsampling and upsampling, and support detailed waveform reconstruction. 2 The SECA modules are incorporated into both the generator and discriminator to adaptively recalibrate channel responses, strengthen speech-relevant features, and suppress noise-related features. VBN and Dropout are further introduced into the discriminator to stabilize and regularize adversarial training and mitigate overfitting. 3 The conventional binary cross-entropy adversarial objective is replaced with a least-squares adversarial objective, and an L1 reconstruction term is included to constrain the enhanced waveform toward its paired clean target. Comprehensive experiments on VoiceBank-DEMAND and THCHS30-DNS, including baseline comparisons, ablation studies, statistical significance tests, and RTF analysis, verify the effectiveness, robustness, component contributions, and inference efficiency of CGAN-SECA. Overall architecture For speech enhancement, we use a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) to generate an enhanced speech signal G ( z ) that approximates the clean speech signal x as closely as possible, where z denotes the random variable [ 19 ]. However, an unconditional GAN provides the generator only with the random variable z and no direct guidance from the noisy input, which complicates clean-speech generation and slows convergence. Conditional Generative Adversarial Network (CGAN)-based speech-enhancement methods address this limitation by conditioning the generator on noisy speech [ 20 – 22 ]. The principle of the CGAN model based speech enhancement method is shown in Fig 1 . In Fig 1 , the noisy speech x c denotes the input to the generator G and is used as its conditional information to achieve accurate control of the generation process. Therefore, the input of the generator G includes not only random variable z , but also the noisy speech x c as conditional information. At this time, the generator G can generate enhanced speech similar to pure speech based on the features of the noisy speech, such as timbre, pitch, spectral characteristics, etc, thereby suppressing noise while preserving key information of the input speech. For example, the generator G can generate enhanced speech with similar harmonic structure and formant characteristics by learning the spectrum distribution of the noisy speech, thus avoiding speech distortion or “over smoothing” phenomenon that may occur in traditional algorithms. In addition, the generator G no longer needs to completely reconstruct the speech signal from random noise, but uses the guidance information from noisy speech x c to generate the target or pure speech more efficiently. Through this conditional control, the generator can obtain more useful information during the training process to accelerate convergence and improve training efficiency. Furthermore, it can finely control the characteristics of the generated speech to ensure that the final enhanced speech better meets the requirements of the pure speech. Since the CGAN has significant advantages in speech enhancement tasks under stationary noise environments, it can provide higher-quality speech in different noise environments, and also improve the stability and robustness of the model in practical applications. However, it still has some problems, such as gradient vanishing or pattern collapse, limited modeling ability, and restricted generalization ability under non-stationary noise environments. 10.1371/journal.pone.0359641.g001 Fig 1 Speech enhancement process of the CGAN. Improvements of the modules in overall architecture To address some existing problems in Fig 1 , we have made improvements to some modules in Fig 1 . to propose the Conditional Generative Adversarial Network fusing Squeeze and Excitation Channel Attention mechanism(CGAN-SECA) based speech enhancement method, as described below. Integration of squeeze and excitation channel attention mechanism to the CGAN The core of channel attention mechanism is to enable the model to automatically learn to assign different importance weights to different feature channels, thereby enhancing useful features and suppressing useless features. Its basic idea is to adaptively weight each channel of the input feature map by learning a vector of channel-attention weights to achieve feature recalibration. Based on this basic idea, we introduce the channel attention mechanism [ 23 ] into deep learning model for speech enhancement to dynamically adjust the importance of each feature channel, suppress noise related feature channels, and preserve speech related feature channels, thereby improving the speech enhancement performance of the model. Specifically, the channel attention mechanism is used to calculate the weight of each feature channel according to different noise environments, then the input features are weighted using these learned coefficients to enhance effective information related to speech and suppress irrelevant features related to noise, thereby enhancing the adaptability and robustness of the model, enabling the model to perform well under different noise conditions. In order to further improve the focus of the model on pure speech while suppressing noise features, the Squeeze and Excitation Channel Attention(SECA) mechanism is employed to enhance the expressiveness and performance of the model by making more fine-grained adjustments to the features of each channel. The structure of the SECA is shown in Fig 2 . Specifically, the SECA mechanism includes two steps: Squeeze and Excitation. In the Squeeze step, the SECA is to apply a squeeze operation on each channel to compress the global information of each channel feature, so that the model can focus on learning global information. In the Excitation step, the weights of the channels are further optimized and the importance of each channel in generating speech [ 24 ] is adjusted. Therefore, the SECA can more effectively suppress noise channels and enhance the response to the speech features, thus improving the speech enhancement effect of the model. 10.1371/journal.pone.0359641.g002 Fig 2 Squeeze and excitation channel attention mechanism. In Fig 2 , the input dimension of the SECA mechanism is ( B , C , H ) , where B is the batch size, C means the number of the channels, and H denotes the height of the feature map (in speech enhancement, it is called the number of time steps). The squeeze part adopts adaptive average pooling. Firstly, the input feature map is compressed into the global average of each channel in the time dimension, where each channel is represented by a scalar value. At this point, the dimension of the output becomes ( B , C , 1 ) . Secondly, the output with dimension ( B , C , 1 ) is flattened into the shape of ( B , C ) for further processing. The excitation part includes two fully connected layers that restore the number of the channels of the dimensional reduction to the original number of the channels C . Again, the Sigmoid activation function is used to compress the output between 0 and 1 to obtain the attention weight for each channel, which determines the importance of each channel in the final feature map. Finally, the weights of the channels are multiplied channel by channel and applied to the original input feature map. Since the SECA mechanism can explicitly model the dependencies between the channels, adaptively reweight feature channels, and enhance the generalization ability of the model, it is introduced into the CGAN to result in the CGAN-SECA based speech enhancement method. Results Datasets and experimental setup VoiceBank-DEMAND and the THCHS30-DNS Challenge dataset were used to evaluate CGAN-SECA under different noise and Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) conditions. VoiceBank-DEMAND pairs clean speech from the VoiceBank corpus with environmental recordings from the DEMAND database, and babble noise [ 32 ]. The training mixtures have SNRs of 0, 5, 10, and 15 dB, whereas the test mixtures have SNRs of 2.5, 7.5, 12.5, and 17.5 dB. The THCHS30-DNS Challenge dataset combines Chinese speech from THCHS30 with noises selected from the DNS Challenge noise set. The mixtures were generated at SNRs of −5, 0, and 5 dB.All waveforms were sampled at 16 kHz and processed using pre-emphasis with a coefficient of 0.95. Each waveform was divided into 16,384-sample segments using a segment shift of 8,192 samples. The generator and discriminator were optimized using RMSprop with a learning rate of 2 × 10 − 4 for 100 epochs. The batch size was 64, and the VBN reference batch contained 128 samples. The L1 reconstruction loss weight was set to 100. All experiments were conducted on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3080 Ti GPU using PyTorch 2.5.1 and CUDA 11.8. PESQ and STOI were used to quantify perceptual speech quality and intelligibility. Comparative results on the VoiceBank-DEMAND and THCHS30-DNS challenge datasets To evaluate the proposed CGAN-SECA, we compared it with a generative adversarial network (GAN), a conditional generative adversarial network (CGAN), the self-attention GAN for speech enhancement (SAGAN) [ 33 ], GDRCNN-ARM [ 16 ], CleanUNet [ 34 ], MetricGAN+ [ 35 ], and CMGAN [ 36 ]. The GAN generator employs a symmetric encoder–decoder architecture composed of stacked convolutional and transposed-convolutional layers, with skip connections used to transfer encoded features, while its discriminator consists of multiple convolutional layers. CGAN follows the same backbone but introduces noisy speech as conditional information for both the generator and the discriminator during adversarial training. SAGAN incorporates self-attention into the generator to improve the modeling of long-range dependencies in speech signals. GDRCNN-ARM consists of an encoder, an intermediate module, and a decoder, combining dilated convolutions, gated recurrent units, multi-head attention, layer-wise decoding, and skip connections for speech feature reconstruction. CleanUNet is a causal waveform-domain U-Net augmented with self-attention. MetricGAN+ employs an improved metric discriminator to guide the generator toward perceptual quality metrics directly. CMGAN combines Conformer-based time–frequency modeling with metric-guided adversarial learning and estimates both magnitude and complex spectral components. All models were retrained locally under the same experimental configuration. Future work will investigate reducing the channel width, replacing standard convolutions with depthwise-separable or grouped convolutions, and applying structured pruning, quantization, and knowledge distillation. These strategies are expected to reduce the model complexity and storage requirements while preserving its enhancement performance and inference efficiency. Group B, in which VBN was removed from the discriminator, achieved PESQ and STOI scores of 2.960 and 0.950, corresponding to decreases of 0.058 and 0.004, respectively, compared with the full model. Nevertheless, VBN primarily stabilizes feature distributions during discriminator training, whereas Dropout reduces excessive dependence on the training data and thereby mitigates overfitting. Their comparatively modest effects on the final PESQ and STOI scores suggest that, in the proposed network, they mainly support stable adversarial training and model generalization. These results indicate that the least-squares adversarial objective is more effective than BCE in improving the perceptual quality and intelligibility of the enhanced speech. This pattern agrees with the loss formulation. The squared-error penalty provides the generator with a continuous optimization signal. This structure transfers multiscale information and reduces the loss of waveform details during downsampling and upsampling. The SECA mechanism adaptively recalibrates channel responses to emphasize speech-related features and suppress noise interference. In the discriminator, VBN and Dropout regularize adversarial training, while the least-squares adversarial objective and L1 reconstruction term jointly improve perceptual quality and waveform fidelity. These components form a coordinated framework for feature extraction, adversarial optimization, and speech reconstruction. The experimental results demonstrate the strong overall performance of CGAN-SECA. On VoiceBank-DEMAND, it achieved PESQ and STOI scores of 3.018 and 0.954, respectively, outperforming most comparison methods and ranking second among all evaluated methods. On THCHS30-DNS, CGAN-SECA achieved the highest average PESQ and STOI and ranked first for both metrics at 0 and 5 dB. Its RTF of 0.00423 was approximately one tenth of that of CMGAN, demonstrating substantially faster inference. The waveform and spectrogram comparisons further confirmed its ability to suppress broadband noise and preserve speech-dominant temporal and spectral structures. The ablation experiments showed that all four improvements contributed to the final performance, with the removal of SECA causing the largest degradation, followed by the replacement of the least-squares objective with binary cross-entropy. Removing VBN or Dropout also resulted in consistent performance reductions, indicating that channel attention, adversarial optimization, and regularization jointly contribute to the enhancement performance. Furthermore, paired-sample tests on all 824 VoiceBank-DEMAND test utterances confirmed statistically significant improvements over SAGAN in both PESQ and STOI. Overall, CGAN-SECA achieves a strong balance among enhancement accuracy, robustness under different noise conditions, and inference efficiency. Although its parameter count remains relatively large, this limitation can be addressed through model compression. Future work will further conduct phase-oriented evaluations by comparing CGAN-SECA with representative time–frequency-domain methods under consistent experimental conditions. Circular phase error, group-delay error, and complex spectral distance will be employed to examine the preservation and reconstruction of phase-related speech information. Conclusion This study presented CGAN-SECA, a waveform-domain conditional generative adversarial network that combines a symmetric encoder–decoder generator, SECA-based channel recalibration, VBN and Dropout regularization, and a least-squares adversarial objective with L1 waveform reconstruction. CGAN-SECA achieved PESQ and STOI scores of 3.018 and 0.954 on VoiceBank-DEMAND, respectively, and obtained the highest average scores of 2.274 and 0.838 on THCHS30-DNS while maintaining an RTF of 0.00423. The ablation results verified the contributions of SECA, VBN, Dropout, and the modified loss function, and the paired-sample tests confirmed statistically significant improvements over SAGAN. Overall, these results support the effectiveness and robustness of CGAN-SECA under different speech, noise, and SNR conditions. Future work will focus on reducing the model parameter count and extending the evaluation to phase-related metrics and resource-constrained deployment scenarios.
