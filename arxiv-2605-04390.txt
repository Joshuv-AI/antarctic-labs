Local isomorphisms for families of projective non-unruled manifolds Thanks: The author is supported by NSFC (No. 12271073 and 12271412). Thanks: Keywords: locally isomorphic, smooth family, coarse moduli space Thanks: MSC(2010): 14D15, 14E30, 14J10
Mu-lin Li

Abstract
. Let π : 𝒳 → S \pi\colon{\cal X}\to S and π : 𝒴 → S \pi\colon{\cal Y}\to S be two smooth families of projective non-uniruled manifolds over a Riemann surface S S (probably non-compact). Suppose these two families are pointwise isomorphic. We prove that there exists an open dense subset U ⊂ S U\subset S such that the two restricted families are locally isomorphic over U U . This partially answers Wehler’s question on locally isomorphic of families of compact complex manifolds.



1.
introduction

Let
\pi:{\cal X}\to S
be a proper holomorphic submersion between complex manifolds
{\cal X}
and
S
. Therefore all the fibers of
\pi
are compact complex manifolds of equal dimension. Such a proper submersion is called a smooth family of compact complex manifolds. Let
\pi:{\cal X}\to S
and
\pi^{\prime}:{\cal Y}\to S
be two families of compact complex manifolds over a complex manifold
S
. We say these
families are pointwise isomorphic if there exists a bi-holomorphism
f_{s}:{\cal Y}_{s}=\pi^{\prime-1}(s)\to{\cal X}_{s}:=\pi^{-1}(s)
for every point
s\in S
.

We say the two families
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are locally isomorphic at
s
if there exists an open neighborhood
U_{s}\subset S
and a bi-holomorphism
g_{U_{s}}:{\cal Y}_{U_{s}}\to{\cal X}_{U_{s}}
such that
s\in U_{s}
and
\pi_{U_{s}}^{\prime}=\pi_{U_{s}}\circ g_{U_{s}}
. Here
\pi_{U_{s}}:{\cal X}_{U_{s}}=\pi^{-1}({U_{s}})\to{U_{s}}
is the restriction of
\pi
, and
\pi^{\prime}_{U_{s}}:{\cal Y}_{U_{s}}=\pi^{\prime-1}({U_{s}})\to{U_{s}}
is the restriction of
\pi^{\prime}
.

In 1977, Wehler posed the following question.

Question 1.1
.

Let
\pi:{\cal X}\to S
and
\pi^{\prime}:{\cal Y}\to S
be two smooth families of compact complex manifolds over a complex manifold
S
. Suppose that these two families are pointwise isomorphic. If the function

s\to\dim H^{0}({\cal Y}_{s},T_{{\cal Y}_{s}})

is constant on
S
, are these two families locally isomorphic at every point of
S
?

Wehler
[
12
]
proved that it is true for families of complex tori and families of compact manifolds with negative curved holomorphic curvature. Meersseman
[
8
]
attempted to prove it for arbitrary families of compact manifolds, but a counterexample constructed by Kirschner
[
5
]
revealed gaps in his proof. This led to the proposal of the following weakened version of the question.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
\pi:{\cal X}\to S
and
\pi^{\prime}:{\cal Y}\to S
be two smooth families of compact complex manifolds over a complex manifold
S
. Suppose that these two families are pointwise isomorphic. If the function

s\to\dim H^{0}({\cal Y}_{s},T_{{\cal Y}_{s}})

is constant on
S
, then there exists an open dense subset
U\subset S
such that the restricted families
\pi_{U}:{\cal X}_{U}=\pi^{-1}({U})\to{U}
and
\pi^{\prime}_{U}:{\cal Y}_{U}=\pi^{\prime-1}({U})\to{U}
are locally isomorphic at every point
s\in U
.

In this paper, we focus on families of projective non-uniruled manifolds. By using the coarse moduli space of polarized Kähler manifolds constructed by Fujiki
[
3
]
and Schumacher
[
10
]
, we establish the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\pi:{\cal X}\to S
and
\pi^{\prime}:{\cal Y}\to S
be two smooth families of projective non-uniruled manifolds over a Riemann surface
S
. Suppose the two families are pointwise isomorphic. Then there exists an open dense subset
U\subset S
such that the restricted families
\pi_{U}:{\cal X}_{U}=\pi^{-1}({U})\to{U}
and
\pi^{\prime}_{U}:{\cal Y}_{U}=\pi^{\prime-1}({U})\to{U}
are locally isomorphic at every point
s\in U
.

The main idea of the proof is as following. For two smooth families of projective non-uniruled manifolds
\pi:{\cal X}\to\Delta
and
\pi^{\prime}:{\cal Y}\to\Delta
, we first construct two new families of polarized projective non-uniruled manifolds
(\pi_{U}:{\cal X}_{U}\to U,\alpha)
and
(\pi^{\prime}_{U}:{\cal Y}_{U}\to U,\beta)
. These two families induce two holomorphic maps
f,\,f^{\prime}:U\to M
satisfying
f(U)=f^{\prime}(U)
, where
M
is the coarse moduli of polarized projective non-uniruled manifolds. Then by using the fact that the moduli functor of polarized projective non-uniruled manifolds is locally complete, we finish the proof.

2.
Preliminaries:Diffeomorphism groups and Moduli space of polarized Kähler manifolds

2.1.
Diffeomorphism groups

First, we recall some basic properties of the space of smooth maps. Let
X
and
Y
be two compact complex manifolds. Denote by
C^{\infty}(Y,X)
the space of smooth maps between
Y
and
X
, endowed with the compact open
C^{\infty}
topology. Let
\diff(Y,X)
be the space of diffeomorphisms between
Y
and
X
(assuming that they are diffeomorphic to each other). We have the following properties.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
9
, 2.6 Lemma]
\diff(Y,X)
is an open subset of
C^{\infty}(Y,X)
in compact open
C^{\infty}
topology.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
6
, Proposition 7]
Let
X,Y,Z
be compact complex manifolds, then the composition

(2.1)

C^{\infty}(Z,Y)\times C^{\infty}(Y,X)\to C^{\infty}(Z,X)

is smooth in compact open
C^{\infty}
topology.

Proposition 2.3
.

The space
\diff(Y,X)
has only countable many connected components.

Proof.

Let
\diff(Y):=\diff(Y,Y)
be the diffeomorphism group of
Y
. It has only countable many connected components because it is locally arc-connected and has the homotopy
type of a countable CW complex by
[
1
, Section 1.1.2]
. By Proposition
2.2
, the space
\diff(Y,X)
is isomorphic to
\diff(Y)
. Thus
\diff(Y,X)
has only countable many connected components.
∎

Then we have the following equality on induced morphisms on cohomologies groups.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
f,g:Y\to X
be two diffeomorphisms in the same connected component of
\diff(Y,X)
. Then the induced morphisms on cohomology groups are equal

f^{*}=g^{*}:H^{2}(X,R)\to H^{2}(Y,R),

where
R=\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
.

2.2.
Moduli space of polarized Kähler manifolds

Definition 2.5
.

A polarized compact Kähler manifold is a pair
(X,\omega_{X})
consisting of a connected compact complex manifold
X
and a Kähler class
\omega_{X}\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

An isomorphism of two polarized compact Kähler manifolds
(X,\omega_{X})
and
(\widetilde{X},\omega_{\widetilde{X}})
is an holomorphic isomorphism
\phi:X\to\widetilde{X}
with
\phi^{*}(\omega_{\widetilde{X}})=\omega_{X}\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 2.6
.

A polarized family of compact Kähler manifolds (parametrized by a complex space
B
) is a pair
(f,\omega_{{\cal X}/B})
consisting of a proper smooth morphism
f:{\cal X}\to B
of complex spaces with connected fibers and an element

\omega_{{\cal X}/B}\in\Gamma(B,R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{R})
such that

(1)

The value of the section
\omega_{{\cal X}_{b}}=(\omega_{{\cal X}/B})(b)
on
b
is a Kähler class on each fiber
{\cal X}_{b}=f^{-1}(b)
.

(2)

\eta(\omega_{{\cal X}/B})=0
where
\eta:\Gamma(B,R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{R})\to\Gamma(B,R^{2}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{{\cal X}})
is the homomorphism induced by the natural inclusion
\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{O}_{{\cal X}}
of sheaves on
\mathcal{X}
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the groupoid fibered category over the category of reduced complex spaces, such that for a reduced complex space
B
the groupoid
\mathcal{M}(B)
is as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(B)=

\displaystyle\bigg\{(f:{\cal X}\to B,\omega_{{\cal X}/B})\,\mbox{is a family of polarized K\"{a}hler manifolds };

\displaystyle({\cal X}_{b},\omega_{{\cal X}_{b}})\,\mbox{is a polarized non-uniruled K\"{a}hler manifold,\, $\forall b\in B$}\bigg\}.

An arrow of
\mathcal{M}(B)
from
(f:{\cal X}\to B,\omega_{{\cal X}/B})
to
(f^{\prime}:{\cal X}^{\prime}\to B,\omega_{{\cal X}^{\prime}/B})
is an isomorphism
\Phi:{\cal X}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}
such that
\Phi^{*}(\omega_{{\cal X}^{\prime}/B})=\omega_{{\cal X}/B}
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\left(f:{\cal X}\to S,\omega_{{\cal X}}\right)
be a polarized family of compact Kähler manifolds. Let
s\in S
. Then we say that
\left(f:{\cal X}\to S,\omega_{{\cal X}}\right)
is locally complete at
s
if for
any polarized deformation
\left(f^{\prime}:{\cal X}^{\prime}\to S^{\prime},\omega_{{\cal X}^{\prime}}\right)
of
({\cal X}_{s},\omega_{{\cal X}_{s}})
, there exists a morphism
\tau:S^{\prime}\to S
, such that
\left(f^{\prime}:{\cal X}^{\prime}\to S^{\prime},\omega_{{\cal X}^{\prime}}\right)
is isomorphic
to the pull-back of
\left(f:{\cal X}\to S,\omega_{{\cal X}}\right)
to
S^{\prime}
. If, further,
\tau
is unique for any
\left(f^{\prime}:{\cal X}^{\prime}\to S^{\prime},\omega_{{\cal X}^{\prime}}\right)
as above, we say that
\left(f:{\cal X}\to S,\omega_{{\cal X}}\right)
, considered as a polarized deformation of
({\cal X}_{s},\omega_{{\cal X}_{s}})
, is the local modular family of
({\cal X}_{s},\omega_{{\cal X}_{s}})
. In this case
\tau
is called the universal map associated to
\left(f^{\prime}:{\cal X}^{\prime}\to S^{\prime},\omega_{{\cal X}^{\prime}}\right)
.

Such a local modular family
\left(f:{\cal X}\to S,\omega_{{\cal X}}\right)
is usually called the Kuranish family of
({\cal X}_{s},\omega_{X_{s}})
. The following Proposition asserts the existence of the Kuranishi family for polarized non-uniruled manifolds.

Proposition 2.8
.

[
3
, Proposition 8]
For any polarized compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega_{X})
such that
Aut_{0}(X)
is a complex torus its local modular family
\left(f:{\cal X}\to S,\omega_{{\cal X}}\right)
with
({\cal X}_{0},\omega_{{\cal X}_{0}})=(X,\omega_{X})
exists. Moreover it is locally complete at every point in a neighborhood of
0
.

By
[
3
, Section 1, Theorem]
,
[
10
, (1.3)Theorem]
and
[
4
, Theorem 5.9]
, there exists a coarse separated moduli complex space
M
for
\mathcal{M}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Proposition 3.4

Theorem 3.5
