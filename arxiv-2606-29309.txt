Abstract
. We introduce a binary matroid approach to the enumeration of mod 2 2 toric-colorable seeds of fixed Picard number. We organize these matroids by their contraction category and enumerate weak pseudomanifold subcomplexes by a dynamic programming algorithm. The main computational step uses a Gray code traversal of the mod 2 2 kernel of the ridge-facet incidence matrix. As the main new result, we find that there are 198,846 198{,}846 mod 2 2 toric-colorable seeds of dimension four and Picard number five. We also check that they all are toric-colorable. Finally, the same framework independently reproduces the Picard number 4 4 enumeration of Choi, Jang, and Vallée much faster than their previous method.

Introduction

A
toric manifold
is a complete nonsingular toric variety.
By the fundamental theorem of toric geometry, toric manifolds are described by complete nonsingular fans.
This description turns geometric questions about toric manifolds into combinatorial questions about cones and lattice vectors.
Thus the classification of toric manifolds is closely related to the classification of certain simplicial spheres with characteristic maps.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete nonsingular fan of dimension
n
, and let
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m}
be its rays.
For each
i
, let
v_{i}
be the primitive generator of
\rho_{i}
.
The fan
\Sigma
determines a simplicial complex
K_{\Sigma}=\{I\subset[m]\mid\cone(v_{i}\mid i\in I)\in\Sigma\}
.
Since
\Sigma
is complete and nonsingular,
K_{\Sigma}
is an
(n-1)
-dimensional piecewise linear (PL) sphere on
[m]
.
The assignment of primitive ray generators gives a map
\lambda_{\Sigma}\colon[m]\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}
given by
i\mapsto v_{i}
.
This map satisfies the characteristic condition

\{\lambda_{\Sigma}(i)\mid i\in I\}
is part of a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
for every
I\in K_{\Sigma}
.

Equivalently, the vectors assigned to every facet of
K_{\Sigma}
form a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

A PL sphere
K
is called
toric-colorable
if it supports a
\mathbb{Z}
-characteristic map.
It is called
fanlike
if it supports a
\mathbb{Z}
-characteristic map whose associated cones form a complete nonsingular fan.
Thus every fanlike PL sphere is toric-colorable, but the converse might not hold.
The fanlike condition is the condition needed to construct toric manifolds.
The toric-colorable condition is a necessary first condition.

In this paper, we mainly use the mod
2
version of this condition.
Let
K
be an
(n-1)
-dimensional PL sphere on
[m]
.
We say that
K
is
mod
2
toric-colorable
, or
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
-colorable
, if there exists a map
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}\colon[m]\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
such that

\{\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}(i)\mid i\in I\}
is linearly independent over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for every
I\in K
.

Equivalently, the vectors assigned to every facet of
K
form a basis of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
.
Every
\mathbb{Z}
-characteristic map reduces to such a mod
2
characteristic map.
Hence every fanlike PL sphere is mod
2
toric-colorable.
This makes mod
2
toric-colorability an important and computable necessary condition in the classification problem.

The mod
2
condition is rephrased as follows in terms of binary matroids.
Indeed, a map
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}\colon[m]\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
defines a binary matroid
M_{\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}}
on
[m]
, whose independent sets are precisely those subsets
I\subset[m]
for which
\{\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}(i)\mid i\in I\}
is linearly independent.
Thus, after identifying a matroid with its independence complex, the mod
2
non-singularity condition for
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}
with respect to
K
is equivalent to the inclusion

K\subseteq M_{\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}}.

This reformulation is the main combinatorial idea used within this paper.

Throughout,
K
is an
(n-1)
-dimensional PL sphere on
[m]
.
Following the toric convention, we call
p=m-n
the
Picard number
of
K
.

The classification of toric manifolds by Picard number is a classical problem in algebraic geometry.
Toric manifolds of Picard number one are isomorphic to projective spaces.
Those of Picard number two were classified by Kleinschmidt
[
11
]
.
The Picard number three case was studied by Batyrev
[
3
]
.
Recently, Choi, Jang, and Vallée completed the classification of complete nonsingular fans of Picard number four in
[
6
]
.

Every PL sphere lies in the wedge class of a wedge-minimal PL sphere, which is called a
seed
.
Equivalently, every PL sphere can be obtained from a seed by a sequence of simplicial wedge operations.
If
K
has dimension
n-1
and
m
vertices, then a wedge operation increases both
n
and
m
by one.
Hence it preserves the Picard number.
By Choi and Park
[
7
]
, every complete nonsingular fan over
K
can be obtained from a complete nonsingular fan over a seed of
K
by the corresponding sequence of wedge operations.
Therefore, the classification of toric manifolds with fixed Picard number reduces to the classification of fanlike seeds with that Picard number and the classification of characteristic maps over them that give complete nonsingular fans.

The finiteness theorem in
[
8
]
states that, for each fixed Picard number, there are only finitely many mod
2
toric-colorable seeds.
Thus the enumeration of mod
2
toric-colorable seeds, and hence the search for fanlike seeds among them, is a finite problem.

Choi, Jang, and Vallée carried out this program for Picard number four.
First, they enumerated all mod
2
toric-colorable seeds of Picard number four by using a GPU algorithm, and found
3{,}153
such seeds
[
5
]
.
Second, they determined which of these seeds are fanlike by testing which characteristic maps give complete nonsingular fans, and found
59
fanlike seeds.
In addition, they classified the characteristic maps over these seeds that yield complete nonsingular fans.
This provides the classification of toric manifolds with Picard number four
[
6
]
.

In this paper, we introduce a binary matroid approach to the enumeration of mod
2
toric-colorable seeds.
For nonsuspended seeds, the associated binary matroids are cosimple.
This reduces the search to cosimple binary matroids of fixed corank.
We organize these matroids by their contraction category and enumerate weak pseudomanifold subcomplexes using dynamic programming and Gray code traversal, then filter them by the PL sphere and seed conditions.

More precisely, we complete here the new case
(p,n)=(5,5)
and also check that the output mod
2
toric-colorable seed PL spheres indeed support integral characteristic map, see Section
5

Equivalently, this is the enumeration of toric-colorable seeds of dimension four and Picard number five.
This case cannot be obtained from the existing database of PL spheres, since it would require a complete database of PL
4
-spheres with ten vertices.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Simplicial complexes, PL spheres, and wedge operation

In all that follows, we let
[m]:=\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
for a positive integer
m
.
A
simplicial complex

K
on the ambient vertex set
[m]
is a collection of subsets of
[m]
such that if
\sigma\in K
and
\tau\subset\sigma
, then
\tau\in K
.
An element of
K
is called a
face
of
K
, and a face with
k+1
elements is called a
k
-face
.
An element
v\in[m]
is called a
ghost
vertex of
K
if
\{v\}\notin K
.
Let
K
be a simplicial complex on
V
and let
K^{\prime}
be a simplicial complex on
V^{\prime}
.
We say that
K
and
K^{\prime}
are
isomorphic
, written
K\cong K^{\prime}
, if there exists a bijection
\phi\colon V\to V^{\prime}
such that
K^{\prime}=\{\phi(\sigma)\mid\sigma\in K\}
.

A simplicial complex
K
is called a
PL sphere
if its geometric realization is piecewise-linearly homeomorphic to the boundary of a simplex.
Every PL sphere is
pure
.
This means that all its maximal faces, with respect to inclusion, have the same dimension.
If this dimension is
n-1
, then the maximal faces are called the
facets
, and the faces of dimension
n-2
are called the
ridges
.
The
dimension
of a pure simplicial complex
K
is the dimension of its maximal faces.
The
Picard number
of a simplicial complex
K
on
[m]
with
k
ghost vertices, and of dimension
n-1
is defined as
m-n-k
.
In particular, if
K
has no ghost vertices, then its Picard number is
m-n
.

Unless otherwise stated, PL spheres considered in enumeration are assumed to have no ghost vertices.
Ghost vertices are allowed mainly when we regard inclusions
K\subseteq M
and links or contractions as complexes on an ambient vertex set.

The
link
of a face
\sigma
in
K
is the subcomplex

K/\sigma:=\{\tau\in K\mid\sigma\cap\tau=\emptyset,\sigma\cup\tau\in K\}.

We regard
K/\sigma
as a simplicial complex on the ambient vertex set
[m]\setminus\sigma
.
In particular, it may have ghost vertices.
When
\sigma=\{v\}
, we denote the link by
K/v
.

The
join
of two simplicial complexes
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
on disjoint vertex sets is the simplicial complex

K_{1}\join K_{2}:=\{\sigma_{1}\cup\sigma_{2}\mid\sigma_{1}\in K_{1},\sigma_{2}\in K_{2}\}.

In all that follows, we let
I
be a
1
-simplex with vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
, and
\partial I
be its boundary, that is, the
0
-sphere.
The
suspension
of a simplicial complex
K
is the join of
K
with a
0
-sphere.
The vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are called
suspended vertices
.

The
(simplicial) wedge operation
on a simplicial complex
K
at a non-ghost vertex
v
of
K
is the simplicial complex

\Wed_{v}(K):=(I\join K/v)\cup(\partial I\join(K\setminus v)),

where
K\setminus v
is the subcomplex of
K
consisting of faces not containing
v
.
In that case, the vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are called
wedged vertices
.
Intuitively, the wedge operation replaces the vertex
v
with an edge
I
.
Faces in the link of
v
are joined with this edge, while faces not containing
v
are joined with the boundary
\partial I
.
The wedge operation increases both the dimension and the number of vertices of
K
by one, hence preserving the Picard number.
It also preserves the PL sphere property
[
7
]
.
By iterating the wedge operation, we can obtain new simplicial complexes from a given one.
A PL sphere
K
is called a
seed
if it is wedge-minimal, that is, if it cannot be obtained from a PL sphere of smaller dimension by a sequence of wedge operations.
Hence, every PL sphere is obtained through iterated wedges starting from a seed.
Note that wedging at either one of the two wedged vertices coming from a wedge at
v
yields isomorphic simplicial complexes.

2.2.
Toric-colorable PL spheres and characteristic maps

A
characteristic map
over an
(n-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex
K
on the vertex set
[m]
is a map

\lambda\colon[m]\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}

such that for each face
\sigma=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\in K
, the set of vectors
\{\lambda(i_{1}),\ldots,\lambda(i_{k})\}
can be extended to a basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, a condition referred to as the
nonsingularity condition
.
A pair
(K,\lambda)
is called a
characteristic pair
.
A simplicial complex
K
is called
toric-colorable
if there exists a characteristic map over
K
.

A
mod
2
characteristic map
is a map
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}\colon[m]\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}

3

3

The superscript
\mathbb{R}
follows the convention in toric topology, where such mod
2
characteristic maps arise in the study of real toric manifolds.

such that for each face
\sigma=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\in K
, the set of vectors
\{\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}(i_{1}),\ldots,\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}(i_{k})\}
is linearly independent over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, a condition referred to as the
mod
2

.
If
K
supports a mod

is
mod

.
In particular, if
K
supports a characteristic map

Let
W
be part of a basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
The
projection
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with respect to

is the quotient map

\pi_{/W}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\rangle

followed by a chosen identification of the quotient with
\mathbb{Z}^{n-k}
.
Let
\lambda\colon[m]\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a map.
A subset
\sigma\subseteq[m]

\{\lambda(i)\mid i\in\sigma\}

.
Given a nonsingular subset

, the projection of

is the composite map

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Proj_{\sigma}\lambda\colon&[m]\setminus\sigma&\to\mathbb{Z}^{n-|\sigma|}\\
&i&\mapsto\pi_{/\{\lambda(v)\mid v\in\sigma\}}(\lambda(i)).\end{array}

In particular, if

and
\sigma
is a face of

\Proj_{\sigma}\lambda

The same definition applies over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

If
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}\colon[m]\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
and
\sigma
is nonsingular, then
\Proj_{\sigma}\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}
is obtained by quotienting
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
by the span of
\{\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}(i)\mid i\in\sigma\}
.

2.3.
A binary matroid rephrasing

A
matroid
is a pure simplicial complex which satisfies the following
augmentation property
: if
\tau
and
\sigma
are two faces of the matroid such that
|\tau|<|\sigma|
, then there exists an element
x\in\sigma\setminus\tau
such that
\tau\cup\{x\}
is also a face of the matroid.
The underlying set of the matroid is called
ground set
.
The faces of the matroid are called
independent sets
, the maximal faces are called
bases
, and their common size is called the
rank
of the matroid.
Note that the rank of a matroid equals its dimension plus one.
An
\mathbb{F}
-representable matroid
is a matroid that can be represented as a collection of linearly independent subsets of columns of a matrix over the field
\mathbb{F}
.
A matroid is
binary
if it is representable over the finite field
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

In particular, any given mod
2
map
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}\colon[m]\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
encodes a binary matroid
M_{\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}}
on the ground set
[m]
.
Its rank is at most
n
.
Moreover, the mod
2
nonsingularity condition with respect to an
(n-1)
-dimensional PL sphere
K
is reinterpreted as an inclusion of simplicial complexes

K\subseteq M_{\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}}.

Therefore,
K
is mod
2
toric-colorable if and only if there exists a binary matroid
M
of rank
n
on the ground set
[m]
such that
K\subseteq M
.

The projection of mod
2
characteristic maps can also be rephrased in terms of binary matroids, as follows.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}\colon[m]\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
be a mod
2

be its associated binary matroid.
Suppose that
\sigma\subset[m]
is nonsingular with respect to
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}
.
After identifying both ground sets with
[m]\setminus\sigma
, we have

M_{\Proj_{\sigma}\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}}=M_{\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}}/\sigma.

Proof.

Let
\tau\subset[m]\setminus\sigma
.
By the definition of the projected map, the vectors
\{(\Proj_{\sigma}\lambda^{\mathbb{R}})(i)\mid i\in\tau\}
are linearly independent if and only if
\{\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}(i)\mid i\in\tau\cup\sigma\}
are linearly independent.
Thus
\tau
is independent in
M_{\Proj_{\sigma}\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}}
if and only if
\tau\cup\sigma
is independent in
M_{\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}}
.
This is exactly the definition of the

M_{\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}}/\sigma
in matroid theory, see

.

In this language, projections of characteristic maps become contractions of binary matroids.
Equivalently, at the level of independence complexes, they become links of independent sets.

A
circuit
is a minimal dependent set.
Equivalently, it is a minimal non-face of the matroid.
A
loop
in a matroid is a circuit of size one, that is, an element that is not part of any basis.
In the context of binary matroids, a loop corresponds to a column of the representing matrix that is all zero.
Two elements in a circuit of size two are called
parallel
, and for binary matroids, they correspond to two identical nonzero columns in the representing matrix.
A matroid is called
simple
if it has no loops or parallel elements.

The
dual
of a matroid
M
on the ground set
E
is the matroid
M^{\ast}
on the same ground set
E
whose bases are the complements of the bases of
M
.
A
coloop
is a loop of the dual matroid
M^{\ast}
, or equivalently, an element that is contained in every basis of
M
.
A cocircuit is a circuit of the dual matroid
M^{\ast}
.
Two elements in a cocircuit of size two are called
series elements
.
A matroid is called
cosimple
if it has no coloops or series elements.

We have the following elementary facts, see
[
12

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
M
be a cosimple binary matroid on the ground set

If

is a cosimple binary matroid on

.
More generally, if
\sigma

M/\sigma
is a cosimple binary matroid on

p
.

Proof.

Since

\rank(M/v)=\rank(M)-1
.
Hence
\corank(M/v)=(m-1)-(\rank(M)-1)=\corank(M)=p
.
Moreover,
(M/v)^{\ast}=M^{\ast}\setminus v
.
Since
M
is cosimple,
M^{\ast}
is simple.
Deletion preserves simplicity.
Thus
(M/v)^{\ast}
is simple, and so
M/v
is cosimple.
The general case is done by induction on
|\sigma|
.
∎

3.
A Gray code technique for enumerating weak pseudomanifolds

3.1.
Recollection

We first recall several properties of PL spheres used in
[
6
]
for the enumeration of toric-colorable seeds of Picard number
4
.
The first property we use is that PL spheres are
weak pseudomanifolds
, which are pure simplicial complexes having each ridge contained in exactly two facets.
In turn, enumerating weak pseudomanifolds is done by linear algebra as follows.

Let
M
be a pure simplicial complex,
\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{F}\}
be its set of facets, and
\{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{R}\}
be its set of ridges.
The
ridge-facet incidence matrix
of
M
is the matrix
A(M)\in{\{0,1\}}^{R\times F}
given by

{A(M)}_{rj}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\tau_{r}\subseteq\sigma_{j},\\
0&\text{if }\tau_{r}\not\subseteq\sigma_{j}.\end{cases}

A pair
(M,K)
of pure simplicial complexes on the same finite set is called a
pure relative pair
if
M
and
K
have the same dimension and
K\subseteq M
.

Example 3.1
.

Let
K
be a PL sphere and let
\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}
be a mod
2
characteristic map over
K
.
Then,
K
is a weak pseudomanifold, and
(M_{\lambda^{\mathbb{R}}},K)
is a pure relative pair.

Let
(M,K)
be a pure relative pair and let
\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{F}\}
be the set of facets of
M
.
The
facet indicator vector of
K
in

is denoted by
\chi^{K}\in{\{0,1\}}^{F}
and is given by

{(\chi^{K})}_{j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\sigma_{j}\text{ is a facet of }K,\\
0&\text{if }\sigma_{j}\text{ is not a facet of }K.\end{cases}

Proposition 3.2

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
(M,K)
be a pure relative pair.
Then

is a weak pseudomanifold if and only if the integer vector

has only coordinates in

.
In particular,
\chi^{K}\in\ker_{2}A(M)
.

Proposition
3.2
gives a direct algorithm for computing all weak pseudomanifold subcomplexes of a given pure simplicial complex

with

In our applications,

will be a cosimple binary matroid.
The algorithm proceeds as follows.

(1)

Compute a mod

of the kernel of the ridge-facet incidence matrix

.

(2)

For every vector

, compute
\chi=BX
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{F}
.
If the integer vector

has only entries in
\{0,2\}
, store

.

In order to improve the total computation time of the enumeration of all

, we will use a method called Gray code enumeration for

as follows.
For

, a

of

is an ordering

of the elements of

such that

and

differ in exactly one coordinate for every

.

Example 3.3
.

The most commonly used Gray code enumeration is the

, defined by

X^{\kappa}=\kappa\oplus(\kappa\gg 1),\qquad 0\leq\kappa\leq 2^{d}-1,

where
\oplus
denotes the bitwise XOR operation, and
\gg
represents a bit shift.
In this encoding, the coordinate that changes between
X^{\kappa-1}
and
X^{\kappa}
is the active bit of
X^{\kappa-1}\oplus X^{\kappa}
.

We use a function

associated with a fixed Gray code enumeration of
\{0,1\}^{d}
.
For
1\leq\kappa\leq 2^{d}-1
, the value
\texttt{Gray}[\kappa]
is the coordinate that changes between
X^{\kappa-1}
and
X^{\kappa}
.

We prepare a Gray code enumeration of weak pseudomanifolds in the mod

kernel of a matrix.

Data:

Proposition 3.4
.

Algorithm
1
returns exactly the nonzero vectors
y\in\ker_{2}(A)
such that the integer vector

belongs to
\{0,2\}^{R}
.

Proof.

The Gray code enumeration visits every nonzero vector
X\in\{0,1\}^{d}
exactly once. Since the columns of
B
form a basis of
\ker_{2}(A)
, the vectors
y=BX
are precisely the nonzero elements of
\ker_{2}(A)
.

We maintain the invariant that
\rho=Ay
over
\mathbb{Z}
.
Initially,
y=0
and
\rho=0
.
At a Gray step, only the coordinates
j\in\supp(B[\beta])
are toggled.
If the new value of
y[j]
is
1
, the contribution of column
j
to each incident row increases by
1
.
If the new value is
0
, it decreases by
1
.
Thus the update

\rho[r]\leftarrow\rho[r]+(2y[j]-1)

preserves the invariant
\rho=Ay
.
Therefore the test
\rho\in\{0,2\}^{R}
is exactly the weak pseudomanifold test.
∎

This reduces the total complexity of the enumeration process as follows.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
M
be a pure simplicial complex of dimension
n-1
, and suppose that the dimension of the mod
2
kernel of
A(M)
is
d
.
Then each column of
A(M)
has exactly
n
nonzero entries.
A classical enumeration uses
\mathcal{O}(F(d+n)2^{d})
elementary operations, while the Gray code enumeration uses
\mathcal{O}(Fn2^{d})
atomic bitwise operations.

Proof.

For
0\leq\kappa\leq 2^{d}-1
, consider the current vector
{y_{\kappa}}=BX_{\kappa}
.

Classical enumeration.
Computing
{y_{\kappa+1}}=BX_{\kappa+1}
from scratch requires
\mathcal{O}(F\cdot d)
bitwise operations.
The integer image
A{y_{\kappa+1}}
is then computed from scratch by summing over all
j\in\supp({y_{\kappa+1}})
.
Since each column
A_{\mathsf{col}}[j]
has exactly
n
nonzero entries, this costs
\mathcal{O}(F\cdot n)
operations.
Since we check whether
\rho\in\{0,2\}^{R}
at each step, the total per-step cost is thus
\mathcal{O}(F(d+n)+R)
, giving
\mathcal{O}((F(d+n)+R)\cdot 2^{d})
overall.
Since
R\leq nF
, the cost is
\mathcal{O}(F(d+n)2^{d})
.

Gray code enumeration.
For
1\leq\kappa<2^{d}
,
X_{\kappa}
and
X_{\kappa+1}
differ only at a single index
\beta\in[d]
.
Thus, we have

{y_{\kappa+1}}={y_{\kappa}}\oplus B[\beta],

which requires
\mathcal{O}(F)
bitwise operations.
Moreover, only the coordinates
j\in\supp(B[\beta])
of
\chi^{K}
are toggled, so only the row sums
\rho[r]
for rows
r\in A_{\mathsf{col}}[j]
need updating.
Since each column
A_{\mathsf{col}}[j]
has exactly
n
nonzero entries and
|\supp(B[\beta])|\leq F
, updating the row sums costs at most
\mathcal{O}(F\cdot n+R)=\mathcal{O}(Fn)
operations per step.
The total cost is therefore
\mathcal{O}(Fn\cdot 2^{d})
.
∎

In the Picard number
4
computations, the values of
n
range from
2
to
11
, while the values of
d
range from
10
to
30
.
Thus the Gray code method removes the repeated matrix-vector multiplication by
B
.
In our implementation, for the Picard number four instances considered in
[
5
]
, this gives about a fourfold speedup.

Remark 3.6
.

The method also parallelizes naturally.
Fix the first
w

X^{\head}\in\{0,1\}^{w}
.
Assign one head vector to each of the
2^{w}
workers.
Each worker initializes
y
and
\rho
from this head vector and then runs a Gray code enumeration on the remaining cube
\{0,1\}^{d-w}
.

It remains to adapt this enumeration algorithm to the dynamic programming method.
For this purpose, we introduce a few more definitions.
A pure simplicial complex is a
weak pseudomanifold with boundary
if each ridge is contained in at most
2

3.2.
Gray code enumeration for weak pseudomanifolds with boundary

We now describe the extension step used in our dynamic programming scheme.
It is adapted from the final enumeration step of
[

]

Given a pure relative pair
(M,L)
with

a weak pseudomanifold with boundary, the algorithm outputs all pure relative pairs
(M,K)
such that

and
K
is a weak pseudomanifold.
Let
\chi^{L}\in\{0,1\}^{F}
denote the facet indicator vector of the facets of

We say that a ridge at index

is

if
(A(M)\chi^{L})_{r}=1
.
It is called

if
(A(M)\chi^{L})_{r}=2
.
Here

is computed over
\mathbb{Z}
.

We now describe all steps of Algorithm
4
.

Phase 1. Unit propagation.

We iteratively derive two sets of constraints on the facets of
M
that are not facets of
L
.
We maintain a vector
\chi^{+}\in\{0,1\}^{F}
of
mandatory
facets (initialized to
\chi^{L}
) and a vector
\chi^{-}\in\{0,1\}^{F}
of
forbidden
facets (initialized to
\mathbf{0}
), and repeat the following until no change occurs.
For each ridge
r
, let
s=|\{j\in A(M)[r]\mid\chi^{+}[j]=1\}|
be the number of mandatory facets already covering
r
, and let
\mathcal{F}_{r}=\{j\in A(M)[r]\mid\chi^{+}[j]=0,\chi^{-}[j]=0\}
be the set of remaining free facets incident to
r
.

•

If
s>2
, the current partial solution is infeasible and we return
\emptyset
.

•

If
s=2
, every free facet incident to
r
must be forbidden: set
\chi^{-}[j]\leftarrow 1
for all
j\in\mathcal{F}_{r}
.

•

If
s=1
and
|\mathcal{F}_{r}|=0
, the current partial solution is infeasible and we return
\emptyset
.

•

If
s=1
and
|\mathcal{F}_{r}|=1
, the unique free facet is forced: set
\chi^{+}[\mathcal{F}_{r}[1]]\leftarrow 1
.

•

If
s=0
and
|\mathcal{F}_{r}|=1
, adding
\mathcal{F}_{r}[1]
would leave
r
incomplete with no further option; set
\chi^{-}[\mathcal{F}_{r}[1]]\leftarrow 1
.

Phase 2. Constrained column echelon form.

Let
B\in\{0,1\}^{F\times d}
be a matrix whose columns form a mod
2
basis of
\ker_{2}(A(M))
.
We compute a reduced column echelon form
\hat{B}[1],\ldots,\hat{B}[d]
of
B
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with pivots
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}
.
The pivot search first uses rows in
\supp(\chi^{+})
, then rows in
\supp(\chi^{-})
, and finally the remaining rows.
This yields a partition of the column indices into

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{+}

\displaystyle=\{i\in[d]\mid p_{i}\in\supp(\chi^{+})\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{-}

\displaystyle=\{i\in[d]\mid p_{i}\in\supp(\chi^{-})\},\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\circ}

\displaystyle=[d]\setminus(\mathcal{I}^{+}\cup\mathcal{I}^{-}).

Because all constrained rows are processed before unconstrained rows, any constrained row that is not selected as a pivot has zero entries in all remaining free generators.
Hence its value is already determined once the coefficients corresponding to constrained pivot rows are fixed.

Phase 3. Initialization from forced basis vectors.

Since
\hat{B}
restricted to the pivot rows is the identity matrix, the generator
\hat{B}[i]
is the unique way of including the mandatory facet
p_{i}
for
i\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
, and must be excluded to avoid the forbidden facet
p_{i}
for
i\in\mathcal{I}^{-}
.
We therefore initialize

and discard the generators indexed by
\mathcal{I}^{-}
entirely, reducing the search space by a factor of
2^{|\mathcal{I}^{+}|+|\mathcal{I}^{-}|}
.
We then verify that
y
is consistent: if any mandatory facet is absent from
\supp(y)
, or any forbidden facet belongs to
\supp(y)
, we return
\emptyset
.
Indeed, for every
y^{\prime}
obtained in the Gray code enumeration, the free generators indexed by

have zero entries on all constrained rows,
that is, on
\supp(\chi^{+})\cup\supp(\chi^{-})
.
Hence

y^{\prime}|_{\supp(\chi^{+})\cup\supp(\chi^{-})}=y|_{\supp(\chi^{+})\cup\supp(\chi^{-})}.

Consequently

satisfies two conditions

\supp(\chi^{+})\subset\supp(y^{\prime})\quad\text{ and }\quad\supp(y^{\prime})\cap\supp(\chi^{-})=\varnothing

if and only if
y
satisfies the same two conditions.

Phase 4. Gray code enumeration.

It remains to enumerate all extensions of

by subsets of the free generators
\{\hat{B}[i]\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}^{\circ}}

y\oplus\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{I}^{\circ}}x_{i}\hat{B}[i],\qquad x_{i}\in\{0,1\},

As previously, rather than checking each of the

\rho[r]=\sum_{j\in A(M)[r]}y[j]
incrementally.
All
2^{|\mathcal{I}^{\circ}|}
candidates, including the initial one

After testing
y

\hat{B}[\beta]

Each flip updates
\rho

n|\supp(\hat{B}[\beta])|\leq nF

A candidate

Thus, recalling that

Proposition 3.8
.

Algorithm

Remark 4.3

In the implementation,

Toric-colorability for the case

For each mod

The search was performed by an exact finite backtracking algorithm.
First, we enumerated all unordered

.

Whenever all vertices of a facet had been assigned, the corresponding set of five columns was required to be one of the admissible subsets.
More generally, a partial assignment was discarded as soon as some partially assigned facet could not be extended to an admissible subset.

0/1

Every complex among the
