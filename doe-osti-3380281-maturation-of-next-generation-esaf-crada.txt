 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 Assessment of current and potential future eSAF production pathways (Power to Liquids,
 eMethanol to Jet, etc.) against current incumbents (HEFA, Alcohol to Jet) evaluating the overall
 costs of production, scalability of process, market demand, and policy/ regulatory/ standards for
 flight.

 Task 1: Project management and workshop sessions between NLR and Equinor throughout
 project duration to align on pathways and assessment assumptions, decision-making processes,
 approaches, time constraints to inform priorities and timing for further evaluation, and
 knowledge transfer session(s) at the conclusion of the work.

 • Workshops:
 o Fall 2024: Anticipate in person workshop on NLR campus or US-based
 o Winter/Early Spring 2025: Anticipate in person workshop in Norway
 • Project Management:
 o Agreed upon meeting frequency, meeting participants, role responsibilities and
 expected timeline at project initiation for continuous progress monitoring and

 At the onset of the project, it was targeted to hold two in-person workshops for the NLR and
 Equinor teams to meet and discuss e-SAF analyses. The first workshop was held shortly after
 project kickoff in November 2024 at Equinor’s Houston facilities. The workshop was held over
 one and half days and was intended to cover NLR’s proposed scope of work. During the
 workshop, NLR presented the findings of their initial market landscape analysis for reaching
 SAF and worked with Equinor partners to focus scope for the following detailed pathway
 analyses (Task 3). Following this November workshop, smaller virtual project coordination
 meetings were held virtually between NLR and Equinor with a frequency of approximately 1-2 a
 month. During these calls each party would provide updates and align on key assumptions.

 The final workshop was intended to be held in person at Equinor headquarters in Norway in May
 of 2025. However, Equinor made a request mid-way through the project to convert this in-
 person workshop into a virtual meeting. In place of a two-day end of project workshop, we
 instead held four one-to-two-hour meetings to cover the final presentation of results across topics
 of: executive summary reporting, techno-economic analysis, life-cycle analysis, and Monte Carlo
 uncertainty analysis.

 Task 2: Market landscape analysis of different emerging eSAF technologies (US DoD TRL 3+)
 and incumbent solutions to inform and determine priorities for further assessment, if appropriate.

 • Create a matrix of alternative solutions with a range of technologies and list of
 technology providers, to be mutually determined

 NLR Task 2 Deliverable: A preliminary technology assessment of emerging e-SAF pathways
 (TRL 3+) to Equinor

 The first analysis deliverable to be provided by NLR was a market landscape analysis for
 different emerging pathways to reach e-SAF. This deliverable was provided as an 80-slide
 PowerPoint presentation during the November 2024 workshop meeting. This landscape analysis
 covered seven key topic areas: (1) an introduction and baselining of the definition of ‘e-SAF’, (2)
 the scope of our study, (3) pathways to directly produce e-SAF from CO2, (4) pathways to
 produce syngas, (5) pathways to produce alcohols, (6) pathways to upgrade syngas/alcohols to
 eSAF, and (7) NLR’s perspective on e-SAF and the discussed pathways.

 A key component of our market landscape analysis, shown below, was to highlight the relative
 technical maturity of each pathway (e.g., TRL) as well as key market players in each space. This
 information was intended to both provide opportunities in business development and potential
 partnerships and also provide a sense of what could be accomplished near and longer term.

 Task 3: Detailed Techno-economic assessment and model development for five selected e-SAF
 pathways

 • Agreed upon e-SAF pathways for assessment include:
 o Co-electrolysis > syngas > MeOH > MTJ e-SAF
 o Lydian-based rWGS > syngas > MeOH > MTJ e-SAF
 o Photocatalytic rWGS > syngas > MeOH > MTJ e-SAF
 o Direct electrochemical MeOH > MTJ e-SAF
 o Direct hydrogenation of CO2 to e-SAF
 • Agreed upon analysis metrics include:
 o Estimated total cost for each pathway for 2025, 2030, and 2040;
 o Single variable sensitivity analyses for 2040 scenario
 o Monte Carlo analyses of average performance required to achieve 2040 cost
 targets
 o Cost assessment of switching to e-SAF vs. continuation of jet fuel utilization with
 o DAC + carbon credits

 NLR Task 3 Deliverable: A process model, including estimated total energy demand, estimated
 cost, and sensitivity plots showing key 2040 cost drivers for the above five pathways.

 The core deliverable of the NLR e-SAF assessment was the development of five detailed Aspen
 Plus chemical process models for the production of e-SAF from CO2. The five pathways were
 selected based on Equinor feedback and involved electrochemistry, alcohol upgrading, and
 thermocatalysis. From the developed process models, a techno-economic analysis was
 performed to calculate the approximate selling price of the e-SAF produced in each pathway.
 Further, for each pathway, various scenarios were studied to provide price targets for near-term
 performance, medium-term performance, and longer-term performance. A high-level summary
 of the results is shown below.

 TEA results showed considerable disparity across the five pathways over the nearest term (2025)
 due largely to the variance in technology readiness level where the estimated SAF selling price
 ranged between $5,930 - $23,370/tonne. However, in the most forward-looking scenarios which
 incorporated performance estimates for the year 2040, that disparity closed and all five pathways
 trended towards similar e-SAF price performance across a narrower range of ~$3,170 -
 $6,650/tonne. Ultimately when compared to current market prices of fossil jet fuel (est.
 $700/tonne), the data revealed that competing directly with conventional fossil-fuel based jet fuel
 will be challenging for e-SAF and may require some policy drivers to promote meaningful
 market adoption.

 Task 4: A lifecycle assessment will be performed with an output of the estimated eSAF carbon
 intensity and water usage for each of the pathways selected in Task 3.

 NLR Task 4 Deliverable: Life cycle emissions assessment of each pathway identified in Task 3,
 for current, 2030, and 2040 emissions

 Per the recommendations of Equinor, the life cycle assessment focused solely on quantifying the
 final carbon intensity score of the produced e-SAF for each pathway in terms of kg CO2/kg
 eSAF. While the LCA considered all major mass and energy flows across the system boundary,
 the primary variable used to assess the carbon intensity score was the source of electricity. For
 the life cycle assessment we considered eight possible sources of electricity, four from Norway
 and four from the United States. Specifically, across Norway we looked at the Norwegian
 electricity grid, onshore wind, offshore, and hydroelectric power. In the United States we
 considered the US electric grid (ERCOT region), nuclear power (ERCOT), solar PV (WECC),
 and a generalized domestic blend of US renewables. The corresponding emissions factors used
 for each electricity source are shown in the Table below. It is worth stressing that although each
 of these sources (excluding US/Norwegian Grid) is considered ‘renewable’, the corresponding
 emissions factors are different in each case. This reflects the different inherent ‘embodied
 emissions’ (e.g., mining, construction, transportation, end of life disposal) associated with the
 life-cycle of generating a kWh of electricity from each pathway.

 5

 Consequently, due to these differences in embodied emissions and emissions factors across the
 different sources of electricity, we show that the carbon intensity can vary widely from source to
 source and just because a process uses ‘renewable electricity’ does not necessarily mean that the
 end fuel can be considered as ‘sustainable aviation fuel’. Specifically, in the five figures below,
 we label via horizontal dashed lines the maximum allowable carbon intensity to be consider as
 ‘sustainable aviation fuel’ for three regions: the UK, the US, and the EU. The calculated carbon
 intensity results shown by the vertical bars reveal that, depending on region, not all pathways
 have a sufficient reduction in carbon intensity relative to conventional jet fuel to meet the
 regulatory definition of SAF. For most pathways, very low carbon intensity electricity (e.g.,
 hydro, nuclear, wind) is required to meet the definition of SAF across all three regions. Note: the
 clusters of three vertical bars correspond to varying energy intensity assumptions for consumed
 hydrogen of 55, 51, and 46 kWh/kg.
