Mechanical properties of the nucleon in the chiral confining model I - formal developments
Guy Chanfray 1 , Hubert Hansen 1 , Bikram Keshari Pradhan 1

Abstract
We discuss the issue of the mechanical stability of the nucleon within a class of models in which massive constituent quarks are subject to a confining potential and are coupled to a surrounding pion cloud enveloping the quark core. The nucleon trial states (either localized factorized wave functions or momentum-projected states) are determined by imposing the von Laue stability condition. This article is primarily devoted to the formal aspects related to the detailed expressions for the total energy (mass), the average pressure, the energy density and the pressure distribution inside the nucleon. It will be accompanied by a complementary article addressing the evolution of nucleon properties with density, associated with the restoration of chiral symmetry.



I
Introduction: trial nucleon states

The aim of this paper is to study the stability of the nucleon in a class of chiral models in which the nucleon consists of a quark core generated by a string-like confining interaction, surrounded by a pion cloud coupled to the quarks via the standard pseudo-vector coupling. The effective quark mass is supposed to be generated by an underlying chiral symmetry-breaking mechanism, such as the one based on the Nambu-Jona-Lasinio (NJL) model. Localized nucleonic state

(12)

I.0.2
Momentum projected nucleon state

A significant source of improvement in the description of nucleon properties is to replace the localized nucleon state, not possessing a well-defined total 3-momentum by a momentum projected nucleon state with well defined momentum
P
, denoted by
|N(\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N})\rangle
, or sometimes for short by
|\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}\rangle
in the formal developments. As in Ref.
Gastao
, this amounts to enriching the factorized Fock-space amplitude by the appropriate projection according to:

\Phi_{\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}}\left({\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3}\right)=\frac{1}{V^{1/2}}N_{\bf{P}_{N}}\Phi\left({\bf p}_{1}\right)\,\Phi\left({\bf p}_{2}\right)\,\Phi\left({\bf p}_{3}\right)\,(2\pi)^{3}\,\delta\left(\bf{P}_{N}-\bf{p}_{1}-\bf{p}_{2}-\bf{p}_{3}\right).

(27)

N_{\bf{P}_{N}}
is a normalization factor, whose explicit expression is

N_{\bf{P}_{N}}=\left(\frac{3}{4\pi b^{2}}\right)^{3/4}\,exp\left(\frac{P^{2}_{N}\,b^{2}}{6}\right),

when using the Gaussian trial ansatz of Eq. (
26
). In the notation
\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}
, the subscript
N
indicates that the state is normalized and carries momentum
P
. Hence, it is implicitly understood that
\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}\equiv\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}
.

In this case, the nucleon wave function reads:

\displaystyle\left(\Psi_{\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}}\right)\left(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{1},\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{2},\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{3}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle 0\right|q(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{1})q(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{2})q(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{3})\left|N(\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N_{\bf{P}_{N}}}{\sqrt{V}}\int d^{3}X\,e^{i\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$X$\unboldmath}}\,\left(\psi\right)(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{1}-\hbox{\boldmath$X$\unboldmath})\left(\psi\right)(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{2}-\hbox{\boldmath$X$\unboldmath})\left(\psi\right)(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{3}-\hbox{\boldmath$X$\unboldmath})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}e^{i\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath}}\,\left(\tilde{\Psi}_{int}\right)(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{1}-\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath},\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{2}-\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath},\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{3}-\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath}\,;\,\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N})

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\Psi}_{int}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{\bf{P}_{N}}\,\int d^{3}Y\,e^{i\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$Y$\unboldmath}}\,\left(\psi\right)(\hbox{\boldmath$x$\unboldmath}_{1}-\hbox{\boldmath$Y$\unboldmath})\left(\psi\right)(\hbox{\boldmath$x$\unboldmath}_{2}-\hbox{\boldmath$Y$\unboldmath})\left(\psi\right)(\hbox{\boldmath$x$\unboldmath}_{3}-\hbox{\boldmath$Y$\unboldmath}).

(28)

Here
\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath}=(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{1}+\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{2}+\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{3})/3
is the position of the center of mass of the three-quark system and
\hbox{\boldmath$x$\unboldmath}_{1}=\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{1}-\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath},\,\hbox{\boldmath$x$\unboldmath}_{2}=\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{2}-\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath},\,\hbox{\boldmath$x$\unboldmath}_{3}=\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{3}-\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath}
represent the position of each of the quarks relative to their center of mass. These relative positions only depend on the Jacobi coordinates
\hbox{\boldmath$\rho$\unboldmath}=\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{1}-\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{2}
and
\hbox{\boldmath$\lambda$\unboldmath}=\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{3}-(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{1}+\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{2})/2
. One sees that this wave function is completely delocalized and appears as the product of a plane wave associated with the total momentum
P
carried by the center of mass and an intrinsic wave function in which the internal structure of the nucleon is encoded. This wave function is translationally invariant in the sense that it consists of a CM plane wave multiplied by an intrinsic wave function that does not depend on the CM position
R
. In the following, we will use the following notation:

|N:\hbox{\boldmath$0$\unboldmath}_{P}\rangle\,\equiv\,|N(\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}=0)\rangle.

(29)

Also note that the discrete
\left|N(\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N})\right\rangle
states satisfying
\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}^{\prime}_{N}|\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}\right\rangle=\delta_{\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}^{\prime}_{N},\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}}
are related to the usual momentum eigenstates of non-relativistic quantum mechanics which obey
\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}^{\prime}|\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}^{\prime}-\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath})
, through the relation
\left|\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}\right\rangle=\sqrt{V}\left|\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}\right\rangle
.

I.0.3
Local quark densities

Let us consider the local densities,
t_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\equiv t_{\Gamma}(x=(0,\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}))
(i.e., implicitly taken at
t=0
in this discussion), associated with local density operators,
\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})=\bar{q}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\,\Gamma\,q(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})
. Examples include the quark kinetic energy density operator,
\mathcal{H}_{QK}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})=\bar{q}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\,(-i\hbox{\boldmath$\gamma$\unboldmath}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\nabla$\unboldmath}_{\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}})\,q(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})
, the scalar density operator,
\hat{\rho}_{S}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})=\bar{q}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\,q(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})
, or the quark number density operator,
\hat{\rho}_{B}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})=\bar{q}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\,\gamma_{0}\,q(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})
.

In the case of a localized nucleon state (see
I.0.1
), a very natural definition of these densities is obtained by just taking the matrix element of the local density operator, i.e,

t_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})=\left\langle 0_{X}\right|\,\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\,\left|0_{X}\right\rangle=3\,\int d^{3}r\,(\bar{\psi})(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\,\Gamma\,(\psi)(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}).

(30)

In the case of a pure momentum eigenstate (see
I.0.2
), this definition makes no physical sense, as translational invariance leads to a uniform density. Hence, one has to find a way to localize the quantum system. Following Wigner
Wigner
, as clearly summarized in
Lorce2019
, a sensitive way consists in replacing the pure momentum eigenstate
\left|\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}\right\rangle
with a localized nucleonic system with average momentum
\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}=\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}
and average position
\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath}_{N}=\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath}
described by the phase space density operator:

\hat{\rho}_{\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath},\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}}=\int\frac{Vd^{3}\Delta}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,e^{-i\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath}}\,\left|\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}-\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}}{2}\right\rangle\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}+\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}}{2}\right|.

(31)

The local density
t_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})
is thus assumed to be given by the expectation value of the operator
\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})
at the spatial point
r
in the statistical ensemble described by the above density operator:

\displaystyle t_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath};\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath};\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Tr\left[\hat{\rho}_{\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath},\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}}\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\right]=\int\frac{Vd^{3}\Delta}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{-i\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath}}\,\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}+\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}}{2}\right|\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\left|\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}-\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}}{2}\right\rangle

(32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\Delta}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{-i\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath}}\,\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}+\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}}{2}\right|\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\left|\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}-\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}}{2}\right\rangle\equiv\left[\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\right]_{WT}(\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath},\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}).

Hence, the local density appears as the Wigner transform of the local density operator taken at the phase-space point
(\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath},\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath})
corresponding to the average position and momentum of the nucleonic system. As a side remark, this notion of the Wigner transform is widely used in the semi-classical approaches in nuclear physics
ChanfrayPRA
;
Schuck89
.
An important consistency check is that, thanks to translational invariance, the volume integral of the density thus defined correctly reproduces the value of the associated “charge” (i.e., the spatially integrated quantity) which is itself well-defined for the pure momentum state,

\int d^{3}r\,t_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath};\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath};\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath})=V\,\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}\right|\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(0)\left|\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}\right\rangle=\int d^{3}r\,\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}\right|\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\left|\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}\right\rangle,

(33)

which does not depend on
R
as expected. Since the physics should not depend on
R
, one could always localize the nucleonic system around
\hbox{\boldmath$R$\unboldmath}=0
. For a nucleon at rest, i.e.,
\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}=0
, the matrix element of the local density operator in the Wigner sense is given by:

\displaystyle\left\langle 0_{P}\right|\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\left|0_{P}\right\rangle_{W}\equiv t_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})=\int\frac{Vd^{3}\Delta}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\right|\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\left|-\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\right\rangle=\int\frac{Vd^{3}\Delta}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,e^{-i\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}}\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\right|\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(0)\left|-\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\right\rangle

(34)

The matrix element of the local density operator,
\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\right|\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(0)\left|-\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\right\rangle
, evaluated between momentum states differing by
\Delta
, may be promoted to the status of a physical observable. Indeed, it can be interpreted as a Sachs form factor
RGS
expressed in the Breit frame (in analogy to the electromagnetic form factors

Xiong
) that may be experimentally accessed through scattering processes involving an external probe coupled to the nucleon via the local density operator
\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})
. This is indeed the case for the components of the energy momentum tensor (EMT), whose matrix elements,
\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\right|\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}(0)\left|-\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\right\rangle
, can be obtained, via a modeling of the gravitational form factors, directly from the generalized parton distributions (GPDs)
Polyakov
;
Lorce2019
, which are accessible in several exclusive processes, such as deeply virtual Compton scattering
DVCS
and meson production
MESPROD
. The components of the associated densities that constitute the static EMT tensor can thus be extracted from the data. In our opinion, this observability certainly reinforces the physical status of
\varepsilon(r)=t^{00}(r)
interpreted as a genuine energy density and
p(r)=t^{ii}(r)/3
interpreted as a genuine mechanical pressure distribution inside the nucleon.

To conclude this discussion relative to the physical nucleon states with well defined 3-momentum, let us note that all the contributions to the Hamiltonian matrix elements (and, more generally, to the energy–momentum tensor) that originate from a translationally invariant Lagrangian do not require the introduction of local (Wigner) densities. However—and this is a crucial point—the confining part of the interaction,
H_{C}=\int d^{3}r\,\mathcal{H}_{C}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})=\int d^{3}r\,W_{C}(r)q^{\dagger}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})q(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})
, which breaks translational invariance, does require a proper definition of the quark number distribution. In reality, the confining interaction experienced by a quark depends on its distance from the center of force, which, in the CCM model, coincides with the position of the string junction, conventionally taken to be at the origin. We therefore assume that the position of the string junction coincides with the mean position of the quasi-localized nucleonic system. Hence, the matrix element in the Wigner sense of the confining interaction is:

\displaystyle\left\langle 0_{P}\right|H_{C}\left|0_{P}\right\rangle_{W}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}r\,\left\langle 0_{P}\right|\mathcal{H}_{C}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\left|0_{P}\right\rangle_{W}=\int d^{3}r\,W_{C}(r)\int\frac{Vd^{3}\Delta}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\right|q^{\dagger}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})q(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\left|-\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\right\rangle

(35)

\displaystyle=

\Phi\left({\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2},{\bf p}_{3}\right)
, solution of the HF Dirac equation. However, in practice, and for the purposes of this paper and the companion one, it will serve as an alternative to the variational principle used in non-relativistic quantum mechanics. In practice, the three-quark Fock-space amplitude will always depend on the Gaussian single-quark Fock-space amplitude :

\Phi(\hbox{\boldmath$p$\unboldmath})=R_{0}(p)=(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}\,\left(\frac{b^{2}}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}\,e^{-b^{2}\,p^{2}/2}.

(82)

Throughout the remainder of this paper, the value of the size parameter
b
will thus be fixed by requiring that the von Laue stability condition is satisfied, and the determination of the “best value” of the quark core size parameter
b
is the central issue of the paper.

IV
Static EMT tensor: Energy density and pressure distribution

IV.1
The static EMT tensor

The static EMT tensor
t^{\mu\nu}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})
is defined from the matrix element evaluated in the Breit frame of the EMT tensor according to
Polyakovor
:

\displaystyle t^{\mu\nu}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\Delta}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,e^{-i\,\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}}\,\left\langle\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}}{2}\left|\,\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}(0,\hbox{\boldmath$0$\unboldmath})\,\right|-\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}}{2}\right\rangle=\int\frac{Vd^{3}\Delta}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left\langle\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}}{2}\left|\,\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}(0,\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\,\right|-\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}}{2}\right\rangle.

(83)

To account for the effect of confining pressure, we also introduce a Wigner density of the “confining pressure potential” that may be viewed as a “static confining pressure”, defined as follows:

\displaystyle p_{C}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\Delta}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,e^{-i\,\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}}\,\left\langle\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}}{2}\left|\,\hat{P}_{C}(0,\hbox{\boldmath$0$\unboldmath})\,\right|-\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}}{2}\right\rangle=\int\frac{Vd^{3}\Delta}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left\langle\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}}{2}\left|\,\hat{P}_{C}(0,\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\,\right|-\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}}{2}\right\rangle.

(84)

At variance with the definition given in
Goeke
;
Polyakov
;
Lorce2019
;
Lorce2021
, where a covariant normalization is used, the momentum states here are normalized either as in non relativistic quantum mechanics,
\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}^{\prime}|\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}^{\prime}-\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath})
, or to one in a large box of volume
V
, (
\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}^{\prime}_{N}|\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}\right\rangle=\delta_{\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}^{\prime}_{N},\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}})
. In the following, we will omit the reference to the arbitrary time
t=0
, since in the Breit frame the two nucleon states,
|-\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}/2\rangle
and
|\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}/2\rangle
, have the same energy. Owing to translational invariance, the matrix element of the EMT component can be identically expressed as:

\left\langle\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}}{2}\left|\,\hat{T}_{Y}(0,\hbox{\boldmath$0$\unboldmath})\,\right|-\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}}{2}\right\rangle=\int\frac{d^{3}y}{V}\,e^{i\,\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$y$\unboldmath}}\,\left\langle\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}}{2}\left|\,\hat{T}_{Y}(o,\hbox{\boldmath$y$\unboldmath})\,\right|-\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}}{2}\right\rangle.

(85)

By construction, the volume integral of the above Wigner density reproduce the expectation value of the corresponding component of the EMT tensor:

\int d^{3}r\,t^{\mu\nu}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})=\int d^{3}y\,\langle 0_{P}|\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}(0,\hbox{\boldmath$y$\unboldmath})|0_{P}\rangle.

(86)

V.2
Pure confining potential

Let us first consider the case in which the pion cloud is ignored, that is, the case in which the quarks experience only the confining potential. The Hartree-Fock equation reduces to:

\frac{\delta\left(\langle|N(\Phi)|H|N(\Phi)\rangle\,-\,3\epsilon_{0}\,\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,|\left|\Phi({\bf p})\right|^{2}\right)}{\delta\Phi^{*}({\bf p}})=0.

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2F_{\pi}}\sum_{\hbox{\boldmath$k$\unboldmath}}\,\frac{k}{\sqrt{2\,\omega_{k}V}}i\,\lim_{\,t^{\prime}\to t^{+}}\left[-i\left\langle N:\hbox{\boldmath$0$\unboldmath}_{X}\right|\mathcal{T}\left((b_{\hbox{\boldmath$k$\unboldmath}a}\,+\,b^{\dagger}_{-\hbox{\boldmath$k$\unboldmath}a})(t),\tilde{S}_{\hbox{\boldmath$k$\unboldmath}a}(t^{\prime})\right)\left|N:\hbox{\boldmath$0$\unboldmath}_{X}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle 3\,\left(\frac{1}{F_{\pi}}\right)^{2}\,\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{k^{2}}{2\omega_{k}}\,\int\frac{id\omega}{2\pi}\,e^{i\omega\eta^{+}}\,\frac{2\omega_{k}\,\Pi_{0}(k,\omega)}{\omega^{2}-\omega_{k}^{2}+i\eta}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{2}\,\left({1\over 2F_{\pi}}\right)^{2}\int{d^{3}k\over(2\pi)^{3}}\,k^{2}\,\Gamma^{2}(k)\,\bigg[\frac{1}{\omega_{k}}\frac{1}{\omega_{k}+\epsilon_{N{\bf k}}}+\frac{32}{25}\,\frac{1}{\omega_{k}}\frac{1}{\omega_{k}+\epsilon_{\Delta{\bf k}}}\bigg].

V.4
nucleon mass

Before possible center of mass correction, the total energy of the nucleon is:

E_{0}=E_{Q}\,+\,E_{\pi}=E_{QK}\,+E_{QM}\,+\,E_{C}\,+\,\frac{1}{2}E_{\pi Q}.

(126)

Although the nucleon state, associated with a factorized wave function as a product of three quark orbitals (Eq.(
14
)), is not a physical state with fixed three-momentum, we will nonetheless attempt to model the (physical) nucleon using a localized state
|N:\hbox{\boldmath$0$\unboldmath}_{X}\rangle
with inclusion of the pion cloud. In that respect, a rough estimate of the nucleon mass can be obtained by subtracting the spurious CM motion according to:

M_{N}=\sqrt{E_{0}^{2}-\langle P^{2}\rangle}=\sqrt{E_{0}^{2}-\frac{9}{2b^{2}}}.

(127)

For this first numerical estimate, we use the NJLSet1 set of parameters
Universe2025
, namely
M_{q}=356.7\,\rm{MeV}
,
M_{\pi}=140\,\rm{MeV}
,
F_{\pi}=91.7\,\rm{MeV}
and the confining potential inspired from the FCM framework (Eq. (
7
)) with
\sigma=0.18\,\rm{GeV}^{2}
and
T_{g}=0.283\,\rm{fm}

Universe2025
.
The first conclusion that can be drawn from this study is that the finite size effect of the pion is absolutely required to ensure the mechanical stability of the nucleon, i.e., to satisfy the stability condition:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\,E_{QK}\,-\,\frac{1}{3}\,P_{C}\,+\,\frac{1}{3}\,E_{\pi Q}\,+\,E_{\pi c}\,-\,\frac{1}{3}\,E_{\pi K}\,-\,E_{\pi M}=0.

(128)

The value of the size parameter
c_{\pi}
, characterizing the extension of the non-local coupling of quarks to the pion field, must be determined as the outcome of a compromise. It must be large enough to prevent the quark core bag from collapsing. However,
c_{\pi}

should not be too large, to both ensure a sufficiently strong pionic attraction, resulting in a nucleon mass that is not too large, and to maintain a reasonably small quark core rms, meaning in practice not exceeding 0.5 fm. Taking
c_{\pi}=0.8
(i.e., the pion size parameter is
\rho_{\pi}=0.30\,\rm{fm}
), we find that the von Laue condition is satisfied for a size parameter
b_{virial}=0.345\,\rm{fm}
, leading to
r_{B}=0.495\,\rm{fm}
. The total energy before CM correction is
E_{0}=1.616\,\rm{GeV}
leading to an estimated nucleon mass is
E_{0}=\sqrt{E_{0}^{2}-\langle P^{2}\rangle}=1.069\,\rm{GeV}
. This value is reasonably close to the physical nucleon mass, especially considering that the perturbative gluon-exchange contribution, not considered here, is expected to lower the mass by about
-100\,\rm{MeV}

Universe2025
. However, an important drawback of these results is the too small value of the axial coupling constant,
g_{A}=1.13
, which is linked to the relatively small value of the size parameter
b=0.345\,\rm{fm}
. This is most likely due to too large negative pressure from the confining force and the pion cloud, which must be counterbalanced by a large Fermi pressure, leading to a small value of the size parameter
b
. We will see, however, in the next section, how this can be improved by considering a physical state,
|N:\hbox{\boldmath$0$\unboldmath}_{P}\rangle
for the nucleon with a well-defined momentum.

V.5
Energy density and pressure distribution in the static CCM nucleonic bag

V.5.1
Quark core contribution to the energy density and pressure of the static CCM nucleonic bag

The quark operators

,

\displaystyle t_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\Delta\,d^{3}y}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,e^{-i\,(\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}\cdot(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}-\hbox{\boldmath$y$\unboldmath})}\,\left\langle\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}}{2}\left|\,\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$y$\unboldmath})\,\right|-\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}}{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\Delta\,d^{3}y}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\int\frac{d^{3}X_{N}}{V}\,e^{-i\,(\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}\cdot(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}-\hbox{\boldmath$y$\unboldmath})}\,e^{-i\,\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$X$\unboldmath}_{N}}\,\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$X$\unboldmath}_{N}\left|\,\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$y$\unboldmath})\,\right|\hbox{\boldmath$X$\unboldmath}_{N}\right\rangle

(130)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}X_{N}}{V}\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$X$\unboldmath}_{N}\left|\,\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}+\hbox{\boldmath$X_{N}$\unboldmath})\,\right|\hbox{\boldmath$X$\unboldmath}_{N}\right\rangle\equiv\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$0$\unboldmath}_{X}\left|\,\hat{T}_{\Gamma}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\,\right|\hbox{\boldmath$0$\unboldmath}_{X}\right\rangle.

(131)

We thus see that the result obtained using this projection procedure leads to the intuitive outcome anticipated at the beginning of Section

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{QK}(r)/3

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\psi\right)^{\dagger}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})[-i\hbox{\boldmath$\alpha$\unboldmath}\cdot\vec{\nabla}_{\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}}]\,\left(\psi\right)(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})=u(r)v^{\prime}(r)-u^{\prime}(r)v(r)\,+\,\frac{2u(r)v(r)}{r}\,

V.5.2
Pion cloud contribution to the energy density and pressure of the “static CCM nucleonic bag”

The calculation of the pion cloud contribution to the static EMT tensor also necessitates a projection procedure. To this end, let us introduce the following Green’s function:

G_{a}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath},t\,;\,\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}^{\prime},t^{\prime})=\sum_{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}}\,\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\left|\,-i\,\mathcal{T}\left(\Phi_{a}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath},t)\,,\,\Phi_{a}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\,\right)\right|-\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\right\rangle.

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\frac{1}{2F_{\pi}}\right)^{2}\sum_{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}}\,\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\left|\,-i\,\mathcal{T}\left(\hbox{\boldmath$\nabla$\unboldmath}_{\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}}\cdot\tilde{\hbox{\boldmath$S$\unboldmath}}^{\dagger}_{a}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath},t)\,,\,\hbox{\boldmath$\nabla$\unboldmath}_{\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}^{\prime}}\cdot\tilde{\hbox{\boldmath$S$\unboldmath}}_{a}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right)\right|-\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\right\rangle.

\displaystyle G_{a}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath},t\,;\,\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}^{\prime},t^{\prime})=\left(\frac{1}{2F_{\pi}}\right)^{2}\int dt_{1}\,d^{3}r_{1}\,dt^{\prime}_{1}\,d^{3}r^{\prime}_{1},\,\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,e^{-i\omega(t-t_{1})}\,e^{i\hbox{\boldmath$k$\unboldmath}\cdot(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}-\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{1})}

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}\int\frac{d^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,e^{i\omega^{\prime}(t^{\prime}-t^{\prime}_{1})}e^{-i\hbox{\boldmath$k$\unboldmath}^{\prime}\cdot(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}^{\prime}-\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}^{\prime}_{1})}\frac{\sum_{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}}\langle\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2|-i\,\mathcal{T}\left(\hbox{\boldmath$\nabla$\unboldmath}_{\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{1}}\cdot\tilde{\hbox{\boldmath$S$\unboldmath}}^{\dagger}_{a}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{1},t_{1})\,,\,\hbox{\boldmath$\nabla$\unboldmath}_{\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{1}^{\prime}}\cdot\tilde{\hbox{\boldmath$S$\unboldmath}}_{a}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}_{1}^{\prime},t_{1}^{\prime})\right)|-\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\rangle}{(\omega^{2}-\omega_{k}^{2}+i\eta)\,({\omega^{\prime}}^{2}-{\omega^{\prime}}_{k}^{2}+i\eta)}.

\displaystyle\Pi_{0D}(\hbox{\boldmath$k$\unboldmath},\hbox{\boldmath$q$\unboldmath};t_{1},t_{1}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\sum_{\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}}\,\langle\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2|\,-i\,\mathcal{T}\left(\tilde{S}^{\dagger}_{\hbox{\boldmath$k$\unboldmath}}(t_{1})\,,\,\tilde{S}_{\hbox{\boldmath$q$\unboldmath}}(t^{\prime}_{1})\right)|-\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\rangle=\int\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\,e^{-i\nu(t_{1}-t^{\prime}_{1})}\,\Pi_{0D}(\hbox{\boldmath$k$\unboldmath},\hbox{\boldmath$q$\unboldmath};\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n,\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}}\left(\frac{\langle\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2|\,\tilde{S}^{\dagger}_{\hbox{\boldmath$k$\unboldmath}}\,|n\rangle\,\langle n|\,\tilde{S}_{\hbox{\boldmath$q$\unboldmath}}\,|-\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\rangle}{\nu-E_{n}+i\eta}\,-\,\frac{\langle\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2|\,\tilde{S}_{\hbox{\boldmath$q$\unboldmath}}\,|n\rangle\,\langle n|\,\tilde{S}^{\dagger}_{\hbox{\boldmath$k$\unboldmath}}\,|-\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\rangle}{\nu+E_{n}-i\eta}\right).

\displaystyle G_{a}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath},t\,;\,\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath},t^{\prime})

Form factors

Let us consider again the matrix elements of a local quark bilinear operator (with

(157)

where again all the states are normalized to one in a large box of volume

. However, when the matrix element is calculated between the states
|N(\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}=\hbox{\boldmath$p$\unboldmath})\rangle
and
|N(\hbox{\boldmath$P$\unboldmath}_{N}=\hbox{\boldmath$p$\unboldmath}^{\prime})\rangle

\hbox{\boldmath$p$\unboldmath}^{\prime}
and

\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}=\hbox{\boldmath$p$\unboldmath}^{\prime}
-

\displaystyle\langle\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2|\,\bar{q}(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})\,\Gamma\,q(\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath})|-\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}_{N}/2\rangle

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V}\,e^{-i\,\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$r$\unboldmath}}\,e^{5b^{2}\Delta^{2}/24}\,\int d^{3}x\,e^{i\,\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$\unboldmath}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$x$\unboldmath}}\,\left(\bar{\psi}\right)_{E}({\bf{x}})\,\Gamma\,\left(\psi\right)_{E}({\bf{x}}),

VII
Summary and perspectives for the in-medium evolution of the nucleon properties

In this paper, we have considered a class of nucleon models incorporating both a confining potential and a standard pseudo-vector coupling of the constituent quarks to the pionic field. One important aspect and original goal of this paper is that the nucleonic wave function is constrained by the mechanical stability (von Laue) condition, which fixes the size of the quark core as resulting from a delicate balance between the positive Fermi pressure and the negative confining and pionic pressures.
