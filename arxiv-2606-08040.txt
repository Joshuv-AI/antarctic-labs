A sequence

In

This theorem demonstrates that some Hardy functions have “better” uniform distribution properties than others. The goal of this paper is to characterize Hardy functions by their quality of uniform distribution. More specifically, we develop a framework of weighted averages that allows us to interpolate between uniform distribution and well distribution, and we characterize precisely where each Hardy function lies in this framework. In particular, both Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
follow from Theorem
A
below. Before stating Theorem
A

Definition 1.4
.

Let

\infty

(1.3)

Let

Remark 1.6
.

If

as

This next lemma, which follows from Lemma

Lemma 1.7

Let

then

\lim_{U(N)-U(M)\to\infty}\frac{1}{U(N)-U(M)}\sum_{n=M}^{N}\Delta U(n)x_{n}=L.

We are now able to discuss our main theorem, which characterizes Hardy functions with respect to weighted uniform distribution.

Theorem A
.

Let
V
and
f
be functions which belong to the same Hardy field and assume that

(i)

For all
p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|f^{(\ell)}(x)-p(x)|^{1/\ell}}{\log(V(x))^{\prime}}=\infty.

(1.5)

(ii)

(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is u.d. mod 1 with respect to
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{V}
.

Taking

for all

is nonincreasing. To see how this portion of

it suffices to notice that when

is nonincreasing, equation (

which has

Lemma 2.1

Suppose that

Proof.

We begin by proving the special case

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{V}(e^{2\pi if(n)})=

\displaystyle e^{2\pi ic\log(V(N))}\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\Delta V(n)}{V(N)}e^{2\pi ic\log(V(n)/V(N))}.

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\Delta V(n)}{V(N)}e^{2\pi ic\log(V(n)/V(N))}=\int_{0}^{1}e^{2\pi ic\log(x)}dx=\int_{0}^{1}x^{2\pi ic}dx=\frac{1}{1+2\pi ic}.

(2.3)

Integrating both sides of equation (

1\prec f(x)\prec x
, since if
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)-p(x)}{x^{n}}\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{Q}
for some
p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
then we know that
(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is w.d. mod 1 by Theorem
1.2
. Similarly, for
q(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
the sequence
(q(n)\operatorname{mod}1)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is periodic and so replacing
f
by
f-q
does not affect uniform distribution. So it suffices to consider the case
q(x)=p(x)=0
.

We will show that if equation (
2.7
) holds then
(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is u.d. mod 1 with respect to
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{V}
and also that if (
2.7
) does not hold then
(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is not u.d. mod 1 with respect to
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{V}
. Since
f
and
V
belong to the same Hardy field, the limit
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(x)|}{\log(V(x))^{\prime}}
always exists in
(0,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}
. We have three cases to consider

•

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(x)|}{\log(V(x))^{\prime}}\in(0,\infty)
,

•

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(x)|}{\log(V(x))^{\prime}}=0
,

•

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(x)|}{\log(V(x))^{\prime}}=\infty
.

In the first case, we may apply Lemma
2.1
to see that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{V}e^{2\pi if(n)}=Ce^{2\pi if(N)}+o_{N\to\infty}(1)
, which in particular means that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{V}e^{2\pi if(n)}
does not exist. So the sequence
(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is not u.d. mod 1 with respect to
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{V}
in this case.

In the second case, pick a Hardy function
\tilde{V}
such that
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(x)|}{\log(\tilde{V}(x))^{\prime}}=1
, and note that
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\log(\tilde{V}(x))}{\log({V}(x))}=0
. By Lemma
2.1
we have that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{\tilde{V}}e^{2\pi if(n)}=Ce^{2\pi if(N)}+o_{N\to\infty}(1)
and in particular,
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{\tilde{V}}e^{2\pi if(n)}
does not exist. By Lemma
1.7
it follows that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{{V}}e^{2\pi if(n)}
also does not exist. So,
(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is not u.d. mod 1 with respect to
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{V}
in this case.

Lastly, for the third case, again pick a Hardy function
R
such that
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|f^{\prime}(x)|}{\log(R(x))^{\prime}}=1
, so that
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\log({V}(x))}{\log(R(x))}=0
. Pick any nonzero
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and note that
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|kf^{\prime}(x)|}{\log(R(x))^{\prime}}\in(0,\infty)
.
By Lemma
2.1
we have that

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{R}e^{2\pi ikf(n)}=Ce^{2\pi ikf(N)}+o_{N\to\infty}(1)

(2.8)

for some
C\in\mathbb{C}
with
|C|<1
. Define
Y_{N,0}=e^{2\pi ikf(N)}
and
Y_{N,m+1}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{R}_{n\leqslant N}Y_{n,m}
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\geqslant 0
. It follows from equation (
2.8
) that
Y_{N,m}=C^{m}e^{2\pi ikf(N)}+o_{N\to\infty}(1)
for
N\in\mathbb{N},m\geqslant 0
. So
\lim_{m\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}|Y_{N,m}-0|=0
and hence by Lemma
1.9
we have
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{U}_{n\leqslant N}e^{2\pi ikf(n)}=0
for all
U
with
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\log({U}(x))}{\log(R(x))}=0
. Taking
U=V
shows that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{V}_{n\leqslant N}e^{2\pi ikf(n)}=0
, which allows us to conclude that
(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is u.d. mod 1 with respect to
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{V}
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.   Proof of
(ii)\implies(i)
in Theorem
A

In this section, we prove the reverse direction of Theorem
A
. More specifically, let
V
and
f
be functions which belong to the same Hardy field and assume that
1\prec\log(V(x))\prec x
. Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
be the smallest positive integer with
|f(x)-q(x)|\preceq x^{\ell}
for some
q(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
, and suppose that
\ell\geqslant 2
. We show that if there exists a
p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
with
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|f^{(\ell)}(x)-p(x)|^{1/\ell}}{\log(V(x))^{\prime}}<\infty
then
(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is not u.d. mod 1 with respect to
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{V}
. First we establish some technical lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an increasing function, let
A<B<C
be elements of
(0,1)
with
B<C-A
, and let
X<Y
be natural numbers with
Y-X>\frac{2}{A}
. Suppose that
(\Delta f(n)\text{ mod }1)\in(A,B)
for all
n\in\{X,\dots,Y\}
. Then there exist natural numbers
Z,W
with
X\leqslant Z<W
,
Z<X+\frac{1}{A}
,
W-Z\geqslant\frac{C-A}{B}-2
, such that
(f(n)\text{ mod }1)\in(A,C)
for all
n\in\{Z,\dots,W\}
.

Proof.

For each
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
f(n+k)=f(n-1)+\sum_{m=n}^{n+k-1}\Delta f(m)
. Let
k
be the smallest natural number such that
\sum_{m=X}^{X+k-1}(\Delta f(m)\operatorname{mod}1)>1
. Then
X+k\leqslant Y
since
Y-X>\frac{2}{A}
, and the sequence
(f(X)\operatorname{mod}1,\dots,f(X+k)\operatorname{mod}1)
must visit every subinterval of
(0,1)
which has length larger than
B
. Let
Z\geqslant X
be the smallest natural number such that
(f(Z)\operatorname{mod}1)\in(A,C)
, and let
W>Z
be the smallest number such that
f(W+1)\operatorname{mod}1\not\in(A,C)
. We know that
W\leqslant Y
since
Y-X>\frac{2}{A}
. Also,
Z\leqslant X+k<X+\frac{1}{A}
and

(W-Z+2)\cdot B\geqslant\sum_{m=Z-1}^{W+1}(\Delta f(m)\operatorname{mod}1)\geqslant C-A,

so
W-Z\geqslant\frac{C-A}{B}-2
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
g
be a function which belongs to a Hardy field such that
\lim_{x\to\infty}g(x)=\infty
and
\lim_{x\to\infty}g^{\prime}(x)=0
. Fix any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then

g^{\prime}\left(x+\frac{1}{g^{\prime}(x)^{\varepsilon}}\right)=g^{\prime}(x)\cdot(1+o_{x\to\infty}(1)).

(3.1)

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\log\left(\frac{g^{\prime}(x+\frac{1}{(g^{\prime}(x))^{\varepsilon}})}{g^{\prime}(x)}\right)\to 0
as
x\to\infty
. To this end, consider

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{g^{\prime}(x+\frac{1}{(g^{\prime}(x))^{\varepsilon}})}{g^{\prime}(x)}\right)=\log\left(g^{\prime}(x+\frac{1}{(g^{\prime}(x))^{\varepsilon}})\right)-\log\left({g^{\prime}(x)}\right)=\int_{x}^{x+\frac{1}{(g^{\prime}(x))^{\varepsilon}}}\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(t)}{g^{\prime}(t)}dt.

(3.2)

\frac{-g^{\prime\prime}(t)}{g^{\prime}(t)}
is a Hardy function which decreases to
0
and so we have

-\int_{x}^{x+\frac{1}{(g^{\prime}(x))^{\varepsilon}}}\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(t)}{g^{\prime}(t)}dt\leqslant\int_{x}^{x+\frac{1}{(g^{\prime}(x))^{\varepsilon}}}\frac{-g^{\prime\prime}(x)}{g^{\prime}(x)}dt=-\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(x)}{(g^{\prime}(x))^{1+\varepsilon}}.

For any
c\in(0,1)
we have that
x^{1+c}g^{\prime}(x)\to\infty
as
x\to\infty
and from this it follows that
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{g^{\prime}(x)}{x^{-(1+\varepsilon/2)}}=\infty
and that
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\log(g^{\prime}(x))}{x^{c}}=0
. So

\lim_{x\to\infty}\left|\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(x)}{(g^{\prime}(x))^{1+\varepsilon}}\right|\leqslant\lim_{x\to\infty}\left|\frac{\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(x)}{g^{\prime}(x)}}{x^{-(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2}/2)}}\right|=\lim_{x\to\infty}\left|\frac{\log(g^{\prime}(x))}{x^{1-(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2}/2)}}\right|=0,

and so the desired limit follows.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
V
and
f
be Hardy functions which increase to
\infty
. Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
with
\ell>1
and suppose that
x^{\ell-1}\prec f(x)-p(x)
for each
p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
and that
f(x)\preceq x^{\ell}
. Additionally, suppose that
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f^{(\ell)}(x)}{((\log V(x))^{\prime})^{\ell}}\in(0,\infty)
. Then for each
\varepsilon>0
, there are arbitrarily large values of
N\in\mathbb{N}
with
(\Delta^{i}f(N)\text{ mod }1)\in(0,\varepsilon\cdot(\Delta^{\ell}f(N))^{i/\ell})
for all
i\in\{1,\dots\ell-1\}
.

Proof.

Let
c_{0}=1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}
and let
c_{i}=\frac{\ell-i}{\ell}
for
i\geqslant 1
so that
c_{0}>c_{1}>\dots>c_{\ell-1}>0
. Note that for any
\varepsilon>0
, the interval
(\Delta^{\ell}f(N)^{c_{0}},\varepsilon\cdot(\Delta^{\ell}f(N))^{c_{1}})
has length equal to
\varepsilon\cdot(\Delta^{\ell}f(N))^{c_{1}}\cdot(1+o_{N\to\infty}(1))
since
\Delta^{\ell}f(N)
decreases to
0
as
N\to\infty
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. There exists an arbitrarily large
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for the interval

I_{1}=(\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1})^{c_{0}},\varepsilon\cdot(\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1}))^{c_{1}}),

we have

(\Delta^{\ell-1}f(N_{1}-1)\text{ mod }1)\not\in I_{1}\text{ and }(\Delta^{\ell-1}f(N_{1})\text{ mod }1)\in I_{1}.

This follows from the fact that
\Delta^{\ell-1}f
increases to infinity,
\Delta^{\ell}f
decreases to
0
, and the interval
I_{1}
has length much larger than
\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1})
when
N_{1}
is large enough. For
n\geqslant N_{1}
, the sequence
(\Delta^{\ell-1}f(n)\text{ mod }1)
takes steps of size
\Delta^{\ell}f(n)\leqslant\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1})
and so it follows that
(\Delta^{\ell-1}f(n)\text{ mod }1)\in I_{1}
for
n\in\{N_{1},\dots,N_{1}+K_{1}\}
, where
K_{1}
is an integer satisfying

K_{1}\geqslant\frac{|I_{1}|}{\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1})}-2=\varepsilon\cdot(\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1}))^{c_{1}-1}\cdot(1+o_{N_{1}\to\infty}(1))=\varepsilon\cdot(\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1}))^{-1/\ell}\cdot(1+o_{N_{1}\to\infty}(1)).

Now, for
i\in\{1,\dots,\ell-1\}
, define the intervals
I_{i}=(A,B_{i})
where
A=\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1})^{c_{0}}
and
B_{i}=\varepsilon\cdot(\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1}))^{c_{i}}
. Then for each
i\in\{1,\dots,\ell-1\}
we have that
B_{i}-A>B_{i-1}
so long as
N_{1}
is large enough.

Claim:
There exist natural numbers,
N_{1}\leqslant\dots\leqslant N_{\ell-1}
and
K_{1},\dots,K_{\ell-1}
with
N_{i}\leqslant N_{1}+\frac{i-1}{A}
and
K_{i}\sim\varepsilon\cdot(\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1}))^{-1/\ell}
, such that
(\Delta^{\ell-i}f(n)\text{ mod }1)\in I_{i}
for all
n\in\{N_{i},\dots,N_{i}+K_{i}\}
and all
i\in\{1,\dots\ell-1\}
.

We prove this claim by induction on
i
. We have already shown the base case
i=1
, and now we show the induction step.

Suppose that we have integers
N_{i}
and
K_{i}
for
i\geqslant 1
such that
(\Delta^{\ell-i}f(n)\text{ mod }1)\in I_{i}
for
n\in\{N_{i},\dots,N_{i}+K_{i}\}
, where
N_{i}\leqslant N_{1}+\frac{i-1}{A}
and
K_{i}\sim\varepsilon\cdot(\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1}))^{-1/\ell}
. Observe that
K_{i}\geqslant\frac{2}{A}=2(\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1}))^{\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}-1}
when
N_{1}
is large. By Lemma
3.1
, there exist
N_{i+1},K_{i+1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(\Delta^{\ell-(i+1)}f(n)\text{ mod }1)\in I_{i+1}
for all
n\in\{N_{i+1},\dots,N_{i+1}+K_{i+1}\}
, where
N_{i}\leqslant N_{i+1}\leqslant N_{i}+\frac{1}{A}<N_{1}+\frac{i}{A}
and

K_{i+1}\geqslant\frac{B_{i+1}-A}{B_{i}}-2\sim\varepsilon\cdot(\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1}))^{c_{i+1}-c_{i}}\sim\varepsilon\cdot(\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1}))^{-1/\ell}

when
N_{1}
is large enough. This completes the induction step and the proof of the claim.

Next, we have
N_{\ell-1}\leqslant N_{1}+K_{1}
since
N_{\ell-1}\leqslant N_{1}+\frac{\ell-2}{A}
and
K_{1}\sim\varepsilon\cdot(\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1}))^{-1/\ell}
which is larger than
\frac{\ell-2}{A}=\frac{\ell-2}{\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1})^{c_{0}}}
when
N_{1}
is large enough. Thus, taking
N=N_{\ell-1}
, we have that
N
lies in each of the intervals
\{N_{i},\dots,N_{i}+K_{i}\}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,\ell-1\}
. So we have

(\Delta^{\ell-i}f(N)\text{ mod }1)\in I_{i}\subseteq(0,\varepsilon\cdot(\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1}))^{\frac{\ell-i}{\ell}})\text{ for all }i\in\{1,\dots\ell-1\}.

Lastly, we can recall that
\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1})\sim\Delta^{\ell}f\left(N_{1}+\frac{\ell-2}{\Delta^{\ell}f(N_{1})^{{1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}}}}\right)
from Lemma
3.2
. Replacing
\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon/2
if necessary, we have

(\Delta^{i}f(N)\text{ mod }1)\in(0,\varepsilon\cdot(\Delta^{\ell}f(N))^{i/\ell})

for all
i\in\{1,\dots\ell-1\}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.4

(
[
5
, Theorem 3.2.8]
)
.

Let
W
be a function which increases to
\infty
and let
(\alpha_{n,N})_{n,N\in\mathbb{N}}
be a nonnegative sequence such that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n,N}=1
. Then the following are equivalent:

•

For each function
F:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and each
\ell\in\mathbb{C}
, if
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{W}F(n)=\ell
then
\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n,N}F(n)=\ell
.

•

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}W(n)\left|\frac{\alpha_{n,N}}{\Delta W(n)}-\frac{\alpha_{n+1,N}}{\Delta W(n+1)}\right|<\infty
, and for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\alpha_{n,N}}{\Delta W(n)}=0
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
be functions which belong to the same Hardy field, and satisfy
1\prec\log(V_{1}(x))\prec x
and
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\log(V_{1}(x))}{\log(V_{2}(x))}\in(0,\infty)
. Let
F:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be any function and let
\ell\in\mathbb{C}
. Then
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{V_{1}}F(n)=\ell
if and only if
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{V_{2}}F(n)=\ell
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{V_{1}}F(n)=\ell
. We will show that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{V_{2}}F(n)=\ell
. Let
\alpha_{n,N}=\frac{\Delta V_{2}(n)}{V_{2}(N)}
for
n\leqslant N
and
\alpha_{n,N}=0
for
n>N
, so that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{V_{2}}F(n)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n,N}F(n)
. Using Theorem
3.4
it suffices to show that

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}V_{1}(n)\left|\frac{\alpha_{n,N}}{\Delta V_{1}(n)}-\frac{\alpha_{n+1,N}}{\Delta V_{1}(n+1)}\right|=\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{V_{2}(N)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}V_{1}(n)\left|\frac{\Delta V_{2}(n)}{\Delta V_{1}(n)}-\frac{\Delta V_{2}(n+1)}{\Delta V_{1}(n+1)}\right|

(3.3)

is finite. Since
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
belong to the same Hardy field, the function
x\mapsto\frac{\Delta V_{2}(x)}{\Delta V_{1}(x)}-\frac{\Delta V_{2}(x+1)}{\Delta V_{1}(x+1)}
also belongs to this Hardy field, and in particular this function is eventually positive or eventually negative. So there is a value of
\sigma\in\{-1,1\}
such that

\left|\frac{\Delta V_{2}(n)}{\Delta V_{1}(n)}-\frac{\Delta V_{2}(n+1)}{\Delta V_{1}(n+1)}\right|=\sigma\cdot\left(\frac{\Delta V_{2}(n+1)}{\Delta V_{1}(n+1)}-\frac{\Delta V_{2}(n)}{\Delta V_{1}(n)}\right)

(3.4)

for all large enough values of
n
. The summation by parts formula says that

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Delta x_{n+1}\cdot y_{n}=x_{N+1}y_{N}-\sum_{n=1}^{N}x_{n}\cdot\Delta y_{n}

(3.5)

for any sequences
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Applying summation by parts to (
3.3
), for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{2}(N)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}V_{1}(n)\left|\frac{\Delta V_{2}(n)}{\Delta V_{1}(n)}-\frac{\Delta V_{2}(n+1)}{\Delta V_{1}(n+1)}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{2}(N)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}V_{1}(n)\cdot\sigma\cdot\left(\frac{\Delta V_{2}(n+1)}{\Delta V_{1}(n+1)}-\frac{\Delta V_{2}(n)}{\Delta V_{1}(n)}\right)+o_{N\to\infty}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{V_{2}(N)}\left(V_{1}(N)\cdot\frac{V_{2}(N+1)}{V_{1}(N+1)}-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Delta V_{1}(n)\cdot\frac{\Delta V_{2}(n+1)}{\Delta V_{1}(n+1)}\right)+o_{N\to\infty}(1).

Since
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{V_{1}(x+1)}{V_{1}(x)}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{V_{2}(x+1)}{V_{2}(x)}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\Delta V_{1}(x+1)}{\Delta V_{1}(x)}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\Delta V_{2}(x+1)}{\Delta V_{2}(x)}=1
(due to our assumption that
\log(V_{1}(x)),\log(V_{2}(x))\prec x
), this becomes

1+o_{N\to\infty}(1)-\frac{\sigma}{V_{2}(N)}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Delta V_{2}(n)(1+o_{n\to\infty}(1))=1-\sigma+o_{N\to\infty}(1).

(3.6)

This proves that (
3.3
) is finite, and so
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{V_{2}}F(n)=\ell
. The other direction follows by symmetry, and so this concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
F:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be any function, let
\ell\in\mathbb{C}
and let
V
be a Hardy function with
\log(x)\prec\log(V(x))\prec x
. If
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{V}F(n)=\ell
then

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(n-N)^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}\cdot F(n)=\ell

(3.7)

where
\sigma_{N}=\frac{\Delta V(N)}{V(N)}
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{n,N}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(n-N)^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}
for
1\leqslant n\leqslant 2N
and
\alpha_{n,N}=0
for
n>2N
. It is clear that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n,N}=1
since
\sum_{n=1}^{2N}\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(n-N)^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}
approximates the Gaussian integral
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(x-N)^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}dx=1
. Then by Theorem
3.4
, it suffices to show that

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}V(n)\left|\frac{\alpha_{n,N}}{\Delta V(n)}-\frac{\alpha_{n+1,N}}{\Delta V(n+1)}\right|<\infty.

(3.8)

Let
W(n)=\log(V(n))
so that
V(n)=e^{W(n)}
and
\sigma_{N}=W^{\prime}(N)\cdot(1+o_{N\to\infty}(1))
. Note that

\frac{V(n)}{V(n+1)}=e^{-\Delta W(n+1)}=1+O_{N\to\infty}(\Delta W(n+1)).

Putting
\eta(n)=\frac{V(n)\alpha_{n,N}}{\Delta V(n)}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{V(n)\alpha_{n,N}}{\Delta V(n)}-\frac{V(n)\alpha_{n+1,N}}{\Delta V(n+1)}\right|=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\eta(n)-\eta(n+1)(1+O_{n\to\infty}(\Delta W(n+1)))\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\eta(n)-\eta(n+1)\right|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\eta(n+1)\cdot O_{n\to\infty}(\Delta W(n+1)).

The second sum is bounded since

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\eta(n+1)\cdot O_{n\to\infty}(\Delta W(n+1))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n,N}\frac{V(n+1)}{\Delta V(n+1)}\cdot O_{n\to\infty}(\Delta W(n+1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n,N}\cdot O_{n\to\infty}(\frac{1}{\Delta W(n+1)})\cdot O_{n\to\infty}(\Delta W(n+1))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n,N}O_{n\to\infty}(1)=O_{N\to\infty}(1).

To bound the other sum above, observe that the ratio

\frac{\eta(n+1)}{\eta(n)}\sim e^{-\frac{(n+1-N)^{2}-(n-N)^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}=e^{\frac{-2n-1+2N}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}

is decreasing in
n
. This shows that
\eta(n)
increases to its maximum and then decreases. So
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\eta(n)-\eta(n+1)\right|\leqslant 2\cdot\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\eta(n)
. We can bound
\sup_{n\leqslant N}\eta(n)
by noting that
\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(n-N)^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}\leqslant\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}=\frac{1}{\Delta W(N)\sqrt{2\pi}}
for all
n
and

\frac{V(n)}{\Delta V(n)}\leqslant\frac{V(N)}{\Delta V(N)}=\frac{1}{1-\frac{V(N-1)}{V(N)}}\leqslant 1+O_{N\to\infty}(\frac{V(N-1)}{V(N)})=1+O_{N\to\infty}(\Delta W(N))

for all
n\leqslant N
. In particular,
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\eta(n)=\frac{O_{N\to\infty}(\Delta W(N))}{\Delta W(N)\sqrt{2\pi}}=O_{N\to\infty}(1)
. We have shown that (
3.8
) holds and so we are done.
∎

Of particular interest to us is the contrapositive implication that if the limit in (
3.7
) does not exist then
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{V}F(n)
does not exist.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
V
and
f
be functions which increase to
\infty
and belong to the same Hardy field. Suppose that
1\prec\log(V(x))\prec x
and that
x^{\ell-1}\prec f(x)\prec x^{\ell}
for some
\ell\geqslant 2
. Additionally, suppose that
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f^{(\ell)}(x)}{((\log V(x))^{\prime})^{\ell}}=\upsilon\in(0,\infty)
. Then there exists a constant
C_{\upsilon}\in\mathbb{C}
and arbitrarily large values of
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(n-N)^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}\cdot e^{2\pi if(n)}=C_{\upsilon}\cdot e^{2\pi if(N)}+o_{N\to\infty}(1)

(3.9)

where
\sigma_{N}=\frac{V(N)}{\Delta V(N)}=\frac{1}{\log(V(N))^{\prime}}\cdot(1+o_{N\to\infty}(1))
and
C_{\upsilon}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-u^{2}/2}e^{2\pi i(\upsilon/\ell!)u^{\ell}}du
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Put
B=\frac{\upsilon}{\ell!}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sigma_{N}^{1/\ell}\cdot\Delta^{\ell}f(N)/\ell!
and let
C_{\upsilon}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-u^{2}/2}e^{2\pi iBu^{\ell}}du
. We will find an arbitrarily large value of
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(n-N)^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}\cdot e^{2\pi if(n)}=C_{\upsilon}\cdot e^{2\pi if(N)}+O(\varepsilon)+o_{N\to\infty}(1).

First, rewrite
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(n-N)^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}e^{2\pi if(n)}
as
\sum_{n=-\infty}^{N-1}\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{n^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}e^{2\pi if(N-n)}
and recall that there exists a constant
A\in(0,\infty)
such that

\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(n-N)^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}-\sum_{n=-\lfloor{A\sigma_{N}\rfloor}}^{\lfloor A\sigma_{N}\rfloor}\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{n^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}\right|<\varepsilon

(3.10)

Then it follows from the triangle inequality that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(n-N)^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}e^{2\pi if(n)}=\sum_{n=-\lfloor{A\sigma_{N}\rfloor}}^{\lfloor A\sigma_{N}\rfloor}\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{n^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}e^{2\pi if(N-n)}+O(\varepsilon).

(3.11)

Recall Newton’s backward difference formula, which says that

f(N-n)=f(N)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}\Delta^{i}f(N)+O_{N\to\infty}(n^{m+1}\cdot\Delta^{m+1}f(N))

(3.12)

for
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By Theorem
3.3
, we can pick an arbitrarily large
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(\Delta^{i}f(N)\text{ mod }1)\in(0,\frac{2\varepsilon}{\upsilon A^{\ell-1}\cdot\ell}\cdot(\Delta^{\ell}f(N))^{i/\ell})\subseteq(0,\frac{\varepsilon}{A^{\ell-1}\cdot\ell}\cdot\sigma_{N}^{-i})

for all
i\in\{1,\dots\ell-1\}
. Using the fact that
\binom{n}{i}
is an integer with
\binom{n}{i}\leqslant n^{i}
, we have
\binom{\lfloor A\sigma_{N}\rfloor}{i}\Delta^{i}f(N)\operatorname{mod}1\in(0,\frac{\varepsilon}{\ell})
for all
i
and so

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}\Delta^{i}f(N)\operatorname{mod}1\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)

(3.13)

for all
n\in\{-\lfloor A\sigma_{N}\rfloor,\dots,\lfloor A\sigma_{N}\rfloor\}
. We can use (
3.12
) to replace
f(N-n)
with

f(N)+(-1)^{\ell}\binom{n}{\ell}\Delta^{\ell}f(N)+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}\Delta^{i}f(N)+O_{N\to\infty}(n^{\ell+1}\cdot\Delta^{\ell+1}f(N)).

The
O_{N\to\infty}({n^{\ell+1}\Delta^{\ell+1}f(N)})
term is
o_{N\to\infty}(1)
by L’Hôpital’s rule, and (
3.13
) says that
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}\Delta^{i}f(N)=O(\varepsilon)
. Additionally, recall that
\binom{n}{\ell}=n^{\ell}/\ell!+O(n^{\ell-1})
and note that
n^{\ell-1}\cdot\Delta^{\ell}f(N)=o_{N\to\infty}(1)
uniformly for
|n|\leqslant A\sigma_{N}
by assumption. Altogether, we have shown that

\displaystyle e^{2\pi if(N-n)}=

\displaystyle e^{2\pi if(N)}\cdot e^{2\pi i(-1)^{\ell}\binom{n}{\ell}\Delta^{\ell}f(N)}\cdot e^{O(\varepsilon)}\cdot e^{o_{N\to\infty}(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2\pi if(N)}\cdot e^{2\pi i(-n)^{\ell}\Delta^{\ell}f(N)/\ell!}+O(\varepsilon)+o_{N\to\infty}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2\pi if(N)}\cdot e^{2\pi i(-n)^{\ell}\cdot B/\sigma_{N}^{\ell}\cdot(1+o_{N\to\infty}(1))}+O(\varepsilon)+o_{N\to\infty}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2\pi if(N)}\cdot e^{2\pi iB(-n/\sigma_{N})^{\ell}}+O(\varepsilon)+o_{N\to\infty}(1)

uniformly for
n\in\{-\lfloor A\sigma_{N}\rfloor,\dots,\lfloor A\sigma_{N}\rfloor\}
, and so (
3.11
) becomes

e^{2\pi if(N)}\cdot\sum_{n=-\lfloor{A\sigma_{N}\rfloor}}^{\lfloor A\sigma_{N}\rfloor}\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{n^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}e^{2\pi iB(-n/\sigma_{N})^{\ell}}+O(\varepsilon)+o_{N\to\infty}(1).

(3.14)

Let
u=-n/\sigma_{N}
. Then the sum in (
3.14
) is a Riemann sum for the integral

\int_{-A}^{A}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-u^{2}/2}e^{2\pi iBu^{\ell}}du=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-u^{2}/2}e^{2\pi iBu^{\ell}}du+O(\varepsilon).

This gives us

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(n-N)^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}\cdot e^{2\pi if(n)}=C_{\upsilon}\cdot e^{2\pi if(N)}+O(\varepsilon)+o_{N\to\infty}(1)

as desired.
∎

We can now give a proof of the implication
(ii)\implies(i)
in Theorem A.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
V
and
f
be functions which belong to the same Hardy field and assume that
1\prec\log(V(x))\prec x
. Pick the smallest
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|f(x)-q(x)|\preceq x^{\ell}
for some
q(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
and assume that
\ell\geqslant 2
. Suppose that there is
p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
such that

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|f^{(\ell)}(x)-p(x)|^{1/\ell}}{\log(V(x))^{\prime}}<\infty.

(3.15)

Then
(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is not u.d. mod 1 with respect to
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{V}
.

Proof.

By the same reasoning as in Theorem
2.2
, it suffices to consider the case
p(x)=q(x)=0
and
f>0
. We will show that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{V}e^{2\pi if(n)}
does not exist. Pick an arbitrarily small
\upsilon>0
and pick a function
{U}
which belongs to the same Hardy field as
f
and
V
and satisfies
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f^{(\ell)}(x)^{1/\ell}}{\log(U(x))^{\prime}}=\upsilon
. It suffices to show that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{U}e^{2\pi if(n)}
does not exist by applying Lemma
1.9
if
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\log(U(x))}{\log(V(x))}=0
and Corollary
3.5
if
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\log(U(x))}{\log(V(x))}\in(0,\infty)
.

Next, we apply Theorem
3.7
to see that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(n-N)^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}\cdot e^{2\pi if(n)}=C_{\upsilon}\cdot e^{2\pi if(N)}+o_{N\to\infty}(1)

(3.16)

holds for infinitely many values of
N
, where
\sigma_{N}=\frac{V(N)}{\Delta V(N)}
and
C_{\upsilon}
is given by the integral
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-u^{2}/2}e^{2\pi i(\upsilon/\ell!)u^{\ell}}du
. For this observation to be of any use to us, we need to know that
C_{\upsilon}
is nonzero. Consider the function
\xi\mapsto C_{\xi}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}/2}e^{2\pi i(\xi/\ell!)x^{\ell}}dx
. This function is continuous and it is a classical fact that
C_{0}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}/2}dx=\sqrt{2\pi}>0
. So
|C_{\upsilon}|>0
so long as
\upsilon>0
is small enough. Then from equation (
3.16
) we have that the limit
\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sigma_{N}\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(n-N)^{2}}{2\sigma_{N}^{2}}}\cdot e^{2\pi if(n)}
does not exist, and from Corollary
3.6
it follows that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\leqslant N}^{U}e^{2\pi if(n)}
does not exist. This concludes the proof.
∎

4.   Proof of
(i)\implies(ii)
in Theorem
A

In this section, we prove the implication
(i)\implies(ii)
in Theorem
A
. This is achieved by proving a result about averages of the form
\frac{1}{s(N)}\sum_{n=N-s(N)}^{N}e^{2\pi if(n)}
and applying Lemma
1.9
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f
be a function which belongs to a Hardy field. Let
\ell\geqslant 2
be the smallest integer such that
|f(x)-q(x)|\preceq x^{\ell}
for some
q(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
. If
s:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is a nondecreasing function with
s(N)\leqslant N-1
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lim_{N\to\infty}|f^{(\ell)}(N)-p(N)|^{1/\ell}\cdot s(N)=\infty
for all
p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
then

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{s(N)}\sum_{n=N-s(N)}^{N}e^{2\pi if(n)}=0.

From this theorem, the implication
(i)\implies(ii)
in Theorem
A
follows. Indeed, for fixed
p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
, if

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|f^{(\ell)}(x)-p(x)|^{1/\ell}}{(\log(V(x)))^{\prime}}=\infty
then we can find a function
U
such that
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|f^{(\ell)}(x)-p(x)|^{1/\ell}}{(\log(U(x)))^{\prime}}\in(0,\infty)
so that any function
s
satisfies
\lim_{N\to\infty}s(N)\cdot\Delta\log(U(N))=\infty
if and only if
s
satisfies
\lim_{N\to\infty}|f^{(\ell)}(N)-p(N)|^{1/\ell}\cdot s(N)=\infty
. Then Theorem
4.1
along with the implication
(4)\implies(3)
in Lemma
1.9
shows that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{V}_{n\leqslant N}e^{2\pi ikf(n)}=0
for any nonzero
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and hence
(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is u.d. mod 1 with respect to
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{V}
.

The proof of Theorem
4.1
goes by induction on
\ell
, with the base case
\ell=1
having already been proved in Section
2
. A helpful tool for reducing the induction step to a previous case is a variant of van der Corput’s trick, which follows from
[
4
, Theorem 2.12]
with
F_{N}=I_{N}
,
G=\mathbb{Z}
, and
H=\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 4.2

(van der Corput’s trick)
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence of complex numbers and let
(I_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of intervals of natural numbers with
|I_{N}|\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
. Suppose that for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in I_{N}}(x_{n+j}\overline{x_{n}})\to 0
as
N\to\infty
. Then
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in I_{N}}x_{n}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a Hardy function with
|h(x)|\prec x^{\ell}
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
s:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be a function which tends to
\infty
. Suppose that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N-s(N),N]}e^{2\pi ih^{\prime}(n)}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
. Then
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{n\in[N-s(N),N]}e^{2\pi ih(n)}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
.

We also need some technical lemmas on bounding exponential sums.

Lemma 4.4

(
[
9
, Theorem 2.2]
)
.

Let
F
be a
C^{2}
real-valued function on an interval
I
and suppose that for some
\lambda>0
and
\alpha\geqslant 1
, we have
\lambda\leqslant|F^{\prime\prime}(x)|\leqslant\alpha\lambda
for
x\in I
. Then

\left|\sum_{x\in I}e^{iF(x)}\right|\ll\alpha\lambda^{1/2}|I|+\frac{1}{\lambda^{1/2}}.

(4.1)

Here the notation
A\ll B
means that there is an absolute constant
C
such that
A\leqslant CB
.

The following is the classical iterated van der Corput inequality, whose proof can be found in section 2.4 of
[
9
]
or as Lemma 2.11 in
[
1
]
.

Lemma 4.5

(
[
1
, Lemma 2.11]
)
.

Let
k
be a positive integer and
K=2^{k}
. Assume that
I=(X_{1},X_{1}+X]\subseteq(X_{1},2X_{1}]
and let
S=\sum_{x\in I}e^{if(x)}
. For any positive
H_{1},...,H_{k}\leqslant C(k)\cdot X
, where
C(k)
is a constant depending only on
k
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{S}{X}\right)^{K}\leqslant 8^{K-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{H_{i}^{K/2^{j}}}+\frac{1}{XH_{1}\cdots H_{k}}\sum_{h_{1}=1}^{H_{1}}\cdots\sum_{h_{k}=1}^{H_{k}}\left|\sum_{x\in I(\mathbf{h})}e^{if_{1}(x)}\right|\right)

(4.2)

where
f_{1}(x)=f(\mathbf{h},x)=h_{1}\cdots h_{k}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}f(x+\mathbf{h}\cdot\mathbf{t})d\mathbf{t}
,
\mathbf{h}=(h_{1},\dots,h_{k})
,
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})
and
I(\mathbf{h})=(X_{1},X_{1}+X-h_{1}-\dots-h_{k}]
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
j\geqslant 3
and let
f
be a
C^{j}
function on an interval
I=(X_{1},X_{1}+X]\subseteq(X_{1},2X_{1}]
, for
X,X_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that
f^{(j)}
is monotone on
I
and that there are constants
\lambda>0
and
\alpha\geqslant 1
with
\lambda\leqslant|f^{(j)}(x)|\leqslant\alpha\lambda
for all
x\in I
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{|I|}\sum_{x\in I}e^{if(x)}\right|\ll\left(\frac{1}{X}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{j-2}}}+\alpha^{\frac{1}{2^{j-2}}}(\lambda X^{j-2})^{\frac{1}{2^{j-1}}}+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda X^{j}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{j-1}}}

(4.3)

and the absolute constant depends only on
j
.

Proof.

We will begin by applying Lemma
4.5
. Let
k=j-2
and put
H_{i}=\min\{\frac{X}{C(k)},\frac{X}{2k}\}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. It is clear that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{H_{i}^{K/2^{i}}}\ll\frac{1}{X}
so it suffices to bound the second term in (
4.2
). Observe that
\lambda h_{1}\cdots h_{k}\leqslant|f_{1}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(x)|\leqslant\alpha\lambda h_{1}\cdots h_{k}
for all
x\in I
, and so we may apply Lemma
4.4
to see that for each
\mathbf{h}=(h_{1},\dots,h_{k})\in[1,H_{1}]\times\cdots\times[1,H_{k}]
,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{x\in I(\mathbf{h})}e^{if_{1}(x)}\right|\ll

\displaystyle\alpha\lambda^{1/2}(h_{1}\cdots h_{k})^{1/2}(X-h_{1}-\cdots-h_{k})+\frac{1}{\lambda^{1/2}(h_{1}\cdots h_{k})^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\alpha\lambda^{1/2}(h_{1}\cdots h_{k})^{1/2}X+\frac{1}{\lambda^{1/2}(h_{1}\cdots h_{k})^{1/2}}.

So far, we have shown that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{X}\sum_{x\in I}e^{if(x)}\right|^{2^{k}}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}+\frac{1}{XH_{1}\cdots H_{k}}\sum_{h_{1}=1}^{H_{1}}\cdots\sum_{h_{k}=1}^{H_{k}}\left(\alpha\lambda^{1/2}(h_{1}\cdots h_{k})^{1/2}X+\frac{1}{\lambda^{1/2}(h_{1}\cdots h_{k})^{1/2}}\right).

(4.5)

Next, we can bound the sum over
\mathbf{h}
by noting that
\sum_{h_{i}=1}^{H_{i}}h_{i}^{1/2}\leqslant H_{i}^{3/2}\leqslant\frac{X^{3/2}}{(2k)^{3/2}}
and
\sum_{h_{i}=1}^{H_{i}}h_{i}^{-1/2}\leqslant 2H_{i}^{1/2}\leqslant\frac{2X^{1/2}}{(2k)^{1/2}}
for each
i
. So,

\displaystyle\sum_{h_{1}=1}^{H_{1}}\cdots\sum_{h_{k}=1}^{H_{k}}\alpha\lambda^{1/2}(h_{1}\cdots h_{k})^{1/2}X+\frac{1}{\lambda^{1/2}(h_{1}\cdots h_{k})^{1/2}}\ll\alpha\lambda^{1/2}X^{3k/2+1}+\frac{X^{k/2}}{\lambda^{1/2}}.

Noting that
H_{1}\cdots H_{k}\gg X^{k}
, we can combine this with (
4.5
) to obtain

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{X}\sum_{x\in I}e^{if(x)}\right|^{2^{k}}\ll

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}+\frac{1}{X^{k+1}}\left(\alpha\lambda^{1/2}X^{3k/2+1}+\frac{X^{k/2}}{\lambda^{1/2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}+\alpha(\lambda X^{k})^{1/2}+\frac{1}{(\lambda X^{k+2})^{1/2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}+\alpha(\lambda X^{j-2})^{1/2}+\frac{1}{(\lambda X^{j})^{1/2}}.

Taking
2^{k}
th roots of both sides gives (
4.3
), which completes the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We will prove the following statement by induction on
\ell
, and the statement of the theorem follows. Suppose that
F
is a function which belongs to a Hardy field and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
with
x^{\ell-1}\prec F(x)\preceq x^{\ell}
. If
s:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is a function with
s(N)\leqslant N-1
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lim_{N\to\infty}F^{(\ell)}(N)\cdot s(N)^{\ell}=\infty
then

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{s(N)}\sum_{n=N-s(N)}^{N}e^{2\pi iF(n)}=0.

(4.6)

The base case
\ell=1
is true by Theorem
2.2
and Lemma
1.9
(c.f. Corollary
1.10
). So suppose that induction hypothesis holds for a given value of
\ell\geqslant 1
. Let
f
be a function which belongs to a Hardy field with
x^{\ell}\prec f(x)\preceq x^{\ell+1}
and let
s:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be a nondecreasing function with
s(N)\leqslant N-1
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lim_{N\to\infty}f^{(\ell+1)}(N)\cdot s(N)^{\ell+1}=\infty
.

There are two cases to consider. If
\lim_{N\to\infty}f^{(\ell+1)}(N)\cdot s(N)^{\ell}=\infty
then we can take
F=f^{\prime}
in the induction hypothesis to see that (
4.6
) holds and it follows from Corollary
4.3
that (
4.6
) holds with
F=f
also.

Now suppose that
\limsup_{N\to\infty}f^{(\ell+1)}(N)\cdot s(N)^{\ell}<\infty
. Then
\lim_{N\to\infty}f^{(\ell+1)}(N)\cdot s(N)^{\ell-1}=0
and we can take
j=\ell+1
,
X=s(N)
,
I=[N-s(N),N]
,
\lambda=f^{(\ell+1)}(N)
,
\alpha=\frac{f^{(\ell+1)}(N-s(N))}{f^{(\ell+1)}(N)}
in order to apply Lemma
4.5
if
\ell=1
or Theorem
4.6
if
\ell\geqslant 2
. We have
X\to\infty
,
X\lambda^{j-2}\to 0
, and
X\lambda^{j}\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
by assumption, and using the argument in the proof of Lemma
3.2
we can see that
\alpha\to 1
as
N\to\infty
. Hence,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{s(N)}\sum_{n=N-s(N)}^{N}e^{2\pi if(n)}=0.

which completes the induction step. This concludes the proof.
∎

As described above, the implication
(i)\implies(ii)
follows immediately from Theorem
4.1
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
V
and
f
be functions which belong to the same Hardy field and assume that
1\prec\log(V(x))\prec x
. Pick the smallest
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|f(x)-q(x)|\preceq x^{\ell}
for some
