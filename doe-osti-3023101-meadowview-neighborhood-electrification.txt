2. Executive Summary

Awarded a Congressionally Directed Spend (CDS) in 2023, Sacramento Municipal Utility
District (SMUD) has successfully executed the Meadowview Neighborhood Electriﬁcation
Project designed to demonstrate the efficacy of residential energy efficiency and
weatherization upgrades for under-resourced communities positively impacting energy use
reductions, ﬁnancial metrics, and indoor air quality. Initially, the project sought to electrify
and upgrade 300 homes in Sacramento’s Meadowview neighborhood. However, as interest
and momentum grew, the project team proved increasingly efficient and was able to
retroﬁt 374 homes. The project built on SMUD’s years of effort increasing energy efficiency,
weatherization, and electriﬁcation for low-income residential customers—moving beyond
incremental improvements toward full home electriﬁcation where it was both feasible and
cost-effective.

By upgrading homes with modern, efficient electric technologies and appliances, the
project improved indoor air quality, lowered utility bills, and reduced the stress families
faced when older appliances failed unexpectedly. Ultimately, the project not only advanced
technical and economic understanding of neighborhood-scale electriﬁcation, but also
delivered tangible public beneﬁts through healthier homes, reduced energy costs, and
expanded access to emerging clean technologies.

Topline result metrics included:

 • Approximately 1,000-2,000 Meadowview residents educated on project beneﬁts
 • 377 assessments completed
 • 374 home retroﬁts completed with either energy efficiency or weatherization
 upgrades
 • 140 completed surveys (37% completion)

 • 94% survey respondents reported Very Satisﬁed or Somewhat Satisﬁed with the
 Program

5. Project Activities Summary

The Sacramento Municipal Utility District’s (SMUD’s) Meadowview Neighborhood
Electrification Project successfully advanced building electrification and weatherization in
one of Sacramento’s most under-resourced communities. Below is a summary of project
activities & results following a structured set of tasks as outlined in the Statement of
Project Objectives (SOPO).

 • Task 1.1- Identify 500 homes: The purpose of this project is to explore a
 concentrated neighborhood-based effort to installing energy efficiency and
 weatherization upgrades with a focus on serving low-income, disadvantaged
 community members. The Meadowview neighborhood of Sacramento was
 identified as having a concentration of customers meeting the low-income
 disadvantaged criteria simultaneously verified via US Census data and SMUD’s
 income eligible discount program, commonly referred to as the ‘Energy Assistance
 Program Rate (EAPR). During grant period of performance, SMUD identified more
 than 500 homes throughout this Meadowview neighborhood eligible to receive
 upgrades. Eligibility confirmation is further verified in Task 2.1 Outreach tactics to
 effectively communicate with eligible customer outlined in Tasks 1.2 and 1.3.

• Task 1.2- Develop Project Outreach Materials: To maximize community engagement
 and ensure equitable access to the Meadowview Neighborhood Electrification
 project, SMUD developed comprehensive outreach and enrollment materials in
 multiple languages. Program staff, Marketing and Communications teams, and
 local neighborhood partners collaborated to identify effective messaging strategies
 and formats for the target audience. In addition to traditional print and digital
 materials, SMUD launched a dedicated project website to provide residents with
 easy access to information and enrollment resources online. Interest in the
 program grew organically through word of mouth, as satisfied participants shared
 their experiences within their networks. SMUD also leveraged its customer service
 representatives and conducted direct phone outreach, allowing staff to answer
 questions, assist with enrollment, and address concerns in real time. This multi-
 faceted approach helped create culturally relevant, accessible, and responsive
 communications, ultimately driving broader interest and participation in the
 program. Examples of outreach materials can be found in Appendix E

• Task 1.3- Reach out to and education at least 500 individuals: Project outreach and
 enrollment efforts were executed by the SMUD Residential Building Electrification
 Team within the Customer Operations Business Unit, in collaboration with SMUD’s
 Marketing team. The team developed and distributed multi-lingual outreach
 materials—including flyers, door hangers, and email messages—to educate the
 community and encourage program participation The materials and content
 developed were then distributed through mail, email, word of mouth from trusted
 community-based organizations and community ambassadors, and SMUD
 Customer Service Representatives. These efforts were highly effective, resulting in
 the engagement and education of thousands of Meadowview residents, exceeding
 the original goal of 500 individuals. Comprehensive outreach materials are available
 in Appendix A.

• Task 2.1- Home Energy Assessments: Once residents expressed interest, they
 underwent a structured enrollment process which included completing intake
 forms and scheduling appointments with SMUD Energy Specialists. Following
 eligibility verification and enrollment, SMUD Energy Specialists performed in-home
 assessments, collaborating with each household to identify unique needs and
 recommend tailored upgrades. Common measures included repairs to windows,
 doors, bug screens, and weatherstripping, as well as electrification of appliances
 (such as replacing gas cooktops with induction or electric alternatives), energy

 efficiency upgrades (like LED lighting), enhanced insulation, and improvements to
 HVAC and water heater systems. Total number of assessments performed: 377

 • Task 2.2-Retrofit a minimum of 350 homes: Following the needs assessment and
 recommendations, residents chose the retrofits and repairs to be performed in their
 homes. Qualified and competitively bid contractors carried out the work, with the
 grant covering the full costs, eliminating financial barriers for participants. Upon
 completion, contractor invoices were processed and paid by SMUD, only allowable
 costs were passed along for reimbursement to the DOE. All unallowable costs were
 excluded from the project expenditure funded by the DOE. These items were fully
 covered by SMUD, ensuring there was no cost to the customer. Total homes served:
 374 with upgrade breakdown available in Appendix D.

 • Task 3.1- Summary Report of Project Scope: To assure quality and document
 satisfaction, Energy Specialists conducted follow-up outreach after the completion
 of home retrofits, using direct mail, email communication, and surveys to collect
 customer feedback. Total number of surveys received: 140

 • Task 3.2- Project Results Summarizing Benefits & Lessons Learned: Survey results,
 presented in Appendix A and B, reflect very high overall customer satisfaction rates,
 with minor issues promptly clarified and addressed by the SMUD team. In the rare
 case of customer dissatisfaction, SMUD promptly rectified the issue and resolved
 all customer issues with the contractor or the installed equipment. However,
 overall satisfaction rates indicated 94% Very Satisfied or Somewhat Satisfied.

In conclusion, the project was able to perform with increased efficiency thereby exceeding
its original performance target metrics including the compilation of over 1,000 potential
households, the direct education of well over 500 residents, and completion of retrofits
and repairs to 374 homes. The Meadowview Neighborhood Electrification Project stands
as an impactful example of residential electrification and weatherization upgrades at scale
deployed by a non-profit publicly owned utility. This work demonstrates the effectiveness
of a neighborhood-based approach to community engagement, energy efficiency, and
equitable access to electrification.

 Appendix B
 Lessons Learned
Meadowview Neighborhood Electriﬁcation

The true heartbeat of the Meadowview Neighborhood Electriﬁcation project lies with the
very people it was designed to serve—SMUD customers. Every step the project team took,
every decision that was made, from outreach and marketing to collaborating with
community partners and hosting events, was guided by their hopes and needs. What began
as a mission to electrify 300 homes blossomed into something greater, as we joyfully
surpassed that goal, bringing clean energy to 374 households. After a year of dedicated feet
on the ground, walking side by side with the community, we are deeply proud to celebrate
this shared success—a testament to the power of working together for a brighter, greener
future.

Lessons Learned:

 1. Trust must be earned
 a. Building trust within a community is never easy—it requires time, presence,
 and a genuine commitment to listen and serve. In Meadowview, this was
 especially true as SMUD launched its electriﬁcation project. SMUD staff
 prioritized being visible and approachable by attending community events,
 partnering with local organizations, and engaging directly with residents in
 their neighborhoods. Early on, this focus on trust was critical: in the ﬁrst
 quarter of the project, only 24 customers participated. However, as trust
 grew and word of mouth spread throughout the community, interest surged—
 by quarters two and three, more than 350 customers had expressed interest
 in joining the program. This example illustrates the importance of building
 relationships, as it allowed us to better understand residents' needs and
 effectively communicate the unfamiliar beneﬁts of home electriﬁcation,
 ultimately accelerating the program’s success.
 b. To foster this trust, our team actively collaborated with trusted community
 organizations invested in Meadowview’s wellbeing, including AARP, local
 Chambers of Commerce’s, Rebuilding Together Sacramento, Asian
 Community Center (seniors), and resettlement agencies, among others.
 SMUD representatives regularly participated in Neighborhood Association
 meetings, school events, library programs, and community center

 activities—demonstrating our genuine commitment to the community. As a
 result, trust grew quickly among both community partners and residents,
 laying the foundation that enabled us not only to meet but to exceed our
 electriﬁcation goal.

2. Outreach efforts are ever changing
 a. Outreach efforts for the Meadowview Neighborhood Electriﬁcation project
 began at least six months before the official contract start date and evolved
 continuously throughout the project. Initial strategies included direct mail
 and email campaigns, neighborhood walks, community event participation,
 social media outreach, a holiday light campaign, multilingual digital and
 radio advertisements, and multiple door-to-door canvassing initiatives. Over
 the course of the contract, these efforts resulted in more than 5,000 letters
 and emails being sent, over 500 doors knocked, and more than 200 hours
 spent engaging with the community at events and pop-up enrollment
 locations. The team also recognized that terminology around electriﬁcation
 could be confusing for many families, prompting ongoing revisions to
 outreach messaging to make it clearer and more accessible.
 b. To ensure no avenue was left unexplored, the team took outreach a step
 further by personally following up with residents who had received mailed
 communications. These follow-up interactions provided an additional
 opportunity to build trust and offer education, allowing customers to ask
 questions and feel more comfortable about the electriﬁcation process. This
 hands-on, personalized approach was met with great enthusiasm by the
 community and played a critical role in successfully completing the
 grant objectives.

 Appendix C
  SMUD Equitable Building Decarbonization Survey
 Results
Last year SMUD contracted and received initial results for a survey covering the impacts
and beneﬁts of SMUD’s Energy Saver Bundle Program, focusing on residential customers
eligible for the Energy Assistance Program Rate (EAPR) between 2021 and 2023.

This survey also begins to address the ﬁnal outstanding question SMUD received from the
DOE in July 2025: “Can you provide an Excel spreadsheet of average electric bill cost
savings from before the retroﬁts to current cost savings?” The available data and survey
responses demonstrate that upgrading home appliances and implementing related
retroﬁts can lead to lower utility bills by decreasing overall energy use and switching to
more efficient appliances. However, it should be noted that the survey data is currently
incomplete, as SMUD has not yet been able to obtain data from the local gas utility, which
limits the ability to fully assess total household cost savings at this time.

Key survey highlights are summarized below.

Key Points and Metrics:

 • 4,071 homes were served during 2021-2023, with measures covering electriﬁcation
 and energy efficiency. Commercial customers were excluded from this evaluation.

 • The evaluation used customer surveys, implementation team and contractor
 interviews, and data analysis methods such as a two-stage Difference-in-Difference
 model with matched control groups for impact measurement.

 • Survey sample: 252 customer surveys analyzed (about 6% representation of the
 upgraded population, with breakdowns by measure type: e.g., 95 HVAC, 55 heat
 pump water heaters, etc.)

Program Findings:

 • Most respondents were very satisﬁed with their upgrades. For example, 85% of heat
 pump water heater recipients felt comfortable and 67% never ran out of hot water.
 For HVAC upgrades, 83% reported improved comfort in summer and 81% in winter.

 • Improvements in sleep: 63% of respondents reported better sleep quality, citing
 improved temperature control as the reason. This is used as a proxy for mental

 health, indicating people are more comfortable and mentally healthier due to the
 upgrades.

 • Improved health: 35% of respondents speciﬁcally attributed health improvements
 to the program. Notable improvements were observed in respiratory concerns and
 allergies; some improvements were also noted for arthritis and chronic pain.
 Importantly, no conditions were reported as worsened because of the program.

 • Quality of life enhancements: 44% of respondents strongly agreed or agreed that
 their quality of life at home increased, a result much higher than a comparable state
 program (9% improvement).

 • High program attribution for non-energy beneﬁts (NEBs), especially comfort, mental
 health (via better sleep), and physical health improvements

Energy Efficiency Impact Findings:

 • The primary evaluation goal was to determine energy efficiency savings, shifts due
 to electriﬁcation, and changes in demand during peak periods.

 • Analyzed 45 distinct energy-saving measures grouped into 16 categories (such as
 HVAC, water heating, weatherization, lighting, etc.).

 • Single family homes saw a 4.8% (469.4KWh) reduction in annualized usage.

 • Multifamily homes aw a 6.3% (383.1KWh) reduction in annualized usage.

 • Impact methodology ensured matched control groups and weather normalization to
 accurately quantify energy impacts.

 • The modeling approach controls for factors like pre/post install usage, weather, and
 month/time of year, providing statistically meaningful estimates of usage and peak
 demand reductions

 • The cost and usage savings due to new electric appliances and other upgrades is
 incomplete because SMUD could not obtain the usage and cost data from the
 natural gas utility in our service territory. This limitation means that the analysis
 cannot fully account for total household energy cost reductions when switching
 from gas to electric appliances, making the estimated savings only partial for
 participating homes.

In summary, the program delivered improved comfort, signiﬁcant non-energy beneﬁts like
better health and sleep, and quantiﬁable energy and demand savings for participating low-
income residential customers.
