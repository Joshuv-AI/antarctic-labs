1 Accomplishments

1.1 What are the major goals of the project?

The goal of this project is to measure charged fusion products from the d(d,p)t reaction in MAST-U
plasmas as a function of time and position with good energy resolution using a system of up to six
charged particle detectors. The data from this new diagnostic will make it possible to determine
the neutral beam ion density profile as a function of R, z, and t with reduced model dependency
and contribute new information to a global analysis of fast ion diagnostic data needed for the
determination of the fast ion distribution function (velocity space tomography). The requested
funding will provide support for one graduate student, a post doctoral associate (at a later stage)
and involve several undergraduate students In order to reach this goal several milestones need to
be reached:

 • Acquire and analyze data with an updated 4-channel system at MAST-U.

 • Based on the new data, design and build a six channel system to be mounted on the recipro-
 cating arm of MAST-U.

 • Design and build a new data acquisition system capable of long pulse operation.

 • Install full system in MAST-U

 • Take data with the full system to study quiescent plasmas and study the effect of various
 instabilities on the

 • measured proton rates and compare to modern models.

The problems encountered during the first operation of NSTX-U in 2016 lead to a change in the
major goals of the project. It is not expected that a six channel detector system will be installed
when NSTX-U resumes operations, but that instead data be taken with an updated system at
MAST-U which has begun plasma operation in 2020. As a consequence we plan to adapt the existing
4-channel system for installation in MAST-U in Summer 2021 and take data during the following

Based on the observed particle rates and noise issues encountered during the first MAST-U measure-
ments we will drop the evaluation of the current mode for the detectors (point 8 in the previous list)
as the diamond detectors are capable of particle rates up to 500 kHz and counting mode provides
considerably more information.

1.2 What was accomplished under these goals?

 • We completed the diamond detector testing at the Tandem Accelerator of the John D. Fox
 Laboratory at Florida State University where we exposed the detectors to a 4 MeV proton
 beam. The detectors were exposed to the primary beam which was heavily defocused and
 send through a 0.1 mm diameter pin hole to reduce its intensity. This allowed us to observe
 instantaneous particle rates up to 500 - 600 kHz. We found no significant signal deterioration
 up to the highest rates observed while a silicon based detector which was tested at the same
 time started to display signal loss similar to what has been perviously observed at MAST in
 2013. The results of these tests were presented during the fall, 2023 DPP meeting.

 • We installed the 4-channel proton detector (PD) on the reciprocating arm (RP) at MAST-
 U in September, 2023. This detector array was instrumented with three new silicon based
 detectors with a 300 micron depletion depth and a 500 mm2 active area. First data showed
 a very large electrical background noise which could be significantly reduced by applying the

 • A new, compact 6 channel pre-amp module has been developed and constructed, specifically
 for the 6-channel diamond detector head. These pre-amps provide more amplification for the
 smaller diamond signals and made it possible to install eight pre-amps as close as possible to
 the end of the RP arm which further reduces noise pickup.

 • New, optimized entrance foils for the detectors have been constructed. Data taken at MU03
 and at MAST in 2013 indicate that one possible reason for the observed signal loss in the
 proton detectors is due to X-ray emission or that a small fraction of beam particles hit the
 detector. We therefore increased the entrance foil thickness to 2.4 micron to block very low-
 energy particles and increase X-ray absorption. This improved the situation but did not
 entirely prevent signal loss.

 • All eight digitizer channels have been employed in data taking during MU04, where two
 channels were dedicated to noise measurements.

 • The new shaft, the completed PD6 with the new cabling has been installed on MAST-U in
 2025 and successfully operated during the experimental campaigns MU04 and MU05 where
 a wealth of data was acquired. The new cabling scheme has indeed dramatically reduced
 the electrical noise and protons detected with the diamond detector array could readily be
 identified and counted without the need of extensive background subtraction techniques. This
 system also regularly measured proton rates above 200 kHz without problems. As an example,
 Fig. 3 shows an example of the PD6 performance for shot 52557 in MU04. This shot is similar
 to previous shots from the MAST 2013 campaign, with a total NBI power of 1.6 MA and a

 • During MU04 we also took data during shots with a plasma current of 1 MA and a total NBI
 power of 3.8 MW. These conditions resulted in the highest proton rates ever measured and
 demonstrate the high counting rate capability of a diamond based proton detector system.
 Fig. 4 shows as an example shot 52593 where detector numbers 5 and 6 measured particle
 rates up to 400 kHz just before a massive instability (probably an IRE) which aligns with
 dips in plasma current and plasma density. Detectors 5 and 6 probe more of the plasma core
 compared to detectors 1 and 2 which have counted at considerably lower rates.

 • The orbit code package orbit mod90 used to calculate trajectories and detector acceptances
 has been extended to produce input files that can be used by the CFP version of FIDASIM to
 calculate rates and expected energy spectra. There still remain significant discrepancies be-
 tween measured proton rates and TRANSP/FIDASIM predictions which need a good amount
 of additional analysis and study as the source of these deviations is not clear.

The improvements described above allowed us to complete points 6, 7, and 9 of the research plan
in section 1.1 describing the major goals of the research plan. To summarize, all points of the
research plan were completed with the exception of point 8 for which not enough FIU as well as
MAST-U resources and time were available. The fact that the detector are able to handle particle
rates up to 400 kHz also makes point 8 less relevant.

1.3 What opportunities for training and professional development has the project
 provided?

This project served as the subject of a Ph.D. thesis (A. Aboutaleb) who defended her thesis in
November, 2024.

The project provided numerous opportunities for undergraduate students to gain research experi-
ence. During classes and as part of a department wide mentoring program, undergraduate physics
students are encouraged to participate in research where this project is featured as a good oppor-
tunity. The current project was listed as part of a work force training grant that has been funded
by the DOE. At the post-doc level this project provide opportunities to gain experience in project
management and the design, realization and communication results of scientific research.
