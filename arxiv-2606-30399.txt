Multiscale Dynamic Dependence Estimation over Networks
Cristian F. Jiménez-Varón, Marina I. Knight, Matthew A. Nunes

Abstract
In numerous scientific and industrial settings, observed multivariate time series are often nonstationary in nature, i.e., comprise data whose second order properties vary over time. An additional feature of many modern datasets is that the cross-dependencies of such series are structured by an underlying network, giving rise to complex interactions between temporal dynamics and network topology. In this article we propose Locally Stationary Wavelet processes on Networks (Net-LSW), a new framework for modelling multiscale, time-varying dependencies that explicitly incorporates the network structure. Unlike traditional multivariate approaches, the Net-LSW process encodes the graph directly in the covariance structure of the process’s random increments. We also introduce the concept of the local partial correlation graph , which connects edges in the graph to non-zero entries in the time- and scale- dependent dependence structure of a nonstationary process. For inference on the local cross-nodal (partial) dependence, we develop a novel subprocess-based estimation scheme and establish its desirable consistency properties. Simulation studies further demonstrate that the proposed framework accurately recovers evolving dependence structures whilst respecting the underlying graph topology. Finally, we apply our framework to daily stock price volatilities across a global bank network, demonstrating its ability to capture multiscale, highly nonstationary dependencies and identify time-varying systemic shifts during major financial shocks, including Brexit and the COVID-19 pandemic. KEYWORDS : graphs; networks; nonstationary time series; spectral estimation; wavelets



1
Introduction

Many modern datasets comprise multivariate time series whose cross-dependencies are structured by an underlying, known network topology. In such settings, the information recorded at each node is often a (second order)
nonstationary
process, giving rise to complex interactions between time-evolving temporal dynamics and the network configuration. Examples of this rich paradigm span a wide array of domains, including structural connections across localised regions in brain networks
(
1
;
15
)
, geographical links in climate monitoring topologies, and multi-channel transaction streams in financial systems
(
12
)
. Because these systems change dynamically over time, modelling them requires robust statistical frameworks capable of capturing the intricate interactions between time-varying temporal mechanics, multi-scale structural behaviours, and the network’s spatial constraints.

To model time-varying systems with evolving spectral properties, the literature on nonstationary time series has traditionally relied on the foundational concept of evolutionary spectra introduced by
34
. This paradigm was subsequently revolutionised by
8
(see also
10
) through the mathematical formalisation of
locally stationary processes
, which model time series whose statistical properties change smoothly over time, allowing them to be approximated locally by stationary processes. While these classical models operate in the Fourier domain, wavelet-based models have emerged as an invaluable tool for extracting highly localised features simultaneously across both time and frequency. The foundation for time-varying spectrum modelling using wavelets is the Locally Stationary Wavelet (LSW) process introduced by
31
This univariate formulation was extended to model localised linear dependencies between pairs of nonstationary series by
35
, and later generalised to larger, unconstrained systems via the Multivariate Locally Stationary Wavelet (MvLSW) framework by
32
.

Recently, a research direction in the statistical literature has been focussed on modelling multivariate time series structured by a known graph topology. In particular, parsimonious time-domain models—most notably Generalised Network Autoregressive (GNAR) processes
(
23
;
24
)
—regularise high-dimensional interactions via node neighbourhood parametrisations, a paradigm recently extended to accommodate time-varying parameters and latent groupings
(
7
;
28
)
. However, models for nonstationary time series which explicitly capturing time-varying, scale-dependent cross dependencies while honouring the underlying graph topology are lacking.

To bridge this gap, this article introduces Locally Stationary Wavelet processes on Networks (
Net-LSW
), a new framework for modelling multi-scale, time-varying dependencies that explicitly incorporates the network structure. Unlike traditional unconstrained multivariate approaches, the Net-LSW process restricts the node-specific amplitudes to a diagonal transfer function matrix and encodes the graph architecture into the covariance structure of the process’s random innovation increments. By embedding the network topology into these internal innovation mechanics, our framework perfectly aligns the time-varying, scale-dependent spectral properties of the system with the physical network architecture.

A primary theoretical contribution of this article is the formal connection between these network-structured innovations and the foundational time-series graphical modelling literature established by
11
. By shifting from the classical stationary Fourier paradigm to our localised wavelet domain model, we introduce a generalised notion of a
local partial correlation graph
. This multi-scale network formulation complements recent process-wide conditional specifications designed for stationary series
(
3
)
, as well as infinite-dimensional operator representations used to study nonstationary graph structures
(
2
)
. Crucially, we establish a fundamental structural equivalence under the Net-LSW framework: the absence of an edge between nodes in the underlying physical network exactly corresponds to a structural zero entry in the associated localised network precision matrix across all wavelet scales and rescaled time points. This direct mathematical alignment allows the inverse evolutionary wavelet spectral domain to be uniquely interpreted as the time-varying reflection of the graph architecture.

To estimate the local cross-nodal dependence quantities under the proposed model, our approach differs fundamentally from both traditional time series graphical models and conventional multivariate wavelet estimation. In the classical time and Fourier domains, tracking nonstationary conditional dependencies requires inverting infinite-dimensional covariance operators
(
2
;
26
, e.g.,)
, a task that is intractable in practice. Conversely, traditional estimation methods for (unconstrained) nonstationary wavelet models apply a joint linear matrix correction across all scales simultaneously
(
32
)
. However, this multi-scale mixing contaminates the network’s topology constraints, meaning that enforced structural zeros in the raw parameters do not map to structural zeros in the final inverse evolutionary spectrum. Drawing intuition from
38
, who introduced a subprocess-based approach in the context of multi-scale wavelet coherence estimation, we resolve this topological misalignment by developing a network-constrained scale-by-scale estimation procedure. This approach enables us to formulate a finite-dimensional, convex, network-penalised optimisation problem for each individual scale. We establish the asymptotic mean-squared consistency of our local smoothed subprocess spectral estimators and prove that optimisation via graphical covariance selection
(
19
)
yields unique, consistent network topology estimators. Finally, since latent subprocesses are unobservable in practice, we introduce a robust projection method to reconstruct them from the aggregate observed coefficients, proving that (asymptotically) their underlying covariance structures match.

We illustrate the utility of our proposed framework by analysing the time-scale dependency structures of daily stock price volatilities across a high-dimensional network of major international banking institutions. In this context, financial networks are conventionally defined by computing directional spillovers via generalised forecast error variance decompositions (GFEVD) in the time domain
(
14
;
12
)
. Using our framework with a GFEVD connectedness graph, we show that direct, cross-bank dependencies shift dynamically across both time windows and wavelet scales, capturing clear structural changes during the Brexit referendum, the COVID-19 pandemic, and global macro-financial shocks.

The remainder of this article is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we establish our network notation and provide a review of the necessary LSW and MvLSW background material. Section
3
details the formal mathematical formulation of the novel Net-LSW process and its essential properties. In Section
4
, we propose the wavelet-domain Local Partial Correlation Graph and prove the structural equivalence between missing network edges and zeros in the localised precision matrix. Section
5
presents our subprocess-based inference framework and establishes its asymptotic consistency theory. Section
6
evaluates finite-sample performance via controlled numerical simulations, and Section
7
demonstrates an application of the framework to real-world data. All technical proofs and auxiliary derivations are deferred to the Appendix.

2
Background

In this section we introduce the pertinent theoretical background for the techniques proposed in this article. We start by providing an overview of the locally stationary wavelet modelling framework.

2.1
Network and Data Framework

In what follows, we assume that the data are observed on a network, here understood as a connected, static graph. We denote the node set as
{\mathcal{N}}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
nodes connected by a set of
m
edges, denoted by
{\mathcal{E}}
. Let the neighbourhood or connection set of node
p
be denoted by
\text{ne}(p)=\{q\in{\mathcal{N}}:(p,q)\in{\mathcal{E}}\}
. The entire network is formally characterised by the graph
{\mathcal{G}}=({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{E}})
, where
{\mathcal{E}}=\cup_{p\in{\mathcal{N}}}\text{ne}(p)
.

The primary scope of this work is to build a framework for the modelling and subsequent analysis of second-order nonstationary processes recorded through time at each node of this known, fixed network
{\mathcal{G}}
. We denote these localised time series as
\{X_{t}^{(p)}\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
, where
p\in{\mathcal{N}}
indexes the network nodes and
t=0,\ldots,T-1
represents discrete time.

The complete collection of these process observations across the network nodes is written as
\{\mathbf{X}_{t}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
, where the vector
{\mathbf{X}}^{{\mathcal{N}}}_{t}
collects the observed values across all nodes
{\mathcal{N}}
at a specific time index
t
. We drop the superscript (
N
) when the time series are treated simply as a standard multivariate process, where no explicit (network) structure is assumed.

2.2
Brief Review of Locally Stationary Wavelet (LSW) Processes

The foundation for multivariate time-varying dependence modelling using wavelets lies in the bivariate locally stationary wavelet process, introduced by
35
. This model extended the univariate LSW framework of
31
to a pair of nonstationary time series,
\{(X_{t}^{(p)},X_{t}^{(q)})\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
, for the purpose of estimating time- and scale-dependent linear dependence between the series. The process is defined such that its second-order structure is characterised by the evolutionary wavelet cross-spectrum
\{S_{j}^{(p,q)}(z)\}_{j}
and the evolutionary wavelet spectra (EWS)
\{S_{j}^{(p)}(z)\}_{j}
and
\{S_{j}^{(q)}(z)\}_{j}
for the individual series, across scales
j=1,\ldots,J=\log_{2}(T)
and rescaled times
z=\frac{k}{T}\in(0,1)
.

A key dependence measure between channels
p
and
q
was introduced in the bivariate LSW setting
(
35
)
to be the locally stationary wavelet coherence at scale
j
and rescaled time
z
,

\rho_{j}^{(p,q)}\left(z\right)=\frac{S^{(p,q)}_{j}\left(z\right)}{\left\{S_{j}^{(p)}\left(z\right)S_{j}^{(q)}\left(z\right)\right\}^{1/2}}.

This measure provides the correlation between the innovations of the two processes at a given time and scale, taking values in
[-1,1]
and having the usual linear dependence interpretation.

The multivariate locally stationary wavelet (MvLSW) framework was developed by
32
as a generalisation to model a collection of
n
nonstationary signals,
\left\{\mathbf{X}_{t}\right\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
, where
\mathbf{X}_{t}
is an
n\times 1
vector of observations. The MvLSW process
\{\mathbf{X}_{t}\}
has a representation

\mathbf{X}_{t}=\sum_{j}\sum_{k}\mathbf{V}_{j}\left(\frac{k}{T}\right)\psi_{j,k}(t)\bm{\xi}_{j,k},

where
\mathbf{V}_{j}(\frac{k}{T})
is an
n\times n
matrix of transfer functions that embeds the spectral and cross-spectral structure;
\{\mathbf{\xi}_{j,k}\}_{j,k}
is a multivariate vector of zero-mean, unit variance, uncorrelated random increments, and
\{\psi_{j,k}\}_{j,k}
is a set of discrete non-decimated wavelets of
31
.

The second-order structure is governed by the collection across scales
j
and rescaled times
z=\frac{k}{T}
of
n\times n
Local Wavelet Spectral (LWS) matrices,
\bm{S}_{j}(z)
, defined as

\bm{S}_{j}\left(z\right)=\mathbf{V}_{j}\left(z\right)\mathbf{V}_{j}\left(z\right)^{\top},

(1)

and whose diagonal elements,
S_{j}^{(p,p)}(\cdotp)
, are the EWS for channel
p
, and the off-diagonal elements,
S_{j}^{(p,q)}(\cdotp)
, are the evolutionary wavelet cross-spectra between channels
p
and
q
. The spectral quantities are in a one-to-one correspondence with their time-domain counterparts, namely the localised process covariance. The localised (cross-)covariance between channels
p
and
q
at rescaled time
z
and lag
\tau
is

c^{(p,q)}\left(z,\tau\right)=\sum_{j}S_{j}^{(p,q)}\left(z\right)\Psi_{j}(\tau),\,\forall z,\,\tau,

(2)

where
\Psi_{jl}(\cdotp)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\psi_{j}(n)\psi_{l}(n+\cdotp)
is the cross-scale autocorrelation wavelet, here expressed at single scale
j=l
(see
22
for their detailed properties). When
p=q
, (
2
) gives the channel
p
localised autocovariance.
Unlike the bivariate LSW framework of
35
, which partitions the dependence structure between the transfer functions and the process innovations, the MvLSW framework of
32
encapsulates the entire spectral structure (including cross-channel dependence) within the transfer function matrices of process ‘amplitudes’,
\{\mathbf{V}_{j}(\frac{k}{T})\}_{j,k}
. This key distinction permits the straightforward estimation of a wavelet partial coherence and leads to a computationally efficient estimation scheme.

The MvLSW framework provides two key spectral-domain measures of cross-channel dependence (
n\times n
matrices) derived from the wavelet spectrum in (
1
):

1.

Wavelet coherence,
\{\bm{\rho}_{j}(\frac{k}{T})\}_{j,k}
: This is a direct extension of the bivariate coherence and captures the overall linear dependence between any pair of signals, potentially including indirect associations.

2.

Wavelet partial coherence,

\{\bm{\eta}_{j}(\frac{k}{T})\}_{j,k}
: This measure quantifies the direct linear association between two channels,
p
and
q
, by removing the linear effects of all other observed signals.

We next propose the
Net-LSW
process that, unlike the MvLSW model of
32
, defines
diagonal
transfer function matrices,
\mathbf{W}_{j}^{{\mathcal{N}}}(\frac{k}{T})
, coupled with a
non-identity covariance of the random increments
,
\bm{\xi}_{j,k}^{{\mathcal{N}}}
. This allows for the natural embedding of the
known
graph topology and directly translates the cross-nodal dependence structure in the edge set.

3
Novel Nonstationary Process over the Nodes of a Network (Net-LSW)

We now give details of our modelling approach for the nonstationary process observed on a
network. We represent nonstationarities in the data by adopting a locally stationary wavelet
(LSW) modelling approach, as introduced by
31
, and adapt it to acknowledge the correlational structure between processes at different nodes. (See also
10
for a summary of locally stationary wavelet modelling,
35
for bivariate LSW processes and
32
for multivariate LSW processes.)

Definition 3.1

(
Net-LSW process).

A sequence of stochastic processes
\{\mathbf{X}_{t}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
is a zero-mean network locally stationary wavelet process over the nodes of a network
{\mathcal{G}}
(Net-LSW), if it admits the following representation in mean-square sense

\mathbf{X}_{t}^{{\mathcal{N}}}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbf{W}_{j}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\left(\frac{k}{T}\right)\psi_{j,k}(t)\bm{\xi}_{j,k}^{{\mathcal{N}}},

(3)

where

•

\{\psi_{j,k}(t)\}_{j,k}
is a discrete non-decimated wavelet family with elements across scales
j=1,\ldots,J=\log_{2}(T)
and times
k
,

•

\mathbf{W}_{j}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\left(z\right)=\left[\mbox{diag}\left(W^{(p,p)}_{j}\left(z\right)\right)\right]_{p\in{\mathcal{N}}}
is an
n\times n
diagonal matrix of individual-node amplitude (transfer) functions at scale
j
and rescaled time
z=\frac{k}{T}
. The transfer functions are assumed to be Lipschitz continuous in
z
, with Lipschitz constants
\{L_{j}^{(p,p)}\}_{j}
collectively admitting
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{j}L_{j}^{(p,p)}<\infty
for each node
p
.

•

\{\bm{\xi}_{j,k}^{\mathcal{N}}\}_{j,k}
is a jointly multivariate normal random sequence of
n\times 1
(column) zero-mean innovation vectors satisfying Properties
3.1
,
3.2
and
3.3
below.

Property 3.1
.

As the proposed model seeks to concomitantly reflect the nonstationarity in the process at each node and the multiscale cross-correlation between signals recorded at different nodes, we define the covariance structure of the increments
\{\bm{\xi}_{j,k}^{\mathcal{N}}\}_{j,k}
to be for any nodes
p,\,q\in{\mathcal{N}}
and time
k
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{cov}(\xi_{j,k}^{(p)},\xi_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}^{(p)})=\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}\delta_{k,k^{\prime}},\mbox{ for any scales }j,\,j^{\prime},

\displaystyle\operatorname{cov}(\xi_{j,k}^{(p)},\xi_{j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}^{(q)})=\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}\lambda_{j}^{(p,q)}\left(\frac{k}{T}\right),

where the functions of rescaled time
\lambda_{j}^{(p,q)}(\cdotp)
are assumed to be Lipschitz continuous through time, and
|\lambda_{j}^{(p,q)}(\cdotp)|\leq 1
with
\lambda_{j}^{(p,p)}(\cdotp)=1
ensuring unit nodal increment variance, across all scales
j
.

Property 3.2
.

At each scale
j
and rescaled time
z
, we define the
n\times n
process increment covariance matrix

\mathbf{\Lambda}_{j}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\left(z\right)=\left[\lambda_{j}^{(p,q)}\left(z\right)\right]_{p,q\in{\mathcal{N}}}
, whose diagonal elements are by convention
\lambda_{j}^{(p,p)}\left(z\right)=1,\forall p\in{\mathcal{N}}
. We denote the
n\times n
increment concentration matrix by
\mathbf{\Gamma}_{j}^{{\mathcal{N}}}(z)=\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{j}^{{\mathcal{N}}}(z)\right)^{-1}
.

The following property formally links the network topology to the concentration matrix of the process increments, providing a multiscale characterization of the graph.

Property 3.3
.

The process structure reflects the underlying network graph
{\mathcal{G}}
such that the absence of an edge,
(p,q)\notin{\mathcal{E}}
, corresponds directly to a zero entry in the increment concentration matrix,

\left[\mathbf{\Gamma}_{j}^{{\mathcal{N}}}(z)\right]_{p,q}=0,\quad\text{for all }j,z.

(4)

We now introduce a time–scale specific edge decomposition of process dependence in our network time series, to which we will refer as the
network cross-spectrum
.

Definition 3.2
.

We define the evolutionary Net-LSW cross-spectrum at scale
j
and rescaled time
z
as the
n\times n
symmetrical and positive semi-definite matrix
\bm{S}_{j}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\left(z\right)=\left[S_{j}^{(p,q)}\left(z\right)\right]_{p,q\in{{\mathcal{N}}}}
, as

\bm{S}_{j}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\left(z\right)=\mathbf{W}_{j}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\left(z\right)\mathbf{\Lambda}_{j}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\left(z\right)\mathbf{W}_{j}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\left(z\right).

(5)

Using the diagonal structure of the amplitude matrix, we explicitly obtain the Net-LSW cross-spectrum associated to nodes
p
and
q

S^{(p,q)}_{j}\left(z\right)=W^{(p,p)}_{j}\left(z\right)\lambda^{(p,q)}_{j}\left(z\right)W^{(q,q)}_{j}\left(z\right).

(6)

5
to consider a transformed version of the spectrum, to exploit its theoretical properties. We introduce this rephrased spectrum next.

Definition 3.3
.

We define the evolutionary Net-LSW
\bm{\beta}
-spectrum at scale
j

z=\frac{k}{T}

n\times n
matrix
\bm{\beta}_{j}^{{\mathcal{N}}}(z)=\left[\beta_{j}^{(p,q)}(z)\right]_{p,q\in{{\mathcal{N}}}}

\bm{\beta}_{j}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\left(z\right)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{J}A_{j,\ell}\bm{S}_{\ell}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\left(z\right),

.

We define the localised cross-covariance between processes at nodes
p

c^{(p,q)}\left(z,\tau\right)=\sum_{j}S_{j}^{(p,q)}\left(z\right)\Psi_{j}(\tau),\,\forall k,\,\tau.

.

For a Net-LSW process

Proposed Local Partial Correlation Graph

We next propose a generalised notion for the partial correlation graph of

Remark 2

(
Notation convention).

Throughout this development, we shall follow the locally stationary paradigm that enhances the process quantities by localising them at rescaled times. As in Section
3

\{\mathbf{X}^{{\mathcal{N}}}_{t}\}

To emphasise the graphical aspect of the data and model, we also establish a matrix-based notation. For any fixed nodal pair

\mathbf{C}(z,\tau)=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\mathbf{A}(z,\tau)&\mathbf{B}(z,\tau)\\

where

.

For a Net-LSW process

5
Net-LSW (Partial) Coherence Estimation: a Subprocess Approach

To establish an estimation methodology for the Net-LSW process, we exploit the fact that the network structure is encoded in the inverse spectral density matrix
\bm{\Theta}=\bm{S}^{-1}
, as established in Section
4.2
. Since the network
{\mathcal{G}}
is assumed to be known, the locations of the zero entries in
\bm{\Theta}
corresponding to the complement of the edge set
{\mathcal{E}}
are known
a priori
. This information can, in principle, be incorporated into the estimation procedure via appropriate regularization.

An initial natural approach to
network-agnostic
estimation amounts to relying on the associated wavelet coefficients
\{\mathbf{d}_{j,k}\}_{j,k}
of the observed
multivariate
process
\{\mathbf{X}_{t}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
. Under a multivariate MvLSW framework with Gaussian innovations and uniformly absolutely summable covariance functions, these coefficients satisfy asymptotically in
T\to\infty

(
25
)

\mathbf{d}_{j,k}\dot{\sim}N_{n}\!\left(\mathbf{0},\bm{\beta}_{j}(k/T)\right),

(16)

where
\{\bm{\beta}_{j}(k/T)\}_{j}
denotes the scale-dependent rephrased wavelet spectra, obtained as in equation (
7
) and (uniquely) connected to the wavelet spectra
\{\bm{S}_{j}(k/T)\}_{j}
through

\bm{S}_{j}\left(k/T\right)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{J}{A}^{-1}_{j,\ell}\bm{\beta}_{\ell}\left(k/T\right),

(17)

where
A^{-1}_{j,\ell}
is the
(j,l)
entry of the inverse inner product operator matrix of
31
.

Stemming from the distribution of the wavelet coefficients in (
16
), one might envisage directly using a typical likelihood-based constrained optimisation formulation with the aim of enforcing the known edge set pattern
E
(see
19
for the a multivariate version lacking the time-series aspect;
21
for a Fourier spectral-domain version for stationary time series). However, such an approach would instead yield the known zeroes in the rephrased inverse spectrum,
\bm{\beta}_{j}^{-1}(k/T)
, and (
17
) highlights that these enforced zero entries would not map to zero entries in the target inverse spectral matrix,
\bm{S}_{j}^{-1}(k/T)
, as needed and indicated by the theoretical evidence in Proposition
4.2
.

Hence we propose a subprocess-based estimation strategy which offers a spectrum
\bm{S}
-targeted approach, as we shall next see. The proofs of the results in this section appear in Appendix
C
.

5.1
Subprocess-based Construction

The subprocess construction has been introduced by
38
in the context of a locally stationary multivariate process augmented with cross-scale interactions. From a multivariate viewpoint, such cross-scale interactions are absent in our setup here, but the interactions between series in our modelling setup is network-informed. For our model, the subprocesses and their corresponding wavelet coefficients are defined in a scale-by-scale manner, as follows.

Rather than operating on the full process, we focus on scale-specific subprocesses, substantially simplifying the relationship between their associated
\bm{\beta}
- and
\bm{S}
-spectra. As a result, the Gaussian likelihood
(
16
)
remains convex when augmented with network-informed penalties on
\bm{S}_{j}^{-1}(k/T)
, yielding a tractable and stable estimation procedure.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\{\mathbf{X}_{t}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\}
be a Net-LSW process as in Definition
3.1
. The
scale-
j
Net-LSW subprocess

\{\mathbf{X}_{j,t}\}
is the component of the signal that exists exclusively at resolution
j
, defined by

\mathbf{X}_{j,t}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{W}^{{\mathcal{N}}}_{j}(k/T)\,\psi_{j,k}(t)\,\bm{\xi}_{j,k}^{{\mathcal{N}}}.

Viewed as a process itself, the scale-
j
subprocess

\{\mathbf{X}_{j,t}\}_{t}
has a local covariance function that is defined as
c_{j}^{(p,q)}(z,\tau)=S_{j}^{(p,q)}(z){\Psi}_{j}(\tau)
, and using techniques akin to
38
, it can be shown to approximate the process covariance,
\left|\operatorname{cov}(X_{j,[zT]}^{(p)},X_{j,[zT]+\tau}^{(q)})-c_{j}^{(p,q)}(z,\tau)\right|=\mathcal{O}(2^{-j}T^{-1})
.

Definition 5.2
.

The
scale
j
-subprocess wavelet coefficient

\{\mathbf{d}_{\ell,k}^{j}\}_{\ell,k}
is the vector obtained by applying the non-decimated wavelet transform at scales
\ell=1,\ldots,J
and time-location
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
to the isolated subprocess
\{\mathbf{X}_{j,t}\}
, namely

\mathbf{d}_{\ell,k}^{j}=\sum_{t}\mathbf{X}_{j,t}\,\psi_{\ell,k}(t),\,\forall\ell,\,k.

Remark 5
.

The superscript
j
identifies the subprocess corresponding to resolution
j
, while the subscripts
(\ell,k)
denote the scale and location indices of the wavelet transform applied to that subprocess.

Remark 6
.

Note that
\mathbf{X}^{\mathcal{N}}_{t}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathbf{X}_{j,t}
follows naturally from the Net-LSW process and subprocess definitions. Hence the non-decimated wavelet coefficient of the full process at scale
\ell
and location
k
satisfies

\mathbf{d}_{\ell,k}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathbf{d}_{\ell,k}^{j}
due to the linearity of the wavelet transform.

5.1.1
Distribution of Subprocess Wavelet Coefficients

To establish a tractable likelihood for the Net-LSW process, we derive the distribution of the subprocess wavelet coefficients for each scale
j
. The scale-
j
subprocess in Definition
5.1
is analysed using a non-decimated wavelet transform, and combining this with the formal Definition
5.2
we obtain the coefficients at an arbitrary analysis scale
\ell
and location
k

\mathbf{d}_{\ell,k}^{j}=\sum_{k^{\prime}}\left(\Psi_{j,\ell}(k^{\prime}-k)\mathbf{W}^{{\mathcal{N}}}_{j}(k^{\prime}/T)\right)\,\bm{\xi}_{j,k^{\prime}}^{{\mathcal{N}}}.

(18)

The statistical properties and asymptotic distribution of these coefficients are formalised in the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\{\mathbf{X}_{j,t}\}
be the scale-
j
subprocess of a Net-LSW process satisfying Definition
3.1
.
Then, the subprocess wavelet coefficients
\mathbf{d}_{\ell,k}^{j}
have mean zero,

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{d}_{\ell,k}^{j}\right]=\mathbf{0},\quad\forall\ell,k,

(19)

and a covariance structure given by

\text{Cov}(\mathbf{d}_{\ell,k}^{j},\mathbf{d}_{\ell,k+\nu}^{j})=A_{j,\ell}^{(\nu)}\bm{S}_{j}(k/T)+\mathcal{O}(T^{-1}),

(20)

where
A_{j,\ell}^{(\nu)}=\sum_{\tau}\Psi_{j}(\tau)\Psi_{\ell}(\tau+\nu)
is the
(j,\ell)
-element of the shifted wavelet inner product matrix
\mathbf{A}^{(\nu)}

(
35
;
22
)
, and
\bm{S}_{j}(\cdotp)
is the wavelet spectral matrix of the original process
\{\mathbf{X}^{\mathcal{N}}_{t}\}
, at scale
j

Furthermore, the contemporaneous subprocess wavelet coefficients at the matching scale (
\ell=j

\nu=0
) asymptotically follow the multivariate normal distribution as
T\rightarrow\infty
for all
(j,k)
pairs

\mathbf{d}_{j,k}^{j}\dot{\sim}N_{n}\left(\mathbf{0},A_{j,j}\bm{S}_{j}(k/T)\right).

(21)

Proof.

See Appendix
C.1
for the proof, which manipulates the scale-
j
subprocess definition coupled with the random vector increment properties. This result highlights that the spectral matrix
\bm{S}_{j}(k/T)
can now be viewed as a (scaled) covariance matrix, a key property we shall use in Section

5.2
Subprocess-based Wavelet Periodogram

For a given scale
j
, we define the raw subprocess wavelet periodogram at time
k
as

\mathbf{I}_{j,k}=\mathbf{d}_{j,k}^{j}\left(\mathbf{d}_{j,k}^{j}\right)^{\top},

(22)

where we note that since the scale-
j
subprocess is active at resolution level
j
, the corresponding wavelet coefficients have been chosen to match that level.

5.3
Smoothing and Consistency

To obtain a consistent estimator for the local wavelet spectrum
\bm{S}_{j}(k/T)
, we reduce the variance of the raw periodogram in (
22

\widehat{\bm{S}}_{j}(k/T)=\frac{1}{A_{j,j}(2M_{T}+1)}\sum_{m=-M_{T}}^{M_{T}}\mathbf{I}_{j,k+m},

(23)

where
M_{T}
is a smoothing bandwidth chosen such that
M_{T}\to\infty
and
M_{T}/T\to 0
as

. Denoting the scale-

subprocess wavelet coefficient entry corresponding to node

and scale-time pair

by

, the cross-nodal spectral activity for any two nodes

is given by the
(p,q)
-element of this matrix, explicitly written as

\widehat{S}_{j}^{(p,q)}(k/T)=\frac{1}{A_{j,j}(2M_{T}+1)}\sum_{m=-M_{T}}^{M_{T}}d_{j,k+m}^{j,(p)}d_{j,k+m}^{j,(q)}.

(

Let

be a Net-LSW process as in Definition

Proof.

The proof in Appendix

5.4
Constrained Likelihood and Node-Wise Estimation

Using the distributional property of the true wavelet subprocess coefficients in (

), the Gaussian log-likelihood for the scale-

subprocess precision is (up to a proportionality constant)

\mathcal{L}(\bm{\Theta}_{j}(z))=\log\operatorname{det}\bm{\Theta}_{j}(z)-\text{Tr}(\widehat{\bm{S}}_{j}(z)\bm{\Theta}_{j}(z)).

Under the Net-LSW framework, the sparsity of

follows the edge set

. Thus, to enforce the network structure, we define the constrained objective function that employs the spectral estimator in (

\mathcal{L}(\bm{\Theta}_{j}(z))=\log\operatorname{det}\bm{\Theta}_{j}(z)-\text{Tr}(\widehat{\bm{S}}_{j}(z)\bm{\Theta}_{j}(z))-\sum_{(p,q)\notin{\mathcal{E}}}l^{(p,q)}_{j}\Theta^{(p,q)}_{j}(z),

(24)

which will be optimised using the paradigm proposed by
19

\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}_{j}^{*}(z)
and the corresponding
\widehat{\bm{S}}_{j}^{*}(z)
at each

.

Let

5.5
Subprocess Approximation via Wavelet projection

In practice, the latent subprocesses
\{\mathbf{X}_{j,t}\}
introduced in Definition
5.1
are not directly observable. Instead, the available data consists of the multivariate process
\{\mathbf{X}_{t}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\}_{t=0}^{T-1}
which, under the Net-LSW structural assumption, connected to its scale-specific components by
\mathbf{X}^{{\mathcal{N}}}_{t}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathbf{X}_{j,t}
. Consequently, performing scale-dependent network inference requires a robust approximation of these latent components from the observed signal.

To isolate the subprocess in practice, an approximation
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}_{j,t}
is constructed via a synthesis of raw analysis coefficients
\mathbf{d}_{j,m}=\sum_{s}\mathbf{X}^{{\mathcal{N}}}_{s}\psi_{j,m}(s)
. Specifically, we approximate the subprocess
\mathbf{X}_{j,t}
by

\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}_{j,t}=\sum_{m}2^{-j}\mathbf{d}_{j,m}\psi_{j,m}(t).

By reordering the summations, the internal term collapses into the autocorrelation wavelet, yielding

\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}_{j,t}=\sum_{s}2^{-j}\mathbf{X}^{{\mathcal{N}}}_{s}\left(\sum_{m}\psi_{j,m}(s)\psi_{j,m}(t)\right)=\sum_{s}2^{-j}\mathbf{X}^{{\mathcal{N}}}_{s}\Psi_{j}(t-s).

(26)

Recalling that the network process may be additively recovered from the (true) subprocesses, i.e.
\mathbf{X}^{{\mathcal{N}}}_{t}=\sum_{j}\mathbf{X}_{j,t}
, we note that this property also holds for the approximated subprocesses, since

\sum_{j}\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}_{j,t}=\sum_{s}\mathbf{X}^{{\mathcal{N}}}_{s}\sum_{j}\left(2^{-j}\Psi_{j}(t-s)\right)=\sum_{s}\mathbf{X}^{{\mathcal{N}}}_{s}\delta_{t-s,0}=\mathbf{X}^{{\mathcal{N}}}_{t},

using the property
\sum_{j}2^{-j}\Psi_{j}(\tau)=\delta_{\tau,0}

(
31
)
.

We subsequently obtain the wavelet coefficients of the approximated subprocess, denoted
\widetilde{\mathbf{d}}_{\ell,k}^{j}
, by re-analysing the approximated subprocess at analysis scale
\ell

\widetilde{\mathbf{d}}_{\ell,k}^{j}=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}_{j,t}\psi_{\ell,k}(t).

(27)

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\{\mathbf{X}_{t}^{{\mathcal{N}}}\}
be a Net-LSW process as in Definition
3.1

26
) and (
27
), respectively. Under the assumptions of uniform local process and subprocess (auto- and cross-) covariance absolute summability, and with

\widetilde{\mathbf{d}}_{j,k}^{j}\dot{\sim}N_{n}\left(\mathbf{0},A_{j,j}\bm{S}_{j}(k/T)\right).

(28)

Proof.

See Appendix
C.4
.
∎

.

6
Simulation Study

To evaluate the finite-sample performance of the proposed Net-LSW framework, we conduct a comprehensive simulation study. The objective is twofold: (1) to assess the model’s ability to recover multi-scale, nonstationary dependence structures constrained by a network topology, and (2) to evaluate the scalability and robustness of our framework under several configurations.

6.1
Data Generating Process

To simulate a Net-LSW process, a time-series realisation
\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_{t}^{{\mathcal{N}}}
is synthesised through a hierarchical multi-scale procedure. Here,
\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_{t}^{{\mathcal{N}}}
denotes a finite realisation of a true underlying Net-LSW process
\mathbf{X}_{t}^{{\mathcal{N}}}
as specified by the properties in Section
3.1
. Using the subprocess-based representation described in Section
5.1
, the simulated Net-LSW process
\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_{t}^{{\mathcal{N}}}
is a superposition of independent subprocesses across a set of active scales
\mathcal{J}
(Remark 6),

\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_{t}^{{\mathcal{N}}}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_{j,t}.

To thoroughly examine the flexibility of our setup, we consider different numbers of active resolution levels and nonstationary parameter modulations. The generation of each scale-specific component
\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_{j,t}
follows a four-stage simulation pipeline:

1.

Network-constrained precision dynamics:
Let
\mathcal{G}=({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{E}})
be a network topology under consideration. For each scale
j
and rescaled time
z=k/T
, we define a time-varying spectral precision matrix
\widehat{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{j}(z)
. The sparsity pattern of
\widehat{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{j}(z)
is strictly constrained by the network’s adjacency, ensuring that
[\widehat{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{j}(z)]_{p,q}=0
if
(p,q)\notin{\mathcal{E}}
for
p\neq q
.

We allocate different nonstationary activity types to control the temporal evolution of the off-diagonal strength parameter
\theta_{j}(z)
at each scale
j\in\mathcal{J}
:

•

Transient burst (Type 1):

\theta_{j}(z)=\begin{cases}0.8&\text{if }0.3\leq z\leq 0.4\\
0.1&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

•

Changepoint (Type 2):

\theta_{j}(z)=\begin{cases}0.1&\text{if }z<0.5\\
0.6&\text{if }z\geq 0.5\end{cases}

•

Linear trend (Type 3):

\theta_{j}(z)=0.2+0.3z\quad z\in[0,1]

•

Piecewise constant step function (Type 4):

\theta_{j}(z)=\begin{cases}-0.7&\text{if }z\leq 0.5\\
0.4&\text{if }z>0.5\end{cases}

•

Uniform CDF (linear transition) (Type 5):

\theta_{j}(z)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }z<0.25\\
2z-0.5&0.25\leq z\leq 0.75\\
1&\text{if }z>0.75\end{cases}

•

Cosine wave (Type 6):

\theta_{j}(z)=0.2\left[1+0.5\cos(2\pi z)\right]

The functional forms of these time-varying modulations are illustrated in Figure
1
.

Figure 1
:
Time-varying profiles for the off-diagonal strength parameter
\theta_{j}(z)
across different nonstationary modulation types.

In multiple-scale configurations, we mix these behaviors across active scales to test the model’s capacity to decouple distinct, overlapping dynamics.

2.

Generation of correlated innovations:
The local spectral density matrix is derived as the inverse of the simulated precision matrix,
\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{j}(z)=\left(\widehat{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{j}(z)\right)^{-1}
. From this matrix, we extract the innovation correlation structure,

\widehat{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{j}(z)=\text{diag}\left(\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{j}(z)\right)^{-1/2}\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{j}(z)\text{diag}\left(\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{j}(z)\right)^{-1/2}.

(33)

At each scale
j
and time point
k
, we sample a multivariate Gaussian innovation vector
\widehat{\bm{\xi}}_{j,k}
,

\widehat{\bm{\xi}}_{j,k}\sim N_{n}\left(\mathbf{0},\widehat{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{j}(z)\right).

3.

Latent subprocess construction:
The innovations are modulated by both the local spectral amplitude and a dyadic scaling factor. Let
\widehat{\mathbf{W}}_{j}(z)=\text{diag}\left(\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{j}(z)\right)^{1/2}
. For each network node
p\in{\mathcal{N}}
, the scaled innovations serve as the inputs to the synthesis step. The subprocess
\widehat{X}_{j,t}^{(p)}
is reconstructed using the
average basis
inverse transform
(
30
)

\widehat{X}_{j,t}^{(p)}=\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\widehat{\mathbf{W}}^{(p,\cdotp)}_{j}(k/T)\widehat{\bm{\xi}}_{j,k}\psi_{j,k}(t).

The simulated Net-LSW process
\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_{t}^{\mathcal{N}}
is then constructed by subprocess aggregation

\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_{t}^{\mathcal{N}}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_{j,t}.

This formulation guarantees that the simulated power at each resolution level matches the theoretical target spectrum while respecting the structural zeros imposed by
{\mathcal{E}}
.

Remark 7

(
Notational conventions).

Throughout this section,
\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_{t}^{\mathcal{N}}
refers exclusively to a simulated realisation of the Net-LSW process. This must be conceptually distinguished from the approximated subprocesses
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}_{j,t}
in Section
5.5
. This distinction forms the basis of our assessment framework: in practice, we treat the realised process
\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_{t}^{\mathcal{N}}
as the sole observed quantity. The estimation task consists of using
\widehat{\mathbf{X}}_{t}^{\mathcal{N}}
to isolate
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}_{j,t}
and recover spectral precision estimates,
\widetilde{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{j}(z)
, under the structural zeros of the fixed network
\mathcal{G}
. This is subsequently validated against the generative truth,
\widehat{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{j}(z)
.

6.2
Performance Evaluation Framework

To evaluate the finite-sample performance of the proposed estimation procedure (summarised in Algorithm
1
), we track its estimation fidelity against the truth.

For a given active scale
j
and time-series length
T
, let
R
denote the total number of independent simulation replicates. Let
\varphi^{(p,q)}_{j}(k/T)
represent a localised target dependence structure at scale
j
and time position
k
—denoting either the true local coherence
\rho^{(p,q)}_{j}(k/T)
or the true local partial coherence
\eta^{(p,q)}_{j}(k/T)
between node pair
(p,q)
. The corresponding estimator obtained from the
r
-th simulation replicate is denoted by
\widetilde{\varphi}^{(r);(p,q)}_{j}(k/T)
.

We assess the quality of the localised dependency measures using three statistical criteria calculated over the network topology: Mean Squared Error (
\mathrm{MSE}
), Squared Bias (
\mathrm{Bias}^{2}
), and Variance (
\mathrm{Var}
) at each scale
j\in\mathcal{J}
. To evaluate the accuracy across the entire network topology and time grid, the summary metrics are defined in a multi-scale manner, using the spatial-temporal expectation over all node paths
p,q\in{\mathcal{N}}
and discrete time points
k=0,\dots,T-1
, namely,

1.

Mean Squared Error (
\mathrm{MSE}
):

\mathrm{MSE}_{j}=\frac{1}{n^{2}\cdot T}\sum_{p=1}^{n}\sum_{q=1}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\left[\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\left(\widetilde{\varphi}^{(r);(p,q)}_{j}(k/T)-\varphi^{(p,q)}_{j}(k/T)\right)^{2}\right].

2.

Squared Bias (
\mathrm{Bias}^{2}
):

\mathrm{Bias}^{2}_{j}=\frac{1}{n^{2}\cdot T}\sum_{p=1}^{n}\sum_{q=1}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\left[\left(\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\widetilde{\varphi}^{(r);(p,q)}_{j}(k/T)\right)-\varphi^{(p,q)}_{j}(k/T)\right]^{2}.

3.

Variance (
\mathrm{Var}
):

\mathrm{Var}_{j}=\mathrm{MSE}_{j}-\mathrm{Bias}^{2}_{j}.

Across all settings, we evaluate the proposed Net-LSW framework against standard unconstrained multivariate LSW (
MvLSW
) estimation
(
32
)
, as well as benchmark performance against the
Oracle
that uses true subprocess information.

Remark 8

(
Implementation details and limitations).

The MvLSW estimates are obtained using the
MvLSW

R
package
(
37
)
, while the constrained optimisation step of the Net-LSW framework (described in Section
5.4
) is performed via the
glasso
package in
R

(
16
;
19
)
. A naive approach might also directly apply the constrained optimisation step to the MvLSW network-agnostic estimates. However, due to the cross-scale bias correction inherent to the multivariate locally stationary wavelet framework, their estimates in practice often suffer from non-positive definiteness (although theoretically this should hold). Consequently, the
glasso
function cannot accept these matrices as valid empirical covariance inputs (recall the underlying
glasso
algorithm relies on recursively partitioning the target matrix by iteratively isolating rows and columns to solve regression problems, as detailed in Algorithm
1
). Hence a non-positive-definite input matrix breaks the optimisation routine, making direct constrained optimisation on the MvLSW estimator impossible in practice. In contrast, the simple form of our proposed estimator that does not require correction renders it always positively definite, both theoretically and practically.

7
Empirical Application: High-Dimensional Global Banking Dynamics

In the previous section, we evaluated the performance of our proposed dependence estimation methodology against the baseline mvLSW alternative for a variety of simulated scenarios. We now consider an application related to modelling financial equity dynamics.
In particular, we study the localised dependence structure of global banks under the Net-LSW framework introduced in Section
3
. Because our framework takes an underlying network topology as a given input, we incorporate a pre-specified banking connectedness graph directly into the estimation of local spectral quantities. This allows us to examine how direct cross-bank dependencies evolve jointly across rescaled time and wavelet scales while strictly preserving the network’s structural zero constraints.

7.1
Data and Volatility Measure

The data we analyse consists of daily equity prices for
n=83
major global banking institutions observed from August 13, 2015 through December 29, 2023, with a sample size of
T=2048
observations. For each institution
i
and time index
t
, volatility is proxied using the range-based estimator of
18

\widetilde{\sigma}_{it}^{2}=0.511(H_{it}-L_{it})^{2}-0.019\Big[(C_{it}-O_{it})(H_{it}+L_{it}-2O_{it})-2(H_{it}-O_{it})(L_{it}-O_{it})\Big]-0.383(C_{it}-O_{it})^{2},

where
H_{it},L_{it},O_{it},C_{it}
are the logarithmic high, low, open, and close prices
(
12
)
.

The multivariate time series

\mathbf{X}_{t}=(X_{1,t},\dots,X_{n,t})^{\top}

is constructed by setting each element
X_{i,t}=\widetilde{\sigma}_{it}^{2}
. Applying the stationarity test of
29
via the
R

locits
package in rejects the null hypothesis of second-order stationarity for all nodal volatility series and justifies treating the resulting vector as a realisation from a multivariate locally stationary process.

7.2
The Global Banking Connectedness Network

Because the Net-LSW framework operates conditional on a known graph topology, an underlying network must be specified prior to spectral estimation. For this application, we obtain our input network by adopting the variance spillover framework of
12
.
More specifically, the time-domain vector autoregressive (VAR) procedure outlined below is used to construct the network. It aggregates historical directional spillovers to form a time-invariant graph architecture, separating the generation of the static graph input from the actual localised time-scale modelling of the nonstationary signals. Following
12
, the centered volatility time series are modelled using a high-dimensional regularised VAR, namely

\mathbf{X}_{t}=\sum_{l=1}^{p_{\text{VAR}}}\Phi_{l}\mathbf{X}_{t-l}+\bm{\varepsilon}_{t},\qquad\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}\sim N(0,\bm{\Sigma}),

(34)

estimated via Elastic Net regularization to accommodate the high dimensionality of the panel.

Using the moving average
(\text{MA}(\infty))
representation
\mathbf{X}_{t}=\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\Xi_{h}\bm{\varepsilon}_{t-h}
implied by the estimated VAR parameters, the generalised forecast error variance decomposition (GFEVD) is computed following
33
and computed using the
ConnectednessApproach

R
package of
17
. For a forecast horizon
H
, the pairwise directional connectedness from bank
q
to bank
p
is

\vartheta^{(p,q)}(H)=\frac{\left(\sigma_{q,q}\right)^{-1}\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\left(e_{p}^{\top}\Xi_{h}\bm{\Sigma}e_{q}\right)^{2}}{\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}e_{p}^{\top}\Xi_{h}\bm{\Sigma}\Xi_{h}^{\top}e_{p}},

(35)

where
e_{p}
and
e_{q}
are selection vectors, and
\sigma_{q,q}
is the
q
th diagonal element of
\bm{\Sigma}
. These directional measures are normalised row-wise to represent relative spillover proportions

\widetilde{\vartheta}^{(p,q)}=\frac{\vartheta^{(p,q)}(H)}{\sum_{q=1}^{n}\vartheta^{(p,q)}(H)}.

(36)

By construction, the GFEVD yields a dense matrix where almost all elements
\widetilde{\vartheta}^{(p,q)}>0
, implying a fully connected network. Rather than attempting to estimate a network data-generation process, our goal is to extract an informative, sparse subgraph that keeps the network connected but removes the weakest links. We achieve this by varying the threshold cutoff
\tau
and sequentially retaining edges in descending order of spillover intensity until the graph forms a connected giant component. The critical threshold
\tau_{\mathrm{crit}}
is defined as the maximum cutoff that prevents the graph from fragmenting, ensuring every pair of bank nodes can reach one another through intermediate pathways. The adjacency matrix is then defined as

\left[\mathbf{A}\right]_{p,q}=\mathbb{I}(\widetilde{\vartheta}^{(p,q)}\geq\tau_{\mathrm{crit}}).

(37)

The resulting graph
{\mathcal{G}}=({\mathcal{N}},{\mathcal{E}})
prescribes the structural network constraints (see Figure
5
) over whose nodes we model the data
\{\mathbf{X}_{t}\}
as a Net-LSW process and whose subsequent nonstationary local time-scale partial coherence structures are analysed.

8
Discussion

In this article, we have addressed the challenge of capturing multi-scale, time-varying linear cross-dependencies in nonstationary multivariate time series, structured by an underlying network topology. While traditional frameworks allow for (unconstrained) dependence estimation, they are network-agnostic and computationally expensive—estimating all pairwise dependencies as if the system were fully connected—thus leading to severe over-parametrization in high dimensions. In contrast, our Locally Stationary Wavelet process on Networks (Net-LSW) framework accommodates the network-induced topological constraints by encoding the existing edge connections directly into the formulation of our proposed model.

The theoretical contributions of this work are twofold. Firstly, we propose a local partial correlation graph which is able to characterise the graphical structure underpinning the observed nonstationary signal in the wavelet domain, in a way that mirrors the classical Fourier partial correlation graphs of
11
in the stationary setting. Specifically, the absence of a physical edge corresponds exactly to a structural zero in the network time-localised precision matrix
\bm{\Theta}_{j}(z)
across all scales
j
and rescaled times
z
. Secondly, we develop a computationally tractable scale-by-scale subprocess-based estimation procedure. By projecting the observed signal to (approximate) latent subprocesses, we are able to form a time-localised, multi-scale, convex network-penalised objective function. We establish that optimizing this objective via graphical covariance selection yields unique, mean-squared consistent estimators, extending classical estimation schemes to the locally stationary wavelet setting. The consistency properties of our estimators are strongly validated by our simulation studies.

In our modelling framework, the network topology is known and fixed across time. A direction for future research would relax the static network assumption, to facilitate time-varying edge inclusion, which would pose theoretical and computational challenges, e.g., requiring complex penalisation.

By definition, the covariance matrix of the subprocess wavelet coefficients is expressed as:

\displaystyle\text{Cov}(\mathbf{d}_{\ell,k}^{j},\mathbf{d}_{\ell,k+\eta}^{j})

3.1

Recalling the definition of the Net-LSW Local Wavelet Spectral (LWS) matrix

{\mathbb{E}}\,|{\zeta}_{j,k}^{j,(p)}|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(T^{-1})
at every node
p
. An application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality at nodes
p,\,q
therefore yields
{\mathbb{E}}({\zeta}_{j,k}^{j,(p)}{\zeta}_{j,k^{\prime}}^{j,(q)})=\mathcal{O}(T^{-1})
at every time
k
, hence we have
{\mathbb{E}}(B1)=\mathcal{O}\left(2^{-j}T^{-1}\right)
.

Since
B2=\widehat{{S}}_{j}^{(p,q)}(k/T)
, the second term is
{\mathbb{E}}(B2)=S_{j}^{(p,q)}(k/T)+\mathcal{O}\left(2^{-j}MT^{-1}\right)

from Proposition
5.2
.

Let us now turn to the last two terms of (
63
). From equation (
62
), we have

{\mathbb{E}}(\zeta_{j,k}^{j,(q)}d_{j,k}^{j,(p)})=\mathcal{O}(2^{j/2}T^{-1/2})
hence
{\mathbb{E}}(B3)=\mathcal{O}(2^{-j/2}T^{-1/2})
and similar for
{\mathbb{E}}(B4)
.
Substituting these terms back into (
63
), we obtain

{\mathbb{E}}(\widehat{\widetilde{S}}_{j}^{(p,q)}(k/T))=S_{j}^{(p,q)}(k/T)+\mathcal{O}(M^{\prime}_{T}),

(64)

where
M^{\prime}_{T}=2^{-j}MT^{-1}\vee 2^{-j/2}T^{-1/2}\rightarrow 0
when
M,\,T\rightarrow\infty
with
MT^{-1}\rightarrow 0
.

Moving onto second order quantities, the general terms that contribute to the variance of
\widehat{\widetilde{S}}_{j}^{(p,q)}(k/T)
are of the form

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big((\widehat{S}_{j}^{(p,q)}(k/T))^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{j,j}^{-2}}{(2M+1)^{2}}\sum\limits_{m=-M}^{M}\sum\limits_{\tau=-M-m}^{M-m}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\widetilde{d}_{j,k+m}^{j,(p)}\widetilde{d}_{j,k+m}^{j,(q)}\widetilde{d}_{j,k+m+\tau}^{j,(q)}\widetilde{d}_{j,k+m+\tau}^{j,(q)}\Big).

Following
20
, the above becomes

\displaystyle\operatorname{var}\Big(\widehat{\widetilde{S}}_{j}^{(p,q)}(k/T)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{j,j}^{-2}}{(2M+1)^{2}}\bigg\{\sum\limits_{m=-M}^{M}\sum\limits_{\tau}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\widetilde{d}_{j,k+m}^{j,(p)}\widetilde{d}_{j,k+m+\tau}^{j,(p)}\Big)\times{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\widetilde{d}_{j,k+m}^{j,(q)}\widetilde{d}_{jj,k+m+\tau}^{j,(q)}\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{m=-M}^{M}\sum\limits_{\tau}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\widetilde{d}_{j,k+m}^{j,(p)}\widetilde{d}_{j,k+m+\tau}^{j,(q)}\Big)\times\ {\mathbb{E}}\Big(\widetilde{d}_{j,k+m+\tau}^{j,(p)}\widetilde{d}_{j,k+m}^{j,(q)}\Big)\bigg\},\mbox{ then akin to~\eqref{eq:expapprox2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{j,j}^{-2}}{(2M+1)^{2}}\sum\limits_{m=-M}^{M}\bigg\{\sum\limits_{\tau}\Big(A_{jj}^{(\tau)}S_{j}^{(p,p)}(k/T)\Big)\times\Big(A_{jj}^{(\tau)}S_{j}^{(q,q)}(k/T)\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\tau}\Big(A_{jj}^{(\tau)}S_{j}^{(p,q)}(k/T)\Big)\times\Big(A_{jj}^{(-\tau)}S_{j}^{(p,q)}(k/T)\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\tau}(|m|+1)\mathcal{O}(M^{\prime\prime}_{T})+\sum\limits_{\tau}(|m|+1)^{2}\mathcal{O}((M^{\prime\prime}_{T})^{2})\bigg\},

where the last equality followed using
\widetilde{d}_{j,k}^{j,(p)}=\zeta_{j,k}^{j,(p)}+{d}_{j,k}^{j,(p)}
coupled with the properties
{\mathbb{E}}\Big(d_{j,k}^{j,(p)}d_{j,k+\tau}^{j,(q)}\Big)=A_{j,j}^{(\tau)}{S}_{j}^{(p,q)}(k/T)+\mathcal{O}(T^{-1})
in (
20
),
{\mathbb{E}}(\zeta_{j,k}^{j,(q)}d_{j,k+\tau}^{j,(p)})=\mathcal{O}(A_{j,j}^{1/2}T^{-1/2})
and
{\mathbb{E}}({\zeta}_{j,k}^{j,(p)}{\zeta}_{j,k+\tau}^{j,(q)})=\mathcal{O}(T^{-1})
above, thus leading to

{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\widetilde{d}_{j,k}^{j,(p)}\widetilde{d}_{j,k+\tau}^{j,(q)}\Big)=A_{j,j}^{(\tau)}{S}_{j}^{(p,q)}(k/T)+\mathcal{O}(M_{T}^{\prime\prime})
, with
M_{T}^{\prime\prime}=2^{j/2}T^{-1/2}
.

Similar arguments to those in the proof of Proposition
5.2
and
retaining highest order terms yield

\displaystyle\operatorname{var}\Big(\widehat{\widetilde{S}}_{j}^{(p,q)}(k/T)\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(M^{-1}\vee 2^{-3j/2}MT^{-1/2}\vee 2^{-j}M^{2}T^{-1}),

thus the proposed smoothed wavelet periodogram
\widehat{\widetilde{S}}_{j}^{(p,q)}(k/T)
is asymptotically mean-squared consistent for the true spectrum as
T\to\infty,M\to\infty,MT^{-1/2}\to 0
.
