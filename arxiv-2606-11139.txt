Abstract

Introduction

Restricted-intersection problems have long been a central topic in the study of extremal set theory and have found numerous striking applications. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\{1,2,\dots\}
, write
[n]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\{1,\dots,n\}
and let

denote the power set of
[n]
. As a classical example of a restricted-intersection result:

Suppose
p\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
is a prime, and let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be a family of
(2p-1)
-subsets of
[n]
. If no two distinct sets in
\mathcal{F}
intersect in exactly
p-1
elements, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n}{0}+\binom{n}{1}+\dots+\binom{n}{p}
.

This is a special case of the celebrated Frankl–Wilson theorem
[
13
]
. Remarkably, it yields exponentially large explicit constructions for diagonal Ramsey numbers; see
[
19
]
for an elegant exposition. Restricted-intersection results, including
[
8
,
13
,
2
]
, apply to distance-related coding theory problems; see
[
3
,
17
,
10
]
. We also refer to
[
12
]
for a comprehensive survey of restricted-intersection problems.

The oddtown and eventown problems provide two famous examples of restricted-intersections in which the constraints are imposed on the parities of set sizes and pairwise intersection sizes. Call
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
an
eventown
(resp.
oddtown

\mathcal{F}
has even (resp. odd) size, and the intersection of any two distinct sets in
\mathcal{F}
has even size. Berlekamp
[
4
]
and Graver
[
16
]
obtained sharp extremal bounds: every oddtown contains at most
n
sets, while every eventown contains at most

sets. The proofs are early paradigms of

in combinatorics. Recently, the oddtown problem has also found unexpected applications to multicolor Ramsey constructions
[

,
27

Consider a natural analogue—
\ell

[

[
5

gives further improved bounds.

The study of

The
{\bm{\alpha}}
-town problem.

For

In their pioneering study of

[

Wei, Zhang, and Ge
[

Determine the sharp asymptotic behavior of

The
{\bm{\alpha}}
-ville problem.

The
{\bm{\alpha}}
-town condition is inherently “asymmetric”, in the sense that it imposes parity constraints on intersection sizes but not on their complements. A natural variant is to impose parity constraints on “agreements” rather than “intersections”.

Specifically, we identify a set family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
with a collection of
\{0,1\}
-vectors
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n}
, where each set is identified with its characteristic vector. For example, the set
\{1,3,6\}\in 2^{[7]}
corresponds to the vector
(1,0,1,0,0,1,0)\in\{0,1\}^{7}
. In this language, “intersection” counts the number of common
1
’s, while “agreement” counts the number of coordinates on which all vectors agree (i.e., the total number of common
0
’s and common
1
’s). For
F_{1},\dots,F_{m}\in 2^{[n]}
, their agreement has cardinality

\Bigl|\bigl(F_{1}\cap\dots\cap F_{m}\bigr)\cup\bigl([n]\setminus(F_{1}\cup\dots\cup F_{m})\bigr)\Bigr|\equiv n+|F_{1}\cap\dots\cap F_{m}|+|F_{1}\cup\dots\cup F_{m}|\pmod{2}.

Hence, for any fixed
n
, prescribing the parity of agreement is equivalent to prescribing the parity of intersection-union
|F_{1}\cap\dots\cap F_{m}|+|F_{1}\cup\dots\cup F_{m}|
. This leads to the study of “ville”.

For
{\bm{\alpha}}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
, a set family
\mathcal{F}
is an
{\bm{\alpha}}
-
ville
if for
i=1,\dots,k
, the modular equality

|F_{1}\cap\dots\cap F_{i}|+|F_{1}\cup\dots\cup F_{i}|\equiv\alpha_{i}\pmod{2}

holds for any distinct
F_{1},\dots,F_{i}\in\mathcal{F}
. Denote by
g_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(n)
the maximum size of an
{\bm{\alpha}}
-ville
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
.

Does an analogue of “the highest-level principle” (see Theorem
3
) hold for
{\bm{\alpha}}
-ville? We do not know how to establish such a statement. However, in contrast to the polynomial growth of
f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(n)
in
n

Theorem

3

g_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(n)
is “often” bounded. To be precise, we introduce the extended set
{\widetilde{\mathbb{F}}}_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathbb{F}_{2}\cup\{*\}
, where
|F_{1}\cap\dots\cap F_{i}|+|F_{1}\cup\dots\cup F_{i}|\equiv*\pmod{2}
indicates that no restriction is imposed on
i
-wise agreements. Note that no addition operation is defined on
{\widetilde{\mathbb{F}}}_{2}^{k}
.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose

are integers with

{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell}^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\bigl(\underbrace{*,\dots,*}_{\ell-1},1,\underbrace{*,\dots,*}_{k-\ell}\bigr)\in{\widetilde{\mathbb{F}}}_{2}^{k}.

Then the sequence
\bigl(g_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell}^{k}}(n)\bigr)_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
is bounded if and only if

2

\binom{-2}{0}&\binom{-2}{1}&\binom{-2}{2}&\binom{-2}{3}&\binom{-2}{4}&\binom{-2}{5}\\
\binom{-1}{0}&\binom{-1}{1}&\binom{-1}{2}&\binom{-1}{3}&\binom{-1}{4}&\binom{-1}{5}\\
\binom{0}{0}&\binom{0}{1}&\binom{0}{2}&\binom{0}{3}&\binom{0}{4}&\binom{0}{5}\\
\binom{1}{0}&\binom{1}{1}&\binom{1}{2}&\binom{1}{3}&\binom{1}{4}&\binom{1}{5}\\
\binom{2}{0}&\binom{2}{1}&\binom{2}{2}&\binom{2}{3}&\binom{2}{4}&\binom{2}{5}\end{pmatrix}

So, it suffices to show for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}
that the matrix
{\bm{M}}_{a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\Bigl(\binom{i+a}{j-1}\bmod 2\Bigr)_{i,j\in[k]}
has full rank over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

We proceed by induction on
a
. Since
{\bm{M}}_{-1}
is lower triangular with diagonal entries
\binom{i-1}{i-1}=1
, the statement holds for
a=-1
. Suppose that
{\bm{M}}_{a}
is invertible. Fix
j
and regard a typical entry
\binom{x}{j-1}=\frac{x(x-1)\cdots(x-j+2)}{(j-1)!}
in the row indexed by
x
as a polynomial in
x
of degree at most
k-1
. For instance, the row
\Bigl(\binom{i+a}{j-1}\bmod 2\Bigr)_{j\in[k]}
is indexed by
x=i+a
. Applying the Lagrange interpolation formula at
a+1,\,a+2,\,\dots,\,a+k
and evaluating at
x=a
, we deduce that

\binom{a}{j-1}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\Biggl(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq t\leq k\\
t\neq s\end{subarray}}\frac{0-t}{s-t}\Biggr)\cdot\binom{a+s}{j-1}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}(-1)^{s-1}\binom{k}{s}\cdot\binom{a+s}{j-1}.

Reducing modulo
2
, the coefficient of
\binom{a+k}{j-1}
equals
1
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, and so
\Bigl(\binom{a}{0},\dots,\binom{a}{k-1}\Bigr)
, the row indexed by
x=a
, is a linear combination in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
of the rows indexed by
x=a+1,\,x=a+2,\,\dots,\,x=a+k
whose coefficient before the row indexed by
x=a+k
is
1
. Consequently, replacing the row indexed by
x=a+k
in matrix
{\bm{M}}_{a}
by the row indexed by
x=a
is an invertible elementary row operation in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Phrased differently, there exists
\varphi\in\operatorname{SL}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
such that
{\bm{M}}_{a-1}=\varphi\cdot{\bm{M}}_{a}
. It follows that the matrix
{\bm{M}}_{a-1}
is invertible. To summarize, the above deductions show that

•

if
a\in\mathbb{Z}
and
{\bm{M}}_{a}\in\operatorname{SL}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
(i.e.,
{\bm{M}}_{a}
is invertible), then
{\bm{M}}_{a-1}\in\operatorname{SL}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
.

The same argument as above also shows that

•

if
a\in\mathbb{Z}
and
{\bm{M}}_{a}\in\operatorname{SL}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
(i.e.,
{\bm{M}}_{a}
is invertible), then
{\bm{M}}_{a+1}\in\operatorname{SL}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
.

(Note that we only need
{\bm{M}}_{a}
to be invertible when
a
is negative for the purpose of proving
Lemma

2
, and so this is unnecessary.) The inductive proof is complete, and hence
{\bm{\Gamma}}_{k}
is invertible.

We thus conclude that
({\bm{\gamma}}^{1})_{[k]},\dots,({\bm{\gamma}}^{k})_{[k]}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
∎

Corollary 5
.

If
t\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
x\in\{2t,2t+1\}
, then vectors
({\bm{\gamma}}^{1})_{[2t]},\dots,({\bm{\gamma}}^{2t-1})_{[2t]},({\bm{\gamma}}^{x})_{[2t]}
span
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2t}
.

Proof.

The case
x=2t
is exactly
Lemma

2
. For the case
x=2t+1
, by definition,
({\bm{\gamma}}^{2t})_{j}\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}\binom{t-1}{j-1}
and
({\bm{\gamma}}^{2t+1})_{j}\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}\binom{-(t+1)}{j-1}
. We adopt the notations associated with
{\bm{\Gamma}}_{2t}
in the proof of
Lemma

2
. The row
({\bm{\gamma}}^{2t+1})_{[2t]}
indexed by
x=-(t+1)
is obtained from the rows
x=t-1,\,x=t-2,\,\dots,\,x=-t
via the same interpolation-based row-replacement, whose leading coefficient is
1
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Hence, the linear expansion of
({\bm{\gamma}}^{2t+1})_{[2t]}
in the basis
({\bm{\gamma}}^{1})_{[2t]},\dots,({\bm{\gamma}}^{2t})_{[2t]}
contains
({\bm{\gamma}}^{2t})_{[2t]}
with coefficient
1
. It follows that the vectors
({\bm{\gamma}}^{1})_{[2t]},\dots,({\bm{\gamma}}^{2t-1})_{[2t]},({\bm{\gamma}}^{2t+1})_{[2t]}
span
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2t}
as well.
∎

For any
{\bm{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\otimes\omega}
, recall that the shifted vector
\sigma({\bm{\gamma}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}(\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3},\dots)
. The following lemma will be useful in the upper bound proofs of
Theorem

3
.

Lemma 6
.

For every positive integer
i
, we have the identities of canonical vectors

\sigma({\bm{\gamma}}^{2i-1})={\bm{\gamma}}^{1}+{\bm{\gamma}}^{3}+\dots+{\bm{\gamma}}^{2i-1},\qquad\sigma({\bm{\gamma}}^{2i})={\bm{\gamma}}^{2}+{\bm{\gamma}}^{4}+\dots+{\bm{\gamma}}^{2i-2}.

In particular, the shifted vector
\sigma({\bm{\gamma}}^{k})
lies in the
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-span of canonical vectors
{\bm{\gamma}}^{1},\dots,{\bm{\gamma}}^{k}
for
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Notice that
\sigma({\bm{\gamma}}^{2})=\bm{0}
is consistent with the identity. Due to standard binomial identities,

\displaystyle\sigma({\bm{\gamma}}^{2i-1})_{j}=({\bm{\gamma}}^{2i-1})_{j+1}\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}\binom{i+j-1}{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}\binom{\ell+j-2}{j-1}\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}({\bm{\gamma}}^{2\ell-1})_{j},

\displaystyle\sigma({\bm{\gamma}}^{2i})_{j}=({\bm{\gamma}}^{2i})_{j+1}=\binom{i-1}{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{i-1}\binom{\ell-1}{j-1}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{i-1}({\bm{\gamma}}^{2\ell})_{j}.\qed

In addition to the algebraic properties of the canonical vectors
{\bm{\gamma}}^{1},{\bm{\gamma}}^{2},\dots
, we will also need a combinatorial tool, known as the
Partition–Sum Lemma

[
18
, Lemma 3]
, to establish
Theorem

3
. For the sake of self-containment, we also include the proof of Partition–Sum Lemma below.

Lemma 7

(Partition–Sum Lemma)
.

Let
{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
and
x,y\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. If
\min\bigl\{f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(x),f_{{\bm{\beta}}}(y)\bigr\}>k
, then

f_{{\bm{\alpha}}+{\bm{\beta}}}(x+y)\geq\min\bigl\{f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(x),f_{{\bm{\beta}}}(y)\bigr\}.

Proof.

Suppose vectors
{\bm{\alpha}}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}),\,{\bm{\beta}}=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k})
and define
{\bm{\gamma}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}{\bm{\alpha}}+{\bm{\beta}}
. Write
a\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(x)
and
b\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}f_{{\bm{\beta}}}(y)
. According to the definitions, there exist

•

an
{\bm{\alpha}}
-town
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{a}\}
on ground set
[x]=\{1,2,\dots,x\}
of size
a
, and

•

a
{\bm{\beta}}
-town
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},\dots,B_{b}\}
on ground set
\{x+1,x+2,\dots,x+y\}
of size
b
.

We claim that the family
\mathcal{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\bigl\{A_{i}\cup B_{i}:i=1,2,\dots,\min\{a,b\}\bigr\}
is a
{\bm{\gamma}}
-town on ground set
[x+y]
of size
\min\{a,b\}
, implying that
f_{{\bm{\gamma}}}(x+y)\geq\min\bigl\{f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(x),f_{{\bm{\beta}}}(y)\bigr\}
. Since the ground sets of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are disjoint,
A_{i}\cup B_{i}
are pairwise distinct, and so
|\mathcal{F}|=\min\{a,b\}
. For any
\ell\in[k]
and any
I\in\binom{\min\{a,b\}}{\ell}
,

\biggl|\bigcap_{i\in I}(A_{i}\cup B_{i})\biggr|=\biggl|\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}\biggr|+\biggl|\bigcap_{i\in I}B_{i}\biggr|\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell}+{\bm{\beta}}_{\ell}\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}{\bm{\gamma}}_{\ell},

meaning that
\mathcal{F}
is a
{\bm{\gamma}}
-town. So, the claim is valid, and the proof is complete.
∎

This Partition–Sum Lemma has the following direct corollary.

Corollary 8
.

Let
{\bm{\alpha}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. If
\min\bigl\{f_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{1}}(x_{1}),\dots,f_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell}}(x_{\ell})\bigr\}>k
, then

f_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{1}+\dots+{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell}}(x_{1}+\dots+x_{\ell})\geq\min\bigl\{f_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{1}}(x_{1}),\dots,f_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell}}(x_{\ell})\bigr\}.

It is worth mentioning that the Partition–Sum Lemma is useful in two different ways.

•

On one hand, given constructions of large
{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}
-towns for
i=1,\dots,\ell
, the lemma yields a large
({\bm{\alpha}}_{1}+\dots+{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell})
-town, which will be useful for establishing the lower bounds in
Theorem

3
.

•

On the other hand, the lemma acts as a strong triangle inequality, allowing us to combine vectors
{\bm{\alpha}}
and
{\bm{\beta}}
from different levels while preserving a smaller upper bound on
f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(n)
. This turns out to be a crucial step in our reductive proof of the upper bounds in
Theorem

3
.

These features make the Partition–Sum Lemma our main technical tool in the proof of
Theorem

3
.

In this section, we deduce the lower bounds restated below in

.

Theorem 9
.

Suppose
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and let
{\bm{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
with
\lv({\bm{\alpha}})=t>0
. Then, as
n\to\infty
, we have

f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(n)\geq\begin{cases}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!)^{1/t}\cdot n^{1/t}\qquad&\text{if $\grd({\bm{\alpha}})=1$},\\
\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!/2)^{1/t}\cdot n^{1/t}\qquad&\text{if $\grd({\bm{\alpha}})=2$}.\end{cases}

We first construct
canonical families

\mathcal{G}_{i}
, which are asymptotically largest
{\bm{\gamma}}^{i}
-towns for canonical vectors
{\bm{\gamma}}^{i}
. We then apply Partition–Sum Lemma to prove
Theorem

9
in full generality. It is worth mentioning that throughout this section we always implicitly assume that
t=\lfloor i/2\rfloor
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
t\in[m]
, we denote by
\binom{[m]}{t}
the family of
t
-element subsets of
[m]
.

•

For
s=1,\dots,m
, we write
A^{(t)}_{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\Bigl\{X\in\tbinom{[m]}{t}:s\in X\Bigr\}
.

In other words, the
star

A^{(t)}_{s}
consists of all
t
-element subsets of
[m]
containing
s
.

Inspired by the previous lower-bound constructions of Johnston–O’Neill
[
18
, Construction 3]
and Wei–Zhang–Ge
[
26
, Construction II.1]
, we introduce set families

\mathcal{F}_{2t}(m)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\Bigl\{A^{(t)}_{s}:s\in[m]\Bigr\},\qquad\mathcal{F}_{2t+1}(m)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\Bigl\{\tbinom{[m]}{t}\setminus A^{(t)}_{s}:s\in[m]\Bigr\}.

As previously remarked in
Section

1
, there exists a
{\bm{\gamma}}^{1}
-town
\mathcal{F}_{1}(m)
of size
2^{\lfloor\frac{m-1}{2}\rfloor}
for every
m
.

Proposition 10
.

Suppose
t=\lfloor i/2\rfloor\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. If
m
is divisible by
2^{t}
, then
\mathcal{F}_{i}(m)
is a
{\bm{\gamma}}^{i}
-town.

Proof.

For
j=1,\dots,t
, recall that
({\bm{\gamma}}^{2t})_{j}\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}\binom{t-1}{j-1}
and
({\bm{\gamma}}^{2t+1})_{j}\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}\binom{-(t+1)}{j-1}
. By constructions,

•

every
j
-wise intersection in
\mathcal{F}_{2t}(m)
has size
\binom{m-j}{t-j}
, and

•

every
j
-wise intersection in
\mathcal{F}_{2t+1}(m)
has size
\binom{m-j}{t}
.

Denote by
\nu_{2}(m)
the largest integer
\lambda
such that
2^{\lambda}
divides
m
. Since
m
is divisible by
2^{t}
,

\nu_{2}(m)\geq t\implies\nu_{2}(m)>\nu_{2}(t!)\implies\begin{cases}\binom{m-j}{t-j}\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}\binom{-j}{t-j}=\binom{t-1}{j-1},\\
\binom{m-j}{t}\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}\binom{-j}{t}=\binom{-(t+1)}{j-1}.\end{cases}\qed

Proof of
Theorem

9
.

We first construct
\mathcal{G}_{i}
using
\mathcal{F}_{i}
. When
i=1
, the families
\mathcal{G}_{1}(n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathcal{F}_{1}(n)
work. Suppose
i\geq 2
then. Observe that
|\mathcal{F}_{i}(m)|=m
and
\mathcal{F}_{i}(m)
has ground set
\binom{[m]}{t}
. To obtain
\mathcal{G}_{i}(n)
, we pick
m_{n,i}
to be the largest integer with
\binom{m}{t}\leq n
and
\nu_{2}(m)\geq t
. It follows that

m_{n,i}=\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!)^{1/t}\cdot n^{1/t},

and so (by
Proposition

10
) the
{\bm{\gamma}}^{i}
-town
\mathcal{G}_{i}(n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathcal{F}_{i}(m_{n,i})
implies
f_{{\bm{\gamma}}^{i}}(n)\geq\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!)^{1/t}\cdot n^{1/t}
.

In the general case, suppose
i_{1}<\dots<i_{\ell}
are indices such that

{\bm{\alpha}}={\bm{\gamma}}^{i_{1}}+\dots+{\bm{\gamma}}^{i_{\ell}}

is the canonical decomposition. Since
\lv({\bm{\alpha}})=t
, we have that
i_{\ell}\in\{2t,2t+1\}
. Moreover,

•

if
\grd({\bm{\alpha}})=1
, then
i_{\ell-1}\leq 2t-1
if it exists;

•

if
\grd({\bm{\alpha}})=2
, then
i_{\ell-1}=2t
and
i_{\ell}=2t+1
.

Choose parameters
(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell})
with
f_{{\bm{\gamma}}^{i_{1}}}(x_{1})=\dots=f_{{\bm{\gamma}}^{i_{\ell}}}(x_{\ell})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}m
such that
x_{1}+\dots+x_{\ell}\leq n
is as large as possible. Then from the Partition–Sum Lemma (
Corollary

8
) we deduce that

f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(n)\geq f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(x_{1}+\dots+x_{\ell})\geq m=\begin{cases}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!)^{1/t}\cdot n^{1/t}\qquad&\text{if $\grd({\bm{\alpha}})=1$},\\
\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!/2)^{1/t}\cdot n^{1/t}\qquad&\text{if $\grd({\bm{\alpha}})=2$},\end{cases}

where the “
=
” follows from an optimization on
m
. The proof of
Theorem

9
is complete.
∎

The upper bounds in

In this section, we deduce the upper bounds restated below in
Theorem

.

Theorem 11
.

Suppose
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and let
{\bm{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
with
\lv({\bm{\alpha}})=t>0
. Then, as
n\to\infty
, we have

f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(n)\leq\begin{cases}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!)^{1/t}\cdot n^{1/t}\qquad&\text{if $\grd({\bm{\alpha}})=1$},\\
\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!/2)^{1/t}\cdot n^{1/t}\qquad&\text{if $\grd({\bm{\alpha}})=2$}.\end{cases}

To prove
Theorem

11
, the following replacement lemma is crucial.

Lemma 12
.

Suppose
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and let
{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
with
\lv({\bm{\alpha}})=t>s=\lv({\bm{\beta}})
. Then,

•

given that
f_{{\bm{\alpha}}+{\bm{\beta}}}(n)\leq\bigl(c+o(1)\bigr)\cdot n^{1/t}
as
n\to\infty
, we have
f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(n)\leq\bigl(c+o(1)\bigr)\cdot n^{1/t}
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Arbitrarily pick a parameter
\delta\in(0,1)
and set
m\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}n^{(t-\delta)/t}
. For
s>0
and
n\to\infty
, we have

m=o(n)\qquad\text{and}\qquad m^{1/s}\geq m^{1/(t-1)}=\omega(n^{1/t}).

It then follows from the Partition–Sum Lemma applied to
{\bm{\alpha}}+{\bm{\beta}}
and
n+m
that

\bigl(c+o(1)\bigr)\cdot n^{1/t}=\bigl(c+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(n+m)^{1/t}\geq f_{{\bm{\alpha}}+{\bm{\beta}}}(n+m)\geq\min\bigl\{f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(n),f_{{\bm{\beta}}}(m)\bigr\}.

We know from
Theorem

9
that
f_{{\bm{\beta}}}(m)=\Omega(m^{1/s})
, and hence
f_{{\bm{\beta}}}(m)=\omega(n^{1/t})
*
*

*

If
s=0
, then
m^{1/s}
is not well-defined. Nevertheless, in this case we still have
f_{{\bm{\beta}}}(m)=\Omega(2^{m/2})=\omega(n^{1/t})
.
. Therefore,

f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(n)\leq\bigl(c+o(1)\bigr)\cdot n^{1/t}.\qed

Proof of
Theorem

11
.

We analyze two cases on
\grd({\bm{\alpha}})
separately.

Case 1.
\grd({\bm{\alpha}})=1
.

We first treat the case
{\bm{\alpha}}_{t}=1
and
{\bm{\alpha}}_{t+1}=\dots={\bm{\alpha}}_{2t}=0
. This follows from an argument of Wei, Zhang, and Ge
[
26
, Lemma IV.1]
. For completeness, we spell out the details below.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{1},\dots,F_{m}\}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be an
{\bm{\alpha}}
-town. Consider the family of
t
-wise intersections

\mathcal{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\biggl\{\bigcap_{j\in T}F_{j}:T\in\tbinom{[m]}{t}\biggr\}.

We claim that all members of
\mathcal{G}
are distinct, and so
|\mathcal{G}|=\binom{m}{t}
. Indeed, if
\bigcap\limits_{j\in T}F_{j}=\bigcap\limits_{j\in T^{\prime}}F_{j}
for some
T\neq T^{\prime}
, then intersecting both sides with
F_{r}\,(r\in T\bigtriangleup T^{\prime})
yields an equality between a
(t+1)
-wise intersection and a
t
-wise intersection of distinct sets, contradicting
{\bm{\alpha}}_{t}=1
while
{\bm{\alpha}}_{t+1}=0
.

For distinct
G,G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
, their intersection is an
\ell
-wise intersection of distinct members of
\mathcal{F}
, where
t+1\leq\ell\leq 2t
. It then follows from
{\bm{\alpha}}_{t+1}=\dots={\bm{\alpha}}_{2t}=0
that
|G\cap G^{\prime}|
is even. Consequently,
\mathcal{G}
is an oddtown on
[n]
, hence
\tbinom{m}{t}=|\mathcal{G}|\leq n
. This implies that
m\leq\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!n)^{1/t}
. Consequently,

f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(n)\leq\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!)^{1/t}\cdot n^{1/t}.

We then reduce the general case to the special case above. We have canonical decomposition

{\bm{\alpha}}=c_{1}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{1}_{[k]}+\dots+c_{2t-1}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t-1}_{[k]}+1\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{x}_{[k]},

where
x\in\{2t,2t+1\}
. Introduce an auxiliary vector
{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
such that

{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}_{[2t]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\bigl(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{t},\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{t}\bigr).

We remark that only the last
t+1
coordinates
{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}_{t}=1
and
{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}_{t+1}=\dots={\bm{\alpha}}^{*}_{2t}=0
matter. Due to
Corollary

5
, the vectors
{\bm{\gamma}}^{1}_{[2t]},\dots,{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t-1}_{[2t]},{\bm{\gamma}}^{x}_{[2t]}
span
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2t}
. Hence, we have linear decomposition

{\bm{\alpha}}_{[2t]}+{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}_{[2t]}=\lambda_{1}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{1}_{[2t]}+\dots+\lambda_{2t-1}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t-1}_{[2t]}+\lambda_{x}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{x}_{[2t]},

where
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{2t-1},\lambda_{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
are uniquely determined. Complete the construction of
{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}
by setting

{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}{\bm{\alpha}}+\lambda_{1}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{1}_{[k]}+\dots+\lambda_{2t-1}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t-1}_{[k]}+\lambda_{x}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{x}_{[k]}.

A crucial observation is that
\lambda_{x}=0
. If not, then
\lambda_{x}=1
, and so
{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}_{[2t]}\in\spa_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\bigl\{{\bm{\gamma}}^{1}_{[2t]},\dots,{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t-1}_{[2t]}\bigr\}
. This implies that
\lv({\bm{\alpha}}^{*}_{[2t]})\leq t-1
, and hence we obtain
f_{{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}_{[2t]}}=\Omega(n^{1/(t-1)})
, thanks to
Theorem

9
. However, it follows from the coordinates
{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}_{t}=1
and
{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}_{t+1}=\dots={\bm{\alpha}}^{*}_{2t}=0
that
f_{{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}_{[2t]}}=O(n^{1/t})
, a contradiction. Therefore, we see that
\lambda_{x}=0
, and hence
{\bm{\alpha}}^{*}={\bm{\alpha}}+\lambda_{1}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{1}_{[k]}+\dots+\lambda_{2t-1}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t-1}_{[k]}
.

Finally, by
Lemma

12
applied to
{\bm{\beta}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\lambda_{1}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{1}_{[k]}+\dots+\lambda_{2t-1}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t-1}_{[k]}
, we conclude that

f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(n)\leq\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!)^{1/t}\cdot n^{1/t}.

Case 2.
\grd({\bm{\alpha}})=2
.

We first claim that there exists
{\bm{\beta}}\in\spa_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\bigl\{{\bm{\gamma}}^{1}_{[k]},\dots,{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t-1}_{[k]}\bigr\}
such that
{\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}{\bm{\alpha}}+{\bm{\beta}}
satisfies

{\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}_{1}={\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}_{2}=0,\qquad{\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}_{3}=1.

We verify this by considering the following cases for
t
.

•

If
t\geq 2
, then
Lemma

2
shows that
{\bm{\gamma}}^{1}_{[3]},{\bm{\gamma}}^{2}_{[3]},{\bm{\gamma}}^{3}_{[3]}
span
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{3}
. So, there exist
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
with

(0,0,1)-{\bm{\alpha}}_{[3]}=\mu_{1}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{1}_{[3]}+\mu_{2}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{2}_{[3]}+\mu_{3}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{3}_{[3]},

and hence
{\bm{\beta}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mu_{1}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{1}_{[k]}+\mu_{2}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{2}_{[k]}+\mu_{3}\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{3}_{[k]}
satisfies
({\bm{\alpha}}+{\bm{\beta}})_{[3]}=(0,0,1)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{3}
.

•

If
t=1
, then there exists
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
such that

{\bm{\alpha}}_{[3]}=\mu\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{1}_{[3]}+{\bm{\gamma}}^{2}_{[3]}+{\bm{\gamma}}^{3}_{[3]}=\mu\cdot(1,1,1)+(0,0,1),

and so
{\bm{\beta}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mu\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{1}_{[k]}
satisfies
({\bm{\alpha}}+{\bm{\beta}})_{[3]}=(0,0,1)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{3}
.

Thanks to
Lemma

12
, it then suffices to show that
f_{{\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}}(n)\leq\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!/2)^{1/t}\cdot n^{1/t}
.

We extend the definition of shifting operator
\sigma
by denoting
\sigma({\bm{\gamma}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}({\bm{\gamma}}_{2},\dots,{\bm{\gamma}}_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k-1}
for each
{\bm{\gamma}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}
. Crucially, we may deduce via
Lemma

6
that both
\sigma({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime})
and
{\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}_{[k-1]}+\sigma({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime})
have level
t
and grade
1
. Indeed, the shifting
\sigma
and truncating
(\bullet)_{[k-1]}
are linear operators with the property that

•

the identity
\sigma({\bm{\gamma}}_{[k]})=\sigma({\bm{\gamma}})_{[k-1]}
holds for every
{\bm{\gamma}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\otimes\omega}
.

Since
{\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}
has level
t
and grade
2
, by introducing unspecified
\bm{*}_{\ell}\in\spa_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\bigl\{{\bm{\gamma}}^{1}_{[\ell]},\dots,{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t-1}_{[\ell]}\bigr\}
we compute

\displaystyle{\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}=\bm{*}_{k}+1\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t}_{[k]}+1\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t+1}_{[k]}\implies\sigma({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sigma(\bm{*}_{k})+\sigma({\bm{\gamma}}^{2t}_{[k]})+\sigma({\bm{\gamma}}^{2t+1}_{[k]})

\displaystyle=\sigma(\bm{*}_{k})+\sigma({\bm{\gamma}}^{2t})_{[k-1]}+\sigma({\bm{\gamma}}^{2t+1})_{[k-1]}

\displaystyle=\bm{*}_{k-1}+\bm{*}_{k-1}+\bigl(\bm{*}_{k-1}+{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t+1}_{[k-1]}\bigr)\qquad\text{by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{lem:shift}}

\displaystyle=\bm{*}_{k-1}+0\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t}_{[k-1]}+1\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t+1}_{[k-1]}.

Since
{\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}_{[k-1]}=\bm{*}_{k-1}+1\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t}_{[k-1]}+1\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t+1}_{[k-1]}
, we obtain
{\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}_{[k-1]}+\sigma({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime})=\bm{*}_{k-1}+1\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t}_{[k-1]}+0\cdot{\bm{\gamma}}^{2t+1}_{[k-1]}
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\{F_{0},F_{1},\dots,F_{m}\}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be an
{\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}
-town with
\mathcal{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\{F_{1},\dots,F_{m}\}
. Write
F_{0}^{\mathsf{c}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}[n]\setminus F_{0}
and

\mathcal{G}_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}F_{0}\cap\mathcal{F}=\bigl\{F_{0}\cap F:F\in\mathcal{F}\bigr\},\qquad\mathcal{G}_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}F_{0}^{\mathsf{c}}\cap\mathcal{F}=\bigl\{F_{0}^{\mathsf{c}}\cap F:F\in\mathcal{F}\bigr\}.

By construction we have that
\mathcal{G}_{1}
is a
\sigma({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime})
-town on
F_{0}
, and that
\mathcal{G}_{2}
is an
\bigl({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}_{[k-1]}+\sigma({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime})\bigr)
-town on
F_{0}^{\mathsf{c}}
. The latter is implied by
(F_{0}^{\mathsf{c}}\cap F_{i_{1}})\cap\dots\cap(F_{0}^{\mathsf{c}}\cap F_{i_{j}})=(F_{i_{1}}\cap\dots\cap F_{i_{j}})\setminus(F_{0}\cap F_{i_{1}}\cap\dots\cap F_{i_{j}})
.

Since
{\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}_{[3]}=(0,0,1)
, we have
\sigma({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime})_{1}\neq\sigma({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime})_{2}
and
\bigl({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}_{[k-1]}+\sigma({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime})\bigr)_{1}\neq\bigl({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}_{[k-1]}+\sigma({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime})\bigr)_{2}
. Thus, a similar argument as in
Case 1
shows that all members of
\mathcal{G}_{1}
and all members of
\mathcal{G}_{2}
are distinct. Because both
\sigma({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime})
and
{\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}_{[k-1]}+\sigma({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime})
have level
t
and grade
1
, the conclusion of
Case 1
implies

m=|\mathcal{G}_{1}|\leq\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!)^{1/t}\cdot|F_{0}|^{1/t},\qquad m=|\mathcal{G}_{2}|\leq\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!)^{1/t}\cdot|F_{0}^{\mathsf{c}}|^{1/t}.

From
\min\bigl\{|F_{0}|,|F_{0}^{\mathsf{c}}|\bigr\}\leq n/2
we obtain
|\mathcal{G}|=m+1\leq\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!)^{1/t}\cdot(n/2)^{1/t}
. Consequently,

f_{{\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime}}(n)\leq\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)\cdot(t!/2)^{1/t}\cdot n^{1/t}.

Combining
Case 1
and
Case 2
above completes the proof of
Theorem

11
.
∎

5
Proof of
Theorem

4

Recall that
{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell}^{k}=\bigl(\underbrace{*,\dots,*}_{\ell-1},1,\underbrace{*,\dots,*}_{k-\ell}\bigr)\in{\widetilde{\mathbb{F}}}_{2}^{k}
. We prove two directions separately.

Proof that
g_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell}^{k}}(n)
is unbounded if
\ell
is not a power of
2
.

There exists
t\in[\ell-1]
such that
\binom{-\ell}{t}\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}1
. Choose
m
to be a sufficiently large integer which is divisible by
2^{t}
.

Consider the canonical construction
\mathcal{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathcal{F}_{2t}(m)
from
Section

3
. That is,

A^{(t)}_{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\Bigl\{X\in\tbinom{[m]}{t}:s\in X\Bigr\},\qquad\mathcal{F}_{2t}(m)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\Bigl\{A^{(t)}_{s}:s\in[m]\Bigr\}.

For any distinct
F_{1},\dots,F_{\ell}\in\mathcal{F}
, from
t<\ell
and
\nu_{2}(m)\geq t>\nu_{2}(t!)
we deduce that

|F_{1}\cap\dots\cap F_{\ell}|=0,\qquad|F_{1}\cup\dots\cup F_{\ell}|=\tbinom{m}{t}-\tbinom{m-\ell}{t}\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}\tbinom{-\ell}{t}\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}1.

It follows that
|F_{1}\cap\dots\cap F_{\ell}|+|F_{1}\cup\dots\cup F_{\ell}|\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}0+1\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}1
. Moreover, we have
|\mathcal{F}_{2t}(m)|=m
.

Upon setting
m
to be the largest integer such that
\binom{m}{\ell}\leq n
, we obtain
g_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell}^{k}}(n)=\Omega(n^{1/\ell})
.

Proof that
g_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell}^{k}}(n)
is bounded if
\ell
is a power of
2
.

Assume
\ell=2^{t}
, where
t\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{1},\dots,F_{m}\}
be an
{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell}^{k}
-ville. Take a coloring
\chi\colon\binom{\mathcal{F}}{\ell}\to\{0,1\}^{D}
, where
D\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}2^{\ell}-1
and

•

each
\ell
-subset
\{F_{i_{1}},\dots,F_{i_{\ell}}\}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
with
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{\ell}
is assigned a
\{0,1\}
-vector of length
2^{\ell}-1
that records, in a fixed order, the parities of all nonempty intersections among
F_{i_{1}},\dots,F_{i_{\ell}}
.

Thanks to Ramsey’s theorem, there exists some sufficiently large
M_{\ell}>0
such that if
m>M_{\ell}
then
\mathcal{F}
contains a subfamily
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},\dots,G_{10\ell^{2}}\}
in which every
\ell
-subset of
\mathcal{G}
receives the same color
\tau
.

We are going to bound
|\mathcal{F}|
by
M_{\ell}
, implying that
g_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell}^{k}}(n)\leq M_{\ell}
.

We first show that
\tau
is
symmetric
:

•

for
s=1,\dots,\ell
, all coordinates of
\tau
corresponding to
s
-wise intersections are equal.

Fix
s\in[\ell]
and consider the distinguished
s
-tuple
(H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{s})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}(G_{\ell},G_{2\ell},\dots,G_{s\ell})
which exists since
|\mathcal{G}|=10\ell^{2}\geq s\ell
. We verify that
(H_{1},\dots,H_{s})
can occupy any positions within an
\ell
-subset of
\mathcal{G}
. This implies the desired symmetry, since every
\ell
-subset of
\mathcal{G}
receives the same color
\tau
. For any
I=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{s}\}\subseteq[\ell]
with
i_{1}<\dots<i_{s}
, define an
\ell
-subset
\mathcal{G}_{I}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
as follows:

•

arrange its elements in increasing order of indices, place
H_{r}
in the
i_{r}
-th position, and fill the other
\ell-s
positions with arbitrary distinct elements of
\{G_{1},\dots,G_{(s+1)\ell}\}\setminus\{H_{1},\dots,H_{s}\}
.

This is possible because there are
\ell-1
available fillers between any consecutive
H_{i},H_{i+1}
, as well as before
H_{1}
and after
H_{s}
. Since each
\ell
-subset of
\mathcal{G}
receives color
\tau
, it follows that
\mathcal{G}_{I}
has color
\tau
, and so the
s
-wise coordinate of
\tau
indexed by
I
equals
\bigl|\bigcap_{r=1}^{s}H_{r}\bigr|\bmod 2
, which is independent of
I
. As
I
ranges over all
\binom{\ell}{s}
choices, all
s
-wise coordinates of
\tau
coincide, proving the desired symmetry.

We then show that
m\leq M_{\ell}
. Fix any distinct
X_{1},\dots,X_{\ell}\in\mathcal{G}
and write for
i=1,\dots,\ell

x_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\bigl|(X_{1}\cap\dots\cap X_{i})\setminus(X_{i+1}\cup\dots\cup X_{\ell})\bigr|,

which are the sizes of Venn-diagram cells grouped by depth. The symmetry of
\tau
yields

|X_{1}\cap\dots\cap X_{\ell}|+|X_{1}\cup\dots\cup X_{\ell}|\overset{\mathbb{F}_{2}}{=}x_{\ell}+\Bigl(\tbinom{\ell}{0}\cdot x_{\ell}+\tbinom{\ell}{1}\cdot x_{\ell-1}+\dots+\tbinom{\ell}{\ell-1}\cdot x_{1}\Bigr).

Since
\ell
is a power of
2
, the binomial coefficients
\binom{\ell}{1},\dots,\binom{\ell}{\ell-1}
are all even, and so the right-hand side is
0
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, contradicting the assumed oddness of
{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell}^{k}
. We thus conclude that
m\leq M_{\ell}
.

6
Concluding remarks

We now introduce a general framework encompassing towns and villes. Let
m\geq 2
and
k\geq 2
be integers, and let
{\bm{I}}=(I_{1},\dots,I_{k})\in\bigl(2^{\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\bigr)^{k}
be a sequence of subsets of
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
. A family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is called an
{\bm{I}}
-town (resp.
{\bm{I}}
-ville) if, for each
j=1,\dots,k
, every
j
-wise intersection (resp. intersection-union) of distinct sets in
\mathcal{F}
has cardinality modulo
m
lying in
I_{j}
. Denote by
f_{m,{\bm{I}}}(n)
(resp.
g_{m,{\bm{I}}}(n)
) the maximum size of an
{\bm{I}}
-town (resp.
{\bm{I}}
-ville) on
[n]
. The ultimate goal is to determine the sharp asymptotic behavior of
f_{m,{\bm{I}}}(n)
and
g_{m,{\bm{I}}}(n)
as
n\to\infty
, for every
m
and every
{\bm{I}}
.

Our main result,
Theorem

3
, resolves the
f_{2,{\bm{I}}}(n)
problem in the case where each
I_{j}
is a singleton. However, the asymptotic behavior of
f_{2,{\bm{I}}}(n)
for general
{\bm{I}}
(equivalently,
f_{{\bm{\alpha}}}(n)
for
{\bm{\alpha}}\in{\widetilde{\mathbb{F}}}_{2}^{k}
) remains much less understood and appears to merit further investigation.

Problem 13
.

Study the sharp asymptotic behavior of
f_{\bm{\alpha}}(n)
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and every
{\bm{\alpha}}\in{\widetilde{\mathbb{F}}}_{2}^{k}
.

As for the function
g
, we have studied the problem of determining
g_{2,{\bm{I}}}(n)
in the case where all but one of the
I_{j}
are equal to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, showing that
g
is unbounded unless the exceptional coordinate is a power of
2
. However, the correct asymptotics of those unbounded
g
remain unknown.

Problem 14
.

Find the sharp asymptotic behavior of
g_{{\bm{\alpha}}_{\ell}^{k}}(n)
when
\ell
is not a power of
2
.

For the more general function
f_{m,{\bm{I}}}(n)
, very little is known. Johnston and O’Neill
[
18
]
deduced

\Omega(n)\leq f_{3,(0,0,\{1,2\})}(n)\leq O(n^{2}),

and we reiterate their open problem
[
18
, Problem 1]
on the correct asymptotics of this quantity.

Problem 15
.

Determine the sharp asymptotic behavior of
f_{3,{\bm{I}}}(n)
for
{\bm{I}}=(0,0,\{1,2\})
.
