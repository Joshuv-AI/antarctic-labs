PUBLIC EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Background: Hydromine is a novel extremely versatile solution to hydrokinetics energy generation,
where the external water flow facing energy sweeping area (&structure) is completely passive. One or
multiple Hydromine units can easily deploy from a multitude of floating or stationary marine
structures, opening a multitude of application and use cases. Proposing Hydromine, it was initially
found that the concept could easily scale into all of the four areas of ARPA-E SHARKS cases, as well
as other applications, this is illustrated in Figure 1. Also, the relatively aggressive cost of energy
targets of the program could be met in the conceptual phase.

The HydroMINE it self have a small internal propeller, similar to a Kaplan turbine, which is driven
by pressure propagated from a set of stationary hydrofoil structure to an internal and separate flow
stream. The internal propeller drives an electric generator, see Figure 2. The size of the stationary
HydroMINE hydrofoil structure can be compared to an equivalent rotor of the same swept area, but
the efficiency is higher than ordinary rotor systems. The internal propeller isolated from debris and
wildlife. From the outside, the floating structure is passive, only yawing slowly with the ocean or
river current water flow direction. HydroMINE is extremely safe, operate with high availability and
low maintenance.

HydroMINE is manufactured completely from rolled steel plates, thus integrating easily with
ordinary marine structures, such as a floating platform. The unit design is completely scalable up to
Mega-Watt rating using only ordinary steel manufacturing techniques.

The team designed a large floating platform carrying three HydroMine units, which easily in the
conceptual phase met a cost of energy well below $0.04 per kWh, exceeding the SHARKS program
goals and impact the nation’s energy system. The naval design was led by Kent Houston Offshore
Engineering LLC, including sea-keeping testing at model scale. Sandia National Laboratories lead
performance computing, co-design optimization and planned testing of a single unit at model scale.
A non-detailed illustration of the developed system is shown in Figure 2.

Key results from component developments have been published, including internal turbine
performance and control, design concept for the water intake to the turbine. Details of the naval
system was published and presented [Houchens et.al. 2023].

As the concept developed, potential real-world sites for deployment was examined with details about
the water conditions. Challenges in identifying large bodies of water with tidal resources or other
current of sufficient strength to warrant the system size. Discussion with potential future go-to-
market and pilot partners pointed towards the Case S3 producing river kinetic energy with a pylon
style Hydromine concept was much more opportune than the original assumptions. A summary of
these key findings is found in reference [Houchens et.al. 2023].

ACCOMPLISHMENTS AND OBJECTIVES

This award allowed the team to explore the concepts developed by Westergaard in details and work
towards a demonstration. Sub-components and their uncoupled performance were documented in great
details, but the system level progress remained absent and challenging, ultimately mutual agreement to
early terminating the project before costly model testing and costly navel engineering design was
undertaken as outline in the original proposal and milestone schedule. The results and concepts were
published for the benefit of future energy research, securing that potential future effort can continue with
an intellectual freedom to operate.

PROJECT ACTIVITIES
The initial vison led to some detailed work on the S4 case to demonstrate the sub-component level
of the technology, which was achieved. However, system level aggregation and real-world
deployment challenges proved the path not to meet the cost of energy goal, not the practical aspect
of a future demonstration. Additional work in task 1 and 2 (still within the scope of the packages)
found S3 case to be a more direct and simpler way to meet the goals. Some challenges remained,
and ultimately a mutual early agreement for termination was executed before the costly work
packages number 3.2 to 3.6, entire 4 and entire 5 were started, as the challenge also continued to
move schedule.
