Executive Summary
The Electrify Monterey Bay project, delivered by Central Coast Energy Services (CCES) under DOE Weatherization
Assistance Program Enhancement & Innovation (E&I) funding, was designed to demonstrate scalable, low-income
residential electrification across the Monterey Bay region over a 36-month performance period. The original
production goal was to electrify at least 50 households; CCES exceeded this benchmark by completing
electrification work in 86 households. Targeting criteria prioritized households located in disadvantaged
communities and/or with an energy burden >6.5% (defined as average household energy costs divided by average
household countable income). Across participating households, the average energy burden was 28%, indicating a
high-need population and supporting the program’s equity and affordability objectives.

Summary of Project Activities and Approach
 • Household intake and eligibility screening focused on disadvantaged community status and high energy
 burden, supplemented by household composition considerations (e.g., seniors, disability, young children)
 to maximize health, safety, and resilience benefits.
 • Dwelling assessment and scope development identified opportunities for partial or full electrification while
 addressing prerequisite health-and-safety or building-envelope needs using leveraged resources where
 feasible.
 • Technology deployment in CCES ServTraq (c) software facilitated cost savings analysis for high-efficiency
 electric end uses, including heat pumps and heat pump water heaters.
 • Participant education and referrals addressed energy conservation and backup-power needs (e.g., referral
 to a portable battery program for medically necessary devices)
 • Workforce development was expanded through leveraged funding: CCES launched a Workforce Education
 Training Center, enabling 26 trainees to participate in supervised field installations and increasing capacity
 for advanced electrification measures.

Key Findings
Overall outcomes demonstrate that the pilot exceeded its participation target while reaching households with
substantial affordability constraints and vulnerability indicators. The program served 207 individuals across 86
households, including a predominance of owner-occupied dwellings. Major measures installed included 66 heat
pumps and 14 heat pump water heaters, along with additional electrification and efficiency measures. Direct
investment (labor + material) in installed measures totaled 86% of the $1,325,425.76 program operation
expenditure budget. Participant feedback was positive: a 57-response telephone survey reported average
satisfaction of 93% with worker performance, 90% with comfort improvements, and 91% with installed measures.
In addition to E&I-funded work, CCES leveraged $333,113.44 in additional grant funding to address repairs,
diagnostics, and complementary measures that improved readiness for electrification and supported overall
performance.

Analyses and Assumptions Supporting Results
Greenhouse-gas (GHG) performance was evaluated by comparing electricity and natural gas consumption from the
baseline utility statements collected at intake to usage one year after service to estimate net change attributable
to installed measures; results are reported as average net GHG reduction per home electrified. For conservatism
and data quality, the current GHG reduction estimate (48% average net reduction) is limited to 59 projects
completed prior to 2026; projects completed in 2026 were excluded due to insufficient post-installation duration.
Additional exclusions were applied where required data could not be obtained.

This summary highlights a strategic opportunity to enhance service delivery, accelerate energy efficiency
outcomes, and reduce administrative overhead by leveraging and managing multiple funding streams through a
single, coordinated entity—minimizing delays and maximizing impact.

costs that accounted for a significant portion of their income before receiving program assistance. By

GHG Reduction Savings
The project also aimed to achieve at least a 45% reduction in GHG emissions. The data indicates that the
project achieved an average net GHG reduction of 48% per home electrified.

This calculation is based on 59 projects completed prior to 2026. Projects completed in 2026 were omitted from
this calculation because insufficient time has passed to fully evaluate the savings impact. The calculation also
excludes 10 sub-metered mobile home projects for which the most recent statement could not be obtained from
the applicant. Additionally, four homes without natural gas were excluded because documentation for the
secondary heating fuel source (wood or propane) was unavailable, and three projects had closed accounts for
which data could not be retrieved.

To calculate the net savings, CCES compared the electricity and natural gas usage from the original statement
collected to the energy and natural gas usage one year after service. The change in usage was then used to
determine the net savings. While the project achieved our minimum GHG reduction goal, not all homes were fully
electrified. In some cases, customers were only interested in partial electrification, and in others, the assessed
natural gas appliance(s) were newer models that didn’t warrant replacement.

Measures Installed
Electrification measures installed through the program helped reduce GHG emissions by replacing non-
functioning, older, or less efficient equipment with high-efficiency electric technologies such as heat pumps and
heat pump water heaters. These upgrades improve household energy efficiency while supporting broader efforts
to transition homes toward cleaner energy systems.

The project delivered a total of 66 heat pumps, 19 windows, 8 cooking appliances, 10 clothes dryers, 14 heat
pump water heaters, 7 floor insulation, 8 refrigerators, 4 ceiling insulation, 4 sliding glass doors, and 3 electric
water heaters, outlining some of the biggest measures taken. These improvements contributed to better indoor
comfort, increased equipment efficiency, and long-term energy savings for participating households.

ServTraq- Lumina Calculator
CCES partnered with Analytica to develop a direct connection for the Heat Pump Savings Calculator. This
collaboration enabled an Application Programming Interface link between CCES's ServTraq Software and
Analytica’s Heat Pump Calculator, allowing for efficient estimation of electrification savings using energy cost data
gathered during program intake, along with dwelling and appliance information collected during the assessment
phase.

The calculator suggested technology options based on the type of heating and cooling appliances and the energy
source used. Additionally, the Heat Pump Calculator provided estimates of monthly bill savings and annual energy
reductions, helping CCES choose the most efficient solutions and offering visual aids to communicate the benefits
of heat pump applications to customers.

Satisfaction Survey
In February, CCES conducted a short 5-minute telephone survey of program participants. CCES collected 57
responses. Survey results indicated strong satisfaction across several key areas:

Satisfaction with worker performance received an average 93%
Comfort level in your home received an average 90%
Satisfaction with measures installed received an average 91%

These results indicate high satisfaction with both the installation process and the improvements made to
participating homes. Respondents reported improved comfort level and positive experiences working with
installation crews.

Part of the project objectives was to include customer referrals to other electrification programs, such as Solar PV.
While no low-income solar PV programs were active in the region, applicants received energy conservation tips and
education on reducing household energy use. In addition, households with medical device needs were referred to a
Portable Battery Program to support power reliability during outages.

Leveraging
Permit costs represented one of the most significant leveraged activities. While the program’s focus was the
Monterey Bay region, projects were completed across 21 cities within three counties. Successfully navigating
varying permit requirements across jurisdictions required familiarity with local regulations and strong coordination
with local agencies.

This knowledge was instrumental in keeping projects on track, ensuring compliance with permit requirements, and
minimizing project delays. Partnership with local jurisdiction and service providers also helped streamline
approvals and accelerate the overall project timeline.

In addition to permit support, CCES leveraged $333,113.44 in additional grant funding to cover the cost of home
repairs and other measures such as energy audits, diagnostic tests, dwelling assessments, smoke alarms, hot water
flow restrictors, infiltration reduction measures, kitchen range hoods, LED lights, doors, insulation, and mechanical

ventilation. These leveraged sources allowed CCES to address critical home conditions and improve the overall
effectiveness of electrification measures installed through the E&I program.

No training activities occurred with DOE E&I funding. However, through other leveraged funding sources, CCES
launched its Workforce Education Training Center program in 2024 and 2025. This resulted in 26 Energy Efficiency
trainees participating in direct job installation activities under the supervision of knowledgeable, experienced crew
leaders and created 47 employment opportunities in total.

These activities provided additional support to the E&I initiative by expanding workforce capacity and
strengthening staff familiarity with the emerging electrification technologies used in the program. Trainees gained
hands-on experience during job installations, particularly with advanced equipment such as heat pumps.
Additionally, part of the training curriculum included DOE weatherization installation specifications to best align
the field experience with the classroom knowledge gained.

Although these training efforts were supported through other funding sources, the experience gained contributed
to the successful implementation of the E&I program and strengthened CCES’s ability to deliver similar
electrification measures in future programs.
