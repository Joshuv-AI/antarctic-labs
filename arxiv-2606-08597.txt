Kikuchi Graphs of Random Hypergraphs are Approximately Johnson
Pravesh K. Kothari

Abstract
We prove that level- ℓ \ell Kikuchi graphs of random 2 ​ r 2r -uniform hypergraphs spectrally approximate Kikuchi graph of the complete 2 ​ r 2r -uniform hypergraph at a sampling rate that is sharp up to a logarithmic factor, in the regime r ≤ ℓ ≤ n / 2 r\leq\ell\leq n/2 . Our proof is based on the matrix Bernstein inequality, but, unlike prior works, we apply it to an appropriate collection of blocks of Johnson eigenspaces. Our analysis relies on a new, simple band-locality property for arbitrary Kikuchi graphs. As an application, we prove that the natural degree- 2 ​ ℓ 2\ell sum-of-squares relaxation for the Max 2 ​ r 2r -XOR problem is “integral” when the input is a planted noisy 2 ​ r 2r -XOR instance on a random hypergraph with ≳ n ⋅ ( n / ℓ ) r − 1 ​ log ⁡ n \gtrsim n\cdot(n/\ell)^{r-1}\log n hyperedges.

Theorem 1.1

(Random Hypergraphs are Kikuchi Sparsifiers)

Let

Concrete instantiations:

When

When

For the threshold value of the sampling rate

The density requirement is sharp up to a

We give a short argument here. For every

1.2
Proof Idea

As we discussed above, it is not difficult to get an
additive
error bound on
\|L_{H}-pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}\|_{2}\lesssim\varepsilon p\|L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}\|_{2}
but this bound is too large to be useful because
L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
has a condition number of
\Omega(\ell)
(or, there are non-zero eigenvalues of
L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
that are smaller by a factor of
\ell
compared to the largest). A natural route would be to work with normalized Laplacians
\tilde{L}_{H}=\sum_{C\in H}\tilde{L}_{C}
where
\tilde{L}_{C}=(pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}})^{-1/2}L_{C}(pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}})^{-1/2}
and the inverse square root is taken on the subspace orthogonal to
\bm{1}
, in which case an additive error bound would suffice. Unfortunately, for this setting, we do not know how to obtain a good estimate on the spectral norm of the “variance term” that controls the error in the application of the matrix Bernstein inequality.

Our approach is based on two simple but crucial (and somewhat miraculous, in the opinion of the author) observations about the relationship between the eigenvalues and eigenspace dimensions of
L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
and the interaction of
arbitrary
Kikuchi graphs with the eigenspaces of
L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
. Let us briefly describe both of these below.

Eigenvalue Decay in
L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
:

By standard results about the structure of the eigenspaces of
L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
, we know that for each
\ell\geq i\geq 0
, there is an eigenspace
E_{i}
of dimension
{n\choose i}-{n\choose{i-1}}
with eigenvalue that roughly scales as
(i/\ell)\|L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}\|_{2}
. When restricted to a single eigenspace, since all the eigenvalues of
L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
are equal,
L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
has condition number
1
, so one could attempt to apply matrix Bernstein analogously to our first failed attempt above. The dimension-dependent loss in the matrix Bernstein, when applied to
E_{i}
, is proportional to
\sqrt{\log\dim(E_{i})}
. It turns out that this improved bound is precisely enough for us to obtain the precise additive error bound that implies the required multiplicative error when restricted to
E_{i}
at the required density
p
. This is based on the following useful coincidence (or, perhaps, a miraculous conspiracy): at the target density,

\log\dim(E_{i})\asymp i\log n\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lambda_{i}(pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}})\asymp_{k}i\log n/\varepsilon^{2}.

That is, the logarithm of the dimension of
E_{i}
scales as the eigenvalue of
pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
on
E_{i}
.

By a union bound on the
\ell+1
different diagonal blocks, we can conclude that
\Pi_{E_{i}}L_{H}\Pi_{E_{i}}\approx\Pi_{E_{i}}L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}\Pi_{E_{i}}
for every
0\leq i\leq\ell
. This does not, on its own, complete the proof of
Theorem

1.1
. This is because
E_{i}
s need not be (and in fact are not, in general) invariant subspaces for
L_{H}
. Thus, we are worried that for a function
f
on
{[n]\choose\ell}
that has projections in multiple
E_{i}
s, the quadratic forms of
L_{H}
and
pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
may not be close.

Band Limited Action of Kikuchi Graphs on
E_{i}
:

The crucial second idea that rescues our proof approach (with one modification) is the observation that
every
Kikuchi graph satisfies a
band locality
property in its action on
E_{i}
s. Specifically, for any
f\in E_{i}
and every
L_{C}
, one has
L_{C}f\in\oplus_{i-k\leq j\leq i+k}E_{j}
, where
k=2r
. That is,
L_{C}
sends an
f\in E_{i}
to only “nearby eigenspaces”. In particular,
f^{\top}L_{C}g=0
whenever
f
and
g
are elements of
E_{i}
and
E_{j}
such that
|i-j|>k
. We prove this band locality property in
Corollary

4.7
.

This band locality does not imply that the per-eigenspace approximation argued above is enough. But if we were to upgrade the previous argument to obtain a multiplicative approximation on
\oplus_{i-k\leq j\leq i+k}E_{j}
for every
j
(instead of single
E_{j}
s), it turns out that we can finish the proof using the band locality property above. This stronger ”per-
2k
-block of eigenspaces” guarantee also holds because the eigenvalues of
L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
on any
E_{j}
for
j\in[i-k,i+k]\cap\{1,2,\ldots,\ell\}
are within an
O_{k}(1)
factor of each other.

Our argument with these two ideas is quite simple and presented in full in
Section

5
.
After preliminaries, we prove the planted-XOR application, then record the Johnson-scheme eigenspace facts and band locality, and finally prove
Theorem

1.1
.

\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(X^{\prime}\cdot L_{H_{e}})<\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(X^{\prime}\cdot L_{H_{g}}),

By a union bound, both of the above events occur with probability at least
0.98
. Condition on this event for the rest of the proof.

Then,
L_{H_{g}}\succeq(1-\delta)p(1/2+\eta)L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
and
L_{H_{e}}\preceq(1+\delta)p(1/2-\eta)L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
. If
\delta\lesssim\eta
, then,
(1+\delta)p(1/2-\eta)<(1-\delta)p(1/2+\eta)
.

Now, as we recall (from the standard eigenvalue formulas for Johnson scheme in Proposition
4.4
)
L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
has a one-dimensional kernel (along the all-ones direction). Indeed, under
r\leq\ell\leq n/2
,
Proposition

4.4
gives
\lambda_{i}>0
for every
i\geq 1
. Let
\Pi
be the projection matrix orthogonal to the all-ones direction. Then, we must have:

\Pi L_{H_{e}}\Pi\prec\Pi L_{H_{g}}\Pi.

Now, observe that if
X^{\prime}\neq\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}
,
\Pi X^{\prime}\Pi
is non-zero. Indeed, a positive semidefinite matrix with diagonal entries equal to
1
whose projection onto
\bm{1}^{\perp}
vanishes must equal
\bm{1}\bm{1}^{\top}
. Thus, taking trace products with
X^{\prime}
on both sides yields:

\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(X^{\prime}\cdot L_{H_{e}})=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(X^{\prime}\cdot\Pi L_{H_{e}}\Pi)<\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(X^{\prime}\cdot\Pi L_{H_{g}}\Pi)=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(X^{\prime}\cdot L_{H_{g}}).

This completes the proof.
∎

4
Johnson Eigenspaces and Band Limited Action of Kikuchi Graphs

A matrix
M
with rows and columns indexed by
{[n]\choose\ell}
is set-symmetric if its entries depend only on
|S\Delta T|
. Such matrices form a commutative algebra called the
Johnson scheme
and thus admit a simultaneous eigenspace decomposition. We work throughout this section in the regime
\ell\leq n/2
. We will need some elementary facts about these eigenspaces and the corresponding eigenvalues. We direct the reader to
[
12
]
for detailed background on eigenvalues and eigenspaces of the Johnson scheme. We will view these matrices as acting on the linear space of all functions
f:{[n]\choose\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Let us define some special functions that will help us in the following.
For
R\subset[n]
, define
p_{R}
as the function such that at any
S
,
p_{R}(S)=\mathbf{1}_{\{R\subset S\}}
.
For
d\geq 0
, define the space of polynomials of degree at most
d
by
F_{d}=\operatorname{span}\{p_{R}:|R|\leq d\}
. We also set
F_{-1}=0
, and once
d\geq\ell
we identify
F_{d}
with
V
. We observe that
F_{d}
is spanned by “homogeneous” degree
d
polynomials. For any
a\in[n]
, let
x_{a}=\mathbf{1}_{(a\in S)}
be the indicator of whether a given input
S
contains the element
a
. Then, observe that degree
d
polynomials are in fact linear combinations of
\prod_{a\in R}x_{a}
for
|R|=d
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
d\leq\ell
,
F_{d}=\operatorname{span}\{p_{R}:|R|=d\}
and

\dim F_{d}=\binom{n}{d}
. In particular,
F_{\ell}=V
.

Proof.

We first prove that
\{p_{R}||R|=d\}
are linearly independent on
{[n]\choose\ell}
by induction on
d
.

For
d=0
, the claim is clear. Suppose
d\geq 1
, and assume
\sum_{|R|=d}a_{R}p_{R}(S)=0

for every
S\in X_{\ell}
. Fix distinct
p,q\in[n]
, and let
Y\subset[n]\setminus\{p,q\}
such that
|Y|=\ell-1
.
Comparing the above relation at
Y\cup\{p\}
and at
Y\cup\{q\}
, we get
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\subset Y\\
|U|=d-1\end{subarray}}\bigl(a_{U\cup\{p\}}-a_{U\cup\{q\}}\bigr)=0

for every such
Y
.

This is a relation among the functions
p_{U}
,
|U|=d-1
, on the
(\ell-1)
-subsets of the ground set
[n]\setminus\{p,q\}
. Since

d-1\leq\min\{\ell-1,n-\ell-1\}
,
the induction hypothesis gives
a_{U\cup\{p\}}=a_{U\cup\{q\}}
for every
U\subset[n]\setminus\{p,q\}
of size
d-1
. Hence any two
d
-sets differing in exactly one element have the same coefficient. The graph on
d
-subsets where two sets are adjacent if they differ in one element is connected, so all
a_{R}
are equal to some constant
a
. Evaluating the original relation at any
S\in X_{\ell}
gives
0=a\binom{\ell}{d}
.
Thus
a=0
. This proves linear independence.

Now let
|Q|<d
. On
X_{\ell}
we have

p_{Q}(S)=\frac{1}{\binom{\ell-|Q|}{d-|Q|}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\supset Q\\
|R|=d\end{subarray}}p_{R}(S).

Indeed, if
Q\subset S
, then the number of
d
-sets
R
with
Q\subset R\subset S

is
\binom{\ell-|Q|}{d-|Q|}
, while if
Q\not\subset S
, both sides vanish. Therefore
F_{d}
is spanned by the functions
p_{R}
with
|R|=d
. Since these functions are linearly independent,
\dim F_{d}=\binom{n}{d}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For
0\leq i\leq\ell
, define
E_{i}=F_{i}\cap F_{i-1}^{\perp}
.
Then

V=E_{0}\oplus E_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{\ell}

orthogonally, and
\dim E_{i}=\binom{n}{i}-\binom{n}{i-1}
.

Proof.

Since
0=F_{-1}\subset F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\cdots\subset F_{\ell}=V
,
we have
F_{i}=F_{i-1}\oplus E_{i}

orthogonally. Iterating gives the orthogonal decomposition of
V
. Also,
\dim E_{i}=\dim F_{i}-\dim F_{i-1}=\binom{n}{i}-\binom{n}{i-1}
.
∎

These spaces are the standard Johnson eigenspaces.
The eigenspaces of the matrices from the Johnson scheme can be described in terms of the more familiar polynomial functions. We record this as the following easy observation.

Lemma 4.3

(Polynomial description)
.

Let
x_{a}(S)=\mathbf{1}_{\{a\in S\}}
and let
f:{[n]\choose\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a degree
\leq d
polynomial in
x_{a}
. Then,
f\in F_{d}
.

Proof.

Every monomial
x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\cdots x_{a_{q}}

with distinct variables is exactly
p_{R}
, where
R=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{q}\}
.
Thus every polynomial of degree at most
d
is a linear combination of functions
p_{R}
with
|R|\leq d
, hence lies in
F_{d}
.
∎

Proposition 4.4

(Eigenvalues)
.

Assume
r\leq\ell\leq n/2
. Let
L=D_{k}-K_{H_{full}}
be the Laplacian of
K_{H_{full}}
, where
D_{k}=d_{k}I
and
d_{k}={\ell\choose r}{{n-\ell}\choose r}
is the degree of any vertex in
K_{H_{full}}
. By the Eberlein polynomial formula for the Johnson scheme
[
1
, Theorem 3.13]
, the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
on
E_{i}
is given by:

\lambda_{i}=\binom{\ell}{r}\binom{n-\ell}{r}-\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{j}\binom{i}{j}\binom{\ell-i}{r-j}\binom{n-\ell-i}{r-j}.

For
r
fixed and
\ell\to\infty
, we have:

\frac{\lambda_{i}}{d_{k}}=1-\left(1-\frac{i}{\ell}\right)^{k/2}\left(1-\frac{i}{n-\ell}\right)^{k/2}+O_{k}\left(\frac{1}{\ell}\right).

In particular, for fixed
k=2r
and
n
sufficiently large depending only on
k
, for every
1\leq i\leq\ell
,

c_{1}\frac{i}{\ell}\leq\frac{\lambda_{i}}{d_{k}}\leq c_{2}\frac{i}{\ell}

for some constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
depending only on
k
.
Indeed, for
i\leq c_{k}\ell
, expanding the exact formula to first order gives
\lambda_{i}/d_{k}=\Theta_{k}(i(1/\ell+1/(n-\ell)))=\Theta_{k}(i/\ell)
. For
i>c_{k}\ell
, the displayed approximation gives
\lambda_{i}/d_{k}=\Theta_{k}(1)=\Theta_{k}(i/\ell)
.

4.1
Band Limited Action of
K_{C}
s

Kikuchi graphs of arbitrary hypergraphs are
not
matrices from the Johnson scheme. Thus,
E_{i}
s are no longer invariant subspaces for the action of arbitrary Kikuchi graphs. Nevertheless, we make
2
2

2

While the proof is simple and short, we are not aware of any work that has made or used this observation in prior work.
the following simple observation that the action of Kikuchi graphs on
E_{i}
is more structured than one might expect.

Lemma 4.5

(Lower Limits from Upper Limits)
.

Let
T:V\to V
be self-adjoint. Suppose that for some
s\geq 0
,
TF_{d}\subseteq F_{d+s}
for every
d
. Then, for every
0\leq i\leq\ell
,

TE_{i}\subseteq\bigoplus_{j=\max(0,i-s)}^{\min(\ell,i+s)}E_{j}.

Proof.

Since
E_{i}\subset F_{i}
, the assumption gives

TE_{i}\subseteq F_{i+s}=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\min(\ell,i+s)}E_{j}
.
We now prove the lower containment.

Let
x\in E_{i}
, and let
y\in E_{j}
with
j<i-s
. Then

Ty\in F_{j+s}\subseteq F_{i-1}
.
Since
x\perp F_{i-1}
, and since
T
is self-adjoint,

\langle Tx,y\rangle=\langle x,Ty\rangle=0
.
Therefore
Tx
has no component in any
E_{j}
with
j<i-s
. This proves the claim.
∎

Corollary 4.6

(
K_{C}
s have a band-limited action)
.

For every
0\leq i\leq\ell
,

K_{C}E_{i}\subseteq\bigoplus_{j=\max(0,i-k)}^{\min(\ell,i+k)}E_{j}.

Proof.

K_{C}
is a symmetric matrix so by Lemma
4.5
it is enough to prove that

K_{C}F_{d}\subseteq F_{d+k}

for every
d
. Since
F_{d}
is spanned by
p_{R}
with
|R|\leq d
, we can restrict attention to the action of
K_{C}
on the functions
p_{R}
. Write
x_{a}(S)=\mathbf{1}_{\{a\in S\}}
.
Then

p_{R}(S\triangle C)=\prod_{a\in R\setminus C}x_{a}\prod_{a\in R\cap C}(1-x_{a})
.
This is a polynomial in the variables
x_{a}
of degree at most
|R|\leq d
.

Also,

\mathbf{1}_{\{|S\cap C|=r\}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\subset C\\
|U|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{u\in U}x_{u}\prod_{v\in C\setminus U}(1-x_{v}).

This is a polynomial of degree at most
|C|=k
. Therefore,

(K_{C}p_{R})(S)=\mathbf{1}_{\{|S\cap C|=r\}}p_{R}(S\triangle C)

is represented by a polynomial of degree at most
d+k
. Applying Lemma
4.3
finishes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.7

(
L_{C}
s have a band-limited action)
.

For every
0\leq i\leq\ell
,

L_{C}E_{i}\subseteq\bigoplus_{j=\max(0,i-k)}^{\min(\ell,i+k)}E_{j}.

Proof.

Write
L_{C}=D_{C}-K_{C}
, where
D_{C}
is the diagonal degree matrix of
K_{C}
. The
K_{C}
term has the desired property by
Corollary

4.6
. It remains to handle
D_{C}
. Since

(D_{C}p_{R})(S)=\mathbf{1}_{\{|S\cap C|=r\}}p_{R}(S),

and
\mathbf{1}_{\{|S\cap C|=r\}}
is a degree-
k
polynomial in the variables
x_{a}
, we have
D_{C}F_{d}\subseteq F_{d+k}
for every
d
. Applying
Lemma

4.5
to the self-adjoint operator
D_{C}
gives the same band-limited conclusion for
D_{C}
, and therefore also for
L_{C}
.
∎

5
Kikuchi Graphs of Random Hypergraphs
\approx
Johnson

In this section, we prove
Theorem

1.1
.

Theorem 5.1

(
Theorem

1.1
restated)
.

Let
k=2r
for a positive integer
r
, let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1/2
, and let
H
be a random
k
-uniform hypergraph with density
p
. For any
r\leq\ell=\ell(n)\leq n/2
, let
L_{H}=D_{H}-K_{H}
be the Laplacian matrix of
K_{H}=K_{H}^{(\ell)}
. If
p
is such that
p{n\choose k}\gtrsim_{k}(n/\varepsilon^{2})(n/\ell)^{k/2-1}\log n
, then, with probability at least
1-1/(100n)
over the draw of
H
,

(1-\varepsilon)pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}\preceq L_{H}\preceq(1+\varepsilon)pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}.

Proof.

For each
0\leq i\leq\ell
, let

W_{i}=\bigoplus_{h=\max(0,i-k)}^{\min(\ell,i+k)}E_{h},\qquad W_{i}^{+}=W_{i}\cap\bm{1}^{\perp},

and let
\Pi_{i}
and
\Pi_{W_{i}}
be the orthogonal projection matrices onto
W_{i}^{+}
and
W_{i}
, respectively. Let
\Pi_{E_{i}}
be the orthogonal projection matrix onto the subspace
E_{i}
. Let
\lambda_{h}
denote the eigenvalue of
L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
on
E_{h}
, and define the positive block scales

\Lambda_{i}^{-}=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in[\max(0,i-k),\min(\ell,i+k)]\\
h\geq 1\end{subarray}}p\lambda_{h},\qquad\Lambda_{i}^{+}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in[\max(0,i-k),\min(\ell,i+k)]\\
h\geq 1\end{subarray}}p\lambda_{h}.

All Laplacians vanish on the all-ones direction, so the
E_{0}
component never contributes; this is why the concentration argument below is stated on
W_{i}^{+}
.

We will prove the following claim, which shows that the sparsification claim holds if we project the matrices down to any
W_{i}^{+}
. The proof relies on a (rank-sensitive) application of matrix Bernstein inequality and exploits the crucial fact that the eigenvalue of
L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
on
E_{i}
is proportional to
\log\dim(E_{i})
:

Claim 5.2
.

Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}\lesssim_{k}\varepsilon
. Suppose
p{n\choose k}\gtrsim_{k}(n/\varepsilon^{\prime 2})(n/\ell)^{k/2-1}\log n
. Then, with probability at least
1-1/(100n)
, for every
0\leq i\leq\ell
,

\left\|\Pi_{i}(L_{H}-pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}})\Pi_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}\Lambda_{i}^{-}.

Consequently,

\Pi_{W_{i}}L_{H}\Pi_{W_{i}}\approx_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}p\Pi_{W_{i}}L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}\Pi_{W_{i}}.

While not true in general, we will exploit the band limited action of Kikuchi graphs on
E_{i}
to show that this is sufficient to imply the theorem with error
O_{k}(\varepsilon^{\prime})
.

Condition on
L_{H}
satisfying the conclusion of the claim above. Let
A=L_{H}-pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
and let
f
be an arbitrary function
{[n]\choose\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. We will prove
f^{\top}L_{H}f\in(1\pm\varepsilon)pf^{\top}L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}f
. Let
f_{i}=\Pi_{E_{i}}f
. Then,
f=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}f_{i}
. Observe that since the
E_{i}
are eigenspaces of
L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
, we have:

f^{\top}pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}f=\sum_{i}f_{i}^{\top}pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}f_{i}

From Corollary
4.7
, we know that
f_{i}^{\top}L_{H}f_{j}=0
if
|i-j|>k
. Since
L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}
is diagonal in the
E_{i}
decomposition, this implies that
f_{i}^{\top}Af_{j}=0
if
|i-j|>k
. From Claim
5.2
applied to each
f_{i}
, we have:

|f_{i}^{\top}Af_{i}|\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}f_{i}^{\top}pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}f_{i}.

Further, for any
i,j
such that
|i-j|\leq k
, the cross term is zero if either
i=0
or
j=0
because
A
kills constants. Otherwise,
f_{i}+f_{j}\in W_{i}^{+}
, so by Claim
5.2
and the above bound, we have:

\displaystyle|f_{i}^{\top}Af_{j}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left|(f_{i}+f_{j})^{\top}A(f_{i}+f_{j})-f_{i}^{\top}Af_{i}-f_{j}^{\top}Af_{j}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}(f_{i}^{\top}pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}f_{i}+f_{j}^{\top}pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}f_{j}).

Thus, we have:

\displaystyle|f^{\top}Af|=|\sum_{0\leq i,j\leq\ell}f_{i}^{\top}Af_{j}|

\displaystyle=|\sum_{0\leq i\leq\ell}f_{i}^{\top}Af_{i}+\sum_{0\leq i\leq\ell}\sum_{j\neq i:|i-j|\leq k}f_{i}^{\top}Af_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq 5k\varepsilon^{\prime}\left(\sum_{i}f_{i}^{\top}pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}f_{i}\right).

Choosing
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon/5k
finishes the proof.

Proof of claim.

Fix
0\leq i\leq\ell
and write
Q_{i}=\dim(W_{i}^{+})
. We know that
L_{H}=\sum_{C\in H_{full}}\bm{1}(C\in H)L_{C}
where
L_{C}
is the Laplacian of
K_{C}
and
\bm{1}(C\in H)
is the indicator random variable for whether
C\in H
. Thus,
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}[\bm{1}(C\in H)]=p
and

X_{C}=(\bm{1}(C\in H)-p)\Pi_{i}L_{C}\Pi_{i}

is a random matrix with mean
0
and spectral norm at most
\|L_{C}\|_{2}\leq 2
with probability
1
. Further, using
L_{C}^{2}=2L_{C}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{C}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}[X_{C}^{2}]

\displaystyle\preceq\sum_{C}p(\Pi_{i}L_{C}\Pi_{i})^{2}

\displaystyle\preceq\sum_{C}p\Pi_{i}L_{C}^{2}\Pi_{i}=2p\Pi_{i}L_{H_{\mathsf{full}}}\Pi_{i}\preceq 2\Lambda_{i}^{+}I.

By
Proposition

4.4
, the eigenvalues inside a
k
-window satisfy
\Lambda_{i}^{+}\leq C_{k}\Lambda_{i}^{-}
. At the assumed density, after increasing the hidden constant,
\Lambda_{i}^{-}\geq C_{k}\varepsilon^{\prime-2}\log(200n^{2}Q_{i})
.

Matrix Bernstein therefore gives

\mathbb{P}\left[\left\|\sum_{C}X_{C}\right\|_{2}\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}\Lambda_{i}^{-}\right]\leq 2Q_{i}\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime 2}(\Lambda_{i}^{-})^{2}}{2(2\Lambda_{i}^{+}+2\varepsilon^{\prime}\Lambda_{i}^{-}/3)}\right)\leq\frac{1}{200n^{2}}.

Thus, with probability at least
1-1/(200n^{2})
,

\left\|\Pi_{i}(L_{H}-pL_{H_{\mathsf{full}}})\Pi_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}\Lambda_{i}^{-}.

A union bound over the
\ell+1
choices of
i
completes the proof of the claim. ∎
