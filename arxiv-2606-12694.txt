A Unified Complexity Bound for Logconcave Sampling
Yunbum Kook, Santosh S. Vempala

Abstract
We give a simple, unified, and nearly tight bound for sampling arbitrary logconcave distributions from a warm start using the 𝖨𝗇 ​ - ​ 𝖺𝗇𝖽 ​ - ​ 𝖮𝗎𝗍 \mathsf{\mathsf{In\text{-}and\text{-}Out}} algorithm along with exponential lifting. The main new ingredient in the analysis is an improved bound on the Poincaré constant of a lifted distribution. As a consequence, the resulting convergence rate is nearly tight for both constrained settings (e.g., Gaussian restricted to a convex body) and well-conditioned settings (e.g., strongly logconcave and smooth densities).

Technical ideas.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, one can check that
\lVert\operatorname{cov}\pi^{Z}\rVert\lesssim\lVert\operatorname{cov}\pi^{X}\rVert+\operatorname{Var}_{\pi^{Z}}T

Improved covariance of the lifted distribution via varentropy

In this section, we prove Lemma
1.3
. Let
\pi^{X}\propto e^{-V}

be a full-dimensional logconcave probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

\pi^{Z}
the lifted distribution (
exp-lift
). Note that
one can generate an
O(1)
-warm start for
\pi^{Z}
from an
M
-warm
start for
\pi^{X}
(see the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 2.15]
).

First claim.

We have already observed that
\operatorname{Var}_{\pi^{Z}}T=\frac{\operatorname{Var}_{\pi^{X}}(\log\pi^{X})+1}{d^{2}}
.
Using (
varentropy
), we establish
\operatorname{Var}T\leq 2/d
. It remains
to pass from this to the full covariance matrix. For every
(u,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}

with
\lVert u\rVert^{2}+s^{2}=1
, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}_{\pi^{Z}}(u^{\mathsf{T}}X+sT)

\displaystyle=u^{\mathsf{T}}\operatorname{cov}\pi^{X}u+2\operatorname{cov}_{\pi^{Z}}(u^{\mathsf{T}}X,sT)+s^{2}\operatorname{Var}_{\pi^{Z}}T\leq\bigl(\lVert\operatorname{cov}\pi^{X}\rVert^{1/2}\,\lVert u\rVert+\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{\pi^{Z}}T}\,|s|\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\lVert\operatorname{cov}\pi^{X}\rVert+\operatorname{Var}_{\pi^{Z}}T)(\lVert u\rVert^{2}+s^{2})\leq\lVert\operatorname{cov}\pi^{X}\rVert+\frac{2}{d}\,.

Taking the supremum over unit
(u,s)
proves the first claim.

Second claim.

The next lemma shows that
O(1/d)
is absorbed by the intrinsic covariance
scale under our ground-set normalization (
ground-set
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume the lower-semicontinuous
convex representative of
V
satisfies (
ground-set
).
Then

\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{cov}\pi^{X})\gtrsim 1\,,\qquad C_{\mathsf{PI}}(\pi^{X})\geq\lVert\operatorname{cov}\pi^{X}\rVert\gtrsim\frac{1}{d}\,.

(2.1)

Proof.

Let
f=f_{X}=e^{-V}/Z_{X}
. Since the logconcave density
f
has
a mode, it should attain a maximum. Indeed, any level set of
V

should be bounded (otherwise, the density is not integrable). By
[
16
, Theorem 1.9]
,

m:=\inf V
is attained.

Let

M:=\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}=\frac{e^{-m}}{Z_{X}}\,.

The ground-set assumption gives
f(x)\geq e^{-10d}M
on
B(0,1)
.
Thus

1=\int f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq e^{-10d}M\,\operatorname{vol}B_{2}^{d}\,,

and hence

M\leq\frac{e^{10d}}{\operatorname{vol}(B_{2}^{d})}\leq(C\sqrt{d})^{d}\,.

(2.2)

Let
\mu=\mathbb{E}X
and
\Sigma_{X}=\operatorname{cov}\pi^{X}
. Since
\pi^{X}
is
full-dimensional and logconcave,
\Sigma_{X}
is positive definite.
The isotropic random vector
Y=\Sigma_{X}^{-1/2}\,(X-\mu)
has density

g(y)\,\mathrm{d}y
, where

g(y)=\sqrt{\det\Sigma_{X}}\,f(\mu+\Sigma_{X}^{1/2}y)\,.

Thus
\lVert g\rVert_{\infty}=\sqrt{\det\Sigma_{X}}\,M
. The Gaussian maximum-entropy
bound gives

h(Y):=\mathbb{E}[-\log g(Y)]\leq\frac{d}{2}\log(2\pi e)\,,

while
h(Y)=\mathbb{E}[-\log g(Y)]\geq-\log\lVert g\rVert_{\infty}
. Therefore
\lVert g\rVert_{\infty}\geq(2\pi e)^{-d/2}=c^{d}
,
and

\sqrt{\det\Sigma_{X}}\,M\geq c^{d}\,.

Combining this with (
2.2
) yields

(\det\Sigma_{X})^{1/d}\gtrsim\frac{1}{d}\,.

The arithmetic–geometric mean inequality gives
\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{X}\geq d\,(\det\Sigma_{X})^{1/d}\gtrsim 1
,
and also
\lVert\Sigma_{X}\rVert\geq(\det\Sigma_{X})^{1/d}\gtrsim 1/d
.
Finally,
C_{\mathsf{PI}}(\pi^{X})\geq\lVert\operatorname{cov}\pi^{X}\rVert
by the Poincaré
inequality on linear functions.
∎

3
Unified complexity for logconcave sampling

The improved covariance estimate has two consequences for sampling
from a warm start (see Theorem
1.1
). For finite Rényi
guarantees, the relevant functional inequality is the Poincaré
inequality, and the covariance improvement directly affects the query
complexity. For an
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
-guarantee, the natural mixing
statement is based on
log-Sobolev inequality
(LSI): A probability
measure
\pi
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to satisfy a logarithmic Sobolev
inequality with constant
C
if for any locally Lipschitz function

f\in L^{2}(\pi)
,

\operatorname{Ent}_{\pi}(f^{2}):=\int f^{2}\log f^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\pi-\int f^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\pi\cdot\log\int f^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\pi\leq 2C\int\lVert\nabla f\rVert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\pi\,,

(
\mathsf{LSI}
)

and the smallest such
C
is referred to as the log-Sobolev constant

C_{\mathsf{LSI}}(\pi)
. Starting from an
O(1)
-warm initial distribution,
the proximal sampler (or INO) mixes at a rate governed by the LSI
constant of the lifted target.

\mathcal{R}_{q}
-guarantee from
\mathcal{R}_{q}
-warmness
(PI).

For a finite Rényi guarantee, the cleanest guarantee is the balanced
warm-start theorem of
[

[
11
]

same exponential-lift sampler with proper restart, but requires a

-warm start for

-guarantees.

Theorem 1.7 of
[
12
]
provides query complexity

from an

warm start in

of the exponential lift.

The restart, rejection-sampling, and

\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
-guarantee from
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
-warmness
(LSI).

The finite-

Proposition 3.1
.

Under (

\varepsilon\in(0,1)

C_{\mathsf{LSI}}(\pi_{\mathrm{tr}}^{Z})\lesssim R_{\varepsilon}\,\lVert\operatorname{cov}\pi^{X}\rVert^{1/2}\log^{1/2}d\,.

Proof.

For unit vector

, we have
\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{\mathrm{tr}}^{Z}}(w^{\mathsf{T}}Z)\leq\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{tr}}^{Z}}[(w^{\mathsf{T}}Z-\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{Z}}w^{\mathsf{T}}Z)^{2}]\leq 2\operatorname{Var}_{\pi^{Z}}(w^{\mathsf{T}}Z)

Thus,

\lVert\operatorname{cov}\pi_{\mathrm{tr}}^{Z}\rVert\leq 2\lVert\operatorname{cov}\pi^{Z}\rVert\underset{\text{Lemma }\ref{lem:main}}{\lesssim}2\lVert\operatorname{cov}\pi^{X}\rVert\,.

Using the LSI bound for logconcave measure with bounded support

C_{\mathsf{LSI}}(\pi_{\mathrm{tr}}^{Z})\lesssim R_{\varepsilon}C_{\mathsf{PI}}(\pi_{\mathrm{tr}}^{Z})^{1/2}\lesssim R_{\varepsilon}\,\lVert\operatorname{cov}\pi_{\mathrm{tr}}^{Z}\rVert^{1/2}\log^{1/2}d\lesssim R_{\varepsilon}\,\lVert\operatorname{cov}\pi^{X}\rVert^{1/2}\log^{1/2}d\,,

which completes the proof.
