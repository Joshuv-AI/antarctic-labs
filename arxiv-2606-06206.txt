Abstract




Abstract
We study some local and global properties of solutions of
-\Delta u-m\left|\nabla u\right|^{q}-e^{u}=0
in a punctured domain
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
, or in an exterior domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
, where
m
is a positive parameter and
q>1
. We study particularly the local behaviour of solutions with an isolated singularity or the asymptotic behaviour for solutions defined in an exterior domain, and also the existence of solutions with the behaviours previously described. These behaviours change drastically according
q
is smaller or larger than
2
. Many results are obtained by introducing various dynamical systems associated to the equation.

.

.

Introduction

The aim of this article is to study local or global behaviour of functions which satisfy an equation of viscous
Chandrasekhar-Hamilton-Jacobi type

-\Delta u-m|\nabla u|^{q}-e^{u}=0,

(1.1
)

either in a punctured domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
or in an exterior domain, as well as the existence of solutions with the behaviour that we put into light. Throughout this article we assume that
m>0
,
N\geq 2
and
q>1
. However, since our results depend strongly on the value of
q
with respect to
\frac{N}{N-1}
and
2
, in most statements we will recall the range of the parameters
q
and
N
. Because the problem is local and invariant by translation, we assume that the equation
(\ref{Na-1})
holds
either in
B_{r_{0}}\setminus\{0\}
or in
B^{c}_{r_{0}}
. This equation exhibits a large variety of phenomena, since besides the diffusion operator, the reaction terms are nonlinear and of a different nature. According to the range of values of the parameters and the solutions, the behaviour can be modelled by two underlying nonlinear equations:

the
viscous Hamilton-Jacobi equation

-\Delta u-m|\nabla u|^{q}=0,

(1.2
)

and the
Emden-Chandrasekhar equation

-\Delta u-e^{u}=0.

(1.3
)

At each occurrence the difficulty is to prove that one of the three terms in the equation is negligible compared to the other two . These two equations admit specific singular solutions which play a fundamental role
for studying
(\ref{Na-1})
, namely for equation
(\ref{Na-3})
solutions under the form
|x|^{2}e^{u(x)}=\omega(\frac{x}{|x|})
for functions
\omega
satisfying a nonlinear equation on
S^{N-1}
, and for equation
(\ref{Na-2})
when
1<q<\frac{N}{N-1}
, solutions under the form
C_{N,q}|x|^{-\beta}
with
C_{N,q}<0
where

\beta=\frac{2-q}{q-1}.

(1.4
)

The following equation of a different Chandrasekhar-Hamilton-Jacobi type

-\Delta u+m|\nabla u|^{q}-e^{u}=0

(1.5
)

has been thoroughly studied by two of the authors in
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
. The main difference with
(\ref{Na-1})
lies in the fact that in
(\ref{Na-4})
the
eikonal equation

m|\nabla u|^{q}-e^{u}=0

(1.6
)

had there an important role for describing the singular solutions.

By a solution
u
(resp. (i) supersolution, (ii) sub-solution) of
(\ref{Na-1})
in a domain
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we mean a function
u\in C^{1}(G)
satisfying the following relations in the sense of distributions in
G
,

-\Delta u-m|\nabla u|^{q}-e^{u}=0,

(1.7
)

\left(\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\phantom{P^{P^{P}}}\text{resp. }\;(i)\;-\Delta u-m|\nabla u|^{q}-e^{u}\geq 0\,,\,\text{ resp. }\;(ii)\;-\Delta u-m|\nabla u|^{q}-e^{u}\leq 0.\right)\quad\quad

(1.8
)

For any function expressed in spherical coordinates
u(x)=u(r,\sigma)
with
(r,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times S^{N-1}
we denote by
\bar{u}(r)
the spherical average of
u
, and we recall that if
u
is super-harmonic (resp. sub-harmonic) in a ball
B_{R}

u(0)\geq\bar{u}(r)
(resp.
u(0)\leq\bar{u}(r)
) for any

.
Furthermore if

is a solution of (
1.1

) (or even a supersolution) then

is a supersolution.

As a consequence of these observations, we prove

Assume

2- If

When supersolutions of

Theorem 1.3

Let

Next we set

\kappa=\frac{(N-1)q-N}{q-1}.

(1.12

We obtain another type of estimates from below for supersolutions, and these estimates are obtained via an estimate of the gradient of

Theorem 1.4

Let

\bar{u}(r)\geq\bar{u}(r_{1})-\frac{q-1}{q-2}\left(\frac{\kappa}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}\left(r^{\frac{q-2}{q-1}}-r_{1}^{\frac{q-2}{q-1}}\right)\quad\text{for all }r\geq r_{1}.

(1.14

Finally, if

(1.15

Next we give our main results concerning the solutions of
(\ref{Na-1})
. Here the Bernstein technique is an essential tool. We first prove that in the case
q>2

isolated singularities are removable
.

Theorem 1.5

Let
q>2
. If
u
is a solution of
(\ref{Na-1})
in
B_{r_{0}}\setminus\{0\}
, then there exists
C=C(N,q,m)>0
such that

|\nabla u(x)|\leq C|x|^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}\quad\text{in }B_{\frac{r_{0}}{2}}\setminus\{0\}.

(1.16
)

As a consequence
u
satisfies

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C^{\prime}|x-y|^{-\beta}\quad\text{for all }(x,y)\in B_{\frac{r_{0}}{2}}\setminus\{0\}.

(1.17
)

Hence
u
can be extended as a continuous solution of
(\ref{Na-1})
in
B_{r_{0}}
.

It is important to notice that since
m>0
and
q>2
the gradient term has a strong regularising effect. This phenomenon points out the fact that the Hamilton-Jacobi equation is dominant and the exponential term is negligible. A similar a priori estimate of the gradient holds for solutions in an exterior domain.

Corollary 1.6

Let
q>2
. If
u
is a solution of
(\ref{Na-1})
in
B^{c}_{r_{0}}
there exists
C=C(N,q,m)>0
such that

|\nabla u(x)|\leq C|x|^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}\quad\text{in }B^{c}_{2r_{0}},

(1.18
)

and, with another constant
C>0
,

|u(x)|\leq C|x|^{\frac{q-2}{q-1}}+\max_{|y|=r_{0}}|u(y)|\quad\text{in }B^{c}_{2r_{0}}.

(1.19
)

When
1<q<2
the effect of the gradient term is not sufficient to counterbalance the exponential reaction term, thus the a priori estimate involves these two terms. This phenomenon has to be put in parallel with
the case
m=0
where no a priori estimate is known when
N\geq 3
.
The counterpart of Theorem
1.5

The eigenvalues

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
are distinct, except in the case
N=3,

q=\frac{3+\sqrt{3}}{2},
or
N=2,q=2+\sqrt{2}
. Since all the eigenvalues are
positive, there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{V}
of
P_{0}
such that all
the trajectories starting from
\mathcal{V}
converge to
P_{0}
as

t\rightarrow\infty.
Adapting carefully the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 28]
, since the system of order 3 is not equivalent to the
equation
(\ref{Na-1})
we show that
for any

u_{0}
there exist a solution
u
satisfying
(\ref{Na-25})
.
\square

Remark.
By a technical adaptation of the above proof it is possible to extend the result to the case
N=1
. The only difference is that the constant

c_{N,q,m}=\frac{q-1}{q-2}\left(\frac{\kappa}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}
in
(\ref{Na-25})
has to be replaced by
c_{1,q,m}=\frac{1-q}{q-2}\left(-\frac{\kappa}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}<0
.

6
Appendix

In this appendix we give an alternative proof of a variant of Assertion 2 in Theorem
1.13
, which does not use the notion of super and sub viscosity solutions but is based upon the geometric theory of dynamical systems. This proof is longer but its field of applications is not restricted to equation
(\ref{Na-1})
and is well adapted to dynamical systems in presence of a saddle point equilibrium. The proof is inspired by a
construction due to Dieudonné
[
13
]
.

Theorem 6.1

Let
N\geq 3
and
q>2
. We can find
r_{0}>0
and
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
|u_{0}|<\epsilon_{0}
there exists a unique radial solution of
(\ref{Na-1})
in
B_{r_{0}}^{c}
satisfying
u(r_{0})=u_{0}
and

\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{\beta}u(r)=-\frac{q-1}{q-2}\left(\frac{\kappa}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}\quad\text{and }\,\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{\frac{1}{q-1}}u_{r}(r)=-\left(\frac{\kappa}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}.

(6.24
)

Proof.
1-
The dynamical system formulation.
We use system
(\ref{T3})
with variable
U(r)=-u(r)
,
\xi(t)=r^{-\beta}U(r)
,
\eta(t)=r^{\frac{1}{q-1}}U_{r}(r)
and
t=\ln r
. We define the vector field

{\mathcal{F}}(\xi,\eta)=(\beta\xi+\eta,-\kappa\eta+m|\eta|^{q}).

with equilibria
(0,0)
and
(\xi_{m},\eta_{m}):=(\xi_{m},\frac{q-2}{q-1}\xi_{m})
where
\xi_{m}=\frac{q-1}{q-2}\left(\frac{\kappa}{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}
. Then

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle D{\mathcal{F}}(\xi_{m},\eta_{m})=\begin{pmatrix}\beta&1\\[5.69054pt]
0&-\kappa+qm\eta_{m}^{q-1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\beta&1\\[5.69054pt]
0&(q-1)\kappa\end{pmatrix}:=Q,\end{array}

(6.25
)

and the eigenvalues of
Q
are
\beta<0<(q-1)\kappa
and corresponding eigenvectors
(1,0)
and
((q-1)\kappa-\beta,1)
. We have seen that
(\xi_{m},\eta_{m})
is a saddle point. We set
x(t)=\xi(t)-\xi_{m}
,
y(t)=\eta(t)-\eta_{m}
and obtain the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle x_{t}=\beta x+y\\[2.84526pt]
y_{t}=(q-1)\kappa y+b_{1}(y)+b_{2}(t,x(t)),\end{array}\right.

(6.26
)

with

b_{1}(y)=\frac{q(q-1)}{2}(\kappa^{q-2}m)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}+o(y^{2})\,\text{ as }\,y\to 0\,\text{ and }\,b_{2}(t,x)=e^{\frac{qt}{q-1}}e^{(-\xi_{m}+x)e^{-\beta t}},

(6.27
)

The matrix
Q
of the linear part of the system
(\ref{W2})
can be put into a diagonal form. If we set
h=\frac{1}{(q-1)\kappa-\beta}>0
and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}&x=X+Y\\
&y=\frac{Y}{h}\end{array}\right.\;\text{ which is equivalent to }\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}&X=x-hy\\
&Y=hy.\end{array}\right.

(6.28
)

then

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}X_{t}=\beta X-h\left(b_{1}(h^{-1}Y)+b_{2}(t,X+Y)\right)\\
Y_{t}=(q-1)\kappa Y+h\left(b_{1}(h^{-1}Y)+b_{2}(t,X+Y)\right).\end{array}\right.

(6.29
)

We set

B(t,X,Y)=b_{1}(h^{-1}Y)+b_{2}(t,X+Y).

Since by
(\ref{W3})
we have

|b_{1}(h^{-1}Y)|=o(Y^{2})\quad\text{as }Y\to 0

we deduce that for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
(Y,Y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfying
\max\{|Y|,|Y^{\prime}|\}\leq\delta
, we have

|b_{1}(h^{-1}Y)-b_{1}(h^{-1}Y^{\prime})|\leq\epsilon(|Y-Y^{\prime}|).

(6.30
)

Next we estimate the second term in
B(t,X,Y)
.

Lemma 6.2

For any
M>0
there exists
a>0
such that for any
(X,X^{\prime},Y,Y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
satisfying
\max\{|X|,|X^{\prime}|,|Y|,|Y^{\prime}|\}\leq M
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}|b_{2}(X+Y,t)-b_{2}(X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime},t)|\leq ae^{\frac{qt}{q-1}-\xi_{m}e^{-\beta t}}\left(|X-X^{\prime}|+|Y-Y^{\prime}|\right).\end{array}

(6.31
)

We set
{\mathcal{U}}(t)=(X(t),Y(t))
and

F(t,X(t),Y(t))=F(t,{\mathcal{U}}(t))=(-hB(t,{\mathcal{U}}(t),hB(t,{\mathcal{U}}(t))).

(6.32
)

The main difference (and difficulty) with Dieudonné’s approach
[
13
, Chapter 13]
is that
F(t,0)
is not zero. We look for solutions satisfying the following integral equation on
[t_{0},\infty)
for some
t_{0}
to be fixed later on,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle X(t)=e^{\beta(t-t_{0})}x_{0}-h\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{\beta(t-s)}B(s,X(s),Y(s))ds\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle Y(t)=-h\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{(q-1)\kappa(t-s)}B(s,X(s),Y(s))ds,\end{array}\right.

(6.33
)

for some
x_{0}
and provided
X^{2}(t)+Y^{2}(t)
is small enough, and more precisely

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle|x_{0}|\leq\frac{\xi_{m}}{3}\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle|X(t)|+|Y(t)|\leq 2\sqrt{X^{2}(t)+Y^{2}(t)}\leq\frac{\xi_{m}}{3}.\end{array}\right.

(6.34
)

2-
The iterative scheme .
We set
{\mathcal{U}}_{n}(t)=(X_{n}(t),Y_{n}(t))
and consider the scheme associated to the integral equations defined in
(\ref{W9})
where
{\mathcal{U}}_{0}=(0,0)
and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle X_{n+1}(t)=e^{\beta(t-t_{0})}x_{0}-h\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{\beta(t-s)}B(s,X_{n}(s),Y_{n}(s))ds\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle Y_{n+1}(t)=-h\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{\beta(t-s)}B(s,X_{n}(s),Y_{n}(s))ds.\end{array}\right.

(6.35
)

When
n=1
we have for
t\geq t_{0}

|X_{1}(t)|\leq e^{\beta(t-t_{0})}\left(|x_{0}|+e^{\beta t_{0}}h\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{2s-\xi_{m}e^{-\beta s}}ds\right)\leq e^{\beta(t-t_{0})}\left(|x_{0}|+c_{2}(t_{0})\right),

(6.36
)

where

c_{2}(t_{0})=e^{\beta t_{0}}\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{2s-\xi_{m}e^{-\beta s}}ds=\int_{e^{t_{0}}}^{\infty}e^{-\xi_{m}e^{\beta X}}XdX\to 0\quad\text{as }t_{0}\to\infty,

and

|Y_{1}(t)|\leq e^{\kappa(q-1)t}\left(c_{3}h\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{\frac{q-2-\kappa(q-1)^{2}}{q-1}s-\xi_{m}e^{-\beta s}}ds\right)\leq c_{4}(t_{0})e^{-\frac{\xi_{m}}{2}e^{-\beta t}},

(6.37
)

where also
c_{4}(t_{0})\to 0
if
t_{0}\to\infty
.

Lemma 6.3

There exists
K>0
such that for any
n\geq 1
there holds

|X_{n+1}(t)-X_{n}(t)|+|Y_{n+1}(t)-Y_{n}(t)|\leq\frac{K}{2^{n}}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}(t-t_{0})}(|x_{0}|+c_{2}).

(6.38
)

Proof.
For
n\geq 1
we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle|X_{n+1}(t)-X_{n}(t)|\leq h\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{\beta(t-s)}\left(|b_{1}(h^{-1}Y_{n}(s))-b_{1}(h^{-1}Y_{n-1}(s))|^{\phantom{P^{L}}}\right.\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{------X_{n+1}(t)-X_{n}(t)}\displaystyle\left.+|b_{2}(s,X_{n}(s),Y_{n}(s))-b_{2}(s,X_{n-1}(s),Y_{n-1}(s))|^{\phantom{P^{L}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\right)ds\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{|X_{n+1}(t)-X_{n}(t)|}\displaystyle\leq\epsilon h\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{\beta(t-s)}|Y_{n}(s)-Y_{n-1}(s)|ds\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{|X_{n+1}(t)-X_{n}(t)|+}\displaystyle+ah\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{\beta(t-s)}e^{\frac{qs}{q-1}-\xi_{m}e^{-\beta s}}\left(|X_{n}(s)-X_{n-1}(s)|+|Y_{n}(s)-Y_{n-1}(s)|\right)ds\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{|X_{n+1}(t)-X_{n}(t)|}\displaystyle\leq A_{1}(t)+A_{2}(t).\end{array}

(6.39
)

By induction we assume that

|X_{n}(t)-X_{n-1}(t)|+|Y_{n}(t)-Y_{n-1}(t)|\leq\frac{K}{2^{n-1}}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}(t-t_{0})}(|x_{0}|+c_{2}).

(6.40
)

Then

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle A_{1}(t)\leq\epsilon h\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{\beta(t-s)}\left(|X_{n}(s)-X_{n-1}(s)|+|Y_{n}(s)-Y_{n-1}(s)|\right)ds\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{I(t)}\displaystyle\leq\frac{(q-1)K\epsilon h}{2^{n-2}(q-2)}e^{\beta(t-{t_{0}})}\left(|x_{0}|+c_{2}\right).\end{array}

(6.41
)

We first choose
\epsilon h\leq\frac{q-2}{16(q-1)}
and we obtain

A_{1}(t)\leq\frac{K}{2^{n+2}}e^{\beta(t-{t_{0}})}\left(|x_{0}|+c_{2}\right).

(6.42
)

Concerning the term
A_{2}(t)
we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}A_{2}(t)\displaystyle\leq ahe^{\beta t_{0}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{2s-\xi_{m}e^{-\beta s}}\left(|X_{n}(s)-X_{n-1}(s)|+|Y_{n}(s)-Y_{n-1}(s)|\right)ds\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{B(t)}\displaystyle\leq\frac{Kah(|x_{0}|+c_{2})}{2^{n-1}}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}(t-t_{0})}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}(t-s)+\frac{qs}{q-1}-\xi_{m}e^{-\beta s}}ds.\end{array}

Since for all
t_{0}\leq s\leq t
there holds

e^{\frac{\beta}{2}(t-s)+\frac{qs}{q-1}-\xi_{m}e^{\frac{q-2}{q-1}s}}\leq e^{\frac{\beta}{2}(t-s)+\frac{qs}{q-1}-\xi_{m}e^{\frac{q-2}{q-1}t_{0}}},

we can take
t_{0}
large enough such that

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle A_{2}s(t)\leq\frac{K}{2^{n+2}}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}(t-{t_{0}})}\left(|x_{0}|+c_{2}\right).\end{array}

(6.43
)

Next we estimate
|Y_{n+1}(t)-Y_{n}(t)|
. We have

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle|Y_{n+1}(t)-Y_{n}(t)|\leq h\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{(q-1)\kappa(t-s)}\left(|b_{1}(h^{-1}Y_{n}(s))-b_{1}(h^{-1}Y_{n-1}(s))|^{\phantom{P^{L}}}\right.\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{-------Y_{n+1}(t)-Y_{n}(t)}\displaystyle\left.+|b_{2}(s,X_{n}(s),Y_{n}(s))-b_{2}(s,X_{n-1}(s),Y_{n-1}(s))\right)ds\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{|Y_{n+1}(t)-Y_{n}(t)|}\displaystyle\leq\epsilon h\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{(q-1)\kappa(t-s)}|Y_{n}(s)-Y_{n-1}(s)|ds\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{|_{n}(t)|}\displaystyle+ah\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{(q-1)\kappa(t-s)}e^{\frac{qs}{q-1}-\xi_{m}e^{-\beta s}}\left(|X_{n}(s)-X_{n-1}(s)|+|Y_{n}(s)-Y_{n-1}(s)|\right)ds\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{|Y_{n+1}(t)-Y_{n}(t)|}\displaystyle\leq A_{3}(t)+A_{4}(t).\end{array}

(6.44
)

By the induction assumption
(\ref{W16})
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle A_{3}(t)\leq\frac{K\epsilon h}{2^{n-1}}(|x_{0}|+c_{2})\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{(q-1)\kappa(t-s)}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}(s-{t_{0}})}ds\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{C(t)}\displaystyle\leq\frac{(q-1)K\epsilon h}{2^{n-2}(2\kappa(q-1)^{2}+q-2)}(|x_{0}|+c_{2})e^{\frac{\beta}{2}(t-t_{0})}.\end{array}

We next choose
\epsilon h\leq\min\left\{\frac{q-2}{16(q-1)},\frac{2\kappa(q-1)^{2}+q-2}{16(q-1)}\right\}
and we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle A_{3}(t)\leq\frac{K}{2^{n+2}}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}(t-{t_{0}})}\left(|x_{0}|+c_{2}\right).\end{array}

(6.45
)

For the last term, we write

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle A_{4}(t)\leq\frac{Kah}{2^{n-1}}\left(|x_{0}|+c_{2}\right)\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{(q-1)\kappa(t-s)+\frac{qs}{q-1}-\xi_{m}e^{-\beta s}+\frac{\beta}{2}(s-t_{0})}ds\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{D(t)}\displaystyle\leq\frac{Kh}{2^{n+2}}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}(t-{t_{0}})}\left(|x_{0}|+c_{2}\right)8a\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{((q-1)\kappa-\beta)(t-s)+\frac{qs}{q-1}-\xi_{m}e^{-\beta s}}ds\end{array}

(6.46
)

Since

\lim_{s\to\infty}\left(\frac{qs}{q-1}-\xi_{m}e^{-\beta s}\right)=-\infty

it follows that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{((q-1)\kappa-\beta)(t-s)+\frac{qs}{q-1}-\xi_{m}e^{-\beta s}}ds=0

Hence there exists
t_{0}
such that for all
t\geq t_{0}
,

\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{((q-1)\kappa-\beta)(t-s)+\frac{qs}{q-1}-\xi_{m}e^{-\beta s}}ds\leq\frac{1}{8a}.

This implies

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle D(t)\leq\frac{K}{2^{n+2}}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}(t-{t_{0}})}\left(|x_{0}|+c_{2}\right).\end{array}

(6.47
)

Combining
(\ref{W18})
,
(\ref{W19})
,
(\ref{W21})
and
(\ref{W23})
we obtain

(\ref{W14})
.
\square

Next we define the weighted space
C_{w}([t_{0},\infty))
with
w(t)=e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(t-t_{0})}
by

\displaystyle C_{w}(t_{0},\infty)=\left\{(\phi,\psi)\in C([t_{0},\infty))\times C([t_{0},\infty)):\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}w(t)(|\phi(t)|+|\psi(t)|)<\infty\right\},

and we denote by
\left\|(\phi,\psi)\right\|_{C_{w}(t_{0},\infty)}
this last quantity.

Proposition 6.4

The sequence
\{(X_{n},Y_{n})\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C_{w}([t_{0},\infty))
. Its limit
{\mathcal{U}}(t)=(X(t),Y(t))
satisfies
(\ref{W9})
. Hence

x=X+Y
and
y=\frac{Y}{h}

satisfy
(\ref{W2})
and
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}(x(t),y(t))=(0,0)
. Thus
(\xi(t),\eta(t))
is a solution of system
(\ref{T3})
on
(t_{0},\infty)
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}(\xi(t),\eta(t))=(\xi_{m},\eta_{m})
.

Proof.
The space
C_{w}([t_{0},\infty))
is complete and we have

\left\|(X_{n+p}-X_{n},Y_{n+p}-Y_{n})\right\|_{C_{w}(t_{0},\infty)}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\left(|x_{0}|+c_{2}\right).

Hence there exists
(X(t),Y(t))=\lim_{t\to\infty}(X_{n}(t),Y_{n}(t))
in this space
C_{w}([t_{0},\infty))
. By
(\ref{W6})
and Lemma
6.2
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}F(t,{\mathcal{U}}_{n}(t))=F(t,{\mathcal{U}}(t))
in
C_{w}([t_{0},\infty))
. Therefore
{\mathcal{U}}(t)=(X(t),Y(t))
satisfies the integral equation
(\ref{W9})
which is equivalent to the system
(\ref{W3-1})
. By the transformation
(\ref{W3-0})
,
(x,y)
satisfies

(\ref{W2})
and
(x(t),y(t))\to(0,0)
when
t\to\infty
.
\square

Returning to the variable
u
by the transformation defining
\xi
and
\eta
, the proof of Assertion 2 in Theorem
1.13
follows with
r_{0}=e^{t_{0}}
.
\square
