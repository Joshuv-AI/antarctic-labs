FINAL TECHNICAL REPORT - DE-SC0021232

Measuring Vibronic Coupling and Ultrafast Charge Delocalization on
Nanocrystal Surfaces Using Ligand-Specific Vibrational Probes
Lead PI: Prof. Munira Khalil, University of Washington, Seattle
Co-PI: Prof. Brandi Cossairt, University of Washington, Seattle

The primary goal of this proposal was to measure and quantify how vibronic ligand-exciton
interactions at the inorganic-organic interface of semiconductor nanocrystals (NCs) govern
femtosecond non-radiative relaxation and charge delocalization mechanisms as a function of
nanocrystal and ligand structures – including NC size, shape, and ligand identity. Molecular-scale
chemical and physical properties and interactions of surface ligands dictate nanocrystal exciton
dynamics on the femto-to-microsecond timescales. Improvements in synthesis, experimental, and
theoretical characterizations have enhanced our understanding of NC photophysics. However,
quantitative descriptions of complex ligand-exciton interactions at the NC surface have remained
elusive, which limits elucidation of relaxation mechanisms that determine overall carrier transfer
dynamics at the exciton-ligand interface. Thus, the central motivation for this proposal was to fill
a significant knowledge gap for judicious selection of ligand-functionalized nanocrystals for
targeted isolation and capture of charge carriers to drive selective organic photocatalytic
transformations in NC photocatalysts and NC-assembled photonic materials. Our unique two-
pronged experimental approach combining nanocrystal synthesis and ligand-specific nonlinear
spectroscopy has resulted in (i) four peer-reviewed publications; (ii) 12+ invited and contributed
posters and talks at national and international scientific meetings and seminars, (iii) two completed
PhD theses, (iv) training of two postdoctoral researchers, three PhD students, and two
undergraduate students, and (v) two manuscripts under preparation. The scientific highlights of
the DOE supported work are outlined below:

Measuring relative energies of ligand binding conformations on nanocluster surfaces with
temperature-dependent FTIR spectroscopy.1 Surface-passivating organic ligands play key roles
in determining the structural and opto-electronic properties of nanocrystals. Common ligands
include long-chain aliphatic hydrocarbons with carboxylate functional groups, such as myristic
acid. Surface carboxylates anchor to nanocrystal surfaces in various binding configurations to
stabilize charges of undercoordinated surface metal ions, providing colloidal stability and a tunable
structural motif (Fig. 1a). The primary objective of our study was to measure the relative energies
between the binding conformations of carboxylate ligands on myristic acid-capped
In37P20(O2C13H27)57 magic-sized clusters (MSCs). We measured temperature-dependent (TD)
FTIR spectra (Fig. 1b) and performed spectral analysis using a custom sampling algorithm
designed in-house to employ a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) global fitting algorithm,
examining the vibrational energetics of bound carboxylate ligands on MSC surfaces. The TD-
FTIR experiments and MCMC algorithm were integral in characterizing the complex ligand
structures and their relative binding populations on the surface, contributing to growing efforts to
characterize the vibrational landscape of surface ligands in NCs. The key results of this study were
that the chelating binding mode is 0.7 ± 0.3 kcal/mol more stable than the monodentate motif, and
the syn–syn bridging conformation is 1.1 ± 0.5 kcal/mol more stable. However, the syn–anti

Motivated by our previous study (see above and Figs 2a-c) to better understand the mechanism
of QD degradation during photocatalysis, we demonstrated that during a reductive dehalogenation
reaction, CdS QDs undergo surface ligand etching, which leads to a loss of colloidal stability and
the formation of microcrystalline cadmium metal deposits (Fig. 2d). We hypothesize that this
results from the accumulation of electrons on the QD surface. Additionally, we demonstrated mild
surface sulfur oxidation and the formation of an ammonium salt byproduct from the hole quencher.
This work contributes to our atomic-level understanding of the reactions occurring at the QD
surface during photocatalysis, enabling the design of more stable and efficient photocatalysts. This
work also highlights the importance of understanding the relationship between charge carrier
dynamics and surface chemistry in producing stable and effective QD photocatalysts.

Surface carboxylate sensitivity to electron and hole relaxation in photoexcited CdS
nanocrystals.4 The development of QDs for selective photo-organic transformation requires
adequate descriptions of ligand-exciton coupling, which impacts charge separation and electronic-
to-vibrational energy transfer (EVET) processes, but remains an elusive and difficult challenge.
To this end, we investigated ligand-exciton vibronic coupling in oleate-capped CdS QDs using
femtosecond tIR spectroscopy. Our primary goal was to determine how native bound carboxylate
ligands couple to the 1S exciton and report on charge delocalization and nonradiative relaxation
dynamics. We observed instantaneous vibronic coupling for all symmetric and asymmetric νCOO
modes, including competing contributions from νCH2 bending vibrations in the (1400–1500 cm-1)
region. Vibrational energy transfer amongst the coupled vibrations occurred within 100 fs. Fig. 3a
shows the normalized FTIR spectrum of oleic acid and CdS QDs, as well as the picosecond decay-
associated spectrum (DAS). The data in Fig. 3a indicate the νCOO and νCH vibrations are the only
modes sensitive to picosecond dynamics in CdS, suggesting the symmetry of ligand vibrations
impacts vibronic coupling, as the asymmetric νCOO stretch showed no analogous decay. Fluence-
dependent tIR experiments revealed that due to preferential anharmonic coupling between νCH
bending and stretching, and the symmetric νCOO modes, the latter are uniquely sensitive to
