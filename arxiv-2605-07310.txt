Lifespan estimate for one dimensional wave equation with semilinear terms of spatial derivative
Ning-An Lai, Cui Ren, Takiko Sasaki, Hiroyuki Takamura

Abstract
This paper studies the upper and lower bounds of the lifespan for the classical solutions to the initial value problems of one dimensional wave equations with non-autonomous semilinear terms including the space-derivative of the unknown function. This is a non-trivial business comparing to the analogous results with time-derivative type semilinear terms, especially for the proof to obtain the sharp upper bound of the lifespan as we have to deal with space dependent weights among iteration procedures of the weighted functional of the solution. Also it is surprising that a part of them reaches to the same ordinary differential inequality for classical semilinear damped wave equations introduced by Li and Zhou (Discrete Contin. Dynam. Systems, 1995, 1(4): 503-520), and we show a simple proof for blow up result from this ordinary differential inequality by iteration argument and slicing method in more general situation.



Keywords
: semilinear wave equation, one dimension, classical solution,
lifespan, space dependent weight.

Mathematics Subject Classification 2020:
primary 35L71, secondary 35B44.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with lifespan estimates of classical solutions
to the Cauchy problem of wave equations,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{tt}-u_{xx}=\frac{|u_{x}|^{p}}{\langle x\rangle^{a}}\quad\text{in ${\bf R}\times(0,T)$},\\
&u(x,0)={\varepsilon}f(x),\quad u_{t}(x,0)={\varepsilon}g(x),\quad x\in{\bf R},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
p>1,a\in{\bf R}
and
{\varepsilon}>0
is a “small” parameter.
The initial data
f
and
g
are smooth functions of compact support.
We are interested in the estimate of the lifespan
T({\varepsilon})
, i.e., the maximal existence time of the solution
defined by

T({\varepsilon}):=\sup\left\{T\in(0,\infty]\ :\ \mbox{the classical solution of (\ref{IVP10}) exists in the time interval $[0,T)$}\right\}.

For autonomous case (
a=0
), it has been well-studied by
Sasaki, Takamatsu and Takamura
[
13
]
and Haruyama, Sasaki and Takamura
[
2
]

that the lifespan estimates satisfy

T({\varepsilon})\sim C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)},

(1.2)

where
T({\varepsilon})\sim A({\varepsilon},C)
stands for the fact that
there exist two positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
independent of
{\varepsilon}

such that

A({\varepsilon},C_{1})\leq T({\varepsilon})\leq A({\varepsilon},C_{2}).

We note that the left inequality means that the solution exists at least to the time
A({\varepsilon},C_{1})
for any initial data,
namely it means long time existence of the solution,
while the right inequality means that there exist some special data for which the solution cannot exist
after the time
A({\varepsilon},C_{2})
,
namely it implies the finite time blow-up of the solution.

The early work on the semilinear wave equation with nonlinear term
|u_{t}|^{p}
, especially the upper bound of the lifespan,
is due to Zhou
[
17
]
,
which also has the same upper bound of (
1.2
).
This result is quite meaningful as it guarantees the sharpness of the general theory for nonlinear wave equations,
we refer to Takamura
[
16
]
for details and all the references on this direction.
The lower bound of lifespan was studied by Kitamura, Morisawa and Takamura
[
6
]

including non-autonomous nonlinear terms with space-variable dependent weight,
namely the problem for which
|u_{x}|^{p}
is replaced with
|u_{t}|^{p}
in (
1.1
) is studied.
The main difficulty to handle the nonlinear term of
|u_{x}|^{p}
appears in the blow-up part
as we have to employ the special weighted functional which was introduced
by Rammaha
[
11
,
12
]
for higher dimensional case like
[
13
]
,
while we have a point-wise positivity of the solution for the nonlinear term
|u_{t}|^{p}
.
See
[
2
]
for details.

The main purpose of this paper is to show that the lifespan
T({\varepsilon})
of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) satisfies

\begin{array}[]{l}T({\varepsilon})\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)/(1-a)}&\mbox{if}\ a<1,\\
\exp\left(C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)}\right)&\mbox{if}\ a=1,\end{array}\right.\\
T({\varepsilon})=\infty\quad\mbox{if}\ a>1.\end{array}

(1.3)

This estimate is the same as the one in
[
6
]
.
We note that the proof of the upper bound of the lifespan, blow-up,
in some case (Theorem
2.2
below)
is reduced to a blow-up result on second order ordinary differential inequality (
4.33
) below.
The remarkable fact is that it is exactly same as (3.18) in Theorem 3.1 by Li and Zhou
[
10
]

which establishes the sharp upper bound of the lifespan of solutions of the Cauchy problem for
classical semilinear damped wave equation in general space dimensions,

u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=|u|^{p}\quad\mbox{in}\ {\bf R}^{n}\times(0,T).

Their proof is organized the comparison and rescaling arguments,
and is available for
0\leq a\leq 1
in (
4.33
) below.
But our proof employs only simple iteration argument and slicing method,
and succeeds to extend the result for any
a\leq 1
.
See Lemma
4.1
below as well as Remark
4.1
.
We note that the iteration argument is originally introduced to nonlinear hyperbolic equations
by John
[
3
]
to show the sub-critical blow-up for semilinear wave equations in three space dimensions.
And the slicing method is firstly introduced by Agemi, Kurokawa and Takamura
[
1
]

to show the critical blow-up for systems semilinear wave equations in three space dimensions.
It is still useful to shorten the proof of the blow-up result especially in the critical case.
See Shao, Sasaki and Takamura
[
14
]
for example.

This paper is organized as follows.
(
1.3
) is divided into three theorems according to three cases in Section 2.
The blow-up part of (
1.3
) is proved in Section 3 and 4 with different conditions
on the data.
The existence part of (
1.3
) is shortly proved in Section 5.

Main results

satisfy

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\,f,\mathrm{supp}\,g\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|x|\leq R\},\quad R\geq 1.

Let

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume (
2.1
) and,

f(x),g(x)\geq 0\ \text{and}\ f(x)\not\equiv 0,

(2.3)

or

f(x),g(x)\geq 0\ \mbox{and}\ g(x)\not\equiv 0.

(2.4)

Then there exists a positive constant

such that the classical solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) cannot exist as far as

satisfies

T>\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)/(1-a)}&\mbox{if}\ a<1,\\
\exp\left(C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)}\right)&\mbox{if}\ a=1\end{array}\right.

(2.5)

for all
{\varepsilon}\in(0,{\varepsilon}_{1}]
, where
C
is a positive constant independent of
\varepsilon
.

Remark 2.1
.

The conclusion of the above theorem implies that

T({\varepsilon})\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)/(1-a)}&\mbox{if}\ a<1,\\
\exp\left(C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)}\right)&\mbox{if}\ a=1.\end{array}\right.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume (
2.1
) and

\displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}}(-e^{x}+e^{-x})f^{\prime}(x)dx\geq 0,\ \int_{{\bf R}}(-e^{x}+e^{-x})\{g^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(x)\}dx\geq 0(\not\equiv 0).

(2.6)

Then, the same conclusion as in Theorem
2.1
holds.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that integration by parts yields

\int_{{\bf R}}(-e^{x}+e^{-x})f^{\prime}(x)dx=\int_{{\bf R}}(e^{x}+e^{-x})f(x)dx,

so that the condition (
2.6
) can be written by
f
and
g
instead of
f^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume (
2.1
).
Then there exists a positive constant
{\varepsilon}_{2}={\varepsilon}_{2}(f,g,p,a,R)
such that
the classical solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) exists as far as
T
satisfies

T<\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)/(1-a)}&\mbox{if}\ a<1,\\
\exp\left(C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)}\right)&\mbox{if}\ a=1,\\
\infty&\mbox{if}\ a>1\end{array}\right.

for all
{\varepsilon}\in(0,{\varepsilon}_{2}]
,
where
C
is a positive constant independent of
\varepsilon
.

Remark 2.3
.

The conclusion of the above theorem implies that

\begin{array}[]{l}T({\varepsilon})\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)/(1-a)}&\mbox{if}\ a<1,\\
\exp\left(C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)}\right)&\mbox{if}\ a=1,\end{array}\right.\\
T({\varepsilon})=\infty\quad\mbox{if}\ a>1.\end{array}

All the theorems above are proved with the concrete expression of a solution
u
of (
1.1
),

u(x,t)={\varepsilon}u^{0}(x,t)+L_{a}(|u_{x}|^{p})(x,t),

(2.7)

where
u^{0}
is a solution of the free wave equation with the same initial data;

u^{0}(x,t):=\frac{1}{2}\{f(x+t)+f(x-t)\}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{x-t}^{x+t}g(y)dy,

(2.8)

and a linear integral operator
L_{a}
for a function
v=v(x,t)
is Duhamel’s term defined by

L_{a}(v)(x,t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{x-t+s}^{x+t-s}\frac{v(y,s)}{(1+y^{2})^{a/2}}\,dy.

(2.9)

The subcritical case

Before proceeding with the proof for the subcritical case, we first establish a key lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

and
p>1
.
Assume that
a<1
, and the function
H=H(t)\in C([E,T))
satisfies

\displaystyle H(t)\geq D_{1}t^{2-a/p},

(3.1)

\displaystyle H(t)\geq D_{2}\int_{E}^{t}ds\int_{E}^{s}r^{1-2p-a/p}|H(r)|^{p}dr

for

with some positive constants

,

and

Then, there exists a positive constant

such that

cannot exist if

T>(DD_{1}^{-1})^{\frac{p-1}{1-a}}.

(3.3)

where

D=\frac{p^{\frac{2p}{(p-1)^{2}}}2^{\frac{2p+1-a}{p-1}}(2p+1-a)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}{\left(D_{2}(p-1)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}},

which satisfies

Proof.

From (
3.1

Under the assumption (
3.2
), we may claim that

\displaystyle H(t)\geq C_{n}(t-E)^{a_{n}}t^{-b_{n}-\frac{1}{p}},

\left\{\begin{aligned} &a_{n+1}=pa_{n}+2+\frac{1-a}{p},\ \ a_{1}=(1-a)/p+2,\\
&b_{n+1}=pb_{n}+2p,\ \ b_{1}=0,\\
&C_{n+1}=\frac{D_{2}C_{n}^{p}}{(a_{n+1})^{2}},\ \ C_{1}=D_{1},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.5)

where

In the following we are going to prove the above claim. It holds by (

\displaystyle H(t)\geq D_{2}t^{-2p-1/p}\int_{E}^{t}ds\int_{E}^{s}r(r-E)^{(1-a)/p}|H(r)|^{p}dr.

Plugging (

) into (

) yields

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle\geq D_{2}C_{n}^{p}t^{-2p-1/p}\int_{E}^{t}ds\int_{E}^{s}r(r-E)^{(1-a)/p}(r-E)^{a_{n}p}r^{-b_{n}p-1}dr

\displaystyle\geq D_{2}C_{n}^{p}t^{-2p-1/p-b_{n}p}\int_{E}^{t}ds\int_{E}^{s}(r-E)^{(1-a)/p+a_{n}p}dr

\displaystyle\geq\frac{D_{2}C_{n}^{p}}{(a_{n}p+2+(1-a)/p)^{2}}t^{-2p-1/p-b_{n}p}(t-E)^{(1-a)/p+a_{n}p+2},

which implies the claim (
3.4
) with (

).

Solving the recurrence relations (
3.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle a_{n}=-\frac{2p+1-a}{1-p}p^{n-1}+\frac{2p+1-a}{p(1-p)},

(3.7)

\displaystyle b_{n}=-\frac{2p}{1-p}p^{n-1}+\frac{2p}{1-p}.

(3.8)

Obviously we have by (
3.7
)

\displaystyle a_{n+1}\leq\frac{2p+1-a}{p-1}p^{n},

which yields by
\eqref{seq}_{3}

\displaystyle C_{n+1}

\displaystyle\geq p^{-2n}\frac{D_{2}C_{n}^{p}(p-1)^{2}}{(2p+1-a)^{2}}=D_{3}\frac{C_{n}^{p}}{p^{2n}}

(3.9)

with

It follows by (

)

\displaystyle\log C_{n+1}

\displaystyle\geq p\log C_{n}-(2n)\log p+\log D_{3},

which further leads to

\displaystyle\log C_{n+1}-(n+1)\frac{2\log p}{p-1}-\left(\frac{2\log p}{(p-1)^{2}}-\frac{\log D_{3}}{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq p\left(\log C_{n}-\frac{2\log p}{p-1}n-\left(\frac{2\log p}{(p-1)^{2}}-\frac{\log D_{3}}{p-1}\right)\right).

Iterating the above inequality to
n=1
, we come to

\displaystyle\log C_{n}\geq p^{n-1}\log\left(\frac{D_{1}D_{3}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{p^{\frac{2p}{(p-1)^{2}}}}\right)+n\frac{2\log p}{p-1}+\log\left(\frac{p^{\frac{2}{(p-1)^{2}}}}{D_{3}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\right).

(3.10)

Substituting (
3.7
), (
4.11
) and (
3.10
) into (
3.4
) yields

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle\geq C_{n}(t-E)^{a_{n}}t^{-b_{n}-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{D_{1}D_{3}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{p^{\frac{2p}{(p-1)^{2}}}}\right)^{p^{n-1}}p^{\frac{2n(p-1)+2}{(p-1)^{2}}}D_{3}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}(t-E)^{a_{n}}t^{-b_{n}-\frac{1}{p}}.

(3.11)

Hence for
t\geq 2E
, it follows that

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{D_{1}D_{3}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{p^{\frac{2p}{(p-1)^{2}}}}\right)^{p^{n-1}}p^{\frac{2n(p-1)+2}{(p-1)^{2}}}D_{3}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{a_{n}}t^{-b_{n}-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{D_{1}D_{3}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}t^{\frac{1-a}{p-1}}}{p^{\frac{2p}{(p-1)^{2}}}2^{\frac{2p+1-a}{p-1}}}\right)^{p^{n-1}}p^{\frac{2n(p-1)+2}{(p-1)^{2}}}D_{3}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}2^{-\frac{2p+1-a}{p(1-p)}}t^{\frac{2p^{2}-3p+a}{p(p-1)}}.

(3.12)

Hence if

\displaystyle\frac{D_{1}D_{3}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}t^{\frac{1-a}{p-1}}}{p^{\frac{2p}{(p-1)^{2}}}2^{\frac{2p+1-a}{p-1}}}>1,

(3.13)

then we find that

D=\frac{p^{\frac{2p}{(p-1)^{2}}}2^{\frac{2p+1-a}{p-1}}(2p+1-a)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}{(D_{2}(p-1)^{2})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}.

This completes the proof of Lemma

\square

\displaystyle K_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{2}K_{n}^{p}}{(2p)^{n+2}m_{n+1}}\left(\frac{l_{n}}{l_{n+1}}\right)^{m_{n+1}-1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{M_{2}K_{n}^{p}}{(2p)^{n+2}(p^{n}\frac{1-a}{p-1})}\left(\frac{l_{n}}{l_{n+1}}\right)^{p^{n}\frac{1-a}{p-1}-1}.

(4.8)

The last factor in (
4
) should be estimated delicately. Setting

\displaystyle b_{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{l_{n}}{l_{n+1}}\right)^{p^{n}}=\left(1+\frac{1}{(2p)^{n+1}}\right)^{-p^{n}},

then

\log b_{n}=-\frac{\log\left(1+\frac{1}{(2p)^{n+1}}\right)}{\frac{1}{p^{n}}}.

Noting that

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{d}{dn}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{(2p)^{n+1}}\right)}{\frac{d}{dn}\frac{1}{p^{j}}}=\frac{-\frac{(2p)^{n}\log(2p)}{(2p)((2p)^{n})^{2}}}{\left(1+\frac{1}{(2p)^{n+1}}\right)\left(-\frac{p^{n}\log p}{(p^{n})^{2}}\right)},

then by l’Hopital’s rule we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\log b_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{p}{2p}\right)^{n}=0,

which means

\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{n}=1,

and hence there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle b_{n}=\left(\frac{l_{n}}{l_{n+1}}\right)^{p^{n}}\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{p^{n_{0}}},~~~for~n>n_{0}.

(4.9)

Also noting that

\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{l_{n}}{l_{n+1}}<1,

then it holds that

\displaystyle b_{n}=\left(\frac{l_{n}}{l_{n+1}}\right)^{p^{n}}\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{p^{n_{0}}},~~~for~n=1,2,\cdots,n_{0}.

(4.10)

We then conclude from (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) that for all

\displaystyle b_{n}=\left(\frac{l_{n}}{l_{n+1}}\right)^{p^{n}}\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{p^{n_{0}}}.

Combining (
4

\displaystyle K_{n+1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{M_{2}K_{n}^{p}}{(2p)^{n+2}(p^{n}\frac{1-a}{p-1})}\left(\frac{l_{n}}{l_{n+1}}\right)^{p^{n}\frac{1-a}{p-1}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{2}K_{n}^{p}}{(2p)^{n+2}(p^{n}\frac{1-a}{p-1})}(b_{n})^{\frac{1-a}{p-1}}\Bigg/\frac{l_{n}}{l_{n+1}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{M_{2}K_{n}^{p}}{2^{\frac{p^{n_{0}}(1-a)}{p-1}}(2p)^{n+2}(p^{n}\frac{1-a}{p-1})}

in this case,
then we will have

C_{3}^{\frac{p-1}{1-a}}{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)/(1-a)}>2l_{\infty}R_{2}\ \mbox{and}\ C_{4}^{p-1}\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}>(k_{\infty}R_{2})^{2}.

This completes the proof for the Theorem
2.2
.

\square

5
Proof of Theorem
2.3

Following Haruyama, Sasaki and Takamura
[
2
]

which corrects a small error in the definition of the function space in the existence part of
Sasaki, Takamatsu and Takamura
[
13
]
for a case of
a=0

(see Remark 3.2 in
[
2
]
),
the desired solution in Theorem
2.3
is constructed in a
L^{\infty}
space.
Its proof will be similar to the one for

u_{tt}-u_{xx}=\frac{|u_{t}|^{p}}{\langle x\rangle^{a}}

by Kitamura, Morisawa and Takamura
[
6
]
,
which has an oversight on the Hölder countinuity of the nonlinear term although.
The existence of a classical solution in
[
6
]
is available for all
p>1

with minor modifications in its proof.

For our case, let us recall the expression of a solution
u
of (
1.1
) in (
2.7
).
Then one can apply the time-derivative to (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) to obtain

u_{t}(x,t)={\varepsilon}u_{t}^{0}(x,t)+L_{a}^{\prime}(|u_{x}|^{p})(x,t)

(5.1)

and

u_{t}^{0}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime}(x+t)-f^{\prime}(x-t)+g(x+t)+g(x-t)\},

where
L_{a}^{\prime}
for a function
v=v(x,t)
is defined by

\begin{array}[]{ll}L_{a}^{\prime}(v)(x,t):=&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{v(x+t-s,s)}{\{1+(x+t-s)^{2}\}^{a/2}}ds\\
&+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{v(x-t+s,s)}{\{1+(x-t+s)^{2}\}^{a/2}}ds.\end{array}

(5.2)

Therefore,
u_{t}
is expressed by
u_{x}
.
On the other hand, applying the space-derivative to (
2.7
) and (
2.8
),
we have a
u_{x}
-closed integral equation,

u_{x}(x,t)={\varepsilon}u_{x}^{0}(x,t)+\overline{L_{a}^{\prime}}(|u_{x}|^{p})(x,t)

(5.3)

and

u_{x}^{0}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime}(x+t)+f^{\prime}(x-t)+g(x+t)-g(x-t)\},

where
\overline{L_{a}^{\prime}}
for a function
v=v(x,t)
is defined by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\overline{L_{a}^{\prime}}(v)(x,t):=&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{v(x+t-s,s)}{\{1+(x+t-s)^{2}\}^{a/2}}ds\\
&-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{v(x-t+s,s)}{\{1+(x-t+s)^{2}\}^{a/2}}ds.\end{array}

(5.4)

Moreover, one more space-derivative to (
5.1
) yields that

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{tx}(x,t)=&\displaystyle{\varepsilon}u_{tx}^{0}(x,t)\\
&\displaystyle+pL_{a}^{\prime}(|u_{x}|^{p-2}u_{x}u_{xx})(x,t)-aL_{a+2}^{\prime}(|u_{x}|^{p}x)(x,t)\end{array}

and

u_{tx}^{0}(x,t):=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime\prime}(x+t)-f^{\prime\prime}(x-t)+g^{\prime}(x+t)+g^{\prime}(x-t)\}.

Similarly, we have that

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{tt}(x,t)=&\displaystyle{\varepsilon}u_{tt}^{0}(x,t)+\frac{|u_{x}(x,t)|^{p}}{(1+x^{2})^{a/2}}\\
&\displaystyle+p\overline{L_{a}^{\prime}}(|u_{x}|^{p-2}u_{x}u_{xx})(x,t)-a\overline{L_{a+2}^{\prime}}(|u_{x}|^{p}x)(x,t)\end{array}

and

u_{tt}^{0}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime\prime}(x+t)+f^{\prime\prime}(x-t)+g^{\prime}(x+t)-g^{\prime}(x-t)\}.

Therefore,
u_{tx}
is expressed by
u_{xx}
and
u_{x}
, so is
u_{tt}
.
Also we have

u_{xx}(x,t)={\varepsilon}u_{xx}^{0}(x,t)+p\overline{L_{a}^{\prime}}(|u_{x}|^{p-2}u_{x}u_{xx})(x,t)-a\overline{L_{a+2}^{\prime}}(|u_{x}|^{p}x)(x,t)

(5.5)

and

u_{xx}^{0}(x,t)=u^{0}_{tt}(x,t).

First, we note the following fact.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
(f,g)\in C_{0}^{2}({\bf R})\times C_{0}^{1}({\bf R})
.
Let
w
be a
C^{1}
solution of (
5.3
) in which
u_{x}
is replaced with
w
.
Then,

u(x,t):=\int^{x}_{-\infty}w(y,t)dy

is a classical solution of (
1.1
) in
{\bf R}\times[0,T]
.

Proof.
This is easy along with the computations above in this section.

\Box

According to Proposition
5.1
, we shall construct a
C^{1}
solution
of (
5.3
) in which
u_{x}
is replaced with the unknown function
w
.
Let
\{w_{j}\}_{j\in{\bf N}}
be a sequence of
C^{1}({\bf R}\times[0,T])
defined by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}w_{j+1}={\varepsilon}u_{x}^{0}+\overline{L_{a}^{\prime}}(|w_{j}|^{p}),\\
w_{1}={\varepsilon}u_{x}^{0}.\end{array}\right.

(5.6)

Then, in view of (
5.5
),
(w_{j})_{x}
has to satisfy

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(w_{j+1})_{x}={\varepsilon}u_{xx}^{0}+p\overline{L_{a}^{\prime}}(|w_{j}|^{p-2}w_{j}(w_{j})_{x})(x,t)-a\overline{L_{a+2}^{\prime}}(|w_{j}|^{p}x)(x,t),\\
(w_{1})_{x}={\varepsilon}u_{xx}^{0},\end{array}\right.

(5.7)

so that the functional space in which
\{w_{j}\}
converges is

X:=\{w\in C^{1}({\bf R}\times[0,T]):\|w\|_{X}<\infty,\mbox{ supp }w\subset\{(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[0,T]:|x|\leq t+R\}\}

which is equipped with a norm

\|w\|_{X}:=\|w\|+\|w_{x}\|

where

\|w\|:=\sup_{(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[0,T]}|w(x,t)|.

We note that (
5.3
) implies that

\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{supp}\ w_{j}\subset\{(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[0,T]:|x|\leq t+R\}\\
\Longrightarrow\mbox{supp}\ w_{j+1}\subset\{(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[0,T]:|x|\leq t+R\}.\end{array}

The following lemma provides us a priori estimate.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
w\in C({\bf R}\times[0,T])
and supp
w\subset\{(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[0,T]:|x|\leq t+R\}
.
Then, the following a priori estimate holds;

\|\overline{L^{\prime}_{a}}(|w|^{p})\|\leq C\|w\|^{p}E(T),

(5.8)

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
T
and
{\varepsilon}
.

E(T)
is defined by

E(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}(T+2R)^{1-a}&\mbox{if}\ a<1,\\
\log(T+2R)&\mbox{if}\ a=1,\\
1&\mbox{if}\ a>1.\end{array}\right.

(5.9)

Proof.
The proof of Proposition
5.2
is almost the same as
the one of Proposition 3.1 in Morisawa, Kitamura and Takamura
[
6
]

because the difference between
L^{\prime}_{a}
and
\overline{L^{\prime}_{a}}
is a sign of the second terms
in (
5.2
) and (
5.4
),
so that we have

\left|\overline{L^{\prime}_{a}}(v)(x,t)\right|\leq L^{\prime}_{a}(|v|)(x,t)\quad\mbox{in}\ {\bf R}\times[0,T].

Therefore the remained part is completely same as the one
of Proposition 3.1 in
[
6
]
.

\Box
