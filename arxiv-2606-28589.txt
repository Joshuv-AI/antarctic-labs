Search for Truth from Reasoning: A Dynamic Representation Editing Framework for Steering LLM Trajectories
Tianlong Wang, Yuhang Wang, Weibin Liao, Xin Gao, Xinyu Ma, Yang Lin, Yasha Wang, Liantao Ma

Abstract
Current approaches to enhance Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning, such as Chain-of-Thought and “Wait” prompts, primarily encourage models to think more , yet often fail to guide them toward Truth . While Representation Editing (RepE) offers a intrinsic control, its application to dynamic reasoning trajectories remains underexplored. In this work, we bridge this gap by investigating the geometry of truth within unfolding reasoning chains. We uncover three critical insights: (1) Truth is encoded at the sentence level and is entangled with latent reasoning patterns; (2) Effective intervention follows an Uncertainty Principle and a Decay Effect , requiring localization to early, high-entropy forks; (3) Naive steering vectors suffer from noise, risking collateral damage to correct trajectories. Based on these findings, we propose DynaSteer , a dynamic RepE framework. DynaSteer employs pattern clustering to disentangle reasoning manifolds and utilizes Fisher-LDA to project purified truth. By dynamically monitoring lookahead entropy, it selectively steers and rolls back trajectories only when necessary. Comprehensive experimental results on several MATH benchmark verify the effectiveness of DynaSteer , and experiments on out-of-domain coding tasks further confirm its generalization ability. Our code is publicly available at https://github.com/tianlwang/DynaSteer .

Introduction

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable proficiency in complex reasoning tasks
(
14
;
28
;
36
)
, largely driven by the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paradigm
(
38
;
42
;
7

9
)
which elicits structured reasoning trajectories. Recent advancements
(
41
;
47
;
4
;
27
)
have further attempted to enhance this capability through inspired prompting strategies, such as the “Aha moment” or “Wait” prompts. While these methods successfully encourage LLMs to generate more tokens and engage in deeper contemplation, they primarily induce the model to
think more
without explicitly
guiding it to steer
toward correct deductions among bifurcating possibilities. Consequently, when LLMs face uncertainty, simply extending the generation length often leads to “hallucination snowballs”
(
46
)
rather than self-correction.

To address this limitation,
Representation Editing (RepE)

(
16
;
37
;
23
)
has emerged as a promising avenue for intrinsic control. Unlike prompting, RepE directly pinpoints and modulates the internal activation patterns of LLMs, offering a more fundamental remedy by guiding the hidden states toward a target concept. However, a critical gap remains in the literature. While RepE has achieved notable success in aligning
static
behavioral attributes, such as language style
(
23
;
32
)
, honesty
(
40
)
, and harmlessness
(
8
)
, its application to the
dynamic reasoning process
remains largely underexplored. Prior works
(
16
;
37
)
predominantly focus on non-reasoning modes or contexts where the conclusion is explicitly visible. The challenge of steering the model towards
Truth
during the intermediate derivation stage,
before
a final answer is reached, presents unique spatiotemporal complexities.

“
Truth
, a binary, geometrically encoded directional signal within the LLM’s activation space, whose sign discriminates whether an ongoing reasoning trajectory ultimately arrives at a verifiable correct or incorrect answer.”

In this work, we conduct a
systematic exploration
of applying RepE to guide LLM reasoning. Our investigation is structured around three pivotal research questions,
Existence
,
Locus
, and
Mitigation
, that bridge the gap between static attribute editing and dynamic reasoning guidance:

1.

Existence of Truth
: Does the
Truth
exist within LLM representations along reasoning trajectories?
A fundamental prerequisite for RepE is that internal representations must distinguish between
Truth
and
Fallacy
. Our probing experiments reveal a crucial nuance: naive token-level probes struggle to discern
Truth
, suggesting it is not encoded in atomic units. However, when elevated to the sentence level, the latent distinction becomes evident. We further discover that the
Truth Steering
is inherently
entangled
with specific reasoning patterns. By disentangling these patterns via clustering, we observe a significant enhancement in discrimination accuracy.

2.

Locus of Intervention
: Where and when is intervention effective?
We identify two critical spatiotemporal dynamics that govern effective steering. First, the
Decay Effect
: intervention is most potent in the early stages of reasoning; as the chain lengthens, the model becomes “entrenched” in its trajectory, rendering late-stage editing ineffective. Second, the
Uncertainty Principle
: effective intervention strictly correlates with high entropy. Editing is necessary only at “reasoning forks” where the model exhibits uncertainty; intervening at low-entropy positions yields negligible benefits or disrupts coherence.

3.

Mitigation of Risk
: How to mitigate the potential risks associated with intervention?
Standard RepE methods typically derive steering vectors using a naive Mean-Difference approach. However, given the entanglement of
Truth
with other high-level semantic concepts, this approach inevitably incorporates noise, posing a risk of corrupting originally correct trajectories. To mitigate this, we propose the use of
Fisher Linear Discriminant Analysis (Fisher-LDA)
. By projecting representations onto a purified subspace, we maximize the separation of
Truth
while minimizing interference with the model’s semantic manifold.

Based on these insights, we propose
DynaSteer
.
DynaSteer
is a dynamic inference-time RepE framework designed to steer reasoning trajectories proactively. It operates by (1) detecting critical reasoning forks via sentence-level lookahead entropy; (2) calculating purified
Truth Steering
using Fisher-LDA within disentangled reasoning clusters; and (3) applying a temporally decaying injection to guide the model’s trajectory while employing a rollback mechanism to correct errors at their inception.

In summary, our main contributions are as follows:

•

Systematic Analysis of Dynamic RepE:
We provide the first comprehensive analysis bridging static attribute editing and dynamic reasoning. We reveal the
Decay Effect
and the
Uncertainty Principle
, establishing that intervention must be localized to early, high-entropy reasoning forks to be effective.

•

Subspace-Projected Steering Mechanism:
We identify that the geometric entanglement of truth and reasoning patterns limits standard RepE. We propose a novel application of Fisher-LDA to project internal representations onto a discriminative subspace, ensuring safe and precise steering.

•

DynaSteer
Framework:
Synthesizing these findings, we introduce
DynaSteer
, an adaptive framework that dynamically monitors uncertainty and intervenes only when necessary. Extensive evaluations conducted in both in-domain and out-of-domain scenarios show that our approach substantially outperforms existing baselines on complex reasoning benchmarks.

Preliminary: Representation Editing

Notation.

We consider an autoregressive Large Language Model (LLM)
\mathcal{M}
with
L
layers and
N
heads. For an input sequence
x
, let
\mathbf{h}^{(l,n)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the internal activation at layer
l
and head
n
, where
d
is the hidden dimension. The model generates the next token based on the final layer representation via a decoding head. We denote a contrastive dataset as
\mathcal{D}=\{(x_{i}^{+},x_{i}^{-})\}_{i=1}^{N}
, where
x^{+}
and
x^{-}
correspond to inputs eliciting the target concept (e.g., truth) and its opposite (e.g., hallucination), respectively.

Core Insight.

Representation Editing (RepE) operates on the geometry of the activation space. Its fundamental justification is the
Linear Representation Hypothesis

(
37
;
16
)
:

RepE assumes that high-level semantic concepts are linearly encoded in the activation space. Specifically, there exists a direction vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the intensity of the concept in any representation
\mathbf{h}
is determined by the projection
\mathbf{h}^{\top}\mathbf{v}
.

Based on this assumption, the steering vector
\mathbf{v}
representing the target concept is typically extracted by computing the difference in means between positive and negative activations:

\mathbf{v}\approx\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}}[\mathbf{h}(x^{+})]-\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}}[\mathbf{h}(x^{-})].

(1)

To control the model’s behavior during inference, we intervene on the hidden state
\mathbf{h}
by injecting this vector with a steering coefficient
\alpha
:

\tilde{\mathbf{h}}=\mathbf{h}+\alpha\cdot\frac{\mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{2}}.

(2)

This linear shift biases the model’s subsequent computations towards the target concept without altering the model weights.

3
Does the
Truth
Exist Within LLM Representations Along Reasoning Trajectories?

The theoretical foundation of RepE rests on the
Linear Representation Hypothesis
(see Sec.
2
).
While prior work
(
16
;
37
;
20
;
44
)
has demonstrated that
Truth
can be encoded in representations containing the answer text, it remains unclear whether
Truth
is also present in reasoning trajectories where the answer is not observable.

Formally, let
\mathcal{M}
denote an autoregressive LLM with
L
layers and
H
heads.
For a given input sequence, let
\mathbf{h}^{(l,n)}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the hidden-state activation at layer
l\in\{1,\dots,L\}
, head
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, and token position
t
.
We investigate whether there exists a global steering vector
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the induced binary decision

\mathrm{sgn}\left(\mathbf{h}^{(l,n)\top}_{t}\mathbf{v}\right)

serves as a reliable discriminator of the correctness of the trajectory.
If such a vector exists, the sign of
\mathbf{h}^{(l,n)\top}_{t}\mathbf{v}
should correlate systematically with the binary ground-truth label
y\in\{+1,-1\}
, where
y=+1
denotes a trajectory leading to
Truth
and
y=-1
denotes one leading to
Fallacy
. We deliberately employ linear probes to test the Linear Representation Hypothesis. By restricting the probe to minimal capacity, we ensure that the observed discriminability reflects the genuine linear structure of the
Truth
signal rather than the probe’s own expressive power, adhering to the probe complexity control principle
(
13
;
6
)
.

Semantic Granularity: From Tokens to Sentences.

Our initial probing experiments focused on the atomic unit of generation: the token. We defined the representation space at the token level, treating each
\mathbf{h}^{(l,n)}_{t}
as an independent sample. In both token-level and sentence-level settings, the hidden state is extracted from the last token’s activation of the input sequence. For token-level probing, the sequence is truncated at any arbitrary position. However, as shown in Fig.
2
(a), our empirical results indicate that the linear separability of
Truth
at this granularity is poor, with classification accuracy saturating near
55%
.
We further elevate the analysis to the
sentence level
, where trajectory segmentation is guided by punctuation (e.g.,
.\n
), restricting truncation exclusively to sentence boundaries. This acts as a filtering mechanism, ensuring hidden states are extracted only where complete semantic units form.
As shown in Fig.
2
(b), by shifting the domain from instantaneous token activations to semantically complete sentence representations, we observe a substantial increase in linear separability, with probing accuracy exceeding
69%
(
16%
\uparrow
) across multiple attention heads. This suggests that the
Truth
is an emergent property of semantic units rather than a localized feature of individual tokens.

Manifold Disentanglement via Pattern Clustering.

Despite the improvements yielded by sentence-level aggregation, the decision boundary between
Truth
and
Fallacy
remains noisy. We hypothesize that this noise arises from
reasoning heterogeneity
: the geometric realization of
Truth
may vary depending on the underlying reasoning pattern
(
3
;
18
)
(e.g., plan generation, fact retrieval). If the manifold of reasoning representations is non-convex or consists of multiple disjoint regions, a single global linear probe will be suboptimal.
To test this, we employ an unsupervised clustering approach to partition the activation space. Let
\mathcal{H}=\{\mathbf{h}_{S_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be the set of collected sentence representations. We apply
k
-means clustering to decompose
\mathcal{H}
into
M
disjoint clusters
\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{M}
, where each cluster conceptually corresponds to a distinct latent reasoning pattern. Within each cluster
\mathcal{C}_{j}
, we independently optimize a local linear probe defined by a cluster-specific
Truth Steering

\mathbf{v}_{j}
.

\max_{\mathbf{v}_{j}}\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}}\mathbb{I}[\mathrm{sgn}(\mathbf{h}^{\top}\mathbf{v}_{j})=y],

(3)

where
\mathbb{I}[\cdot]
is the indicator function. As shown in Fig.
2
(b) and (d), post-clustering analysis reveals a dramatic improvement in separability, with intra-cluster classification accuracy reaching approximately
78%
(
23%
\uparrow
).
We further illustrate the separability of these reasoning patterns using a t-SNE visualization in Fig.
7
.
This finding implies that while the
Truth
signal is strong, it is
entangled
with the pattern of reasoning. The global geometry of
Truth
is likely piecewise linear, becoming linearly accessible only when the latent reasoning patterns are disentangled.

Summary.

Our investigation confirms the existence of a recoverable
Truth
signal, subject to specific structural constraints.

Takeaway
: We validate the core prerequisite for RepE: a discernible Truth Direction exists within the LLM activation space. However, this direction is not encoded in isolated token-level activations but emerges in sentence-level aggregates. Furthermore, the linearity of this signal is significantly enhanced when latent reasoning patterns are disentangled via clustering.

4
Where and When Is Intervention Effective?

Having established the existence of the
Truth
, we now turn to the optimization of the intervention policy. A naive application of RepE at every time step is computationally prohibitive and risks degrading generation quality. Therefore, we seek to identify the optimal spatiotemporal coordinates for intervention.
We analyze this along two orthogonal axes: the
spatial locus
(identifying critical decision points) and the
temporal locus
(determining the effective window of opportunity).

Spatial Locus: Entropy-Based Selection.

Reasoning is not a uniform process; it consists of deterministic transitions (e.g., syntactic completion) and stochastic decision points, which we term
reasoning forks
. We hypothesize that intervention is only necessary at these forks, where the model’s internal confidence wavers between competing logical paths. To quantify this, we utilize the Shannon entropy
(
43
)
of the next-token distribution. Let
P(x_{t+1}\mid x_{\leq t})
denote the probability distribution over the vocabulary
\mathcal{V}
at time step
t
. The local uncertainty is given by:

\mathcal{H}(t)=-\sum_{w\in\mathcal{V}}P(w\mid x_{\leq t})\log P(w\mid x_{\leq t}).

(4)

Previous works
(
35
;
11
)
observe that peaks in
\mathcal{H}(t)
typically align with the initiation of new logical steps. Based on this, we investigate a
Selective Editing
strategy. Let
\tau
be a dynamic threshold derived from the distribution of entropy values within a trajectory (e.g., the top-
k
percentile). The intervention set is defined as
\mathcal{I}_{\text{select}}=\{t\mid H(t)>\tau\}
.

We investigate the relationship between the intrinsic
Recovery Rate
and entropy quantiles.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{neg}
denote the set of sentence-level segments belonging to trajectories that originally result in incorrect answers (
y=-1
).
For a given segment
s\in\mathcal{S}_{neg}
, we generate
N
independent stochastic rollouts. Let
\{y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{N}\}
denote the binary correctness labels of the resulting trajectories for these rollouts. We define the segment
s
as
recoverable
if the correct answer exists within the sampled space. Formally, the recoverability indicator
\mathcal{R}(s)
is defined as:

\mathcal{R}(s)=\mathbb{I}\left[\exists n\in\{1,\dots,N\}:y_{n}=1\right],

(5)

where
\mathbb{I}[\cdot]
is the indicator function. The
Recovery Rate
for a group of segments is then calculated as the expected value of
\mathcal{R}(s)
.

As illustrated in Fig.

, we observe a distinct correlation: for segments with low entropy, the
Recovery Rate
is minimal (peaking at only
12.3%
), indicating that the model is deeply “entrenched” in the fallacy and consistently reproduces the error even under stochastic sampling. Conversely, at high-entropy forks, the
Recovery Rate
rises significantly to
58.3%
. This suggests that at these uncertain moments, the
Truth
is still accessible within the model’s hypothesis space. Thus, entropy serves as a highly effective proxy for identifying the spatial locus of intervention.

Temporal Locus: The Decay Effect.

Beyond spatial selection, the timing of intervention relative to the overall trajectory is critical. We investigate the
temporal sensitivity
of the model’s intrinsic error-correction capability. We divide the reasoning trajectories into
5
relative temporal segments
T_{1},\dots,T_{5}
.
Our experiments, as shown in Fig.
3
, reveal a pronounced monotonic decay in this intrinsic potential:
\mathcal{R}(T_{1})\gg\mathcal{R}(T_{5})
. Specifically, segments located in the early stage (
T_{1}
) exhibit a hig
Recovery Rate
of
62.9%
, whereas those in the final stage (
T_{5}
) drop significantly to
24.9%
.

We term this phenomenon the
Decay Effect
. It suggests that autoregressive reasoning exhibits strong path dependence. As the model generates more tokens consistent with an initial fallacy, its internal state becomes “entrenched” in that error. The hidden states
\mathbf{h}_{t}
at later time steps
t\gg 1
are conditioned on a long history of incorrect tokens
x_{<t}
, making the incorrect trajectory a deep local minimum in the energy landscape. This implies that the “window of opportunity” for effective steering is transient. Interventions must be
anticipatory
: corrections are most feasible at the inception of a reasoning error (
T_{1}
), before the trajectory collapses into a deep local minimum from which even stochastic exploration cannot escape.

Summary.

The integration of spatial and temporal analyses defines a precise regime for efficient RepE.

Takeaway
: Effective intervention is highly localized in both space and time. Spatially, editing is strictly necessary only at high-entropy reasoning forks. Temporally, intervention must occur early in the derivation process, as the model’s susceptibility to correction decays rapidly due to the entrenchment of logical errors over time.

5
How to Mitigate the Potential Risks Associated with Intervention?

While the previous sections established the existence of
Truth
and the optimal timing for its application, a critical safety challenge remains. As shown in Fig.
4
, the distributions of representations for
Truth
(
\mathbf{h}^{+}
) and
Fallacy
(
\mathbf{h}^{-}
) are not perfectly disjoint. This overlap creates the risk of
False Positive Intervention
, where the steering vector inadvertently perturbs a correct reasoning process towards
Fallacy
. In this section, we analyze this risk through a geometric lens and propose a subspace projection method to mitigate it.

The Risk of Naive Mean-Difference Steering.

Standard RepE typically estimates the steering direction using the difference of class means. Let
\mathcal{D}^{+}
and
\mathcal{D}^{-}
be the sets of hidden representations for
Truth
and
Fallacy
, respectively. The Mean-Difference (MD) vector is defined as:

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{\text{MD}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mu}^{+}-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-},

(6)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}^{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}^{c}|}\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in\mathcal{D}^{c}}\mathbf{h},c\in\{+,-\}.

(7)

The intervention strength at inference time is proportional to the projection scalar
\mathbf{h}^{\top}\mathbf{v}_{\text{MD}}
. Ideally, this projection should act as a robust discriminator: maximizing intensity for
Fallacy
while vanishing (or becoming negative) for
Truth
.

However, the density plots in Fig.
4
reveal that upon projecting
h
onto
\mathbf{v}_{\text{MD}}
, a significant distributional overlap emerges, where a portion of
Truth
samples is indistinguishable from
Fallacy
.
This entanglement is further quantified in Fig.
5
: when the intervention strength for
Fallacy
is normalized to
1.000
,
Truth
samples inadvertently inherit a substantial strength of
0.394
.
This makes naive RepE a
losing proposition
: the expected utility is negative because the harm caused by corrupting
Truth
trajectories outweighs the benefit of repairing
Fallacy
ones.

. Finally, (d) shows the dynamic inference stage, where lookahead entropy monitoring identifies trajectories that need correction, applying an adaptive aggregated steering to guide the reasoning.

Fisher-LDA for
Truth
Purification.

We attribute the failure of naive mean-difference steering to the extreme
anisotropy
of the activation space, where non-informative outlier dimensions dominate the variance
(
30
)
. To purify the
Truth
signal, we employ Fisher Linear Discriminant Analysis (Fisher-LDA) to find a direction that maximizes class separability while suppressing within-class noise.

Let
\boldsymbol{\delta}=\boldsymbol{\mu}^{+}-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-}
be the mean difference between
Truth
and
Fallacy
representations. We define the within-class covariance matrix
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}
as:

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}=\sum_{c\in\{+,-\}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{c}|}\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in\mathcal{D}^{c}}(\mathbf{h}-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{c})(\mathbf{h}-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{c})^{\top}.

(8)

The optimal steering axis
\mathbf{w}^{*}
is derived by maximizing the Rayleigh Quotient
(
29
)
, yielding the closed-form solution
\mathbf{w}^{*}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\delta}
(see Appendix
B
for the detailed derivation). Under the Fisher-LDA paradigm, the adaptive intervention intensity during inference is computed as:

\eta(\mathbf{h})=\alpha\cdot\tanh\left(\text{ReLU}\left(\frac{\mu_{p\_proj}-h_{proj}}{\sigma_{p\_proj}}\right)\right),

(9)

where
h_{proj}=\mathbf{w}^{*\top}\mathbf{h}
, and
\mu_{p\_proj},\sigma_{p\_proj}
are the mean and standard deviation of positive samples in the projected space. This formulation acts as an asymmetric gate: the
ReLU
ensures that intervention is selectively applied only to representations lagging behind the
Truth
center, while the Z-score normalization and
tanh
function provide scale-invariant alignment and numerical stability against outliers. Finally,
\alpha
serves as a hyperparameter that regulates the global magnitude of the intervention.

As illustrated in Fig.
4
and Fig.
5
, projecting
h
onto the purified axis
\mathbf{w}^{*}
significantly reduces the distributional overlap compared to the naive mean-difference. This ensures that
Truth
representations receive minimal perturbation, thereby transforming RepE from a risky heuristic into a controlled and safe mechanism.

Summary.

We address the geometric entanglement of truth and semantics to ensure safe intervention.

Takeaway
: Naive mean-difference steering risks corrupting correct reasoning due to significant distributional overlap. By employing Fisher-LDA, we identify a purified subspace that maximizes the signal-to-noise ratio of the truth direction. This significantly reduces collateral damage to correct trajectories, ensuring a positive cost–benefit trade-off for the intervention.

6
Methodology

Building upon the empirical evidence of a recoverable
Truth
signal (Sec.
3
) and the spatiotemporal constraints of intervention (Sec.
4
), we introduce
DynaSteer
, a framework that bridges the gap between static representation editing and dynamic reasoning trajectories.

6.1
Consistency-based
Truth
Annotation

To align the representation space with semantic reasoning steps rather than atomic tokens,
DynaSteer
operates at the level of trajectory sentences. Let a reasoning trajectory generated by the model
\mathcal{M}
be decomposed into a sequence of sentences
\mathcal{T}=(S_{1},S_{2},\dots,S_{T})
, where each sentence
S_{t}
represents a coherent semantic unit, such as a clause or a deduction step.

DynaSteer
employs a consistency-based filtering strategy to construct a robust dataset
\mathcal{D}
. For a given sentence
S_{t}
located at a high-entropy reasoning fork, the framework performs
N
stochastic rollouts to generate a set of final answers
\{y_{1},\dots,y_{N}\}
. The semantic label
L(S_{t})
for the hidden representations associated with this sentence is assigned via consensus:

L(S_{t})=\begin{cases}+1(\text{{Truth}})&\text{if }y_{n}=1\forall n\in\{1,\dots,N\},\\
-1(\text{{Fallacy}})&\text{if }y_{n}\neq 1\forall n\in\{1,\dots,N\},\\
\bot(\text{Discard})&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(10)

Subsequently,
DynaSteer
collects the internal hidden states
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
corresponding to accepted sentences (
L(S_{t})\neq\bot
) to form the paired dataset
\mathcal{D}=\{(\mathbf{h}_{i},L(S_{i}))\}
.

6.2
Truth
-Relevant Head Location

Not all attention heads encode the
Truth
signal equally. To ensure computational efficiency and intervention precision,
DynaSteer
identifies the specific subset of heads that act as the locus for truth differentiation. Using the labeled dataset
\mathcal{D}
, it trains linear probes on the hidden states of each head across all layers.

DynaSteer
ranks the heads based on their probing accuracy in distinguishing
Truth
(
+1
) from
Fallacy
(
-1
) and selects the top-
k
heads, denoted as
\mathcal{H}_{\text{top}}
, to serve as the intervention targets. Consequently, the subsequent clustering and steering operations are restricted exclusively to representations
\mathbf{h}^{(l,h)}
where
(l,h)\in\mathcal{H}_{\text{top}}
.

6.3
Manifold Disentanglement & Truth Purification

As established in Sec.
3
, the geometry of
Truth
is entangled with latent reasoning patterns.
DynaSteer
addresses this via a two-stage offline process: separating reasoning modes and then purifying the truth direction within those modes.

Manifold Disentanglement via Pattern Clustering.

DynaSteer
hypothesizes that the activation space consists of multiple reasoning manifolds. To disentangle these, it computes the raw difference vector
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}=\mathbf{h}_{i}^{+}-\mathbf{h}_{i}^{-}
for pairs of positive and negative forks derived from the same context.
DynaSteer
applies
k
-means clustering to the set of difference vectors
\{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}\}
, partitioning the space into
M
disjoint clusters
\{\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{M}\}
. Each cluster
\mathcal{C}_{m}
conceptually represents a distinct reasoning pattern (e.g., symbolic logic vs. arithmetic derivation).

Truth
Purification via Fisher-LDA.

Within each reasoning cluster
\mathcal{C}_{m}
, to extract a purified steering direction,
DynaSteer
employs Fisher-LDA (Sec.
5
).

For a specific cluster
\mathcal{C}_{m}
and head, let
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{m}^{+}
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{m}^{-}
denote the centroids of the
Truth
and
Fallacy
classes, respectively.
DynaSteer
computes the within-class scatter matrix
\Sigma_{w,m}
and calculates steering vector
\mathbf{v}_{m}
:

\mathbf{v}_{m}=\Sigma_{w,m}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{m}^{+}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{m}^{-}).

(11)

The vector
\mathbf{v}_{m}
defines the normal to the hyperplane that best discriminates truth from fallacy within the specific reasoning mode
m
.

6.4
Dynamic Reasoning via Aggregated Steering

During the online inference phase,
DynaSteer
applies the pre-computed steering vectors dynamically. This process is governed by entropy monitoring and a rollback mechanism to handle the path-dependent nature of generation.

Lookahead Entropy Monitoring.

To avoid unnecessary perturbations,
DynaSteer
adopts a selective intervention strategy. As the model generates a candidate sentence
S_{\text{cand}}
, the framework computes its average token entropy
H(S_{\text{cand}})
. It employs a threshold
\gamma
, set to the
80^{\text{th}}
percentile of the training entropy distribution, following prior work
(
35
)
.

•

If
H(S_{\text{cand}})\leq\gamma
, the sentence is deemed deterministic and reliable; generation proceeds normally.

•

If
H(S_{\text{cand}})>\gamma
, the sentence represents a high-uncertainty reasoning fork, triggering the intervention mechanism.

Temporal Decay and Dynamic Strength Calculation.

When intervention is triggered at step
t
,
DynaSteer
calculates the steering parameters. First, to account for the
Decay Effect
, we define a temporal gating coefficient
\beta(t)=\mathbb{I}[t\leq\tau_{\text{decay}}]
, where
\tau_{\text{decay}}
is a pre-defined threshold. Intervention is applied only when
\beta(t)=1
.
Second,
DynaSteer
computes the dynamic intervention strength
\eta_{m}(\mathbf{h})
for each reasoning cluster
\mathcal{C}_{m}
according to Eq.
9
.

Multi-Steering Aggregation and Rollback.

The final steering is an aggregate of the purified directions from all reasoning clusters. The edited hidden state
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}
is formulated as:

\tilde{\mathbf{h}}=\mathbf{h}+\beta(t)\sum_{m=1}^{M}\eta_{m}(\mathbf{h})\mathbf{v}_{m}.

(12)

Crucially, mere injection is insufficient if the candidate sentence
S_{\text{cand}}
has already committed to an error. Therefore,
DynaSteer
executes a
Rollback
: it discards
S_{\text{cand}}
and forces the model to traverse the reasoning fork again, now guided by the purified
Truth
signal.

Ablation Studies.
We conduct ablation studies on all evaluation datasets using Qwen3-1.7B. As shown in Table

Furthermore, we investigate the choice of truth purification algorithms by replacing Fisher-LDA with the conventional Mean Difference (MD) method and Singular Value Decomposition (SVD). Both alternatives lead to noticeable performance drops. While SVD is numerically stable, it is an unsupervised approach that captures the maximum variance direction, which is not necessarily the most discriminative for separating truth from fallacy. In contrast, Fisher-LDA directly optimizes the between-class over within-class variance ratio using explicit
Truth
labels, extracting a significantly more robust and precise steering direction. These results collectively validate the necessity of the proposed components in enabling
DynaSteer
to accurately identify and extract truthful representations.
We further investigate two additional variants: removing Entropy Monitoring, i.e., applying truth steering to all sentences, and removing Temporal Decay. Notably, eliminating these components does not lead to a significant drop in performance. This is because steering low-entropy sentences is largely ineffective, as the model exhibits high confidence on such sentences and they are therefore difficult to edit meaningfully. Similarly, sentences appearing at the end of the reasoning trajectory often correspond to deeply entrenched errors, making it difficult to fully correct the model’s behavior.
Although these designs do not provide clear performance gains, we emphasize their advantages in inference efficiency. By editing only a subset of sentences, Entropy Monitoring and Temporal Decay reduce inference token consumption by approximately

Limitation, Discussion and Conclusion

Despite the promising results obtained in our work, it is important to acknowledge the limitations.
Current mainstream research regarding guiding LLMs through reasoning forks predominately relies on Reinforcement Learning (RL). Notably,
35
explicitly demonstrates that leveraging GRPO on reasoning forking tokens can substantially elevate the model’s reasoning capabilities. We compared
DynaSteer
against GRPO under identical experimental settings. The results indicate that GRPO achieves a performance of
84.6\%
(
6.4\%\uparrow
), whereas
DynaSteer
achieves
82.3\%
(
4.0\%\uparrow
).
We hypothesize that this performance gap primarily stems from the geometric nature of the
Truth
.
DynaSteer
assumes that
Truth Steering
can be captured by
linear steering vectors
. However, the actual manifold of
Truth
within complex reasoning trajectories involves
high-dimensional, non-linear dependencies
. RL methods like GRPO update the underlying policy parameters, enabling the model to internalize these complex, non-linear mappings more effectively than an external linear intervention.

Nevertheless, we claim a stark contrast on computational cost: GRPO requires approximately 160GB of VRAM and spans up to 15 hours of training time to converge. In contrast,
DynaSteer
is a training-free framework that directly modulates the LLM during inference. Consequently, while RL methods offer a higher upper bound in performance,
DynaSteer
makes a distinct contribution to resource-constrained communities.

Conclusion.
In this work, we present a systematic exploration of Representation Edit into the dynamics of
Truth
within LLM reasoning trajectories, moving beyond static attribute editing to dynamic process control. Our investigation establishes the theoretical foundations for dynamic intervention: confirming that the
Truth
exists but is geometrically entangled with reasoning patterns, and revealing that the window for effective correction is transient, constrained by the
Decay Effect
.

To operationalize these insights, we introduce
DynaSteer
, a RepE framework that leverages pattern clustering and Fisher-LDA,
DynaSteer
successfully disentangles the
Truth Steering
from other high-level semantic concepts, addressing the safety risks inherent in naive representation editing. Furthermore, our entropy-aware monitoring ensures that intervention is precise and economical, applied only at critical reasoning forks. Empirical results across diverse benchmarks confirm that
DynaSteer
not only mitigates hallucinations but also achieves superior performance compared to strong baselines. Ultimately, this work offers a resource-efficient alternative to reinforcement learning for steering LLMs, paving the way for more reliable, self-correcting AI systems that seek
Truth
from the very inception of their reasoning.

Appendix A
Related Work

Representation Editing for
Truth

Representation Editing (RepE) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for interpretability and control, aiming to locate and intervene on high-level concepts encoded within the linear activation space of LLMs
(
48
;
19
;
10
)
. In the context of
Truth
,
16
proposed Inference-Time Intervention (ITI), which identifies attention heads correlated with truthfulness and shifts their activations to suppress hallucinations. Similarly,
24
investigated the geometry of truth, revealing that truthful statements often align along specific linear directions in the representation space regardless of the topic. However, a critical limitation of these approaches is their reliance on
static
editing derived from datasets containing explicit answers or simple true/false statements.
There is a notable lack of exploration into dynamic representation editing, specifically regarding how to calculate truth directions within unfolding reasoning trajectories where the final answer is latent and not yet observable.

Reinforcing LLM Reasoning

Enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models has predominantly relied on Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigms. Recent advancements have shifted focus from outcome-based supervision to process-oriented approaches, where the model is guided at critical “reasoning forks”. For instance,
21
demonstrated that Process Reward Models (PRMs) can significantly improve mathematical reasoning by verifying intermediate steps. Furthermore,
31
introduced Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which leverages group-level comparisons at reasoning forks to efficiently steer the model toward correct trajectories, yielding substantial performance gains.

35
further demonstrates that leveraging GRPO on reasoning forking tokens can substantially elevate the model’s reasoning capabilities.

Despite their effectiveness, these RL-based methods suffer from prohibitive computational costs, often requiring massive GPU resources (e.g., hundreds of gigabytes of VRAM) and extensive training time, rendering them largely inaccessible to resource-constrained research communities.

Appendix B
Derivation of the Fisher-LDA Steering Vector

In this section, we provide the detailed mathematical derivation for the purified steering vector
\mathbf{w}^{*}
used in Section
5
.

Rayleigh Quotient Objective

Let
\mathcal{D}^{+}=\{\mathbf{h}^{+}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N^{+}}
and
\mathcal{D}^{-}=\{\mathbf{h}^{-}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N^{-}}
denote the sets of hidden state activations (defined in Section
2
) corresponding to
Truth
and
Fallacy
trajectories, respectively. Let
N=N^{+}+N^{-}
be the total sample size.
Let
\boldsymbol{\delta}=\boldsymbol{\mu}^{+}-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-}
be the mean difference vector, where
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{+}
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-}
are the class centroids.
Consistent with Eq.
1
and the definition in Section
5
, the within-class covariance matrix
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}
is defined as:

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}=\sum_{c\in\{+,-\}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}^{c}|}\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in\mathcal{D}^{c}}(\mathbf{h}-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{c})(\mathbf{h}-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{c})^{\top}.

(13)

To find the optimal direction
\mathbf{w}
, we maximize the Rayleigh Quotient
\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{w})
, which represents the ratio of the
inter-class signal
to the
intra-class noise
:

\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{w})=\frac{(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\delta})^{2}}{\mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{w}}.

(14)

By minimizing the denominator, we penalize directions aligned with high-variance outlier dimensions, thereby aligning
\mathbf{w}
with the most discriminative
Truth
features.

Analytical Solution and Regularization

To maximize
\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{w})
, we set its gradient with respect to
\mathbf{w}
to zero. Using the quotient rule:

\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{w})=\frac{2(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\delta})\boldsymbol{\delta}(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{w})-(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\delta})^{2}(2\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{w})}{(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{w})^{2}}=\mathbf{0}.

(15)

Assuming
\mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\delta}\neq 0
, we can divide by
2(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\delta})
and rearrange the terms to obtain the generalized eigenvalue problem:

\left(\frac{\mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\delta}}\right)\boldsymbol{\delta}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{w}.

(16)

Since the term in the parenthesis is a scalar
\lambda
, this implies
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}\mathbf{w}\propto\boldsymbol{\delta}
. Assuming
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}
is invertible, the analytical closed-form solution is:

\mathbf{w}^{*}\propto\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\delta}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{+}-\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-}).

(17)

In practical LLM scenarios,
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}
may become singular when the number of samples
N<d
. Even when
N>d
, the matrix often becomes ill-conditioned due to high multi-collinearity. To ensure numerical stability, we employ shrinkage estimation to obtain a positive-definite estimate:

\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{w}=(1-\zeta)\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{w}+\zeta\mathbf{I},

(18)

where
\zeta\in[0,1]
is the shrinkage coefficient (determined by the Ledoit-Wolf estimator). The final discriminant axis used in
DynaSteer
is
\mathbf{w}^{*}=\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{w}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\delta}
.

Construction of Contrastive Pairs.
To reliably estimate the
Truth Steering
vector, we meticulously construct positive and negative contrastive samples from the training set. Specifically, we prompt the LLM to generate reasoning trajectories. At each generated sentence boundary, we compute the lookahead entropy. When the entropy exceeds a pre-defined threshold—indicating a critical reasoning fork—we pause the primary generation and conduct
N
independent stochastic rollouts from that exact state (in our experiments,
N=10
). We then evaluate the final answers of all
N

Truth
(
+1

Fallacy
(
-1
). To ensure geometric purity of the representations and to eliminate noise, any ambiguous cases—where the rollouts produce a mix of correct and incorrect answers—are strictly discarded. This highly rigorous filtering strategy guarantees that the resulting contrastive pair dataset robustly captures the intrinsic directional difference between truth and fallacy.
