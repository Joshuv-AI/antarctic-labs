Schrödinger equations and fluctuation theorems for collisionless plasma systems Preprint: AIP/123-QED
H. Sugama

Abstract
The fluctuation theorem and detailed fluctuation theorem are formulated for classical systems whose governing equations can be written in Schrödinger-type equations and which possess either a unitary or an antiunitary time-reversal operator. The initial state vector is treated as a random variable drawn from a time-reversal-symmetric probability distribution, and a stochastic relative entropy defined from its probability density is used to formulate these theorems. The framework is applied to two collisionless plasma systems: the linear Vlasov-Poisson and linear gyrokinetic systems. For the linear Vlasov-Poisson system, the governing equations are recast into Schrödinger form, and Hamiltonian eigenvectors corresponding to Case-Van Kampen modes are derived to construct explicit solutions. The stochastic relative entropy is interpreted as entropy generation associated with Landau damping, in which electric-field energy is transferred from the lowest Hermite state to higher-order Hermite states acting as thermal reservoirs. For a specific class of initial distributions, a new analytical expression for the probability density function of the stochastic relative entropy is derived and validated numerically. For the linear gyrokinetic system in a uniform magnetic field, the governing equations are likewise transformed into Schrödinger form, and the corresponding time-reversal operators are identified. The state-vector space is constructed as a tensor product of species, perpendicular-velocity, and parallel-velocity spaces. The resulting state vectors decompose into two orthogonal components: one coupled to electromagnetic fluctuations and the other corresponding to ballistic modes. These results establish a nonequilibrium statistical-mechanical framework for collisionless plasma dynamics and provide useful examples for future quantum-computing applications to plasma simulations.

To explicity show that the integrand on the right-hand side of

\displaystyle P({\bf z}_{B}|{\bf z}_{A})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\Gamma[\bm{\psi}(0)]\;P_{Y}(\bm{\eta}[0;\bm{\psi}(0)];0)

(50)

\displaystyle\mbox{}\times\delta^{2N_{z}}(\bm{\zeta}[0;\bm{\psi}(0)]-{\bf z}_{A})

\displaystyle\mbox{}\times\delta^{2N_{z}}(\bm{\zeta}[\tau;\bm{\psi}(0)]-{\bf z}_{B}),

where

\displaystyle\log\left[\frac{P_{Y}[\bm{\eta}[0;\bm{\psi}(0)];0]}{P_{Y}[\bm{\eta}[\tau;\bm{\psi}(0)];0]}\right]

\displaystyle\log\left[\frac{P_{Y}[\bm{\eta}[0;\bm{\psi}(0)];0]}{P_{Y}[\bm{\eta}[0;\bm{\psi}(\tau)];0]}\right],

Then, we use

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76219ptP({\bf z}_{B},\Delta S^{\prime}|{\bf z}_{A})

\displaystyle\int d\Gamma(0)\;P_{Y}[{\bf y}(0);0]\;\delta^{2N_{z}}({\bf z}(0)-{\bf z}_{A})

\displaystyle\mbox{}\times\delta^{2N_{z}}({\bf z}(\tau)-{\bf z}_{B})\;\delta\bigl(\Delta S_{Y}[{\bf z}_{A},{\bf y}(0);\tau]-\Delta S^{\prime}\bigr)

\displaystyle\int d\Gamma[\bm{\psi}(0)]\;P_{Y}[\bm{\eta}[0;\bm{\psi}(0)];0]

\displaystyle\mbox{}\times\delta\bigl(\Delta S_{Y}[\bm{\zeta}[0;\bm{\psi}(0)],\bm{\eta}[0;\bm{\psi}(0)];\tau]-\Delta S^{\prime}\bigr).

Using Eqs. (

\displaystyle\langle\Delta S_{Y}\rangle_{{\bf z}_{A}}

\displaystyle\int d\Gamma_{y}(0)P_{Y}[{\bf y}(0);0]\log\left[\frac{P_{Y}[{\bf y}(0);0]}{P_{Y}[{\bf y}(\tau);0]}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0,

III
Linear Vlasov-Poisson System

Following Ref.
Sugama2025
, we reformulate the linearized Vlasov-Poisson system as a Schrödinger equation.
Details about Hamiltonian eigenvectors associated with the Case-Van Kampen (CVK) modes, time-reversal operators,
and a newly derived approximate expression for the probability density function of the stochastic relative entropy
(which are not included in Ref.
Sugama2025
)
are provided in this section and Appendices D and E.

III.1
Linearized Vlasov-Poisson equations and invariant

The electron distribution function in the two-dimensional phase space
(x,v)
at time
t

is denoted by

f(x,v,t)
.
It is obtained from the six-dimensional distribution function
F(x,y,z,v_{x},v_{y},v_{z},t)

as
f(x,v_{x},t)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv_{y}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv_{z}F(x,y,z,v_{x},v_{y},v_{z},t)
,
and
F
is assumed to be independent of
y
and
z
.
We decompose
f(x,v,t)
into a Maxwellian equilibrium,

f_{0}(v)=\pi^{-1/2}(n_{0}/v_{T})\exp(-v^{2}/v_{T}^{2})

and a perturbation
f_{1}(x,v,t)
.
Here,

v_{T}\equiv\sqrt{2}v_{t}\equiv\sqrt{2T/m}
,
where
n_{0}
and
T
are the equilibrium electron density and temperature, respectively,
and
m
is the electron mass.
Assuming a collisionless plasma,
we employ
the linearized Vlasov equation
Nicholson
,

\frac{\partial f_{1}(x,v,t)}{\partial t}+v\frac{\partial f_{1}(x,v,t)}{\partial x}-\frac{e}{m}E(x,t)\frac{\partial f_{0}(v)}{\partial v}=0,

(61)

where
-e
is the electron charge and
the nonlinear term
-(e/m)E(x,t)\partial f_{1}(x,v,t)/\partial v
is neglected.
Ions are assumed to have a uniform density
n_{0}
, and the ion motion
is ignored because the ion mass is much larger than the electron mass.
The electric field
E(x,t)
in the
x
-direction is determined
from Poisson’s equation,

\frac{\partial E(x,t)}{\partial x}=-4\pi e\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\,f_{1}(x,v,t).

(62)

We assume the system to be periodic with period
L
in the
x
-direction,
and impose the constraint condition,
\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx\,E(x,t)=0
.
Here, we do not consider an equilibrium electric field that could
give rise to an inhomogeneous equilibrium distribution of electrons.

It can be shown that

D[f_{1}]\equiv\int_{-L/2}^{+L/2}\frac{dx}{L}\biggl[\frac{[E(x,t)]^{2}}{8\pi n_{0}T}+\frac{1}{n_{0}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;\frac{[f_{1}(x,v,t)]^{2}}{2f_{0}(v)}\biggr],

(63)

is conserved for any solution of the linearized Vlasov-Poisson equations.
From Eqs. (
62
) and (
63
), one finds that
the invariant functional
D[f_{1}]
takes a quadratic form with respect to
f_{1}
.
It is well known that
the energy and the Gibbs entropy per single electron,
defined by

{\cal E}\equiv(n_{0}L)^{-1}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx\,[\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dvfmv^{2}/2+E^{2}/8\pi]

and

S_{f}\equiv-(n_{0}L)^{-1}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dvf\log f
,
respectively,
are conserved in the nonlinear Vlasov-Poisson system
although they are not in the linear system.
As noted in Ref.
Sugama2025
,
we obtain the relation,

D[f_{1}]={\cal E}^{(2)}/T-S_{f}^{(2)}
,
where
{\cal E}^{(2)}
and
S_{f}^{(2)}
denote the second-order terms in the expansions of
the
{\cal E}
and
S_{f}
,
respectively,
with respect to the ordering parameter
\alpha\sim f_{1}/f_{0}
,
characterizing the perturbation amplitude
Maekaku
.

III.2
Representing functions in velocity space as ket vectors

We consider the solution

f_{1}(x,v,t)
of the linearized Vlasov-Poisson equations written as

f_{1}(x,v,t)=\mbox{Re}[f_{1}(k,v,t)\exp(ikx)]
,
and define the normalized time and velocity by

\tau\equiv kv_{T}t

and
\xi\equiv v/v_{T}
, respectively.
Using the Hermite polynomials,

H_{n}(\xi)\equiv(-1)^{n}e^{\xi^{2}}d^{n}(e^{-\xi^{2}})/d\xi^{n}\;(n=0,1,2,\cdots)
,
we introduce

h_{n}(\xi)\equiv\frac{e^{-\xi^{2}/2}}{\pi^{1/4}}\frac{H_{n}(\xi)}{(2^{n}n!)^{1/2}},

(64)

which satisfy

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\xi\;h_{n}(\xi)h_{n^{\prime}}(\xi)=\delta_{nn^{\prime}}
.
We also define the dimensionless function

\widetilde{f}(\kappa,\xi,\tau)
by

f_{1}(k,v,t)=(n_{0}/v_{T})h_{0}(\xi)\widetilde{f}(\kappa,\xi,\tau),

(65)

where the normalized wavenumber is defined by
\kappa\equiv k\lambda_{D}

with the Debye length
\lambda_{D}\equiv\omega_{p}/v_{t}

and the plasma frequency
\omega_{p}=(4\pi n_{0}e^{2}/m)^{1/2}
.
The linearized Vlasov–Poisson equations then reduce to

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\widetilde{f}(\kappa,\xi,\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi\widetilde{f}(\kappa,\xi,\tau)+\kappa^{-2}\xi h_{0}(\xi)

(66)

\displaystyle\mbox{}\times\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\xi^{\prime}\;h_{0}(\xi^{\prime})\widetilde{f}(\kappa,\xi^{\prime},\tau).

Following the notation of quantum mechanics,
QM

we associate complex-valued functions of the normalized velocity
\xi

with ket vectors denoted by
|\;\rangle
.
Thus, the ket vectors represent electron distributions in velocity space rather than in position space.
The basis vectors
|n\rangle
and
|\xi^{\prime}\rangle
correspond to
the functions
h_{n}(\xi)
and
\delta(\xi-\xi^{\prime})
, respectively.
The bra vector conjugate to
|u\rangle
is denoted by
\langle u|
.
Then,
\delta(\xi-\xi^{\prime})
and
h_{n}(\xi)
are expressed as

\langle\xi|\xi^{\prime}\rangle=\delta(\xi-\xi^{\prime})

(67)

and

\langle\xi|n\rangle=h_{n}(\xi),

(68)

respectively, and the orthonormality condition satisfied by
h_{n}(\xi)
is rewritten as

\langle n|n^{\prime}\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\xi\;\langle n|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|n^{\prime}\rangle=\delta_{nn^{\prime}}.

(69)

The sets
\{|\xi\rangle\}_{-\infty<\xi<+\infty}
and

\{|n\rangle\}_{n=0,1,2,\cdots}

form two orthonormal bases satisfying the closure relation,

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|\xi\rangle d\xi\langle\xi|=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|n\rangle\langle n|=\widehat{1},

(70)

where
\widehat{1}
denotes the identity operator.
We define the operators

\widehat{\Xi}\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\xi\;|\xi\rangle\xi d\xi\langle\xi|

(71)

and

\widehat{N}\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|n\rangle n\langle n|,

(72)

which satisfy

\widehat{\Xi}|\xi\rangle=\xi|\xi\rangle

and

\widehat{N}|n\rangle=n|n\rangle
.
A representation of state vectors and operators refers to expressing them as column vectors and matrices of complex numbers with respect to a chosen set of basis vectors, and it depends on that choice.
QM

The representations associated with

\{|\xi\rangle\}_{-\infty<\xi<+\infty}

and

\{|n\rangle\}_{n=0,1,2,\cdots}

are referred to as the

\{\Xi\}
and
\{N\}
representations, respectively.

We define the ket vector

|\widetilde{f}(\tau)\rangle

by

\langle\xi|\widetilde{f}(\tau)\rangle=\widetilde{f}(\kappa,\xi,\tau),

(73)

where the
\kappa
-dependence is omitted in the notation
|\widetilde{f}(\tau)\rangle
for simplicity.
Equation (
66
) can then be written as

i\frac{d}{d\tau}|\widetilde{f}(\tau)\rangle=\widehat{\Xi}\bigl(\widehat{1}+\kappa^{-2}|0\rangle\langle 0|\bigr)|\widetilde{f}(\tau)\rangle.

(74)

Since

\widehat{\Xi}(\widehat{1}+\kappa^{-2}|0\rangle\langle 0|)

is not Hermitian,
Eq. (
74
) does not
take a form of the Schrödinger equation,
and
\langle\widetilde{f}(\tau)|\widetilde{f}(\tau)\rangle

is not conserved.
The invariant
D[f_{1}]
of the linearized Vlasov-Poisson system is expressed as

\displaystyle D[f_{1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\biggl[\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\xi\;[\widetilde{f}(\kappa,\xi,\tau)]^{2}

(75)

\displaystyle\mbox{}+\kappa^{-2}\Big|\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\xi\;h_{0}(\xi)\widetilde{f}(\kappa,\xi,\tau)\Bigr|^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left[\langle\widetilde{f}(\tau)|\widetilde{f}(\tau)\rangle+\kappa^{-2}\langle\widetilde{f}(\tau)|0\rangle\langle 0|\widetilde{f}(\tau)\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\langle\widetilde{f}(\tau)|\bigl(\widehat{1}+\kappa^{-2}|0\rangle\langle 0|\bigr)|\widetilde{f}(\tau)\rangle.

We define the Hermitian operator
\widehat{A}
by

\displaystyle\widehat{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{A}^{\dagger}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|n\rangle\left(1+\kappa^{-2}\delta_{n0}\right)^{1/2}\langle n|

(76)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{1}+|0\rangle\bigl[\left(1+\kappa^{-2}\right)^{1/2}-1\bigr]\langle 0|,

where
\dagger
represents the Hermitian-conjugate operator.
Its inverse and square are

\displaystyle\widehat{A}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|n\rangle\left(1+\kappa^{-2}\delta_{n0}\right)^{-1/2}\langle n|

(77)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{1}+|0\rangle\bigl[\left(1+\kappa^{-2}\right)^{-1/2}-1\bigr]\langle 0|

and

\widehat{A}^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|n\rangle\left(1+\kappa^{-2}\delta_{n0}\right)\langle n|=\widehat{1}+|0\rangle\kappa^{-2}\langle 0|,

(78)

respectively.

Defining the state vector

|\psi(\tau)\rangle\equiv\widehat{A}|\widetilde{f}(\tau)\rangle
,
and using Eqs. (
75
) and (
78
),
the invariant becomes

D[f_{1}]=\frac{1}{4}\langle\widetilde{f}(\tau)|\widehat{A}^{2}|\widetilde{f}(\tau)\rangle=\frac{1}{4}\langle\psi(\tau)|\psi(\tau)\rangle.

(79)

Therefore,

\langle\psi(\tau)|\psi(\tau)\rangle

is conserved, and
the time evolution operator
\widehat{U}(\tau)
defined by

|\psi(\tau)\rangle=\widehat{U}(\tau)|\psi(0)\rangle

is unitary.
We define the Hamiltonian operator by

\widehat{H}=\widehat{A}\,\widehat{\Xi}\,\widehat{A},

(80)

which is manifestly Hermitian.
Applying
\widehat{A}
to both sides of Eq. (
74
)
and using Eq. (
80
), we find that

|\psi(\tau)\rangle
satisfies
the Schrödinger equation shown in Eq. (
2
) and that
the time evolution operator is expressed as

\widehat{U}(\tau)=\exp(-i\tau\widehat{H})
.

Using

2\xi H_{n}(\xi)=H_{n+1}(\xi)+2nH_{n-1}(\xi)
,
we can express
the Hamiltonian
\widehat{H}
in Eq. (
80
) and the Schrödinger equation
in the
\{N\}
representation as

\widehat{H}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{n+1+\kappa^{-2}\delta_{n0}}\Bigl(|n+1\rangle\langle n|+|n\rangle\langle n+1|\Bigr),

(81)

and

i\,d\psi_{n}(\tau)/d\tau=\sum_{n^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}H_{nn^{\prime}}\psi_{n^{\prime}}(\tau)
,
respectively,
where

\psi_{n}(\tau)\equiv\langle n|\psi(\tau)\rangle

and

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581ptH_{nn^{\prime}}\equiv\langle n|\widehat{H}|n^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big[\delta_{n,n^{\prime}+1}\sqrt{n+\kappa^{-2}\delta_{n^{\prime}0}}+\delta_{n+1,n^{\prime}}\sqrt{n^{\prime}+\kappa^{-2}\delta_{n0}}\big].

In Eq. (
81
),

|n+1\rangle\langle n|
and
|n\rangle\langle n+1|

act as creation and annihilation operators, respectively, on the basis states
basis vectors
|n\rangle

(n=0,1,2,\cdots)
.
They describe the energy transfer in Landau damping
from macroscopic to increasingly fine velocity-space structures
through transitions among the discrete states

|n\rangle

(n=0,1,2,\cdots)
.

III.3
Time-reversal operators in the linear Vlasov-Poisson system

We here define the time-reversal unitary and antiunitary operators,

\widehat{T}
and
\widehat{K}
, for the linear Vlasov-Poisson system.
They need to satisfy the conditions given by Eqs. (
260
)
and (
261
) in Appendix A.

The time-reversal unitary operator
\widehat{T}
is
the linear operator [see Eq. (
258
)] defined by

\widehat{T}|\xi\rangle=|-\xi\rangle\hskip 8.53581pt(-\infty<\xi<+\infty).

(83)

Equivalently, it is defined by

\widehat{T}|n\rangle=(-1)^{n}|n\rangle\hskip 8.53581pt(n=0,1,2,\cdots).

(84)

It follows that

\widehat{T}=\widehat{T}^{-1}=\widehat{T}^{\dagger}

as required from Eq. (
260
).
Furthermore,

\widehat{T}\,\widehat{\Xi}=-\widehat{\Xi}\,\widehat{T},\hskip 8.53581pt\widehat{T}\,\widehat{A}=\widehat{A}\,\widehat{T},

(85)

where
\widehat{\Xi}
and
\widehat{A}

III.5

Hereafter, we consider a finite set of CVK state vectors,

IV.2
Derivation of the Schrödinger equation for the linear gyrokinetic system

We define dimensionless variables

\xi
and
X
by

\xi\equiv v_{\parallel}/v_{Ta}
and

X\equiv v_{\perp}^{2}/v_{Ta}^{2}

to represent
parallel and perpendicular coordinates in the velocity-space.

f_{aM}

expressed as

(126)

where

l_{0}(X)\equiv e^{-X/2}
and

\tau\equiv k_{\parallel}v_{c}t

\displaystyle\hskip 34.1433pt\mbox{}\times\bigg(\phi_{\bf k}(\tau)-\frac{v_{Ta}}{c}\xi A_{\parallel{\bf k}}(\tau)\bigg)+J_{1}(\sqrt{2b_{a}X})

\displaystyle\mbox{}\hskip 34.1433pt\times\frac{v_{Ta}}{c}\sqrt{X}\frac{B_{\parallel{\bf k}}(\tau)}{k_{\perp}}\bigg\}\bigg]

IV.4

We here define time-reversal unitary and antiunitary

The time reversal unitary operator
\widehat{T}
is defined as a

\widehat{T}|a\,j\,\xi\rangle=|a\,j\,-\xi\rangle,

(245)

\widehat{T}|a\,j\,n\rangle=(-1)^{n}|a\,j\,n\rangle.

(246)

in the linear Vlasov-Poisson system,

\widehat{T}
defined above
corresponds to the transformation from the perturbed gyrocenter
distribution function

We find from

\widehat{T}\,\widehat{A}=\widehat{A}\,\widehat{T}
,

\widehat{T}\,\widehat{H}_{\rm I}=-\widehat{H}_{\rm I}\,\widehat{T}
,

\widehat{T}\,\widehat{H}_{\rm I\!I}=-\widehat{H}_{\rm I\!I}\,\widehat{T}
,
and

\widehat{T}\,\widehat{H}=-\widehat{H}\,\widehat{T}
.

\widehat{K}
is

(247)

\widehat{K}|a\,j\,n\rangle=|a\,j\,n\rangle.

),
(

and (

\widehat{K}\,\widehat{A}_{\rm I}=\widehat{K}_{\rm I}\,\widehat{T}

V
Conclusions

This study presents a framework for applying the fluctuation theorem and the detailed fluctuation theorem to classical systems
whose
dynamics are governed by Schrödinger-type equations and which
possess either a unitary or an antiunitary time-reversal operator.
To apply these theorems, the initial state vector is treated as a random variable obeying a specified time-reversal-symmetric probability distribution, and the stochastic relative entropy is defined from the probability distribution of the random state vector.
The linear Vlasov-Poisson and linear gyrokinetic systems are investigated as examples of collisionless plasmas to which the fluctuation theorems can be applied.
In both systems, an invariant proportional to the squared perturbed distribution function exists, which serves as the basis for defining the state vector satisfying the Schrödinger equation.
Unitary and antiunitary time-reversal operators are also identified for these systems.

In the linear Vlasov-Poisson system, the Hamiltonian eigenvectors correspond to the Case–Van Kampen (CVK) modes, and a discrete subset of these eigenvectors is used to construct the solutions for which the fluctuation theorem is formulated.
In this example, the stochastic relative entropy is interpreted as the entropy generated through Landau damping, which transfers the electric-field energy of the
n=0
Hermite state to thermal reservoirs consisting of the
n\geq 1
Hermite states.
In addition, a novel analytical expression for the probability density function of the stochastic relative entropy is derived under specific conditions, and its validity is verified numerically.

The governing equations of the linear gyrokinetic system are also transformed into Schrödinger form, and the corresponding unitary and antiunitary time-reversal operators are defined.
Thus, the fluctuation theorems can also be applied to this system.
The tensor product of the three vector spaces representing particle species, perpendicular velocity space, and parallel velocity space is employed to define the state vectors.
The state vectors, Hamiltonian, and time-evolution operator decompose into two classes.
The first class is associated with perpendicular velocity-space structures generated by the zeroth- and first-order Bessel functions, whereas the second is orthogonal to the first.
An analytical expression is obtained for the solution in the second class, which represents a ballistic mode that decays rapidly and independently of electromagnetic fluctuations.
We also show how the solution in the first class can be represented using basis vectors in the tensor-product state-vector space.

The present study contributes to nonequilibrium statistical-mechanical formulations of collisionless plasma phenomena and to future applications of quantum-computing algorithms to plasma simulations.
Numerical investigations of the present framework for the linear gyrokinetic system, along with extensions to other systems, remain topics for future study.

Appendix F
Orthonormal Basis Vectors in
{\cal E}_{ps}\otimes{\cal E}_{v_{\perp}}

We here present two kinds of orthonormal basis vectors,
\{|a\,l_{j}\rangle\}

and
\{|a\,j\rangle\}
, in the vector space

{\cal E}_{ps}\otimes{\cal E}_{v_{\perp}}
.
The Laguerre polynomials are used to define
\{|a\,l_{j}\rangle\}

while
\{|a\,j\rangle\}
are defined so as to efficiently describe
the linear gyrokinetic system as shown in Sec. IV.

F.1
Orthonormal basis vectors
\{|a\,l_{j}\rangle\}

The Laguerre polynomials
L_{j}(X)

(j=0,1,2,\cdots)
are defined by

L_{j}(X)\equiv\sum_{r=0}^{j}(-1)^{r}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j\\
r\end{array}\right)\frac{X^{r}}{r!}\equiv\frac{e^{X}}{j!}\frac{d^{j}}{dX^{j}}\Big(e^{-X}X^{j}\Big),

(304)

from which, for example, we have

L_{0}(X)=1
,

L_{1}(X)=1-X
,
and

L_{2}(X)=1-2X+\frac{1}{2}X^{2}
.
They satisfy the orthogonality relation,

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-X}L_{j}(X)L_{j^{\prime}}(X)dX=\delta_{jj^{\prime}}
.
Using the Laguerre polynomials,
we define functions
l_{j}(X)
and ket vectors
|l_{j}\rangle

(j=0,1,2,\cdots)

in
{\cal E}_{v_{\perp}}
by

l_{j}(X)\equiv\langle X|l_{j}\rangle\equiv e^{-X/2}L_{j}(X),

(305)

which satisfy

\displaystyle\langle l_{j}|l_{j^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\langle l_{j}|X\rangle dX\langle X|l_{j^{\prime}}\rangle

(306)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}l_{j}(X)l_{j^{\prime}}(X)dX=\delta_{jj^{\prime}}.

The basis vectors
\{|a\,l_{j}\rangle\}

in
{\cal E}^{\times}\equiv{\cal E}_{ps}\otimes{\cal E}_{v_{\perp}}
are defined by

|a\,l_{j}\rangle\equiv|a\rangle\otimes|l_{j}\rangle,

(307)

for which the orthonormality condition,

\langle a\,l_{j}|a\,l_{j}\rangle=\langle a|a^{\prime}\rangle\langle l_{j}|l_{j^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta_{aa^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}

(308)

holds.

F.2
Orthonormal basis vectors
\{|a\,j\rangle\}

We consider functions
l^{a}_{j}(X)
and ket vectors

|l^{a}_{j}\rangle

(j=0,1,2,\cdots)
in
{\cal E}_{v_{\perp}}

which are related to each other by

l^{a}_{j}(X)=\langle X|l^{a}_{j}\rangle.

(309)

In the case of

,
we define
l^{a}_{0}(X)
by

l^{a}_{0}(X)\equiv\langle X|l^{a}_{0}\rangle\equiv C_{a0}l_{0}(X)J_{0}(\sqrt{2b_{a}X}),

(310)

where
b_{a}\equiv k_{\perp}^{2}c^{2}m_{a}T_{a}/(e_{a}B)^{2}
and

l_{0}(X)\equiv e^{-X/2}
[see Eq. (
305
)].
The coefficient
C_{a0}
on the right-hand side of
Eq. (
310
) is determined
from the normalization condition,

\langle l^{a}_{0}|l^{a}_{0}\rangle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\big[l^{a}_{0}(X)\big]^{2}dX=1
,
as

C_{a}=1/\sqrt{\Gamma_{0}(b_{a})}.

(311)

We see that

C_{a}\rightarrow 1
and
l^{a}_{0}(X)\rightarrow l_{0}(X)

b_{a}\rightarrow+0
.

Next, the function
l^{a}_{1}(X)

\langle l^{a}_{1}|l^{a}_{1}\rangle=1

Following the Gram-Schmidt algorithm,

\displaystyle l^{a}_{1}(X)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle C_{a1}\bigg[l_{0}(X)\sqrt{X}J_{1}(\sqrt{2b_{a}X})-l^{a}_{0}(X)

\displaystyle\mbox{}\times\int_{0}^{+\infty}l^{a}_{0}(X^{\prime})l_{0}(X^{\prime})\sqrt{X^{\prime}}J_{1}(\sqrt{2b_{a}X^{\prime}})dX^{\prime}\bigg],

In deriving Eq. (

F.2

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-X}\sqrt{X}J_{0}(\sqrt{2b_{a}X})J_{1}(\sqrt{2b_{a}X})dX
