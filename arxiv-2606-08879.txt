Detector Resolution and Observable Infrared Memory in QED
Takeshi Fukuyama

Abstract
Infrared divergences in QED cancel in inclusive observables through the Bloch–Nordsieck and KLN mechanisms. However, this cancellation removes only the unphysical infrared regulator. The detector resolution scale ω max \omega_{\max} , which specifies the maximum energy of unresolved soft photons, remains in the observable cross section. We emphasize that this surviving scale has a natural interpretation as a coarse-graining scale in the reduced density matrix of the hard sector. Soft photons below ω max \omega_{\max} are not observed and are effectively traced over. The corresponding soft-sector overlap therefore becomes resolution dependent, D i ​ j = D i ​ j ​ ( ω max ) D_{ij}=D_{ij}(\omega_{\max}) . Observable infrared memory is consequently defined not only by the asymptotic soft sector itself, but also by the resolution scale separating observed and unobserved infrared degrees of freedom. This provides a bridge between the traditional infrared-safe cross-section formulation and the modern interpretation of soft photons as carriers of infrared memory and quantum information.



I
Introduction

Infrared divergences in quantum electrodynamics arise from the universal
coupling of charged particles to photons of arbitrarily small energy.
In the conventional formulation, individual Fock-space amplitudes are
infrared divergent. Physical observables are obtained only after virtual
soft-photon corrections are combined with real soft-photon emission below
a finite experimental resolution scale.

The standard Bloch–Nordsieck and Kinoshita–Lee–Nauenberg mechanisms
show that the unphysical infrared regulator cancels in inclusive
observables

infrared problem. However, the inclusive observable still depends on the
detector resolution scale. This scale is the maximum energy
\omega_{\max}
of unresolved soft photons

The purpose of this Letter is to emphasize that this surviving scale is
not merely a technical experimental parameter. It specifies which soft
modes are unobserved and therefore effectively traced over. In the
density-matrix formulation,
\omega_{\max}
defines a coarse-graining
scale for the infrared sector. This observation gives a direct
connection between the traditional infrared-safe cross section and the
modern interpretation of soft photons in terms of memory and infrared
quantum information.

II
Infrared cancellation and the surviving resolution scale

Let
\lambda
denote an auxiliary photon mass introduced as an infrared
regulator. Virtual soft-photon corrections contain logarithmic terms of
the form

\delta_{\rm virt}^{\rm IR}=-\frac{2\alpha}{\pi}\ln\frac{{\bf q}^{2}}{m^{2}}\ln\frac{m}{\lambda}+\cdots.

(1)

On the other hand, the real emission of unresolved soft photons with
energy below the detector resolution scale
\omega_{\max}
gives

d\sigma_{\rm soft}=\frac{2\alpha}{\pi}\left[\ln\frac{{\bf q}^{2}}{m^{2}}\ln\frac{\omega_{\max}}{\lambda}+\cdots\right]d\sigma_{\rm el}.

(2)

Thus the infrared-sensitive combination is

\ln\frac{\omega_{\max}}{\lambda}-\ln\frac{m}{\lambda}=\ln\frac{\omega_{\max}}{m}.

(3)

The auxiliary photon mass
\lambda
cancels, while the detector resolution
scale
\omega_{\max}
remains.

Therefore the infrared-safe observable is not independent of infrared
resolution. Rather, it is a quantity defined at a finite resolution,

d\sigma_{\rm incl}=d\sigma_{\rm incl}(\omega_{\max}).

(4)

The conventional statement that infrared divergences cancel should
therefore be understood more precisely: the unphysical regulator
dependence cancels, but the physical resolution dependence survives.

IV
Observable memory

Electromagnetic memory is encoded in the asymptotic soft-photon sector. In principle, the full memory information is contained in the zero-frequency limit of the radiative field. In practice, however, no detector has access to arbitrarily soft photons. Any observation necessarily involves a finite infrared resolution scale. Soft modes below this scale are effectively unobserved and are traced over in the reduced density matrix description.

The overlap factor
(D_{ij}(\omega_{\max}))
therefore measures how much information carried by the soft sector remains accessible at a given resolution. Since the memory effect is encoded in the same soft degrees of freedom, its observable manifestation is necessarily resolution dependent. Observable memory is thus not determined solely by the asymptotic soft sector itself, but also by the coarse-graining scale that separates observed and unobserved infrared modes.

Soft photons are not merely unobserved radiation. They carry asymptotic
information about the scattering process.
From the modern viewpoint, the infrared sector
of gauge theory is closely related to soft theorems,
asymptotic symmetries, and electromagnetic memory

Strominger
.
The preceding discussion suggests that observable infrared memory is not
defined solely by the formal zero-frequency soft sector. It is also
defined relative to the resolution scale that separates observed and
unobserved infrared degrees of freedom. In this sense,

\omega_{\max}

(18)

is a coarse-graining scale for observable memory.

This does not mean that the soft sector becomes a stochastic classical
environment. The full hard-plus-soft state remains coherent. What is
lost in the reduced hard density matrix is not destroyed information,
but information stored in correlations with unresolved soft photons.
Therefore

{\rm Tr}_{\omega<\omega_{\max}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad{\rm reduced\text{-}state\ decoherence},

(19)

but not information loss.
The infrared sector of QED should therefore be viewed as a coherent
information reservoir rather than a stochastic bath. The detector
resolution scale determines how much of this infrared information is
included in the observed hard sector and how much is left in the
unresolved soft sector.

V
Discussion and Conclusions

The usual infrared-safe cross section and the density-matrix
description of soft-photon information are two aspects of the
same underlying structure. The Bloch–Nordsieck cancellation
removes the unphysical photon mass regulator
\lambda
, but
leaves the physical resolution scale
\omega_{\max}
. This
surviving scale defines the boundary between observed and
unresolved soft modes.

Consequently, observable infrared memory should be regarded
as a resolution-dependent quantity. The memory itself is
associated with the coherent soft sector, but its observable
manifestation depends on the coarse graining imposed by the
detector. This provides a direct bridge between the
Berestetskii–Lifshitz–Pitaevskii description
LL
of infrared-safe observables and
the modern interpretation of soft photons as carriers of
infrared memory and quantum information.

The reduced density matrix obtained by tracing over
unresolved soft photons naturally inherits this dependence.
The overlap factor
D_{ij}(\omega_{\max})
therefore
encodes not only infrared decoherence but also the finite
resolution at which the infrared sector is observed.
From this viewpoint, the detector resolution scale

\omega_{\max}
plays the role of a coarse-graining scale
for observable infrared memory.

This interpretation provides a direct connection between
infrared-safe observables, reduced-state decoherence, and
observable memory.
