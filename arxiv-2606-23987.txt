Totally Disconnected Semigroup Compactifications: Non-Introversion of the Full Boolean Algebra of Clopen Sets
Joshua Basman Monterrubio, Thomas Czyzowicz, Ross Stokke, Emily Thevenot

Abstract
In terms of the existence of a single clopen set and two related nets, we characterize when the full Boolean algebra, B ⁡ ( G ) {\mathboondoxfrak B}(G) , of clopen subsets of a topological group G G is left introverted. We employ this characterization to show that when G G is a first countable, σ \sigma -compact, totally disconnected locally compact group, B ⁡ ( G ) {\mathboondoxfrak B}(G) is left introverted if and only if G G is compact or discrete, thus providing a strong positive answer to a question posed in [ 10 ] . Examples of clopen sets and nets witnessing our non-introversion theorem are presented. Some hereditary properties of left introversion of B ⁡ ( G ) {\mathboondoxfrak B}(G) are proved and then employed to extend our main result to other classes of topological groups. MSC codes: Primary 22A10, 22A20, 06E15, 54D35 Key words and phrases: semigroup compactification, totally disconnected locally compact group, Boolean algebra, Stone space, introversion, Arens product

Let

For

x\in B,\ B\in\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(x)\text{ and }\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(x)\in\lambda_{\cal{B}}(B)\text{ are equivalent statements.}

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose that
B
is a clopen subset of a topological group
G
for which

(N
B
):

there are nets
(a_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
in
B
and
(c_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}
in
G
such that
\lim c_{\kappa}=e_{G}
, and for each
\kappa
in

\gamma_{\kappa}
in
\Gamma
such that
c_{\kappa}a_{\gamma}\notin B
for
\gamma\geq\gamma_{\kappa}
.

If
{\cal{B}}
is any
G
-invariant Boolean algebra of clopen sets containing

{\mathcal{V}}
in
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
such that
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
is not open. Thus, any Boolean algebra of clopen sets containing
B
is not left introverted.

Proof.

Let
{\cal{B}}
be a
G
-invariant Boolean algebra of clopen sets that contains
B
. Since
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
is compact, there is a subnet
(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(a_{\gamma_{i}}))_{i\in I}
of
(\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(a_{\gamma}))_{\gamma}
and
{\mathcal{V}}
in
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
such that
\lim_{i}\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(a_{\gamma_{i}})={\mathcal{V}}
in
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}G
. Each
a_{\gamma_{i}}
is in
B
, equivalently each
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(a_{\gamma_{i}})
belongs to the closed set
\lambda_{\cal{B}}(B)
, so
{\mathcal{V}}\in\lambda_{\cal{B}}(B)
. Hence,
B=B\cdot e_{G}\in{\mathcal{V}}
, which means that
e_{G}=\lim_{\kappa}c_{\kappa}\in{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B
. Supposing that

is open, some

{\mathcal{V}}\cdot B

, there is some

in

such that
\nu_{\mathcal{B}}(a_{\gamma_{i}})\in\lambda_{\cal{B}}(B\cdot c_{\kappa})

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
G
be a topological group. Then

{\mathboondoxfrak B}
is
not
left introverted if and only if
there is a clopen subset
B
of
G
with property (N
B
).

Proof.

Supposing that
{\mathboondoxfrak B}
is not left introverted, there is a
{\mathboondoxfrak B}
-ultrafilter
{\mathcal{V}}
and a clopen subset

of
G

{\mathcal{V}}\cdot A
is not clopen; since
G\backslash({\mathcal{V}}\cdot A)={\mathcal{V}}\cdot(G\backslash A)

{\mathcal{V}}\cdot A

e_{G}\in\overline{{\mathcal{V}}\cdot A}\backslash{\mathcal{V}}\cdot A

\lim_{\kappa}c_{\kappa}=e_{G}
and let

. Since

is not in
{\mathcal{V}}\cdot A

does not belong to the
{\mathboondoxfrak B}
-ultrafilter
{\mathcal{V}}
, so

; equivalently,

(a_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}

. Assuming without loss of generality that each

Proposition 2.3
.

If
G
is either a discrete group or a compact group, then
{\mathboondoxfrak B}
is introverted.

Proof.

Trivially,
{\mathboondoxfrak B}
is introverted when

is discrete. For

B\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}

\nu_{\mathboondoxfrak B}(s)\cdot B=s\cdot B,B\cdot\nu_{\mathboondoxfrak B}(s)=B\cdot s\in{\mathboondoxfrak B}

and every
{\mathboondoxfrak B}

is compact. Hence,
{\mathboondoxfrak B}
is introverted when

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
G
be a first countable,
\sigma
-compact tdlc group. Then
{\mathboondoxfrak B}
is left introverted if and only if
G
is discrete or compact.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
is nondiscrete and noncompact. Let
(C_{n})
be a local basis at the identity composed of compact open subgroups of
G
(e.g., see
[
6
, Theorem 7.8]

is not discrete, we can assume that each
C_{n+1}
is properly contained in
C_{n}

C_{1}
, and

is
\sigma

has countable, infinite index in

\{a_{n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
be a complete set of representatives of right cosets of

, without redundancies. Observe that because

\lim_{n}a_{n}=\infty

of
G

a_{n}\notin K
for large enough
n
).
Let

B_{n}:=C_{n}a_{n}\quad\text{ and }\quad B:=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}B_{n},

an open subset of
G
. To see that
B
is also closed, take
y
in
\overline{B}
and a sequence
(y_{k})_{k}
in
B
that converges to
y
. Suppose that for each
N
,
\{y_{k}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
is
not
contained in
\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}B_{n}
. We can then recursively build subsequences
(y_{k_{l}})_{l}
of
(y_{k})_{k}
and
(B_{n_{l}})
of
(B_{n})_{n}
such that
y_{k_{l}}\in B_{n_{l}}=C_{n_{l}}a_{n_{l}}
for each
l
in
{\mathbb{N}}
. But then
y_{k_{l}}a_{n_{l}}^{-1}\in C_{n_{l}}
for each

, so
\lim_{l}y_{k_{l}}a_{n_{l}}^{-1}=e_{G}
; therefore
\lim_{l}a_{n_{l}}=\lim_{l}(y_{k_{l}}a_{n_{l}}^{-1})^{-1}y_{k_{l}}=e_{G}\cdot y=y
, a contradiction because
\lim_{l}a_{n_{l}}=\infty
. We conclude that the sequence
(y_{k})_{k}
is contained in the closed set
\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}B_{n}
for some
N
, so

belongs to
\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}B_{n}
, and therefore belongs to

. Hence,
B
is clopen.

For each
k
, take
c_{k}\in C_{k}\backslash C_{k+1}
, so
\lim c_{k}=e_{G}
. To see that
B
satisfies (N
B
), fix

and suppose that
n\geq k+1
. Since
c_{k}\notin C_{n}
,
c_{k}a_{n}\notin C_{n}a_{n}=B_{n}
. For

,
C_{1}a_{n}
and
C_{1}a_{m}
are disjoint, so
C_{k}a_{n}
and
C_{m}a_{m}=B_{m}
are disjoint; thus,
c_{k}a_{n}\notin B_{m}
. As
c_{k}a_{n}\notin\bigcup_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}B_{m}=B
,

satisfies property (N
B
). By Theorem

,
{\mathboondoxfrak B}
is not left introverted. ∎

The following lemma will be known, but we could not find a reference.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
G
is a noncompact locally compact group, then it contains a noncompact,
\sigma
-compact open subgroup.

Proof.

If
G
has a compact symmetric neighbourhood
U
of the identity such that the
\sigma
-compact open subgroup
H_{U}:=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}U^{n}
is noncompact, then we are done, so assume that any such
H_{U}
is compact. If we have chosen compact neighbourhoods
U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}
of the identity such that
H_{U_{1}}\lneq H_{U_{2}}\lneq\cdots\lneq H_{U_{k}}
, then, since
G
is noncompact, we can choose a compact symmetric set
U_{k+1}
that strictly contains
H_{U_{k}}
; so
H_{U_{k+1}}\gneq H_{U_{k}}
. We obtain a strictly increasing sequence of compact open subgroups of
G
,
(H_{U_{n}})_{n}
, and conclude that the
\sigma
-compact open subgroup
H:=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}H_{U_{n}}
, which is covered by the open subgroups

, is noncompact.
∎

H
to be a noncompact,
\sigma
-compact open subgroup of
G
(via Lemma
2.7
); if
G
satisfies condition (i), let
H=G
. By Proposition
2.6
, it suffices to show that
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(H)
is not left introverted. Using the Kakutani-Kodaira Theorem
[
6
, Theorem 8.7]
, take
N
to be a compact normal subgroup of
H
such that
H/N
is metrizable; in the case that
G
satisfies condition (iii), assume as well that
N
is contained in
\bigcap U_{n}
. In any case,
N
is not open, so
H/N
is a first countable,
\sigma
-compact, noncompact, nondiscrete tdlc group, so
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(H/N)
is not left introverted by Theorem
2.4
. By Proposition
2.6
,
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(H)
is not left introverted, as needed.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

1. If
G
is a
\sigma
-compact locally compact group, then there is a sequence of neighbourhoods
(U_{n})
of the identity such that
\bigcap U_{n}=\{e_{G}\}
(which has empty interior if
G
is nondiscrete) if and only if
G
is metrizable.

2. Let
G
be a locally compact group with connected component of the identity,
G_{e}
. If
G/G_{e}
satisfies one of the three conditions stated in Proposition
2.8
, then
{\mathboondoxfrak B}
is not left introverted by Proposition
2.6
.

Remark 2.10
.

The theory of semigroup compactifications is commonly developed in the setting of semitopological semigroups
[
1
]
. The main concepts from
[
10
]
extend to this context as follows:

Let
S
be a semitopological semigroup. For a subset
B
of
S
and
s\in S
, let

B\cdot s=\ell_{s}^{-1}(B)
and
s\cdot B=r_{s}^{-1}(B)
, where
\ell_{s},r_{s}:S\rightarrow S:t\mapsto st,ts
.

The definitions of left (right) introversion of a
S
-invariant Boolean algebra
{\cal{B}}
of clopen subsets of
S
, and left (right) Arens product on
\nu_{\cal{B}}S
, are now defined exactly as before, and it is not difficult to check that Lemma 3.2 and Theorem 3.3 of
[
10
]
remain true, following nearly verbatim proofs; one only needs to note that “homomorphism” should replace “isomorphism” in the statement of Lemma 3.2(i) and a few modest notational changes are required in the proof of Theorem 3.3(ii). Notice as well that if
S
, a semitopological semigroup with at least one left identity
e
, replaces
G
in Proposition
2.1
, then the proposition still holds, with the same proof. Using this generalized version of Proposition
2.1
, we conclude with another example.

Consider the Sorgenfrey line,
\mathbb{R}_{l}
, a zero dimensional, first countable, non-locally compact, non-
\sigma
-compact Hausdorff space. Observe that
(\mathbb{R}_{l},+)
is a topological semigroup (addition is jointly continuous on
\mathbb{R}_{l}
), but not a topological group (e.g.,
\lim 1/n=0
but
\lim(-1/n)\neq 0
in
\mathbb{R}_{l}
). To see that
{\mathboondoxfrak B}(\mathbb{R}_{l})
is not left introverted, let
B=\bigcup_{n\geq 2}[n,n+1/n)
, a clopen subset of
\mathbb{R}_{l}
, and let
a_{n}=n
,
c_{k}=1/k
. Then
(a_{n})_{n\geq 2}
is contained in
B
,
\lim c_{k}=0
, but
c_{k}+a_{n}\notin B
for
n\geq k
, so condition (N
B
) is satisfied.
