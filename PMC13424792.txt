Open Gluteus Reconstruction with Inlay Enthesis Allograft and Onlay Acellular Dermal Allograft

Here, we present the technique and utilization of open gluteal reconstruction with allograft augmentation utilizing an inlay biphasic cancellous enthesis allograft and an onlay acellular dermal allograft.

Background

The gluteus medius and minimus share many similarities regarding origin, insertion, and function. Both originate along the gluteal surface of the ilium and insert on the greater trochanter. The gluteus medius acts as the primary hip abductor and receives assistance from the gluteus minimus.5,12

Tears are more prevalent among the middle-aged population, particularly those aged ≥50 years. Women are far more likely to experience gluteal tears compared with men. These tears are typically secondary to chronic degeneration of the tendon, ultimately leading to a tear.7,8,14

Entheses are the tendon-to-bone interfaces and exhibit intricate histologic architecture. The transition from tendon-to-bone histology enables the junction to offload significant amounts of mechanical stress.4,11,13 When repairing full-thickness tears with tendon-to-bone anchor repairs, this cellular organization is disrupted and improperly recreated, which does not recapitulate the histologic make-up of the tendon-to-bone interface. 13 The biphasic enthesis allograft provides a more natural scaffold to foster proper cellular organization within this junction and increases attachment strength.

Specifically, the inlay allograft is double-sided, consisting of the cancellous and corticocancellous sides. The corticocancellous side simulates the natural architecture of the enthesis attachments, and the cortical side provides a scaffold for the bony matrix. 3 This arrangement of cellular biology resembles native biology and improves tendinous healing. As for the acellular human dermal allograft, this graft provides a biological scaffold with less immunological reactivity that fosters cellular growth without the damaging reactivity of synthetic implants.1,2 This graft can be used as an independent adjunct or in tandem for further support.

Open gluteus medius/minimus reconstruction with inlay biphasic enthesis allograft and onlay acellular dermal allograft is indicated for patients who experience a chronic, retracted, massive gluteal tear with atrophy of the muscle belly and tendons. Makridis et al 9 found that muscle atrophy may be a negative prognostic finding. This combined use of an inlay enthesis graft optimizes the repair interface between the tendon and bone, while the onlay acellular dermal allograft provides an immediate scaffold for strength and support of the muscle-tendon tissue.2,10

Indications

Our case presentation is of a 75-year-old female who presents with a 1-year history of right hip pain. She does not note a specific hip injury. Now, her pain is dull and exacerbated by walking, standing, or sleeping on the affected side. She notes weakness but denies any numbness, tingling, or low back pain. She has failed conservative treatment at this point with anti-inflammatories and a greater trochanteric bursal injection.

On physical examination, she walks with an antalgic gait and has an intact range of motion. She has a positive flexion, abduction, and external rotation (FABER) and flexion, adduction, and internal rotation (FADIR) test, with a painful arc from 1 to 3 o'clock. She reports tenderness to palpation over the greater trochanter, and she has some diminished strength with hip abduction.

On preoperative radiographs, anteroposterior pelvis and Dunn lateral views were obtained, which showed a slightly narrowed joint space, consistent with a Tönnis grade 1, and no evidence of any fractures or abnormalities. On the magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the left hip, a full-thickness gluteus medius tear with retraction is seen along with moderate fat atrophy. Because of the full-thickness tear and nature of the tendons, open gluteal reconstruction with inlay biphasic enthesis allograft and onlay acellular dermal allograft was recommended to optimize healing.

Technique Description

The patient is secured in the lateral decubitus position on the operating room table. A lateral skin incision is made directly over the greater trochanter. After the subcutaneous tissue is sharply dissected, the iliotibial band fascia is divided along its fibers with a scalpel, exposing the peritrochanteric space. Upon identification of the gluteal tendons, a trochanteric bursectomy is then performed with a rongeur. After hemostasis, a traction suture is placed in a figure-of-8 fashion into the gluteus medius tendon.

The gluteal footprint for the gluteal tendon insertion sites on the greater trochanter is then identified and decorticated with a Cobb and a bur to aid in the regenerative response after the repair. The tendon suture construct is then passed through the gluteus medius tendon in a posterior-to-anterior fashion with 6 mattress sutures. The onlay graft will be incorporated within this construct after the placement of the inlay graft.

The onlay allograft is measured to appropriate dimensions for fixation to the torn tendon.

Implantation of 2 triple-loaded anchors is then prepared and placed at the gluteus medius and minimus insertion sites, and the superoposterior and lateral facet of the greater trochanter.

The enthesis graft is prepared and soaked in normal saline on the back table. Soaking time should surpass 15 minutes before implantation. Adequate soaking time is paramount to allow for increased pliability of the graft, as proper graft fixation depends on the compression created under the tendon repair construct after suture fixation. Furthermore, during the preparation phase, ensure that the previously created excavation zone on the gluteal tendon footprint is of adequate depth for the inlay insertion. An excavated zone with a target depth of a few millimeters is appropriate. The depth of the graft zone should match the thickness of the biphasic enthesis allograft to facilitate the repair.

The enthesis allograft is pressed fit into the slightly excavated zone of the gluteal footprint with the cortical side facing down and the cancellous side facing up. The cancellous side will have a corresponding black marking, indicating the superficial facing side of the graft. A spinal needle is then placed, here, into the enthesis graft for visualization throughout the procedure. The tendon will be pulled over the graft, and compression will hold the biphasic enthesis graft construct in place until the dermal allograft is introduced and fixed to the tendon superficially and then tied down.

Attention is then turned to the placement of the onlay human dermal allograft. It is fixated in place with Vicryl sutures placed both anteriorly and posteriorly. Sutures from the first anchor are passed through both the tendon and allograft in mattress fashion. This will be done 6 times for each anchor, for a total of 6 mattress suture constructs. The mattress configuration of the proximal row is then tied in sequential order through both the tendon and allograft. One suture limb from each mattress is then loaded into a knotless anchor. Therefore, each anchor will receive a total of 6 suture limbs. This process will be completed twice to form the distal row. One anchor will be placed anteriorly and the other posteriorly. After removing the axillary anchor sutures, the final allograft-tendon complex will be visualized.

Technical Pearls

When preparing the biphasic cancellous graft, ensure about 15 minutes of soaking in saline or bodily fluid, as this increases the flexibility of the graft and enables a cleaner fixation because of the increased compressibility. Additionally, sufficient gluteal footprint preparation should be prioritized to promote regenerative processes at the footprint. It is important to be mindful of the spacing between the medial and lateral row anchors so that maximum footprint coverage can be attained. Finally, ensure the proper orientation of the enthesis graft before placement, with the marked side of the graft pointing superficially. 6

4-Phase Rehabilitation Protocol

With regard to the rehabilitation protocol, physical therapy generally started on day 1 after the operation. The patient's range of motion is typically restricted with a fitted hip brace for 6 weeks. They are restricted to 20 lbs of weightbearing and are provided with a continuous passive motion device until 90º of hip flexion is achieved. During this phase, the patient's hip brace limits hip abduction and adduction to neutral. Between 6 and 12 weeks, they may begin progression to full weightbearing status with a transition out of their hip brace. They are also beginning strengthening exercises in physical therapy. Between 12 and 24 weeks, they are allowed to ambulate without assistive devices and return to previous activity as tolerated. After 24 weeks, active individuals are allowed to initiate running programs and sports-specific drills. They may discontinue physical therapy if necessary and return to activities by 4 to 6 months.

Discussion/Conclusion

One-year outcomes have been promising after gluteal reconstruction with human dermal allograft. This study included revision cases after retears in which the index gluteal repair was performed endoscopically. As seen here, the use of an allograft alongside gluteal repair showed satisfactory patient-reported outcomes and patient-acceptable symptomatic state. Additionally, no cases or retears were reported 1 year after surgery. 2 Additional research on enthesis grafts is needed in the hip, as most of the current literature is pertinent to rotator cuff repairs, although outcomes are promising.
