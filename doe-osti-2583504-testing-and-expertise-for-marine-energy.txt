Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

This TEAMER project allows NREL to use and augment a custom special-purpose testing
system to perform accelerated-life testing of rotating seals for use in the Marine Renewable
Energy (“MRE”) industry. The requirements of this industry are different from most applications
including marine, and relevant performance information is lacking. In order to achieve low costs
with extremely effective and reliable operation over long periods deployed underwater, more
study of the best seals and arrangements of seals must be performed. By developing a rigorous
framework for such testing, NREL hopes to greatly improve understanding of the relevant
factors and provide significant data to the industry. Further, this work can inform standards
developments for this critical component of MRE systems, leading to maturation and improved
acceptance of the industry in commerce.

Summary of Research Results:

Task 1: The first 2 months of this project (starting after execution of this contract) will be
dedicated to setting up the Participant test equipment, preparing the required Safe Work Permits
and instrumenting and setting up the data acquisition system. This will include:

 • Receive and check out Participant test equipment
 • Have NREL safety department inspect equipment, get it approved for operation including
 electrical safety check out and safe work permitting
 • Make any necessary adjustments to ensure we can eventually get to unattended operation.
 This includes setting sensors with interlocks such as temperature.
 • Perform attended testing initially to validate safety protocols and gain experience with
 equipment. The ultimate goal is to go to unattended testing where we can check it twice
 per day to log hours, however, an initial attended period is necessary.
 • Selection of other seal types and procurement of additional equipment/test articles.

Task 2: The third month will be dedicated to normal operation of the Participant test equipment
as outlined in the Test Report provided by Participant. This time will be used for NREL to gain
an understanding into the requirements and limitations of the test setup, and to identify ways in
which the process can be improved. In this time, we will run and collect data using the same
configuration as Participant did to enable NREL to compare data and ensure normal operation.

Task 3: Month 4 will be used for NREL and Participant to discuss possible modifications to be
made to the Participant test equipment that were identified during normal operation in Task 2
and implement any NREL/Participant agreed upon modifications to the Participant test
equipment. NREL will not make any modifications to the Participant test equipment unless
approved by Verdant.
