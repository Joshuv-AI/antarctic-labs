Poster 245. Improving Rotator Cuff Repair With Tissue Engineering and Biotherapeutics
Authors: John M Tokish, Jose Iturregui, Cara Lai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:To utilize biomechanical, genomic and histological analysis to evaluate the efficacy of rotator cuff healing using a bioengineered patch that mimics the tendon to bone enthesis.Methods:We assessed the biologic and mechanical effect of rotator cuff repair augmentation with the following interventions: (a) an electrospun biomimetic patch made of biodegradable, FDA-approved polycaprolactone (PCL), (b) a sheet of FDA-approved amnion matrix, a resilient membrane that lines the placenta's inner cavity, rich in extracellular matrix proteins, growth factors, and cytokines, (c) an amnion combination patch (i.e., a patch combining a growth factor rich, dehydrated amnion matrix between multilayered synthetic biodegradable scaffolding) and tested these against a sham repair (control) and the traditional rotator cuff repair. We used a well-established tendon detachment and repair Sprague-Dawley rat model. Forty-three male Sprague-Dawley rats weighing 350-600g were divided into four groups with the following interventions: traditional repair, biomimetic patch repair, amnion patch repair, and combination patch repair. A sham repair was conducted on the contralateral side of each rat. We assessed the repairs with mechanical testing, histological evaluation of cellular infiltrates, and gene expression characterization of progenitor cells and differentiated cells after wound healing. All assessments were conducted during terminal study at eight weeks post-surgery. We evaluated repairs with histological testing, gene expression evaluation, and mechanical testing. Histological evaluation was performed using Ide's (2009) modified tendon maturity scoring system with hematoxylin-eosin. We conducted fibrosis scoring with Masson's Trichrome staining. Gene expression was evaluated with a custom rat RT2 Profiler array (Qiagen), and uniaxial tensile loading was conducted with an MTS Exceed benchtop load test frame. Cross-sectional area was measured with three-dimensional non-contact scanning: measurements were averaged across the irregular surface of each tendon to calculate stress and strain.Results:Biomechanical testing showed higher average ultimate failure load in the novel repair groups than in the traditional repair group. Yield load in the electrospun (mean = 4.3, SD=2.3), amnion (mean = 4.5, SD=3.1), and combination patch (mean = 4.8, SD= 2.2) groups were higher than that in the traditional repair (mean = 4.1, SD=1.9). Histological evaluation with the Ide tendon maturity scoring system demonstrated that the combination patch (mean = 18, SD= 2.0) performed comparably to the traditional repair (mean = 19.3, SD= 3.2). However, the combination patch and electrospun patch exhibited an exaggerated immune response (Multinucleated giant cells, Langhan's Type) and fibrotic growth that warrant further investigation. Gene expression demonstrates that all electrospun products induce greater immune response and presence of mesenchymal stem cells than all other treatment categories.Conclusions:Regenerative tendon repair augmentation methods (biomimetic electrospun patch, amnion, and combination patch) provide a biomechanical advantage over traditional repair and while the combination patch induces a strong immune and fibrotic response; the cellular and mechanical advantages of the combination patch warrant further study.
