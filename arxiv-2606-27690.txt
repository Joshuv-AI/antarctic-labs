Asymptotic stability for monotone traveling kinks of the dissipative Boussinesq problem
Atanas Stefanov

1.1.
Traveling kinks for the viscous Boussinesq equation

Assuming traveling wave ansatz with speed

u(t,x)=\phi(x-\sigma t),\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\phi(x)=\phi_{\pm}

(1.4)

B\phi^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}+\sigma\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}+(\sigma^{2}-1)\phi^{\prime\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(\phi^{2})^{\prime\prime}

We focus on heteroclinic solutions

2
2

2

Here, and thereafter, we do not distinguish between fronts and backs, depending on

B\phi^{\prime\prime}+\sigma\phi^{\prime}+(\sigma^{2}-1)\phi=\frac{1}{2}(\phi^{2}+k).

\frac{k}{2}=(\sigma^{2}-1)\phi_{+}-\frac{1}{2}\phi_{+}^{2}=(\sigma^{2}-1)\phi_{-}-\frac{1}{2}\phi_{-}^{2}

Excluding

\sigma^{2}-1=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\phi_{+}^{2}-\phi_{-}^{2}}{\phi_{+}-\phi_{-}}=\frac{1}{2}(\phi_{+}+\phi_{-})

This shows that a necessary condition relating the heteroclinic values

Preliminaries

This section establishes notation and preliminary results needed for the sequel. First, the standard spatial Lebesgue spaces are denoted by

L^{p}(\mathbf{R})
, while the Sobolev space
H^{1}(\mathbf{R})
also makes an appearance. Specifically, we record their respective norms
5
5

5

Hereafter, we use the notation
\|f\|
to denote
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R})}
.

\|f\|_{p}=\left(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|f(x)|^{p}dx\right),\|f\|_{H^{1}}=\|f^{\prime}\|+\|f\|,

One can also define
H^{k}(\mathbf{R})=\{f:\|f\|_{H^{k}}=\|f^{(k)}\|+\|f\|\}
and
H^{\infty}(\mathbf{R})=\cap_{k=1}^{\infty}H^{k}(\mathbf{R})

We define the Fourier transform and its inverse by

\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\xi}dx,f(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\hat{f}(\xi)e^{2\pi ix\xi}d\xi.

We frequently employ the Sobolev embedding estimate and its refinement, the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}},2\leq p\leq\infty

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|u^{\prime}\|^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}},2\leq p\leq\infty.

In particular,
H^{1}(\mathbf{R})
is an algebra, since
\|u^{2}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbf{R})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}
.

2.1.
Some reductions for the viscous Boussinesq

We now derive a solution formula for the forced viscous Boussinesq problem. Our goal is to establish a local well-posedness result for the nonlinear evolution problem (
1.3
), subject to the boundary conditions as in (
1.14
). To this end, consider kinks
\phi
, solving (
1.4
), and the decomposition
u(t,x)=\phi(x-\sigma t)+w(t,x)
. Then,
w
solves

(2.3)

w_{tt}-w_{txx}+Bw_{xxxx}-w_{xx}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}(2\phi(\cdot-\sigma t)w+w^{2}).

We supplement (
2.3
) with initial data
w_{0}(x)=w(0,x)\in H^{1}(\mathbf{R}),w_{1}(x)=w_{t}(0,x)\in L^{2}(\mathbf{R})
.

Based on this formulation, we consider the following forced linear viscous Boussinesq problem:

(2.4)

\left\{\begin{matrix}q_{tt}-q_{txx}+Bq_{xxxx}-q_{xx}=\partial_{x}^{2}(N(t,x)),\\
q(0,x)\in H^{1}(\mathbf{R}),q_{t}(0,x)\in L^{2}(\mathbf{R}).\end{matrix}\right.

Note that the initial data for (2.4) are now localized, with the nonlinearity appearing under a second derivative—the natural formulation arising from the nonlinear problem
(
2.3
). The next step is to construct the Green’s function.

2.2.
Green’s function for the free viscous Boussinesq problem

Applying the spatial Fourier transform to (
2.4
) with zero right-hand side yields the characteristic equation

\lambda^{2}+4\pi^{2}\xi^{2}\lambda+(16\pi^{4}\xi^{4}B+4\pi^{2}\xi^{2})=0,

which has the solutions

\lambda_{\pm}(\xi)=-2\pi^{2}\xi^{2}\pm\sqrt{4\pi^{4}\xi^{4}(1-4B)-4\pi^{2}\xi^{2}}.

Note that
\lambda_{\pm}(\xi)
is always complex valued for small
|\xi|
, however, under the assumption
B>0
, there is

(2.5)

\Re\lambda_{-}<\Re\lambda_{+}<-c_{0}\xi^{2},

indicating a parabolic behavior. An elementary calculation shows that the free solution of (
2.4
) can be written in the form

\hat{q}(t,\xi)=\frac{\lambda_{-}(\xi)e^{t\lambda_{+}(\xi)}-\lambda_{+}(\xi)e^{t\lambda_{-}(\xi)}}{\lambda_{-}(\xi)-\lambda_{+}(\xi)}\hat{q}(0,\xi)+\frac{e^{t\lambda_{+}(\xi)}-e^{t\lambda_{-}(\xi)}}{\lambda_{+}(\xi)-\lambda_{-}(\xi)}\hat{q}_{t}(0,\xi),

where the second formula makes sense even for values
\xi:\lambda_{+}(\xi)=\lambda_{-}(\xi)
.

A simple application of the Duhamel’s formula yields the solution to the full problem (
2.4
) as follows

(2.6)

\displaystyle\hat{q}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{-}(\xi)e^{t\lambda_{+}(\xi)}-\lambda_{+}(\xi)e^{t\lambda_{-}(\xi)}}{\lambda_{-}(\xi)-\lambda_{+}(\xi)}\hat{q}(0,\xi)+\frac{e^{t\lambda_{+}(\xi)}-e^{t\lambda_{-}(\xi)}}{\lambda_{+}(\xi)-\lambda_{-}(\xi)}\hat{q}_{t}(0,\xi)

\displaystyle-4\pi^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\hat{G}(t-s,\xi)[\xi^{2}\hat{N}(s,\xi)]ds,

where

\hat{G}(t,\xi)=\frac{e^{t\lambda_{+}(\xi)}-e^{t\lambda_{-}(\xi)}}{\lambda_{+}(\xi)-\lambda_{-}(\xi)}.

We have the following Lemma.

Lemma 1
.

There exists constants
C,c_{0}
, so that the Green function

satisfies

(2.7)

\displaystyle|\hat{G}(t,\xi)|\leq C(1+\xi^{2})^{-1},

(2.8)

\displaystyle|\hat{G}(t,\xi)|\leq Ce^{-c_{0}t|\xi|^{2}}

Proof.

For large values of
|\xi|
, we have
|4\pi^{4}\xi^{4}(1-4B)-4\pi^{2}\xi^{2}|\sim\xi^{4}
, which means that
|\lambda_{+}(\xi)-\lambda_{-}(\xi)|\sim\xi^{2}
. So, for
|\xi|>>1
,

|\hat{G}(t,\xi)|=\frac{|e^{t\lambda_{-}(\xi)}-e^{t\lambda_{+}(\xi)}|}{|\lambda_{+}(\xi)-\lambda_{-}(\xi)|}\leq C\xi^{-2}.

where we have taken advantage of the elementary inequality

|e^{t\lambda_{\pm}(\xi)}|=e^{t\Re\lambda_{\pm}(\xi)}\leq 1
. For values of
\xi
ranging in a compact set

|\hat{G}(t,\xi)|=e^{t\Re\lambda_{+}(\xi)}\frac{|e^{t\lambda_{-}(\xi)-\lambda_{+}(\xi)}-1|}{|\lambda_{+}(\xi)-\lambda_{-}(\xi)|}\leq C.

Either way, we obtain (
2.7
). The bound (
2.8
), which in view of (

, follows from (
2.5
).

\displaystyle\|(1+|\xi|)\frac{\lambda_{-}(\xi)e^{t\lambda_{+}(\xi)}-\lambda_{+}(\xi)e^{t\lambda_{-}(\xi)}}{\lambda_{-}(\xi)-\lambda_{+}(\xi)}\hat{w}_{0}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(1+|\xi|)\hat{w}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|w_{0}\|_{H^{1}},
