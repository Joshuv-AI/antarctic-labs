Novel protein biomarkers for risk stratification and personalized medicine
Authors: Erik Moedt, Wenjun Ju, Viji Nair, Petter Bjornstad, Matthias Kretzler, Hiddo J L Heerspink
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and hypothesisAlbuminuria is widely used to monitor chronic kidney disease (CKD) and treatment response but is subject to variability and primarily reflects glomerular injury. Urinary clusterin and epidermal growth factor (uEGF) are associated with tubular injury and repair, respectively, and may serve as mechanism-informed pharmacodynamic biomarkers. We investigated responses of urinary clusterin and uEGF to endothelin receptor antagonists (ERAs) and sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i), respectively, in CKD.MethodsWe evaluated changes in urinary clusterin-to-creatinine ratio (uCLU/Cr) and uEGF-to-creatinine ratio (uEGF/Cr) in two randomized CKD trial populations. Effects of ERA on uCLU/Cr and endothelin-1 (ET-1) were assessed during the atrasentan enrichment phase of SONAR (Study of Diabetic Nephropathy with Atrasentan), and effects of SGLT2i on uEGF/Cr were examined in DAPA-CKD (Dapagliflozin and Prevention of Adverse Outcomes in Chronic Kidney Disease) across different CKD etiologies, diabetes status, and glycemic control. Associations between uEGF/Cr and intrarenal EGF messenger RNA (mRNA) expression were examined using kidney biopsy single-cell RNA sequencing data.ResultsAfter 6 weeks of treatment with atrasentan, uCLU/Cr decreased by 46.3% (95% confidence interval [CI]: −57.8 to −37.1), while serum ET-1 increased by 23.4% (95% CI 19.2–27.4). There were no effects on serum clusterin and only a numerical decrease in urinary ET-1. Baseline uCLU/Cr correlated significantly with urinary ET-1 (Pearson r = 0.65, P < .0001). In DAPA-CKD, dapagliflozin attenuated the decline in uEGF/Cr over 1 year, with heterogeneity across CKD etiologies. Relative increases were greatest in diabetic kidney disease and absent in glomerulonephritis and hypertensive nephropathy. Similarly, effects were larger in those with type 2 diabetes and higher baseline HbA1c. Tubular EGF mRNA expression correlated positively with uEGF/Cr.ConclusionsUrinary clusterin and uEGF are mechanism-informed pharmacodynamic biomarkers reflecting distinct intrarenal pathways engaged by ERA and SGLT2i therapy, respectively. These biomarkers complement albuminuria by capturing tubular injury and repair, providing a more comprehensive biological assessment of treatment effects and heterogeneity in CKD.

INTRODUCTION

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) remains a major global health burden and is characterized by substantial heterogeneity in disease trajectory and treatment response [1,2]. This heterogeneity partly reflects the diverse etiologies underlying CKD, including diabetic kidney disease (DKD), glomerulonephritis (GN), and hypertensive nephropathy (HTN), which together account for the majority of cases of kidney failure around the world [3,4]. The progression of CKD can be attenuated by effective pharmacological therapies. Available treatment options have expanded considerably over the past decade. Large outcome trials have demonstrated that sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) reduce the risk of kidney disease progression across a broad population of patients with varying CKD etiologies [5,6]. Selective endothelin receptor antagonists (ERAs) have also been shown to confer kidney protection in patients with CKD, with robust evidence in those with type 2 diabetes and in people with IgA nephropathy [7–10]. Despite these therapeutic advances, substantial heterogeneity in both CKD progression and treatment response persists, underscoring the need for biomarkers that can better stratify individual patients and predict who will benefit most from these novel therapies.

Urinary proteins are used in clinical practice to diagnose and monitor CKD progression. They are also used as pharmacodynamic biomarkers to monitor efficacy of kidney protective therapies since many interventions shown to reduce the progression of CKD also reduce urinary proteins. Although urinary protein measures, including the urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) and urinary protein-to-creatinine ratio (UPCR), are clinically meaningful, both are subject to considerable biological and analytical variability [11,12]. Moreover, they primarily reflect glomerular permeability and capture only a limited dimension of kidney health, leaving key pathophysiological processes such as tubular injury and repair largely unaccounted for [13].

Urinary epidermal growth factor (uEGF) has emerged as a promising biomarker of tubular health and regenerative capacity [14,15]. EGF is predominantly produced by epithelial cells of the thick ascending limb of Henle and the distal convoluted tubule and signals through the epidermal growth factor receptor, which is widely expressed throughout the kidney [16,17]. Reduced urinary excretion of EGF has consistently been associated with tubulointerstitial damage, fibrosis, and accelerated kidney function decline in patients with type 2 diabetes, findings that have subsequently been confirmed in CKD populations without diabetes [13,18–22]. Because the degree and nature of impaired tubular health differ across CKD etiologies, treatment-induced changes in uEGF may vary according to underlying disease. Notably, uEGF reflects intrarenal production rather than glomerular filtration, supporting its specificity for tubular integrity. Experimental studies and human kidney biopsy data demonstrate that SGLT2 inhibition is associated with increased tubular EGF expression and activation of downstream regenerative pathways [17,18]. In clinical trial populations, SGLT2i attenuates the decline in uEGF over time, and early increases in uEGF are independently associated with a lower risk of adverse kidney outcomes [18,22].

Clusterin is a multifunctional glycoprotein involved in tissue remodeling, apoptosis, oxidative stress responses, and protein homeostasis [23]. In the kidney, clusterin expression is low under physiological conditions but is strongly upregulated in response to tubular injury [23–25]. Urinary clusterin has been identified as one of the earliest markers of proximal tubular damage, alongside kidney injury molecule-1, reflecting injury within the tubulointerstitial compartment rather than glomerular pathology [23,24]. Elevated urinary clusterin levels have been associated with worse kidney function and an increased risk of CKD progression [24,26]. Emerging evidence suggests that urinary clusterin reflects activation of the endothelin system. In the SONAR (Study of Diabetic Nephropathy with Atrasentan) trial, treatment with the selective ERA atrasentan led to rapid and pronounced reductions in urinary clusterin, and early treatment-induced decreases were strongly associated with improved long-term kidney outcomes [26].

We here investigate the pharmacodynamic responses of uEGF to SGLT2i and clusterin to ERA therapy, evaluating their potential utility as pharmacodynamic biomarkers that can be used as an adjunct to UACR or UPCR. To further elucidate the mechanistic link between clusterin and endothelin system activation, we examined associations between endothelin signaling and both urinary and systemic clusterin, as well as the effects of atrasentan on these biomarkers, in participants from the SONAR trial. In participants from the DAPA-CKD (Dapagliflozin and Prevention of Adverse Outcomes in Chronic Kidney Disease) trial, we evaluated the pharmacodynamic response of uEGF to dapagliflozin across different CKD etiologies. In addition, we evaluated whether uEGF levels reflect intrarenal EGF expression using pseudobulk single-cell RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) from kidney biopsies of people on standard care with and without SGLT2i treatment. By characterizing these biomarker responses, we aimed to clarify their biological relevance and explore their potential role as pharmacodynamic tools for monitoring therapeutic response and enabling early efficacy assessment in clinical trials.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Patients and study design

We included participants from the SONAR and the DAPA-CKD trials. SONAR assessed the safety and efficacy of atrasentan in patients with nephropathy and type 2 diabetes. DAPA-CKD assessed the safety and efficacy of dapagliflozin in participants with CKD with and without type 2 diabetes. Both trials were international, randomized, and double-blinded, and detailed study designs and eligibility criteria have been published previously [5,8].

In brief, SONAR enrolled 5117 participants with type 2 diabetes, eGFR ≥ 25 and <75 ml/min/1.73 m2, and UACR ≥300 and ≤5000 mg/g. After a screening and run-in period, participants entered an enrichment phase, during which all received atrasentan 0.75 mg once daily for 6 weeks. Subsequently, participants were randomized in a double-blind manner to continued atrasentan or placebo. The median follow-up was 2.2 years and the primary endpoint was a composite of sustained doubling of serum creatinine, kidney failure, or kidney-related death.

DAPA-CKD enrolled 4304 participants with eGFR ≥ 25 and <75 ml/min/1.73 m2 and UACR ≥ 200 and ≤5000 mg/g, who were randomized to once-daily treatment with dapagliflozin 10 mg or matched placebo, and followed for a median of 2.4 years. Predefined CKD etiologies included DKD, GN, HTN, and a miscellaneous category comprising chronic pyelonephritis, renal artery stenosis, chronic interstitial nephritis, obstructive nephropathy, and other or unknown causes.

Written informed consent was obtained from all participants in both trials, and the study protocols received approval from local ethics committees. The trials were performed in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines and were registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (SONAR: NCT01858532 and DAPA-CKD: NCT03036150).

Biomarker measurements

Clusterin was quantified in urine and serum samples from the SONAR trial with an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) using the Human Clusterin SimpleStep ELISA Kit (Abcam, Waltham, MA, USA). Plates were analyzed using the Biotek Synergy HT ELISA microplate reader with GEN5 software (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Quality control samples with low, medium, and high concentrations were included on each ELISA plate. For urinary clusterin, the interassay coefficients of variation (CVs) were 3.4%, 1.9%, and 2.3% for low, medium, and high quality controls, respectively. For serum clusterin, the corresponding CVs were 12.7%, 8.2%, and 8.3%.

Endothelin-1 (ET-1) was quantified in urine and serum using the Ella Automated Immunoassay System (Proteinsimple, San Jose, CA, USA). Quality control samples with low and high concentrations were included in each cartridge. For urinary ET-1 (uET-1), the interassay CVs for low and high quality controls were 5.5% and 2.9%, respectively. For serum ET-1 (sET-1), the corresponding CVs were 2.3% and 4.4%.

EGF was quantified in urine samples from the DAPA-CKD trial using the Mesoscale QuickPlex SQ 120 platform (Meso Scale Diagnostics, Rockville, MD, USA), a high-performance electrochemiluminescence immunoassay. DAPA-CKD urine samples were assessed between March and May 2025. Pooled samples were included on each plate, with a corresponding average interassay CV of 16.4%. Among 5075 samples, 451 were measured in duplicate, yielding an average CV of 2.6%.

For all biomarker measurements, baseline and follow-up samples from each participant were analyzed on the same assay plate to minimize interplate variability.

Statistical analyses

Continuous variables with approximately normal distributions are summarized as means with standard deviations. Skewed variables, including UACR, clusterin, ET-1, and uEGF, are presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs). Categorical variables are reported as frequencies and percentages. Urinary biomarker concentrations were normalized to urine creatinine.

To evaluate the short-term treatment effect of atrasentan, geometric means of biomarker concentrations were calculated at baseline (week 0) and after 6 weeks of treatment. Percentage changes from baseline were estimated using difference in log-transformed values and expressed as median percentage change. Within-participant changes between week 0 and week 6 were assessed using paired t-tests applied to log-transformed values. Associations between ET-1 and clusterin were assessed using Pearson correlation coefficients based on log-transformed biomarker concentrations.

Baseline uEGF-to-creatinine ratio (uEGF/Cr) was summarized by CKD etiology using geometric means, and differences across etiologies were assessed on log-transformed uEGF/Cr values using linear models with analysis of variance. Placebo-corrected effects of dapagliflozin on the year-1 change in uEGF/Cr were evaluated using analysis of covariance including randomized treatment and log-transformed baseline uEGF/Cr as covariates. Effect modification was assessed using interaction terms for CKD etiology and type 2 diabetes status, and for baseline HbA1c as a continuous variable.

Results with P < .05 were considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted using R version 4.4.2 (R Project for Statistical Computing, http://www.r-project.org).

Validation of uEGF/Cr as a marker of tubular EGF expression

To examine the correlation between uEGF/Cr and kidney EGF mRNA expression, we performed pseudobulk single-cell RNA-seq on kidney biopsies. A detailed description of this cohort and the methods has been published previously [18]. Briefly, study participants were adolescents and young adults from the type 2 diabetes and metabolic bariatric surgery clinics at the Children’s Hospital Colorado at the Anschutz Medical Campus in Aurora, Colorado. This analysis included 16 participants (12–21 years of age, type 2 diabetes onset at <18 years of age, diabetes duration 1–10 years, and HbA1c < 11%) who underwent optional research kidney biopsies, 10 of whom were treated with an SGLT2i. Single-cell RNA-seq was performed on kidney samples from 10 patients treated with an SGLT2i (9 with canagliflozin, 1 with empagliflozin) and 6 patients receiving standard care. Details of the analysis across kidney cell types have been previously published. To investigate the association between EGF RNA expression and SGLT2i treatment, we performed pseudobulk analysis on thick ascending limb and distal convoluted tubule cells expressing EGF mRNA, aggregating single-cell expression by sample at the cell-type level to approximate bulk RNA-seq profiles [27,28]. EGF mRNA expression was then correlated with uEGF measured by ELISA in the same participants using Pearson correlation.

Effect of atrasentan on clusterin and endothelin-1

Six weeks of treatment with atrasentan resulted in a significant reduction in urinary clusterin excretion and an increase in sET-1 levels (Fig. 1). The geometric mean urinary clusterin-to-creatinine ratio (uCLU/Cr) decreased from 0.44 mg/g (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.38–0.50) at baseline to 0.26 mg/g (95% CI 0.22–0.30) at week 6, corresponding to a median decrease of 46.3% (95% CI −57.8 to −37.1). The geometric mean sET-1 increased from 3.74 pg/ml (95% CI 3.60–3.89) at baseline to 4.77 pg/ml (95% CI 4.62–4.93) at week 6, corresponding to a median increase of 23.4% (95% CI 19.2–27.4). No significant treatment effects were observed for serum clusterin (sCLU).

Effects of 6 weeks treatment with atrasentan on (A) uCLU/Cr (n = 307), (B) serum clusterin (n = 284), (C) uET-1/Cr (n = 37), and (D) serum ET-1 (n = 283) in SONAR participants.

For urinary ET-1-to-creatinine ratio (uET-1/Cr), the majority of uET-1 measurements were below the lower limit of quantification (74.5%), resulting in a substantially reduced sample size (n = 37). The geometric mean uET-1/Cr decreased from 3.30 pg/mg (95% CI 2.06–5.30) at baseline to 2.06 pg/mg (95% CI 1.65–2.58) at week 6 corresponding to a median decrease of 28.6% (95% CI −80.6–52.2; Fig. 1C).

Association between endothelin signaling and clusterin

We observed a strong positive correlation between baseline uET-1/Cr and uCLU/Cr (Pearson r = 0.65, P < .0001; Fig. 2). There was no statistically significant correlation between the change in uET-1/Cr and uCLU/Cr (Pearson r = 0.38, P = .15), possibly due to the small sample size.

Effect of dapagliflozin on uEGF/Cr by etiology

At baseline, uEGF/Cr was similar across CKD etiologies, including DKD, HTN, and miscellaneous, but was significantly lower in participants with GN compared with DKD (P = .001) and HTN (P = .01; Fig. 3A). After 1 year of treatment, dapagliflozin attenuated the decline in uEGF/Cr compared with placebo by 9.7% (95% CI 5.0–14.3) in the overall population (Fig. 3B). When stratified by CKD etiology, dapagliflozin had no significant effect on uEGF/Cr in participants with HTN or GN. In contrast, a significant relative increase of 12.4% (95% CI 7.0–17.7) in uEGF/Cr was observed in participants with DKD, and a numerical increase of 26.5% (95% CI −2.4–55.5) in those with miscellaneous etiologies, corresponding to a significant treatment-by-etiology interaction (P for interaction = .01). In addition, treatment effects differed by diabetes status, with a larger relative increase in uEGF/Cr observed in participants with type 2 diabetes (12.8%, 95% CI 7.4–18.3) compared with those without diabetes (2.6%, 95% CI −6.1–11.3; P for interaction = .04). Treatment effects also increased with higher baseline HbA1c levels (P for interaction = .03).

DISCUSSION

In this study, we evaluated short-term pharmacodynamic responses of urinary clusterin and uEGF to ERA and SGLT2i therapy, respectively, across two large, well-characterized CKD trial populations. In SONAR, treatment with atrasentan was associated with a rapid and pronounced reduction in urinary clusterin together with an increase in circulating ET-1, consistent with effective endothelin receptor blockade. In DAPA-CKD, dapagliflozin attenuated the decline in uEGF/Cr over 1 year, with heterogeneity of response across CKD etiologies. Notably, relative increases in uEGF/Cr were most pronounced in participants with DKD, whereas no effects were observed in GN or HTN. In addition, effects of dapagliflozin on uEGF/Cr were larger in participants with type 2 diabetes compared to those without, and increased with higher baseline HbA1c levels. Finally, uEGF/Cr correlated with intrarenal EGF mRNA expression, supporting uEGF as a noninvasive marker of tubular EGF expression in diabetes. Together, these findings support urinary clusterin and uEGF as mechanism-informed pharmacodynamic biomarkers reflecting distinct intrarenal pathways engaged by ERA and SGLT2i therapy, respectively.

Urinary clusterin decreased rapidly following 6 weeks of atrasentan treatment, supporting clusterin as a sensitive pharmacodynamic marker of ERA therapy [26]. We observed a strong correlation between uET-1 and urinary clusterin, supporting a mechanism of action based link between intrarenal endothelin activity and tubular injury-related biomarker responses. This finding aligns with biopsy-based evidence demonstrating that ERA therapy suppresses intrarenal clusterin expression, reinforcing the concept that changes in urinary clusterin primarily reflect modulation of intrarenal endothelin signaling rather than systemic effects [26]. Although the reduction in uET-1/Cr following ERA treatment did not reach statistical significance in our study, another study demonstrated that compared to placebo sitaxsentan reduced uET-1/Cr by 28%, an effect size in the same order of magnitude as observed in this study [29]. The lack of statistical significance in this study may therefore be attributed to the small sample size.

Baseline uEGF/Cr was lower in participants with GN as CKD etiology compared with DKD and HTN in our study, consistent with more advanced impairment of tubular health in GN. Dapagliflozin attenuated the decline in uEGF/Cr over 1 year, with effects most pronounced in DKD and absent in GN and HTN, suggesting that SGLT2i-related modulation of tubular regenerative pathways depends on underlying disease mechanisms. These findings highlight that uEGF may be particularly informative for monitoring pharmacodynamic responses to SGLT2 inhibition in DKD. Similarly, we observed larger effects of dapagliflozin on uEGF/Cr among participants with type 2 diabetes and among those with higher baseline HbA1c levels, although these findings should be interpreted cautiously. Prior analyses from CREDENCE, which enrolled only participants with type 2 diabetes, showed no effect modification by baseline HbA1c on kidney outcomes despite a broad glycemic range, suggesting that the present observations may reflect chance [22]. Importantly, the observed changes in uEGF/Cr were supported by kidney biopsy data, demonstrating a positive correlation between uEGF levels and EGF mRNA expression in tubular cells in participants with or without SGLT2i treatment, strengthening the interpretation that uEGF reflects intrarenal biological effects.

Urinary protein measures such as UACR and UPCR remain central to CKD management and clinical trials, yet are limited by substantial biological and analytical variability and primarily reflect glomerular injury [11–13]. Urinary clusterin and uEGF provide complementary information by capturing distinct aspects of tubular injury and repair. As both biomarkers are measured in urine, they can be assessed using the same samples collected for UACR or UPCR, facilitating pragmatic integration into clinical research and potentially routine care. Combining multiple biomarkers that reflect different pathophysiological pathways may reduce uncertainty in monitoring disease progression and treatment response. Concordant changes across multiple biomarkers may provide greater confidence in biological signal than changes in any single marker alone. The utility and implementation of biomarkers are being actively investigated within the Personalized Drug Response: Implementation and Evaluation in CKD consortium, funded by the European Union [30].

Strengths of this study include the use of large, well-characterized randomized trial populations, evaluation of pharmacodynamic responses across two distinct therapeutic pathways, and the use of biologically grounded and analytically validated urinary biomarkers. The availability of kidney biopsy data for mechanistic validation of uEGF further strengthens interpretation of the findings. Several limitations should be acknowledged. Analyses were conducted within clinical trial populations and may not be fully generalizable to clinical practice. In SONAR, biomarker responses were assessed during a short, nonplacebo-controlled enrichment phase. In addition, a substantial proportion of uET-1 measurements were below the lower limit of quantification, limiting power to detect treatment-related changes.

In conclusion, urinary clusterin is a robust short-term pharmacodynamic biomarker of ERA therapy closely linked to intrarenal endothelin activity, while uEGF reflects SGLT2i-associated modulation of tubular health and reveals heterogeneity of treatment response across CKD etiologies. Together, these findings highlight the value of mechanism-specific urinary biomarkers as complements to albuminuria for treatment monitoring, patient stratification, and improved assessment of disease progression in CKD clinical trials.
