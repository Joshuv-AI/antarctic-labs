A forecast model for ionospheric TEC during solar flare events based on low-latitude GPS stations data
Authors: R. Mukesh, Punyawi Jamjareegulgran, S. Riswana Fathima, J. Rubashri, P. Priyavarshini, J. Priyadharshini, Sarat C. Dass, S. Kiruthiga, A. R. S. Sahanaa, M. Sudhandra, S. Safana, S. Karthick, Prasert Kenpankho, Supawit Nambut, Pattawut Wongsak
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Solar flares can strongly disturb the ionosphere and significantly affect radio communication and satellite navigation systems. In this paper, the Ionospheric responses to four X-class solar flare events that occurred during the 25th solar cycle were investigated through the prediction of Total Electron Content (TEC). A Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) algorithm-based deep learning approach, combined both with the Adaptive Gradient Algorithm (Adagrad) optimiser and without an optimiser, was implemented to forecast TEC variations. A Deep Belief Network with Adagrad optimiser (DBN-Adagrad) is a deep learning model used to provide a clear comparison with the proposed KAN-based approach. The proposed models were analysed with three sets of dataset split strategies, and also the models were validated with Leave future out cross-validation. A geomagnetically quiet day on 28 April 2025, with Kp < 3, is also considered as a reference for evaluating model performance under undisturbed ionospheric conditions. These KAN-based TEC predictions were compared with TEC predictions based on the DBN-Adagrad, IRI-2020 model and with GNSS–derived TEC observations at the HYDE (Hyderabad, India) and CPN (Chumphon, Thailand) stations. The proposed KAN and DBN models used the important solar and geomagnetic parameters, such as Ap, Kp indices, SSN, and the 10.7 cm solar radio flux, as input parameters during quiet and disturbed conditions. Four major solar flares (SF) events on 26th October 2024 (X1.86 SF), 13th May 2025 (X1.21 SF), 14th May 2025 (X2.7 SF), and 25th May 2025 (X1.1 SF), were analysed orderly in this study. Moreover, model performance was evaluated using standard statistical measures, including Willmott’s Index of Agreement (WIA), Kling-Gupta Efficiency (KGE) and Spearman’s Correlation Coefficient (SCC). As for the X1.86 SF event (first SF) observed over HYDE and CPN stations, the KAN with Adagrad optimiser and DBN with Adagrad optimiser models achieved KGE values of 0.8586,0.7885 and 0.8573,0.8248, respectively, demonstrating their good performance as compared to other models (KAN without optimiser and IRI-2020 models). Overall, the results showed that the KAN model with Adagrad optimiser and DBN with Adagrad optimiser generally provided TEC predictions with improved accuracy at both stations as compared to other models, except on May 13th, 2025, over the HYDE station, where the KAN without optimiser performed better. As for the X1.1 SF event on 25th May 2025 (fourth SF) over HYDE station, two KAN models with Adagrad optimiser and without optimiser exhibited nearly equal performance. This study demonstrated that AI-driven deep learning models can effectively capture TEC variations during strong SF events and play a valuable role in improving the reliability of global navigation satellite systems (GNSS) under disturbed space weather conditions.

Introduction

Solar flares (SFs) are an immense explosion on the Sun that happens when energy stored in twisted magnetic fields due to plasma motion is suddenly released, causing immediate, abnormal ionisation in the lower layers of the ionosphere, thereby increasing electron density. Total Electron Content (TEC) is the integration of electron density along the whole path between a satellite and its receiver. Radio waves are affected by the presence of electrons. The more electrons in the path of the radio wave, the more the radio signal will be affected. Since communication and GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System) systems use radio frequencies, navigation systems are also affected. TEC is a good parameter to monitor for potential space-weather impacts. During a SF event, large amounts of X-ray and extreme ultraviolet radiation are emitted and reach the Earth in approximately eight minutes1,2. This high-energy radiation is quickly absorbed by the dayside ionosphere, which triggers strong photoionisation and rapidly increases the free electron population. The release of additional electrons causes a rapid rise in the electron density. Consequently, the ionospheric TEC increases sharply during solar flare events3–9.

Wulansari et al.10 analysed the Ionospheric response to a strong solar flare of X1.1 class that occurred on November 8th, 2013. GNSS observation data were collected from the Sumatran GPS Array (SuGAR) stations distributed across Indonesia. Ionospheric electron density disturbances were analysed by using the GNSS-TEC method, such as the detection of Sudden Ionospheric Disturbance (SID) using multiple GPS stations and the Detection of SID using all GPS satellites. The results suggested that the GNSS-TEC method was highly effective for understanding the Ionospheric disturbances caused by X-class flare events. Chakraborty et al.11 investigated TEC variations during X-class solar flares and geomagnetic storm conditions, highlighting the combined influence of solar and geomagnetic activity on ionospheric behaviour. Sanjay Kumar and Singh12, Singh et al.13, Domingo et al.14 investigated the solar flares at low latitude and showed that the magnitude of TEC enhancement depended on the class of the solar flares. Han et al.15 evaluated four ML algorithms (NN, LSTM, ANFIS, GBDT) for short-term TEC prediction at low-latitude IGS stations during high solar activity, finding that the GBDT model outperformed the others. Chen et al.16 investigated TEC prediction using deep learning approaches, such as spatio-temporal graph neural networks, for global ionospheric TEC prediction. Lobanova et al.17 examined TEC variations at the Mikhnevo Geophysical Observatory during X-class flares on 6th–7th September 2017, showing improved prediction accuracy during the recovery phase using a quiet-day background detrending approach. Zhao et al.18 investigated an Ionospheric TEC prediction model based on Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD) and heterogeneous ensemble learning. TEC data from six GNSS stations along with solar and geomagnetic activity parameters, were used for TEC prediction. Three heterogeneous models, namely BiLSTM, BiTCN and DBN, were used for final prediction. Modal performance was evaluated using R2, RMSE, MAPE and MAE metrics. The results showed that the proposed model achieved higher accuracy and stability during geomagnetic disturbances, demonstrating strong adaptability in low-latitude regions. Pietrow et al.19 analysed spectroscopic characteristics of X2.2 and X9.3 class solar flares on 6th September 2017, demonstrating that Hα-based indices reliably represent flare timing and strength. Zhou et al.20 analysed the influence of ionosphere disturbances on GNSS signal and positioning due to the X9.3 solar flare event on September 6th, 2017 and the M1.6 solar flare event on October 9th, 2021. Saharan et al.21 investigated the ionospheric response to 450 solar flares that occurred during 2014 (the peak of solar cycle 24) via GPS TEC signals from Bangalore (IGS station at low latitude). Previous research indicated that the ionospheric effects of solar flares depended on multiple parameters such as flare intensity, solar geometry, local time, and background space weather conditions. Observations from the GNSS-derived TEC, VLF signals and satellite measurements revealed strong regional and event-dependent variability in TEC enhancements, particularly over low-altitude and equatorial regions.

Although several machine learning and signal processing methods have been developed for TEC prediction, many of these approaches struggle to capture the fast and nonlinear TEC changes associated with extreme flare activity. Their performances were often affected by seasonal variability and geomagnetic disturbances. To effectively capture the non-linear and dynamic nature of the ionospheric variations during solar flares, a robust alternative modelling strategy is necessary. The Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) model addresses this need by learning multivariate nonlinear relationships through flexible univariate functions. Hence, the main contribution of this research is the development of a KAN-based framework for TEC prediction during solar flares. To predict the TEC variabilities, the solar and geomagnetic parameters, as well as the Adaptive Gradient Algorithm (Adagrad) optimiser, are also applied along with the KAN algorithm (namely, KAN-Adagrad). Finally, it can be seen that the KAN-Adagrad model generally provides competitive accuracy for multivariate time-series forecasting under solar flare conditions as compared to the TEC values predicted by KAN without an optimiser (namely, the KAN model), Deep Belief Network with Adagrad (namely, DBN-Adagrad) and the IRI-2020 model (IRI-2020). While various deep learning models, such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), have been commonly applied in the ionospheric TEC prediction, they typically require a large amount of data for training, and are not able to capture fast nonlinear changes of TEC during extreme solar flare events. In this paper, we choose the Deep Belief Network (DBN) as a comparison model in this study, because the hierarchical pre-training strategy of the DBN model can learn complex feature representations from the limited training sample sets. In addition, the Adagrad optimiser is used to train the DBN model (called DBN-Adagrad) to provide a consistent comparison with the proposed KAN-Adagrad model. Note that IRI, International Reference Ionosphere, is an international project sponsored by the International Union of Radio Science (URSI) and the Committee on Space Research (COSPAR). It constructs an empirical standard model of the ionosphere to provide monthly averages of electron density, TEC, F2-layer critical frequency, etc., with the ionospheric altitude22. The latest version of the IRI model is the IRI-2020 model.

Data and methodology

In this research, we focused on the impacts on ionospheric TEC variations created by four SF events, such as X1.86 SF, X1.21 SF, X2.7 SF and X1.1 SF that occurred on 26th October 2024, 13th May 2025, 14th May 2025 and 25th May 2025, respectively, on the ionosphere. A geomagnetically quiet day on 28 April 2025, with Kp < 3, is considered as a reference to evaluate model performance under undisturbed ionospheric conditions at both the HYDE and CPN stations. We considered four prediction models to study TEC variations in this work, for instance, the KAN model without optimiser (so-called KAN model), the KAN algorithm with Adagrad optimiser (so-called KAN-Adagrad), the Deep Belief Network with Adagrad (so-called DBN-Adagrad) and the IRI-2020 model (so-called IRI-2020). Moreover, Statistical parameters [e.g., Willmott’s Index of Agreement (WIA), Kling-Gupta Efficiency (KGE), and Spearman Correlation Coefficient (SCC)] are also used to evaluate the performance of the KAN model, KAN-Adagrad model, DBN-Adagrad model and IRI-2020 model.

Firstly, one set of TEC data over Hyderabad station (HYDE), India, was obtained from the IONOLAB website.

The TEC data obtained from the IONOLAB were extracted with 576 samples per day. To reduce the complexity and noise of the data, it was then down-sampled by taking the average of every 24 measurements, resulting in 24 data points per day, which were used as model inputs. Note that IONOLAB is a research group and laboratory that focuses on imaging and monitoring the ionosphere23. The latitude and longitude of the HYDE station are 17.42°N and 78.55°E, respectively. Secondly, another set of TEC values was processed from a GNSS receiver at Chumphon station (CPN), Thailand. The latitude and longitude of the CPN station are 10.72°N and 99.37°E, respectively. Both HYDE and CPN stations are located in low-latitude regions. Thirdly, the TEC values predicted by the IRI-2020 model (namely, IRI-2020 predicted TEC) were also collected and processed over HYDE and CPN stations. The input parameters given to the proposed KAN model included solar and geomagnetic indices such as sunspot number (SSN), 10.7 cm solar radio flux (F10.7), as well as Ap and Kp indices. These parameters were taken from NASA’s OMNIWeb. X-class solar flares are rare and extreme events, which naturally limit the availability of observational data. In this study, four events with intensities ranging from X1.1 to X2.7 were selected to capture different levels of ionospheric disturbances. A similar event-based approach has also been adopted in previous ionospheric TEC studies5. Note that OMNIWeb is a NASA website that provides a collection of near-Earth solar wind and magnetic field data24.

As shown in Fig. 1, the forecasting framework integrates solar/geomagnetic indices and TEC observations of solar flares into a unified modelling pipeline. Four solar flare dates, a quiet day, and their corresponding training periods were selected to capture both disturbed and quiet ionospheric conditions, ensuring the models are exposed to diverse scenarios. TEC data was down-sampled to maintain consistency and reduce noise. The modelling stage comprised three models: KAN with Adagrad optimiser, KAN without optimiser and DBN with Adagrad optimiser. Each branch follows a structured training pipeline, beginning with parameter initialisation and proceeding through propagation, optimisation, or pretraining steps. Convergence was tested in each case. If convergence is achieved, it yields an optimised or converged model; if it is not, it yields partially trained models. The converged and optimised models proceed to the evaluation stage. Performance evaluation was conducted using three: Weighted Index of Agreement (WIA), Spearman’s Correlation Coefficient (SCC) and Kling-Gupta Efficiency (KGE). These metrics provided a balanced assessment of predictive accuracy, correlation strength and overall efficiency.

Normalisation means scaling of input data; Z-score standardisation was applied to centre and scale TEC values, thus improving convergence and stabilising training.

Analysis of training data size and dataset split strategy

To determine the optimum training data size for TEC prediction, the KAN-Adagrad and DBN-Adagrad models were trained using three different training data sizes: 30 days, 60 days, and 100 days. The predicted TEC values were compared with the actual TEC values from the test data, and the Mean Absolute Error (MAE) was calculated. The size of the training data is an important factor in time-series prediction, as it affects both the accuracy and generalisation ability of the model31. Table 3 presents the MAE values for both models under the three training data sizes. From Table 3, it is observed that the KAN-Adagrad model gives the best performance with 100 days of training data, achieving the lowest MAE of 3.5215 TECU. Since this provides more stable and accurate results, the 100-day dataset was selected for the main experiments in this study. After selecting the training data size, the 100-day dataset was divided using an 80/20 split. The first 80% of the data was used for training, and the remaining 20% was used for testing. This split was done in a chronological order to preserve the natural sequence of TEC variations and to ensure that future data was not used during training. This helps to avoid information leakage and provides a more reliable evaluation of the model performance31.

As the dataset is divided chronologically, the data of the observed solar flare event will not overlap across the training and testing 80/20 boundary, but the data of other solar flares that may have occurred on the training dates may create an impact on the prediction. In this study, the prediction horizon was explicitly defined as 24 h ahead, corresponding to one full diurnal cycle of ionospheric variation. This rolling window approach provides predictions aligned with the practical monitoring needs, as daily forecasts are most useful for space weather monitoring, providing insights for solar flare-driven ionospheric variability. The forecasting setup was designed to operate in a forward-prediction mode, not in a retrospective fitting. 72 h were used as a training sequence to generate forecasts for the subsequent 24 h. This makes sure that the models were evaluated under realistic temporal constraints, and the chronological 80/20 train-test split preserved event order, simulating operation deployment conditions. The models were assessed only on unseen data to reflect true forecasting conditions.

Model validation using PSIS-LFO-CV

The predictive performance of the models was assessed using Leave-Future-out Cross Validation (LFO-CV) methodology, as shown in previous studies32, and to tackle possible over-fitting and time-series data splitting concerns. This approach is based on the assumption that only data from the past can be used to forecast future data. This is ideal for use with ionospheric TEC data, for which the time order needs to be maintained. The PSIS-LFO-CV (Pareto Smoothed Importance Sampling) validation was performed for two events: X1.21 SF (CPN station) and X1.86 SF (HYDE station), X.1.21 SF (CPN) as shown in Fig. 2 and the values for both events are shown in Tables 4 and 5, since the TEC prediction results and model behaviour for the remaining events and stations were similar. As in the X1.21 SF event at CPN station, the Pareto-k diagnostic plot shows that the majority of the prediction points are within the acceptable range (k < 0.5) with the occasional refit points at h = 19 and h = 22, demonstrating good predictive behaviour for both models. The performance metrics of PSIS-LFO-CV, such as Total Expected Log Predictive Density (ELPD), Mean Pointwise ELPD and number of refits for all the solar flare events and stations studied are summarised in Table 6. These results suggest that both KAN-Adagrad and DBN-Adagrad models perform well under solar flare conditions without overfitting33.

Results and discussion

TEC prediction during X1.86 SF on October 26th, 2024

The X1.86 SF that happened on October 26, 2024, originated from the Sun’s active region AR 3873, which was located on the southeastern limb of the Sun’s visible disk. The event began at approximately 05:57 UTC, reached its peak at 07:17 UTC, and ended around 07:56 UTC. Due to its X-class magnitude and Earth-facing side of the sun, the flare caused a substantial increase in ionization level and noticeable anomalies in ionospheric TEC.

TEC, solar and geomagnetic variations during X1.86 SF

Figure 3 shows the TEC values over HYDE and CPN stations along with the solar and geomagnetic indices, including F10.7 solar radio flux, Ap index, SSN, and Kp index for the period from 28th September 2024 to 28th October 2024. The TEC values at both HYDE and CPN stations show regular diurnal variations throughout the observation period. A significant geomagnetic disturbance was observed around 10th–11th October 2024, where the Ap index reached approximately 294 nT and the Kp index exceeded 8, indicating a severe geomagnetic storm event prior to the X1.86 SF on 26th October 2024. The F10.7 solar radio flux values ranged from 184 to 322 sfu, confirming high solar activity during Solar Cycle 25. The SSN values ranged from 135 to 225, further indicating the active solar conditions. On 26th October 2024, the X1.86 SF event caused noticeable variations in the TEC values at both HYDE and CPN stations, associated with the sudden enhancement in solar radiation during the solar flare.

This SF event occurred on 26th October 2024 from 05:57 UTC to 07:56 UTC, where it reached its peak intensity at 07:17 UTC. The KAN model was input with four parameters, such as (a) SSNs ranging from 122 to 246, (b) Kp values ranging from 0 to 47, (c) Ap values ranging from 0 to 39 and (d) F10.7 ranging from 183 to 253. Around 08:00 UTC, a significant rise in SSN and F10.7 was observed due to the impact of a solar flare, as shown in Fig. 3. On 23rd October, 2024, the IRI-2020–predicted TEC values under-estimated the True TEC values and DBN-Adagrad predicted TEC values over-estimated the True TEC values when compared to other models. Around 08:00 UTC on 24th October 2024, there was a fluctuation in the True TEC value. This event was closely predicted by the DBN-Adagrad model and slightly predicted by the KAN-Adagrad model when compared with other models. On 25th October 2024, the KAN-Adagrad model closely predicted the True TEC values and the DBN-Adagrad model also slightly predicted well, as compared to the KAN (without the optimiser) model and the IRI-2020 model. The IRI-2020 model overestimated the True TEC values continuously when compared to the KAN (without optimiser), DBN-Adagrad and the KAN-Adagrad model on 26th -28th October 2024. On 26th October 2024, a noticeable fluctuation in the True TEC values was observed around 15:00 UTC. Since an X1.86 solar flare occurred on the same day and enhanced solar activity conditions were indicated by SSN values greater than 60 and F10.7 values exceeding 120 sfu, this TEC enhancement may be associated with increased solar activity. Although all models overestimated the fluctuation, the KAN-Adagrad model provided a closer prediction to the observed TEC values during the peak period. On the same day, there was a noticeable fluctuation at the end of the day that was under-predicted by all models. On 28th October 2024, all prediction models overestimated the True TEC values. Overall, for almost all six days, the KAN-Adagrad model and DBN-Adagrad model performed well as compared to other models. The KAN model performed moderately as compared to the IRI-2020 model, whereas the IRI-2020 model under-estimated and over-estimated the True TEC values consistently.

TEC prediction during X1.21 SF on 13th May 2025

The X1.86-class solar flare that happened on May 13, 2025, initiated from the Sun’s active region AR 4086, which was located on the western edge of the solar disk. The event began at approximately 15:25 UTC, reached its peak at 15:38 UTC, and ended around 15:44 UTC. Due to its X-class magnitude, the flare caused a moderate increase in ionization level and anomalies in ionospheric TEC.

TEC, Solar and Geomagnetic variations during X1.21 SF

Figure 8 shows the TEC values over HYDE and CPN stations along with the solar and geomagnetic indices, including F10.7 solar radio flux, Ap index, SSN, and Kp index from 15th April 2025 to 16th May 2025. This observation period covers the X1.21 SF event on 13th May 2025. The F10.7 solar radio flux ranged from 112 to 178 sfu, and the SSN values varied between 53 and 212, indicating variable solar conditions. A geomagnetic disturbance was observed around 16th–17th April 2025, where the Ap index reached approximately 177 nT and the Kp index exceeded 7, indicating a strong geomagnetic storm. Strong enhancements of TEC values were observed at both stations during this disturbed period. On 13th May 2025, the X1.21 SF event produced moderate TEC enhancements at both HYDE and CPN stations, associated with the enhancement in solar radiation during the solar flare.

On 23rd May 2025, all four studied prediction models under-predicted the True TEC values during the TEC peak hours (09:00 UTC to 13:00 UTC); however, both KAN models and DBN-Adagrad performed slightly better than the IRI-2020 model. On 24th May 2025, both KAN models and DBN-Adagrad slightly over-predicted the TEC values around 13:00 UTC, but they provided better performance than the IRI-2020 model. On solar flare event day (25th May 2025), the KAN-Adagrad and DBN-Adagrad models provided better performance than the other two models, and the KAN model showed strong under-prediction towards the end of that day. After the solar flare event, the KAN-Adagrad and DBN-Adagrad models maintained better performance as compared to the other two models. On 26th May 2025, noticeable rapid fluctuations in the True TEC values were observed, particularly during the peak period. These fluctuations may be associated with the X1.1 solar flare, along with SSN and F10.7 values exceeding their threshold levels (SSN > 60 and F10.7 > 120 sfu). During this period, all four models under-predicted the True TEC values. In contrast, the KAN model showed slight over-prediction on 27th May 2025, particularly during the TEC peak period around 15:00 UTC. Overall, the results suggested that the IRI-2020 model consistently under-predicted the True TEC values, and the KAN model provided moderate performance throughout the study period. Moreover, the KAN-Adagrad and DBN-Adagrad models gave the best performance among all four studied prediction models, although some TEC fluctuations were not accurately predicted.

Conclusion

This paper presented the prediction of ionospheric TEC during four solar flare (SF) events using the Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) with and without the Adaptive Gradient Algorithm (Adagrad) optimiser and compared the results with the DBN-Adagrad model, IRI-2020-predicted TEC values, and the observed TEC at Hyderabad (HYDE), India and Chumphon (CPN), Thailand. The developed models were trained using chronologically arranged datasets and validated through the PSIS-LFO Cross-Validation approach. Four solar flare events, namely X1.86 (26 October 2024), X1.21 (13 May 2025), X2.7 (14 May 2025), and X1.1 (25 May 2025), were analysed in this study. The obtained results demonstrated that both KAN-Adagrad and DBN-Adagrad generally achieved higher predictive accuracy than the KAN and IRI-2020 models for most of the investigated events. During the X1.86 flare event, KAN-Adagrad produced the highest performance at HYDE (WIA = 0.9677, KGE = 0.8586, SCC = 0.9533), while DBN-Adagrad slightly showed better performance than KAN-Adagrad at CPN with WIA, KGE, and SCC values of 0.9533, 0.8248, and 0.9458, respectively. For the X1.21 flare event, KAN showed the best performance at HYDE (WIA = 0.9777, KGE = 0.8845, SCC = 0.9387), whereas DBN-Adagrad provided better agreement with the observed TEC values at CPN (WIA = 0.9391, KGE = 0.8223, SCC = 0.8893). Similarly, during the X2.7 flare event, both DBN-Adagrad and KAN-Adagrad showed good predictive capability at HYDE, with DBN-Adagrad achieving slightly higher statistical performance (WIA = 0.9803, KGE = 0.9086, SCC = 0.9459) than KAN-Adagrad (WIA = 0.9724, KGE = 0.8985, SCC = 0.9383). At CPN, DBN-Adagrad again showed the best overall performance, with WIA, KGE, and SCC values of 0.9477, 0.8444, and 0.9101, respectively. For the X1.1 flare event, both KAN and KAN-Adagrad performed well at HYDE, while KAN-Adagrad achieved the highest predictive accuracy at CPN (WIA = 0.9270, KGE = 0.8012, SCC = 0.8739). The quiet-day analysis further demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed models, where KAN-Adagrad consistently achieved the best overall performance at both HYDE and CPN stations. In general, the incorporation of the Adagrad optimiser improved the predictive capability of the KAN architecture, resulting in higher agreement, efficiency, and correlation with the observed TEC values. The consistently high WIA and SCC values obtained across different solar flare intensities indicate that the proposed KAN-Adagrad framework is capable of effectively capturing the temporal variability of TEC under both disturbed and quiet ionospheric conditions. Overall, the results suggest that KAN-Adagrad provides a reliable and effective approach for TEC prediction, especially under disturbed space weather conditions such as solar flares. These findings suggest that KAN-based approaches can be effectively used for TEC prediction during solar flare events. However, the proposed model requires further validation using TEC data obtained from different latitude GNSS stations, during different phases of the solar cycle and other solar flare events, to assess its robustness and generalizability.
