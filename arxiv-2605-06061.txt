Geometry-Aware Simplicial Message Passing
Elena Xinyi Wang, Bastian Rieck

Abstract
The Weisfeiler–Lehman (WL) test and its simplicial extension (SWL) characterize the combinatorial expressivity of message passing networks, but they are blind to geometry, i.e., meshes with identical connectivity but different embeddings are indistinguishable. We introduce the Geometric Simplicial Weisfeiler–Lehman (GSWL) test, which incorporates vertex coordinates into color refinement for geometric simplicial complexes. In addition, we show that (i) the expressivity of geometry-aware simplicial message passing schemes is bounded above by GSWL, and (ii) that there exist parameters such that the discriminating power of GSWL is matched by these schemes on any fixed finite family of geometric simplicial complexes. Combined with the Euler Characteristic Transform (ECT), a complete invariant for geometric simplicial complexes, this yields a geometric expressivity characterization together with an approximation framework. Experiments on synthetic and mesh datasets serve to validate our theory, showing a clear hierarchy from combinatorial to geometry-aware models.

Background

2.1
The Weisfeiler–Lehman (WL) test and GNNs

The

-WL test
[

]
iteratively refines vertex colors by hashing each vertex’s color together with the multiset of its neighbors’ colors, i.e.,

c_{v}^{(t+1)}=\mathrm{HASH}\!\left(c_{v}^{(t)},\;\{\!\{c_{u}^{(t)}:u\in\mathcal{N}(v)\}\!\}\right).

(1)

Xu et al.
[
32
]
and Morris et al.
[
18
]
proved that message-passing GNNs with injective aggregation are exactly as powerful as
1
-WL.
This characterization has been central to understanding and improving GNN architectures.

2.2
Simplicial complexes and the simplicial Weisfeiler–Lehman (SWL) test

A simplicial complex
K
on a vertex set
V
is a collection of nonempty subsets of
V
that is closed under taking subsets
[
14
]
.
A
k
-simplex
\sigma=\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{k}\}
has dimension
k
.
We write
\partial\sigma
for the set of codimension-1 faces (boundary) and
\mathrm{coface}(\sigma)
for the set of simplices of dimension

containing

(coboundary).
Bodnar et al.
[
6

defined the Simplicial WL (SWL) test by initializing all simplices of the same dimension with the same color and refining via boundary, coboundary, lower-adjacent, and upper-adjacent neighborhoods.
They show that boundary and upper adjacencies suffice to reach the converged SWL partition.
Here, we work with boundary and coboundary adjacencies,
2
2

2

We recall that two simplices are boundary-adjacent when one is a face of the other, and coboundary-adjacent when one is a coface of the other. Thus,
\sigma
aggregates from
\partial\sigma
(one dimension below) and from
\mathrm{coface}(\sigma)
(one dimension above).

which yield the
same

Specifically, our coordinate-recovery argument (Lemma
1
) tracks how vertex information propagates up the Hasse diagram one dimension at a time, and this propagation rate underlies the depth bound
L\geq\dim(K)
in Theorem
4
.

An
embedded
simplicial complex
(K,\mathbf{x})
consists of an abstract complex
K
together with an injective map
\mathbf{x}\colon\mathrm{vert}(K)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
assigning coordinates to vertices.
For a simplex
\sigma=\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{k}\}
, we define coordinate-derived features, i.e.,

\begin{split}\text{Vertices:}&\quad\mathbf{x}_{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
\text{Edges }\{u,v\}:&\quad\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x}_{u}+\mathbf{x}_{v}),\;\|\mathbf{x}_{u}-\mathbf{x}_{v}\|,\\
\text{Triangles }\{u,v,w\}:&\quad\tfrac{1}{3}(\mathbf{x}_{u}+\mathbf{x}_{v}+\mathbf{x}_{w}),\;\mathrm{area}(\{u,v,w\}).\end{split}

(2)

Two embedded complexes
(K_{1},\mathbf{x}_{1})
and
(K_{2},\mathbf{x}_{2})
are

\phi\colon K_{1}\to K_{2}
such that
\mathbf{x}_{2}\circ\phi=\mathbf{x}_{1}
on vertices.
Note that SWL, which ignores coordinates, cannot distinguish
(K,\mathbf{x})
from
(K,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
for any two embeddings of the same abstract complex.

2.3
The Euler characteristic transform

For an embedded complex
(K,\mathbf{x})
, direction
\nu\in S^{d-1}
, and threshold
t\in\mathbb{R}
, define the sublevel set
K_{\nu,t}:=\{\sigma\in K:t_{\nu}(\sigma)\leq t\}
, where

t_{\nu}(\sigma):=\max\{\mathbf{x}_{u}\cdot\nu:u\in\mathrm{vert}(\sigma)\}

is the entry time of
\sigma
in the direction-
\nu
filtration.
The
Euler characteristic transform
is

\mathrm{ECT}(K,\mathbf{x})(\nu,t)=\chi(K_{\nu,t})=\sum_{\sigma\in K}(-1)^{\dim\sigma}\,\mathbf{1}\{t_{\nu}(\sigma)\leq t\}.

(3)

Since
\tau\subseteq\sigma
implies
t_{\nu}(\tau)\leq t_{\nu}(\sigma)
(the maximum is taken over a subset of vertices),
K_{\nu,t}
is closed under faces and is therefore a subcomplex of
K
.

Theorem 1

(Turner et al.
[
27
]
, Curry et al.
[
10
]
, Ghrist et al.
[
12
]
)
.

Fix a finite abstract simplicial complex
K
.
Two embeddings
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\colon\mathrm{vert}(K)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
that satisfy
\mathrm{ECT}(K,\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{ECT}(K,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
as functions on
S^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}
are equal as embeddings.
Equivalently, the map
\mathbf{x}\mapsto\mathrm{ECT}(K,\mathbf{x})
is injective.

In particular, fixing the abstract complex
K
, the ECT recovers the vertex coordinates in the ambient coordinate system, which is the form of completeness used throughout our downstream results.

3
Geometric simplicial Weisfeiler–Lehman test

We modify SWL by initializing vertices with their coordinates instead of a uniform color, while leaving the adjacency structure (boundary and coboundary) unchanged.
After a sufficient number of refinement steps, each simplex recovers the coordinates of all its vertices.

Definition 1

(GSWL)
.

Fix an embedded simplicial complex
(K,\mathbf{x})
with injective
\mathbf{x}
. Define
colors recursively by

c^{(0)}_{\sigma}:=\begin{cases}(0,\,\mathbf{x}_{v})&\text{if }\sigma=\{v\}\text{ is a vertex,}\\
(k,\,\Phi_{k}(\mathbf{x}_{v_{0}},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{v_{k}}))&\text{if }\sigma=\{v_{0},\dots,v_{k}\}\text{ with }k\geq 1,\end{cases}

where
\Phi_{k}
is a fixed permutation-invariant function of the vertex coordinates of
\sigma
(e.g., sorted tuple of coordinates, or the empty feature if only dimension is used).
For
\ell\geq 0
,

c^{(\ell+1)}_{\sigma}:=\mathrm{HASH}\!\left(c^{(\ell)}_{\sigma},\;\{\!\{c^{(\ell)}_{\tau}:\tau\in\partial\sigma\}\!\},\;\{\!\{c^{(\ell)}_{\rho}:\rho\in\mathrm{coface}(\sigma)\}\!\}\right),

where
\mathrm{HASH}
is injective.
We call two embedded complexes
GSWL-
L
equivalent
, denoted by
K_{1}\equiv_{L}K_{2}
, if
\{\!\{c^{(L)}_{\sigma}:\sigma\in K_{1}\}\!\}=\{\!\{c^{(L)}_{\sigma}:\sigma\in K_{2}\}\!\}
.

The key difference to SWL
[
6
]
is the initialization: Each simplex receives features derived from its vertex coordinates rather than a uniform color.
In particular, vertices carry their coordinates, and higher-dimensional simplices carry
\Phi_{k}
, a
permutation-invariant
function of their vertex coordinates such as edge midpoint and length, or triangle centroid and area, as used in our experiments (Section

Since this initialization is strictly finer than SWL’s constant coloring on non-degenerate embeddings, GSWL is
at least as powerful
as SWL on abstract complexes and
strictly more powerful
on embedded complexes.
The choice
\Phi_{k}\equiv\emptyset
(dimension-only initialization for non-vertex simplices) is the minimal instance of Definition
1
that still suffices for Lemma
1
and all downstream results; richer choices of
\Phi_{k}
only refine the coloring further.
Notice that for a vertex
\sigma=\{v\}
the boundary
\partial\sigma
is empty, so
\mathrm{HASH}
receives the empty multiset in the boundary slot; analogously for top-dimensional simplices in the coboundary slot.
We adopt the convention that
\mathrm{HASH}
is well-defined on, and injective in, inputs that include empty multisets.

1
illustrates the induction in Lemma

Lemma 1

(Coordinate recovery)
.

Let
(K,\mathbf{x})
be an embedded simplicial complex whose vertex map

\mathbf{x}\colon\mathrm{vert}(K)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is injective, and let

\sigma=\{v_{0},\dots,v_{k}\}
be a
k
-simplex of
K
. If
L\geq k
, then

c^{(L)}_{\sigma}
determines the unordered set

\{\mathbf{x}_{v_{0}},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{v_{k}}\}
.
In particular, the entry time

t_{\nu}(\sigma)=\max_{u\in\mathrm{vert}(\sigma)}\mathbf{x}_{u}\cdot\nu
is determined
for every
\nu\in S^{d-1}
.

4
A geometry-aware simplicial architecture and its expressivity

We now introduce a message-passing architecture matching the boundary/coboundary structure of GSWL.
We prove that it cannot exceed GSWL in expressivity, but can match GSWL on any fixed finite family.
Each simplex is updated by

h_{\sigma}^{(\ell+1)}=\mu_{\ell}\!\left(h_{\sigma}^{(\ell)},\;\sum_{\tau\in\partial\sigma}\phi_{\ell}(h_{\tau}^{(\ell)}),\;\sum_{\rho\in\mathrm{coface}(\sigma)}\psi_{\ell}(h_{\rho}^{(\ell)})\right),

(4)

where
\tau
ranges over the boundary faces of
\sigma
,
\rho
ranges over the cofaces of
\sigma
, and
\mu_{\ell}
,
\phi_{\ell}
,
\psi_{\ell}
are

\phi_{\ell}
and
\psi_{\ell}
applied before summation.
Figure
2

\sigma

We initialize this architecture with
h_{\sigma}^{(0)}=E(c_{\sigma}^{(0)})
for a deterministic encoder
E
.
For instance, we can set
E(\dim\sigma,f)\coloneqq W_{\dim\sigma}\,f+b_{\dim\sigma}
, i.e., a per-dimension linear map that acts on the coordinate-derived features introduced in Section
2
(vertex coordinates, edge midpoint and length, triangle centroid and area).
We obtain the following

Theorem 2

(Upper bound)
.

Fix
L\geq 0
. Suppose
K_{1}\equiv_{L}K_{2}
under GSWL. Then for every
choice of parameters in the architecture (
4
) and every

,

\eta_{k}

Vice versa, the architecture matches GSWL on finite families of simplicial complexes.

Definition 2

(Finite interpolation property)
.

A function class
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\{f:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{q}\}

\{(\xi_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}

Standard MLP classes (feedforward networks with ReLU activation and at least one hidden layer of sufficient width) satisfy this property; cf. Xu et al.

Theorem 3

(Finite-family realizability)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite family of finite embedded simplicial complexes and fix
L\geq 0
. For
\ell\in\{0,\dots,L\}
, let

\mathscr{C}_{\ell}\;:=\;\{c^{(\ell)}_{\sigma}:\sigma\in K,\ K\in\mathcal{F}\}

Then, for any hidden dimension

, there exist parameters in the architecture (
4
) and a readout

5
Connection to the Euler characteristic transform

The bounds in Section
4
characterize expressivity in terms of GSWL, but GSWL is a hashing procedure,
not
a geometric invariant.
To give the characterization geometric content, we connect GSWL to the Euler Characteristic Transform.
It is a complete invariant, which means realizing it is equivalent, up to sampling and approximation, to distinguishing
all
embedded complexes.
In this section, we combine the finite-family lower bound (Theorem
3
) with the coordinate recovery results (Lemma
1
) to show that our proposed architecture can compute sampled ECT values exactly on finite families, and approximate the full ECT on bounded classes.

5.1
Exact realization on finite families

Theorem 4

(Exact sampled ECT on finite families)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite family of embedded simplicial complexes
(K,\mathbf{x})
of dimension at most
D
, fix finite sets
\mathcal{V}\subset S^{d-1}
and
T\subset\mathbb{R}
, and let
L\geq D
.
There exist parameters in the architecture (
4
) and a simplex-wise map
\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{H}\to\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}|\cdot|T|}
such that, for every
(K,\mathbf{x})\in\mathcal{F}
,

\sum_{\sigma\in K}(-1)^{\dim\sigma}\Psi(h^{(L)}_{\sigma})=\bigl(\mathrm{ECT}(K,\mathbf{x})(\nu,t)\bigr)_{(\nu,t)\in\mathcal{V}\times T}.

(5)

The theorem holds for arbitrary finite choices of directions
\mathcal{V}
and thresholds
T
, and thus recovers the ECT exactly at the queried grid points.
Since each directional slice
t\mapsto\mathrm{ECT}(K,\mathbf{x})(\nu,t)
is a piecewise-constant function with jumps only at the finitely many entry times
{t_{\nu}(\sigma):\sigma\in K}
, a grid
T
containing all such values across
\mathcal{F}
suffices to reconstruct the full step function for each direction.
The depth requirement
L\geq D
matches the dimension of the complex; for example,
L=2
suffices for triangle meshes.
More generally, increasing depth expands the portion of the Hasse diagram from which coordinate information can be aggregated, which is consistent with the monotonic improvements observed in our depth ablation experiments (Section
6
).

5.2
Approximation on bounded classes via ECT stability

Theorem
4
provides exact computation on finite families.
To extend to
infinite
classes, we rely on a stability result for the ECT under perturbations of the embedding.

George et al. 2025
define a distance between the ECTs of two embeddings
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
of the same abstract complex
K
, i.e.,

d_{\mathrm{ECT}}\bigl(\mathrm{ECT}(K,\mathbf{x}),\,\mathrm{ECT}(K,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\bigr):=\int_{\nu\in S^{d-1}}\|\mathrm{ECC}_{\nu}(K,\mathbf{x})-\mathrm{ECC}_{\nu}(K,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\|_{1}\,d\nu,

(6)

and prove the following bound.

Theorem 5

(
George et al. 2025
)
.

Let
K
be a finite abstract simplicial complex and let
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}:\mathrm{vert}(K)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be two embeddings. Then

d_{\mathrm{ECT}}\bigl(\mathrm{ECT}(K,\mathbf{x}),\,\mathrm{ECT}(K,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\bigr)\;\leq\;2\,C_{K}\,C_{d}\sum_{v\in\mathrm{vert}(K)}\|\mathbf{x}(v)-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}(v)\|_{2},

where
C_{K}
depends on the combinatorial structure of
K
and
C_{d}
depends on the ambient dimension
d
.

This yields a clean approximation result under the following boundedness assumption, which is readily satisfied by essentially any real-world dataset.

Assumption 1

(Bounded embeddings)
.

Fix a finite abstract simplicial complex
K
and a class
\mathcal{C}
of embeddings
\mathbf{x}:\mathrm{vert}(K)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}
has image in a common compact set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The realizability construction of Theorem
3
encodes GSWL colors as distinct basis vectors, which is injective but not continuous in the vertex coordinates.
To leverage the stability theorem, we therefore consider a variant architecture with a vertex-coordinate skip channel,

h^{(\ell+1)}_{v}=\bigl[\,\mathbf{x}_{v}\,\big\|\,g^{(\ell+1)}_{v}\,\bigr],

(7)

where
g^{(\ell+1)}_{v}
is produced by (
4
). This preserves
\mathbf{x}_{v}
throughout all layers, so the map
h^{(L)}_{v}\mapsto\mathbf{x}_{v}
is simply a projection onto the first
d
coordinates and therefore continuous.

Theorem 6

(Approximation of the full ECT)
.

Adopt Assumption
1
and use the skip-channel architecture (
7
) with
L\geq\dim(K)
. We then have:

(a)

Exact recovery.
The linear projection
R\colon\mathbb{R}^{d+H^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
onto the first
d
coordinates yields
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{v}:=R(h^{(L)}_{v})=\mathbf{x}_{v}
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}
, and hence
d_{\mathrm{ECT}}\bigl(\mathrm{ECT}(K,\mathbf{x}),\mathrm{ECT}(K,\hat{\mathbf{x}})\bigr)=0
.

(b)

MLP recovery.
If
R
is required to lie in an MLP class with the universal approximation property, then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
R
such that
d_{\mathrm{ECT}}\bigl(\mathrm{ECT}(K,\mathbf{x}),\mathrm{ECT}(K,\hat{\mathbf{x}})\bigr)<\varepsilon
uniformly over
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}
.

This result extends Theorem
4
in two directions: It applies to infinite (bounded) embedding classes and approximates the full ECT function, rather than a finite sampled grid. The extension relies on the continuous coordinate-recovery property ensured by the skip-channel; without continuity, the stability bound cannot be applied.
As a consequence, we can show under which conditions distinct embeddings can be separated.

Corollary 1

(Separation of embeddings)
.

Adopt Assumption
1
and suppose the ECT is injective on
\mathcal{C}
with

\inf_{\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}}d_{\mathrm{ECT}}\bigl(\mathrm{ECT}(K,\mathbf{x}),\mathrm{ECT}(K,\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\bigr)\;=:\;\gamma>0
.
Then for every
\varepsilon<\gamma/2
, the readout
R
from Theorem
6
produces
\mathrm{ECT}(K,\hat{\mathbf{x}})
that distinguishes distinct embeddings.
Namely,
\mathrm{ECT}(K,\hat{\mathbf{x}})\neq\mathrm{ECT}(K,\hat{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}})
if
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
.

Positive separation is given for any finite
\mathcal{C}
on which the ECT is injective.
For infinite
\mathcal{C}
, it is a substantive margin assumption, which does
not
follow from compactness and injectivity, since a continuous injective map on a non-discrete compact set typically has infimum-zero pairwise distances along sequences
\mathbf{x}_{n}\to\mathbf{x}
.
Sufficient conditions therefore require either
\mathcal{C}
to be discrete (e.g., a finite collection or a fixed
\delta
-net of representative embeddings) or an explicit margin to be imposed.

Remark 2
.

Assumption
1
fixes a single abstract complex
K
because the stability theorem (Theorem
5
) only bounds ECT distance between embeddings of the same
K
.
For a class of embedded complexes with varying abstract structure, Theorem
6
can be applied within each abstract-complex orbit, getting an approximation on each orbit.
Since the ECT is a complete invariant, distinct embedded complexes are always separated in
d_{\mathrm{ECT}}
, whether they differ in abstract structure or only in embedding.
Choosing the per-orbit approximation error
\varepsilon
smaller than half the minimum ECT gap across the full mixed class then results in separation throughout.

Sanity checks and generalization

Having discussed the necessity of all components, we perform several “sanity checks” and generalization experiments.
Concerning

[

This dataset is notable for its absence of geometrical information; and indeed, we observe that both combinatorial and geometry-aware SMP achieve an accuracy of about

6.5
Limitations

We identify some limitations in our experiments.
First, we find that all synthetic experiments saturate at nigh-perfect accuracy or near-zero MSE, which is partially due to the dataset size.
Thus, these experiments prove the existence of an expressivity gap but do not constitute a stress test of our architecture on highly-challenging tasks.
Our experiments on FAUST provide a more meaningful benchmark, even though the dataset size limits the statistical power.
Moreover, our completeness argument hinges on the availability of sufficiently many directions and thresholds for the ECT.
For finite complexes, the ECT is a constructible function with finitely many critical values, so a finite grid does suffice in principle.
Finally, our baselines could also incorporate other point cloud classification models
[
23
,
30
]
. However, our main focus lies on
characterizing expressivity
, and we find that
our baselines already help in isolating the contribution of higher-order simplices, and support our claims.

7
Conclusion

We introduce the
Geometric Simplicial Weisfeiler–Lehman
(GSWL) test, a color refinement procedure for embedded simplicial complexes, and establish its relationship to geometry-aware simplicial message-passing architectures.
Our proposed architecture is bounded above by GSWL (Theorem
2
), and matches GSWL exactly on finite families (Theorem
3
). In fact, we can show that
all
such message-passing schemes are bounded above by GSWL, and the class admits parameter choices that match GSWL exactly on any finite family.
Combining this with simplex-wise coordinate recovery yields exact computation of sampled ECT values (Theorem
4
), i.e., by relying solely on local message passing, we can reproduce a complete geometric invariant.
A recent result
[
11
]
further extends this to
\varepsilon
-approximation of the full ECT on bounded classes (Theorem
6
).
Since the ECT is
complete
for embedded simplicial complexes, our results provide a
geometric expressivity characterization
analogous to the WL–GNN equivalence for graphs.
We verify this theoretically and experimentally, observing that
on datasets with shared abstract connectivity, combinatorial models perform provably worse, whereas our geometry-aware model succeeds.
Coboundary ablations confirm that higher-dimensional information flow is necessary for tasks where geometric matters.
As for future work, it would be interesting to extend the framework to function-valued ECT recovery (i.e., outputting
\mathrm{ECT}(K)(v,t)
for an arbitrary query
(v,t)
) and higher-dimensional complexes.
