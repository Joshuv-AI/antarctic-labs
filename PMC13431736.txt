Presbyopic Glaucoma Patients Exhibit Greater Contact Lens Under-Correction Than Non-Glaucomatous Subjects
Authors: Masahiko Ayaki, Akiko Hanyuda, Kazuno Negishi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivePrevious studies have suggested that presbyopia may progress faster in patients with glaucoma. This study aimed to compare glaucoma patients and controls to assess the severity of presbyopia in terms of the preference for contact lens (CL) correction.MethodsWe compared 457 glaucoma patients and 500 controls aged 40–69 years, assessing near add power, CL-corrected visual acuity, under-correction with CL (CL power- full correction power), and glaucoma and dry eye-related parameters. Regression analyses were used to evaluate the relationship between the near-add power and ocular parameters. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis estimated the age at which the near add power reached +3.00 D.ResultsThe mean near add power was 2.27 ± 0.84 D in the control group and 2.47 ± 0.71 D in the glaucoma group (p < 0.01). The CL-corrected visual acuity (LogMAR) was 0.02 ± 0.10 and 0.04 ± 0.11 (p < 0.01), and the under-correction was 0.28 ± 0.69 D and 0.43 ± 0.94 D (p < 0.01), respectively. The glaucoma group showed poorer dry eye-related parameters, and under-correction was associated with myopia, anisometropia, near-add power, lower Schirmer test values, and superficial punctate keratopathy. Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed that glaucoma patients reached +3.00 D near add power significantly earlier than the controls (P<0.01).ConclusionPatients with glaucoma preferred more under-corrected CLs than controls. Our results further confirmed the presence of severe presbyopia in individuals with glaucoma.

Introduction

Glaucoma is a chronic optic neuropathy characterized by progressive retinal ganglion cell loss and visual field deterioration.1 Medical treatment has proven effective for reducing pressure and slowing visual field decline,2,3 with recent advances in prostanoid analogues4 and fixed combinations improving patients’ adherence and treatment outcomes.5 However, the ocular surface side effects of these treatments are sometimes significant enough to necessitate changes or discontinuation of medications.6–8

Glaucoma patients face unique challenges when using contact lenses (CLs), as many experience ocular surface problems, despite being myopic and CL users.9,10 Glaucoma medications significantly affect CL use because many eye drops contain preservatives such as benzalkonium chloride, which can cause ocular surface toxicity leading to dry eye, tear film instability, and superficial punctate keratitis.6–8 Tear film instability can affect the regularity of the anterior ocular surface and may influence corneal refractive power.11 Additionally, the use of contact lenses significantly affects ocular surface conditions and constitutes a confounding factor impacting the study’s results.12

Presbyopia is an age-related decline in accommodative ability that affects near vision and quality of life in aging populations.13,14 While its progression is primarily attributed to lenticular sclerosis and ciliary muscle dysfunction,15,16 emerging evidence suggests that ocular comorbidities may influence its onset and severity.17,18 The acceleration amplitude begins to decline in early adulthood,19 but presbyopic symptoms typically emerge in the 40s and worsen over time.20

Despite the high prevalence of both glaucoma and presbyopia in the elderly and their shared pathophysiological sites, including the ciliary body, ciliary muscles, trabecular meshwork, and uveoscleral outflow,21 the relationship between these two conditions remains underexplored. The impact of glaucoma on near vision correction, including retinal nerve fiber thinning, visual field loss, and ocular surface alterations, has not been fully elucidated.17 Understanding this relationship is critical for optimizing optical correction strategies in glaucoma patients who often face challenges, such as decreased reading speed and central sensitivity.22–24

Reduced contrast sensitivity, localized visual field impairment, and ocular surface alterations caused by long-term topical medications may increase the need for stronger near additions in patients with glaucoma to achieve adequate reading performance. Their CL selection may be also influenced by disease-related factors to minimize ocular surface irritation, enhance tear film stability, and provide consistent optical quality to compensate for functional visual deficits. In contrast, control subjects typically base their near-addition and CL choices simply on refractive status and presbyopic progression, without the additional constraints imposed by glaucomatous visual dysfunction.

This study aimed to investigate the differences in near add power, CL power, and related ocular parameters between glaucoma patients and non-glaucoma controls. Since CL-corrected visual acuity reflects preferred vision in daily life, it is presumed that patients with presbyopic glaucoma may use under-corrected CL powers adapted to the progression of presbyopia. Furthermore, we sought to identify clinical factors associated with increased near add power and under-correction of CLs in glaucoma patients, with particular attention to dry eye and the severity and medication use of glaucoma.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

This clinic-based, cross-sectional study investigated the prevalence of specific ophthalmological conditions among healthy individuals attending the Otake Eye Clinic in Kanagawa, Japan. The Institutional Review Board and Ethics Committee of the Kanagawa Medical Association approved the study (approval date, 12 November, 2018; permission number, krec2059006), which was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The need for consent was waived by the Institutional Review Board of Kanagawa Medical Association since this study was conducted in an opt-out manner, meaning that participants were informed and could choose not to participate. Information about this clinical study was disclosed on the website of the ophthalmology clinic as part of the opt-out process. The notice stated that the data were de-identified before analysis and any patients could decline participation if they did not wish to participate. The study involved a retrospective chart review of consecutive patients visiting the clinic from December 1, 2018, to October 31, 2024. The Institutional Review Board and Ethics Committee of Keio University School of Medicine approved this study (approval date, 31 May, 2024; approval number, 20241019) to permit authorship of authors (KN, AH, and MA) who were appointed at the Keio University School of Medicine. The protocol was registered in the UMIN Clinical Trials Registry (UMIN000051891) on 15 August, 2023.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

We included participants aged 40–69 years with bilateral phakic eyes and best-corrected visual acuity above 20/30 who were visiting for CL prescriptions of daily disposable soft CL, biweekly disposable soft CL, and rigid gas-permeable hard CL. The exclusion criteria were a history of corneal or intra-ocular surgeries, including ocular laser treatment, glaucoma surgery, refractive or cataract surgeries, and moderate-to-severe cataract.

Ophthalmological Examinations and Diagnosis of Glaucoma

The participants underwent a comprehensive ophthalmologic evaluation, including best-corrected visual acuity, slit-lamp examination, intraocular pressure measurement, fundus examination, visual acuity corrected with CL, and standard automated perimetry using the Humphrey Visual Field Analyzer Swedish Interactive Threshold Algorithm–Standard 24–2 program (Carl Zeiss Meditec, Dublin, CA, USA). Patients with cataracts or other non-glaucomatous ocular conditions that could cause visual field defects were excluded following basic eye examination. Glaucoma was diagnosed when at least two reliable visual field tests confirmed glaucomatous visual field defects consistent with glaucomatous optic disc changes. Open angle glaucoma and normal tension glaucoma were included.

Binocular near-add power was measured at a distance of 30 cm using a Bankoku near-acuity chart (Handaya Inc., Tokyo, Japan). The test is designed to quantitatively assess near visual acuity. It presents standardized optotypes at a fixed reading distance at 30 cm, allowing measurement of the visual acuity and representation in decimal acuity. After determining the distance refractive correction of the patient, the minimal additional power required to achieve near acuity above 20/25 was measured in 0.25 D increments and recorded as near-add power. The prevalence of near add power of 1.50 D or more and 3.00D or more was calculated by analyzing the distribution of near add power measurements among the study participants. Undercorrection was calculated as (spherical equivalent of prescribed CL – spherical equivalent of full correction with spectacles). The prescribed CL power was converted into a spectacle prescription, assuming a vertex distance of 12 mm.

Dry eye-related examinations included the Schirmer test, tear break-up time, and corneal staining. The Schirmer test was performed by placing a standardized filter paper strip (Ayumi Pharmaceutical, Tokyo, Japan) in the lower conjunctival fornix for 5 minutes without anesthesia. Tear volume is assessed by the length of moisture migration along the strip, providing an objective indicator of lacrimal gland function. Tear break-up time was measured using a fluorescein filter paper strip (Ayumi Pharmaceutical, Tokyo, Japan) wetted with saline with excess flicked off and applied at the lower lid margin. The tear break-up time was defined as the time interval between the third blink and the appearance of the first dark spot on the cornea. This value was calculated from the mean of three measurements. Positive corneal staining appeared one minute after ocular administration of fluorescein dye and was observed with a yellow barrier filter under cobalt blue illumination of the slit lamp.

Optical Coherence Tomography

Optical coherence tomography (RS-3000, software version 1.4.2.1; Nidek, Aichi, Japan) was used to measure the macular retinal nerve fiber layer (mRNFL), ganglion cell layer (GCL), inner plexiform layer (IPL) (GCL/IPL), mRNFL+GCL+IPL [GCC], and full macula from macular cube scans of a fovea-centered 6×6 mm square area. For peripapillary RNFL imaging, raster scanning over a 6×6 mm area centered on the optic disc center was conducted at a scan density of 512 A-scans (horizontal) × 128 B-scans (vertical). Peripapillary RNFL measurements were obtained using a 3.45 mm diameter circle that was automatically centered around the optic disc. The 12 RNFL sectors (each covering 30°) were numbered starting from the 1 o’clock position.

Statistical Analysis

Participants were classified into glaucoma and control groups based on the diagnostic criteria. Participant demographics and ophthalmological parameters are presented as the mean ± standard deviation for continuous variables and as percentages for categorical variables for each group. t-tests and chi-squared tests were used to compare parameters between the two groups, as appropriate. To explore possible ophthalmic parameters associated with near-add power and under-correction of CL, Spearman correlation analyses were conducted for each of the two groups, aiming of identifying potential predictive factors. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was applied to compare the age distribution at which a near add power of +3.00 D was reached. Age was used as the time scale, and the event was defined as reaching a near add power of +3.00 D. Subjects who had not reached +3.00 D at the time of examination were considered censored at their current age. The Log rank test was used to compare glaucoma patients and controls. Because accommodative amplitude declines from early childhood, a near add power threshold of +3.00 D was set as the endpoint representing a clinically stable stage of presbyopia. All analyses were performed using StatFlex (Atech, Osaka, Japan), with a two-sided P-value of < 0.05 (two-sided) considered to indicate a significant difference.

There were no significant differences in age or astigmatism between the control and glaucoma groups. There were significant differences in spherical equivalent, anisometropia, Schirmer test value, tear break-up time, and superficial punctate keratopathy between the two groups. Near add power, the prevalence of near add power ≥ 1.50D and 3.00D, under-correction of CL, and visual acuity with CL (LogMAR) were greater in the glaucoma group than in the control group (Table 1). The proportion of multifocal CL users did not differ between groups. All parameters were worse in glaucoma-related clinical features, and intra-ocular pressure was lower in the glaucoma group. The use of dry eye medication was more prevalent in the glaucoma group.

Regression analysis of near add power and ocular parameters in the glaucoma group indicated that age and the use of dry eye medication were significantly associated with near add power (Table 2). Regression analysis of under-correction and ocular parameters in the glaucoma group indicated a significant association between under-correction and spherical equivalent, anisometropia, near add power, mean deviation, GCC thickness, Schirmer test value, superficial punctate keratitis, and use of dry eye medication (Table 3 and Figure 1). Among the categorized glaucoma medications, there were no associations with near-add power or under-correction adjusted for age and sex (Tables 2 and 3). To improve clarity, the results were categorized into findings related to presbyopia severity and to CL under-correction. Presbyopia-related findings showed that near add power and a prevalence of near add power ≥1.50 D and ≥3.00 D were higher in the glaucoma group, while also reaching +3.00 D near add power earlier than controls. In contrast, CL-related findings showed greater under-correction in the glaucoma group, which was associated with refractive status, near add power, glaucoma-related parameters, and ocular surface-related parameters. This structured presentation highlights the novelty of our findings: glaucoma patients showed both more severe presbyopia and a greater preference for under-corrected CL prescriptions.

Discussion

The present results suggest that glaucoma patients prefer under-correction of CLs compared with controls, which could provide additional evidence for accommodation decline in glaucoma patients. This study also confirmed that glaucoma patients exhibit significantly higher near add power, consistent with previous research18 stating that glaucoma was associated with higher near add power. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis reinforced that patients with glaucoma appeared to reach higher add-power levels at younger ages within this sample. The use of dry eye medication was associated with near add power, suggesting that ocular surface disease may contribute to near vision impairment in glaucoma patients. Dry eye pathology may exacerbate near vision difficulties, possibly by degrading optical quality or inducing accommodative micro-fluctuation.25,26 No specific glaucoma medication category was associated with under-correction or near add power, suggesting that the disease burden and ocular surface status, rather than drug class, are the primary drivers of visual compromise. In the glaucoma group, near add power was directly linked to under-correction of the CL, indicating that glaucoma patients may compensate for focusing difficulty by using under-corrected CL. Under-correction was associated with dry eye-related indicators, suggesting that presbyopic symptoms in glaucoma patients may be exacerbated by ocular surface conditions.

The results of regression analysis suggest anisometropic patients may accept under-correction to manage binocular balance rather than for presbyopia related reasons. Previous investigations have indicated that near add power increases with thinner GCC,27 whereas our results indicate greater under-correction with thicker GCC. This apparent conflict is further complicated by a large study with >4000 samples28 describing the association of eye strain with thick GCC and blurring with thin GCC. Near-add power is a measurable value, whereas under-correction is a personal preference, influenced by lifestyle, job, working distance, and other individual factors, rather than physiological status. Therefore, the results of this study should be carefully interpreted. Nevertheless, the present study clearly demonstrated that patients with glaucoma prefer undercorrected CL power. CL-corrected visual acuity reflects habitual visual performance in presbyopic individuals, and may serve as an indicator of visual function in older adults. Glaucoma patients may be unaware of worsened near add power and near vision compared with individuals without glaucoma, attributing their focusing difficulty to aging or glaucoma itself.

The present findings emphasize the importance of individualized near vision correction strategies in glaucoma patients, particularly in those with dry eye symptoms or advanced optic nerve damage. The strengths of this study are as follows: A large sample size provides strong statistical power and reduces the risk of random errors. Direct comparison between individuals with and without glaucoma allows for the clear identification of differences in presbyopia progression and CL preferences. Multiple outcome measures, including near add power, CL-corrected visual acuity, under-correction with CL, and dry eye-related parameters, strengthened the validity of the findings. This study links presbyopia severity in glaucoma patients with clinical practice and the findings are directly applicable to optometrists and ophthalmologists managing glaucoma patients.

This study had several limitations. The participants were CL users, and most of them had myopia and dry eye; therefore, caution should be exercised when applying these results to the general population. This cross-sectional comparative study could not establish causality or track the longitudinal progression of the presbyopia. Near-add power is an indirect measure of presbyopia severity and may not fully capture near-vision performance in daily life. This study relies on patients’ self-reported adherence to CL wear. Future studies should include objective or quantitative examinations to validate these findings. Systemic comorbidities were considered and adjusted in the regression analyses as potential confounding variables. Lifestyle, hobbies, sports, jobs, and the usual working distance of participants could affect their preference for CL-corrected visual acuity and should be included in future studies. The Kaplan-Meier analysis estimating time to +3.00 D near add power assumes uniform progression, potentially oversimplifying individual variability. The heterogeneity of glaucoma severity was not adjusted, although it may have distinct effects on the progression of presbyopia.

In conclusion, patients with glaucoma preferred more under-corrected CL prescriptions than controls. In the control group, under-correction was also associated with near add power and ocular surface-related parameters, indicating that the observed CL prescription preference is not specific to glaucoma but is amplified in patients with glaucoma. The greater magnitude of under-correction and higher near add power in the glaucoma group suggest that presbyopic symptoms in patients with glaucoma may be further exacerbated by ocular surface conditions. As CL-corrected visual acuity reflects preferred vision in daily life, it is presumed that glaucoma patients use CL powers adapted to the progression of presbyopia. The present study further confirms the considerable severity of presbyopia in individuals with glaucoma while acknowledging the parallel findings observed in the control group. These findings may inform tailored CL prescriptions and presbyopia management in glaucoma patients by emphasizing near add power, ocular surface status, and tolerance of under-correction.
