1.
Introduction

Let
\sigma
be the normalised surface measure on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}
in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For a Schwartz function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
, we define the spherical averages

\displaystyle A_{r}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}}f(x+ry)\sigma(dy),

and if
k=0,1,2,\ldots
, we write

\displaystyle A_{r}^{(k)}f(x)

\displaystyle=r^{k}(\tfrac{d}{dr})^{k}A_{r}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}}r^{k}(\tfrac{d}{dr})^{k}f(x+ry)\sigma(dy).

The corresponding maximal function is then defined as

\displaystyle A_{*}^{(k)}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sup\{\lvert A_{r}^{(k)}f(x)\rvert:r\in(0,\infty)\}.

The classical spherical maximal function, introduced by Stein in
[
21
]
, corresponds to
k=0
. For
k>0
,
A_{r}^{(k)}f
can be expressed in terms of Stein’s generalised spherical means, also studied in
[
21
]
, and the maximal inequality for the latter easily leads to the following result.

Theorem I

(a variant of Stein’s maximal inequality)
.

Suppose that

(1)

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=0,1,2,\ldots,\qquad d\geqslant 2k+3,\qquad\frac{d}{d-k-1}<p<\frac{d-2}{k},

with the convention that
(d-2)/k=\infty
if
k=0
. Then
A_{\smash{*}}^{(k)}
satisfies the strong type inequality

(2)

\displaystyle\lVert A_{*}^{(k)}f\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{k,p}\lVert f\rVert_{p},

with a constant
C_{k,p}
that is independent of the dimension
d
. Consequently,
A_{\smash{r}}^{(k)}
and
A_{\smash{*}}^{(k)}
extend continuously to all of
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Although the maximal inequality for spherical means
A_{r}f
(Theorem 1 in
[
21
]
) is well known, the estimate for Stein’s generalised spherical means (Theorem 2 therein) is much less familiar. The aim of this note is to present this latter result in a more accessible form and to draw broader attention to it.

Our interest in derivatives of the spherical means
A_{r}f
and the associated maximal functions
A_{\smash{*}}^{(k)}f
stems from the study of maximal inequalities for truncated Riesz transforms in
[
10
]
. In the companion paper
[
13
]
, Theorem
I
leads to a more direct proof of the dimension-free estimate of Kucharski, Wróbel and Zienkiewicz from
[
12
]
, which improved special cases covered in
[
11
,
14
]
, and a dimension-dependent inequality from
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
.

First order derivatives
A_{\smash{r}}^{(1)}=r\tfrac{d}{dr}A_{r}
appear naturally in the study of the spherical maximal operator
A_{\smash{*}}^{(0)}
, also on spaces more general than
\mathbb{R}^{d}
; see, for example, Section 6.1 in
[
1
]
by Bagchi, Hait, Roncal and Thangavelu on spherical means on the Heisenberg group. Other results partially related to the estimates of
A_{\smash{r}}^{(1)}
include bounds for the
p
-variation of
r\mapsto A_{r}f
by Jones, Seeger and Wright in
[
9
]
, and Beltran, Oberlin, Roncal, Seeger and Stovall in
[
2
]
.

In
[
3
]
, Bourgain proved the maximal estimate for spherical means
A_{r}f
when
d=2
and
p>2
. For a simplified argument and further discussion, we refer to
[
20
]
by Mockenhaupt, Seeger and Sogge.

The left endpoint
p=d/(d-k-1)
in (
1
) is best possible, as can be seen by considering any nonnegative and nonzero
f
. On the other hand, the right endpoint is closely related to the regularity of solutions of the wave equation and Sogge’s local smoothing conjecture. Miao, Yang and Zheng in
[
19
]
observed that Bourgain and Demeter’s decoupling theorem from
[
4
]
extends the range of the maximal inequality for Stein’s generalised spherical means. When
d\geqslant 2k+3
, this improves the right endpoint in Theorem
I
to
p=(d-1)/k
, which is shown to be optimal by considering
f
equal to mollifications of
\sigma
; a detailed discussion and further results are given by Liu, Shen, Song and Yan in
[
15
]
.

The above extensions of Stein’s estimates lead to the following more general variant of Theorem
I
. This result is best possible when either
d\geqslant 2k+3
, or
k=0
and
d=2
. The only range of parameters for which the validity of the maximal inequality (
2
) remains unknown is thus
k\geqslant 1
,
d=2k+2
, and
2<p<2+\tfrac{1}{k}
.

Theorem II

(extension due to Bourgain, Demeter, Miao, Yang, Zheng)
.

The maximal estimate (
2
) holds under the more general condition:

(3)

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=1,2,\ldots,\qquad d\geqslant 2k+3,\qquad\frac{d}{d-k-1}<p<\frac{d-1}{k};

or

(4)

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=0,\qquad d\geqslant 2,\qquad\frac{d}{d-1}<p.

The constants in Stein’s original result, as well as its extensions discussed above, depend on the dimension. However, Stein observed in
[
23
]
that maximal estimates for certain operators improve with the dimension. While
[
23
]
, as well as the detailed proof given by Stein and Strömberg in
[
24
]
, focused on the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function, the inequality for the spherical maximal function
A_{\smash{*}}^{(0)}f
with constants independent from the dimension follows by the same argument, and the case of
A_{\smash{*}}^{(k)}f
for an arbitrary
k\geqslant 0
is no different.

Many subsequent works have focused on maximal inequalities for averages over dilations of a given convex body; we refer to the survey by Deleaval, Guédon and Maurey
[
6
]
. A variety of results also exist in other geometrical settings. We do not attempt a full literature review here, and we only mention the recent contribution by Ganguly and Ghosh
[
8
]
for the Heisenberg group, and the survey by Bourgain, Mirek, Stein and Wróbel
[
5
]
for integer lattices.

2.
Details

Below by
C
we denote a generic, typically large, constant. If
C
depends on parameters, we always list them explicitly in the subscript. The value of
C
can change even within a single equation or inequality, so, for example,
C+C=C
.

2.1.
Stein’s generalised spherical averages

In
[
21
]
, Stein introduced a holomorphic family of generalised spherical means
M_{\smash{r}}^{(\alpha)}
of order
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. When
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(that is,
f
is in the Schwartz class of smooth, rapidly decaying functions),
r>0
and
\re\alpha>0
, then

(5)

\displaystyle M_{r}^{(\alpha)}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{B}}f(x+ry)(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{\alpha-1}dy.

Note that
M_{\smash{r}}^{(\alpha)}
is usually
not
a true averaging operator: we have
M_{\smash{r}}^{(\alpha)}f(x)=\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(\tfrac{d}{2}+\alpha)f(x)
when
f
is constant on
B(x,r)
, the ball of radius
r
centred at
x
.

If we denote by
\xi x
the usual dot product in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, by
\mathscr{F}
the Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}f(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\xi x}f(x)dx,

and by
J_{\nu}
the Bessel function of the first kind, then (see p. 171 in
[
25
]
)

(6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}M_{r}^{(\alpha)}f(\xi)

\displaystyle=2^{d/2+\alpha-1}\pi^{d/2}\lvert r\xi\rvert^{1-d/2-\alpha}J_{d/2+\alpha-1}(\lvert r\xi\rvert)\mathscr{F}f(\xi).

Here, again,
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
r>0
and
\re\alpha>0
. However, the expression
t^{-\nu}J_{\nu}(t)
is an entire function of
\nu\in\mathbb{C}
for every
t>0
, and a bounded function of
t>0
for every
\nu\in\mathbb{C}
(see
Eq. 10.2.2
in
[
27
]
). Therefore, equation (
6
) defines a smooth function
M_{\smash{r}}^{(\alpha)}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
.

2.2.
Connection with derivatives of spherical means

By the differentiation rule for the Bessel function (
Eq. 10.6.6
in
[
27
]
):

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigl[t^{\nu}J_{\nu}(t)\bigr]

\displaystyle=t^{\nu}J_{\nu-1}(t),

we have, for
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
r>0
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\bigl[r^{d+2\alpha-2}\mathscr{F}M_{r}^{(\alpha)}f(\xi)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{d/2+\alpha-1}\pi^{d/2}}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{d+2\alpha-2}}\,\frac{d}{dr}\biggl[\lvert r\xi\rvert^{d/2+\alpha-1}J_{d/2+\alpha-1}(\lvert r\xi\rvert)\mathscr{F}f(\xi)\biggr]

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{d/2+\alpha-1}\pi^{d/2}}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{d+2\alpha-3}}\,\lvert r\xi\rvert^{d/2+\alpha-1}J_{d/2+\alpha-2}(\lvert r\xi\rvert)\mathscr{F}f(\xi)

\displaystyle=2r^{d+2\alpha-3}\mathscr{F}M_{r}^{(\alpha-1)}f(\xi).

Applying the inverse Fourier transform, we get, by the dominated convergence theorem,

(7)

\displaystyle M_{r}^{(\alpha-1)}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2r^{d+2\alpha-3}}\,\frac{d}{dr}\bigl[r^{d+2\alpha-2}M_{r}^{(\alpha)}f(x)\bigr].

Observe that, by (
5
) and integration in spherical coordinates, we have

\displaystyle r^{d}M_{r}^{(1)}f(x)

\displaystyle=r^{d}\int_{\mathbb{B}}f(x+ry)dy=\int_{B(x,r)}f(z)dz=\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(\tfrac{d}{2})}\int_{0}^{r}t^{d-1}A_{t}f(x)dt.

Combining this with (
7
) for
\alpha=1
, we find that

(8)

\displaystyle M_{r}^{(0)}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2r^{d-1}}\,\frac{d}{dr}\bigl[r^{d}M_{r}^{(1)}f(x)\bigr]=\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(\tfrac{d}{2})}\,A_{r}f(x)

(which also follows from (
6
) and the expression for the Fourier transform of
\sigma
, see p. 154 in
[
25
]
). Iterating (
7
) for
\alpha=0,-1,\ldots,-k+1
, and combining the result with (
8
), we find that

\displaystyle M_{r}^{(-k)}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}r^{d-2k-2}}\,\biggl(\frac{1}{r}\,\frac{d}{dr}\biggr)^{k}\bigl[r^{d-2}M_{r}^{(0)}f(x)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{2^{k}\Gamma(\tfrac{d}{2})r^{d-2k-2}}\,\biggl(\frac{1}{r}\,\frac{d}{dr}\biggr)^{k}\bigl[r^{d-2}A_{r}f(x)\bigr].

If we expand the derivatives on the right-hand side and use
A_{r}^{(j)}f(x)=r^{j}(\tfrac{d}{dr})^{j}A_{r}f(x)
, we get

\displaystyle M_{r}^{(-k)}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{2^{k}\Gamma(\tfrac{d}{2})}\biggl(A_{r}^{(k)}f(x)+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}C_{d,k,j}A_{r}^{(j)}f(x)\biggr).

Solving this triangular system of linear equations for
r^{k}A_{\smash{r}}^{(k)}f(x)
leads to

(9)

\displaystyle A_{r}^{(k)}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{k}\Gamma(\tfrac{d}{2})}{\pi^{d/2}}\biggl(M_{r}^{(-k)}f(x)+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}C_{d,k,j}M_{r}^{(-j)}f(x)\biggr).

2.3.
Maximal inequality for Stein’s generalised spherical averages

Theorem 2 in
[
21
]
states that for every
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, the maximal function

\displaystyle M_{*}^{(\alpha)}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sup\{\lvert M_{r}^{(\alpha)}f(x):r\in(0,\infty)\}

satisfies the strong type inequality on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
:

(10)

\displaystyle\lVert M_{*}^{(\alpha)}f\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{d,\alpha,p}\lVert f\rVert_{p}

whenever

(11)

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle\geqslant 3,\qquad\frac{d}{d+\alpha-1}<p<\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{d-2}{-\alpha}&&\text{if }\alpha<0,\\
&\infty&&\text{if }\alpha\geqslant 0.\end{aligned}\right.

In
[
3
]
Bourgain proved (
10
) for
d=2
and
p>2
. Using the decoupling theorem of Bourgain and Demeter from
[
4
]
, Miao, Yang and Zheng in
[
19
]
extended the range further to

(12)

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle\geqslant 2,\qquad\frac{d}{d+\alpha-1}<p<\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{2d-6}{-4\alpha+1-d}&&\text{if }\alpha<-\dfrac{(d-1)^{2}}{2d+2},\\
&\frac{d-1}{-\alpha}&&\text{if }-\dfrac{(d-1)^{2}}{2d+2}\leqslant\alpha<0,\\
&\infty&&\text{if $\alpha\geqslant 0$.}\end{aligned}\right.

It is a challenging open problem to determine the optimal range of
p
for which (
10
) holds.

2.4.
Maximal inequality for derivatives of spherical averages

The relation (
9
) between the derivatives
A_{\smash{r}}^{(k)}
of spherical averages and Stein’s generalised spherical averages
M_{\smash{r}}^{(\alpha)}
, and the maximal inequality (
10
) for the latter, applied with
\alpha=0,-1,-2,\ldots,-k
, immediately lead to the maximal inequality (
2
) given in Theorem
I
, but with a constant
C_{d,k,p}
that depends also on the dimension. The range of admissible parameters
d,k,p
given in (
1
) follows directly from condition (
11
) with
\alpha=-k
, as the condition then also holds for
\alpha=0,-1,-2,\ldots,-k
.

We now argue that the extension described in Theorem
II
follows if we use (
12
) instead of (
11
). If (
4
) holds, that is, if
k=0
,
d=2
and
p>2
, we clearly have (
12
) with
\alpha=-k=0
, as desired. Suppose now that (
3
) is satisfied, that is,

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\geqslant 1,\qquad d\geqslant 2k+3,\qquad\frac{d}{d-k-1}<p<\frac{d-1}{k}.

We claim that condition (
12
) holds with
\alpha=0,-1,-2,\ldots,-k
.

Fix
j=0,1,\ldots,k
. Clearly,

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle>\frac{d}{d-k-1}\geqslant\frac{d}{d-j-1}=\frac{d}{d+\alpha-1},

which is the lower bound of (
12
). Since
d\geqslant 2k+3
and
(d-3)(d+1)\leqslant(d-1)^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle-\alpha

\displaystyle=j\leqslant k\leqslant\frac{d-3}{2}\leqslant\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{2d+2},

so that the upper bound of (
12
) follows from

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle<\frac{d-1}{k}\leqslant\frac{d-1}{j}=\frac{d-1}{-\alpha}.

This shows our claim, and hence completes the proof of Theorem
II
, but with a constant
C_{d,k,p}
in the maximal inequality (
2
) that depends on the dimension.

2.5.
Independence of the dimension

In order to prove that the constant in the maximal inequality (
2
) in Theorems
I
and
II
can be chosen independently of the dimension
d
, one adapts in a straightforward way Stein’s argument from
[
23
]
, described thoroughly in
[
24
]
. The key observation is that
A_{r}f(x)
can be evaluated by averaging lower dimensional spherical means of
f
over all hyperplanes which pass through
x
. For completeness, we provide the details.

We now make the dimension explicit in the notation and write
\mathbb{S}_{d}
,
\sigma_{d}
,
A_{\smash{\kern 0.5ptd,r}}^{\kern-0.5pt(k)}
and
A_{\smash{\kern 0.5ptd,*}}^{\kern-0.5pt(k)}
instead of
\mathbb{S}
,
\sigma
,
A_{\smash{r}}^{(k)}
and
A_{\smash{*}}^{(k)}
. We denote by
O_{d}
the group of orthogonal
d\times d
matrices, and by
\Sigma_{d}
the normalised Haar measure on
O_{d}
. As in equation (4.1) in
[
24
]
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}_{d}}f(y)\sigma_{d}(dy)

\displaystyle=\int_{O_{d}}\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{S}_{d-1}\times\{0\}}f(Ly)(\sigma_{d-1}\times\delta_{0})(dy)\biggr)\Sigma_{d}(dL),

where
\sigma_{d-1}\times\delta_{0}
is the product measure concentrated on
\mathbb{S}_{d-1}\times\{0\}
. Applying the above identity to
f(x+ry)
instead of
f(y)
leads to

\displaystyle A_{d,r}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{d}}f(x+ry)\sigma_{d}(dy)

\displaystyle=\int_{O_{d}}\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{S}_{d-1}\times\{0\}}f(x+rLy)(\sigma_{d-1}\times\delta_{0})(dy)\biggr)\Sigma_{d}(dL)

\displaystyle=\int_{O_{d}}\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{S}_{d-1}\times\{0\}}f\circ L(L^{-1}x+ry)(\sigma_{d-1}\times\delta_{0})(dy)\biggr)\Sigma_{d}(dL)

\displaystyle=\int_{O_{d}}(A_{d-1,r}\otimes\id)(f\circ L)(L^{-1}x)\Sigma_{d}(dL),

where
(A_{d-1,r}\otimes\id)f(x)
is the operator
A_{d-1,r}
applied to the restriction
f(\cdot,x_{d})
of
f
to the hyperplane
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\{x_{d}\}
that contains
x
, evaluated at
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-1})
. By the dominated convergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle A_{d,r}^{(k)}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{O_{d}}(A_{d-1,r}^{(k)}\otimes\id)(f\circ L)(L^{-1}x)\Sigma_{d}(dL),

and hence

(13)

\displaystyle\lvert A_{d,*}^{(k)}f(x)\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{O_{d}}(A_{d-1,*}^{(k)}\otimes\id)(f\circ L)(L^{-1}x)\Sigma_{d}(dL).

If the maximal inequality on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})
for
A_{\smash{\kern 0.5ptd-1,r}}^{\kern-0.5pt(k)}
holds with a constant
c_{d-1,k,p}
, then, by convexity of the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
norm, tensorisation and invariance of the Lebesgue measure under
L
, we have

(14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \lVert A_{d,*}^{(k)}f\rVert_{p}&\leqslant\int_{O_{d}}\lVert(A_{d-1,*}^{(k)}\otimes\id)(f\circ L)\lVert_{p}\Sigma_{d}(dL)\\
&\leqslant c_{d-1,k,p}\int_{O_{d}}\lVert f\circ L\lVert_{p}\Sigma_{d}(dL)=c_{d-1,k,p}\lVert f\lVert_{p},\end{aligned}

and so the maximal inequality on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
A_{\smash{\kern 0.5ptd,r}}^{\kern-0.5pt(k)}
holds with the same constant
c_{d-1,k,p}
.

2.6.
Fractional orders

Iterating (
7
), we find that if
\alpha=-k+\beta
, where
k=0,1,2,\ldots
and
\beta\in(0,1)
, then

\displaystyle M_{r}^{(\alpha)}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}r^{d-2k+2\beta-2}}\,\biggl(\frac{1}{r}\,\frac{d}{dr}\biggr)^{k}\bigl[r^{d+2\beta-2}M_{r}^{(\beta)}f(x)\bigr].

Using (
5
), integrating in spherical coordinates, and using the dominated convergence theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle M_{r}^{(\alpha)}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}r^{d-2k+2\beta-2}}\,\biggl(\frac{1}{r}\,\frac{d}{dr}\biggr)^{k}\biggl[\frac{r^{d+2\beta-2}}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{\mathbb{B}}f(x+ry)(1-\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{\beta-1}dy\biggr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}r^{d-2k+2\beta-2}}\,\biggl(\frac{1}{r}\,\frac{d}{dr}\biggr)^{k}\biggl[\frac{2\pi^{d/2}r^{d+2\beta-2}}{\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma(\tfrac{d}{2})}\int_{0}^{1}t^{d-1}(1-t^{2})^{\beta-1}A_{rt}f(x)dt\biggr]

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{2^{k-1}\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma(\tfrac{d}{2})}\int_{0}^{1}t^{d-1}(1-t^{2})^{\beta-1}\frac{1}{r^{d-2k+2\beta-2}}\biggl(\frac{1}{r}\,\frac{d}{dr}\biggr)^{k}\bigl[r^{d+2\beta-2}A_{rt}f(x)\bigr]dt.

If we expand the derivatives on the right-hand side, we identify
M_{\smash{r}}^{(\alpha)}f(x)
with a linear combination of fractional derivatives of Erdélyi–Kober type of standard spherical means
A_{r}f(x)
:

\displaystyle M_{r}^{(\alpha)}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}C_{d,\alpha,j}r^{2j}\int_{0}^{1}t^{d+j-1}(1-t^{2})^{\beta-1}A_{rt}^{(j)}f(x)dt.

Note that the argument involving averaging over all orthogonal transformations used in the previous section does not apply directly to
M_{r}^{(\alpha)}
.

2.7.
Vector valued functions

Suppose that
f
takes values in some Banach space
X
of sequences; thus,
f=(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots)
, where
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots
are Borel functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Consider the maximal inequality of Fefferman–Stein type:

(15)

\displaystyle\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert A_{*}^{(k)}f(x)\rVert_{X}^{p}dx\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{d,k,p,X}\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert f(x)\rVert_{X}^{p}dx\biggr)^{1/p},

where we denoted
A_{*}^{(k)}f=(A_{*}^{(k)}f_{1},A_{*}^{(k)}f_{2},\ldots)
. We claim that if (
15
) holds in some dimension
d
, then it also holds in every higher dimension, with the same constant.

To prove this claim, we follow the argument from Section
2.5
. We apply (
13
) to
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots
and use the convexity of the norm on
X
:

\displaystyle\lVert A_{d,*}^{(k)}f(x)\rVert_{X}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{O_{d}}\bigl\lVert(A_{d-1,*}^{(k)}\otimes\id)(f\circ L)(L^{-1}x)\bigr\rVert_{X}\Sigma_{d}(dL).

Hence, denoting

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{X,p}

\displaystyle=\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lVert f(x)\rVert_{X}^{p}dx\biggr)^{1/p}

and repeating the calculation in (
14
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert A_{d,*}^{(k)}f\rVert_{X,p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{O_{d}}\lVert(A_{d-1,*}^{(k)}\otimes\id)(f\circ L)\lVert_{X,p}\Sigma_{d}(dL)

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{d-1,k,p,X}\int_{O_{d}}\lVert f\circ L\lVert_{X,p}\Sigma_{d}(dL)=c_{d-1,k,p,X}\lVert f\lVert_{X,p}.

It follows that
c_{d,k,p,X}\leqslant c_{d-1,k,p,X}
, as claimed.

The above observation was suggested to the author by Błażej Wróbel. The case
k=0
and
X=\ell^{q}
corresponds to the Fefferman–Stein inequality for spherical averages, proved under the assumption that
d\geqslant 3
and
p,q\in(\tfrac{d}{d-1},d)
in
[
7
]
. Extensions to
k>0
are given in the companion paper
[
13
]
.

2.8.
Remark

The proof of the maximal inequality (
10
) for Stein’s generalised spherical averages
M_{\smash{r}}^{(\alpha)}
in
[
21
]
can be easily adapted so that it leads to a constant
C_{d/2+\alpha,p}
that depends on the dimension
d
and the order
\alpha
only through the value of
\tfrac{d}{2}+\alpha
. As this fact does not seem to have important implications, we only sketch the necessary modification of the proof of Theorem 2 in
[
21
]
. Instead of a compactly supported function
\varphi
, we choose
\varphi(x)=\pi^{-d/2}e^{-\lvert x\rvert^{2}}
. Then, we apply Stein’s maximal theorem for symmetric Markovian semigroups (with a constant independent of the dimension thanks to Rota’s martingale trick and the Burkholder–Davies–Gundy inequality; see p. 106 in Section IV.4 in
[
22
]
, or
[
26
]
) instead of the estimate for the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function.
