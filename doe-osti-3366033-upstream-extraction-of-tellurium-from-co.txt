3. Executive Summary:

  A major barrier to increasing CdTe PV module production is the availability of tellurium (Te),
  an element necessary to manufacture CdTe PV cells that currently has a very limited supply.
  Most Te is obtained from ore extracted for copper mining, but the current processing
  methods only extract 3-4% of the available Te in this ore, with the rest being discarded
  during other processing steps. This project aims to increase Te supply by developing an
  innovative extraction method that will recover more Te from the copper ore earlier in the
  process. This method will also extract toxic elements like antimony and arsenic, making the
  copper mining process more environmentally friendly.

  Tellurium is essential for CdTe thin-film PV technology, serving as a key enabling material in
  various compounds – notably CdTe and ZnTe. Currently, about 90% of the global tellurium
  supply originates as a by-product of the copper mineral processing, smelting, and refining
  value chain. However, it is estimated that currently only 3-4% of the tellurium contained in
  the ore ever reaches the market (see Exhibit 1). Tellurium is typically marketed in
  concentrate form (e.g., Cu2Te, TeO2) or 99.99% Te metal. In all cases, it must be purified and
  undergo further refining to meet the standards required by semiconductor manufacturers.

  Current recovery methods extract tellurium from anode slimes generated during the
  electrolytic refining of copper anodes to grade A copper cathodes. Anode slimes are a
  mixture of insoluble elements such as gold, silver, selenium, tellurium, and others. Only 3-5%
  of the tellurium contained in the copper ore remains present in the anode slimes, according
  to a report by the American Smelting and Refining Company [3]. Therefore, current recovery
  rates of tellurium are low and incidental, as no copper plant optimizes its processes to
  maximize the production of this metal. Moreover, since low tellurium revenues do not justify
  investment in tellurium extraction processes, copper processors typically only remove
  tellurium as needed to maximize copper recovery. The proposed process could significantly
  improve tellurium recovery at a modest increase in cost. Deploying this approach at scale
  could also decentralize the tellurium supply chain, which is heavily concentrated in China.
  The project's objective is to extract tellurium at the outset of the copper production value
  chain, starting from copper concentrate. This holds the potential to enhance overall
  tellurium recovery by approximately 50-75% compared to current recovery rates for suitable
  ore bodies. In detail, the goals and critical success factors of the project are the following:
● Goal 1: Demonstrate the replicability of bench scale test results, conducted in batch leaching
  trials, in a continuous leaching circuit for industrially-relevant copper concentrate samples.
  Success Criterion: Elemental extraction values for tellurium, antimony and arsenic meet
  threshold levels established in small scale experiments at reagent consumptions not higher
  than seen from bench scale results. Threshold levels will depend on the concentrate being
  tested.
● Goal 2: Demonstrate the ability to operate the continuous leaching circuit with solution
  recycling at rates that ensure economic viability of the process.
  Success Criterion: Achieving extractions not more than 10% lower compared to the single
  pass leaching process while achieving the required recycling ratio (current estimates put the
  required recycling ratio at least 80%).
● Goal 3: Determine industrially-relevant process parameters for a continuously operating
  leaching circuit, which are usable in future process development and scale up.
  Success Criterion: All relevant data is collected and evaluated to allow for detailed
  engineering.

5. Background:

  The alkaline sulfide leaching of copper concentrates is highly selective for impurity elements in
  the concentrate. The proposed process is described in Exhibit 2.

  Exhibit 2 Proposed Process Concept
  At the core of the process is a series of chemical leaching reactions used to release the desired
  elements. A solution of sodium hydroxide (NaOH) and sodium sulfide (Na 2S) is combined with
  copper concentrate at elevated temperatures. This alkaline environment facilitates the reaction
  between impurities (e.g. arsenic, antimony, and tellurium) and sulfide ions, forming soluble
  compounds. The leaching process works according to the exemplary reactions provided below
  [4,5,14], the exact reaction occurring with the copper concentrates that will be used in the
  project will depend on the mineralogical species present. Preliminary work has indicated that
  tellurium can be present as Bi2Te3, PbTe or as Ag2Te for example, among others [19].
  Leaching of arsenic in alkaline sulfide medium
  2 Cu3AsS4 (solid) + 3 Na2S (aqueous) → 2Cu2S (solid) + 2 Na3AsS4 (aqueous)
  Leaching of tellurium in alkaline sulfide medium
  Na2TeO4 (solid) + 4Na2S (aqueous) + 4H2O → Na2TeS4 (aqueous) + 8NaOH (aqueous)
  Bi2Te3 (solid) + 3Na2S (aqueous) → Bi2S3 (solid) + 3Na2Te (aqueous)
  Leaching of antimony in alkaline sulfide medium
  NaSb(OH)6 (solid) + 4Na2S (aqueous) = Na3SbS4 (aqueous) + 6NaOH (aqueous)
  Valuable metals needed by the copper industry (e.g. copper, gold and silver) are not affected by
  this treatment.
  The proposed process is innovative due to its selectivity and practicality in recovering tellurium
  [14,15]. Previously, it has been considered primarily for the removal of antimony (Sunshine
  Process [6,17]) from gold concentrates and the simple removal of arsenic from copper
  concentrates [7,16,18]. This process has not been applied to the deportment and recovery of
  antimony and tellurium from copper concentrates.
  Though the technology has shown promise in bench-scale testing with various copper
  concentrates, the project will further investigate the recyclability of the leach solution used, the
  continuous operation and eventual recovery of antimony and tellurium, and the stabilization of
  arsenic. This will be achieved using a continuous leaching circuit. Based on results obtained over
  the past year, tellurium yields range from 40-70% and antimony yields from 60-80%. Additionally,
  the process will remove 80-90% of arsenic from the material.
  Compared to traditional impurity removal methods, such as roasting and smelting, this alkaline
  sulfide leaching process is highly efficient and avoids generating airborne emissions, which
  remain a concern in the global copper smelting industry and imposes significant operational cost
  to the industry [8,9]. The technology's positive environmental impact is notable: by effectively

 Page 6 of 26

removing a significant portion of arsenic from the feedstock, the process reduces the risk of
arsenic pollution during copper production. This can lead to significant improvements in air and
water quality near smelting operations that treat these concentrates, benefiting both local
communities and ecosystems. Additionally, it has the potential to contain the presence of arsenic
to the mine site and allows for stabilization in the tailings management facility. This is in place at
all mine sites. The concept further allows critical mineral values – such as antimony and tellurium
– to be recovered. Beyond the reduction in contractual penalties [10] (for above-limit
concentrations of elements such as As and Sb) incurred to the mining operator, the recovery of
antimony and tellurium adds another layer of economic viability.
Relevance and Outcomes
The primary significance of the proposed project lies in its potential to significantly expand the
global supply of tellurium. This aligns with the overarching goal outlined in Topic Area 2 of the
FOA, specifically supporting the expansion of the “domestic supply chain for CdTe PV material
production, particularly increasing the availability of tellurium for module manufacturers,
including reclamation of materials from end-of-life modules". An estimate of the magnitude is
given below.
Implementing this process at an average-sized copper mine with an annual average concentrate
output of 200kt Cu concentrate and a tellurium concentration of 250 g/t Cu-concentrate at an
extraction of 60-75% could produce 30-37.5 additional metric tons of tellurium per year. The
primary sites for implementation of this technology would be located in South America, , due to
the high arsenic content of the ores present in the region. An additional 200-300 metric ton of
tellurium could enter the market in the long run, solving environmental and impurity issues in
the copper value chain while simultaneously increasing tellurium output to be used in solar
power module production.
This project aims to increase the technology readiness level (TRL) to 4, providing sufficient results
to inform further scale up of the process and demonstrate economic feasibility to prospective
clients and future partners implementing the technology.

Innovation and Impacts
Tellurium is presently generated as a by-product within the copper industry, primarily during the
concluding phases of its processes for impurity management. As a result, significant quantities of
tellurium are lost in streams where recovery is uneconomical. The proposed project targets
tellurium at the incoming stages of the copper value chain. For example, for any 1000 kg of Te
contained in copper concentrates about 270 kg are entering the market (see Exhibit 1) and could
be utilized for thin-film photovoltaics. The proposed process enables the recovery of
approximately 500 kg at the beginning of the copper value chain and potentially an additional
135 kg at the conclusion of the copper process. This results in a net gain of 365 kg new tellurium
units available to the market for refining and conversion to thin-film solar compounds. In turn,

 Page 8 of 26

  this represents a possible increase of approximately 230+% over the current supply per 1000 kg
  of Te contained in the concentrate – increasing the availability of Te towards the PV application.
  Current tellurium supply chains rely largely on Chinese production, which in the current
  geopolitical context is becoming an unreliable source. Based on the latest survey by the
  International Copper Study Group, China accounted for 50% of the global smelter production of
  primary copper in 2023 [11]. Therefore, it represents an equal share in the global production of
  primary tellurium.
  Implementing the proposed process at the mine site will diversify the supply at a global scale.
  While China holds a strong position in smelting, mining activities are primarily located in other
  regions, such as South America, Africa, and Southeast Asia. For example, South America is home
  to 43% of global copper mining output, with Chile, Peru and Mexico being the key producers in
  the region. The majority of the produced copper concentrate is shipped to smelters in China [11].
  Final application for the developed technology is anticipated to be licensed to or operated by
  mining companies that are exploiting ore bodies high in tellurium and arsenic. This is common in
  various cases involving Peruvian mines [12], exemplified by projects like the Canariaco Norte
  Project in the Lambayeque region of Peru [13]. As such, the target market for the process would
  be mines and producers of copper concentrate in this region. This contributes to diversifying the
  tellurium supply and diverting a portion of the metal away from China.
