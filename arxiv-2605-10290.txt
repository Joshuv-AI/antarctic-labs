Characterizing the Generalization Error of Random Feature Regression with Arbitrary Data-Augmentation
Lucas Morisset, Alain Durmus, Adrien Hardy

Abstract
This paper aims at analyzing the regularization effect that data augmentation induces on supervised regression methods in the proportional regime, where the number of covariates grows proportionally to the number of samples. We provide a tight characterization of the test error, measured in mean squared error, in terms only of the population quantities of the true data, as well as first and second order statistics of the augmentation scheme. Our results are valid under misspecified feature maps, and for any network architecture where only the last readout layer is trained, and the rest of the network is either frozen or randomly initialized. We specify our results in the case of Gaussian data, and show that our asymptotic characterization is tight in this setting.

Data augmentation (DA) is now a standard ingredient in modern machine learning pipelines,
with extensive empirical evidence reporting improvements in generalization across modalities and tasks

It is often used to encode task-relevant symmetries directly into the training procedure,

It has also been identified as one of the most effective regularization techniques across both supervised learning settings

;

;

Domain-specific augmentation pipelines have been central to progress in computer vision

;
3

;
20
;

Despite these empirical successes, the benefits of DA remain highly task- and data-dependent, and augmentation schemes are often engineered in an ad hoc manner

;

;
23
;
16

In contrast with this rich empirical literature, comprehensive theoretical analyses of DA remain relatively scarce.
Two classical starting points are, first, the interpretation of additive Gaussian noise as a form of explicit (ridge-like)
regularization

;
24
, and second, the idea that leveraging distributional invariances and group
structure in the learning objective helps decrease the variance of the model without increasing its bias

.
Yet, when applied to modern and complex augmentation schemes, these works either provide only upper bounds on the generalization error
24
,
or require very strong assumptions on the data distribution (e.g. approximate invariance under the augmentation scheme

The present manuscript is motivated by the need for a tractable theoretical framework capable of capturing augmentation schemes used in deep learning,
including those that may induce strong dependence across observations without preserving the data distribution.

Among the tractable proxy models used to study neural networks, random feature networks, obtained by training only the last layer of a random representation, have emerged as particularly natural candidates

4
;
10
;
14
;
18
.
Beyond their mathematical convenience, random networks at initialisation have proven to be a valuable theoretical testbed, and can capture empirically observed phenomena such as double-descent
28

and benign overfitting

As a result, tractable proxies for networks at initialisation, such as random feature models
4
;
10
;
14
;
18
;
31
;
32
,
have received considerable attention.
Remarkably, it has also been observed that structured random networks, whose weights are sampled to match certain statistics of trained models, can achieve performance comparable to that of
fully trained networks
15
. In some cases, matching second-order statistics alone is sufficient, which further supports random feature models as a natural
testbed for deep learning. This perspective has led to a comprehensive theory of random feature models

;
39
;
6
;
32
.
Yet this theory has so far remained confined to the i.i.d. setting, and a central technical challenge of the present work is to extend it to the dependent regime induced by data augmentation.

More precisely, we aim to provide a precise asymptotic characterization of the out-of-sample error of feature regression trained with data augmentation.
To this end, we consider a general augmentation framework that covers a broad class of modern augmentation schemes.
Our results apply to any class of architecture with a trained linear readout and either frozen or randomly initialized representation, and they reveal that data augmentation can
induce subtle and sometimes competing effects on generalization. Our contributions are as follows:

•

We derive deterministic equivalents for the generalization error of random-feature regression trained with data augmentation under possible misspecification,
extending previous results from
10
;
14
;
18
;
31
;
32
to the dependent setting.
In particular, we obtain a closed-form deterministic quantity that approximates the true generalization error in the proportional regime,

i.e.
, when the number of features scales proportionally with the sample size. We also derive explicit non-asymptotic bounds for the corresponding approximation error
as a function of the ridge parameter and the sample size.

•

Beyond the test error, we derive deterministic equivalents for the resulting estimator, as well as for key quadratic forms and transformations arising in the generalization analysis,
which we believe are of independent interest.

•

We empirically validate the accuracy of the deterministic equivalents on synthetic Gaussian experiments across a range of dimensions, aspect ratios, augmentation strengths, and ridge parameters.
We also investigate how data augmentation affects the bias and variance components of the error when the feature model is misspecified.
One of our main findings is that augmentation can lead to a substantial reduction in variance and improved generalization over a broad range of ridge parameters,
without necessarily inducing a monotonic increase in bias, contrary to the usual intuition drawn from correctly specified settings.

To improve performance various DA strategies have been proposed. In this paper, we model a data augmentation scheme as a random transformation

(\tau_{\mathtt{x}},\tau_{\mathtt{y}})
acting on feature/label pair. More precisely we consider
\tau_{\mathtt{x}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathsf{E}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

and

is a measurable space. Then, a data augmentation scheme is completed by specifying a distribution

over

(\tau_{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{Z}_{i},\eta),\tau_{\mathtt{y}}(\mathtt{Z}_{i},\eta))

is distributed according to

and is independent of the dataset

. This framework covers both input-only and label-dependent augmentations.
Based on
(\tau_{\mathtt{x}},\tau_{\mathtt{y}},\mathcal{T})
, we can then consider the augmented risk functions defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Aug}}(\theta)=\;\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bigl\{\tau_{\mathtt{y}}(\mathtt{Z}_{i},\eta)-\theta^{\top}\varphi(\tau_{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{Z}_{i},\eta))\bigr\}^{2}\;\middle|\;\mathtt{Z}\right]\;,

(3)

and consider the estimator obtained by minimizing a linear combinaison of the two previous risk functions: for
\lambda\geqslant 0
and
\alpha\in\left[0,1\right]

\hat{\theta}_{\alpha,\lambda}:=\arg\min_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\ (1-\alpha)\mathcal{L}(\theta)+\alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Aug}}(\theta)+\lambda\|\theta\|_{2}^{2}\;.

(4)

Note that we could set
\alpha=0
up to a change of
(\tau_{\mathtt{x}},\tau_{\mathtt{y}},\mathcal{T})
.
We do not adopt this presentation, however, as it would make our assumptions and results harder to state.

Our main object of interest is the generalization error of
\hat{\theta}_{\alpha,\lambda}

\mathcal{G}(\alpha,\lambda):=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\bigl\{\varphi(X)^{\top}\hat{\theta}_{\alpha,\lambda}-Y\bigr\}^{2}\Bigr]\;,

(5)

where
(X,Y)
is an independent test pair drawn from the same distribution as the training data.
In contrast to previous works
24
,
we do not suppose that the augmentation scheme is unbiased,
i.e., for some

it may hold that

we introduce the first moments of the DA scheme defined for any

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathtt{x}}(z)=\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi(\tau_{\mathtt{x}}(z,\eta))\right]\;,\quad\mu_{\mathtt{y}}(z)=\mathbb{E}\left[\tau_{\mathtt{y}}(z,\eta)\right]\;,

\displaystyle\Lambda(z)=\mathrm{Cov}[\varphi(\tau_{\mathtt{x}}(z,\eta))]\;,\qquad\Omega(z)=\mathrm{Cov}[\varphi(\tau_{\mathtt{x}}(z,\eta)),\tau_{\mathtt{y}}(z,\eta)]\;.

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathtt{Z})=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda(\mathtt{Z}_{i})\;,\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega(\mathtt{Z})=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Omega(\mathtt{Z}_{i})\;,

as well as their population counterparts,

\displaystyle\overline{\Lambda}=\mathbb{E}\left[\Lambda(z)\right]\;,\quad\overline{\Omega}=\mathbb{E}\left[\Omega(z)\right]\;.

(9)

These quantities are of natural interest, as they quantify the implicit regularization effect induced by the DA scheme

;
24

Indeed, writting a bias variance decomposition on (

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Aug}}(\theta)=\dfrac{1}{n}\|\mu_{\mathtt{y}}(\mathtt{Z})-\theta^{\top}\mu_{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{Z})\|_{2}^{2}-2\theta^{\top}\Omega(\mathtt{Z})+\theta^{\top}\Lambda(\mathtt{Z})\theta+\mathrm{c}\;,

where here,

Thus the first order condition in (

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{\alpha,\lambda}=\mathrm{R}_{\alpha,\lambda}\mathrm{H}_{\alpha}\;,

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\alpha}=\left((1-\alpha)\mathrm{H}+\alpha\mathrm{H}^{\prime}+\alpha\Omega(\mathtt{Z})\right)\;,\qquad\mathrm{R}_{\alpha,\lambda}=\left((1-\alpha)\mathrm{C}+\alpha\mathrm{C}^{\prime}+\alpha\Lambda(\mathtt{Z})+\lambda\mathrm{I}_{p}\right)^{-1}\;,

\displaystyle\makebox[276.00105pt][c]{$\mathrm{C}^{\prime}=\dfrac{1}{n}\mu_{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{Z})\mu_{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{Z})^{\top}\;,\quad\mathrm{H}^{\prime}=\dfrac{1}{n}\mu_{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{Z})\mu_{\mathtt{y}}(\mathtt{Z})^{\top}\;.$}

More unexpectedly, and contrary to common intuition, our simulations show that DA
impacts the model’s bias in a non-monotonic way, under feature misspecification.

In all experiments, we consider a synthetic regression problem in which the training data

5
Conclusion

We developed a high-dimensional theory for supervised feature regression trained with
arbitrary data augmentation, allowing for substantial perturbations of the covariates.
In the proportional regime, we derived deterministic equivalents for the augmented
ridge estimator and its out-of-sample error, together with explicit non-asymptotic
error controls in terms of
n
and the ridge parameter
\lambda
. Technically,
our analysis relies on resolvent methods that accommodate the non-i.i.d. structure induced by augmentation via the coupled objects
(\varphi(\mathtt{X}_{i}),\mu_{\mathtt{x}}(\mathtt{Z}_{i}))
.

Beyond prediction formulas, our results provide a mechanistic view of how
augmentation changes learning under feature misspecification:
it alters the effective covariance through a mean-feature component
and an additional, generally anisotropic variance term.
Our simulations validate the accuracy of the deterministic equivalents
and suggest that, in misspecified feature models, augmentation can improve
generalization over a wide range of
\lambda
while not necessarily increasing
the bias, so that gains are driven primarily by variance reduction.

Yet, our results cannot be directly applied by practitioners to predict when
a DA scheme is beneficial in the finite-sample setting. A key open direction is to turn our population-level formulas into a practical,
data-driven and quantitative criterion that could be used by practitioners.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\mathbf{u}^{\top}X_{\mathtt{Z}_{1}}\mid\mathtt{Z}_{1})

(50)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(Y_{\mathtt{Z}_{1}}\mid\mathtt{Z}_{1})

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathrm{c}\mathtt{L}_{\mathtt{y}}^{2},

(51)

almost surely.
We first control
\Lambda(\mathtt{Z})
. By independence of the
\mathtt{Z}_{i}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\Lambda(\mathtt{Z}))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\Lambda(\mathtt{Z})-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda(\mathtt{Z})]\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right]=\dfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\Lambda(\mathtt{Z}_{1})-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda(\mathtt{Z}_{1})]\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right]

(52)

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{\mathrm{c}}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\Lambda(\mathtt{Z}_{1})\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right]\leqslant\dfrac{\mathrm{c}p}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathrm{Var}(X_{\mathtt{Z}_{1}}\mid\mathtt{Z}_{1})\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\right].

(53)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathrm{Var}(X_{\mathtt{Z}_{1}}\mid\mathtt{Z}_{1})\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|\mathbf{u}\|_{2}=1}\mathrm{Var}(\mathbf{u}^{\top}X_{\mathtt{Z}_{1}}\mid\mathtt{Z}_{1})\leqslant\mathrm{c}\mathtt{L}_{\mathtt{x}}^{2},

(54)

by (
50
). Therefore

\mathrm{Var}(\Lambda(\mathtt{Z}))\leqslant\mathrm{c}\dfrac{p}{n}\mathtt{L}_{\mathtt{x}}^{4}.

(55)

We now control
\Omega(\mathtt{Z})
. The same independence argument gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\Omega(\mathtt{Z}))

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{\mathrm{c}}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathrm{Cov}(X_{\mathtt{Z}_{1}},Y_{\mathtt{Z}_{1}}\mid\mathtt{Z}_{1})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

(56)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\mathrm{c}}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sup_{\|\mathbf{u}\|_{2}=1}\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf{u}^{\top}X_{\mathtt{Z}_{1}},Y_{\mathtt{Z}_{1}}\mid\mathtt{Z}_{1})\right)^{2}\right]\;.

(57)

By Cauchy–Schwarz, (
50
), and (
51
),

\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf{u}^{\top}X_{\mathtt{Z}_{1}},Y_{\mathtt{Z}_{1}}\mid\mathtt{Z}_{1})^{2}\leqslant\mathrm{c}\mathtt{L}_{\mathtt{x}}^{2}\mathtt{L}_{\mathtt{y}}^{2}

(58)

uniformly over
\|\mathbf{u}\|_{2}=1
. Hence

\mathrm{Var}(\Omega(\mathtt{Z}))\leqslant\mathrm{c}\dfrac{1}{n}\mathtt{L}_{\mathtt{x}}^{2}\mathtt{L}_{\mathtt{y}}^{2}.

(59)

∎

Example 1

(Heteroskedastic noise injection)
.

Fix

, independent of

\mathtt{Z}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\eta\eta^{\top}\right]=\mathrm{I}_{k}\;.

(60)

Let

\displaystyle s_{\mathtt{x}}

\displaystyle:\mathbb{R}^{d(n)}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d(n)\times k}\;,

\displaystyle s_{\mathtt{y}}

\displaystyle:\mathbb{R}^{d(n)}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{1\times k}

\displaystyle\|s_{\mathtt{x}}(z)-s_{\mathtt{x}}(z^{\prime})\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\|s_{\mathtt{y}}(z)-s_{\mathtt{y}}(z^{\prime})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathtt{y}}(z)

(Salt-and-pepper noise injection)

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathtt{y}}(z,\bm{\eta})

\displaystyle=y\;,

(71)

.

(Label-aware mixtures of augmentations)
.

Fix

Consequently, the framework covers a broad family of practical data-augmentation pipelines, including schemes whose strength

B.1
Concentration of large random matrices: Useful lemmas

We begin by recalling a standard fact regarding the spectral properties of the covariance matrix of a Lipschitz concentrated random vector.
This simple observation will be useful for bounding operator norms of covariance matrices throughout the paper,
thus allowing us to derive bounds that depend only on the Lipschitz concentration parameter of the data.

Lemma 1

(Operator norm bound for the covariance matrix of a Lipschitz concentrated vector)
.

Let
Z
be a centered random vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is
\kappa
-Lipschitz concentrated. Then,

\|\mathbb{E}[ZZ^{\top}]\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leqslant\kappa^{2}\;.

(89)

Proof.

By definition of the operator norm, we write,

\|\mathbb{E}[ZZ^{\top}]\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sup_{\|u\|_{2}=1}u^{\top}\mathbb{E}[ZZ^{\top}]u=\sup_{\|u\|_{2}=1}\mathrm{Var}(u^{\top}Z)\;.

(90)

For any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the mapping
z\mapsto u^{\top}z
is
\|u\|_{2}
-Lipschitz, thus
u^{\top}Z
is
\kappa\|u\|_{2}
-sub-Gaussian from the Lipschitz concentration assumption.
In particular, for unit vectors
u
, we have
\mathrm{Var}(u^{\top}Z)\leqslant\kappa^{2}
. The claimed bound follows.
∎

A more general result holds for any Lipschitz concentrated random matrix,

Lemma 2

(Spectral norm with Lipschitz–concentrated rows)
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}
have independent rows
A_{1},\dots,A_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Assume each row is
\kappa
–Lipschitz concentrated.
Then there exists
\mathrm{c}>0
such that for all
s>0
,

\mathbb{P}\Big(\,\|A-\mathbb{E}[A]\|_{\mathrm{op}}\ \geqslant\ \mathrm{a}\,\kappa\,(\sqrt{p}+\sqrt{d})\;+\;s\,\Big)\ \leqslant 2\,\exp(-\,{\mathrm{c}\,s^{2}}/{\kappa^{2}})\;,

(91)

and,

\mathbb{P}\Big(\,\|A-\mathbb{E}\left[A\right]\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geqslant 2\mathrm{a}\,\kappa\,(\sqrt{p}+\sqrt{d})\Big)\ \leqslant 2\,\exp(-\,{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{a}_{1}^{2}(p+d)})\;.

(92)

with
\mathrm{a}
defined as,

\mathrm{a}=\sqrt{2\log(9)}\;.

(93)

Furthermore, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, and for three constants
\mathrm{c}_{k},\mathrm{c}_{k}^{\prime}
, we have,

\mathbb{E}\left[\|A-\mathbb{E}\left[A\right]\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{k}\right]\leqslant\mathrm{c}_{k}\kappa^{k}(\sqrt{p}+\sqrt{d}+1)^{k}+\mathrm{c}_{k}^{\prime}\kappa^{k-1}(\sqrt{p}+\sqrt{d})^{k-1}\;.

(94)

Proof.

The proof follows the standard
\varepsilon
–net argument (see, e.g., Lemma 5.2 and Lemma 5.4 of
36
).
We consider
\varepsilon
-nets of the unit spheres
\mathbb{S}^{p-1}
and
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, i.e., sets

\mathsf{N}\subset\mathbb{S}^{p-1}
and
\mathsf{M}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

x\in\mathbb{S}^{p-1}

\mathsf{M}

For

For any matrix

\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\;=\;\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{p-1},\,y\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}x^{\top}By\;\leqslant\frac{1}{1-2\varepsilon}\,\max_{x\in\mathsf{N},\,y\in\mathsf{M}}x^{\top}By\;\leqslant 2\,\max_{x\in\mathsf{N},\,y\in\mathsf{M}}x^{\top}By\;.

For any

(97)

Since the map

Since the

\displaystyle=2^{k-1}k\left[(\mathrm{a}_{1}\kappa(\sqrt{p}+\sqrt{d}))^{k-1}\Gamma(1)\kappa+\left(\sqrt{2}\kappa\right)^{k-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)\right]\;.

Combining both terms and using that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|A-\mathbb{E}\left[A\right]\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{k}\right]

(110)

for appropriate constants

.
∎

Now, recall the definition of the variance of a random matrix. For any
A\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(A)=\mathbb{E}\left[\|A-\mathbb{E}\left[A\right]\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right]\;.

(111)

Note that this definition is consistent with the one of the variance of a random variable living in a general Hilbert space.
We prove the following standard result about the variance of a product of random matrices,
although it holds in any Hilbert space, we state it here only in the case of
A\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
.

Lemma 3

(Variance of the product of random matrices)
.

Let
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
be two random matrices. Then,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(A_{1}A_{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\|\|A_{1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\mathrm{Var}(A_{2})+2\|\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right]\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\mathrm{Var}(A_{1}),

(112)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(A_{1}A_{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\|\|A_{2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\mathrm{Var}(A_{1})+2\|\mathbb{E}\left[A_{1}\right]\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\mathrm{Var}(A_{2}),

(113)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Var}(A_{1}A_{2}A_{1})

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\|\|A_{1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|_{\infty}^{4}\mathrm{Var}(A_{2})+8\|\mathbb{E}\left[A_{1}\right]\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\|\|A_{1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\mathrm{Var}(A_{2})\;.

(114)

Proof.

We prove the first inequality, the second one follows from the same arguments. We have that,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(A_{1}A_{2})=\mathrm{Var}(A_{1}\{A_{2}-\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right]\}+A_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right])\leqslant 2\mathrm{Var}(A_{1}\{A_{2}-\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right]\})+2\mathrm{Var}(A_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right])\;.

(115)

Using
\mathrm{Var}(A)\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[\|A\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right]
, it follows that,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(A_{1}\{A_{2}-\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right]\})\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[\|A_{1}\{A_{2}-\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right]\}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right]\leqslant\|\|A_{1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\mathrm{Var}(A_{2})\;.

(116)

Respectively, and by definition of
\mathrm{Var}
, we have,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(A_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right])=\mathbb{E}\left[\|\{A_{1}-\mathbb{E}\left[A_{1}\right]\}\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right]\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right]\leqslant\|\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right]\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\mathrm{Var}(A_{1})\;.

(117)

The two first inequalities follow.
We proove the last inequality in a similar way, indeed we have,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(A_{1}A_{2}A_{1})\leqslant 2\mathrm{Var}(A_{1}\{A_{2}-\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right]\}A_{1})+2\mathrm{Var}(A_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right]A_{1})\;,

(118)

Using the previously derived upper bounds, we have,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(A_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right]A_{1})

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\|\|A_{1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\mathrm{Var}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right]A_{1}\right)+2\|\mathbb{E}\left[A_{1}\right]\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\|\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right]\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\mathrm{Var}(A_{1})

(119)

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\|\|A_{1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|_{\infty}^{2}\|\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right]\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\mathrm{Var}(A_{1})

(120)

in addition,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(A_{1}\{A_{2}-\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right]\}A_{1})\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[\|A_{1}\{A_{2}-\mathbb{E}\left[A_{2}\right]\}A_{1}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right]\leqslant\|\|A_{1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|_{\infty}^{4}\mathrm{Var}(A_{2})\;.

(121)

The statement follows.
∎

B.2
Concentration of random quadratic forms

Moving on, we now restate a few usual results about the concentration of quadratic forms in the context of stochastic domination
Definition

1
and Lipschitz concentration
Definition

2
.
We start by highlighting a few standard properties of stochastic domination, and how it relates to non-asymptotic concentration bounds.
Our first result shows the additivity and multiplicative properties of
Definition

1
.

Lemma 4

(Stability of the stochastic domination)
.

Let
(U_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(U_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, and
(V_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be four sequences of non-negative random variables.
If
U_{n}\prec_{s}V_{n}
and
U_{n}^{\prime}\prec_{s}V_{n}^{\prime}
, then it holds,

U_{n}+U_{n}^{\prime}\prec_{s}V_{n}+V_{n}^{\prime}\;,\quad\text{and}\quad U_{n}U_{n}^{\prime}\prec_{s}V_{n}V_{n}^{\prime}\;.

(122)

Proof.

Fix
\delta>0
and
D>0
. From
U_{n}\prec_{s}V_{n}
and
U_{n}^{\prime}\prec_{s}V_{n}^{\prime}
, by definition of stochastic domination,
for any choice of
\delta>0
and
D>0
there exists
N(\delta,D)
such that for all
n\geqslant N(\delta,D)
,

\mathbb{P}\!\left(U_{n}>n^{\delta}V_{n}\right)\leqslant n^{-D}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}\!\left(U_{n}^{\prime}>n^{\delta}V_{n}^{\prime}\right)\leqslant n^{-D}.

(123)

Additivity.
Applying the union-bound, we have,
for
n\geqslant N(\delta,D+1)
,

\mathbb{P}\!\left(U_{n}+U_{n}^{\prime}>n^{\delta}(V_{n}+V_{n}^{\prime})\right)\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{P}\!\left(U_{n}>n^{\delta}V_{n}\right)+\mathbb{P}\!\left(U_{n}^{\prime}>n^{\delta}V_{n}^{\prime}\right)\;\leqslant\;2n^{-D+1}\leqslant n^{-D},

(124)

where the last inequality holds for sufficiently large
n
.
Thus
U_{n}+U_{n}^{\prime}\prec_{s}V_{n}+V_{n}^{\prime}
.

Multiplicativity.

Similarly as in the additive case, we have thanks to the union bound,

\mathbb{P}\!\left(U_{n}U_{n}^{\prime}>n^{\delta}V_{n}V_{n}^{\prime}\right)\leqslant\mathbb{P}\!\left(U_{n}>n^{\delta/2}V_{n}\right)+\mathbb{P}\!\left(U_{n}^{\prime}>n^{\delta/2}V_{n}^{\prime}\right)\leqslant 2n^{-(D+1)}\leqslant n^{-D},

(125)

for all sufficiently large
n
. Hence
U_{n}U_{n}^{\prime}\prec_{s}V_{n}V_{n}^{\prime}
.

Both claims hold for arbitrary
\delta>0
and
D>0
, which completes the proof.
∎

We now clarify the connection between stochastic domination and classical concentration of measure results.
To this end, introduce the following definition, which can be seen as an extension of the definition of a sub-Gaussian random variable to sequences
of random variables, such as in
Definition

1
.

Definition 3
.

We say that a sequence of real random variables
(U_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sub-Gaussian sequence with parameters
(\sigma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
if for there exists
\mathrm{c}\geqslant 0
and
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda\leqslant\lambda_{0}
,
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{e}^{\lambda U_{n}}]\leqslant\exp(\mathrm{c}\lambda^{2}\sigma_{n}^{2})
.
If
(U_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is complex valued, we say similarly that it is a sub-Gaussian sequence with parameters
(\sigma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
if its real and imaginary parts are sub-Gaussian sequences with parameters
(\sigma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

We observe that several common tail bounds imply stochastic domination in the appropriate regimes.

Lemma 5

(Stochastic domination from sub-Gaussian and sub-Exponential concentration)
.

Let
(U_{n})_{n}
be a sequence of complex random variables. Suppose there exist constants
\mathrm{c}_{1},\mathrm{c}_{2},\alpha>0
and a sequence
(\sigma_{n})_{n}
of positive real numbers such that, for all
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(|U_{n}|>t\right)\leqslant\mathrm{c}_{1}\exp\left(-\mathrm{c}_{2}\dfrac{t^{\alpha}}{\sigma_{n}^{\alpha}}\right)\;.

(126)

Then, it follows that

|U_{n}|\prec_{s}\sigma_{n}\;.

(127)

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon,D>0
. For all
n\geqslant 1
, we may write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|X_{n}|>n^{\varepsilon}\sigma_{n}\right)\leqslant\mathrm{c}_{1}\exp\left(-\mathrm{c}_{2}\dfrac{(n^{\varepsilon}\sigma_{n})^{\alpha}}{\sigma_{n}^{\alpha}}\right)=\mathrm{c}_{1}\exp\left(-\mathrm{c}_{2}n^{\alpha\varepsilon}\right).

(128)

The exponential decay ensures that, for sufficiently large
n
,
\mathrm{c}_{1}\exp\left(-\mathrm{c}_{2}n^{\alpha\varepsilon}\right)\leqslant n^{-D}
. Therefore, the conclusion follows by the definition of stochastic domination.
∎

To conclude this subsection, we provide a version of the Hanson-Wright inequality in the framework of stochastic domination.
To this end, we first recall the usual statement of the Hanson-Wright inequality:

Lemma 6

(Hanson-Wright inequality)
.

Let
(Z_{n})_{n}
be a sequence of centered
d
dimensional random vector such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
Z_{n}
is
\sigma_{n}
-Lipschitz concentrated.
For a sequence of deterministic matrix
(\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n})_{n}
,
it holds for all
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|Z_{n}^{\top}\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}Z_{n}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{\top}\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}Z_{n}]\right|>t\right)\leqslant 2\exp\left(-c\min\left\{\dfrac{t^{2}}{\sigma_{n}^{4}\|\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}\|_{F}^{2}},\dfrac{t}{\sigma_{n}^{2}\|\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}}\right\}\right)\;,

(129)

Stated as a stochastic domination result, we have,

\left|Z_{n}^{\top}\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}Z_{n}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{\top}\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}Z_{n}]\right|\prec_{s}\sigma_{n}^{2}\|\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}\|_{F}\;.

(130)

And, the moments of such quadratic forms are bounded by,

\mathbb{E}\left[|Z_{n}^{\top}\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}Z_{n}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{\top}\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}Z_{n}]|^{k}\right]\leqslant\mathrm{c}_{k}\sigma_{n}^{2k}\|\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}\|_{F}^{k}\;.

(131)

Where
c,\;(c_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}>0
are universal constants, that don’t depend on
n
,
Z_{n}
nor
\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}
.

Proof.

The first part of the theorem is a direct application of Theorem 2.4. of
1
.
The second part follows from
Lemma

5
.
We prove the third part by writting the moments in integral form as, and using (
129
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|Z_{n}^{\top}\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}Z_{n}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{\top}\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}Z_{n}]|^{k}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}kt^{k-1}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|Z_{n}^{\top}\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}Z_{n}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{\top}\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}Z_{n}]\right|>t\right)dt

(132)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2k\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{k-1}\exp\left(-c\min\left\{\dfrac{t^{2}}{\sigma_{n}^{4}\|\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}\|_{F}^{2}},\dfrac{t}{\sigma_{n}^{2}\|\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}}\right\}\right)dt\;.

(133)

Let
u=t/(\sigma_{n}^{2}\|\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}\|_{F})
and
v=\|\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}/\|\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}\|_{F}
. The integral becomes:

\displaystyle\leqslant 2k(\sigma_{n}^{2}\|\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}\|_{F})^{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{k-1}\exp\left(-c\min\left\{u^{2},\dfrac{u}{v}\right\}\right)du\;.

(134)

Splitting the integral into two parts, we have:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{k-1}e^{-c\min\{u^{2},u/v\}}du

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{k-1}e^{-cu^{2}}du+\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{k-1}e^{-cu/v}du=\dfrac{\Gamma(k/2)}{2c^{k/2}}+\dfrac{\Gamma(k)}{c^{k}}\;.

(135)

Where we have used
v<1
. We can conclude that:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|Z_{n}^{\top}\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}Z_{n}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{n}^{\top}\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}Z_{n}]|^{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathrm{c}_{k}\sigma_{n}^{2k}\|\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}\|_{F}^{k}\;,

(136)

where
\mathrm{c}_{k}=2k\left(\Gamma(k/2)/(2c^{k/2})+\Gamma(k)/(c^{k})\right)
.
∎

In what follows, it will also be necessary to address the scenario in which
\bm{\mathrm{A}}_{n}
is a random matrix that is independent of
X_{n}
.
Specifically, we establish the following result:

Corollary 1

(Concentration of random quadratic forms)
.

Consider a sequence of centered
d
-dimensional random vectors
(Z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that are
\sigma_{n}
-Lipschitz concentrated,
and a sequence of random matrices
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that are
\kappa_{n}
-Lipschitz concentrated and independent of
(Z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Then, the deviation from its expectation is stochastically dominated by,

|Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}\right]|\prec_{s}\sigma_{n}^{2}\|A_{n}\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\kappa_{n}\|\mathbb{E}\bigl[Z_{n}Z_{n}^{\top}\bigr]\|_{\mathrm{F}}\;.

(137)

And for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, its
k
-th moment satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}\right]\right|^{k}\right]\leqslant\mathrm{c}_{k}\left(\sigma_{n}^{2k}\mathbb{E}\left[\|A_{n}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{k}\right]+\kappa_{n}^{k}\|\mathbb{E}\bigl[Z_{n}Z_{n}^{\top}\bigr]\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{k}\right)\;,

(138)

where
(\mathrm{c}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}>0
are universal constants.

Proof.

We establish the result by decomposing the deviation into two components and analyzing each separately.
First, we write the triangular inequality:

|Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}\right]|\leqslant|Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}\mid A_{n}\right]|+|\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}\mid A_{n}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}\right]|\;.

(139)

By applying
Lemma

6
conditionally on
A_{n}
, we obtain the high probability bound:

\mathbb{P}\left(|Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}\mid A_{n}\right]|\geqslant t\mid A_{n}\right)\leqslant 2\exp\left(-\mathrm{c}\min\left\{\dfrac{t^{2}}{2\sigma_{n}^{4}\|A_{n}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}},\dfrac{t}{\sigma_{n}\|A_{n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}}\right\}\right)\;.

(140)

Since
\|A_{n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leqslant\|A_{n}\|_{\mathrm{F}}
almost surely, it follows from the definition of stochastic domination that:

|Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}\mid A_{n}\right]|\prec_{s}\sigma_{n}^{2}\|A_{n}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\;.

(141)

Now turning to the second term in (
139
), we express
the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}\mid A_{n}\right]
as a function of
A_{n}
.
Specifically, defining
f_{n}:\bm{\mathrm{A}}\mapsto\mathrm{tr}\bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl[Z_{n}Z_{n}^{\top}\bigr]\bm{\mathrm{A}}\bigr)
, we get,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}\mid A_{n}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}\right]|=|f_{n}(A_{n})-\mathbb{E}\left[f_{n}(A_{n})\right]|\;.

(142)

Since
f_{n}
is
\|\mathbb{E}\bigl[Z_{n}Z_{n}^{\top}\bigr]\|_{\mathrm{F}}
-Lipschitz, and
A_{n}
is
\kappa_{n}
-Lipschitz concentrated, thus from
Lemma

5
we get,

|f_{n}(A_{n})-\mathbb{E}\left[f_{n}(A_{n})\right]|\prec_{s}\kappa_{n}\|\mathbb{E}\bigl[Z_{n}Z_{n}^{\top}\bigr]\|_{\mathrm{F}}\;.

(143)

The first statement of the corollary follows from the additivity property of stochastic domination (
Lemma

4
).
For the second statement, use
(a+b)^{k}\leqslant 2^{k-1}(a^{k}+b^{k})
and take the expectation in (
139
), to get,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}\right]\right|^{k}\right]\leqslant 2^{k-1}\bigg(

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}\mid A_{n}\right]\right|^{k}\mid A_{n}\right]\right]

(144)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\left|f_{n}(A_{n})-\mathbb{E}\left[f_{n}(A_{n})\right]\right|^{k}\right]\bigg)\;.

(145)

The previous function
f_{n}
being
\|\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}Z_{n}^{\top}\right]\|_{\mathrm{F}}
-Lipschitz, we get from the Lipschitz concentration assumption on
A_{n}
, as well as the well-known moment bound on sub-Gaussian random variables that:

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|f_{n}(A_{n})-\mathbb{E}\left[f_{n}(A_{n})\right]\right|^{k}\right]\leqslant\mathrm{c}_{k}\kappa_{n}^{k}\|\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}Z_{n}^{\top}\right]\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{k}\;.

(146)

Moreover, applying the moment bound of
Lemma

6
conditionally on
A_{n}
yields the almost sure bound,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}-\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{\top}A_{n}Z_{n}\mid A_{n}\right]\right|^{k}\mid A_{n}\right]\leqslant\mathrm{c}_{k}\sigma_{n}^{2k}\|A_{n}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{k}\;,

(147)

for some large enough universal constants
(\mathrm{c}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}>0
. The second statement follows.
∎

B.3
Concentration through a non Lipschitz mapping

This final subsection is dedicated to showing that random variables satisfying
Definition

2
preserve satisfying concentration properties when composed with a non Lipschitz mapping.

More precisely, we will consider a differentiable map
f:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
, and notice that on any compact set
\mathsf{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
, the function
f_{\mid\mathsf{K}}
is Lipschitz.
The concentration of
f(Z)
will then follow from finding a trade-off between the grows of the Lipschitz constant of
f_{\mid\mathsf{K}}
and the probability of the event
\{Z\notin\mathsf{K}\}
.
We will denote
\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}
the Lipschitz constant of
f
on
\mathsf{K}
:

\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}:=\ \sup_{(x,y)\in\mathsf{K}^{2}}\{|f(x)-f(y)|/\|x-y\|_{2}\}\;.

(148)

In addition, if
\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}<+\infty
, consider the Moreau envelop
g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}
of and the Moreau residual
\delta_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}
defined as,

g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(z)=\inf_{x\in\mathsf{K}}\{f(x)+\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}\|x-z\|_{2}\}\;,\quad\text{and}\quad\delta_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(Z)\ :=\ \mathbb{E}\big[\,|f(Z)-g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(Z)|\,\big]\;.

(149)

If
\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}=+\infty
, we use the convention
\delta_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}=+\infty
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{C}
and
\mathsf{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a
compact set. Assume that
\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}<+\infty
. Then,

g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}
defined in (
149
) is a Lipschitz extension of
f
restricted to

\mathsf{K}
, i.e., it is a Lipschitz function from
\mathbb{R}^{k}
to

\mathbb{R}
such that for any
x\in\mathsf{K}
,
f(x)=g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(x)
.
Furthermore, for any random variable
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and any
z_{0}\in\mathsf{K}
, it holds,

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(Z)\leqslant\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|f(Z)|^{2}\right]^{1/2}+|f(z_{0})|+\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}\mathbb{E}\left[\|Z-z_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(Z\notin\mathsf{K}\right)^{1/2}

(150)

Proof.

Fix
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and any
x\in\mathsf{K}
. By the triangle inequality,

f(x)+\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}\|z_{1}-x\|\;\leqslant\;f(x)+\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}\big(\|z_{2}-x\|+\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|\big)\;=\;\big(f(x)+\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}\|z_{2}-x\|\big)+\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|.

(151)

Taking the infimum over
x\in\mathsf{K}
yields

g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(z_{1})\;\leqslant\;g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(z_{2})+\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|.

(152)

Exchanging the roles of
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
gives

|g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(z_{1})-g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(z_{2})|\;\leqslant\;\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|,

(153)

so
g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}
is
\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}
-Lipschitz on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. We now show that
g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}
coincides with
f
on
\mathsf{K}
.
Let
z\in\mathsf{K}
, since
f_{\mid\mathsf{K}}
is
\mathtt{L}^{f}_{\mathsf{K}}
-Lipschitz, we have for any
x\in\mathsf{K}
,

\displaystyle f(z)\leqslant f(x)+\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}\|z-x\|_{2}\;.

(154)

taking the infimum over
x\in\mathsf{K}
yields
f(z)\leqslant g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(z)
.
Furthermore, taking
x=z
in the definition of
g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(z)
gives
g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(z)\leqslant f(z)
, proving the equality.
Finally, let
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
to be any random vector, without loss of generality (and up to replacing
\mathsf{K}
by
\mathsf{K}-z_{0}
and
f(\cdot)
by
f(\cdot+z_{0})
), we assume
z_{0}=0
.
Leveraging that
f-g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}
is zero on
\mathsf{K}
, we write,

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(Z)=\mathbb{E}\left[|f(Z)-g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(Z)|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathsf{K}^{\mathrm{c}}}(Z)\right]\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[|f(Z)|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathsf{K}^{\mathrm{c}}}(Z)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[|g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(Z)|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathsf{K}^{\mathrm{c}}}(Z)\right]

(155)

We now bound the two terms in the right-hand side. For the first term, we have by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|f(Z)|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathsf{K}^{\mathrm{c}}}(Z)\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[|f(Z)|^{2}\right]^{1/2}\mathbb{P}(Z\notin\mathsf{K})^{1/2}\;.

(156)

The second term can be bounded thanks to the Lipschitz property of
g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(Z)|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathsf{K}}(Z)\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant|g_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(0)|\mathbb{P}(Z\notin\mathsf{K})+\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}\mathbb{E}\left[\|Z\|_{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathsf{K}^{\mathrm{c}}}(Z)\right]

(157)

\displaystyle\leqslant|f(0)|\mathbb{P}(Z\notin\mathsf{K})+\mathtt{L}_{\mathsf{K}}^{f}\mathbb{E}\left[\|Z\|_{2}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\mathbb{P}(Z\notin\mathsf{K})^{1/2}\;,

(158)

where the last inequality followed from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, as well as the fact that
0\in\mathsf{K}
.
The claimed upper bound follows.
∎

Lemma 8

(Concentration through locally Lipschitz maps, all
t>0
)
.
