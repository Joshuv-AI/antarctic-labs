Towards an HRS Category in TermCOMP
Johannes Niederhauser, Aart Middeldorp

Abstract
We show that there is a simple syntactically-defined subclass of higher-order benchmarks in the termination problem database for which rewriting according to Nipkow’s higher-order rewrite systems (HRSs) and rewriting according to a beta-first strategy in the semantics of TermCOMP’s higher-order category coincide. This lays the formal foundation for an HRS (sub)category in TermCOMP which would allow more tools to compete against each other.

Introduction

Higher-order rewriting comes in many different flavors. Popular
choices include Jouannaud and Okada’s algebraic functional systems
(AFSs)
[
6
,
7
]
and higher-order rewrite systems (HRSs) as
defined by Wolfram and Nipkow
[
19
,
12
]
. Both formalisms
consider terms of the simply-typed
\lambda
-calculus. AFSs use plain
matching and augment the rewrite system with a dedicated rule for

\beta
-reduction whereas HRSs perform
rewriting modulo
\beta\eta
. In order to keep the matching problem and
therefore rewriting with HRSs manageable, left-hand sides are
typically restricted to Miller’s patterns (PATs)
[
13
]
, resulting in
the subclass of pattern rewrite systems (PRSs)
[
12
]
. Recently, we
introduced another subclass of HRSs by extending PRSs to deterministic
higher-order pattern rewrite systems (DPRSs)
[
14
]
. DPRSs
use Yokoyama et al.’s deterministic higher-order patterns (DHPs) as
left-hand sides. Like PATs, DHPs enjoy a deterministic matching problem of
linear complexity
[
20
]
.

Since 2010, a category for higher-order rewriting has been part of the
annual international termination competition (TermCOMP).
1
1

1

https://termination-portal.org/wiki/Termination_Competition

Except for a demonstration category for HRSs in 2018, the higher-order
category has always considered AFS semantics. In 2024, the
termination problem database (TPDB) was ported to the new ARI format
which is also used in the confluence competition.
2
2

2

https://project-coco.uibk.ac.at/
A discussion about
the higher-order format ensued, and a proposal by Kop was implemented.
3
3

3

https://github.com/orgs/TermCOMP/discussions/86
This
year, TermCOMP provides a reference describing the syntax and
semantics of its categories for the first time
[
5
]
. In
that document, Kop defines the semantics of the higher-order category
as a restricted version of her algebraic functional systems with
meta-variables (AFSMs)
[
10
]
which we shall refer to as

simply-typed meta-substitution rewrite systems
(STMRSs). The
definition of STMRSs is given such that other popular flavors
of higher-order rewriting can be accommodated easily. A subcategory
for AFSs is established by simply restricting the eligible benchmarks.
For PRSs, Kop defines a restricted class of STMRSs where
termination of the strategy which prefers
\beta
-reduction
coincides with universal termination of the corresponding PRS.
This idea is based on a result in Kop’s PhD thesis
[
10
, Theorem 3.5]
.

In this paper, we extend STMRSs such that
they can model a more general class of HRSs including our DPRSs. To
that end, we introduce the ad-hoc definition of

extended pattern rewrite systems
(EPRSs) which constitute a
generalization of DPRSs
where we do not enforce deterministic matching. EPRSs are not meant to
be useful in their own right, but they simplify the development in
this paper and allow us to focus on the properties of DPRSs which are
needed in order to obtain our main goal. Instead of just proving the
relationship between EPRSs and STMRSs with respect to termination,
we show that any step in an EPRS can
be modeled by a step followed by
\beta
-normalization in
STMRSs. This implementation result lays the foundation for comparing also
other properties besides termination. Furthermore, our result extends and
strengthens similar results in the literature such
as Kop’s implementation of PRSs in AFSMs
[
10
, Section 3.3]

as well as van Oostrom and van Raamsdonk’s implementation of PRSs
in Klop’s combinatory systems with explicit
\beta

[
17
]
.

Extended Pattern Rewrite Systems

In this section, we present a subclass of HRSs which
generalizes Nipkow’s PRSs
[
12
]
. The rewrite systems we
want to target are the DPRSs recently introduced by us
[
14
]
.
DPRSs use Yokoyama et al.’s DHPs
[
20
]

as left-hand sides. While DHPs and therefore DPRSs
have desirable properties with respect to matching,
unification and ultimately confluence, the emphasis
on termination in this paper allows us to ignore
a large part of the involved definition of DHPs.
Thus, the following definition subsumes both PATs and DHPs but is not
meant to be practically useful.

Definition 2
.

A term
s\in\mathsf{NF}({\beta})
is an
extended pattern

(EPAT)
if
every free occurrence of a variable
F
is in a subterm

Fu_{1}\cdots u_{n}
such that each
u_{i}
is
\eta
-equivalent to
a non-abstraction and does not contain free variables.

Example 3
.

Consider the sort
\mathsf{a}
, the function symbol
\mathsf{f}:\mathsf{a}\to\mathsf{a}

as well as the variables

x:\mathsf{a}
,
y:\mathsf{a}
and

Z:\mathsf{a}\to(\mathsf{a}\to\mathsf{a})\to\mathsf{a}\in\mathcal{V}
.
The terms
\lambda x.Z\>x\>\mathsf{f}
,

\lambda x.Z\>(\mathsf{f}\>x)\>\mathsf{f}
and

\lambda x.Z\>(\mathsf{f}\>x)\>(\lambda w.\mathsf{f}\>w)
are EPATs while

\lambda x.Z\>y\>\mathsf{f}
and

\lambda x.Z\>x\>(\lambda w.\mathsf{f}\>(\mathsf{f}\>w))
are not.

Definition 4
.

A rule
\ell\rightarrow r
is an
extended pattern rule

if
\ell
is an
EPAT
. An
extended pattern rewrite system

(EPRS)
is a set of extended pattern rewrite rules.

Example 5
.

Consider the sorts
\mathsf{a}
,
\mathsf{b}
and
\mathsf{u}
, the function symbols

\mathsf{inl}:\mathsf{a}\to\mathsf{u}
,
\mathsf{inr}:\mathsf{b}\to\mathsf{u}
and

\mathsf{case}:\mathsf{u}\to(\mathsf{a}\to\mathsf{u})\to(\mathsf{b}\to\mathsf{u})\to\mathsf{u}

as well as the variables
x:\mathsf{a}
,
y:\mathsf{b}
,

,

,

and

\displaystyle\mathsf{case}\>(\mathsf{inl}\>x)\>F\>G

\displaystyle\mathsf{case}\>(\mathsf{inr}\>y)\>F\>G

models a case distinction operation on the disjoint union datatype

[

the EPRS presented in Example

is also an STMRS. In general,
any STMRS which only consists of rules typed by a sort
is also an EPRS. For EPATS with non-abstractions
as arguments of free variables, we have already
seen that matching with
\beta
-normal meta-substitutions
(as done in STMRSs) is as powerful as full higher-order matching (as
done in EPRSs) because the implicit
\beta
-reduction in
meta-substitution application is sufficient for
\beta
-normalization.
However, applying meta-substitutions to right-hand sides does not
yield
\beta
-normal terms in general. Hence, applying a rewrite step in an
STMRS and its corresponding EPRS might yield different terms. On top
of that, rules are used in their
\eta
-expanded form in EPRS rewrite
steps while no such transformation is done for STMRS rewrite steps.
In the next section, we will establish a syntactic subclass of STMRSs (and
therefore an syntactically definable subset of higher-order benchmarks
in TPDB) which can implement their corresponding EPRSs by normalized
rewriting
[
11
]
with respect to
\beta
-reduction.

\mathcal{I}\mathsf{m}({\gamma^{-\lambda}})\subseteq\mathsf{NF}(\beta\eta^{-1})
. Thus, we can use Lemmata

18
,
19
and
16
to obtain

\ell\gamma{\updownarrow_{\beta}^{\eta}}=\ell(\gamma^{-\lambda})^{\lambda}{\updownarrow_{\beta}^{\eta}}=\ell\gamma^{-\lambda}{\downarrow_{\beta}}=\ell\gamma^{-\lambda}

which means we also have
s=C[\ell\gamma^{-\lambda}]
.
We can finish the proof as in the previous case. ∎

7
Conclusion

For terms in
\mathsf{NF}(\eta^{-1})
, Theorem
15
yields the correspondence

\Rightarrow_{\beta}^{!}\cdot\Rightarrow_{\mathcal{R}}\cdot\Rightarrow_{\beta}^{!}\cdot\Rightarrow_{\mathcal{R}}\cdot\Rightarrow_{\beta}^{!}\cdots\qquad=\qquad\Rightarrow_{\beta}^{!}\cdot\rightarrow_{\mathcal{R}}\cdot\rightarrow_{\mathcal{R}}\cdots

of rewrite sequences. Hence, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 20
.

Given a well-behaved
STMRS

\mathcal{R}
,

\Rightarrow_{\beta}^{!}\cdot\Rightarrow_{\mathcal{R}}
is terminating
on terms in
\mathsf{NF}(\eta^{-1})
if and only if

\rightarrow_{\mathcal{R}}
is terminating when viewing
\mathcal{R}
as an
EPRS
.

This lays the foundation for an EPRS subcategory of the higher-order
category in TermCOMP: One simply has to make sure to only use
well-behaved STMRSs by filtering TPDB accordingly or automatically
transforming all STMRS benchmarks to well-behaved STMRSs.
Given a well-behaved STMRS, termination of the corresponding
EPRS coincides with termination of normalized rewriting
[
11
]
of
the given STMRS with respect to
\beta
-reduction.
For second-order STMRSs, meta-substitution cannot create
\beta
-redexes,
so on terms in
\mathsf{NF}(\beta\eta^{-1})
there is even a one-to-one correspondence between
steps in a well-behaved STMRS and its corresponding EPRS.

Regarding future work, one could consider dropping the restriction
to terms in
\mathsf{NF}(\eta^{-1})
in along the lines of
the development in
[
10
, Section 2.3.2]
.
Furthermore, it could be worthwhile to generalize Theorem
15

to more general subclasses of HRSs. Note that it will not
be possible to go for all HRSs
since meta-substitution is strictly less powerful than
full higher-order substitution (see e.g. Example
14
).
Finally, by establishing the implementation result of Theorem
15

instead of a direct proof of , we also provide
a solid foundation for transferring properties other than
termination from EPRSs to STMRSs.
