Abstract
. We prove that commensurators of a geometrically rigid residually finite hyperbolic group have bounded average distortion.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a one-ended torsion-free hyperbolic group.
Paulin
[
15
]
, building on the Rips machine (cf. Bestvina–Feighn
[
2
]
), showed that
|\Out(G)|<\infty
if and only if
G
does not split over a virtually cyclic group.
A problem motivating this paper is to try to generalize this result to commensurators of
G
, i.e. to isomorphisms between finite index subgroups of
G
.

A one-ended hyperbolic group for which
\Out(G)
is finite is called
rigid
.
Bowditch
[
4
]
provides a geometric characterization of rigidity: a one-ended hyperbolic group is rigid if and only if either (1)
G
surjects to a triangle group with finite kernel or (2) the Gromov boundary
\partial G
is connected and does not contain a cut pair of points.
If
G
is as in (1), then it is virtually a surface group, and hence virtually non-rigid.
We therefore focus on case (2) which we call
geometrically rigid
.
We remark that if
G
is torsion-free, the two notions are the same.

Trying to generalize Paulin’s result to commensurators, we run into two problems:
First, one cannot define an “outer commensurator group”, since the inner automorphisms do not form a normal subgroup in
\Comm(G)
.
Second, since
|\Out(G)|<\infty
whenever
G
is geometrically rigid, one can
still consider the index
[\Comm(G):G]

for such groups. However,
there are (geometrically) rigid groups for which
[\Comm(G):G]=\infty
.
For example, arithmetic uniform lattices in
\SO(n,1)
for
n\geq 3
are such.
This is a more substantial problem.

Let us elaborate more on this last example:
Let
M
be a hyperbolic
n
-manifold (
n\geq 3
) and let
G
be its fundamental group.
Let
\phi:H_{1}\to H_{2}
be an isomorphism between finite index subgroups of
G
.
Then, by Mostow Rigidity
[
14
]

\phi
is induced by an isometry
\overline{\Phi}:M_{1}\to M_{2}
between the finite covers
M_{1},M_{2}
of
M
corresponding to
H_{1},H_{2}
.
Alternatively, the lift
\widetilde{\Phi}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
of
\overline{\Phi}
to the universal cover
\mathbb{H}^{n}
of
M
is a
\phi
-equivariant isometry of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

Since in this paper we work with countable/discrete groups, we would like to phrase the existence of
\widetilde{\Phi}
more intrinsically in terms of
G
.
This can be done by approximating
\widetilde{\Phi}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
by a
\phi
-equivariant map
\Phi:G\to G
which is a
(C,C)
-quasi-isometry (with respect to the word metric on
G
).
Importantly, the constant
C
here is
independent
of the map
\phi
(but might depend on
G
and its word metric).
We conjecture that this is indeed the case for all geometrically rigid hyperbolic groups:

Conjecture 1.1
.

If
G
is a geometrically rigid hyperbolic group, then there exist a constant
C=C(G)
such that every abstract commensurator
\phi:H_{1}\to H_{2}
admits a
\phi
-equivariant map
\Phi:G\to G
which is
(C,C)
-quasi-isometry.

This conjecture can be seen as an interpretation of 0.3.C in Gromov’s
Hyperbolic Groups

[
10
]
, and has interesting implications.
In this paper we show that a variant of this conjecture holds – namely, the “average distortion” of commensurators is uniformly bounded.

Average Distortion of commensurators

Let
G
be a hyperbolic group.
Let
X
be a graph on which
G
acts freely and cocompactly, e.g., its Cayley graph with respect to some finite generating set
S
.
Let
\phi:H\to G
be an injective map where
H\leq G
has finite index. Consider a
\phi
-equivariant (cellular) map
\Phi:X\to X
sending vertices to vertices and edges to edge-paths.
We denote by
\ell(\Phi(e))
the length of the edge-path
\Phi(e)
.
Assume
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
are representatives of the
H
-orbits of edges in
X
(note that
k=k_{0}\cdot[G:H]
where
k_{0}
is the number of edge

-orbit representatives).
Define the
total distortion
of
\Phi
by

\TD(\Phi)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\ell(\Phi(e_{i}))

and the
average distortion
of
\Phi

\AD(\Phi)=\tfrac{1}{k}\TD(\Phi)\propto\tfrac{1}{[G:H]}\TD(\Phi).

Denote
\AD(\phi)=\min_{\Phi}\AD(\Phi)
where the minimum is taken over all
\phi
-equivariant cellular maps
\Phi:X\to X
.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a geometrically rigid, residually finite, hyperbolic group.
Let
X
be a graph with a free and cocompact
G
-action.
There exists a finite index subgroup
\dot{G}\leq G
and a constant

such that for every finite index subgroup
H\leq\dot{G}
and every
injective homomorphism
\phi:H\to G
, we have

(1.1)

\AD(\phi)\leq C.

If, moreover, the image of
\phi
has finite index in
G
then

(1.2)

\frac{1}{C}\leq\AD(\phi)\leq C.

We note that the existence of a constant
C
such that (

) holds is equivalent to the existence of a constant
C^{\prime}
such that

(1.3)

\frac{1}{C^{\prime}}\cdot[G:H]\leq\TD(\Phi)\leq{C^{\prime}}\cdot[G:H].

for the map
\Phi
that minimizes
\TD(\Phi)
among all
\phi
-equivariant maps
\Phi:X\to X
.
We also note that while the constant

depends on

Lemma

7.1
).

Commensurators of negatively curved manifolds

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold of dimension
\geq 3
of negative sectional curvature with residually finite fundamental group. Then, there exists
C\geq 1
and a cover
\dot{M}
such that any homotopy equivalence

is homotopic to a differentiable map

such that

\tfrac{1}{\Vol(M_{1})}\int_{T^{1}M_{1}}\|D_{x}f(v)\|\;\mathbf{d}\vol(x,v)\leq C

Average distortion of translation length

For

\ell(g)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\|g^{n}\|

\phi(g)

between finite index subgroups. However,
\phi

.
To remedy this, we need to lift

to
H
.

Definition 1.4
.

The

to

is the power

for the minimal

such that

. The exponent

to
H
.

Given

\ell(\phi,g)=\tfrac{1}{m}\ell(\phi(g^{m}))

Unlike the stable translation length,

is not invariant under conjugation by elements of

To fix this, let

be a set of representatives of

\AL(\phi,g)=\frac{1}{[G:H]}\sum_{i=1}^{[G:H]}\ell(\phi,t_{i}^{-1}gt_{i})

.

Let

Then there exists

such that for every finite index subgroup

.

We note that both

are invariant under the following equivalence relation: Let

We recall that the

]

\mathcal{F}
whose vertices are connectors and edges are segments in
Y
is called the
singular pattern associated with
\Phi
(and
q
)
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that regular segments are allowed to cross each other: Indeed, if two edges, say
f,f^{\prime}
, appear in different orders in the quasigeodesic paths
q(e_{1}),q(e_{2})
, then the regular segment between
\mathbf{c}_{e_{1},f},\mathbf{c}_{e_{2},f}
intersects the one between
\mathbf{c}_{e_{1},f^{\prime}},\mathbf{c}_{e_{2},f^{\prime}}
.
In
[
7
]
, each intersecting regular segment is replaced by two non-intersecting singular segments. We do not do this in order to get a correspondence with compactly supported 1-cochains, as explained in
Section

2.3
.
Though we will not use this fact, we remark that by
[
7
, Lemme I.1(e)]
the number of intersections in a 2-simplex is bounded.

The singular pattern
\mathcal{F}
is
H
-invariant, and so it gives rise to a singular pattern
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
on
\overline{Y}=Y/H
.
The graph
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
is a finite graph on the compact space
\overline{Y}
, and so it makes sense to define its size
|\overline{\mathcal{F}}|
to be the total number of regular connectors in
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
.

Now, let
\Phi:Y^{1}\to X^{1}
be a
\phi
-equivariant extension of
\Phi:Y^{0}\to X^{0}
to the edges of
Y
.
Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{t}
be representatives of
H
-orbits of edges in
Y
(note that
t\leq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D)\cdot[G:H]
), define

\TD_{Y}(\Phi):=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\ell(\Phi(e_{i}))

If we assume further that
\Phi:Y^{1}\to X^{1}
maps each edge to a geodesic path (e.g. if
\Phi
minimizes
\TD_{Y}(\Phi)
), then

\TD_{Y}(\Phi)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}d(\Phi(\partial e_{i}))

where
d(\Phi(\partial e_{i}))
is the distance between the images of the two endpoints
\partial e_{i}
of
e_{i}
.

Every edge of
X
is an edge of
Y
, so the edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{t}
contain
H
-orbit representatives of the edges of
X
.
Hence,

\TD(\Phi|_{X})\leq\TD_{Y}(\Phi).

The number of connectors of
\mathcal{F}
on an edge
e
of
Y
is the size of
q(e)=q(\Phi(\partial e))
which by property
(Q1)
can be bounded below by
d(\Phi(\partial e))
.
Thus, if
\Phi:Y\to X
maps edges to geodesics, then

\TD_{Y}(\Phi)\leq|\overline{\mathcal{F}}|.

Combining these two inequalities we get

(2.1)

\TD(\Phi)\leq|\overline{\mathcal{F}}|.

By property
(Q4)
, the number of dead-ends in each 2-simplex
\Delta
is at most
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
, and so the total number of dead-ends in
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
is at most
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\vol(\overline{Y})
, where
\vol(\overline{Y})
denotes the number of 2-simplices in
\overline{Y}
.
Thus,

(2.2)

\#\{\text{dead-ends in }\overline{\mathcal{F}}\}\leq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\vol(\overline{Y}).

2.3.
Tracks and compactly supported 1-cochains

The connected components of the singular pattern
\mathcal{F}
(or
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
) are called
tracks
.
Denote by
|\lambda|
the number of regular connectors in a track
\lambda
, then clearly

(2.3)

|\overline{\mathcal{F}}|=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{F}}\text{ track}}|\lambda|

and

(2.4)

\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{F}}\text{ track}}\#\{\text{dead-ends of }\lambda\}=\#\{\text{dead-ends of }\overline{\mathcal{F}}\}.

Each track
\lambda
of
\mathcal{F}
corresponds to a single edge
f
in
X
, in particular it is embedded in
Y
and its set-wise stabilizer in (the torsion-free group)
H
is trivial. It follows that
\lambda
embeds in
\overline{Y}
under the quotient
Y\to\overline{Y}
.
In particular, each track of
\mathcal{F}
is compact.

By property
(Q1)
, each track
\lambda
in
\mathcal{F}
intersects each edge of
Y
at most once.
Thus the track
\lambda
gives rise to the element in
C_{c}^{1}(Y)
which is the set of all edges
\lambda
meets. By abuse of notation we denote
\lambda\in C_{c}^{1}(Y)
as well.
This abuse of notation is justified since the track
\lambda
can be reconstructed (up to a small isotopy that does not pass through vertices of
Y
) from the corresponding 1-cochain in
C_{c}^{1}(Y)
. Indeed, there is a regular connector of the track on each edge of the 1-cochain: for a 2-simplex
\Delta
we connect two regular connectors by a regular segment in
\Delta
if they are the only two connectors on
\partial\Delta
; otherwise the regular connectors on
\partial\Delta
are connected to singular connectors in the interior of
\Delta
.
Since the number of regular connectors in
\lambda
is the size of the support of the corresponding 1-cochain, there is no ambiguity in the notation
|\lambda|
.

It is easy to see that the coboundary
\mathbf{d}\lambda
of a track
\lambda\subset\mathcal{F}
is supported in the set of 2-simplices that contain a dead-end of the track
\lambda
. In particular,

(2.5)

|\mathbf{d}\lambda|\leq\#\{\text{dead-ends of }\lambda\}

At first glance one might suspect that (
2.5
) should be an equality, however
|\mathbf{d}\lambda|
could be strictly smaller than
\#\{\text{dead-ends of }\lambda\}
. This happens when
\lambda
meets all three of the sides of a 2-simplex
\Delta
, as there are three dead-ends of
\lambda
in
\Delta
but
\Delta
is only counted once in
|\mathbf{d}\lambda|
.

A word of caution is in order: The last two paragraphs concern only tracks in
\mathcal{F}
and not
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
. A track in
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
might intersect the same edge of
\overline{Y}
multiple times and so does not correspond as nicely to a 1-cochain on
\overline{Y}
.
To remedy that, for a track
\overline{\lambda}
in
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
we will interpret
|\mathbf{d}\overline{\lambda}|
as
|\mathbf{d}\lambda|
for some (any) lift
\lambda
of
\overline{\lambda}
to
\mathcal{F}
.
By doing so, (
2.5
) remains true also for tracks in
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
.

Summary of
Section

2

Combining (
2.1
),(
2.3
), and
(
2.5
), (
2.4
), (
2.2
), (
1.5
) we get the following.

Proposition 2.3
.

For
D\geq 0
, the singular pattern
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
defined above satisfies

(2.6)

\TD(\Phi)\leq\sum_{\overline{\lambda}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{F}}}|\overline{\lambda}|\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{\overline{\lambda}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{F}}}|\mathbf{d}\overline{\lambda}|\leq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D)[G:H].

To prove inequality (
1.1
) of
A
it remains to show that

\sum_{\overline{\lambda}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{F}}}|\overline{\lambda}|\leq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\sum_{\overline{\lambda}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{F}}}|\mathbf{d}\overline{\lambda}|.

One way of achieving this inequality is to prove that for each track
\lambda\subset\mathcal{F}
we have

(2.7)

|\lambda|\leq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}|\mathbf{d}\lambda|.

While we do not prove exactly this for all tracks, the gist of the argument is to show that this inequality holds for
most
of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
.

We discuss (
2.7
) and its relation to the classical Cheeger constant in the next section.
As we said, we do not prove that (
2.7
) holds for all tracks but only for most of them. In fact, some tracks might have no dead-ends, and so
|\mathbf{d}\lambda|=0
.
We call such tracks
regular
.
Equivalently, a track is regular if all its connectors are regular. It is called
singular
otherwise.

The following proposition takes care of bounding regular tracks. Its proof is contained in the proof of
[
12
, Proposition 6.1]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

If
Y
is one-ended and
\Phi
minimizes
\TD_{Y}(\Phi)
then

(2.8)

\sum_{\lambda\subset\overline{\mathcal{F}}\text{ is regular}}|\lambda|\leq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\vol(\overline{Y})

We will not make direct use of
Proposition

2.4
in our proof of
A
.
However, we find it useful to mention it here, as it helps shift the focus to the task at hand – bounding the size of the singular tracks.
In addition, as each track is either regular or contains a dead-end, one can bound the number of tracks by combining the bound on regular tracks (
2.8
) and the bound on dead-ends (
2.2
).

Corollary 2.5

(
[
12
, Proposition 6.1]
)
.

If
\Phi
minimizes
\TD(\Phi)
then the number of tracks in
\mathcal{F}
is at most
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\vol(\overline{Y})
.

3.
Connection to higher dimensional Cheeger constants

Let
Z
be a simplicial complex.
Every 0-cochain
\nu\in C^{0}_{c}(Z)
can be viewed as a finite set of vertices in
Z
. Its co-boundary
\mathbf{d}\nu
is the set of edges connecting
\nu
and its complement
Z^{0}-\nu
.
For an infinite
Z
, we define its Cheeger constant by

(3.1)

h(Z):=\inf_{\nu\in C^{0}_{c}(Z)}\frac{|\mathbf{d}\nu|}{|\nu|}.

If a group acts geometrically on a locally finite graph, then its Cheeger constant vanishes if and only if the group is amenable. Non-elementary hyperbolic groups are non-amenable (see
[
10
]
), and so
h(Z)>0
.
It follows that there exists a constant
c=c(Z)
for all
\nu\in C^{0}_{c}(Z)
we have
|\nu|\leq c|\mathbf{d}\nu|
.

The desired inequality (
2.7
) alludes to a co-dimension-1 analogue of the co-dimension-0 Cheeger constant
h(Z)
.

Definition 3.1

(coboundary Cheeger constant)
.

Let
B^{i+1}_{c}(Z)\subseteq C^{i+1}_{c}(Z)
be the set of (compactly supported)
(i+1)
-coboundaries.
For
\alpha\in B^{i+1}_{c}(Z)
let
\MP(\alpha)
be the size of the minimal primitive of
\alpha
i.e.

\MP(\alpha):=\min_{\lambda\in C_{c}^{i}(Z):\alpha=\mathbf{d}\lambda}|\lambda|

and define the
i
-th coboundary Cheeger constant
to be

(3.2)

h^{i}(Z):=\inf_{0\neq\alpha\in B^{i+1}_{c}(Z)}\frac{|\alpha|}{\MP(\alpha)}

Remark 3.2
.

We have
h(Z)=h^{0}(Z)
since if
Z
is infinite then
\nu\in C^{0}_{c}(Z)
is the unique (finitely supported) primitive of
\mathbf{d}\nu
.

In
Section

6
,
we will prove that rigid hyperbolic groups have positive co-dimension-1 Cheeger constant:

Theorem B
.

If
G
is a geometrically rigid hyperbolic group, and
G
acts freely cocompactly on a simply connected simplicial complex
Z
, then
h^{1}(Z)>0
.

Remark 3.3
.

It is worth mentioning as an aside how
B
differs from a somewhat related (co)homological characterization of hyperbolicity. Gersten
[
8
]
showed that vanishing of the second
l^{\infty}
cohomology for a finitely presented group characterizes hyperbolicity. Further, Allcock and Gersten
[
1
]
(in particular the proof of Theorem 4.5 in
[
1
, p. 731]
) showed that a finitely presented group
G
is (word) hyperbolic if and only if the first unreduced
l^{1}
homology group and the second reduced
l^{1}
homology group of
G
with real coefficients both vanish. However, in
B
above, we are using
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
coefficients, and as the remark below shows,
h^{1}(Z)>0

does not
characterize hyperbolicity.

Remark 3.4
.

B
is false when
G
splits over
\mathbb{Z}
: To see this, consider
\alpha\in C^{2}_{c}(Z)
with
|\alpha|=2
, i.e.
\alpha
is supported on two simplices, say
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
. If
\alpha
happens to be a co-boundary then for any primitive
\lambda\in C^{1}_{c}(Z)
with
\alpha=\mathbf{d}\lambda
, necessarily,
|\lambda|
is at least the distance
d(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})
. If
G
splits over
\mathbb{Z}
then we can find a complex
Z
which splits over a triangulated strip
[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}
. Any two simplices
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
in the strip give rise to a co-boundary
\alpha=\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}\in C^{2}_{c}(Z)
. So
\frac{|\alpha|}{\MP(\alpha)}\leq\frac{2}{d(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2})}
. By taking
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
to be arbitrarily far simplices in the strip we get
h^{1}(Z)=0
.

Note that
B
does not prove that inequality (
2.7
) holds for all
\lambda\in C^{1}_{c}(Z)
, but rather only for minimal ones:

Definition 3.5
.

A 1-cochain
\lambda\in C^{1}_{c}(Z)
is
minimal
(or a
minimizer
) if
|\lambda|=\MP(\mathbf{d}\lambda)
, i.e. if it is the minimal primitive of its coboundary.

Using this definition we can reformulate
B
as stating that: for
G
and
Z
as in the theorem there exists
c=c(Z)
such that if
\lambda\in C^{1}_{c}(Z)
is minimal then
|\lambda|\leq c|\mathbf{d}\lambda|.

4.
Locally minimizing tracks

The goal of this section is to prove that if
\Phi
minimizes
\TD(\Phi)
then most of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
is “locally minimizing”.

4.1.
Simple subtracks and the injectivity radius

Definition 4.1
.

A
subtrack
is a connected induced subgraph of
\mathcal{F}
or
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
.

Let
d^{*}(\cdot,\cdot)
be the intrinsic path metric on
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
(or
\mathcal{F}
) as a graph.
Note that the
d^{*}
distance between connectors in distinct tracks of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
is
\infty
.
For a connector
\mathbf{c}
and
R\geq 0
, we denote by
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{R}(\mathbf{c})
the ball of radius
R
in
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
.
This ball is a subtrack of
\mathcal{F}
, as it is necessarily connected.

If
e,e^{\prime}
are the edges of
Y
containing the connectors
\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{F}
respectively, then

(4.1)

d(e,e^{\prime})\leq D\cdot d^{*}(\mathbf{c},\mathbf{c}^{\prime}),\text{ and }\mathcal{B}^{*}_{R}(\mathbf{c})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{D\cdot R}(e).

Definition 4.2
.

A subtrack is
simple
if it meets each edge at most once.

In
\mathcal{F}
, all (sub)tracks are simple, but this is not necessarily the case for
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
.
The next lemma shows
that they are simple for scales which are smaller than the injectivity radius.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\bar{x}
be a vertex of
\overline{Y}
, the
injectivity radius
\injrad(\overline{Y},\bar{x})
of
\overline{Y}
at
\bar{x}
is the maximal
R
such that the quotient map
Y\to\overline{Y}
is injective on
\mathcal{B}(x,R)
for any lift
x\in Y^{0}
of
\bar{x}
.
The
injectivity radius
\injrad(\overline{Y})
of
\overline{Y}
is given by
\min_{\bar{x}\in\overline{Y}^{0}}\injrad(\overline{Y},\bar{x})
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Any subtrack of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
contained in
\mathcal{B}(\bar{x},\injrad(\overline{Y},\bar{x}))
is simple.
In particular, if
\injrad(\overline{Y})\geq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,R)
then for every connector
\mathbf{c}
in
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
, the subtrack
\mathcal{B}_{*}(\mathbf{c},R)
is simple.

Proof.

Let
\rho=\injrad(\bar{x},\overline{Y})
, and let
\overline{\lambda}
be a subtrack contained in
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(\bar{x})
. Let
x
be a lift of
\bar{x}
to
Y
. Since any subtrack of
\mathcal{F}
is simple, the lift
\lambda
of the subtrack
\overline{\lambda}
to
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x)
is simple.
The quotient map is injective on
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x)
so
\overline{\lambda}
is also simple in
\overline{Y}
.

By (
4.1
), the subtrack
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{R}(\mathbf{c})
is contained in the ball
\mathcal{B}_{D\cdot R+D}(x)
for the vertex
x
incident on the edge
e
containing
\mathbf{c}
. This implies the second part of the lemma when
\injrad(\overline{Y})\geq D\cdot R+D={\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,R)
.
∎

4.2.
The congruence locus

Definition 4.5
.

A subtrack is
regular
if it consists only of regular connectors and segments.
Two regular simple subtracks
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}
in
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
are
congruent
if they meet the same edges of
\overline{Y}
.
A regular simple subtrack
\lambda^{\prime}
is
N
-congruent
if
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
contains
N
distinct regular simple subtracks congruent to
\lambda^{\prime}
.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
R\geq 0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{c}
be a connector of a track
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
. Then
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
is
R
-locally
N
-congruent at
\mathbf{c}
if the ball
B^{*}_{R}(\mathbf{c})
is
N
-congruent.

The
R
-locally
N
-congruence locus

\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{R,N}
is the induced subgraph of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
on the set of
R
-locally
N
-congruent connectors.

The next lemma says that “most” of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
is contained in the
R
-locally
N
-congruence locus.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N},D\geq 0
and
R\geq 0
and assume
\injrad(\overline{Y})\geq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,R)
. Then

(4.2)

|\overline{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{R,N}|\leq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,R)\cdot N\cdot\vol(\overline{Y}).

Proof.

If
\rho={\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,R)
, then for every connector
\bar{\mathbf{c}}
of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
, the subtrack
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{R}(\mathbf{c})
is a subtrack in
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(\bar{x})
for some
\bar{x}\in\overline{Y}^{0}
.
If we assume
\injrad(\overline{Y})\geq\rho
then by
Lemma

4.4
,
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{R}(\bar{\mathbf{c}})
is simple for every connector
\bar{\mathbf{c}}
in
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
.

A connector
\bar{\mathbf{c}}
belongs to
\overline{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{R,N}
if
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{R}(\bar{\mathbf{c}})
is either not regular or not
N
-congruent. We give a bound in each case.

The number of dead-ends in
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(\bar{x})
is bounded by
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,\rho)
since the number of dead-ends in a 2-simplex is bounded by
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
.
Thus, the number of subtracks in
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(\bar{x})
that are not regular is bounded by
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,\rho)
.
It follows that the number of
\bar{\mathbf{c}}
for which
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{R}(\bar{\mathbf{c}})
is not regular is bounded by
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,R)\vol(\overline{Y})
.

The number
P
of congruence classes of simple subtracks in
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(\bar{x})
is bounded by
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,\rho)
.
Therefore, at most
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,\rho)\cdot N
of them are not
N
-congruent.
It follows that the number of connectors
\bar{\mathbf{c}}
for which
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{R}(\bar{\mathbf{c}})
is regular but not
N
-congruent is at most
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,\rho)\cdot N\cdot\vol(\overline{Y})
.
∎

4.3.
The congruence locus is locally minimizing

Lemma 4.8
.

For all
\kappa\geq 0
, and
N\geq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(\kappa)
, the following holds.
Let
\gamma
(resp.
\gamma^{\prime}
) be a simple
\kappa
-quasigeodesic path in
X
, and assume that it passes through the edges
f_{1},\dots,f_{N}
(resp.
f^{\prime}_{1},\dots,f^{\prime}_{N}
) in that order.
If
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{N}\}=\{f^{\prime}_{1},\dots,f^{\prime}_{N}\}
then either:

(i)

d(f_{1},f_{1}^{\prime})\leq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(\kappa)
and
d(f_{N},f_{N}^{\prime})\leq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(\kappa)
, or

(ii)

d(f_{1},f_{N}^{\prime})\leq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(\kappa)
and
d(f_{N},f_{1}^{\prime})\leq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(\kappa)
.

If moreover,
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
share an endpoint then
(i)
holds.

Proof.

Consider the subpath
\eta
of
\gamma
between
f_{1},f_{N}
, and the subpath
\eta^{\prime}
of
\gamma^{\prime}
between
f_{1}^{\prime},f_{N}^{\prime}
.
By our assumption
f_{1},f_{N}
are edges of
\eta^{\prime}
, and so by the Morse lemma,
\eta
is
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(\kappa)
close to a subpath of
\eta^{\prime}
.
Similarly,
\eta^{\prime}
is
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(\kappa)
close to a subpath of
\eta
.
The lemma follows.
∎

Definition 4.9
.

Let
R\geq 0
. A connector
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{F}
is
R
-locally minimizing
if
\mathcal{B}^{*}_{R}(\mathbf{c})
is minimal.

The following is the key result of this section, it shows that if
\Phi
minimizes
\TD_{Y}(\Phi)
then the congruence locus is locally minimizing.
Essentially the idea is that if a subtrack of the congruence locus is not minimal, then it cobounds a region with a minimizer of its coboundary (see
Figure

2
). A local change to
\Phi
in this region will reduce
\TD_{Y}(\Phi)
, contradicting minimality.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
D,R\geq 0
. If
\injrad(\overline{Y})\geq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,R)
and
N\geq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,R)
, then for every connector
\bar{\mathbf{c}}\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{R,N}^{0}
, if
\Phi
minimizes
\TD_{Y}(\phi)
, then
\bar{\mathbf{c}}
is
R
-locally minimizing.

Proof.

We will show that any subtrack
\overline{\lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{*}_{R}(\bar{\mathbf{c}})
is minimizing (and get in particular that
\overline{\lambda}=\mathcal{B}^{*}_{R}(\bar{\mathbf{c}})
is minimizing).
Consider its lift
\lambda
to
Y
.
By the definition of the congruence locus,
\overline{\lambda}
(and so
\lambda
) is a regular, simple,
N
-congruent subtrack.
Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}
be the
N
congruent copies of
\lambda
.
See
Figure

2

Figure 2.
The complex
Y
is on the left: the congruence tracks
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}
are shown in red and their edges in pink; a minimizing track
\beta
is shown in blue and its edges in light blue; and the region
\nu
is in purple.
The space
X
is on the right: The edges
f_{1},\dots,f_{N}
corresponding to
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}
are in red; the image of
\nu
under
\Phi
, in purple, has to be far away from
f_{i}
, while its image under
\Psi
, in orange, is closer, and thus
\Psi
has lower total distortion.

Let
\beta
be a minimizer with
\mathbf{d}\beta=\mathbf{d}\lambda
and assume for the sake of contradiction that
|\beta|<|\lambda|
.
By replacing
\lambda
by
\lambda-(\lambda\cap\beta)
and
\beta
by
\beta-(\lambda\cap\beta)
we may assume that
\lambda\cap\beta=\emptyset
.

The 1-cochain
\beta+\lambda

5.
Tautness

A 1-cochain is “taut” if it is contained in (a uniformly bounded neighborhood of) the convex hull of its coboundary.
The goal of this section is to prove that if
\lambda
is a local minimizer then it is taut.
This will be done in three steps: first showing that a taut primitive exists, then showing that any minimizer is taut and finally showing that local tautness implies global tautness.

6.
Thickness

In the previous section we saw that each locally minimizing track is taut. In particular,
|\lambda|
can be bounded by
|\conv(\mathbf{d}\lambda)|
.
In this section, we will use the fact that
G
is geometrically rigid to show that
|\conv(\mathbf{d}\lambda)|\leq|\mathbf{d}\lambda|
.
This is done by first showing that
\conv(\mathbf{d}\lambda)
is “barycentrically thick” - which roughly says that it does not contain long bottlenecks.

6.1.
Geometrically rigid hyperbolic groups

Definition 6.1
.

A hyperbolic group
G
is
geometrically rigid
if it is one-ended and
\partial G
does not contain a cut pair.

For a point
x_{0}\in X
and
\xi\in\partial X
,
\rho\geq r\geq 0
define the
annulus

A_{r}(x_{0},\xi,\rho)=\{\zeta\in\partial X\;|\;\rho-r\leq\left<\zeta,\xi\right>_{x_{0}}\leq\rho+r\}

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
G
be a geometrically rigid hyperbolic group. There exists
\Delta
such that for all
x_{0}
and
\xi\in\partial X
and
\rho\geq r
with
\rho-r\geq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
, the set
A_{r}(x_{0},\xi,\rho)
is path connected in
A_{r+\Delta}(x_{0},\xi,\rho)

Proof.

The set
A_{r}(x_{0},\xi,\rho)
can roughly be regarded as the difference of two balls in the visual metric.
The lemma then follows from
[
11
, Lemma 6.1]
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
G
be a geometrically rigid hyperbolic group, then for all
\epsilon\geq 0
, for all

Let
\epsilon\geq 0
, and

. Let
\gamma

.

Let

Let

.

Consider a point

It follows that

In other words,

By

, for

are connected by a path

By

In fact, using

6.2.
Local minimizers are plump

Definition 6.4
.

For

-slender

.

.

Lemma 6.5
.

For
\epsilon\geq 0
,

,

,

-plump at

.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon\geq 0
, and let

be such that every ball in

is contractible.
Denote

Assume that

.

and

.

as a 1-cochain in

.
If

then

is minimal in

.

is a 1-cocycle in

.
Since

Since

.

By

, there exists

6.3.
Barycentrical thickness of coboundaries

Definition 6.6
.

Let
M\geq 0
.
A point
x
is an
M
-barycenter of

if it is at distance
M
from the three sides of a geodesic triangle spanned by
y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}
.

A set

is
M
-barycentrically thick
if
every point in
\conv(A)
is an
M
-barycenter of a geodesic triangle spanned by points in
A
.

Remark 6.7
.

Roughly speaking, the following are equivalent:

(1)

A
is barycentrically thick

(2)

The nearest point projection of
A
to any geodesic is coarsely connected – that is, it cannot be separated into two sets whose projections to the geodesic are far.

(3)

The convex hull
\conv(A)
is plump away from points of
A
.

We have already proved that local minimizers are taut (
Lemma

5.10
) and plump away from their coboundary (
Lemma

6.5
).
In view of the equivalence above, we must have that the coboundary of local minimizers is barycentrically thick.
We make this precise in the next lemma.

Lemma 6.8
.

For
D\geq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
,
R\geq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D)
,
M\geq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,R)
if
\lambda

then
\mathbf{d}\lambda
is
M
-barycentrically thick.

Proof.

Let
C=\conv(\mathbf{d}\lambda)
.
Consider
x\in C
.
Our goal is to show that
x
is an
M
-barycenter of the points in
\mathbf{d}\lambda
.

Claim
.

For
D\geq{\boldsymbol{\delta}},\epsilon\geq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D),L\geq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,\epsilon)
and
R\geq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(D,\epsilon,L)
either

or
C
is
(L,\epsilon)
-plump at
x
.

Proof of Claim:

Assume that
d(x,\mathbf{d}\lambda)\geq R
and
C
is
(L,\epsilon)
-slender at

.
For
\epsilon\geq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
and
L\geq{\boldsymbol{\delta}}(\epsilon)
, the ball
\mathcal{B}(x,100\epsilon)\cap C
disconnects

into two components
C_{-},C_{+}
.
Each of
C_{-},C_{+}
must contain points of
\mathbf{d}\lambda
.
Since
\lambda
is connected, it therefore must contain some
\mathbf{c}\in\lambda\cap\mathcal{B}(x,100\epsilon)
.

By

5.10
, there exists

such that
\lambda\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{T}(C)
.

If
C
is
(L,\epsilon)
-slender then
\lambda
is
(L-100\epsilon,T+\epsilon)
-slender at

.
For large enough

and

this is impossible by
Lemma

6.5
.
∎

We divide the rest of the proof of the lemma into the two possible cases:

Case 1.

d(x,\mathbf{d}\lambda)\leq R
:

Say
d(x,y)\leq R
for some
y\in\mathbf{d}\lambda
. Then for
M\geq R
,
x
is an
M
-barycenter of the (very) degenerate geodesic triangle spanned by

.

Case 2.

d(x,\mathbf{d}\lambda)\geq R
and
and
C
is
(L,\epsilon)
-plump at

:

By definition of the convex hull
x\in[x_{-},x_{+}]
for some
x_{-},x_{+}\in\mathbf{d}\lambda
.
Since
C
is
(L,\epsilon)
-plump, there exists some point

.
Again, by definition of
C
,
y\in[y_{-},y_{+}]
for some
y_{-},y_{+}\in\mathbf{d}\lambda
.

Let
m_{-}\in[x_{-},x_{+}]
be the barycenter of the triangles

. That is,
m_{-}
is the point on
[x_{-},x_{+}]
at distance
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
from both
[x_{-},y_{-}]
and
[x_{+},y_{-}]
.
Similarly, define
m_{+}\in[x_{-},x_{+}]
to be a
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
-barycenter of the triangle

.

By considering the points

in the
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
-thin quadrilateral

Lemma 8.2
.

Let

be a characteristic subgroup of finite index with finite center. Then, the restriction map

Proof.

Let

Every automorphism of

\mathcal{L}
denotes the kernel of the map

.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{L}
then
\phi|_{K}=\id
, and for all

\phi(g)=gk
for some
k\in K
.
Let

be coset representatives of
K\leq G
, and let
