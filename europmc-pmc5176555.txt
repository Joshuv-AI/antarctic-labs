Further observations on the treatment of
kala-azar:
These include treatments which can be divided
under the following heads :?
(1) Treatment with tartar emetic and anti-
monyl sodium tartrate given intramuscularly.
(2) Treatment with lithium emetic given
intramuscularly.
(3) Treatment with aniline emetic given
intravenously.
(4) Treatment with antimonious oxide in a
state of fine sub-division given intravenously.
(5) Treatment with Luargol.
(6) Treatment with colloidal oxide of antimony.
(7) Treatment with intravenous injection of
bismuth tart, solubilis.
Treatments under (1) to (5) have been
described in detail in my treatise " Kala-azar, Its
Treatment," recently published by Butterworth
& Co. (India). They will not, therefore, be des-
cribed in detail here. The following conclusions
can be drawn from them :?
(1) Case treated with intramuscular injection
of tartar emetic and antimonyl sodium tartrate
improved remarkably. The injections were pain-
ful (dose 1 cc. to 1 ] cc. of 2 per cent, solution
given every 4th or 5th day).
(2) Case treated with lithium emetic given
intramuscularly showed much improvement in
blood condition but patient left hospital before
treatment was completed (dose '1 to '3 grammes).
The injections were painful.
(3) Cases treated with aniline antimonyl
tartrate given intravenously recovered completely
(dose 1 cc. to 10 cc. of 2 per cent, solution). The
febrile reactions as well as vomiting, purging or
rigors were much less common with this salt
than with potassium or sodium emetic. I cannot,
at present, state whether it is superior to sodium
or potassium emetic in its therapeutic effects.
4. Case treated with intravenous injection of
antimonious oxide in a state of fine subdivision was
subsequently treated with metallic antimony and
recovered completely (dose?'75 grs. to H grs.).
5. Case treated with Luargol showed improve-
ment in the blood condition, but the parasites
persisted. Patient left hospital before treatment
was completed.
6. The treatment of kala-azar with colloidal
oxide of antimony.
In the addendum to my treatise, a reference

7. Cases treated with intravenous injection of
bismuth tart, solubilis (bismuth sodium tartrate).
In a paper read before the Royal Society of
Medicine (January 9, 1909), Cuslmy pointed out
that arsenic, antimony, and bismuth killed
trypanosomes in the concentration of 1 : 200,000.
According to his observations bismuth proved too
poisonous to the host and the damage done was

I have given bismuth tart, solubilis intra-
venously in a few cases of kala-azar. The toxic
symptoms observed by Cushny in experimental

V. It is frequently observed that when the blood
of a kala-azar patient is mixed with excess of
distilled water, a white flocculent precipitate
forms. While this reaction is present in a large
majority of cases of kala-azar, it has also sometimes
been observed in other diseases, e.g., phthisis,
cancer of the liver, cirrhosis of the liver, chronic
malaria, and cases of enlarged spleen in which no
L. D. bodies were found on spleen puncture.
It has also been found that this precipitate is
obtained by mixing the separated serum from the
corpuscles with excess of distilled water. The
red corpuscles when dissolved in distilled water
do not give rise to any stich precipitate. The
precipitate is soluble in a solution of sodse bicarb,
and also in dilute acetic acid, as well as in normal
saline. It is insoluble in distilled water. It
seems that the precipitate is of the nature of a
globulin and is probably easily precipitated on
account of the diminished alkalinity of the blood
in kala-azar. Whether it is due to the presence
of any excess of globulin in the serum of kala-
azar cases, due to disintegration of leucocytes, or
whether this globulin is of a specific nature, has
not yet been determined. As regards its diag-
nostic importance, it is not of much value because,
as stated before, a similar precipitate has been
found in other diseases.
One remarkable property of this globulin-like
substance is that a solution of it in normal saline
inhibits the action of the complement in a
hemolytic system.
