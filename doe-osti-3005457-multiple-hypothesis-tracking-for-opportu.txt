Multiple Hypothesis Tracking for Opportunistic Transmitter Identification
Motivation

The exploitation of radio frequency (RF) signals from Low Earth Orbit (LEO) communication
satellites for the purposes of navigation is an active field of research. In this alternative
navigation system, navigation observables are passively extracted from existing RF signals.
Because these signals are not designed for navigation purposes and often have proprietary
signal structures that are unknown to the receiver, they lack much of the information
required for navigation systems or such information is unavailable to the receiver. This
information includes (1) the signal transmission time required for calculating the range
measurement to the satellite, (2) ephemeris and clock corrections and (3) unique
identifiers to distinguish between satellites. Recent work has been done to address these
problems. Specialized signal processing techniques to passively extract navigation
observables with limited knowledge of the signal characteristics have been developed [1].
Satellite ephemerides can be estimated using publicly available two line element sets
(TLEs) coupled with an orbital propagator. Advanced estimation techniques such as SLAM-
type tracking and differential positioning have been developed to mitigate the inherent
errors of TLEs [2] [3] [4] [5]. The study of stability of the lower quality clocks common to
communication systems has also been performed to develop dynamic models for
estimation of relevant clock terms [6]. One metaphorical stone still left unturned however
is that of associating received measurements with the predicted satellite ephemerides.
Prior works utilized post-processed data and assumed the origin of a particular
measurement was known. This is a reasonable concession to focus on other critical
aspects of the technology. However, as opportunistic RF navigation technology matures
and nears real-time implementation, a method for data association is needed.

This effort seeks to address the need for passive data association – a design which does
not require any decoding or demodulation of the received signal. Previous work has made
use of a simple heuristic approach and demonstrated its efficacy on the OneWeb
constellation. Associations were made based on a comparison of the normalized
innovation squared (NIS) value between all potential satellites. The satellite which yielded
the smallest NIS was selected [7]. It was shown in this work that geometric ambiguities
between satellites can cause incorrect associations. The OneWeb constellation is forgiving
in this regard – orbits are spaced far apart relative to anticipated receiver errors so incorrect
associations only occurred when the receiver errors were large. Applying this approach to a

larger constellation such as Starlink has been shown to be unusable even under near
perfect receiver and satellite states. This method failed because greedy algorithms such as
the minimum NIS approach, are rarely able to correct themselves if the initial association
was wrong. In the Starlink constellation, there are often multiple satellites that yield similar
NIS values. Selecting the smallest of these values at a single measurement epoch is not a
reliable method.

A Solution

To combat this limitation, a deferred logic approach is proposed. A multiple hypothesis
tracking (MHT) framework specialized for opportunistically received RF measurements will
be developed. A high-level overview of this framework is described in the following section.
The backbone of this algorithm is the use of a likelihood function to compare the
probability of each potential association. As such, a specialized likelihood function for
opportunistic transmitter identification will also be proposed. Lastly, the MHT algorithm
will be evaluated using simulated trajectories with ambiguous transmitter geometries.
Transmitter, receiver and measurement errors are modeled in these simulations and varied
to determine their effects on the algorithm’s ability to correctly associate measurements.
Geometries were designed to have at least one measurement epoch in which predicted
measurements were close to each other. Examples of these transmitter geometries are
shown in Fig. 1. The ability of the MHT algorithm to correctly associate transmitters with
received measurements will be compared with the NIS method. Additionally, the time of
convergence, or the amount of time required to correctly associate a transmitter with a
received measurement, will be used to evaluate the feasibility of this proposed framework
on a real-system.

This work makes the following contributions: (1) the likelihood function for an opportunistic
RF data association scenario will be derived, (2) a multiple hypothesis tracking framework
will be developed for opportunistic RF data association and (3) the proposed framework
will be evaluated on ambiguous transmitter geometries and will be compared to a heuristic
approach.

Multiple Hypothesis Tracking Framework

The primary idea surrounding the MHT is to delay making an association decision until the
likelihood of a particular association exceeds a specified threshold. The basic framework
of the MHT developed for this work is shown in Fig. 2. It consists of two categories of
estimation filters, the primary and association filters. The primary filter is designed to
localize the receiver. It is assumed for this work the primary filter does not track the
transmitter’s states. An extended Kalman filter (EKF) will be used for the primary filter. The
association filter is denoted by the dashed blue line box in Fig. 2. Its purpose is to estimate
the receiver and all candidate transmitter states during the association process. When an
association has been made, it will hand off its most recent estimates of the receiver and
selected satellite’s states to the primary estimation filter. The focus of this work is in
developing the association filter. Investigation of different primary filter designs is left for
future work.

The association filter is first initialized with the candidate transmitters’ and receiver’s
states. If the primary filter is already in operation, the most recent state estimate will be
used to initialize the receiver states in the association filter. Transmitters that fall outside of
the receiver’s visibility region are removed from consideration. States are propagated using
an appropriate dynamics model until a measurement is received. The likelihood function,
also known as the track score, is then calculated for each candidate transmitter and
received measurement combination. Associations with low tracks scores are removed
from consideration to mitigate computational complexity. Track scores are combined with
all previously calculated scores for each association and then converted to the
association’s probability. A tunable threshold is used to determine the acceptance of
associations. If none of the association probabilities exceed this threshold, the MHT will
save all remaining track scores and continue propagating the receiver’s and transmitters’
states. If any of the association probabilities exceed this threshold, the constituent
transmitter’s states are sent to the primary navigation filter for a measurement update and
the MHT algorithm will terminate until a measurement from a new source is received. If
unassociated measurements still remain after one association has been made, the MHT
algorithm will continue, removing the associated transmitter(s) from consideration, until all
remaining measurements are associated.
