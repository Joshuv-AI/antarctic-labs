Multigrid Preconditioning for FEEC using Mass-Lumping and Transforming Smoothers
Radovan Dabetić

Abstract
For PDEs naturally posed in the de Rham complex, structure-preserving mixed and saddle-point finite element discretizations typically produce indefinite linear systems. We propose a multigrid preconditioning framework that combines mass-lumped (explicitly invertible) FEEC mass matrices with transforming smoothers that map the operator to a block form with positive definite diagonal blocks, enabling Gauss-Seidel-type relaxation on the transformed system. Under mild h h -uniform norm-equivalence assumptions (and for trivial topology), we prove stability of the mass-lumped systems, and by extension spectral equivalence between the mass-lumped and original FEEC operators, which motivates using multigrid cycles designed for the mass-lumped operators as preconditioners for the consistent FEEC systems. While our primary focus is on algorithmic design rather than formal convergence theory, extensive numerical experiments on the Hodge-Dirac operator, mixed Hodge-Laplacians, and a magnetostatics saddle-point system in 2D and 3D demonstrate the robustness of the approach.



Keywords
: Multigrid, Mixed Finite Elements, Saddle-Point Systems, Transforming Smoothers, Distributive Relaxation, Hodge-Dirac Operator, Hodge-Laplacian, Maxwell’s Equations, Magnetostatics, Boundary Value Problem, FEEC, DEC

Introduction

As elucidated in
[
5
]
, solving the linear system arising from a finite-element discretization of the mixed formulation of the Hodge-Laplacian in
H_{0}(\CURL)
requires special care due to the indefiniteness and saddle-point form of the system. In
[
5
]
, a multigrid approach using a variable V-cycle on the Schur complement is given and under some assumptions, it is proven that the condition number of the preconditioned system is
\mathcal{O}(1)
.

In this article, we propose an alternative multigrid strategy that utilizes
transforming smoothers
on a mass-lumped system, in which the mass matrices possess explicit inverses. Our approach is motivated by the work in
[
7
]
, where spectral equivalence was shown between the systems obtained from FEEC and Discrete Exterior Calculus (DEC) discretizations of the Hodge–Dirac operator, and multigrid schemes based on transforming smoothers were observed to be efficient in practice.

To illustrate the mechanism of transforming smoothers, consider the concrete example of the mixed formulation of the Hodge-Laplacian on 1-forms. Formally, the system has the form (in vector proxies)

\begin{pmatrix}-I&-\DIV\\
\GRAD&\CURL\CURL\end{pmatrix}.

(1.1)

On the discrete level, we get a symmetric, but indefinite, linear system, hence smoothers like Gauss-Seidel or Jacobi cannot be expected to work.

At the continuous level, this operator can be (at least formally) transformed as follows:

\begin{pmatrix}-I&-\DIV\\
\GRAD&\CURL\CURL\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}-I&-\DIV\\
\GRAD&I\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}I-\DIV\GRAD&0\\
-\GRAD&\CURL\CURL-\GRAD\DIV\end{pmatrix}

(1.2)

If the discrete system mimics this behavior, we can use this transformed system for smoothening, as the transformed system is lower-block-triangular and the diagonal blocks are positive definite.

The necessity of mass-lumping arises naturally when attempting to replicate this continuous composition at the discrete level. Let
V_{h}
denote the chosen finite element space, and let
V_{h}^{\prime}
be its dual space. The standard Galerkin discretizations of the system and the transformation yield discrete operators
\mathsf{A}_{h}:V_{h}\to V_{h}^{\prime}
and
\mathsf{T}_{h}:V_{h}\to V_{h}^{\prime}
, respectively. Consequently, the direct composition
\mathsf{A}_{h}\mathsf{T}_{h}
is ill-defined: the range of
\mathsf{T}_{h}
lies in the dual space
V_{h}^{\prime}
, whereas the domain of
\mathsf{A}_{h}
is
V_{h}
.

To bridge these spaces, we must interpose the discrete Riesz isomorphism
\mathsf{R}_{h}:V_{h}^{\prime}\to V_{h}
, yielding the well-defined composition
\mathsf{A}_{h}\mathsf{R}_{h}\mathsf{T}_{h}
. In the matrix-vector setting, evaluating this exact discrete Riesz map is equivalent to multiplying by the inverse of the consistent mass matrix. Because the inverse of the mass matrix is dense in general, computing the exact composition is prohibitively expensive. This necessitates approximating the Riesz map by replacing the consistent mass matrix with a diagonal one. The mass-lumping will (in general) not lead to a consistent discretization, but it may still be useful as a preconditioner, as will be motivated in
Section

3
.

We will employ this approach to solve problems for the Hodge-Dirac operator, Hodge-Laplacian and a saddle-point problem arising from Maxwell’s equations (the magnetic potential in the magnetostatic case in the Coulomb gauge), which all fit into the framework of FEEC.

In
Section

2
, we introduce notation relating to differential forms and state the problems of interest.

In
Section

3
, the problems on the discrete level are formulated and it is proven that under relatively mild conditions on the mass-lumping, we have spectral equivalence of the mass-lumped and original problems.

In
Section

4
, the multigrid algorithms and smoothing procedures are described in detail. Finally, we show some empirical results in
Section

5
, demonstrating that this is a valid approach and that it merits further investigation.

\displaystyle\oplus_{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$$\perp_{\textnormal{FEEC}}$$}][c]{\perp_{\textnormal{ML}}}}

Moreover,

\square

Proposition 3.9
(Poincaré Inequality in Mass-Lumped Norms).

For
u_{h}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k,\perp_{\textnormal{ML}}}_{h}
it holds that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{k}\lesssim{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathsf{d}^{k}_{h}u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{k+1}.

\square

Proof 3
.

Let us decompose
u_{h}
into a
FEEC-orthogonal
decomposition

u_{h}=\mathsf{d}^{k-1}_{h}\alpha_{h}+\beta_{h}+\gamma_{h},

(3.1)

where
\alpha_{h}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k-1,\perp_{\textnormal{FEEC}}}_{h},\beta_{h}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k,\perp_{\textnormal{FEEC}}}_{h}
and
\gamma_{h}\in\mathfrak{H}^{k}_{h}
.

We now decompose
\alpha_{h},\beta_{h}
and
\gamma_{h}
using a
mass-lumped-orthogonal
decomposition, i.e.

\displaystyle\alpha_{h}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{d}^{k-2}_{h}\bar{\alpha}_{h}+\tilde{\alpha}_{h}+\alpha_{h,0},

\displaystyle\qquad\bar{\alpha}_{h}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k-2,\perp_{\textnormal{ML}}}_{h},\;\alpha_{h,0}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{H}}^{k-1}_{h},

\displaystyle\beta_{h}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{d}^{k-1}_{h}\bar{\beta}_{h}+\tilde{\beta}_{h}+\beta_{h,0},

\displaystyle\qquad\bar{\beta}_{h}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k-1,\perp_{\textnormal{ML}}}_{h},\;\tilde{\beta}_{h}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k,\perp_{\textnormal{ML}}}_{h},\;\beta_{h,0}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{H}}^{k}_{h},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\gamma_{h}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{d}^{k-1}_{h}\bar{\gamma}_{h}+\tilde{\gamma}_{h}+\gamma_{h,0},

\displaystyle\qquad\bar{\gamma}_{h}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k-1,\perp_{\textnormal{ML}}}_{h},\;\tilde{\gamma}_{h}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k,\perp_{\textnormal{ML}}}_{h},\;\gamma_{h,0}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{H}}^{k}_{h}.

We note that due to
\mathsf{d}^{k}_{h}\gamma_{h}=0
(it is harmonic) and
\tilde{\gamma}_{h}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k,\perp_{\textnormal{ML}}}_{h}
, we may imply that
\tilde{\gamma}_{h}=0
. We thus get the following form of the mass-lumped-orthogonal Hodge-decomposition of
u_{h}
:

u_{h}=\mathsf{d}^{k-1}_{h}\left[\tilde{\alpha}_{h}+\bar{\beta}_{h}+\bar{\gamma}_{h}\right]+\left[\tilde{\beta}_{h}\right]+\left[\beta_{h,0}+\gamma_{h,0}\right].

(3.3)

Recall that by the assumption of the lemma, we have
u_{h}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k,\perp_{\textnormal{ML}}}_{h}
, so by uniqueness of the decomposition, we follow
u_{h}=\tilde{\beta}_{h}
.
The rest essentially follows from applying the FEEC Poincaré inequality (see
[
2
, Theorem 5.2]
and also
[
12
]
) to
\beta_{h}
, or more precisely

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{k}

\displaystyle={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\tilde{\beta}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{k}\lesssim{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\beta_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{k}

\displaystyle[\text{Orthogonality of \eqref{eq:beta_h_decomp}}]

\displaystyle\lesssim\norm{\beta_h}_{{L^{2}}\Lambda^{k}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq\norm{\beta_h}_{{H}\Lambda^{k}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle[\text{\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{assumption:norm_eq}}]

\displaystyle\lesssim\norm{\dh[k]\beta_h}_{{L^{2}}\Lambda^{k+1}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle[\text{FEEC Poincar\'{e} \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{Arnold2018}{}{}, Theorem 5.2]}}]

\displaystyle=\norm{\dh[k]u_h}_{{L^{2}}\Lambda^{k+1}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle[\text{\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:feec_hodge}}]

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathsf{d}^{k}_{h}u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{k+1}

\displaystyle[\text{\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{assumption:norm_eq}}],

which concludes the proof.

\square

3.2
Hodge-Dirac Operator

3.2.1
Discrete Problem

In the discrete case, for the Dirac operator we seek
\mathfrak{u}_{h}\in\mathring{V}^{h}
such that

\mathcal{D}_{h}\left(\mathfrak{u}_{h},\mathfrak{v}_{h}\right):=\left\langle\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{u}_{h},\mathfrak{v}_{h}\right\rangle_{{L^{2}}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}+\left\langle\mathfrak{u}_{h},\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{v}_{h}\right\rangle_{{L^{2}}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}=\left\langle\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{v}_{h}\right\rangle_{{L^{2}}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}\quad\forall\mathfrak{v}_{h}\in\mathring{V}^{h}.

(3.4)

For

\mathcal{D}_{h}\left(\cdot,\cdot\right):\left(\mathring{V}^{h},\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{{L^{2}}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}\right)\times\left(\mathring{V}^{h},\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{{L^{2}}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}\right)\to\mathbb{R},

let
\mathbb{D}_{h}:\left(\mathring{V}^{h},\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{{L^{2}}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}\right)\to\left(\mathring{V}^{h},\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{{L^{2}}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}\right)^{\prime}
denote the induced operator and define

\mathsf{D}_{h}:=\mathsf{R}_{h}\mathbb{D}_{h}:\left(\mathring{V}^{h},\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{{L^{2}}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}\right)\to\left(\mathring{V}^{h},\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{{L^{2}}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}\right).

Equivalently,
\mathsf{D}_{h}
can be written as

\mathsf{D}_{h}=\mathsf{d}+\delta^{h}.

When replacing the
L^{2}
by the Sobolev norms, we can also regard the bilinear form as

\mathcal{D}_{h}\left(\cdot,\cdot\right):\left(\mathring{V}^{h},\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{{H}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}\right)\times\left(\mathring{V}^{h},\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{{H}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}\right)\to\mathbb{R},

in which case we denote the induced operator by
\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{h}:\left(\mathring{V}^{h},\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{{H}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}\right)\to\left(\mathring{V}^{h},\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{{H}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}\right)^{\prime}
.

Remark 3.10
.

By choosing a basis and instantiating
\mathbb{D}_{h}
as an explicit matrix, we retrieve the usual Galerkin matrix using the chosen basis of the finite element space.

\square

Applying mass-lumping (replacing the inner products), we get the corresponding bilinear form

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{h}\left(\mathfrak{u}_{h},\mathfrak{v}_{h}\right):=\left\llbracket\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{u}_{h},\mathfrak{v}_{h}\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket\mathfrak{u}_{h},\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{v}_{h}\right\rrbracket,

(3.5)

and the induced linear map
\widetilde{\mathbb{D}}_{h}:\left(\mathring{V}^{h},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket\right)\to\left(\mathring{V}^{h},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket\right)^{\prime}
. Analogously, we define the endomorphism
\widetilde{\mathsf{D}}_{h}:=\tilde{\mathsf{R}}_{h}\widetilde{\mathbb{D}}_{h}
. Equivalently,

\widetilde{\mathsf{D}}_{h}=\mathsf{d}_{h}+\tilde{\delta}^{h}.

We denote the induced map of the bilinear form on the mass-lumped discrete Sobolev space by
\overline{\widetilde{\mathbb{D}}}_{h}:\left(\mathring{V}^{h},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{{H}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}\right)\to\left(\mathring{V}^{h},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{{H}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}\right)^{\prime}
.

Using the Poincaré inequality, we can prove stability for the mass-lumped problem.

Proposition 3.11
(Inf-Sup Inequality,
[
12
, Theorem 6]
).

For all nonzero
\mathfrak{u}_{h}\in\mathring{V}^{h}
, there exists a nonzero
\mathfrak{v}_{h}\in\mathring{V}^{h}
satisfying

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathfrak{u}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{H}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathfrak{v}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{H}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}\lesssim\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{h}\left(\mathfrak{u}_{h},\mathfrak{v}_{h}\right).

\square

Proof 4
.

The proof is almost the same as the one from
[
12
, Theorem 6]
, but with the norms replaced by the mass-lumped variants. We will go through it for completeness’ sake.

Take the Hodge decomposition of

\mathfrak{u}_{h}=\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{r}_{h}+\mathfrak{w}_{h}

with
\mathfrak{r}_{h}\in\left(\ker\mathsf{d}_{h}\right)^{\perp}
and define
\mathfrak{v}_{h}:=\mathfrak{r}_{h}+\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{u}_{h}
.

The Poincaré inequality from
Proposition

3.9
together with the orthogonality of the Hodge decomposition from
Lemma

3.8
yields

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathfrak{v}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{H}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathfrak{r}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{H}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{u}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{H}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq c_{p}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{r}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{u}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathfrak{u}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{H}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)},

where
c_{p}\geq 1
is given through the Poincaré inequality and we used that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{u}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{H}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{u}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
. Note that
c_{p}\geq 1
has to hold, as we are estimating
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathfrak{v}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{H}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}
by its exterior derivative.

Substituting this into the bilinear form and once again using the Hodge decomposition and Poincaré inequality gives

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{h}\left(\mathfrak{u}_{h},\mathfrak{v}_{h}\right)

\displaystyle={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{u}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\left\llbracket\mathfrak{u}_{h},\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{r}_{h}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{u}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\left\llbracket\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{r}_{h},\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{r}_{h}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{u}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{w}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{r}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{u}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\frac{1}{2c_{p}^{2}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathfrak{w}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathsf{d}\mathfrak{r}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2c_{p}^{2}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathfrak{u}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{H}\Lambda\left(\Omega\right)}^{2},

where the last inequality follows from

. Combining this with (
3.6
) yields the statement.

\square

4
Multigrid

To avoid repeating derivations for the Hodge-Dirac, Hodge-Laplacian, and magnetostatics problems, we introduce a unified generic notation. We will develop a multigrid method for the
mass-lumped systems
using transforming smoothers, which will then serve as a preconditioner for the standard FEEC problem (see
Section

4.1
).

Let
H_{h}
and
\tilde{H}_{h}
denote the finite element spaces for the standard FEEC and mass-lumped discretizations, respectively. They are connected by a canonical isomorphism
\mathsf{J}_{h}:H_{h}\to\tilde{H}_{h}
(with dual
\mathsf{J}_{h}^{*}
) whose operator norms, along with its inverse, are
h
-uniformly bounded. The associated bilinear forms
\mathcal{G}_{h}\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{h}\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
induce the standard and mass-lumped Galerkin operators mapping to their respective dual spaces:

\mathbb{G}_{h}:H_{h}\to H_{h}^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{h}:\tilde{H}_{h}\to\tilde{H}_{h}^{\prime}.

Using the corresponding Riesz isomorphisms
\mathsf{H}_{h}:H_{h}^{\prime}\to H_{h}
and
\tilde{\mathsf{H}}_{h}:\tilde{H}_{h}^{\prime}\to\tilde{H}_{h}
, we can define the endomorphisms
\mathsf{G}_{h}:=\mathsf{H}_{h}\mathbb{G}_{h}
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}_{h}:=\tilde{\mathsf{H}}_{h}\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{h}
.

To formulate a multi-grid algorithm, we need a hierarchy of nested triangulations
\mathcal{T}_{h_{0}},\mathcal{T}_{h_{1}},\dots,\mathcal{T}_{h_{L}}
of the underlying domain. The grid levels are indexed by
\ell=0,1,\dots,L
, where
\ell=0
corresponds to the coarsest grid and
\ell=L
represents the finest target resolution. Each level is characterized by a mesh-width
h_{\ell}
, satisfying
h_{0}>h_{1}>\dots>h_{L}
. This sequence induces a nested hierarchy of FEM spaces:

H_{h_{0}}\subset H_{h_{1}}\subset\dots\subset H_{h_{L}}=H_{h}.

In what follows, we define the multigrid algorithm explicitly for the mass-lumped endomorphism
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}_{h}
. One could also work directly with
\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{h}
, and doing so requires adjusting the transfer operators and smoothers we are going to define below. However, both formulations remain algebraically equivalent. The reason we like to work with endomorphisms is to define the transforming smoothers, as elucidated in
Section

1
.

4.1
Preconditioning the FEEC Problems

To see how a multigrid method developed for the mass-lumped endomorphism
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}_{h}
is applied to solve the original FEEC Galerkin system, consider the target linear system

\mathbb{G}_{h}u=g,

(4.1)

where
g\in H_{h}^{\prime}
is a given right-hand side. We can construct a preconditioner
\mathsf{B}:H_{h}^{\prime}\to H_{h}
by using the inverse of the mass-lumped system:

\mathsf{B}\approx(\mathsf{J}_{h}^{*}\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{h}\mathsf{J}_{h})^{-1}=\mathsf{J}_{h}^{-1}(\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{h})^{-1}(\mathsf{J}_{h}^{*})^{-1}=\mathsf{J}_{h}^{-1}(\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}_{h})^{-1}\tilde{\mathsf{H}}_{h}(\mathsf{J}_{h}^{*})^{-1}.

(4.2)

Replacing the exact inverse
(\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}_{h})^{-1}
with the action of a multigrid cycle
\mathsf{M}_{\textnormal{MG}}\approx(\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}_{h})^{-1}
, we obtain the action of the preconditioner for the original FEEC problem:

\mathsf{B}:=\mathsf{J}_{h}^{-1}\mathsf{M}_{\textnormal{MG}}\tilde{\mathsf{H}}_{h}(\mathsf{J}_{h}^{*})^{-1}.

(4.3)

Evaluating the preconditioner for a given fine-grid residual
r\in H_{h}^{\prime}
therefore requires three steps:

1.

Map the FEEC residual to the mass-lumped dual space via
(\mathsf{J}_{h}^{*})^{-1}
, and apply the mass-lumped Riesz isomorphism
\tilde{\mathsf{H}}_{h}
to obtain the primal vector
\tilde{\mathsf{H}}_{h}(\mathsf{J}_{h}^{*})^{-1}r\in\tilde{H}_{h}
. Because
\tilde{\mathsf{H}}_{h}
corresponds to the inverse of a diagonal mass matrix, this operation is explicit and cheap.

2.

Apply a multigrid cycle
\mathsf{M}_{\textnormal{MG}}
.

3.

Map back to the original FEEC space using the inverse isomorphism
\mathsf{J}_{h}^{-1}
.

In practice,
\mathsf{J}_{h}
will correspond to an identity matrix (we use the same basis for
H_{h}
and
\tilde{H}_{h}
), so on the levels of vectors and matrices, it is a no-op.

Note that even if the multigrid method were to yield a self-adjoint operator, the resulting preconditioned operator is indefinite. Standard Krylov subspace methods, such as Preconditioned MINRES (or Preconditioned Conjugate Gradient (PCG), but our problem is fundamentally indefinite, so PCG is not applicable in any case), fundamentally require the preconditioner to be positive definite to induce a valid inner product. Because our preconditioner lacks this property, these algorithms are not applicable. Consequently, we are forced to use solvers for generic, non-symmetric and indefinite linear systems like GMRES to solve the preconditioned system.

4.2
Transfer Operators

To formulate the coarse-grid correction scheme, we define appropriate transfer operators between adjacent levels in our grid hierarchy. Consider a fine grid level
\ell

h_{\ell}

Because the spaces are nested, we have a canonical prolongation operator

4.5
Error Operator

Here, we briefly introduce the error operator and describe how it relates to the convergence of the method. For more details, see
[
10
]
.

Because the multigrid algorithm (
Algorithm

1
) constitutes a linear stationary iterative process, its convergence can be characterized by a single error propagation operator (or iteration matrix), denoted by
\mathsf{E}_{h_{\ell}}
. Let
u
be the exact solution to the discrete system
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}_{h_{\ell}}u=f
. For an approximation
u^{(m)}
obtained after
m
multigrid cycles, the error is defined as
e^{(m)}=u-u^{(m)}
. Due to the linearity of the method, this error evolves according to the relation
e^{(m)}=\mathsf{E}_{h_{\ell}}^{m}e^{(0)}
, where
e^{(0)}
is the error of the initial guess.

Following standard multigrid theory (cf.
[
10
, Lemma 7.1.4]
), the operator
\mathsf{E}_{h_{\ell}}
is defined recursively across the grid hierarchy. On the coarsest level, the exact solve eliminates the error entirely, yielding the base case
\mathsf{E}_{h_{0}}=0
. For any finer level
\ell\geq 1
, the error propagation operator is given by:

\mathsf{E}_{h_{\ell}}=\underbrace{\left[\Id-{\mathsf{T}}^{\mathrm{R}}_{h_{\ell}}\mathsf{Q}_{h_{\ell}}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}_{h_{\ell}}\right]^{\nu_{2}}}_{\text{post-smoothing}}\underbrace{\left[\Id-\hat{\mathsf{P}}_{h_{\ell-1}}\left(\Id-\mathsf{E}_{h_{\ell-1}}^{\gamma}\right)\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{-1}_{h_{\ell-1}}\check{\mathsf{P}}_{h_{\ell}}\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}_{h_{\ell}}\right]}_{\text{coarse-grid correction}}\underbrace{\left[\Id-{\mathsf{T}}^{\mathrm{R}}_{h_{\ell}}\mathsf{Q}_{h_{\ell}}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}_{h_{\ell}}\right]^{\nu_{1}}}_{\text{pre-smoothing}}.

(4.8)

The long-term convergence behavior of the algorithm is strictly governed by the spectral radius
\rho(\mathsf{E}_{h_{\ell}})
. The method achieves optimal,
h
-uniform asymptotic convergence if this radius is bounded away from unity independently of the mesh size, i.e.,
\rho(\mathsf{E}_{h_{\ell}})\leq\rho_{\text{asymp}}<1
.

6
Conclusion

In conclusion, we have presented a new multigrid approach for Hodge-Dirac operators and mixed formulations of the Hodge-Laplacian, as well as a saddle-point problem arising from magnetostatics within the framework of differential forms.

Its central feature is the use of transforming smoothers applied to mass-lumped systems, whose mass matrices possess explicit inverses and whose algebraic structure replicates desirable properties on the discrete level. The resulting transformed systems exhibit a block-triangular structure with positive definite diagonal blocks, making them amenable to smoothing, in contrast to the original systems, where standard schemes like Jacobi or Gauss-Seidel are not applicable.

Under mild conditions on the mass-lumping, we established stability of the mass-lumped problem, and by extension spectral equivalence to the FEEC problem.

Numerical experiments demonstrate that the proposed multigrid methods, when employed as preconditioners for
GMRES
, perform well in practice. Moreover, tests on meshes with nontrivial topologies show that the method remains robust in the presence of harmonic forms. These observations motivate further investigation of extensions (for example, to Stokes problems or problems with coefficient jumps) as well as a rigorous theoretical study encompassing a convergence theory for the multigrid scheme and a proof establishing uniform bounds on the
GMRES
iteration counts.
