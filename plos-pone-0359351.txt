Spatial layout planning of cable based on a hybrid method combining interference obstacle local post-insertion and improved Dubins curves

Xiaoyang Zhang, Zhigang Xu, Songkai Liu, Bing Cai, Bo Deng, Chao Kang

ABSTRACT
Cables in three-dimensional (3D) wall-adherent routing must simultaneously satisfy terminal direction vectors and bending curvature constraints, making forward design and precise pre-cutting a significant challenge. To address this, this paper proposes a hybrid planning method, termed IOLPDC, which combines interference obstacle local post-insertion with improved Dubins curves. The approach first utilizes an undirected graph spanning tree to unfold the 3D routing environment into a two-dimensional (2D) plane. Within this 2D domain, a multi-waypoint Dubins curve is introduced, and a post-insertion strategy is developed. This strategy generates a highly efficient Dubins path by initially ignoring obstacles, followed by binary-tree detouring, pruning, and redundant waypoint elimination to achieve a high-quality feasible path satisfying both directional and curvature constraints. Additionally, for local non-wall-adherent regions arising during 3D reconstruction, an engineering transition solver based on a 3D Dubins path is implemented via a Differential Evolution algorithm. Simulation results in a planar environment demonstrate that compared with Hybrid A* and RRT*Dubins, the proposed IOLPDC method reduces path length by 13.23% and 8.51%, and shortens median planning time by 50.00% and 34.38%, respectively. Experimental verification confirms that cables pre-cut according to the planned lengths precisely match the actual routed paths, effectively eliminating the traditional “cut-to-fit” process and demonstrating strong engineering practicality.

1 Introduction Cable path planning is a pivotal stage in the development of complex systems, such as aerospace vehicles, transportation equipment, and high-end mechatronic systems. The quality of cable routing directly governs assembly efficiency, structural reliability, and long-term operational safety; furthermore, it serves as a decisive factor affecting overall system performance [ 1 – 3 ]. In complex spatial environments, such as equipment cabins with densely arranged structures and narrow channels, traditional manual routing proves inefficient and inconsistent, failing to meet the rigorous demands of intelligent assembly and forward design [ 4 , 5 ]. Consequently, automated cable path planning under diverse engineering constraints has emerged as a critical research priority in computer-aided design and intelligent manufacturing. Early methodologies primarily relied on classical graph search algorithms, such as the Lee, Dijkstra, and A* algorithms [ 6 – 9 ]. While these methods provide intuitive and deterministic solutions for geometric shortest paths, they focus predominantly on path length while neglecting the essential curvature constraints required in engineering. To bridge this gap, many studies have adopted a two-stage strategy: initially generating an obstacle-avoiding polyline path and subsequently smoothing it via post-processing techniques, such as B-spline fitting or fillet rounding, to satisfy minimum bending radius requirements [ 10 , 11 ]. For instance, Kim et al. utilized B-spline optimization following a JPS-Theta search to generate natural routing shapes, while Qu et al. employed ant colony optimization for 3D branch pipelines with added fillets [ 12 , 13 ]. However, these two-stage strategies exhibit a fundamental limitation: post-processing smoothing can cause the trajectory to deviate from the original plan, potentially leading to collisions with previously avoided obstacles. This issue is particularly pronounced when the bending radius constraint is large relative to the available free space. To overcome the limitations of post-processing methods for curvature constraints, the field of mobile robot path planning has developed a series of methods that directly incorporate curvature constraints into the planning process, providing useful algorithmic references for cable routing. These include Hybrid A* with non-holonomic extensions, RRT*Dubins using Dubins curves as connection primitives, and the KBQ-RRT algorithm that achieves smooth motion planning in complex constrained environments through dual-tree optimization [ 14 – 18 ]. Dang et al. [ 19 ] introduced improved analytic expansions and motion primitive fine-tuning in Hybrid A*, effectively reducing collision risk and improving path smoothness for non-holonomic robots. Ye et al. [ 20 ] employed bidirectional tree optimization and motion primitive expansion in KBQ-RRT* to achieve smooth motion planning in complex environments. Although these curvature-constrained path planning methods have been successful in mobile robot scenarios, their direct application to cable routing faces fundamental challenges. When obstacles are dense, and the space is too narrow, Dubins paths may become infeasible [ 21 ]. RRT-based methods, due to random sampling, produce path solutions with high variance and many redundant bends, which significantly increase the need for clamps, saddles, and assembly costs; and Hybrid A* suffers from a sharp drop in search efficiency in cluttered environments. Beyond curvature constraints, cable routing must also satisfy wall-adherent requirements to reduce vibration and prevent interference with moving parts [ 22 , 23 ]. Hermansson et al. [ 24 ] used a Cosserat rod model and a contact potential energy field to make flexible harnesses automatically adhere to structural surfaces during assembly motion. Yang et al. [ 25 ] proposed an A*-ant colony hybrid algorithm that forces paths to be generated on equipment surfaces through wall-adherence penalty terms, clearance constraints, and surface mesh search spaces. Reducing the 3D wall-adherent routing problem to a 2D plane is also a feasible strategy. Rambeaud and Zeghloul [ 26 ] proposed a shortest path planning algorithm on polyhedral surfaces based on an unfolding strategy, whose core idea is to unfold the 3D polyhedral surface into a 2D plane, thus converting the 3D path planning problem into a classic 2D shortest path problem. Li et al. [ 27 ] used a similar method to unfold the developable structural surfaces inside complex aerospace cabins uniformly into a 2D plane. However, existing methods usually focus only on the smoothness of paths in the 2D unfolded plane and lack a systematic framework for managing local “out-of-plane” bending transitions that arise during the reconstruction of 2D paths into 3D space, particularly for cases where the start/end direction vectors of the cable are not coplanar with the laying surface. Based on the preceding analysis, cable path planning faces dual challenges: first, the simultaneous satisfaction of terminal orientation vectors and minimum bending radii. Second, the lack of systematic methods for addressing out-of-plane direction vectors during wall-adherent routing. To address these issues, this paper proposes a hybrid method combining interference obstacle local post-insertion and improved Dubins curves (IOLPDC). The primary innovations are summarized as follows: (1) A local post-insertion strategy for interfering obstacles is proposed. An initial baseline Dubins path is first generated ignoring obstacles, then binary-tree detouring, pruning, and redundant point elimination are performed for interfering obstacles. This approach avoids path distortion caused by post-processing smoothing from the outset, shifting the paradigm from the traditional “obstacle-avoidance-then-smoothing” sequence to a curvature-native planning approach, ensuring the exact reproducibility of path length in actual laying. (2) An undirected graph spanning tree is used to reduce the 3D wall-adherent environment to a 2D plane. For local cable segments where the start/end direction vectors are not coplanar with the laying surface when restoring the 2D routing path to the 3D space, a differential evolution algorithm is used to solve a 3D Dubins engineering transition path for corresponding local post-processing, ensuring the engineering feasibility of the planned cable path. The structure of this paper is as follows: Section 2 describes the planar unfolding method for the 3D wall-adherent cable routing space. Section 3 details methods about the IOLPDC algorithm framework. Section 4 validates the method through algorithm comparison and experiments. Section 5 concludes the paper and discusses future work. 2 Representation of 2D unfolding environments for 3D cables space To facilitate spatial cable routing, a planar unfolding strategy is adopted to convert the 3D spatial problem into a more tractable 2D equivalent. This study adapts the Denavit-Hartenberg (D-H) parameter convention—originally developed for robotic joint-link transformations—to model the unfolding process [ 28 ]. The unfolding of the routing space is conceptualized as a kinematic chain consisting of multiple rigid bodies undergoing rotational motion around common edges. Specifically, the shared edges between adjacent surfaces are treated as revolute joint axes. Local reference frames are established on each surface, with each traversed face serving as a geometric “link” within the kinematic chain. Following the D-H convention, the reference frame for each surface is systematically defined. Given that cables are subject to curvature constraints, the planned trajectory must maintain C 1 continuity (smoothness). To address this, this paper adopts the Dubins curve concept for multi-waypoint scenarios. As established by Dubins, the shortest planar path between two directed points under a maximum curvature constraint consists of maximum-curvature circular arcs and straight-line segments [ 29 ]. For planar cable planning, the layout area is denoted as G ( x , y ) . The routing requires the determination of the allowable bending radius r and vector parameters for the initial and terminal points, P s ( x s , y s , θ s ) and P t ( x t , y t , θ t ) . The optimal curve is selected by identifying the pattern with the minimum length. 10.1371/journal.pone.0359351.g002 Fig 2 Six fundamental Dubins path patterns under C 1 continuity constraints: (a) LRL, (b) LSL, (c) LSR, (d) RLR, (e) RSL, (f) RSR. Standard Dubins paths are primarily suited for shortest-path searches in obstacle-free environments. However, since actual cable environments contain various obstacles, the path must incorporate a series of waypoints to facilitate detouring. Consequently, a multi-waypoint Dubins curve planning method is adopted. Assume there are n waypoints P 1 , P 2 , … , P i , … , P n between the start point S and terminal point T ( Fig 3 ). The algorithm initiates by generating an initial baseline Dubins path between the initial and terminal points, momentarily disregarding all obstacles. Subsequently, the path is checked for interference with obstacles in a sequential manner. Upon detecting a collision, the algorithm identifies detour waypoints to modify the trajectory. Once an obstacle-free path is established, a redundancy check is performed iteratively to eliminate unnecessary waypoints. Finally, by traversing all potential detour branches, the most efficient feasible path is selected as the final trajectory. As obstacles are post-inserted into the path sequentially, a deterministic insertion order must be established. This study adopts a sorting method based on a proximity coefficient W i , calculated for each obstacle as: W i = T i − S i (9) where T i represents the Euclidean distance from the obstacle center to the cable terminal point, and S i denotes the distance from the obstacle center to the cable start point. As illustrated in Fig 5(b) , this ordering follows the intuitive progression of cable laying from the starting position to the destination and serves to maintain a consistent processing sequence during path updates. 3.3.2 Obstacle interference detection. A critical component of the proposed method is the accurate determination of path-obstacle interference. Given the 2D nature of the unfolded environment and the multi-segment structure of the improved Dubins path, the trajectory is decomposed into its constituent linear segments and circular arcs. Interference detection is then performed independently for each geometric primitive against the polygonal obstacles. In cable routing and related engineering domains, such as piping and equipment layout, approximating obstacles as polygons or bounding boxes is a standard simplification strategy to balance computational complexity with algorithmic feasibility [ 21 , 27 ]. Accordingly, in the proposed framework, obstacles are modeled as polygons to facilitate efficient interference detection while maintaining high fidelity to real-world geometric constraints. Under the proposed local post-insertion strategy, let N denote the total number of obstacles involved in the final detour paths. The number of vertices per convex polygon obstacle is assumed to be a constant, m . In the PSO algorithm, P represents the population size, I denotes the maximum number of iterations, and the maximum number of waypoints in a single path is of the same order as N , denoted as O ( N ) . Space Complexity: The algorithm maintains all explored binary-tree branches and their associated trajectory data. Each branch stores a sequence of waypoints, each defined by planar coordinates and an orientation angle; thus, the maximum storage required per path is O ( N ) . For a total of N obstacles requiring detours, the worst-case number of branch paths is 2 N , leading to an overall space complexity of O ( 2 N · N ) . In practical scenarios, if K obstacles are situated on the environment boundary, the complexity is reduced to O ( 2 N − K · N ) . Time complexity: For a single optimization of an N-waypoint problem using PSO, the worst-case time complexity is O ( P · I · N ) . For path search involving N waypoints, from sequential obstacle insertion to sequential redundant point detection, assuming a total of 2 N PSO optimizations, the worst-case time complexity for a single feasible path solution is O ( 2 P · I · N 2 ) . For K obstacles located on the environment boundary, the maximum number of binary-tree branches is 2 N − K , so the worst-case time complexity of the complete binary-tree search is: T ( N ) = O ( 2 N − K + 1 · P · I · N 2 ) (20) It should be noted that the computational complexity of each interference detection and tangent point search is O ( m ) (where m is the number of obstacle vertices), which is negligible compared to the cost of the PSO optimization for improved Dubins paths; therefore, the above complexity analysis mainly reflects the dominant costs of PSO and post-insertion iterations. Although the expression grows exponentially, in practice the pruning mechanism significantly reduces the number of effective branches, and N is the number of obstacles actually involved in all detour paths, which is usually much smaller than the total number of global obstacles M . Moreover, the complexity of this algorithm is independent of the overall environment size, allowing it to complete planning within acceptable time for practical cable layout scenarios. 3.4 Cable bending processing from planar path to 3D spatial environment 3.4.1 Problem description. Given 3D points P 0 and P 1 with direction vectors d 0 and d 1 , the goal is to solve for a 3D Dubins engineering transition path using an arc-straight-arc pattern. This engineering transition solver constructs the curve as a sequence comprising arc S 0 (from P 0 to E ), straight segment L E F (from E to F ), and arc S 1 (from F to P 1 ). Continuity is maintained such that the initial tangent of S 0 aligns with d 0 , and its terminal tangent aligns with L E F , as shown in Fig 11 . A hybrid planning framework, designated as IOLPDC, was developed, incorporating a systematic mapping between 3D environmental surfaces and 2D unfolded planes. The cornerstone of this framework is a local post-insertion strategy that initiates by generating an unconstrained Dubins path, followed by binary-tree-based detours for conflicting obstacles and iterative redundancy pruning. Unlike the traditional “avoidance-before-optimization” paradigm, this approach significantly mitigates computational overhead while preventing the path distortion inherent in conventional post-processing smoothing. Experimental validation within the tested multi-surface environments demonstrated that IOLPDC outperforms Hybrid A* and RRT*Dubins, reducing path lengths by 13.23% and 8.51%, and shortening median planning times by 50.00% and 34.38%, respectively. The integration of undirected spanning trees and the DE-optimized 3D Dubins engineering transition solver successfully resolved the challenges associated with dimensionality reduction and non-coplanar transitions, ensuring a high-fidelity reconstruction of the cable path throughout the entire 3D-to-2D planning and 3D restoration process. From an industrial perspective, this research transforms the traditional “cut-to-fit” on-site workflow into a forward design paradigm, enabling precise pre-fabrication and streamlined installation. While the current 3D-to-2D unfolding mechanism is optimized for developable surfaces (e.g., electrical cabinets), its potential for complex pipeline layout planning remains significant. It should be noted that the 3D-to-2D unfolding mechanism proposed in this study is predicated on the assumption that structural surfaces are developable (e.g., those typical of electrical cabinets) and is currently inapplicable to non-developable geometries, such as hyperbolic panels. Consequently, future research will explore approximate unfolding techniques for such complex surfaces, while simultaneously extending the current 3D Dubins engineering transition solver from the standard “arc-segment-arc” configuration to more sophisticated multi-arc coupled models for intricate environments. Furthermore, to bridge the gap between algorithmic validation and industrial practice, future investigations will transition from simplified polygonal obstacle representations to high-fidelity geometric models, thereby enhancing the overall engineering robustness and applicability of the method. Highlights A novel local post-insertion strategy (IOLPDC) is proposed to integrate curvature constraints directly into the planning process, avoiding path distortion from traditional smoothing. A 3D Dubins engineering transition solver based on differential evolution is presented to handle non-coplanar orientation vectors, ensuring high fidelity between planned and actual cable installation. An undirected graph spanning tree method is developed to systematically unfold complex 3D wall-adherent environments into 2D planes for simplified path optimization.
