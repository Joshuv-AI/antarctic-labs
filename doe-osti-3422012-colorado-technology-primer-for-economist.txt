 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 NLR will assist the Colorado School of Mines (Mines) in supporting a series of one-week
 training workshops. This proposed training program will be two, week-long summer school
 sessions in each of the next two years to help give early career economists and social scientists a
 solid introduction and grounding on the technical components of the electrical distribution,
 transmission, and generation systems as well as basic to advanced overview of clean energy and
 traditional generation systems.

 CRADA benefit to DOE, Participant, and US Taxpayer:

 • Assists laboratory in achieving programmatic scope
 • Enhances the laboratory’s core competencies
 • Uses the laboratory’s core competencies
 • Enhances U.S. competitiveness by utilizing DOE developed intellectual property and/or
 capabilities.

 Summary of Results:

 Task Descriptions and Estimated Completion Dates

 Day O Evening: Arrival

 Participants arrive at Denver International Airport and are transported to lodging in or near
 Golden. We will offer advice on how to get to Golden and arrange shuttle service if there is
 demand, however we suspect ride sharing services like Uber and Lyft will be the preferred
 choice of participants.

 Day 1 Late Morning: Welcome and Goals of the Week. This session will be a short session to
 welcome participants, let them introduce themselves, and provide a description of the goals of
 the summer school.

 Day 1 Afternoon: Principles of Power System Planning and Operations
 (O'Neill/Coddington/Ammerman)

 Discussions in this session will include basic principles of power systems planning
 (identification of grid needs and options) and operations (real-time control of grid assets). The
 training will also cover the modeling tools involved in these practices, including an overview of
 generation and transmission expansion models (covering cost-benefit analysis and capacity
 valuation), understanding generating unit economic dispatch and how it is modeled, and the
 basics of power flow. The subject matter will be presented in the context of increasing variable
 generation on electric systems and how flexibility options need to be incorporated and
 economically incentivized.

 2

 Understanding this necessitates explaining the basics of ancillary grid services that are impacted
 by the changing resource mix on the grid. An overview of solar and wind power forecasting will
 be provided as an example of integration practices and to showcase the need for information
 processing and high-speed communication. We will then focus on the linkages between
 distribution system modeling and transmission system modeling and explain the challenges for
 utilities navigating the world of increasing DERs

 Day 2 Morning: Field Trip; NLR Tour

 Visit the NLR Energy Systems Integration Facility (https://www.nlr.gov/esif/). This facility hosts
 groundbreaking research involving Grid Modernization with a focus on integration of new
 technologies at the distribution level. Highlights include laboratories that demonstrate residential
 and small commercial scale photovoltaics, energy storage, electric vehicles, smart homes and
 appliances, and smart meters. There are labs working on integration of these technologies into
 the distribution management systems.

 Day 2 Afternoon: Industrial Organization of Electricity Markets (Maniloff and Kiesling)

 The electricity industry's business model is determined a web of interlocking regulatory and
 market forces. Some firms act as regulated monopolists who can internalize most of the costs and
 benefits of their decision to, for example, invest in distributed generation. Others compete for the
 right to put electricity on the transmission grid and thus might see investment in distributed
 generation as a competitor and use their market and political power to discourage such
 investment. In order to understand how to these firms make decisions, one must understand
 mechanism by which revenues flow to firms and what costs are borne by the firm and which by
 other entities. The subject matter presented here will be a detailed and specific discussion of the
 industrial organization of electricity markets, both regulated and deregulated. As many of the
 participants in this summer school will have taken a general industrial organization and/or
 environmental economics graduate course, this session will build on their general knowledge to
 provide specific context as it relates to decisions made in the electricity industry.

 Day 3 Morning: Distribution System Principles and the evolving interface with the bulk power
 system (Coddington/Ammerman)

 This session will be focused on the electrical utility systems that are designed to deliver power
 and energy to the end-user, the electric distribution system. That same distribution system is now
 being asked to host DER and allow the free-flow of energy from DER to consumers.

 The session will focus on the basics of the distribution system and its design, and will include
 material that will allow the student to better understand how the system is designed, the basic
 components used within distribution circuits, how voltage levels are maintained (and why that is
 critically important), how protection devices are designed into the system in order to ensure safe
 and reliable distribution system operation, and more.

 3

 The session will then move into the integration of DER and the operational challenges that come
 with DER including voltage regulation, response to abnormal conditions, islanding, etc. The
 instructors will provide background on the standards and codes that are necessary and critical for
 the proper operation of the grid and the DER tied to it, and how those standards and codes are
 used by regulatory agencies.

 Day 3 Afternoon: Presentation by an industry distribution company representative with industry
 experience with integrating DER and other smart grid technologies. This is likely to be a speaker
 from Xcel Energy or another local utility. Representatives will include an operator/manager of a
 central district system such as the system which serves the University of Colorado, and whose
 presentation will focus on the unique challenges of operating a campus utility system.

 Day 4 Afternoon: Critical Issues for distribution systems moving forward
 (Coddington/Ammerman)

 The session will wrap up the electric system overview with a discussion of how electric utilities
 plan for new load growth and to maintain high levels of reliability - a function known as Electric
 Distribution Planning (EDP). EDP planners are now being asked to include DERs rather than
 just load growth when considering the long-term needs for distribution systems. Some states,
 such as California, Hawaii, New York, Minnesota, Massachusetts and Washington, D.C., are
 asking utilities to plan their distribution systems for both load and DERs, and that approach will
 have a significant impact on the cost factors for rate design in the utility territories in those states.
 Another important topic that will be covered will be grid modernization and the deployment of
 systems such as Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI), or smart meters, and other devices
 that are being used to automate the distribution systems throughout the world.

 Day 5 Morning: Understanding the Highs and Lows of Multidisciplinary Research
 (Lange/Pless/Arent)

 This session will discuss some of the main issues that economic and social science researchers
 should consider when undertaking research with engineers and technologists. Such
 multidisciplinary research is often derailed through different expectations around timing and
 expertise of the people involved. For instance: How are referees at engineering journals different
 than within social science? What are the different norms of practice within each community?
 What is the best approach to engage utilities or other potential data providers? And more. We
 will share our experiences and learned best practices so that the participants leave the summer
 school fully aware of the task ahead of them with respect to working across disciplines.

 Lastly, voluntary social events are planned for summer school participants, including a short hike
 to one of the Table Mountains in Golden. These will allow the participants and instructors to
 learn more about each other in an informal social atmosphere.

 Colorado School of Mines, in close collaboration with NLR, hosted 14 academics and
 professionals during a weekend session held October 22-24 of 2021. Classroom activities took
 place at the CSM campus. COVID-19 restrictions did not allow us to visit NLR facilities.

 4

 Speakers from CSM and NLR made up the bulk of the sessions in the primer, but additional
 speakers from several industry organizations also spoke and interacted with the participants.

 The following table lists the attendees of the primer session held in October 2021. There was a
 nice mix of backgrounds for the participants. There were 25 applicants (12 female and 12 non-
 white applicants). We initially accepted 16 applicants to participate (10 female and 6 non-white).
 When we put out the Call for Participants it was expected that international travel would open
 up, however it did not. So we have 6 people who we initially expected to attend but they were
 not allowed to enter the United States.

 NLR provided advice and guidance on the training workshop agenda, advised and made
 introductions and helped secure outside speakers for the workshop, as needed, and in mutual
 agreement with CSM.

 CSM and NLR leads used their previous experience with what aspects of the Primer are the most
 useful in our and the previous participants’ opinions to shrink the schedule. The link between the
 practical applications, as the industry speakers provide, and the theoretical systems, as the NLR
 and academic speakers provide, is a strength of the Primer. The Principal Investigator from
 CSM and co-lead from NLR noted that the participants interacted with each other not only
 during the classroom time, but also during extracurricular activities, which is seen as a metric of
 success in that it may lead to future collaboration.
