Simion Breaz, Andrei Mărcuş and George Ciprian Modoi

Abstract
. Consider an adjoint triple of triangle functors between two nice enough triangulated categories. In this paper, we are looking for conditions under which the silting, respectively, cosilting property ascends or descends via at most left, respectively, at most right adjoint.



Introduction

Let
\varphi_{*}:\mathcal{D}_{2}\to\mathcal{D}_{1}
be a triangle functor between two nice enough (for simplicity, let say, compactly generated) triangulated categories. Assume that it has a left adjoint
\varphi^{*}:\mathcal{D}_{1}\to\mathcal{D}_{2}
, or a right adjoint
\varphi^{!}:\mathcal{D}_{1}\to\mathcal{D}_{2}
, or sometimes both. Consider two objects
T_{1},C_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
and denote
T_{2}=\varphi^{*}T_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
and
C_{2}=\varphi^{!}C_{1}
. The main question investigated in the present paper is to find validity conditions for the following implications: "
T_{1}
is silting
\Rightarrow T_{2}
is silting", "
T_{2}
is silting
\Rightarrow T_{1}
is silting", "
C_{1}
is cosilting
\Rightarrow C_{2}
is cosilting" or "
C_{2}
is cosilting
\Rightarrow C_{1}
is cosilting". For the first two, we use the terminology "The silting property ascends, respectively, descends via
\varphi^{*}
", and for the last two, we will say "The cosilting property ascends, respectively, descends via
\varphi^{!}
".

The ascending and descending question for various properties is an old subject of investigation in commutative algebra. The aspect envisioned here is the possibility to transfer the property from the global situation to the local one, and conversely. For example, if
\varphi:A\to B
is a morphism of commutative rings, then the property "being projective" relative to an
A
-module ascends via induction functor

\varphi^{*}=-\otimes_{A}B:\hbox{\rm Mod-}{A}\to\hbox{\rm Mod-}{B}
. If, moreover,
\varphi
is faithfully flat, then the same property descends via
\varphi^{*}
(see
[
15
]
). Some notions as (co)silting are properly defined at the derived level. Therefore, we need to replace the tensor functor with the derived version
\varphi^{*}=-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{A}B:\mathbf{D}({A})\to\mathbf{D}({B})
. This is the main subject of investigation in
[
9
]
. In the present paper, we generalize some results in
[
9
]
(see Example
3.3
, for the precise relationship between the main settings here and there). Instead of the derived category
\mathbf{D}({A})
, over a ring
A
, we consider here an arbitrary compactly generated triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
. The commutativity of
A
is translated here in the hypothesis that
\mathcal{D}
is a closed tensor triangulated such that the tensor unit
\mathbb{S}
is itself compact and silting. Note that this hypothesis is general enough to include not only the derived category over a commutative ring, but also the homotopy category of spectra. We are able to translate, in the new more general setting, all the main results in
[
9
]
related to the ascending part. However, the descending part seems to be more complicated and interesting. Even if we found a way to interpret faithfully flatness in the new setting, for the descending of the silting property, we were forced to assume in Theorem
5.1
and Corollary
5.2
a stronger hypothesis (compared with the respective result in
[
9
]
), namely
T_{1}
to be compact.
Generalizing known results may be interesting by itself, but without telling something new about the original situation may be considered of limited value. Theorem
5.6
shows that this is not the case here. The general approach allowed us to focus on the relevant properties, leading to a proof of the fact that the descending property holds under some mild hypotheses not considered before.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
1
the main notions are defined and the main settings are established. In Section
2
some characterizations for the (co)silting property are recalled through some properties that remind us of the definition of (co)tilting objects. Necessarily and sufficient conditions for these properties to migrate via the functors
\varphi^{*}
or
\varphi^{!}
are also recorded. The fact that the (co)silting property ascends in tensor triangulated categories is proved in Section
3
, more precisely in Theorem
3.5
. Moreover, if
T_{1}
and
T_{1}^{\prime}
are equivalent silting objects in
\mathcal{D}_{1}
, then the induced silting objects
T_{2}
and
T_{2}^{\prime}
are equivalent in
\mathcal{D}-2
, and an analogous statement holds for the cosilting case (see Corollary
3.6
). In Section
4
we interpret the faithfully flatness in terms of adjunctions between compactly generated triangulated categories, and we call quasi faithfully flat such an adjunction. With the hypothesis that the adjunction is quasi faithfully flat we prove in Corollary
4.5
that if the silting of finite type objects
T_{1}
and
T_{1}^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{D}_{1}
induces equivalent silting (necessarily of finite type) objects in
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, then
T_{1}
and
T_{1}^{\prime}
are also equivalent. Finally, in Section
5
, we formulate and prove our versions for the descending results for silting in Corollary
5.2
and for cosilting in Theorem
5.6
.

1.
Preliminaries and notations

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category, whose shift functor is denoted by
(-)[1]
. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
the
n
-th iteration of the shift functor is denoted by
(-)[n]
. All our subcategories are full therefore, we identify a subcategory with the respective class of objects. If
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
is such a class, we denote
\mathcal{X}[n]=\{X[n]\mid X\in\mathcal{X}\}
, for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, for
N\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
we set
\mathcal{X}[N]=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{X}[n]
; if
\mathcal{X}=\{X\}
is a singleton, we suppress unnecessary brackets, and we write simply
X[N]
for this class. Often we write
[<0]
,
[\leq 0]
etc. instead of
[N]
for the respective subset of
\mathbb{Z}
.

For
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
, we say that
X
is orthogonal to
Y
, provided that
\mathcal{D}(X,Y)=0
. This notion extends easily from objects to classes of objects as follows: If
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
we write
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
for the class of all abelian groups
\mathcal{D}(X,Y)
with
X\in\mathcal{X}
and all
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
. So we say that
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are orthogonal if
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})=0
. We define

\mathcal{X}^{\perp}=\{Y\in\mathcal{D}\mid\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X},Y)=0\}\hbox{ and }{{}^{\perp}\mathcal{X}}=\{Y\in\mathcal{D}\mid\mathcal{D}(Y,\mathcal{X})=0\}.

Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
be a subcategory. We call this subcategory:

•

closed under extensions
if for every triangle

X\to Y\to Z\to X[1]
in
\mathcal{D}
we have
Y\in\mathcal{X}
, whenever
X,Z\in\mathcal{X}
;

•

(co)smashing
if it is closed under coproducts (products) which exits in
\mathcal{D}
;

•

(co)suspended
if it is closed under extensions and positive (negative) shifts. If coproducts exist in
\mathcal{D}
then we can construct
\mathrm{Susp}(\mathcal{X})
as the smallest suspended and smashing subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
containing
\mathcal{X}
.

A
torsion pair
in
\mathcal{D}
is a pair
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
of subcategories
\mathcal{D}
such that:

(1)

Both
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
are closed under direct summands.

(2)

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})=0
.

(3)

Every object
X\in\mathcal{D}
lies in a triangle
U\to X\to V\to U[1]
with
U\in\mathcal{U}
and
V\in\mathcal{V}
.

It is clear that if
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a torsion pair, then
\mathcal{U}={{}^{\perp}\mathcal{V}}
and
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{U}^{\perp}
. Consequently
\mathcal{U}
is closed under coproducts and
\mathcal{V}
is closed under products that exist in
\mathcal{D}
. Moreover, both of them are closed under extensions. We say that
\mathcal{U}
is the
aisle
, respectively
\mathcal{V}
is the
coaisle

of the torsion pair
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
. Let
\mathcal{G}
be a set of objects from
\mathcal{D}
. We say that the torsion pair
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is
generated by
\mathcal{G}
if
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{G}^{\perp}
.

A torsion pair is called
t-structure
(
weight structure
, or briefly
w-structure
) if in addition
\mathcal{U}
is closed under positive (respectively negative) shifts, that is, if
\mathcal{U}
is (co)suspended. Note that the closure of
\mathcal{U}
under positive (negative) shifts, that is,
\mathcal{U}[1]\subseteq\mathcal{U}
(respectively,
\mathcal{U}[-1]\subseteq\mathcal{U}
) is equivalent to
\mathcal{V}[-1]\subseteq\mathcal{V}
(respectively,
\mathcal{V}[1]\subseteq\mathcal{V}
). For a t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
in
\mathcal{D}
the triangle

U\to X\to V\to U[1]
given in the definition of a torsion pair above is functorial in
X\in\mathcal{D}
and gives rise to a right adjoint
X\mapsto U
of the inclusion functor
\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{D}
and to a left adjoint
X\mapsto V
of the inclusion functor
\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{D}
(see
[
4
]
).
We call this triangle the
t-truncation triangle
associated to
X
. In the case of a w-structure, we still call a triangle as above a
w-truncation triangle
associated to
X\in\mathcal{D}
, but this triangle is not more unique in general (see
[
7
]
). A t-structure (or a w-structure)
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is called
nondegenerate
if
\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{U}[n]=0=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{V}[n]
.

The next result is a slight generalization of a well-known result. Actually, the result is classical provided that the torsion pairs considered below are t-structures, and the (straightforward) proof depends only on the fact that the aisle and the coaisle are left, respectively, right orthogonal to each other.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
be two triangulated categories each of them being endowed a torsion pair
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{1})
, respectively
(\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{V}_{2})
. If
\varphi^{*}:\mathcal{D}_{1}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{D}_{2}:\varphi_{*}
is an adjoint pair, then
\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{U}_{1})\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{2}
if and only if
\varphi_{*}(\mathcal{V}_{2})\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{1}
.

In the hypotheses of the above lemma, we say that
\varphi^{*}
is
right exact
and
\varphi_{*}
is
left exact
with respect to the respective torsion pairs (sometimes these torsion pairs are tacitly understood), provided that
\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{U}_{1})\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{2}
, respectively
\varphi_{*}(\mathcal{V}_{2})\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{1}
. If the torsion pairs are t-structures (or w-structures) and we use the more classical terminology left/right t-exact (or w-exact). A functor
which is both left and right t-exact (w-exact) will be called simply
t-exact
(w-exact).

A TTF triple is a triple
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})
of subcategories of
\mathcal{D}
such that both
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
and
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})
are torsion pairs. This TTF triple is called (co)suspended, provided that
\mathcal{V}
is closed under positive (negative) shifts, that is, provided that
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a w-structure (t-structure) and
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})
is a t-structure (respectively, w-structure).

We assume that
\mathcal{D}
has coproducts. An object
T\in\mathcal{D}
is called
silting
if it induces a t-structure
(T[<0]^{\perp},T[\geq 0]^{\perp})
. Dually, if
\mathcal{D}
has products, an object
C\in\mathcal{D}
is called cosilting if it induces a t-structure
({{}^{\perp}C[\leq 0]},{{}^{\perp}C[>0]})
. This t-structure is called the (co)silting t-structure induced by
T
(respectively
C
). Two (co)silting objects are said to be equivalent if they induce the same t-structure. If we assume that the ambient triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
is compactly generated, then the (co)silting t-structure extends to a suspended TTF triple

\left({{}^{\perp}(T[<0]^{\perp})},T[<0]^{\perp},T[\geq 0]^{\perp}\right),\hbox{repectively }\left({{}^{\perp}C[\leq 0]},{{}^{\perp}C[>0]},({{}^{\perp}C[.0]})^{\perp}\right).

More precisely, according to
[
1
, Theorem 4.11]
there is a bijective correspondence between:

(1)

equivalence classes of silting objects;

(2)

suspended TTF triples
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})
for which the t-structure
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})
is nondegenerate and
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{S}^{\perp}
for a a set (and not a proper class) of objects
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
.

The equivalence between two silting objects can be tested in a simple way:

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category with coproducts and let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two silting objects in
\mathcal{D}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

T
and
T^{\prime}
are equivalent.

(ii)

\mathrm{Add}(T)=\mathrm{Add}(T^{\prime})
.

(iii)

T^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Add}(T)
.

(iv)

T\in\mathrm{Add}(T^{\prime})
.

Proof.

The implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) follows from
[
13
, Theorem 2]
, where
\mathrm{Add}(T)
is described in terms of the silting t-structure. The implications (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) and (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iv)
are obvious, and we only have to show (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i), since for (iv)
\Rightarrow
(i) we only have to twist
T
and
T^{\prime}
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i). Let
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})
and
(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{W}^{\prime})
be the
t
-structures induced by
T
, respectively
T^{\prime}
. Observe that
T[N]^{\perp}=\mathrm{Add}(T)[N]^{\perp}
, for any
N\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, therefore

\mathcal{V}=\mathrm{Add}(T)[<0]^{\perp}\hbox{ and }\mathcal{W}=\mathrm{Add}(T)[\geq 0]^{\perp},

and similar for
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime}
.
Condition (iii) implies
\mathrm{Add}(T^{\prime})\subseteq\mathrm{Add}(T)
, therefore we get:

\mathcal{V}=\mathrm{Add}(T)[<0]^{\perp}\subseteq\mathrm{Add}(T^{\prime})[<0]^{\perp}=\mathcal{V}^{\prime}

henceforth
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}={{}^{\perp}\mathcal{V}^{\prime}}\subseteq{{}^{\perp}\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{W}
. On the other hand, we have:

\mathcal{W}=\mathrm{Add}(T)[\geq 0]^{\perp}\subseteq\mathrm{Add}(T^{\prime})[\geq 0]^{\perp}=\mathcal{W},

which means that
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
and we are done.
∎

Remark that a dual statement holds for the cosilting case, showing that two cosilting objects
C
and
C^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{D}
are equivalent if and only if
\mathrm{Prod}(C)=\mathrm{Prod}(C^{\prime})
, where
\mathrm{Prod}(C)
denotes the class of all direct summands of products of arbitrary copies of
C
.

Setting 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
two triangulated categories.

(L)

Suppose that
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
have coproducts, and
\varphi_{*}:\mathcal{D}_{2}\to\mathcal{D}_{1}
is a triangle functor that has a left adjoint
\varphi^{*}:\mathcal{D}_{1}\to\mathcal{D}_{2}
. Fix an object
T_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
and denote
T_{2}=\varphi^{*}T_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
.

(R)

This is the dual of (1). More precisely, suppose that
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
have products and
\varphi_{*}:\mathcal{D}_{2}\to\mathcal{D}_{1}
is a triangle functor that has a right adjoint
\varphi^{!}:\mathcal{D}_{1}\to\mathcal{D}_{2}
. Fix an object
C_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
and denote
C_{2}=\varphi^{!}C_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
.

(LR)

This is the conjunction between (1) and (2). More precisely, suppose that
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
have both products and coproducts, and that
\varphi^{*}\dashv\varphi_{*}\dashv\varphi^{!}
is an adjoint triple of triangle functors. Fix two objects
T_{1},C_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
and denote
T_{2}=\varphi^{*}T_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
,
C_{2}=\varphi^{!}C_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
.

Suppose that we are in the hypotheses of Setting
1.3
(L). We say that a property
ascends via
\varphi^{*}
, if
T_{2}
satisfies the respective property in
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, whenever
T_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
does. For the converse implication, we say that the property
descends via
\varphi^{*}
. Examples of properties of interest for us are silting, silting of finite type etc.

Analogously, suppose that we are in the hypotheses of Setting
1.3
(R). We say that a property, for example, being a cosilting object,
ascends via
\varphi^{!}
, provided that the following implication holds: If
C_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
satisfies the respective property, then
C_{2}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
does.
For the converse implication, we will say that the property descends via
\varphi^{!}
.

Remark that we can define ascending or descending for a property in a more general case; the categories
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
have not to be triangulated (often they are module categories), they need not to have (co)products etc. But in this paper, we are concerned only with the case mentioned above.

We will use the following result several times. Note that its proof follows straightforwardly from the adjunction:

Lemma 1.4
.

(1)

Assume Setting
1.3
(L). Then, for every
N\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, we have

T_{2}[N]^{\perp}=\varphi_{*}^{-1}(T_{1}[N]^{\perp})\hbox{ and consequently }\varphi_{*}(T_{2}[N]^{\perp})\subseteq T_{1}[N]^{\perp}.

(2)

Assume Setting
1.3
(R). Then, for every
N\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, we have

{}^{\perp}C_{2}[N]=\varphi_{*}^{-1}(^{\perp}C_{1}[N])\hbox{ and consequently }\varphi_{*}(^{\perp}C_{2}[N])\subseteq^{\perp}{C_{1}[N]}.

Since a triangle functor
\varphi_{*}:\mathcal{D}_{2}\to\mathcal{D}_{1}
commutes with shifts, we do not need additional brackets in the expression
\varphi_{*}X[n]
, with
X\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
, and similar for its adjoints. Note also that
{}^{\perp}{C[N]}
should be read as
{}^{\perp}(C[N])
.

2.
Formal characterizations for ascending property

In the sequel, we will employ the following characterization of silting objects:

Proposition 2.1
.

[
8
, Corollary 2.7]

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category with coproducts. An object
T\in\mathcal{D}
is silting if and only if

S1.

T
is semi-rigid, that is,
T\in T[<0]^{\perp}
.

S2.

T[<0]^{\perp}
is closed under coproducts.

S3.

T
generates
\mathcal{D}
, that is,
T[\mathbb{Z}]^{\perp}=\{0\}
.

Remark that the equality
\mathrm{Loc}(T)=\mathcal{D}
implies condition S3 above; indeed, if
X\in T[\mathbb{Z}]^{\perp}
, then
X\in\mathrm{Loc}(T)^{\perp}
. But for the converse implication we need some additional assumptions.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume Setting
1.3
(L). Then the following statements hold true:

(a)

T_{2}
satisfies S1 if and only if
\varphi_{*}T_{2}\in T_{1}[<0]^{\perp}
.

(b)

If
T_{1}
satisfies S2 and
\varphi_{*}
preserves coproducts, then
T_{2}
satisfies S2.

(c)

If
T_{1}
satisfies S3 and
\varphi_{*}
reflects zero objects, then
T_{2}
satisfies S3.

(d)

If
T_{2}
satisfies S3 then
\varphi_{*}
reflects zero objects.

Proof.

The statement (a) follows by the adjunction isomorphism

\mathcal{D}_{1}(T_{1}[<0],\varphi_{*}T_{2})\cong\mathcal{D}_{2}(\varphi^{*}T_{1}[<0],T_{2})=\mathcal{D}_{2}(T_{2}[<0],T_{2}).

(b). Let
(X_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of objects in
T_{2}[<0]^{\perp}
. Then we have

\mathcal{D}_{1}(T_{1}[<0],\varphi_{*}X_{i})\cong\mathcal{D}_{2}(\varphi^{*}T_{1}[<0],X_{i})=\mathcal{D}_{2}(T_{2}[<0],X_{i})=0,

hence
\varphi_{*}X_{i}\in T_{1}[<0]^{\perp}
, for all
i\in I
. Using S2 for
T_{1}
and the hypothesis that
\varphi_{*}
preserves coproducts, we deduce
\varphi_{*}(\coprod X_{i})\cong\coprod\varphi_{*}X_{i}\in T_{1}[<0]^{\perp}
. Thus

\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(T_{2}[<0],\coprod X_{i}\right)=\mathcal{D}_{2}\left(\varphi^{*}T_{1}[<0],\coprod X_{i}\right)\cong\mathcal{D}_{1}\left(T_{1}[<0],\varphi_{*}(\coprod X_{i})\right)=0,

which shows S2 for
T_{2}
.

(c). Let
X\in T_{2}[\mathbb{Z}]^{\perp}
. Using again the adjunction isomorphism

\mathcal{D}_{1}(T_{1}[\mathbb{Z}],\varphi_{*}X)\cong\mathcal{D}_{2}(\varphi^{*}T_{1}[\mathbb{Z}],X)=\mathcal{D}_{2}(T_{2}[\mathbb{Z}],X)=0

we deduce
\varphi_{*}X\in T_{1}[\mathbb{Z}]^{\perp}=\{0\}
. Since
\varphi_{*}
is supposed to reflect zero objects, it follows that
X=0
.

(d). Suppose that
\varphi_{*}X=0
. Then

\mathcal{D}_{2}(T_{2}[\mathbb{Z}],X)=\mathcal{D}_{2}(\varphi^{*}T_{1}[\mathbb{Z}],X)\cong\mathcal{D}_{1}(T_{1}[\mathbb{Z}],\varphi_{*}X)=0,

hence S3 for
T_{2}
implies
X=0
.
∎

We summarize the above results in the following Proposition. It contains an equivalent condition to the ascending property for silting objects, condition which follows formally from the adjunction property:

Proposition 2.3
.

In Setting
1.3
(L), suppose that
\varphi_{*}
preserves coproducts and reflects zero objects, and that
T_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
be a silting object. The following are equivalent:

(i)

T_{2}
is silting in
\mathcal{D}_{2}
;

(ii)

\varphi_{*}T_{2}\in T_{1}[<0]^{\perp}
.

Moreover, if the above equivalent conditions hold, then
\varphi_{*}
is t-exact and
\varphi^{*}
is right t-exact w.r.t. the silting t-structures induced by
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
.

Proof.

The equivalence follows from the characterization of a silting object given in Proposition
2.1
and the transfer of the properties S1-S3 from
T_{1}
to
T_{2}=\varphi^{*}T_{1}
established in Lemma
2.2
.

Further, the inclusions
\varphi_{*}(T_{2}[<0]^{\perp})\subseteq T_{1}[<0]^{\perp}

and
\varphi_{*}(T_{2}[\geq 0]^{\perp})\subseteq T_{1}[\geq 0]^{\perp}
follow from Lemma
1.4
. Finally, the right t-exactness of
\varphi^{*}
follows using Lemma
1.1
and the above established t-exactness of
\varphi_{*}
.
∎

Note that everything in this section can be dualized in order to get results about cosilting objects. For the convenience of the reader, we formulate the dual results that we will apply in the sequel.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
8
, Corollary 2.9]

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category with products. An object
C\in\mathcal{D}
is cosilting if and only if

C1.

C
is semi-corigid, that is,
C\in{{}^{\perp}C[>0]}
.

C2.

{{}^{\perp}C[>0]}
is closed under products.

C3.

C
cogenerates
\mathcal{D}
, that is,
{{}^{\perp}C[\mathbb{Z}]}=\{0\}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume Setting
1.3
(R). Then the following statements hold true:

(a)

C_{2}
satisfies C1 if and only if
\varphi_{*}C_{2}\in{{}^{\perp}C_{1}[>0]}
.

(b)

If

3.
Ascending for the (co)silting property in tensor triangulated categories

Recall that a
symmetric monoidal category
is a category
\mathcal{D}
endowed with a bifunctor

-\otimes-:\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}
called
tensor product
, and an object
\mathbb{S}\in\mathcal{D}
such that the tensor product is associative, commutative and has
\mathbb{S}
as neutral element up to natural isomorphisms, isomorphisms which have to satisfy some compatibility conditions, see
[
12
, Chapter VII, Section 7]
. Such a category is called
closed
if for every
X\in\mathcal{D}
the functor
-\otimes X:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}
has a right adjoint
\hom(X,-):\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}
; this adjoint is called the
internal hom functor
. Note that if we fix the second variable in the internal hom functor, we get a contravariant functor
\hom(-,Y):\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{D}
which sends coproducts into products. A
tensor-triangulated category
is a triangulated category endowed with a symmetric monoidal structure such that the tensor product is triangulated in both variables. If, in addition,
\mathcal{D}
is closed, then
\hom(X,-)
and
\hom(-,Y)
are triangle functors for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
. Note also that the adjunction isomorphism
\mathcal{D}(X\otimes Y,Z)\cong\mathcal{D}(X,\hom(Y,Z))
has an enriched version:

\hom(X\otimes Y,Z)\cong\hom(X,\hom(Y,Z)),

natural in all three variables. Indeed, this follows from the Yoneda lemma and the chain of natural isomorphisms:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(-,\hom(X\otimes Y,Z))

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{D}(-\otimes X\otimes Y,Z)\cong\mathcal{D}(-\otimes X,\hom(Y,Z))

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{D}(-,\hom(X,\hom(Y,Z))).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{D},\otimes,\mathbb{S})
be a closed tensor-triangulated category with coproducts. Assume that
\mathbb{S}
is silting, consider an arbitrary t-structure
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})
in
\mathcal{D}
, and an object
X\in\mathbb{S}[<0]^{\perp}
.

(a)

For every
V\in\mathcal{V}
we have
V\otimes X\in\mathcal{V}
.

(b)

For every
W\in\mathcal{W}
we have
\hom(X,W)\in\mathcal{W}
.

Proof.

Note that if
\mathcal{D}
is a closed tensor-triangulated category, then for every
Y\in\mathcal{D}
the (covariant) functor
-\otimes Y
is triangulated and preserves coproducts, therefore it preserves homotopy colimits too. The (contravariant) functor
\hom(-,Y)
is triangulated and sends coproducts into products therefore it converts homotopy colimits into homotopy limits.

Since
X\in\mathbb{S}[<0]^{\perp}
it follows by
[
13
, Theorem 2]
(see also
[
1
, Proposition 4.8]
or
[
14
, Section 4]
) that
X\cong\mathrm{hocolim}_{n\geq 0}X_{n}
where

X_{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi_{-1}}}{{\to}}X_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi_{0}}}{{\to}}X_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi_{1}}}{{\to}}X_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi_{2}}}{{\to}}\ldots

is a sequence such that
X_{-1}=0
and for all
n\geq 0
there is a triangle

X_{n-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi_{n-1}}}{{\to}}X_{n}\to P_{n}\to X_{n-1}[1]

with
P_{n}\in\mathrm{Add}(\mathbb{S}[n])
. Clearly,
X_{0}\cong P_{0}
.

(a) Since
V\oplus\mathbb{S}\cong V
belongs to
\mathcal{V}
, the tensor product commutes with coproducts and
\mathcal{V}
is smashing, closed under summands and positive shifts, we obtain
V\otimes P_{n}\in\mathcal{V}
for all
n\geq 0
. Since
\mathcal{V}
is closed under extensions as well, an easy inductive argument shows that
V\otimes X_{n}\in\mathcal{V}
, for all
n\geq 0
. But
\mathcal{V}
is closed under homotopy colimits, thus

V\otimes X\cong V\otimes\mathrm{hocolim}_{n\geq 0}X_{n}\cong\mathrm{hocolim}_{n\geq 0}(V\otimes X_{n})\in\mathcal{V}.

(b) is the dual of (a).
∎

Let
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\otimes,\mathbb{S}_{1})
and
(\mathcal{D}_{2},\otimes,\mathbb{S}_{2})
be two symmetric monoidal categories. Recall that a functor
\varphi^{*}:\mathcal{D}_{1}\to\mathcal{D}_{2}
is called
lax monoidal
if it comes togheter with a natural morphism
\varphi^{*}(-)\otimes\varphi^{*}(-)\to\varphi^{*}(-\otimes-)
and a morphism
\mathbb{S}_{2}\to\varphi^{*}\mathbb{S}_{1}
. If in addition these morphisms are supposed to be isomorphisms, then the functor is called
strong monoidal
(see
[
12
, p. 255]
). An adjunction
\varphi^{*}:\mathcal{D}_{1}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{D}_{2}:\varphi_{*}
between them is called
monoidal
if
\varphi^{*}
is strong monoidal, case in which
\varphi_{*}
is automatically lax monoidal, by
[
10
, Theorem 1.5]
.

Setting 3.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\otimes,\mathbb{S}_{1})
and
(\mathcal{D}_{2},\otimes,\mathbb{S}_{2})
be two closed tensor-triangulated categories.
Suppose that both
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
have products and coproducts, and consider an adjoint triple of triangulated functors
\varphi^{*}\dashv\varphi_{*}\dashv\varphi^{!}
, with the property that
\varphi^{*}:\mathcal{D}_{1}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{D}_{2}:\varphi_{*}
is a monoidal adjunction.

Example 3.3
.

Let
A
and
B
be two commutative rings, and let
\varphi:A\to B
be a unital ring homomorphism. It induces a (derived) restriction functor

\varphi_{*}:\mathbf{D}({B})\to\mathbf{D}({A})\hbox{, given by }\varphi_{*}Y=\RHom_{B}(B,Y)\cong Y\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{B}B.

This functor has both a left and a right adjoint, namely the (derived) induction and coinduction functors

\varphi^{*},\varphi^{!}:\mathbf{D}({A})\to\mathbf{D}({B})\hbox{, where }\varphi^{*}X=X\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{A}B\hbox{, and }\varphi^{!}X=\RHom_{A}(B,X).

Then
\mathbf{D}({A})
and
\mathbf{D}({B})
are compactly generated. Since the rings are commutative, it follows that the derived tensor product induces a tensor triangulated structure on the derived categories. Moreover, they are closed, the internal hom functor being
\RHom
. Finally, it is clear that
\varphi^{*}=-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{A}B
is strong monoidal, therefore all hypotheses of Setting
3.2
are satisfied.

The following is a sort of Eilenberg-Watts theorem for monoidal adjunctions between tensor-triangulated categories.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume Setting
3.2
, and suppose further that
\mathcal{D}_{1}=\mathrm{Loc}(\mathbb{S}_{1})
. Then there is an isomorphisms

X\otimes\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2}\to\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}X\hbox{ and }\hom(\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2},X)\to\varphi_{*}\varphi^{!}X,

that are natural in
X\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
.

Proof.

Apply the functor
-\otimes\varphi\mathbb{S}_{2}
to the unit
\id_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}\to\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}
of the adjunction between
\varphi^{*}
and
\varphi_{*}
, then use the natural morphism coming from the fact that
\varphi_{*}
is lax monoidal and the neutrality of
\mathbb{S}_{2}
with respect to the tensor product in
\mathcal{D}_{2}
in order to get the composite natural transformation between functors
\mathcal{D}_{1}\to\mathcal{D}_{1}
:

(-)\otimes\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2}\to\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}(-)\otimes\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2}\to\varphi_{*}(\varphi^{*}(-)\otimes\mathbb{S}_{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}(-).

But
\varphi^{*}
is strong, therefore evaluating this natural morphism at
\mathbb{S}_{1}
we get an isomorphism
\mathbb{S}_{1}\otimes\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2}\cong\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2}\cong\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}\mathbb{S}_{1}
. Since both functors
-\otimes\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2}
and
\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}
are triangulated and coproduct preserving, the morphism is actually an isomorphism evaluated at any
X\in\mathrm{Loc}(\mathbb{S}_{1})=\mathcal{D}_{1}
.

For the second isomorphism, we only have to note that
\varphi_{*}\varphi^{!}
is the right adjoint of
\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}
, and to use the first one.
∎

4.
Quasi faithfully flat adjunction

In what follows, we need some additional terminology. Recall that an object
P
of a triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
is called
compact
if the functor
\mathcal{D}(P,-)
preserves coproducts. We write
\mathcal{D}^{c}
for the full subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
consisting of all compact objects. Furthermore, the category
\mathcal{D}
is called
compactly generated
if
\mathcal{D}^{c}
is essentially small and generates
\mathcal{D}
(in the triangulated sense), that is,
{\mathcal{D}^{c}}^{\perp}=\{0\}
.

Note that if
\mathcal{D}
is compactly generated, then both
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}}
satisfy Brown representability. It follows
\mathrm{Loc}(T)=\mathcal{D}
for any object
T
satisfying
T[\mathbb{Z}]^{\perp}=\{0\}
, in particular for any silting object. Another consequence is that
\mathcal{D}
also has products.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a compactly generated triangulated category and denote by
\mathcal{D}^{c}
its subcategory consisting of all compact objects. By assumption,
\mathcal{D}^{c}
is essentially small, and the category of all additive (contravariant) functors
\mathcal{D}^{c}\to\mathrm{Ab}
lives in the same universe as
\mathcal{D}
. We call a
(right)
\mathcal{D}^{c}
-module
a functor as before and, we denote by
\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
the category of all such functors. We consider the restricted Yoneda functor
\mathbf{y}:\mathcal{D}\to\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
,
\mathbf{y}(X)=\mathcal{D}(-,X)|_{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
. A triangle
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\to}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}X[1]
in
\mathcal{D}
is said to be
pure
if the induced sequence
0\to\mathbf{y}(X)\to\mathbf{y}(Y)\to\mathbf{y}(Z)\to 0
is exact in

\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
. In this case, we say that
i
is a pure monomorphism,
p
is a pure
epimorphism,
f
is a phantom map. Furthermore,
X
is a pure subobject and
Z
a pure quotient of
Y
. It is clear that a map
f
is phantom if and only if
\mathbf{y}(f)=0

An object
D\in\mathcal{D}
is called
pure projective
if it is projective with respect to pure exact triangles, that is, the sequence

0\to\mathcal{D}(D,X)\to\mathcal{D}(D,Y)\to\mathcal{D}(D,Z)\to 0

is exact for every pure triangle
X\to Y\to Z\to X[1]
. Dually we define
pure injective
objects.
We recall that an object
P\in\mathcal{D}
is pure projective exactly if
\mathbf{y}(P)
is projective in
\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
. Another characterization for the same property is that
P\in\mathrm{Add}(\mathcal{D}^{c})
. (see
[
5
, Lemma 8.1 and Proposition 8.4]
). For the dual notion hold
D\in\mathcal{D}
to be pure injective if and only if
\mathbf{y}(D)
is injective in
\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathcal{D}^{c}}
if and only if the summation map
D^{(I)}\to D
factors through the canonical map
D^{(I)}\to D^{I}
. (see
[
11
, Theorem 1.8]
). The last condition can be formulated as follows: for each set
I
, there is a morphism

f:D^{I}\to D
such that
f\circ\rho_{i}=1_{D}
, where
\rho_{i}:D\to D^{I}
is the canonical section, for each
\in I
(see
[
17
, Definition 5.1]
).

In the rest of this section we made the assumptions of Setting
1.3
(LR) and, in addition, we suppose that
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are compactly generated.

Proposition 4.1
.

The functor
\varphi^{*}
preserves pure projective objects, and the functor
\varphi^{!}
preserves pure injective objects.

Proof.

Since
\varphi_{*}
is exact and preserves coroducts, it follows that
\varphi^{*}
preserves compact objects. Further, it preserves directs summands of coproducts of compact objects, that is, pure projective objects.

The functor
\varphi^{!}
preserves products, therefore it preserves pure injective objects, by
[
17
, Lemma 5.3]
.
∎

We will say that the adjunction
\varphi^{*}\dashv\varphi_{*}
is
quasi faithfully flat
, provided that the functor
\varphi^{*}:\mathcal{D}_{1}\to\mathcal{D}_{2}
reflects the phantom maps, that is,
f:X\to X^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{D}_{1}
is phantom, whenever
\varphi^{*}f:\varphi^{*}X\to\varphi^{*}X^{\prime}
is phantom in
\mathcal{D}_{2}
. The terminology comes from the fact that in in Example
3.3
, if the morphism of rings
\varphi:A\to B
is faithfully flat, then
[
9
, Proposition 2.7]
says that the derived induction and derived restriction functors form a quasi faithfully flat adjoint pair.

A TTF-triple
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})
is called
compactly generated
if there is a set of compact objects
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{c}
such that
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{P}^{\perp}
.
A (co)silting object is called
of (co)finite type
if the (co)silting TTF-triple is compactly generated.

Proposition 4.2
.

If the silting property ascends via
\varphi^{*}
, then the property "silting of finite type" ascends via
\varphi^{*}
.
If the cosilting property ascends via
\varphi^{!}
, then the property "cosilting of cofinite type" ascends via
\varphi^{!}
.

Proof.

Let
T_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
be a silting object of finite type, and let
T_{2}=\varphi^{*}T_{1}
. By the hypothesis
T_{2}
is silting. Let
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{1}^{c}
be a set of compact objects such that
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}=T_{1}[<0]^{\perp}
. Since
\varphi^{*}
preserves compacts, we infer that
\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{P})\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{2}^{c}
. For an object
Y\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
we have the the sequence of equivalent statements (the first of them comes from Lemma
1.4
):

\displaystyle Y\in T_{2}[<0]^{\perp}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\varphi_{*}Y\in T_{1}[<0]^{\perp}\Leftrightarrow\varphi_{*}Y\in\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{P},\varphi_{*}Y)=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{2}(\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{P}),Y)=0\Leftrightarrow Y\in\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{P})^{\perp}

showing that
T_{2}[<0]^{\perp}=\varphi^{*}(\mathcal{P})^{\perp}
, so
T_{2}
is of finite type.

For cosilting objects of cofinite type, a similar argument works.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If

is a quasi faithfully flat adjunction, then for every

.

The conclusion follows immediately from the assumption on

, since
\varphi^{*}

.
∎

.

and

and

.

Proof.

We will prove (2) since in the cosilting case is somehow surprising that we have to deal with

instead of

. For the silting case, the argument is similar.

For simplicity we will denote by

,

, the respective cosilting t-structures. By Lemma

, we know that

. From

for all

, therefore

. Since
C_{1}
is pure injective,

is closed under pure subobjects, therefore

according to Lemma

.

∎

, we know that
\mathbb{S}_{2}\in\mathrm{add}(T_{2})[-l]*\ldots*\mathrm{add}(T_{2})[l]
, for some
l\geq 0
. Therefore,

\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2}\in\mathrm{add}(\varphi_{*}T_{2})[-l]*\ldots*\mathrm{add}(\varphi_{*}T_{2})[l]=\mathrm{add}(T_{1}\otimes\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2})[-l]*\ldots*\mathrm{add}(T_{1}\otimes\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2})[l].

We conclude this paragraph, recording the inclusion
\mathrm{Loc}(\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2})\subseteq\mathrm{Loc}(T_{1}\otimes\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2})
.

As we noted in the proof of Theorem
3.5
,
\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2}\in\mathbb{S}_{1}[<0]^{\perp}
, therefore
\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2}=\mathrm{hocolim}_{n\geq 0}X_{n}
, where
X_{n-1}\to\mathcal{X}_{n}\to P_{n}\to X_{n-1}[1]
is a triangle with
X_{-1}=0
, and
P_{n}\in\mathrm{Add}(\mathbb{S}_{1}[n])
, for all
n\geq 0
(see
[
13
, Theorem 2]
; the argument was already used in the proof of Lemma
3.1
). Since the tensor functor is triangulated and commutes with coproducts, we obtain
T_{1}\otimes\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2}\cong\mathrm{hocolim}_{n\geq 0}(T_{1}\otimes X_{n})
.
Inductively, we get
T_{1}\otimes X_{n}\in\mathrm{Loc}(T_{1})
, and therefore
T_{1}\otimes\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2}\in\mathrm{Loc}(T_{1})
. This implies further
\mathrm{Loc}(T_{1}\otimes\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2})\subseteq\mathrm{Loc}(T_{1})
. The chain of inclusions

\mathcal{D}_{1}=\mathrm{Loc}(\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2})\subseteq\mathrm{Loc}(T_{1}\otimes\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2})\subseteq\mathrm{Loc}(T_{1})\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{1}

shows the desired equality.
∎

Let
(\mathcal{D},\otimes,\mathbb{S})
be a compactly generated closed tensor-triangulated category. For any two objects
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
there is morphism
\hom(X,\mathbb{S})\otimes Y\to\hom(X,Y)
, natural in both variables. The object

is called

if this morphism is an isomorphism for all
Y\in\mathcal{D}
. (see
[
16
, Definition 1.3]
). The category
\mathcal{D}
is called
rigidly-compactly generated
, provides that the rigid and compact objects coincide. It is clear that in such categories the tensor unit
\mathbb{S}
must be compact since it is rigid. Note that if

[

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a rigidly-compactly generated closed tensor-triangulated category. Following
[
6
]
, we construct a (contravariant) functor
(-)^{+}:\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{D}
, called duality, as follows: For every object
X\in\mathcal{D}
the functor
\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{S},X\otimes-),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}):\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\hbox{\rm Mod-}{\mathbb{Z}}
is cohomological and sends coproducts to products. By Brown representability theorem, it must be representable. The object representing it is unique up to a natural isomorphism and will be denoted by
X^{+}
. Explicitly, we have:

\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{S},X\otimes-),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathcal{D}(-,X^{+}).

From the very definition of the functor
(-)^{+}
, it follows that it converts coproducts into products. According to
[
6
, Proposition 3.13]
, the triple
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D},(-)^{+})
is a duality triple in the sense of
[
6
, Definition 3.4]
. In particular,
D^{+}
is pure injective for any
D\in\mathcal{D}
, and for any
X\in\mathcal{D}
there is a natural map
i_{X}:X\to X^{++}
which is a pure monomorphism. Using these properties, the proof of the following lemma is straightforward.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a rigidly-compactly generated closed tensor-triangulated category. The following are equivalent for an object
X\in\mathcal{D}
:

(i)

X\in\mathcal{D}
is pure injective.

(ii)

The canonical map
i_{X}:X\to X^{++}
splits.

(iii)

X
is a direct summand in an object of the form
D^{+}
, for some
D\in\mathcal{D}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a rigidly-compactly generated closed tensor-triangulated category. Then we have the following isomorphisms, natural in all variables:

(1)

X^{+}\cong\hom(X,\mathbb{S}^{+})
.

(2)

\mathcal{D}(X,Y^{+})\cong\mathcal{D}(Y,X^{+})
, that is
(-)^{+}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}
is a contravariant self-adjoint.

(3)

\hom(X,Y^{+})\cong(X\otimes Y)^{+}\cong\hom(Y^{+},X)
, natural in
X
and
Y
.

Proof.

(1) is (contained in)
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
(ii).

(2) Using (1) and the adjunction isomorphism we have:

\mathcal{D}(X,Y^{+})\cong\mathcal{D}(X,\hom(Y,\mathbb{S}^{+}))\cong\mathcal{D}(X\otimes Y,\mathbb{S}^{+})\cong\mathcal{D}(Y\otimes X,\mathbb{S}^{+})\cong\mathcal{D}(Y,X^{+}).

(3) Use the same argument as for (2), but with the enriched version of the adjunction isomorphism.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

In Setting
3.2
, assume that
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are rigidly-compactly generated closed tensor-triangulated categories. Then:

(1)

There is a natural isomorphism
(\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}X)^{+}\cong\varphi_{*}\varphi^{!}(X^{+}),
for all
X\in\mathcal{D}_{1}

(2)

Assume also that
\varphi_{*}(Y^{+})\cong(\varphi_{*}Y)^{+}
, for all
Y\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
. Then here is a natural isomorphism
(\varphi^{*}X)^{+}\cong\varphi^{!}(X^{+}),
for all
X\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
.

Proof.

(1). We apply Proposition
3.4
and Lemma
5.4
to get the chain of isomorphisms

(\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}X)^{+}\cong(\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2}\otimes X)^{+}\cong\hom(\varphi_{*}\mathbb{S}_{2},X^{+})\cong\varphi_{*}\varphi^{!}(X^{+}).

(2). For all
X\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
and all
Y\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
we obtain the isomorphisms

\mathcal{D}_{2}(Y,(\varphi^{*}X)^{+})\cong\mathcal{D}_{2}(\varphi^{*}X,Y^{+})\cong\mathcal{D}_{1}(X,\varphi_{*}(Y^{+})),

and

\mathcal{D}_{2}(Y,\varphi^{!}(X^{+}))\cong\mathcal{D}_{1}(\varphi_{*}Y,X^{+})\cong\mathcal{D}_{1}(X,(\varphi_{*}Y)^{+}).

Now the conclusion follows from the hypothesis and the Yoneda lemma.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Assume the hypotheses made in Setting
3.2
. In addition, suppose
\mathcal{D}_{1}
is a rigidly-compactly generated closed tensor-triangulated category and the adjunction
\varphi^{*}\dashv\varphi_{*}
is quasi faithfully flat. If
C_{1}
is a pure injective object in
\mathcal{D}_{1}
, that cogenerates
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
C_{2}=\varphi^{!}C_{1}
is a cosilting (necessarily pure injective) object in
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, then
C_{1}
is cosilting in
\mathcal{D}_{1}
.

Proof.

Since
C_{1}
is pure injective, Lemma
5.3
implies that
C_{1}
is a direct summand in an object of the form
D^{+}
, for some
D\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
. By Lemma
4.3
, the triangle

D\to\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}D\to E\to D[1]

is pure in
\mathcal{D}_{1}
. As we have already noted, the triple
(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{1},(-)^{+})
is a duality triple, therefore the triangle

D^{+}[-1]\to E^{+}\to(\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}D)^{+}\to D^{+}

splits, according to
[
6

. This shows that
D^{+}
is a direct summand in
(\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}D)^{+}
. We learned in Proposition
5.5

(\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}D)^{+}\cong\varphi_{*}\varphi^{!}(D^{+})

D^{+}
is a direct summand in
\varphi_{*}\varphi^{!}(D^{+})

\varphi_{*}\varphi^{!}(D^{+})\to D^{+}
of the adjunction
\varphi_{*}\dashv\varphi^{!}

C_{1}

\varphi_{*}\varphi^{!}C_{1}
.
Next, we want to verify all conditions C1, C2 and C3 from Proposition

\varphi_{*}\varphi^{!}C_{1}=\varphi_{*}C_{2}\in{{}^{\perp}C_{1}[>0]}

∎

Let
(\mathcal{D},\otimes,\mathbb{S})
be a rigidly-compactly generated triangulated category, and suppose
\mathbb{S}
is silting. Denote
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}=\mathbb{S}[<0]^{\perp},\mathcal{D}^{>0}=\mathbb{S}[\geq 0]^{\perp})
the t-structure determined by

(\mathcal{D}^{\leq n},\mathcal{D}^{>n})=(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}[-n],\mathcal{D}^{>0}[-n])
, for all

; sometimes is convenient to write
\mathcal{D}^{\geq n+1}=\mathcal{D}^{>n}
. We say that

is

-bounded
, if there are integers

, such that

By shifting conveniently we obtain

T[-a]\in\mathrm{Add}(\mathbb{S})*\mathrm{Add}(\mathbb{S})[1]*\ldots*\mathrm{Add}(\mathbb{S})[b-a]\in\mathcal{D}^{>-l}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},

where
l=b-a+1
is the length of
T
.
Replacing
T
with its shift as before, we always can consider that an
\mathbb{S}
-bounded complex appears on the last form.
Notice that
T\in\mathrm{Add}(\mathbb{S})*\ldots*\mathrm{Add}(\mathbb{S})[l-1]
, for some

means that there is a chain of triangles

P^{-i}[i]\to T_{i}\to T_{i+1}\to P^{-i}[i+1],\ (0\leq i<l),

with
P^{-i}\in\mathrm{Add}(\mathbb{S})
, for all
0\leq i<l
,
T_{0}=T
, and
T_{l}=0
. Applying the duality functor
(-)^{+}
to this chain of triangles, we obtain

. We say that

is
\mathbb{S}^{+}
-cobounded.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{D},\otimes,\mathbb{S})
be a rigidly-compactly generated triangulated category. Then
\mathbb{S}^{\perp}={{}^{\perp}\mathbb{S}^{+}}
. In particular,
\mathbb{S}
is silting if and only if
\mathbb{S}^{+}
is cosilting, and in this case the respective silting and cosilting t-structures coincide, up to a shift with
\pm 1
; more precisely
({{}^{\perp}\mathbb{S}^{+}}[\leq 0],{{}^{\perp}\mathbb{S}^{+}}[>0])=(\mathcal{D}^{\leq-1},\mathcal{D}^{>-1})
.

Proof.

For
X\in\mathcal{D}
we have

\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{S},X)=0\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{S}\otimes X)=0\Leftrightarrow\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{S},\mathbb{S}\otimes X),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})=0\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{D}(X,\mathbb{S}^{+})=0.

Further, for an object
X\in\mathcal{D}
we reformulate the relation
X\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq-1}
in turn as:
X[-1+n]\in\mathbb{S}^{\perp}
, for all
n>0
, then
X[-1+n]\in{{}^{\perp}\mathbb{S}^{+}}
, for all
n>0
, and finally
X\in{{}^{\perp}\mathbb{S}^{+}}[\leq 0]
, and similar for the coaisle.
∎

Recall that a class

Lemma 5.8
.

Let

be a rigidly-compactly generated triangulated category, such that

is a silting object.

is a silting,

-bounded object of finite type, and

-cobounded cosilting object.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
5.3
the pure injectivity of

is immediate. Moreover because

is
\mathbb{S}
-bounded, the dual

is
\mathbb{S}^{+}
-cobounded as we noticed above.
Observe that for

and

the chain of isomorphisms

\mathcal{D}\left(X,C[N]\right)=\mathcal{D}\left(X,T^{+}[N]\right)\cong\mathcal{D}\left(X,T[-N]^{+}\right)\cong\mathcal{D}\left(T[-N],X^{+}\right)

if and only if

.
For showing that

, Theorem 2.8]
. In order to do that, we have to verify three conditions:

Because

is of finite type, it follows that

is definable.
This implies that it is closed under taking double dual, in particular, since

For the last condition, since
