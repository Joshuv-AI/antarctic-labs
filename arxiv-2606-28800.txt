Viscosity in Error Upper Bound for a Consistent Splitting Scheme of the Navier–Stokes Equations
M Nader Alhomsi, Jiahong Wu, Xiaoming Zheng

Abstract
This paper investigates the role of viscosity in the error upper bounds of a consistent splitting scheme for the Navier–Stokes equations proposed by Huang and Shen [ 5 ] . In their original analysis the viscosity is fixed to unity. By following and extending their proof methodology while keeping the viscosity ν \nu symbolic, we obtain an H 1 H^{1} velocity error bound that contains negative powers of ν \nu , indicating that the scheme is not robust as ν → 0 \nu\to 0 . To establish this bound we refine a theorem in [ 8 ] on the constant in the Stokes pressure estimate, which is crucial to the error analysis. A targeted numerical experiment based on a perturbation of the Kovasznay flow corroborates this analytical prediction: the scheme of [ 5 ] blows up at high Reynolds number, and a comparison with a fully implicit Newton solver and with the time-dependent Stokes counterpart of the same scheme localizes the failure to the explicit treatment of the convection term.

6
Conclusions

This work refines the analysis of the GSAV consistent-splitting scheme proposed in
[
5
]
in two directions. First, the lower bound on the key parameter
k
is reduced from
5
to
4
in the stability analysis of the time-dependent Stokes equations (spatial dimension
d=2
or
3
) and in the convergence analysis of the full Navier–Stokes equations (
d=2
). One benefit of this reduction is a smaller error, as discussed in Remark
3.2
and observed numerically in Example 1 (Figure
1
).
Second, the viscosity is extended from unity to arbitrary positive values in both problems. For the Navier–Stokes equations, the resulting upper bound on the velocity error in the
H^{1}
norm is found to depend on negative powers of the viscosity (see Theorem
4.4
and Remark
4.5
).

Following
[
2
]
, a numerical scheme for the Navier–Stokes equations is called
robust
if the
L^{2}
error bound of the velocity contains no negative powers of the viscosity, and
non-robust
otherwise. A rough
L^{2}
estimate can be obtained by combining the Poincaré inequality (
10
) with the
H^{1}
bound (
46
), namely

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{e}^{n}\|\;\leq\;C\,C_{5.3}\,\delta t^{2}.

(159)

Although the constant
C_{5.3}
involves negative powers of
\nu
(see formula (
155
)), this estimate alone does not establish that the scheme is non-robust:
C_{5.3}
is only an upper bound, and the inequality could be loose.
To resolve this, we design a carefully chosen perturbation of the Kovasznay flow with smooth and viscosity-independent data and compare the GSAV scheme of
[
5
]
against a fully implicit Newton solver (taken as the robust reference) and against the time-dependent Stokes counterpart of the same GSAV discretization. The experiment confirms that the GSAV scheme is not robust at high Reynolds number, and the comparison localizes the cause to its explicit treatment of the convection term.

Theorem A.1

(Refined Theorem 1 of

Let

(160)

When

Setup.

Given

by

\nabla p_{S}:=(\Delta\mathcal{P}-\mathcal{P}\Delta){\bf u}.

(161)

Let
\Phi(x)=dist(x,\partial\Omega)

For any

Note
\Omega_{s}
is a tubular neighborhood of
\partial\Omega

Since

C^{3}

.
Then

\Omega_{s}

Let

Lemma A.2

(Cutoff decomposition and remainders)
.

Define the tangential projector
T(x):=I-{\bf n}(x){\bf n}(x)^{T}
on
\Omega_{s}
and

Then

\Delta{\bf u}_{\parallel}=\xi\,T\,\Delta{\bf u}+R_{2},

(162)

\Delta{\bf u}_{\perp}=(1-\xi)\Delta{\bf u}+\xi\,({\bf n}{\bf n}^{T})\Delta{\bf u}+R_{1},

(163)

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle:=2\nabla\xi\cdot\nabla(T{\bf u})+(\Delta\xi)\,T{\bf u}+\xi\,\Delta(T{\bf u})-\xi\,T\,\Delta{\bf u},

(164)

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle:=-R_{2}.

(165)

Finally, there exists a constant

\|R_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|R_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{R}\|\nabla{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\text{ where }C_{R}\triangleq\frac{2C}{\min\{1,s\}}.

(166)

Proof.

Step 1: decomposition and support.
