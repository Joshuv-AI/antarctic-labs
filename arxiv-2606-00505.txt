Prescribed realisation of longest runs in continued fractions
Ying Wai Lee

Abstract
. Exceptional longest-run behaviour in continued fraction expansions is studied through the interaction between fixed-symbol runs and the overall longest run. For every prescribed partial quotient value and every admissible growth scale, a full Hausdorff dimensional set of irrational numbers is constructed on which the longest run of the prescribed value has exactly the prescribed asymptotic growth and, for every initial length, uniquely realises the overall maximum. It follows that the symbol responsible for the overall longest run can be fixed in advance without any loss of Hausdorff dimension. Thus, the known full-dimensional exceptional-set results for fixed-symbol longest-run growth and for overall longest-run growth are simultaneously strengthened, while the maximising symbol in the overall problem is shown to be fully prescribable.



1.
Introduction

Continued fractions of irrational numbers in the unit interval are a classical topic in number theory and dynamical systems. For any
x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
, there exists a unique sequence of positive integers
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
x
admits the continued fraction expansion:

\displaystyle x=[a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\dots]\coloneqq\frac{1}{\displaystyle a_{1}+\frac{1}{\displaystyle a_{2}+\frac{1}{a_{3}+\cdots}}},

where for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{n}\coloneqq a_{n}(x)\in\mathbb{N}
is referred to as the
n
-th partial quotient of
x
. Define the Gauss map
T:[0,1]\to[0,1]
by
T(0)\coloneqq 1
and for any
x\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle T(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{x}-\left\lfloor\frac{1}{x}\right\rfloor.

The partial quotients of an irrational number can be generated by iterating the Gauss map. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
, the
n
-th partial quotient of
x
is given by

\displaystyle a_{n}(x)=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{T^{n-1}(x)}\right\rfloor.

Longest-run problems for continued fraction expansions have been studied from fixed-symbol and overall points of view. Define, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the fixed-symbol longest-run function
L_{n}:[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}\times\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
by, for any
x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle L_{n}(x,\lambda)\coloneqq\max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n-j}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{1}_{a_{j+i}(x)=\lambda};

equivalently,
L_{n}(x,\lambda)
is the maximum length of a consecutive run of the value
\lambda
within the first
n
partial quotients of
x
. Define, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the overall longest-run function
R_{n}:[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{N}
by, for any
x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
,

\displaystyle R_{n}(x)\coloneqq\max_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}}L_{n}(x,\lambda);

equivalently,
R_{n}(x)
is the maximum length of a consecutive run over all values within the first
n
partial quotients of
x
. For example, one obtains
L_{30}(\pi-3,1)=3
and
L_{30}(\pi-3,2)=R_{30}(\pi-3)=4
from the first 30 partial quotients of
\pi-3
:

\displaystyle\pi-3=[7,15,1,292,\underline{1,1,1},2,1,3,1,14,2,1,1,\underline{2,2,2,2},1,84,2,1,1,15,3,13,1,4,2,\ldots]

where the underlined blocks show the longest runs.

Longest-run problems originate in classical probability theory. For independent symbolic processes, the logarithmic order of the longest run goes back to the Erdős–Rényi law of large numbers
[
1
]
; sharper refinements for the longest head-run were later obtained by Erdős and Révész
[
2
]
. Since then, longest-run problems have become a standard object in the study of rare events and extreme symbolic patterns. In the continued-fraction system equipped with the natural absolutely continuous reference measure, such as the Gauss measure, the partial quotients form a stationary symbolic process which is mixing but not independent, and whose one-symbol distribution is highly non-uniform. These dynamical and probabilistic features make longest-run problems for continued fraction expansions a natural analogue of the classical run-length problem, now in the setting of a non-uniformly expanding dynamical system with a countable alphabet.

Typical longest-run behaviours of partial quotients, in the sense of full Lebesgue measure, have been studied in the recent literature. Song–Zhou
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
established the first-order behaviour for the fixed-symbol longest-run function: for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
and almost every
x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{L_{n}(x,\lambda)}{\log{n}/\log{\tau_{\lambda}}}=\frac{1}{2},\qquad\tau_{\lambda}\coloneqq\frac{\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+4}}{2}.

Wang–Wu
[
8
, Theorem 1.3]
established the first-order behaviour of the overall longest-run function: for almost every
x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_{n}(x)}{\log{n}/\log{\varphi}}=\frac{1}{2},\qquad\varphi\coloneqq\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}.

Recently, Lee
[
5
, Theorem 2]
obtained eventual two-sided additive refinements of the above first-order laws with double-logarithmic error terms: for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
,
c>1/2
, almost every
x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
and sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\left|L_{n}(x,\lambda)-\frac{\log{n}}{2\log{\tau_{\lambda}}}\right|\leq\frac{c\log{\log{n}}}{\log{\tau_{\lambda}}},\qquad\left|R_{n}(x)-\frac{\log{n}}{2\log{\varphi}}\right|\leq\frac{c\log{\log{n}}}{\log{\varphi}}.

A central topic in the study of atypical behaviour of longest-run functions concerns the set of numbers whose longest-run growth deviates from the first-order law satisfied by almost every number. Recent work has focused on the Hausdorff dimension of sets defined by prescribed growth rates of longest-run functions. Song–Zhou
[
6
, Corollary 1.3]
proved that for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in[0,+\infty]
,

\displaystyle\dim{\left\{x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{L_{n}(x,\lambda)}{\log{n}/\log{\tau_{\lambda}}}=\alpha\right\}}=1.

Wang–Wu
[
8
, Theorem 1.3]
obtained a more general exact-growth result for the overall longest-run: for any unbounded non-decreasing sequence of positive integers
(\delta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, if

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\delta_{n+\delta_{n}}}{\delta_{n}}=1

then

\displaystyle\dim{\left\{x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_{n}(x)}{\delta_{n}}=1\right\}}=1.

Related
\limsup
- and
\liminf
-type exceptional sets were also studied in the literature
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
.

The results above prescribe either the longest run of a fixed symbol, or the longest run after maximising over all symbols. However, they do not address the interaction between these two prescriptions. In particular, if the growth of fixed-symbol longest-run is prescribed for a fixed value, it is not automatic that this prescribed symbol also realises the overall longest run. Conversely, prescribing the growth of overall longest-run does not specify which partial quotient value is responsible for the maximal blocks.

In the present work, a full-dimensional exceptional-set theorem is established, in which the growth of a fixed-symbol longest run is prescribed and the overall longest run is uniquely realised by that same prescribed symbol at all times. As consequences, the single theorem is a genuine simultaneous refinement of the results of Song–Zhou and Wang–Wu: not only can the fixed-symbol and overall longest-run growth be prescribed on a full-dimensional set, but the overall longest run can be forced to be uniquely realised at any preassigned partial quotient value.

2.
Main results

The following definition isolates, in functional notation, the exact hypothesis appearing in the above result of Wang–Wu
[
8
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Definition 1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be a function.
f
is said to be admissible, if
f
is non-decreasing, unbounded, and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{f(n+f(n))}{f(n)}=1.

Theorem
1
is the main theorem of the present work.

Theorem 1
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
and admissible
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\dim{\mathfrak{E}_{\lambda,f}}=1,

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{E}_{\lambda,f}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{L_{n}(x,\lambda)}{f(n)}=1\text{ and }\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.\text{for any $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and $\mu\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{\lambda\}$, }\frac{L_{n}(x,\lambda)}{L_{n}(x,\mu)}>1\right\}.

The first corollary records the fixed-symbol exact-growth consequence of Theorem
1
. Compared with the fixed-symbol exceptional-set theorem of Song–Zhou, it gives a refinement in which the prescribed growth scale is allowed to be any admissible function. Thus this corollary isolates the fixed-symbol part of the main theorem after the additional dominance condition is discarded.

Corollary 1
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
and admissible
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\dim{\left\{x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{L_{n}(x,\lambda)}{f(n)}=1\right\}}=1.

The second corollary records the overall exact-growth consequence of Theorem
1
. Compared with the theorem of Wang–Wu for the overall longest-run function, it recovers the same full-dimensional exact-growth statement. Thus this corollary shows that the main theorem contains the known overall result as a special consequence, with the additional information that the maximising symbol may be prescribed.

Corollary 2
.

For any admissible
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\dim{\left\{x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_{n}(x)}{f(n)}=1\right\}}=1.

The third corollary records the prescribed-symbol dominance consequence of Theorem
1
. Unlike the previous fixed-symbol and overall exact-growth results, this statement has no direct analogue in the earlier literature. Thus this corollary gives a new full-dimensional phenomenon: the partial quotient value responsible for the overall longest run can be fixed in advance.

Corollary 3
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\dim{\left\{x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}:\text{for any $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and $\mu\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{\lambda\}$, }\frac{L_{n}(x,\lambda)}{L_{n}(x,\mu)}>1\right\}}=1.

3.
Basic lemmas

Let
\mathbb{N}^{*}\coloneqq\{\emptyset\}\cup\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{N}^{n}
be the finite word space. The standard convention is adopted that juxtaposition of two or more finite words denotes their concatenation. If a finite word is put inside continued-fraction brackets, the brackets denote the finite continued fraction whose entries are the letters of that word. The same convention is used when a finite word is followed by an infinite continued-fraction tail.

Define
\mathcal{I}(\emptyset)\coloneqq[0,1]
and for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
\mathcal{I}(\omega)
to be the continued-fraction cylinder of
\omega
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\omega)\coloneqq\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\left\{x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}:a_{i}(x)=\omega_{i}\right\}}.

Define
K:\mathbb{N}^{*}\to\mathbb{N}
to be the continuant function; that is
K(\emptyset)\coloneqq 1
and for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
K(\omega)
is the denominator of the fraction
[\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}]
.

Lemma 1
.

For any
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
K(\omega_{1})K(\omega_{2})\leq K(\omega_{1}\omega_{2})\leq 2K(\omega_{1})K(\omega_{2})
.

Proof.

The claim is immediate when one of the words is empty; it suffices to handle the non-trivial case. By the continuant identity, one obtains
K(\omega_{1}\omega_{2})=K(\omega_{1})K(\omega_{2})+K({\omega_{1}}\partial)\,K(\partial\omega_{2})
,
where
{\omega_{1}}\partial
denotes
\omega_{1}
with its last digit removed and
\partial\omega_{2}
denotes
\omega_{2}
with its first digit removed. The result follows from
0\leq K({\omega_{1}}\partial)\leq K(\omega_{1})
and
0\leq K(\partial\omega_{2})\leq K(\omega_{2})
.
∎

Define, for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda^{0}\coloneqq\emptyset
and for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the long-run word
\lambda^{n}\coloneqq(\lambda,\lambda,\ldots,\lambda)\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
.

Lemma 2
.

For any
\omega\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
K(\omega)\geq\varphi^{n-1}
. For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
K(\lambda^{n})\leq{\tau_{\lambda}}^{n}
.

Proof.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
K(\omega)\geq K(1^{n})\geq\varphi^{n-1}
. One obtains from the standard continuant recurrence formula that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
K(\lambda^{n+1})=\lambda\,K(\lambda^{n})+K(\lambda^{n-1})
and

\displaystyle K(\lambda^{n})=\frac{{\tau_{\lambda}}^{n+1}-(-\tau_{\lambda})^{-n-1}}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+4}}\leq{\tau_{\lambda}}^{n}.

∎

Define
T_{\emptyset}\coloneqq\mathrm{id}_{[0,1]}
. Define, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega=(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, the prefix map
T_{\omega}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
by, for any
\xi\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle T_{\omega}(\xi)\coloneqq\frac{p_{n}+\xi p_{n-1}}{q_{n}+\xi q_{n-1}},

where
p_{0}\coloneqq 0
,
q_{0}\coloneqq 1
and for any
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
p_{k}
and
q_{k}
are coprime positive integers so that
p_{k}/q_{k}=[\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k}]
.

Lemma 3
.

For any
\omega\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, one obtains that
T_{\omega}([0,1])=\mathcal{I}(\omega)
and for any
\xi\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4K(\omega)^{2}}\leq\left|T_{\omega}^{\prime}(\xi)\right|\leq\frac{1}{K(\omega)^{2}};

in particular,
T_{\omega}
is bi-Lipschitz and preserves the Hausdorff dimension of subsets of
[0,1]
.

Proof.

The claim is immediate when the word is empty; it suffices to handle the non-trivial case. Pick any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. By differentiation and the standard formula
|p_{n-1}q_{n}-p_{n}q_{n-1}|=1
, one obtains that for any
\xi\in[0,1]
,
\left|T_{\omega}^{\prime}(\xi)\right|=1/(q_{n}+\xi q_{n-1})^{2}
. Since
0\leq q_{n-1}\leq q_{n}=K(\omega)
, one obtains that for any
\xi\in[0,1]
,
K(\omega)\leq q_{n}+\xi q_{n-1}\leq 2K(\omega)
. Since
T_{\omega}^{\prime}
is bounded by positive constants on
[0,1]
, one obtains from applying the mean value theorem that
T_{\omega}
is bi-Lipschitz.
∎

Define, for any
B\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{B}\coloneqq\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}:a_{n}(x)\in\{1,\ldots,B\}\right\}.

Lemma 4
.

For any
B,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\nu\in\{1,\dots,B\}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\dim{\mathcal{F}_{B}}=\dim{\left(\mathcal{I}(\nu)\cap\mathcal{F}_{B}\right)}.

Proof.

Pick any
n,B\in\mathbb{N}
and
\nu\in\{1,\dots,B\}^{n}
.
By the definition of the prefix map
T_{\nu}
, one obtains that for any
\xi\in{\mathcal{F}_{B}}
,
T_{\nu}(\xi)=[\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n},a_{1}(\xi),a_{2}(\xi),\ldots]
; hence,
\mathcal{I}(\nu)\cap\mathcal{F}_{B}=T_{\nu}(\mathcal{F}_{B})
. By Lemma
3
,
T_{\nu}
is bi-Lipschitz on
\mathcal{F}_{B}
and preserves Hausdorff dimension.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces. Let
g:X\to Y
be a surjective function. Suppose there exist
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
C>0
such that for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
,

\displaystyle d_{Y}(g(x_{1}),g(x_{2}))\leq C\left(d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})\right)^{\alpha}.

Then
\dim{Y}\leq\dim{X}/\alpha
.

Proof.

Pick any
s>\dim{X}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\mathcal{H}^{s}(X)=0
, there exists an open cover
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
of
X
such that
\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}}(\diam{U})^{s}<\varepsilon
and for any
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
,
\diam{U}<\varepsilon
. Since
g
is surjective,
(g(U))_{U\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}}
covers
Y
. Then for any
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
,
\diam{g(U)}\leq C\,(\diam{U})^{\alpha}
, and

\displaystyle\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}}(\diam{g(U)})^{s/\alpha}\leq C^{s/\alpha}\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}}(\diam{U})^{s}<C^{s/\alpha}\varepsilon.

Hence, for any
s>\dim{X}
,
\mathcal{H}^{s/\alpha}(Y)=0
and
\dim{Y}\leq s/\alpha
.
∎

The next lemma is a reformulation of
[
8
, Lemma 2.5]
in the present functional notation.

Lemma 6
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
.
Suppose
f
is admissible.
Then for any
C\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{f(n+Cf(n))}{f(n)}=1.

4.
Construction of a Cantor-type set

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
and
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be a function. Define
H_{\lambda}\coloneqq\lfloor{\log{\tau_{\lambda}}}/{\log{\varphi}}\rfloor+1\in\mathbb{N}
. Pick any
M\in\mathbb{N}
. Define
t_{0}\coloneqq 1
and for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle r_{k}\coloneqq f(t_{k-1}),\qquad m_{k}\coloneqq\left\lfloor\sqrt{r_{k}}\right\rfloor,\qquad g_{k}\coloneqq MH_{\lambda}r_{k},\qquad p_{k}\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{g_{k}}{m_{k}}\right\rfloor,\qquad q_{k}\coloneqq g_{k}-p_{k}m_{k},

and

\displaystyle s_{k}\coloneqq f(t_{k-1}+g_{k}),\qquad d_{k}\coloneqq 2p_{k}+2+s_{k},\qquad l_{k}\coloneqq g_{k}+d_{k},\qquad t_{k}\coloneqq t_{k-1}+l_{k}.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The quantity
r_{k}
is the target scale at the beginning of stage
k
, while
s_{k}
is the target scale associated with the end of the retained free length
g_{k}
. The length
g_{k}
specifies the number of free digits retained at stage
k
. The auxiliary length
m_{k}
tends to infinity while satisfying
\lim_{k\to\infty}m_{k}/r_{k}=0
; it will be used to subdivide the free digits into shorter pieces, so that accidental runs remain negligible compared with the target scale. The integers
p_{k}
and
q_{k}
are the quotient and remainder obtained when the free length
g_{k}
is divided into pieces of length
m_{k}
. Finally,
d_{k}
,
l_{k}
, and
t_{k}
record respectively the non-free length to be added at stage
k
, the total length of stage
k
, and the starting position of stage
k+1
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
and
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be a function.
Suppose
f
is admissible. Then for any
M\in\mathbb{N}
,

(1)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{s_{k}}{r_{k}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{s_{k-1}}{r_{k}}=1,\qquad\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{p_{k}}{r_{k}}=0,

and there exists
K_{\lambda,f,M}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
if
k\geq K_{\lambda,f,M}
then

(2)

\displaystyle l_{k}\leq(MH_{\lambda}+2)\,r_{k}.

Proof.

Since
f
is unbounded and non-decreasing, one obtains that
(t_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is strictly increasing and
\lim_{k\to\infty}r_{k}=\lim_{k\to\infty}f(t_{k-1})=+\infty
. By Lemma
6
, one obtains

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{f(t_{k-1}+MH_{\lambda}f(t_{k-1}))}{f(t_{k-1})}=1;

hence,
\lim_{k\to\infty}{s_{k}}/{r_{k}}=1
. Since
\lim_{k\to\infty}r_{k}=+\infty
, one obtains
\lim_{k\to\infty}m_{k}=+\infty
.

Since for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
p_{k}m_{k}\leq g_{k}=MH_{\lambda}r_{k}
, one obtains
0\leq{p_{k}}/{r_{k}}\leq{MH_{\lambda}}/{m_{k}}
; hence,
\lim_{k\to\infty}{p_{k}}/{r_{k}}=0
. Note that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\frac{l_{k}}{r_{k}}=\frac{g_{k}+d_{k}}{r_{k}}=MH_{\lambda}+\frac{2p_{k}}{r_{k}}+\frac{2}{r_{k}}+\frac{s_{k}}{r_{k}};

hence,
\lim_{k\to\infty}{l_{k}}/{r_{k}}=MH_{\lambda}+1
and there exists
K_{\lambda,f,M}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
if
k\geq K_{\lambda,f,M}
then (
2
) holds.

One obtains that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
if
k\geq K_{\lambda,f,M}+1
then

\displaystyle t_{k-1}=t_{k-2}+l_{k-1}\leq t_{k-2}+(MH_{\lambda}+2)\,r_{k-1}=t_{k-2}+(MH_{\lambda}+2)\,f(t_{k-2}),

and

\displaystyle 1\leq\frac{f(t_{k-1})}{f(t_{k-2})}\leq\frac{f(t_{k-2}+(MH_{\lambda}+2)\,f(t_{k-2}))}{f(t_{k-2})}.

By Lemma
6
, one obtains
\lim_{k\to\infty}{f(t_{k-1})}/{f(t_{k-2})}=1
; hence,
\lim_{k\to\infty}{r_{k-1}}/{r_{k}}=1
. Thus,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{s_{k-1}}{r_{k}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{s_{k-1}}{r_{k-1}}\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{r_{k-1}}{r_{k}}=1.

∎

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
and
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be a function. Pick any
M,B\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose
B\geq\lambda+2
.
Define, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
U_{k}\in\{1,\dots,B\}^{g_{k}}
, the expanded block
U^{+}_{k}\in\{1,\dots,B\}^{l_{k}}
by:

\displaystyle U^{+}_{k}\coloneqq U_{k,1}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}U_{k,2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\cdots U_{k,p_{k}}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}U_{k,p_{k}+1}\sigma_{1}\lambda^{s_{k}}\sigma_{2},

where
\sigma_{1}\coloneqq\lambda+1
and
\sigma_{2}\coloneqq\lambda+2
are the separator digits,
U_{k,1},\dots,U_{k,p_{k}}\in\{1,\dots,B\}^{m_{k}}
and
U_{k,p_{k}+1}\in\{1,\dots,B\}^{q_{k}}
are the unique words satisfying

\displaystyle U_{k}=U_{k,1}U_{k,2}\cdots U_{k,p_{k}}U_{k,p_{k}+1},

with the convention that
\{1,\ldots,B\}^{0}\coloneqq\{\emptyset\}
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\lambda,f,M,B}\coloneqq\left\{\left[U^{+}_{1}U^{+}_{2}U^{+}_{3}\cdots\right]:U_{k}\in\{1,\dots,B\}^{g_{k}}\text{ for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$.}\right\}\subset\mathcal{F}_{B}.

Define
\zeta_{\lambda,f,M,B}:\mathcal{F}_{B}\to\mathcal{K}_{\lambda,f,M,B}
by for any
y\in\mathcal{F}_{B}
,

\displaystyle\zeta_{\lambda,f,M,B}(y)\coloneqq\left[U^{+}_{y,1}U^{+}_{y,2}U^{+}_{y,3}\cdots\right],

where the partial quotients of
y
are partitioned into consecutive words
U_{y,1},U_{y,2},U_{y,3},\ldots
so that
y=[U_{y,1}U_{y,2}U_{y,3}\cdots]
and for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
U_{y,k}\in\{1,\dots,B\}^{g_{k}}
.

Define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{\lambda,f}

5.
Continuant distortion

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
. Define
C_{\lambda}\coloneqq 8(\lambda+1)(\lambda+2)

and

\displaystyle\eta_{\lambda,M,\alpha}\coloneqq\frac{(1-\alpha)MH_{\lambda}\log\varphi-\alpha\log\tau_{\lambda}}{4}>0.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
and
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be a function. Suppose
f
is admissible. Then for any

then for any

\displaystyle K(U^{+}_{k})^{\alpha}\leq 2^{-2\alpha}K(U_{k}).

Proof.

Pick any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. By Lemma
1
, one obtains

\displaystyle K(U^{+}_{k})\leq{C_{\lambda}}^{p_{k}+1}K(\lambda^{s_{k}})K(U_{k}).

By Lemma
2
, one obtains

\displaystyle\log{\frac{K(U^{+}_{k})^{\alpha}}{K(U_{k})}}\leq\alpha(p_{k}+1)\log C_{\lambda}+\alpha s_{k}\log\tau_{\lambda}-(1-\alpha)(g_{k}-1)\log\varphi.

By the definition of
(g_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, one obtains

\displaystyle\log{\frac{K(U^{+}_{k})^{\alpha}}{K(U_{k})}}\leq\alpha(p_{k}+1)\log C_{\lambda}+\alpha(s_{k}-r_{k})\log\tau_{\lambda}-4\eta_{\lambda,M,\alpha}r_{k}+(1-\alpha)\log{\varphi}.

By Proposition
1
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}r_{k}=+\infty
, one obtains that for all sufficiently large

\displaystyle\log{\frac{K(U^{+}_{k})^{\alpha}}{K(U_{k})}}\leq-2\eta_{\lambda,M,\alpha}r_{k}\leq-2\alpha\log 2.

The result follows after exponentiating.
∎

, one obtains

\displaystyle\dim{\mathcal{F}_{B}}=\dim{\left(\mathcal{I}\!\left(V_{\nu}\right)\cap\mathcal{F}_{B}\right)}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\dim{J_{\nu}}.

By the finite stability of Hausdorff dimension,

\displaystyle\dim{\mathcal{K}_{\lambda,f,M,B}}=\max_{\nu\in\{1,\ldots,N\}}\dim{J_{\nu}}\geq\alpha\dim{\mathcal{F}_{B}}.

∎

Proposition 5
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
and
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be a function. Suppose
f
is admissible. Then
\dim{\mathfrak{F}_{\lambda,f}}=1
.

Proof.

By Propositions
2
and
4
, for any
M,B\in\mathbb{N}
, if
B\geq\lambda+2
then

\displaystyle\dim{\mathfrak{F}_{\lambda,f}}\geq\dim{\mathcal{K}_{\lambda,f,M,B}}\geq\sup_{0<\alpha<M/(M+1)}\alpha\dim{\mathcal{F}_{B}}=\frac{M}{M+1}\dim{\mathcal{F}_{B}}.

By the classical fact
[
3
,
4
]
that
\lim_{B\to\infty}\dim{\mathcal{F}_{B}}=1
, one obtains that for any
M\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\dim{\mathfrak{F}_{\lambda,f}}\geq\frac{M}{M+1}.

Therefore,
\dim{\mathfrak{F}_{\lambda,f}}\geq 1
, and the reverse inequality is trivial.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Note that
\mathfrak{F}_{\lambda,f}=\bigcup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{F}_{\lambda,f,N}
, where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{\lambda,f,N}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{x\in[0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}:\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{L_{n}(x,\lambda)}{f(n)}=1\text{ and }\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.\text{for any $m\in\mathbb{N}$ and $\mu\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{\lambda\}$, if $m\geq N$ then }\frac{L_{m}(x,\lambda)}{L_{m}(x,\mu)}>1\right\}.

By Proposition
5
and the countable stability of Hausdorff dimension,
\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}{\dim{\mathfrak{F}_{\lambda,f,N}}}=1
.

Pick any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
y\in\mathfrak{F}_{\lambda,f,N}
. Define
x\coloneqq T_{\lambda^{N}}(y)
. One claims that for any
\mu\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{\lambda\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
L_{n}(x,\lambda)>L_{n}(x,\mu)
. In the case of
n<N
, the first
n
partial quotients of
x
are all equal to
\lambda
. One obtains
L_{n}(x,\lambda)=n>L_{n}(x,\mu)=0
. In the case of
n\geq N
and
m\coloneqq n-N<N
, the initial block
\lambda^{N}
has length larger than the entire tail segment of length
m
. One obtains
L_{n}(x,\lambda)\geq N>m\geq L_{n}(x,\mu)
.

In the case of
n\geq N
and
m\geq N
, one obtains that every
\mu
-run among the first
N+m
partial quotients of
x
is contained entirely in the tail corresponding to the first
m
partial quotients of
y
. Thus,
L_{n}(x,\mu)=L_{m}(y,\mu)
. Since
y\in\mathfrak{F}_{\lambda,f,N}
and
m\geq N
, one obtains
L_{m}(y,\lambda)>L_{m}(y,\mu)
. Every
\lambda
-run among the first
m
partial quotients of
y
also occurs among the first
N+m
partial quotients of
x
; hence,
L_{m}(y,\lambda)\leq L_{N+m}(x,\lambda)=L_{n}(x,\lambda)
. Hence,
L_{n}(x,\lambda)\geq L_{m}(y,\lambda)>L_{m}(y,\mu)=L_{n}(x,\mu)
. The claim is proved for all cases.

Note that for any
m,N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle L_{m}(y,\lambda)\leq L_{N+m}(x,\lambda)\leq N+L_{m}(y,\lambda).

Since
f
is admissible, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
and sufficiently large
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
N\leq f(m)
and

\displaystyle 1\leq\frac{f(m+N)}{f(m)}\leq\frac{f(m+f(m))}{f(m)}.

Thus,
\lim_{m\to\infty}f(m+N)/f(m)=1
. By
\lim_{m\to\infty}{L_{m}(y,\lambda)}/{f(m)}=1
, one obtains from the squeeze theorem that
\lim_{m\to\infty}{L_{N+m}(x,\lambda)}/{f(N+m)}=1
; hence
x\in{\mathfrak{E}}_{\lambda,f}
, and for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle T_{\lambda^{N}}\!\left(\mathfrak{F}_{\lambda,f,N}\right)\subset{\mathfrak{E}}_{\lambda,f}.

By Lemma
3
, one obtains that for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\dim{{\mathfrak{E}}_{\lambda,f}}\geq\dim{T_{\lambda^{N}}\!\left(\mathfrak{F}_{\lambda,f,N}\right)}=\dim{\mathfrak{F}_{\lambda,f,N}}.

Therefore,
\dim{\mathfrak{E}_{\lambda,f}}\geq 1
, and the reverse inequality is trivial.
∎
