Introduction

Worrying about the physical and mental complications of pregnancy, as well as the fear of childbirth, are major sources of stress for pregnant women [1]. It is estimated that 1 in 5 pregnant women experiences a fear of childbirth, with 6 to 13% experiencing severe and debilitating fear that can lead to additional complications [2]. Psychological factors such as depression and anxiety, negative mood after delivery, nutritional disorders, and the unconscious choice of delivery method also contribute to this fear. The fear of childbirth has been shown to play a significant role in the decision to undergo a cesarean section among Iranian women [2–4]. Primiparous women, who are unfamiliar with the birthing process, often experience a reasonable level of fear that is typically controlled during pregnancy and childbirth [5, 6]. However, irrational fear can manifest as daily anxiety, nightmares, and physical symptoms, leading many women to request an elective cesarean section [7] (4). There are several reasons cited for the fear of natural childbirth, including fear of pain, fear of death, fear of unexpected problems, poor self-efficacy, concern about sexual problems after childbirth, and concern about the baby’s health [8]. Feeling lonely during pregnancy and childbirth has also been identified as a predictor of pain and fear during childbirth [1]. Social support in this situation can help reduce the anxiety and pain of childbirth [9]. Fear of childbirth is an important problem during pregnancy. Effective psychological and social factors play a role in its creation during and after childbirth, such as fear of the unknown, the mother’s own personal characteristics, genetic backgrounds that make a person vulnerable to stress, adverse experience, improper communication of the medical staff, worry about the baby’s health, the presence of an unstable personality in terms of mood, anxiety disorders, as well as the lack of encouragement and social support [1]. In Fisher’s study, the fear of childbirth was shown to have two dimensions: the personal dimension which includes fear of pain, fear of losing self-control, and previous experience of fear (retrospective fear), and the social dimension which includes fear of the unknown, fear of scary stories, and fear of the child’s health (prospective fear). In the social dimension, it argues that the social context affects not only the identity of women but also their understanding of childbirth as an abstract event, so that social relationships and informal networks supporting pregnant women, as well as the strong support of midwives, can strengthen the belief that childbirth is a physiological and controllable process [10]. In Haynes et al.‘s study, it was shown that characteristics such as being a primiparous child, being a smoker, low self-confidence, depression, general anxiety, and dissatisfaction with marital relationships increase the risk of fear of childbirth in women [11]. Every woman who gets pregnant for the first time. Pregnant women, especially first-time mothers, who express fear of childbirth require special care to manage their pregnancy and have a positive birthing experience [8]. Even if it does not result in a cesarean section, fear of childbirth can have detrimental effects on pregnancy and delivery. These effects include reduced blood flow and oxygen supply to the uterus, increased abnormal fetal heart rates, and higher chances of premature birth. Fear and anxiety can trigger the production of substances such as catecholamines, cortisol, epinephrine, and beta-endorphins, which can impact cervical dilation, uterine contractions, labor progression, and increase pain and anxiety levels [7]. Understanding the concept of fear of childbirth in pregnant women is crucial, especially for medical professionals like midwives and nurses. Analyzing this concept using a hybrid model, which combines theoretical analysis and experimental observations, can provide valuable insights into this complex phenomenon. The hybrid model proves particularly helpful in clarifying concepts that are often unclear at the bedside. By applying this model, the goal of this study is to gain a deep understanding of the concept of fear of childbirth in pregnant women, including its characteristics, antecedents, and consequences. This research aims to analyze the concept of fear of childbirth based on the experiences and perspectives of Iranian pregnant women, as no previous study has focused on clarifying this phenomenon.

Method

In this study, the hybrid concept analysis model will be used to analyze the concept of fear of childbirth in pregnant women The Schwartz-Barcott and Kim model, introduced in 1986, provides a valuable approach for creating, developing, and analyzing concepts in nursing. This research methodology is commonly used to clarify concepts that may be unclear. By utilizing the hybrid model, concepts can be improved, resulting in new and more comprehensive definitions. In certain instances, entirely new definitions may even arise [12]. The hybrid model combines both inductive and deductive approaches, producing a refined definition that offers clear distinctions between compliance and therapeutic alliance concepts. This refined definition helps in selecting appropriate nursing and midwifery interventions that promote clients’ health goals. This model consists of three theoretical phase s: literature review, field work, and final analysis.

Phase 1: literature review

The first phase involves systematically reviewing existing literature to determine the general nature of the concept, including its definition and measurement. At the end of this phase, the researcher will specify the operational definition for the field work phase. To ensure a comprehensive search, all desired Persian and English articles from 2005 to 2026 will be obtained using keywords such as fear of childbirth, pregnant women, outcomes, causes, and partum. Boolean operators (and, or, *) will be used to combine these keywords in various combinations. Databases such as Elsevier, PubMed, Web of Sciences, Cochrane, Scopus, CINAHL, and PROQUEST will be searched. The inclusion criteria for articles include descriptive, analytical, experimental, or semi-experimental studies that investigate the fear of childbirth in pregnant women. Articles must also clearly state their goals, work methods, and results to be considered for the study. Exclusion criteria include articles with inappropriate or irrelevant content, articles presented in conferences, letters to the editor, and articles with insufficient data. The reference lists of all related articles will also be reviewed. The scientific validity of articles and sources will be assessed using the Consort checklist, which consists of 25 general items and 37 specific items covering various sections of the article. After collecting and analyzing the studies, a list of behaviors, antecedents, and consequences related to fear of childbirth will be extracted [13].

Phase 2: fieldwork

This phase involves collecting clinical data to strengthen and redifine the concept established in the first phase. It integrates analysis from the first phase with existing experimental observations. This phase occurs simultaneously with the first phase and focuses on the experimental aspect of the process. qualitative study is comparative content analysis.

At this stage, the qualitative study will be conducted, using semi-structural interviews. The setting of this phase of the research will be 2 educational hospitals afflicted to Isfahan university of medical sciences. All of the interviews will be conducted in a calm and private place selected based on the preference of the participants. Potential questions include: How do you experience childbirth? How do you feel when encountering heavy bleeding or prolonged labor? How do you feel about being alone in the hospital? What is your understanding of uterine contractions and how do you feel about them? The duration of the interviews will vary based on participant characteristics. To ensure thorough analysis of the data, the interviews will be recorded. Immediately following each session, the recorded conversations will be played in their entirety. Verbal and non-verbal interactions (field notes) will also be considered during the interview process. The collected data will be analyzed using a directional content analysis method.

Participants

The participants of the qualitative phase of this research consist of pregnant women, their spouses, and mothers. The participants will be selected using purposeful sampling method. We will consider the diversity of the age and previous births, and educational qualifications in pregnant women. We will interview the participants after we evaluate their inclusion criteria. Then, we will obtain their informed consent. The interviews will continue until the data is repeated and we reach data saturation.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The criteria for participating in the study will include being a first-time pregnant woman or having had multiple pregnancies, providing informed consent, and being willing to communicate and participate in interviews.

Qualitative data collection and analysis

The qualitative data analysis method for this study will be comparative qualitative content analysis. We will use a directed content analysis approach to analyze the qualitative data related to the phenomenon of fear of childbirth, its consequences, and antecedents. Prior to the analysis, we will establish predetermined categories, including behaviors, antecedents, and consequences of fear of childbirth in pregnant women. We will read the interview text twice independently to ensure a comprehensive understanding of pregnant women’s experiences. We will highlight or underline relevant words, phrases, sentences, or paragraphs that represent their fear of childbirth experiences. Antecedents and consequences of the fear of childbirth will be highlighted using different colored highlighters. We will merge codes representing similar experiences into categories. Two researchers will compare these categories and reach a consensus through discussion. Categories with similar conceptual meanings will be integrated into themes. Additionally, we will conduct a peer review, involving two members of the scientific team in the midwifery group who will provide their opinions on the identified categories.

Trustworthiness

To assure the validity and reliability of the findings of the research, we conducted semi-structured interviews at various times and locations. This approach aligns with Strobert and Carpenter’s (2011) four criteria of validity or acceptability, reliability or similarity, transferability, and confirmability.

To validate the findings, in-depth interviews will be conducted at various time points, ensuring a diverse range of participants in terms of age, previous births, and educational qualifications. The method of participant review will be used to confirm the accuracy of data and code extraction. Experts will review the data to ensure that the identified categories align with the participants’ statements. In this study, the researcher will establish a rapport with the participants to create a conducive environment for open interviews and to gather detailed data. Additionally, the researcher’s presence in the research field will facilitate a deeper understanding and build trust with the participants.

To confirm the transferability of the research, the findings will be presented to individuals who did not participate in the study to assess the similarity between the results and their own experiences. Expert review will also be conducted. To ensure the verifiability of the text, a selection of codes and categories extracted from the interviews will be shared with the researcher’s colleagues and members of the executive committee who have knowledge of qualitative research analysis but did not participate in the study. They will be asked to verify the accuracy of the data coding process. In this research, the originality of the study will be enhanced through purposeful sampling of participants, obtaining informed consent, and respecting the participants’ nature. The researcher will seek guidance and supervision from associate professors who have expertise in the field of labor and delivery, as well as drawing on their own 12 years of experience in the field. The researcher has also completed theoretical courses on research methods and attended a workshop on qualitative research methods.

Ethical consideration

Ethical approval for this study has been obtained by the ethics committee affiliated with Medical Education Research Center, Isfahan University of Medical Sciences, Isfahan, Iran (IR.MUI.NUREMA.REC.1401.095), in compliance with the Helsinki Declaration. Ethical considerations include explaining the research objectives to the participants, optional participation in this project, completing the written informed consent form by the participants and confidentiality of all information related to the individual and institutional of the study in reporting participation.

Limitations and recommendations

Limitations of the study include potential non-cooperation of some pregnant women due to physical and mental conditions during the qualitative interviews, which will be addressed by conducting interviews at appropriate times with prior coordination.

Phase 3: the analytical phase

The final analysis phase will involve a comprehensive analysis that combines the results from the previous two phase s. We will compare the fear of childbirth concept in the articles with the findings from the fieldwork phase, highlighting any differences between the data obtained from the fieldwork and the theoretical phase. We will then extract and report these differences.

Discussion

The present study aims to provide an overview of the concept of fear of childbirth by reviewing texts, articles, tools, and the perceptions of pregnant women based on their lived experiences. The concept of “fear of childbirth” encompasses various types of anxiety and fear that women experience in relation to pregnancy and childbirth. Understanding the characteristics of this concept can help develop it in health systems and improve care before and during pregnancy [14]. Previous studies have presented various definitions and tools for fear of childbirth. For example, Nielson et al. (2018) conducted a study evaluating fear of childbirth in 9 countries [15], while Danker et al. (2019) conducted a systematic review that found a relationship between fear during pregnancy and stress, anxiety, depression, lack of social support, need for psychiatric care, and traumatic stress symptoms related to prolonged labor, use of epidurals, and childbirth complications [16]. The study also found that a previous negative birth experience or surgical delivery is the strongest predictor of fear in pregnant women. Additionally, fear of childbirth has been associated with adverse obstetric outcomes such as maternal request for cesarean delivery, preterm delivery, prolonged delivery, postpartum depression, and post-traumatic stress [17].

Fear of childbirth is a concern not only for midwives but also for health policy providers. Clarifying this concept within the socio-cultural context can enhance the effectiveness of prevention programs in each country’s health system. Danker et al. (2019) suggested developing, testing, evaluating, and implementing concepts based on the theory of care for prenatal and intrapartum support for pregnant women with high or severe fear of childbirth. To address the fears of pregnant women and provide comprehensive prenatal care, midwives can improve their competency by using a valid assessment tool to identify the level of fear of childbirth and by understanding the comprehensive definition of this concept, its features, and its impact on women’s real experiences [16]. The results of the systematic review also shed light on women’s experiences of fear of childbirth through the metaphor “at the point of no return” and emphasized the importance of support that addresses their existential issues and allows them to express their feelings, experiences, and expectations during pregnancy, childbirth, and postpartum. Women who have had a previous negative birth experience require support that builds trust in maternity care professionals and encourages them to seek good care. The findings of this study can guide the healthcare team in providing optimal care during, before, and after childbirth.
