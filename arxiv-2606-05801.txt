Asymptotically-flat Black holes in Bumblebee gravity: Exact solutions and Thermodynamics
Jinbo Yang, Zhan-Feng Mai, Dicong Liang, Lijing Shao

Abstract
We construct analytic solutions to the bumblebee gravity theory in static and spherically symmetric spacetimes, where the bumblebee vector field admits only a non-vanishing temporal component. In particular, we identify the parameter space that allows for asymptotically flat black hole solutions. We further investigate the thermodynamic properties of these black holes and obtained the analytic formulas for the Y Y charge and X X potential, which were introduced in the prior work to ensure the Smarr relation and the first law of black hole thermodynamics. Using the new analytic results, we verify the numerical findings reported in early work and uncover multiple cases missed in the previous numerical analysis. These include: (i) an unbounded charge-mass ratio when the non-minimal coupling parameter ξ \xi is larger than 2 ​ κ 2\kappa , (ii) the emergence of a traversable wormhole configuration for overcharged solutions with ξ < 0 \xi<0 , (iii) the non-monotonic turning behavior of the Hawking temperature as a function of the charge-mass ratio, and (iv) the presence of two divergent points in the constant- Y Y heat capacity..

IV
Thermodynamics

In this section, we study black hole thermodynamics.
The analytic expression of the solution (
14
) allows us to scan the whole parameter space in a lower cost than the numerical approach
35
.
As a result, we not only confirm the numerical results of
35

IV.1
Entropy and Temperature

As pointed out by the reference
1
, the bumblebee vector field
b^{\mu}
may affect the Wald entropy of the Schwarzschild-like black hole. The relation of entropy and area may be different from the black holes in general relativity. However, this is not the case for the above solutions (
14
) in which
b^{\mu}
only contains the temporal component.
To manifest this property, we express
b^{\mu}
in terms of the Killing vector
\chi^{\mu}=\partial x^{\mu}/\partial t
as follows:

\displaystyle b^{\mu}=

\displaystyle-\frac{b_{t}}{h}\frac{\partial x^{\mu}}{\partial t}=-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\kappa}}\,\frac{u+1}{\beta s(s-2)}\big(u+s-1\big)^{1-s}\,\chi^{\mu}\,.

(39)

The vector field
b^{\mu}
vanishes on the bifurcate surface since the Killing vector field
\chi^{\mu}
vanishes there. Hence the entropy of the bumblebee black hole is still
S=A_{H}/4
, where
A_{H}
denotes the horizon area. Particularly, the entropy
S
is

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle~4\pi a^{2}|s|^{2(1-s)}e^{2(2-\gamma)}\big(\gamma+s-1\big)^{2(s-1)}\,.

(40)

On the other hand, the black hole temperature
T
is given by
T=\kappa_{H}/(2\pi)
. Choosing the inverse of the ADM mass
M
as the unit for temperature, the result of surface gravity
\kappa_{H}
given in Section
II
yields

\displaystyle TM=

\displaystyle~\frac{M}{4\pi}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}h}{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}=\frac{|s|^{2(s-1)}}{8\pi\gamma}e^{2\gamma-2}(\gamma-1+s)^{1-2s}[(s-1)\gamma+1]\,.

(41)

Then, combining it with the analytic result for the charge-mass ratio in Section
III
, we reproduce the
T
-
|Q|/M
relations in the upper panel of Fig.
11
, which matches the results in Ref.
35
.
Furthermore, we found some missing cases in the previous work.
As shown in the lower panel of Fig.
11

\xi=0.49\kappa

|Q|/M
relation exhibits a turning point, and thus has a degenerate region in which the same

It is distinct from the decreasing case
\xi=0.47\kappa

\xi=0.51\kappa

Nevertheless, we now observe that the scanning step used in Ref.

\displaystyle\delta Y=\frac{\delta M-T\delta S}{X}\,,\quad M=2TS+XY\,.

(42)

More concretely, the

We require the following two conditions:
(i)

(ii)

These two conditions suggest the following ansatz for constructing

\displaystyle M=a\tilde{M}(\gamma)\,,\quad S=a^{2}\tilde{S}(\gamma)\,,\quad Y=a\tilde{Y}(\gamma)\,,

where the argument
\gamma
means that functions
\{\tilde{M}(\gamma),\tilde{S}(\gamma),\tilde{Y}(\gamma)\}
only depend on the variable
\gamma

We emphasize that
\tilde{M}(\gamma)
and
\tilde{S}(\gamma)
can be read from Eq. (
30
) and Eq. (
40

According to Eq. (
18
), there is

.
Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta M-T\delta S=

\displaystyle~\big(\tilde{M}-1\big)\delta a+a\bigg(\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\tilde{M}}{{\mathrm{d}}\gamma}-\frac{1}{2\tilde{S}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\tilde{S}}{{\mathrm{d}}\gamma}\bigg)\delta\gamma\,.

(44)

If we require the above expression to be equal to
X\delta Y=X\tilde{Y}\delta a+aX\delta\tilde{Y}
, there should be

\displaystyle\tilde{M}-1=

\displaystyle~X\tilde{Y}\,,

(45)

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\tilde{M}}{{\mathrm{d}}\gamma}-\frac{1}{2\tilde{S}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\tilde{S}}{{\mathrm{d}}\gamma}=

\displaystyle~X\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\tilde{Y}}{{\mathrm{d}}\gamma}\,,

(46)

where the first equation confirms the Smarr relation

, and the second one implies that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{M}-1}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}(\tilde{M}-1)}{{\mathrm{d}}\gamma}-\frac{1}{2\tilde{S}(\tilde{M}-1)}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\tilde{S}}{{\mathrm{d}}\gamma}=

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{\tilde{Y}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\tilde{Y}}{{\mathrm{d}}\gamma}\,.

(47)

It hints at constructing the analytic

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=aY_{0}~e^{-\gamma}\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma+s-1}\frac{\sqrt{\big|1-\gamma^{2}\big|}}{\gamma}\,,

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=Y_{0}^{-1}~e^{\gamma}\sqrt{\bigg|\frac{1-\gamma}{1+\gamma}\bigg|}\,.

V
Conclusion

We construct the first exact, analytic, asymptotically flat black hole solutions (
14
) in static spherically symmetric spacetime for the bumblebee gravity with a purely temporal bumblebee field.
We focus on systematic parameter space classification and thermodynamic analysis in this study, but leave studies of a full uniqueness theorem, global causal structure analysis, and critical phenomena to future work.

To accommodate the non-trivial analytic structures of these solutions, a systematic approach has been further developed to investigate their spacetime geometry, with which the asymptotic flatness is confirmed and the parameter space regime corresponding to black hole solutions (
14
) is constrained.
The parameter regime yielding black holes can be categorized into five distinct cases, depending on the value of the non-minimal coupling
\xi
. This refined classification is summarized in Table
2
.
Utilizing these new analytic results, explicit expressions for the charge-mass ratio, black hole temperature, and
Y
charge have been derived.
We rigorously show that the charge-mass ratio is unbounded for
\xi>2\kappa
. The reason why numerical studies failed in this regime is that accumulated numerical errors are incurred when evaluating the function
f
, as illustrated in Fig.
10
.

The thermodynamics of bumblebee black holes have also been re-investigated. Most results from the prior work
35
are confirmed, including the successful introduction of the
Y
charge and
X
potential via the requirements of integrability and the Smarr relation, as well as the analysis of the heat capacity
C_{Y}
for constant-
Y
processes.
Moreover, previously unreported cases have been identified. For
\xi=0.49\kappa
, the temperature
T_{H}
and the capacity
C_{Y}
profiles exhibit a turning behavior as
|Q/M|
approaches unity. For
\xi
greater than approximately
4.74\kappa
,
C_{Y}
diverges twice.

Furthermore, these analytic solutions, including the additional exact static bumblebee solutions where the VEV
b_{\mu}
possesses only a temporal component (see Appendix
A
), provide the rigorous analytic foundation for ongoing work, including detailed investigations of the quasinormal modes of asymptotically flat bumblebee black holes and wormholes, a key topic for connecting theoretical predictions with observational data.
We emphasize that the coexistence of black holes and wormholes within the same static solution (
14
) suggests that there may exist dynamical solutions in bumblebee theory describing black hole–wormhole conversion
13
;
44
;
45
;
51
, a promising direction for future work.
For instance, when
\xi<0
, an excessively large value of
\gamma
leads to a spacetime geometry containing a traversable wormhole. In addition, the spatial geometry which resembles the “Python’s lunch” type (see Ref.
6
) appears when
\xi=\kappa
and
-1<\gamma<0
.
Moreover, other exact solutions presented in Appendix
A
also seem to contain situations of wormhole geometry.
Such wormhole configurations that appeared in the vacuum solutions were unrecognized in previous studies.
Further exploration of their spacetime geometry and the derivation of observational signatures will be a valuable direction.

A.1
Extreme family

We first consider the case of a constant
g^{\mu\nu}b_{\mu}b_{\nu}
, namely,

g^{\mu\nu}b_{\mu}b_{\nu}=-\frac{b_{t}^{2}}{h}=-b^{2}\,,

(60)

where
b
is a constant.
Then Eq. (
58
) gives

(b^{2}\kappa-2)\bigg(\frac{{\mathrm{d}}b_{t}}{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}\bigg)^{2}=\frac{2\alpha b_{t}^{2}}{(\rho^{2}+\alpha)^{2}}\,,

(61)

and Eq. (
59
) becomes

(\xi-2\kappa)\frac{\rho^{2}+\alpha}{b_{t}}\bigg(\frac{{\mathrm{d}}b_{t}}{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}\bigg)^{2}=(\xi-\kappa)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}\bigg((\rho^{2}+\alpha)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}b_{t}}{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}\bigg)\,.

(62)

The unique set of consistent parameter values is

b^{2}={\frac{2}{\kappa}}\,,\quad\alpha=0\,.

(63)

The reason is as follows. If
b^{2}\kappa\neq 2
and
\alpha\neq 0
, Eq. (
61
) implies

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}b_{t}}{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}=\frac{\lambda\,b_{t}}{\rho^{2}+\alpha}\,,

(64)

where
\lambda
should be a non-zero constant satisfying

(b^{2}\kappa-2)\lambda^{2}=2\alpha\,.

(65)

However, substituting Eq. (
64
) into Eq. (
62
) yields

(\xi-2\kappa)\frac{\lambda^{2}b_{t}}{\rho^{2}+\alpha}=(\xi-\kappa)\lambda\frac{db_{t}}{d\rho}=(\xi-\kappa)\frac{\lambda^{2}\,b_{t}}{\rho^{2}+\alpha}\,,

(66)

which leads to a contradiction, as
\kappa b_{t}/(\rho^{2}+\alpha)\neq 0
and
\lambda\neq 0
.
Conversely, if
b^{2}\kappa=2
, then
\alpha=0
, and vice versa.
Therefore, the only consistent parameter values are those given in Eqs. (
63
), which automatically satisfy Eq. (
61
).
Moreover, when
\alpha=0
, Eq. (
62
) becomes

(s-2)\frac{\rho^{2}}{b_{t}}\bigg(\frac{{\mathrm{d}}b_{t}}{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}\bigg)^{2}=(s-1)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}\bigg(\rho^{2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}b_{t}}{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}\bigg)\,,

(67)

where
s=\xi/\kappa
for short.
When
s=1
, Eq. (
67
) gives a trivial solution: a constant
b_{t}
and a Minkowski spacetime up to some coordinate transformation. For
s\neq 1
, there is

\frac{s-2}{s-1}\bigg(\rho^{2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}b_{t}}{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}\bigg)=b_{t}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}b_{t}}\bigg(\rho^{2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}b_{t}}{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}\bigg)\,.

(68)

Integrating the equation, we have

\frac{s-2}{s-1}\log\bigg(\frac{b_{t}}{\beta}\bigg)=\log\bigg(\frac{\rho^{2}}{\beta l}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}b_{t}}{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}\bigg)\,,

(69)

where we introduced
\beta
and
l
to ensure the argument of
the logarithm is dimensionless.
The parameter
\beta
also acts as an integration constant.
Treating
1/\rho
as a function of
b_{t}
, this equation becomes integrable, such that

\displaystyle l\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}{\rho^{2}}=\bigg(\frac{b_{t}}{\beta}\bigg)^{-\frac{s-2}{s-1}}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}b_{t}}{\beta}\;\rightarrow\;C-\frac{l}{\rho}

\displaystyle=(s-1)\,\bigg(\frac{b_{t}}{\beta}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s-1}}\,.

(70)

Therefore, we obtain the following solutions for
b_{t}(\rho)
and
h(\rho)
:

\displaystyle b_{t}(\rho)=\beta\bigg({\frac{C}{s-1}}+{\frac{l}{1-s}}{\frac{1}{\rho}}\bigg)^{s-1}\,,

\displaystyle h(\rho)={\frac{\kappa}{2}}\beta^{2}\bigg({\frac{C}{s-1}}+{\frac{l}{1-s}}{\frac{1}{\rho}}\bigg)^{2(s-1)}\,.

(71)

If
C=0
, the function

does not tend to a constant as
\rho\rightarrow+\infty
, resulting in a metric that is not asymptotically flat. To ensure asymptotic flatness, we must impose the additional condition
C\neq 0
. After rescaling
t
and
\rho
, we obtain the following expressions

b_{t}(\rho)=\pm\sqrt{{\frac{2}{\kappa}}}\bigg(1+{\frac{\rho_{c}}{\rho}}\bigg)^{s-1}\,,\quad h(\rho)=\bigg(1+{\frac{\rho_{c}}{\rho}}\bigg)^{2(s-1)}\,.

(72)

We name this solution as the extreme family since it reduces to the extreme Reissner–Nordström (RN) solution when
\xi=0
. The same solution was also constructed in Ref.
27
;
52
.

A.2

.

(III) Solutions for the case of
\xi=2\kappa

It would be interesting to compare the case of
\xi=2\kappa
(i.e.,
s=2
) with the decoupling case
\xi=0
(i.e.,
s=0
). They share the same
\psi(w)
and
z(w)
(hence the same
\rho(w)
), but different
h(w)
and
b_{t}(w)
.
It seems that constructing the coordinate transformation with
r
and
w
for the RN black hole naturally yields the exact solution for the case of
\xi=2\kappa
. However, it is not easy to seek the appropriate expression for such a coordinate transformation. Instead, the common
\{\psi(w),z(w)\}
for
s=0
and
s=2
suggests choosing an alternative ansatz.

First, let us review the RN spacetime:

{\mathrm{d}}s^{2}=-\bigg(1-\frac{2M}{r^{2}}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r^{2}}\bigg){\mathrm{d}}t^{2}+\bigg(1-\frac{2M}{r^{2}}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r^{2}}\bigg)^{-1}{\mathrm{d}}r^{2}+r^{2}{\mathrm{d}}\Omega^{2}\,.

(107)

Simply comparing it with Eq. (
57
), we have
r^{2}h=r^{2}-2Mr+Q^{2}=(r-M)^{2}+Q^{2}-M^{2}
.
To match our ansatz Eq. (
57
), we introduce

\rho=r-M\,,\quad\alpha=Q^{2}-M^{2}\,.

(108)

Hence the RN metric becomes

ds^{2}=-\frac{\rho^{2}+\alpha}{(\rho+M)^{2}}dt^{2}+\frac{(\rho+M)^{2}}{\rho^{2}+\alpha}d\rho^{2}+(\rho+M)^{2}d\Omega^{2}\,.

(109)

It hints at the following new ansatz:

\displaystyle h(\rho)

\displaystyle=w^{2}(\rho){h_{\text{RN}}}(\rho)=w^{2}(\rho)\frac{\rho^{2}+\alpha}{(\rho+{m_{\text{RN}}})^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle r^{2}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}_{\text{RN}}(\rho)}{w(\rho)^{2}}=\frac{(\rho+{m_{\text{RN}}})^{2}}{w(\rho)^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle b_{t}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\pm\sqrt{\frac{2}{\kappa}}\,\frac{w(\rho)}{\rho+{m_{\text{RN}}}}\bigg(\big(1+w(\rho)\big)\big(\rho^{2}+\alpha\big)\bigg)^{1/2}\,,

(110)

where we introduce
{m_{\text{RN}}}
to avoid misidentifying the ADM mass
M
for the case of
\xi=2\kappa
with the mass of the RN black hole.
Then the
t
-
t
component of Eq. (
2
) and the
t
component of Eq. (
3
) imply

\displaystyle 0=2({m_{\text{RN}}}^{2}+\alpha)w+2(\rho+{m_{\text{RN}}})(\rho^{2}+2{m_{\text{RN}}}\rho-\alpha)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}w}{{\mathrm{d}}\rho}+(\rho+{m_{\text{RN}}})^{2}(\rho^{2}+\alpha)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}w}{{\mathrm{d}}\rho^{2}}\,,

(111)

and we obtain

w(\rho)=\frac{\rho+{m_{\text{RN}}}}{\rho^{2}+\alpha}(C+\beta\rho)\,.

(112)

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}s^{2}=-\frac{(C+\beta\rho)^{2}}{\rho^{2}+\alpha}{\mathrm{d}}t^{2}+\frac{\rho^{2}+\alpha}{(C+\beta\rho)^{2}}{\mathrm{d}}\rho^{2}+\bigg(\frac{{\rho^{2}+\alpha}}{C+\beta\rho}\bigg)^{2}{\mathrm{d}}\Omega^{2}\,,

\displaystyle b_{t}(\rho){\mathrm{d}}t=\pm\sqrt{\frac{2}{\kappa}}\,\frac{C+\beta\rho}{\rho^{2}+\alpha}\sqrt{\rho^{2}+\alpha+(\rho+{m_{\text{RN}}})(C+\beta\rho)}~{\mathrm{d}}t\,.

(113)

However, it does not completely solve the EoMs.
Substituting the above results into Eq. (
58
) and Eq. (
59
), we obtain the following constraint on the parameters
\alpha
,
\beta
,
C
and
{m_{\text{RN}}}
:

\displaystyle C^{2}-2C(2+\beta){m_{\text{RN}}}+\beta^{2}{m_{\text{RN}}}^{2}=4(1+\beta)\alpha\,.

(114)

To have solutions describing a spacetime with an asymptotically flat region, we should require
\beta\neq 0
.
Obviously, there is a special case satisfying Eq. (
117
):

\displaystyle\beta=-1\,,\quad{m_{\text{RN}}}=C\,,

(115)

where the corresponding
results are

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}s^{2}=-\frac{(\rho-C)^{2}}{\rho^{2}+\alpha}{\mathrm{d}}t^{2}+\frac{\rho^{2}+\alpha}{(\rho-C)^{2}}{\mathrm{d}}\rho^{2}+\bigg(\frac{{\rho^{2}+\alpha}}{\rho-C}\bigg)^{2}{\mathrm{d}}\Omega^{2}\,,

\displaystyle b_{t}(\rho){\mathrm{d}}t=\mp\sqrt{\frac{2}{\kappa}}\,\frac{\rho-C}{\rho^{2}+\alpha}\sqrt{\alpha+C^{2}}~{\mathrm{d}}t\,.

(116)

For the case of
\beta\neq-1
,
one can introduce
\alpha=\beta^{2}\bar{\alpha}
and rescale
t=\bar{t}/\beta
,
\rho=\beta{\bar{\rho}}
,
{m_{\text{RN}}}=\beta^{2}\mu
and
C=\beta^{2}\,\nu
, such that Eq. (
117
) becomes

\displaystyle\nu^{2}-2(2+\beta)\nu\mu+\beta^{2}\mu^{2}=4\bar{\alpha}\frac{1+\beta}{\beta^{2}}\,.

(117)

and the solutions become

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}s^{2}=-\frac{({\bar{\rho}}+\nu)^{2}}{{\bar{\rho}}^{2}+\bar{\alpha}}{\mathrm{d}}\bar{t}^{2}+\frac{{\bar{\rho}}^{2}+\bar{\alpha}}{({\bar{\rho}}+\nu)^{2}}{\mathrm{d}}{\bar{\rho}}^{2}+\bigg(\frac{{{\bar{\rho}}^{2}+\bar{\alpha}}}{{\bar{\rho}}+\nu}\bigg)^{2}{\mathrm{d}}\Omega^{2}\,,

\displaystyle b_{t}{\mathrm{d}}t=\pm\sqrt{\frac{2}{\kappa}}~\frac{{\bar{\rho}}+\nu}{{\bar{\rho}}^{2}+\bar{\alpha}}\bigg(\sqrt{(1+\beta)}~{\bar{\rho}}+\frac{\beta(\nu+\beta\mu)}{2\sqrt{(1+\beta)}}\bigg)~{\mathrm{d}}\bar{t}\,.

(118)

Hence these two cases contain a common formula for the line-element, though the bumblebee vector field configurations are different.

Moreover, the stealth Schwarzschild solution can appear in the situation of
\alpha<0
, if
\nu
takes an appropriate value. For simplicity, we introduce
\alpha=-a^{2}
and consider
\nu=a
. Hence, the common factor
({\bar{\rho}}+a)
in
({\bar{\rho}}+a)^{2}
and
{\bar{\rho}}-a^{2}
cancels. The metric becomes

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}s^{2}=-\frac{{\bar{\rho}}+a}{{\bar{\rho}}-a}{\mathrm{d}}\bar{t}^{2}+\frac{{\bar{\rho}}-a}{{\bar{\rho}}+a}~{\mathrm{d}}{\bar{\rho}}^{2}+\big({\bar{\rho}}-a\big)^{2}d\Omega^{2}\,.

(119)

Then, shifting
r={\bar{\rho}}-a
, one can recover the usual expression of the Schwarzschild metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\frac{r-2a}{r}d\bar{t}^{2}+\frac{r}{r-2a}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\,.

(120)

The case of
\nu=-a
also leads to the stealth Schwarzschild solution.
However, choosing
C=\pm a
for Eqs. (
116
) yields the Schwarzschild solution, rather than the stealth Schwarzschild solution, because the bumblebee vector field vanishes due to
\alpha+C^{2}=0
.
