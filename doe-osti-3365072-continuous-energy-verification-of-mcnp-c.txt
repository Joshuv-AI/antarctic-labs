 Continuous-Energy Verification of MCNP Calculations
 Using One-Group Spherical and Slab Criticality
 Benchmarks
 Avneet Sood

                                              April 9, 2026

 Abstract
 This work presents a continuous-energy Monte Carlo verification study of one-
 group spherical and slab criticality benchmarks using the MCNP®1 code. Classical
 tabulations and newly generated benchmark solutions obtained by direct numerical
 evaluation by the author [1, 2, 3] are considered. The benchmarks span weakly to
 strongly multiplying regimes and provide analytically defined critical radii as functions
 of a single parameter, c.
 A one-group, continuous-energy representation was implemented for MCNP simu-
 lations using pseudo-isotope cross sections definitions. These cross-sections had trans-
 port and scattering properties that were held fixed while ν was adjusted to reproduce
 c. Criticality calculations were performed using the KCODE option with sufficiently large
 particle populations and cycle counts to ensure statistical convergence.
 The analytical critical dimension was used directly as input along with the specified
 nuclear cross sections and the calculated keff was reported using the final combined
 collision/absorption/track-length estimator. Across all cases examined, the Monte
 Carlo results are statistically consistent with unity within reported uncertainties, demon-
 strating agreement between the continuous-energy implementation and the analytical
 one-group benchmarks.

1 Introduction
Monte Carlo neutron transport methods are widely used for criticality analysis because of
their ability to represent complex geometries and detailed physics with minimal approxima-

tion. Verification against analytically defined transport benchmarks provides confidence that
numerical implementations reproduce known solutions of the underlying transport equations.
 Classical one-group criticality benchmarks derived from singular-eigenfunction transport
theory provide a rigorous yet geometrically simple verification problem. Two canonical
geometries are commonly considered: the homogeneous sphere and the infinite slab with
vacuum boundaries. In each case, the critical dimension is determined analytically as a
function of the multiplication parameter c under isotropic scattering and vacuum boundary
conditions. While the material parameter c is independent of geometry, the corresponding
critical dimension depends on leakage characteristics specific to the spatial configuration,
providing a stringent multi-geometry verification test. This characteristic is particularly
helpful when meshes are used to generate these simplified geometries.
 The present work documents MCNP’s ability to reproduce analytically defined, one-group
spherical and slab geometry criticality benchmarks using a continuous-energy implementa-
tion. Classical benchmark cases [4] and newly generated extended benchmark cases are
considered [2, 3]. The focus of this paper is on verification of MCNP-computed keff using the
new analytically-derived critical radius as input. Earlier verification studies employing the
analytic benchmark test set are documented by Parsons et al. [5] using a multi-group imple-
mentation, and Brown [6] with a continuous-energy representation on the original analytic
test set.

2 Benchmark Problems
Analytically-defined, homogeneous, one-group, isotropically scattering, sphere and slab with
vacuum boundary condition benchmarks are defined in this work. In both cases, critical-
ity corresponds to keff = 1. The parameter c is prescribed and the corresponding critical
dimension (either Rcrit or bcrit ) is determined.

2.1 Spherical benchmarks
The spherical geometry benchmarks consists of a homogeneous sphere of radius R with
vacuum boundary conditions. The benchmark quantity of interest is the critical radius
Rcrit (c), expressed in mean free paths. Reference values are taken from benchmark data
obtained by direct numerical evaluation [1, 2].

2.2 Slab benchmarks
The slab geometry benchmarks consists of a homogeneous slab of half-thickness b with vac-
uum boundary conditions at z = ±b. The benchmark quantity of interest is the critical
half-thickness bcrit (c). Reference values are taken from the benchmark data obtained by
direct numerical evaluation [3].

3 Monte Carlo Modeling Approach in MCNP
3.1 Geometry and boundary conditions
Two geometries were modeled: a homogeneous sphere and a homogeneous slab, each with
vacuum boundary conditions. A single spherical cell of radius R was defined and surrounded
by vacuum for the spherical geometry. Similarly, a planar geometry was constructed with
boundaries at z = ±b, representing a slab of thickness 2b. Vacuum boundary conditions were
applied at both surfaces. In all cases, the system consists of a single homogeneous region
with no external neutron source. The initial neutron source was sampled uniformly within
the fissile region corresponding to the geometry under consideration.

3.2 One-group cross sections
3.2.1 Definition

A single one-group macroscopic cross-section set corresponding to the Pub-239(b) benchmark
(Problem 8: Pub-1-0-SP)[4] was used. The total, scattering, fission, and capture macroscopic
cross sections were held fixed at their benchmark values, and only the mean number of
neutrons emitted per fission, ν, was varied to reproduce the desired benchmark parameter
c. For each target benchmark value of c, the corresponding value of ν was computed by
rearranging Eq. (1) while holding all macroscopic cross sections fixed.
 This approach preserves the transport mean free path and scattering kernel across all
cases and isolates neutron multiplication as the sole varying parameter. Differences in critical

4 Results: keff Verification
4.1 Spherical Benchmark Verification
Table 2 summarizes the MCNP continuous-energy verification results for the one-group
spherical benchmark Problem 8 (Pub-1-0-SP). For each benchmark value of the parame-
ter c, the analytically critical radius was used directly as input to MCNP. The reported keff
values are the final estimated combined collision/absorption/track-length estimator from
MCNP version 6.3, together with the associated estimated standard deviation.
 All calculations were performed using MCNP’s continuous-energy mode. Criticality was
estimated using the standard k-eigenvalue capability using the KCODE option, with 105 source
particles per cycle, 250 inactive cycles for source convergence, and 5000 active cycles for tally
accumulation. This choice of parameters was made to ensure adequate convergence of the
fission source distribution and to reduce cycle-to-cycle statistical correlations in the reported

the analytically critical sphere using a spherically symmetric source definition.
 Convergence was assessed by examining the stability of cycle-by-cycle keff estimates and
the final combined estimator. No variance-reduction techniques were employed; all calcula-
tions used analog transport.

4.2 Slab benchmark verification
Table 3 summarizes the MCNP continuous-energy verification results for the one-group slab
benchmarks. For each value of c, the analytically determined critical half-thickness bcrit
was used as input. All simulation parameters were identical to those used in the spherical
calculations, with geometry-specific modifications limited to the spatial definition and source
distribution.
 The slab results exhibit statistical consistency with unity across all values of c, with all
cases falling within approximately ±1.5 standard deviations of unity. The distribution of
positive and negative deviations is balanced, and no systematic dependence on c is observed.
The level of agreement is comparable to that observed for the spherical benchmarks, indicat-
ing that the continuous-energy implementation accurately reproduces analytically defined
critical conditions for both geometries despite their differing leakage characteristics.

4.3 Discussion
The MCNP-computed keff values are statistically consistent for both geometries with unity
across the full range of c. No systematic bias is observed as a function of geometry or
multiplication strength. The results confirm that the continuous-energy implementation
reproduces analytically defined critical configurations for both spherical and slab systems
using a consistent one-group representation.
 The agreement observed for both spherical and slab geometries is significant because
these configurations represent fundamentally different leakage regimes. The spherical sys-
tem exhibits curvature-driven leakage, while the slab geometry is dominated by planar leak-
age. The ability of MCNP to reproduce analytically defined critical conditions in both cases
using identical one-group cross sections and only geometry-dependent spatial definitions pro-
vides strong evidence that the continuous-energy implementation correctly captures neutron
transport behavior independent of geometry.

5 Conclusions
This work presents a continuous-energy Monte Carlo verification of one-group spherical and
slab criticality benchmarks using MCNP. The historical benchmark tabulations of Kaper,
Lindeman, and Leaf and newly generated benchmark solutions obtained by direct numer-
ical evaluation of the defining transport equations were used to define analytically critical
configurations spanning weakly to strongly multiplying regimes.

 6

 Verification was demonstrated independently for both geometries using analytically de-
termined critical dimensions derived from classical and newly generated benchmark data.
The analytically critical radius was used directly as input and the resulting keff was eval-
uated using MCNP’s combined estimator. Statistical agreement with unity was observed
across all cases, with no evidence of systematic bias as a function of c.
 By holding transport and scattering cross sections fixed and varying only ν, the cal-
culations preserve the underlying one-group transport physics while enabling direct com-
parison with analytic solutions. These results extend the documented verification basis
for continuous-energy Monte Carlo treatment of one-group criticality problems. This work
extends earlier Monte Carlo verification studies of analytic benchmark test sets [4, 6] by ex-
tending the continuous-energy implementation and by exercising newly defined benchmark
cases beyond the classical tabulations.

A.3 Generation of One-Group Continuous-Energy Cross Sections
The one-group continuous-energy cross section files used in this work were generated using
the simple ace.pl utility distributed with MCNP [6]. This tool provides a lightweight
mechanism for constructing custom ACE-format pseudo-isotopes directly from user-specified
one-group macroscopic cross section data, and is intended for code verification and testing
applications rather than for general-purpose nuclear data evaluation.
 In the present work, simple ace.pl was used to generate a single-energy-point ACE table
with isotropic scattering and fixed total, scattering, absorption, and fission macroscopic cross
sections corresponding to the selected benchmark problem. The energy grid was collapsed
to a single point, eliminating energy dependence and enforcing one-group transport behavior
consistent with the analytical benchmark definition. For each benchmark case, the transport
and scattering cross sections were held fixed, and only ν was modified to reproduce the target
value of the benchmark material parameter c.
 The resulting ACE files define verification-only pseudo-isotopes that are referenced in
MCNP through the XS1 option, ensuring that the continuous-energy Monte Carlo calcula-
tions employ the intended one-group transport physics. Additional details on the simple ace.pl
utility, its input options, and its intended usage are documented in the MCNP user man-
ual [8], where it is described as a supported method for constructing simplified ACE-format
data for testing and verification purposes.
