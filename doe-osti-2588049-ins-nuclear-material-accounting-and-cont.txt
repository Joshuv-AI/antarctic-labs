2.0 Objectives
 • Provide background information on adhesive TID processes; including requesting, issuance,
 application, voiding and removing.
 • Demonstrate recommended techniques and methods for performance and record creation.
 • Provide common improper methods observed throughout adhesive TID processes
 • Follow an example detailed procedure for all adhesive TID processes which may be
 observed or implemented within a facility.

3.0 Structure
Each section of the Adhesive TID Process Demonstration is broken down into subparts as
referenced in the outline contained in the Contents section, these are referenced as Activity
Sections. The structure of the Adhesive TID Process Demonstration items is, to the best of the
writer’s ability, in the order of events that the NMAC mitigating techniques are performed. The video
adaptations are also broken into these segments for ease of viewing and instruction. Each video
segment created is accompanied with guides for implementation during use. During the course of
Y25, funding allowed for two of the four adhesive mylar TID videos to be made, the subsequent two
others are scripted and ready for video creation if future funding becomes available. The scripts are
provided in this document. It is noteworthy, that the environment and characters are all consistent
across these and other NMAC videos of this nature, making the video creation process quicker than
this initial year.

The individual sections housed within contents are intended to be able to be played either as a full
instructional video or in discrete sections as desired by the instructors for the specific training
activities. The script and associated videos are designed to follow INS NMAC Kit procedures.

Throughout all process sections for Adhesive TID Process Demonstration, areas which are unique
to adhesive mylar TIDs are in green, while the remainder black text is intended to be applicable
generically for all TIDs.

Throughout collective Adhesive TID Process Demonstration there are 3 characters present. For all,
please see Attachment: Character Roster for specific information carried through all NMAC videos.

 1) Facility Operator #1 (Sam Gourdy)
 2) Facility Operator #2 (Alex Mordor)

5.0 Setting
For Activity Section 1- Requesting/Issuing TID, the activity section may occur in any location outside
of the chosen nuclear material facility area from the subsequent sections. The characters must be
attired in “Outside of nuclear material handling areas” clothing per Attachment: Character Roster.

All remaining activity sections shall take place in a nuclear material facility area. Therefore, the
characters must be attired in “Inside of nuclear material handling areas” clothing per Attachment:
Character Roster.

For ease, the smaller containers referenced throughout VI. Detailed Script should take place on a
bench top, clear of clutter. It is recommended to have similar containers in the background with
TIDs already applied, present but not interacted with, to prompt the feel of an operating facility
location. The location itself is to be contained in a single Material Balance Area (MBA). This is
referenced as MBA 42 for all requests and updates mentioned. Visually, this area and all activities
should be included within a small room or section (denoted with tape on the floor). Throughout the
activities, the containers must not leave the designated MBA 42.

3.0 Structure
Each section of the Wire TID Process Demonstration is broken down into subparts as referenced in
the outline contained in the Contents section, these are referenced as Activity Sections. The
structure of the Wire TID Process Demonstration items is, to the best of the writer’s ability, in the
order of events that the NMAC mitigating techniques are performed. The video adaptations are also
broken into these segments for ease of viewing and instruction. Each video segment created is
accompanied with guides for implementation during use. During the course of Y25, funding allowed
for one of the four multi-lok wire TID videos to be made, the subsequent three others are scripted
and ready for video creation if future funding becomes available. The scripts are provided in this
document. It is noteworthy, that the environment and characters are all consistent across these
and other NMAC videos of this nature, making the video creation process quicker than this initial
year.

The individual sections housed within contents are intended to be able to be played either as a full
instructional video or in discrete sections as desired by the instructors for the speciﬁc training
activities. The script and associated videos are designed to follow INS NMAC Kit procedures.

Throughout all process sections for Wire TID Process Demonstration, areas which are unique to
Multi-lok wire TIDs are in purple, while the remainder black text is intended to be applicable
generically for all TIDs.

2.0 Objectives
 • Demonstrate and provide proceduralized steps for proper material weighing to ensure that
 all materials being tracked in a physical inventory are accurately weighed and logged.
 • Verify the weight of materials against the declared weight and ensure that discrepancies are
 identiﬁed and resolved.
 • Emphasizes accuracy, safety, and proper documentation, with a strict adherence to the
 two-person rule to ensure the integrity of the inventory data.

3.0 Structure
The script is designed to cover the broad outline and structure as detailed below for an item with a
TID:

 1. Preparation
 a. Veriﬁcation of scale calibration.
 b. Ensuring the scale is functioning correctly before use.

 2. Weighing Process
 a. Weighing individual cans and ensuring that the weight is consistent with the
 declared weight.
 b. Recording the weight in the Inventory Weighing Form.
 c. Verifying each reading with the second team member.

 3. Final Review
 a. Summing the total weight of all materials.
 b. Performing a ﬁnal calibration check.
 c. Submitting the completed inventory records for approval.

 The item weight measurement takes place in a secure area of the facility where materials are stored. The area is equipped with balance

 The space includes:

 • Balance Scale: A calibrated scale for weighing materials.
 • Inventory Logs: A form where each weight is recorded for veriﬁcation.
 • Calibration Weights: A set of standard weights used to check the scale’s accuracy.
 • Materials: Labeled containers (such as cans) to be weighed.
