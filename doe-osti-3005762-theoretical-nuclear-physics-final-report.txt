 Final Report on DOE Award No. DE-SC0015266

 Che-Ming Ko, Texas A&M University

 This award from the Department of Energy supported the PI, Che-Ming Ko of Texas
A&M University, for the period from April 1, 2022 to August 31, 2025 at an amount of
$150,000 to carry out the project entitled “Theoretical nuclear physics”. The scientific goal
of the project was to develop transport models for heavy ion collisions at various energies and
with both stable and rare isotopes and to carry out systematic comparisons of theoretical
predictions with experimental data. Results from this project has led to the publication
of 14 papers in and the submission of 10 papers to peer-reviewed journals, the publication
of 3 papers in and the submission of 1 paper to conference proceedings. Also, the PI has
presented 12 invited talks at international conferences and workshops. In the following, a
brief summary of these results is given.
 1) Transport model study of intermediate-energy heavy ion collisions. The
PI is a member of the Transport Model Evaluation Project (TMEP) Collaboration, which
was formed in 2015 to understand the different conclusions on the density dependence of the
nuclear symmetry energy that were extracted from measured charged pion ratio in heavy ion
collisions using different transport models of the BUU or the QMD type. During this funding
period, besides having published the review article “Transport model comparison studies of
intermediate-energy heavy-ion collisions’” in Progress in Particle and Nuclear Physics based
on results from 14 BUU and 12 QMD codes [1], the collaboration also published a paper on
the comparison of pion production in transport simulations of heavy-ion collisions at 270A
MeV under controlled conditions [2]. This comparison study has identified sensitive aspects
of a simulation with respect to pion observables and suggested optimal procedures in some
cases.
 The PI and his collaborators have also used the isospin-dependent relativistic Vlasov-
Uehling-Uhlenbeck (RVUU) model, which he developed in 1980s, to study charged pion
 √
production in Au+Au collisions at sN N = 2.4 GeV measured by the HADES Collabo-
ration at GSI [3] and found that to describe the measured pion rapidity distributions and
transverse momentum spectra in various collision centralities requires a density-dependent
Delta resonance production cross section that is smaller than its value measured in free
space.

 2) Light nuclei production in heavy ion collisions. The PI and his collaborators
have developed a kinetic approach to the production of light nuclei, such as deuteron, triton,
helium-3 and the alpha particle, in heavy ion collisions by including them in the transport
model as dynamic degrees of freedom with their production and destruction described by
nucleon and pion catalyzed multi-nucleon reactions and their reverse reactions. They have
found that the measured yields of light nuclei in central Au + Au collisions at energies of
0.25A GeV - 1.0A GeV by the FOPI Collaboration can be well described after including
their Mott effects due to the Pauli blocking of their internal momentum states on their
binding energies [4]. An unexpectedly abundant alpha clustering was found in the warm
and dense matter produced in these collisions [5], which will have profound implications
for modeling the nuclear equation of state and describing supernovae and neutron star
mergers. For relativistic heavy heavy ion collisions at RHIC and the LHC, assuming light
nuclei are initially produced statistically from the hadronization hypersurface of the viscous
hydrodynamic model MUSIC with a crossover type of equation of state, the pion catalyzed
multi-nucleon reactions was found to have little effects on the deuteron yield but reduce the
triton and helium-3 yields by about a factor of 1.8 from their initial values [6], which is in
excellent agreement with the latest experimental data and thus provides the evidence for the
importance of hadronic re-scattering effects on the little bang nucleosynthesis in relativistic
 √
heavy-ion collisions. In pp collisions at sN N = 13 TeV, the pion-catalyzed production of
light nuclei was shown to give rise to a pion-deuteron femtoscopic correlation that peaks
near the Delta resonance mass [7] as observed in the ALICE experiments.
 Based on an extended Nambu-Jona-Lasinio model with an eight-quark scalar-vector in-
teraction to allow the critical point to have a flexible location in the QCD phase diagram, the
PI and his collaborators have studied the effect of a first-order phase transition on the yield
ratio Nt Np /Nd2 of produced proton (p), deuteron (d), and triton (t). It was found that the
large density fluctuations generated by the spinodal instability during the first-order phase
transition could survive the subsequent fast expansion of the hadronic matter and lead to an
 √
enhanced yield ratio Nt Np /Nd2 in central collisions at sN N = 3 − 5 GeV [8, 9]. Discussions
on the potential use of the yield ratio Nt Np /Nd2 to search for the QCD critical point was
published by the PI as a research highlight paper in Nuclear Science and Technique [10].
 Using the nucleon coalescence model based on the kinetic freeze-out nucleons from the
hybrid model of MUSIC hydrodynamics and UrQMD hadronic transport model, the PI and

 3

his collaborator have studied anti-deuteron production and its event-by-event fluctuation in
 √
Pb+Pb collisions at sN N = 5.02 TeV [11]. A clear suppression of the anti-deuteron to
antiproton yield ratio was found in peripheral collisions as in the measurements from the
ALICE Collaboration. For those theoretical events that fit the measured net-proton number
fluctuation, the model can also describe the net-proton number fluctuation and the negative
correlation between the antiproton and anti-deuteron multiplicities at large pseudorapidity
acceptance as observed in ALICE experiments.
 Using the coalescence model with the phase-space information of proton and neutron
pairs from a multiphase transport (AMPT) model at the kinetic freezeout, the PI and his
 √
collaborators have studied jet-associated deuteron production in pp collisions at s = 13
 √
TeV and p-Pb collisions at sN N = 5.02 TeV [12]. Although the in-jet coalescence factor for
deuterons of low transverse momentum, given by the ratio of the in-jet deuteron number to
the square of the in-jet proton number, is found to be larger than the coalescence factor in
the medium perpendicular to the jet direction by a factor of about 10 in pp collisions and 25
in p-Pb collisions as observed in the ALICE measurements at the LHC, such enhancements
mainly come from coalescence of nucleons inside the jet with nucleons in the medium. Only
at higher transverse momentum can deuteron production from the coalescence of nucleons
inside the jet dominate over that from the medium nucleons, providing a new window to
probe the phase-space structure of nucleons inside jets.
 Using the coalescence model based on the kinetic freezeout baryons from heavy ion col-
lisions, the PI and his collaborators have also studied the angular distribution of the decay
product of polarized hypertriton and found it to be highly sensitive to the spin configuration
of its wave function [13], providing thus a novel way to determine its spin structure.
 3) Relativistic heavy ion collisions. Using the AMPT+MUSIC+URQMD hybrid
model, the PI and his collaborators have studied the global and local spin polarizations of
Lambda hyperon as a function of the freeze-out temperature of the Lambda spin degree
 √
of freedom in the hadronic phase of Au+Au collisions at sN N = 19.6 GeV by including
contributions from both thermal vorticity and shear in the hadronic matter [14]. With the
Lambda spin freeze-out temperature dropping from the hadronization temperature of 160
MeV to 110 MeV at the kinetic freeze-out, both the global and local spin polarizations of
hyperons due to the thermal vorticity decrease by a factor of 2, while those due to the thermal
shear decrease quickly and become negligibly small already at 140 MeV. These results suggest

 4

the importance of taking into account the evolution of the Lambda spin degree of freedom
in the hadronic stage of relativistic heavy-ion collisions when theoretical predictions are
compared with experimental measurements. However, the PI and his collaborators have
found in another study that the Lambda spin polarization only decreases by 7-12% during the
hadronic stage of relativistic heavy ion collisions because of its small spin-flip scattering cross
section with a pion through the Σ∗ (1358) resonance [15], which justifies the assumption in
theoretical studies that compare the lambda polarization calculated at the chemical freezeout
to the measured one at the kinetic freezeout, as pointed out by the PI in an invited talk at
The 20th International Conference on Strangeness in Quark Matter [16]. This conclusion
remains valid after treating the Σ∗ resonance dynamically by including the time evolution
of its number and polarization in the spin-dependent kinetic equations [17].
 Taking kinetically freeze-out nucleons from an isotropic and thermalized fireball of con-
stant vorticity and including quantum corrections up to h̄2 in the coalescence model for light
nuclei production, the PI and his collaborators have demonstrated that polarized nucleons
lead to global spin alignment of the unstable nucleus 4 Li, which can be measured through
its strong decays via 4 Li → 3 He + p [18]. This study offers a promising method to probe
the spin dynamics, vortical structure, and spin-dependent equation-of-state of the nuclear
matter produced in heavy-ion collisions.
 Based on the spin wave function of the hypertriton, the PI and his collaborators have
derived a simple relation among the polarizations of proton, Λ, and hypertriton [19]. Mea-
suring the Λ and hypertriton polarizations via their self-analyzing weak decays thus provides
a practical experimental avenue for accessing the spin polarizations of protons and neutrons,
instead of the more challenging direct measurement that requires external polarimeters based
on secondary interactions.
 Within the coalescence model based on nucleons and Lambda hyperons from a micro-
scopic hybrid hydro model with a hadronic afterburner for nuclear cluster production in
 √
Pb-Pb collisions at sN N = 5.02 TeV, the PI and his collaborators have found a softening
of the hypertriton transverse momentum spectrum compared to that of helium-3 as a re-
sult of its larger size, contrary to the prediction from the blast-wave model which assumes
that hypertriton and helium-3 are thermally produced at the kinetic freeze-out of heavy-ion
collisions [20].
 The PI and his collaborators have also carried out a more consistent study of the pro-

 5

duction of chiral partners K1 and K ∗ in heavy ion collisions at the LHC by including the
effect due to non-unity hadron fugacities during the evolution of produced hadronic matter
and using temperature-dependent K1 mass due to chiral symmetry restoration [21]. An en-
hanced K1 /K ∗ ratio was found in peripheral and midcentral collisions compared to the case
of using temperature-independent K1 mass without chiral symmetry restoration, confirming
its use as a good signature for chiral symmetry restoration in the hot dense matter produced
in relativistic heavy ion collisions.
 The PI and his collaborators have further used the well-tuned kaon phase-space dis-
tributions from the Hydro-Coal-Frag model to study the production of f0 (980) from K K̄
coalescence [22, 23]. With reasonable values for the f0 (980) radius, their results success-
fully reproduce the differential elliptic flow measured by the CMS Collaboration and also
agree with the transverse momentum spectra measured by the ALICE Collaboration. These
results in heavy ion collisions are consistent with the K K̄ molecular picture of the f0 (980).
 Moreover, the PI and his collaborators have studied dilepton production using a partonic
transport model that is based on an extended Nambu-Jona-Lasinio (NJL) model [24]. They
have found that the extracted temperature from dileptons of intermediate masses agrees well
with the temperature of the partonic matter. Also, the extracted in-medium quark masses
are found to decrease with increasing dilepton temperature, implying that dilepton produc-
tion can serve as a probe of chiral symmetry restoration in high energy heavy ion collisions.
They have further investigated the influence of initial nuclear quadrupole deformation, char-
 √
acterized by the parameter β22 , on dilepton production in U + U collisions at sN N = 193
GeV [25]. Their results show a clear linear dependence of dilepton yields on β22 in both the
low-mass region (LMR, < 1 GeV/c2 ) and intermediate-mass region (IMR, 1 − 3 GeV/c2 ) of
the dilepton spectrum for the most central collisions. Also, dilepton production in the IMR
region exhibits a stronger sensitivity to nuclear deformation than in the LMR, reflecting
the dominance of earlier partonic processes in this mass range. These results suggest that
precise measurements of dilepton yields in relativistic heavy ion collisions can provide a
complementary probe of the nuclear deformation parameter β2 of 238 U.
 In collaboration with his colleague at Texas A&M University, Rainer Fries, the PI has also
developed a general formalism for computing the recombination probabilities of two particles
into bound states with well-defined orbital angular momentum quantum number, in which
the interaction is given by an isotropic harmonic oscillator potential in 3 dimensions [26–

 6

28]. This formalism makes it possible to extend their earlier study, where only low lying
hadronic states are included, to include all physical hadrons in the particle data book when
shower partons in an energetic jet are converted to hadrons. The resulting algorithm for
hadronization via recombination has been adopted by the JETSCAPE Collaboration into
its general code package.
