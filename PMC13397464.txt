Safety of roasted seeds from Plukenetia volubilis L. as a traditional food from a third country pursuant to Regulation (EU) No 2015/2283
Authors: EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens (NDA), Dominique Turck, Torsten Bohn, Montaña Cámara, Jacqueline Castenmiller, Stefaan De Henauw, Ángeles Jos, Alexandre Maciuk, Inge Mangelsdorf, Breige McNulty, Androniki Naska, Kristina Pentieva, Alfonso Siani, Frank Thies, Margarita Aguilera‐Gómez, Thomas Frenzel, Francesca Marcon, Harry J McArdle, Peter Moldeus, Monika Neuhäuser‐Berthold, Marcello Laganaro, Emanuela Turla, Karen Ildico Hirsch‐Ernst
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, the EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens (NDA) was asked to deliver an opinion on roasted seeds from Plukenetia volubilis L. as a Traditional Food from a third country (TF) pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2015/2283. Roasted seeds from Plukenetia volubilis L. (commonly known as sacha inchi) have been consumed in Peru for more than 25 years. The applicant intends to place on the European Union (EU) market roasted sacha inchi seeds to be consumed as a whole food. This TF had previously been subject to a TF notification under the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283. During the assessment of that notification, EFSA identified safety concerns related to uncertainties regarding the presence of alkaloids in the TF. In the current application, the applicant provided additional data addressing the concern on alkaloids. These analyses identified trigonelline as the only quantifiable alkaloid present in the TF, a finding that is also supported by data available in the literature. Dietary exposure to trigonelline, for example, through coffee consumption at amounts higher than those determined in the TF, has not been associated with adverse effects. The Panel considers that the newly submitted evidence sufficiently characterises the alkaloid fraction of the TF, addresses the previously identified uncertainties and resolves the safety objections raised during the earlier assessment of the TF notification. Furthermore, based on the composition and the proposed conditions of use, the Panel considers that the consumption of the TF is not nutritionally disadvantageous. The Panel concludes that the TF, roasted seeds from Plukenetia volubilis L., is safe under the proposed conditions of use.Keywords: inchi, Plukenetia volubilis L., sacha, seeds, third country, traditional food

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference as provided by the requestor

On 30 November 2018, the companies Promperu, Agroindustrias Osho SAC, Amazon Health Products SAC, Comercio Alternativo de Productos No Tradicionales y Desarrollo para Latino América Perú (Candela) and Shanantina SAC (ʽthe applicantʼ) submitted to the Commission a notification of the intention to place roasted seeds from Plukenetia volubilis L. on the Union market as a traditional food from a third country (TF) in accordance with Article 14 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283. 1 The applicant requested for roasted seeds from Plukenetia volubilis L. to be consumed as such by the general population.

Duly reasoned safety objections to the placing on the market within the Union of roasted seeds from Plukenetia volubilis L. were submitted to the Commission by the Authority within the 4‐month period laid down in Article 15(2) of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283.

Pursuant to Article 15(5) of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283, the Commission issued a Commission Implementing Decision not authorising the placing on the market within the Union of roasted seeds from Plukenetia volubilis L. as a TF under Regulation (EU) 2015/2283. The applicant may submit an application to the Commission in accordance with Article 16 of that Regulation.

On 17 March 2021, the applicant submitted an application for the authorisation of roasted seeds from Plukenetia volubilis L. as a TF pursuant to Article 16 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 following the requirements of Articles 4, 5, 6 and 8 of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2468 2 of 20 December 2017 laying down administrative and scientific requirements concerning traditional foods from third countries in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2015/2283.

In accordance with Article 16 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283, the European Commission asks the European Food Safety Authority to provide a scientific opinion on the safety of roasted seeds from Plukenetia volubilis L. on the Union market as a TF and the opinion should take into account the requirements of Article 17 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283.

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The safety assessment of this TF is based on the data supplied in the application which consists of the initial Article 14 TF notification, duly reasoned safety objections referred to in Article 15(2) of Regulation (EU) No 2015/2283, the applicant's responses to those safety objections and information submitted by the applicant following EFSA's requests for supplementary information. 3

The EFSA Technical Report on the notification of roasted seeds from Plukenetia volubilis L. as TF presents the duly reasoned safety objections raised by EFSA following its evaluation of the Article 14 TF notification (EFSA, 2020).

Administrative and scientific requirements for TF applications referred to in Article 16 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 are listed in Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2468.

A common and structured format on the presentation of TF applications is described in the EFSA guidance on the preparation and presentation of TF notification and TF application (EFSA NDA Panel, 2016). As indicated in this guidance, it is the duty of the applicant to provide all of the available scientific data (including both data in favour and not in favour) that are pertinent to the safety of the TF.

Methodologies

The assessment follows the methodology set out in the EFSA guidance on TF notifications and TF applications (EFSA NDA Panel, 2016) and the principles described in the relevant existing guidance documents from the EFSA Scientific Committee. The legal provisions for the assessment are laid down in Article 17 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 and in Article 10 of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2468.

The assessment of this TF application concerns only the risks that might be associated with consumption of the TF under the proposed conditions of use, and is not an assessment of the efficacy of the TF with regard to any claimed benefit.

ASSESSMENT

Introduction

The TF which is the subject of this application consists of roasted seeds from Plukenetia volubilis L., hereinafter referred as ‘sacha inchi’. Roasted sacha inchi seeds have been consumed for more than 25 years in Peru. The TF falls under category (iv) of Article 3(2)(a) of Regulation (EU) No 2015/2283, i.e. food consisting of, isolated from or produced from plants or their parts. The applicant intends to place on the EU market roasted sacha inchi seeds to be consumed as a whole food.

This TF was previously notified as a TF in accordance with Article 14 of Regulation (EU) No 2015/2283. As a result of that evaluation, EFSA concluded that the TF posed a safety risk to human health due to missing information on the quantity and type of alkaloids present in the TF. Thus, EFSA raised safety objections to the placing on the market of the TF within the EU (EFSA, 2020).

Identity of the TF

The TF consists of roasted seeds from Plukenetia volubilis L. (family: Euphorbiaceae). According to the Plants of the World Online database, 4 Plukenetia volubilis L. is an accepted scientific name. Sajorium volubile (L.) Baill is a synonym. Common names are sacha inchi seeds and Inca peanuts.

Production process

After shelling and peeling, sacha inchi seeds are washed in hot water and then roasted in a roasting machine at temperatures up to 120°C (EFSA, 2020).

Compositional data

In the initial TF notification, given the uncertainty regarding the alkaloids that may be present in the TF, EFSA concluded that the TF may pose a risk to human health and raised safety objections to its placing on the market within the EU (EFSA, 2020).

New data in response to the safety objections

In the present application, the applicant provided additional data in response to the safety objections on alkaloids raised by EFSA.

The applicant provided two review articles reporting the occurrence of tropane and pyrrolizidine alkaloids in plants belonging to the Euphorbiaceae family in general, but without any reference to genus or species within that family (Dreger et al., 2009; Matsuura & Fett‐Neto, 2015).

The applicant analysed five TF batches using liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) methods targeting these alkaloid classes. Tropane and pyrrolizidine alkaloids were below their respective limits of quantification (LOQs), ranging from 1 to 5 μg/kg. Other classes of alkaloids have been reported in the Euphorbiaceae family (EFSA Compendium of Botanicals 5 ). The analyses conducted by the applicant targeting specific classes of alkaloids resulted in contents below the LOQs. 6

The applicant analysed one batch of the TF to investigate the presence of alkaloids. Sequential solvent extractions with solvents of increasing polarity were applied to the defatted pressed cake derived from the TF, followed by analysis using a titrimetric method. The total alkaloid content was quantified at 0.0058% (58 mg/kg). However, 6%–8% of this fraction was identified as oleamide, which is not an alkaloid, while the remaining 92%–94% of the fraction was not chemically characterised.

Following EFSA's request to further characterise the unidentified alkaloid fraction, the applicant performed an additional analysis on the TF. The Panel identified deficiencies in the extraction procedure applied prior to this analysis, noting that the resulting fraction mainly consisted of polyunsaturated fatty acid esters. The Panel therefore considered the extraction method not suitable for alkaloid isolation and requested further analyses.

In response, the applicant referred to a metabolomic study on raw sacha inchi seeds by Le et al. (2024), where metabolomic profiling was performed using a combination of proton nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy (1H‐NMR), gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS), liquid chromatography–quadrupole time‐of‐flight mass spectrometry (LC‐QTOF) and global natural products social (GNPS) molecular networking. This study reported a total alkaloid content of approximately 20 mg/kg in raw seeds and identified trigonelline as the only quantifiable alkaloid present in raw sacha inchi seeds.

Based on this evidence, the applicant analysed five batches of raw sacha inchi seeds and five batches of the TF using a targeted validated LC–MS/MS method for trigonelline, a high‐resolution mass spectrometry untargeted screening and an investigation of other nitrogen‐containing metabolites following an appropriate extraction process. Trigonelline was consistently identified as the main nitrogen‐containing compound at concentrations ranging from 51 to 175 mg/kg dry weight in raw seeds and from 52 to 111 mg/kg dry weight in roasted seeds. Quantitative results were confirmed using two complementary mass spectrometry platforms. No other signals indicative of other alkaloid classes were detected in the high‐resolution screening.

The Panel notes that trigonelline contents in the TF from this analysis are consistent with amounts of alkaloids and trigonelline reported in the literature (Le et al., 2024; Srichamnong et al., 2018) and align with a previous analysis of the TF quantifying total alkaloids (i.e. 58 mg/kg in one TF batch).

The applicant provided the publication by Konstantinidis et al. (2023), which reported trigonelline contents ranging from 3100 to 7500 mg/kg in coffee beans, which are also usually subject to roasting processing, and from 2000 to 7200 mg/kg in coffee beverages. Additionally, trigonelline is also present in other plant‐derived food commodities such as soybean and rice in significantly lower content (Ashihara et al., 2015). The Panel considers that this evidence supports systemic exposure to trigonelline from the background diet, which has not been associated with adverse effects.

Overall, the Panel considers that the newly submitted evidence sufficiently characterises the alkaloid fraction of the TF, addresses the previously identified uncertainties and resolves the safety objections raised during the earlier assessment of the TF notification.

Specifications

Following the identification of trigonelline in the TF, the applicant proposed to include in the specifications the parameter ‘Total alkaloids (expressed as trigonelline equivalents): ≤ 150 mg/kg’. However, as trigonelline is the only quantifiable alkaloid identified in the TF and it is not considered a compound of safety concern (see Section 3.4.1), the Panel considers that the inclusion of a parameter referring to either alkaloids in general or specifically to trigonelline is not necessary.

The applicant proposed including parameters such as saturated and polyunsaturated fatty acids, vitamin E and sodium, which relate to the nutritional profile of the TF rather than to its specifications. The applicant also proposed to include total saponins (< 0.05 mg/kg) and lectins (< 100 mg/kg) in the specifications. However, considering that these compounds are not of safety concern at the reported amounts and, more generally, for tree nuts (see Section 3.8), the Panel considers that their inclusion in the specifications is not necessary.

Regarding microbiological parameters, the Panel considers that the specifications should include a single parameter for ‘Total yeasts and moulds count’, with a maximum limit of 10 colony forming units (CFU)/g, and ‘Total aerobic microbial count’, with a maximum limit of 5000 CFU/g. For Escherichia coli, the Panel proposes expressing the limit as CFU, with a maximum value of 10 CFU/g.

The maximum levels for contaminants established for nuts and seeds under Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/915 should also apply to the TF.

The Panel considers that the information provided on the specifications of the TF is sufficient.

Experience of continued food use in a third country

As indicated in the EFSA Technical Report (EFSA, 2020), the applicant demonstrated that the TF has been consumed in Peru for over 25 years by submitting several publications describing the consumption of sacha inchi seeds, either roasted or cooked.

The Panel considers that the information provided confirms the continued use of the TF as part of the diet in Peru.

Proposed conditions of use in the EU market

The applicant intends to place on the EU market roasted sacha inchi seeds to be consumed as a whole food. The proposed target population is the general population.

According to the applicant, the TF is intended to serve as an alternative to seeds and nuts already consumed in the EU.

Nutritional information

In the initial TF notification (EFSA, 2020), the applicant provided compositional data for five TF batches. The proximate profile of the TF is comparable to the profile of other seeds such as peanuts and almonds. 7 , 8

Antinutritional substances, such as saponins and lectins, were analysed in the TF. The content of saponins in the TF, which is reduced by washing the seeds before roasting, was reported to be below 0.05 mg/kg. The content of lectins in the TF was reported to be below 100 mg/kg. Contents observed in the TF were also supported by scientific publications (EFSA, 2020).

Legumes (e.g. soybeans, chickpeas, kidney beans) are known to contain high amounts of saponins and lectins (Lagarda‐Diaz et al., 2017; Shi et al., 2004; Silva et al., 2023); also, grains (e.g. wheat and barley) are known to have high contents of lectins (van Buul & Brouns, 2014). The Panel notes that contents of saponins and lectins in tree nuts (e.g. almond, hazelnut, walnut, cashew, pecan) are generally low compared with those in legumes and grains, which have a long history of use in the EU diet.

Overall, based on the composition and the proposed conditions of use, the Panel considers that the consumption of the TF is not nutritionally disadvantageous.

DISCUSSION

The TF subject of this application consists of roasted seeds from Plukenetia volubilis L. (commonly known as sacha inchi). Roasted sacha inchi seeds have been consumed in Peru for more than 25 years. The applicant intends to place on the EU market roasted sacha inchi seeds to be consumed as a whole food.

This TF had previously been subject to a TF notification under the provisions of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283. During the assessment of that notification, EFSA identified safety concerns related to uncertainties regarding the presence of alkaloids in the TF. In the current application, the applicant provided additional data addressing the concern on alkaloids. The new analyses identified trigonelline as the only quantifiable alkaloid present in the TF, a finding that is also supported by data available in the literature. Dietary exposure to trigonelline, for example, through coffee consumption at amounts higher than those determined in the TF, has not been associated with adverse effects.

The Panel considers that the newly submitted evidence sufficiently characterises the alkaloid fraction of the TF, addresses the previously identified uncertainties and resolves the safety objections raised during the earlier assessment of the TF notification. Furthermore, based on the composition and the proposed conditions of use, the Panel considers that the consumption of the TF is not nutritionally disadvantageous.

CONCLUSION

The Panel concludes that the TF, roasted seeds from Plukenetia volubilis L., are safe under the proposed conditions of use.
