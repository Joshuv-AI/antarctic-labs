Detection of PPMV-1 antibodies in pigeons using a blocking ELISA based on an NP-specific monoclonal antibody
Authors: Zhi Zhao, Jianing Huang, Xiaoge Duan, Shuling Meng, Hailan Chen, Changting Li, Jiakang He, Tianchao Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPigeon paramyxovirus type 1 (PPMV-1) is a prevalent infectious agent causing Newcastle disease in pigeons, yet serological methods for detecting PPMV-1-specific antibodies remain limited. In this study, we developed a blocking enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) based on a monoclonal antibody (mAb) targeting the nucleocapsid (NP) protein of PPMV-1. The B-cell epitopes of the NP protein were computationally predicted, and the core antigenic domain was expressed in Escherichia coli and purified to generate specific mAbs. Following hybridoma technology, a high-affinity mAb (6B8) was produced and used to establish the blocking ELISA. Assay conditions were systematically optimized using checkerboard titration, including antigen concentration, serum dilution, incubation time, blocking buffer, and blocking duration. Analysis of known pigeon serum samples established a cut-off value of 26.87% inhibition (PI%), with an analytical sensitivity of 1:400, representing the highest dilution at which all tested positive sera remained detectable. The assay showed no cross-reactivity with other common avian pathogens, except for limited cross-reaction with Newcastle disease virus La Sota strain. Intra- and inter-batch coefficients of variation were all below 10%. Clinical validation using 86 pigeon serum samples demonstrated a concordance rate of 97.67% (84/86) compared with the hemagglutination inhibition (HI) test. In conclusion, this monoclonal antibody-based blocking ELISA provides a sensitive, specific, and reliable tool for large-scale serological surveillance of PPMV-1 in pigeons.Keywords: PPMV-1, Blocking ELISA, Monoclonal antibody, NP protein, Pigeon

Introduction

Pigeon paramyxovirus type 1 (PPMV-1), a variant of Newcastle disease virus (NDV) with a strong host preference for pigeons, is a primary causative agent of infectious diseases in pigeons (Kuiken et al., 2018; Zhang, et al., 2022). Infected pigeons typically exhibit severe clinical signs involving the central nervous, digestive, and respiratory systems (Abd El-Ghany, 2023). Outbreaks are particularly common in young and unvaccinated pigeons, often characterized by high mortality (Teske, et al., 2013). Since its first report in the early 1980s, PPMV-1 has continued to cause periodic outbreaks worldwide (Biancifiori and Fioroni, 1983; Martiny, et al., 2025; Rogers, et al., 2021; Zhan, et al., 2022). The frequent movement of breeding pigeons and racing pigeons further facilitates viral transmission, resulting in substantial economic losses due to both disease and culling (Hamouda, et al., 2024). Although vaccination remains the primary strategy for epidemic control, the widely used commercial La Sota vaccine provides limited cross-protection against PPMV-1, contributing to vaccine failures in the field (Zhang, et al., 2025).

Effective surveillance of PPMV-1 infection requires reliable detection methods. Several molecular assays, including real-time PCR, loop-mediated isothermal amplification assay (LAMP), and lateral flow immunochromatography assay (LFIA), have been developed for direct detection of PPMV-1 (Ciftci, et al., 2019; Liang, et al., 2021; Nevel, 2022). However, serological assays that detect PPMV-1-specific antibodies remain essential for accurate assessment of infection status and herd immunity, particularly in the context of vaccination campaigns (Wang, et al., 2022). Among serological techniques, enzyme-linked immunosorbent (ELISA) is widely used for large-scale antibody monitoring. In particular, blocking ELISA offers distinct advantages. For example, it eliminates the need for specific secondary antibodies, thereby overcoming species barriers because and improving detection specificity (Wang, et al., 2025). The performance of a blocking ELISA heavily depends on both the quality of the coated antigen and the availability of a high-affinity monoclonal antibody (mAb) that specifically recognizes the target antigen. Therefore, identification of an immunodominant region of the NP protein and generation of a specific mAb against this region are essential prerequisites for establishing a robust blocking ELISA.

The PPMV-1 genome encodes six structural proteins: nucleoprotein (NP), phosphoprotein (P), matrix (M), fusion (F), hemagglutinin-neuraminidase (HN), and large polymerase (L), as well as two non-structural proteins (V and W) (Akhtar, et al., 2016). Most reported antibodies against PPMV-1 have targeted the two surface glycoproteins, HN and F (Heiden, et al., 2014), which are major targets of virus-neutralizing antibodies and play essential roles in viral attachment and membrane fusion (Zhang, et al., 2025). In contrast, the NP protein is a highly abundant internal structural protein that is relatively more conserved than HN and F proteins and strongly immunogenic, making it a promising target for serological assay development (Mebatsion, et al., 2002; Silva, et al., 2014).

In this study, we identified the conserved and immunodominant region of the PPMV-1 NP protein through computer-aided epitope prediction. The recombinant NP protein was expressed and purified, and then used both for immunizing mice to generate mAbs and as the coating antigen for ELISA. Using hybridoma technology, we obtained a high-affinity mAb specifically recognizing the NP protein. Based on this mAb, we developed a novel blocking ELISA for sensitive and specific detection of PPMV-1 antibodies in pigeon sera. This work provides a robust technological platform for clinical diagnosis and large-scale epidemiological surveillance of PPMV-1 infection in pigeons.

Material and methods

Animals, viruses, cells, plasmids, and reagents

Female BALB/c mice (6-8 weeks) were purchased from SPF (Beijing) biotechnology co., Ltd. (Beijing, China, Permission No. SCXK (Jing) 2024-0001). The PPMV-1 GX5G strain were isolated and preserved in the laboratory of the Veterinary Poultry Disease Prevention Team, College of Animal Science and Technology, Guangxi University. Escherichia coli (E. coli) BL21 (DE3) competent cells and the pET-28a expression vector were maintained at the Guangxi Academy of Sciences. The mouse SP2/0 myeloma cell line was donated by Professor Zhanhui Wang in China Agricultural University (Beijing, China). Freund’s adjuvant, Hypoxanthine-Aminopterin-Thymidine (HAT) and Hypoxanthine-Thymidine (HT) media supplement were procured from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). The HRP-labeling kit was purchased from Biodragon (Beijing, China).

Prediction of B-cell epitopes of the NP protein

The NP protein sequence of PPMV-1 GX5G strain was obtained from the Veterinary Poultry Disease Prevention Team, College of Animal Science and Technology, Guangxi University. Physicochemical properties of the NP protein were analyzed using Expasy ProtParam online tool (https://web.expasy.org/protparam/), and protein domains were predicted using the SMART online tool (http://smart.embl-heidelberg.de/). Transmembrane regions and signal peptides were analyzed using TMHMM (http://www.cbs.dtu.dk/services/TMHMM/) and SignalP (www.cbs.dtu.dk/services/SignalP/), respectively. Linear B-cell epitopes of the NP protein were predicted using BepiPred 2.0 (http://www.iedb.org/) with a threshold of 0.5. A 3D structure of the NP protein was predicted via a homology modeling method using Discovery Studio Software (San Diego, CA, USA), and potential B-cell epitopes were visualized using PyMOL (version 2.5.0). Sequence alignments were performed using MEGA 12 and visualized by ESPript (https://espript.ibcp.fr/ESPript/cgi-bin/ESPript.cgi).

Expression and purification of recombinant truncated NP protein

Recombinant truncated NP proteins were expressed using a prokaryotic expression system. Briefly, viral RNA was extracted from PPMV-1 using a Virus DNA/RNA Extraction Kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) and reverse-transcribed into cDNA using a HiScript® III 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). Specific primers targeting the NP gene were designed as follows: forward 5′-GAATACGAGCAGCTCCTCGC-3′ and reverse 5′-GTCCTGAGATGG CCCATGAGT-3′. The partial NP gene fragment was amplified by PCR using 2 × Taq Master Mix (GenStar, Beijing, China), purified by gel extraction, and cloned into the pET-28a vector linearized through Nde I and Xho I. The recombinant plasmid was transformed into E. coli BL21 (DE3), and positive clones were confirmed by colony PCR. Recombinant NP protein expression was induced by adding 1 mM isopropyl-β-d-1-thiogalactoside (IPTG) for 12 h at 16 °C. After sonication of the bacterial culture, the lysate was cleared by centrifugation, and the recombinant protein was purified by Ni-affinity chromatography (Smart-Lifesciences, Changzhou, China). The purified recombinant NP protein was verified by SDS-PAGE.

Production and characterization of monoclonal antibody against recombinant NP protein

Four 6-8-week-old female BALB/c mice were immunized subcutaneously at multiple sites with 0.2 mL of purified recombinant truncated NP protein (1 mg/mL) emulsified 1:1 (v/v) with Freund’s complete adjuvant. Booster immunizations were performed on days 21 and 35 using the same dose of truncated NP protein emulsified with Freund's incomplete adjuvant. Antisera were collected 7 days after the third immunization, and titers were assessed by indirect ELISA. The antiserum titer was defined as the dilution yielding a positive-to-negative (P/N) ratio greater than 2.1.

Monoclonal antibodies were generated using hybridoma technology. The mouse with the highest-titer received an intraperitoneal booster injection of 0.3 mL of truncated NP protein (1 mg/mL) without adjuvant. Three days after the booster, splenocytes were harvested and fused with SP2/0 myeloma cells at a ratio of 10:1 using polyethylene glycol (PEG) 1500 (Sigma, MO, USA) for 60 s at 37 °C. Fused cells were cultured in selective HAT medium for 7 days. Hybridoma supernatants were screened by indirect ELISA to identify clones secreting NP-specific mAbs. Positive hybridoma cells from a single source were subcloned using HT medium. After multiple rounds of subcloning, positive hybridoma clones were injected intraperitoneally into pristine mice at a density of 5 × 105 cells/mL (0.5 mL per mouse). Ascitic fluid was collected approximately 10 days later and stored at −20 °C. Ascites antibody titers were determined by icELISA using serial dilutions. The antibody titer was defined as the highest dilution yielding a positive reaction, as determined by a P/N value greater than 2.1.

Characterization of monoclonal antibodies

The protein concentration of mAb 6B8 and 10D5 were determined using a BCA Protein Assay Kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Antibody subtypes were identified using an antibody subtype identification kit (Biodragon, Chengdu, China). All operations were performed according to the manufacturer's instructions. To evaluate the antigen specificity of mAbs, extracts of NDV La Sota, avian influenza virus (AIV), chicken reovirus (ChRV), Salmonella enteritidis (S. enteritidis), and E. coli O157 were coated onto ELISA plates and tested with mAb 6B8 and 10D5 by indirect ELISA. The cross-reactivity (CR) value was calculated as CR = [OD450 (other pathogen)/OD450 (PPMV-1)] × 100%.

An indirect immunofluorescence assay (IFA) was also performed to validate mAbs 6B8 and 10D5 against PPMV-1, following previously described protocols with minor modifications (Wang, et al., 2016). Briefly, BHK-21 cells were infected with the PPMV-1 at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 1 and cultured at 37 °C with 5% CO2 for 36 h. Cells were washed with PBS and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min, followed by permeabilization with 0.5% Triton X-100 for 60 min at room temperature. The mAbs were then applied and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. After three washes with PBS, cells were incubated with Alexa Fluor 594-labeled goat anti-mouse IgG (Jackson ImmunoResearch, Pennsylvania, USA) at 37 °C for 30 min. Following three additional washes, cells were observed and imaged using a fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan). Antiserum was used as a positive control, and DMEM was used as a negative control.

Development and optimization of the blocking ELISA

The purified mAb 6B8 was labeled with horseradish peroxidase (HRP) to establish a blocking ELISA for the detection of PPMV-1 antibodies. The HRP-mAb-6B8 conjugate was characterized using a UV-Visible spectrophotometer (Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan) in the 200-300 nm range. For stability evaluation, the HRP-conjugated mAb 6B8 was stored at 4 °C or −20 °C and tested after 0, 7, and 14 d using representative positive and negative pigeon serum samples. For −20 °C storage, the conjugated was maintained in buffer containing 50% glycerol to prevent freezing. The blocking ELISA was developed as previously described (Xiao, et al., 2025). Briefly, purified NP protein was diluted in carbonate buffer (pH 9.6) and coated onto 96-well ELISA plate at various concentrations (100 μL/well) overnight at 4 °C. Plate was blocked with blocking buffer (150 μL/well) for different durations at 37 °C and then washed three times with PBST. Subsequently, diluted serum samples (100 μL/well) was added and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. After three washes with PBST, HRP-mAb-6B8 (100 μL/well) was added and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. Following another wash step, the color was developed by introducing 100 μL of TMB substrate for 15 min at 37 °C. The reaction was stopped by adding 50 μL of 2 M sulfuric acid, and the absorbance at 450 nm (OD450) was measured using a microplate reader.

To optimize assay sensitivity, checkerboard titration was performed to determine the optimal concentration of NP protein and HRP-conjugated mAb 6B8. Determination of Cut-off value, diagnostic specificity, and sensitivity

To determine the cut-off value, 50 negative serum samples were tested using the developed blocking ELISA. The cut-off value (X) was defined as the mean PI% of the negative sera. Samples were interpreted as follows: negative when PI% ≤ X + 2SD, borderline when X + 2SD < PI% ≤ X + 3SD, and positive when PI% > X + 3SD.

To evaluate specificity, seven serum samples against AIV H5, AIV H9, fowl adenovirus (FAdV), infectious bronchitis virus (IBV), avian reovirus (ARV), avian metapneumovirus (aMPV) and NDV La Sota were tested by blocking ELISA. OD450 and PI% values were recorded from three independent replications.

Sensitivity was assessed using three serum samples (strong-positive, middle-positive and weak-positive) at serial dilutions ranging from 1:25 to 1:1000.

Evaluation with clinical samples

A total of 86 pigeon serum samples were analyzed using both the established blocking ELISA and the hemagglutination inhibition (HI) tests, which was performed according to standard protocols (Sano and Ogawa, 2014). The agreement rate between the two methods was calculated as follows: Agreement rates = (number of true-positive samples + number of true-negative samples) / total number of samples × 100%. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 8 (San Diego, CA, USA). In addition, a ROC-based sample size analysis was performed using MedCalc (MedCalc Software Ltd., Ostend, Belgium) to assess the adequacy of the clinical validation sample size.

Results and discussion

Advantage domain analysis of the NP protein

Understanding the physicochemical properties of structural proteins is essential for antigen epitope design (Negahdaripour, et al., 2018). ProtParam analysis showed that the NP protein of PPMV-1 contains 529 amino acid residues, with a predicted molecular weight of 58 kDa and an isoelectric point of 7.99. The instability index, lipid solubility coefficient, and grand average of hydropathicity (GRAVY) calculated as 41.04, 77.85 and −0.372, respectively. The negative GRAVY value indicates that the NP protein is generally hydrophilic (Kyte and Doolittle, 1982), a property favorable for antigen preparation. SMART analysis identified a Pfam-annotated domain spanning amino acids 54-473, suggesting a critical role in PPMV-1 genome packaging and ribonucleoprotein complex formation (Fig. 1A).

Overexpression of transmembrane proteins or signal peptides often leads to inclusion body formation (Satheeshkumar, et al., 2018). In our analysis, no signal peptide or transmembrane helices were predicted using SignalP and TMHMM (Fig. S1 and Fig. S2), respectively, suggesting that the NP protein is likely to be expressed in a soluble form, and advantage for recombinant protein production.

Identification of epitope peptides is a critical step for high affinity antibody production (Wang, et al., 2022). As shown in Fig. 1B, three potential linear B-cell epitopes were predicted at amino acid regions 40-60, 140-200 and 440-520. Three-dimensional structural analysis further demonstrated that all three potential epitopes are located on the surface of the NP protein (Fig. 1C), indicating their potential accessibility to antibodies. Based on these predictions, the region spanning amino acids 7-482 of the NP protein was selected as the antigen to maximize the inclusion of immunodominant epitopes.

The selection of the NP protein as the target antigen was strategically driven by its high conservation among PPMV-1 isolates and strong immunogenicity. Unlike the surface glycoproteins HN and F, which exhibit genotype-related variability (Zhang, et al., 2025), the NP protein is highly conserved across different NDV and PPMV-1 strains (Akhtar, et al., 2016). This conservation makes NP an ideal target for serological assays that require broad reactivity across circulating strains. Moreover, the predicted absence of transmembrane domains and signal peptides facilitated soluble expression of the recombinant protein in E. coli, avoiding the formation of inclusion bodies that often complicate antigen preparation (Satheeshkumar, et al., 2018). The identification of three surface-exposed B-cell epitopes (aa 40–60, 140–200, and 440–520) further validated our design, as surface accessibility is a key determinant of epitope immunogenicity (Negahdaripour, et al., 2018). This further supports the selection of the truncated NP region (aa 7-482), which includes the majority of immunodominant epitopes and improves expression efficiency compared with the full-length protein, although it may not cover all potential epitopes present in the full-length NP protein.

Expression and purification of recombinant NP protein

The NP gene fragment was successfully amplified and cloned into the pET-28a vector. Colony PCR confirmed the presence of the insert, yielding a fragment of the expected size (1,428 bp) (Fig. 1D). SDS-PAGE analysis showed that the recombinant NP protein was predominantly expressed in the soluble fraction of bacterial lysates, with an apparent molecular weight of approximately 53 kDa (Fig. 1E), which is consistent with previous reports on soluble expression of NDV NP protein in E. coli (Nath, et al., 2020). After purification by Ni-affinity chromatography, the recombinant NP protein was obtained with high purity, although a small number of contaminating proteins remained visible in the elution fraction (Fig. 1E, lane 6). The purified protein was deemed suitable for subsequent immunization and ELISA coating.

Production of monoclonal antibodies against the NP protein

To generate NP-specific mAbs, BALB/c mice were immunized with the truncated NP protein (Fig. 2A), and antiserum titers were determined by indirect ELISA. After the third immunization, all mice developed a detectable antibody response against the NP protein. Among them, antisera from mouse No.1 and No.2 showed titers exceeding 1:256000, with mouse No.1 exhibiting the highest OD450 values (Fig. 2B and C). These results indicate that the truncated NP protein is highly immunogenic, and mouse No.1 was selected for cell fusion.

After three rounds of subcloning, two hybridoma cell lines (6B8 and 10D5) were established, and ascitic fluid was prepared. The mAbs 6B8 and 10D5 exhibited antibody titers of 1:320000 and 1:20000, respectively (Fig. 2D and E). These titers are comparable to or higher than those reported in previous studies on NDV mAbs (Moharam, et al., 2021), confirming the effectiveness of the computationally guided antigen design strategy for generating high-affinity mAbs.

Characterization of monoclonal antibody

The antibody concentrations of mAbs 6B8 and 10D5 were determined to be 36 mg/mL and 24 mg/mL, respectively, using the BCA Protein Assay kit. Both purified mAbs were classified as the IgM/κ subtype (Fig. 3A and B). Specificity analysis revealed that mAbs 6B8 and 10D5 reacted strongly with the homologous PPMV-1 antigen and showed moderate cross-reactivity with the NDV La Sota strain, whereas no obvious reactivity was observed with AIV, ChRV, S. enteritidis, and E. coli O157.(Fig. 3C and D). The observed cross-reactivity is likely attributable to the sequence conservation of the NP protein between PPMV-1 and NDV. Sequence alignment analysis revealed that the NP proteins of PPMV-1 GX5G and NDV La Sota shared 88.9% amino acid identity (Fig. S3). Nevertheless, the lower reactivity toward La Sota compared with the homologous PPMV-1 antigen suggests that antigenic differences remain between the two viruses. This finding is consistent with previous reports indicating incomplete cross-protection between La Sota and PPMV-1 (He, et al., 2020; Zhang, et al., 2025), further highlighting the need for PPMV-1-specific diagnostic reagents.

The reactivity of mAbs against PPMV-1 was further evaluated by IFA. Both 6B8 and 10D5 produced strong fluorescence in PPMV-1-infected BHK-21 cells (Fig. 3E), demonstrating that both mAbs recognize conformational B-cell epitopes of PPMV-1 expressed in transfected cells. Based on its superior performance (higher titer and stronger signal), mAb 6B8 was selected for subsequent blocking ELISA development.

It is noteworthy that both mAbs 6B8 and 10D5 were classified as the IgM/κ subtype. While IgG mAbs are more commonly used in ELISA, IgM antibodies possess high avidity due to their pentameric structure, which may enhance binding to multivalent antigens such as viral nucleocapsids (Buchner, et al., 2025). In our system, the high avidity of mAb 6B8 likely contributed to the robust competition with serum antibodies, as reflected by the low cut-off value and high analytical sensitivity.

Optimization of blocking ELISA reaction conditions

The blocking ELISA was developed as schematically illustrated in Fig. 4A. Successful conjugation of HRP to mAb 6B8 was confirmed by UV-visible spectrophotometry. Compared with the individual components, the HRP-mAb 6B8 conjugate showed distinct absorption characteristics with peaks at approximately 250 nm and 280 nm (Fig. 4B). To evaluate the stability of the HRP-conjugated mAb 6B8, the conjugate was stored at 4 °C and −20 °C for 0, 7, and 14 d and tested using representative positive and negative pigeon serum samples. No obvious changes in OD450 values were observed under either storage condition throughout the study period, indicating good short-term stability of the conjugate (Fig. S4). As the conjugate was stored with 50% glycerol at –20°C, freeze-thaw cycles did not occur during the storage period.

Optimal assay parameters are critical for establishing a robust blocking ELISA (Fontana, et al., 2020). Using checkerboard titration, the optimal coating concentration of NP protein was determined to be 1 μg/mL (Fig. 4C). The optimal serum dilution was 1:25, with an incubation time of 60 min at 37 °C (Fig. 4D). The HRP-conjugated mAb 6B8 was optimally used at a 1:8000 dilution with a 30 min incubation at 37°C (Fig. 4E). For blocking conditions, 5% BSA with a 60-min incubation yielded the best performance (Fig. 4F and G).

Determination of the cut-off value, specificity, sensitivity and reproducibility

Cut-off value. Following previously established protocols for blocking ELISA development (Liu, et al., 2014; Wang, et al., 2022), the cut-off value was determined using 50 negative pigeon serum samples. As shown in Fig. 5A, the mean PI% value was 17.35% with a standard deviation (SD) of 4.76%. Accordingly, the cut-off value was set at 26.87% (mean + 2SD). Samples with PI% ≤ 26.87% were considered negative, those with 26.87% < PI% ≤ 31.63% (mean + 3SD) were considered doubtful, and those with PI > 31.63% were considered positive.

Analytical performance of the blocking ELISA for detection of PPMV-1 antibodies. (A) Determination of the cutoff value based on 50 negative pigeon serum samples. (B) Specificity evaluation against other avian pathogens. (C) Sensitivity analysis using serially diluted positive serum samples. Specificity. To assess analytical specificity, the blocking ELISA was tested against seven serum samples specific for other avian pathogens. As shown in Fig. 5B, no cross-reactivity was observed with AIV H5, AIV H9, FAdV, IBV, ARV, or aMPV antisera. A moderate level of cross-reactivity was detected with NDV La Sota antiserum. The results indicate that the developed blocking ELISA is highly specific for PPMV-1 antibody detection. The observed cross-reactivity with NDV La Sota is not expected to compromise the performance of the assay in pigeon samples, as PPMV-1 remains the predominant avian paramyxovirus circulating in pigeons.

Sensitivity. Analytical sensitivity was evaluated using three serum samples representing strong-positive, middle-positive and weak-positive reactivity, each tested at serial dilutions. As shown in Fig. 5C, the strong-positive sample remained detectable at a dilution of 1:800, while the middle- and weak-positive samples were detectable at 1:400. This corresponds to an analytical sensitivity of 1:800, 1:400, and 1:400 for strong-, middle-, and weak-positive sera, respectively, which compares favorably with a previously reported NDV indirect ELISA based on recombinant HN antigen, which showed a sensitivity of 1:400 (Mohan, et al., 2006). These results demonstrate that the developed blocking ELISA exhibits favorable analytical sensitivity.

Reproducibility. To evaluate assay reproducibility, three PPMV-1 positive samples (strong-, middle- and weak-positive) and one negative sample were tested in triplicate in both intra-assay and inter-assay experiments. As summarized in Table 1, the coefficients of variation (CVs) for intra-assay tests ranged from 0.38% to 6.51%, and those for inter-assay tests ranged from 2.29% to 7.07%. These low CV values confirm that the blocking ELISA has excellent reproducibility.

Clinical application

The clinical performance of the blocking ELISA was evaluated using 86 pigeon serum samples, with the hemagglutination inhibition (HI) test serving as the reference method. The blocking ELISA results were interpreted using the established cut-off value of 26.87%. As shown in Table 2 and Table S1, the blocking ELISA identified 15 positive and 71 negative samples, whereas the HI test identified 17 positive and 69 negative samples. The overall agreement rate between the two methods was 97.67% (84/86). No clinical sample was classified as doubtful according to the established interpretation criteria.

The two discordant samples (HI-positive but blocking ELISA-negative) may be attributed to differences in the antibody specificities detected by the two assays: the HI assay primarily measures hemagglutination-inhibiting antibodies directed against the viral surface glycoprotein HN, whereas the developed blocking ELISA detects NP-specific antibodies through competitive inhibition. This distinction in the antigen targets likely accounts for the observed discordance. Nevertheless, the overall agreement rate of 97.67% demonstrated a high level of concordance between the developed blocking ELISA and the HI assay, supporting its applicability for serological surveillance of PPMV-1 infection. To further evaluate the diagnostic performance of the blocking ELISA, ROC curve analysis was performed using the 86 clinical serum samples and the HI assay as the reference method. The analysis yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.9954 (95% CI: 0.9887-1.000; P < 0.0001), demonstrating excellent diagnostic accuracy (Fig. S5). A ROC-based sample size analysis indicated that the sample size used in this study was sufficient for reliable evaluation of diagnostic performance. The optimal threshold identified by the Youden index was 19.35% (Youden index = 0.9855). This value was lower than the statistically derived cut-off value of 26.87% (mean + 2SD), reflecting the different statistical approaches used for threshold determination. The ROC-derived threshold was determined by maximizing the combined diagnostic sensitivity and specificity, whereas the cut-off value of 26.87% was established independently based on the distribution of negative reference sera. Using this predefined cut-off, the blocking ELISA achieved an overall agreement of 97.67% with the HI assay in the clinical validation panel.

Limitations and future perspectives

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the sample size for clinical validation (n = 86) was relatively modest, and the study was conducted in a single geographic region (Guangxi, China). Future studies with larger sample sizes from multiple regions are needed to further validate the assay's robustness. Second, the mAb 6B8 is of the IgM subtype, which may pose challenges for large-scale commercial production due to potential batch-to-batch variability. Generation of an IgG-subtype mAb recognizing the same epitope could be pursued for improved consistency. Third, further evaluation using antisera against additional pigeon pathogens, other avian paramyxovirus serotypes, and virulent NDV genotypes would be valuable to fully assess the specificity of the assay.

Despite these limitations, the developed blocking ELISA represents a significant advance for PPMV-1 serology. Future work should focus on validating the assay with a larger panel of field samples from different geographical regions, exploring the use of an IgG mAb for improved commercial scalability, and adapting the assay for other sample types.

Conclusion

In summary, we successfully generated a high-affinity monoclonal antibody (mAb 6B8) specifically recognizing the nucleocapsid (NP) protein of PPMV-1 using hybridoma technology combined with computational epitope prediction. Based on this mAb, we developed a novel blocking ELISA for sensitive and specific detection of PPMV-1 antibodies in pigeon sera. The assay demonstrated a cut-off value of 26.87%, an analytical sensitivity of 1:800, and excellent reproducibility (intra- and inter-batch CVs all below 10%). Clinical validation with 86 pigeon serum samples showed a 97.67% agreement rate with the hemagglutination inhibition test. Taken together, the established NP-specific monoclonal antibody-based blocking ELISA provides a reliable, rapid, and high-throughput serological tool for large-scale epidemiological surveillance of PPMV-1 infection and evaluation of vaccine efficacy in pigeons.
