Note a . Draft Presidential Determination Letters
Decision

The members will review the draft Determination letters to the President
and attempt to clear them in their agencies for the concurring signatures.

The Treasury member will inform the Chairman as to who in his agency will
sign these letters.

Pis cuss ion

Draft letters to the President regarding Control Act Determinations were
given to the members with the request that they attempt to clear them with their
agencies and to determine a time when signatures could be obtained. (Requested
for Friday, December 20th.)

The letters were discussed and some minor revisions agreed upon.

The Treasury member said he had a problem in that the individuals who
formerly signed these letters had left the Department and no one else at their
level was familiar enough with this program to sign the letters without a full
briefing.

Note b. Rolling Mill Discussions
Decision

The Chairman will notify the Chairman of Working Group I that that Group
is expected to review promptly the comment,s and proposals of the technicians who
have now been selected to represent the U.S. in the technical review of rolling
mills, taking into consideration any recommendation and language for limits to
which the U.S. could agree in changing present coverage of the definition. If
any agency represented on WG-I has any objection to the WG-I position the issue
would be presented to EDAC for resolution.

Any proposal to C0C0M which comes out of the technical discussions on
rolling mills will be reviewed and approved by EDAC before approval by the U.S.
in C0C0M.

Discussion

The Defense member wondered if a request could be made to postpone the
proposed discussion of rolling mills until after the Consultative Group meeting.
In the subsequent discussion of the entire rolling mill problem, it was brought
out that there were differences of view among the Executive Committee members
on exactly what had been decided with respect to U.S. participation in the tech-
nical discussions.

The members were in agreement that the State proposal to inform all of the
Embassies in Washington bilaterally of the intention of the U.S. to call a meeting
of the CG was desirable. They further felt that once the U.S. proposes a CG we
would be at liberty to hold bilaterals or trilaterals with anyone.

There was then a discussion of the work the Executive Committee must do in
preparation for a CG meeting. It was felt that at the next EC meeting Mr. George’
draft of September 2b, 1957 entitled "Exceptions to Security Controls - Principles
and Procedures" should be reviewed. This paper covers prior commitments, minimum
shipments, etc. and the Chairman felt that each of those aspects which has not
already been discussed should be considered and a judgment made as to whether they
are recommendations the EC should make for a US position andj if so, whether they
should be made part of the package," "or whether they should be given to the negoti-
ators as matters to be handled by them when the time comes .

With respect to the reports from the Ad Hoc Subcommittee on List Review, it
was felt that they should not be reviewed item by item because this Committee
does not have the competence for such a review, but members should ask the Ad Hoc
Committee to supply for EC consideration, the justification not now documented for
any challenged items or proposals and make alternative recommendations on any
elements of the proposal which they consider undesirable or inappropriate. After
discussion of the most expeditious way to accomplish this review, the members
accepted the Chairman’s proposal as shown in Decision above.
