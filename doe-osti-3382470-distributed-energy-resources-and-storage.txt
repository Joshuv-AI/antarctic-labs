STUDY CONCLUSIONS
• Substation backfeeding was the limiting factor in all scenarios.
 ▪ Other violations were observed to occur after backfeeding.
• Inverter controls and storage can improve hosting capacity by managing system conditions.
 ▪ Volt/VAR can greatly increase reactive power demand.
 ▪ Local storage can alleviate many issues by dealing with DER generation “at the source.”
• Different hosting capacity results were reported using identical control configurations.
• This points to a need for specific hosting capacity scenario definitions to prevent misreporting.

 Deer Isle and Stonington
The towns of Deer Isle and Stonington share the island of Deer Isle, located off coast of Maine.

Per their Energy Technology Innovation Partnership Project (ETIPP) application, the towns seek to ensure the long-term viability
of their communities by:
 • Increasing the resilience of the island’s fragile distribution system
 • Gaining some measure of independence from and mitigation for electricity outages
 • Shielding residents from price spikes
 • Reducing the island’s dependence on fossil fuels.

Project workstreams included the following:
 • Information and data gathering, focusing on the Deer Isle distribution grid, ongoing efforts to maintain and improve
 distribution grid reliability, electricity consumption, electricity costs, and the regulatory environment
 • Technical and economic modeling of behind-the-meter photovoltaic (PV) and PV + battery systems
 • Technical and economic modeling of a community-scale battery system
 • Assessment of potential energy generation technology options.

This presentation touches on aspects of the first three workstreams.

NOTE: Unless otherwise noted, the content in this presentation is from the project final report.

 Facility-Scale BESS Sizing
Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) Characterization
• A BESS is characterized by both power capacity and energy (storage) capacity.
• Consider a BESS with a power capacity of 20 kW and an energy capacity of 60 kWh. The capacity of this
  system would be described as 20 kW/60 kWh or 20 kW-3 hour.

Sizing of Power Capacity
• Approaches for determining the desired BESS power capacity include:
  o Size the power capacity to meet the facility peak load. This is most common for facilities with small
 peak loads such as residences, the Deer Isle Town Office, and the Stonington Connectivity Hub.
  o Size the power capacity to meet the critical loads. This is more common done for facilities with larger
 peak loads where sizing the power capacity to less than the peak load can result in significant cost
 savings.
• The Town Office peak load is about 9 kW. The Connectivity Hub peak load is unknown but is assumed to
  be similar. A reasonable BESS power capacity would be 12–15 kW.

                                                                                           National Laboratory of the Rockies | 22

 Facility-Scale BESS Sizing
Sizing of Energy Capacity
• For behind-the-meter applications, a useful way to begin thinking about BESS energy capacity is in terms
  of “hours of autonomy”—the length of time the BESS can serve the average load. The average loads of the
  Town Office and Connectivity Hub are 2.2 kW and 2.0 kW, respectively. Approximately 10 kWh of energy
  storage capacity will provide roughly 4 hours of autonomy for the Town Office and 5 hours of autonomy
  for the Connectivity Hub.
 o For low-consumption facilities such as the Town Office and Connectivity Hub, sizing the BESS energy
 storage to provide several hours to a day of autonomy is reasonable.
• In general, the initial increment of energy storage provides the most value, with additional increments of
  storage providing progressively less value (“decreasing marginal utility” in econ-speak).

 Community-Scale BESS
Based on the community’s estimated average and peak loads of 2,440 kW and 4,200 kW, respectively, the group examined
the feasibility of deploying a BESS with a capacity of roughly 4 MW/8 megawatt-hours (MWh). This would provide roughly 3
hours of autonomy.

 Community-Scale BESS
 Considerations
Space Requirements (Examples):
• One Tesla Megapack (3.9 megawatt-hours [MWh]) = one 30-ft shipping container; weight: 84,000 pounds. (Tesla 2026)
• One LG Energy M48218P5B (6.8 MWh) = one 40-ft shipping container; weight: 120,000 pounds. (LG Energy Solution 2021)

Siting: To provide maximum value and reliability, the BESS should be interconnected to the distribution “trunk line” running down
to Stonington and near the most important loads, most of which are located near the Stonington waterfront.

Distribution System Upgrades: Interconnection of a community BESS may require upgrades to the distribution system.

Dispatch Strategy: There is a slight trade-off between using the BESS for backup power and using it to manage system voltage and
provide other system benefits. In practice, it should be possible to obtain most of the benefits of both with tolerable risk.

Loads Served: Stonington waterfront vs. whole island vs. whole circuit.

Ownership Structure: From the perspective of the communities, the preferred ownership model is for Versant to own and operate
the BESS as part of its rate base.

STATUS: In part because of this analysis and the resulting discussions, Versant—with the support of the towns’ leadership—applied
for funding from a subsequently terminated DOE grant program for a community-scale BESS to be sited near the Stonington
waterfront.
