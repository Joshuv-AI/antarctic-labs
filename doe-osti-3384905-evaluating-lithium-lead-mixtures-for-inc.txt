 ABSTRACT

This project investigates hypo- and hyper-eutectic liquid lithium-lead (PbLi) mixtures as a coolant in
fusion blanket systems, with a focus on testing the compatibility of compositions with better tritium
breeding ratios (TBR). The 12-month study concentrates on static corrosion experiments, comparing
silicon carbide (SiC) corrosion rates in Li-rich and eutectic Li-Pb mixtures, and examining interactions
between Mo and PbLi. The research seeks to determine the maximum operating temperatures for this
material combination, acknowledging the balance between Li-Pb melting temperature and achievable
TBR to unlock new opportunities for blanket design. This work lays the groundwork for future
collaboration between Kyoto Fusioneering and Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL). Findings could
enhance the TBR of fusion blanket designs, and impact fusion energy development by confirming
material compatibility between different Li-Pb mixtures with SiC and Mo at higher temperatures,
including at temperatures relevant for very high-temperature blankets (1,000 °C), which could enable
higher electricity conversion efficiencies and commercial applications using process heat.

 1. STATEMENT OF OBJECTIVES

The primary objective of this research is to evaluate the compatibility of CVD SiC and Mo specimens
after exposure to various PbLi mixtures for 1,000 h at 600, 800 and 1,000 °C. The impact of capsule
material on compatibility will also be studied. Materials characterization after exposure includes mass
change, microstructural and compositional evaluation and phase identification for a selected number of
specimens.

 3. TECHNICAL DISCUSSION OF WORK PERFORMED BY ALL PARTIES

3.1 Introduction

Lithium-lead (Li-Pb) as emerged as a leading tritium breeder and coolant for future fusion reactors and
ITER DEMOnstration power plant (DEMO). Historically, research has centered on the eutectic
composition (83 at.% Pb and 17 at.% Li [1]), although the exact eutectic composition is subject to debate
[2], due to its attractive melting point and volumetric heat capacity which, with appropriate selection of
structural materials will allow fusion reactors to operate at elevated temperatures using high temperature
structural materials and potentially SiC [3, 4]. Increasing Pb content in PbLi mixtures has been shown to
affect corrosion behavior of Reduced Activation Ferritic Martensitic (RAFM) steels in PbLi mixtures [5].
Increased oxidation or nitridation of RAFM steel observed depending on Pb and Li ratios [5]. The
compatibility of CVD SiC and SiC/SiC specimens in eutectic PbLi has been evaluated in SiC capsules [6]
and crucibles [7] and results varied significantly in terms of mass change and thickness of observed
corrosion products. Futhermore, significant dissimilar material interactions between SiC and Be and SiC
and FeCrAl have also been shown [8, 9]. This report addresses the compatibility of CVD SiC and Mo
with various LiPb mixtures between 600 and 1,000 °C and nature of capsule material.

3.2.3 Characterization

 Following a 1,000-hour exposure period, the specimens were cleaned with a standard 1:1:1 mixture of
 acetic acid, ethanol, and hydrogen peroxide, and their pre- and post-exposure weights were recorded
 using a Mettler Toledo XP205 analytical balance featuring an accuracy of ±0.04 mg. Initial
 characterization of the polished specimen cross-sections was performed via light microscopy.
 Subsequent microstructural and elemental analyses were conducted using a ZEISS scanning electron
 microscope (SEM) to acquire secondary electron (SE) and backscattered electron (BSE) micrographs
 alongside energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) data. Surface chemical composition of the
 exposed SiC samples were obtained from X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). A Thermo
 Scientific Model K-Alpha XPS instrument calibrated to Au 4f7/2 at 84.00 eV was used. Core shell
 spectra of Si 2p, C 1s, O 1s, Pb 4f, and Li 1s were collected using Al X-ray beam (1486.6 eV) with a
 400 𝜇𝑚 X-ray spot size. Survey and high-resolution spectra were collected with a pass energy of 200
 eV and 50 eV, respectively. The data was analyzed by the CasaXPS software. The deconvolution of
 high-resolution core level spectra was performed by Gaussian peak shape and a Shirley type
 background. C 1s at 284.8 eV was used as the standard to correct for peak shifts due to surface
 charging.

3.3 Results

3.3.1 SiC in Mo capsules

Mass change

CVD SiC specimens showed decreasing mass losses with increasing Li content in hypereutectic PbLi
mixtures (Figure 2). Mass loss was the lowest in the eutectic PbLi mixture (Figure 2a) after 1,000 h at
600 °C. Similar trends were observed at 800 °C with decreasing mass loss with increasing Li content in
hyper-eutectic PbLi mixtures (Figure 2b). Mass losses were larger at 800 than 600 °C (Figure 2b). In
60Pb40Li mass gains were measured at 800 °C (Figure 1b) while mass losses were measured at 600 °C
(Figure 1a).

3.4 Conclusions and Future work

CVD SiC and TZM Mo were exposed in various PbLi mixtures (hypo- and hyper-eutectic and eutectic
mixtures) at 600, 800 and 1,000 °C using Mo and SiC capsules. Results showed that increasing Li content
in PbLi mixtures lead to decreased mass losses and in some cases change from mass losses to mass gains.
Similar trends were observed at 600 and 800 °C with increasing mass losses and mass gains with
increasing temperature. These observations confirm observations by Kondo et al where decreasing Pb to
PbLi mixtures changed corrosion mechanism of RAFM steel from oxidation to nitridation [5]. In the
present experiment increasing Li content in PbLi lead to increased oxidation behavior.
Experiment failed at 1,000 °C due to capsule leakage and Pb contamination of the box furnace that
prevented further examination of exposed specimens within the budget of the CRADA. Additional
capsule development need to be performed to be able to repeat results from past experiments [6]. Results
also identified varying compatibility results by varying capsule material. Transition from mass losses to
mass gains and PbLi-induced oxidation was observed for the CVD SiC coupons where SiC capsules were
used instead of Mo capsules. The formed oxide was severely cracked and porous at 800 °C and resulted in
subsurface cracking of CVD SiC at 600 and 800 °C. The decreased surface to volume ratio in the case of
SiC specimens in SiC capsule could explain the observed increased oxidation behavior as well as
presence of sintering agents.
