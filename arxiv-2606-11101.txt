Supersymmetry of the static Reissner–Nordström black hole in Bertotti–Robinson ( 𝐀𝐝𝐒 𝟐 × 𝕊 𝟐 \mathrm{AdS}_{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2} )
Andrea Di Pinto, and Adriano Viganò

Abstract
We examine supersymmetry of charged and accelerating black holes embedded in a Bertotti–Robinson universe, in the context of N = 2 \pazocal{N}=2 , D = 4 D=4 supergravity. After a review of the solution, we study the constraints that guarantee supersymmetry and explicitly compute the Killing spinors of the spacetime. We show that the supersymmetric solution saturates the BPS bound, and we use this result to compute the mass of the black hole and to analyze the thermodynamics of the solution. Finally, we present a generalization of the extremal solution to include the cosmological constant.

Introduction

Supergravity theories are recognized as the low-energy limit of string theories, and as such they represent a way to pave our understanding of M-theory and, more generally, quantum gravity.

Black holes are fundamental objects in our Universe, and play a prominent role in supergravity:

Among exact solutions in supergravity, a prominent role is played by bosonic backgrounds preserving supersymmetry, since they are stable and less susceptible to quantum corrections.
Supersymmetric solutions are characterized by the existence of Killing spinors, which highly constrain the geometry of the spacetime:

2.1
Horizons and causal structure

Before analyzing the supersymmetry properties of the spacetime (
2
), it is worth clarifying the nature of the event and acceleration horizons, depending on the ranges of the parameters.

The horizons are given by the roots of
\Delta_{r}
, which is a fourth-order polynomial in
r
.
Its discriminant is equal to
16m^{2}(\alpha^{2}-B^{2})\bigl[(1-B^{2}m^{2})^{2}-4m^{2}(\alpha^{2}-B^{2})\bigl]^{2}
, and thus its sign is controlled by the quantity
(\alpha^{2}-B^{2})
.
Assuming that
\alpha
,
B
and
m
are all positive, we can distinguish among three cases.

•

\alpha>B
:
the discriminant is positive or null, thus
\Delta_{r}
has four real roots, given by

r_{-}=r_{0}\,,\qquad r_{+}=r_{0}+\frac{2m}{1-B^{2}m^{2}}\,,\qquad r_{a\pm}=r_{0}\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-B^{2}}}\,,

(7)

which are all distinct when the discriminant is strictly positive, while

can coincide with either
r_{a+}
or
r_{a-}
when the discriminant vanishes, i.e. when
2m\alpha=1+B^{2}m^{2}
.
The ordering of the four roots is established by the ranges of the parameters:
more precisely, we notice that
1-B^{2}m^{2}<1
always, therefore

provided that
\alpha>B
.
Conversely, the relative positions of the horizons are determined by the specific values of the parameters, and thus different orderings are possible (however, it always holds that
r_{a-}<r_{-}<r_{a+}

–

r_{a-}<r_{-}<r_{+}<r_{a+}
:
when
Bm<1
and
2m\alpha<1+B^{2}m^{2}
;

–

r_{a-}<r_{-}<r_{+}=r_{a+}
:
when
Bm<1
and
2m\alpha=1+B^{2}m^{2}
;

–

r_{a-}<r_{-}<r_{a+}<r_{+}
:
when
Bm<1
and
2m\alpha>1+B^{2}m^{2}
;

–

r_{a-}<r_{+}<r_{-}<r_{a+}
:
when
Bm>1
and
2m\alpha<1+B^{2}m^{2}
;

–

r_{a-}=r_{+}<r_{-}<r_{a+}
:
when
Bm>1
and
2m\alpha=1+B^{2}m^{2}
;

–

r_{+}<r_{a-}<r_{-}<r_{a+}
:
when
Bm>1

2m\alpha>1+B^{2}m^{2}

Not all the horizons must be simultaneously present:

Moreover, depending on the relative positions, the horizons acquire different meanings:

Since we are mainly interested in the extremal limit (described below in section

•

\alpha=B
:
the discriminant is null, thus there are two pairs of equal real roots given by

r_{-}=r_{0}\,,\qquad r_{+}=\frac{r_{0}}{1-B^{2}m^{2}}\,.

(8)

Here

Since

•

\alpha<B

the discriminant is negative, thus there are two real roots (and two complex roots)

In this case no acceleration horizon is present, and

2.2
Bertotti–Robinson universe in accelerating coordinates

For our purposes in what follows, it is useful to consider the subcase

2.3
Extremal Reissner–Nordström black hole in Bertotti–Robinson

Another subcase of particular interest for us is the extremal subcase of the previous general solution.

4
Supersymmetry conditions and Killing spinors

In the bosonic sector of this theory,
\psi_{\mu}=0
, the field equations following from (
12

Hence, spacetimes that satisfy these field equations represent possible background solutions of gauged
\pazocal{N}=2
supergravity.
Furthermore, being
\psi_{\mu}=0

18

\hat{\nabla}_{\mu}\epsilon=0\,.

(21)

A spinor

21

The basic integrability conditions for (
21

\hat{R}_{\mu\nu}\,\epsilon=0\,,

(22)

where

\hat{R}_{\mu\nu}=\bigl[\hat{\nabla}_{\mu},\hat{\nabla}_{\nu}\bigr]\,,

(23)

is referred to as the supercurvature.
Moreover, the integrability conditions for Killing spinors (
22
) are also equivalent to

\det\bigl(\hat{R}_{\mu\nu}\bigr)=0\,.

(24)

It is important to note that the basic integrability conditions (
22

They ensure that a Killing spinor exists locally, but there may be topological reasons that prevent their global existence.
For this reason, one must either consider a sufficient set of higher integrability conditions

or return to the original differential equation (
21
).

g_{\mu\nu}\,\mathrm{J}^{\mu}\mathrm{J}^{\nu}=-\frac{\Bigl[\bigl(|c_{1}|^{2}+|c_{2}|^{2}\bigr)-\bigl(c_{1}^{*}c_{2}+c_{2}^{*}c_{1}\bigr)w\Bigr]\bigl(1-\frac{m}{r}\bigr)^{2}}{\bigr[1-2B\cos\theta(r-m)+B^{2}(r-m)^{2}\bigl]}\,.

(30)

5
BPS bound and Majumdar–Papapetrou spacetime

Provided that the spacetime preserves half of the supersymmetries, it is natural to wonder if the black hole saturates the BPS bound
Witten:1978mh
, i.e. if

\mathrm{M}^{2}\geq|\pazocal{Z}|^{2}\,,

(31)

is satisfied with the equality.

The central charge is given by
3
3

3

One can find the central charge for the Einstein–Maxwell theory from the general action for
\pazocal{N}=2
,
D=4
matter-coupled supergravity
Andrianopoli:1996cm
by choosing the prepotential
X^{0}=1/2
.

\pazocal{Z}=\mathrm{Q}+i\mathrm{P}
, and in particular

\mathrm{Q}=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma}F^{\mu\nu}dS_{\mu\nu}=mw\,,\qquad\mathrm{P}=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma}{}^{\star}F^{\mu\nu}dS_{\mu\nu}=m\sqrt{1-w^{2}}\,,

(32)

where
{}^{\star}F^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{-g}}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}
is the Hodge dual of the Faraday tensor
F^{\mu\nu}
.
Equation (
32

m=0
, i.e. in the absence of the black hole, the charges are zero as expected, since the background has no monopoles (consistent with the results of
Hu:2026slp
).

If the central charge can be easily found, it is not clear how to compute the total mass of the spacetime because of the asymptotics:
the spacetime is not asymptotically flat, so the ADM and Komar formalisms are not applicable;
the Komar integral, in particular, leads to a divergent result.
One might appeal to a surface charge computation, however even in that case it is not clear which form the mass generator at infinity should take, thus we lack of a direct calculation
Hu:2026slp
.
We can, however, make use of the Majumdar–Papapetrou form of the metric (
11
) to deduce the mass in the extremal case.

Indeed, we can introduce cylindrical coordinates for the extremal Reissner–Nordström-Bertotti–Robinson black hole (
11
)

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\theta\sqrt{\Delta_{r}\Delta_{\theta}}}{\Omega^{2}}=\frac{(r-m)\sin\theta}{\bigl[1+B^{2}(r-m)^{2}-2B(r-m)\cos\theta\bigl]}\,,

(33a)

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{r}}\,\cos\theta-B^{2}\Delta_{r}}{\Omega^{2}}=\frac{(r-m)\cos\theta-B^{2}(r-m)^{2}}{\bigl[1+B^{2}(r-m)^{2}-2B(r-m)\cos\theta\bigl]}\,,

(33b)

to find

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-U^{-2}{dt}^{2}+U^{2}\bigl({d\rho}^{2}+{dz}^{2}+\rho^{2}{d\varphi}^{2}\bigr)\,,

(34a)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=w\,U^{-1}dt+\sqrt{1-w^{2}}\,Vd\varphi\,,

(34b)

with functions

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}}+\frac{1}{B\sqrt{\rho^{2}+\bigl(z+\frac{1}{B}\bigr)^{2}}}\,,

(35a)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\frac{mz}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}}+\frac{z+\frac{1}{B}}{B\sqrt{\rho^{2}+\bigl(z+\frac{1}{B}\bigr)^{2}}}-\frac{1}{B}\,.

(35b)

The extremal solution thus belongs to the Majumdar–Papapetrou class
Majumdar:1947eu
;
Papaetrou:1947ib

To interpret this result, we can further introduce Cartesian coordinates
x=\rho\cos\varphi
,
y=\rho\sin\varphi
, and rewrite the solution in the more familiar form

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-U^{-2}{dt}^{2}+U^{2}\bigl({dx}^{2}+{dy}^{2}+{dz}^{2}\bigr)\,,

(36a)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=w\,U^{-1}dt+\sqrt{1-w^{2}}\frac{V}{x^{2}+y^{2}}(xdy-ydx)\,,

(36b)

where now

U=\frac{m}{|\vec{x}|}+\frac{1}{B|\vec{x}-\vec{x}_{B}|}\,,\qquad V=\frac{mz}{|\vec{x}|}+\frac{z-z_{B}}{B|\vec{x}-\vec{x}_{B}|}-\frac{1}{B}\,,

(37)

and we defined the vectors
\vec{x}=(x,y,z)
and
\vec{x}_{B}=(0,0,-1/B)
.
Our solution differs from the most known form of the Majumdar–Papapetrou solution, which indeed represents a collection of
N
charged extremal black holes, in the absence of the unit term in the definition of
U
.

From the last expression, we can interpret the extremally charged black hole embedded in the Bertotti–Robinson universe as a collection of two sources, one located at the origin of the axes and with mass
m
, and the other located at the point
\vec{x}_{B}
and with mass equal to the position along the
z-
axis
1/B
.
This picture is consistent with the Bertotti–Robinson spacetime as the near-horizon limit of the extremal Reissner–Nordström black hole.

The natural generalization of the solution (
36

N

U=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{h_{i}}{|\vec{x}-\vec{x}_{i}|}\,,

(38)

where
h_{i}
denote the sources and
\vec{x}_{i}
the positions of the sources.
Such a solution is known in the literature:
it represents the near-horizon limit of
N
Reissner–Nordström black holes
Maldacena:1998uz
.

The general form contains a collection of
N
generic sources, which can be chosen at will to represent a black hole of mass
m_{i}=h_{i}
or a Bertotti–Robinson field of strength
1/B_{i}=h_{i}
(i.e. the near-horizon limit of a charged hole) in various positions.
At first sight, the freedom to interpret a source as a black hole or as a magnetic field may be disorienting, however it fits into the equivalence principle:
indeed, once we have chosen the role of the sources, it is possible to perform a change of coordinates (a translation plus a rescaling), to convert a hole into a field, and vice versa.
Physically, a change of coordinates corresponds to a change in the observer, thus it makes perfect sense that by translating the position of the observer and rescaling her ruler, it is possible to zoom in or zoom out the hole, thus observing a “small” hole (the source
m_{i}
) or a “large” hole (the field
1/B_{i}
).

The form (
36

31
).
According to the theorem proved in
Gibbons:1982fy
, the only solution to the Einstein–Maxwell equations saturating the BPS bound (
31
) whose supercovariantly constant spinor
\epsilon
is time-independent and whose current vector
\mathrm{J}^{\mu}=\bar{\epsilon}\gamma^{\mu}\epsilon
is timelike, must belong to the Majumdar--Papapetrou metrics.
4
4

4

One can express our metric in the form given in
Gibbons:1982fy
by setting
U=W
and
w=\cos\theta
.

Then, the total mass of the spacetime is given by

\mathrm{M}=|\pazocal{Z}|=m\,,

(39)

which is the expected result.

It is worth noticing that the computation of the total mass cannot be pursued by means of a Komar integral, which gives a divergent result, nor by a phase space computation since, as stressed above and in
Hu:2026slp
, it is not clear how to choose the proper normalization for the timelike generator.
This makes the result (
39
) precious, being the only accessible way to compute the total mass of the (extremal) black hole, and shows the power of supersymmetry techniques in the investigation of black hole physics.

As a further comment on the Majumdar–Papapetrou metric (
34

\lim_{B\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{B\sqrt{\rho^{2}+\bigl(z+\frac{1}{B}\bigr)^{2}}}=1\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\lim_{B\rightarrow 0}U=1+\frac{m}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}}\,,

(40)

which is the single Reissner–Nordström black hole written in cylindrical form.
On the other hand, for

6
Thermodynamics of the extremal solution

We can evaluate the thermodynamic quantities in the extremal case
5
5

5

In a similar way, the general case is discussed in appendix
D
.
in order to check the validity of the Smarr formula
Smarr:1972kt
and the first law of black hole mechanics

, using the mass derived from the saturation of the BPS bound (

In particular, the horizon area
\mathrm{A}
and the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy
\mathrm{S}

;

are

\mathrm{A}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\int_{0}^{\pi}\sqrt{g_{\varphi\varphi}g_{\theta\theta}}\Bigg\rvert_{r=m}\!=4\pi m^{2}\,,\qquad\mathrm{S}=\frac{\mathrm{A}}{4}=\pi\mathrm{M}^{2}\,,

(41)

while the surface gravity of the horizon
\kappa

vanish, as expected since the black hole is extremal

\kappa=\sqrt{-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}\nabla^{\mu}\xi^{\nu}}\Bigg\rvert_{r=m}\!=0\,,\qquad\mathrm{T}=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}=0\,,

(42)

where

\Phi_{H}=-A_{\mu}\xi^{\mu}\Big\rvert_{r=m}\!=0\,,\qquad\qquad\Phi=\Phi_{H}+\Phi_{int}=\Phi_{int},

(43)

is a gauge constant, which can always be set to zero for an asymptotically flat spacetime. However, since the solution under consideration is not asymptotically flat, we leave it free and, if necessary, subsequently fix it in order to satisfy the thermodynamic laws.
Indeed, by setting

to eliminate the magnetic component of the electromagnetic field, it follows that both the Smarr law

\mathrm{M}=2\,\mathrm{T}\,\mathrm{S}+\Phi\,\mathrm{Q}\,,

Finally, restoring

\mathrm{M}^{2}=\frac{\mathrm{S}}{4\pi}+\frac{\mathrm{Z}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\pi\,\mathrm{Z}^{4}}{4\,\mathrm{S}}\,,

\mathrm{Z}=|\pazocal{Z}|=\sqrt{\mathrm{Q}^{2}+\mathrm{P}^{2}}

7
Cosmological generalization of the extremal solution

As shown by Kastor and Traschen in
Kastor:1992nn
, it is possible to generalize the Majumdar–Papapetrou solution describing a configuration of multi-centered extremal Reissner–Nordström black holes by introducing a
positive
cosmological constant
\Lambda>0
in the following way:
6
6

6

For simplicity, we write here only the electric generalization
w=1
, while the full electromagnetic case can be obtained by a duality rotation of the electromagnetic field, leaving the metric unchanged.

ds^{2}=-U^{-2}{d\tau}^{2}+a^{2}(\tau)\,U^{2}\bigl({dx}^{2}+{dy}^{2}+{dz}^{2}\bigr)\,,\qquad A=U^{-1}d\tau\,,

(47)

where

U=1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{m_{i}}{a(\tau)|\vec{x}-\vec{x}_{i}|}\,,\qquad a(\tau)=e^{\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda}{3}}\,\tau}\,.

(48)

However, we note that under a transformation of the time coordinate

\tau\mapsto\sqrt{\frac{3}{\Lambda}}\log\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda}{3}}t\,,

(49)

we obtain a solution wherein the metric and the potential can again be written in the usual Majumdar–Papapetrou form, whereas the function
U
is now time-dependent due to an extra term which is proportional to the time coordinate
t
and the cosmological constant
\Lambda
:

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-U^{-2}{dt}^{2}\,+\,

\displaystyle U^{2}\bigl({dx}^{2}+{dy}^{2}+{dz}^{2}\bigr)\,,\qquad A=U^{-1}dt\,,

(50a)

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda}{3}}\,t+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{m_{i}}{|\vec{x}-\vec{x}_{i}|}\,.

(50b)

This represents a direct cosmological generalization, with a positive cosmological constant
\Lambda>0
, of the single extremal Reissner–Nordström black hole in a Bertotti–Robinson universe (
11
), obtained by setting

N=2
,
\vec{x}_{1}=(0,0,1/B)
,
m_{1}=1/B
,
\vec{x}_{2}=0
,
m_{2}=m
in (
50
).

On the other hand, although the two metrics given by equations (
47
) and (
50
) are related by the coordinate transformation (
49
), the first metric (
47
) does not directly contain the extremal Reissner–Nordström black hole in a Bertotti–Robinson universe (
11
) as a direct subcase for
\Lambda\rightarrow 0
. This is due to the fact that the transformation given by equation (
49
) diverges in the limit
\Lambda\rightarrow 0
, so the two solutions are diffeomorphic only for
\Lambda\neq 0
.

Moreover, the solution written as equation (
50
) with
N=2
may be viewed as a cosmological spacetime interpolating between the extremal Reissner–Nordström black hole in Bertotti–Robinson at
t=0
and the solution describing a pair of extremal Reissner–Nordström black holes at
t=\sqrt{\frac{3}{\Lambda}}
. From this perspective, the spacetime describes an observer who, at
t=0
, is in the near-horizon region of the extremal Reissner--Nordström black hole generating the Bertotti--Robinson field,
7
7

7

We recall that the Bertotti–Robinson spacetime is the near-horizon limit of an extremal Reissner–Nordström black hole.
and, as time evolves, the cosmological expansion driven by
\Lambda>0
causes the two full extremal charged black holes to be perceived again.

Finally, we underline that these generalizations cannot be used as bosonic backgrounds in gauged supergravity, since in that case a
negative
cosmological constant is required, whereas these solutions are defined only for a
positive
(nonnegative) one, due to the appearance of the cosmological constant under a square root.

8
Conclusions

In this article, we studied the supersymmetry of a novel class of solutions describing a possibly charged and accelerating black hole embedded in a spacetime filled with an electromagnetic field of the Bertotti–Robinson type.

In particular, we found that the only subcases satisfying the necessary condition for supersymmetry are the Bertotti–Robinson background, the extremal Reissner–Nordström black hole, both of which were already known to be supersymmetric, and their combination, representing an extremal Reissner–Nordström black hole embedded in Bertotti–Robinson.
For this last subcase, we explicitly obtained the corresponding Killing spinors, from which we computed the associated currents, the thermodynamic quantities and the electromagnetic charges.
Moreover, we found that this subcase also belongs to the Majumdar–Papapetrou class, which allowed us to obtain the total mass of the spacetime, and therefore to verify that the BPS bound is saturated and that the laws of black hole mechanics are satisfied; and finally to derive the generalization with a
positive
cosmological constant.

There are some natural extensions of our work that can be considered:
for instance, one can study the supersymmetry properties of the general black holes in Bertotti–Robinson presented in
Ovcharenko:2025cpm
which includes, e.g., rotation.
Another possibility is the addition of NUT, swirling and Melvin parameters to the solution of
Ovcharenko:2025cpm
, as it has been done for some subcases in
Astorino:2025lih
;
Astorino:2026okd
;
Barrientos:2026shy
;
Ma:2026otg
, in order to establish the supersymmetry of the most general case.

The inclusion of a negative cosmological constant is by far the most compelling extension, since it would imply the generalization to the gauged supergravity realm, which represents the low-energy limit of string theories and M-theory.
Being all the supersymmetric solutions of
\pazocal{N}=2
,
D=4
supergravity classified
Caldarelli:2003pb
, one might look for the extension in such a class.

Finally, in analogy with the corresponding spacetimes without a Bertotti–Robinson electromagnetic field, and recalling that the extremal Reissner–Nordström black hole in Bertotti–Robinson (
11
) belongs to the Majumdar–Papapetrou (MP) class
Majumdar:1947eu
;
Papaetrou:1947ib
, as we stated in section
5
, we conjecture that the addition of a NUT parameter to this spacetime, as well as an extremal rotating generalization, namely the extremal Kerr–Newman black hole in Bertotti–Robinson, should both be contained in the rotating generalization of the MP class, known as the Israel–Wilson–Perjés (IWP) class
Perjes:1971gv
;
Israel:1972vx
.

(67b)

Finally, the tensor bilinear defines an antisymmetric
2-
form,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}f_{\mu\nu}\,dx^{\mu}\wedge dx^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n,m=1}^{2}\bar{\epsilon}_{n}\gamma_{\mu\nu}\,\epsilon_{m}\,dx^{\mu}\wedge dx^{\nu}

(68a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=-\frac{\sqrt{1-w^{2}}}{r\,\Omega^{3}}\Bigl[\,\Xi_{r}\,dx^{t}\wedge dx^{r}+\Delta_{r}\,\Xi_{\theta}\,dx^{t}\wedge dx^{\theta}+\Delta_{r}\sin^{2}\theta\,\Xi_{\varphi}\,dx^{t}\wedge dx^{\varphi}\Bigr]\\
&\quad+\frac{r\,w\sin\theta}{\Omega^{3}}\Bigl[\,\Xi_{\varphi}\,dx^{r}\wedge dx^{\theta}+\,\Xi_{\theta}\,dx^{\varphi}\wedge dx^{r}+\,\Xi_{r}\,dx^{\theta}\wedge dx^{\varphi}\Bigr]\,.\end{split}

(68b)

Moreover, a key property of the bilinears constructed from the Killing spinors is that the vector bilinear (
64a
) is, by construction, a Killing vector, timelike in this case, while the tensor bilinear (
68a
) is an antisymmetric Killing–Yano
2-
form.
This allows us to construct a rank
-2
symmetric Killing tensor

\displaystyle K^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\,\partial_{\nu}

\displaystyle=f^{\mu}_{\;\;\rho}f^{\rho\nu}\partial_{\mu}\,\partial_{\nu}

(69a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=-\frac{(1-w^{2})\,\Xi^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}\partial_{t}\,\partial_{t}+\frac{\Delta_{r}\bigl(\,\Xi^{2}_{r}\,-w^{2}\,\Xi^{2}\bigr)}{r^{4}}\partial_{r}\,\partial_{r}\\
&\quad+\frac{\bigl(\Delta_{r}\,\Xi^{2}_{\theta}\,-w^{2}\,\Xi^{2}\bigr)}{r^{4}}\partial_{\theta}\,\partial_{\theta}+\frac{\bigl(\Delta_{r}\sin^{2}\theta\,\Xi^{2}_{\varphi}\,-w^{2}\,\Xi^{2}\bigr)}{r^{4}\sin^{2}\theta}\partial_{\varphi}\,\partial_{\varphi}+\\
&\quad+\frac{2\Delta_{r}}{r^{4}}\Bigl[\,\Xi_{r}\,\Xi_{\theta}\,\partial_{r}\,\partial_{\theta}+\,\Xi_{r}\,\Xi_{\varphi}\,\partial_{r}\,\partial_{\varphi}+\,\Xi_{\theta}\,\Xi_{\varphi}\,\partial_{\theta}\,\partial_{\varphi}\Bigr]\,,\end{split}

(69b)

where

\displaystyle\Xi^{2}=\Xi^{2}_{r}\,+\Delta_{r}\,\Xi^{2}_{\theta}\,+\Delta_{r}\sin^{2}\theta\,\Xi^{2}_{\varphi}\,.

(70)

Appendix D
Thermodynamics and charges of the non-extremal case

In the non-extremal case, i.e. metric (
2a
), the black hole possesses both an inner and an outer horizon,
r_{H}=(r_{-},r_{+})
, whose relative positions, as discussed in section
2.1
, depend on the specific values of the parameters. Since the thermodynamic quantities may vary depending on the horizon at which they are evaluated, we compute the entropy and temperature on both horizons,
r_{-}=r_{0}
and
r_{+}=r_{0}+\frac{2m}{1-B^{2}m^{2}}
.
Specifically, we have that the the surface gravity
\kappa
and the temperature
\mathrm{T}
at the horizon, which correspond to the following quantities

\kappa=\sqrt{-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}\nabla^{\mu}\xi^{\nu}}\Bigg\rvert_{r=r_{H}}=\frac{|\Delta^{\prime}(r_{H})|}{2\,r_{H}^{2}}\,,\qquad\mathrm{T}=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\,,

(71)

with
\xi=\partial_{t}
the timelike Killing vector, results in

\displaystyle\mathrm{\kappa}_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{r_{+}^{2}}\bigl[1-(r_{+}-r_{0})^{2}\bigl(\alpha^{2}-B^{2})\bigr]\,,\qquad\quad\,\,\,\mathrm{\kappa}_{-}=\frac{m}{\,r_{0}^{2}}\,,

(72a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{2\pi\,r_{+}^{2}}\bigl[1-(r_{+}-r_{0})^{2}\bigl(\alpha^{2}-B^{2})\bigr]\,,\qquad\mathrm{T}_{-}=\frac{m}{2\pi\,r_{0}^{2}}\,.

(72b)

In a similar way, the horizon area
\mathrm{A}
and the entropy
\mathrm{S}
,

\mathrm{A}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\int_{0}^{\pi}\sqrt{g_{\varphi\varphi}g_{\theta\theta}}\Bigg\rvert_{r=r_{H}}\!\,,\qquad\mathrm{S}=\frac{\mathrm{A}}{4}\,,

(73)

are found to be:

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi\,r_{+}^{2}}{\bigl[1-(r_{+}-r_{0})^{2}\bigl(\alpha^{2}-B^{2})\bigr]}\,,\qquad\mathrm{A}_{-}=4\pi\,r_{0}^{2}\,,

(74a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\,r_{+}^{2}}{\bigl[1-(r_{+}-r_{0})^{2}\bigl(\alpha^{2}-B^{2})\bigr]}\,,\qquad\,\mathrm{S}_{-}=\pi\,r_{0}^{2}\,.

(74b)

From these results, it follows straightforwardly that

2\,\mathrm{T}_{-}\,\mathrm{S}_{-}=2\,\mathrm{T}_{+}\,\mathrm{S}_{+}=m\,.

(75)

In contrast to temperature and entropy, we find that the charges are independent of the radius at which the integral is evaluated, so the following results hold in general

\mathrm{Q}=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma}F^{\mu\nu}dS_{\mu\nu}=\frac{\alpha\,r_{0}}{B}w\,,\,\quad\,\mathrm{P}=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma}{}^{\star}F^{\mu\nu}dS_{\mu\nu}=\frac{\alpha\,r_{0}}{B}\sqrt{1-w^{2}}\,,

(76)

where we recall that
{}^{\star}F^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{-g}}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}
is the Hodge dual of the Faraday tensor
F^{\mu\nu}
.

In the extremal limit (
11
), for which
r_{+}=r_{-}
, these results converge to those obtained in section
5
and section
6
, where in particular we obtain that the temperature
\mathrm{T}
reduces to zero, as expected.
