Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) has prepared a new set of iodine-bearing sorbents. The sorbent
materials used for this activity were supplied by IONEX (NexSorb-99) or C&CS (AC6120-7%). Metal
canisters were previously prepared by ORNL in 2022. They are 2 in. long by 1.5 in. in diameter and are
made of 316 stainless steel.1 In the current activity, six canisters were loaded with iodine-exposed
samples of either 25 g of Ag+ AC6120-7% (HIP-1), 25 g of Ag0 AC6120-7% (HIP-2), or 27 g of Ag+
NEXSORB-99 (HIP-3, HIP-4, HIP-5, HIP-6, respectively), as shown in Figure 1. The purpose of
preparing multiple canisters of the latter material was to accommodate recombination for a single large-
scale test or enable several small-scale replicate tests. After filling with the respective sample from the
current activity as described below, the canisters were welded closed with trepan lids and vacuum sealed
using a ⅛ in. tube port (Figure 1, bottom). Reserved portions of the iodine-loaded samples were sent for
neutron activation analysis (NAA) to determine the total iodine content. The samples for NAA are listed
in Table 1.

The welded canisters containing these samples were sent to American Isostatic Presses (AIP) Inc. on June
10th with the requested experimental conditions that mimic previous studies: 900°C and 300 MPa with a
3 h hold time. A variety of other conditions are offered by AIP Inc.; however, the same pressing
conditions as done in FY 2022 would allow a direct comparison with previous data published by
ORNL/ANL.1 The resulting, pressed samples (Figure 2) were shipped to ANL.

 METHODS

Sample reduction. Ag0 AC6120-7% was prepared by adding Ag+ AC6120-7% under a flow of 4% H2/Ar
in an oven at 270°C for 10 days, following a previous procedure.1

Static exposure tests. Thin bed, static loading of iodine (I2(g)) adsorption by Ag-bearing materials was
performed at the Iodine Off-gas Testing and Abatement (IOTA) Laboratory at ORNL. A piece of steel
mesh was fitted into an Ace Glass column equipped with bead-to-bead, Teflon-lined coupling clamps and
glass caps at both ends providing an airtight seal. Either 25 g (Ag+ or Ag0 AC6120-7%) or 100 g (Ag+
NEXSORB-99) was added to the mesh in an even layer up to 3 pellets thick. Several grams (2–5 g) of
I2(s) crystals were added to either a stainless steel mesh plate or open glass jar within the same container.
The reactor was sealed and placed inside a box furnace set to 150°C for 14–15 days, as shown in Figure 1
(top left). The sorbent material was removed, and nitrogen (Airgas, Ultra High Purity) was passed over
the sorbent material overnight at room temperature (~20 °C) to remove any physiosorbed iodine.

Neutron activation analysis. At ORNL’s High Flux Isotope Reactor (HFIR), iodine was analyzed in
these novel sorbent materials by measurement of the 25 min half-life 128I product of the neutron capture
reaction 127I(n,γ)128I. A high-purity germanium (HPGe)-based gamma ray spectrometer was used to
quantify the 442 keV emission line of 128I. Neutron irradiations were typically 5–30 s in duration, and up
to 4 half-lives of the product 128I were required to reduce the detector dead time to 25% or less. No sample
preparation, dissolution, or preconcentration were required. Counting occurred 30–50 cm from the
detector end cap using a coaxial p-type HPGe detector having a relative efficiency of 45%. The counting
duration was limited to 20 min or less to ensure that corrections for decay during counting would
contribute minimally to the overall uncertainty. Signals were processed digitally using the Mirion LYNX
signal analyzer operated in the loss-free counting mode using the add-back method.2
