Rectifiability of free boundaries in singular diffusion problems
Rafayel Teymurazyan

Abstract
. For minimizers of a degenerate diffusion functional with a singular reaction term, we prove that the free boundary is ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -rectifiable. The argument relies on a suitable integrability property, derived from a pointwise gradient estimate, combined with a Hausdorff dimension estimate for a portion of the zero set.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 35R35. Secondary 35A21, 35J70

1.
Introduction

We study the rectifiability of the free boundary of minimizers of

(1.1)

J_{\Omega}(v):=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{|Dv|^{p}}{p}+v^{\gamma}\right)\,dx

over

(1.2)

\mathbb{K}:=\left\{v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega):\,\,v-g\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega),\ v\geq 0\mbox{ a.e. in }\Omega\right\}.

Here
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain,
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
p\geq 2
and
g\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is a non-negative function. As shown in
[
3
, Proposition 2.1]
, minimizers satisfy

(1.3)

\Delta_{p}u=\gamma u^{\gamma-1}\quad\text{in}\quad\{u>0\}\cap\Omega,

where
\Delta_{p}u:=\mathrm{div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du)
is the
p
-Laplacian. Since
\gamma<1
, the right-hand side in (
1.3
) blows up as
u\to 0^{+}
, making the study of the behavior of minimizers near the free boundary
\partial\{u>0\}
highly nontrivial.

The problem was studied by Phillips in the linear setting (
p=2
) in
[
13
,
14
]
(see also
[
1
,
2
]
), proving that minimizers are locally of the class
C^{1,\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma}}
, and that the free boundary has locally finite
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure and is rectifiable. As the approach in
[
13
,
14
]
relies on the linearity of the operator, it does not extend directly to
p>2
. Nevertheless, using geometric tangential analysis, several of these results were established in the degenerate setting in
[
4
,
3
]
.

Despite recent advances, the rectifiability of the free boundary remained open in the nonlinear setting. In this note, we close that gap by proving that the free boundary has a measure-theoretic outward normal at
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-almost every point. More precisely, our main result shows that if
u
is a minimizer of (
1.1
), then locally,

(1.4)

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\{u>0\}\setminus\partial_{\operatorname{red}}\{u>0\})=0.

The proof relies on two main ingredients: the integrability of a nonlinear transformation of
u
, derived from the pointwise gradient estimate established in
[
3
]
, and a Hausdorff dimension estimate for a portion of the zero set. Since
\{u>0\}
has locally finite perimeter, its reduced boundary is locally
(n-1)
-rectifiable,
[
11
, Corollary 16.1]
. Hence, (
1.4
) establishes the same property for the free boundary
\partial\{u>0\}
.

The limiting cases
\gamma=0
and
\gamma=1
correspond to the cavity and obstacle problems, respectively. For
\gamma=0
, rectifiability was obtained in
[
7
, Corollary 9.2]
. For
\gamma=1
, the free boundary has locally finite
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
[
10
,
6
]
and is rectifiable for
p=2
,
[
5
]
. For
p>2
, the free boundary of the obstacle problem is countably
(n-1)
-rectifiable under a concavity condition that excludes constant obstacles,
[
9
, Theorem 1.8]
.

The functional (
1.1
) models the competition between nonlinear diffusion and a singular reaction term. Such structures arise in reaction-diffusion models with thresholding effects, where the unknown represents quantities such as density, pressure, or concentration. In these settings, the positivity set corresponds to an active region, while the zero phase represents inactive zones, and the free boundary describes the interface between them.

This note is organized as follows. After listing the main notations and some known results in
Section

2
, in
Section

3
we prove an integrability property,
Lemma

3.1
. In
Section

4
, we show that portions of the zero set with vanishing Lebesgue measure are negligible in a certain Hausdorff dimension,
Lemma

4.1
. Finally,
Section

5
extends (
1.4
) into the degenerate setting,
Theorem

5.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we gather the known results on minimizers of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) that will be used in this work. We start by introducing some notations and recalling several definitions that are used throughout the work.

2.1.
Definitions

The ball of radius
r
centered at
x
is denoted by
B_{r}(x)
, and we write
B_{r}:=B_{r}(0)
. Also
J_{r}:=J_{B_{r}}
, where
J_{B_{r}}
is defined by (
1.1
).
|E|
is the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of the set
E
, and
\mathcal{H}^{d}(E)
denotes its
d
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

Definition 2.1
.

A point
x\in B_{1}
is said to be on the reduced free boundary of
\{u>0\}
in
B_{1}
, and we write
x\in\partial_{\operatorname{red}}\{u>0\}
, if there exists a unique unit vector
\nu(x)
such that

\int_{B_{r}(x)}\left|\chi_{\{u>0\}}-\chi_{\{y\,:\,(y-x)\cdot\nu(x)<0\}}\right|\,dy=o(r^{n}),

as
r\to 0
.

By De Giorgi’s structure theorem, the reduced boundary of a set of locally finite perimeter is a locally
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-
rectifiable set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see, for example,
[
11
, Corollary 16.1]
). Thus, the reduced free boundary is the subset of the free boundary, where
\{u>0\}
has a tangent plane in measure with normal
\nu(x)
.

Definition 2.2
.

A nonnegative function
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is a local minimizer of (
1.1
) in
B_{r}
, if

J_{r}(u)\leq J_{r}(v),

for all
v\in W^{1,p}(B_{r})
such that
v-u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(B_{r})
.

2.2.
Known results

As it is established in
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
, minimizers of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) exist,
0\leq u\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
, and satisfy the Euler-Lagrange equation (
1.3
),
[
3
, Proposition 2.1]
. Furthermore,
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
, we have

(2.1)

\|u\|_{C^{1,\beta}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C,

for every
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
, and
C>0
depends only on
\operatorname{dist}(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)
,
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
,
p
,
\gamma
and
n
. Here
\beta:=\left\{\alpha_{p}^{-},\frac{\gamma}{p-\gamma}\right\}
,
\alpha_{p}
is the optimal (unknown for
n\geq 3
) regularity exponent for the gradient of
p
-harmonic functions, and
c^{-}
means any real number less than
c
. Moreover, as shown in
[
4
, Theorem 4.1 and Theorem 6.1]
, across the free boundary points, one has

(2.2)

u\leq Cr^{\frac{p}{p-\gamma}}\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,B_{r}(y),

for any
y\in\partial\{u>0\}\cap\Omega^{\prime}
, and for any
r\in(0,r_{0})
, with some universal constants
C>0
and
r_{0}>0
. For
x_{0}\in\overline{\{u>0\}}
, we also have the following non-degeneracy for minimizers,
[
3
, Proposition 4.1]

(2.3)

\sup_{\partial B_{r}(x_{0})}u\geq cr^{\frac{p}{p-\gamma}},

for any
r<\operatorname{dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega)
. Here
c>0
depends only on
p
,
\gamma
and
n
. Additionally, the following positive density result holds,
[
3
, Corollary 4.2]
,

(2.4)

\frac{|\{u>0\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})|}{|B_{r}(x_{0})|}\geq\tau,

where
x_{0}\in\Omega^{\prime}\cap\partial\{u>0\}
,
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
,
r>0
is small enough, and
\tau\in(0,1)
depends only on
\Omega^{\prime}
,
p
,
\gamma
and
n
. Furthermore, we know that
\partial\{u>0\}
has locally finite
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-measure,
[
3
, Theorem 6.1]
, and
\{u>0\}
has locally finite perimeter in
\Omega
,
[
3
, Remark 6.1]
(see also
[
8
, Theorem 4.5.11]
and
[
11
,
14
]
).

The result below is from
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
. It provides a pointwise gradient estimate which is vital in our analysis.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
u
is a local minimizer of (
1.1
) in
B_{1}
, then there exists
C>0
, depending only on
p
,
\gamma
and
n
, such that

|Du(x)|^{p}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{p-\gamma}\,u^{\gamma}(x),\quad x\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}.

The next result is from
[
12
, Proposition 3.3]
(see also
[
14
, Theorem 2.8]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
p>1
and
u
is a minimizer of (
1.1
), then

\Delta_{p}u=\gamma u^{\gamma-1}\chi_{\{u>0\}}\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega

in the sense of distributions.

We close this section by recalling
[
3
, Proposition 5.1]
, revealing that in a universally flat regime, the function
u^{\frac{p-\gamma}{p}}
is
p
-subharmonic.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
u
is a local minimizer of (
1.1
) in
B_{1}
, then there exists
\varepsilon_{\star}>0
, depending only on
p
,
\gamma
and
n
, such that

\Delta_{p}\left(u^{\frac{p-\gamma}{p}}\right)\geq 0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\left\{0<u\leq\varepsilon_{\star}^{\frac{p}{p-\gamma}}\right\}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}.

3.
An integrability property

In this section, we prove integrability of the
p
-Laplacian of a nonlinear transform of minimizers, which is used later to obtain our main result. Using
Theorem

2.1
, we extend the corresponding result from
[
14
, Corollary 2.7]
for
p=2
to the nonlinear setting with
p\geq 2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
u
is a minimizer of (
1.1
), and
x_{0}\in\partial\{u>0\}
, then

\Delta_{p}(u^{\frac{p-\gamma}{p}})\in L^{1}\left(\{u>0\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})\right),

for
r>0
small.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\varphi^{\prime}\geq 0
, and

\varphi(t):=\begin{cases}0\quad\textrm{if}\quad t\leq\frac{1}{2},\\
t\quad\textrm{if}\quad t\geq 1.\end{cases}

For each
0<\delta<\varepsilon
, set

\varphi_{\delta}(t):=\delta\varphi\left(\frac{t}{\delta}\right)\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,u_{\varepsilon}:=\min\left\{u^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},\varepsilon\right\},

where
\alpha:=\frac{p}{p-\gamma}
. Since
\varphi_{\delta}^{\prime}\geq 0
and

D\varphi_{\delta}(u_{\varepsilon})=0\,\,\,\text{on}\,\,\,\{u\geq\varepsilon^{\alpha}\},

then

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle:=\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D(u^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})|^{p-2}D(u^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})\cdot D\varphi_{\delta}(u_{\varepsilon})\,dx

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\int_{\{u<\varepsilon^{\alpha}\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}|D(u^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})|^{p}\varphi^{\prime}_{\delta}(u_{\varepsilon})\,dx\geq 0

On the other hand, taking
\delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and integrating by parts, we get

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{r}(x_{0})}\varphi_{\delta}(u_{\varepsilon})|D(u^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})|^{p-2}D(u^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})\cdot\nu\,dx

(3.2)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\varphi_{\delta}(u_{\varepsilon})\Delta_{p}(u^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})\,dx:=I_{1}-I_{2}.

Since

D(u^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})=\frac{1}{\alpha}u^{-\frac{\gamma}{p}}Du,

then employing
Theorem

2.1
, we obtain

|D(u^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})|^{p-2}D(u^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})\cdot\nu\leq\frac{1}{\alpha^{p-1}}\left[u^{-\frac{\gamma}{p}}|Du|\right]^{p-1}\leq\frac{C}{\alpha^{p-1}}\|u\|^{p-\gamma}_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}.

Hence, the integrand in
I_{1}
is bounded and therefore

(3.3)

|I_{1}|\leq C,

where
C>0
is independent of
\varepsilon>0
.
Combining (
3
)-(
3.3
), we get

(3.4)

I_{2}\leq I_{1}\leq C.

Observe now that

(3.5)

I_{2}=\int_{\{u\leq\varepsilon^{\alpha}\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}\varphi_{\delta}(u)\Delta_{p}(u^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})\,dx+\int_{\{u>\varepsilon^{\alpha}\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}\Delta_{p}(u^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})\,dx.

For
\varepsilon>0
small enough, according to
Proposition

2.2
,

\Delta_{p}(u^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})\geq 0\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\{0<u\leq\varepsilon^{\alpha}\},

hence, the first term in the right-hand side of (
3.5
) is nonnegative, which combined with (
3.4
), yields

\int_{\{u>\varepsilon^{\alpha}\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}\Delta_{p}(u^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})\,dx\leq I_{2}\leq C.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
in the last inequality, we obtain the desired result.
∎

4.
Negiligibility of a certain zero set

In this section, we show that part of the free boundary away from the reduced boundary is negligible in a certain Hausdorff dimension. We conclude it from the following result, which extends the corresponding statement for
p=2
to any
p\geq 2
. Its proof follows the ideas of
[
14
, Lemma 3.1]
; the additional terms arising from the nonlinearity are controlled using the pointwise gradient estimate from
Theorem

2.1
. We provide the details below.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
u
is a minimizer of (
1.1
),
B_{3r}\subset\Omega
and
|\{u=0\}\cap B_{3r}|=0
, then

(4.1)

\mathcal{H}^{n-\frac{2-\gamma}{p-\gamma}p}\left(\{u=0\}\cap B_{r}\right)=0.

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1.
First, we show that

(4.2)

u^{\gamma-2}\in L^{1}(B_{2r}).

Let
\xi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{3r})
,
\xi\geq 0
and
\xi\equiv 1
on
B_{2r}
. Then for any
\varepsilon\geq 0
, the function
u+\varepsilon\xi\in\mathbb{K}
. Setting

I(\varepsilon):=J(u+\varepsilon\xi),

using the Dominated Convergence Theorem and recalling
Proposition

2.1
, one can easily check that

(4.3)

I^{\prime}(\varepsilon)=\int_{\Omega}(|D(u+\varepsilon\xi)|^{p-2}D(u+\varepsilon\xi)\cdot D\xi+\gamma(u+\varepsilon\xi)^{\gamma-1}\xi)\,dx

and

\displaystyle I^{\prime\prime}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{3r}}|D(u+\varepsilon\xi)|^{p-2}|D\xi|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{3r}}(p-2)|D(u+\varepsilon\xi)|^{p-4}(D(u+\varepsilon\xi)\cdot D\xi)^{2}\,dx

(4.4)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{3r}}\gamma(\gamma-1)(u+\varepsilon\xi)^{\gamma-2}\xi^{2}\,dx.

Thus,
I(\varepsilon)
attains its minimum at
\varepsilon=0
, and thanks to (
4.3
) and
Proposition

2.1
, one has

I^{\prime}(0)=\int_{\Omega}(|Du|^{p-2}Du\cdot D\xi+\gamma u^{\gamma-1}\xi)\,dx=0.

Also, by the Monotone Convergence Theorem,
I^{\prime}
is continuous on
[0,1)
and differentiable on
(0,1)
. Hence, there must exist
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
such that
I^{\prime\prime}(\varepsilon_{k})\geq 0
. Thus, recalling the definition of
\xi
, employing (
4
) and
Theorem

2.1
, we estimate

\gamma(1-\gamma)\int_{B_{2r}}(u+\varepsilon_{k})^{\gamma-2}\,dx\leq\gamma(1-\gamma)\int_{B_{3r}}(u+\varepsilon_{k}\xi)^{\gamma-2}\xi^{2}\,dx\leq C,

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}
,
\|D\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}
,
p
,
\gamma
and
n
. Using the Monotone Convergence Theorem one more time, we obtain
u^{\gamma-2}\in L^{1}(B_{2r})
.

Step 2.
Next, we show that (
4.2
) implies (
4.1
).

Recall that if
g\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(B_{2r})
, and
s>0
, then (see, for example,
[
8
, 2.10.19 (4)]
or
[
11
, Theorem 5.8, see also Section 6.2]
)

(4.5)

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\rho^{s-n}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}g\,dx=0\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,\mathcal{H}^{n-s}\,\,\,\text{a.e.}\,\,\,x_{0}\in B_{2r}.

If
x_{0}\in\partial\{u>0\}\cap B_{r}
, then by (
2.2
), one has
u<Cr^{\alpha}
, where
\alpha:=\frac{p}{p-\gamma}
and
C>0
depends only on
p
,
\gamma
and
n
. Hence, for
\rho<\frac{r}{3}
, one has

\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u^{\gamma-2}\,dx>C\rho^{n-\alpha(2-\gamma)},

that is

\rho^{\alpha(2-\gamma)-n}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u^{\gamma-2}\,dx>C.

Consequently, (
4.5
) gives
\mathcal{H}^{n-\alpha(2-\gamma)}(\partial\{u>0\}\cap B_{r})=0
. To finish the proof, observe that
\partial\{u>0\}\cap B_{r}=\{u=0\}\cap B_{r}
since
|\{u=0\}\cap B_{r}|=0
.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

If
u
is a minimizer of (
1.1
), then

\mathcal{H}^{n-\frac{2-\gamma}{p-\gamma}p}\left(\partial\{u>0\}\setminus\overline{\partial_{\operatorname{red}}\{u>0\}}\right)=0.

Proof.

First, we observe that

(4.6)

x\in\partial\{u>0\}\setminus\overline{\partial_{\operatorname{red}}\{u>0\}}\iff|\{u=0\}\cap B_{r}(x)|=0.

Indeed, we know that locally
|\partial\{u>0\}|=0
(since the free boundary has locally finite
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure,
[
3
, Theorem 6.1]
). This means that
|\{u=0\}|=|\operatorname{int}\{u=0\}|
. Observe that if
x\in\partial\{u>0\}
, then for some
r>0
,

(4.7)

x\in\partial\{u>0\}\setminus\partial(\operatorname{int}\{u=0\})\iff|\{u=0\}\cap B_{r}(x)|=0.

From the general theory of sets with locally finite perimeter (see, for example,
[
11
, Proposition 12.19, (15.3) and (16.27)]
), one has
x\in\partial\{u>0\}\setminus\overline{\partial_{\operatorname{red}}\{u>0\}}
if and only if for some
r>0
either

(4.8)

|\{u=0\}\cap B_{r}(x)|=0

or

(4.9)

|\{u>0\}\cap B_{r}(x)|=0.

But (
2.4
) excludes (
4.9
). Thus, only (
4.8
) is possible, and (
4.6
) follows.

Note that (
4.7
) yields

\overline{\partial_{\operatorname{red}}\{u>0\}}=\partial\left(\operatorname{int}\{u=0\}\cap\Omega\right).

We then cover
\partial\{u>0\}\setminus\overline{\partial_{\operatorname{red}}\{u>0\}}
with balls
\left\{B_{r(x)}(x)\right\}
satisfying (
4.8
). By (
4.1
), for each
x\in\partial\{u>0\}\setminus\overline{\partial_{\operatorname{red}}\{u>0\}}
, one has

\mathcal{H}^{n-\frac{2-\gamma}{p-\gamma}p}\left(\{u=0\}\cap B_{\frac{r(x)}{3}}(x)\right)=0,

and selecting a countable subcovering from
\left\{B_{r(x)}(x)\right\}
gives the desired result.
∎

5.
Rectifiability of the free boundary

We now have all the ingredients to prove (
1.4
), extending
[
14
, Theorem 3.2]
for
p=2
to the nonlinear setting with
p\geq 2
.

Theorem 5.1
.

If
u
is a minimizer of (
1.1
), then there exists
r_{0}>0
, depending only on
p
,
\gamma
and
n
, such that for any
r\leq r_{0}
and
x_{0}\in\partial\{u>0\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})
, one has

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left((\partial\{u>0\}\setminus\partial_{\operatorname{red}}\{u>0\})\cap B_{r}(x_{0})\right)=0.

Proof.

Using Federer’s decomposition for sets with locally finite perimeter,
[
8
, Section 4.5.6, page 478]
, we can decompose the free boundary
\partial\{u>0\}
as follows:

\partial\{u>0\}=\partial_{\operatorname{red}}\{u>0\}\cup A\cup B,

where
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(B)=0
and
x_{0}\in A
if and only if either

(5.1)

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{|\{u>0\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})|}{|B_{r}(x_{0})|}=0

or

(5.2)

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{|\{u=0\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})|}{|B_{r}(x_{0})|}=0

We aim to show that
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(A)=0
. As before, (
2.4
) excludes (
5.1
). Thus,
x_{0}\in A
if and only if (
5.2
) holds. Below, we show that it is not possible.

Step 1.
Note that

u^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left|\frac{p}{p-1}-\frac{|Du|^{p}}{u^{\gamma}}\right|\in L^{1}\left(\{u>0\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})\right),

where
\alpha:=\frac{p}{p-\gamma}
. Indeed, it follows from
Lemma

3.1
, as by direct calculation, using (
1.3
), in
\{u>0\}
one has

\Delta_{p}(u^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})=\frac{\gamma}{\alpha^{p-1}}\frac{p-1}{p}u^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left[\frac{p}{p-1}-\frac{|Du|^{p}}{u^{\gamma}}\right].

Recalling (
4.5
), for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
a.e.
x_{0}\in A
, we have

(5.3)

\lim_{r\to 0}r^{1-n}\int_{\{u>0\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}u^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left|\frac{p}{p-1}-\frac{|Du|^{p}}{u^{\gamma}}\right|\,dx=0.

Set

u_{k}(y):=\frac{u(x_{0}+r_{k}y)}{r_{k}^{\alpha}},\,\,\,y\in B_{1}.

By (
2.1
),
u_{k}
is uniformly bounded in
B_{\frac{1}{2}}
. Thus, there exists a function
u_{0}
such that, up to a subsequence,

u_{k}\to u_{0}\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,C^{1}(B_{\frac{1}{2}}).

Furthermore, since
u_{k}
is a local minimizer in
B_{\frac{1}{2}}
, so is
u_{0}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle r^{1-n}\int_{\{u>0\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}u^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left|\frac{p}{p-1}-\frac{|Du|^{p}}{u^{\gamma}}\right|\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\{u_{k}>0\}\cap B_{1}}u_{k}^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left|\frac{p}{p-1}-\frac{|Du_{k}|^{p}}{u_{k}^{\gamma}}\right|\,dx.

Therefore, using Fatou’s lemma and (
5.3
), we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\{u_{k}>0\}\cap B_{1}}u_{k}^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left|\frac{p}{p-1}-\frac{|Du_{k}|^{p}}{u_{k}^{\gamma}}\right|\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\{u_{0}>0\}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}}u_{0}^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left|\frac{p}{p-1}-\frac{|Du_{0}|^{p}}{u_{0}^{\gamma}}\right|\,dx

and hence,

(5.4)

\frac{p}{p-1}=\frac{|Du_{0}|^{p}}{u_{0}^{\gamma}}\,\,\,\text{on}\,\,\,\{u_{0}>0\}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}.

Step 2.
Next, we show that

(5.5)

|\{u_{0}=0\}\cap B_{\frac{1}{4}}|=0.

Indeed, if
|\{u_{0}=0\}\cap B_{\frac{1}{4}}|>0
, then
u_{0}=0
on a ball
B_{\rho}(z)\subset B_{\frac{1}{4}}
. Thus,

(5.6)

u_{k}\to 0\,\,\,\text{on}\,\,\,B_{\rho}(z).

As
x_{0}\in A
, (
5.2
) yields, up to a subsequence,

|\{u_{k}=0\}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}|\to 0,\,\,\,\text{as}\,\,\,k\to\infty.

Hence, for
k
large enough, there exist
y_{k}\in B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(z)
such that
u_{k}(y_{k})>0
. Since
u_{k}
is a local minimizer, by (
2.3
) one has

\sup_{\partial B_{\frac{\rho}{4}}}u_{k}\geq c\rho^{\alpha},

which contradicts (
5.6
). Hence, (
5.5
) holds.

Step 3.
In the final step, we show that (
5.5
) implies that
u_{0}
is one-dimensional, leading to a contradiction. Indeed, since
u_{0}
is a local minimizer, it satisfies (
1.3
). Direct calculation then reveals that in
\{u_{0}>0\}
one has

(5.7)

v\Delta_{p}v=\frac{\gamma}{\alpha^{p-1}}-(\alpha-1)(p-1)|Dv|^{p},

where

v:=u_{0}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

On the other hand, using (
5.4
), one gets

(5.8)

|Dv|^{p}=\frac{p}{p-1}\alpha^{-p}:=|a|^{p},

therefore, (
5.7
) yields in
\{u_{0}>0\}

v\Delta_{p}v=\alpha^{-p}(\gamma\alpha-p(\alpha-1))=0,

and since
\{v>0\}=\{u_{0}>0\}
, we conclude that
v
is
p-
harmonic in its positivity set. As by (
5.8
) it also has a constant magnitude of gradient, then
v
is, in fact, harmonic in its positivity set. A harmonic function with a constant magnitude of the gradient is affine. Indeed, since

0=\Delta(|Dv|^{2})=2|D^{2}v|^{2},

then
D^{2}v\equiv 0
. Thus,

v(x)=a\cdot x+b.

Since
v
is nonnegative and vanishes at the origin, then
v(x)=(a\cdot x)^{+}
. Thus,
v(x)=|a|(\nu\cdot x)^{+}
, where
\nu:=a/|a|
is a unit vector. Rotating the coordinates, we get

v(x)=|a|x_{n}^{+}\,\,\,\text{on}\,\,\,B_{\frac{1}{4}}.

Therefore,

\{u_{0}=0\}=\{x_{n}=0\}.

On the other hand, by (
5.5
) and
Lemma

4.1
(note that
\frac{2-\gamma}{p-\gamma}p>1
), one should have

\infty=\mathcal{H}^{n-\frac{2-\gamma}{p-\gamma}p}\left(\{x_{n}=0\}\cap B_{\frac{1}{4}}\right)=\mathcal{H}^{n-\frac{2-\gamma}{p-\gamma}p}\left(\{u_{0}=0\}\cap B_{\frac{1}{4}}\right)=0,

a contradiction.
∎
