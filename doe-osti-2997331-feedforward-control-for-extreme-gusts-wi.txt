PROBLEM
 SETUP

OBJECTIVE

• Demonstrate feed-forward controller leveraging far-field sensing (i.e., whole-farm sensing
  as opposed to near-turbine sensing from lidar) that reduces likelihood of premature
  failure during extreme wind events as per DLCs

BASIC APPROACH

• Use a threshold yaw error for both yawing actions and shutdown decisions.
• Allows for a clear deadband to minimize unnecessary FF control action, allowing for FB
  control to do the bulk of the yaw adjustments.
• Will start with a Yaw Action Threshold of 10 degrees, and will use the V27 model’s
  MaxYawError value of 25 degrees for the shutdown threshold.

BASIC APPROACH

EXTENDING THE REACH OF THE FEEDFORWARD CONTROLLER

Yaw action
• Splitting the yaw action refers to preemptively timing the yaw action, such that half of
  the angular displacement of that action is completed prior to the future wind field
  arriving, and the other half after the future wind field has arrived.
• By splitting the yaw action, the total reach of the yaw controller is extended beyond its
  normal maxYawError.
• Compared to feedback yaw controller, this approach halves magnitude of yaw
  misalignment during large changes in wind direction.

Pitch action
• Adding pitch derating further extends the reach of the feedforward controller by
  preemptively adjusting the blade loading

IMPLEMENTATION

*Note: emergency-stop functionality disabled for these simulations

SUMMARY

SUMMARY

QoIs definitively advantaged by FF yaw + pitch:
 • Yaw-bearing moment
 • Blade-root flapwise moment
FF control strategy effects more change on DLC 1.4 than DLC 3.3
Cost/benefit analysis of the above QoI changes would help understand techno-economic
value of far-field feedforward (FFFF) control
Limitations of the study:
 • Disabled e-stop
 • Unintended dithering of blade pitch produces pessimistic predictions of peak loads in
 several QoIs, especially main-shaft torque
 • Relatively “stiff” research-scale turbine
 • Realistic processing of radar data to obtain preview horizon not considered
