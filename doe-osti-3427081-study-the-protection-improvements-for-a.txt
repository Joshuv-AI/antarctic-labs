 Project Overview Project Period: 3/2024–5/2026

Challenges: Weak grid area with high IBRs
• Very low short-circuit ratio
 (≦1.5), typical weak grid with
 high renewables
• Existing system instability (e.g.,
 oscillations)
• Frequent maloperation and
 tripping of power quality and
 protective relays.

Project goal: The National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) collaborates with Florida Power & Light
Company (FPL) to identify and predict the power system stability and protection reliability issues for a
weak grid area with high renewables and develop mitigation solutions to resolve those issues.
Project objectives: (1) Use hardware inverters to validate the black-box grid-following (GFL) and grid-
forming (GFM) inverter models. (2) Evaluate inverter-based resource (IBR) fault ride-through performance
and perform system stability analysis. (3) Evaluate the reliability and adequacy of existing protection
schemes.                                                                                                NLR | 3

  Summary of Identified Protection Challenges
• When IBRs are the only source for the fault current, relay R1 and R4 still operate
  very reliably; however, relay R2 and R3 face some challenges:
 • There is no negative-sequence current injection→ 32Q and 32P are not working.
 • The fault current is low → IBC is less than Z50P, and the mho element will not work.
 • There is a very oscillatory current → delayed responses (asserts after four cycles).
 • Fault identification logic (FID) uses 87 differential elements → no problem.

Proposed Enhanced Protection Strategies

 Strategy for SLG Fault: Prioritizing Zero-Sequence
 Directionality
• The delayed tripping during AG faults under IBR-only scenarios was due to “best choice
 ground directional element” logic (causing the delayed decision in the distance element).
• With the default ORDER = QV setting, the relay prioritizes the negative-sequence element
 32Q.
• Suggest changing the order from QV to V:
 • Disregard the 32Q and rely on the zero-sequence directional element 32V for ground faults.
 • The zero-sequence mutual coupling effect did not affect the 32V.

 Key Takeaways
• We developed a framework using EMT simulation for protection relay studies:
  Transformer modeling is critical; it requires proper model validation at each step.
• Differential elements operate reliably for all conditions (including the 87FID).
• Distance and directional elements that rely on regulated negative-sequence current face
  challenges for the IBR-only scenario, including 32Q; Z1P/Z2P/Z1G/Z2G; and some
  overcurrent supervision elements.
• The proposed enhanced protection works reliability with high security:
 • For the SLG fault: Change to ORDER = V or increase the restraining factors a2 and k2.
 • For the LL/LLG fault: enhanced phase distance element (positive phase angle, 27P
 and load encroachment).
• The second solution is to use IBRs that are IEEE 2800-2022 compliant. This is more
  practical than protection engineers rerunning protection studies and testing and updating
  relay logics.                                                                      NLR | 38
