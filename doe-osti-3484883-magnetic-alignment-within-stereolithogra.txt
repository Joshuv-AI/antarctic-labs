Executive Summary of CRADA Work:
 This research adds new capability to LLNL’s core competencies, further advancing LLNL’s research
 leadership in additive manufacturing by enabling magnetic alignment of filler particles during polymer
 vat polymerization. This work includes development of methods and materials to precisely control
 mechanical properties throughout printed components. Spatial control of stiffness, shape change,
 optical properties, etc. is used to optimize performance of parts for various applications in LLNL
 projects. For example, stiffness of novel photocured silicones was increased up to 10x, with up to 7x
 anisotropy in different directions, enabling components that effectively damp vibrations in one
 direction while being structurally supporting in another direction, reducing weight and footprint
 compared to existing designs.

 Technical Objectives:
 • Install and learn the use of Flux 3D printer with the Flux Developer software, kinetic mixing and
 heating and magnetic alignment capabilities
 • Commission the tool by performing builds, gathering data, and characterization of solids filled and
 magnetically aligned parts
 • Understanding how this new capability will further benefit the lab with later industrial partnerships

 LLNL provided feedback to Participant on the Flux Developer software and Flux 3D printer when: 1)
 testing Participant’s commercial feedstocks, 2) testing standard LLNL resins (such as PR48), and 3)
 testing specialized LLNL resins. In turn, Participant provided printing and engineering support, the
 offline Flux Developer software and a collaborative effort to generate voxel-by-voxel magnetic
 alignment 3D field maps, while assisting the evolution of the related in their Flux Developer software
 and Compass software user interface.

 Actual Accomplishments:
 All technical objectives were accomplished. The tasks were also completed:

 • Task 1: Project planning. Regularly scheduled meetings for the entire CRADA period

 • Task 2: Software testing: Flux Developer was installed and implemented, low volume
 resin baths were developed and implemented for more rapid turnaround

 • Task 3: Participant’s materials were printed satisfactorily and usage knowledge
 transferred

 • Task 4: LLNL’s materials were developed, including magnetically aligned fibers and
 shape-change particles in standard acrylates as well as novel silicones

 Technology Transfer Activities:
 This CRADA has contributed to at least two manuscripts in preparation for submission to major
 journals. Conference presentations include 2024 Fall MRS and 2025 Spring MRS. This work has led to
 numerous collaborations, including those with Duke Prof. Xiaoyue Ni, Harvard prof. Jennifer Lewis,
 Penn. Prof. Shu Yang, and Omnia Products. The materials and technologies developed, including
 integration into topology optimization, automated testing, and further materials development, are
 being further developed in ongoing internal projects.
