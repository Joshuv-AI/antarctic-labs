On a q q -Skew Amitsur’s Theorem
Aristide F. J.-C. Launois

Abstract
. Let R R be an algebra over an uncountable field, σ \sigma a locally torsion automorphism and δ \delta a locally nilpotent left σ \sigma -derivation such that q ​ σ ​ δ = δ ​ σ q\sigma\delta=\delta\sigma , where q q is a nonzero scalar. We show that the constant part of the Jacobson radical of the Ore extension R ⁡ [ x ; σ , δ ] R[x;\sigma,\delta] is nil. This partially answers a question of Greenfeld, Smoktunowicz and Ziembowski posed in 2019. As a corollary, we employ Shin’s 2024 result to prove a q-skew Amitsur’s theorem whenever the field is additionally assumed to be of characteristic zero. That is, the Jacobson radical of R ⁡ [ x ; σ , δ ] R[x;\sigma,\delta] is N ⁡ [ x ; σ , δ ] N[x;\sigma,\delta] for some nil ideal N N of R R .

1.
Introduction

Let
R
be a ring. By ring we shall mean a noncommutative associative ring not necessarily with 1. By ideal we will mean a two-sided ideal unless otherwise stated. Given an automorphism
\sigma:R\to R
, a
\sigma
-derivation
is an additive map
\delta:R\to R
satisfying the twisted Leibniz rule,

\delta(ab)=\sigma(a)\delta(b)+\delta(a)b,

for all
a,b\in R
. Throughout this paper,
\sigma:R\to R
will denote an automorphism and
\delta:R\to R
a
\sigma
-derivation. Given
R
,
\sigma
, and
\delta
, we can form the
Ore extension

R[x;\sigma,\delta]
which is a free left
R
-module with basis
1,x,x^{2},\dots
with multiplication rule defined by

xr=\sigma(r)x+\delta(r)

for all
r\in R
. Since
R[x;\sigma,\delta]
is a free left
R
-module, we can define the (left)
degree
of an element

f=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{n}x^{n}\in R[x;\sigma,\delta]

to be the largest
i
such that
a_{i}\neq 0
. We denote the degree by
\deg(f)
.

If, for every
a\in R
, there exists an
n=n(a)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\delta^{n}(a)=0
, we say that
\delta
is
locally nilpotent
. If, for every
a\in R
, there exists an
n=n(a)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sigma^{n}(a)=a
, we say that
\sigma
is
locally torsion
. Note that when
R
is an algebra over a field
k
we assume that the derivation and automorphism are
k
-linear.

There are many important objects arising in various parts of mathematics that can be described using (iterated) Ore extensions. For example, universal enveloping algebras of some Lie algebras, quantum groups, and rings of differential operators, see for instance
[
5
, Prologue]
. The Jacobson radical is the most well studied radical and allows us to understand the structure and representation theory of rings and algebras. In 1956, Amitsur proved that the Jacobson radical of a polynomial ring
J(R[x])=I[x]
, where
I=J(R[x])\cap R
is a nil ideal of
R

[
1
]
. Whilst the result is very powerful, it does not tell us what the nil ideal is. It is natural to ask if it is the largest nil ideal of the ring, the upper nilradical
\text{Nil}^{*}(R)
. It turns out that this is equivalent to another famous problem in ring theory
[
9
, Exercise 10.25]
, the Koethe conjecture. In its original form it asks whether the sum of two left nil ideals is also nil
[
7
]
which was first posed almost a century ago. In 1972, Krempa showed that the problem is equivalent to whether polynomial rings over nil rings are Jacobson radical
[
8
, Theorem 2]
. In 2000, Smoktunowicz showed that polynomial rings over nil rings need not be nil
[
12
]
. This strengthened suspicions that the Koethe conjecture may be answered in the negative in general. It is known to hold for Noetherian and P.I. rings.

Motivated by understanding the structure of Ore extensions and the Koethe conjecture, there has been work done on generalising Amitsur’s result to Ore extensions. In 1980, Bedi and Ram showed that, when
\delta=0
and
\sigma
is locally torsion, a ‘skew’ Amitsur’s theorem holds
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
. In the same paper they prove that, in general,
J(R[x;\sigma])\neq(J(R[x;\sigma])\cap R)[x;\sigma]

[
2
, Example 3.5]
. However, they gave a nice characterisation of the Jacobson radical in this case. On the contrary, in 1983, Ferrero, Kishimoto, and Motose showed that, for
\sigma=1
, the equality
J(R[x;\delta])=(J(R[x;\delta])\cap R)[x;\delta]
holds
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
. In the same paper it was shown that
J(R[x;\delta])\cap R
is nil if
R
is commutative
[
3
, Theorem 3.4]
. This was followed up to prove a skew Amitsur theorem in 2017 where Smoktunowicz showed that
J(R[x;\delta])\cap R
is nil whenever
\delta
is locally nilpotent and
R
is an algebra over a field of characteristic
p>0

[
13
, Theorem 2]
. In general, however, we cannot expect
J(R[x;\delta])\cap R
to be nil, as was shown by Smoktunowicz
[
13
, Theorem 1]
. In 2014, Smoktunowicz and Ziembowski showed that
J(R[x;\delta])\cap R
is nil whenever
\delta
is locally nilpotent and
R
is an algebra over an uncountable field
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Recently, in 2024, Shin extended the results of Bedi and Ram to give a characterisation of the Jacobson radical when
R
is an algebra over a field
k
of characteristic zero and
\delta
is
q
-skew

[
10
, Theorem 1]
, that is,
q\sigma\delta=\delta\sigma
for some
q\in k\backslash\{0\}
. In the same paper it was shown that, for an algebra over a field of characteristic
p>0
, a locally torsion automorphism
\sigma
, and a locally nilpotent 1-skew derivation
\delta
, the ideal
J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])\cap R
is nil
[
10
, Theorem 3]
. However, whether or not a q-skew Amitsur’s theorem holds in the case of a general Ore extension remains open.

We will prove the following result following the ideas of Smoktunowicz and Ziembowski in
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra where
k
is an uncountable field,
\sigma
a locally torsion
k
-algebra automorphism, and
\delta
a
k
-linear
\sigma
-derivation which is locally nilpotent and
q
-skew. Then

I=\{a\in R:ax^{t}\in J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])\}

is a nil ideal of
R
for any
t\in\mathbb{N}
.

For
t=0
, this result gives a partial answer to the following open question posed by Greenfeld, Smoktunowicz and Ziembowski in 2019
[
6
]
.

Question 2
.

[
6
, Question 6.17]

Consider
J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])\cap R
. Is it nil if we assume that
\delta
is locally nilpotent? What if we assume that
\sigma
is locally torsion?

As a corollary, we will show that a
q
-skew Amitsur’s theorem holds whenever the field is additionally assumed to be of characteristic zero.

2.
Supporting Lemmas

Recall that an element
r\in R
is
quasi-invertible
if there exists an
s\in R
such that

r+s+rs=0.

We say that
s
is the
quasi-inverse
of
r
. Since any ring can be embedded into a ring
R^{1}
with unit, this can be equivalently written as
(1+r)(1+s)=1
in
R^{1}
. It follows that quasi-inverses are unique.

Lemma 3
.

Let
s,s^{\prime}\in R
be quasi-inverses of
r\in R
. Then
s=s^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By the previous remark, in
R^{1}

(1+s)(1+r)=1=(1+r)(1+s^{\prime}).

Thus,

1+s=(1+s)(1+r)(1+s^{\prime})=1+s^{\prime}.

Hence,
s=s^{\prime}
as required.
∎

It is well known that every element of the Jacobson radical of a ring is quasi-invertible, see for instance
[
9
, Exercise 4.4]
.

In
[
6
]
, Greenfeld, Smoktunowicz and Ziembowski showed that the Ore power series ring
R[[x;\sigma,\delta]]
is well defined whenever the derivation is locally nilpotent.

Theorem
.

[
6
, Theorem 5.3]

Let
R
be a ring,
\sigma
an endomorphism of
R
and
\delta
a
\sigma
-derivation that is locally nilpotent. Then the natural extension of multiplication from
R[x;\sigma,\delta]
to
R[[x;\sigma,\delta]]
is well defined.

3.
A
q
-Skew Amitsur’s Theorem

Recall that a nil ideal is one in which every element is nilpotent. In
[
1
]
, Amitsur proved the following well-known theorem.

Theorem
.

(Amitsur’s Theorem)

Let
R
be a ring. Then

J(R[x])=(J(R[x])\cap R)[x].

Moreover,
J(R[x])\cap R
is a nil ideal of
R
.

We would like to generalise this to Ore extensions, that is,

(1)

J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])=(J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])\cap R)[x;\sigma,\delta]
;

(2)

and
J(R[x,\sigma,\delta])\cap R
is a nil ideal of
R
.

We call (1) ‘Amitsur’s property’. If both (1) & (2) hold we say that a skew Amitsur’s theorem holds.

In order for the above to even be well-defined, we note the following result of Shin. Recall that we say
\delta
is a
q
-skew

\sigma
-derivation of an algebra over a field
k
if there exists a
q\in k\backslash\{0\}
such that
q\sigma\delta=\delta\sigma
.

Lemma
.

[
10
, Lemma 1 & 2]

Let
R[x;\sigma,\delta]
be an Ore extension such that
\sigma
is an automorphism and
\delta
is
q
-skew. Then
N=J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])\cap R
is a
(\sigma,\delta)
-stable ideal. That is,
\sigma(N)\subseteq N
and
\delta(N)\subseteq N
.

We will need the following useful formulas that can be found in
[
4
, Section 2.5]
.

Proposition 4
.

[
4
, Section 2.5]

Let
\sigma
be an endomorphism of
R
and
\delta
a
q
-skew
\sigma
-derivation on
R
. Then, for any
a,b\in R
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

x^{n}a=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}_{q}\sigma^{i}\delta^{n-i}(a)x^{i}

and

\delta^{n}(ab)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}_{q}\sigma^{n-i}\delta^{i}(a)\delta^{n-i}(b),

where
\binom{n}{i}_{q}
is the evaluation at
t=q
of the polynomial function

\binom{n}{i}_{t}=\frac{(t^{n}-1)(t^{n-1}-1)\cdots(t-1)}{(t^{i}-1)(t^{i-1}-1)\cdots(t-1)(t^{n-i}-1)(t^{n-i-1}-1)\cdots(t-1)},

called a
q
-binomial coefficient
. Note that, for
q=1
, we recover the usual binomial coefficients.

Iterating this formula we obtain the following.

Lemma 5
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra where
k
is a field,
\sigma
a
k
-algebra automorphism, and
\delta
a
k
-linear

\sigma
-derivation which is
q
-skew. Then, for any
a\in R
and
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(ax^{n})^{m}=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m-1}=0}^{n}aF_{i_{m-1}}(aF_{i_{m-2}}(\cdots aF_{i_{1}}(a))))x^{i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{m-1}+n},

where we define the map
F_{i}:R\to R
by

F_{i}(a):=\binom{n}{i}_{q}\sigma^{i}\delta^{n-i}(a).

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
m
. For
m=1
, the result follows immediately by Proposition
4
. Similarly, by Proposition
4

\displaystyle(ax^{n})^{m+1}=(ax^{n})(ax^{n})^{m}

\displaystyle=ax^{n}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m-1}=0}^{n}aF_{i_{m-1}}(aF_{i_{m-2}}(aF_{i_{m-3}}(\cdots aF_{i_{1}}(a))))x^{i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{m-1}+n}

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m-1},i_{m}=0}aF_{i_{m}}(aF_{i_{m-1}}(aF_{i_{m-2}}(\cdots aF_{i_{1}}(a))))x^{i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{m}+n}.

∎

We also notice some bounds on the nilpotency degree of elements of
R
. For any
a\in R
and a locally nilpotent derivation
\delta
on
R
, we denote by
\text{nildeg}(a)=N
the number such that
\delta^{N}(a)=0
and
\delta^{N-1}(a)\neq 0
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra where
k
is a field,
\sigma
a
k
-algebra automorphism, and
\delta
a
k
-linear

\sigma
-derivation which is locally nilpotent and
q
-skew. Let
a,b\in R
and
N_{a}=\text{nildeg}(a),N_{b}=\text{nildeg}(b)
. Then

(1)

\text{nildeg}(a+b)\leq\max\{N_{a},N_{b}\}
;

(2)

\text{nildeg}(\lambda\sigma^{s}\delta^{d}(a))\leq\max\{0,N_{a}-d\}
;

(3)

\text{nildeg}(ab)\leq N_{a}+N_{b}
;

for any
\lambda\in k
and
s,d\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

(1)

Firstly,

\delta^{\max\{N_{a},N_{b}\}}(a+b)=\delta^{\max\{N_{a},N_{b}\}}(a)+\delta^{\max\{N_{a},N_{b}\}}(b)=0,

which proves the inequality.

(2)

We only need to show that
\text{nildeg}(\sigma(a))\leq N_{a}
and similarly
\text{nildeg}(\delta(a))\leq N_{a}-1
so that the result follows by induction on the degree of
\lambda\sigma^{s}\delta^{d}
. Note that

\delta^{N_{a}-1}(\delta(a))=\delta^{N_{a}}(a)=0.

Moreover,

\delta^{N_{a}}(\sigma(a))=\delta^{N_{a}-1}(q\sigma\delta(a))=q\delta^{N_{a}-1}(\sigma\delta(a))=q^{N_{a}}\sigma(\delta^{N_{a}}(a))=0,

as required.

(3)

By the product formula in Proposition
4
,

\delta^{N_{a}+N_{b}}(ab)=\sum_{i=0}^{N_{a}+N_{b}}\binom{N_{a}+N_{b}}{i}_{q}\sigma^{N_{a}+N_{b}-i}\delta^{i}(a)\delta^{N_{a}+N_{b}-i}(b)=0,

which vanishes since for all
i
we either have that
i\geq N_{a}
or we have that
N_{a}+N_{b}-i\geq N_{b}
.

∎

For
f\in R[x;\sigma,\delta]
, let
(f)_{k}
denote the (left) coefficient at
x^{k}
in
f
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra where
k
is a field,
\sigma
a
k
-algebra automorphism, and
\delta
a
k
-linear

\sigma
-derivation which is locally nilpotent and
q
-skew. Let
a\in R
and
n
be some large natural number such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(ax^{n})^{i}
has finite degree
d
and
n>\text{nildeg}(a)
. Then for all sufficiently large
m
we have that

((ax^{n})^{m})_{dn}=0.

Proof.

By Lemma
5
, we have that

((ax^{n})^{m})_{dn}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\dots,i_{m-1}=0\\
i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{m-1}+n=dn\end{subarray}}^{n}aF_{i_{m-1}}(aF_{i_{m-2}}(aF_{i_{m-3}}(\cdots aF_{i_{1}}(a)))).

In particular, the indices in the sum satisfy

i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{m-1}=(d-1)n.

Suppose that
((ax^{n})^{m})_{dn}\neq 0
. Then at least one term in the sum must be nonzero. We show that
m
is uniformly bounded from above which will prove our claim.

Let
N=\text{nildeg}(a)
and put

N_{j}:=\text{nildeg}(aF_{i_{j}}(aF_{i_{j-1}}(aF_{i_{j-2}}(\cdots aF_{i_{1}}(a)))))

for
1\leq j\leq m-1
with
N_{0}:=N
. By Lemma
6
, we have that

\displaystyle N_{j}

\displaystyle\leq N+\text{nildeg}(F_{i_{j}}(aF_{i_{j-1}}(\cdots aF_{i_{1}}(a))))

\displaystyle\leq N+N_{j-1}-(n-i_{j})

\displaystyle=N+N_{j-1}-n+i_{j}.

It follows by iterating that

\displaystyle N_{j}

\displaystyle\leq N-n+i_{j}+N_{j-1}

\displaystyle\leq N-n+i_{j}+N-n+i_{j-1}+N_{j-2}

\displaystyle=(j+1)N-jn+\sum_{k=1}^{j}i_{k}

for all
1\leq j\leq m-1
.

Noting that
N_{j}\geq 0
for all
j
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq N_{m-1}

\displaystyle\leq mN-(m-1)n+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}i_{k}

\displaystyle=mN-(m-1)n+(d-1)n

\displaystyle=-m(n-N)+dn.

Thus, since
n-N>0
by assumption,

m(n-N)\leq dn\implies m\leq\frac{dn}{n-N},

as required.
∎

We are now ready to begin the proof of Theorem
1
. The proof follows the ideas of
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra where
k
is an uncountable field,
\sigma
a
k
-algebra automorphism, and
\delta
a
k
-linear

\sigma
-derivation which is locally nilpotent and
q
-skew. Let
a\in R
such that there exists
t\in\mathbb{N}
with
ax^{t}\in J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])
. Then there exists some
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a\sigma^{n}(a)\sigma^{2n}(a)\cdots\sigma^{(d-1)n}(a)=0
for any
n\geq t
such that
n>\text{nildeg}(a)
.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrarily large natural number
n\geq t
with
n>\text{nildeg(a)}
. Then since
ax^{t}\in J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])
so is
ax^{n}
. Thus,
ax^{n}
has quasi-inverse in
R[x;\sigma,\delta]
. Note that it has a quasi-inverse in
R[[x;\sigma,\delta]]
given by

f=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-ax^{n})^{i}.

So, by Lemma
3
, these two inverses are equal. Thus,

f\in R+Rx+Rx^{2}+\cdots+Rx^{d_{1}-1},

for some
d_{1}
.

Similarly, we obtain that the quasi-inverses of
\lambda ax^{n}
are
f_{\lambda}
, where

f_{\lambda}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-\lambda ax^{n})^{i}\in R+Rx+Rx^{2}+\cdots+Rx^{d_{\lambda}-1},

for some
d_{\lambda}
and all
\lambda\in k
. Then, considering

k=\bigcup_{d=0}^{\infty}\{\lambda\in k:\deg(f_{\lambda})=d-1\}

and that
k
is uncountable it follows that, for some
d
, we have infinitely many
\lambda\in k
such that
f_{\lambda}\in R+Rx+\cdots+Rx^{d-1}
. Hence, the
x^{dn}
coefficient of
f_{\lambda}
is zero for infinitely many
\lambda\in k
.

Note that

(f_{\lambda})_{dn}=((-\lambda ax^{n})^{d})_{dn}+((-\lambda ax^{n})^{d+1})_{dn}+\cdots+((-\lambda ax^{n})^{m})_{dn}=0

and such an
m
exists by Lemma
7
.

Since
\delta
and
\sigma
are
k
-linear,

(-\lambda)^{d}((ax^{n})^{d})_{dn}+(-\lambda)^{d+1}((ax^{n})^{d+1})_{dn}+\cdots+(-\lambda)^{m}((ax^{n})^{m})_{dn}=0.

Since this is true for infinitely many
\lambda
, by a Vandermonde matrix argument, we have that
((ax^{n})^{d})_{dn}=a\sigma^{n}(a)\sigma^{2n}(a)\cdots\sigma^{(d-1)n}(a)=0.

∎

Theorem 1
.

Let
R
be a
k
-algebra where
k
is an uncountable field,
\sigma
a locally torsion
k
-algebra automorphism, and
\delta
a
k
-linear
\sigma
-derivation which is locally nilpotent and
q
-skew. Then

I=\{a\in R:ax^{t}\in J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])\}

is a nil ideal of
R
for any
t\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Let
a\in I
. By Lemma
8
, it follows that

a\sigma^{n}(a)\sigma^{2n}(a)\cdots\sigma^{(d-1)n}(a)=0,

for any
n\geq t
with
n>\text{nildeg}(a)
and some
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
\sigma
is locally torsion there exists an
m=\text{tordeg}(a)
such that
\sigma^{m}(a)=a
. We can choose
n
above to be arbitrarily large. If we choose
n
to be a nonzero multiple of
m
, then

a\sigma^{n}(a)\sigma^{2n}(a)\cdots\sigma^{(d-1)n}(a)=a^{d}=0.

∎

Noting that
J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])\cap R
is an ideal such that
ax^{t}\in J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])
for every
a\in J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])\cap R
and every
t\in\mathbb{N}
, this new result partially answers a question of Greenfeld, Smoktunowicz and Ziembowski.

Question 9
.

[
6
, Question 6.17]

Consider
J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])\cap R
. Is it nil if we assume that
\delta
is locally nilpotent? What if we assume that
\sigma
is locally torsion?

We are now in a position to prove a
q
-skew Amitsur’s Theorem.

Corollary 10
.

Let
R
be an algebra over an uncountable field of characteristic zero. Let
\sigma
and
\delta
be a locally torsion automorphism and a locally nilpotent
q
-skew
\sigma
-derivation on
R
respectively. Then

J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])=(J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])\cap R)[x;\sigma,\delta],

and
J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])\cap R
is a nil ideal of
R
.

Proof.

By
[
10
, Theorem 1]
, we have that

J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])=I\cap J(R)+I_{0},

where
I=\{a\in R:ax\in J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])\}
and
I_{0}=\{\sum_{i\geq 1}a_{i}x^{i}:a_{i}\in I\}
. By Theorem
1
, it follows that
I
is a nil ideal of
R
. Thus,
I\subseteq J(R)
. Hence,

J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])=I[x;\sigma,\delta].

Thus,
J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])\cap R=I[x;\sigma,\delta]\cap R=I
is a nil ideal of
R
and

J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])=(J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])\cap R)[x;\sigma,\delta],

as required.
∎

To end this paper we suggest some possible directions of study.

Question 11
.

Does there exist an Ore extension
R[x;\sigma,\delta]
satisfying the assumptions of Corollary
10
such that
J(R[x;\sigma,\delta])\cap R
is not the nilradical?

Question 12
.

Does Theorem
1
hold if
q\sigma\delta\neq\delta\sigma
? Does Corollary
10
hold if
\sigma
is only locally finite?

We conjecture that the above question will be answered in the affirmative.

Even in the case of
\sigma=1
the following is still open.

Question 13
.

If
R
is an algebra over a field of characteristic 0, is
J(R[x;\delta])\cap R
nil?
