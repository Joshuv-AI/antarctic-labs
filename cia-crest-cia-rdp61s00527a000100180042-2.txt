A memorandum to the Chairman entitled "Action Proposal for Review of
Multilateral Security Control Program" was presented for the members' con-
sideration by the Commerce member who called attention to the fact that the
memorandum was intended only as a working paper, not as the official view of
the Department of Commerce, and that this particular approach was based on
the belief that the principal factor on which decision must be made was in-
telligence information which could be obtained with respect to aspects of the
bloc's industrial military economy. He noted that the memorandum asked for a
thorough review and the presentation of relevant intelligence on the bloc
situation, on the basis of certain broad categories.

The State member said his comments on the paper would be addressed to the
general proposal without expressing a position on one of the possibilities
(i.e. that we would have a sector approach). If that came out of the exercise,
he would have to reserve until he had obtained the views of his agency. He
remarked that the reason we had been able previously to achieve a fairly com-
prehensive control program rather rapidly was that we and all other COCOM members
had started with a common estimate of the situation over which we were trying to
maintain controls. He felt now that we lacked this common estimate. He would
suggest that among these studies, and not least among them, should be a study
which would, if possible, find not only what the other PCs estimated the situation
now to be but also see if there was a common ground among us which we could use
as the basis for an agreed multilateral program of controls. With regard to the
proposed time schedule, he noted that he had not discussed it in State, and under-
stood that this was a work paper, not a position paper. However, his comment,
as a wo iking level comment, would be that it could not possibly be met.

With reference to the proposal that timing of U.S. proposals be for a CG
meeting no earlier than November 15, the Chairman remarked that it seemed to
him that we would not be able to hold off PCs 1 proposals in COCOM for 1958 List II
quota arrangements until this date, particularly if the current List II dis-
cussions proved extremely difficult (as anticipated) and had to be continued
after the recess. It was his judgment that we would have to say something
objective and sue for a new approach much earlier than November 15 «

The Defense member agreed with State that we had to reexamine the program
and determine whether our objectives could be sold, or we had little chance of
doing anything on an item basis, or any other basis. He suggested that this was
a veiy important assignment for the State Department.

The Chairman inquired of CIA whether the intelligence review envisaged by
para. 2 of the Commerce member* s paper was an assignment which the intelligence
community could accept and on which they could give some relevant data and
studies within a reasonable length of time, the Commerce member having suggested
