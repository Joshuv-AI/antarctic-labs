Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

Let
p
be any prime number and
\wp
be the maximal ideal in
\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. Let
v
be the
p
-adic valuation on
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
normalised such that
v(p)=1
. Let
G_{p}
denote the absolute Galois group
{\mathrm{Gal}}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}|\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.
Let
E
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and let
a_{p}\in E
satisfy
\nu:=v(a_{p})>0
. Given an integer
k\geq 2
and
a_{p}
as above, the symbol
V_{k,a_{p}}
denotes the unique irreducible two-dimensional crystalline representation of
G_{p}
with Hodge-Tate weights
0
and
k-1
, such that the characteristic polynomial of the crystalline Frobenius is
X^{2}-a_{p}X+p^{k-1}
. The value
\nu=v(a_{p})
is referred to as the ‘slope’ of the representation
V_{k,a_{p}}
.
One can choose a Galois stable integral lattice in
V_{k,a_{p}}
and reduce it modulo
\wp
. The semisimplification of this reduction of
V_{k,a_{p}}
, denoted by
\bar{V}_{k,a_{p}}
, is known to be independent of the choice of the lattice. For odd primes or sufficiently large primes, the explicit structure of
\bar{V}_{k,a_{p}}
under various conditions can be found in the literature. However, due to technical difficulties, the prime
p=2
has been avoided in most of these works. In this article we attempt to fill this gap in the existing literature, by treating the problem for
p=2
at small slopes.

The method of using local Langlands correspondence (LLC) for the reduction of Galois representations was first introduced by Breuil in 2003
[
B03b
]
.
In this method, one reduces modulo
p
the unitary
p
-adic Banach space representation
B(V_{k,a_{p}})
which is attached to
V_{k,a_{p}}
via the
p
-adic LLC; a functorial construction of the
p
-adic LLC using
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules can be found in
[
C10
]
. Berger proved in
[
B10
]
that the reduction
\overline{B(V_{k,a_{p}})}
lies in the image of mod-
p
LLC and corresponds to
\bar{V}_{k,a_{p}}
under the same. Since mod
p
LLC is an injective correspondence, it is enough to compute
\overline{B(V_{k,a_{p}})}
to determine
\bar{V}_{k,a_{p}}
.
This way of computing the reduction in the ‘other side’ of LLC is found to be particularly effective at small slopes, applied in the papers
[
BG09
]
,
[
BG13
]
,
[
GG15
]
,
[
BG15
]
,
[
BGR18
]
,
[
A21
]
,
[
GR25
]
,
[
NP25
]
. More details about our methodology can be found in §2, with focus on the special prime
p=2
.

Before stating our main theorems, we will introduce some notations. Let
I_{p}
be the inertia subgroup of
G_{p}
and

\omega_{1},\omega_{2}:I_{p}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}
denote the fundamental characters of level one and two respectively. Note that
\omega_{1}
can be extended to all of
G_{p}
as the cyclotomic character mod
p
, also denoted by
\omega_{1}
here. For
n\geq 1
and
p+1\nmid n
, let
\rho=\mathrm{ind}(\omega_{2}^{n})
denote the unique irreducible mod
p
representation of
G_{p}
such that
\det(\rho)=\omega_{1}^{n}
and
\rho|_{I_{p}}=\omega_{2}^{n}\oplus\omega_{2}^{np}
. For
p=2
, the character
\omega_{2}
is of order
3
. Let us also mention here that for
p=2
, the fundamental character of level one is the trivial character on
G_{p}
and up to twists by unramified characters, any irreducible two-dimensional representation of
G_{p}
over
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{ind}(\omega_{2})
. Now let us state our first result, proved in §3, giving the complete structure of the reduction
\bar{V}_{k,a_{2}}
when the slope
v(a_{2})
is less than
1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k\geq 4
,
0<v(a_{2})<1
. For
r:=k-2
, let
\alpha^{\prime}:=\frac{a_{2}^{2}-2r^{2}}{2a_{2}}
,
\tau^{\prime}:=v\left(\alpha^{\prime}\right)
and
t:=v(r-1)
. Then we have:

(1)

If
\tau^{\prime}<t
, then
\bar{V}_{k,a_{2}}\cong\mathrm{ind}(\omega_{2})
.

(2)

If
\tau^{\prime}\geq t
, then
\bar{V}_{k,a_{2}}\cong\mu_{\lambda}\oplus\mu_{\lambda^{-1}}
,

where
\lambda\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}_{2}
is determined by the equation
\lambda^{2}+c\lambda+1=0
with
c=\overline{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{r-1}}\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{2}.

An easy analysis of the quantity
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}(k,a_{2})
and its valuation
\tau^{\prime}
defined above leads us to the following quick consequences:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let us assume
k\geq 2
and
0<v(a_{2})<1
. Then

(a) if
k
is even, the reduction
\bar{V}_{k,a_{2}}
must be irreducible.

(b) if
\bar{V}_{k,a_{2}}
is reducible, then
k
is odd and the slope
v(a_{2})=1/2
.

We can compare Theorem
1.1
with the main result in
[
BG13
]
, where a similar dichotomy is stated for odd primes, slopes in
(0,1)
and
k\equiv 3\mod(p-1)
. There the shape of
\bar{V}_{k,a_{p}}
is shown to be determined by
\tau:=v(\alpha)
, where
\alpha:=\frac{a_{p}^{2}-pr}{pa_{p}}
. This can also be seen as a special case of the Zig-zag phenomenon conjectured in
[
G21
]
and proved in
[
G22
]
. However, note that the relevant expression
\alpha^{\prime}
in Theorem
1.1
above is slightly different from
\alpha
, or the general expression in the Zig-zag conjecture. In fact for
p=2
, the values
t=v(r-1)
and
\tau=v(\alpha)
solely cannot determine the structure of
\bar{V}_{k,a_{2}}
in all cases. For a comparative study, we refer to the examples in Table

.

Next let us state our second result giving a complete structure of
\bar{V}_{k,a_{2}}
when slope

equals
1
. The proof of this theorem can be found in §4, and some relevant numerical examples can be found in Table

,
\lx@sectionsign 5
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
k\geq 4
and
v(a_{2})=1
. For
r:=k-2
, let

\alpha:=\frac{a_{2}^{2}-2^{2}{r\choose 2}}{2a_{2}},\tau:=v(\alpha)
and
t:=v(r-2)
. Then we have:

(1)

If
\tau\leq t-1
, then
\bar{V}_{k,a_{2}}\cong\mu_{\lambda}\oplus\mu_{\lambda^{-1}}
, where
\lambda\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{\times}
satisfies
\lambda^{2}-c\lambda+1=0
,

with
c=\overline{\frac{a_{2}}{2}+\frac{r-2}{2\alpha}}
unless
r-2=\alpha=0
, in which case we take
c=\overline{\frac{a_{2}}{2}}
.

(2)

If
\tau>t-1,\,\bar{V}_{k,a_{2}}\cong\mathrm{ind}(\omega_{2})
.

To avoid confusion, we caution the reader that the symbol
t
here is different from that in Theorem
1.1
. Analysing the valuation of
\alpha=\alpha(k,a_{2})
defined in Theorem
1.3
, we observe the following, which is in contrast with part
(a)
of Corollary
1.2
above.

Corollary 1.4
.

If
v(a_{2})=1
and
k\geq 3
is odd, then
\bar{V}_{k,a_{2}}
must be irreducible.

Theorem
1.3
can be compared with similar cases for odd primes, e.g., the ‘
b=2
’ part of Theorem 1.1 of
[
BGR18
]
, or in general with the
\nu=1
case of the Zigzag conjecture. We note that though the determining factors
\tau=v(\alpha)
and
t=v(k-4)
remain the same for odd primes (
>3
) and
p=2
, the behavior of the general reduction
\bar{V}_{k,a_{p}}
with variation of
\tau
and
t
are not quite the same for odd and even primes. From both the theorems above, we observe a very common trend in number theory: the even prime
p=2
behaves differently than the other primes.

Let us mention here that the condition
k\geq 4
in the main theorems is justified since the reduction is well-known for
2\leq k\leq 3=p+1
by a general result of Fontaine and Edixhoven in
[
E92
]
valid for all primes including
2
. The same result can be used to check the Corollaries
1.2
and
1.4
for
k=2
and
3
. The smallest weight treated in this paper is
k=4
, for which our answer coincides with the one predicted by Breuil in a comment after Theorem
1.4
in
[
B03b
]
. We also note that by the main result of
[
BLZ04
]
and
[
BL22
]
, the shape of
\bar{V}_{k,a_{2}}
is known when
v(a_{2})>\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\rfloor
. However, the slope range
(0,1]
treated in this article is even lower than this bound for all weights
k\geq 4
.

Now let us give a brief outline of the proof of our theorems. Applying Local Langlands correspondence, the problem of determining
\bar{V}_{k,a_{2}}
reduces to computing the reduction of certain lattice
\Theta_{k,a_{2}}
in the automorphic side. One knows that the reduction
\bar{\Theta}_{k,a_{2}}
is a quotient of an induced representation
\mathcal{I}(V_{r})
, with
r=k-2
. For notations
V_{r}
, we refer to
\lx@sectionsign 2
below. Further in the case of slope
<1
, we checked that
\bar{\Theta}_{k,a_{2}}
is in fact a quotient of
\mathcal{I}(V_{0})
. Then the most tricky part of the proof is to distinguish between the possibilities of reducible and irreducible reductions, discussed in detail in
\lx@sectionsign 3
. For the case of slope one, discussed in
\lx@sectionsign 4
, our preliminary analysis shows that generically (for
r>4
) two sub-quotients
\mathcal{I}(V_{0})
and
\mathcal{I}(V_{1})
of
\mathcal{I}(V_{r})
can possibly contribute to
\bar{\Theta}_{k,a_{2}}
. Further we show that in most cases
\mathcal{I}(V_{1})
contributes, and
\bar{\Theta}_{k,a_{2}}

is either reducible or irreducible depending on whether
\tau\leq t-1
or
\tau>t-1
. However, in some special cases, in addition to
\mathcal{I}(V_{1})
the factor
\mathcal{I}(V_{0})
may also contribute to
\bar{\Theta}_{k,a_{2}}
resulting in a reducible answer. This can possibly occur only if
\tau=t-1
and moreover if
\overline{2/a_{2}}=\overline{(2-r)/2\alpha}\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{\times}
.

The primary hindrance as to why the methodology for odd primes could not be emulated in the case of
p=2
is technical. For instance, the identity
\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}[\mu]=0
is significantly used in the calculation for odd primes whereas it fails trivially for
p=2
. Also some of the combinatorial lemmas which were crucial in the analysis of functions on the Bruhat-Tits tree in
[
BG13
]
and
[
BGR18
]
do not hold as such for
p=2
. We have given the modified versions of these lemmas for
p=2
in this paper. While the modification appears to be minor in some cases, it has a non-trivial impact on the calculation leading to completely different results for
p=2
.

2.
background

In this section, we will briefly recall the method of applying the local Langlands correspondence to compute the mod-
p
reduction of crystalline representations. This method is applicable for all primes, but in this paper we will consider the specific case of
p=2
.

2.1.
Hecke operators and mod-2 LLC

Let
G
and
K
denote the groups
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
respectively. We shall denote the compact induction functor
\mathrm{ind}_{K\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}}^{G}
by
\mathcal{I}
, with
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
being the centre of the group
G
. For any
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra
R
, the symmetric power
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}R^{2}
is modeled by the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
r
in two variables
X
and
Y
over
R
. The group
K
acts on the polynomials in
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}R^{2}
by

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot f(X,Y)=f(aX+cY,bX+dY).

This action is extended to an action of
K\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
by making the scalar matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right)
act trivially.
Given
g\in G
and
v=v(X,Y)\in{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}{R^{2}}
, the symbol
\left[g,v\right]
denotes the
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}R^{2}
-valued function on
G
defined as follows:

[g,v](g^{\prime})=\begin{cases}(g^{\prime}g)\cdot v&\text{ if }g^{\prime}\in K{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}g^{-1}\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is known that a general element of
\mathcal{I}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}R^{2})
is a finite sum of elementary functions of the above type. Next let us recall the Hecke operator
T
, a distinguished operator acting on
\mathcal{I}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}R^{2})
defined by the formula:

(2.1)

T[g,v(X,Y)]=\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}:\mu^{p}=\mu}\left[g\begin{pmatrix}p&\mu\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},v(X,pY-\mu X)\right]+\left[g\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{pmatrix},v(pX,Y)\right]

It can be checked that
T
is a
G
-linear map from
\mathcal{I}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}R^{2})
to itself.

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the group
\Gamma:={\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
acts naturally on the space
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2})
, and this action can be further inflated to an action of
K
. Let this representation space
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{2})
be denoted by the symbol
V_{r}
. If
0\leq r\leq p-1
is an integer,
\lambda\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and
\eta:\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}
is a smooth character, then we know

\pi(r,\lambda,\eta):=\frac{\mathcal{I}(V_{r})}{(T-\lambda)}\otimes\eta

is a smooth admissible representation of
G
over
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and is reducible if and only if
(r,\lambda)\in\{(0,\pm 1),(p-1,\pm 1)\}
. If irreducible, these representations are known as principal series when
\lambda\neq 0
and supercuspidals when
\lambda=0
. All smooth admissible irreducible representations of
G
are sub-quotients of
\pi(r,\lambda,\eta)
for some tuple
(r,\lambda,\eta)
due to work of Barthel-Livne
[
BL94
]
and Breuil
[
B03a
]
. For a complete statement of classification of smooth admissible irreducible representations of
G
, we refer to Theorem
4.4
of
[
Bre07
]
.

We shall now discuss the semi-simple mod-
p
local Langlands correspondence. A general statement can be found in definition
1.1
of
[
B03b
]
. Here we will state the specific version for
p=2
. Let
\lambda\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{\times}
and
\eta
be a smooth character of
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\times}
. Then the mod-
2
local Langlands correspondence can be stated as:

Galois side

Automorphic side

\displaystyle\mathrm{ind}(\omega_{2})\otimes\eta

\displaystyle\leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\pi(0,0,\eta)

\displaystyle(\mu_{\lambda}\oplus\mu_{\lambda^{-1}})\otimes\eta

\displaystyle\leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\pi(0,\lambda,\eta)^{ss}\oplus\pi(0,\lambda^{-1},\eta)^{ss}

We note that unlike the odd primes, the statement of mod
p
LLC for
p=2
does not involve a variable
r
. This is not surprising since any irreducible two-dimensional representation of
G_{p}
for
p=2
can be expressed as
\mathrm{ind}(\omega_{2})\otimes\eta
and one knows that in the automorphic side
\pi(1,\lambda,\eta)^{ss}\cong\pi(0,\lambda,\eta)^{ss}
. The later isomorphism can be verified from Theorem
1.3
of
[
B03a
]
for supercuspidals; part
2(b)
of corollary
36
of
[
BL94
]
for principal series, and the description of composition factors for the reducible cases given in parts
2(b)
and
2(c)
of Theorem
30
of
[
BL94
]
.

Let
B(V_{k,a_{2}})
be the unitary
2
-adic Banach space representation attached to
V_{k,a_{2}}
in the sense of
[
BB10
]
. We recall that by the compatibility of
p
-adic and mod-
p
LLC by Breuil and Berger, the image of
\bar{V}_{k,a_{2}}
under mod-
2
LLC coincides with
\overline{B(V_{k,a_{2}})}
.
We now consider the locally algebraic representation
\Pi_{k,a_{2}}
:=
\dfrac{\mathcal{I}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{k-2}\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{2}^{2})}{(T-a_{2})}
and let the image of
\mathcal{I}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{k-2}\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{2})
in
\Pi_{k,a_{2}}
be denoted by
\Theta_{k,a_{2}}
. The explicit description for
\Theta_{k,a_{2}}
is given by the following formula:

\Theta_{k,a_{2}}=\frac{\mathcal{I}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{k-2}\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{2})}{(T-a_{2})(\mathcal{I}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{k-2}\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{2}^{2}))\cap\mathcal{I}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{k-2}\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{2})}.

One knows that

\Theta_{k,a_{2}}
is a lattice in
\Pi_{k,a_{2}}
in the sense of
[
B03b
]
. Further
B(V_{k,a_{2}})
is the completion of
\Pi_{k,a_{2}}
with respect to the ‘gauge’ of
\Theta_{k,a_{2}}
in the context of locally convex topology. The semi-simplified reduction
\overline{B(V_{k,a_{2}})}
is isomorphic to
\bar{\Theta}_{k,a_{2}}^{ss}:=(\Theta_{k,a_{2}}\otimes\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{2})^{ss}
.
Thus to compute
\bar{V}_{k,a_{2}}
, it is enough to compute
\bar{\Theta}_{k,a_{2}}^{ss}
and then use the injectivity property of mod-
2
LLC.

2.2.
Filtration of
V_{r}

There is a natural map
P:\mathcal{I}(V_{r})\to\bar{\Theta}:=\bar{\Theta}_{k,a_{2}}.

Let us define

\theta(X,Y):=X^{2}Y-XY^{2},
which is fixed under the action of

{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
. Then the submodules

V_{r}^{(n)}:=\begin{cases}\theta^{n}V_{r-3n}&\text{ if }r\geq 3n\\
0&\text{ if }r<3n\end{cases}

for
n\geq 0
define a filtration of
V_{r}
. The following lemma enables us to characterize elements in
V_{r}^{(n)}
for
n=1
and
2
, which will be particularly useful in our context.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
S(X,Y)=\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j\leq r}a_{j}X^{r-j}Y^{j}\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[X,Y]
. Then

(1)

S\in V_{r}^{(1)}\iff a_{0}=a_{r}=0
and
\sum_{j}a_{j}=0\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{2}

(2)

S\in V_{r}^{(2)}\iff a_{0}=a_{1}=a_{r-1}=a_{r}=0
and

\displaystyle\sum_{j}a_{j}=\sum_{j}ja_{j}=0\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{2},\,i.e.,\displaystyle\sum_{j\text{ even }}a_{j}=0
and
\displaystyle\sum_{j\text{ odd }}a_{j}=0
in
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
.

The proof of the above lemma is elementary and is left to the reader. Let
X_{r-m}:=\langle X^{r-m}Y^{m}\rangle_{K}\subseteq V_{r}
for
0\leq m\leq r
. With this notation, we now recall Remark
4.4
from
[
BG09
]
for a general prime
p
and state the version specific for
p=2
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
v(a_{2})<n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\mathcal{I}(V_{r}^{(n)})\subseteq\ker P
.

Further if
0\leq m<v(a_{2})
and
r\geq 3m+2
, then
\mathcal{I}(X_{r-m})\subseteq\ker P
.

We now state a proposition which gives the structure of
V_{r}/V_{r}^{(1)}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

For
r\geq 2
, we have a split short exact sequence of
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[\Gamma]
-modules:

(2.2)

0\rightarrow V_{1}\rightarrow\frac{V_{r}}{V_{r}^{(1)}}\rightarrow V_{0}\rightarrow 0,

where the injection is given by

X\longmapsto\overline{X^{r}},\,Y\longmapsto\overline{Y^{r}}
and the surjection is given by
\underset{0\leq j\leq r}{\sum}c_{j}\overline{X^{r-j}Y^{j}}\longmapsto\underset{1\leq j\leq r-1}{\sum}c_{j}\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{2}.

Proof.

Let
X_{r}^{(1)}:=X_{r}\cap V_{r}^{(1)}
. We have
X_{r}/X_{r}^{(1)}=\langle\overline{X^{r}},\overline{Y^{r}}\rangle_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{2}}.
We have
V_{1}\cong\frac{X_{r}}{X_{r}^{(1)}}
is a submodule of
\frac{V_{r}}{V_{r}^{(1)}}
by
(4.5)
in
[
G78
]
. By dimension count, the quotient
\frac{V_{r}}{X_{r}+V_{r}^{(1)}}
is one-dimensional and hence isomorphic to
V_{0}
. Thus the short exact sequence follows. The one-dimensional fixed subspaces of upper unipotents in
V_{1}
and
\frac{X_{r}}{X_{r}^{(1)}}
are spanned by
X
and
\overline{X^{r}}
respectively. By
K
-linearity, the elements
X
and
Y=w.X
map to
\overline{X^{r}}
and
\overline{Y^{r}}
respectively up to a scalar. By exactness, both the elements
\overline{X^{r}},\overline{Y^{r}}\in\frac{V_{r}}{V_{r}^{(1)}}
map to zero in
V_{0}
. We note that the space
V_{r}/V_{r}^{(1)}
is spanned by the images of
X^{r},Y^{r}
and
X^{r-1}Y
which are linearly independent. Since the surjection map is non-zero, the image of
X^{r-1}Y
must map to
1
up to a scalar. Now the result follows as
X^{r-j}Y^{j}\equiv X^{r-1}Y\mod V_{r}^{(1)}
for
1\leq j\leq r-1
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Note that for all
n\geq 1
and
r\geq 3n+2
, we have
V_{r}^{(n)}/V_{r}^{(n+1)}\cong V_{r-3n}/V_{r-3n}^{(1)}
and therefore the structure of
V_{r}^{(n)}/V_{r}^{(n+1)}
for any
n
is also given by (
2.2
) above.

2.3.
Analysis of the Hecke operator
T

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-algebra. The induction space
\mathcal{I}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}R^{2})
can be identified with the space of finitely supported
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}R^{2}
- valued functions on the Bruhat-Tits tree of
G=GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{2})
. This is known to be a tree of uniform valency
3
with its vertices corresponding to integral lattices in
\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{2}
up to homothety. It follows that the vertices are in one to one correspondence with the cosets of
G/K\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
for
p=2

Let us define

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
P(X,Y)\in{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}R^{2}
where
R
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-algebra. Let
\lambda\in I_{n},\,n\geq 0
.
Then the action of
T=T^{+}+T^{-}
on
\mathcal{I}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}(R^{2}))
can be explicitly stated as follows:

\displaystyle T^{+}\left[g_{n,\lambda}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[g_{n+1,\lambda}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,2Y)\right]+\left[g_{n+1,\lambda+2^{n}}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,2Y-X)\right]\,\forall\,n\geq 0,

\displaystyle T^{-}\left[g_{n,\lambda}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left[g_{n-1,\lambda}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(2X,Y)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n-1}\\
\left[g_{n-1,[\lambda]_{n-1}}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(2X,X+Y)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n}\setminus I_{n-1}\end{cases}\forall\,n\geq 1,

\displaystyle T^{+}\left[g_{n,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[g_{n+1,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(2X,Y)\right]+\left[g_{n+1,\lambda+2^{n}}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(2X-Y,Y)\right]\,\forall\,n\geq 0,

\displaystyle T^{-}\left[g_{n,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left[g_{n-1,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(X,2Y)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n-1}\\
\left[g_{n-1,[\lambda]_{n-1}}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(X+Y,2Y)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n}\setminus I_{n-1}\end{cases}\,\forall\,n\geq 1,

\displaystyle T^{-}\left[g_{0,0}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[g_{0,0}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(2X,Y)\right],

\displaystyle T^{-}\left[g_{0,0}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[g_{0,0}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,2Y)\right].

Proof.

We use the description of
T^{+}
and
T^{-}
given in (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) with
g=g_{n,\lambda}^{i}
with
\lambda\in I_{n}
,
n\geq 0
and for
i=0
or
1
. The last two formulae follow directly, as we know that
g_{0,0}^{0}\cdot g_{0,0}^{1}=g_{0,0}^{1}
and
g_{0,0}^{1}\cdot g_{1,0}^{0}=g_{0,0}^{0}
. For the rest, we check that

\displaystyle g_{n,\lambda}^{0}\cdot g_{1,0}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{n+1,\lambda}^{0}\text{ and }

\displaystyle g_{n,\lambda}^{0}\cdot g_{1,1}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{n+1,\,\lambda+2^{n}}^{0}\text{ for }n\geq 0,

\displaystyle g_{n,\lambda}^{0}\cdot g_{0,0}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2^{n}&2\lambda\\
0&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\begin{cases}2\cdot g_{n-1,\lambda}^{0}&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n-1},\\
2\cdot g_{n-1,[\lambda]_{n-1}}^{0}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n}\setminus I_{n-1}\end{cases}\text{ for }n\geq 1.

Now applying (
2.3
) with the matrix identities above, we have

\displaystyle T^{+}\left[g_{n,\lambda}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,2Y)\right]+\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda+2^{n}}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,2Y-X)\right]\text{ for }n\geq 0.

\displaystyle T^{-}\left[g_{n,\lambda}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left[2\cdot g_{n-1,\lambda}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(2X,Y)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n-1},\\
\left[2\cdot g_{n-1,[\lambda]_{n-1}}^{0}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right),\kern 5.0ptP(2X,Y)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n}\setminus I_{n-1}.\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left[g_{n-1,\lambda}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(2X,Y)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n-1},\\
\left[g_{n-1,[\lambda]_{n-1}}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(2X,X+Y)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n}\setminus I_{n-1}\end{cases}\text{ for }n\geq 1.

In the last step we use that
2\in\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\times}
acts trivially on
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}(R^{2})
, the matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in K
takes
P(2X,Y)
to
P(2X,X+Y)
.

Similarly on the left side of the tree, we have

\displaystyle g_{n,\lambda}^{1}\cdot g_{1,0}^{0}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&0\\
2^{2}\lambda&2^{n+1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}2\cdot g_{n-1,\,\lambda}^{1}&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n-1},\\
2\cdot g_{n-1,\,[\lambda]_{n-1}}^{1}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n}\setminus I_{n-1}\end{cases}\text{ for }n\geq 1,

\displaystyle g_{n,\lambda}^{1}\cdot g_{1,1}^{0}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\
2^{2}\lambda&\>\>2\lambda+2^{n+1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{n+1,\,\lambda+2^{n}}^{1}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\
-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\text{ and}

\displaystyle g_{n,\lambda}^{1}\cdot g_{0,0}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{n+1,\,\lambda}^{1}\text{ for }n\geq 0.

Hence by formula (
2.4
) and the matrix identities above we obtain

\displaystyle T^{+}\left[g_{n,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(2X,Y)\right]+\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda+2^{n}}^{1},\,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\
-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot P(X,2Y-X)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(2X,Y)\right]+\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda+2^{n}}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(2X-Y,Y)\right]\text{ for }n\geq 0,

since the matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\
-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in K
takes
P(X,2Y-X)
to
P(2X-Y,Y)
. Further we have

\displaystyle T^{-}\left[g_{n,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left[2\cdot g_{n-1,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(X,2Y)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n-1},\\
\left[[2\cdot g_{n-1,\,[\lambda]_{n-1}}^{1},\,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot P(X,2Y)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n}\setminus I_{n-1},\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left[g_{n-1,\,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(X,2Y)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n-1},\\
\left[g_{n-1,\,[\lambda]_{n-1}}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(X+Y,2Y)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n}\setminus I_{n-1}\end{cases}\text{ for }n\geq 1.

The last step follows since
2\in\mathbb{Q}_{2}^{\times}
acts trivially on
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}(R^{2})
and the matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in K
takes
P(X,2Y)
to
P(X+Y,2Y)
.
∎

The following lemma is to be used while finding the image of the Hecke operator by transferring the computation from the left side of the tree to the right side of the same.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
\lambda\in I_{n}
,
w=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and let
P(X,Y)\in{\mathrm{Sym}}^{r}R^{2}
where
R
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-algebra. Then we have:

(2.5)

\displaystyle T^{+}\left[g_{n,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptw\cdot P(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&\lambda\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot T^{+}\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right]

(2.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&\lambda-2^{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot T^{+}\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda+2^{n}}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right].

(2.7)

\displaystyle T^{-}\left[g_{n,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptw\cdot P(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&\lambda\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot T^{-}\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right]

(2.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&\lambda-2^{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot T^{-}\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda+2^{n}}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right].

Proof.

If
\lambda\in I_{n}
for some
n\geq 0
, then we note that

(2.9)

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&\lambda\end{pmatrix}\cdot g_{n+2,\,\lambda}^{0}=g_{n+1,\,\lambda}^{1}\cdot w\text{ and }\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&\lambda\end{pmatrix}\cdot g_{n+2,\,\lambda+2^{n+1}}^{0}=g_{n+1,\,\lambda+2^{n}}^{1}\cdot w.

Using the formula for
T^{+}
in Lemma
2.5
and the matrix identities above, we get that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&\lambda\end{pmatrix}\cdot T^{+}\left[g_{n+1,\lambda}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[g_{n+1,\lambda}^{1}\cdot w,\kern 5.0ptP(X,2Y)\right]+\left[g_{n+1,\lambda+2^{n}}^{1}\cdot w,\kern 5.0ptP(X,2Y-X)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[g_{n+1,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(Y,2X)\right]+\left[g_{n+1,\lambda+2^{n}}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(Y,2X-Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{+}\left[g_{n,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptw\cdot P(X,Y)\right].

Now using Lemma
2.5
, and the matrix identities

(2.10)

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&\lambda-2^{n}\end{pmatrix}\cdot g_{n+2,\,\lambda+2^{n}}^{0}=g_{n+1,\lambda}^{1}\cdot w\text{ and }\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&\lambda-2^{n}\end{pmatrix}\cdot g_{n+2,\,\lambda+2^{n}+2^{n+1}}^{0}=g_{n+1,\,\lambda+2^{n}}^{1}\cdot w\hskip 4.2679pt\forall\,n\geq 0,

we get that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&\lambda-2^{n}\end{pmatrix}\cdot T^{+}\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda+2^{n}}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda}^{1}\cdot w,\kern 5.0ptP(X,2Y)\right]+\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda+2^{n}}^{1}\cdot w,\kern 5.0ptP(X,2Y-X)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(Y,2X)\right]+\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda+2^{n}}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(Y,2X-Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{+}\left[g_{n,\,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptw\cdot P(X,Y)\right].

To find the explicit expression for
T^{-}
, we first consider the matrix identity

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&\lambda\end{pmatrix}\cdot g_{n,\lambda}^{0}=\begin{cases}g_{n-1,\,\lambda}^{1}\cdot w&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n-1},\\
g_{n-1,\,[\lambda]_{n-1}}^{1}\cdot w\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n}\setminus I_{n-1}.\end{cases}

Using the above identity and Lemma
2.5
twice, we get that

\displaystyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&\lambda\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cdot T^{-}\left[g_{n+1,\,\lambda}^{0},\kern 5.0ptP(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left[g_{n-1,\,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptw\cdot P(2X,Y)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n-1}\\
\left[g_{n-1,\,[\lambda]_{n-1}}^{1},\kern 5.0ptw\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\cdot P(2X,Y)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n}\setminus I_{n-1}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left[g_{n-1,\,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(2Y,X)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n-1}\\
\left[g_{n-1,\,[\lambda]_{n-1}}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(2Y,X+Y)\right]&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n}\setminus I_{n-1}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{-}\left[g_{n,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptP(Y,X)\right]=\,T^{-}\left[g_{n,\lambda}^{1},\kern 5.0ptw\cdot P(X,Y)\right].

Similarly using Lemma
2.5
and the matrix identities

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&\lambda-2^{n}\end{pmatrix}\cdot g_{n,\lambda}^{0}=\begin{cases}g_{n-1,\lambda}^{1}\cdot w\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\

g_{n-1,[\lambda]_{n-1}}^{1}\cdot w&\text{ if }\lambda\in I_{n}\setminus I_{n-1}\end{cases}

we get equation (
2.8
).

We shall now state a few combinatorial lemmas which are crucial in our analysis.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
1\leq n\leq r-1
and
t=v(r-1)
,

(3.1)

v\left({r-1\choose n}\right)+n\geq t+1.

Proof.

We check that

\displaystyle n+v\left({r-1\choose n}\right)

\displaystyle=n+v\left(\frac{(r-1)!}{n!(r-1-n)!}\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle n+\sum_{i=1}^{n}v(r-i)-v(n!)

\displaystyle\geq n+v(r-1)-v(n!)

\displaystyle=t+n-v(n!)

Using Legendre’s formula, we get that
n-v(n!)=S_{2}(n)
, where
S_{2}(n)
is the sum of digits in the
2
-adic expansion of
n
. Clearly
S_{2}(n)\geq 1
. Therefore

v({r-1\choose n})+n\geq t+1.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

The lemma above is non-trivial only if
r
is odd. It is vacuously true if
r
is even, i.e.,
t=0
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
3\leq n\leq r
and
t=v(r-1)
we have

(3.2)

v\left({r\choose n}\right)+n\geq t+2.

Proof.

For
n\geq 3
, we note that

\displaystyle n+v\left({r\choose n}\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle n+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}v(r-i)-v(n!)

\displaystyle=S_{2}(n)+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}v(r-i).

If
r
is even, then the RHS above is
\geq 1+v(r)+v(r-1)+v(r-2)\geq 3=t+3>t+2
. If
r
is odd and
n=3
, then
S_{2}(n)=2
and so the RHS

=2+v(r-1)=t+2
.
If
r
is odd and
n>3
, then RHS
\geq 1+v(r-1)+v(r-3)\geq t+2
.
∎

\displaystyle F(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y^{r}-X^{r-1}Y

(3.4)

\displaystyle G(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X^{r}-XY^{r-1}

(3.5)

\displaystyle H(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{0<j<r}{r\choose j}X^{r-j}Y^{j}

(3.6)

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(X,Y)-rX^{r-1}Y-rX^{r-2}Y^{2}

(3.7)

\displaystyle H^{\prime\prime}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(Y,X)=H(X,Y)-rXY^{r-1}-rX^{2}Y^{r-2}.

Keeping in mind the formula for the Hecke operator
T
, we next compute few congruences for the polynomials defined above. Let

\delta:\mathbb{N}\to\{0,1\}
be the characteristic function defined as

(3.8)

\delta(r)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }r\text{ is even,}\\
0&\text{ if }r\text{ is odd.}\end{cases}

Lemma 3.4
.

We have the following congruences modulo
2^{t+2}
where
t=v(r-1)
:

(a)

F(X,2Y)\equiv-2X^{r-1}Y
.

(b)

F(X,2Y-X)\equiv 2\delta(r)X^{r}+2(r-1)X^{r-1}Y-2r(r-1)X^{r-2}Y^{2}.

(c)

F(2X,Y)\equiv\begin{cases}Y^{r}-2^{r-1}X^{r-1}Y&\text{ if }r=2,3\\
Y^{r}&\text{ for }r\geq 4.\end{cases}

Proof.

For all
r>1
, we have
r>r-2\geq v(r-1)=t
, thus
r\geq t+2
. Therefore

\displaystyle F(X,2Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{r}Y^{r}-2X^{r-1}Y\equiv-2X^{r-1}Y\mod 2^{t+2}.

For part
(b)
, we check that

\displaystyle F(X,2Y-X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2Y-X)^{r}-X^{r-1}(2Y-X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r}2^{j}{r\choose j}(-X)^{r-j}Y^{j}-2X^{r-1}Y+X^{r}

\displaystyle\equiv

We shall now state a combinatorial lemma which shall be used extensively in the subsequent lemmas and propositions.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
r\geq 3
, and
t:=v(r-2)
. Then for all

, we have
v\left({r\choose j}\right)+j\geq t+2

Proof.

By applying Legendre’s formula, if

(4.7)

v\left({r\choose j}\right)+j=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}v(r-i)\right)+j-v(j!)=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}v(r-i)+S_{2}(j)

where

is the sum of the coefficients in the

-adic expansion of
j

Clearly

. Thus for all

, we have

\displaystyle v\left({r\choose j}\right)+j\geq v(r)+v(r-1)+v(r-2)+1\geq t+2.

If
j>r

{r\choose j}=0
and hence the inequality trivially holds, proving the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.3

and

\displaystyle H(X,2Y)
