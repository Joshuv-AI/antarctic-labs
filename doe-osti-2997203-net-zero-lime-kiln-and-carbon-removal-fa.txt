I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

 The Project Objective for DE-FE0032248, Net-Zero Lime Kiln and Carbon Removal Facility, was to execute
 and complete the initial design of a commercial-scale, advanced carbon capture system that separates
 CO2 with at least 95% capture efficiency from process streams at the Carmeuse Kentucky (KY) lime plant
 that will be retrofitted to utilize sustainably sourced biomass (SSB) alone or in combination with natural
 gas, and/or coal, with at least a 20-year available feedstock supply and remaining asset life. This eighteen-
 month project was to maximize utilization of SSB (up to 100%) and co-locate the carbon storage reservoirs
 within the existing industrial facility. The advanced carbon capture (CC) technology (TRL 6) was to allow
 capture of more than 400,000 metric tonne per year (TPY) of CO2 suitable for geologic carbon storage,
 with the potential to generate more than 100,000 metric tons of net-negative carbon emissions every
 year through the capture and sequestration of biogenic CO2 emissions for a marginal cost of $25/net
 tonne CO2e removed.

 The project team was led by Electricore, Inc.; Carmeuse Lime, Inc. served as the host site and project
 developer; Carmeuse Technologies was responsible for the conceptual design of the lime plant and
 technical advisement; Kiewit served as the EPC partner; FLSmidth was responsible for the oxyfuel kiln
 design; Air products provided the air separation and CO2 purification unit design; and Visage Energy
 provided the community benefits expertise.

 Table 1 shows the Success Criteria for the project. The project is deemed a success as the team has
 completed the initial engineering design for the commercial-scale, advanced carbon capture system that
 separates CO2 with at least 95% capture efficiency from process streams at an existing industrial lime
 facility and all required deliverables are complete and accepted.

 Subtask 1.2 – Technology Maturation Plan
 The Recipient shall develop a Technology Maturation Plan (TMP) that describes the current technology
 readiness level (TRL) of the proposed technology/technologies, relates the proposed project work to
 maturation of the proposed technology, describes the expected TRL at the end of the project, and
 describes any known post project research and development necessary to further mature the
 technology. The initial TMP is due 90 days after award and should be updated as needed throughout
 the project period of performance. A final TMP should be submitted 90 days prior to project
 completion.

 During the period of performance the team developed the preliminary Technology Maturation Plan
 (TMP) and a final TMP. The draft Technology Maturation Plan (TMP) was submitted October 25, 2023,
 and the final TMP was submitted October 29, 2024. The specified Technology Readiness Assessment
 (TRA) system being studied in this initial engineering design study is the integration of the oxyfuel
 technology together with Air Products’ air separation and CO2 purification technologies at Carmeuse’s
 Black River operation in Butler, KY. The oxyfuel calciner technology consists of four critical
 components:

 5
 5

 • A calciner fired with natural gas and/or biomass under 100% oxyfuel conditions that
 calcines carbonates in an environment with high CO2 concentrations (>90% dry CO2
 concentration);
 • A preheater that preheats part of the carbonate feed before entering the calciner while
 cooling down the high CO2 concentration flue gases;
 • A lime cooler that cools the calcined product after the calciner using ambient air ;
 • An air-based preheater that preheats the remaining carbonate feed before entering the
 calciner using the recovered heat in the air-based calcined product cooler.

 The integrated TRA system will serve as the first commercial deployment of a carbon neutral lime
 plant design aimed at decarbonizing lime production, a highly energy-intensive and hard-to-abate
 industry. The system was at TRL 6 at the beginning of the project. The project is an initial engineering
 design study looking at integrating the TRA system for the decarbonization of lime production at
 Carmeuse’s Black River operation in Butler, KY. The project scope does not include any testing of the
 TRA system, subsystems or components and therefore is not intended to mature the TRA system.

b) Task 2.0 – Prepare Initial Engineering Design Package
 The development of the Initial Engineering Design Package for the Net-Zero Lime Kiln and Carbon
 Removal Facility involved the completion of the following tasks:

 Subtask 2.1 – Prepare the Design Basis
 The Recipient shall determine the design basis that includes research / business objectives, summary
 of the proposed project, site characteristics/ambient conditions, fuel feedstock conditions, flue gas
 characteristics, and environmental and permitting requirements.

 Subtask 2.2 – Perform Preliminary Engineering
 The Recipient shall perform a preliminary engineering design of the Inside Battery Limits (ISBL)
 systems. The preliminary engineering design shall include process and utility flow diagrams, mass and
 energy balances, a system description and sizing of the major ISBL equipment.

Subtask 2.3 – Complete Detailed Engineering on Inside Battery Limits (ISBL) Systems
The Recipient shall perform detailed engineering and design on the following ISBL process systems,
equipment and associated components (piping, instrumentation, structural steel, foundations and
electrical): biomass grinding/drying, limestone handling, crushing/drying, product lime handling,
baghouse and storage; air separation unit (oxygen generation), lime flash calciner, calciner flue gas
treatment, and CO2 cooling and compression for transport and sequestration.

Subtask 2.4 – Complete Detailed Engineering on Outside Battery Limits (OSBL) Systems
The Recipient will develop the OSBL engineering and design of all OSBL systems, including but not
limited to interconnecting pipe and ductwork, solids conveying systems, and balance of plant systems,
such as potable and cooling water, drains, service and instrument air, and water treatment.

Subtask 2.5 – Conduct and document a Process Hazard Analysis
The Recipient will conduct a Process Hazard Analysis, which will function as a limited Hazards and
Operability (HAZOP) study, at the selected host site to identify potential hazards and functional flaws.
The Process Hazard Analysis will include preliminary recommendations for safeguards and
improvements to lower the risk of identified hazards and operational failures from occurring. The
scope of the study will be limited by the engineering and design completed for the initial engineering
design study.

Subtask 2.6 – Prepare Capital and Operating Cost Estimates
The Recipient shall prepare and provide a capital cost estimate consistent with an Association of the
Advancement of Cost Engineering (AACE) Class 4 level estimate which will have an expected accuracy
range of -15% to -30% on the low side and +20% to +50% on the high side. The Recipient shall also
estimate the operating costs of the overall process which shall also include annual an estimate of
annual maintenance costs.

Process Hazard Analysis
A Process Hazards Analysis (PHA) study was conducted using a focused HAZOP methodology. This
focused HAZOP differed slightly from traditional HAZOP in that a reduced list of causes was used. This

 9
 9

PHA was conducted in accordance with applicable regulatory requirements (e.g., OSHA
29CFR1910.119 PSM), and industry best practices [e.g., American Institute of Chemical Engineers
(AIChE), Center for Chemical Process Safety (CCPS), "Guidelines for Hazard Evaluation Procedures",
Third Ed."].

The Focused HAZOP study included the piping, equipment and instrumentation associated with the
new Limestone Storage and Processing Unit, the new Biomass Storage and Processing Unit, and the
new Lime Handling and Storage Unit. This was referred to as ISBL. The study also included the Balance
of Plant (BOP), which was referred to as OSBL. The final part of the study includes connections to Air
Products Air Separation Unit (ASU), and CO2 Purification and Compression Unit, but it did not include
any of the internal piping, equipment, or instrumentation for the Air Products units.

As a result of this PHA, the Team identified a total of 26 recommendations. These recommendations
were assigned a risk ranking during the review. The risk ranking (1 to 25) is a product of the severity
(1 to 5) and likelihood (1 to 5). The colors as well as description of severity and likelihood levels were
determined from the Risk Matrix provided for the study. The matrix does not provide any description
of the various risk levels, nor does it define a tolerable risk.

The highest risk ranking was 12 and three (3) recommendations had this risk. The three (3)
recommendations with the highest risk ranking are all related to the use of high purity oxygen , which
is new to the site with this Project. High purity oxygen will cause any flammable material, even just a
fingerprint inside a pipe, to burn intensely, possibly causing the piping to fail and create a personnel
burn hazard. The recommendations with the second highest risk ranking are related to the potential
for the biomass to self-ignite and also cause a personnel burn hazard. The last recommendation with
a "red" risk is related to the possible drowning hazard for personnel working around the raw water
pumps.

All the recommendations with associated risk ranking are listed in the Recommendation Summary
below. All recommendations were edited such that each is a standalone item. That is the
recommendation text includes not only what the team felt needed to be done, but also a brief
description of the consequence to be mitigated.

d) Task 4.0 – Perform a Technology Environmental Health and Safety (EH&S) Risk Assessment
 The Recipient shall perform an EH&S analysis of proposed technologies prepared in the format of
 Appendix R of the FOA DE-FOA-0002614 revision 0003.

 A Technology Environmental Health & Safety Risk assessment was prepared as a final deliverable for
 the Pre-FEED of Carmeuse’s Net-Zero Lime Kiln and Carbon Removal Facility Project, located in Butler,
 Kentucky. The expected emissions are reviewed utilizing the initial engineering and design package,
 from gases emissions, liquid emissions, ancillary emissions to the solid emissions. Both normal and
 upset conditions were considered and the potential environmental, health and safety effects were
 discussed together with the regulation compliance.

 The assessment found no concerns in terms of EH&S effects or legislative compliance that would
 prevent the proposed process from advancing to a further stage of development and evaluation. The
 cryogenic CO2 process vitally has lower health risks compared to the traditional amine absorption
 process as it does not use chemicals.

 However, new fuels and chemicals, such as biomass, oxygen, carbon dioxide, diesel, caustic,
 coagulant, and sodium chlorite etc., will be deployed to site when the new facility becomes
 operational. These substances are flammable, explosive, corrosive, and toxic, hence the design and
 operator training program must be monitored to ensure the health and safety of the people.

 Hydrogen Cyanide higher than OSHA/ACGIH recommended exposure limits is anticipated in the flue
 gas condensate from the CO2 purification unit to wastewater treatment. The highly toxic cyanide is a
 byproduct of the limestone calcination process and cannot be avoided with current feedstocks to the
 GSFC. Cyanide removal technology and Cyanide safety program are recommended to provide O&M
 personnels with knowledge on safe handling of the highly poisoning substance.

e) Task 5.0 – Perform a Life Cycle Analysis (LCA)
 The Recipient will prepare a LCA in accordance with Appendix P of the FOA DE-FOA-0002614 revision
 0003.

 As required under contract, the initial LCA was submitted to DOE on November 22, 2023. The Initial
 LCA showed the Proposed Product System is projected to reduce the global warming potential and is
 expected to result in a carbon negative product. Moving from the Comparison Product System to the
 Proposed Product System showed a range of improvements in GWP, all of which had over 99%
 emission reductions.

 15
 15

 A final LCA with the inputs from the design work was submitted at the conclusion of the project. The
 LCA shows the proposed product system has a negative value associated with the capture of CO2
 emissions derived from combustion of Biomass – BECCS. The Proposed Product System considers
 renewable energy accounting for transition of Scope 1 emissions to Scope 2 and is reflected in the
 BCA.

 This analysis was performed using openLCA to model the cradle-to-gate life cycle for each system. The
 100-year global warming potential (GWP-100), eutrophication potential, acidification potential, ozone
 depletion potential, particulate matter formation potential, photochemical smog formation potential,
 and water consumption for both systems are evaluated, calculated using the TRACI 2.1 impact
 method. This analysis shows that the Proposed Product System reduces GWP-100 by 117% from the
 Comparison Product System, is carbon negative, at -0.29 kg CO2/kg lime product, and, when
 considering avoided emissions, is carbon negative at -1.06 kg CO2/kg lime product.

 PROCESS DESCRIPTION

 A. INTRODUCTION

 This process description covers the operation of the FLSmidth limestone fines
 2-string gas suspension flash calcination plant. The Gas Suspension Calciner
 (GSC) is oxyfuel fired and the plant includes the following areas:

 1. Cyclone Preheater Circuit (String 1): Six cyclones operating in series transfer
 heat from the flash calciner off gas to the incoming dried, sized limestone
 fines. Most of the feed material to the system is fed to this string. This string
 is also referred to as the “CO2 Generating String”.
 a. Off Gas Circuit: The circuit includes Gas Conditioning Tower (GCT), ID
 Fan before Bagfilter, Bagfilter, ID fan after Bagfilter, Exhaust Gas
 Recirculation booster fan, shutoff gates, and main stack 1. The purpose
 of each equipment is described below:
 i. GCT: The GCT operates only during startup/upset conditions and
 at reduced feed operation mode to cool down the off-gas
 temperature to protect the Bagfilter.
 ii. ID fan before Bagfilter: This fan will ensure zero to slight negative
 gas pressure at the Bagfilter inlet to minimize false air intake into
 the Bagfilter.
 iii. Bagfilter: De-dust the preheater tower’s off-gas.
 iv. ID fan after Bagfilter: This fan will ensure the suction of the
 dedusted gases through the Bagfilter.
 v. EGR booster fan: This fan if needed will return the needed off-gas
 amount to the flash calciner to ensure sufficient velocity through
 the calciner for material lifting. Off gases not recirculated will be
 vented to main stack 1.
 vi. Shutoff gates: This circuit included three shut-off gates to add
 flexibility and better control to the system.

  2. Cyclone Preheater Circuit (String 2): Four cyclones operating in series transfer
 heat from the cooling cyclones string off gas to the incoming dried and sized
 limestone fines. A fraction of the feed material to the system is fed to this
 string.

 a. Off Gas Circuit: The circuit includes a bleed air damper, ID Fan before
 Bagfilter, Bagfilter, ID fan after Bagfilter, shutoff gates, and main stack
 2. The purpose of each piece of equipment is described below:
 i. Bleed air damper: Assures the off-gas temperature does not
 exceed the allowed maximum to protect the ID fan and Bagfilter.
 ii. ID fan before Bagfilter: This fan will assure constant draft and
 stable profile across string 2 preheater tower.
 iii. Bagfilter: De-dust the preheater tower’s off-gas.
 iv. ID fan after Bagfilter: This fan will ensure the suction of the
 dedusted gases through the Bagfilter.
 v. Shutoff gates: This circuit included two shut-off gates to add
 flexibility and better control to the system.

  3. Flash Calciner Circuit: The preheated and partially calcined limestone fines
 from both preheat strings are fed and fully calcined in the high-temperature
 flash calciner. The lime produced is transferred to a holding vessel that
 completes the carbonate decomposition. The vessel off gas is de-dusted in a
 cyclone. The captured dust is directed to a product cooler. The cyclone dust-
 laden off gas is directed to string 2 top stage cyclone inlet. The calciner is
 designed to fire 100% biomass or 100% natural gas and can receive a rich O2
 recycled stream.

  4. Cyclone Cooling Circuit: Three cyclones operating in series transfer heat from
 the flash calciner circuit lime product collected and discharged from the
 holding vessel to the incoming ambient air stream. Top-stage cooling cyclone
 dust-laden off gas is directed to string 2 top stage cyclone inlet.

  5. Product Cooler Circuit: The partially cooled material from the cooling cyclone
 circuit is finished cooled in the product cooler. The solids are cooled by passing
 them over a stationary cooled grid of plates.

 a. Off Gas Circuit: The circuit includes a bagfilter to de-dust cooler vent air
 and ID fan to assure suction of the dedusted gases through the bagfilter.

  6. Hot Gas Generator (HGG): The hot gas generator is 100% natural gas fired.

 Business Case Analysis - Black River Net Zero Lime Kiln and Carbon Removal Facility

 Project Overview

 The business case analysis evaluates the ﬁnancial viability of the proposed project – Net Zero Lime
 Kiln and Carbon Removal Facility based on key ﬁnancial metrics supported by the Class IV Pre-
 FEED cost estimates.

 The Financial metrics considers 50/50 cost share plus the support of 50% cost share for the ﬁrst 2
 years of OPEX during ramp up; modelling support for commercialization referenced in DOE FOA
 2936. The development of the TEA considers the replacement of production capacity from 3 long
 rotary kilns producing lime from older more energy intensive technology with a new oxy-fuel ﬁred
 energy efficient ﬂash calciner. Some key inputs to the ﬁnancial modelling include;

 • Inﬂation: 3%
 • 20 yr asset life
 • Cost of Capital: 12.5%
 • No Green Premiums for Net Zero products
 • LKD can be blended with ﬁnal products
 • 50,000 Carbon Dioxide Removal Credits can be sold at $150 / t CO2 for the BECCS process
 • CO2 Capture rate of 99% applies to all operating time
 • 45Q tax credit applies for 12 yrs, no future credit year 13 +
 • CO2 sequestration fee - $26.71 / t CO2e
 • Green Electricity procured at $0.03 / kwh premium to derive net negative emissions based
 on LCA
 • 25 additional headcount to operate ASU, CPU, Waste Water Treatment plant, Biomass
 loading / unloading and processing

 Financial Metrics

          •   Net Present Value (NPV): $12,389,502

 • Internal Rate of Return (IRR): 13.5%

 • Return on Investment (ROI): 4.1%

 • Payback Period: 5.2 years

 • Return on Average Capital Employed (ROACE) over 20 years: 31.2%

Analysis

 1. Net Present Value (NPV)

 o The NPV of $12,389,502 indicates that the project is expected to generate
 substantial value over its lifetime. A positive NPV suggests that the project’s returns
 exceed the costs, making it a ﬁnancially sound investment. Despite the positive
 NPV, the project has inherent risks based on some key assumptions;

 1. 50% cost share

 2. 45Q with 12 year duration

 3. LKD produced can be blended with ﬁnal product therefore increasing
 overall sales volumes at lime prices. Furthermore, a key impact to the
 project

The TEA demonstrates that despite a positive NPV with 50% cost share, the project has signiﬁcant
negative cashﬂow in years 13+ as a result of the loss of 45Q tax credits. Without assurance of a
long lasting program valorizing sequestered CO2, the assets would generate negative cashﬂow
after the payback period but with signiﬁcant asset life remaining, the increased OPEX costs to
continue producing a net zero product strain the economic viability of the project.

 2. Internal Rate of Return (IRR)

 o With an IRR of 13.5%, the project is expected to generate a return that is higher than
 the cost of capital. This rate of return is attractive and indicates that the project may
 be proﬁtable.

 o A sensitivity analysis including, reduced product volumes, increased biomass fuel
 costs, ASU/CPU downtime have signiﬁcant negative impact to the project ﬁnancial
 performance. The project assumptions detailed earlier represent an optimistic
 operating curve over the lifetime of the project and therefore, a risk analysis with
 consideration of operating cost changes, capex overruns, reduced CDR value etc is
 required to mange the due diligence of a large scale commercial project.

 3. Return on Investment (ROI)

 o An ROI of 4.1% reﬂects the efficiency of the investment. While this ﬁgure is modest,
 it still represents a positive return, suggesting that the project has potential to
 generate more value than the initial investment.

 4. Payback Period

 o The payback period of 5.2 years means that the project will recover its initial
 investment within this timeframe. This relatively short payback period enhances the
 project’s attractiveness by reducing the risk associated with long-term investments.
 With certainty on a future for 45Q beyond 12 yrs, the project attractiveness
 signiﬁcantly increases given the potential for a 5.2 yrs payback period.

 74

 5. Return on Average Capital Employed (ROACE)

 o A ROACE of 31.2% over 20 years demonstrates the project’s ability to generate
 signiﬁcant returns on the capital employed. This high percentage indicates efficient
 use of capital and strong long-term proﬁtability potential.

Conclusion

Based on the ﬁnancial metrics provided, the projects optimistic operating scenario appears to be a
viable and proﬁtable investment. The positive NPV, attractive IRR, and high ROACE suggest that the
project will generate substantial value over its lifetime with the assumed cost share. The modest
ROI and reasonable payback period further support the project’s potential. However, a risk
assessment with consideration of changes to inputs of OPEX, CAPEX, regulatory change – 45Q etc
is required to complete the due diligence to be considered for a project producing goods in a
variable market like Lime production.

There are several potential pitfalls to consider for a project that relies heavily on 45Q tax credits for
CO2 sequestration, especially when the ﬁnancial metrics indicate negative cash ﬂow after the 12-
year period:

 1. Dependency on Tax Credits

 o The project’s ﬁnancial viability is signiﬁcantly tied to the 45Q tax credits. Once these
 credits expire after 12 years, the project may struggle to maintain positive cash ﬂow,
 leading to ﬁnancial instability.

 o .

 2. Market and Policy Risks

 o Changes in government policies or market conditions could impact the availability
 or value of tax credits. If the 45Q program is altered or discontinued, the project’s
 ﬁnancial projections could be severely affected.

 o The 45Q tax credit accounts for more than $35 million annually making up 50-80%
 of annual cashﬂow in the ﬁrst 12 years. A projects cashﬂow so heavily dependent on
 a tax credit and regulatory change must have a de-risking plan to valorize the net
 zero products long term

 3. Operational Costs

 o The ongoing operational and maintenance costs of CO2 sequestration technology
 could be higher than anticipated. Without the offsetting tax credits, these costs
 might outweigh the revenue generated, leading to negative cash ﬂow. Furthermore,
 new processes like Air Separation Units, CO2 compression and waste water
 treatment are energy intensive requiring signiﬁcant increases in power demand,
 employee headcount and consumables relative to the baseline lime kilns.

 75

 4. Technological Risks

 o The technology used for CO2 sequestration may face performance issues or require
 upgrades over time. These additional costs are not considered in the TEA and could
 further strain the project’s ﬁnances, especially after the tax credits expire.

 5. Revenue Uncertainty

 o The project aims to supply a net zero lime product suitable for construction grade
 and other lime markets. Lime markets can be volatile as seen with the signiﬁcant
 shift in current demand at the facility for lime products serving the power generation
 markets.

 o The project may face challenges in maximizing annual production capacity
 generating sufficient revenue from all sources over the 20 year life modelled.
 Furthermore, ﬂuctuations in the price of CO2 credits or other market-driven factors
 can lead to additional uncertainty for the net zero lime kiln project.

 6. Environmental and Regulatory Compliance

 o Maintaining compliance with environmental regulations can be costly. Any changes
 in regulations could increase operational costs or require additional investments in
 technology and infrastructure.

 7. Long-term Sustainability

 o The project’s long-term sustainability is questionable if it cannot generate positive
 cash ﬂow without the tax credits. This could impact investor conﬁdence and the
 ability to secure future funding. Furthermore, without green premiums, the
 incremental OPEX costs will strain re-investment.

 8. Economic Viability

 o The overall economic viability of the project may be compromised if it cannot adapt
 to changing ﬁnancial conditions. This includes the ability to pivot to new revenue
 streams or reduce Capex and Opex costs effectively.

Given these potential pitfalls, it’s crucial to develop a comprehensive risk mitigation strategy. This
could include securing long term green premium pricing, securing valid high quality contracts for
Carbon Removal Credits, investigating more efficient and cost effective Oxygen supply and CO2
compression technology, and closely monitoring policy changes to adapt quickly.

The efforts conducted through the support of the DOE to execute a Pre-FEED study and produce the
Capex / Opex cost estimates for the Net Zero Lime with an Oxy-Fuel Flash Calciner has been
invaluable. The baseline results from this Pre-FEED will allow for more detailed consideration in
optimizing the overall process, design consideration and value chain. Exploring the opportunity to
reduce Capex and Opex costs, managing regulatory uncertainty, promoting / contracting for green
premiums, long term contracting for CDR’s, and consideration of alternative fuel selections will
allow for de-risking the project and better leverage underwriting opportunities for future phases of
the project.
