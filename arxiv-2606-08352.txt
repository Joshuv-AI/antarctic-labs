Finite-Scale One-Component Regularity via Harmonic Pressure for the 3D Navier–Stokes Equations
Runlong Yu

Abstract
We study a finite-scale one-component regularity mechanism for suitable weak solutions of the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations. The results are organized in three layers. The first layer is unconditional. Under a fixed scale-invariant local bound Φ ⁡ ( 1 ) = A ⁡ ( 1 ) + E ⁡ ( 1 ) + C ⁡ ( 1 ) + D ⁡ ( 1 ) ≤ M , \Phi(1)=A(1)+E(1)+C(1)+D(1)\leq M, smallness of the critical vertical-component quantity C 3 ​ ( 1 ) = ∫ Q 1 | u 3 | 3 ​ 𝑑 x ​ 𝑑 t C_{3}(1)=\int_{Q_{1}}|u_{3}|^{3}\,dx\,dt yields a positive lower bound, depending only on M M , for the local regularity radius at the origin. The proof converts one-component smallness into approximation by the two-and-a-half-dimensional limiting class and then into Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg smallness at a smaller scale. The pressure approximation is measured in a quotient by spatially harmonic functions. This pressure topology reflects a genuine obstruction: time-dependent harmonic pressures may have bounded scale-invariant L 3 / 2 L^{3/2} -oscillation while their pointwise gradients lie beyond the control provided by the available scale-invariant quantities. The second layer is a conditional logarithmic refinement. A prepared two-shadow comparison package replaces the abstract compactness modulus by a logarithmic modulus and gives a logarithmic finite-scale decay. The third layer is a conditional relaxed-shadowing refinement. The comparison class is enlarged to smooth no-stretching horizontal flows V = ( v h , 0 ) V=(v_{h},0) , with the comparison pressure allowed to have ∂ 3 π ≠ 0 \partial_{3}\pi\neq 0 . The resulting vertical residual pairs with the small component u 3 u_{3} in the relative-energy identity. Under the buffered strong-flow and localized relaxed stability inputs stated below, this gives a power-type relaxed harmonic approximation and a power-type finite-scale decay. The unconditional theorem is separated from the logarithmic and power-type assumptions; the latter two layers identify the quantitative stability mechanisms needed to upgrade the compactness modulus.

Introduction

The local regularity theory for the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations is built on scale-invariant estimates, pressure decompositions, compactness, and epsilon-regularity. In the unit parabolic cylinder

Q_{1}=B_{1}(0)\times(-1,0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R},

we consider

\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=0,\qquad\nabla\cdot u=0.

(1.1)

The Navier–Stokes scaling is

u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t),\qquad p_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{2}p(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t).

For
0<r\leq 1
, define

\displaystyle A(r)

\displaystyle=\esssup_{-r^{2}<t<0}\frac{1}{r}\int_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle E(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\int_{Q_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\,dt,\qquad C(r)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{Q_{r}}|u|^{3}\,dx\,dt,

\displaystyle D(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{Q_{r}}|p-(p)_{B_{r}}(t)|^{3/2}\,dx\,dt,\qquad C_{3}(r)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{Q_{r}}|u_{3}|^{3}\,dx\,dt,

and set

\Phi(r)=A(r)+E(r)+C(r)+D(r),\qquad\Psi(r)=C(r)+D(r).

These quantities are invariant under the Navier–Stokes scaling.

small at a critical scale, the flow should approach a regime with
partially lower-dimensional structure. For the vertical component

u_{3}
, the formal limiting velocity has the form

v=(v_{h},0),\qquad\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{h}=0.

The limiting equations retain horizontal transport and full three-dimensional diffusion:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v_{h}-\Delta v_{h}+(v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})v_{h}+\nabla_{h}q=0,\\
\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{h}=0,\\
\partial_{3}q=0.\end{cases}

(1.2)

We refer to (
1.2
) as the two-and-a-half-dimensional limiting system. The horizontal velocity may still depend on
x_{3}
, and the Laplacian remains the full operator
\Delta=\Delta_{h}+\partial_{3}^{2}
.

The preceding intuition hides several analytic difficulties. Smallness of
u_{3}
leaves the horizontal velocity
u_{h}
largely uncontrolled. The nonlinear term
(u_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})u_{h}
may remain large, and the Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg criterion requires smallness of the combined velocity-pressure quantity
\Psi=C+D
. Thus the main task is to show that one-component smallness forces, at a smaller scale, a full CKN quantity to fall below the universal threshold.

The pressure creates a second and more delicate issue. Locally, the pressure decomposes into a Calderon–Zygmund part determined by the quadratic velocity terms and a spatially harmonic part. The Calderon–Zygmund part is compact under strong
L^{3}
-convergence of velocities. The harmonic part may oscillate sharply in time while preserving bounded scale-invariant
L^{3/2}
-oscillation. Consequently, the natural approximation topology is the quotient

p\approx q+h,\qquad\Delta h(\cdot,t)=0.

This quotient is forced by a simple obstruction: within the limiting class (
1.2
), the full pressure gradient can be arbitrarily large under a fixed bound on
\Phi
. The proof below makes this obstruction explicit and then uses harmonic oscillation decay to recover the pressure contribution needed for CKN smallness.

A third difficulty concerns rates. Compactness gives an approximation modulus
\omega_{M,\theta}(s)\to 0
as
s\downarrow 0
, leaving the dependence on
C_{3}(1)
implicit. Logarithmic and power-type rates require quantitative shadowing estimates, local pressure reconstruction, and careful treatment of cutoff errors in the relative-energy argument. These estimates form separate stability problems. This paper separates the unconditional compactness theorem from the conditional quantitative mechanisms that would replace the compactness modulus by explicit logarithmic or power-type rates.

The results are organized into three layers. The first layer is an unconditional finite-scale theorem. Under the fixed a priori bound
\Phi(1)\leq M
, there exist
\varepsilon_{*}(M)>0
and
\rho_{*}(M)>0
such that

C_{3}(1)\leq\varepsilon_{*}(M)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r_{\mathrm{reg}}(0,0)\geq\rho_{*}(M).

The constants are qualitative, since they come from compactness, while the conclusion is finite-scale because the radius depends only on
M
.

The second layer is a conditional logarithmic refinement. We formulate a prepared comparison package which, once available, gives

\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{harm}}_{\theta/4}(u,p;M)\leq C_{M,\theta}|\log C_{3}(1)|^{-\sigma}.

The main stability mechanism is a two-shadow estimate. A rough limiting shadow propagates the smoothing error, and a smoothed limiting shadow supplies the comparison object. This arrangement keeps the smoothing error outside the large Gronwall factor generated by the smoothed flow.

The third layer is a conditional relaxed-shadowing route toward a power-type estimate. The comparison class is enlarged to smooth horizontal no-stretching flows
V=(v_{h},0)
satisfying

\partial_{t}v_{h}-\Delta v_{h}+(v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})v_{h}+\nabla_{h}\pi=0,\qquad\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{h}=0,

with
\partial_{3}\pi
allowed. In the full three-dimensional equation this leaves a vertical residual
(0,0,\partial_{3}\pi)
, and the relative-energy identity pairs this residual with the small component
u_{3}
. Together with harmonic pressure reconstruction, this gives a conditional power-type finite-scale decay.

2
Main results

We state the results in three layers. The first one is unconditional and is the main theorem. The second and third are conditional refinements which identify additional quantitative inputs.

2.1
Unconditional finite-scale theorem

Theorem 2.1

(Finite-scale one-component regularity)
.

For every
M\geq 1
, there exist constants

\varepsilon_{*}(M)>0,\qquad\rho_{*}(M)>0,

such that the following holds. Let
(u,p)
be a suitable weak solution of (
1.1
) in
Q_{1}
. If

\Phi(1)\leq M,\qquad C_{3}(1)\leq\varepsilon_{*}(M),

then
u
is regular in
Q_{\rho_{*}(M)}
. In particular,

r_{\mathrm{reg}}(0,0)\geq\rho_{*}(M).

The proof gives a decay estimate with a compactness modulus.

Theorem 2.2

(Finite-scale decay with compactness modulus)
.

Let
M\geq 1
and
0<\theta<1/2
. There exist a nondecreasing modulus

\omega_{M,\theta}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty),\qquad\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\omega_{M,\theta}(s)=0,

and constants
K(M,\theta)\geq 1
,
r_{0}(M,\theta)\in(0,\theta)
, such that every suitable weak solution in
Q_{1}
satisfying
\Phi(1)\leq M
and
C_{3}(1)=\delta
obeys

\Psi(r)\leq K(M,\theta)r+K(M,\theta)r^{-2}\omega_{M,\theta}(\delta)

(2.1)

for every
0<r<r_{0}(M,\theta)
.

The contrapositive gives the corresponding concentration statement.

Corollary 2.3

(One-component concentration near singular points)
.

Let
M\geq 1
, and let
\varepsilon_{*}(M)
be as in Theorem
2.1
. If
(u,p)
is suitable in
Q_{1}
,
\Phi(1)\leq M
, and
(0,0)
is singular, then

C_{3}(1)>\varepsilon_{*}(M).

More generally, if
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})
is singular and
(u,p)
is suitable in
Q_{R}(z_{0})
, then for every
0<r\leq R
such that
\Phi(z_{0},r)\leq M
, one has

C_{3}(z_{0},r)>\varepsilon_{*}(M).

2.2
Conditional logarithmic layer

The logarithmic layer replaces the abstract compactness modulus by a logarithmic modulus under a prepared comparison estimate. The precise assumption is stated in Section
7
. It gives, for a smoothing parameter
\ell
, an estimate of the form

\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{harm}}_{\theta/4}\lesssim\ell^{a}+\ell^{-N}\delta^{b}+\exp(C\ell^{-N})\delta^{b}.

Optimizing
\ell

(Conditional logarithmic harmonic-pressure approximation)

Assume the prepared comparison estimate, Assumption
7.3
. Let
M\geq 1
and
0<\theta<1/2
. Then there exist constants

C_{M,\theta}\geq 1,\qquad\sigma>0,\qquad\delta_{M,\theta}\in(0,1),

Q_{1}
satisfying
\Phi(1)\leq M

\delta=C_{3}(1)\leq\delta_{M,\theta}

Consequently,

for all sufficiently small

, and after decreasing

r_{\mathrm{reg}}(0,0)\geq c_{M,\theta}|\log\delta|^{-\sigma/3}.

(2.4)

Conditional relaxed-shadowing power layer

The power layer uses a larger comparison class. It is conditional on two inputs stated in Section
8

Theorem 2.5

(Conditional relaxed-shadowing power-type decay)

Assume Assumptions
8.3
and

Preliminaries

For
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
, define

B_{r}(x_{0})=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|x-x_{0}|<r\},\qquad Q_{r}(z_{0})=B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0}).

When
z_{0}=(0,0)
, write
B_{r}=B_{r}(0)
and
Q_{r}=Q_{r}(0,0)
. The spatial average is

(f)_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(t)=\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}f(x,t)\,dx.

For general centers, the quantities
A,E,C,D,C_{3},\Phi,\Psi
are defined by translation and scaling.

Definition 3.1

(Suitable weak solution)
.

A pair
(u,p)
is a suitable weak solution of (
1.1
) in a parabolic cylinder
Q
if

u\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(Q)\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}(Q),\qquad p\in L^{3/2}(Q),

(u,p)
solves (
1.1
) in distributions,
\nabla\cdot u=0
, and the local energy inequality holds: for every nonnegative
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q)
and a.e.
t
,

\displaystyle\int|u(x,t)|^{2}\phi(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle+2\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int|\nabla u|^{2}\phi\,dx\,ds

(3.1)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int|u|^{2}(\partial_{s}\phi+\Delta\phi)\,dx\,ds+\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int(|u|^{2}+2p)u\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx\,ds.

The pressure is defined up to a function of time. All pressure quantities below are invariant under this change.

We use

r_{\mathrm{reg}}(z_{0})=\sup\{r>0:u\in L^{\infty}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))\}.

Only positivity and universal changes of scale are used.

Theorem 3.2

(CKN epsilon regularity)
.

There exist universal constants
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}>0
,
\kappa\in(0,1/2)
, and
C_{\mathrm{CKN}}<\infty
such that the following holds. If
(u,p)
is suitable in
Q_{r}(z_{0})
and

\Psi(z_{0},r)=C(z_{0},r)+D(z_{0},r)\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}},

then
u
is regular in
Q_{\kappa r}(z_{0})
, and

\esssup_{Q_{\kappa r}(z_{0})}|u|\leq C_{\mathrm{CKN}}r^{-1}.

In particular,
r_{\mathrm{reg}}(z_{0})\geq\kappa r
.

Lemma 3.3

(Scale-invariant interpolation)
.

For every suitable weak solution and every
0<r\leq 1
,

C(r)\leq CA(r)^{3/4}E(r)^{3/4}+CA(r)^{3/2}.

Proof.

For a.e.
t
, the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality on
B_{r}
gives

\left\|u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{3}(B_{r})}^{3}\leq C\left\|u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{r})}^{3/2}\left\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{r})}^{3/2}+Cr^{-3/2}\left\|u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{r})}^{3}.

Integrating in time, using Holder’s inequality, and dividing by
r^{2}
gives the claim.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(Fixed-scale nesting of pressure oscillations)
.

Let
0<r<R
. Then, for a.e. time,

\int_{B_{r}}|p-(p)_{B_{r}}(t)|^{3/2}\,dx\leq C\int_{B_{R}}|p-(p)_{B_{R}}(t)|^{3/2}\,dx.

Consequently, whenever
Q_{r}\subset Q_{R}
,

D(r)\leq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{2}D(R).

Proof.

For
p_{0}=3/2
, the mean minimizes the
L^{p_{0}}
-distance up to a universal constant. Hence for any constant
c
,

\int_{B_{r}}|p-(p)_{B_{r}}|^{p_{0}}\,dx\leq C\int_{B_{r}}|p-c|^{p_{0}}\,dx.

Choose
c=(p)_{B_{R}}
and use
B_{r}\subset B_{R}
. Integrating over the shorter time interval and multiplying by
r^{-2}
gives the scale-invariant estimate.
∎

For a pressure-like scalar
f
, set

D_{f}(r)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{Q_{r}}|f-(f)_{B_{r}}(t)|^{3/2}\,dx\,dt.

Lemma 3.5

(Harmonic corrector decay)
.

Let
0<r\leq R/2
, and let
h\in L^{3/2}(Q_{R})
satisfy

\Delta h(\cdot,t)=0\quad\text{in }B_{R}

for a.e.
t\in(-R^{2},0)
. Then

D_{h}(r)\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{5/2}D_{h}(R).

In particular, if
h
is harmonic in space in
Q_{\theta}
and
D_{h}(\theta)\leq B
, then for
0<r\leq\theta/2
,

D_{h}(r)\leq C\theta^{-5/2}Br^{5/2}\leq C\theta^{-5/2}Br.

Proof.

For a.e.
t
, harmonic oscillation estimates give

\int_{B_{r}}|h-(h)_{B_{r}}(t)|^{3/2}\,dx\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{9/2}\int_{B_{R}}|h-(h)_{B_{R}}(t)|^{3/2}\,dx.

Integrate over
(-r^{2},0)
, enlarge the time interval on the right to
(-R^{2},0)
, and multiply by
r^{-2}
.
∎

Lemma 3.6

(Pressure decomposition and decay)
.

There is a universal constant
C
such that, whenever
0<r\leq R/2
,

D(r)\leq C\frac{r}{R}D(R)+C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{2}C(R).

(3.2)

More precisely, if in
B_{3R/4}
one writes

p=p_{\mathrm{loc}}+p_{\mathrm{harm}},\qquad p_{\mathrm{loc}}=R_{i}R_{j}(\chi u_{i}u_{j}),

where
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{R})
and
\chi\equiv 1
on
B_{3R/4}
, then
p_{\mathrm{harm}}
is harmonic in space and

D_{p_{\mathrm{harm}}}(r)\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{5/2}D_{p_{\mathrm{harm}}}(R),

while

\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{Q_{r}}|p_{\mathrm{loc}}|^{3/2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{2}C(R).

Proof.

The local part is controlled by Calderon–Zygmund estimates:

\left\|p_{\mathrm{loc}}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{R})}\leq C\left\|u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{3}(B_{R})}^{2}.

The harmonic part is controlled by Lemma
3.5
. Combining the two parts and using Lemma
3.4
gives (
3.2
).
∎

4
The limiting system and the harmonic pressure obstruction

Writing
u=(u_{h},u_{3})
, the Navier–Stokes equations become

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{h}-\Delta u_{h}+(u_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})u_{h}+u_{3}\partial_{3}u_{h}+\nabla_{h}p

\displaystyle=0,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{3}-\Delta u_{3}+u_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}u_{3}+u_{3}\partial_{3}u_{3}+\partial_{3}p

\displaystyle=0,

(4.2)

and

\nabla_{h}\cdot u_{h}+\partial_{3}u_{3}=0.

If
u_{3}^{(n)}\to 0
strongly in
L^{3}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and
u^{(n)}\to v
strongly locally, then
v=(v_{h},0)
,
\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{h}=0
, and the limiting equations are

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v_{h}-\Delta v_{h}+(v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})v_{h}+\nabla_{h}q=0,\\
\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{h}=0,\\
\partial_{3}q=0.\end{cases}

(4.3)

The horizontal velocity may depend on
x_{3}
, and the full Laplacian remains present.

Proposition 4.1

(Absence of a full pressure-gradient bound)
.

There exists a universal number
M_{*}\geq 1
with the following property. For every
L>0
, there is a smooth solution
(v,q)
of (
4.3
) in
Q_{1/2}
such that

\Phi_{v}(1/2)\leq M_{*},\qquad\left\|\nabla q\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1/4})}>L.

Consequently, every proposed estimate of the form

\left\|\nabla q\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1/4})}\leq K(\Phi_{v}(1/2))

fails for the full limiting class.

Proof.

Choose
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((-1,1))
with
\varphi^{\prime}\not\equiv 0
, and choose
t_{0}\in(-1/16,-1/32)
. For
H\gg 1
, set

\varepsilon_{H}=H^{-1/2},\qquad\delta_{H}=H^{-3/2},\qquad a_{H}(t)=\varepsilon_{H}\varphi\left(\frac{t-t_{0}}{\delta_{H}}\right).

For
H
sufficiently large, the support of
a_{H}
is contained in
(-1/16,-1/32)
. Define

v_{H}(x,t)=(a_{H}(t),0,0),\qquad q_{H}(x,t)=-a_{H}^{\prime}(t)x_{1}.

Then
v_{H,3}=0
,
\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{H,h}=0
,
\partial_{3}q_{H}=0
, and

\partial_{t}v_{H,h}+\nabla_{h}q_{H}=0,\qquad\Delta v_{H,h}=0,\qquad(v_{H,h}\cdot\nabla_{h})v_{H,h}=0.

Thus
(v_{H},q_{H})
solves (
4.3
). Since
v_{H}
is spatially constant,

A_{v_{H}}(1/2)\lesssim\varepsilon_{H}^{2},\quad E_{v_{H}}(1/2)=0,\quad C_{v_{H}}(1/2)\lesssim\varepsilon_{H}^{3}.

Moreover
(q_{H})_{B_{r}}(t)=0
, and

\int|a_{H}^{\prime}(t)|^{3/2}\,dt=\varepsilon_{H}^{3/2}\delta_{H}^{-1/2}\int|\varphi^{\prime}(s)|^{3/2}\,ds\sim 1.

Thus
D_{q_{H}}(1/2)
is uniformly bounded. On the other hand,

\left\|\nabla q_{H}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1/4})}=\left\|a_{H}^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\sim\frac{\varepsilon_{H}}{\delta_{H}}=H\to\infty.

The proposition follows.
∎

Remark 4.2

A suitable solution of (

5
Approximation modulo harmonic pressures

For
0<\theta<1/2
, define

\mathcal{H}(Q_{\theta})=\{h\in L^{3/2}(Q_{\theta}):\Delta h(\cdot,t)=0\text{ in }B_{\theta}\text{ for a.e. }t\in(-\theta^{2},0)\}.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{M}(Q_{\theta})
be the class of suitable solutions
(v,q)
of (
4.3
) in
Q_{\theta}
, with
v=(v_{h},0)
,
\partial_{3}q=0
, and

\Phi_{v}(\theta)\leq K_{0}(M,\theta),

where
K_{0}(M,\theta)
is chosen large enough to contain all compactness limits of sequences satisfying
\Phi(1)\leq M
.

For
(u,p)
,
(v,q)
, and
h\in\mathcal{H}(Q_{\theta})
, set

\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{harm}}_{\theta}((u,p),(v,q);h)=\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}\int_{Q_{\theta}}|u-v|^{3}\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}\int_{Q_{\theta}}|p-q-h|^{3/2}\,dx\,dt.

Define the harmonic-pressure excess

\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{harm}}_{\theta}(u,p;M)=\inf_{(v,q)\in\mathcal{L}_{M}(Q_{\theta})}\inf_{h\in\mathcal{H}(Q_{\theta})}\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{harm}}_{\theta}((u,p),(v,q);h).

(5.1)

Theorem 5.1

(Qualitative finite-scale approximation)
.

Let
M\geq 1
and
0<\theta<1/2
. There exists a nondecreasing modulus

\omega_{M,\theta}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty),\qquad\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\omega_{M,\theta}(s)=0,

such that every suitable weak solution
(u,p)
of (
1.1
) in
Q_{1}
satisfying
\Phi(1)\leq M
obeys

\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{harm}}_{\theta}(u,p;M)\leq\omega_{M,\theta}(C_{3}(1)).

(5.2)

Proof.

Suppose the conclusion fails. Then there exist
\eta_{0}>0
and suitable weak solutions
(u^{(n)},p^{(n)})
in
Q_{1}
such that

\Phi_{u^{(n)}}(1)\leq M,\qquad C^{(n)}_{3}(1)\to 0,

and

\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{harm}}_{\theta}(u^{(n)},p^{(n)};M)\geq\eta_{0}\qquad\text{for all }n.

The uniform energy bound gives, after passing to a subsequence,

u^{(n)}\rightharpoonup v\quad\text{weakly in }L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}(Q_{\sigma}),\qquad u^{(n)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}v\quad\text{in }L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(Q_{\sigma})

for every
\sigma<1
. After fixing pressure representatives by subtracting functions of time, the equation gives a uniform bound for
\partial_{t}u^{(n)}
in

L^{3/2}((-\sigma^{2},0);W^{-1,3/2}(B_{\sigma})).

Aubin–Lions gives strong convergence in
L^{2}(Q_{\sigma})
. Interpolation with the uniform
L^{10/3}
-bound yields

u^{(n)}\to v\quad\text{strongly in }L^{3}_{\mathrm{loc}}(Q_{1}).

(5.3)

Since
u^{(n)}_{3}\to 0
strongly in
L^{3}(Q_{1})
, the limit has the form
v=(v_{h},0)
, and
\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{h}=0
.

Set

\widetilde{p}^{(n)}=p^{(n)}-(p^{(n)})_{B_{1}}(t).

After extraction,

\widetilde{p}^{(n)}\rightharpoonup q\quad\text{weakly in }L^{3/2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(Q_{1}).

Fix
\rho
with
\theta<\rho<1
, and choose
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{\rho})
equal to one on a neighborhood of
B_{\theta}
. In
Q_{\theta}
, decompose

\widetilde{p}^{(n)}=P^{(n)}+H^{(n)},\qquad P^{(n)}=R_{i}R_{j}(\chi u_{i}^{(n)}u_{j}^{(n)}),

where
H^{(n)}(\cdot,t)
is harmonic in
B_{\theta}
for a.e. time. By (
5.3
),

P^{(n)}\to P:=R_{i}R_{j}(\chi v_{i}v_{j})\quad\text{strongly in }L^{3/2}(Q_{\theta}).

Because
\widetilde{p}^{(n)}\rightharpoonup q
and
P^{(n)}\to P
strongly,
H^{(n)}=\widetilde{p}^{(n)}-P^{(n)}
converges weakly in
L^{3/2}(Q_{\theta})
, after extraction, to
H=q-P
. The limit is harmonic in space. Set

h^{(n)}=H^{(n)}-H+(p^{(n)})_{B_{1}}(t).

Then
h^{(n)}\in\mathcal{H}(Q_{\theta})
, and

p^{(n)}-q-h^{(n)}=P^{(n)}-P\to 0\quad\text{strongly in }L^{3/2}(Q_{\theta}).

Together with (
5.3
), this gives

\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{harm}}_{\theta}((u^{(n)},p^{(n)}),(v,q);h^{(n)})\to 0.

It remains to identify the limit. Passing to the limit in the horizontal momentum equation gives

\partial_{t}v_{h}-\Delta v_{h}+(v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})v_{h}+\nabla_{h}q=0.

The vertical equation gives, in distributions,

\partial_{3}p^{(n)}=-\partial_{t}u^{(n)}_{3}+\Delta u^{(n)}_{3}-\nabla\cdot(u^{(n)}u^{(n)}_{3}),

and the right-hand side tends to zero in distributions using
u^{(n)}_{3}\to 0
in
L^{3}
, the uniform energy bounds, and (
5.3
). Hence
\partial_{3}q=0
.

The suitability of the limit follows from the standard stability of the local energy inequality under this convergence. Indeed, for every fixed nonnegative test function
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q_{\theta})
, the terms containing
|u^{(n)}|^{2}
and
|u^{(n)}|^{2}u^{(n)}
pass to the limit by the strong
L^{3}
-convergence. The pressure term also passes to the limit, since
\widetilde{p}^{(n)}\rightharpoonup q
in
L^{3/2}
and
u^{(n)}\to v
strongly in
L^{3}
, so

\int\widetilde{p}^{(n)}u^{(n)}\cdot\nabla\phi\,\,dx\,dt\longrightarrow\int qv\cdot\nabla\phi\,\,dx\,dt.

The subtracted time-dependent pressure averages do not contribute because
u^{(n)}
is divergence-free. Lower semicontinuity gives the dissipative term. Thus
(v,q)\in\mathcal{L}_{M}(Q_{\theta})
, after increasing
K_{0}(M,\theta)
if necessary. This is incompatible with the lower bound on
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{harm}}_{\theta}
.

Define

\omega_{M,\theta}(\delta)=\sup\{\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{harm}}_{\theta}(u,p;M):(u,p)\text{ suitable in }Q_{1},\ \Phi(1)\leq M,\ C_{3}(1)\leq\delta\}.

The preceding contradiction argument proves
\omega_{M,\theta}(\delta)\to 0
as
\delta\downarrow 0
. The supremum is finite by a direct fixed-scale comparison: choose
(v,q)=(0,0)\in\mathcal{L}_{M}(Q_{\theta})
and choose the harmonic corrector
h=(p)_{B_{\theta}}(t)
, which is constant in space. Then

\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{harm}}_{\theta}(u,p;M)\leq C_{\theta}\{C(\theta)+D(\theta)\}\leq C_{\theta}M,

using fixed-scale nesting. Replacing
\omega_{M,\theta}
by its monotone envelope gives a nondecreasing modulus.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Strong convergence of the full pressure oscillation

p^{(n)}-(p^{(n)})_{B_{\theta}}(t)\longrightarrow q-(q)_{B_{\theta}}(t)\quad\text{in }L^{3/2}(Q_{\theta})

requires more than
\Phi(1)\leq M
and strong

-convergence of velocities. The Calderon–Zygmund part is strongly compact, while the harmonic part may fail to be strongly compact in time. The quotient in (

Theorem 6.1

7
A logarithmic refinement under a prepared comparison package

The preceding sections prove a finite-scale theorem with a qualitative compactness modulus. This section records a semi-quantitative logarithmic refinement of the same mechanism. The purpose is to isolate a precise stability estimate which, if proved, upgrades the abstract modulus in Theorem
5.1
to a logarithmic rate.

Throughout this section write

\delta=C_{3}(1),

and fix
M\geq 1
and
0<\theta<1/2
. Constants may depend on
M
and
\theta
, but not on
\delta
or the smoothing parameter
\ell
. We use the parabolic energy norm

\left\|W\right\|_{\mathcal{Z}(I\times B)}^{2}=\left\|W\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(I\times B)}^{2}+\left\|\nabla W\right\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}(I\times B)}^{2}.

The dual residual norm
\mathcal{Y}
is any norm for which

|\langle F,W\rangle|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla W\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\|F\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}

for the localized test functions used below. An
L^{2}_{t}H^{-1}_{x}
-type norm is a useful model.

Proposition 7.1

(Model two-shadow stability)
.

Let
I=(s,T)
, and let
B\Subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a ball. This is a model estimate in an ideal localized setting. Assume that
U,V,V^{\ell}
are horizontal vector fields on
I\times B
, horizontally divergence-free, and that either no boundary terms arise, for instance in the full-space, periodic, or compactly supported setting, or that the localization has already been accompanied by a horizontal Helmholtz/Bogovskii-type correction so that the test fields below remain horizontally divergence-free and the pressure pairings vanish. Under these corrected local equations, assume

\displaystyle\partial_{t}U-\Delta U+(U\cdot\nabla_{h})U+\nabla_{h}P

\displaystyle=F_{\delta},

(7.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}V-\Delta V+(V\cdot\nabla_{h})V+\nabla_{h}Q

\displaystyle=0,

(7.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}V^{\ell}-\Delta V^{\ell}+(V^{\ell}\cdot\nabla_{h})V^{\ell}+\nabla_{h}Q^{\ell}

\displaystyle=0.

(7.3)

Assume also that

V(s)=U(s),\qquad V^{\ell}(s)=J_{\ell}U(s),

where
J_{\ell}
is a smoothing operator, and that

\int_{s}^{T}\left\|\nabla_{h}V(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\,dt\leq\Lambda.

(7.4)

Finally assume

\left\|F_{\delta}\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}(I\times B)}\leq A_{\delta},\qquad\left\|U(s)-J_{\ell}U(s)\right\|_{L^{2}(B)}\leq A_{\ell}.

(7.5)

Then, on every smaller cylinder
I^{\prime}\times B^{\prime}\Subset I\times B
,

\left\|U-V^{\ell}\right\|_{\mathcal{Z}(I^{\prime}\times B^{\prime})}\leq C_{B^{\prime},B,I^{\prime},I}e^{C\Lambda}(A_{\ell}+A_{\delta}).

(7.6)

Thus, in this model or corrected local setting, the smoothing error
A_{\ell}
is multiplied only by the delayed norm of the rough shadow
V
, while the high norm of
V^{\ell}
is avoided.

Proof.

Under the ideal or already-corrected localization assumptions in the statement, the energy test by the difference fields produces no uncompensated boundary, divergence, or pressure terms. Without such a correction, the following computation should be read only as a formal model; the closed localized estimate is precisely part of the prepared comparison input in Assumption
7.3
.

Set
W=U-V
. Subtracting (
7.2
) from (
7.1
) gives

\partial_{t}W-\Delta W+(U\cdot\nabla_{h})W+(W\cdot\nabla_{h})V+\nabla_{h}(P-Q)=F_{\delta}.

Testing by
W
, the transport term with velocity
U
vanishes because
\nabla_{h}\cdot U=0
, and the pressure term vanishes because
W
is horizontally divergence-free. Therefore

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|W\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla W\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\|\nabla_{h}V\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|W\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\langle F_{\delta},W\rangle.

By the definition of
\mathcal{Y}
,

\langle F_{\delta},W\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla W\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\|F_{\delta}\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}.

Since
W(s)=0
, Gronwall’s inequality and (
7.4
) yield

\left\|U-V\right\|_{\mathcal{Z}(I^{\prime}\times B^{\prime})}\leq Ce^{C\Lambda}A_{\delta}.

(7.7)

Next set
Z=V-V^{\ell}
. Subtracting (
7.3
) from (
7.2
), and writing

(V\cdot\nabla_{h})V-(V^{\ell}\cdot\nabla_{h})V^{\ell}=(V^{\ell}\cdot\nabla_{h})Z+(Z\cdot\nabla_{h})V,

one obtains

\partial_{t}Z-\Delta Z+(V^{\ell}\cdot\nabla_{h})Z+(Z\cdot\nabla_{h})V+\nabla_{h}(Q-Q^{\ell})=0.

Testing by
Z
, the transport term with velocity
V^{\ell}
vanishes because
\nabla_{h}\cdot V^{\ell}=0
, and the pressure term vanishes. Hence

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|Z\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla Z\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\|\nabla_{h}V\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|Z\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Since
Z(s)=U(s)-J_{\ell}U(s)
, Gronwall gives

\left\|V-V^{\ell}\right\|_{\mathcal{Z}(I^{\prime}\times B^{\prime})}\leq Ce^{C\Lambda}A_{\ell}.

(7.8)

Combining (
7.7
) and (
7.8
) proves (
7.6
).
∎

Remark 7.2
.

The identity

(V\cdot\nabla_{h})V-(V^{\ell}\cdot\nabla_{h})V^{\ell}=(V^{\ell}\cdot\nabla_{h})(V-V^{\ell})+((V-V^{\ell})\cdot\nabla_{h})V

is the key point. In the ideal or divergence-corrected setting, the term containing
V^{\ell}
cancels in the energy estimate by horizontal incompressibility. The coefficient that remains is
\nabla_{h}V
, with the high coefficient
\nabla_{h}V^{\ell}
absent. Thus the smoothing error is propagated by the rough shadow. In a bounded cylinder, the cutoff, pressure, and horizontal-divergence corrections must be supplied by the prepared comparison package rather than by this model calculation alone.

Assumption 7.3

(Prepared comparison estimate)
.

and

Remark 7.4
.

The term

7.1
explains why the smoothing error itself is kept outside that exponential factor; the actual localized cutoff and pressure corrections are part of the present assumption.

Theorem 7.5

(Logarithmic harmonic-pressure approximation)
.

Suppose Assumption
7.3
holds. Then there exist constants
C^{\prime}_{M,\theta}\geq 1
,
\sigma>0
, and
\delta_{M,\theta}\in(0,1)
such that every suitable weak solution
(u,p)
in
Q_{1}
satisfying
\Phi(1)\leq M
and
\delta=C_{3}(1)\leq\delta_{M,\theta}
obeys

\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{harm}}_{\theta/4}(u,p;M)\leq C^{\prime}_{M,\theta}|\log\delta|^{-\sigma}.

(7.10)

One may take
\sigma=a/N
, after decreasing
\delta_{M,\theta}
if necessary.

Proof.

By Assumption
7.3
,

\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{harm}}_{\theta/4}(u,p;M)\leq C_{M,\theta}\left(\ell^{a}+\ell^{-N}\delta^{b}+\exp(C_{M,\theta}\ell^{-N})\delta^{b}\right)

for every
0<\ell<\ell_{0}
. Set
L=|\log\delta|
. For
\delta
sufficiently small, choose

\ell=\left(\frac{2C_{M,\theta}}{bL}\right)^{1/N}.

Then
0<\ell<\ell_{0}
, and

C_{M,\theta}\ell^{-N}=\frac{b}{2}L.

Consequently,

\exp(C_{M,\theta}\ell^{-N})\delta^{b}=\exp\left(\frac{b}{2}L\right)\exp(-bL)=\exp\left(-\frac{b}{2}L\right)=\delta^{b/2}.

Moreover

\ell^{a}=\left(\frac{2C_{M,\theta}}{bL}\right)^{a/N}\leq C_{M,\theta}L^{-a/N},

and

\ell^{-N}\delta^{b}=\frac{bL}{2C_{M,\theta}}e^{-bL}\leq CL^{-a/N}

for all sufficiently large
L
. Also
\delta^{b/2}\leq CL^{-a/N}
. Hence (
7.10
) holds with
\sigma=a/N
.
∎

Corollary 7.6

(Logarithmic finite-scale decay and radius)
.

Suppose Assumption
7.3
holds. For every
M\geq 1
and
0<\theta<1/2
, there exist constants
C_{M,\theta}\geq 1
,
\sigma>0
, and
\delta_{M,\theta}\in(0,1)
such that if
\Phi(1)\leq M
and
\delta=C_{3}(1)\leq\delta_{M,\theta}
, then

\Psi(r)\leq C_{M,\theta}r+C_{M,\theta}r^{-2}|\log\delta|^{-\sigma}

(7.11)

for all sufficiently small
r
. In particular, after decreasing
\delta_{M,\theta}
,

r_{\mathrm{reg}}(0,0)\geq c_{M,\theta}|\log\delta|^{-\sigma/3}.

(7.12)

Proof.

The decay estimate (
7.11
) follows by repeating the proof of Theorem
6.1
, replacing
\omega_{M,\theta}(\delta)
by the logarithmic bound (
7.10
) at scale
\theta/4
. Let
L=|\log\delta|
, and choose
r=L^{-\sigma/3}
. Then

r^{-2}L^{-\sigma}=L^{2\sigma/3}L^{-\sigma}=L^{-\sigma/3},\qquad r=L^{-\sigma/3}.

Thus (
7.11
) gives
\Psi(r)\leq C_{M,\theta}L^{-\sigma/3}
. For
L
sufficiently large, this is below
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}
, and Theorem
3.2
gives (
7.12
).
∎

Theorem
2.4
is Theorem
7.5
and Corollary
7.6
.

8
Relaxed shadowing and conditional power-type approximation

The compactness and logarithmic layers compare the solution with the strict limiting system (
4.3
). The relaxed-shadowing layer enlarges the comparison class. The goal is to avoid a nonlinear projection onto the strict compatibility condition
\partial_{3}q=0
.

8.1
Weighted vertical pressure compatibility

Let
Q=\omega_{h}\times I_{3}\times I_{t}\Subset Q_{3/4}
, where
\omega_{h}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
I_{3}=(a,b)
. Choose
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I_{3})
with
\int_{I_{3}}\zeta=1
. For a scalar
f
, define

\langle f\rangle_{\zeta}(x_{h},t)=\int_{I_{3}}\zeta(y)f(x_{h},y,t)\,dy,\qquad\Pi_{\zeta}f=f-\langle f\rangle_{\zeta}.

For
\varphi_{h}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, set
f_{\varphi}=\nabla_{h}\cdot\varphi_{h}
, and define

g_{\varphi}(x_{h},x_{3},t)=f_{\varphi}(x_{h},x_{3},t)-\zeta(x_{3})\int_{I_{3}}f_{\varphi}(x_{h},y,t)\,dy.

Then
\int_{I_{3}}g_{\varphi}\,dx_{3}=0
. The vertical antiderivative

B_{\varphi}(x_{h},x_{3},t)=\int_{a}^{x_{3}}g_{\varphi}(x_{h},s,t)\,ds

is compactly supported in the vertical variable and satisfies
\partial_{3}B_{\varphi}=g_{\varphi}
. Indeed,
g_{\varphi}
has zero
x_{3}
-average and is supported away from the endpoints of
I_{3}
, because both
\varphi_{h}
and
\zeta
are compactly supported in the vertical interval; hence
B_{\varphi}
vanishes near
a
and near
b
. Define

\left\|\varphi_{h}\right\|_{Y_{\zeta}(Q)}=\left\|(\partial_{t}+\Delta)B_{\varphi}\right\|_{L^{3/2}(Q)}+\left\|\nabla_{h}B_{\varphi}\right\|_{L^{3}(Q)}+\left\|\partial_{3}B_{\varphi}\right\|_{L^{3}(Q)}.

Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1})
satisfy
\chi\equiv 1
on
B_{3/4}
. Write

p^{hh}=R_{a}R_{b}(\chi u_{a}u_{b}),\qquad p^{\mathrm{rem}}=2R_{a}R_{3}(\chi u_{a}u_{3})+R_{3}R_{3}(\chi u_{3}^{2}),\qquad a,b\in\{1,2\}.

After subtracting a function of time,

p=p^{hh}+p^{\mathrm{rem}}+h\quad\text{in }Q_{3/4},

where
h(\cdot,t)
is harmonic in space. Set

P=p^{hh}+h=p-p^{\mathrm{rem}}.

Lemma 8.1

(Vertical pressure remainder)
.

If
\Phi(1)\leq M
and
C_{3}(1)=\delta\leq 1
, then

\left\|p^{\mathrm{rem}}\right\|_{L^{3/2}(Q_{3/4})}\leq C(M)\delta^{1/3}.

The same estimate holds on every smaller cylinder
Q_{\rho}\Subset Q_{3/4}
, with a constant depending on
\rho
.

Proof.

The boundedness of Riesz transforms on
L^{3/2}
gives

\left\|p^{\mathrm{rem}}\right\|_{L^{3/2}}\leq C\left\|u_{h}u_{3}\right\|_{L^{3/2}}+C\left\|u_{3}^{2}\right\|_{L^{3/2}}.

Holder’s inequality yields

\left\|u_{h}u_{3}\right\|_{L^{3/2}(Q_{1})}\leq\left\|u_{h}\right\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}\left\|u_{3}\right\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}\leq C(M)\delta^{1/3},

and
\left\|u_{3}^{2}\right\|_{L^{3/2}}=\left\|u_{3}\right\|_{L^{3}}^{2}=\delta^{2/3}\leq\delta^{1/3}
.
∎

Lemma 8.2

(Weighted vertical pressure compatibility)
.

For every product cylinder
Q\Subset Q_{3/4}
and every
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I_{3})
with
\int_{I_{3}}\zeta=1
,

\left\|\nabla_{h}\Pi_{\zeta}P\right\|_{Y^{\prime}_{\zeta}(Q)}\leq C(M,Q,\zeta)\delta^{1/3}.

Equivalently,

\left\|\nabla_{h}(P-\langle P\rangle_{\zeta})\right\|_{Y^{\prime}_{\zeta}(Q)}\leq C(M,Q,\zeta)C_{3}(1)^{1/3}.

Proof.

For
\varphi_{h}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, integration by parts gives

\langle\nabla_{h}\Pi_{\zeta}P,\varphi_{h}\rangle=\langle\partial_{3}P,B_{\varphi}\rangle.

(8.1)

Indeed,

\langle\nabla_{h}\Pi_{\zeta}P,\varphi_{h}\rangle=-\int_{Q}\Pi_{\zeta}Pf_{\varphi}\,dx\,dt=-\int_{Q}Pg_{\varphi}\,dx\,dt=-\int_{Q}P\partial_{3}B_{\varphi}\,dx\,dt.

Since
B_{\varphi}
is compactly supported, this equals
\langle\partial_{3}P,B_{\varphi}\rangle
.

Because
P=p-p^{\mathrm{rem}}
, the vertical momentum equation gives

\partial_{3}P=-\partial_{t}u_{3}+\Delta u_{3}-\nabla_{h}\cdot(u_{h}u_{3})-\partial_{3}(u_{3}^{2})-\partial_{3}p^{\mathrm{rem}}.

Pairing with
B_{\varphi}
and moving derivatives to the test function gives

\displaystyle|\langle\partial_{3}P,B_{\varphi}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|u_{3}\right\|_{L^{3}}\left\|(\partial_{t}+\Delta)B_{\varphi}\right\|_{L^{3/2}}+\left\|u_{h}u_{3}\right\|_{L^{3/2}}\left\|\nabla_{h}B_{\varphi}\right\|_{L^{3}}

\displaystyle+\left\|u_{3}^{2}\right\|_{L^{3/2}}\left\|\partial_{3}B_{\varphi}\right\|_{L^{3}}+\left\|p^{\mathrm{rem}}\right\|_{L^{3/2}}\left\|\partial_{3}B_{\varphi}\right\|_{L^{3}}.

Using
\Phi(1)\leq M
,
C_{3}(1)=\delta
, and Lemma
8.1
, we obtain

|\langle\nabla_{h}\Pi_{\zeta}P,\varphi_{h}\rangle|\leq C(M,Q,\zeta)\delta^{1/3}\left\|\varphi_{h}\right\|_{Y_{\zeta}(Q)}.

Taking the supremum proves the lemma.
∎

8.2
Relaxed no-stretching comparison

The relaxed comparison system is

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v_{h}-\Delta v_{h}+(v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})v_{h}+\nabla_{h}\pi=0,\\
\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{h}=0.\end{cases}

(8.2)

Set
V=(v_{h},0)
. Then
\nabla\cdot V=0
, and

\partial_{t}V-\Delta V+(V\cdot\nabla)V+\nabla\pi=(0,0,\partial_{3}\pi).

(8.3)

The pressure is allowed to satisfy
\partial_{3}\pi\neq 0
. The vertical vorticity
\omega=\partial_{1}v_{2}-\partial_{2}v_{1}
satisfies

\partial_{t}\omega-\Delta\omega+v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\omega=0,

and the three-dimensional vortex-stretching term is absent.

Assumption 8.3

(Buffered relaxed strong bound)
.

For every
M\geq 1
and every sufficiently small fixed
\theta>0
, the solution
(v_{h},\pi)
of (
8.2
) generated from the good-time horizontally divergence-free datum constructed below exists on a forward buffered cylinder containing the target cylinder, and satisfies

\left\|V\right\|_{C^{1}}+\left\|\pi\right\|_{C^{1}}\leq K_{B}(M,\theta)

(8.4)

on that buffered cylinder.

Remark 8.4
.

This assumption is suggested by the scalar no-stretching vorticity equation and the parabolic buffer between the good time and the target cylinder. A fully unconditional power-rate theorem requires this local buffered regularity estimate, either proved directly or supplied by an applicable cited theorem with matching local hypotheses.

Fix
0<\theta<1/16
, and set

I_{-}:=(-16\theta^{2},-9\theta^{2}).

Averaging the local energy and vertical-component bounds gives a good time
s\in I_{-}
such that

\int_{B_{4\theta}}|u(x,s)|^{2}\,dx+\theta^{2}\int_{B_{4\theta}}|\nabla u(x,s)|^{2}\,dx\leq C(M,\theta),

(8.5)

and

\int_{B_{4\theta}}|u_{3}(x,s)|^{3}\,dx\leq C(\theta)\delta.

(8.6)

Using a finite family of product charts and horizontal Helmholtz projections on each
x_{3}
-slice, one obtains a patched horizontal datum
a_{s}
with
\nabla_{h}\cdot a_{s}=0
locally and

\left\|u_{h}(s)-a_{s}\right\|_{H^{-m}(B_{3\theta})}+\left\|u_{3}(s)\right\|_{H^{-m}(B_{3\theta})}\leq C(M,\theta)\delta^{1/3}

(8.7)

for
m
sufficiently large. Indeed, in a product chart the horizontal projection error is generated by

u_{h}-\mathbb{P}_{h}u_{h}=\nabla_{h}\Delta_{h,D}^{-1}\nabla_{h}\cdot u_{h}=-\nabla_{h}\Delta_{h,D}^{-1}\partial_{3}u_{3},

and the derivative is transferred to a smooth test function in the
H^{-m}
-pairing.

Let
(v_{h},\pi)
solve (
8.2
) with
v_{h}(s)=a_{s}
, and set
V=(v_{h},0)
.

Assumption 8.5

(Localized relaxed weak–strong stability)
.

Assume
\Phi(1)\leq M
,
C_{3}(1)=\delta\leq 1
, and Assumption
8.3
. For the relaxed comparison field
V
constructed from the good-time projected datum, there exist constants
C_{S}(M,\theta)\geq 1
and
\gamma>0
such that

\left\|u-V\right\|_{L^{3}(Q_{2\theta})}\leq C_{S}(M,\theta)\delta^{\gamma}.

(8.8)

Remark 8.6
.

Formally, this is suggested by a localized relative-energy inequality for
W=u-V
. The residual in (
8.3
) contributes

\int\partial_{3}\pi\,u_{3},

which is small because
\partial_{3}\pi
is bounded by Assumption
8.3
and
u_{3}
is small in
L^{3}
. In a bounded local cylinder, cutoff and pressure terms must be treated carefully. Assumption
8.5
records the closed local estimate needed for the conditional theorem.

8.3
Pressure reconstruction and power-type decay

The pressure
\pi
in (
8.2
) differs from the pressure entering
D(r)
. Define the local Navier–Stokes-compatible pressure generated by
V
:

Q_{V}=R_{i}R_{j}(\chi V_{i}V_{j})=R_{a}R_{b}(\chi v_{a}v_{b}),\qquad a,b\in\{1,2\},

where
\chi
is equal to one on the target interior cylinder.

Proposition 8.7

(Pressure reconstruction)
.

Under Assumptions
8.3
and
8.5
, there exists a pressure corrector
h
, harmonic in space on
Q_{\theta}
, such that

\left\|p-Q_{V}-h\right\|_{L^{3/2}(Q_{\theta})}\leq C(M,\theta)\left\|u-V\right\|_{L^{3}(Q_{2\theta})}.

(8.9)

Consequently,

\left\|p-Q_{V}-h\right\|_{L^{3/2}(Q_{\theta})}\leq C(M,\theta)\delta^{\gamma},

with
\gamma
as in (
8.8
).

Proof.

Use the local pressure decomposition

p=R_{i}R_{j}(\chi u_{i}u_{j})+h,

where
h
is harmonic in space on the interior cylinder. Then

p-Q_{V}-h=R_{i}R_{j}(\chi(u_{i}u_{j}-V_{i}V_{j})).

Calderon–Zygmund estimates give

\left\|p-Q_{V}-h\right\|_{L^{3/2}(Q_{\theta})}\leq C\left\|u\otimes u-V\otimes V\right\|_{L^{3/2}(Q_{2\theta})}.

Since

u\otimes u-V\otimes V=(u-V)\otimes u+V\otimes(u-V),

the right-hand side is bounded by
C(M,\theta)\left\|u-V\right\|_{L^{3}(Q_{2\theta})}
, using the local
L^{3}
-bound for
u
and the buffered strong bound for
V
.
∎

Define the relaxed harmonic excess

\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{rel},\mathrm{harm}}_{\theta}(u,p;V,Q_{V},h)=\theta^{-2}\int_{Q_{\theta}}|u-V|^{3}\,dx\,dt+\theta^{-2}\int_{Q_{\theta}}|p-Q_{V}-h|^{3/2}\,dx\,dt.

Theorem 8.8

(Hybrid relaxed harmonic approximation)
.

Assume
\Phi(1)\leq M
,
C_{3}(1)=\delta\leq 1
, and Assumptions
8.3
and
8.5
. Then there exist a smooth relaxed no-stretching comparison pair
(V,Q_{V})
and a spatially harmonic pressure corrector
h\in\mathcal{H}(Q_{\theta})
such that

\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{rel},\mathrm{harm}}_{\theta}(u,p;V,Q_{V},h)\leq C(M,\theta)\delta^{\Gamma}

(8.10)

for some
\Gamma>0
depending only on the exponent in Assumption
8.5
.

Proof.

By Assumption
8.5
,
\left\|u-V\right\|_{L^{3}(Q_{\theta})}\leq C(M,\theta)\delta^{\gamma}
. Hence

\theta^{-2}\int_{Q_{\theta}}|u-V|^{3}\,dx\,dt\leq C(M,\theta)\delta^{3\gamma}.

By Proposition
8.7
,

\theta^{-2}\int_{Q_{\theta}}|p-Q_{V}-h|^{3/2}\,dx\,dt\leq C(M,\theta)\delta^{3\gamma/2}.

Set
\Gamma=\min\{3\gamma,3\gamma/2\}
, decreasing it if necessary.
∎

The comparison pair is smooth in the buffered target cylinder. Thus there exist
\alpha>0
and
C(M,\theta)
such that

C_{V}(r)+D_{Q_{V}}(r)\leq C(M,\theta)r^{\alpha},\qquad 0<r<r_{H}(M,\theta).

(8.11)

For a
C^{1}
-comparison one may take any fixed
\alpha\leq 1
; with more regularity one obtains better exponents. The argument below only uses positivity of this exponent.

Theorem 8.9

(Conditional hybrid power-type finite-scale decay)
.

Assume Assumptions
8.3
and
8.5
. There exist constants
C_{H}(M,\theta)\geq 1
,
\alpha>0
,
\Gamma>0
, and
r_{H}(M,\theta)>0
such that every suitable weak solution in
Q_{1}
with
\Phi(1)\leq M
and
C_{3}(1)=\delta\leq 1
obeys

\Psi(r)\leq C_{H}(M,\theta)r^{\alpha}+C_{H}(M,\theta)r^{-2}\delta^{\Gamma},\qquad 0<r<r_{H}(M,\theta).

(8.12)

Proof.

Let
(V,Q_{V},h)
be the comparison triple from Theorem
8.8
. For the velocity part,

|u|^{3}\leq C|V|^{3}+C|u-V|^{3},

so

C_{u}(r)\leq CC_{V}(r)+Cr^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}}|u-V|^{3}\,dx\,dt.

Using (
8.11
) and (
8.10
),

C_{u}(r)\leq C(M,\theta)r^{\alpha}+C(M,\theta)r^{-2}\delta^{\Gamma}.

For the pressure, write

p=Q_{V}+h+g,\qquad g=p-Q_{V}-h.

The comparison pressure decays by (
8.11
). The harmonic part is controlled by Lemma
3.5
; its fixed-scale oscillation is bounded by
C(M,\theta)
from the decomposition and (
8.10
). The error
g
contributes
Cr^{-2}\delta^{\Gamma}
. Thus

D_{p}(r)\leq C(M,\theta)r^{\alpha}+C(M,\theta)r^{-2}\delta^{\Gamma}.

Adding the estimates for
C_{u}(r)
and
D_{p}(r)
gives (
8.12
).
∎

Corollary 8.10

(Conditional power-type CKN scales)
.

Under Assumptions
8.3
and
8.5
, the decay estimate (
8.12
) yields an explicit admissible CKN scale

r_{\delta}\simeq\delta^{\Gamma/(\alpha+2)}.

Consequently, there are constants
c_{H}(M,\theta)>0
and
\delta_{H}(M,\theta)>0
such that, if
0<C_{3}(1)=\delta\leq\delta_{H}(M,\theta)
, then

r_{\mathrm{reg}}(0,0)\geq c_{H}(M,\theta)\delta^{\Gamma/(\alpha+2)}.

This traceable scale is not intended to be optimal for very small
\delta
. The same decay estimate also gives the stronger fixed-radius statement: after possibly decreasing
\delta_{H}(M,\theta)
, there exists
\rho_{H}(M,\theta)>0
such that

r_{\mathrm{reg}}(0,0)\geq\rho_{H}(M,\theta)

for all
0<\delta\leq\delta_{H}(M,\theta)
.

Proof.

Fix a constant
0<c_{0}\leq 1
, for instance
c_{0}=1
, and set

r_{\delta}=c_{0}\delta^{\Gamma/(\alpha+2)}.

Choose
\delta_{H}\in(0,1]
sufficiently small so that

c_{0}\delta_{H}^{\Gamma/(\alpha+2)}<r_{H}(M,\theta)

and

C_{H}(M,\theta)\bigl(c_{0}^{\alpha}+c_{0}^{-2}\bigr)\delta_{H}^{\alpha\Gamma/(\alpha+2)}\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}.

Then, for every
0<\delta\leq\delta_{H}
, one has
0<r_{\delta}<r_{H}(M,\theta)
. Applying (
8.12
) at the scale
r_{\delta}
gives

\displaystyle\Psi(r_{\delta})

\displaystyle\leq C_{H}r_{\delta}^{\alpha}+C_{H}r_{\delta}^{-2}\delta^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=C_{H}c_{0}^{\alpha}\delta^{\alpha\Gamma/(\alpha+2)}+C_{H}c_{0}^{-2}\delta^{-2\Gamma/(\alpha+2)}\delta^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=C_{H}\bigl(c_{0}^{\alpha}+c_{0}^{-2}\bigr)\delta^{\alpha\Gamma/(\alpha+2)}\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}.

Theorem
3.2
gives

r_{\mathrm{reg}}(0,0)\geq\kappa r_{\delta}=\kappa c_{0}\delta^{\Gamma/(\alpha+2)}.

Thus the first conclusion holds with
c_{H}(M,\theta)=\kappa c_{0}
.

For the fixed-radius statement, choose

r_{M}=\min\left\{\frac{r_{H}(M,\theta)}{2},\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}}{2C_{H}(M,\theta)}\right)^{1/\alpha}\right\}.

Then
C_{H}r_{M}^{\alpha}\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}/2
. Decrease
\delta_{H}
, if necessary, so that

C_{H}r_{M}^{-2}\delta_{H}^{\Gamma}\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}/2.

For every
0<\delta\leq\delta_{H}
, (
8.12
) gives
\Psi(r_{M})\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}
. Hence Theorem
3.2
gives

r_{\mathrm{reg}}(0,0)\geq\rho_{H}(M,\theta),\qquad\rho_{H}(M,\theta)=\kappa r_{M}.

∎

Theorem
2.5
follows from Theorem
8.9
and Corollary
8.10
.

9
Remarks and limitations

Remark 9.1

(Finite-scale and qualitative character)
.

The unconditional theorem is finite-scale because it produces a positive radius
\rho_{*}(M)
at which regularity is guaranteed. The constants
\rho_{*}(M)
and
\varepsilon_{*}(M)
are obtained through the compactness modulus
\omega_{M,\theta}
, so the statement remains qualitative.

Remark 9.2

(Role of harmonic pressure)
.

The proof keeps estimates of
\left\|\nabla q\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
for the complete limiting pressure outside the argument. The non-harmonic pressure is controlled by local Calderon–Zygmund estimates in finite
L^{s}
-spaces. The harmonic part is kept in the excess and later controlled by harmonic oscillation decay.

Remark 9.3

(What the logarithmic layer proves)
.

The logarithmic layer records Assumption
7.3
as an input and shows that a specific prepared comparison estimate, with an exponential-in-
\ell^{-1}
stability loss but with the smoothing error outside that exponential, is enough to replace the compactness modulus by a logarithmic one. This isolates the next quantitative problem.

Remark 9.4

(What the relaxed layer proves)
.

The relaxed layer uses a smooth no-stretching comparison pair modulo harmonic pressure in place of an exact strict limiting-system solution satisfying
\partial_{3}q=0
. This relaxed comparison is sufficient for
\Psi=C+D
-decay because the CKN criterion requires smallness of
C+D
at one scale.

Remark 9.5

(Remaining quantitative inputs)
.

The remaining inputs for a power-rate theorem are Assumptions
8.3
and
8.5
. The first asks for a buffered strong bound for the relaxed no-stretching comparison flow. The second asks for a localized relaxed weak–strong stability estimate controlling cutoff and pressure terms. Once these inputs are available, the pressure reconstruction argument gives the power-type decay estimate (
8.12
).

Remark 9.6

(Possible refinements)
.

A stronger quantitative theorem may follow from one of two approaches. The first is to prove the buffered relaxed strong bound directly from the scalar no-stretching vorticity equation and close the localized relaxed weak–strong stability estimate with cutoffs and pressure terms. The second is to establish a Reynolds-stress harmonic approximation theorem in which the smoothing commutator contributes as a static subscale energy with no Gronwall amplification. Both approaches aim to replace the abstract compactness modulus by a traceable logarithmic or power rate.
