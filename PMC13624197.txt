IP-ConTex: detail-consistent texture generation with image prompt

Lei Wang, Jieqing Feng

Abstract

Textures are critical for enhancing the visual fidelity and diversity of three-dimensional (3D) models. Recently, generative models have significantly advanced texture generation. However, fine-grained control of the generation process remains challenging. Hence, we propose IP-ConTex, which is a novel image-guided texture-generation method that introduces appearance control into the diffusion process to ensure detail-consistent results. First, synchronized multiview diffusion is employed to maintain structural and layout consistency across multiple views. Subsequently, a new appearance-control module is designed for the pretrained diffusion model. By leveraging cross-attention control, self-attention control, diffusion feature synchronization, and color adjustment, appearance information from the reference image is effectively extracted and injected into the texture-generation process. Experimental results demonstrate that IP-ConTex successfully transfers appearance details to 3D geometries without fine-tuning or optimization, thus achieving high-quality and detail-consistent texture generation.

Introduction

Owing to rapid digitalization, fields such as digital twins, architectural design, films, and games increasingly use three-dimensional (3D) assets in content creation to enhance visual effects and user interaction. Textures are critical in improving the visual realism of 3D geometric models. These 3D models must not only yield high-quality and diverse appearances but also enable fine-grained control over the generation process. Recent advances in generative artificial intelligence have introduced new paradigms for 3D content creation. In particular, denoising diffusion models [, ] have demonstrated remarkable performance in text-to-image generation tasks and have been effectively applied to texture-generation tasks.

Current text-to-image diffusion models [] generate high-quality images that align well with textual descriptions. However, texture generation remains a significant challenge. It requires the generated texture to conform to the user’s prompts in appearance as well as fit the geometric surface and maintain consistency across multiple views. Hence, researchers have investigated various methods for generating high-quality textures, including score distillation sampling (SDS) [] to indirectly leverage the pretrained diffusion model as a prior [, ], or directly generating multiview images with diffusion models and subsequently aggregating them into texture space [, ]. Nevertheless, most existing methods rely solely on text prompts as control conditions. Compared with text, images can convey user intent more intuitively and accurately. Existing image-guided texture-generation methods either achieve only style consistency while compromising appearance details from the reference image or require time-intensive fine-tuning to capture those details. Thus, achieving efficient and precisely controlled texture generation remains a significant challenge.

Therefore, for image-guided texture generation, we propose IP-ConTex, which is a novel texture-generation method that leverages a pretrained two-dimensional (2D) image diffusion model. IP-ConTex enables detail-preserving texture generation while maintaining multiview consistency without requiring fine-tuning or optimization. First, it uses a synchronized multiview diffusion framework [] that shares the generated content across views in UV space at each denoising timestep, thereby ensuring structural and layout consistency across views. Subsequently, the appearance of the reference image is injected into the generation process via a novel appearance-control module. This module achieves fine-grained control and multiview consistency using four components: cross-attention control, self-attention control, diffusion feature synchronization, and color adjustment. Finally, experiments demonstrate that IP-ConTex can achieve stronger appearance preservation while maintaining competitive multiview consistency compared with existing state-of-the-art methods.

The main contributions of the proposed method are as follows:A novel image-guided texture generation method, IP-ConTex, is proposed, which introduces an appearance-control module to achieve fine-grained appearance control during texture generation. The method yields high-quality, detail-consistent textures for arbitrary 3D geometric models without fine-tuning or additional optimization.A diffusion feature-synchronization strategy is proposed, which fuses intermediate U-Net feature maps across views in UV space at each denoising timestep, thus enabling fine-grained appearance details to propagate consistently across all views and effectively suppressing blurring and ghosting artifacts in the final texture.A hybrid attention scheme combining cross-image self-attention and multiview shared attention is designed for the multiview texture-generation setting. It establishes semantic correspondence between the reference image and target views while promoting appearance consistency across views with considerable viewpoint disparities.

Related studies

Transferring the appearance of a specified image into a 3D model is challenging. Early studies [–] on texture generation focused on synthesizing tileable texture maps from example patterns. Additionally, researchers [, ] investigated the correlation between geometric surface features and local texture variations, thus enabling geometry-aware texture synthesis. However, owing to inadequate perceptual understanding of both images and geometry, conventional methods fail to generate semantically rich textures on complex 3D structures. Meanwhile, owing to the rapid advancement of deep learning, convolutional neural networks have proven to be highly effective in image-related tasks. Various generative models, such as generative adversarial networks [, ], variational autoencoders [, ], and recent diffusion models[, ], have been developed and applied to texture-generation tasks. Some methods, including Texturify [], Mesh2Tex [], and PointUV [], attempt to generate textures directly from 3D data by designing specialized texture representations for supervised learning. However, despite the abundance of 2D image data, high-quality 3D datasets are scarce and cannot be easily obtained and annotated. Therefore, the generalizability of these methods is limited owing to the low diversity of 3D training data. Recently, diffusion models trained on large-scale 2D image datasets have emerged as promising alternatives for high-quality 3D content generation. These models offer superior visual fidelity and flexible multimodal conditioning, thus rendering them suitable for image-guided texture generation.

Using pretrained 2D diffusion models for texture generation

Owing to significant advancements in image generation, recent texture-generation methods have leveraged pretrained 2D diffusion models to overcome the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality 3D training datasets. These methods have significantly promoted the progress of texture generation, thereby enabling the generation of diverse and high-quality textures for 3D geometric models. Existing methods that utilize 2D diffusion models for texture generation can be broadly categorized into two groups.

The first category of methods uses SDS[] or related techniques such as variational score distillation (VSD) [] to indirectly employ the pretrained diffusion model as a prior. SDS enables the use of generative knowledge from diffusion models to optimize parameterized representations (e.g., neural radiance fields), thereby establishing the foundation for many recent 3D generation frameworks. These methods use views generated by diffusion models as supervisory signals to guide the optimization of texture parameters. One representative method is latent paint [], which is among the first to apply SDS to latent diffusion models []. It propagates SDS-based gradients to a latent-space texture map through differentiable rendering. However, latent-space optimization at low resolutions typically results in blurring and loss of fine details in the generated RGB textures. Hence, researchers have proposed novel texture parameterizations to improve texture quality within the SDS-based optimization framework. For instance, Paint-it [] introduces deep convolutional physically based rendering parameterization. Instead of directly optimizing pixel values in the texture map, it optimizes a neural proxy that can simulate the physical surface properties, thus yielding more realistic textures. SceneTex [] proposes a multiresolution texture field to implicitly encode the appearance of 3D scenes, thereby enabling flexible learning of both low- and high-frequency appearance features. Despite these advancements, SDS-based optimization methods require long convergence times, yield overly saturated colors, and lack output diversity. Consequently, the resulting textures do not exhibit the quality and realism achieved through direct 2D image generation.

The second category of methods directly uses the diffusion model to denoise images rendered from multiple viewpoints and then back-projects the generated views into UV space to update the texture map. These methods typically incorporate geometric information—such as depth or normal maps—to guide or condition the denoising process, thereby ensuring alignment between the generated texture and the underlying 3D geometry. Specifically, the view images are rendered sequentially from different camera positions. At each viewpoint, a 2D diffusion model performs inpainting based on partial content from neighboring views, thus allowing more detailed textures to be generated while improving computational efficiency. For example, TEXTure [] and Text2Tex [] progressively generate local textures from multiple predefined viewpoints. Missing regions are inpainted, and the inpainted results are then backprojected into the texture space to iteratively refine the texture map. However, because of the asynchronous denoising of multiple views, the resulting texture lacks global consistency constraints. Consequently, this progressive texture update strategy results in inconsistencies across views and thus visible seams. Moreover, these methods are susceptible to the Janus problem, wherein similar frontal appearances are generated for both the front and back views, thereby resulting in unrealistic ‘multi-faced’ textures. Additionally, the generated views may contain inconsistent lighting, thus hindering their applicability in downstream applications such as relighting or physically based rendering. Hence, researchers have proposed improved approaches within this category of methods. Paint3D [] trains a diffusion model directly in UV space and adopts a coarse-to-fine texture-generation strategy. Initially, images generated from each viewpoint are used as the initial texture map. Subsequently, a position map of the geometric model is used as a conditioning input to refine the texture map and remove lighting information. Similarly, Meta 3D TextureGen [] focuses on refining texture maps in UV space. MVPaint [] introduces a multiview generation model that first generates synchronized multiview images to mitigate the Janus problem. Subsequently, it fills in unobserved regions in the 3D point cloud, thereby minimizing dependence on the continuity of UV unwrapping. Finally, it applies a super-resolution model and seam-smoothing algorithm to further enhance texture quality. Unlike methods such as TEXTure, some recent methods conduct multiview denoising and texture aggregation synchronously to promote multiview consistency. For example, TexFusion [] iteratively denoises each view at every timestep and updates a shared latent texture map accordingly. Meanwhile, SyncMVD [] fuses denoised content from all views at each denoising timestep. These methods synchronize multiview denoising on a shared texture map, thus effectively improving the multiview consistency of the generated texture.

Generating textures with image prompts

Most existing methods focus on generating textures guided by textual prompts. However, accurately describing complex and fine-grained patterns using only text is challenging. By contrast, images provide a more intuitive method for conveying user intent. Several methods have been proposed to investigate image-guided texture generation. TEXTure [] proposes a method to extract texture information from an existing textured model or several images. It employs text inversion [] to learn a specialized token that represents the reference appearance. This token, along with the reference images, is then used to fine-tune the pretrained diffusion model using DreamBooth [], thereby enabling the generation of personalized textures for new 3D models. Similarly, TextureDreamer [] applies DreamBooth fine-tuning to extract appearance features from sparse input images and then uses VSD optimization to transfer textures onto arbitrary 3D geometries. In addition to fine-tuning-based methods, other methods such as Paint3D [] and FlexiTex [] utilize IP-Adapter [] to inject appearance information from reference images directly into the diffusion model during the generation process. StyleTex [] extracts style features from a reference image to guide consistent texture generation. AlignTex [] trains an AlignNet to align generated images with the input multiview artwork and a 3D mesh. TexTrans [] utilizes multiview reference images to transfer 3D textures.

Existing image-guided texture-generation methods can effectively extract appearance information from reference images. However, these methods either incur additional computational costs owing to fine-tuning or only achieve style-level consistency, thus rendering it difficult to preserve fine-grained appearance details. In 2D image generation and editing, some methods generate images that are consistent with the appearance of a reference image [–] or perform complex nonrigid editing [–]. Although these approaches successfully preserve appearance details, they are typically limited to single-image applications and cannot be generalized to 3D domains. By contrast, 3D texture generation requires not only conformity to the reference image but also 3D consistency across multiple views. Hence, we propose a novel method that enables image-guided detail-consistent texture generation without requiring fine-tuning or additional optimization, thereby improving both efficiency and consistency. Unlike prior approaches that either apply appearance control to single images or adopt image prompts only through cross-attention, our method introduces a dedicated feature-level synchronization mechanism and a hybrid attention design that accommodate the geometric constraints of multiview texture generation.

Terminology on consistency

To avoid ambiguity, we distinguish three related but different notions herein. Style consistency refers to the similarity of global visual characteristics—such as overall color tone or artistic style—between the generated result and reference image. Appearance preservation refers to the consistent transfer of specific visual attributes from the reference image, including local patterns, fine-grained details, and characteristic surface structures. 3D consistency refers to the agreement of generated content across viewpoints, such that the same surface region of the 3D model is assigned compatible appearance when observed from different views.

Appearance control

The goal of appearance control is to generate textures that exhibit the appearance of an object in the input reference image while preserving fine-grained visual details. Recent studies [, , ] demonstrated that the cross- and self-attention mechanisms within a denoising network encode rich semantic information regarding the generated image. By leveraging the semantic understanding capabilities of the diffusion network, the proposed appearance-control module injects appearance information from the reference image into the texture-generation process.

The appearance-control module comprises four components, as shown in Fig. . First, we apply cross-attention control using IP-Adapter [] to establish the initial structural layout and style of the texture during the early denoising stages. The generated content is synchronized across different views using the synchronized multiview diffusion framework introduced in subsection “”. Second, self-attention control retrieves content from the reference image and then injects it into the target view to preserve the appearance details. Third, to ensure the consistent propagation of appearance details across views, we propose a diffusion feature-synchronization method. Finally, we perform color adjustment using adaptive instance normalization (AdaIN) [] to align the color distribution of the generated latent-view images with that of the reference image at each timestep. Cross-attention control

Recent studies [] demonstrated that cross-attention maps typically encode conceptual information regarding an object and are associated with the semantics of input prompts, whereas self-attention maps capture shape, structure, and fine-grained visual details. IP-Adapter [] is a lightweight image prompt adapter designed for Stable Diffusion models. It employs a decoupled cross-attention mechanism that allows images to be used directly as prompts to control both the style and semantics of the generated images. An additional cross-attention block is integrated to process the image features in each cross-attention layer of the U-Net diffusion model.

Within the synchronized multiview diffusion framework, the structural layout of each target view is approximately established during the early denoising stages. Although a synchronized diffusion strategy enables information exchange across overlapping regions between adjacent views, eliminating inconsistencies in the structural layout or color style across different views remains challenging. Since the structure and style established in the early denoising stage significantly affect the final image appearance, our method injects appearance information from the reference image into the generation process via IP-Adapter and applies it to all views. Compared with text prompts, image prompts provide more explicit and detailed guidance. Therefore, the denoising of each view is guided more consistently, thus promoting global consistency of semantics and style across views. However, using IP-Adapter may introduce new visual features that can compromise appearance consistency. Self-attention control

Although IP-Adapter can generate images with stylistic similarity to the reference image, it fails to synthesize highly consistent appearance details. Previous studies [, ] demonstrated that modifying the key and value features of the self-attention layer offers finer control over the generated content and enables the transfer of detailed appearance information between images. Therefore, we introduce cross-image attention based on the self-attention layer of the diffusion model to implicitly establish semantic correspondences between images. Specifically, we use the Query features from the target view image, and the key and value features from the reference image. This cross-image attention allows the diffusion model to establish semantic correspondence between two images, thereby injecting detailed appearance information from the reference image into the target view during the generation process.

Diffusion feature synchronization

By controlling the cross-attention and self-attention during the denoising of the target views, the appearance information from the reference image can be effectively injected into the generated views. Moreover, attending to adjacent views further enhances the appearance consistency across all views. In texture generation, 3D consistency must be maintained across views, which implies that the content corresponding to the same spatial location of the 3D model should remain consistent across different views. Otherwise, blurring and ghosting artifacts will emerge in the final aggregated texture.

The synchronized multiview diffusion strategy described in subsection “” is applied only during the early denoising stages to rapidly establish structural consistency across views. Because the appearance details are gradually refined throughout the denoising process, maintaining 3D consistency during denoising is essential. Hence, we propose a diffusion feature-synchronization strategy to promote 3D consistency of appearance details across multiple views.

The intermediate features of the U-Net within the diffusion model contain rich semantic and structural information regarding the images. Prior studies [] demonstrated that different layers of the U-Net decoder capture different levels of detail; the lower layers primarily capture coarse semantic information, whereas the higher layers capture finer appearance details. Results

This section presents the experimental results and an analysis of the proposed texture-generation method, IP-ConTex. We first introduce the key implementation details, including the parameter settings and experimental configurations. Next, we compare IP-ConTex with three state-of-the-art image-guided texture-generation methods. The effectiveness of the core modules is evaluated via ablation studies. Finally, we analyze the time efficiency of the proposed method.

During texture baking, each generated view was projected onto the UV texture space based on the rasterized face indices and barycentric coordinates. The geometric models used in the experiments are sourced from the Objaverse dataset []. Paint3D adopts progressive generation to obtain an initial texture map and is similarly susceptible to the Janus problem. Although Paint3D and our method utilize UV space, both methods rely on it in fundamentally different manners. Paint3D performs texture refinement directly in the UV space and is thus sensitive to the continuity and regularity of UV parameterization. By contrast, our method synthesizes appearance in a rendered multiview image space and uses the UV space primarily for synchronization and aggregation across views. Consequently, severe UV seams or fragmented UV charts exert less effect on the generation quality of the proposed method. This robustness to topological complexity and surface discontinuities aligns with broader research challenges commonly addressed through subdivision surfaces and geometry-simulation decoupling [–]. Paint3D uses IP-Adapter to inject the reference-image appearance into the generation process via cross-attention, thus ensuring style and semantic consistency. Nevertheless, it fails to preserve fine appearance details. Finally, owing to the limitations of SDS, textures generated by PGC-3D tend to exhibit overly saturated colors and low consistency with the reference image, i.e., colors and patterns that differ significantly from the input are typically generated.

The proposed IP-ConTex adopts a synchronized multiview diffusion [] framework to promote structural and layout consistency across different views, thus resulting in significantly improved texture quality. During generation, we followed SyncMVD [] by adding directional text prompts to each view, which effectively alleviated the Janus problem. For image guidance, IP-ConTex integrates an appearance-control module that does not require fine-tuning. It relies on simple attention control and a feature-synchronization strategy to effectively extract appearance details from the reference image and then transfers them to a geometric model. Thus, IP-ConTex not only generates textures that highly resemble the reference image but also achieves more natural colors and richer details, thereby offering notable advantages in terms of appearance fidelity and 3D consistency.

Ablation study

To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed appearance-control module for texture generation, this section presents a series of ablation experiments. By comparing the texture-generation results with and without each control component, we analyze the individual contribution of each component relative to the overall generation performance.

Cross-attention control

IP-ConTex utilizes IP-Adapter to embed reference-image information into the cross-attention layer, thus enabling appearance features from the reference image to be injected into the generation process during the early denoising stages. Texture results without cross-attention control are shown in Fig. a. Notably, the back of the bear model failed to reproduce the expected clothing color and structure. By contrast, the other three models exhibited minimal changes. This is because the bird and shoe models exhibit mirrored appearances on both the front and back, and reference images are available for these views during generation. Additionally, the back of the clothing model has a relatively simple structure. Therefore, when the structure varies significantly between the views and reference images, cross-attention control is particularly important. This enables the extraction of semantic information from the reference image and affects both the structural layout and color styling during the early denoising stage.Fig. 6Results of ablation experiments on appearance-control module. For each model, the magenta rectangle shows input reference image. Each model is visualized from two viewpoints[42492_2026_232_Fig6_HTML.webp]

Self-attention control

In addition to injecting reference-image information through cross-attention control, IP-ConTex employs self-attention control to query the content from the reference image during the generation of each view. Figure b shows the texture-generation results when cross-attention control is used instead of self-attention control. Under this setting, both the bear and clothing models failed to reproduce the key appearance details in the reference image, whereas the bird and shoe models generated texture patterns that were inconsistent with the reference image. These results demonstrate that cross-attention is insufficient for transferring fine-grained image features. By contrast, self-attention control is crucial for preserving the detailed appearance characteristics and ensuring fidelity to the reference image.

Diffusion feature synchronization

IP-ConTex uses diffusion feature synchronization during the generation of each view to align appearance details and ensure 3D consistency across views. Figure c shows the texture-generation results without feature synchronization. The bear and clothing models exhibited blurred textures and loss of fine details. This degradation occurred because detailed features from the reference image are generated only in the main view and fail to propagate to other views, thus resulting in an averaging effect of high-frequency details during the final texture-map aggregation. Moreover, the bird and shoe models exhibited ghosting artifacts, which is attributable to inadequate 3D consistency. The content generated in each view is inconsistently mapped to the same surface regions of the model. These results demonstrate that the proposed diffusion feature-synchronization strategy is essential for effectively propagating high-frequency appearance details across views and maintaining 3D-consistent textures.

Color adjustment

After each denoising timestep, IP-ConTex applies AdaIN to align the feature statistics of each view’s latent representation with those of the reference image. This adjustment ensures that the latent images better match the color distribution of the reference. As shown in Fig. d, omitting AdaIN results in textures with color tones that deviate from the reference image. By contrast, applying AdaIN results in textures with color distributions that are more consistent with the reference. This may be because the latent images of each view are aggregated in the UV space and then projected onto each view. This process may change the feature statistics. Therefore, AdaIN is essential for maintaining color fidelity by correcting the feature statistics at each denoising timestep.

Time efficiency

As shown in Table , the proposed IP-ConTex effectively balances generation quality and computational efficiency. Compared with optimization- or fine-tuning-based methods, IP-ConTex does not require per-sample training. Specifically, TEXTure requires approximately 120 min per sample owing to the intensive DreamBooth fine-tuning process, whereas PGC-3D requires approximately 30 min for SDS-based optimization. By contrast, IP-ConTex completes the entire texture-generation process in approximately 4 min (approximately 263 s) on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPU—comparable to the feedforward speed of Paint3D (approximately 3 min)—while maintaining higher detail consistency. Furthermore, IP-ConTex operates with a manageable peak GPU memory of 17.5 GB, thus rendering it accessible to standard consumer-grade hardware. Discussion

Superiority

IP-ConTex provides a promising solution for image-guided texture generation. Benefiting from the proposed appearance-control module, IP-ConTex can generate high-quality textures while preserving the appearance details of the reference image, thereby affording fine-grained control over the generation process. In terms of texture quality, IP-ConTex incorporates a synchronized diffusion module to ensure multiview consistency. Regarding detail preservation, the appearance-control module maintains detailed consistency between the generated textures and image prompts. Additionally, IP-ConTex is time efficient, as it does not require lengthy fine-tuning or optimization, thus enabling texture generation within minutes. Overall, the proposed approach reduces the cost and barriers to entry for 3D asset creation and facilitates the generation of high-quality, diverse textures for 3D models.

Limitations and future studies

Although IP-ConTex performs well in generating consistent and high-quality textures, it presents certain limitations. First, undesired lighting information is embedded in the generated textures. For example, in Fig. , the heel of the second shoe model retains the highlight artifacts originating from the reference image, which is not conducive to downstream tasks such as relighting. Future studies can address this issue by designing novel textural representations that disentangle lighting from appearance. Second, the use of a fixed set of 11 predefined viewpoints may result in incomplete surface coverage, particularly for models with complex geometries and occluded regions. This may generate texture holes in insufficiently observed areas during generation. To mitigate this issue, an adaptive viewpoint sampling strategy can be developed to ensure greater surface coverage.

Another practical scenario is that multiple reference images from different viewpoints may be available. In the current implementation, we focused on a single-reference setting for a simple and clear analysis. Nevertheless, the proposed framework can be extended to this case by associating each reference image with its corresponding main view, followed by applying cross-image self-attention using a matched reference latent for that view. Subsequently, the appearance information from these reference-guided views can be propagated to the remaining views through shared self-attention and diffusion feature synchronization. Resolving inconsistent appearance cues across different reference images remains a challenge, as it requires techniques such as viewpoint-aware selection or confidence-weighted fusion. A systematic study of multireference guidance remains a promising direction for future research.

Although IP-ConTex demonstrates excellent performance in preserving appearance consistency, it fails to effectively manage reference images containing high-frequency repetitive patterns, as illustrated in Fig. . Specifically, when the reference image contains dense stripes, checkerboard patterns, fine logos, or intricate embroidery, the generated textures may appear oversmoothed, while repetitive patterns may be attenuated during cross-view propagation and texture fusion. We attribute this limitation to the tradeoff between multiview consistency and high-frequency detail preservation. As the proposed framework repeatedly synchronizes and fuses information across views to maintain 3D consistency, minor discrepancies in the pattern phase, alignment, or sampling between different views may accumulate, thus resulting in an averaging effect in the fused texture space. Consequently, while the overall structure and style remain coherent, extremely fine repetitive details are not consistently preserved. A promising direction for future work is to incorporate detail-aware refinement mechanisms, such as patch-level appearance transfer, frequency-aware fusion, or texture super-resolution modules, to better preserve high-frequency repetitive details while maintaining cross-view consistency.Fig. 7Failure case on high-frequency repetitive textures. Conclusions

In this study, we proposed IP-ConTex, a novel method for detail-consistent texture generation guided by image prompts. By leveraging a pretrained 2D image diffusion model, IP-ConTex generates high-quality textures that preserve the appearance details of a reference image for a specified 3D geometric model. The proposed method comprises two key modules: synchronized multiview diffusion and appearance control. The former ensures structural and layout consistency across multiple views by sharing denoised content through a latent-space texture map at each timestep. The latter injects appearance information into the generation process and propagates it across views using cross-attention control, self-attention control, diffusion feature synchronization, and a color-adjustment strategy. Experimental results demonstrate that IP-ConTex effectively preserves fine-grained appearance details and achieves superior texture quality compared with existing state-of-the-art methods.
