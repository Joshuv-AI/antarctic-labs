Lorentzian Regularization of the Type IIB Superstring Torus Vacuum
Thomas Junkai Wang

Abstract
We study the one-loop torus vacuum of Type IIB Superstring theory through sector-resolved modular integrals. Building on the i ​ ε i\varepsilon -prescription and the E s E_{s} -regularized modular-integral framework of Manschot and Wang ManschotWang2024 , we construct regularized sector functionals for the closed oriented torus before the final GSO projection. The construction keeps the unprojected spin-sector data explicit and fixes the compact-domain and cusp contributions within a single modular prescription. We also independently cross-check the result with the Lorentzian-inversion reconstruction of modular integrals by Baccianti et al. BacciantiChandraEberhardtHartmanMizera2025 . This provides a first direct regularized construction of the unprojected sectors of the Type IIB Superstring torus vacuum.

Introduction

One-loop amplitudes occupy a distinguished place in perturbative string theory because they are the first amplitudes whose definition depends simultaneously on modular geometry, on the global structure of the integration contour, and on the spectrum of intermediate string states. At genus one, the worldsheet modulus

\mathcal{F}

y\to\infty

Standard references on genus-one string perturbation theory therefore frame one-loop amplitudes as modular integrals whose analytic content is inseparable from the geometry of the cusp itself

In the low-energy expansion of genus-one type II amplitudes, this structure is reorganized in terms of modular graph functions and their generalizations, which capture the modular dependence left after the puncture integrations have been expanded order by order in momenta

The advantage of the vacuum specialization is that it isolates the modular regularization step from the extra kinematic structure carried by punctures and Koba–Nielsen factors.

2
The type IIB torus amplitude

The closed-string input for the regularized calculation is the genus-one type
IIB torus vacuum written before the GSO cancellation has been contracted. The
vacuum integral itself is standard, but its sector data must be fixed with some
care because the later regularized sums act directly on their Fourier
coefficients. We therefore fix the torus measure, the spin-structure sum, the

SO(8)
-character rewriting, and the auxiliary sector blocks before the
final GSO projection in a single convention.

2.1
The torus modulus and the genus-one measure

We write the complex structure modulus of the torus as

\tau=x+\mathrm{i}y,\qquad q=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau},\qquad\bar{q}=\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\bar{\tau}},

(11)

with
y>0

fundamental domain

\mathcal{F}=\left\{\tau\in\mathbb{H}:-\frac{1}{2}\leq\operatorname{Re}\tau\leq\frac{1}{2},\quad|\tau|\geq 1\right\},

(12)

which represents the moduli space
\mathbb{H}/SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
of oriented genus-one
worldsheets

;
Polchinski1998Vol2

cusp at
y\to\infty
will later be the region in which the Lorentzian
prescription and the regularized modular-integral map become relevant, but at
the present stage it enters only through the standard measure.

With an overall normalization denoted by
\mathcal{N}

Z^{\rm IIB}_{0,1}=\mathrm{i}\mathcal{N}\int_{\mathcal{F}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{\tau}}{y^{6}}\,\mathcal{Z}_{\rm IIB}(\tau,\bar{\tau}).

(13)

At the operator level, (

trace over the closed-string Hilbert space. Suppressing the standard ghost and
gauge-fixing factors, the worldsheet trace has the form

\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}}\!\left(q^{L_{0}-c/24}\bar{q}^{\bar{L}_{0}-c/24}\right),

with the spin-structure sum and the
SO(8)
-character basis providing a
convenient packaging of that trace in the supersymmetric vacuum sector

;
Polchinski1998Vol2

(15)

2.2
Spin structures and the type IIB torus integrand

The spin-structure sum fixes the integrand before the character rewriting. In

\mathcal{Z}_{\rm IIB}(\tau,\bar{\tau})=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta}=0}^{1}(-1)^{\alpha+\beta+\bar{\alpha}+\bar{\beta}}\frac{\vartheta_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)^{4}\,\vartheta_{\bar{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}(\bar{\tau})^{4}}{|\eta(\tau)|^{24}}.

Here

2.4
Holomorphic building blocks and Fourier data

To expose that common data, define the holomorphic building blocks

A_{V}(\tau)=\frac{V_{8}(\tau)}{\eta(\tau)^{8}},\qquad A_{S}(\tau)=\frac{S_{8}(\tau)}{\eta(\tau)^{8}}.

3
Cusp regularization

The Euclidean modular domain supplies the geometric input for the later
regularized mode blocks. A truncated fundamental domain separates into a compact
keyhole region and a cusp strip above height one. On the strip, horizontal
Fourier orthogonality projects a single mode
q^{m}\bar{q}^{n}
onto the diagonal

m=n
. The Lorentzian prescription and the generalized-exponential-integral
tail both act on this diagonal strip contribution; neither is needed to derive
the Euclidean projection itself.

3.1
Upper half-plane and fundamental domain

We work on the upper half-plane

\mathbb{H}=\{x+\mathrm{i}y\in\mathbb{C}:\;y>0\},

(36)

with the torus modulus written as

\tau=x+\mathrm{i}y,\qquad q=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau},\qquad\bar{q}=\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\bar{\tau}}.

(37)

The sign in the anti-holomorphic nome is chosen so that the factor
q^{m}\bar{q}^{n}
decays exponentially as
y\to\infty
whenever
m+n>0
.

The standard modular fundamental domain for the oriented torus is

\mathcal{F}=\left\{\tau\in\mathbb{H}:-\frac{1}{2}\leq\operatorname{Re}\tau\leq\frac{1}{2},\quad|\tau|\geq 1\right\},

(38)

which is the usual representative of
\mathbb{H}/SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
in genus-one perturbation theory and in the classical theory of modular forms
GreenSchwarzWitten1988
;
Polchinski1998Vol2
;
Serre1973Course
;
Apostol1990ModularFunctions
;
Zagier2008Elliptic
. Since
y>0
, the condition
|\tau|\geq 1
is equivalent inside the vertical strip to

y\geq\sqrt{1-x^{2}}.

(39)

Thus the same domain may be written as

\mathcal{F}=\left\{x+\mathrm{i}y:-\frac{1}{2}\leq x\leq\frac{1}{2},\quad y\geq\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right\}.

(40)

This form displays the lower boundary and isolates the non-compact cusp at

y\to\infty
.

3.2
Truncation at finite height

To separate the cusp from the compact part of the domain, we introduce a height cutoff
Y>1
and define

\mathcal{F}_{Y}=\mathcal{F}\cap\{y\leq Y\}.

(41)

The restriction
Y>1
ensures that the cutoff lies above the unit-circle boundary of the fundamental domain across the full interval
x\in[-1/2,1/2]
. The truncated domain
\mathcal{F}_{Y}
is compact and provides the natural Euclidean starting point for later regularization.

For a single Fourier mode we define the truncated mode integral

L_{m,n,s}(Y)=\int_{\mathcal{F}_{Y}}\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{\tau}\;y^{-s}q^{m}\bar{q}^{n}.

(42)

At this stage
m
,
n
, and
s
are fixed parameters, and no subtraction
term is included. In the vacuum application one sets
s=6
, but the geometric
projection does not require that specialization. The object
(
42
) is only a cutoff Euclidean integral over the truncated
domain.

3.3
Compact keyhole region

The compact part of the domain is the keyhole region below height one,

\mathcal{F}_{1}=\left\{x+\mathrm{i}y:-\frac{1}{2}\leq x\leq\frac{1}{2},\quad\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\leq y\leq 1\right\}.

(43)

Its associated mode integral is

K_{m,n,s}=\int_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{\tau}\;y^{-s}q^{m}\bar{q}^{n}.

(44)

Because both coordinates are bounded on
\mathcal{F}_{1}
, this integral is finite for fixed
(m,n,s)
. The compact keyhole region is therefore not the source of any cusp sensitivity; that role belongs entirely to the strip above height one.

3.4
Cusp strip and domain decomposition

The complementary strip between heights one and
Y
is

S_{1,Y}=\left\{x+\mathrm{i}y:-\frac{1}{2}\leq x\leq\frac{1}{2},\quad 1\leq y\leq Y\right\}.

(45)

Up to the common boundary at
y=1
, which has measure zero for the two-dimensional integral, the truncated domain decomposes as

\mathcal{F}_{Y}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup S_{1,Y}.

(46)

This follows immediately from (
40

x
in the strip, the allowed range in
\mathcal{F}_{Y}
is
\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\leq y\leq Y
, which is the union of the compact interval
\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\leq y\leq 1
and the strip interval
1\leq y\leq Y
.

We define the strip contribution of a single mode by

R_{m,n,s}(Y)=\int_{S_{1,Y}}\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{\tau}\;y^{-s}q^{m}\bar{q}^{n}.

(47)

The decomposition (
46

L_{m,n,s}(Y)=K_{m,n,s}+R_{m,n,s}(Y).

(48)

At this stage the decomposition is purely Euclidean, and all dependence on the
cutoff height

3.5
Wedge measure and Fourier projection

The wedge measure convention used throughout the calculation is

\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{\tau}=-2\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

(49)

Substituting (
37
) into a Fourier mode gives

q^{m}\bar{q}^{n}=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(m-n)x}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi(m+n)y}.

(50)

The
x
- and
y
-dependences therefore factorize on the strip, which is the basic reason the cusp contribution is easy to analyze before any regularization is applied.

Assume from now on that
m-n\in\mathbb{Z}
, as happens in the Fourier expansion of the torus integrand. Then the horizontal integral is the standard Fourier projection on the unit interval,

\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(m-n)x}=\delta_{m,n}.

(51)

If
m=n
, the integral is one. If
m-n=k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, then

\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}kx}=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}k}-\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\mathrm{i}k}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}k}=\frac{\sin(\pi k)}{\pi k}=0.

(52)

Equation (
51
) is therefore exact and shows that the strip contribution can only survive on diagonal modes.

Substituting (
49
) and (
50
) into (
47

R_{m,n,s}(Y)=-2\mathrm{i}\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\mathrm{d}x\int_{1}^{Y}\mathrm{d}y\;y^{-s}\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(m-n)x}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi(m+n)y}.

(53)

Using the projection (
51
), only the diagonal part remains. On the diagonal
m=n
, the exponential becomes
\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi my}
, and the strip contribution reduces to

R_{m,n,s}(Y)=-2\mathrm{i}\,\delta_{m,n}\int_{1}^{Y}\mathrm{d}y\;y^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi my}.

(54)

This is the main result of the section. It shows that every off-diagonal mode has vanishing strip contribution, while every diagonal mode is controlled by a one-dimensional tail integral over the cusp direction.

Equation (
54
) is the endpoint of the Euclidean analysis. No
regularized mode block, generalized exponential integral, or Lorentzian cycle
has entered. The result is the geometric input needed later: the cutoff domain
splits into a compact keyhole part and a cusp strip, and the strip acts
diagonally on the Fourier labels.

4
Lorentzian prescription

The Euclidean cusp strip gives a diagonal long-tube contribution, but the
Euclidean integral alone is not the physical one-loop prescription. The
non-separating cusp corresponds to propagation over a long proper time, and the
worldsheet integral must be interpreted with the string-theoretic analogue of
the Feynman
i\varepsilon
prescription. The required replacement is a
Lorentzian continuation of the long-tube variable. Its action on a single
diagonal Fourier mode is the contour-level input from which the regularized
mode blocks will be evaluated.

4.1
Euclidean strip and Lorentzian continuation

In the strip region
y\geq 1
, the torus develops a long cylinder whose length is measured by the proper-time variable
y=\operatorname{Im}\tau
. This is the worldsheet regime in which intermediate string states propagate for a long time. Witten’s formulation of the string-theoretic
i\varepsilon
prescription emphasizes that the physical Lorentzian amplitude is not obtained by keeping this proper time on the positive real axis all the way to infinity
Witten2013IEpsilon
. Instead, after a sufficiently large Euclidean segment, the contour must be continued into a complex direction that implements the same causal choice as the field-theory Feynman prescription.

The same degeneration region is where modern worldsheet-cut constructions
detect the discontinuity structure of one-loop amplitudes

EberhardtMizera2022Cuts
. From the present vacuum point of view, this
means that the cusp strip cannot be treated as an arbitrary Euclidean tail if
the regularized modular integral is to remain compatible with the Lorentzian
definition of string amplitudes. The Euclidean domain decomposition identifies
the strip that must be replaced by a physically meaningful continuation.

The vacuum problem uses only a narrow specialization of the general contour
story. Witten’s prescription is formulated for the full one-loop integration
cycle in the presence of general degeneration loci
Witten2013IEpsilon
.
Here there are no puncture moduli and no Koba–Nielsen kernel. The only modulus
that needs a causal prescription is the long-tube parameter itself. The
calculation therefore reduces to the cusp-level contour object that survives in
the vacuum case.

4.2
The long-tube continuation

Let
T_{0}>1
be a large reference height in the cusp. The Euclidean proper-time segment from
1
to
T_{0}
is retained on the real axis, but beyond
T_{0}
the contour is continued vertically into the complex plane. Concretely, we introduce the cusp contour

\Gamma(T_{0})=[1,T_{0}]\cup\{\,T_{0}+\mathrm{i}t:\;t\geq 0\,\}.

(55)

The first piece is the Euclidean segment, while the second piece is the Lorentzian tail. In the variable
y
this is the simplest contour that matches the string-theoretic
i\varepsilon
prescription at the non-separating cusp: one proceeds along real Euclidean proper time up to a large cutoff and then turns upward into the imaginary direction
Witten2013IEpsilon
;
ManschotWang2024
;
WangThesis2025
.

The parameter
T_{0}
is not an additional regularization parameter. It marks
the height at which the contour is decomposed into a compact Euclidean segment
and a Lorentzian tail. Different choices of
T_{0}
give different
decompositions of the same contour prescription. The final regularized block is
therefore independent of
T_{0}
, although the intermediate Euclidean and
Lorentzian contributions separately depend on it.
Appendix
B
gives the corresponding
contour-independence derivation.

At the level of the degeneration coordinate

q=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau},

(56)

the Euclidean strip approaches the degeneration point
q=0
radially, whereas the Lorentzian continuation turns into a contour of fixed modulus and varying phase. On the vertical segment
y=T_{0}+\mathrm{i}t
,

q=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}x}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi T_{0}}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}t}.

(57)

Thus the contour (
55
) is the proper-time representative of a
cycle around the degeneration point in the complexified
q
-plane. This is
the cusp-level realization of the physical
i\varepsilon
choice.

4.3
Single-mode contour decomposition

The Euclidean strip acts only on diagonal modes. For a diagonal term
m=n
,
the strip integrand reduces to

y^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi my}.

(58)

The Euclidean tail and the prescribed Lorentzian contour are distinct objects.
The ordinary Euclidean tail is

T^{E}_{m,s}=\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,y^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi my}.

(59)

The corresponding prescribed strip contour is

T^{\Gamma}_{m,s}(T_{0})=\int_{\Gamma(T_{0})}\mathrm{d}z\,z^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi mz},

(60)

where
z
is the complexified proper-time variable. Equation
(
60
) is a definition of the contour contribution
associated with the prescription before it is split into its finite Euclidean
part and its vertical tail.

Writing the second segment of (
55

z=T_{0}+\mathrm{i}t
with
t\geq 0
, one obtains

\int_{\Gamma(T_{0})}\mathrm{d}z\,z^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi mz}=\int_{1}^{T_{0}}\mathrm{d}y\,y^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi my}\;+\;\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\,(T_{0}+\mathrm{i}t)^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi m(T_{0}+\mathrm{i}t)}.

(61)

The first term is the finite Euclidean segment, and the second term is the
Lorentzian vertical contribution. The vertical tail itself is

T^{\rm vert}_{m,s}(T_{0})=\int_{T_{0}}^{T_{0}+\mathrm{i}\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,z^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi mz}.

(62)

This decomposition has two structural
consequences. First, the prescription is diagonal mode by mode because the
horizontal strip projection has already produced
\delta_{m,n}
. Second, the
Lorentzian contribution is complex even when the Euclidean integrand is real;
the contour therefore carries the analytic data that later appears as the
imaginary part of the regularized modular integral.

The formula (
61
) is the vacuum specialization of the
general contour prescription. In a non-vacuum amplitude the same continuation
coexists with puncture moduli, worldsheet Green functions, and kinematic branch
structure. In the vacuum problem these data are absent, and the only remaining
analytic issue is the cusp tail of a diagonal Fourier mode.

4.4
From Euclidean strip to Lorentzian strip contribution

Combining the diagonal strip projection with the vertical tail
(
62
), the Lorentzian tail contribution that enters the
regularized block is

R^{\rm Lor}_{m,n,s}(T_{0})=-2\mathrm{i}\,\delta_{m,n}\int_{T_{0}}^{T_{0}+\mathrm{i}\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,z^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi mz}.

(63)

Equivalently,

R^{\rm Lor}_{m,n,s}(T_{0})=-2\mathrm{i}\,\delta_{m,n}\left[\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\,(T_{0}+\mathrm{i}t)^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi m(T_{0}+\mathrm{i}t)}\right].

(64)

This contour expression is the Lorentzian-prescribed counterpart of the
diagonal cusp tail after the finite Euclidean segment has been included in the
compact keyhole domain. Evaluating (
63
) in a form adapted to
Fourier-mode regularization gives the generalized exponential integral and
turns the contour-level object into a special-function block that can be summed
against the auxiliary-sector Fourier coefficients. The construction separates
the Euclidean projection, the vertical Lorentzian contour, and the resulting
special-function evaluation.

5
Regularized mode integrals

The cusp contribution has two distinct pieces: a compact keyhole integral that
is present for every Fourier mode, and a long-tube contribution that survives
only after horizontal projection onto the diagonal
m=n

contour fixes the latter contribution, while the generalized exponential
integral gives its explicit mode-by-mode value. This produces the regularized
block

The full contour equivalence between the string-theoretic

5.1
Generalized exponential integrals

The generalized exponential integral is normalized as

E_{s}(z)=\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-zt}t^{-s}\mathrm{d}t,\qquad\Re(s)>1.

For

E_{s}(z)=z^{\,s-1}\int_{z}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-u}u^{-s}\mathrm{d}u,

The first form is the most convenient one for the vacuum discussion, because
the strip tail is already written as an integral over

form makes the relation to the standard special-function literature explicit.

The constant mode is included by continuity:

E_{s}(0)=\frac{1}{s-1},\qquad\Re(s)>1.

(67)

In particular, for the type IIB torus vacuum where

E_{6}(0)=\frac{1}{5}.

(68)

This value will later control the constant-mode contribution, but for the
moment it is simply the

captures all diagonal cusp tails.

5.2
From the Lorentzian contour to the

After the compact keyhole region has been cut off at height

The contour-level tail entering the regularized block is therefore

R^{\rm Lor}_{m,n,s}(T_{0})=-2\mathrm{i}\,\delta_{m,n}\int_{T_{0}}^{T_{0}+\mathrm{i}\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,z^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi mz}.

(70)

This differs from the full contour

of

For the non-negative diagonal modes that occur in the Type IIB vacuum,

\int_{T_{0}}^{T_{0}+\mathrm{i}\infty}\mathrm{d}z\,z^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi mz}=T_{0}^{\,1-s}E_{s}(4\pi mT_{0}),\qquad m\geq 0,\quad\Re(s)>1.

(71)

This is the vacuum specialization of the Lorentzian/

the regularized modular-integral framework of
Manschot and Wang

The parameter

The canonical vacuum choice is

5.3
Definition of the regularized mode block

Let
\mathcal{F}_{1}
be the compact keyhole region of the truncated fundamental
domain. The regularized mode block is

L^{r}_{m,n,s}=\int_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{\tau}\,y^{-s}q^{m}\bar{q}^{n}-2\mathrm{i}\,\delta_{m,n}E_{s}(4\pi m).

(78)

This is the final block-level object used in the present paper. The first
term is the compact-domain integral, present for every mode. The second term is
the analytic cusp contribution, present only on the diagonal. The subtraction
language of regularization is therefore a shorthand for the underlying
geometric decomposition: the full block is a compact contribution plus a
Lorentzian-prescribed analytic tail.

For the finite-mode evaluation, (
78
) is written in terms of the
real compact integral

I_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}^{\rm real}(m,n,s)=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\mathrm{d}x\int_{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}^{1}\mathrm{d}y\;y^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(m-n)x}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi(m+n)y}.

(79)

Since

\int_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{\tau}\,y^{-s}q^{m}\bar{q}^{n}=-2\mathrm{i}\,I_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}^{\rm real}(m,n,s),

(80)

the block can equivalently be written as

L^{r}_{m,n,s}=-2\mathrm{i}\Bigl[I_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}^{\rm real}(m,n,s)\,+\,\delta_{m,n}E_{s}(4\pi m)\Bigr].

(81)

This is the form used in the numerical evaluation.

5.4
Vacuum specialization and branch convention

For the type IIB torus vacuum one specializes to

s=6,

(82)

so that

L^{r}_{m,n,6}=\int_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{\tau}\,y^{-6}q^{m}\bar{q}^{n}-2\mathrm{i}\,\delta_{m,n}E_{6}(4\pi m).

(83)

In this vacuum setting the diagonal argument
4\pi m
lies on the
non-negative real axis, so the block does not cross the negative-real branch
cut relevant to genuine threshold problems. Nevertheless, the analytic
continuation convention should be fixed already here, because the same
special-function block is the object that later generalizes to polar and
non-vacuum contexts. The principal branch convention is

\operatorname{Arg}z\in(-\pi,\pi].

(84)

With this convention, for
x>0
,

E_{N}(-x+\mathrm{i}0)-E_{N}(-x-\mathrm{i}0)=-\,2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\frac{x^{N-1}}{\Gamma(N)},\qquad N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

(85)

Here the discontinuity is upper boundary value minus lower boundary value and

\operatorname{Log}(-x+\mathrm{i}0)=\log x+\mathrm{i}\pi
. With the opposite
discontinuity convention, the sign changes. The vacuum modes considered here
never require (
85
); the formula fixes the convention of
the special-function block for later non-vacuum applications.

The regularized construction is linear:

I^{r}[f+g]=I^{r}[f]+I^{r}[g],\qquad I^{r}[f]=\sum_{m,n}F(m,n)L^{r}_{m,n,s}.

(86)

This linearity allows the GSO projection to be applied after the auxiliary
sector data have been regularized. Once the diagonal cusp contribution has
been incorporated into
L^{r}_{m,n,6}
, the remaining vacuum calculation is
governed by the type IIB sector Fourier coefficients and by the compact-keyhole
integrals appearing in the first term of (
83
).

6
Sector amplitudes

The sector calculation is the central construction of the paper. It combines
the Fourier coefficient matrices
F_{XY}(m,n)
of the four auxiliary Type IIB
sector terms with the regularized mode block
L^{r}_{m,n,6}
. Their pairing
defines an auxiliary sector integral for each
X,Y\in\{V,S\}

contraction is not applied at this stage, so the quantities below are not
physical vacuum amplitudes; they are the sector-level objects on which the
Lorentzian and
E_{s}
-regularized prescriptions are compared.

6.1
Definition of the sector sums

For each auxiliary sector,

\mathcal{Z}_{XY}(\tau,\bar{\tau})=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}F_{XY}(m,n)q^{m}\bar{q}^{n}.

(87)

The corresponding regularized sector integral is defined mode by mode by

I^{r}_{XY}=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}F_{XY}(m,n)L^{r}_{m,n,6}.

(88)

This sector integral is the primary regularized quantity computed in the
vacuum problem. The index
6
is not an adjustable regulator; it is fixed by
the measure
d\tau\wedge d\bar{\tau}\,y^{-6}
in the original torus vacuum
integral (
13
).

The auxiliary numerical value uses the amplitude convention

\mathcal{A}_{XY}=\mathrm{i}I^{r}_{XY}.

(89)

With the wedge convention
d\tau\wedge d\bar{\tau}=-2\mathrm{i}\,dx\,dy
, the blocks

L^{r}_{m,n,6}
that occur in the present vacuum problem are naturally
imaginary, and (
89
) converts the sector value into the real
normalization used for the numerical sector datum.

To separate exact coefficient algebra from numerical convergence, introduce the finite Fourier cutoff

I^{r,(M)}_{XY}=\sum_{m,n=0}^{M}F_{XY}(m,n)L^{r}_{m,n,6},\qquad\mathcal{A}^{(M)}_{XY}=\mathrm{i}I^{r,(M)}_{XY}.

(90)

At finite
M
, all sector identities below are identities between finite sums. The later limiting process only determines the common value of those sums.

The finite evaluations quoted below use deterministic quadrature for the
compact keyhole integrals, exact generation of the
SO(8)
Fourier
coefficients, and the special-function representation of the cusp and compact
pieces through
E_{s}
and incomplete-gamma functions. The sector sums are
then evaluated with a finite Fourier cutoff, while the same holomorphic
degeneracies may also be reconstructed from the
\Gamma(2)
Rademacher
coefficient construction described in Section

6.2
Mode expansion into keyhole and cusp terms

Substituting the block (

Define the compact keyhole mode integral

In real variables,

K_{m,n}=-2\mathrm{i}\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\mathrm{d}x\int_{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}^{1}\mathrm{d}y\,y^{-6}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(m-n)x}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi(m+n)y}.

6.3
Diagonal and off-diagonal parts

The full sector sum decomposes into a diagonal part and an off-diagonal part,

I^{r}_{XY}=I^{r,\mathrm{diag}}_{XY}+I^{r,\mathrm{off}}_{XY}.

(95)

The diagonal contribution contains both the compact keyhole integral and the cusp subtraction,

(97)

The off-diagonal modes have no cusp subtraction because the cusp strip projection kills them.

unoriented one-loop normalization comparisons
AngelantonjSagnotti2002
;
WangThesis2025
. In the closed type IIB sector the block used here is instead

A(\tau)=\frac{\vartheta_{2}(\tau)^{4}}{2\eta(\tau)^{12}}=8+128q+\mathcal{O}(q^{2}),

(116)

so the sector coefficient is
8\cdot 8
, not
16
.

Using the amplitude convention (
89
), the constant-mode contribution in one auxiliary sector is

\mathcal{A}^{XY}_{00}=\mathrm{i}\,F_{XY}(0,0)L^{r}_{0,0,6}.

(117)

Equivalently, by (
107
),

\mathcal{A}^{XY}_{00}=2F_{XY}(0,0)\Bigl[I_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}^{\rm real}(0,0,6)+E_{6}(0)\Bigr].

(118)

Finally, using (
112
) and (
114
),

\mathcal{A}^{XY}_{00}=2\cdot 64\cdot\frac{4}{9\sqrt{3}}=\frac{512}{9\sqrt{3}}.

(119)

This is an exact single-sector zero-mode normalization datum. It is not the full sector value, because non-constant modes have not been included, and it is not a physical type IIB vacuum energy, because the physical GSO combination is treated separately.

8
GSO projection of the sector functionals

The regularized quantities constructed in Section
6

are sector functionals. The present section applies the physical Type IIB GSO
projection to those sector functionals. Its role is not to introduce a new
sector value, but to show that the sector-level regularization is compatible
with the standard supersymmetric cancellation after the signed spin-structure
contraction is imposed.

8.1
Auxiliary sectors and coefficient identities

The sector-resolved integrands
\mathcal{Z}_{VV}
,
\mathcal{Z}_{VS}
,
\mathcal{Z}_{SV}
, and

\mathcal{Z}_{SS}
enter before the physical spin-structure contraction and are
therefore auxiliary. The physical type IIB torus integrand is the signed
combination

\mathcal{Z}_{\rm IIB}=\mathcal{Z}_{VV}-\mathcal{Z}_{VS}-\mathcal{Z}_{SV}+\mathcal{Z}_{SS}
, which is the sector
expansion of
(V_{8}-S_{8})(\bar{V}_{8}-\bar{S}_{8})/|\eta|^{16}
. The Jacobi
identity
V_{8}=S_{8}
is used below in its coefficient-level form, after the
regularized sector sums have been defined. Each sector has the Fourier
expansion

\mathcal{Z}_{XY}(\tau,\bar{\tau})=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}F_{XY}(m,n)q^{m}\bar{q}^{n}.

(120)

The holomorphic identity
A_{V}=A_{S}
gives

F_{VV}(m,n)=F_{VS}(m,n)=F_{SV}(m,n)=F_{SS}(m,n)

(121)

for every
m,n
. Define the GSO-signed coefficient

F_{\rm GSO}(m,n)=F_{VV}(m,n)-F_{VS}(m,n)-F_{SV}(m,n)+F_{SS}(m,n).

(122)

Substitution of (
121
) gives

F_{\rm GSO}(m,n)=0\qquad\text{for all }m,n\geq 0.

(123)

This is the coefficient-level form of the supersymmetric cancellation. It precedes any integration, any cutoff, and any numerical approximation.

Equivalently, with signs

\sigma_{V}=+1,\qquad\sigma_{S}=-1,

(124)

one may write

F_{\rm GSO}(m,n)=\sum_{X,Y\in\{V,S\}}\sigma_{X}\sigma_{Y}F_{XY}(m,n)=\bigl[a_{V}(m)-a_{S}(m)\bigr]\bigl[a_{V}(n)-a_{S}(n)\bigr].

(125)

The final expression is zero because
a_{V}(k)=a_{S}(k)
for every
k
. Thus
the cancellation is an identity in the Fourier data and is independent of any
finite-cutoff sector evaluation.

8.2
Commutativity with the regularized sum

The regularized sector integrals are

I^{r}_{XY}=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}F_{XY}(m,n)L^{r}_{m,n,6}.

(126)

Because
L^{r}_{m,n,6}
is the same block for all four sectors, the physical signed integral can be formed after the sector regularization:

I^{r}_{\rm IIB}=I^{r}_{VV}-I^{r}_{VS}-I^{r}_{SV}+I^{r}_{SS}=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}F_{\rm GSO}(m,n)L^{r}_{m,n,6}.

(127)

Using (
123
),

I^{r}_{\rm IIB}=0.

(128)

Equation (
128
) expresses the commutativity between modular
regularization and the type IIB GSO projection in the vacuum problem. The
Fourier coefficients may be contracted before regularization, giving zero mode
by mode, or the auxiliary sectors may be regularized first and contracted
afterwards.

With the amplitude convention
\mathcal{A}_{XY}=\mathrm{i}I^{r}_{XY}
, the same identity gives

\mathcal{A}_{\rm IIB}=\mathcal{A}_{VV}-\mathcal{A}_{VS}-\mathcal{A}_{SV}+\mathcal{A}_{SS}=0.

(129)

8.3
Diagonal projector for cusp tails

The cusp part of the regularized block is more specific than the full signed
vacuum functional: only the diagonal tail survives the horizontal strip
projection.
Let
D
denote the diagonal projector on Fourier coefficients,

(DF)(m)=F(m,m).

(130)

The cusp part of the regularized sector functional can then be written as

I^{r,\mathrm{cusp}}_{XY}=-2\mathrm{i}\sum_{m\geq 0}(DF_{XY})(m)E_{6}(4\pi m).

(131)

The GSO contraction commutes with
D
, since both operations act only on the
coefficient array:

DF_{\rm GSO}=D(F_{VV}-F_{VS}-F_{SV}+F_{SS})=DF_{VV}-DF_{VS}-DF_{SV}+DF_{SS}.

(132)

Using (
123
), this gives

(DF_{\rm GSO})(m)=F_{\rm GSO}(m,m)=0.

(133)

Hence the diagonal cusp contribution to the physical signed functional is

\displaystyle I^{r,\mathrm{cusp}}_{\rm IIB}

\displaystyle=-2\mathrm{i}\sum_{m\geq 0}F_{\rm GSO}(m,m)E_{6}(4\pi m)

(134)

\displaystyle=0.

This coefficient-level identity is the input relevant to the
E_{s}
tail; it
is stronger than a cancellation of final numerical sector values.

No negative-axis branch contribution is used in the proof above. The Type IIB
vacuum sector has non-polar support
m,n\geq 0
, so the diagonal argument

4\pi m
lies on the non-negative real axis before the GSO contraction is
applied. If one formally continues the same diagonal block to a negative
argument, the convention in Section
5.4
gives, for
x>0
,

\operatorname{Disc}E_{6}(-x):=E_{6}(-x+\mathrm{i}0)-E_{6}(-x-\mathrm{i}0)=-\,2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\frac{x^{5}}{\Gamma(6)},

(135)

where the discontinuity is the upper boundary value minus the lower boundary
value. For a formally continued diagonal mode with
m<0
, one has

x=-4\pi m
. The discontinuity of the block would then be

\operatorname{Disc}L^{r}_{m,n,6}=-2\mathrm{i}\,\delta_{m,n}\,\operatorname{Disc}E_{6}(4\pi m)=-4\pi\,\delta_{m,n}\frac{(-4\pi m)^{5}}{\Gamma(6)}\qquad(m<0).

(136)

For this continued expression, a signed discontinuity would be

\operatorname{Disc}I^{r}_{\rm IIB}=\sum_{m<0}F_{\rm GSO}(m,m)\,\operatorname{Disc}L^{r}_{m,m,6}.

(137)

This last formula is not used in the vacuum calculation. In the actual vacuum
sector treated here the support condition
m,n\geq 0
makes the formal
negative-mode sum empty before any physical conclusion is drawn. The only
active input needed in the proof is the diagonal coefficient identity
(
133
).

9
Summary and Discussions

We have formulated the Type IIB torus vacuum as a regularized modular
calculation on the sector-resolved Fourier data of the oriented closed torus.
The construction separates three ingredients that are often compressed in the
final supersymmetric answer: the Lorentzian prescription at the non-separating
cusp, its equivalent
E_{s}
-regularized mode block, and the algebraic GSO
contraction of the four spin-structure sectors. The sector functionals provide
a definite closed-string test case for the regularized long-tube functional
before the signed projection is taken.

Each torus sector is paired with the
E_{s}
-regularized block

L^{r}_{m,n,6}
, using the Type IIB closed left-right coefficients

F_{XY}(m,n)
, before the physical GSO projection is applied. This
construction gives the exact zero-mode normalization

\mathcal{A}^{XY}_{00}=\frac{512}{9\sqrt{3}}

and the finite all-mode sector value given in
Appendix
D
. The subsequent Type IIB GSO contraction
is the coefficient-level implementation of the Jacobi identity in the

SO(8)
-character organization of the genus-one torus integrand

GreenSchwarzWitten1988
;
Polchinski1998Vol2
.

Appendix
F
packages the construction as an identity
of sector functionals. The non-polar vacuum expansion, uniform convergence,
and zero-mode projector allow the sector Fourier series to be inserted into
the same regularized functional, while the non-negative contour lemma
identifies the Lorentzian vertical tail with the corresponding
E_{s}
tail
after specialization to the vacuum measure. The sector values are the objects
on which the Lorentzian and
E_{s}
-regularized prescriptions are compared
before the physical projection is applied. The exact physical projection gives

I^{r}_{\rm IIB}=0,\qquad\mathcal{A}_{\rm IIB}=0.

The sector values are regularized data for the four spin-structure components
of the oriented Type IIB torus integrand. Since the vacuum has no puncture
integrations and no Koba–Nielsen factor, the calculation gives a
closed-string setting in which the measure, contour prescription, branch
convention, sector contraction, and finite-cutoff sector value can be specified
explicitly

;
WangThesis2025
.

The same modular language becomes genuinely dynamical for non-vacuum amplitudes. A one-loop four-point amplitude contains puncture integrations and a Koba–Nielsen kernel,

\mathcal{A}_{4}^{(1)}=\mathcal{R}^{4}\int_{\mathcal{F}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\tau}{\tau_{2}^{5}}\int_{(T^{2})^{3}}\prod_{i}\mathrm{d}^{2}z_{i}\,\exp\!\left[\sum_{i<j}s_{ij}G(z_{ij}|\tau)\right],

(138)

and its low-energy expansion is organized by modular graph functions and
related non-holomorphic modular objects

GreenRussoVanhove2008
;
DHokerGreenVanhove2015ModularStructure
;
DHokerGreenGurdoganVanhove2017
.
In that setting the cusp data are tied to genuine threshold structure and
worldsheet unitarity rather than to a final supersymmetric zero. The vacuum
calculation isolates the corresponding regularization mechanism in the closed
oriented torus setting: the Lorentzian/
E_{s}
prescription defines the sector

\Gamma(2)

cusp-theoretic description of the holomorphic degeneracies entering that same

BacciantiChandraEberhardtHartmanMizera2025

In this form the Type IIB torus vacuum connects the long-tube contour

Appendix A
Conventions and normalizations

The conventions used repeatedly in the main text are collected here. None of
them is individually exotic, but several of them interact in places where sign
errors, factor-of-two mistakes, or naming drift would otherwise obscure the
logic of the vacuum calculation. In particular, the distinction between the
closed-string sector coefficient
64
and the holomorphic comparison
coefficient
16
, as well as the sign of the
generalized-exponential-integral tail, depends on keeping the conventions
stable from the outset.

A.1
Nome, theta functions, and eta function

The torus modulus is

\tau=x+\mathrm{i}y,\qquad y>0,

(139)

and the nome is defined by

q=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau}.

(140)

This is the convention used throughout the present paper and in the associated
coefficient-generation code. It differs from the alternative mathematical
convention
q_{\rm math}=\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}\tau}
, so all exponentials in the
Fourier factors must be fixed relative to (
140
).

The Dedekind eta function is

\eta(\tau)=q^{1/24}\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n}),

(141)

and the relevant Jacobi theta constant is

\vartheta_{2}(\tau)=2q^{1/8}\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})(1+q^{n})^{2}.

(142)

For the
SO(8)
character rewriting, we also use
\vartheta_{3}
and

\vartheta_{4}
in the standard genus-one superstring conventions

GreenSchwarzWitten1987
;
GreenSchwarzWitten1988
;
Polchinski1998Vol2
. The
Jacobi identity takes the form

\vartheta_{3}(\tau)^{4}-\vartheta_{4}(\tau)^{4}-\vartheta_{2}(\tau)^{4}=0.

(143)

A.2
SO(8)
characters and sector blocks

The transverse
SO(8)
characters are

V_{8}(\tau)=\frac{\vartheta_{3}(\tau)^{4}-\vartheta_{4}(\tau)^{4}}{2\eta(\tau)^{4}},\qquad S_{8}(\tau)=\frac{\vartheta_{2}(\tau)^{4}}{2\eta(\tau)^{4}}.

(144)

Equation (
143
) implies

V_{8}(\tau)=S_{8}(\tau).

(145)

The holomorphic sector block used in the vacuum computation is

A_{V}(\tau)=\frac{V_{8}(\tau)}{\eta(\tau)^{8}}=\frac{\vartheta_{2}(\tau)^{4}}{2\eta(\tau)^{12}},

(146)

and similarly for
A_{S}
. Therefore

A_{V}(\tau)=A_{S}(\tau)=8+128q+1152q^{2}+\mathcal{O}(q^{3}).

(147)

The factor
1/2
in (
146
) is the reason the constant term of the
closed-string sector block is
8
, not
16
.

For comparison, the purely holomorphic quantity

\frac{\vartheta_{2}(\tau)^{4}}{\eta(\tau)^{12}}=16+256q+\mathcal{O}(q^{2})

(148)

appears naturally in open or unoriented one-loop normalization comparisons and
serves here only as a convention comparison
AngelantonjSagnotti2002
. It is not the closed type
IIB left-right sector block used in the vacuum theorem.

The constants
16
,
8
, and
64
therefore enter at different stages of
the normalization. The first is the constant term of the holomorphic
comparison series
\vartheta_{2}^{4}/\eta^{12}
. The second is the constant term
of the closed-string holomorphic sector block

A_{V}=V_{8}/\eta^{8}
, after the
SO(8)
-character normalization has been
included. The third is the closed left-right coefficient

F_{VV}(0,0)=8\cdot 8
, which is the coefficient inserted into the Type IIB
sector functional.

A.3
Wedge measure and Fourier factors

With

\tau=x+\mathrm{i}y,\qquad\bar{\tau}=x-\mathrm{i}y,

(149)

one has

\mathrm{d}\tau=\mathrm{d}x+\mathrm{i}\mathrm{d}y,\qquad\mathrm{d}\bar{\tau}=\mathrm{d}x-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{d}y,

(150)

and therefore

\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{\tau}=-2\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

(151)

This is the source of the common factor
-2\mathrm{i}
in the regularized block.

Using the nome convention (
140
),

q^{m}\bar{q}^{n}=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(m-n)x}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi(m+n)y}.

(152)

Hence the horizontal strip projection is

\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(m-n)x}\mathrm{d}x=\delta_{m,n},

(153)

which is why the cusp tail enters only on diagonal modes.

A.4
Generalized exponential integral

The present paper uses

E_{s}(z)=\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-zt}t^{-s}\mathrm{d}t,\qquad\Re(s)>1.

(154)

For
z>0
this is equivalent to the incomplete-gamma representation

E_{s}(z)=z^{s-1}\int_{z}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-u}u^{-s}\mathrm{d}u,

(155)

which is the form often used in the literature following
Manschot and Wang

ManschotWang2024
;
WangThesis2025
. The constant value is

E_{s}(0)=\frac{1}{s-1},\qquad E_{6}(0)=\frac{1}{5}.

(156)

In the vacuum application, the diagonal argument is always
4\pi m\geq 0
, so
the branch-cut issues relevant to genuine threshold problems do not yet arise.

A.5
Amplitude convention

The regularized mode block is written as

L^{r}_{m,n,s}=\int_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{\tau}\,y^{-s}q^{m}\bar{q}^{n}-2\mathrm{i}\,\delta_{m,n}E_{s}(4\pi m).

(157)

Because of the wedge measure (
151
), the vacuum block is
typically imaginary. The sector integral is therefore written as

I^{r}_{XY}=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}F_{XY}(m,n)L^{r}_{m,n,6},

(158)

while the real amplitude convention is

\mathcal{A}_{XY}=\mathrm{i}I^{r}_{XY}.

(159)

This convention is what turns the exact zero-mode block

L^{r}_{0,0,6}=-8\mathrm{i}/(9\sqrt{3})

into the real exact contribution

\mathcal{A}_{00}=512/(9\sqrt{3})
.

These conventions keep the main text free of repeated local definitions. They
also stabilize the comparisons on which the present analysis depends: the

SO(8)
character normalization, the sign of the cusp tail, the

q
-expansion coefficients, and the distinction between auxiliary sector data
and the signed Type IIB projection.

Appendix B
Contour deformation and cutoff independence

The Lorentzian contour at the non-separating cusp and its
generalized-exponential-integral tail are written in the main text at the
canonical cutoff
T_{0}=1
. Keeping
T_{0}
explicit makes
the contour dependence visible before the canonical specialization. The discussion
contains four ingredients: the contour deformation in the long-tube variable,
the equivalent
q
-plane description near
q=0
, the non-negative
Lorentzian tail evaluation used in the vacuum proof, and the cancellation of
the splitting-height dependence after the compact part and the tail are
combined.

B.1
Euclidean segment and Lorentzian tail

Let
T_{0}\geq 1
be a cutoff height in the cusp. The Euclidean long-tube
variable is

y=\operatorname{Im}\tau,

(160)

and the basic Lorentzian continuation replaces the semi-infinite Euclidean tail
by the contour

\Gamma(T_{0})=[1,T_{0}]\cup\{\,T_{0}+\mathrm{i}t_{L}:\;t_{L}\geq 0\,\}.

(161)

The first segment is the finite Euclidean proper-time interval. The second
segment is the vertical Lorentzian branch selected by the string-theoretic
Feynman prescription
Witten2013IEpsilon
;
ManschotWang2024
;
WangThesis2025
.
In the vacuum problem there are no additional puncture moduli, so the contour
issue is concentrated entirely in this one long-tube variable.

For a diagonal Fourier mode
m=n
, the cusp factor is

q^{m}\bar{q}^{m}=\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi my}.

(162)

Hence the Lorentzian tail takes the form

\int_{\Gamma(T_{0})}\mathrm{d}z\,z^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi mz}=\int_{1}^{T_{0}}\mathrm{d}y\,y^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi my}\;+\;\mathrm{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t_{L}\,(T_{0}+\mathrm{i}t_{L})^{-s}\mathrm{e}^{-4\pi m(T_{0}+\mathrm{i}t_{L})}.

(163)

This formula displays two structural features: the intermediate contour
decomposition depends on the height
T_{0}
, and the Lorentzian branch is
intrinsically complex even when the Euclidean integrand is real.

B.2
q
-plane picture

The same contour has a simple interpretation in the degeneration variable

q=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau}=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}x}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi y}.

(164)

Along the Euclidean strip, increasing
y
moves
q
radially toward the
degeneration point
q=0
. Along the Lorentzian segment
y=T_{0}+\mathrm{i}t_{L}
, one
instead obtains

q=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}x}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi T_{0}}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}t_{L}}.

(165)

Thus the modulus
|q|=\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi T_{0}}
is fixed, while the phase winds with

t_{L}
. In the
q
-plane, the Lorentzian continuation is therefore a circle
or spiral around
q=0
at fixed radius. This is the vacuum analogue of the
general picture emphasized in detailed treatments of the stringy

i\varepsilon
prescription: Euclidean proper time approaches the degeneration
point radially, while the Lorentzian continuation circles that same point in
the complexified variable
Witten2013IEpsilon
;
WangThesis2025
.

Equation (
165

identifies the contour with the degeneration geometry of the torus rather than
with an arbitrary split of the real axis. The contour prescribes how the
modular integral approaches the non-separating cusp.

B.3
Non-negative tail evaluation

The Type IIB vacuum proof only requires the non-negative diagonal modes

m\geq 0

evaluation in terms of the generalized exponential integral. Let

First suppose

G.3
Data entering the four-graviton threshold

Beyond the first threshold relation, a four-graviton application introduces
additional channel and puncture data. The first class consists of
channel polynomials: exact low-level
Q
-coefficient extractions, their
channelwise cut integrals, and the matching expressions that compare the cut
data with the corresponding diagonal
E_{s}
-branch structure. The second
class comes from the puncture integrals: puncture-local simplex formulae,
moment and beta-function representations, and chamber-local symbolic
coefficient packages. The third class concerns the threshold walls themselves,
where near-diagonal, wider off-diagonal, and pair-resolved Fourier
organizations must be glued across the first physical wall at
s=4

These ingredients place cut expressions, puncture-local coefficients, and
Fourier, Laurent, and logarithmic branch data in a common threshold language.
The formulas displayed above are local in the threshold chamber. A complete
four-point cusp/Fourier generator would additionally require the full
worldsheet kernel and the puncture integrations over all modular images.

G.4
Separation from the vacuum functional

The vacuum functional depends on the Type IIB torus integrand, the
regularized Lorentzian and
E_{s}
mode blocks, the convergence and projector
identities of Appendix
F
, and the coefficient-level
GSO identity. The four-graviton threshold quantities introduced above belong
to a separate non-vacuum problem.

The threshold calculation is included to identify the nearest non-vacuum
setting in which the same Fourier-block organization acquires kinematic
content. Once punctures and Koba–Nielsen factors are restored, the cusp data
are tied to physical thresholds rather than to the sector functionals of the
vacuum torus. The four-graviton formulas in this appendix therefore serve as
a separate threshold comparison, with definitions and consistency conditions
that are independent of the vacuum calculation.
