Close-Out Project Description for Koepke's Dept of Energy grant entitled

Toroidal Alfven Wave Coupling (Nonlinear Wave-Wave Interactions) on DIII-D

A. Overall goal, Scientific Rationale, and Fundamental question or problem being
addressed 2020-2024:

Overall goal

Connect DIII-D physics with space plasma phenomena. In this case of using the toroidal
Alfvén eigenmodes and frequency-chirping Reversed-Shear Alfvén eignmodes in DIII-D, we
will document how the nonlinear interactions among dipolar Kinetic Alfvén Wave
eigenmodes in space plasmas may determine saturation levels of these fluctuations. We
seek evidence of nonlinear energy transfer and wave-wave coupling during 3-wave
interactions mediated by a much lower-frequency mode. In FY2019, we found evidence of
nonlinear “wave-wave” interactions in 175 relevant shots of archival DIII-D data. Toroidal
mode number was identified and spectrograms were produced from each shot’s Mirnov-
coil data. Bispectral analysis was performed using a preliminary version of a new user-
friendly code derived from a 1995 M.S. thesis at WVU. These results formed a part of a May
2019 M.S. thesis at WVU.

 In FY2020, during the proposal preparation, we significantly improved the bispectrum code
and used it to analyze fluctuations in TJ-II stellarator and T-10 tokamak. Dominant nonlinear
interactions and operating parameters in DIII-D were next identified. The
objective/motivation of this DIII-D proposed project is to quantify and explain how
nonlinear wave-wave interactions influence or control Toroidal Alfvén Eigenmode (TAE)
amplitude. A TAE spectrum will be self-driven and/or externally driven to the largest
possible amplitude. Another mode (either a TAE or possibly a magnetic field perturbation)
will be imposed and changes will be observed in mode frequency and relative mode
amplitude.

Scientific rationale:

Plasma waves in the intermediate frequency range, between the ion and electron
gyrofrequencies, are important to the dynamics of space plasmas and are vital to the
evolution of the radiation belts, magnetic-field-reconnection processes, and particle-
energy-transfer at the interfaces between boundary layers.

Improved understanding of how nonlinear Alfven eigenmode interactions saturate mode
amplitudes could lead to the development of a predictive capability for ion energization in
the magnetosphere. Intellectual insights from in-depth experimental interpretation will
motivate new expts, expanded conclusions, and the next generation of modeling nonlinear
wave-wave coupling. Improved understanding of nonlinear Alfven eigenmode interactions
and how they saturate mode amplitude could lead to the development of a predictive
capability for ion energization in the magnetosphere.

Intellectual insights from in-depth experimental interpretation will motivate new
experiments, expanded conclusions, and the next generation of modeling nonlinear wave-
wave coupling and wave-wave-wave coupling.

Anticipated outcome: Improved understanding of how nonlinear Alfven eigenmode

B. Results achieved that were related to the question or problem being addressed:

Analyzing archival data from DIII-D and upgrading the WVU bispectral analysis capabilities
using electro-nic nonlinear oscillators, audio signals, Cluster spacecraft data, Texas
Helimak data, TJ-II stellarator data, and T-10 tokamak data has resulted in new tools to
interpret nonlinear wave-wave coupling. The first Figure illustrates DIII-D Shot 153593 as
analyzed with the upgraded conventional bispectral analysis algorithm. The second Figure
illustrates both the bicoherence magnitude and phase for DIII-D Shot 170803. The third
Figure illustrates both the bicoherence magnitude and bispectrogram, i.e., the time-
resolved bispectrum, for DIII-D Shot 170803.

 TAEFL, a reduced-dimensionality Landau-fluid-closure model for energetic particle driven
instabilities (D. A. Spong, Long-term Landau-fluid simulation of Alfvén eigenmode
Instabilities, Plasma and Fusion Research 9, 3403077, 2014) was applied to the DIII-D
experiment case where several Alfvén instabilities are present. This type of model has
several features that make it useful for the simulation of long-lived nonlinear states: (a) it is
a continuum model so particle noise and associated error accumulation over time is not an
issue, (b) it is computationally efficient so longer time interval modeling is feasible, and (c)
a variety of source/sink models can be used and nonlinear coupling effects can be turned
on and off to test different mechanisms.

 Fundamental question or problem being addressed: How do nonlinear wave-wave
interactions control mode amplitude in curvilinear geometries (toroidal, dipolar)? How
does nonlinear and linear energy transfer processes compete in such cases?

 Facility novelty of existing DIII-tokamak capabilities needed for success: The DIII-tokamak
at General Atomics (San Diego, CA) is the nation's plasma confinement chamber having
the largest size dimension, highest product of plasma density:plasma
temperature:trapped-plasma duration. The facility has the most complete diagnostic
portfolio for acquiring data and an extroordinary range of data acquisition resolution.

 Specific relevant area of plasma physics: Nonlinear interactions among Alfvenic modes
arise in space and astrophysical plasma environments, such as the solar wind and Earth’s
magnetosphere, as well as in fusion plasmas. In the solar wind, these nonlinear
interactions mediate the turbulent cascade of energy from large to small scales. In the
magnetosphere, it is proposed that kinetic Alfven eigenmodes energize ionospheric ion in
geomagnetic storms. What sets amplitude of these eigenmodes? In fusion plasmas, Alfven
eigenmodes have been observed to interact nonlinearly. Although saturated TAE amplitude

may be controlled by scattering of energetic particles, nonlinear energy transfer is another
potential saturation mechanism. Can we control TAE saturated amplitude in DIII-D by
imposing additional modes that tap energy nonlinearly?

Diagnostics used: ECE, Toroidal-mode magnetic pick-up, B-dot measurement in space
and time,

Interferometers, time-averaged spectra plus raw time series, time-resolved transport.

Facility novelty of existing/new DIII-D capabilities needed for success: (Why DIII-D?):

Significant controllability for driving TAEs. Thorough diagnosis of modes. Capability to
enable a thorough study of nonlinear interactions in curvilinear field geometry. Thomson
Scattering, Electron Cyclotron Emission, Glow Discharge Cleaning, Resonant Magnetic

Perturbation control, Electron Cyclotron Current Drive control are all existing and needed
capabilities that can be employed simultaneously.

E. Summary of scientific findings

We controlled TAE saturated amplitude in DIII-D by imposing additional modes that tap
energy nonlinearly. The technical and diagnostic approach closely resembled shots 153593
and 170803.

We drove a TAE spectrum to largest possible amplitude. We imposed another mode and
observed changes in mode frequency and relative mode amplitude.

The proposed project was high payoff because (1) the Frontier Science Experiments
campaign allowed unique tokamak conditions for discovery plasma science and (2)
archived DIII-D data exhibited signatures of three-wave coupling and energy transfer
among TAE.

 The project was low risk because investigators are experts in (1) ion-cyclotron, Alfven,
lower-hybrid, and whistler waves, (2) nonlinear wave-wave interactions, (3) time-resolved
bi-coherence and high-order bispectal analysis, and (4) the interrelationship between
plasma experiments in the lab and in space. The project had disciplinary impact because
an improved understanding of how nonlinear Alfven eigenmode interactions saturate mode
amplitudes should lead to the development of a more predictive capability for ion
energization in the magnetosphere.

BicAn: An integrated, open-source framework for polyspectral analysis

Greg Riggsa, Mark Koepke, Tyler Matheny

We present a novel platform for higher-order spectral analysis of time series data. Code is
developed for Python and Matlab. The theory, utility, and applications of such analyses is
summarized. Direct estimation of coherence (n = 2), bicoherence (n = 3),

and tricoherence (n = 4) spectra are given for test signals; higher-order (n >4) spectra are
inferred at single points in polyfrequency space. Quantification of uncertainty for
nonstationary processes is considered, and applications to nonlinear dynamics research
are given. which is submitted in 2025 at Computer Physics Communications.
