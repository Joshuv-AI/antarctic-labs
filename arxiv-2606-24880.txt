Stability Checking of Markov Jump Linear Systems via Probabilistic Temporal Logic (Extended Version)
Lena Becker, Holger Hermanns

Abstract
Markov jump linear systems (MJLSs) model dynamical phenomena subject to random switching among multiple linear modes, driven by an underlying Markov chain. Classical notions such as mean and mean-square stability characterize the long-term asymptotic behaviour of the first and second moments of an MJLS , but they can be overly conservative or even misleading when only a specific subset of initial conditions is of interest. We tackle this challenge through the lens of model checking, where reasoning about specific sets of initial conditions is intrinsic to the approach. We begin by formalizing probabilistic computation tree logic (PCTL) on MJLSs , enabling the specification of state-based temporal properties for these systems. Building on this foundation, we extend the logic to capture moment-based stability properties relative to a prescribed set of initial states. While we ultimately do not obtain a decision procedure for the entire base logic, the logical extensions can be handled – albeit with some technical subtleties – by exploiting linear-algebraic techniques.

Introduction

Dynamical systems are a fundamental tool for modelling the evolution of real-world processes over time, typically with the objective of designing controllers that ensure a desired system behaviour.
In many applications, however, the underlying dynamics are subject to uncertainty and may exhibit abrupt changes, e.g. in case of system failures or volatile environmental circumstances, inducing switches between different modes of dynamics, each covering the behaviour in a specific situation.
We here focus on models where these switches are probabilistic in nature, and thus governed by a Markov chain.
In such
Markov jump linear systems (MJLSs)

[
7
,
8
]
, modes are mapped to distinct system dynamics, adding discrete behaviour to the continuous-state system.
These system dynamics are typically assumed to be linear, as many systems can be locally approximated by linear dynamics near operating points.

MJLSs
have been used to model a wide range of application domains including flight control
[
12
]
, power systems analysis
[
21
]
, and robotic systems such as lower-limb exoskeletons
[
22
]
.

In settings where the system cannot be controlled, or where the controller is already fixed, one must rely on analysis techniques to assess its behaviour.
A commonly analysed property of dynamical systems in this context is their stability, i.e. whether the system’s state converges in the long run, independently of the initial conditions.
The stability analysis of
MJLSs
not only needs to take into account the dynamical behaviour of all system modes, but also the long-run behaviour of the underlying Markov chain.
It may yield counterintuitive results:
even if each individual dynamics considered in isolation is stable, the
MJLS
as a whole may exhibit unstable behaviour, and vice versa.

In the literature, different notions of stability for
MJLSs
have been explored, prominently among them
mean stability

[
20
]
– analysing the evolution of the first moment, and
mean-square stability

[
9
,
7
,
11
]
– accounting for cancellation effects due to opposing outliers by considering the second moment as the stability criterion of choice.
Both stability notions are typically characterized via eigenvalue analysis of specific operators that capture the combined continuous and discrete probabilistic dynamics of the
MJLS
.
In doing so, it is standard to aim for global guarantees covering all initial states.
This perspective may however be overly conservative in practice.
In particular, instability might arise only for a small fraction of initial states that may not even be of practical relevance, e.g. if corresponding to physically unreachable configurations, such as physically impossible operating regions of a drone.

Despite its intuitive importance, the
MJLS
stability problem for a given set of initial states has not been tackled in the literature, at least to the best of our knowledge.
It is a genuine model-checking problem, so we attack it by embedding appropriate logical operators into a typical temporal-logic framework.
Probabilistic model checking, based on logics such as
probabilistic computation tree logic (PCTL)
, enables the specification of temporal properties with respect to specific sets of initial states
[
14
]
.

PCTL
for
MJLSs
arises naturally by conservatively extending work on
PCTL
for
discrete-time Markov chains (DTMCs)

[
3
]
, as well as by specializing work for
general Markov processes (GMPs)

[
23
]
.
When turning to the model-checking problem for this logic, however, it turns out that the latter work must necessarily be incomplete, because of a Skolem-hardness result
[
6
]
that applies to a specific fragment of
PCTL
model checking on
MJLSs
(and thus equally on
GMPs
from
[
23
]
).
We proceed by extending the logic with operators to capture moment-based stability properties and discuss the intricacies when checking properties that include such operators.
Altogether, we arrive at a logic that enables the specification of refined stability-like properties as well as their combination with classical reachability or branching-time objectives.
A computational example enriches the exposition.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows:

Section 2
develops relevant background on
MJLSs
.
In
Section 3
, we define a semantics for classical
PCTL
for
MJLSs
and give an algorithm to verify such
PCTL
properties.
Furthermore, we discuss limitations in model checking reachability properties due to the uncountable state space of
MJLSs
.
Finally, in
Section 4
, we extend
PCTL
with the aforementioned new operators that capture the first and second moments of
MJLSs
and describe an algorithm to verify these properties.

Contrasting our approach, existing work on the verification of such systems is based on abstractions into finite-state models
[

A

Markov reward models (MRMs)

extend

[
1
]

The use of multiplicative rewards instead
[

yields a modelling formalism that closely resembles

This prevents modelling dependencies across multiple continuous dimensions.
Further, since only positive rewards are supported, behaviours that rely on sign changes or cancellations are excluded.
The authors consider a notion of expectation that is similar in spirit to our treatment of continuous state variables, but oscillatory behaviour is handled by distinguishing between limit superior and limit inferior.
In contrast, we consider a notion based on limits of Cesàro-averaged expected values.

Linear (discrete-time) dynamical systems (LDSs)

given by
{x(k+1)=Ax(k)}

]
.

As we show in

MJLSs
.

Markov jump linear systems (MJLSs)

Stability of
MJLSs

There are different notions of stability for
MJLSs
.
In the following, we summarize well-established results on
mean
and
mean-square stability
, which analyse the system state’s expected value and uncentred variance, respectively, on the long run
[
20

7
]
.
All results in the remainder of this section stem from
[
7
]
, notationally adjusted to our setting.
The first and second (uncentred) moment at time
k\in\mathbb{N}
of an
MJLS

\mathfrak{J}
with initial state
(x,\theta)\in S_{\mathfrak{J}}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(x,\theta)}\left[x(k)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\hskip-15.0pt\mathrlap{(x_{0},\theta_{0})\cdots(x_{k},\theta_{k})\in\mathit{P\!aths}_{k}\left((x,\theta)\right)}}x_{k}\cdot\mathit{Pr}\left(\mathit{Cyl}\left(\theta_{0}\cdots\theta_{k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{(x,\theta)}\left[x(k)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{(x,\theta)}\left[x(k)x(k)^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}{}\right]=\sum_{\hskip-15.0pt\mathrlap{(x_{0},\theta_{0})\cdots(x_{k},\theta_{k})\in\mathit{P\!aths}_{k}\left((x,\theta)\right)}}x_{k}x_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}{}\cdot\mathit{Pr}\left(\mathit{Cyl}\left(\theta_{0}\cdots\theta_{k}\right)\right)

Note that, as usual for multivariate random variables, the first moment is a vector of the first moments of each random variable, whereas the second (uncentred) moment is an
n\times n
symmetric matrix.
In this matrix, position
(i,j)
represents the covariance between random variable
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
, which are the respective continuous variables in our case.
Further note that while classically, variance is defined as the centred second moment
\mathbb{E}\left[(X-\mathbb{E}\left[X\right])^{2}\right]
, this definition uses the uncentred moment.
However,
\mathbb{E}\left[(X-\mathbb{E}\left[X\right])^{2}\right]=\mathbb{V}\left(X\right)-(\mathbb{E}\left[X\right])^{2}
.

Definition 1

An
MJLS

\mathfrak{J}
is

iff for

,

\exists\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\,\forall(x,\theta)\in S_{\mathfrak{J}}.\,\mathbb{E}_{(x,\theta)}\left[x(k)\right]\to\mu

and it is

iff for

\exists v\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}.\,\forall(x,\theta)\in S_{\mathfrak{J}}.\,\mathbb{V}_{(x,\theta)}\left[x(k)\right]\to v

Mean-square stability obviously implies mean stability.
Due to the quantifier ordering, both stability notions require the limit value to be the same for all initial states.
For our definition of
MJLSs
, this means that the only possible instantiation of

To check an
MJLS

For some initial state

and Markov chain mode

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(x,\theta)}\left[x(k)\;\middle|\;\theta(k)=\theta^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\hskip-15.0pt\mathrlap{(x_{0},\theta_{0})\cdots(x_{k},\theta^{\prime})\in\mathit{P\!aths}_{k}\left((x,\theta)\right)}}x_{k}\cdot\mathit{Pr}\left(\mathit{Cyl}\left(\theta_{0}\cdots\theta^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{(x,\theta)}\left[x(k)\;\middle|\;\theta(k)=\theta^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\hskip-15.0pt\mathrlap{(x_{0},\theta_{0})\cdots(x_{k},\theta^{\prime})\in\mathit{P\!aths}_{k}\left((x,\theta)\right)}}x_{k}x_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}{}\cdot\mathit{Pr}\left(\mathit{Cyl}\left(\theta_{0}\cdots\theta^{\prime}\right)\right)

Note that the sum of

The analogous property for

holds as well. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(x,\theta)}\left[x(k+1)\;\middle|\;\theta(k+1)=j\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}P\left(i,j\right)\cdot A_{i}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{(x,\theta)}\left[x(k)\;\middle|\;\theta(k)=i\right]

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{(x,\theta)}\left[x(k+1)\;\middle|\;\theta(k+1)=j\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}P\left(i,j\right)\cdot A_{i}\cdot\mathbb{V}_{(x,\theta)}\left[x(k)\;\middle|\;\theta(k)=i\right]\cdot A_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}{}

\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{E}_{(x,\theta)}\left[x(k+1)\;\middle|\;\theta(k+1)=1\right]\\

\mathbb{E}_{(x,\theta)}\left[x(k+1)\;\middle|\;\theta(k+1)=m\right]\end{bmatrix}=\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{J}}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{E}_{(x,\theta)}\left[x(k)\;\middle|\;\theta(k)=1\right]\\

Probabilistic Computation Tree Logic for
MJLS

Originally established to specify properties on probabilistic systems such as Markov chains
[
14
]
,
PCTL
is a temporal logic that suits well our modelling framework of
MJLSs
.
We define its syntax as usual:

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle\Coloneqq\mathit{true}\quad|\quad a\quad|\quad\Phi_{1}\land\Phi_{2}\quad|\quad\neg\Phi\quad|\quad\mathcal{P}_{J}\left(\varphi\right)

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\Coloneqq\mathcal{X}\,\Phi\quad|\quad\Phi_{1}\mathbin{\mathcal{U}^{\leq k}}\Phi_{2}\quad|\quad\Phi_{1}\mathbin{\mathcal{U}}\Phi_{2}

where
a\in\mathit{AP}_{x}\sqcup\mathit{AP}_{\theta}
,
J\subseteq[0,1]
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We denote the step-bounded fragment of
PCTL
with
PCTL
-
, i.e. all constructs except the unbounded until.
We define
\mathit{AP}_{x}
and
\mathit{AP}_{\theta}
as the set of atomic propositions on the continuous state and the discrete state, respectively, with
\sqcup
denoting disjoint union.
States
(x,\theta)\in S_{\mathfrak{J}}
can be labelled with atomic propositions:

L((x,\theta))=\left\{a\in\mathit{AP}_{x}\nonscript\;\middle|\allowbreak\nonscript\;\mathopen{}x\in\Pi_{a}\right\}\sqcup\left\{a\in\mathit{AP}_{\theta}\nonscript\;\middle|\allowbreak\nonscript\;\mathopen{}a\in L_{\theta}(\theta)\right\}

where
\Pi_{a}
are convex polytopes, which are bounded convex subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that can be represented equivalently either as the intersection of finitely many half-spaces or as the convex hull of finitely many vertices
[
24
]
.
We restrict the labelling of the continuous state with atomic propositions to convex polytopes, since this class of sets provides a suitable balance between expressiveness and computational tractability.
In particular, membership of a state in a convex polytope can be checked efficiently via its halfspace representation
[
13
]
.
Further, the model-checking algorithm presented below requires repeated set operations and transformations.
Convex polytopes are well suited as a basis for this, since operations such as affine (and, thus, linear) transformations and intersection preserve the polytopic structure
[
24
]
.
Thus, the sets arising during the recursive evaluation of formulas remain computationally manageable.
By contrast, more general classes of sets may fail to retain such a tractable representation under these operations, making them less suitable for algorithmic verification.

Let
\mathfrak{J}
be an
MJLS
,
a\in\mathit{AP}_{x}\sqcup\mathit{AP}_{\theta}
, and
(x,\theta)\in S_{\mathfrak{J}}
.
We define the satisfaction relation
\vDash
of
PCTL
state formulas analogously to Markov chains
[
3
]
:

Adding Stability Properties to
PCTL

Our main motivation, as stated earlier, lies in the fact that a negative answer to the stability analysis of an
MJLS
may be overly conservative.
Two scenarios immediately come to mind in which a more refined answer may be useful:

1.

Even though the moment converges for all initial states, it does not converge to the same value (i.e. the origin) for all initial states.
Thus, the
MJLS
is not considered stable.
However, in this scenario, convergence itself might be considered of higher importance than convergence to the origin.

2.

For some set of initial states, the moment diverges.
Again, the
MJLS
is not considered stable.
However, these initial states may be insignificant, e.g. because they correspond to impossible configurations in the real-world process.
In this case, one would like to restrict the stability analysis to the set of relevant initial states.

4.2
Discussion on Realizability

This section has laid out the mathematical foundations for the analysis of stability-related
PCTL
properties.
As is often the case for algorithms of this kind, however, several challenges arise when implementing them in practice.
The most immediate one is that exact arithmetics over irrational numbers is generally not directly realizable.
Even when restricting
MJLSs
to rational system dynamics and transition probabilities – which both may be reasonable assumptions – some difficulties persist:
In the case of
\text{Sat}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\Pi}\right)
and
\text{Sat}\left(\mathcal{V}_{\Xi}\right)
, the eigenpairs of
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{T}
have to be computed.
Determining the eigenvalues of a
k\times k
matrix is equivalent to finding the roots of a polynomial of degree
k
, for which no general closed-form solution exists when
k>4
(see Abel-Ruffini theorem
[
10
]
).
This frontier is exceeded easily, for instance already for
m=3
and
n=2
in first moment analysis and for
m=2
and
n=2
in second moment analysis.
As a consequence, an exact analytical computation becomes impossible for some systems, and the analysis results can only be as accurate as the underlying numerical approximation.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have addressed
PCTL
model checking for
MJLS
, enriched with propositions that allow reasoning about stability in a way that can take into consideration specific initial state sets. This enables a novel view on
MJLSs
that so far has been impossible to express and decide, based on characterizations of the convergence of the first and second moments, relative to a given initial state.
For the
PCTL
fragment excluding the until operator, we have provided a straightforward decision algorithm, while we have highlighted that the general model-checking problem for step-unbounded properties is open and closely related to other longstanding open problems.
For the logic enriched with stability operators (until aside), we have succeeded in developing a corresponding model-checking procedure that harvests linear-algebraic techniques.
More broadly, our results show that probabilistic model checking provides a natural framework for analysing state-dependent stability properties of
MJLSs
beyond classical global stability notions.
Due to the setup of our logic, stability characteristics can be nested inside ordinary
PCTL
formulas, but the converse is not supported.
While it is possible to define an extended
PCTL
semantics also covering this variant, the model-checking problem for the resulting logic needs to be considered open.
This is an interesting problem for future work.

(x,\theta)\nvDash\Phi_{1}\land(x,\theta)\nvDash\Phi_{2}
, and

3.

(x,\theta)\vDash\Phi_{1}\land(x,\theta)\nvDash\Phi_{2}
.

For cases
1
and
2
,
\mathit{Pr}\left(\left\{\pi\in\mathit{P\!aths}\left((x,\theta)\right)\nonscript\;\middle|\allowbreak\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\pi\vDash\Phi_{1}\mathbin{\mathcal{U}^{\leq k}}\Phi_{2}\right\}\right)
reduces to
\mathit{Pr}\left(\mathit{P\!aths}\left((x,\theta)\right)\right)=1
and
\mathit{Pr}\left(\emptyset\right)=0
, respectively.
These results coincide with
U_{k}\left((x,\theta)\right)
.

For
3
, note that

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}\mathit{Pr}\left(\left\{\pi\in\mathit{P\!aths}\left((x,\theta)\right)\nonscript\;\middle|\allowbreak\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\pi\vDash\Phi_{1}\mathbin{\mathcal{U}^{\leq k}}\Phi_{2}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathit{Pr}\left(\left\{\pi\in\mathit{P\!aths}\left((x,\theta)\right)\nonscript\;\middle|\allowbreak\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\exists j\leq k.\,\pi[j]\vDash\Phi_{2}\land\forall 0\leq i<j.\,\pi[i]\vDash\Phi_{1}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathit{Pr}\left(\bigcup_{j=0}^{k}\left\{\pi\in\mathit{P\!aths}\left((x,\theta)\right)\nonscript\;\middle|\allowbreak\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\pi[j]\vDash\Phi_{2}\land\forall 0\leq i<j.\,\pi[i]\vDash\Phi_{1}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\ref{it:sat1notsat2}}}}{{=}}\mathit{Pr}\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}\left\{\pi\in\mathit{P\!aths}\left((x,\theta)\right)\nonscript\;\middle|\allowbreak\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\pi[j]\vDash\Phi_{2}\land\forall 0\leq i<j.\,\pi[i]\vDash\Phi_{1}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta^{\prime}=1}^{m}P\left(\theta,\theta^{\prime}\right)\cdot\mathit{Pr}\left(\bigcup_{j=0}^{k-1}\right.\begin{multlined}\{\pi\in\mathit{P\!aths}\left((A_{\theta}x,\theta^{\prime})\right)\;|\;{}\\
\left.\vphantom{\bigcup_{j=0}^{k-1}}\pi[j]\vDash\Phi_{2}\land\forall 0\leq i<j.\,\pi[i]\vDash\Phi_{1}\}\right)\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta^{\prime}=1}^{m}P\left(\theta,\theta^{\prime}\right)\cdot\mathit{Pr}(\begin{multlined}\{\pi\in\mathit{Paths}((A_{\theta}x,\theta^{\prime}))\;|\;\\
\exists j\leq k-1.\,\pi[j]\vDash\Phi_{2}\land\forall 0\leq i<j.\,\pi[i]\vDash\Phi_{1}\})\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta^{\prime}=1}^{m}P\left(\theta,\theta^{\prime}\right)\cdot\mathit{Pr}\left(\left\{\pi\in\mathit{P\!aths}\left((A_{\theta}x,\theta^{\prime})\right)\nonscript\;\middle|\allowbreak\nonscript\;\mathopen{}\pi\vDash\Phi_{1}\mathbin{\mathcal{U}^{\leq k-1}}\Phi_{2}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{IH}}}{{=}}\sum_{\theta^{\prime}=1}^{m}P\left(\theta,\theta^{\prime}\right)\cdot U_{k-1}\left((A_{\theta}x,\theta^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=U_{0}\left((x,\theta)\right)+\mathbf{1}_{\text{Sat}\left(\Phi_{1}\right)\setminus\text{Sat}\left(\Phi_{2}\right)}\left((x,\theta)\right)\sum_{\theta^{\prime}=1}^{m}P\left(\theta,\theta^{\prime}\right)\cdot U_{k-1}\left((A_{\theta}x,\theta^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=U_{k}\left((x,\theta)\right)

∎
