 Executive Summary

Laboratory perform characterization analyses of Tank 14H in support of closure activities, specifically preliminary cease
waste removal. This report provides important characterization of the prepared filtrate and air-dried solids in the
January 2026 Tank 14H sample, HTF-14-26-1,2,3,4,5,6, for I-129, Tc-99, Cs-137, and Sr-90. The results reported here
were utilized by SRMC to declare preliminary cease waste removal for Tank 14H.

1.0 Introduction
Tank 14H, a Type II storage tank, completed construction in 1956 and entered service in 1957 as an H-Canyon
waste receipt tank. From 1957 through 1959, Tank 14H received high heat waste from the H-Canyon Plutonium-
Uranium Reduction Extraction (PUREX) process and H-Modified (HM) process high-heat waste until 1960 where
waste leakage into the Tank 14H annulus was detected and further high-heat waste receipts were diverted to
another waste tank. In 1965, Tank 14H was filled to capacity at 1,061,000 gallons from HM low-heat waste and
Thorium Recover Extraction (THOREX) low-heat waste transfers. In 1968, supernatant was removed from Tank
14H in preparation for a sludge removal campaign and, in 1969, a sludge removal campaign (waste removal
phase 1) was completed. In 1970, Tank 14H received supernatant and refilled to capacity. Tank 14H remained
idle (excluding minor transfers and receipts) until 1978 when the majority of the supernatant was removed and
the tank became inactive. Tank 14H has about 50 leak sites.1 The waste that leaked into the secondary
containment has been contained within the annular pan and has not reached the surrounding soils.2 From 2025
to 2026, the waste removal phase 2 was completed, which composed of two mixing campaigns involving
approximately 583,000 gallons of water being added. After the campaign was completed, approximately 3,800
gallons (visually estimated) of sludge remained in the tank.1

Savannah River Mission Completion (SRMC) Waste Removal and Tank Closure requested via a Technical Task
Request (TTR)3 that the Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) characterize samples from tanks in F and H
Tank Farms as they transition into closure mode for requirements involving inhalation dose potential for low-
rem/high-rem transfers and documented safety analysis closure storage mode determination.4 The TTR
stipulates that additional scope can be requested as necessary. As part of this additional scope, SRMC requested
via email (see Appendix A) that SRNL perform density and radionuclide analyses to assist with preliminary cease
waste removal (PCWR) determination.5 The samples were taken from a single location as PCWR demonstrates a
qualitative assessment that the Consolidated General Closure Plan for F-Area and H-Area Waste Tank Systems
performance objectives will not be challenged. The quantitative assessment is later performed on closure mode
samples that are sampled from multiple locations in the tank.

2.0 Experimental Procedure
Six Tank 14H samples (HTF-14-26-1, HTF-14-26-2, HTF-14-26-3, HTF-14-26-4, HTF-14-26-5, HTF-14-26-6)
containing approximately 200 mL of slurry were delivered on January 21, 2026 to SRNL in stainless steel dip
bottle containers. Prior to pulling the samples, Tank 14H was mixed for at least 8 hours. These samples were
pulled without foil from the tank bottom through the 3’6” Riser within 30 minutes of pump shutdown. The dip
bottles were turned on their side and scraped along the tank bottom to ensure solids collection as needed. Upon
receiving the stainless steel dip bottle samples, the contents were transferred into plastic bottles with their
corresponding respective labels that listed nuclear materials control and accountability (NMC&A) serial numbers
(See Figure 1).

Figure 1. HTF-14-26-1, HTF-14-26-2, HTF-14-26-3, HTF-14-26-4, HTF-14-26-5, HTF-15-26-6 Sample Poly Bottles Containing
 “As-Received” Slurry.

HTF-14-26-1, HTF-14-26-2, and HTF-14-26-3 were composited together through filtration over a pre-weighed
0.45 µm nylon filter in order to separate the solid phase from the liquid phase. Likewise, HTF-14-26-4, HTF-14-
26-5, and HTF-14-26-6 were also composited together through filtration over a separate pre-weighed 0.45 µm
nylon filter. The filtrates from the two evolutions were composited into a single filtrate sample. All future
aliquots taken from the filtrate sample were taken (using pipettes with the tips remaining uncut) after vigorously
mixing the sample for at least 15 seconds (using the manipulator). The solids on the filters were air-dried for
approximately 5 days; the filters containing solids were weighed daily until the difference in weight between the
previous day and the current day were less than or equal to 0.05 grams. The approximately 7.1 grams of solids
from the two filters were then composited into a single plastic poly bottle (See Figure 2). The solids were mixed
in the bottle using a spatula.

The solids bulk and filtrate densities were measured gravimetrically using containers of known volume. Peroxide
fusion digestion preparations utilizing sodium peroxide and nitric acid6 and aqua regia digestions7 were
performed on the solids, each in triplicate, prior to Sr-90 (samples digested by peroxide fusions), Tc-99 and Cs-
137 (samples digested by aqua regia) measurements.
To measure I-129 in the solids (in triplicate) in the Shielded Cells, an aliquot of solids (approximately 0.7-0.9
grams for each replicate) was transferred into a bottle containing potassium iodide tracer. Nitric acid was added
to the bottle containing the sample and KI and left overnight to digest the sample. On the following day (further
delay would lead to iodine loss from sublimation), sodium hydroxide solution was added to the bottle and left
overnight to precipitate the sludge solids. After a day, the caustic sample was added to a bottle containing
crystalline silicotitanate (CST) and monosodium titanate (MST) to remove Cs-137, Sr-90, and actinides from the
sample. Additional sodium hydroxide solution was added to the bottle and then filtered through a 0.2 µm filter.
The resulting filtrate was measured for I-129.
In order to achieve lower I-129 detection limits in the filtrate, larger aliquots of sample (approximately 100 mL
each) were utilized for the triplicate replicates. In the shielded cells, the sample was added to potassium iodide
(used as a tracer) in deionized water. The sample/KI mixture was then transferred to a bottle containing CST and
MST for decontamination. The suspension was filtered (0.45 µm filter) to separate the CST and MST from the
filtrate containing the I-129 and KI tracer. The CST and MST solids were further washed with sodium hydroxide
to ensure all iodine was collected in the filtrate. A subsequent CST/MST strike was performed on the filtrate
followed by filtering and sodium hydroxide washing to ensure decontamination. The entire filtrate for each
replicate was utilized for I-129 analysis.
2.1 Radiochemical Analyses
2.1.1 Gamma
Aqua regia digested sample and liquid sample aliquots were prepared and diluted in the laboratory to levels
appropriate for gamma pulse height analysis on high purity germanium (HPGe) detectors. Each sample was
counted in a calibrated geometry for a length of time to obtain the minimum method uncertainty of 5.00% for
Cs-137.

2.1.2 I-129
Filtrates from the shielded cells were acidified prior to addition of Bio-Rad ammonium molybdophosphate
(AMP), which must be in acidic condition for successful filtration and removal of Cs-137. These additions and
filtrations were performed twice to reduce interference. After reduction with sodium sulfite, the iodine was
precipitated to AgI using silver nitrate and then separated by filtration. The filter was then counted in a
calibrated geometry using low energy photon/x-ray, thin-windowed, semi-planar, HPGe spectrometers. The
filters were then folded and placed into rabbits for yield determination of the elemental iodine by neutron
activation analysis (NAA). A known amount of dissolved tracer was irradiated, along with the samples, to
quantify yield on a per sample basis to correct for losses during separation. A blank sample was included to track
for contamination throughout the method.

 3

2.1.3 Sr-90
Peroxide fusion digested sample and liquid sample aliquots were first spiked with elemental Sr to trace
separation efficiency on a per sample basis. Two blank samples were spiked with a known Sr-90 standard to
calibrate the samples’ final results. A non-spiked blank sample was included to track for contamination
throughout the method. A serial dilution of one of the samples was also included as an additional quality control
(QC) check for data quality. The samples were run through crown-ether-based solid phase extractant to separate
the Sr-90 from the matrix. Equal aliquots were measured by Inductively Coupled Plasma Emission Spectroscopy
(ICP-ES) and liquid scintillation counting (LSC). ICP-ES measured the elemental Sr for recovery purposes. The
blank spikes allowed for correction of all samples due to being spiked with a known amount of Sr-90. The LSC
counts measured gross beta at a calibrated energy range to convert from cpm to dpm Sr-90.

2.1.4 Tc-99
Aqua regia digested sample and liquid sample aliquots were spiked with separated Tc-99m tracer that was
produced in-house from Mo-99 using the NAA facility. Two blank samples were spiked with a known Tc-99
standard to calibrate the samples’ final results. A non-spiked blank sample was included to track for
contamination throughout the method. To minimize volatility, the sample solutions were made caustic before
extracting the Tc-99 using Eichrom TEVA resin columns. After this purification, the samples were then run
through an Eichrom TEVA filter to collect the Tc-99 for counting. The filters were folded and placed into LSC
vials, where the Tc-99m yields were measured by an HPGe well detector to correct for losses during the
radiochemical separations. After waiting several days for the Tc-99m to decay, so as not to cause interference,
the samples were counted by LSC to attain a gross beta value. Calibrated efficiencies allowed the gross beta cpm
value to be converted to dpm Tc-99 after recovery correction.

The radionuclide concentrations were higher in the air-dried solids compared to the filtrate which is expected as
the air-dried solids are composed of dried residual supernatant along with the insoluble solids. The Sr-90
concentration was much higher in the air-dried solids than the filtrate as Sr-90 tends to be in the solid phase in
caustic media. The concentrations of the radionuclides cannot be directly compared as the concentrations are
on different bases, e.g., concentration of radionuclide in a gram of solids or a milliliter of filtrate. In order to
directly compare the two bases, a conversion would be needed utilizing weight percent solids data of the
sample. In this HTF-14-26-1/2/3/4/5/6 composite sample, the weight percent solids measurement was not
performed.

In Table 2, the density analyses are reported for the HTF-14-26-1/2/3/4/5/6 composite sample. The filtrate
density was similar to pure water as the solids in the tank had been previously washed with water during the
cleaning process. The solid samples were prepared by filtration followed by air drying. Additionally, the solids
were mixed with a spatula; therefore, the particle sizes of the solids and void volumes between the solids can
vary. A smaller, less varied void volume would lead to a higher density.

4.0 Conclusions
SRNL performed characterization analyses of Tank 14H in support of closure activities for PCWR. This report
documents the Cs-137, I-129, Sr-90, Tc-99, and density results from the characterization of the solids and filtrate
from the January 2026 Tank 14H sample, HTF-14-26-1/2/3/4/5/6, as well as provides the details on the specific
sample preparations and radiochemical methods utilized. The results reported were utilized by SRMC to
propose to cease waste removal activities in Tank 14H and enter the sampling and analysis phase.5
