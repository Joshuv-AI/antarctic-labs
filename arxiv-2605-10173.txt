Just Previsions
Jean Goubault-Larrecq

Abstract
Previsions are positively homogeneous functionals, and are generalized forms of integration functionals. We investigate previsions—just previsions, not sublinear or superlinear previsions as in previous work. We show that every prevision can be expressed as an infimum of sublinear previsions, and as a supremum of superlinear previsions under mild conditions. This extends to homeomorphisms between spaces of previsions and certain hyperspaces over spaces of sublinear or superlinear previsions, which can also be characterized in terms of orthogonality relations, making the construction a variant of a double powerspace construction. Keywords: Prevision, double hyperspace construction, continuous valuation.

Introduction

Let
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be the space of extended non-negative real numbers

\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{\infty\}
. Given a topological space
X
, we write

{\mathcal{L}}X
for the set of all lower semicontinuous maps from
X
to

\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. A
prevision
is a positively homogenous,
Scott-continuous map from
{\mathcal{L}}X
to
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
(we will define all
missing terms in Section
2
). A
linear
prevision
can be thought of as a functional

h\in{\mathcal{L}}X\mapsto\int h\,d\mu
for some measure
\mu
on
X
,
or more precisely for some continuous valuation
\mu
on
X
, and
previsions that satisfy the more lenient constraints of being
sublinear or superlinear were advocated as models of mixtures of
non-deterministic and probabilistic choice in
[
8
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to examine what can be said about
previsions: just previsions, with no sublinearity, superlinearity of
linearity involved. We will see that every prevision is a pointwise
infimum of sublinear previsions, and also a pointwise supremum of
superlinear previsions under mild assumptions. This extends to
homeomorphisms between
spaces
of previsions and certain
hyperspaces over spaces of sublinear or superlinear previsions. We
will obtain those homeomorphisms as restrictions of retractions of
well-known convex hyperspaces over spaces of sublinear or superlinear
previsions. However, the hyperspaces that are homeomorphic to our
spaces of previsions cannot be defined by convexity properties alone,
and we elucidate what they are (partially) using orthogonality
relations. We will argue that this is related to the
double
hyperspace
construction
[
4
]
.

Preliminaries

2.1
Topology and order.

For background on topology, we refer
to
[
9
]
. We write
int({A})
for the interior of

A
,
cl(A)
for the closure of
A
, and
\mathcal{O}X
for the lattice of open subsets of
X
. The
specialization preordering
\leq
of a topological space
X
is
defined on points
x,y\in X
by
x\leq y
if and only if every open
neighborhood of
x
contains
y
. We write
\mathop{\downarrow}
for downward closure
and
\mathop{\uparrow}
for upward closure with respect to
\leq
. The closure of
a point
x
is
\mathop{\downarrow}x
.

We will also say that
x
is
below

y
and that
y
is

above

x
when
x\leq y
. A space is
T_{0}
if and only if

\leq
is antisymmetric,
T_{1}
if and only if
\leq
is the equality
relation.

A family
D
of elements of a preordered set
P
is
directed
if
and only if it is non-empty and every pair of elements of
D
has an
upper bound in
D
. In case
P
is a poset (a partially ordered set),
we write
\sup\nolimits^{\scriptstyle\uparrow}D
, or
\sup\nolimits^{\scriptstyle\uparrow}_{i\in I}x_{i}
when

D={(x_{i})}_{i\in I}
, for the supremum of a directed family
D
, if
it exists; similarly, we write
\bigcup\nolimits^{\scriptstyle\uparrow}_{i\in I}U_{i}
for the union of
a directed family of subsets
U_{i}
of a fixed set.

A function
f\colon P\to Q
between posets is
monotonic
if
and only if for all
x,x^{\prime}\in P
,
x\leq x^{\prime}
implies

f(x)\leq f(x^{\prime})
. It is
Scott-continuous
if and only if
f

is monotonic and for every directed family
{(x_{i})}_{i\in I}
with a
supremum
x
in
P
, the (necessarily directed) family of elements

f(x_{i})
has
f(x)
as supremum. Scott-continuity is equivalent to
continuity with the respective Scott topologies on
P
and
Q
. The

Scott topology
on a poset
P
consists of those subsets

U
—the
Scott-open subsets
of
P
—that are upwards closed
(
x\in U
and
x\leq x^{\prime}
implies
x^{\prime}\in U
) and such that every
directed family
D
that has a supremum in
U
intersects
U
. That
is most useful in the context of
dcpo
s (short for
directed-complete posets), namely posets in which every directed
family has a supremum.

We see
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
as a complete lattice under the usual ordering
\leq
.
A function
f\colon X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
that is continuous from
X
to

\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with its Scott topology is known as a
lower
semicontinuous
function. The set
{\mathcal{L}}X
of lower
semicontinuous functions from
X
to
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
will be ordered
pointwise. When seen as a topological space, we will always assume
that
{\mathcal{L}}X
has the Scott topology of that ordering.

2.2
Hyperspaces.

The closed subsets of a topological space
X
form its
Hoare
hyperspace

\mathcal{H}_{0}X
. We order it by inclusion. The
lower
Vietoris topology
on
\mathcal{H}_{0}X
has a subbase of open sets of the
form
\Diamond U\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{C\in\mathcal{H}_{0}X\mid C\cap U\neq\emptyset\}
, where
U
ranges over
\mathcal{O}X
. The specialization
ordering of that topology is inclusion, and we will write
\mathcal{H}_{0{\mathit{v}}}X
for

\mathcal{H}_{0}X
with the lower Vietoris topology. The set
\mathcal{H}X
is
equal to
\mathcal{H}_{0}X
minus the empty set. With the subspace topology
induced by the inclusion in
\mathcal{H}_{0{\mathit{v}}}X
, which we also call the lower
Vietoris topology, we obtain a space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathit{v}}X
, with the same subbase of
open sets.

A subset
Q
of a space
X
is compact if and only if every open cover
of
Q
has a finite subcover; no separation is required. Alexander’s
subbase lemma states that given any subbase
\mathcal{B}
, of the
topology of
X
, a subset
Q
of
X
is compact if and only if every
open cover of
Q
by elements of
\mathcal{B}
has a finite subcover.
A space
X
is
locally compact
if and only if every point has a
base of compact neighborhoods. There is a weaker property,

core-compactness
, which we will sometimes refer to, but which
we will not define here
[
9
, Section 5.2.1]
.

A saturated subset is one that is equal to the intersection of its
open neighborhoods, or equivalently, that is upward-closed with
respect to the specialization preordering
\leq
. The compact
saturated subsets of
X
form its
Smyth hyperspace

\mathcal{Q}_{0}X
.
We let
\mathcal{Q}X
be
\mathcal{Q}_{0}X
minus the empty set. Both are
ordered by reverse inclusion, which is also the specialization
ordering of the
upper Vietoris topology
. The latter has a base
of open sets of the form

\Box U\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}X\text{ (resp. $\mathcal{Q}X$)}\mid Q\subseteq U\}
.

One may compose
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
in two ways, and in most cases
the order of composition does not matter. Historically, the first
result of this kind is due to Flannery and Martin
[
5
]
. It
was extended to all dcpos by Heckmann
[
12
]
, and to all
locales by Vickers and Townsend
[
21
]
, with
definitions of the respective Hoare and Smyth powerdomains or
powerlocales that are close, although not identical. In the setting
of topological spaces, the relevant results are due to de Brecht and
Kawai
[
4
]
. For every topological space
X
, there is a
map
\sigma_{X}\colon\mathcal{H}_{0{\mathit{v}}}{\mathcal{Q}_{0{\mathit{v}}}X}\to\mathcal{Q}_{0{\mathit{v}}}{\mathcal{H}_{0{\mathit{v}}}X}
, defined by:

\displaystyle\sigma_{X}(\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{C\in\mathcal{H}_{0{\mathit{v}}}X\mid\forall Q\in\mathcal{C},C\cap Q\neq\emptyset\},

(2.1)

and it is a topological embedding. It is a homeomorphism if and only
if
X
is
consonant

[
4
, Theorem 6.13]
, namely if
and only if the Scott topology on
\mathcal{O}X
has a subbase of open
subsets of the form

\blacksquare Q\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{U\in\mathcal{O}X\mid Q\subseteq U\}
, where

Q
ranges over
\mathcal{Q}_{0}X
. The inverse is the map

\tau_{X}\colon\mathcal{Q}_{0{\mathit{v}}}{\mathcal{H}_{0{\mathit{v}}}X}\to\mathcal{H}_{0{\mathit{v}}}{\mathcal{Q}_{0{\mathit{v}}}X}
, defined by:

\displaystyle\tau_{X}(\mathcal{Q})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{0{\mathit{v}}}X\mid\forall C\in\mathcal{Q},C\cap Q\neq\emptyset\}.

(2.2)

2.3
Continuous valuations.

A
continuous valuation
on a space
X
is a map

\nu\colon\mathcal{O}X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
that is
strict

(
\nu(\emptyset)=0
),
modular
(for all
U,V\in\mathcal{O}X
,

\nu(U)+\nu(V)=\nu(U\cup V)+\nu(U\cap V)
) and
Scott-continuous. We say that
\nu
is a
probability
valuation if and only if
\nu(X)=1
, and a

subprobability
valuation if and only if
\nu(X)\leq 1
.
Continuous valuations are an alternative to measures that have become
popular in domain theory
[
13
,
14
]
. The first
results that connected continuous valuations and measures are due to
Saheb-Djahromi
[
18
]
and Lawson

[
17
]
. The most informative results on this matter
are the following. In one direction, every measure on the Borel

\sigma
-algebra of
X
induces a continuous valuation on
X
by
restriction to the open sets, if
X
is hereditarily Lindelöf
(namely, if every directed family of open sets contains a cofinal
monotone sequence). This is an easy observation, and one half of
Adamski’s theorem
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
, which states
that a space is hereditary Lindelöf if and only if every measure on
its Borel
\sigma
-algebra restricts to a continuous valuation on its
open sets. By definition, a
\tau
-smooth
measure is one whose
restriction to the open sets is a continuous valuation: hence all
measures on hereditarily Lindelöf spaces are
\tau
-smooth. In the
other direction, every continuous valuation on a space
X
extends to
a measure on the Borel sets provided that
X
is an LCS-complete space

[
3
, Theorem 1]
, a class of spaces that includes the
locally compact sober spaces, the quasi-Polish spaces and therefore
the locally compact Hausdorff spaces and the Polish spaces.

Let
\mathbf{V}X
denote the space of continuous valuations on a space
X
,
with the following
weak topology
. Its subbasic open sets are

[U>r]\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{\nu\in\mathbf{V}X\mid\nu(U)>r\}
, where

U\in\mathcal{O}X
and
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We define its subspace
\mathbf{V}_{1}X
of
probability valuations and
\mathbf{V}_{\leq 1}X
(subprobability)
similarly. The specialization ordering of each is the

stochastic ordering

\leq
given by
\nu\leq\nu^{\prime}
if and only
if
\nu(U)\leq\nu^{\prime}(U)
for every
U\in\mathcal{O}X
.

For all
h\in{\mathcal{L}}X
and
\nu\in\mathbf{V}X
, the integral

\int h\,d\nu
is best defined by the Choquet formula

\int_{0}^{\infty}\nu(h^{-1}(]t,\infty]))\,dt
, using an indefinite
Riemann integral
[
20
, Section 4]
. Then

\int h\,d\nu
is Scott-continuous in
h
and in
\nu
; it is also
linear in
h
and
\nu
, meaning that it preserves finite sums and
products by scalars from
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. The weak topology on
\mathbf{V}X
(and
its subspaces
\mathbf{V}_{\leq 1}X
,
\mathbf{V}_{1}X
) has an alternative
subbase of open sets of the form

[h>r]\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{\nu\in\mathbf{V}X\mid\int h\,d\nu>r\}
, where

h\in{\mathcal{L}}X
and
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
; this was originally observed by Jung

[
15
, Theorem 3.3]
for
\mathbf{V}_{\leq 1}X
and

\mathbf{V}_{1}X
.

2.4
Previsions.

Previsions
are an elaboration on Walley’s notion of prevision
in economics
[
22
]
. We will borrow most of what we need
from
[
10
]
, see also the errata
[
11
]
. A

prevision
on a space
X
is a Scott-continuous map

F\colon{\mathcal{L}}X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
that is
positively homogeneous

in the sense that
F(ah)=aF(h)
for all
a\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and

h\in{\mathcal{L}}X
. (The notation
ah
makes sense even if
a=0
, with
the convention that
0.\infty=0
.) There is a space
\mathbf{P}X
of
previsions on
X
, whose topology is generated by sets

[h>r]\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{F\mid F(h)>r\}
,
h\in{\mathcal{L}}X
,
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Every continuous valuation
\nu
on
X
gives rise to a prevision

\Lambda\colon h\mapsto\int h\,d\nu
. Such a prevision is

linear
, in the sense that (it is positively homogeneous and)

\Lambda(h+h^{\prime})=\Lambda(h)+\Lambda(h^{\prime})
for all

h,h^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}X
. Let
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{lin}}X
be the subspace of
\mathbf{P}X

of linear previsions. Conversely, every linear prevision

\Lambda\in\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{lin}}X
gives rise to a continuous valuation

U\mapsto\Lambda(\chi_{U})
, where
\chi_{U}
is the characteristic map of
the open set
U
, and the two constructions are inverse of each other.
Additionally, those two constructions define continuous maps between

\mathbf{V}X
and
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{lin}}X

[
20
, Satz 4.16]
. We will
therefore equate continuous valuations with linear previsions.

A prevision
F
is
sublinear
(resp.,
superlinear
) if and
only if
F(h+h^{\prime})\leq F(h)+F(h^{\prime})
(resp.,
\geq
) for all

h,h^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}X
. We will write
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{sub}}X
(resp.
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{super}}X
) for the subspace of
\mathbf{P}X
consisting of
sublinear (resp. superlinear) previsions. This were written as

\mathbf{P}_{{{\mathtt{A}}{\mathtt{P}}}}X
(resp.
\mathbf{P}_{{{\mathtt{D}}{\mathtt{P}}}}X
) in
[
10
]
.

Among the continuous valuations, there are the (sub)probability
valuations. Similarly, we say that a prevision
F
is

subnormalized
(resp.,
normalized
) iff

F({\mathbf{1}}+h)\leq{\mathbf{1}}+F(h)
(resp.,
=
) for every
h\in{\mathcal{L}}X
,
where
{\mathbf{1}}
is the constant function with value
1
. If
F
is
subnormalized (resp. normalized), then
F({\mathbf{1}})\leq 1
(resp.
=
); the converse holds if
F
is linear. The homeomorphism

\mathbf{V}X\cong\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{lin}}X
restricts to homeomorphisms between

\mathbf{V}_{\leq 1}X
(resp.,
\mathbf{V}_{1}X
) and the subspace

\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\leq 1}X
(resp.,
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{lin}}^{1}X
) of subnormalized
(resp., normalized) linear previsions on
X
. We write

\mathbf{P}^{\leq 1}X
,
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{sub}}^{\leq 1}X
,

\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{super}}^{\leq 1}X
,
\mathbf{P}^{1}X
,
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{super}}^{\leq 1}X
,

\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{super}}^{1}X
for the corresponding spaces of (sub)normalized,
plain/sublinear/superlinear previsions. For short, we write

\mathbf{P}^{\circledast}X
,
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{sub}}^{\circledast}X
,
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{super}}^{\circledast}X
,

\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{lin}}^{\circledast}X
where
\circledast
can be nothing, “
\leq 1
”, or
“
1
”.

2.5
Cones.

Spaces such as
{\mathcal{L}}X
,
\mathbf{V}X
or
\mathbf{P}X
have
additional algebraic structure: they are cones, in the following
sense. A
cone
is a commutative monoid
(\mathfrak{C},+,0)
(or just

\mathfrak{C}
for short) with an action
a,x\mapsto a\cdot x
of the
semi-ring
\mathbb{R}_{+}
on
\mathfrak{C}
, that is:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}0\cdot x=0&(ab)\cdot x=a\cdot(b\cdot x)&1\cdot x=x\\
a\cdot 0=0&a\cdot(x+y)=a\cdot x+a\cdot y&(a+b)\cdot x=a\cdot x+b\cdot x\end{array}

(2.3)

for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
x,y\in\mathfrak{C}
. We sometimes write
ax
for

a\cdot x
. In other words, a cone is just like a vector space
except that one cannot take opposites or multiply by negative scalars
in general. Following Keimel
[
16
]
, a

semitopological
cone is a cone
\mathfrak{C}
with a topology that makes
both
+
and
\cdot
separately continuous, where
\mathbb{R}_{+}
has the Scott
topology of
\leq
. Scalar multiplication is then automatically
jointly continuous
[
16
, Corollary 6.9 (c)]
. A

topological
cone is a semitopological cone in which
+
(and
therefore
\cdot
, too) is jointly continuous.
{\mathcal{L}}X
, with the
Scott topology and pointwise addition and scalar multiplication, is
always a semitopological cone; it is a topological cone if
X
is
locally compact, or more generally core-compact.
\mathbf{V}X
, with the
weak topology and pointwise addition and scalar multiplication, is
always a topological cone
[
16
, Example 5.11]
.

We will often write
x+_{\alpha}y
for the convex combination

\alpha\cdot x+(1-\alpha)\cdot y
. A subset
A
of a cone
\mathfrak{C}
is

convex
if and only if it contains every convex combination

x+_{\alpha}y
of points
x,y\in A
, where
\alpha\in[0,1]
.
Every intersection of convex sets is convex, every directed union of
convex sets is convex. A map is
affine
if and only if it
preserves convex combinations. Inverse and direct images of convex
sets under affine maps are convex.

The smallest convex subset containing
A
is its
convex hull

\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}A
. This is also the set of barycenters

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\cdot x_{i}
where
n\geq 1
and
\vec{a}
ranges over

\Delta_{n}
. We write
\Delta_{n}
for the simplex

\{\vec{a}\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=1\}
, and we use the
convention that
\vec{a}
abbreviates
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
.

In a semitopological cone, the closure of a convex set is convex

[
16
, Lemma 4.10]
. It follows that the
closed
convex hull

\mathop{\overline{\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}}}A
of any subset
A
is equal to the closure
cl(\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}A)
of the convex hull of
A
.

Given a semitopological cone
\mathfrak{C}
, a function
F\colon\mathfrak{C}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

is
positively homogeneous
if and only if
F(a\cdot x)=aF(x)
for all
a\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
x\in\mathfrak{C}
. Hence previsions on
X
are
the same thing as positively homogeneous, lower semicontinuous
functions from
{\mathcal{L}}X
to
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

F
is
superlinear
(resp.
sublinear
) if and only if it
is positively homogeneous and satisfies
F(x+x^{\prime})\geq F(x)+F(x^{\prime})

(resp.
\leq
) for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{C}
.

The
upper Minkowski functional
of a subset
A
of a cone
\mathfrak{C}
,
which Keimel writes as
F^{A}
, and which we prefer to write as
M^{A}
,
maps every
x\in C
to
\sup\{r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\mid x\in r\cdot A\}
,
where the sup is taken in
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, the sup of the empty set is
0
,
and
r\cdot A\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\text{def}}}}\{r\cdot y\mid y\in A\}
(see

[
16
, Section 7]
). In a semitopological cone
\mathfrak{C}
,
there is an order isomorphism between the poset
\mathcal{O}^{*}\mathfrak{C}
of proper
open subsets of
\mathfrak{C}
, ordered by inclusion, and the poset
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{hom}}\mathfrak{C}

of positively homogeneous, lower semicontinuous maps from
\mathfrak{C}
to

\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. In one direction, every positively homogeneous, lower
semicontinuous map
F\colon\mathfrak{C}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
defines a proper open
subset
F^{-1}(]1,\infty])
of
\mathfrak{C}
. The inverse operation sends
every proper open subset
U
of
\mathfrak{C}
to
M^{U}
. Additionally,
M^{U}
is
superlinear if and only if
U
is convex (and proper), and sublinear
if and only if
\mathfrak{C}\smallsetminus U
is convex (and non-empty).

Keimel’s
sandwich theorem

[
16
, Theorem 8.2]

states that in a semitopological cone
C
, given a lower
semicontinuous superlinear map
q\colon C\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and a sublinear
map
p\colon C\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that

, there is a lower semicontinuous linear map

5

It will be profitable to revisit the de Brecht-Kawai construction of

polarities, a practical way of forming Galois connections between

2
]
. A

between posets

and

and

if and only if

.
Then

and

between
\mathrm{Im}\,{{{}^{\perp}\_}}
and
\mathrm{Im}\,{\_^{\perp}}

by inclusion and the other one by reverse inclusion. Additionally,

\mathrm{Im}\,{{{}^{\perp}\_}}
is exactly the collection of elements

such
that

, and
\mathrm{Im}\,{\_^{\perp}}
is the

A polarity, or an orthogonality relation, is any binary relation

between elements of two sets
{\mathtt{Q}}

and
{\mathtt{H}}

and

The de Brecht-Kawai construction relating

Lemma 6.11

4.

\Lambda
