Difference of Convex Programming in the Wasserstein Space with Applications to MMD Optimization
Clément Bonet, Pierre-Cyril Aubin-Frankowski, Youssef Mroueh

Abstract
Optimizing functionals over the space of probability measures is now ubiquitous in machine learning. A widely used approach is to perform the optimization directly over the Wasserstein space, but many objective functionals of practical interest are non-convex along Wasserstein geodesics, making the analysis of standard first-order methods challenging. In this work, we study a class of objectives over the Wasserstein space that admit a difference-of-convex (DC) decomposition and we lift the classical convex-concave procedure (CCCP) to this setting. Under smoothness and strong convexity assumptions on the convex components of the decomposition, we prove almost stationarity along the iterates of the resulting algorithm. Our main focus is on the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) and the Energy Distance (ED) functionals, for which we develop explicit Wasserstein DC decompositions, and establish local convergence of the scheme under mild assumptions. Empirically, we show that well-chosen DC decompositions yield faster and more stable convergence than Wasserstein gradient descent on these MMD objectives.

Contributions.

In this work, we focus on developing an optimization scheme on the Wasserstein space tailored for the non-convex objectives that can be written as a difference of convex objectives. To do so, we introduce the
Wasserstein Convex-Concave Procedure
(WCCCP), and analyze its theoretical convergence, proving almost stationary along its iterates. The closest work to ours is

Notation.

We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the space of probability distributions with second finite moments, and by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
its restriction to absolutely continuous measures with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Given
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we denote by
\mathrm{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\ \int\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)
the squared-Wasserstein distance, where
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
is the set of couplings between
\mu
and
\nu
, and
\Pi_{o}(\mu,\nu)
is its subset of optimal couplings. The metric space
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathrm{W}_{2})
is called the Wasserstein space. For any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we denote by
L^{2}(\mu)
the Hilbert space of functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\int\|f(x)\|_{2}^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty
equipped with the norm
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}=\int\|f(x)\|_{2}^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)
and with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}
. Given
\mathrm{T}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the pushforward measure of
\mu
.

Background

We begin by recalling a few facts on optimization on the Wasserstein space. More precisely, we recall the notion of Wasserstein gradient, of total convexity on the Wasserstein space, and some classical optimization schemes on
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathrm{W}_{2})
. For more details about Wasserstein gradient flows, we refer to
e.g.

(
4
;
48
)
. Then, we provide a brief introduction to the convex-concave procedure on Euclidean spaces.

Wasserstein gradient.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
be a functional. It admits a Wasserstein gradient
\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu)\in L^{2}(\mu)
at
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if for all
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\gamma\in\Pi_{o}(\mu,\nu)
, the following first order Taylor expansion is satisfied
(
19
;
48
)

\mathcal{F}(\nu)=\mathcal{F}(\mu)+\int\langle\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu)(x),y-x\rangle\ \mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)+o\big(\mathrm{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)\big).

(1)

When it exists, the Wasserstein gradient may not be unique in
L^{2}(\mu)
in general. Nonetheless, there is only one gradient living in the tangent space
T_{\mu}\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L^{2}(\mu)
which is a Hilbert space. Hence, by Hilbert’s decomposition theorem, any gradient can be decomposed as a part in
T_{\mu}\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and an orthogonal part
\xi(\mu)
which satisfies
\int\langle\xi(\mu),y-x\rangle\ \mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)=0
, see
(
48
, Proposition 2.11)
. Thus, without loss of generality, we always work with the unique
\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu)\in T_{\mu}\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for a Wasserstein differentiable functional, using the shorthand W-differentiable for such functionals.

Classical functionals from
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathbb{R}
include potential energies
\mathcal{V}(\mu)=\int\mathrm{V}\mathrm{d}\mu
and interaction energies
\mathcal{W}(\mu)=\frac{1}{2}\iint\mathrm{W}(x-y)\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)
for
\mathrm{V},\mathrm{W}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, with
\mathrm{W}
symmetric. They are both differentiable provided
\mathrm{V}
and
\mathrm{W}
are differentiable and smooth enough
(
48
)
, and their Wasserstein gradients read respectively as
\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{V}(\mu)=\nabla\mathrm{V}
and as the convolution
\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{W}(\mu)=\nabla\mathrm{W}*\mu
. In this work, we will mostly focus on functionals obtained as a sum of interaction and potential energies, as they include in particular the Maximum Mean Discrepancy
(
6
)
.

Convexity in the Wasserstein space.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\mathrm{T},\mathrm{S}\in L^{2}(\mu)
. Given
\phi:L^{2}(\mu)\to\mathbb{R}
convex and Gateaux differentiable, the Bregman divergence on
L^{2}(\mu)
between
\mathrm{T}
,
\mathrm{S}
is defined as
(
34
)

\mathrm{D}_{\phi}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{S})=\phi(\mathrm{T})-\phi(\mathrm{S})-\langle\nabla\phi(\mathrm{S}),\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{S}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

(2)

Given
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
W-differentiable, we can define the Bregman divergence in
L^{2}(\mu)
associated to the lifted functional
\mathrm{T}\mapsto\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu)
as

\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mu}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{S})=\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu)-\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{S}_{\#}\mu)-\langle\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{S}_{\#}\mu)\circ\mathrm{S},\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{S}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)},

(3)

using the chain rule for the gradient of
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\mu}:\mathrm{S}\mapsto\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{S}_{\#}\mu)
which implies
\nabla\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\mu}(\mathrm{S})=\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{S}_{\#}\mu)\circ\mathrm{S}
, see
(
18
, Proposition 1)
. Note that for
\mathcal{F}(\mu)=\int\tfrac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\mu
, this reduces to
\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mu}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{S})=\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{S}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}
. Let
\alpha\geq 0
, we say that
\mathcal{F}
is
\alpha
-totally convex
(
25
;
76
;
62
)
if for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\mathrm{T},\mathrm{S}\in L^{2}(\mu)
,
\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mu}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{S})\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}\|\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{S}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}
. Equivalently, it satisfies

\forall t\in[0,1],\ \mathcal{F}\big(\big((1-t)\mathrm{T}+t\mathrm{S}\big)_{\#}\mu\big)\leq(1-t)\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu)+t\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{S}_{\#}\mu)-\alpha\frac{t(1-t)}{2}\|\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{S}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}.

(4)

If this result holds only for
\mathrm{S}=\mathrm{Id}
and for
\mathrm{T}
the gradient of a convex function, this corresponds to the less restrictive notion of strong convexity along geodesics
(
4
)
.

Wasserstein Gradient Descent.

Optimization on
L^{2}(\mu)
and on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are very much intertwined in practice, see
e.g.

(
18
;
31
)
. For instance, the Wasserstein Gradient Descent (WGD) over a W-differentiable functional
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
which is defined for all
k\geq 0
,
\tau>0
, as
\mu_{k+1}=\big(\mathrm{Id}-\tau\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu_{k})\big)_{\#}\mu_{k}
, can be written at each iteration in two steps: first solving an optimization problem on
L^{2}(\mu_{k})
to get a map
\mathrm{T}_{k+1}\in L^{2}(\mu_{k})
, then pushing forward
\mu_{k}
by
\mathrm{T}_{k+1}
,
i.e.

\begin{cases}\mathrm{T}_{k+1}=\argmin_{\mathrm{T}\in L^{2}(\mu_{k})}\ \tfrac{1}{2\tau}\|\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{Id}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{k})}^{2}+\langle\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu_{k}),\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{Id}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{k})}\\
\mu_{k+1}=(\mathrm{T}_{k+1})_{\#}\mu_{k}.\end{cases}

(5)

It can be shown to converge if
\mathcal{F}
is smooth along
t\mapsto\big((1-t)\mathrm{Id}+t\mathrm{T}_{k+1})_{\#}\mu_{k}
and convex along geodesics. Other first-order algorithms have been lifted from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For instance, replacing the squared
L^{2}
distance in (
5
) by a Bregman divergence (
2
) allows to lift the Mirror descent algorithm
(
11
;
53
)
to
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(
18
)

The Convex-Concave Procedure.

A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is DC if it can be written as the difference of two convex functions,
i.e.
if there exists
f^{+},f^{-}
two convex functions such that
f=f^{+}-f^{-}
. Every
C^{1}
function with Lipschitz gradient is DC
(
42
, Section II)
. To minimize such functions, a popular algorithm is the Convex-Concave Procedure (CCCP)
(
91
)
. This algorithm amounts to linearizing the concave part around the current iterate
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which by convexity gives the lower bound,

\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ f^{-}(x)\geq f^{-}(x_{k})+\langle\nabla f^{-}(x_{k}),x-x_{k}\rangle,

(6)

which entails an upper bound on
f=f^{+}-f^{-}
, and the majorization-minimization

x_{k+1}=\argmin_{x}\ f^{+}(x)-f^{-}(x_{k})-\langle\nabla f^{-}(x_{k}),x-x_{k}\rangle,\,\forall k\geq 0.

(7)

When both
f^{+}
and
f^{-}
are differentiable, the iterates satisfy
\nabla f^{+}(x_{k+1})=\nabla f^{-}(x_{k})
by the first order conditions. This algorithm belongs to the more general family of Difference-of-Convex algorithms (DCA)
(
66
;
49
)
, and is related to different optimization algorithms including Frank-Wolfe
(
92
)
, the Mirror and Bregman proximal descent
(
32
)
or the Proximal gradient algorithm
(
67
)
.

The first convergence analysis of CCCP focused on obtaining asymptotic convergence, showing that it converges towards a stationary point under some assumptions
(
77
;
47
)
. Then, several works such as
(
92
;
1
;
32
)
derived non-asymptotic convergence rates. In particular, the algorithm was shown to converge in
O(1/k)
in terms of the squared norm of the gradient. More recently,
(
61
)
provided an analysis of CCCP under a generalized convexity perspective, though in finite dimensions. Linear rates were also derived under Polyak-Łojaziewicz inequalities adapted to DC functions
(
1
;
32
;
61
;
60
)
.

Wasserstein CCCP

In this section, we first introduce the Wasserstein Convex-Concave Procedure (WCCCP) to minimize difference-of-convex functions on the Wasserstein space as well as our assumptions. Then we provide a theoretical analysis in several settings, including the convex and non-convex ones. Finally, we discuss how we can implement these schemes in practice. All the proofs are deferred to Appendix
F
.

3.1
Convex-Concave Procedure in the Wasserstein Space

We focus on the problem of minimizing
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
where
\mathcal{F}
can be decomposed as

\mathcal{F}(\mu)=\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mu)-\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu),\quad\forall\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(8)

with
\mathcal{F}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{-}:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
both totally convex. Moreover, we assume that
\mathcal{F}^{-}
is W-differentiable, and will assume
\mathcal{F}^{+}
W-differentiable on a case-by-case basis. In contrast to
(
55
)
, we do not restrict
\mathcal{F}^{-}
to be a potential energy.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
\mathcal{F}^{-}
is totally convex, for any
\mathrm{T}\in L^{2}(\mu)
,

\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}^{-}}^{\mu}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{Id})\geq 0\iff\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu)\geq\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu)+\langle\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu),\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{Id}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

(9)

Hence, we have the following upper bound on
\mathcal{F}
:

\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu)=\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu)-\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu)\leq\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu)-\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu)-\langle\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu),\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{Id}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

(10)

We define the Wasserstein Convex-Concave Procedure (WCCCP) as the majorization-minimization based on the upper bound in (
10
) at each iteration
k\geq 0
,
i.e.
given
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}_{k+1}=\argmin_{\mathrm{T}\in L^{2}(\mu_{k})}\ \mathrm{J}(\mathrm{T})\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu_{k})-\langle\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu_{k}),\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{Id}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{k})}

(11)

\displaystyle\mu_{k+1}=(\mathrm{T}_{k+1})_{\#}\mu_{k}.

This extends CCCP
(
91
)
to the Wasserstein space, CCCP being recovered when
\mathcal{F}^{+}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-}
are potential energies. Restricting the optimization to measures of the form
\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu_{k}
for
\mathrm{T}\in L^{2}(\mu_{k})
is without loss of generality in two important cases:
i)
as done in our experiments, for empirical target and initial distributions with the same number of particles;
ii)
if
\mu_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, as by Brenier’s theorem
(
22
)
, there always exists an OT map between
\mu_{k}
and any
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. While for greater generality we could instead optimize over couplings with first marginal
\mu_{k}
, the subproblems on
L^{2}(\mu_{k})
are more tractable and reflect practical implementations.

We assume existence and uniqueness in (
11
) for simplicity of exposition. Sufficient conditions are
\mathrm{T}\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu_{k})
strictly convex, coercive and lower semicontinuous over
L^{2}(\mu_{k})
, for all
k
. We now study the theoretical convergence of the WCCCP in several settings.

Theoretical Analysis in the Non-Convex Case

We first observe that in general, similarly to the analysis of

)

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mu_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some

. (

\mathrm{T}_{k+1}=\argmin_{\mathrm{T}\in L^{2}(\mu_{k})}\ \mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}^{-}}^{\mu_{k}}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{Id})+\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu_{k}),

(12)

and, if

is W-differentiable, to (Mirror Descent)

\mathrm{T}_{k+1}=\argmin_{\mathrm{T}\in L^{2}(\mu_{k})}\ \mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}^{+}}^{\mu_{k}}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{Id})+\langle\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu_{k}),\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{Id}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{k})}.

These equivalences allow us to provide a convergence rate in the case where

satisfies convexity assumptions relative to

.

We have for all

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{k}_{\mathcal{F}^{+}}=\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mu_{k})-\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mu_{k+1})-\langle\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu_{k}),\mathrm{Id}-\mathrm{T}_{k+1}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{k})}.

(16)

Assume that
\mathcal{F}^{+}
is W-differentiable. Then, for all

,
\mathcal{D}^{k}_{\mathcal{F}^{+}}=\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}^{+}}^{\mu_{k}}(\mathrm{Id},\mathrm{T}_{k+1})

The condition

.

Assume that

Proposition 4
.

Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is bounded from below. Then, for all
K\geq 1
,

0\leq\min_{k\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}}\mathcal{D}^{k}_{\mathcal{F}^{+}}\leq\frac{\mathcal{F}(\mu_{0})-\inf\mathcal{F}}{K}.

(19)

Assuming that either
\mathcal{F}^{+}
or
\mathcal{F}^{-}
is strongly convex along iterates, we can also obtain a sublinear convergence result for the squared distance between iterates and the norm of the W-gradient, which guarantees that at least one iterate of WCCCP is almost a stationary point.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\alpha^{+},\alpha^{-}\geq 0
such that
\alpha^{+}+\alpha^{-}>0
. Assume
\mathcal{F}^{+}
to be W-differentiable and that
\mathcal{F}^{+}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-}
are respectively
\alpha^{+}
and
\alpha^{-}
totally-convex. Then for all
K\geq 1
,

\min_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\ \mathrm{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu_{k},\mu_{k+1})\leq\min_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\ \|\mathrm{T}_{k+1}-\mathrm{Id}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{k})}^{2}\leq\frac{2}{\alpha^{+}+\alpha^{-}}\frac{\big(\mathcal{F}(\mu_{0})-\mathcal{F}(\mu_{K})\big)}{K}.

(20)

Furthermore, if
\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{+}
satisfies the Lipschitz condition:

\|\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mu_{k+1})\circ\mathrm{T}_{k+1}-\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mu_{k})\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{k})}\leq L\|\mathrm{T}_{k+1}-\mathrm{Id}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{k})}\,\forall k\geq 0,

(21)

then

\min_{0\leq k\leq K-1}\ \|\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu_{k})\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{k})}^{2}\leq\frac{2L^{2}}{\alpha^{+}+\alpha^{-}}\frac{\big(\mathcal{F}(\mu_{0})-\mathcal{F}(\mu_{K})\big)}{K}.

(22)

3.3
Computing WCCCP

To solve the WCCCP scheme (
11
), we need to minimize at each iteration
\mathrm{J}
on
L^{2}(\mu_{k})
. First, we observe that if
\mathcal{F}^{+}
is convex along curves of the form
t\mapsto\big((1-t)\mathrm{T}+t\mathrm{S}\big)_{\#}\mu_{k}
for any
\mathrm{S},\mathrm{T}\in L^{2}(\mu_{k})
, then
\mathrm{J}
is convex on
L^{2}(\mu_{k})
as
\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}^{+}}^{\mu_{k}}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{S})\geq 0
, see
Section

2
.

If
\mathcal{F}^{+}
is W-differentiable, then
\mathrm{T}\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu_{k})
is Fréchet differentiable and taking the first order conditions in (
11
), we get the equivalent update (
14
).
If
\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mu)=\int\mathrm{V}\mathrm{d}\mu
with
\mathrm{V}
strictly convex, then
\mathrm{T}_{k+1}=\nabla\mathrm{V}^{*}\circ\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu_{k})
with
\mathrm{V}^{*}(x)=\sup_{y}\langle x,y\rangle-\mathrm{V}(y)
the Legendre transform of
\mathrm{V}
. For more general functionals, the problem is implicit and, to the best of our knowledge, cannot be found in closed-form.

Nonetheless, since
\mathrm{J}
is convex on
L^{2}(\mu_{k})
, under the Lipschitz condition (
21
) (corresponding to
L
-smoothness of
\mathrm{T}\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu_{k})
for all
k\geq 0
), we can perform a gradient descent on
L^{2}(\mu_{k})
, which is of the form, for
0<\tau\leq 1/L
and
\tilde{\mathrm{T}}_{k}^{0}=\mathrm{Id}
,

\forall\ell\geq 0,\ \tilde{\mathrm{T}}_{k}^{\ell+1}=\tilde{\mathrm{T}}_{k}^{\ell}-\tau\big(\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{+}\big((\tilde{\mathrm{T}}_{k}^{\ell})_{\#}\mu_{k}\big)\circ\tilde{\mathrm{T}}_{k}^{\ell}-\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu_{k})\big).

(23)

Then, we approximate the solution of (
11
) by
\tilde{\mathrm{T}}_{k+1}=\tilde{\mathrm{T}}^{L}_{k}
and
\mu_{k+1}=(\tilde{\mathrm{T}}_{k+1})_{\#}\mu_{k}
. If
\mu_{k}^{\ell}=(\tilde{\mathrm{T}}_{k}^{\ell})_{\#}\mu_{k}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{x_{i}^{k,\ell}}
, then the update translates to the particles as

\forall k\geq 0,\ell\geq 0,\ x_{i}^{k,\ell+1}=x_{i}^{k,\ell}-\tau\big(\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mu_{k}^{\ell})(x_{i}^{k,\ell})-\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu_{k})(x_{i}^{k,0})\big).

(24)

In practice, one can follow this procedure for any
\tau
small enough, and we run the algorithm for
0<k\leq K
outer steps (
11
), and, at each
k
,
0<\ell\leq M
inner steps of (
24
) to minimize
\mathrm{J}
. Alternatively one could rely on root-finding algorithms instead, such as Newton’s method
(
95
)
.

3.4
Connection with Wasserstein Proximal Gradient Algorithm

55
proposed to solve the DC problem on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by using a Wasserstein Proximal Gradient Descent scheme
(
69
)
, alternating between a gradient descent step on
-\mathcal{F}^{-}
and a JKO step on
\mathcal{F}^{+}
,
i.e.
using

\begin{cases}\nu_{k+1}=\big(\mathrm{Id}+\tau\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu_{k})\big)_{\#}\mu_{k}\\
\mu_{k+1}=\argmin_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\ \frac{1}{2\tau}\mathrm{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu_{k+1})+\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mu).\end{cases}

(25)

In
Proposition

6
, we show that this is equivalent to minimizing an upper bound similar to that of WCCCP (
10
), with an additional quadratic cost.

Proposition 6
.

Assume
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and that
\mu_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
implies
\nu_{k+1}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then (
25
) is equivalent to

\begin{cases}\tilde{\mathrm{T}}_{k+1}=\underset{\mathrm{T}\in L^{2}(\mu_{k})}{\argmin}\ \mathcal{F}^{+}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu_{k})-\langle\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu_{k}),\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{Id}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{k})}+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{Id}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{k})}^{2}\\
\mu_{k+1}=(\tilde{\mathrm{T}}_{k+1})_{\#}\mu_{k}.\end{cases}

(26)

Hence, this scheme can be seen as a lifting of CCCP algorithms with additional quadratic terms
(
75
)
which have been in particular used to deal with the stochastic setting
(
58
;
90
;
26
)
. Note that it would also be possible to use instead a Bregman divergence on
L^{2}(\mu_{k})
, and that it would be equivalent with a Bregman proximal gradient scheme, see
e.g.

(
18
, Appendix F)
.
Furthermore the WCCCP algorithm can be seen as minimizing in (
12
) a regularization
\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu_{k})+\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}^{-}}^{\mu_{k}}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{Id})
of
\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu_{k})
, and (
26
) hence corresponds to replacing
\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu_{k})
by
\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu_{k})+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{Id}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{k})}^{2}
in our analysis.

55
focused on functionals whose concave part is a potential energy,
i.e.

\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu)=\int\mathrm{V}^{-}\mathrm{d}\mu
for
\mathrm{V}^{-}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
a convex function. This includes a large class of functionals, and in particular the KL divergence with a possibly non log-concave target if the potential of the target admits a DC decomposition
\mathrm{V}=\mathrm{V}^{+}-\mathrm{V}^{-}
. Our theory in
Section

3
would also cover these objectives, up to replacing the gradient by the unique subgradient of the negative entropy in the tangent space, as discussed in
(
4
, Chapter 10)
, and verifying that the measure stays regular enough at each iteration, which can be enforced through a regularization
(
88
)
to avoid failures
(
87
)
. In the next section, we focus instead on the Maximum Mean Discrepancy, which can be decomposed as a DC function, whose concave part is a sum of an interaction and of a potential energies.

4
DC Decomposition for the Maximum Mean Discrepancy

We now focus on finding a DC decomposition to the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD)
(
38
)
. Given
k:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
a kernel, the squared MMD is defined as

\forall\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \mathrm{MMD}_{k}^{2}(\mu,\nu)=\iint k(x,y)\ \mathrm{d}(\mu-\nu)(x)\mathrm{d}(\mu-\nu)(y).

(27)

It is well known that the squared MMD distance can be decomposed as a sum of an interaction energy and a potential energy
(
6
)
,
i.e.

\mathcal{F}(\mu)=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{MMD}_{k}^{2}(\mu,\nu)=\frac{1}{2}\iint k(x,y)\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)+\int\mathrm{V}\ \mathrm{d}\mu+c(\nu),

(28)

with
\mathrm{V}(x)=-\int k(x,y)\ \mathrm{d}\nu(y)
and
c(\nu)=\frac{1}{2}\iint k(x,y)\ \mathrm{d}\nu(x)\mathrm{d}\nu(y)
. The first term is an interaction term and the second a potential. This objective is in general not (geodesically) convex, but only semi-convex
(
6
, Proposition 5)
,
i.e.

\lambda
-totally convex with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, the performance of WGD to minimize it depends heavily on the kernel as observed in
e.g.

(
6
;
44
;
41
)
.

For an
L
-smooth kernel
k
,
i.e.
satisfying
\|\nabla k(x,y)-\nabla k(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\|_{2}^{2}\leq L\big(\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}+\|y-y^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\big)
for all
x,x^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
55
proposed to use, for
\alpha\geq L
,

\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mu)=\alpha\int\|x\|_{2}^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)+\frac{1}{2}\iint k(x,y)\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)+c(\nu),\,\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu)=\int\big(\alpha\|x\|_{2}^{2}-\mathrm{V}(x)\big)\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)

(29)

as a DC decomposition of (
28
). Instead, we propose to obtain a Difference-of-Convex function for this objective by decomposing the kernel itself. For this, we focus on translation-invariant kernels.

MMD with translation-invariant kernel.

A large class of useful kernels are the translation-invariant one,
i.e.
those of the form
k(x,y)=\psi(x-y)
for some symmetric function
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

(
74
;
57
)
. This class includes, among others, the Riesz (a.k.a negative distance) kernel with
\psi(z)=-\|z\|_{2}
, the Gaussian kernel with
\psi(z)=e^{-\|z\|_{2}^{2}/(2h)}
or the inverse multiquadric kernel with
\psi(z)=(c^{2}+\|z\|_{2}^{2})^{-\alpha}
and
\alpha>1

(
57
)
. Assuming that
\psi
admits a DC decomposition
\psi=\psi^{+}-\psi^{-}
, we obtain the following DC decomposition of the MMD.

Proposition 7
.

Let
k
be a translation-invariant kernel of the form
k(x,y)=\psi(x-y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with a
\psi
admitting a DC decomposition
\psi=\psi^{+}-\psi^{-}
,
\psi^{+},\psi^{-}
being
\alpha^{+},\alpha^{-}\geq 0
convex. Then (
28
) admits the DC decomposition
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}^{+}-\mathcal{F}^{-}
where for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mu)=\tfrac{1}{2}\iint\psi^{+}(x-y)\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)+\int\mathrm{V}^{-}\mathrm{d}\mu+c(\nu),&\mathrm{V}^{-}(\cdot)=\int\psi^{-}(\cdot-y)\ \mathrm{d}\nu(y),\\
\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu)=\tfrac{1}{2}\iint\psi^{-}(x-y)\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)+\int\mathrm{V}^{+}\mathrm{d}\mu,&\mathrm{V}^{+}(\cdot)=\int\psi^{+}(\cdot-y)\ \mathrm{d}\nu(y),\end{array}\right.

(30)

and
\mathcal{F}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{-}
are respectively
\alpha^{-}
and
\alpha^{+}
totally convex. Note that
\psi^{+},\psi^{-}
can always be chosen symmetric as, by symmetry of
\psi
,
2\psi(x)=\psi(x)+\psi(-x)=\psi^{+}(x)+\psi^{+}(-x)-\psi^{-}(x)-\psi^{-}(-x)
. They are also locally Lipschitz since they are convex and have full domain.

We now focus on the subclass of radial kernels,
i.e.
those for which there exists
q:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\psi(z)=q(\|z\|_{2}^{2})
. For such kernels, DC decompositions can be obtained via a Jordan decomposition

\begin{cases}q_{+}(x)=q(0)+(q^{\prime}(0)+A)x+\int_{0}^{x}(x-t)\max\big(0,q^{\prime\prime}(t)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t,\,\\
q_{-}(x)=Ax-\int_{0}^{x}(x-t)\min\big(0,q^{\prime\prime}(t)\big)\,\mathrm{d}t,\end{cases}

(31)

whenever these integrals of the second derivative are computable, and taking
A\geq\max(0,-q^{\prime}(0))
to ensure that
q_{+}
and
q_{-}
are nondecreasing (hence
\psi_{+}
and
\psi_{-}
are convex). Alternatively if
q
is analytic,
i.e.

q(x)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}a_{i}x^{i}
, then we can set
I^{+}\coloneqq\{i\,|\,a_{i}\geq 0\}
and
I^{-}\coloneqq\{i\,|\,a_{i}\leq 0\}
,
q_{+}(x)=\sum_{i\in I^{+}}a_{i}x^{i}
,
q_{-}(x)=\sum_{i\in I^{-}}a_{i}x^{i}
. These two choices are explored for the Gaussian kernel (Gauss Jordan vs cosh/sinh) in
Section

5
, and their forms are detailed in
Table

1
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
or a compact convex subset and
S_{*}=\sup_{x,y\in\Omega}\ \|x-y\|_{2}
. Define for
\psi(z)=q(\|z\|_{2}^{2})
,

\underline{\lambda}[q]\coloneqq\inf_{0\leq s\leq S_{*}}\min\bigl\{2q^{\prime}(s),\,2q^{\prime}(s)+4sq^{\prime\prime}(s)\bigr\},\quad\overline{\Lambda}[q]\coloneqq\sup_{0\leq s\leq S_{*}}\ \max\bigl\{2q^{\prime}(s),2q^{\prime}(s)+4sq^{\prime\prime}(s)\bigr\}.

Having
\underline{\lambda}[q_{\pm}]\geq 0
and finite
\overline{\Lambda}[q_{\pm}]

\mathcal{F}^{\pm}
, allows for a sufficient condition on a DC decomposition of
q
and therefore of the MMD. Owing to this decomposition we can apply
Proposition

7
and all the convergence results of
Section

3

Proposition 8
.

Let
q\in C^{2}([0,S_{*}])
. Assume there exists
q_{\pm}\in C^{2}([0,S_{*}])

\alpha^{\pm}=\underline{\lambda}[q_{\pm}]

\underline{\lambda}[q_{+}]+\underline{\lambda}[q_{-}]>0

1.

for

i.e.

2.

for

\Omega

6
Conclusion

In this work, we lifted the convex-concave procedure to the Wasserstein space, and analyzed its convergence in the convex and non-convex settings. Then, we used it to minimize the Maximum Mean Discrepancy, and showed improved performance over the Wasserstein Gradient Descent for the negative distance and Gaussian kernel. Nonetheless, the improved convergence of WCCCP strongly depends on the choice of the DC decomposition of the kernel. Future work will therefore focus on better understanding the impact of different DC decompositions on the performance, with the goal of designing more effective, automatic, and adaptive decomposition strategies
(
2
)
. On the theoretical side, taking into account the inexact solvers used for the inner optimization problems would also be a natural avenue for future work.

Appendix A
Limitations

Our empirical findings show that the improved convergence of WCCCP on MMD strongly depends on the choice of the DC decomposition of the kernel
k
. Our current strategy is restricted to a few simple decomposition of the kernels, without taking into account the geometry of the optimization problem. Hence, future work could focus on developing automatic and adaptative ways to find DC decompositions of the kernels better suited to the problem of minimizing the MMD,
e.g.
based on algebraic decompositions and polynomial DC decompositions minimizing a suitable objective
[
16
,
2
,
59
]
.

This work also focused on DC decompositions of MMD, which can be written as a sum of simple functionals, which are potential and interaction energies. For MMD with the ubiquitous radial kernels, we were thus able to derive DC decompositions based on decompositions of functions on
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. Future work could focus on the problem of deriving DC decomposition of more complex functionals.

On the theoretical part, as highlighted in
Section

1
, there are versions of Polyak-Łojaziewicz (PL) inequalities for both the Wasserstein space
[
14
,
50
]
and DC functions
[
1
,
32
,
61
,
60
]
. So far our framework does not include them despite preliminary research. We discuss reasons for this in
Appendix

C
.

We also did not take into account that we actually solve each inner scheme approximately, which leads to a gap between theory and practice.

Appendix B
Theoretical Analysis of WCCCP in the Convex Case

We focus in this section on the case where
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}^{+}-\mathcal{F}^{-}
is also convex along a curve of interest which we will detail. For this, we will leverage
Proposition

1
, where we showed that (
11
) is equivalent to both a mirror descent and a Bregman proximal descent in the Wasserstein space. Consequently, on the one hand, we will inherit the results from
[
18
]
for mirror descent, and on the other hand we will derive novel convergence results for Bregman proximal descent.

Relative convexity and smoothness.

First, we need to introduce the notions of relative convexity and smoothness. Let
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
. Following
[
18
]
, we say that
\mathcal{F}
is
\alpha
-convex relative to
\mathcal{G}:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
along
t\mapsto\big((1-t)\mathrm{T}+t\mathrm{S}\big)_{\#}\mu
if
\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mu}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{S})\geq\alpha\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mu}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{S})
. Equivalently, we have that
\mathcal{F}-\alpha\mathcal{G}
is convex along this curve, and

\forall t\in[0,1],\ \mathcal{F}(\mu_{t})\leq(1-t)\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu)+t\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{S}_{\#}\mu)-\alpha t(1-t)\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mu}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{S}).

(33)

Likewise,
\mathcal{F}
is
\beta
-smooth relative to
\mathcal{G}
along this curve if
\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\mu}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{S})\leq\beta\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mu}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{S})
. These notions enable lifting the notion of relative convexity and smoothness
[
53
,
10
]
to
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for differentiable functionals.

If the convexity holds for all
\mathrm{T},\mathrm{S}\in L^{2}(\mu)
,
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{G}(\mu)=\int\tfrac{1}{2}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu
, then we say that
\mathcal{F}
is
\alpha
-totally convex
[
25
,
76
,
62
]
. Following
[
4
]
, if convexity only holds for
\mathrm{T}=\mathrm{Id}
and
\mathrm{S}
gradient of convex functions, it coincides with strong convexity along geodesics
[
4
]
. Convexity along generalized geodesics corresponds to both
\mathrm{T}
and
\mathrm{S}
being the gradients of convex functions. Examples of totally convex functionals include potential and interaction energies, provided
\mathrm{V},\mathrm{W}
are convex, lower semi-continuous and have a negative part with quadratic growth
[
4
, Section 9.3]
. Note also that all three notions of convexity are equivalent for continuous functionals (for
d\geq 2
)
[
25
,
62
]
.

Bregman Wasserstein distance.

Similarly to
[
18
]
, let us introduce the Bregman Optimal Transport problem
\mathrm{W}_{\phi}
associated with a Wasserstein differentiable functional
\phi:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
, defined for
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as

\mathrm{W}_{\phi}(\nu,\mu)\coloneqq\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\nu,\mu)}\ \phi(\nu)-\phi(\mu)-\int\langle\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\phi(\mu)(y),x-y\rangle\ \mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y).

(34)

By
[
18
, Proposition 15]
, if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then there exists
\mathrm{T}\in L^{2}(\mu)
such that
\mathrm{W}_{\phi}(\nu,\mu)=\mathrm{D}_{\phi}^{\mu}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{Id})
,
i.e.
this problem admits an optimal transport map.

Convergence results.

We now provide a first convergence result for
\mathcal{F}
smooth and strongly convex relative to
\mathcal{F}^{+}
, relying on the mirror descent formulation (
13
) and the results of
[
18
]
. More precisely,
\mathcal{F}
needs to be smooth along iterates and convex along what would be the analog of geodesics on the space
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathrm{W}_{\mathcal{F}^{+}})

.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
be a functional admitting a DC decomposition
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}^{+}-\mathcal{F}^{-}
with both
\mathcal{F}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{-}
Wasserstein differentiable on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and

,

iterates of (
11
). Assume that for all
k\geq 0

\mu_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(35)

Moreover, if

the minimizer of

Proof.

The assumptions imply that we can use
[

for the mirror descent formulation (

∎

Appendix C
Enquiry on Polyak-Łojaziewicz Inequality for DC Functionals

An analog of the DC PL inequality of
[
61
, Definition 6.1]
, writing it only using the Bregman divergences of
\mathcal{F}^{+}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-}
, reads as follow

Definition C.1
.

We say that
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}^{+}-\mathcal{F}^{-}
satisfies the Wasserstein DC PL inequality analogue to
[
61
, Definition 6.1]
, if there exists
\eta_{1}\geq 0
,
\eta_{2}\geq 0
such that
\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}>0
and for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\eta_{1}\big(\mathcal{F}(\mu)-\inf\mathcal{F}\big)+\eta_{2}\big(\mathcal{F}(\bar{\mathrm{T}}_{\#}\mu)-\inf\mathcal{F}\big)\leq\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}^{-}}^{\mu}(\bar{\mathrm{T}},\mathrm{Id})+\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}^{+}}^{\mu}(\mathrm{Id},\bar{\mathrm{T}})

(38)

where
\bar{\mathrm{T}}
solves WCCCP, e.g. in its version (
12
), giving
\bar{\mathrm{T}}=\argmin_{\mathrm{T}\in L^{2}(\mu)}\ \mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}^{-}}^{\mu}(\mathrm{T},\mathrm{Id})+\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{T}_{\#}\mu)
.

Under this ideal condition, we show that we have the following linear convergence rate, analogously to
[
32
, Lemma 1]
and
[
61
, Theorem 6.2]
.

Proposition C.11
.

Assume
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the Wasserstein DC-PL inequalities (
38
). Then, for all
k\geq 0
,

\mathcal{F}(\mu_{k})-\inf\mathcal{F}\leq\left(\frac{1-\eta_{1}}{1+\eta_{2}}\right)^{k}\big(\mathcal{F}(\mu_{0})-\inf\mathcal{F}\big).

(39)

If
\eta_{1}\geq 1
, then we can set
\eta_{1}=1
and we have convergence in one step.

Proof.

Since
\mu_{k+1}=(\mathrm{T}_{k+1})_{\#}\mu_{k}
, (
38
) gives

\displaystyle\eta_{1}\big(\mathcal{F}(\mu_{k})-\inf\mathcal{F}\big)+\eta_{2}\big(\mathcal{F}(\mu_{k+1})-\inf\mathcal{F}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}^{-}}^{\mu_{k}}(\mathrm{T}_{k+1},\mathrm{Id})+\mathrm{D}_{\mathcal{F}^{+}}^{\mu_{k}}(\mathrm{Id},\mathrm{T}_{k+1})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:iterates_difference_gap}}}{{=}}\mathcal{F}(\mu_{k})-\mathcal{F}(\mu_{k+1}).

Rearranging, we obtain

\mathcal{F}(\mu_{k+1})-\inf\mathcal{F}\leq\frac{1-\eta_{1}}{1+\eta_{2}}\big(\mathcal{F}(\mu_{k})-\inf\mathcal{F}\big)\leq\left(\frac{1-\eta_{1}}{1+\eta_{2}}\right)^{k+1}\big(\mathcal{F}(\mu_{0})-\inf\mathcal{F}\big).

(40)

∎

Although
[
61
, Lemma 6.3]
in the Euclidean case shows that
[
32
, Definition 1]
implies their
[
61
, Definition 6.1]
, the latter is difficult to check directly for a given functional and is more of a target inequality to establish. We thus want to argue that
[
32
, Definition 1]
is more promising to stand as DC PL inequality and has received a more developed discussion of when it holds,
e.g.
under strong convexity. However this definition rests upon Fenchel duality on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for which there is no publicly available counterpart on the Wasserstein space at the time of this submission. Consequently it is unclear for now whether
Definition

C.1
holds for functionals with strongly convex DC decompositions.

While
[
55
, Theorems 4 and 5]
do rest upon a Łojaziewicz inequality, the latter is unrelated to Bregman divergences.
55
achieve instead their linear rates using the extra Wasserstein regularization appearing in (

Section

3.4
, one cannot use their theory without the Wasserstein regularization as otherwise their constant become vacuous.

Appendix D
DC Theory for MMD

Let
k
be a translation-invariant kernel of the form
k(x,y)=\psi(x-y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
\psi
admitting a DC decomposition
\psi=\psi^{+}-\psi^{-}
. Then by
Proposition

7
, the squared MMD (
28
) admits the DC decomposition
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}^{+}-\mathcal{F}^{-}
where for all
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{F}^{+}(\mu)=\tfrac{1}{2}\iint\psi^{+}(x-y)\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)+\int\mathrm{V}^{-}\mathrm{d}\mu+c,&\mathrm{V}^{-}(\cdot)=\int\psi^{-}(\cdot-y)\ \mathrm{d}\nu(y),\\
\mathcal{F}^{-}(\mu)=\tfrac{1}{2}\iint\psi^{-}(x-y)\ \mathrm{d}\mu(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)+\int\mathrm{V}^{+}\mathrm{d}\mu,&\mathrm{V}^{+}(\cdot)=\int\psi^{+}(\cdot-y)\ \mathrm{d}\nu(y),\end{array}\right.

(41)

and
\mathcal{F}^{+},\mathcal{F}^{-}
are both totally convex. Moreover, is
\psi^{+}
and
\psi^{-}
are respectively
\alpha^{+}\geq 0
and
\alpha^{-}\geq 0
strongly convex, then
\mathcal{F}^{+}
is
\alpha^{-}
-totally convex and
\mathcal{F}^{-}
is
\alpha^{+}
-totally convex as they inherit the strong convexity of the potential, while the interaction terms are only convex, see
[
4
, Section 9.3]
.

We will now focus on radial kernels. Let
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a nonempty compact convex set. Consider the geodesically convex set
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
.
Let

S_{*}\coloneqq\sup_{x,y\in\Omega}\|x-y\|_{2}.

(42)

We consider in what follows:

\psi^{\pm}(z):=q_{\pm}(\|z\|_{2}^{2}),\quad z\in\Omega-\Omega\coloneqq\{x-y\,|\,x,y\in\Omega\},

(43)

where
q_{\pm}\in C^{2}([0,S_{*}])
.
The MMD functional
\mathcal{F}
is therefore defined as a function of
q^{\pm}.

D.1
Strong Convexity of
\mathcal{F}^{-}
and
\mathcal{F}^{+}
for Radial Kernels

We first study the Hessian of radial functions, in order to derive conditions for
\psi_{\pm}
to be (strongly) convex and being able to apply
Proposition

7
.

Let
q\in C^{2}([0,S_{*}])
and define:

\underline{\lambda}[q]\coloneqq\inf_{0\leq s\leq S_{*}}\min\bigl\{2q^{\prime}(s),\,2q^{\prime}(s)+4sq^{\prime\prime}(s)\bigr\},\quad\overline{\Lambda}[q]\coloneqq\sup_{0\leq s\leq S_{*}}\max\bigl\{2q^{\prime}(s),\,2q^{\prime}(s)+4sq^{\prime\prime}(s)\bigr\}.

(44)

We use the shorthands
\lambda_{\pm}:=\underline{\lambda}[q_{\pm}]
and
\Lambda_{\pm}:=\overline{\Lambda}[q_{\pm}]
. First, we compute the Hessian of
\psi(z)=q(\|z\|_{2}^{2})
and bound its eigenvalues using
\underline{\lambda}[q]
and
\overline{\Lambda}[q]
.

Lemma D.1

(Radial Hessian Bounds)
.

Let
\psi(z)=q(\|z\|_{2}^{2})
on
z\in\Omega-\Omega
, we have

\nabla^{2}\psi(z)=2q^{\prime}(\|z\|_{2}^{2})I_{d}+4q^{\prime\prime}(\|z\|_{2}^{2})zz^{\top}.

(45)

Hence, we have the following bounds on
\nabla^{2}\psi
:

\underline{\lambda}[q]I_{d}\preceq\nabla^{2}\psi(z)\preceq\overline{\Lambda}[q]I_{d},\quad z\in\Omega-\Omega.

(46)

If
\underline{\lambda}(q)\geq 0,
then
\psi
is convex on
\Omega-\Omega
.

Proof.

See
Section

F.10
.
∎

Now, building on the previous Lemma, we deduce sufficient condition under which
\psi
is convex.

Lemma D.2

(Sufficient Condition for Convexity)
.

Assume
q\in C^{2}([0,S^{*}])
such that
q^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
and
q^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq 0
for all
s\in[0,S^{*}]
, then
z\mapsto\psi(z)=q(\|z\|_{2}^{2})
is convex in
\Omega-\Omega
, and
\overline{\Lambda}(q)\geq\underline{\lambda}(q)\geq 0
.

Proof of Lemma
D.2
.

We always have
\overline{\Lambda}(q)\geq\underline{\lambda}(q)

D.3
Critical points and Local Convergence

In the following we analyze the conditions under which we obtain local convergence of WCCCP for the MMD functional with base space
\Omega
. All the statements are in the weak topology and assume a continuous kernel
k
.

First,we consider a condition on the initialization
w.r.t.
the critical value gap as follows:

Assumption 1
.

The set
\Omega
is convex and compact and the following holds true:

1.

Strong Convexity & Lipschitz Continuity.

\mathcal{F}^{+}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-}
are totally strongly convex,
\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{+}
is Lipschitz continuous, and
\mu\mapsto\|\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
is lower semicontinuous;

2.

Critical-value gap
.
Setting

c_{*}\coloneqq\inf\{\mathcal{F}(\mu):\|\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0,\mu\neq\nu\},

the initialization
\mu_{0}
satisfies:

\mathcal{F}(\mu_{0})<c_{*}.

Under Assumption
1
, we have the following local convergence result, interpreted as: if one starts with a near optimal initialization, by descent, there must be convergence. The condition
\mathcal{F}(\mu_{0})<c_{*}
entails that
\nu
is an isolated critical point.

Proposition D.14

(Local Convergence of CCP for MMD under Critical Value Gap Assumption)
.

Under Assumption
1
, the sequence produced by WCCCP converges to
\nu
, i.e.,
\mu_{k}\to\nu
.

Proof.

Let
(\mu_{k})_{k}
be the sequence produced by WCCCP.
The compactness of
\Omega
implies the compactness of
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
, hence we can extract a subsequence
\mu_{k_{j}}\to\mu^{*}
in
\mathrm{W}_{2}
.
Using Theorem
D.1
(satisfied under the first point in Assumption
1
)
\|\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu_{k_{j}})\|_{L_{2}(\mu_{k_{k}})}\to 0
.
By lower semicontinuity of
\mu\mapsto\|\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu)\|_{L_{2}(\mu)}
, we have
\|\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu^{*})\|_{L_{2}(\mu^{*})}=0
and
\mu^{*}
is then a critical point.
Since under our assumptions WCCCP guarantees descent of values and under Assumption
1
we have
\mathcal{F}(\mu_{0})<c_{*}
, we obtain:

\mathcal{F}(\mu_{*})\leq\mathcal{F}(\mu_{0})<c_{*}.

By the definition of
c^{*}
, the limit
\mu^{*}
must be equal to
\nu
(
\mu^{*}=\nu
). We finally conclude that every subsequence
\mu_{k_{j}}
converges to
\nu
, and hence
\mu_{k}\to\nu
.
∎

The following proposition does not require compactness but assumes local quadratic growth of the MMD and uniqueness of critical points in a local neighborhood of
\nu
, and it guarantees local convergence:

Proposition D.15

(Local Convergence of CCP under Local Quadratic Growth for the MMD)
.

Assume there exists
r>0
such that over the ball

B_{r}(\nu)=\Big\{\mu\,\Big|\,\mathrm{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)\leq r\Big\},

1.

\mathcal{F}
has local quadratic growth at
\nu
, i.e., there exists
c>0
such that for all
\mu\in B_{r}(\nu)
:

\mathcal{F}(\mu)\geq c\mathrm{W}^{2}_{2}(\mu,\nu);

2.

\nu
is the unique critical point in
B_{r}(\nu)
;

3.

the initialization
\mu_{0}
satisfies :

\mathcal{F}(\mu_{0})<cr^{2}.

Then under the assumptions of Theorem
D.1
we have local convergence of WCCCP iterates:
\mu_{k}\to\nu
.

Proof.

Assume local quadratic growth of
\mathcal{F}
in
B_{r}(\nu)
, meaning we have for
\mu\in B_{r}(\nu)
:

\mathcal{F}(\mu)-\mathcal{F}(\nu)\geq c\mathrm{W}^{2}_{2}(\mu,\nu),

since
\mathcal{F}(\nu)=0
we have

\mathcal{F}(\mu)\geq c\mathrm{W}^{2}_{2}(\mu,\nu).

On the boundary (
\mathrm{W}^{2}_{2}(\mu,\nu)=r^{2}
) we have therefore

\mathcal{F}(\mu)\geq cr^{2}.

Consider an initialization
\mu_{0}
such that

\mathcal{F}(\mu_{0})<cr^{2},

since WCCCP is descending under our assumptions, we have therefore

\mathcal{F}(\mu_{k_{j}})\leq\mathcal{F}(\mu_{0})<cr^{2},

and hence by local quadratic growth

c\mathrm{W}^{2}_{2}(\mu_{k_{j}},\nu)\leq\mathcal{F}(\mu_{k_{j}})\leq\mathcal{F}(\mu_{0})<cr^{2}.

From this we conclude that

\mathrm{W}^{2}_{2}(\mu_{k_{j}},\nu)<r^{2},

and all iterations remain in the ball of radius
r
around
\nu
without touching the boundary.

By the compactness of the Wasserstein ball we can extract a subsequence
\mu_{k_{j}}\to\mu^{*}
. Under the assumptions of Theorem
D.1
we have
\|\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu_{k_{j}})\|_{L_{2}(\mu_{k_{k}})}\to 0
. Hence, by continuity we have
\|\nabla_{\mathrm{W}_{2}}\mathcal{F}(\mu^{*})\|_{L_{2}(\mu^{*})}=0
and
\mu^{*}
is a critical point. Since
\nu
in the unique critical point in that neighborhood,
\mu^{*}=\nu
.
∎
