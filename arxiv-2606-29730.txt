Abstract
. Let M M be a closed, connected, smooth n n -dimensional manifold. We prove that M M is dominated by the underlying space of the n n -skeleton of a finite simplicial complex. Furthermore, the total number of simplices in the n n -skeleton is bounded above by a constant depending only on n n and the embolic volume of M M .

Primary 53C23, Secondary 53C20.

1.
Introduction

Gromov’s Betti number theorem gives an upper bound for the total Betti number of a closed Riemannian manifold in terms of a lower bound on its sectional curvature and an upper bound on its diameter. Weiss
[
13
]
showed a stronger version of this theorem in terms of domination. In the study of systolic inequalities, Gromov also established a curvature-free upper bound for the total Betti number. In this note, we show that Gromov’s curvature-free upper bound admits a generalization in terms of domination.

For compact Riemannian manifolds of sectional curvature bounded from below, Gromov’s Betti number theorem states that the total Betti number is bounded above by a positive constant depending on the dimension, a lower bound on sectional curvature and the diameter, see
[
8
]
or
[
13
, Theorem A]
. In
[
13
]
, Weiss modified Gromov’s argument to show that the total Betti number in Gromov’s theorem can be replaced by the number of cells in a finite dominating CW-complex.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
M
be a closed, connected, n-dimensional Riemannian manifold, with sectional curvature bounded from below by
-\kappa^{2}
(
\kappa>0
). Then there exists a constant
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(n)
, depending only on
n
, such that the manifold
M
is dominated by a CW-complex with number of cells at most

\mathcal{C}^{1+\kappa D},

where
D
is the diameter of
M
.

Two notions of
domination
are commonly used. Following
[
5
, Definition 2.1]
, an oriented connected closed
n
-dimensional manifold
M
is dominated by another oriented connected closed
n
-dimensional manifold
N
if there exists a continuous map
f:N\to M
of nonzero degree. Domination in the sense of
Whitehead is a different notion; see Whitehead
[
14
]
or Hatcher
[
12
, Appendix]
. Throughout this paper, unless otherwise stated, we use the notion of domination in Whitehead’s sense.

Definition 1.2

(Whitehead domination)
.

A topological space
X
is said to be dominated by a CW-complex
Y
if there exist continuous maps
r:X\to Y
and
s:Y\to X
, such that
s\circ r:X\to X
is homotopic to the identity map.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional manifold.
For a Riemannian metric
g
defined on
M
, denote by
\vol_{g}(M)
the Riemannian volume, and by
\inj(M,g)
the injectivity radius. The
embolic volume
of
M
, denoted
\emb(M)
, is defined by

\inf_{g}\frac{\vol_{g}(M)}{\inj(M,g)^{n}},

where the infimum is taken over all Riemannian metrics
g
on
M
. Berger’s embolic inequality (see (
1.3
) in the following) implies that
\emb(M)
is positive if
M
is compact. The main result of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
be a closed, connected, and smooth
n
-dimensional manifold. There exist positive constants
C_{n}
and
C_{n}^{\prime}
, depending only on
n
, such that
M
is dominated by the underlying space of the
n
-skeleton
\mathcal{N}^{(n)}

of a finite simplicial complex
\mathcal{N}
. Furthermore, the total number of simplices in the
n
-skeleton
\mathcal{N}^{(n)}
is at most

(1.1)

C_{n}(1+\emb(M))^{n+1}\exp{\left(C_{n}^{\prime}\sqrt{\log{(1+\emb(M))}}\right)}.

In
[
10
, Section 6.4.C.]
, Gromov proved that the total Betti number of a closed aspherical manifold is bounded above by a positive constant depending only on its systolic volume and dimension. The same result holds for embolic volume, since embolic volume is an upper bound for systolic volume.
Theorem
1.3
strengthens this conclusion by controlling the size of a finite simplicial complex dominating the manifold, in analogy with Weiss’s theorem in
[
13
]
.

The quantitative bound (
1.1
) in Theorem
1.3
is obtained through Gromov’s covering techniques. The same approach can be applied to establish the following bound on the homological complexity of closed essential manifolds. A closed
n
-dimensional manifold
M
is
essential
if there exists a continuous map
f:M\to K
from
M
to an aspherical topological space
K
, such that the image
f_{*}([M])
of the fundamental class
[M]
is nontrivial in
H_{n}(K;G)
, with
G=\mathbb{Z}
if
M
is orientable, and
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
M
is non-orientable.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
M
be a closed, essential, smooth
n
-dimensional manifold.
Then there exist a finite simplicial complex
\mathcal{N}
and a continuous map
f:M\to|\mathcal{N}^{(n)}|
from
M
to the underlying space of the
n
-skeleton
\mathcal{N}^{(n)}
, such that
f_{*}([M])\neq 0
in
H_{n}(|\mathcal{N}^{(n)}|;G)
, with
G=\mathbb{Z}
if
M
is orientable, and
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
M
is non-orientable.
Moreover, the total number of simplices of
\mathcal{N}^{(n)}
is at most

(1.2)

C_{n}(1+\emb(M))^{n+1}\exp{\left(C_{n}^{\prime}\sqrt{\log{(1+\emb(M))}}\right)},

where
C_{n}
and
C_{n}^{\prime}
are positive constants depending only on
n
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold.
Berger’s embolic inequality states that

(1.3)

\inj(M,g)^{n}\leq c(n)\vol_{g}(M),

where
c(n)
is a constant depending only on
n
(see
[
1
, Section 7.2.4.]
and the references therein). For a compact manifold
M
, the reciprocal
1/\emb(M)
is the optimal constant in the corresponding embolic inequality over all Riemannian metrics on
M
.
Determining exact embolic volumes is generally an open problem. Based on Croke’s work, we know that the smallest
c(n)
is equal to the reciprocal of the embolic volume of the
n
-sphere
S^{n}
,

\emb(M)\geq\emb(S^{n}).

Moreover,
\emb(S^{n})
is known to be equal to
\frac{\sigma_{n}}{\pi^{n}}
, where
\sigma_{n}
is the volume of the standard Euclidean
n
-sphere. According to
[
1
, Section 7.2.4.1, p. 357]
, the
n
-sphere
S^{n}
is the only compact manifold for which the precise embolic volume is known.
In
[
4
, Proposition 14]
(also see Berger
[
1
, Theorem 149, p. 355]
), Croke proved a local embolic inequality: if
0<R\leq\frac{1}{2}\inj(M,g)
, then

(1.4)

\vol_{g}(B(p,R))\geq\beta_{n}R^{n}

holds for every ball
B(p,R)
centered at
p
with radius
R
in a compact
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, where
\beta_{n}
is a constant depending only on
n
. A non-optimal value for
\beta_{n}
is given by Croke in
[
4
]
(also see
[
1
, Section 7.2.4.]
).

2.
Nerve and filling radius

Let
X
be a topological space, and let
\mathcal{U}
be an open cover of
X
.

Definition 2.1
.

The nerve of
\mathcal{U}
, denoted by
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{U})
, is the simplicial complex defined as follows:

(1)

For each
U\in\mathcal{U}
, there is a corresponding vertex
u
;

(2)

The vertices
u_{i_{0}},u_{i_{1}},\cdots,u_{i_{k}}
span a
k
-simplex if and only if
U_{i_{0}}\cap U_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap U_{i_{k}}\neq\emptyset
.

We denote by
|\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{U})|
the underlying polyhedron of the nerve
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{U})
.
Suppose that
\{\varphi_{U}:X\to[0,1]\,|\,U\in\mathcal{U}\}

is a locally finite partition of unity subordinate to
\mathcal{U}
. The associated
Alexandrov map

\Phi:X\to|\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{U})|
is defined by

\Phi(x)=\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}}\varphi_{U}(x)\hskip 1.11115pt\hskip 1.11115ptu.

Since the partition of unity is locally finite, the sum is finite at each point.
Moreover,
\Phi:X\to|\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{U})|
is continuous. We refer to
[
6
, VIII, §5]
for more details regarding the Alexandrov map.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold, and let
\mathrm{dist}_{g}
denote the distance function induced by
g
.
Let
L^{\infty}(M)
be the space of all bounded Borel functions on
M
. On
L^{\infty}(M)
, we define the supremum norm,

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}=\sup_{x\in M}|f(x)|.

The metric space
(M,\mathrm{dist}_{g})
is embedded into
L^{\infty}(M)
by the Kuratowski embedding
K:M\to L^{\infty}(M)
, which is defined by

K(x)(y)=\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,y)

for all
x,y\in M
.

Let
G
be the coefficient group for the homology. The fundamental class
[M]
is the generator of
H_{n}(M;G)
, with
G=\mathbb{Z}
if
M
is orientable, and
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
M
is non-orientable. Denote by
N_{r}(K(M))
the
r
-neighborhood of
K(M)
in
L^{\infty}(M)
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
filling radius
of
(M,g)
, denoted
\FillRad(M,g)
, is defined to be the infimum of
r>0
such that the image
K_{*}([M])
of the fundamental class vanishes in
H_{n}(N_{r}(K(M));G)
.

Gromov
[
10
]
proved systolic inequalities on closed essential manifolds using filling radius.

Definition 2.3
.

A closed
n
-dimensional manifold
M
is
essential
if there exists a continuous map
f:M\to X
from
M
to an aspherical topological space
X
, such that
f_{*}([M])\neq 0
in
H_{n}(X;G)
. Here the coefficient group
G=\mathbb{Z}
if
M
is orientable, and
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
M
is non-orientable.

Remark 2.4
.

All aspherical closed
n
-manifolds are essential. Moreover,
n
-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{n}
is also essential.

In a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, the homotopy 1-systole
\sys\pi_{1}(M,g)
is defined to be the length of a shortest non-contractible closed curve. By definition, the homotopy 1-systole is related to the injectivity radius by
2\inj(M,g)\leq\sys\pi_{1}(M,g)
.

Theorem 2.5

(Gromov
[
10
, Lemma 1.2.B.]
, also see
[
3
, Theorem 3.3.]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed essential Riemannian manifold. Then

\sys\pi_{1}(M,g)\leq 6\,\FillRad(M,g).

The following lemma is a simplicial version of
[
11
, Lemma 6]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{V}=\{B(p_{j},R_{j})\}_{j=1}^{N}
be an open cover of
a closed Riemannian manifold
M
by metric balls,
where
R_{j}\leq R_{0}
and
R_{0}>0
.
If the Alexandrov map
\Phi:M\to|\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{V})|
satisfies
{\Phi}_{*}([M])=0
, then the filling radius of
M
must be at most
R_{0}
.

In
[
11
]
, Guth uses the rectangular nerve rather than the simplicial nerve. The proof of Lemma
2.6
is obtained by adapting Guth’s argument in our setting.

Proof.

Using the Kuratowski embedding
K:M\to L^{\infty}(M)
, we define an affine map

\Psi\colon|\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{V})|\longrightarrow L^{\infty}(M)

by sending each vertex
u_{j}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{V})
to
K(p_{j})
, and extending
\Psi
simplicially on
|\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{V})|
. If
x=\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}u_{j_{i}}
lies in a simplex, choose

q\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}B(p_{j_{i}},R_{j_{i}}).

Then

\displaystyle\|\Psi(x)-K(q)\|_{\infty}=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}K(p_{j_{i}})-K(q)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\left\|K(p_{j_{i}})-K(q)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\dist_{g}(p_{j_{i}},q)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}R_{j_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle R_{0}.

Thus
\Psi(|\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{V})|)\subseteq N_{R_{0}}(K(M))
. Similarly, for every
p\in M
,
\|\Psi(\Phi(p))-K(p)\|_{\infty}<R_{0}
,
so
\Psi\circ\Phi
and
K
are joined by a straight-line homotopy inside the neighborhood
N_{R_{0}}(K(M))
. If
\Phi_{*}([M])=0
, then
K_{*}([M])=0
in
H_{n}(N_{R_{0}}(K(M));G)
. This proves the lemma.
∎

The following proposition is used in the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed essential
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold.
For a cover
\mathcal{V}=\{B(p_{j},R_{j})\}_{j=1}^{N}
of open metric balls, let the radii
R_{j}
be at most
\frac{1}{8}\inj(M,g)
. Then the Alexandrov map
\Phi:M\to|\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{V})|
satisfies
\Phi_{*}([M])\neq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Phi_{*}([M])=0
in
H_{n}(|\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{V})|;G)
. Here
G=\mathbb{Z}
if
M
is orientable, and
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
M
is non-orientable.
By Lemma
2.6
,
\FillRad(M,g)\leq\frac{1}{8}\inj(M,g)
. On the other hand, Theorem
2.5
yields that

\inj(M,g)\leq\frac{1}{2}\sys\pi_{1}(M,g)\leq 3\FillRad(M,g)\leq\frac{3}{8}\inj(M,g)

holds for every closed essential Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, thus leading to a contradiction.
∎

3.
Gromov’s covering argument

In this section, we introduce Gromov’s covering technique, which first appeared in
[
10
, Section 5.3.]
.
This section is an adaptation of Gromov’s work.
Moreover, some additional explanations are provided to clarify the original arguments. We also refer to
[
2
]
for the application to Betti numbers.

Let
\theta>0
and
R_{0}>0
.

Definition 3.1
.

A metric ball
B(p,R)
in an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is called
\theta
-admissible

if either
R=R_{0}
and
\vol_{g}\left(B(p,5R_{0})\right)\leq 5^{n+\theta}\vol_{g}(B(p,R_{0}))
, or
0<R<R_{0}
and the following two conditions hold:

(1)

\vol_{g}(B(p,5R))\leq 5^{n+\theta}\vol_{g}(B(p,R)),

(2)

\vol_{g}(B(p,5R^{\prime}))>5^{n+\theta}\vol_{g}(B(p,R^{\prime}))
whenever
R<R^{\prime}\leq R_{0}
.

In Proposition
A.3
of Appendix A, we show that at any point in a complete Riemannian manifold
M
, there exists a unique
\theta
-admissible ball.

For appropriately chosen positive constants
\theta
and
R_{0}
, we use
\theta
-admissible metric balls to construct an open cover of the Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
. Throughout this section, the constant
R_{0}
is chosen to be

(3.1)

R_{0}=\frac{1}{8n+16}\,\inj(M,g).

The constant
\theta
is then defined as

(3.2)

\theta=\sqrt{\log_{5}{\frac{\vol_{g}(M)}{\beta_{n}\hskip 1.11115ptR_{0}^{n}}}},

where
\beta_{n}
is the constant from Croke’s local embolic inequality (
1.4
). Then
\theta
must be positive, since
\vol_{g}(M)>\vol_{g}(B(p,R_{0}))\geq\beta_{n}R_{0}^{n}
by (
1.4
), and
B(p,R_{0})\neq M
according to our choice of
R_{0}
.

We next show that a cover of the manifold
M
can be constructed using the
\theta
-admissible balls.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
M
be a closed connected Riemannian manifold. Then there exists a finite collection
\{B(p_{j},R_{j})\}_{j=1}^{N}
of pairwise disjoint
\theta
-admissible balls, indexed so that
R_{1}\geq R_{2}\geq\cdots\geq R_{N}
, such that

\mathcal{U}=\{B(p_{j},2R_{j})\}_{j=1}^{N}

is an open cover of
M
.

Proof.

We define the radius function
R:M\to(0,+\infty)
by assigning
R(p)
the radius of the unique
\theta
-admissible ball centered at
p
.
By Lemma
A.4
, the function
R
is upper semicontinuous. Hence the function
R
attains its maximum on every compact subset.

Now we set
K_{1}=M
. Then choose
p_{1}\in K_{1}
such that
R_{1}=R(p_{1})
is the maximum of
R
on
K_{1}
.
Suppose that pairwise disjoint balls

B(p_{1},R_{1}),\cdots B(p_{j-1},R_{j-1})

have already been chosen.
Set
K_{j}=M\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{j-1}B(p_{i},2R_{i})
. If
K_{j}\neq\emptyset
, then choose
p_{j}\in K_{j}

maximizing the radius function
R(p)
on
K_{j}
.
Set
R_{j}=R(p_{j})
.
We add the ball
B(p_{j},R_{j})
to the collection.
Continue this process until no further balls can be added.
By Proposition
A.3
,
at any point in
M
there exists a unique
\theta
-admissible ball. Moreover, Lemma
A.4
implies that the radius of
\theta
-admissible balls has a positive lower bound when
M
is compact.
Let
\rho=\inf\{R(p):p\in M\}>0
. Then

\dist_{g}(p_{i},p_{j})\geq 2R_{i}\geq 2\rho

for every
i<j
.
Therefore all the chosen centers
p_{j}
are
2\rho
-separated.
Hence the above procedure terminates after finitely many steps.
By the construction,
R_{1}\geq R_{2}\geq\cdots\geq R_{N}
.
The above construction immediately yields that the collection
\{B(p_{j},2R_{j}):j=1,\cdots,N\}
is an open cover of
M
. By the construction, the procedure continues whenever
K_{j}=M\setminus\bigcup_{i<j}B(p_{i},2R_{i})
is nonempty. Therefore, when the process terminates, we must have
K_{N+1}=\emptyset
. Hence,

M=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}B(p_{j},2R_{j}).

Next we show that balls in the collection

\{B(p_{1},R_{1}),\cdots,B(p_{N},R_{N})\}

are mutually disjoint. Note that at the
j
th step, we choose

p_{j}\in K_{j}=M\setminus\bigcup_{i<j}B(p_{i},2R_{i}).

Thus, for every
i<j
,

\mathrm{dist}_{g}(p_{i},p_{j})\geq 2R_{i}.

Since the compact sets
K_{j}
are nested, their maximal radii satisfy
R_{j}\leq R_{i}
.
Consequently,

\mathrm{dist}_{g}(p_{i},p_{j})\geq 2R_{i}\geq R_{i}+R_{j}.

Therefore, the open balls
B(p_{i},R_{i})
and
B(p_{j},R_{j})
are disjoint.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

In Berger’s covering trick lemma (see
[
1
, Lemma 125, §7.2]
), a much simpler technique is used to construct an open cover using metric balls with the same radius. In Lemma
3.2
, the construction of an open cover by admissible balls is more delicate.

Given a
\theta
-admissible metric ball
B(p,R)
, for convenience,
choose the unique nonnegative integer
k
such that

5^{-k}R_{0}\leq R<5^{-k+1}R_{0}.

Recall that the constant
\theta
is chosen in (
3.2
). Then the exponent
k
is controlled by
\theta
.

Lemma 3.4
.

The exponent
k
satisfies
k<\theta
.

Proof.

If
k=0
, then the result is immediate because
\theta>0
. Now assume
k\geq 1
.
By Croke’s local embolic inequality (
1.4
), we have

\displaystyle\vol_{g}(M)

\displaystyle\geq\vol_{g}(B(p,5R_{0}))

\displaystyle>5^{n+\theta}\vol_{g}(B(p,R_{0}))

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt\vdots

\displaystyle>5^{k(n+\theta)}\vol_{g}(B(p,5^{-k+1}R_{0}))

\displaystyle>5^{k(n+\theta)}\vol_{g}(B(p,R))

\displaystyle\geq 5^{k(n+\theta)}\hskip 1.11115pt\beta_{n}R^{n}

\displaystyle\geq 5^{k(n+\theta)}\beta_{n}(5^{-k}R_{0})^{n}.

Hence we obtain an upper bound on
k
,

\displaystyle k<

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{\theta}\cdot\log_{5}{\frac{\vol_{g}(M)}{\beta_{n}R_{0}^{n}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\theta.

∎

The triangle inequality implies the following useful fact.

Lemma 3.5
.

In the open cover
\mathcal{U}=\{B(p_{j},2R_{j}):j=1,2,\cdots,N\}
, if
B(p_{i},2R_{i})\cap B(p_{j},2R_{j})\neq\emptyset
and
i\geq j
, then
B(p_{i},R_{i})\subseteq B(p_{j},5R_{j})
.

Denote by
T
the number of pairwise intersections in the open cover
\mathcal{U}
. Hence
T
is the number of edges in the 1-skeleton of the nerve
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{U})
.
For
1\leq j\leq N
, suppose that in the cover
\mathcal{U}
, each ball in the subcollection
\{B(p_{j_{1}},2R_{j_{1}})
,
B(p_{j_{2}},2R_{j_{2}})
,
\cdots
,
B(p_{j_{T_{j}}},2R_{j_{T_{j}}})\}
has nonempty intersection with the ball
B(p_{j},2R_{j})
, and
j_{i}>j
for
1\leq i\leq T_{j}
. Then we have
T=T_{1}+T_{2}+\cdots+T_{N-1}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
M
be a closed smooth
n
-dimensional manifold.
Then there exists a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
, such that the number
T
of pairwise intersections in the open cover
\mathcal{U}
is bounded above in terms of the embolic volume
\emb(M)
as follows,

T\leq C_{n}\emb(M)\exp{\left(C_{n}^{\prime}\sqrt{\log{(1+\emb(M)})}\right)},

where
C_{n}
and
C_{n}^{\prime}
are positive constants depending only on
n
.

The proof of Proposition
3.6
is an adaptation of Gromov’s argument in
[
10
, Section 5.3.]
. For completeness, we include the proof.

Proof.

In the construction of
\theta
-admissible metric balls
B(p_{j},R_{j})\subseteq M
, we assume that there exists a nonnegative integer
k_{j}
, such that

5^{-k_{j}}R_{0}\leq R_{j}<5^{-k_{j}+1}R_{0},

where the exponent
k_{j}
satisfies
k_{j}<\theta
, see Lemma
3.4
.

By Lemma
3.5
and Croke’s local embolic inequality (
1.4
), we have the following estimate for the number
T
of pairwise intersections of balls in
\mathcal{U}
,

\displaystyle\vol_{g}(M)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\vol_{g}(B(p_{j},R_{j}))

\displaystyle\geq 5^{-n-\theta}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\vol_{g}(B(p_{j},5R_{j}))

\displaystyle\geq 5^{-n-\theta}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{T_{j}}\vol_{g}(B(p_{j_{i}},R_{j_{i}}))

\displaystyle\geq 5^{-n-\theta}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{T_{j}}\beta_{n}R_{j_{i}}^{n}

Hence we have

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\vol_{g}(M)}{\beta_{n}R_{0}^{n}}5^{n\cdot\sqrt{\log_{5}{\frac{\vol_{g}(M)}{\beta_{n}R_{0}^{n}}}}}\cdot 5^{n+\theta}

on

Appendix A
Existence of admissible balls

Let
(M,g)
be an n-dimensional Riemannian manifold, and
let
\mathrm{Sc}_{p}
denote the scalar curvature at a point
p\in M
.
For the volume of balls with small radii in
(M,g)
, we have the following expansion in terms of the scalar curvature
\mathrm{Sc}_{p}
.

Theorem A.1

(see
[
7
, Theorem 3.98, p. 168]
)
.

Let
B(p,r)
be a ball centered at
p
with radius
r
in an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
. As the radius
r
approaches zero, the volume of
B(p,r)
satisfies

(A.1)

\vol_{g}(B(p,r))=r^{n}\omega_{n}\left[1-\frac{\text{Sc}_{p}}{6(n+2)}r^{2}+o(r^{3})\right],

where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of the unit ball in Euclidean
n
-space.

The following lemma concerns the growth of the volume of balls in a Riemannian manifold. It is a slight modification of
[
11
, Lemma 1]
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
p\in M
be any point, and let
R_{0}>0
be a fixed constant.
Then for any
\theta>0
, there exists at least one
R\in(0,R_{0}]
, such that

(A.2)

\vol_{g}(B(p,5R))\leq 5^{n+\theta}\vol_{g}(B(p,R)).

Proof.

We define the density function of radius
r
at
p\in M
as

(A.3)

\dens_{p}(r)=\frac{\vol_{g}(B(p,r))}{r^{n}}.

According to Theorem
A.1
, we have

(A.4)

\lim_{r\to 0}\dens_{p}(r)=\omega_{n}.

If we assume that (
A.2
) is not true, then

\displaystyle\vol_{g}(B(p,5R))>5^{n+\theta}\vol_{g}(B(p,R))

holds for all
0<R\leq R_{0}
, which implies

\dens_{p}(5R)>5^{\theta}\dens_{p}(R).

Iterating this inequality yields:

\displaystyle\dens_{p}(5R_{0})

\displaystyle>5^{\theta}\dens_{p}(R_{0})

\displaystyle>5^{2\theta}\dens_{p}(5^{-1}R_{0})

\displaystyle\hskip 20.0pt\vdots

\displaystyle>5^{\ell\hskip 0.91052pt\theta}\dens_{p}(5^{-\ell+1}R_{0}).

However, this leads to a contradiction. As
\ell\to\infty
, identity (
A.4
) implies that the right-hand side of

(A.5)

\dens_{p}(5\hskip 0.55557ptR_{0})>5^{\ell\hskip 0.4429pt\theta}\dens_{p}(5^{-\ell+1}R_{0}),

tends to infinity.
∎

We next show that, for any
\theta>0
and any fixed constant
R_{0}>0
, there exists a unique
\theta
-admissible ball
B(p,R)
centered at
p
with radius
R\leq R_{0}
.

Proposition A.3
.

For any point
p
in an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, there exists a unique
\theta
-admissible metric ball
B(p,R)
.

Proof.

Let

(A.6)

R=\sup_{r}\{\left.0<r\leq R_{0}\right|\vol_{g}(B(p,5r))\leq 5^{n+\theta}\vol_{g}(B(p,r))\}.

Lemma
A.2
implies that the supremum
R
exists, and
R>0
. Then
R\in(0,R_{0}]
. Next we prove
B(p,R)
is a
\theta
-admissible ball.
Since the function

r\mapsto\vol_{g}(B(p,5r))-5^{n+\theta}\vol_{g}(B(p,r))

is continuous, a sequence
r_{i}
in the set

\{0<r\leq R_{0}:\vol_{g}(B(p,5r))\leq 5^{n+\theta}\vol_{g}(B(p,r))\}

with
r_{i}\to R
gives

\vol_{g}(B(p,5R))\leq 5^{n+\theta}\vol_{g}(B(p,R)).

If
R<R_{0}
, then we have

\vol_{g}(B(p,5R^{\prime}))>5^{n+\theta}\vol_{g}(B(p,R^{\prime}))

for any
R<R^{\prime}\leq R_{0}
. Therefore, according to the definition,
B(p,R)
is an admissible ball.

The maximality property in the definition of
R
also proves the uniqueness. Now suppose
\tilde{R}
is another radius of an admissible ball centered at
p
. If
\tilde{R}=R_{0}
, then we must have
R=R_{0}=\tilde{R}
since
R
is the supremum of all the radii satisfying the first admissibility condition.
We may therefore assume that
\tilde{R}<R_{0}
.
Since
\tilde{R}
satisfies the first admissibility condition

\vol_{g}(B(p,5\tilde{R}))\leq 5^{n+\theta}\vol_{g}(B(p,\tilde{R})),

we have
\tilde{R}\leq R
. If
\tilde{R}<R
, then the second admissibility condition for
\tilde{R}
, evaluated at
R
, gives

\vol_{g}(B(p,5R))>5^{n+\theta}\vol_{g}(B(p,R)),

contradicting the fact that
R
satisfies the first admissibility condition. Hence we must have
\tilde{R}=R
.
∎

Define
R:M\to(0,R_{0}]
by assigning
R(p)
the unique radius of
\theta
-admissible balls centered at
p\in M
.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
M
be a closed Riemannian manifold. Then

(1)

R
is upper semicontinuous.

(2)

R
has a positive lower bound on
M
.

Proof.

(1)

To prove that the function
R
is upper semicontinuous, we define

F(p,r)=\vol_{g}(B(p,5r))-5^{n+\theta}\vol_{g}(B(p,r)).

Then the function
F
is continuous in variables
(p,r)
. Moreover, by Proposition
A.3
,

R(p)=\sup\{r\in(0,R_{0}]:F(p,r)\leq 0\}.

Suppose

, and pass to a subsequence such that

R(p_{m})\to L=\limsup_{m\to\infty}R(p_{m}).

If
L=0
, then
L\leq R(p)
is immediate. Now suppose
L>0
.
Since
F(p_{m},R(p_{m}))\leq 0
, the continuity implies
F(p,L)\leq 0
. Hence we have
L\leq R(p)
.
Therefore,

\limsup_{m\to\infty}R(p_{m})\leq R(p),

and this gives the conclusion that the function

is upper semicontinuous.

(2)

Note that the volume function
\mathrm{V}:M\to\mathbb{R}
of balls defined by

(A.7)

\mathrm{V}(p)=\vol_{g}(B(p,5R_{0}))

is continuous. Hence if we suppose the infimum of the radii of
\theta
-admissible balls is zero, then there exists a sequence
\{p_{i}\in M|i=1,2,\cdots\}
of points in
M
, such that the radius

of

-admissible balls at

Choose

. Since

, we may further assume

.
Now consider the density function (

) defined in the proof of Lemma

. Since every radius

exceeds

, iterating the second admissibility condition gives

\dens_{p_{i}}(5R_{0})>5^{(k(i)+1)\,\theta}\dens_{p_{i}}(5^{-k(i)}R_{0})

holds. Here

when

. However, the function

has a maximum value on

, since the volume function

defined in (

) is continuous. On the other hand, for sufficiently large

, the radius

is at most

. Then Croke’s local embolic inequality (

) gives

\dens_{p_{i}}(5^{-k(i)}R_{0})\geq\beta_{n}.

Therefore the left-hand side of the inequality (

) has an upper bound, but the right-hand side of (

) tends to infinity, resulting in a contradiction.

∎
