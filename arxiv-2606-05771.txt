Exotic and inverted Kloosterman sums over semisimple algebras
Daqing Wan, Dingxin Zhang

Abstract
. We introduce exotic Kloosterman sums and exotic inverted Kloosterman sums attached to non-commutative finite-dimensional semisimple algebras over a finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} , and prove their reduction formulae to exotic Kloosterman and exotic inverted Kloosterman sums over commutative étale 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} algebras. We then obtain square-root estimates for these sums; for inverted sums an explicit correction term may appear.

Primary: 11L40; Secondary: 11M38

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field, and fix a nontrivial additive
character
\psi\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Recall
that the classical twisted hyper-Kloosterman sum is

\mathrm{Kl}(a;\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{m})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\\
x_{1}\cdots x_{m}=a\end{subarray}}\chi_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\chi_{m}(x_{m})\psi(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m}),\qquad a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}.

Classical Kloosterman sums are basic objects in the theory of exponential
sums.
A variant of them, called
inverted Kloosterman sums
, appears in
the theory of Ramanujan graphs:

\mathrm{IKl}(a;\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{m})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\\
x_{1}\cdots x_{m}=a\\
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\chi_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\chi_{m}(x_{m})\psi\left(\frac{1}{x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m}}\right),\qquad a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}.

Deligne
[
1
]
and Katz

[
7
]
introduced
“exotic” generalizations of Kloosterman sums, namely Kloosterman
sums over finite étale
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebras. Already in Katz’s
note on inverted Kloosterman sums, their exotic variants were
considered
[
8
]
. These exotic inverted
Kloosterman sums, together with related norm-trace counting problems,
have recently been studied by many authors

[
10
,
11
,
6
,
12
]
.

A related direction concerns exponential sums over matrix groups, including
matrix Kloosterman sums in the sense of
Erdélyi–Tóth and their collaborators

[
4
,
3
,
2
]
, work of
Zelingher
[
13
]
, and
hypergeometric exponential sums over reductive groups in work of
Fu–Li
[
5
]
. In this paper,
we extend the two constructions above from the étale case to
finite-dimensional semisimple
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebras. Thus the sums
below may also be viewed as exponential sums over spaces of matrices.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
M
be a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra over

\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Denote by
M^{\times}
the group of invertible
elements of
M
, by
\mathrm{Trd}\colon M\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
the

reduced trace
, and by
\mathrm{Nrd}\colon M\to\mathbb{F}_{q}

the
reduced norm
. Let

\chi\colon M^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a multiplicative
character. Then for
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, we define the

Kloosterman sum
of the semisimple
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra

M
associated to the multiplicative character
\chi
by

\mathrm{Kl}_{M}(a;\chi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in M^{\times}\\
\mathrm{Nrd}(x)=a\end{subarray}}\chi(x)\psi(\mathrm{Trd}(x)).

Remark 1.2
.

By Wedderburn’s theorem and the triviality of the Brauer group of a
finite field, any finite-dimensional semisimple

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra
M
is isomorphic to

(1.3)

M\simeq M_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{1}}})\times\cdots\times M_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{k}}}).

We shall denote by

B=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{1}}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{k}}}

the center of
M
.

In this paper, we restrict to
determinant-type characters
:
under the identification (
1.3
), we say

\chi=(\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k})
is of determinant type if it comes
from a multiplicative character of
B^{\times}
, pulled back along
the determinant map

M^{\times}\longrightarrow B^{\times},\qquad(A_{1},\ldots,A_{k})\longmapsto(\det A_{1},\ldots,\det A_{k}).

Thus, for

x=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{k})\in M^{\times}
,

\chi(x)=\prod_{i}\chi_{i}(\det A_{i}).

Under the identification (
1.3
), the reduced norm and
reduced trace of
M
are given by the following formulas:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Nrd}(A_{1},\ldots,A_{k})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Norm}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{i}}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\det A_{i}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Trd}(A_{1},\ldots,A_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{i}}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\operatorname{Tr}A_{i}).

With this notation, we set

(1.4)

B^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{1}}}^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{k}}}^{n_{k}}.

Concretely, an element of
(B^{\prime})^{\times}
is a tuple

(x_{ij})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant k,\,1\leqslant j\leqslant n_{i}}
with every coordinate
nonzero. This is a finite étale
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra. Let

\eta
be the character of
(B^{\prime})^{\times}
given by

(1.5)

\eta((x_{ij}))=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\chi_{i}(x_{ij}).

Our first main result is the following reduction formula. It reduces
the Kloosterman sum over the noncommutative algebra
M
to the
commutative étale algebra
B^{\prime}
, where Katz’s estimates apply.

Theorem 1.6
.

With notation as above, and with
\chi
of determinant type, for every

a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, we have

\mathrm{Kl}_{M}(a;\chi)=q^{N}\cdot\mathrm{Kl}_{B^{\prime}}(a;\eta),\qquad N=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}\binom{n_{i}}{2}.

For the trivial multiplicative character and
M=M_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
Theorem
1.6
recovers Kim’s formula
[
9
]

\mathrm{Kl}_{M_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}(a;\mathbb{1})=q^{\binom{n}{2}}\,\mathrm{Kl}_{n}(a).

For general semisimple
M
, it reduces the problem to Katz’s
commutative finite-étale case. Combining with Katz’s square-root
cancellation estimate for exotic Kloosterman sums on étale

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebras gives the following square-root cancellation
estimate.

Corollary 1.7
.

We have

|\mathrm{Kl}_{M}(a;\chi)|\leqslant(\dim B^{\prime})\,q^{\frac{\dim M-1}{2}}=(d_{1}n_{1}+\cdots+d_{k}n_{k})q^{\frac{d_{1}n_{1}^{2}+\cdots+d_{k}n_{k}^{2}-1}{2}}.

Proof.

Since
B^{\prime}
is a commutative étale algebra over

\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the sum
\mathrm{Kl}_{B^{\prime}}(a;\eta)
is the
so-called
exotic Kloosterman sum
studied by

[
7
]
. In

[
7
, Theorem 8.8.5]
,
it is shown that for any étale
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra
E
, any
multiplicative character

\theta\colon E^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, and any

a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, we have

|\mathrm{Kl}_{E}(a;\theta)|\leqslant(\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}E)q^{\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}E-1}{2}}
. Since

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}M=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}B^{\prime}+2N
,
the corollary follows from this estimate applied to
B^{\prime}
and
the reduction formula,
Theorem
1.6
.
∎

Remark 1.8
.

The determinant-type hypothesis is automatic except for one small
exception. For
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, every multiplicative character

G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
factors through the determinant unless

G=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
. Equivalently, the determinant gives
the abelianization of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, except that

\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\simeq S_{3}
. In this exceptional case

\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\times}
is trivial, while the sign character of

S_{3}
gives a non-determinant-type character. Consequently, for a
product of matrix algebras, the only multiplicative characters not of
determinant type are obtained by multiplying by sign characters on the
factors
M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
. Thus, if
M
has no

M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
-factor, every multiplicative character of

M^{\times}
is of determinant type.

Next, let us turn to the inverted sums.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
M
be a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra over

\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let

\chi\colon M^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a multiplicative
character. For
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, we define the

inverted Kloosterman sum
over
M
, associated to the
multiplicative character
\chi
, by

\mathrm{IKl}_{M}(a;\chi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in M^{\times}\\
\mathrm{Nrd}(x)=a\\
\mathrm{Trd}(x)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\chi(x)\,\psi\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{Trd}(x)}\right).

We shall also prove the corresponding reduction formula for inverted
Kloosterman sums, with an explicit correction term. We recall the
definition of the algebra

B^{\prime}
(
1.4
) and the multiplicative
character
\eta
on
(B^{\prime})^{\times}

(
1.5
).

Theorem 1.10
.

Assume that
\chi
is of determinant type, and let

a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Denote by

\nu\colon(B^{\prime})^{\times}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
the norm
map. Thus

\nu((x_{ij}))=\prod_{i}\mathrm{Norm}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{i}}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x_{i1}\cdots x_{in_{i}}).

Put

N=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}\binom{n_{i}}{2},

and let

\mathcal{B}_{M}
be the product of the groups of invertible upper
triangular matrices in the factors

\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{i}}})
. Then the following
assertions hold.

(1)

If
\eta
is nontrivial on
\ker(\nu)
, then

\mathrm{IKl}_{M}(a;\chi)=q^{N}\,\mathrm{IKl}_{B^{\prime}}(a;\eta).

(2)

If
\eta
is trivial on
\ker(\nu)
, equivalently if

\eta=\rho\circ\nu
for some multiplicative character

\rho
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, then

\mathrm{IKl}_{M}(a;\chi)=q^{N}\,\mathrm{IKl}_{B^{\prime}}(a;\eta)-\rho(a)\frac{\#M^{\times}-\#\mathcal{B}_{M}}{q(q-1)}.

Equivalently, since
\#\mathcal{B}_{M}=q^{N}\#(B^{\prime})^{\times}
,
we have

\mathrm{IKl}_{M}(a;\chi)+\rho(a)\frac{\#M^{\times}}{q(q-1)}=q^{N}\,\left(\mathrm{IKl}_{B^{\prime}}(a;\eta)+\rho(a)\frac{\#({B}^{\prime})^{\times}}{q(q-1)}\right).

Combining the reduction theorem with the estimates in

[
6
, Theorems 1.1–1.2]
for

\mathrm{IKl}_{B^{\prime}}(a;\eta)
gives the corresponding square-root
estimate.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let

M=M_{n_{1}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{1}}})\times\dots\times M_{n_{k}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{k}}}),\qquad n=\sum d_{i}n_{i}^{2}\geqslant 2,\qquad m=\sum d_{i}n_{i}.

In the notation of Theorem
1.10
, the
following estimates hold.

(1)

Suppose that
p\nmid m
.

•

If
\eta
is trivial on
\ker(\nu)
, then

\left|{\rm IK}_{M}(a,\chi)+\rho(a)\frac{\#M^{\times}}{q(q-1)}\right|\leqslant 2m\cdot q^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

•

If
\eta
is nontrivial on
\ker(\nu)
, then

|{\rm IK}_{M}(a,\chi)|\leqslant 2m\cdot q^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

(2)

Suppose that
p\mid m
, and that either
p>2
or

m\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
.

•

If
\eta
is trivial on
\ker(\nu)
, then

\left|{\rm IK}_{M}(a,\chi)+\rho(a)\frac{\#M^{\times}}{q(q-1)}\right|\leqslant m\cdot q^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

•

If
\eta
is nontrivial on
\ker(\nu)
, then

|{\rm IK}_{M}(a,\chi)|\leqslant m\cdot q^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

In the wild case
p\mid m
, the estimate is stronger: the
coefficient is
m
, not
2m
, as in the tame case
p\nmid m
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

We shall begin by considering the case where
k=1
and no
multiplicative character is present. The following lemma is due to Kim

[
9
]
, but we include the
proof for completeness. The presentation is slightly different from
Kim’s proof, which uses an induction procedure.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
F=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}
, let

\psi_{F}=\psi\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{F/\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, and let

Q=q^{d}
. For
e\geqslant 1
and
y\in F^{\times}
,

(2.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\in\mathrm{GL}_{e}(F)\\
\det A=y\end{subarray}}\psi_{F}(\operatorname{Tr}A)=Q^{\binom{e}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\ldots,x_{e}\in F^{\times}\\
x_{1}\cdots x_{e}=y\end{subarray}}\psi_{F}(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{e}).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{e}
be the group of invertible upper
triangular matrices, and let
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
be the subgroup of upper
triangular matrices whose diagonal entries are all
1
. Thus an
element of
\mathcal{U}
has the form

1+\sum_{r<s}u_{rs}E_{rs}.

For
w\in S_{e}
, choose a monomial matrix
\dot{w}
, meaning a
matrix with exactly one nonzero entry in each row and each column,
whose nonzero entries occur in the same positions as the permutation
matrix of
w
. We choose these nonzero entries so that

\det\dot{w}=1
, and take
\dot{1}=1
. Concretely, one may start
with the usual permutation matrix and rescale one of its nonzero
entries by the inverse of its determinant.

In this matrix setting, the Bruhat decomposition says that

\mathrm{GL}_{e}(F)=\bigsqcup_{w\in S_{e}}\mathcal{U}_{w}(F)\dot{w}\mathcal{B}(F),

where
\mathcal{U}_{w}
is a subset of
\mathcal{U}
, described as follows. Write
w

in one-line notation

w=(w(1),w(2),\ldots,w(e)).

An
inversion
of
w
is a pair of positions
a<b
for which

w(a)>w(b)
. For each such inversion, allow one free
upper-triangular entry in position
(w(b),w(a))
. These are indeed
upper-triangular positions, since
w(b)<w(a)
. All other
upper-triangular entries are set equal to
0
. Thus

\mathcal{U}_{w}=\left\{1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a<b\\
w(a)>w(b)\end{subarray}}u_{ab}E_{w(b),w(a)}:u_{ab}\in F\right\}.

For example, if
e=3
and
w=(2,3,1)
, the inversions are

(1,3)
and
(2,3)
, and

\mathcal{U}_{w}(F)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&u&v\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}:u,v\in F\right\}.

The number of free coordinates in
\mathcal{U}_{w}
is therefore the number of
inversions of
w
, denoted
\ell(w)
, and

\#\mathcal{U}_{w}(F)=Q^{\ell(w)}
.

Denote the left-hand side of (
2.2
) by
R_{y}
.
The disjoint union above gives the cell decomposition

R_{y}=\sum_{w\in S_{e}}R_{y,w},

where each summand is

R_{y,w}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathcal{U}_{w}(F),\,b\in\mathcal{B}(F)\\
\det(u\dot{w}b)=y\end{subarray}}\psi_{F}(\operatorname{Tr}(u\dot{w}b)).

Fix
w\in S_{e}
. Since
\det\dot{w}=1
, the condition
\det(u\dot{w}b)=y
is simply

\det b=y
. Also

\operatorname{Tr}(u\dot{w}b)=\operatorname{Tr}(b(u\dot{w}))
, and for
each fixed
u
, the change of variables
c=bu
preserves
\mathcal{B}(F)

and the determinant. Hence

R_{y,w}=\#\mathcal{U}_{w}(F)\,S_{y}(\dot{w}),

where, for
X\in\mathrm{GL}_{e}(F)
and
\alpha\in F^{\times}
, we put

S_{\alpha}(X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mathcal{B}(F)\\
\det b=\alpha\end{subarray}}\psi_{F}(\operatorname{Tr}(bX)).

To compute
S_{y}(\dot{w})
, expand the trace. If
X=(X_{rs})
and
b=(b_{rs})
, then

\operatorname{Tr}(bX)=\sum_{r}b_{rr}X_{rr}+\sum_{r<s}b_{rs}X_{sr}.

This is the key point: each entry below the diagonal of
X
appears
as the coefficient of one freely varying upper-triangular entry of

b
.

Now suppose
w\neq 1
. Then
\dot{w}
has a nonzero entry below the
diagonal, say in position
(s,r)
with
s>r
. In the trace
formula above, the variable
b_{rs}
therefore occurs with
coefficient
X_{sr}\neq 0
. Summing over
b_{rs}\in F
, we get

\sum_{b_{rs}\in F}\psi_{F}(X_{sr}\,b_{rs})=0

by linear independence of characters.
Hence
S_{y}(\dot{w})=0
and
R_{y,w}=0
for every
w\neq 1
.

It remains to treat the identity cell
w=1
. Here
\dot{1}=1
, so no upper off-diagonal variable
appears in
\operatorname{Tr}(b)
. Only the diagonal entries of

b
remain, and therefore

S_{y}(1)=Q^{\binom{e}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\ldots,x_{e}\in F^{\times}\\
x_{1}\cdots x_{e}=y\end{subarray}}\psi_{F}(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{e}).

Since
\#\mathcal{U}_{1}(F)=1
, the cell
w=1
contributes

S_{y}(1)
, which is exactly the right-hand side of
(
2.2
). All other cells contribute zero.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Write
F_{i}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{i}}}
,

\psi_{i}=\psi\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{F_{i}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, and

\mathrm{Norm}_{i}=\mathrm{Norm}_{F_{i}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}
. Grouping first by the
componentwise determinants
y_{i}=\det A_{i}
, we obtain

\mathrm{Kl}_{M}(a;\chi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y_{i}\in F_{i}^{\times}\\
\prod_{i}\mathrm{Norm}_{i}(y_{i})=a\end{subarray}}\prod_{i}\chi_{i}(y_{i})\,R_{i}(y_{i}),

where

R_{i}(y_{i})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A_{i}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}(F_{i})\\
\det A_{i}=y_{i}\end{subarray}}\psi_{i}(\operatorname{Tr}A_{i}).

Applying Lemma
2.1
to each factor gives

R_{i}(y_{i})=q^{d_{i}\binom{n_{i}}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i1},\ldots,x_{in_{i}}\in F_{i}^{\times}\\
x_{i1}\cdots x_{in_{i}}=y_{i}\end{subarray}}\psi_{i}(x_{i1}+\cdots+x_{in_{i}}).

Substituting and expanding the product over
i
yields

\mathrm{Kl}_{M}(a;\chi)=q^{\sum_{i}d_{i}\binom{n_{i}}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{ij}\in F_{i}^{\times}\\
\prod_{i}\mathrm{Norm}_{i}(x_{i1}\cdots x_{in_{i}})=a\end{subarray}}\prod_{i,j}\chi_{i}(x_{ij})\psi\!\left(\sum_{i}\mathrm{Tr}_{F_{i}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x_{i1}+\cdots+x_{in_{i}})\right).

The remaining sum is precisely
\mathrm{Kl}_{B^{\prime}}(a;\eta)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.10

We view
(B^{\prime})^{\times}
as the group of invertible diagonal matrices in

\prod_{i}\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{i}}})
, and let

\nu\colon(B^{\prime})^{\times}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
be the norm map. For
each factor, let
S_{n_{i}}
be the permutation group on

\{1,\ldots,n_{i}\}
, and set

W=S_{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{n_{k}}.

For
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})\in W
, write

\ell_{d}(w)=\sum_{i}d_{i}\,\ell(w_{i}),

where
\ell(w_{i})
is the inversion number of
w_{i}
in one-line
notation, as in Lemma
2.1
.
Put

N=\sum_{i}d_{i}\binom{n_{i}}{2}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
L\colon\mathbb{A}^{r}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a
nonconstant affine-linear function. Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathbb{A}^{r}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\\
L(z)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\psi(L(z)^{-1})=-q^{r-1}.

This is the inverted-character analogue of the cancellation identity

\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\psi(cz)=0
for
c\neq 0
.

Proof.

Every value of
L
occurs exactly
q^{r-1}
times. Hence the
sum equals

q^{r-1}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\psi(u^{-1})=q^{r-1}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\psi(u)=-q^{r-1}.

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

With notation as above, for every
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
,

(3.3)

\mathrm{IKl}_{M}(a;\chi)=q^{N}\,\mathrm{IKl}_{B^{\prime}}(a;\eta)-\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W\\
w\neq 1\end{subarray}}q^{N-1+\ell_{d}(w)}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in(B^{\prime})^{\times}\\
\nu(t)=a\end{subarray}}\eta(t).

The sum over
w\neq 1
is interpreted as zero if
W=\{1\}
.

Proof.

Write
F_{i}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{i}}}
. In the
i
-th factor

\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}(F_{i})
, let
\mathcal{B}_{i}
be the group of
invertible upper triangular matrices, and let
\mathcal{U}_{i}
be the
subgroup of upper triangular matrices with diagonal entries all equal
to
1
.

For each
w_{i}\in S_{n_{i}}
, choose a determinant-one monomial
representative
\dot{w}_{i}
, as in the proof of
Lemma
2.1
. For

w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})\in W
, put

\dot{w}=(\dot{w}_{1},\ldots,\dot{w}_{k})
. Also put

\mathcal{B}_{M}=\prod_{i}\mathcal{B}_{i}(F_{i})
. The product of the
matrix cell decompositions is the disjoint union

M^{\times}=\bigsqcup_{w\in W}\mathcal{U}_{w}\,\dot{w}\,\mathcal{B}_{M},

where

\mathcal{U}_{w}=\mathcal{U}_{w_{1}}(F_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{U}_{w_{k}}(F_{k}).

Here
\mathcal{U}_{w_{i}}(F_{i})\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{i}(F_{i})
is the
small affine space from Lemma
2.1
: for

w_{i}=(w_{i}(1),\ldots,w_{i}(n_{i}))
, one allows a free upper-triangular
entry
u_{w_{i}(b),w_{i}(a)}
for each inversion
a<b
with

w_{i}(a)>w_{i}(b)
, and sets all other upper off-diagonal entries to

0
. Thus

\#\mathcal{U}_{w}=q^{\sum_{i}d_{i}\ell(w_{i})}=q^{\ell_{d}(w)}.

Decompose the inverted sum accordingly:

(3.4)

\mathrm{IKl}_{M}(a;\chi)=\sum_{w\in W}\mathrm{IKl}_{w}(a;\chi),

where

\mathrm{IKl}_{w}(a;\chi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{w},\,b\in\mathcal{B}_{M}\\
\mathrm{Nrd}(u^{\prime}\dot{w}b)=a\\
\mathrm{Trd}(u^{\prime}\dot{w}b)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\chi(b)\psi(\mathrm{Trd}(u^{\prime}\dot{w}b)^{-1}).

The determinant-type character is unchanged by
u^{\prime}
and by the
determinant-one representative
\dot{w}
.

Fix
w
. Since the representatives
\dot{w}_{i}
have determinant

1
, the condition
\mathrm{Nrd}(u^{\prime}\dot{w}b)=a
is simply

\mathrm{Nrd}(b)=a
. Also, by cyclicity of the matrix trace in each factor,

\mathrm{Trd}(u^{\prime}\dot{w}b)=\mathrm{Trd}(bu^{\prime}\dot{w})
. For fixed

u^{\prime}
, the change of variables
c=bu^{\prime}
preserves

\mathcal{B}_{M}
, the reduced norm, and the character. Hence

\mathrm{IKl}_{w}(a;\chi)=\#\mathcal{U}_{w}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\mathcal{B}_{M}\\
\mathrm{Nrd}(c)=a\\
\mathrm{Trd}(c\dot{w})\neq 0\end{subarray}}\chi(c)\psi(\mathrm{Trd}(c\dot{w})^{-1}).

Every upper triangular matrix
c\in\mathcal{B}_{M}
decomposes uniquely
as
c=tu
, where
t\in(B^{\prime})^{\times}
is viewed as a diagonal
matrix and
u\in\mathcal{U}
is upper-unitriangular. Here

\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}_{1}(F_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{U}_{k}(F_{k})

is the full product of upper-unitriangular groups, an affine space of
dimension
N=\sum_{i}d_{i}\binom{n_{i}}{2}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Using
cyclicity once more,
\mathrm{Trd}(tu\dot{w})=\mathrm{Trd}(u\dot{w}t)
. Substituting

c=tu
gives

(3.5)

\mathrm{IKl}_{w}(a;\chi)=q^{\ell_{d}(w)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in(B^{\prime})^{\times}\\
\nu(t)=a\end{subarray}}\eta(t)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathcal{U}\\
\mathrm{Trd}(u\dot{w}t)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\psi(\mathrm{Trd}(u\dot{w}t)^{-1}).

When
w=1
,
\mathrm{Trd}(ut)=\mathrm{Trd}(t)
for every
u\in\mathcal{U}
.
Thus (
3.5
) gives

(3.6)

\mathrm{IKl}_{1}(a;\chi)=q^{N}\mathrm{IKl}_{B^{\prime}}(a;\eta).

If instead
w\neq 1
, then some
w_{i}
is a non-identity permutation,
so its monomial representative has a nonzero entry below the diagonal.
Say this entry is in row
s
, column
r
, with
s>r
. In the
factor
\mathcal{U}_{i}
, the coordinate
u_{rs}
is a free variable.
In this factor, put
Y_{i}=\dot{w}_{i}t_{i}
. Then

\operatorname{Tr}(u_{i}Y_{i})=\operatorname{Tr}(Y_{i})+\sum_{a<b}(u_{i})_{ab}(Y_{i})_{ba},

so
u_{rs}
occurs in
\operatorname{Tr}(u_{i}\dot{w}_{i}t_{i})
with
coefficient
(Y_{i})_{sr}\neq 0
. Here the corresponding entry of

\dot{w}_{i}
is nonzero, and every diagonal entry of
t_{i}
is nonzero.

After applying the field trace

\mathrm{Tr}_{F_{i}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, the resulting function of the

F_{i}
-variable
u_{rs}
is still nonconstant, because the pairing

(c,z)\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}_{F_{i}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(cz)
is nondegenerate. Thus

u\longmapsto\mathrm{Trd}(u\dot{w}t)

is a nonconstant affine-linear function on the
N
-dimensional

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-affine space
\mathcal{U}
. Lemma
3.1

therefore gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathcal{U}\\
\mathrm{Trd}(u\dot{w}t)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\psi(\mathrm{Trd}(u\dot{w}t)^{-1})=-q^{N-1}.

Substituting in (
3.5
) yields, for
w\neq 1
,

(3.7)

\mathrm{IKl}_{w}(a;\chi)=-q^{\ell_{d}(w)}q^{N-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in(B^{\prime})^{\times}\\
\nu(t)=a\end{subarray}}\eta(t).

Adding these contributions (
3.6
),(
3.7
) in
(
3.4
) gives

\mathrm{IKl}_{M}(a;\chi)=q^{N}\,\mathrm{IKl}_{B^{\prime}}(a;\eta)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W\\
w\neq 1\end{subarray}}q^{N-1+\ell_{d}(w)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in(B^{\prime})^{\times}\\
\nu(t)=a\end{subarray}}\eta(t).

This is (
3.3
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.10
.

For the first part of the theorem, assume that
\eta
is nontrivial
on
\ker(\nu)
. For
w\neq 1
, the fiber
\nu^{-1}(a)
is a
coset of the finite subgroup
\ker(\nu)\subseteq(B^{\prime})^{\times}
.
Since
\eta
is nontrivial on
\ker(\nu)
, character orthogonality
on this finite group gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in(B^{\prime})^{\times}\\
\nu(t)=a\end{subarray}}\eta(t)=\sum_{t\in\nu^{-1}(a)}\eta(t)=0.

Hence the second term in Proposition
3.2

vanishes, giving the claimed formula.

For the second part, assume that
\eta
is trivial on
\ker(\nu)
, so

\eta=\rho\circ\nu
for a character

\rho
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. Then every element
t
with

\nu(t)=a
has the same
\eta
-value, namely
\rho(a)
. Hence

\sum_{\nu(t)=a}\eta(t)=\rho(a)\#\nu^{-1}(a)=\rho(a)\frac{\#(B^{\prime})^{\times}}{q-1}.

This gives the equivalent formula

\mathrm{IKl}_{M}(a;\chi)=q^{N}\mathrm{IKl}_{B^{\prime}}(a;\eta)-\rho(a)\frac{\#(B^{\prime})^{\times}}{q-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W\\
w\neq 1\end{subarray}}q^{N-1+\ell_{d}(w)}.

Finally,

\#\mathcal{B}_{M}=\#(B^{\prime})^{\times}q^{N},\qquad\#M^{\times}=\#\mathcal{B}_{M}\sum_{w\in W}q^{\ell_{d}(w)}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{\#(B^{\prime})^{\times}}{q-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W\\
w\neq 1\end{subarray}}q^{N-1+\ell_{d}(w)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\#(B^{\prime})^{\times}q^{N-1}}{q-1}\left(\sum_{w\in W}q^{\ell_{d}(w)}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\#\mathcal{B}_{M}}{q(q-1)}\left(\frac{\#M^{\times}}{\#\mathcal{B}_{M}}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\#M^{\times}-\#\mathcal{B}_{M}}{q(q-1)}.

Substituting this into (
3.3
) proves the second
formula and completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

One may put these sums in a broader representation-theoretic form. Let

G
be a reductive group over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let

\delta\colon G\to\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
be a one-dimensional
representation, and let
\rho\colon G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
be another
finite-dimensional representation. A natural Kloosterman sum attached
to this data is

\mathrm{Kl}_{G,\rho,\delta}(a)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in G(\mathbb{F}_{q})\\
\delta(g)=a\end{subarray}}\psi(\operatorname{Tr}(\rho(g))),\qquad a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}.

One may also insert a multiplicative character of
G(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

In this paper, the sums we consider are associated to the group

G=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{Res}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_{i}}}}\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}.

Note that
G\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

is isomorphic to a direct product

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\underbrace{\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}\times\cdots\times\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}}_{d_{i}\text{ copies}}.

The character
\delta
is induced by the determinant morphisms, and

\rho
is given by the direct sum of the standard representations of
the factors.

Such Kloosterman sums associated to reductive groups and their
representations do
not
satisfy square-root cancellation in
general. The point is that non-identity Bruhat cells may contribute.
For instance, take
G=\mathrm{GL}_{4}
, let
\delta=\det
, and let

\rho=\wedge^{2}
be the second exterior power of the standard
representation. If
U
is the upper-unitriangular subgroup,

t=\operatorname{diag}(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})
, and
w=(12)(34)
, then
for the corresponding permutation matrix
\dot{w}
one computes

\operatorname{Tr}(\wedge^{2}(u\dot{w}t))=-t_{1}t_{2}-t_{3}t_{4}+t_{1}t_{3}u_{12}u_{34},\qquad u\in U.

Hence

\sum_{u\in U(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\psi(\operatorname{Tr}(\wedge^{2}(u\dot{w}t)))=q^{5}\psi(-t_{1}t_{2}-t_{3}t_{4}),

which is not zero.

Thus the vanishing of the non-identity cells in the ordinary
Kloosterman sum considered above is a special feature of the reduced
trace on products of general linear groups, not a general phenomenon
for arbitrary representations.

On the other hand, if one allows matrix coefficients in the definition,
then, as a general feature of the
\ell
-adic Fourier transform,
square-root cancellation does hold if the matrix
coefficient is generic. See
[
5
]
.
