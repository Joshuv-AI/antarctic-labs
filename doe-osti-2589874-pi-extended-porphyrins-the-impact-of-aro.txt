3. Executive Summary
 Largely π-extended carbon-rich materials represent fascinating yet challenging frontiers in
 chemistry and materials science as it offers numerous opportunities in molecular electronics. pi-
 Extended porphyrins possess unique set of electronic and photophysical properties and are very
 attractive for a wide range of applications. However, the synthesis and functionalization of pi-
 extended porphyrins had been very challenging. To explore and expand this intriguing research
 field, it is essential to develop synthetic tools to make them accessible. Funded by the Department
 of Energy Basic Energy Science, my laboratory addressed these synthetic challenges by
 developing concise and versatile methods for pi-extended porphyrins. Significant progress has
 been made in multiple directions, including fusing Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons (PAH),
 aromatic heterocycles, antiaromatic component, and cross-conjugated component to porphyrins
 as well as push-pull porphyrins. The availability of these methods has made it possible to rationally
 design and synthesize functionalized pi-extended porphyrins. Unprecedented electronic and
 photophysical properties have been discovered for these pi-extended porphyrins.
4. Summary of Project Activities
 The overall hypothesis of the project is: fusion of aromatic components to the porphyrin periphery
 will impact the electronic and photophysical properties of the resulting pi-extended porphyrin
 systems.
 Overall approaches used: development of concise and versatile synthetic tools through
 functionalization of porphyrins at the beta, beta-positions.
 Selected research highlights and achievements are included in the following section.
 1) Acene-fused porphyrins: the impact of porphyrin on the stability of acenes.
 Acenes holds huge potentials in new technologies ranging from energy storage, organic
 electronics to catalysis due to their exceptional charge mobility. Organic electronic materials
 based on acenes show promise to make revolutionary transformations in a range of applications
 such as photovoltaics, organic field-effect transistors (OFETs), light emitting devices (OLEDs).
 However, one critical problem associated with higher acenes is their low stability. For
 example, the half lifetime of pentacene is in the range of several minutes in most organic solvents.

 1

  We developed a new strategy to stabilize pentacene through fusing bulky nickel porphyrins to
  pentacene at porphyrin beta, beta-positions. The porphyrin-fused pentacene displayed unusually
  high stability with an estimated half-life of > 945 h (39 d) (Angew. Chem. Int. Ed., 2020, 45 (132),
  20250-20257. https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1882798); ACS Phys. Chem. Au, 2022, 2, 6, 468-
  481. https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1879136). These new strategies pave the way to stabilize
  higher acenes such as octacene and nonacene, the electronic properties of which are mainly
  studied computationally. Very recently, ammonia sensors constructed from porphyrin-fused
  pentacene achieved exception-ally high sensitivity with a limit of detection (LOD) of 83 ppb (ACS
  Sens, 2024, 9 (2), 883-894 https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/2589710, and ACS Sens, 2025,
  accepted). The acene- and other PAH-fused porphyrins have also displayed much broadened
  and red-shifted absorptions, significantly enhancing porphyrins’ light harvesting ability (Org. Lett.
  2020, 22, 18, 7078–7082. https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/acs.orglett.0c02313).
2) Aromatic heterocycle-fused porphyrins: the impact of aromatic heterocycles on the
 photophysical properties of porphyrins
 Incorporation of heteroatom(s) into largely π-extended structures can provide control of the
  electronic structure and has become a critical strategy to manipulate the materials to possess
  desirable properties, such as electrochemical activity, optical absorption spectra,
  photoluminescence, redox behavior, charge transfer, bandgap tuning, and other characteristics.
  The development of new methods to introduce heteroatoms or heterocycles is very important to
  facilitate the growth of this field. On the other hand, photo-induced electron transfer (ET) process
  plays a pivotal role in light-to-energy conversion in nature and in artificial light conversion systems
  for photovoltaics, photosynthesis, and photocatalysis. High CS efficiency and long-lived CS state
  are found to be critical to realize efficient solar energy conversion. Despite significant efforts to
  develop artificial molecular systems mimicking the natural photosynthetic reaction centers, it
  remains a challenge to bring the lifetimes of CS states to the microseconds (µs) range.
  Recently, we have demonstrated that fusion of a benzoimidazoisoindole component at porphyrin
  b, b-positions can remarkably lengthen the CS lifetime of to 30-40 µs (Chem. Sci., 2022, 13, 9880.
  https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1882798, Highlighted in Inside Cover Page. Selected in
  Emerging Frontiers in Aromaticity; ChemSusChem, 2023, 16, e202202289.
  https://doi.org/10.1002/cssc.202202289. Selected in Advances in Engineering). Our results
  suggest that fusion of aromatic heterocycles can change their electronic and photophysical
  properties and significantly influence the ET process of the corresponding D-A systems. These
  results further indicate: 1) unconventional structural factor can promote long-lived CS state
  through facilitating the ISC process; 2) beta-functionalization of porphyrin may lead to new
  scenarios of ET process.
 Other heterocycles such as thiophene- and azepine-fused porphyrins have also displayed
  unconventional properties of porphyrins such as reversed trend of fluorescence quantum yield
  (Chem. Eur. J., 2023, e202302013l https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1999286; J. Porphyrin
  Phthalocyanine, 2025, accepted) and extremely short fluorescence lifetime and large Stokes
  shifts (manuscript to be submitted).
3) Oligomeric porphyrin systems: the competition between energy transfer and electron
 transfer

 2

 Manipulating energy and electron transfer is crucial because these processes are
fundamental to life and technology. Controlled energy and electron movement drive everything
from biological metabolism and photosynthesis to artificial solar cells, batteries, and advanced
electronics. Controlling these transfers is not an easy task. We have developed several dimeric
and trimeric porphyrins systems based on p-extended porphyrins. By systematically varying the
type of linker (non-conjugated, linearly conjugated, and cross-conjugated) and metal, we can
effectively manipulate both energy transfer and electron transfer (ChemSusChem, 2023, 16 (8),
e202202289. https://doi.org/10.1002/cssc.202202289. Selected in Advances in Engineering; J.
Phys. Chem. C, 2019, 123, 36, 22066-22073. https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/2589712; Chem.
Eur. J., 2017, 23, 12805-12814. Highlighted in Inside Cover Page; Chem. Commun., 2024, 60,
10696-10699. https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2024/cc/d4cc03102d).
4) Push-pull porphyrin systems
 Push-pull molecular systems are important for their ability to tune optoelectronic properties,
enabling a range of applications like nonlinear optics (NLO), organic solar cells (OSCs), and
fluorescent sensors. Almost all push-pull molecular systems are constructed through the meso-
positions of porphyrins. Our research developed push-pull molecular systems through porphyrin
b-positions, which displayed much enhanced solar cell stability through attaching two linker
groups, which are difficult to achieve through meso-positions. Our push-pull porphyrins have also
demonstrated high power conversion efficiency comparable to the best currently available dye (J.
Mater.              Chem.               A            2021,             9,           27692-27700.
https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2021/ta/d1ta07814c; J. Mater. Chem. A, 2019, 7,
10712-10722.       https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2019/ta/c9ta01846h/unauth;    J.
Porphyrins Phthalocyanines, 2019, 23, 599-610. https://doi.org/10.1142/S1088424619500536).
We have also built other push-pull systems that showed interesting electronic and photophysical
properties (Chem. Commun., 2018, 54, 5303-5306. https://doi.org/10.1039/C7CC09743C; Can.
J. Chem., 2018, 96 (9), 881-889. https://doi.org/10.1139/cjc-2017-0416; Phys. Chem. Chem.
Phys., 2017, 19, 13182-13188. https://doi.org/10.1039/C7CP00024C; Chem. Eur. J., 2025,
e202404165. https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/2589701; J. Phys. Chem. A., 2023,127(43), 9040-
9051. https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/2589712; etc.)
