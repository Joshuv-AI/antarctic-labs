Analysis of psychomotor latency, spatial reasoning, and metabolic health markers in adolescent gamers: A comparative study of the impact of the confounding triad of sleep, diet, and screen modality
Authors: Saksham Shah, Reshu Gupta, Garvita Mathur
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:The purpose of this research is to understand the complex relationships of the intensity of electronic gaming and the neurocognitive (Reaction Time, IQ, Spatial IQ) and bodily (BMI, Skinfolds, Visual Acuity) components. Of particular interest was stating for the first time the novelty of the ‘Confounding Triad’ of sleep, food (caloric) gaming, and the specific screen type of the gaming interface.MATERIALS AND METHODS:A comparative cross-sectional study was carried out on a stratified random sample of 200 male adolescents (ages 13 to 21) from northern India. Participants were stratified into gamers (≥5 hours/week, n = 122) and non-gamers (<5 hours/week, n = 78). The psychomotor speed was measured using a digital Audio-Visual Reaction Timer, IQ was measured using the Stanford-Binet Intelligence Scale, and Spatial Quotient via online standardized battery, while subcutaneous adiposity was measured using Harpenden skinfold calipers. The confounders were measured using the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), a 24-hour dietary recall, and Non-Gaming Screen Time (NGST) logs. Mann–Whitney U tests were used for group comparisons, and Generalized Linear Models (GLM) and Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA) were used to determine independent effects.RESULTS:Compared to non-gamers, gamers displayed faster rates across Auditory, Visual, and Aim Reaction Times (P < 0.001) and better Spatial Quotient (P = 0.001). The GLM for Visual Reaction Time demonstrated a dose-dependent improvement for hours spent playing games (β = −2.15, P < 0.001) that was present even when considering the negative effect on performance from poor sleep (β = +4.35 per unit increase in PSQI, P < 0.001). Gaming status was significant for the ANCOVA for skinfold thickness (P = 0.035), but dietary caloric intake was the more important variable (η2 = 0.19, P < 0.001). No significant difference was found in IQ (P = 0.36) or in visual acuity.CONCLUSION:The findings indicate that regular practice of interactive gaming provides players with better cognitive and psychomotor skills, even when sleep deprivation is considered. With that said, dietary surplus imposes greater metabolic hazards than gaming behaviors. Therefore, health interventions are encouraged not only to regulate gaming hours but to prioritize the teaching of digital hygiene.

Introduction

The early 21st-century digital era has seen significant shifts due to the rapid adoption of electronic gaming. No longer the niche pastime of a small number of users, electronic gaming has become a primary method of cognitive and social engagement for more than 3.2 billion people worldwide.[1] The most notable demographic shift is the adolescent category, due to the digital integration and the active immersion during the critical periods[2] of the developmental stages of neuroplasticity.[3] Unlike traditional forms of media, such as television or social media, where users remain passive, today’s active focus video games require players to construct and utilize a coherent action plan,[4] engage in rapid central nervous system interactions that sustain high-voltage visual and cognitive stimuli, and respond to visual and motor demands on the order of milliseconds.[5] This unique form of cognitive loading is theorized to result in significant neuroadaptive changes, particularly among the visual streams and oculo-motor systems that together enhance cognitive functioning.[6789]

While recognizing the likely benefits of screen time, the majority of the public health sector focuses on the negatives, primarily viewing electronic gaming as a sedentary activity that leads to metabolic dysregulation, disturbed sleep, and obesity,[10] with increasing evidence also linking excessive screen exposure to adverse cognitive and physiological outcomes.[11] A significant overall methodological flaw of the majority of papers on this topic is the lack of differentiation between the physiological effects of the gaming interface and the behavioral byproducts of the gaming lifestyle. This research begins to remedy this through the segregation of the “Confounding Triad” of sleep, caloric intake, and screen time. Until these variables are accounted for, the question of whether the unhealthy gamer phenotype is a result of biology or is a consequence of habitual dysregulation remains unanswered.

This study is the first of multivariate analyses to separate, for the purposes of clarity, sedentary energy expenditure and caloric intake together, which reveal the potential cognitive and metabolic effects of interactive gaming. By elucidating the relative effects of gaming and hyper-caloric snacking on cognitive load, this research moves away from the dualistic and probably politically charged narrative, “Gaming is Bad” or “Gaming is Good”, to an evidence-based, more sophisticated narrative. This is fundamental for the design of health promotion activities that are congruent with the behavioral tendencies of adolescents.

For this research, we utilized a multi-faceted conceptual framework that marries the PASS (Planning, Attention, Simultaneous, and Successive) cognitive processing model[12] and the socioecological model of digital well-being. In the PASS model, cognitive tasks are divided and processed in three functional neurological units: the brain stem (arousal and attention), posterior cortex (simultaneous and successive), and frontal lobes (planning and executive control). Action gaming predominantly engages the Attention-Arousal and Successive units, requiring intense focus and a series of motor skills.[6]

Moreover, the recipient’s socioecological viewpoint considers the user-driven influence on the engineering of the digital ecosystem, and the user’s biological influence on the digital device system. We employed an Input-Process-Output framework to articulate these relations: (a) Input Factors included biological dimorphism, the user’s age, and the specific game elements, genre, and level of interactivity (b) The Process (The Confounding Triad) refers to sleep quality, gaming dietary behavior, and active to passive gameplay screen time ratios during the gaming process, and (c) Output refers to health status indicators, which are categorized neurocognitive deficits and overall poor physical health, and health status indicators regarding latency and spatial reasoning (deficits), and poor adiposity and cardiovascular fitness (indicators). This framework enables the researcher to evaluate the hypothesis that the cognitive correlates of gaming are due to neural exercise and the lifestyle mediators are the metabolic risks.

Study strength and novelty

The primary strength of this study lies in its methodological rigor, particularly the use of stratified random sampling from a number of different locations, which will help generalize the findings of this study to urban North Indian male adolescents. This study is the first within its area to isolate the confounding triad as a novel contribution, and is therefore the first to do so to incorporate a more causal approach to analyzing the effects of gaming beyond simple correlational analysis. In addition, the use of advanced technology with a 120 HZ display to measure reaction time is a first of its kind and thus represents a more sophisticated approach to measuring psychomotor performance digitally.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

A cross-sectional comparative study design was utilized to concurrently assess gaming experience and health effects in a heterogeneous sample of male adolescents. Data collection was done in October-November 2023 at secondary and tertiary schools in Northern India. The cultural context of the study is unique because while young people in urban India are rapidly adopting a globalized digital culture, they continue to follow specific social and dietary practices. The institutional setting made it possible to recruit a study sample that was homogeneous in terms of level of education, work demands, and daily activities, thus reducing the variability caused by different lifestyle factors [Figure 1].

Study participants and sampling

In this study, 200 male participants were recruited through stratified random sampling in the age range of 13–21 years. Choosing to solely include male participants is a methodological consideration to examine the effect of gaming on psychomotor latency with an undiluted biological focus. A considerable amount of research indicates that reaction time and the associated cognitive processes demonstrate significant sexual dimorphism,[13] with males exhibiting faster motor responses, though they may have different sensitivities[14] to certain metabolic stressors. By exclusively recruiting male participants, the study is designed to avoid biological moderation that would mask the primary research focus of the study; the impact of gaming on cognitive speed.

Participant stratification

With the structured activity log created by the participants a week prior to the main data collection, it was possible to quantify the gaming behavior of the participants. The threshold of the ‘Gamer’ cohort was set to 5 hours a week for 6 consecutive months, justified by neuroplasticity studies in which measurable structural/functional changes in the brain’s motor and visual pathways occur above this duration.

Gamers (n = 122): Meet exposure threshold and gaming practice usually exceeds 14 hours/week.

Non-Gamers (n = 78): Reported gaming practice below 5 hours a week or none.

The structured activity logs helped mitigate the impact of recall bias. Participants reported the exact times and durations of all screen activities and the research team classified ‘active’ gaming and ‘passive’ non-gaming screen time (NGST) activities, which included social media and video streaming.

Data collection tool and technique

The collection of data was streamlined using standardized and validated physiological and neurocognitive instruments, which ensured higher reliability and comparability across studies.

Neurocognitive measurements

Based on prior data, Reaction Time (RT) is described as Psychomotor Reaction Time, recorded using a digital Audio-Visual Reaction Timer from Anand Agencies (Pune, India).[16] The four-digit chronoscope has a least count of 1 ms, and it has been cross-validated against an electronic signal delay test, which has a measurement of accuracy of ±1 ms.[17] The testing protocol consisted of:

Audio-visual and RT: Participants pressed a button with their dominant hand in response to a pure tone auditory stimulus.

Audio-visual and RT: Participants pressed a button with their dominant hand in response to a pure tone auditory stimulus.

Visual RT: Participants pressed a button with their dominant hand in response to an LED stimulus.

Visual RT: Participants pressed a button with their dominant hand in response to an LED stimulus.

For each modality, three trials were conducted, and the final data point was the average of each to reduce intra-individual variability.

Aim Reaction Time: To obtain a measure of hand-eye coordination and asynchronous RT relevant to gaming, a purpose-built application was used on an Apple iPad Pro 11 (4th Generation) due to its 120 Hz screen and high touch-input sampling rate, which means it has minimal hardware-induced latency. Participants were required to tap each of 30 target stimuli, randomly distributed across the screen, as quickly as possible.

Intelligence and Spatial Reasoning: The Stanford-Binet Intelligence Scale (Online Adaptation) was used to evaluate IQ,[18] a validated instrument for assessing fluid reasoning and working memory.

Spatial Quotient (SQ) was measured using a standardized reasoning battery that involved mental rotation and 3D visualization tasks.

Physiological and anthropometric markers

Body Mass Index (BMI): BMI was calculated based on standard protocols (BMI = weight (kg)/height (m2)).[19] Weight was measured using a digital scale (0.1 kg accuracy) and height was measured using a stadiometer (0.1 cm accuracy).

Skinfold Thickness: Subcutaneous abdominal fat was measured using the Harpenden Skinfold Calipers (constant measuring pressure of 10 gm/mm²) to move beyond the restrictions of BMI which does not differentiate between fat and lean mass.[20] This caliper gives a clear representation of the metabolic risk of adolescents.

Visual Acuity: Distance vision was evaluated in a 6-meter range using standardized Snellen charts under controlled lighting.

Specific measures for the “Confounding Triad” were formulated as follows:

Sleep Quality (PSQI): The Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI)[21] takes into account seven different areas of sleep structure over the last month, giving an overall score where >5 suggests clinically significant sleep disturbance.

Dietary Estimation: A 24-hour dietary recall was used to estimate the total caloric intake for the day. The values were based on the standard Indian food composition tables of the Indian Council of Medical Research - National Institute of Nutrition (ICMR-NIN).[22]

Statistical analysis method

The IBM Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 26.0[23] (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) was used for statistical analysis. All continuous data distributions were assessed for normality using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Normally distributed data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Non-normally distributed data (reaction time, SQ, and skinfold thickness) are expressed as median and interquartile range (IQR).

The independent samples t-test was used for the between-group comparison for normally distributed variables, while the Mann–Whitney U test was used for the independent samples comparison for nonparametric data.[24] To analyze only the specific effects of gaming intensity on visual reaction time, a generalized linear model (GLM) was performed with gaming hours as the main predictor and Age, PSQI score, and NGST as continuous covariates to obtain independent beta coefficients (β). The metabolic markers were assessed using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) with caloric intake and sleep quality as covariates, and effect sizes were reported as partial eta squared (n2).[25] All statistical tests were performed two-tailed, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Out of 200 male adolescents with an average age of 16.4 ± 2.2 years, given that age is a key stage for cognitive and physical development, the demographic analysis shows that both the gamer and non-gamer groups were comparable for age (P = 0.460). However, the groups diverged significantly for several other lifestyle behaviors. Gamers were more exposed to weekly gaming (14.8 ± 8.5 vs. 1.1 ± 1.5 hours, P < 0.001), and were also likely to have higher non-gaming screen time (4.6 ± 1.3 vs. 3.8 ± 1.4 hours/day, P < 0.030).

During the analysis of sleep and diet, significant lifestyle stressors were associated with the “gamer phenotype”. The gamer cohort had higher global PSQI scores (6.8 ± 2.4) vs. non-gamers (4.2 ± 1.6, P < 0.001). All of the average gamers thus were in the clinical range for poor sleep quality (PSQI > 5).[21] Moreover, gamers also estimated daily caloric intake to be significantly higher[26] (2650 ± 420 vs. 2280 ± 340 kcal, P < 0.001) which supports the results that gaming behaviors and positive energy balance are associated [Table 1].

Neurocognitive outcomes

Gamers displayed significantly shorter reaction times in all measured modalities (Audio, Visual, and Aim RTs). This finding is part of a more comprehensive study that used the Mann-Whitney U test and reported latency in response times of gamers repeatedly being lower than that of non-gamers (P < 0.001). In addition, gamers demonstrated a significantly greater Spatial Quotient (P = 0.001),[27] indicating that the benefits of gaming transfer to higher-order reasoning.[28] However, no statistically significant variation in Intelligence Quotient was noted (P = 0.36). This indicates that gaming does not enhance general «g-factor» intelligence, but rather enhances processing speed and visuospatial mapping in particular [Table 2].

Independent effects and the confounding triad

A generalized linear model (GLM) was used to tackle the Confounding Triad and measure the independent effect of hours of gaming on visual reaction time (VRT) with sleep quality (PSQI), age, and non-gaming screen time held constant. The model produced a strong, negative, gaming hours coefficient (β = −2.15, P < 0.001), indicating that for each hour of gaming per week, reaction time was faster (improved) by 2.15 ms.

On the other hand, the effect of sleep quality on performance was significant and degrading. Reaction times were slower by 4.35 ms with every 1 unit increase in PSQI score (β = +4.35, P < 0.001). Importantly, non-gaming screen time (NGST) was found to have no significant effect on RT (β = +0.85, P = 0.48), thus demonstrating that the cognitive benefits of gaming are not due to screen time, but due to the gaming interface [Table 3].

Metabolic risk and adiposity

The analysis centered around metabolism and abdominal skinfold thickness to subcutaneously measure fat more accurately than BMI. Using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA), we found that gaming status (Gamer vs. Non-Gamer) was significantly linked to adiposity (P = 0.035); however, the effect was not very noticeable (Partial η2 = 0.04). In comparison, estimated daily caloric intake was the overwhelmingly dominant predictor, explaining 19% of the unique variance in skinfold thickness (Partial η2 = 0.19, P < 0.001). This shows that the metabolic risk usually associated with gaming is not related to the activity, but due to the caloric excess from dietary habits [Table 4].

Discussion

The core finding of this investigation, the dominant reaction time of gamers, participates in and broadens the most recent studies in digital neurobiology. Gamers were raw latency responders, 50–60 ms faster than average in all modalities. This difference in response time means a lot in high-stakes environments, such as in surgery or high-speed sports. Given the high-end standardization and 120 Hz Aim RT metrics tablet, the result captures the synthesis of visual detection, cognitive assessment, and motor response, especially of the 3-part response metrics described.

Resilience and cognitive reserve

Perhaps the most significant insight generated by this study is the resilience of reaction time among gamers, especially in the presence of clinically significant sleep deficits. In general, sleep deprivation, especially when it leads to adenosine accumulation in the basal forebrain, causes global cortical slowing, which is intertwined with a net downward revision of attention span.[29] Therefore, gamers (mean PSQI 6.8) should have outperformed the non-gamers (mean PSQI 4.2), especially considering that the non-gamers had high levels of sleep. It seems then that, in addition to this, gamers have also developed an efficiency enhancement in neural transmission along the sensorimotor integration pathways that is distinct and is called a Cognitive Reserve.[30]

We propose that long-term high-speed gaming causes the dorsal visual stream and parietal cortex to functionally hypertrophy.[31] Thus, the brain learns to ignore stimuli and process them faster even when the baseline arousal is reduced due to tiredness.

Gamers show an average Spatial Quotient (SQ) score that is higher than their average Intelligence Quotient (IQ) score. This indicates that gaming is not a cognitive booster in the general sense. Instead, it suggests that gaming enhances cognitive processes specifically related to visual-spatial mapping and rapid cognitive visualization. These processes are critical for the effective use of advanced three-dimensional virtual environments. This is significant considering the close correlation between spatial visualization ability and the professions of engineering, architecture, and laparroscopic surgery. It indicates that the potential value of high-altitude gaming in education is significantly understated.

Dismantling the obese gamer narrative

The metabolic modeling in ANCOVA analysis is an important counter to the overly simplistic narrative of gaming and health. It was found that metabolic risk was not associated with video gaming, but rather an outcome of the “Confounding Triad”.[32] After controlling for caloric intake and video gaming, the data showed that the variance for lipid accumulation that caloric intake explained was almost five times greater than that of gaming status. This confirms that sedentary behavior is not a primary risk factor until combined with hypercaloric intake.[33] This has also been found with other cohorts, like the UK and Mexico, where excessive screen time and high BMI are associated, but are the result of irregular sleep times and an increase in the consumption of sugary drinks.[34]

The proposal of the “Sympathetic Suppression” hypothesis was a result of the observed paradoxical dip in BMI among the heaviest gamers (>25 hours/week). It is suggested that the extreme adrenergic surge associated with high-stakes competitive play may inhibit appetite centers in the hypothalamus, contrasting with the hedonic snacking observed in casual or moderate gamers. Although it may be a speculative theory, it shows the importance of future focus longitudinal neuroendocrine studies. The relationship between gaming and metabolic outcomes is complex and remains not fully understood.

Screen modality and interactivity

This study has dismantled the homogeneous interpretation of “screen time” by hanging the Non-Gaming Screen Time (NGST) variable constant. The absence of a significant relationship between NGST and Reaction Time is evidence that the cognitive gains are a result of interactivity.

Watching a stream is passive, and it doesn’t train the brain. However, playing the game actively does. This leaves the potential cognitive benefits of interactive gameplay excluded from public health initiatives.

Suggestions for further research

Further research should also focus on shifting from cross-sectional correlations to longitudinal intervention research, randomized controlled trials (RCTs). The hypothesized mechanisms of long-term potentiation (LTP) and functional hypertrophy would provide insight through the use of controlled doses of targeted game types (e.g., sandbox games vs. MOBAs) to non-gamers and the concurrent measure of their neurophysiological activity via fMRI or EEG.

For the next era of research, the use of the gold-standard objective physiological tools is also key. Future research should use the following:

PSQI recalls should also be ignored while the actigraphy or polysomnography measures objectively record sleep durations and fragmentation.

PSQI recalls should also be ignored while the actigraphy or polysomnography measures objectively record sleep durations and fragmentation.

The ‘Competitive Gamer Hypophagia’ hypothesis can be effectively tested by using DEXA scanning to objectively measure visceral fat and assess lean versus adipose tissue.

The ‘Competitive Gamer Hypophagia’ hypothesis can be effectively tested by using DEXA scanning to objectively measure visceral fat and assess lean versus adipose tissue.

During gaming, research should assess sympathetic suppression by measuring the neuroendocrine profiles of cortisol and catecholamine levels in the game to investigate sympathetic suppression.

Finally, it is important to repeat this research in female and different age groups to identify possible sexual dimorphism and age-related specificity in the cognitive and metabolic changes connected with electronic gaming.

Conclusion

This study makes an extensive correction to the dualistic story of video gaming and the adolescent child’s health. Video gaming enhances cognitive psychomotor speed and enhances the ability to reason spatially, and is Little affected by the performance decrements resulting from sleep deprivation. The “unhealthy gamer” phenotype is not due to the video gaming interface. The unhealthy phenotype gamer is a by-product of the ‘Confounding Triad’ of high-calorie unregulated diets, poor sleep habits, and compensatory sedentary behavior.

The thrust health policy implications of this study are far-reaching. Policy frameworks clearly impact health outcomes, as seen with the WHO 2020 Guidelines on Physical Activity and Sedentary Behaviour,[35] which offer a benchmark of 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity to children and adolescents to offset the health risks of long sedentary bouts. Our evidence suggests that rather than a focus on reducing screen time too broadly, health promotion can be more efficiently achieved through a focus on ‘Digital Hygiene’ that incorporates elements of nutrition and sleep education within the digital frameworks.

If adolescents are educated to manage their metabolic health by controlling the caloric intake[9] and the amount of sleep they achieve, they can achieve the cognitive training benefits of interactive video gaming without compromising their health.

This approach is aimed at the overall well-being of the digital-native generation, ensuring that digital gaming is used as a means of cognitive development rather than an obesity contributor.
