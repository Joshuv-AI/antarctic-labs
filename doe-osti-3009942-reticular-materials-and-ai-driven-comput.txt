1. Accomplishments
Project objectives (exploratory):
 • Establish a molecular-level picture of alkali-metal ion uptake from dilute aqueous solutions in
 hydrolytically robust metal-organic frameworks (MOFs), including the coupled roles of
 hydration, confinement, and transport barriers.
 • Develop transferable molecular simulation tools (data-driven many-body models and simulation
 protocols) to predict ion uptake thermodynamics and kinetics across families of porous
 frameworks.
 • Synthesize and test functionalized and multivariate MOFs (MTV-MOFs) that incorporate ion-
 binding motifs and evaluate their performance in competitive synthetic seawater.
 • Generate actionable design rules to guide next-generation sorbents for critical-metal recovery
 (specifically Li+) from complex, dilute feedstocks.

1.1 Mechanistic understanding of alkali-metal ion uptake in MOF-808 (Chemical Science)
In the primary outcome of this project, we combined molecular simulations, free-energy calculations, and
enhanced-sampling methods with MTV-MOF synthesis and characterization to quantify both the
thermodynamics and kinetics of alkali-metal ion uptake in MOF-808 from aqueous solution [Chemical
Science 16, 12129–12138 (2025)].
 • Large-pore (LP) environments in MOF-808 provide a similarly favorable environment for Li+,
 Na+, and K+, indicating little intrinsic selectivity in the LPs. The computed free-energy change for
 transfer from bulk water to LPs is approximately -4.1 kcal/mol for each ion.
 • Small-pore (SP) environments exhibit distinct thermodynamic and kinetic preferences that can
 govern uptake. In particular, uptake depends strongly on whether the SP is hydrated or
 dehydrated, highlighting the critical role of water structure under confinement.
 • For transfer from hydrated LPs to a dehydrated SP, Li+ faces the highest entry barrier due to its
 strong hydration shell, while K+ shows the strongest thermodynamic preference for uptake in the
 dehydrated SP. Representative LP → dehydrated SP transfer free energies are approximately -4.5,
 -6.8, and -7.4 kcal/mol for Li+, Na+, and K+, respectively.
 • For transfer to a hydrated SP, the thermodynamic ordering reverses and Li+ becomes the most
 stable alkali ion in the SP. Representative LP → hydrated SP transfer free energies are
 approximately -6.6, -4.6, and -4.0 kcal/mol for Li+, Na+, and K+, respectively.

 • Water entry into dehydrated SPs is kinetically hindered (free-energy barriers exceeding ~26
 kcal/mol), suggesting that early-stage ion uptake into SPs can occur faster than full pore
 hydration.
Beyond thermodynamic driving forces, we quantified transport barriers that control the dynamics of ion
access to the smallest pore environments:
 • Predicted entry barriers (hydrated LP → dehydrated SP): ~0.4, ~2.5, ~6.1 kcal/mol for K+, Na+,
 and Li+, respectively (i.e., ordering: Li+ > Na+ > K+).
 • Predicted exit barriers (dehydrated SP → hydrated LP, kcal/mol): ~15.9, ~32.3, ~52.8 kcal/mol
 for Li+, Na+, and K+, respectively. The reverse ordering reflects increasingly strong
 confinement/stabilization once an ion is inside a dehydrated SP.
 • These results underscore the importance of coupled thermodynamics and kinetics: small pores
 can provide selectivity, but access depends on dehydration penalties and pore-window transport
 barriers.
Collectively, these findings deliver a quantitative, molecular-level framework that explains why
selectivity can be weak in larger hydrated pores and why small pores (and their hydration state) can
dominate selectivity outcomes. They also define actionable design directions for improving Li+
selectivity, including tuning pore-window sizes and introducing functional binding environments that
compensate Li+ dehydration costs.

1.2 MOF-808 functionalization and ion-uptake measurements in synthetic seawater
To connect molecular-level insights to practical sorbent development, we synthesized and evaluated
functionalized MOF-808 materials under synthetic seawater conditions (highly competitive ionic
mixtures).
 • MOF-808 was functionalized at the Zr SBU using dicarboxylic acids (e.g., aspartic acid and
 glutamic acid), creating pendant carboxylate groups inside pores.
 • Functionalization levels were quantified as the percentage of formate sites replaced by the
 functional molecule (representative values: aspartic acid ~48%, glutamic acid ~53%, fumaric acid
       ~60%, succinic acid ~33%).
 • Ion-uptake measurements demonstrated strong uptake of major monovalent ions (Na+, K+). Under
 these highly competitive conditions, Li+ uptake remained low, reinforcing that Li+ selectivity
 requires pore environments that provide strong and specific stabilization relative to abundant
 competing ions.

1.3 Multivariate MOFs with crown-ether motifs
We pursued multivariate MTV-MOF strategies to incorporate explicit ion-recognition motifs into robust
MOF backbones. Crown-ether-containing linkers (12-crown-4 and 14-crown-4 derivatives) were
synthesized and integrated into Zr-UiO-66 and Al-MIL-53 frameworks.
 • Systematic synthesis established approximately linear relationships between the starting crown-
 ether linker fraction in the synthesis mixture and the final incorporation fraction in the
 framework, enabling composition-controlled MTV-MOF samples.
 • Ion-uptake measurements in synthetic seawater showed that crown-ether functionalization and
 MOF topology strongly influence uptake. In particular, MIL-53(Al) variants showed suppressed
 Na+ uptake (not detected) under the test conditions, supporting a promising direction for Li+/Na+
 discrimination.

1.4 Transferable computational tools and workflows for porous materials in water
A key enabling outcome of the project was the development of simulation infrastructure that can be
applied across framework families and can support future AI-guided materials discovery.
 • Molecular models for MOF inorganic nodes (including MOF-808 and Zr-UiO-66 SBUs) to
 enable molecular dynamics simulations in aqueous electrolyte environments; MOF-808 node
 parameters were fit to ab initio data using optimization strategies (including genetic algorithms).
 • Protocols to compute hydration/solvation thermodynamics under confinement and to map ion and
 water potentials of mean force (PMFs) through pore windows using enhanced sampling.
 • A workflow to quantify guest (water/ion) capacity and infiltration behavior by combining slab-
 based molecular dynamics simulations with Connolly-surface free-volume analysis.
 • Automation scripts to generate multivariate MOF structures by controlled linker substitution,
 enabling systematic composition sweeps and integration with data-driven/AI models.

1.5 Derivative outcomes enabled by the award
The conceptual and computational advances developed under this award also enabled related publications
and directions beyond the central MOF-808 ion-uptake study:
 • 2D COFs: We showed that intermolecular interactions can override simple electron-
 donating/withdrawing intuition in controlling stacking and electronic properties of functionalized
 2D covalent organic frameworks (COFs). These insights are broadly relevant to composition-
 structure-property relationships in reticular materials for photoenergy conversion [Chemistry of
        Materials 37, 6881–6891 (2025)].
 • Inorganic cluster networks: We demonstrated electroreduction-driven assembly of
 polyoxometalate coordination networks, illustrating how redox-state control can direct inorganic
 connectivity motifs in extended solids [Inorganic Chemistry 64, 1630–1636 (2025)].

2. Impacts
Scientific and technical impacts of the award include:
 • Quantitative design rules for ion uptake from dilute aqueous solutions in MOFs, explicitly
 identifying how hydration structure, dehydration penalties, confinement, and transport barriers
 control uptake outcomes.
 • A clarified physical picture for why selectivity is weak in larger hydrated pores and why small
 pores (and their hydration state) can dominate selectivity and kinetics.
 • Experimental demonstration of functionalization strategies (carboxylates; crown ethers) in robust
 MOFs, including evidence that topology and pore chemistry can significantly alter uptake
 signatures (e.g., suppressed Na+ uptake in MIL-53(Al) under the tested conditions).
 • Transferable simulation workflows and structure-generation tools that can accelerate screening
 and rational design of porous sorbents for critical-metal recovery and other aqueous separations.
 • Training of researchers in integrated experimental-computational approaches spanning synthesis,
 ion-uptake measurements, molecular simulation, and free-energy methods.
Broader context
MOFs are an enabling platform for separations and resource recovery because their pore environments
can be tuned at the molecular level. This project contributes to that broader effort by defining mechanistic

separations, including mining Li+ from dilute solutions.
