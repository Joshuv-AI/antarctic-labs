3. Executive Summary
The Verde Ductless Heat Pump (DHP) Program was implemented to expand access to
high-efficiency electric heating and cooling technologies for households in priority
communities. Funded by the U.S. Department of Energy’s Building Technologies Office
under award DE-EE0010132, the project focused on deploying ductless heat pumps in
residential settings while strengthening community engagement, contractor
partnerships, and program delivery systems.
The project successfully exceeded its primary installation goal. While the original
milestone target was 200 installations, the program ultimately completed 234 ductless
heat pump installations, significantly expanding access to energy-efficient heating and
cooling systems for households in the Energy Trust of Oregon service area. These
installations support improved home comfort and reduced household energy burden
associated with residential heating.
The project also successfully achieved its outreach and participant engagement
milestones. Over the course of the program, the Verde team identified more than 620
potential participants and completed 373 home energy assessments, creating a
strong pipeline of eligible households for installation. Selection criteria prioritized
households within Energy Trust service territory and populations aligned with priority
community definitions.

In addition to technology deployment, the program emphasized equitable workforce and
contractor participation. The project partnered with minority- and women-owned
contractors, including The Heat Pump Store and Peak Electric, to perform installations
and support program delivery. These partnerships contributed to expanding contractor
participation in energy efficiency deployment and strengthened collaboration between
community-based organizations and technical service providers.
Throughout the project, Verde also improved internal systems for program management
and service delivery. A new project database was developed to track installations,
participant engagement, and contractor coordination, improving operational efficiency
and supporting the management of hundreds of projects at different stages of
development.
The project also included participant education and follow-up activities designed to
ensure households could fully benefit from the installed technologies. Community
workshops were conducted in both English and Spanish to provide education on heat
pump use, energy efficiency, and program benefits.
Overall, the project demonstrated that community-based organizations can play a
critical role in accelerating energy-efficient technology adoption while ensuring equitable
access to energy efficiency investments. The program’s results illustrate the technical
feasibility, economic viability, and social value of deploying ductless heat pump
technologies through community-driven delivery models.

5. Background
Residential buildings account for a significant portion of national energy consumption,
largely due to the continued use of fossil-fuel-based heating systems such as natural
gas furnaces, oil boilers, and propane heaters. Residential heating using high-efficiency
heat pump technologies has emerged a strategy for improving household energy
efficiency.
Ductless heat pumps (DHPs), also referred to as mini-split heat pumps, represent a
mature and widely deployed technology capable of delivering both heating and cooling
with significantly higher efficiency than conventional systems. Modern heat pumps
operate by transferring heat rather than generating it through combustion or electrical
resistance. As a result, these systems can achieve efficiencies that are two to four times
greater than traditional heating technologies. The U.S. Department of Energy and
numerous energy research institutions have identified heat pump adoption as a pathway
for reducing residential energy consumption.
Recent studies and market analyses indicate that heat pumps are particularly well
suited for deployment in existing residential buildings, especially in regions with
moderate climates such as the Pacific Northwest. In these regions, ductless heat pumps
provide an effective retrofit solution because they can be installed without extensive
modifications to existing ductwork. This makes them especially attractive for older
housing stock and smaller homes where ducted systems are not feasible or cost-
effective.
Despite the technical maturity and performance advantages of heat pump technologies,
several barriers continue to limit widespread adoption. These barriers include upfront
installation costs, limited awareness among homeowners, contractor capacity
constraints, and challenges associated with navigating available incentive programs.
These barriers can be particularly significant for low- and moderate-income households,
who often face higher energy burdens and may lack access to financing or information
about energy efficiency programs.
Community-based organizations have increasingly played an important role in
addressing these barriers by providing outreach, education, and program navigation
services. By leveraging trusted community relationships and culturally responsive
engagement strategies, these organizations can help connect households with available
incentives and technical resources while supporting equitable access to energy
efficiency technologies.
The Verde Ductless Heat Pump Program was developed within this context to expand
access to heat pump technology among households in priority communities. The
program was designed to combine technology deployment with community
engagement, contractor partnerships, and participant education. By working with
minority- and women-owned contractors and aligning program eligibility with priority
population criteria, the project sought to ensure that the benefits of residential building
upgrades were accessible to households historically underserved by energy efficiency
programs.

In addition to expanding technology deployment, the project aimed to demonstrate the
effectiveness of community-based delivery models for residential programs. Through a
combination of outreach, home energy assessments, contractor coordination, and
participant education, the program sought to create a replicable framework for
accelerating heat pump adoption while improving household comfort and reducing
energy costs.
The lessons learned from this project contribute to a growing body of research and
programmatic experience demonstrating that targeted outreach, contractor
engagement, and streamlined program administration are essential components of
successful residential initiatives. As federal, state, and local governments invest in
building upgrades, programs such as the Verde Ductless Heat Pump Program provide
valuable insights into how community-driven approaches can support large-scale
adoption of energy-efficient technologies.

6. Project Objectives
The objective of this project was to expand access to high-efficiency ductless heat
pump technologies for low-income households and priority communities within the
Energy Trust of Oregon service territory.
The program specifically targeted households in communities historically underserved
by energy efficiency programs, including low-income, immigrant, refugee, and
communities of color.

The project supported:
• Expanding deployment of energy-efficient electric heating technologies
• Reducing residential energy consumption associated with fossil-fuel heating
• Increasing participation of historically underserved communities in energy efficiency
programs
• Strengthening partnerships between community organizations, utilities, and
contractors
• Building organizational capacity to manage large-scale energy efficiency programs

7. Project Results and Discussion
Task 1 – Participant Identification and Outreach
The project significantly exceeded outreach goals. The original milestone target was to
identify 600 potential participants. The program ultimately identified over 620
potential participants, creating a robust pipeline of households interested in
participating in the program.
Eligibility criteria were established to ensure the program served priority communities
while remaining within the service territory of Energy Trust of Oregon. Outreach efforts
included community engagement events, partnerships with local organizations, and
direct engagement with households.

Task 2 – Preparation and Contractor Engagement
The project successfully established partnerships with minority- and women-owned
contractor firms. Four contractors, The Heat Pump Store, Peak Electric, Mt. Hood
Ductless, MPS—were formally engaged to perform program installations.
Over the course of the project:
• 373 home energy assessments were completed
• Contractor partnerships were strengthened through regular coordination
• Installation workflows were refined to improve efficiency

 Page 6 of 31

 Verde

These preparation activities helped ensure installations could proceed efficiently once
households were approved for participation.

Task 3 – Heat Pump Installations
The project exceeded its primary installation target. The original milestone required 200
installations, and the program ultimately completed 234 ductless heat pump
installations.
Some installations experienced delays due to equipment availability constraints and
household scheduling issues; however, the project team worked closely with
participants and contractors to successfully complete installations within the extended
project timeline.
The additional installations were made possible in part through effective use of
remaining incentive funds and strong contractor coordination.

Task 4 – Participant Education and Follow-Up
The project included several participant education and engagement activities designed
to ensure households understood how to operate and benefit from their new heat pump
systems.
Program workshops were conducted in English and Spanish, to increase accessibility
and participant engagement. Workshops provided information on:
• Heat pump operation and maintenance
• Energy efficiency practices
• Household energy cost savings
Participant follow-up also provided the program team with valuable feedback on
installation experiences and program improvements.

9. Opportunities
The success of the Verde Ductless Heat Pump Program demonstrates the potential for
community-based organizations to play a key role in scaling residential initiatives.
Current gaps include:
• Additional geographic areas
• Contractor networks to increase installation capacity
• Additional energy efficiency measures such as weatherization
• Partnerships with utilities and community organizations to increase participation
This project developed a strong foundation of systems, partnerships, and operational
workflows for residential programs.

10. Products
Although no peer-reviewed publications were produced under this award at the time of
submission, the project generated several operational and educational products that
supported program implementation and may inform future residential programs.
Program Implementation Resources
The project produced several internal program management tools designed to improve
coordination between program staff, contractors, and participating households. These
resources include:
 • A project tracking database used to manage participant information, installation
 status, contractor coordination, and program metrics. The database allowed the
 project team to track hundreds of participant records and installations across
 multiple stages of development, including outreach, assessment, installation
 scheduling, and completion.
 • Installation tracking templates used to monitor installation progress, contractor
 assignments, and milestone completion.
 • Program reporting templates developed to support quarterly reporting
 requirements and internal program evaluation.

Program Coordinators played a central role in participant engagement and day-to-day
program operations. Their responsibilities included:

workshops were delivered in English and Spanish and focused on:

Coordinators helped ensure households could fully benefit from the installed systems.

13. Participant Perspectives and Community Outreach

Improving Home Comfort with a Ductless Heat Pump
I first learned about the program through a friend I was working with. He had already
received a ductless heat pump through Verde and told me how much it had improved
comfort in his home. Hearing about his experience made me curious, so I decided to
apply as well.
The application process was simple, and once I was approved, the installation
happened surprisingly quickly. The installation team was very professional and
respectful. They took the time to explain what they were doing and made sure I
understood how the system worked. That made me feel comfortable and confident
about the whole process. From beginning to end, the experience felt smooth and well
organized.
Since the heat pump was installed, the difference in our home has been huge. The
system works wonderfully. It keeps the house cool during warm weather, and it does so
without using a lot of electricity, which is very important for our household. The air feels
clean and comfortable, and the temperature stays consistent throughout the home.
Everyone in the house has noticed the change; including our pets. I have a very fluffy
dog who loves cooler temperatures, and he seems especially happy now that the house
stays cool and comfortable. Seeing how much of a difference this system has made for
all of us has been amazing.

For our family, receiving this heat pump has truly improved the comfort of our home. It is
something we appreciate every day.

Discovering Comfort Through Community Connection
I first learned about Verde while I was graduating from a community leadership program
with Latino Network. During one of our sessions, a staff member from Verde came to
speak about the services they offer. I was interested right away, so after the
presentation I approached her to ask for more information. She took my contact
information and connected me with the person managing the heat pump program. That
is how I first learned about the opportunity.
Once I was approved, it took some time before the installation could happen because
there was a high demand for the program. When a spot finally opened up, they
scheduled my appointment. The installation process itself was very easy for me. I didn’t
have to worry about managing the work or supervising anything. I gave the team access
to the space they needed, and they handled everything professionally.
Before the installation, our home did not have air conditioning, and during the summer
the heat inside the house was very intense. It was uncomfortable not only for me but
also for my dogs. I had previously purchased a wall air conditioner, but it wasn’t strong
enough to cool the whole house. Since the heat pump was installed, the difference has
been enormous. The home stays comfortable, and the system works much better than
the individual units we used before.
One of the things that surprised me the most was the electricity cost. Many people
assume that installing a system like this will increase their utility bills, but in my case it
has actually been more efficient. I used to pay more when I relied on several small
cooling units than I do now with the heat pump.
Now, whenever I meet someone who might benefit from the program, I tell them about
my experience. I share the contact information and encourage them to reach out
because the program helped me so much. I always tell people that it’s worth trying.
Many of us assume we won’t qualify for programs like this. I had the same thought
because my income often makes me ineligible for many types of assistance. But I
decided to apply anyway, and it turned out that I did qualify based on my household
situation.
For me, the experience was a pleasant surprise. I am very grateful for the opportunity
because the heat pump has made a real difference in the comfort of my home.

Finding Comfort at Home
My name is Sandy Snavely. I moved into my condominium about five months ago.
Living in the Pacific Northwest means experiencing everything from very cold weather to
very hot temperatures throughout the year. When I moved in, I was on a limited budget
and trying to figure out how to make my home comfortable. I quickly realized that my
heating system was very old, and I was in real need of a better solution.

I live in a condominium community for residents over 55, and many of my neighbors had
already replaced their heating systems with mini-split heat pumps. Fortunately, one of
the people who helps residents apply for the program attends my church. I spoke with
him about my situation, and he connected me with a representative from Verde. They
came to meet with me, explained the program, and helped me fill out the application.
The process was simple and supportive, and I felt comfortable every step of the way.
When I learned that I had been approved, I was ecstatic. It did not take long before the
installation appointment was scheduled. Two technicians came to my home to complete
the work. One was the electrician who worked in the attic installing the electrical
components. He was incredibly skilled and thorough. At one point, we started talking
about how my garbage disposal had not been working. Before leaving, he walked into
the kitchen, quickly figured out the issue, and fixed it for me. That was completely
beyond what I expected, and it showed how much care he put into his work.
The other technician, Marty, installed the mini-split unit inside my home. He treated my
home with deep respect and was thoughtful about everything he did. He kept the noise
level low, worked carefully while cutting through the drywall, and cleaned up completely
before he left. Later, when I stepped outside, I even noticed that the weeds around the
installation area had been pulled. It was such a small gesture, but it meant a lot. It made
the whole experience feel personal and respectful, which is not always the case when
you live alone and have workers in your home.
Since the heat pump was installed, the difference has been remarkable. Before, my
heater in the living room was not functioning properly. Now, I can wake up in the
morning, go into the living room with my tea and a book, and simply feel comfortable. I
no longer need to wrap myself in a blanket just to sit and read. When the weather began
warming up, the system adjusted beautifully to the changing temperatures. After a few
days of adjusting the thermostat, I found the perfect temperature setting, and since then
I have barely touched it. The system keeps the house comfortable automatically.
Although I have only lived in my home for a few months, I have already noticed that my
most recent electricity bill was the lowest one I have received so far. That was a huge
relief and gives me confidence that the system will also perform well during the winter.
When people ask me about the program, I always tell them that I cannot think of a
reason not to apply. The application process was easy, and the people at Verde were
genuinely kind and helpful. You could feel that they were excited to help people improve
their homes.
In my community, many residents are retired and living on fixed incomes, and replacing
a heating system is simply not affordable. I was honestly amazed that an organization
existed that could provide something like this. At first, I almost felt guilty accepting the
help because I wondered if someone else might need it more. But I also knew how
much I needed it. In the end, the experience has been wonderful, and the heat pump
has truly improved my quality of life at home.
