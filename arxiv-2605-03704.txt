Singular semilinear elliptic equations in nondivergence form
Agnieszka Kałamajska, Dalimil Peša, Artur Rutkowski

Abstract
. We study the singular semilinear equation − P ​ u = f u γ -Pu=\frac{f}{u^{\gamma}} on a bounded domain Ω \Omega with Dirichlet condition u ≡ 0 u\equiv 0 on ∂ Ω \partial\Omega , where P P is a second-order elliptic differential operator in nondivergence form. We obtain the existence of a solution under the assumptions that Ω ∈ C 1 , 1 \Omega\in C^{1,1} and P P has C 1 C^{1} coefficients, as well as the uniqueness of solutions in L 1 ​ ( Ω ) L^{1}(\Omega) , under the assumptions that Ω ∈ C 2 \Omega\in C^{2} and P P has C 2 C^{2} coefficients. Our proofs are based on a novel combination of tools, such as recently obtained nonlinear variants of Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities, estimates of Green functions, and new variants of Kato-type inequalities.

Primary 35J75, 35J61; Secondary 35D30, 35J08.

Keywords and phrases:
nondivergence form operator, singular elliptic equations, semilinear elliptic equations, existence and uniqueness.

1.
Introduction

In this paper
we are interested in the following Dirichlet problem for a singular semilinear equation with Dirichlet condition

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-Pu=\displaystyle\frac{f}{u^{\gamma}}\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
u\equiv 0\quad&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded domain,
d\geq 2
,

f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
is nonnegative almost everywhere,
\gamma>0
,
u

is nonnegative almost everywhere, and
Pu
is
an extension of
a second-order uniformly elliptic operator in nondivergence form:

(
P
)

Pu(x):=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}(x)\frac{\partial^{2}u(x)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}},\ \ u\in W^{2,1}_{loc}(\Omega)

with the symmetric matrix
\mathbf{A}(x)=[a_{ij}(x)]_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
,
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, satisfying

(
\mathbf{A}
)

\displaystyle c_{\mathbf{A}}\|\xi\|^{2}\leq\mathbf{A}(x)\xi\cdot\xi\leq C_{\mathbf{A}}\|\xi\|^{2},\quad x\in\overline{\Omega},\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\quad\text{ for some }0<c_{\mathbf{A}}\leq C_{\mathbf{A}}<\infty.

We first give a brief explanation of the notions of solution used in the paper. The complete definitions are given in Section
2
below.
The boundary condition is interpreted as

(Tr)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}|u|=0,\ {\rm where}\ \ \Omega_{\epsilon}=\{x\in\Omega:\delta_{\Omega}(x)<\epsilon\}.

When
u\in W^{2,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
, the operator
Pu
can be computed almost everywhere in
\Omega
in the classical sense, but in general we expect the solutions to be less regular.

If
\mathbf{A}\in C^{2}(\Omega)
and
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
, then we can extend the operator to the distributional setting:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\langle-Pu,\phi\rangle

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\Omega}uP^{*}\phi,\ {\rm where}\ \ \phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),

(1.3)

\displaystyle P^{*}\phi

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{div}\left(\mathbf{A}\nabla\phi\right)+\operatorname{div}\left(\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{A})\phi\right)\hbox{\rm--- the adjoint operator}

and
\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}:=(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}^{1},\dots,\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}^{d})^{T}
, where
\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}^{i}
is the standard divergence operator applied to the column
\mathbf{A}^{i}
of the matrix
\mathbf{A}
.
Note that
P^{*}\phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})
.

With more precise information about
u
, that is, when
u\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
, we can verify that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\langle-Pu,\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{A}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi+(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla u)\phi,\ \ \phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

Here, it suffices to assume that
\mathbf{A}\in C^{1}(\Omega)
.

The distributional formulations (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) lead to two slightly different definitions of solutions: we show existence in the framework of (
1.4
) in Theorem
1.1
, which requires only
\mathbf{A}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
, and uniqueness for (
1.2
) in Theorem
1.2
, which imposes more regularity on
\mathbf{A}
, specifically
\mathbf{A}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
, but allows less regular solutions
u
.

We also require that
f/u^{\gamma}
be locally integrable, so that the right-hand side of (
1.1
) is a distribution given by a locally integrable function. Then the equality
-Pu=f/u^{\gamma}
can be interpreted as the equality of two distributions.

Studies of singular semilinear equations of the form similar to (
1.1
) were initiated over half a century ago with the works of Fulks and Maybee
[
22
]
, Stuart
[
40
]
, and Crandall, Rabinowitz, and Tartar
[
12
]
. Since then, the topic has been extensively investigated to cover broader cases of
P
,
f
, and
\gamma
, as well as various generalizations or perturbations of (
1.1
). We refer to the recent paper
[
39
]
for an exposition of the existing results.

Our problem can be considered as a multidimensional counterpart of the Emden–Fowler equation, originally an ODE of the form
u^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(x)=Cx^{\beta}u^{\alpha}
, where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. This equation and its prominent generalizations, Lane–Emden–Fowler
type equations, have been applied in science, electrical engineering, astrophysics, and the analysis of non-Newtonian fluids
[
11
,
23
,
35
,
43
]
.

More recently, Boccardo and Orsina
[
6
]
made a substantial contribution to this class of equations by investigating the existence of solutions to (
1.1
)
for operators
P
in divergence form, i.e. when
Pu=\operatorname{div}\left(\mathbf{A}\nabla u\right)
, and with nonnegative
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Elliptic operators in nondivergence form are of considerable interest in the theory of stochastic processes, as they form an important subclass of generators of diffusion processes, see e.g.
[
3
, Section 6.7.3]
. Stochastic analysis largely facilitates the construction and estimates of integral kernels, e.g. the Green function or the Poisson kernel; we refer to
[
9
,
44
]
in this context.

Our first main result is the following theorem on the existence of solutions to (
1.1
) with the same range of

and
\gamma
as in
[

]
, but for

in nondivergence form. For the symbols and notions used in the formulation, see Section

(existence and regularity)

Assume that

The proof of Theorem

and consists in constructing the solution by approximating it with the solutions to regularized problems. The approximate solutions are obtained by the Schauder Fixed Point Theorem. Then, the key points are to prove that the approximating sequence is monotone and enjoys suitable a priori Sobolev-type estimates; the approach to these two issues strongly depends on the exact setting of the problem.

Despite a large number of papers that followed
[

]
, it seems that (

for singular semilinear problems with additional nonlinear gradient terms.

Although the formulation of the problem seems almost the same as in

•

A crucial new tool we used to prove the existence result are the recently obtained a priori estimates related to the nonlinear Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality:

deduced from

•

We use the representation of solutions with the Green function

•

We apply a Kato-type inequality for strong solutions of equations in nondivergence form, given in Lemma

Let us mention that the regularity results obtained in Theorem

For operators in divergence form, a similar uniqueness result was given by Oliva and Petitta

. We note however, that the class of solutions that we consider,

, is wider than the one used in
[

, where

is assumed. The structure of the proof is similar to that in
[

, where the authors relied strongly on a Kato-type inequality, but we need to overcome certain obstacles resulting from considering the operator in nondivergence form and a weaker type of solutions. We outline the proof and the differences below.

1)

The first major step of the proof is to show that

is integrable with respect to the weight
\delta_{\Omega}
, which is done in Lemma

. This consists of several elements:

•

In
[

]
, for

as test functions. In our case, we first need to extend the class of the test functions from

to all

such that

. To this end we use a Friedrichs-type lemma about mollifiers, see Section

below.

•

The weighted integrability is obtained with the use of a special test function
\xi
solving
-P^{*}\xi=1
. We need the bound

(closely related to Hopf lemma, see Remark

) for this function, which is proved in Lemma
4.1
ii)(c)

To achieve this, we use the Green representation of solutions to

and the sharp estimates of the adjoint Green function

.

•

The last auxiliary result for the weighted integrability is Lemma
4.4
, where we show that
\xi
can be approximated from below by functions

such that

are uniformly bounded from above. The adjoint operator

has an essentially different structure than

2)

After establishing the weighted integrability, we further extend the class of test functions to the set

consisting of

such that

is a very weak solution in the sense of Definition

3)

The last step is to prove the Kato-type inequality for very weak solutions of equations in nondivergence form (Lemma

The paper is organized as follows. Section

3.1.
Four auxiliary lemmas

Given

First, let us explain the strategy. The estimate will follow from

We shall now verify the assumptions of the required theorems. We do not repeat their full formulation because of its substantial length and the fact that we only require a specific case of the more general inequalities. We only explain how individual assumptions are verified, staying consistent with the notation of
[

Lemma 3.3

(existence and regularity of strong solutions to

Assume that

Furthermore, the following statements hold.

(a)

(b)

(d)

Since

[

The Sobolev embeddings with

is continuous up to the boundary (see e.g.
[

Moreover,

Let us prove the strict positivity of

It is known, that the solution is represented by Green formula, which holds for almost every

where

in

, see
[

, Theorem 2.3]
.
The strict positivity of

inside

follows from the nontriviality of

and
sharp estimates, in particular strict positivity of the Green function:

(3.5)

\displaystyle\textnormal{for}\ d=2:\qquad G_{\Omega}(x,y)\approx\ln\bigg(1+\frac{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)}{\lVert x-y\rVert^{2}}\bigg),\quad x,y\in\Omega,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\textnormal{for}\ d\geq 3:\qquad G_{\Omega}(x,y)\approx\lVert x-y\rVert^{2-d}\bigg(1\wedge\frac{\delta_{\Omega}(x)}{\lVert x-y\rVert}\bigg)\bigg(1\wedge\frac{\delta_{\Omega}(y)}{\lVert x-y\rVert}\bigg),\quad x,y\in\Omega,

see
[
27
]
,
[
44
]
,
[
45
]
, and
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
for the explicit formulation of (
3.6
).
This gives the statement

.

Let us prove the statement

.
Since

is nontrivial, there exists an open set

, relatively compact in
\Omega
such that

is also nontrivial on

. Therefore, for any open

such that

,

\displaystyle\bigg(1\wedge\frac{\delta_{\Omega}(x)}{\lVert x-y\rVert}\bigg)\gtrsim\delta_{\Omega}(x)\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\frac{\delta_{\Omega}(x)\delta_{\Omega}(y)}{\lVert x-y\rVert^{2}}\approx\delta_{\Omega}(x),\quad x\in\Omega\setminus V,\ y\in U.

Consequently, for

\displaystyle u(x)\gtrsim\delta_{\Omega}(x)\int_{U}F(y)\lVert x-y\rVert^{2-d}\bigg(1\wedge\frac{\delta_{\Omega}(y)}{\lVert x-y\rVert}\bigg)\,dy\gtrsim\delta_{\Omega}(x)\int_{U}F(y)\,dy\gtrsim\delta_{\Omega}(x),\quad x\in\Omega\setminus V,

and for

, since

close to

\displaystyle u(x)\gtrsim\delta_{\Omega}(x)\int_{U}F(y)\,dy\gtrsim\delta_{\Omega}(x),\quad x\in\Omega\setminus V.

Since we also have

in

, we find that

for all

.

As for the other estimate,
\partial\Omega
is
C^{1,1}
,

, and

on
\partial\Omega
, so for

close to the boundary there is a unique

such that

Since

is also bounded in

, this proves

.

Let us prove the statement

.
Since

for all

, we have for all

(3.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}(u(x)^{\gamma+1})

\displaystyle=(\gamma+1)u(x)^{\gamma}\partial_{i}u(x),

(3.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{ij}(u(x)^{\gamma+1})

\displaystyle=(\gamma+1)\gamma u(x)^{\gamma-1}\partial_{i}u(x)\partial_{j}u(x)+(\gamma+1)u(x)^{\gamma}\partial_{ij}u(x),

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{(2)}(u(x)^{\gamma+1})\|

\displaystyle\leq(\gamma+1)\gamma u(x)^{\gamma-1}\|\nabla u(x)\|^{2}+(\gamma+1)u(x)^{\gamma}\|\nabla^{(2)}u(x)\|,

almost everywhere in

are bounded, we have

for all

and

, we are in a position to use Lemma

, which together with the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{(2)}(u(x)^{\gamma+1})\|\lesssim\int_{\Omega}u^{\gamma}\|\nabla^{(2)}u\|\lesssim\|u\|_{W^{2,1}(\Omega)}<\infty.

This proves

Remark 3.4
.

The usual formulation of the Hopf lemma asserts the positivity of the inward normal derivatives for positive functions
u
satisfying
-Pu\geq 0
. The estimate
u\gtrsim\delta_{\Omega}
following from
(c)
easily implies this property; it is also equivalent if
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
, because for such functions the Hopf lemma implies that the inward normal derivative must be uniformly positive by the interior ball condition for
C^{1,1}
domains.

We will prove a Kato-type inequality for strong solutions of
-Pu=F
. A different version will be given in Lemma
4.10
for very weak solutions. In the present statement we assume more regularity on the solution and the right-hand side, but due to that we are able to work with
\mathbf{A}
which is only once differentiable. Results of this type are known in various settings, see e.g.
[
15
]
, but we could not find a reference covering our case, so we give a proof.

Lemma 3.5

(a Kato-type inequality for strong solutions)
.

Assume that
\Omega\in C^{1,1}
and that
\mathbf{A}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies (

A

). Suppose that
F\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and that
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
is a strong solution to (
2.4
). Then,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{A}\nabla(u_{+})\cdot\nabla\phi+(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla(u_{+}))\phi\leq\int_{\{u>0\}}F\phi,\quad\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\ \phi\geq 0.

Proof.

First note that by Lemma
3.3
the strong solution
u
to (
2.4
) indeed exists and
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
.
For
\epsilon>0
consider convex
\Phi_{\epsilon}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{\epsilon}(x)=((x^{3}+\epsilon^{3})^{1/3}-\epsilon)\textbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(x).

We have

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{2}}{(x^{3}+\epsilon^{3})^{\frac{2}{3}}}\textbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(x),\qquad\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}(x)=\frac{2x\epsilon^{3}}{(x^{3}+\epsilon^{3})^{\frac{5}{3}}}\textbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(x),

therefore
\Phi_{\epsilon}(0)=0
,
0\leq\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\leq 1
and
\Phi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(0)=0
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\Phi_{\epsilon}(x)\to x_{+},\quad\Phi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(x)\to\textbf{1}_{(0,\infty)}(x),\quad\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\epsilon}(x)\to 0

pointwise as
\epsilon\to 0
. By the chain rule, (

A

), and the convexity of
\Phi
we have

\displaystyle-P(\Phi_{\epsilon}(u))=-\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u)Pu-\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}(u)\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla u\otimes\nabla u\leq\Phi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(u)F.

Integrating against nonnegative
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-P(\Phi_{\epsilon}(u))\phi\leq\int_{\Omega}\Phi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(u)F\phi.

The right-hand side converges to
\int_{\Omega}F\textbf{1}_{\{u>0\}}\phi
, as
\epsilon\to 0
, by the Dominated Convergence Theorem. For the left-hand side we use integration by parts and the Dominated Convergence Theorem:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-P\Phi_{\epsilon}(u)\phi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{A}\nabla\Phi_{\epsilon}(u)\cdot\nabla\phi+((\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})\cdot\nabla\Phi_{\epsilon}(u))\phi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u)\mathbf{A}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi+((\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})\cdot\nabla u)\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u)\phi

\displaystyle\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{A}(\textbf{1}_{\{u>0\}}\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\phi+((\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})\cdot(\textbf{1}_{\{u>0\}}\nabla u))\phi.

It is known that
\textbf{1}_{\{u>0\}}\nabla u=\nabla(u_{+})
a.e., see e.g.
[
24
, Lemma 7.6]
. Therefore, we get the estimate in (
3.10
) for nonnegative
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. By density, since
u_{+}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, the formula also holds for
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
which are nonnegative almost everywhere.
∎

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Explanation of the strategy

The proof consists of first solving the problem with regularized data: the function

and the nonlinearity
\tau(s)=s^{-\gamma}
. Then we obtain the solution to the original problem as the limit of solutions to regularized problems. We divide the proof into steps.

3.2.1.
Construction of the approximating sequence of the regularized solutions

We first define the truncation operator for functions
h\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
:

h^{{\langle n\rangle}}(x):={\rm min}\{h(x),n\}.

We consider
the mapping

S_{n}\colon H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\text{ where }\psi:=S_{n}v

is the unique strong solution to the problem (see Lemma
3.3
and (
3.3
))

(3.11)

-P\psi=\frac{f^{{\langle n\rangle}}(x)}{(v^{2}+\frac{1}{n})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}=f^{{\langle n\rangle}}(x)\tau_{n}(v)=:G_{n}(v),\text{ where }\tau_{n}(s):=\frac{1}{(s^{2}+\frac{1}{n})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}.

We will show that

(a)

for every fixed
n
,
S_{n}
is well-defined, continuous on
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
S_{n}v
is positive in
\Omega
;

(b)

S_{n}
admits a fixed point, which is positive in
\Omega
.

This fixed point will be referred to as

.

We start with the assertion
(a)
.
The positivity of
S_{n}v
and the fact that
S_{n}
is well defined as the map into
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

follows from Lemma
3.3
, because
G_{n}(v)\geq 0
is nontrivial and belongs to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, when

.

In order to show that

is continuous, we estimate as follows: for

we have

\displaystyle|G_{n}(v_{1})(x)-G_{n}(v_{2})(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(f^{{\langle n\rangle}}(x))^{2}\bigg|\frac{1}{(v_{1}(x)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}-\frac{1}{(v_{2}(x)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}^{2}(f^{{\langle n\rangle}}(x))^{2}|v_{1}(x)-v_{2}(x)|^{2},

where

is the Lipschitz constant of the map
\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto(t^{2}+1/n)^{-\gamma/2}
that depends only on

and
\gamma
. Now, let
\psi_{1}=S_{n}v_{1}
and
\psi_{2}=S_{n}v_{2}
. Then, we have
-P(\psi_{2}-\psi_{1})=G_{n}(v_{2})-G_{n}(v_{1})
and

\|G_{n}(v_{1})-G_{n}(v_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, which together with (

is continuous:

\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|G_{n}(v_{1})-G_{n}(v_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Let us now show

.
By inequality (
3.3
) in Lemma
3.3
, we deduce that for every
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
\psi=S_{n}v
belongs to
H^{2}(\Omega)
and satisfies the Dirichlet condition.
Since
\tau_{n}(v)\leq n^{\gamma/2}
,
f^{{\langle n\rangle}}\leq n
, by (

)

(3.12)

\|\psi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim n^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}},

where the constant depends on

,
\Omega
, and

from (

), but not on

and

. This, and the compactness of the embedding

, see

, implies that

is a compact map on

. Since

is continuous on

and maps the space

into its relatively compact subset, by the Schauder Fixed Point Theorem

we find that it has a fixed point

3.2.2.
Continuity of the approximate solutions up to the boundary and monotonicity of the sequence at fixed

By Lemma

we find that

for every

.
We will now verify that for the functions

obtained above the sequence

Let

and note that on the set

we have

\displaystyle\frac{f^{{\langle m\rangle}}(x)}{\left(u_{m}^{2}(x)+\frac{1}{m}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}-\frac{f^{{\langle n\rangle}}(x)}{\left(u_{n}^{2}(x)+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\leq 0.

Then, by Lemma
3.5

(3.13)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{A}\nabla(u_{m}-u_{n})_{+}\cdot\nabla\phi+(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla(u_{m}-u_{n})_{+})\phi\\
&\leq\int_{\{u_{m}>u_{n}\}}\left(\frac{f^{{\langle m\rangle}}(x)}{\left(u_{m}^{2}(x)+\frac{1}{m}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}-\frac{f^{{\langle n\rangle}}(x)}{\left(u_{n}^{2}(x)+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\right)\phi\leq 0\end{split}

holds for every

such that

.
The inequality (

Local boundedness of the approximate solutions from below

We now check that the sequence

such that

\inf\{u_{n}(x):x\in\omega,\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}\geq c_{\omega}.

Indeed, since
(u_{n}(x))
is nondecreasing, for every

and

(3.14)

u_{n}(x)\geq u_{1}(x)\geq{\rm min}_{\omega}u_{1}=:c_{\omega}>0.

The strict positivity of

.

3.2.4.
Boundedness of the compositions of the approximate solutions in

We now prove that the functions

.

Indeed, by Lemma

\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{\mathbf{A}}^{2}u_{n}^{\gamma-1}\leq C\int_{\Omega}-Pu_{n}u_{n}^{\gamma}=C\int_{\Omega}f^{{\langle n\rangle}}\frac{u_{n}^{\gamma}}{\left(u_{n}^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\leq C\int_{\Omega}f<\infty,

where the second step of the calculation holds because

. By this and the Poincaré inequality,

.

3.2.5.
Convergence of the compositions

We deduce that there exists
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
for any
q\in\left[1,\frac{2d}{d-2}\right)
(for

we put
\frac{2d}{2-2}:=\infty
) we have

(3.16)

w_{n}\rightarrow w\ {\rm in}\ L^{q}(\Omega)\ {\rm and}\ \nabla w_{n}\rightharpoonup\nabla w\ \hbox{\rm weakly in}\ L^{2}(\Omega),\ \ {\rm as}\ n\to\infty.

For this, we first note that there is a measurable almost everywhere finite function
w
such that we have
w_{n}\nearrow w
everywhere in
\Omega
, since the sequence
(w_{n})
is pointwise increasing and bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Furthermore, by Section
3.2.4
and the compact Sobolev embeddings
[
19
, Section 5.7]
,
any subsequence of
(w_{n})
has a subsequence converging in

, whenever

. Since
(w_{n})
converges to

almost everywhere, all these converging subsequences must converge to the same limit

in
L^{q}(\Omega)
. This implies that the whole sequence converges to

in
L^{q}(\Omega)
. Indeed, if it was not true, then we would find a subsequence converging in
L^{q}(\Omega)
to some
\widetilde{w}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
not a.e. equal to

, which contradicts the almost everywhere convergence.

A similar argument via contradiction shows that
(w_{n})
must also converge weakly to

in
H^{1}(\Omega)
.

3.2.6.
Convergence of the approximating sequence and regularity

We will show that for

,

(3.17)

\displaystyle\textnormal{if}\ \gamma\in[1,\infty):

\displaystyle\quad u_{n}\rightarrow u\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega)\text{ and }\nabla u_{n}\rightharpoonup\nabla u\ \hbox{\rm weakly in}\ L^{2}_{loc}(\Omega),

(3.18)

\displaystyle\text{if }\gamma=1:

We first show that

We now check that

4.2.

In order to make the first extension, we will use a Friedrichs-type lemma. The following statement might be known, but we were unable to locate a satisfactory formulation in the literature, so we give a proof for the sake of completeness. See e.g.

Recall that

Lemma 4.2

(Friedrichs-type lemma)
.

Let
Lv=\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla^{(2)}v+b\cdot\nabla v+cv
with

and

\displaystyle\|L(v\ast\varphi_{\epsilon})\|_{\infty}\leq C(\|Lv\|_{\infty}+\|v\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)})

Proof.

Since

are bounded, we have

\displaystyle|(b\cdot\nabla v)\ast\varphi_{\epsilon}+(cv)\ast\varphi_{\epsilon}|\leq(\|b\|_{\infty}+\|c\|_{\infty})\|v\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}.

Therefore it remains to estimate the second-order term. We have by the Dominated Convergence Theorem and integration by parts that

\mathbf{A}\cdot(\nabla^{(2)}(v\ast\varphi_{\epsilon}))(x)=\mathbf{A}(x)\cdot\int_{B(x,\epsilon)}\nabla^{(2)}v(y)\varphi_{\epsilon}(x-y)\,dy,

whence

\displaystyle|\mathbf{A}\cdot(\nabla^{(2)}(v\ast\varphi_{\epsilon}))(x)|\leq

\displaystyle|\varphi_{\epsilon}\ast(\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla^{(2)}v)(x)|

\displaystyle+|\mathbf{A}\cdot(\nabla^{(2)}(v\ast\varphi_{\epsilon}))(x)-\varphi_{\epsilon}\ast(\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla^{(2)}v)(x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|Lv\|_{\infty}+(\|b\|_{\infty}+\|c\|_{\infty})\|v\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\bigg|\int_{B(x,\epsilon)}(\mathbf{A}(x)-\mathbf{A}(y))\cdot\nabla^{(2)}v(y)\varphi_{\epsilon}(x-y)\,dy\bigg|.

We integrate by parts in the last integral and use the triangle inequality, getting

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{B(x,\epsilon)}(\mathbf{A}(x)-\mathbf{A}(y))\cdot\nabla^{(2)}v(y)\varphi_{\epsilon}(x-y)\,dy\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(x,\epsilon)}|(\mathbf{A}(x)-\mathbf{A}(y))\nabla v(y)\cdot\nabla\varphi_{\epsilon}(x-y)|\,dy+\int_{B(x,\epsilon)}\|\mathbf{A}\|_{C^{1}(\omega)}|\nabla v(y)|\varphi_{\epsilon}(x-y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\|\mathbf{A}\|_{C^{1}(\omega)}\bigg(\int_{B(0,\epsilon)}\|x-y\|\epsilon^{-d-1}\nabla\varphi_{1}(y/\epsilon)\,dy+1\bigg)\leq C\|v\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)},

where

u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and
F\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
satisfy
-Pu=F
in the distributional sense, that is,

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}uP^{*}\phi=\int_{\Omega}F\phi,\quad\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega).

Then,

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}uP^{*}\phi=\int_{\Omega}F\phi,\quad\phi\in\mathcal{A},

where
\mathcal{A}
is as in (
4.4
).

Proof.

Take
\phi\in\mathcal{A}
. Then, since
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
, for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
we have
\phi\ast\varphi_{\epsilon}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. By the Dominated Convergence Theorem,
\int F\phi\ast\varphi_{\epsilon}\to\int F\phi
. Furthermore, as
\phi\in W^{2,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
, we have
P^{*}(\phi\ast\varphi_{\epsilon})\to P^{*}\phi
almost everywhere as
\epsilon\to 0
. By the definition of
\mathcal{A}
we have
P^{*}\phi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, therefore the statement of the lemma follows from Lemma
4.2
and the Dominated Convergence Theorem.
∎

4.3.
Weighted integrability of
f/u^{\gamma}

For the purpose of establishing the weighted integrability of
f/u^{\gamma}
, we will use the special test function
\xi
, a strong solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-P^{\ast}\xi=1\quad&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\
\xi=0\quad&{\rm on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

The existence and uniqueness of
\xi
is given by Lemma
4.1
, whence we also have
\xi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\xi>0
in
\Omega
, and
\xi\approx\delta_{\Omega}
.

Since
\xi
cannot be used directly as a test function, we will approximate it by compactly supported functions, as in the following result.

Lemma 4.4

(approximation of
\xi
)
.

Assume that
\Omega\in C^{2}
and that
\mathbf{A}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies (

A

). Then there exists an increasing sequence of positive functions
\xi_{n}\nearrow\xi
such that
\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
(see (
4.4
))
and for almost every
x\in\Omega

-P^{*}\xi_{n}(x)\leq D_{\mathbf{A}},

where
D_{\mathbf{A}}=1+\|(\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}))_{+}\|_{\infty}\|\xi\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Let us start by the construction of
\xi_{n}
.

Consider a convex function
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\Phi
is nondecreasing,
\Phi(x)=0
for
x<\delta
for some
\delta>0
,
\Phi
is Lipschitz with constant
1
(in particular
\Phi(t)\leq t
and
\Phi^{\prime}\leq 1
),
and
\frac{\Phi(t)}{t}\nearrow 1
as
t\to\infty
.

As an example function
\Phi
we can consider
\Phi(t):=\varphi_{\epsilon}*{\rm max}\{0,t-1\}
, where
\varphi_{\epsilon}
is the standard mollifier.

Define

\xi_{n}(x):=\frac{1}{n}\Phi(n\xi(x)).

By the regularity and decay of
\xi
and the definition of
\Phi
we have
\xi_{n}\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
.
Furthermore,

\xi_{n}(x)=\frac{\Phi(n\xi(x))}{n\xi(x)}\xi(x)\leq\xi(x).

and
\Phi(n\xi)/n\xi
converge to 1 as
n\to\infty
, and increase since
\Phi
is convex. This implies that

\xi_{n}(x)\nearrow\xi(x)

for every
x\in\Omega
.

We will show that
the following estimate holds almost everywhere:

\displaystyle-P^{*}\xi_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)+\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})(\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)\cdot\xi-\frac{1}{n}\Phi(n\xi)).

Indeed,
\xi_{n}\in W^{2,1}_{c}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\nabla\xi_{n}

\displaystyle=\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)\nabla\xi,

\displaystyle\nabla^{(2)}\xi_{n}

\displaystyle=\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)\nabla^{(2)}\xi+n\Phi^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(n\xi)\nabla\xi\otimes\nabla\xi,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{A}\nabla\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})\cdot\nabla\xi_{n}+\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla^{(2)}\xi_{n}

\displaystyle=\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})\cdot\nabla\xi+\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla^{(2)}\xi

\displaystyle\quad+n\Phi^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(n\xi)\mathbf{A}\nabla\xi\cdot\nabla\xi

\displaystyle\geq\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{A}\nabla\xi),

where we have used the convexity of
\Phi
and (

A

), which imply
\Phi^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(n\xi)(\mathbf{A}\nabla\xi\cdot\nabla\xi)\geq 0
.
We also have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}\xi_{n})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})\xi_{n}+(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})\cdot\nabla\xi_{n}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})\frac{1}{n}\Phi(n\xi)+\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})\cdot\nabla\xi.

Consequently,

\displaystyle-P^{*}\xi_{n}

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{A}\nabla\xi_{n})-\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}\xi_{n})

\displaystyle\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{1oszac}}}{{\leq}}-\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)[\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{A}\nabla\xi)]-\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla\xi-\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})\frac{1}{n}\Phi(n\xi)

\displaystyle=-\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)[\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{A}\nabla\xi)+\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}\xi)]

\displaystyle\quad-\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)[\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla\xi-\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}\xi)]

\displaystyle\quad-\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})\frac{1}{n}\Phi(n\xi)

\displaystyle=\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)[-P^{*}\xi]+\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)[\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})\xi]-\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})\frac{1}{n}\Phi(n\xi)

\displaystyle=\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)+\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})\left(\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)\,\xi-\frac{1}{n}\Phi(n\xi)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1+\|(\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}))_{+}\|_{\infty}\|\xi\|_{\infty}.

The last estimate holds because

\frac{1}{n\xi}\Phi(n\xi)=\frac{\Phi(n\xi)-\Phi(0)}{n\xi}=\Phi^{\prime}(\zeta)

for some
\zeta\in(0,n\xi)
by the Lagrange Mean Value Theorem, whence the convexity of
\Phi
implies

0\leq\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)-\frac{1}{n\xi}\Phi(n\xi)\leq\Phi^{{}^{\prime}}(n\xi)\leq 1.

It remains to show that
\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
. By recalling (
4.1
) and applying (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) we have

\begin{split}P^{\ast}\xi_{n}&=\Phi^{\prime}(n\xi)P^{\ast}\xi+n\Phi^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(n\xi)\mathbf{A}\nabla\xi\cdot\nabla\xi+\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A})(\xi_{n}-\Phi^{\prime}(n\xi)\xi)\end{split}

As
P^{\ast}\xi=1\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
0\leq\Phi^{\prime}\leq 1
,
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R})
,
\xi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
,
\mathbf{A}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies (

A

), and
\xi_{n}\in C^{1}_{c}(\overline{\Omega})
, we obtain
P^{\ast}\xi_{n}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, hence
\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
, which ends the proof.
∎

Consider

\mathcal{G}=\{u\in L^{1}(\Omega):u\geq 0,\ -Pu\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega),\ -Pu\geq 0\}.

The following lemma shows that
-P(\mathcal{G})\subseteq L^{1}(\Omega,\delta_{\Omega})
.

Lemma 4.5

(weighted integrability of elements
F\in P(\mathcal{G})
)
.

Assume that
\Omega\in C^{2}
and that
\mathbf{A}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies (

A

). If a nonnegative function
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and a nonnegative measurable function
F\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
satisfy
-Pu=F
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
, i.e.

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}uP^{\ast}\phi=\int_{\Omega}F\phi,\qquad\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),

then
F\in L^{1}(\Omega,\delta_{\Omega})
.

Proof.

Let
\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
be the sequence increasing to
\xi
given in Lemma
4.4
. Lemma
4.3
implies that
\xi_{n}
can be taken as test functions in the equation. Using the Fatou lemma, the equation for
u
, and the estimate on
-P^{*}\xi_{n}
in Lemma
4.4
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F\xi\leq\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}F\xi_{n}=\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}-\int_{\Omega}uP^{*}\xi_{n}\leq D_{\mathbf{A}}\int_{\Omega}u=D_{\mathbf{A}}\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}<\infty,

which ends the proof, as
\xi\gtrsim\delta_{\Omega}
by Lemma
4.1
part
ii)
ii)(c)
.
∎

4.4.
Very weak solutions

Following
[
42
]
we define

(4.7)

C^{1,P}_{0}(\overline{\Omega}):=\{\phi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}):\phi\equiv 0\ \textnormal{pointwise on}\ \partial\Omega\ {\rm and}\ P^{*}\phi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\}.

The expression
P^{*}\phi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
should be understood in the sense of distributions, but under the assumptions of Lemma
4.1
it implies that
\phi\in W^{2,1}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
, so
P^{*}\phi
can be computed almost everywhere, yielding a function in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, the same Lemma implies that
\phi\in C^{1,P}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies
\phi\lesssim\delta_{\Omega}
. In
[
42
, Definition 2.3]
, the space
C^{1,P}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})
is used as the space of the test functions to define the so-called very weak solutions.

Definition 4.6

(very weak solutions)
.

We say that
v
is a very weak solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-Pu=F\quad&{\rm in}\ \Omega\\
u\equiv 0\quad&{\rm on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{cases}

if

(4.8)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}uP^{*}\phi=\int_{\Omega}F\phi,\quad\phi\in C^{1,P}_{0}(\overline{\Omega}).

Remark 4.7
.

The Dirichlet boundary condition is not explicitly specified in the definition, but it is not an omission. The zero boundary values are manifested by the fact that there is no boundary integral in (
4.8
) despite the fact that the test functions are not compactly supported. See
[
42
, Definition 2.3]
for an analog for nonzero boundary values.

The following lemma is one of the key arguments to deduce uniqueness. We believe that it is of independent interest, because of the very weak assumptions on the solution.

Lemma 4.8

(distributional solution is a very weak solution)
.

Assume that
\Omega\in C^{2}
and that
\mathbf{A}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies (

A

). Assume that nonnegative
v\in L^{1}(\Omega)
satisfies
-Pv=F\in L^{1}(\Omega,\delta_{\Omega})
in the distributional sense:

(4.9)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}vP^{*}\phi=\int_{\Omega}F\phi,\quad\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),

and
v
satisfies the zero trace condition (
Tr
). Then
v
is also a very weak solution to
-Pu=F
.

Proof of Lemma
4.8
.

Let us fix for every small
\epsilon>0
a function
\eta_{\epsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
0\leq\eta_{\epsilon}\leq 1
,
\eta_{\epsilon}(x)=1
for
\delta_{\Omega}(x)>2\epsilon
,
\eta_{\epsilon}(x)=0
for
\delta_{\Omega}(x)<\epsilon
, and
\|\nabla\eta_{\epsilon}\|_{\infty}\leq C\epsilon^{-1}
and
\|\nabla^{(2)}\eta_{\epsilon}\|_{\infty}\leq C\epsilon^{-2}
for some fixed
C>0
independent of
\epsilon
.

First, recall that by Lemma
4.3
the class of the functions can be extended to

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\{\phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)\cap W^{2,1}(\Omega):P^{*}\phi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\}.

Then, for any
\phi\in C^{1,P}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\epsilon>0
, we have

(4.10)

\begin{split}P^{*}(\eta_{\epsilon}\phi)&=\eta_{\epsilon}P^{*}\phi+2\mathbf{A}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\eta_{\epsilon}+\phi(\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{A}\nabla\eta_{\epsilon})+\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla\eta_{\epsilon})\\
&=\eta_{\epsilon}P^{*}\phi+2\mathbf{A}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\eta_{\epsilon}+\phi(\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla^{(2)}\eta_{\epsilon}+2\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla\eta_{\epsilon})\end{split}

Hence,
\phi\eta_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{A}
and so

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}vP^{*}(\eta_{\epsilon}\phi)=\int_{\Omega}F\eta_{\epsilon}\phi,\quad\phi\in C_{0}^{1,P}(\Omega).

Since
\phi(x)\lesssim\delta_{\Omega}(x)
(see Lemma
4.1
part
ii)

ii)(b)
) and
F\in L^{1}(\Omega,\delta_{\Omega})
, the right-hand side converges by the Dominated Convergence Theorem to
\int_{\Omega}F\phi
as
\epsilon\to 0
.

It remains to show that the left-hand side converges to
-\int_{\Omega}vP^{*}\phi
, for which we use (
4.10
). By the Dominated Convergence Theorem and the boundedness of
P^{*}\phi
we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}v\eta_{\epsilon}P^{*}\phi=\int_{\Omega}vP^{*}\phi.

Furthermore,
\phi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\phi\lesssim\delta_{\Omega}
, therefore by the properties of
\eta_{\epsilon}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v|2\mathbf{A}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\eta_{\epsilon}+\phi(\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{A}\nabla\eta_{\epsilon})+\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla\eta_{\epsilon})|\lesssim\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\int_{\{y\in\Omega:\delta_{\Omega}(y)<2\epsilon\}}v,

which converges to 0 by the Dirichlet condition (
Tr
) for
v
. This ends the proof.
∎

4.5.
The Kato-type inequality for very weak solutions

The lemma below is an a priori bound for solutions to linear equations with the right-hand side in the weighted
L^{1}
space. We refer to e.g.
[
14
]
for more results and context on equations of this type.

Lemma 4.9

(the estimates in the
L^{1}
framework)
.

Assume that
\Omega\in C^{2}
and that
\mathbf{A}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies (

A

). Suppose that
F\in L^{1}(\Omega,\delta_{\Omega})
and
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
is a very weak solution to
-Pu=F
. Then, there exists
C=C(d,\Omega,c_{\mathbf{A}},\|\mathbf{A}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})})
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{1}(\Omega,\delta_{\Omega})}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
there exists a unique weak solution
\phi_{u}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-P^{*}\phi_{u}={\rm sgn}\,u\quad&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\
\phi_{u}\equiv 0\quad&{\rm on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

We also have
\phi_{u}\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
,
-P^{\ast}\phi_{u}={\rm sgn}\,u
a.e. in
\Omega
and
\phi_{u}\equiv 0
pointwise on
\partial\Omega
. Lemma
4.1
part
ii)

ii)(b)
and the fact that
\|{\rm sgn}\,u\|_{\infty}\leq 1
together imply that
\|\phi_{u}/\delta_{\Omega}\|_{\infty}
is bounded independently of
u
. We also have
\phi_{u}\in C^{1,P}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})
, so we can use it as the test function in (
4.8
), getting

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u|=-\int_{\Omega}uP^{*}\phi_{u}=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{u}F\leq C\int_{\Omega}|F|\delta_{\Omega},

with
C
independent of
u
, which ends the proof.
∎

The proof of the next lemma is similar to that given by Oliva and Petitta
[
38
, Lemma 2.9]
for operators of the form
\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{A}\nabla u)
.

Lemma 4.10

(The Kato-type inequality for very weak solutions)
.

Assume that
\Omega\in C^{2}
and that
\mathbf{A}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies (

A

). Assume that
F\in L^{1}(\Omega,\delta_{\Omega})
and let
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
be a very weak solution to
-Pu=F
. Then,

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}u_{+}P^{*}\phi\leq\int_{\Omega\cap\{u\geq 0\}}\phi F

holds for all
\phi\in C^{1,P}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\phi\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
F_{n}\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
approximate
F
in the
\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}(\Omega,\delta_{\Omega})}
norm, see e.g.
[
21
, Proposition 7.9]
. Then, by Lemma
3.3
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-Pu_{n}=F_{n}\quad&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\
u_{n}\equiv 0\quad&{\rm on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{cases}

has a unique strong solution
u_{n}
such that
u_{n}\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
. By the interior regularity theory, see e.g.
[
24
, Theorem 9.19]
, and the Sobolev embedding we also have
u_{n}\in C^{2}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, by Lemma
4.9
we have
\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. By taking a subsequence we can assume that
u_{n}
also converge almost everywhere to
u
.

Consider convex functions
\Phi_{\epsilon}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
that satisfy
\Phi_{\epsilon}(0)=0
,
\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(x)=1
for
x\geq 0
,
\Phi_{\epsilon}(x)\geq-\epsilon
for
x<0
,
0\leq\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\leq 1
,
\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}\leq C\epsilon^{-1}
. Note that
\Phi_{\epsilon}(u_{n})\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
and

\displaystyle P(\Phi_{\epsilon}(u_{n}))=\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{n})Pu_{n}+\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}(u_{n})\mathbf{A}\nabla u_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{n}.

For
\phi\in C^{1,P}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})
we have (by Lemma
4.1
)
\phi\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
. Thus, if
\phi\geq 0
, then using integration by parts, (

A

), and the convexity of
\Phi
,

(4.11)

\begin{split}-\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}(u_{n})P^{\ast}\phi=-\int_{\Omega}P\Phi_{\epsilon}(u_{n})\phi&=-\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{n})Pu_{n}\phi-\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}(u_{n})(\mathbf{A}\nabla u_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{n})\phi\\
&\leq-\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{n})Pu_{n}\phi\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{n})F_{n}\phi,\end{split}

where the last equality holds thanks to
u_{n}
being strong solutions. We now examine the convergence of the leftmost and the rightmost expressions in (
4.11
). Since
\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}
is bounded, we have
|\Phi_{\epsilon}(u_{n})-\Phi_{\epsilon}(u)|\leq C|u_{n}-u|
, therefore since
\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\to 0
and
P^{*}\phi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{\epsilon}(u_{n})P^{\ast}\phi-\Phi_{\epsilon}(u)P^{\ast}\phi\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\|P^{*}\phi\|_{\infty}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\to\infty}0.

For the rightmost expression we use the fact that every
\phi\in C^{1,P}_{0}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies
\phi\lesssim\delta_{\Omega}
, which follows from Lemma
4.1

ii)(b)
:

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{n})F_{n}\phi-\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u)F\phi\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|F_{n}-F\|_{L^{1}(\Omega,\delta_{\Omega})}+\int_{\Omega}|\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{n})-\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u)||F|\delta_{\Omega}.

Both expressions tend to 0: the first one because of the assumption on
F_{n}
and the second one due to the Dominated Convergence Theorem, because
u_{n}
converge to
u
a.e. and the integrand is majorized by
2|F|\delta_{\Omega}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}(u)P^{\ast}\phi\leq\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u)F\phi.

We have
|\Phi_{\epsilon}(u)|\leq\epsilon+u_{+}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and
\Phi_{\epsilon}(u)\to u_{+}
as
\epsilon\to 0
. At the same time,
|\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u)|\leq 1
and, due to its monotonicity,
\Phi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u)\to\textbf{1}_{u\geq 0}
as
\epsilon\to 0
. Finally,
P^{\ast}\phi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
|F\phi|\lesssim|F|\delta_{\Omega}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Hence, we obtain the statement of the lemma by the Dominated Convergence Theorem.
∎

4.6.
Proof of the uniqueness theorem

We are now ready to prove our main uniqueness theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
u,v\in L^{1}(\Omega)
be distributional solutions to (
2.3
). By Lemma
4.5
we find that
F_{u}:=f/u^{\gamma}
,
F_{v}:=f/v^{\gamma}
belong to
L^{1}(\Omega,\delta_{\Omega})
. Furthermore, by Lemma
4.8
,
u
is a very weak solution to
-Pu=F_{u}
and
v
is a very weak solution to
-Pv=F_{v}
. By subtracting the very weak formulations, we obtain

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}(u-v)P^{*}\phi=\int_{\Omega}\phi(F_{u}-F_{v}),\quad\phi\in C^{1,P}_{0}(\Omega).

If we select a test function satisfying
\phi\geq 0
, then by Lemma
4.10
we get

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}(u-v)_{+}P^{*}\phi\leq\int_{\Omega\cap\{u\geq v\}}\phi(F_{u}-F_{v})\leq 0,

with the last inequality following from the fact that
F_{u}\leq F_{v}
on
\{u\geq v\}
. In particular, by taking
\phi=\xi
defined in Section
4.3
, we get
(u-v)_{+}\equiv 0
. By switching signs we also get
(v-u)_{+}\equiv 0
, which means that
u=v
almost everywhere.
∎

5.
Final discussion

With additional information about
f
we can deduce better regularity of
u
.

Remark 5.1

(better regularity)
.

When
f
has better integrability properties, we obtain better regularity. For example, if

(5.1)

\frac{f}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{\gamma}}\in L^{p}(\Omega)

for some
1<p<\infty
, we obtain that
u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
. This follows from the elliptic regularity theory
[
24
, Theorem 9.15]
and the fact that
u(x)\gtrsim\delta_{\Omega}(x)
for all
x\in\Omega
.

The condition (
5.1
) holds for example if we assume that
f\in W^{\lceil\gamma\rceil,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, where
\lceil\gamma\rceil
is the least integer larger than or equal to
\gamma
, as follows from the famous characterization of this space via the distance function: it holds for
f\in W^{m,p}(\Omega)
that

f\in W^{m,p}_{0}(\Omega)\iff\frac{f}{\delta_{\Omega}(x)^{m}}\in L^{p}(\Omega),

see e.g.
[
29
, Theorem 1]
for this result in Lipschitz domains and e.g.
[
4
]
,
[
18
, Theorem V.3.4 and Remark X.6.8]
,
[
32
]
, and
[
37
]
for further extensions and additional context (with the Introduction of
[
37
]
providing a nice modern overview). More precise conditions could also be expressed in terms of the fractional Sobolev spaces, see e.g.
[
17
, Theorem 3]
or
[
31
]
.

Open problems
.

Let us finally state some open problems that we believe to be of interest.

Open Problem 5.2
.

In this paper we have examined the solutions to the PDE of the form

(5.2)

-Pu=f(x)\tau(u),

where
u
is positive,
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
is positive and

\tau(u)=u^{-\gamma}
and the operator
P
is second-order in nondivergence form.
One could ask about existence and uniqueness of solutions to (
5.2
) with the possibly general nonlinearity

\tau(\cdot)
and other operators
P
, for example ones with less regular matrices
\mathbf{A}
, or nonlocal operators with variable coefficients (see
[
5
]
for the case of the fractional Laplacian).

Open Problem 5.3
.

In Theorem
1.1

(b)
it was shown that the solution
u
belongs to
H^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,q}(\Omega)
with any
1\leq q<\frac{2}{\gamma}
, for
\gamma\in(1,2)
. It would be interesting to know if
u
belongs to
W_{0}^{1,1}(\Omega)
, when
\gamma=2
and if one could conclude some more precise regularity when
\gamma>2
.
