Final Technical Report for DOE grant: Energy Transport and Dissipation above Aurorae
DOE Grant Number : DE-SC0022066
Principal Investigator: Christopher Chaston
Institution: University of California at Berkeley

1. What are the major goals of the project?

The objective is to exploit measurements from the Reimei Spacecraft to resolve the energy transport and
plasma dynamics supporting energy conversion above bright discrete aurora.

2. What was accomplished under these goals?

In the final period of performance, we analyzed the electrodynamics of several auroral arcs observed by the
Reimi spacecraft for which the imaging and particle measurements were suitable for the application of the
Poisson solver approach developed in previous years. This involved the evaluation of the 2-dimensional
projection of the electrostatic and vector potentials, energy deposition and field-aligned current and the
perpendicular field vectors. These quantities allowed a decomposition of the forces active in defining the
form and evolution of the auroral arc, the energy flows powering the energy deposition and the dissipation
of this energy as field-aligned electron acceleration. One of these events was selected for publication and
is included in a manuscript describing the new analysis technique and the plasma dynamics active within
the arc. This work is currently under review with the Journal of Geophysical Research (See Publications
Detail).

To demonstrate Figure 1 shows a snapshot of measurements returned by the Poisson solver technique for
the auroral arc considered in the aforementioned publication. The panels shown capture the dynamics of a
rapidly evolving auroral arc as a consequence of magnetic tearing and the sheared flow in the plasma across
the arc. This occurs as a form of extreme guide field magnetic reconnection that provides the magnetic field
topology shown by the solid black contours on panels c to m. This process defines the morphology of the
magnetic and inertial forces acting on the plasma comprising the arc as shown in panels g and h. These
forces in turn determine the evolution of the auroral form manifest in changes between image frames as
described in the manuscript. The associated energy flows into the arc shown in panels i, j and k deliver
energy to support dissipation. The divergence of these energy flows shown in panels l and m capture the
operation of energy conversion to support dissipation (luminosity) within the arc. While we cannot claim
universality of this physics for auroral arc formation from these results alone, similar dynamics have been
identified in alternate arcs considered as part of this research.

The completion of this final period of research has built on that performed in previous years including the
performance of 3-D fluid kinetic simulation and the quantification and conservation of energy transport to
achieve our goal to resolve the energy transport and plasma dynamics supporting energy conversion
above bright discrete aurora. This has included:

1. The development of an innovative approach for the analysis of auroral imagery that, when combined
 with magnetically connected particle measurements, images the detailed plasma dynamics operating
 through auroral arcs. This technique provides movies of the plasma dynamics active within with auroral
 arcs that confirm the results from the fluid kinetic simulation performed earlier as part of this research
   (Chaston, 2024; Chaston, 2025).

2. The imaging of the forces active on the plasmas within the acceleration region defining the evolving
 forms observed (Chaston, 2025).

3. The imaging of energy transport within auroral arcs and of the divergence of these flows (Chaston,
 2025).

4. The demonstration that the divergence of the full Poynting vector including components along and
 across the geomagnetic field match the energy conversion occurring thorough the arc both locally and
 in integral form (Chaston, 2024, Chaston, 2025).

5. The resolution of the operation of magnetic tearing with an auroral arc through a complete
 decomposition of the fields, forces and energy flows supporting dissipation in the arc (Chaston, 2025).

3. What opportunities for training and professional development has the project provided?

A postdoctoral scholar has in part been supported by this research funding. The PI mentors this individual's
research on this project. He receives instruction in data analysis approaches and interpretation as well as
taking part in ongoing discussions of the relevant theory.

4. How have the results been disseminated to communities of interest?

Since the last report, this research has formed the subject matter for all, or part of, two contributed
conference papers, one invited seminar and one published journal paper with another currently under
review. The PI also took part in a community forum published on the University of California News website
concerning the large geomagnetic storm of May 2004 in which aurora was visible from California. The PI
also co-organizer and was science program coordinator for a conference held at UC Berkeley in April 2025
and a symposium on auroral dynamics as part of the IAGA-IASPEI general assembly in Lisbon during
September 2025.

IMPACT

1. What is the impact on the development of the principal discipline(s) of the project?

The aurora is perhaps the most obvious manifestation of the coupling of the Earth to the space plasma
environment in which it is embedded. ‘Textbook’ understanding of this coupling is based on idealized
models that do not describe observations of rapidly evolving auroral forms characteristic of bright auroral
displays. The research performed advances understanding of the plasma dynamics responsible for these
features. The type of measurements developed through this research are to our knowledge not possible in
any other plasma environment and are accessible elsewhere only via numerical simulations. Multipoint
observations, for example provide slices through the region of interest, whereas here the region is imaged
in 2‐D as a function of time with quantitative validation of the imaging along the spacecraft trajectory from
direct plasma measurements. This capacity impacts auroral and space plasma physics by facilitating new
insights into energy transport through the auroral acceleration region. The development of the analysis
technique performed in this research is perhaps the first imaging of the operation of magnetic reconnection
ever performed in a natural system. This approach provides movies of the operation of this process in
electromagnetic fields, flows, energy transport, and dissipation.

2. What is the impact on other disciplines?

Energy transport and conversion is central to the understanding of plasma contexts extending from the near‐
space environment to the solar corona and planetary and pulsar magnetospheres. In the research performed
we have examined the relationship between transport, in the form of fluid motions, and dissipation, manifest
as optical emission, for signatures of basic plasma processes such as shear flow instabilities and magnetic
reconnection. These processes are known and/or thought to be widespread in space and astrophysical
plasmas where energy conversion is occurring, yet it is often the case that the operation of these processes
cannot be resolved nor can the evolution they drive be followed. The research performed therefore has
impact beyond the physics of the aurora because it has provided the capacity to image fundamental plasma

processes associated with the transport and conversion of energy that is not resolvable in other natural
contexts.

6. What is the impact on society beyond science and technology?

The aurora is a natural phenomenon of general societal interest and is often the subject of documentaries
for public consumption. The research performed in this reporting period provides a methodology for the
extraction of the contributions to energy transport that support auroral particle energization. This goes to
the core of how the aurora works and therefore, given the public interest in this natural phenomenon, this
research has societal impact. This societal impact was manifested through public outreach from invited
participation in the news article The Northern Lights came to California — but will it happen again? |
University of California.
