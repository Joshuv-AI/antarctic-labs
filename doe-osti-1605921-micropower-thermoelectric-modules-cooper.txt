 Overall Project Report:

 Below is a set of captured ppt slides from the final work done under the project. Figure 1
 illustrates the overall device concept that was underlying our drive to incorporate and understand
 thermoelectric devices for solar energy conversion with integrated storage. A key factor was that
 at the time the project was initiated there was very little understanding on how to accurately
 measure thermoelectric conversion efficiency. We initiated partnerships with a number of
 thermoelectric suppliers including MicroPower to validate conversion efficiency and if sufficient
 incorporate in the overall system.

 Figure 4 illustrates the typical conversion curves for a TE device and the following one the
 power conversion curves for the MicroPower devices. The first figure illustrates the
 methodology employed in the MicroPower CRADA work. The second illustrates actual data
 from their devices. Note the conversion efficiencies for isolated devices is good for this
 generation of devices but is not repeatable and in fact there were numerous device failures. Not
 surprising in this generation device but the overall brittle character of the devices made
 incorporation into an array prohibitive. We also note that micropower TE also could not operate
 to the desired temperatures.

 Figure 4
 We visited MicroPower on a number of occasions both to learn more about their technology and
 to understand why we were seeing high failure rates. The visits were valuable and led to
 enhanced collaboration and the development of improved measurement capabilities on both sides
 but there were still too many failures. The picture in Figure 5 shows visit of Phil Parilla and
 Michele Olsen of NREL and their measurement station.

 Summary of TEG testing measurements:

 • We have measured 8 different TEG modules (5 different models) from 4 TEG
 manufacturers.
 • Only the Marlow and Micropower TEGs show promise for high efficiency. But complete
 calibration was not possible due to many device failures.
 • We made additional modifications to the testing apparatus to enable more even clamping
 to reduce the potential of damaging the TEGs. We measured all provided devices
 • This experience will inform design decisions for mounting the TEGs in the STEALS V1
 prototype.

 Overall path forward:

 • We fully evaluated the Micropower devices under the CRADA and extrapolated the
 performance and that of the other TE companies against the needed performance.
 • Production of devices from all suppliers was not sufficient for the prototype
 • Consequently we could not move forward with the development of a larger scale
 prototype knowing that the device yield and performance were not sufficient.
 • At the current time TE devices were forgone for the potential of the Stirling engine.
 • Subsequently this decision was borne out with much higher system performance.

 Task 3: NREL will conduct initial reliability testing as a function of temperature

 Second generation of thermoelectric modules were never received from MicroPower Global to
 continue with reliability testing as a function of temperature.

 Task 4: NREL will conduct reliability testing versus cycle life at temperature

 Second generation of thermoelectric modules were never received to continue with life cycle
 testing as a function of temperature. The overall project was testing thermoelectric modules from
 Marlow, JPL and MicroPower Global. The modules from Marlow and JPL were taken forward
 into the more complicated integration phase and the modules from MicroPower Global were not
 advanced due to lack of performance and temperature capability.

 Task 5: MicroPower will design incorporation of MicroPower modules into STEALS
 testing platform (design phase)

 Task 5 was never initiated based on the results of Tasks 1-4 due to lack of sufficient
 performance. These tests were not applicable to the MicroPower Global modules because we
 went forward with other suppliers.

 Task 6: Construction and evaluation of Final TE array for 1kW scale

 Task 6 was never initiated based on the results of Tasks 1-4 due to lack of sufficient
 performance. These tests were not applicable to the MicroPower Global modules because we
 went forward with other suppliers.

 Task 7: MicroPower and NREL will perform a technoeconomic analysis task for
