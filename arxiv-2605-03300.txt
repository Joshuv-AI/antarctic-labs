Smoothed estimation of Wasserstein barycenters
P.Pengtao Li, C.Changbo Zhu, XXiaohui Chen

Abstract
This paper studies the statistical estimation of exact Wasserstein barycenters. Existing non-asymptotic results for empirical barycenters exhibit a severe curse of dimensionality. Motivated by the semi-dual formulation of the barycenter problem and its associated Sobolev optimization geometry, we develop a smoothness-aware approach that combines density estimation with Sobolev geometric structure to estimate the population barycenter. We establish nonparametric convergence rates for estimating both the barycenter functional and its minimizer, demonstrating how smoothness can substantially improve statistical performance.

Introduction

Optimal transport (OT) has emerged as a central tool for comparing and averaging probability distributions, with applications spanning statistics, machine learning, computer vision, and the natural sciences
(
38
,
44
,
32
,
37
,
18
,
43
)
.
Among its many constructions, the Wasserstein barycenter based on the theory of OT provides a “horizontal" notion of the mean for distribution-valued data, capturing subtle geometric structure that is insensitive to “vertical" or pointwise averaging. Formally, the Wasserstein barycenter was introduced in
(
1
)
as an intrinsic notion of averaging under the
2
-Wasserstein metric. Given
m
probability measures
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}
supported on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite second moments and a barycentric coordinate vector
\bm{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{m})
satisfying
\omega_{j}\geq 0
and
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\omega_{j}=1
, the Wasserstein barycenter
\bar{\mu}_{m}
is defined as any minimizer of the weighted variance functional
V:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
via

\displaystyle\min_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)}\Big\{V(\mu):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\omega_{j}}{2}W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\mu_{j})\Big\},

(1)

where
W_{2}(\mu,\nu)
denotes the 2-Wasserstein distance between
\mu
and
\nu
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
is the space of all probability measures on
\Omega
with finite second moments. Despite its wide applications, the Wasserstein barycenter exhibits several structural properties that make both computation and statistical estimation intrinsically challenging. In contrast to the variational formulation of the Euclidean average, the objective functional
V
is not geodesically convex in the natural
W_{2}
geometry beyond two-marginal measures
(
3
,
13
)
. Moreover in such regime
m\geq 3
, the Wasserstein barycenter has much less regularity than the Euclidean sample mean, and it is unstable to small perturbations, even when its uniqueness is ensured
(
23
,
34
,
9
,
44
)
.

In practice, one often only has access to samples from the probability measures and must deal with the additional discretization and stochastic errors when approximating the population Wasserstein barycenter. In particular, if all input measures are discrete such as empirical distributions from samples, then the resulting barycenters are also discrete, and one can always choose a version that is provably sparse
(
4
)
, imposing a fundamental obstacle in obtaining a reliable estimator of an absolutely continuous population barycenter. As a consequence of these roadblocks to performing statistical estimation of Wasserstein barycenters, most of the existing results focus on the regularized barycenter
(
6
,
10
,
15
)
. With entropy regularization, the multi-marginal system (
1
) is much better posed with smoothness and stability, and the estimation of the associated barycenters admits substantially stronger and more reliable statistical guarantees
(
25
)
. Nonetheless, this comes at the cost of altering the underlying optimal transport geometry, with unfavorable dependence on the (small) regularization parameter.

1.1
Contributions

This paper narrows this gap by studying the statistical sample complexity for estimating the
exact
Wasserstein barycenter from point cloud data. Our motivation stems from recent advances in barycenter optimization that reveal strong duality in the Sobolev geometry for the Kantorovich potentials
(
22
,
21
)
. By integrating such optimization geometry and density estimation technique, we introduce a semi-dual formulation-based approach that utilizes the smoothness of the marginal input distributions.
Under a standard sampling model and assumptions, we establish nonparametric estimation rates for the barycenter cost functional
\mathscr{E}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}):=\min_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)}V(\mu)
and the Wasserstein barycenter
\bar{\mu}_{m}
. While existing non-asymptotic bounds (cf. (
2
) below) for empirical Wasserstein barycenters
(
9
)
apply under minimal assumptions and consequently suffer from severe curse-of-dimensionality effects, our analysis shows that additional smoothness of the marginal distributions can substantially improve statistical rates in a way similar to density estimation under the
W_{p}
metric
(
28
)
. As an extension of our result, we also derive sample complexity guarantees under a two-layer sampling model. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to establish smoothness-aware nonparametric rates for exact Wasserstein barycenter related quantities.

1.2
Related work

There are relatively scarce works establishing non-asymptotic statistical guarantees for estimating unregularized Wasserstein barycenters from data. Suppose that for each
j\in[m]
, one observes independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) samples
\{X_{j1},\dots,X_{jn}\}\sim\mu_{j}
, and that the samples across different
j
are independent.
In this setting,
9
studied the empirical Wasserstein barycenter associated with the empirical measures
\hat{\mu}_{j}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{X_{ji}}
, defined as
\hat{\mu}^{(n)}_{m}\in\argmin_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\hat{\mu}_{j}).
Under suitable regularity conditions, they established the following error bounds

\mathbb{E}W_{2}^{2}(\hat{\mu}^{(n)}_{m},\bar{\mu}_{m})\lesssim\begin{cases}n^{-1/12},&d<4,\\
n^{-1/12}(\log n)^{1/6},&d=4,\\
n^{-1/3d},&d>4.\end{cases}

(2)

When
\mu_{j}
’s are further discrete measures with
N
finite support points, an improved
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N/n})
rate of convergence for estimating barycenter functional was recently obtained
(
31
)
.

There is another parallel line of works considering the convergence of barycenter problems with measures being directly observed (without point clouds or further discretization) and sampled in the Wasserstein space, i.e., the barycenter associated with
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
defined as

\mu^{*}\in\displaystyle\argmin_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)d\mathbb{P}(\nu).

Suppose that
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}
are random elements drawn independently from
\mathbb{P}
, and every
\mu\in\text{supp}(\mathbb{P})
is the pushforward of the barycenter
\mu^{*}
by the gradient of a
\kappa
-strongly convex and
\lambda
-smooth function with
\lambda-\kappa<1
(a.k.a. Assumption
4
in Section
4
),
24
showed that

\mathbb{E}W_{2}^{2}(\bar{\mu}_{m},\mu^{*})\lesssim m^{-1},

where
\bar{\mu}_{m}
is the equally weighted barycenter of
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}
. Specializing to Gaussian measures,
13
derived global algorithmic convergence rates of gradient methods for estimating the Bures-Wasserstein barycenter together with their statistical sample complexity. Under milder assumptions,
9

\mathbb{E}W_{2}(\bar{\mu}_{m},\mu^{*})\lesssim m^{-1/30}.

2
Smoothness-aware semi-dual approach

In this section, we introduce the semi-dual formulation-based approach for barycenter estimation that leverages the smoothness of the marginal input distributions. The resulting estimator is shown to mitigate the curse-of-dimensionality in Section
3
.

2.1
Semi-dual formulation of barycenter cost functional

We begin with the dual formulation of the Wasserstein barycenter functional
\mathscr{E}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m})=\min_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)}V(\mu)
in (
1
). Define

\mathscr{D}_{[\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}]}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m-1})=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\omega_{i}\int f_{i}^{c}\,d\mu_{i}+\omega_{m}\int f_{\text{mix}}^{c}\,d\mu_{m},

(3)

where

f_{\text{mix}}=-\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{\omega_{j}}{\omega_{m}}f_{j}
. As shown by
22
, for absolutely continuous probabilities
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}
, strong duality holds:

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m})=\sup_{f_{1},\dots,f_{m-1}}\mathscr{D}_{[\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}]}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m-1}),

(4)

where the supremum is over continuous functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{m-1}
. One could easily check that for
a_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
(f_{*,1}+a_{1},\dots,f_{*,m-1}+a_{m-1})
also optimizes the equation above if

(f_{*,1},\dots,f_{*,m-1})
does. To account for this translation invariance of dual potentials, we distinguish the optimal dual potential
(f_{*,1},\dots,f_{*,m-1})
with
\sup_{x_{i}\in\Omega}f_{*,i}(x_{i})=0,\,i\in[m-1]
from now on. We refer to
\mathbf{f}_{*}:=(f_{*,1},\dots,f_{*,m-1},f_{*,\text{mix}})
as the Kantorovich potential and
the unique Wasserstein barycenter
\bar{\mu}_{m}
can be characterized by

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}_{m}=T_{f^{c}_{*,i}}\sharp\mu_{i}=T_{f^{c}_{*,\text{mix}}}\sharp\mu_{m},

(5)

where
T_{h}:=\text{id}-\nabla h
.

2.2
Smoothed barycenter estimation

Based on the semi-dual formulation (
4
) of the barycenter functional, we are now ready to present our smoothing approach for barycenter estimation in this section. We work with samples
\{X_{ji}:j\in[m],\,i\in[n]\}
and first construct a smoothed estimator of the marginal probability density from the data. Concretely, it is obtained by expanding the unknown density in a sufficiently regular wavelet basis
(
19
)
, estimating each wavelet coefficient via its empirical average from the samples, and then truncating the resulting expansion
(
28
,
26
,
36
,
40
)
. In particular, for density
\mu_{j}
with upper and lower bounds and certain regularity (i.e., Assumptions
1
-
2
below), one can construct a smoothed version
\tilde{\mu}_{j}
of the empirical distribution with required level of accuracy as in Lemma
3.1
.
Then we solve the semi-dual (
3
) with the smoothing estimate of the marginal densities and reconstruct the barycenter using the pushforward relation (
5
). Define

\mathbb{F}_{\alpha,\beta}:=\Bigl\{f\in C^{1,1}(\Omega)\;\big|\;\text{ for any}\>x,y\in\Omega,\;\varphi(\cdot):=\frac{\|\cdot\|^{2}}{2}-f(\cdot),\\
\frac{\alpha}{2}\,\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)-\langle\nabla\varphi(y),x-y\rangle\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}\Bigr\}.

(6)

Our pseudo-algorithm is summarized in Algorithm
1
.

Algorithm 1

Smoothness-aware barycenter estimation procedure.

1:

Data points
X_{j1},\dots,X_{jn}
for
j\in[m]
.

2:

Construct smoothed density estimator
\tilde{\mu}_{j}
from
X_{j1},\dots,X_{jn}
using truncated wavelet expansion.

3:

Maximize
\widetilde{\mathscr{D}}(\mathbf{f}):=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\omega_{i}\int f_{i}^{c}\,d\tilde{\mu}_{i}+\omega_{m}\int f_{\text{mix}}^{c}\,d\tilde{\mu}_{m}
over
\mathbb{F}^{m}_{\alpha,\beta}
and extract the solution
(\tilde{f}_{*,1},\dots,\tilde{f}_{*,\text{mix}})
.

4:

Set
\tilde{\mathbf{f}}_{*}=(\tilde{f}_{*,1},\dots,\tilde{f}_{*,m-1})
and
\widetilde{\mathscr{E}}_{n}=\widetilde{\mathscr{D}}(\tilde{\mathbf{f}}_{*})
.

5:

return
Smoothed empirical barycenter
\tilde{\mu}_{m}^{(n)}=T_{\tilde{f}_{*,i}^{c}}\sharp\tilde{\mu}_{i}
.

In practice, the optimization step in Algorithm
1
is solved using a Sobolev gradient ascent (SGA) algorithm proposed in
22
, which focuses on the algorithmic convergence rate for absolutely continuous marginal input distributions over a broader potential functional class
\mathbb{F}^{m}_{0,\infty}
. Different from existing sub-linear algorithmic guarantees
(
22
,
21
)
, statistical sample complexity needs to operate on a smaller subclass of potentials with
0<\alpha\leq\beta<\infty
. Within this
\mathbb{F}^{m}_{\alpha,\beta}
class, we will see that the semi-dual objective functional has a strong curvature lower bound in the Sobolev geometry; see Lemma
2.3
. This strong curvature property turns out to be a key structural ingredient enabling our nonparametric convergence analysis in Section
3
. We remark that our procedure is agnostic to the construction of an estimator

\tilde{\mu}_{j}

from discrete samples, namely one can adopt other density estimators than the wavelet expansion, and our analysis is robust to the choice of the initial density estimation.

2.3
Sobolev optimization geometry

In this section, we recognize an appropriate optimization dual
geometry for the Wasserstein barycenter problem (
1
), which will serve as a foundation for subsequent statistical complexity in Section
3
. Specifically, by identifying the Sobolev geometric structure of the semi-dual functional
\mathscr{D}_{[\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}]}(\cdot)
that
respects the structure of the Wasserstein barycenter, we pave the way for nonparametric rate of convergence.

We endow the dual optimization problem (
3
)
with a Sobolev product–Hilbert geometry, which will play a central role in our statistical analysis.
Specifically, let

\mathscr{H}_{m-1}:=(\dot{\mathbb{H}}^{1}(\Omega))^{m-1}

denote the Cartesian product of
(m-1)
copies of the homogeneous Sobolev space

\dot{\mathbb{H}}^{1}(\Omega)
. For

\bm{\varphi}:=(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{m-1}),\bm{\psi}:=(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{m-1})\in\mathscr{H}_{m-1}
, we define the
weighted inner product

\langle\bm{\varphi},\bm{\psi}\rangle_{\mathscr{H}_{m-1}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\omega_{i}\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla\varphi_{i}(x),\nabla\psi_{i}(x)\rangle\,dx,

and the associated norm
\|\bm{\varphi}\|_{\mathscr{H}_{m-1}}^{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\omega_{i}\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla\varphi_{i}(x)\|^{2}\,dx
. Since
\mathscr{H}_{m-1}
is a finite product of Hilbert spaces,
its dual space is identified as
\mathscr{H}_{m-1}^{\prime}=(\dot{\mathbb{H}}^{-1}(\Omega))^{m-1}
, where the duality pairing is taken componentwise. We shall consider the gradient of
\mathscr{D}_{[\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}]}(\mathbf{f})
as an element in the dual space
\mathscr{H}_{m-1}^{\prime}
.

Definition 2.1

(Gradient of the dual objective)
.

Define
\nabla\mathscr{D}:\mathscr{H}_{m-1}\to\mathscr{H}_{m-1}^{\prime}
as

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla\mathscr{D}_{[\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}]}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m-1}),(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{m-1})\right\rangle:=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\omega_{i}\int\varphi_{i}\,d\,\left(T_{f_{\text{mix}}^{c}}\sharp\mu_{m}-T_{f_{i}^{c}}\sharp\mu_{i}\right).

(7)

In particular, we readily get the following expression for the norm of the above gradient.

Proposition 2.2

(Norm of dual objective gradient)
.

\|\nabla\mathscr{D}_{[\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}]}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m-1})\|_{\mathscr{H}_{m-1}^{\prime}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\omega_{i}\|T_{f^{c}_{i}}\sharp\mu_{i}-T_{f^{c}_{\text{mix}}}\sharp\mu_{m}\|^{2}_{\dot{\mathbb{H}}^{-1}}\right)^{1/2}.

Under Sobolev geometry, we have the strong concavity of the dual objective functional
\mathscr{D}_{[\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}]}(\cdot)
.

Lemma 2.3

(Strong concavity of the dual objective)
.

Given
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}
absolutely continuous with
\mu_{i}\geq L,i\in[m]
and
\mathbf{g}:=(g_{1},\dots,g_{m-1})\in\mathbb{F}^{m-1}_{\alpha,\beta}
and
\mathbf{f}:=(f_{1},\dots,f_{m-1})\in\mathbb{F}^{m-1}_{\alpha,\beta}
with

\frac{\|\cdot\|^{2}}{2}-g_{\text{mix}}(\cdot)
convex, it holds that for
\lambda=\frac{L\alpha^{d+1}}{\beta^{2}}
,

\mathscr{D}_{[\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}]}(\mathbf{g})-\mathscr{D}_{[\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}]}(\mathbf{f})-\langle\nabla\mathscr{D}_{[\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}]}(\mathbf{f}),\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{f}\rangle\leq-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{f}\|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}_{m-1}}.

(8)

3
Sample complexity analysis

In this section, we study the convergence rate of the proposed estimator
\tilde{\mu}_{m}^{(n)}
to the population Wasserstein barycenter
\bar{\mu}_{m}
as a function of the per-distribution sample size
n
. Under standard assumptions in the literature, we establish nonparametric estimation rates for both the barycenter functional and the Wasserstein barycenter.

3.1
Assumptions

We begin by introducing a set of regularity assumptions on the input measures
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
. These conditions ensure well-posedness of the barycenter problem, stability of optimal transport maps, and control of the statistical error induced by finite sampling.

Assumption 1
.

For each
i\in[m]
,
\mu_{i}
is compactly supported on
\Omega
and absolutely continuous with its density
L\leq\mu_{i}\leq U,
, for some
L,U\in(0,\infty)
.

Assumption 2
.

\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{H}^{s}(\ell;L)
for some constants
s,\ell,L\in(0,+\infty)
, with

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{s}(\ell;L)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{H}^{s}(\ell)\cap\{f,f\geq L\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{s}(\ell)

\displaystyle:=\{f\in L^{2}(\Omega),\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}}\leq\ell,\int f=1,f\geq 0\}.

Assumptions
1
-
2
have been widely used in the nonparametric density estimation literature
(
28
,
39
)
, as well as in the optimal transport literature
(
20
,
17
,
16
,
14
,
9
)
.

Assumption 3
.

The Kantorovich potential
\mathbf{f}_{*}\in\mathbb{F}_{\alpha,\beta}^{m}
for some
0<\alpha\leq\beta<\infty
.

Assumption
3
is a regularity condition on the Brenier
potentials transporting the Wasserstein barycenter
\bar{\mu}_{m}
to each input measure
\mu_{i}
.
Similar regularity assumptions have been exploited in the study of optimal transport,
both for convergence analysis and for the design of computational algorithms. For example,
the same condition was adopted by
24
to
derive parametric rates of convergence for empirical barycenters as the number
of input measures
m
increases. Motivated by the regularity theory of Caffarelli

(
8
)
,
29
proposed an algorithm for optimal
transport between discrete measures by enforcing the Brenier potential
\varphi_{i}
to be
strongly convex and smooth. Under the same type of assumptions,
20

derived minimax estimation rates for the Brenier potential.
The goal of the
present work is to study the sample complexity of Wasserstein barycenters.

For instance, suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is convex and compact, and that
\mu_{i}
and
\bar{\mu}_{m}
satisfy

L\leq\bar{\mu}_{m}(x)\leq U\text{ and }\mu_{i},\bar{\mu}_{m}\in C^{1,\tau}(\Omega)

for some constant
\tau\in(0,1)
. Let
\varphi_{i}
be the Brenier potential such that

(\nabla\varphi_{i})\sharp\bar{\mu}_{m}=\mu_{i}.

By the boundary regularity theory for the Monge–Ampère equation
(
8
)
, the potential
\varphi_{i}
is strongly convex on
\Omega
and belongs to
C^{3,\tau}(\Omega)
. Consequently, one could conclude that there exist constants
\alpha,\beta>0

such that each
\varphi_{i}
is
\alpha
-strongly convex and
\beta
-smooth on
\Omega
.

Many commonly used input measures satisfy the above boundedness and smoothness conditions.
For example, truncated Gaussian densities or densities obtained from kernel density
estimators are naturally bounded above and below by positive constants on a compact domain

\Omega
, provided the kernel and bandwidth are chosen appropriately. If, in addition, the Wasserstein barycenter
\bar{\mu}_{m}
is also bounded below and above on

\Omega
and belongs to
C^{1,\tau}(\Omega)
, then the regularity assumptions are satisfied, and the associated Brenier potentials transporting
\bar{\mu}_{m}
to each

\mu_{i}
are strongly convex and smooth on
\Omega
.

For a concrete example satisfying Assumptions

1
-
3
, we
let
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{d\times d}
denote the set of
d\times d
positive definite matrices,
\xi_{0}
be a spherically symmetric
d
-dimensional random vector with law
P_{0}
satisfying Assumption
1
-
2
,
assumed to be absolutely continuous with finite second moment, and consider the family of probability distributions obtained from the reference law
P_{0}

via positive definite affine transformations,

\mathcal{F}(P_{0}):=\bigl\{\mathcal{L}(A\xi_{0}+m):\;A\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{d\times d},\;m\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\bigr\}.

By Theorem 3.10 in
2
, if
\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{P}_{0})
for all
i\in[m]
, then the Wasserstein barycenter
\bar{\mu}_{m}
also belongs to
\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{P}_{0})
. Moreover, since both the barycenter
\bar{\mu}_{m}
and the marginal distributions
\mu_{i}
,
i\in[m]
, are elliptically symmetric, the optimal transport map between them is affine, as shown in Section 3 of
27
.

3.2
Main results

This section presents our main results on the statistical sample complexity of estimating the Wasserstein barycenter
\bar{\mu}_{m}
and related quantities under the following setting. The measures
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}
are treated as fixed, and for each
j\in[m]
we observe an i.i.d. sample
X_{j1},\dots,X_{jn}
drawn from
\mu_{j}
. All observations are independent across both
j
and sample indices.

We begin by establishing two auxiliary results concerning the smoothed empirical distributions and the associated Kantorovich potentials
\mathbf{f}^{*}
. The first result concerns the existence of a density estimator with optimal convergence rates under both the Wasserstein distance and the homogeneous negative Sobolev norm. This lemma formalizes the fact that over smooth density classes with uniform upper and lower bounds, minimax-optimal estimation in
W_{2}
automatically yields optimal rates in the
\dot{\mathbb{H}}^{-1}
norm. The equivalence between these two metrics under boundedness assumptions allows us to treat them interchangeably up to universal constants, which will be convenient in later arguments.

Lemma 3.1

(Smoothed empirical marginal distribution)
.

For any
s\geq 0
, there exists an estimator

4
Application: sample complexity of two-layer sampling model

In many applications of distributional data analysis, the fundamental analytical unit is a probability distribution rather than an individual observation. Representative examples include regression analysis with distributional predictor or response
(
12
,
42
,
43
,
5
)
, and distributional principal component analysis

(
7
,
11
)
. In these settings, each data object is naturally modeled as a probability measure
\mu_{j}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and statistical analysis is performed directly at the level of distributions.

In practice, however, the distributions
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}
are not directly observed. Instead, for each
j\in[m]
, one only has access to an i.i.d. sample
X_{j1},\dots,X_{jn}\sim\mu_{j}
. This leads to a two-layer sampling scheme. At the first layer, the latent distributions
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m}
are independently drawn from a population distribution
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, but remain unobserved. At the second layer, conditional on each
\mu_{j}
, an i.i.d. sample
\{X_{ji}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is observed. The full dataset thus consists only of the second-layer samples
\{X_{ji}:j\in[m],\,i\in[n]\}
.

Our results enable theoretical guarantees for this widely used two-layer sampling framework, explicitly accounting for the randomness arising from both the first-layer sampling of distributions and the second-layer sampling of observations. In particular, under uniform barycentric weights
\omega_{j}=1/m
, we could establish the statistical convergence rate of the smoothed empirical barycenter
\tilde{\mu}_{m}^{(n)}
toward the population barycenter
\mu^{*}
associated with
\mathbb{P}
. This result follows by combining our second-layer sample complexity analysis with existing guarantees for the first-layer sampling of distributions
(
24
)
, under suitable regularity conditions. To this end, we adopt the following assumption, introduced in
24
, which ensures a parametric convergence rate for the first-layer sampling.

Assumption 4
.

Every
\mu\in\text{supp}(\mathbb{P})
is the pushforward of
\mu^{*}
by the gradient of an
\kappa
-strongly convex and
\lambda
-smooth function and
\lambda-\kappa<1
.

We note that the above Assumption
4
used in
24
is stronger than our Assumption
3
, which is imposed for the second-layer analysis. In particular, it enforces a specific relationship between the strong convexity and smoothness parameters, whereas in Assumption
3
the strong convexity parameter may be arbitrarily small and the smoothness parameter coule be arbitrarily large. Under Assumptions
1
–
4
, we can unify the results for both sampling layers in two-layer sampling model and obtain the following bound.

Theorem 4.1

(Two-layer sampling model sample complexity)
.

Under Assumptions
1
-
4
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}W_{1}(\tilde{\mu}_{m}^{(n)},\mu^{*})\lesssim\begin{cases}m^{-1/2}+n^{-\frac{1+s}{2d+4s}},&d\geq 3,\\[6.00006pt]
m^{-1/2}+n^{-1/4}(\log n)^{1/2},&d=2,\\[3.99994pt]
m^{-1/2}+n^{-1/4},&d=1.\end{cases}

Here,
\lesssim
hides constant depending on
\lambda,\kappa,\mathbb{P},\alpha,\beta,s,d,\Omega,L,U
.

Remark 4.2
.

Under milder conditions, the state-of-the-art
9
implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}W_{1}(\tilde{\mu}_{m}^{(n)},\mu^{*})\lesssim\begin{cases}m^{-1/2}+n^{-1/6d},&d\geq 5,\\[6.00006pt]
m^{-1/2}+n^{-1/24}(\log n)^{1/12},&d=4,\\[3.99994pt]
m^{-1/2}+n^{-1/24},&d\leq 3.\end{cases}

Decompose
W_{1}(\tilde{\mu}_{m}^{(n)},\mu^{*})\leq W_{1}(\tilde{\mu}_{m}^{(n)},\bar{\mu}_{m})+W_{1}(\bar{\mu}_{m},\mu^{*})
and the proof then follows directly from Corollary 4.4 in
24
and Theorem
3.4
.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
\eta
be a continuous function on
\Omega
. Then at points where the relevant gradients exist, it holds true that

\bullet

\eta^{c}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|T_{\eta^{c}}(x)-x\right\|^{2}-\eta\left(T_{\eta^{c}}(x)\right)
,

\bullet

\nabla\eta\left(T_{\eta^{c}}(x)\right)=T_{\eta^{c}}(x)-x
,

\bullet

\eta^{c}(y)=\frac{1}{2}\|y\|^{2}-\theta^{*}(y)
, with
\theta(\cdot)=\frac{\|\cdot\|^{2}}{2}-\eta(\cdot)
,

\bullet

\nabla\eta^{c}(y)=y-\nabla\theta^{*}(y)
with
\theta(\cdot)=\frac{\|\cdot\|^{2}}{2}-\eta(\cdot)
,

\bullet

\eta(\cdot)
is c-concave
\iff\theta(\cdot):=\frac{\|\cdot\|^{2}}{2}-\eta(\cdot)
is convex.

See Section 1.2 of
35
.
∎

Lemma B.2
.

Provided that

T=\nabla\eta
for some
\lambda
-strongly convex and
\Lambda
-smooth function
\eta
,

\|T\sharp\rho\|_{\dot{\mathbb{H}}^{-1}}\leq C_{T}\|\rho\|_{\dot{\mathbb{H}}^{-1}},

where
C_{T}=\Lambda/\lambda^{d/2}
.

Note that

\displaystyle\|T\sharp\rho\|_{\dot{\mathbb{H}}^{-1}}=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{\dot{\mathbb{H}}^{1}}\leq 1}\int\psi(y)d(T\sharp\rho)(y)=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{\dot{\mathbb{H}}^{1}}\leq 1}\int\psi(T(y))d\rho(y).

Write
\varphi(\cdot)=\psi(T(\cdot))
. Chain rule gives that
\nabla\varphi(x)=(\nabla T(x))^{T}\nabla\psi(T(x))
so that

|\nabla\varphi(x)|\leq\Lambda|\nabla\psi(T(x))|.

As a result,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\int|\nabla\varphi(x)|^{2}dx\leq\Lambda^{2}\int|\nabla\psi(T(x))|^{2}dx=\Lambda^{2}\int|\nabla\psi(y)|^{2}|\det DT^{-1}(y)|dy\leq\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{d}}\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

This means that for
C_{T}=\Lambda/\lambda^{d/2}
,

\displaystyle\int\psi(y)d(T\sharp\rho)(y)=\int\varphi(y)d\rho(y)=C_{T}\int\frac{\varphi(y)}{C_{T}}d\rho(y)\leq C_{T}\|\rho\|_{\dot{\mathbb{H}}^{-1}}.

∎

Lemma B.3
.

Suppose that
\phi
is
\alpha
-strongly convex and
\beta
-smooth, namely

\displaystyle\alpha I\preceq\nabla^{2}\phi(x)\preceq\beta I,

then
\phi^{*}
is
1/\alpha
-smooth and
1/\beta
-strongly convex, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}I\preceq\nabla^{2}\phi^{*}(x)\preceq\frac{1}{\alpha}I.

(i) From the strong convexity of
\phi
, we obtain that for all
x_{1},x_{2}
,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\phi(x_{1})-\nabla\phi(x_{2}),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq\alpha\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2}.

Write
y_{i}=\nabla\phi(x_{i})
for
i=1,2
and we have
Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields that

\displaystyle\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|\geq\alpha\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|.

Note that
\nabla\phi^{*}(\cdot)=(\nabla\phi)^{-1}(\cdot)
, we get
x_{i}=(\nabla\phi)^{-1}(y_{i})=\nabla\phi^{*}(y_{i})
. Thus,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi^{*}(y_{1})-\nabla\phi^{*}(y_{2})\|\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|,

and the
1/\alpha
smoothness of
\nabla\phi^{*}
is proved.

(ii) From the equivalent definition of smoothness of
\phi
, we obtain that for all
x_{1},x_{2}
,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\phi(x_{1})-\nabla\phi(x_{2}),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{\beta}\|\nabla\phi(x_{1})-\nabla\phi(x_{2})\|^{2}.

Write
y_{i}=\nabla\phi(x_{i})
for
i=1,2
and we have
Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields that

\displaystyle\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|\geq\frac{1}{\beta}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|.

Similarly note that
x_{i}=\nabla\phi^{*}(y_{i})
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi^{*}(y_{1})-\nabla\phi^{*}(y_{2})\|\geq\frac{1}{\beta}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|,

which indicates the
1/\beta
strong convexity of
\phi^{*}
.
∎

Lemma B.4
.

Suppose that
\psi
is
\alpha
-strongly convex and
\beta
-smooth. Then we have that

\displaystyle\alpha\|\nabla\phi^{*}(y)-\nabla\psi^{*}(y)\|\leq\|\nabla\psi(\nabla\phi^{*}(y))-\nabla\phi(\nabla\phi^{*}(y))\|\leq\beta\|\nabla\phi^{*}(y)-\nabla\psi^{*}(y)\|.

(i) The
\beta
-smoothness of
\psi
gives that for any

,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi(x)-\nabla\psi(z)\|\leq\beta\|x-z\|.

Take
\nabla\psi(z)=y=\nabla\phi(x)
and we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi(x)-\nabla\phi(x)\|\leq\beta\|\nabla\phi^{*}(y)-\nabla\psi^{*}(y)\|.

Namely,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi(\nabla\phi^{*}(y))-\nabla\phi(\nabla\phi^{*}(y))\|\leq\beta\|\nabla\phi^{*}(y)-\nabla\psi^{*}(y)\|.

(ii) The
\alpha
-strong convexity of
\psi
gives that for any
x,z
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi(x)-\nabla\psi(z)\|\geq\alpha\|x-z\|.

Take
\nabla\psi(z)=y=\nabla\phi(x)
and we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi(x)-\nabla\phi(x)\|\geq\alpha\|\nabla\phi^{*}(y)-\nabla\psi^{*}(y)\|.

Namely,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi(\nabla\phi^{*}(y))-\nabla\phi(\nabla\phi^{*}(y))\|\geq\alpha\|\nabla\phi^{*}(y)-\nabla\psi^{*}(y)\|.

∎
