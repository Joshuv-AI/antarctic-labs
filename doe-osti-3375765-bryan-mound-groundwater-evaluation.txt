1. INTRODUCTION
The Bryan Mound site, located within the Gulf Coast region of Texas, overlies the Chicot aquifer, a
significant groundwater resource composed of poorly consolidated sand, silt, mud, and clay deposits.
This aquifer serves as an important source of water for municipal, industrial, and agricultural uses in
Brazoria County. Understanding the hydrogeologic characteristics and groundwater quality at Bryan
Mound is essential for effective environmental management and resource protection.

This report aims to support groundwater characterization efforts by assessing groundwater quality
and mapping regional groundwater flow directions within the aquifer system. The study integrates
geophysical log analyses, water quality estimations, and groundwater elevation mapping using well
data and digital elevation models. The resulting insights provide a foundation to informed potential
groundwater monitoring and management strategies tailored to the site’s specific hydrogeologic
conditions.

2. AQUIFERS AT BRYAN MOUND
The Gulf Coast region of Texas is characterized by a system of aquifers that plays a crucial role in
supplying groundwater for various uses including agriculture, industry, and municipal needs. Among
these aquifers, the Chicot and Evangeline aquifers are particularly significant (Figure 2-1). The
Chicot aquifer, being the shallowest of the two, serves as a source of groundwater for Brazoria
County. This aquifer is composed of quaternary to late tertiary deposits containing sand, silt, mud,
and clay that are poorly consolidated. Its accessibility has made it a vital resource for local
communities and industrial applications.

Figure 2-1. Cross section of Gulf Coast aquifers present near Bryan Mound. Only the Chicot Aquifer is present
 at the site. Modified from Baker 1977, 1983, Chowdhury and Mace, 2003, and Kasmarek and Robinson, 2004.

The Evangeline aquifer typically lies beneath the Chicot aquifer. However, its presence at the Bryan
Mound SPR site is notably absent. The Evangeline aquifer may not have been deposited at the
Bryan Mound site due to a lack of accommodation during deposition. Alternatively, the uplift of the
Bryan Mound Salt Dome may have led to the intrusion or erosion of the Evangeline aquifer,
preventing its preservation at the site. Because the Evangeline sediments are absent, the Chicot
sediments are directly above, and in contact with, the Bryan Mound salt dome caprock.

3. GW QUALITY FROM BM-MOW-1
The groundwater quality within the Chicot aquifer above Bryan Mound is not fully characterized by
sampling and testing. The Texas Water Development Board (TWDB) provided a base of USDW
determination using a broad correlation with offsite wells. Their determination was that the base of
USDW occurs at a depth of 750 feet; however, the water quality above salt domes can be variable
and is often of poorer quality.

4. REGIONAL GW FLOW
Regional GW flow modeling by the TWDB has shown that the Chicot aquifer has been heavily
influenced by municipal use (Kasmarek and Robinson, 2004). They have shown through measured
and simulated potentiometric surfaces that there is a significant cone of depression within the Chicot
aquifer related to use in Harris County (Figure 4-1). As a result, the regional flow direction of the
groundwater in areas proximal to Harris County is toward Harris County. Within Brazoria County,
that direction is roughly to the north and away from the gulf coast. This is in the opposite direction
of the original, unaltered flow direction of the groundwater, which would have been towards the
gulf.

While the cone of depression from Harris County is likely influencing the groundwater flow
direction at Bryan Mound, the municipal and historical industrial use in Freeport, Texas is likely
having a larger influence on the flow direction as it is approximately two miles from Bryan Mound.
Industrial uses have shifted towards using water from the Brazos River as subsidence from water
well use began causing concerns in Freeport (Ratzlaf, 1980, Reddy et al., 2015).

 within Brazoria County may be directed toward a large cone of depression in Harris County. Modified from

To map the groundwater elevation and get a sense of flow direction within the Chicot aquifer at
Bryan Mound, water well information was gathered from the TWDB Groundwater database,
including the State of Texas Well Reports. The well information was used to map the groundwater
elevation for the most recent measurements (Figure 4-2) around Bryan Mound. The depth to the top
of groundwater is reported relative to ground level. To convert to a subsea (SS) elevation, the depth
of the groundwater was subtracted from the ground elevation. If no ground elevation information
was reported in the well records, the ground elevation from a USGS DEM at the well location was
used.

No groundwater wells are present at Bryan Mound; therefore, the groundwater elevation from
around the site was used to infer the elevations at the site. This is believed to be a reasonable
approach as the Chicot Aquifer does not appear to have a significant structural influence on the salt
dome geometry. Therefore, the regional groundwater flow direction is not expected to be largely
affected by the dome

The groundwater elevation map (Figure 4-3) shows that the groundwater elevation within the Chicot
is decreasing (down-gradient) to the north-northeast, suggesting flow paths in that direction. This is
consistent with reports that municipal and historical industrial use had a large impact on the
groundwater flow direction (Kasmarek and Robinson, 2004). Because there is no data on Bryan
Mound, the magnitude of the elevation change is inferred, and groundwater flow velocity was not
mapped.

5. SUMMARY
This study presents a comprehensive evaluation of the groundwater conditions within the Chicot
aquifer at and around the Bryan Mound site. The Chicot aquifer, composed of quaternary to late
tertiary poorly consolidated sediments, serves as an important groundwater source in Brazoria
County. Water quality estimates derived from induction log resistivity data indicate that groundwater
within the aquifer is generally brackish, with total dissolved solids (TDS) values ranging between
1,000 and 10,000 mg/L. Two distinct trends in TDS with depth suggest possible density
stratification within the aquifer.

Regional groundwater flow analysis, supported by data from the Texas Water Development Board
and groundwater well records, reveals that groundwater flow near Bryan Mound is influenced by
municipal and industrial water use, particularly the large cone of depression in Harris County. Flow
directions in Brazoria County, including near Bryan Mound, are generally oriented north-northeast,
away from the Gulf Coast, contrasting with the natural pre-development flow toward the Gulf.
Although no groundwater wells are present directly at Bryan Mound, inferred groundwater
elevations and flow directions based on surrounding well data provide a reasonable understanding of
local hydrogeologic conditions. The findings underscore the importance of continued groundwater
monitoring and characterization to support sustainable water resource management and
environmental protection in the region.
