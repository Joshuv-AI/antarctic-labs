How Can Size and Ceiling Bounds Affect the Complexity of Nonuniform Automata Families?
Tomoyuki Yamakami

Abstract
In the past literature, families of two-way finite automata and pushdown automata having limited state complexity (i.e., the total number of inner states) and stack-state complexity (i.e., the total number of inner states multiplied by the total number of strings “pushable” to a stack), have been studied in direct connection to (mainstream) space-bounded complexity classes equipped with Karp-Lipton style advice of limited size when all inputs given to the automata have bounded length. Here, we acknowledge two major factors—size and ceiling—of such families, which have a significant impact on the complexity of finite and pushdown automata families, where the “size” refers to (stack-)state complexity and the “ceiling” refers to an input’s length bound. In this line of study, we further explore those effects caused by different sizes and ceilings. Keywords. nonuniform state complexity, promise problem, stack-state complexity, size, ceiling, advice

k
-m-reducible
, for short) to
{\cal K}
(denoted
{\cal L}\leq^{\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2\mathrm{FCT}_{k}}_{m}{\cal K}
) if there exist a family
\{(f_{n},D_{n})\}_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
of partial functions in
\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2\mathrm{FCT}_{k}
and another p-bounded, p-honest function
d:{\rm Nature}\to{\rm Nature}
that satisfy the following three conditions for any
n\in{\rm Nature}
and for any
x\in\Sigma^{*}
: (i)
L_{n}^{(+)}\cup L_{n}^{(-)}\subseteq D_{n}
, (ii)
x\in L_{n}^{(+)}
implies
f_{n}(x)\in K_{d(n)}^{(+)}
, and (iii)
x\in L_{n}^{(-)}
implies
f_{n}(x)\in K_{d(n)}^{(-)}
.
When neither counters nor time bounds are required for reduction machines, we instead write
{\cal L}\leq^{2\mathrm{F}}_{m}{\cal K}
.

Given a family
{\cal L}
of promise problems, the special notation
\leq^{\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2\mathrm{FCT}_{k}}_{m}\!({\cal L})
denotes the collection of all families of promise problems that are 2FCT
k
-m-reducible to
{\cal L}
.
Moreover, for a class
{\cal C}
of families of promise problems,
\leq^{\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2\mathrm{FCT}_{k}}_{m}\!({\cal C})
expresses the union
\bigcup_{{\cal L}\in{\cal C}}\leq^{\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2\mathrm{FCT}_{k}}_{m}\!({\cal L})
. We also define
\leq^{2\mathrm{F}}_{m}\!({\cal L})
if neither counters nor time bounds are needed.

It is known in

[
28
,
29
]
that
(1) for any
k\geq 4
,
\mathrm{2DCT}_{k}=\mathrm{2DCT}_{4}
,
(2) for any
k\geq 3
,
\mathrm{2DPDCT}_{k}=\mathrm{2DPDCT}_{3}
, and
(3)
\mathrm{2DCT}_{4}/\mathrm{poly}=2\mathrm{D}/\mathrm{poly}
and
\mathrm{2DPDCT}_{3}/\mathrm{poly}=\mathrm{2DPD}/\mathrm{poly}
. The nondeterministic cases also hold for (1)–(3).
Moreover, it is shown in
[
28
,
29
]
that

2\mathrm{D}\neq\mathrm{2DCT}
,
2\mathrm{U}\neq\mathrm{2UCT}
, and
2\mathrm{N}\neq\mathrm{2NCT}
, where “U” indicates “unambiguous” computation.

Proposition 3.1

(1)
\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2DPDCT}_{3}=\>\leq^{\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2\mathrm{FCT}_{2}}_{m}\!(\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2DPDCT}_{3})=\>\leq^{\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2\mathrm{FCT}_{2}}_{m}\!(\mathrm{1DPDCT}_{3})
.
(2)
\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2DCT}_{4}=\>\leq^{\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2\mathrm{FCT}_{2}}_{m}\!(\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2DCT}_{4})=\>\leq^{\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2\mathrm{FCT}_{2}}_{m}\!(\mathrm{1DCT}_{4})
.
The same equalities hold for nondeterministic computations.

We remark that the inclusions
\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2DPDCT}_{3}\subseteq\>\leq^{\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2\mathrm{FCT}_{2}}_{m}\!(\mathrm{1DPDCT}_{3})
and
\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2NPDCT}_{3}\subseteq\>\leq^{\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2\mathrm{FCT}_{2}}_{m}\!(\mathrm{1NPDCT}_{3})
are reminiscent of
[
19
, Lemma 3]
, in which the language recognized by a polynomial-time two-way pushdown automaton can be
\mathrm{L}
-m-reducible to an appropriate context-free language.

In Proposition
3.1
(2), when inputs provided to underlying machines are polynomially bounded, we can eliminate the full use of “counters”, even from reduction functions.

Corollary 3.2

(1)
2\mathrm{D}/\mathrm{poly}=\>\leq^{2\mathrm{F}}_{m}\!(1\mathrm{D}/\mathrm{poly})=\>\leq^{2\mathrm{F}}_{m}\!(2\mathrm{D}/\mathrm{poly})
.
(2)
\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2DPD}/\mathrm{poly}=\>\leq^{2\mathrm{F}}_{m}\!(\mathrm{1DPD}/\mathrm{poly})=\>\leq^{2\mathrm{F}}_{m}\!(\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2DPD}/\mathrm{poly})
.

By an instant application of Corollary
3.2
, we conclude that, if
1\mathrm{D}/\mathrm{poly}=1\mathrm{N}/\mathrm{poly}
, then
2\mathrm{D}/\mathrm{poly}=2\mathrm{N}/\mathrm{poly}
, which is unknown to hold without any assumptions.
See also Proposition
6.3
(1).

Beyond the above corollary, it is not clear that

\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2DPD}=\>\leq^{2\mathrm{F}}_{m}\!(\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2DPD})

and
2\mathrm{D}=\;\leq^{2\mathrm{F}}_{m}\!(2\mathrm{D})
.

In a similar vein to Proposition
3.1
, we further assert the following closure property.

Proposition 3.3

\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2^{\log^{k}\mathrm{2DCT}_{4}}=\>\leq^{\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2\mathrm{FCT}_{2}}_{m}\!(\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2^{\log^{k}\mathrm{2DCT}_{4}})=\>\leq^{\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2\mathrm{FCT}_{2}}_{m}\!(2^{\log^{k}\mathrm{1DCT}_{4}})
.

Similarly to Corollary
3.2
, we obtain from Proposition
3.3
the following result, in which the use of counters and the runtime bounds in the proposition are eliminated.

Corollary 3.4

2^{\log^{k}2\mathrm{D}}/\mathrm{poly}=\>\leq^{2\mathrm{F}}_{m}\!(2^{\log^{k}2\mathrm{D}}/\mathrm{poly})=\>\leq^{2\mathrm{F}}_{m}\!(2^{\log^{k}1\mathrm{D}}/\mathrm{poly})
.

3.2
Translation between Automata Families and Advised Turing Machines

The second technical tool is to utilize underlying connections between families of finite automata and advised Turing machines and also between pushdown automata families and advised auxiliary pushdown automata.
Such connections were first discussed by Berman and Lingas
[
3
]
and by Sakoda and Sipser
[
18
]
and have been in a central subject of recent research.

These connections help us exploit collapse/separations of (mainstream) complexity classes.
Here, we present two technical propositions, which will be frequently used in later sections. We say that a function on
{\rm Nature}
is
2dft-constructible
if there is a 2dft
M
such that
M
on each input of the form
1^{n}
produces the string
1^{f(n)}
on its write-once output tape.
The first key proposition is given as follows.

Proposition 3.5

Let
f,g,s,d
be any functions on
{\rm Nature}
with
f(n)\geq 1
for all
n\in{\rm Nature}
. Assume that
d
is nondecreasing and that
f
is 2dft-constructible. Assume also that the inverse
f^{-1}
of
f
exists on the image of
f
. Let
{\cal L}
denote a family
\{(L_{n}^{(+)},L_{n}^{(-)})\}_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
of promise problems over an alphabet
\Sigma
. Assume that
{\cal L}
has
f(n)
-ceiling.

(1) If
{\cal L}
is solved by a family
\{N_{n}\}_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
of
g(n)
-size 2-choice 2nfa’s (resp., 2npda’s), then there exists an NTM (resp., an naux-pda)
N
such that
N
recognizes the language
K
using
O(\log{e(|w|)})
space with the help of
O(e(|w|))
-size advice, where
K=\{1^{f(n)-|x|-1}\#x\mid n\in{\rm Nature},|x|\leq f(n),x\in L(N_{n})\}
and
e(|w|)=g\circ f^{-1}(|w|)
.

(2) If the above-defined language
K
is recognized by an NTM (resp., an naux-pda)
M
using
s(|w|)
space with
d(|w|)
-size advice for inputs
w
, then
(i) for each
n
,
L_{n}^{(+)}\subseteq\{x\mid|x|\leq f(n),1^{f(n)-|x|-1}\#x\in K\}
and
L_{n}^{(-)}\subseteq\{x\mid|x|\leq f(n),1^{f(n)-|x|-1}\#x\in\overline{K}\}
and (ii) there exists a family
\{M_{n}\}_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
of
e^{\prime}(n)
-size 2nfa’s (resp., 2npda’s) solving
{\cal L}
, where
e^{\prime}(n)=2^{O(s\circ f(n))}+O(d\circ f(n))
.

(3) The above statements (1)–(2) also hold in the deterministic case.

The following proof argument of Proposition
3.5
looks quite different from the arguments given in
[
25
,
24
,
28
,
29
]
, where
[
29
]
particularly provides a standard, general framework to those arguments.

The first key proposition (Proposition
3.5
) intends to first translate finite automata (as well as pushdown automata) into NTMs (as well as naux-pda’s). Conversely, the following second key proposition first translates NTMs (and naux-pda’s) to finite automata (and pushdown automata), compensating the first key proposition.

Proposition 3.6

Let
f,g,s,d
be any functions on
{\rm Nature}
with
f(n)\geq 1
for all
n\in{\rm Nature}
. Assume that
d
is nondecreasing and that
f
is 2dft-constructible. Assume also that the inverse
f^{-1}
of
f
exists on the image of
f
. Let
K
denote any language over an alphabet
\Sigma
.

(1) If
K
is recognized by an NTM (resp., an naux-pda)
N
using
s(|x|)
space with
d(|x|)
-size advice for inputs
x
, then there exists a family
\{N_{n}\}_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
of
e(n)
-size
O(1)
-choice 2nfa’s (resp., 2npda’s) solving
{\cal L}=\{(L_{n}^{(+)},L_{n}^{(-)})\}_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
, where
L_{n}^{(+)}=\{x\mid|x|=f(n),x\in K\}
,
L_{n}^{(-)}=\{x\mid|x|=f(n),x\in\overline{K}\}
, and
e(n)=2^{O(s\circ f(n))}+O(d\circ f(n))
. This family
{\cal L}
has
f(n)
-ceiling.

(2) If the above-defined family
{\cal L}
is solved by a family
\{M_{n}\}_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
of
g(n)
-size 2nfa’s (resp., 2npda’s), then (i)
K
is identified with
\{x\mid n\in{\rm Nature},|x|=f(n),x\in L(M_{n})\}
and (ii) there exists an NTM (resp., an naux-pda)
M
that recognizes the language
K
using
O(\log{e^{\prime}(|x|)})
space with the help of
O(e^{\prime}(|x|)^{2})
-size advice for inputs
x
, where
e^{\prime}(|x|)=g\circ f^{-1}(|x|)
.

(3) The statements (1)–(2) also hold in the deterministic case.

Promise Problem Families of Polynomial Ceilings

Let us first recall the purpose of this work, which is to study the effect of various ceilings (as well as various sizes) on the overall complexity of families of promise problems.
Throughout this section, we wish to study the computational complexity of nonuniform automata families particularly having polynomial ceilings. Most of our results are illustrated in Fig.

.

4.1
Complexity of Polynomial-Size Automata Families

.

\Box

\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}=\mathrm{2DPD}/\mathrm{poly}=\mathrm{etime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2DPD}/\mathrm{poly}=\mathrm{etime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}

Proof Sketch.

It is obvious that
\mathrm{etime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2DPD}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq\mathrm{etime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}
, and
\mathrm{etime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2DPD}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq\mathrm{2DPD}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}
.
It thus suffices to verify that
\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq\mathrm{etime}\mbox{-}\mathrm{2DPD}/\mathrm{poly}
.
For any family
{\cal L}=\{(L_{n}^{(+)},L_{n}^{(-)})\}_{n\in{\rm Nature}}

\Sigma

\{N_{n}\}_{n\in{\rm Nature}}

{\cal L}
.
By Proposition
3.5
(1) with a suitable polynomial

. By an advised version of Cook’s result
[

]
,

also belongs to
\mathrm{DAuxPDA},\!\mathrm{SPTI}(O(\log{n}),\infty)/\mathrm{poly}

4.2
Complexity of Superpolynomial-Size Automata Families

In contrast to Section
4.1
, we wish to explore the roles of various nonuniform automata families of superpolynomial size.

We start with the following easy claim.

Proposition 4.4

\mathrm{ALL}/\mathrm{poly}=2^{2\mathrm{D}}/\mathrm{poly}=2^{2\mathrm{N}}/\mathrm{poly}=2^{\mathrm{2DPD}}/\mathrm{poly}=2^{\mathrm{2NPD}}/\mathrm{poly}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that
\mathrm{ALL}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq 2^{2\mathrm{D}}/\mathrm{poly}
. We take any family
{\cal L}=\{(L_{n}^{(+)},L_{n}^{(-)})\}_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
in
\mathrm{ALL}/\mathrm{poly}
over an alphabet
\Sigma
. There is a polynomial
p
satisfying
L_{n}^{(+)}\cup L_{n}^{(-)}\subseteq\Sigma^{\leq p(n)}
for all
n\in{\rm Nature}
.
For each index
n\in{\rm Nature}
, we define a 2dfa
M_{n}
as follows. The set
Q_{n}
consists of all tuples of the from
(j,s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{j},b)
with
j\in[p(n)]
,
s_{1},\ldots,s_{j}\in\Sigma
, and
b=L_{n}^{(+)}(s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{j})
, where
L_{n}^{(+)}(x)
denotes the acceptance (1) or the rejection (0) of
x
to
L_{n}^{(+)}
.
Consider the following algorithm.
On input
x
of the form
s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{m}
with
s_{i}\in\Sigma
, we process input symbols
s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{m}
one by one together with changing inner states
(1,s_{1},b_{1}),(2,s_{1}s_{2},b_{2}),\ldots,(m,s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{m},b_{m})
in this order, and finally output
b_{m}
. This algorithm can be implemented on a suitable 2dfa with
Q_{n}
as a set of inner states.
Since
|Q_{n}|\leq 2^{O(p(n))}
, we conclude that
{\cal L}
belongs to
2^{2\mathrm{D}}/\mathrm{poly}
.

\Box

Theorem
4.1
is extendable to
2^{O(\log^{k}{n})}
-size automata families for various values of
k\geq 2
.

Theorem 4.5

For any integer
k\geq 2
,
\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2^{\log^{k}2\mathrm{N}}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2^{\log^{k}\mathrm{2NPD}}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq 2^{\log^{k+1}2\mathrm{D}}/\mathrm{poly}
.

Proof.

The first inclusion is obvious. Thus, we focus on the second inclusion between
\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2^{\log^{k}\mathrm{2NPD}}/\mathrm{poly}
and
2^{\log^{k+1}2\mathrm{D}}/\mathrm{poly}
.

Take an arbitrary family
{\cal L}=\{(L_{n}^{(+)},L_{n}^{(-)})\}_{n\in{\rm Nature}}
in
\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2^{\log^{k}\mathrm{2NPD}}/\mathrm{poly}
over alphabet
\Sigma
. There are two constants
a,c>0
and a family
\{M_{n}\}_{nb\in{\rm Nature}}
of
2^{c\log^{k}n+c}
-size 2npda’s solving
{\cal L}
in time
(n|x|)^{c}+c
. We also assume that
{\cal L}
is of
(n^{a}+a)
-ceiling. We set
f(n)=n^{a}+a
and
g(n)=2^{c\log^{k}n+c}
. We then define the language
K
as
\{1^{f(n)-|x|-1}\#x\mid n\in{\rm Nature},|x|\leq f(n),x\in L(M_{n})\}
. Without loss of generality, we assume by Lemma
2.1
that
M_{n}
makes at most
2
-choices. Note that
e(n)=g\circ f^{-1}(n)=2^{c\log^{k}(n-a)^{1/a}+c}\leq 2^{c^{\prime}\log^{k}n+c^{\prime}}
for a suitable constant
c^{\prime}>0
.
Proposition
3.5
(1) provides an naux-pda
N
for
K
. ,
Since the runtime of
M_{n}
on input
x
is
(n|x|)^{c}+c
for a constant
c>0
,
N
runs in time
n^{O(1)}
. Since
\log{e(|w|)}=O(\log^{k}|w|)
,
N
uses space
O(\log^{k}|w|)
.
Therefore,
K
falls in
\mathrm{NAuxPDA},\!\mathrm{SPTI}(O(\log^{k}n),n^{O(1)})/2^{O(\log^{k}n)}
.

As Ruzzo
[
17
]
demonstrated,
\mathrm{NAuxPDA},\!\mathrm{SPTI}(O(\log^{k}n),n^{O(1)})
is included in
\mathrm{DSPACE}(O(\log^{k+1}n))
. We remark that his proof can be carried out even in the presence of advice. We thus immediately obtain
K\in\mathrm{DSPACE}(O(\log^{k+1}n))/2^{O(\log^{k}n)}
. There exists a DTM
M
that recognizes
K
using space at most
c\log^{k+1}n+c
with
2^{a\log^{k}n+a}
-size advice for suitable constants
a,c>0
.
We set
s(n)=c\log^{k+1}n+c
and
d(n)=2^{a\log^{k}n+a}
. Note that
e^{\prime}(n)=2^{O(\log^{k+1}n)}+2^{O(\log^{k}n)}=2^{O(\log^{k+1}n)}
because
s\circ f(n)=O(\log^{k+1}n)
, and
d\circ f(n)=2^{\log^{k}n)}
. By Proposition
3.5
(2),
{\cal L}
belongs to
2^{\log^{k+1}2\mathrm{D}}/\mathrm{poly}
.
\Box

Theorem
4.5
indicates that the complexity of
\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2^{\log^{k}2\mathrm{N}}/\mathrm{poly}
is relatively high. How high is it? Is it higher than, say,
\mathrm{2DPD}/\mathrm{poly}
? As a partial answer to this question, we demonstrate that at least
\mathrm{ptime}\mbox{-}2^{\log^{k}2\mathrm{D}}/\mathrm{poly}
is not “small” enough to be included in
\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}
