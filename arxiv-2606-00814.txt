Theory for the Rydberg states of helium: quantum defect extensions and comparison with experiment up to n = 102 n=102 for the singlet and triplet P P -states
G. W. F. Drake 1,∗, Aaron T. Bondy 2

Abstract
High precision variational calculations for helium in Hylleraas coordinates are used to obtain a combination of quantum defect expansions for the nonrelativistic energy and 1 / n 1/n expansions for the relativistic and quantum electrodynamic (QED) corrections. The extrapolations based on direct calculations for the singlet and triplet P P -states up to principal quantum number n = 35 n=35 provide ionization energies of the 1 ​ s ​ n ​ p 1 ​ P 1 1snp\;^{1}P_{1} and P c 3 {}^{3}P_{c} (centroid) states up to n = 102 n=102 with accuracies better than ± \pm 1 kHz. The calculated ionization energies are combined with 28 measured transition frequencies to obtain values for the ionization energy of the 1 ​ s ​ 2 ​ s 3 ​ S 1 1s2s\;^{3}S_{1} state. The final result of 1152 842 742.705(16) MHz differs from theory by 0.474 ± 0.052 0.474\pm 0.052 MHz, and provides a strong confirmation of the 9 σ \sigma disagreement between theory and experiment obtained previously by quantum defect extrapolation of experimental data to the series limit. An analysis of the quantum defect method is presented, and second-order mass polarization (recoil) terms are identified that vary as 1 / n 2 1/n^{2} in lowest order. The nonrelativistic part provides a theoretical justification for the effective reduced-mass Rydberg R M ( + ) R_{M}^{(+)} based on the phenomenological model of a Rydberg electron scattering from a He + core. The Ritz expansion for the nonrelativistic energy is verified to an unprecedented 20-figure accuracy.

\mu/M

the value for any other
X(\mu/M^{\prime})
close to
X(\mu/M)
is then very well approximated by the linearized expression

X(\mu/M^{\prime})=X(0)+\frac{\mu}{M^{\prime}}X^{(1)}

(19)

In Tables

According to the usual formulation of QDT, the term energies for a

(20)

\delta(n*)=\delta_{0}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{(n-\delta)^{2}}+\frac{\delta_{4}}{(n-\delta)^{4}}+\cdots

(21)

]

The finite nuclear mass case requires further discussion due to the mass polarization term in the Hamiltonian, which arises physically from the motion of the nucleus in the center-of-mass (CM) frame. Table
7
lists the first- and second-order matrix elements of the mass polarization operator
-\nabla_{1}\mbox{\boldmath{$\cdot$}}\nabla_{2}
. The first-order term is calculated directly as a matrix element of the infinite mass wave function, and the second-order term is estimated according to the finite-difference formula

\langle\nabla_{1}\mbox{\boldmath{$\cdot$}}\nabla_{2}\rangle^{(2)}\simeq\frac{E(\mu/M)-E(0)+(\mu/M)\langle\nabla_{1}\mbox{\boldmath{$\cdot$}}\nabla_{2}\rangle}{(\mu/M)^{2}}

(22)

It therefore approximates the second-order term and sums to infinity the perturbation series in
\mu/M
at the assumed CODATA value for
\mu/M
.

To further reveal the significance of these matrix elements in terms of QDT, Table
8
lists the coefficients in a
1/n
expansion
fit to the data in Table
7
. The first-order terms decrease in proportion to
1/n^{3}
as expected, but the second-order terms have a
leading
-2/n^{2}
dependence that is state-independent. It is therefore essential to subtract it from the input energies for the finite mass case
before performing a QDT fit. The corrected fitting formula is thus

E_{n}=-\frac{R_{M}}{n*^{2}}-\frac{R_{M}}{n^{2}}\left(\frac{\mu}{M}\right)^{2}

(23)

The same
-(R_{M}/n^{2})(\mu/M)^{2}
was derived previously in the asymptotic limit of high-
L
Rydberg states by use of Jacobi coordinates
Drachman86
;
DrakeYan92
, and included in previous discussions of QDT
Advances
. The results in Table
7
confirm the asymptotic result for low-
L

P
-states with the same asymptotic coefficient. This is the form of the quantum defect expansion used to obtain the
\delta_{i}
parameters as listed in the bottom half of Table
6
. A further relativistic correction is discussed in the following section.

Alternatively, if the quantum defect is small, the value of the reduced mass Rydberg can be adjusted to take the
-(R_{M}/n^{2})(\mu/M)^{2}
term into account with an expression of the form

E_{n}=-R_{M}^{(+)}\frac{1}{n*^{2}}

(24)

with
R_{M}^{(+)}
defined to be

R_{M}^{(+)}=R_{\infty}\frac{M+m_{e}}{M+2m_{e}}

(25)

in place of
R_{M}=R_{\infty}\frac{M}{M+m_{e}}
. Physically, this is the Rydberg for an electron outside a He
+
core instead of a bare He
++
nucleus. This provides a theoretical justification for what is commonly done on phenomenological grounds in quantum defect fits to data.
Note that

\frac{R_{M}^{(+)}}{R_{M}}=\frac{1}{1-(\mu/M)^{2}}=1+\left(\frac{\mu}{M}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\mu}{M}\right)^{4}+\cdots

(26)

and so Eqs. (
23
) and (
24
) are equivalent to order
(\mu/M)^{2}
with an error term of order
(\mu/M)^{4}
.

IV.2
Relativistic and QED Corrections

Similarly,
1/n
expansions can be obtained for all the other matrix elements required to calculate the relativistic and QED corrections. The results are listed in Table
9
for the spin-independent terms and Table
10
for the spin-dependent terms. Each matrix element is expressed in the form

\langle X\rangle=c(X)_{0}+\sum_{t=3}^{8}\left[c(X)_{t}^{(0)}+\frac{\mu}{M}c(X)_{t}^{(1)}\right]/n^{t}

(27)

for each operator
X=p_{1}^{4},H_{2},
etc., where
c(X)_{t}^{(0)}
is the coefficient of
1/n^{t}
for infinite nuclear mass, and

c(X)_{t}^{(1)}
is the finite mass correction coefficient obtained by finite differencing for the case of
4
He. They therefore sum to infinity the perturbation series in
\mu/M
, at the assumed value for
4
He, and provide an excellent approximation for slightly adjusted values. The leading

c(X)_{0}
term is zero except for the one-electron operators
p_{1}^{4}
,
\delta({\bf r}_{1})
, and
Q_{1}
. The leading terms of course cancel from the ionization energy with the corresponding hydrogenic He
+
terms at the series limit, and so can be dropped.

One exception to the above
\mu/M
dependence is the relativistic recoil term
\tilde{\Delta}_{2}
. It is of order
m_{e}/M
in lowest order, but the
\mu/M
correction has a
1/n^{2}
dependence that is state-independent with the value
8/3
. The complete matrix element can therefore be written in the form

(m_{e}/M)\tilde{\Delta}_{2}=(m_{e}/M)[\tilde{\Delta}_{2}^{(0)}+(\mu/M)\tilde{\Delta}_{2}^{(1)}]

(28)

with

\tilde{\Delta}_{2}^{(1)}=\alpha^{2}\left(\frac{8}{3n^{2}}\right)+\tilde{\Delta}_{\rm 2,res}^{(1)}

(29)

where
\tilde{\Delta}_{\rm 2,res}^{(1)}
is the residual part multiplied by
(\mu m_{e}/M^{2})
that decreases as
1/n^{3}
or faster. It is the
1/n
coefficients of residual part
\tilde{\Delta}_{\rm 2,res}^{(1)}
that are tabulated in Table
9
.

In addition to the above, it is known from previous work on asymptotic expansions
DrakeYan92
;
Advances
that the mass polarization correction to the
B_{1}+B_{4}
terms in the Breit interaction have a leading
1/n^{2}
dependence. With the definition

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{M}\langle B_{1}^{X}+B_{4}^{X}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{M}\left[\langle B_{1}^{X}+B_{4}^{X}\rangle^{(0)}\right.

(30)

\displaystyle\left.\mbox{}+\frac{\mu}{M}\langle B_{1}^{X}+B_{4}^{X}\rangle^{(1)}\right]

then, according to Eq. (86) of Ref.
DrakeYan92
,

\langle B_{1}^{X}+B_{4}^{X}\rangle^{(1)}=\frac{5}{12}\alpha^{2}\left(\frac{\mu}{M}\right)^{2}+O(\alpha^{2}/n^{3})

(31)

For
Z=2
, and with the approximation
\mu\simeq m_{e}
, the two contributions combine to give a total of
(\frac{8}{3}-\frac{5}{3})\alpha^{2}(\mu/M)^{2}=\alpha^{2}(\mu/M)^{2}
, and so
the total recoil term is thus (in Rydbergs)

E_{n}^{(2)}=-\frac{R_{M}}{n^{2}}\left(\frac{\mu}{M}\right)^{2}(1-2\alpha^{2})

(32)

The quantum defect formula (
23
) should correspondingly be modified to read

E_{n}=-\frac{R_{M}}{n*^{2}}-\frac{R_{M}}{n^{2}}\left(\frac{\mu}{M}\right)^{2}(1-2\alpha^{2})

(33)

If relativistic effects are included, or for experimental data,
E_{n}^{(2)}
is the quantity that should be subtracted from (negative) binding energies before performing a quantum defect fit, and then added back in at the end. Otherwise, the nonrelativistic Eq. (
23
) should be used, or alternatively Eq. (
24
) with the adjusted
R_{M}^{(+)}

Rydberg.

VI
Conclusions and Discussion

The main approach if this work is to use a quantum defect fit only for the pure nonrelativistic binding energies, obtained by direct calculation up to
n=35
. The resulting quantum defect parameters shown in Table
6
are of unprecedented accuracy, and reproduce the full 20-figure accuracy of the calculated energies over the entire range of
n
up to 35. The much smaller relativistic and QED corrections are represented to sufficient accuracy as purely
1/n
expansions. The expansions themselves reveal important contributions that vary as
1/n^{2}
and come from second-order mass polarization terms of order
(\mu/M)^{2}
, either directly, or indirectly through recoil corrections to the terms
\tilde{\Delta}_{2}
and
\langle B_{1}+B_{4}\rangle
. These
1/n^{2}
terms were derived previously in the asymptotic limit of high-
L
Rydberg states
Drachman86
;
DrakeYan92
. The present work demonstrates that they are present also for
P
-states. It remains to be shown that they are present with the same state-independent coefficients for
S
-states. It is essential to subtract them before performing a quantum defect fit in order to avoid a
1/n^{2}
contamination of the main
1/n*^{2}
quantum defect fit. These second-order recoil terms provide a formal justification for using the effective
R_{M}^{(+)}
reduced mass Rydberg in place of the formally correct
R_{M}
in performing quantum defect fits.

There are two main points of significance to these results. First, each of the 28 measured transition frequencies
Clausen2021
;
Clausen2025

provides an independent measure of the
2\;^{3}S_{1}
ionization energy, using a Ritz quantum defect fit only for the nonrelativistic energy for
n>35
. The close agreement with the experimental data analyzed with a standard quantum defect fit
Clausen2021
;
Clausen2025
provides a strong confirmation of QDT and the Ritz expansion, at least in the low-
Z
region where relativistic effects are not too large.

The second significance is that the present results provide an unequivocal confirmation of a 9
\sigma
disagreement with the theoretical ionization energy of the
2\;^{3}S_{1}
state
Patkos2021
of
0.474\pm 0.052
MHz, wth experiment lying lower than theory. The discrepancy is particularly puzzling since it is almost the same for the case of
3
He
Clausen2025c
, and it
is not apparent in the
2\;^{3}S_{1}-2\;^{1}S_{0}
isotope shift
Eikema2025
;
Steinebach2026
. It therefore does not depend on the particular isotope or neutron number. In the absence of errors on the theoretical side, or unexpectedly large higher-order QED terms, Cong et al.
Cong2026
discuss possible explanations involving new particles affecting the electron-electron interaction. They are able to exclude two of the four possible interactions just based on the sign of the discrepancy for the
2\;^{3}S_{1}
state, and most of the remainder, except possibly for a light scalar boson as the only viable candidate. It is not yet clear whether the discrepancy is also present for the singlet spectrum since the same
m\alpha^{7}
terms

Patkos2021
have not yet been calculated to reduce the theoretical uncertainty. Parallel high precision experiments and theory for the triplet spectrum of Li
+

Yerokhin2023
would be very interesting as the next case to study.

Note Added:
After this work was completed, we learned of calculations based on the correlated B-spline method
Chi2026
that are in similarly good agreement with the experimental QD extrapolation of Clausen et al. Close agreement with the present calculation provides an important confirmation of both sets of results.
