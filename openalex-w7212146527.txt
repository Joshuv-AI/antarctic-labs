Feasibility and safety of intravenous tolvaptan administration
immediately upon ICU admission after cardiac surgery with
cardiopulmonary bypass
Masahiko Narita1,2 · Kentaro Shirakura2 · Eijiro Hikasa1 · Hana Fukuta1 · Yuya Tamaru1 · Tomoki Nakatsu1 ·
Takayuki Kadohama1 · Nobuyuki Akasaka3 · Hiroyuki Kamiya2
Received: 18 January 2026 / Accepted: 18 July 2026
© The Author(s) 2026

Abstract
Purpose Tolvaptan sodium phosphate (TSP; Samtas®, Otsuka Pharmaceutical, Tokyo, Japan) is a novel intravenous
aquaretic diuretic; however, evidence for its use in patients after cardiac surgery is limited. We evaluated the early postoperative administration of TSP in patients who underwent cardiac surgery.
Methods We enrolled 91 patients who underwent cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass at our institution. Of these,
46 received TSP immediately after intensive care unit admission, and 45 served as controls. After propensity score matching,
20 matched pairs were analyzed. A subgroup analysis was performed according to the renal function in TSP-treated patients.
Results In the propensity score-matched cohort, the TSP group showed a trend toward adequate early postoperative urine
output and a gradual negative fluid balance. Regarding safety, electrolyte abnormalities did not increase in the TSP group.
No patient in either group developed significant acute kidney injury. Similar trends were observed in patients with a reduced
renal function.
Conclusions In this selected cohort, intravenous TSP administration immediately upon ICU admission after cardiac surgery
with cardiopulmonary bypass was feasible and not associated with any major safety concerns, including in patients with a
reduced renal function. TSP also tended to reduce day-to-day fluid balance fluctuations with an adequate urine output.
Keywords Cardiac surgery · Diuretics · Fluid management · Postoperative management · Tolvaptan sodium phosphate

Tolvaptan is an oral, selective vasopressin receptor-2
(V2) antagonist that promotes electrolyte-free diuresis
without activating the renin–angiotensin system or stimulating the sympathetic nervous system [4, 5]. It is effective
in achieving rapid body weight reduction in patients with
fluid overload after cardiac surgery [4, 6–9]. Tolvaptan
sodium phosphate (TSP; Samtas®, Otsuka Pharmaceutical, Tokyo, Japan), a prodrug formulation for intravenous
administration, has been available commercially in Japan
since May 2022. It is a therapeutic option for patients with
a restricted oral intake or impaired gastrointestinal absorption. Although TSP has shown potential efficacy in clinical practice for treating congestive heart failure [10, 11],

intensive care unit (ICU) immediately after cardiac surgery.
In this study, we investigated the feasibility and safety of
TSP in this patient population. Furthermore, as the optimal
timing of tolvaptan administration remains unclear [5, 7,
12], our findings may contribute to ongoing discussions by
clarifying the clinical outcomes associated with early postoperative TSP use.
Oral tolvaptan exerts renoprotective effects and is associated with favorable outcomes in patients with chronic
kidney disease (CKD) following cardiac surgery, without
causing any renal function deterioration [13–15]. Therefore, in this study, we divided TSP-treated patients into
two groups according to the CKD stage and compared their
clinical outcomes.

Methods
Study design
This single-center, retrospective observational study
enrolled 201 consecutive patients who underwent open
cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass at our institution between June 2022 and December 2025. The exclusion
criteria were as follows: (1) patients who underwent offpump coronary artery bypass grafting or thoracoabdominal
aortic replacement; (2) urgent or emergent cases in which
an accurate preoperative body weight could not be determined, as perioperative fluid balance management was the
primary focus of this study; (3) preoperative CKD stage
IV with an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) < 30
mL/min/1.73 m²; (4) patients receiving preoperative hemodialysis (HD); (5) a low cardiac output with an ejection fraction < 30%; and (6) death within 6 days postoperatively.
For the subgroup analysis, the 46 patients who received
TSP immediately after ICU admission were divided into
two groups based on their CKD stage (Fig. 1). Patients with
CKD stage 1 or 2, defined as an eGFR ≥ 60 mL/min/1.73 m²,
were classified into the normal renal function group (N
group, n = 21). Patients with CKD stage 3a or 3b, defined as
an eGFR of 30–59 mL/min/1.73 m², were classified into the
reduced renal function group (R group, n = 25).
This study was approved by our Institutional Review
Board, which waived the requirement for informed patient
consent owing to the retrospective nature of the study (IRB
number: 24A011S-21).

Surgical procedure
Surgical procedures were performed via mid-sternotomy in
almost all cases; two cases were performed via a right lateral

13

incision for minimally invasive cardiac surgery. CPB was
established using standard techniques under normothermic
or moderately hypothermic conditions. Myocardial protection was maintained using intermittent antegrade and retrograde blood cardioplegia techniques. For aortic surgery,
selective cerebral perfusion was performed using the open
distal technique at a rectal temperature of 28 °C.

Postoperative management
Following surgery, all patients were admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU). The hemodynamic function and hourly
urine output were monitored, and the daily fluid balance,
including the total intake and output, was recorded during
the ICU stay. An arterial blood gas analysis was performed
every hour from ICU admission to the fourth hour and every
4 and 6 h thereafter until the patient was transferred to the
general ward. Chest radiography and blood tests were performed until postoperative day (POD) 7. Body weight was
measured daily until discharge. Electrocardiographic monitoring was continued from ICU admission until discharge.
Computed tomography (CT) was performed 7 ± 2 days
postoperatively.
In the TSP group, 16 mg of TSP was administered immediately after ICU admission after confirming the absence of
significant hypernatremia, defined as a serum sodium concentration of < 155 mEq/L. Additional doses of TSP were
administered after the day of surgery based on the urine
output and the patient’s clinical condition. In both the TSP
and control groups, intravenous carperitide and furosemide
were administered within the first few days after surgery
if needed. Our routine prescription was oral furosemide
20–40 mg/day and spironolactone 25–50 mg/day from POD
1. Additionally, oral tolvaptan was prescribed as needed
until the patients returned to their preoperative body weight.
The incidence of postoperative complications was also
evaluated in this study. Any postoperative decline in renal
function was assessed using the following definitions:
Acute kidney injury (AKI) was classified into stages 1, 2,
and 3 according to the Kidney Disease Improving Global
Outcomes criteria. Stage 1 AKI is defined as an absolute
increase in the serum creatinine level of ≥ 0.3 mg/dL from
baseline. Stage 2 is defined as a doubling of creatinine from
baseline, and stage 3 is defined as a tripling of creatinine,
a new initiation of dialysis, or an absolute creatinine value
≥ 4.0 mg/dL [16]. Pericardial effusion was evaluated using
postoperative CT and diagnosed when the thickest part of
the fluid accumulation exceeded 20 mm. Postoperative
atrial fibrillation (POAF) was defined as new-onset atrial
fibrillation after surgery in patients with no history of atrial
fibrillation.

Results
After applying the exclusion criteria, 91 patients were eligible for analysis. Among them, 46 patients who received
TSP immediately after ICU admission were assigned to the
TSP group, whereas 45 patients who did not receive TSP
served as the control group. Propensity score matching was
performed, yielding 20 matched pairs for a comparative
analysis (Fig. 1).
Forty patients were included in the propensity scorematched analysis. The baseline characteristics of the
matched cohorts are summarized in Table 1. After matching, the baseline characteristics, including preoperative oral
medications, were generally balanced between the TSP and
control groups, although some residual differences remained
between the groups. The surgical procedures and operative
variables were also comparable between the two groups.
CKD stage 3a was more frequent in the TSP group, whereas
stage 3b was more frequent in the control group; however,
the serum creatinine and eGFR levels were similar between
the two groups.
Figure 2A shows the hourly urine output during the first
24 h after the ICU admission. In both groups, urine output
was highest during the first hour after ICU admission, with
mean hourly urine outputs of 295.0 ± 140.5 mL in the TSP
group and 230.5 ± 155.6 mL in the control group. Thereafter,
the urine output gradually decreased. Hourly urine output at
4–6 h after ICU admission was significantly higher in the
TSP group than that in the control group.
Figure 2B illustrates the time courses of the serum
sodium and potassium concentrations after ICU admission. In the TSP group, the serum sodium levels showed
a gradual upward trend from 1 h after ICU admission,
although the degree of increase was limited. In both
groups, the serum sodium levels showed a downward
trend after approximately 8 h postoperatively, and overall, no significant intergroup difference was observed in
the time course of serum sodium concentrations. Within
24 h of ICU admission, three patients (15.0%) in the
TSP group developed hypernatremia (150–154 mEq/L),
and one patient (5.0%) developed marked hypernatremia

13

(≥ 155 mEq/L). All four patients had serum sodium concentrations between 150 and 154 mEq/L upon ICU admission, and in all cases, serum sodium levels normalized by
the following day without the need for specific intravenous therapy for sodium correction. In the control group,
two patients (10.0%) developed hypernatremia (150–154
mEq/L), which resolved spontaneously by the following
day without specific treatment. In both groups, the serum
potassium levels showed a gradual downward trend and
stabilized at approximately 4.0 mEq/L. No patient developed hyperkalemia (> 5.0 mEq/L) within 24 h of ICU
admission.
As shown in Fig. 2C, no significant differences were
observed in the daily urine output between the two groups
from POD 1 to POD 3. On POD 4 and 5, urine output tended
to be higher in the control group. The daily fluid balance
gradually shifted toward a negative balance from POD 1 in
the TSP group, whereas a negative balance was observed
from POD 2 onward in the control group. There were no
significant differences between the two groups in the proportion of patients requiring carperitide administration or
continuous intravenous furosemide infusion. Likewise, no
significant differences were observed in the cumulative
postoperative oral diuretic doses prescribed up to POD 7.
Postoperative changes in body weight in both groups are
shown in Fig. 2D. The median time to return to preoperative
body weight was 5.0 (3.3–8.0) days in the TSP group and
5.5 (3.3–9.5) days in the control group, with no significant
intergroup difference.
The postoperative outcomes are shown in Table 2. The
surgical mortality rate was 0% in both groups. Postoperative catecholamine requirements were comparable, and only
a few patients in either group required high-dose catecholamine support. The postoperative renal function declined
to AKI stage 1 in seven patients (35.0%) in the TSP group
and four patients (20.0%) in the control group, with no significant intergroup difference. No patient in either group
developed AKI stage 2 or higher, and none required continuous hemodiafiltration (CHDF) or conversion to hemodialysis. There were no significant differences between the
two groups in terms of the incidence or timing of POAF, and
all affected patients recovered to sinus rhythm before discharge. Postoperative pericardial effusion occurred in only
one patient (5.0%) in the TSP group and resolved with conservative management. The postoperative oral medications
listed in Table 2 include all medications administered after
surgery, including those discontinued before discharge.
Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists and loop diuretics
were prescribed to nearly all patients, and β-blockers were
administered in most cases.

No significant differences were observed in age, sex, height,
body weight, or preoperative comorbidities between the two
groups (Table 3). Aortic surgery was performed more frequently in the R group, which was reflected in the longer
operative and CPB times. The postoperative fluid balance
was significantly higher in the R group.
The hourly urine output from ICU admission to 24 h
postoperatively peaked in the first hour in both groups and
then gradually declined (Fig. 3A). A statistically significant
difference in the hourly urine output between the groups was
observed only during the 7–12 h postoperative interval, with
a lower output in the R group. No significant differences
were observed during the other time intervals. The changes
in serum sodium and potassium concentrations after TSP
administration are presented in Fig. 3B. The serum sodium
levels were slightly higher in the R group, although the difference was not significant (p = 0.454), and the incidence
of hypernatremia was comparable between the groups
(Table 4). The serum potassium levels showed a similar
downward trend in both groups and remained stable overall,
with no significant intergroup differences (p = 0.911).
As shown in Fig. 3C, daily urine output on POD 2 was
significantly higher in the R group (2488.3 ± 795.3 mL/day
in the N group vs. 3416.8 ± 1499.5 mL/day in the R group;
p = 0.011) but showed no significant differences on other
postoperative days. The daily fluid balance also shifted
toward a negative balance in the R group on POD 2, corresponding to the increased urine output on that day; however, no significant intergroup difference was observed in
the overall postoperative course. Postoperative changes in
body weight in both groups are shown in Fig. 3D. No significant intergroup differences were observed at any postoperative time point. The mean time to return to preoperative
weight was comparable between the groups (5.4 ± 3.9 days
in the N group vs. 5.9 ± 4.9 days in the R group; p = 0.704),
as was the proportion of patients achieving this by POD 6
(76.2% in the N group vs. 72.0% in the R group; p = 0.747).
Although a higher proportion of patients in the R group

required continuous intravenous furosemide infusion and
carperitide administration and tended to receive larger
amounts of postoperative oral diuretics, the differences were
not statistically significant.
No significant differences were observed in the 30-day
mortality or ICU length of stay between the groups. Regarding postoperative renal function, stage 1 AKI was more
frequent in the R group, although the difference was not
significant (9.5% in the N group vs. 28.0% in the R group,
p = 0.114). Stage 2 AKI occurred in one patient in the R
group. No significant differences were observed between
the two groups in terms of the incidence or timing of POAF.

Discussion
This study showed that (1) TSP administration immediately after cardiac surgery was feasible and did not raise
any major immediate safety concerns; (2) the TSP group
showed a trend toward adequate urine output from the early
postoperative period and less postoperative weight gain; (3)
no apparent worsening of the postoperative outcomes was
observed in association with TSP administration; and (4)
similar trends in feasibility and safety were also observed in
patients with a reduced renal function.
Hypernatremia is a well-recognized adverse event associated with tolvaptan therapy [4, 17]. Previous studies have
reported that tolvaptan use after cardiac surgery induces an
increase in the serum sodium levels in treated patients [4,
6, 8, 9]. In this study, TSP was administered intravenously
at a dose of 16 mg, raising concerns that the rapid onset
of aquaresis could lead to clinically significant hypernatremia. However, although the serum sodium levels in the TSP
group increased after administration compared to those in
the control group, the degree of increase was limited, and
the overall time course did not differ significantly between
the two groups. Additionally, the incidence of hypernatremia and marked hypernatremia was not significantly different between the groups. Taken together, these findings
suggest that TSP administration is not associated with an
increased occurrence of clinically relevant hypernatremia.
In this study, we used a relatively permissive withholding criterion for TSP (serum sodium ≥ 155 mEq/L at ICU
admission). None of the patients met this criterion; therefore, TSP was administered to all patients in the TSP group.
This approach was adopted because, consistent with routine
postoperative management after cardiac surgery, an arterial
blood gas analysis was performed every 1–2 h after ICU
admission, allowing for close monitoring of serum sodium
and other electrolytes. In retrospect, all four patients in
the TSP group who developed hypernatremia had a serum
sodium level ≥ 150 mEq/L upon ICU admission. Prior

Surgery Today

studies have indicated that higher baseline sodium levels
may predispose patients to hypernatremia [9, 17]. Collectively, our findings suggest that restricting immediate
postoperative TSP administration to patients with an ICU
admission serum sodium level of < 150 mEq/L further
enhances safety.
The serum potassium levels followed a similar postoperative course in both groups, and immediate TSP administration after ICU admission was not associated with instability
in the serum potassium levels or hyperkalemia. One possible explanation is that, unlike other diuretics, TSP induces
diuresis without increasing urinary electrolyte excretion [4,
5], which may account for the relative stability of the serum
potassium levels. This stable electrolyte profile may be considered a potentially favorable finding in the present study.
In the present study cohort, immediate postoperative
TSP administration was not associated with hemodynamic
instability. The requirement for vasoactive/inotropic agents
after ICU admission was not significantly greater in the TSP
group, which may support the feasibility of TSP administration. However, this observation should be interpreted cautiously because the present study was limited to patients
with a relatively low operative risk, including the exclusion
of those with severely impaired cardiac function. In other
words, it cannot be ruled out that the preservation of hemodynamic stability may have been observed only in patients
with relatively favorable preoperative conditions. Nevertheless, because oral tolvaptan has been reported to provide
safe and effective diuresis even in patients with heart failure
with a reduced ejection fraction [18], further studies should
evaluate the clinical outcomes of TSP in cohorts that include
patients with an impaired cardiac function.
TSP administration was not associated with an increased
incidence of major postoperative complications, including renal complications. Postoperative AKI was limited to
stage 1 in both groups and was observed more frequently
in the TSP group, although the difference was not statistically significant. Considering that stage 1 AKI was defined
according to the relatively strict criterion of an increase in
serum creatinine of ≥ 0.3 mg/dL from baseline, the observed
incidence may still fall within an acceptable postoperative range after cardiac surgery. In addition, no patient in
the TSP group developed stage 2 or higher AKI or required
renal replacement therapy.
POAF is a frequent complication after cardiac surgery,
with a reported incidence of approximately 15–40% [19–
21], and the incidence observed in both groups in the present study was within this range. Although the incidence was
numerically lower in the TSP group than in the control group,
the difference was not statistically significant, and given the
limited sample size and the non-standardized postoperative
diuretic management after TSP administration, it is difficult

13

to conclude that TSP itself reduced the occurrence of POAF.
Oral tolvaptan has also been reported to suppress POAF
after open-heart surgery [22]; however, further evidence is
needed to clarify whether TSP exerts a similar effect.
The time to return to preoperative body weight and the
proportion of patients who achieved this by POD 6 did not
differ between the two groups, suggesting broadly comparable postoperative fluid management outcomes between
the groups. However, the postoperative urine output profiles
differed between the groups. In the control group, urine output appeared to peak later, and the daily fluid balance tended
to shift more substantially toward a negative balance during
this period. In contrast, in the TSP group, the urine output
peak appeared earlier, and the daily fluid balance tended to
remain mildly but consistently negative. Although the fluid
balance should not be interpreted as a clinical outcome,
and these observations do not demonstrate a direct clinical
benefit of TSP administration, they may suggest that TSP
administration is not negatively associated with the early
resolution of postoperative fluid overload.
The optimal timing of tolvaptan administration after
cardiac surgery remains debatable [5, 7, 12]. Tolvaptan is
a selective V2 receptor antagonist that inhibits free water
reabsorption via the aquaporin-2 (AQP2) system, which is
regulated by arginine vasopressin (AVP) [23]. In patients
with heart failure, responders to tolvaptan exhibit appropriately high urinary AQP2 levels relative to the plasma AVP
levels [24]. Yokoyama et al. investigated the postoperative
plasma AVP and urinary AQP2 levels in patients who underwent cardiac surgery. In their study, plasma AVP peaked at
6 h postoperatively, whereas urinary AQP2 peaked at 12 h
postoperatively [5]. Consequently, the urinary AQP2-toplasma AVP ratio is low immediately after surgery, but it
increases at a relatively later stage, which may represent
a more appropriate time for tolvaptan administration [5].
However, in the present study, an adequate urine output was
obtained in the TSP group from the early period after ICU
admission, namely during the 0–1 h and 2–3 h intervals, and
urine output at 4–6 h after ICU admission was significantly
higher than that in the control group. Although these findings do not establish the superiority of immediate administration in terms of achieving early diuresis, they suggest that
the TSP administration strategy used in the present study
should not be excluded as a therapeutic option and it may be
considered a feasible postoperative strategy.

Consistent with these observations, in the present study,
TSP administration may have contributed to the absence
of postoperative electrolyte instability or unfavorable postoperative outcomes in the reduced renal function group.
However, this subgroup analysis by renal function should
be interpreted with caution because of the small sample
size and the presence of factors that may have influenced
the results, including significant differences in the proportion of aortic surgery, operative time, and cardiopulmonary
bypass time. Nevertheless, given that postoperative fluid
balance management is often challenging in patients with
an impaired renal function, these findings support the feasibility of TSP as a therapeutic option for this population.
This study has several limitations. First, this was a retrospective analysis of prospectively collected observational
data from our institutional database. Moreover, because the
TSP and control groups were derived from sequential time
periods, some residual historical bias may have remained
despite propensity score matching, and unmeasured perioperative factors may have also affected the observed findings. Second, the sample size was small. This limitation
was particularly relevant to the subgroup analyses, which
included only a small number of patients and may have been
affected by residual confounding. Third, the study population consisted of elective surgical patients, and those with
a higher operative risk were excluded. Therefore, further
studies including such high-risk patients, particularly those
with an impaired cardiac function, are needed to determine whether the feasibility and safety of TSP observed
in the present study are also applicable to these populations. Fourth, postoperative management after POD 1 was
not standardized, as the continuation of TSP and the use of
additional diuretics were individualized based on the clinical findings and physician discretion. Therefore, postoperative outcomes cannot be solely attributed to immediate
