ABSTRACT: This study examines how Brazilian publications on Building Information Modeling for
Facility Management (BIM–FM) compare with those published in internationally indexed journals. A
comparative systematic mapping was conducted using four thematic protocols addressing (i) smart
technologies in Facility Management, (ii) BIM capability and organizational maturity, (iii) semantic
interoperability and data integration, and (iv) building operation, maintenance, and asset management.
Thirty peer-reviewed studies published between 2022 and 2026 were analyzed through an integrated
framework combining thematic, qualitative, scientometric, bibliometric, and traceability analyses.
Studies were assessed according to Innovation Level, Scientific Contribution, and Alignment with the
BIM–FM Knowledge Frontier, alongside journal rankings, indexing coverage, and citation metrics. The
findings indicate that internationally indexed publications show stronger alignment with emerging
BIM–FM trends, particularly the integration of BIM with Digital Twins, Artificial Intelligence,
Internet of Things technologies, semantic interoperability, predictive maintenance, and intelligent asset

INTRODUCTION
The integration of Building Information Modeling (BIM) and Facility Management
(FM) has emerged as an important research frontier in the built environment, reflecting the
global shift toward data-driven digital transformation and extending BIM beyond design and
construction to support integrated asset management throughout the building life cycle
(ABIDEEN et al., 2022). This convergence also contributes to the United Nations Sustainable
Development Goals (SDGs) 9, 11, and 12 by promoting technological innovation, more efficient
building operation, and improved resource management (ABIDEEN et al., 2022).
Despite advances in BIM–FM integration and interoperability through IFC and
OpenBIM, persistent challenges remain, including data fragmentation, outdated information
models, limited process maturity, and difficulties in maintaining consistent information
throughout the asset life cycle (OTRANTO; JUNIOR; PELLANDA, 2025). Existing buildings
present additional challenges related to data acquisition and the alignment between BIM
models and operational requirements (KERN; ROSA; BRAGANÇA, 2024). Moreover,
emerging technologies such as IoT, Digital Twins, and Extended Reality still require more
mature integration frameworks, validation methods, and operational workflows for large-scale
implementation (WIJERATHNA et al., 2024).
These technological differences are also reflected in the scientific literature. Brazilian
research has predominantly focused on BIM adoption, implementation barriers, organizational
maturity, and localized applications, whereas international research increasingly investigates
integrated solutions combining IoT, Digital Twins, Artificial Intelligence, Extended Reality,
and open data frameworks for operation and maintenance (ROSÁRIO DA SILVA et al., 2024),
this distinction is accompanied by differences in publication visibility and dissemination,
particularly given the concentration of advanced BIM–FM research in internationally
established journals (PAIVA; BRITO, 2019).
However, this pattern is not unique to Brazil. Research communities in countries such
as China, Argentina, Mexico, and South Africa have developed different trajectories of
scientific internationalization, influenced by institutional, linguistic, and structural factors
(BEIGEL, 2021; DOS SANTOS; RODRIGUES; DA SILVA NEUBERT, 2023). While

Against this background, this study addresses the following research question: How
does the technological and thematic profile of BIM–FM research published in Brazilian
journals compare with that observed in internationally disseminated research, and how do these
profiles coexist with differences in publication visibility, indexing, and scientific
dissemination?
To address this question, the study adopts a comparative systematic mapping approach,
using BIM–FM research as the unit of analysis. Rather than seeking to represent the entire
body of BIM–FM literature, the study examines a systematically constructed corpus based on
predefined eligibility criteria, focusing on thematic orientation, technological development,
and publication ecosystems. This approach enables a comparative examination of Brazilian and
international research beyond conventional measures of productivity or scientific impact.
METHODOLOGY
Research Design And Analytical Framework
This study adopted a comparative systematic mapping approach combined with the
construction of a purposive bibliographic corpus and complementary thematic, qualitative,
scientometric, and editorial analyses. The methodological design was developed to characterize
and compare Brazilian and internationally disseminated research in Building Information
Modeling for Facility Management (BIM–FM), with particular attention to technological
development, research themes, and patterns of scientific dissemination.
The systematic mapping approach was used to identify, organize, and characterize
relevant publications according to predefined thematic protocols and eligibility criteria. Rather

SEARCH STRATEGY
Search Protocol Design
An exploratory search was initially conducted using the LeapSpace platform to identify
recurring terminology, emerging topics, and alternative keywords in the international BIM–
FM literature. Based on this preliminary assessment, the review was organized into four
thematic protocols: Protocol I – Smart Technologies Applied to Facility Management; Protocol
II – BIM Capability and Maturity; Protocol III – Semantic Interoperability and Data
Integration; and Protocol IV – Building Operation and Maintenance Management.
Each protocol was implemented using specific search strategies while maintaining
conceptual equivalence between Brazilian and international databases. Protocol I addressed
BIM integration with IoT, Artificial Intelligence, machine learning, monitoring, and
predictive maintenance (HAIYIRETE; ZHANG; GAO, 2024); Protocol II focused on BIM
capability, maturity, governance, and implementation models (KHALEEL; HILAL, 2023);
Protocol III addressed interoperability, semantic modeling, ontologies, and data integration
(AKBARIEH; TETERVOV, 2026); and Protocol IV covered operation, maintenance, decision
support, and Facility Management applications (PATACAS; DAWOOD; KASSEM, 2020).

Search Strategy and Study Selection
Searches were adapted to the indexing structure of each database. Scopus and Web of
Science used TITLE–ABS–KEY fields, while Brazilian databases searched across available
fields. Records were filtered by document type, publication period, and subject area, followed
by duplicate removal. The review included peer-reviewed journal articles on BIM applications
in Facility Management, including operation and maintenance, interoperability, BIM maturity,
digital transformation, and asset management, while excluding reviews, conceptual studies,
unrelated built-environment research, and studies focused exclusively on design or
construction.
Eligible studies from 2022–2026 were then purposively selected using thematic relevance
and bibliometric/editorial indicators, including Google Scholar h5-index, SJR quartile, Qualis
classification, and citation counts. When no eligible study was available for a given year, the
second-ranked article from the preceding year was used to maintain temporal continuity.
Construction of the Analytical Corpus
The final corpus was established through duplicate removal, thematic refinement,
eligibility screening, and purposive selection. Rather than pursuing exhaustive literature
coverage or a predetermined sample size, the procedure prioritized information-rich studies
with strong thematic relevance, scientific significance, methodological diversity, and potential
to support comparisons between Brazilian and international BIM–FM research.
Scientometric and Editorial Characterization of the Bibliographic Portfolios
The selected studies were characterized using complementary scientometric and
editorial indicators, including journal indexing, SJR quartiles, Google Scholar h5-index,
citation counts, and coverage in Scopus, Web of Science, SciELO, and the CAPES Journal
Portal. These indicators were considered jointly to characterize differences in scientific
visibility, editorial positioning, and dissemination environments rather than to rank individual
publications or assess scientific quality through a single metric. Together, the thematic,
scientometric, and editorial analyses provided the basis for comparing technological

Methodological Limitations
This study has limitations related to the construction and comparison of bibliographic
portfolios from distinct publication ecosystems. The smaller Brazilian corpus and differences
in database indexing required complementary manual searches, but some relevant studies may
remain unidentified. The portfolios were also constructed using different combinations of
bibliographic sources, which may have influenced their composition. Moreover, the 30-study
corpus was designed for comparative analytical purposes rather than statistical
representativeness, so the observed patterns should not be generalized to the entire BIM–FM
research field.
The qualitative classifications of Innovation Level, Scientific Contribution, and
Alignment with the BIM–FM Knowledge Frontier also involve interpretive judgment.
Although predefined criteria and author consensus were used, these categories do not constitute
formally validated measurement instruments and should therefore be understood as analytical
classifications rather than objective measures of research quality. Finally, indicators such as
SJR, h5-index, citation counts, database indexing, and Qualis classification capture different
dimensions of publication visibility and should not be treated as interchangeable measures of
scientific quality or impact. The observed relationships between publication ecosystems and
research profiles are likewise associative rather than causal. Future studies could address these
limitations through larger longitudinal datasets, harmonized retrieval strategies, formal interrater reliability procedures, and comparisons involving additional national research
ecosystems.
Qualitative Evaluation Criteria
A qualitative assessment was conducted to examine the technological evolution,
scientific contribution, and alignment with the BIM–FM research frontier. Three dimensions
were considered: Innovation Level, Scientific Contribution, and Alignment with the BIM–FM
Knowledge Frontier, based on each study’s objectives, methodology, technologies, results, and
contributions. Assessments were conducted independently by the authors and consolidated
through consensus.

Bim-fm research themes
Characterization of the scientific portfolios
The scientific portfolio was characterized according to authors, publication year,
journal, country of origin, and thematic protocol. The complete characterization of the 30
selected studies, including identification numbers and thematic classifications, is available as
supplementary material in the Zenodo repository: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23042114.
The international portfolio presents a relatively balanced distribution across the four
protocols, with emphasis on semantic interoperability, Digital Twins, Artificial Intelligence
(AI), Internet of Things (IoT), and intelligent decision support for building operation and
maintenance. In contrast, the Brazilian portfolio focuses mainly on process digitalization, BIM
maturity assessment, and operational management, indicating a narrower technological scope.
Differences are also observed in publication venues and research groups. International
studies are predominantly published in journals such as Automation in Construction, Energy
and Buildings, Computers in Industry, and SoftwareX, with recurring contributions from
established research groups, including those led by Haidar Hosamo and Gokcen Yilmaz.
Brazilian publications are more dispersed across journals and, despite recurring contributors
such as Sergio Scheer, tend to emphasize the adaptation and application of established
technologies.

Thematic and technological analysis of bim–fm research
To provide a more detailed characterization of the selected studies, a comparative
analysis of their thematic and technological attributes was conducted. The analysis considers
the technologies employed, Facility Management (FM) applications, level of innovation,
degree of digital integration, research topic, scientific contribution, and alignment with the
BIM–FM research frontier. The qualitative classifications were assigned based on the
assessment framework described in Section 2.5, which was grounded in an integrated analysis
of the objectives, methodology, technologies, results, and contributions reported in each study.
The complete thematic and technological analysis of the 30 studies included in the
systematic literature review is available as supplementary material in the Zenodo repository,
available at: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23046660. The supplementary material provides
the detailed assessment of each study, including its FM application, thematic contribution,
associated technologies, degree of digital integration, innovation level, and alignment with the
BIM–FM research frontier.
The findings suggest a paradigm shift within the BIM–FM ecosystem, in which BIM is
evolving from a tool primarily used for modeling and documentation into a central data
infrastructure supporting asset life-cycle management. The international portfolio
demonstrates a high degree of technological convergence, characterized by data-driven and
interoperable digital architectures. This pattern is consistent with the observations of Luo et al.
(2021), who emphasized the need for interoperable ecosystems to support life-cycle information
management, and Hakimi and Abudayyeh (2024), who identified these technologies as
representing the current research frontier in BIM–FM.
In contrast, the Brazilian portfolio is predominantly oriented toward process
digitalization, information structuring, and the development of organizational BIM maturity.
This emphasis appears to reflect the current stage of BIM adoption in Brazil, particularly
within the public sector, where institutional, regulatory, and managerial conditions remain

in which BIM functions as a central information infrastructure, increasingly combined with
Digital Twins, IoT, AI, and semantic technologies to support predictive maintenance,
continuous monitoring, and operational optimization (HODAVAND et al., 2025; HOSAMO
et al., 2022, 2023a). Despite these advances, interoperability, scalability, semantic
standardization, sensing infrastructure, and cloud dependence remain important challenges,
while applications are still concentrated in specific building systems and facility types.
Brazilian studies, in contrast, primarily focus on BIM digitalization, information organization,
maintenance, and heritage documentation, with limited integration between digital models and
operational systems. Although some studies advance BIM–FM frameworks and predictive
energy simulation (PUSHPANATHAN et al., 2026), the Brazilian portfolio shows limited
adoption of IoT, Digital Twins, semantic interoperability, and continuous synchronization.
This difference reflects distinct research priorities: international research increasingly explores
intelligent, data-driven ecosystems, whereas Brazilian research remains more focused on
establishing the organizational, technological, and informational foundations for BIM–FM
implementation.

International research increasingly develops BIM capability and maturity models

subjectivity and support systematic organizational improvement. However, reliable
information, interoperability, and consistent information requirements remain critical
challenges. Brazilian studies primarily address organizational BIM maturity, adoption barriers,
and digital transformation, with comparatively limited attention to FM-specific capabilities.
Research highlights constraints related to training, software and infrastructure costs,
organizational capacity, and workflow reorganization (ROCHA; CÂNDIDO; BARROS
NETO, 2025; VASCONCELOS; GERMANO, 2023). Although Brazilian studies have
advanced automated maturity assessment and BIM–Lean integration, the literature remains
largely focused on consolidating the organizational and technological foundations required for
more specialized BIM–FM applications.
Protocol III — Semantic Interoperability and Ontologies
International studies identify information fragmentation, semantic limitations of
OpenBIM standards, and risks to data integrity during information exchange as major barriers
to BIM–FM integration. Semantic Web technologies, ontologies, IoT integration, and
automated reasoning are increasingly proposed to address these limitations by improving
semantic consistency, interoperability, information retrieval, and automated decision-making.
In contrast, only one Brazilian study identified within the predefined search protocol addressed
ontologies and semantic interoperability, indicating that this research area remains
underrepresented in the Brazilian BIM–FM literature. This limited representation may be
associated with the interdisciplinary expertise required across BIM, FM, data science, Semantic
Web technologies, and information systems (OLIVEIRA SENA et al., 2024). Consequently,
the limited development of semantic approaches may constrain information reuse and
interoperability throughout the asset life cycle.

Protocol IV — Building Operation, Maintenance, and Asset Management
International studies increasingly integrate BIM with Digital Twins, IoT, AI,
optimization algorithms, and AR to support predictive maintenance, fault detection, workorder management, interoperability, and operational decision-making (HOSAMO et al., 2023b;
KAMAL; TAGHADDOS; METVAEI, 2022; PALHA; HÜTTL; DA COSTA E SILVA, 2024;
ZHAO et al., 2022). Nevertheless, high implementation costs, data standardization,
professional expertise, and maintenance of accurate as-built models remain relevant barriers.
Brazilian studies, in contrast, primarily focus on information requirements, conceptual
frameworks, interoperability, and process standardization. Wojciechowski and Schramm
(2025) propose a COBie-based information-requirement model for maintenance management,
while Teixeira and Scheer (2024) demonstrate the practical application of BIM, terrestrial laser
scanning, and automated rule checking for space management. However, neither incorporates
emerging technologies such as Digital Twins, AI, or IoT. Overall, Brazilian research remains
focused on consolidating the informational and organizational foundations of BIM–FM,
whereas international research increasingly emphasizes automation, real-time monitoring, and
predictive maintenance.

Before examining the bibliometric indicators, we considered the possibility that the
differences observed between the Brazilian and international portfolios might be explained by
differences in the scientific capacity of the researchers involved. To explore this possibility, we
examined the Scopus Author ID profiles of the authors of studies published in Brazilian
journals. In all selected studies, at least one author had Scopus-indexed publications addressing
the same research topic as the study included in this review, including articles published in
international journals.

Digital Twins and Heritage Building Information Modeling (HBIM), as well as studies by
Bianca Maria Vasconcelos on Machine Learning applications for accident prevention in the
construction industry. Although these examples do not support broad generalizations, they
indicate that researchers affiliated with the Brazilian research ecosystem contribute to

Research evaluation and publication strategies
Qualis Periódicos is the principal journal evaluation instrument used by CAPES in the
assessment of Brazilian graduate programs. Although it was not designed as a direct measure
of journal scientific quality (BRASIL, 2023), its use as an indicator of editorial prestige may
influence publication strategies. Its methodology incorporates indicators such as the Google
Scholar h5-index, which has broader coverage than Scopus and Web of Science. Consequently,
high Qualis classifications do not necessarily correspond to equivalent levels of international
indexing, citation visibility, or scientific dissemination.
Similar tensions between national evaluation systems and international bibliometric
indicators have been reported in China (HUANG et al., 2021; WANG; HALFFMAN;
ZHANG, 2023), Russia (TRETYAKOVA, 2023), and Indonesia (YUSUF, 2026). Conversely,
the Strategy Evaluation Protocol (SEP) adopted in the Netherlands illustrates a more formative
approach that emphasizes research diversity and societal relevance rather than journal rankings
(BRASIL; TREVISOL, 2025).
The Brazilian case can therefore be interpreted as part of a broader challenge in research
evaluation: balancing the strengthening of national scientific ecosystems with international

CONCLUSIONS
This study compared Brazilian and internationally disseminated BIM–FM research
from technological, thematic, scientometric, and editorial perspectives. The findings indicate
that the international portfolio shows stronger alignment with the contemporary BIM–FM
research frontier, particularly through the integration of Building Information Modeling
(BIM), Digital Twins, the Internet of Things (IoT), Artificial Intelligence (AI), ontologies,
semantic interoperability, and predictive maintenance. In contrast, the Brazilian portfolio
predominantly addresses BIM adoption, organizational maturity, process digitalization,
information structuring, and context-specific operational applications.
These differences should not be interpreted as evidence of limited scientific capacity
among Brazilian researchers. The analysis identified Brazilian authors with internationally
indexed publications addressing the same emerging technological domains, indicating that the

technological profile of studies published in Brazilian journals should not be equated with the
technological capabilities of the national research community.
The integrated thematic, qualitative, bibliometric, and traceability analyses indicate
that differences in publication environments, indexing coverage, citation structures, research
evaluation practices, and internationalization are associated with differences in the
dissemination of BIM–FM research. However, the study does not establish a causal
relationship between evaluation mechanisms, publication strategies, and technological research
orientation. The findings should therefore be interpreted as patterns within the selected
publication ecosystems rather than as direct measures of research quality or technological
capability.
The study has several limitations. The analysis was based exclusively on the scientific
literature and did not experimentally validate the identified technologies or solutions. In
addition, the relatively small Brazilian corpus and differences in database coverage and
indexing may have influenced portfolio composition. The 30 selected studies constitute an
analytically constructed comparative corpus rather than a statistically representative sample of
the BIM–FM literature. Finally, the classifications of Innovation Level, Scientific
Contribution, and Alignment with the BIM–FM Knowledge Frontier involve interpretive
judgment and were not formally validated as measurement instruments.
Future research should employ larger and more harmonized datasets, longitudinal
comparisons, and broader analyses of national research evaluation and publication systems to
investigate whether the patterns identified here persist across different contexts and periods.
From a practical perspective, strengthening the international indexing of Brazilian journals,
improving editorial practices, and expanding the dissemination of research addressing
emerging BIM–FM technologies may contribute to greater international visibility. These
potential effects should, however, be empirically investigated rather than assumed.
