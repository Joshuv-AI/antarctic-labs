 Microgrid Benefits

• Supports reliable energy access through a variety of generation
  sources
• Can operate independently from the grid, continuing to provide
  power to facilities connected to the microgrid
• Can provide resilience against power outages
• In some cases, microgrids may sell power back to the grid
• Microgrids that incorporate localized energy generation resources
  can have additional benefits.

                                                         National Laboratory of the Rockies   |   6

 Microgrid Challenges

 Microgrids are just one way to improve the energy reliability and
recovery of an electric system and do have potential challenges to
 be considered.
• Microgrids may have high up-front costs
• Regulation may complicate microgrid projects
• The need for growth in a microgrid project can be underestimated,
 leading to the selection of components that might make it hard to add
 more energy resources in the future
• Microgrids are complex systems that require specialized skills to operate
 and maintain
• Controls and communication systems could increase cybersecurity risks.

 Microgrid Cost

• The main driver of cost for a microgrid is its size, as measured by generation
  capacity
• A microgrid project’s cost depends on location, size, and complexity.

 What is Needed for a Microgrid?

• Control of power and load balance
 o Controllable power source
 o Controllable load demand
 o Power storage
• Control of power quality
 o Voltage support
 o Frequency support
 o Power factor (var) support
• System controls
 o Integrated controls that prioritize and control loads will reduce storage and inverter requirements.
 o Renewable energy input forecasting and load forecasting capability for controls will optimize assets.
• Ability to synchronize with grid
• Ability to disconnect from grid during outages.
