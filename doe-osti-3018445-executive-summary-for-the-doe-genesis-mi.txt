 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

 This document provides a concise overview of a research program developed in support of the DOE Genesis Mission,
illustrating how a modern semantic AI system can accelerate conceptual exploration in fundamental physics. The work
summarized here accompanies three Fermilab Technical Notes that present a speculative—yet rigorously structured—
framework for a pre-geometric cosmology emerging from a finite spectral substrate.
 The core idea is that spacetime geometry, causal structure, and vacuum layering can arise from a replication-driven
instability in an initially non-spatial frequency domain. A simple 1 → n replication rule generates:

 • an emergent temporal order (discrete ticks),

 • spatial adjacency and effective dimensionality,

 • a limiting causal speed analogous to c,

 • a two-layer vacuum structure,

 • reheating and matter/radiation production,

 • and a multi-peaked stochastic gravitational-wave spectrum.

The resulting cosmological history resembles standard FRW evolution but originates from an entirely different micro-
scopic mechanism.
Role of AI in the Research Process. The conceptual development was carried out collaboratively between the
author and OpenAI’s ChatGPT 5.1, used as:

 1. a real-time translator between informal intuition and technical formalism,

 2. a generator of intermediate mathematical structures consistent with stated axioms,

 3. a tool for maintaining global logical coherence across many iterations,

 4. a mechanism for exploring alternative formulations and checking internal consistency,

 5. and a rapid prototyper for organizing scientific narratives in LaTeX form.

AI did not introduce physical claims autonomously; rather, it enabled the author to express, extend, and test concep-
tual reasoning that would otherwise be inaccessible or prohibitively slow to develop. This project demonstrates that
semantic AI can substantially accelerate early-stage theoretical ideation while keeping scientific responsibility firmly
with the human researcher.
Relevance to the DOE Genesis Mission. The Genesis Mission highlights the importance of AI-powered acceler-
ation of scientific discovery. The present work exemplifies this approach by showing how:

 • AI can help bridge gaps in mathematical and conceptual language,

 • hybrid human–AI reasoning can generate structured speculative models,

 • iterative refinement across multiple threads can be efficiently coordinated,

 • and complex conceptual frameworks can be explored within weeks rather than months.

 2

  While the cosmological model described here is speculative and not (yet) predictive at the quantitative level, it
demonstrates how AI can amplify human creativity and systematically organize unconventional scientific exploration.
This is central to the Genesis Mission’s objective of using AI not merely as a computational tool but as an intellectual
accelerator.
Next Steps. The program will proceed with:
 1. mathematical formalization of the replication dynamics;
 2. numerical toy models of spectral-to-geometric emergence;
 3. derivation of gravitational-wave signatures;
 4. exploration of possible embeddings into quantum-field-theoretic language;
 5. comparison with observational constraints from LISA, PTAs, and ground-based detectors.
  The conceptual framework and its current formulation are presented in detail in three companion Fermilab Technical
Notes: FERMILAB-TM-2923, FERMILAB-TM-2924, FERMILAB-TM-2925. Together, these documents outline the
foundation and forward trajectory of a research program that leverages modern AI systems to explore unconventional
early-universe dynamics.
