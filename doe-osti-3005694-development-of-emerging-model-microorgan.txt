Abstract
The metabolic diversity of microorganisms in nature represents a largely untapped source of
valuable compounds that are difficult or impossible to produce in the limited number of available
model systems. Efforts to produce longer-chain alcohols, such as hexanol, in organisms like
Escherichia coli have met with limited success; production of C6 and larger products remains
low, highlighting the challenges of extending chain elongation pathways beyond a single cycle.
Megasphaera elsdenii naturally condenses acetyl-CoA to efficiently generate C4–C8 organic
acids, making it a promising candidate for producing fuels and chemicals from lactate and plant-
derived carbohydrates. This high native flux through the chain elongation pathway offers the
potential for higher yields and titers of medium-chain products, such as hexanol, compared to
conventional hosts. Recent advances—most notably the development of a transformation method
for M. elsdenii—have further opened the organism to detailed physiological studies and
bioengineering. While full development of M. elsdenii as a hexanol-producing platform was not
achieved, significant progress was made in understanding its metabolism and building
foundational genetic tools for future engineering.

To evaluate the potential of M. elsdenii for hexanol production, we pursued three primary
objectives: (1) reconstructing and refining a genome-scale metabolic model to guide strain
design; (2) developing advanced genetic tools to enable more efficient and flexible strain
engineering; and (3) engineering strains capable of producing hexanol and related molecules at
high yield and titer from lignocellulosic sugars. Although not all objectives were fully realized,
the project achieved several key advances. We developed upp and pyrF as counter-selectable
markers and designed modular gene deletion plasmids containing homology arms, a
chloramphenicol resistance marker, and flanking site-specific recombination sites. This system
enabled construction of multiple gene deletion mutants, including deletions of genes implicated
in organic acid production. Deletion of Mels_0742, annotated as a propionyl-CoA transferase,
eliminated propionate production, reduced valerate levels, and redirected metabolism toward
increased butyrate and hexanoate production. Additionally, expression of a heterologous adhE2
gene from Clostridium acetobutylicum enabled butanol production, demonstrating proof-of-
concept for redirecting native metabolism toward alcohol biosynthesis. Collectively, these results
establish a functional genetic system and demonstrate initial success in metabolic pathway
rewiring—laying the groundwork for future development of M. elsdenii as a platform for
medium-chain alcohol production.

Summary
To expand the genetic toolbox for Megasphaera elsdenii and enable construction of gene
deletions for further metabolic studies, we developed a counter-selection strategy using the upp
gene. This gene encodes uracil phosphoribosyltransferase, an enzyme that converts the uracil
analog 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) into a toxic compound. A plasmid (pLAR151) containing the cat
gene (chloramphenicol acetyltransferase) and ~1000 base pairs of homology upstream and
downstream of upp was cloned in Escherichia coli. pLAR151 was then transformed into wild-
type M. elsdenii ATCC 25940. The outgrowth was plated on Reinforced Clostridial Medium
(RCM) containing thiamphenicol (5 µg/mL) to select for plasmid uptake. Colonies were then
transferred to RCM with 5-FU for counter-selection. Deletion of upp in the M. elsdenii
chromosome generated strain AG5855, which was resistant to >300 µg/mL 5-FU. This strain
enabled counter-selection of plasmids containing wild-type alleles.

Using this upp counter-selection system in combination with thiamphenicol selection, we
constructed several gene deletion mutants in M. elsdenii. Our first target was MELS_0742,
annotated as a propionate-CoA transferase, which was identified as the most highly expressed
among four such enzymes based on RNA-seq and proteomic analysis (on lactate). To investigate
its role in organic acid metabolism, we deleted MELS_0742 in the AG5855 (Δupp) background
to generate strain JWME04. The deletion plasmid contained the cat gene flanked by upstream
and downstream homology regions and ΦC31 attachment sites, along with a wild-type copy of
upp to enable counter-selection. The ΦC31 sites allow future marker removal by transient
expression of ΦC31 integrase. Growth comparisons on glucose and lactate revealed that the
ΔMELS_0742 mutant grew similarly to wild-type on glucose but showed impaired growth on
lactate. HPLC analysis revealed complete loss of propionate and significant reduction in valerate,
along with increased production of butyric and hexanoic acids. These results suggest that
MELS_0742 catalyzes the conversion of propionyl-CoA to propionate and that its deletion
redirects carbon flux toward longer-chain organic acids.

Using a similar deletion strategy, we also constructed mutants lacking MELS_0464 and
MELS_0743, which are thought to participate in the acrylate pathway. Deletion of MELS_0464,
another putative propionate-CoA transferase, had little effect on growth or fermentation,
indicating possible functional redundancy among propionate-CoA transferases. Deletion of
MELS_0743, annotated as a glyoxalase family protein, resulted in a phenotype nearly identical to
ΔMELS_0742, including complete loss of propionate and increased production of butyric and
hexanoic acids. However, further analysis revealed that the stop codon of MELS_0742 had been
inadvertently disrupted during the construction of the MELS_0743 mutant, likely explaining the
observed phenotype. The plasmid has since been corrected, and efforts are underway to
reconstruct a proper ΔMELS_0743 mutant. Additional deletion mutants of MELS_0746,
MELS_0437, and MELS_1631—annotated as lactoyl-CoA dehydratase subunit β, 4-
hydroxybutyrate CoA-transferase, and propionate CoA-transferase, respectively—have also been
constructed and are awaiting phenotypic characterization.

In addition to upp, we developed a second counter-selectable system by deleting pyrF, which is
required for uracil biosynthesis and converts 5-fluoroorotic acid (5-FOA) into a toxic compound.
We confirmed that ΔpyrF strains cannot grow in defined medium lacking uracil. Using this
system, we generated restriction enzyme deletion strains JWME05 (ΔMELS_1617) and JWME06

(ΔMELS_0052) in the JWME03 (ΔpyrF) background. We are currently assessing transformation
efficiency of these strains using plasmids containing pyrF to test complementation. These
experiments aim to determine whether deletion of restriction endonuclease genes improves
transformation efficiency by allowing uptake of unmethylated DNA and bypassing host
methylation constraints.

Finally, we demonstrated the feasibility of using M. elsdenii as a metabolic engineering platform
by producing butanol. Heterologous expression of adhE2 from Clostridium acetobutylicum in
wild-type ATCC 25940 resulted in butanol production at 5.3 mM, representing 13% of the
carbon detected in fermentation products. This result provides proof-of-concept that native
metabolism in M. elsdenii can be redirected toward alcohol production through targeted genetic
modification.
