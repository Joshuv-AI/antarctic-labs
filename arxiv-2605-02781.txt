Uniqueness of almost periodic outer flows on the hyperfinite type II 1 {\rm II}_{1} factor Thanks: CH is supported by ERC Advanced Grant NET 101141693
Cyril Houdayer, Amine Marrakchi

Abstract
. We show that any almost periodic outer flow α : ℝ ↷ R \alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R on the hyperfinite type II 1 {\rm II}_{1} factor with Connes’​ spectrum Γ ⁡ ( α ) = ℝ \Gamma(\alpha)=\mathbb{R} satisfies the Rokhlin property and thus is unique up to cocycle conjugacy. The proof relies on a key cocycle perturbation result for type III {\rm III} amenable equivalence relations. As a byproduct of our methods, we also show that every almost periodic factor of type III 1 {\rm III}_{1} with separable predual has an extremal almost periodic faithful normal state.

1.
Introduction and statement of the main results

1.1.
Uniqueness of almost periodic outer flows on
R

By the fundamental work of Connes
[
Co75b
]
, there is a unique amenable type
{\rm II}_{1}
factor with separable predual: it is isomorphic to the unique hyperfinite type
{\rm II}_{1}
factor
R
of Murray–von Neumann
[
MvN43
]
. Moreover, Connes
[
Co75a
]
showed that there is a unique automorphism
\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(R)
up to outer conjugacy when the corresponding action
\mathbb{Z}\curvearrowright R
is outer, i.e.​
\theta^{n}\notin\operatorname{Inn}(R)
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.

A next natural step is to classify continuous flows
\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
on the hyperfinite type
{\rm II}_{1}
factor. This problem, which has been pushed forth by Takesaki since the early 1980s, is still open. In order to state the classification problem for flows
\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
on the hyperfinite type
{\rm II}_{1}
factor, we follow Kawahigashi’s beautiful exposition on Popa’s W
∗
-news blog
[
Po25
]
.

A natural replacement of the outer conjugacy for a single automorphism is the cocycle conjugacy for a flow. However, it is not straightforward to define the right notion of “outerness” for a flow. We should keep in mind that there is a certain similarity between the classification of flows on the hyperfinite type
{\rm II}_{1}
factor up to cocycle conjugacy and the classification of amenable type
{\rm III}
factors and their modular automorphism group. In that respect, an important invariant for the cocycle conjugacy class of a flow
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
is Connes’​ spectrum
\Gamma(\alpha)
, which is a closed subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
. The condition
\Gamma(\alpha)=\mathbb{R}
is equivalent to the factoriality of the crossed product von Neumann algebra
R\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}
. However, unlike the analogous situation of the modular automorphism group
\sigma^{\varphi}
of a type
{\rm III}_{1}
factor
M
, the condition
\Gamma(\alpha)=\mathbb{R}
does not necessarily imply that
\alpha
is
outer
, i.e.​ that
\alpha_{t}\notin\operatorname{Inn}(R)
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. A stronger assumption that one can make on
\alpha
is
strict outerness
which means that
R^{\prime}\cap(R\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{C}1
. If
\alpha
is strictly outer, then
\Gamma(\alpha)=\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha
is outer. Note that for a type
{\rm III}_{1}
factor
M
, the modular automorphism group
\sigma^{\varphi}
is always strictly outer by Connes–Takesaki relative commutant theorem
[
CT76
]
.

As in the classification of amenable type
\rm III_{\lambda}
factors with
\lambda\in[0,1)

[
Co72
,
Co75b
]
, Kawahigashi
[
Ka87
]
obtained a complete classification of flows
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
up to stable conjugacy in the case when
\Gamma(\alpha)\neq\mathbb{R}
. Similar to the uniqueness of the amenable type
{\rm III_{1}}
factor
[
Co85
,
Ha85
]
, it is natural to expect that there is a unique flow
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
up to cocycle conjugacy in the case when
\Gamma(\alpha)=\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha
is outer. Kawahigashi
[
Ka88a
]
settled this question affirmatively under the extra assumption that
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
fixes pointwise a Cartan subalgebra
A\subset R
.

More recently, Masuda–Tomatsu
[
MT12
]
developed a conceptual approach to classifying flows on von Neumann algebras up to cocycle conjugacy. Notably, they showed that a flow
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
on the hyperfinite type
{\rm II}_{1}
factor which satisfies the Rokhlin property is unique up to cocycle conjugacy. This enabled them to obtain alternative proofs of Kawahigashi’s classification results. Following
[
Ki95
,
Ka00
,
MT12
]
, we say that the flow
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
satisfies the
Rokhlin property
if for every
p\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a unitary
u_{p}\in\mathcal{U}(R_{\alpha,\omega})
in the
(\alpha,\omega)
-equicontinuous part of the central sequence algebra such that
\alpha^{\omega}_{t}(u_{p})=\exp({\rm i}tp)u_{p}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

To state the conjecture explicitly, we let
\sigma^{\infty}=\mathbin{\overline{\bigotimes}}_{\mathbb{N}}\sigma
be the infinite tensor product action on
R=\mathbin{\overline{\bigotimes}}_{\mathbb{N}}\mathord{\text{\rm M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
where
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright\mathord{\text{\rm M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is any faithful flow on a finite dimensional factor, for example

\sigma:t\mapsto\operatorname{Ad}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&\exp({\rm i}t\lambda)&0\\
0&0&\exp({\rm i}t\mu)\end{array}\right)\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathord{\text{\rm M}}_{3}(\mathbb{C}))

with
\lambda,\mu>0
and
\lambda/\mu\notin\mathbb{Q}
. The following conjecture mentioned by Kawahigashi in
[
Po25
]
describes the main open problem on the classification of continuous flows on the hyperfinite type
{\rm II}_{1}
factor.

Conjecture
.

Let
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
be a flow on the hyperfinite type
{\rm II}_{1}
factor. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

\alpha
is cocycle conjugate to
\sigma^{\infty}
.

(2)

\alpha
satisfies the Rokhlin property.

(3)

\alpha
is strictly outer, i.e.
R^{\prime}\cap(R\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{C}1
.

(4)

\Gamma(\alpha)=\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha
is outer.

As we explained above, for any flow
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
, we have
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
by
[
MT12
]
and moreover we have
(2)\Rightarrow(3)\Rightarrow(4)
.

In this paper, we settle the above conjecture for the class of
almost periodic
flows
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
. A flow
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
is almost periodic if the closure
K=\overline{\left\{\alpha_{t}\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}}<\operatorname{Aut}(R)
is a compact subgroup. The class of almost periodic flows
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
is quite large and contains all flows arising from higher dimensional simple noncommutative tori
[
Ki95
,
Ka88b
]
.

Theorem A
.

Let
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
be an almost periodic flow such that
\Gamma(\alpha)=\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha
is outer. Then
\alpha
has the Rokhlin property, hence it is cocycle conjugate to
\sigma^{\infty}
.

We emphasize the fact that Theorem
A
holds for
arbitrary
almost periodic flows
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
with no extra assumptions on the fixed-point subalgebra
R^{\alpha}\subset R
. Prior to our work, the uniqueness of almost periodic flows on the hyperfinite type
{\rm II}_{1}
factor up to cocycle conjugacy was only known in some very specific cases such as minimal almost periodic flows (i.e.​
(R^{\alpha})^{\prime}\cap R=\mathbb{C}1
)
[
MT12
, Theorem 6.12]
. In
[
Ka88b
]
, an interesting example of an ergodic almost periodic flow (i.e.​
R^{\alpha}=\mathbb{C}1
) is also treated.

We also point out that Theorem
A
holds more generally for almost periodic actions of arbitrary second countable locally compact abelian groups on the hyperfinite
{\rm II}_{1}
factor (see Theorem
7.1
below).

The proof of Theorem
A
divides into two independent steps. First, we prove the theorem in the case where the flow
\alpha
is
prime
, i.e.​ when the fixed point algebra
R^{\alpha}
is a factor. We exploit the structure of prime almost periodic flows on the hyperfinite
{\rm II}_{1}
factor
[
Jo82
,
OPT79
]
to show by hand that
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright R
has the Rokhlin property. Our argument is inspired by
[
MT12
, Theorem 6.12]
and
[
Ki95
, Proposition 2.5]
.

The second step, which is the key novelty of our paper, is a reduction from the general case to the prime case by a cocycle perturbation argument. In fact, we prove that every almost periodic flow
\alpha
such that
\Gamma(\alpha)=\mathbb{R}
is cocycle conjugate to an almost periodic flow that is prime.

Theorem B
.

Let
M
be a factor with separable predual and
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright M
an almost periodic flow such that
\Gamma(\alpha)=\mathbb{R}
. Then
\alpha
is cocycle conjugate to an almost periodic flow
\beta:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright M
such that
M^{\alpha}\subset M^{\beta}
and
M^{\beta}
is a factor.

More precisely, we have
\beta_{t}=\operatorname{Ad}(v_{t})\circ\alpha_{t}
for some continuous homomorphism
v:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{Z}(M^{\alpha}))
.

Quite unexpectedly, in order to prove Theorem
B
, one has to deal with type
{\rm III}
equivalence relations, even if one is only interested in the case where
M
is a
{\rm II}_{1}
factor. Indeed, the almost periodicity of the flow
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright M
implies that
M^{\alpha}
is stably normalized by the eigenvectors of
\alpha
and this induces an amenable equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
on the center
\mathcal{Z}(M^{\alpha})
. The morphism
v:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{Z}(M^{\alpha}))
of Theorem
B
, is obtained by studying the cohomology of
\mathcal{R}
and it turns out that this equivalence relation can be of arbitrary type (possibly type
{\rm III}
) even when
M
is a
{\rm II}_{1}
factor. This phenomenon was already observed in
[
BHV15
]
.

1.2.
Cohomology of amenable ergodic equivalence relations

The proof of Theorem
B
relies on a key result regarding the cohomology of amenable ergodic nonsingular equivalence relations, which is of independent interest. In order to state this result, we introduce some further terminology.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be an ergodic nonsingular equivalence relation defined on a diffuse standard probability space
(X,\nu)
. Let
G
be a second countable locally compact group with a left invariant Haar measure
m_{G}
and
c:\mathcal{R}\to G
a measurable cocycle. Following
[
GS91
]
, we say that the cocycle
c
is
ergodic
if for every nonnull measurable subset
Y\subset X
, the essential image of
c|_{\mathcal{R}_{Y}}:\mathcal{R}_{Y}\to G
is equal to
G
. Here,
\mathcal{R}_{Y}=\mathcal{R}\cap(Y\times Y)
denotes the restricted ergodic nonsingular equivalence relation on the diffuse standard probability space
(Y,\nu_{Y})
, where
\nu_{Y}=\frac{1}{\nu(Y)}\nu|_{Y}
. Clearly, if
\ker(c)<\mathcal{R}
is an ergodic subequivalence relation and the essential image of
c
is equal to
G
, then
c
is ergodic, but the converse is not true (except when
G
is discrete).

In
[
GS91
]
, Golodets–Sinelshchikov proved on the one hand, that if a cocycle
c:\mathcal{R}\to G
is ergodic, then it is always
cohomologous
to a measurable cocycle
d:\mathcal{R}\to G
for which
\ker(d)<\mathcal{R}
is ergodic. On the other hand, they proved that if
\mathcal{R}
is amenable (or hyperfinite by
[
CFW81
]
), then for any countable dense subgroup
\Lambda<G
, the cocycle
c
is cohomologous to a measurable cocycle
d:\mathcal{R}\to G
with values in
\Lambda
.

Now, assume at the same time that
\mathcal{R}
is amenable and that
c:\mathcal{R}\to G
is ergodic (under these conditions,
G
must be amenable). Can we then find a measurable cocycle
d:\mathcal{R}\to G
cohomologous to
c
such that at the same time
\ker(d)<\mathcal{R}
is ergodic and
d
takes values in a dense countable subgroup? If such a cocycle
d
exists, then it must take values in an
amenable
dense countable subgroup of
G
. Our key result shows that this necessary condition is actually sufficient.

Theorem C
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be an ergodic equivalence relation and
c:\mathcal{R}\to G
a measurable cocycle with values in a second countable locally compact group
G
. Suppose that
\mathcal{R}
is amenable, that
c
is ergodic and that
G
contains a dense countable amenable subgroup
\Lambda<G
.

Then
c
is cohomologous to a measurable cocycle
d:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
with values in
\Lambda
and such that the subequivalence relation
\ker(d)<\mathcal{R}
is ergodic.

The proof of Theorem
C
relies on Golodets–Sinelshchikov’s classification theorem for measurable cocycles on amenable ergodic nonsingular equivalence relations (see
[
GS91
, Theorem 3.1]
) and a novel construction of model actions using the notion of adjoint flows of
[
VV22
]
.

1.3.
Extremal almost periodic weights

Let
M
be a factor with separable predual and denote by
\mathcal{P}(M)
the set of all faithful normal semifinite weights on
M
. Following
[
Co72
,
Co74
]
, we say that
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}(M)
is
almost periodic
if the modular automorphism group
\sigma^{\varphi}:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright M
is almost periodic. Almost periodic weights are of the utmost importance in the classification theory of type
{\rm III}
factors. Indeed, Connes
[
Co74
]
showed that when
M
is a type
{\rm III}
factor, to any almost periodic weight
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}(M)
, one can associate a canonical discrete decomposition of the form
M=N\rtimes_{\theta}\Gamma
, where
N
is a type
{\rm II_{\infty}}
von Neumann algebra and
\theta:\Gamma\curvearrowright N
is a trace-scaling action for some countable subgroup
\Gamma<\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
. Using the above discrete decomposition, the classification problem for type
{\rm III}
factors reduces to the classification of type
{\rm II_{\infty}}
von Neumann algebras and their outer automorphisms. In
[
Co72
,
Co75a
,
Co75b
]
, Connes applied this approach with great success to the structure and the classification of factors of type
{\rm III_{\lambda}}
with
\lambda\in[0,1)
.

Let
M
be a type
{\rm III}
factor with separable predual and
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}(M)
an almost periodic weight. We then simply say that
M
is an almost periodic type
{\rm III}
factor. We say that
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}(M)
is an
extremal
almost periodic weight if the centralizer von Neumann algebra
M_{\varphi}
is a factor. Any extremal almost periodic weight
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}(M)
gives rise to a
factorial
discrete decomposition
M=N\rtimes_{\theta}\Gamma
, where
N
is a type
{\rm II_{\infty}}
factor. While every type
{\rm III}_{\lambda}
factor for
\lambda\in(0,1)
has an extremal periodic weight, namely the
\frac{2\pi}{|\log(\lambda)|}
-periodic weight, it is known that a type
{\rm III}_{0}
factor has no extremal almost periodic weight
[
Co72
]
. In
[
Co74
]
, Connes proved that any
full
almost periodic factor of type
{\rm III}_{1}
with separable predual has an extremal almost periodic weight. In his argument, the fullness assumption is essential. As a byproduct of Theorem
B
, we are able to remove the fullness assumption.

Theorem D
.

Let
M
be an almost periodic factor of type
{\rm III}_{1}
with separable predual. Then
M
has an extremal almost periodic weight
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}(M)
. In particular,
M
has a factorial discrete decomposition.

We use Theorem
D
and its more precise version (see Theorem
8.2
) to obtain a new characterization of extremal almost periodic weights (see Corollary
8.3
below).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Cocycle conjugacy

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra with separable predual,
G
a second countable locally compact group and
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
a continuous action. We denote by
M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
the crossed product von Neumann algebra generated by
M
and a copy of the left regular representation
\lambda_{\alpha}:G\to\mathcal{U}(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G):g\mapsto\lambda_{\alpha}(g)
in such a way that

\forall g\in G,\forall x\in M,\quad\alpha_{g}(x)=\lambda_{\alpha}(g)x\lambda_{\alpha}(g)^{*}.

A strongly continuous map
u:G\to\mathcal{U}(M):g\mapsto u_{g}
is said to be a
1
-
cocycle
for
\alpha
if
u_{gh}=u_{g}\alpha_{g}(u_{h})
for all
g,h\in G
. We denote by
\mathord{\text{\rm Z}}^{1}(\alpha,G,M)
the space of all
1
-cocycles for
\alpha
. If
u\in\mathord{\text{\rm Z}}^{1}(\alpha,G,M)
, then we may define a new continuous action
\alpha^{u}:G\curvearrowright M
by the formula
\alpha_{g}^{u}=\operatorname{Ad}(u_{g})\circ\alpha_{g}
for every
g\in G
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta:G\curvearrowright M
be continuous actions. We say that
\alpha
and
\beta
are
cocycle conjugate
if there exist an automorphism
\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(M)
and a
1
-cocycle
u\in\mathord{\text{\rm Z}}^{1}(\alpha,G,M)
such that

\forall g\in G,\quad\alpha^{u}_{g}=\theta\circ\beta_{g}\circ\theta^{-1}.

If we can take
\theta=\text{\rm id}_{M}
, we say that
\alpha
and
\beta
are
cocycle equivalent
.

If
\alpha
and
\beta
are cocycle conjugate with
\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(M)
and
u\in\mathord{\text{\rm Z}}^{1}(\alpha,G,M)
as in Definition
2.1
, then the mapping

\pi_{\alpha,\beta}:M\rtimes_{\beta}G\to M\rtimes_{\alpha}G:x\lambda_{\beta}(g)\mapsto\theta(x)u_{g}\lambda_{\alpha}(g)

extends to a well-defined isomorphism such that
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}(M)=M
. In other words, the inclusions
M\subset M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
and
M\subset M\rtimes_{\beta}G
are isomorphic.

2.2.
The Rokhlin property

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra with separable predual. We denote by
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},M)
the unital
\mathord{\text{\rm C}}^{*}
-algebra of all norm bounded sequences in
M
. Fix a nonprincipal ultrafilter
\omega\in\beta(\mathbb{N})\setminus\mathbb{N}
. Define the
\mathord{\text{\rm C}}^{*}
-subalgebras
\mathcal{I}_{\omega},\mathcal{M}^{\omega}\subset\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},M)
by the formulae

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\left\{x=(x_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},M)\mid x_{n}\to 0\text{ $\ast$-strongly as }n\to\omega\right\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\omega}

\displaystyle=\left\{x=(x_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},M)\mid x\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\subset\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\text{ and }\mathcal{I}_{\omega}x\subset\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\right\}.

The quotient
\mathord{\text{\rm C}}^{*}
-algebra
M^{\omega}=\mathcal{M}^{\omega}/\mathcal{I}_{\omega}
is a von Neumann algebra, and we simply call it the
ultraproduct von Neumann algebra

[
Oc85
]
. For any faithful normal state
\varphi\in M_{*}
, the assignment
\mathcal{M}^{\omega}\to\mathbb{C}:(x_{n})_{n}\mapsto\lim_{n\to\omega}\varphi(x_{n})
induces a faithful normal state on
M^{\omega}
, which we write
\varphi^{\omega}
.
There is a natural normal embedding
M\subset M^{\omega}
with faithful normal expectation
\mathord{\text{\rm E}}_{M}:M^{\omega}\to M
which satisfies
\varphi^{\omega}=\varphi\circ\mathord{\text{\rm E}}_{M}
. For any
(x_{n})_{n}\in\mathcal{M}^{\omega}
, we denote by
(x_{n})^{\omega}
its image in
M^{\omega}
. We also consider the
asymptotic centralizer

M_{\omega}=(M^{\prime}\cap M^{\omega})_{\varphi^{\omega}}
, which is the centralizer with respect to the ultraproduct state
\varphi^{\omega}
of the central sequence algebra
M^{\prime}\cap M^{\omega}

[
Co74
,
AH12
]
. We observe that the asymptotic centralizer
M_{\omega}
does not depend on the choice of the faithful normal state
\varphi\in M_{\ast}
.

Let
G
be a second countable locally compact group and
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
a continuous action. The ultraproduct action
\alpha^{\omega}:G\curvearrowright M^{\omega}
is quite discontinuous in general. Following
[
MT12
, Section 3]
, we introduce the von Neumann subalgebra
M^{\omega}_{\alpha}\subset M^{\omega}
of all
(\alpha,\omega)
-equicontinuous elements on which the restriction of the ultraproduct action
\alpha^{\omega}:G\curvearrowright M^{\omega}
becomes continuous. For this, let us fix a faithful normal state
\varphi\in M_{\ast}
. A sequence
(x_{n})_{n}\in\mathcal{M}^{\omega}
is said to be
(\alpha,\omega)
-
equicontinuous
if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an open neighborhood
U\subset G
of the neutral element
e\in G
such that

\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\mid\forall g\in U,\|\alpha_{g}(x_{n})-x_{n}\|_{\varphi}^{\sharp}<\varepsilon\right\}\in\omega.

Denote by
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}^{\omega}\subset\mathcal{M}^{\omega}
the unital
\mathord{\text{\rm C}}^{*}
-subalgebra of all
(\alpha,\omega)
-equicontinuous elements. We observe that
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}^{\omega}
does not depend on the choice of the faithful normal state
\varphi\in M_{\ast}
. Then we have
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}\subset\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}^{\omega}\subset\mathcal{M}^{\omega}
and the quotient
\mathord{\text{\rm C}}^{*}
-algebra
M_{\alpha}^{\omega}=\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}^{\omega}/\mathcal{I}_{\omega}
is a von Neumann subalgebra of
M^{\omega}
. Moreover
M_{\alpha}^{\omega}\subset M^{\omega}
is globally invariant under the ultraproduct action
\alpha^{\omega}
and the restriction
\alpha^{\omega}:G\curvearrowright M_{\alpha}^{\omega}
defines a continuous action. We also consider the
(\alpha,\omega)
-equicontinuous part
M_{\alpha,\omega}=M_{\alpha}^{\omega}\cap M_{\omega}
of the asymptotic centralizer and the corresponding continuous action
\alpha^{\omega}:G\curvearrowright M_{\alpha,\omega}
.

Following
[
MT12
, Section 4]
, we introduce the Rokhlin property for continuous actions of arbitrary abelian second countable locally compact groups on von Neumann algebras.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be an abelian second countable locally compact group and
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
a continuous action. We say
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
satisfies the
Rokhlin property
if for every
p\in\widehat{G}
, there exists a unitary
u_{p}\in\mathcal{U}(M_{\alpha,\omega})
such that
\alpha^{\omega}_{g}(u_{p})=\langle g,p\rangle u_{p}
for every
g\in G
.

We observe that the Rokhlin property in Definition
2.2
does not depend on the choice on the nonprincipal ultrafilter
\omega\in\beta(\mathbb{N})\setminus\mathbb{N}
.

3.
The Maharam extension and the adjoint flows

3.1.
The Maharam extension

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra. Then, up to a unique isomorphism, there exists a unique triple
(\widetilde{M},\tau,\theta)
where
\widetilde{M}
is a von Neumann algebra containing
M
,
\tau
is a faithful semifinite trace on
\widetilde{M}
and
\theta:\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright\widetilde{M}
is an action that scales the trace
\tau
, that is
\tau\circ\theta_{\lambda}=\lambda\tau
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
. We call
(\widetilde{M},\tau,\theta)
the
noncommutative flow of weights
of
M
(see
[
Ta03
, Chapter
{\rm XII}
]
).

Suppose that
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
is a continuous action of a locally compact group
G
. Then
\alpha
admits a unique extension
\tilde{\alpha}:G\curvearrowright\widetilde{M}
that preserves the trace
\tau
and commutes with the scaling action
\theta
. We call
\tilde{\alpha}
the
Maharam extension
of
\alpha
.

Example 3.1
.

Suppose that
\alpha:G\curvearrowright(X,\nu)
is a nonsingular action on a
\sigma
-finite measure space. We can view
\alpha
as an action on an abelian von Neumann algebra
M
. The explicit formula for its Maharam extension is given as follows. Letting
M=\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(X,\nu)
, we have
\widetilde{M}=\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(X\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+},\nu\otimes m)
with trace
\tau=\nu\otimes m
where
m
is the measure on
\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
defined by
\text{\rm d}m(\lambda)=\lambda^{-2}\text{\rm d}\lambda
, and the trace scaling action
\theta
is given by
\theta_{\lambda}(x,s)=(x,\lambda^{-1}s)
. The Maharam extension
\widetilde{\alpha}:G\curvearrowright(X\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+},\nu\otimes m)
is given by

\widetilde{\alpha}_{g}(x,s)=(\alpha_{g}(x),\omega(g,x)s),\quad g\in G,\;(x,s)\in X\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}

where
\omega(g,x)=\frac{\text{\rm d}\nu(\alpha_{g}(x))}{\text{\rm d}\nu(x)}
is the
Radon–Nikodym cocycle
.

3.2.
Adjoint flows

Let
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright M
be a continuous flow where
M
is a von Neumann algebra. Let
(\widetilde{M},\tau,\theta)
be the noncommutative flow of weights of
M
where
\theta:\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright\widetilde{M}
scales the trace
\tau
. Let
\widetilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright\widetilde{M}
be the natural extension of
\alpha
to
\widetilde{M}
. Recall that
\widetilde{\alpha}
commutes with
\theta
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

M^{(t,\alpha)}=\left\{x\in\widetilde{M}\mid\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+},\;\widetilde{\alpha}_{\lambda^{t}}(x)=\theta_{\lambda}(x)\right\}.

We denote by
\alpha^{(t)}:\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright M^{(t,\alpha)}
the restriction of
\widetilde{\alpha}
to
M^{(t,\alpha)}
. Observe that
M^{(0,\alpha)}=M
and
\alpha^{(0)}=\alpha
.

Take
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
. We claim that for any flow
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright M
, the flow
(\alpha^{(t)})^{(s)}
is naturally isomorphic to
\alpha^{(t+s)}
.

Indeed, let
N=M^{(t,\alpha)}
and
\beta=\alpha^{(t)}
. Then, by definition,
N
is the fixed point algebra of
\widetilde{M}
under the trace scaling action
\theta^{\prime}:\lambda\mapsto\widetilde{\alpha}_{\lambda^{-t}}\circ\theta_{\lambda}
. Thus
(\widetilde{M},\tau,\theta^{\prime})
is naturally identified with the noncommutative flow of weights of
N
. Since
\widetilde{\alpha}|_{N}=\beta
and
\widetilde{\alpha}
preserves
\tau
and commutes with
\theta^{\prime}
, we then see that the natural extension
\widetilde{\beta}
of
\beta
to the noncommutative flow of weights of
N
is identified with
\widetilde{\alpha}
. Therefore,
N^{(s,\beta)}
is the fixed point algebra of
\widetilde{M}
under the action

\lambda\mapsto\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda^{-s}}\circ\theta^{\prime}_{\lambda}=\widetilde{\alpha}_{\lambda^{-s}}\circ\theta^{\prime}_{\lambda}=\widetilde{\alpha}_{\lambda^{-s}}\circ\widetilde{\alpha}_{\lambda^{-t}}\circ\theta_{\lambda}=\widetilde{\alpha}_{\lambda^{-(t+s)}}\circ\theta_{\lambda}.

This shows that
N^{(s,\beta)}=M^{(t+s,\alpha)}
and
\beta^{(s)}
is the restriction of
\widetilde{\beta}=\widetilde{\alpha}
to
N^{(s,\beta)}=M^{(t+s,\alpha)}
, which is precisely
\alpha^{(t+s)}
.

Example 3.2
.

Suppose that
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright(X,\nu)
is a nonsingular flow on a
\sigma
-finite measure space. We can view
\alpha
as an action on an abelian von Neumann algebra and construct a new nonsingular flow
\alpha^{(t)}
. The explicit formula for this flow is given as follows. Letting
M=\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(X,\nu)
, we have
\widetilde{M}=\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(X\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+},\nu\otimes m)
with trace
\tau=\nu\otimes m
where
m
is the measure on
\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
defined by
\text{\rm d}m(\lambda)=\lambda^{-2}\text{\rm d}\lambda
, and the trace scaling action
\theta
is given by
\theta_{\lambda}(x,s)=(x,\lambda^{-1}s)
. The Maharam extension
\widetilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright(X\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+},\nu\otimes m)
is given by

\widetilde{\alpha}_{\lambda}(x,s)=(\alpha_{\lambda}(x),\omega(\lambda,x)s)

where
\omega(\lambda,x)=\frac{\text{\rm d}\nu(\alpha_{\lambda}(x))}{\text{\rm d}\nu(x)}
is the Radon–Nikodym cocycle. Then for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the algebra
M^{(t,\alpha)}
consists of all functions
F\in\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(X\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+})
such that

F(\alpha_{\lambda^{t}}(x),\lambda\omega(\lambda^{t},x)s)=F(x,s)

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
and all
(x,s)\in X\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright M
be a flow. We denote by
\alpha^{\mathord{\text{\rm op}}}
the flow given by
(\alpha^{\mathord{\text{\rm op}}})_{\lambda}=\alpha_{\lambda^{-1}}
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
. Then it is easy to see that
M^{(t,\alpha^{\mathord{\text{\rm op}}})}=M^{(-t,\alpha)}
and
(\alpha^{\mathord{\text{\rm op}}})^{(t)}=(\alpha^{(-t)})^{\mathord{\text{\rm op}}}
. Define
\alpha^{\dagger}=(\alpha^{(1)})^{\mathord{\text{\rm op}}}=(\alpha^{\mathord{\text{\rm op}}})^{(-1)}
. Then it follows from the foregoing that
(\alpha^{\dagger})^{\dagger}
is naturally isomorphic to
\alpha
. The flow
\alpha^{\dagger}
coincides with the
adjoint flow
of
\alpha
defined in
[
VV22
]
in the case where
M
is an abelian von Neumann algebra.

When
\alpha
is trace preserving, it is easy to see that
\alpha^{(t)}\cong\alpha
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, we have
\alpha^{\dagger}\cong\alpha^{\mathord{\text{\rm op}}}
. Note that in
[
VV22
, Section 3]
, it is incorrectly stated that if
\alpha
is trace preserving, then
\alpha^{\dagger}\cong\alpha
.

The construction
(M,\alpha)\mapsto(M^{(t,\alpha)},\alpha^{(t)})
is functorial with respect to
\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
-equivariant isomorphisms. More precisely, if
\beta:\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright N
is another flow and
\pi:M\rightarrow N
is an isomorphism of von Neumann algebras that conjugates
\alpha
with
\beta
, then
\pi
extends to an isomorphism
\widetilde{\pi}:\widetilde{M}\rightarrow\widetilde{N}
that conjugates
\widetilde{\alpha}
with
\widetilde{\beta}
and
\theta_{M}
with
\theta_{N}
. In particular,
\widetilde{\pi}
restricts to an isomorphism from
M^{(t,\alpha)}
onto
N^{(t,\beta)}
that conjugates
\alpha^{(t)}
with
\beta^{(t)}
.

Set
\operatorname{Aut}(M,\alpha)=\left\{\pi\in\operatorname{Aut}(M)\mid\pi\text{ commutes with }\alpha\right\}
. By taking
M=N
, we see that the map

\operatorname{Aut}(M,\alpha)\to\operatorname{Aut}(M^{(t,\alpha)},\alpha^{(t)}):\pi\mapsto\pi^{(t)}

induces a continuous homomorphism.
In particular, if
G
is a locally compact group and
\rho:G\curvearrowright M
is a continuous action that commutes with a flow
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright M
, then we obtain a continuous action
\rho^{(t)}:G\curvearrowright M^{(t,\alpha)}
that commutes with
\alpha^{(t)}
. Again, we have that the action
(\rho^{(t)})^{(s)}
is naturally isomorphic to
\rho^{(t+s)}
for every
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
.

4.
Induced equivalence relations

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a nonsingular equivalence relation on a measure space
(X,\nu)
and let
c:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
be a cocycle with values in a second countable locally compact group
G
. Let
\rho:G\curvearrowright(Y,\eta)
be a nonsingular action. The
induced equivalence relation

\mathcal{R}_{\rho,c}
is the nonsingular equivalence relation on
(X\times Y,\nu\otimes\eta)
defined by

(x,y)\sim_{\mathcal{R}_{\rho,c}}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\Longleftrightarrow x\sim_{\mathcal{R}}x^{\prime}\text{ and }y^{\prime}=\rho_{c(x^{\prime},x)}(y).

When
\rho:G\curvearrowright G
is the left translation action, the equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{\rho,c}
is simply denoted
\mathcal{R}_{c}
and it is called the
skew product equivalence relation
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a nonsingular equivalence relation on
(X,\nu)
with Radon–Nikodym cocycle
\delta:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
. Let
c:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
be a cocycle with values in the second countable locally compact group
G
. Let
\rho:G\curvearrowright(Y,\eta)
be a nonsingular action with Radon–Nikodym cocycle
\sigma:G\times Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
. Then the Radon–Nikodym cocycle
\omega:\mathcal{R}_{\rho,c}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
of
\mathcal{R}_{\rho,c}
with respect to
\nu\otimes\eta
is given by

\omega(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=\delta(x,x^{\prime})\sigma(c(x,x^{\prime}),y^{\prime})

for all
(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}_{\rho,c}
.

Proof.

The Radon–Nikodym cocycle
\delta
is characterized by

\int_{X}\sum_{x\sim_{\mathcal{R}}x^{\prime}}f(x,x^{\prime})\delta(x,x^{\prime})\>\text{\rm d}\nu(x^{\prime})=\int_{X}\sum_{x^{\prime}\sim_{\mathcal{R}}x}f(x,x^{\prime})\>\text{\rm d}\nu(x)

for every positive measurable function
f
on
X\times X
.

For every positive measurable function
f
on
X\times Y\times X\times Y
, we have

\int_{X\times Y}\sum_{(x,y)\sim_{\mathcal{R}_{\rho,c}}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})}f(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\omega(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\>\text{\rm d}\nu(x^{\prime})\text{\rm d}\eta(y^{\prime})=

\int_{X\times Y}\sum_{(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\sim_{\mathcal{R}_{\rho,c}}(x,y)}f(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\>\text{\rm d}\nu(x)\text{\rm d}\eta(y)=

\int_{X\times Y}\sum_{x^{\prime}\sim_{\mathcal{R}}x}f(x,y,x^{\prime},\rho_{c(x^{\prime},x)}(y))\>\text{\rm d}\nu(x)\text{\rm d}\eta(y)=

\int_{X\times Y}\sum_{x^{\prime}\sim_{\mathcal{R}}x}f(x,\rho_{c(x,x^{\prime})}(y^{\prime}),x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\sigma(c(x,x^{\prime}),y^{\prime})\>\text{\rm d}\nu(x)\text{\rm d}\eta(y^{\prime})=

\int_{X\times Y}\sum_{x\sim_{\mathcal{R}}x^{\prime}}f(x,\rho_{c(x,x^{\prime})}(y^{\prime}),x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\sigma(c(x,x^{\prime}),y^{\prime})\delta(x,x^{\prime})\>\text{\rm d}\nu(x^{\prime})\text{\rm d}\eta(y^{\prime})=

\int_{X\times Y}\sum_{(x,y)\sim_{\mathcal{R}_{\rho,c}}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})}f(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\sigma(c(x,x^{\prime}),y^{\prime})\delta(x,x^{\prime})\>\text{\rm d}\nu(x^{\prime})\text{\rm d}\eta(y^{\prime}).

This shows the desired equality.
∎

Let
\mathcal{R}
be an ergodic nonsingular equivalence relation on
(X,\nu)
. Let
c:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
be a cocycle with values in a second countable locally compact group
G
. The right translation action
\theta:G\curvearrowright X\times G
given by
\theta_{g}(x,h)=(x,hg^{-1})
preserves the skew product equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{c}
. Therefore,
\theta
induces a
G
-action on the space of ergodic components
(X\times G)/{\mathcal{R}_{c}}
. This
G
-action is called the
Mackey range
of
c
.

On the functional level, the Mackey range is simply the restriction of the right translation action
\theta:G\curvearrowright\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(X\times G)
to the algebra of
\mathcal{R}_{c}
-invariant functions
\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(X\times G)^{\mathcal{R}_{c}}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be an ergodic nonsingular equivalence relation on
(X,\nu)
. Let
c:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
be a cocycle with values in a second countable locally compact group
G
. The cocycle
c
is ergodic if and only if its Mackey range is trivial or equivalently if and only if
\mathcal{R}_{c}
is ergodic.

Example 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a nonsingular equivalence relation on
(X,\nu)
with its Radon–Nikodym cocycle
\delta:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
. Then we see from Lemma
4.1
that the equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{\delta}
preserves the measure
\nu\otimes m
on
X\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
, where
m
is the measure on
\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
defined by
\text{\rm d}m(\lambda)=\lambda^{-2}\text{\rm d}\lambda
. The equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{\delta}
is called the
Maharam extension
of
\mathcal{R}
.

The action
\theta:\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright X\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
given by
\theta_{\lambda}(x,s)=(x,s\lambda^{-1})
scales the measure
\nu\otimes m
in the sense that
(\nu\otimes m)(\theta_{\lambda}(A))=\lambda(\nu\otimes m)(A)
for all
A\subset X\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
and all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
.

The Mackey range of
\delta
is called the
Krieger flow
of
\mathcal{R}
. It is the
\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
-action induced by
\theta
on the ergodic components of the Maharam extension
\mathcal{R}_{\delta}
.

We say that an ergodic equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
is of type
{\rm III}_{1}
if its Radon–Nikodym cocycle
\delta
is ergodic or equivalently if its Maharam extension
\mathcal{R}_{\delta}
is ergodic, or in other words, if the Krieger flow of
\mathcal{R}
is trivial.

We end this section with the following useful ergodicity lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be an ergodic nonsingular equivalence relation on
(X,\nu)
. Let
c:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
be a cocycle with values in a second countable locally compact group
G
. Let
\rho:G\curvearrowright(Y,\eta)
be a nonsingular action. If
c
is ergodic, then
\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(X\times Y,\nu\otimes\eta)^{\mathcal{R}_{\rho,c}}=\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(Y)^{\rho}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:X\times G\times Y\rightarrow X\times Y
be the map defined by
\varphi(x,g,y)=(x,\rho_{g}(y))
. Take
f\in\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(X\times Y,\nu\otimes\eta)^{\mathcal{R}_{\rho,c}}
. Then

f\circ\varphi\in\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(X\times G\times Y,\nu\otimes m_{G}\otimes\eta)^{\mathcal{R}_{c}\times\Delta_{Y}}

where
\mathcal{R}_{c}
is the skew product equivalence relation on
X\times G
and
\Delta_{Y}
is the diagonal (or trivial) equivalence relation on
Y
. Since
c
is ergodic, then
\mathcal{R}_{c}
is ergodic by Proposition
4.2
, hence
f\circ\varphi
depends only on the third variable. We conclude that
f
depends only on the second variable and is
\rho
-invariant.
∎

5.
Cohomology of amenable ergodic equivalence relations

Our goal in this section is to prove Theorem
C
. Observe that Theorem
C
is somehow a combination of the following two lemmas.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
GS91
, Lemma 1.6]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be an ergodic nonsingular equivalence relation and let
c:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
be a cocycle with values in a second countable locally compact group
G
.

Assume that
c
is ergodic. Then
c
is cohomologous to a cocycle
d:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
such that the subequivalence relation
\ker(d)<\mathcal{R}
is ergodic.

Lemma 5.2

(
[
GS91
, Proposition 1.2]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be an ergodic nonsingular equivalence relation and let
c:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
be a cocycle with values in a second countable locally compact group
G
.

Assume that
\mathcal{R}
is amenable and let
\Lambda<G
be a dense countable subgroup. Then
c
is cohomologous to a cocycle
d:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
with values in
\Lambda
.

Unfortunately, we do not know how to prove Theorem
C
by combining the inductive constructions of Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
in a single construction. In fact, we observe that the assumption that
\Lambda
is amenable is essential in Theorem
C
while it is not in Lemma
5.2
.

Instead of a direct proof based on an inductive construction, we will prove Theorem
C
by using the full power of the classification of cocycles on amenable equivalence relations obtained in
[
GS91
]
. Let us recall this classification theorem.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a nonsingular equivalence relation on
(X,\nu)
,
G
a second countable locally compact group and
c,d:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
two measurable cocycles.
We say that
c
and
d
are
weakly equivalent
if there exists
\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{R})
such that
c
and
d\circ(\theta\times\theta)
are cohomologous.

Theorem 5.3

(
[
GS91
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be an ergodic amenable nonsingular equivalence relation on a standard probability space
(X,\nu)
. Let
\omega:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
be the Radon–Nikodym cocycle associated to
\nu
. Let
c,d:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
be two ergodic cocycles with values in a second countable locally compact group
G
.

Then
c
and
d
are weakly equivalent if and only if the Mackey ranges of the cocycles
c\times\omega,d\times\omega:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
are conjugate.

Thanks to this classification theorem, we can prove Theorem
C
by building model cocycles with prescribed Mackey range that satisfy the desired properties. More precisely we will prove the following.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
G
be a second countable locally compact group. Let
\rho:G\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright(Y,\eta)
be any ergodic nonsingular action. Then there exists an ergodic nonsingular equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
on a probability space
(X,\nu)
and a cocycle
c:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
such that the Mackey range of
c\times\omega:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
is isomorphic to
\rho
, where
\omega:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
is the Radon–Nikodym cocycle.

Moreover, the following holds :

(1)

If
G
is amenable, we can take
\mathcal{R}
to be amenable.

(2)

If
\rho|_{\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}}
is ergodic, we can take
c
such that
\ker(c)
is ergodic.

(3)

If
\Lambda<G
is a dense countable subgroup, we can take
c
with values in
\Lambda
.

Any two of these conditions can be satisfied simultaneously. If
\Lambda
is amenable, the three of them can be satisfied simultaneously.

We first observe that Theorem
5.4
holds in the case where
\rho:G\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
is the action given by
\rho_{(g,\lambda)}(x)=\lambda x
for all
(g,\lambda,x)\in G\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
. This means that
c
is ergodic and
\omega
is a coboundary (
\mathcal{R}
is measure preserving).

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
G
be a second countable locally compact group. Then there exists an ergodic probability measure preserving equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
with an ergodic cocycle
c:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
.

Moreover, the following holds :

(1)

If
G
is amenable, we can take
\mathcal{R}
to be amenable.

(2)

We can take
c
such that
\ker(c)
is ergodic.

(3)

If
\Lambda<G
is a dense countable subgroup, we can take
c
with values in
\Lambda
.

Any two of these conditions can be satisfied simultaneously. If
\Lambda
is amenable, the three of them can be satisfied simultaneously.

Proof.

If
G
is amenable, it is known that there exists an ergodic cocycle
c:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
where
\mathcal{R}
is a hyperfinite ergodic probability measure preserving equivalence relation
[
GS83a
,
GS83b
]
. Thanks to Lemma
5.1
, up to replacing
c
with a cohomologous cocycle, we can ensure that
\ker(c)
is ergodic. If we use instead Lemma
5.2
, we can ensure that
c
takes its values in
\Lambda
.

If
G
is not necessarily amenable, let
F
be a nontrivial finite group and consider the wreath product
H=\Lambda\ltimes F^{(\Lambda)}
with its natural free and ergodic probability measure preserving action on the compact group
(X,\nu)=F^{\Lambda}
. Let
\mathcal{R}
be the associated orbit equivalence relation. Denote by
p:H\to\Lambda
the canonical quotient homomorphism. Let
c:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
be the cocycle defined by
c(hx,x)=p(h)
for all
h\in H
and
x\in X
. Then
\ker(c)
is ergodic since it contains the orbit equivalence relation of
F^{(\Lambda)}
and
c:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G
is ergodic since
\Lambda<G
is dense. Clearly, if
\Lambda
is amenable, then
H
is also amenable, hence
\mathcal{R}
is amenable.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.4
.

Let
\rho:G\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright(Y,\eta)
be an ergodic nonsingular action. Thanks to Theorem
5.5
, we can take a measure preserving equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{0}
on a probability space
(X_{0},\nu_{0})
and an ergodic cocycle
c_{0}:\mathcal{R}_{0}\rightarrow G
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be an ergodic amenable nonsingular equivalence relation on a probability space
(T,\zeta)
such that the Radon–Nikodym cocycle
\delta:\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
is ergodic, i.e.
\mathcal{T}
is of type
{\rm III}_{1}
.

On
(X,\nu)=(X_{0}\times T\times Y,\nu_{0}\otimes\zeta\otimes\eta)
consider the induced nonsingular equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}=(\mathcal{R}_{0}\times\mathcal{T})_{\rho,c_{0}\times\delta}
. Define the cocycle

c:\mathcal{R}\ni(x,t,y,x^{\prime},t^{\prime},y^{\prime})\mapsto c_{0}(x,x^{\prime})\in G.

Let us compute the Mackey range of
c\times\omega
where
\omega
is the Radon–Nikodym cocycle of
\mathcal{R}
with respect to
\nu
.

Let
\sigma:G\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\times Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
be the Radon–Nikodym cocycle of
\rho
. Then the cocycle
\omega:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
is given by

\omega(x,t,y,x^{\prime},t^{\prime},y^{\prime})=\delta(t,t^{\prime})\sigma(c_{0}(x,x^{\prime}),\delta(t,t^{\prime}),y^{\prime}).

The product cocycle
c\times\omega:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow G\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
is given by

(c\times\omega)(x,t,y,x^{\prime},t^{\prime},y^{\prime})=\left(c_{0}(x,x^{\prime}),\delta(t,t^{\prime})\sigma(c_{0}(x,x^{\prime}),\delta(t,t^{\prime}),y^{\prime})\right).

We want to compute the algebra

\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(X_{0}\times T\times Y\times G\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+})^{\mathcal{R}_{c\times\omega}}

of all invariant functions for the skew product equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}_{c\times\omega}
.

Consider the action
\kappa:G\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\curvearrowright Y\times G\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
given by

(g,\lambda)\cdot(y,h,s)=(\rho(g,\lambda)y,gh,\lambda\sigma(g,\lambda,y)s).

Then we have the equality

\mathcal{R}_{c\times\omega}=(\mathcal{R}_{0}\times\mathcal{T})_{\kappa,c_{0}\times\delta}.

Since
c_{0}\times\delta:\mathcal{R}_{0}\times\mathcal{T}\rightarrow G\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}
is ergodic, we conclude by Lemma
4.4
that

\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(X_{0}\times T\times Y\times G\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+})^{\mathcal{R}_{c\times\omega}}=\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(Y\times G\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+})^{\kappa}.

We can regard these
\kappa
-invariant functions in
\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(Y\times G\times\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+})^{\kappa}
as
G
-equivariant functions in
\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(G,\mathord{\text{\rm L}}^{\infty}(Y)^{(1,\alpha)})
where

6.
The cocycle perturbation theorem

Our goal in this section is to prove Theorem
B
by using Theorem
C
. In fact, we will prove the following more general version for arbitrary locally compact abelian groups.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
M
be a factor with separable predual. Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
be an action of a second countable locally compact abelian group
G
. Suppose that
\alpha
is almost periodic and that
M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
is a factor. Then
\alpha
is cocycle equivalent to an action
\beta:G\curvearrowright M
such that
\beta
is almost periodic,
M^{\alpha}\subset M^{\beta}
and
M^{\beta}
is a factor.

More precisely, we can choose
\beta
of the form
\beta_{g}=\operatorname{Ad}(v_{g})\circ\alpha_{g},\;g\in G
for some continuous morphism
v:G\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{Z}(M^{\alpha}))
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra and let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
be a continuous action of a locally compact abelian group
G
. Let
v:G\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{Z}(M^{\alpha}))
be a continuous morphism and define a new action
\beta:G\curvearrowright M
by
\beta_{g}=\operatorname{Ad}(v_{g})\circ\alpha_{g}
for all
g\in G
. Then
M^{\alpha}\subset M^{\beta}
and
\mathcal{Z}(M^{\beta})\subset\mathcal{Z}(M^{\alpha})
.

Proof.

It is clear that
M^{\alpha}\subset M^{\beta}
. Take
x\in\mathcal{Z}(M^{\beta})
. Since
v_{g}\in M^{\beta}
,
x
commutes with
v_{g}
hence
\alpha_{g}(x)=\beta_{g}(x)=x
for all
g\in G
. Thus,

and we conclude that
x\in M^{\alpha}\cap\mathcal{Z}(M^{\beta})\subset\mathcal{Z}(M^{\alpha})
.
∎

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete group
\Gamma
,
(X,\nu)
a standard probability space,

any second countable locally compact group. A cocycle

We denote by
\mathord{\text{\rm Z}}^{1}(\theta,G)

\mathord{\text{\rm B}}^{1}(\theta,G)

Theorem

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
G

We will reduce this cohomology problem for actions that are not necessarily free to a cohomology problem for equivalence relations by using the following proposition.

Proposition 6.4

Let

\Gamma

Let

be the quotient map. Then
\ker(p)=\ker(\theta)=N
because

is abelian and
\theta
is ergodic. Since
\mathcal{R}
is hyperfinite
[

]

acting freely on

Now we can use Theorem

7.
The Rokhlin property for almost periodic actions

Our goal in this section is to prove Theorem
A
and more generally, the following version for almost periodic actions of locally compact abelian groups.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
G
be a second countable locally compact abelian group. Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright R
be an almost periodic action on the hyperfinite
{\rm II}_{1}
factor such that
R\rtimes_{\alpha}G
is a factor and
\alpha
is outer. Then
\alpha
has the Rokhlin property.

Thanks to Theorem
6.1
, it is sufficient to prove Theorem
7.1
under the assumption that
\alpha
is prime. To deal with this case we combine the ideas of
[
Ki95
, Proposition 2.5]
and
[
MT12
, Theorem 6.12]
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
G
be a second countable locally compact abelian group. Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright R
be a continuous action on the hyperfinite
{\rm II}_{1}
factor that is almost periodic, prime and outer. Then
\alpha
has the Rokhlin property.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma<\widehat{G}
be the point spectrum of
\alpha
and write
\alpha=\kappa\circ\iota
where
\kappa:\widehat{\Gamma}\curvearrowright R
is a continuous action and
\iota:G\rightarrow\widehat{\Gamma}
is the dual inclusion of
\Gamma<\widehat{G}
. Of course, we have

, hence
\kappa
is a prime action. We distinguish three cases.

Case 1:
\alpha
is ergodic, i.e.​
R^{\alpha}=\mathbb{C}
.
Thanks to
[
OPT79
]
, we know that
\kappa
is isomorphic to the dual action on a twisted group von Neumann algebra
\mathord{\text{\rm L}}_{\Omega}(\Gamma)
for some symplectic bicharacter

. This means that

is generated by unitaries
(u_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}
such that
\tau(u_{a})=0
for all

,

\kappa_{g}(u_{a})=\langle g,a\rangle u_{a}
for all

for all

\widehat{\Gamma}\times\widehat{G}

\langle a,g\rangle\langle\varphi(a),b\rangle=1

\langle a,g\rangle=\overline{\langle\varphi(a),b\rangle}=\overline{\Omega(a,b)}=\Omega(b,a)

\alpha_{g}(u_{a})=\langle a,g\rangle u_{a}=\Omega(b,a)u_{a}=u_{b}u_{a}u_{b}^{*}

\Gamma/H
on
N_{\omega}
is free and liftable, we know by Ocneanu’s 1-cocycle vanishing theorem
[
Oc85
, Proposition 7.2]
that there exists
z\in\mathcal{U}(N_{\omega})
such that
c(a)=\theta_{a}^{\omega}(z)z^{*}
for every
a\in\Gamma
. This means that
\theta_{a}^{\omega}(vz^{*})=vz^{*}
for all
a\in\Gamma
. It then follows that
uv^{*}z
commutes with
u_{a}
for every
a\in\Gamma
. Since
uv^{*}z
also commutes with
N
by construction, we conclude that
uv^{*}z\in R^{\prime}\cap R^{\omega}
. We thus obtained a unitary
U=uv^{*}z\in\mathcal{U}(R_{\alpha,\omega})
such that

\alpha^{\omega}_{g}(U)=\alpha^{\omega}_{g}(uv^{*}z)=\alpha_{g}^{\omega}(u)v^{*}z=\langle g,p\rangle uv^{*}z=\langle g,p\rangle U

for all
g\in G
. This shows that
\alpha
has the Rokhlin property.
∎

We conclude this section by observing that for any almost periodic action
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
of a locally compact abelian group on an arbitrary factor,
\alpha
is strictly outer if and only if
\alpha
is outer and
M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
is a factor. More generally, we can describe the relative commutant
M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)
when
\alpha
is not outer.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
M
be a factor. Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M
be an action of a locally compact abelian group
G
. Suppose that
\alpha
is almost periodic and let
E^{\alpha}:M\rightarrow M^{\alpha}
be the canonical
\alpha
-invariant faithful normal conditional expectation. Let
H=\{g\in G\mid\alpha_{g}\in\operatorname{Inn}(M)\}
and for every
h\in H
, write
\alpha_{h}=\operatorname{Ad}(v_{h})
for some
v_{h}\in\mathcal{U}(M)
. Let
\chi:H\rightarrow\widehat{G}
be the morphism defined by
\alpha_{g}(v_{h})=\langle g,\chi(h)\rangle v_{h}
for all
g\in G
and
h\in H
.

Assume that
M\rtimes G
is a factor. Then the following hold:

(1)

\chi
is injective.

(2)

The unitaries
(v_{h})_{h\in H}
are pairwise orthogonal in
M
with respect to
E^{\alpha}
. In particular, if
M
has separable predual, then
H
is at most countable.

(3)

M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)
is generated by the unitaries
(v_{h}^{*}u_{h})_{h\in H}
. In particular,
\alpha
is outer if and only if
M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)=\mathbb{C}1
.

Proof.

(1) Take
h\in\ker(\chi)
. Then
v_{h}\in M^{\alpha}
. Since
\alpha_{h}=\operatorname{Ad}(v_{h})
, we know that
v_{h}^{*}u_{h}\in M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)
. Since
v_{h}\in M^{\alpha}
,
v_{h}^{*}u_{h}
commutes with
u_{g}
for every
g\in G
. Thus,
v_{h}^{*}u_{h}
is in the center of
M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
, which is trivial by assumption. We conclude that
h=1
.

(2) Take
h,k\in H
with
h\neq k
. We have
\alpha_{g}(v_{h}^{*}v_{k})=\langle g,\chi(h^{-1}k)\rangle v_{h}^{*}v_{k}
for every
g\in G
. Hence
E^{\alpha}(v_{h}^{*}v_{k})=\langle g,\chi(h^{-1}k)\rangle E^{\alpha}(v_{h}^{*}v_{k})
for all
g\in G
. Since
\chi
is injective, we know that
\chi(h^{-1}k)
is a nontrivial character in
\widehat{G}
and we conclude that
E^{\alpha}(v_{h}^{*}v_{k})=0
.

(3) Let
\operatorname{Aut}(M,\alpha)=\{\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(M)\mid\theta\text{ commutes with }\alpha\}
. Observe that we have a natural continuous homomorphism
\pi:\operatorname{Aut}(M,\alpha)\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)
such that
\pi(\theta)|_{M}=\theta
and
\pi(\theta)
fixes
\mathord{\text{\rm L}}(G)
for all
\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(M,\alpha)
. Moreover,
\pi(\alpha_{g})=\operatorname{Ad}(u_{g})
for all
g\in G
. Since
\alpha
is almost periodic, it follows that the inner action
\pi\circ\alpha
is also almost periodic. Let
\beta:G\curvearrowright M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)
be the restriction of
\pi\circ\alpha
to
M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)
. Then
\beta
is almost periodic. Since
M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
is a factor, we also know that
\beta
is ergodic. Therefore,
M^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)
is generated by unitaries
w_{p}
for some
p\in\widehat{G}
such that
\alpha_{g}(w_{p})=\langle p,g\rangle w_{p}
.

This means that
\operatorname{Ad}(w_{p})(u_{g})=\langle p,g\rangle u_{g}
for all
g\in G
. Hence
\widehat{\alpha}_{p}=\operatorname{Ad}(w_{p})
where
\widehat{\alpha}:\widehat{G}\curvearrowright M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
is the dual action. Since
M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
is a factor, there exists a nontrivial
g\in G
such that
\widehat{\alpha}_{q}(w_{p})=\langle q,g\rangle w_{p}
for all
q\in\widehat{G}
. This means that
v_{g}=u_{g}w_{p}^{*}
is fixed by
\widehat{\alpha}
, hence
v_{g}\in M
. Since
w_{p}=v_{g}^{*}u_{g}
commutes with
M
, we conclude that
\alpha_{g}=\operatorname{Ad}(v_{g})
, hence
g\in H
.
∎

We note that the classification of almost periodic flows
\alpha:G\curvearrowright R
on the hyperfinite
{\rm II}_{1}
factor is not complete since the case where
R\rtimes_{\alpha}G
is a factor and
\alpha
is not outer is not covered by our main theorem. This case has no analogue in the classification of amenable type
{\rm III}
factors. We make the following conjecture.

Conjecture
.

Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright R
be an almost periodic action on the hyperfinite
{\rm II}_{1}
factor such that
R\rtimes_{\alpha}G
is a factor. Then
\alpha
is classified up to cocycle conjugacy by the characteristic invariant
(H,\chi)
where
H=\{h\in G\mid\alpha_{h}\in\operatorname{Inn}(M)\}
and
\chi:G\times H\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
is the bicharacter defined by
\chi(g,h)=\alpha_{g}(v)v^{*}
for every
g\in G
,
h\in H
and
v\in\mathcal{U}(R)
such that
\operatorname{Ad}(v)=\alpha_{h}
.

8.
Almost periodic factors of type
{\rm III}_{1}

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
D
. Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra with separable predual and
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{P}(M)
. Following
[
Ta03
, Chapter
{\rm VIII}
]
, we say that
\varphi
and
\psi
are
commuting
if
\psi=\psi\circ\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. This relation is actually symmetric.

Recall that if
h
is a nonsingular positive selfadjoint operator affiliated with
M_{\varphi}
, then we can define a new weight
\varphi(h\,\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(M)
. This weight
\varphi(h\,\cdot)
is characterized by the fact that its Connes–Radon–Nikodym derivative with respect to
\varphi
is the map
\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{U}(M):t\mapsto h^{\text{\rm i}t}
(see
[
Ta03
, Lemma
{\rm VIII}
.2.8]
).

We record the following well-known fact (see
[
Ta03
, Corollary
{\rm VIII}
.3.6]
).

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra with separable predual and
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{P}(M)
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

The weights
\varphi
and
\psi
are commuting.

(2)

There exists a nonsingular positive selfadjoint operator
h
affiliated with
M_{\varphi}
such that
\psi=\varphi(h\,\cdot)
.

In that case, we have
M_{\varphi}\subset M_{\psi}
if and only if
h
is affiliated with
\mathcal{Z}(M_{\varphi})
.

We prove the following more precise version of Theorem
D
.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
M
be a factor of type
{\rm III}_{1}
with separable predual. Then for every almost periodic weight
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}(M)
, there exists an extremal almost periodic weight
\psi\in\mathcal{P}(M)
such that
\psi
and
\varphi
are commuting and
M_{\varphi}\subset M_{\psi}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha=\sigma^{\varphi}:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright M
. Then
M^{\alpha}=M_{\varphi}
. Since
M
is of type
{\rm III}_{1}
, we know that
M\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}\cong c(M)
is a factor. By Theorem
6.1
, we can thus find a one-parameter unitary group
u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{Z}(M_{\varphi})):t\mapsto u_{t}
such that the action
\beta:t\mapsto\operatorname{Ad}(u_{t})\circ\alpha_{t}
is almost periodic and
M^{\beta}
is a factor. By the Connes–Radon–Nikodym cocycle theorem
[
Co72
]
, we know that
\beta=\sigma^{\psi}
for some
\psi\in\mathcal{P}(M)
. Then
\psi
is almost periodic, it commutes with
\varphi
and
M_{\psi}=M^{\beta}
is a factor that contains
M_{\varphi}=M^{\alpha}
.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
8.2
, we obtain the following new characterization of extremal almost periodic weights.

Corollary 8.3
.

Let
M
be a type
{\rm III}_{1}
factor with separable predual and
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}(M)
an almost periodic weight. The following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

\varphi
is extremal.

(2)

M_{\varphi}\subset M
is a maximal semifinite von Neumann subalgebra.

We can deduce Corollary
8.3
from Theorem
8.2
.

Proof of Corollary
8.3
.

We use the equivalence proved in Lemma
8.1
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
Suppose that
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}(M)
is an extremal almost periodic weight and denote by
\mathbb{R}<K
a dense embedding into a second countable compact group together with the corresponding continuous extension
\alpha:K\curvearrowright M
of the modular automorphism group
\sigma^{\varphi}:\mathbb{R}\curvearrowright M
. Then we have
M_{\varphi}=M^{\alpha}
. Let
M_{\varphi}\subset P\subset M
be an intermediate semifinite von Neumann subalgebra. Since
(M^{\alpha})^{\prime}\cap M=(M_{\varphi})^{\prime}\cap M=\mathbb{C}1
,
[
ILP96
, Theorem 3.15]
implies that
P\subset M
is the range of a faithful normal conditional expectation and thus
P\subset M
is necessarily globally invariant under
\sigma^{\varphi}
. Denote by
\tau\in\mathcal{P}(P)
a trace on
P
and by
h
the unique nonsingular positive selfadjoint operator affiliated with
P
such that
\varphi|_{P}=\tau(h\,\cdot)
. Then for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and every
x\in M_{\varphi}\subset P
, we have
x=\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x)=h^{\text{\rm i}t}xh^{-\text{\rm i}t}
. Since
(M_{\varphi})^{\prime}\cap M=\mathbb{C}1
, we have that
h=\lambda 1
for
\lambda>0
and so
M_{\varphi}=P
.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
Suppose that
M_{\varphi}\subset M
is a maximal semifinite von Neumann subalgebra. By Theorem
8.2
, there exists an extremal almost periodic weight
\psi\in\mathcal{P}(M)
such that
\psi
and
\varphi
are commuting and
M_{\varphi}\subset M_{\psi}
. By maximality, we obtain
M_{\varphi}=M_{\psi}
. Then we have
(M_{\varphi})^{\prime}\cap M=(M_{\psi})^{\prime}\cap M=\mathbb{C}1
and so
\varphi
is extremal.
∎
