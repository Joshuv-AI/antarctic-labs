Summary of Grid Reliability Challenges

 Summary of Grid Reliability Challenges
• Large Load Reductions during Grid Disturbances
 ̶ Data center loads are very sensitive to voltage disturbances. They follow narrow tolerance limits on the
 disturbance in the grid voltage (ITIC/CMEMA limits, IEEE 449-1995) for triggering transfer of the load to a backup.
 ̶ The sudden loss of large loads can be detrimental to grid stability.
• Oscillations
 ̶ High concentration of power electronics with fast controls inside data centers make them prone to oscillations.
 ̶ The oscillations can impact the reliability of both the data centers and the grid. Oscillations from data centers can
 also impact nearby rotating generators by creating torsional stresses.
• Rapid Load Fluctuations
 ̶ Rapid load fluctuations can create power quality issues, such as flicker and harmonics. They can also impact the
 life of nearby rotating generators.
• Appropriate Dynamic Models are Not Easily Available
 ̶ Dynamic models of data centers are critical for evaluating their impact on grid reliability. They are also required
 for the interconnection process and finding the root-cause of stability events.
 ̶ Modeling requirements and performance guidelines from grid operators are evolving rapidly.

Solutions

 Solutions
• Large Load Reductions during Grid Disturbances and Load Fluctuations
 ◦ Could be mitigated by appropriate grid codes and performance standards for data
 center loads
 – E.g.: Few grid operators have developed limits on active power ramp rates from large loads
 ◦ UPS and BESS with energy storage are being developed to limit active power ramp rates
 from data centers during normal operation as well as grid disturbances

• Oscillations
 ◦ Could be derisked using high-fidelity vendor EMT models of data centers for evaluating
 such problems – development of EMT models and modeling requirement are the next
 steps.
 ◦ Development of damping and impedance-based specifications can further derisk such
 problems.

Summary

 Summary
• Generally, two types of power electronic converters in the data center power
  systems can interface with the grid:
 ◦ AC UPS
 ◦ PSU
• Tens to hundreds of AC UPS, each rated at few megawatts, are present in a large
  data center
 ◦ Power electronic converters inside AC UPS can interact with the grid when the AC UPS is
 operated in so-called double conversion mode
• Tens to hundreds of thousands of PSUs, each rated at few kilowatts, are present
  in a large data center
 ◦ Power electronic converters inside PSU can interact with the grid when the AC UPS is
 operated in so-called bypass mode

What Causes Loss of Large Datacenter Loads

 What Causes Loss of Large Datacenter Loads
• Answer: Most AC UPS follow stringent limits from IEEE Std. 446-1995 and ITIC on
 allowable grid voltage disturbance at its output.
• AC UPS switches from grid to the backup supply if voltage disturbance to the
 load exceeds IEEE Std. 446-1995 or ITIC (CBEMA) limits.
 o E.g.: 30% grid voltage dip longer than 20 ms (CBEMA)
• In contrast to UPS, inverter-based resources (IBRs) are required to remain
 connected to the grid during severe voltage variations.
 • E.g.: 30% grid voltage dip longer than 3 seconds (IEEE 2800)
• AC UPS will need to stay connected and ride-through grid disturbances to meet
 evolving performance requirements for large electronic loads from grid
 operators (e.g., ERCOT NOGRR282).

Evolution of AC UPS Technology and Alternate Options

 Evolution of AC UPS Technology and Alternate Options
 • AC UPS continues to represent the most common approach for bridging gap between grid failure and long-term
 backup solution such as diesel gensets in data centers
 o Certain vendors are integrating long-term battery energy storage in AC UPS to mitigate grid reliability challenges from AI
 data centers, meet evolving grid code requirements, enable data centers to provide grid flexibility, and eliminate backup
 resources such as diesel gensets

 • Alternate Options
 o Low efficiency, limitations from space and scalability, and failure risks are pushing the industry away from AC UPS.
 Alternate options being considered include:
 ▪ Modular AC UPS rated at 50 to 200 kW with similar technology and functions as centralized UPS (e.g., Eaton 93PM
 UPS family)
 ▪ Eliminate AC UPS and Move Backup to Racks: Battery Backup Units (BBU) or Local Energy Storage (LES) inside IT racks
 ▪ DC Architecture: There is a push to transition to from ac to dc distribution of the power delivery system for IT racks to
 reduced the number of power conversion stages, improve efficiency, as well as reduce cost and space requirements.
 ̶ DC UPS or Solid-State Transformer (SST) will be the key technology for this transition.
 ̶ There is also a push from the industry to move to ±400 Vdc or +800 Vdc architecture for IT racks from existing +48 Vdc and
 +12 Vdc architecture to increase the power density of IT racks towards 1 MW scale.

Summary: AC UPS

 Summary: AC UPS
• Multimegawatt AC UPS is the main component in datacenter power systems that directly
  interfaces with the grid
 ◦ Its interface with the grid is similar to inverters used in PV and energy storage power plants.
 ◦ However, AC UPS is sometimes bypassed by a static switch during normal operation to reduce
 losses and improve efficiency.
 – For the bypassed operation mode, detailed modeling of power converters inside AC UPS is not
 required for grid reliability studies.
 – Nonetheless, AC UPS still controls the transfer of load from grid to backup during grid disturbances,
 which must be properly modeled in the data center models used for power system reliability studies.

• Alternative solutions to eliminate the need of AC UPS are also being explored by advanced
  data center developers.
 ◦ However, the evolving grid code requirements are making AC UPS more attractive for data centers

Summary: PSUs

 Summary: PSUs
• PSUs are the power electronic converters seen by the grid within a data
  center power system during normal operation when AC UPS is operated in
  the bypass mode.
• Single-phase PFC ac-dc converters are the front-end within a PSU and they
  must be accurately modeled for evaluating control interactions between
  datacenters and power grids.
 ◦ The switching frequency of PSUs range from 10s to 100s of kHz. The current
 control bandwidths range from few to 10s of kHz. These high frequency dynamics
 do not penetrate the grid; hence, switching frequency and current control
 dynamics can be ignored in the PSU models used for grid reliability studies.

 Intermediate Bus Converters (IBC) and Voltage Regulators (VRs)

 IBC (Input: 40~60 Vdc, Output: 12 Vdc)

 Photo: S. Shah (NLR)

• IBCs step down PSU output voltage to 12 Vdc in case of 48 Vdc architecture of IT racks.
• VRs are multiphase buck converters that step down 12 Vdc to ~1 Vdc for supplying to GPUs.
• IBCs and VRs do not directly interact with the grid as they are isolated by AC UPS and/or PSUs.
 ◦ Their dynamics can be ignored in the simulation models of data centers used for grid reliability studies.

Summary

 Summary
• When the synchronous generator supplies the emulated data center load
 without any support from BESS, its shaft experiences large oscillations, as
 observed from the generator output frequency.
 ̶ Such large variations in the speed of a conventional generator can result in fatigue-induced
 shaft failure.

• The output of the synchronous generator remains almost flat when the
 fluctuations in the datacenter load profile are absorbed by the BESS.
 ̶ This eliminates torsional oscillations in the conventional generator, thereby reducing risks of
 fatigue-induced shaft failure.
 ̶ The effectiveness of a BESS in absorbing load fluctuations depends on the speed of load
 monitoring system, the control bandwidth of BESS, and its electrical distance from the load.

RD Opportunities: Hardware Testing

 R&D Opportunities: Hardware Testing
• Testing of AC UPS
 ◦ Grid simulators can create datacenter load profiles at megawatt scale for testing the performance of

 – Fluctuating AI load can cause voltage sags that might cause AC UPS to switch to backup power during normal operation

 – Validation of EMT models of datacenter equipment through hardware testing

 ◦ Integrate AC UPS with renewable generation; microgrids
 ◦ Integration with long-term storage to eliminate/reduce need for diesel gensets

• New Technology Development
