Response Letter: Reconsidering Intraoperative Hip Positioning in Single-Position Lateral Lumbar Interbody Fusion – A Commentary on “Optimal Positioning for Single-Position Lateral Lumbar Interbody Fusion”
Authors: Worawat Limthongkul, Natavut Prasertkul, Pakawas Praisarnti, Maruay Tanayavong, Surachat Jaroenwareekul, Wicharn Yingsakmongkol, Weerasak Singhatanadgige, Vit Kotheeranurak
Published: 2026

We sincerely thank the author for the thoughtful comments and for the interest in our study examining hip positioning during single-position lateral lumbar interbody fusion (LLIF) [1].

We agree that paraspinal muscle relaxation under general anesthesia may influence lumbar lordosis and contribute to additional sagittal flattening. As correctly highlighted, the transition from an active to a passive stabilizing system can alter spinal alignment. In our study, radiographs were obtained in awake individuals to isolate the pure biomechanical effect of hip positioning without the confounding influence of anesthesia. We have acknowledged this limitation in the manuscript and emphasized that intraoperative lordosis reduction may be greater under general anesthesia, reinforcing the importance of using a lordosis-optimizing configuration [2].

We also agree that the proposed positioning strategy may not be universally applicable, particularly in patients with severe deformity, sagittal imbalance, or prior lumbar fusion, where structural constraints limit positional correction [3-5]. Our intention was to define a physiologic baseline response to hip positioning rather than a universal clinical prescription. As such, we emphasize that intraoperative positioning should always be individualized based on patient-specific pathology and surgical objectives.

We agree that all right lateral decubitus position configurations result in a reduction of lumbar lordosis compared with standing. In this context, the 30° femoral neck configuration represents the position with the least reduction in lordosis, rather than true maintenance of physiologic alignment. We appreciate the author’s insightful remarks, which further strengthen the clinical interpretation of our findings and highlight important directions for future research in operative settings.
