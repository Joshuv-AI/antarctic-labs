Inexact Proximal Point and Tseng Algorithms with Nonsummable Errors to Solve Monotone Inclusions
Ba Khiet Le, Boris S. Mordukhovich, Michel A. Théra

Abstract
In this paper, we establish, for the first time in the literature, the convergence of the practical versions of the Inexact Proximal Point Algorithm (IPPA) and the Inexact Tseng Algorithm (ITA) for computing approximate solutions to monotone inclusions in Hilbert spaces under the the presence of nonsummable errors . Our approach relies on Tikhonov regularization, the contraction property of the associated monotone operators, and the recently developed R-continuity theory. The proposed techniques and results can be extended to analyze various important inexact algorithms in optimization-related problems with nonsummable errors.



Keywords.
Inexact Proximal Point Algorithm, Inexact Tseng Algorithm, nonsummable errors, approximate solutions, Tikhonov regularization, R-continuity.

AMS Subject Classification.
65K05, 65K10, 49J52, 49J53.

1
Introduction and Motivations

This paper concerns the study of efficient algorithms to solve the
monotone inclusion

0\in Ax,

(1)

where
A:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is a set-valued
maximally monotone
operator defined on a
Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}
. Various algorithms for solving (
1
) and related optimization problems have been largely developed over the years; see, e.g.,
[
2
,
11
,
14
,
15
]
and the references therein. Recent years have witnessed strong attention to
inexact
versions of such algorithms aiming to find approximate solutions under errors in computations. The reader can find various inexact algorithms of this type in
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
12
,
13
]
among other publications.

One of the most classical methods to solve the inclusion problem (
1
) is the
Proximal Point Algorithm
(
PPA
) proposed by Rockafellar
[
14
]
in the form

x_{k+1}=J_{\gamma A}x_{k},\qquad x_{0}\in\mathcal{H},

where
J_{\gamma A}:=(I+\gamma A)^{-1}
denotes the
resolvent
of
\gamma A
. It is well known that
J_{\gamma A}
is
firmly nonexpansive
; see, e.g.,
[
2
,
11
,
14
]
.

In many practical situations, the resolvent operator cannot be computed exactly and must therefore be approximated numerically. This naturally leads to the
inexact iteration

x_{k+1}=J_{\gamma A}x_{k}+y_{k+1},\qquad x_{0}\in\mathcal{H},

(2)

where
(y_{k})
represents an error sequence satisfying the estimate

\|y_{k}\|\leq\delta
with some prescribed tolerance
\delta>0
. This requires to seek constructive conditions ensuring the
convergence
of the sequence
(x_{k})
generated by (
2
).

Most existing convergence results for inexact proximal methods assume that the error sequence is
summable
, i.e., they require that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|y_{k}\|<\infty,

which implies, in particular, that
y_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. While being mathematically convenient, this assumption is often difficult to guarantee in practical computations. By contrast, the
bounded-error condition

\|y_{k}\|\leq\delta\;\mbox{ for all }\;k=1,2,\ldots.

(3)

is significantly more realistic from the computational viewpoint. Other commonly used relative error criteria employed, e.g., in the aforementioned publications, may also be difficult to verify at each iteration.

Having the above in mind, the
first goal
of this work is to investigate the
well-posedness
of
PPA
under the
sole boundedness assumption
on the errors (
3
). To the best of our knowledge, this setting
has not yet been studied
in the literature.

Our approach is based on the
Tikhonov regularization
of (
1
). More precisely, we consider the
Inexact Proximal Point Algorithm
(
IPPA
) defined by

x_{k+1}=J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}x_{k}+y_{k+1}=(I+\gamma\widetilde{A})^{-1}x_{k}+y_{k+1},

where
\widetilde{A}
is an approximation of the operator
A
given in the form

\widetilde{A}:=A+\epsilon I,

where
I
is the identity operator, where
\epsilon,\delta>0
are fixed parameters, and where the error sequence
(y_{k})
satisfies the estimate in (
3
).

Under the assumption that
A^{-1}
is
R-continuous
at the origin (see below), we establish the convergence of
(x_{k})
to an approximate solution of (
1
). This result is particularly relevant in applications, since exact solutions are generally unattainable in finite precision computations, whereas approximate solutions are sufficient for most practical purposes. Our analysis further reveals that the well-posedness/stability of the
PPA
is essentially induced by the
strongly monotone regularization term
rather than by the classical summability assumption on the errors in the previous publications.

The
second goal
of this paper is the extension of the proposed approach to solving the
composite monotone inclusion problem
given in the form

0\in Ax+Bx,

where
A:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is a set-valued maximally monotone operator, and where
B:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is a single-valued monotone operator. In this framework, we particularly investigate the corresponding
Inexact Tseng Algorithm
(
ITA
) applied to the Tikhonov regularized problem. The key idea consists in rewriting the iteration in the form

x_{k+1}=Tx_{k}+t_{k+1},\qquad k=0,1,\ldots,

(4)

where
T:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is a well-defined single-valued operator satisfying a suitable
contractive property
, and where the estimate

\|t_{k+1}\|\leq\delta

holds with some given tolerance
\delta>0
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall several preliminary notions and results from nonlinear analysis, mainly related to monotone operators and R-continuous mappings. Sections
3
and
4
are devoted to the convergence analysis of
IPPA
and
ITA
, respectively. Concluding remarks and some topics of the future research are presented in the final Section
5
.

2
Preliminaries from Nonlinear Analysis

Throughout this paper,
\mathcal{H}
denotes a real Hilbert space endowed with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and the associated norm
\|\cdot\|
.

Let
F:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
be a set-valued operator/multifunction. The
domain
,
range
,
graph
, and set of
zeros
of
F
are defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle{\rm dom}F:

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathcal{H}\;|\;F(x)\neq\varnothing\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{ran}F:

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{H}}F(x),

\displaystyle\operatorname{gra}F:

\displaystyle=\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\;|\;y\in F(x)\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{zer}F:

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathcal{H}\;|\;0\in F(x)\}.

The
inverse
operator of
F
is defined by

F^{-1}(y)=\{x\in\mathcal{H}\;|\;y\in F(x)\}.

It is straightforward to verify that

{\rm dom}F=\operatorname{ran}F^{-1},\qquad\operatorname{ran}F={\rm dom}F^{-1}.

The operator
F
is said to be
monotone
if we have

\langle x^{*}-y^{*},x-y\rangle\geq 0\;\mbox{ for all }\;(x,x^{*}),(y,y^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}F.

The operator
F
is called
maximally monotone

if
F
is monotone and there is no monotone operator

G:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
such that

\operatorname{gra}F\subsetneq\operatorname{gra}G.

Furthermore,
F
is called
\alpha
-strongly monotone
with modulus
\alpha>0
if

\langle x^{*}-y^{*},x-y\rangle\geq\alpha\|x-y\|^{2}

whenever
(x,x^{*}),(y,y^{*})\in\operatorname{gra}F
. The
resolvent
of
F
is given by

J_{F}:=(I+F)^{-1}.

It is well known by the classical Minty theorem that if
F
is maximally monotone, then
J_{F}
is single-valued, everywhere defined on
\mathcal{H}
, and firmly nonexpansive. Furthermore, if
F
is
\alpha
-strongly monotone, then the operator
J_{F}
is
\frac{1}{1+\alpha}
-Lipschitz continuous and hence is contractive; see, e.g.,

[
2
,
11
,
14
]
for more details and discussions.

Finally in this section, we recall several versions of the notions of
R-continuity
and the
compact R-continuity
of a set-valued mapping
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
at a point
{\bar{x}}
in its domain. These notions introduced and developed in
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
provide, in particular, useful tools for the convergence analysis of the algorithms.

Definition 1
.

The set-valued mapping
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is called
R-continuous
at
{\bar{x}}\in{\rm dom}\,\mathcal{A}
if there exist a number
\sigma>0
and a nondecreasing function
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfying
\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\rho(r)=\rho(0)=0
such that we have the inclusion

\mathcal{A}(x)\subset\mathcal{A}({\bar{x}})+\rho(\|x-{\bar{x}}\|)\mathbb{B}\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in\mathbb{B}({\bar{x}},\sigma)

(5)

with the
continuity modulus function

\rho
and
radius

\sigma
. When
\sigma=\infty
,
\mathcal{A}
is called
globally R-continuous
at
{\bar{x}}
. The inclusion in (
5
) means that for each
y\in\mathcal{A}(x)
, there exists
{\bar{y}}\in\mathcal{A}({\bar{x}})
satisfying the estimate

\|y-{\bar{y}}\|\leq\rho(\|x-{\bar{x}}\|)

for all
x\in\mathbb{B}({\bar{x}},\sigma)
. We further say that

•

\mathcal{A}
is
R
-Lipschitz continuous
at
{\bar{x}}
with modulus
L>0
if
\rho(r)=Lr
.

•

\mathcal{A}
is
R
-Hölder continuous
at
{\bar{x}}
if
\rho(r)=Lr^{\theta}
for some
L>0,\theta>0
.

Definition 2
.

A set-valued mapping
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is called
compactly
R-continuous
at
{\bar{x}}\in{\rm dom}\,\mathcal{A}
if for every compact set
K\subset{\mathcal{H}}
, there exists a number
\sigma>0
such that we can find a nondecreasing function
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfying
\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\rho(r)=\rho(0)=0
with

\mathcal{A}(x)\cap K\subset\mathcal{A}({\bar{x}})+\rho(\|x-{\bar{x}}\|)\mathbb{B}\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in\mathbb{B}({\bar{x}},\sigma).

Similarly to Definition
1
, we say that

•

\mathcal{A}
is
compactly
R
-Lipschitz continuous
at
{\bar{x}}
with modulus
L>0
if
\rho(r)=Lr
.

•

\mathcal{A}
is
compactly
R
-Hölder continuous
at
{\bar{x}}
if
\rho(r)=Lr^{\theta}
for some

L,\theta>0
.

Definition 3
.

Given a set-valued mapping
A:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
, we say that the
graph
of
{\cal A}
is
(
sequentially
)

closed at a point

\bar{x}\in{\rm dom}\,{\cal A}
if for any sequences
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
and any sequence
y_{k}\in{\cal A}(x_{k})
converging to some
y
as
k\to\infty
, we have
y\in{\cal A}(\bar{x})
.

Finally in this section, we formulate several statements related to R-continuity and compactly R-continuity obtained in the recent papers
[
9
,
10
]
.

Proposition 1
.

[
9
]

If
\mathcal{A}
is locally compact around zero, i.e., there is
\sigma>0
such that
\mathcal{A}(\sigma\mathbb{B}\setminus\{0\})
belongs to a compact set, then the compact R-continuity of
{\cal A}
at zero yields the R-continuity of
{\cal A}
at this point.

Theorem 1
.

[
10
]

If
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
has the closed graph at zero and is locally compact around zero, then
\mathcal{A}
is R-continuous at zero.

Theorem 2
.

[
9
]

The set-valued mapping
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is compactly R-continuous at zero and
\mathcal{A}(0)
is closed if and only if its graph is closed at this point.

3
Convergence of
IPPA

In this section, we study the convergence of
IPPA
applied to the Tikhonov regularized problem for finding an approximate solution to (
1
).

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that
A:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is a maximally monotone operator, and let

S:=\operatorname{zer}A\neq\varnothing.

Assume further that
A^{-1}
is R-continuous at
0
with the modulus function
\rho
. Let
(x_{k})
be the sequence generated by
IPPA
as follows:

x_{k+1}=J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}x_{k}+y_{k+1}=(I+\gamma\widetilde{A})^{-1}x_{k}+y_{k+1},\qquad x_{0}\in\mathcal{H},

with the notation

\widetilde{A}=A+\epsilon I,

and the bounded-error estimate

\|y_{k+1}\|\leq\delta,

where
\epsilon,\delta>0
are some fixed parameters. Then it holds that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}d(x_{k},S)\leq\delta(1+\gamma^{-1}\epsilon^{-1})+\rho(\epsilon a)

with the notation

a:=\|\widetilde{x}\|,

where
\widetilde{x}
stands for the minimal norm element of
S
. Moreover, by choosing

\delta=\epsilon^{2},

we get the convergence condition

\limsup_{k\to\infty}d(x_{k},S)\leq\epsilon^{2}+\gamma^{-1}\epsilon+\rho(\epsilon a)\to 0\;\;{\rm as}\;\;\epsilon\to 0.

Proof.

Consider the sequence

w_{k}:=x_{k}-y_{k}

along which we have the equalities

\displaystyle w_{k+1}

\displaystyle=J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}x_{k}-J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}(x_{k}-y_{k})+J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}(x_{k}-y_{k})

\displaystyle=J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}x_{k}-J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}(x_{k}-y_{k})+J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}(w_{k}).

Denote by
x^{*}
the unique solution to the Tikhonov-regularized problem

0\in\widetilde{A}(x^{*})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^{*}=J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}x^{*}.

Observe that
\widetilde{A}
is maximally and
\epsilon
-strongly monotone, and hence the resolvent
J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}
is
\kappa
-Lipschitz continuous with the modulus

\kappa:=\frac{1}{1+\gamma\epsilon}<1.

Therefore, we arrive at the relationships

\displaystyle\|w_{k+1}-x^{*}\|

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}x_{k}-J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}(x_{k}-y_{k})+J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}(w_{k})-J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}x^{*}\Bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\|J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}x_{k}-J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}(x_{k}-y_{k})\|+\|J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}(w_{k})-J_{\gamma\widetilde{A}}x^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\|y_{k}\|+\kappa\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\delta+\kappa\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|.

Iterating the resulting inequality yields

\displaystyle\|w_{k+1}-x^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{k+1}\|w_{0}-x^{*}\|+\kappa\delta(1+\kappa+\cdots+\kappa^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{k+1}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|+\frac{\kappa\delta}{1-\kappa}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{k+1}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|+\delta\gamma^{-1}\epsilon^{-1},

which brings us to the estimate

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|\leq\delta\gamma^{-1}\epsilon^{-1}.

Observe furthermore that

x^{*}\in A^{-1}(-\epsilon x^{*}).

Since
A^{-1}
is R-continuous at
0
, it tells us that

x^{*}\in A^{-1}(-\epsilon x^{*})\subset A^{-1}(0)+\rho(\epsilon\|x^{*}\|)\mathbb{B}=S+\rho(\epsilon\|x^{*}\|)\mathbb{B},

which implies in turn that

d(x^{*},S)\leq\rho(\epsilon\|x^{*}\|).

Moreover, it follows from
[
8
]
that

\|x^{*}\|\leq\|\widetilde{x}\|=a,

and thus we arrive at the estimate

d(x^{*},S)\leq\rho(\epsilon a).

We have the inequality

\displaystyle d(x_{k},S)\leq\|x_{k}-w_{k}\|+\|w_{k}-x^{*}\|+d(x^{*},S).

Passing there to the limit as
k\to\infty
gives us the claimed condition

\limsup_{k\to\infty}d(x_{k},S)\leq\delta(1+\gamma^{-1}\epsilon^{-1})+\rho(\epsilon a).

Letting finally
\delta=\epsilon^{2}
completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 1
.

The following remarks are in order:

(i)
Theorem
3
shows that for all
k
sufficiently large, the iterate
x_{k}
is an
approximate solution
to the original inclusion problem (
1
) provided that
\delta
is sufficiently small and hence
\epsilon=\sqrt{\delta}
is small as well.

(ii)
If
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it is sufficient to assume that
A^{-1}
is
compactly R-continuous
at
0
since
x^{*}
belongs to the compact set
a\mathbb{B}
. Then this condition automatically holds since
A
is a maximally monotone operator, which implies that the graph of
A^{-1}
is closed and hence
A^{-1}
is compactly R-continuous at
0
by Theorem
2
.

(iii)
In practice,
errors cannot be summable
. Indeed, just simple functions such as
\sin x,e^{x},\sqrt{x},\ldots
have small positive errors. This indicates that the summable error assumption is not the reason to ensure the stability of
PPA
but the
strong monotonicity
is, which makes the resolvent to be
contractive
. Note that even if the operator
A
is only maximally monotone, there is some strong monotonicity part of
A
. It means that
\|J_{\gamma A}x-J_{\gamma A}y\|\leq\kappa\|x-y\|
for some
\kappa\in(0,1)
,
\gamma>0,
and
x,y
belong to the designated region. Therefore, we also obtain the convergence to approximate solutions if
PPA
is applied to the original operator
A
with some suitable initial point
x_{0}
and stepsize
\gamma
. For instance, if
A(x)=x-\sin x
, then it is maximally monotone but not strongly monotone on
[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
. However, this operator is strongly monotone on
C=\{x:\epsilon\leq|x|\leq\pi/2\}
for all small positive
\epsilon
. Thus if
x_{0}\in C
, then
PPA
applied to
A
converges to an appropriate approximation of the exact solution
x=0
to (
1
) in the presence of nonsummable errors.

4
Convergence of
ITA

In this section, we study the
composite inclusion problem
in the summation form

0\in Ax+Bx,

(6)

where
A:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is a maximally monotone operator while
B:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is an
L
-Lipschitz continuous and maximally monotone one. The
regularized inclusion
problem associated with (
6
) is given as follows

0\in Ax+Bx+\epsilon x=\widetilde{A}x+Bx,

(7)

where
\widetilde{A}=A+\epsilon I
for some small
\epsilon>0
.

Consider the
Inexact Tseng Algorithm (ITA)
to solve the regularized inclusion (
7
) associated with (
6
) and formulated as

\begin{cases}y_{k}=J_{\gamma\tilde{A}}(x_{k}-\gamma Bx_{k})+z_{k},\\
x_{k+1}=y_{k}+\gamma(Bx_{k}-By_{k}),\end{cases}

where
z_{k}
is the error in the step using the resolvent. Denoting
w_{k}:=y_{k}-z_{k}
for
k\geq 0
and recalling that
B
is Lipschitz continuous, we can rewrite
ITA
in the form

\begin{cases}w_{k}=J_{\gamma\tilde{A}}(x_{k}-\gamma Bx_{k}),\;k\geq 0,\\
x_{k+1}=w_{k}+\gamma(Bx_{k}-Bw_{k})+t_{k+1},\end{cases}

where
\|t_{k+1}\|\leq\delta
for some numbers
\gamma,\delta>0
. The latter admits the equivalent representation

x_{k+1}=Tx_{k}+t_{k+1},\;k\geq 0,\;x_{0}\in\mathcal{H},

where the well-defined mapping
T:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is given by

\begin{cases}y=J_{\gamma\tilde{A}}(x-\gamma Bx),\\
Tx=y+\gamma(Bx-By).\end{cases}

(8)

The following lemma of its own interest plays a crucial role in establishing the convergence of
ITA
in the subsequent Theorem
5
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
x^{*}
be the unique fixed point of
T
. Choose
\gamma>0
such that
\gamma^{2}L^{2}\leq 1-2\gamma\varepsilon
. Then the operator
\|T(\cdot)-x^{*}\|:\mathcal{H}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is
\kappa
-Lipschitz continuous with the modulus

\kappa=\sqrt{1-\gamma\epsilon}<1.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
T
is single-valued being defined on the whole space
\mathcal{H}
since
\tilde{A}
is a maximally monotone operator. Note that
\tilde{A}+B
is strongly maximally monotone. Thus
T
has a unique fixed point
x^{*}
, which satisfies the inclusion
0\in\tilde{A}x^{*}+Bx^{*}
. Let
x\in\mathcal{H}
and
y=J_{\gamma\tilde{A}}(x-\gamma Bx)
. Then we clearly get

\frac{y-x}{\gamma}+Bx\in-\tilde{A}y

(9)

together with the aforementioned inclusion

Bx^{*}\in-\tilde{A}x^{*}.

Since
\tilde{A}
is
\epsilon
-strongly monotone, it follows that

\left\langle\frac{y-x}{\gamma}+{B}x-{B}x^{*},\,y-x^{*}\right\rangle\leq-\epsilon\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}.

Combining the obtained inequality with the monotonicity of
B
tells us that

\left\langle\frac{y-x}{\gamma}+{B}x-{B}y,\,y-x^{*}\right\rangle\leq-\epsilon\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}.

(10)

By
\gamma>0
, we deduce from (
8
) and (
10
) that

\langle Tx-x,y-x^{*}\rangle\leq-\gamma\epsilon\|y-x^{*}\|^{2},

which implies in turn that

\displaystyle\langle Tx-x^{*},y-x^{*}\rangle\leq\langle x-x^{*},y-x^{*}\rangle-\gamma\epsilon\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\|Tx-x^{*}\|^{2}+\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|Tx-y\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}+\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|x-y\|^{2}-2\gamma\epsilon\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\|Tx-x^{*}\|^{2}\leq\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}+\gamma^{2}\|Bx-By\|^{2}-\|x-y\|^{2}-2\gamma\epsilon\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}.

Remembering that
B
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous and that

\gamma^{2}L^{2}\leq 1-2\gamma\varepsilon

brings us to the inequalities

\|Tx-x^{*}\|^{2}\leq\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma\epsilon(\|x-y\|^{2}+\|y-x^{*}\|^{2})\leq(1-\gamma\epsilon)\|x-x^{*}\|^{2},

which verify the claim of the lemma.
∎

Now we are ready to establish the convergence of
ITA
with a constructive estimate for approximate solutions to (
6
).

Theorem 5
.

Let
A:\mathcal{H}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
be an arbitrary maximally monotone operator, and let the operator
B:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
be an
L
-Lipschitz continuous and maximally monotone. Denote

S:=\operatorname{zer}(A+B)\neq\varnothing

and assume that
(A+B)^{-1}
is R-continuous at
0
with modulus function
\rho
. Let
(x_{k})
be the sequence generated by the
ITA
applied to the regularized problem (
7
). Choose
\gamma>0
such that
\gamma^{2}L^{2}\leq 1-2\gamma\varepsilon
.
Then we have the estimate

d(x_{k},S)\leq\delta(1+2\gamma^{-1}\epsilon^{-1})+\rho(\epsilon a)

(11)

with the notation

a:=\|\widetilde{x}\|,

where
\widetilde{x}
stands for the minimal norm element of
S
.
By choosing

\delta=\epsilon^{2},

we obtain the convergence condition

d(x_{k},S)\leq\epsilon^{2}+2\gamma^{-1}\epsilon+\rho(\epsilon a)\to 0\;\;{\rm as}\;\;\epsilon\to 0.

Proof.

It follows that

x_{k+1}=Tx_{k}+t_{k+1},\;k=0,1,\ldots.

Let
z_{k}:=x_{k}-t_{k}
, and let
x^{*}
be the unique fixed point of
T
that also satisfies the inclusion
0\in\tilde{A}x^{*}+Bx^{*}
. Using Lemma
4
, we get

\displaystyle\|z_{k+1}-x^{*}\|=\|Tx_{k}-x^{*}\|\leq\kappa\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|\leq\kappa(\|z_{k}-x^{*}\|+\|t_{k}\|)\leq\kappa(\|z_{k}-x^{*}\|+\delta),

where
\kappa=\sqrt{1-\gamma\epsilon}<1
. The induction procedure gives us the estimates

\displaystyle\|z_{k+1}-x^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{k+1}\|z_{0}-x^{*}\|+\kappa\delta(1+\kappa+\cdots+\kappa^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{k+1}\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|+\delta\frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa}

which consequently yield the inequalities

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|z_{k}-x^{*}\|\leq\delta\frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa}\leq\delta\frac{1+\kappa}{1-\kappa^{2}}\leq 2\delta\gamma^{-1}\epsilon^{-1}.

It is obviously to observe that

x^{*}\in(A+B)^{-1}(-\epsilon x^{*}).

Since
(A+B)^{-1}
is R-continuous at
0
, it follows that

d(x^{*},S)\leq\rho(\epsilon\|x^{*}\|)\leq\rho(\epsilon a).

Note furthermore that

\displaystyle d(x_{k},S)\leq\|x_{k}-z_{k}\|+\|z_{k}-x^{*}\|+d(x^{*},S),

which implies in turn that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}d(x_{k},S)\leq\delta(1+2\gamma^{-1}\epsilon^{-1})+\rho(\epsilon a).

By choosing
\delta=\epsilon^{2}
, we complete the proof of the theorem.
∎

5
Concluding Remarks and Future Research

In this paper, we propose a new approach to the design and justification of
practical inexact algorithms
for solving optimization-related problems governed by monotone inclusions in Hilbert spaces. The novel characteristic feature of such algorithms is incorporating
nonsummable errors
, which is the realistic case in practical applications. Our approach is based on the
Tikhonov regularization
of monotone inclusions and the usage of quite recent results on
R-continuity
of set-valued mappings.

The main attention of this paper is paid to inexact versions of the two algorithms named the
Inexact Proximal Point Algorithm
and the
Inexact Tseng Algorithm
in the presence of nonsummable errors, which are more realistic in comparison with the standard summability assumption on errors in computations.

Our analysis shows that the regularization term plays a crucial role in ensuring the stability of the algorithms under bounded perturbations. The proposed technique is sufficiently flexible and can be potentially extended to the study of other important inexact algorithms to solve optimization-related problems in the presence of nonsummable perturbations under bounded-error conditions. This goal constitutes a promising direction for the
future research
.
