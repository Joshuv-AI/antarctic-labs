Outcomes of the project

 The goal of this project was to improve density functional theory (DFT) for systems
where conventional semilocal approximations are limited by self-interaction error (SIE) and by
near-degeneracy or strong many-electron effects. While DFT remains the only broadly practical
first-principles framework for large-scale materials simulations, its predictive accuracy is often
challenged in situations involving stretched bonds, charge transfer, transition-metal chemistry,
magnetic couplings, band gaps, and correlated electronic states. This project addressed these
limitations by developing physically grounded and numerically robust exchange–correlation
functionals that retain the efficiency of modern DFT while extending its predictive scope.

 Over the course of the award, the research advanced along several closely connected
directions. First, it clarified the numerical and formal limitations of existing meta-generalized
gradient approximation (meta-GGA) functionals and delivered improved semilocal building
blocks, most notably within the r²SCAN family. Second, it addressed the long-standing gauge
ambiguity in combining semilocal and exact exchange energy densities by designing exchange
functionals directly in a gauge aligned with exact exchange, thereby avoiding ad hoc gauge
corrections. Third, it introduced new paradigm systems based on non-integer nuclear charges
(NNC), enabling controlled and essentially exact studies of the competition between strong
correlation and charge transfer. Fourth, it explored the incorporation of exact constraints and
physically motivated norms into machine-learning approaches to exchange–correlation
functional design.

 These methodological developments were accompanied by applications to representative
materials systems, including transition-metal oxides, lattice dynamics, dinuclear transition-metal
complexes, heavy-fermion compounds, cuprates, and infinite-layer nickelates. The results
demonstrated that improved density functionals can provide quantitative insight into complex
electronic structures, including competing magnetic states, correlated insulating behavior, and
band-structure trends relevant to energy and quantum materials.

 Key scientific accomplishments include: (i) identifying the role of self-interaction error in
phase stability and electronic structure, exemplified by TiO₂ and transition metal monoxides; (ii)
introducing smoother iso-orbital indicators that improve numerical stability without sacrificing
accuracy; (iii) establishing the r²SCAN framework as a balanced and widely applicable semilocal
functional; (iv) advancing gauge-consistent exchange constructions, including recent progress
toward Hartree-gauge meta-GGA functionals; and (v) creating new benchmark systems that
provide a systematic framework for functional development.

 Overall, the project advanced density functional theory by combining exact constraints,
numerical stability, and physical insight into a unified design strategy. These advances improve
the reliability of first-principles predictions for materials relevant to energy applications and
provide a foundation for future developments, including nonlocal functionals and machine-
learning-based approaches.
