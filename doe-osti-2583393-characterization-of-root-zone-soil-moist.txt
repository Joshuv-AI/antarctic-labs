II PROJECT OBJECTIVES AND ACCOMPLISHMENTS
(1) Goal: The scientific objective to the proposed research is to develop a proto-type downscaled
(<1 km) version of the root-zone soil moisture product called SoilMERGE or SMERGE. This
objective is linked as root zone soil moisture is a critical climate variable that has a more direct
influence on plant growth than precipitation. Therefore, the developed downscaled version of
SMERGE can be used as input into biodiversity informatic techniques (i.e., maximum entropy
modeling) Development of downscaled SMERGE will be facilitated by Department of Energy
(DOE) resources specifically data from the DOE Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM)
facility and modeling platforms such as the Energy Exascale Earth System Model (E3SM).


(2) Accomplishments

Soil MERGE or SMERGE is a NASA funded root zone soil moisture product that covers the
continental United States. Most noteworthy is that this is a long duration product spanning 1979
to 2019. As originally conceived, SMERGE had a 0125-degree spatial resolution and represents
an integrated estimate of soil moisture between 0 to 40 cm. The course spatial resolution of
SMERGE is an impediment for broad dissemination of this product as it is not at desired, sub-
kilometer field scale that is of greatest utility to agricultural producers. This forms the motivation
to explore downscaling methodologies that can provided a desired field-scale version of
SMERGE.

Study area for prototype validation straddles Kansas and Oklahoma, which is where the default
version of SMERGE performs the best (Figure 1). Four methods were used to validate
downscaled SMERGE, which include: (1) sparse in situ moisture data; (2) actual root zone soil
moisture retrievals from NASA’s Airborne Microwave Observatory of Subcanopy and Surface
(AirMOSS) campaign at the Marena Oklahoma Soil Moisture Active Passive In Situ Testbed
(MOISST) site; (3) NDVI benchmarking across the entire study area; and (4) examination of
performance of antecedent soil moisture compared with streamflow at a watershed scale.

In situ comparisons were made using data from the United States Department of Energy
Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) observatory, United States Climate Reference
Network (USCRN), Soil Climate Analysis Network (SCAN), and Soil moisture Sensing
Controller and oPtimal Estimator (SoilSCAPE). Three periods were examined: era 1 (2016 to
2019), era 2 (2012 to 2015), and era 3 (2003 to 2007). Table 1 illustrates improvement for most
versions of downscaled SMERGE during era 1 for ARM sites in two regions. Three machine
learning approaches were tested (Random Forest, RF), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost),
Gradient Boosting (GBoost).

Figure 1 depicts the results from MOISST. At this site correlation is boosted in the downscaled
versus the default version by 0.4 compared with default SMERGE. Maximum correlation for RF
were noted at 400 and 3000 m with lessened performance at the resolutions between 700 to 2000
m. Conversely, XGBoost and GBoost had maximum objective metrics around 1400 to 2000 m
with lower values at other resolutions. It is noteworthy to indicate that the optimal spatial
resolution for sparse in situ and MOISST sites during eras 1 and 2 was < 700 m.

Based on sparse in situ and MOISST comparisons, Tobin et al. (2025) developed a downscaled
version of SMERGE at a 500 m spatial resolution. Performance of default and downscaled
SMERGE was evaluated with ranked correlation using NDVI. Downscaled SMERGE has
consistently higher correlations than default SMERGE. XGBoost and GBoost versions of
downscaled SMERGE outperform downscaling based on RF. Downscale SMERGE represents
landscape variations in soil moisture with a much great fidelity than default SMERGE (Figure
2).

At a watershed scale, antecedent downscaled SMERGE based on XGBoos outperforms other
versions of SMERGE and streamflow for most intensive precipitation events (>35 mm / day;
Figure 3). These results are comparable to those found for default SMERGE during the original
development of default SMERGE (Tobin et al. 2019).

V IMPACT

The ultimate intent of this project is to develop statewide, downscaled versions of SMERGE
across the Great Plains. This root zone soil moisture will facilitate historical analysis at a field
scale, which is desirable for diverse stakeholders such as ranchers and farmers. Root zone soil
moisture (RZSM) is a critical climate variable important to a diverse range of hydrologic and

ecologic applications. Specific applications that can be potentially supported by downscaled
SMERGE include agricultural productivity, soil carbon fluxes, surface water hydrology
including storm-event response, and historical drought analysis.
