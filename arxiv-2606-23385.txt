A Catalogue of the Lie–Amaldi Classification with Structures Identified
H. Azad

Abstract
A catalogue of the Lie–Amaldi classification, together with their structure, is given using Claude. It is explained in the paper why this structural description is correct and what guidelines were given to Claude that made this possible. This catalogue will not be published elsewhere.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification.
17B10, 17B66, 32M25.

Key words and phrases.
Vector field, nilpotent Lie algebra,
rank of center, Lie–Amaldi classification.

1
General Remarks

The Lie–Amaldi classification of finite dimensional algebras of vector fields
remained relatively inaccessible till Hillgarter wrote down the lists in his
thesis
[
7
]
.

However, to date, no one has apparently identified the structure of these algebras. In this
paper we identify the algebras according to their algebraic structure. We have
done this using Claude.

The reasons that the information about the structures can be considered correct
is that it matches the following general results, which were obtained
independently of the Lie–Amaldi classification.

Proposition 1
.

Finite dimensional semisimple algebras of vector fields in at most
3
variables
can only be of rank at most
3
. The rank
3
algebras have all their simple
factors of type
A
. The only algebra that is not of type
A
is
B_{2}
and it can
only be realized as vector fields in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.

Proposition 2
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a Levi-decomposable algebra of vector fields in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, then its Levi
complement can only be of type
A_{1}
. If
\mathfrak{g}
is a Levi-decomposable algebra of
vector fields in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
, then its Levi complement can only be of type
A_{1}
,

A_{1}\times A_{1}
or
A_{2}
.

Moreover, the classifications of the nilpotent non-abelian algebras in
Section 7 are special cases of the main results of
[
2
]
.

For a proof see
[
1
]
and references therein. Moreover, for
2
variables,
this matches the structure given in
[
6
]
.

As is well known, for theoretical reasons, the classification of solvable
algebras is necessarily incomplete. See
[
5
]
and
[
8
]
.

The guidelines given to Claude

The guidelines given to Claude were:

Opening statement.

I am a mathematician working on the classification of finite-dimensional
Lie algebras of vector fields in three variables. I will be working through
Hillgarter’s thesis systematically. We are working over the complex numbers
\mathbb{C}

throughout.

Protocol.
For each Lie algebra of vector fields I give you, follow this
exact protocol without deviation:

1.

Compute the Killing form using SymPy and determine its rank.

2.

If the Killing form is degenerate, find the radical and the Levi factor
separately.

3.

Find the Cartan subalgebra. Over
\mathbb{C}
, use complex linear combinations if
necessary.

4.

Compute all roots with respect to the Cartan subalgebra.

5.

Identify the positive roots and simple roots.

6.

Write down the Cartan matrix and Dynkin diagram.

7.

State the conclusion.

Rules.

•

Never guess or conjecture the answer at any stage. Let the computation speak.

•

Never compute brackets by hand. Always use SymPy.

•

Never proceed to the next step without completing the previous one.

•

If I correct you, adjust immediately and do not repeat the error.

•

Keep responses concise. I am reading on a small screen.

•

We are always working over
\mathbb{C}
. Use complex Cartan elements when necessary.

Theoretic Basis

A few remarks on the programs used for identifying the structure of Lie algebras
are in order. The main references are
[
9
]
and
[
4
]
. The radical of a
Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
can be obtained from the annihilator of the commutator
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
of

\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the Killing form. For semisimple Lie algebras, one needs
just one semisimple element to enlarge to a full Cartan algebra. The fact that if

X
is an element of a semisimple algebra, its semisimple and nilpotent parts are
in the centre of the centralizer of
X
is very useful. The Dynkin diagram can
then be obtained from the list of roots; see e.g. Section 2 of
[
3
]
.

The primitive/imprimitive dichotomy can be read off the structure of the algebra.
A subalgebra
J
of an algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of vector fields that is normalized by
\mathfrak{g}
and
whose rank (the maximal dimension of its leaves) is less than the dimension of the
space gives a foliation that is invariant under
\mathfrak{g}
; the algebra is then imprimitive.
In particular:

•

An abelian algebra of non-maximal rank always gives an invariant foliation.

•

Similarly, the centre
Z(\mathfrak{g})
of a non-abelian nilpotent algebra gives an
invariant foliation, whose leaf through a point
p
is

\langle\,X(p):X\in Z(\mathfrak{g})\,\rangle
.

•

If the radical has an invariant foliation, it also gives an invariant foliation
of the whole algebra.

To show that an algebra
\mathfrak{g}
has no invariant foliation, one has to show that there
are no families of curves or surfaces that are left invariant by
\mathfrak{g}
. Here determining
the invariant families of curves and surfaces under abelian algebras of maximal rank
is helpful.

Amaldi’s groups of Type A are abstractly isomorphic to his plane groups

Type D groups are direct products of the plane groups with the standard copy of

\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
on the
z

\langle\,\partial_{z},\ z\partial_{z},\ z^{2}\partial_{z}\,\rangle

Levi-decomposable. The kernel
K=\langle\varphi_{j}(x,y)\partial_{z}\rangle

z\partial_{z}

abelian and never nilpotent. Hence it is Levi-decomposable when its plane base

Closing Remarks

Comparison with the main results of
[
2
]

show that the listing of nilpotent algebras in Amaldi is
incomplete. As solvable non-abelian algebras are abelian extensions of
nilpotent algebras, they are solvable algebras of the normalizers of abelian
algebras or of the algebras listed in [Azad, Biswas and Ghanam, loc. cit.].
Such an algebra can be described algorithmically only if the normalizer of its
commutator can be constructed algorithmically. This seems to be the main reason
why solvable algebras of vector fields in three or more variables and nilpotent
algebras in four or more variables cannot be classified in general in any
reasonable way.
