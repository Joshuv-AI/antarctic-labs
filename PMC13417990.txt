Multifunctional bacterial cellulose/polyvinyl alcohol-based films: the role of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles in enhancing antibacterial, antioxidant and tangerine preservation performance
Authors: Thuy Thi Thanh Nguyen, Dat Tien Huynh, Hien Van Nguyen, Trinh Huu Le, Thuong Hoai Pham, Huong Nguyen Thien Tran, Tuan Ngoc Nguyen, Thuan Van Tran
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Here, we synthesized an innovative nanocomposite film (BPAZ) by blending bacterial cellulose (BC) with polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) and reinforced it with Ag/ZnO nanoparticles. Structural and morphological analyses confirmed the uniform dispersion of Ag/ZnO in the BC/PVA matrix. The BPAZ films exhibited enhanced antibacterial and antioxidant properties compared with the BC/PVA film. Remarkably, the BPAZ film containing 0.20 wt% Ag/ZnO (BPAZ-0.20) obtained the strongest antibacterial activity and antioxidant capacity. Also, this film showed good tensile strength (27.45 MPa), low water vapor permeability (0.39 × 10−10 g s−1 m−1 Pa−1), and low oxygen permeability (1.75 × 10−9 kg s−1 m−1). Preservation trials on Vietnamese tangerines revealed that the BPAZ-0.20 wt% film effectively extended fruit shelf life up to 15 days at ambient temperature and minimized weight loss. Therefore, the BPAZ nanocomposite film is a promising candidate for active food packaging.

Introduction

The growing environmental concerns associated with packaging films prepared from synthetic petroleum-based polymers, such as polyethylene, polypropylene, polystyrene, and polyamide, have received significant attention due to their non-biodegradability and long-term ecological deterioration.1 To address these challenges, biopolymer-based packaging materials have emerged as promising alternatives since they offer advantages such as low toxicity, biocompatibility, biodegradability, renewability, and excellent film-forming properties.2 Biopolymers derived from plants, animals, microorganisms, and agricultural byproducts, including pectin, starch, alginate, chitosan, collagen, keratin, gelatin, casein, xanthan gum, proteins, and cellulose, are often used for developing effective, multifunctional, and safe food packaging materials.3

Among these biopolymers, bacterial cellulose (BC) is a promising material for biodegradable packaging films. As a microbial-derived form of cellulose, BC possesses high chemical purity, a high degree of polymerization, excellent mechanical properties, biocompatibility, and biodegradability.4 Structurally, BC consists of β-d-glucopyranose units linked via β-(1,4)-glycosidic bonds. It can be produced from nata de coco via bacterial fermentation of coconut water, as well as from various waste materials such as citrus juice, spoiled fruits, prickly pear peels, and vinegar industry residues.5 Unlike plant-derived cellulose, BC is free of lignin and hemicellulose and exhibits a unique three-dimensional reticulated network structure that facilitates interactions with other components. However, dried BC films suffer from limited flexibility and plasticity and lack inherent antimicrobial properties.6 This drawback restricts their applications in active food packaging. To address this, blending BC with other polymers is required to enhance functional properties.

Blending BC with polymers such as poly(vinyl alcohol) (PVA), poly(ethylene glycol), polyvinylpyrrolidone, sodium alginate, proteins, and gelatin could effectively improve flexibility and durability of the resulting films.7 Among these, PVA received attention due to its excellent film-forming ability, tensile strength, flexibility, transparency, oxygen barrier properties, and biodegradability.8 Moreover, this polymer is safe for food applications and was evaluated by the Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives in 2003.9 Incorporating PVA into BC matrices enhances mechanical strength, water resistance, and overall appearance compared to films made from individual polymers.10

Nevertheless, unmodified BC/PVA films often exhibit no antimicrobial activities. The integration of antimicrobial agents and functional nanomaterials into BC/PVA matrices was reported.11 Metal and metal oxide nanoparticles, such as silver (Ag), copper oxide (CuO), zinc oxide (ZnO), and titanium dioxide (TiO2), are widely studied for their potent antimicrobial and functional properties.12,13 For example, Ag nanoparticles are well known for their strong antibacterial activity, mediated by the release of Ag+ ions that can disrupt bacterial cell membranes, damage proteins, and interfere with DNA replication.14 Meanwhile, ZnO nanoparticles exhibit antibacterial properties primarily by generating reactive oxygen species (ROS) and disrupting membranes.15 However, when they are used individually, ZnO often exhibits limited antibacterial activity due to particle agglomeration and rapid electron-hole recombination, whereas Ag nanoparticles alone may be unstable and costly.16

By integrating Ag with ZnO, the Ag/ZnO nanocomposite exhibits enhanced antibacterial performance through synergistic interactions between the two components. Ag nanoparticles can improve charge separation on the ZnO surface and promote ROS generation, thereby increasing bacterial cell damage. In addition, the combined system provides multiple antibacterial mechanisms simultaneously, resulting in larger inhibition zones against both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria.17 Remarkably, Ag-doped ZnO nanoparticles (Ag/ZnO) exhibit broad-spectrum antimicrobial, antioxidant, antifungal, anticancer, and anti-inflammatory activities.18 Thus, addition of Ag/ZnO into BC/PVA matrices not only enhances bioactivity but also improves mechanical performance for advanced food packaging applications.

Although the investigation of BC/PVA films incorporated with metals/metal oxides was conducted in several literature studies, the effect of factors such as metal doping and physicochemical, antibacterial and antioxidant properties was rarely reported. Moreover, the use of these biofilms in fruit and food packaging remains unexplored comprehensively. Here, the present study aims to design antioxidant, antibacterial, and biodegradable composite films for fruit preservation. BC/PVA films were combined with biosynthesized Ag/ZnO nanoparticles at varying concentrations. The effect of Ag/ZnO incorporation on the morphology, structure, and physicomechanical properties of BC/PVA films was examined. Additionally, the antioxidant and antibacterial activities as well as effectiveness in preserving tangerines under ambient conditions of the resulting films were discussed.

Materials and methods

Materials

Bacterial cellulose was supplied by the Faculty of Biological Sciences, Nong Lam University, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. PVA (87–90% hydrolyzed, with an average molecular weight of 30 000–70 000) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, USA. Fresh tangerines from a local farmer in Dong Thap province, Vietnam, were purchased without any pretreatments. The selected tangerines had a dark greenish color and a typical diameter of approximately 5–6.5 cm.

Preparation of biofilms

As shown in Fig. 1, BC films were obtained from pineapple peel waste after cultivating microorganisms in liquid Hestrin-Schramm (HS) medium under static conditions for 7 d. After purification, the BC was mixed with distilled water at a ratio of 1 : 2 (w/v), then blended and screened through a 0.25 mm mesh to obtain a homogeneous BC slurry. Separately, 5 g of PVA was dissolved in 100 mL of distilled water at 80 °C and stirred for 2 h. The PVA solution was then poured into the BC slurry at a volume ratio of BC : PVA = 50 : 50 and mixed thoroughly to form a BC/PVA solution. Next, glycerol (30% w/w, based on the weight of PVA) and 0.3 g of citric acid were added to the BC/PVA solution, and the mixture was stirred for 60 min at 70 °C. Subsequently, five portions of biosynthesized Ag/ZnO nanoparticles were incorporated into the polymer solution with different concentrations (0.05%, 0.10%, 0.15%, 0.20%, and 0.25% w/v) and continuously stirred at 70 °C for another 60 min. Meanwhile, a control film without Ag/ZnO nanoparticles was also prepared using the same procedure. Finally, the film-forming solutions were sonicated for 10 min to remove bubbles, cast into glass Petri dishes (150 mm × 25 mm), and then dried at 50 °C in an oven for 12 h. The dried films were named BPAZx, where x = 1–5 corresponds to the addition of Ag/ZnONPs (0.05–0.25%). The formulations of the composite films are summarized in Table S1.

Schematic illustration of the production of BPAZ films.

Physicochemical properties of the films

Barrier properties of the films including water vapor permeability (WVP), oxygen permeability (OP), and water contact angle (WCA) were measured based on previous studies. These measurements are detailed in the SI.

Similarly, water solubility and swelling ratio were referred from published studies. In addition, mechanical properties such as tensile strength (TS), Young's modulus, and elongation at break (EAB) and color measurement are detailed in the SI.

Antibacterial test

The antibacterial activity of BP-based nanocomposite films was evaluated against E. coli, S. aureus, S. typhimurium, and B. cereus from the culture collection of the Faculty of Biological Sciences, Nong Lam University, using the agar well diffusion assay. Inhibition zone diameters were measured in millimeters.

Tangerine packaging test

Fresh tangerines were washed with a 200 mg L−1 sodium hypochlorite solution for 2 min and randomly divided into three groups. The control group remained uncoated, while the others were unpacked with BP or BPAZ4 films. All samples were then stored under ambient conditions, and photographs were recorded throughout storage. Weight loss, ascorbic acid content, pH, and total soluble solids were monitored to assess the effectiveness of the preservation treatment.

Results and discussion

Characterization of films

Morphology

Fig. 2 displays the digital images of BP and BPAZx (x = 1–5) films containing different concentrations of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles (0–0.25%). The BP films exhibited a flexible, glossy, homogeneous, smooth, and semi-transparent appearance with a slightly whitish hue. In contrast, the BPAZx films displayed a translucent yellow-brown color, which intensified as the Ag/ZnO nanoparticle concentration increased from 0.05% to 0.25% (w/v). This color change is primarily due to the enhancement of light absorption as a result of the surface plasmon resonance of Ag nanoparticles. As combined with increased light scattering caused by ZnO, they influence the optical properties of the films.

Visual appearance and SEM micrographs of BP and BPAZx (x = 1–5) films.

The surface morphology of the reference BP film and BPAZx (x = 1–5) nanocomposite films was examined using SEM. According to Fig. 2, the BP film exhibited a smooth and uniform surface without obvious cracks or pores. This observation indicates good compatibility and blending between BC and PVA molecules. Conversely, incorporation of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles into the polymer matrix resulted in a progressively rougher surface as the nanoparticle concentration increased. Thus, there is a good correlation between filler content and surface texture. Mallakpour et al. also reported similar morphological changes in chitosan-tragacanth gum nanocomposite films containing varying percentages of ZnO and ZnO@Ag nanoparticles.19 These surface modifications are expected to influence the mechanical properties such as tensile strength of the films.

Elemental composition

To confirm the elemental composition, EDX analysis was performed on the BPAZ4 film (Fig. 3). The spectrum revealed characteristic peaks corresponding to C, O, Zn, and Ag. Peaks below 0.8 keV were attributed to C and O, while Ag peaks appeared between 2.5 and 3.5 keV. Zn peaks were observed near 1 keV and within the range of 8.5–9.7 keV. These results confirm the successful incorporation of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles and their good compatibility with the BC/PVA matrix.

EDX spectrum of the BPAZ4 nanocomposite film.

Surface chemistry

According to Fig. 4A, the pristine BP film exhibited characteristic absorption bands of its polymeric constituents. A broad band at 3442.78 cm−1 was assigned to O–H stretching vibrations of hydroxyl groups from bacterial cellulose and polyvinyl alcohol, indicating extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding. The peak at 2956.04 cm−1 corresponded to C–H stretching, while the band at 1630.39 cm−1 was attributed to O–H bending of adsorbed water.20 In the 1000–1200 cm−1 region, absorptions characteristic of polysaccharides were observed, corresponding to C–O–C and C–O stretching. Peaks at 1097.02 and 764.14 cm−1 were assigned to C–O–C asymmetric stretching and β-glycosidic linkages of the cellulose backbone, respectively.21 The absorption at 1403.73 cm−1 was attributed to –CH2 bending vibrations, which may originate from PVA. In BPAZ nanocomposite films, notable changes were observed in the hydroxyl stretching region.

FTIR spectra (A) and XRD patterns (B) of BP and BPAZx (x = 1–5) films.

A strong and broad band between 3100 and 3600 cm−1 indicated the formation of hydrogen bonding.22 The interactions between Ag/ZnO nanoparticles and hydroxyl groups of both bacterial cellulose and polyvinyl alcohol can be suggested as follows. Before adding Ag and ZnO nanoparticles, the interaction between BC and PVA is mainly based on hydrogen bonds (Fig. S1a). Indeed, citric acid plays a role in the carboxylation of the OH group on the BC. In other words, this esterification increases hydrophilicity of the BC at the interface. Hydrogen bonds are formed based on cross-linked bonds between H atoms of OH of PVA and O atoms of COOH/OH groups of the functionalized BC. After adding Ag and ZnO nanoparticles, there are more interactions such as H bonding, metal-coordination bonds and electrostatic interactions (Fig. S1b). These nanoparticles act as nanofillers that hinder the interactions between the hydroxyl groups in BC/PVA and water molecules.

Crystallinity

The XRD patterns of BP and BPAZx films are shown in Fig. 4B. The neat BP film exhibited characteristic crystalline peaks at 2θ values of 14.75° (101), 17.13° (101̄), and 19.60° (002) of BC23 and 22.66° (101) of PVA.24 This finding confirmed strong compatibility between BC and PVA. In BPAZx films, additional peaks at 2θ = 31.89°, 35.85°, 38.36°, 48.16°, 56.79°, 63.13°, and 68.30° were indexed to the (100), (002), (101), (102), (110), (103), and (200) planes of crystalline ZnO, while reflections at 38.36°, 44.78°, and 64.90° were assigned to the (111), (200), and (220) planes of face-centerered cubic (FCC) Ag. Incorporation of Ag/ZnO did not significantly alter the diffraction pattern of BC/PVA films, key evidence of excellent matrix-nanoparticle compatibility. Moreover, slight intensity variations with increasing nanoparticle content suggest hydrogen bonding interactions. Therefore, BPAZ films exhibit a two-phase structure consisting of a BC/PVA matrix embedded with Ag/ZnO nanoparticles.

Physicochemical properties of films

Thickness and color properties

Film thickness is of critical significance for food packaging because this factor directly affects durability, barrier properties, and preservation performance. As shown in Table 1, the average thickness of the BP film was 0.140 mm, which decreased slightly to 0.133 mm upon incorporation of 0.05 wt% Ag/ZnO. The interaction between Ag/ZnO NPs and the polymeric matrix may have resulted in a denser film structure and a reduction in the free volume of the matrix. When the nanoparticle concentration increased, however, the thickness rose to 0.170 mm at 0.15 wt% Ag/ZnO. This trend can be explained by the filler effect of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles. With their high surface area and surface energy, they might interact strongly with the polymer chains through hydrogen bonding. These interactions facilitate uniform dispersion of nanoparticles into the matrix and, hence, minimize agglomeration in obtained films.25

Thickness, optical properties, and color parameters of BP and BPAZ films

The incorporation of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles induced significant changes in the color parameters of BP and BPAZ films. The lightness (L*) of the neat BP film was 40.59, which increased slightly in BPAZ1 (43.79) and BPAZ2 (45.35). However, higher loadings (BPAZ4 and BPAZ5) reduced L* values to 36.93 and 37.45, respectively, which resulted in darker films. In terms of chromaticity, the neat BP and low-loading BPAZ films exhibited negative a* values (−2.85 to −3.13), which reflected a greenish tint for these samples. BPAZ4 (1.11) and BPAZ5 (1.70) shifted toward positive values, or a reddish hue. Meanwhile, the b* values gradually increased from 2.07 in BP to 8.02 in BPAZ5 with yellowish coloration at higher Ag/ZnO contents. This red-yellow shift is likely related to the localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR) of Ag nanoparticles, which typically imparts yellow to brown tones in polymeric matrices. ZnO nanoparticles were found to cause yellowing at elevated concentrations.26 The total color difference (ΔE) rose consistently from 20.34 for BP to 25.24 for BPAZ5; thus, a substantial visual change was induced by nanoparticle addition. A similar trend was observed for CMC/okra mucilage-based films incorporated with ZnO nanoparticles.27 Thus, the incorporation of Ag/ZnO not only enhances light-blocking but also provides a visually appealing appearance.

Moisture content, water solubility, and swelling ratio

Water affinity is a critical factor that influences the functional performance of packaging films. Reduced hydrophilicity and permeability enhance barrier properties and improve preservation efficiency. The moisture content (MC) of the BP and BPAZx films (x = 1–5) is presented in Fig. 5A. The pure BP film exhibited a relatively high MC of 15.3% because BC contains a large amount of hydroxyl (–OH) groups that readily interact with PVA chains.28 Incorporation of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles significantly reduced the MC to 7.15%. This reduction suggests that Ag/ZnO nanoparticles interact with hydroxyl groups in the polymer matrix to form H bonds and reduce the availability of hydrophilic sites.

Moisture content (A), water solubility (B), swelling ratio (C), and stress–strain curves (D) of BP and BPAZ films.

According to Fig. 5B, the water solubility (WS) of BP films decreased from 59.99% to 47.51% with the incorporation of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles. This decrease can be explained by the fact that Ag/ZnO nanofillers hinder the interactions between the hydroxyl groups in BC/PVA and water molecules. Moreover, increasing the Ag/ZnO concentration enhanced the cohesiveness of the BP matrix, which resulted in a more compact network structure and improved water resistance. Helmiyati et al. also observed that ZnO nanoparticles enhance the stability of carboxymethyl cellulose/PVA films by forming hydrogen bonds, thereby restricting water molecules from disrupting the polymer matrix.29

To investigate the effect of Ag/ZnO nanoparticle content as reinforcing fillers, the swelling ratio of BP and BPAZ films was evaluated (Fig. 5C). Incorporation of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles significantly reduced swelling, with values decreasing to 77.54% at a concentration of 0.25%, compared to the neat BP film. This reduction can be attributed to the nanoparticles acting as solid fillers that occupy free volume and limit water penetration.30 As a result, the water-holding capacity of the composite films tends to be reduced. Interestingly, the swelling ratio of BPAZ films showed no substantial variation within the concentration range of 0.05–0.25%. This finding suggests that the matrix reached a saturation point for nanoparticle interaction. The same finding was reported for Ag/ZnO nanoparticles incorporated into a carboxymethyl cellulose/polyvinyl alcohol hydrogel.31

Fig. 5D illustrates the effect of Ag/ZnO nanoparticle incorporation on the tensile strength of BP films. The addition of 0.05% Ag/ZnO resulted in a slight decrease in tensile strength. The formation of intermolecular interaction between nanoparticles and hydroxyl functional groups on polymer chains was likely to reduce the native interactions between BC and PVA. However, increasing the nanoparticle concentration to 0.10–0.20% significantly improved tensile strength. This enhancement can be attributed to stronger interfacial interactions (e.g., metal-coordination bonds and electrostatic interactions) between Ag/ZnO nanoparticles and the polymer matrix,32 which reinforce the composite structure and improve stress transfer efficiency. The decrease in tensile strength at 0.25% Ag/ZnO incorporation is mainly due to their agglomeration at higher concentrations.33 In this case, more stress-concentrating sites are created to disrupt the uniform polymer matrix. Additionally, excessive nanoparticles can interfere with polymer–polymer interactions, reduce cohesive bonding, and cause microstructural defects that weaken the film.

WVP, OP, CDP, and contact angle

Controlling oxygen, carbon dioxide, and water vapor permeability is essential for maintaining the quality and shelf life of packaged food. As shown in Fig. 6A–C, the neat BP film exhibited relatively high WVP (0.93 × 10−10 g s−1m−1 Pa−1), OP (5.39 × 10−9 kg s−1 m−1), and CDP (0.88 × 10−9 kg s−1 m−1). Thus, this film showed limited barrier performance. Incorporation of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles significantly reduced these values. Indeed, the BPAZ5 film showed the lowest WVP, OP, and CDP at 0.35 × 10−10 g s−1 m−1 Pa−1, 1.48 × 10−9 kg s−1 m−1, and 0.26 × 10−9 kg s−1 m−1, respectively. The uniform dispersion and strong compatibility of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles in the BC/PVA matrix may reinforce the polymer network and create a tortuous pathway that hinders the diffusion of water vapor and gases. Additionally, the rigid structure of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles reduces free volume and strengthens physical cross-links with polymer chains to obtain a more compact and mechanically stable composite. It was also reported that the incorporation of ZnO nanoparticles into a tamarind kernel powder matrix decreased the water vapor transmission rate from 33.35 to 18.04 g h−1 m−2.34

Water vapor permeability (A), oxygen permeability (B), carbon dioxide permeability (C), and water contact angle (D) of BP and BPAZ films.

Wetting behavior is a key indicator of surface properties and is commonly assessed by measuring the water contact angle. As shown in Fig. 6D, the neat BP film exhibited a hydrophilic behavior with a WCA of 38.73°. Increasing Ag/ZnO content increases the WCA from 42.6° (BPAZ1) to 96.2° (BPAZ5). Thus, there is a transition from hydrophilic to hydrophobic film surfaces because nanoparticles reduce the exposure of hydroxyl groups and the spreading of water droplets. Consequently, films with higher nanoparticle loadings improved water resistance. A similar increase in hydrophobicity has been reported for ZnO NPs and anthocyanins incorporated into carboxymethyl cellulose/starch-based films.35 These results indicate that Ag/ZnO nanoparticles can be strategically used to modulate the surface wettability and barrier performance of BC/PVA composite films.

Mechanical properties and thermal analysis

Table 2 presents the mechanical behavior of neat BP and BPAZx (x = 1–5). The neat BP film exhibited a tensile strength of 17.32 MPa, a Young's modulus of 337.54 MPa, and an elongation at break of 11.58%. Incorporating Ag/ZnO nanoparticles increased tensile strength and stiffness, and the highest tensile strength of 27.45 MPa was reached at 0.20 wt% loading. Ag/ZnO nanoparticles might form small clusters in the polymer matrix and enhance tensile strength during solution casting.36 The mechanical performance of nanocomposites is significantly influenced by nanoparticle size and interfacial interactions. Smaller nanoparticles enable more effective stress transfer between the polymer matrix and the fillers. In contrast, larger particles reduce this efficiency, even when the interphase is relatively thick.37 Although the Ag/ZnO concentration in the BPAZ5 film (0.25 wt%) was higher than that in BPAZ4, the tensile strength slightly went down. Due to the high surface energy of nanoparticles, they cause aggregation or agglomeration, thereby reducing the effective interfacial area and limiting mechanical reinforcement.

In contrast, the elongation at break of BPAZ films showed a slight decrease compared with the neat BP film. These results indicate that Ag/ZnO nanoparticles act as reinforcing fillers via the formation of additional physical cross-links between the polymer chains and the nanoparticles. Such cross-links facilitate more efficient distribution and enhance the stability of the polymer network, which improve the mechanical strength and rigidity of the polymer matrix.38 As the Ag/ZnO content reached 0.20 wt%, the interaction between Ag/ZnO nanoparticles and BC/PVA is the most favorable. The thermal properties of BP and BPAZ films were investigated by thermogravimetric analysis, and the corresponding TGA and DTG curves are presented in Fig. 7A and B. The TGA and DTG curves of the BP film indicated that thermal degradation occurred in four major stages. The first stage, in the range of 75 °C to 160 °C, exhibited approximately 6.70% weight loss, which was attributed to the evaporation of absorbed and bound water molecules. The second stage (160–250 °C) showed about 26.57% weight loss due to the initial decomposition of amorphous regions, hydroxyl-containing groups in the polymer matrix, and partial cleavage of glycosidic linkages within the BC chains.39 The third stage (250–380 °C) represented the main thermal degradation process, with approximately 31.94% weight loss, corresponding to the decomposition of the cellulose/PVA polymer backbone, including the breakdown of glycosidic bonds and polymer chains. The final degradation stage (380–600 °C) exhibited a further 33.37% weight loss due to the decomposition of residual cellulose structures and carbonized components.40

TGA (A) and DTG curves (B) of BP and BPAZ films.

For the BPAZ film, the thermal degradation behavior was similar to that of the BP control film and also occurred in four major stages. Remarkably, during the third stage (250–380 °C), the weight decreased from 71.24% to 25.47%, corresponding to degradation of the cellulose/PVA polymer backbone. The DTG curve showed the maximum degradation peak at around 321 °C, indicating the highest decomposition rate of the BPAZ film. The slightly lower thermal stability of BPAZ compared with the BP film may be due to interactions between BC and PVA, as well as interfacial interactions between the BC/PVA matrix and Ag/ZnO nanoparticles, which could promote heat transfer within the composite structure. Notably, an additional weight loss of approximately 19.42% in the 380–600 °C range was attributed to further carbonization and decomposition of residual BC and PVA structures. Meanwhile, a residual mass of approximately 6.0% remained at high temperatures, which was significantly higher than that of the BP film (0.41%). This higher residual mass indicated improved thermal resistance of the BPAZ film at elevated temperatures, attributed to the incorporation of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles. These nanoparticles promoted stronger interfacial interactions with the polymer matrix and acted as thermally stable inorganic barriers, thereby delaying thermal decomposition and increasing solid residue formation.41

Antibacterial and antioxidant properties

Antibacterial properties

The antimicrobial activity of the pure BP and BPAZ nanocomposite films was evaluated using the agar disc diffusion method against E. coli, B. cereus, S. typhi, and S. aureus. As shown in Fig. 8A and B, the neat BP film exhibited no antibacterial activity. Composite films, e.g., bacterial cellulose/PVA/ZnONPs and bacterial cellulose/PVA/AgNPs, exhibited limited antibacterial activities (Fig. S2). Indeed, bacterial cellulose/PVA/ZnONPs showed an inhibition zone (6.5 mm) against B. Cereus, while no antibacterial activity was recorded against the other bacterial strains. There are better antibacterial results for bacterial cellulose/PVA/AgNPs when this film obtains higher inhibition zones (6.0–7.9 mm) for E. coli, B. cereus, and S. Aureus.

Effect of the Ag/ZnO nanoparticles on the antimicrobial activity (A and B) and antioxidant activity (C) of BP and BPAZ films.

Meanwhile, incorporating Ag/ZnO nanoparticles imparted strong antimicrobial properties to the BPAZ films, as evidenced by the inhibition zones formed against all tested bacteria. For Gram-positive B. cereus, the inhibition zones increased progressively from 5.97 mm (BPAZ1) to 10.43 mm (BPAZ4), followed by a slight decrease to 9.37 mm in BPAZ5. A similar trend was observed for S. aureus, where the inhibition zone increased from 7.13 mm (BPAZ1) to 17.02 mm (BPAZ4) and then slightly reduced to 15.57 mm in BPAZ5. For the Gram-negative strains, the inhibition zones against E. coli were 9.47 mm (BPAZ1), 14.73 mm (BPAZ2), 14.70 mm (BPAZ3), 15.23 mm (BPAZ4), and 15.17 mm (BPAZ5), while those against S. typhi ranged from 7.27 mm (BPAZ1) to 12.03 mm (BPAZ4). Among all tested films, BPAZ4 (0.20% Ag/ZnO) consistently exhibited the largest inhibition zones against all four bacterial strains. Therefore, this film possessed superior antibacterial effectiveness compared to the other BPAZ films. This enhanced performance can be attributed to the optimal nanoparticle concentration in BPAZ4, which enables efficient release of Ag+ ions and generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) such as ˙O2−, ˙OH, and H2O2.42 These agents react with the bacterial cells and cause membrane disruption. It was also reported that ZnO nanoparticles incorporated into the CMC/PVA films exhibited more potent antibacterial activity against S. aureus than E. Coli.43 Similarly, the antibacterial performance of the chitosan/gelatin blended film was influenced by adding various concentrations of Co-doped ZnO (1.0, 2.5, 3.5, and 4.5 wt%).44 Their results indicated that the film containing 3.5% Co-doped ZnO nanoparticles showed the highest inhibition zone against E. coli compared to the other concentrations tested. In this study, BPAZ4 obtained a balance between nanoparticle dispersion and antibacterial activity, which plays a key role in broad-spectrum antimicrobial food packaging applications. Moreover, the antibacterial results in this study were comparable with previously reported results (Table S2).

Antioxidant properties

The antioxidant activity of bacterial cellulose/PVA blended films containing various loadings of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles was determined using the DPPH assay. As shown in Fig. 8C, increasing the concentration of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles up to 0.25 wt% in the BP film gradually increased DPPH radical activity. Notably, the BP film containing 0.20 wt% Ag/ZnO NPs exhibited the highest DPPH radical scavenging activity at 66.08%. This result was higher than that of the films incorporated with 0.15 wt% Ag/ZnO NPs (55.25%) and 0.10 wt% Ag/ZnO NPs (46.5%) and that of dialdehyde cellulose-based films as reported recently.45 In contrast, films containing 0.05 wt% and 0.25 wt% Ag/ZnO NPs showed the lowest activities (40.25% and 40.83%, respectively). The enhanced antioxidant activity observed at 0.20 wt% Ag/ZnO was due to the uniform dispersion of nanoparticles, which facilitates efficient electron transfer and the controlled generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) capable of neutralizing free radicals. In this context, Ag/ZnO nanoparticles served as both electron donors and acceptors and stabilized reactive radicals through electron transfer (R˙ + e− → R−).46

Preservation of Vietnamese tangerines

Vietnamese tangerine (Citrus reticulata Blanco) is a fruit of high nutritional and economic value, and hence, this fruit was selected to compare the preservation performance of the BP and BPAZ films. Here, we evaluated the appearance of tangerines based on their bright green and glossy peel as important indicators of fruit quality. As shown in Fig. 9A, after 3 d of storage, the uncoated group was the first to change color from green to yellow, and the fruit became slightly wilted after 15 d. This appearance reflects natural ripening and moisture loss. Similarly, the BP- and BPAZ-coated groups began changing color starting from the sixth day of storage. By the 15th day, tangerines coated with the BP film turned dark yellow and showed signs of wilting. In contrast, tangerines coated with the BPAZ films still retained their green-yellow appearance and glossy peel even after 15 d of storage. As such, the coating with the BPAZ films offered effectiveness in preserving fruit quality.

Photographs (A), weight loss (B), pH (C), ascorbic acid content (D), and TSS content (E) of Vietnamese tangerines treated with uncoated, BP, and BPAZ films during storage.

Because postharvest weight loss reflects the level of water evaporation and nutrient degradation, this parameter is significant for assessing fruit freshness and storability. As shown in Fig. 9B, the weight of tangerines gradually decreased during storage over periods. However, the uncoated group exhibited a higher rate of weight loss compared with the BP- and BPAZ-coated groups. Thus, the coatings effectively delayed water loss and extended the fruits' shelf life. Radi et al. also observed that weight loss increased with prolonged storage.47 Similarly, Ruan et al. found that the largest loss occurred in the control group (uncoated sample).48

Changes in pH values of tangerines for each group are presented in Fig. 9C. In the BPAZ film group, the pH increased significantly during the initial stage, followed by a slight rise from the 6th to 12th day. After 15 d of storage, pH of tangerines showed the lowest value among all groups. This suggests a minor change in compositions (e.g., vitamin C) of tangerines. Previously, the film prepared by incorporating g-C3N4 and graphene oxide into the konjac glucomannan matrix gave the same result in tangerine preservation.49

Ascorbic acid (AA) is a natural antioxidant that protects fruits from oxidative stress and preserves their physical integrity and quality during storage. As shown in Fig. 9D, the AA content of Vietnamese tangerines initially increased and then gradually decreased. Compared with uncoated fruits, Vietnamese tangerines treated with the BPAZ film exhibited the highest AA content after 15 d at room temperature. Similarly, Xu et al. observed this trend of tangerines coated with chitosan/montmorillonite composite films.50

Total soluble solids (TSSs) contain water-soluble constituents such as sugars, organic acids, vitamins, and minerals in tangerines, and hence, TSS content is a critical indicator of nutritional quality. As illustrated in Fig. 9E, the TSS of uncoated tangerines increased progressively during storage. By contrast, BP- and BPAZ-coated samples exhibited slight fluctuations around the third and sixth days at room temperature. After 15 d, BPAZ-coated tangerines displayed the lowest TSS, followed by BP-coated and uncoated fruits. These findings suggest that the BPAZ coating effectively retains more stable TSS levels throughout the storage period.

Conclusion

In this study, films were developed by blending BC with PVA and incorporating Ag/ZnO nanoparticles to enhance the shelf life of Vietnamese tangerines. The results revealed that incorporation of Ag/ZnO nanoparticles did not alter the original structure of BP films but led to reductions in moisture content, water solubility, swelling ratio, and water vapor permeability. Importantly, this incorporation led to a significant improvement in the mechanical strength and water contact angle. The BPAZ film containing 0.20 wt% Ag/ZnO NPs exhibited excellent antibacterial activity against E. coli, B. cereus, S. typhi, and S. aureus and the strongest antioxidant activity. This film also preserved Vietnamese tangerines for up to 15 d at room temperature. Therefore, the BPAZ film can be used as an active packaging material to extend the shelf life and maintain the quality of fresh fruits.
