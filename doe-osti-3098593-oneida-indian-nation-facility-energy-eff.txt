1.0 Executive Summary
The Oneida Indian Nation (“Oneida”) implemented a comprehensive energy efficiency project across
nearly 30 facilities over the course of the project period. Grounded in findings from a 2018 energy
audit, the project installed LED lighting, high-efficiency HVAC systems, programmable thermostats,
variable speed drives, and kitchen exhaust controls across 28 Nation facilities and the Turning Stone
Resort and Casino (TSRC) campus.

All energy efficiency measures were installed and operational by December 31, 2024. The project
received a no-cost extension to December 31, 2025, ensuring all systems achieved the required 12-
month verification period. Energy savings were tracked using B3 Benchmarking software throughout
the verification period.

Cumulative savings documented across the full verification period (2023 Q1 through 2025 Q4) reflect
the ongoing performance of the installed measures. Monthly and quarterly variations were observed
due to weather patterns, changes in facility usage and occupancy, and fluctuations in energy prices.
These variations are consistent with the nature of energy efficiency projects and do not diminish the
documented performance of the implemented measures.

This project strengthens the Nation’s energy infrastructure, reduces operational costs, and advances
the Nation’s commitment to environmental stewardship and sustainable development.

2.0 Background
The Oneida Indian Nation is a federally recognized sovereign nation in central New York State. Oneida
owns and operates four casinos/resorts, a chain of gas stations and convenience stores, full-service
marinas, and other enterprises. Together with Nation government services, these businesses employ
more than 4,000 year-round and approximately 200 seasonal workers, making Oneida Indian Nation
the single largest employer in Oneida and Madison Counties.

Committed to sustainable development, Oneida prioritizes pollution prevention, waste reduction,
energy conservation, and preservation of its cultural and natural heritage.

Energy Audit Foundation

Oneida Indian Nation hired a consultant to establish baseline energy use throughout Nation-owned
buildings. Comprehensive ASHRAE Level II energy audits were completed in 2018, analyzing 40
buildings using B3 Benchmarking software. An Energy Usage Intensity (EUI) figure was calculated for
each building, combining electricity, natural gas, and steam data into a single comparable metric. Of
the 40 buildings analyzed, 28 had actual EUIs higher than benchmark comparison values. Multiple
energy efficiency measures (EEMs) were identified and ranked by Savings-to-Investment Ratio (SIR) for
prioritization.

A supplemental audit was conducted in April 2019 for the TSRC campus, which comprises 12 facilities
metered as a single unit. This audit identified additional EEM opportunities including parking lot
lighting, chiller variable speed drives, and kitchen exhaust hood controls.

The EEMs selected for this project were identified as the most impactful from an energy efficiency
standpoint and the most cost-effective, with the most favorable payback periods.

3.0 Project Objectives
The project aimed to implement energy efficiency measures across 28 facilities to reduce energy
consumption, lower operational costs, and decrease greenhouse gas emissions. Projected annual
targets established at project inception included cost savings exceeding $450,000, energy reduction of
over 4 million kWh and 50,000 therms, and greenhouse gas emissions reduction exceeding 3,000
metric tons CO₂-equivalent.

The project was organized into six tasks:

Task 1 — Project Start-Up: Establish the project team, define roles and responsibilities, and review
procurement policies and procedures.

Task 2 — Secure Equipment and Vendors: Complete equipment specifications, issue requests for
proposals, select qualified vendors, and execute contracts.

Task 3 — Install Nation Building EEMs: Implement LED lighting, programmable thermostats, and HVAC
upgrades across 28 Nation facilities.

Task 4 — Install TSRC EEMs: Install LED parking lot lighting, variable speed drives for chiller
optimization, and kitchen exhaust hood controls at TSRC.

Task 5 — Verification of Energy and Cost Savings: Monitor and verify energy savings for a minimum of
12 months using B3 Benchmarking software and utility billing data.

Task 6 — Project Monitoring and Reporting: Provide regular updates to DOE, attend program review
meetings, and submit required quarterly and final reports.

4.0 Description of Activities Performed
The project was implemented from 2020 through 2025. While the COVID-19 pandemic created
significant challenges — including facility closures, workforce furloughs, supply chain disruptions, cost
increases, and contractor shortages — the Oneida Indian Nation successfully completed all planned
energy efficiency measures.

Equipment Procurement and Vendor Selection (2020–2021)

Oneida Indian Nation completed equipment specifications and issued requests for proposals for all
project components. Pandemic-related cost increases of 10–15% above pre-pandemic levels affected
procurement. Contractor availability was further constrained by labor shortages and business closures
during the pandemic period. DOE approved a cost share reduction to 25% for expenditures made after
March 1, 2020, providing critical relief. All necessary vendor contracts were executed by the end of
2021.

TSRC Campus Implementation (2021–2022)

TSRC EEM installations included 29 LED parking lot light poles with new fixtures, variable speed drives
for chiller optimization, and demand-controlled kitchen exhaust hood controls. Pre-installation work
included repair of damaged wiring connecting the light poles and fixtures, which was discovered during
the project preparation phase. All TSRC measures were installed and operational by December 31,
2022, with verification initiated January 1, 2023.

Nation Facilities Implementation (2021–2024)

Installation of interior and exterior LED lighting, programmable thermostats, and HVAC upgrades
across 28 Nation facilities proceeded in phases over multiple years. During the course of
implementation, facilities staff identified that some HVAC units originally targeted for replacement had
already been replaced under the Nation’s standard 20-year equipment refresh schedule and were not
due for upgrade. The Director of Facilities and staff reassessed all HVAC units and identified eleven
units due for replacement with higher-efficiency models. All LED lighting installations were complete
across all 28 facilities by June 30, 2024. HVAC unit procurement and installation was completed by
December 31, 2024.

Energy Savings Verification (2023–2025)

The Nation used B3 Benchmarking software to track energy consumption across all facilities.
Verification began January 1, 2023, for completed TSRC projects, with Nation facilities added to the
tracking system as installations were completed. Because final EEMs were installed near the original
project end date, the Nation requested and received a no-cost extension to December 31, 2025,
ensuring all systems achieved the required 12-month monitoring period.

Technologies Deployed

LED lighting (interior and exterior) was installed across all 28 Nation facilities and TSRC parking areas.
Variable speed drives were installed on TSRC chiller systems. Programmable thermostats were
deployed for optimized HVAC scheduling across Nation facilities. Eleven high-efficiency HVAC units
were installed at various Nation facilities. Demand-controlled kitchen exhaust systems were installed at

Impact on the Oneida Indian Nations’ Energy and Sustainability Goals

Economic Sustainability: Documented cost savings reduce operational expenses across Oneida’s
facilities and strengthen the Nation’s economic position. The installed measures are expected to
continue delivering cost reductions on an ongoing basis.

Environmental Stewardship: The documented reduction in greenhouse gas emissions reflects Oneida’s
commitment to environmental protection on its ancestral homelands.

Energy Security: Reduced energy consumption decreases Oneida’s reliance on external energy sources
and reduces exposure to energy price fluctuations.

Infrastructure Modernization: Updated lighting, HVAC, and controls systems improve operational
reliability and are expected to reduce maintenance demands over their useful lives.

6.0 Lessons Learned
The COVID-19 pandemic was the most significant challenge encountered, causing facility closures,
workforce furloughs, supply chain disruptions, cost increases, and contractor shortages throughout the
implementation period.

Oneida, in partnership, with the Department of Energy, took various steps to mitigate the challenges
posed in order to stay within the project budget. For example, The COVID-19 pandemic impacted this
project in unanticipated ways. When facilities and operations began to re-open in 2020, costs for
supplies and equipment increased 10-15% above pre-pandemic levels, and delivery times have
increased and become more irregular. Contractors changed their work schedules to follow pandemic
guidelines that serve to minimize potential staff exposure to illness, adding to the length of time
needed to complete projects. This increase in project time reduced the number of contractors
available to bid on projects. However, in November 2020, the US Department of Energy Office of
Indian Energy implemented a formal process to consider cost share reduction requests intended to
alleviate the financial impacts of COVID-19. The Oneida Indian Nation submitted a request for a cost
share reduction which was approved in January 2021. The Nation was then able to restructure its
project budget to reflect the approved change to a 25% cost share on expenditures made after March
1, 2020, which greatly helped keep the overall project within budget.

As time went on and costs leveled out, Oneida continued to monitor cost impacts closely as contracts
and purchase orders were fulfilled and looked for opportunities to save on expenses. For example, in
preparation for the Turning Stone parking lot light project component, the Facilities Department
discovered significant damage to the wiring connecting the light poles and fixtures. This damage
required repair/replacement before the new light poles and fixtures could be installed, which added to
both the time and the costs related to this project component. To mitigate this issue, Oneida
reevaluated the condition of the light poles and determined that the number of poles to be completely
replaced could be reduced to 29. This decreased the costs associated with the poles and increased the
costs for electrical work, but did not impact the total project cost significantly.

Internal and external staffing issues exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic also presented challenges,
but project managers adequately managed those challenges through adjusting project timelines,
shifting internal resources and working with the Department of Energy to obtain no-cost-extensions so
that the project could continue within budget.

Due to historical challenges that occurred throughout the grant period (e.g., COVID-19 delays, staffing
shortages, supply chain issues), in order to obtain a full year of verification, Oneida worked with the
Department of Energy to obtain no-cost-extensions through December 31, 2025. This allowed Oneida
to determine and realize its objectives of reducing energy consumption and greenhouse gas emissions
across nearly 30 facilities as planned.
