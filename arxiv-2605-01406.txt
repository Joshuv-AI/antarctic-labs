Special solutions of q q -Heun equation by q q -integral transformations
Ayaka Murakami, Kouichi Takemura

Abstract
. We obtain special solutions of the q q -Heun equation which are expressed as finite summations of q q -hypergeometric functions. These solutions are obtained by considering the q q -integral transformations of the polynomial-type solutions.

1.
Introduction

The
q
-Heun equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\{a_{2}x^{2}+a_{1}x+a_{0}\}g(x/q)-\{b_{2}x^{2}+b_{1}x+b_{0}\}g(x)

\displaystyle+\{c_{2}x^{2}+c_{1}x+c_{0}\}g(xq)=0,\qquad a_{2}a_{0}c_{2}c_{0}\neq 0

was introduced by Hahn
[
3
]
in 1971.
As
q\to 1
, the
q
-Heun equation tends to Heun’s differential equation (see
[
3
,
15
]
).
Although special cases of equation (
1.1
) had appeared in some papers (e.g.
[
7
,
19
]
), it seems that the discovery of Hahn had been forgotten for a long time because the paper
[
3
]
had not been cited before 2016.
The
q
-Heun equation was rediscovered by the second author
[
15
]
through study of integrable systems, namely of degenerations of Ruijsenaars-van Diejen system
[
18
,
9
]
.
Let
T_{x}^{\pm 1}
be the
q
-shift operator defined by
T_{x}^{\pm 1}g(x)=g(q^{\pm 1}x)
and

(1.2)

\displaystyle A^{\langle 4\rangle}(x;h_{1},h_{2},l_{1},l_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta)

\displaystyle\;=x^{-1}(x-q^{h_{1}+1/2}t_{1})(x-q^{h_{2}+1/2}t_{2})T_{x}^{-1}+q^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}x^{-1}(x-q^{l_{1}-1/2}t_{1})(x-q^{l_{2}-1/2}t_{2})T_{x}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\{(q^{\alpha_{1}}+q^{\alpha_{2}})x+q^{(h_{1}+h_{2}+l_{1}+l_{2}+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})/2}(q^{\beta/2}+q^{-\beta/2})t_{1}t_{2}x^{-1}\}

be the operator which is obtained by degenerating the one variable Ruijsenaars-van Diejen operator four times.
Then, the
q
-Heun equation admits the expression as

(1.3)

\displaystyle A^{\langle 4\rangle}(x;h_{1},h_{2},l_{1},l_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta)g(x)=Eg(x),\quad\text{$E\in\mathbb{C}$}.

Here, the parameter
b_{1}
in equation (
1.1
) essentially corresponds to the eigenvalue
E
.
We regard the parameter
b_{1}
or the eigenvalue
E
as an accessory parameter, which is a
q
-analogue of the accessory parameter of Heun’s differential equation.

The Heun equation or Heun’s differential equation is a standard form of the second order linear differential equation with four regular singularities on the Riemann sphere, and it is written as

(1.4)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}^{2}y}{{\rm d}z^{2}}+\left(\frac{\gamma}{z}+\frac{\delta}{z-1}+\frac{\epsilon}{z-t}\right)\frac{{\rm d}y}{{\rm d}z}+\frac{\alpha\beta z-B}{z(z-1)(z-t)}y=0,

under the condition
\gamma+\delta+\epsilon=\alpha+\beta+1
.
The parameter
B
in equation (
1.4
) is called the accessory parameter, and it depends on the global structure of solutions.
Although it is difficult to calculate the global structure of solutions for each
B\in\mathbb{C}
, some special solutions are known for special values of the accessory parameter
B
and the other parameters.
In particular, there exist special solutions to equation (
1.4
) which are expressed as finite summation of the hypergeometric functions (see
[
14
]
), and they are related to integral transformations of solutions of Heun’s differential equation.
Kazakov and Slavyanov
[
5
]
essentially obtained the integral transformation as follows.
Let
v(w)
be a solution of Heun’s differential equation in the form

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}^{2}v}{{\rm d}w^{2}}+\left(\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{w}+\frac{\delta^{\prime}}{w-1}+\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{w-t}\right)\frac{{\rm d}v}{{\rm d}w}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}w-B^{\prime}}{w(w-1)(w-t)}v=0,

and let
C
be a suitable integral path in the
w
-plane.
Then, the function

\displaystyle y(z)=\int_{C}v(w)(z-w)^{-\alpha}{\rm d}w

is a solution to equation (
1.4
), if the parameters satisfy

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma+1-\alpha,\qquad\delta^{\prime}=\delta+1-\alpha,\qquad\epsilon^{\prime}=\epsilon+1-\alpha,\qquad\alpha^{\prime}=2-\alpha,

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}=-\alpha+\beta+1,\qquad B^{\prime}=B+(1-\alpha)(\epsilon+\delta t+(\gamma-\alpha)(t+1)).

Note that the integral transformation of the Heun equation was also derived by the middle convolution
[
12
,
13
]
where the integral path was taken as the Pochhammer contours, and the integral transformation was applied to obtain the special solutions of the Heun equation which are expressed as finite summations of the hypergeometric equation
[
14
]
.

A formal
q
-integral transformation of the
q
-Heun equation was found in
[
10
]
through the study of the
q
-middle convolution on the linear
q
-difference equations which are related to the
q
-Painlevé VI.
A rigorous form of the
q
-integral transformation of the
q
-Heun equation was formulated by developing a theory of the kernel function identity in
[
17
]
, which we describe in Theorem
2.2
.

It is known that the degenerated Ruijsenaars-van Diejen operator in equation (
1.2
) preserves a finite-dimensional space if the parameters satisfies a relation
[
16
]
, and it is related to the polynomial-type solutions of the
q
-Heun equations
[
6
]
.
On the other hand, the
q
-Heun equation may have a hypergeometric solution, if the parameters are special.
The
{}_{r}\phi_{r-1}

\displaystyle{}_{r}\phi_{r-1}\biggl(\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}a_{1},\cdots,a_{r}\\
b_{1},\cdots,b_{r-1}\end{array};q,z\biggr)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1};q)_{n}\cdots(a_{r};q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}(b_{1};q)_{n}\cdots(b_{r-1};q)_{n}}z^{n}.

It is well known that the basic hypergeometric function

satisfies the basic (or the

whose expression is simpler than the

-Heun equation (

The variant of the

\displaystyle(x-q^{h_{1}+1/2}t_{1})(x-q^{h_{2}+1/2}t_{2})g(x/q)+q^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}(x-q^{l_{1}-1/2}t_{1})(x-q^{l_{2}-1/2}t_{2})g(qx)

\displaystyle-[(q^{\alpha_{1}}+q^{\alpha_{2}})x^{2}+Ex+p(q^{1/2}+q^{-1/2})t_{1}t_{2}]g(x)=0,

\displaystyle p=q^{(h_{1}+h_{2}+l_{1}+l_{2}+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})/2},\quad E=-p\{(q^{-h_{2}}+q^{-l_{2}})t_{1}+(q^{-h_{1}}+q^{-l_{1}})t_{2}\}.

This equation is obtained by restricting the parameters of the

-Heun equation in the form of equation (

It was established in

that the following functions

\displaystyle\quad\times{}_{3}\phi_{2}\biggl(\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}q^{\lambda_{1}-h_{1}+l_{1}+\alpha_{1}},q^{\lambda_{1}-h_{1}+l_{2}+\alpha_{1}}t_{2}/t_{1},q^{-h_{1}+1/2}x/t_{1}\\

\displaystyle h_{2}(x)=x^{\lambda_{1}}\frac{(q^{\lambda_{1}-h_{2}+\alpha_{1}+1/2}x/t_{2};q)_{\infty}}{(q^{-h_{2}+1/2}x/t_{2};q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad\times{}_{3}\phi_{2}\biggl(\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}q^{\lambda_{1}-h_{2}+l_{1}+\alpha_{1}}t_{1}/t_{2},q^{\lambda_{1}-h_{2}+l_{2}+\alpha_{1}},q^{-h_{2}+1/2}x/t_{2}\\

2.
q
-integral transformation and gauge-transformation

2.1.
Preliminaries

The
q
-shifted factorials are defined by

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}:=\begin{cases}1,&n=0,\\
(1-a)(1-aq)\cdots(1-aq^{n-1}),&n=1,2,\cdots,\\
1/(aq^{n};q)_{-n},&n=-1,-2,\cdots,\end{cases}

\displaystyle(a;q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{k}).

We have

(2.1)

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}=\frac{(a;q)_{\infty}}{(aq^{n};q)_{\infty}},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q;q)_{k}}=0,\qquad\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}_{<0}.

We use the notation such that

\displaystyle(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m};q)_{n}=(a_{1};q)_{n}\cdots(a_{m};q)_{n},\quad(n\in\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}).

We adopt the definition of the theta function as

(2.3)

\displaystyle\vartheta_{q}(t):=(t,q/t,q;q)_{\infty}.

Then, we have

Lemma 2.1
.

If
K
is an integer, then

\displaystyle\frac{\vartheta_{q}\left(q^{K}t\right)}{\vartheta_{q}\left(q^{K}s\right)}=\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{K}\frac{\vartheta_{q}\left(t\right)}{\vartheta_{q}\left(s\right)}.

The summation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\xi\infty}f(s){\rm{d}}_{q}s=(1-q)\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n}\xi f(q^{n}\xi)

is called the Jackson integral, where
\xi\neq 0
.
The usual integration on
(0,+\infty)
is obtained by the limit
q\to 1
.

2.2.

We recall

Theorem 2.2

(
[
17

,
q
-integral transformations of

Assume that the function

\displaystyle A^{\langle 4\rangle}(s;h^{\prime}_{1},h^{\prime}_{2},l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2},\alpha^{\prime}_{1},\alpha^{\prime}_{2},\beta^{\prime})h(s)=E^{\prime}h(s)

and

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{L\to+\infty}\frac{h(s)}{s^{(h^{\prime}_{1}+h^{\prime}_{2}-l^{\prime}_{1}-l^{\prime}_{2}-\alpha^{\prime}_{1}-\alpha^{\prime}_{2}+\beta^{\prime}+2)/2}}\big|_{s=q^{L}\xi}=C_{1},\;\lim_{K\to-\infty}\frac{h(s)}{s^{-\alpha^{\prime}_{1}}}\big|_{s=q^{K}\xi}=C_{2}

for some constants

, and the variable
\xi
is independent of the variable

or it is proportional to

(i. e.

where

is independent of

Let

,

be the parameters which satisfy

(2.5)

\displaystyle\chi=(l^{\prime}_{1}+l^{\prime}_{2}-h^{\prime}_{1}-h^{\prime}_{2}-\alpha^{\prime}_{1}+\alpha^{\prime}_{2}-\beta^{\prime})/2,\quad\mu=\mu_{0}+1+\chi,

\displaystyle E=q^{\mu_{0}+\alpha_{1}-\alpha^{\prime}_{1}}E^{\prime},\quad\beta=-\beta^{\prime}-\chi,\quad\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{1}-\alpha^{\prime}_{1}+\alpha^{\prime}_{2}-\chi,

\displaystyle l_{i}=l^{\prime}_{i}+\mu_{0},\quad h_{i}=h^{\prime}_{i}+\mu_{0}+\chi,\quad i=1,2.

Set

\displaystyle P_{\mu,\mu_{0}}^{(1)}(x,s):=\frac{(q^{\mu}s/x;q)_{\infty}}{(q^{\mu_{0}}s/x;q)_{\infty}},\;P_{\mu,\mu_{0}}^{(2)}(x,s):=\left(\frac{x}{s}\right)^{\mu-\mu_{0}}\frac{(q^{-\mu_{0}+1}x/s;q)_{\infty}}{(q^{-\mu+1}x/s;q)_{\infty}}.

(i) The Jackson integral

(2.6)

\displaystyle g(x)=x^{-\alpha_{1}}\int^{\xi\infty}_{0}s^{-(h^{\prime}_{1}+h^{\prime}_{2}-l^{\prime}_{1}-l^{\prime}_{2}-\alpha^{\prime}_{1}-\alpha^{\prime}_{2}+\beta^{\prime}+2)/2}h(s)P^{(1)}_{\mu,\mu_{0}}(x,s)\,d_{q}s

(2.7)

\displaystyle A^{\langle 4\rangle}(x;h_{1},h_{2},l_{1},l_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta)g(x)=Eg(x)+(1-q)(k_{2}(x)-k_{1}(x)),

where

\displaystyle k_{1}(x)=C_{1}x^{-\alpha_{1}}q^{\mu_{0}+\alpha_{1}+h^{\prime}_{1}+h^{\prime}_{2}+\chi}(q^{\beta^{\prime}}-1)t_{1}t_{2},

\displaystyle k_{2}(x)=C_{2}x^{-\alpha_{1}}\frac{\vartheta_{q}(q^{-\mu_{0}-\chi}x/\xi)}{\vartheta_{q}(q^{-\mu_{0}+1}x/\xi)}\xi^{\chi+1}q^{\mu_{0}+\alpha_{1}}(q^{\alpha^{\prime}_{2}-\alpha^{\prime}_{1}}-1).

(ii) The Jackson integral

(2.8)

\displaystyle g(x)=x^{-\alpha_{1}}\int_{0}^{\xi\infty}s^{-(h_{1}^{\prime}+h_{2}^{\prime}-l_{1}^{\prime}-l_{2}^{\prime}-\alpha_{1}^{\prime}-\alpha_{2}^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime}+2)/2}h(s)P_{\mu,\mu_{0}}^{(2)}(x,s){\rm{d}}_{q}s

converges and it satisfies

(2.9)

\displaystyle A^{\langle 4\rangle}(x;h_{1},h_{2},l_{1},l_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta)g(x)=Eg(x)+(1-q)(k_{2}(x)-k_{1}(x)),

where

\displaystyle k_{1}(x)=C_{1}x^{-\alpha_{1}+\chi+1}\xi^{-\chi-1}\frac{\vartheta_{q}\big(q^{-\mu_{0}+1}x/\xi\big)}{\vartheta_{q}(q^{-\mu_{0}-\chi}x/\xi)}q^{\mu_{0}+\alpha_{1}+h^{\prime}_{1}+h^{\prime}_{2}+\chi}\big(q^{\beta^{\prime}}-1\big)t_{1}t_{2},

\displaystyle k_{2}(x)=C_{2}x^{-\alpha_{1}+\chi+1}q^{\mu_{0}+\alpha_{1}}\big(q^{\alpha^{\prime}_{2}-\alpha^{\prime}_{1}}-1\big).

Note that (
2.5
) is equivalent to

(2.10)

\displaystyle\chi=(h_{1}+h_{2}-l_{1}-l_{2}+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}-\beta)/2,\quad\mu=\mu_{0}+1+\chi,

\displaystyle E^{\prime}=q^{-\mu_{0}+\alpha^{\prime}_{1}-\alpha_{1}}E,\quad\beta^{\prime}=-\beta-\chi,\quad\alpha^{\prime}_{2}=\alpha^{\prime}_{1}-\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\chi,

\displaystyle l^{\prime}_{i}=l_{i}-\mu_{0},\quad h^{\prime}_{i}=h_{i}-\mu_{0}-\chi,\quad i=1,2

In the theorem, if

, then equations (

\displaystyle A^{\langle 4\rangle}(x;h_{1},h_{2},l_{1},l_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta)g(x)=Eg(x),

and we obtain the

We give comments on the

The

Inversely, degenerations of the
