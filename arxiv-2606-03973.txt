Abstract
. We show that the lower bound in the majorizing measures theorem holds for a large class of random vectors. Specifically, suppose X ∼ μ X\sim\mu is a centered random vector in 𝐑 n \mathbf{R}^{n} with C KL ​ ( μ ) = sup θ , η ∈ 𝐑 n θ ≠ η KL ( μ θ ∥ μ η ) ‖ θ − η ‖ 2 2 < ∞ , C_{\rm KL}(\mu)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta,\eta\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\\ \theta\neq\eta\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{KL}(\mu_{\theta}\;\|\;\mu_{\eta})}{\|\theta-\eta\|_{2}^{2}}<\infty, where μ θ \mu_{\theta} denotes the law of the translate θ + X \theta+X . Then, for every nonempty, bounded T ⊂ 𝐑 n T\subset\mathbf{R}^{n} , C KL ​ ( μ ) ​ 𝐄 μ [ sup t ∈ T ⟨ X , t ⟩ ] ≳ γ 2 ​ ( T ) , \sqrt{C_{\rm KL}(\mu)}\,\operatorname*{\mathbf{E}}\nolimits_{\mu}\Big[\sup_{t\in T}\,\langle X,t\rangle\Big]\gtrsim\gamma_{2}(T), where the righthand side denotes Talagrand’s generic chaining functional. This result recovers, as a special case, the lower bound in the majorizing measures theorem for centered Gaussian processes. Our argument critically relies on the rate-distortion integral, recently introduced by J. Liu [ 3 ] .

The conclusion follows from Liu’s data processing estimate.
Specifically, for

c_{1}(a)=\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}-1}}{a^{2}},\quad c_{2}(a)=\sqrt{\frac{a^{2}}{a^{2}-1}\,h\Big(\frac{1}{a^{2}}\Big)},\quad\mbox{and}\quad h(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p).

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi_{m}(t,\alpha)\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\geqslant 2\,\sup_{a>1}c_{1}(a)\Big\{I_{m}(t)-c_{2}(a)\diam(F)\Big\},

Moreover, it holds that

.
Indeed, pick

. For

We have

2\max\{I_{m}(s),I_{m}(t)\}\geqslant\int_{0}^{\tfrac{\diam(F)}{2}}\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{p(r)}}+\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{q(r)}}\,\,\mathrm{d}r\\
\geqslant\Big(\inf_{p\in[0,1]}\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{p}}+\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{1-p}}\Big)\,\frac{\diam(F)}{2}\geqslant\sqrt{\log 2}\,\diam(F),

as the infimum is attained at
