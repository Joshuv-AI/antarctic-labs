 Overview

This report is to summarize the proceedings of the conference “Novel Quantum States of Matter
in Moiré Materials,” which was held at the Aspen Center for Physics, March 12 – 17, 2023.
The conference organizers thank the Department of Energy (DOE) for providing funding support
for the conference.

The advent of moiré quantum materials has opened a highly tunable platform for exploring the
interplay between electronic structure, interactions, symmetry, and topology. Since the discovery
of correlated insulator states and superconductivity in magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene
(MATBG), a growing variety of moiré systems have emerged, revealing novel correlated and
topological phenomena such as quantum anomalous Hall systems, generalized Wigner crystals,
and fractional Chern insulators. This rich phenomenology continues to attract intense theoretical
and experimental interest.

The conference successfully brought together a vibrant community of researchers working on
moiré materials. With a well-balanced mix of experimentalists and theorists, the event featured
high-quality invited and contributed talks that addressed the most pressing questions in the field.
Topics included the nature of superconductivity in twisted bilayer graphene, the extension of
Chern insulating phases to zero field, the potential for topological superconductivity in twisted
systems, insights into Kondo physics, and the realization of fractional Chern insulators in twisted
transition metal dichalcogenides.

The conference also included dedicated sessions for breaking news and emerging results, which
ensured that participants were exposed to the latest developments. Funding support was effectively
used to enable the participation of early career researchers, including those from underrepresented
groups, fostering a supportive and collaborative environment. Overall, the conference achieved its
goals of advancing scientific dialogue, promoting interdisciplinary exchange, and catalyzing future
research directions in the field of moiré quantum materials.

 Conference Summary and Outcomes
The Aspen Winter Conference on moiré materials was successfully held at the Aspen Center for
Physics (ACP), bringing together a distinguished group of researchers from around the world. The
conference provided a timely and dynamic forum for presenting cutting-edge research and
fostering collaboration in the rapidly evolving field of moiré quantum materials.

Conference Format
 Meetings were held in the mornings and afternoons with about four hours off in the
afternoon. Discussion, moderated by the session leader, followed the talks. The talks were
organized into thematically integrated sessions. Each session began with the Session Chair
introducing the topic and connecting it to the broader themes of the conference. Sessions were
comprised of three to four invited talks, each lasting for 25 minutes and with 5-10 additional
minutes allocated for discussions. Due to the large number of interested poster presenters, we
hosted two poster sessions, and added on two sessions of “lightning talks” for poster presenters to
present their research. We also hosted a “Physics Café” featuring two senior scientists who
engaged with the public to provide an overview of the workshop topics, have a Q&A with
attendees, and provide an overview of the Public Lecture that immediately followed the “Physics
Café.” The Public Lecture featured a talk by Prof. Pablo Jarillo-Herrero (MIT) on the discovery of
magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene (MATBG).

Conference Themes and Engagement
  The conference was organized around five major themes:

 • Graphene-based moiré systems: Talks explored multilayer structures, twist angle
 disorder, and lattice relaxation effects, offering new perspectives on correlated phases.
 • TMD moiré systems: Presentations revealed the rich phase space of TMD
 heterostructures, including bandwidth-tuned Mott transitions and Wigner crystals, while
 addressing the intriguing absence of superconductivity.
 • Superconductivity: Discussions focused on the robustness of superconductivity in
 multilayer graphene and its enhancement in TBG on TMD substrates, as well as the
 potential of twisted BSCCO heterostructures for topological superconductivity.
 • Topological phases and magnetism: The quantized anomalous Hall effect and orbital-
 driven magnetism were key topics, with new data and theoretical interpretations shared.

 • Theory: Theoretical contributions spanned predictions of flat bands, modeling of
 correlated phases, and exploration of optimal conditions for fractional Chern insulators.

Scientific Highlights and Achievements
 The conference showcased the remarkable expansion of the field beyond graphene-based
bilayer systems into multilayer graphene and transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs), with
robust discussions on superconductivity, correlated insulators, and topological phases. Invited
talks and contributed presentations addressed key open questions, including the pairing
mechanisms in twisted bilayer graphene (TBG), the role of spin-orbit coupling in TMDs, and the
emergence of one-dimensional moiré structures exhibiting Luttinger liquid behavior.
 Theoretical and experimental synergy was a central theme throughout the conference.
Presentations highlighted how theoretical models — from simplified frameworks to advanced ab
initio and many-body calculations — continue to guide experimental efforts and vice versa.
Notably, discussions on fragile topology, Berry curvature distribution, and quantum metric in flat
bands provided fresh insights into the conditions for realizing fractional Chern insulators and other
exotic phases.

Speaker and Attendee Recruitment and Support
 We identified highly qualified female key speakers as well as other female key participants
(note: two of the four conference organizers are female.). We advertised the conference broadly to
attract a deep pool of participants, including junior scientists. All participants were invited to
present a poster. We successfully obtaining external funding (in addition to the DOE) to help pay
the costs of junior attendees.

Impact
 The conference successfully met its goal of fostering interdisciplinary exchange and
collaboration. The ACP’s welcoming environment facilitated informal discussions and small-
group interactions that sparked new ideas and potential research partnerships. Funding support was
effectively used to enable participation by early career researchers and individuals from
underrepresented groups, enhancing the breadth of the event. Over the five-day conference, there
were:
 • 89 workshop participants (speakers and attendees)
 • 24 speakers
 • Two poster sessions featuring 33 presenters
 • Two “lightning talks” events featuring poster presenters
 • Two public events “Physics Café” and a Public Lecture

Funding Support Summary
 The DOE funding supported 17 participants (9 postdoctoral researchers and 8 graduate
students) to attend the conference. 10 of the DOE-funded participants requested to be a poster

presenter. Applicants to the conference indicated their financial need and the organizers and an
ACP committee determined the awards. The registration fee was $500 per attendee, which the
Aspen Center for Physics used to cover the overhead costs of the facility (heating, access to
internet, logistic support). Participants receiving funding support were required to travel on
economy class tickets. Support was capped in order to encourage participants to limit their travel
expenses. All attendees were required to attend for the full meeting period.

Conclusion
 The Aspen Winter Conference on moiré materials achieved its objectives of highlighting
recent developments, promoting cross-disciplinary dialogue, and catalyzing future research
directions. The depth and breadth of the speaker lineup, combined with the collaborative
atmosphere of the ACP, made this a landmark event in the field of moiré quantum materials.
