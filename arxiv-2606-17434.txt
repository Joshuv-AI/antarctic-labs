1.
Introduction and main results

Let
L=-\Delta+V
be a Schrödinger operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
. Throughout this paper,
we assume that the potential
V
is nonnegative and belongs to the reverse Hölder class
RH_{q}
for some
q>n/2
, that is, there exists a constant
C>0

such that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}V(x)^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}\leq C\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}V(x)dx

holds for every ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Following
[
19
]
, we define the critical radius function
\rho(\cdot)
related to
L
by

\displaystyle\rho(x):=\sup\left\{r>0:\frac{r^{2}}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}V(y)dy\leq 1\right\},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The Lipschitz spaces
\Lambda^{\alpha}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
adapted to

L
were first introduced by Bongioanni, Harboure and Salinas
[
1
]
in the case
0<\alpha<1
via first difference,
and then extended by De León-Contreras and Torrea
[
5
]
to the case
0<\alpha<2
via second difference.

Definition 1.1
.

(cf.
[
5
, Definition 1.1]
)
For
0<\alpha<2
, the Lipschitz space
\Lambda^{\alpha}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
adapted to
L
is defined as
the space of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\Lambda^{\alpha}_{L}}:=\|\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}f(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\|f(\cdot+z)+f(\cdot-z)-2f(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{|z|^{\alpha}}<\infty.

Remark 1.2
.

(i)

When
V\equiv 0
,
we have
\rho\equiv+\infty
and hence
\|\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}f(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}=0
,
so the Lipschitz space
\Lambda_{-\Delta}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(
0<\alpha<2
) adapted to
the Laplacian
-\Delta
coincides with
the classical homogeneous Lipschitz space
\dot{\Lambda}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is defined as the space of all measurable functions

f
such that

\|f\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{\alpha}}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\|f(\cdot+z)+f(\cdot-z)-2f(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{|z|^{\alpha}}<\infty.

(ii)

When
V\equiv 1
, we have
\rho\equiv 1

and hence
\|f(\cdot)\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
, so
the Lipschitz space
\Lambda_{-\Delta+\operatorname{Id}}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(
0<\alpha<2
) adapted to
the operator
-\Delta+\operatorname{Id}
(where
\operatorname{Id}
is the identity operator) coincides with
the classical inhomogeneous Lipschitz space
\Lambda^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is defined as the space of all measurable functions
f

such that

\|f\|_{{\Lambda}^{\alpha}}:=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\|f(\cdot+z)+f(\cdot-z)-2f(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{|z|^{\alpha}}<\infty.

The Lipschitz spaces
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
adapted to
L
have been analyzed by several authors.
Bongioanni
et al.
in
[
1
]
first introduced the space
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for
0<\alpha<1
, via the norm

\|f\|_{\Lambda^{\alpha}_{L}}:=\|\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}f(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\|f(\cdot+z)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{|z|^{\alpha}},

and showed that this space coincides with the space
BMO_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is
defined as the space of locally integrable functions such that

\int_{B}\left|f(x)-\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}f(y)dy\right|dx\leq C|B|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{n}}\quad\text{for every ball }B=B(x_{B},r_{B}),

and

\int_{B}|f(x)|dx\leq C|B|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{n}}\quad\text{if }r_{B}\geq\rho(x_{B}).

They also study the behavior of the fractional integral
L^{-\beta/2}
on
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
0<\alpha<1
,
\beta\geq 0

and
\alpha+\beta<\min\{1,2-(n/q)\}
.
Ma
et al.
in
[
18
]
prove a characterization of the space
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for
0<\alpha<1
, in
terms of Carleson measures and fractional derivatives of any order of the Poisson semigroup
e^{-t\sqrt{L}}
, and studied the behavior of the
fractional powers
L^{\beta/2}
(positive and negative) and multipliers of Laplace transform type on the space
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
De León-Contreras and Torrea
[
5
]
recently extended the
definition of
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
0<\alpha<2
via second difference (see Definition
1.1
), and
generalized the regularity results obtained in
[
18
]
.
In the case of the Hermite operator
H=-\Delta+|x|^{2}
, the associated Hölder spaces
C^{k,\alpha}_{H}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
,
were investigated by Stinga and Torrea
[
21
]
.

The purpose of the present paper is to provide
new characterizations of
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in terms of Littlewood-Paley-type decompositions
and Carleson measures, for
0<\alpha<2-(n/q)
. To state our results explicitly, we need to introduce some notions and terminologies.
First we give the definition of the test functions adapted to the operator
{L}
, which was first introduced by
Kerkyacharian and Petrushev
[
15
]
.

Definition 1.3
.

(cf.
[
15
, Section 5.2]
)
A function
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to a
test function adapted to
{L}
,
if
\phi\in\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{Dom}({L}^{m})
and for each
m,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{(m,\ell)}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|x|)^{\ell}|{L}^{m}\phi(x)|<\infty.

We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the set of all test functions adapted to
{L}
.

Endowed with the family
\{\|\cdot\|_{(m,\ell)}\}_{m,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of semi-norms,
\mathcal{S}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Frechét space;
see
[
15
, Proposition 5.3]
.
Elements in the topological dual of
\mathcal{S}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
are naturally considered as

distributions adapted to
{L}
.
The duality pairing between
f\in\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
g\in\mathcal{S}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
will be denoted by
(f,g)
.

Note that when
L=-\Delta
, the space
\mathcal{S}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(resp.
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) coincides
with the classical Schwartz function space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(resp. the classical tempered distribution space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
);
see
[
15
, Proposition 5.6]
.

For
f\in\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we define

{L}^{m}f
as an element in
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

({L}^{m}f,\phi)=(f,{L}^{m}\phi)\quad\text{for all }\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

We also need the notion of generalized polynomials adapted to
{L}
which are defined as follows.

Definition 1.4
.

(see
[
8
, Section 3.2]
) A distribution
P\in\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be a
generalized polynomial adapted to

L

if there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
{L}^{m}P=0
in
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The space of all generalized polynomials adapted to
L

is denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Since
V
is nonnegative and locally integrable on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
the Schrödinger operator
L=-\Delta+V
, originally defined on
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
extends to a densely defined nonnegative self-adjoint operator
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is also denoted by
L
.

Denote by
\{E(\lambda):\lambda\geq 0\}
the spectral resolution of
L
. For any Borel measurable function
\Phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, the “multiplier operator”
\Phi(\sqrt{L})

can be defined by the spectral theorem according to the prescription

\Phi(\sqrt{L})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(\sqrt{\lambda})dE(\lambda).

To recall some results concerning smooth functional calculus related to
L
, we introduce
the class
\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
as follows:

(1.2)

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}([0,\infty)):=\big\{\Phi\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty)):&\;\Phi\text{ can be extended }\\
&\text{ to an even Schwartz function on }\mathbb{R}\big\}.\end{split}

Observe that if
\Phi\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
and
\operatorname{supp}\Phi\subset[a,b]
for some
0<a<b<+\infty
,
then
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
.
An interesting result in
[
15
, Proposition 5.3]
is that if
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
,
then the operator
\Phi(\sqrt{L})
is an integral operator with a kernel
K_{\Phi(\sqrt{L})}(\cdot,\cdot)
such that

(1.3)

\begin{split}K_{\Phi(\sqrt{L})}(x,\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\ &\text{with }x\text{ fixed }\\
&\text{and }K_{\Phi(\sqrt{L})}(\cdot,y)\in\mathcal{S}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\ \text{with }y\text{ fixed}.\end{split}

This result holds for all (abstract) nonnegative self-adjoint operator whose heat kernel
obeys a pointwise Gaussian estimate, and in particular, this holds for Schrödinger operators with nonnegative locally integrable potentials
(see (
3.1
) in Section
3
).

Assuming that
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
, we continue discussing
properties of the operator
\Phi(\sqrt{L})
.
From
[
15
, Proposition 5.3]
we see that
\Phi(\sqrt{L})
maps continuously
\mathcal{S}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into

\mathcal{S}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Hence
\Phi(\sqrt{L})
extends to be a continuous operator

\Phi(\sqrt{{L}}):\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

in the following sense: for any
f\in\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\big(\Phi(\sqrt{{L}})f,\phi\big):=\big(f,\Phi(\sqrt{L})\phi\big),\quad\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Note that (cf.
[
8
, Proposition 3.2]
) the distribution
\Phi(\sqrt{{L}})f
defined above coincides with the function

(1.5)

\displaystyle\Phi(\sqrt{L})f(x)=\big(f,K_{\Phi(\sqrt{L})}(x,\cdot)\big),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Remark 1.5
.

Note that if
f\in\Lambda^{\alpha}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
f
can be regarded as a distribution
in
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Indeed, from Definition
1.1
we see that

\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a subspace of the classical homogeneous Lipschitz space
\dot{\Lambda}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and hence any function
f\in\Lambda^{\alpha}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

have at most polynomial growth at infinity. See

[
9
, Section 6.3.1]
.

Our first main result is the following

Theorem 1.6
.

Assume that
|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:V(x)>0\}|\neq 0
.
Let
0<\alpha<2-(n/q)
and let
\Phi\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
satisfy

(1.6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}\Phi\subset[1/2,2]\quad\text{and}\quad|\Phi(\lambda)|\geq c>0\ \text{ for }\ 3/5\leq\lambda\leq 5/3.

(i)

If
f\in\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{{L}})f\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty.

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for all
f\in\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{{L}})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|f\|_{\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}}.

(ii)

For a distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, if

(1.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{{L}})f\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty,

then there exists a generalized polynomial
P_{f}\in\mathcal{P}_{L}
such that

f-P_{f}\in\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Furthermore, there exists a constant
C^{\prime}
such that
for all
f\in\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\|f-P_{f}\|_{\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}}\leq C^{\prime}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{{L}})f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Remark 1.7
.

Littlewood-Paley characterization of the classical Lipschitz spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
was established
by Frazier, Jawerth and Weiss
[
7
]
and Triebel
[
22
, §2.6.1]
.
Lipschitz spaces on stratified groups were studied by Folland
[
6
]
and Krantz
[
16
]
, and
a characterization of Lipschitz spaces on stratified groups in terms of Littlewood-Paley decompositions
was proved by Hu
[
14
]
. Littlewood-Paley characterizations of some multi-parameter Lipschitz spaces
were established in
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

Now we turn to the Carleson measure characterization of
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
. The Carleson measure space
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}
is defined as the set
of all nonnegative Borel measures
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
such that

(1.8)

\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}:=\sup_{B}\frac{\mu(\widehat{B})}{|B|^{1+\frac{2\alpha}{n}}}<\infty,

where the supremum is over all balls
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

\widehat{B}:=\{(x,t):x\in B,\ 0<t<r_{B}\}
, with
r_{B}
being the radius of
B
.

For each
\gamma>0
, we denote

\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{f:f\text{ is measurable on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ and }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{n+\gamma}}dx<\infty\right\}.

We then set

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\bigcup_{\gamma>0}\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let
\Theta_{(k)}(\cdot)
be the function on
[0,\infty)
defined by

(1.9)

\displaystyle\Theta_{(k)}(\lambda):=\lambda^{2k}e^{-\lambda^{2}},\quad\lambda\in[0,\infty).

It is clear that
\Theta_{(k)}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
. Hence, for any
t>0
and any
f\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f
a well-defined distribution according to (
1.4
), and this
distribution coincides with a function given by (
1.5
).
In what follows, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we defined
the associated measure
d\mu_{(k,f)}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}:=\{(x,t):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t>0\}
by

(1.10)

\displaystyle d\mu_{(k,f)}:=\big|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f(x)\big|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}=\big|(t^{2}L)^{k}e^{-t^{2}L}f(x)\big|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}.

Then, according to (
1.8
),

(1.11)

\begin{split}\|d\mu_{(k,f)}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}&=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\atop B:\text{ ball}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|B|^{1+\frac{2\alpha}{n}}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{B}\big|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f(x)\big|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}.\end{split}

With the above notation, our second main result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.9
.

Assume that
|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:V(x)>0\}|\neq 0
. Let
0<\alpha<2-(n/q)
and
k\in\mathbb{N}

with
k>{\alpha}/{2}
. Then we have:

(i)

If
f\in\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
f\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
d\mu_{(k,f)}\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}
. Furthermore,
there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
f\in\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(1.12)

\displaystyle\big\|d\mu_{(k,f)}\big\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\leq C\|f\|_{\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}}^{2}.

(ii)

If
f\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
d\mu_{(k,f)}\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}
, then
f\in\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Furthermore, there exists a constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}}^{2}\leq C^{\prime}\big\|d\mu_{(k,f)}\big\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}.

Remark 1.10
.

As noted earlier,
Ma
et al.
in
[
18
]
obtained a characterization of the space
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for
0<\alpha<1
, in
terms of Carleson measures and fractional derivatives of any order of the Poisson semigroup; see
[
18
, Theorem 1.3]
. Their approach is
based on the following two facts (cf.
[
1
, Proposition 4]
and
[
18
, Theorem 4.5]
):

•

for
0<\alpha<1
,
\Lambda^{\alpha}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=BMO_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
;

•

for
0<\alpha<1
,
BMO_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
the dual of the Hardy space
H_{L}^{\frac{\alpha}{n+\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

However, for
\alpha\geq 1
,
the
H^{\frac{n}{n+\alpha}}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
–
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
duality is unclear so far.
Thus, to prove the Carleson measure characterization of
\Lambda^{\alpha}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
0<\alpha<2-(n/q)
, we
will use a different strategy, which relies on Calderón reproducing formulae adapted to the operator
L
proved
in
[
15
,
8
]
, the heat semigroup characterization of
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
established in
[
5
]
,
and suitable almost orthogonality estimates.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we collect some useful facts and tools, including
some properties of the critical radius function
\rho
, the inhomogeneous and homogeneous Calderón reproducing formulae adapted to the operator
L
,
and heat semigroup characterization of
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In Section
3
, we
present some useful kernel estimates, which play an important role in the proofs of main results. Section
4
and
Section
5
are devoted to the proofs of Theorem
1.6
and Theorem
1.9
, respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Properties of the critical radius function

We will use the following properties of the critical radius function
\rho
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(i)

There exist constants
0<\theta<1
and
C>0
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\rho(y)\leq C\rho(x)\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\right)^{\theta}.

(ii)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
\lambda>0
and for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

with
y\in B(x,\lambda\rho(x))
,

\displaystyle\rho(y)\leq C(1+\lambda)^{\theta}\rho(x).

Proof.

For (i), see
[
19
, Lemma 1.4 (c)]
. Assertion (ii) follows easily from (i).
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

The following two conditions are equivalent:

(i)

|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:V(x)>0\}|\neq 0
.

(ii)

\rho(x)<+\infty
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

First we show
\textrm{(i)}\Rightarrow\textrm{(ii)}
.
Denote
E:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:V(x)>0\}
. Since
|E|>0
,
there exists a ball
B(x_{0},r_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
|B(x_{0},r_{0})\cap E|>0
.
Using the fact that
V(y)>0
for all
y\in B(x_{0},r_{0})\cap E
, we have

\int_{B(x_{0},r_{0})}V(y)dy\geq\int_{B(x_{0},r_{0})\cap E}V(y)dy>0.

It follows that

\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{|B(x_{0},r_{0})|}\int_{B(x_{0},r_{0})}V(y)dy=:c_{0}>0.

From
[
19
, Lemma 1.2]
we see that for every
R\geq r_{0}
,

\displaystyle\frac{R^{2}}{|B(x_{0},R)|}\int_{B(x_{0},R)}V(y)dy\geq C\left(\frac{R}{r_{0}}\right)^{\delta}\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{|B(x_{0},r_{0})|}\int_{B(x_{0},r_{0})}V(y)dy\geq c_{0}C\left(\frac{R}{r_{0}}\right)^{\delta},

where
\delta:=2-(n/q)>0
.
Hence for sufficiently large
R
, we have

\frac{R^{2}}{|B(x_{0},R)|}\int_{B(x_{0},R)}V(y)dy>1.

This implies
\rho(x_{0})<+\infty
. It then follows from (i) of Lemma
2.1

that
\rho(x)<+\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Next we show
\textrm{(ii)}\Rightarrow\textrm{(i)}
. Assume, for the sake of a contradiction,
that
|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:V(x)>0\}|=0
, i.e.,
V(x)=0
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every
r>0
, we have

\frac{r^{2}}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}V(y)dy=0\leq 1.

This implies that
\rho(x)=+\infty
, which is a contradiction with (ii).
∎

2.2.
Calderón reproducing formulae

We record the inhomogeneous and homogeneous Calderón reproducing formulae adapted to the operator

L
, which will play an important role in our approach.

First we recall the inhomogeneous Calderón reproducing formula adapted to
L
. See
[
15
, Proposition 5.5]
).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\Phi_{0},\Phi\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
such that
\operatorname{supp}\Phi_{0}\subset[0,2]
,
\operatorname{supp}\Phi\subset[1/2,2]
, and

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}(\lambda)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Phi(2^{-j}\lambda)=1,\quad\forall\lambda\in[0,\infty).

Then for any
f\in\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle f=\Phi_{0}(\sqrt{L})f+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f

with convergence in
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

To introduce the homogeneous Calderón reproducing formula adapted to
L
, we need to work with the
factor space
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/\mathcal{P}_{L}
. For any sequence
\{f_{j}+\mathcal{P}_{L}\}_{j}\subset\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/\mathcal{P}_{L}
,

f_{j}+\mathcal{P}_{L}\rightarrow f+\mathcal{P}_{L}
if and only if there exist
\{P_{j}\}_{j}\subset\mathcal{P}_{L}

and
P\in\mathcal{P}_{L}

such that

f_{j}+P_{j}\rightarrow f+P\quad\text{in }\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

In the following, we will usually not explicitly distinguish between
f\in\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
its equivalence class
f+\mathcal{P}_{L}
, and we will occasionally write
f\in\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/\mathcal{P}_{L}
.

The following homogeneous Calderón reproducing formula adapted to
L
was established in
[
8
, Theorem 3.9]
).

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\Phi\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
such that
\operatorname{supp}\Phi\subset[1/2,2]
and

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi(2^{-j}\lambda)=1,\quad\forall\lambda\in(0,\infty).

Then for any
f\in\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f

with convergence in
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/\mathcal{P}_{L}
. More precisely,
there exist a sequence of generalized polynomials
\{P_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{P}_{L}

and
P\in\mathcal{P}_{L}
such that

\displaystyle f+P=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sum_{j=-k}^{\infty}\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f+P_{k}\right)\quad\text{in }\;\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

To construct Littlewood-Paley decompositions, we will use the following fact. For the proof, we refer to

[
13
, Lemma 3.6]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
\Phi_{0},\Phi\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
satisfy the following conditions:

\displaystyle|\Phi_{0}(\lambda)|>0\text{ on }\{0\leq\lambda<2\varepsilon\}\quad\text{and}\quad|\Phi(\lambda)|>0\text{ on }\{\varepsilon/2<\lambda<2\varepsilon\}

for some
\varepsilon>0
. Then we have:

(i)

There exist
\Psi_{0},\Psi\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
such that
\operatorname{supp}\Psi_{0}\subset[0,2\varepsilon]
,
\operatorname{supp}\Psi\subset[\varepsilon/2,2\varepsilon]
, and

\Psi_{0}(\lambda)\Phi_{0}(\lambda)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Psi(2^{-j}\lambda)\Phi(2^{-j}\lambda)=1,\quad\forall\lambda\in[0,\infty).

(ii)

There also exixs
\Omega\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
such that
\operatorname{supp}\Omega\subset[\varepsilon/2,2\varepsilon]
such that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega(2^{-j}\lambda)\Phi(2^{-j}\lambda)=1,\quad\forall\lambda\in(0,\infty).

2.3.
Heat semigroup characterization of
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

In this subsection we review the heat semigroup characterization of
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

established by De León-Contreras and Torrea
[
5
]
. First, we recall the following definition.

Definition 2.6
.

(
[
5
]
)
We say that a measurable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies the
heat size condition for

L
if

(i)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\frac{|x|}{t}}|f(x)|dx<\infty
for every

t>0
;

(ii)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\partial_{t}^{\ell}e^{-tL}f(x)=0
for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We denote

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\big\{f:f\text{ is measurable }\text{and
satisfies the heat size condition for }L\big\}.

Recall that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\Theta_{(k)}(\cdot)
is a function on
[0,\infty)
defined by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Theta_{(k)}(\lambda):=\lambda^{2k}e^{-\lambda^{2}},\quad\lambda\in[0,\infty).

Then for any
f\in\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{k}e^{-tL}f=(-L)^{k}e^{-tL}f=(-1)^{k}t^{-k}(tL)^{k}e^{-tL}f=(-1)^{k}t^{-k}\Theta_{(k)}(\sqrt{tL})f.

Hence, by changing variables
t=s^{2}
, we have

(2.3)

\begin{split}\sup_{t>0}t^{k-({\alpha}/{2})}\|\partial_{t}^{k}e^{-tL}f\|_{L^{\infty}}&=\sup_{t>0}t^{-({\alpha}/{2})}\|\Theta_{(k)}(\sqrt{tL})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&=\sup_{s>0}s^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(s\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}.\end{split}

With the above notation, we can state the
heat semigroup characterization
of
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
obtained in
[
5
]
as follows.

Proposition 2.7
.

(
[
5
, Theorem 1.5]
)

Assume that
|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:V(x)>0\}|\neq 0
.
Let
0<\alpha<2-(n/q)
and
k:=\lfloor\alpha/2\rfloor+1
.

(i)

If
f\in\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
f\in\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
.
Furthermore,
there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
f\in\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\|\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}f(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|f\|_{\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}}.

(ii)

If
f\in\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
, then

f\in\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Furthermore,
there exists a constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\|f\|_{\Lambda^{\alpha}_{L}}\leq C^{\prime}\Big(\|\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}f(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big).

Remark 2.8
.

Although the condition
|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:V(x)>0\}|\neq 0
is not explicitly
claimed in
[
5
, Theorem 1.5]
, it is actually needed. Indeed, to prove that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}f(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\Longrightarrow f\in\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

the authors in
[
5
]
establish the following estimate (see
[
5
, Lemma 2.6]
)

(2.5)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{\ell}e^{-tL}f(x)|\leq C_{\ell}\|\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}f(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\frac{\rho(x)^{\alpha}}{t^{\ell}}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\right)^{-M},

and then claim that using this estimate one can deduce (
2.4
) (see
[
5
, Proposition 2.7]
).
However, it is worth noting that
if
|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:V(x)>0\}|=0
, i.e.,
V(x)=0
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then obviously

\rho\equiv+\infty
, and hence (
2.5
) is trivial, and from (
2.5
) we can not
deduce the implication (
2.4
).

The next result shows that

\|\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}f(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}
is controlled by
\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}
, provided that
f\in\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proposition 2.9
.

(
[
5
, Proposition 2.9]
)

Let
\alpha>0
,
k:=\lfloor\alpha/2\rfloor+1
and
f\in\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then
\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
implies
\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}f(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
;
furthermore, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\|\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}f(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

The following result shows that we may replace the integer
k
in Propositions
2.7
and

2.9
by
any integer
m\geq k
.

Proposition 2.10
.

(
[
5
, Proposition 2.4]
)

Let
\alpha>0
and
k:=\lfloor\alpha/2\rfloor+1
. Let
m
be an arbitrary integer such that
m\geq k
.
Then there exists a positive constant
C
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\displaystyle C^{-1}\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(m)}(t\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

3.
Kernel estimates

When restricted to
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the operator
L=-\Delta+V
is formally self-adjoint.
It is well known that (see, e.g.,
[
20
]
) the closure of
L|_{C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is the unique self-adjoint extension
of
L|_{C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
; we denote this extension also by the symbol
L
.

Let
p_{t}(x,y)
be the heat kernel, i.e., the integral kernel of the semigroup
e^{-tL}
generalized
by
L
. Since the potential
V
is nonnegative, it follows from the Feynman-Kac formula that

p_{t}(x,y)
has a Gaussian upper bound:

(3.1)

\displaystyle 0\leq p_{t}(x,y)\leq(4\pi t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}\right).

When
V
belongs to the reverse Hölder
class
RH_{q}
for some
q>n/2
, the estimate (
3.1
) can be improved
as follows; see
[
4
, Theorem 2.11]
and
[
17
, Theorem 1]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
N>0
, there exist positive constants
c
and
C=C_{N}
such that for all
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle 0\leq p_{t}(x,y)\leq Ct^{-\frac{n}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\right)\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(y)}\right)^{-N}.

Recall that
\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
is defined by (
1.2
), and note that
for any
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
,

\Phi^{(2\nu+1)}(0)=0\quad\text{for every }\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Based on the Gaussian upper bound of the heat kernel
p_{t}(x,y)
, the following kernel estimates
can be deduced by smooth functional calculus; see
[
3
, Lemma 2.3]
and
[
15
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
. Then for every
t>0
, the operator

\Phi(t\sqrt{L})
is an integral operator; furthermore, for any
N>0
, there exists a constant
C=C_{N}>0

such that for all
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\big|K_{\Phi(t\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|\leq Ct^{-n}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{-N},

where
K_{\Phi(t\sqrt{L})}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the integral kernel of the operator
\Phi(t\sqrt{L})
.

If we assume further that
\Phi
has compact support and vanishes near the origin, then we can improve the estimate of the kernel
K_{\Phi(t\sqrt{L})}(\cdot,\cdot)
as follows.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
such that
\operatorname{supp}\Phi\subset[0,R]
for some
R>0
.
Then for any
N_{1},N_{2}>0
, there exists a constant
C=C_{N_{1},N_{2}}>0
such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\big|K_{\Phi(t\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|\leq Ct^{-n}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{-N_{1}}\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho(x)}+\frac{t}{\rho(y)}\right)^{-N_{2}}.

Proof.

Define a function

\Psi(\lambda):=e^{\lambda^{2}}\Phi(\lambda)
,
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
. Then
\Phi(t\sqrt{L})=e^{-t^{2}L}\Psi(t\sqrt{L})
, and hence

(3.3)

\displaystyle K_{{{\Phi}}(t\sqrt{L})}(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}p_{t}(x,z)K_{\Psi(t\sqrt{L})}(z,y)dz.

By Proposition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|p_{t}(x,z)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-n}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{ct^{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho(x)}+\frac{t}{\rho(z)}\right)^{-2N_{2}}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-n}\left(1+\frac{|x-z|}{t}\right)^{-\left(N_{1}+n+1\right)}\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho(x)}\right)^{-2N_{2}}.

Meanwhile, since
\Phi\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
and
\operatorname{supp}\Phi\subset[0,R]
, we have
\Psi\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
, and hence by Proposition
3.2

(3.5)

\displaystyle\big|K_{\Psi(t\sqrt{L})}(z,y)\big|\lesssim t^{-n}\left(1+\frac{|z-y|}{t}\right)^{-N_{1}}.

Inserting (
3
) and (
3.5
)
into (
3.3
), and using the elementary inequality

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{|x-z|}{t}\right)^{-N_{1}}\left(1+\frac{|z-y|}{t}\right)^{-N_{1}}\leq\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{-N_{1}},

we obtain

\displaystyle\big|K_{\Phi(t\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-n}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{-N_{1}}\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho(x)}\right)^{-2N_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}t^{-n}\left(1+\frac{|x-z|}{t}\right)^{-(n+1)}dz

(3.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-n}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{-N_{1}}\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho(x)}\right)^{-2N_{2}}.

Since

\Phi(t\sqrt{L})
is a self-adjoint operator, (
3
) also implies that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\big|K_{\Phi(t\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|=\big|K_{\Phi(t\sqrt{L})}(y,x)\big|\lesssim t^{-n}\left(1+\frac{|y-x|}{t}\right)^{-N_{1}}\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho(y)}\right)^{-2N_{2}}.

Combining (
3
) and (
3.7
) gives that

\displaystyle\big|K_{\Phi(t\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-2n}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{-2N_{1}}\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho(x)}\right)^{-2N_{2}}\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho(y)}\right)^{-2N_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq t^{-2n}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{-2N_{1}}\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho(x)}+\frac{t}{\rho(y)}\right)^{-2N_{2}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\big|K_{\Phi(t\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|\lesssim t^{-n}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{-N_{1}}\left(1+\frac{t}{\rho(x)}+\frac{t}{\rho(y)}\right)^{-N_{2}},

as desired.
∎

Using the kernel estimate given in Proposition
3.2
, one may derive the following almost orthogonality estimate. See, e.g.,
[
2
, Lemma 3.8]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Phi,\Psi\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
such that
(\cdot)^{-2M}\Psi(\cdot)\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
.
Then for any
N>0
, there exists
C>0
such that
for all
t\geq s>0
and all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\big|K_{\Phi(t\sqrt{L})\Psi(s\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|\leq C\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{2M}t^{-n}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{-N}.

In particular, if
\Phi,\Psi\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
and both of them vanish near the origin, then
for any
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
N>0
, there exists
C>0
such that
for all
t,s>0
and all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\big|K_{\Phi(t\sqrt{L})\Psi(s\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|\leq C\left(\frac{s}{t}\wedge\frac{t}{s}\right)^{2M}(s\vee t)\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{s\vee t}\right)^{-N}.

4.
Littlewood-Paley characterization of
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of (i) of Theorem
1.6

Let
f\in\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
k=\lfloor{\alpha}/{2}\rfloor+1
.
Then by (i) of Proposition
2.7
, we have
f\in\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}},

where
\Theta_{(k)}(\cdot)
is defined by (
1.9
).
Hence, it suffices to show that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Since
|\Theta_{(k)}(\lambda)|>0
on
(1/2,2)
, by (ii) of Lemma
2.5
there exists
\Omega\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
such that
\operatorname{supp}\Omega\subset[1/2,2]
and

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega(2^{-\ell}\lambda)\Theta_{(k)}(2^{-\ell}\lambda)=1,\quad\forall\lambda\in(0,\infty).

Using Proposition
2.4
, it follows that

(4.2)

\displaystyle f=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega(2^{-\ell}\sqrt{L})\Theta_{(k)}(2^{-\ell}\sqrt{L})f\quad\text{in }\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/\mathcal{P}_{L}.

Since
\Phi
vanishes near the origin, we have
\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})P=0
for every
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{L}
.
Indeed, if
P\in\mathcal{P}_{L}
, then
L^{m}P=0
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Hence

\displaystyle\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})P

\displaystyle=2^{-2jm}(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})^{-2m}\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})(L^{m}P)

(4.3)

\displaystyle=2^{-2jm}\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})(L^{m}P)

\displaystyle=0,

where
\Psi
is a function on
[0,\infty)
defined by

\Psi(\lambda):=\lambda^{-2m}\Phi(\lambda)
. The second identity in (
4
) is justified by the fact that
\Psi\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
.

Using (
4
), it follows from (
5.15
) that for every
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Omega(2^{-\ell}\sqrt{L})\Theta_{(k)}(2^{-\ell}\sqrt{L})f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Omega(2^{-\ell}\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\Theta_{(k)}(2^{-\ell}\sqrt{L})f(y)dy.

Consequently,

\displaystyle 2^{j\alpha}\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)

(4.4)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{(j-\ell)\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Omega(2^{-\ell}\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big[2^{\ell\alpha}\Theta_{(k)}(2^{-\ell}\sqrt{L})f(y)\big]dy.

Since both
\Phi
and
\Omega
belong to
\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
and vanish near the origin, it follows from
Proposition
3.4
that for any
M\in\mathbb{N}
,
there exists a constant
C
such that for all
j,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
and all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\big|K_{\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Omega(2^{-\ell}\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|\lesssim 2^{-2|j-\ell|M}2^{(j\wedge\ell)n}(1+2^{j\wedge\ell}|x-y|)^{-(n+1)}.

Inserting this into (
4
), and choosing
M>\alpha/2
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2^{j\alpha}\big|\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\big\|\Theta_{(k)}(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-2|j-\ell|M}2^{(j-\ell)\alpha}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}2^{(j\wedge\ell)n}(1+2^{j\wedge\ell}|x-y|)^{-(n+1)}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|j-\ell|(2M-\alpha)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Since this estimates holds for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, (
4.1
) follows.

Proof of (ii) of Theorem
1.6

Since
\operatorname{supp}\Phi\subset[1/2,2]
and
|\Phi(\lambda)|>0
for
3/5\leq\lambda\leq 5/3
,
there exists
\Psi\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
such that
\operatorname{supp}\Psi\subset[1/2,2]
and

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi(2^{-j}\lambda)\Phi(2^{-j}\lambda)=1,\quad\forall\lambda\in[0,\infty).

Hence, by the homogeneous Calderón reproducing formula (cf. Proposition
2.4
),
we see that for any
f\in\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there holds

(4.5)

\displaystyle f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f\quad\text{in }\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{P}_{L}.

We divide the rest of the proof into three steps.

Step 1.
We show that the series of functions

(4.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)

converges uniformly on any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

|\tilde{f}(x)|\leq C\Big(\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{{L}})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\rho(x)^{\alpha},

where
\tilde{f}
is the sum function of the series (
4.6
).

To prove that the series (
4.6
) converges uniformly on any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
by the Heine-Borel (finite covering) theorem
it suffices to show that for every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the series (
4.6
) converges uniformly on the ball
B(x_{0},\rho(x_{0}))
.
Now let
x_{0}
be an arbitrary point in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
By (ii) of Lemma
2.1
, there exist positive constants
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
,
such that for all
x\in B(x_{0},\rho(x_{0}))
, we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle A_{1}\rho(x_{0})\leq\rho(x)\leq A_{2}\rho(x_{0}).

Note that the constants
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are independent of
x_{0}
and
x
.
In addition, since
|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:V(x)>0\}|\neq 0
, by
Lemma
2.2
we have

\rho(x)<+\infty
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
2^{-j}\leq\rho(x_{0})
, and all
x\in B(x_{0},\rho(x_{0}))
,

\displaystyle|\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-j\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|K_{\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|\big|2^{j\alpha}\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(y)\big|dy

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-j\alpha}\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}2^{jn}(1+2^{j}|x-y|)^{-(n+1)}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)2^{-j\alpha},

where for the second inequality we used the kernel estimate in proposition
3.2
.
Since
\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}
is finite and the constant term series

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}:\ 2^{-j}\leq\rho(x_{0})}2^{-j\alpha}

converges, using Weierstrass M-test it follows that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}:\ 2^{-j}\leq\rho(x_{0})}\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)

converges uniformly on
B(x_{0},\rho(x_{0}))
. Furthermore, for all
x\in B(x_{0},\rho(x_{0}))
,

(4.8)

\begin{split}&\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}:\ 2^{-j}\leq\rho(x_{0})}\big|\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)\big|\\
&\quad\quad\lesssim\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}:\ 2^{-j}\leq\rho(x_{0})}2^{-j\alpha}\\
&\quad\quad\lesssim\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\rho(x_{0})^{\alpha}\\
&\quad\quad\sim\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\rho(x)^{\alpha},\end{split}

where the last equivalence is justified by (
4.7
).

If
j\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
2^{-j}>\rho(x_{0})
, we let
M:=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor+1
, and write, for all
x\in B(x_{0},\rho(x_{0}))
,

\displaystyle|\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|K_{\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(y)\big|\big|dy

\displaystyle\quad=2^{j(M-\alpha)}\rho(x_{0})^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{2^{-j}}{\rho(x_{0})}\Big)^{M}\big|K_{\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|\big|2^{j\alpha}\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(y)\big|dy

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2^{j(M-\alpha)}\rho(x_{0})^{M}\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{2^{-j}}{\rho(x_{0})}\Big)^{M}\big|K_{\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|dy.

By Proposition
3.3
with
2^{-j}
in place of
t
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{2^{-j}}{\rho(x_{0})}\Big)^{M}\big|K_{\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|dy

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{2^{-j}}{\rho(x_{0})}\Big)^{M}\frac{2^{jn}}{(1+2^{j}|x-y|)^{(n+1)}}\Big(\frac{2^{-j}}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{-M}dy

\displaystyle\quad\sim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{2^{jn}}{(1+2^{j}|x-y|)^{(n+1)}}dy

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim 1,

where we also used (
4.7
). It follows that

\displaystyle|\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)|\lesssim\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\rho(x_{0})^{M}2^{j(M-\alpha)}.

Since the constant term series

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}:\ 2^{-j}>\rho(x_{0})}2^{j(M-\alpha)}

converges, applying the Weierstrass M-test we see that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}:\ 2^{-j}>\rho(x_{0})}\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)

converges uniformly on
B(x_{0},\rho(x_{0}))
. Moreover, for all
x\in B(x_{0},\rho(x_{0}))
we have

(4.9)

\begin{split}&\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}:\ 2^{-j}>\rho(x_{0})}\big|\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)\big|\\
&\quad\quad\lesssim\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\rho(x_{0})^{M}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}:\ 2^{-j}>\rho(x_{0})}2^{j(M-\alpha)}\\
&\quad\quad\sim\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\rho(x_{0})^{M}\rho(x_{0})^{\alpha-M}\\
&\quad\quad\sim\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\rho(x)^{\alpha},\end{split}

where the last equivalence used (
4.7
).

Therefore, we have proved that the series (
4.6
) converges uniformly on
B(x_{0},\rho(x_{0}))
.
Since
x_{0}
is an arbitrary point in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it follows that the series (
4.6
) converges pointwise on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We denote by
\tilde{f}
the sum function of the series (
4.6
). Then by (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) we have, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(4.10)

\displaystyle|\tilde{f}(x)|\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)\big|\lesssim\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\rho(x)^{\alpha}.

In other words,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\|\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}\tilde{f}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Note that, by (i) of Lemma
2.1
,
\rho
is at most of polynomial growth. Hence
(
4.10
) implies that
\tilde{f}
can naturally be regarded
as a distribution in
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Step 2.
We next show that

(4.12)

\displaystyle\tilde{f}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f\quad\text{in }\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

i.e., for every
h\in\mathcal{S}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\rightarrow-\infty\\
N_{2}\rightarrow+\infty\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\tilde{f}(x)-\sum_{N_{1}\leq j\leq N_{2}}\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)\right]h(x)dx=0.

Let
\varepsilon
be an arbitrary positive real number. We write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\tilde{f}(x)-\sum_{N_{1}\leq j\leq N_{2}}\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)\right]h(x)dx

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{B(0,R)}\left[\tilde{f}(x)-\sum_{N_{1}\leq j\leq N_{2}}\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)\right]h(x)dx

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B(0,R)}\left[\rho(x)^{-\alpha}\tilde{f}(x)-\sum_{N_{1}\leq j\leq N_{2}}\rho(x)^{-\alpha}\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)\right]\rho(x)^{\alpha}h(x)dx

\displaystyle\quad=:I_{1}+I_{2},

where
R
is a sufficiently large radius which will be determined later.

By (
4.10
) we have, for any
N_{1},N_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
(
N_{1}<N_{2}
) and any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\left|\rho(x)^{-\alpha}\tilde{f}(x)-\sum_{N_{1}\leq j\leq N_{2}}\rho(x)^{-\alpha}\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\rho(x)^{-\alpha}|\tilde{f}(x)|+\rho(x)^{-\alpha}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)|

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

On the other hand,
since
0<\rho(0)<+\infty
,
by (i) of Lemma
2.1
, we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle\rho(x)\leq\rho(0)\Big(1+\frac{|x|}{\rho(0)}\Big)^{\theta}\lesssim(1+|x|)^{\theta}.

This along with the fact that
h\in\mathcal{S}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
implies that

\rho(x)^{\alpha}|h(x)|\lesssim(1+|x|)^{-n-1}.

Therefore, if
R
is sufficiently large, we have

(4.15)

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leq C\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B(0,R)}(1+|x|)^{-n-1}dx<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

We now fix an
R
such that (
4.15
) holds, and then estimate the term
I_{1}
. Since the series (
4.6
) converges uniformly on any compact subset of

\mathbb{R}^{n}
(by Step 1), there exist
N_{1}^{0},N_{2}^{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
, with
N_{1}^{0}<N_{2}^{0}
, such that
for any
N_{1}<N_{1}^{0}
and
N_{2}>N_{2}^{0}
, and for all
x\in B(0,R)
,

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{f}(x)-\sum_{N_{1}\leq j\leq N_{2}}\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2|B(0,R)|\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}}.

It follows that for any
N_{1}<N_{1}^{0}
and
N_{2}>N_{2}^{0}

(4.16)

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\leq|B(0,R)|\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}\frac{\varepsilon}{2|B(0,R)|\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Combining (
4.16
) and (
4.15
), we see that for any
N_{1}<N_{1}^{0}
and
N_{2}>N_{2}^{0}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left[\tilde{f}(x)-\sum_{N_{1}\leq j\leq N_{2}}\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x)\right]h(x)dx<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon.

Hence (
4.13
) is true.

Step 3.
We show that there exists a generalized polynomial

P\in\mathcal{P}_{L}
such that
f-P=\tilde{f}
and
\tilde{f}\in\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

(4.17)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{f}\|_{\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}}\lesssim\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

On the one hand, the series (
4.6
) converges in
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/\mathcal{P}_{L}
to
f
(see (
4.5
)). On the other hand, the series (
4.6
) converges
in
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
\tilde{f}
(see (
4.12
)), and hence also
converges
in
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/\mathcal{P}_{L}
to
\tilde{f}
. Here, we do not explicitly distinguish between

g\in\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
its equivalence class
g+\mathcal{P}_{L}
, as noted earlier on p. 7.
Since
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/\mathcal{P}_{L}
can be identified with the dual of a Frechét space (see
[
8
, Proposition 3.7]
), it is a Hausdorff space and hence any convergent sequence in
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/\mathcal{P}_{L}
must have a unique limit.
It follows that
f=\tilde{f}

as elements in
\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/\mathcal{P}
; in other words, there
exists a generalized polynomial
P\in\mathcal{P}_{L}
such that

f-P=\tilde{f}.

It remains to show (
4.17
).

Recall that
\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}\tilde{f}(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(cf. (
4.11
)). This implies that

f\in\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by
[
5
, Proposition 2.7]
. Hence it follows from (ii) of Proposition
2.7
that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{f}\|_{\Lambda^{\alpha}_{L}}\lesssim\|\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}\tilde{f}(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Invoking (
4.11
), we further see that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{f}\|_{\Lambda^{\alpha}_{L}}\lesssim\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Therefore, in order to show
(
4.17
), it suffices to prove that

(4.18)

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}t^{-\alpha}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

To show (
4.18
), we start with the identity
(
4.12
). From this identity it follows that
for each
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(4.19)

\displaystyle\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})\tilde{f}(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(x).

Let
\ell_{0}
be the (unique) integer such that
t\in[2^{-\ell_{0}},2^{-\ell_{0}+1})
. Then
it follows that

\displaystyle t^{-\alpha}\big|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})\tilde{f}(x)\big|

\displaystyle\sim 2^{\ell_{0}\alpha}\big|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})\tilde{f}(x)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{(\ell_{0}-j)\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|K_{\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|2^{j\alpha}|\Phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{(\ell_{0}-j)\alpha}\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)

(4.20)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|K_{\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|dy.

From the definition of
\Theta_{(k)}(\cdot)
(cf. (
1.9
)), we see that
(\cdot)^{-2k}\Theta_{(k)}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
. Meanwhile, since
\Psi\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
and
\operatorname{supp}\Psi\subset[1/2,2]
, we also have

(\cdot)^{-2k}\Psi(\cdot)\in\mathcal{A}([0,\infty))
.
Hence we may apply Proposition
3.4
to have

\displaystyle\big|K_{\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{t}{2^{-j}}\wedge\frac{2^{-j}}{t}\Big)^{2k}(t\vee 2^{-j})^{-n}\big[1+(t\vee 2^{-j})^{-1}|x-y|\big]^{-(n+1)}

\displaystyle\sim 2^{-2|j-\ell_{0}|k}2^{(j\wedge\ell_{0})n}(1+2^{j\wedge\ell_{0}}|x-y|)^{-(n+1)}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|K_{\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})\Psi(2^{-j}\sqrt{L})}(x,y)\big|dy\lesssim 2^{-2|j-\ell_{0}|k}.

Inserting this into (
4
) we obtain

\displaystyle t^{-\alpha}\big|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})\tilde{f}(x)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{(\ell_{0}-j)\alpha}2^{-2|j-\ell_{0}|k}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|j-\ell_{0}|(2k-\alpha)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\nu\alpha}\|\Phi(2^{-\nu}\sqrt{L})f\|_{L^{\infty}},

where we used the fact that
2k-\alpha>0
.
Since this estimate holds for all
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with implicit constant independent of
t
and
x
,
we see that (
4.18
) is true.

5.
Carleson measure characterization of
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

The main aim of this section is to prove Theorem
1.9
.
We first present two lemmas containing useful facts.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:V(x)>0\}|\neq 0
.
If
f\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
f
satisfies the heat size condition for
L
.

Proof.

Since
f\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists
\gamma>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{n+\gamma}}dx<\infty.

Hence, for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)|}{e^{{\frac{|x|}{t}}}}dx\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)|}{(1+\frac{|x|}{t})^{n+\gamma}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{n+\gamma}(1+t)^{-(n+\gamma)}}dx

\displaystyle\leq(1+t)^{n+\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{n+\gamma}}dx

\displaystyle<\infty.

It remains to show that for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\partial_{t}^{\ell}e^{-tL}f(x)=0
.

To show the last property, we write

(5.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\ell}e^{-tL}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}^{\ell}p_{t}(x,y)f(y)dy,

where
p_{t}(x,y)
is the heat kernel of
L
.
By
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
, for any
N>0
, there exist constants
c,C_{N}>0
such that

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{\ell}p_{t}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}t^{-\ell-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(y)}\right)^{-N}

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}^{\prime}t^{-\ell-\frac{n}{2}}\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{-(n+\gamma)}\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\right)^{-N}.

By the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{-(n+\gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{|y|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{-(n+\gamma)}\left(1+\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{n+\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sqrt{t})^{n+\gamma}(1+|y|)^{-(n+\gamma)}\left(1+\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{n+\gamma}.

It follows that

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{\ell}p_{t}(x,y)|\lesssim t^{-\ell-\frac{n}{2}}(1+\sqrt{t})^{n+\gamma}(1+|y|)^{-(n+\gamma)}\left(1+\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{n+\gamma}\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\right)^{-N}.

Inserting this into (
5.1
) gives

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{\ell}e^{-tL}f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\ell-\frac{n}{2}}(1+\sqrt{t})^{n+\gamma}\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\right)^{-N}\left(1+\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{n+\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(y)|}{(1+|y|)^{n+\gamma}}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\ell-\frac{n}{2}}(1+\sqrt{t})^{n+\gamma}\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\right)^{-N}\left(1+\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{t}}\right)^{n+\gamma}.

We now fix an arbitrary
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and choose
N>\max\{0,\gamma-2\ell+2\}
.
Then for any
t>\max\{1,|x|^{2}\}
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle|(tL)^{\ell}e^{-tL}f(x)|\lesssim t^{-1}\rho(x)^{\gamma-2\ell+2}.

Since
|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:V(x)>0\}|\neq 0
, we have
\rho(x)<+\infty
by Lemma
2.2
.
Hence (
5.2
) implies that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\partial_{t}^{\ell}e^{-tL}f(x)=0
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that
|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:V(x)>0\}|\neq 0
.
Let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, i.e.,
P
is a generalized polynomial adapted to
L
.
Let further
P\in\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, i.e.,
P
is a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the heat size condition for

. Then
P(x)=0
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Since

, there exists

such that

\displaystyle L^{m}P=0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

If

) implies that

for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Now we assume that

. On the one hand, (

) implies that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{m}e^{-tL}P=(-L)^{m}e^{-tL}P=(-1)^{m}e^{-tL}(L^{m}P)=0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{S}_{L}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

On the other hand, the condition

implies that

is a pointwise well-defined
measurable function given by

\partial_{t}^{m}e^{-tL}P(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}^{m}p_{t}(x,y)P(y)dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where

is the heat kernel of

.
Therefore,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{m}e^{-tL}P(x)=0\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Let
0<\alpha<2-n/q
such that
\lfloor\alpha/2\rfloor+1\leq m
. Let
k:=\lfloor\alpha/2\rfloor+1
. By (
2.3
) and
(
5.4
), we have

\sup_{t>0}\|\Theta_{(m)}(t\sqrt{L})P\|_{L^{\infty}}=\sup_{t>0}t^{m-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|\partial_{t}^{m}e^{-tL}P\|_{L^{\infty}}=0.

Then using Propositions
2.9
and
2.10

\displaystyle 0\leq\|\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}P(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\sup_{t>0}\|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})P\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C^{\prime}\sup_{t>0}\|\Theta_{(m)}(t\sqrt{L})P\|_{L^{\infty}}=0.

Consequently,
\|\rho(\cdot)^{-\alpha}P(\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}=0

(5.5)

\displaystyle\rho(x)^{-\alpha}P(x)=0\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Since

2.2

. This together with (

for a.e.

.

Proof of (i) of Theorem
1.9

We first show that
\Lambda_{L}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Note that the condition

To show the inequality (

), we pick any

. Let

with

\displaystyle\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}2^{2\ell\alpha}\sum_{j=\ell}^{\infty}2^{-(j-\ell)(4k-2\varepsilon)}\int_{1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{2^{\ell n}\big|\Theta_{(k)}(2^{-j}t\sqrt{L})f(z)\big|^{2}}{(1+2^{\ell}|y-z|)^{2N-n-1}}dz\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}2^{2\ell\alpha}\sum_{j=\ell}^{\infty}2^{-(j-\ell)(4k-2\varepsilon)}\int_{2^{-j}}^{2^{-j+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{2^{\ell n}\big|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f(z)\big|^{2}}{(1+2^{\ell}|y-z|)^{2N-n-1}}dz\frac{dt}{t}.

To prove (
5.18
), it suffices to show that there exists a constant

C>0
such that for every

\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
and evert
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(5.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=\ell}^{\infty}2^{-(j-\ell)(4k-2\varepsilon)}\int_{2^{-j}}^{2^{-j+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{2^{\ell n}\big|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f(z)\big|^{2}}{(1+2^{\ell}|y-z|)^{2N-n-1}}dz\frac{dt}{t}\leq C2^{-2\ell\alpha}\big\|d\mu_{(k,f)}\big\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}},

provided that
\varepsilon<2k
and
N\geq n+1
.

Fix each
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, we denote

\mathcal{X}_{\ell}:=2^{-\ell-k_{0}}\mathbb{Z}^{n},

where
k_{0}
is a fixed positive integer such that
2^{-k_{0}}<1/\sqrt{n}
. Then we have the following properties.

(i)

For each
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}=\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}B(x,2^{-\ell+1}).

(ii)

There exists a fixed integer
m_{0}
such that for each
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(5.20)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\chi_{B(x,2^{-\ell+1})}(z)\leq m_{0}\quad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(iii)

There exist a constant
C\geq 1
such that for all
x\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(5.21)

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in B(x,2^{-\ell+1})}(1+2^{\ell}|y-z|)\leq C\inf_{z\in B(x,2^{-\ell+1})}(1+2^{\ell}|y-z|).

Note that for each
j\geq\ell
,

\displaystyle\int_{2^{-j}}^{2^{-j+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{2^{\ell n}\big|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f(z)\big|^{2}}{(1+2^{\ell}|y-z|)^{2N-n-1}}dz\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\int_{2^{-j}}^{2^{-j+1}}\int_{B(x,2^{-\ell+1})}\frac{2^{\ell n}\big|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f(z)\big|^{2}}{(1+2^{\ell}|y-z|)^{2N-n-1}}dz\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\left(\sup_{z\in B(x,2^{-\ell+1})}\frac{2^{\ell n}}{(1+2^{\ell}|y-z|)^{2N-n-1}}\right)\int_{0}^{2^{-\ell+1}}\int_{B(x,2^{-\ell+1})}\big|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f(z)\big|^{2}dz\frac{dt}{t}.

By
(
1.11
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2^{-\ell+1}}\int_{B(x,2^{-\ell+1})}\big|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f(z)\big|^{2}dz\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq|B(x,2^{-\ell+1})|^{1+\frac{2\alpha}{n}}\big\|d\mu_{(k,f)}\big\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\sim 2^{-2\ell\alpha}\big\|d\mu_{(k,f)}\big\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}|B(x,2^{-\ell+1})|.

Using this and (
5.21
), it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{2^{-j}}^{2^{-j+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{2^{\ell n}\big|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f(z)\big|^{2}}{(1+2^{\ell}|y-z|)^{2N-n-1}}dz\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim 2^{-2\ell\alpha}\big\|d\mu_{(k,f)}\big\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\left(\inf_{z\in B(x,2^{-\ell+1})}\frac{2^{\ell n}}{(1+2^{\ell}|y-z|)^{2N-n-1}}\right)|B(x,2^{-\ell+1})|

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2^{-2\ell\alpha}\big\|d\mu_{(k,f)}\big\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\int_{B(x,2^{-\ell+1})}\frac{2^{\ell n}}{(1+2^{\ell}|y-z|)^{2N-n-1}}dz.

By (
5.20
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell}}\int_{B(x,2^{-\ell+1})}\frac{2^{\ell n}}{(1+2^{\ell}|y-z|)^{2N-n-1}}dz\leq m_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{2^{\ell n}}{(1+2^{\ell}|y-z|)^{2N-n-1}}dz\lesssim 1,

provided that
N\geq n+1
. Hence

\displaystyle\int_{2^{-j}}^{2^{-j+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{2^{\ell n}\big|\Theta_{(k)}(t\sqrt{L})f(z)\big|^{2}}{(1+2^{\ell}|y-z|)^{2N-n-1}}dz\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim 2^{-2\ell\alpha}\big\|d\mu_{(k,f)}\big\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}.

Consequently, if
\varepsilon<2k
, then

\displaystyle\text{LHS}\;(\ref{eq:desired})

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-2\ell\alpha}\big\|d\mu_{(k,f)}\big\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}\sum_{j=\ell}^{\infty}2^{-(j-\ell)(4k-2\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-2\ell\alpha}\big\|d\mu_{(k,f)}\big\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}},

as desired.

Step 4.
With (
5.18
) in hand, we aim to complete the proof of (ii) of Theorem
1.9
.

Since (
5.18
) holds,
by Theorem
1.6
there exists a generalized polynomial
P_{f}\in\mathcal{P}_{L}

such that
f-P_{f}\in\Lambda^{\alpha}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

(5.22)

\displaystyle\|f-P_{f}\|_{\Lambda^{\alpha}_{L}}\lesssim\big\|d\mu_{(k,f)}\big\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}.

Hence, by (i) of Proposition
2.7
,

f-P_{f}\in\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
On the other hand, since
f\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, from Lemma
5.1

we see that
f\in\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Therefore,
P_{f}=P_{f}-f+f\in\mathcal{H}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
It then follows by Lemma
5.2
that
P_{f}(x)=0
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
From this and (
5.22
), we infer that
f\in\Lambda^{\alpha}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\|f\|_{\Lambda^{\alpha}_{L}}\lesssim\big\|d\mu_{(k,f)}\big\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}}
.
