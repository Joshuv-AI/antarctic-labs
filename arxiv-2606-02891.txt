Global Unknown Estimation: A Statistical Framework for Wireless Distributed Learning This work was supported in part by the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada.
Yicheng Qu, Ali Bereyhi, Ben Liang

Abstract
Over-the-air computation (AirComp) is widely used for model aggregation in wireless distributed learning. Although it enhances communication efficiency, we believe the AirComp aggregation has limited effectiveness due to the difference between its target problem and that of distributed learning. In this paper, we develop a rigorous formulation for optimal model aggregation in wireless distributed learning. Using this formulation, we show that AirComp aggregation generally assumes a mismatched statistical model for local parameters. We then propose a statistical framework for model aggregation, called global unknown estimation (GUE). It captures the statistical relation between the local and global model parameters, allowing to interpret model aggregation as an inference task. We validate the efficiency of GUE through numerical experiments. Our results show that, in the low SNR regime, GUE can reduce the required power for model aggregation by approximately 15 dB compared to AirComp aggregation. Remarkably, this gain is obtained without additional computational overhead.

I
Introduction

In distributed learning, multiple clients collaboratively train a model, potentially with the help of a central server, without sharing their local datasets. A well-known approach is federated learning
[
1
]
,
where the training is carried out through multiple rounds of communication: in each round, clients train local models on their own datasets and share the models with the server. The server
aggregates
these models into a global model and broadcasts it to the clients to initiate the next round of training. This paradigm has gained significant importance in modern large-scale and privacy-sensitive applications
[
2
]
,
as it allows computation to be pushed closer to where data are stored. Under perfect communication links, the model transmission and aggregation can be readily performed
[
3
]
.

In practical wireless networks, however, the clients are connected through noisy communication links with imperfect channel conditions. This introduces a fundamental bottleneck
[
4
]
. To address this challenge, various approaches for efficient model aggregation in these systems have been proposed. Among these proposals,
aircomp
(
aircomp
) has been widely accepted as the state-of-the-art
[
5
]
. In this approach, instead of recovering transmitted signals separately and then aggregating them afterwards, the server directly estimates the desired aggregation by leveraging the superposition of waveforms over the wireless channel
[
6
]
.
This enables simultaneous transmissions over the same channel, enhancing the communication efficiency.

From the statistical viewpoint,
aircomp
implicitly assumes that realizing the predefined aggregation policy that is deployed in an error-free network,
e.g.
, weighted averaging, is the optimal approach to aggregate a global update from the noisy received signals. Although this approach leads to a valid aggregation, its optimality is not guaranteed. In fact, using
aircomp
, the global update is determined by minimizing the
computation error
between the aggregated update and a predefined function of local models. How explicitly this computation error affects the learning performance, however, remains unclear.

Several studies characterize the efficiency of
aircomp
aggregation by connecting its computation error to learning metrics. The work in
[
7
]
shows that the computation error determines an upper bound on the optimality gap of the learning algorithm.
The studies in
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
extend this analysis to settings with imperfect
csi
(
csi
) and heterogeneous data, respectively, and use this connection to propose adaptive aggregation schemes. The study in
[
10
]
considers a joint design in settings with both imperfect
csi
and heterogeneous data and develops an alternative aggregation scheme that directly minimizes an upper bound on the optimality gap. This idea is further extended to digital aggregation in
[
11
]
by implementing a coded
aircomp
approach via lattice coding. All existing studies commonly focus on optimality bounds in terms of computation error and assume that minimizing this error
directly
enhances learning performance.

In this study, we challenge this assumption and show that there is no strong connection between these two metrics. Intuitively, the goal of model aggregation is not to realize a predefined function, but rather to provide an
efficient estimate
of the global update from the received signals. This describes a distributed inference task, in which a global unknown is to be estimated from a set of distributed observations; see for instance
[
12
,
13
]
. Despite its intuitive connection, this framework has not been adapted to distributed learning. Our study aims to establish a novel framework that exploits the connection between these two concepts for efficient model aggregation in wireless networks.

I-A

Contributions

This work proposes a new framework, called
gue
(
gue
), for model aggregation in wireless distributed learning systems.
gue
deviates from the conventional
aircomp
approach and interprets the aggregation task as a distributed inference problem. This enables us to define the notion of
optimal
aggregation and specify it under a postulated prior distribution for local parameters. Our experiments demonstrate that
gue
can significantly enhance the power efficiency of model aggregation by shifting its focus from
computation error minimization
to the
optimal estimation of global model parameter
.

More precisely, the main contributions of this work are as follows:

•

We formulate model aggregation as a statistical inference problem and define the notion of
optimal aggregation
. By fitting
aircomp
aggregation into this framework, we show that
aircomp
aggregates the model under a
mismatched
assumption on local model parameters. This finding explains the restricted efficiency of
aircomp
aggregation.

•

To remedy the inadequacy of
aircomp
, we propose the
gue
framework, which models the aggregation target as a deterministic unknown and treats the local updates as its noisy observations. Using this statistical model, we design a novel aggregation scheme that determines a
ml
(
ml
) estimator of the aggregation target from the local parameters. We study the complexity and efficiency of the proposed scheme and compare it against the
aircomp
method.

•

We validate the efficiency of
gue
through extensive experiments. Numerical results depict that
gue
offers substantial enhancement in learning performance compared to conventional aggregation via
aircomp
, especially at the low
snr
(
snr
) regime. Our investigation further demonstrates that minimizing the computation error does not necessarily lead to better learning performance. This supports our conjecture that the implicit assumption in
aircomp
does not adequately capture the statistical goal of model aggregation in distributed learning.

II
System Model and Problem Formulation

II-A

System Model

II-A
1
Distributed Learning Setting

Let
\mathcal{D}_{k}
denote the local dataset at client
k
. For a sample
\zeta_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{k}
and a model vector
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbbmss{R}^{L}
, client
k
computes the sample loss of the model as
f({\mathbf{a}},\zeta_{i})
for some loss metric
f
. The
local loss
of the model at client
k
is hence given by

F_{k}({\mathbf{a}})=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_{k}|}\sum_{\zeta_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{k}}f({\mathbf{a}},\zeta_{i}).

(1)

The clients aim to train a global model jointly over the global dataset,
i.e.
,
\mathcal{D}=\cup_{k}\mathcal{D}_{k}
. This learning task can be cast as the distributed minimization of the
global loss function,
which is defined in terms of the local loss functions as

\displaystyle F({\mathbf{a}})=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|}\sum_{k}|\mathcal{D}_{k}|F_{k}({\mathbf{a}}).

(2)

For this task, the clients invoke the following mechanism over
T

rounds
. At the beginning of round
t\in[T]
, the clients set their models to the current global model
{\mathbf{a}}_{t-1}
, broadcast by the server through an error-free downlink channel. Client
k
then updates the model locally by reducing its local loss
F_{k}({\mathbf{a}})
through multiple iterations of a gradient-based optimizaer. Let
{\mathbf{a}}_{k,t}
be the latest model vector at client
k
, and the normalized local model
\bar{{\mathbf{a}}}_{k,t}
is obtained by

\bar{{\mathbf{a}}}_{k,t}[\ell]=\frac{{\mathbf{a}}_{k,t}[\ell]-\mu_{k,t}}{\nu_{k,t}},

(3)

with the sample mean
\mu_{k,t}
and variance
\nu_{k,t}^{2}
computed as

\displaystyle\mu_{k,t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}{\mathbf{a}}_{k,t}[\ell],

(4a)

\displaystyle\nu_{k,t}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}({\mathbf{a}}_{k,t}[\ell]-\mu_{k,t})^{2}.

(4b)

The local parameters in
\bar{{\mathbf{a}}}_{k,t}
are then sent to the server to be
aggregated
into a global model
{\mathbf{a}}_{t}
. Note that it is also common to send the model difference
\Delta{\mathbf{a}}_{k,t}={\mathbf{a}}_{k,t}-{\mathbf{a}}_{t-1}
, and our study can be directly applied to such scenario.

In the rest of this work, we focus on
federated learning
, where the local parameters are aggregated proportionally to their local data size. Our proposed method and analysis can be straightforwardly applied to other distributed learning systems.

II-A
2
Communication Setting

The aggregation is carried out directly by analog transmissions through a wireless
mac
(
mac
) between the
K
clients and the server. We assume that the clients are single-antenna, while the server is equipped with an
N
-antenna array. Since the signal in the communication system contains both in-phase and quadrature components, the transmitted model parameters are converted into the
complex
domain. Hence, the clients transmit their local parameters,
i.e.
,
\bar{{\mathbf{a}}}_{k,t}
, in
\lceil\frac{L}{2}\rceil
resource blocks. We assume synchronized transmission, such that in block
i\in[\lceil\frac{L}{2}\rceil]
, with
\ell=2i-1
, the clients send their
\ell
-th and
(\ell+1)
-th parameters,
i.e.
,
\bar{{\mathbf{a}}}_{k,t}[\ell]+\mathrm{j}\,\bar{{\mathbf{a}}}_{k,t}[\ell+1]
, synchronously. Without loss of generality, we focus on a sample block in an arbitrary round and denote the entry sent by client
k
as
u_{k}\in\mathbbmss{C}
,
i.e.
,
u_{k}=\bar{{\mathbf{a}}}_{k,t}[\ell]+\mathrm{j}\,\bar{{\mathbf{a}}}_{k,t}[\ell+1]
for an odd index
\ell
at round
t
. In the sequel, we drop the indices
\ell
and
t
for simplicity.

Client
k
constructs its transmit signal from its parameter as
x_{k}=\beta_{k}u_{k}
with the
scaling factor

\beta_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
that satisfies
\lvert\beta_{k}\rvert^{2}\leq P
for some transmit power
P>0
. It then sends
x_{k}
over its uplink channel. The server receives the signal
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{N}
, which is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\mathbf{h}}_{k}x_{k}+{\mathbf{z}},

(5)

with
{\mathbf{h}}_{k}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{N}
denoting the vector of channel coefficients from client
k
to the server, and
{\mathbf{z}}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{N}
being zero-mean complex-valued additive white Gaussian noise with variance
\sigma_{z}^{2}
,
i.e.
,
{\mathbf{z}}\sim\mathcal{C}\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\sigma^{2}_{z}\mathbf{I}_{N})
. Defining
\mathbf{x}=[x_{1},\ldots,x_{K}]^{\mathsf{T}}
and
\mathbf{u}=\left[u_{1},\ldots,u_{K}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}
, we can compactly represent the received signal as
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{u}+{\mathbf{z}}
, where
\mathbf{B}=\mathsf{diag}\left\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{K}\right\}
and
\mathbf{H}=\left[{\mathbf{h}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{h}}_{K}\right]
. In the sequel, we refer to
\mathbf{B}
as the power matrix and
\mathbf{H}
as the channel matrix, which are assumed to be perfectly known at the server.

In addition to the data transmission, the pair of sample mean and sample variance
(\mu_{k},\nu_{k})
is sent to the server without any transmission error,
e.g.
, using orthogonal channels with error correction codes. They are used by the server in recovering a properly scaled and shifted estimate of the global model.

II-B

Problem Formulation

Let
\theta\in\mathbbmss{C}
be the
ground-truth
global parameter that the server needs to estimate after each transmission block,
i.e.
,
\theta={\mathbf{a}}_{t}^{*}[\ell]+\mathrm{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{t}^{*}[\ell+1]
. Here,
{\mathbf{a}}_{t}^{*}
denotes the global model at round
t
in the centralized scenario, where all local datasets are available at the server. The server aims to compute
\theta
by aggregating local parameters from the received signal
\mathbf{y}
.
In general, this operation is described by an estimator
\hat{\theta}\left(\cdot\right):\mathbbmss{C}^{N}\mapsto\mathbbmss{C}
, which extracts
\theta
from the received signal.

To be able to rigorously define an
efficient
mapping, we need to define the notion of
optimal aggregation
.

Definition 1

(Optimal aggregation)
:

The optimal aggregation from
\mathbf{y}
is defined as

\displaystyle g(\mathbf{y})=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\hat{\theta}\left(\cdot\right):\mathbbmss{C}^{N}\mapsto\mathbbmss{C}}\mathcal{C}(\theta,\hat{\theta}(\mathbf{y})),

(6)

for some cost function
\mathcal{C}
.

According to Definition
1
, the conventional aggregation problem can then be interpreted as an inference problem. This interpretation indicates that the design of the optimal aggregation depends on the statistical model of the ground-truth global parameter
\theta
.

In the sequel, we show that the conventional aggregation scheme based on
aircomp
can fit into this formulation by considering a basic statistical representation for the ground truth. Despite its popularity, we see that
aircomp
does not capture the most effective strategy for distributed learning. We hence propose an alternative model for the ground truth, through which we can develop a new over-the-air aggregation scheme.

III
AirComp Aggregation and Its Inadequacy

III-A

AirComp Aggregation

With perfect links, the global model parameter is computed as a weighted average of local model parameters. Let
\mathbf{w}
specifies the client weights,
e.g.
, by data size ratio as
\mathbf{w}[k]={|\mathcal{D}_{k}|}/{|\mathcal{D}|}
. To recover the normalization process, the server aggregates the local models as

f(\mathbf{u})=\mathbf{w}^{\prime\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\mu},

(7)

where
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}[k]=\mathbf{w}[k]\nu_{k}
and
\bm{\mu}[k]=\mu_{k}
. Conventionally, this aggregation scheme is directly extended to noisy transmission, where the server aims to realize an estimate of global model parameter from its received signal
\mathbf{y}
,
i.e.
, setting
\theta=f(\mathbf{u})
. The aggregation in this case is interpreted as over-the-air computation of the linear function
f(\mathbf{u})
,
i.e.
, analog function computation
[
6
]
. This paradigm is often referred to as over-the-air aggregation
[
5
]
.

The use of
aircomp
for aggregation implicitly imposes the following (mismatched) model on the local parameters: they are modeled as
iid
(
iid
) Gaussian vectors
\mathbf{u}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_{k})
,
with mean zero and unit variance
[
14
]
.
1
1

1

The zero mean and unit variance follow from the normalization of local parameters.
Since
f(\mathbf{u})
is linear, this implies the global model parameter
\theta
is a Gaussian random variable. The conventional solution to the computation task in this setting is given by the
mmse
(
mmse
) estimator, defined as

g_{\mathsf{MMSE}}(\mathbf{y})=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{\hat{\theta}}\mathbbm{E}_{p(\theta|\mathbf{y})}\left[\lvert\theta-\hat{\theta}\rvert^{2}\right]=\mathbbm{E}_{p(\theta|\mathbf{y})}\left[\theta\right].

(8)

This describes a Gaussian
mmse
estimation, whose solution is given by
[
15
]

g_{\mathsf{MMSE}}(\mathbf{y})=(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{w}^{\prime})^{\mathsf{H}}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{H}}+\sigma^{2}_{z}\mathbf{I}_{N})^{-1}\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\mu}.

(9)

In (
9
), the aggregation is computed directly from
\mathbf{y}
in terms of the channel and customized power matrix
\mathbf{B}
. To design
\mathbf{B}
, we evaluate the
mse
(
mse
) between the ground-truth and the estimated parameter as

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left[\lvert\theta-\hat{\theta}\rvert^{2}\right]=\mathbbm{E}\left[\lvert f(\mathbf{u})-g_{\mathsf{MMSE}}(\mathbf{y})\rvert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{w}^{\prime\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{w}^{\prime})^{\mathsf{H}}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{H}}+\sigma^{2}_{z}\mathbf{I}_{N})^{-1}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{w}^{\prime},

(10)

and set the power matrix
\mathbf{B}
by solving

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{B}}\quad

\displaystyle(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{w}^{\prime})^{\mathsf{H}}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{H}}+\sigma^{2}_{z}\mathbf{I}_{N})^{-1}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}~

(11)

s.t.

\displaystyle|\beta_{k}|^{2}\leq P\quad\forall k\in[K],

which minimizes the expected aggregation error.

III-B

AirComp as Optimal Aggregation

The
aircomp
aggregation approach is readily fitted to our formulation of optimal aggregation. For this scheme, the cost function
\mathcal{C}
determines the squared error between
\theta
and its estimate,
i.e.
,
\mathcal{C}(\theta,\hat{\theta})=\mathbbm{E}_{p(\theta|\mathbf{y})}\left[\lvert\theta-\hat{\theta}\rvert^{2}\right]
.

This interpretation of
aircomp
aggregation as an optimal aggregation scheme reveals its inadequacy for distributed learning. In fact,
aircomp
aggregation relies on the assumption that the local parameters are drawn from
statistically independent processes
and that the ground-truth is explicitly computed as
\theta=f(\mathbf{u})
. Nevertheless, from a statistical learning point of view, this assumption is not valid. In federated learning, the local model parameters are computed by backpropagation over the same model architecture with the same initial point
{\mathbf{a}}_{t-1}
. Assuming statistically independent parameters is hence unrealistic in this case. Following this observation, we design a new optimal aggregation scheme under a more realistic statistical model for the local parameters.

IV
Global Unknown estimation

To properly capture the goal of distributed learning in our formulation, we propose a new scheme,
gue
, which sets the entries of the centralized global model as the estimation target,
i.e.
,
\theta={\mathbf{a}}^{*}_{t}[\ell]+\mathrm{j}{\mathbf{a}}^{*}_{t}[\ell+1]
. To this end, the ground-truth global parameter
\theta
is characterized as a
deterministic unknown
. Since the local model training essentially aims to approximate such unknown, the local parameters are modeled as sample estimators of
\theta
, which compute an erroneous estimate of
\theta
due to their limited local dataset
\mathcal{D}_{k}
. With this model, the local parameters are described by a joint distribution parameterized with
\theta
, which we denote by
p_{\theta}(\mathbf{u})
. As a result, the distribution of the received signal
\mathbf{y}
is also parameterized with
\theta
and obtained by

p_{\theta}(\mathbf{y})=\int p_{\theta}(\mathbf{u})p(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{u})d\mathbf{u},

(12)

where
p(\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{u})
describes the channel.

Motivated by the results of
[
16
]
, we assume
p_{\theta}(\mathbf{u})
to be a Gaussian distribution. The mean and covariance of this distribution are obtained from the postulated distribution defined later. Building on the distribution, we derive the optimal aggregation in
gue
and analyze its efficiency and complexity.

IV-A

Postulated Distribution

In
gue
framework, the
kl
(
kl
) divergence is employed as the cost function,
i.e.
,

\mathcal{C}(\theta,\hat{\theta})=\int p_{\theta}(\mathbf{y})\log\frac{p_{\theta}(\mathbf{y})}{p_{\hat{\theta}}(\mathbf{y})}d\mathbf{y}.

(13)

With this cost function, the optimal aggregation describes a standard
ml
estimator which estimates the ground-truth
\theta
from
\mathbf{y}
by maximizing the likelihood
p_{\theta}(\mathbf{y})
.

Consistent with
aircomp
aggregation, we assume that the weighted average function
f(\mathbf{u})
describe the optimal aggregation
under perfect communication links
,
i.e.
, when the server has access to local parameters
\mathbf{u}
. Considering the cost function in
gue
, this means that
f(\mathbf{u})
is an
ml
estimator of
\theta
from the samples collected in
\mathbf{u}
,
i.e.
,

f(\mathbf{u})=\hat{\theta}_{\mathsf{ML}}(\mathbf{u}),

(14)

and we use this assumption to specify the distribution
p_{\theta}\left(\mathbf{u}\right)
.

Definition 2
:

The postulated distribution of local parameter
\mathbf{u}
is a parameterized family
p_{\theta}(\mathbf{u})
, for which
f(\mathbf{u})
serves as an
ml
estimator of
\theta
.

The above definition enables us to analytically specify
p_{\theta}(\mathbf{u})
. To this end, let
p_{\theta}(\mathbf{u})
belong to a Gaussian family,
i.e.
,
\mathbf{u}\sim\mathcal{CN}((\theta-\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\mu})\mathbf{v},\mathbf{G})
for some
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{K}
and
\mathbf{G}\in\mathbbmss{C}^{K\times K}
. The postulated distribution is then specified by
\mathbf{v}[k]={1}/({K\mathbf{w}^{\prime}[k]})
and
\mathbf{G}=\mathsf{diag}\left\{{1}/({K\mathbf{w}^{\prime}[1]^{2}}),\ldots,{1}/({K\mathbf{w}^{\prime}[K]^{2}})\right\}
. To show this, we note that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{w}^{\prime\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{u}-\theta+\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\mu},

(15)

where the complex partial derivative operator is defined as

\nabla_{\theta}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\Re(\theta)}+\mathrm{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\Im(\theta)}\right).

(16)

Setting this derivative to
0
, we have

\hat{\theta}_{\mathsf{ML}}(\mathbf{u})=\mathbf{w}^{\prime\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\mu}=f(\mathbf{u}),

(17)

which satisfies the condition in (
14
).

Note that, similar to the assumed prior in AirComp,
p_{\theta}(\mathbf{u})
contains no correlation term. However, the independence observed in
gue
is for a fixed
\theta
. Furthermore, it is not an assumption; it is induced by treating
f(\mathbf{u})
as an
ml
estimator under the Gaussian model. This is in contrast to AirComp, which enforces independence by design.

IV-B

Aggregation via GUE

Considering the postulated distribution, the received signal is described in terms of the unknown
\theta
by the Gaussian distribution

\mathbf{y}\sim\mathcal{CN}((\theta-\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\mu})\mathbf{r},\bm{\Sigma})
,
where
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{v}
and
\bm{\Sigma}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{H}}+\sigma^{2}_{z}\mathbf{I}_{N}
. The optimal aggregation via
gue
is hence given by the
ml
estimator of
\theta
from
\mathbf{y}
. Determining the derivative of log-likelihood as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{y})=\mathbf{r}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}(\mathbf{y}-(\theta-\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\mu})\mathbf{r}).

(18)

We set it to zero to find the solution as

\theta=\frac{\mathbf{r}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{y}}{\mathbf{r}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{r}}+\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\mu}.

(19)

Hence, the optimal aggregation in the
gue
framework is

g_{\mathsf{ML}}(\mathbf{y})=\frac{(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{v})^{\mathsf{H}}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{H}}+\sigma^{2}_{z}\mathbf{I}_{N})^{-1}\mathbf{y}}{(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{v})^{\mathsf{H}}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{H}}+\sigma^{2}_{z}\mathbf{I}_{N})^{-1}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{v}}+\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\mu}.

(20)

It is worth noting that while this estimator is computed linearly from
\mathbf{y}
, the linear beamforming vector is different from that of the
mmse
estimator. This follows from the fact that this estimator takes a different statistical model from the one considered in
aircomp
.

IV-C

Efficiency of GUE Aggregation

It is straightforward to see that
gue
determines an
unbiased
estimator of the ground-truth
\theta
with minimum variance given by the
crlb
(
crlb
). To show this, we first note that since the
gue
estimator is a linear function of
\mathbf{y}
as shown in (
19
), it is also Gaussian, with mean
\theta\mathbf{t}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{r}
and variance
\mathbf{t}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Sigma}\mathbf{t}
, where

\mathbf{t}=\frac{\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{r}}{\mathbf{r}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{r}}
.
Simplifying, we can write

g_{\mathsf{ML}}(\mathbf{y})\sim\mathcal{CN}(\theta,\frac{1}{\mathbf{r}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{r}}),

(21)

which concludes that

g_{\mathsf{ML}}(\mathbf{y})
is an unbiased estimator.
The estimation error is thus characterized by its variance.

We next show that
g_{\mathsf{ML}}(\mathbf{y})
achieves the
crlb
. Under the regularity conditions,
the
crlb
states that the variance of any
unbiased
estimator
\hat{\theta}(\mathbf{y})
is lower bounded by the
reciprocal of its Fisher information,
i.e.
,

\mathbb{E}_{p_{\theta}(\mathbf{y})}\left[\lvert\hat{\theta}(\mathbf{y})-\theta\rvert^{2}\right]\geq\frac{1}{\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{y}}(\theta)},

(22)

where the Fisher information is defined as

\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{y}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{p_{\theta}(\mathbf{y})}\left[|\nabla_{\theta}\log p_{\theta}(\mathbf{y})|^{2}\right].

(23)

For the
gue
estimator, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{y}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{p_{\theta}(\mathbf{y})}[|\mathbf{r}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}(\mathbf{y}-(\theta-\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\mu})\mathbf{r})|^{2}],

\displaystyle=\mathbf{r}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{r},~

(24)

whose reciprocal coincides with the variance of
g_{\mathsf{ML}}\left(\mathbf{y}\right)
. Since the Fisher information exists, and integration and differentiation are interchangeable, the regularity conditions are satisfied and
g_{\mathsf{ML}}\left(\mathbf{y}\right)
achieves the minimum variance.

Considering the unbiasedness of the
gue
estimator, we can design the power matrix
\mathbf{B}
to minimize the variance of the estimator, or equivalently, maximize the Fisher information. This leads to the following power allocation policy:

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{B}}\quad

\displaystyle(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{v})^{\mathsf{H}}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{B}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{H}}+\sigma^{2}_{z}\mathbf{I}_{N})^{-1}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{v}

(25)

s.t.

\displaystyle|\beta_{k}|^{2}\leq P\quad\forall k\in[K].

Note that this power allocation policy constitutes the same type of optimization as in
aircomp
aggregation,
i.e.
, (
11
).

IV-D

Complexity of
gue
Aggregation

The overall complexity of either
aircomp
or
gue
comprises two components: (i) model aggregation, and (ii) power allocation policy. The subsequent discussion compares these components for these two methods.

Considering the complexity of model aggregation, both methods compute their estimator of the global model via linear receive beamforming,
i.e.
, as
\mathbf{b}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{w}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\mu}
. The beamforming vector
\mathbf{b}
is computed differently in these approaches; see (
9
) and (
20
). The two methods share the same order of computation complexity for determining
\mathbf{b}
from the channel and power matrix: both approaches require a matrix inversion of complexity
\mathcal{O}(N^{3})
.

With respect to power allocation, we note that the design problems in (
11
) and (
25
) impose the same computational complexity on the system. In fact, both problems are non-convex, and their exact solutions are intractable. We thus approximate the solution iteratively via
slsqp
(
slsqp
)
[
17
]
. In each iteration,
slsqp
builds a quadratic approximation of the objective and a linear approximation of the constraints around the current optimization variable. These approximations define a quadratic program (QP) with a least-squares penalty, which is solved by a standard QP solver; see
[
18
, Chapter 25]
.

From the above discussions, we can conclude that
aircomp
and
gue
impose the same computational overhead on the system. The performance gains, reported in our numerical experiments, are hence obtained at no extra computation cost.

VI
Conclusions

We developed a new framework for model aggregation in wireless distributed learning. By fitting
aircomp
to this framework, we showed that conventional over-the-air aggregation based on
aircomp
implicitly considers a mismatched assumption on the local model parameters. Based on this finding, we proposed an alternative aggregation scheme, called
gue
, which directly estimates the global model from its noisy observation. Our experimental results depict significant gains, showing close-to-ideal learning performance with
gue
at low
snr
. The results of this study suggest that over-the-air aggregation can be effectively addressed at more realistic operating points,
e.g.
, at significantly lower
snr
, than what earlier studies have reported.
