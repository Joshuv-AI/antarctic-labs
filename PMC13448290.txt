TLSB-33 PATTERNS OF CNS PROGRESSION AND OUTCOMES IN PATIENTS WITH METASTATIC BREAST CANCER TREATED WITH SACITUZUMAB GOVITECAN
Authors: Catarina Martins Maia, Danielle A Bazer, Lakene Raissa Kentsa, Amy Parampil, Justus Reiss, Mohamed R Sherief, Yuan Zhou, Daniel Heinemann Martinez, Cesar Santa-Maria, Solmaz Sahebjam, David O Kamson
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSacituzumab-govitecan (SG) is a Trop-2-directed antibody-drug conjugate that is FDA-approved for later-line therapy in metastatic triple-negative and HR-positive/HER2-negative breast cancer (MBC).Data on SGs central nervous system (CNS) activity is limited. We evaluated compartment-specific activity of SG, focusing on CNS and calvarial disease.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed Johns Hopkins Electronic Medical records (query closed April 2024).We included patients ≥18 years old, with breast cancer and ≥1 month of SG. We assessed demographics, patterns of disease progression, progression-free (PFS) and overall survival (OS).ResultsSeventy-three women were included (60% White, 29% Black), with a median age of 58 (range 25-85).Tumors were ER positive (49.3%) and PR positive (60.2%). 74% received SG in ≥ 3rd-line setting. Median time to progression was 4.6 months (range 0.5-16.4 [IQR 2.8-8.2]).Before starting SG, 7 patients (9.6%) had CNS metastases and 5 (6.8%) had calvarial metastases. During follow-up on SG, 7 (9.6%) developed new CNS metastases, 2 (2.7%) developed new calvarial metastases. Progression of pre-existing CNS disease occurred in 13 (17.8%) (including 5.5% dural/leptomeningeal), and calvarial progression in 3 (4.1%).Mean CNS PFS was 13 months (CI95% 11.3-14.6) in the whole group. Mean CNS metastasis-free survival (i.e. no prior CNS involvement) was 14.1 (CI95% 12.6-15.7) months. Calvarial mPFS was prolonged (15.7 months [CI95% 14.6-16.9]), whereas visceral mPFS was shortest (5.4 months [CI95% 4.1-6.7]).Median OS was 8.9 months (95% CI 5.2–12.6), with no significant difference by presence of prior CNS metastases (7.7 [CI95% 1.9-13.5] vs 9.7 months [CI95% 5.6-13]), p = 0.2).Eleven (15.1%) of known causes of death were deemed neurologic.ConclusionSG showed modest activity in this heavily pretreated MBC cohort, with outcomes driven by visceral disease whereas CNS or calvarial progression occurred more than twice as late. Prior CNS metastases did not worsen OS. These findings suggest potential CNS-activity, offset by limited visceral disease control, warranting further investigation.
