 Executive Summary
In accordance with DOE M 435.1-1, Radioactive Waste Management Manual,
requirements, the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Hanford Field Office (HFO)
prepared this Annual Summary Report (ASR) for fiscal year (FY) 2025 for the Hanford
Site Composite Analysis (CA) reported in DOE/RL-2019-52, Composite Analysis for Low-
Level Waste Disposal in the Hanford Site Central Plateau (FY 2020) (hereinafter referred
to as the Hanford Site Composite Analysis). The preparation of this ASR follows the
technical specifications provided in DOE-STD-5002-2017, Disposal Authorization
Statement and Tank Closure Documentation.

The planning phase of current Hanford Site CA was conducted in FY 2015. The scoping
phase for preparation of the CA was conducted in FY 2016 to 2017, which included the
development of the scope and detailed technical approach for the CA. The analysis
phase commenced following approval of the scope and technical approach by the
Hanford Site Groundwater/Vadose Zone Executive Council in June 2017 (CP-60649,
Summary Analysis: Hanford Site Composite Analysis Update). Due to a shift in priority
with respect to modeling resources, completion of the draft updated CA was delayed until
FY 2020. A decisional draft was submitted to DOE-RL in September 2020 and, following
comment resolution, the draft CA (issued as Revision 0) was submitted to DOE’s Low-
Level Radioactive Waste Federal Review Group (LFRG) review team in December 2020.
Webinars providing background and overview of the updated Hanford Site CA were
provided to the LFRG review team in December 2020 and January 2021. An onsite
review was conducted in February 2021, and the findings of the LFRG review team were
issued in April 2021.

The review team identified 8 Key Issues, 15 Secondary Issues, 13 Observations, and
6 Best Management Practices for the Revision 0 document. Corrective Actions were
drafted to address the above 8 Key Issues (as well as the 15 Secondary Issues) and the
13 Observations and submitted in Issue Resolution Forms to the LFRG. After closing all
8 key issues, and 13 of the 15 secondary issues, the Revision 2 of the CA document
(DOE/RL-2019-52, Rev. 2) was approved by DOE on September 20, 2023.

The following two secondary issues remain open and will be closed after field data
become available for the following:

• HCA-S09-CA17-01 “The CA lacks discussion on the validation of the vadose zone
 modeling to available observed data,” and

discoveries, proposed actions, and new information identified for the reporting period of fiscal year
(FY) 2025 (October 1, 2024, to September 30, 2025) of the Hanford Site Composite Analysis (CA)
(DOE/RL-2019-52, Composite Analysis for Low-Level Waste Disposal in the Hanford Site Central Plateau
(FY 2020)). The U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) requires the Hanford Site maintain the CA and
performance assessments (PAs) according to DOE M 435.1-1, Radioactive Waste Management Manual
for DOE O 435.1, Radioactive Waste Management.

• DOE/RL-2021-26, Performance Assessment of Active Trenches in 200 East and West Low-Level

• CP-60089, Performance Assessment for the Environmental Restoration Disposal Facility, Hanford
 Site; and

• The on-going PA of Waste Management Area (WMA) A/AX at the Hanford Site.

 2 Cumulative Effects of Changes
Based on the information and discussion presented above as well as in Appendix B with respect to the
Hanford Site CA plus the review of the individual ASRs for the 200 East and 200 West Low-Level Burial
Grounds (LLBG) PAs (DOE/HFO-2025-32, Annual Summary Report (FY 2025): Performance
Assessment for the Disposal of Low Level Waste in the 200 East Area Burial Grounds, and
DOE/HFO-2025-33, Annual Summary Report (FY 2025): Performance Assessment for the Disposal of
Low Level Waste in the 200 West Area Burial Grounds), Environmental Restoration Disposal Facility
(ERDF) PA (DOE/HFO-2025-34, Annual Summary Report (FY 2025): Performance Assessment for the
Environmental Restoration and Disposal Facility), and Integrated Disposal Facility (IDF) PA
(DOE/HFO-2025-35, Annual Summary Report (FY 2025): Performance Assessment for the Integrated
Disposal Facility), no changes or information were noted that would have a significant effect individually.

The Hanford Site CA simulates the vadose zone for the past, present, and future to represent the
distribution of water and radiological contaminants in the vadose zone from past practices and future
releases. Monitoring and remediation activities are ongoing within the domain of the CA and were
considered as part of the development of the CA, which ultimately evaluates peak dose starting from the
time of assumed future site closure. For the Hanford Site CA, site closure was assumed to occur in
calendar year (CY) 2070 based on the Hanford Site baseline disposition at the time of analysis, and dose
projections calculated for a 1,000-year compliance period (CY 2070 to 3070) as well as for an additional
9,000-year postcompliance period (CY 3070 to 12070).

Many past-practice disposals involved large liquid discharges of waste, with the associated hydraulic
influences on vadose zone hydraulics (including transient perched water impacts above the
Cold Creek unit in the 200 West Area and the enduring perched water impact below the B Complex in
the 200 East Area). These impacts then influence the subsurface transport of radionuclide contaminants
in the impacted vadose zone in these locations. Appendix B provides summary detail for cleanup and
monitoring activities occurring within the domain of the CA for the reporting period. The ongoing
performance of these activities is consistent with the assumptions of the CA, and elements with potential
to cause significant effects to the CA were considered as identified in Section 1. Individual consideration
of these elements does not cause significant effects relative to the assumptions or conclusions of the CA.
Considered cumulatively, the net effect of changes would be an expectation of a lower overall peak dose
at the time of closure.

3.4 Integrated Disposal Facility
While the CA supports the IDF PA, the IDF remains in a pre-operational state and has not yet received

3.5 US Ecology Commercial LLW Disposal Facility
The US Ecology commercial LLW disposal facility is included in the CA because this facility is located on
the Central Plateau where there is potential for its releases to contribute to cumulative dose in the
groundwater pathway. Details for the disposed wastes in the US Ecology commercial LLW disposal
facility are presented in its annual utilization report (US Ecology, 2016, US Ecology Washington – 2016
Facility Utilization Report). The latest Environmental, Social & Governance Report was published in 2020
(Ecology, 2020, 2020 Environmental, Social & Governance Report).

 4 Monitoring
Monitoring of water and air for contaminants (both radiological and chemical) is an ongoing program

5.1 Remediation Science and Technology
The Hanford Site uses science and technology to provide new knowledge, data, and tools necessary to
accomplish the site cleanup mission. This mission includes investigating technologies and approaches to
improve characterization and remediation of contaminated soil sites and groundwater, as well as
resolving key technical issues that help to inform and influence decisions for remediation, waste
management, and site closure. Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL) has conducted applied
science and technology work that supports Hanford Site soil and groundwater characterization and
remediation activities. Work has been funded directly from DOE-RL through the Deep Vadose Zone
Program, under the site soil and groundwater contractor (Central Plateau Cleanup Company), and the
tank operations contractor (Hanford Tank Waste Operations and Closure (H2C)).

Summaries of applied science and technology efforts in FY 2025 relevant to radionuclide migration in the
Central Plateau are as follows:
• Continued technical evaluation of hybrid ion exchange (IX) resins to support future deployment of
 multi-contaminant (technetium, uranium, chromium, and iodine) groundwater treatment within the
 Hanford 200 West Pump & Treat (P&T) facility. In alignment with DOE’s goal to reduce groundwater
 treatment costs while optimizing removal of multiple contaminants, FY 2025 flow-through column
 studies assessed two resin configurations with potential application at the facility: (1) mixed-bed
 columns containing multiple resins blended together and (2) in-series columns with resins stacked
 sequentially. These bench-scale performance results build on previous PNNL evaluations of hybrid
 ion exchange (IX) media and will guide recommendations for larger-scale testing and alternative
 treatment-train designs, including potential evaluation using the Intermediate-Scale Test Platform
 (ISTP) developed in FY 2024.

• Completed the final phase of performance testing to evaluate in situ remediation technologies for
 inorganic and radionuclide contaminants in the 200-DV-1 Operable Unit. Testing began in FY 2022
 and included laboratory evaluations of nine gas, liquid, and particulate-phase technologies to
 determine the rate and longevity of contaminant immobilization using relevant site-specific conditions.
 Results will be documented in a final report in FY 2026. Ongoing collaboration with key stakeholders,
 including sponsors, regulators, and tribal representatives, aims to deliver a comprehensive approach
 to technology effectiveness evaluations for consideration of future field-scale implementation.

• Maintained and enhanced PHOENIX (PNNL-Hanford Online Environmental Information Exchange),
 ensuring secure, reliable access to contemporary and historical Hanford Site data and strengthening
 transparency and engagement among DOE, regulators, and stakeholder communities. PHOENIX’s
 public-facing web-GIS tools support data visualization and evaluation of cleanup progress and in FY
 2025, cyber security and application updates improved sustainability, security and user experience.
 PHOENIX also continued to support monthly updates to the Remediation Dashboard providing clear
 visualization of Hanford groundwater treatment system performance and progress.

• Continued to mature and expand data analytics and visualization capabilities within SOCRATES
 (Suite Of Comprehensive Rapid Analysis Tools for Environmental Sites; socrates.pnnl.gov),
 strengthening end-users’ ability to evaluate remedy performance, identify optimization opportunities,
 communicate findings, and design exit strategies that lead to site closure. In FY 2025, an emphasis
 for SOCRATES has been to transition from feature development to deployment for routine use by
 Hanford Site staff. Progress was also made toward a long-term stewardship plan for the continuous
 use, hosting, operation, and maintenance of SOCRATES, including future integration with PHOENIX.

• Advanced the use of machine-learning and data-driven techniques to support remedy optimization for
 the 200 West P&T system. In FY 2025, enhancements were made to the computational P&T
 optimization tools originally developed in FY 2023. Enhancements include expanded multi-objective
 optimization capabilities to evaluate extraction strategies that improve contaminant mass recovery
 while maintaining required hydraulic pumping capacities. These updates enable rapid assessment of
 well-network adjustments, pumping configurations, and P&T capacity needs to meet cleanup

 5-3

 objectives and minimize active pumping timeframes. The enhanced optimization functions are being
 integrated into the SOCRATES platform to provide a user-accessible interface for scenario
 exploration and strategy evaluation, facilitating more informed and effective decision-making.

• Completed laboratory-scale evaluation of a silica gel technology designed to isolate vertical intervals
 in existing Hanford groundwater wells, enabling depth-discrete contaminant measurements by
 minimizing unwanted vertical flow and mixing during sampling. Building on promising FY 2024 results,
 additional experimental testing and numerical modeling in FY 2025 assessed gel longevity to inform
 potential future field-scale pilot testing. Successful deployment of this technology into the filter pack
 annulus of existing wells would reduce uncertainty in long-term remediation performance monitoring
 by providing a more accurate representation of the vertical distribution of contaminants in the
 groundwater well-aquifer system and ultimately support more targeted and cost-effective P&T
 remediation strategies.

• Continued advancing new approaches to identify discrete high-concentration contamination zones to
 better focus groundwater P&T extraction. Understanding the locations of persistent, high-
 concentration zones is critical for efficient contaminant mass removal and progress to closure with
 P&T remedy. These zones can be challenging to identify, particularly with long-screened wells,
 because samples commonly represent flow-weighted averages of concentrations in the aquifer
 surrounding the well. In FY 2025, field-based demonstrations were completed in four new P&T
 extraction wells (prior to connection with the P&T facility) to understand the vertical distribution of
 aquifer hydraulic properties and contaminant concentrations. This information will improve
 understanding and prediction of contaminant mass removal in these P&T wells and inform better
 screen design, placement, and construction to more effectively target high-concentration zones that
 prolong remediation in the Hanford 200 Areas.

• Evaluated the performance of spectral induced polarization (SIP) as a minimally invasive, real-time
 geophysical tool for monitoring delivery, distribution, and performance of in situ remediation
 technologies. Cost-effective approaches to characterize subsurface conditions and monitor
 biogeochemical reactions before, during, and after amendment injections are needed. In FY 2025,
 multi-scale laboratory experiments evaluated SIP to monitor delivery, subsequent geochemical
 reactions, and formation of solid-phases with liquid apatite forming solutions. Liquid amendments that
 form apatite have been used successfully in the past at Hanford and are likely to be considered for
 future treatments. SIP has the potential to provide field-based confirmation and indication of
 remediation performance for these future treatment activities.

• Continued to maintain a long-term monitoring program for the prototype Hanford barrier (PHB). It is
 expected that surface barriers will be a part of the future remediation strategy to contain vadose zone
 contaminant mass flux at levels where groundwater concentration limits will not be exceeded. The
 PHB, located in the 200 East Area of the Hanford Site, has been monitored for about 25 years with
 neutron moisture logging and direct measures of water drainage. Continued maintenance of the
 drainage system as well as collection and comparison of standard and geophysical estimates of
 water content provides unique information on the maturation of a surface barrier..

• Completed the first phase of development and evaluation of aqueous chemistry formulations to better
 represent key vadose-zone porewater and groundwater compositions across the Hanford Site. In
 FY 2025, cluster analysis and spatial analysis identified distinguishing factors influencing subsurface
 aqueous chemistry. This information will support interpretation and refinement of the conceptual site
 model for hydraulically connected areas, connecting groundwater contaminant plumes to vadose
 zone sources. Future geochemical and geophysical evaluations related to remediation of Hanford
 vadose zone and groundwater will rely on this information to ensure specific waste site and operable
 unit conditions are fully represented.

5.2 Cumulative Impact Evaluation
The CIE is a set of tools used to perform an evaluation of cumulative impacts on the effects of cleanup
decisions regarding groundwater quality in the Hanford Site Central Plateau. The technical approach for
the development of the CIE is documented in a technical approach document (DOE/RL-2018-69,
Cumulative Impact Evaluation Technical Approach Document). The CIE is analogous to the CA for the
Hanford Site but is focused on providing a cumulative impacts evaluation for CERCLA and RCRA
regulatory needs for groundwater remediation and protection, while the CA is focused on meeting
DOE O 435.1 requirements for a dose assessment. The CIE and the CA use many of the same models
but applied for different timeframes (1,000 years from present for the CIE; 1,000- and 10,000-years
postclosure for the CA), for different contaminants (radionuclides and hazardous chemicals for the CIE;
radionuclides only for the CA), and different source types (liquid discharge sites only for the CIE; liquid
discharge and solid waste disposal sites for the CA).

The CIE has a separate maintenance program that was developed based on experience with the
maintenance programs for DOE O 435.1 assessments (although the CIE is not subject to DOE O 435.1
maintenance requirements). The CIE maintenance program is presented in CP-67156, CIE Maintenance
Plan, and the first annual maintenance report issued under the CIE maintenance program is CP-68492,
Rev. 4, Cumulative Impact Evaluation Annual Maintenance Report for FY 2025.

The CIE annual maintenance report documents and evaluates the updates and changes on the Hanford
Site Central Plateau potentially affecting the CIE for FY 2025. The maintenance program covers three
major facets of the CIE and their supporting components. The primary CIE modeling facets that are
subjects of the annual maintenance report are the inventory, vadose zone, and saturated zone, which are
supported by various components and information sources. Some of the components that support these
facets include geoframework models, the Hanford Site disposition baseline (HSDB) database, the
recharge evolution tool, and other supporting data inputs and processing utilities. The CIE facets are
maintained to ensure the most current and best available information is utilized to support the models.
The purpose of this report is to document changes to the supporting information that may be useful to
enhance the CIE as a tool to support the decision making process.

The current set of models and computational tools developed for each CIE facet (inventory, vadose zone,
and saturated zone) for evaluating groundwater impacts are supported by various model package reports
(MPRs) and environmental calculation files (ECFs). Each facet and component is documented in detail
through an MPR and/or ECF, which describes the modeling architecture, software, inputs, and outputs.
The MPR also includes a recommendations chapter for future updates of the model along with any data
needs.

5.3 Carbon-14 Mobilization and Mobility
Development activities for the Hanford Site CA revealed the need to prepare for a future disposal facility
for surplus production reactor blocks consistent with DOE decisions regarding this waste form
(58 FR 48509, “Record of Decision; Decommissioning of Eight Surplus Production Reactors at the
Hanford Site, Richland, Washington”; 75 FR 43158, “Amended Record of Decision for the
Decommissioning of Eight Surplus Production Reactors at the Hanford Site, Richland, WA”;
DOE/EIS-0119F, Addendum (Final Environmental Impact Statement): Decommissioning of Eight Surplus
Production Reactors at the Hanford Site, Richland, Washington; DOE/EIS-0119, Final Environmental
Impact Statement, Decommissioning of Eight Surplus Production Reactors at the Hanford Site, Richland,
Washington; DOE/EIS-0199, Record of Decision: Decommissioning of Eight Surplus Production Reactors
at the Hanford Site, WA). This anticipated additional source on the Central Plateau was not included in
the detailed evaluation in DOE/RL-2019-52 in response to Key Issue HCA-K02-CA09-01. Only after a
disposal facility for this source is sited, designed, and has an approved PA will it be included in the
detailed evaluation of the CA.

However, preliminary work to evaluate this future source using CA tools revealed that the waste form
release model for carbon-14 currently available is excessively conservative (a zero-order release model)

 5-5

and the most defensible sorption value for carbon-14 from this source (zero, representing no sorption for
this radionuclide in the Hanford Site’s subsurface environment) is also excessively conservative, but R&D
is necessary to reduce these sources of conservatism. Such R&D is worth pursuing to prepare for a future
PA that will be required by to DOE to pursue a disposal concept and facility for this waste form. R&D
recommendations were passed through the LFRG co-chairs to the multi-university Consortium for Risk
Evaluation with Stakeholder Participation (CRESP).

CRESP reported no information on this initiative during this reporting period.

6.1 Changes in Site Land Use and Remediation Plans
There have been no changes in site land use in FY 2025 that will affect the Central Plateau Remediation
planning for implementing approved decision documents.

The overall cleanup strategy for the 195 km2 (75 mi2) Central Plateau is described in DOE/RL-2009-10.
The cleanup strategy is the result of work that considered input from the regulatory agencies, Tribal
Nations, the public, and stakeholders. Selection of cleanup remedies that are consistent with
the reasonably anticipated future land use is one of the foundational elements of the Central Plateau
strategy. The strategy calls for cleanup on the Central Plateau to be organized into the following three
major components:

• Inner Area: The final footprint area of the Hanford Site that will be dedicated to waste management
 and containment of residual contamination.

• Outer Area: All of the Central Plateau beyond the boundary of the Inner Area.

• Groundwater: Contaminant plumes underlying the Central Plateau and originating from waste sites
 on the Central Plateau.
These components are consistent with land uses designated for the Central Plateau as follows:

• DOE/EIS-0222F, Final Hanford Comprehensive Land-Use Plan Environmental Impact Statement;
 subsequent ROD (64 FR 61615, “Record of Decision: Hanford Comprehensive Land-Use Plan
 Environmental Impact Statement [HCP EIS]”);

 Land-Use Plan Environmental Impact Statement);

• Subsequent ROD (73 FR 55824, “Amended Record of Decision for the Hanford Comprehensive
 Land-Use Plan Environmental Impact Statement”);

• And 2015 supplement analysis (DOE/EIS-0222-SA-02, Supplement Analysis of the Hanford
 Comprehensive Land-Use Plan Environmental Impact Statement).

in the surrounding 145 km2 (55 mi2) area (which includes the majority of the Outer Area).

B1.2 Integrated Disposal Facility Performance Assessment
The Integrated Disposal Facility (IDF) is a near-surface disposal facility for LLW and MLLW generated at
the Hanford Site. Waste that will be disposed of in the IDF includes vitrified low-activity waste produced at
the Hanford Tank Waste Treatment and Immobilization Plant (WTP), secondary wastes from the WTP,
and other Hanford secondary waste generated during Resource Conservation and Recovery Act of 1976
(RCRA)-related activities. Figure B-2 shows the location of the IDF. In a 2001 memorandum (Scott, 2001,
“Disposal Authorization for the Hanford Site Low-Level Waste Disposal Facilities – Revision 2”),
DOE approved DOE/ORP-2000-24, Hanford Immobilized Low-Activity Waste Performance Assessment:
2001 Version. Continuation of the disposal authorization statement (DAS) (Frei, 2003, “Review of
the Annual Summary of the Hanford Immobilized Low-Activity Waste Performance Assessment for 2003”)
was based in part on RPP-15834, Integrated Disposal Facility Risk Assessment. This PA is maintained in
accordance with DOE/ORP-2000-01, Maintenance Plan for the Hanford Integrated Disposal Facility
Performance Assessment, until 2012 when the Tank Closure and Waste Management Environment
Impact Statement (hereinafter referred to as the TC & WM EIS) (DOE/EIS-0391, Final Tank Closure and
Waste Management Environmental Impact Statement for the Hanford Site, Richland, Washington (TC &
WM EIS)) was completed.

comments received at the scoping workshops was approved by the DOE Hanford Site

Groundwater/Vadose Zone Executive Council, and modeling for the updated PA commenced.
The summary analysis is a tool used to meet DOE direction (1301789, 2012) by describing the technical

use in a probabilistic, integrated system model completed in the third quarter of FY 2017. The system
model was used to evaluate parameter uncertainty and assess the long-term dose and groundwater
impact from constituents that would be released from the facility over the next 10,000 years.

In FY 2024, significant advancements were made in the preparation of the "FY2024 Data Package for
EWG ILAW Glass Release Calculations to Support IDF Draft for WRPS Review," which plays a pivotal
role in the management of immobilized low-activity waste (ILAW) at the Hanford Site's Integrated
Disposal Facility (IDF). This data package consolidates and updates critical information regarding
Enhanced Waste Glass (EWG) release calculations, incorporating data from previous evaluations and
new findings from the 2024 ILAW glass program summary report. The report focuses on the application of
the ILAW glass corrosion model (IGCM) to predict the long-term performance of ILAW glass waste forms,
specifically assessing their potential contaminant release over compliance periods extending up to 10,000
years. By providing detailed parameters for calculating fractional release rates (FRRs) based on various
glass compositions and site-specific backfill properties, the report enhances the understanding of how
these waste forms will behave in the IDF environment.

This shift towards utilizing EWG compositions represents a significant change in the approach to waste
management at the IDF, necessitating a UDQE followed by a Special Analysis to thoroughly evaluate the
impact on the IDF PA dose. The findings from FY 2024 were instrumental in informing this analysis, which
is essential for understanding the long-term implications of the new glass compositions on public health
and environmental resources. Additionally, the results will need to be incorporated into the risk budget
tool, ensuring that all potential risks associated with the disposal of EWG are adequately assessed and
managed. The ongoing updates to this document will ensure that the IDF's operational strategies remain
informed by the latest scientific insights, thereby supporting effective and safe management of radioactive
waste and reinforcing the facility's commitment to environmental stewardship.

The following six items were updated in FY 2025:

• Waste Acceptance Criteria for the IDF (IDF-00002) were revised and issued as Revision 1
 (published February 2025). The revision was cleared for public release on February 11, 2025,
 following Central Plateau Cleanup Company (CPCCo) information clearance (sign-offs and release
 record on file). The updated WAC maintains alignment with DOE O 435.1 and the Hanford RCRA
 Permit and explicitly carries forward the compliance framing in Section 1.0 (purpose/scope;
 Washington Administrative Code [WAC] 173-303 applicability). Major changes in Rev. 1 include (1)
 addition of Grout Standards (Section 3.4) with an accompanying crosswalk of acceptable formulations
 (Appendix J) that tie directly to the IDF PA performance assumptions; (2) updates to the radiological
 acceptance limits (Appendix G); (3) reference to use of Waste Shipping and Receiving
 Plans (WSRPs) in Section 2.5; and (4) clarification edits throughout. The new Grout Standards
 specify the evaluated formulations (e.g., Hanford Grout Mix 5 for debris macroencapsulation and non-
 debris stabilization/solidification, and a hydrated-lime formulation for Effluent Treatment Facility (ETF)
 brines) and require generator certification to target compositions with Appendix J serving as the
 controlling crosswalk. Rev. 1’s Appendix G provides the updated radionuclide concentration limits
 used for acceptance screening (e.g., tabulated limits for H-3, I-129, Cs-137, Ni-59/63, etc.). Together,
 these Waste Acceptance Criteria changes preserve the linkage to the approved IDF PA and RCRA

 B-12

 permit conditions while incorporating FY 2024/2025 PA maintenance outcomes into waste-form,
 dose-basis, and acceptance-limit controls.

• The Integrated Waste Tracking Requirements Document (IWTRD; HFO-72080) was revised to
 ensure consistency with the updated IDF Waste Acceptance Criteria (IDF-00002, Rev. 1) and
 supporting IDF Performance Assessment documentation. The IWTRD defines the data management,
 verification, and documentation requirements for tracking waste from generation through acceptance
 into the IDF. The FY 2025 revision clarified interfaces between DOE-ORP, CPCCo, and the tank
 operations contractor and updated waste stream definitions and tracking requirements to align with
 current facility operations and regulatory expectations. These updates improve traceability, quality
 assurance, and recordkeeping to support compliance with DOE O 435.1 and the Hanford RCRA
 Permit, ensuring consistent implementation of waste acceptance and transfer procedures as the IDF
 transitions toward active operations.

• The Solid Waste Technical Requirements Document (SWTRD; CPCC-01210) was updated to
 reflect alignment with the revised IDF Waste Acceptance Criteria (IDF-00002, Rev. 1) and current PA
 assumptions. The SWTRD establishes the technical and design requirements for packaging,
 handling, and disposal of solid waste destined for the IDF. The FY 2025 revision incorporated
 updates to waste form specifications, container performance standards, and grout formulation
 references consistent with the new Grout Standards introduced in the Waste Acceptance Criteria.
 Editorial changes were also made to improve clarity, remove outdated references, and ensure
 consistency with DOE O 435.1 and the Hanford RCRA Permit. Collectively, these updates maintain
 technical continuity between waste generators and IDF operations as preparations continue for

B2.1.1 Waste Management Area C
The development of a Phase 2 RFI/CMS for the Hanford SST system has also been completed at
WMA C (RPP-RPT-58339, Rev. 0 and RPP-RPT-59329, Rev. 0, respectively). The goal of this RFI/CMS
process is to identify and evaluate actions that may be necessary to protect human health and
the environment from subsurface soil contamination associated with the past operations in the SST
system for WMA C.

The corrective measures study recommendation for a preferred alternative for WMA C is implementation
of an isolation barrier and infiltration barrier system (Alternative 4). The estimated cost to implement
Alternative 4 is between approximately $19 and $41 million dollars. Implementation of Alternative 4 would
mitigate human health and environment risks by placing concrete isolation barriers over localized areas
where shallow soil contamination levels exceed risk thresholds. The infiltration barrier system would
reduce infiltration and slow the migration of contaminants to the groundwater. While the infiltration barrier
would not reduce the anticipated peak impacts to groundwater, there would be a reduction in contaminant
flux over the long term. The isolation barriers would reduce the risk of direct-contact exposure to
acceptable human health risk levels and reduce infiltration, pending placement of the final closure cap
that will be installed as a part of WMA C closure. This final closure cap represents the permanent
risk-mitigation measure for protecting human health and the environment for WMA C and will be installed
as part of the closure process after implementing the preferred alternative (Alternative 4) of this corrective
measure study.

64144, Supporting Information for Corrective Action Decision-Making Process and Closure at Waste
Management Area C, Rev. 0) was submitted to Ecology on July 24, 2024. This document was cited as
supplemental information for inclusion to the Hanford Facility RCRA permit renewal for Revision 9A,

B3.1 Interim Surface Barriers
As a part of the commitments made between DOE and Ecology under Milestone M-045-56, interim
surface barriers have been constructed over portions of the 241-T, 241-TY, 241-SX, 241-U, and
241-TX Tank Farms. The barriers minimize meteoric water from infiltration into contaminated vadose
zone soils within the tank farms. Infiltration of water into subsurface soils with preexisting contamination
can accelerate the mobilization of contaminants toward the water table. The interim surface barriers at
241-T Tank Farm and 241-TY Tank Farm were constructed as part of the Interim Surface Barrier
Demonstration Project (PNNL-16538, T Tank Farm Interim Surface Barrier Demonstration – Vadose Zone
Monitoring Plan, and PNNL-19772, T-TY Tank Farm Interim Surface Barrier Demonstration—Vadose
Zone Monitoring Plan) and were completed in 2008 and 2010, respectively. The interim surface barriers
at 241-SX Tank Farm were completed in 2019, and the interim surface barrier at 241-TX Tank Farm was
completed in 2021.

The design for the newest interim surface barrier at the 241-U Tank Farm was completed in 2021 and
construction of an evapotranspiration ET basin was completed in FY 2022. Final construction of
the interim surface barrier at the 241-U Tank Farm was completed in October of 2023.

Monitoring is conducted in accordance with RPP-RPT-61684, Maintenance and Performance Monitoring
Plan for the Interim Barriers Program. The annual interim surface barrier monitoring report for FY 2024
(RPP-RPT-65587, FY 2024 Interim Surface Barriers Annual Monitoring Report) was released in July
2025.

In FY 2022, as a part of an Agreed Order to mitigate past leaks at Tanks 241-T-111 and 241-B-109, DOE
reached agreement with Ecology in Agreed Order Docket No. 21304 to design and construct new interim
surface barriers at 241-T and 241-B Tank Farms by September 31, 2028.

In FY 2023, a design consideration report (RPP-RPT-64363, 241-T Tank Farm Interim Surface Barrier
Design Considerations Report) for the new T Tank Farm Interim Surface Barrier (ISB) and an associated

farm was completed.

 B4 Central Plateau CERCLA Remedial Activities
CERCLA remedial activities directed by DOE-HFO are pertinent to the Hanford Site Composite Analysis
because they result in planned redistributions of radioactive inventory considered in the Hanford Site
Composite Analysis in time, location, and waste form. Updated knowledge and information acquired while
conducting remedial actions have the potential to change the analysis evaluated in the Hanford Site
Composite Analysis; these are reviewed in this section to assess any such impact.

The Central Plateau is located near the middle of the Hanford Site and covers an area of approximately
195 km2 (75 mi2). Most activities are concentrated in two main processing areas: the 200 East Area and
the 200 West Area. The Central Plateau contains facilities formerly used in the plutonium production
process, including five large chemical processing facilities (commonly known as canyons) During FY 2025
no remedial activities or removal actions were conducted at the Central Plateau facilities.

Key elements of the Central Plateau are shown on Figure B-9. The approach for Central Plateau cleanup
focuses on the following three major components:
• Inner Area: the final footprint area of the Hanford Site that will be dedicated to waste management
 and containment of residual contamination.

• Outer Area: contains the balance of the Central Plateau.

• Groundwater: composed of contaminant plumes underlying the Central Plateau and originating from
 waste sites on the Central Plateau.
Several operating waste disposal facilities in the Inner Area will continue to receive waste from
Hanford Site cleanup activities and limited offsite sources. ERDF was constructed for the disposal of
waste generated during cleanup of the Hanford Site. Additional cells will be constructed in ERDF
as needed to implement cleanup decisions. The LLW or radioactive mixed waste generated from
Hanford Site activities have been disposed in LLBGs or mixed waste trenches, as appropriate. IDF has a
final status Hanford RCRA Permit for disposal of wastes generated from radioactive liquid waste tank
cleanup.

As CERCLA units are remediated, changes will occur in the composite analysis inventory. When waste
has been removed from one of these sites, it is considered “generated” waste and is added to
the inventory of the specific disposal facility for which it is intended. These inventory changes are tracked
in the various PA annual summary reports and are included in the most recent facility PAs. These
changes will be reflected in the location of the activity but not necessarily as a change in the total site
inventory unless unexpected circumstances are encountered. Some residual activity may remain at
a waste sites after remediation has been completed but is not expected to contribute to the Composite
Analysis inventory given that remediation will be completed in accordance with the final remedy identified
in the ROD.

B4.5 Other Central Plateau Remediation Activities
Other remediation activities on the Central Plateau (aside from source and groundwater OU activities) are
presented in this section. For FY 2025, confined aquifer monitoring and ERDF operations are activities
reported in this category.

B4.5.1 Confined Aquifer Monitoring
Although most Hanford Site groundwater contamination is found in the unconfined aquifer, wells in
deeper aquifers are monitored because of potential downward movement of contamination and their
potential migration offsite through confined aquifers. There is no evidence of offsite migration via
the confined aquifers. Appendix D of DOE/RL-2024-41, Hanford Site Groundwater Monitoring Report
for 2024, provides more detailed information about confined aquifer monitoring.

One confined aquifer occurs within sand and gravel at the base of the Ringold Formation. Carbon
tetrachloride, nitrate, and technetium-99 have contaminated this unit in a portion of the 200 West Area
where the upper confining unit is absent. New wells were installed in recent years to monitor and
remediate this contamination. The Ringold Formation confined aquifer is the uppermost aquifer in a
region east of the 200 East Area (within portions of the 200-BP-5 and 200-PO-1 OUs). Iodine-129 and
tritium are detected in wells at this location, but the contamination has not migrated farther to the east
and/or southeast.

Groundwater within basalt fractures and joints, interflow contacts, and sedimentary interbeds make up
the upper basalt-confined aquifer system. No significant contamination is detected in the basalt-confined
aquifer except in the northwestern 200 East Area, where poor well construction and temporary drilling
effects allowed local migration of groundwater from the overlying unconfined aquifer.

Leachate Monitoring. Each ERDF cell is double lined to collect leachate resulting from water added as
a dust suppressant and precipitation. The liner is sloped to a sump in each cell, and the leachate is
pumped from the sump to holding tanks. The leachate is then pumped to the 200 West Area P&T or
the Effluent Treatment Facility for treatment.

ERDF leachate is sampled for constituents identified in EPA/AMD/R10-99/038 and CP-60070,
Environmental Restoration Disposal Facility Leachate Sampling and Analysis Plan, formerly WCH-173
Rev. 2. The 1999 ERDF ROD amendment (EPA/AMD/R10-99/038) delisted leachate and identified
the necessary sampling parameters. Leachate samples are obtained directly from the holding tanks.
Constituents detected in ERDF leachate samples are then compared with the groundwater monitoring
analyte list to determine whether additional analytes should be added to the groundwater PA project.
Leachate data are also evaluated for trends and compared to specific action levels. Target analytes for
groundwater monitoring are consistent with the leachate monitoring program. Based on groundwater
sampling and leachate data, no impact to groundwater has occurred from ERDF facility or operations.
An annual report is prepared for ERDF that summarizes the leachate and groundwater monitoring data,
providing conclusions and recommendations as appropriate. The most recent report is
ERDF-00202, Groundwater, Leachate, and Lysimeter Monitoring and Sampling at the Environmental
Restoration Disposal Facility, Calendar Year 2024.

Current Inventory Estimates. The ERDF annual summary report will provide the annual activity inventory
of key radionuclides placed in ERDF from the inception of ERDF operations (July 1, 1996) through
September 30, 2025. In more than two decades since operation began, over 2.25E+05 Ci (inventory is
decayed to Year 2035 which is the assumed closure date) has been disposed at ERDF.

A DOE O 435.1 PA (the revised ERDF PA [WCH-520, subsequently renumbered to CP-60089, Rev. 0 in
2016]) was completed in FY 2013 (Section B1.5), and the revised DAS was issued in November 2013.
The DAS was revised again in 2024 to authorize the construction of Supercell 11. The inventory data
package developed for that PA (WCH-479) indicates the ERDF inventory estimate is conservative. ERDF
inventories are derived from data accumulated in the Waste Management Information System (WMIS)
database, which documents the ERDF waste acceptance process using waste profiles, onsite waste
tracking forms (online manifest for wastes that will be shipped to ERDF), truck scale weights, and
disposal records to calculate and accumulate the disposed inventory. One of the functions of the WMIS
database is to ensure that waste above ERDF waste acceptance criteria limits (ERDF-00011) is not
accepted for ERDF disposal. The waste acceptance process biases system inputs (e.g., profiles and
onsite waste tracking forms [i.e., ERDF manifest]) to the highest expected levels before comparison with
waste acceptance criteria. The net effect of this bias is to inflate ERDF radionuclide inventory. While this
bias does not allow for precise knowledge of the actual inventory, it does provide assurance that
inventory limits are not being exceeded, which is the intent of this process. Because of this deliberate
bias, it is inappropriate to expect that the listed ERDF inventories will match best estimate inventories
prepared for other purposes (e.g., the PA inventory data package or the inventory for the Hanford Site
Composite Analysis).

In the FY 2024 annual summary report for the ERDF PA, a special analysis was performed. The purpose
of the SA is to evaluate the effect of the additional disposal capacity from the approved construction of
Supercell 11 on performance of ERDF and to demonstrate compliance with the performance objectives
and performance measures identified in DOE M 435.1-1. Comparison of the dose results of the special
analysis against various performance objectives and performance metrics demonstrated that ERDF would
continue to comply with the DOE O 435.1 requirements at closure even after accounting for the additional
disposal capacity from construction of Supercell 11.

 C1 History of Hanford Site Composite Analysis Maintenance
DOE O 435.1, Radioactive Waste Management, requires that the Hanford Site maintain site performance
assessments (PAs) and composite analyses. Requirements for composite analysis maintenance under
DOE M 435.1-1, Radioactive Waste Management Manual, are the same as those for PA maintenance
and are described in Chapter 3 of DOE, 1999, Maintenance Guide for U.S. Department of Energy
Low-Level Waste Disposal Facility Performance Assessments and Composite Analyses. The plan for
maintaining the Hanford Site Composite Analysis (PNNL-11800, Composite Analysis for Low-Level Waste
Disposal in the 200 Area Plateau of the Hanford Site; PNNL-11800, Addendum to Composite Analysis for
Low-Level Waste Disposal in the 200 Area Plateau of the Hanford Site, Addendum 1) is described in

Washington (multiple revisions). Revision 2 of DOE/RL-2000-29 was approved in 2004 (Talarico, 2004,
“Low-Level Disposal Facility Federal Review Group Review of Maintenance Plan for the Composite
Analysis of the Hanford Site, Southeast Washington, April 2003”). Revision 3 of DOE/RL-2000-29 was
prepared in fiscal year (FY) 2017 to conform to DOE-STD-5002-2017, Disposal Authorization Statement
and Tank Closure Documentation, and was issued in FY 2018.

DOE M 435.1-1 requires routine review and revision of PAs and composite analyses. The objective of
routine review and revision is to ensure that the PAs and composite analyses are updated appropriately
whenever changes in their bases (e.g., assumptions and parameters) are contemplated or affected in
order to maintain the validity and effectiveness of the controls that are based on the PA and composite
analysis. These reviews provide a mechanism for routine assessment of the site plans (e.g., remediation,
closure, decommissioning, and land use) developed from the results of a composite analysis. This review
process allows potential problems to be identified and managed at an early stage. The revisions ensure
cohesive documentation, providing a reasonable basis to conclude that U.S. Department of Energy
requirements for radiological protection of the public and the environment will be met in the future.
The composite analysis is a planning tool that allows for evaluation of the cumulative effects of all sources
of radioactive materials that may interact with those in the Low-Level Waste Disposal Facility. The impact
of future activities on the dose to hypothetical future members of the public can be evaluated using
the composite analysis, and the results can be used to develop land-use plans, remediation plans, or
long-term stewardship documents. The annual review of the composite analysis is used to determine
whether actual and planned conditions are consistent with those contained in the composite analysis.
Revisions and special analyses provide a mechanism for evaluating conditions not originally included in
the composite analysis to determine if these conditions could be accommodated without violating
the conclusions of the composite analysis.

Chapter 4 in DOE G 435.1-1, Implementation Guide for use with DOE M 435.1-1, states the following:

 IV.P (4) Performance Assessment and Composite Analysis Maintenance. The performance
 assessment and composite analysis shall be maintained to evaluate changes that could
 affect the performance, design, and operating bases for the facility. Performance
 assessment and composite analysis maintenance shall include the conduct of research, field
 studies, and monitoring needed to address uncertainties or gaps in existing data.
 The performance assessment shall be updated to support the final facility closure. Additional
 iterations of the performance assessment and composite analysis shall be conducted as
 necessary during the post-closure period.

 Performance assessments and composite analyses shall be reviewed and revised when
 changes in waste forms or containers, radionuclide inventories, facility design and
 operations, closure concepts, or the improved understanding of the performance of
 the waste disposal facility in combination with the features of the site on which it is located
 alter the conclusions or the conceptual model(s) of the existing performance assessment or
 composite analysis.
