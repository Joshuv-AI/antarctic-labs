Punicalagin alleviates senile osteoporosis by inhibiting the senescence of bone mesenchymal stem cells via NRF2/HO-1 pathway
Authors: Yijun Wang, Yi Qin, Tianhao Wang, Wenhao Zhang, Kai Chen, Dechun Geng, Zhanjun Yan, Yaozeng Xu, Shujun Lv
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe senescence and abnormal osteogenic differentiation of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) are important causes of senile osteoporosis. Punicalagin (PUN), a natural polyphenol extracted from pomegranate peel, has demonstrated antioxidant activity in preclinical studies. This study aims to investigate the inhibitory effect of PUN on the aging of BMSCs and its therapeutic effect on senile osteoporosis.MethodsThe senescence of BMSCs were induced by hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) to assess the therapeutic effect of PUN. Intracellular reactive oxygen species measurements, the senescence β-galactosidase staining, western blotting, and reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) were conducted to evaluate the senescence of BMSCs. Alkaline phosphatase staining, Alizarin red S staining, western blotting, and RT-qPCR were performed to assess the osteogenic differentiation of senescent BMSCs. Transcriptome sequencing was conducted to explore the underlying mechanism. In vivo, the therapeutic effects of PUN were investigated in aging rats with senile osteoporosis.ResultsPUN reversed the senescence of BMSCs and promoted the osteogenic differentiation in a dose-dependent manner. Mechanistically, PUN activated the nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (NRF2)/heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) pathway to protect BMSCs from oxidative stress. In aging rats with senile osteoporosis, intraperitoneal injection of PUN can promote osteogenesis and reduce aging-related bone loss in vivo.ConclusionPUN alleviates senile osteoporosis by inhibiting the senescence of BMSCs via NRF2/HO-1 pathway, representing a candidate therapeutic drug for aging-related bone diseases.Keywords: Senile osteoporosis, Mesenchymal stem cells, Cellular senescence, NRF2, Punicalagin

Introduction

Senile osteoporosis is a prevalent disease among the elderly, characterized by reduced bone mineral density and compromised microstructure, which increases the risk of fractures and disability. With the global aging population, the incidence of senile osteoporosis is constantly rising, which has greatly impaired the health of the elderly [1,2]. Bone loss is regarded as an inevitable outcome of aging. The senescence of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) is one of the main causes of bone loss [3]. Senescent BMSCs are characterized by increased apoptosis, impaired mitochondrial network dynamics, and enhanced adipogenic differentiation, leading to a reduction in bone formation [4]. It has been reported that transplantation of young mouse-derived BMSCs into aged mice can significantly relieve the senescence of BMSCs and bone loss [5]. Therefore, therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring the function of senescent BMSCs have great potential in preventing and treating senile osteoporosis.

Oxidative stress plays an important role in the process of aging. Oxidative damage caused by reactive oxygen species (ROS) contributes to the hallmarks of aging, which lead to various age-related diseases [6]. In senile osteoporosis, BMSCs subjected to oxidative stress may undergo a process of premature senescence, with enhanced adipogenic differentiation and weakened osteogenic differentiation [7,8]. Some antioxidants, including resveratrol, lycopene, oleuropein, could have therapeutic effects on senile osteoporosis via combating ROS and inhibiting the senescence of BMSCs [9]. However, the pharmacokinetics and target affinity of these antioxidants vary greatly, resulting in some clinical trials not achieving the expected results [10]. In addition, the synergistic effect of different antioxidants often achieves better therapeutic effects than that of a single antioxidant [11,12]. Hence, identifying more antioxidants is conducive to optimizing the antioxidant strategy for treating diseases.

Punicalagin (2,3-(S) hexahydroxydiphenoyl-4,6-(S,S)-gallagyl-d-glucose, PUN) is the most abundant ellagitannin extracted from pomegranate peel [13]. Since pomegranate peel is the main by-product of pomegranate production, PUN is widely available and has broad application prospects [14,15]. As a natural polyphenol with antioxidant activity, PUN has been shown to attenuate oxidative damage and promote osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs [16]. In addition, our previous study indicated the protective effects of PUN on osteoporosis by inhibiting osteoclastogenesis and inflammation [17]. Nevertheless, it remains unclear whether PUN can delay the aging of BMSCs and alleviate senile osteoporosis.

In this study, we found that both in vivo and in vitro, PUN reverses the senescence of BMSCs and restores their osteogenic differentiation ability. Mechanistically, PUN activates the nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (NRF2)/heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) pathway and protects BMSCs from oxidative damages. With the antioxidant and anti-aging properties, PUN can alleviate senile osteoporosis and serve as a candidate therapeutic drug for aging-related diseases.

Materials and methods

The extraction and culture of BMSCs

BMSCs were isolated from bone marrow of the Sprague-Dawley rats and cultured in complete a-MEM. BMSCs were exposed to hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) to induce premature aging. In addition, BMSCs were cultured with the osteogenic induction medium to induce osteogenic differentiation, which was complete a-MEM sup plemented with 10 mM β-glycerophosphate, 0.25 mM ascorbate, and 0.1 μM dexamethasone.

Cell cytotoxicity assay

The BMSCs were seeded on 96-well plates at a density of 5000 cells/well. After incubation with different concentrations of H2O2 or PUN for 24 h and 72 h, the culture medium was replaced with 100 μl a-MEM containing 10% Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, Yeasen, China) and incubated at 37°C for 1 h. Cell viability was calculated on the basis of absorbance at 450 nm.

Intracellular ROS measurements

The intracellular ROS levels of BMSCs were measured with a ROS assay kit (Beyotime, China) containing DCFH-DA. DCFH-DA can pass through the cell membrane and hydrolyzed to generate DCFH, which then is oxidized by ROS to produce fluorescent DCF. BMSCs were incubated with DCFH-DA and then observed under a fluorescence microscope (Zeiss, Germany). The fluorescence intensity was analyzed by ImageJ software.

Senescence β-galactosidase staining

The senescence β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) staining was carried out with an SA-β-gal staining kit (Beyotime, China). BMSCs were incubated with staining solution at 37°C without CO2 overnight. The samples were observed under a microscope and analyzed by ImageJ software.

Western blot

Total proteins from BMSCs were extracted using RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, China) and subjected to gel electrophoresis. The primary antibodies used in Western blot included CDKN1A (P21, ABclonal, China; A19094), CDKN2A (P16, Proteintech, China; 28416-1-AP), runt-related transcription factor 2 (RUNX2, ABclonal, China; A2851), osteocalcin (OCN, ABclonal, China; A20800), NRF2 (ABclonal, China; A21176), HO-1 (ABclonal, China; A1346), Lamin B1 (ABclonal, China; A11495). Band quantification was performed using ImageJ software.

RT-qPCR analysis

Total RNA from BMSCs was extracted using FreeZol Reagent (Vazyme, China), and the reverse transcription reaction was conducted with the HiScript IV All-in-One Ultra RT SuperMix (Vazyme, China). Subsequently, gene expression was assessed with the SYBR Color qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, China) using a CFX96 Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad, USA). Alkaline phosphatase staining and enzyme activity detection

On day 7 of osteogenic induction, Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) staining was carried out with the BCIP/NBT ALP Color Development Kit (Beyotime, China), and the ALP-positive area was analyzed by ImageJ software. The ALP enzyme activity was investigated using an ALP Assay Kit (Beyotime, China), and the absorbance was determined at 405 nm.

Alizarin red S staining

On day 21 of osteogenic induction, Alizarin red S (ARS) staining was performed with the ARS Staining solution (Beyotime, China). The ARS-positive area was analyzed by ImageJ software. Subsequently, the calcium crystals were dissolved with hexadecyl pyridinium chloride, and the absorbance was recorded at 562 nm.

Transcriptome sequencing

After H2O2 intervention, BMSCs were collected from the PUN group and the control group. Transcriptome sequencing were performed by OE Biotech Co (China). Thresholds for statistically differential expression were set as q values < 0.05 and |log2foldchange|>1. GO enrichment analysis, KEGG enrichment analysis, and GSEA were conducted among differentially expressed genes.

Animal experiment

The Animal Ethics Committee of Soochow University authorized the animal experiments (approval number: SUDA20240129A07). The 18-month-old Sprague-Dawley rats were regarded as aging rats, while 3-month-old young Sprague-Dawley rats were used as control. The 18-month-old aged rats separately received intraperitoneal injection of PBS, Pun (20 mg/kg/d), and PUN (20 mg/kg/d) + ML385 (30 mg/kg/d) for four weeks. All rats were sacrificed on day 28 and femurs were collected for Micro-CT analysis and histological staining.

Statistical analysis

The results were presented as mean ± standard deviation. One-way analysis of variance with Tukey's test was conducted to verify the significance of differences among various groups. The statistical analysis was performed with the GraphPad Prism 8.0 software, and the statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Results

PUN reverses the senescence of BMSCs induced by H2O2

H2O2 was used to induce the senescence of BMSCs, and the appropriate concentration of H2O2 was determined by cell cytotoxicity assay. As shown in Fig. 1A and B, after incubation with H2O2 for 24 h and 72 h, no significant cytotoxicity is observed when the concentration of H2O2 was 100 μM or lower. Therefore, a concentration of 100 μM was selected for inducing the senescence of BMSCs. Similarly, the appropriate concentration of PUN was also screened out using cell cytotoxicity assay. When the concentration was 20 μM or lower, no obvious cytotoxicity was observed in BMSCs after 24 h and 72 h of incubation with PUN. Hence, in the subsequent experiments of PUN intervention, a concentration of 20 μM was selected as the high concentration, while a concentration of 10 μM was chosen as the low concentration (Fig. 1C and D). The ROS level in BMSCs induced with H2O2 was significantly higher than that in the control group, while PUN decreased the ROS level in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 1E and H). SA-β-gal staining was performed to label prematurely senescent BMSCs. The results showed that H2O2 induce the senescence of BMSCs, while PUN reversed the aging process (Fig. 1G and I). Consistently, the expression of aging marker proteins P21 and P16 both increased after H2O2 intervention, while PUN exhibited an inhibitory effect on them (Fig. 1F–J, and K). Moreover, RT-qPCR revealed that several senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) genes were reduced in PUN group compared to that in H2O2 group, such as interleukin-1β (Il-1β), Il-6, C–C motif chemokine ligand 2 (Ccl2), and matrix metalloproteinase 3 (Mmp3) (Fig. 1L-O). These results showed that PUN can reverse the senescence of BMSCs induced by H2O2 in a dose-dependent manner.

PUN enhances the osteogenic differentiation of senescent BMSCs

BMSCs induced by H2O2 were treated with Pun at concentrations of 10 μM and 20 μM to assess the effects of Pun on osteogenic differentiation. On day 7 of osteogenesis induction, ALP staining revealed that PUN significantly increased the expression and enzyme activity of ALP (Fig. 2A–C). Consistently, On day 21 of osteogenic induction, ARS staining showed that Pun significantly promoted the matrix mineralization of BMSCs (Fig. 2D–F). The expression of osteogenic marker protein, including runt-related transcription factor 2 (RUNX2) and osteocalcin (OCN), were decreased by H2O2 intervation and reversed by PUN in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 2G–I). Moreover, RT-qPCR was conducted to assess the expression of osteogenic-related genes, such as Runx2, Alpl, collagen type I alpha 1 chain (Col1a1), bone gamma-carboxyglutamate protein (Bglap). These osteogenic-related genes were reduced in H2O2-induced senescent BMSCs, and up-regulated by PUN intervention (Fig. 2J-M). Overall, Pun promotes the osteogenic differentiation of senescent BMSCs.

Transcriptome sequencing reveals that PUN inhibits the senescence of BMSCs via NRF2 pathway

To explore the therapeutic mechanism of PUN, transcriptome sequencing was conducted on the H2O2-induced BMSCs treated with PUN. The volcano plot of differentially expressed genes showed 133 up-regulated genes and 254 down-regulated genes by comparing the PUN group with the control group (Fig. 3A). Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) revealed a positive correlation with the NRF2 pathway and a negative correlation with cellular senescence in the PUN group (Fig. 3B and C). Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis indicated that PUN reduced the response of BMSCs to H2O2 stimulus (Fig. 3D). Moreover, the Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis showed that PUN regulated the p53 pathway and cell cycle, which are related to cellular senescence (Fig. 3E). These results suggested that PUN inhibits the senescence of BMSCs via NRF2 pathway.

Inhibition of NRF2 counteracted the therapeutic effect of PUN

To confirm the role of NRF2 in the therapeutic effect of PUN, an NRF2 inhibitor ML385 was administered, which can inhibit the expression of downstream target genes of NRF2. The appropriate concentration of H2O2 was determined by cell cytotoxicity assay. After incubation with ML385 for 24 h and 72 h, no significant cytotoxicity is observed when the concentration of ML385 was 2.5 μM or lower (Fig. 4A and B). Hence, a concentration of 2.5 μM was selected for inhibiting NRF2 pathway. Western blot of nuclear protein showed that under the intervention of H2O2, ML385 counteracted the effect of PUN in regulating the nuclear translocation of NRF2 (Fig. 4C–F). Nuclear translocation of NRF2 can activate the transcription of cytoprotective genes, among which heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) plays a dominant role. Western blot showed that the expression of HO-1 was increased by PUN and reduced by ML385. Consistently, ML385 counteracted the regulatory effects of PUN on P16 and RUNX2 expression, indicating that the therapeutic effects of PUN in inhibiting aging and promoting osteogenesis were suppressed (Fig. 4D and F-H). Overall, it could be confirmed that PUN exert its therapeutic effects through the NRF2/HO-1 pathway.

Pun alleviates senile osteoporosis in aging rats

The therapeutic effects of PUN are investigated in vivo based on aging rats with senile osteoporosis. Micro-CT analysis of trabeculae under the distal femur growth plate revealed a significant bone loss in aging rats, manifested as lower bone volume/tissue volume (BV/TV), trabecular number (Tb.N), and bone mineral density (BMD) compared to the control. The administration of PUN recovered the BMD to 76.9% of the control group, indicating alleviatived bone loss in aging rats, whereas the NRF2 inhibitor ML385 counteracted the therapeutic effect (Fig. 5A and D-F). In addition, hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining indicated that the PUN group exhibited more trabeculae than the aging group, further verifying the Micro-CT results (Fig. 5B and G). Calcein staing was conducted to evaluate the rate of bone formation, which showed PUN treatment increased the mineral apposition rate (MAR) of aging rats. However, when ML385 was administered simultaneously, the efficacy of PUN was not significant (Fig. 5C and H). These data indicated that Pun can alleviate senile osteoporosis in aging rats.

Immunohistochemical staining of trabeculae under the distal femur growth plate was performed to further verify the mechanism by which Pun alleviates senile osteoporosis. As shown in Fig. 6A, aging rats were characterized by low levels of HO-1 and OCN, as well as high levels of P16. PUN increased the expression of HO-1 and OCN while reduced the expression of P16. On the contrary, the NRF2 inhibitor ML385 inhibited the expression of HO-1 and OCN while promote the expression of P16. These results comfirmed that Pun inhibit the aging of BMSCs and promote osteogenic differentiation in vivo via NRF2/HO-1 pathway (Fig. 6B–D).

Discussion

Pomegranates are widely cultivated all over the world. Every year, three million tons of pomegranates are used for juicing, which is the main processing method of pomegranates. The pomegranate peel is usually the main by-product of pomegranate production, accounting for half of the total weight of the fruit. Discarded pomegranate peels not only may cause pollution to the environment but also represent a serious waste of resources. Therefore, how to utilize pomegranate peels is worth studying [18]. It is reported that pomegranate peel extract has multiple therapeutic effects, such as antioxidant, anti-inflammation, and anti-tumor. These biochemical properties are attributed to its high polyphenol content, including ellagitannins, phenolic acids, and flavonoids [19]. As one of the main ellagitannins in pomegranate peel, PUN has broad application prospect in the field of medicine.

The normal bone homeostasis in organism is maintained jointly by osteoblast-mediated bone formation and osteoclast-mediated bone resorption [20]. The progression of aging leads to a decline in the proliferation and differentiation ability of BMSCs, which in turn causes bone homeostasis imbalance and bone loss. Current anti-osteoporosis medications are mainly focused on inhibiting the differentiation and function of osteoclasts, including estrogen, bisphosphonates, and denosumab [21]. Although these drugs effectively alleviate bone loss, they ignore the lack of bone formation associated with aging, which potentially explains why long-term use of these drugs often leads to unsatisfactory complications [22,23]. Multiple cell types in the bone marrow microenvironment exhibit features of senescence, including BMSCs and immune cells. However, senescence in immune cells may differ fundamentally from that in BMSCs and its definition has always been controversial [24,25]. It is difficult to distinguish between cellular senescence and inflammatory state in immune cells. The inflammatory factors released by immune cells overlap highly with SASP, and certain cell types, such as macrophages, can reversibly express senescence markers depending on their activation status [26]. Moreover, immune cells typically have a shorter lifespan than BMSCs and are in a state of constant renewal, making it difficult to exert a sustained effect on bone marrow microenvironment [27]. Previous studies have shown that clearance of senescent myeloid cells has limited effects on senile osteoporosis, while targeting senescent BMSCs effectively prevents age-related bone loss [28,29]. Hence, alleviating the aging of BMSCs is a promising strategy for treatment of senile osteoporosis.

Oxidative stress serves as a critical mediator of cell senescence. The aging process is accompanied by mitochondrial dysfunction and impaired antioxidant system, which lead to aberrant ROS accumulation and oxidative stress [30]. Excessive ROS can cause the oxidative damage of various biomolecules, especially DNA, which actives the p53/p21 axis and enforces irreversible cell cycle arrest [31]. Subsequently, cells enter into a hypersecretory state termed SASP and induces secondary senescence through paracrine effects [32]. Therefore, oxidative stress is a potential therapeutic target for inhibiting the senescence of BMSCs in senile osteoporosis. Studies have shown the efficacy of antioxidant therapy in the treatment of aging-related bone loss [33,34]. As a natural polyphenol with antioxidant effects, PUN has become a candidate drug for senile osteoporosis. In this study, we found that PUN inhibits the senescence of BMSCs and promotes their osteogenic differentiation by activating the NRF2/HO-1 pathway, thereby alleviating aging-related bone loss. NRF2 is an important component of the cellular antioxidant system, regulating the transcription of genes coding for antioxidant proteins and thereby protecting against oxidative damage [35]. NRF2 has been proven to play a significant role in the treatment of many diseases, including inflammation, cardiovascular diseases, and cancer [36,37]. The activation of the NRF2 pathway by drugs involves two aspects: directly increasing the expression of NRF2 protein, or promoting the nuclear translocation of NRF2, which is indispensable for the activation of downstream genes like HO-1 [38,39]. In this study, we demonstrated that PUN can activate the NRF2 pathway by promoting the nuclear translocation of NRF2, rather than directly affecting the protein expression level of NRF2.

Conclusion

This study indicated that PUN can inhibit the senescence of BMSCs and restore their osteogenic differentiation ability by activating the NRF2/HO-1 pathway. Intraperitoneal injection of PUN can effectively promote osteogenesis in vivo and alleviates aging-related bone loss. In conclusion, PUN alleviates senile osteoporosis by inhibiting the senescence of BMSCs, holding great promise in the treatment of aging-related bone diseases.
