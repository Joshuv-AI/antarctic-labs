Congestion levels on existing infrastructure
Background
Congestion occurs when available least-cost energy cannot be delivered to some loads because
transmission facilities do not have sufficient capacity to deliver the energy.1 Each of these
transmission limits is formulated as a constraint in the models used by system operators to clear
the market and economically dispatch available resources. When a constraint is binding (i.e., its
limit is reached) it has an associated shadow price – the marginal value of relieving the constraint
(i.e., of increasing the asset’s flow limit) – that is determined as a byproduct of solving the dispatch
model. Binding constraints are the primary reason that locational marginal prices vary within a
region. Therefore, binding constraints and their shadow prices offer information about the value
of upgrading individual elements of the existing system.

Guide to report contents
This report contains one page dedicated to each ISO and RTO in the U.S. documenting empirical
outcomes related to binding constraints during 2022 through 2024. In each case, results are
presented separately for the day-ahead and real-time markets, as changing conditions and day-
ahead decisions regularly result in different constraints binding in each market, even for the same
operating time period. Additionally, the day-ahead markets have an hourly resolution while the
real-time markets have a 5-minute resolution.

For each system operator and market timeline, the report contains two maps of infrastructure
elements that are color-coded based on congestion characteristics. The first map provides
information on how often each element has a binding constraint, that is, the frequency of
congestion on the element. The second map reflects the intensity of congestion, measured as the
sum of shadow prices. This metric is equivalent to the average shadow price multiplied by the
number of times the constraint binds; it is agnostic to the capacity or flow limit of the element.
Shadow prices can be positive or negative depending on the modeling conventions and
mathematical formulations used. Here, we standardize to positive shadow prices, taking the
absolute value where necessary. Finally, we converted all shadow prices to $2024 using a GDP-
based deflator. Some constrained elements are not shown on the map because we were
unsuccessful in matching them to a geospatial database of transmission system elements. For all
maps, the color scales are delineated into approximately the following buckets: 0-25th percentile,
25th-50th percentile, 50th-75th percentile, 75th-90th percentile, 90th-95th percentile, 95th percentile
and above.

For each system operator and market timeline, the report also contains a table of key constraint
details. The elements listed are in the top 15 based on either frequency or intensity, regardless of
whether they are depicted in the associated map. The table contains the name of the constrained
element (which may also be referred to as monitored element or monitored facility), the number
of constrained periods and the sum of associated shadow prices.
