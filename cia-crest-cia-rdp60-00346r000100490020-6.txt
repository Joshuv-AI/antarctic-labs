5= R escheduling of Section 83M

The Committee approved the Air Force proposal, submitted
in memorandum of 22 October 1957, for substitution of Section 83M
(Air Forces) on Bulgaria in lieu of Section 83M on Hungary for
February 1958. The reason for the substitution is the current
unstable situation in Hungary, especially as it relates to the status
of the Hungarian Air Forces,

6, NIS Annuals

The Committee discussed at some length the proposed
NIS Annuals, a prototype of which was handed to the members at the
last meeting for examination by their agencies (refer to Item 7 NCM 253).
It, was the general consensus that while the NIS Annuals would require
an appreciable, but presently indeterminate, amount of effort by all
of the contributing agencies, it was mandatory that the Chapters I be
kept reasonably up to date,. Suggestions were made for possible
alternate means of doing this, but the Committee decided to proceed
with the present prototype as the basis for the NIS Annuals and to
request the Chapter I Task Group to develop with representatives
from the member agencies an outline guide and production schedule
for Committee approval.

The Committee agreed in principle to the chairman's
proposal that Chapters I be made available to Cabinet officers,
heads of independent agencies., and other senior officials who have

9. Proposed Relocat ion of "Naval Air Arm"

The Navy member recommended by memorandum of
31 October 1957 that the Committee approve relocating coverage
of "Naval Air Arm" from Section 83 (Air Forces) to Section 82
(Naval Forces) and that the NIS Standard Instructions be amended
accordingly. The Navy member felt that the close inter-relationships
between naval surface, subsurface, and air components will be beat
reflected by treating all aspects of a nation's Naval Forces in
Section 82 (Naval Forces).

The Air Force member pointed out that the original
allocation of the "Naval Air Arm" to the Air Force (with Navy
Participation) had been made at the highest levels at the start of
the NIS program. He could not, therefore, take a position on the
Committee at variance with that policy decision.

Committee discussion revealed that the Navy member was
not satisfied with the present coverage of the "Naval Air Arm" and
was concerned over the problem of improving it which, he felt, could
be better done as an integral part of Section 82.

The chairman suggested in lieu of Navy's proposal that
ONI regularly review for adequacy "Naval Air Arm" contributions
prepared by AFCIN to ensure they are meeting Navy's requirements.
The Navy member took the chairman's suggestion under advisement.
