Project Overview
We aim to develop a framework for solving high-energy-density science problems using
quantum computers. Classical simulations often require a tradeoff between accuracy and
efficiency, especially in warm dense matter, where quantum degeneracy and thermal effects
compete. Sparse and challenging experiments further increase modeling uncertainty. For
example, uncertainty in the electrical conductivity of iron under Earth’s core conditions
contributes to estimates of the inner core’s age that differ by nearly 2 billion years. In
collaboration with Sandia National Laboratories and Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL), we
combine expertise in quantum algorithms, optimization, and device integration to demonstrate
small-scale quantum simulations on an LLNL quantum processor.
Because calibration is essential for high-fidelity simulations, we automate it using surrogate
models and feedback-based methods. By optimizing a quantum limited amplifier, resonator and
qubit-drive frequencies and amplitudes, we demonstrated simultaneous calibration of six
qubits, a process that can take hours manually. We also demonstrated a two-qubit dynamical
mean-field theory simulation and optimized single-qubit gates to reduce phase errors. Circuits
with one, two, and three two-qubit gates were tested, and the circuit with one gate agreed
most closely with the ideal Hamiltonian simulation. These results establish a pathway toward
scalable, high-fidelity quantum simulations for high-energy-density science.

Mission Impact
Quantum computers could eventually simulate material systems more accurately and
efficiently than classical computers, with significant implications for stockpile stewardship and
other national security applications. By demonstrating a representative high-energy-density
science problem on near-term quantum hardware, this interlab LDRD assess the potential
impact of quantum computing and strengthen collaboration among LANL, LLNL, and SNL. This
project also advanced scientific simulations on quantum computers for high energy density
science, one of the most promising applications of quantum computers articulated in a
February 2023 5-year program plan. This project ultimately aims to better understand this
promise, to accelerate the timeline for adopting quantum processing units for NNSA
applications, and to develop strong interlab collaborations through mission-focused co-design.
