Electron Affinity as a Landscape Gap

The EA proxy is defined as the gap between the function value

Results

This section presents the three
\mathrm{IE}_{1}

three derived checks for
\mathrm{EA}
,
\chi_{M}
, and
\eta

shared-kernel scale unification tested in the final subsection.
Predictions 1–2 are ratio-level identities (heavy noble-gas

\varphi^{1/4}
and halogen/alkali
\varphi^{2}

within-period ordering-and-anomaly claim across periods 2–4.
NIST ionization energies are much more precise than the

Benchmarking philosophy.

For each observable below we report the candidate set, the
classes excluded (and why), and the retained set on which the
metric is computed. Exclusions are confined to two physically
motivated categories: (a) the textbook half-filled and
closed-subshell anomaly sites

\{p^{3},d^{5},f^{7},s^{2},d^{10}s^{2}\}
, where Hund-exchange and
shell-closure stabilizations dominate and are not represented
by the smooth landscape; and (b) cases where well-understood
relativistic or first-row screening shifts dominate the
comparison and would mask the within-period landscape content
(specifically, period 1 and the Ne/Ar pair in Prediction 1, the
F/Li and Ts/Fr pairs in Prediction 2, period 7 throughout, and a

Prediction 1: Heavy Noble-Gas Ratio

The first prediction is a single fixed asymptotic value for the

1

\frac{\mathrm{IE}_{1}(G_{p})}{\mathrm{IE}_{1}(G_{p+1})}\;\approx\;\varphi^{1/4}\approx 1.1278\qquad\text{for heavy }G_{p}\text{ (Ar and above)}.

For a noble gas

6
Conclusion

We have proposed a compact phenomenological framework in
which a single dimensionless landscape

J_{\!\mathrm{chem}}(\rho)=\cosh(\rho\ln\varphi)-1
on the
noble-gas-centred coordinate
\rho=d/L_{p}
provides analytical
proxies for the four central within-period atomic observables

\mathrm{IE}_{1}
,
\mathrm{EA}
,
\chi_{M}
, and
\eta
. The outward step

\Delta J_{\!\mathrm{chem}}^{+}
is assigned to
\mathrm{IE}_{1}
, the inward gap

\Delta J_{\!\mathrm{chem}}^{-}=J_{\!\mathrm{chem}}(1)-J_{\!\mathrm{chem}}(\rho)
is reused for
both
\mathrm{EA}
and
\eta
with two independent per-period scales,
and
\chi_{M}
follows by Mulliken’s identity. The four observables
share the same dimensionless shape (sign, monotonicity, halogen
position, anomaly localization, cross-period ordering); absolute
energies enter through fitted per-period scales that cancel in
the EA–
\eta
ratio.

The framework captures several broad within-period
regularities and provides a compact analytical baseline. At the
period-averaged scale level, the shared-kernel relation

\mathrm{EA}/\eta\approx C^{(p)}_{\mathrm{EA}}/C^{(p)}_{\eta}
is
supported on period-4 NIST data: the empirical nine-atom mean

\overline{\mathrm{EA}/\eta}=0.180
agrees with the predicted constant

0.182
to better than
1\%

(Section
5.7
); per-atom scatter is
substantial (
\sigma\approx 0.13
), so this should be read as a
period-mean benchmark rather than a per-atom proportionality law. At the shape level, the
within-period
\mathrm{IE}_{1}
envelope (Prediction 3) reproduces the
full noble-gas-to-alkali ordering across periods 2–6 and
localizes every upward deviation exactly on the textbook anomaly
sites
\{p^{3},d^{5},f^{7},s^{2},d^{10}\}
(26 of 34 atoms across
periods 2–4 on the monotone descent; 8 deviations on the listed
anomaly sites). The two golden-ratio identities,

\mathrm{IE}_{1}(G_{p})/\mathrm{IE}_{1}(G_{p+1})\approx\varphi^{1/4}
on three heavy
noble-gas pairs and

\mathrm{IE}_{1}(\text{halogen})/\mathrm{IE}_{1}(\text{alkali})\approx\varphi^{2}
on
four within-period pairs, agree with NIST data to MAD

\approx 1\%
and
\approx 5\%
, respectively, capturing the
golden-ratio rescaling of the absolute-energy structure encoded
by the per-period scale sequence
E_{p}
.

The shared-kernel form
\Delta J_{\!\mathrm{chem}}^{-}
further
provides single-parameter analytical fits to empirical
\mathrm{EA}

across periods 4–6 (MAE
0.3
–
0.4
eV; the period-5 fit
explains only
\sim\!7\%
of variance and should be read as
sign-and-trend agreement rather than quantitative match), to
Pearson hardness
\eta
across periods 2–4 (MAE
\sim 1
eV on
noble-gas maxima up to
10.8
eV), and to Mulliken
\chi_{M}

across a 15-atom four-class benchmark (
R^{2}=0.73
); the
within-class halogen ordering F
>
Cl
>
Br
>
I is
not

recovered by the present single-coordinate kernel. Two natural
extensions follow directly: a critically evaluated

\mathrm{IE}_{1}(\mathrm{Og})
benchmark to refine the heavy-noble-gas
test and a class-dependent kernel mixing
\Delta J_{\!\mathrm{chem}}^{+}
and

\Delta J_{\!\mathrm{chem}}^{-}
to resolve the within-class halogen ordering,
which we identify as the next target.

Appendix A
Reciprocal Cost Functional

The model’s input cost function
J
is the unique
solution on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
of the functional equation

J(xy)+J(x/y)=2J(x)J(y)+2J(x)+2J(y),

(22)

under the regularity conditions of continuity, normalization

J(1)=0
, stationarity
J^{\prime}(1)=0
, and positive
curvature
J^{\prime\prime}(1)>0
at the reference ratio
x=1
. The
unique solution is

J(x)\;=\;\tfrac{1}{2}(x+x^{-1})-1\;=\;\cosh\!\bigl(\ln x\bigr)-1,

(23)

(Corollary 3.1 of Ref.
5
).
J
inherits
from cosh the properties
J(1)=0
,
J(x)=J(x^{-1})

(reciprocal symmetry),
J(x)>0
for
x\neq 1
, and divergence
as
x\to 0^{+}
or
x\to\infty
. The uniqueness proof and
applications of
J
are developed in
Refs.
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
.

The chemical landscape
J_{\!\mathrm{chem}}(\rho)
used in the body
(Eq. (
3
)) is the restriction of
J
to
the geometric scale
x=\varphi^{\rho}
with
\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
and
\rho\in[0,1)
, giving

J_{\!\mathrm{chem}}(\rho)=\cosh(\rho\ln\varphi)-1
.

In this paper,
\varphi
is taken as a model input motivated by
Fibonacci scaling.
