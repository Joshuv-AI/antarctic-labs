Pushing the Primordial Frontier: Exact Linear Solutions in Multifield Inflation
Javier Huenupi a, Claudio Muñoz b, Gonzalo A. Palma c, Spyros Sypsas d

Abstract
We present exact analytic solutions for the linear dynamics of a two-field inflationary system in which the primordial curvature perturbation ζ \zeta is coupled to an isocurvature perturbation σ \sigma of entropy mass μ \mu . The solutions are valid for arbitrary values of μ \mu and the dimensionless interaction strength λ \lambda , within a quasi-de Sitter background. They therefore provide analytic control over the strongly coupled regime in which ζ \zeta interacts with light isocurvature fields, commonly associated with rapid-turn inflation. As a first application, we derive the amplitude of the primordial power spectrum in closed form, obtaining an expression that interpolates between the weakly coupled, strongly coupled, light-field, and heavy-field regimes. These results open the way to analytic studies of multifield observables beyond the power spectrum, including non-Gaussianity, particle production, and loop corrections.

Basic Setup.–

In the simplest two-field realization of multifield inflation, the scalar sector contains the primordial curvature perturbation
\zeta
and a single isocurvature perturbation
\sigma
of entropy mass
\mu
. We work in units where
c=\hbar=M_{\rm Pl}=1
. The quadratic action governing the linear dynamics of this system is
Achucarro:2016fby

\displaystyle S=\int d^{4}xa^{3}\Bigg[

\displaystyle\epsilon\left(\dot{\zeta}-\lambda\frac{H}{\sqrt{2\epsilon}}\sigma\right)^{2}-\frac{\epsilon}{a^{2}}(\nabla\zeta)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\dot{\sigma}^{2}-\frac{1}{2a^{2}}(\nabla\sigma)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\sigma^{2}\Bigg],

(1)

where a dot denotes a derivative with respect to cosmic time
t
. Here
a=a(t)
is the scale factor,
H=\dot{a}/a
is the Hubble expansion rate,
\epsilon=-\dot{H}/H^{2}
is the first slow-roll parameter, and
\lambda
is the dimensionless coupling controlling the interaction between the two perturbations. The action (
1
) describes fluctuations around a background trajectory followed by two homogeneous scalar fields in a nonlinear sigma-model target space. In this setting,
\lambda
is related to the turning rate
\Omega
of the inflationary trajectory through

\lambda=-2\Omega/H.

(2)

We assume a quasi-de Sitter, approximately constant-turn regime in which both
\epsilon
and
\lambda
vary slowly.

To quantize the system, it is convenient to introduce the canonically normalized curvature perturbation
\zeta_{c}\equiv\sqrt{2\epsilon}\zeta
. Neglecting slow-roll suppressed corrections from the time dependence of
\epsilon
, the canonical momenta conjugate to
\zeta_{c}
and
\sigma
are
\Pi_{\zeta}=a^{3}(\dot{\zeta}_{c}-\lambda H\sigma)
and
\Pi_{\sigma}=a^{3}\dot{\sigma}
. The fields and their canonical momenta obey the equal-time commutation relations

\displaystyle\Big[\zeta_{c}({\bm{x}},t),\Pi_{\zeta}({\bm{x}}^{\prime},t)\Big]

\displaystyle=i\delta^{(3)}({\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}),

(3)

\displaystyle\Big[\sigma({\bm{x}},t),\Pi_{\sigma}({\bm{x}}^{\prime},t)\Big]

\displaystyle=i\delta^{(3)}({\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{\prime}),

(4)

with all other commutators vanishing. These relations are implemented by expanding the fields as

\displaystyle\zeta_{c}({\bm{x}},t)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\sum_{b}\!\int_{\bf k}\!\bigg[\zeta_{b}(k,t)\hat{a}_{b}({\bm{k}})+\zeta^{*}_{b}(k,t)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{b}(-{\bm{k}})\bigg]e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf x}},

(5)

\displaystyle\sigma({\bm{x}},t)\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\sum_{b}\!\int_{\bf k}\!\bigg[\sigma_{b}(k,t)\hat{a}_{b}({\bm{k}})+\sigma^{*}_{b}(k,t)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{b}(-{\bm{k}})\bigg]e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf x}},\quad\,

(6)

where
\int_{\bf k}\equiv(2\pi)^{-3}\int d^{3}k
. The index
b=1,2
labels the two independent mode solutions, and
\zeta_{b}(k,t)
and
\sigma_{b}(k,t)
are the corresponding mode functions. The creation and annihilation operators satisfy

\Big[\hat{a}_{b}({\bm{k}}),\hat{a}_{c}^{{\dagger}}({\bm{k}}^{\prime})\Big]=\delta_{bc}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}({\bm{k}}-{\bm{k}}^{\prime}).

(7)

Working to leading order in slow roll, and taking
\lambda
and
\mu/H
to be constant, the equations of motion following from (
1

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}(D_{z}\zeta_{c})-\frac{2}{z}D_{z}\zeta_{c}+\zeta_{c}=0,

(8)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dz^{2}}\sigma-\frac{2}{z}\frac{d}{dz}\sigma+\left[1+\frac{\mu^{2}}{H^{2}z^{2}}\right]\sigma=\frac{\lambda}{z}D_{z}\zeta_{c},

(9)

where we have defined
z\equiv k/Ha(t)
. The covariant derivative

appearing in these equations is defined by

D_{z}\zeta_{c}\equiv\frac{d}{dz}\zeta_{c}+\frac{\lambda}{z}\sigma.

(10)

We highlight that, unlike previous studies assuming small or large values for the dimensionless coupling, here,
\lambda
is completely free.

The variable

measures the physical wavenumber

in units of the Hubble scale

and the mode is deep inside the horizon; horizon crossing occurs at

and the mode becomes superhorizon. The main observable of interest is the dimensionless primordial power spectrum

Bogoliubov Decomposition.–

In the absence of interactions (
\lambda=0
), the canonical curvature perturbation
\zeta_{c}
and the isocurvature perturbation
\sigma
decouple. The corresponding mode functions are

\displaystyle\zeta_{b}(k,z)

\displaystyle=\delta_{b1}u_{0}(k,z),

(12)

\displaystyle\sigma_{b}(k,z)

\displaystyle=\delta_{b2}u_{\mu}(k,z),

(13)

where
u_{0}(k,z)
and
u_{\mu}(k,z)
are the standard Bunch–Davies mode functions for a massless and a massive scalar, respectively. They are given by

\displaystyle u_{0}(k,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{iH}{\sqrt{2k^{3}}}\left(1-iz\right)e^{iz},

(14)

\displaystyle u_{\mu}(k,z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{H}{\sqrt{2k^{3}}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{i\theta_{\nu}}z^{3/2}H_{\nu}^{(1)}\left(z\right),

(15)

where
H_{\nu}^{(1)}(z)
is the Hankel function of the first kind, with index

\nu=\sqrt{\frac{9}{4}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{H^{2}}},

(16)

and
\theta_{\nu}=\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\nu-\frac{3}{2}\right)
. Using these decoupled solutions as a basis, we write the interacting mode functions as

\displaystyle\zeta_{b}(k,z)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{b}^{+}(z)u_{0}(k,z)+\zeta_{b}^{-}(z)u^{*}_{0}(k,z),

(17)

\displaystyle\sigma_{b}(k,z)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{b}^{+}(z)u_{\mu}(k,z)+\sigma_{b}^{-}(z)u^{*}_{\mu}(k,z).

(18)

The functions
\zeta_{b}^{+}(z)
,
\zeta_{b}^{-}(z)
,
\sigma_{b}^{+}(z)
, and
\sigma_{b}^{-}(z)
are time-dependent Bogoliubov coefficients. This rewriting is exact: all effects of the interaction are encoded in the evolution of these coefficients.

The equations of motion (
8
) and (
9
) can then be recast as first-order equations for the Bogoliubov coefficients. Ordering them as
(\zeta_{b}^{+},\zeta_{b}^{-},\sigma_{b}^{+},\sigma_{b}^{-})^{T}

\zeta_{b}^{-}\\
\sigma_{b}^{+}\\
\sigma_{b}^{-}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\mathcal{P}(z)\\
\mathcal{Q}(z)&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\zeta_{b}^{+}\\
\zeta_{b}^{-}\\
\sigma_{b}^{+}\\
\sigma_{b}^{-}\end{array}\right),

(19)

where
\mathcal{P}(z)

\mathcal{Q}(z)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(z)

\displaystyle=-i\frac{\lambda k^{3}}{H^{2}z^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{u_{0}^{*}}^{\prime}u_{\mu}&{u_{0}^{*}}^{\prime}u_{\mu}^{*}\\

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(z)

\displaystyle=-i\frac{\lambda k^{3}}{H^{2}z^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{0}^{\prime}u_{\mu}^{*}&{u_{0}^{*}}^{\prime}u_{\mu}^{*}\\

\displaystyle(\zeta_{1}^{+},\zeta_{1}^{-},\sigma_{1}^{+},\sigma_{1}^{-})

Fourth-order formulation and exact solutions.–

We now turn to the exact solution of the coupled system. A detailed derivation will be presented in the companion article
Huenupi_etal_2
. Starting from (
19

\lambda\neq 0
, one may eliminate three of the four Bogoliubov coefficients to obtain a fourth-order differential equation for any one of them. For instance, the coefficient
\zeta^{+}

\displaystyle\Bigg[

\displaystyle z^{2}\frac{d^{4}}{dz^{4}}+4z(1+iz)\frac{d^{3}}{dz^{3}}\!+\!\left(\frac{9}{4}-\nu^{2}+10iz-4z^{2}\right)\!\!\frac{d^{2}}{dz^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left(i\frac{5}{2}-2i\nu^{2}-4z\right)\!\frac{d}{dz}-\lambda^{2}\Bigg]\zeta^{+}=0.

(27)

The derivation of (
27

Equation (

\displaystyle f_{3}(z)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{\widehat{D}}_{\nu,+}M\left(\frac{i\lambda}{2},1,-2iz\right),

\displaystyle f_{4}(z)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{\widehat{D}}_{\nu,-}M\left(-\frac{i\lambda}{2},1,-2iz\right).

), we obtain

\frac{\Delta_{\zeta}}{\Delta_{0}}=\left|\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{\nu}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{\nu}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{\nu}{2}+\frac{i\lambda}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{\nu}{2}+\frac{i\lambda}{2}\right)}\right|^{2}.

(48)

This expression reduces to
\Delta_{\zeta}/\Delta_{0}=1
when
\lambda=0
, as expected. A simple illustrative case is
\nu=1/2
, corresponding to
\mu^{2}/H^{2}=2
, or equivalently to a conformally coupled massless scalar in de Sitter space. In this case,
\mathcal{\widehat{D}}_{\nu,\pm}=1
, and the result simplifies to

\frac{\Delta_{\zeta}}{\Delta_{0}}=\frac{\sinh(\pi\lambda)}{\pi\lambda}.

(49)

The heavy-field regime,
\mu^{2}/H^{2}>9/4
, follows by analytic continuation,
\nu\to i\rho
, with
\rho=\sqrt{\mu^{2}/H^{2}-9/4}
. After simplification, the power spectrum becomes

\frac{\Delta_{\zeta}}{\Delta_{0}}=\frac{\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{i\rho}{2}\right)\right|^{4}}{\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{i}{2}(\lambda+\rho)\right)\right|^{2}\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{i}{2}(\lambda-\rho)\right)\right|^{2}}.

(50)

In the large-mass limit,
\mu^{2}\gg H^{2}
, or equivalently
\rho\gg 1
, this expression admits a simple expansion. At fixed
\lambda
, one finds

\frac{\Delta_{\zeta}}{\Delta_{0}}=1+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\rho^{2}}+\frac{3\lambda^{2}(1+\lambda^{2})}{8\rho^{4}}+\mathcal{O}(\rho^{-6}).

(51)

The leading correction reproduces the standard effective-single-field result obtained by integrating out a heavy isocurvature mode
Tolley:2009fg
;
Shiu:2011qw
;
Cespedes:2012hu
;
Achucarro:2012sm
;
Achucarro:2012yr
;
Noumi:2012vr
, while the subleading terms give controlled corrections to the heavy-mass expansion. This provides a nontrivial check of the exact expression above. Fig.
2
shows the power-spectrum ratio
\Delta_{\zeta}/\Delta_{0}
as a function
\mu/H
and of
\lambda
, in the exact limit
z\to 0
. As expected, on superhorizon scales, the amplitude of
\zeta
is enhanced substantially for small values of
\mu/H
.

Discussion.–

Equations (
28
)–(
31
) are the central result of this Letter. They provide exact analytic control over the linear dynamics of coupled curvature and isocurvature perturbations in a two-field, approximately constant-turn inflationary background, for arbitrary values of the interaction strength
\lambda
and entropy mass
\mu
. This includes the strongly coupled light-field regime associated with rapid-turn inflation, which has often required numerical treatments or analytic approximations tailored to special limits. As a first application, we used these solutions to derive the amplitude of the primordial power spectrum
\Delta_{\zeta}
in closed form, obtaining expressions that interpolate between the light- and heavy-field regimes and between weak and strong coupling.

These results provide a new analytic starting point for the phenomenology of multifield inflation, particularly in regimes relevant to the cosmological-collider program
Arkani-Hamed:2015bza
;
Lee:2016vti
;
Flauger:2016idt
;
Chen:2016uwp
. Because the solutions (
28
)–(
31
) are non-perturbative in the interaction strength, they offer controlled access to strongly coupled regimes where an expansion in
\lambda
is not reliable. They can be used, for example, to describe finite-duration turns by matching constant-turn solutions across different intervals, and should serve as building blocks for analytic studies of higher-point functions, particle production, primordial-black-hole formation, scalar-induced gravitational waves, and loop corrections to primordial correlators
Weinberg:2005vy
;
Weinberg:2006ac
;
Senatore:2009cf
;
WangXianyuZhong:2021
;
XianyuZhang:2022
. We leave these applications, together with extensions to genuinely time-dependent backgrounds, for future work.
