LAN property for the parameter of the jump rate in mean field interacting systems of neurons
Aline Duarte, Dasha Loukianova, Aurélien Velleret

Abstract
. In the context of a large system of N N neurons interacting through spike events in a mean-field regime as N → ∞ N\to\infty , we characterize the estimation of a multidimensional parameter in the spiking rate, when the neural states are observed over a fixed time horizon. We first prove the local asymptotic normality (LAN) property and leverage classical theory to establish the asymptotic efficiency of the maximum likelihood estimator. While the theory of Ibragimov and Hasminski yields strong results, up to global asymptotic minimax bound, its applicability appears currently limited to models without state resets at spike times. Following then Höpfner’s classical approach, we nevertheless derive, in a general setting including neuron reset, the consistency, asymptotic normality and local asymptotic minimax optimality of the estimator. Keywords : Local Asymptotic Normality (LAN); Mean-field regime; Interacting particle system; Multidimensional parameter estimation; Jump rate estimation; Maximum likelihood estimator (MLE); Asymptotic minimax optimality AMS Classification 2010: 62F12, 62F10, 60K35 (primary); 92B20, 60G55 (secondary)

In the present work, we investigate what insights the mean-field limit can provide about the actual finite neuronal system from a statistical standpoint. More specifically, we focus on understanding how a given neuron responds to incoming stimuli through its spiking rate function, which characterizes its ability to react by generating sequences of action potentials. In the recent paper

2.
Model, assumptions and main results

2.1.
Finite system

Let
(\pi^{j}),\;j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
be a family of i.i.d. Poisson Random Measures on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
with the intensity
{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}z\,\nu({\rm d}u)
, where
\nu
a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}.

We consider the following model of interacting spiking neurons:

(1)

{\rm d}X_{t}^{i,N,\theta}=b(X_{t}^{i,N,\theta}){\rm d}t+\frac{1}{{N}}\sum_{j\neq i}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\mathbf{1}_{\left\{z\leq f_{\theta}(X_{s-}^{j,N,\theta})\right\}}\pi^{j}({\rm d}t,{\rm d}z,{\rm d}u)\\
+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(X_{s-}^{i,N,\theta})\,\mathbf{1}_{\left\{z\leq f_{\theta}(X_{s-}^{i,N,\theta})\right\}}\pi^{i}({\rm d}t,{\rm d}z,{\rm d}u)\,,\;\quad X_{0}^{i,N,\theta}{=X_{0}^{i}}\;

The jumping rate
f
depends on a parameter
\theta
that lies in a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We impose the following assumptions on the system (
1
)

Assumption 1
.

The parameter set
\Theta
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with non-empty interior
\mathring{\Theta}.

Assumption 2
.

(i)

The function

|b(x)|\leq C(1+|x|).

(ii)

The function

is locally Lipschitz-continuous and bounded

(iii)

For all
\theta\in\Theta
the function

is Lipschitz-continuous and bounded.

(iv)

The measure

satisfies:
\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{p}\nu(dx)<\infty

(v)

The initial condition is i.i.d. distributed according to a law

with some exponential moment, i.e. there exists

Under the above assumption, for each fixed

2.2.
Limit system

As the system size

tends to infinity, system (

Here we denoted

and
\bar{\mu}^{\theta}
the law on the Skorokhod space of

whereas

denotes its projection on the

2

Assumption 3

(1)

The function

Under Assumption

(3)

Assumption 4
.

There exists

\left|\frac{f_{\theta}}{f_{\theta^{\prime}}}(y)-1-(\theta-\theta^{\prime})^{\mathsf{T}}\cdot\frac{\dot{f}_{\theta^{\prime}}}{f_{\theta^{\prime}}}(y)\right|\leq C\,\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|^{2}.

For any
\theta\in\Theta,

{\theta^{*}}\in\Theta,
the likelihood ratio process

(4)

We omit in the following the dependency on the trajectory

.

For all
{\theta^{*}}\in\Theta,
denote

(5)

I_{t}^{{\theta^{*}}}=\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\mu}^{{\theta^{*}}}_{s}\Big[\frac{(\dot{f}_{{\theta^{*}}})^{\otimes 2}}{f_{\theta^{*}}}\Big]{\rm d}s=\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\mu}^{{\theta^{*}}}_{s}\Big[\frac{\dot{f}_{{\theta^{*}}}\cdot[\dot{f}_{{\theta^{*}}}]^{\mathsf{T}}}{f_{\theta^{*}}}\Big]{\rm d}s\,,

Assumption 5

For all

tends to 0 in probability.

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathcal{K}_{t}^{N,({\theta^{*}}+h/\sqrt{N})/{\theta^{*}}}\leq-\frac{3}{20}\,h^{\mathsf{T}}I_{t}^{{\theta^{*}}}h\,.

Generally on the event
\mathcal{H}^{N}_{loc}=\{\mathcal{K}_{t}^{N,({\theta^{*}}+h/\sqrt{N})/{\theta^{*}}}\leq-\frac{1}{10}\,h^{\mathsf{T}}I_{t}^{{\theta^{*}}}h\}
,
since
(I_{t}^{\theta})_{\theta}
is uniformly elliptic,
we can upper-bound the exponential of
\mathcal{K}_{t}^{N,({\theta^{*}}+h/\sqrt{N})/{\theta^{*}}}

by
C\,\|h\|^{-r}
for some constant
C
.
In the case of any
h\in B\big(0,\bar{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{k}}\sqrt{N}\big)
,
we upper-bound the exponential by
1

and exploit (
49
)
to deduce that the complementary of
\mathcal{H}^{N}

is included in the following rare event:

(50)

\mathcal{I}^{N,{\theta^{*}}}=\{\|I_{t}^{N,{\theta^{*}}}\|\leq\frac{5}{6}\|I_{t}^{{\theta^{*}}}\|\}\,.

That
for any
r>0
,

\mathbb{P}_{{\theta^{*}}}^{N}[\mathcal{I}^{N,{\theta^{*}}}]\leq C_{0}\,N^{-r}
for some constant
C_{0}

is a consequence of Lemma
5.3

and the two facts that
I_{t}^{{\theta^{*}}}/6

is elliptic
and the moment of order
2r
of
\bar{\mu}^{{\theta^{*}}}_{s}
is upper-bounded,
uniformly in
{\theta^{*}}
and
s\in[0,t]
.

The argument is quite similar when
\theta
is in a close vicinity of some value
\vartheta
such that

\|\vartheta-{\theta^{*}}\|\geq\bar{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{k}}
, where we exploit
Assumption
6
,
see also Remark
2.3
, and that
k(r)<0
for any
r\neq 1
, to justify that

(51)

\mathfrak{K}_{t}^{\theta/{\theta^{*}}}=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\mathrm{k}\Big(\frac{f_{\theta}}{{f}_{{\theta^{*}}}}\Big)\big(x\big)\,f_{{\theta^{*}}}(x)\,\bar{\mu}^{{\theta^{*}}}_{s}({\rm d}x)\,{\rm d}s

is uniformly lower-bounded. Provided
\Theta
is bounded, we can exploit that
\|h\|=O(\sqrt{N})
and directly relate to moments of order
N

for the convergence of
\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(\mathcal{K}_{t}^{N,\theta/{\theta^{*}}}\big)\big]
. This is done by considering the restriction to the following event:

(52)

\mathcal{H}^{N,\theta/{\theta^{*}}}_{gl}=\{\mathcal{K}_{t}^{N,\theta/{\theta^{*}}}\leq-\frac{N}{2}\mathfrak{K}_{t}^{\theta/{\theta^{*}}}\}\,.

With the upper-bound of the exponential by
1
,
we exploit that the complementary of
\mathcal{H}^{N}_{gl}

is included in the following rare event:

(53)

\mathfrak{J}^{N,\theta/{\theta^{*}}}=\Big\{-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\mathrm{k}\Big(\frac{f_{\theta}}{{f}_{{\theta^{*}}}}\Big)\big(x\big)\,f_{{\theta^{*}}}(x)\,\mu^{N,{\theta^{*}}}_{s}({\rm d}x)\,{\rm d}s\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{K}_{t}^{\theta/{\theta^{*}}}\Big\}\,.

The set
\Theta^{2}\setminus\{(\vartheta,{\theta^{*}}):\|\vartheta-{\theta^{*}}\|<\bar{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{k}}\}
is compact
and the function
:(\vartheta,{\theta^{*}})\mapsto\mathfrak{K}_{t}^{\vartheta/{\theta^{*}}}
is continuous and positive. So it admits a positive minimizer
\mathfrak{K}^{\wedge}_{t}
.
Consequently,

\mathfrak{J}^{N,\theta/{\theta^{*}}}\subset\widehat{\mathfrak{J}}^{N,\theta/{\theta^{*}}}
, where

(54)

\widehat{\mathfrak{J}}^{N,\theta/{\theta^{*}}}=\Big\{\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\mathrm{k}\Big(\frac{f_{\theta}}{{f}_{{\theta^{*}}}}\Big)(x)\,f_{{\theta^{*}}}(x)\,\big[\bar{\mu}^{{\theta^{*}}}_{s}-\mu^{N,{\theta^{*}}}_{s}\big]({\rm d}x)\,{\rm d}s\Big|\geq\frac{1}{4}\mathfrak{K}_{t}^{\wedge}\Big\}\,.

Because the functions
f_{\theta}
are uniformly lower- and upper-bounded by positive constants,
we deduce a uniform upper-bound of
the Lipschitz contant of the function
f_{{\theta^{*}}}\cdot\mathrm{k}\Big(\frac{f_{\vartheta}}{{f}_{{\theta^{*}}}}\Big)
for any
\theta,{\theta^{*}}\in\Theta
, as stated in the upcoming Fact
5.5
.
Thus, there exists a constant
\mathfrak{K}_{t}^{*}>0
not depending on
N
,
\theta
nor
{\theta^{*}}
(provided the last two belong to
\Theta
) such that:

(55)

\widehat{\mathfrak{J}}^{N,\theta/{\theta^{*}}}\subset\Big\{\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu^{N,{\theta^{*}}}_{t},\bar{\mu}^{{\theta^{*}}}_{t})\geq\mathfrak{K}_{t}^{*}\Big\}\,.

The fact that
for any
r>0
,

\mathbb{P}_{{\theta^{*}}}^{N}[\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu^{N,{\theta^{*}}}_{t},\bar{\mu}^{{\theta^{*}}}_{t})\geq\mathfrak{K}_{t}^{*}]\leq C_{+}\,N^{-r}
for some constant
C_{+}

is a consequence of Lemma
5.3
.
With Fact
5.5
, the proof of (
29
)
is then complete.
∎

Fact 5.5
.

For any
M>0
, there exists
L>0
such that the function
f\cdot\mathrm{k}[g/f]
is
L
-Lispchitz continuous
for any two functions
f
and
g

that are
1
-Lipschitz continuous with values in
[1/M,M]
.

Proof.

First note the following inequality for any
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{*}
,
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
:

|f_{1}\cdot\mathrm{k}[g_{1}/f_{1}]-f_{2}\cdot\mathrm{k}[g_{2}/f_{2}]|\leq 2|f_{1}^{1/4}g_{1}^{3/4}-f_{2}^{1/4}g_{2}^{3/4}|+\frac{3}{2}|g_{1}-g_{2}|+\frac{1}{2}|f_{1}-f_{2}|\,.

The function
:(a,b)\in[-\ln M,\ln M]^{2}\mapsto\exp[\frac{1}{4}a+\frac{3}{4}b]
and
:c\in[1/M,M]\mapsto\ln c
being Lipschitz, the result is then concluded by considering
a_{1}=\ln f_{1}=\ln f(x_{1})
,

a_{2}=\ln f_{2}=\ln f(x_{2})
,

b_{1}=\ln g_{1}=\ln g(x_{1})
,
and
b_{2}=\ln g_{2}=\ln g(x_{2})
.
∎
