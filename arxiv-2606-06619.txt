On the structure of complete G 2 G_{2} -solitons
Haozhao Li, Yuanqing Ma, Kai Zheng

Abstract
. In this work, we establish compactness theorems for complete gradient G 2 G_{2} -solitons under the assumptions of a lower bound on the scalar curvature and a broad growth condition on the potential function associated with the gradient vector field. After first proving Gromov-Hausdorff convergence for such sequences, we sharpen this result by deriving epsilon-regularity estimates. As a consequence, we obtain smooth convergence provided there is a uniform energy bound at half the dimension.



1.
Introduction

A
G_{2}
-structure on a 7-dimensional manifold
M
is determined by a positive 3-form
\varphi
. More precisely, the positivity of
\varphi
is defined via the positivity of the quadratic form

\displaystyle G_{\varphi}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{6}i_{X}\varphi\wedge i_{Y}\varphi\wedge\varphi,\quad\forall X,Y\in TM.

This quadratic form naturally induces a Riemannian metric
g_{\varphi}
through

(1.1)

\displaystyle g_{\varphi}\cdot dv_{g_{\varphi}}=G_{\varphi},

where
dv_{g_{\varphi}}
is the volume form corresponding to
g_{\varphi}
.
The Hodge star operator
\ast_{\varphi}
and the codifferential

\delta=(-1)^{k}\ast_{\varphi}d\ast_{\varphi}

acting on
k
-forms are then defined with respect to the metric
g_{\varphi}
. The 4-form associated to a
G_{2}
-structure
\varphi
is given by

\psi=\ast_{\varphi}\varphi.

Let
\Omega^{k}
denote the space of all smooth
k
-forms on
M
.
For a given
G_{2}
-structure, the spaces
\Omega^{2}
and
\Omega^{3}
split as direct sums of irreducible
G_{2}
-representations:

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{2}_{7}\oplus\Omega^{2}_{14},\quad\Omega^{3}=\Omega^{3}_{1}\oplus\Omega^{3}_{7}\oplus\Omega^{3}_{27}.

See Bryant
[
7
]
*Section 2.6.
The subspaces are characterised as follows

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Omega^{2}_{7}&=\left\{u\lrcorner\varphi\mid u\in\Gamma(TM)\right\}=\left\{\ast_{\varphi}(\alpha\wedge\psi)\mid\alpha\in\Omega^{1}\right\}\\
&=\left\{\beta\in\Lambda^{2}\mid\ast_{\varphi}(\varphi\wedge\beta)=2\beta\right\};\\
\Omega^{2}_{14}&=\left\{\beta\in\Omega^{2}\mid\ast_{\varphi}(\psi\wedge\beta)=0\right\}\\
&=\left\{\beta\in\Omega^{2}\mid\ast_{\varphi}(\varphi\wedge\beta)=-\beta\right\};\end{aligned}\right.

and

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Omega^{3}_{1}&=\left\{f\varphi\mid f\in C^{\infty}(M)\right\};\\
\Omega^{3}_{7}&=\left\{u\lrcorner\psi\mid u\in\Gamma(TM)\right\}=\left\{\ast_{\varphi}(\alpha\wedge\varphi)\mid\alpha\in\Omega^{1}\right\}\\
&=\left\{\eta\in\Omega^{3}\mid\ast_{\varphi}[\varphi\wedge\ast_{\varphi}(\varphi\wedge\eta)]=4\eta\right\};\\
\Omega^{3}_{27}&=\left\{\eta\in\Omega^{3}\mid\eta\wedge\varphi=0,\eta\wedge\psi=0\right\}.\end{aligned}\right.

If
\varphi
is closed, then the torsion form

\tau=\delta\varphi

is a 2-form lying in
\Omega_{14}^{2}
and satisfies the following system of exterior differential equations:

(1.4)

\displaystyle d\psi=\tau\wedge\varphi=-\ast_{\varphi}\tau,\quad\tau\wedge\psi=0.

A closed
G_{2}
-structure
\varphi
is said to be
torsion-free
if it is coclosed, i.e.

\tau=0.

On a compact manifold, this condition is equivalent to the nonlinear harmonicity equation

\displaystyle\triangle_{\varphi}\varphi=0.

The
G_{2}
-Laplacian flow is the gradient flow of Hitchin’s volume functional with respect to a natural gradient-type metric on the space of closed
G_{2}
-structures and is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi=\triangle_{\varphi}\varphi,

see Bryant
[
7
]
*Section 6.2 Remark 16.

A
G_{2}
-soliton is a self-similar solution of this
G_{2}
-Laplacian flow.

Definition 1.1
.

A
G_{2}
-Laplacian soliton, or simply a
G_{2}
-soliton,

(M,\varphi,g,X),

consists of a closed
G_{2}
-structure
\varphi
together with a vector field
X
and a constant
\lambda
satisfying

(1.5)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\varphi}\varphi=\lambda\varphi+L_{X}\varphi\quad\text{on }M.

We say the
G_{2}
-soliton is expanding, steady, or shrinking according to whether

\displaystyle\lambda>0,\quad\lambda=0,\quad\text{or}\quad\lambda<0,

respectively.

If the vector field
X
is the gradient of a function, that is, if

X=\nabla f

for some smooth function
f
, then the
G_{2}
-soliton is called a gradient soliton and is denoted by

(M,\varphi,g,f).

A
G_{2}
-soliton is called torsion-free if the underlying
G_{2}
-structure is torsion-free. A
G_{2}
-soliton is said to be complete if the associated Riemannian metric (
1.1
) is complete.

So far, no compact, non-torsion-free
G_{2}
-solitons have been found. More precisely, shrinking solitons do not exist, and steady solitons are all torsion-free; see Lin
[
41
]
*Corollary 1 (note that the sign of
\lambda
is reversed in their convention). Moreover,
G_{2}
-solitons with
X=0
are also torsion-free, as shown in Lotay and Wei
[
42
]
*Proposition 9.2. Thus, the only remaining possibility is an expanding soliton with a nonzero vector field
X
, but such a soliton cannot occur on homogeneous manifolds by Podestà and Raffero
[
50
]
*Corollary 2.2.

There are, however, numerous constructions of complete, non-compact
G_{2}
-solitons
[
7
,
34
,
37
,
44
,
45
,
20
,
35
,
36
]
. In
[
43
]
*Theorem 1.1, Ng derives a necessary condition for the existence of gradient
G_{2}
-solitons on homogeneous spaces. More recently, complete gradient Laplacian solitons have been constructed in
[
5
,
22
,
21
,
28
,
26
]
. The uniqueness of complete gradient shrinkers with conical ends is proved by Haskins, Khan, and Payne
[

]
*Theorem 1.1.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the set of complete gradient
G_{2}
-solitons

(M,\varphi,g,f,p)

each endowed with a chosen basepoint
p
.

A natural problem is to determine which singular models are required in order to compactify the moduli space
\mathcal{M}
.
Our aim is to analyze the potential singular models by employing singularity analysis methods from Riemannian convergence theory.

The structure theory has long served as a powerful framework for studying critical metrics, such as
minimal submanifolds,
Yang-Mills connections,
harmonic maps,
Einstein metrics,
Bach-flat metrics,
Ricci solitons,
Ricci flows, extremal Kähler metrics, and Willmore surfaces, among others. In this paper, we broaden the scope of the structure theory to encompass
G_{2}
-solitons. Our work attempts to provide a more systematic treatment of the
G_{2}
-soliton in this context, though much remains to be done.

We begin by fixing some standard notation.
We use

\displaystyle Rm,\quad Ric,\quad S

to denote the Riemann curvature tensor, the Ricci curvature, and the scalar curvature of the Riemannian metric
g
, respectively. We write
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space.
We set

d(x,p)

to represent the geodesic distance, with respect to
g
, between a point
x
and the basepoint
p
. Given any
k
-form

\eta=\frac{1}{k!}\eta_{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}\,dx^{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}},

we consider two norms: the tensor norm

|\eta|=g_{\varphi}^{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdots g_{\varphi}^{i_{k}j_{k}}\,\eta_{i_{1}\dots i_{k}}\eta_{j_{1}\dots j_{k}},

and the associated form norm

\|\eta\|=g(\eta,\eta)=\frac{1}{k!}|\eta|.

In studying of gradient Ricci solitons

\mathrm{Ric}+\nabla^{2}f=\lambda g,

we have a trace equation and a conservation law

S+\triangle f=\lambda\cdot n,\quad S+|\nabla f|^{2}-2\lambda f=\text{constant},

see Hamilton’s work on the Ricci flow
[
25
, Section 20, p. 84]
. These identities are crucial tools, for instance, in controlling the growth of the potential function
f
, as well as in studying volume growth, see
[
8
]
.

In Section
2
, we obtain analogous identities for
G_{2}
-solitons. In this context, the corresponding conservation law is more intricate, as indicated in (
2.10
):

\displaystyle\nabla[S+|\nabla f|^{2}+\tfrac{2\lambda}{3}f]=\tfrac{2}{3}S\nabla f-2\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits A,

where,
A
is the symmetry product of the torsion form
\tau
,
A_{ij}=-\frac{1}{2}{\tau_{i}}^{k}\tau_{kj}
, see Definition
2.1
. Nonetheless, we can still employ Liouville-type theorems to the trace equation (
2.7
):

\displaystyle-3\triangle f-7\lambda=2S\leq 0,

benefited from the fact that the scalar curvature of a closed
G_{2}
-structure is always non-positive. That part of discussion are collected in a separated article
[
potentialfunction
]
.

We introduce the following subclass of
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 1.3
.

We define the moduli space

\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F)

to be the subset of
\mathcal{M}
consisting of complete gradient
G_{2}
-solitons that satisfy the following properties:

(a)

the scalar curvature is uniformly bounded from below,

\displaystyle S\geq-\Lambda,

where
\Lambda(x)
is a smooth function on
M
,

(b)

the potential function
f\in C^{2}(M)
attains its global minimum at the basepoint
p
and satisfies

\displaystyle|\nabla f|\leq F(d(x,p)),

where
F
is a smooth, positive, non-decreasing function on

\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,\infty).

We then define

\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F,\underline{\nu})

to be the subset of
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F)
consisting of gradient
G_{2}
-solitons that also satisfy the following non-collapsing requirement:

(c)

the local version of Perelman’s
\nu
-entropy (see Section
3.3
) admits a uniform lower bound

\displaystyle\nu\left(B_{g}(x,r),g,r\right)\geq\underline{\nu},\quad\forall x\in M,\quad r\in(0,1).

The space
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F)
contains at least the following explicit examples: a shrinking
G_{2}
-soliton constructed by Fowdar
[
22
]
, a cohomogeneity-one,
Sp(2)
-invariant shrinking
G_{2}
-soliton and a cohomogeneity-one,
SU(3)
-invariant steady
G_{2}
-soliton obtained by Haskins and Nordström
[
28
]
, and a homogeneous steady
G_{2}
-soliton due to Fino and Raffero
[
21
]
; see Section
2.5
. These examples exhibit different possible growth behaviours of the potential function, which naturally leads one to look for corresponding compactness theorems for general function
F
.

For a
G_{2}
-soliton, we write the Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature by

\displaystyle Ric_{f}=Ric+\mathop{\rm Hess}\nolimits f.

This tensor can be controlled in terms of the scalar curvature as

\displaystyle\frac{-\lambda+S}{3}g\leq Ric_{f}\leq\frac{-\lambda-S}{3}g,

see Lemma
2.14
; a complete proof is given in
[
60
]
*Theorem 2.15. As a consequence, every manifold in the class
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F)
satisfies both a Laplacian comparison theorem and a volume comparison theorem, obtained by adapting the work of Wei and Wylie
[
57
]
to this framework. In particular, these manifolds have the volume doubling property, and this property implies the weak compactness of
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F)
through Gromov’s ball-packing argument
[
24
]
; see Section
3.1
.

Definition 1.4
.

We define the
f
-weighted volume element by

dv_{f}:=e^{-f}dv,

where
dv
is the volume form determined by
g
.
The associated weighted volume measure
m_{f}
and the normalised measure are given by

\displaystyle m_{f}(\Omega)=|\Omega|_{f}=\int_{\Omega}dv_{f},\quad\mu_{f}=\frac{m_{f}}{\int_{B_{1}(p)}dm_{f}}.

Each manifold in
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F)
naturally defines a metric measure space

(M,d,\mu_{f}),

where
d
denotes the distance structure induced by the Riemannian metric
g
.

Any limit of a sequence in
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F)
naturally gives rise to such a metric measure space.

Theorem 1.5

(Weak compactness)
.

Let
\left(M_{i},\varphi_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i}\right)
be a sequence in

\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F).

Then, after possibly passing to a subsequence, the associated sequence of metric measure spaces

\left(M_{i},d_{i},f_{i},\mu_{f_{i}},p_{i}\right)

converges in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff sense to a metric measure space

\left(M_{\infty},d_{\infty},f_{\infty},\mu_{\infty},p_{\infty}\right),

see Definition
3.11
.

Furthermore, the limit space has weak regular-singular decomposition

M_{\infty}=\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{S},

see Definition
3.12
.

The resulting limit space may be highly singular and may even exhibit collapse. Nonetheless, Huang, Li and Wang
[
31
]
*Theorem 1.5, Proposition 4.8 establishes that the regular part enjoys specific convexity properties and that the tangent cone depends Hölder continuously in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology, extending the work of Colding and Naber
[
18
]
on manifolds with a lower Ricci curvature bound.
We summarise these consequences in Section
3.2
.

Under the additional non-collapsing assumption
(c)
, the Gromov-Hausdorff convergence can be strengthened to pointed
C^{1,\alpha}
convergence for sequences in
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F,\underline{\nu})
. This follows from a general compactness theorem of H.Z. Li, Y. Li and B. Wang
[
39
]
*Theorem 10.2, which extends Cheeger-Naber’s result
[
12
]
to certain metric measure spaces by exploiting estimates in harmonic coordinates
[
3
]
, Anderson’s gap lemma
[
3
]
, Colding’s volume continuity theorem
[
17
]
, and the characterisation of tangent cones by Cheeger and Colding
[
9
,
10
]
, namely that every tangent cone is a metric cone. We summarise these arguments in Section
3.4
.

Theorem 1.6

(Pointed
C^{1,\alpha}
compactness)
.

Let
\left(M_{i},\varphi_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i}\right)
be a sequence in

\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F,\underline{\nu}).

Then the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff convergence obtained in Theorem

can in fact be upgraded to pointed

.
Equivalently, after passing to a subsequence, the associated sequence

\left(M_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i}\right)

converges in the pointed

sense to a metric space

\left(M_{\infty},g_{\infty},f_{\infty},p_{\infty}\right).

Furthermore, the limiting space
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
singular space and the regular-singular decomposition satisfies properties stated
in Definition
3.25
.

In this article, we consider the following question:

Question 1.7
.

Could we prove the smooth convergence of the
G_{2}
-structures
\varphi_{i}
on the regular part?

Definition 1.8

(Conifold
G_{2}
-soliton)
.

A length space

\left(M,d,g,f,p\right)

is called a conifold
G_{2}
-soliton, if it is a
C^{\infty}
singular space in the sense of Definition

G_{2}
-structure

such that

Remark 1.9
.

The term “conifold” was introduced in Chen and Wang
[
13
]
*Definition 2.1, where it was proved that the moduli space of non-collapsed Calabi-Yau conifolds is compact with respect to the pointed
C^{\infty}
topology.

To further enhance the regularity of both the convergence and the limiting object on the regular set, we now demonstrate that it is enough to prove epsilon regularity theorems, assuming either a curvature bound at half the dimension or a sufficiently small entropy bound.

In summary, we show that

Theorem 1.10

(Epsilon regularity)
.

Let
\left(M_{i},\varphi_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i}\right)
be a sequence in

\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F,\underline{\nu}).

Assume that at every point of the regular part, there exists a sufficiently small ball on which either the local entropy is sufficiently small

\underline{\nu}=-\epsilon.

Then the pointed
C^{1,\alpha}
convergence obtained in Theorem
1.6
actually upgrades to pointed
C^{\infty}
convergence (see Definition
3.50
), and the corresponding limit is a conifold
G_{2}
-soliton (see Definition
3.33
).

Finally, we establish a compactness theorem for complete, non-compact gradient
G_{2}
-solitons within the subclass
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F,\underline{\nu})
under the Riemann curvature
L^{\frac{7}{2}}
bound.

Theorem 1.11

(Pointed
C^{\infty}
compactness)
.

Let
\left(M_{i},\varphi_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i}\right)
be a sequence in

\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F,\underline{\nu})

with a uniform

bound on the Riemann curvature:

(1.6)

\int_{B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},R)}\left|Rm_{g_{i}}\right|_{g_{i}}^{\frac{7}{2}}\leq E(R)<\infty.

Then a subsequence of
\left(M_{i},\varphi_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i}\right)
converges, in the pointed

sense, to a smooth
G_{2}
-soliton.

2.
G_{2}
-soliton

In this section, we begin with a brief review of
G_{2}
-solitons: one approach is via the exterior differential system associated with the torsion form, and the other is through curvature equations. We then derive several fundamental formulas and estimates for
G_{2}
-solitons that will be used in subsequent parts of the paper. Throughout this note, we assume that the
G_{2}
-structure is a closed 3-form.

2.1.
Torsions and curvatures

Definition 2.1
.

We introduce the symmetric tensors

\displaystyle A_{ij}:=-\frac{1}{2}{\tau_{i}}^{k}\tau_{kj},\quad B_{ij}:={\varphi_{i}}^{pq}\nabla_{p}\tau_{qj}.

More precisely, the trace of the tensor
A
equals the squared norm of the torsion form
\tau
:

\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits A=\|\tau\|^{2}.

For a closed
G_{2}
-structure
\varphi
, its Hodge Laplacian

\triangle_{\varphi}\varphi=d\tau

admits a decomposition into irreducible
G_{2}
-modules, see
[
42
]
*Section 2.2.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
\varphi
is a closed
G_{2}
-structure. Then

\displaystyle\triangle_{\varphi}\varphi=d\tau=\frac{1}{7}\|\tau\|^{2}\varphi+i_{\varphi}h_{0},\quad h_{0}=\frac{1}{2}B_{ij}-\frac{3}{14}\|\tau\|^{2}g_{ij}+\frac{1}{2}A_{ij}.

Proof.

Since
\tau\in\Omega_{14}^{2}
, we employ the characterisation in (
1.3
) and invoke
[
60
]
*Proposition 2.19 to obtain
\pi_{7}^{3}d\tau=0
together with the explicit formula for
\pi_{1}^{3}d\tau
.
To confirm that

\pi_{1}^{3}d\tau=\frac{1}{7}\|\tau\|^{2}\varphi,

we compute

\displaystyle g(\varphi,d\tau)dv

\displaystyle=d\tau\wedge\ast_{\varphi}\varphi=d(\tau\wedge\ast_{\varphi}\varphi)-\tau\wedge d(\ast\varphi)=\tau\wedge\ast_{\varphi}\tau=\|\tau\|^{2}dv.

The
\Omega_{27}^{3}
-component is given by
i_{\varphi}h_{0}
, where
h_{0}
is a trace-free symmetric
2
-tensor introduced in Lemma
2.2
; see also
[
42
]
*Equation (2.20). Combining these components yields the identities claimed in the lemma.
∎

We next recall the expression for
d\tau^{3}
from
[
7
]
*Corollary 3 and for
L_{X}\varphi
from
[
42
]
*Section 9.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(M,\varphi,g,X)
be a
G_{2}
-soliton. Then

\displaystyle L_{X}\varphi=di_{X}\varphi=(\tfrac{1}{7}\|\tau\|^{2}-\lambda)\varphi+i_{\varphi}h_{0},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}d\tau^{3}=-\frac{1}{7}\|\tau\|^{4}dv+\tau^{2}\wedge\pi_{27}^{3}(di_{X}\varphi).

Proof.

By combining the soliton equation (
1.5
) with the decomposition Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

\lambda\varphi+L_{X}\varphi=\triangle_{\varphi}\varphi=\frac{1}{7}\|\tau\|^{2}\varphi+i_{\varphi}h_{0}.

Using the Cartan formula,

L_{X}\varphi=di_{X}\varphi,

we then obtain the first identity. For the second, observe that

\frac{1}{3}d\tau^{3}=\tau^{2}\wedge d\tau.

Using Lemma
2.2
again and substituting (

), we find

\tau^{2}\wedge\varphi=-\tau\wedge\ast_{\varphi}\tau=-\|\tau\|^{2}dv.

Finally, we substitute the decomposition of

to obtain the stated formula.
∎

.

The

\displaystyle j_{\varphi}(\gamma)(X,Y)=\ast_{\varphi}\big(i_{X}\varphi\wedge i_{Y}\varphi\wedge\gamma\big).

Lemma 2.5

\displaystyle Ric(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\|\tau\|^{2}g-\frac{1}{4}j_{\varphi}\Big[d\tau-\frac{1}{2}\ast_{\varphi}(\tau\wedge\tau)\Big],\quad S(g)=-\frac{1}{2}\|\tau\|^{2}\leq 0.

Definition 2.6
.

The torsion tensor

is the skew-symmetric tensor

\displaystyle T_{ij}=-T_{ji}=-\frac{1}{2}\tau_{ij}.

Using this, we can rewrite Definition

Lemma 2.7
.

\displaystyle A_{ij}=-2{T_{i}}^{k}T_{kj},\quad B_{ij}=-2{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}\nabla_{p}T_{qj},\quad\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits A=-2S,\quad S=-|T|^{2}.

Definition 2.8
.

We set

\displaystyle\lambda_{S}:=-\frac{\lambda}{3}+\frac{S}{3},\quad\mathcal{K}:=\frac{S}{3}g+A=-\frac{|T|^{2}}{3}g-2{T_{i}}^{k}T_{kj}.

Using Lemma

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits\mathcal{K}=\frac{1}{3}S,\quad\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits A=-2\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits({T_{i}}^{k}T_{kj})=-2T^{ak}\nabla_{a}T_{kj}.

If

\displaystyle\delta(i_{X}\varphi)=0.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold, i.e. a compact manifold without boundary. Then:

•

shrinking
G_{2}
-solitons do not exist;

•

any steady
G_{2}
-soliton is necessarily torsion-free;

•

any expanding
G_{2}
-soliton with

must be torsion-free.

Consequently, the only compact
G_{2}
-solitons that can possess nonzero torsion are expanding
G_{2}
-solitons with

.

Proof.

Integrating the scalar curvature equation (

) over a closed

\displaystyle\int_{M}Sdv=-\frac{7\lambda}{2}.

Using the non-positivity of the scalar curvature Lemma

If

Integrating these over the closed manifold

must vanish. Thus, we reach the final conclusion.
∎

2.2.1.
Formulas for scalar curvature and the potential function

We set

X=\nabla f

and refer to
f
as the potential function.
From now on, we denote by

\triangle:=\nabla^{\ast}\nabla

the Bochner Laplacian associated with the metric
g
, where
\nabla^{\ast}
is the formal
L^{2}
-adjoint of the covariant derivative acting on smooth tensor fields. Note that the notation
\triangle_{\varphi}
will be used for the Hodge Laplacian on differential forms, which may cause some confusion. Recall that on functions, the Bochner Laplacian agrees with the
Laplace-Beltrami operator and differs from the Hodge Laplacian only by a sign.

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
(M,\varphi,g,\nabla f)
be a gradient
G_{2}
-soliton. Then

(2.5)

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi=Rm*\varphi+\lambda\varphi+\mathcal{L}_{\nabla f}\varphi,

(2.6)

\displaystyle Ric_{f}:=Ric+\nabla^{2}f=-\frac{\lambda}{3}g+\mathcal{K},\quad i_{\nabla f}T=0,

(2.7)

\displaystyle-3\triangle f-7\lambda=2S\leq 0,\quad\nabla S=-\frac{3}{2}\nabla\triangle f,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\mathcal{K}=\frac{1}{3}\nabla S+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits A=-\frac{1}{6}\nabla S+Ric(\nabla f),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\nabla S=2Ric(\nabla f)-2\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits A,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\nabla[S+|\nabla f|^{2}+\tfrac{2\lambda}{3}f]=\tfrac{2}{3}S\nabla f-2\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits A,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\triangle|\nabla f|^{2}=|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\langle\nabla S,\nabla f\rangle+Ric(\nabla f,\nabla f).

Define the weighted
f
-Laplace operator by

\triangle_{f}(\cdot):=\triangle(\cdot)-(\nabla f,\nabla\cdot).

Then we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle\triangle_{f}f

\displaystyle=-\frac{7\lambda}{3}-\frac{2S}{3}-|\nabla f|^{2},

(2.13)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\triangle_{f}|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+Ric_{f}(\nabla f,\nabla f)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2}{3}\langle\nabla S,\nabla f\rangle-\langle\nabla|\nabla f|^{2},\nabla f\rangle.

Proof.

By applying the Weitzenböck formula, which relates the Hodge Laplacian to the Bochner Laplacian, the soliton equation (
1.5
) can be rewritten as (
2.5
). The second relation in (
2.6
) follows from

0=\delta i_{X}\varphi=\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits(X)+2i_{X}T\quad\text{and}\quad\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits(\nabla f)=0,

see
[
27
]
*Page 5050.
The identities in (
2.7
) are obtained by taking the trace of (
2.6
),

S+\triangle f=\frac{-7}{3}\lambda+\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits\mathcal{K},

inserting the expression from Lemma
2.9
.

For the first equality in (
2.8
), we compute the divergence of
\mathcal{K}
using Definition
2.8
:

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\nabla S+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits A.

To obtain the second equality in (
2.8
), we compute
\nabla S
using the soliton equation.
The second contracted Bianchi identity gives

\nabla S=2\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\,Ric.

Using the soliton equation and the Bochner formula for the gradient of a function, we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla S

\displaystyle=-2\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\nabla^{2}f)+2\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle=-2[\nabla\triangle f+Ric(\nabla f)]+2\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\mathcal{K}.

Using the scalar curvature identity (
2.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla S=\frac{4}{3}\nabla S-2Ric(\nabla f)+2\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\mathcal{K},

which yields the second identity for
div\mathcal{K}
in (
2.8
).
Equating the two expressions for
div\mathcal{K}
in (
2.8
) gives (
2.9
).
To obtain (
2.10
), we use the soliton equation (
2.6
) directly:

\displaystyle\nabla|\nabla f|^{2}=2\langle\nabla f,\nabla^{2}f\rangle=2\mathcal{K}(\nabla f)-\frac{2\lambda}{3}g(\nabla f)-2Ric(\nabla f).

Now, adding the second identity in (
2.8
) to this identity, we obtain

(2.14)

\displaystyle\nabla[S+3|\nabla f|^{2}+2\lambda f]

\displaystyle=6\mathcal{K}(\nabla f)-6\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\mathcal{K}.

From (
2.6
) we also have

T_{kj}f^{j}=0.

Inserting the expression for
\mathcal{K}
from Definition
2.8
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\nabla f)=\frac{S}{3}g_{ij}f^{j}-2{T_{i}}^{k}T_{kj}f^{j}=\frac{S}{3}f_{i}.

Substituting this formula together with the first identity in (
2.8
) into the preceding relation (
2.14
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\nabla[S+3|\nabla f|^{2}+2\lambda f]=2S\nabla f-6\Big[\frac{1}{3}\nabla S+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits A\Big].

A straightforward rearrangement of the terms gives (
2.10
).

The formula (
2.11
) follows from substituting (
2.7
) into the Bochner identity

\frac{1}{2}\triangle|\nabla f|^{2}=|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+\langle\nabla\triangle f,\nabla f\rangle+Ric(\nabla f,\nabla f).

Finally, combining

\triangle_{f}f=\triangle f-|\nabla f|^{2}

with the
f
-Bochner identity

\frac{1}{2}\triangle_{f}|\nabla f|^{2}=|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+\langle\nabla\triangle_{f}f,\nabla f\rangle+Ric_{f}(\nabla f,\nabla f),

we deduce (
2.12
) and (
2.13
).
∎

Remark 2.19
.

Since the right-hand side of (
2.14
) (see also (
2.10
)) is no longer identically zero, we cannot conclude that the terms following the gradient on the left-hand side are constant. This loss is significant, as it plays an important role in determining the gradient estimate of
f
in the context of Ricci solitons.

2.3.
Associated flow and higher derivatives

We recall that the
G_{2}
-Laplacian flow of the 3-form is given by

(2.15)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi=\triangle_{\varphi}\varphi.

The induced evolution of the metric is the Ricci flow with an additional nonlinear term
\mathcal{K}
from Definition
2.8
,

(2.16)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g=-2Ric+2\mathcal{K}.

We now recall the self-similar solution to the
G_{2}
-Laplacian flow that arises from the soliton equation (
1.5
).

Definition 2.20
.

We introduce the scaling factor

\displaystyle s(t)=\frac{2\lambda t+3}{3}>0,\quad\text{i.e.}\quad\lambda t>-\frac{3}{2}.

For the shrinking, steady, and expanding cases, the time parameter
t
ranges over

(-\infty,-\frac{3}{2\lambda}),\quad(-\infty,+\infty),\quad(-\frac{3}{2\lambda},+\infty),

respectively.

Definition 2.21
.

We define a time-dependent vector field by

\displaystyle\tilde{X}(t)=s(t)^{-1}X,

which generates a one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms
\sigma(t)
via

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sigma(t)=\tilde{X}(\sigma(t)),\quad\sigma(0)=id.

If the ordinary differential equation
u_{t}=F(u)
with positive initial data admits a global solution, then
\tilde{X}
is complete, and hence the diffeomorphisms
\sigma(t)
are defined on the entire time interval specified above.

Definition 2.22

(Associated
G_{2}
-Laplacian flow)
.

The associated
G_{2}
-Laplacian flow corresponding to a
G_{2}
-soliton is the triple consisting of the rescaled
G_{2}
-structure, Riemannian metric, and potential function

(2.17)

\displaystyle\varphi(t)=s(t)^{\frac{3}{2}}\sigma(t)^{\ast}\varphi,\quad g(t)=s(t)\sigma(t)^{\ast}g,\quad f(t)=\sigma(t)^{\ast}f.

The soliton

(M,\varphi,g,f)

is recovered as the time slice at
t=0
of the associated
G_{2}
-Laplacian flow.

Lemma 2.23
.

The tensors
A
and
\mathcal{K}
are invariant under this rescaling, namely

\displaystyle A(t)=\sigma(t)^{\ast}A,\quad\mathcal{K}(t)=\sigma(t)^{\ast}\mathcal{K}.

Proof.

For brevity, write
s:=s(t)
and
\sigma:=\sigma(t)
. The curvatures scale as

(2.18)

\displaystyle Rm_{g(t)}=s\sigma^{\ast}Rm_{g},\quad Ric_{g(t)}=\sigma^{\ast}Ric_{g},\quad S_{g(t)}=s^{-1}\sigma^{\ast}S_{g}.

For
k
-forms, using the expressions for the Hodge star and the codifferential, we obtain

\displaystyle\ast_{\varphi(t)}=s^{\frac{7}{2}-k}\sigma^{\ast}\ast_{\varphi},\quad\delta_{\varphi(t)}=s^{-1}\sigma^{\ast}\delta_{\varphi},\quad\triangle_{\varphi(t)}=s^{-1}\sigma^{\ast}\triangle_{\varphi}.

Taking
k=3
and using
\psi=\ast\varphi
and
-2T=\delta\varphi
, we deduce

\displaystyle\psi(t)=s^{2}\sigma^{\ast}\psi,\quad T(t)=s^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma^{\ast}T,\quad\triangle_{\varphi(t)}\varphi(t)=s^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma^{\ast}\triangle_{\varphi}\varphi,

see also
[
27
]
*Lemma 2.1.
This yields the claim.
∎

Lemma 2.24
.

The families
g(t)
and
\varphi(t)
solve (
2.16
) and (
2.15
), respectively. Moreover,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(t)=|\nabla_{g(t)}f(t)|_{g(t)}^{2}\geq 0.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.23
, we compute

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g(t)

\displaystyle=\sigma(t)^{\ast}\!\left[\frac{2\lambda}{3}g+L_{X}g\right]=\sigma(t)^{\ast}[-2Ric(g)+2\mathcal{K}]=-2Ric(g(t))+2\mathcal{K}(t).

As shown in
[
42
]
*Page 224, the rescaled
G_{2}
-form
\varphi(t)
from Definition
2.22

satisfies the flow equation (
2.15
). The evolution of
f(t)
follows immediately from the definition of
\sigma(t)
in Definition
2.21
.
∎

Lemma 2.25
.

For all
x\in M
,

\displaystyle f(x,t)\leq f(x,0),\quad t\in(-\infty,0],

\displaystyle f(x,t)\geq f(x,0),\quad t\in[0,+\infty).

Proof.

This follows by integrating
\partial_{t}f
in time in Lemma
2.24
.
∎

Analogous identities to those in Proposition
2.18
hold along the associated flow.

Lemma 2.26
.

Along the associated flow, we have

(2.19)

\displaystyle Ric_{g(t)}+\nabla_{g(t)}^{2}f=-\frac{\lambda}{3s(t)}g+\mathcal{K}_{g(t)},

(2.20)

\displaystyle-\triangle f-\frac{7\lambda}{3s(t)}=\frac{2}{3}S\leq 0,

(2.21)

\displaystyle\nabla\bigl[S+|\nabla f|^{2}+\frac{2\lambda}{3s(t)}f\bigr]=\frac{2}{3}S\nabla f+4T^{ak}\nabla_{a}T_{kj}.

Finally, we establish estimates for the higher-order derivatives of the Riemann curvature tensor for
G_{2}
-solitons by exploiting the associated flow. We recall the Shi-type estimates of the
G_{2}
-Laplacian flow by Lotay and Wei
[

*Theorem 4.2, Theorem 4.4, Remark 4.5.

Lemma 2.27
.

Let
\varphi(t)
be a
G_{2}
-Laplacian flow defined on a relatively compact ball
B_{2R}(p)
. Assume that
|Rm(t)|_{g(t)}\leq K
on
B_{2R}(p)\times[0,\frac{1}{K}]
. Then for every
k\geq 0
we have

|\nabla^{k}Rm(t)|_{g(t)}\leq C(k,R,K)\,t^{-\frac{k}{2}}K\quad\text{on }B_{R}(p)\times\Big[0,\frac{1}{K}\Big].

Proposition 2.28
.

Let
(M,\varphi,g,\nabla f)
be a gradient
G_{2}
-soliton.
Suppose that
|Rm|_{g}\leq K
on
B_{2R}(p)
. Then, for every
k\geq 0
,

|\nabla^{k}Rm|_{g}R^{k+2}\leq C(k,K)\quad\text{on }B_{R}(p).

Proof.

From the rescaled metric (
2.17
), together with the corresponding curvature scaling relation (
2.18
), we have

|Rm(t)|_{g(t)}=s^{-1}\,|Rm|_{g}\circ\sigma(t)\leq s^{-1}K.

Next, apply the derivative estimates from Lemma
2.27
to the associated flow provided by Lemma
2.24
on a fixed finite time interval
I
away from the initial time and on the unit ball. Using in addition the family of diffeomorphisms from Definition
2.21
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}Rm|_{g}

\displaystyle=|\nabla^{k}Rm|_{g}\circ\sigma(0)=|\nabla^{k}Rm|_{g}\circ\sigma(t)=s^{\frac{k+2}{2}}|\nabla^{k}Rm(t)|_{g(t)}

\displaystyle\leq s^{\frac{k+2}{2}}\,C(k,K)\,t^{-\frac{k}{2}}\,s^{-1}=s^{\frac{k}{2}}\,C(k,K)\,t^{-\frac{k}{2}}\leq C(k,K).

Finally, we scale the unit ball back to
B_{R}(p)
, which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.29
.

In Section

, we will refine these higher-order estimates by providing more explicit constants. Specifically, the constant will be improved to

multiplied by a constant

.

2.4.1.
Laplacian of the Riemann curvature tensor, the Ricci curvature tensor, and the scalar curvature

The following lemma is obtained by a straightforward computation.

Lemma 2.30
.

Let
(M,\varphi,g,\nabla f)
be a gradient
G_{2}
-soliton. Then
the Riemann curvature tensor satisfies

(2.22)

\displaystyle\triangle R_{ijkl}=\Big(R_{qlij,k}f_{q}-R_{qkij,l}f_{q}\Big)

\displaystyle-R_{qikp}R_{qjlp}-R_{qjkp}R_{iqlp}-R_{qpkl}R_{ijpq}-R_{qilp}R_{qjpk}-R_{qjlp}R_{iqpk}

\displaystyle+2\lambda_{S}R_{ijkl}+\Big(R_{qlij}A_{qk}-R_{qkij}A_{ql}\Big)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{3}\Big(S_{ik}g_{lj}-S_{jk}g_{li}-S_{il}g_{kj}+S_{jl}g_{ki}\Big)

\displaystyle+\Big(A_{lj,ik}-A_{li,jk}-A_{kj,il}+A_{ki,jl}\Big).

For the Ricci tensor we have

(2.23)

\displaystyle\triangle R_{ik}

\displaystyle=R_{ik,q}f_{q}+2R_{qikp}R_{qp}+2\lambda_{S}R_{ik}+\Big(R_{qi}A_{qk}-R_{qkij}A_{qj}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3}\Big(-S_{ik}+\triangle Sg_{ki}\Big)+\Big(-A_{ji,jk}-A_{kj,ij}+\triangle A_{ki}\Big).

The scalar curvature fulfills

(2.24)

\displaystyle\triangle S

\displaystyle=S_{p}f_{p}-2|Ric|^{2}-\frac{2\lambda}{3}S+\frac{2}{3}S^{2}+2<Ric,A>-2\nabla^{j}\nabla_{i}{A^{i}}_{j}.

Furthermore, employing the contraction notation
\ast
with respect to the metric
g
, these relations can be compactly written as

(2.25)

\displaystyle\triangle_{f}Rm=

\displaystyle\,Rm\ast Rm+Rm\ast T\ast T+\sum_{p+q=2}\nabla^{p}T\ast\nabla^{q}T,

(2.26)

\displaystyle\triangle_{f}Ric=

\displaystyle\,Rm\ast Ric+Rm\ast T\ast T+\sum_{p+q=2}\nabla^{p}T\ast\nabla^{q}T,

(2.27)

\displaystyle\triangle_{f}S=

\displaystyle\,-2|Ric|^{2}-\frac{2\lambda}{3}S+\frac{2}{3}S^{2}+2<Ric,A>-2\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits A.

Proof.

We first derive the identity (
2.22
) for
Rm
.
In normal coordinates, applying the second Bianchi identity

R_{ijkl,p}+R_{ijlp,k}+R_{ijpk,l}=0

to the term
R_{ijkl,p}
gives

\displaystyle\Delta R_{ijkl}=R_{ijkl,pp}

\displaystyle=-\bigl(R_{ijlp,kp}+R_{ijpk,lp}\bigr).

Using the commutation formula for covariant derivatives,

T_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s},kp}-T_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s},pk}=\sum_{1\leq t\leq s}R_{qi_{t}kp}T_{i_{1}\cdots i_{t-1}qi_{t+1}\cdots i_{s}},

we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta R_{ijkl}

\displaystyle=-\bigl(R_{ijlp,pk}+R_{ijpk,pl}\bigr)+Rm\ast Rm.

Here

\displaystyle Rm\ast Rm

\displaystyle=-R_{qikp}R_{qjlp}-R_{qjkp}R_{iqlp}-R_{qlkp}R_{ijqp}-R_{qpkp}R_{ijlq}

\displaystyle\quad-R_{qilp}R_{qjpk}-R_{qjlp}R_{iqpk}-R_{qplp}R_{ijqk}-R_{qklp}R_{ijpq}.

By applying the first Bianchi identity to the third and eighth terms, we may rewrite this as

\displaystyle Rm\ast Rm

\displaystyle=-R_{qikp}R_{qjlp}-R_{qjkp}R_{iqlp}+R_{qpkl}R_{ijpq}-R_{qk}R_{ijlq}

\displaystyle\quad-R_{qilp}R_{qjpk}-R_{qjlp}R_{iqpk}-R_{ql}R_{ijqk}.

For the first term in
\Delta Rm
, the first Bianchi identity yields

\displaystyle R_{ijlp,pk}=R_{lpij,pk}=-R_{lpjp,ik}-R_{lppi,jk}=-R_{lj,ik}+R_{li,jk}.

We now insert the soliton equation (
2.6
) and use the commutation formula to simplify the expression
R_{lj,i}-R_{li,j}
:

\displaystyle R_{lj,i}-R_{li,j}

\displaystyle=(-f_{lj}+\lambda_{S}g_{lj}+A_{lj})_{i}-(-f_{li}+\lambda_{S}g_{li}+A_{li})_{j}

\displaystyle=R_{qlij}f_{q}+(\lambda_{S}g_{lj})_{i}-(\lambda_{S}g_{li})_{j}+A_{lj,i}-A_{li,j}.

Substituting the soliton equation again to replace
f_{qk}
in these terms, we get

\displaystyle-R_{ijlp,pk}

\displaystyle=(R_{lj,i}-R_{li,j})_{k}

\displaystyle=[R_{qlij}f_{q}+(\lambda_{S}g_{lj})_{i}-(\lambda_{S}g_{li})_{j}+A_{lj,i}-A_{li,j}]_{k}

\displaystyle=R_{qlij,k}f_{q}+R_{qlij}f_{qk}+(\lambda_{S}g_{lj})_{ik}-(\lambda_{S}g_{li})_{jk}+A_{lj,ik}-A_{li,jk}

\displaystyle=R_{qlij,k}f_{q}-R_{qlij}R_{qk}+\lambda_{S}R_{klij}+R_{qlij}A_{qk}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{S_{ik}}{3}g_{lj}-\frac{S_{jk}}{3}g_{li}+A_{lj,ik}-A_{li,jk}.

Similarly, for the second term we find

\displaystyle R_{ijpk,pl}

\displaystyle=(R_{kj,i}-R_{ki,j})_{l}

\displaystyle=R_{qkij,l}f_{q}-R_{qkij}R_{ql}+\lambda_{S}R_{lkij}+R_{qkij}A_{ql}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{S_{il}}{3}g_{kj}-\frac{S_{jl}}{3}g_{ki}+A_{kj,il}-A_{ki,jl}.

Putting all of these identities together, we obtain the desired equation for
Rm
.

To derive equation (
2.23
) for
Ric
, we now contract (
2.22
) over the indices
j,l
, and apply the first Bianchi identity to the second term in the first line, which gives

\displaystyle R_{qjij,k}=R_{qi,k},\quad R_{qkij,j}=R_{qjik,j}=-R_{qk,i}+R_{qi,k}.

To complete the derivation of (
2.23
), it only remains to contract the five terms appearing in the second line. We assert that the sum of the three middle terms is zero:

\displaystyle R_{qjkp}R_{iqjp}+R_{qpkj}R_{ijpq}+R_{qijp}R_{qjpk}=0,

which again follows from the first Bianchi identity. Indeed, define

II_{ki}=R_{qjkp}R_{iqjp}=-R_{qpjk}R_{qpji}.

Then

\displaystyle II_{ki}

\displaystyle=R_{qpkj}R_{ijpq}

\displaystyle=(R_{qkjp}+R_{qjpk})(R_{ipqj}+R_{iqjp})

\displaystyle=R_{qkjp}R_{ipqj}+R_{qkjp}R_{iqjp}+R_{qjpk}R_{ipqj}+R_{qjpk}R_{iqjp}

\displaystyle=-R_{pjkq}R_{ipjq}+2II_{ki}-R_{qjkp}R_{iqjp}.

This shows the claimed identity. Using in addition

\sum_{i}A_{ii}=-2S,

we finally deduce the desired equation for
Ric
:

\displaystyle\triangle R_{ik}

\displaystyle=R_{ik,q}f_{q}+2R_{qikp}R_{qp}+(R_{qi}A_{qk}-R_{qkij}A_{qj})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3}\Big(7S_{ik}-2S_{ik}+\triangle Sg_{ki}\Big)

\displaystyle+(-2S_{ik}-A_{ji,jk}-A_{kj,ij}+\triangle A_{ki}).

Finally, we derive the scalar curvature equation (
2.24
) by contracting (
2.23
) once more and using

A_{ij,ji}=A_{ij,ij}-R_{qj}A_{qj}+R_{qi}A_{qi}=A_{ij,ij}.

We could also derive the equation for the scalar curvature directly by taking the divergence of (
2.9
), and using

\displaystyle g^{ij}\nabla_{i}(2R_{jp}f_{p})

\displaystyle=2g^{ij}(R_{jp,i}f_{p}+R_{jp}f_{pi})

\displaystyle=S_{p}f_{p}+2g^{ij}R_{jp}(-R_{pi}+\lambda_{S}g_{pi}+A_{pi})

\displaystyle=S_{p}f_{p}-2|Ric|^{2}+2\lambda_{S}S+2R_{ip}A_{pi}.

Putting everything together, we obtain (
2.24
), as claimed.
∎

2.4.2.
Laplacian of the torsion tensor

Thanks to the presence of the
G_{2}
-structure, we obtain additional identities involving the torsion tensor
T
.
Expressed in terms of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
associated with the metric
g
, the torsion tensor satisfies

(2.28)

\displaystyle T_{ij}=\frac{1}{24}\nabla_{i}\varphi_{pqr}\,{\psi_{j}}^{pqr},

(2.29)

\displaystyle\nabla^{i}\varphi_{jkl}=T^{ip}\psi_{pjkl}.

The dual
4

(2.30)

\displaystyle\psi_{ijkl}=\varphi_{pij}\varphi_{qkl}g^{pq}-g_{ik}g_{jl}+g_{il}g_{jk},

(2.31)

\displaystyle\nabla_{p}\psi_{ijkl}=-T_{pi}\varphi_{jkl}+T_{pj}\varphi_{kli}-T_{pk}\varphi_{lij}+T_{pl}\varphi_{ijk}.

From the first and second Bianchi identities we deduce the contraction relations

(2.32)

\displaystyle R_{ipqr}\varphi^{pqr}=0,

(2.33)

\displaystyle R_{ijpq,r}\varphi^{pqr}=0.

When
\varphi
is closed, the torsion tensor
T
satisfies the following contraction properties:

(2.34)

\displaystyle T_{pq}\varphi^{pqk}=0,

(2.35)

\displaystyle T_{pq}\psi^{pqij}=-2T^{ij},

(2.36)

\displaystyle\nabla^{p}T_{pj}=0,

its covariant derivative is given by

(2.37)

\displaystyle 4\nabla_{k}T_{ij}=

\displaystyle R_{jkpq}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}-R_{ikpq}{\varphi_{j}}^{pq}+R_{jipq}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq}

\displaystyle-2T_{jp}T_{kq}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}+2T_{ip}T_{kq}{\varphi_{j}}^{pq}+2T_{ip}T_{jq}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq},

and the Ricci tensor can be expressed as

(2.38)

\displaystyle R_{ij}={\varphi_{i}}^{pq}\nabla_{p}T_{qj}-{T_{i}}^{k}T_{kj}.

Lemma 2.32
.

If
\varphi
is closed, then

\displaystyle 4\Delta T_{ij}

\displaystyle=2R_{jp,q}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}+R_{jkpq}{T^{kr}}\varphi_{ari}{\varphi}^{apq}

\displaystyle-2R_{ip,q}{\varphi_{j}}^{pq}-R_{ikpq}{T^{kr}}\varphi_{arj}{\varphi}^{apq}

\displaystyle+4R_{jkir}{T^{kr}}+2R_{jipq}T^{pq}

\displaystyle-2\nabla^{k}T_{jp}T_{kq}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}+2T_{jp}{A_{q}}^{r}{\psi_{ri}}^{pq}

\displaystyle+2\nabla^{k}T_{ip}T_{kq}{\varphi_{j}}^{pq}-2T_{ip}{A_{q}}^{r}{\psi_{rj}}^{pq}

\displaystyle+\nabla^{k}T_{ip}T_{jq}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq}+T_{ip}\nabla^{k}T_{jq}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq}-2T_{ip}{A^{p}}_{j}.

Proof.

Using (
2.37
), we compute each term in local coordinates:

\displaystyle 4\Delta T_{ij}

\displaystyle=4\nabla^{k}\nabla_{k}T_{ij}

\displaystyle=\nabla^{k}(R_{jkpq}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}-R_{ikpq}{\varphi_{j}}^{pq}+R_{jipq}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq})

\displaystyle\quad+2\nabla^{k}(-T_{jp}T_{kq}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}+T_{ip}T_{kq}{\varphi_{j}}^{pq}+T_{ip}T_{jq}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq}).

1st term

For the first term we use (
2.29
):

\displaystyle I:=\nabla^{k}(R_{jkpq}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq})=\nabla^{k}R_{jkpq}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}+R_{jkpq}{T^{kr}}{\psi_{ri}}^{pq}.

By the second Bianchi identity

R_{jkpq,k}=-R_{jkqk,p}-R_{jkkp,q}=-R_{jq,p}+R_{jp,q},

we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}=\nabla^{k}R_{jkpq}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}=(-R_{jq,p}+R_{jp,q}){\varphi_{i}}^{pq}=2R_{jp,q}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}.

Substituting (
2.30
) into the second part of
I
gives

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=R_{jkpq}{T^{kr}}{\psi_{ri}}^{pq}

\displaystyle=R_{jkpq}{T^{kr}}\big[\varphi_{ari}{\varphi_{b}}^{pq}g^{ab}-{g_{r}}^{p}{g_{i}}^{q}+{g_{r}}^{q}{g_{i}}^{p}\big]

\displaystyle=R_{jkpq}{T^{kr}}\varphi_{ari}{\varphi}^{apq}+2R_{jkir}{T^{kr}}.

Altogether this yields

\displaystyle I=2R_{jp,q}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}+R_{jkpq}{T^{kr}}\varphi_{ari}{\varphi}^{apq}+2R_{jkir}{T^{kr}}.

2nd term

In the same way, the second term is

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=-\nabla^{k}(R_{ikpq}{\varphi_{j}}^{pq})

\displaystyle=-2R_{ip,q}{\varphi_{j}}^{pq}-R_{ikpq}{T^{kr}}\varphi_{arj}{\varphi}^{apq}-2R_{ikjr}{T^{kr}}.

Exchanging
k,r
we get

\displaystyle-R_{ikjr}{T^{kr}}=-R_{irjk}{T^{rk}}=R_{jkir}{T^{kr}},

which matches the last term in
I
.

3rd term

For the third term, we employ (
2.29
), (
2.35
) and (
2.33
):

\displaystyle\nabla^{k}(R_{jipq}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq})

\displaystyle=\nabla^{k}R_{jipq}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq}+R_{jipq}\nabla^{k}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq}

\displaystyle=R_{jipq}\nabla^{k}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq}=R_{jipq}T^{kr}{\psi_{rk}}^{pq}

\displaystyle=2R_{jipq}T^{pq}.

4th term

For the fourth term we use (
2.29
) and (
2.36
):

\displaystyle IV

\displaystyle:=2\nabla^{k}(T_{jp}T_{kq}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq})=2\nabla^{k}T_{jp}T_{kq}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}+2T_{jp}T_{kq}\nabla^{k}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}.

From Lemma
2.7
we know

\displaystyle\nabla^{k}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}=T^{kr}{\psi_{ri}}^{pq}.

Hence the second part of
IV
simplifies to

\displaystyle IV_{2}

\displaystyle:=2T_{jp}T_{kq}\nabla^{k}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}=2T_{jp}T_{kq}T^{kr}{\psi_{ri}}^{pq}=-2T_{jp}{A_{q}}^{r}{\psi_{ri}}^{pq}.

Using (
2.30
) again,

\displaystyle IV_{2}

\displaystyle=-2T_{jp}{A_{q}}^{r}(\varphi_{ari}{\varphi_{b}}^{pq}g^{ab}-{g_{r}}^{p}{g_{i}}^{q}+{g_{r}}^{q}{g_{i}}^{p})

\displaystyle=-2T_{jp}{A_{q}}^{r}\varphi_{ari}\varphi^{apq}+T_{jp}{A_{i}}^{p}+2ST_{ji}.

Thus the fourth term is

\displaystyle IV=2\nabla^{k}T_{jp}T_{kq}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}-2T_{jp}{A_{q}}^{r}{\psi_{ri}}^{pq}.

5th term

Analogously, the fifth term becomes

\displaystyle 2\nabla^{k}(T_{ip}T_{kq}{\varphi_{j}}^{pq})=2\nabla^{k}T_{ip}T_{kq}{\varphi_{j}}^{pq}-2T_{ip}{A_{q}}^{r}{\psi_{rj}}^{pq}.

6th term

From (
2.29
) and (
2.35
) we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla^{k}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq}=T^{kr}\psi_{rkpq}=2T_{pq}.

Therefore, the sixth term is

\displaystyle\nabla^{k}(T_{ip}T_{jq}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq})

\displaystyle=\nabla^{k}T_{ip}T_{jq}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq}+T_{ip}\nabla^{k}T_{jq}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq}+2T_{ip}T_{jq}T_{pq}

\displaystyle=\nabla^{k}T_{ip}T_{jq}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq}+T_{ip}\nabla^{k}T_{jq}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq}-2T_{ip}{A^{p}}_{j}.

Adding these six contributions gives the claimed identity.
∎

Proposition 2.33
.

Let
(M,\varphi,g,\nabla f)
be a gradient
G_{2}
-soliton. Then

\displaystyle 4\Delta T_{ij}=

\displaystyle-{R_{qpj}}^{r}f_{r}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}-\frac{4}{3}T^{kl}\nabla_{q}T_{kl}\cdot{\varphi_{ij}}^{q}-4\nabla_{q}{T_{j}}^{k}T_{kp}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}-4{T_{j}}^{k}\nabla_{q}T_{kp}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}

\displaystyle+{R_{qpi}}^{r}f_{r}{\varphi_{j}}^{pq}+\frac{4}{3}T^{kl}\nabla_{q}T_{kl}\cdot{\varphi_{ji}}^{q}+4\nabla_{q}{T_{i}}^{k}T_{kp}{\varphi_{j}}^{pq}+4{T_{i}}^{k}\nabla_{q}T_{kp}{\varphi_{j}}^{pq}

\displaystyle+R_{jkpq}{T^{kr}}\varphi_{ari}{\varphi}^{apq}-R_{ikpq}{T^{kr}}\varphi_{arj}{\varphi}^{apq}

\displaystyle+4R_{jkir}{T^{kr}}+2R_{jipq}T^{pq}

\displaystyle-2\nabla^{k}T_{jp}T_{kq}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}+2T_{jp}{A_{q}}^{r}{\psi_{ri}}^{pq}

\displaystyle+2\nabla^{k}T_{ip}T_{kq}{\varphi_{j}}^{pq}-2T_{ip}{A_{q}}^{r}{\psi_{rj}}^{pq}

\displaystyle+\nabla^{k}T_{ip}T_{jq}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq}+T_{ip}\nabla^{k}T_{jq}{\varphi_{k}}^{pq}-2T_{ip}{A^{p}}_{j}.

In a more compact schematic form, this can be written as

\displaystyle\triangle T=Rm\ast\nabla f\ast\varphi+Rm\ast T\ast\varphi\ast\varphi+Rm\ast T+\nabla T\ast T\ast\varphi+T\ast T\ast T\ast\psi.

Proof.

Substituting the soliton equation (

)

Ric+\nabla^{2}f=\lambda_{S}\cdot g+A

into the first term of Lemma

\displaystyle R_{jp,q}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}=(-f_{jp,q}+\tfrac{1}{3}S_{q}\cdot g_{jp}+A_{jp,q}){\varphi_{i}}^{pq}.

For the first contribution in this expression, swapping the indices

and therefore

\displaystyle f_{jp,q}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}=\frac{1}{2}(f_{jp,q}-f_{jq,p}){\varphi_{i}}^{pq}.

Using the Ricci commutation formula

we deduce that

\displaystyle f_{jp,q}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}=\frac{1}{2}{R_{qpj}}^{r}f_{r}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}.

For the remaining two contributions, differentiating Lemma

\displaystyle S_{q}=-2T^{kl}\nabla_{q}T_{kl},

\displaystyle A_{jp,q}=(-2{T_{j}}^{k}T_{kp})_{q}=-2\nabla_{q}{T_{j}}^{k}T_{kp}-2{T_{j}}^{k}\nabla_{q}T_{kp}.

Putting these pieces together, the first term can be rewritten as

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}{R_{qpj}}^{r}f_{r}{\varphi_{i}}^{pq}

Carrying out the same computation for

and

∎

2.4.3.
Bounds for the
G_{2}
-structure expressed via the Riemann curvature

Before stating the relation between higher-order derivatives of the
G_{2}
-structure
\varphi
and the curvature, we introduce the notions of domination and equivalence.

Definition 2.35

(Domination and equivalence)
.

We say that a quantity
a
dominates
b
, written
b\lesssim a
, if there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that

b\leq C\cdot a.

We say that
a
is equivalent to
b
, denoted
a\thickapprox b
, if there exists a constant
C\geq 1
with

C^{-1}\cdot a\leq b\leq C\cdot a.

The constants
C
are independent of the particular choice of metric and depend only on the dimension.

The inequalities we establish show that all derivatives of

\varphi,\quad\psi,\quad T,\quad A

are bounded above by a constant multiple of products involving the curvature
Rm
and its derivatives
\nabla^{k}Rm
.

Definition 2.36
.

For any
k\geq 0
, we set a vector

v_{k}:=(n_{0},\cdots,n_{k})

such that
2n_{0}
and other components are non-negative integers, that is

2n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq k.

For each vector
v_{k}
, we associate it with a non-negative integer

\|v_{k}\|:=\sum_{0\leq i\leq k}(i+2)n_{i}=2n_{0}+3n_{1}+\cdots+(k+2)n_{k}.

Definition 2.37
.

We also define a notion of the curvature
Rm
regarding to the vector
v_{k}

\|Rm\|_{v_{k}}:=\prod_{0\leq i\leq k}|\nabla^{i}Rm|^{n_{i}}:=|Rm|^{n_{0}}|\nabla Rm|^{n_{1}}\cdots|\nabla^{k}Rm|^{n_{k}}.

Proposition 2.38
.

Then we obtain that

(2.39)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}\varphi|\lesssim\sum_{\|v_{k}\|=k}\|Rm\|_{v_{k}},

(2.40)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}\psi|\lesssim\sum_{\|v_{k}\|=k}\|Rm\|_{v_{k}},

(2.41)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}T|\lesssim\sum_{\|v_{k}\|=k+1}\|Rm\|_{v_{k}},

(2.42)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}A|\lesssim\sum_{\|v_{k}\|=k+2}\|Rm\|_{v_{k}},

Moreover,
\nabla^{k}S
has the same estimate as
\nabla^{k}A
.

Proof.

The proof is based on (
2.29
) and (
2.31
),

(2.43)

\displaystyle\nabla\varphi=T\ast\psi,\quad\nabla\psi=T\ast\varphi,

together with the expressions for
\nabla T
in (
2.37
) and for
\nabla A
in Lemma
2.7
,

(2.44)

\displaystyle\nabla T=Rm\ast\varphi+T\ast T\ast\varphi,\quad\nabla A=\nabla T\ast T.

For
k=0,1,2
, the required estimates will be established in Lemma
2.39
below.
We now use induction to obtain the higher order estimates. Assume that the desired bounds hold up to order
k
; we will verify them for order
k+1
.
Differentiate (
2.43
) and (
2.44
) a total of
k
times to get

(2.45)

\displaystyle\nabla^{k+1}\varphi=\sum_{p+q=k}\nabla^{p}T\ast\nabla^{q}\psi,\quad\nabla^{k+1}\psi=\sum_{p+q=k}\nabla^{p}T\ast\nabla^{q}\varphi,

\displaystyle\nabla^{k+1}T=\sum_{p+q=k}\nabla^{p}Rm\ast\nabla^{q}\varphi+\sum_{p+q+r=k}\nabla^{p}T\ast\nabla^{q}T\ast\nabla^{r}\varphi,

\displaystyle\nabla^{k+1}A=\sum_{p+q=k+1}\nabla^{p}T\ast\nabla^{q}T.

First, by the induction hypothesis we obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k+1}\varphi|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{p+q=k}|\nabla^{p}T|\,|\nabla^{q}\psi|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{p+q=k}\sum_{\|v_{p}\|=p+1}\prod_{0\leq i\leq p}|\nabla^{i}Rm|^{n_{i}}\sum_{\|v_{q}\|=q}\prod_{0\leq j\leq q}|\nabla^{j}Rm|^{\tilde{n}_{j}}.

We extend
v_{p}
and
v_{q}
to vectors
v^{p}_{k+1}
and
v^{q}_{k+1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
by appending zero entries. We then define a new vector
v_{k+1}
by adding the corresponding components of
v^{p}_{k+1}
and
v^{q}_{k+1}
. A direct computation shows that

\|v_{k+1}\|=p+1+q=k+1.

Therefore, (
2.39
) is valid for
k+1
. An identical reasoning proves (
2.40
) for
k+1
as well.

Next, we estimate

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k+1}T|\lesssim\sum_{p+q=k}|\nabla^{p}Rm|\,|\nabla^{q}\varphi|+\sum_{p+q+r=k}|\nabla^{p}T|\,|\nabla^{q}T|\,|\nabla^{r}\varphi|.

In the first sum, corresponding to
|\nabla^{p}Rm|
, we introduce a vector
v_{p}
whose
(p+1)
-st component is
1
and whose remaining components are all zero. By definition, this gives
\|v_{p}\|=p+2
. Using (
2.39
), we obtain

|\nabla^{q}\varphi|\lesssim\sum_{\|v_{q}\|=q}\|Rm\|_{v_{q}}.

We now view these vectors as elements of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
by padding them with zeros and then add them together to form a new vector
v_{k}
satisfying

\|v_{k}\|=\|v_{q}\|+\|v_{p}\|=q+p+2=k+2.

For the second sum, the vectors
v_{p}
,
v_{q}
, and
v_{r}
again combine to form a vector
v_{k}
in the same manner, and

\|v_{k}\|=\|v_{p}\|+\|v_{q}\|+\|v_{r}\|=(p+1)+(q+1)+r=k+2.

By appending a zero entry to
v_{k}
, we construct a vector
v_{k+1}
, from which (
2.41
) follows.

Finally, for
A
we apply (
2.45
) and obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k+1}A|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{p+q=k+1}|\nabla^{p}T|\,|\nabla^{q}T|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{p+q=k+1}\sum_{|v_{p}|=p+1}\prod_{0\leq i\leq p}|\nabla^{i}Rm|^{n_{i}}\sum_{|v_{q}|=q+1}\prod_{0\leq j\leq q}|\nabla^{j}Rm|^{n_{j}}.

Here
v_{p}
and
v_{q}
combine to form a new vector
v_{k+1}
, as in the preceding argument, and

\|v_{k+1}\|=\|v_{p}\|+\|v_{q}\|=p+q+2=k+3.

This establishes (
2.42
).
∎

Lemma 2.39
.

The estimates in Proposition
2.38
are valid for
k=0,1,2
, namely

\displaystyle|\varphi|\lesssim C,\quad|\psi|\lesssim C,\quad|T|\lesssim|Rm|^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad|A|\lesssim|Rm|,

\displaystyle|\nabla\varphi|\lesssim|Rm|^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad|\nabla\psi|\lesssim|Rm|^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad|\nabla T|\lesssim|Rm|,\quad|\nabla A|\lesssim|Rm|^{\frac{3}{2}},

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}\varphi|\lesssim|Rm|,\quad|\nabla^{2}\psi|\lesssim|Rm|,\quad|\nabla^{2}T|\lesssim|\nabla Rm|+|Rm|^{\frac{3}{2}},

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}A|\lesssim|Rm|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla Rm|+|Rm|^{2}.

Proof.

Using
|T|^{2}=-S
from (
2.38
), we immediately obtain (
2.41
) and (
2.42
) for
k=0
:

\displaystyle|T|=|S|^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim|Rm|^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad|A|\lesssim|T|^{2}=|S|\lesssim|Rm|.

For
\varphi
and
\psi
, (
2.39
) and (
2.40
) for

follow from the fixed norms

\displaystyle|\varphi|=7\times 3!,\qquad|\psi|=7\times 4!.

For

2.43
) gives

\displaystyle|\nabla\varphi|\lesssim|T|\lesssim|Rm|^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad|\nabla\psi|\lesssim|T|\lesssim|Rm|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

From (

\displaystyle|\nabla T|\lesssim|Rm|+|T|^{2}\lesssim|Rm|,\quad|\nabla A|\lesssim|\nabla T|\,|T|\lesssim|Rm|^{\frac{3}{2}},

which are precisely (
2.41
) and (
2.42
) for
k=1
.
For
k=2
, (
2.45
) becomes

(2.46)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\varphi

\displaystyle=\sum_{p+q=1}\nabla^{p}T\ast\nabla^{q}\psi,\quad\nabla^{2}\psi=\sum_{p+q=1}\nabla^{p}T\ast\nabla^{q}\varphi,

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}T

\displaystyle=\sum_{p+q=1}\nabla^{p}Rm\ast\nabla^{q}\varphi+\sum_{p+q+r=1}\nabla^{p}T\ast\nabla^{q}T\ast\nabla^{r}\varphi,

\displaystyle=\sum_{p+q=2}\nabla^{p}T\ast\nabla^{q}T.

∎

Lemma 2.41
.

The scalar curvature of

is bounded, namely

\displaystyle S=-\frac{12t^{2}}{(4b^{2}+t^{2})^{2}}\in\Bigl[-\frac{3}{4b^{2}},0\Bigr],

\displaystyle-\lambda+S\in[\frac{3}{2b^{2}},\frac{9}{4b^{2}}],\quad 7\lambda+2S\in[-\frac{69}{4b^{2}},-\frac{63}{4b^{2}}].

In addition, the lower bound of

Proof.

By

\displaystyle\tau_{1}=-\frac{2f_{2}^{2}}{f_{2}^{2}}\tau_{2}=\frac{4t^{3}}{4b^{2}+t^{2}}.

\displaystyle|\tau|^{2}=\frac{\tau_{1}^{2}}{f_{1}^{4}}+2\frac{\tau_{2}^{2}}{f_{2}^{4}}=\frac{24t^{2}}{(4b^{2}+t^{2})^{2}}.

Consequently, the scalar curvature is

We see that the function

.

Lemma 2.42
.

M_{2}

The complete, gradient steady Ricci solitons that are

3.1.
Pointed Gromov-Hausdorff convergence

We recall the Gromov-Hausdorff convergence theory.

Definition 3.1
.

A map
\Phi:A\to B
between metric spaces
(A,d_{A})
and
(B,d_{B})

is called an
\varepsilon
-isometry
if
\Phi
is

(1)

almost distance-preserving:

|d_{A}(x_{1},x_{2})-d_{B}(\Phi(x_{1}),\Phi(x_{2}))|<\epsilon,\quad\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in A;

(2)

almost surjective:

\text{for all }y\in B,\ \exists\,x\in A\quad\text{such that}\quad d_{B}(\Phi(x),y)<\epsilon.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that a sequence of pointed metric spaces
(M_{i},d_{i},p_{i})
converges to
(M_{\infty},d_{\infty},p_{\infty})

in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense, written

(M_{i},d_{i},p_{i})\xrightarrow{pGH}(M_{\infty},d_{\infty},p_{\infty}),

if for every
R>0
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}

such that for all
i\geq N
, there exists an
\epsilon
-isometry

\Phi_{i}\colon B_{R}(p_{i})\subset M_{i}\to B_{R}(p_{\infty})\subset M_{\infty}

satisfying the basepoint condition

d\bigl(\Phi_{i}(p_{i}),\,p_{\infty}\bigr)<\varepsilon.

Definition 3.3
.

We denote

(M_{i},d_{i},f_{i},p_{i})\xrightarrow{pGH}(M_{\infty},d_{\infty},f_{\infty},p_{\infty})

if, in addition, the following convergence properties for the potential functions hold:

•

(M_{i},d_{i},p_{i})\xrightarrow{pGH}(M_{\infty},d_{\infty},p_{\infty})
,

•

for every point
x=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}x_{i}
with
x_{i}\in M_{i}
we have
f_{\infty}(x)=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}f_{i}(x_{i})
,

•

the limit function
f_{\infty}
is Lipschitz.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\left(M_{i},\varphi_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i}\right)\in\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F)
be a sequence.
Then, after possibly extracting a subsequence, the corresponding complete pointed length spaces converge in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense to a complete pointed length space
(M_{\infty},d_{\infty},f_{\infty},p_{\infty})
, that is,

(M_{i},d_{i},f_{i},p_{i})\xrightarrow{pGH}(M_{\infty},d_{\infty},f_{\infty},p_{\infty}).

The proof will be presented at the end of this section.

3.1.1.

-solitons

In this subsection, we gather the necessary results for use later on.

Definition 3.5
.

For any ball
B_{r}(q)\subset B_{R}(p)
,
we set

\displaystyle K_{R}:=\frac{1}{n-1}\frac{-\lambda+\inf_{B_{R}(p)}S}{3}.

With this notation, from Lemma

\displaystyle Ric_{f}\geq\frac{-\lambda+S}{3}g\geq(n-1)\cdot K_{R}\cdot g\quad\text{ on }B_{r}(q).

Remark 3.6
.

When the scalar curvature has a uniform lower bound, the quantity
K_{R}
becomes independent of

. Under this hypothesis, we note that
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F)
fulfills the assumptions required of the space
\mathcal{N}_{m}(F,K)
as defined in

*Definition 1.3 .

We recall the

\mathrm{sn}_{K_{R}}(t)

\mathrm{sn}_{K_{R}}(t)=\begin{cases}K_{R}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sin\bigl(K_{R}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,t\bigr),\quad t\leq\dfrac{\pi}{2}K_{R}^{-\frac{1}{2}}&\text{if }K_{R}>0,\\[3.00003pt]

This function is non-decreasing on its domain.
Let

denote the mean curvature of a geodesic sphere in the

of constant sectional curvature

m_{K_{R}}(t)=\begin{cases}(n-1)K_{R}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\cot\!\bigl(K_{R}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,t\bigr)&\text{if }K_{R}>0,\\[3.00003pt]

We denote

the radial area distortion in the model space

.
We also write

for the volume of the ball of radius

\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(r)=\omega_{6}\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{sn}_{K_{R}}^{6}(t)\,dt.

The mean curvature comparison was established in

]
*Theorem 1.1 under a lower bound condition on the Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature. We now apply that result in our framework.

Lemma 3.7

(Laplacian comparison)
.

Let

be any ball, and suppose that

satisfies the lower bound given in (
3.1
) and that
|\nabla f(x)|\leq F(d(x,p))
. Then, throughout

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}d=\Delta d-\partial_{r}f\leq m_{K_{R}}+F(R).

In geodesic polar coordinates
(r,\theta)
, the weighted volume element can be expressed in terms of the area distortion function
\mathcal{A}(r,\theta)
and the standard volume element on the unit sphere

as

dv_{f}=\mathcal{A}_{f}(r,\theta)\,dr\wedge dS^{n-1},\quad\mathcal{A}_{f}(r,\theta)=e^{-f}\mathcal{A}(r,\theta).

The volume comparison result is a consequence of the Laplacian comparison established in
[
57
]
*Theorem 1.2.

Lemma 3.8

(Volume comparison)
.

Under the same hypotheses as in Lemma
3.7
, for any
0<s<r
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{A}_{f}(r)}{\mathcal{A}_{f}(s)}\leq e^{-\partial_{r}f\cdot(r-s)}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{K_{R}}(t)}{\mathcal{A}_{K_{R}}(s)}\leq e^{F(R)\cdot(r-s)}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{K_{R}}(t)}{\mathcal{A}_{K_{R}}(s)},

\displaystyle\frac{|B_{r}(q)|_{f}}{|B_{s}(q)|_{f}}\leq\frac{\int_{0}^{r}e^{-\partial_{r}f\cdot t}\mathcal{A}_{K_{R}}(t)dt}{\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\partial_{r}f\cdot t}\mathcal{A}_{K_{R}}(t)dt}\leq e^{F(R)r}\,\frac{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(r)}{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(s)}.

Proof.

Starting from the identities

\partial_{r}\log\mathcal{A}_{f}=\Delta_{f}d,\quad\partial_{r}\log\mathcal{A}_{K_{R}}=m_{K_{R}},

the Laplacian comparison yields

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\log\mathcal{A}_{f}(r)\leq\partial_{r}\log\bigl[e^{F(R)r}\mathcal{A}_{K_{R}}(t)\bigr],

so that the ratio
\frac{\mathcal{A}_{f}}{e^{F(R)r}\mathcal{A}_{K_{R}}}
is not increasing in

[

]
*Lemma 3.2.
∎

Lemma 3.9

(Volume doubling)
.

Under the same assumptions as in Lemma

, we obtain

\displaystyle|B_{2r}(q)|_{f}\leq C\,|B_{r}(q)|_{f}

where

when

and

when

Consequently, every

satisfies the volume doubling property.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of the volume comparison theorem Lemma
3.8
, by substituting
r
and
2r
respectively for the variables

and

appearing there. We then estimate the constant via

\displaystyle\frac{|B_{2r}(q)|_{f}}{|B_{r}(q)|_{f}}\leq e^{2F(R)r}\,\frac{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(2r)}{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(r)}.

When

, the volume ratio
\frac{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{0}(2r)}{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{0}(r)}
in the Euclidean space is

.
In the case

(i.e., on a spherical space), we use the classical Laplacian comparison with Euclidean space to obtain

\frac{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(2r)}{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(r)}\leq 2^{7}.

For the case

(hyperbolic space), we compute

\frac{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(2r)}{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(r)}=\frac{\int_{0}^{2r}\sinh(at)^{6}dt}{\int_{0}^{r}\sinh(at)^{6}dt},\quad a=(-K_{R})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Change variables

to

varies from

\int_{0}^{2r}\sinh(at)^{6}\,dt=2\int_{0}^{r}\sinh(2as)^{6}ds.

Using the double-angle formula

\sinh(2as)^{6}=\bigl(2\sinh(as)\cosh(as)\bigr)^{6}=2^{6}\sinh(as)^{6}\cosh(as)^{6}.

Since

Substituting this estimate back, we find

which yields

\frac{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(2r)}{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(r)}\leq 2^{7}\cosh(ar)^{6}=2^{7}\cosh\bigl(\sqrt{-K_{R}}\,r\bigr)^{6}.

3.1.3.
Non-inflating

Another use of the volume comparison result is to obtain an upper bound for the volume of an arbitrary geodesic ball.

Lemma 3.10
.

Under the hypotheses of Lemma

, the manifold

is non-inflating. That is, for every

|B_{r}(q)|\leq e^{F(R)r}|B_{r}(p)|_{f}\leq e^{2F(R)r}\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(r).

Proof.

By our assumptions, we can apply the volume comparison in Lemma

\displaystyle\frac{|B_{r}(q)|_{f}}{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(r)}\leq e^{F(R)r}\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{|B_{s}(q)|_{f}}{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(s)}.

Moreover, since

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{0}(s)}{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(s)}=1,

it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{|B_{r}(q)|_{f}}{\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(r)}\leq e^{F(R)r-f(q)}.

Recalling the definition of the weighted volume and substituting the above estimate, we obtain

\displaystyle|B_{r}(q)|

\displaystyle\leq e^{\sup_{B_{r}(q)}f}\,|B_{r}(q)|_{f}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\sup_{B_{r}(q)}f+F(R)r-f(q)}\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(r)\leq e^{2F(R)r}\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{K_{R}}(r).

This yields the required upper bound.

for every bounded continuous function

Definition 3.17

3.4.

Definition 3.22
.

A sequence of complete Riemannian manifolds

is said to converge in the pointed

(M_{i},g_{i},p_{i})\xrightarrow{pC^{1,\alpha}}(M_{\infty},g_{\infty},p_{\infty}),

if the following conditions are satisfied:

•

converge to
(M_{\infty},d_{\infty},p_{\infty})
in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense,

(M_{i},d_{i},p_{i})\xrightarrow{pGH}(M_{\infty},d_{\infty},p_{\infty}).

•

For each

there is a sequence of diffeomorphisms onto their images

\sigma_{i,R}:B_{R}(p_{\infty})\cap\mathcal{R}\to M_{i}

such that, for every compact set

,

\displaystyle\sigma_{i,R}^{\ast}(g_{i})\xrightarrow{C^{1,\alpha}}g_{\infty}\quad\text{on }K.

Moreover, we write

(M_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i})\xrightarrow{pC^{1,\alpha}}(M_{\infty},g_{\infty},f_{\infty},p_{\infty})

if, in addition,

•

f_{i}\xrightarrow{C^{\alpha}}f_{\infty}\text{ on }K
.

.

Restricted to a compact set

is actually an
\epsilon_{i}
-isometry from

for some

. In particular, convergence in the pointed

Remark 3.24
.

Convergence in the pointed

sense is a variant of what is often called Cheeger-Gromov convergence (which is typically

Definition 3.31

Remark 3.49
.

For a Ricci shrinker satisfying

(3.20)

Ric+\nabla^{2}f=\frac{g}{2},

we obtain, by taking the trace,

(3.21)

S+\triangle f=\frac{n}{2}.

In addition, applying the second contracted Bianchi identity yields

Combining these two relations gives

\triangle f-|\nabla f|^{2}+f=\text{const}.

Starting from

and

]

In Li-Li-Wang
[

]
*Page 28, they apply the bootstrapping argument
for the conformal change of metric

\tilde{g}=e^{-\frac{2f}{m-2}}g,

where

denotes the dimension of the Ricci shrinker. This conformal transformation removes the

Then they perform the rescaling

\bar{g}=\rho^{-2}\tilde{g}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{f}=f-f(q),

where

at the point

3.5.6.
Smooth convergence on regular part

Definition 3.50
.

A sequence of complete
G_{2}
-structures

(M_{i},\varphi_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i})

is said to converge in the pointed
C^{\infty}
topology, denoted

(M_{i},\varphi_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i})\xrightarrow{pC^{\infty}}(M_{\infty},\varphi_{\infty},g_{\infty},f_{\infty},p_{\infty}),

if the following hold:

•

The associated pointed length spaces satisfy

(M_{i},d_{i},p_{i})\xrightarrow{pGH}(M_{\infty},d_{\infty},p_{\infty}).

•

For every
R>0
there exists a sequence of diffeomorphisms onto their images

\sigma_{i,R}:B_{R}(p_{\infty})\cap\mathcal{R}\to M_{i}

such that, for each compact subset
K\subset B_{R}(p_{\infty})\cap\mathcal{R}
,

–

The Riemannian metrics converge smoothly

\displaystyle\sigma_{i,R}^{\ast}(g_{i})\xrightarrow{C^{\infty}}g_{\infty}\quad\text{on }K.

–

The
G_{2}
-structures converge smoothly

\displaystyle\sigma_{i,R}^{\ast}(\varphi_{i})\xrightarrow{C^{\infty}}\varphi_{\infty}\quad\text{on }K.

•

The potential functions converge smoothly

f_{i}\xrightarrow{C^{\infty}}f_{\infty}\text{ on }K.

Question 3.51
.

Within the regular part of the regular-singular decomposition described in Theorem
3.26
, is it possible to strengthen the convergence from

to smooth convergence?

Theorem 3.52
.

Let

(M_{i},\varphi_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i})

be a sequence of complete

-solitons belonging to the class
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F,\underline{\nu})

-soliton

By pointed Gromov-Hausdorff convergence, as in Definition

, we obtain a family of

-isometries

. To promote this to

Motivated by recent progress on Ricci solitons

4.
Epsilon regularity under small entropy

Theorem 4.1

(Epsilon regularity)
.

Let
(M^{7},\varphi,g,f,p)
be a complete gradient
G_{2}
-soliton in
\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F,\underline{\nu})
defined on the unit ball
B_{1}:=B(p,1)
. Assume moreover that the Riemann curvature at the basepoint
p
is nonzero.

Suppose that the local
\nu
-functional of
g
admits a lower bound that is strictly greater than
-\epsilon
, where
\epsilon
is the constant determined by the Gap Theorem
4.4
, i.e.

\displaystyle\nu(B_{1},g,\tau=1)\geq-\epsilon.

Then for every integer
k\geq 0
, there exists a constant
C=C(k,n)
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{B(p,1)}|\nabla^{k}Rm|\,d^{2+k}(\cdot,\partial B_{1})\leq C.

We proceed by adopting the same line of reasoning as in
[
53
]
*Theorem 3.5, Theorem 3.6.

4.1.
Local rescaling procedure

We now describe the
local rescaling procedure
, which is used to control local curvature of Riemannian metric.

For each point
x\in M
, we define

Q_{x}:=|Rm(g)|_{g}(x)

to be the norm of the Riemann curvature tensor of
g
at
x
. The distance function associated with
g
will be denoted by
d
. We fix a ball

B:=B_{g}(p,R)

of radius
R
centered at
p
with respect to the metric
g
.

The local quantity of interest is the curvature scale-weighted norm

(4.1)

\displaystyle F_{x}^{2}:=Q_{x}\cdot d^{2}(x,\partial B),\quad\forall\;x\in B.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
Q_{p}\neq 0
and let
R>0
.
Then there exists a point
q\in M
and a positive number

(4.2)

\displaystyle r<F_{p}\leq F_{q}

such that, for the rescaled metric

(4.3)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}=Q_{q}\cdot g,

one has

\displaystyle|\widetilde{Rm}(\tilde{g})|(q)=1\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{B_{\tilde{g}}(q,r)}|\widetilde{Rm}(\tilde{g})|\leq\frac{1}{\bigl(1-rQ^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{p}R^{-1}\bigr)^{2}}.

Proof.

The proof is divided into several steps.

Step 1: choose a maximum point
p
.

Since the Riemann curvature is bounded on
B
, the function
F_{x}
must vanish along the boundary of
B
.
On the other hand,
F_{p}>0
at the basepoint
p
.
Hence the maximum of
F^{2}_{x}
is achieved at an interior point of
B
,
denote by
q
:

(4.4)

\displaystyle F_{q}^{2}=Q_{q}d^{2}(q,\partial B)=\sup_{x\in B}F^{2}_{x}\geq F_{p}^{2}=Q_{p}R^{2}>0.

here, we take
x=p
in (
4.1
) and substitute in the last identity. Note that a priori
q
need not coincide with
p
. In particular,

Q_{q}\geq Q_{p}.

We will now use the weighted curvature quantity in (
4.4
) to derive a bound for the Riemann curvature near this maximum point
q
.

Step 2: distance estimate near
q
.
In this step we control the weight
d(\cdot,\partial B)
in a neighbourhood of
q
.
From the first identity in (
4.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle d(q,\partial B)=Q_{q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}F_{q}.

Choose a positive constant
r<F_{p}\leq F_{q}
such that the ball
B_{g}(q,Q_{q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}r)
with center
q
is contained in
B
.

Then for any
x\in B_{g}(q,Q_{q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}r),
the triangle inequality yields

(4.5)

\displaystyle d(x,\partial B)\geq d(q,\partial B)-d(x,q)\geq Q_{q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(F_{q}-r).

Step 3: curvature estimate near
q
.
We now control the curvature norm.
From the second relation in (
4.4
) we have

\displaystyle Q_{x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{F_{q}^{2}}{d^{2}(x,\partial B)},\quad\forall x\in B_{g}(q,Q_{q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}r).

Substituting the distance lower bound (
4.5
) into this inequality, we get

\displaystyle Q_{x}\leq\frac{F^{2}_{q}}{Q_{q}^{-1}(F_{q}-r)^{2}}=\frac{Q_{q}}{(1-rF_{q}^{-1})^{2}}.

Using (
4.4
), i.e.

F_{q}\geq F_{p}=Q^{\frac{1}{2}}_{p}R,

we then obtain

(4.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{g}(q,Q_{q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}r)}Q_{x}\leq\frac{Q_{q}}{(1-rQ^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{p}R^{-1})^{2}}.

Step 4: rescaling the metric at
q
.

Finally, we rescale the metric to obtain a uniform curvature bound.
With this rescaling
\tilde{g}=Q_{q}\cdot g
from (
4.3
), the ball
B_{g}(q,Q_{q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}r)
is transformed into
B_{\tilde{g}}(q,r)
, and the Riemann curvature tensor along with its norm become

\widetilde{Rm}=Q_{q}\cdot Rm,\quad|\widetilde{Rm}|=Q_{q}^{-1}\cdot|Rm|.

In particular, at the point
q
the curvature norm is normalised to

\displaystyle|\widetilde{Rm}|(q)=1.

Moreover, the local curvature estimate (
4.6
) transforms into

(4.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{\tilde{g}}(q,r)}|\widetilde{Rm}|\leq\frac{1}{(1-rQ^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{p}R^{-1})^{2}},

which is precisely the bound we need.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

In the context of Lemma
4.2
, there exists a point
q\in M
and a constant
r>0
such that, for the rescaled metric

\tilde{g}=Q_{q}\cdot g,

we have

\displaystyle|\widetilde{Rm}(\tilde{g})|(q)=1\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{B_{\tilde{g}}(q,r)}|\widetilde{Rm}(\tilde{g})|\leq 4.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
4.2
with

\displaystyle R=2Q_{p}^{-\frac{1}{2}}r.

Inserting this choice of
R
in the last equality in (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle F_{p}^{2}=Q_{p}R^{2}=4r^{2}>r^{2},

namely

That verifies (
4.2
).
Furthermore, we compute that

rQ^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{p}R^{-1}<\frac{1}{2}F_{p}Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{p}R^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}.

Thus, the corollary is proved.
∎

4.2.

Theorem 4.4

(Gap theorem)
.

There exists a small dimensional constant
\epsilon:=\epsilon(n)
such that any complete
G_{2}
-soliton with zero scalar curvature and bounded soliton potential
|\partial f|
satisfies

\displaystyle\nu(M,g)<-\epsilon.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Assume that for some sequence
\epsilon_{i}\to 0,
there is a sequence of complete, non-flat
G_{2}
-solitons with vanishing scalar curvature and bounded soliton potential
f
, denoted
(M_{i},g_{i})
, such that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\nu(M_{i},g_{i})\geq-\epsilon_{i}.

Step 1: construction of a rescaled sequence.

Apply the local rescaling procedure (Corollary
4.3
) to each complete non-flat
G_{2}
-soliton
(M_{i},g_{i})
to obtain a sequence of rescaled metrics
\tilde{g}_{i}
, points
q_{i}\in M_{i}
, and radii

such that the Riemann curvature tensor
Rm_{i}
of
\tilde{g}_{i}
satisfies

(4.9)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{Rm_{i}}|(q_{i})=1,\quad\sup_{B_{\tilde{g}_{i}}(q_{i},r_{i})}|\widetilde{Rm_{i}}|\leq 4.

Step 2: injectivity radius.

By the monotonicity and scale invariance of
\nu
in Lemma
3.19
, we obtain

\displaystyle\nu(B_{\tilde{g}_{i}}(q_{i},r_{i}))\geq\nu(M_{i},\tilde{g}_{i})=\nu(M_{i},g_{i})\geq-\epsilon_{i}.

Combining (
4.8
) with Lemma

, we get a uniform lower bound on the volume of geodesic balls. Then, a theorem of Cheeger-Gromov-Taylor
[

]
*Page 42 yields a positive lower bound for the injectivity radius in the smaller ball

which is a dimensional constant

.

Step 3: regularity improvement.

Using the derivative estimates from Proposition
2.28
, Proposition
2.38
, Proposition
3.35
, the

-solitons enjoy uniform bounds on all higher-order derivatives:

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}\tilde{g}_{i}|\leq C(k),\quad\forall\;k\geq 0.

Hence, we obtain

\tilde{g}_{i}
to a smooth limit

-soliton:

\displaystyle(M_{i},\tilde{g}_{i},q_{i})\longrightarrow(M_{\infty},\tilde{g}_{\infty},q_{\infty}).

Step 4: contradiction.

Passing to the limit in (

), we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{Rm_{\infty}}|(q_{\infty})=1.

The
\nu
-functional is also continuous under this convergence,
(F)
in Lemma
3.19
, and (
4.8
) implies

\displaystyle\nu(M_{\infty},\tilde{g}_{\infty},1)\geq 0.

However, the rigidity statement
(D)
in Lemma
3.19
shows that
(M_{\infty},\tilde{g}_{\infty})
must be isometric to the Euclidean space. Consequently,

\displaystyle|\widetilde{Rm_{\infty}}|(q_{\infty})=0,

which contradicts (
4.10
). This completes the proof.
∎

4.3.
Proof of Theorem
4.1

Proof.

We begin by establishing the case
k=0
using a contradiction argument.
Suppose there exists a sequence
(M_{i},g_{i},p_{i}),
and let
B_{i}:=B(p_{i},1)
be the unit ball, such that

\displaystyle\nu(B_{i},g_{i},\tau=1)\geq-\epsilon,\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{B_{i}}|Rm_{i}|(\cdot)\,d_{g_{i}}^{2}(\cdot,\partial B_{i})\rightarrow\infty.

Here
Rm_{i}
denotes the Riemann curvature tensor of the metric
g_{i}
.

Following the notation introduced in Section
4.1
, we define

\displaystyle Q_{i,x}:=|Rm_{i}|(x),\quad F^{2}_{i,x}:=|Rm_{i}|(x)\,d_{g_{i}}^{2}(x,\partial B_{i}),\quad\forall\;x\in B_{i}.

Let
q_{i}
be a point in
B(p_{i},1)
at which the function
F^{2}_{i,x}
attains its maximum.
Then, by our contradictory assumption, we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle Q_{i,q_{i}}\geq F^{2}_{i,q_{i}}\rightarrow\infty.

Step 1: construction of the rescaling sequence.

Define the rescaled metric by

\tilde{g}_{i}=Q_{i,q_{i}}\cdot g_{i}.

Since
|Rm_{i}|(p_{i})\neq 0,
we can apply Lemma
4.2
with basepoint
p_{i}
and obtain

(4.12)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{Rm}(\tilde{g}_{i})|(q_{i})=1\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{B_{\tilde{g}_{i}}(q_{i},r_{i})}|\widetilde{Rm}(\tilde{g}_{i})|\leq\frac{1}{\bigl(1-r_{i}Q_{i,p_{i}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)^{2}}.

The radius
r_{i}
is chosen as in Corollary
4.3
to satisfy the requirement in Lemma
4.2
,

\displaystyle r_{i}:=\frac{F_{i,q_{i}}}{2}<F_{i,q_{i}}.

This choice ensures, in view of (
4.11
), that the ball

B_{g_{i}}\bigl(q_{i},Q_{i,q_{i}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}r_{i}\bigr)

is contained in
B_{i}
and the rescaled Riemann curvature has a uniform bound:

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{\tilde{g}_{i}}(q_{i},r_{i})}|\widetilde{Rm}(\tilde{g}_{i})|\leq 4.

Step 2: injectivity radius.

We now use the entropy bound to obtain a uniform lower bound on the injectivity radius of
\tilde{g}_{i}
. The entropy is preserved under rescaling and is monotone (non-decreasing) on the domain; see
(C)
and
(B)
in Lemma
3.19
. Hence

(4.13)

\begin{split}\nu\bigl(B_{\tilde{g}_{i}}(q_{i},r_{i}),\tilde{g}_{i},\tau=1\bigr)&=\nu\bigl(B_{g_{i}}(q_{i},Q_{i,q_{i}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}r_{i}),g_{i},\tau=1\bigr)\\
&\geq\nu(B_{i},g_{i},\tau=1)\\
&\geq-\bar{\epsilon}.\end{split}

From here on, the argument is identical to
Step 2
in the proof of Theorem
4.4
, which yields

\displaystyle\inf_{B_{\tilde{g}_{i}}(q_{i},r_{i}-1)}\operatorname{inj}_{\tilde{g}_{i}}>c(n).

Step 3: contradiction.

Set

M_{i}=B_{\tilde{g}_{i}}(q_{i},r_{i}-1).

By the derivative estimates Proposition
2.28
, Proposition
2.38
, Proposition
3.35
, on these domains we obtain uniform higher-order bounds for the metrics:

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}\tilde{g}_{i}|\leq C(k),\quad\forall k\geq 0.

Consequently, by the Cheeger-Gromov theorem we have
C^{\infty}
convergence of
\tilde{g}_{i}
to a smooth
G_{2}
-manifold,

\displaystyle(M_{i},\tilde{g}_{i},q_{i})\longrightarrow(M_{\infty},\tilde{g}_{\infty},q_{\infty}).

The scalar curvature for
\tilde{g}_{i}
takes the form

S(\tilde{g}_{i})=Q_{i,q_{i}}^{-1}\cdot S(g_{i}).

Thus the limit metric
\tilde{g}_{\infty}
has zero scalar curvature.
On the other hand, (
4.13
) implies

\displaystyle\nu(M_{\infty},\tilde{g}_{\infty},\tau=1)\geq-\epsilon.

By (
4.12
),
\tilde{g}_{\infty}
is non-flat

\displaystyle|\widetilde{Rm}(\tilde{g}_{\infty})|(q_{\infty})=1,

contradicting the Gap Theorem
4.4
. Thus, Theorem
4.1
is proved.
∎

4.4.
Compactness of compact solitons

We present an application of the epsilon regularity theorem of compact
G_{2}
-solitons.
Let
\lambda_{M}\leq 1
be a non-negative constant such that

\displaystyle 7\log\lambda_{M}\geq-\epsilon.

Here
\epsilon
is the constant that appears in the Gap Theorem
4.4
.
Let
\mathcal{M}(\epsilon,r_{0},\sigma,D)
denote the moduli space of
G_{2}
-solitons satisfying the following properties:

•

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in M}\mathbf{I}(B(x,r_{0}),g)\geq\lambda_{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}(\mathbb{R}^{7})
,

•

\displaystyle-S,|\nabla f|\leq\sigma
,

•

\displaystyle\operatorname{diam}(M,g)\leq D
.

Theorem 4.5
.

The space
\mathcal{M}(\epsilon,r_{0},\sigma,D)
is compact in the pointed
C^{\infty}
topology.

Proof.

Using the relation between the
\nu
-functional and the isoperimetric constant from Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\nu(B(x,r_{0}^{2}),g,r_{0}^{2})\geq 7\log\lambda_{M}-r_{0}^{2}\sigma,\quad\forall\,x\in M.

For any smaller radius
r\leq r_{0},
the monotonicity property
(B)
of
\nu
in Lemma
3.19
yields

\displaystyle\nu(B(x,r^{2}),g,r^{2})\geq\nu(B(x,r_{0}^{2}),g,r_{0}^{2})\geq 7\log\lambda_{M}-r^{2}\sigma.

We now choose
r
sufficiently small so that

\displaystyle\nu(B(x,r^{2}),g,r^{2})\geq-\bar{\epsilon}\geq-\epsilon.

Hence, by the
\epsilon
-regularity result Theorem
4.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{B(x,r^{2})}|\nabla^{k}Rm|\,d^{2+k}(\cdot,\partial B(x,r^{2}))\leq C,

which in particular implies

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}Rm|\leq Cr^{-4-2k},\quad\text{on }B\bigl(x,\tfrac{r^{2}}{2}\bigr).

By selecting finitely many such balls
B\bigl(x,\tfrac{r^{2}}{2}\bigr)
to cover the compact manifold
M
and using the diameter bound, we obtain a uniform positive lower bound for the injectivity radius.
Finally, Hamilton’s compactness theorem then implies the desired compactness.
∎

5.
Smooth convergence on uniform
L^{\frac{7}{2}}
curvature bounds

In this section, we prove the following theorem, following Anderson’s argument
[
2
]

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\left(M_{i},\varphi_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i}\right)
be a sequence in

\mathcal{M}(\Lambda,F,\underline{\nu})

with a uniform
L^{\frac{7}{2}}
bound on the Riemann curvature:

(5.1)

\int_{B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},R)}\left|Rm_{g_{i}}\right|_{g_{i}}^{\frac{7}{2}}\leq E(R)<\infty.

Then a subsequence of
\left(M_{i},\varphi_{i},g_{i},f_{i},p_{i}\right)
converges, in the pointed
C^{\infty}
sense, to a
G_{2}
-soliton without any singularities.

5.1.
Epsilon regularity under an
L^{\frac{7}{2}}
curvature bound

We set

\displaystyle u:=|Rm|.

Recall that we are assuming the potential function
f
fulfills the gradient bound
(b)
given in Definition
1.3
, that is,

(5.2)

\displaystyle|\nabla f|^{2}\leq F(R)\text{ in }B_{R}.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(M,\varphi,g,f,p)
be a gradient
G_{2}
-soliton obeying (
5.2
). Suppose that

(5.3)

\|u\|_{\frac{7}{2};B_{r},f}^{\frac{7}{2}}=\int_{B_{r}}|Rm|^{\frac{7}{2}}\,dv_{f}\leq\epsilon

for some sufficiently small
\epsilon
, where
dv_{f}=e^{-f}dv
and
r<1
. Then

(5.4)

\|u\|_{\infty,B_{r/2},f}\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)}{r^{2}}\,\delta,\quad\delta=\epsilon^{\frac{2}{7}}

where
C_{s}
denotes the Sobolev constant.

We use Lemma
2.30
together with Proposition
2.38
to derive the following differential inequality.

Lemma 5.3

(Differential inequality for
u
)
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
5.2
, we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle u\Delta_{f}u\geq-Cu^{2}(u+F)-\frac{1}{4}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}.

Proof.

From the curvature equation (
2.25
) in Lemma
2.30
, together with Lemma
2.39
, it follows that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\left|\Delta Rm\right|\leq C\left(u+F\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\nabla Rm\right|+Cu^{2}.

Using the identity

\displaystyle\langle\triangle Rm,Rm\rangle+|\nabla Rm|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\triangle|Rm|^{2}=|Rm|\triangle|Rm|+|\nabla|Rm||^{2},

we deduce

\displaystyle u\triangle u

\displaystyle=\langle\triangle Rm,Rm\rangle+|\nabla Rm|^{2}-|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-|\triangle Rm|u+|\nabla Rm|^{2}-|\nabla u|^{2}.

Substituting (
5.6
) into this inequality gives

(5.7)

\displaystyle u\triangle u\geq-Cu(u+F)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\nabla Rm\right|-Cu^{3}+|\nabla Rm|^{2}-|\nabla u|^{2}.

By the elementary inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle-Cu(u+F)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\nabla Rm\right|\geq-Cu^{2}(u+F)-\frac{1}{8}|\nabla Rm|^{2}.

Inserting this estimate into (
5.7
),
and adding

\displaystyle-u\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\right\rangle\geq-2u^{2}|\nabla f|^{2}-\frac{1}{8}|\nabla u|^{2}\geq-2u^{2}F-\frac{1}{8}|\nabla u|^{2},

we arrive at

\displaystyle u\triangle_{f}u=u\triangle u-u\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-Cu^{2}(u+F)-\frac{1}{8}|\nabla Rm|^{2}-2u^{2}F-\frac{1}{8}|\nabla u|^{2}-Cu^{3}+|\nabla Rm|^{2}-|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-Cu^{2}(u+F)+\frac{7}{8}|\nabla Rm|^{2}-\frac{9}{8}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Applying the Kato inequality
|\nabla|Rm||\leq|\nabla Rm|
to the middle term, we get

\displaystyle u\triangle_{f}u\geq-Cu^{2}(u+F)-\frac{1}{4}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2},

which proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.4

(Integral inequality)
.

Let
p\geq\frac{7}{2}
. Under the assumptions of Proposition
5.2
, and for a cut-off function
\eta
supported in a domain
\Omega
(to be specified later), the following estimate holds:

\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla(\eta u^{\frac{p}{2}})\right|^{2}e^{-f}\leq\frac{Cp}{2}\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}u^{p+1}e^{-f}+\frac{CFp}{2}\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}u^{p}e^{-f}+\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{2}e^{-f}.

Proof.

Multiplying (
5.5
) by
\eta^{2}u^{p-2}
gives

(5.8)

\displaystyle\eta^{2}u^{p-1}(-\Delta_{f}u)

\displaystyle\leq\eta^{2}u^{p-2}\left(Cu^{2}(u+F)+\frac{1}{4}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\eta^{2}u^{p+1}+CF\eta^{2}u^{p}+\frac{1}{4}\eta^{2}u^{p-2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}.

Integrating (
5.8
), we deduce

(5.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla(\eta^{2}u^{p-1})\right\rangle e^{-f}=\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}u^{p-1}(-\Delta_{f}u)e^{-f}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}u^{p+1}e^{-f}+CF\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}u^{p}e^{-f}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}u^{p-2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}e^{-f}.

A direct computation yields

(5.10)

\displaystyle\left|\nabla(\eta u^{\frac{p}{2}})\right|^{2}=\frac{p^{2}}{4}\eta^{2}u^{p-2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+p\eta u^{p-1}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla\eta\right\rangle+u^{p}\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{2}.

Furthermore,

(5.11)

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla(\eta^{2}u^{p-1})\right\rangle=(p-1)\eta^{2}u^{p-2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+2\eta u^{p-1}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla\eta\right\rangle.

Combining (
5.10
) with (
5.11
), we obtain

(5.12)

\displaystyle\left|\nabla(\eta u^{\frac{p}{2}})\right|^{2}-\frac{p}{2}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla(\eta^{2}u^{p-1})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p^{2}}{4}-\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\right)\eta^{2}u^{p-2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+u^{p}\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{2}-\frac{p^{2}}{4}\right)\eta^{2}u^{p-2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+u^{p}\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{2}.

Integrating (
5.12
) and inserting (
5.9
), we arrive at

(5.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla(\eta u^{\frac{p}{2}})\right|^{2}e^{-f}=\frac{p}{2}\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla u,\nabla(\eta^{2}u^{p-1})\rangle e^{-f}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{p}{2}-\frac{p^{2}}{4}\right)\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}u^{p-2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}e^{-f}+\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{2}e^{-f}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cp}{2}\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}u^{p+1}e^{-f}+\frac{CFp}{2}\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}u^{p}e^{-f}+\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{2}e^{-f}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{p}{2}-\frac{p^{2}}{4}+\frac{p}{8}\right)\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}u^{p-2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}e^{-f}.

Since
p\geq\frac{7}{2}
, it follows that

\frac{p}{2}-\frac{p^{2}}{4}+\frac{p}{8}<\frac{p}{2}\left(\frac{5}{4}-\frac{p}{2}\right)<0.

Hence the coefficient of the last integral in (
5.13
) is nonpositive, and this term can be discarded. Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla(\eta u^{\frac{p}{2}})\right|^{2}e^{-f}\leq\frac{Cp}{2}\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}u^{p+1}e^{-f}+\frac{CFp}{2}\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}u^{p}e^{-f}+\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{2}e^{-f},

which concludes the proof of Lemma
5.4
.
∎

5.2.
Moser iteration: proof of Proposition
5.2

For each

, we introduce a decreasing sequence of radii and an increasing sequence of exponents given by

r_{1}=r,\quad r_{k}:=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)r,\quad p_{1}=\frac{7}{2},\quad p_{k}=\left(\frac{7}{5}\right)^{k-1}p_{1}.

Obviously
r_{k}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Next, we choose a cut-off function
\eta_{k}
satisfying
\eta_{k}=1
on
B_{r_{k+1}}
,
\eta_{k}=0
outside
B_{r_{k}}
, and

(5.14)

\left|\nabla\eta_{k}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{(r_{k}-r_{k+1})^{2}}=\frac{C2^{2k}}{r^{2}}.

For later use, set

(5.15)

I_{k}=\left[\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\left(\eta_{k}u^{\frac{p_{k}}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}e^{-f}\right]^{\frac{n-2}{n}}.

Since
n=7

\frac{n}{n-2}=\frac{7}{5}
.

Applying the Sobolev inequality in Lemma
3.15
to

and then using Lemma
5.4
, we obtain

(5.16)

\displaystyle I_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{s}r_{k}^{2}}{V_{f}^{2/n}}\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\left|\nabla(\eta_{k}u^{\frac{p_{k}}{2}})\right|^{2}e^{-f}+\frac{C_{s}}{V_{f}^{2/n}}\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{s}r_{k}^{2}}{V_{f}^{2/n}}\times\bigg(Cp_{k}\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}+1}e^{-f}

\displaystyle+Cp_{k}F\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}+\int_{B_{r_{k}}}u^{p_{k}}\left|\nabla\eta_{k}\right|^{2}e^{-f}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{C_{s}}{V_{f}^{2/n}}\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}.

Step 1: Estimation of
I_{1}
.

Taking
k=1
in the inequality (
5.16
) and using the small energy assumption (
5.3
), we get

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\left[\int_{B_{r}}\left(\eta_{1}u^{\frac{p_{1}}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}e^{-f}\right]^{\frac{n-2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{s}r^{2}}{V_{f}^{2/n}}\left(C\int_{B_{r}}\eta_{1}^{2}u^{p_{1}+1}e^{-f}+CF\int_{B_{r}}\eta_{1}^{2}u^{p_{1}}e^{-f}+\int_{B_{r}}u^{p_{1}}Cr^{-2}e^{-f}\right)+\frac{C_{s}}{V_{f}^{2/n}}\epsilon

\displaystyle\frac{CC_{s}r^{2}}{V_{f}^{2/n}}\int_{B_{r}}\eta_{1}^{2}u^{p_{1}+1}e^{-f}+\frac{CC_{s}r^{2}}{V_{f}^{2/n}}\left(F+r^{-2}\right)\epsilon.

Step 2: Non-collapsing.

To further control the leading term on the right-hand side, we use the non-collapsing estimate from Lemma

V(r)\geq\kappa_{1}r^{n},

where the constant

Plugging these estimates and

(5.19)

I_{1}\leq C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)r^{-2}\epsilon.

Step 3: Estimation of
I_{k}
.
We now return to obtain an upper bound for
I_{k}
in (
5.16
) by estimating the first term and the remaining terms separately. Apart from the first term, the other terms in (

(5.20)

\displaystyle\frac{C_{s}r_{k}^{2}}{V_{f}^{2/n}}\left(Cp_{k}F\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}+\int_{B_{r_{k}}}u^{p_{k}}\left|\nabla\eta_{k}\right|^{2}e^{-f}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{C_{s}}{V_{f}^{2/n}}\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CC_{s}r^{2}}{V_{f}^{2/n}}\left(p_{k}F+\frac{2^{2k}}{r^{2}}\right)\int_{B_{r_{k}}}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}.

We bound the first term on the right-hand side of (

) by applying Hölder’s inequality.
Set

(5.21)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}+1}e^{-f}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}e^{-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}f}\cdot ue^{-\frac{1}{q}f}

By the relation

\left(\int_{B_{r_{k}}}u^{q}e^{-f}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\left(\int_{B_{r_{2}}}u^{q}e^{-f}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq I_{1}^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\leq\left[C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)r^{-2}\epsilon\right]^{\frac{2}{n}}.

We further decompose

Applying Hölder’s inequality to the first factor in (

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\left(\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}\right)^{q^{\prime}}e^{-f}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\left(\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{n(n-2)}{n^{2}-2n+4}}e^{\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{n^{2}-2n+4}(-f)}\left(\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{n^{2}-2n+4}}e^{\frac{2n}{n^{2}-2n+4}(-f)}

Hence

\displaystyle\left[\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\left(\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}\right)^{q^{\prime}}e^{-f}\right]^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\left(\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-2}}e^{-f}\right]^{\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{n^{2}}}\left(\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}

\displaystyle=\left(I_{k}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\left(\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}.

By inserting these estimates into (
5.21
), choosing a fixed constant
A
, and applying Young’s inequality
x^{a}y^{1-a}\leq ax+(1-a)y\leq x+y
, we obtain the following upper bound for the first term in (
5.16
):

(5.23)

\displaystyle p_{k}\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}+1}e^{-f}

\displaystyle\leq p_{k}\left(I_{k}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\left(\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\left[C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)r^{-2}\epsilon\right]^{\frac{2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{A}I_{k}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\left[(Ap_{k})^{\frac{n}{2}}C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)r^{-2}\epsilon\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}\right]^{\frac{2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{A}I_{k}+(Ap_{k})^{\frac{n}{2}}C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)r^{-2}\epsilon\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}.

Substituting (
5.23
) together with (
5.20
) into (
5.16
), we arrive at

\displaystyle I_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CC_{s}r^{2}}{V_{f}^{2/n}}\left[\frac{1}{A}I_{k}+(Ap_{k})^{n/2}C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)r^{-2}\epsilon\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\eta_{k}^{2}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{CC_{s}r^{2}}{V_{f}^{2/n}}\left[\left(p_{k}F+\frac{2^{2k}}{r^{2}}\right)\int_{B_{r_{k}}}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}\right].

Step 4: Iterated inequality.

Similarly to (
5.18
), we also choose
A(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)=2CC_{s}\kappa_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n}}
so that

\frac{CC_{s}r^{2}}{V_{f}^{2/n}A}\leq CC_{s}\kappa_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n}}A^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}.

A simple rearrangement then yields

I_{k}\leq C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)\left[p_{k}+\left(p_{k}^{\frac{n}{2}}+2^{2k}\right)r^{-2}\right]\int_{B_{r_{k}}}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}.

We know

p_{k}^{\frac{n}{2}}=[\frac{7}{2}(\frac{7}{5})^{(k-1)}]^{\frac{7}{2}}\leq C2^{2k},\quad C=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{7}{2}\right)^{7/2}.

So we can simplify this inequality as

I_{k}\leq C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)\frac{2^{2k}}{r^{2}}\int_{B_{r_{k}}}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}.

Consequently, for any integer
k\geq 2
, we obtain the following iterated inequality

(5.24)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{r_{k+1}}}u^{p_{k+1}}e^{-f}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{k+1}}}\leq\left[\int_{B_{r_{k}}}\left(\eta_{k}u^{\frac{p_{k}}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}e^{-f}\right]^{\frac{n-2}{np_{k}}}

\displaystyle=I_{k}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\leq\left[C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)\frac{2^{2k}}{r^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\left[\int_{B_{r_{k}}}u^{p_{k}}e^{-f}\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}.

Step 5: Iteration.

On the closed ball
B_{r}
, both the volume
V_{f}
and the function
f
are bounded from above. Therefore, by the usual Moser iteration argument, we obtain

(5.25)

\left\|u\right\|_{\infty,B_{r/2},f}\leq\prod_{k=2}^{\infty}\left[C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)\frac{2^{2k}}{r^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\left[\int_{B_{r_{2}}}u^{p_{2}}e^{-f}\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{2}}}

Substituting (
5.22
) into (
5.25
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{\infty,B_{r/2},f}\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{k=2}^{\infty}\left[C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)\frac{2^{2k}}{r^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\left[C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)r^{-2}\epsilon\right]^{\frac{2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)\frac{2^{2k}}{r^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\epsilon^{\frac{2}{n}}.

Recall that
p_{1}=7/2
and, for
k\geq 1
, the sequence is given by
p_{k}=p_{1}\left(\frac{7}{5}\right)^{k-1}
.
Using the identity
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p_{k}}=1
and the geometric series formula, we obtain

\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{\frac{2k}{p_{k}}}\leq C,\qquad\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}r^{\frac{-2}{p_{k}}}=r^{-2}.

Consequently, we deduce

(5.26)

\left\|u\right\|_{\infty,B_{r/2},f}\leq C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)r^{-2}\epsilon^{\frac{2}{7}}.

This completes the proof of (
5.4
).

5.3.
Shi-type estimates

Shi-type estimates for the Laplacian flow were obtained in
[
42
]
, and these in turn imply the Shi-type estimates for
G_{2}
-solitons stated in Proposition
2.28
. For our subsequent arguments, we need an explicit constant in this estimate, so we provide the proof below.

Lemma 5.5
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
5.2
, for any positive integer
k
, we have

(5.27)

\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)}{r^{k+2}}\epsilon\text{ in }B_{r/4}.

Before presenting the proof of Lemma
5.5
, we first state an application of the higher order curvature estimates. The following lemma gives the higher order derivative bounds for
\varphi,\psi,T,A
, and
f
. This Lemma also leads up to the inductive method in Lemma
5.5
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Under the assumptions in Proposition
5.2
, If the Riemannian curvature satisfies

(5.28)

\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)}{r^{k+2}}\epsilon

for any positive integer
k\leq n
in the ball
B_{ar}
, then we have

(5.29)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}\varphi|\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)}{r^{k}}\epsilon,

(5.30)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}\psi|\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)}{r^{k}}\epsilon,

(5.31)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}T|\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)}{r^{k+1}}\epsilon,

(5.32)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}A|\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)}{r^{k+2}}\epsilon,

(5.33)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k+2}f|\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)}{r^{k+2}}\epsilon,

for any
k\leq n
in the ball
B_{ar}
.
Moreover,
\nabla^{k}S
has the same estimate as
\nabla^{k}A
.

Proof.

From the inequality (
2.39
) and the Definition
2.37
, we apply (
5.28
) to have

(5.34)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}\varphi|\lesssim\sum_{\|v_{k}\|=k}\|Rm\|_{v_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{0\leq i\leq k}|\nabla^{i}Rm|^{n_{i}}=|Rm|^{n_{0}}|\nabla Rm|^{n_{1}}\cdots|\nabla^{k}Rm|^{n_{k}}\leq\frac{C}{r^{\sum_{i=0}^{k}(i+2)n_{i}}}

Recall Definition
2.36
that

k=\|v_{k}\|=\sum_{0\leq i\leq k}(i+2)n_{i}.

So, we have (
5.29
).
Similarly, from Proposition
2.38
, we have

(5.35)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}\psi|\leq C(k)\sum_{\|v_{k}\|=k}\|Rm\|_{v_{k}}\leq\frac{C(k)}{r^{k}},

(5.36)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}T|\leq C(k)\sum_{\|v_{k}\|=k+1}\|Rm\|_{v_{k}}\leq\frac{C(k)}{r^{k+1}},

(5.37)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}A|\leq C(k)\sum_{\|v_{k}\|=k+2}\|Rm\|_{v_{k}}\leq\frac{C(k)}{r^{k+2}},

Since
\nabla^{2}f=\lambda_{S}g+A-Ric
, we have

(5.38)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k+2}f|\leq C(k)\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|+C(k)\left|\nabla^{k}A\right|\leq\frac{C(k)}{r^{k+2}}.

Moreover,
S=-|T|^{2}=T*T
and
A=T*T
, so
\nabla^{k}S
has the same estimates as
\nabla^{k}A
.
∎

5.3.1.
Proof of Lemma
5.5

We argue by induction on
k
. For
k=0
, Proposition
5.2
gives

\left|Rm\right|\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)}{r^{2}}\epsilon\text{ in }B_{r/2}.

Assume now that (
5.27
) holds in a slightly smaller ball, namely

(5.39)

\left|\nabla^{j}Rm\right|\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,j)}{r^{j+2}}\epsilon\text{ in }B_{3r/8},\quad\forall j\leq k-1.

We then establish the corresponding estimate for order
k
.

To derive local estimates, we introduce a cutoff function
\eta
. We then calculate

(5.40)

\displaystyle\triangle\left(\eta^{2}\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\eta^{2}\triangle\left(\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}\right)+\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}\triangle\left(\eta^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+2\left\langle\nabla\left(\eta^{2}\right),\nabla\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}\right\rangle.

Estimate the third term in (
5.40
).

Applying a basic quadratic inequality together with Kato’s inequality, we can estimate the third term in (
5.40
) by

\displaystyle 2\left\langle\nabla\left(\eta^{2}\right),\nabla\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle 8\eta\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|\left\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}\left|\nabla^{k+1}Rm\right|^{2}-32\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{2}\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}.

Estimate the first term in (
5.40
).

The first term in (
5.40
) can be written as

(5.41)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\triangle\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}=\left\langle\nabla^{k}Rm,\triangle\nabla^{k}Rm\right\rangle+\left|\nabla^{k+1}Rm\right|^{2}.

Thus, it remains to derive an upper bound for
\left|\triangle\nabla^{k}Rm\right|
.
To obtain an explicit formula, we start from (
2.25
), which yields

(5.42)

\triangle Rm=\nabla f*\nabla Rm+Rm\ast Rm+Rm\ast T*T+T*\nabla^{2}T+\nabla T*\nabla T.

Recall the Ricci identity

(5.43)

\triangle\nabla^{k}Rm=\nabla^{k}\triangle Rm+Rm*\nabla^{k}Rm+\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}\nabla^{l}Rm*\nabla^{k-l}Rm.

Substituting (
5.42
) into (
5.43
), we obtain the expression of
\triangle\nabla^{k}Rm
:

(5.44)

\displaystyle\triangle\nabla^{k}Rm=\nabla^{k}\big(\nabla f*\nabla Rm+Rm\ast Rm

\displaystyle+Rm\ast T*T+T*\nabla^{2}T+\nabla T*\nabla T\big)

\displaystyle+Rm*\nabla^{k}Rm+\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}\nabla^{l}Rm*\nabla^{k-l}Rm

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla^{k}\left(\nabla f*\nabla Rm\right)+\nabla^{k}\left(Rm*T*T\right)+T*\nabla^{k+2}T+\nabla T*\nabla^{k+1}T

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=2}^{k}\nabla^{l}T*\nabla^{k+2-l}T+Rm*\nabla^{k}Rm+\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}\nabla^{l}Rm*\nabla^{k-l}Rm.

Estimate
\left|\triangle\nabla^{k}Rm\right|
.

For the first term in (
5.44
), we invoke the soliton equation
\nabla^{2}f=\lambda_{S}g+A-Ric
together with the bound on
A
from (
5.32
). This gives

\displaystyle\nabla^{l}f=\nabla^{l-2}\nabla^{2}f\thickapprox\nabla^{l-2}(A+Rm)\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)}{r^{l}}\epsilon.

Using these estimates, we obtain

(5.45)

\displaystyle\left|\nabla^{k}\left(\nabla f*\nabla Rm\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(k)\left(|\nabla f|\left|\nabla^{k+1}Rm\right|+\sum_{l=2}^{k+1}\left|\nabla^{l}f\right|\left|\nabla^{k+2-l}Rm\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(k)\left(|\nabla f|\left|\nabla^{k+1}Rm\right|+\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)}{r^{k+4}}\epsilon\right).

For the second through fifth terms, we apply the inductive assumption (
5.39
) together with inequality (
5.31
), which yields

(5.46)

\left|\nabla^{l}T\right|\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,l)}{r^{l+1}}\epsilon\quad\forall\,l\leq k\quad\text{ in }B_{3r/8}.

For the higher derivatives
\nabla^{k+1}T
and
\nabla^{k+2}T
, estimate (
2.41
) gives

(5.47)

\left|\nabla^{k+1}T\right|\leq C(k)\left(\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|+\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)}{r^{k+2}}\epsilon\right)

and

(5.48)

\left|\nabla^{k+2}T\right|\leq C(k)\left(\left|\nabla^{k+1}Rm\right|+|Rm|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|+\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)}{r^{k+3}}\epsilon\right).

By substituting (
5.46
), (
5.47
), (
5.48
), and (
5.45
) into (
5.44
), we arrive at

(5.49)

\displaystyle\left|\triangle\nabla^{k}Rm\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(k)\Big((|\nabla f|+|T|)\left|\nabla^{k+1}Rm\right|+|Rm|\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|

\displaystyle+\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)}{r^{k+4}}\epsilon\Big).

Estimate
\triangle\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}
.

Plugging (
5.49
) into (
5.41
), we have

(5.50)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\triangle\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left|\nabla^{k+1}Rm\right|^{2}

\displaystyle-C(k)\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|(|\nabla f|+|T|)\left|\nabla^{k+1}Rm\right|

\displaystyle-C(k)|Rm|\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}-\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)\epsilon}{r^{k+4}}.

From
|\nabla f|^{2}\leq F
and
|T|^{2}=-S\leq|Rm|
, we have

(5.51)

\displaystyle-C(k)\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|(|\nabla f|+|T|)\left|\nabla^{k+1}Rm\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla^{k+1}Rm\right|^{2}-C(k)\left(|\nabla f|+|T|\right)^{2}\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla^{k+1}Rm\right|^{2}-C(k)\left(F+\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)\epsilon}{r^{2}}\right)\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}.

By the inductive hypothesis, we have

(5.52)

\displaystyle\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)\epsilon}{r^{k+4}}=\frac{\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{r}\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{r^{k+3}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}\frac{\epsilon}{r^{2}}-\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)\epsilon}{r^{2k+6}}.

Combining the three inequalities above, we have

(5.53)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\triangle\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla^{k+1}Rm\right|^{2}-C(C_{s},\kappa,F,k)\left(F+\frac{\epsilon}{r^{2}}\right)\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)\epsilon^{2}}{r^{6+2k}}.

Cutoff function.

Choosing a rotationally symmetric cut-off function
\eta
satisfying
\eta=1
in
B_{r/4}
,
\eta=0
outside
B_{3r/8}
and

(5.54)

\left|\nabla\eta\right|\leq\frac{C}{r}.

Now we consider the laplacian operator
\triangle=g^{ij}(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\partial_{k})
.
Since the bound of Riemannian curvature
\left|Rm\right|\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F)}{r^{2}}\epsilon
, by integral, we have

\frac{1}{2}\leq g^{ij}\leq 2\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\right|\leq\frac{2}{r}\quad\text{in}\quad B_{r}.

Now we should estimate the injective radius. From Klingenberg’s theorem, we know the injective radius is bounded below by

\min(\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{K}},l)
where
K
is the upper bound of sectional curvature and
2l
is the length of the shortest geodesic loop.
Since
\left|Rm\right|\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\kappa,F)}{r^{2}}\epsilon
in
B_{r}
, we know
\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{K}}\geq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{C\epsilon}}r>r
.
A theorem of Cheeger-Gromov-Taylor
[
11
]
*Page 42 shows that the lower bound of
l
depends on the volume lower bound
\kappa_{1}
in the non-collapsing property
V_{r}\geq\kappa_{1}r^{n}
where
\kappa_{1}
only depends on Perelman’s functional
\underline{\nu}
.
So for a small radius
r
, we can choose a proper local coordinate in a smaller ball
B_{3r/4}
because the injective radius is larger than
3r/4
.
After choosing a geodesic polar coordinates, we have

\left|\partial_{r}\partial_{r}\eta\right|\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\partial_{\theta}\eta\right|=0.

Direct calculation shows that

(5.55)

|\triangle\eta|\leq\left|g^{rr}\partial_{r}\partial_{r}\eta-\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\partial_{k}\eta\right|\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}}.

Estimate
\triangle\left(\eta^{2}\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}\right)
.

Substituting the third term estimate (
5.3.1
), the first term estimate (
5.53
), and the bounds for the cutoff function (
5.54
) and (
5.55
) into (
5.40
), we obtain

(5.56)

\displaystyle\triangle\left(\eta^{2}\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\eta^{2}\left|\nabla^{k+1}Rm\right|^{2}-C\left(F+\frac{\epsilon}{r^{2}}+\eta\triangle\eta-|\nabla\eta|^{2}\right)\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}-\frac{C\epsilon^{2}}{r^{6+2n}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\eta^{2}\left|\nabla^{k+1}Rm\right|^{2}-C_{1}\left(F+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}-\frac{C\epsilon^{2}}{r^{6+2k}}.

All the
C
’s depend on
C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k
. Similarly, we obtain

(5.57)

\displaystyle\triangle\left(\left|\nabla^{k-1}Rm\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}-C_{2}\left(F+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\left|\nabla^{k-1}Rm\right|^{2}-\frac{C\epsilon^{2}}{r^{4+2k}}

\displaystyle\geq\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}-\frac{C\epsilon^{2}}{r^{4+2k}}.

In deriving the final inequality, we have applied the induction hypothesis (
5.39
):
\left|\nabla^{k-1}Rm\right|\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k-1)}{r^{k+1}}\epsilon
.

Maximum principle.

Defining the auxiliary function

v=\eta^{2}\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}+C_{1}\left(F+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\left|\nabla^{k-1}Rm\right|^{2},

we derive

(5.58)

\displaystyle\Delta v

\displaystyle\geq\eta^{2}\left|\nabla^{k+1}Rm\right|^{2}-\frac{C\epsilon^{2}}{r^{6+2k}}-C_{1}\left(F+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\frac{C\epsilon^{2}}{r^{4+2k}}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{C_{3}(C_{s},\underline{\nu},F,k)\epsilon^{2}}{r^{6+2k}}\quad\text{in }B_{r}.

We introduce the function
e^{\frac{\alpha x_{1}}{r}}
, where
x_{1}
denotes the first coordinate, and set

w=v+e^{\frac{\alpha x_{1}}{r}}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{r^{4+2k}},\qquad\alpha=C_{3}+6.

Using the bounds

\frac{1}{2}\leq g^{ij}\leq 2\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\right|\leq\frac{2}{r}\quad\text{in}\quad B_{r},

we obtain

(5.59)

\displaystyle\Delta w

\displaystyle\geq\Delta v+\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{r^{2}}-\frac{2}{r}\frac{\alpha}{r}\right)e^{\frac{\alpha x_{1}}{r}}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{r^{4+2k}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha^{2}-4\alpha-2C_{3}}{2r^{2}}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{r^{4+2k}}\geq 0.

Hence, by the maximum principle,

w\leq\sup_{\partial B_{r}}w.

Substituting the definition of
w
yields

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{3r/4}}\left(\eta^{2}\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}+C_{1}\left(F+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\left|\nabla^{k-1}Rm\right|^{2}+e^{\frac{\alpha x_{1}}{r}}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{r^{4+2k}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle\sup_{\partial B_{3r/4}}\left(\eta^{2}\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|^{2}+C_{1}\left(F+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\left|\nabla^{k-1}Rm\right|^{2}+e^{\frac{\alpha x_{1}}{r}}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{r^{4+2k}}\right).

Now applying the inductive hypothesis and using that
\eta=1
in
B_{r/4}
and
\eta=0
outside
B_{3r/8}
, we conclude

\sup_{B_{r/4}}\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|\leq\frac{C(C_{s},\kappa,F,k)\,\epsilon}{r^{2+k}}.

5.4.
Convergence

In this section, we will prove the compactness Theorem
5.1
.
Using the higher-order curvature estimates together with the volume non-collapsing condition, we can extract a subsequence that converges in the pointed smooth sense.
We adopt the approach used by Anderson
[
2
]
*Section 5; see also Haslhofer and Müller
[
29
]
.

Step 1: An upper bound on the number of singular components.

Let
\left(M_{i},\phi_{i},g_{i},f_{i}\right)
be a sequence of
G_{2}
-solitons satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
5.1
.
Fix a large
R
, and let
\epsilon
be the constant given by the epsilon regularity theorem, Proposition
5.2
.

For a small radius
r>0
, we choose a maximal
\frac{r}{2}
-separated set
\{x_{i}^{k}\}
in
B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},R)\subset M_{i}
.
Then the geodesic balls
B_{g_{i}}(x_{i}^{k},\frac{r}{4})
are pairwise disjoint, and the balls
B_{g_{i}}(x_{i}^{k},r)
cover
B_{g_{i}}(p_{i},R)
.
Define

D_{i}(r,R)=\bigcup\left\{B_{g_{i}}(x_{i}^{k},r/2)\ \Big|\ \int_{B_{g_{i}}(x_{i}^{k},r)}|Rm|^{\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f}\leq\epsilon\right\},

and

L_{i}(r,R)=\bigcup\left\{B_{g_{i}}(x_{i}^{k},r/2)\ \Big|\ \int_{B_{g_{i}}(x_{i}^{k},r)}|Rm|^{\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f}\geq\epsilon\right\}.

Using the volume doubling property from Lemma
3.9
, we can show that the number of points
x_{i}^{k}
whose associated balls lie in
L_{i}(r,R)
is bounded.

Step 2: convergence of regular parts.

According to the epsilon regularity estimate Proposition
5.2
and the Shi-type estimates Lemma
5.5
, we have estimates for curvature away from those singular points
x_{j}
in
L_{i}(r,R)
.

\left|Rm\right|\leq\frac{C\epsilon}{r^{2}},\quad\left|\nabla^{k}Rm\right|\leq\frac{C\epsilon}{r^{2+k}}.

Then the curvature bounds yield corresponding bounds for the
G_{2}
-structures by Lemma
5.6
, and the metric bounds provided by Proposition
3.35
can be applied. The injectivity radius bound is obtained in the same manner as in the argument of
Step 2
in the proof of Theorem
4.4
.
By smooth convergence theorem, a subsequence of
D_{i}(r,R)
converges to a smooth manifold
D(r,R)
with a smooth metric
g_{\infty}(r,R)
.
Now we choose two sequences

\left\{r_{j}\right\}\rightarrow 0,\quad r_{j+1}\leq\frac{r_{j}}{2},\quad\left\{R_{j}\right\}\rightarrow\infty,\quad R_{j+1}\geq 2R_{j}.

We set

\tilde{D}_{i}(r_{j},R_{j})=\bigcup_{l\leq j}D_{i}(r_{l},R_{l}),

which gives the inclusions

\tilde{D}_{i}(r_{1},R_{1})\subset\tilde{D}_{i}(r_{2},R_{2})\subset\cdots\subset M_{i}.

By the argument above, each
\tilde{D}_{i}(r_{j},R_{j})
converges to
\tilde{D}(r_{j},R_{j})
smoothly.
We set
D=\bigcup\tilde{D}(r_{j},R_{j})
such that the induced metric
g_{\infty}
coincides with
g_{\infty}(r_{j},R_{j})
in
\tilde{D}(r_{j},R_{j})
.

In summary, we know on the regular part,
the sequence of
G_{2}
-solitons
\left(M_{i},\varphi_{i},g_{i},f_{i}\right)
smoothly converge to
\left(D,\varphi_{\infty},g_{\infty},f_{\infty}\right)

and the limit satisfies the
G_{2}
-soliton equation.

Let
M_{\infty}
denote the metric completion of
D
. Following
Anderson
[
2
]
*Page 479, we have that

M_{\infty}=D\cup\left\{p_{l}\right\}

where the set of additional points
\left\{p_{l}\right\}
is locally finite. These points
\left\{p_{l}\right\}
are referred to as the curvature singularities of
M_{\infty}
.

Step 3: Multifold.

Let
N
denote the number of singular points of
\left(M_{\infty},\varphi_{\infty},g_{\infty},f_{\infty}\right)
, and write these singular points as

\{p_{l}\mid l=1,\dots,N\}.

Since
N
is finite, we can choose
r_{0}>0
so that
2r_{0}
is strictly smaller than the distance between any two distinct singular points. Then

U_{l}=B_{g_{\infty}}(p_{l},r_{0})\subset M_{\infty}

is a punctured neighborhood of
p_{l}
, and moreover

\int_{U_{l}}|Rm|^{\frac{7}{2}}<\infty.

For
0<s<t<r_{0}
, define the annulus

A(s,t)=B_{g_{\infty}}(p_{l},t)\setminus B_{g_{\infty}}(p_{l},s)\subset U_{l}.

As
s\to 0
, we have

\int_{A\left(\frac{s}{2},2s\right)}|Rm|^{\frac{7}{2}}\to 0.

Hence, by the epsilon regularity estimate Proposition
5.2
and the Shi-type estimates Lemma
5.5
, there exists a function
\epsilon(r)
such that

|\nabla^{k}Rm|(x)\leq\frac{C\,\epsilon(r)}{r^{2+k}},

where
r=d(x,p_{l})
and
\epsilon(r)\to 0
as
r\to 0
.

Next, choose a sequence
\{s_{j}\}
with
s_{j}\to 0
and
s_{j+1}\leq\frac{s_{j}}{2}
. Then the curvature
|Rm|
and all its covariant derivatives
|\nabla^{k}Rm|
on the rescaled annuli

A_{j}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2},2\Bigr)=\bigl(A(\tfrac{s_{j}}{2},2s_{j}),\,g_{\infty}s_{j}^{-2}\bigr)

tend to
0
as
s_{j}\to 0
.

By the smooth convergence theorem, a subsequence of the annuli
A_{j}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2},2\bigr)
converges smoothly to a flat manifold
A_{\infty}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2},2\bigr)
with finitely many connected components.

Repeating this construction for the family of annuli
A\bigl(\tfrac{s}{k},ks\bigr)
for every positive integer
k
, and then passing to a diagonal subsequence, we obtain in the limit a flat manifold
A_{\infty}
.

As shown in
[
2
]
, after blowing up around a singular point, the resulting tangent cone is a union of finitely many flat cones over spherical space forms.

Step 4: Orbitfold.

We now show that
U\setminus\{p_{i}\}
is connected.

In
[
1
]
*Lemma 1.2, the argument uses the Ricci curvature volume comparison theorem together with non-collapsing assumptions. In the case of a
G_{2}
-soliton, the Bakry-Émery volume comparison Lemma
3.10
can be used in place of the Ricci volume comparison theorem, thanks to the bounds on

f\text{ and }|\nabla f|

stated in
(b)
in Definition
1.3
.

Thus, by following the proof of
[
1
]
*Lemma 1.2 step by step with this substitution, we deduce that
U\setminus\{p_{i}\}
has exactly one connected component. Equivalently, the tangent cone at any singular point
p_{i}
consists of a single flat cone whose link is a spherical space form

S^{n-1}/\Gamma.

Step 5: Absence of singular points.

In dimension
n=7
, the tangent cone has the form

A_{\Gamma}:=(0,r)\times S^{6}/\Gamma.

Since
S^{6}
is an even-dimensional sphere, the only possible groups
\Gamma
are

\Gamma=\left\{e\right\}\quad\text{or}\quad\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

If
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, then
S^{6}/\Gamma
is
\mathbb{RP}^{6}
, which is non-orientable.
Consequently, the tangent cone
A_{\Gamma}
would also be non-orientable, which is incompatible with the orientation induced by the
G_{2}
-structure. Hence we must have

\Gamma=\left\{e\right\}.

In this situation, the tangent cone at a putative singular point is simply Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and thus no singular point can actually occur.
We therefore obtain the convergence Theorem
5.1
.
