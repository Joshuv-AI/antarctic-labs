The Conway knot has infinite concordance order
Chiara Donatone, Marc Kegel, Lukas Lewark, Paula Truöl

Abstract
. We examine how the Rasmussen invariant, satellite operations, and null-homologous twists can be used to establish infinite order of knots in the smooth concordance group. As an application, we show that the Conway knot has infinite concordance order.



1.
Introduction

A knot in the
3
-sphere
S^{3}
is
slice
if it bounds a smoothly and properly embedded disk in
B^{4}
, the
4
-ball bounded by
S^{3}
. Two knots
K

and
J
are
concordant
if the connected sum
K\#{-J}
is slice. Here
-J
is the
inverse
of
J
, which is the image of
J
under an orientation-reversing diffeomorphism of
S^{3}
with the opposite orientation on the knot. Knots up to concordance form an abelian group, the
concordance group

\mathcal{C}
. The group operation is induced by connected sum, slice knots represent the identity element, and the additive inverse of a concordance class
[K]
is
[-K]
. While the study of the group
\mathcal{C}
has advanced in recent years, particularly since the advent of Heegaard Floer and Khovanov homology theories, its structure—let alone its isomorphism type—remains mysterious. In particular, the structure of the torsion subgroup of
\mathcal{C}
is an open problem (see e. g.
[
4
, Problem 1.38]
). The
concordance order
of a knot
K
is its order in
\mathcal{C}
, i. e. the least positive integer
n
for which the connected sum
K^{\#n}
of
n
copies of
K
is slice, or infinity if no such
n
exists.

Theorem 1
.

The Conway knot has infinite concordance order.

It had previously been known that the Conway knot
C
is not slice, as demonstrated in the celebrated work of Piccirillo
[
39
]
. Piccirillo’s proof uses a clever construction involving finding a knot
K^{\prime}
with the same
0
-trace as
C
. According to the trace embedding lemma (see
Section

2
), this gives
K^{\prime}
the same sliceness status as
C
. However, computing Rasmussen’s
s
invariant
[
42
]
for
K^{\prime}
shows that
K^{\prime}
is not slice.

One difficulty in determining the non-sliceness of the Conway knot was that all known sliceness invariants vanished for this knot; in particular, all known additive invariants, which could otherwise have been used to prove
Theorem

1
, vanish on
C
. For this reason, we use an additional technique called
twisting
.

Definition 2
.

For knots
J,K\subset S^{3}
, we say that
K
is obtained from
J
by a
right-handed null-homologous twist
,
or simply, a
twist
, if there is an unknot
\alpha\subset S^{3}\setminus J
with
\lk(\alpha,J)=0
such that
(-1)
-surgery on
S^{3}
along
\alpha
turns
J
into
K
.
We say that a real-valued knot invariant
y
satisfies the
twist inequality
if
y(K)\leq y(J)
whenever
K
is obtained from
J
by a twist.

For example, a positive-to-negative crossing change is a special type of twist. (This is why twisting is also called a
positive-to-negative generalised crossing change
, or
adding a generalised negative crossing

[
8
]
.)
Alongside many other knot concordance invariants, the Rasmussen
s
invariant satisfies the twist inequality (see
Section

2
). Another key element in our proof of
Theorem

1
is the behaviour of satellite operations under twists. Recall that a
pattern

P
is a knot in the standard solid torus
S^{1}\times D^{2}
, and
P(K)
denotes the satellite knot with pattern
P
and companion
K
; see, for example,
[
45
, Section 4D]
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
J,K\subset S^{3}
be two knots that can be transformed into the unknot
U
via a finite sequence of concordances and twists. If a knot concordance invariant
y
exists that satisfies the twist inequality and a pattern
P
such that
{y(P(K))\neq y(P(U))}
, then
K\#J
is not slice. In particular,
K
has infinite concordance order.

To prove
Theorem

1
using
Theorem

3
, we must first find a suitable pattern
P
. Building on Piccirillo’s work
[
38
,
39
]
, we construct for all knots
K
with unknotting number
1
and unknotting crossing
c
a pattern
P_{K,c}
such that the satellite knot
P_{K,c}(J)
has the same
0
-trace as
K\#J
for every knot
J
. By the trace embedding lemma, the sliceness statuses of
K\#J
and
P_{K,c}(J)
are identical.

Experiments suggest that
P_{K,c}(U)\neq K
unless
K
is a twist knot or a twisted Whitehead double (see, for example,
[
18
, Section 5]
). We are particularly interested in the pattern
P_{K,c}
when it additionally satisfies
s(P_{K,c}(U))\neq 0
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
K
be a knot with unknotting number
1
and unknotting crossing
c
. If
s(P_{K,c}(U))\neq 0
, then
K
has infinite concordance order.

According to KnotInfo
[
26
]
, the only prime knot with at most
11
crossings for which the concordance order is currently unknown is the Conway knot
C=K11n34
.
Theorem

1
thus concludes the computation of concordance orders for knots with at most 11 crossings.
However, the following question remains open.

Question 5
.

Does the concordance class
[C]
of the Conway knot
C=K11n34
generate a
\mathbb{Z}
-summand of
\mathcal{C}
?

The infinite concordance order of a knot is often demonstrated using knot concordance homomorphisms that do not vanish for the knot in question. In fact, the concordance class
[K]
of a given knot

generates a
\mathbb{Z}
-summand of
\mathcal{C}
if and only if there is a knot concordance homomorphism to
\mathbb{Z}
that takes the value
\pm 1
on
K
. We do not know if the Conway knot admits such a homomorphism.

While finishing this paper, the authors learned that Golla–Pinzón-Caicedo are preparing a paper that answers

positively, using different methods.

in

.

Section

2.
Twists, monotonicity, and non-sliceness

Unless stated otherwise, all manifolds are assumed to be oriented, compact, and smooth throughout. We consider manifolds up to orientation-preserving diffeomorphism and links up to isotopy. Let us begin with the definition of a relation on the concordance group

Similar relations have been considered e. g. in

Definition 6

Remark 7

Equivalently,
[K]\preceq[J]
if and only if there is a smooth, proper, null-homologous embedding

and

The relation

\displaystyle[K]\preceq[J]\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad[M\#K]\preceq[M\#J].

Next, we consider functions

Theorem 11

(Restatement of

)
.

Let

(b)

Proof.

We first prove

\displaystyle[U]\preceq[J]\Rightarrow[K]\preceq[K\#J]\Rightarrow y(P(K))\leq y(P(K\#J))\Rightarrow y(P(U))<y(P(K\#J)).

∎

3.
Piccirillo patterns

In this section, we create the pattern
P_{K,c}
associated with an unknotting crossing
c
of a knot
K
with unknotting number
1
, as promised
in the introduction, and use it to show that the Conway knot has infinite concordance order. This pattern will arise from an RBG link construction, which is a general method of creating knots that share a trace or a surgery. There are several slightly different versions of this construction, where the main idea is always to describe a surgery diagram or Kirby diagram of a manifold using a
3
-component link such that two pairs of these components cancel each other out. See for example
[
1
,
3
,

,

,

,

,

Definition 15
.

An oriented 3-component link
L=R\cup B\cup G
in
S^{3}
is called an

if

(a)

R\cup B
is isotopic to
\mu_{B}\cup B
;

(b)

R\cup G
is isotopic to
\mu_{G}\cup G
,
where
\mu_{B}
and

denote meridians of
B
and
G

(c)

is labelled with a dot, and
B
and
G
are labelled with integers
b
and

.

Let
L=R\cup B\cup G
be an RBG link. Since

is an unknot labelled with a dot, we can interpret

as a Kirby diagram of the
4

obtained from

by attaching a

of

We write

We denote by

Let

Proof.

We write
