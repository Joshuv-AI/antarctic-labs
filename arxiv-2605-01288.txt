A Theory of Saddle Escape in Deep Nonlinear Networks
Divit Rawal, Michael R. DeWeese

Abstract
In deep networks with small initialization, training exhibits long plateaus separated by sharp feature-acquisition transitions. Whereas shallow nonlinear networks and deep linear networks are well studied, extending these analyses to deep nonlinear networks remains challenging. We derive an exact identity for the imbalance of Frobenius norms of layer weight matrices that holds for any smooth activation and any differentiable loss and use this to classify activation functions into four universality classes. On the permutation-symmetric submanifold, the identity combines with an approximate balance law to reduce the full matrix flow to a scalar ODE, giving a critical-depth escape time law τ ⋆ = Θ ⁡ ( ε − ( r − 2 ) ) \tau_{\star}=\Theta(\varepsilon^{-(r-2)}) governed by the number r r of layers at the bottleneck scale rather than the total depth L L . We find that this same r − 2 r-2 exponent is recovered under He-normal initialization with r r bottleneck layers rescaled by ε \varepsilon , where the symmetry manifold is preserved by the flow but not attracting. We find close agreement between our theory and numerical simulations.

2
Imbalance Identity and Activation Classes

Before proceeding to the scalar reduction of the gradient flow ODE, we present an exact identity that holds for the full network, for any smooth activation, and for any differentiable loss. It controls the evolution of the layer imbalance
\Delta_{l}\doteq\|W_{l+1}\|_{F}^{2}-\|W_{l}\|_{F}^{2}
and identifies a functional of the activation that dictates whether layer norms are conserved, drift at cubic order, or drift at quadratic order.

Theorem 1

(Imbalance identity)
.

Let
\sigma\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, let
\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)}[\ell(f(x;W),y)]
for any differentiable per-example loss
\ell

\dot{W}_{l}=-\nabla_{W_{l}}\mathcal{L}

\varphi_{\sigma}(z)\doteq z\sigma^{\prime}(z)-\sigma(z),

(1)

extended entrywise to vectors. Then for each

\derivative{\Delta_l}{t}=2\mathbb{E}\left[\bigl\langle W_{l+1}^{\top}\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\ell,\varphi_{\sigma}(z_{l})\bigr\rangle\right],

(2)

where
\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\ell
denotes the per-example gradient of
\ell(f(x;W),y)
with respect to the pre-activation
z_{l+1}(x;W)
, and the expectation is over
(x,y)
.

A full proof of

(Classification of smooth activations)

3.2
Leading-Order Origin Expansion

The scalar chain
eq.

3
is a composition of multiplications by
X_{\ell}
and activations
\alpha u+O(u^{q})

arises from choosing, at each of the

and weight

The first nonlinear insertion contributes at
X

f(X,g)=\sqrt{N}\alpha^{L-1}\Bigl(\prod_{m=1}^{L}X_{m}\Bigr)g+O(\|X\|^{L+q-1}),

(6)

and

\derivative{X_\ell}{t}=K^{(\sigma)}\prod_{m\neq\ell}X_{m}+R_{\ell}(X),\qquad K^{(\sigma)}\doteq\frac{\beta_{1}h_{\sigma}\alpha^{L-1}}{\sqrt{N}},

(7)

with
|R_{\ell}(X)|=O(\|X\|^{\min(L+q-2,2L-1)})
, simplifying to
O(\|X\|^{L+1})
for Class B activations. The leading drive

is identical across layers; the content of the reduction lies in the differences
X_{\ell}^{2}-X_{1}^{2}
, where the
\prod_{m\neq\ell}
structure survives into the first nonlinear correction and drives a further cancellation.

4
Escape Laws and the Critical-Depth Exponent

The near-balance above allows us to reduce the escape-time asymptotics of the
L
-dimensional reduced flow of
eq.

4
to a one-dimensional quadrature. Two distinct facts contribute. First, one-dimensional quadrature is generic: wherever the analytic reduced field is nonvanishing, the flow-box theorem
[
13

yields coordinates

in which the flow transverses only
U

I_{i}
are invariants, so the escape time is a one-dimensional integral. Second, near the small balanced ray the change of variables takes the specific near-identity shape
U_{\ell}=X_{\ell}+O(s^{q})
, giving the balanced-product flow
\dot{U}_{\ell}=\Phi(U)\prod_{m\neq\ell}U_{m}
whose invariants preserve the deep-linear balance law at leading order. Along any orbit the squared differences
U_{\ell}^{2}-U_{1}^{2}
are exactly constant in the normal-form coordinates, so with
U\doteq U_{1}
and gaps
D_{\ell}\doteq U_{\ell}(0)^{2}-U_{1}(0)^{2}
the dynamics collapse to

\frac{dU}{dt}=\Phi(U,D)\sqrt{\prod_{\ell\geq 2}(U^{2}+D_{\ell})},\qquad\Phi(U,D)=K^{(\sigma)}\bigl(1+\lambda U^{q-1}+\rho(U,D)\bigr),

(8)

where
\Phi
is the one-dimensional drive,
\rho(U,D)=O(U^{q})
, and
\lambda
is the analytic obstruction to full
1

weight in the escape integrand, produces a resonance at

The polynomial exponent

6
Discussion

We have shown that the homogeneity deficit
\varphi_{\sigma}(z)=z\sigma^{\prime}(z)-\sigma(z)
classifies activations into four regimes and controls escape from the zero saddle. On the symmetric submanifold the matrix flow reduces to a one-dimensional integral; off the manifold at He-normal init, a signal-energy argument on
\gamma(W)=\mathbb{E}[fg]
yields the same exponent
\tau_{\star}=\Theta(\varepsilon^{-(r-2)})
, identifying
r
as the quantity that sets the plateau. Prior work on deep linear networks exploits the exact conservation law
W_{\ell+1}^{\top}W_{\ell+1}-W_{\ell}W_{\ell}^{\top}=\mathrm{const}
; our identity generalizes it beyond only linear or ReLU activations, setting the escape-time depth-dependence of
\tau_{\star}
.

Our analysis identifies the first mode escape time and provides approximations to successive escape times — an exact theory of modewise escape times in multi-mode teacher-student networks remains open and is an exciting area of future research. We also note that our framework is asymptotic as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
; understanding training dynamics in arbitrarily large initialization networks is another important avenue for future work. More generally, an ab initio theory of the learning dynamics in deep neural networks remains an important goal for the field.

Proof and Extension of
Theorem

1

We give the derivation of

1
, state the matrix-valued refinement, and record the almost-everywhere version for ReLU.

A.1
Full Derivation of
Theorem

1

Fix a layer index
l\in\{1,\ldots,L-1\}
. Write
\ell
for the per-example loss and
\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{E}[\ell]
for the population loss, with expectation taken over the input distribution (and any label noise). The pre- and post-activations are
z_{l}=W_{l}h_{l-1}
and
h_{l}=\sigma(z_{l})
, with
h_{0}=x
the input. We use
\langle A,B\rangle_{F}\doteq\tr(A^\top B)
for the Frobenius inner product and
\odot
for the Hadamard (entrywise) product.

Under gradient flow
\dot{W}_{l}=-\nabla_{W_{l}}\mathcal{L}
,

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\|W_{l}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\derivative{t}\tr(W_l^\top W_l)

\displaystyle=\tr(\dot W_l^\top W_l)+\tr(W_l^\top\dot W_l)

\displaystyle=2\tr(W_l^\top\dot W_l)

\displaystyle=2\langle W_{l},\dot{W}_{l}\rangle_{F}

\displaystyle=-2\langle W_{l},\nabla_{W_{l}}\mathcal{L}\rangle_{F}.

To prove our claim, all that remains is to express
\langle W_{l},\nabla_{W_{l}}\mathcal{L}\rangle_{F}
as an expectation in pre-activation space.

The per-example chain rule gives

\displaystyle\nabla_{W_{l}}\ell

\displaystyle=\nabla_{z_{l}}\ell\cdot h_{l-1}^{\top},

a rank-one outer product between the upstream gradient
\nabla_{z_{l}}\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and the downstream activation
h_{l-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Taking the expectation over the data distribution and substituting,

\displaystyle\langle W_{l},\nabla_{W_{l}}\mathcal{L}\rangle_{F}

\displaystyle=\tr\bigl(W_{l}^{\top}\mathbb{E}[\nabla_{z_{l}}\ell\cdot h_{l-1}^{\top}]\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tr(W_l^\top\nabla_{z_l} \ell\cdot h_{l-1}^\top)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tr(h_{l-1}^\top W_l^\top\nabla_{z_l} \ell)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tr((W_l h_{l-1})^\top\nabla_{z_l} \ell)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tr(z_l^\top\nabla_{z_l} \ell)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\nabla_{z_{l}}\ell,z_{l}\rangle\bigr],

where between the second and third lines we have used linearity of
\tr
and
\mathbb{E}
, between the third and fourth lines we use cyclicity of the trace, and between the fourth and fifth lines we substitute
z_{l}=W_{l}h_{l-1}
. Combining,

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\|W_{l}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\nabla_{z_{l}}\ell,z_{l}\rangle\bigr].

(18)

Backpropagation relates the upstream gradients at adjacent layers. Differentiating
h_{l}=\sigma(z_{l})
entrywise and
z_{l+1}=W_{l+1}h_{l}
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{z_{l}}\ell

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial h_{l}}{\partial z_{l}}\right)^{\top}\nabla_{h_{l}}\ell=\diag(\sigma^{\prime}(z_{l}))\cdot W_{l+1}^{\top}\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\ell=\sigma^{\prime}(z_{l})\odot\bigl(W_{l+1}^{\top}\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\ell\bigr),

where the first equality is the Jacobian–vector chain rule, the second uses the Jacobian of
\sigma
applied entrywise (a diagonal matrix with entries
\sigma^{\prime}(z_{l})_{i}
) along with the affine Jacobian of
z_{l+1}
in
h_{l}
, and the third rewrites the diagonal multiplication as a Hadamard product.

Take the inner product of the backprop identity with
z_{l}
. Using the Hadamard inner product identity
\langle a\odot b,c\rangle=\langle b,a\odot c\rangle
for vectors
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{z_{l}}\ell,z_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\sigma^{\prime}(z_{l})\odot(W_{l+1}^{\top}\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\ell),z_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle W_{l+1}^{\top}\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\ell,\sigma^{\prime}(z_{l})\odot z_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle W_{l+1}^{\top}\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\ell,z_{l}\odot\sigma^{\prime}(z_{l})\rangle.

Separately, the forward pass
z_{l+1}=W_{l+1}\sigma(z_{l})=W_{l+1}h_{l}
gives

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\ell,z_{l+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\ell,W_{l+1}\sigma(z_{l})\rangle=\langle W_{l+1}^{\top}\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\ell,\sigma(z_{l})\rangle.

Subtracting the two lines inside the expectation, the common factor
W_{l+1}^{\top}\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\ell
pairs against
z_{l}\odot\sigma^{\prime}(z_{l})-\sigma(z_{l})=\varphi_{\sigma}(z_{l})
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\nabla_{z_{l}}\ell,z_{l}\rangle\bigr]-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\ell,z_{l+1}\rangle\bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle W_{l+1}^{\top}\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\ell,\varphi_{\sigma}(z_{l})\rangle\bigr].

Applying (
18
) at layers
l
and
l+1
and subtracting,

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\bigl(\|W_{l+1}\|_{F}^{2}-\|W_{l}\|_{F}^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle=-2\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\ell,z_{l+1}\rangle\bigr]+2\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle\nabla_{z_{l}}\ell,z_{l}\rangle\bigr]

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{E}\bigl[\langle W_{l+1}^{\top}\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\ell,\varphi_{\sigma}(z_{l})\rangle\bigr],

which is
eq.

2
. ∎

B.1

inherits the entry-pattern of

itself.

Lemma 14

(Ansatz invariance)
.

Suppose

satisfies

3

and scalars

By

3

Layer 1.

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigl[\nabla_{z_{1}}\ell\cdot x^{\top}\bigr]=\mathbf{1}_{N}\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigl[q_{1}(g)x^{\top}\bigr].

\mathbf{1}_{N}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{\prime}(z_{l})\odot\bigl(W_{l+1}^{\top}\nabla_{z_{l+1}}\mathcal{L}\bigr)

Layer

\sqrt{N}\alpha^{L-2}\bigl(\prod_{m=2,m\neq\ell}^{L}X_{m}\bigr)\rho(X_{1}g)&(\ell\geq 2).\end{cases}

For

\ell\geq 2
. For

Theorem 16

(Formal balanced normal form)

There exist unique formal series

Matching powers of

C.2

The formal balanced normal form is the near-balanced-ray statement; it rests on the fact that the analytic reduced vector field is scalarizable on any nonvanishing open set of the foliation, and that escape time is a one-dimensional quadrature of the resulting scalar speed.

Remark

(Analytic scalarization is generic)

Theorem 18

Exact-to-leading-order accuracy.

Pass to

Lemma 19

(Bottleneck-shell asymptotics)
.

Fix
\eta\in(0,1)

uniformly in
Y

C
is an absolute constant. In particular,
E_{j}(Y,\eta)\to 0
as
\eta\to 0
, uniformly in
Y
and

.

Proof.

Partition
[s_{1}^{2},1]
into shells
[s_{j}^{2},s_{j+1}^{2}]

: indices
i\leq j
have
s_{i}^{2}-s_{1}^{2}\leq s_{j}^{2}\leq Y
, and more precisely
s_{i}^{2}/Y\leq s_{i}^{2}/s_{j}^{2}\leq\eta^{2(j-i)}\leq 1
, so
\sqrt{Y+s_{i}^{2}-s_{1}^{2}}=\sqrt{Y}(1+r_{i})

\sqrt{Y+s_{i}^{2}-s_{1}^{2}}=s_{i}(1+r_{i}^{\prime})

. One factor of
\sqrt{Y}
from the bare term plus

absorbing this into a single constant.
∎

Remark

Three features of the preceding argument are worth pointing out:

(iii)

E.2
Structural Instability of the Block-Mean Saddle: Proof

The following proposition supplies an analytic proof that the block-mean Jacobian
eq.

54
has a real positive eigenvalue whenever the mixed loop gain exceeds unity. The argument is a continuous-deformation Schur complement driven by the Perron–Frobenius theorem.

Proposition 25

(Schur–Perron positive eigenvalue)
.

Let
A,D\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be diagonal matrices with strictly positive entries, and let
B,C\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\geq 0}
be entrywise nonnegative. Define

J\doteq\begin{pmatrix}-A&B\\
C&-D\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{M}\doteq D^{-1}CA^{-1}B.

If
\rho(\mathcal{M})>1
, then
J
has a real eigenvalue
\lambda_{\star}>0
, with a corresponding eigenvector
(x_{\star},y_{\star})
satisfying
x_{\star},y_{\star}\geq 0
and
y_{\star}\neq 0
. If in addition
\mathcal{M}
is irreducible, then
x_{\star},y_{\star}>0
and
\lambda_{\star}
is simple.

Proof.

For each
\lambda\geq 0
, the diagonal matrices
A+\lambda I
and
D+\lambda I
have strictly positive entries, so they are invertible with nonnegative inverses. Define

K(\lambda)\doteq(D+\lambda I)^{-1}C(A+\lambda I)^{-1}B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\geq 0}.

(56)

Entrywise,
[K(\lambda)]_{ij}=\sum_{k}\frac{C_{ik}}{d_{i}+\lambda}\cdot\frac{B_{kj}}{a_{k}+\lambda}
, which is a continuous, strictly decreasing function of
\lambda
on
[0,\infty)
. By continuity of the spectral radius on entries of nonnegative matrices,
\lambda\mapsto\rho(K(\lambda))
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
, and by monotonicity of the Perron root in the entrywise partial order on nonnegative matrices
[
20
, Corollary 8.1.19]
,
\lambda\mapsto\rho(K(\lambda))
is monotonically nonincreasing.

At
\lambda=0
,
K(0)=D^{-1}CA^{-1}B=\mathcal{M}
, so
\rho(K(0))=\rho(\mathcal{M})>1
by hypothesis. As
\lambda\to\infty
,
[K(\lambda)]_{ij}=O(\lambda^{-2})
entrywise, so
\|K(\lambda)\|_{\infty}\to 0
and
\rho(K(\lambda))\leq\|K(\lambda)\|_{\infty}\to 0
. Using the intermediate value theorem, there exists
\lambda_{\star}\in(0,\infty)
with

\rho(K(\lambda_{\star}))=1.

(57)

By the Perron–Frobenius theorem for nonnegative matrices, the spectral radius
\rho(K(\lambda_{\star}))=1
is an eigenvalue of
K(\lambda_{\star})
, realized by a nonnegative eigenvector
y_{\star}\geq 0
,
y_{\star}\neq 0
:

K(\lambda_{\star})y_{\star}=y_{\star},\qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad(D+\lambda_{\star}I)^{-1}C(A+\lambda_{\star}I)^{-1}By_{\star}=y_{\star}.

(58)

Define
x_{\star}\doteq(A+\lambda_{\star}I)^{-1}By_{\star}
, which is nonnegative entrywise since
(A+\lambda_{\star}I)^{-1}
is diagonal with positive entries and
By_{\star}\geq 0
. Multiplying
eq.

58
by
D+\lambda_{\star}I
on the left yields
C(A+\lambda_{\star}I)^{-1}By_{\star}=(D+\lambda_{\star}I)y_{\star}
, i.e.,

Cx_{\star}=(D+\lambda_{\star}I)y_{\star}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad Cx_{\star}-Dy_{\star}=\lambda_{\star}y_{\star}.

(59)

Likewise, the definition of
x_{\star}
rearranges to

(A+\lambda_{\star}I)x_{\star}=By_{\star}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad-Ax_{\star}+By_{\star}=\lambda_{\star}x_{\star}.

(60)

Stacking
eqs.

60
and
59
:

J\begin{pmatrix}x_{\star}\\
y_{\star}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-Ax_{\star}+By_{\star}\\
Cx_{\star}-Dy_{\star}\end{pmatrix}=\lambda_{\star}\begin{pmatrix}x_{\star}\\
y_{\star}\end{pmatrix},

so
(x_{\star},y_{\star})
is a real eigenvector of
J
with real eigenvalue
\lambda_{\star}>0
. The nonnegativity of
x_{\star},y_{\star}
is inherited from the nonnegative Perron eigenvector and the positivity of
(A+\lambda_{\star}I)^{-1}
,
B
,
C
. If
\mathcal{M}
is irreducible, then so is
K(\lambda_{\star})
(since
K(\lambda_{\star})
has the same zero pattern as
\mathcal{M}
for all
\lambda\geq 0
), and the Perron–Frobenius theorem in irreducible form gives
y_{\star}>0
strictly entrywise and the Perron root simple; then
x_{\star}>0
follows, and simplicity of
\lambda_{\star}
as an eigenvalue of
J
follows from the analogous simplicity statement for
K(\lambda_{\star})
.
∎

Remark

(Converse and tightness)
.

The proof also gives the converse: if
\rho(\mathcal{M})\leq 1
, then by monotonicity
\rho(K(\lambda))\leq\rho(\mathcal{M})\leq 1
strictly for every
\lambda>0
, so
K(\lambda)
has no eigenvalue equal to
1
for any
\lambda>0
; unwinding the eigenvector construction above,
J
then has no positive real eigenvalue (it can still have eigenvalues with positive real part from complex-conjugate pairs, but those are a separate, stronger instability mechanism). Thus
\rho(\mathcal{M})>1
is exactly the loop-gain condition for a real positive eigenvalue, and is the natural generalization to block coupling of the scalar criterion
bc/(ad)>1
from the
n=1
case.

Section

E.2
applies to
eq.

54
once
A_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon},C_{\varepsilon},D_{\varepsilon}
are identified as the Jacobian blocks extracted from the Hermite-moment expansion of
Sections

E.4
,
E.6
and
E.5
. The required hypotheses (
A_{\varepsilon},D_{\varepsilon}
diagonal positive,
B_{\varepsilon},C_{\varepsilon}
entrywise nonnegative) are verified structurally from the Hermite-moment definitions in those appendices: diagonal positivity is the decay-rate sign of each decoupled block; nonnegativity of
B_{\varepsilon},C_{\varepsilon}
follows from the closed forms (
62
) and (
64
), each of whose coupling entries is
(\mathrm{positivescalar})\cdot\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{\prime}(Uz)^{2}\cdot(\mathrm{nonnegativeHermitepolynomial})]
. The residual

in

is

and strictly higher order in the small-

E.3
A No-Go Theorem: Strict No-Closure of the Row-Moment Hierarchy

The two-block form of
Section

E.2
presumes that the Jacobian has already been reduced to a finite
2\times 2
block structure
J=\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}-A&B\\
C&-D\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]
. In the underlying PDE dynamics, however, the coupling blocks
B,C
are obtained by truncating an infinite hierarchy of row moments: the within-block row distribution at each layer admits a Hermite expansion
p_{\ell}(w)=\sum_{p\geq 0}Q_{\ell}^{(p)}He_{p}(w)
, and the Jacobian acts on the graded Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{p\geq 0}\mathcal{H}_{p}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{p}
is the block of degree-
p
coefficients. The finite-dimensional blocks used in the main text correspond to a principal-submatrix truncation to degrees
p\leq K
for some fixed
K
. The natural question is whether this truncation is faithful: that is, does the finite Perron root
\lambda_{K}
agree with, or converge finitely to, the infinite-dimensional Perron root
\lambda_{\infty}
?
Theorem

26
answers in the negative.

Theorem 26

(Strict no-closure)
.

Let
\mathcal{J}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{J})\subset\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
be a densely defined linear operator on the graded Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{p\geq 0}\mathcal{H}_{p}
whose matrix representation in the Hermite basis has the Schur block form

\mathcal{J}=\begin{pmatrix}-\mathcal{A}&\mathcal{B}\\
\mathcal{C}&-\mathcal{D}\end{pmatrix},

with
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{D}
diagonal and strictly positive (decay rates), and
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}
entrywise nonnegative in the Hermite basis. Let
\mathcal{M}\doteq\mathcal{D}^{-1}\mathcal{C}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\mathcal{B}
be the loop-gain operator (a bounded, entrywise-nonnegative operator on the off-diagonal grading), and assume
\mathcal{M}
is irreducible and has Perron spectral radius
\rho(\mathcal{M})>1
.Assume moreover that the infinite loop-gain operator
\mathcal{M}
is compact, that its principal truncations
M_{K}=P_{K}\mathcal{M}P_{K}
, canonically embedded in the full sequence space, converge to
\mathcal{M}
in operator norm, and that
\rho(\mathcal{M})
is a simple isolated Perron eigenvalue with a strictly positive eigenvector whose projection outside every finite degree cutoff is nonzero. For each
K\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{J}_{K}
denote the principal-submatrix truncation of
\mathcal{J}
to degrees
p\leq K
, and let
\lambda_{K}>0
(resp.
\lambda_{\infty}>0
) denote the Perron positive eigenvalue of
\mathcal{J}_{K}
(resp.
\mathcal{J}
) supplied by
Section

E.2
(resp. its infinite-dimensional analog). Then

\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{3}<\cdots<\lambda_{K}<\lambda_{K+1}<\cdots<\lambda_{\infty},

(61)

strictly, for every
K\geq 1
. In particular, no finite truncation captures
\lambda_{\infty}
exactly.

Proof.

Fix
K\geq 1
. The Schur reduction of
Section

E.2
gives
\lambda_{K}
as the unique positive root of
\rho(K_{K}(\lambda))=1
, where
K_{K}(\lambda)=(\mathcal{D}_{K}+\lambda I)^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{K}(\mathcal{A}_{K}+\lambda I)^{-1}\mathcal{B}_{K}
is the Perron loop-gain operator restricted to degrees
p\leq K
, and analogously for
K+1
and for the infinite-dimensional object. Since
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{D}
are diagonal and
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}
are entrywise nonnegative,
K_{K+1}(\lambda)
is a principal-submatrix extension of
K_{K}(\lambda)
with the added row and column entrywise nonnegative.

By the Perron–Frobenius monotonicity theorem for nonnegative matrices used earlier
[
20
, Corollary 8.1.19]
, the Perron root is monotone under principal-submatrix extension:
\rho(K_{K}(\lambda))\leq\rho(K_{K+1}(\lambda))
for every
\lambda\geq 0
. Irreducibility of
\mathcal{M}
lifts to irreducibility of each
K_{K}(\lambda)
(the zero pattern is shared), and the strict form of monotonicity under irreducibility with a nonzero added row gives the strict inequality
\rho(K_{K}(\lambda))<\rho(K_{K+1}(\lambda))
at every
\lambda\geq 0
for which the added row of
\mathcal{C}
(or column of
\mathcal{B}
) has at least one nonzero entry coupled by irreducibility. By hypothesis
\mathcal{M}
couples every grading to the Perron eigenvector (irreducibility), so this strict inequality holds at
\lambda=\lambda_{K}
:

\rho(K_{K+1}(\lambda_{K}))>\rho(K_{K}(\lambda_{K}))=1.

Since
\lambda\mapsto\rho(K_{K+1}(\lambda))
is continuous and strictly decreasing in
\lambda
(by the same entrywise-monotonicity argument used in the proof of
Section

E.2
, now at level
K+1
), and tends to zero as
\lambda\to\infty
, the unique root of
\rho(K_{K+1}(\cdot))=1
must lie strictly above
\lambda_{K}
. Hence
\lambda_{K+1}>\lambda_{K}
, strictly.

The limit
\lambda_{K}\to\lambda_{\infty}
as
K\to\infty
, with
\lambda_{K}<\lambda_{\infty}
for every finite
K
, follows from the same argument applied to the principal-submatrix extension
\mathcal{J}_{K}\subset\mathcal{J}
: the Perron root of the infinite-dimensional operator strictly dominates every finite truncation.
∎

Corollary 27

(Escape-time no-closure)
.

Let
\tau_{\star}^{(K)}
(resp.
\tau_{\star}^{(\infty)}
) denote the escape time predicted by the
K
-truncated (resp. full) row-moment dynamics. Under the hypotheses of
Theorem

26
,

\tau_{\star}^{(K)}>\tau_{\star}^{(\infty)}\qquad\text{for every finite }K,

with strict inequality.

Proof.

The escape time along the unstable direction is
\tau_{\star}=\lambda^{-1}\log(1/\varepsilon)\cdot(1+o(1))
on the linearized flow, and strict monotonicity
\lambda_{K}<\lambda_{K+1}<\lambda_{\infty}
of
Theorem

26
gives
\tau_{\star}^{(K)}>\tau_{\star}^{(K+1)}>\tau_{\star}^{(\infty)}
strictly.
∎

We note that a quantitative rate of the gap
\lambda_{\infty}-\lambda_{K}
is to our knowledge, open and is left to future work. This does not affect our work in this paper as we only use the no-go theorem proved above.

K

point along

and layers

. The off-block perturbation is the rank-
1
entry
W_{2}[i\in B_{k},j\in B_{k^{\prime}}]=C_{kk^{\prime}}/N
with

. Our goal is the scalar evolution of

under gradient flow.

Why

.

We work with the off-block entries of
W_{2}
because every deeper layer
W_{\ell}
(
\ell\geq 3

1
off-block perturbation, but with an additional
\rho_{\mathrm{op}}^{\ell-2}
suppression from passing through
\ell-2

\ell

\rho_{\mathrm{op}}(k^{\prime})\sim\varepsilon

of

of

Fix

and write

\Delta h_{i}^{(3)}(x)=\frac{C}{N}\sum_{j\in B_{k^{\prime}}}\sigma(W_{1}[j,:]x).

Every row

To propagate this perturbation to the output we pass through layers

, defining the downstream product

Projecting the gradient and summing over the rank-

(62)

\alpha_{kk^{\prime}}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{K}\mathbb{E}_{z_{k^{\prime}}}[\sigma^{\prime}(U_{k^{\prime}}z_{k^{\prime}})^{2}],\qquad\beta_{kk^{\prime}}=\frac{a_{q}\alpha}{K}\mathbb{E}_{z_{k^{\prime}}}[z_{k^{\prime}}^{q}\sigma^{\prime}(U_{k^{\prime}}z_{k^{\prime}})],

, the factor
\alpha^{2}/K
(resp.
a_{q}\alpha/K
) collects the linear-branch contributions from the target-block downstream chain and the
1/K
block-size normalization, and the expectations reduce to standard Hermite moments of
\sigma^{\prime}
. The remainder is
\mathcal{R}_{kk^{\prime}}=O(D_{k}U_{k^{\prime}}^{2}C)+O(D_{k}U_{k^{\prime}}^{q+1})+O(C^{2})
.

Setting
\dot{C}_{kk^{\prime}}=0
in the linearized equation gives the quasi-steady balance
C_{kk^{\prime}}^{\star}=(\beta_{kk^{\prime}}/\alpha_{kk^{\prime}})U_{k^{\prime}}^{q-1}
; the downstream product
D_{k}
cancels in the ratio, so
\Phi_{kk^{\prime}}
depends on the source block
k^{\prime}
alone, not on the target chain. Variation of constants on
\dot{C}=-a(t)C+b(t)
from
C_{kk^{\prime}}(0)=0
, with
a(s)=\alpha_{kk^{\prime}}D_{k}(X(s))U_{k^{\prime}}(s)
and
b(s)=\beta_{kk^{\prime}}D_{k}(X(s))U_{k^{\prime}}(s)^{q}
, gives the Duhamel form

C_{kk^{\prime}}(t)=e^{-\int_{0}^{t}a(s)ds}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\int_{0}^{s}a(u)du}b(s)ds+(\text{higher-order}).

(63)

On a pre-escape plateau with
U_{k^{\prime}}(s)\sim\varepsilon
and
D_{k}(X(s))\sim\varepsilon^{L-2}
, the constant-coefficient approximations
a\sim\alpha_{kk^{\prime}}\varepsilon^{L-1}
and
b\sim\beta_{kk^{\prime}}\varepsilon^{L-2+q}
reduce
eq.

63
to the elementary closed form
C_{kk^{\prime}}(t)=(\beta_{kk^{\prime}}/\alpha_{kk^{\prime}})U_{k^{\prime}}^{q-1}(1-e^{-a\varepsilon^{L-1}t})
, agreeing with the quasi-steady graph at long times.

E.5
Derivation of the Layer-
1
Cross-Block Duhamel Equation

The layer-
1
cross-block perturbation
\eta_{k,m}
replaces
W_{1}[j,:]=X_{1,k}v_{k}^{\top}
(
j\in B_{k}
) with
W_{1}[j,:]=X_{1,k}v_{k}^{\top}+\eta_{k,m}v_{m}^{\top}
for
m\neq k
. The derivation of
\dot{\eta}_{k,m}
is structurally parallel to
Section

E.4
: linearizing
\sigma(z_{1}[j])=\sigma(U_{k}z_{k})+\eta z_{m}\sigma^{\prime}(U_{k}z_{k})+O(\eta^{2})
(with
U_{k}\doteq X_{1,k}
and
z_{k}\doteq v_{k}^{\top}x
,
z_{m}\doteq v_{m}^{\top}x
independent under the isotropic input law), propagating through the balanced downstream chain
\bar{D}_{k}(X)\doteq\alpha^{L-2}\prod_{\ell=2}^{L}X_{\ell,k}
(starting at layer
2
since the perturbation enters at layer
1
, unlike
D_{k}
of
Section

E.4
which starts at layer
3
), and projecting
-\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{L}
onto the Gaussian input measure (the cross-block pairing
\mathbb{E}[z_{m}z_{k^{\prime\prime}}]=\delta_{mk^{\prime\prime}}
selects a single block-
m
source term, and the self-interaction contributes a decay) yields

\dot{\eta}_{k,m}=-\tilde{\alpha}_{k,m}(X)\eta_{k,m}+\tilde{\beta}_{k,m}(X)+\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{k,m}(X,\eta),

(64)

with
\tilde{\alpha}_{k,m}(X)=\bar{D}_{k}(X)^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{z}[\sigma^{\prime}(U_{k}z)^{2}]+O(\varepsilon)
and
\tilde{\beta}_{k,m}(X)=\bar{D}_{k}(X)\bar{D}_{m}(X)\cdot\mu_{k,m}(U)\cdot(\text{positive scalar})
, where
\mu_{k,m}(U)\doteq\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)}[z^{2}\sigma^{\prime}(U_{k}z)\sigma(U_{m}z)]+U_{m}\,\mathbb{E}_{z}[\sigma^{\prime}(U_{k}z)\sigma^{\prime}(U_{m}z)]
is the leading-order cross-block Hermite moment extracted from the Stein-pairing expansion. The first factor
\mathbb{E}_{z}[\sigma^{\prime}(U_{k}z)^{2}]
is nonnegative as a square. The second factor
\mu_{k,m}(U)=\mathbb{E}_{z}[z^{2}\sigma^{\prime}(U_{k}z)\sigma(U_{m}z)]+U_{m}\,\mathbb{E}_{z}[\sigma^{\prime}(U_{k}z)\sigma^{\prime}(U_{m}z)]
is checked to be positive on the pre-escape plateau (
U_{k},U_{m}>0
), for each of the activations used in the paper. For odd
\sigma
(tanh, erf, sin; Class B) the first summand
\mathbb{E}_{z}[z^{2}\sigma^{\prime}(U_{k}z)\sigma(U_{m}z)]
vanishes by parity:
z^{2}
is even,
\sigma^{\prime}(U_{k}z)
is even (derivative of odd),
\sigma(U_{m}z)
is odd, so the integrand is odd and the Gaussian integral is exactly zero. Positivity of
\mu_{k,m}
is then inherited from the second summand alone: the integrand
\sigma^{\prime}(U_{k}z)\sigma^{\prime}(U_{m}z)
is a product of two even functions, positive at
z=0
(where both derivatives equal
\alpha>0
), and the Gaussian expectation is strictly positive by continuity for small
U_{k},U_{m}
(explicitly,
\mathbb{E}_{z}[\sigma^{\prime}(U_{k}z)\sigma^{\prime}(U_{m}z)]=\alpha^{2}+O(U_{k}^{2}+U_{m}^{2})
), so
\mu_{k,m}(U)=U_{m}\alpha^{2}(1+O(\varepsilon^{2}))>0
on the plateau since
U_{m}\sim\varepsilon>0
. For non-odd
\sigma
(GELU, Swish, softplus; Classes C/D) both summands contribute nontrivially; direct integration against the Hermite basis at
U_{k},U_{m}=\varepsilon
gives a positive leading coefficient. This is a finite calculation, not a monotonicity hypothesis on
\sigma
, and in particular does not require
\sigma
to be monotone (sin is covered by the odd-parity argument above). Comparing (
64
) with (
62
), both are of the form
\dot{\theta}=-a(X)\theta+b(X)+\mathcal{R}
with
a(X)\geq 0
and
b(X)\geq 0
, so the Jacobian block
B_{\varepsilon},C_{\varepsilon}
of
eq.

54
inherits entrywise nonnegativity and
A_{\varepsilon},D_{\varepsilon}
are diagonal positive. On the pre-escape plateau
\bar{D}_{k}\asymp\varepsilon^{L-2}
, so every entry of
B_{\varepsilon},C_{\varepsilon}
is
\Theta(\varepsilon^{L-2})
and the Perron loop-gain matrix
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}\doteq D_{\varepsilon}^{-1}C_{\varepsilon}A_{\varepsilon}^{-1}B_{\varepsilon}
(notation of
Section

E.2
) is
O(1)
independent of
\varepsilon
at leading order, verifying the hypotheses of
Section

E.2
.

E.6
Stage-
k
Saddle Fixed-Point Equations

After stages
1,\ldots,k-1
have escaped, the first
k-1
modes sit on a post-escape manifold on which the scalars
U_{j}=X_{1,j}
(for
j<k
) are dependent on the teacher coefficients via the deep-linear balance law
U_{j}^{L-1}=\beta_{j}/K^{(\sigma)}+O(\varepsilon^{q-1})
, and the downstream chain along each of these modes has relaxed to the balanced ray. The
k
th active mode carries
U_{k}=U\sim\varepsilon
that has not yet escaped, and the downstream chain along this mode sits near the small-balanced cone. Modes
j>k
are also at
O(\varepsilon)
but decouple from the
k
th stage to leading order through the quasi-steady off-block coupling of
eq.

62
. The effective gradient flow for
U_{k}
therefore decomposes as
\dot{U}_{k}=-\partial_{U_{k}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}(U_{k};U_{1:k-1})
.

Expand
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}
in powers of
U_{k}
at fixed upstream
U_{1:k-1}
: the three leading contributions are a residual forcing
F_{k}(U_{1:k-1})
from the un-fitted modes
\geq k
(gradient of the squared loss at
U_{k}=0
), a quadratic Hessian coupling with coefficient
H_{k}(U_{1:k-1})
, and the deep-linear self-drive of the
k
th mode’s own imbalance. Collecting higher-order terms into
R_{k}(U)
,

\dot{U}_{k}=F_{k}(U_{1:k-1})-H_{k}(U_{1:k-1})U_{k}+K^{(\sigma)}U_{k}^{L-1}+R_{k}(U),

(65)

with
R_{k}(U)=O(U_{k}^{L+q-2})+O(U_{k}^{2}\max_{j<k}U_{j})+O(U_{k}^{3})
.

Scaling chain.

Abbreviate
\rho_{k}\doteq\rho_{\mathrm{op}}(k)
. We use the following three scaling relations:

(\mathrm{S1})\ \rho_{k}^{L-2}\asymp\varepsilon^{r_{k}-2},\qquad(\mathrm{S2})\ U_{k}\sim\rho_{k},\qquad(\mathrm{S3})\ F_{k},H_{k}\text{ scale as powers of }\rho_{k},

(66)

where (S1) is the bottleneck-shell evaluation (
Section

C.3
) with
r_{k}
the critical depth of mode
k
, (S2) is the normalized-profile condition
U_{k}=\rho_{k}u_{k}
with
u_{k}\in(0,\theta)
(
u_{k}=O(1)
on the plateau), and (S3) is established next. All exponents below are expressed in
\rho_{k}
and converted to
\varepsilon
via (S1) only at the final step.

Derivation of (S3).
On the stage-
k
block-aligned ansatz, the derivative of the network output in the first-layer scale propagates through the
k
th mode’s chain linearly at
U_{k}=0
:
\partial_{U_{k}}f|_{U_{k}=0}
equals
\alpha^{L-1}\cdot(\text{block-size factor})\cdot(v_{k}^{\top}x)\cdot\prod_{\ell=2}^{L}X_{\ell,k}
, each of the
L-1
downstream layers contributing a factor of scale
\rho_{k}
, so

\partial_{U_{k}}f\big|_{U_{k}=0}=\Theta(\rho_{k}^{L-1})\cdot(v_{k}^{\top}x).

The residual forcing is the Gaussian expectation of this feature against the un-fitted teacher residual
\sum_{j\geq k}\beta_{j}\sigma(v_{j}^{\top}x)
; orthogonality
v_{k}\perp v_{j}
for
j>k
selects
j=k
alone, and Stein’s identity gives

F_{k}(U_{1:k-1})=c_{k}\beta_{k}\rho_{k}^{L-1}\bigl(1+O(\varepsilon^{q-1})\bigr),\qquad c_{k}>0

(67)

(the correction term absorbs
O(\varepsilon^{q-1})
contributions from the fitted residual
U_{j}^{L-1}-\beta_{j}/K^{(\sigma)}
). The Hessian coefficient is the second variation,

H_{k}(U_{1:k-1})=\mathbb{E}[(\partial_{U_{k}}f|_{0})^{2}]+O(\text{residual}\cdot\partial^{2}_{U_{k}}f|_{0})=O(\rho_{k}^{2(L-1)}),

(68)

the dominant contribution being the square of the same feature. Both
F_{k}
and
H_{k}
are independent of
U_{k}
.

Ordering of the three terms on the plateau.

Using (S2) to put
U_{k}\sim\rho_{k}
and (S3) to put
F_{k}=\Theta(\rho_{k}^{L-1})
,
H_{k}=O(\rho_{k}^{2(L-1)})
, each of the three terms in (
65
) is a power of
\rho_{k}
:

forcing
F_{k}

=

\Theta(\rho_{k}^{L-1})
,

linear drag
H_{k}U_{k}

=

O(\rho_{k}^{2L-1})
,

self-drive
K^{(\sigma)}U_{k}^{L-1}

=

\Theta(\rho_{k}^{L-1})
.

The drag is higher order by a factor of
\rho_{k}^{L}
relative to both the forcing and the self-drive, i.e.
H_{k}U_{k}=O(\rho_{k}^{2L-1})=O(\rho_{k}^{L})\cdot\rho_{k}^{L-1}
; since
\rho_{k}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
by (S1), the drag is asymptotically negligible relative to the other two, and
F_{k}+K^{(\sigma)}U_{k}^{L-1}
is the effective right-hand side. The forcing
F_{k}>0
has no dependence on
U_{k}
and seeds escape from any
U_{k}\in[0,\theta\rho_{k}]
; in particular
U_{k}=0
is
not
locally attracting, because the linearization at
U_{k}=0
is
\dot{U}_{k}=F_{k}+O(\rho_{k}^{2L-1})>0
with
F_{k}>0
bounded below in
\rho_{k}^{L-1}
.

Escape time.

On the rescaled state
u_{k}=U_{k}/\rho_{k}\in(0,\theta)
, the effective RHS
F_{k}+K^{(\sigma)}U_{k}^{L-1}
rescales (using (
67
) and
U_{k}^{L-1}=\rho_{k}^{L-1}u_{k}^{L-1}
) to

\rho_{k}\dot{u}_{k}=c_{k}\beta_{k}\rho_{k}^{L-1}+K^{(\sigma)}\rho_{k}^{L-1}u_{k}^{L-1}+(\text{drag},O(\rho_{k}^{2L-1})),

i.e.
\dot{u}_{k}=\Gamma_{k}(1+u_{k}^{2})^{(L-1)/2}\cdot(1+O(\rho_{k}^{L}))
after absorbing the forcing into the
+1
and the self-drive into the
u_{k}^{2}
term inside the bracket (the chain-moment identity of
eq.

52
fixes the form of the bracket; the same-order forcing term supplies the constant
\Gamma_{k}=K_{L}^{(k,\sigma)}\rho_{k}^{L-2}
). Integrating from
u_{k}(0)=0
to
u_{k}=\theta
,

t_{k}=\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{du_{k}}{\Gamma_{k}(1+u_{k}^{2})^{(L-1)/2}}\bigl(1+O(\rho_{k}^{L})\bigr)=\Gamma_{k}^{-1}\mathcal{I}_{L}(\theta)\bigl(1+O(\rho_{k}^{L})\bigr).

Applying (S1) at the final step,

t_{k}=\Theta\bigl(\Gamma_{k}^{-1}\bigr)=\Theta\bigl(\rho_{k}^{-(L-2)}\bigr)=\Theta\bigl(\varepsilon^{-(r_{k}-2)}\bigr),

with
\mathcal{I}_{2}(\theta)=\mathrm{arcsinh}(\theta)
,
\mathcal{I}_{3}(\theta)=\arctan(\theta)
, and
\mathcal{I}_{L}(\theta)=\theta{}_{2}F_{1}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{L-1}{2};\tfrac{3}{2};-\theta^{2}\bigr)
for
L\geq 4
. The drag correction is
O(\rho_{k}^{L})=O(\varepsilon^{L(r_{k}-2)/(L-2)})
relative, absorbed into the
(1+o(1))
of the escape law
Theorem

6
. The successive ratio
t_{k+1}/t_{k}=\Gamma_{k+1}^{-1}/\Gamma_{k}^{-1}=(\beta_{k}/\beta_{k+1})\cdot(\rho_{k}/\rho_{k+1})^{L-2}
depends on mode weights and critical depths alone, through (S1).

E.7
Proof of the Escape-Time Homotopy Identity

We prove
Section

E.1
via Liouville duality. Let
\Theta_{\nu}
denote the escape-time function of the
f_{\nu}
-flow: the unique solution of the Liouville transport equation

f_{\nu}\cdot\nabla\Theta_{\nu}=-1

(69)

with boundary data
\Theta_{\nu}\equiv 0
on the threshold hypersurface
\{h=0\}
. Along any
f_{\nu}
-trajectory
x_{\nu}(t)
starting from the shared initial state
x_{0}
, integrating
eq.

69
along characteristics gives the orbit identity
\Theta_{\nu}(x_{\nu}(t))=T(\nu)-t
. Evaluating at
t=0
,

T(\nu)=\Theta_{\nu}(x_{0}),\qquad\text{so}\qquad T^{\prime}(\nu)=\partial_{\nu}\Theta_{\nu}(x_{0}).

Transport equation for
\partial_{\nu}\Theta_{\nu}
.
Differentiating
eq.

69
in
\nu
gives
f_{\nu}\cdot\nabla(\partial_{\nu}\Theta_{\nu})=-(\partial_{\nu}f_{\nu})\cdot\nabla\Theta_{\nu}
, with boundary data
\partial_{\nu}\Theta_{\nu}\equiv 0
on
\{h=0\}
(inherited from
\Theta_{\nu}\equiv 0
there). Along the
f_{\nu}
-flow,
\frac{d}{dt}[\partial_{\nu}\Theta_{\nu}(x_{\nu}(t))]=-[(\partial_{\nu}f_{\nu})\cdot\nabla\Theta_{\nu}](x_{\nu}(t))
. Integrating from
0
to
T(\nu)
and using
\partial_{\nu}\Theta_{\nu}(x_{\nu}(T(\nu)))=0
,

T^{\prime}(\nu)=\partial_{\nu}\Theta_{\nu}(x_{0})=\int_{0}^{T(\nu)}\bigl[(\partial_{\nu}f_{\nu})\cdot\nabla\Theta_{\nu}\bigr](x_{\nu}(t))dt.

(70)

Identification with the escape-time adjoint.
Define the escape-time adjoint along the trajectory by

p_{\nu}(t)\doteq-\nabla\Theta_{\nu}(x_{\nu}(t)).

(71)

Then
p_{\nu}
satisfies the three stated conditions:

(i)

Adjoint ODE.
Differentiating
\nabla(f_{\nu}\cdot\nabla\Theta_{\nu})=\nabla(-1)=0
gives
(D^{2}\Theta_{\nu})f_{\nu}=-(\partial_{x}f_{\nu})^{\top}\nabla\Theta_{\nu}
, so
\frac{d}{dt}[\nabla\Theta_{\nu}(x_{\nu}(t))]=(D^{2}\Theta_{\nu})f_{\nu}=-(\partial_{x}f_{\nu})^{\top}\nabla\Theta_{\nu}
. Negating,
\dot{p}_{\nu}=-(\partial_{x}f_{\nu})^{\top}p_{\nu}
.

(ii)

Normalization.
From
eq.

69
,
p_{\nu}(t)\cdot f_{\nu}(x_{\nu}(t))=-\nabla\Theta_{\nu}\cdot f_{\nu}=+1
along the trajectory.

(iii)

Terminal condition.
On
\{h=0\}
,
\nabla\Theta_{\nu}
is proportional to the unit conormal
\nabla h/\|\nabla h\|
(because
\Theta_{\nu}\equiv 0
on
\{h=0\}
forces
\nabla\Theta_{\nu}
tangent to its level set to vanish), and the normalization
\nabla\Theta_{\nu}\cdot f_{\nu}=-1
fixes the proportionality:

\nabla\Theta_{\nu}(x_{\nu}(T(\nu)))=\frac{-\nabla h}{\nabla h\cdot f_{\nu}}\Big|_{T(\nu)},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad p_{\nu}(T(\nu))=\frac{\nabla h}{\nabla h\cdot f_{\nu}}\Big|_{T(\nu)},

matching the terminal condition in
Section

E.1
.

Substituting
\nabla\Theta_{\nu}=-p_{\nu}
into
eq.

70
,

T^{\prime}(\nu)=-\int_{0}^{T(\nu)}p_{\nu}(t)\cdot(\partial_{\nu}f_{\nu})(x_{\nu}(t))dt=A(\nu),

and integration on
[0,1]
yields
eq.

55
.
