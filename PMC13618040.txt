Binding Does
Not Mean Stabilizing: High-Affinity Porphyrins
Exhibit Opposite Effects on G‐Quadruplex Stability

Cristina Carucci, Monica Mura, Francesca Cherchi, Giuseppe Satta, Aamir Saeed, Barbara Jachimska, Francesca Mocci, Massimo Carraro, Andrea Salis

Abstract

Binding affinity is often used as a predictor of G-quadruplex
(G4)
stabilization. Here, we show that ligand binding and quadruplex stabilization
can be thermodynamically decoupled. Two closely related tetracationic
porphyrins bind the human telomeric G4 with comparable affinities
approaching 106 M–1 but induce opposite
thermal responses. TMPyP4 stabilizes the quadruplex (+9 °C),
whereas PL7 destabilizes it (−6 °C) despite a similar
binding free energy. Thermodynamic analysis reveals distinct binding
mechanisms, with TMPyP4 association driven primarily by favorable
enthalpy and PL7 binding dominated by entropy. Temperature-dependent
circular dichroism measurements indicate that PL7 promotes redistribution
within the telomeric G4 conformational ensemble rather than stabilization
of the native hybrid fold. These findings demonstrate that high affinity
does not necessarily imply G4 stabilization and establish affinity
and thermal stabilization as independent descriptors of ligand–quadruplex
interactions.

The interaction of small molecules
with G-quadruplex (G4) DNA is commonly interpreted through the lens
of binding affinity, which is often taken as a proxy for structural
stabilization and biological relevance.
,
 G4s are non-canonical
nucleic acid structures formed by stacked guanine tetrads and stabilized
by monovalent cations.
−

 Because of their involvement in gene regulation and genome stability,
considerable efforts have been devoted to the discovery of ligands
capable of selectively recognizing and stabilizing G4 structures.
−

,−

 Among G4 ligands, cationic porphyrins represent one
of the most extensively investigated families owing to their strong
electrostatic and π-stacking interactions with quadruplex DNA.
−

 However, increasing evidence suggests that binding and stabilization
may not necessarily be coupled. Zamiri et al. reported that ligand
binding may induce distortion or refolding, while Joshi and co-workers demonstrated that TMPyP3 and TMPyP4 porphyrins
strongly bind a parallel G4 within the human MRP1 gene while simultaneously destabilizing its folded structure.
,
 A recent review further highlighted ligand-induced G4 destabilization
as an emerging outcome of ligand binding.

To this end, we selected TMPyP4, a well-established G4-binding
porphyrin, as a benchmark ligand and compared it with the recently
synthesized PL7, a structurally related tetracationic porphyrin (). Motivated by these observations, we sought to determine
whether the apparent disconnect between affinity and stabilization
extends beyond the previously reported MRP1/TMPyP3–TMPyP4 system.
Using UV–Vis binding studies, thermal denaturation, circular
dichroism spectroscopy, and molecular docking, we investigated their
interactions with duplex DNA and the human telomeric Tel22 G4. The
CD spectrum of G4, measured in the presence of potassium phosphate
buffer (Figure S1, Supporting Information
file), shows a positive band at 218 nm, a negative band at 238 nm
and an intense positive band at 292 nm indicative of a K+ driven hybrid Tel22 G4 structure,
,
 in agreement
with previous CDs reported in the literature.
−

 Porphyrin binding
to duplex and G4 DNA was assessed by UV–Vis titrations in potassium
phosphate buffer ( and Figure S2). The resulting
spectrophotometric curves were used to determine the corresponding
binding constants (K
b) using the Benesi–Hildebrand
method (Figure S3–S4, Table S1). Both
porphyrins exhibited strong affinity for both DNA forms, with binding
constants in the 104–106 M–1 range and a clear preference for G4 over ds-DNA (). The magnitude of the binding
constants and the observed hypochromic and bathochromic shifts are
consistent with strong π–π stacking and electrostatic
interactions at the DNA interface, indicating effective complex formation
in both systems. Van’t Hoff analysis of the temperature-dependent
binding constants yielded the thermodynamic parameters ΔH and
ΔS from the slope and intercept of the corresponding linear
plots (Figure S5). These parameters provide
insight into the molecular forces governing ligand association and
reveal whether binding is primarily driven by enthalpic or entropic
contributions.
,

Although TMPyP4 and PL7 exhibit comparable binding
affinities toward
G4 DNA, their thermodynamic signatures differ substantially (). TMPyP4 binding
is characterized by a favorable enthalpic contribution (ΔH = −17 kJ mol–1) accompanied by
a moderate entropic gain, whereas PL7 binding displays an unfavorable
enthalpy (ΔH = +15 kJ mol–1) compensated by a markedly larger entropy increase (ΔS = +170 J mol–1 K–1). These results indicate that similar overall free energies of binding
can arise from distinct molecular driving forces. In particular, the
strongly entropy-driven association observed for PL7 may reflect a
larger contribution from solvent release, counterion displacement,
and ligand-induced conformational rearrangements within the quadruplex
structure.

To determine whether these distinct binding thermodynamics
translate
into different structural outcomes, thermal denaturation experiments
were performed. Thermal denaturation measurements, shown in , reveal an opposite
trend of the melting profiles due to the different spectral responses
of ds-DNA and G4. UV absorption arises mainly from π →
π* electronic transitions of the nucleobases, whose intensity
is strongly affected by base stacking. In ds-DNA, base stacking within
the double helix reduces the absorbance at 260 nm. As the temperature
increases, thermal denaturation disrupts hydrogen bonding and π–π
stacking, producing a characteristic hyperchromic increase in absorbance. In G4 DNA, the stacking of G-quartets also contributes
significantly to the spectroscopic response; however, unfolding is
more effectively monitored at 295 nm, where disruption of Hoogsteen
hydrogen bonding and quartet stacking results in a progressive decrease
in absorbance.
,
 The melting temperature (T
m) was determined as the midpoint of the spectroscopic
unfolding transition (Figure S6) and used
to compare the relative stability of DNA in the absence and presence
of the porphyrins. Accordingly, positive and negative shifts in T
m relative to ds-DNA or G4 indicate thermal
stabilization and destabilization, respectively ().

Thermal denaturation experiments reveal that both
TMPyP4 and PL7
stabilize ds-DNA, as shown by positive ΔT
m values in potassium phosphate buffer (+2.0 °C and +2.8
°C, respectively). While TMPyP4 increases the G4 melting temperature
(+9.0 °C), indicating stabilization of the folded structure,
PL7 induces a significant destabilization (ΔT
m = −6.0 °C). This difference is particularly
noticeable given the similar binding affinity of the two ligands toward
G4. Despite differing only in the nature of the peripheral pyridinium
substituents, the two porphyrins produce opposite effects on G4 thermal
stability.

To better understand the origin of the opposite thermal
responses
induced by TMPyP4 and PL7, circular dichroism (CD) spectroscopy was
used to monitor the evolution of G4 topology upon ligand binding and
heating ().
In the absence of ligand, the CD spectrum recorded at 30 °C displays
a dominant positive band near 290 nm, a secondary contribution in
the 260–275 nm region, and a negative band around 240 nm, consistent
with a predominantly hybrid telomeric G4 topology in potassium-phosphate.
,,
 Upon heating, the overall spectral
profile is initially retained but progressively loses intensity while
preserving the characteristic features of the hybrid fold (A). At elevated temperatures,
the marked attenuation of the 290 nm band indicates progressive disruption
of the quadruplex structure, including the loss of ordered G-quartet
stacking and thermal unfolding. In the presence of TMPyP4, however,
the CD spectrum at 30 °C retains the characteristic features
of the hybrid telomeric G4 topology (B). Upon heating, the overall spectral profile
remains largely unchanged apart from a gradual decrease in intensity,
indicating preservation of the native quadruplex architecture over
a broad temperature range. This behavior is consistent with the positive
ΔT
m observed in thermal denaturation
experiments. The observed shifts reflect the relative stabilization
of the conformational states involved in the thermal transition and
cannot be interpreted solely in terms of ligand affinity for the folded
G4. Binding to partially unfolded or alternative conformations may
also contribute to the apparent thermal response. Since state-specific
binding constants for these conformations were not determined here,
such contributions cannot be excluded.

A markedly different behavior is observed for PL7
(C). At 30
°C, the CD spectrum
remains characteristic of a folded G4 structure. However, upon heating,
the contribution in the 260–270 nm region becomes progressively
more pronounced, whereas the intensity of the band near 290 nm decreases.
This change of the CD signal is consistent with a greater contribution
from parallel G4 conformations rather than with a simple loss of quadruplex
folding.
,−

 These results suggest that PL7
modifies the equilibrium among various conformational states. The
complex temperature dependence may reflect either a gradual redistribution
among coexisting conformational states or one or more discrete conformational
transitions. Accordingly, the apparent decrease in T
m should not be interpreted solely as global G4 unfolding.
Preferential PL7 binding to specific conformational states, including
states populated at higher temperature, may also contribute to the
observed thermal response. The present CD data do not allow state-specific
binding affinities to be determined. To investigate the structural
basis of the contrasting thermal and conformational effects induced
by TMPyP4 and PL7, molecular docking studies were performed. Special
emphasis was placed on the unusual behavior of PL7, which displays
high G4 affinity while reducing quadruplex thermal stability. To account
for the structural polymorphism of human telomeric G4s in K+ solution, calculations were performed on both the experimentally
determined hybrid-1 (PDB ID: 2HY9; ) and hybrid-2 (PDB ID: 2JPZ; Figure S7) topologies,
which have been reported to coexist in equilibrium in K+ solution.
,
 The interactions between the
studied porphyrins and the Tel22 G4 may arise from π–π
stacking between the porphyrin macrocycle and exposed nucleobases,
either in the tetrads or in the loops; electrostatic attraction between
the pyridinium groups and the negatively charged backbone; and hydrogen
bonding between the peripheral groups of the ligand and loop or groove
residues. The relative contribution of these interactions is expected
to depend on the binding geometry. Consistent with this view, the
docking calculations indicate the accessibility of multiple binding
modes involving stacking, groove-associated, and hydrogen-bonding
interactions. In the hybrid-1 structure (2HY9), terminal G-tetrads
are partially shielded by loop nucleobases, limiting the accessibility
of ideal end-stacking geometries. Accordingly, docking predicts several
alternative PL7 binding modes involving loop-associated and groove-associated
regions in addition to external tetrad binding (A–C). Similar results were obtained
for the hybrid-2 structure (2JPZ, Figure S7), where both external and lateral binding modes were identified.
Overall, the calculations indicate that multiple energetically accessible
binding modes are possible in both telomeric G4 topologies. Such binding-mode
diversity may contribute to the strongly entropy-driven association
of PL7 by enabling interactions with a broader conformational ensemble
of telomeric G4 structures. Interactions involving loops and grooves,
which are known to influence telomeric G4 topology and dynamics, may
play a key role in the shifting of the conformational equilibrium.
−
,,−

 Although docking calculations cannot uniquely identify
the experimentally populated binding mode, the predicted structural
diversity provides a plausible framework for interpreting the apparent
decoupling between affinity and thermal stabilization.

Despite comparable K
b values, TMPyP4
and PL7 exhibit distinct thermodynamic signatures and opposite effects
on quadruplex thermal stability, revealing a decoupling between ligand
binding and G4 stabilization. While binding affinity reflects the
overall free energy of association, thermal stability reports on the
persistence of the folded quadruplex topology.
,
 Building on previous observations,
,
 our results
demonstrate that high-affinity ligands can exert markedly different
effects on G4 structure and stability. Combined thermodynamic, spectroscopic,
and computational analyses suggest that ligand-induced conformational
redistribution can destabilize the native quadruplex fold despite
favorable binding. Together, these results challenge the assumption
that stronger binding implies greater stabilization and redefine affinity
and stability as independent determinants of G4-ligand interactions.
