Closure Atlases and Local-to-Global Obstructions in Finite Closure Systems
Jaehwan Kim

Abstract
. This paper studies finite closure operators on overlapping finite universes and gives an exact local-to-global obstruction criterion for conservative globalization. Given a finite family of local closure systems, its atlas-generated closure is obtained by repeatedly applying the local closure operators to the parts visible in each chart. This closure is the least global closure operator extending all chart closures. A chart-visible obstruction is a consequence produced by this global propagation that lies inside a chart but is not validated by that chart’s own closure operator. The main theorem proves that a finite closure atlas has a global conservative realization exactly when no such obstruction occurs; in that case the atlas-generated closure itself is the conservative realization. The obstruction condition is finite and directly computable. The paper also records the indexed representation layer motivating the terminology. For a finite closure system, an indexed truth space selects closed theories as contexts and represents each element by the region of selected closed theories containing it. Closure consequence is always sound for region inclusion, and the full indexed space of all closed theories recovers the original closure consequence exactly; reduced indexed spaces can therefore create spurious region consequences by deleting separating closed theories. A formal opposite gives a four-region membership decomposition–only one, only the other, both, and neither–unless additional separation assumptions are imposed. Finally, overlap-compatible local closed theories glue by canonical union under the atlas-generated closure. The framework is finite, structural, and closure-theoretic; the logical terminology is used only as an interpretation of the underlying closure data.

1.
Introduction

This paper studies finite closure operators on finite sets and their local-to-global behavior under overlaps. The guiding problem is structural: given several local closure systems on overlapping finite universes, when can they be realized as exact restrictions of one global closure operator, and what obstruction prevents such a realization? The indexed truth-space language used below is one interpretation of the same closure data. The technical content is closure-theoretic: least generated closures, conservative restriction, finite obstruction detection, and gluing of compatible closed sets.

The starting point is a finite closure system

\mathcal{C}=(\Sigma,\operatorname{cl}),

where
\Sigma
is a finite universe, often read as a set of sentences, and
\operatorname{cl}:\mathcal{P}(\Sigma)\to\mathcal{P}(\Sigma)
is an extensive, monotone, idempotent operator. A closed set
T=\operatorname{cl}(T)
is called a closed theory when the elements of
\Sigma
are read logically. For
S\subseteq\Sigma
and
\varphi\in\Sigma
, the notation
S\vdash_{\operatorname{cl}}\varphi
means precisely
\varphi\in\operatorname{cl}(S)
. Thus the logical terminology is not an additional semantics; it is notation for a finite closure operator.

The central construction is a finite closure atlas

\mathfrak{A}=\{(\Sigma_{\alpha},\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in A}

whose local universes may overlap. Its global universe is
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in A}\Sigma_{\alpha}
. Local propagation is encoded by

G_{\mathfrak{A}}(X)=X\cup\bigcup_{\alpha\in A}\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(X\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}),\qquad X\subseteq\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}},

and iterating this finite monotone operator to a fixed point yields the atlas-generated closure
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
. This is the least global closure operator extending all local chart closures. In particular, any global closure operator that preserves the local implications must contain every consequence generated by
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
.

The main theorem gives an exact obstruction criterion for conservative globalization. A global conservative realization is a global closure operator
D
such that every chart is recovered exactly by restriction:

D(S)\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}=\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S)\qquad(\alpha\in A,\ S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}).

A chart-visible obstruction is a triple
(\alpha,S,\varphi)
such that
S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}
,
\varphi\in\Sigma_{\alpha}
, and

\varphi\in C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)\qquad\text{but}\qquad\varphi\notin\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S).

Thus the least global propagation has forced, inside chart
\alpha
, a consequence not sanctioned by that chart itself. The theorem proves

\operatorname{Obs}(\mathfrak{A})=\varnothing\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathfrak{A}\text{ has a global conservative realization},

and in the obstruction-free case
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
itself is that realization. Since all universes are finite, the criterion also gives a direct finite algorithm: iterate
G_{\mathfrak{A}}
on every chart-local premise set and compare the result with the corresponding local closure.

The single-system material supplies the representation and comparison language used before the atlas theorem. An indexed truth space over
\mathcal{C}
selects a finite family of closed theories,

\mathfrak{X}=(\Sigma,\operatorname{cl},I,\tau),\qquad\tau:I\to\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}),

and represents each element
\varphi\in\Sigma
by the region

V_{\mathfrak{X}}(\varphi)=\{i\in I:\varphi\in\tau(i)\}.

Closure consequence always implies truth-region inclusion. Conversely, when all closed theories in
\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl})
are used as indices, truth-region inclusion recovers closure consequence exactly. This identifies precisely what is lost when one replaces a closure system by a reduced family of selected closed theories: omitted closed theories may be the separating witnesses that distinguish nonconsequence from consequence.

A second auxiliary layer is decision bookkeeping. For an element
a
and a designated formal opposite
\bar{a}
, no logical behavior of
\bar{a}
is assumed from the notation alone. The selected index set decomposes into four regions: indices containing
a
but not
\bar{a}
, indices containing
\bar{a}
but not
a
, indices containing both, and indices containing neither. The familiar positive-negative-undecided trichotomy appears only after imposing an additional opposite-separation condition. This layer is not used in the proof of the local-to-global obstruction theorem; it only separates finite membership bookkeeping from any implicit semantics of negation.

Conservative extension supplies the bridge between the single-system and atlas settings. If
\mathcal{C}_{Y}=(\Sigma_{Y},\operatorname{cl}_{Y})
extends
\mathcal{C}_{X}=(\Sigma_{X},\operatorname{cl}_{X})
, conservativity means that no new old-universe consequences are introduced:

\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(S)\cap\Sigma_{X}=\operatorname{cl}_{X}(S)\qquad(S\subseteq\Sigma_{X}).

For full indexed truth spaces, this is equivalent to an exact pullback identity for old truth regions along reducts of closed theories. The atlas theorem imposes the same conservative-restriction requirement simultaneously for several overlapping charts.

The final technical result is a canonical gluing statement. After fixing the atlas-generated closure, any family of locally closed theories that agrees exactly on overlaps has a union that restricts back to the original local theories and is closed under
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
. This result concerns compatible local closed theories; it is separate from the obstruction theorem, which concerns conservative realization of local consequence relations.

The formal results of the paper do not depend on any stronger philosophical thesis. They are finite statements about closure operators, closed sets, local propagation, and conservative restriction. The terms “truth region,” “closed theory,” and “formal opposite” are used to motivate one intended interpretation, but the definitions and proofs stand as order-theoretic closure constructions.

3.
Finite Closure Systems and Indexed Truth Spaces

We begin with the single-system case. The finiteness assumption is not needed for every definition in this section, but it will be used later when the obstruction theorem is converted into an explicit finite algorithm. The section has two roles: first, to fix the closure consequence relation of a finite closure operator; second, to compare that relation with the weaker information visible through a chosen indexed family of closed sets, called closed theories in the intended logical reading. This distinction is the base case for every later comparison between local and global closure data.

Definition 3.1

(Finite closure system, consequence, and closed theories)
.

A
finite closure system
is a pair

\mathcal{C}=(\Sigma,\operatorname{cl}),

where
\Sigma
is a finite set of sentences and

\operatorname{cl}:\mathcal{P}(\Sigma)\to\mathcal{P}(\Sigma)

is extensive, monotone, and idempotent; that is, for all
S,U\subseteq\Sigma
,

(i)

S\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(S)
,

(ii)

if
S\subseteq U
, then
\operatorname{cl}(S)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(U)
,

(iii)

\operatorname{cl}(\operatorname{cl}(S))=\operatorname{cl}(S)
.

For
S\subseteq\Sigma
and
\varphi\in\Sigma
, write

S\vdash_{\mathcal{C}}\varphi\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\varphi\in\operatorname{cl}(S).

Equivalently, one may write
S\vdash_{\operatorname{cl}}\varphi
. When the closure system is clear from context, we simply write
S\vdash\varphi
.

A subset
T\subseteq\Sigma
is a
closed theory
of
\mathcal{C}
if
\operatorname{cl}(T)=T
. Write

\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl})=\{T\subseteq\Sigma:\operatorname{cl}(T)=T\}

for the set of all closed theories of
\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 3.2

(Raw sets and closed theories)
.

A subset
S\subseteq\Sigma
is an arbitrary set of sentences; it need not be closed. A closed theory is a stabilized object
T=\operatorname{cl}(T)
. Thus
S\vdash\varphi
does not mean that
\varphi\in S
. It means that
\varphi
belongs to the closed theory generated by
S
.

Definition 3.3

(Finite indexed truth space and truth regions)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=(\Sigma,\operatorname{cl})
be a finite closure system. A
finite indexed truth space
over
\mathcal{C}
is a tuple

\mathfrak{X}=(\Sigma,\operatorname{cl},I,\tau),

where
I
is a finite index set and

\tau:I\to\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl})

assigns a closed theory to each index. The elements of
I
are called
indices
or, when helpful,
contexts
. In the main examples and representation theorems below, the relevant index sets are nonempty.

For
\varphi\in\Sigma
, the
truth region
of
\varphi
in
\mathfrak{X}
is

V_{\mathfrak{X}}(\varphi)=\{i\in I:\varphi\in\tau(i)\}.

For
S\subseteq\Sigma
, define

V_{\mathfrak{X}}(S)=\{i\in I:S\subseteq\tau(i)\}=\bigcap_{\psi\in S}V_{\mathfrak{X}}(\psi),

with
V_{\mathfrak{X}}(\varnothing)=I
. When
\mathfrak{X}
is clear from context, we may write
V(\varphi)
and
V(S)
.

Definition 3.4

(Region consequence)
.

For
S\subseteq\Sigma
and
\varphi\in\Sigma
, write

S\models_{\mathfrak{X}}\varphi

if

V_{\mathfrak{X}}(S)\subseteq V_{\mathfrak{X}}(\varphi).

Thus every selected index whose assigned closed theory contains all sentences in
S
also contains
\varphi
. This relation depends on the chosen indexed truth space
\mathfrak{X}
. It should not be confused with closure consequence
S\vdash_{\mathcal{C}}\varphi
.

Lemma 3.5

(Truth-region soundness)
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}=(\Sigma,\operatorname{cl},I,\tau)
be a finite indexed truth space over
\mathcal{C}=(\Sigma,\operatorname{cl})
. For all
S\subseteq\Sigma
and
\varphi\in\Sigma
,

S\vdash_{\mathcal{C}}\varphi\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_{\mathfrak{X}}(S)\subseteq V_{\mathfrak{X}}(\varphi).

Equivalently,
S\vdash_{\mathcal{C}}\varphi
implies
S\models_{\mathfrak{X}}\varphi
.

Proof.

Assume
S\vdash_{\mathcal{C}}\varphi
, so
\varphi\in\operatorname{cl}(S)
. Let
i\in V_{\mathfrak{X}}(S)
. Then
S\subseteq\tau(i)
. By monotonicity of
\operatorname{cl}
,

\operatorname{cl}(S)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(\tau(i)).

Since
\tau(i)
is a closed theory,
\operatorname{cl}(\tau(i))=\tau(i)
. Hence
\operatorname{cl}(S)\subseteq\tau(i)
. Since
\varphi\in\operatorname{cl}(S)
, it follows that
\varphi\in\tau(i)
, and therefore
i\in V_{\mathfrak{X}}(\varphi)
. This proves the inclusion.
∎

Remark 3.6

(Soundness need not be completeness)
.

Lemma
3.5
says that closure consequence is always sound for truth-region inclusion. The converse need not hold for an arbitrary indexed truth space: the chosen index set may omit closed theories that distinguish
S
from
\varphi
. The exact converse is recovered when all closed theories are used as indices.

Definition 3.7

(Full indexed truth space)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=(\Sigma,\operatorname{cl})
be a finite closure system. The
full indexed truth space
of
\mathcal{C}
is

\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}=(\Sigma,\operatorname{cl},\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}),\mathrm{id}),

where the index set is
\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl})
and the assignment map is the identity,
\mathrm{id}(T)=T
. Thus every closed theory is used as an index. Since
\Sigma
is finite,
\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl})
is finite; it is nonempty because
\operatorname{cl}(\varnothing)
is closed.

Theorem 3.8

(Full truth-region representation)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=(\Sigma,\operatorname{cl})
be a finite closure system, and let
\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}
be its full indexed truth space. For every
S\subseteq\Sigma
and every
\varphi\in\Sigma
,

V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(S)\subseteq V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(\varphi)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\vdash_{\mathcal{C}}\varphi.

Equivalently,

S\models_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}\varphi\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\vdash_{\mathcal{C}}\varphi.

More generally, for
S,U\subseteq\Sigma
,

V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(S)\subseteq V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(U)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad U\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(S).

Proof.

We first prove the sentence case. If
S\vdash_{\mathcal{C}}\varphi
, then Lemma
3.5
, applied to the indexed truth space
\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}
, gives

V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(S)\subseteq V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(\varphi).

Conversely, assume

V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(S)\subseteq V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(\varphi).

By idempotence,
\operatorname{cl}(S)
is closed, so
\operatorname{cl}(S)\in\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl})
. By extensiveness,
S\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(S)
, hence
\operatorname{cl}(S)\in V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(S)
. The assumed inclusion gives
\operatorname{cl}(S)\in V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(\varphi)
. Since the assignment in
\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}
is the identity, this means
\varphi\in\operatorname{cl}(S)
, i.e.
S\vdash_{\mathcal{C}}\varphi
.

For the general statement, first assume
V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(S)\subseteq V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(U)
. For each
\psi\in U
, we have
V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(U)\subseteq V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(\psi)
, hence
V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(S)\subseteq V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(\psi)
. By the sentence case,
\psi\in\operatorname{cl}(S)
. Therefore
U\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(S)
.

Conversely, assume
U\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(S)
, and let
T\in V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(S)
. Then
T\in\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl})
and
S\subseteq T
. By monotonicity,

\operatorname{cl}(S)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(T)=T.

Since
U\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(S)
, it follows that
U\subseteq T
, so
T\in V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(U)
. Therefore
V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(S)\subseteq V_{\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{C}}}(U)
.
∎

Example 3.9

(Full space: a separating closed theory)
.

Let

\Sigma=\{p,q\}.

Let
\operatorname{cl}
be the closure operator generated by the single rule
p\vdash q
. Thus

\operatorname{cl}(\varnothing)=\varnothing,\qquad\operatorname{cl}(\{q\})=\{q\},\qquad\operatorname{cl}(\{p\})=\operatorname{cl}(\{p,q\})=\{p,q\}.

The closed theories are

T_{0}=\varnothing,\qquad T_{1}=\{q\},\qquad T_{2}=\{p,q\}.

In the full indexed truth space these three closed theories are all used as indices, and the truth regions are

V(p)=\{T_{2}\},\qquad V(q)=\{T_{1},T_{2}\}.

Hence

V(p)\subsetneq V(q),

which represents the valid closure consequence
p\vdash q
. The converse containment fails because

T_{1}\in V(q)\setminus V(p).

This single closed theory is the separating witness for
q\nvdash p
. The example is therefore the smallest useful model of the full representation theorem: entailment is region inclusion, and non-entailment is witnessed by a closed theory in the full index set.

T_{0}=\varnothing
T_{1}=\{q\}
T_{2}=\{p,q\}

V(q)
V(p)
V(p)\subsetneq V(q)
, with
T_{1}\in V(q)\setminus V(p)
.

Counterexample 3.10

(Reduced space and spurious region consequence)
.

Use the same closure system as in Example
3.9
, but omit the separating closed theory
T_{1}=\{q\}
. Thus the reduced indexed truth space has

I^{\prime}=\{T_{0},T_{2}\}=\{\varnothing,\{p,q\}\}.

Inside this reduced space,

V_{I^{\prime}}(p)=\{T_{2}\}=V_{I^{\prime}}(q).

Consequently

\{q\}\models_{I^{\prime}}p.

This is a
spurious region consequence
: it is true as a statement about the reduced index family, but it is not a closure consequence of the underlying system, since

\{q\}\nvdash p.

The reason is exactly visible: the omitted closed theory
T_{1}=\{q\}
is the witness that
q
can hold without
p
. Thus reduced indexed spaces can collapse distinct truth regions and create false local entailments relative to the original closure operator.

Remark 3.11

(Transition to decision data)
.

This section studies positive truth regions only. It does not yet compare a sentence with a designated opposite, nor does it impose consistency or inconsistency conditions on indexed theories. The next section adds exactly this bookkeeping layer. The result remains finite and structural: truth regions are still subsets of the chosen index set, not open sets in an assumed topology.

4.
Decision Decompositions

The representation theorem above concerns positive occurrence of sentences in indexed closed theories. To discuss decision status one must choose, in addition, which sentence is being treated as the opposite of a given sentence. This section adds only that bookkeeping layer. It is auxiliary to the later atlas theorem: the proof of the local-to-global obstruction criterion uses closure propagation and conservative restriction, not decision signatures or decision distances. The point of the section is more limited and more precise: truth-region language should not silently import a semantics of negation.

Accordingly, the notation below is intentionally weak. A designated symbol
\bar{a}
is simply another sentence whose truth region is compared with the truth region of
a
. Any exclusivity, inconsistency, explosion, or classical behavior must come either from the closure operator itself or from an explicitly imposed condition.

Definition 4.1

(Formal opposites and four-region decision decomposition)
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}=(\Sigma,\operatorname{cl},I,\tau)
be a finite indexed truth space. For a chosen sentence
a\in\Sigma
, a
formal opposite
of
a
is a designated sentence
\bar{a}\in\Sigma
. No inference rule relating
a
and
\bar{a}
is assumed unless it is explicitly present in the closure operator or imposed as an additional condition.

Given such a pair
(a,\bar{a})
, define

P_{a}=V_{\mathfrak{X}}(a)\setminus V_{\mathfrak{X}}(\bar{a}),\qquad N_{a}=V_{\mathfrak{X}}(\bar{a})\setminus V_{\mathfrak{X}}(a),

B_{a}=V_{\mathfrak{X}}(a)\cap V_{\mathfrak{X}}(\bar{a}),\qquad U_{a}=I\setminus\bigl(V_{\mathfrak{X}}(a)\cup V_{\mathfrak{X}}(\bar{a})\bigr).

The tuple

\mathcal{D}_{4}^{\mathfrak{X}}(a)=(P_{a},N_{a},B_{a},U_{a})

is the
four-region decision decomposition
of
a
in
\mathfrak{X}
. The four regions record, respectively, the contexts containing
a
only, containing
\bar{a}
only, containing both, and containing neither. When
\mathfrak{X}
is clear from context, we write simply
\mathcal{D}_{4}(a)
.

Definition 4.2

(Local opposite-separation)
.

The indexed truth space
\mathfrak{X}
is
opposite-separated at
a
if

B_{a}=\varnothing.

Equivalently, no indexed closed theory contains both
a
and
\bar{a}
. For a family of formal-opposite pairs,
\mathfrak{X}
is
opposite-separated
if this condition holds for every pair under consideration.

Remark 4.3

(Partition and the opposite-separated reduction)
.

The definitions give the disjoint partition

I=P_{a}\sqcup N_{a}\sqcup B_{a}\sqcup U_{a},

and

V_{\mathfrak{X}}(a)=P_{a}\sqcup B_{a},\qquad V_{\mathfrak{X}}(\bar{a})=N_{a}\sqcup B_{a}.

The four-region decomposition can be displayed as the following membership table:

\bar{a}\in\tau(i)

\bar{a}\notin\tau(i)

a\in\tau(i)

\begin{array}[]{c}B_{a}\\[-2.0pt]
\scriptstyle a\text{ and }\bar{a}\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{c}P_{a}\\[-2.0pt]
\scriptstyle a\text{ only}\end{array}

a\notin\tau(i)

\begin{array}[]{c}N_{a}\\[-2.0pt]
\scriptstyle\bar{a}\text{ only}\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{c}U_{a}\\[-2.0pt]
\scriptstyle\text{neither}\end{array}

When
\mathfrak{X}
is opposite-separated at
a
, the region
B_{a}
is empty,
so the decomposition reduces to the usual positive–negative–undecided
trichotomy.

Definition 4.4

(Optional finite summaries)
.

The
four-valued decision state function
of
a
is

\delta_{a}^{(4)}:I\to\{+,-,\pm,0\},\qquad\delta_{a}^{(4)}(i)=\begin{cases}+&i\in P_{a},\\
-&i\in N_{a},\\
\pm&i\in B_{a},\\
0&i\in U_{a}.\end{cases}

The symbol
\pm
records simultaneous occurrence of
a
and
\bar{a}
in the assigned closed theory. It does not specify any paraconsistent or explosive inference rule.

The
four-region decision signature
of
a
is

\sigma_{4}(a)=\bigl(|P_{a}|,|N_{a}|,|B_{a}|,|U_{a}|\bigr).

If
I\neq\varnothing
, its normalized version is

\bar{\sigma}_{4}(a)=\left(\frac{|P_{a}|}{|I|},\frac{|N_{a}|}{|I|},\frac{|B_{a}|}{|I|},\frac{|U_{a}|}{|I|}\right).

For selected sentences
a,b\in\Sigma
with designated formal opposites
\bar{a},\bar{b}
, define

d_{4}(a,b)=\bigl|\{i\in I:\delta_{a}^{(4)}(i)\neq\delta_{b}^{(4)}(i)\}\bigr|.

These are finite summaries of
\mathcal{D}_{4}
, not replacements for the underlying regions. They are not used in the obstruction theorem.

Remark 4.5

(Pseudometric status of
d_{4}
)
.

The quantity
d_{4}
is the ordinary Hamming distance between the state functions
\delta_{a}^{(4)}
and
\delta_{b}^{(4)}
, pulled back along the assignment
a\mapsto\delta_{a}^{(4)}
. Hence it is a pseudometric on the selected sentences equipped with formal opposites. It may vanish on distinct sentences, and

d_{4}(a,b)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\delta_{a}^{(4)}=\delta_{b}^{(4)}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathcal{D}_{4}(a)=\mathcal{D}_{4}(b).

This is a bookkeeping fact, not a structural input to the atlas construction.

Example 4.6

(A nonempty both-region)
.

Let
\Sigma=\{a,\bar{a}\}
, and let
\operatorname{cl}(S)=S
be the identity closure. The full indexed truth space has one index for each subset of
\Sigma
:

I=\mathcal{P}(\Sigma)=\{\varnothing,\{a\},\{\bar{a}\},\{a,\bar{a}\}\}.

The truth regions are

V(a)=\{\{a\},\{a,\bar{a}\}\},\qquad V(\bar{a})=\{\{\bar{a}\},\{a,\bar{a}\}\}.

Therefore

P_{a}=\{\{a\}\},\quad N_{a}=\{\{\bar{a}\}\},\quad B_{a}=\{\{a,\bar{a}\}\},\quad U_{a}=\{\varnothing\}.

The both-region is nonempty because neither the closure system nor the index family prevents simultaneous occurrence of the two designated sentences.

Example 4.7

(Signatures do not determine decompositions)
.

Let
\Sigma=\{a,\bar{a},b,\bar{b}\}
with identity closure, and take two indices
i_{1},i_{2}
with

\tau(i_{1})=\{a,\bar{b}\},\qquad\tau(i_{2})=\{\bar{a},b\}.

For
a
, one has
P_{a}=\{i_{1}\}
and
N_{a}=\{i_{2}\}
. For
b
, one has
P_{b}=\{i_{2}\}
and
N_{b}=\{i_{1}\}
. In both cases the both-region and undecided region are empty, so

\sigma_{4}(a)=\sigma_{4}(b)=(1,1,0,0).

Nevertheless the two state functions disagree at both indices, and therefore
d_{4}(a,b)=2
. Thus the signature records only the sizes of the four regions; it does not determine their positions in the common index set.

Remark 4.8

(Role of decision decompositions)
.

The essential content of this section is the four-region partition. The optional summaries above can be useful when comparing finite region-level behavior inside a fixed indexed truth space, but they are secondary to the actual regions and play no role in the proof of the local-to-global obstruction criterion. With this bookkeeping separated from closure propagation, the next section turns to conservative comparison between finite closure systems.

5.
Conservative Extensions and Truth-Region Pullback

The previous sections treated the internal structure of a single indexed truth space. We now record the comparison principle needed before several local systems can be assembled into an atlas. The intended situation is that a larger language may introduce new sentences and new consequences involving those sentences, while leaving the consequences expressible in an older language unchanged.

This is the role of conservative extension. In full truth spaces, conservativity has a precise truth-region form: old truth regions in the larger system are exact inverse images of old truth regions in the smaller system under the reduct map. This one-extension result will become the chart-by-chart requirement in the atlas theorem.

Definition 5.1

(Conservative extension of finite closure systems)
.

Let

\mathcal{C}_{X}=(\Sigma_{X},\operatorname{cl}_{X}),\qquad\mathcal{C}_{Y}=(\Sigma_{Y},\operatorname{cl}_{Y})

be finite closure systems with
\Sigma_{X}\subseteq\Sigma_{Y}
. We say that
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
is a
conservative extension
of
\mathcal{C}_{X}
if, for every
S\subseteq\Sigma_{X}
,

\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(S)\cap\Sigma_{X}=\operatorname{cl}_{X}(S).

Thus closing an old-language set inside the larger system and then restricting back to the old language gives exactly the same old consequences as closing it inside the old system.

Lemma 5.2

(Preservation of old consequence)
.

If
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
is a conservative extension of
\mathcal{C}_{X}
, then for every
S\subseteq\Sigma_{X}
and every
\varphi\in\Sigma_{X}
,

S\vdash_{\mathcal{C}_{X}}\varphi\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\vdash_{\mathcal{C}_{Y}}\varphi.

Proof.

By definition,

S\vdash_{\mathcal{C}_{X}}\varphi\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\varphi\in\operatorname{cl}_{X}(S).

Since the extension is conservative,

\operatorname{cl}_{X}(S)=\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(S)\cap\Sigma_{X}.

Because
\varphi\in\Sigma_{X}
, membership in
\operatorname{cl}_{X}(S)
is equivalent to membership in
\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(S)
. This is exactly the asserted equivalence.
∎

For
T\subseteq\Sigma_{Y}
, write

T\upharpoonright_{X}:=T\cap\Sigma_{X}

for its old-language reduct. This is only set-theoretic restriction; the following proposition explains why it sends closed theories to closed theories under conservativity.

Lemma 5.3

(Reducts of closed theories are closed)
.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
is a conservative extension of
\mathcal{C}_{X}
. If

T\in\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}_{Y}),

then

T\cap\Sigma_{X}\in\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}_{X}).

Proof.

Let
T_{X}=T\cap\Sigma_{X}
. We show that
\operatorname{cl}_{X}(T_{X})=T_{X}
. By conservativity,

\operatorname{cl}_{X}(T_{X})=\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(T_{X})\cap\Sigma_{X}.

Since
T_{X}\subseteq T
, monotonicity gives

\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(T_{X})\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(T)=T,

because
T
is
\operatorname{cl}_{Y}
-closed. Therefore

\operatorname{cl}_{X}(T_{X})=\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(T_{X})\cap\Sigma_{X}\subseteq T\cap\Sigma_{X}=T_{X}.

The reverse inclusion follows from extensiveness of
\operatorname{cl}_{X}
. Hence
\operatorname{cl}_{X}(T_{X})=T_{X}
, so
T_{X}
is
\operatorname{cl}_{X}
-closed.
∎

Definition 5.4

(Reduct map on full truth spaces)
.

Let

\mathfrak{F}_{X}=(\Sigma_{X},\operatorname{cl}_{X},\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}_{X}),\mathrm{id}),\qquad\mathfrak{F}_{Y}=(\Sigma_{Y},\operatorname{cl}_{Y},\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}_{Y}),\mathrm{id})

be the full indexed truth spaces of
\mathcal{C}_{X}
and
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
. If
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
is conservative over
\mathcal{C}_{X}
, define

\rho:\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}_{Y})\to\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}_{X}),\qquad\rho(T)=T\cap\Sigma_{X}.

This map is well-defined by Lemma
5.3
.

Proposition 5.5

(The reduct map is surjective)
.

If
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
is a conservative extension of
\mathcal{C}_{X}
, then the reduct map

\rho:\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}_{Y})\to\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}_{X})

is surjective.

Proof.

Let
C\in\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}_{X})
. Define

\widehat{C}=\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(C).

Then
\widehat{C}\in\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}_{Y})
by idempotence. Its reduct is

\rho(\widehat{C})=\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(C)\cap\Sigma_{X}.

By conservativity,

\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(C)\cap\Sigma_{X}=\operatorname{cl}_{X}(C).

Since
C
is
\operatorname{cl}_{X}
-closed,
\operatorname{cl}_{X}(C)=C
. Hence
\rho(\widehat{C})=C
. Therefore every old closed theory is the reduct of some new closed theory.
∎

Theorem 5.6

(Truth-region pullback under conservative extension)
.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
is a conservative extension of
\mathcal{C}_{X}
, and let

\rho:\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}_{Y})\to\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}_{X})

be the reduct map. For every old-language sentence
\varphi\in\Sigma_{X}
,

V_{\mathfrak{F}_{Y}}(\varphi)=\rho^{-1}\bigl(V_{\mathfrak{F}_{X}}(\varphi)\bigr).

More generally, for every
S\subseteq\Sigma_{X}
,

V_{\mathfrak{F}_{Y}}(S)=\rho^{-1}\bigl(V_{\mathfrak{F}_{X}}(S)\bigr).

Proof.

Let
T\in\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}_{Y})
. First let
\varphi\in\Sigma_{X}
. Then

T\in V_{\mathfrak{F}_{Y}}(\varphi)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\varphi\in T.

Since
\varphi\in\Sigma_{X}
, this is equivalent to

\varphi\in T\cap\Sigma_{X}=\rho(T).

Equivalently,

T\in V_{\mathfrak{F}_{Y}}(\varphi)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\rho(T)\in V_{\mathfrak{F}_{X}}(\varphi).

This proves the sentence case.

For
S\subseteq\Sigma_{X}
, the same argument gives

T\in V_{\mathfrak{F}_{Y}}(S)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\subseteq T\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\subseteq T\cap\Sigma_{X}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\rho(T)\in V_{\mathfrak{F}_{X}}(S).

Thus
V_{\mathfrak{F}_{Y}}(S)=\rho^{-1}(V_{\mathfrak{F}_{X}}(S))
.
∎

Corollary 5.7

(Old full region consequence is preserved)
.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
is a conservative extension of
\mathcal{C}_{X}
. For old-language
S\subseteq\Sigma_{X}
and
\varphi\in\Sigma_{X}
,

V_{\mathfrak{F}_{X}}(S)\subseteq V_{\mathfrak{F}_{X}}(\varphi)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V_{\mathfrak{F}_{Y}}(S)\subseteq V_{\mathfrak{F}_{Y}}(\varphi).

Proof.

By Theorem
3.8
, the first truth-region inclusion is equivalent to
S\vdash_{\mathcal{C}_{X}}\varphi
, and the second is equivalent to
S\vdash_{\mathcal{C}_{Y}}\varphi
. These two consequence statements are equivalent by Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Example 5.8

(Conservative extension adding only a new-language consequence)
.

Let
\Sigma_{X}=\{p\}
, with identity closure
\operatorname{cl}_{X}(S)=S
. Let
\Sigma_{Y}=\{p,q\}
, and define
\operatorname{cl}_{Y}
by the single rule
p\vdash_{Y}q
. Thus

\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(\varnothing)=\varnothing,\qquad\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(\{q\})=\{q\},\qquad\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(\{p\})=\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(\{p,q\})=\{p,q\}.

For every
S\subseteq\Sigma_{X}
, closing in the larger system and restricting back to
\Sigma_{X}
gives

\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(S)\cap\Sigma_{X}=S=\operatorname{cl}_{X}(S).

Hence
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
is conservative over
\mathcal{C}_{X}
, even though it adds the new-language consequence
p\vdash_{Y}q
.

The full closed theories of
\mathcal{C}_{X}
are
\varnothing
and
\{p\}
. The full closed theories of
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
are

\varnothing,\qquad\{q\},\qquad\{p,q\}.

The reduct map is

\rho(\varnothing)=\varnothing,\qquad\rho(\{q\})=\varnothing,\qquad\rho(\{p,q\})=\{p\}.

Equivalently, its fibers are summarized by

T\in\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}_{Y})

\rho(T)\in\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{cl}_{X})

\varnothing

\mapsto

\varnothing

\{q\}

\mapsto

\varnothing

\{p,q\}

\mapsto

\{p\}

For the old sentence
p
,

V_{\mathfrak{F}_{X}}(p)=\{\{p\}\},\qquad V_{\mathfrak{F}_{Y}}(p)=\{\{p,q\}\}.

Thus

V_{\mathfrak{F}_{Y}}(p)=\rho^{-1}\bigl(V_{\mathfrak{F}_{X}}(p)\bigr).

This illustrates the point of conservativity: new sentences and new mixed-language consequences may appear, but old-language truth regions pull back exactly.

Counterexample 5.9

(A nonconservative extension creates an old consequence)
.

Let
\Sigma_{X}=\{p,q\}
with identity closure, and let
\Sigma_{Y}=\Sigma_{X}
with closure generated by the rule
p\vdash_{Y}q
. Then

\operatorname{cl}_{X}(\{p\})=\{p\},\qquad\operatorname{cl}_{Y}(\{p\})\cap\Sigma_{X}=\{p,q\}.

So
\mathcal{C}_{Y}
is not conservative over
\mathcal{C}_{X}
. The old-language consequence

p\vdash_{Y}q

has been created by the extension, although

p\nvdash_{X}q.

Equivalently, the extension has created the old truth-region containment corresponding to
p\vdash q
. This is precisely the behavior excluded by the pullback theorem.

Remark 5.10

(Cardinality is not preserved by pullback)
.

The equality

V_{\mathfrak{F}_{Y}}(\varphi)=\rho^{-1}(V_{\mathfrak{F}_{X}}(\varphi))

preserves the old truth region structurally, but it need not preserve cardinal summaries. The reduct map may have fibers of different sizes. In Example
5.8
,

\rho^{-1}(\varnothing)=\{\varnothing,\{q\}\}\quad\text{but}\quad\rho^{-1}(\{p\})=\{\{p,q\}\}.

Thus cardinality-based summaries, including decision signatures, depend on the ambient indexed space or on an explicit measure on it.

Remark 5.11

(Scope of the comparison theory)
.

General refinement maps between selected indexed families are useful for comparing chosen subspaces, but the present manuscript only needs the closure-level result above: conservative extension preserves old consequence and induces exact pullback on old truth regions in full truth spaces. The next section replaces the single inclusion
\Sigma_{X}\subseteq\Sigma_{Y}
by several overlapping chart languages. Conservative realization will then mean that every chart is preserved in the same sense simultaneously.

6.
Closure Atlases and Atlas-Generated Closure

We now pass from a single conservative extension to a finite family of local closure systems. The purpose is to study when these local consequence relations can be regarded as restrictions of one global consequence relation. The local systems may have overlapping sentence universes, and their interactions can force consequences not visible from any one chart alone. This section constructs the canonical global closure generated by such interactions; the next section asks when that canonical closure is conservative over each chart.

Definition 6.1

(Finite closure atlas)
.

A
finite closure atlas
is a finite family of finite closure systems

\mathfrak{A}=\{(\Sigma_{\alpha},\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in A}.

The finite set
A
is the
chart index set
. The
global sentence universe
of the atlas is

\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in A}\Sigma_{\alpha}.

When the atlas is clear from context, we write simply
\Sigma
for
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}
. For charts
\alpha,\beta\in A
, their
overlap language
is

\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}=\Sigma_{\alpha}\cap\Sigma_{\beta}.

Remark 6.2

(No topology or prior compatibility is assumed)
.

The word “atlas” is used only to indicate a finite family of local closure systems with possibly overlapping languages. No topological structure is assumed. Moreover, the definition does not require any prior agreement on overlaps. If two charts disagree on an overlap, or if several charts jointly force a consequence that one chart denies, this will be detected later by the obstruction set.

Definition 6.3

(Atlas step operator and atlas-generated closure)
.

For
X\subseteq\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}
, define the
atlas step operator

G_{\mathfrak{A}}:\mathcal{P}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}})\to\mathcal{P}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}})

by

G_{\mathfrak{A}}(X)=X\cup\bigcup_{\alpha\in A}\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(X\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}).

Thus
G_{\mathfrak{A}}(X)
is obtained by allowing each chart
\alpha
to close the part of
X
visible in its own sentence universe
\Sigma_{\alpha}
.

Define
G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{0}(X)=X
and, recursively,

G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{n+1}(X)=G_{\mathfrak{A}}(G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{n}(X)).

Since
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}
is finite, the increasing sequence

G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{0}(X)\subseteq G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{1}(X)\subseteq G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}(X)\subseteq\cdots

stabilizes after finitely many steps. The
atlas-generated closure
of
X
is

C_{\mathfrak{A}}(X)=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{n}(X),

equivalently the stable value of the iteration of
G_{\mathfrak{A}}
starting from
X
.

Lemma 6.4

(Monotonicity of the atlas step operator)
.

If
X\subseteq Y\subseteq\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}
, then

G_{\mathfrak{A}}(X)\subseteq G_{\mathfrak{A}}(Y).

Proof.

If
X\subseteq Y
, then
X\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}\subseteq Y\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}
for each chart
\alpha
. Monotonicity of
\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}
gives

\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(X\cap\Sigma_{\alpha})\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(Y\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}),

and the defining formula for
G_{\mathfrak{A}}
then yields
G_{\mathfrak{A}}(X)\subseteq G_{\mathfrak{A}}(Y)
.
∎

Proposition 6.5

(Atlas-generated closure)
.

The map

C_{\mathfrak{A}}:\mathcal{P}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}})\to\mathcal{P}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}})

is a closure operator.

Proof.

Extensiveness is immediate from
G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{0}(X)=X
. If
X\subseteq Y
, Lemma
6.4
gives
G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{n}(X)\subseteq G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{n}(Y)
for every
n
, so taking unions gives
C_{\mathfrak{A}}(X)\subseteq C_{\mathfrak{A}}(Y)
.

For idempotence, finiteness of
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}
implies that the increasing sequence
G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{0}(X)\subseteq G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{1}(X)\subseteq\cdots
stabilizes. Choose
N
with

C_{\mathfrak{A}}(X)=G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{N}(X)=G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{N+1}(X).

Then
G_{\mathfrak{A}}(C_{\mathfrak{A}}(X))=C_{\mathfrak{A}}(X)
. Hence every iterate starting from
C_{\mathfrak{A}}(X)
is again
C_{\mathfrak{A}}(X)
, and therefore

C_{\mathfrak{A}}(C_{\mathfrak{A}}(X))=C_{\mathfrak{A}}(X).

Thus
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
is a closure operator.
∎

Definition 6.6

(Global extension of local chart closures)
.

Let

D:\mathcal{P}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}})\to\mathcal{P}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}})

be a closure operator. We say that
D

extends the local chart closures
if for every chart
\alpha\in A
and every
S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}
,

\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S)\subseteq D(S).

This condition says that every local consequence remains valid globally. It is weaker than conservative realization, which is introduced in the next section.

Theorem 6.7

(Minimality of the atlas-generated closure)
.

The atlas-generated closure
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
is the least global closure operator extending all local chart closures. More explicitly:

(i)

C_{\mathfrak{A}}
extends each local chart closure;

(ii)

if
D:\mathcal{P}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}})\to\mathcal{P}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}})
is any closure operator extending all local chart closures, then for every
X\subseteq\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}
,

C_{\mathfrak{A}}(X)\subseteq D(X).

Proof.

First fix a chart
\alpha\in A
and a local set
S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}
. Since
S\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}=S
, the definition of
G_{\mathfrak{A}}
gives

\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S)=\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S\cap\Sigma_{\alpha})\subseteq G_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)\subseteq C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S).

Thus
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
extends every local chart closure.

Now let
D
be any closure operator on
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}
extending every local chart closure. We prove by induction on
n
that

G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{n}(X)\subseteq D(X)

for every
n\geq 0
.

For
n=0
, this is just
X\subseteq D(X)
, which follows from extensiveness of
D
. Suppose the inclusion holds at stage
n
, and put

S_{\alpha}=G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{n}(X)\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}.

Since
D
extends local chart closures,

\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S_{\alpha})\subseteq D(S_{\alpha}).

By the induction hypothesis,

S_{\alpha}\subseteq G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{n}(X)\subseteq D(X).

Monotonicity and idempotence of
D
give

D(S_{\alpha})\subseteq D(D(X))=D(X).

Hence
\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S_{\alpha})\subseteq D(X)
for every
\alpha
. Combining this with
G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{n}(X)\subseteq D(X)
, we obtain

G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{n+1}(X)=G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{n}(X)\cup\bigcup_{\alpha\in A}\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S_{\alpha})\subseteq D(X).

The induction is complete. Taking the union over all
n
yields

C_{\mathfrak{A}}(X)=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{n}(X)\subseteq D(X).

Together with the first paragraph and Proposition
6.5
, this proves that
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
is the least global closure operator extending the local chart closures.
∎

Remark 6.8

(Extension versus conservative realization)
.

The minimality theorem concerns extension of local consequence:

\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S)\subseteq D(S).

A global conservative realization satisfies the stronger condition

D(S)\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}=\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S)\qquad(S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}).

Thus
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
always extends the local charts, but it may fail to be conservative over them. The obstruction set introduced in the next section measures exactly this possible failure.

(Propagation through overlapping charts without obstruction)
.

Let

Let
\operatorname{cl}_{1}

because chart

r

C_{\mathfrak{A}}(\{p\})=\{p,q,r\}.

Thus the atlas-generated closure forces the global consequence

p

Therefore
E_{\alpha,S}=\varnothing
for every chart

. Hence
\operatorname{Obs}(\mathfrak{A})=\varnothing

is itself a global conservative realization. The point is that obstruction is not propagation itself; obstruction occurs only when propagated information returns as a chart-visible consequence not already validated by that chart.

7.
Local-to-Global Obstructions

The atlas-generated closure
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
is the least global closure operator extending all local chart closures. Extension alone is not enough for a faithful globalization: a global closure may preserve every local implication while also creating a new implication whose premises and conclusion already lie in one chart language. Such a consequence is visible locally, but not locally valid. The obstruction set records exactly these chart-visible excess consequences.

Definition 7.1

(Global conservative realization)
.

Let

\mathfrak{A}=\{(\Sigma_{\alpha},\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in A}

be a finite closure atlas, with global sentence universe
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}
. A closure operator

D:\mathcal{P}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}})\to\mathcal{P}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}})

is a
global conservative realization
of
\mathfrak{A}
if for every chart
\alpha\in A
and every local premise set
S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}
,

D(S)\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}=\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S).

Equivalently, the local consequence relation of chart
\alpha
is recovered exactly by restricting the global closure back to
\Sigma_{\alpha}
. This is stronger than extension: extension requires only
\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S)\subseteq D(S)
, while conservative realization also forbids new
\Sigma_{\alpha}
-sentences from appearing in
D(S)
.

Definition 7.2

(Chart excess and obstruction set)
.

For a chart
\alpha\in A
and a local premise set
S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}
, define the
chart excess
of the atlas-generated closure over chart
\alpha
at
S
by

E_{\alpha,S}=\bigl(C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}\bigr)\setminus\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S).

Thus
E_{\alpha,S}
consists of the
\alpha
-visible consequences that are forced by propagation through the whole atlas but are not consequences in chart
\alpha
itself. The
obstruction set
is

\operatorname{Obs}(\mathfrak{A})=\{(\alpha,S,\varphi):S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha},\ \varphi\in E_{\alpha,S}\}.

Equivalently,
(\alpha,S,\varphi)\in\operatorname{Obs}(\mathfrak{A})
iff

S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha},\qquad\varphi\in\Sigma_{\alpha},\qquad\varphi\in C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S),\qquad\varphi\notin\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S).

The theorem below is the technical core of the manuscript. The point is not merely that obstructions prevent conservative globalization. The canonical closure
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
is minimal among all global extensions, so every possible global realization must contain the consequences generated by
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
. Therefore any chart-visible excess produced by
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
is unavoidable. Conversely, if
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
produces no such excess, then the canonical closure itself already realizes the atlas conservatively.

Theorem 7.3

(Local-to-global obstruction criterion)
.

For a finite closure atlas
\mathfrak{A}
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathfrak{A}
has a global conservative realization;

(ii)

the atlas-generated closure
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
is a global conservative realization of
\mathfrak{A}
;

(iii)

\operatorname{Obs}(\mathfrak{A})=\varnothing
.

Equivalently, conservative globalizability is exactly the condition that

C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}=\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S)

for every chart
\alpha\in A
and every local set
S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

The implication
\textup{(ii)}\Rightarrow\textup{(i)}
is immediate. We prove
\textup{(i)}\Rightarrow\textup{(iii)}
and
\textup{(iii)}\Rightarrow\textup{(ii)}
.

Assume first that
D
is a global conservative realization. Then for each
S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}
,

\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S)=D(S)\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}\subseteq D(S),

so
D
extends every local chart closure. By the minimality of
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
,

C_{\mathfrak{A}}(X)\subseteq D(X)

for every
X\subseteq\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}
.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
(\alpha,S,\varphi)\in\operatorname{Obs}(\mathfrak{A})
. Then
S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}
,
\varphi\in\Sigma_{\alpha}
,
\varphi\in C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)
, and
\varphi\notin\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S)
. Since
C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)\subseteq D(S)
, we have

\varphi\in D(S)\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}.

Conservativity of
D
over chart
\alpha
gives

D(S)\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}=\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S),

contradicting
\varphi\notin\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S)
. Hence
\operatorname{Obs}(\mathfrak{A})=\varnothing
.

Conversely, assume
\operatorname{Obs}(\mathfrak{A})=\varnothing
. By Proposition
6.5
,
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
is a closure operator on
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}
. It remains only to prove conservative restriction. Fix
\alpha\in A
and
S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}
. Since
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
extends the local chart closures,

\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S)\subseteq C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}.

For the reverse inclusion, if
\varphi\in C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}
but
\varphi\notin\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S)
, then
(\alpha,S,\varphi)\in\operatorname{Obs}(\mathfrak{A})
, contradicting emptiness. Therefore

C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S).

The two inclusions give

C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}=\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S)

for every chart
\alpha
and every local premise set
S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}
. Thus
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
is a global conservative realization.
∎

Corollary 7.4

(Finite obstruction algorithm)
.

There is a finite algorithm which, given a finite closure atlas
\mathfrak{A}
, computes
\operatorname{Obs}(\mathfrak{A})
. For each chart
\alpha\in A
and each local subset
S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}
, compute
C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)
by iterating
G_{\mathfrak{A}}
, form

E_{\alpha,S}=\bigl(C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}\bigr)\setminus\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(S),

and output all triples
(\alpha,S,\varphi)
with
\varphi\in E_{\alpha,S}
. The resulting finite set is exactly
\operatorname{Obs}(\mathfrak{A})
. Consequently,
\mathfrak{A}
has a global conservative realization if and only if the algorithm returns the empty set.

Proof.

There are finitely many charts, and each
\Sigma_{\alpha}
has finitely many subsets. For fixed
\alpha
and
S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}
, the sequence

S=G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{0}(S)\subseteq G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{1}(S)\subseteq G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}(S)\subseteq\cdots

is increasing and contained in the finite set
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}
. It therefore stabilizes after finitely many steps, and its stable value is
C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)
. The formula for
E_{\alpha,S}
then lists exactly the chart-visible excess consequences at
(\alpha,S)
. Taking all charts and all local premise sets therefore outputs precisely
\operatorname{Obs}(\mathfrak{A})
. The final equivalence follows from Theorem
7.3
.
∎

Remark 7.5

(Pseudocode form)
.

The finite procedure can be written as follows.

O := empty set
Sigma := union_alpha Sigma_alpha

for each alpha in A:
for each S subset Sigma_alpha:
X := S
repeat:
Y := X union union_beta cl_beta(X intersection Sigma_beta)
if Y = X:
break
X := Y
C := X

E := (C intersection Sigma_alpha) minus cl_alpha(S)
for each phi in E:
O := O union {(alpha,S,phi)}

return O

The fixed point
X
reached by the loop is
C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)
.

Remark 7.6

(Finite complexity)
.

Let
m=|\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}|
and
m_{\alpha}=|\Sigma_{\alpha}|
. The algorithm tests

\sum_{\alpha\in A}2^{m_{\alpha}}

local subsets. For each tested subset
S
, the iteration of
G_{\mathfrak{A}}
stabilizes after at most
m-|S|\leq m
strict growth stages. Thus the number of global iteration rounds is bounded by

m\sum_{\alpha\in A}2^{m_{\alpha}}.

This is a finite decidability bound, not an efficiency claim.

Example 7.7

(A chart-visible obstruction from pairwise compatible overlaps)
.

This example adds a third chart to the propagation pattern above. The added chart makes the propagated conclusion visible locally while refusing it locally.

Let the global sentence set be
\{p,q,r\}
, and consider three charts

\Sigma_{pq}=\{p,q\},\qquad\Sigma_{qr}=\{q,r\},\qquad\Sigma_{pr}=\{p,r\}.

Let
\operatorname{cl}_{pq}
be generated by
p\vdash_{pq}q
, let
\operatorname{cl}_{qr}
be generated by
q\vdash_{qr}r
, and let
\operatorname{cl}_{pr}
be identity closure on
\{p,r\}
. Thus the
pr
-chart explicitly has

p\nvdash_{pr}r.

The pairwise overlaps are singleton languages:

\Sigma_{pq}\cap\Sigma_{qr}=\{q\},\qquad\Sigma_{qr}\cap\Sigma_{pr}=\{r\},\qquad\Sigma_{pq}\cap\Sigma_{pr}=\{p\}.

On these singleton overlaps there is no direct pairwise disagreement: restricting either adjacent chart to the overlap gives the identity closure on the singleton language. Nevertheless, the atlas has no global conservative realization.

Indeed, compute the atlas-generated closure of
S=\{p\}\subseteq\Sigma_{pr}
. The
pq
-chart first adds
q
:

G_{\mathfrak{A}}(\{p\})=\{p,q\}.

Then the
qr
-chart adds
r
:

G_{\mathfrak{A}}^{2}(\{p\})=\{p,q,r\}.

Thus

C_{\mathfrak{A}}(\{p\})=\{p,q,r\}.

Restricting this global closure back to the
pr
-chart gives

C_{\mathfrak{A}}(\{p\})\cap\Sigma_{pr}=\{p,r\},

whereas locally

\operatorname{cl}_{pr}(\{p\})=\{p\}.

Therefore

r\in C_{\mathfrak{A}}(\{p\})\qquad\text{but}\qquad r\notin\operatorname{cl}_{pr}(\{p\}).

So

(pr,\{p\},r)\in\operatorname{Obs}(\mathfrak{A}).

By Theorem
7.3
, no global conservative realization exists. This example shows why pairwise overlap agreement is weaker than the obstruction criterion: the failure appears only after propagation through more than one chart.

Remark 7.8

(Pairwise compatibility is insufficient)
.

In the preceding example, no pair of charts directly disagrees on its overlap. The failure is caused by the interaction

p\vdash_{pq}q,\qquad q\vdash_{qr}r.

Any global closure extending the first two charts must satisfy
p\vdash r
. But the third chart, whose language contains
p
and
r
, requires
p\nvdash_{pr}r
. The obstruction is therefore genuinely local-to-global: it appears only after consequences are propagated through the atlas.

Remark 7.9

(Premise sets in obstructions)
.

The obstruction set permits arbitrary finite local premise sets
S\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}
. This is intentional. Multi-premise local consequences can produce chart-visible failures not detected by singleton-premise paths. Theorem
7.3
gives the closure-level realization problem for finite closure atlases; the next section treats the separate problem of gluing selected local closed theories.

8.
Compatible Local Closed Theories

The obstruction theorem concerns closure systems: it asks when the local consequence relations of an atlas can be recovered as conservative restrictions of a single global closure relation. We now turn to a related but simpler gluing problem for selected local closed theories. Here the canonical closure
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
is fixed in advance, and the question is whether local closed theories can be represented by a single global closed set. Exact agreement on overlaps, together with local closedness, is enough.

Definition 8.1

(Compatible local closed theories and canonical union)
.

Let

\mathfrak{A}=\{(\Sigma_{\alpha},\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in A}

be a finite closure atlas. A
local closed-theory family
on
\mathfrak{A}
is a tuple

\mathbf{T}=(T_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}

such that
T_{\alpha}\subseteq\Sigma_{\alpha}
and
\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(T_{\alpha})=T_{\alpha}
for every
\alpha\in A
.

The family is
overlap-compatible
if, for every pair
\alpha,\beta\in A
,

T_{\alpha}\cap\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}=T_{\beta}\cap\Sigma_{\alpha\beta},

where
\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}=\Sigma_{\alpha}\cap\Sigma_{\beta}
. Thus every sentence visible in two charts has the same membership status in the corresponding local theories.

For such a family, its
canonical union
is

T^{\cup}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in A}T_{\alpha}\subseteq\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}.

If a global set
T\subseteq\Sigma_{\mathfrak{A}}
restricts exactly to every
T_{\alpha}
, then necessarily
T=T^{\cup}
. Hence
T^{\cup}
is the only possible underlying set of an exact global representative of the local family.

Counterexample 8.2

(Overlap incompatibility prevents exact gluing)
.

Let

\Sigma_{1}=\{p,q\},\qquad\Sigma_{2}=\{q,r\},

with identity closures on both charts. Take local closed theories

T_{1}=\{p,q\},\qquad T_{2}=\{r\}.

Both are locally closed, but they disagree on the overlap
\Sigma_{12}=\{q\}
:

T_{1}\cap\Sigma_{12}=\{q\},\qquad T_{2}\cap\Sigma_{12}=\varnothing.

No global set
T\subseteq\{p,q,r\}
can restrict exactly to both local theories. If
T\cap\Sigma_{1}=T_{1}
, then
q\in T
; if
T\cap\Sigma_{2}=T_{2}
, then
q\notin T
. Thus overlap compatibility is not a decorative hypothesis: it is necessary even before any closure operation is applied.

Lemma 8.3

(Union-restriction lemma)
.

If
\mathbf{T}=(T_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
is overlap-compatible, then for every
\alpha\in A
,

T^{\cup}\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}=T_{\alpha}.

Proof.

The inclusion
T_{\alpha}\subseteq T^{\cup}\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}
is immediate. Conversely, let
\varphi\in T^{\cup}\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}
. Since
\varphi\in T^{\cup}
, there is some
\beta\in A
such that
\varphi\in T_{\beta}
. Because
T_{\beta}\subseteq\Sigma_{\beta}
and
\varphi\in\Sigma_{\alpha}
, we have
\varphi\in\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}
. By overlap compatibility,

T_{\alpha}\cap\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}=T_{\beta}\cap\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}.

Thus
\varphi\in T_{\alpha}
. Hence
T^{\cup}\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}\subseteq T_{\alpha}
, proving the equality.
∎

Theorem 8.4

(Canonical gluing theorem)
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a finite closure atlas, and let
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
be its atlas-generated closure. If
\mathbf{T}=(T_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
is an overlap-compatible local closed-theory family, then

T^{\cup}\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}=T_{\alpha}

for every
\alpha\in A
, and

C_{\mathfrak{A}}(T^{\cup})=T^{\cup}.

Thus compatible local closed theories glue exactly under the canonical atlas-generated closure.

Proof.

The restriction identity is Lemma
8.3
. It remains to prove that
T^{\cup}
is
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
-closed. By the same lemma,

T^{\cup}\cap\Sigma_{\alpha}=T_{\alpha}

for every
\alpha
. Since each local theory is closed,

\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(T^{\cup}\cap\Sigma_{\alpha})=\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(T_{\alpha})=T_{\alpha}.

Therefore the atlas step operator satisfies

G_{\mathfrak{A}}(T^{\cup})=T^{\cup}\cup\bigcup_{\alpha\in A}\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha}(T^{\cup}\cap\Sigma_{\alpha})=T^{\cup}\cup\bigcup_{\alpha\in A}T_{\alpha}=T^{\cup}.

Thus
T^{\cup}
is fixed by
G_{\mathfrak{A}}
. All subsequent iterates are again
T^{\cup}
, so the stable value defining
C_{\mathfrak{A}}(T^{\cup})
is
T^{\cup}
itself.
∎

Remark 8.5

(Relation to the obstruction theorem)
.

Theorem
8.4
does not require
\operatorname{Obs}(\mathfrak{A})=\varnothing
. It is a statement about the closure operator
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
, which exists for every finite closure atlas. If the obstruction set is empty, then Theorem
7.3
says that
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
is also a global conservative realization of the atlas. In that case the compatible local family glues into a closed theory of the canonical conservative realization. If the obstruction set is nonempty, the same set
T^{\cup}
is still closed under
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
, but
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
is not conservative over all charts.

Example 8.6

(Successful canonical gluing)
.

Let

\Sigma_{1}=\{p,q\},\qquad\Sigma_{2}=\{q,r\}.

Let
\operatorname{cl}_{1}
be generated by
p\vdash_{1}q
, and let
\operatorname{cl}_{2}
be generated by
q\vdash_{2}r
. Choose local closed theories

T_{1}=\{p,q\},\qquad T_{2}=\{q,r\}.

They are closed because
T_{1}
already contains the
\operatorname{cl}_{1}
-consequence of
p
, and
T_{2}
already contains the
\operatorname{cl}_{2}
-consequence of
q
. The overlap language is

\Sigma_{12}=\{q\},

and both local theories restrict to
\{q\}
on that overlap. Hence the family is overlap-compatible.

The canonical union is

T^{\cup}=T_{1}\cup T_{2}=\{p,q,r\}.

By the union-restriction lemma,

T^{\cup}\cap\Sigma_{1}=T_{1},\qquad T^{\cup}\cap\Sigma_{2}=T_{2}.

Moreover, each chart sees a closed local theory when it restricts
T^{\cup}
to its own language, so one atlas step adds nothing:

G_{\mathfrak{A}}(T^{\cup})=T^{\cup}.

Therefore

C_{\mathfrak{A}}(T^{\cup})=T^{\cup}.

The example displays the exact content of the gluing theorem: compatibility gives the prescribed restrictions, and local closedness makes the canonical union globally closed under
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
.

Remark 8.7

(No second canonical obstruction)
.

Theorem
8.4
is elementary. Its role is to separate two questions. The nontrivial obstruction problem is the closure-level problem of Section
7
: whether local consequence relations globalize conservatively. Once the canonical closure
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
has been fixed, exactly overlap-compatible closed local theories have no further canonical gluing obstruction. Stronger questions about gluing under larger noncanonical conservative realizations lead to the conservative-completion problems mentioned in the outlook, but they are not part of the present paper.

9.
Discussion, Limitations, and Outlook

The preceding sections establish a finite closure-theoretic framework for comparing local and global closure data. The main object is a finite closure atlas

\mathfrak{A}=\{(\Sigma_{\alpha},\operatorname{cl}_{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in A},

whose chart closures propagate through overlaps by the step operator
G_{\mathfrak{A}}
. Iterating this operator produces the atlas-generated closure
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
, the least global closure operator extending the local chart closures.

The main theorem gives an exact finite answer to the conservative-globalization problem. The obstruction set records precisely where this least global propagation creates a chart-visible consequence not already present in the corresponding chart. The local-to-global obstruction criterion proves the equivalence

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}\text{ has a global conservative realization}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow C_{\mathfrak{A}}\text{ is a global conservative realization}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\operatorname{Obs}(\mathfrak{A})=\varnothing.

Thus, in the finite setting considered here, there is no hidden obstruction beyond the chart-visible excess detected by
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
. The criterion is also effective: one computes
C_{\mathfrak{A}}(S)
for finite chart-local premise sets and compares the result with the relevant local closure.

The indexed truth-space layer records one interpretation of the same closure data. An indexed truth space represents an element
\varphi
by its region

V_{\mathfrak{X}}(\varphi)=\{i\in I:\varphi\in\tau(i)\}

inside a selected finite family of closed theories. The full truth-region representation theorem shows that, when every closed theory is used as an index, region inclusion recovers the original closure consequence relation exactly. Reduced indexed spaces are therefore not neutral summaries: by omitting separating closed theories, they may validate region consequences that are not consequences of the underlying closure operator.

The decision-bookkeeping layer is also separate from the atlas obstruction theorem. For an element
a
and a designated formal opposite
\bar{a}
, the general decomposition is the four-region partition

I=P_{a}\sqcup N_{a}\sqcup B_{a}\sqcup U_{a},

where the both-region
B_{a}=V(a)\cap V(\bar{a})
records indices whose assigned closed theories contain both designated elements. This is only membership data in the chosen indexed space. It is not a semantics of negation, and it does not by itself assert anything about contradiction, paraconsistency, or classical negation.

The gluing theorem is deliberately separate from the closure-level obstruction. Once
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
has been fixed, overlap-compatible local closed theories glue by ordinary union:

T^{\cup}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in A}T_{\alpha}.

Compatibility guarantees that
T^{\cup}
restricts back to the prescribed local theories, and local closedness guarantees

C_{\mathfrak{A}}(T^{\cup})=T^{\cup}.

Thus exact compatible local closed theories have no further canonical gluing obstruction. The nontrivial obstruction studied in the paper lies in conservative globalization of local consequence relations, not in the final union step for already compatible closed local data.

Limitations and nonclaims

The framework is intentionally finite. Infinite universes or infinite atlases would require additional hypotheses, transfinite iteration, or a different construction of least global closure. None of those extensions is treated here. The paper also does not define a topology of truth regions, even though the terminology of regions suggests possible later geometric or topological refinements. At this stage, truth regions are subsets of a finite index set, and closure atlases are finite combinatorial objects.

The notation
\bar{a}
denotes a designated formal opposite, not full logical negation unless extra structure is supplied. Likewise, the both-region
B_{a}
should not be read as a claim about contradiction or about which logic of contradiction is appropriate. The paper deliberately avoids using paradoxes or independence phenomena as arguments against ordinary classical reasoning. Nothing proved here implies that classical logic is false, that the law of excluded middle fails globally, or that standard foundations are invalid.

The results should also not be read as a general semantics of mathematical truth. The paper studies closure operators and finite families of closed theories as mathematical data. Its claims are structural: how region representations behave, how conservative extensions preserve old consequence, how closure atlases generate global closure, and when that global closure is conservative over each chart.

Relation to the broader program

The phrase
Liberal Mathematics
is best understood here as the name of a broader research program concerned with indexed, local, and theory-relative mathematical structure. The present article proves only one finite closure-theoretic component of that program. Its content is independent of any stronger philosophical thesis: the definitions and theorems stand or fall as claims about finite closure systems, finite indexed truth spaces, and finite closure atlases.

This restraint is important. The paper does not propose a replacement for classical logic or for standard foundations. It gives a small mathematical language for a specific closure-theoretic phenomenon: elements may be represented by regions across selected closed theories, and local closure systems may or may not admit conservative global realization. That is the level at which the contribution should be evaluated.

Outlook: conservative completions

The canonical gluing theorem uses the least global closure
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
. A natural next problem concerns noncanonical global conservative realizations. Such realizations may add mixed-premise consequences that are invisible inside every single chart while still preserving all chart-internal consequence relations.

For example, take identity charts on

\Sigma_{1}=\{p,r\},\qquad\Sigma_{2}=\{q,r\},

and local closed theories
T_{1}=\{p\}
and
T_{2}=\{q\}
. The canonical union is
T^{\cup}=\{p,q\}
, and
r
is not forced by the atlas-generated closure. Nevertheless, a noncanonical conservative global closure may add the mixed rule
\{p,q\}\Rightarrow r
, because no single chart sees both premises at once.

This points toward a later theory of conservative global completions of compatible local data. Such a theory would ask which mixed consequences can be added without changing any chart-internal closure relation, and how the resulting finite family of admissible global closures should be organized. Those questions are not used in the present paper. The present contribution is the first finite layer: indexed truth spaces, four-region decision decompositions, conservative extension, closure-atlas obstruction, and canonical gluing under the atlas-generated closure.
