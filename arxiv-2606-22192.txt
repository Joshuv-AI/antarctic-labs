Ensemble Feedback Methods for Families of Linear Systems
Michael Schönlein, Fabian Wirth

Abstract
We consider feedback stabilization for one-parameter families of finite-dimensional linear systems over compact parameter sets in the complex field. Classical ensemble feedback induces compact control operators and therefore cannot modify the essential spectrum of the associated multiplication operators describing the free motion of the system. This precludes stabilization in many infinite-dimensional settings. To address this issue, multiplication feedback operators are introduced. For systems with constant Hermite indices, an analogue of Heymann’s lemma is proved, as well as a pole placement theorem, and stabilization results. The relation of pointwise and ensemble controllability is investigated. For systems with nonconstant Hermite indices, corresponding results are obtained under additional assumptions on the structure of the parameter set.



Keywords:

Families of systems, ensemble controllability, stabilization, feedback, matrix multiplication operator, spectrum

The paper is organized as follows. In the remainder we introduce notation, recall basic concepts and discuss the novelty of this paper. In Section
2
we recall some facts about matrix multiplication operators. In Proposition
2.2
it is shown that the spectrum of multiplication operators on the space of continuous functions on a perfect parameter space is essential;
a fact we were not able to locate in the literature. Some basics concerning the stability of semigroups generated by bounded operators are recalled. In Section
3
we start our investigation of the stabilization problem using static state feedback. We distinguish between ensemble feedback and multiplication feedback in which the feedback operator is allowed to be parameter dependent. If
{\mathbf{P}}
is perfect, then ensemble feedback usually does not allow for stabilization as the essential spectrum is not changed by a compact perturbation. In the case of multiplication feedback we consider the case that the Hermite indices are constant over the parameter space. In this case we are able to give a proof of a version of Heymann’s lemma that provides explicit formulas for the desired feedback. In this way the pole-placement and stabilization theorems can be obtained. An application of approximation theoretic results is used to show that if Hermite indices are constant and certain assumptions on the parameter set that make approximation theoretic results applicable are satisfied, then a multiplication feedback can generate a uniformly ensemble reachable system from a pointwise reachable one. In Section
4
we briefly indicate an alternative route to parameter-dependent versions of Heymann’s lemma that analyze what we call the Heymann bundle over the parameter set
{\mathbf{P}}
. In this language the veracity of a parametrized Heymann lemma translates into the existence of a global section of the respective bundle. For the simple cases that the parameter set is an interval or a circle a positive answer can be provided here without any assumption on the Hermite indices. We conclude in Section
5
and point to further open questions. In the Appendix, Section
6

We also provide a short proof of a folklore result that is frequently used without proof. Namely that for a continuous square matrix function with continuous injective eigenfunctions and pairwise disjoint and simple spectra there exists a continuous diagonalization.

Novelty and main contribution

The recent contributions metioned in the introduction of this paper all deal with open-loop inputs. In this work, we consider feedback in combination with open-loop inputs of the form

u(t,x(t))=\mathcal{K}x(t)+v(t)

given by a feedback operator
\mathcal{K}
defined on
X_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
. We will distinguish between the cases where the range of the feedback operator
\mathcal{K}
is finite and infinite dimensional.

Feedback operator with finite dimensional range
. An essential assumption in ensemble control is that the inputs are independent of the parameters. In view of this fact, it is natural to consider feedback operators with finite dimensional range, i.e.

\mathcal{K}:X_{n}({\mathbf{P}})\to{\mathbb{C}}^{m}.

In this context, this work explores classical system-theoretic properties. More precisely, in Theorem
3.1
we will show that ensemble reachability is invariant under ensemble feedback with finite dimensional range. This is a well-known property in mathematical systems theory. On the other hand, however, to our surprise we show in Theorem
3.4
that such feedback operators are not appropriate for stabilization. We note that this holds for all infinite-dimensional families of linear systems defined on arbitrary Banach spaces.

Feedback operator with infinite dimensional range
. Motivated by the limitations of feedback operators with finite dimensional range, we also treat the case of infinite-dimensional range. Given that necessary and sufficient conditions for ensemble controllability depend on the particular Banach space under consideration, we limit ourselves to the Banach space of continuous functions. Here we investigate feedback multiplication operators
of the form

\mathcal{K}:C_{n}({\mathbf{P}})\to C_{m}({\mathbf{P}}),\quad{\mathcal{K}}f(\theta)=K(\theta)f(\theta),\qquad K\in C_{m,n}({\mathbf{P}}).

This type of feedback operators was recently considered in
[
39
]
, where also the case
K(\theta)\equiv K\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
is considered for the controlled harmonic oscillator. A main tool in our analysis will be the Hermite indices of a pair
(A.B)
, which are recalled at the beginning of Section
3
. Under the assumption of constant Hermite indices (as a function of
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
) a concrete construction is provided that yields a parametrized version of Heymann’s lemma,
[
25
]
,
[
26
, Lemma 8.4.7]
. This lemma famously provides a key simplification in the proof of the pole placement theorem and similarly a parametrized pole placement result is provided here. This result can then be used as a basis for a novel stabilization result.
In Theorem
3.13
we provide new sufficient conditions such that there is a
K\in C_{m,n}({\mathbf{P}})
so that the continuous family

(A+BK,B)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})

is uniformly ensemble reachable. We note that this result improves Theorem 3 in
[
39
]
, as Theorem
3.13
does not require the application of transformations in the state-space and the input space.

For the case of nonconstant Hermite indices we restrict our attention to parameter sets that are intervals or circles. Also in this case a parameterized version of Heymann’s lemma is shown which relies largely on standard tools from algebraic geometry. For more general parameter sets, we believe more tools from homotopy theory or obstruction theory would have to be applied, see e.g.
[
27
, Chapter VI]
, but this is beyond the scope of the present paper.

In this section we will show that for the matrix multiplication operator defined on the space of continuous function

8
) and choosing
k
sufficiently large, we obtain a contradiction. This shows the assertion.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

The statement of Proposition
2.2
(ii) is evidently false without the assumption that
{\mathbf{P}}
is perfect. In this case, an isolated point
\theta^{*}
in
{\mathbf{P}}

,

Stability of linear infinite dimensional systems

f

Let
A\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})
and
\mathcal{A}
be the associated mulitplication operator on
X_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
.
In order to study the stabilization problem for the class of infinite dimensional systems given by (

) and (

), it is required to consider the stability properties of the uncontrolled system

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{x}(t)&=\mathcal{A}x(t)\\

(9)

The multiplication operator
\mathcal{A}
generates the semigroup

\displaystyle T(t)\colon X_{n}({\mathbf{P}})\to X_{n}({\mathbf{P}}),\quad T(t)f(\theta):=e^{tA(\theta)}f(\theta).

It follows from

Proposition 2.4
.

Let

and let

denote the corresponding matrix multiplication operator defined on a Banach space

. Then, for the linear system (

) the following statements are equivalent.

1.

The origin is uniformly asymptotically stable, i.e. one has

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|T(t)\|=0.

2.

The origin is exponentially stable, i.e. there are constants
M\geq 1
and
\gamma>0
such that
\|T(t)\|\leq Me^{-\gamma t}
.

3.

The spectrum of
\mathcal{A}
is contained in the open left half plane, i.e.

\sigma(\mathcal{A})\subset\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\mid\operatorname{Re}z<0\}.

Note that

which generates an asymptotically stable semigroup in the sense that

for all

, but the semigroup is not uniformly asymptotically stable.

3
Static state feedback for families on Banach spaces

In this section we continue the systematic investigation of feedback methods for systems described by (
5
) or (
6
).
We will distinguish two possible ways of implementing a linear static state feedback. We speak of
ensemble feedback operators
, if the feedback is given by a bounded linear operator

\mathcal{F}:X_{n}({\mathbf{P}})\to{\mathbb{C}}^{m}.

(11)

More generally, we also consider
multiplication feedback operators
given by multiplication operators, i.e. by linear, bounded feedback operators defined by
K\in C_{m,n}({\mathbf{P}})
and which are
of the form

\mathcal{K}:C_{n}({\mathbf{P}})\to C_{m}({\mathbf{P}}),\quad({\mathcal{K}}x)(\theta)=K(\theta)x(\theta),\quad x\in C_{n}({\mathbf{P}}),\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}.

(12)

Recall that a central point in the theory of ensemble reachability is that the input
u
does not depend on the parameter
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
. Rather, it serves as an input applied simultaneously for all parameters. From this point of view the feedback operators satisfying (
11
) appear to be more natural.

We emphasize that, because
\mathcal{F}
has finite dimensional range, ensemble feedback operators are automatically compact, cf.
[
8
, Theorem 4.10]
. Natural choices for such feedback operators might be given by the integral operator with a kernel
K\in C_{m,n}({\mathbf{P}})
defined by

\mathcal{F}f=\int_{{\mathbf{P}}}K(\theta)f(\theta)\operatorname{d}\!\theta,\qquad f\in C_{n}({\mathbf{P}}),

or the weighted average operator (for fixed
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N}\in{\mathbf{P}}
,
K_{1},\ldots,K_{N}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times n}
)

\mathcal{F}f=\sum_{k=1}^{N}K_{k}f(\theta_{k}),\qquad f\in C_{n}({\mathbf{P}}).

Note that, the latter serve as examples and the subsequent analysis is not limited to these choices.

3.1
Ensemble feedback

Let
X_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
denote a separable Banach space of functions from the parameter space
{\mathbf{P}}
to
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
such that Assumption
1.1
holds. Thus the multiplication operators
\mathcal{A}\colon X_{n}({\mathbf{P}})\to X_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
and
\mathcal{B}\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\to X_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
are bounded linear. Then, using an bounded linear ensemble feedback operator
\mathcal{F}\colon X_{n}({\mathbf{P}})\to{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, the overall systems is given by

\begin{split}\dot{x}(t)&=\left(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\,\mathcal{F}\right)x(t)+\mathcal{B}u(t)\end{split}

(13)

The first result is obtained by using arguments of
[
26
, Chapter 8]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Ensemble reachability is invariant under ensemble feedback)
.

Let
X_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
satisfy Assumption
1.1
. Consider
A\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})
and
B\in X_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
with associated bounded linear operators
\mathcal{A}\in L(X_{n}({\mathbf{P}}))
,
\mathcal{B}\in L({\mathbb{C}}^{m},X_{n}({\mathbf{P}}))
. Then for every
\mathcal{F}\in L(X_{n}({\mathbf{P}}),{\mathbb{C}}^{m})
the pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is ensemble reachable if and only if
(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{BF},\mathcal{B})
is ensemble reachable.

Proof.

Using the characterization of ensemble reachability in (
7
),
we shall show that for any ensemble feedback operator
\mathcal{F}\in L(X_{n}({\mathbf{P}}),{\mathbb{C}}^{m})
it holds that

\operatorname{span}\left\{\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{A}^{k}\mathcal{B}\,|\,k=0,1,2,3,...\right\}=\operatorname{span}\left\{\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F})^{k}\mathcal{B}\,|\,k=0,1,2,3,...\right\}.

(14)

We first show
\supseteq
by induction. To see this, we verify that for all
k=1,2,3,...
one has

(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F})^{k}~\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{B}\ \subseteq\ \mathcal{A}^{k}\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{B}+\cdots+\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{B}.

Let
k=1
and
f\in\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{B}
. Then, as
\mathcal{F}f\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
we get

(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F})f=\mathcal{A}f+\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}f\in\mathcal{A}\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{B}+\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{B}.

Supposing the claim is true for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
we consider
k+1
, i.e.

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F})^{k+1}f

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F})(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F})^{k}f=(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F})g

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}g+\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}g,

for some
g\in\mathcal{A}^{k}\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{B}+\cdots+\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{B}
. Using again that
\mathcal{F}g\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
it follows that
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}g\in\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{B}
. Hence, we obtain

(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F})^{k}~\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}^{k}\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{B}+\cdots+\operatorname{Im}\mathcal{B}.

This completes the induction and
\supseteq
is shown for (
14
).

To see that in (
14
) also
\subseteq
holds, it is sufficient to apply
\supseteq
to

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}:=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}
and

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}:=-\mathcal{F}
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

As it is easy to miss, we point out that the finite-dimensionality of
\mathcal{F}
is used explicitly when we need to conclude that
\mathcal{BF}g
lies in the image of
\mathcal{B}
. In comparison, we note that
it will be a consequence of Theorem
3.13
that ensemble reachability is not an invariant under multiplication feedback.

We now turn to the stabilization problem and recall the definition, cf.
[
36
,
46
]
.

Definition 3.3
.

A pair
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times X_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
is called (ensemble) stabilizable if there is exists a linear bounded ensemble feedback operator
\mathcal{F}\colon X_{n}({\mathbf{P}})\to{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
such that the closed-loop system defined by
\mathcal{A+BF}
is exponentially stable.

We note that this definition is in line with the classical infinite-dimensional literature. There it is common to demand the existence of a bounded linear feedback operator so that the closed-loop system is exponentially stable, cf.
[
13
, Definition 8.1.1]
,
[
17
, Ch. VI, Definition 8.23]
. This is because in infinite-dimensions uniform asymptotic stability (or equivalently exponential stability) is in general not characterized in terms of the spectrum. cf.
[
47
]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let Assumption
1.1
hold and
assume furthermore that
{\mathbf{P}}
is perfect.
Suppose the pair
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times X_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
is not exponentially stable. Then, it is not exponentially stabilizable by ensemble feedback, i.e., for any bounded linear ensemble feedback operator
\mathcal{F}\colon X_{n}({\mathbf{P}})\to{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
the closed-loop system
\mathcal{A+BF}
is not exponentially stable.

Proof.

As the range of
\mathcal{F}
is finite dimensional, the operators
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{F}
are compact, cf.
[
12
, Ch.VI, §3, Prop. 3.5 (b)]
.
As the essential spectrum of a
\mathcal{A}
is invariant under compact perturbations, cf.
[
38
, Thm. 5.10]
, we conclude from Proposition
2.2
that

\sigma(\mathcal{A})=\sigma_{\operatorname{ess}}(\mathcal{A})=\sigma_{\operatorname{ess}}(\mathcal{A+BF}).

The assertion then follows from Proposition
2.4
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We note that Theorem
3.4
is false for trivial reasons, when the assumption is dropped that
{\mathbf{P}}
does not have isolated points. If
q\in{\mathbf{P}}
is isolated, then it may happen that
A_{|{\mathbf{P}}\setminus\{q\}}
defines an exponentially stable system and it suffices to stabilize the finite-dimensional pair
(A(q),B(q))
. This is easily possible without destroying stability properties away from
q
by choosing
K(\theta)=0
,
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}\setminus\{q\}
.

3.2
Multiplication feedback

Motivated by the disappointing result of Theorem
3.4
, we now intend to study a different class of feedback operators. The second contribution of this paper is to show that feedbacks using multiplication operators can be used for stabilization. For ensembles defined in the space of continuous functions, we will study
multiplication feedback operators
given by multiplication operators, i.e. feedback operators of the form

\mathcal{K}:C_{n}({\mathbf{P}})\to C_{m}({\mathbf{P}}),\quad({\mathcal{K}}x)(\theta)=K(\theta)x(\theta),

where
K\in C_{m,n}({\mathbf{P}})
. This class of feedback operators was recently considered in
[
39
]
, where also the case
K(\theta)\equiv K\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
is considered for the controlled harmonic oscillator. In this section we will provide (Theorem
3.13
) new sufficient conditions so that a continuous ensemble becomes uniformly ensemble reachable by applying a mixture of open-loop inputs and multiplication feedback operators of the form

u(t,x(t,\theta))=u(t)+K(\theta)x(t,\theta),\qquad K\in C_{m,n}({\mathbf{P}}).

We note that Theorem
3.13
in this paper improves a recent result in
[
39
, Theorem 3]
, as it does not require the application of transformations in the state-space and the input space.

A crucial step in the construction procedure is an ensemble version of the classical Heymann Lemma. To this end, we recall some facts concerning Hermite indices. For a reachable pair
(A,B)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times m}
, we consider the list (a permutation of the columns the Kalman matrix of
(A,B)
)

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}b_{1}&Ab_{1}&\cdots&A^{n-1}b_{1}&\cdots&b_{m}&Ab_{m}&\cdots&A^{n-1}b_{m}.\end{matrix}

Then, we select from left to right the first linearly independent columns

b_{1},\ldots,A^{h_{1}-1}b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,A^{h_{2}-1}b_{2},\ldots,b_{m},\ldots,A^{h_{m}-1}b_{m}.

The corresponding exponents
h_{1},...,h_{m}
are called the
Hermite indices
of
(A,B)
, where
h_{i}:=0
if
b_{i}
is not selected, see also
[
28
, Scheme II, Sec. 6.4.6]
. Note that the Hermite indices of a pair
(A,B)
are not feedback invariant, in contrast to the Kronecker and the controllability indices, cf. e.g.
[
4
,
26
]
. In this context, we note that
[
4
, Thm. 2.1]
characterizes the possible Hermite indices that can be assigned through feedback in terms of the Kronecker indices of a system.

The following lemma generalizes a frequently-used preparatory step in the proof of Heymann’s lemma to the ensemble case; see
[
25
]
for the original statement. We need the following notation: For a given reachable pair
(A,B)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times m}
with Hermite indices
h_{1},...,h_{m}
, and the convention
h_{0}:=0
, we set

H_{j}:=\sum_{i=0}^{j}h_{i},\quad j=0,\ldots,m.

(15)

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}
be nonempty and compact. Let
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
be pointwise reachable and suppose that the Hermite indices are constant. Then, there are
v_{1},...,v_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in C_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
defined by setting
x_{0}(\theta):=0,\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
, and

x_{k}(\theta):=A(\theta)x_{k-1}(\theta)+B(\theta)v_{k},\qquad\theta\in{\mathbf{P}},k=1,\ldots,n,

(16)

such that for all
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
the vectors
x_{1}(\theta),...,x_{n}(\theta)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

are linearly independent in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
h_{1},...,h_{m}
denote the Hermite indices. Then, for every
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
the vectors

\displaystyle b_{1}(\theta),...,A(\theta)^{h_{1}-1}b_{1}(\theta),...,b_{m}(\theta),...,A(\theta)^{h_{m}-1}b_{m}(\theta)

(17)

are linearly independent in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Using the notation introduced in (
15
), we set the vectors
v_{1},...,v_{n}
as

v_{k}:=\begin{cases}e_{l}&\text{ if }k=H_{l-1}+1\text{ and }H_{l-1}<H_{l},\\
0&\text{ else.}\end{cases}

As the Hermite indices are independent of
\theta
this causes no problems. Also, without loss of generality we assume that
b_{1}\neq 0
, otherwise all the indices in the following arguments are shifted to the first nonzero column. Then
H_{0}=0
,
H_{1}=h_{1}>0
and so
v_{1}=e_{1}
,
v_{2}=\ldots=v_{h_{1}-1}=0
.
By construction, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}x_{1}&=Ax_{0}+Bv_{1}=Be_{1}=b_{1}\\
x_{2}&=Ax_{1}+Bv_{2}=Ab_{1}\\
&\,\,\,\vdots\\
x_{h_{1}}&=A^{h_{1}-1}b_{1}.\end{split}

(18)

The list
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{h_{1}})
is linearly independent by the definition of the Hermite indices. For the next index,
h_{1}+1=H_{1}+1
, consider the next nonzero Hermite index in the list
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m})
, say
h_{l_{2}}
. This means
H_{1}=H_{2}=\ldots=H_{l_{2}-1}<H_{l_{2}}
and
h_{1}+1=H_{l_{2}-1}
. Hence
v_{h_{1}+1}=e_{l_{2}}
and
v_{h_{1}+2}=\ldots=v_{H_{l_{2}}-1}=0
. We thus obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}x_{h_{1}+1}&=A^{h_{1}}b_{1}+Bv_{h_{1}+1}=A^{h_{1}}b_{1}+b_{l_{2}}\\
x_{h_{1}+2}&=A^{h_{1}+1}b_{1}+Ab_{l_{2}}+Av_{h_{1}+2}=A^{h_{1}+1}b_{1}+Ab_{l_{2}}\\
&\,\,\,\vdots\\
x_{h_{1}+h_{l_{2}}}&=A^{h_{1}+h_{l_{2}}-1}b_{1}+A^{h_{l_{2}}-1}b_{l_{2}}.\end{split}

(19)

As the vectors
(A^{h_{1}}b_{1},\ldots,A^{h_{1}+h_{l_{2}}-1}b_{1})
are in the span of
(x_{1},\dots,x_{h_{1}})
by the construction of the Hermite indices, we obtain that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{H_{l_{2}}})
are linearly independent.
This procedure now repeats by choosing the next nonzero Hermite index. All steps are identical and the procedure terminates in a basis
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
∎

As the vectors
x_{1},...,x_{n}
are defined by the pair
(A,B)
and its Hermite indices, we will use the notation

M_{A,B}:=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}&x_{2}&\cdots&x_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

For later use, we fix the following fact.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}
be nonempty and compact. Let
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
be pointwise reachable and suppose that the Hermite indices are constant. Then, for every
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
the vectors
x_{1}(\theta),...,x_{n}(\theta)
constructed in Lemma
3.6
satisfy

\det M_{A,B}(\theta)=\det\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}(\theta)&\cdots&x_{n}(\theta)\end{pmatrix}\neq 0.

Now we are ready to prove the following parameter version of Heymann’s Lemma.

Lemma 3.8

(Parameter version of Heymann’s Lemma)
.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}
be nonempty and compact. Let
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
be pointwise reachable and suppose that the Hermite indices are constant. Then, there are
K\in C_{m,n}({\mathbf{P}})
and
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that the single-input pair

\displaystyle\left(A+BK,Bv\right)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times C_{n}({\mathbf{P}})

is pointwise reachable.

Proof.

Fix
v_{1},...,v_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
and
x_{1},...,x_{n}\in C_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
as in Lemma
3.6
and an arbitrary
v_{n+1}\in C_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
. Then, we define the matrix-valued function

K:{\mathbf{P}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n},\theta\mapsto K(\theta)=\operatorname{col}(v_{2},...,v_{n},v_{n+1}(\theta))\,\operatorname{col}(x_{1}(\theta),...,x_{n}(\theta))^{-1}.

By Corollary
3.7
and since taking the inverse is a continuous operation, we have
K\in C_{m,n}({\mathbf{P}})
. By construction,
K(\theta)x_{k}(\theta)=v_{k+1}(\theta)
,
k=1,\ldots,n
, and with (
16
) it holds

\displaystyle B(\theta)v_{1}

\displaystyle=x_{1}(\theta),

\displaystyle\left(A(\theta)+B(\theta)K(\theta)\right)x_{k}(\theta)

\displaystyle=A(\theta)x_{k}(\theta)+B(\theta)K(\theta)x_{k}(\theta)=A(\theta)x_{k}(\theta)+B(\theta)v_{k+1}(\theta)

\displaystyle=x_{k+1}(\theta),\quad k=1,\ldots,n-1.

Therefore

\left(A(\theta)+B(\theta)K(\theta)\right)^{k}B(\theta)v_{1}=x_{k+1}(\theta),\quad k=1,...,n-1.

Thus, for every
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
, the Kalman matrix of the single-input system given by
\left(A(\theta)+B(\theta)K(\theta),B(\theta)v_{1}\right)
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}B(\theta)v_{1}\!&\!\left(A(\theta)+B(\theta)K(\theta)\right)B(\theta)v_{1}&\!\!\cdots\!\!&\left(A(\theta)+B(\theta)K(\theta)\right)^{n-1}B(\theta)v_{1}\end{pmatrix}=M_{A,B}(\theta).

From Corollary
3.7
and the Kalman rank criterion, we conclude that
\left(A+BK,Bv_{1}\right)
is pointwise reachable.
∎

With this version of Heymann’s lemma at hand, we can prove the pole placement theorem in the next statement. Before doing so, we need to point out a significant difference between Heymann’s original lemma and Lemma
3.8
: In the original lemma, any nonzero
b\in\operatorname{im}B
can be used to obtain a reachable pair
(A+BK,b)
. We will discuss extensions in this direction in Section
4
.

Theorem 3.9

(Parameter-dependent pole placement)
.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}
be nonempty and compact. Let
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
be pointwise reachable and suppose that the Hermite indices are constant. Then, for every tuple of continuous functions
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}\colon{\mathbf{P}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
there is a matrix function
K\in C_{m,n}({\mathbf{P}})
such that

\sigma(\mathcal{A+BK})=\bigcup_{\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}}\{\lambda_{1}(\theta),...,\lambda_{n}(\theta)\}.

(20)

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}\in C({\mathbf{P}})
. By Lemma
3.8
, there are a
\tilde{K}\in C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
and a
v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
such that
(A+B\tilde{K},Bv)
is pointwise reachable. Consider the family of polynomials
\{p_{\theta}\mid\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}\}
given by

p_{\theta}(z):=\big(z-\lambda_{1}(\theta)\big)\cdots\big(z-\lambda_{n}(\theta)\big)=z^{n}-p_{n-1}(\theta)z^{n-1}+\cdots+p_{1}(\theta)z+p_{0}(\theta).

Note that the coefficients
p_{0},...,p_{n-1}
depend continuously on the parameter
\theta
. With this at hand and using Ackermann’s Formula, cf.
[
18
, Theorem 6.19]
, we define

\tilde{k}\in C_{1,n}({\mathbf{P}}),\quad\tilde{k}(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}0&\cdots&0&1\end{pmatrix}\,T(\theta)^{-1}\,p_{\theta}\big(A(\theta)+B(\theta)\tilde{K}(\theta)\big),\quad\theta\in{\mathbf{P}},

where
T(\theta)
denotes the Kalman matrix corresponding to the single-input system
(A(\theta)+B(\theta)\tilde{K}(\theta),B(\theta)v)
,
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
. It then follows from Ackermann’s formula that

\sigma(A(\theta)+B(\theta)\tilde{K}(\theta)+B(\theta)v\tilde{k}(\theta))=\{\lambda_{1}(\theta),\ldots,\lambda_{n}(\theta)\},\quad\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}.

Defining
K\in C_{m,n}({\mathbf{P}})
by

K(\theta):=\tilde{K}(\theta)+v\tilde{k}(\theta),\quad\theta\in{\mathbf{P}},

we obtain a continuous matrix function, for which
(
20
) holds by an application of Proposition
2.2
.
∎

After finishing the preliminary steps, we are able to state the main results of this section. The first result says that the class of multiplication ensenmble feedback operators is suitable for exponential stabilization.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}
be nonempty and compact. Suppose
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
is pointwise reachable and has constant Hermite indices. Then, there is a multiplication ensemble feedback operator
\mathcal{K}:C_{n}({\mathbf{P}})\to C_{m}({\mathbf{P}})
such that
\mathcal{A+BK}
is exponentially stable.

Proof.

Choose continuous functions
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}\colon{\mathbf{P}}\to\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\mid\operatorname{Re}z<0\}
. Using Proposition
2.2
and Theorem
3.9
, we conclude that there is a
K\in C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
such that

\sigma(\mathcal{A+BK})=\bigcup_{\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}}\{\lambda_{1}(\theta),...,\lambda_{n}(\theta)\}\subset\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\mid\operatorname{Re}z<0\}.

The assertion then follows from Proposition
2.4
.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

We emphasize that for the latter statement it is not required that the parameter set
{\mathbf{P}}
is perfect, because it is not important for the result whether or not the entire spectrum is essential. A key feature is that the multiplication feedback operator is not compact. Because of this, it can be applied to shift the entire spectrum to the open left half plane.

Our second main result, Theorem
3.13
, is concerned with sufficient conditions for the existence of multiplication feedback operators so that the mixed open-loop and feedback system becomes uniform ensemble reachability. We note that the sufficiency conditions are verifiable just in terms of the matrices
A(\theta)
and
B(\theta)
. The proof of Theorem
3.13
will use a modified version of sufficient conditions for uniform ensemble reachability of single-input systems. The details are provided in the Appendix
6
.

Remark 3.12
.

In
[
15
, Thm 3, Thm. 4, Cor. 3]
it is shown that uniform ensemble reachability is a subtle interplay between spectral properties of the matrix function
A
, system theoretic properties of the pairs
(A(\theta),B(\theta))
and topological properties of the parameter space
{\mathbf{P}}
. The significance of the next result is that, if the system-theoretic property that the Hermite indices are constant holds, the spectral properties can be put in place by using not just open-loop inputs but a mixture
u(t)+K(\theta)x(t,\theta)
of open-loop inputs and feedback. Therefore, the next statement is of particular interest for the cases where a family of systems is to be steered (approximately) to a desired family of states rather than just stabilizing it.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}
be nonempty, compact with empty interior and
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbf{P}}
is connected. Suppose
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
is pointwise reachable and has constant Hermite indices. Then, there is a
K\in C_{m,n}({\mathbf{P}})
such that
(A+BK,B)
is uniformly ensemble reachable.

Proof.

Choose continuous and injective functions
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}:{\mathbf{P}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that

\lambda_{k}({\mathbf{P}})\cap\lambda_{l}({\mathbf{P}})=\emptyset\qquad\text{for all }l\neq k.

By Lemma
3.8
, Theorem
3.9
, there is a
K(\cdot):=\tilde{K}(\cdot)+v\tilde{k}(\cdot)\in C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
such that the single-input pair

\left(A(\theta)+B(\theta)K(\theta),B(\theta)v\right)

is pointwise reachable and such that

\sigma(A(\theta)+B(\theta)K(\theta))=\{\lambda_{1}(\theta),\ldots,\lambda_{n}(\theta)\},\quad\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}.

(21)

It remains to show that
(A+BK,B)
is uniformly ensemble reachable.

Since the vector
v
does not depend on the parameter, it follows that
B(\theta)v\in\operatorname{Im}B(\theta)
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{span}

\displaystyle\left\{\theta\mapsto\left(A(\theta)+B(\theta)\big(\tilde{K}(\theta)+v\tilde{k}(\theta)\big)\right)^{k}B(\theta)v\,\,|\,k=0,1,2,3,...\right\}

\displaystyle\subset\operatorname{span}\left\{\theta\mapsto\left(A(\theta)+B(\theta)K(\theta)\right)^{k}b_{j}(\theta)\,\,|\,j=1,...m,\,k=0,1,2,3,...\right\}.

As (
21
) is satisfied and the system
\left(A+BK,Bv\right)
is pointwise reachable, we may apply Proposition
6.1
in the appendix, and conclude that
\left(A+BK,Bv\right)
is uniformly ensemble reachable, i.e.´

\displaystyle\operatorname{span}\left\{\theta\mapsto\left(A(\theta)+B(\theta)\big(\tilde{K}(\theta)+v\tilde{k}(\theta)\big)\right)^{k}B(\theta)v\,\,|\,k=0,1,2,3,...\right\}

is dense in
C_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
by (
7
). Consequently,

\displaystyle\operatorname{span}\left\{\theta\mapsto\left(A(\theta)+B(\theta)K(\theta)\right)^{k}b_{j}(\theta)\,\,|\,j=1,...m,\,k=0,1,2,3,...\right\},

is also dense in
C_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
and, by (
7
),
(A+BK,B)
is uniformly ensemble reachable. This shows the assertion.
∎

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}
be nonempty, compact with empty interior and
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbf{P}}
be connected. Suppose
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
is pointwise reachable and has constant Hermite indices. Then there is a
K\in C_{m,n}({\mathbf{P}})
such that
A+BK
is exponentially stable and
(A+BK,B)
is uniformly ensemble reachable.

Proof.

It suffices to choose the
\lambda_{i}:{\mathbf{P}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
in the proof of Theorem
3.13
such that they are continuous and injective functions whose images are in the open left-half plane and pairwise disjoint.
∎

To end this section, we comment on the relation of Theorem
3.13
to
[
39
, Theorem 3]
. Apart from pointwise reachability, that is supposed in both results, the two theorems make an assumption on structure indices of the matrix pair
(A,B)
.

More precisely, Theorem
3.13
supposes that the Hermite indices are constant, whereas
[
39
, Theorem 3]
assumes that the Kronecker indices are constant.

A central difference between the two indices is that the Kronecker indices are invariant under feedback, cf.
[
18
, Lemma 6.16]
, whereas the Hermite indices are not invariant under feedback, cf.
[
4
]
,
[
26
, Chapter 6.6, Exercise 23]
.

Regarding the conclusions of the theorems, we note that Theorem
3.13
provides the existence of a continuous feedback matrix
K
such that
(A+BK,B
is uniformly ensemble reachable. In contrast,
[
39
, Theorem 3]
shows that
(A,B)
is feedback equivalent to an uniformly ensemble reachable pair. More precisely,
[
39
, Theorem 3]
proves that there are continuously invertible matrices
T\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})
and
S\in C_{m,m}({\mathbf{P}})
and a feedback matrix
K\in C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
such that the pair

\left(T(A-BS^{-1}K)T^{-1},TBS^{-1}\right)

is uniformly ensemble reachable.

4
Intervals and Circles as Parameter Spaces

In this section we depart from the assumption of constant Hermite indices. In this case the constructions become less concrete, and we require further assumptions on the parameter space
{\mathbf{P}}
, but on the the other hand more general statements of Heymann’s lemma can be obtained. We start with a general observation.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}
be a compact parameter space and let
v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be such that

B(\theta)v\neq 0,\qquad\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}.

For each
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
, consider the set

E_{\theta}:=\Bigl\{F\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times n}:(A(\theta)+B(\theta)F,\;B(\theta)v)\text{ is reachable}\Bigr\}.

(22)

If we assume that
(A,B)
is pointwise reachable, then, by Heymann’s lemma,
E_{\theta}
is nonempty for every
\theta\in P
. More precisely, if we consider the determinant of the Kalman matrix

\Phi(\theta,F):=\det\!\bigl[B(\theta)v,\,(A(\theta)+B(\theta)F)B(\theta)v,\,\dots,\,(A(\theta)+B(\theta)F)^{n-1}B(\theta)v\bigr],

then
E_{\theta}=\{F:\Phi(\theta,F)\neq 0\}
, hence
E_{\theta}
is the complement of a proper algebraic subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times n}
. In particular,
E_{\theta}
is dense, open and path connected. We define the
Heymann bundle

E:=\bigl\{(\theta,F)\in{\mathbf{P}}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times n}:F\in E_{\theta}\bigr\}.

(23)

Since
\Phi
is continuous,
E
is an open subset of the trivial bundle

{\mathbf{P}}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times n}
, and the projection

\pi:E\to{\mathbf{P}},\qquad(\theta,F)\mapsto\theta,

is continuous.

The problem of constructing a parameter-dependent feedback may now be formulated as a section problem: does there exist a continuous map

s:{\mathbf{P}}\to E

such that
\pi\circ s=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbf{P}}
? Writing
s(\theta)=(\theta,K(\theta))
, this is equivalent to finding a continuous map

K:{\mathbf{P}}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times n}

for which

(A(\theta)+B(\theta)K(\theta),\,B(\theta)v)

is reachable for every
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
.

The existence of such a section is naturally related to the topology of the parameter space
{\mathbf{P}}
and touches questions of homotopy theory and obstruction theory. A detailed analysis of this context is beyond the scope of this paper. However, we can present a simple case in which
{\mathbf{P}}
is an interval, circle or homeomorphic image thereof.

With this we obtain a further parameter dependent version of Heymann’s lemma.

Lemma 4.1

(Parameter-dependent Heymann lemma)
.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}
be a compact interval or a circle, or a homeomorphic image thereof. Let
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
be pointwise reachable. Then, for every
v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
with the property that
B(\theta)v\neq 0
for all
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
,
there exists
K\in C_{m,n}({\mathbf{P}})
such that the single-input pair

\displaystyle\left(A+BK,Bv\right)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times C_{n}({\mathbf{P}})

is pointwise reachable.

Proof.

We will construct a suitable finite open cover of intervals with associated constant feedback values for each open set in the cover. On the intersections these values will be patched. Without loss of generality we let
{\mathbf{P}}=[0,1]
. The case of a circle is treated in the same way as will be described.
Concretely, using the notation introduced in (
22
) and (
23
):

For every
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
choose
F_{\theta}\in E_{\theta}
. As
\Phi
varies continuously in
\theta
, there is a (relatively) open interval
U_{\theta}
around
\theta
such that
F_{\theta}\in E_{\eta}
for all
\eta\in U_{\theta}
. Choose a finite open subcover
\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\}
of this open cover of
{\mathbf{P}}
and denote the associated feedback values by
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}
. As
{\mathbf{P}}=[0,1]
, we may assume that the
U_{i}
are ordered in such a way, that
U_{i}\cap U_{i+1}\neq\emptyset
,
i=1,\ldots,k-1
and by reducing the size of
U_{i}
if necessary, we may assume that
U_{i}\cap U_{j}=\emptyset
if
|i-j|\geq 2
. For an index
i
, fix
\eta\in U_{i}\cap U_{i+1}
. Then
F_{i},F_{i+1}\in E_{\eta}
and as each fibre is connected there exists a continuous path
\gamma:[0,1]\to E_{\eta}
with
\gamma(0)=F_{i}
,
\gamma(1)=F_{i+1}
. By continuity of
\Phi
, there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that
[\eta-\varepsilon,\eta+\varepsilon]\subset U_{i}\cap U_{i+1}
and
\gamma([0,1])\subset E_{\vartheta}
for all
\vartheta\in[\eta-\varepsilon,\eta+\varepsilon]
.
Now define a continuous function
K:U_{i}\cup U_{i+1}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times n}
by setting

\displaystyle K(\theta)=\left\{\begin{matrix}F_{i}&\quad&\theta\in(\inf U_{i},\eta-\varepsilon),\\
\gamma\left(\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\theta-\frac{\eta-\varepsilon}{2\varepsilon}\right)&&\theta\in[\eta-\varepsilon,\eta+\varepsilon],\\
F_{i+1}&&\theta\in(\eta+\varepsilon,\sup U_{i+1}).\end{matrix}\right.

(24)

By construction
K
is continuous and
K(\theta)\in E_{\theta}
for all
\theta\in U_{i}\cup U_{i+1}
. It is clear that this construction may be performed on each of the finitely many overlaps
U_{i}\cap U_{i+1}
. In this way the desired continuous
K
is constructed.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

In comparison the two different versions of Heymann’s lemma, that is, Lemma
3.6
and
4.1
, have different strengths and weaknesses. While Lemma
3.6
asserts the existence of a vector
v
for which the reduction of input dimension can be performed, the construction of the appropriate feedback only works for this particular vector and does not readily extend to other potential candidates
v
with
B(\theta)v\neq 0
everywhere. On the other hand Lemma
4.1
shows the existence of a suitable feedback for every
v
with nonvanishing image under
B(\cdot)
but it is not shown that such a
v
exists. Note, in addition, that the proof of Lemma
4.1
also works for
v\in C_{m}({\mathbf{P}})
but we have refrained from assuming this as for the application in the proof of Theorem
4.5
a constant
v
is required.

The following results can now be proved just as Theorems
3.9
,
3.10
,
3.13
before.

Theorem 4.3

(Parameter-dependent pole placement)
.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}
be a compact interval or a circle. Let
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
be pointwise reachable and assume that there exists a
v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
with
B(\theta)v\neq 0
for all
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
. Then, for every tuple of continuous functions
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}\colon{\mathbf{P}}\to{\mathbb{C}}

such that

\sigma(\mathcal{A+BK})=\bigcup_{\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}}\{\lambda_{1}(\theta),...,\lambda_{n}(\theta)\}.

(25)

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}
be a compact interval or a circle. Suppose that the pair
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
is pointwise reachable and that there exists a
v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
with

. Then, there is a multiplication ensemble feedback operator
\mathcal{K}:C_{n}({\mathbf{P}})\to C_{m}({\mathbf{P}})
such that
\mathcal{A+BK}
is exponentially stable.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
{\mathbf{P}}
be a compact interval. Suppose
(A,B)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times C_{n,m}({\mathbf{P}})
is pointwise reachable and that there exists a
v\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
with
B(\theta)v\neq 0
for all

. Then there exists a
K\in C_{m,n}({\mathbf{P}})

(A+BK,B)
is uniformly ensemble reachable.

5
Conclusions

This paper considers feedback methods for one-parameter families of linear systems, where the domain of the input operator is finite-dimensional. The parameter space is assumed to be compact subset in the complex plane. In Theorem
3.4
we show that feedback operators with finite-dimensional range are not appropriate to stabilize such an unstable family of linear systems. To overcome this limitation, we consider multiplication feedback operators for families of linear systems. In particular, we investigate families of linear systems defined over the space of continuous functions. In this context, in Lemma
3.8
we prove a parameter-version of Heymann’s Lemma for continuous families with constant Hermite indices. Based on this, in Theorem
3.10
we show that pointwise reachable continuous families of linear systems can be exponentially stabilized by a multiplication feedback operator if the family has constant Hermite indices.
Moreover, it is shown Theorem
3.13
that a pointwise reachable family with constant Hermite indices can be turned into an uniformly ensemble reachable family by a multiplication feedback operator if the complement of the parameter space in connected. In the case of nonconstant Hermite indices a parameterized version of Heymann’s lemma has been obtained in the case that the parameter set is an interval or a circle. Using the tools presented in that section generalizations to more complicated case should be possible, e.g. for parameter sets of covering dimension
1
.

Appendix

The next result is a mild refinement of
[
15
, Corollary 4]
in the sense that it puts weaker assumptions on the properties of the parameter space
{\mathbf{P}}
. We note that this is partially contained in
[
14
]
. In general, ensemble reachability as defined in Definition
1.3
(iii) is an approximation property in the space
X_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
. For the space of continuous functions, approximation of continuous functions on compact subsets
{\mathbb{C}}
is a classical topic. It is well-known in the theory of complex approximation, that we have to impose topological conditions on the parameter space
{\mathbf{P}}
, in particular that it is has empty interior and its complement
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbf{P}}
is connected, cf.
[
19
, Ch. 2, § 3,B]
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\mathbf{P}
be nonempty, compact, and with empty interior such that
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbf{P}}
is connected. Then, a pair
(A,b)\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})\times C_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
is uniformly ensemble reachable if the following conditions are satisfied:

(a)

(A(\theta),b(\theta))
is reachable for all
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
.

(b)

For all distinct parameters
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbf{P}
, the spectra
\sigma\big(A(\theta)\big)
and
\sigma\big(A(\theta^{\prime})\big)
are disjoint.

(c)

For each
\theta\in\mathbf{P}
, the eigenvalues of
A(\theta)
are simple.

The next lemma will be useful in the proof and may also be of independent interest.

Lemma 6.2
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
6.1
, there is a continuously invertible
T\in C_{n,n}({\mathbf{P}})
such that for every
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
it holds

\displaystyle T(\theta)^{-1}A(\theta)T(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}(\theta)&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\lambda_{n}(\theta)\end{pmatrix},\quad T(\theta)^{-1}b(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\vdots\\
1\end{pmatrix},

where
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}:\mathbf{P}\to\mathbb{C}
are continuous and injective eigenvalue functions.

Proof.

As all the eigenvalues of
A
are simple, it follows from classical perturbation theory, cf.
[
29
, Ch. II § 5.2, Thm. 5.1]
, that the eigenvalues depend continuously on
\theta
and that there are continuous functions
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}:\mathbf{P}\to\mathbb{C}
such that for each parameter
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
we have

\sigma(A(\theta))=\{\lambda_{1}(\theta),\dots,\lambda_{n}(\theta)\}
.

Let
\lambda_{i}({\mathbf{P}})
denote the image of
\lambda_{i}:\mathbf{P}\to\mathbb{C}
. As
{\mathbf{P}}
is compact also
\lambda_{i}({\mathbf{P}})
is compact.
Assumptions (b) and (c) imply
that
\lambda_{i}({\mathbf{P}})\cap\lambda_{j}({\mathbf{P}})=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
. Together with assumption (c), we conclude that the functions
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
are injective.

For each
i\in\{1,...,n\}
, let
\Gamma_{i}
be a contour enclosing
\lambda_{i}({\mathbf{P}})
such that for all
j\neq i
the images
\lambda_{j}({\mathbf{P}})
are outside
\Gamma_{i}
. Define the associated Riesz projection

P_{i}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}(zI-A(\theta))^{-1}\,\operatorname{d}\!z.

Then
P_{i}(\theta)
depends continuously on
\theta
, has rank one, and projects onto the eigenspace corresponding to
\lambda_{i}(\theta)
, cf.
[
29
, Ch. II § 5.1, Thm. 5.1]
.

For every
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
we have
P_{i}(\theta)b(\theta)\neq 0
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, as otherwise the Kalman rank condition fails. More concretely, if, say,
P_{1}(\theta)b(\theta)=0
, then using
[
29
, I.(5.21)]
we have
for all
k
that
P_{1}(\theta)A^{k}(\theta)b(\theta)=A^{k}(\theta)P_{1}(\theta)b(\theta)=0
and so the image of the Kalman matrix is contained in the lower dimensional subspace
\ker P_{1}(\theta)
, a contradiction.

For
i=1,...,n
, we define

v_{i}(\theta):=P_{i}(\theta)b(\theta)\neq 0.

By construction, for every
i=1,...,n
it holds that

A(\theta)v_{i}(\theta)=\lambda_{i}(\theta)v_{i}(\theta).

Then, by setting

S_{1}(\theta):=(v_{1}(\theta),\dots,v_{n}(\theta))

we obtain a continuous diagonalization of
A(\theta)
. By the reachability assumtption it follows that every entry of
\tilde{b}(\theta):=S_{1}(\theta)^{-1}b(\theta)
is nonzero. Defining the continuously invertible matrix
S_{2}(\theta)=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(\tilde{b}_{1}(\theta)^{-1},...,\tilde{b}_{n}(\theta)^{-1}\bigr)
, a direct computation shows that the transformation
T(\theta):=S_{1}(\theta)S_{2}(\theta)
yields the desired structure. This shows the assertion.
∎

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

Based on
[
45
, Cor. 3.1.2]
, it is shown in
[
41
, Thm. 1]
that the discrete-time and the continuous-time statements are equivalent. Hence, it is sufficient to treat the discrete-time case. Furthermore, using Lemma
6.2
, we may assume that

\displaystyle A(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}(\theta)&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\lambda_{n}(\theta)\end{pmatrix},\quad b(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\vdots\\
1\end{pmatrix},

where
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}
denote the continuous eigenvalue functions with pairwise disjoint images. It follows from
[
41
, Thm. 1]
that the pair
(A,b)
is uniformly ensemble reachable, if and only if
for every
f\in C_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
and every
\varepsilon>0
there is a polynomial
p\in\mathbb{C}[z]
such that

\displaystyle\max_{\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}}\|p(A(\theta))b(\theta)-f(\theta)\|<\varepsilon.

(26)

So, let
f\in C_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
and
\varepsilon>0
be fixed. To prove the statement, we will construct a polynomial
p\in\mathbb{C}[z]
satisfying (
26
). We make the ansatz

p(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{k}(z)q_{k}(z),

(27)

where the polynomials
p_{1},...,p_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}[z]
and
q_{1},...,q_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}[z]
are constructed in two separate steps.

In a first step, denoting
f=\begin{pmatrix}f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\end{pmatrix}^{\top}
, we show that exist polynomials
p_{1},...,p_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}[z]
such that

\max_{\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}}\|p_{i}(\lambda_{i}(\theta))-f_{i}(\theta)\|<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}\quad\text{ for all }i=1,..,n.

(28)

We note that, by conditions (b) and (c), the functions
\lambda_{i}\colon{\mathbf{P}}\to\lambda_{i}({\mathbf{P}})
are homeomorphisms onto their respective image. Thus, condition (
28
) is equivalent to

\max_{z\in\lambda_{i}({\mathbf{P}})}\|p_{i}(z)-f_{i}(\lambda_{i}^{-1}(z))\|<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}\quad\text{ for all }i=1,..,n.

(29)

To see that such polynomials exist, we will use Mergelyan’s Theorem
[
19
, Ch. III, § 2, Sec. A, Theorem 1]
. In order to apply it, we have to verify that sets
\lambda_{1}({\mathbf{P}}),...,\lambda_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
are compact with empty interior and that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\lambda_{i}({\mathbf{P}})
is connected for all
i=1,...,n
. Obviously, the sets
\lambda_{1}({\mathbf{P}}),...,\lambda_{1}({\mathbf{P}})
are compact with empty interior. Moreover, it follows from
[
7
, Corollary 2.2 and 2.5]
that the sets
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\lambda_{i}({\mathbf{P}})
,
i=1,...,n
are connected. Hence, we can apply Mergelyan’s Theorem showing the existence of polynomials
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}[z]
satisfying (
28
).

Second we establish the existence of suitable polynomials
q_{1},...,q_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}[z]
with the aim to approximate the indicator functions of
\lambda_{i}({\mathbf{P}})
. To this end, let

\lambda({\mathbf{P}})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}({\mathbf{P}})

and recall that the assumptions (b) and (c) imply that the sets
\lambda_{1}({\mathbf{P}}),...,\lambda_{n}({\mathbf{P}})
are pairwise disjoint.
To get the polynomials
q_{1},...,q_{n}
, we will use Runge’s Theorem
[
19
, Ch. III, § 1, Sec. B, Theorem 2]
. Choose pairwise disjoint regions
U_{i}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
that properly contain
\lambda_{i}({\mathbf{P}})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, and set
U=\cup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}
. Moreover, we choose holomorphic functions
h_{1},...,h_{n}:U\to\mathbb{C}
such that their restrictions to
\lambda({\mathbf{P}})
satisfy

\displaystyle h_{i}|_{\lambda({\mathbf{P}})}\colon\lambda({\mathbf{P}})\to{\mathbb{C}},\quad h_{i}(z)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }z\in\lambda_{i}({\mathbf{P}})\\
0&\text{ if }z\in\lambda({\mathbf{P}})\setminus\lambda_{i}({\mathbf{P}}).\end{cases}

By applying Runge’s Theorem to
h_{1},...,h_{n}
, there are polynomials
q_{1},...,q_{n}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\lambda_{i}({\mathbf{P}})}|q_{i}(z)-h_{i}(z))|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3\,\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{i,j}},

where

\alpha_{i,j}:=\sup_{\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}}|p_{i}(\lambda_{j}(\theta))|.

The final step is to see that the proposed polynomial
p\in\mathbb{C}[z]
defined in (
27
) satisfies (
26
). It holds that

\displaystyle\max_{\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}}\|p(A(\theta))b(\theta)-f(\theta)\|\leq\max_{\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}}\sup_{i=1,...,n}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{j}(\lambda_{i}(\theta))q_{j}(a_{i}(\theta))-f_{i}(\theta)\right|.

By construction, for each
i\in\{1,...,n\}
and each
\theta\in{\mathbf{P}}
it holds that

\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{j}(\lambda_{i}(\theta))q_{j}(\lambda_{i}(\theta))-f_{i}(\theta)\right|\\
\leq|p_{i}(\lambda_{i}(\theta))q_{i}(\lambda_{i}(\theta))-f_{i}(\theta)|+\left|\sum_{j=1,\,j\neq i}^{n}p_{j}(\lambda_{i}(\theta))q_{j}(\lambda_{i}(\theta))\right|\\
\leq|p_{i}(\lambda_{i}(\theta))-f_{i}(\theta)|+|p_{i}(\lambda_{i}(\theta))|\cdot|q_{i}(\lambda_{i}(\theta))-1|+\sum_{j=1,\,j\neq i}^{n}|p_{j}(\lambda_{i}(\theta))|\cdot|q_{j}(\lambda_{i}(\theta))|<\varepsilon.

This shows the assertion.
∎
