From local to global asymptotic behaviour of orthogonal polynomials
R. Bessonov, A. Nicolau

Abstract
. Let { φ n ∗ } \{\varphi^{*}_{n}\} be the sequence of reflected orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle 𝕋 \mathbb{T} generated by a measure μ \mu of Szegő class, and let D μ D_{\mu} be the Szegő function of μ \mu . We prove the uniform Cesàro asymptotics sup z ∈ Γ ζ ( 1 n ​ ∑ k = 0 n − 1 | | φ k ∗ ​ ( z ) ​ D μ ​ ( z ) | 2 − 1 | ) → 0 , n → ∞ , \sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta}}\Biggl(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Bigl||\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-1\Bigr|\Biggr)\to 0,\qquad n\to\infty, for almost all Stolz angles Γ ζ \Gamma_{\zeta} , ζ ∈ 𝕋 \zeta\in\mathbb{T} . 1.
Introduction

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}=\{z:\;|z|=1\}
. Consider its Radon-Nikodym decomposition,
\mu=w\,dm+\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
. Here and in what follows
m
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
normalized by
m(\mathbb{T})=1
, the function
w
belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})=L^{1}(m)
, and
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
is the singular part of
\mu
. The measure
\mu
is said to belong to the Szegő class
\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
if
\log w\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, or, equivalently,

(1.1)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log w\,dm>-\infty.

Measures of Szegő class play a prominent role in the theory of orthogonal polynomials
[
32
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
31
]
. With each
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
one can associate the unique outer function
D_{\mu}
in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z:|z|<1\}
satisfying
|D_{\mu}|^{2}=w
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
and such that
D_{\mu}(0)>0
. This function is called the Szegő function of
\mu
. Let
\{\varphi_{n}\}
be the sequence of orthogonal polynomials in
L^{2}(\mu)
,
\deg\varphi_{n}=n
, and let
\varphi_{n}^{*}
be the corresponding reflected polynomials defined by
\varphi_{n}^{*}(z)=z^{n}\overline{\varphi_{n}(1/\bar{z})}
. We use the standard normalization

(1.2)

\|\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=1,\qquad\varphi_{n}^{*}(0)>0,\qquad n\geqslant 0.

Szegő theorem determines the asymptotic behavior of reflected polynomials
\varphi_{n}^{*}
in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. It says that

\sup_{n\geqslant 0}\varphi_{n}^{*}(0)<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T}),

and, moreover, for every
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
we have

(1.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{*}_{n}(z)D_{\mu}(z)=1,

uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
. See Theorem 2.4.1 and Theorem 2.7.15 in Simon book
[
30
]
for the proof. One of the most studied questions in the theory of orthogonal polynomials asks under which assumptions on the orthogonality measure
\mu
, relation (
1.3
) holds almost everywhere on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
. Let us briefly describe known results (the reader familiar with the history of the problem can go directly to Section
2
for the results of the present paper).

1.1.
Regular measures, uniform convergence

When studying uniform convergence of
\{\varphi^{*}_{n}\}
on
\mathbb{T}
, it is natural to require smoothness properties on the measure
\mu
. Let us state apparently the best known result in this direction. Let
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
be such that
\mu=w\,dm
and
\log w
is a continuous function in
\mathbb{T}
. Assume its modulus of continuity
\omega(t)=\sup\{|\log w(\xi)-\log w(z)|:|\xi-z|\leqslant t\}
satisfies the following Dini condition

(1.4)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}\,dt<\infty.

Then (
1.3
) holds uniformly on
\mathbb{T}
. Geronimus and Golinski
[
16
]
attributed this result to Grenander and Szegő
[
19
]
. A proof can be found in Golinski
[
17
]
and a local version, that is, considering the modulus of continuity in an arc, in Badkov
[
4
]
. It is worth mentioning that the condition on the continuity of
\log w
is not sufficient and (
1.4
) is really needed. Actually Golinski
[
18
]
proved that there exist weights
w
such that
\log w
is a continuous function on
\mathbb{T}
whose modulus of continuity satisfies
\omega(t)\log(t)\to 0
as
t\to 0
, such that
\{\varphi^{*}_{n}\}
does not converge uniformly on
\mathbb{T}
.

1.2.
Steklov problem for weights uniformly separated from zero

Given
0<\delta<1
, a probability measure
\mu=w\,dm
is in the Steklov class
S_{\delta}
if
w\geqslant\delta
at almost every point in
\mathbb{T}
. To emphasize the role of the measure
\mu
, the orthonormal polynomials with respect to the measure
\mu
will be denoted by
\varphi_{n}(z,\mu)
. In 1921, Steklov raised the conjecture of deciding if
\sup_{n}|\varphi_{n}(z,\mu)|<\infty
for any
z\in\mathbb{T}
provided
\mu\in S_{\delta}
. This conjecture was solved in the negative by Rahmanov
[
29
]
leading to the problem of finding the right asymptotics of
\|\varphi_{n}(z,\mu)\|_{\infty}=\sup\{|\varphi_{n}(z,\mu)|:z\in\mathbb{T}\}
. Consider
M(n,\delta)=\sup\{\|\varphi_{n}(z,\mu)\|_{\infty}:\mu\in S_{\delta}\}
. Aptekarev, Denisov and Tulyakov
[
3
]
proved the remarkable result that fixed
0<\delta<1,

M(n,\delta)
is comparable to
\sqrt{n}
. Later Denisov
[
8
]
considered positive measures
\mu=w\,dm
with
w,\,1/w\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and proved that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\sup\{\|\varphi_{n}(z,\mu)\|_{\infty}:1\leqslant w\leqslant 1+\varepsilon\}\leqslant Cn^{C\varepsilon}
if
0<\varepsilon<1
and
\sup\{\|\varphi_{n}(z,\mu)\|_{\infty}:1\leqslant w\leqslant T\}\leqslant Cn^{1/2-C/T}
if
T>2
. Moreover these estimates are sharp up to the value of the constant
C
. See also Ambroladze
[
2
]
for continuous weights, Denisov and Rush
[
9
]
for
\rm{BMO}
weights, and Alexis, Aptekarev, Denisov
[
1
]
for Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
weights.

1.3.
Averaged convergence, general Szegő measures

In 1991, Máté, Nevai, and Totik
[
22
]
proved a fundamental result on Christoffel functions generated by general measures
\mu
of Szegő class
\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
. In the equivalent language of orthogonal polynomials, it establishes Cesàro convergence

(1.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}=1

for Lebesgue almost every point
z
on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
. In 2008, Findley
[
13
]
showed that (
1.5
) holds almost everywhere on an open arc
I\subset\mathbb{T}
if we assume that the measure
\mu
is regular in the sense of Ullman and the
local
Szegő condition holds, i.e.,
\log w\in L^{1}(I)
. Originally, the study of asymptotic behaviour of Christoffel functions was motivated by the problem of Cesàro summability of Fourier series generated by orthogonal polynomials
[
23
]
, in particular, by the previous work of Freud
[
14
]
. Surprisingly, (
1.5
) turned out to be very important in the seemingly unrelated area of universality properties of orthogonal polynomials. The first application of (
1.5
) in this context is due to Lubinsky
[
21
]
, it fast became a standard method. Totik
[
33
]
discusses a general idea of usage of (
1.5
) in universality problems and gives another proof of Findley’s result. A recent breakthrough by Eichinger, Lukić, and Simanek
[
12
]
showed that Szegő condition (
1.1
) is not really needed for universality, this phenomenon is much more general. Still, under the Szegő condition we know
the scale
at which universality holds (
\sim 1/n
), and this knowledge follows directly from (
1.5
), see the discussion after Theorem 1.2 in
[
12
]
. In the Szegő case we also know a qualitative estimate for
the rate of convergence
of universality limits, see
[
7
]
. This information is not available in the general non-Szegő case due to a usage of compactness in
[
12
]
.

1.4.
Assumptions on the side of recurrence coefficients

In the next portion of convergence results, assumptions on the orthogonality measure
\mu
are formulated in terms of the so-called recurrence coefficients. Let us recall that orthogonal polynomials generated by a probability measure
\mu
with an infinite support on
\mathbb{T}
satisfy the following recurrence relation:

(1.6)

\varphi_{n+1}(z)=\frac{z\varphi_{n}(z)-\overline{a_{n}}\varphi_{n}^{*}(z)}{\sqrt{1-|a_{n}|^{2}}},\quad n\geqslant 0,

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
and some numbers
a_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
,
n\geqslant 0
, that are called recurrence coefficients of
\mu
. It is also known that any sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}\subset\mathbb{D}
is the sequence of recurrence coefficients of a unique probability measure
\mu
with an infinite support on
\mathbb{T}
. See Sections 1.5, 1.7 in
[
30
]
for the proofs of these facts. Heuristically, small recurrence coefficients
\{a_{n}\}
give rise to “regular” measures
\mu
, Szegő functions
D_{\mu}
, and reflected orthogonal polynomials
\varphi_{n}^{*}
. For example, we have
\mu=m
,
D_{\mu}=1
,
\varphi_{n}^{*}=1
,
n\geqslant 0
, for identically zero recurrence coefficients
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
. It is therefore natural to ask about the almost sure convergence property

(1.7)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{*}_{n}(z)D_{\mu}(z)=1\;\;\mbox{for Lebesgue almost every }z\in\mathbb{T},

in terms of the size of the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
. Using formula
(2.4.35)
in Simon
[
30
]
for polynomials
P=\varphi_{k}
, one can see that

(\overline{D_{\mu}(0)^{-1}}D_{\mu}(z)^{-1},\varphi_{k})_{L^{2}(w\,dm)}=\overline{\varphi_{k}(0)},\qquad k\geqslant 0.

Denote by
S
a subset of
\mathbb{T}
such that
m(S)=1
,
\mu_{\mathbf{s}}(S)=0
, and let
\chi_{S}
be the characteristic function of
S
. It is known that
\chi_{S}D_{\mu}^{-1}
can be approximated in
L^{2}(\mu)
by analytic polynomials, see formula
(2.4.34)
in
[
30
]
. It follows that
\chi_{S}D_{\mu}^{-1}
belongs to the closed linear span of the orthonormal sequence
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 0}
in
L^{2}(\mu)
. Then basic Fourier analysis tells us that

(1.8)

\chi_{S}(z)\overline{D_{\mu}(0)^{-1}}D_{\mu}(z)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\varphi_{k}(0)}\varphi_{k}(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{T},

in the sense of convergence in
L^{2}(\mu)
. Moreover, we have

\overline{\varphi_{n}^{*}(0)}\varphi_{n}^{*}(z)-\overline{\varphi_{n}(0)}\varphi_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\overline{\varphi_{k}(0)}\varphi_{k}(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C},\quad n\geqslant 1,

see
(2.2.42)
in
[
30
]
. From here and the Szegő asymptotic relation (
1.3
) at
z=0
we see that (
1.7
) holds if and only if the orthonormal series

(1.9)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}\varphi_{k}

converges pointwise almost everywhere on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
for the special choice of Fourier coefficients
c_{k}=\overline{\varphi_{k}(0)}
. The problem of pointwise convergence of orthogonal series was studied in much detail by Menshov. One of his most striking results
[
25
]
says that for each
\varepsilon>0
and any square-summable sequence of coefficients
\{c_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 0}
the condition

\sum_{k:\;0<|c_{k}|<1}|c_{k}|^{2}\left(\log\log\frac{1}{|c_{k}|}\right)^{2+\varepsilon}\left(\log\frac{1}{|c_{k}|}\right)^{2}<\infty

implies the pointwise convergence of (
1.9
) almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
. In particular, the above condition holds if

\sum_{k\geqslant 0}|c_{k}|^{p}<\infty

for some
0<p<2
. Another theorem of Menshov
[
24
]
(obtained independently by Rademacher
[
28
]
) says that condition

\sum_{k\geqslant 1}|c_{k}|^{2}\log^{2}k<\infty,

is also sufficient for the pointwise convergence of (
1.9
) almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
. Using the fact that the product
\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-|a_{k}|^{2})
converges to a positive number for every
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
(this is another version of the Szegő theorem, see Theorem 2.7.14 in
[
30
]
), and the formula

\varphi_{k+1}(0)=-\frac{\overline{a_{k}}}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})}},\qquad k\geqslant 0,

from Section 1.5 in
[
30
]
, we see from Menshov theorems that any of the assumptions

(1.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\;a_{k}\neq 0}|a_{k}|^{2}\left(\log\log\frac{1}{|a_{k}|}\right)^{2+\varepsilon}\left(\log\frac{1}{|a_{k}|}\right)^{2}<\infty\;\;\mbox{ for some }\;\;\varepsilon>0,

(1.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geqslant 0}|a_{k}|^{p}<\infty\;\;\mbox{ for some }\;\;0<p<2,

(1.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geqslant 1}|a_{k}|^{2}\log^{2}k<\infty,

implies (
1.7
). Similarly, yet another Menshov result
[
25
]
implies that assumption

\sum_{k\geqslant 3}|a_{k}|^{2}(\log\log k)^{2}<\infty

guarantees the Cesàro convergence

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)=1

almost everywhere on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
. The reader can find more information on Menshov’s works and their subsequent development in the review paper by Ul’yanov
[
34
]
.

1.5.
A theorem by Poltoratski and Denisov’s counterexample

Theory of orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle (OPUC) is strongly related to the spectral theory of self-adjoint differential operators with simple spectrum. Let us mention two important papers in that field related to our work. In
[
26
]
, a nonlinear Carleson theorem for Dirac operators is proved, i.e., a continuous version of the statement

\lim_{n\to\infty}|\varphi^{*}_{n}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|=1\;\;\mbox{for every $\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})$ and Lebesgue almost every $z\in\mathbb{T}$.}

An error in
[
26
]
was found by Gevorg Mnatsakanyan. A new variant of the proof is avaliable in the form of arXiv preprint
[
27
]
.

In recent preprints
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
, Denisov provides a counterexample to a strong version of the nonlinear Carleson theorem. He actually constructs
[
10
]
a measure
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
such that (
1.7
) fails.

2.
Main results

Consider a signed Borel measure
\mu
of finite variation on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
. Let us denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mu,z)
the Poisson integral of
\mu
at
z\in\mathbb{D}
, given by

\mathcal{P}(\mu,z)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-\bar{\xi}z|^{2}}\,d\mu(\xi),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

For
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
and
\mu=u\,dm
, we set
\mathcal{P}(u,z)=\mathcal{P}(\mu,z)
. Consider now a probability measure
\mu=w\,dm+\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
on
\mathbb{T}
that belongs to the Szegő class
\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
. Recall that the latter means
\log w\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
. Our analysis of asymptotic behaviour of orthogonal polynomials will be largely connected with the following entropy function of
\mu
:

\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)=\log\mathcal{P}(\mu,z)-\mathcal{P}(\log w,z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

By Jensen inequality,
\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)\geqslant 0
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Take
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
and consider the Stolz angle with vertex at
\zeta
, i.e., the convex hull

\Gamma_{\zeta}=\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}\nolimits\bigl(\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\;|z|\leqslant 1/2\},\,\{\zeta\}\bigr).

Here the constant
1/2
can be replaced by any constant
r\in(0,1)
without essential changes in the forthcoming statements and their proofs.
Properties of the Poisson kernel imply that for every
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
we have

(2.1)

\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to\zeta,\\
z\in\Gamma_{\zeta}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)=0

for Lebesgue almost every
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
. Sometimes we will also require more regularity from the measure
\mu
than just the membership in the Szegő class
\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
. Take
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
and set
z_{n}(\zeta)=(1-2^{-n})\zeta
for
n\geqslant 0
. Note that
\{z_{n}(\zeta)\}
is a sequence in
\Gamma_{\zeta}
approaching the point
\zeta
exponentially fast. As we will check (see the discussion after Proposition
2.4
below), both conditions

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\mathcal{K}(\mu,z_{n}(\zeta))<\infty,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\sqrt{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z_{n}(\zeta))}<\infty,

are weaker than the Dini condition (
1.4
) or its local version on an arc containing
\zeta
. In particular, assumptions (
2.2
), (
2.3
) do not force
w
to be continuous near
\zeta
. Observe also that (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) are the discrete versions of conditions

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathcal{K}(\mu,r\zeta)}{1-r}\,dr<\infty,\qquad\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{K}(\mu,r\zeta)}}{1-r}\,dr<\infty.

Our main results are the following theorems.

.

Let

, then

In particular, (
2.4
) holds for Lebesgue almost every
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
for any

Let

\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
be such that

\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta}}\Biggl(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigl|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)-1\bigr|\Biggr)\to 0,\qquad n\to\infty.

and
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}

) holds at

established in

. Besides the fact that (

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume that
\mu=w\,dm
is such that
u=\log w
is bounded on
\mathbb{T}
and its Fourier coefficients
\hat{u}(k)=(u,z^{k})_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}
satisfy

(2.9)

\sum_{k\geqslant 1}|\hat{u}(k)|^{2}\log k<\infty.

Then (
2.2
) holds at Lebesgue almost every
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mu=w\,dm
be a probability measure in
\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
and let
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
. Assume that
\log w
is bounded at a neighborhood of
\zeta
and

(2.10)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{|\log w(\xi)-\log w(\zeta)|^{2}}{|\xi-\zeta|}\,dm(\xi)<\infty.

Then (
2.2
) holds at
\zeta
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\mu=w\,dm
be a probability measure in
\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
, let
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
and
I_{n}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{T}:|\xi-\zeta|<2^{-n}\}
,
n\geqslant 0
. Assume that
\log w
is bounded in a neighborhood of
\zeta
and

(2.11)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{|I_{n}|}\int_{I_{n}}\bigl|\log w-\log w(\zeta)\bigr|^{2}\,dm\right)^{1/2}<\infty.

Then (
2.3
) holds at
\zeta
.

Theorem
2.3
and Proposition
2.6
imply the classical result of Grenander and Szegő and its improvement by Badkov (see Section
1.1
). Indeed, for
t=|\xi-\zeta|
we have

|\log w(\xi)-\log w(\zeta)|\lesssim|w(\xi)-w(\zeta)|\leqslant\sup_{|z_{1}-z_{2}|\leqslant t}|w(z_{1})-w(z_{2})|=\omega(t)

if
w
is positive and continuous near
\zeta
and
t
is small enough (as usual, we write
g_{1}\lesssim g_{2}
if
g_{1}\leqslant cg_{2}
for a constant
c
that does not depend on the parameters under consideration, i.e., in the above case, on
t
). Therefore, (
2.11
) is weaker than the classical Dini condition (
1.4
):

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{|I_{n}|}\int_{I_{n}}\bigl|\log w-\log w(\zeta)\bigr|^{2}\,dm\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\omega(|I_{n}|)\lesssim

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{-n}}^{2^{-n+1}}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}\,dt\leqslant\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}\,dt<\infty,

or its local version on an arc. Since our estimates for the remainders are qualitative, Theorem

on any open arc of

where

We would like to emphasize that assumptions in Propositions

-

(or, more generally, assumptions (

)–(

)–(

)–(

)–(

)–(

Next, we apply our results to the study of the asymptotic behaviour of the polynomial reproducing kernels

(2.12)

k_{\mu,n}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{\overline{\varphi_{n}^{*}(z_{2})}\varphi_{n}^{*}(z_{1})-\overline{\varphi_{n}(z_{2})}\varphi_{n}(z_{1})}{1-\overline{z_{2}}z_{1}}

\displaystyle k_{\mu,n}(z_{1},z_{2})=|D_{\mu}^{-1}(\zeta)|^{2}\frac{1-\bar{z}_{2}^{n}z_{1}^{n}}{1-\bar{z}_{2}z_{1}}\bigl(1+r_{n}(z_{1},z_{2})\bigr),

(2.14)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup\{\bigl|r_{n}(z_{1},z_{2})\bigl|\colon z_{1,2}\in\mathbb{C},\,|z_{1,2}-\zeta|\leqslant A/n\}=0,

for every

and almost every point

. Moreover, a bound for the rate of convergence in (

) is known, see
[
7
]
. Let us write
g_{1}\asymp g_{2}
for two functions
g_{1}
,
g_{2}
if
g_{1}\lesssim g_{2}
and
g_{2}\lesssim g_{1}
. Recall that the hyperbolic distance in

is defined by

(2.15)

d_{H}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+\rho(z_{1},z_{2})^{2}}{1-\rho(z_{1},z_{2})^{2}},\qquad\rho(z_{1},z_{2})=\left|\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{1-\overline{z_{2}}z_{1}}\right|.

Note that

for two points

,
z_{2}
in some Stolz angle

if and only if

. We prove the following extension of the local universality relation (

).

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
and

be such that

has a non-zero finite non-tangential limit at

if (

) holds,

3.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall the basic theory of orthogonal polynomials and collect some results from
[
7
]
. Given a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}
, its Schur function
f
is defined by the relation

(3.1)

\frac{1+zf(z)}{1-zf(z)}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1+\bar{\xi}z}{1-\bar{\xi}z}\,d\mu(\xi),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Taking the real part in (
3.1
), we get

(3.2)

\frac{1-|zf(z)|^{2}}{|1-zf(z)|^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-\bar{\xi}z|^{2}}\,d\mu(\xi)=\mathcal{P}(\mu,z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Since expressions in (
3.2
) are positive in
\mathbb{D}
, we have
|zf(z)|<1
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
. In other words,
zf
is an analytic mapping from
\mathbb{D}
into itself. By Schwarz lemma and the maximal modulus principle, the same is true about
f
(with the exceptional case when
f
is a unimodular constant, it corresponds to the case where
\mu
is the point mass measure concentrated at a point of
\mathbb{T}
). Conversely, any analytic mapping
f
from
\mathbb{D}
into itself leads to a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}
defined by formula (
3.1
). It is not difficult to check that in this correspondence the finite Blaschke products generate probability measures
\mu
supported on finite subsets of
\mathbb{T}
. In what follows we will always exclude this case from consideration assuming that
\mu
is not a finite linear combination of point masses.

Consider a pair
\mu=w\,dm+\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
,
f
related by (
3.1
). Fatou’s theorem (see Theorem 5.3 in Section I.5 in Garnett
[
15
]
) implies that for Lebesgue almost every
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
we have

(3.3)

\frac{1-|f(\xi)|^{2}}{|1-\xi f(\xi)|^{2}}=w(\xi).

Note that
\mathop{\rm Re}(1-zf(z))\geqslant 0
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, hence the function
1-zf
is outer in
\mathbb{D}
(see Corollary 4.8 in Section II.4 in Garnett
[
15
]
). Hence, we have
\log|1-\xi f|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
and

(3.4)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log{|1-\xi f(\xi)|^{2}}\,dm(\xi)=\log|1-zf(z)|^{2}\Bigr\rvert_{z=0}=0.

This formula and (
3.3
) imply

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log{(1-|f|^{2})}\,dm=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log w\,dm,

where both sides are finite or equal to
-\infty
simultaneously. In other words, we have
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
if and only if
\log(1-|f|^{2})\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
.

Let us now recall the definition of Schur’s algorithm. Take a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}
with an infinite support and consider the Schur function
f
of
\mu
. Define the analytic functions
f_{n}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
by Schur’s algorithm:

(3.5)

zf_{n+1}(z)=\frac{f_{n}(z)-f_{n}(0)}{1-\overline{f_{n}(0)}f_{n}(z)},\quad n\geqslant 0,\qquad f_{0}(z)=f(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

and let
\mu_{n}
be the measures generated by
f_{n}
via (
3.1
). Note that
f_{0}=f
and
\mu_{0}=\mu
. Szegő theorem says that the measure
\mu
is in the Szegő class
\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
if and only if
\sum_{n}|f_{n}(z)|^{2}<\infty
for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and, moreover, in this case

(3.6)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log w\,dm=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log(1-|f|^{2})\,dm=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\log(1-|f_{n}(0)|^{2}).

See Theorem 2.7.14 and Theorem 3.1.4 in Simon
[
30
]
. In
[
5
]
, the following generalization of (
3.6
) was found:

(3.7)

\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\log\left(1+(1-|z|^{2})\frac{|f_{k}(z)|^{2}}{1-|f_{k}(z)|^{2}}\right),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

for every
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
. Nonnegativity of summands in this formula implies that the entropy decreases in the Schur algorithm, that is,

(3.8)

\mathcal{K}(\mu_{n+1},z)\leqslant\mathcal{K}(\mu_{n},z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},\quad n\geqslant 0.

Let us also mention the useful relation

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)=\log(1-|zf(z)|^{2})-\mathcal{P}(\log(1-|f|^{2}),z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

that follows from the definition of
\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)
, formula (
3.2
) and the fact that
1-zf
is an outer function in
\mathbb{D}
(in particular,
\mathcal{P}(\log|1-\xi f(\xi)|^{2},z)=\log|1-zf(z)|^{2}
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
). The same formula for
\mu_{n}
,
f_{n}
reads as

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\mu_{n},z)=\log(1-|zf_{n}(z)|^{2})-\mathcal{P}(\log(1-|f_{n}|^{2}),z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},\quad n\geqslant 0.

Further, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
, we define

(3.11)

\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,n}(z)=\frac{z\varphi_{n-1}(z)-\overline{f_{n-1}(\lambda)}\varphi_{n-1}^{*}(z)}{\sqrt{1-|f_{n-1}(\lambda)|^{2}}},\quad n\geqslant 0,

where we set
f_{-1}\equiv 0
,
z\varphi_{-1}\equiv 1
,
\varphi_{-1}^{*}\equiv 1
. Let also
\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,n}^{*}(z)=z^{n}\overline{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,n}(1/\bar{z})}
be the corresponding reflected polynomials. Note that

(3.12)

\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,n}^{*}(z)=\frac{\varphi_{n-1}^{*}(z)-zf_{n-1}(\lambda)\varphi_{n-1}(z)}{\sqrt{1-|f_{n-1}(\lambda)|^{2}}},\quad n\geqslant 0,

while the usual reflected polynomials
\varphi_{n}^{*}
can be written in the form

\varphi_{n}^{*}(z)=\frac{\varphi_{n-1}^{*}(z)-zf_{n-1}(0)\varphi_{n-1}(z)}{\sqrt{1-|f_{n-1}(0)|^{2}}}=\varphi_{0,n}^{*}(z),\quad n\geqslant 0,

as follows from Szegő recurrence (
1.6
) and Geronimus theorem,

a_{n}=f_{n}(0),\quad n\geqslant 0,

see Theorem 3.1.4 in Simon
[
30
]
. In particular, for
n\geqslant 1
we have

(3.13)

\frac{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,n}^{*}(z)}{\varphi_{n}^{*}(z)}\frac{\sqrt{1-|f_{n-1}(\lambda)|^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-|f_{n-1}(0)|^{2}}}=\frac{1-z\overline{f_{n-1}(\lambda)}b_{n-1}(z)}{1-z\overline{f_{n-1}(0)}b_{n-1}(z)},\qquad b_{n-1}=\frac{\varphi_{n-1}}{\varphi^{*}_{n-1}},

which yields the estimate

(3.14)

\frac{1-|f_{n-1}(\lambda)|}{1+|f_{n-1}(0)|}\leqslant\left|\frac{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,n}^{*}(z)}{\varphi_{n}^{*}(z)}\right|\frac{\sqrt{1-|f_{n-1}(\lambda)|^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-|f_{n-1}(0)|^{2}}}\leqslant\frac{1+|f_{n-1}(\lambda)|}{1-|f_{n-1}(0)|},

that will allow us to switch between
\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,n}^{*}
and
\varphi_{n}^{*}
provided that
f_{n-1}(\lambda)
,
f_{n-1}(0)
are small. Note that (
3.14
) trivially holds also for
n=0
because
\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,0}^{*}=\varphi_{0}^{*}=1
. The main feature of the polynomials
\{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,n}\}
is the entropy bound

(3.15)

\mathcal{K}(\nu_{\lambda,n},\lambda)\leqslant\mathcal{K}(\mu,\lambda),\qquad n\geqslant 0,\quad\nu_{\lambda,n}=\frac{dm}{|\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,n}^{*}|^{2}},

see Corollary 4 in
[
5
]
. Relation (
3.15
) could be rewritten in the form

(3.16)

\mathcal{P}\left(\left|\frac{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,n}^{*}(\lambda)}{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,n}^{*}}-1\right|^{2},\lambda\right)\leqslant e^{\mathcal{K}(\mu,\lambda)}-1,

see Lemma 2.3 in
[
7
]
. The bound (
3.16
) has important consequences that we state and prove below in Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
. These lemmas are not specific for orthogonal polynomials as they work for any polynomials that satisfy entropy bounds of type (
3.16
) with the small enough right hand side. For consistency, we will use notation
\varphi^{*}
for a polynomial without zeroes in the open unit disk. The lemmas will be used later for
\varphi^{*}=\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,n}^{*}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varphi^{*}
be a polynomial of degree at most
n
without zeroes in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, and let
\varphi(z)=z^{n}\overline{\varphi^{*}(1/\bar{z})}
be the corresponding reflected polynomial. Denote by

the Blaschke product generated by
\varphi
,
\varphi^{*}

\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
,

\Gamma_{\zeta}=\mathop{\mathrm{conv}}\nolimits\bigl(\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\;|z|\leqslant r\},\,\{\zeta\}\bigr)

\mathcal{P}\left(\left|\frac{\varphi^{*}(\lambda)}{\varphi^{*}}-1\right|^{2},\lambda\right)\leqslant\eta_{0}

\sup_{z:\;d_{H}(z,\lambda)\leqslant A}\bigl|b(z)-\alpha(\lambda)z^{n}\bigr|\leqslant\varepsilon,

\lambda

).

Proof.
Put
E^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)=\{\xi\in\mathbb{T}:|\varphi^{*}(\lambda)/\varphi^{*}(\xi)-1|>\varepsilon\}

We have

(3.19)

\sup_{z:\;d_{H}(z,\lambda)\leqslant A}\int_{E^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-\bar{\xi}z|^{2}}\,dm(\xi)\to 0,\qquad\eta\to 0.

Since on the set
E_{\varepsilon}(\lambda)
the argument of
\varphi^{*}
(to be denoted by
\arg\varphi^{*}

We now see from (

\sup_{z:\;d_{H}(z,\lambda)\leqslant A}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|b(\xi)-\alpha(\lambda)\xi^{n}|\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-\bar{\xi}z|^{2}}\,dm(\xi)\lesssim\varepsilon

if

is small enough. This relation and the analyticity of

,
z^{n}
imply (
3.18
) provided that (

) holds with

small enough. ∎

\left|\frac{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,n}^{*}(\lambda)}{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda,n}^{*}(z)}-1\right|\leqslant ce^{2A}\sqrt{\mathcal{K}(\nu_{\lambda,n},\lambda)},

for every
z\in\partial\Omega_{n}
, where
\Omega_{n}
is a domain in
\mathbb{C}
with a piece-wise smooth boundary
\partial\Omega_{n}
such that
B(\zeta,A/n)\subset\Omega_{n}\subset B(\zeta,4A/n)
,
B(\zeta,r)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\;|\zeta-z|\leqslant r\}
. Here, the constant
c
is universal.

Let
\eta_{0}
be the best possible
\eta_{0}
in Lemma
3.2
, Lemma
3.3
for the choice of parameters
A_{1}=2
,
A_{2}=8
,
A=2
. In other words, let
\eta_{0}
be the maximum of the all numbers
\eta_{0}
for which
both
Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
work for
A_{1}=2
,
A_{2}=8
,
A=2
. Take a point
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
such that (
2.1
) holds. Set
\lambda_{j}=(1-1/j)\zeta
and define

(3.24)

n_{0}=\min\Bigl\{n\geqslant 8A:\;e^{\mathcal{K}(\mu,\lambda_{j})}-1\leqslant\eta_{0}\mbox{ for all }j\geqslant n\Bigr\}.

We arrive at the following estimates.

Lemma 3.4
.

For every
n\geqslant n_{0}
and
0\leqslant k\leqslant n
, we have

(3.25)

\left|\frac{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(z)}{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(\lambda_{n})}-1\right|\lesssim\sqrt{\mathcal{K}(\mu,\lambda_{n})},\qquad z\in B(\zeta,1/n).

Consequently, we have

(3.26)

\left|\frac{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(z)}{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(\lambda_{n})}\right|^{2}=1+O(\sqrt{\mathcal{K}(\mu,\lambda_{n})}),\qquad z\in B(\zeta,1/n),

where the constant in
O(\cdot)
does not depend on
\mu
,
k
, and
z
.

Proof.
Take
n\geqslant n_{0}
,
0\leqslant k\leqslant n
. Let us use Lemma
3.2
for
\varphi^{*}=\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}
with the parameters
\tilde{n}=k
,
\tilde{A}_{1}=k/n
,
\tilde{A}_{2}=8k/n
in place of
n
,
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
. We have

\max(\tilde{A}_{1},\tilde{A}_{2})/\tilde{n}=8/n\leqslant 8/n_{0}\leqslant 1/2
, as required in Lemma
3.2
. We also have

\lambda_{\tilde{n}}=(1-\tilde{A}_{1}/\tilde{n})\zeta=(1-1/n)\zeta=\lambda_{n}.

Note that
\tilde{A}_{1}\leqslant 1
,
\tilde{A}_{2}\leqslant 8
, therefore, the optimal (i.e., largest possible) number
\tilde{\eta}_{0}
in Lemma
3.2
for this choice of parameters satisfies
\eta_{0}\leqslant\tilde{\eta}_{0}
. Let us check the main assumption (
3.20
) in Lemma
3.2
:

(3.27)

\mathcal{P}\left(\left|\frac{\varphi^{*}(\lambda_{n})}{\varphi^{*}}-1\right|^{2},\lambda_{n}\right)=e^{\mathcal{K}(\nu_{\lambda_{n},k},\lambda_{n})}-1\leqslant e^{\mathcal{K}(\mu,\lambda_{n})}-1\leqslant\eta_{0}\leqslant\tilde{\eta}_{0}.

Lemma
3.2
now tells us that
\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}
has no zeroes in
B(\zeta,\tilde{A}_{2}/\tilde{n})=B(\zeta,8/n)
. This information will be used at the last part of the proof.

Next, we use Lemma
3.3
for
\tilde{n}=k
,
\tilde{A}=2k/n
in place of
n
,
A
. With this choice of parameters, we have

\tilde{\lambda}=(1-\tilde{A}/2\tilde{n})\zeta=\lambda_{n}
. Moreover,

\mathcal{K}(\nu_{\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{n}},\tilde{\lambda})\leqslant\mathcal{K}(\mu,\tilde{\lambda})=\mathcal{K}(\mu,\lambda_{n})\leqslant e^{\mathcal{K}(\mu,\lambda_{n})}-1\leqslant\eta_{0}\leqslant\tilde{\eta}_{0},

for
n\geqslant n_{0}
. Here
\tilde{\eta}_{0}
is the largest possible number in Lemma
3.3
for
\tilde{n}
,
\tilde{A}
, and we have used the fact that

. We see that

(3.28)

\left|\frac{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(\lambda_{n})}{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(z)}-1\right|=\left|\frac{\tilde{\varphi}_{\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{n}}^{*}(\tilde{\lambda})}{\tilde{\varphi}_{\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{n}}^{*}(z)}-1\right|\lesssim e^{2\tilde{A}}\sqrt{\mathcal{K}(\nu_{\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{n}},\tilde{\lambda})}\lesssim\sqrt{\mathcal{K}(\mu,\lambda_{n})},

for every

, where
\Omega_{\tilde{n}}
is a domain in

with a piece-wise smooth boundary

. In the last chain of inclusions we have used the fact that
\tilde{A}/\tilde{n}=2/n
,
4\tilde{A}/\tilde{n}=8/n
. Then from the maximum modulus principle for the function
1/\tilde{\varphi}^{*}_{\lambda_{n},k}
in the domain
\Omega_{\tilde{n}}
, we obtain

(3.29)

\left|\frac{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(\lambda_{n})}{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(z)}-1\right|\lesssim\sqrt{\mathcal{K}(\mu,\lambda_{n})},\qquad z\in\Omega_{\tilde{n}}.

In other words, (

) holds everywhere in

, not just at the boundary

. We used here the fact that
\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}
has no zeroes in

, hence the function

is analytic in

The bounds (

and Theorem

.

4.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

Define the nonnegative functions
\eta_{n}
,
\tilde{\eta}_{n}
in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
by

(4.1)

\displaystyle\eta_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{K}(\mu_{k},z),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\log\frac{1}{1-|f_{k}(z)|}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
. We have

\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta}}\eta_{n}(z)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
such that (
2.1
) holds.

Proof.
The estimate (
3.8
) gives
\eta_{n}(z)\leqslant\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)
for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
n\geqslant 1
. Hence, it is sufficient to prove that
\sup\{\mathcal{K}(\mu_{n},z):|z|<r\}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, for any
0<r<1
. Observe that (
3.9
) gives

\mathcal{K}(\mu_{n},z)\leqslant-\mathcal{P}(\log(1-|f_{n}|^{2}),z)\leqslant-\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log(1-|f_{n}|^{2})\,dm

and one only needs to check that the last integral tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
. In turn, this follows from Szegő formula (
3.6
) for the pair
\mu_{n}
,
f_{n}
, i.e., from

(4.3)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log(1-|f_{n}|^{2})\,dm=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\log(1-|f_{k}(0)|^{2}).

Since by (
3.6
) we have
\sum_{k\geqslant 0}|f_{k}(0)|^{2}<\infty
for every
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
, the right hand side in (
4.3
) indeed tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
. ∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
, and let

\Gamma_{\zeta,n}=\Gamma_{\zeta}\cap\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\,|z-\zeta|\geqslant 1/n\},\qquad n\geqslant 1.

We have
\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}}\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
such that (
2.1
) holds.

Proof.
We first deal with
z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}
such that
\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)\leqslant\log 2
. Let us divide the set of indices
I_{n}=\{k:0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1\}
into two subsets

(4.4)

\displaystyle I_{n,s}

\displaystyle=\left\{k\in I_{n}:\;\;|f_{k}(z)|\leqslant 1/2\right\},

(4.5)

\displaystyle I_{n,b}

\displaystyle=\left\{k\in I_{n}:\;\;|f_{k}(z)|>1/2\right\}.

For
k\in I_{n,s}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in I_{n,s}}\log\frac{1}{1-|f_{k}(z)|}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|f_{k}(z)|\leqslant\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|f_{k}(z)|^{2}}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{(1-|z|^{2})\sum_{k=0}^{n}|f_{k}(z)|^{2}}\lesssim\sqrt{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)},

where in the last estimate we have used (
3.7
) and the fact that
x\lesssim\log(1+x)
for
x\in[0,1]
. Next, for
k\in I_{n,b}
we have

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in I_{n,b}}\log\frac{1}{1-|f_{k}(z)|}\leqslant\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in I_{n,b}}\frac{1+|f_{k}(z)|}{1-|f_{k}(z)|^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{k\in I_{n,b}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})|f_{k}(z)|^{2}}{1-|f_{k}(z)|^{2}}.

Each summand in the last sum satisfies

0\leqslant\frac{(1-|z|^{2})|f_{k}(z)|^{2}}{1-|f_{k}(z)|^{2}}\leqslant 1,

for otherwise we cannot have
\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)\leqslant\log 2
, see (
3.7
). Thus, one can use again the elementary inequality
x\lesssim\log(1+x)
for
x\in[0,1]
and obtain

(4.7)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in I_{n,b}}\log\frac{1}{1-|f_{k}(z)|}\lesssim\sum_{k\in I_{n,b}}\log\left(1+\frac{(1-|z|^{2})|f_{k}(z)|^{2}}{1-|f_{k}(z)|^{2}}\right)\leqslant\mathcal{K}(\mu,z).

Combining (
4.6
), (
4.7
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in I_{n,s}}\log\frac{1}{1-|f_{k}(z)|}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in I_{n,b}}\log\frac{1}{1-|f_{k}(z)|}

(4.8)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)}+\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)\lesssim\sqrt{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)},

for every
z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}
such that
\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)\leqslant\log 2
. Moreover, the constants in (
4.8
) are universal. Thus, the result will follow from (
2.1
) if we show that
\tilde{\eta}_{n}
tends to zero uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
. In turn, this will follow if we show that

\max_{|z|\leqslant r}|f_{n}(z)|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
for every
r\in(0,1)
. So, let us take
z
with
|z|\leqslant r<1
and estimate

|f_{n}(z)|^{2}\leqslant\log\frac{1}{1-|f_{n}(z)|^{2}}=-\log\left(1-|\mathcal{P}(f_{n},z)|^{2}\right)\leqslant\\
\leqslant-\mathcal{P}\left(\log(1-|f_{n}|^{2}),z\right)\lesssim-\frac{1+r}{1-r}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log(1-|f_{n}|^{2})\,dm,

where we used Jensen’s inequality for the convex function
x\mapsto-\log(1-x^{2})
and the basic bound for the Poisson kernel. Then, as in the proof of Lemma
4.1
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log(1-|f_{n}|^{2})\,dm=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\log(1-|f_{k}(0)|^{2})=0,

and the result follows. ∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mu=w\,dm+\mu_{\mathbf{s}}
be a measure in
\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
, let
D_{\mu}
be the corresponding Szegő function, and let
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
be the sequence of orthonormal polynomials generated by
\mu
, see (
1.2
). We have

(4.9)

\varphi_{n}^{*}D_{\mu}=\frac{O_{n}}{1-zb_{n}f_{n}},\qquad b_{n}=\frac{\varphi_{n}}{\varphi_{n}^{*}},\qquad n\geqslant 0,

for the outer function
O_{n}
defined by the conditions
O_{n}(0)>0
and
|O_{n}|^{2}=1-|f_{n}|^{2}
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.
Recall that the reflected polynomials
\varphi_{n}^{*}
have no zeroes in the closed unit disk, see, e.g., Theorem 1.7.1 in Simon
[
30
]
. Therefore,
b_{n}
is a finite Blaschke product,
\mathop{\rm Re}(1-zb_{n}f_{n})\geqslant 0
in
\mathbb{D}
, and the function
1-zb_{n}f_{n}
is outer (see Corollary 4.8 in Section II.4 in Garnett
[
15
]
). From here we see that functions
\varphi_{n}^{*}D_{\mu}
and
O_{n}(1-zb_{n}f_{n})^{-1}
are outer as well. Note that they have positive value at
0
. Thus, we only need to check that

(4.10)

|\varphi_{n}^{*}D_{\mu}|=\left|\frac{O_{n}}{1-zb_{n}f_{n}}\right|,

almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
. Taking the square and using the definitions of
D_{\mu}
,
O_{n}
, we see that (
4.10
) is equivalent to

|\varphi_{n}^{*}|^{2}w=\frac{1-|f_{n}|^{2}}{|1-zb_{n}f_{n}|^{2}}

almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
. This formula is due to S. Khrushchev, see Theorem 2 on page 173 in
[
20
]
. ∎

Lemma 4.4
.

We have

(4.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Bigl|\log(1-zb_{k}(z)f_{k}(z))\Bigr|\leqslant\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z),

(4.12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\log|O_{k}(z)|^{-2}\leqslant\eta_{n}(z)+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z),

for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
. In (
4.11
), we deal with the main branch of the logarithm,
\log 1=0
.

Proof.
Inequality (
4.11
) is immediate from the definition of
\tilde{\eta}_{n}
and the estimate

\Bigl|\log(1-zb_{k}(z)f_{k}(z))\Bigr|\leqslant\log\frac{1}{1-|zb_{k}(z)f_{k}(z)|}\leqslant\log\frac{1}{1-|f_{k}(z)|},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

To prove (
4.12
), we write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\log|O_{k}(z)|^{-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{P}(\log|O_{k}|^{-2},z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{P}(\log(1-|f_{k}|^{2}),z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{K}(\mu_{k},z)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\log(1-|zf_{k}(z)|^{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant\eta_{n}(z)+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z).

First equality here holds because
O_{k}
is an outer function for each
k\geqslant 0
. In the second line we used the fact that that
|O_{k}|^{2}=1-|f_{k}|^{2}
on
\mathbb{T}
,
k\geqslant 0
. Equality in the third line follows from (
3.10
), and the last estimate from
-\log(1-|zf_{k}(z)|^{2})\leqslant-\log(1-|f_{k}(z)|)
. ∎

Below we will use the bound

(4.13)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|D_{\mu}(z)\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)|^{2}\leqslant 1+\delta_{n}(\mu,z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

where

\delta_{n}(\mu,z)=2\sqrt{\frac{e^{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)}-1}{n(1-|z|^{2})}}+4\frac{e^{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)}-1}{n(1-|z|^{2})}.

For the proof of (
4.13
), see Lemma 2.2 in
[
6
]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

We have

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Bigl||\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-1\Bigr|\lesssim\delta_{n}(\mu,z)+\eta_{n}(z)+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z),

for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.
Fix
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Let us divide the set of indices
J_{n}=\{k:0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1\}
into two subsets,

(4.14)

\displaystyle J_{n,s}

\displaystyle=\left\{k\in J_{n}:\;\;\Bigl|\log|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|\Bigr|\leqslant 1\right\},

(4.15)

\displaystyle J_{n,b}

\displaystyle=\left\{k\in J_{n}:\;\;\Bigl|\log|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|\Bigr|>1\right\}.

For
k\in J_{n,s}
, we have

\Bigl||\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-1\Bigr|\lesssim\Bigl|\log|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}\Bigr|\leqslant\Bigl|\log|O_{k}(z)|^{2}\Bigr|+\Bigl|\log|1-zb_{k}(z)f_{k}(z)|^{2}\Bigr|,

where in the second inequality we have used Lemma
4.3
. From (
4.11
), (
4.12
) we obtain

(4.16)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in J_{n,s}}\Bigl||\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-1\Bigr|\lesssim\eta_{n}(z)+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

To handle indices
k\in J_{n,b}
, we use (
4.13
). Together with (
4.16
) it gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in J_{n,b}}|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|D_{\mu}(z)\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)|^{2}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in J_{n,s}}|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+\delta_{n}(\mu,z)-\frac{|J_{n,s}|}{n+1}+O(\eta_{n}(z)+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z))

(4.17)

\displaystyle=\frac{|J_{n,b}|}{n+1}+\delta_{n}(\mu,z)+O(\eta_{n}(z)+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z)).

It follows that

(4.18)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in J_{n,b}}\Bigl||\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-1\Bigr|\leqslant 2\frac{|J_{n,b}|}{n}+\delta_{n}(\mu,z)+O(\eta_{n}(z)+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z)).

Recall that the sets
I_{n,s}
,
I_{n,b}
were defined in (
4.4
), (
4.5
), respectively. We have

\frac{|J_{n,b}|}{n}=\frac{|J_{n,b}\cap I_{n,s}|}{n}+\frac{|J_{n,b}\cap I_{n,b}|}{n}\leqslant\frac{|J_{n,b}\cap I_{n,s}|}{n}+\frac{|I_{n,b}|}{n}.

Note that

\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\log\frac{1}{1-|f_{k}(z)|}\geqslant\frac{|I_{n,b}|}{n}\log 2,

and if

, Lemma
4.3
gives
\log|O_{k}(z)|^{-2}\gtrsim 1
. Then (
4.12
) yields

\frac{|J_{n,b}\cap I_{n,s}|}{n}\lesssim\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\log|O_{k}(z)|^{-2}\leqslant\eta_{n}(z)+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z).

We deduce that

(4.19)

\frac{|J_{n,b}|}{n}\lesssim\eta_{n}(z)+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z).

Taking into account (
4.18
), this completes the proof. ∎

Lemma
4.5
together with Lemma
4.1
and Lemma

will give us the asymptotic relation

(4.20)

\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Bigl||\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-1\Bigr|\to 0,\qquad n\to\infty.

In the remaining piece
\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta,n}:=\Gamma_{\zeta}\setminus\Gamma_{\zeta,n}
of the Stolz angle

in the following form:

\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta,n}=\Gamma_{\zeta}\cap\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\,|z-\zeta|<1/n\},\qquad n\geqslant 1.

We also define

(4.21)

\displaystyle R_{1}(n)

\displaystyle=\sup_{z_{1,2}\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta,n}}\left|\frac{D_{\mu}(z_{1})}{D_{\mu}(z_{2})}\right|^{2},

(4.22)

\displaystyle R_{2}(n)

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}}(\delta_{n}(\mu,z)+\eta_{n}(z)+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z)).

It is clear that
R_{1}(n)\to 1
and
R_{2}(n)\to 0
for every

such that (

) holds and
|D_{\mu}|
has a non-zero finite non-tangential limit at
\zeta
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
. For every
\Lambda\subset\{0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1\}
we have

(4.23)

\sup_{z\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta,n}}\frac{\sum_{k\in\Lambda}\Bigl||\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-1\Bigr|}{n}\lesssim\frac{R_{1}(n)|\Lambda|}{n}+R_{1}(n)R_{2}(n),

for all
n\geqslant 1
.

Proof.
Take
z\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta,n}
and consider
\lambda(z)=rz
where
r\in(1-1/n,1)
is chosen so that
|\lambda(z)|=1-1/n
. Note that
\lambda(z)\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}
. Since
\varphi_{k}^{*}
has no zeroes in
\mathbb{D}
, we have

|\varphi_{k}^{*}(\lambda(z))|^{2}\geqslant\left(\frac{1+r}{2}\right)^{k}|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)|^{2}\gtrsim|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)|^{2},\qquad 0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1,

see Lemma 2.3 in
[
6
]
. Next, consider the sets
J_{n,s}
,
J_{n,b}
defined in (
4.14
), (
4.15
) with
\lambda(z)
in place of
z
.
We have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\Lambda}|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim R_{1}(n)\sum_{k\in\Lambda}|\varphi_{k}^{*}(\lambda(z))D_{\mu}(\lambda(z))|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim R_{1}(n)\Bigl(\sum_{k\in\Lambda\cap J_{n,s}}1+\sum_{k\in\Lambda\cap J_{n,b}}|\varphi_{k}^{*}(\lambda(z))D_{\mu}(\lambda(z))|^{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim R_{1}(n)|\Lambda|+R_{1}(n)\sum_{k\in J_{n,b}}|\varphi_{k}^{*}(\lambda(z))D_{\mu}(\lambda(z))|^{2}.

Now the claim follows from (
4.17
), (
4.19
) applied to
\lambda(z)
in place of

. ∎

In the next proof we switch from the polynomials
\varphi^{*}_{n}
to polynomials
\tilde{\varphi}^{*}_{\lambda,n}
defined in (

). The fact that polynomials
\tilde{\varphi}^{*}_{\lambda,n}
have a small distortion near

(see (

for

z\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta,n}
by using the asymptotics of
\varphi^{*}_{n}(\lambda_{n})
at the point
\lambda_{n}=(1-1/n)\zeta
in the region
\Gamma_{\zeta,n}
. The latter asymptotics was already found in (

).

Given
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, let us consider the sets
I_{n,s}(\varepsilon)
,

defined by

\displaystyle I_{n,s}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\left\{0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1:\;\;|f_{k-1}(\lambda)|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\},

(4.25)

\displaystyle I_{n,b}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\left\{0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1:\;\;|f_{k-1}(\lambda)|>\varepsilon\right\},

where, as before
f_{-1}=0
. By construction, see (

), for every
k\in I_{n,s}(\varepsilon)
we have

(4.26)

\Biggl|\left|\frac{\tilde{\varphi}^{*}_{\lambda,k}(z)}{\varphi^{*}_{k}(z)}\right|^{2}-1\Biggr|\lesssim\varepsilon,\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
, be such that (
2.1
) holds and
|D_{\mu}|
has a non-zero finite non-tangential limit at
\zeta
, let
n_{0}
be defined by (
3.24
),
\lambda_{n}=(1-1/n)\zeta
. For
\varepsilon>0
, consider the set
I_{n,s}(\varepsilon)
from (
4.24
) generated by
\lambda=\lambda_{n}
. We have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta,n}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in I_{n,s}(\varepsilon)}\Bigl||\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-1\Bigr|\right)\lesssim\varepsilon.

Proof.
At first, observe that for every
k\in I_{n,s}(\varepsilon)
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
relation (
4.26
) implies

\Bigl||\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-|\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}\Bigr|\lesssim\varepsilon|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}.

Therefore, we have

\sum\limits_{k\in I_{n,s}(\varepsilon)}\Bigl||\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-1\Bigr|\lesssim\sum\limits_{k\in I_{n,s}(\varepsilon)}\Bigl||\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-1\Bigr|+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}.

Recall that

\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}\leqslant 1+\delta_{n}(\mu,z)

by (
4.13
), and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta,n}}\delta_{n}(\mu,z)=0
. Thus, we only need to check that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta,n}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k\in I_{n,s}(\varepsilon)}\Bigl||\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-1\Bigr|\right)=0.

For
z\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta,n}
and
0\leqslant k\leqslant n
,
n\geqslant n_{0}
, with
n_{0}
from (
3.24
), relation (

) gives

\frac{1+O(\sqrt{\mathcal{K}(\mu,\lambda_{n})})}{R_{1}(n)}\leqslant\left|\frac{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)}{\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(\lambda_{n})D_{\mu}(\lambda_{n})}\right|^{2}\leqslant R_{1}(n)\cdot(1+O(\sqrt{\mathcal{K}(\mu,\lambda_{n})})),

where

is defined in (

. It follows that

\sup_{z\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta,n}}\Bigl||\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-|\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(\lambda_{n})D_{\mu}(\lambda_{n})|^{2}\Bigr|\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}|\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(\lambda_{n})D_{\mu}(\lambda_{n})|^{2},

for some sequence
\varepsilon_{n}
such that

.

\sup_{z\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta,n}}\bigl||\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-1\bigr|\lesssim\bigl||\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(\lambda_{n})D_{\mu}(\lambda_{n})|^{2}-1\bigr|+\varepsilon_{n}|\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(\lambda_{n})D_{\mu}(\lambda_{n})|^{2}.

Finally, Lemma
4.5
for the point

gives

\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k\in I_{n,s}(\varepsilon)}\bigl||\tilde{\varphi}_{\lambda_{n},k}^{*}(\lambda_{n})D_{\mu}(\lambda_{n})|^{2}-1\bigr|\lesssim\delta_{n}(\mu,\lambda_{n})+\eta_{n}(\lambda_{n})+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(\lambda_{n}),

and the claim follows from Lemma
4.1
, Lemma
4.2
, and the fact that
\lambda_{n}\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}
. ∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
2.2

The proof of Theorem
2.2
uses the same tools as the proof of Theorem
2.1
: Khrushchev-type formula (
4.9
) and the entropy function
\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)
. The latter controls Schur functions of
\mu
that appear in (
4.9
), see Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
. Recall that we already know the averaged asymptotic behaviour of
|\varphi_{n}^{*}D_{\mu}|
under assumption (
2.1
), that is, when
\lim_{r\to 1}\mathcal{K}(\mu,r\zeta)=0
, see Theorem
2.1
. The stronger assumption (
2.2
), that we reproduce here,

(2.2)

\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\mathcal{K}(\mu,z_{n}(\zeta))<\infty,\qquad z_{n}(\zeta)=(1-2^{-n})\zeta,

will allow us to pass from
|\varphi_{n}^{*}D_{\mu}|
to
\varphi_{n}^{*}D_{\mu}
. For this we utilize the fact that
\varphi_{n}^{*}D_{\mu}
is an outer function in
\mathbb{D}
with a positive value at
0
, hence
\log\varphi_{n}^{*}D_{\mu}
is a properly defined analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
which is completely determined by its real part
\log|\varphi_{n}^{*}D_{\mu}|
via the operator of harmonic conjugation,

\mathcal{Q}:u\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(\xi)\mathop{\rm Im}\left(\frac{1+\bar{\xi}z}{1-\bar{\xi}z}\right)dm(\xi),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Assumption (
2.2
) will allow us to prove that
\mathop{\rm Im}\log\varphi_{n}^{*}D_{\mu}=\mathcal{Q}(\log|\varphi_{n}^{*}D_{\mu}|)
is small in
\Gamma_{\zeta}
, hence
\log\varphi_{n}^{*}D_{\mu}
is close to
\log|\varphi_{n}^{*}D_{\mu}|
, which, in turn, is close to
0
. This will lead to the fact that
\varphi_{n}^{*}D_{\mu}
converges to
1
in the strong Cesàro sense uniformly in
\Gamma_{\zeta}
, i.e., to the conclusion of Theorem
2.2
.

Let us recall the definition of truncated cones

\Gamma_{\zeta,n}=\{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta}:|z-\zeta|\geqslant 1/n\}.

We start with a simple lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}}|(\mathcal{Q}u)(z)|}{n}=0,\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{T}.

Proof.
For every
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
and
n\geqslant 1
, we have

(5.1)

\frac{\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}}|(\mathcal{Q}u)(z)|}{n}\leqslant\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{2|u(\xi)|}{n|1-\bar{\xi}z|}\,dm(\xi)\leqslant 2\|u\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
be a function with
\mathcal{Q}u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
and such that
\|u-u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\leqslant\varepsilon
. Applying (
5.1
) to
u-u_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}}|(\mathcal{Q}u)(z)|}{n}\leqslant

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}}|(\mathcal{Q}u-\mathcal{Q}u_{\varepsilon})(z)|}{n}+

\displaystyle+\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}}|(\mathcal{Q}u_{\varepsilon})(z)|}{n}\leqslant 2\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, the lemma follows. ∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
are such that (
2.2
) holds. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\mathop{\rm Im}\log O_{k}(z)|=0.

Proof.
We will use outer functions
O_{n}
from Lemma
4.3
. Set
\alpha_{k}(z)=\mathop{\rm Im}\log O_{k}(z)
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
,
k\geqslant 0
. Given a positive integer
n
, let
K_{n}
be the integer part of
\log_{2}n
. We first show that there exists a constant
c(\mu,\zeta)>0
such that

(5.2)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\alpha_{k}(z_{j+1})-\alpha_{k}(z_{j})|\leqslant c(\mu,\zeta)\mathcal{K}(\mu,z_{j}),\qquad 1\leqslant j\leqslant K_{n},

where we set
z_{j}=z_{j}(\zeta)=(1-2^{-j})\zeta
for brevity. Fix
1\leqslant j\leqslant K_{n}
. We have

\displaystyle|\alpha_{k}(z_{j+1})-\alpha_{k}(z_{j})|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\log(1-|f_{k}|^{2})|\left|\frac{1+\bar{\xi}z_{j+1}}{1-\bar{\xi}z_{j+1}}-\frac{1+\bar{\xi}z_{j}}{1-\bar{\xi}z_{j}}\right|dm

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\log(1-|f_{k}|^{2})|\frac{1-|z_{j}|^{2}}{|1-\bar{\xi}z_{j}|^{2}}dm

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}(\mu_{k},z_{j})-\log(1-|z_{j}f_{k}(z_{j})|^{2}).

Last identity is formula (
3.10
). Since by (
3.8
) we have
\mathcal{K}(\mu_{k},z)\leqslant\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)
for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
, summing up over
k
for fixed
j
and using
|z_{j}f_{k}(z_{j})|\leqslant|f_{k}(z_{j})|
, we obtain

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\alpha_{k}(z_{j+1})-\alpha_{k}(z_{j})|\lesssim\mathcal{K}(\mu,z_{j})-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\log(1-|f_{k}(z_{j})|^{2}).

As in (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), consider the sets
I_{n,s}=\left\{0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1\colon|f_{k}(z_{j})|\leqslant 1/2\right\}
and
I_{n,b}=\left\{0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1\colon|f_{k}(z_{j})|>1/2\right\}
. Since
j\leqslant K_{n}
, we have
1/n\lesssim 1-|z_{j}|^{2}
and then

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in I_{n,s}}\log(1-|f_{k}(z_{j})|^{2})

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in I_{n,s}}|f_{k}(z_{j})|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-|z_{j}|^{2})|f_{k}(z_{j})|^{2}\lesssim\mathcal{K}(\mu,z_{j}),

where in the last estimate we have used (
3.7
). Next we consider the contribution coming from indices in
I_{n,b}
. Since
c_{1}(\mu,\zeta)=\sup_{j\geqslant 0}\mathcal{K}(\mu,z_{j})
is finite, formula (
3.7
) gives

\sup_{k,j}\frac{(1-|z_{j}|^{2})|f_{k}(z_{j})|^{2}}{1-|f_{k}(z_{j})|^{2}}\leqslant e^{c_{1}(\mu,\zeta)}.

It follows that for
k\in I_{n,b}
we have

\displaystyle-(1-|z_{j}|^{2})\log(1-|f_{k}(z_{j})|^{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1-|z_{j}|^{2}}{1-|f_{k}(z_{j})|^{2}}\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\frac{(1-|z_{j}|^{2})|f_{k}(z_{j})|^{2}}{1-|f_{k}(z_{j})|^{2}}\leqslant

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{2}(\mu,\zeta)\log\left(1+\frac{(1-|z_{j}|^{2})|f_{k}(z_{j})|^{2}}{1-|f_{k}(z_{j})|^{2}}\right),

with the constant

c_{2}(\mu,\zeta)=\sup_{0\leqslant y\leqslant e^{c_{1}(\mu,\zeta)}}\frac{y/4}{\log(1+y)}.

Since
1/n\lesssim 1-|z_{j}|^{2}
, we deduce from (
3.7
) the bound

-\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k\in I_{n,b}}\log(1-|f_{k}(z_{j})|^{2})\leqslant c_{2}(\mu,\zeta)\mathcal{K}(\mu,z_{j}).

This finishes the proof of (
5.2
). Now using (
5.2
) and the fact that
\alpha_{k}(z_{0})=\mathop{\rm Im}\log O_{k}(0)=0
for all
k\geqslant 0
, a telescopic sum argument shows that

(5.3)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\alpha_{k}(z_{j+1})|\leqslant c(\mu,\zeta)\sum_{s=0}^{j}\mathcal{K}(\mu,z_{s}),\qquad 1\leqslant j\leqslant K_{n}.

Next, given
z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}
, let
j(z)\geqslant 1
be the integer such that
|z_{j(z)}|<|z|\leqslant|z_{j(z)+1}|
. Repeating the proof of (
5.2
), we obtain

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\alpha_{k}(z)-\alpha_{k}(z_{j(z)})|\leqslant c(\mu,\zeta)\mathcal{K}(\mu,z_{j(z)}),

with a possibly larger constant
c(\mu,\zeta)
that still does not depend on
n
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then (
5.3
) gives

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\alpha_{k}(z)|\leqslant c(\mu,\zeta)\sum_{s=0}^{j(z)}\mathcal{K}(\mu,z_{s}).

Recall that measures
\mu_{n}
,
n\geqslant 0
, were defined using the Schur’s algorithm (
3.5
). Since
\mathcal{K}(\mu_{n},z)\leqslant\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)
for every
n\geqslant 0
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
by (
3.8
), our construction gives
c(\mu_{n},\zeta)\leqslant c(\mu,\zeta)
for every
n\geqslant 0
. Now take a large number
\ell\geqslant 0
and apply the first part of the proof to
\mu_{\ell}
in place of
\mu
. We obtain

(5.4)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\alpha_{\ell+k}(z)|\leqslant c(\mu_{\ell},\zeta)\sum_{s=0}^{j(z)}\mathcal{K}(\mu_{\ell},z_{s})\leqslant c(\mu,\zeta)\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(\mu_{\ell},z_{s}).

Note that the last expression tends to
0
as
\ell\to\infty
. Indeed, assumption (
2.2
) implies that
\sum_{s=s_{0}}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(\mu_{\ell},z_{s})\leqslant\sum_{s=s_{0}}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(\mu,z_{s})\to 0
as
s_{0}\to\infty
, and we have shown in the proof Lemma
4.1
that
\mathcal{K}(\mu_{\ell},z_{s})\to 0
for each fixed point
z_{s}
as
\ell\to\infty
(note that Lemma
4.1
holds under the assumption (
2.1
) which is weaker than (
2.2
)). Since for each fixed
\ell\geqslant 0
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s=0}^{\ell}|\alpha_{s}(z)|\leqslant\sum_{s=0}^{\ell}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}}|\alpha_{s}(z)|}{n}=0,

by Lemma
5.1
, relation (
5.4
) implies the claim. ∎

We are now ready to prove the uniform convergence on the truncated cones
\Gamma_{\zeta,n}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
are such that (
2.2
) holds. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)-1|=0.

Proof.
For
z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}
, consider the sets of indices

\displaystyle J_{n,s}

\displaystyle=\{0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1\colon|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|\leqslant 2\},

\displaystyle J_{n,b}

\displaystyle=\{0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1\colon|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|>2\}.

By Lemma
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in J_{n,b}}\Bigl|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)-1\Bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Bigl||\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-1\Bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta_{n}(\mu,z)+\eta_{n}(z)+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z),

which by Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
tends to
0
uniformly on
\Gamma_{\zeta,n}
as
n\to\infty
. Next, Lemma
4.4
says that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Bigl|\log(1-zb_{k}(z)f_{k}(z))\Bigr|\leqslant\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\log|O_{k}(z)|^{-2}\leqslant\eta_{n}(z)+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

We also have
\varphi_{k}^{*}D_{\mu}=O_{k}(1-zb_{k}f_{k})^{-1}
by Lemma
4.3
. Using the elementary estimate

|re^{i\theta}-1|\leqslant|r-1|+|e^{i\theta}-1|\lesssim|\log r|+|\theta|,

for
0<r\leqslant 2
,
|\theta|\leqslant\pi
, we get for
k\in J_{n,s}

\displaystyle|\varphi_{k}^{*}D_{\mu}-1|

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl|\log|O_{k}(1-zb_{k}f_{k})^{-1}|\bigr|+\bigl|\mathop{\rm Im}\log O_{k}(1-zb_{k}f_{k})^{-1}\bigr|,

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigl|\log|O_{k}|\bigr|+2\bigl|\log(1-zb_{k}f_{k})^{-1}\bigr|+|\mathop{\rm Im}\log O_{k}|,

\displaystyle\leqslant\log|O_{k}|^{-2}+2\bigl|\log(1-zb_{k}f_{k})\bigr|+|\mathop{\rm Im}\log O_{k}|.

Then

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in J_{n,s}}|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)-1|\lesssim\eta_{n}(z)+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z)+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in J_{n,s}}|\mathop{\rm Im}\log O_{k}(z)|,

which by Lemma
4.1
, Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
5.2
tends to
0
uniformly on
\Gamma_{\zeta,n}
. This finishes the proof. ∎

Next we focus on the uniform convergence on
\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta,n}=\Gamma_{\zeta}\setminus\Gamma_{\zeta,n}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
be such that
D_{\mu}
has a non-zero finite non-tangential limit at
\zeta
. Assume that condition (
2.2
) holds at
\zeta
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta,n}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)-1|=0.

Proof.
For
z\in\Gamma^{\prime}_{\zeta,n}
, let
J_{n,b}(z)=\{0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1\colon|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|>2\}
. Observe that

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in J_{n,b}(z)}|\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)-1|\lesssim\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in J_{n,b}(z)}\bigl||\varphi_{k}^{*}(z)D_{\mu}(z)|^{2}-1\bigr|,

would be a more accurate notation for this function. However, we will deal with
\tilde{b}_{n}
only at the same point
z
, therefore, the short notation
\tilde{b}_{n}
should not lead to misunderstanding. By definition, we have

\displaystyle|\tilde{b}_{n}(z)-b_{n}(z)|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{zb_{n-1}(z)-\overline{f_{n-1}(z)}}{1-zb_{n-1}(z)f_{n-1}(z)}-\frac{zb_{n-1}(z)-\overline{f_{n-1}(0)}}{1-zb_{n-1}(z)f_{n-1}(0)}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2|f_{n-1}(0)|+2|f_{n-1}(z)|}{(1-|f_{n-1}(0)|)(1-|f_{n-1}(z)|)}.

It follows that
|\tilde{b}_{n}(z)-b_{n}(z)|\lesssim|f_{n-1}(0)|+|f_{n-1}(z)|
if
\max\{|f_{n-1}(z)|,|f_{n-1}(0)|\}\leqslant 1/2
. Moreover, we have

|\tilde{b}_{n}(z)-\alpha_{z}z^{n}|^{2}\lesssim e^{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)}-1,\qquad|\alpha_{z}|=1,

by Lemma 2.5 in
[
7
]
and (
3.15
). Then (
7.13
) follows. To prove (
7.14
), we estimate

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=n/2}^{n}|b_{k}(z)|\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{e^{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)}-1}+\frac{2}{n(1-|z|)}+

\displaystyle+\frac{2|\{n/2\leqslant k\leqslant n:\;|f_{k-1}(z)|>1/2\}|}{n}+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{e^{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)}-1}+\frac{2}{n(1-|z|)}+\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z),

where
\tilde{\eta}_{n}(z)
is defined in (
4.2
). Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}(\delta_{n},\Delta_{n})}\frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=n/2}^{n}|b_{k}(z)|=0,

for any sequences
\Delta_{n}\to 1
,
\delta_{n}\to\infty
with
\delta_{n}=o(n)
, by (
2.1
) and Lemma
4.2
(for the definition of

\Gamma_{\zeta,n}(\delta_{n},\Delta_{n})
, see (
7.2
)). At the same time, we have
\varphi_{n}^{*}\to D_{\mu}^{-1}
,
\varphi_{n}\to 0
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
by Szegő theorem, hence

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta}(\Delta_{n})}\frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=n/2}^{n}|b_{k}(z)|=0

for some sequence
\Delta_{n}\to 1
. Since
\Gamma_{\zeta}(\Delta_{n})\cup\Gamma_{\zeta,n}(\delta_{n},\Delta_{n})=\Gamma_{\zeta,n}(\delta_{n})
, see (
7.2
)-(
7.4
), this implies that

\mathcal{B}_{n}:=\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}(\delta_{n})}\frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=n/2}^{n}|b_{k}(z)|\to 0,\quad n\to\infty.

Now given a positive integer
N
, pick the positive integer
n
with
2^{n-1}\leqslant N<2^{n}
. Then

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}|b_{k}(z)|\leqslant\frac{2}{2^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}2^{j}\mathcal{B}_{2^{j}}

which tends to
0
as
N\to\infty
. This implies (
7.14
). ∎

Lemma 7.4
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and every complex numbers
x_{k}
,
y_{k}
, such that

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(|x_{k}-1|+\bigl||x_{k}|^{2}-1\bigr|+|y_{k}-1|+\bigl||y_{k}|^{2}-1\bigr|\right)<\varepsilon

we have

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|x_{k}y_{k}-1|<6\varepsilon.

Proof.
Define
I_{n,s}=\{0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1\colon|x_{k}|+|y_{k}|\leqslant 4\}
and
I_{n,b}=\{0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1\colon|x_{k}|+|y_{k}|>4\}
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in I_{n,s}}|x_{k}y_{k}-1|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in I_{n,s}}|x_{k}-1||y_{k}|+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\in I_{n,s}}|y_{k}-1|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{4}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|x_{k}-1|+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|y_{k}-1|<4\varepsilon.

On the other hand, for
k\in I_{n,b}
we have either
|x_{k}|>2
or
|y_{k}|>2
, hence

\displaystyle|x_{k}y_{k}-1|

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+\max(|x_{k}|^{2},|y_{k}|^{2}),

\displaystyle\leqslant\max(|x_{k}-1|,|y_{k}-1|)+2\max(\bigl||x_{k}|^{2}-1\bigr|,\bigl||y_{k}|^{2}-1\bigr|),

\displaystyle\leqslant 2(|x_{k}-1|+\bigl||x_{k}|^{2}-1\bigr|+|y_{k}-1|+\bigl||y_{k}|^{2}-1\bigr|),

and our assumption implies the bound
n^{-1}\sum_{k\in I_{n,b}}|x_{k}y_{k}-1|<2\varepsilon
. ∎

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
be such that
D_{\mu}
has a non-zero finite non-tangential limit at
\zeta
. If (
2.2
) holds at
\zeta
, then we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\Gamma_{\zeta}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigl|r_{k}(z_{1},z_{2})\bigl|=0

for the functions
r_{n}
defined in (
7.1
).

Proof.
Given a sequence
\delta_{n}\to\infty
,
\delta_{n}=o(n)
, let

\displaystyle\Omega_{n}(\delta_{n})

\displaystyle=\{(z_{1},z_{2}):\;z_{1}\mbox{ or }z_{2}\in\Gamma_{\zeta,n}(\delta_{n})\},

\displaystyle\Omega^{\prime}_{n}(\delta_{n})

\displaystyle=\{(z_{1},z_{2}):\;z_{1},z_{2}\in\Gamma_{\zeta}\setminus\Gamma_{\zeta,n}(\delta_{n})\}.

It follows from Theorem 1.1 in
[
7
]
that there exists a sequence
\delta_{n}\to\infty
,
\delta_{n}=o(n)
, such that the numbers

\mathcal{E}_{1,n}=\sup_{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\Omega^{\prime}_{n}(\delta_{n})}|r_{n}(z_{1},z_{2})|

tend to
0
as
n\to\infty
. Therefore, we need to estimate
r_{n}(z_{1},z_{2})
for the pairs
(z_{1},z_{2})
in
\Omega_{n}(\delta_{n})
. Set
b_{n}=\varphi_{n}/\varphi_{n}^{*}
. Using the formula (
2.12
) written in the form

k_{\mu,n}(z_{1},z_{2})=\varphi_{n}^{*}(z_{1})\overline{\varphi_{n}^{*}(z_{2})}\frac{1-b_{n}(z_{1})\overline{b_{n}(z_{2})}}{1-\bar{z}_{2}z_{1}},\qquad b_{n}=\frac{\varphi_{n}}{\varphi^{*}_{n}},

we express funtions
r_{n}
in (
7.1
) in the form

\displaystyle r_{n}(z_{1},z_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{(\overline{D_{\mu}(z_{2})}D_{\mu}(z_{1})\varphi_{n}^{*}(z_{1})\overline{\varphi_{n}^{*}(z_{2})}-1)(1-b_{n}(z_{1})\overline{b_{n}(z_{2})})}{1-\bar{z}_{2}^{n}z_{1}^{n}}+

\displaystyle+\frac{\bar{z}_{2}^{n}z_{1}^{n}-b_{n}(z_{1})\overline{b_{n}(z_{2})}}{1-\bar{z}_{2}^{n}z_{1}^{n}},

by means of an algebraic manipulation. For
(z_{1},z_{2})\in\Omega_{n}(\delta_{n})
and
n/2\leqslant k\leqslant n
, we have
|1-\bar{z}_{2}^{k}z_{1}^{k}|\asymp 1
. Consider the numbers

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2,n}=

\displaystyle\sup_{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\Omega_{n}(\delta_{n})}\frac{2}{n}\sum_{n/2\leqslant k\leqslant n}|r_{k}(z_{1},z_{2})|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\Gamma_{\zeta}}\frac{2}{n}\sum_{n/2\leqslant k\leqslant n}|\overline{D_{\mu}(z_{2})}D_{\mu}(z_{1})\varphi_{k}^{*}(z_{1})\overline{\varphi_{k}^{*}(z_{2})}-1|+

\displaystyle+\sup_{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\Omega_{n}(\delta_{n})}\frac{2}{n}\sum_{n/2\leqslant k\leqslant n}|z_{1}^{k}\bar{z}_{2}^{k}|+\sup_{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\Omega_{n}(\delta_{n})}\frac{2}{n}\sum_{n/2\leqslant k\leqslant n}|b_{k}(z_{1})b_{k}(z_{2})|.

Theorem
2.1
, Theorem
2.2
and Lemma
7.4
imply that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\Gamma_{\zeta}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{0\leqslant k\leqslant n-1}|\overline{D_{\mu}(z_{2})}D_{\mu}(z_{1})\varphi_{k}^{*}(z_{1})\overline{\varphi_{k}^{*}(z_{2})}-1|=0.

The last relation, the fact that
\delta_{n}\to\infty
, and Lemma
7.3
give
\mathcal{E}_{2,n}\to 0
(recall that
b_{n}
is a Blaschke product, so
|b_{n}|<1
in
\mathbb{D}
and one can apply Lemma
7.3
to the point
z
(
=z_{1}
or
z_{2}
) lying in
\Gamma_{\zeta,n}(\delta_{n})
for estimating
\frac{2}{n}\sum_{n/2\leqslant k\leqslant n}|b_{k}(z_{1})b_{k}(z_{2})|
). Combining this fact with
\mathcal{E}_{1,n}\to 0
, we see that the quantities

\mathcal{E}_{3,n}=\sup_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\Gamma_{\zeta}}\frac{2}{n}\sum_{n/2\leqslant k\leqslant n}|r_{k}(z_{1},z_{2})|

also tend to zero as
n\to\infty
. At the same time, for every integer
\ell\geqslant 1
we have

\sup_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\Gamma_{\zeta}}\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant 2^{\ell}}|r_{k}(z_{1},z_{2})|\leqslant\frac{\mathcal{E}_{3,2^{\ell}}}{2}+\frac{\mathcal{E}_{3,2^{\ell-1}}}{4}+\frac{\mathcal{E}_{3,2^{\ell-2}}}{8}+\ldots+\frac{\mathcal{E}_{3,2}}{2^{\ell}}.

This estimate and the fact that
\mathcal{E}_{3,n}\to 0
yield the statement. ∎

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
\mu\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
be such that
D_{\mu}
has a non-zero finite non-tangential limit at
\zeta
. If (
2.3
) holds at

, then we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\Gamma_{\zeta}}\bigl|r_{n}(z_{1},z_{2})\bigl|=0

for the functions
r_{n}
defined in (
7.1
).

Proof.
As in the proof of Lemma
7.3
, for each
z\in\mathbb{D}
we set
b_{n}=\varphi_{n}/\varphi_{n}^{*}
,
\tilde{b}_{n}=\tilde{\varphi}_{z,n}/\tilde{\varphi}_{z,n}^{*}

|\tilde{b}_{n}(z)-b_{n}(z)|\lesssim|f_{n-1}(0)|+|f_{n-1}(z)|

,
|f_{n-1}(0)|
are less than
1/2
. By Lemma
6.1
, the last assumption holds for all

n

|\tilde{b}_{n}(z)-\alpha_{z}z^{n}|^{2}\lesssim e^{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)}-1

by Lemma 2.5 in

. Here,

\alpha_{z}=\frac{\overline{\tilde{\varphi}_{z,n}^{*}(z)}}{\tilde{\varphi}_{z,n}^{*}(z)}=\frac{\overline{\varphi_{n}^{*}(z)}}{\varphi_{n}^{*}(z)}(1+o(1))=\frac{\overline{D_{\mu}(z)^{-1}}}{D_{\mu}(z)^{-1}}(1+o(1)),

6.1
and Theorem
2.3
. Since

\alpha_{z}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\Gamma_{\zeta}}|b_{n}(z_{1})\overline{b_{n}(z_{2})}-\bar{z}_{2}^{n}z_{1}^{n}|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\Gamma_{\zeta}}|\overline{\alpha z_{2}^{n}}\cdot\alpha z_{1}^{n}-\bar{z}_{2}^{n}z_{1}^{n}|=0.

and the uniform asymptotic relation (

) in Theorem

. ∎

,

Proof of Proposition
2.6
.
Denote
u=\log w
. Since the value
\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)
of the entropy of
\mu
at any point
z\in\mathbb{D}
is invariant under multiplication of
\mu
by a constant, we can assume that
u(\zeta)=0
. Since
u
is bounded at a neighborhood of
\zeta
and condition (
2.3
) is local (provided that
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
as we always assume), we can suppose that
u
is bounded on
\mathbb{T}
and vanishes on
\mathbb{T}\setminus I_{0}
. We have

\mathcal{P}(w,z)=\mathcal{P}(e^{u},z)=1+\mathcal{P}(u,z)+O\left(\mathcal{P}(u^{2},z)\right),

and, since
\mathcal{P}(u,z)^{2}\leqslant\mathcal{P}(u^{2},z)
,

\log\mathcal{P}(w,z)=\mathcal{P}(u,z)+O\left(\mathcal{P}(u^{2},z)\right).

At the same time, we have
\mathcal{P}(\log w,z)=\mathcal{P}(u,z)
by definition. Thus, we have

\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)\lesssim\mathcal{P}(u^{2},z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Standard estimates of the Poisson kernel give (recall that
u=0
on
\mathbb{T}\setminus I_{0}
)

\mathcal{P}(u^{2},z_{n}(\zeta))\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{2k-n}\int_{I_{k}}u^{2}\,dm.

Thus,

\sqrt{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z_{n}(\zeta))}\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{k-n/2}\left(\int_{I_{k}}u^{2}\,dm\right)^{1/2},

and hence

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z_{n}(\zeta))}\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{k/2}\left(\int_{I_{k}}u^{2}\,dm\right)^{1/2}.

The result follows. ∎

Proof of Proposition
2.5
.
As in the proof of Proposition
2.6
, we denote
u=\log w
, assume that
u(\zeta)=0
and
u
is bounded on
\mathbb{T}
, and then

\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)\lesssim\mathcal{P}(u^{2},z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

We see that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(\mu,z_{n}(\zeta))\lesssim\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}(u^{2},z_{n}(\zeta)),

and one only needs to use the estimate

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1-|z_{n}(\zeta)|^{2}}{|1-z_{n}(\zeta)\overline{\xi}|^{2}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dr}{|\xi-r\zeta|^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|\xi-\zeta|},\quad\xi,\zeta\in\mathbb{T},

to complete the proof. ∎

Proof of Proposition
2.4
.
We need to show that condition (
2.2
) holds for almost every point
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
. Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
2.6
, we arrive at the estimate

\mathcal{K}(\mu,r\zeta)\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{T}}(u(\xi)-u(\zeta))^{2}\frac{1-r^{2}}{|\xi-r\zeta|^{2}}\,dm(\xi),\qquad 0\leqslant r<1,\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{T}.

Denote by
A
the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}
normalized by
A(\mathbb{D})=1
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)}{1-|z|^{2}}\,dA(z)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathcal{K}(\mu,r\zeta)r}{1-r^{2}}dr\,dm(\zeta)\lesssim

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(u(\xi)-u(\zeta))^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dr}{|\xi-r\zeta|^{2}}\,dm(\xi)\,dm(\zeta).

Since

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dr}{|\xi-r\zeta|^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|\xi-\zeta|},\quad\xi,\zeta\in\mathbb{T},

we deduce that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)}{1-|z|^{2}}\,dA(z)\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{(u(\xi)-u(\zeta))^{2}}{|\xi-\zeta|}\,dm(\xi)\,dm(\zeta).

Denoting
\zeta=e^{2\pi i\theta}
and
\xi=e^{2\pi i(\theta+h)}
, the last double integral rewrites as

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{|1-e^{2\pi ih}|}\int_{0}^{1}(u(e^{2\pi i\theta})-u(e^{2\pi i(\theta+h)}))^{2}\,d\theta\,dh.

The Fourier coefficients of the real-valued function
\theta\mapsto u(e^{2\pi i\theta})-u(e^{2\pi i(\theta+h)})
with respect to the basis

\{e^{2\pi ik\theta}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
in
L^{2}[0,1]
are
(1-e^{2\pi ikh})\hat{u}(k)
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then, Parseval’s identity gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{(u(\xi)-u(\zeta))^{2}}{|\xi-\zeta|}\,dm(\xi)\,dm(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}|\hat{u}(k)|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|1-e^{2\pi ikh}|^{2}}{|1-e^{2\pi ih}|}dh

\displaystyle\asymp\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}|\hat{u}(k)|^{2}\log(2+|k|).

Hence assumption (
2.9
) implies

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)}{1-|z|^{2}}\,dA(z)<\infty.

Fubini theorem then gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Gamma_{\zeta}}\frac{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}dA(z)\,dm(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}m(\{\zeta\in\mathbb{T}:z\in\Gamma_{\zeta}\})dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)}{1-|z|^{2}}\,dA(z),

from where we deduce that

\int_{\Gamma_{\zeta}}\frac{\mathcal{K}(\mu,z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}\,dA(z)<\infty,

for almost every
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
. Hence condition (
2.2
) holds for almost every
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
. ∎
