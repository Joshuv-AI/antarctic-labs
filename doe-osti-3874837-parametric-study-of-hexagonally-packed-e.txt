1.0 SUMMARY

This technical document presents criticality calculations for hexagonal arrangements of epoxy-
mounted plutonium samples under parametrized geometries and moderation conditions. These
calculations were performed with the intent of establishing trends in the multiplication factor of
large sample collections under realistic parameters (e.g., sample sizes, sample geometries, and
epoxy mounting considerations) seen in metallography operations. As concluded in Section 5.0,
to the extent that a large number of epoxy-mounted samples could become packed closely
together, the multiplication factor (keff) remains bounded by a typically considered, 4.5 kg
monolithic plutonium metal item.

This technical document was produced in accordance with NCS-AP-005 [Ref. (1)].

2.0 DESCRIPTION AND PROBLEM STATEMENT

2.1 Metallographic Operations

Metallographic characterization of plutonium (Pu) samples typically includes pre-processing
through mechanical or chemical means. Metallographic samples may be of non-standard shapes
and sizes, with masses and physical dimensions constrained by the production method of the
samples or the dimensions of the investigative equipment to be used. Samples are typically small
in mass and geometries are often non-compact, flat, or otherwise of a non-idealized shape.

2.2 Epoxy/Resin Mounting

Preparation of the samples for characterization may include mounting the sample in epoxy,
which is produced through the combination of an epoxy resin and binder within a standardized
cup. The sample may either be enclosed within the cured epoxy or attached through other means.
Both the epoxy resin and binder may be composed of hydrocarbons which pose particular
reflection and moderation concerns for criticality safety. Specifications for the standardized cups,
in which sample mounting is performed, are included in Figure 1, per the manufacturer [Ref.
(2)]. The height to which the cups are filled is contingent upon the needs of the operator. The
resulting geometry of mounted samples is largely dependent on cup sizes.

2.3 Motivating Considerations

There may be a need to store samples at location subsequent to the metallographic operations.
Accumulations of epoxy-mounted samples could provide interstitial moderation, necessitating
evaluation of acceptable storage parameters. Limits on the number of samples have been drawn
from NCS-TECH-18-001 [Ref. (3)], which examined arrays of plutonium metal with interstitial
water moderation. These limits are derived from highly conservative modeling assumptions
made in Reference (3) and may result in unnecessarily restrictive controls on operations.

By considering actual parameters for the mass of plutonium within epoxy samples, arrangement
of samples, and spacing representative of equipment used, a more tailored approach may be
taken that compares the resulting keff values for collections of 4.5 kg of plutonium within close-
packed arrays to a standard 4.5 kg, full density (19.84 g/cc) Pu metal ingot, as what is considered
a bounding normal item in Reference (4) and in other typical criticality safety evaluations.

3.0 METHODOLOGY

Calculations were performed using MCNP6.3 with input files generated through a custom
Python script. The MCNP6.3 calculations utilized the ENDF/B-VII.1 cross section sets on the
Cutthroat computational cluster, with relevant code and validation information found in NCS-
TECH-26-010 [Ref. (5)] and approval for use in NCS-MEMO-26-0010 [Ref. (6)]. As the intent
of this document is to compare the keff (kcalc + 2σ) of the dispersed hexagonally arranged system
to that of a monolithic ingot, no upper subcritical limit (USL) is derived or compared against.
Relevant however, is the keff value of 0.865 (≈ 0.86 used herein) for a monolithic 4.5 kg Pu ingot
with 1-in. 4π reflection. For validation purposes an Area of Applicability comparison is included
below in Table 1.

3.1 Modeling Conventions

Both normal and abnormal flooding considerations are evaluated to demonstrate effects on keff
across different parameters that define arrays of epoxy-mounted samples.

3.1.1 Model Parameters

Extending upon the work performed in NCS-TECH-18-001 [Ref. (3)], hexagonally arranged
pieces of plutonium were modeled. To cover the range of possible representative conditions of
the system, dimensional parameters including the number of, the bundling and stacking of, and
the spacing between epoxy-mounted samples were considered. To represent the range of possible
sample configurations, the height of the epoxy clad from the sample surface was parametrized
while constrained by the maximal height of the sample cup. The total 4.5 kg mass was modeled
as evenly distributed amongst the samples, which determined the volume of each individual
sample. A constant height-to-diameter ratio of 1 was used for the cylindrical samples. In
practice, samples to be mounted in epoxy typically have significantly less compact geometries.
All samples are placed centered radially and axially within their epoxy mounts.

3.1.2 Array Configuration

A hexagonal arrangement was determined to be an appropriate representation for the
accumulation of material, as the elements are as closely packed as possible given that they are
mounted in cylindrical geometries. Bundling in the lateral dimensions was assumed to take the
form of a regular hexagon, while stacking of the samples was modeled by adding identical
layers. Through such parameterizations of possible arrangements, a sweep of close-packed
configurations that bound realistic, sporadic, or randomly placed collocations of samples may be
made. Representative images of the geometry are included in Figure 2 and Figure 3.

3.1.3 Pu Epoxy Thickness

Per the manufacturer standards given above, the epoxy mounting cups are available in a variety
of sizes. For the purposes of our calculations only the most common cup size with a diameter of
1.25 in. is considered. All epoxy is modeled as high-density polyethylene (HDPE) (0.97 g/cc),
which is deemed bounding of actual epoxy resin and binder likely to be used.

3.1.4       Spacing

To represent possible interstitial moderation introduced to the system during a water intrusion
upset, the distance from edge-to-edge of each sample was varied from nonexistent (touching) up
to a 1 in. spacing. Figures showing the spacing extremes are included below.

3.1.5 Material Specifications

Materials used in the calculations follow specifications given in NCS-TECH-18-024 [Ref. (7)].
The calculations in this document utilized the following materials:

 • Plutonium metal

 • Water

 • Air

 • HDPE

To represent dry conditions, air was used as the interstitial material, as opposed to water was
used to represent flooded conditions. HDPE was used as a stand-in for the epoxy material.
Where appropriate, thermal scattering law (S(α,β)) keywords were included in the material card.
For the calculation considering the monolithic ingot, the plutonium was modeled at full
theoretical density (19.84 g/cc). For all other calculations, plutonium was modeled at a more
representative credible density other than theoretical.

4.0 ANALYSIS

Results from the parametric criticality calculations for both the dry and flooded conditions are
included below. For both cases, at least 1-in. 4π water reflection was added to the model, which
can be seen in Figure 2 as the red-colored material in the model. For the normal condition, the
material immediately surrounding the epoxy was modeled as air, which can also be seen in
Figure 2 as the yellow material in the model.

4.1 Normal Condition Analysis

As mentioned above, the following parameters were considered for normal conditions: a number
of rings between 4 and 10; a number of layers between 1 and 15; an epoxy height ranging from
the minimal height of a sample to a maximum height of 1 in. (as set by the cup depth in Figure
1); and item radial spacings up to 1 in. For each unique combination of rings, layers, epoxy
heights, and radial spacings, only the highest keff value across epoxy heights are reported and
compared against a keff of 0.86.

5.0 CONCLUSION

The results from the calculations included in this document demonstrate that under typical
conditions for epoxy mounted samples, including a range of possible epoxy mount height and
hexagonal arrangements, the samples remain bounded by a 4.5 kg monolithic ingot. Furthermore,
these considerations were extended into analysis of credible abnormal conditions where
interstitial moderation is present, and similar conclusions were drawn where under realistic
parameters the system remains bounded by a monolithic ingot. While there is the potential that
configurations with higher numbers of samples could yield higher keff values, the feasibility of
retaining those samples in credibly compact configurations would pose a continuous challenge.
Realistic configurations with large sample counts greater than those which were considered in
this document (>4000), will likely form less-compact geometries and fall within the bounding
envelope evaluated.

5.1 Caveats

In this document, the most common epoxy mount diameter of 1.25 in. was considered for the
purposes of modeling, though other sizes could see increased use in the future. Preliminary
investigation into the smaller epoxy radius of 1 in. diameter was performed, with results
indicating a handful of configurations where the calculated keff exceeded that of the limit set by
the 4.5 kg monolithic ingot. Of the cases that exceeded the limit, the maximal keff value
calculated was 0.869, which corresponded to 15-layer, 5-ring configuration. While this value
exceeds that of the limit set by the monolithic ingot, the collection it represents is that of 915
samples each with a 4.92 gram mass. When considering the heterogeneity in sample sizes and
arrangements, these too are likely realistically bounded by the ingot. Graphs including the results
of these calculations are included in Appendix A, in Figure 10 and Figure 11. Further
examination is still recommended for cases involving mounts not of the typical epoxy diameter.
