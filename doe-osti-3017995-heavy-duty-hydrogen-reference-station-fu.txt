 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 This project will provide valuable information on (1) reference station design, (2) exploration of
 design concepts for a fueling performance test device, and (3) modeling of station capacity. The
 work applies to heavy-duty (HD) hydrogen fueling stations with large dispensing capacity and
 high flowrates servicing HD hydrogen trucks such as class 8 trucks in long haul applications.
 Additionally, a 4th area will provide near-real-time verification of fuel quality with on-site
 hydrogen contaminant detectors (HCDs) for use at both light-duty (LD) and HD stations. The
 work leverages national lab capabilities including staff and equipment at SNL, NLR, and ANL
 with collaboration and funding cost share from California agencies (CEC, SCAQMD, and GO-
 Biz). This project will provide tools and information that lead to more efficient design,
 acceptance, and commissioning of these larger capacity, higher flowrate stations serving HD
 applications. The HCD work will benefit both LD and HD stations.

 Note: The 4th area (HCD) mentioned above continued outside of this CRADA and will not be
 included in this report. There are no tasks included in this CRADA for HCD and the summary
 text for it should have been removed prior to this CRADA contract execution but remained in
 error and is included here for completeness.

 Summary of Research Results:

 Purpose

 The purpose of this project is to continue the research partnership between national laboratories,
 DOE, and California public agencies for the advancement of hydrogen fueling infrastructure for
 HD vehicles. This research partnership will provide design considerations and risk analysis for
 HD hydrogen fueling stations, design concepts for a HD fueling performance test device, and a
 model to evaluate the dispensing capacity of HD hydrogen stations.

 A hydrogen fueling infrastructure is necessary to support the emerging LD and HD fuel cell
 electric vehicle (FCEV) market, including both a network of fueling stations and the availability
 of hydrogen fuel that meets required fuel quality specifications. Fuel quality verification is
 mandated before a fueling station is certified for commercial hydrogen dispensing and
 periodically thereafter for the operational life of the fueling station. This infrastructure would
 benefit by the on-site HCDs for near-real-time verification of fuel quality, preferably at the
 nozzle just before dispensing of the hydrogen into the FCEV. Note: The HCD work referenced
 here was continued outside of this CRADA.

 The research completed for HD hydrogen fueling is summarized below by task with the original
 CRADA “Discussion” sections followed by a “Results” summary of the work completed in each
 task. The completed outputs of the project are published separately and accessible as provided in
 the reference section.

 Task 1.1: SNL will develop reference station designs for HD vehicles

 1. Survey HD truck manufacturers and truck fueling infrastructure providers to guide
 details of station design (e.g., station capacity, number of hoses, number of
 simultaneous connections to truck, truck storage tank limitations, delivered or H 2
 produced on-site).
 2. Economically evaluate various configurations and capacities of 700 bar and survey
 guided dispensing concept stations, with electrolyzer produced and liquid hydrogen
 (LH2) delivered.
 3. Determine equipment required and station layout designs for incorporating hydrogen
 fueling into existing truck stops/gas station.
 Contractors Deliverables: SNL-Report on reference station designs for HD vehicles.

 3

 Task 1.2: SNL will identify gaps in NFPA 2, Hydrogen Technologies Code, and perform
 quantitative risk assessment on reference station to demonstrate how codes and standards apply
 to HD station concepts

 1. Identify gaps in NFPA 2 that do/should not apply to HD infrastructure.
 2. Perform quantitative risk assessment of a reference station from task 1.1.3, analyzing
 how NFPA 2 requirements scale, apply, and affect HD station risk.
 Contractors Deliverables: SNL-Report on risk analysis of an HD vehicle fueling station.

 on the HD equipment needed for the different station configurations and identifies gaps in NFPA
 code for HD stations. The tasks were completed with input from project partners and external

 Contractors Deliverables: NLR-Report draft with list of equipment for proposed test

 Task 2.2: NLR will explore test device concepts for other pressures and temperatures guided by

 1. Test device concepts will be evaluated for safety and potential to accommodate

 Contractors Deliverables: NLR-Finalized report from Task 2.1 and Task 2.2 including

 Task 2 Results: HD HyStEP

 This task created design documents that would aid in the final design and build of a hydrogen
 fueling performance test device for HD hydrogen station dispensers with high flowrates of up to
 300 g/s. This device specification is like the Hydrogen Station Equipment Performance
 (HyStEP) device [4] built for LD hydrogen stations but with larger tank volumes and hardware
 that supports higher flowrates for HD stations.

 The design for this task is referred to as the HD HyStEP. The documents created include a
 summary of what is needed, a detailed piping and instrumentation diagram, bill of materials,
 cause and effects matrix, operation manual outline, and process hazard analysis for the system
 [5]. Also included are results of analysis using H2FillS [6] to determine maximum venting rates
 of the device to protect the tanks from being subjected to temperatures below -40°C as they are
 brought to low pressure after a test.

 7

 Desired work outcomes were coordinated with members of the SAE J2601-5 committee and
 other stakeholders to make sure the device would be able to go through the test methodology,
 like what is outlined in CSA HGV4.3 for LD vehicles but to verify the adherence to the HD
 fueling protocols in SAE J2601-5 for fueling at pressures of 35 and 70 MPa. In addition to the
 fueling performance, consideration was given to add a mass flow meter to measure mass
 dispensed, a particulate sampling device, and an inline contaminant detector. These are outlined
 in the summary document.

 Task 3.1: NLR will develop an HD station capacity model for stations with 70 MPa fueling and
 on-site production

 1. The modeling effort will be based on a mass-flow model as with the LD station
 model. Thermal modeling will then be considered where thermal effects lead to
 significant errors in capacity predictions.
 2. Testing results from the HD station and HD vehicle simulator at NLR’s Hydrogen
 Infrastructure Testing and Research Facility will be used to compare actual
 dispensing capacity with modeled capacity.
 3. An example station template will be developed to allow for easy modification of
 station parameters to apply to different station designs.
 Contractors Deliverables: NLR-Draft executable model to evaluate HD hydrogen fueling
 station based on high pressure (70 MPa) and on-site production.

 Task 3.2: NLR will develop an HD station capacity model with liquid and pipeline delivery

 1. The model from Task 3.1 will be expanded to include liquid and pipeline delivery
 station types
 2. Refinements and bug fixes will be made to deliverable from Task 3.1.
 Contractors Deliverables: NLR-Publicly available, executable model that can be used by
 H2@Scale community to evaluate HD hydrogen fueling station dispensing capacity
 based on the specified station equipment and the expected vehicle storage and demand
 profile. NLR will host the executable model on NLR’s website.

 Task 3 Results: HyCap 1.0

 This task developed the HD Hydrogen station Capacity tool (HyCap 1.0), which is available
 from the NLR website [7]. Users of the tool provide their major hydrogen station equipment
 specifications in a spreadsheet input file and the model provides the number of trucks and
 amount of hydrogen that the equipment could dispense in a day based on the selected station
 demand profile. The tool is intended for gaseous hydrogen (GH2) dispensing from the station to
 the HD vehicle at nominal pressures up to 70 MPa. It also works for light duty stations.

 The tool was first designed with only mass flow, but through analysis, it was quickly determined
 that the amount of hydrogen dispensed from station tanks for HD vehicles would cause large
 temperature drops and therefore lower the pressure available from those tanks. This thermal
 effect is now modeled in the HyCap tool.

 10

 The tool was first developed for gaseous delivered hydrogen and on-site production. Later the
 tool was updated to also handle liquid hydrogen delivery to the station and pipeline delivery.
 Several reviews were held with external stakeholders as well as with partners in this CRADA to
 obtain input on the tool’s capabilities. Many iterations with external stakeholders helped to
 identify bugs and additional capability needs as they exercised the tool with their own station
 designs. An example upgrade included the ability to dispense directly to vehicle from vaporizer
 at a liquid hydrogen station rather than going through high pressure buffer storage.

 Types of stations available to model in HyCap:

 • Gas delivery: The station stores gaseous hydrogen storage tanks and replenishes those
 storage tanks with hydrogen delivered by truck(s).
 • Liquid storage and delivery: If users select this option, the station is equipped with a
 liquid storage system consisting of a liquid hydrogen storage tank, pump, and vaporizer.
 The liquid hydrogen storage system can get replenished by delivery trucks.
 • Trailer swap: The station stores gaseous hydrogen storage tanks and swaps out storage
 tanks with low hydrogen (mass) level with full tanks delivered by trailers.
 • Pipeline hydrogen supply: The station stores gaseous hydrogen storage tanks and
 replenishes those storage tanks with hydrogen supplied from a pipeline.
 • On-site production: The station is equipped with an electrolyzer and produces hydrogen.
 The produced hydrogen is then accumulated in the gaseous hydrogen storage tanks.

 Task 4: ANL Review of Station Design Concepts

 ANL will provide guidance on how to use its Heavy-Duty Refueling Station Analysis Model
 (HDRSAM) [8] and will review station design concepts for various HD fueling performance
 requirements.

 Task 4 Results:

 For this task, ANL participated in review meetings and provided helpful comments on the work
 using their experience with LD and HD hydrogen fueling stations. Items such as component
 costs and equations from ANL’s HDRSAM model [8] were used for evaluation of components
 most likely to be used at HD hydrogen refueling stations.

 The outcome from Task 4 included guidance for this CRADA project that helped to decide what
 equipment would most likely be used and their implications on cost and performance.

 Conclusion

 This project continued the research partnership between national laboratories, DOE, and
 California public agencies for the advancement of hydrogen fueling infrastructure for HD
 vehicles. The output included published reports, models, and specification documents useful to
 the HD hydrogen community going forward.
