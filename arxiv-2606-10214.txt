Distribution of Sandpile groups of random directed bipartite graphs
Deepesh Singhal

Abstract
. Fix a prime p p and a constant 1 p < α ≤ 1 \frac{1}{p}<\alpha\leq 1 . Consider the random directed Erdős–Rényi bipartite graph G → ​ ( n , ⌈ α ​ n ⌉ , v ) \vec{G}(n,\lceil\alpha n\rceil,v) with bipartition ( V 1 , V 2 ) (V_{1},V_{2}) of sizes | V 1 | = n |V_{1}|=n and | V 2 | = ⌈ α ​ n ⌉ |V_{2}|=\lceil\alpha n\rceil , and edge probability 0 < v < 1 0<v<1 . Bhargava, DePascale and Koenig [ 1 ] conjectured a limiting distribution for the p p -Sylow subgroup of the sandpile group of G → ​ ( n , ⌈ α ​ n ⌉ , v ) \vec{G}(n,\lceil\alpha n\rceil,v) as n → ∞ n\to\infty . We prove this conjecture. Similar results have previously been proved by computing the expected number of surjections from the random abelian p p -group onto H H , for each finite abelian p p -group H H . However, in the case of p p -Sylow subgroups of sandpile groups of random directed bipartite graphs, these surjective moments often diverge to infinity, despite the conjectured limiting distribution having finite moments. We resolve this by restricting to a high-probability subset of graphs on which the surjective moments are well-behaved, and discarding a rare exceptional set of graphs whose contribution to the distribution vanishes but whose contribution to the surjective moments often diverges. Computing the conditional surjective moments on the good set and applying Wood’s universality theorem yields the desired convergence in distribution.



1.
Introduction

Fix a prime
p
throughout the paper. Define the
q
-shifted factorials

\left(x;q\right)_{i}:=(1-x)(1-xq)\cdots(1-xq^{i-1}),\quad\left(x;q\right)_{\infty}:=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}(1-xq^{j}).

Let
\vec{\Gamma}
be a directed graph on vertices
1,\dots,n
without any loops. Write
\deg(i,j)
for the number of directed edges
i\to j
, and set
\deg(j)=\sum_{i:i\neq j}\deg(i,j)
, the in-degree of the vertex.
Define the Laplacian
L(\vec{\Gamma})
by

L_{ij}=\begin{cases}\deg(i,j),&i\neq j,\\
-\deg(j),&i=j,\end{cases}\qquad\text{so that}\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{ij}=0\ \text{ for all }j.

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{n}
be the subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
consisting of vectors that sum up to
0
.
Hence
L:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}
satisfies
\text{Im}(L)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{n}
.
We define the
sandpile group
of
\vec{\Gamma}
as
S_{\vec{\Gamma}}:=\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{n}/\text{Im}(L)
.
If
\vec{\Gamma}
is strongly connected, then
\text{corank}(L)=1
and
S_{\vec{\Gamma}}
is a finite abelian group.

Let
\vec{G}(n,v)
denote the directed Erdős-Rényi random graph on
n
vertices in which each ordered pair
i\to j
with
i\neq j
is included independently with probability
v\in(0,1)
. For fixed
v
,
\vec{G}(n,v)
is strongly connected with probability tending to
1
as
n\to\infty
. Consequently, the sandpile group
S_{\vec{G}(n,v)}
is a finite abelian group with probability tending to
1
.

For
0<u<p
, the measure
P_{\infty,u}
on finite abelian
p
-groups is defined as

P_{\infty,u}(G):=\frac{|G|^{\log_{p}(u)}}{|\text{Aut}(G)|}\left(\tfrac{u}{p};\tfrac{1}{p}\right)_{\infty}.

Koplewitz
[
10
, Theorem 1]
shows that as
n\rightarrow\infty
, the distribution of the Sylow
p
-subgroups of the sandpile group of a directed Erdős-Rényi random graph with
n
vertices converges to
P_{\infty,\frac{1}{p}}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
10
, Theorem 1]

Consider a finite abelian
p
-group
G
. Then for a random directed graph
\vec{G}(n,v)
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[(S_{\vec{G}(n,v)})_{p}\cong G\right]=P_{\infty,\frac{1}{p}}(G).

Nguyen and Wood
[
13
, Theorem 1.6]
determined the distribution of the entire sandpile group of a directed Erdős-Rényi random graph as

.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
13
, Theorem 1.6]

Consider a finite abelian group
G
. Then for a random directed graph
\vec{G}(n,v)
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[S_{\vec{G}(n,v)}\cong G\right]=\frac{1}{|G||\text{Aut}(G)|}\prod_{k=2}^{\infty}\zeta(k)^{-1}.

be positive integers and fix

. The

We are most interested in the case where

We write

[

Consider constants

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[(S_{\vec{G}_{\alpha}(n,v)})_{p}\cong G\right]=P_{\infty,\frac{1}{p}}(G).

1.1.
Random Matrix model

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be the ring of
p
-adic integers. Fix
0<\epsilon<1
for the rest of the paper.
A random variable
x
taking values in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is called
\epsilon
-balanced
if for each
a\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
,

\mathbb{P}[x\equiv a\pmod{p}]<1-\epsilon.

We consider a random matrix model for the Laplacian of
\vec{G}_{\alpha}(n,v)
as a random matrix
L_{n_{1},n_{2}}\in\mathbb{M}_{n_{1}+n_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
as follows:

L_{n_{1},n_{2}}=\begin{bmatrix}-\sum b_{1j}&0&\dots&0&a_{11}&a_{12}&\dots&a_{1n_{2}}\\
0&-\sum b_{2j}&\dots&0&a_{21}&a_{22}&\dots&a_{2n_{2}}\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&&&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&-\sum b_{n_{1}j}&a_{n_{1}1}&a_{n_{1}2}&\dots&a_{n_{1}n_{2}}\\
b_{11}&b_{21}&\dots&b_{n_{1}1}&-\sum a_{i1}&0&\dots&0\\
b_{12}&b_{22}&\dots&b_{n_{1}2}&0&-\sum a_{i2}&\dots&0\\
\vdots&&&\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
b_{1n_{2}}&b_{2n_{2}}&\dots&b_{n_{1}n_{2}}&0&0&\dots&-\sum a_{in_{2}}\\
\end{bmatrix},

where all
a_{ij}
and
b_{ij}
are
\epsilon
-balanced and independent.
Now,
L_{n_{1},n_{2}}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}})
. Let
Z_{n}
consist of the vectors in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
whose entries sum to
0
, so
\text{Im}(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})\subseteq Z_{n_{1}+n_{2}}
. We denote
G(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})=Z_{n_{1}+n_{2}}/\text{Im}(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})
.
In particular, if
a_{ij}
and
b_{ij}
took values
1
and
0
with probability
v
and
1-v
respectively, then
L_{n}^{(\alpha)}
would be the random matrix arising from the Laplacian of the random graph
\vec{G}_{\alpha}(n,v)
.
Moreover, in the (high probability) event that
\vec{G}_{\alpha}(n,v)
is connected, the random finite abelian
p
-group
G(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})
would become isomorphic to the
p
-Sylow subgroup of
S_{\vec{G}_{\alpha}(n,v)}
.

One of the difficulties of working with
L_{n_{1},n_{2}}
is that the diagonal entries depend on other entries of the matrix. We therefore consider a related random matrix whose diagonal entries are independent.
This strategy of replacing the Laplacian model with an independent diagonal model was first used by Wood in the proof of
[
17
, Theorem 6.2]
.
Consider the random matrix
M_{n_{1},n_{2}}\in\mathbb{M}_{n_{1}+n_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
defined as follows:

M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=\begin{bmatrix}c_{1}&0&\dots&0&a_{11}&a_{12}&\dots&a_{1n_{2}}\\
0&c_{2}&\dots&0&a_{21}&a_{22}&\dots&a_{2n_{2}}\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&&&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&c_{n_{1}}&a_{n_{1}1}&a_{n_{1}2}&\dots&a_{n_{1}n_{2}}\\
b_{11}&b_{21}&\dots&b_{n_{1}1}&d_{1}&0&\dots&0\\
b_{12}&b_{22}&\dots&b_{n_{1}2}&0&d_{2}&\dots&0\\
\vdots&&&\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
b_{1n_{2}}&b_{2n_{2}}&\dots&b_{n_{1}n_{2}}&0&0&\dots&d_{n_{2}}\\
\end{bmatrix},

where all
a_{ij}
and
b_{ij}
are
\epsilon
-balanced and
c_{j}
and
d_{j}
are Haar uniform and mutually independent.
Note that
M_{n_{1},n_{2}}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}})
, so we can consider the cokernel
\text{Coker}(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}/\text{Im}(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})
.

For
0<\alpha\leq 1
, we will denote
M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=M_{n,\lceil\alpha n\rceil}
and
L_{n}^{(\alpha)}=L_{n,\lceil\alpha n\rceil}
.
We will see that
\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})
is easier to analyze than
G(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})
, and that its analysis helps us study
G(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})
.

We will prove the following:

Theorem 1.6
.

Given
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha\leq 1
and a finite abelian
p
-group
G
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})\cong G\right]=P_{\infty,1}(G).

Theorem 1.7
.

Given
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha\leq 1
and a finite abelian
p
-group
G
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[G(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})\cong G]=P_{\infty,\frac{1}{p}}(G).

Note that Theorem
1.7
directly implies Theorem
1.4
.

2.
Strategy

Previous results about the distribution of sandpile groups of random graphs and cokernels of random matrices have been proven using the following strategy developed by Wood
[
17
]
. Suppose
G_{n}
is a sequence of random finite abelian
p
-groups and
\nu
is the conjectured distribution as
n\to\infty
.
The first step is to show that the surjective moments match. That is, for every finite abelian
p
-group
H
, we have

(1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[|\text{Sur}(G_{n},H)|]=\mathbb{E}[|\text{Sur}(G,H)|],

where
G
is a finite abelian
p
-group chosen according to
\nu
.
The next step is to use a result of Wood
[
17
]
, which says that if (
1
) is satisfied and the moments are not too big, then for every finite abelian
p
-group
H

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[G_{n}\cong H]=\nu(H).

The surjective moments of
P_{\infty,u}
were computed by Wood
[
18
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
18
, Lemma 3.2]

Suppose we are given a constant
0<u<p
and
X
is a random finite abelian
p
-group distributed according to
P_{\infty,u}
,
Then for every finite abelian
p
-group
G
, we have

\mathbb{E}[|\text{Sur}(X,G)|]=|G|^{\log_{p}(u)}.

Based on this, we should expect that for each finite abelian
p
-group
H
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[|\text{Sur}(\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(\alpha)}),H)|]=1,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[|\text{Sur}(G(L_{n}^{(\alpha)}),H)|]=\frac{1}{|H|}.

However, this is not always the case, as we will show in Section
7
. These limits sometimes blow up to infinity.
The issue here is that a small subset of matrices contribute greatly to the surjective moments while contributing minimally to the distribution. We can get around this problem by restricting ourselves to a subset of the matrices whose probability goes to
1
as
n\to\infty
, and computing the moments on this subset.

2.1.
Independent diagonal model

We denote

(2)

\displaystyle\Gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})

\displaystyle=\Big\{1\leq i\leq n_{1}\mid c_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p}\Big\}

and

\displaystyle\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})=|\Gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})|.

Since the
c_{i}
are Haar-uniform, we should expect
\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})
to be concentrated around
\frac{1}{p}n_{1}
. Given a constant
\rho>0
, we consider the subset of matrices for which
|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\frac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}
. We show in Corollary
8.1.1
that the probability of this goes to
1
as
n\to\infty
.

Given a function
f
that takes
M_{n_{1},n_{2}}
as input, we denote the conditional expectation as

\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[f(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})]=\mathbb{E}[f(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})\mid|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}].

We also denote the expectation restricted to the event
|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}
as

\mathbb{E}_{\rho}^{\prime}[f(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})]=\mathbb{P}\left[|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}\right]\times\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[f(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})].

We will show that once we restrict ourselves to this subset, we get the right surjective moments and the issue of moments blowing up to infinity is resolved.

Proposition 2.2
.

Given
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha\leq 1
,
0<\rho<\min(\frac{1}{p},\alpha-\frac{1}{p})
and a finite abelian
p
-group
G
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[|\text{Sur}(\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(\alpha)}),G)|]=1.

Note that the restriction
\rho<\alpha-\frac{1}{p}
is to avoid the matrices for which
\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})>n_{2}=\lceil\alpha n\rceil
. These are the matrices responsible for the large surjective moments.
If
\alpha=1
, then no matrix can have
\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})>n_{2}=n
. In fact, if
\alpha=1
, then the issue of moments blowing up does not arise and one could work directly with the raw moments
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\big|\text{Sur}(\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(1)}),G)\big|]
.

Once we have the moments, we can show that the desired distribution occurs using the following result of Wood.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
17
, Theorem 8.3]

If
X_{n}
is a sequence of random finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules and
Y
is a random finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module such that for every finite abelian
p
-group
G
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[|\text{Sur}(X_{n},G)|]=\mathbb{E}[|\text{Sur}(Y,G)|]\leq|\Lambda^{2}G|.

Then for every finite abelian
p
-group
G
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[X_{n}\cong G]=\mathbb{P}[Y\cong G].

Fix

Let

p
-group distributed according to

\mathbb{P}\left[G_{n}\cong G\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})\cong G\mid|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n\right].

Therefore, Proposition
2.2
says that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[|\text{Sur}(G_{n},G)|]=1.

Therefore from Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.3
we see that for each finite abelian
p
-group
G
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})\cong G\mid|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n\right]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[G_{n}\cong G]=P_{\infty,1}(G).

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})\cong G]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})\cong G\mid|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]\times\mathbb{P}[|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}[\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})\cong G\mid|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|>\rho n]\times\mathbb{P}[|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|>\rho n].

The result follows since Corollary
8.1.1
says that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]=1
.
∎

Moreover, to prove Proposition
2.2
, it is enough to compute the Hom-moments.

Proposition 2.4
.

Given
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha\leq 1
,
0<\rho<\min(\frac{1}{p},\alpha-\frac{1}{p})
and a finite abelian
p
-group
G
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[|\text{Hom}(\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(\alpha)}),G)|]=|\{\text{Subgroups of }G\}|.

We show that the computation of Hom-moments indeed leads to the surjective moments.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

The result follows from Proposition
2.4
by induction on the size of
G
.
∎

2.2.
Laplacian model

Similarly to the independent diagonal model denote

\displaystyle\Gamma(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})

\displaystyle=\Big\{1\leq i\leq n_{1}\mid\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}b_{ij}\equiv 0\pmod{p}\Big\}

and

\displaystyle\gamma(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})=|\Gamma(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})|.

Even though
b_{ij}
are not necessarily Haar uniform, as
n_{2}
gets bigger
\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}b_{ij}
would get close to being uniformly distributed
\mod p
. Therefore, we should still expect
\gamma(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})
to be concentrated around
\frac{1}{p}n_{1}
. Given a constant
\rho>0
, we consider the subset of matrices for which
|\gamma(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\frac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}
. We show in Lemma
8.2
that the probability of this goes to
1
as
n\to\infty
.

Also as before, for a function
f
that takes
L_{n_{1},n_{2}}
as input, we denote

\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[f(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})]=\mathbb{E}[f(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})\mid|\gamma(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}],

and

\mathbb{E}_{\rho}^{\prime}[f(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})]=\mathbb{P}[|\gamma(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}]\times\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[f(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})].

Once we restrict ourselves to this subset, then the issue of moments blowing up to infinity gets resolved and we get the right moments.

Proposition 2.5
.

Given
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha\leq 1
,
0<\rho<\min(\frac{1}{p},\alpha-\frac{1}{p})
and a finite abelian
p
-group
G
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[|\text{Sur}(G(L_{n}^{(\alpha)}),G)|]=\frac{1}{|G|}.

As in the independent diagonal model, the restriction
\rho<\alpha-\frac{1}{p}
is to avoid the matrices for which
\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})>n_{2}=\lceil\alpha n\rceil
. These are the matrices responsible for the large surjective moments.
These matrices correspond to those bipartite directed graphs for which the number of vertices in
V_{1}
whose in-degree is divisible by
p
is larger than
|V_{2}|=\lceil\alpha n\rceil
.
If
\alpha=1
, then no matrix can have
\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})>n_{2}=n
. In fact, if
\alpha=1
, then the issue of moments blowing up does not arise and one could work directly with the raw moments
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\big|\text{Sur}(G(L_{n}^{(1)}),G)\big|]
.

We show that Proposition
2.5
implies Theorem
1.7
.

Theorem
1.7
.

Given
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha\leq 1
and a finite abelian
p
-group
G
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[G(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})\cong G]=P_{\infty,\frac{1}{p}}(G).

Proof of Theorem
1.7
assuming Proposition
2.5
.

Fix
0<\rho<\min(\frac{1}{p},\alpha-\frac{1}{p})
.
Proposition
2.5
, Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.3
imply that for each finite abelian
p
-group
G
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[G(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})\cong G\mid|\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]=P_{\infty,\frac{1}{p}}(G).

Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[G(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})\cong G]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[G(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})\cong G\mid|\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]\times\mathbb{P}[|\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}[G(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})\cong G\mid|\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|>\rho n]\times\mathbb{P}[|\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|>\rho n].

The result follows since Lemma
8.2
says that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[|\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]=1
.
∎

Therefore, our goal now is to prove Proposition
2.4
and Proposition
2.5
.

3.
Setup for Independent diagonal model

Fix a finite abelian
p
-group
G
for this and the next section.
Similarly to
[
17
]
, we can decompose the expected number of homomorphisms to
G
as a sum of probabilities.
Since
M_{n_{1},n_{2}}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho}^{\prime}[|\text{Hom}(\text{Coker}(M_{n_{1},n_{2}}),G)|]

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)}\mathbb{P}\left[F\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}\right].

For each
F\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)
,

(3)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}[F\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}]\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq[n_{1}]:\\
||S|-\frac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}[F\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }\Gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})=S].\end{split}

Denote
A_{j}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{1j}\\
\vdots\\
a_{n_{1}j}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}}
,

B_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}b_{i1}\\
\vdots\\
b_{in_{2}}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{2}}
.
Denote the standard basis of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}}
as
u_{1},\dots,u_{n_{1}}
and that of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{2}}
as
v_{1},\dots,v_{n_{2}}
.
Also note that
F
can be decomposed into
F_{1}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}},G)
and
F_{2}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{2}},G)
.
Since the columns of
M_{n_{1},n_{2}}
are independent of each other, we see that

(4)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }\Gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})=S]\\
&=\prod_{i\in S}\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})\text{ and }c_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p}]\\
&\quad\quad\times\prod_{i\in[n_{1}]\setminus S}\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})\text{ and }c_{i}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}]\\
&\quad\quad\times\prod_{j\in[n_{2}]}\mathbb{P}[F_{1}(A_{j})=-d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})].\end{split}

We therefore want to estimate the probabilities appearing in (
4
). The vectors
A_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}}
and
B_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{2}}
have independent,
\epsilon
-balanced entries. We will first compute such probabilities, in the case when the entries are Haar-uniform.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose we are given
F\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},G)
with
\text{Im}(F)=H
. Let
X\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
be a random vector whose entries are independent and Haar-uniform.
Then for each
h\in H
, we have

\mathbb{P}\big[F(X)=h\big]=\frac{1}{|H|}.

Proof.

Note that for each
g\in H
,
F^{-1}(g)
is a translation of
\ker(F)
. Since the Haar measure is translation invariant, each
F^{-1}(g)
must have the same Haar measure. Finally, since the measure of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
is
1
, the measure of each
F^{-1}(g)
is
\frac{1}{|H|}
.
∎

In the case where the entries of
X
are
\epsilon
-balanced but not Haar-uniform, then we cannot compute the probability with such a simple method.
In the next subsection, we will describe how to partition
\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)
, how to estimate the probabilities for
F
in certain parts and how to bound them for
F
in other parts.

3.1.
Codes and depth

In this subsection, we recall some material due to Wood
[
17
,
18
]
.
Let
V=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
with the standard basis
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
. Denote
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
. Given a subset
\sigma\subseteq[n]
, we have a distinguished submodule
V_{\setminus\sigma}\subseteq V
, which is generated by
v_{i}
satisfying
i\notin\sigma
and another distinguished submodule
V_{\sigma}\subseteq V
, which is generated by
v_{i}
satisfying
i\in\sigma
. The notions of code and
\delta
-depth were defined by Wood
[
18
]
. Our definitions are slightly different from hers.

Definition 3.2
.

Consider
F\in\text{Hom}(V,G)
. It is called a code of distance
d
with image
H
if for every subset
\sigma\subseteq[n]
with
|\sigma|<d
, we have
F(V_{\setminus\sigma})=F(V)=H
.

This means not only is
F
surjective to
H
, but it remains surjective to
H
as long as you do not throw away too many basis vectors.
If
\text{Im}(F)=H
and
F
is not a code, it means we can throw away a few basis vectors and shrink the image. The depth of
F
measures the extent to which we can shrink the image without throwing away too many basis vectors.

Definition 3.3
.

Given
\delta>0
and
F\in\text{Hom}(V,G)
, the
\delta
-depth of
F
is defined to be the largest positive integer
D
for which there is a subset
\sigma\subseteq[n]
with
|\sigma|<\log_{p}(D)\delta n
such that
[F(V):F(V_{\setminus\sigma})]=D
, or
1
if there is no such
\sigma
.

If such a
\sigma
exists, then we say that
F
is of
\delta
-type
(F(V),F(V_{\setminus\sigma}))
. If there is no such
\sigma
, then we set the
\delta
-type to be
(F(V),F(V))
.

It is noted in
[
18
, Remark 2.5]
that if
F
has
\delta
-depth
1
, then it is a code of distance
\delta n
.

Given subgroups
T\subseteq H\subseteq G
, we denote

\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,T):=\{F\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},G):\delta-\text{type }(H,T)\}.

This gives a cover

\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},G)=\bigcup_{H\subseteq G}\bigcup_{T\subseteq H}\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,T).

In general, this union need not be disjoint, since a given
F
may have
\delta
-type
(H,T)
for more than one choice of
T
. However, the pieces
\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,H)
consist of codes with image
H
, and these pieces are pairwise disjoint.

First consider the case
T=H
, so all
F\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,H)
are codes of distance
\delta n
with image
H
. For such
F
, Wood shows that the probability is close to
\frac{1}{|H|}
even when
X
is
\epsilon
-balanced.

Lemma 3.4
.

[
18
, Lemma 2.1]

Suppose
F\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},G)
is a code of distance
d
with image
H
. Let
X\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
be a random vector whose entries are independent and
\epsilon
-balanced.
Then for every
f\in H
, we have

\left|\mathbb{P}[F(X)=f]-\frac{1}{|H|}\right|\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon d}{|G|^{2}}\Big).

Next consider the case
T\subsetneq H
. For
F\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,T)
, the probability can be bigger than
\frac{1}{|H|}
, but Wood shows that it can still be bounded as follows.

Lemma 3.5
.

[
18
, Lemma 2.7]

Suppose we are given subgroups
T\subsetneq H\subseteq G
,
Let
X\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
be a random vector whose entries are independent and
\epsilon
-balanced.
Then for every
F\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,T)
and
f\in G
, we have

\mathbb{P}[F(X)=f]\leq(1-\epsilon)\left(\frac{1}{|T|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right).

Proof.

Actually the result in
[
18
, Lemma 2.7]
is for
f=0
, but the same proof goes through for any
f\in G
.
∎

Corollary 3.5.1
.

Suppose we are given
\delta>0
and subgroups
T\subseteq H\subseteq G
.
Denote

e=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }T=H\\
1-\epsilon&\text{if }T\subsetneq H.\end{cases}

Let
X\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
be a random vector whose entries are independent and
\epsilon
-balanced.
Then for every
F\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,T)
and for every
f\in G
, we have

\mathbb{P}[F(X)=f]\leq e\left(\frac{1}{|T|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right).

Proof.

First, consider the case
T=H
, so
F
is a code of distance
\delta n
with image
T
. Now, if
f\notin T
, then the probability is zero and the inequality holds trivially, whereas if
f\in T
, then the result follows from Lemma
3.4
.

Next, consider the case
T\subsetneq H
. Then the result follows from Lemma
3.5
.
∎

We will also need a bound for the probability that
F(X)
belongs to another subgroup.

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose we are given
\delta>0
, subgroups
T\subseteq H\subseteq G
and another subgroup
T^{\prime}\subseteq G
.
Let
X\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
be a random vector whose entries are independent and
\epsilon
-balanced.
Then for every
F\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,T)
, we have

\mathbb{P}[F(X)\in T^{\prime}]\leq\left(\frac{|T\cap T^{\prime}|}{|T|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right).

Proof.

Denote
D=[H:T]
.
Consider the map
\pi:H\twoheadrightarrow H/(H\cap T^{\prime})
. So
\pi\circ F\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},H/(H\cap T^{\prime}))
. Moreover,
F(X)\in T^{\prime}
if and only if
(\pi\circ F)(X)=0
.
There is some
\sigma\subseteq[n]
with
|\sigma|\leq\delta n\log_{p}(D)
such that
F(V_{\setminus\sigma})=T
.

Also,

F\in\text{Hom}(V_{\setminus\sigma}\oplus V_{\sigma},G)=\text{Hom}(V_{\setminus\sigma},G)\oplus\text{Hom}(V_{\sigma},G),

so we can decompose
F=(F_{\setminus\sigma},F_{\sigma})
and
X=(X_{\setminus\sigma},X_{\sigma})
. Note that

(\pi\circ F)(X)=(\pi\circ F_{\setminus\sigma})(X_{\setminus\sigma})+(\pi\circ F_{\sigma})(X_{\sigma}).

We will show that
\pi\circ F_{\setminus\sigma}
is a code of distance
\delta n
with image
\pi(T)
. Assume for the sake of contradiction that this is not true. This means there is some proper subgroup
L\subsetneq\pi(T)
and
\tau\subseteq[n]\setminus\sigma
with
|\tau|<\delta n
such that
(\pi\circ F)(V_{\setminus(\sigma\cup\tau)})\subseteq L
.
Hence
F(V_{\setminus(\sigma\cup\tau)})\subseteq\pi^{-1}(L)
. We also know that
F(V_{\setminus(\sigma\cup\tau)})\subseteq F(V_{\setminus\sigma})=T
, and hence
F(V_{\setminus(\sigma\cup\tau)})\subseteq\pi^{-1}(L)\cap T
.
Since
L
is a proper subgroup of
\pi(T)
, it follows that
\pi^{-1}(L)\cap T
is a proper subgroup of
T
. This means that
D^{\prime}=[H:\pi^{-1}(L)\cap T]\geq pD
. Moreover,
|\sigma\cup\tau|<\delta n\log_{p}(D)+\delta n\leq\delta n\log_{p}(D^{\prime})
. However, this implies that the
\delta
-depth of
F
is at least
D^{\prime}
, which is a contradiction. We conclude that
\pi\circ F_{\setminus\sigma}
is a code of distance
\delta n
with image
\pi(T)
.

Therefore, by Lemma
3.4
, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[F(X)\in T^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[(\pi\circ F)(X)=0]=\mathbb{P}[(\pi\circ F_{\setminus\sigma})(X_{\setminus\sigma})=-(\pi\circ F_{\sigma})(X_{\sigma})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[(\pi\circ F_{\setminus\sigma})(X_{\setminus\sigma})=-(\pi\circ F_{\sigma})(X_{\sigma})\mid(\pi\circ F_{\sigma})(X_{\sigma})\in\pi(T)\right]\mathbb{P}[(\pi\circ F_{\sigma})(X_{\sigma})\in\pi(T)]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{|\pi(T)|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)\times 1.

The result follows since
|\pi(T)|=\frac{|T|}{|T\cap T^{\prime}|}
.
∎

We will also need bounds on the sizes of
\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,T)
. We start with the case
T\subsetneq H
.

Lemma 3.7
.

[
17
, Lemma 5.2]

Given subgroups
T\subsetneq H\subseteq G
, denote
D=[H:T]>1
. Then we have

|\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,T)|\leq\binom{n}{\lceil\delta\log_{p}(D)n\rceil-1}|T|^{n}D^{\delta\log_{p}(D)n}.

Denote the binary entropy function as
H(\alpha)=-\alpha\log_{2}(\alpha)-(1-\alpha)\log_{2}(1-\alpha)
. It is known that
\binom{a}{b}\leq 2^{H(\frac{b}{a})a}
and that
H(\alpha)\leq 2\sqrt{\alpha}
. It follows that
\binom{a}{b}\leq 2^{2\sqrt{ab}}
.
The following corollary follows at once.

Corollary 3.7.1
.

Given
D>1
and subgroups
T\subsetneq H\subseteq G
with
[H:T]=D
, we have

|\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,T)|\leq 2^{2n\sqrt{\delta\log_{p}(|G|)}}|T|^{n}D^{\delta\log_{p}(D)n}.

Given a group
G
, let
K_{G}
be the number of subgroups of
G
.
We estimate the size of
\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,H)
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Consider
0<\delta<\frac{1}{6\log_{p}(|G|)}
.
There is a constant
c_{G,\delta}>0
(depending on
G
and
\delta
), such that for each subgroup
H\subseteq G

|H|^{n}\Big(1-K_{G}\exp(-c_{G,\delta}n)-K_{G}2^{-n}\Big)\leq|\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,H)|\leq|H|^{n}.

Proof.

The upper bound follows from the fact that

\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,H)\subseteq\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},H).

Moreover,

\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},H)\setminus\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,H)=\bigcup_{T\subsetneq H}\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,T)\cup\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},T).

The number of
T
is at most
K_{G}
, and

|\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},T)|=|T|^{n}\leq\frac{|H|^{n}}{2^{n}}.

Denote
[H:T]=D>1
. Then we know that

|\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,T)|\leq 2^{2n\sqrt{\delta\log_{p}(|G|)}}|H|^{n}D^{-n+\delta\log_{p}(D)n}\leq|H|^{n}\Big(2^{2\sqrt{\delta\log_{p}(|G|)}-1+\delta\log_{p}(|G|)}\Big)^{n}.

The bound on
\delta
also ensures that
2\sqrt{\delta\log_{p}(|G|)}-1+\delta\log_{p}(|G|)<0
. The result follows.
∎

4.
Independent diagonal model

We have fixed a finite abelian
p
-group
G
.
Recall that given
\delta>0
, we can partition

\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}},G)=\bigcup_{H_{1}\subseteq G}\bigcup_{T_{1}\subseteq H_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\delta}(H_{1},T_{1}),

\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{2}},G)=\bigcup_{H_{2}\subseteq G}\bigcup_{T_{2}\subseteq H_{2}}\mathcal{S}_{n_{2},\delta}(H_{2},T_{2}).

We will show the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

Given
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha\leq 1
and
0<\rho<\min(\frac{1}{p},\alpha-\frac{1}{p})
and a finite abelian
p
-group
G
, there is
\delta>0
such that for any subgroups
T_{1}\subseteq H_{1}\subseteq G
,
T_{2}\subseteq H_{2}\subseteq G
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\lceil\alpha n\rceil,\delta}(H_{2},T_{2})}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }T_{1}=H_{1}=T_{2}=H_{2}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

This will imply Proposition
2.4
.

Proposition
2.4
.

Given
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha\leq 1
,
0<\rho<\min(\frac{1}{p},\alpha-\frac{1}{p})
and a finite abelian
p
-group
G
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[|\text{Hom}(\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(\alpha)}),G)|]=|\text{Subgroups of }G|.

Proof of Proposition
2.4
assuming Proposition
4.1
.

Proposition
4.1
implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}^{\prime}[|\text{Hom}(\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(\alpha)}),G)|]=|\text{Subgroups of }G|.

The result follows from Corollary
8.1.1
.
∎

On each piece, our goal is to estimate

\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}].

This probability can be decomposed as shown in (
3
) and (
4
).

4.1.
Main term

In this subsection, the only requirement we put on
\delta
is that
0<\delta<\frac{1}{3\log_{p}(|G|)}
and we focus on the case when
T_{1}=H_{1}=T_{2}=H_{2}
. Denote this subgroup as
H
.
This means that
F_{1}
is a code of distance
\delta n_{1}
with image
H
and
F_{2}
is a code of distance
\delta n_{2}
with image
H
.

We start by estimating the probability that
(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0
and
\Gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})=S
for a fixed subset
S\subseteq[n_{1}]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha\leq 1
,

\alpha n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq n_{1}
and
S\subseteq[n_{1}]
.
If
H
is a subgroup of
G
,
F_{1}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}},G)
is a code of distance
\delta n_{1}
with image
H
and
F_{2}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{2}},G)
is a code of distance
\delta n_{2}
with image
H
. Then, for sufficiently large values of
n_{1}
(depending on
G,\epsilon,\delta,\alpha
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }\Gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})=S]-\frac{1}{p^{|S|}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{n_{1}-|S|}\frac{1}{|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p^{|S|}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{n_{1}-|S|}4n_{1}\frac{1}{|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big).

Proof.

We estimate each term in the product given by (
4
). First, for
i\in S
,
we know that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})\text{ and }c_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[c_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p}]\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})\mid c_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})\mid c_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p}].

Moreover, Lemma
3.4
tells us that

\left|\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})\mid c_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p}]-\frac{1}{|H|}\right|\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n_{2}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big).

For
i\in[n_{1}]\setminus S
, we similarly see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})\text{ and }c_{i}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[c_{i}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}]\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})\mid c_{i}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}]

\displaystyle=(1-\tfrac{1}{p})\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})\mid c_{i}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}].

Moreover, Lemma
3.4
tells us that

\left|\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})\mid c_{i}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}]-\frac{1}{|H|}\right|\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n_{2}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big).

Finally for
j\in[n_{2}]
, Lemma
3.4
tells us that

\left|\mathbb{P}[F_{1}(A_{j})=-d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})]-\frac{1}{|H|}\right|\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big).

Now, in order to apply Corollary
8.3.1
, we need

\frac{\exp\big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\big)}{2^{\frac{1}{2n_{1}}}-1}\leq\frac{1}{|H|}.

This inequality holds for sufficiently large
n_{1}
, since the limit of the left hand side is
0
. Therefore, Corollary
8.3.1
tells us that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\frac{1}{p^{|S|}}(1-\frac{1}{p})^{n_{1}-|S|}}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }\Gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})=S]-\frac{1}{|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2(n_{1}+n_{2})\frac{1}{|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big).

The result follows.
∎

Next, we sum over the different subsets
S\subseteq[n_{1}]
that satisfy
||S|-\frac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}
.

Corollary 4.2.1
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha\leq 1
,
0<\rho<\min(\frac{1}{p},\alpha-\frac{1}{p})
,
\alpha n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq n_{1}
and
H
is a subgroup of
G
.
Suppose that
F_{1}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}},G)
is a code of distance
\delta n_{1}
with image
H
and
F_{2}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{2}},G)
is a code of distance
\delta n_{2}
with image
H
.
Then, for sufficiently large values of
n_{1}
(depending on
G,\epsilon,\delta,\alpha
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}]-\frac{1}{|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 4n_{1}\frac{1}{|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)+\frac{2}{|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\exp\big(-2\rho^{2}n_{1}\big).

Proof.

We sum the estimate of Lemma
4.2
over the different
S\subseteq[n_{1}]
with
||S|-\frac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}]-\frac{1}{|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{k:|k-\frac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\tbinom{n_{1}}{k}\tfrac{1}{p^{k}}\big(1-\tfrac{1}{p}\big)^{n_{1}-k}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 4n_{1}\frac{1}{|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big).

Lemma
8.1
implies that

\left|\frac{1}{|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{k:|k-\frac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\binom{n_{1}}{k}\frac{1}{p^{k}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{n_{1}-k}-\frac{1}{|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\right|\leq\frac{2}{|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\exp\big(-2\rho^{2}n_{1}\big).\qed

We now sum over the different
F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,H)
,
F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,H)
. Here we will use our estimate for the size of
\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,H)
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha\leq 1
,
0<\rho<\min(\frac{1}{p},\alpha-\frac{1}{p})
,
H
is a subgroup of
G
and
\delta
is a constant satisfying
0<\delta<\frac{1}{3\log_{p}(|G|)}
.
Then, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,H)}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\lceil\alpha n\rceil,\delta}(H,H)}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]=1.

Proof.

Let
n_{1}=n
and
n_{2}=\lceil\alpha n\rceil
.
Corollary
4.2.1
implies that

\displaystyle\Bigg|\left(\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\delta}(H,H)}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{2},\delta}(H,H)}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}]\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad-\frac{|\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\delta}(H,H)||\mathcal{S}_{n_{2},\delta}(H,H)|}{|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 4n_{1}|H|\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)+2\exp\big(-2\rho^{2}n_{1}\big).

Lemma
3.8
tells us that

|H|^{n_{1}}-K_{G}|H|^{n_{1}}\Big(\exp(-c_{G,\delta}n_{1})+2^{-n_{1}}\Big)\leq|\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\delta}(H,H)|\leq|H|^{n_{1}},

and

|H|^{n_{2}}-K_{G}|H|^{n_{2}}\Big(\exp(-c_{G,\delta}n_{2})+2^{-n_{2}}\Big)\leq|\mathcal{S}_{n_{2},\delta}(H,H)|\leq|H|^{n_{2}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Big||\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\delta}(H,H)|\times|\mathcal{S}_{n_{2},\delta}(H,H)|-|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq K_{G}|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\Big(\exp(-c_{G,\delta}n_{1})+2^{-n_{1}}+\exp(-c_{G,\delta}n_{2})+2^{-n_{2}}\Big).

This implies

\bigg|\frac{|\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\delta}(H,H)|\times|\mathcal{S}_{n_{2},\delta}(H,H)|}{|H|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}-1\bigg|\leq 2K_{G}\Big(\exp(-c_{G,\delta}\alpha n_{1})+2^{-\alpha n_{1}}\Big).

The result follows.
∎

4.2.
Error term

We are now left with the case when
T_{1}
,
H_{1}
,
T_{2}
,
H_{2}
are not all the same.
So at least of the following hold:

•

T_{1}\subsetneq H_{1}
;

•

T_{2}\subsetneq H_{2}
;

•

T_{1}\neq T_{2}
.

We will actually split this further into the following cases:

•

T_{1}\subsetneq H_{1}
;

•

T_{2}\subsetneq H_{2}
;

•

T_{1}\not\subseteq T_{2}
;

•

T_{2}\not\subseteq T_{1}
and
\alpha\neq 1
;

•

T_{1}=H_{1}
,
T_{2}=H_{2}
,
T_{1}\subsetneq T_{2}
and
\alpha=1
.

For
t\in\{1,2\}
, denote

e_{t}=\begin{cases}1-\epsilon&\text{if }T_{t}\subsetneq H_{t}\\
1&\text{if }T_{t}=H_{t}.\end{cases}

We will consider
F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})
and
F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{2},\delta}(H_{2},T_{2})
and bound

\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}].

We start by bounding the probability that
(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0
and
\Gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})=S
for a fixed subset
S\subseteq[n_{1}]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha\leq 1
and
0<\rho<\min(\frac{1}{p},\alpha-\frac{1}{p})
.
Fix
S\subseteq[n_{1}]
.
Consider
F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})
and
F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{2},\delta}(H_{2},T_{2})
.
Assuming
\alpha n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq n_{1}
and that
n_{1}
is sufficiently large in terms of
G,\alpha,\delta,\epsilon
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }\Gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})=S]

\displaystyle\leq(\tfrac{1}{p})^{|S|}(\tfrac{1}{|G|})^{n_{1}-|S|}\frac{e_{1}^{n_{2}}e_{2}^{|S|}}{|T_{1}|^{n_{2}}|T_{2}|^{|S|}}\left(1+4|G|n_{1}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\prod_{i\in[n_{1}]\setminus S}\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})=F_{1}(u_{i})].

Proof.

Recall from (
4
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }\Gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})=S]

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in S}\mathbb{P}[c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})+F_{2}(B_{i})=0\text{ and }c_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p}]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\prod_{i\in[n_{1}]\setminus S}\mathbb{P}[c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})+F_{2}(B_{i})=0\text{ and }c_{i}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\prod_{j\in[n_{2}]}\mathbb{P}[d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})+F_{1}(A_{j})=0].

For each
j\in[n_{2}]
, we know from Corollary
3.5.1
that

\mathbb{P}[F_{1}(A_{j})=-d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})]\leq e_{1}\left(\frac{1}{|T_{1}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right).

Similarly, for each
i\in S
, we know by Corollary
3.5.1
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})+F_{2}(B_{i})=0\text{ and }c_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})\mid c_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p}]\mathbb{P}[c_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p}]

\displaystyle\leq e_{2}\left(\frac{1}{|T_{2}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n_{2}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)\times\frac{1}{p}.

Finally, consider
i\in[n_{1}]\setminus S
. Note that even though
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the value of
c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})
only depends on
c_{i}\pmod{|G|}
.
Therefore, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})+F_{2}(B_{i})=0\text{ and }c_{i}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-kF_{1}(u_{i})]\mathbb{P}[c_{i}\equiv k\pmod{|G|}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})=F_{1}(u_{i})].

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }\Gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})=S]

\displaystyle\leq(\tfrac{1}{p})^{|S|}(\tfrac{1}{|G|})^{n_{1}-|S|}e_{1}^{n_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{|T_{1}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)^{n_{2}}e_{2}^{|S|}\left(\frac{1}{|T_{2}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n_{2}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)^{|S|}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\prod_{i\in[n_{1}]\setminus S}\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})=F_{1}(u_{i})].

If
n_{1}
is sufficiently large to ensure

\frac{\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)}{2^{\frac{1}{2n_{1}}}-1}<\frac{1}{|G|}\leq\min\Big(\frac{1}{|T_{1}|},\frac{1}{|T_{2}|}\Big),

then we are done by Corollary
8.3.1
.
∎

Next, we sum over the different subsets
S\subseteq[n_{1}]
that satisfy
||S|-\frac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}
and also over different
F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})
and
F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{2},\delta}(H_{2},T_{2})
.

For each
F_{1}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}},G)
, there is some
\sigma\subseteq[n_{1}]
with
|\sigma|\leq\delta\log_{p}(D_{1})n_{1}
such that
F_{1}(V_{\setminus\sigma})\subseteq T_{1}
. We will consider fixed subsets
\sigma\subseteq[n_{1}]
and sum over those
F_{1}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}},G)
for which
F_{1}(V_{\setminus\sigma})\subseteq T_{1}
. We will denote the sum over such
F_{1}
as
\sum_{F_{1}:\sigma}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha\leq 1
and
0<\rho<\min(\alpha-\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{p})
.
Assume that the subgroups
T_{1}\subseteq H_{1}\subseteq G
and
T_{2}\subseteq H_{2}\subseteq G
satisfy at least one of the following

•

T_{1}\subsetneq H_{1}
;

•

T_{2}\subsetneq H_{2}
;

•

T_{1}\not\subseteq T_{2}
;

•

T_{2}\not\subseteq T_{1}
and
\alpha\neq 1
.

Assuming
\delta>0
is sufficiently small in terms of these, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\lceil\alpha n\rceil,\delta}(H_{2},T_{2})}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]=0.

Proof.

Denote
D_{1}=[H_{1}:T_{1}]
,
D_{2}=[H_{2}:T_{2}]
,
n_{1}=n
and
n_{2}=\lceil\alpha n\rceil
.
First, consider a fixed
S\subseteq[n_{1}]
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{2},\delta}(H_{2},T_{2})}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0\text{ and }\Gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})=S]

\displaystyle\leq(\tfrac{1}{p})^{|S|}(\tfrac{1}{|G|})^{n_{1}-|S|}\frac{e_{1}^{n_{2}}e_{2}^{|S|}}{|T_{1}|^{n_{2}}|T_{2}|^{|S|}}\left(1+4|G|n_{1}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{2},\delta}(H_{2},T_{2})}\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})}\prod_{i\in[n_{1}]\setminus S}\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})=F_{1}(u_{i})].

We will fix a choice of
F_{2}
, and bound the sum

\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})}\prod_{i\in[n_{1}]\setminus S}\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})=F_{1}(u_{i})].

We write
V=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}}
.
For a subset
\sigma\subseteq[n_{1}]
, denote by
V_{\sigma}
the subgroup of
V
generated by
u_{i}
for
i\in\sigma
and
V_{\setminus\sigma}
to be the subgroup of
V
generated by
u_{i}
for
i\notin\sigma
.
Each
F_{1}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}},G)
with
\delta
-depth
D_{1}
and type
(H_{1},T_{1})
, has some
\sigma\subseteq[n_{1}]
with
|\sigma|=\delta\log_{p}(D_{1})n_{1}
such that
F_{1}(V_{\setminus\sigma})\subseteq T_{1}
and
F_{1}(V_{\sigma})\subseteq H_{1}
.

We fix a choice of
\sigma\subseteq[n_{1}]
(in addition to fixing
F_{2}
and
S
).

•

For
i\in S\cap\sigma
, we have
|H_{1}|
choices for
F_{1}(u_{i})
.

•

For
i\in S\setminus\sigma
, we have
|T_{1}|
choices for
F_{1}(u_{i})
.

•

For
i\in\sigma\setminus S
,
F_{1}(u_{i})\in H_{1}
, so

\sum_{h\in H_{1}}\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})=h]=\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})\in H_{1}]\leq|(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}|=(1-\tfrac{1}{p})|G|.

•

For
i\in(S\cup\sigma)^{c}
,
F_{1}(u_{i})\in T_{1}
, so

\sum_{h\in T_{1}}\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})=h]=\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})\in T_{1}]=(1-\tfrac{1}{p})|G|\times\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})\in T_{1}].

By Lemma
3.6
, this is at most

(1-\tfrac{1}{p})|G|\times\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{2}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n_{2}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right).

We conclude that the sum over those
F_{1}
corresponding to
\sigma
is bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{1}:\sigma}\prod_{i\in[n_{1}]\setminus S}\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})=F_{1}(u_{i})]

\displaystyle\leq|H_{1}|^{|S\cap\sigma|}|T_{1}|^{|S\setminus\sigma|}(1-\tfrac{1}{p})^{n_{1}-|S|}|G|^{n_{1}-|S|}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{2}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n_{2}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)^{n_{1}-|S\cup\sigma|}

\displaystyle\leq D_{1}^{|\sigma|}|T_{1}|^{|S|}(1-\tfrac{1}{p})^{n_{1}-|S|}|G|^{n_{1}-|S|}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{2}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n_{2}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)^{n_{1}-|S|-|\sigma|}

\displaystyle\leq|T_{1}|^{|S|}(1-\tfrac{1}{p})^{n_{1}-|S|}|G|^{n_{1}-|S|}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{2}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)^{n_{1}-|S|}\left(\frac{D_{1}|T_{2}|}{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}\right)^{|\sigma|}.

Once
n_{1}
is large enough to ensure,

\frac{\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)}{2^{\frac{1}{n_{1}}}-1}<\frac{1}{|G|},

then by Corollary
8.3.1
we have

\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{2}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)^{n_{1}-|S|}\leq\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|^{n_{1}-|S|}}{|T_{2}|^{n_{1}-|S|}}\left(1+2n_{1}|G|\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right).

By summing over
\sigma\subseteq[n_{1}]
with
|\sigma|=\delta\log_{p}(D_{1})n_{1}
, we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n_{1},\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})}\prod_{i\in[n_{1}]\setminus S}\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})=F_{1}(u_{i})]

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n_{1}}{\delta\log_{p}(D_{1})n_{1}}|T_{1}|^{|S|}(1-\tfrac{1}{p})^{n_{1}-|S|}|G|^{n_{1}-|S|}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|^{n_{1}-|S|}}{|T_{2}|^{n_{1}-|S|}}\left(1+2n_{1}|G|\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)\left(\frac{D_{1}|T_{2}|}{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}\right)^{\delta\log_{p}(D_{1})n_{1}}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\lceil\alpha n\rceil,\delta}(H_{2},T_{2})}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0\text{ and }\Gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})=S]

\displaystyle\leq(\tfrac{1}{p})^{|S|}(\tfrac{1}{|G|})^{n_{1}-|S|}\frac{e_{1}^{n_{2}}e_{2}^{|S|}}{|T_{1}|^{n_{2}}|T_{2}|^{|S|}}\left(1+4|G|n_{1}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\times\binom{n_{2}}{\delta n_{2}\log_{p}(D_{2})}|T_{2}|^{n_{2}}D_{2}^{\delta n_{2}\log_{p}(D_{2})}\times\binom{n_{1}}{\delta\log_{p}(D_{1})n_{1}}|T_{1}|^{|S|}(1-\tfrac{1}{p})^{n_{1}-|S|}|G|^{n_{1}-|S|}

\displaystyle\times\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|^{n_{1}-|S|}}{|T_{2}|^{n_{1}-|S|}}\left(1+2n_{1}|G|\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)\left(\frac{D_{1}|T_{2}|}{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}\right)^{\delta\log_{p}(D_{1})n_{1}}.

Rearranging the terms and using the fact that
\binom{a}{b}\leq 2^{2\sqrt{ab}}
, we see that the above expression is at most

\displaystyle(\tfrac{1}{p})^{|S|}(1-\tfrac{1}{p})^{n_{1}-|S|}e_{1}^{n_{2}}e_{2}^{|S|}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{1}|}\right)^{n_{2}-|S|}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{2}|}\right)^{n_{1}-n_{2}}

\displaystyle\times 2^{2n_{2}(\delta\log_{p}(D_{2}))^{1/2}}2^{2n_{1}(\delta\log_{p}(D_{1}))^{1/2}}D_{2}^{\delta n_{2}\log_{p}(D_{2})}\left(\frac{D_{1}|T_{2}|}{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}\right)^{\delta\log_{p}(D_{1})n_{1}}

\displaystyle\times\left(1+4|G|n_{1}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)\left(1+2n_{1}|G|\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right).

Since
e_{1},e_{2}\leq 1
,
n_{2}=\lceil\alpha n\rceil
and
(\frac{1}{p}-\rho)n\leq|S|\leq(\frac{1}{p}+\rho)n
we see that

\displaystyle e_{1}^{n_{2}}e_{2}^{|S|}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{1}|}\right)^{n_{2}-|S|}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{2}|}\right)^{n_{1}-n_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(e_{1}^{\alpha}e_{2}^{\frac{1}{p}-\rho}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{1}|}\right)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}-\rho}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{2}|}\right)^{1-\alpha}\right)^{n}\frac{|T_{2}|}{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}.

Next, note that

\displaystyle 2^{2n_{2}(\delta\log_{p}(D_{2}))^{1/2}}2^{2n_{1}(\delta\log_{p}(D_{1}))^{1/2}}D_{2}^{\delta n_{2}\log_{p}(D_{2})}\left(\frac{D_{1}|T_{2}|}{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}\right)^{\delta\log_{p}(D_{1})n_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2(\log_{p}(D_{2}))^{1/2}}D_{2}^{\log_{p}(D_{2})}\left(2^{2\alpha(\log_{p}(D_{2}))^{1/2}}2^{2(\log_{p}(D_{1}))^{1/2}}D_{2}^{\alpha\log_{p}(D_{2})}\left(\frac{D_{1}|T_{2}|}{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}\right)^{\log_{p}(D_{1})}\right)^{n\sqrt{\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2(\log_{p}(|G|))^{1/2}}|G|^{\log_{p}(|G|)}\left(2^{4(\log_{p}(|G|))^{1/2}}|G|^{3\log_{p}(|G|)}\right)^{n\sqrt{\delta}}.

Further note that for sufficiently large
n
, we have

\left(1+4|G|n_{1}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)\left(1+2n_{1}|G|\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)<2.

We write

B=2^{4(\log_{p}(|G|))^{1/2}}|G|^{3\log_{p}(|G|)},\quad\quad b=\frac{|T_{2}|}{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}2^{2(\log_{p}(|G|))^{1/2}}|G|^{\log_{p}(|G|)}2.

Then we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\lceil\alpha n\rceil,\delta}(H_{2},T_{2})}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0\text{ and }\Gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})=S]

\displaystyle\leq(\tfrac{1}{p})^{|S|}(1-\tfrac{1}{p})^{n_{1}-|S|}b\left(e_{1}^{\alpha}e_{2}^{\frac{1}{p}-\rho}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{1}|}\right)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}-\rho}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{2}|}\right)^{1-\alpha}B^{\sqrt{\delta}}\right)^{n}.

By summing over different
S
, we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\lceil\alpha n\rceil,\delta}(H_{2},T_{2})}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]

\displaystyle\leq b\left(e_{1}^{\alpha}e_{2}^{\frac{1}{p}-\rho}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{1}|}\right)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}-\rho}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{2}|}\right)^{1-\alpha}B^{\sqrt{\delta}}\right)^{n}.

Note that
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
,
\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{1}|}
and
\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{2}|}
are all at most one.
Now, at least one of the following holds

•

T_{1}\subsetneq H_{1}
.
In this case
e_{1}<1
and
\alpha>0
, so
e_{1}^{\alpha}<1
.

•

T_{2}\subsetneq H_{2}
.
In this case
e_{2}<1
and
\frac{1}{p}-\rho>0
, so
e_{2}^{\frac{1}{p}-\rho}<1
.

•

T_{1}\not\subseteq T_{2}
.
In this case
\frac{|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|}{|T_{1}|}<1
and
\alpha-\frac{1}{p}-\rho>0
, so
\Big(\frac{|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|}{|T_{1}|}\Big)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}-\rho}<1
.

•

T_{2}\not\subseteq T_{1}
and
\alpha\neq 1
.
In this case
\frac{|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|}{|T_{2}|}<1
and
1-\alpha>0
, so
\Big(\frac{|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|}{|T_{2}|}\Big)^{1-\alpha}<1
.

This means that in all cases

e_{1}^{\alpha}e_{2}^{\frac{1}{p}-\rho}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{1}|}\right)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}-\rho}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{2}|}\right)^{1-\alpha}<1.

We can therefore choose a sufficiently small
\delta
to ensure that

e_{1}^{\alpha}e_{2}^{\frac{1}{p}-\rho}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{1}|}\right)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}-\rho}\left(\frac{|T_{2}\cap T_{1}|}{|T_{2}|}\right)^{1-\alpha}B^{\sqrt{\delta}}<1.

The result follows.
∎

We are only left with the case
T_{1}=H_{1}
,
T_{2}=H_{2}
,
T_{1}\subsetneq T_{2}
and
\alpha=1
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose
\alpha=1
and
0<\rho<\min(\alpha-\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{p})
.
Assume that the subgroups
T_{1}\subseteq H_{1}\subseteq G
and
T_{2}\subseteq H_{2}\subseteq G
satisfy
T_{1}=H_{1}
,
T_{2}=H_{2}
and
T_{1}\subsetneq T_{2}
.
For
\delta>0
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(T_{1},T_{1})}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(T_{2},T_{2})}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]=0.

Proof.

Since
\alpha=1
,
n_{1}=n_{2}=n
.
We have
e_{1}=e_{2}=1
,
F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(T_{1},T_{1})
are codes of distance
\delta n
with image
T_{1}
and
F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(T_{2},T_{2})
are codes of distance
\delta n
with image
T_{2}
.

By the independence of columns of
M_{n_{1},n_{2}}
, we have

\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0]=\prod_{i\in[n_{1}]}\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})]\prod_{j\in[n_{2}]}\mathbb{P}[F_{1}(A_{j})=-d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})].

For each
i\in[n]
, we know by Corollary
3.5.1
that

\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})]\leq\frac{1}{|T_{2}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{2}}\Big).

For each
j\in[n]
, we know from Corollary
3.5.1
that

\mathbb{P}[F_{1}(A_{j})=-d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})]\leq\frac{1}{|T_{1}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{2}}\Big).

However, if
F_{2}(v_{j})\in T_{2}\setminus T_{1}
, then we can get a tighter upper bound. Since
F_{1}(A_{j})
can only take values in
T_{1}
, if
F_{1}(A_{j})=-d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})
then
d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})\in T_{1}
. If
F_{2}(v_{j})\notin T_{1}
, this forces
d_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.
We conclude that if
F_{2}(v_{j})\in T_{2}\setminus T_{1}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[F_{1}(A_{j})=-d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[F_{1}(A_{j})=-d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})\mid d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})\in T_{1}]\mathbb{P}[d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})\in T_{1}]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{|T_{1}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)\mathbb{P}[d_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{p}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{1}{|T_{1}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right).

Since
F_{2}
is a code of distance
\delta n
with image
T_{2}
, we know that

|\{j\in[n]:F_{2}(v_{j})\notin T_{1}\}|\geq\delta n.

It follows that

\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0]\leq\left(\frac{1}{|T_{2}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{\delta n}\left(\frac{1}{|T_{1}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right)^{n}.

Once
n
is large enough to ensure,

\frac{\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{2}}\Big)}{2^{\frac{1}{2n}}-1}<\frac{1}{|G|},

then by Corollary
8.3.1
we know that

\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0]\leq\Big(\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{\delta n}\frac{1}{|T_{2}|^{n}|T_{1}|^{n}}\left(1+4n|G|\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right).

Since
|\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(T_{1},T_{1})|\leq|T_{1}|^{n}
and
|\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(T_{2},T_{2})|\leq|T_{2}|^{n}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(T_{1},T_{1})}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(T_{2},T_{2})}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(T_{1},T_{1})}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(T_{2},T_{2})}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0]

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{\delta n}\left(1+4n|G|\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{2}}\Big)\right).

The result follows.
∎

Notice that Proposition

, Proposition

and Proposition

. We have seen that Proposition

5.
Setup for Laplacian model

We continue to have a fixed finite abelian
p
-group
G
, and we wish to estimate the expected number of surjections from
G(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})
to
G
.

We start by turning the problem of computing the moments of the Laplacian model, into a sum of certain probabilities of the independent diagonal model. This is very similar to the argument of Wood in
[
17
]
.

We denote
\text{Sur}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)
to be the set of those maps in
\text{Sur}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)
, that are still surjective when restricted to
Z_{n_{1}+n_{2}}
. Note that every map in
\text{Sur}(Z_{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)
, can be extended to
|G|
maps in
\text{Sur}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)
.
Since
L_{n_{1},n_{2}}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},Z_{n_{1}+n_{2}})
, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho}^{\prime}[|\text{Sur}(G(L_{n_{1},n_{2}}),G)|]

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\text{Sur}(Z_{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)}\mathbb{P}[F\circ L_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)\text{ and }|\gamma(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{F\in\text{Sur}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)}\mathbb{P}[F\circ L_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)\text{ and }|\gamma(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}].

We will relate this to our computations with
M_{n_{1},n_{2}}
.
Recall that
M_{n_{1},n_{2}}
includes
c_{j}
and
d_{i}
as its entries, but these do not appear in
L_{n_{1},n_{2}}
. Therefore, the
c_{j}
and
d_{i}
are independent of the entries of
L_{n_{1},n_{2}}
.
Moreover, they are Haar-uniform, so we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[F\circ L_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)\text{ and }|\gamma(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[F\circ L_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)\text{ and }|\gamma(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}]

\displaystyle\times|G|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\mathbb{P}[c_{i}\equiv-\sum_{j}b_{ij}\pmod{|G|}]\prod_{j=1}^{n_{2}}\mathbb{P}[d_{j}\equiv-\sum_{i}a_{ij}\pmod{|G|}]

\displaystyle=|G|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\mathbb{P}\Big[F\circ L_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)\text{ and }|\gamma(L_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{ and }d_{j}\equiv-\sum_{i}a_{ij}\pmod{|G|}\text{ and }c_{i}\equiv-\sum_{j}b_{ij}\pmod{|G|}\Big]

\displaystyle=|G|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\mathbb{P}\Big[F\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{ and }d_{j}+\sum_{i}a_{ij}\equiv 0\pmod{|G|}\text{ and }c_{i}+\sum_{j}b_{ij}\equiv 0\pmod{|G|}\Big].

Denote
F_{0}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})
, where
F_{0}
maps a vector to the sum of its entries mod
|G|
. This means that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho}^{\prime}[|\text{Sur}(G(L_{n_{1},n_{2}}),G)|]

\displaystyle\!\!\!=|G|^{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{F\in\text{Sur}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{P}\Big[(F,F_{0})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}),|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}\Big].

Note that
F\in\text{Sur}^{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G)
if and only if
(F,F_{0})\in\text{Sur}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}))
.
Denote the projections from
G\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})
to
G
as
\pi_{1}
, and to
\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
as
\pi_{2}
.
Denote

\text{Sur}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})):=\{F\in\text{Sur}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})):\pi_{2}\circ F=F_{0}\}.

We conclude that

(5)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{\rho}^{\prime}[|\text{Sur}(G(L_{n_{1},n_{2}}),G)|]\\
&=|G|^{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{F\in\text{Sur}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}))}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{P}\Big[F\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}\Big].\end{split}

Note that the sum in (

(G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})
-moment of
\text{Coker}(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})
.
The difference is that we are only summing over some of the surjections from
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}
to
G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
.
We will use results from the previous section to estimate the probability that
F\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0
and
|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}

Also denote

\text{Hom}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},G\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})):=\{F\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},G\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})):\pi_{2}\circ F=F_{0}\}.

For
F\in\text{Sur}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}))
, let
F_{1}
be the restriction of
F
to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}}

F_{2}

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{2}}

F_{2}\in\text{Hom}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{2}},G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})

.
This means that if
F_{1}
is of type
(H_{1},T_{1})
and
F_{2}

H_{1}+H_{2}=G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
.
Moreover, since

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}

\pi_{2}
is surjective when restricted to
H_{1}
,

H_{2}

\pi_{2}(H_{1})=\pi_{2}(T_{1})=\pi_{2}(H_{2})=\pi_{2}(T_{2})=\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}.

Definition 5.1
.

We call a subgroup
H
of
G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
,
\pi_{2}
-full if
\pi_{2}(H)=\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
.

Given
\pi_{2}
-full subgroups
T\subseteq H\subseteq G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
, we denote

\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(H,T):=\{F\in\text{Hom}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}):\delta-\text{type }(H,T)\}.

We will estimate the size of
\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(H,T)
. We start with the case
T\subsetneq H
.
Note that the size of
\mathcal{S}_{n,\delta}(H,T)
was of the order of
|T|^{n}
, but the size of
\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(H,T)
should be smaller because each basis element has to be mapped to something of the form
(g,1)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Consider
D>1
and subgroups
T\subsetneq H\subseteq G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
such that
H
,
T
are both
\pi_{2}
-full and
[H:T]=D
. Then we have

|\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(H,T)|\leq 2^{n\sqrt{2\delta\log_{p}(|G|)}}\frac{|T|^{n}}{|G|^{n}}D^{\delta\log_{p}(D)n}.

Proof.

Any
F\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(H,T)
would have a
\sigma\subseteq[n]
, such that
|\sigma|<\delta\log_{p}(D)n
and
F(V_{\setminus\sigma})\subseteq T
,
F(V_{\sigma})\subseteq H
. By enlarging
\sigma
, we can assume that
|\sigma|=\lceil\delta\log_{p}(D)n\rceil-1
.

Now there are
\binom{n}{\lceil\delta\log_{p}(D)n\rceil-1}
choices for
\sigma
and once we choose
\sigma
then:

•

For
i\in\sigma
,
F(v_{i})\in H\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}(1)
. Since
H
is
\pi_{2}
-full
|H\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}(1)|=\frac{|H|}{|G|}=D\frac{|T|}{|G|}
, so
F(v_{i})
has
D\frac{|T|}{|G|}
choices.

•

For
i\not\in\sigma
,
F(v_{i})\in T\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}(1)
. Since
T
is also
\pi_{2}
-full
|T\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}(1)|=\frac{|T|}{|G|}
, so
F(v_{i})
has
\frac{|T|}{|G|}
choices.

The result follows since
\binom{n}{\lceil\delta\log_{p}(D)n\rceil-1}\leq 2^{n\sqrt{\delta\log_{p}(|G|^{2})}}
.
∎

For ease of notation we denote
L_{G}=K_{G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}}
, the number of subgroups of
G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
. We will now estimate the size of
\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(H,H)
for
H=G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
. We do not need this estimate for other

.

Lemma 5.3
.

Consider
0<\delta<\frac{1}{6\log_{p}(|G|)}
.
There is a constant
c_{G,\delta}>0
(depending on
G
and
\delta
), such that

|G|^{n}\Big(1-L_{G}\exp(-c_{G,\delta}n)-L_{G}2^{-n}\Big)\leq|\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z},G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})|\leq|G|^{n}.

Proof.

The upper bound follows from the fact that

\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z},G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})\subseteq\text{Hom}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\text{Hom}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})\setminus\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z},G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{T\subsetneq G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z},T)\cup\text{Hom}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},T).

The number of
T
is at most
L_{G}
, and

|\text{Hom}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},T)|=\frac{|T|^{n}}{|G|^{n}}\leq\frac{|G|^{2n}}{2^{n}|G|^{n}}.

Denote
[G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}:T]=D>1
. Then we know that

|\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z},T)|\leq 2^{n\sqrt{2\delta\log_{p}(|G|)}}\frac{|G|^{2n}}{|G|^{n}}D^{-n+\delta\log_{p}(D)n}\leq|G|^{n}\Big(2^{\sqrt{2\delta\log_{p}(|G|)}-1+\delta\log_{p}(|G|^{2})}\Big)^{n}.

The bound on
\delta
also ensures that
\sqrt{2\delta\log_{p}(|G|)}-1+2\delta\log_{p}(|G|)<0
. The result follows.
∎

Now consider
F\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(H,T)
, for
\pi_{2}
-full subgroups
T\subseteq H\subseteq G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
. Let
X\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
be a random vector whose entries are independent and
\epsilon
-balanced.

•

If
T=H
, then Lemma
3.4
implies that for every
f\in H
, we have

\left|\mathbb{P}[F(X)=f]-\frac{1}{|H|}\right|\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big).

•

Denote

e=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }T=H\\
1-\epsilon&\text{if }T\subsetneq H.\end{cases}

Then by Corollary
3.5.1
for every
f\in G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\mathbb{P}[F(X)=f]\leq e\left(\frac{1}{|T|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right).

•

In Lemma
3.6
we bounded the probability that
F(X)
belongs to another subgroup.
However, for the Laplacian model, we will need to bound the probability that
F(X)
belongs to another subgroup and
\pi_{2}(F(X))\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.

We first bound the probability that
F(X)
belongs to another subgroup and

, for

with

.

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose
\delta>0
and
T,T^{\prime}\subseteq G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
are
\pi_{2}
-full subgroups.
Let
X\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
be a random vector whose entries are independent and
\epsilon
-balanced.
Then, for every
F\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(T,T)
we have

\mathbb{P}[F(X)\in T^{\prime}\text{ and }\pi_{2}(F(X))\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}]\leq\left((1-\tfrac{1}{p})\frac{|T\cap T^{\prime}|}{|T|}+|G|^{2}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right).

Proof.

We know that
T
and
T^{\prime}
are all
\pi_{2}
-full.
We divide the proof into two cases based on whether
T\cap T^{\prime}
is
\pi_{2}
-full or not.

(1)

Case 1:
T\cap T^{\prime}
is not
\pi_{2}
-full. In this case, if
F(X)\in T^{\prime}
, then
F(X)\in T\cap T^{\prime}
, so
\pi_{2}(F(X))\notin(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Therefore,

\mathbb{P}[F(X)\in T^{\prime}\text{ and }\pi_{2}(F(X))\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}]=0.

The result follows.

(2)

Case 2:
T\cap T^{\prime}
is
\pi_{2}
-full. Then we know that

|(T\cap T^{\prime})\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}((\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times})|=(1-\tfrac{1}{p})|(T\cap T^{\prime})|.

Therefore, Lemma
3.4
says that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[F(X)\in T^{\prime}\text{ and }\pi_{2}(F(X))\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[F(X)\in(T\cap T^{\prime})\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}((\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times})]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\tfrac{1}{p})|T\cap T^{\prime}|\left(\frac{1}{|T|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right).

The result follows.∎

Next, we bound the probability that
F(X)
belongs to another subgroup and
\pi_{2}(F(X))\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, for

without the requirement that

. This leads to a slightly weaker bound.

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose
\delta>0
,
T\subseteq H\subseteq G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
are
\pi_{2}
-full subgroups, and
T^{\prime}\subseteq G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
, is another
\pi_{2}
-full subgroup.
Let
X\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
be a random vector whose entries are independent and
\epsilon
-balanced.
Then, for every
F\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(H,T)

\mathbb{P}[F(X)\in T^{\prime}\text{ and }\pi_{2}(F(X))\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}]\leq\left((1-\tfrac{1}{p})\frac{\min(|T|,|T^{\prime}|)}{|T|}+|G|^{2}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right).

Proof.

Denote
D=[H:T]
.
We know that
H
,
T
and
T^{\prime}
are all
\pi_{2}
-full.
We divide the proof into two cases based on whether
T\cap T^{\prime}
is
\pi_{2}
-full or not.

(1)

Case 1:
T\cap T^{\prime}
is not
\pi_{2}
-full. Since
T
and
T^{\prime}
are both
\pi_{2}
-full, this implies that
T\cap T^{\prime}
is a proper subset of both
T
and of
T^{\prime}
. This means that

|T\cap T^{\prime}|\leq\tfrac{1}{p}\min(|T|,|T^{\prime}|)\leq(1-\tfrac{1}{p})\min(|T|,|T^{\prime}|).

Moreover, Lemma
3.6
implies that

\mathbb{P}[F(X)\in T^{\prime}\text{ and }\pi_{2}(F(X))\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}]\leq\mathbb{P}[F(X)\in T^{\prime}]\leq\left(\frac{|T\cap T^{\prime}|}{|T|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right).

The result follows.

(2)

Case 2:
T\cap T^{\prime}
is
\pi_{2}
-full.
There is some
\sigma\subseteq[n]
with
|\sigma|\leq\delta n\log_{p}(D)
such that
F(V_{\setminus\sigma})=T
.
Recall that
V_{\setminus\sigma}
is the submodule of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
generated by those basis vectors
u_{i}
for which
i\notin\sigma
. Let
V_{\sigma}
be the submodule generated by
u_{i}
with
i\in\sigma
. Now,

F\in\text{Hom}(V_{\setminus\sigma}\oplus V_{\sigma},G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})=\text{Hom}(V_{\setminus\sigma},G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})\oplus\text{Hom}(V_{\sigma},G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}),

so we can decompose
F=(F_{\setminus\sigma},F_{\sigma})
and
X=(X_{\setminus\sigma},X_{\sigma})
. We know that

F(X)=F_{\setminus\sigma}(X_{\setminus\sigma})+F_{\sigma}(X_{\sigma}).

Thus
F(X)\in T^{\prime}
if and only if
F_{\setminus\sigma}(X_{\setminus\sigma})\equiv-F_{\sigma}(X_{\sigma})\pmod{T^{\prime}}
.
Similarly,
\pi_{2}(F(X))\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
if and only if
\pi_{2}(F_{\setminus\sigma}(X_{\setminus\sigma}))\not\equiv-\pi_{2}(F_{\sigma}(X_{\sigma}))\pmod{p}
.
By conditioning based on the value of
F_{\sigma}(X_{\sigma})
, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[F(X)\in T^{\prime}\text{ and }\pi_{2}(F(X))\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{h_{0}\in H}\mathbb{P}[F_{\sigma}(X_{\sigma})=h_{0}]\mathbb{P}[F_{\setminus\sigma}(X_{\setminus\sigma})\equiv-h_{0}\pmod{T^{\prime}}\text{ and }\pi_{2}(F_{\setminus\sigma}(X_{\setminus\sigma}))\not\equiv-\pi_{2}(h_{0})\pmod{p}].

For
h_{0}\in H
, define

\displaystyle S_{1}(h_{0})

\displaystyle=\{h\in T:h\equiv-h_{0}\pmod{T^{\prime}}\},

\displaystyle S_{2}(h_{0})

\displaystyle=\{h\in T:h\equiv-h_{0}\pmod{T^{\prime}}\text{ and }\pi_{2}(h)\not\equiv-\pi_{2}(h_{0})\pmod{p}\}.

This means that,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[F(X)\in T^{\prime}\text{ and }\pi_{2}(F(X))\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{h_{0}\in H}\mathbb{P}[F_{\sigma}(X_{\sigma})=h_{0}]\mathbb{P}[F_{\setminus\sigma}(X_{\setminus\sigma})\in S_{2}(h_{0})].

We will show that for each
h_{0}
,
|S_{2}(h_{0})|\leq(1-\frac{1}{p})|T\cap T^{\prime}|
.

•

If
S_{1}(h_{0})=\emptyset
, then this is trivially true.

•

If
S_{1}(h_{0})\neq\emptyset
, then pick
h_{1}\in S_{1}(h_{0})
. It follows that
S_{1}(h_{0})=h_{1}+T\cap T^{\prime}
. Since
\pi_{2}(T\cap T^{\prime})=\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
(this is because we are in Case 2), it follows that
|S_{2}(h_{0})|\leq(1-\frac{1}{p})|T\cap T^{\prime}|
.

By an identical argument to the one in Lemma
3.6
, we see that
F_{\setminus\sigma}
is a code of distance
\delta n
with image
T
.
Therefore, Lemma
3.4
says that

\mathbb{P}[F_{\setminus\sigma}(X_{\setminus\sigma})\in S_{2}(h_{0})]\leq|S_{2}(h_{0})|\left(\frac{1}{|T|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right).

The result follows since
|S_{2}(h_{0})|\leq(1-\frac{1}{p})|T\cap T^{\prime}|
.

∎

If we take
T^{\prime}=G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
, then

. We note this in a corollary.

.

\delta>0
,

-full subgroups.
Let
X\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}

\epsilon
-balanced.

\mathbb{P}[\pi_{2}(F(X))\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}]\leq\left((1-\tfrac{1}{p})+|G|^{2}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right).

Proof.

Take
T^{\prime}=G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
.

Recall the following:

(1)

We had shown in (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho}^{\prime}[|\text{Sur}(G(L_{n_{1},n_{2}}),G)|]

\displaystyle=|G|^{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{F\in\text{Sur}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}+n_{2}},G\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}))}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{P}\Big[F\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}\Big].

(2)

Each

\pi_{2}
-full subgroup of

.
On each such piece, our goal is to estimate

.

.

6.1.
Main term

In this subsection, we consider the case

and
F_{2}
are codes of distance
\delta n_{1}
and

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose
\frac{1}{p}<\alpha\leq 1
,
0<\rho<\min(\frac{1}{p},\alpha-\frac{1}{p})
and
0<\delta<\frac{1}{6\log_{p}(|G|)}
and
L_{G}
,
c_{G,\delta}
be the constants from Lemma

.
Suppose
\alpha n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq n_{1}
.
Denote
S_{1}=\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n_{1},\delta}(G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z},G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})
and
S_{2}=\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n_{2},\delta}(G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z},G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})
.
Then, for sufficiently large values of
n_{1}
(depending on
G,\epsilon,\delta,\alpha

\displaystyle\left||G|^{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}\left(\sum_{F_{1}\in S_{1}}\sum_{F_{2}\in S_{2}}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}]\right)-\frac{1}{|G|}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 4n_{1}|G|\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{4}}\Big)+\frac{2}{|G|}\exp\big(-2\rho^{2}n_{1}\big)+\frac{2L_{G}}{|G|}\exp(-c_{G,\delta}\alpha n_{1})+\frac{2L_{G}}{|G|}2^{-\alpha n_{1}}.

Proof.

We know from Corollary
4.2.1
that for sufficiently large values of
n_{1}
, for each
F_{1}\in S_{1}
and
F_{2}\in S_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}]-\frac{1}{|G|^{2(n_{1}+n_{2})}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 4n_{1}\frac{1}{|G|^{2(n_{1}+n_{2}-1)}}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{4}}\Big)+\frac{2}{|G|^{2(n_{1}+n_{2})}}\exp\big(-2\rho^{2}n_{1}\big).

We sum over these
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
.
As an upper bound we use
|S_{1}|\leq|\text{Hom}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}},G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})|=|G|^{n_{1}}
,
|S_{2}|\leq|\text{Hom}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{2}},G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})|=|G|^{n_{2}}

\displaystyle\Bigg|\left(\sum_{F_{1}\in S_{1}}\sum_{F_{2}\in S_{2}}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}]\right)-\frac{|S_{1}||S_{2}|}{|G|^{2(n_{1}+n_{2})}}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 4n_{1}\frac{1}{|G|^{n_{1}+n_{2}-2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{4}}\Big)+2\frac{1}{|G|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\exp\big(-2\rho^{2}n_{1}\big).

By Lemma
5.3
we see that

|G|^{n_{1}}-L_{G}|G|^{n_{1}}\Big(\exp(-c_{G,\delta}n_{1})+2^{-n_{1}}\Big)\leq|S_{1}|\leq|G|^{n_{1}},

|G|^{n_{2}}-L_{G}|G|^{n_{2}}\Big(\exp(-c_{G,\delta}n_{2})+2^{-n_{2}}\Big)\leq|S_{2}|\leq|G|^{n_{2}}.

\Big||S_{1}||S_{2}|-|G|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\Big|\leq L_{G}|G|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\Big(\exp(-c_{G,\delta}n_{1})+2^{-n_{1}}+\exp(-c_{G,\delta}n_{2})+2^{-n_{2}}\Big).

\bigg|\frac{|S_{1}||S_{2}|}{|G|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}-1\bigg|\leq 2L_{G}\Big(\exp(-c_{G,\delta}\alpha n_{1})+2^{-\alpha n_{1}}\Big).

We conclude that

\displaystyle\left||G|^{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}\left(\sum_{F_{1}\in S_{1}}\sum_{F_{2}\in S_{2}}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}]\right)-\frac{1}{|G|}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|G|^{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}\Bigg|\left(\sum_{F_{1}\in S_{1}}\sum_{F_{2}\in S_{2}}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n_{1},n_{2}})-\tfrac{1}{p}n_{1}|\leq\rho n_{1}]\right)-\frac{|S_{1}||S_{2}|}{|G|^{2(n_{1}+n_{2})}}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{1}{|G|}\bigg|\frac{|S_{1}||S_{2}|}{|G|^{n_{1}+n_{2}}}-1\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 4n_{1}|G|\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{4}}\Big)+2\frac{1}{|G|}\exp\big(-2\rho^{2}n_{1}\big)+2L_{G}\frac{1}{|G|}\Big(\exp(-c_{G,\delta}\alpha n_{1})+2^{-\alpha n_{1}}\Big).

The result follows.
∎

.

6.2.
Error term

We are now left with the case when
T_{1}
,
H_{1}
,
T_{2}
,
H_{2}
,

are not all the same.
Since
H_{1}+H_{2}=G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
, at least of the following must hold:

•

T_{1}\subsetneq H_{1}
;

•

T_{2}\subsetneq H_{2}
;

•

For

Proposition 6.4

\displaystyle\leq(\tfrac{1}{p})^{|S|}(\tfrac{1}{|G|})^{n_{1}-|S|}\frac{e_{1}^{n_{2}}e_{2}^{|S|}}{|T_{1}|^{n_{2}}|T_{2}|^{|S|}}\left(1+4|G|^{2}n_{1}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n_{2},\delta}(H_{2},T_{2})}\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n_{1},\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})}\prod_{i\in[n_{1}]\setminus S}\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})=F_{1}(u_{i})].

We will fix a choice of
F_{2}
, and bound the sum

\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n_{1},\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})}\prod_{i\in[n_{1}]\setminus S}\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})=F_{1}(u_{i})].

We write

.

For

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})\in T_{1}\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}(1)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})\in T_{1}\text{ and }\pi_{2}(F_{2}(B_{i}))\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}].

Denote

A=\begin{cases}|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|&\text{if }T_{2}=H_{2}\\
\min(|T_{1}|,|T_{2}|)&\text{if }T_{2}\subsetneq H_{2}.\end{cases}

By Lemma

, this is at most

\left((1-\tfrac{1}{p})\frac{A}{|T_{2}|}+|G|^{2}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n_{2}}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right).

We conclude that the sum over those
F_{1}
corresponding to
\sigma
is bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{1}:\sigma}\prod_{i\in[n_{1}]\setminus S}\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})=F_{1}(u_{i})]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{|H_{1}|}{|G|}\right)^{|S\cap\sigma|}\left(\frac{|T_{1}|}{|G|}\right)^{|S\setminus\sigma|}\left((1-\tfrac{1}{p})+|G|^{2}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right)^{|\sigma\setminus S|}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\left((1-\tfrac{1}{p})\frac{A}{|T_{2}|}+|G|^{2}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right)^{n_{1}-|S\cup\sigma|}.

\left(\frac{|H_{1}|}{|G|}\right)^{|S\cap\sigma|}\left(\frac{|T_{1}|}{|G|}\right)^{|S\setminus\sigma|}(1-\tfrac{1}{p})^{n_{1}-|S|}\left(\frac{A}{|T_{2}|}\right)^{n_{1}-|S\cup\sigma|}\left(1+2n_{1}\frac{|G|^{2}}{1-\frac{1}{p}}|G|^{2}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right).

\left(\frac{A}{|T_{2}|}\right)^{n_{1}-|S\cup\sigma|}\leq\left(\frac{A}{|T_{2}|}\right)^{n_{1}-|S|-|\sigma|}
and for sufficiently large

\left(1+2n_{1}\frac{|G|^{2}}{1-\frac{1}{p}}|G|^{2}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right)\leq 2.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{1}:\sigma}\prod_{i\in[n_{1}]\setminus S}\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})=F_{1}(u_{i})]

\displaystyle\leq 2(1-\tfrac{1}{p})^{n_{1}-|S|}\left(\frac{|T_{1}|}{|G|}\right)^{|S|}\left(\frac{A}{|T_{2}|}\right)^{n_{1}-|S|}\left(\frac{D_{1}|T_{2}|}{A}\right)^{|\sigma|}.

By summing over
\sigma\subseteq[n_{1}]
with
|\sigma|=\delta\log_{p}(D_{1})n_{1}
, we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n_{1},\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})}\prod_{i\in[n_{1}]\setminus S}\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{P}[lF_{2}(B_{i})=F_{1}(u_{i})]

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n_{1}}{\delta\log_{p}(D_{1})n_{1}}2(1-\tfrac{1}{p})^{n_{1}-|S|}\left(\frac{|T_{1}|}{|G|}\right)^{|S|}\left(\frac{A}{|T_{2}|}\right)^{n_{1}-|S|}\left(\frac{D_{1}|T_{2}|}{A}\right)^{\delta\log_{p}(D_{1})n_{1}}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n_{1},\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n_{2},\delta}(H_{2},T_{2})}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0\text{ and }\Gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})=S]

\displaystyle\leq(\tfrac{1}{p})^{|S|}(\tfrac{1}{|G|})^{n_{1}-|S|}\frac{e_{1}^{n_{2}}e_{2}^{|S|}}{|T_{1}|^{n_{2}}|T_{2}|^{|S|}}\left(1+4|G|^{2}n_{1}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right)

Since

,

, we see that

\displaystyle 2^{2n_{2}\sqrt{\delta\log_{p}(D_{2})}}2^{2n_{1}\sqrt{\delta\log_{p}(D_{1})}}D_{2}^{\delta n_{2}\log_{p}(D_{2})}\left(\frac{D_{1}|T_{2}|}{A}\right)^{\delta\log_{p}(D_{1})n_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2^{2\sqrt{2\log_{p}(|G|)}}2^{2\sqrt{2\log_{p}(|G|)}}|G|^{4\log_{p}(|G|)}|G|^{8\log_{p}(|G|)}\right)^{n\sqrt{\delta}}.

Further note that for sufficiently large
n
, we have

\left(1+4n_{1}|G|^{2}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta\alpha n_{1}}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right)<2.

We write

Then we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{\lceil\alpha n\rceil,\delta}(H_{2},T_{2})}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0\text{ and }\Gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})=S]

\displaystyle\leq 4|G|^{2}\frac{1}{|G|^{n+\lceil\alpha n\rceil}}(\tfrac{1}{p})^{|S|}(1-\tfrac{1}{p})^{n-|S|}\left(e_{1}^{\alpha}e_{2}^{\frac{1}{p}-\rho}\left(\frac{A}{|T_{1}|}\right)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}-\rho}\left(\frac{A}{|T_{2}|}\right)^{1-\alpha}B^{\sqrt{\delta}}\right)^{n}.

S

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(H_{1},T_{1})}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{\lceil\alpha n\rceil,\delta}(H_{2},T_{2})}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]

\displaystyle\leq 4|G|^{2}\frac{1}{|G|^{n+\lceil\alpha n\rceil}}\left(e_{1}^{\alpha}e_{2}^{\frac{1}{p}-\rho}\left(\frac{A}{|T_{1}|}\right)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}-\rho}\left(\frac{A}{|T_{2}|}\right)^{1-\alpha}B^{\sqrt{\delta}}\right)^{n}.

Note that
e_{1}
,
e_{2}

\frac{A}{|T_{1}|}

\frac{A}{|T_{2}|}
are all at most one. Moreover, at least one of the following holds

•

T_{1}\subsetneq H_{1}
.
In this case

\alpha>0

•

T_{2}\subsetneq H_{2}
.
In this case

•

•

T_{2}\not\subseteq T_{1}
,

In this case

,

Proposition 6.5
.

Suppose
\alpha=1
and
0<\rho<\min(\alpha-\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{p})
.
Consider
\pi_{2}
-full subgroups
T_{1}\subsetneq T_{2}\subseteq G\oplus\mathbb{Z}/|G|\mathbb{Z}
.
For
\delta>0
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}|G|^{2n-1}\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(T_{1},T_{1})}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(T_{2},T_{2})}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]=0.

Proof.

Since
\alpha=1
,
n_{1}=n_{2}=n
.
We have
e_{1}=e_{2}=1
,
F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(T_{1},T_{1})
are codes of distance
\delta n
with image
T_{1}
and
F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(T_{2},T_{2})
are codes of distance
\delta n
with image
T_{2}
.

By the independence of columns of
M_{n_{1},n_{2}}
, we have

\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0]=\prod_{i\in[n_{1}]}\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})]\prod_{j\in[n_{2}]}\mathbb{P}[F_{1}(A_{j})=-d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})].

For each
i\in[n]
, we know by Corollary
3.5.1
that

\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})]\leq\frac{1}{|T_{2}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big).

For each
j\in[n]
, we know from Corollary
3.5.1
that

\mathbb{P}[F_{1}(A_{j})=-d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})]\leq\frac{1}{|T_{1}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big).

However, if
F_{2}(v_{j})\in T_{2}\setminus T_{1}
, then we can get a tighter upper bound. Since
F_{1}(A_{j})
can only take values in
T_{1}
, if
F_{1}(A_{j})=-d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})
then
d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})\in T_{1}
. If
F_{2}(v_{j})\notin T_{1}
, this forces
d_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.
We conclude that if
F_{2}(v_{j})\in T_{2}\setminus T_{1}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[F_{1}(A_{j})=-d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[F_{1}(A_{j})=-d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})\mid d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})\in T_{1}]\mathbb{P}[d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})\in T_{1}]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{|T_{1}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right)\mathbb{P}[d_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{p}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{1}{|T_{1}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right).

Since
F_{2}
is a code of distance
\delta n
with image
T_{2}
, we know that

|\{j\in[n]:F_{2}(v_{j})\notin T_{1}\}|\geq\delta n.

It follows that

\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n_{1},n_{2}}=0]\leq\left(\frac{1}{|T_{2}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right)^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{\delta n}\left(\frac{1}{|T_{1}|}+\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right)^{n}.

Once
n
is large enough to ensure,

\frac{\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big)}{2^{\frac{1}{2n}}-1}<\frac{1}{|G|^{2}},

then by Corollary
8.3.1
we know that

\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0]\leq\Big(\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{\delta n}\frac{1}{|T_{2}|^{n}|T_{1}|^{n}}\left(1+4n|G|^{2}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right).

Since
|\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(T_{1},T_{1})|\leq\frac{|T_{1}|^{n}}{|G|^{n}}
and
|\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(T_{2},T_{2})|\leq\frac{|T_{2}|^{n}}{|G|^{n}}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(T_{1},T_{1})}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(T_{2},T_{2})}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0\text{ and }|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(T_{1},T_{1})}\sum_{F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{n,\delta}(T_{2},T_{2})}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|G|^{2n}}\Big(\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{\delta n}\left(1+4n|G|^{2}\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\delta n}{|G|^{4}}\Big)\right).

The result follows.
∎

Proposition 7.1
.

Consider
0<\alpha<1
and
m>\frac{2-\alpha}{1-\alpha}
.
If all
a_{ij}
and
b_{ij}
in
M_{n}^{(\alpha)}
are Haar-uniform, then we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[|\text{Hom}(\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(\alpha)}),(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{m})|]=\infty.

Proof.

Denote
G=(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{m}
,
n_{1}=n
and
n_{2}=\lceil\alpha n\rceil
. We know that

\mathbb{E}[|\text{Hom}(\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(\alpha)}),(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{m})|]=\sum_{F_{1}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}},G)}\sum_{F_{2}\in\text{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{2}},G)}\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0].

Since the columns of
M_{n}^{(\alpha)}
are independent, we see that

\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0]=\prod_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})]\prod_{j=1}^{n_{2}}\mathbb{P}[F_{1}(A_{j})=-d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})].

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. As a lower bound, we restrict ourselves to
F_{1}\in\text{Sur}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}},G)
and
F_{2}\in\text{Sur}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{2}},H)
.

By Lemma
3.1
, we know that

\mathbb{P}[F_{1}(A_{j})=-d_{j}F_{2}(v_{j})]=\frac{1}{|G|}=\frac{1}{p^{m}}.

•

If
F_{1}(u_{i})\in H
, then by Lemma
3.1
, we know that

\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})]=\frac{1}{p}.

•

If
F_{1}(u_{i})\notin H
, then

\mathbb{P}[c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})\in H]=\mathbb{P}[c_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{p}]=\frac{1}{p}.

Therefore, by Lemma
3.1
, we know that

\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})]=\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})\mid c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})\in H]\mathbb{P}[c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})\in H]=\frac{1}{p^{2}}.

We see that regardless of whether
F_{1}(u_{i})
is in
H
or not we have

\mathbb{P}[F_{2}(B_{i})=-c_{i}F_{1}(u_{i})]\geq\frac{1}{p^{2}}.

We conclude that

\mathbb{P}[(F_{1},F_{2})\circ M_{n}^{(\alpha)}=0]\geq p^{-m\lceil\alpha n\rceil-2n}\geq p^{-(m\alpha+2)n-m}.

Since
|\text{Sur}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{1}},G)|=p^{mn}(1+o(1))
and
|\text{Sur}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{2}},H)|=p^{\alpha n}(1+o(1))
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\text{Hom}(\text{Coker}(M_{n}^{(\alpha)}),(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{m})|]

\displaystyle\geq p^{-(m\alpha+2)n-m}p^{(m+\alpha)n}(1+o(1))

\displaystyle=p^{-m}(1+o(1))\big(p^{m(1-\alpha)+\alpha-2}\big)^{n}.

Since
m>\frac{2-\alpha}{1-\alpha}
, this goes to infinity as
n\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma 8.1
.

Given
\rho>0
, we have

\bigg|1-\sum_{k:|k-\frac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{1}{p^{k}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{n-k}\bigg|\leq 2\exp\big(-2\rho^{2}n\big).

Proof.

The result follows from Hoeffding’s inequality.
∎

Corollary 8.1.1
.

Given
\rho>0
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[|\gamma(M_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]=1.

Lemma 8.2
.

Given
\rho>0
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[|\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\leq\rho n]=1.

Proof.

Denote
n_{2}=\lceil\alpha n\rceil
.
For each
1\leq i\leq n
, we denote

\beta_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}b_{ij}\equiv 0\pmod{p}\\
0&\text{ if }\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}b_{ij}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}.\end{cases}

Therefore
\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}
.

Consider the map
F_{0}
from
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n_{2}}
to
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
that sends each basis vector to
1
. So
\sum_{j}b_{ij}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
if and only if
F_{0}(B_{i})=0
. Note that
F_{0}
is a code of distance
n_{2}
with image
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
.
Lemma
3.4
tells us that for each
i
,

\left|\mathbb{P}[F_{0}(B_{i})=0]-\frac{1}{p}\right|\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon n_{2}}{p^{2}}\Big)\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\alpha n}{p^{2}}\Big).

Now,

\mathbb{E}[\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\beta_{i}]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}[F_{0}(B_{i})=0],

and hence

\left|\mathbb{E}[\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})]-\frac{1}{p}n\right|\leq n\exp\Big(-\frac{\epsilon\alpha n}{p^{2}}\Big).

Next,

\text{Var}(\beta_{i})=\mathbb{P}[F_{0}(B_{i})=0](1-\mathbb{P}[F_{0}(B_{i})=0])\leq 1.

Moreover, since the
\beta_{i}
are independent, it follows that

\text{Var}(\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)}))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\text{Var}(\beta_{i})\leq n.

Notice that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n\big)^{2}\Big]=\text{Var}(\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)}))+\big(\mathbb{E}[\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})]-\tfrac{1}{p}n\big)^{2}\leq n+n^{2}\exp\Big(-\frac{2\epsilon\alpha n}{p^{2}}\Big).

So by Chebyshev’s inequality, we have

\mathbb{P}[|\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\geq\rho n]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n\big)^{2}\big]}{(\rho n)^{2}}\leq\frac{n+n^{2}\exp\Big(-\frac{2\epsilon\alpha n}{p^{2}}\Big)}{\rho^{2}n^{2}}.

This implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[|\gamma(L_{n}^{(\alpha)})-\tfrac{1}{p}n|\geq\rho n]=0.\qed

Lemma 8.3
.

Suppose we have
n\geq 2
,
0<k_{0}\leq x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
and
0<y<k_{0}(2^{1/n}-1)
, then we have

\left(1-\frac{2ny}{k_{0}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-y)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}+y)\leq\left(1+\frac{2ny}{k_{0}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}.

Proof.

Consider
f_{1}(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}+t)
,

g_{1}(t)=(1+\frac{2nt}{k_{0}})\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}
,

f_{2}(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-t)
and

g_{2}(t)=(1-\frac{2nt}{k_{0}})\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}
. Now we have
f_{1}(0)=g_{1}(0)
and for
0\leq t\leq k_{0}(2^{1/n}-1)
, we have

\displaystyle f_{1}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}(x_{i}+t)\leq\frac{n}{k_{0}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}(x_{i}+t)=\frac{n}{k_{0}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}x_{i}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}(1+\frac{t}{x_{i}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{k_{0}}\Big(\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}x_{i}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{t}{k_{0}}\Big)^{n}\leq\frac{2n}{k_{0}}\Big(\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}x_{i}\Big)=g_{1}^{\prime}(t).

We can therefore conclude that
f_{1}(y)\leq g_{1}(y)
. Next, we have
f_{2}(0)=g_{2}(0)
and for
0\leq t\leq k_{0}(2^{1/n}-1)
, we have

\displaystyle-f_{2}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}(x_{i}-t)\leq\frac{n}{k_{0}-t}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}(x_{i}-t)\leq\frac{2n}{k_{0}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}x_{i}=-g_{2}^{\prime}(t).

We conclude that
g_{2}(y)\leq f_{2}(y)
.
∎

Corollary 8.3.1
.

Suppose we have
n\geq 2
and real numbers
k_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{n},y
for which
0<k_{0}\leq x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
,
0<y<k_{0}(2^{1/n}-1)
and
|x_{i}^{\prime}-x_{i}|\leq y
, then we have

\left|\prod_{i=1}^{n}x^{\prime}_{i}-\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right|\leq\frac{2ny}{k_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}.
