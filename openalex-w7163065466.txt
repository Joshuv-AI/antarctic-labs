Abstract
Boranils, difluoroboron complexes with imine and phenoxy moieties, were synthesized by iodide-promoted demethylative borylation. The use of
appropriate amounts of BF3•OEt2, Bu4NI, and Et3N enabled the highly efficient synthesis of boranils. Boranils containing heteroaromatics and
highly π-extended boranils were also readily obtained by this method. Their physical properties were studied, and the properties were
consistent with those calculated by DFT calculations.
Keywords: boranil, boron, demethylative borylation.

Typically, boranils are synthesized by the reaction of BF3•OEt2
and imines derived from anilines and benzaldehydes bearing a

proach is synthetically useful, the obtained yields are often mod­
erate, possibly due to the intrinsic instability of hydroxyaryl
units. This issue becomes even more pronounced for hydroxysubstituted heteroaryl substrates, which are particularly unstable
and hence difficult to incorporate.
One approach to stabilizing hydroxyaryl groups is methyl
protection. Consequently, borylation reactions via the
demethylation of methyl-protected phenol derivatives have
been extensively studied.40–48 Hatakeyama40 and Müllen41 in­
dependently reported demethylative borylation reactions for
synthesizing double helicenes (Fig. 2B and C). We previously
reported an iodide-promoted demethylative borylation of
o-(2-pyridyl)anisole derivatives for synthesizing oxaborines
(Fig. 2D),49 and recently extended this strategy to the synthesis
of N,O-bidentate difluoroborons (Fig. 2E).50 Notably, these
N,O-bidentate boron complexes were obtained in high yields
even with heteroaryls bearing a methoxy group. These findings
encouraged us to explore the demethylative synthesis of bora­
nils from stable methyl-protected substrates (Fig. 2F). As an­
ticipated, several boranils were efficiently obtained.
Therefore, we herein report the first example of an iodidepromoted demethylative borylation strategy for the synthesis
of boranils.

Next, functional groups were introduced at the para-position

Substrates bearing either electron-donating or -withdrawing

electron-donating groups, were obtained in excellent yields
(2f: 92%; 2g: quant). This method was applicable to the syn­
thesis of more π-expanded boranils. Boranils 2j–m bearing a
naphthyl group were obtained in good to excellent yields.
The structure of 2j was confirmed by X-ray crystallography
(CCDC 2514376). Next, we attempted to synthesize boranils,
including heteroaromatic moieties. Unfortunately, the reac­
tion of substrate 1o bearing a benzo[b]furan moiety yielded
a complex mixture, and the desired product 2o was not ob­
tained, likely due to the instability of the substrate. In contrast,
boranils 2n and 2p, bearing an indole or a benzo[b]thiophene
skeleton, were each obtained in 75% yield. Substrate 1q, bear­
ing two reaction sites, also afforded the corresponding boranil
2q in 59% yield. A wide variety of boranils were obtained in
high yields. Notably, this method enables the synthesis of bor­
anils bearing heteroaryl groups, such as 2n and 2p.
We next examined the gram-scale telescoped synthesis of 2a
from aniline and o-methoxybenzaldehyde (Fig. 4). In the pres­
ence of MS 3A, aniline was treated with o-methoxybenzaldehyde
(1 equiv) for the generation of 1a. After removing MS 3A and the
solvent, the mixture was subjected to standard conditions, yield­
ing 1.24 g of 2a (84% yield). Thus, gram-scale 2a can be readily
obtained through a telescoped two-step procedure from com­
mercially available substrates.

Highly π-extended boranil derivative 2q also exhibited ab­

λem: 501 nm). Interestingly, incorporation of an indole unit
led to a substantial red shift in absorption, as observed for
2n (440 nm). As expected, the synthesized boranil derivatives
displayed relatively large Stokes shifts, ranging from 2,964 to
11,415 cm−1.
The FL quantum yields (Φ) of most boranil derivatives were
generally low (<0.12), except for compounds 2j, 2n, and 2q.
Compound 2j, featuring a zigzag-shaped π-extended struc­
ture, 2n, bearing an N-methylindole moiety, and 2q, incorpor­
ating two BF2 units in a zigzag-shaped π-extended
configuration, exhibited markedly enhanced FL quantum
yields of 0.63, 0.54, and 0.70, respectively. Notably, the rela­
tively high quantum yield observed for 2n highlights the utility
of incorporating heteroaryl units such as indole into the bora­
nil framework. These results indicate that the direction and
mode of π-extension are crucial in governing the optical prop­
erties of boranil derivatives. Furthermore, the experimentally
observed absorption and emission wavelengths were in good
agreement with those predicted by time-dependent density
functional theory (TD-DFT) calculations. It should be noted
that the present TD-DFT analysis is primarily intended to ra­
tionalize the transition energies and orbital characters of the
absorption and emission processes, rather than to quantita­
tively account for the FL quantum yields. The large differences
in the quantum yields among structurally related derivatives
are therefore most likely influenced not only by the electronic
transition energies but also by non-radiative deactivation
processes, which are sensitive to subtle structural and con­
formational factors. Thus, the enhanced Φ values observed
for 2j, 2n, and 2q should be regarded as experimentally estab­
lished structure–property trends, while the TD-DFT results
provide complementary information on the transition energies
and orbital contributions. The low-energy absorption bands
are largely attributed to S0→S1 transitions with predominant
HOMO→LUMO character; however, for 2n and 2q,
additional contributions from HOMO−1→LUMO and

HOMO−1→LUMO+1 transitions were also observed, respect­
ively. The corresponding emissions calculated for the optimized
excited states are predominantly described by the HOMO–
LUMO transitions (see the Supplementary Information).

3.

Conclusion

An efficient iodide-promoted demethylative borylation proto­
col for the synthesis of boranils was designed and successfully
executed from methyl-protected substrates. The cooperative
use of BF3•OEt2, Bu4NI, and Et3N enabled high-yield boranil
formation while mitigating the instability issues associated
with hydroxyaryl precursors. This method exhibited broad
substrate generality, allowing the synthesis of π-extended
and heteroaromatic boranils, and was operationally simple,
as demonstrated by a gram-scale telescoped synthesis from
commercially available starting materials.
The synthesized boranil derivatives exhibited strong UV–vis
absorption, large Stokes shifts, and structure-dependent FL
behavior. In particular, enhanced FL quantum yields were ob­
served for boranils with a zigzag-shaped π-extension or mul­
tiple BF2 units, highlighting the importance of π-conjugation
topology in controlling the optical properties. The good agree­
ment between the experimental absorption/emission wave­
lengths and TD-DFT-calculated transition energies further
supports the proposed structure–property relationship, while
the observed differences in FL quantum yields are likely asso­
ciated with non-radiative deactivation processes that are not
directly evaluated in the present calculations. This demethyla­
tive borylation strategy can provide a versatile platform for de­
veloping functional boron-based luminophores.
