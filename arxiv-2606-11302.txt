Ferromagnetism from the geometry of localized wavefunctions in moiré systems
Miguel Gonçalves, Sarang Gopalakrishnan

Abstract
We present a mechanism for ferromagnetism in narrow bands consisting of Anderson-localized states. We exploit single-particle localization to derive a controlled theory of exchange interactions within the narrow band. For quasiperiodic systems with a half-filled moiré band, we show that the critical interaction strength for ferromagnetism is highly sensitive to the geometry of real-space overlaps between localized orbitals: we find well-defined “resonances” at which ferromagnetism sets in for interaction energies that are far lower than the gap to other bands. Near these resonances, all the approximations in our theory are controlled, so our critical point predictions are quantitative . We show examples both in one and two dimensions. Our work identifies a route to ferromagnetism based on the geometry of real-space wavefunctions, distinct from previously found mechanisms based on the quantum geometry of Bloch bands.

The concrete models we consider are quasiperiodic Hubbard models in one or two dimensions, though our approach generalizes to other geometries (as well as to narrow Anderson-localized impurity bands beyond the quasiperiodic setting). Our analysis proceeds as follows: we derive an effective Hamiltonian projected onto the narrow band, and exploit wavefunction localization to recast this as a hopping Hamiltonian for a single magnon. Using a combination of exact diagonalization and analytical techniques, we determine the stability of the fully polarized state against magnons: when it is stable, the ferromagnet is at least a local minimum of the energy, and we present numerical evidence that it is in fact the global minimum.

Ferromagnetic ground state
.—We will specialize to the case where the lowest narrow band is at half filling. In the ferromagnetic phase, the ground state at this filling is a fully polarized Slater determinant,
\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle=\prod_{\alpha}\gamma_{\uparrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}\left|0\right\rangle
. The ferromagnet is unstable when adding “simple” excitations above it—in particular, spin-flip excitations, which correspond to removing a spin-
\uparrow
particle and adding a spin-
\downarrow
particle—decreases the energy. To study these excitations, it suffices to explore a two-body Hilbert space above the ferromagnetic ground state. This simplification allows us to reach large enough system sizes to converge the results to the thermodynamic limit.
The main text focuses on the lowest-energy narrow-band projection and on the local stability of ferromagnetism (i.e. one spin-flip with respect to
\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle
). To complement these results, in the SM we also show additional numerical results supporting the robustness of our conclusions upon including higher-energy states in the projection and upon considering additional spin sectors.

Charge excitations
.—Whether the ferromagnet is stable depends on the spin-flip spectrum; however, to understand the properties of spin-flip excitations (which are made up of a particle and a hole) it will be helpful to first discuss the even simpler problem of charge
\pm 1
excitations above the fully polarized state. Charge
-1
excitations consist of removing a spin-
\uparrow
electron from the fully polarized ground state
\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle
. The states
\gamma_{\uparrow,\alpha}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle
are exact eigenstates of the Hamiltonian since they are annihilated by the interactions,
so the charge
-1
spectrum is trivially
\epsilon^{(-)}_{\alpha}=-\epsilon_{\uparrow,\alpha}
.
On the other hand, charge
+1

are obtained by adding a single spin-
\downarrow

this added electron interacts with the spin-
\uparrow

The projected Hamiltonian closes exactly in the basis of states with exactly one spin-
\uparrow

\begin{split}R_{\alpha\beta}^{(+)}=&\left\langle\Uparrow\right|\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}\bar{H}\gamma_{\downarrow,\beta}^{\dagger}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle-E_{\Uparrow}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\\
=&\delta_{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{\downarrow,\alpha}+U\bar{n}_{\uparrow}\cdot\rho_{\alpha,\beta}^{\downarrow},\end{split}

(4)

where
[\bar{n}_{\uparrow}]_{\bf r}=\sum_{\lambda}|\psi_{{\bf r}}^{\uparrow,\lambda}|^{2}
is the spin-
\uparrow

[\rho_{\alpha\beta}^{\eta}]_{\bf r}=(\psi^{\eta,\alpha}_{\bf r})^{*}\psi^{\eta,\beta}_{\bf r}
. Here the dot product
a\cdot b
is defined as
\sum\nolimits_{\mathbf{r}}a_{\mathbf{r}}b_{\mathbf{r}}
. The eigenvalues of

give the exact charge +1 spectrum.
In the localized phase, the interacting contribution to Eq.

\epsilon^{(+)}_{\alpha}\approx\epsilon_{\alpha}+U\,\mathrm{IPR}(\psi^{\alpha})\,.

(5)

Thus, to a good approximation, the charge +1 excitation eigenstates coincide with the single-particle eigenstates and are localized in the localized phase.

Discussion.—

We identified a mechanism for ferromagnetism in narrow bands of Anderson-localized states, governed by the geometry of single-particle eigenstate overlaps. In particular, we showed that the critical interaction strength required to stabilize a fully polarized ferromagnet can be reduced well below the gap to higher-energy bands by increasing the wavefunction detuning between neighboring localized eigenstates, and thus suppressing higher-order processes where the “particle” and “hole” that make up a magnon get far away from one another.
The wavefunction detuning here introduced is distinct from the more conventional quantum geometry, which corresponds to Kohn’s localization tensor in the absence of translational invariance
50
. While quantum geometry characterizes the spatial extent of single-particle states, the wavefunction detuning here introduced probes the relative geometry of pairs of localized eigenstates. In particular, it measures the component of the real-space overlap profile that remains effective in the ferromagnetic exchange. In this sense, it captures a real-space geometric property of eigenstate overlaps that is particularly natural in Anderson localized phases.

The framework introduced here for studying magnetism in localized bands has wide applications beyond the current setting. We explicitly derived the effective model for magnons for quasiperiodic systems, but our derivation generalizes directly to disordered impurity bands. It would be interesting to explore magnon spectra (as well as the localization of finite-frequency magnons) in such bands more generally.
More broadly, our work opens an important direction for the study of magnetism in correlated systems without translation symmetry, where the interplay between single-particle localization, multifractality, and interactions can generate
mechanisms not available in periodic Bloch bands. We expect this landscape to be further enriched in systems with nontrivial topology, where the real-space geometric effects identified here may coexist with, or be modified by, Berry curvature, quantum metric, and topological obstructions.

Appendix II
Charge excitations and locator expansion

In this section, we provide details on the charge +1 excitations. We start by deriving the effective one-body Hamiltonian for these excitations in Sec.
II.1
. We then derive a locator expansion in Sec.
II.2
around
V=\infty
to obtain analytical expressions for the charge +1 excitation spectrum, that we show in detail in Sec.
II.3
.
For the locator expansion, we will focus on the one-dimensional Aubry-André model with
t_{2}=t_{3}=0
for simplicity, but it can be straightforwardly generalized to the remaining models studied in the main text.

II.1
Effective charge +1 Hamiltonian

Charge +1 excitations above the fully polarized state
\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle=\prod_{\alpha}\gamma_{\uparrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}\left|0\right\rangle
are spanned by the basis elements
\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle
. To derive the effective one-body Hamiltonian, we evaluate separately the projected non-interacting and interacting contributions. Writing

\bar{H}=\bar{H}_{0}+\bar{H}_{U}

(S2.12)

with

\bar{H}_{0}=\sum_{m,\eta}\epsilon_{m}\gamma_{\eta,m}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\eta,m},\qquad\bar{H}_{U}=U\sum_{mm^{\prime}n^{\prime}n}V_{\uparrow\downarrow,mm^{\prime}n^{\prime}n}\gamma_{\uparrow,m}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\downarrow,m^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\downarrow,n^{\prime}}\gamma_{\uparrow,n},

(S2.13)

we will evaluate the single-particle excitation matrix defined in the main text, that we reproduce here for clarity,

R_{\alpha\beta}^{(+)}=\left\langle\Uparrow\right|\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}\bar{H}\gamma_{\downarrow,\beta}^{\dagger}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle-E_{\Uparrow}\delta_{\alpha\beta}.

(S2.14)

For the non-interacting part, since
\bar{H}_{0}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle=E_{\Uparrow}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle
and
[\bar{H}_{0},\gamma_{\downarrow,\beta}^{\dagger}]=\epsilon_{\beta}\gamma_{\downarrow,\beta}^{\dagger}
, we obtain

\left\langle\Uparrow\right|\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}\bar{H}_{0}\gamma_{\downarrow,\beta}^{\dagger}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle=(E_{\Uparrow}+\epsilon_{\beta})\delta_{\alpha\beta}.

(S2.15)

For the interaction term,
\bar{H}_{U}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle=0
, so we have:

\left\langle\Uparrow\right|\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}\bar{H}_{U}\gamma_{\downarrow,\beta}^{\dagger}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle=\left\langle\Uparrow\right|\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}[\bar{H}_{U},\gamma_{\downarrow,\beta}^{\dagger}]\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle.

(S2.16)

Further using
\{\gamma_{\downarrow,n^{\prime}},\gamma_{\downarrow,\beta}^{\dagger}\}=\delta_{n^{\prime}\beta}
and
\{\gamma_{\uparrow,n},\gamma_{\downarrow,\beta}^{\dagger}\}=0
, we have

[\gamma_{\uparrow,m}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\downarrow,m^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\downarrow,n^{\prime}}\gamma_{\uparrow,n},\gamma_{\downarrow,\beta}^{\dagger}]=-\delta_{\beta n^{\prime}}\gamma_{\uparrow,m}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\downarrow,m^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\uparrow,n}.

(S2.17)

Acting with
\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}
from the left and then taking the expectation value in
\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle
gives

\left\langle\Uparrow\right|\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}[\gamma_{\uparrow,m}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\downarrow,m^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\downarrow,n^{\prime}}\gamma_{\uparrow,n},\gamma_{\downarrow,\beta}^{\dagger}]\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle=\delta_{\alpha m^{\prime}}\delta_{\beta n^{\prime}}\left\langle\Uparrow\right|\gamma_{\uparrow,m}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\uparrow,n}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle=\delta_{\alpha m^{\prime}}\delta_{\beta n^{\prime}}\delta_{mn}.

(S2.18)

which yields

\left\langle\Uparrow\right|\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}\bar{H}_{U}\gamma_{\downarrow,\beta}^{\dagger}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle=U\sum_{m}V_{\uparrow\downarrow,m\alpha\beta m}.

(S2.19)

As discussed in the main text, the projected many-body problem closes exactly in this sector and reduces to the one-body matrix

R_{\alpha\beta}^{(+)}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{\alpha}+R^{U}_{\alpha\beta},\qquad R^{U}_{\alpha\beta}=U\bar{n}_{\uparrow}\cdot\rho_{\alpha,\beta}^{\downarrow},

(S2.20)

where

[\bar{n}_{\eta}]_{\bf r}=\sum_{\lambda}|\psi_{\bf r}^{\eta,\lambda}|^{2}

(S2.21)

is the spin-
\eta
electron density, and
[\rho_{\alpha\beta}^{\eta}]_{\bf r}=(\psi^{\eta,\alpha}_{\bf r})^{*}\psi^{\eta,\beta}_{\bf r}
. Additionally, recall that the dot product
a\cdot b
is defined as
\sum\nolimits_{\mathbf{r}}a_{\mathbf{r}}b_{\mathbf{r}}
. The eigenvalues of
R_{\alpha\beta}^{(+)}
give the exact charge +1 spectrum in the band-projected theory.

Deep in the localized phase, each narrow-band eigenstate is peaked on a different moiré cell. Therefore
(\psi_{\bf r}^{\alpha})^{*}\psi_{\bf r}^{\beta}
is exponentially small for
\alpha\neq\beta
(we drop the spin indices for simplicity), and the matrix
R_{\alpha\beta}^{(+)}
becomes approximately diagonal. Using the same argument, the dominant contribution to
\bar{n}_{\uparrow}
also comes from orbital
\alpha
, which gives

R^{U}_{\alpha\beta}\approx U\sum_{j}|\psi_{j}^{\alpha}|^{4}\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}=U\,\mathrm{IPR}(\psi^{\alpha})\,\delta_{\alpha\beta},

(S2.22)

and hence

\epsilon^{(+)}_{\alpha}\approx\epsilon_{\alpha}+U\,\mathrm{IPR}(\psi^{\alpha}),

(S2.23)

which is the diagonal approximation quoted in Eq. (
5
) of the main text. To obtain explicit analytical expressions, we must therefore derive the single-particle energies and the inverse participation ratios in the localized regime. To do so, we will employ the locator expansion in the next section.

II.2
Locator expansion around the atomic limit

We now perform a locator expansion
2
;
18
around the limit
V=\infty
(or equivalently
t=0
) for the one-dimensional Aubry-André model. We will be interested in inspecting the localization properties of the single-particle eigenstates, and in particular in obtaining an analytical expression for their IPR, that we can replace in Eq.
S2.23
to analytically compute the charge +1 excitation energies. We rewrite the spinless Hamiltonian for the one-dimensional Aubry-André model in the single-particle basis
\left|j\right\rangle=c^{\dagger}_{j}\left|0\right\rangle
, where
c^{\dagger}_{j}
creates an electron at site
j
, as

H_{0}=t\sum_{j}(\left|j\right\rangle\left\langle j+1\right|+\left|j+1\right\rangle\left\langle j\right|)+V\sum_{j}v_{j}\left|j\right\rangle\left\langle j\right|,\qquad v_{j}=\cos(2\pi\tau j+\phi).

(S2.24)

For the derivation it is convenient to work with open boundary conditions. In the atomic limit, the highest-energy states of the
narrow band originate from resonant neighboring sites, so we isolate a two-site cluster
C=\{j,j+1\}
and partition the chain into sites
L=\{1,\ldots,j-1\}
,
C
, and
R=\{j+2,\ldots,N\}
. In this basis,

E-H_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}E-H_{LL}&b_{L}&0&0\\
b_{L}^{\dagger}&E-Vv_{j}&-t&0\\
0&-t&E-Vv_{j+1}&b_{R}^{\dagger}\\
0&0&b_{R}&E-H_{RR}\end{array}\right),

(S2.25)

where
b_{L}=-t\left|j-1\right\rangle
and
b_{R}=-t\left|j+2\right\rangle
. Equivalently,

E-H_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}E-H_{LR}&u\\
u^{\dagger}&E-H_{C}\end{array}\right),

(S2.26)

with

H_{LR}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}H_{L}&0\\
0&H_{R}\end{array}\right),\qquad H_{C}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}Vv_{j}&t\\
t&Vv_{j+1}\end{array}\right),\qquad u=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}b_{L}\\
b_{R}\end{array}\right).

(S2.27)

The cluster Green’s function is therefore

G_{CC}(E)=\bigl(E-H_{C}-\Sigma(E)\bigr)^{-1},\qquad\Sigma(E)=u^{\dagger}(E-H_{LR})^{-1}u,

(S2.28)

where the self-energy takes the diagonal form

\Sigma(E)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}t^{2}g_{j-1}^{L}(E)&0\\
0&t^{2}g_{j+2}^{R}(E)\end{array}\right).

(S2.29)

Here

g_{j}^{R}(E)=\frac{1}{E-Vv_{j}-t^{2}g_{j+1}^{R}(E)},\qquad g_{N+1}^{R}=0,

(S2.30)

g_{j}^{L}(E)=\frac{1}{E-Vv_{j}-t^{2}g_{j-1}^{L}(E)},\qquad g_{0}^{L}=0,

(S2.31)

which are the standard left/right locator recursions.

The poles of
G_{CC}(E)
determine the renormalized energies. Ignoring the self-energy as a first step, one obtains for the poles

E_{\pm}^{0,j}=V\left(\frac{v_{j}+v_{j+1}}{2}\pm\Omega_{j}\right),\qquad\Omega_{j}=\sqrt{\left(\frac{\Delta_{1}^{(j)}}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{t}{V}\right)^{2}},

(S2.32)

where
\Delta_{i}^{(j)}=v_{j}-v_{j+i}
. Treating the self-energy perturbatively then yields

E_{\pm}^{j}=V\epsilon_{\pm}^{0,j}+\frac{t^{2}}{V}\,\delta\epsilon_{\pm}^{j}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{t^{4}}{V^{3}}\right),

(S2.33)

with
\epsilon_{\pm}^{0,j}=E_{\pm}^{0,j}/V
and

\delta\epsilon_{\pm}^{j}=\frac{1}{2(\epsilon_{\pm}^{0,j}-v_{j-1})}\left(1\pm\frac{\Delta_{1}^{(j)}}{2\Omega_{j}}\right)+\frac{1}{2(\epsilon_{\pm}^{0,j}-v_{j+2})}\left(1\mp\frac{\Delta_{1}^{(j)}}{2\Omega_{j}}\right).

(S2.34)

The lowest narrow band corresponds to the lower branch
E_{-}^{j}
. In the localized regime the ordering of these orbitals is inherited from the atomic limit, and the orbital at filling
\nu=\alpha/N_{B}
, with
\alpha=0,\cdots,N_{B}-1
the narrow-band eigenstate index, can be associated with a position
j_{\nu}
satisfying

2\pi\tau j_{\nu}=\pi+\frac{\nu\pi}{\ell}+2\pi n,\qquad\ell=(1-\tau)^{-1},

(S2.35)

where
\ell
is the moiré length. Explicitly, this yields

\epsilon_{\alpha}\approx E_{-}^{j_{\nu}},\qquad\nu=\alpha/N_{B}.

(S2.36)

To compute the interacting contribution, we also need the eigenfunctions. For a tridiagonal chain, the ratio of Green’s functions can be written directly in terms of the locator functions. For
r\geq 1
,

\frac{G_{j+r,j}(E)}{G_{j,j}(E)}=t^{r}\prod_{s=1}^{r}g_{j+s}^{R}(E),\qquad\frac{G_{j-r,j}(E)}{G_{j,j}(E)}=t^{r}\prod_{s=1}^{r}g_{j-s}^{L}(E).

(S2.37)

Using the spectral representation

G_{ij}(E)=\sum_{\alpha}\frac{\psi_{i}^{\alpha}(\psi_{j}^{\alpha})^{*}}{E-\epsilon_{\alpha}},

(S2.38)

one finds, at a simple pole
E=\epsilon_{\alpha}
,

\frac{\psi_{j+r}^{\alpha}}{\psi_{j}^{\alpha}}=t^{r}\prod_{s=1}^{r}g_{j+s}^{R}(\epsilon_{\alpha}),\qquad\frac{\psi_{j-r}^{\alpha}}{\psi_{j}^{\alpha}}=t^{r}\prod_{s=1}^{r}g_{j-s}^{L}(\epsilon_{\alpha}).

(S2.39)

Evaluating these expressions at
\epsilon_{\alpha}\approx E_{-}^{j_{\nu}}
generates the full locator expansion for the orbital
\psi^{\alpha}\equiv\psi^{j_{\nu}}
. In practice, the perturbed eigenfunction can be reconstructed recursively from a small set of component ratios. Defining

\Delta_{\pm,i}^{(j)}\equiv\epsilon_{\pm}^{0,j}-v_{j+i},

(S2.40)

we first use the cluster eigenvalue equation at
E=E_{\pm}^{j}
,

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}E_{\pm}^{j}-Vv_{j}-t^{2}g_{j-1}^{L}(E_{\pm}^{j})&-t\\
-t&E_{\pm}^{j}-Vv_{j+1}-t^{2}g_{j+2}^{R}(E_{\pm}^{j})\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{j}^{\alpha}\\
\psi_{j+1}^{\alpha}\end{array}\right)=0,

(S2.41)

which gives

\begin{array}[]{c}\dfrac{\psi_{j+1}^{\alpha}}{\psi_{j}^{\alpha}}=\dfrac{t}{E_{\pm}^{j}-Vv_{j+1}-t^{2}g_{j+2}^{R}(E_{\pm}^{j})}\\
=\dfrac{t}{V\Delta_{\pm,1}^{(j)}}\left[1+\dfrac{t^{2}}{V^{2}\Delta_{\pm,1}^{(j)}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\Delta_{\pm,2}^{(j)}}-\delta\epsilon_{\pm}^{j}\right)\right]+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\dfrac{t^{5}}{V^{5}}\right).\end{array}

(S2.42)

Similarly, using Eq. (
S2.39
) together with the recursion for
g_{j+2}^{R}(E)
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{c}\dfrac{\psi_{j+2}^{\alpha}}{\psi_{j+1}^{\alpha}}=\lim_{E\to E_{\pm}^{j}}t\,g_{j+2}^{R}(E)\\
=\dfrac{t}{V\Delta_{\pm,2}^{(j)}}\left[1+\dfrac{t^{2}}{V^{2}\Delta_{\pm,2}^{(j)}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\Delta_{\pm,3}^{(j)}}-\delta\epsilon_{\pm}^{j}\right)\right]+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\dfrac{t^{5}}{V^{5}}\right).\end{array}

(S2.43)

Repeated use of Eq. (
S2.39
) then yields the further right-tail amplitudes

\frac{\psi_{j+3}^{\alpha}}{\psi_{j+1}^{\alpha}}=\frac{t^{2}}{V^{2}\Delta_{\pm,2}^{(j)}\Delta_{\pm,3}^{(j)}}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{t^{4}}{V^{4}}\right),\qquad\frac{\psi_{j+4}^{\alpha}}{\psi_{j+1}^{\alpha}}=\frac{t^{3}}{V^{3}\Delta_{\pm,2}^{(j)}\Delta_{\pm,3}^{(j)}\Delta_{\pm,4}^{(j)}}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{t^{5}}{V^{5}}\right).

(S2.44)

On the left side of the resonant cluster, one analogously finds

\begin{array}[]{c}\dfrac{\psi_{j-1}^{\alpha}}{\psi_{j}^{\alpha}}=\lim_{E\to E_{\pm}^{j}}t\,g_{j-1}^{L}(E)\\
=\dfrac{t}{V\Delta_{\pm,-1}^{(j)}}\left[1+\dfrac{t^{2}}{V^{2}\Delta_{\pm,-1}^{(j)}}\left(\dfrac{1}{\Delta_{\pm,-2}^{(j)}}-\delta\epsilon_{\pm}^{j}\right)\right]+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\dfrac{t^{5}}{V^{5}}\right),\end{array}

(S2.45)

with the further left-tail amplitudes

\frac{\psi_{j-2}^{\alpha}}{\psi_{j}^{\alpha}}=\frac{t^{2}}{V^{2}\Delta_{\pm,-1}^{(j)}\Delta_{\pm,-2}^{(j)}}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{t^{4}}{V^{4}}\right),\qquad\frac{\psi_{j-3}^{\alpha}}{\psi_{j}^{\alpha}}=\frac{t^{3}}{V^{3}\Delta_{\pm,-1}^{(j)}\Delta_{\pm,-2}^{(j)}\Delta_{\pm,-3}^{(j)}}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{t^{5}}{V^{5}}\right).

(S2.46)

In practice, we are interested in the lower branch
E_{-}^{j}
, but keeping the
\pm
notation at this stage makes the derivation transparent. Together with normalization, Eqs. (
S2.42
)–(
S2.46
) determine the full perturbed eigenfunction that we used for the analytical calculations shown in the main text, in Fig.
2
.

The resulting closed-form expression for the IPR,
\mathrm{IPR}(\psi^{\alpha})=\sum_{j}|\psi_{j}^{\alpha}|^{4}
, is lengthy, so below we will quote the asymptotic forms relevant for the lowest and highest-energy narrow-band states, respectively
\nu=0
and
\nu=1
.

Introducing
\lambda=t/V
, one finds near
\nu=0
,

\begin{array}[]{c}\mathrm{IPR}(\psi^{\nu\approx 0})\approx 1-\frac{\lambda^{2}\csc^{4}(\pi/\ell)\left(\ell^{2}-2\pi^{2}\nu^{2}+3\pi^{2}\nu^{2}\csc^{2}(\pi/\ell)\right)}{\ell^{2}}\\
\approx 1-\frac{\lambda^{2}\ell^{4}}{\pi^{4}}(1+3\nu^{2}),\qquad(\lambda\ll 1,\;\nu\approx 0,\;\ell\gg 1),\end{array}

(S2.47)

so the lowest-energy eigenstates depart smoothly from atomic states (with
\mathrm{IPR}\approx 1
).

For the highest-energy states of the narrow band, the exact local resonance
\Delta_{1}^{(j_{1})}=0
makes the expansion non-analytic at
\lambda=0
. The origin of this non-analyticity is the factor

\Omega_{j_{\nu}}=\sqrt{\left(\frac{\Delta_{1}^{(j_{\nu})}}{2}\right)^{2}+\lambda^{2}},

(S2.48)

which enters both the energies and the eigenvectors. Near
\nu=1
, the relevant small parameter is therefore not
\lambda
alone, but rather the ratio
(\nu-1)/\lambda
. In the regime
(\nu-1)/\lambda\ll 1
, one obtains

\begin{array}[]{c}\mathrm{IPR}(\psi^{\nu\approx 1})\approx\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}(\nu-1)^{2}}{2\ell^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\sin^{2}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{\ell}\right)-\frac{1}{8}\csc^{2}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{\ell}\right)\sec^{2}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{\ell}\right)\right]\\
-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{16}\csc^{4}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{\ell}\right)\sec^{2}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{\ell}\right),\qquad\big[(\nu-1)/\lambda\ll 1,\;\nu\approx 1\big],\end{array}

(S2.49)

The leading correction is the
\mathcal{O}(1/\lambda^{2})
term proportional to
(\nu-1)^{2}\sin^{2}(\pi/\ell)/\ell^{2}
, which quantifies how rapidly the IPR departs from the perfectly resonant value
1/2
as one moves away from the band edge. The locator expansion therefore explains the monotonic decrease of the IPR across the narrow band: for large
V
, the eigenstates at the bottom of the band are almost atomic, while the top of the band is formed by nearly perfect two-site resonances.

Appendix III
Spin-flip spectrum and effective magnon Hamiltonian

In the main text we showed that one-spin-flip excitations are described by a projected two-particle problem and that, in the localized regime, a narrow collective magnon branch sets the lowest-energy excitations. In this section we provide a compact derivation of the exact projected two-particle Hamiltonian, show in detail why the number of magnon bound modes equals the number of orbitals in the narrow band in the large-
V
limit, and then derive the effective low-energy Hamiltonian for the magnons.

III.1
Effective two-particle spin-flip Hamiltonian

One-spin-flip excitations above the fully polarized state are spanned by

\left|\alpha,\beta\right\rangle=S_{\alpha\beta}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle,\qquad S_{\alpha\beta}\equiv\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\uparrow,\beta},

(S3.52)

where
\alpha
labels the added spin-
\downarrow
particle and
\beta
the removed spin-
\uparrow
particle. The exact projected spin-flip problem therefore reduces to the two-particle matrix

\mathcal{H}^{\textrm{$\downarrow\uparrow$}}_{(\lambda\delta,\alpha\beta)}\equiv\left\langle\Uparrow\right|S_{\lambda\delta}^{\dagger}\bar{H}S_{\alpha\beta}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle-E_{\Uparrow}\delta_{\lambda\alpha}\delta_{\delta\beta}.

(S3.53)

The non-interacting contribution is immediate from the commutators

[\bar{H}_{0},\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}]=\epsilon_{\alpha}\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}^{\dagger},\qquad[\bar{H}_{0},\gamma_{\uparrow,\beta}]=-\epsilon_{\beta}\gamma_{\uparrow,\beta},

(S3.54)

together with
\bar{H}_{0}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle=E_{\Uparrow}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle
. Therefore

\bar{H}_{0}\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\uparrow,\beta}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle=(E_{\Uparrow}+\epsilon_{\alpha}-\epsilon_{\beta})\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\uparrow,\beta}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle,

(S3.55)

which yields

\left\langle\Uparrow\right|S_{\lambda\delta}^{\dagger}\bar{H}_{0}S_{\alpha\beta}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle-E_{\Uparrow}\delta_{\lambda\alpha}\delta_{\delta\beta}=(\epsilon_{\alpha}-\epsilon_{\beta})\delta_{\lambda\alpha}\delta_{\delta\beta}.

(S3.56)

For the interaction term, since
\bar{H}_{U}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle=0
, we can write

\left\langle\Uparrow\right|S_{\lambda\delta}^{\dagger}\bar{H}_{U}S_{\alpha\beta}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle=\left\langle\Uparrow\right|\gamma_{\uparrow,\delta}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\downarrow,\lambda}[\bar{H}_{U},\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\uparrow,\beta}]\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle.

(S3.57)

Expanding the commutator gives two contributions. The first one is the same matrix element already encountered in the charge +1 problem:

\left\langle\Uparrow\right|\gamma_{\uparrow,\delta}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\downarrow,\lambda}[\bar{H}_{U},\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}]\gamma_{\uparrow,\beta}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle=R^{U}_{\lambda\alpha}\delta_{\delta\beta}.

(S3.58)

The second one comes from the interaction acting on the removed spin-
\uparrow
electron and produces the direct particle-hole binding term,

\left\langle\Uparrow\right|\gamma_{\uparrow,\delta}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\downarrow,\lambda}\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}[\bar{H}_{U},\gamma_{\uparrow,\beta}]\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle=-U\,V_{\uparrow\downarrow,\beta\lambda\alpha\delta}.

(S3.59)

Combining both pieces with Eq. (
S3.56
), we obtain the exact projected two-particle Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}^{\textrm{$\downarrow\uparrow$}}_{(\lambda\delta,\alpha\beta)}=\left[(\epsilon_{\alpha}-\epsilon_{\beta})\delta_{\lambda\alpha}+R^{U}_{\lambda\alpha}\right]\delta_{\delta\beta}-U\,V_{\uparrow\downarrow,\beta\lambda\alpha\delta}.

(S3.60)

This completes the derivation of Eq. (
6
) in the main text. The two terms also have a transparent meaning. The first term provides the contribution from independent (unbound) particle-hole excitations, while the second term defines the particle-hole interaction/binding matrix responsible for the creation of magnon bound modes.

In Figs.
S7
and
S8
we show some examples demonstrating the robustness of the magnon spectrum obtained by diagonalizing the Hamiltonian in Eq.
S3.60
for different sizes.

III.2
Bound mode counting

A useful way of organizing Eq. (
S3.60
) is to separate the continuum matrix from the binding term. Writing

\mathcal{H}^{\downarrow\uparrow}_{(\lambda\delta,\alpha\beta)}=R_{(\lambda,\delta),(\alpha,\beta)}-U\sum_{j}\mathcal{V}_{(\lambda,\delta),j}\mathcal{V}^{\dagger}_{j,(\alpha,\beta)},

(S3.61)

with

\mathcal{V}_{(\lambda,\delta),j}=\psi_{j}^{\delta}\left(\psi_{j}^{\lambda}\right)^{*},\qquad R_{(\lambda,\delta),(\alpha,\beta)}=\left[(\epsilon_{\alpha}-\epsilon_{\beta})\delta_{\lambda\alpha}+R^{U}_{\lambda\alpha}\right]\delta_{\delta\beta},

(S3.62)

we can express the full spin-flip Hamiltonian as

\mathcal{H}^{\downarrow\uparrow}=R-U\mathcal{V}\mathcal{V}^{\dagger}.

(S3.63)

States inside the particle-hole continuum satisfy
\det(R-E)=0
, whereas the collective modes that are well isolated from the continuum are obtained from

\det\!\left(1-\mathcal{V}^{\dagger}\frac{U}{R-E}\mathcal{V}\right)=0.

(S3.64)

This reduces the bound-state problem from an
N_{B}^{2}\times N_{B}^{2}
matrix to an
N\times N
one, with
N=L^{d}
, implying that there are at most
N
bound eigenmodes. However, as discussed in the main text, we only see a single low energy magnon branch with
N_{B}
eigenmodes.
This counting becomes particularly transparent for large
V
, with
U\gg W
. In this limit, the denominator in Eq. (
S3.64
) is dominated by the interaction term and the eigenstates are very localized, so that effectively
R_{\lambda\alpha}\approx U\delta_{\lambda\alpha}
. Equation (
S3.64
) then gives

\mathcal{V}^{\dagger}\frac{U}{R-E}\mathcal{V}\approx\frac{U}{U-E}|\Pi|^{2},\qquad\Pi_{ij}=\sum_{\alpha}\psi_{i}^{\alpha}(\psi_{j}^{\alpha})^{*}.

(S3.65)

For large
V
,
\Pi
is approximately diagonal and only has support on the
N_{B}
sites where the narrow-band eigenstates are localized (within each moiré cell). Therefore
|\Pi|^{2}
has rank
N_{B}
up to exponentially suppressed corrections in
\ell/\xi
, where
\ell
is the moiré length and
\xi
is the localization length. Therefore there are exactly
N_{B}
collective bound modes. At strictly infinite
V
these modes have exactly zero energy and reduce to the local spin flips
\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\uparrow,\alpha}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle
. At finite but large
V
, they hybridize and acquire a finite bandwidth.

III.3
Effective magnon Hamiltonian

We now derive the effective Hamiltonian acting within the low-energy magnon manifold. As discussed in the main text, in the localized regime and for
U>U^{*}
the relevant low-energy subspace is spanned by the local spin-flip states
\left|\alpha,\alpha\right\rangle
. We therefore introduce the projectors

P=\sum_{\alpha}\left|\alpha,\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha,\alpha\right|,\qquad Q=1-P,

(S3.66)

where
Q
projects onto the non-local particle-hole configurations
\left|\lambda,\delta\right\rangle
with
\lambda\neq\delta
. Integrating out this high-energy sector gives the exact Feshbach-Schur complement

H_{\mathrm{eff}}(E)=H_{PP}-H_{PQ}(H_{QQ}-E)^{-1}H_{QP}.

(S3.67)

We are interested in the magnon branch that evolves continuously from the zero-energy manifold at
V=\infty
, so throughout this subsection we set
E=0
in Eq. (
S3.67
).

We will follow the notation of the main text, using the definitions

[\rho_{\alpha\beta}]_{\mathbf{r}}=(\psi^{\alpha}_{\mathbf{r}})^{*}\psi^{\beta}_{\mathbf{r}},\qquad\Delta_{\alpha\beta}=\epsilon_{\alpha}-\epsilon_{\beta}.

(S3.68)

Additionally, as in the main text, we assume for simplicity that the Hamiltonian is
SU(2)
-symmetric and therefore suppress the spin labels in the single-particle eigenfunctions. In what follows, we will also use that the Hamiltonians studied in this work are real so the eigenfunctions can be chosen real (but of course the derivation can be straightforwardly generalized).
In the localized phase, every inter-orbital product between different moiré cells brings a factor of order
e^{-\ell/\xi}
, where once again
\ell
is the moiré length and
\xi
is the localization length. Because of this, we can greatly simplify the effective Hamiltonian by keeping only the terms to lower-order in powers of
e^{-\ell/\xi}
, as we will show below.

Before evaluating individual matrix elements, let us note that
SU(2)
symmetry fixes the diagonal entries of
H_{\mathrm{eff}}
once the off-diagonal ones are known. The uniform local spin flip mode

\left|\psi_{0}\right\rangle=\sum_{\alpha}\left|\alpha,\alpha\right\rangle=\left(\sum_{\alpha}\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\uparrow,\alpha}\right)\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle

(S3.69)

is obtained by acting with the total spin-lowering operator on
\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle
, and therefore must remain a zero mode of the effective Hamiltonian. One can check directly from Eq. (
S3.60
) that

\sum_{\beta}(H_{PP})_{\alpha\beta}=0,

(S3.70)

and similarly

\sum_{\beta}(H_{QP})_{(\lambda\delta),(\beta\beta)}=R^{U}_{\lambda\delta}-U\sum_{\mathbf{r}}\sum_{\beta}|\psi^{\beta}_{\mathbf{r}}|^{2}(\psi^{\lambda}_{\mathbf{r}})^{*}\psi^{\delta}_{\mathbf{r}}=0,

(S3.71)

which implies
\sum_{\beta}\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}=0
and therefore

\sum_{\beta}(H_{\mathrm{eff}})_{\alpha\beta}=0.

(S3.72)

Hence

(H_{\mathrm{eff}})_{\alpha\alpha}=-\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}(H_{\mathrm{eff}})_{\alpha\beta},

(S3.73)

so it is sufficient to compute the off-diagonal matrix elements explicitly and the diagonal ones then follow automatically.

We start from the direct projection of Eq. (
S3.60
) into the
P
subspace. For matrix elements between two local spin flips we obtain

\displaystyle(H_{PP})_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\alpha,\alpha\right|\mathcal{H}^{\downarrow\uparrow}\left|\beta,\beta\right\rangle

(S3.74)

\displaystyle=R^{U}_{\alpha\alpha}\delta_{\alpha\beta}-U\,V_{\uparrow\downarrow,\beta\alpha\beta\alpha}

\displaystyle=U\sum_{\lambda\neq\alpha}\|\rho_{\alpha\lambda}\|^{2}\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}-U\,\|\rho_{\alpha\beta}\|^{2}(1-\delta_{\alpha\beta}).

In particular, for
\alpha\neq\beta
the direct coupling is simply

(H_{PP})_{\alpha\beta}=-U\|\rho_{\alpha\beta}\|^{2}\,\,\,(\alpha\neq\beta).

(S3.75)

This is the first, negative semi-definite term appearing in Eq. (
8
) in the main text.

We next evaluate the virtual correction

\Sigma\equiv H_{PQ}H_{QQ}^{-1}H_{QP},\qquad\textrm{with }\,(H_{\mathrm{eff}})_{\alpha\beta}=(H_{PP})_{\alpha\beta}-\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}.

(S3.76)

Given a state
\left|\alpha,\alpha\right\rangle
,
H_{PQ}
will couple it to states
\left|\alpha,\beta\right\rangle
and
\left|\beta,\alpha\right\rangle
with
\beta\neq\alpha
. In general, it can also couple to states
\left|\gamma,\beta\right\rangle
, with
\gamma\neq\beta\neq\alpha
, but these will involve overlaps of eigenstates living in three different moiré cells (instead of two), which will be higher-order in powers of
e^{-\ell/\xi}
. Additionally, we can restrict
\alpha
and
\beta
to orbitals at
nearest-neighbor moiré cells
, since more distant orbitals involve higher powers of
e^{-\ell/\xi}
. In what follows, we will employ these approximations and keep track of the error in powers of
e^{-\ell/\xi}
.

Using Eq. (
S3.60
), we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\alpha,\alpha\right|H_{PQ}\left|\alpha,\beta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\alpha,\alpha\right|\mathcal{H}^{\downarrow\uparrow}\left|\alpha,\beta\right\rangle

(S3.77)

\displaystyle=\left[(\epsilon_{\alpha}-\epsilon_{\beta})\delta_{\alpha\alpha}+R^{U}_{\alpha\alpha}\right]\delta_{\alpha\beta}-U\,V_{\uparrow\downarrow,\beta\alpha\alpha\alpha}

\displaystyle=-U\sum_{\mathbf{r}}(\psi^{\beta}_{\mathbf{r}})^{*}\psi^{\alpha}_{\mathbf{r}}|\psi^{\alpha}_{\mathbf{r}}|^{2}\,\,(\alpha\neq\beta)

\displaystyle=-U\,\rho_{\alpha\alpha}\cdot\rho_{\alpha\beta}\,\,(\alpha\neq\beta).

This relation is exact for
\alpha\neq\beta
: there is no additional localized-expansion approximation for this term.
This is no longer the case for the second coupling, for which we find

\left\langle\alpha,\alpha\right|H_{PQ}\left|\beta,\alpha\right\rangle=U\sum_{\lambda\neq\alpha}V_{\uparrow\downarrow,\lambda\alpha\beta\lambda}=U\,\rho_{\beta\beta}\cdot\rho_{\alpha\beta}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\ell/\xi}),

(S3.78)

Here, the leading term comes from
\lambda=\beta
, since every term with
\lambda\neq\beta
contains at least one additional inter-moiré overlap and is therefore suppressed by an extra factor
e^{-\ell/\xi}
. This asymmetry between
\left|\alpha,\beta\right\rangle
and
\left|\beta,\alpha\right\rangle
can be understood by recalling that the first index in the basis represents an electron with spin-down and the second one represents a hole with spin-up. Hermiticity then gives the leading-order
H_{QP}
matrix elements,

\left\langle\alpha,\beta\right|H_{QP}\left|\beta,\beta\right\rangle=U\,\rho_{\alpha\alpha}\cdot\rho_{\alpha\beta}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\ell/\xi}),

(S3.79)

\left\langle\beta,\alpha\right|H_{QP}\left|\beta,\beta\right\rangle=-U\,\rho_{\beta\beta}\cdot\rho_{\alpha\beta}.

(S3.80)

The first of these inherits the same localized-expansion approximation as Eq. (
S3.78
) with
\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta
, whereas the second one is exact for the same reason as Eq. (
S3.77
).

For the same two intermediate states, the relevant diagonal
Q
-space matrix elements follow from the same exact Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle\left\langle\alpha,\beta\right|H_{QQ}\left|\alpha,\beta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\alpha\beta}+R^{U}_{\alpha\alpha}-U\,V_{\uparrow\downarrow,\beta\alpha\alpha\beta}

(S3.81)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\alpha\beta}+U\sum_{\lambda\neq\beta}\|\rho_{\alpha\lambda}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\alpha\beta}+U\|\rho_{\alpha\alpha}\|^{2}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-\ell/\xi}),

\displaystyle\left\langle\beta,\alpha\right|H_{QQ}\left|\beta,\alpha\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\beta\alpha}+R^{U}_{\beta\beta}-U\,V_{\uparrow\downarrow,\alpha\beta\beta\alpha}

(S3.82)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\beta\alpha}+U\sum_{\lambda\neq\alpha}\|\rho_{\beta\lambda}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\beta\alpha}+U\|\rho_{\beta\beta}\|^{2}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-\ell/\xi}).

In each case the explicit interaction term cancels the corresponding
\lambda=\beta
or
\lambda=\alpha
contribution inside
R^{U}
, leaving a sum whose dominant piece is the local
\lambda=\alpha
or
\lambda=\beta
term. The off-diagonal element inside this reduced two-state
Q
block is

\left\langle\alpha,\beta\right|H_{QQ}\left|\beta,\alpha\right\rangle=-U\,V_{\uparrow\downarrow,\alpha\alpha\beta\beta}=\mathcal{O}(e^{-\ell/\xi}),

(S3.83)

and couplings from these states to any other
Q
configuration contain at least one additional inter-orbital overlap. Consequently, replacing
H_{QQ}
by the diagonal contributions above includes all contributions up to order
\mathcal{O}(e^{-\ell/\xi})
.

Substituting all the leading matrix elements into Eq. (
S3.76
), we obtain for
\alpha\neq\beta

\Sigma_{\alpha\beta}=-U\left[\frac{(\rho_{\alpha\alpha}\cdot\rho_{\alpha\beta})^{2}}{\Delta_{\alpha\beta}/U+\|\rho_{\alpha\alpha}\|^{2}}+\frac{(\rho_{\beta\beta}\cdot\rho_{\alpha\beta})^{2}}{\Delta_{\beta\alpha}/U+\|\rho_{\beta\beta}\|^{2}}\right]+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\ell/\xi}).

(S3.84)

Combining Eqs. (
S3.75
) and (
S3.84
), the off-diagonal matrix elements of the effective Hamiltonian become

(H_{\mathrm{eff}})_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{J_{\alpha\beta}}{2},\qquad\alpha\neq\beta,

(S3.85)

where we defined the effective exchange couplings
J_{\alpha\beta}
as

J_{\alpha\beta}\approx-2U\|\rho_{\alpha\beta}\|^{2}\Bigg[1-\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{\alpha\beta}}{1+\frac{\Delta_{\alpha\beta}}{U\|\rho_{\alpha\alpha}\|^{2}}}-\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{\beta\alpha}}{1+\frac{\Delta_{\beta\alpha}}{U\|\rho_{\beta\beta}\|^{2}}}\Bigg]+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2\ell/\xi}).

(S3.86)

The first term inside the brackets comes from the direct projection into the local-spin-flip manifold, while the second and third terms come from virtual excursions into the non-local particle-hole sector. To see explicitly why these off-diagonal matrix elements are naturally interpreted as exchange couplings, we define the spin operators

\mathbf{S}_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{\uparrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}&\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}\bm{\sigma}\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{\uparrow,\alpha}\\
\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}\end{pmatrix}\qquad S_{\alpha}^{+}=\gamma_{\uparrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha},\qquad S_{\alpha}^{-}=\gamma_{\downarrow,\alpha}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\uparrow,\alpha},

(S3.87)

so that
\left|\alpha,\alpha\right\rangle=S_{\alpha}^{-}\left|\Uparrow\right\rangle
.
H_{\textrm{eff}}
is then the projection of a
SU(2)
-symmetric Heisenberg Hamiltonian given by

H_{\mathrm{S}}=\sum_{\alpha<\beta}J_{\alpha\beta}\left(\mathbf{S}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{\beta}-\frac{1}{4}\right),

(S3.88)

in the one magnon subspace. In particular, its one-magnon matrix elements are

\left\langle\alpha,\alpha\right|H_{\mathrm{S}}\left|\beta,\beta\right\rangle=\frac{J_{\alpha\beta}}{2},\qquad\alpha\neq\beta,

(S3.89)

and

\left\langle\alpha,\alpha\right|H_{\mathrm{S}}\left|\alpha,\alpha\right\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}J_{\alpha\beta},

(S3.90)

in agreement with the exchange coupling identification in Eq.
S3.85
.

We finish this section by comparing the ground-state energy of the full two-particle Hamiltonian in Eq.
S3.60
and of the effective Hamiltonian in Eq.
S3.88
, in Fig.
S9
. We can see that the results become asymptotically exact as
V
increases, in both 1D and 2D.

Appendix IV
Robustness of narrow-band projection

In the main text, we focused on studying the stability of the fully-polarized ferromagnetic state against charge and spin-flip excitations in the limit where the Hamiltonian was projected into the narrow-band. In this section, we numerically verify the stability of these results. In Sec.
IV.1
we start by verifying the robustness of the critical interaction strength
U_{c}
against including higher-energy single-particle states (beyond the narrow-band) on our projected Hamiltonian in Eq.
2
. Then in Sec.
IV.2
, we will verify the robustness of the fully polarized ferromagnet beyond the simplest single magnon excitation. In particular, we will perform exact diagonalization for small system sizes in all the different possible spin sectors and provide numerical evidence for the global stability of the fully-polarized ferromagnet.

IV.1
Exact diagonalization results including higher-energy states

To test the robustness of the narrow-band projection, we enlarge the projected Hilbert space by retaining the lowest
N_{\mathrm{states}}
single-particle orbitals of the non-interacting problem, while keeping the total number of electrons fixed to the half-filled narrow-band value
N_{e}=N_{B}
. In this way, the fully polarized reference state still has
S_{z}^{\mathrm{max}}=N_{B}/2
, but electrons are allowed to visit higher-energy orbitals above the narrow band. We then compute using exact diagonalization the lowest many-body energy in the sector
S_{z}=S_{z}^{\mathrm{max}}-1
and compare it with the energy
E_{\Uparrow}
of the fully polarized state.

The results are shown in Fig.
S10
. The critical interaction
U_{c}
corresponds to the interaction above which we have
E(S_{z}^{\mathrm{max}}-1)-E_{\Uparrow}=0
, i.e., the ground-state in the
S_{z}=S_{z}^{\mathrm{max}}-1
spin sector becomes the
S=S_{z}^{\mathrm{max}}
fully-polarized state. Panel Fig.
S10
(a) illustrates the bipartite 1D point
t_{2}=0
, where it can be seen that
U_{c}
increases strongly as higher-energy states are added to the projection. This is consistent with the discussion in the main text and the illustration in Fig.
1
(c): near the bipartite limit, mixing with remote states significantly disfavors a fully polarized ground state. By contrast, panels Fig.
S10
(b,c) show non-bipartite 1D and 2D examples for parameters where
U_{c}
is significantly smaller than the gap to higher-energy states,
\Delta_{G}
(corresponding to particular choices of
t_{2}
for the parameters studied in Fig.
4
). In this case, the results are essentially independent of
N_{\mathrm{states}}
, indicating that the small-
U_{c}
ferromagnetic transition found with the narrow-band projection is well converged. These results support the picture emphasized in the main text: once the wavefunction detuning induces a
U_{c}
well below the gap to remote states, the low-energy ferromagnetic physics becomes insensitive to band mixing.
