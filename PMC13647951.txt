The PTPN1/N2 Peptide
Inhibitor Achieves Potent, Long-Acting
Glycemic Control and Weight-Loss Dual Efficacy in Mice

Yiying Shi, Xianmin Yang, Fanxu Meng, Anton Feenstra, Jin Chen, Zongwen Gu, Xiang Gao, Wenqing Yao, Guozhen Dong, Liyan Gong, Han Wu, Chuanliang Zhang, Yuchao Gu

Abstract

Type 2 diabetes mellitus and obesity are closely linked
metabolic
disorders driven by insulin and leptin resistance. Current long-acting
therapies capable of addressing both conditions are dominated by incretin-based
agents, which primarily achieve efficacy through appetite suppression
and are frequently associated with tolerability limitations and metabolic
rebound after treatment discontinuation. Alternative strategies that
correct underlying signaling defects without suppressing food intake
remain lacking. Protein tyrosine phosphatases PTPN1 and PTPN2 act
as coordinated intracellular negative regulators of insulin and leptin
signaling by dephosphorylating key molecules within the IRS-AKT and
JAK2-STAT3 pathways. Although genetic studies have established these
phosphatases as attractive therapeutic targets, translation has been
hindered by the absence of long-acting inhibitors with robust in vivo efficacy. Here we report the rational design of Y5, a chemically engineered BimBH3-derived peptide analogue
that functions as a dual inhibitor of PTPN1 and PTPN2. Fatty-acid
conjugation and hapten modification confer high enzymatic potency,
enhanced proteolytic stability, strong albumin binding, and prolonged
half-life and systemic exposure. Y5 restores cellular
insulin and leptin signaling in vitro, leading to
coordinated activation of IRS-1, AKT, and STAT3 under resistant conditions.
In diabetic db/db mice, Y5 once-weekly administration
induces sustained glycemic control, improves glucose tolerance, and
reduces circulating insulin levels, consistent with enhanced insulin
sensitivity. In DIO mice, Y5 induces pronounced and sustained
weight loss while preserving food intake, demonstrating a potential
mechanism distinct from incretin-based therapies. Y5 selectively
reduces adiposity, improves hepatic steatosis, and is well tolerated
across dosing regimens. These findings establish long-acting dual
inhibition of PTPN1 and PTPN2 as a mechanistically distinct therapeutic
strategy for T2DM and obesity. By reversing hormone resistance rather
than suppressing appetite, Y5 defines an alternative
paradigm for achieving long-acting glycemic control and weight loss in vivo.

Introduction

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and obesity
are tightly interconnected
metabolic disorders whose global prevalence continues to rise despite
substantial advances in pharmacotherapy.
−

 Although lifestyle interventions,
such as increased physical activity, strict dietary regimens, and
existing pharmacotherapies, have shown limited long-term efficacy,
many patients struggle to manage T2DM and/or obesity. In recent years, incretin-based agents, particularly glucagon-like
peptide-1 receptor (GLP-1R) agonists and related multiagonists, have
emerged as the dominant clinical approach capable of delivering both
glycemic control and weight loss.
−

 However, these therapies achieve efficacy primarily through sustained
suppression of appetite, a mechanism intrinsically associated with
gastrointestinal adverse effects, diminished eating-related quality
of life, and clinically significant metabolic rebound following treatment
discontinuation.
−

 In addition, a meta-analysis revealed that discontinuation of GLP-1
receptor agonists could lead to consistent and clinically meaningful
rebound effects across multiple cardiometabolic parameters, including
body weight, waist circumference, glycemic control, and blood pressure. The near-exclusive reliance on appetite-driven
strategies highlights a fundamental limitation of current therapies
and underscores the need for alternative approaches capable of correcting
metabolic dysfunction independently of caloric intake reduction. Consequently,
there emerges a new and unmet subdivided clinical need for new weight-loss
pharmacotherapies capable of delivering robust, clinically meaningful
weight loss without suffering from gastrointestinal adverse effects,
affecting appetite, or requiring dietary adjustments.

Insulin
and leptin resistance represent central pathophysiological
drivers linking hyperglycaemia and excess adiposity. At the intracellular
level, protein tyrosine phosphatases PTPN1 (also known as PTP1B) and
PTPN2 (also known as TC-PTP) function as coordinated negative regulators
of insulin and leptin signaling by dephosphorylating key components
of the insulin receptor, IRS-AKT and JAK2-STAT3 pathways, including direct negative regulation of insulin
and leptin signaling,
−

 modulation of hypothalamic energy homeostasis and glucose metabolism,
,
 as well as T-cell based immune responses.
−

 These physiological studies suggested
the potential for the inhibition of PTPN1/2 for the treatment of obesity
and T2DM via improving and/or reversing insulin and leptin resistance.
,,−

 And theoretically, it will not cause the aforementioned side effects
such as nausea, vomiting, and appetite loss.

Insulin resistance
is a common feature of type 2 diabetes and obesity.
PTPN1 is a negative regulator of both insulin and leptin signaling
to dephosphorylate the activated insulin receptor (IR) and/or insulin
receptor substrates (IRS)
−

 and was generally regarded as a promising therapeutic
target for metabolic diseases such as diabetes and obesity.
,
 PTPN2 also could contribute to the regulation of insulin receptor
signaling by catalyzing insulin receptor dephosphorylation to limit
the duration of insulin signaling. PTPN1/2 functioned in a coordinated
manner to achieve an overall regulation of phosphorylation and signaling
of insulin receptor. Moreover, PTPN1/2
exert their inhibitory effects on leptin signaling by inducing the
dephosphorylation of JAK2 and STAT3, respectively.

Despite compelling biological
validation, translation of PTPN1/N2
inhibition into effective therapies has remained elusive. Small-molecule
inhibitors targeting the highly conserved and polar catalytic pockets
of phosphatases have demonstrated limited cellular efficacy, poor
selectivity, and unfavorable pharmacokinetic properties, precluding
potent target engagement in vivo.
,
 Consequently, no PTPN1/PTPN2-directed agents have previously achieved
potent long-acting glycemic control or meaningful weight loss in animal
models of metabolic disease. Thus, the effective modulation of intracellular
phosphatases may require a fundamentally different molecular strategy
capable of achieving prolonged, tissue-relevant inhibition rather
than transient enzymatic blockade.
,

Here
we report a long-acting peptide-based strategy that overcomes
these translational barriers by enabling dual inhibition of PTPN1
and PTPN2 with potent long-acting glycemic control and weight loss
dual efficacy in mice. By rationally engineering a BimBH3-derived
peptide through fatty-acid conjugation and hapten modification, we
generated Y5 (a), a dual PTPN1/PTPN2 inhibitor with exceptional proteolytic
stability, prolonged systemic exposure, and favorable tissue distribution.
We demonstrate that Y5 restores insulin and leptin signaling,
achieves potent long-acting glycemic control, and induces pronounced
weight loss indicated by unchanged food intake in multiple mouse models
of metabolic disease. These findings establish dual PTPN1/PTPN2 inhibition
as a mechanistically distinct and therapeutically viable alternative
to incretin-based approaches.

Results

Design and Synthesis of Dual-Targeting Long-Acting Peptide Inhibitors

Given the coordinated roles of PTPN1 and PTPN2 in attenuating insulin
and leptin signaling, we reasoned that durable correction of metabolic
dysfunction would require simultaneous inhibition of both phosphatases.
To address this challenge, we explored a peptide-based strategy capable
of engaging the conserved phosphatase active sites while enabling
prolonged systemic exposure. We selected the BH3 domain of the pro-apoptotic
protein Bim as a starting scaffold based on its established ability
to engage protein–protein interaction interfaces and its previously
reported activity against PTPN1. Importantly, we subsequently found
that this scaffold, after appropriate chemical modification, also
displays inhibitory potential against PTPN2, thereby establishing
the feasibility of designing dual PTPN1/PTPN2 inhibitors.

To
achieve long-acting dual PTPN1/PTPN2 inhibition, we systematically
engineered the BimBH3 core sequence through two complementary modifications:
(i) fatty acid conjugation to promote high-affinity serum albumin
binding, a well-established strategy for extending the plasma half-life
of peptide therapeutics; and (ii) hapten (DNP) modification to enhance
molecular stability and systemic persistence. Notably, we further
leveraged an antibody-recruitment strategy. Endogenous antibodies
against small-molecule haptens naturally exist in human serum, constituting
1–8% of total antibodies. By conjugating the DNP hapten to
the peptide, these peptide analogues can bind to these pre-existing
antibodies, forming immune complexes that benefit from FcRn-mediated
recycling via the antibody Fc fragment, thereby potentially contributing
to prolonged half-life via a mechanism that warrants future investigation.
This design not only overcomes the pharmacokinetic and intracellular
target-engagement limitations that have constrained prior phosphatase-directed
approaches, but also achieves a dual half-life extension mechanism
(albumin binding plus antibody recruitment) while reducing immunogenicity
risks by utilizing the natural antibody repertoire.

Based on
this rationale, we designed and synthesized series of
analogues (Y1–Y8) by varying two structural parameters: the
N-terminal fatty acid type (C16 acid vs C18 diacid) and the length
of the DNP hapten linker attached to the Lys2 side chain
(Lys, Dap, Dab, Orn). These modifications were systematically investigated
for their effects on PTPN1/PTPN2 inhibitory activity, metabolic stability,
and long-acting biological performance.

The designed BimBH3
analogues () were synthesized via Fmoc-based solid-phase
peptide synthesis (SPPS) using 2-chlorotrityl chloride (2-Cl-CTC)
resin, followed by standard cleavage protocols to yield the target
peptides ().
Postsynthesis, crude products underwent purification through preparative
reversed-phase HPLC equipped with C18 columns. Final compound integrity
was verified through dual analytical characterization: molecular masses
were confirmed by electrospray ionization mass spectrometry (ESI-MS),
while purity levels (>95% in all cases) were determined by analytical
HPLC. Full synthetic validation data, including chromatograms (Figures S1–S9) and mass spectral analyses
(Table S1), are provided in the Supporting Information.

Discovery of a Potent PTPN1/N2 Dual Inhibitor Y5 Derived from BimBH3 Peptide

Evaluation of PTPN1/N2 Inhibitory Activity

Given the
synergistic roles of PTPN1 and PTPN2 in insulin and leptin signaling
pathway dephosphorylation, we investigated the dual-target inhibitory
potential of the eight analogues. We assessed the inhibitory potency
of DNP conjugated fatty acid analogues against human recombinant PTPN1/N2
through a two-stage screening process. Initial compound evaluation
at 40 μM revealed significant inhibitors (>50% inhibition),
which subsequently underwent IC50 determination ().

The DNP-modified mimetics all demonstrated markedly
enhanced enzymatic
inhibition compared to the lead compound BimBH3-SM6.
All synthesized analogues showed improved PTPN1 inhibitory activity
compared to the lead compound. In the C16 acid series (Y1–Y4),
all compounds exhibited good PTPN1 inhibition (IC50 = 0.629–0.144
μM), but none showed detectable PTPN2 inhibition. In the present
C18 diacid series, only Y5 (bearing the longest Lys-DNP
linker) displayed measurable PTPN2 inhibition. Notably, our previous
work demonstrated that PTPN2 activity can also be achieved with certain
C16-containing analogues when combined with an additional fatty-acid
modification at Lys2 (e.g., D5 in Zhang et
al.). This suggests that PTPN2 engagement
is determined by the combined effects of fatty-acid type, linker length,
and the nature of the Lys2 substituent, rather than by
any single structural feature.

Y5 exhibits potent
PTPN1 inhibition and moderate PTPN2
inhibition (∼48-fold selective). Y5 inhibited
PTPN1 with an IC50 of 116 nM, representing an approximately
8-fold increase in potency compared with the parent BimBH3 analogue.
Importantly, Y5 concurrently inhibited the closely related
phosphatase PTPN2 with micromolar potency, establishing a dual-target
inhibitory profile consistent with the coordinated regulation of insulin
and leptin signaling by these enzymes. This enhanced activity likely
originates from two structural advantages: (i) the octadecanedioic
acid moiety promotes optimal target engagement through improved albumin-mediated
binding, and (ii) the strategically placed DNP on the lysine side
chain minimizes steric hindrance while preserving hapten accessibility.

Evaluation of Albumin Binding Capacity for BimBH3 Mimetic Peptides

To assess whether the fatty-acid modification conferred functional
albumin binding, we evaluated the inhibitory activity of Y5 in the presence of human serum albumin. Inclusion of 2% albumin
resulted in a reduced PTPN1 inhibition activity, consistent with strong,
reversible sequestration by albumin (). This ∼50-fold decrease in potency
is consistent with strong, noncovalent binding to the fatty acid-binding
sites on albumin, a well-established mechanism for prolonging the
circulatory half-life of peptide therapeutics, which could validate
the pharmacokinetic rationale behind the fatty acid and DNP dual conjugation.
This behavior is characteristic of long-acting peptide therapeutics
and supports the premise that albumin binding contributes to sustained
systemic exposure rather than diminishing intrinsic target engagement.

Structural Analysis of BimBH3 Analogues by Circular Dichroism

Circular dichroism (CD) spectroscopy was employed to characterize
the secondary structure of BimBH3 mimetic peptides in the far-ultraviolet
(UV) region (180–260 nm), a critical wavelength range for probing
peptide conformational features. This technique enables rapid assessment
of secondary structure composition and identification of functional
conformations such as α-helices or β-sheets. Analysis
of hapten-conjugated analogues Y1–Y8 in peptide-compatible
buffer revealed minimal canonical secondary structure formation. CD
spectra demonstrated low α-helical content across all analogues,
with no observable signatures of ordered β-sheet or turn motifs.
The structural profiles resembled those of small molecules, exhibiting
predominantly disordered conformations ().

Molecular Docking Predicts the Mode of Action between Y5 and PTPN1/N2

Molecular docking analyses further
supported the dual inhibitory activity of Y5 by revealing
stable binding modes and large binding areas with the catalytic pockets
of both PTPN1 and PTPN2 (). Y5 engaged extensive hydrogen-bonding
networks and hydrophobic interactions with conserved residues critical
for phosphatase activity, yielding predicted binding energies consistent
with the observed biochemical inhibition. The ability of a single
peptide scaffold to accommodate subtle structural differences between
PTPN1 and PTPN2 provides a structural rationale for its concurrent
targeting of both enzymes. In this study, we simulated the interaction
patterns between PTPN1/2 and the BimBH3 Analogue Y5.
The crystal structures of PTPN1 and PTPN2 were obtained from the Protein
Data Bank (PDB IDs: 9CYO and 7UAD,
with resolutions of 1.94 Å and 2.04 Å, respectively). The
protein structures were prepared by removing all heteroatoms and crystallographic
water molecules. Protonation states of ionizable residues were assigned
at pH 7.4. A short energy minimization was then performed primarily
to alleviate any atomic clashes introduced during the addition of
hydrogen atoms. The 3D structure of the BimBH3 analogue Y5 was sketched in ChemDraw 2023 and subsequently geometry-optimized
using the MMFF94 force field in Chem3D to obtain a low-energy conformation.
Its predominant ionization state at pH 7.4 was assigned to ensure
physiological relevance. Molecular docking was performed using AutoDock
Vina, and the results are depicted in .

The docking results revealed binding free energies
of −5.8
kcal/mol and −5.2 kcal/mol for Y5 with PTPN1 and
PTPN2, respectively, indicating relatively favorable binding affinity
consistent with in vitro inhibition for both targets.
These computational results are consistent with the in vitro enzyme inhibition assays, further supporting the potent inhibitory
potential of Y5 against PTPN1/2. Specifically, the high-affinity
binding of Y5 to the active sites of PTPN1 and PTPN2
is stabilized by synergistic combinations of extensive hydrogen bonds
and hydrophobic interactions. For PTPN1, a dense network of hydrogen
bonds is formed with key residues, including P31, D53, H54, R56, K58,
H60, E75, S80, K102, and K252 (the yellow part in the a). This intricate hydrogen-bonding
network is critical for specifically anchoring and stabilizing the
compound within the binding pocket. Concurrently, hydrophobic interactions
involving residues such as F30, P31, D53, H54, K58, L71, K73, P206,
and P210 further stabilize the complex by encapsulating the nonpolar
regions of Y5 (the orange part in the a). Notably, several critical
residues, such as H54 and K58, play dual roles: their polar atoms
form hydrogen bonds, while their aliphatic/aromatic moieties contribute
to hydrophobic effects. Similarly, for PTPN2, an even more extensive
network of hydrogen bonds is formed with residues including K60, 61L,
Q62, D67, Q103, Q104, N163, P207, and H209 (the yellow part in the b). This is complemented
by a hydrophobic shell formed by residues such as K60, Q62, L99, P207,
and Y254, which interact with the nonpolar regions of Y5 (the orange part in the b).

Molecular Dynamics Simulations Reveal Stable Binding of Y5 to PTPN1/N2

To further validate the binding modes
predicted by molecular docking and to assess the stability and specificity
of Y5 interactions with PTPN1 and PTPN2 from a dynamic
perspective, we performed 50 ns all-atom molecular dynamics simulations.
The following results systematically compare the binding behavior
of Y5 with the two phosphatases across multiple metrics,
including center-of-mass distance, minimum atomic distance, ligand
and protein backbone root-mean-square deviation (RMSD), and energy
fluctuations ().

For the PTPN1–Y5 complex, the
center-of-mass
(COM) distance remained relatively stable within the range of 1.9–2.3
nm, predominantly fluctuating between 2.0 and 2.3 nm, with no sustained
increase or directional drift. This indicates that Y5 remains stably associated with PTPN1 without undergoing dissociation.
Consistently, the minimum atomic distance was maintained between 0.145
and 0.200 nm (primarily 0.15–0.18 nm), which is well below
the typical van der Waals contact distance (∼0.3–0.4
nm), demonstrating persistent close contacts likely mediated by hydrogen
bonding and hydrophobic interactions. These observations are in agreement
with the molecular docking results. From a structural perspective,
the backbone RMSD of PTPN1 fluctuated within 0.2–0.8 nm without
any continuous upward trend, suggesting that the overall protein structure
remained stable without significant unfolding or large-scale rearrangements.
The observed fluctuations likely reflect inherent conformational flexibility,
potentially associated with an induced-fit binding mechanism. Meanwhile,
the RMSD of Y5 increased rapidly during the initial phase
and subsequently stabilized within the range of 0.4–0.6 nm,
indicating that the ligand underwent conformational adaptation before
adopting a relatively stable binding conformation. In terms of system
stability, the kinetic energy remained steady at approximately 2.49
× 105 kJ/mol, while the total energy fluctuated minimally
around −1.11 × 106 kJ/mol, indicating that
the system had reached thermodynamic equilibrium. Collectively, these
results demonstrate that Y5 forms a stable and tightly
bound noncovalent complex with PTPN1.

In contrast, for the PTPN2–Y5 complex, the
center-of-mass (COM) distance fluctuated between approximately 3.0
and 6.0 nm and exhibited a decreasing trend from an initially larger
separation, indicating that the ligand gradually approached the protein
and established binding during the simulation. Meanwhile, the minimum
atomic distance remained consistently within 0.125–0.142 nm,
demonstrating persistent and close atomic contacts between the two,
which provide direct evidence for stable noncovalent interactions.
Furthermore, the RMSD of Y5 fluctuated within the range
of 0.35–0.85 nm, suggesting that the ligand underwent conformational
adjustments during the binding process. In comparison, the backbone
RMSD of PTPN2 increased rapidly at the initial stage and stabilized
after approximately 10 ns, maintaining fluctuations within 0.25–0.35
nm. This indicates that the protein structure remained largely stable
without significant perturbations throughout the simulation. In terms
of energetics, both the kinetic energy and total energy exhibited
stable fluctuations. The kinetic energy remained within 320,000–327,000
kJ/mol, while the total energy fluctuated around −1.45 ×
106 kJ/mol without any noticeable drift, suggesting that
the system had reached a well-equilibrated thermodynamic state. Overall,
these results indicate that Y5 forms a stable interaction
complex with PTPN2 during the simulation.

Y5
In Vitro Proteolytic Stability
against DPP-IV and Plasma

Dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-IV),
a serine protease responsible for rapid peptide degradation, exhibits
substrate specificity for polypeptides containing proline (Pro) or
alanine (Ala) at the penultimate N-terminal position. Its catalytic
triad (Ser630, Asp708, His740) and hydrophobic substrate-binding pocket
enable precise recognition of conformations matching X-Pro/Ala motifs.
Mechanistically, DPP-IV cleaves the peptide bond between residues
2 and 3 of susceptible polypeptides, releasing N-terminal dipeptides
(e.g., X-Pro or X-Ala fragments). Structural analysis of the native
BimBH3 peptide revealed an Ala residue at position 2, rendering it
vulnerable to DPP-IV-mediated proteolysis. To identify this degradation
pathway and improve metabolic stability, we strategically substituted
this Ala residue with a noncanonical amino acid designed to disrupt
DPP-IV recognition while preserving target engagement.

To assess
the resistance of mimetic peptide Y5 to DPP-IV-mediated
degradation, we conducted in vitro degradation resistance
assays. As shown in a, Y5 exhibited significantly improved stability
with a half-life of 13.78 h, representing a 45-fold increase over
the original BimBH3 peptide and a 4.5-fold enhancement
compared to SM-6. These results demonstrate that the
dual strategy of targeted amino acid substitution and DNP conjugation
effectively enhances proteolytic stability by mitigating DPP-IV recognition
and cleavage.

Prolonging drug half-life enhances therapeutic
outcomes by improving
patient compliance, stabilizing systemic exposure, and sustaining
efficacy-critical advantages for chronic disease management. Based
on Y5’s demonstrated resistance to DPP-IV degradation
(in vitro t1/2 = 13.78 h), we further
evaluated its pharmacokinetic stability in rat plasma (b). Y5 exhibited
markedly improved plasma stability with a half-life of 15.78 h, representing
a 31-fold increase over BimBH3 and a 1.33-fold improvement
compared to SM-6.

To further clarify the respective
contributions of the fatty acid
and DNP modifications to stability, we synthesized two control analogues: Y5-DNP only (containing only the Lys2-DNP modification,
with no fatty acid) and Y5-C18 only (containing only
the N-terminal C18 diacid modification, with Ala2 and no
DNP). Their DPP-IV stability and mouse plasma stability were then
evaluated under identical conditions. For DPP-IV stability, the half-lives
of the parent BimBH3, SM-6, Y5, Y5-DNP only,
and Y5-C18 only were 0.307, 3.054, 13.78, 1.312, and
2.649 h, respectively (a). For mouse plasma stability, the half-lives of the same
compounds were 0.510, 11.90, 15.78, 3.493, and 10.36 h, respectively
(b). The dual-modified Y5 exhibited substantially longer half-lives than either single-modified
control. The fatty-acid-only analogue (Y5-C18 only) already
provided considerable stabilization, and the DNP-only analogue (Y5-DNP only) also afforded a modest improvement. Importantly,
the combination of the two modifications achieved the greatest effect,
demonstrating a synergistic contribution of the fatty acid and DNP
modifications to overall stability. In summary, Y5 was
a promising long-acting therapeutic candidate for T2DM and/or obesity
therapy, with potential of cellular uptake and function, and maintenance
of stable drug levels and minimize dosing frequency in clinical applications.

Y5 Restores Cellular Insulin and Leptin Signaling
by Enhancing Phosphorylation of Downstream Signaling Molecules

Insulin and leptin resistance represent convergent signaling defects
underlying hyperglycaemia and excess adiposity. At the molecular level,
impaired phosphorylation of insulin receptor substrates and downstream
AKT, together with blunted JAK2-STAT3 activation, are hallmarks of
disrupted insulin and leptin responsiveness. Because PTPN1 and PTPN2
directly dephosphorylate key nodes within both pathways, we hypothesized
that effective dual inhibition would restore signaling competence
under insulin- and leptin-resistant conditions.

A critical step
in this process is the phosphorylation of insulin receptor substrate
1 (IRS-1) and its key downstream signaling molecules, such as AKT.
Phosphorylated AKT (p-AKT) subsequently serves as the primary mediator
of glucose uptake and metabolic regulation. Similarly, the leptin
signaling pathway is also important for glucose metabolism. Impaired
transduction of the JAK2-STAT3 pathway downstream of leptin signaling
is a central feature of leptin resistance, specifically manifested
as a significant reduction in tyrosine phosphorylation levels of STAT3.
Therefore, detecting the level of p-STAT3 is a key indicator for assessing
leptin sensitivity in cells. A lower levels of p-IRS1, p-AKT and p-STAT3
indicate impaired insulin/leptin signaling. Consequently, increased
levels of p-IRS1, p-AKT, and p-STAT3 can signify restored insulin/leptin
sensitivity. PTPN1 negatively regulates insulin signal transduction
by dephosphorylating IRS-1 and exerts a negative regulatory effect
on the leptin signaling pathway by dephosphorylating JAK2 in the JAK2-STAT3
pathway.

We sought to determine whether the analogue Y5 enhances
the phosphorylation of downstream signal molecules via PTPN1 inhibition
to restore signaling sensitivity in the insulin and leptin pathways. As shown in a, the results indicated that treatment with Y5 resulted in a robust and dose-dependent restoration of
insulin and leptin signaling. Specifically, Y5 markedly
increased the phosphorylation of IRS-1 and AKT in response to insulin
stimulation, while simultaneously enhancing STAT3 phosphorylation
following leptin exposure. These effects were observed across a broad
concentration range and occurred without detectable changes in total
PTPN1 protein levels, indicating that Y5 acts by functionally
relieving phosphatase-mediated signal attenuation rather than altering
phosphatase expression.

Quantitative analysis revealed that Y5 restored downstream
signaling with high potency, yielding half-maximal effective concentrations
in the nanomolar to low micromolar range for AKT, IRS-1 and STAT3
phosphorylation. The coordinated enhancement of insulin- and leptin-responsive
signaling nodes supports a model in which dual PTPN1/PTPN2 inhibition
reinstates hormonal sensitivity at the cellular level. Notably, the
simultaneous restoration of insulin and leptin signaling by Y5 distinguishes this mechanism from incretin-based therapies,
which primarily act through appetite suppression and central satiety
pathways. By directly enhancing intracellular hormone sensitivity, Y5 provides a promising mechanistic framework capable of exhibiting
glycemic control and body-weight reduction observed in vivo, independent of changes in food intake.

investigated whether Y5 possesses the pharmacokinetic

Glycemic Control Efficacy and Weight-Gain Reduction Benefit
of Y5 in db/db Mice

The restoration of insulin
and leptin signaling at the cellular level raises a critical translational
question whether sustained dual inhibition of PTPN1 and PTPN2 could
produce durable metabolic benefits in vivo under
conditions of severe insulin resistance. To address this, we evaluated
the long-term efficacy of Y5 in leptin receptor-deficient
db/db mice, a well-established model of advanced type 2 diabetes characterized
by hyperglycaemia, hyperinsulinaemia, and progressive weight gain.
Based on promising results from preliminary experiments, the long-term
antidiabetic and weight-loss benefit of the candidate analogue Y5 was further evaluated in a 7-week study using db/db diabetic
mouse models (a). Mice received once-weekly subcutaneous injections of Y5 at doses of 50 nmol/kg (0.10 mg/kg), 100 nmol/kg (0.20 mg/kg), and
200 nmol/kg (0.40 mg/kg). Semaglutide (100 nmol/kg, MW = 4113.58,
i.e., 0.41 mg/kg) was used as the positive control. Fasting blood
glucose monitoring indicated that at most time points, once-weekly
s.c. administration of Y5 at all three dose levels showed
obvious glycemic control efficacy, with trends consistent with that
observed in the semaglutide treatment group (b). This superior performance was reflected
in the sustained suppress of gain of fasting blood glucose levels
observed over the long-term treatment period, suggesting that Y5 could offer a competitive alternative therapy with enhanced
therapeutic benefits in long-term diabetes management.

Oral glucose tolerance testing (OGTT) revealed
marked reductions
in glucose excursion and area under the curve compared with vehicle-treated
controls (c
and d), indicating that Y5 improves
the capacity to handle acute glycemic challenges. To further assess
the mechanism underlying glycemic improvement, we examined circulating
insulin and C-peptide levels at the end of the treatment period. Y5-treated mice exhibited significantly reduced serum insulin
and C-peptide concentrations relative to controls (e and f), consistent with enhanced insulin sensitivity rather than increased
insulin secretion. This profile contrasts with insulinotropic or incretin-based
mechanisms and aligns with the restoration of intracellular insulin
signaling observed in vitro.

Given the downward
trend in body weight in the Y5 treatment
groups, lipid metabolism parameters were also measured after long-term
treatment. Results in g–i show that plasma levels
of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), triglycerides (TG),
and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) in the semaglutide
and Y5 treatment groups were slightly lower than those
in the saline control group. These results suggest that Y5 can improve lipid metabolism in db/db mice to some extent. Furthermore,
relevant safety indicators were assessed, including hepatotoxicity
markers (AST, ALT) and nephrotoxicity markers (CREA, UREA) (j–m). It is worth noting that the CREA level in the
0.5 μmol/kg Y5 group was significantly lower than
that in the control group (p < 0.05), while the
UREA level was higher. Collectively, these data suggest that Y5 was well tolerated and did not exhibit significant toxicity
during the long-term treatment period, supporting the feasibility
of sustained PTPN1/PTPN2 inhibition in vivo. These
observations indicate that the metabolic benefits of Y5 are not accompanied by detectable organ toxicity under the dosing
regimen employed.

In parallel with glycemic improvement, Y5 treatment
attenuated body-weight gain in db/db mice over the course of the study
(n) without
reducing food intake (o). Although the magnitude of weight reduction was modest
relative to incretin-based therapy, Y5 consistently slowed
the progressive weight increase characteristic of this model, suggesting
a direct metabolic effect rather than secondary weight loss driven
by reduced caloric intake. To further investigate the effect of Y5 on adipogenesis, mice with obvious weight-gain reduction
were euthanized via CO2 inhalation at the end of the experiment,
and various adipose tissues were isolated and examined. Analysis of
adipose tissue depots (q) revealed that Y5 selectively reduced fat mass
across multiple white adipose tissue compartments, including perirenal
and inguinal depots, as well as brown adipose tissue (q–u). Specifically, after Y5 treatment, the mass of brown
adipose tissue (BAT), perirenal white adipose tissue (pWAT), inguinal
white adipose tissue (iWAT), and epididymal white adipose tissue (eWAT)
in obese mice showed a decreasing trend. Notably, the BAT and pWAT
mass in mice treated with 100 nmol/kg and 50 nmol/kg Y5 were significantly lower than those in the control group (p < 0.01, r and s). These reductions indicate
that Y5-mediated modulation of body weight is driven
primarily by decreased adiposity rather than loss of lean mass, consistent
with enhanced metabolic efficiency downstream of improved insulin
and leptin signaling. Further investigations in long-term and chronic
obesity models (e.g., diet-induced obesity mice) are imperative to
validate the weight-loss therapeutic potential of Y5.

Y5 Induces Weight Loss and Improves Metabolic Health
in DIO Mice

Leptin resistance is a defining feature of common
obesity, we next examined whether dual inhibition of PTPN1 and PTPN2
could overcome established metabolic dysfunction in diet-induced obese
(DIO) mice. Unlike db/db mice, which lack functional leptin receptor
signaling, DIO mice retain an intact but resistant leptin axis, thereby
providing a stringent model to assess whether restoration of leptin
sensitivity translates into meaningful weight loss independent of
appetite suppression.

Mice received subcutaneous administration
of Y5 at doses of 25, 50, or 75 nmol/kg, or vehicle control,
once every 3 days for a total treatment period of 6 weeks, followed
by a 2-week treatment-free observation phase. Body weight and food
intake were monitored throughout the study. Notably, Y5-induced weight loss was comparable in magnitude to that achieved
with semaglutide, despite fundamentally distinct mechanisms of action.
Weight reduction became evident within the first 2 weeks of treatment
and was maintained throughout the dosing interval, consistent with
long-acting target engagement. As illustrated in the c and d, all Y5-treated groups exhibited significant weight
loss compared to the vehicle control group. At the end of the 6-week
treatment period, the high-dose group (75 nmol/kg) showed a reduction
of −15.63%, while the medium- (50 nmol/kg) and low-dose (25
nmol/kg) groups exhibited reductions of −6.87% and −7.85%,
respectively. Notably, this weight-lowering effect became even more
pronounced during the 2-week off-treatment period, with the high-,
medium-, and low-dose groups achieving weight reductions of −17.21%,
−7.14%, and −7.92%, respectively, indicating a sustained
and enhanced efficacy after drug withdrawal. Analysis of cumulative
food intake revealed no significant differences between any Y5-treated groups and the vehicle control, suggesting that
appetite suppression was not a contributing mechanism. In addition,
serum insulin (e) and leptin (f) levels measured at the end of the study showed no significant
improvement in insulin and leptin secretion in Y5 treated
DIO mice, indicating that the significant weight-loss efficacy was
attributable to the amelioration of insulin and leptin resistance.
These results were consistent with the cellular effects of Y5 treatment, which significantly ameliorated insulin and leptin resistance.

As illustrated in b, treatment with Y5 did not
significantly alter
cumulative food consumption in DIO mice. Food intake remained comparable
to vehicle-treated controls throughout the study, indicating that
the observed weight loss might not be driven by reduced caloric intake.
These findings demonstrate that Y5 induces body-weight
reduction through a potential appetite-independent mechanism as indicated
by unchanged food intake.

Currently, GLP-1 receptor agonists,
primarily function through
potent and sustained suppression of central appetite, leading to reduced
energy intake. This mechanism is frequently associated with gastrointestinal
adverse effects, including nausea and vomiting, which can compromise
patient adherence and quality of life. In comparison, Y5 induces body-weight reduction through a mechanism that is not primarily
driven by reduced caloric intake, as indicated by unchanged cumulative
food intake; however, direct measurements of energy expenditure are
needed to establish the underlying mechanism. This innovative mechanism,
independent of chronic appetite suppression, may effectively circumvent
the limitations associated with sustained satiety induction. The unique
pharmacological profile of Y5 underscores its considerable
potential and distinctive therapeutic value in the development of
next-generation antiobesity agents.

Discussion

Current long-acting pharmacotherapies capable
of treating both
type 2 diabetes and obesity are dominated by incretin-based agents,
which primarily achieve efficacy through sustained suppression of
appetite. While clinically effective, this mechanism is intrinsically
linked to gastrointestinal adverse effects, compromised eating-related
quality of life, and a pronounced tendency toward metabolic rebound
upon treatment discontinuation. In this study, we establish dual inhibition
of PTPN1 and PTPN2 as a fundamentally distinct therapeutic strategy
that dissociates weight loss and glycemic control from appetite suppression.
By restoring intracellular insulin and leptin signaling, the long-acting
peptide Y5 achieves durable metabolic benefits in multiple
mouse models of diabetes and obesity without reducing food intake,
thereby redefining how these interconnected diseases may be pharmacologically
addressed.

The metabolic efficacy of Y5 can be
explained by a
unified mechanism centered on enhanced hormone sensitivity rather
than supraphysiological hormone signaling. PTPN1 and PTPN2 act as
convergent intracellular brakes on both insulin and leptin pathways,
attenuating IRS-AKT and JAK2-STAT3 signaling. Dual inhibition of these
phosphatases by Y5 restores signal transduction under
resistant conditions, leading to improved insulin responsiveness,
reduced compensatory hyperinsulinaemia, and reactivation of leptin-mediated
regulation of energy balance. This mechanism accounts for the coordinated
improvement in glycemic control, reduction in adiposity, and preservation
of food intake observed across models, and distinguishes Y5 from incretin-based agents that primarily act through central satiety
pathways.

Notably, the selective PTPN1 inhibitor trodusquemine
(MSI-1436)
has been reported to induce appetite-independent fat-specific weight
loss in DIO mice. This observation, together
with our findings that Y5 (a dual PTPN1/N2 inhibitor)
also induces weight loss without reducing food intake, suggests that
PTPN1 inhibition alone may be sufficient for certain appetite-independent
metabolic effects. However, direct comparisons between trodusquemine
and Y5 are complicated by their distinct chemical scaffolds,
pharmacokinetic profiles, and potential off-target effects. Future
studies using selective inhibitors or genetic models will be needed
to dissect the individual roles of PTPN1 and PTPN2 in energy balance
regulation, and to determine whether dual inhibition offers advantages
beyond PTPN1-selective targeting.

An important mechanistic insight
of this study is the context-dependent
but indispensable role of PTPN2 in mediating the metabolic effects
of dual phosphatase inhibition. While PTPN1 predominantly constrains
insulin receptor signaling, PTPN2 functions as a critical negative
regulator of leptin sensitivity through dephosphorylation of STAT3
downstream of JAK2. In diet-induced obesity, leptin resistance arises
primarily from impaired STAT3 activation rather than loss of signaling
components, rendering inhibition of PTPN1 alone insufficient to restore
leptin responsiveness. The moderate potency against PTPN2 is consistent
with the concept that sustained, partial inhibition of this phosphatase
is sufficient to relieve chronic suppression of STAT3 signaling in vivo.

Y5 treatment restores STAT3
phosphorylation in cell
lines and induces pronounced weight loss in diet-induced obese mice
without reducing food intake, indicating reactivation of leptin signaling
rather than appetite suppression. Notably, effective engagement of
the leptin axis does not require equivalent inhibitory potency against
PTPN2 compared with PTPN1. Instead, sustained, partial inhibition
of PTPN2 enabled by the long-acting pharmacokinetic profile of Y5 appears sufficient to relieve chronic suppression of STAT3
signaling. This principle of functional convergence rather than equal
potency explains how dual PTPN1/PTPN2 inhibition simultaneously corrects
insulin and leptin resistance in vivo.

Several
limitations of the current study warrant consideration.
First, although Y5 demonstrates robust efficacy in rodent
models, further evaluation in nonrodent species will be required to
assess long-term safety, tissue-specific target engagement and translational
pharmacokinetics. Direct evidence for DNP-specific antibody binding
and FcRn-mediated recycling was not obtained in this study, and the
contribution of this mechanism to Y5’s prolonged
half-life remains to be experimentally validated in the further studies.
However, the DNP-hapten strategy has been extensively validated as
a generalizable platform for peptide half-life extension in the literature,
and our application of this strategy to an intracellular phosphatase
inhibitor represents a conceptual extension of this established approach.
Second, while Y5 restores insulin and leptin signaling
across multiple tissues, the relative contributions of central versus
peripheral phosphatase inhibition remain to be fully delineated. Third,
the molecular docking used to predict the binding mode employed a
rigid-receptor model that does not fully account for protein conformational
flexibility; thus, the precise binding geometry requires further validation
through cocrystallization. Nevertheless, the long-acting, peptide-based
strategy described here directly addresses historical barriers associated
with phosphatase drug discovery and provides a tractable framework
for clinical translation. Importantly, by enhancing endogenous hormone
sensitivity rather than suppressing appetite, dual PTPN1/PTPN2 inhibition
may offer a differentiated therapeutic profile with improved tolerability
and durability compared with existing incretin-based approaches.

In summary, this study identifies dual inhibition of PTPN1 and
PTPN2 as a viable and mechanistically distinct approach for the treatment
of type 2 diabetes and obesity. Through long-acting peptide-mediated
suppression of these intracellular phosphatases, metabolic homeostasis
is restored by enhancing insulin and leptin sensitivity rather than
by suppressing appetite or augmenting hormonal signaling. The resulting
long-acting glycemic control and weight loss observed in multiple
mouse models support a conceptual shift in how metabolic disease can
be therapeutically addressed. However, direct measurements of energy
expenditure (e.g., oxygen consumption, CO2 production)
and thermogenic markers were not performed. While unchanged food intake
indicates that appetite suppression is not the primary driver, the
precise contribution of increased energy expenditure to Y5-induced weight loss remains to be determined.

Together, our
findings establish PTPN1/PTPN2 inhibition as a nonincretin
strategy that directly targets the molecular basis of insulin and
leptin resistance, offering a promising alternative framework for
durable metabolic therapy.

Experimental Section

Peptide Synthesis and Circular Dichroism (CD) Spectroscopy

All BimBH3 analogues were synthesized employing standard solid-phase
peptide synthesis (SPPS) with the Fmoc strategy on 2-chlorotrityl
chloride (2-Cl-CTC) resin in glass reaction vessels, following established
protocols. The synthesis commenced with the assembly of the decapeptide
backbone sequence. Subsequently, a coupling reaction was performed
using DNP-modified amino acids Fmoc-X­(DNP)-OH (X = Lys, Dab, Dap,
Orn) at 4 equiv relative to the resin, 4.8 equiv of HBTU, HOBt, and
DIEA in 5 mL of DMF. This reaction proceeded at 40 °C under a
nitrogen atmosphere for 1 h. The resin was then thoroughly washed
with DMF (4×), and the coupling efficiency was verified by the
Kaiser test. In cases where the test indicated complete coupling,
the synthetic steps used for the decapeptide were repeated to extend
the peptide chain at the N-terminus by successively coupling Ile and
a fatty acid (either octadecanedioic acid or hexadecanoic acid), employing
the same coupling reagents and procedures as described for the DNP-modified
amino acids.

For the synthesis of the Lys9-fluorescein
(Flu)-labeled analogue (Lys9-Flu-Y5), Fmoc-Lys­(Alloc)-OH
was incorporated following the assembly of the linear peptide chain
on the resin. The Alloc protecting group was then selectively removed
via two 20 min treatments with 0.1 equiv of Pd­(PPh3)4 in DCM. Finally, fluorescein labeling was achieved by coupling
5-carboxyfluorescein (5-FAM) under the Fmoc strategy for 6 hours under
nitrogen protection and in the absence of light. Detailed procedures
for peptide synthesis, labeling, and purification are provided in
the Supporting Information.

CD spectra
of the selected analogues were recorded on a Jasco J-810
spectropolarimeter over a wavelength range of 180–260 nm, using
peptide concentrations of 0.2–0.6 mg/mL. Measurements were
carried out in a 1.0 mm path length cell with a bandwidth of 1.0 nm,
a response time of 2 s, and an average of 3 accumulations. All spectra
were acquired at room temperature under a nitrogen atmosphere and
are expressed as mean residue molar ellipticity [θ], calculated
according to the equation
[ao6c05930_m001.webp][θ]=1000×mdeg/(lc)
where mdeg is the raw CD signal in millidegrees, c is the peptide molar concentration (mM), and l is the cell path length (mm).

Assay of Enzyme Inhibitory Activity In Vitro

The inhibitory activities of peptide mimetics against recombinant
human PTPN1 (Sino Biological Inc., catalog no.: 10304-H07E) and PTPN2
(Sino Biological Inc., catalog no.: 10570-HNCB) were evaluated by
monitoring the hydrolysis of para-nitrophenyl phosphate
(pNPP). Peptide analogues were dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO)
and aliquoted into a transparent 96-well polystyrene plate. Subsequently,
MES buffer, pNPP, and the respective phosphatase were added to each
well. The enzymatic reaction was performed at room temperature, and
the catalysis of pNPP was continuously monitored at 405 nm for 4 min.
Dose–response curves for each analogue were generated, and
the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) was calculated
using GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software Inc.).

Molecular Docking Studies

The crystal structures of
PTPN1 (PDB ID: 9CYO, resolution: 1.94 Å) and PTPN2 (PDB ID: 7UAD, resolution: 2.04
Å) were retrieved from the Protein Data Bank (PDB) and used as
receptor models.. Prior to docking, both receptor and ligand structures
were preprocessed by removing water molecules and heteroatoms, followed
by protonation and energy minimization to obtain the most stable conformations.
Semiflexible molecular docking was performed using AutoDock Vina (version
1.1.2). Systematic sampling of ligand positions, orientations, and
rotatable bonds was conducted within the defined active site region.
Multiple rounds of efficient search were performed using the scoring
function to identify the ligand conformation with the lowest binding
free energy. The docking results obtained from AutoDock Vina were
visualized using PyMOL (version 2.0, Schrödinger, LLC) to analyze
specific binding interactions, such as hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic
interactions.

Molecular Dynamics Simulations

All molecular dynamics
(MD) simulations were performed using GROMACS (version 2023.3). The
system was solvated with explicit water molecules and neutralized
by adding counterions. Long-range electrostatic interactions were
treated with the particle mesh Ewald (PME) method, and both electrostatic
and van der Waals interactions were cutoff at 1.0 nm. Energy minimization
was first carried out using the steepest descent algorithm until the
maximum force dropped below 1000 kJ mol–1 nm–1. The system was then equilibrated in two steps: (i)
a 100 ps NVT ensemble at 300 K using a V-rescale thermostat, and (ii)
a 100 ps NPT ensemble at 1 bar using a Berendsen barostat. During
equilibration, heavy atoms were position-restrained. Finally, a 50
ns production MD run was conducted under NPT conditions (300 K, 1
bar). All simulations were accelerated with mixed-precision calculations
on GPU nodes (NVIDIA A100).

DPP-IV Stability

To evaluate the stability of the peptide
mimetics against DPP-IV enzyme, a stock solution (1 mg/mL) was prepared
by dissolving the peptide in sodium phosphate buffer (20 mM, pH 7.0).
The reaction was initiated by adding DPP-IV (4.5 μL, ≥45
U/mg) to the solution, followed by incubation at 37 °C. At designated
time points, 100 μL aliquots were withdrawn and quenched with
200 μL of 0.1 M sodium bicarbonate. The remaining peptide content
was analyzed by RP-HPLC at 214 nm. Peak areas corresponding to the
peptide at different time points were integrated, and the data were
fitted using GraphPad Prism 8 to determine the half-life by plotting
the residual peptide amount versus time.

Plasma Stability

The plasma stability of the peptide
mimetics was assessed by adding 200 μL of the peptide solution
(0.01–0.04 M in phosphate-buffered saline, PBS, pH 7.4) to
200 μL of 40% mouse plasma (Lot no. MP20039, Shanghai Yuanye
Bio-Technology Co., Ltd.) at 37 °C. At predetermined time points
(0, 0.5, 1, 3, 10, 16, 22, and 40 h), 20 μL aliquots were rapidly
withdrawn and immediately quenched with an equal volume of 0.2% TFA
in acetonitrile. The resulting mixture was centrifuged at 14,000 rpm
for 10 min at 4 °C to remove precipitated proteins. The supernatant
was subsequently injected into an analytical HPLC system and analyzed
at 214 nm. The residual peptide content at each time point was calculated
based on the integrated peak area, and the data were imported into
GraphPad Prism 8. The half-life was determined by fitting the percentage
of remaining peptide versus time.

Cell Culture and Western Blot

HepG2 cells were cultured
in Minimum Essential Medium (MEM) supplemented with 10% (v/v) fetal
bovine serum, 100 μg/mL streptomycin, and 100 U/mL penicillin.
The cells were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator
with an atmosphere of 95% air and 5% CO2. For the experiments,
cells were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 5 × 105 cells per well and allowed to reach 60–70% confluence.
To induce insulin or leptin resistance, the cells were treated with
10–6 mol/L insulin and/or 100 μmol/L palmitic
acid (PA) for 24 h. Following this induction period, the medium was
replaced with fresh medium containing different concentrations of Y5, and the cells were incubated for an additional 24 h. Subsequently,
the cells were stimulated with 100 nM insulin or 100 nM leptin for
30 min.

After the treatments, the cells were washed twice with
cold PBS. Total protein was then extracted using RIPA lysis buffer
supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. The lysates
were incubated on ice for 10 min, followed by centrifugation at 12,000
rpm for 10 min at 4 °C to collect the supernatant. The protein
concentration of the supernatants was determined using a BCA assay
kit. Equal amounts of protein from each sample were separated by 8%
or 12.5% SDS-PAGE and transferred PVDF membranes. The membranes were
blocked with 5% nonfat milk in TBST for 1 h at room temperature to
prevent nonspecific binding. Thereafter, the membranes were incubated
with the appropriate primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C, followed
by incubation with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary
antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized
using an infrared laser imaging system.

Pharmacokinetic Evaluation

The fluorescence-based PK
method has been validated in previous work by Dai et al., where fluorescence-labeled analogues were used
for PK studies with results validated by LC-MS/MS (verification via
cross-study data). Prior to administration, Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats
were weighed and received a single subcutaneous injection of the fluorescently
labeled analogue Lys9-Flu-Y5 at a dose of
100 nmol/kg. At predetermined time points postinjection, blood samples
(80 μL) were collected from the tail vein and mixed with 320
μL of PBS containing EDTA (pH 8.0). The blood samples were centrifuged
at 3000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C to separate plasma. The resulting
plasma samples were then analyzed using a multimode microplate reader
(synergy H4, Biotek) with excitation and emission wavelengths set
at 490 and 520 nm, respectively. Plasma concentration–time
profiles were generated using GraphPad Prism 8, and pharmacokinetic
parameters were estimated with Phoenix WinNonlin software (Academic
Version, Pharsight Corp., Mountain View, CA, USA).

Biodistribution Analysis

The temporal distribution
profile of Lys9-Flu-Y5 was evaluated in Kunming
mice divided into two groups (n = 3 per group) following
subcutaneous administration of the compound at a dose of 100 nmol/kg.
Mice were maintained in the dark postinjection to minimize photobleaching
and anesthetized with isoflurane at scheduled time points (0, 0.5,
1, 2, and 4 h). Whole-body fluorescence imaging was conducted using
a small animal in vivo imaging system (FMT 2500;
PerkinElmer, Waltham, MA, USA) with an excitation wavelength of 520
nm. Imaging data were quantitatively analyzed using TrueQuant software
(version 3.0).

Antidiabetic and Antiobesity Efficacy in db/db and Diet-Induced
Obese Mice

All experimental procedures involving animals
were reviewed and approved by the Laboratory Animal Ethics Committee
of Ocean University of China (approval no. SYXK (Shandong) 20210009).
The study was conducted at the Marine Biomedical Research Institute
of the university, in strict accordance with the NIH Guide for the
Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (eighth edition, 2011), under the
oversight of the Department of Science and Technology of Shandong
Province.

Male db/db mice (8–9 weeks old) were housed
under standard conditions (24 ± 2.0 °C, normal light-dark
cycle) with bedding and purified water provided, which was refreshed
every 2 days. After a one-week acclimation period, mice exhibiting
fasting blood glucose levels exceeding 11 mM were selected and randomly
divided into five groups (n = 5 per group): a vehicle
control, a positive control, and three dose-groups (high, medium,
low) of test compound Y5. All groups received once-weekly
subcutaneous injections: the positive control was administered semaglutide,
the Y5 groups received respective doses of compound Y5, and the vehicle control received an equal volume of physiological
saline. Blood glucose levels were monitored twice per week via tail
vein sampling using an ACCU-CHEK Performa glucometer (Roche Diabetes
Care GmbH, Mannheim, Germany). Body weight was measured twice weekly,
while food and water consumption were recorded daily. In a parallel
study using diet-induced obese (DIO) mice, housing conditions were
similar. These mice received subcutaneous administration of compounds
once every 3 days, with body weight and food intake monitored daily
throughout the treatment period.

Glucose Tolerance Test (GTT)

The area under the curve
(AUC) for blood glucose was calculated from actual measured values
following an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT), which was performed
to assess the ability of the peptides to restore normoglycemia. After
an overnight fast, mice received a subcutaneous (s.c.) injection of
the designated peptides, semaglutide, or vehicle, prior to an oral
glucose challenge (2 g/kg). Blood glucose was measured immediately
before the glucose load (t = 0) to establish any peptide-induced alterations
in baseline levels, and subsequently at 30, 60, 90, and 120 min postadministration.

Biomarker Analysis

Fresh serum samples were analyzed
directly after gentle mixing to aliquot the supernatant for testing.
Prior to instrumental analysis, sample volume was verified to be sufficient
for all intended assays. The required sample volume ranged from 2
to 35 μL per analyte, with cumulative consumption increasing
with the number of biomarkers assessed. In cases of limited sample
volume or high-plex assays, samples were appropriately diluted at
a predetermined factor. Notably, significant hemolysis was considered
a potential interference in assay accuracy. The biomarkers were measured
using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed
using GraphPad Prism. Comparisons between multiple groups at a single
time point were conducted using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s
multiple comparisons test. Changes over time between treatment groups
were analyzed by two-way ANOVA with treatment and time as factors.
Data are presented as the mean ± SD, and individual data points
represent biological replicates (individual mice). A two-tailed p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.
Data distribution was assumed to be normal and was not formally tested.
