 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 By projecting the solar capacity until 2050 at a granular, census tract level, we aim to define
 Participant’s Total Addressable Market for Electra's registration service and examine the
 volumes and locations of panels reaching end-of-life with corresponding state policies and
 planned recycling locales. This dual-faceted data will be pivotal in developing a sustainable, per-
 watt pricing strategy for our state central funds and key areas for collection hubs, ensuring these
 volumes can adequately support our collection, hauling, and recycling provider network.

 Summary of Research Results:

 Through the collection of geospatial solar deployment and projected recycling data, the ability to
 model the collection and reuse of energy technologies and their critical materials has been
 augmented. Additionally, through a study of the regulatory environment at federal and state
 levels highlights key locations where solar modules may be successfully recaptured for reuse or
 recycling. Additionally, this analysis informs future work which may seek to differentiate
 transport mechanisms for hazardous and non-hazardous waste along with expanding state-level
 regulatory analysis.

 Financial models were developed to estimate the potential addressable market for
 decommissioned solar panels. These models helped determine necessary fees to support business
 operations, including the cost of collection and transportation of solar modules.

 Task 1: Assemble Database of Solar Installations in United States

 The Contractor will assemble regional data detailing Solar installations in the United States with
 a focus on the state of Washington, starting with information in the USPVDatabase
 (https://eerscmap.usgs.gov/uspvdb/) and cross referencing this with data in the open access NLR
 tool Photovoltaic Lifetime Cost and Environmental (PVICE). The RELOG tool may also be used
 to generate data. Where feasible, this data will be further expanded to provide Participant with an
 Excel file detailing existing and projected installations of solar modules and their end of life
 (EOL) volumes though 2050.

 Data from an existing NLR tool, (Regional Energy Deployment System) ReEDs, was leveraged
 to provide regionalized information on anticipated solar deployment in various scenarios. This
 will inform current trends in solar deployment for the Participant to manipulate their own
 assumptions related to lifetime and decommissioning. This data was assembled in an excel file
 and units were converted to improve compatibility with other datasets. Initial datasets were
 expedited and delivered to the participant at their request. An additional dataset reporting
 industrial and residential solar deployment was generated at the interest of the participant. A
 photovoltaic (PV) decommissioning dataset based on the initial PV deployment scenarios was
 prepared, however the client indicated the industrial plus residential dataset was of more value to
 their later tasks. This was deemed the most valuable way to go, requiring modeling reruns to
 adjust PV lifetime across multiple scenarios, including a reduced lifetime of 15 years to
 investigate the impacts of reduced PV lifetime on recycling volumes. Multiple scenarios were
 identified and captured for the participant, including both utility and residential scale
 deployment. PVICE was used to identify current levels of modules coming offline and forecast
 the levels of EOL panels through 2050 in a few scenarios, particularly minding accelerated

 decommissioning trends that may arise due to the rapid evolution of module technology and
 subsequent impact on installation economics.

 Task 2: Collect Solar Module Disposal/Recycling Policies in the US by State

 The Contractor will research solar module EOL regulations and policies in US states to support
 the development of cost models to be performed by Participant in Task 3. This report will
 investigate any mix of up to 4 jurisdictions (currently assuming - federal, Washington State,
 Oregon and California) agreed between the Participant and the contractor for:

 a) PV system decommissioning requirements which may require a specific disposition for
 PV modules and/or specific management requirements for PV modules
 b) solid waste management requirements that apply to the handling, storage, transport, and
 treatment of PV modules prior to recycling or reuse.
 c) regulatory exceptions, exclusions, or alternative regulatory provisions that may apply to
 the solid waste management requirements under certain scenarios (e.g., some states have
 adopted the transferred based recycling exclusions and some have not)
 d) other state and federal transportation and shipping requirements (e.g., if shipments cross
 state lines Department of Transportation requirements are likely triggered)
 e) other policies that may implicate the handling, storage, transport and treatment of PV
 modules prior to recycling.

 A report was prepared with a focus on Washington, Oregon, and California detailing the federal
 and state regulatory landscape surrounding the decommissioning, reuse, handling, storage,
 transport, recycling, and disposal of PV modules. This report includes a distinction between
 hazardous and non-hazardous waste and interstate packaging and shipping regulations.
 Additionally, exclusions, exemptions, and other options are highlighted as they may be relevant
 to the Participant. This includes regulations around transportation and potential penalties for non-
 compliance. This report will improve estimates and planning for the recapture of solar modules
 at the end of their service lifetimes.

 Task 3: Modeling: State-by-State Registration Fees

 a) Participant will use Forecastr to develop a model that projects solar panel registration
 fees and adoption rates in Washington state and 4 additional states identified through
 NLR's policy research.
 b) Participant will generate conservative revenue estimates for years 1-5 based on projected
 registration fees and adoption rates, focusing initially on Washington state.
 c) Participant will analyze potential market fluctuations and their impact on registration fee
 revenue using scenario analysis.
 d) Participant will deliver a report summarizing the findings, including the most likely
 scenarios and their potential impact on Electra's business model.

 The original plan for Task 3 was to utilize the Forecastr tool to create a financial model that
 would project solar panel registration fees and adoption rates on a state-by-state basis, generating
 conservative revenue projections for Washington and four other states. However, the modeling
 process experienced delays due to an extended timeline in obtaining data from NLR.

 Due to this delay, we initially used placeholder data in Forecastr and began building early
 financial assumptions based on mock data. However, as our understanding of the financial
 complexity grew—specifically, the need to model two separate but interrelated flows (Electra’s
 operating revenue and the state-restricted collection funds)—we transitioned to a custom-built
 financial model in Google Sheets developed by Enduring Planet.

 This financial model used NLR’s projected solar deployment and PV waste data to calculate
 long-term revenue potential and validate our pricing strategy of $0.04/watt. The model includes
 adoption and revenue projections over a 20-year timeframe and allows for dynamic scenario
 analysis. Due to the lack of funding following the September 2024 Solar Prize Finals, work on
 further refining the model has paused. Nonetheless, the foundational structure provides a viable
 baseline to validate the economic sustainability of Electra’s Product Stewardship program,
 especially for Washington State.

 Task 4: Modeling: Costs of Collecting, Hauling & Recycling

 a) Participant will use Forecastr to create a model that projects the costs associated with
 collecting, hauling, and recycling solar panels in Washington state and 4 additional states.
 b) Participant will Incorporate data from NLR's research on retired panels and end-of-life
 volumes to estimate future costs accurately.
 c) Participant will develop best-case and worst-case scenarios to identify potential risks and
 mitigation strategies.
 d) Participant will provide a detailed report on the projected costs, possible scenarios, and
 recommended strategies to minimize financial risks.

 The initial plan was to use Forecastr to develop a cost model based on NLR’s projected volumes
 of retired solar panels. However, substantial delays in receiving the necessary datasets prevented
 us from integrating NLR’s into our financial modeling or prize submission.

 In response, we developed our own estimates for PV retirement volumes by leveraging publicly
 available installation data, industry decommissioning trends, and internal assumptions around
 average panel lifespan and early failure rates. This allowed us to move forward in building a
 cost-of-revenue model (referred to internally as “Cost of Revenue”) using a custom Google
 Sheets framework developed by Enduring Planet. The decision to move away from Forecastr
 was also driven by the complexity of our financial structure, which involves two separate but
 interlinked accounts: (1) a state-restricted fund that covers collection, hauling, and recycling, and
 (2) Electra’s internal operating account.

 The model projects per-watt costs for logistics and recycling services across Washington. It
 includes best-case and worst-case scenarios that account for regional variability in transportation
 costs, panel density, and reuse potential. Despite conservative assumptions, our projections
 confirmed that Electra’s proposed $0.04/watt takeback fee is generally sufficient to cover long-
 term logistics and processing costs, with an expected average COGS of $0.034–$0.038/watt.

 Task 5: Modeling: Electra Revenue

 a) Participant will use Forecastr and the fractional CFO to build a comprehensive revenue
 model that integrates registration fees, drop-off fees, recycling costs and subscription
 fees.
 b) Based on NLR's policy research, participant will generate 5-year revenue forecasts for
 Electra, starting with Washington state and expanding to 4 additional states.
 c) Participant will analyze various scenarios, such as the impact of natural disasters or
 changes in solar panel adoption rates, on Electra's revenue and cash flow.
 d) Participant will develop a plan for allocating resources to marketing and reuse projects
 while maintaining a healthy cash runway.
 e) Participant will deliver a detailed report on Electra's projected revenue, cash flow
 management, and growth strategies for the next 5 years.

 We created a customizable and comprehensive revenue model that integrates multiple cost
 components, including registration fees, drop-off logistics, recycling costs, and operational
 overhead. While the model was originally intended to expand across five states, progress was
 limited to Washington State due to time and funding constraints.

 Additionally, our collaboration with the fractional CFO team could not extend beyond the initial
 scope. Despite these setbacks, the model is designed for scalability and can incorporate state-
 specific inputs when funding becomes available. This foundation ensures that future efforts can
 resume and expand the modeling process without starting from scratch.
