Purpose
Imaging opportunities (viewable conjunctions) of Resident Space Objects (RSOs) by satellites are not
continuously discovered.

We propose to continuously produce and report viewable conjunctions among objects in orbit. Viewable conjunctions are events
in space and time when a satellite may favorably view a Resident Space Object (RSO). Favorability is defined by a set of constraints,
e.g., solar illumination, distance between observer and target, orbital location for viewable event. Computing viewable
conjunctions requires calculation of orbital propagation while considering constraints based on the state vectors of position,
velocity, with covariance for both satellite and RSO.

We propose two parallel lanes of effort: acceleration and research. The objective of acceleration is to avoid missed opportunities
and reduce latency for satellite maneuver requests through continuous prediction and reporting of viewable conjunctions. The effort
will begin by deploying currently available software on dedicated systems and continue with optimizing the code for high-
performance computing hardware. The research lane aims to expand RSO inspection and modeling capabilities. Among our current
research ideas are spectral characterization of RSO materials and planning multiple observations to recover RSO 3D form.

Computing resources at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) are available for the acceleration work. Laika, Maxar conjunction
prediction dashboard software, and Bluesim, Maxar orbital propagation software, are expected to be the first software in the
acceleration lane. Laike and Bluesim are to be provided by the sponsor, and output will be made accessible through its dashboard.
Deliverables will follow a gated schedule to the sponsor. ORNL will provide progressively more robust viewable conjunction
assessments from both modelled and actual ephemerides (see Figure 1).

Background
The U.S. Space Surveillance Network identifies detects, tracks, and catalogs RSOs. Their objective of conjunction assessments (CA) is
collision prediction and avoidance. The 18th and 19th Space Defense Squadrons (SDS), are responsible for monitoring man-made
objects in orbit, providing collision avoidance support for the satellite lifecycle all the way to deorbit. Due to the complex
gravitational effects and drag of Earth’s extended atmosphere on cislunar objects, RSO location and velocity information require
regular observation to correct predicted motion. These observations typically occur every 8 hours but may lag as much as 24 hours.
SDS data, software, and methodologies center on calculating intersections and near intersections of objects in orbit around the
Earth, but they can also be used to predict viewable conjunctions. Among the data they produce are RSO state vectors. State
vectors describe the location-velocity values and covariance.

The following section gives cursory explanations of the 3 most important aspects of the viewable conjunction assessments: the
orbital propagation algorithm, the varied ephemeris input, and the available software.

Algorithm
There are a few options when searching for orbital propagation algorithms. The progenitor of all such algorithms was the Simplified
General Perturbations 4 (SGP4) model developed in 1970 to propagate near-Earth satellite trajectories. Many different updates
have been made since SGP4 was made available but the underlying algorithm has remained basically the same even though
manifested in different software algorithms, refactors, and extended utilities. A notable official implementation of the SGP4
algorithm is the Special Perturbations Ephemeris propagator (SPEPH). As of 2022, it was the best-available trajectory propagation
algorithm used by the United States Space Force (USSF) and is export controlled [2]. Some notable commercial and government
capabilities are shared in Table 1 below. No High-performance computing (HPC) implementations of SGP4-like algorithms are
known.

Ephemeris Inputs
There are many different ephemeris formats but only some are appropriate for use in the viewable conjunction assessment
proposed in this document. Covariance information is required to compute uncertainties in the motion and thus a statistical
confidence in capturing acceptable RSO images. For the sake of positional accuracies, the format must include the covariance values
for position and velocity. Accuracies are highly dependent on orbit (LEO, MEO, GEO) and many other factors. The generic on-orbit
ephemeris format, the Modified ITC Ephemeris format, and the Orbital Ephemeris message (OEM) format are appropriate. They
include the position/velocity covariance matrix outlines the covariances involving positions and velocities. It is useful to explain
more about the formats that come as TLEs and VCMs.

Available Software
Many orbital propagation software tools incorporate covariance propagation to analyze and understand the uncertainty associated
with satellite orbits. Covariance models uncertainties in the estimated orbital state (position and velocity). Propagating this
covariance along with the orbit allows users to predict how these uncertainties evolve over time and understand the confidence
level of their orbit predictions and thus the probability of successful RSO imaging. A requirement is that the software processes
tracking data and generates orbit ephemeris with realistic covariance and is used for accurate orbit estimates. As seen in Table 1,
there are varied prices and extension capabilities.

Laika doesn’t host orbital calculators beyond BlueSim (Maxar), but it can be integrated with the following orbital propagation
calculators tools:
 • STK (Ansys) High-fidelity orbital modeling and visualization
 • FreeFlyer Runge-Kutta 89 [K89] and other integrators for precise propagation
 • Orekit Open-source Java library for orbital mechanics
 • GMAT NASA’s open-source mission analysis tool

Proposed Methodology and Software
Convergence predictions from the ORNL acceleration lane will be required to match the results from the sponsor’s current system.
Reducing differences in the software/algorithm we accelerate will ease this validation process. Hence, we prefer to start with the
software the sponsor is using (Laika and BlueSim). If using sponsor software proves impractical, ORNL will transition to other
available software (e.g. those from Table 1). Writing new software is a last resort, extending through commercial software APIs (e.g.
C or python). Note that the validation process requires sets of orbit propagation results to be delivered from the sponsor to ORNL.
Once validated, the focus will shift to acceleration in the HPC environment and prediction of viewable convergences. We anticipate
extending the functionality and complexity of the result, and decreasing the execution time, through HPC. This work will require and
produce respectively:

Data:

 • Collect ephemerides for several satellites and RSOs. Collection and format validation will need to be automated for real-
 time mission support but can be manual for initial validation.

 • From the sponsor, collect definitions of the constraints that define viewable conjunctions and orbit propagations for
 validation.

Deliverables:

 • Validation report

 • Computed view conjunctions, Single, multiple, and spatial/spectral observation predictions for proposed tasking.

 • Determination of hardware required for continuous VC computations output. This will begin with a baseline evaluation of
 off-the-shelf software and be updated was optimizations for CPU and GPU come online.

Proposed Investigation for High Performance Computing
As previously stated, imaging opportunities of Resident Space Objects by satellites are currently not continuously discovered. To
allow for continuous discovery we suggest:

 1. Conjunction calculations must be done quickly so that there is time to plan the imaging/tasking.
 2. Satellite pointing/tasking is often queued months in advance so to shift that schedule, we need to be as confident and have
 as much lead time as possible.
 3. The SDS observation gathering and orbit prediction must be automated, and fast
 4. There are potentially over 1 million pieces of space debris
 5. Hence, we need automated processing and sufficient hardware.

We propose prototyping and baselining existing ORNL HPC hardware with the goal of determining the hardware required for
continuous viewable conjunction predictions. These steps will include investigation into the appropriate mix of CPU and GPU
compute. The handling of data distribution and assembling for dissemination are also important aspects of the investigation and
implementation.

Proposed Investigation for Detection Enhancement
One view of a RSO from a single viewer gives a partial description of object. To produce a full spatial description of a RSO, multiple
views are required. The production of the “next best look” would be a benefit to the effort of viewable conjunctions. Requesting
the calculation of what two views of a RSO from two complementary aspects would give a more comprehensive spatial
understanding of the RSO (e.g. potential 3D model, mensuration, etc…). We propose that ORNL provides an additional capability to
existing sponsor conjunction assessment that would deliver over a period of time where multiple, complementary views of a
particular RSO can be assessed and reported so that tasking can be provided forward.
Spatial description of RSOs is valuable and additional material or thermal information about RSOs would add additional information
to more completely characterize the RSO. Many satellites that could be used for viewable conjunctions have spectral acquisition
capabilities outside of the visible range and into other electromagnetic regions. One could involve such metadata outside of the
visible region in tasking to allow for information such as material identification and thermal exchange information. Such information
could inform the viewer on answering questions such as: (1) what is the potential lifecycle of this RSO due to its rigid or
fragmentable material composition? (2) Is this RSO undergoing any thermal or electronic effects that may help to characterize its
use or functionality?
To explore the creation of 3-dimensional CAD models from multiple viewable conjunctions, prior work has been conducted by
Rochester Institute of Technology (RIT). Such construction could be considered if of interest to the sponsor. See references [5] and
[6].

Conclusion
We propose two lanes of investigation to improve viewable conjunction workflows. First, acceleration, optimization of software and
hardware to avoid missed opportunities and reduce latency. Second, research, multi-spectral and repeat viewing to produce 3D
models with component material classification.
