Nonparametric inference on Fokker-Plank and McKean-Vlasov models
Adriana Laurindo Monteiro, Roberto Imbuzeiro Oliveira

Abstract
We propose a kernel-based estimator of the velocity field governing the transport and diffusion of d-dimensional interacting particles. Assuming the initial positions are i.i.d with law μ 0 \mu_{0} , we establish consistency of the estimator with an explicit mean-squared error rate of O ( h 2 + N − 2 / ( d + 2 ) ) ) O(h^{2}+N^{-2/(d+2)})) , where h h denotes the time-discretization step and N N the number of particles. The analysis covers two distinct settings: the Fokker–Planck equation, where we recover the underlying potential function, and the McKean–Vlasov equation, where we deconvolve the interaction kernel driving the mean-field dynamics.



Keywords:
Inference on PDE, nonparametric estimation, McKean-Vlasov model, Fokker-Planck equation.

Introduction

1.1
Motivation and related work

Systems of interacting particles are of strong interest in applied mathematics.
These models can simulate the dynamics of various events in biology
(
9
)
, social sciences
(
13
)
, finance
(
11
)
and deep learning
(
2
)
. Two of the most important systems are described by famous partial differential equations: the Fokker-Planck and McKean-Vlasov.

It is worth to note that Fokker-Planck equation (
1
) (FPE) and some others evolution partial differential equations (PDE)

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\diver\Big(\mu_{t}(-\nabla V_{t}-\nabla\log\mu_{t})\Big)=0

(1)

can be viewed as a gradient flow in the Wasserstein space,
(
24
)
. This connection between the theories of optimal transport and gradient flows is a key ingredient in the study of the dynamics of stochastic gradient descent method on neural networks
(
7
)
. Here
\mu_{t}:\Pi^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
denotes the density of a random particle’s position and
V_{t}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a potential function that defines the drift term
\nabla V_{t}
and represents an external force in the motion of each particle.

A McKean-Vlasov model
(
20
)
, which can be written as the following PDE

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\diver\Big(\mu_{t}(-\nabla V_{t}+F\ast\mu_{t}-\nabla\log\mu_{t})\Big)=0,

(2)

describes phenomena, for instance, in fluid mechanics
(
19
)
and electromagnetism
(
12
)
. In this case, the function
F:\Pi^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
denotes an interaction kernel force applied to the system.

Besides using the modelling power of these PDE’s, plenty of works have the main purpose of finding the solution
\mu_{t}
. Examples include numerical methods
(
3
)
, variational methods
(
17
)
and more recently physics-informed neural-networks
(
23
)
. All of them try to directly learn or approximate the density function.

In this paper we focus in a different quantity. We are interested in estimate the velocity field that governs the evolution of the particle’s positions. Our non-parametric framework gives results for both FPE and MVE models in a unified manner. Some works in this context of statistical inference for PDE’s usually focus on one model like
(
18
)
and
(
25
)
.

In
(
25
)
they explore a property called self consistency of the FPE to estimate its solution using a neural network. Recently, the same approach is applied in more general settings such as MVE in
(
26
)
. Although the work in
(
18
)
is also based on kernel estimators, they do not discretize the time-interval and more importantly, they observe a stochastic system of interacting particles.

Also in the stochastic setting, we can cite
(
4
)
,
(
8
)
and
(
22
)
that are related to our problem. The first proposes a Nadaraya-Watson kernel estimate for the vector field very similar to the one proposed here. Although they give asymptotic distribution and consistency, they do not provide convergence rate.

(
8
)
builds a kernel-based estimator as well, based on an estimator of the density. A different approach for multidimensional inference is used in
(
22
)
, where convergence rates are given for a penalized least squares estimator of the vector field.

1.2
Setting

Let
\Pi^{d}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}/R{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
be a torus, the cube
[-R/2,R/2]^{d}
with periodic boundary conditions for some
R>0
. Consider a particle evolving in
\Pi^{d}
in the time interval
[0,T]
according to a velocity field
v_{t}
with random initial position distributed as
\mu_{0}
, an
R
-periodic density function defined in
\Pi^{d}
. Suppose the original position is
x
and
f(x,t)
denotes its position in time
t
, where
f:[0,T]\times\Pi^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. The evolution in time of the transport phenomena governed by
v_{t}
is described by the following ordinary differential equation (ODE):

\begin{cases}\frac{df(x,t)}{dt}=v_{t}(f(x,t))\\
f(x,0)=x.\end{cases}

(3)

With Lipschitz conditions on
v_{t}
, one can prove existence and unicity of a flow
X_{t}
given by integration in time

X_{t}(x)\coloneqq x+\int_{0}^{t}v_{s}(f(x,s))ds,

(4)

where the sum is considered modulus
R
. Imposing
R
-periodicity on the velocity field
v
for all
t\in[0,T]
implies the same periodicity for
X_{t}(x)-x
as pointed in
(
26
)
. Therefore, we can define for any
t\in[0,T]
an
R
-periodic push-forward density
\mu_{t}\coloneqq(X_{t})_{\#}\mu_{0}
that is a weak solution of the so called continuity equation for
v_{t}
:

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\diver(\mu_{t}v_{t})=0.

(5)

Note that the
\mu_{t}:[0,T]\times\Pi^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
are time-varying densities defined in
\Pi^{d}
. As proved respectively in
(
6
)
and
(
15
)
the FPE and MVE both have classical solution on the torus
\Pi^{d}
, in the sense that
\mu
is a smooth function in time and space variables satisfying (
5
). Besides that, as we are going to prove in section (
6
), the densities are bounded from above and from below uniformly in time. Therefore, we can assume the existence of
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\leq\mu_{t}\leq\lambda_{2}\text{ for all }t\in[0,T].

(6)

Our problem is to observe a deterministic system of
N
independent and identically distributed interacting particles and try to estimate
v_{t}
. Specifically, we have for each
i=1,\ldots,N
an ODE

\begin{cases}\frac{df(x_{i},t)}{dt}=v_{t}(f(x_{i},t))\\
f(x_{i},0)=x_{i},\end{cases}

(7)

where, for a function
f:[0,T]\times\Pi^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
f(x_{i},t)
represents the position of a particle in time
t
originally located at
x_{i}
. We will adopt the notation
X_{t}^{i}
to denote
f(x_{i},t)
, i.e., the position of a particle in time
t
that was located at
x_{i}
when
t=0.
With this notation, the random vectors
(X_{0}^{1},\ldots,X_{0}^{N})\sim\mu_{0}^{\otimes N}
are such that
(X_{0}^{1},\ldots,X_{0}^{N})=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
and the random functions
(X_{t}^{1},\ldots,X_{t}^{N})\sim\mu_{t}^{\otimes N}
, where
\mu_{t}
is the push-forward density already defined.

Our estimation method is based on given observations in specific instants of time. Considering a time-step
1
1

1

Let
t_{0}=0
and
t_{M}=T
.

h=T/M
, formally our data is

\displaystyle X_{t_{j}}^{1},\ldots,X_{t_{j}}^{N},\text{ for }j=0,1,\ldots,M

(8)

\displaystyle(X_{0}^{1},\ldots,X_{0}^{N})\sim\mu_{0}^{\otimes N}.

(9)

Note that the derivative in time of the position is given by
v_{t}
. The approximation

\displaystyle\frac{X_{t_{j+1}}^{i}-X_{t_{j}}^{i}}{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}\approx v_{t}(X_{t_{j}}^{i})

(10)

motivates the definition of a kernel-based estimator for
v_{t}
:

v_{\theta}(t_{j},x)=\frac{1}{Z_{\theta}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{\theta}(x)\frac{X_{t_{j+1}}^{i}-X_{t_{j}}^{i}}{t_{j+1}-t_{j}},\text{ for }j=0,\ldots,M-1

(11)

where
\kappa_{\theta}
and
Z_{\theta}
are respectively a kernel function and a normalizing factor to be defined later and
\theta\in(0,1)
. For simplicity, we will drop the parameter
\theta
and denote
v_{\theta}(t_{j},x)
by
\hat{v}_{t_{j}}(x)
.

Using the estimator for
v_{t}
defined in (
11
), we consider estimators for our two models, FPE and MVE. In the Fokker Planck case we have:

v(t,x)=-\nabla V_{t}(x)-\nabla\log\mu_{t}(x).

(12)

Defining an estimator for the density, the goal is to estimate the term
\nabla V_{t}
:

\widehat{\nabla V_{t_{j}}}=-\widehat{v}_{t_{j}}(x)-\widehat{\nabla\log\mu_{t_{j}}(x)}.

(13)

In the McKean-Vlasov model the velocity field is given by:

v(t,x)=-\nabla V_{t}(x)+F\ast\mu_{t}(x)-\nabla\log\mu_{t}(x),

(14)

where the convolution is defined as
\int_{\Pi^{d}}F(x-y)\mu_{t}(y)dy
.
We will consider
V_{t}\equiv 0
and the goal is to estimate
F
using deconvolution techniques:

\mathcal{F}[v_{t}+\nabla\log\mu_{t}]=\mathcal{F}[F]\cdot\mathcal{F}[\mu_{t}]\Longrightarrow\mathcal{F}[\widehat{F}]=\frac{\mathcal{F}[\hat{v}_{t}+\widehat{\nabla\log\mu_{t}]}}{\hat{\mathcal{F}}[\mu_{t}]}.

(15)

1.3
Results and organization of the paper

The main result is that our estimator is
O(h^{2}+N^{-2/(d+2)})
in mean-squared error. An advantage of the framework developed here over other approaches such as
(
25
)
and
(
4
)
is that we can compute the rate of convergence.

Theorem 1
.

(MSE OF
\hat{v}_{t_{j}}
; informal)
Fix
x\in\Pi^{d}
. For
\hat{v}_{t_{j}}
defined in (
11
), there exists a constant
C
independent of
N
and
\theta
such that

{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{v}_{t_{j}}(x)-v_{t_{j}}(x)\|^{2}\leq C(h^{2}+N^{-2/(d+2)}).

(16)

In general, inference methods for vector fields are restricted to specific PDEs, such as
(
18
)
and
(
25
)
. Since many of these equations can be recovered from the continuity equation, our result can be applied to different models in a straightforward manner, which is the content of the following two results.

Theorem 2
.

(MSE DRIFT; informal)
Fix
x\in\Pi^{d}
. For
\widehat{\nabla V_{t_{j}}}(x)
defined in (
13
), there exists a constant
C
independent of
N
and
\theta
such that

{\mathbb{E}}\|\widehat{\nabla V_{t_{j}}}(x)-\nabla V_{t_{j}}(x)\|^{2}\leq C(h^{2}+N^{-6/(d+6)}).

(17)

Although the deconvolution techniques are enough to get consistency, we do not establish a convergence rate for the McKean Vlasov case, like in
(
18
)
. The estimator here is mainly based on
(
16
)
.

Theorem 3
.

(Consistency of kernel-interaction estimator; informal)
The estimator
\hat{F}
defined in (
15
) satisfies

{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{F}-F\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=o(1)\text{ as }N\to\infty.

(18)

In Section
2
we give details on how the estimators are defined, assumptions, as well as notation. Section
3
is devoted to the main theorem: we establish an upper bound on the mean squared error of
\hat{v}_{t_{j}}
using properties of the kernel and the regularity estimates of
\mu_{t}
. Both Section
4
and
5
are concerned with applying this result on respectively estimating the potential and interaction kernel functions.
All of these findings rely on strong properties of the densities
\mu_{t}
given by the structure of the continuity equation. Norms of the derivatives up to order
2
are bounded uniformly in time, as computations in Section
6
show. Finally, Section
7
contains technical lemmas needed for calculations, such as concentration inequalities and inequalities involving moments of our kernel estimators.

2
Construction of estimators and assumptions

2.1
Kernel estimators for
v_{t}

Let
\kappa:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,+\infty)
be a kernel function with
\supp(\kappa)\subset[-R/2,R/2]^{d}
that satisfies

\displaystyle\int\kappa(u)du=1,\int u\kappa(u)du=0,\int\kappa^{2}(u)du<\infty.

(19)

If we take the parameter
0<\theta<1
and consider the function
y\mapsto\kappa_{\theta}(y)=\kappa(y/\theta)/\theta^{d}
, then
\supp(\kappa_{\theta})\subset[-R\theta/2,R\theta/2]^{d}\subset[-R/2,R/2]^{d}
.
Consider the periodized version of the kernel
\kappa_{\theta}
in the following way:

\displaystyle\tilde{\kappa_{\theta}}(x)=\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\kappa_{\theta}(x-Rz),\text{ for all }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

(20)

Note that the previous series converges since there is a unique non-zero element. Indeed, for each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
there is a unique integer such that
x-Rz\in\supp{\kappa_{\theta}}
. It is well-known that
R
-periodic functions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
can be identified with functions in the torus that satisfy the analogous properties (
19
) in
\Pi^{d}
. From now on, we are going to consider this identification and use
\kappa_{\theta}
to denote the function on torus identified with its periodized version (
20
).

Suppose also that the kernel satisfies for all
y\in\Pi^{d}

\|\nabla\kappa(y)\|\leq C_{1}|\kappa(y)|.

(21)

Example 4
.

Consider the Epanechnikov kernel supported on the interval
[-R/2,R/2]:

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}(x_{1})=a_{1}(1-x_{1}^{2})\text{1}_{|x_{1}|\leq R/2}

(22)

where
a_{1}
is the normalizing constant. We can construct a d-dimensional kernel using the spherical version of (
22
):

\displaystyle\kappa_{S}(x)=c_{S}\kappa_{1}(x^{\top}x),

(23)

where
c_{S}
is a normalizing constant. Choosing
R=2+2\sqrt{2}
,
\kappa_{S}
satisfies (
21
).

Denoting
\frac{X_{t_{j+1}}^{i}-X_{t_{j}}^{i}}{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}
by
Y_{j,i}
, define for
x\in\Pi^{d}

\kappa^{i,j}_{\theta}(x)=\kappa_{\theta}\left(x-X_{t_{j}}^{i}\right)

and

Z_{\theta}(t_{j},x):=\frac{1}{\theta^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa\left(\frac{x-X_{t_{j}}^{i}}{\theta}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa^{i,j}_{\theta}(x).

Any finite measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
can also be periodized to obtain a measure on
\Pi^{d}
. If
\mu
is a probability measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we can take

\displaystyle\nu(A)=\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\mu(A+Rz).

(24)

In the following, we are considering periodized versions of gaussians measures. We will add noise terms to each system and to each time-step of discretization:

\left\{\epsilon_{j,i}\right\}_{j,i=0,1}^{M-1,N}\overset{\text{i.i.d}}{\sim}N(0,\sigma^{2}I),\mbox{ and }\bar{\epsilon}_{j,i}\coloneqq\frac{\epsilon_{j+1,i}-\epsilon_{j,i}}{t_{j+1}-t_{j}},

where
I
is the identity matrix on
\Pi^{d}
and
\epsilon_{j,i}
are independent of
X_{t_{j}}^{i}
, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and all
j\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}
. We propose the following estimators for
j=0,1,\ldots,M-1:

\hat{v}_{t_{j}}(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}e_{1},\text{ if }Z_{\theta}(t_{j},x)=0,\\
\frac{1}{Z_{\theta}(t_{j},x)}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa^{i,j}_{\theta}(x)(Y_{j,i}+\epsilon_{j,i}),\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

and

\bar{v}_{t_{j}}(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}e_{1},\text{ if }Z_{\theta}(t_{j},x)=0,\\
\frac{1}{Z_{\theta}(t_{j},x)}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa^{i,j}_{\theta}(x)(Y_{j,i}+\bar{\epsilon}_{j,i}),\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

When
t=t_{M}
, we can define the estimators as the last time step:

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{t_{M}}(x)=\hat{v}_{t_{M-1}}(x)

(25)

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{t_{M}}(x)=\bar{v}_{t_{M-1}}(x).

(26)

Remark 5
.

Interpolation between time steps

Our estimators are defined for
t=t_{0},\ldots,t_{M-1},t_{M}
. We can interpolate them for other time steps, when
t\in(t_{j},t_{j+1})
for some
j=0,\ldots,M
as

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{t}(x)\coloneqq\hat{f}_{t_{j}}(x)+\frac{t-t_{j}}{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\hat{f}_{t_{j+1}}(x)-\hat{f}_{t_{j}}(x)).

Consistency theorems together with Lipschitz continuity in time of
f_{t}
guarantees an uniform approximation when
h\to 0
and
N\to\infty:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\mathbb{E}}|\hat{f}_{t}(x)-f_{t}(x)|^{2}\to 0.

Indeed, if
t\in(t_{j},t_{j+1})
for some
j=0,\ldots,M-1
and writing
\lambda\coloneqq\frac{t-t_{j}}{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}
:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}|\hat{f}_{t}(x)-f_{t}(x)|^{2}={\mathbb{E}}|(1-\lambda)\hat{f}_{t_{j}}(x)+\lambda\hat{f}_{t_{j+1}}(x)-f_{t}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1-\lambda)^{2}{\mathbb{E}}|\hat{f}_{t_{j}}(x)-f_{t_{j}}(x)|^{2}+C\lambda^{2}{\mathbb{E}}|\hat{f}_{t_{j+1}}(x)-f_{t_{j+1}}(x)|^{2}+C|f_{t_{j}}(x)-f_{t}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;+C\lambda^{2}|f_{t_{j+1}}(x)-f_{t_{j}}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq O(h^{2}+N^{-2/d+2})+2CL^{2}h^{2}.

2.2
Density and derivative density estimators

Consider the following estimators of the density
\mu_{t_{j}}
and of its derivative, respectively, for
j=0,1,\ldots,M
and
x\in\Pi^{d}
:

\hat{\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N\theta^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa\left(\frac{x-X_{t_{j}}^{i}}{\theta}\right)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{\theta}^{i,j}(x).

(27)

and

\widehat{\nabla\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N\theta^{d+1}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla\kappa\left(\frac{x-X_{t_{j}}^{i}}{\theta}\right)=\frac{1}{N\theta}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla\kappa_{\theta}^{i,j}(x).

(28)

When
t\in(t_{j},t_{j+1})
for some
j=0,\ldots,M
, we are going to linearly interpolate it between time-steps as we did in Remark
5
and the uniform approximation follows by Remark
6

2.3
Deconvolution estimator

For a function
f:\Pi^{d}\to\Pi^{d}
with
f^{i}\in L^{1}(\Pi^{d})
and a measure
\nu
on
\Pi^{d}
, consider respectively its Fourier transforms

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[f](m)=\left(\int_{\Pi^{d}}f^{1}(x)\exp(-2\pi im^{\top}x)dx,\ldots,\int_{\Pi^{d}}f^{d}(x)\exp(-2\pi im^{\top}x)dx\right),m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\nu](m)=\int_{\Pi^{d}}\exp(-2\pi im^{\top}x)d\nu(x),m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

The Fourier transform of the empirical measure given by
\mu_{t_{j}}
is

\hat{\mathcal{F}}[\mu_{t_{j}}](u)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\exp(-iu^{\top}X_{t_{j}}^{i}),\text{ for }u\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

(29)

Let
\ell_{s}(u)\coloneqq(1+\|u\|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}
, for
s\geq 0
and
A_{s}=\left\{u\in\Pi^{d}\mid\left|\frac{\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}[\mu_{t_{j}}](u)}{\ell_{s}(u)}\right|^{2}\geq\alpha\right\}
for
\alpha>0
.
The estimator of the interaction kernel is going to be defined using a regularized version of its Fourier transform:

{\mathcal{F}}[\widehat{F^{i}}]_{s}\coloneqq\frac{{\mathcal{F}}[\hat{v}_{t_{j}}+\widehat{\nabla\log\mu_{t_{j}}}]\cdot\overline{\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}[\mu_{t_{j}}]}}{|\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}[\mu_{t_{j}}]|^{2}}\cdot\text{1}_{A_{s}}.

(30)

When we apply this estimator in Section
5
, we will need conditions that guarantee that the denominator in this expression does not vanish.

2.4
Assumptions

We work under some smoothness assumptions on the velocity field and on the initial density
\mu_{0}.

2.4.1
Assumption 1

The vector field is Lipschitz in space and time with constant
L
(also called bi-Lipschitz):

\|v_{t}(x)-v_{k}(y)\|\leq L\cdot\left(|t-k|+\|x-y\|\right).

(31)

This assumption guarantees Lipschitz continuity in space uniformly in time. Then we get existence and uniqueness of global solution of the ODE and the flow
X_{t}^{i}
is well-defined.

2.4.2
Assumption 2

Also, for regularity results of the density we need the velocity field to satisfy for any space-time variables
x\in\Pi^{d}
and
t\in[0,T]

\max\left\{\|\nabla\diver v_{t}(x)\|,\|\nabla v_{t}(x)\|_{op}\right\}\leq L_{v}

(32)

and the initial density needs to satisfy for all
x\in\Pi^{d}

\|\nabla\log\mu_{0}(x)\|\leq C.

(33)

2.4.3
Assumption 3

For high-order estimates, we need boundedness of high-order derivatives. Suppose that for any space-time variables
x\in\Pi^{d}
and
t\in[0,T]

\max\left\{\|\nabla^{2}\diver v_{t}(x)\|_{op},\|\nabla^{2}v_{t}(x)\|_{op},\right\}\leq L_{v}.

(34)

and the initial density needs to satisfy for all
x\in\Pi^{d}

\|\nabla^{2}\log\mu_{0}(x)\|\leq C.

(35)

Remark 6
.

The assumptions on the vector field
v_{t}
and the density
\mu_{t}
imply Lipschitz continuity of
\mu_{t}
in time. Indeed, for
t_{1}<t_{2}
in
[0,T]
and
x\in\Pi^{d}
fixed

\displaystyle|\mu(t_{1},x)-\mu(t_{2},x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}|\partial_{s}\mu(s,x)|ds

(36)

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}|\mu(s,x)\diver v_{s}(x)+\langle\nabla\mu(t,x),v_{s}(x)\rangle|ds

(37)

\displaystyle\leq|t_{1}-t_{2}|\cdot\sup_{t,x}|\diver v_{s}(x)|\cdot|\mu(t,x)|+\sup_{t,x}\|\nabla\mu(t,x)\|\cdot\|v_{s}(x)\|

(38)

\displaystyle\leq C|t_{1}-t_{2}|.

(39)

3
Estimation of the vector field

Our main theorem explores the structure given by the continuity equation and the kernel estimators to obtain an explicit rate of convergence of
\hat{v}_{t_{j}}
in mean squared error.

Theorem 7
.

(MSE OF
\hat{v}_{t_{j}}
)
Fix
x\in\Pi^{d}
. Assume that Assumptions 1 and 2 holds,
h=t_{j+1}-t_{j}
is the size of time discretization and the kernel
\kappa
satisfies
\supp(\kappa)\subset[-R/2,R/2]^{d}
.
Additionally, consider
0<\theta\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}/\sqrt{2}
.
Then there exists a constant
C
independent of
N
and
\theta
such that

Lemma 9
.

Define the function
\delta:(0,+\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
as follows

\delta(h)\coloneqq\frac{X(t_{j}+h,x)-X(t_{j},x)}{h}-v_{t_{j}}(X(t_{j},x)),

where

is the flux defined by equation (

\displaystyle\|\delta(h)\|\leq 2LCh.

(58)

Proof.

Integrating in time (

\displaystyle\frac{X(t_{j}+h,x)-X(t_{j},x)}{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j}+h}v_{s}(X(s,x))ds.

Rewriting the function

we have

\displaystyle\|\delta(h)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j}+h}\left\|v_{s}(X(s,x))-v_{t_{j}}(X(t_{j},x))\right\|ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\cdot(h+\sup_{s\in[t_{j},t_{j}+h]}\left\|X(s,x)-X(t_{j},x)\right\|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\cdot\left(h+\sup_{s\in[t_{j},t_{j}+h]}\left\|h\cdot\frac{X(s,x)-X(t_{j},x)}{h}\right\|\right)

(61)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\cdot h\left(1+\sup_{s\in[t_{j},t_{j}+h]}\left\|\frac{X(s,x)-X(t_{j},x)}{h}\right\|\right).

(62)

Write

\displaystyle\|\delta(h)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\cdot h\left(1+\sup_{r\in[0,h]}\left\|\frac{X(t_{j}+r,x)-X(t_{j},x)}{r}\right\|\right)

(63)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L\cdot h\left(1+\sup_{r\in[0,h]}\left\|\delta(r)+v_{t_{j}}(X(t_{j},x))\right\|\right)

(64)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\cdot h\left(1+\sup_{r\in[0,h]}\left\|\delta(r)\|+\|v_{t_{j}}(X(t_{j},x))\right\|\right).

4
Potential estimator: Fokker-Planck case

Depending on the vector field
v_{t}
in (
5
), we can end up with different PDE’s. An important one is Fokker-Planck Equation (FPE):

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\diver(\mu_{t}(-\nabla V_{t}))=\Delta\mu_{t},

obtained when

v_{t}(x)=-\nabla V_{t}(x)-\nabla\log\mu_{t}(x).

(74)

Using our estimator for
v_{t}
and the expression (
74
), we can now build an estimator for the potential term
\nabla V_{t}
:

\widehat{\nabla V_{t}}(x)=-\widehat{v}_{t}(x)-\widehat{\nabla\log\mu_{t}(x)}.

In order to bound MSE of
\widehat{\nabla V_{t}}
, note that

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\nabla V_{t}}(x)-\nabla V_{t}(x)\|=\|-\widehat{v}_{t}(x)-\widehat{\nabla\log\mu_{t}(x)}-(-v_{t}(x)-\nabla\log\mu_{t}(x))\|

(75)

\displaystyle\leq\|\widehat{v}_{t}(x)-v_{t}(x)\|+\|\widehat{\nabla\log\mu_{t}(x)}-\nabla\log\mu_{t}(x)\|.

(76)

Again using
\|a+b\|^{2}\leq 2\|a\|^{2}+2\|b\|^{2}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\widehat{\nabla V_{t}}(x)-\nabla V_{t}(x)\|^{2}\leq 2{\mathbb{E}}\|\widehat{v}_{t}(x)-v_{t}(x)\|^{2}+2{\mathbb{E}}\|\widehat{\nabla\log\mu_{t}(x)}-\nabla\log\mu_{t}(x)\|^{2}.

(77)

Since we already have bounded the first term, it remains to do the same for the second one.

To do this, define the following estimator of the derivative of log-density for
j=0,\ldots,M:

\displaystyle\widehat{\nabla\log\mu_{t_{j}}(x)}=\widehat{\left(\frac{\nabla\mu_{t_{j}}(x)}{\mu_{t_{j}}(x)}\right)}\coloneqq\begin{cases}0,\text{ if }Z_{\theta}(t_{j},x)=0,\\
\frac{1}{\theta}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla\kappa\left(\frac{x-X_{t_{j}}^{i}}{\theta}\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa\left(\frac{x-X_{t_{j}}^{i}}{\theta}\right)},\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

(78)

When
t\in(t_{j},t_{j+1})
for some
j=0,\ldots,M
, we are going to linearly interpolate it between time-steps as we did in Remark
5
and the uniform approximation also holds by Remark
23
.

The idea is to work with the numerator and denominator separately. This section is mainly based in Chapter 3 of
(
10
)
.

4.1
Kernel density estimator

Consider the following estimator of the density
\mu_{t_{j}}
, for
j=0,\ldots,M
:

\hat{\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\kappa_{\theta}^{i,j}(x).

(79)

When
t\in(t_{j},t_{j+1})
for some
j=0,\ldots,M
, we are going to linearly interpolate it between time-steps as we did in Remark
5
.

Lemma 10
.

(MSE kernel density estimator)
Suppose that
\int_{\Pi^{d}}u\kappa(u)du=0
and
\eta=\int_{\Pi^{d}}\kappa^{2}(u)du<\infty.
Then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\hat{\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)-\mu_{t_{j}}(x)\big]^{2}\leq\frac{C_{2}^{2}\theta^{4}}{4}\|\nabla^{2}\mu_{t_{j}}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\frac{\|\mu_{t_{j}}\|_{\infty}}{N\theta^{d}}\eta,\text{ for }j=0,\ldots,M.

(80)

Proof.

First we need to bound the bias term:

\displaystyle\bias\hat{\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)={\mathbb{E}}\hat{\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)-\mu_{t_{j}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\theta^{d}}\int_{\Pi^{d}}\kappa\left(\frac{x-y}{\theta}\right)\mu_{t_{j}}(y)dy-\mu_{t_{j}}(x)

(81)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Pi^{d}}\kappa(u)\mu_{t_{j}}(x-\theta u)du-\mu_{t_{j}}(x)

(82)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Pi^{d}}\kappa(u)\left(\mu_{t_{j}}(x-\theta u)-\mu_{t_{j}}(x)\right)du.

(83)

By the Taylor expansion of
\mu_{t_{j}}
and using the mean zero assumption on the kernel
\kappa
, there exists a constant
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\hat{\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)-\mu_{t_{j}}(x)\leq\frac{C_{2}\theta^{2}}{2}\|\nabla^{2}\mu_{t_{j}}\|_{\infty}.

(84)

For the variance, we have:

\displaystyle\V\kappa\left(\frac{x-X_{t_{j}}^{i}}{\theta}\right)\leq{\mathbb{E}}\kappa^{2}\left(\frac{x-X_{t_{j}}^{i}}{\theta}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Pi^{d}}\kappa^{2}\left(\frac{x-y}{\theta}\right)\mu_{t_{j}}(y)dy

(85)

\displaystyle=\theta^{d}\int_{\Pi^{d}}\kappa^{2}(u)\mu_{t_{j}}(x-\theta u)du

(86)

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{d}\|\mu_{t_{j}}\|_{\infty}\int_{\Pi^{d}}\kappa^{2}(u)du

(87)

so

\displaystyle\V\hat{\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{N^{2}\theta^{2d}}\V\kappa\left(\frac{x-X_{t_{j}}^{i}}{\theta}\right)

(88)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mu_{t_{j}}\|_{\infty}}{N\theta^{d}}\eta.

(89)

Since
{\mathbb{E}}\big[\hat{\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)-\mu_{t_{j}}(x)\big]^{2}=\bias^{2}\hat{\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)+\V\hat{\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\hat{\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)-\mu_{t_{j}}(x)\big]^{2}\leq\frac{C_{2}^{2}\theta^{4}}{4}\|\nabla^{2}\mu_{t_{j}}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\frac{\|\mu_{t_{j}}\|_{\infty}}{N\theta^{d}}\eta.

(90)

∎

4.2
Kernel derivative density estimator

Consider the following estimator of the gradient of the density
\mu_{t_{j}}
:

\widehat{\nabla\mu}_{t}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N\theta}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla\kappa_{\theta}^{i,j}(x),\text{ for }j=0,\ldots,M.

(91)

When
t\in(t_{j},t_{j+1})
for some

, we are going to linearly interpolate it between time-steps as we did in Remark
5
. Lipschitz continuity of (

) is obtained by Lemma

and Remark

Lemma 11
.

(MSE kernel derivative density estimator)
Suppose the kernel is supported in the torus
\Pi^{d}
.

Then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\widehat{\nabla\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)-\nabla\mu_{t_{j}}(x)\|^{2}\leq R^{2}\theta^{2}\|\nabla^{2}\mu_{t_{j}}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\frac{\|\mu_{t_{j}}\|_{\infty}}{N\theta^{d+2}}\eta.

(92)

Proof.

The mean vector can be written using the partial derivatives with respect to
y
, instead of
x
:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\widehat{\nabla\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{\theta^{d+1}}\left(\int\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}\kappa\left(\frac{x-y}{\theta}\right)\mu_{t_{j}}(y)dy,\ldots,\int\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{d}}\kappa\left(\frac{x-y}{\theta}\right)\mu_{t_{j}}(y)dy\right)

(93)

In order to use Divergence Theorem, define the vector field

H(y)=\left(\kappa\left(\frac{x-y}{\theta}\right),0,\ldots,0\right).

Note that

\displaystyle\diver H(y)=\frac{-1}{\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}\kappa\left(\frac{x-y}{\theta}\right)

(94)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\mu_{t_{j}}(y),H(y)\rangle=\kappa\left(\frac{x-y}{\theta}\right)\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}\mu_{t_{j}}(y)

(95)

\displaystyle\diver(\mu_{t_{j}}H)(y)=\kappa\left(\frac{x-y}{\theta}\right)\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}\mu_{t_{j}}(y)+\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}\kappa\left(\frac{x-y}{\theta}\right)\mu_{t_{j}}(y)

(96)

Using Divergence Theorem, the boundary term in the integral vanishes:

\displaystyle\int_{\Pi^{d}}\diver(\mu_{t_{j}}H)(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Pi^{d}}\langle(\mu_{t_{j}}H)(y),\hat{n}\rangle dy

(97)

\displaystyle=0,

(98)

where
\hat{n}
is the outward unit normal vector. Then,

\displaystyle 0=\int\diver(\mu_{t_{j}}H)(y)dy=\int\mu_{t_{j}}(\diver H(y))dy+\int\langle\nabla\mu_{t_{j}}(y),H(y)\rangle dy

(99)

which gives

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{\theta}\int\mu_{t_{j}}(y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}\kappa\left(\frac{x-y}{\theta}\right)dy=-\int\kappa\left(\frac{x-y}{\theta}\right)\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}\mu_{t_{j}}(y)dy.

(100)

Repeating the same argument for each coordinate,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\widehat{\nabla\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\theta^{d+1}}\left(\theta\int\kappa\left(\frac{x-y}{\theta}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}\mu_{t_{j}}(y)dy,\ldots,\theta\int\kappa\left(\frac{x-y}{\theta}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{d}}\mu_{t_{j}}(y)dy\right)

(101)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\theta^{d}}\int\kappa\left(\frac{x-y}{\theta}\right)\nabla\mu_{t_{j}}(y)dy.

(102)

Therefore, changing variables

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\widehat{\nabla\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)-\nabla\mu_{t_{j}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Pi^{d}}\kappa\left(u\right)(\nabla\mu_{t_{j}}(x-\theta u)-\nabla\mu_{t_{j}}(x))du

and Mean Value Inequality

\displaystyle\|{\mathbb{E}}\widehat{\nabla\mu}_{t_{j}}(x)-\nabla\mu_{t_{j}}(x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq R^{2}\theta^{2}\|\nabla^{2}\mu_{t_{j}}\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Finally, the variance is bounded by using (

5
Kernel-interaction estimator: McKean-Vlasov case

In this section we are going to consider the McKean-Vlasov equation. Now,
v_{t}=F\ast\mu_{t}-\nabla\log\mu_{t}
where
F:\Pi^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and the goal is to estimate
F
using deconvolution techniques provided this quotient is properly defined:

\mathcal{F}[v_{t}]=\mathcal{F}[F]\cdot\mathcal{F}[\mu_{t}]\Longrightarrow\mathcal{F}[\widehat{F}]=\frac{\mathcal{F}[\hat{v}_{t}+\widehat{\nabla\log\mu_{t}}]}{\hat{\mathcal{F}}[\mu_{t}]}.

(120)

This estimator is an adaptation to the torus of
(
16
)
estimator. It is important to say that although the Fourier transform on the torus gives a sequence, nor a function, the same useful properties of Fourier transforms for functions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
still holds, as one can check in
(
14
)
.

Note that
F
is constant in the time variable, which means that we can choose
t^{*}\in{t_{j_{0}},t_{j_{1}},\ldots,t_{j_{M}}}
and define for
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

\hat{\mathcal{F}}[\mu_{t^{*}}](u)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\exp(-iu^{\top}X_{kt^{*}}).

(121)

This approach seeks an upper bound on the Fourier transform of
F
, which can be reduced to a bound on the Fourier transform of its coordinate functions. Indeed, since

\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Pi^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|F^{i}\|_{L^{2}(\Pi^{d},{\mathbb{R}})},

(122)

it is sufficient to obtain a bound on
F^{i}.

As mentioned in
(
16
)
, the inverse operation of a convolution is not continuous. Then, in order to make this problem well posed, we are going to regularize the denominator in (
120
). To do so, let us consider the well-known Sobolev space
H_{s}
defined by:

H_{s}\coloneqq\left\{f\in L^{2}(\Pi^{d})\mid\|f\|_{s}^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{u\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\ell_{s}^{2}(u)|\mathcal{F}[f](u)|^{2}<\infty\right\},

(123)

where
\ell_{s}(u)\coloneqq(1+\|u\|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}
, for
s\geq 0.
When
f\in L^{2}(\Pi^{d})
, the Fourier transform is an isometry:
\|\mathcal{F}[f]\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
.
Note that
\|\cdot\|_{s}^{2}\geq\|\cdot\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi^{d})}
, since
\ell_{s}^{2}(u)\geq 1
for all
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.
Therefore, upper bounding
{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{F^{i}}-F^{i}\|^{2}_{s}
means upper bounding
{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{F^{i}}-F^{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Pi^{d})}
.

Take
\alpha>0
and consider the set
A_{s}=\left\{u\in\Pi^{d}\mid\left|\frac{\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}[\mu_{t^{*}}](u)}{\ell_{s}(u)}\right|^{2}\geq\alpha\right\}
. We are going to adopt the notation
\hat{g}_{t}=\hat{v}_{t^{*}}+\widehat{\nabla\log\mu_{t^{*}}}
and define the estimator of
F^{i}
through its regularized Fourier transform:

{\mathcal{F}}[\widehat{F^{i}}]_{s}\coloneqq\frac{{\mathcal{F}}[\hat{g}_{t^{*}}^{i}]\cdot\overline{\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}[\mu_{t^{*}}]}}{|\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}[\mu_{t^{*}}]|^{2}}\cdot\text{1}_{A_{s}}.

(124)

The regularized version of
{\mathcal{F}}[F^{i}]
is the following:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}[{F^{i\alpha}}]\coloneqq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}[F^{i}]\cdot\text{1}_{A_{s}}

(125)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathcal{F}}[g_{t^{*}}^{i}]\cdot\overline{{\mathcal{F}}[\mu_{t^{*}}]}}{|{\mathcal{F}}[\mu_{t^{*}}]|^{2}}\cdot\text{1}_{A_{s}}.

(126)

Our previous results give us that
{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{g}_{t^{*}}^{i}-g_{t^{*}}^{i}\|_{L^{2}}=o(1)
as
N\to\infty
and

(127)

Proof.

(129)

(130)

) goes to zero.

In order to use Dominated Convergence Theorem, let us see that this function is dominated:

6
Regularity estimates of
\mu_{t}

In order to obtain bounds on derivatives of
\log\mu_{t}(x)
that are used in this chapter, we need the following definition:

Definition 18
.

A density
\mu
is called
C-
good if for any
x\in\Pi^{d}

\left\{\|\nabla\log\mu(x)\|,\|\nabla^{2}\log\mu(x)\|_{op}\right\}\leq C.

(148)

The following result is derived from the proof of Lemma 8 in
(
25
)
. It guarantees boundedness of the gradient and the Hessian of the log probability of all instants of time.

Theorem 19
.

Suppose that
\mu_{0}
is
L_{0}-
good. Given
T>0
, let the vector field
v_{t}
satisfies for any
x\in\Pi^{d}
and any
t\in[0,T]

\{\|\nabla\diver v_{t}(x)\|,\|\nabla^{2}\diver v_{t}(x)\|_{op}\}\leq L_{v}

(149)

and

\{\|\nabla v_{t}(x)\|_{op},\|\nabla^{2}v_{t}(x)\|_{op}\}\leq L_{v}.

(150)

Then for all
t\in(0,T]
,
\mu_{t}
is
L_{t}-
good, where
L_{t}
depends on
L_{0}
and
L_{v}
.
As a consequence, we obtain for all
x\in\Pi^{d}

\displaystyle\{\|\nabla\mu_{t}(x)\|,\|\nabla^{2}\mu_{t}(x)\|_{op}\}\leq C_{t}.

(151)

Proof.

To prove that
\nabla\log\mu_{t}
is bounded, we can use the integral form of Gronwall’s inequality since by Proposition 2 in
(
25
)
,

\nabla\log\mu_{t}(x)=\nabla\log\mu_{0}(x(0))-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla\diver v_{s}(x(s))ds-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla v_{s}(x(s))^{\top}\nabla\log\mu_{s}(x(s))ds.

With assumptions
148
and
149
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\log\mu_{t}(x)\|\leq(L_{0}+tL_{v})\exp(tL_{v})\leq(L_{0}+TL_{v})\exp(TL_{v})\eqqcolon P_{1}.

(152)

Together with the previous inequality and property
159
, we get for free an upper bound of the gradient of the density:

\displaystyle P_{1}\geq\|\nabla\log\mu_{t}(x)\|=\left\|\frac{\nabla\mu_{t}(x)}{\mu_{t}(x)}\right\|\Longrightarrow\|\nabla\mu_{t}(x)\|\leq\lambda_{2}\cdot P_{1}\eqqcolon M_{1}.

(153)

For the second order term, we need Proposition 3 in
(
25
)
:

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\log\mu_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\nabla^{2}\log\mu_{0}(x(0))-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla^{2}\diver v_{s}(x(s))ds-\int_{0}^{t}(\nabla^{2}\log\mu_{s}(x(s)))^{\top}J[v_{s}(x(s))]ds

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}(J[v_{s}(x(s))])^{\top}\nabla^{2}\log\mu_{s}(x(s))ds-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla^{2}v_{s}(x(s))\otimes_{1}\nabla\log\mu_{s}(x(s))ds

so if
B_{1}=(L_{0}+TL_{v})\exp(TL_{v})

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}\log\mu_{t}(x)\|_{op}

\displaystyle\leq L_{0}+tL_{v}+2\int_{0}^{t}L_{v}\|\nabla^{2}\log\mu_{s}(x(s))\|_{op}ds+\int_{0}^{t}L_{v}(L_{0}+tL_{v})\exp(tL_{v})ds

\displaystyle=L_{0}+tL_{v}(1+B_{1})+2L_{v}\int_{0}^{1}\|\nabla^{2}\log\mu_{s}(x(s))\|_{op}ds.

By Gronwall,

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}\log\mu_{t}(x)\|_{op}\leq(L_{0}+tL_{v}(1+B_{1}))\exp(2tL_{v})\leq(L_{0}+TL_{v}(1+B_{1}))\exp(2TL_{v})\eqqcolon P_{2}.

We can also get for free an upper bound of the Hessian of the density. Indeed,

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}\mu_{t}(x)\|_{op}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{2}\left\|\frac{\nabla^{2}\mu_{t}(x)}{\mu_{t}(x)}\right\|_{op}

(154)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{2}\left\|\frac{\nabla^{2}\mu_{t}(x)}{\mu_{t}(x)}-\frac{(\nabla\mu_{t}(x))^{\top}\nabla\mu_{t}(x)}{\mu_{t}^{2}(x)}\right\|_{op}+\lambda_{2}\|\frac{(\nabla\mu_{t}(x))^{\top}\nabla\mu_{t}(x)}{\mu_{t}^{2}(x)}\|_{op}

(155)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{2}\|\nabla^{2}\log\mu_{t}(x)\|_{op}+\lambda_{2}\left\|\frac{(\nabla\mu_{t}(x))^{\top}\nabla\mu_{t}(x)}{\mu_{t}^{2}(x)}\right\|_{op}

(156)

\displaystyle\leq(\lambda_{2}P_{2}+\lambda_{2}\frac{M_{1}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}).

(157)

∎

Using fewer hypothesis than the last theorem, we obtain boundedness of
\mu_{t}
uniformly in time.

Corollary 20
.

Suppose that

\|\nabla\log\mu_{0}(x)\|\leq L_{0}\mbox{ and }\max\{\|\nabla\diver v_{t}(x),\nabla v_{t}(x)\|\}\leq L_{v}.

(158)

Then for for all
t\in[0,T]
and all
x\in\Pi^{d}
, there existss positive constants
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
such that

\lambda_{1}\leq\mu_{t}(x)\leq\lambda_{2}.

(159)

Proof.

With these assumptions we get boundedness of
\|\nabla\log\mu_{t}(x)\|
. It implies by Mean Value Inequality that
\log\mu_{t}(x)
is L-Lipschitz. Since
\Pi^{d}
is bounded, we get for
x,y\in\Pi^{d}:

-L\diam\Pi^{d}\leq\log\frac{\mu_{t}(x)}{\mu_{t}(y)}\leq L\diam\Pi^{d}.

Using
\int_{\Pi^{d}}\mu_{t}(y)dy=1,
we get

\exp(-L\diam\Pi^{d})\leq\mu_{t}(x)Leb(\Pi^{d})\leq\exp(L\diam\Pi^{d}).

Note that
\diam\Pi^{d}=R\sqrt{d}
and
Leb(\Pi^{d})=R^{d}.

∎

We can control the first and second moments of

Lemma 21

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{2}\|\kappa\|_{\infty}}{\theta^{2d}}\int_{\Pi^{d}}\kappa\left(\frac{-y}{\theta}\right)dy

Lemma 22
.

)

Lemma 23

For

\displaystyle+\nabla\mu_{t}(x)\diver v_{t}(x)+\mu_{t}(x)\nabla\diver v_{t}(x)

(179)

\displaystyle\leq C.

(180)

Note that we can exchange the order of time and space derivatives since the density
