GTMDs, orbital angular momentum, and pretzelosity
Brean Maynard, Peter Schweitzer

Abstract
The leading Generalized Transverse Momentum Dependent parton distributions (GTMDs) are studied in the bag model. The model description is shown to be theoretically consistent. The orbital angular momentum is studied in terms of the GTMD F 1 , 4 q F_{1,4}^{q} and Ji sum rule. Analytical proofs of the associated sum rules are given. A deeper relationship between orbital angular momentum and the pretzelosity TMD is established in this model.

2
Definition and properties of leading GTMDs

In quark models with no gluon degrees of freedom, the fully unintegrated
quark-quark correlator is defined as
[
2
]

W_{ss^{\prime}}^{[\Gamma]q}\left(P,k,\Delta\right)=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{4}z}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{ik\cdot z}\bra{p^{\prime},s^{\prime}}\bar{\psi}^{q}(-\tfrac{z}{2})\,\Gamma\,\psi^{q}(\tfrac{z}{2})\ket{p,s}

(1)

where
P=\frac{1}{2}(p^{\prime}+p)
,
\Delta=p^{\prime}-p
, and nucleon states are normalized covariantly as

\langle p^{\prime},s^{\prime}|p,s\rangle=2p^{0}\,\delta^{(3)}(\vec{p}^{\,\prime}-\vec{p}^{\,})\delta_{s^{\prime}s}
. We define the light-cone components of a generic
four-vector
a^{\mu}
as
a^{\mu}=(a^{+},\vec{a}_{T},a^{-})
with
a^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(a^{0}\pm a^{3})
.
In this notation, the GTMDs related to
\Gamma=\gamma^{+}
are defined as
[
2
]

\displaystyle{\cal M}^{[\gamma^{+}]q}_{ss^{\prime}}\,

\displaystyle=\iint dk^{+}dk^{-}\,\delta(k^{+}-xP^{+})W_{ss^{\prime}}^{[\gamma^{+}]q}(P,k,\Delta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2M}\bar{u}\left(p^{\prime},s^{\prime}\right)\left[F_{1,1}^{q}+\frac{i\sigma^{i+}k_{T}^{i}}{P^{+}}F_{1,2}^{q}+\frac{i\sigma^{i+}\Delta_{T}^{i}}{P^{+}}F_{1,3}^{q}+\frac{i\sigma^{ij}k_{T}^{i}\Delta_{T}^{j}}{M^{2}}F_{1,4}^{q}\right]u(p,s),

(2)

where the nucleon spinors are normalized as

\bar{u}(p,s^{\prime})u(p,s)=2M\delta_{s^{\prime}s}
and
M
denotes the
nucleon mass.
The GTMDs are in general complex valued and depend on the 5 variables

x,\;\xi,\;\vec{k}_{T}^{2},\;\vec{k}_{T}\cdot\vec{\Delta}_{T},\;\vec{\Delta}_{T}^{2}

with
\xi
and
t
defined as
[
2
]

\xi=-\,\frac{1}{2}\;\frac{\Delta^{+}}{P^{+}}=\frac{p^{+}-p^{\prime+}}{p^{+}+p^{\prime+}}\,,\quad\quad t=\Delta^{2}=-\,\frac{\xi^{2}(4M^{2}+\vec{\Delta}_{T}^{\,2})}{1-\xi^{2}}-\vec{\Delta}_{T}^{\,2}\,.

(3)

The variable
t
will be needed below for GPDs.

GTMDs posses multiple properties of particular interest.
Hermiticity of the correlator implies the property
[
2
]

F_{1,j}^{q\,*}(x,\xi,\vec{k}_{T}^{2},\vec{k}_{T}\cdot\vec{\Delta}_{T},\vec{\Delta}_{T}^{2})=\pm F_{1,j}^{q}(x,-\xi,\vec{k}_{T}^{2},-\vec{k}_{T}\cdot\vec{\Delta}_{T},\vec{\Delta}_{T}^{2})\quad\text{with}\quad\begin{cases}+&\text{for}\ j=1,\;3,\;4,\\
-&\text{for}\ j=2.\end{cases}

(4)

In the forward limit
\Delta^{\mu}\to 0
, the variables

\xi,\;\vec{k}_{T}\cdot\vec{\Delta}_{T},\;\vec{\Delta}_{T}^{2}
become zero.
In this limit, the GTMD
F_{1,1}^{q}
is related to the unpolarized TMD

[
77
]
as follows
[
2
]

F_{1,1}^{q}(x,0,\vec{k}_{T}^{2},0,0)=f_{1}^{q}(x,\vec{k}_{T}^{2}).

(5)

Integrating out transverse quark momenta, the GTMDs
F_{1,1}^{q}
,
F_{1,2}^{q}
,
F_{1,3}^{q}

give rise to the unpolarized GPDs as follows

\displaystyle H^{q}(x,\xi,t)=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\vec{k}_{T}\left[F_{1,1}+2\xi^{2}\left(\frac{\vec{k}_{T}\cdot\vec{\Delta}_{T}}{\vec{\Delta}_{T}^{2}}F_{1,2}^{q}+F_{1,3}^{q}\right)\right]

(6a)

\displaystyle E^{q}(x,\xi,t)=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\vec{k}_{T}\left[-F_{1,1}^{q}+2\left(1-\xi^{2}\right)\left(\frac{\vec{k}_{T}\cdot\vec{\Delta}_{T}}{\vec{\Delta}_{T}^{2}}F_{1,2}^{q}+F_{1,3}^{q}\right)\right].

(6b)

The GTMD
F_{1,4}^{q}
is of particular interest because upon integration over
x
and
transverse quark momenta with the weight
\vec{k}_{T}^{2}/M^{2}
it gives access to the contribution
of quark orbital momentum to the nucleon spin according to
[
7
]

{\mathcal{L}}_{z}^{q}=-\int\limits_{-1}^{1}dx\int d^{2}k_{T}\;\frac{\vec{k}_{T}^{\,2}}{M^{2}}\,F_{1,4}^{q}\left(x,0,\vec{k}_{T}^{\,2},0,0\right)\,,

(7)

which is often referred to as the canonical or Jaffe-Manohar orbital angular
momentum because, through a gauge-invariant extension, it is related to the
Jaffe-Manohar nucleon spin decomposition
[
78
]
.

By choosing the Dirac matrices
\Gamma=\gamma^{+}\gamma_{5}
or
i\sigma^{j+}\gamma_{5}

yields respectively the leading GTMDs
G_{1,j}
with
1\leq j\leq 4
or
H_{1,j}

with
1\leq j\leq 8
. Choosing Dirac matrices other than that yields subleading
GTMDs
[
2
]
. In this work, we will focus on the GTMDs related to

\Gamma=\gamma^{+}
and feature one of the GTMDs related to
\Gamma=i\sigma^{j+}\gamma_{5}
.
A study of all GTMDs in the bag model will be presented elsewhere
[
79
]
.

3
The bag model

In the bag model,
N_{c}=3
non-interacting quarks are confined in a color singlet state
within a spherical cavity (“bag”) of radius
R
by means of appropriate boundary conditions

[
45
,
46
,
47
]
. The single-quark ground state wave function
for a massless quark in momentum space is given by

\Psi_{s}(t,\vec{k})=e^{-i\varepsilon_{0}t}\,A\binom{t_{0}(k)\chi_{s}}{t_{1}(k)\,\vec{k}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\,\chi_{s}},

(8)

where
\varepsilon_{0}=\omega_{0}/R
is the ground-state energy,

\omega_{0}\approx 2.04
is the lowest positive solution of the
transcendental equation
\omega=(1-\omega)\tan{\omega}
, and

\sigma^{i}
are the Pauli matrices with
\chi_{s}
denoting the
two-component Pauli spinors.
The functions
t_{\ell}
for
\ell=0,1
are defined in terms of
spherical Bessel functions
j_{\ell}
as follows

t_{\ell}(k)=\frac{\int_{0}^{1}du\;u^{2}j_{\ell}(ukR)j_{\ell}(u\omega_{0})}{k^{\ell}}\,.

(9)

Notice that we define
t_{1}(k)
with a factor of
1/k
which differs
from what is often used in literature such that in our work
t_{0}(k)
is
dimensionless while
t_{1}(k)
has dimension 1/mass. This definition
will simplify the notation later on. The wave function is normalized as

\int d^{3}k\;\Psi_{s^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\vec{k})\Psi_{s}(\vec{k})=A^{2}\int d^{3}k\bigl(t_{0}^{2}+\vec{k}^{2}t_{1}^{2}\bigr)\delta_{ss^{\prime}}=\delta_{ss^{\prime}}

(10)

with the normalization constant

A=\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigl[\bigl(\omega_{0}R^{3}\bigr)/\bigl((\omega_{0}-1)j_{0}^{2}(\omega_{0})\bigr)\bigr]^{\!1/2}
.

The bag radius is fixed in terms of the nucleon mass
M
via the
relation
M=\frac{4}{3}N_{c\,}\epsilon_{0}
. The calculations are conveniently
carried out for “flavorless” quarks with the flavor dependence
assigned based on the SU(4) spin-flavor symmetry by means of the
spin-flavor factors
[
80
]
, with
N_{q}
for spin-independent and

P_{q}
for spin-dependent nucleon matrix elements, given for the proton by
(with
u
and
d
being interchanged for the case of the neutron)

N_{u}=\frac{N_{c}+1}{2},\quad N_{d}=\frac{N_{c}-1}{2},\quad P_{u}=\frac{N_{c}+5}{6},\quad P_{d}=\frac{-N_{c}+1}{6}\,.

(11)

For more detailed introductions to the model, we refer to Refs.
[
45
,
46
,
47
]
.

4
Calculation of GTMDs in the model

In the bag model, it is convenient to use the Breit frame where

P^{\mu}=(P^{0},0,0,0)
and
\Delta^{\mu}=(0,\vec{\Delta})
and

t=-\vec{\Delta}^{2}
.
In order to ensure the validity of the Ji sum rule in the bag model,
we will work in the large-
N_{c}
limit
[
52
,
76
]

where
M={\cal O}(N_{c})
and
P^{0}=M+{\cal O}(N_{c}^{-1})
and we will neglect

{\cal O}(N_{c}^{-1})
corrections. The behavior of the variables is

k^{i}_{T}={\cal O}(N_{c}^{0})\,,\quad\Delta^{i}_{T}={\cal O}(N_{c}^{0})\,,\quad x={\cal O}(N_{c}^{-1})\,,\quad\xi=-\Delta^{3}/(2M)={\cal O}(N_{c}^{-1})\,,\quad t={\cal O}(N_{c}^{0})\,.

(12)

The expressions in Eq. (
2
) can be understood as

2\times 2
matrices in nucleon helicity indices. In order to evaluate
these expressions, it is convenient to take traces with the unit and
Pauli matrices. Evaluating in this way in the large
N_{c}
limit the nucleon
spinor expressions on the right-hand side of Eq. (
2
) for
“flavorless quarks” yields the results

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\rm tr}_{\rm spin}\left[{\cal M}^{[\,\gamma^{+}]}\;\mathds{1}\;\right]

\displaystyle=\phantom{-\,}F_{1,1}\,,

(13a)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\rm tr}_{\rm spin}\left[{\cal M}^{[\gamma^{+}]}\;\sigma_{x}\right]

\displaystyle=\phantom{-\,}\frac{ik_{y}}{M}\,F_{1,2}+\frac{i\Delta_{y}}{M}\,F_{1,3}\,,

(13b)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\rm tr}_{\rm spin}\left[{\cal M}^{[\gamma^{+}]}\;\sigma_{y}\right]

\displaystyle=-\,\frac{ik_{x}}{M}\,F_{1,2}\,-\,\frac{i\Delta_{x}}{M}\,F_{1,3}\,,

(13c)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\rm tr}_{\rm spin}\left[{\cal M}^{[\gamma^{+}]}\;\sigma_{z}\right]

\displaystyle=\phantom{-\,}\frac{i\bigl(k_{x}\Delta_{y}-k_{y}\Delta_{x}\bigr)}{M^{2}}\,F_{1,4}\,,

(13d)

where we write for more clarity
\vec{k}=(k_{x},k_{y},k_{z})
and similarly for

\vec{\Delta}
and neglect terms suppressed in large-
N_{c}
limit.
Taking into account the quark flavor factors (
11
),
the large-
N_{c}
scaling of GTMDs obtained from this expansion reads

F_{1,1}^{q}=N^{q}\mathcal{O}(N_{c}),\qquad F_{1,2}^{q}=P^{q}\mathcal{O}(N_{c}^{2}),\qquad F_{1,3}^{q}=P^{q}\mathcal{O}(N_{c}^{2}),\qquad F_{1,4}^{q}=P^{q}\mathcal{O}(N_{c}^{3}).

(14)

Note that different flavor combinations have different
N_{c}
-orders.
In unpolarized case, the isoscalar flavor combination
N^{u}+N^{d}=N_{c}
is
leading while the isovector flavor combination
N^{u}-N^{d}=1
is subleading.
In polarized case it is vice versa with
P^{u}+P^{d}=1
and
P^{u}-P^{d}=\frac{N_{c}+2}{3}
.
Our choice to apply the large-
N_{c}
expansion in
Eq. (
13
) before projecting on definite
flavors simplifies the model evaluation and corresponds to the scheme
used in
[
76
]
.
1
1

1

In an alternative
large-
N_{c}
scheme, one could include the SU(4) spin-flavor
in Eq. (
13
) before expanding the
spinor expressions. Then one would proceed with separate model
calculations for the
(u\pm d)
cases. In the strict large-
N_{c}

limit, both schemes yield the same results.

The correlator on the left-hand side of Eq. (
2
)
is given in the bag model (for flavorless quarks) in the large-
N_{c}
limit
by the expression

{\cal M}^{[\gamma^{+}]}_{ss^{\prime}}=M\;\bar{\phi}_{s^{\prime}}(\vec{k}+\tfrac{1}{2}\vec{\Delta})(\gamma^{0}+\gamma^{3})\phi_{s}(\vec{k}-\tfrac{1}{2}\vec{\Delta})\Bigr|_{k_{z}=xM-\epsilon_{0}}\,.

(15)

where we indicate that
k_{z}=xM-\varepsilon_{0}
is fixed.
Evaluating this expression in the model yields the results

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\rm tr}_{\rm spin}\left[\,{\cal M}^{[\gamma^{+}]}\;\mathds{1}\;\right]

\displaystyle=MA^{2}\Bigl[t_{0}^{-}t_{0}^{+}+(k_{z}-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{z})t_{1}^{-}t_{0}^{+}+(k_{z}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{z})t_{0}^{-}t_{1}^{+}+(\vec{k}^{\,2}-\tfrac{1}{4}\,\vec{\Delta}^{2})t_{1}^{-}t_{1}^{+}\Bigr]_{k_{z}=xM-\epsilon_{0}}\,,

(16a)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\rm tr}_{\rm spin}\left[{\cal M}^{[\gamma^{+}]}\,\sigma_{x}\right]

\displaystyle=MA^{2}\Bigl[i\,k_{y}(t_{0}^{-}t_{1}^{+}-t_{1}^{-}t_{0}^{+}-\Delta_{z}t_{1}^{-}t_{1}^{+})+i\,\Delta_{y}(\tfrac{1}{2}t_{0}^{-}t_{1}^{+}+\tfrac{1}{2}t_{1}^{-}t_{0}^{+}+k_{z}t_{1}^{-}t_{1}^{+})\Bigr]_{k_{z}=xM-\epsilon_{0}}\,,

(16b)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\rm tr}_{\rm spin}\left[{\cal M}^{[\gamma^{+}]}\,\sigma_{y}\right]

\displaystyle=MA^{2}\Bigl[i\,k_{x}(t_{1}^{-}t_{0}^{+}-t_{0}^{-}t_{1}^{+}+\Delta_{z}t_{1}^{-}t_{1}^{+})-i\,\Delta_{x}(\tfrac{1}{2}t_{0}^{-}t_{1}^{+}+\tfrac{1}{2}t_{1}^{-}t_{0}^{+}+k_{z}t_{1}^{-}t_{1}^{+})\Bigr]_{k_{z}=xM-\epsilon_{0}}\,,

(16c)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\,{\rm tr}_{\rm spin}\left[{\cal M}^{[\gamma^{+}]}\,\sigma_{z}\right]

\displaystyle=MA^{2}\Bigl[-\,i\,(k_{x}\Delta_{y}-k_{y}\Delta_{x})t_{1}^{+}t_{1}^{-}\Bigr]_{k_{z}=xM-\epsilon_{0}}

(16d)

where we introduced the notation

t_{\ell}^{\pm}=t_{\ell}\left(|\vec{k}\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\vec{\Delta}|\right)\,.

(17)

Comparing the coefficients in
Eqs. (

16
)
we obtain the following results for the GTMDs

\displaystyle F_{1,1}^{q}

\displaystyle=N^{q}A^{2}M\;\Bigl[t_{0}^{-}t_{0}^{+}+k_{z}(t_{0}^{-}t_{1}^{+}+t_{1}^{-}t_{0}^{+})+\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{z}(t_{0}^{-}t_{1}^{+}-t_{1}^{-}t_{0}^{+})+(\vec{k}^{\,2}-\tfrac{1}{4}\,\vec{\Delta}^{2})t_{1}^{-}t_{1}^{+}\Bigr]_{k_{z}=xM-\epsilon_{0}}\,,

(18a)

\displaystyle F_{1,2}^{q}

\displaystyle=P^{q}A^{2}M^{2}\Bigl[\;t_{0}^{-}t_{1}^{+}-\;t_{1}^{-}t_{0}^{+}-\Delta_{z}t_{1}^{-}t_{1}^{+}\Bigr]_{k_{z}=xM-\epsilon_{0}}\,,

(18b)

\displaystyle F_{1,3}^{q}

\displaystyle=P^{q}A^{2}M^{2}\Bigl[\tfrac{1}{2}t_{0}^{-}t_{1}^{+}+\tfrac{1}{2}t_{1}^{-}t_{0}^{+}+k_{z}t_{1}^{-}t_{1}^{+}\Bigr]_{k_{z}=xM-\epsilon_{0}}\,,

(18c)

\displaystyle F_{1,4}^{q}

\displaystyle=P^{q}A^{2}M^{3}\Bigl[-t_{1}^{+}t_{1}^{-}\Bigr]_{k_{z}=xM-\epsilon_{0}}\,.

(18d)

5
Orbital angular momentum from GTMD
F_{1,4}^{q}

It is customary to define an
x
-dependent orbital angular momentum distribution
function
{\mathcal{L}}_{z}^{q}(x)
in terms of
F_{1,4}^{q}
with
\xi\to 0
and
\Delta_{T}^{i}\to 0

as follows

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{z}^{q}(x)=-\int d^{2}k_{T}\;\frac{\vec{k}_{T}^{\,2}}{M^{2}}\,F_{1,4}^{q}\left(x,0,\vec{k}_{T}^{\,2},0,0\right)\,,

(22)

such that upon integration over
x
, one obtains
{\mathcal{L}}_{z}^{q}=\int dx\,{\mathcal{L}}_{z}^{q}(x)

in Eq. (
7
). When evaluating
x
-integrals in the bag model,
it is convenient to proceed as follows (
R(\vec{k})
below denotes a generic
function of
\vec{k}
and
\vec{\Delta}
)

M\int\limits_{-1}^{1}\!dx\int\!d^{2}k_{T}\bigr[R(\vec{k})\bigl]_{k_{z}=xM-\epsilon_{0}}=M\int\limits_{-1}^{1}\!dx\int\!d^{3}k\,R(\vec{k})\delta(k_{z}+\epsilon_{0}-xM)=\int\!d^{3}k\,{R}(\vec{k})\,.

(23)

The final result is obtained by substituting
x\to y=xM
and using the large-
N_{c}

limit to replace the integration interval
-M\leq y\leq M
by the whole
y
-axis.
In this way, using the bag model expression for

A different (historically earlier) way to define quark orbital angular momentum

7
Proof of Ji sum rule

There is an important difference between the spin sum rule in Eq. (
28
)
and the Ji sum rule. In order to illustrate this, we present here explicit analytical
proofs of the quark flavor, momentum and Ji sum rule which are given by

\displaystyle\int dxf_{1}^{q}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N^{q},

(33)

\displaystyle\int dx\sum_{q}x\,f_{1}^{q}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,

(34)

\displaystyle\int dx\;x(H^{q}+E^{q})(x,0,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2J^{q}.

(35)

To evaluate the quark flavor sum rule (
33
)
we proceed as in Eq. (
23
) whereby
an odd term (under
k_{z}\to-k_{z}
) drops out, and we obtain

\int dxf_{1}^{q}(x)=N^{q}A^{2}\int d^{3}k\bigl(t_{0}^{2}+\vec{k}^{2}t_{1}^{2}\bigr)

which proves the sum rule (
33
) owing to the
wave function normalization in Eq. (
10
).
The proof of the spin sum rule in Eq. (
28
) is conceptually
similar in that it can be traced back to the normalization of the quark
wave function. In other words, in the bag model the proofs of these two
sum rules rely on the normalization of the quark wave function in
Eq. (
10
) and SU(4) spin-flavor factors. This alone is
not sufficient to prove the sum rules (
34
)
and (
35
).

In order to evaluate the momentum sum rule, we use the expression for
the unpolarized TMD in Eq. (
20
) and carry out steps which
are analogous to those in Sec.
5
, which yields

\int dx\;x\,f_{1}^{q}(x)=\frac{N^{q}A^{2}}{M}\;\int d^{3}k\Bigl[\epsilon_{0}(t_{0}^{2}+\vec{k}^{\,2}t_{1}^{2})+\tfrac{2}{3}k^{2}t_{0}t_{1}\Bigr].

(36)

In order to proceed, we explore the equation of motion which is given
in coordinate space by
(i\not{\partial}-m_{q})\Psi_{s}(t,\vec{r})=0

for
|\vec{r}^{\,}|<R
where

\Psi_{s}(t,\vec{r})=\int d^{3}k\,e^{-i(\epsilon_{0}t-\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r})}\phi(\vec{k})

is the Fourier transform of the flavorless wave function in Eq. (
8
)
(we use the same notation for the function and its Fourier transform) and
m_{q}
is the quark
mass which we will eventually set to zero. With the understanding that
\Psi_{s}(t,\vec{r})=0

for
|\vec{r}^{\,}|>R
the following trivial identity

\int d^{3}r\,F(t,\vec{r})\Gamma(i\not{\partial}-m_{q})\Psi_{s}(t,\vec{r})=0

(37)

holds for any function
F(t,\vec{r})
and any Dirac matrix
\Gamma
. Choosing

F({t,}\vec{r})=\bar{\Psi}_{s^{\prime}}(t,\vec{r})=\int d^{3}k^{\prime}\,e^{i(\epsilon_{0}t-\vec{k}^{\prime}\!\cdot\vec{r})}\bar{\phi}(\vec{k}^{\prime})

and
\Gamma=\mathds{1}
yields (so far we kept
m_{q}\neq 0
for didactic
purposes to indicate the Dirac equation, but now we set it to zero)

\displaystyle\;\;\int\!\frac{d^{3}r}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\bar{\Psi}_{s^{\prime}}(t,\vec{r})(i\not{\partial}-m_{q})\Psi_{s}(t,\vec{r})=\int\!\!d^{3}k\,\bar{\phi}_{s^{\prime}}(\vec{k})(\epsilon_{0}\gamma^{0}-\vec{k}\cdot\vec{\gamma})\phi_{s}(\vec{k})=A^{2}\!\!\int\!\!d^{3}k\Bigl[\epsilon_{0}(t_{0}^{2}+\vec{k}^{\,2}t_{1}^{2})-2k^{2}t_{0}t_{1}\Bigr]\delta_{ss^{\prime}}=0,

which implies the identity

\int d^{3}k\,[2k^{2}t_{0}t_{1}]=\epsilon_{0}\int d^{3}k(t_{0}^{2}+\vec{k}^{\,2}t_{1}^{2})
.
Making use of this identity in Eq. (
36
) yields

\int dx\;x\,f_{1}^{q}(x)=N^{q}\,\frac{4\,\epsilon_{0}}{3M}\,A^{2}\!\!\int d^{3}k\Bigl[t_{0}^{2}+\vec{k}^{\,2}t_{1}^{2}\Bigr]=\frac{N^{q}}{N_{c}}

(38)

where in the last step we used Eq. (
10
) and
M=\frac{4}{3}N_{c\,}\epsilon_{0}
.
Summing over flavors yields the momentum sum rule (
34
).

Turning our attention to the Ji spin sum rule, we first note that

H^{q}(x,0,0)
and
E^{q}(x,0,0)
both contain a contribution from
f_{1}^{q}(x)
but with
opposite signs, see Eqs. (
32a
,
32b
).
Thus, the only piece in these GPDS that could have been literally related to the
momentum sum rule cancels out exactly in the Ji spin sum rule which appears to be
a manifestly genuine off-forward effect in the sense that nothing known from the
forward case – not even in a simple quark model – can help one to prove this sum
rule. However, we shall see that upon invoking an additional ingredient, namely the
explicit consideration of off-forward kinematics, a proof of the Ji spin sum rule
can be formulated analogously to the momentum sum rule. Note that both sum rules
are ultimately rooted in the conservation of the energy momentum tensor,
but in complimentary ways.

Starting with the model expressions for
H^{q}(x,0,0)
and

E^{q}(x,0,0)
in Eqs. (
32
) and using symmetry arguments yields

\int dx\,x\bigl(H^{q}(x,0,0)+E^{q}(x,0,0)\bigr)=P^{q}A^{2}\,\int d^{3}k\Bigl[\tfrac{2}{3}\,\epsilon_{0}k(t_{0}t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{0}^{\prime}t_{1})+2\epsilon_{0}t_{0}t_{1}+\tfrac{2}{3}\,\vec{k}^{2}t_{1}^{2}\Bigr].

(39)

Next we use Eq. (
37
) with

F({t,}\vec{r})=\bar{\Psi}_{s^{\prime}}(t,\vec{r})\,e^{-i\vec{\Delta}\cdot\vec{r}}
and

\Gamma=\gamma^{3}
. After evaluating the model expression and tracing with the
unit matrix in nucleon spin indices (and setting
m_{q}=0
) we obtain the identity

\displaystyle K(\vec{\Delta}^{\,})=A^{2}\,\int d^{3}k\Bigl[2k_{z}\bigl(-t_{0}^{-}t_{0}^{+}+\epsilon_{0}t_{0}^{+}t_{1}^{-}-\epsilon_{0}t_{0}^{-}t_{1}^{+}+t_{1}^{-}t_{1}^{+}\bigr)+\Delta_{z}\bigl(t_{0}^{-}t_{0}^{+}-\epsilon_{0}t_{0}^{+}t_{1}^{-}-\epsilon_{0}t_{0}^{-}t_{1}^{+}+t_{1}^{-}t_{1}^{+}\bigr)\Biggr]=0\,.

(40)

Taking the derivative of the above expression with respect to
\Delta_{z}

and setting
\vec{\Delta}
to zero yields the identity

\frac{\partial K(\vec{\Delta}^{\,})}{\partial\Delta_{z}}\biggl|_{\vec{\Delta}=0}=A^{2}\int d^{3}k\biggl[t_{0}^{2}-2\epsilon_{0}t_{0}t_{1}+\tfrac{1}{3}k^{2}t_{1}^{2}+\tfrac{2}{3}\epsilon_{0}kt_{0}^{\prime}t_{1}-\tfrac{2}{3}\epsilon_{0}kt_{0}t_{1}^{\prime}\biggr]=0\,.

(41)

Multiplying Eq. (
41
) by
P^{q}
and adding this “zero”
to Eq. (
39
) we obtain

\int dx\,x\bigl(H^{q}(x,0,0)+E^{q}(x,0,0)\bigr)=P^{q}A^{2}\,\int d^{3}k\Bigl[t_{0}^{2}+\vec{k}^{2}t_{1}^{2}\Bigr]=P^{q}\,,

(42)

where we made use of Eq. (
10
) in the final step. This
proves the Ji sum rule in the bag model.

Note that in the proof of the momentum sum rule (
34
),
we had to make use of the bag model expression for the
nucleon mass
M=\frac{4}{3}N_{c\,}\epsilon_{0}
. It is instructive to recall how this
result is obtained. Namely, it follows from minimizing the nucleon mass as a function
of
R
,
M(R)=N_{c}\epsilon_{0}+\frac{4}{3}\pi R^{3}B
where
\epsilon_{0}=\omega_{0}/R
and
B

is the bag constant. The result
M=\frac{4}{3}N_{c\,}\epsilon_{0}
follows from setting

M^{\prime}(R)=0
and eliminating
B
. The condition
M^{\prime}(R)=0
is the virial theorem in the
bag model and is equivalent to the von Laue condition, which is ultimately connected to
the conservation of the energy-momentum tensor in the bag model
[
76
]

and in general theory
[
89
]
.

This illustrates the important difference between the spin (
28
) and
quark flavor (
33
) sum rules on the one hand and the momentum
(
34
) and Ji (
35
) sum rules on the other hand.
The former merely require the normalization condition for the quark wave functions
(
10
), i.e. are based on the mere construction of quark flavor
and spin quantum numbers within the SU(4) spin-flavor symmetry underlying this model.
The latter require in addition to that the use of the bag model equation of motion for
the single quarks,
(i\not{\partial}-m_{q})\Psi_{s}(t,\vec{r})=0
, i.e. explicitly
invokes the dynamics of the model. For the momentum sum rule, one further ingredient is
needed, namely the determination of the nucleon mass,
M^{\prime}(R)=0
, where all
N_{c}
quarks
collectively contribute along with the bag constant.

Finally, we discuss the sum rule for the anomalous gravitomagnetic moment
related to the energy-momentum tensor form factor
B(t)

[
11
]
. Based on the
results for the momentum sum rule (
38
) and Ji sum rule
(
42
), we obtain

B^{q}(0)=\int dx\,x\,E^{q}(x,0,0)=P^{q}-\frac{N^{q}}{N_{c}}\,.

(43)

Numerically, we obtain the results
B^{u}(0)=\frac{2}{3}
and
B^{d}(0)=-\frac{2}{3}

for the proton and three colors. Summing over all flavors in the bag model, we
find that the total anomalous gravitomagnetic moment of the nucleon vanishes,

B(0)=\sum_{q}B^{q}(0)=0\,,

(44)

which is expected and equivalent to the Ji sum rule
[
11
]
.

At this point, it is instructive to comment on the large-
N_{c}
limit, which we
used when deriving the results in (
13

Thereafter, we included in Eq. in (

(

One could be concerned if this is a consistent scheme. In fact, other schemes
could be applied as well, see footnote

and we have explicitly demonstrated that our results correctly satisfy them.
Moreover, inserting the spin-flavor factors (

(45)

This shows that one may use our scheme or truncate the expansion after leading
(or subleading) order. This will affect the numerical values for
B^{q}(0)
, but
the total anomalous gravitomagnetic moment of the proton is always zero, and
the Ji sum rule is always correctly satisfied.

8
Orbital angular momentum and the pretzelosity GTMD

The pretzelosity TMD
h_{1T}^{\perp q}(x,k_{T}^{2})
is related in certain quark models
to orbital angular momentum. It is instructive to briefly review the model
studies which lead to this insight. In the light-cone representation and taking
into account effects of Melosh-Wigner rotations, it was observed in
Ref.
[
36
]
that the difference between the transversity PDF
h_{1}^{q}(x)

and helicity PDF
g_{1}^{q}(x)
is related to quark orbital angular momentum.
In Ref.
[
35
]
it was shown that, in the bag model,
the difference of helicity and transversity TMDs is given in terms
of the transverse moment of pretzelosity TMD,

g_{1}^{q}(x,k_{T}^{2})-h_{1}^{q}(x,k_{T}^{2})=h_{1T}^{\perp(1)q}(x,k_{T}^{2})
where

h_{1T}^{\perp(1)q}(x,k_{T}^{2})=\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{2M^{2}}h_{1T}^{\perp q}(x,k_{T}^{2})
.
This in turn paved the way to the insight that pretzelosity is related
to quark orbital angular momentum as
[
37
]

{\mathcal{L}}_{z}^{q}=-\int dx\int d^{2}\vec{k}_{T}\;\frac{\vec{k}_{T}^{\,2}}{2M^{2}}\,h_{1T}^{\perp q}(x,\vec{k}^{2}_{T})\,.

(46)

This result is supported in the
light-cone SU(6) quark-spectator model
[
37
]
,
bag model
[
38
]
,
covariant parton model
[
39
,
40
]
,
light-front constituent quark model
[
7
]
, and
chiral quark-soliton model restricted to three-quark sector
[
7
]
.
Models like the spectator model of Ref.
[
43
]
or the light-front
models of Refs.
[
44
]
do not
support Eq. (
46
). Spherical symmetry of quark wave functions
was shown to be an important prerequisite for Eq. (
46
) to hold
in a quark model
[
42
]
. In models where this symmetry is not
present and in QCD, Eq. (
46
) is not supported
[
3
]
.

Although not general, it is nevertheless interesting to ask the question why
the Eq. (
46
) holds in certain models. The observation that
some model symmetry must be present
[
42
]
sheds light on a necessary
technical requirement. But it would be interesting to gain further insights. For that
we inspect the
x
-dependence. In the bag model, the expression for the pretzelosity TMD
(rewritten in the notation of this work) is given by

[
38
]

h_{1T}^{\perp{q}}(x,\vec{k}_{T}^{\,2})=P^{q}A^{2}M^{3}\bigl[\,-2t_{1}^{2}\,\bigr].

(47)

Comparing to the model expression of the GTMD

F_{1,4}^{q}(x,\,\xi,\,\vec{k}_{T}^{\,2},\vec{k}_{T}\cdot\vec{\Delta}_{T},\,\vec{\Delta}_{T}^{\,2})

in Eq. (
18d
) in the limit
\xi\to 0
and
\vec{\Delta}_{T}\to 0
, we find in the
bag model the following relation

2F_{1,4}^{q}(x,0,\vec{k}_{T}^{\,2},0,0)=h_{1T}^{\perp q}(x,\vec{k}_{T}^{\,2}).

(48)

This is an interesting observation. It shows that the relation is deeper than what one
might conclude from the mere observation that the same model expression
{\mathcal{L}}_{z}^{q}
results
from integrating
2F_{1,4}^{q}(x,0,\vec{k}_{T}^{\,2},0,0)
and
h_{1T}^{\perp}(x,\vec{k}_{T}^{\,2})

over
dx
and
d^{2}k_{T}
in Eqs. (
7
,
46
). In fact, the relation holds
at the level of the integrands in Eqs. (
7
,
46
) before integrations
over
x
and transverse parton momenta are carried out.

At this point, one may wonder whether the relation of pretzelosity and orbital angular momentum
in the bag model could be still deeper. In fact, the pretzelosity TMD can be obtained as the
forward limit of one of the GTMDs related to the Dirac structure
\Gamma=i\sigma^{j+}\gamma_{5}
.
This Dirac structure defines 8 leading GTMDs
H_{1,l}^{q}
with
l=1,\;\dots\,,\;8
. The GTMD
containing the pretzelosity TMD as a forward limit is
H_{1,4}^{q}
, and the relation is given by

h_{1T}^{\perp{q}}(x,\vec{k}_{T}^{2})=H_{1,4}^{q}(x,0,\vec{k}_{T}^{2},0,0)\,.

(49)

In order to investigate if there is deeper relation, it is instructive
to derive the bag model expression for
H_{1,4}^{q}
by repeating the steps
in Eqs. (
13
-
18
) for

\Gamma=i\sigma^{j+}\gamma_{5}
. Details of this calculation
(and results for the other
H_{1,l}^{q}
,
l\neq 4
)
will be reported elsewhere
[
79
]
. Here we shall content ourselves
with quoting the result for
H_{1,4}^{q}
which is given by

H_{1,4}^{q}=P^{q}A^{2}M^{3}\Bigl[-2\,t_{1}^{+}\,t_{1}^{-}\Bigr].

(50)

In the forward limit, we reproduce Eq. (
49
)
from (
50
).
A comparison of Eqs. (
18d
) and (
50
)
yields the relation

H_{1,4}^{q}(x,\,\xi,\,\vec{k}_{T}^{\,2},\vec{k}_{T}\cdot\vec{\Delta}_{T},\,\vec{\Delta}_{T}^{\,2})=2F_{1,4}^{q}(x,\,\xi,\,\vec{k}_{T}^{\,2},\vec{k}_{T}\cdot\vec{\Delta}_{T},\,\vec{\Delta}_{T}^{\,2}).

(51)

This is the desired result revealing a deeper connection: in the bag model,
pretzelosity appears to be connected to orbital angular momentum because the
GTMDs
F_{1,4}^{q}
and
H_{1,4}^{q},
as functions of all
5
variables, are related to
each other according to Eq. (
51
). To the best of our knowledge,
this relation has not been observed in a model before.

It will be interesting to explore along the lines of Ref.
[
42
]

whether some symmetry of quark wave functions might be a necessary (sufficient)
condition for the existence of the relation (
51
) in a model,
and whether there are potentially other models which support such a relation.

Conclusions

We presented a study of the leading GTMDs
F^{q}_{1,1}
,
F^{q}_{1,2}
,
F^{q}_{1,3}
,
F^{q}_{1,4}

associated with
\Gamma=\gamma^{+}
in the bag model in the large-
N_{c}
limit.
The results satisfy Hermiticity properties and correctly reproduce known relations to TMDs and GPDs
with
F^{q}_{1,1}
relating to the TMD
f_{1}^{q}(x,\vec{k}_{T}^{2})
and
F^{q}_{1,1}
,
F^{q}_{1,2}
,
F^{q}_{1,3}

relating to the GPDs
H^{q}(x,\xi,t)
and
E^{q}(x,\xi,t)
. This demonstrates, within
the limitations of the model, the theoretical consistency of the bag model.
This includes the polynomiality property, which was not explicitly discussed in
this work, but verified in
[
52
,
54
]
.

Only
F^{q}_{1,4}
was studied previously in the bag model
[
59
]
.
Our results for
F^{q}_{1,4}
are in agreement with those of Ref.
[
59
]
.
To the best of our knowledge, the GTMDs
F^{q}_{1,1}
,
F^{q}_{1,2}
,
F^{q}_{1,3}
have not been
studied in the bag model before and constitute novel results. We applied the obtained results
to study the orbital angular momentum distributions as defined via
F^{q}_{1,4}
and the
Ji sum rule. As observed in prior model studies, the
x
-dependencies of these two partonic
distributions differ, but the integrated contributions
{\mathcal{L}}_{z}^{q}
coincide, which is expected
in quark models. The numerical results for the
u
- and
d
-quark contribution to
{\mathcal{L}}_{z}^{q}

agree with what is typically found in quark models at low hadronic scales. We furthermore
obtained several interesting new insights.

One interesting new insight is based on our analytical proofs for the total quark angular momentum
sum rule for the
J^{q}
contribution to the nucleon spin
(i) as the sum of quark orbital angular momentum (obtained via
F_{1,4}^{q}
) and quark spin
(obtained via the helicity PDF
g_{1}^{q}
) which is often referred to as the Jaffe-Manohar spin sum rule, and
(ii) using the Ji spin sum rule.
Both spin sum rules were previously tested numerically in the bag model within an accuracy of better than

1\,\%
, the former in Ref.
[
59
]
and the latter in Refs.
[
52
,
76
,
59
]
.

Analytical proofs show something numerical tests cannot reveal.
The Jaffe-Manohar spin sum rule is satisfied in the bag model based on quark wave function
normalization and spin-flavor assignment in SU(4) spin-flavor symmetry and is analogous to
the quark flavor sum rule, which can be proven in a similar way.
The proof of the Ji sum rule is more sophisticated,
and requires the explicit use of the equation of motion,
i.e. it invokes the dynamics in the model. From this point of view, the proof of the Ji spin
sum rule resembles that of the momentum sum rule. It would be interesting to see whether similar
distinctions between Jaffe-Manohar and Ji sum rules can also be made in other models.

Another interesting insight concerns the connection between orbital angular momentum and the
pretzelosity TMD. It has been observed in several quark models that the
x
-integral of the
(1)-transverse moment of the pretzelosity TMD is related to
{\mathcal{L}}_{z}^{q}
. In this work, we have
shown that this relation is deeper and holds on the level of the
x
-integrands. In fact,
in the bag model, the pretzelosity TMD
h_{1T}^{\perp q}(x,\vec{k}{}^{2}_{T})
coincides with

2F_{1,4}^{q}(x,0,\vec{k}{}^{2}_{T},0,0)
in the forward limit. Finally, we went one step further
and demonstrated that, in the bag model, the relation
H_{1,4}^{q}=2F_{1,4}^{q}
holds even when
all the GTMD variables
x
,
\xi
,
\vec{k}{}^{2}_{T}
,
\vec{k}{}_{T}\cdot\vec{k}{}^{2}_{T}
and

\vec{\Delta}{}^{2}_{T}
are respectively kept non-zero or are not integrated over.
Here,
H_{1,4}^{q}
is the GTMD which contains the pretzelosity TMD as a limiting case in the
forward limit. To the best of our knowledge, this extension of the relation between pretzelosity
and orbital angular momentum in terms of GTMDs has not been encountered in model studies before.

It is important to keep in mind that models have limitations. Such relations can be found unsupported
in quark models exhibiting a lesser degree of symmetry or can be spoiled by relaxing certain model
assumptions. Models with gauge field degrees of freedom cannot be expected to support such relations,
and in QCD all GTMDs are independent functions and describe complementary aspects of nucleon structure.
However, quark models have been found to catch some gross features of TMDs and GPDs observed in
phenomenology or lattice QCD studies. It would be therefore interesting to see whether this might be
true also in the case of the GTMDs
F_{1,4}^{q}
and
H_{1,4}^{q}
— at least in some region of the variables,
for instance, valence-like
x
, small
\vec{k}{}_{T}^{2}<M^{2}
or moderate
\vec{\Delta}{}_{T}^{2}<M^{2}
.

It will be therefore interesting to see if our findings can be confirmed in other quark model studies
and perhaps even in lattice QCD — exactly or as approximate relations. The ultimate testing ground
will be provided by phenomenological studies. The path to rigorous factorization proofs and phenomenological
tests of GTMDs is long. Until then such model relations can be helpful guidelines to prepare the first
estimates for GTMD-related observables. We can look forward to learn more about non-perturbative properties
of GTMDs from models.
