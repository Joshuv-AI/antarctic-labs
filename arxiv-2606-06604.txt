On the Absolute Geometry of Spec ⁡ 𝐙 \operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z} and the Fargues-Fontaine curve
Alain Connes, Caterina Consani

Abstract
A guiding principle in P. Scholze’s p p -adic geometry asserts that the points of Spec ⁡ 𝐙 \operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z} over an algebraically closed perfectoid field of characteristic p p are classified, up to equivalence, by its untilts. In this paper, we give a concrete geometric realization and a generalization of this paradigm. We construct the absolute 𝐅 1 \mathbf{F}_{1} -arithmetic curve ( Spec ⁡ 𝐙 ) 𝐅 1 (\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}} by pulling back the 𝐅 1 \mathbf{F}_{1} -structure sheaf of the arithmetic site to Spec ⁡ ( 𝐙 ) \operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf{Z}) . We demonstrate that the absolute curve ( Spec ⁡ 𝐙 ) 𝐅 1 (\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}} provides a common geometric origin for fundamental structures in p p -adic Hodge theory, complex analytic geometry, and the adelic scaling site. The moduli space of points of ( Spec ⁡ 𝐙 ) 𝐅 1 (\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}} over an arbitrary perfectoid field C C , modulo intrinsic symmetries, canonically parameterizes the space of all perfectoid fields with the same tilt, providing a universal, characteristic-independent geometric realization of Scholze’s heuristic. Evaluating the points of ( Spec ⁡ 𝐙 ) 𝐅 1 (\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}} over the field 𝐂 \mathbf{C} of complex numbers reveals, at each prime p p , that the non-trivial points canonically form two principal homogeneous spaces (torsors) over the Weil groups W p = 𝐐 p × W_{p}=\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times} and W ∞ = 𝐂 × W_{\infty}=\mathbf{C}^{\times} . Quotienting the archimedean orbit by the discrete Frobenius symmetries yields the complex Tate curve with modulus q = p − 1 q=p^{-1} . We show that this elliptic curve canonically decomposes as the product of its real locus—which exactly recovers the adelic periodic orbit C p = 𝐑 + × / p 𝐙 C_{p}=\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}/p^{\mathbf{Z}} —and a p p -independent phase space that emerges naturally as a real analogue of the Fargues–Fontaine curve. In contrast, equipping the same stalk with the p p -adic topology yields the field 𝐐 p \mathbf{Q}_{p} together with the p p -adic Tate curve 𝐐 p × / p 𝐙 \mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}/p^{\mathbf{Z}} .

3.1
Dense embeddings in local fields

We begin by analyzing the local fields obtained as completions of a point of the
\mathbf{F}_{1}

Let

Unless

In order to formulate a general theory of

Lemma 3.1

Assume that

4
4

4

Proof.

We first prove necessity (
\Rightarrow

Assume there exists a homomorphism

Step 1.

Suppose

\ell\cdot 1_{K}=0

For any

\phi(\ell H)=0

Because

Since

Replacing

Using additivity, for any integer

Step 3.

Because

Suppose

We now prove sufficiency (

If

∎

Let

(i)

3.4
Points of
(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}
over a field and the tilt

Before imposing the analytic locality conditions required to evaluate the
\mathbf{F}_{1}
-curve
(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}=(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z},\mathcal{F})
in a perfectoid field, it is worth analyzing the points of that curve evaluated in a general field
F
endowed with the coarse topology. This purely algebraic perspective reveals that the fundamental operation of
p
-adic Hodge theory—tilting—is intrinsically encoded into the absolute stalks of
(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}
.

Let
F
be an arbitrary field. We consider the algebraic points of
(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}
at the prime
p
with values in
F
. By the base-change adjunction (
[
CC4
, Proposition 2.2]
), a
\mathbf{F}_{1}
-algebra morphism

\rho:\mathcal{F}_{p}=\mathbf{F}_{1}[T^{H_{p}^{+}}]\to HF
corresponds uniquely to a
multiplicative monoid homomorphism

\chi:(\mathbf{Z}[1/p]_{+},+)\longrightarrow(F,\cdot),\ \ \chi(0)=1.

To understand the moduli space of such morphisms, we evaluate
\chi
on the
canonical generators of the stalk
. For each integer
n\geq 0
, let:

x_{n}:=\chi\left(\frac{1}{p^{n}}\right)\in F.

Because
\chi
is a monoid homomorphism, it must respect the additive structure of the stalk. The relation
\frac{1}{p^{n-1}}=p\cdot\frac{1}{p^{n}}
in
\mathbf{Z}[1/p]_{+}
translates multiplicatively in
F
to the condition:

x_{n-1}=\chi\left(\frac{1}{p^{n-1}}\right)=\chi\left(\frac{p}{p^{n}}\right)=\left(\chi\left(\frac{1}{p^{n}}\right)\right)^{p}=x_{n}^{p}.

Consequently, the homomorphism
\chi
is
uniquely determined
by the sequence
(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)
of elements in
F
satisfying the recursive relation
x_{n}^{p}=x_{n-1}
. Such sequences are precisely the elements of the inverse limit of
F
under the
p
-th power map. Conversely, given such a sequence one extends it multiplicatively defining:

\chi(a/p^{k})=x_{k}^{a}\quad\text{for all }a\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}

and one obtains in this way a monoid homomorphism
\chi:(\mathbf{Z}[1/p]_{+},+)\longrightarrow(F,\cdot)
.
In the foundational language of perfectoid geometry, the inverse limit

F^{\flat}:=\varprojlim_{x\mapsto x^{p}}F

defines the underlying multiplicative monoid of the tilt of
F
.
We have thus established the following canonical bijection:

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
F
be an arbitrary field endowed with the coarse topology. The map
\chi\mapsto\chi^{\flat}
from points of the
\mathbf{F}_{1}
-curve
(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}
at the prime
p
with values in

to the tilt
F^{\flat}

(\chi^{\flat})_{n}:=\chi\left(\frac{1}{p^{n}}\right)

(6)

is a canonical bijection
\beta
with the tilt
F^{\flat}

\beta:{\rm Pts}_{p}((\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}},F)\to F^{\flat}.

(7)

This proposition establishes that at the purely algebraic level, the
\mathbf{F}_{1}
-stalk at
p
of
\mathcal{F}
acts as a universal tilting functor; evaluating it over any field

automatically extracts its tilt

.

It is very interesting to see how this moduli space collapses when we endow the field
F
with the

topology. In this case, the continuity condition imposed on the points of the
\mathbf{F}_{1}
-curve acts as a rigid arithmetic filter. In the natural topology of the stalk, the sequence

converges additively to

. For the homomorphism
\chi

\lim_{n\to\infty}\chi(p^{n})=1\in F.

Because

is endowed with the discrete topology, a convergent sequence must be eventually constant. Thus, there exists an integer

Since

p

to be a
p
-power root of unity. Consequently, one sees that the image of
\chi

\mu_{p^{\infty}}(F)

-power order.

Because the target

This reveals a topological trichotomy governing the

1.

Coarse Topology:
The moduli space is the full tilt

.

2.

Discrete Topology:
The moduli space isolates the
p
-power roots of unity

In the following part we shall see that:

3.

Analytic Topology:
When

\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
of the prime field. Any non-zero element

for some integer

|z|^{m}=1

|z|=1
. Consequently, for any two distinct elements
a,b\in\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}

a-b
is a non-zero element of
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}

|a-b|=1
. This proves that the induced metric on
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
is the trivial discrete metric. Because
q\neq p

q
-power roots of unity

are entirely contained in
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}

\mu_{q^{\infty}}\subset\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
with the quotient group
\mathbf{Q}_{q}/\mathbf{Z}_{q}
.
Given any homomorphism
\phi\in{\rm{Hom}}(\mathbf{Z}[1/q],\mu_{q^{\infty}})

to obtain an element
x_{n}=\phi(1/q^{n})\in\mathbf{Q}_{q}/\mathbf{Z}_{q}

q^{n}
yields a well-defined element:

z_{n}=q^{n}x_{n}\in\mathbf{Q}_{q}/q^{n}\mathbf{Z}_{q}.

Because
\phi

qx_{n+1}=x_{n}
, which implies:

z_{n+1}=q^{n+1}x_{n+1}=q^{n}(qx_{n+1})=q^{n}x_{n}=z_{n}\pmod{q^{n}\mathbf{Z}_{q}}.

Thus, the sequence
(z_{n})
defines a unique element in the inverse limit
\varprojlim(\mathbf{Q}_{q}/q^{n}\mathbf{Z}_{q})
, which is exactly the additive group
\mathbf{Q}_{q}
. This provides an isomorphism between
{\rm{Hom}}(\mathbf{Z}[1/q],\mu_{q^{\infty}})
and
\mathbf{Q}_{q}
.

Thus, the space of non-trivial local morphisms
\mathcal{M}_{q}=\operatorname{Hom}_{loc}(\mathbf{F}_{1}[T^{H_{q}^{+}}],HF)\setminus\{1\}
is parameterized exactly by the non-zero elements
\mathbf{Q}_{q}\setminus\{0\}=\mathbf{Q}_{q}^{\times}
. To obtain the geometric points, we must quotient by the canonical symmetries of the stalk, which is
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{Q}_{q})\cong\mathbf{Q}_{q}^{\times}
. The action of
\mathbf{Q}_{q}^{\times}
on
\mathcal{M}_{q}
corresponds to the multiplicative action of
\mathbf{Q}_{q}^{\times}
on itself, which is strictly transitive. Therefore, the quotient
\mathcal{M}_{q}/\mathbf{Q}_{q}^{\times}
collapses to a single trivial point and so there is no continuous geometry at
q\neq p
.

Case 2: The generic point
x=\eta
.

The stalk of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}
at
x=\eta
is
\mathbf{F}_{1}
, corresponding to the group
H=\{0\}
. By Proposition
3.3
, the set of places
\Sigma_{H}
is empty. There is no dense embedding into any local field, meaning the locality condition cannot be fulfilled. Thus, the generic point yields no continuous local geometry.

Case 3: The closed point
x=p

The stalk is here
\mathcal{F}_{p}=\mathbf{F}_{1}[T^{\mathbf{Z}[1/p]_{+}}]
. As established in Theorem
3.9
, the locality condition
\lim_{n\to\infty}y^{p^{n}}=1
is highly non-trivial because the Frobenius map
z\mapsto z^{p}
is strictly contracting on
1+\mathfrak{m}_{F}
(since
|y^{p}-1|=|y-1|^{p}
).
Thus, the non-trivial local
F
-points at

0<|y-1|<1
.

By Corollaries
3.14
and
3.15
, quotienting this space of local morphisms by the canonical symmetries of the stalk (
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})\cong\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}
) yields exactly the untilts of

modulo Frobenius, which are the closed points of the Fargues-Fontaine curve. Moreover the trivial local
F
-point

corresponds by Remark
3.13
to the trivial untilt of
F
, and this corresponds precisely to the inclusion of this trivial untilt in the Scholze heuristic.
∎

Theorem
3.16
provides a geometric validation of Scholze’s heuristic. The
\mathbf{F}_{1}
-arithmetic curve

(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}

acts as an absolute geometric sieve. When probed by a field of characteristic
p
, the ultrametric dynamics and the canonical topos symmetries rigidly collapse the geometry at all primes
q\neq p
, reducing each of their fibers to a single, trivial point. On the other hand, the geometry at the prime

completely opens up: the
\mathbf{F}_{1}
-structure automatically generates the cyclotomic embeddings, and the fiber reveals the infinite-dimensional moduli space related to the set of closed points of the Fargues-Fontaine curve (including the trivial untilt as required by Scholze’s heuristic).

3.10
The universal tilting functor and the geometric sieve

Let
C
be a perfectoid field of residue characteristic
p
.
Theorem
3.9
provides a far-reaching extension of Scholze’s heuristic principle.

The prime
p
plays a distinguished role, giving rise to the full moduli space of untilts. We now contrast this behavior with that of the primes
\ell\neq p
, where the geometry collapses. The resulting dichotomy yields the global geometric sieve.

Throughout the remainder of this part, we assume that
C
is algebraically closed.

Case 1: The mismatched primes (
\ell=q\neq p
).

Because
q
is coprime to
p
, we have
|q|_{C}=1
. For any element
x=1+y\in 1+\mathfrak{m}_{C}

x^{q}=1+qy+\dots+y^{q}
. Since
|q|_{C}=1
and
|y|_{C}<1

|x^{q}-1|_{C}=|x-1|_{C}
.

Thus, the
q

isometry
on the principal units. An isometric sequence
x_{0}^{q^{n}}
cannot converge to
1
unless it is eventually exactly equal to
1
. Therefore, continuity forces
x_{0}\in\mu_{q^{\infty}}(C)
. The analytic topology at
q\neq p
rigidly collapses, yielding the exact same moduli space as if
C
were endowed with the discrete topology:

\operatorname{Hom}_{\text{cont}}(\mathbf{Z}[1/q]_{+},C)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\text{group}}(H_{q},\mu_{q^{\infty}}(C))

Case 2: The matched prime (
\ell=p
).

By Theorem
3.9
, the continuous geometric points at
p
form the open unit disk
D=1+\mathfrak{m}_{C^{\flat}}
. Removing the trivial point
x_{0}=1
(which corresponds to the characteristic
p
untilt
C^{\flat}
) and quotienting by the equivariant
\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}
-action yields exactly the set of closed points of the Fargues–Fontaine curve
\mathcal{X}_{C^{\flat},\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
.

We have thus established the following theorem, which serves as the absolute realization of Scholze’s paradigm.

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
C
be an algebraically closed perfectoid field of residue characteristic
p
. Evaluating the
\mathbf{F}_{1}
-curve
(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}
over

acts as a strict geometric sieve:

1.

At every prime
\ell\neq p
, the ultrametric mismatch forces the analytic topology to behave discretely, collapsing the moduli space to a single
\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}^{\times}
-orbit.

2.

At the prime
p
, the
\mathbf{F}_{1}
-stalk acts as a universal tilting functor. The moduli
\mathcal{M}_{p}/\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}
of (local) geometric points coincides with the moduli space of all untilts of

4
Points of
(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}
over
\mathbf{C}

Having analyzed the geometric points of the
\mathbf{F}_{1}
-curve
(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}=(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z},\mathcal{F})
over perfectoid fields, we now investigate its evaluation over the field of complex numbers
\mathbf{C}
. At a fixed prime
p
, the absolute geometry naturally detects two places: the non-archimedean place
p
and the archimedean place
\infty
. The space of non-trivial geometric points at
p
,

\mathcal{M}_{p}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf{F}_{1}[T^{H_{p}^{+}}],H\mathbf{C})\setminus\{1\},

carries a natural symmetry structure whose precise description requires some care.

A first attempt to define a Frobenius-type action would be to let a complex number
w\in\mathbf{C}^{\times}
act on a continuous local morphism

\mathcal{M}_{p}\ni\rho:H_{p}^{+}\to\mathbf{C}^{\times}

by the rule:

(w\cdot\rho)(t)=\rho(t)^{w}
.
However, as soon as
w\notin\mathbf{Z}
, complex exponentiation becomes multivalued, rendering this expression ill-defined on
\mathbf{C}^{\times}
.

The key observation is that this ambiguity is canonically eliminated by the locality condition satisfied by
\rho
, namely continuity with respect to a place

v\in\Sigma_{H_{p}}=\{p,\infty\}
.
As we shall see in this section, locality selects the appropriate branch structure and leads to a well-defined extension of the Frobenius symmetries.

In §
4.1
, we prove that when
v=\infty
, the archimedean topology forces every local morphism
\rho
to factor through the universal covering map

\exp:\mathbf{C}\longrightarrow\mathbf{C}^{\times}
.
Consequently,
\rho
admits a unique lift of the form

\widetilde{\rho}(t)=zt
,

z\in\mathbf{C}
,
and the action of an element
w\in\mathbf{C}^{\times}
becomes the ordinary scalar action on the Lie algebra:

w\cdot\widetilde{\rho}(t)=(wz)t
.
Transporting this action through the exponential map yields a canonical and unambiguous action of the local Weil group

W_{\infty}=\mathbf{C}^{\times}

on the moduli space of archimedean points
\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}
.

This action reveals a deeper geometric structure: the moduli spaces of points of
(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}
naturally arise as torsors (principal homogeneous spaces) under suitable Weil groups. More precisely, we shall see that the space

\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}

of non-trivial archimedean points is parametrized by

\widetilde{\mathbf{C}^{\times}}\setminus\{0\},

which forms a canonical torsor over
W_{\infty}
. Two fundamental geometric quotients emerge from this structure:

1.

The Tate curve
E_{p}
.

The complex Tate curve

E_{p}:=\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}

inherits the torsor structure and becomes a principal homogeneous space under the quotient group

W_{\infty}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}
.

2.

The projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbf{R})
.

The continuous symmetries of the completed stalk are encoded by the subgroup

\mathbf{R}^{\times}=W_{\infty}^{\sigma}
,
where
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(W_{\infty})
denotes complex conjugation. The quotient

\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbf{R})\cong(\widetilde{\mathbf{C}^{\times}}\setminus\{0\})/\mathbf{R}^{\times}

therefore inherits a natural torsor structure under

W_{\infty}/W_{\infty}^{\sigma}\cong S^{1}
.

In §
4.4
we show that for the place
v=p
the ambiguity is resolved canonically by the local Weil group
W_{p}=\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}
, and that the non-trivial points
\mathcal{M}_{p}^{p}
form a single orbit of this group.

4.1
Geometric
\mathbf{C}
-points local for
\infty\in\Sigma_{H_{p}}

We first determine the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}
of points local for the place
\infty
.
By Proposition
2.3
, any such point corresponds uniquely to a monoid homomorphism:

\rho:(H_{p}^{+},+)\longrightarrow(\mathbf{C},\cdot)

which is continuous with respect to the archimedean topology on
H_{p}^{+}
, induced by the standard inclusion
H_{p}\subset\mathbf{R}
, and the standard Euclidean topology on
\mathbf{C}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

(i)
The map

\rho:\mathbf{C}\to{\rm Pts}_{p}^{\infty}((\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}},\mathbf{C}),\quad\rho(z):(H_{p}^{+},+)\longrightarrow(\mathbf{C},\cdot),\ \ \rho(z)(t):=\exp(zt),\ \forall t\in H_{p}^{+},

defines a canonical bijection of
\mathbf{C}
with the space of
\mathbf{C}
-points of
(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}
at
p
, local for the archimedean place
\infty
.

(ii)
Under the bijection defined in
(i)
, the action of
\mathbf{R}^{\times}=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbf{R})
corresponds to the scaling action on
\mathbf{C}
, and the Frobenius action of
p\in p^{\mathbf{Z}}
on the stalk corresponds to multiplication by
p
.

(iii)
The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}
of non-trivial archimedean points forms a canonical torsor over the local Weil group
W_{\infty}=\mathbf{C}^{\times}
.

Proof.

(i)
Let
\rho:H_{p}^{+}\to\mathbf{C}
be a continuous monoid homomorphism. By definition,
\rho(0)=1
. Because
\rho
is continuous at
0
, there exists a neighborhood of
0
in
H_{p}^{+}
such that
\rho(t)\neq 0
for all
t
in this neighborhood.

We claim that
\rho(t)\neq 0
for all
t\in H_{p}^{+}
. Suppose for contradiction that
\rho(t_{0})=0
for some
t_{0}>0
. For any integer
n\geq 1
, the homomorphism property implies:

\left(\rho\left(\frac{t_{0}}{p^{n}}\right)\right)^{p^{n}}=\rho(t_{0})=0\implies\rho\left(\frac{t_{0}}{p^{n}}\right)=0.

However, in the archimedean topology, the sequence
t_{0}/p^{n}
converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
. Continuity then demands that
\rho(t_{0}/p^{n})\to\rho(0)=1
, which contradicts
\rho(t_{0}/p^{n})=0
. Thus,
\rho
never vanishes, and its image lies entirely in
\mathbf{C}^{\times}
.

Because
H_{p}^{+}
is dense in
\mathbf{R}_{\geq 0}
, the continuous homomorphism
\rho
extends uniquely to a continuous monoid homomorphism
\tilde{\rho}:\mathbf{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbf{C}^{\times}
. Furthermore, since
\mathbf{R}_{\geq 0}
generates the group
\mathbf{R}
and
\mathbf{C}^{\times}
is a group,
\tilde{\rho}
extends uniquely to a continuous group homomorphism from
\mathbf{R}
to
\mathbf{C}^{\times}
.

By standard Lie group theory, any continuous one-parameter subgroup of
\mathbf{C}^{\times}
lifts through the universal covering map
\exp:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{C}^{\times}
. Therefore, there exists a unique complex number
z\in\mathbf{C}
such that for all
t\in H_{p}^{+}
:

\rho(t)=\exp(zt).

This establishes a canonical bijection between the continuous morphisms
\rho
and the parameter space
\mathbf{C}
.

Finally, we determine the action of the canonical symmetries. The action of
\lambda\in\mathbf{R}^{\times}
on the completed stalk is given by pre-composition with multiplication by
\lambda
. Acting on the morphism
\rho_{z}(t)=\exp(zt)
, we obtain:

(\lambda\cdot\rho_{z})(t)=\rho_{z}(\lambda\,t)=\exp(z(\lambda\,t))=\exp((\lambda\,z)t)=\rho_{\lambda\,z}(t).

This shows
(ii)
, in particular the Frobenius action on the stalk translates exactly to multiplication by
p
on the parameter space
\mathbf{C}
.

(iii)
By
(i)
, the scaling action of
W=\mathbf{C}^{\times}
on
\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}
comes from the canonical action of
W
on the universal cover of
\mathbf{C}^{\times}
. While the isomorphism given in
(i)
depends on the choice of the covering
\exp:\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{C}^{\times}
the action of
W
is independent of this choice, and
\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}
is a principal homogeneous space for this action.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

It is important to emphasize that the moduli space of
\mathbf{C}
-points is not merely a topological space, but it canonically inherits a complex analytic structure directly from the target field. For any
t\in H_{p}^{+}
, the pointwise evaluation defines a map from the moduli space of continuous homomorphisms to
\mathbf{C}
:

\operatorname{ev}_{t}:\operatorname{Hom}_{\text{cont}}(H_{p}^{+},\mathbf{C})\longrightarrow\mathbf{C},\quad\rho\longmapsto\rho(t).

By demanding that these evaluation maps be holomorphic, the moduli space is canonically endowed with a complex analytic structure. Under the parameterization
\rho_{z}(t)=\exp(zt)
, the evaluation maps
\operatorname{ev}_{t}(z)=\exp(zt)
are entire functions, demonstrating that this induced complex structure coincides exactly with the standard complex structure of the plane
\mathbf{C}
.

4.1.1
Quotient by the automorphisms of the completed stalk

At the archimedean place
\infty\in\Sigma_{H_{p}}
, the completion of the group
H_{p}
is the real line
\mathbf{R}
, whose full group of continuous scaling symmetries is
\mathbf{R}^{\times}
. The completion of the stalk
H_{p}^{+}
is the positive real line
\mathbf{R}_{\geq 0}
and this reduces the symmetry group to the subgroup
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}\subset\mathbf{R}^{\times}
.
It is natural to compare the geometric quotient

{\rm Pts}_{p}^{\infty}((\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}},\mathbf{C})/\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}

with the non-archimedean construction of the closed points of the Fargues–Fontaine curve. The trivial point of the moduli space corresponds to
z=0
, namely to the neutral element
\mathbf{1}
. Removing this point leaves the moduli space of non-trivial points

\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}={\rm Pts}_{p}^{\infty}((\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}},\mathbf{C})\setminus\{0\}\cong\mathbf{C}^{\times}.

By Proposition
4.1
(ii), the symmetry group
\mathbf{R}^{\times}
acts on the parameter space
\mathbf{C}^{\times}
by ordinary real scalar multiplication. Taking the quotient by this full continuous symmetry group yields the orbit space

\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}/\mathbf{R}^{\times}\cong\mathbf{C}^{\times}/\mathbf{R}^{\times}.

Geometrically, this quotient parametrizes the real lines through the origin in the plane
\mathbf{C}\cong\mathbf{R}^{2}
, and is therefore naturally identified with the real projective line

\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbf{R})
. But the automorphism group of the completed stalk is the subgroup
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}\subset\mathbf{R}^{\times}
and the analogue of the closed points of the Fargues-Fontaine curve is the double cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbf{R})

given by the following archimedean analogue of Corollary
3.15
.

Corollary 4.3
.

(i)
The quotient space
\mathcal{X}_{\infty}:=\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}/\mathbf{R}^{\times}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbf{R})
forms a canonical torsor over
W/W^{\sigma}
.

(ii)
The quotient space
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\infty}:=\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}/\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}
by the automorphism group of the completed stalk, is the unramified double cover of the projective line
\mathcal{X}_{\infty}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbf{R})
, with the deck transformation given by the element
-1\in W_{\infty}
.

Thus, the same construction underlies both the non-archimedean and archimedean settings. Namely, after removing the trivial point and quotienting by the automorphism group of the completed stalk, one obtains the space of closed points of the Fargues–Fontaine curve in the perfectoid case, while over
\mathbf{C}
one recovers the double cover
\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\infty}
of the projective line.

4.1.2
The Tate curve
E_{p}

The Tate curve
E_{p}=\mathbf{C}^{\times}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}
arises from quotienting the space of non-trivial local morphisms
\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}
by the discrete arithmetic symmetry group
p^{\mathbf{Z}}
of the uncompleted stalk
H_{p}
generated by the Frobenius action
z\mapsto pz
. This yields the complex elliptic curve:

E_{p}=\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}\cong\mathbf{C}^{\times}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}.

The isomorphism in display follows from Proposition
4.1
,
(i)
. By
(iii)
the space
E_{p}=\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}
forms a torsor over the quotient group
W_{\infty}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}
.

By lifting via the exponential map
w\mapsto\exp(2\pi iw)
, we see that
E_{p}\cong\mathbf{C}/\Lambda_{p}
, where the lattice is
\Lambda_{p}=\mathbf{Z}\oplus\mathbf{Z}\tau
, with modular parameter
\tau=i\frac{\log p}{2\pi}
. This is exactly the complex analytic realization of the Tate curve with parameter
q=1/p
. Because
\tau
is purely imaginary,
E_{p}
is a rectangular torus defined over
\mathbf{R}
.

It follows from Remark
4.2
that the space of non-trivial points
\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}\cong\mathbf{C}^{\times}
carries a canonical structure of complex manifold, and that the Frobenius action

z\mapsto pz

acts by bi-holomorphisms. Consequently, the quotient

E_{p}=\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}

inherits a canonical structure of Riemann surface.

This constitutes a significant refinement of the underlying
\mathbf{F}_{1}
-geometry. The absolute geometry produces not merely a topological torus, but a rigid complex-analytic object that intrinsically encodes the invariants of the associated elliptic curve, including the modular parameter

\tau=i\frac{\log p}{2\pi}

and the corresponding
j
-invariant. The canonical complex structure on
E_{p}
therefore promotes the quotient naturally to an algebraic curve over
\mathbf{C}
.

Since
E_{p}
is a compact Riemann surface, one may consider its field of global meromorphic functions. These are precisely the multiplicatively periodic meromorphic functions on
\mathbf{C}^{\times}
satisfying

f(pz)=f(z)
.
This field is an algebraic function field of transcendence degree one over
\mathbf{C}
, generated by the Weierstrass
\wp
-function and its derivative. The algebraic relations among these generators determine a projective algebraic curve, whose structure sheaf is recovered by considering meromorphic functions with prescribed finite sets of poles and passing to the corresponding localizations.

Thus, the evaluation of
\mathbf{F}_{1}
-morphisms yields considerably more than an analytic space: it canonically reconstructs the full algebraic curve structure of the Tate curve. Moreover, the torsor structure of
E_{p}
under its group of translations is precisely the one induced by the action of

W_{\infty}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}=\mathbf{C}^{\times}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}

described above.

4.2
The real structure

The symmetries of the archimedean points are further enriched by the arithmetic of the target field
\mathbf{C}
. The complex conjugation
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbf{C}/\mathbf{R})
acts naturally on the moduli space of geometric points by conjugating the target values:
\rho^{\sigma}(t)=\overline{\rho(t)}
. On the parameter space
\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}\cong\mathbf{C}^{\times}
, this corresponds to standard complex conjugation
z\mapsto\bar{z}
.

Crucially, because the Frobenius action (multiplication by
p
) is strictly real, it commutes with
\sigma
. Consequently, complex conjugation descends to an
anti-holomorphic involution
on the Tate curve
E_{p}=\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}\cong\mathbf{C}^{\times}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}
, endowing it with a canonical real structure. We may explicitly determine the real locus
E_{p}(\mathbf{R})
by finding the fixed points of
\sigma
on the quotient. A class
[z]
is fixed if and only if
\bar{z}=p^{n}z
for some integer
n
. Taking absolute values forces
p^{n}=1
, hence
n=0
and so
z\in\mathbf{R}^{\times}
.
The real locus of the Tate curve is therefore:

E_{p}(\mathbf{R})=\mathbf{R}^{\times}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}.

Because
\mathbf{R}^{\times}
decomposes multiplicatively into positive and negative reals,
\mathbf{R}^{\times}=\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}\sqcup\mathbf{R}_{-}^{\times}
, and the Frobenius action preserves the sign, the real locus splits into two disjoint connected components:

E_{p}(\mathbf{R})=(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}/p^{\mathbf{Z}})\sqcup(\mathbf{R}_{-}^{\times}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}).

Geometrically, this reveals that the real points of the Tate curve consist of two canonical copies of the periodic orbit
C_{p}=\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}
.

4.3
Rectangular decomposition of
E_{p}

The relationship between the Tate curve
E_{p}
, the periodic orbit
C_{p}
, and the analogue of the Fargues–Fontaine curve:

\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\infty}:=\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}/\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times},

can be made completely explicit by analyzing the exact sequence of the underlying symmetry groups.
Consider the exact sequence associated to the positive real scaling symmetries:

1\to\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}\to\mathbf{C}^{\times}\to\mathbf{C}^{\times}/\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}\to 1.

This sequence is canonically split by the absolute value map
z\mapsto|z|
, yielding the polar decomposition
\mathbf{C}^{\times}\cong\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}\times S^{1}
.

Because the Frobenius generator
p
is a positive real number, its action on
\mathbf{C}^{\times}
is entirely confined to the
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}
factor. Consequently, the geometric quotient defining the Tate curve splits globally as a product of two real
1
-dimensional manifolds:

E_{p}=\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\infty}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}=(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}/p^{\mathbf{Z}})\times\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\infty}=C_{p}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\infty}.

This canonical decomposition precisely isolates the arithmetic data from the geometric data. The first factor is the periodic orbit
C_{p}
(from the adelic scaling site), which carries the entire dependence on the prime
p
(having length
\log p
). The second factor
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\infty}
is a purely geometric phase circle that is totally independent of
p
, and has been described in Corollary
4.3
(ii).

The product decomposition
E_{p}=C_{p}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\infty}
explains the origin of the rectangular complex structure of the Tate curve. Writing a point in
E_{p}
in polar coordinates as
z=\lambda e^{i\theta}
, the canonical nowhere-vanishing holomorphic
1
-form on the elliptic curve is given by the logarithmic differential:

\frac{dz}{z}=\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}+id\theta.

This decomposition reveals that the complex structure on
E_{p}
is uniquely and canonically determined by intertwining the invariant real
1
-forms (the Haar measures) of its two constituent Lie groups: namely, the invariant measure
d\lambda/\lambda
on the arithmetic orbit
C_{p}
, and the invariant measure
d\theta
on the geometric phase circle
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\infty}
of length
2\pi
. The modular parameter of the elliptic curve,
\tau=i\frac{\log p}{2\pi}
, is therefore exactly the ratio of the integrals of these two canonical real differentials over their respective fundamental domains. We summarize this finding in the following:

Theorem 5

(Geometric Decomposition of the Tate Curve)
.

The complex Tate curve
E_{p}
of (
4
),
admits a canonical global decomposition as a product of two real
1
-dimensional manifolds, perfectly isolating the arithmetic from the geometric data:

E_{p}\cong C_{p}\times\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\infty}.

The first factor of this decomposition is the periodic orbit
C_{p}=\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}
of the adelic scaling site. It constitutes the connected component of the real locus of
E_{p}
and carries the entire dependence on the prime
p
. The second factor
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\infty}
is the (
p
-independent) real analogue of the Fargues-Fontaine-curve.

Furthermore, this product decomposition canonically determines the rectangular complex structure of the Tate curve. The canonical nowhere-vanishing holomorphic
1
-form on
E_{p}
is given by:

\omega=\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}+id\theta

which uniquely intertwines the invariant real Haar measure
d\lambda/\lambda
of the arithmetic orbit
C_{p}
with the invariant real Haar measure
d\theta
of the
p
-independent geometric factor
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\infty}
.

4.4
Geometric
\mathbf{C}
-points local for
p\in\Sigma_{H_{p}}

The absolute nature of the
\mathbf{F}_{1}
-stalk
\mathcal{F}_{p}=\mathbf{F}_{1}[T^{H_{p}^{+}}]
(
H_{p}^{+}=\mathbf{Z}[1/p]_{+}
) allows one to evaluate it over the complex numbers
\mathbf{C}
using, this time, the
p
-adic topology on the stalk. This corresponds to looking at the non-archimedean place
p
with values in
\mathbf{C}
. The change in topology on the domain (with respect to the archimedean case discussed before) completely transforms the moduli space, revealing a profound symmetry.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
H_{p}^{+}
be endowed with the
p
-adic topology, and
\mathbf{C}
with the standard Euclidean topology. The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{p}^{p}
of continuous local
\mathbf{C}
-points of
(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{Z})_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}
is canonically in bijection with the
p
-adic field
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
. Under this bijection, the Frobenius action of
p\in p^{\mathbf{Z}}
on the stalk corresponds to multiplication by
p
on
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
.

Proof.

Let
\rho:H_{p}^{+}\to\mathbf{C}
be a continuous monoid homomorphism. The
p
-adic topology on
H_{p}^{+}
dictates that
p^{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Continuity therefore requires that
\rho(p^{n})\to\rho(0)=1\in\mathbf{C}
.

Let
z=\rho(1)\in\mathbf{C}
. The continuity condition implies
z^{p^{n}}\to 1
. Writing
z=\exp(2\pi i\theta)
for some
\theta\in\mathbf{R}/\mathbf{Z}
, this requires
p^{n}\theta\to 0\pmod{1}
. However, the multiplication-by-
p
map on
\mathbf{R}/\mathbf{Z}
strictly expands distances in a neighborhood of
0
. A sequence cannot converge to
0
under a strictly expanding map unless it is eventually exactly
0
. Thus, there exists an integer
N
such that
p^{N}\theta\equiv 0\pmod{1}
, meaning
\theta\in\mathbf{Z}[1/p]
. Consequently,
z
is a
p
-power root of unity.

Because
\rho(1/p^{k})^{p^{k}}=z
, every element in the image of
\rho
must also be a
p
-power root of unity. The image of
\rho
thus lies entirely within
\mu_{p^{\infty}}(\mathbf{C})
. Because the target of
\rho
is a group,
\rho
extends uniquely to a group homomorphism
H_{p}=\mathbf{Z}[1/p]\to\mu_{p^{\infty}}(\mathbf{C})
.

Fixing a canonical isomorphism
\mu_{p^{\infty}}(\mathbf{C})\cong\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p}
, the moduli space is given by

\operatorname{Hom}(H_{p},\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p})\simeq\mathbf{Q}_{p}.

Finally, the Frobenius action of
p
on the stalk is given by
(p\cdot\rho)(t)=\rho(pt)
. On the defining sequence, this yields
x_{n}\mapsto\rho(p/p^{n})=x_{n-1}=px_{n}
. Under the inverse limit isomorphism, this corresponds exactly to multiplication by
p
on
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
.
∎

5
Outlook

In
[
CC3
]
, we developed the characteristic-
1
geometry of the periodic orbit
C_{p}
as an analogue of an elliptic curve. The identification of the absolute
\mathbf{F}_{1}
-geometry as a universal receptacle for the closed points of the Fargues–Fontaine curve naturally leads to a functorial connection with the characteristic-
1
(equivalently, idempotent) geometry of the scaling site
\mathscr{S}
introduced in
[
CC3
]
.

Recall that the scaling site

\mathscr{S}=([0,\infty)\rtimes\mathbf{N}^{\times},\mathcal{O})

is a semiringed topos whose underlying space is the half-line
[0,\infty)
endowed with the multiplicative action of
\mathbf{N}^{\times}
. Its structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}
consists of continuous, convex, piecewise affine functions with integral slopes.

A remarkable feature of the analytic geometry of the Fargues–Fontaine curve is that it admits a canonical tropicalization into this characteristic-
1
framework.

Indeed, analytic functions on the covering adic space of the Fargues–Fontaine curve are obtained by completing the ring (we follow the notation of
[
FF
]
)

\mathbf{A}_{\inf}\!\left[\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{[\varpi]}\right]

with respect to a family of Gauss valuations
v_{s}
, where the parameter

s\in(0,\infty)

plays the role of a logarithmic radius. The behavior of these valuations is governed by a fundamental result of
p
-adic Hodge theory (
cf.

[
Lurie
, Lecture 11, Proposition 5]
):

Proposition
.

Let
f
be a nonzero element of
\mathbf{A}_{\inf}\left[\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{[\varpi]}\right]
. Then the map
s\mapsto v_{s}(f)
determines a function

v_{\bullet}(f):(0,\infty)\longrightarrow\mathbf{R}

which is piecewise linear with integer slopes. Moreover, it is concave (that is, the slopes are decreasing).

This proposition provides the analytic bridge to characteristic-
1
geometry. Indeed, applying a global sign change yields the tropicalization map

\tau(f)(s):=-v_{s}(f)
.
Since the valuation profile
v_{\bullet}(f)
is concave, piecewise linear, and has integral slopes, it follows that
\tau(f)
is convex, piecewise affine, and again has integral slopes.

Consequently, for every nonzero analytic function
f
in the uncompleted period ring, the tropicalization
\tau(f)
defines a global section of the characteristic-
1
structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}
of the scaling site
\mathscr{S}
. In this way, the family of logarithmic Gauss valuations gives rise to a canonical functor from characteristic-
0
analytic geometry to the idempotent geometry of the scaling site.

Thus, the scaling site over the Boolean semifield
\mathbf{B}
emerges as the canonical tropicalization of the covering space of the Fargues–Fontaine curve.

A natural next step is to investigate the behavior of Frobenius eigenspaces, such as
B^{\varphi=p}
in
[
FF
]
, under descent to the periodic orbit

C_{p}=\mathbf{R}_{+}^{\times}/p^{\mathbf{Z}}
,
and to clarify their relationship with the elliptic-curve geometry developed in
[
CC3
]
. We leave this question for future work.
