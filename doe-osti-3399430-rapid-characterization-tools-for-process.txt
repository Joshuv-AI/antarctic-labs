 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 In this project Prenexus Health, Inc. (Prenexus) and NLR will develop rapid characterization
 tools to improve the Prenexus production process. First, we will develop a tool to predict the
 overall performance of the Prenexus manufacturing process converting high-fiber sugarcane to
 xylo-oligosaccharides (XOS) (both the overall process yield and the composition of the XOS
 product) using rapid characterization of the incoming feedstock and knowledge of key process
 parameters. This tool will dramatically increase Prenexus’ understanding of the overall process.
 Second, we will develop a tool to predict the concentration of soluble oligomeric and monomeric
 xylose and organic acids in multiple process streams at-line in the Prenexus process, enabling
 real-time process control of the conversion process.

 Summary of Research Results:

 Task 1: Process Performance Prediction Based on Composition of Lignocellulosic Biomass
 Composition

 The goal of Task 1 is to develop a robust and rapid prediction model to predict the performance
 of the Prenexus XOS production process based on rapid characterization of the incoming
 feedstock, knowledge of process parameters and performance, and detailed characterization of
 resulting product.

 Prenexus Health, Inc. grows and uses seven independent varieties of high fiber sugarcane in its
 xylooligosaccharide (XOS) production process. These varieties were developed by the USDA in
 Louisiana and were chosen by Prenexus based on agronomic characteristics, for example time of
 maturity in the field and biomass yield (tons/acre). However, very little information is available
 about how well these varieties perform in the production process, and how agronomic factors
 such as harvest time affect feedstock composition and in turn the overall process performance
 and optimal process operating conditions.

 Prenexus Health has developed a proprietary feedstock storage method that eliminates “just in
 time harvesting and delivery” thus the crop is harvested on a quarterly or semiannual basis and
 then stored until use. Prenexus will provide NLR with raw material prior to storage and stored
 just before use to determine if any changes have occurred that potentially impact process
 performance and/or yield. Information learned from this phase will determine whether the
 previous model accurately predicts composition or whether a new correlation model must be
 developed. This information will also be used to narrow down the number of varieties Prenexus
 has in the field and potentially to identify the best time of year to harvest and store.

 2

 NLR has also developed an analytical method using high-pressure liquid chromatography with
 coupled evaporative light scattering detection and mass spectroscopy detection (LC-ELSD-MS)
 to assess degree of polymerization and sugar type (C5 or C6) for various sugar polymers.
 Prenexus previously worked with NLR to use this method to characterize the polymer chain
 length of the Prenexus final product. The data generated from this method will provide a critical
 measure of process performance in addition to overall process yield information.

 In Task 1 the Prenexus/NLR team will develop a comprehensive predictive model of the
 Prenexus process performance based on the composition of the incoming sugarcane feedstock
 and process operational parameters. As part of this work, the team will develop a rapid
 characterization tool to predict the sugarcane compositional analysis based on its’ near-infrared
 (NIR) spectra, including samples before and after storage.

 Subtask 1.1: Development of a Feedstock Rapid Characterization Tool

 In Subtask 1.1 the Prenexus/NLR team will develop a rapid tool for sugarcane characterization
 based on Near-infrared (NIR) spectroscopy. NLR has previously developed a mixed herbaceous
 NIR compositional analyses model which includes energy cane samples. Prenexus will collect
 and provide NLR with samples of the energy cane feedstock, including all seven varieties
 harvested at various times of the year. NLR will use these samples to expand the existing mixed
 herbaceous feedstock model by collecting NIR spectra of these samples and determining their
 composition using standard wet chemical analysis methods.

 1. Perform wet chemical analysis on up to 120 samples provided by Prenexus (M6)
 2. Expand existing mixed herbaceous feedstock compositional analysis model with
 additional sugarcane samples. (M9)

 Subtask 1.2: Process Data Collection/Organization and Product Characterization

 In Subtask 1.2 Prenexus will collect and organize all process data associated with the conversion
 of the sugarcane feedstock samples collected in Subtask 1.1. These data will include process
 variables such as hot water ‘cook’ time and temperature and downstream filtration/separation
 performance. In addition, Prenexus will collect representative XOS product samples that
 correspond to the feedstock samples and process data and deliver these samples to NLR for xylo-
 oligomer analysis.

 1. Perform LC/ELSD/MS analysis of up to 120 Prenexus XOS samples (M9)

 3

 Subtask 1.3: Multivariate/Machine Learning Modeling

 In Subtask 1.3 NLR will develop a validated predictive model to correlate the process yield and
 composition of the xylo-oligomer product as a function of the biomass feedstock composition,
 storage method and the process conversion conditions. The model inputs will be a combination
 of numerical (feedstock composition) and categorical (‘cook’ conditions) values, requiring a
 somewhat more sophisticated modeling approach than that used in Subtask 1.1. The modeling
 process will be iterative and will require continual communication with Prenexus to confirm the
 overall approach and the intermediate modeling results.

 1. Develop a machine learning model to predict the overall process yield and XOS profile
 of material produced by Prenexus from knowledge of the incoming feedstock
 composition and process operational parameters. (M12)

 Task 2: Rapid Analysis of in-process Mixed Carbohydrate Streams

 The goal of this Task is to develop a rapid characterization method for the concentration of
 xylose (both oligomeric and monomeric) and organic acids in Prenexus XOS production process
 streams.

 The Prenexus XOS production process involves the thermochemical pretreatment of biomass and
 then a series of filtration and chromatography unit operations of the liquid extract to ultimately
 recover product meeting purity specifications. Currently each step is monitored for yield and
 purity using NLR established wet chemical analysis methods. The data generated are used to (1)
 adjust cook process conditions for subsequent batches based on oligomer/monomer ratios, total
 oligomer (kg) present in each batch, and residual xylan present in the post-cooked solids, and (2)
 ensure the filtration steps are performing up to specification and potentially adjust those
 operating conditions (for example pressure, diafiltration ratios, etc.) and equipment (molecular
 weight cutoff). Prenexus currently analyzes hundreds of different samples per week ranging in
 product purity of approx. 40% (crude extracts) to approx. 80% (final product). These analytical
 methods, while robust, are performed offline and are both time consuming and labor intensive.

 In Task 2 the Prenexus/NLR team will develop chemometric models for rapid characterization of
 the soluble oligomeric and monomeric xylose and organic acids in five different process streams
 within the Prenexus process. These chemometric models can then be used at-line or in-line to
 provide quick feedback to monitor operating conditions as well as yield and product purity.
 Work in this Task will be divided into two subtasks, with Prenexus responsible for Subtask 2.1
 and NLR responsible for Subtask 2.2.

 4

 Subtask 2.1: Process Sample Collection & Characterization

 In Subtask 2.1, Prenexus will collect at least 100 in-process liquid stream samples from four
 different process streams and 50 post-cooked solids within the Prenexus process for a total of at
 least 450 unique samples. Prenexus will measure the soluble monomeric and oligomeric xylose
 and organic acid concentration of the liquid samples and the xylan content of the solid samples
 using NLRestablished wet chemical methods of acid hydrolysis and High-performance liquid
 chromatography (HPLC) analysis (NLR/TP-510-42623 Determination of Sugars, By-products,
 and Degradation Products in Liquid Fraction Process Samples: Laboratory Analytical Procedure;
 and NLR/TP-510- 42618 Determination of Structural Carbohydrates and Lignin in Biomass.).
 Prenexus will freeze these samples and periodically ship groups of these samples to NLR for
 NIR spectroscopy in Subtask 2.2.

 Subtask 2.2: NIR Spectroscopy and Rapid Characterization Tool Development

 In Subtask 2.2, NLR will collect near-infrared (NIR) spectra of all samples provided by Prenexus
 using a Metrohm XDS NIR spectrometer. The liquid samples will be scanned in transflectance
 mode, using a pathlength of 0.5mm. The solid samples will be scanned in reflectance mode.
 Careful temperature control of the samples will be maintained, since liquid-phase NIR
 spectroscopy is very sensitive to small fluctuations in temperature. Once spectra from all samples
 are collected, NLR will develop multivariate calibration models for oligomeric, monomeric, and
 total soluble glucose concentration and organic acid concentration for the liquid samples using
 standard multivariate analysis tools. NLR will also develop the calibration models for residual
 xylan remaining in the postcooked solids. The resulting analytical models (and all supporting
 spectroscopy data) will be provided to Prenexus for implementation at their facility.

 1. Collect NIR spectra of Prenexus liquid and solid samples using laboratory
 spectrometers (M14)
 2. Develop NIR chemometric models for Prenexus liquid and solids samples for use at-
 line or in-line (M18)

 Task 3: Documentation & Outreach

 The goal of Task 3 is to broadly disseminate the results of the project so that other bioeconomy
 stakeholders can take advantage of the results of this work. We believe that the tools developed
 in this project, in addition to helping Prenexus, will serve as guides to substantially de-risk
 similar implementation work at larger biofuels facilities in the future. Publication of the results
 of this work (including the underlying data) is critical to ensuring maximum benefit to industry
 stakeholders.

 1. Collaborate with Prenexus to submit at least one article to a peer-reviewed journal and
 one article to an industry trade journal (M20)
