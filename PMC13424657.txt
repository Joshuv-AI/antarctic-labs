Effectiveness of Blood Perfusion Assessment by Indocyanine Green Fluorescence Angiography in the Prevention of Anastomotic Leakage after Rectal Cancer Surgery in Japan: A Retrospective Study Using the Diagnosis Procedure Combination Database
Authors: Takao Amaike, Makoto Okawara, Yasuki Akiyama, Jun Nagata, Kensuke Nitta, Masumi Yamauchi, Yoshihisa Fujino, Kazunori Shibao, Kiyohide Fushimi, Shinya Matsuda, Keiji Hirata
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground: Several studies have reported the potential effectiveness of indocyanine green fluorescence angiography (ICG-FA) for preventing anastomotic leakage (AL) in rectal cancer surgery; however, its clinical benefit remains uncertain. The purpose of this study was to investigate the effectiveness of ICG-FA in rectal cancer surgery in Japan using real-world data. Methods: We retrieved data from the Diagnosis Procedure Combination database in Japan for rectal cancer surgeries between April 2018 and March 2022. A total of 68,022 cases were registered, and 55,299 eligible patients were analyzed. Using a two-level structure of nested individuals from 1,057 hospitals, we applied multilevel logistic regression. Results: A total of 40,013 laparoscopic (72.4%), 7,464 robotic (13.5%), and 7,822 open (14.1%) operations were performed. The rate of AL was 4.3% in the ICG-FA group and 5.1% in the non-ICG-FA group. After adjustment for patient- and hospital-level factors, ICG-FA was associated with a lower risk of AL (OR 0.87, 95% CI 0.76–1.00, p = 0.043); however, after additional adjustment for fiscal year, the association was no longer statistically significant (OR 0.91, 95% CI 0.79–1.04, p = 0.167). Conclusions: ICG-FA may be associated with a lower risk of AL after rectal cancer surgery in real-world settings, although the benefit was not statistically significant after additional adjustment for fiscal year. Keywords: anastomotic leakage, indocyanine green, rectal cancer, rectal cancer surgery, Diagnosis Procedure Combination (DPC) database

Introduction

Rectal cancer surgery is one of the most technically demanding procedures in colorectal surgery. The frequency of anastomotic leakage (AL) after rectal cancer surgery is 11% to 19%[1-5]. In addition, AL is associated with local recurrence[6].

There are various factors that cause AL, and one of the important factors is the blood flow at the anastomosis. Recently, Watanabe et al. reported on the efficacy of blood perfusion assessment by indocyanine green fluorescence angiography (ICG-FA) in the reduction of AL rate in the EssentiAL trial (7.6% vs. 11.8%; relative risk: 0.645; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.422–0.987; p = 0.041)[7]. In Japan, the use of ICG-FA for assessing blood flow was covered by insurance from 2018 to the present day.

Previously, we have reported some analysis results using the Diagnostic Procedure Combination (DPC) database that is a Japanese nationwide patient data repository for various fields[8-10]. In the present study, we investigated the effectiveness of ICG-FA in reducing the incidence of AL after rectal cancer surgery using DPC data. The DPC contains vast amounts of data on patients admitted to major hospitals in Japan and is believed to reflect “real-world” conditions in Japanese medical fields. We consider this study to be one of the larger studies evaluating the effectiveness of ICG-FA in rectal surgery.

Methods

Study design

This study was a retrospective cohort study of the AL risk of surgery for rectal cancer with ICG-FA and without ICG-FA.

Data source

In this retrospective study, we used data from the DPC database, a diagnostic group classification system in Japan that is linked to the comprehensive payment system for medical fees. The DPC database consists of discharge information and reimbursement data, and it includes the following information: age, sex, TNM stage, diagnosis, comorbidities, complications coded by the International Statistical Classiﬁcation of Diseases and Related Health Problems, 10th Revision (ICD-10)[11], duration of anesthesia, amount of blood transfused, drugs and medical supplies used, duration of hospital stay, discharge outcome, and cost. The data were collected by the DPC Research Institute, a general incorporated association. In addition, we could extract the type of surgery performed during hospitalization using the original Japanese code (K-code) assigned by the Ministry of Health, Labor and Welfare of Japan. Furthermore, the use of ICG-FA was identified using the Japanese medical fee surcharge code for intraoperative vascular imaging (K939-02), which is billed as an additional reimbursement when fluorescence angiography is performed during surgery. Because this surcharge is routinely claimed when ICG-FA is used, the presence of this code allowed us to identify the use of ICG-FA in the DPC database. However, we could not collect some data: blood test data, vital signs, duration of operation, blood loss, preoperative chemotherapy and radiation therapy, degree of lymph node dissection and whether that included lateral lymph node dissection.

Patient selection

In this study, we collected data from the DPC database for rectal cancer surgeries from April 2018 to March 2022, and all personally identifiable data were removed. We extracted patients who were treated for rectosigmoid cancer and rectal cancer with the relevant ICD-10 codes (C19 and C20, respectively). Among them, patients who underwent rectal resection were extracted using the K-code described above. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee at the University of Occupational and Environmental Health, Japan (R4-045). All procedures met the provisions of the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments. The requirement for informed consent was waived because this study used anonymized retrospective data.

Endpoint

In the present study, we set the incidence of AL as the endpoint. Because it was difficult to investigate the detailed postoperative course from DPC data, we considered patients who were registered with the disease name of disruption of wound, not elsewhere classified (ICD-10: T81.3), or underwent ileostomy or colostomy after rectal resection to have AL.

Statistical analysis

In this study, we analyzed the risk of AL by multilevel logistic regression using a two-level structure of individuals nested within the 1,057 hospitals. The two-level structure was made up of patient-related and hospital-related factors. We performed age-sex adjusted model and multivariable model (Model 1). Model 1 included sex, age, body mass index (BMI), activities of daily living (ADL), smoking, surgical procedure, disease (ICD-10: C19 or C20, representing rectosigmoid and rectal cancer location), clinical TNM stage, coexisting disease, maintenance dialysis, preoperative blood transfusion, duration of anesthesia as patient-related factors, and hospital volume as hospital-related factor. Patients were divided into four equal-size groups according to hospital volume (1–14, 15–25, 26–39, and 40 cases or more per year). Model 2 included the fiscal year of the surgery as a covariate in addition to Model 1. We conducted preliminary analysis stratified by tumor site (C19 or C20) by the same analysis to detect the heterogeneity of association between subgroup. Stata, version 18 (StataCorp., College Station, TX, USA) was used for all statistical analyses.

Results

Patient demographics

A total of 68,022 patients who underwent surgery for rectosigmoid and rectal cancer were registered in the DPC database during the study period. Of these, 12,723 patients were excluded because of the following factors: polysurgery, colostomy, or ileostomy before or on the day of rectal surgery, missing data (TNM stage, BMI, ADL, duration of anesthesia, smoking), and 55,299 patients from 1,057 institutions were analyzed. Of these patients, 46,775 patients underwent rectal cancer surgery without ICG-FA (ICG− group) and 8,524 patients underwent surgery with ICG-FA (ICG+ group) (Fig. 1).

Trends in ICG-FA penetration rate in Japan

The proportion of institutions that adopted ICG-FA was 9% (87 institutions) in 2018. Since then, the cumulative proportion of institutions that adopted ICG-FA gradually increased, reaching 30% (261 institutions) in 2021 (Fig. 2). Among hospitals categorized by case volume, those with higher case volumes showed higher adoption rates of ICG-FA throughout the study period, as illustrated in Figure 2. From 2019 onward, the rate of adoption showed a steady upward trend across all volume categories. Figure 3 shows the overall proportion of ICG-FA use among all patients. Accordingly, the number of patients who underwent ICG-FA during rectal cancer surgery increased from 625 (4%) in 2018 to 3,236 (25%) in 2021 among all patients. Among patients treated at institutions where ICG-FA had been introduced, the proportion reached 62% in 2021.

Risk of AL after rectal cancer surgery

Table 2 shows the association between ICG-FA and AL after rectal cancer surgery. The risk of AL was 5.1% in the ICG− group and 4.3% in the ICG+ group. In the age–sex adjusted model, there was a significant difference between the ICG− and ICG+ groups (OR: 0.84; 95% CI: 0.74–0.96; p = 0.013). Hospital volume remained an independent risk factor; however, in the multivariable model (Model 1), after adjustment for both patient-level and hospital-level factors, the odds ratio for AL remained significantly lower in the ICG+ group than in the ICG− group (OR: 0.87; 95% CI: 0.76–1.00; p = 0.043). In Model 2, after adjusting for the fiscal year in which the surgery was performed, the odds ratio remained lower in the ICG+ group, but the difference was no longer statistically significant (OR: 0.91; 95% CI: 0.79–1.04; p = 0.167). The marginal risk of AL, estimated using Model 2, was 4.7% in the ICG+ group and 5.1% in the ICG− group.

Discussion

In this nationwide retrospective study using the DPC database, the use of ICG-FA during rectal cancer surgery was associated with a slightly lower risk of AL after adjustment for patient- and hospital-level factors. This finding was observed across a large number of institutions with varying surgical volumes, suggesting that the potential benefit of ICG-FA may be reproducible in routine clinical practice. Institutional surgical volume was included as a hospital-level factor in the multilevel model, and the association between ICG-FA use and AL was evaluated after adjustment for hospital volume, suggesting that the observed association was not explained solely by differences in surgical volume among institutions. After additional adjustment for fiscal year, the association between ICG-FA and AL was attenuated and was no longer statistically significant. This suggests that temporal improvements in surgical technique and perioperative management over the study period may have influenced the incidence of AL.

The increase in the use of ICG-FA after 2018 may partly be explained by the introduction of national insurance reimbursement for intraoperative blood flow assessment using indocyanine green in colorectal surgery in Japan, which likely facilitated wider adoption of this technique.

AL remains a major complication after rectal cancer surgery and is influenced by multiple patient-, tumor-, and surgical-related factors[12-15]. Because inadequate perfusion is considered one potential contributor to AL, ICG-FA has been introduced as an objective method for intraoperative perfusion assessment[16-18].

Several randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have evaluated the effectiveness of ICG-FA for the prevention of AL, with inconsistent results[7,19-21]. Some studies, including the EssentiAL and FLAG trials, demonstrated a reduction in AL with the use of ICG-FA, whereas others, such as the PILLAR trial and a multicenter randomized trial evaluating intraoperative angiography, did not show a statistically significant decrease in AL despite changes in the resection line based on perfusion assessment. In addition, more recent randomized trials, including the AVOID trial and other contemporary studies, have also reported mixed results regarding the effectiveness of ICG-FA[22-24]. These findings suggest that, although ICG-FA may help to identify inadequate blood flow intraoperatively, its impact on clinical outcomes remains variable. A recent high-quality systematic review and meta-analysis by Ryan et al. reported that intraoperative use of ICG-FA may be associated with a reduced risk of anastomotic leakage, particularly in left-sided and rectal colorectal resections[25]. The authors also suggested that future research should focus on implementation strategies and identifying specific subgroups that may benefit most from ICG-FA, rather than conducting further general efficacy trials.

However, despite these findings, the overall evidence from randomized controlled trials remains heterogeneous, and the effectiveness of ICG-FA in routine clinical practice is still debated. In this context, our nationwide study using real-world data provides complementary evidence reflecting actual clinical settings across institutions with varying surgical volumes. This study also adds value by reflecting diverse institutional and surgical practices in real-world settings.

Our preliminary analysis suggested that ICG-FA may be particularly beneficial for preventing AL in rectosigmoid cancer; however, this finding remains hypothesis-generating because no significant interaction was observed between tumor site and ICG-FA use (p for interaction = 0.226). A potential reason for this is that postoperative AL in rectal cancer surgery is influenced by many factors—such as low anastomosis, pelvic anatomy, and technical difficulty—in addition to poor perfusion; therefore, the proportion of the overall outcome that can be improved by ICG is relatively small. In contrast, in rectosigmoid cancer, perfusion assessment is more directly linked to outcomes, making the effects of ICG easier to detect. Furthermore, in the rectal group, the heterogeneity of cases, residual confounders, and the influence of adjunctive preventive measures may have further obscured the observed effects.

To our knowledge, this is one of the few studies evaluating the effectiveness of ICG-FA for the prevention of AL after rectal cancer surgery using a nationwide DPC database. This study has several limitations that should be interpreted in the context of its retrospective design and the characteristics of the DPC database.

First, because this was an observational study using administrative data, causal inferences regarding the effectiveness of ICG-FA cannot be definitively established. Although multilevel logistic regression was performed to adjust for both patient- and hospital-level factors, residual confounding from unmeasured variables remains possible.

Second, the definition of AL was necessarily indirect. Detailed postoperative clinical findings, radiologic results, and severity grading based on the Clavien–Dindo or International Study Group of Rectal Cancer (ISREC) classifications are not available in the DPC database. Therefore, AL was identified using diagnostic codes (ICD-10: T81.3) and records of ileostomy or colostomy creation after rectal resection. This approach may have resulted in misclassification, including the inclusion of non-anastomotic wound complications and the inability to capture mild or subclinical AL. However, because the same definition was applied uniformly across all patients, such misclassification is unlikely to differ systematically between the ICG-FA and non-ICG-FA groups and would be expected to attenuate, rather than exaggerate, the observed association.

Third, important surgical and oncological factors known to influence AL risk—such as tumor height from the anal verge, tumor location (rectosigmoid vs rectal), extent of mesorectal excision, number of stapler firings, intraoperative blood loss, and use of neoadjuvant therapy—could not be assessed because these data are not captured in the DPC database. Because the baseline risk of AL differs according to tumor height and tumor location, the inability to precisely stratify these factors may have influenced the observed incidence of AL in this study.

Despite these limitations, the major strength of this study lies in its large-scale, nationwide design, encompassing a broad spectrum of institutions and surgeons. While randomized controlled trials provide high internal validity under controlled conditions, the present study offers complementary real-world evidence reflecting routine colorectal surgical practice in Japan. Our findings suggest that the use of ICG-FA may be associated with a lower risk of clinically relevant AL following rectal cancer surgery in real-world settings.
