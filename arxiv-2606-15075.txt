The graded Grothendieck group 𝑲 0 gr \bm{K}_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}} is full for weighted Leavitt path algebras
Remarl Joseph Damalerio, Roozbeh Hazrat, Tran Giang Nam

Abstract
. The Graded Classification Conjecture asserts that the graded Grothendieck group K 0 gr K_{0}^{\mathrm{gr}} is a complete invariant for the classes of Leavitt path algebras and graph C ∗ C^{*} -algebras. The conjecture remains open, as neither a proof nor a counterexample is currently known. In this article, we extend the study of this invariant to the class of vertex-weighted Leavitt path algebras. We show that K 0 gr K_{0}^{\mathrm{gr}} distinguishes weighted Leavitt path algebras from ordinary (unweighted) Leavitt path algebras. We further prove that an isomorphism between the graded Grothendieck groups of weighted Leavitt path algebras induces an isomorphism between the corresponding semilattices of vertex-generated ideals. In addition, we show that K 0 gr K_{0}^{\mathrm{gr}} classifies the classical Leavitt algebras L 𝗄 ​ ( n , n + k ) L_{\mathsf{k}}(n,n+k) . Next, we prove that K 0 gr K_{0}^{\mathrm{gr}} is a full functor on the category of all weighted Leavitt path algebras. Consequently, in the special case where all weights are equal to 1 1 , we recover the lifting theorem established independently by Arnone and Vas for Leavitt path algebras. This confirms one direction of the Graded Classification Conjecture for Leavitt path algebras.

1.
Introduction

Given a graph
E
and a field
\mathsf{k}
, Abrams and Aranda Pino in
[
2
]
, and independently Ara, Moreno, and Pardo in
[
6
]
, introduced the
Leavitt path algebra

L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
, which is a quotient of a path algebra obtained by imposing some additional relations amongst the generators. These Leavitt path algebras generalise the algebras
L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,1+k)
constructed by Leavitt
[
19
]
, as universal rings without invariant basis number, and are also the discrete, purely algebraic version of graph
C^{*}
-algebras. We refer the reader to
[
1
]
and
[
9
]
for a detailed history and overview of Leavitt path algebras.

The classification of Leavitt path algebras has received a lot of attention, as they are closely related to the classification of graph
C^{*}
-algebras and those of symbolic dynamics. However, while graph
C^{*}
-algebras have been classified by means of
K
-theoretic invariants (see, e.g.,
[
25
,
26
,
10
]
), an analogous classification for Leavitt path algebras remains conjectural. The Graded Classification Conjecture for Leavitt path algebras states that the graded Grothendieck group
K^{\operatorname{gr}}_{0}
, is a complete invariant for
these algebras (
[
9
,
12
]
,
[
1
, §7.3.4]
):

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
E
and
F
be finite graphs and
\mathsf{k}
a field.

(1)

For any order preserving
\mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}]
-module homomorphism
\phi:K^{\operatorname{gr}}_{0}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E))\rightarrow K^{\operatorname{gr}}_{0}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(F))
with

\phi([L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)])=L_{\mathsf{k}}(F)
, there exists a unital
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathsf{k}
-homomorphism
\psi:L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)\rightarrow L_{\mathsf{k}}(F)
such that
K^{\operatorname{gr}}_{0}(\psi)=\phi
.

(2)

If in
(1)
, the
\phi
is an isomorphism, then there exists a unital
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathsf{k}
-isomorphism
\psi:L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)\rightarrow L_{\mathsf{k}}(F)
such that
K^{\operatorname{gr}}_{0}(\psi)=\phi
.

G. Arnone in
[
7
]
and L. Vas in
[
27
]
independently answered part (1) of Conjecture
1.1
in the affirmative, using different approaches. In fact Arnone showed that the lifting map can be diagonal preserving graded
*
-homomorphism, while Vas established the result for graphs that may contain infinitely many edges. Part (2) of the Graded Classification Conjectures remains open, as neither a proof nor a counterexample is currently known. The graded Grothendieck group
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}
is closely related to Krieger’s dimension group
[
18
]
, thereby connecting the classification of Leavitt path algebras with the classification of shift of finite types in symbolic dynamics
[
20
]
. For a comprehensive account of the graded classification conjectures and their connections to related topics, we refer the reader to
[
9
]
.

Whereas Leavitt path algebras model Leavitt algebras
L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,1+k)
, weighted Leavitt path algebras were introduced by the second author in
[
11
]
to cover the more general Leavitt algebras
L_{\mathsf{k}}(n,n+k)

[
19
]
, which cannot, in general, be realised as ordinary Leavitt path algebras. We also note that weighted Leavitt path algebras are closely connected to sandpile models (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
15
,
16
]
).
Since weighted Leavitt path algebras are also naturally
\mathbb{Z}
-graded rings, it is natural to investigate whether the graded Grothendieck group
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}
carries structural information about these algebras analogous to that provided by
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}
in the ordinary Leavitt path algebra setting.

In this article, we show that
K^{\operatorname{gr}}_{0}
detects the presence of weights, thereby distinguishing weighted Leavitt path algebras from their unweighted counterparts. This is not the case when one uses the ordinary
K_{0}
-group. Indeed, for
k,l,n\in\mathbb{N}
, there are order isomorphisms

K_{0}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(1,1+k))\cong K_{0}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(n,n+k))\cong K_{0}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(n+l,n+k+l))\cong\mathbb{Z}_{k}.

Nevertheless, the algebras are pairwise non-isomorphic: the first is an ordinary Leavitt path algebra, while the others arise only as weighted Leavitt path algebras.

For a vertex-weighted graph
(E,w)
, we show that
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}
is a full functor, that means, Conjecture
1.1
(1) holds for vertex-weighted graphs. By specializing to the case where all weights are equal to one, we recover the corresponding results of Arnone and Vas mentioned above. Furthermore, we obtain that every order preserving
\mathbb{Z}
-module isomorphism between
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w))
and
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(F,w))
induces an isomorphism between the semilattices of all vertex-generated ideals of
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
and
L_{\mathsf{k}}(F,w)
, which provides further evidence in support of Conjecture
1.1
(2) for vertex-weighted graphs.

To do so, we begin by defining the graph monoid
M_{(E,w)}
and the talented monoid
T_{(E,w)}
associated with a vertex-weighted graph
(E,w)
. We show that
T_{(E,w)}
is a cancellative monoid and that its Grothendieck group completion is precisely the graded Grothendieck group
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w))
. The cancellativity of
T_{(E,w)}
implies that it coincides with the positive cone of
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w))
, allowing one to work directly with the monoid rather than with the
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}
-group. Working with the talented monoid has the advantage of being independent of the algebra itself while still providing detailed information about its elements (such as minimal elements, atoms, and related invariants) and, consequently, about the geometry of the underlying graph, including the number of cycles and their lengths. Moreover, we use Bergman machinery
[
8
]
and its graded version developed in
[
14
]
to establish Conjecture
1.1
(1) for vertex-weighted graphs.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we prove that the graph monoid associated with an acyclic vertex-weighted graph is cancellative (Theorem
2.6
). Consequently, the talented monoid associated with a vertex-weighted graph is cancellative (Proposition
2.20
(2)). We also give nice connections between the graph monoid
M_{(E,w)}
of a vertex-weighted graph
(E,w)
and the talented monoid
T_{(E,w)}
(Proposition
2.20
). Moreover, we show that, in general, the talented monoid associated with a vertex-weighted graph cannot be realized as the talented monoid of an ordinary graph (Example
2.16
). In Section
3
, based on Section
2
, we demonstrate that the positive cone of the graded Grothendieck group
K^{\operatorname{gr}}_{0}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w))
of the Leavitt path algebra of a vertex-weighted graph
(E,w)
is exactly the talented monoid of
(E,w)
(Corollary
3.4
), and show that the graded Grothendieck group
K^{\operatorname{gr}}_{0}
distinguishes the class of vertex-weighted Leavitt path algebras from the unweighted class (Theorem
3.7
). Moreover, we prove that every order preserving
\mathbb{Z}
-module isomorphism between
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w))
and
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(F,w))
provides an isomorphism between the semilattices of all vertex-generated ideals of
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
and
L_{\mathsf{k}}(F,w)
(Theorem
3.9
). In Section
4
, we prove that Conjecture
1.1
(1) holds for vertex-weighted graphs (Theorems
4.1
and
4.5
) and show that the graded Grothendieck group
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}
classifies the Leavitt algebras
L_{\mathsf{k}}(n,n+k)
(Theorem

2.
Weighted graphs and graph monoids

The main goal of this section is to establish the graph monoid associated with an acyclic vertex-weighted graph is cancellative (Theorem
2.6
). Also, we completely describe the structure of all order ideals of the graph monoid associated with a vertex-weighted graph (Theorem
2.12
) via the lattice of all saturated hereditary subsets. Moreover, we give several connections between the graph monoid
M_{(E,w)}
associated with a vertex-weighted graph
(E,w)
and the talented monoid
T_{(E,w)}
(Proposition
2.20
); in particular, we provide a lattice isomorphism between the lattice of all order ideals of
M_{(E,w)}
and the lattice of all
\mathbb{Z}
-order ideals of
T_{(E,w)}
.

We start by setting some terminology and notation about graphs. A
(directed) graph
is a quadruple
E=(E^{0},E^{1},s,r)
, where
E^{0}
and
E^{1}
are sets and
s,r:E^{1}\rightarrow E^{0}
are maps. Throughout, “graph” will always mean “directed graph”. The elements of
E^{0}
are called
vertices
and the elements of
E^{1}

edges
. (We allow the empty set to be viewed as a graph with
E^{0}=E^{1}=\varnothing.
) If
e
is an edge, then
s(e)
is called its
source
and
r(e)
its
range
. If
v
is a vertex and
e
an edge, we say that
v

emits

e
if
s(e)=v
, and
v

receives

e
if
r(e)=v
. An edge
e
is called a
loop at

v
in case
s(e)=v=r(e)
.
A vertex is called a

sink
if it emits no edges.
A vertex is called
regular
if it is not a sink and does not emit infinitely many edges. The subset of
E^{0}
consisting of all the regular vertices is denoted by
E^{0}_{\operatorname{reg}}
. Similarly, the subset of
E^{0}
consisting of all the sinks is denoted by
E^{0}_{\operatorname{sink}}
.

A graph is called
row-finite
if any vertex emits a finite number (possibly zero) of edges. The graph
E
is called
finite
if
E^{0}
and
E^{1}
are finite sets. In this paper we assume throughout that all graphs are row-finite, and thus
E^{0}_{\operatorname{reg}}
consists of all vertices which are not sinks.

A
path

p
in
E
is
a sequence
p=e_{1}e_{2}\cdots e_{n}
of edges in
E
such that

r(e_{i})=s(e_{i+1})
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. We define
s(p)=s(e_{1})
, and
r(p)=r(e_{n})
.
By definition, the
length

|p|
of
p
is
n
. We assign the length zero to vertices. A
closed path
(based at
v
) is a path
p
such that
s(p)=r(p)=v
. A
cycle
(based at
v
) is a closed path
p=e_{1}e_{2}\cdots e_{n}
based at
v
such that
s(e_{i})\neq s(e_{j})
for any
i\neq j
. We say a vertex
v\in E^{0}
has
no bifurcation
if
|s^{-1}(u)|\leq 1
for all
u\in T(v)
, where
T(v)
is the usual tree of the vertex
v
.

A
weighted graph
is a pair
(E,w)
, where
E
is a graph and
w:E^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{+}
is a map. If
e\in E^{1}
, then
w(e)
is called the
weight
of
e
.
For each regular vertex
v
in a weighted graph
(E,w)
we set
w(v):=\max\{w(e)\mid e\in s^{-1}(v)\}
. This gives a map (called
w
again)
w:E_{\operatorname{reg}}^{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{+}
.
A weighted graph is called a
vertex-weighted graph
if, for each regular vertex
v
, all edges emitted from
v
have the same weight,
i.e., if
w(e)=w(e^{\prime})
for all
e,e^{\prime}\in s^{-1}(v)
. In this case coincides with the weight of any edge emitting from
v
. A weighted graph
(E,w)
is called
acyclic
if the graph
E
has no cycles.

Let
(E,w)
be a weighted graph. An edge
e\in E^{1}
is called
unweighted
if
w(e)=1
and
weighted
if
w(e)>1
. The subset of
E^{1}
consisting of all unweighted edges is denoted by
E_{uw}^{1}
and the subset consisting of all weighted edges by
E_{w}^{1}
.

The notion of the monoid associated to a weighted graph was introduced by the second author in
[
11
]
and later generalised to arbitrary weighted graphs by R. Preusser in
[
21
]
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
21
, Definition 11]
)
.

Let
(E,w)
be a row-finite vertex-weighted graph.
We define the
weighted graph monoid
of
(E,w)
to be

M_{(E,w)}\ \ :=\mathbb{F}_{E}\ \Big/\ \Big\langle w(v)v=\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}r(e)\mid v\in E_{\operatorname{reg}}^{0}\Big\rangle,

(2.1)

where
\mathbb{F}_{E}
is the free commutative monoid on the set
E^{0}
of vertices of
E
.

We note that for an unweighted graph
E
(that is, when the weight function is constantly equal to
1
), the graph monoid (
2.1
) reduces to the commutative monoid
M_{E}
originally defined in
[
6
]
. Moreover, there exist weighted graph monoids that cannot be realised as monoids associated to unweighted graphs (see, for example,
[
15
, Example 3.10]
).

Let
(E,\omega)
be a vertex weighted graph. There is an explicit description of the congruence on the free commutative monoid
\mathbb{F}_{E}
given by the defining relations of
M_{(E,\omega)}
in Definition
2.1
, as follows. For
v\in E^{0}
, define the “
\operatorname{{\bf r}}
-transform on
\mathbb{F}_{E}
” by setting

\operatorname{{\bf r}}(\omega(v)v)\ :=\ \sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}r(e)\text{ for al }v\in E^{0}_{\operatorname{reg}}.

(2.2)

The nonzero elements of
\mathbb{F}_{E}
can be written uniquely up to permutation as
\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}v_{i}
, where
v_{i}
are distinct vertices and
k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. Define a binary relation

\rightarrow_{1}
on
\mathbb{F}_{E}
by

\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}v_{i}\ \longrightarrow_{1}\ \Big(\sum_{i\neq j}k_{i}v_{i}\Big)+(k_{j}-w(v_{j}))v_{j}+\operatorname{{\bf r}}(w(v_{j})v_{j}),

(2.3)

whenever
j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
and

k_{j}\geq w(v_{j})
. Let
\rightarrow
be the transitive and reflexive closure of
\rightarrow_{1}

on
\mathbb{F}_{E}
. Namely

a\rightarrow b\ \ \ \text{ if }a=b,\ \mbox{or}\ a=a_{0}\rightarrow_{1}a_{1}\rightarrow_{1}\dots\rightarrow_{1}a_{k}=b.

(2.4)

Finally, let
\sim
be the congruence on
\mathbb{F}_{E}
generated by the relation
\rightarrow
. That is,
a\sim b
in case there is a string
a=a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{n}=b
in
\mathbb{F}_{E}
such that
a_{i}\rightarrow_{1}a_{i+1}
or
a_{i+1}\rightarrow_{1}a_{i}
for each
0\leq i\leq n-1
. Then

M_{(E,w)}=\mathbb{F}_{E}/\sim.

To avoid cumbersome equivalence class notation, is standard (but not technically correct) to denote the elements of
M_{(E,w)}
and the elements of
\mathbb{F}_{E}
using the same symbols. For instance, we will sometimes write
a=b
in
M_{(E,w)}
for elements
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{E}
.

The relation
2.4
may be interpreted as follows. Let

x=\sum_{i}k_{i}x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{E}
,
x_{i}\in E^{0}
.

If
k_{i}\geq w(x_{i})
, then we may “let the vertex
x_{i}
flow” by replacing the term
w(x_{i})x_{i}
with the sum of the vertices adjacent to
x_{i}
. More precisely, we obtain that

y_{1}=\left(\sum_{j\neq i}k_{j}x_{j}\right)+(k_{i}-w(x_{i}))x_{i}+\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(x_{i})}r(e),

and write
x\to y_{1}
.
Observe that this operation is permitted only when the summand
k_{i}x_{i}
appears in the expression for
x
and satisfies
k_{i}\geq w(x_{i})
.

Repeating this procedure and allowing a vertex of
y_{1}
to flow, we obtain another element
y_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{E}
such that
y_{1}\to y_{2}
. In general, the relation
\to_{1}
is applied successively to vertices appearing in the decomposition of elements of
\mathbb{F}_{E}
. By definition of the relation
\to
, every element
y\in\mathbb{F}_{E}
satisfying
x\to y
is obtained from
x
through a finite sequence of such vertex flows.

Next we recall the “confluence” property of the monoid
M_{(E,w)}
, established by Abrams and the second author in
[
3
, Lemma 3.1]
. Informally, this property asserts that whenever two elements
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{E}
represent the same element in the quotient monoid

M_{(E,w)}=\mathbb{F}_{E}/\sim,

there exists a common element of
\mathbb{F}_{E}
to which both
a
and
b
flow.

Lemma 2.2

(The Confluence Lemma)
.

Let
(E,w)
be a vertex-weighted graph and
M_{(E,w)}
its associated monoid. For
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{E}
, we have
a=b
in
M_{(E,w)}
(i.e.,
a\sim b
in
\mathbb{F}_{E}
) if and only if there exists
c\in\mathbb{F}_{E}
such that
a\rightarrow c
and
b\rightarrow c
.

Consequently, we obtain the following result, which follows immediately from
[
11
, Theorem 5.21]
, where Bergman algebras are used in the proof.

Corollary 2.3
.

For any vertex weighted graph
(E,\omega)
, the monoid
M_{(E,\omega)}
is conical, i.e., for all
x,y\in M_{(E,\omega)}
,
x+y=0
implies
x=y=0.

Proof.

Let
x
and
y
be two elements in
M_{(E,\omega)}
such that
x+y=0
. Then, by The Confluence Lemma,
x+y\rightarrow 0
in
\mathbb{F}_{E}
. Suppose that
there is a vertex
w\in E^{0}
appearing in the presentation of
x
. We note that any possible transformations of
w
or its multiple would give a vertex and subsequently any further transformations always contain a vertex. Thus
x+y
can not be transformed to
0
, a contradiction.
∎

The next part of this section is to show that the weighted graph monoid associated to an acyclic vertex-weighted graph is cancellative. To do so, we first establish several useful results. Recall that for a graph
E=(E^{0},E^{1},s,r)
, there exists a preorder “
\geq
” on
E^{0}
defined by
u\geq v

whenever there exists a path
p
in
E
such that
s(p)=u
and
r(p)=v
. We write
u>v
if
u\geq v
and
u\neq v
. We also note that if
E
is acyclic, then the preorder is exactly a partial order.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(E,w)
be a finite acyclic vertex-weighted graph, and let
a
be an arbitrary element of
M_{(E,w)}
. Then
a
can be written in the form

a=\sum_{v\in E^{0}}k_{v}v,

(2.5)

where
0\leq k_{v}<w(v)
for every
v\in E^{0}_{\operatorname{reg}}
.

Proof.

If
a=0
, then the conclusion is obvious. Hence, we can assume that
a\neq 0
. Then, there exists a nonempty subset
X\subseteq E^{0}
such that

a=\sum_{v\in X}m_{v}v,

where
m_{v}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Let

H_{X}:=\{v\in X\mid v\in E^{0}_{\operatorname{reg}}\text{ and }m_{v}\geq w(v)\}.

If
H_{X}=\emptyset
, then
a
is already in the form of (
2.5
). Otherwise, we denote by
H_{X}^{\max}
the set of all maximal elements of
H_{X}
with respect to the partial ordering
\geq
. This set is not empty as
E
is a finite acyclic graph. For
u\in H_{X}^{\max}
, we write
m_{u}=q_{u}w(u)+r_{u}
, where
0\leq r_{u}<w(u)
. Since
w(u)u=\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(u)}r(e)
, it follows that

m_{u}u=r_{u}u+\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(u)}q_{u}r(e).

Therefore, we obtain that

a=r_{u}u+\sum_{v\in H^{\max}_{X}\setminus\{u\}}m_{v}v+\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(u)}q_{u}r(e)+\sum_{v\in X\setminus H^{\max}_{X}}m_{v}v.

Continuing this same process now for
v\in H^{\max}_{X}\setminus\{u\}
, we see that after
|H^{\max}_{X}|
steps we obtain that

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in H^{\max}_{X}}r_{u}u+\sum_{u\in H^{\max}_{X}}\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(u)}q_{u}r(e)+\sum_{v\in X\setminus H^{\max}_{X}}m_{v}v

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in H^{\max}_{X}}r_{u}u+\sum_{v\in r(s^{-1}(H^{\max}_{X})))\cup(X\setminus H^{\max}_{X})}m^{\prime}_{v}v.

Let

X_{1}=X\cup r(s^{-1}(H^{\max}_{X}))
and
H_{X_{1}}:=\{v\in X_{1}\mid v\in E^{0}_{\operatorname{reg}}\text{ and }m^{\prime}_{v}\geq w(v)\}
.

If
H_{X_{1}}=\emptyset
, then the conclusion is obvious.
Otherwise, we denote by
H^{\max}_{X_{1}}
the set of all maximal elements of
H_{X_{1}}
. Since
E
is acyclic, it follows that
H^{\max}_{X}\cap H^{\max}_{X_{1}}=\varnothing

and for each
v\in H^{\max}_{X_{1}}
, there exists a vertex
u\in H^{\max}_{X}
such that
u>v
. Continuing the above same process now for
v\in H^{\max}_{X_{1}}
, we see that after
|H^{\max}_{X_{1}}|
steps we obtain that

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in H^{\max}_{X}}r_{u}u+\sum_{x\in H^{\max}_{X_{1}}}r_{x}x+\sum_{v\in r(s^{-1}(H^{\max}_{X_{1}}))\cup(X_{1}\setminus(H^{\max}_{X}\cup H^{\max}_{X_{1}}))}m^{\prime\prime}_{v}v,

where
r_{u}<w(u)
for all
u\in H^{\max}_{X}
and

r_{x}<w(x)
for all
x\in H^{\max}_{X_{1}}
.

Continuing this same process now on the set
X_{2}:=X_{1}\cup r(s^{-1}(H^{\max}_{X_{1}}))
, we see that after
n_{0}
steps we obtain that
H_{X_{n_{0}}}=\varnothing
, thus finishing the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(E,w)
be a finite acyclic vertex-weighted graph, and let
a
be a nonzero element of
\mathbb{F}_{E}
. Then there exist a positive integer
n
and a nonzero element

a^{\prime}=\sum_{v\in E^{0}_{\operatorname{sink}}}n_{v}v\in\mathbb{F}_{E}

such that
na\rightarrow a^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
a\in\mathbb{F}_{E}\setminus\{0\}
, there exists a nonempty subset
X\subseteq E^{0}
such that

a=\sum_{v\in X}m_{v}v,

where
m_{v}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
. We now define
H_{0}=X
and for
n\geq 1
we define inductively

H_{n}=r(s^{-1}(H_{n-1}))\cup(H_{n-1}\cap E^{0}_{\operatorname{sink}}).

Since
E
is finite and acyclic, there exists a natural number
n_{0}
such that
H_{n_{0}}
is a nonempty subset of
E^{0}_{\operatorname{sink}}
. Among these numbers, we may choose
n_{0}
to be minimal. For
z\in H_{1}\setminus E^{0}_{\operatorname{sink}}
, there exist a vertex
u\in X
and an edge
f\in s^{-1}(u)
such that
z=r(f).
Let
k_{u}
be the smallest positive integer such that
k_{u}m_{u}=l_{u}w(u)
for some
l_{u}\in\mathbb{N}
. We then have

k_{u}a=k_{u}m_{u}u+\sum_{v\in X\setminus\{u\}}k_{u}m_{v}v

and

w(u)u=\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(u)}r(e)=z+\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(u)\setminus\{f\}}r(e),

where
r(e)\in H_{1}
for all
e\in s^{-1}(u)\setminus\{f\}
. Therefore, we obtain that

k_{u}a=\sum_{v\in X\setminus\{u\}}k_{u}m_{v}v+l_{u}z+\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(u)\setminus\{f\}}l_{u}r(e).

Continuing this same process now for
v\in H_{1}\setminus(E^{0}_{\operatorname{sink}}\cup\{z\})
, we see that after at most
|H_{1}\setminus E^{0}_{\operatorname{sink}}|
steps we obtain that

n^{\prime}a=\sum_{v\in H_{1}}m^{\prime}_{v}v,

where
n^{\prime}\geq 1
and each
m^{\prime}_{v}\geq 1
. Continuing this same process now on the set
H_{1}
, we see that after
n_{0}
steps we obtain that

na=\sum_{v\in H_{n_{0}}}n_{v}v,

where
n\geq 1
and each
n_{v}\geq 1
, thus finishing the proof.
∎

Let
(E,w)
be a weighted graph. A
weighted subgraph

X
of
(E,w_{E})
is the weighted graph

X=(X^{0},X^{1},r_{X},s_{X},w_{X})

such that
X^{0}\subseteq E^{0}
,
X^{1}\subseteq E^{1}
, and
r_{X},s_{X}
are respectively the restrictions of
s_{E},r_{E}
on
X^{1}
and

w_{X}(e)=w_{E}(e)
for all
e\in X^{1}
.

A weighted subgraph
X
is called
complete
if

s^{-1}_{X}(v)=s^{-1}_{E}(v)

for all
v\in X^{0}_{\operatorname{reg}}
.
By
[
11
, Lemma 5.19]
, the weighted graph
E
can be expressed as the direct limit

(E,w)=\varinjlim(E_{i},w_{i}),

where
\{(E_{i},w_{i})\}
is a directed system of finite complete weighted subgraphs of
(E,w)
.

We are now in a position to state the first main result of this section.

Theorem 2.6
.

For every acyclic vertex-weighted graph
(E,w)
, the monoid
M_{(E,w)}
is cancellative.

Proof.

Let
(E,w)
be an arbitrary acyclic vertex-weighted graph. By
[
11
, Lemma 5.19 (1)]
, it follows that

(E,w)=\varinjlim(E_{i},w_{i}),

where each
(E_{i},w_{i})
is a finite complete weighted subgraphs of
(E,w)
. Since
(E,w)
is an acyclic vertex-weighted graph, each
(E_{i},w_{i})
is also an acyclic vertex-weighted graph. By
[
11
, Lemma 5.19 (2) and Theorem 5.21]
, the monoid
M_{(E,w)}
can be realised as the directed limit

M_{(E,w)}=\varinjlim M_{(E_{i},w_{i})}.

From this observation, it suffices to prove that
M_{(E,w)}
is cancellative in the case where
(E,w)
is a finite acyclic vertex-weighted graph.

Assume that
(E,w)
is a finite acyclic vertex-weighted graph. Let
a,b,c
be elements of
\mathbb{F}_{E}
such that
a+c=b+c
in
M_{(E,w)}
. Equivalently,
a+nc=b+nc
in
M_{(E,w)}
for all
n\geq 1
.
We claim that
a=b
in
M_{(E,w)}
. The conclusion is immediate when
c=0
. Thus, we may assume that
c\neq 0
. Then, by Lemma
2.5
, there exist a positive integer
n
and a nonzero element

c^{\prime}=\sum_{v\in E^{0}_{\operatorname{sink}}}k_{v}v\in\mathbb{F}_{E}

such that
nc\rightarrow c^{\prime}.
Consequently, we have

nc=c^{\prime}
in
M_{(E,w)}.

By Lemma
2.4
, there exist elements

a^{\prime}=\sum_{v\in E^{0}}n_{v}v
and
b^{\prime}=\sum_{v\in E^{0}}v_{v}v\in\mathbb{F}_{E}

such that

a=a^{\prime}
and
b=b^{\prime}
in
M_{(E,w)},

where
m_{v}<w(v)
and
n_{v}<w(v)
for all
v\in E^{0}_{\operatorname{reg}}
. From these observations, it follows that

a^{\prime}+c^{\prime}=b^{\prime}+c^{\prime}
in
M_{(E,w)}
.

By the Confluence Lemma
2.2
, there exits an element
x\in\mathbb{F}_{E}
such that
a^{\prime}+c^{\prime}\rightarrow x
and
b^{\prime}+c^{\prime}\rightarrow x
.
By the construction of
a^{\prime}
,
b^{\prime}
and
c^{\prime}
, neither
a^{\prime}+c^{\prime}
nor
b^{\prime}+c^{\prime}
admits any further reductions. Hence,

a^{\prime}+c^{\prime}=x=b^{\prime}+c^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{F}_{E}
. This implies that
a^{\prime}=b^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{F}_{E}
. Consequently, we have

a=b
in
M_{(E,w)}
.
This finishes the proof.
∎

We note that for every (unweighted) graph

is cancellative if and only if no cycle in

). However, Example

Question 2.8
.

Can one characterise all vertex-weighted graphs whose graph monoids are cancellative?

In the remainder of this section, we describe the structure of weighted graph monoids
M_{(E,\omega)}
in terms of saturated hereditary subsets of
E
. To do so, we need to recall some useful notions.

Definition 2.9

(
[
15
, Definition 2.7]
)
.

Let
(E,\omega)
be a vertex-weighted graph and
H\subseteq E^{0}
.

(1) The set
H
is called
hereditary
if for any
e\in E^{1}
,
s(e)\in H
implies
r(e)\in H
.

(2) The set
H
is
saturated
if whenever
v\in E_{\operatorname{reg}}^{0}
with the property that
r(s^{-1}(v))\subseteq H
, then
v\in H
.

(3) The
hereditary saturated closure
of
H
, denoted by
\overline{H}
, is the
smallest hereditary and saturated subset of
E^{0}
containing
H
.

We denote by
\mathcal{H}_{(E,\omega)}
the set of all saturated hereditary subsets of
E^{0}
.

Example 2.10
.

Let
(E,\omega)
be the following vertex weighted graph

w
v
s
u
(e,2)
(f,2)
(g,2)
(h,2)

where all edges have weight
2
. Then we have

\mathcal{H}_{(E,\omega)}=\big\{\varnothing,\{s,u\},E^{0}\big\}.

Let
(E,\omega)
be a vertex-weighted graph and
H\in\mathcal{H}_{(E,\omega)}
. We denote by
(E_{H},\omega_{r})
the
restriction weighted graph

E_{H}^{0}:=H
,
E_{H}^{1}:=\{e\in E^{1}\mid s(e)\in H\}

and the source, range and weight function in

are exactly the source, range and weight function in

restricted to

.

The following proposition provides that every nonempty saturated hereditary subset
H
of a vertex-weighted graph
E
induces a weighted graph submonoid
M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
of
M_{(E,\omega)}
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
(E,\omega)
be a vertex-weighted graph and
H\in\mathcal{H}_{(E,\omega)}
. Then, the map

\psi_{H}:M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}\rightarrow M_{(E,\omega)}
, defined by
v\mapsto v
, is an injective homomorphism of monoids. Consequently,

M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
is a submonoid of
M_{(E,\omega)}
.

Proof.

Clearly the map
\psi_{H}:M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}\rightarrow M_{(E,\omega)},v\mapsto v
is well-defined.
Let
a,b\in M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
such that
a=b
in
M_{(E,\omega)}
. By the Confluence
Lemma
2.2
, there exists an element
c\in\mathbb{F}_{E}
such that
a\rightarrow c
and
b\rightarrow c
. Since
H
is hereditary, all the transformations occur in
E_{H}
, and so
c\in\mathbb{F}_{E_{H}}
. This implies that
a=b
in
M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
. Hence,
\psi_{H}
is an injective homomorphism of monoids, thus finishing the proof.
∎

Recall (see
[
1
, Page 131]
) that an
order-ideal
of a commutative monoid
M
is a submonoid
I
of
M
such that, for any
x,y\in M
, if
x+y\in I
then
x,y\in I
. An order-ideal may also be described as a submonoid
I
of
M
which is hereditary with respect to the canonical preorder
\leq
on
M
:
x\leq y
and
y\in I
imply
x\in I
, where the preorder
\leq
on
M
is defined by
setting
x\leq y
if
y=x+m
for some
m\in M
. For each
X\subseteq M
, the order-ideal of
M
generated by
X
is the set

\langle X\rangle:=\big\{m\in M\mid m\leq\sum^{n}_{i=1}x_{i},\ n\in\mathbb{N}^{+},\ x_{i}\in X\big\}.

The set
\mathcal{L}(M)
of order-ideals of
M
forms a complete lattice such that the join of two elements
I,J
is exactly the order-ideal of
M
generated by
I+J
.

We are now in a position to establish the second main result of this section describing order-ideals of the graph monoid
M_{(E,\omega)}
of a vertex-weighted graph
(E,\omega)
, which is an analogue of the corresponding result for sandpile monoids established in
[
16
, Theorem 2.10]
.

Theorem 2.12
.

For any vertex weighted graph
(E,\omega)
, the following statements hold:

(1)

0
and
M_{(E,\omega)}
are two order-ideals of
M_{(E,\omega)}
;

(2)
For all
H\in\mathcal{H}_{(E,\omega)}
,
M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
is an order-ideal of
M_{(E,\omega)}
;

(3)
For every order-ideal
I
of
M_{(E,\omega)}
,
I=M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
, where
H=I\cap E^{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{(E,\omega)}
;

(4)

\mathcal{H}_{(E,\omega)}\cong\mathcal{L}(M_{(E,\omega)})
as lattices.

Proof.

(1) By Corollary
2.3
, the zero ideal is an order-ideals of
M_{(E,\omega)}
. Also,
M_{(E,\omega)}
is obviously an order-ideals of
M_{(E,\omega)}
.

(2) Let
H
be an element in
\mathcal{H}_{(E,\omega)}
. By Proposition
2.11
,
M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
is a submonoid of
M_{(E,\omega)}
. Assume that
x
any
y
are elements in
M_{(E,\omega)}
with
x\leq y
and
y\in M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
. Then,
y=x+z
in
M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
for some
z\in M_{(E,\omega)}
. By the Confluence Lemma, there exists an element
c\in\mathbb{F}_{E}
such that
y\rightarrow c
and
x+z\rightarrow c
. Since
y\in M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
,
\text{supp}(y)\subseteq E^{0}_{H}
, and so
\text{supp}(c)\subseteq E^{0}_{H}
. Assume that
x\notin M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
. Then, there exists a vertex
v\in\text{supp}(x)
such that
v\notin E^{0}_{H}
. Since
H
is saturated and hereditary, any possible transformations of
v
or its multiple would give a vertex which is not in
E_{H}
and subsequently any further transformations always contain a vertex which is not in
E_{H}
. This shows that
x+y
cannot be transformed to
c
, a contradiction, and so
x\in M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
. Thus
M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
is an order-ideal of
M_{(E,\omega)}
.

(3) Let
I
be an order-ideal of
M_{(E,\omega)}
and
H:=I\cap E^{0}
. We claim that
H\in\mathcal{H}_{(E,\omega)}
. Indeed, let
e\in E^{1}
with
v:=s(e)\in H
. We then have
v\in I
and
\sum_{f\in s^{-1}(v)}r(f)=\omega(v)v\in I
, and so
r(f)\in I
for all
f\in s^{-1}(v)
(since
I
is an order-ideal of
M_{(E,\omega)}
); in particular,
r(e)\in I
. Hence,
H
is hereditary.
Now let
v
be a regular vertex in
E
with
r(s^{-1}(v))\subseteq I
. Then,
\omega(v)v=\sum_{f\in s^{-1}(v)}r(f)\in I
, and so
v\in I
. Therefore,
H
is saturated, showing the claim.

We next prove that
I=M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
. Since
H\subseteq I
and
I
is a submonoid of
M_{(E,\omega)}
, we have
M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}\subseteq I
. Let
x
be a nonzero element in
I
. Since
I
is an order-ideal of
M_{(E,\omega)}
, we must have
\text{supp}(x)\subseteq I
, and so
\text{supp}(x)\subseteq H
. This implies that
x\in M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
, that means,
I\subseteq M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})}
.

(4) Let

\phi:\mathcal{H}_{(E,\omega)}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}(M_{(E,\omega)})

be the map defined by:

H\longmapsto\phi(H)=M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})},

and let

\psi:\mathcal{L}(M_{(E,\omega)})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{(E,\omega)}

be the map defined by:

I\longmapsto\psi(I)=I\cap E^{0}.

It is obvious that both maps are order-preserving. Moreover, we have
\psi(\phi(H))=\psi(M_{(E_{H},\omega_{r})})=H
for every
H\in\mathcal{H}_{(E,\omega)}
, and hence
\psi\phi=id_{\mathcal{H}_{(E,\omega)}}.
Conversely, by item (3), we obtain that
\phi\psi=id_{\mathcal{L}(M_{(E,\omega)})}.
Therefore,
\phi
and
\psi
are order-preserving mutually inverse maps. Consequently, we obtain that

\mathcal{H}{(E,\omega)}\cong\mathcal{L}(M{(E,\omega)})

as lattices, thus finishing the proof.
∎

Next we define the “talented” version of the weighted graph monoids (

).

Definition 2.14
.

Let
(E,w)
be a row-finite vertex-weighted graph.
We define the
talented monoid
of
(E,w)
to be

T_{(E,w)}\ \ :=\\
\Big\langle v(i)\mid v\in E^{0},\ i\in\mathbb{Z}\Big\rangle\ \Big/\ \Big\langle w(v)v(i)=\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}r(e)(i+1)\,\big|\,v\in E_{\operatorname{reg}}^{0},i\in\mathbb{Z}\Big\rangle,

(2.6)

We should mention that for a directed row-finite graph

originally defined in

A
\Gamma
-monoid
, where
\Gamma
is an abelian group, consists of a commutative monoid
M
equipped with a group action of
\Gamma
by monoid homomorphisms. The image of an element
m\in M
under the action of a group element
\gamma\in\Gamma
is denoted by
{}^{\gamma}m
. A monoid homomorphism
\phi:M\longrightarrow M^{\prime}
is called a
\Gamma
-homomorphism
if
\phi({}^{\gamma}m)={}^{\gamma}\phi(m)
for all
m\in M
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

There is a well-defined action of
\mathbb{Z}
on the monoid
T_{(E,w)}
defined on the generators by
{}^{n}v(i):=v(i+n)
, where
i,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and extends to all of the monoid.
Throughout we write
v:=v(0)
.
We denote
1_{E}:=\sum_{v\in E^{0}}v
, a distinguished order unit element both in
M_{(E,w)}
and
T_{(E,w)}

For clarification, we illustrate Definition
2.14
by presenting the following examples.
We first recall the notion of refinement for monoids.
A commutative monoid
M
is called a
refinement monoid
if for any equality

a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}\;=\;b_{1}+\cdots+b_{n},

with
a_{i},b_{j}\in M
, there exist elements
x_{ij}\in M
such that

a_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{ij}\quad\text{for all }i=1,\dots,m,

and

b_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{ij}\quad\text{for all }j=1,\dots,n.

We shall show that only the talented monoids of unweighted Leavitt path algebras are refinement (Proposition
3.6
).

Example 2.15
.

Let
E
be a graph with two loops of weight one. Then

T_{E}=\frac{\langle v(i)\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\rangle}{\langle v(i)=2v(i+1)\rangle}.

One can see that
T_{E}\cong\mathbb{N}[1/2]
, with the action
{}^{1}n=1/2n
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}[1/2]
.

On the other hand, for the weighted graph
F
consisting of two loops of weight 2, we have

T_{(F,w)}=\frac{\langle v(i)\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\rangle}{\langle 2v(i)=2v(i+1)\rangle}.

We obtain that
T_{(F,w)}
is cancellative but not a refinement monoid. The cancellative property follows from Proposition
2.20
(2) below. Assume that
T_{(F,w)}
is a refinement monoid. Since
2v(0)=2v(1)
, there exist elements
a_{ij}\in T_{(F,w)}
,
1\leq i,j\leq 2
, such that

v(0)=a_{11}+a_{12}=a_{21}+a_{22}\text{ and }v(1)=a_{11}+a_{21}=a_{12}+a_{22}

in
T_{(F,w)}
. By Confluence Lemma
2.2
, we have
a_{i1}+a_{i2}\rightarrow v(0)
for all
i
. It follows that for each
i
, either
a_{i1}=0
and
a_{i2}=v(0)
, or
a_{i2}=0
and
a_{i1}=v(0)
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
a_{11}=0
and
a_{12}=v(0)
. We then have
v(1)=v(0)+a_{22}
in
T_{(F,w)}
. By Confluence Lemma
2.2
, both
v(1)
and
v(0)+a_{22}
flow to
the same element in the free monoid, and so
v(0)+a_{22}\rightarrow v(1)
. This implies that
a_{22}\rightarrow nv(0)
for some

. Therefore,

v(1)=v(0)+a_{22}=(n+1)v(0)=2v(1)+(n-1)v(0)

in

. Since
T_{(F,w)}
is cancellative,
v(1)+(n-1)v(0)=0
, and hence
v(1)=0
, a contradiction. Thus
T_{(F,w)}
is not a refinement monoid.

Example 2.16
.

Consider the graph
E
, where all untagged edges are assigned weight one.

v
w
z
(e,2)
(f,2)

The commutative monoid

Now consider the talented monoid

. It is easy to see that

In the remainder of this section, we heavily use the concept of the covering graph of a weighted graph.

3.
Weighted Leavitt path algebras and the graded Grothendieck group

The aim of this section is to compute the graded Grothendieck group of the Leavitt path algebra of a vertex-weighted graph in terms of its talented monoid (Corollary

We begin this section by briefly recalling the notion of weighted Leavitt path algebras, which are algebras associated with weighted graphs. We refer the reader to

Definition 3.1
.

-algebra generated by
\{v,e_{i},e_{i}^{*}\mid v\in E^{0},e\in E^{1},1\leq i\leq w(e)\}

,

(ii)

\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}e_{i}e_{j}^{*}=\delta_{ij}v,\text{ where }v\in E_{\operatorname{reg}}^{0}\text{ and }1\leq i,j\leq w(v)

(iv)

of the graph

We next discuss the smash product of weighted Leavitt path algebras. For a

There is no groupoid model for weighted Leavitt path algebras. However, the following lemma once again highlights the effectiveness of graded

Lemma 3.3
.

For any vertex-weighted graph
(E,w)
and any field
\mathsf{k}

\phi\colon L_{\mathsf{k}}(\overline{E},\overline{w})\rightarrow L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)\#\mathbb{Z}

\phi(v^{(n)})=v{P_{-n}},\qquad\phi(e_{i}^{(n)})=e_{i}P_{-n-1},\qquad\phi(e_{i}{{}^{(n)}}^{*})=e_{i}^{*}{P_{-n}},

,

, and

-algebras.

Proof.

To show that the map

is a well-defined

-algebra homomorphism, it suffices to verify that

preserves the defining relations of the weighted Leavitt path algebra of the covering graph. We verify relation (iii) of Definition

and leave the remaining verifications to the reader.
Let

\displaystyle\phi\left(\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v^{(n)})}e_{i}^{(n)}(e_{j}^{(n)})^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}\phi(e_{i}^{(n)})\phi((e_{j}^{(n)})^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}\bigl(e_{i}P_{-n-1}\bigr)\bigl(e_{j}^{*}P_{-n}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}e_{i}e_{j}^{*}P_{-n}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}e_{i}e_{j}^{*}\right)P_{-n}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}vP_{-n}

\displaystyle=\phi(\delta_{ij}v^{(n)}),

Since

as a

-algebras, it follows that

is surjective. Since weighted Leavitt path algebras have normal forms
[

, one can define the well-define inverse map to

w_{1}(1)=w_{1}(1)+x
would imply

, a contradiction, and so

is not refinement.

2. Suppose (P2) fails. Then there is a vertex

, such that

, where

and

and

is not refinement.

3. Suppose (P3) fails. Then there are two weighted edges

which are not in line, but
T(r(e))\cap T(r(f))\not=\varnothing
. If

then P(1) fails, and thus

is not refinement by part 1. So suppose
v:=s(e)
and
w:=s(f)
and
v\not=w
.
If P(2) also fails then

is already not refinement. Thus there are paths without bifurcation from
r(e)
and
r(f)
to a vertex
z
. It follows that there are
n,m>1
, such that
nv(i)=kw(j)
, where

.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
nv=kw(j)
, for some
j\in\mathbb{N}
. The rest of the argument is rather similar to part 1. If
T_{(E,w)}
is refinement, we then have

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=a_{11}+\cdots+a_{1k}

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=a_{21}+\cdots+a_{2k}

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=a_{n1}+\cdots+a_{nk}

\displaystyle w(j)

\displaystyle=a_{1i}+\cdots+a_{ni}\text{ where }1\leq i\leq k

As there is no transformation on

allowed, Confluence Lemma
2.2
gives that
a_{i1}+\cdots+a_{ik}\rightarrow v
. Thus there should be an edge

with

and

and all

flows to

which after the next transformation they arrive to

. However it is then impossible that the same

on the level -1, flow to
w
on level
0
as
v\not=w
. A contradiction, and thus
T_{(E,w)}
is not refinement.

Suppose (P4) fails. Then there is an edge
e\in E^{1}_{w}
and a cycle
c
based at some vertex
w\in T(r(e))
, such that
e
does not belongs to
c
. If any of the conditions P(1) to P(3) fail, then

is not refinement and we are done. So assume conditions P(1) to P(3) hold. Thus there is a path without bifurcation from
v:=s(e)
to
w
the base of the cycle

. Hence, in
T_{(E,w)}
, we have

, for some

. The P(1) to P(4) conditions guarantee the cycle

has no exits, and there is no path returning to

. Therefore there is a natural number

for some

. Combining these two equations we have

nmv=mw(k)=w(k+k^{\prime})=nv(k^{\prime}).

If
T_{(E,w)}
is refinement, then there are
a_{ij}
that flows from level -1 to

to reach to

. This means that

is on a cycle. But

was not part of the cycle

emits more than one edge, a contradiction.
∎

As a corollary of Proposition

Theorem 3.7
.

The graded Grothendieck group

distinguishes the class of vertex-weighted Leavitt path algebras from the unweighted class.

Proof.

Let
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
be a weighted Leavitt path algebra. Suppose there is an unweighted graph
F
such that there is an order preserving
\mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}]
-module isomorphism

. Since the positive cones of

-groups coincide with the talented monoids, we have

. But the monoid

is refinement. Therefore, by Proposition
3.6
, the weighted graph

satisfies Preusser’s conditions. Thus

is isomorphic to a unweighted Leavitt path algebras.
In fact one can establish a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded isomorphism, by assigning appropriate grading to edges (see the isomorphism between the Leavitt path algebras in Theorem 5.2.1 of
[

]
).

On the other hand, suppose that

cannot be realised as

for any unweighted graph

. Then

cannot be graded isomorphic to a unweighted Leavitt path algebra. So it cannot be isomorphic to a Leavitt path algebra. Otherwise Preusser’s condition implies that

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
(E,w)
be a vertex-weighted graph and
\mathsf{k}
a field. Then, there is a semilattice isomorphism between the semilattices of hereditary and saturated subsets of
E^{0}
and the (graded) ideals of
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)
generated by vertices.

Proof.

We denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\text{ver}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,\omega))
the set of all ideals of
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,\omega)
generated by vertices. It is obvious that
(\mathcal{L}_{\text{ver}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,\omega)),\subseteq)
is a join-semilattice. We next claim that

\mathcal{H}_{(E,\omega)}\cong\mathcal{L}_{\text{ver}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,\omega))

as lattices. Indeed, let
I
be a nonzero ideal of
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,\omega)
generated by vertices. It is obvious that
I\cap E^{0}\neq\emptyset
.
We show that
I\cap E^{0}
is a hereditary and saturated subset of
E^{0}
. Let
e\in E^{1}
with
v:=s(e)\in I
. We then have
e_{i}=ve_{i}\in I
for all
1\leq i\leq\omega(e)
, and so
r(e)=\sum_{1\leq i\leq\omega(v)}e_{i}^{*}e_{i}\in I
. This implies that
I\cap E^{0}
is hereditary. Let
v\in E_{\operatorname{reg}}^{0}
with the property that
r(s^{-1}(v))\subseteq I\cap E^{0}
. We then have
r(e_{1})\in I
for all
e\in s^{-1}(v)
, and so
e_{1}=e_{1}r(e_{1})\in I
for all
e\in s^{-1}(v)
. Therefore, we obtain that
v=\sum_{e\in s^{-1}(v)}e_{1}e_{1}^{*}\in I
, and so
I\cap E^{0}
is saturated, as desired.

On the other hand if
H
is hereditary and saturated and
I(H)
is an ideal of
L(E,\omega)
generated by
H
, then
I(H)\cap E^{0}=H
by

[
15
, Theorem 2.10]
. From these observations, we immediately obtain that the corresponding
\alpha:\mathcal{L}_{\text{ver}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,\omega))\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{(E,\omega)}
, defined by
I\longmapsto I\cap E^{0}
, is a join-semilattice isomorphism, thus finishing the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.9

Let

4.
K_{0}^{\mathrm{gr}}
is a full functor

The main aim of this section is to prove the following lifting theorem, which gives that Conjecture
1.1
(1) holds for vertex-weighted graphs (Theorem
4.1
), and show that the graded Grothendieck group
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}
classifies the Leavitt algebras
L_{\mathsf{k}}(n,n+k)
(Theorem
4.6
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(E,w)
and

(F,w)
be finite vertex-weighted graphs and
\mathsf{k}
a field. For any order preserving
\mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}]
-module homomorphism
\phi:K^{\operatorname{gr}}_{0}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w))\rightarrow K^{\operatorname{gr}}_{0}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(F,w))
with

\phi([L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)])=L_{\mathsf{k}}(F,w)
, there exists a unital
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathsf{k}
-homomorphism
\psi:L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w)\rightarrow L_{\mathsf{k}}(F,w)
such that
K^{\operatorname{gr}}_{0}(\psi)=\phi
.

In order to prove this theorem, we need to recall the graded version of Bergman machinery developed in
[
14
]
.
In that work, Bergman’s universal constructions were extended to the setting of graded rings. It was shown that any conical
\Gamma
-monoid can be realised as a graded non-stable
K
-theory of a
\Gamma
-graded ring, which satisfies a “weak” universal property. We collect here the results from
[
14
]
that will be needed in the subsequent sections.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
R
be a
\Gamma
-graded
\mathsf{k}
-algebra. Suppose that
M
is a
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module and
P
a graded finitely generated projective
R
-module. Then there exists a
\Gamma
-graded
R\text{-ring}_{\mathsf{k}}
,
S
, with a universal graded module homomorphism
f:S\otimes_{R}M\rightarrow{}S\otimes_{R}P
; that is, given any
\Gamma
-graded
R\text{-ring}_{\mathsf{k}}
,
T
, and any graded
T
-module homomorphism
g:T\otimes_{R}M\rightarrow{}T\otimes_{R}P
, there exists a unique graded homomorphism
S\rightarrow{}T
of
R\text{-rings}_{\mathsf{k}}
such that
g=T\otimes_{S}f
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
R
be a
\Gamma
-graded
\mathsf{k}
-algebra,
M
a graded
R
-module,
P
a graded projective
R
-module, and
f:M\xrightarrow{}P
a graded module homomorphism. Then there exists a
\Gamma
-graded
R\text{-ring}_{\mathsf{k}}

S
such that
S\otimes_{R}f=0
, and
S
is universal for this property: Given any
\Gamma
-graded
R\text{-ring}_{\mathsf{k}}

T
with
T\otimes_{R}f=0
, there exists a unique graded homomorphism of
R\text{-rings}_{\mathsf{k}}
,
S\xrightarrow{}T
.

For graded finitely generated projective
R
-modules
P
and
Q
, we can adjoin a universal graded isomorphism between
\overline{P}
and
\overline{Q}
by first freely adjoining a graded map
h:\overline{P}\xrightarrow{}\overline{Q}
, and
\overline{h}:\overline{Q}\xrightarrow{}\overline{P}
using Theorem
4.2
and then using Theorem
4.3
to force that
1-h\overline{h}=0
and
1-\overline{h}h=0
. We denote the resulting
\Gamma
-graded

by

S:=R\langle h,h^{-1}\mid\overline{P}\cong_{\operatorname{gr}}\overline{Q}\rangle.

When the field
\mathsf{k}
is concentrated in degree zero, we can compute the non-stable graded

-theory of the graded ring

(
[
14
, §7]
).

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
R
be a
\Gamma
-graded
\mathsf{k}
-algebra, where
\mathsf{k}
is concentrated in degree zero. Let
P
and
Q
be nonzero graded finitely generated projective
R
-modules and
S:=R\langle h,h^{-1}\mid\overline{P}\cong_{\operatorname{gr}}\overline{Q}\rangle.

Then is a
\Gamma
-monoid isomorphism

\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{gr}}(S)\cong\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{gr}}(R)\big/\big\langle[P]=[Q]\big\rangle

given by tensoring.

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
4.1
. In fact, by using the graded Bergman construction, we obtain a more general result.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let

be a finite vertex-weighted graph,
\mathsf{k}
a field, and

\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathsf{k}
-algebra. Let

\mathbb{Z}
-monoid homomorphism with

\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathsf{k}
-algebra homomorphism

\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{gr}}(\psi)=\phi

Proof.

Consider the semisimple
\mathsf{k}

|E^{0}|
-copies of the field
\mathsf{k}
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebra concentrated in degree zero.
Then

\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{gr}}(R)\cong\prod_{E^{0}}\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{gr}}(\mathsf{k})\cong\big\langle v(i)\mid v\in E^{0},\,i\in\mathbb{Z}\big\rangle,

with the
\mathbb{Z}

, for

in (

Let

We construct the graded algebra

The proofs of Theorem
4.2
and

show that

is

, where

is a vertex-weighted graph with the same vertices as

and other vertices do not emit any edges. Namely, if

We are now in a position to establish the fullness of the graded Grothendieck group functor on the category of all weighted Leavitt path algebras.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By Corollary
3.4
, it follows that

T_{(E,w)}\cong\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E,w))
. By Proposition
2.20

the positive cone of

is precisely

. Since
\phi

\mathbb{Z}

\phi:\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E))\rightarrow\mathcal{V}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(F)).

The result now follows from Theorem

∎

We finish the paper by showing that the
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}
-group is a complete invariant for Leavitt algebras. Note that in comparison, there is an order isomorphism
K_{0}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(n,n+k))\cong K_{0}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(n+l,n+k+l))
, for

, although

.

Theorem 4.6
.

The graded Grothendieck group
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}
classifies Leavitt algebras
L_{\mathsf{k}}(n,n+k)
, where
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Suppose that there is order preserving
\mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}]
-module
isomorphism
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(n,n+k))\cong K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(n^{\prime},n^{\prime}+k^{\prime}))
. Since the positive cones coincide with the talented monoids, we obtain a
\mathbb{Z}
-isomorphism
T_{(n,n+k)}\cong T_{(n^{\prime},n^{\prime}+k^{\prime})}
. Now Proposition
2.20
gives an isomorphism on the level of monogenic monoids
M_{(n,n+k)}\cong M_{(n^{\prime},n^{\prime}+k^{\prime})}
. It is known that every monogenic monoid is completely determined, up to isomorphism, by its index
n
and period
k
.
So it follows
n=n^{\prime}
and

.
