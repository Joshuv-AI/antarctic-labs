1.
Introduction

The discrete Heisenberg algebra arises as an associative algebra generated by elements

u,u^{*},n
subject to the relations

[n,u]=-u,\quad[n,u^{*}]=u^{*},\quad[u,u^{*}]=p,

where
p
is the rank-one projection onto the vacuum state. Equipped with the
commutator
[x,y]=xy-yx
, this algebra inherits a Lie structure. Graded extensions
yield Lie superalgebras, broadening the theoretical scope
[
11
,
2
]
.

A faithful representation on the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
identifies
u

with the unilateral shift
U
,
u^{*}
with its adjoint
U^{*}
, and
n
with the
number operator
Ne_{k}=ke_{k}
. The norm closure of the generated algebra yields the
discrete Toeplitz
C^{*}
-algebra
[
6
]
. While this infinite-dimensional
setting provides a rich topological framework for spectral analysis and noncommutative
deformations
[
5
,
1
]
, practical control synthesis requires
finite-dimensional approximations. Specifically, we work with compressions of Toeplitz
operators to the subspace
\mathrm{span}\{e_{0},\ldots,e_{n-1}\}\subset\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
;
the finite-dimensional algebra
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
serves as the concrete representation
algebra.

Remark 1.1

(Scope of the Toeplitz motivation)
.

The discrete Toeplitz
C^{*}
-algebra motivates the structural setting of this paper
but does not enter the proofs in an essential way, and its role is purely heuristic.
The Rota-Baxter identity (
1.1
) is imposed directly on linear maps

P:M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and reduces to an explicit system of quadratic
constraints on the matrix entries of
P
, verifiable by direct computation.

A rigorous reduction from the infinite-dimensional Toeplitz algebra to
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})

would require the invariance of the subspace
\mathrm{span}\{e_{0},\ldots,e_{n-1}\}

under all operators in the range of
P
. This condition fails already for the most
natural generator: the unilateral shift
U
maps
e_{n-1}
to
e_{n}
, which lies outside

\mathrm{span}\{e_{0},\ldots,e_{n-1}\}
, so the subspace is not invariant under
U
.
Consequently, no Rota-Baxter identity from the Toeplitz algebra compresses to one in

M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
via this subspace, and any such reduction would additionally require
spectral tools (Wiener–Hopf factorization, Fredholm index) that are not developed here.
This extension is deferred to future work. All algebraic results in
Sections
3
–
6
are proved entirely within

M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
; the Toeplitz algebra is cited only as the source of structural
intuition.

Within this setting, Rota-Baxter operators play a central role. A linear map

P:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
satisfies the Rota-Baxter identity of weight
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
:

(1.1)

P(x)P(y)=P(xP(y))+P(P(x)y)+\lambda P(xy),\quad\forall\,x,y\in\mathcal{A}.

Originally introduced to formalize integration and summation
[
10
]
,
Rota-Baxter operators also generate solutions to classical and modified Yang-Baxter
equations, thereby linking associative and Lie structures
[
8
]
. In
operator algebras, they induce algebraic deformations that can model cumulative effects,
filtering, or memory-dependent interactions.

In control theory, sliding mode control (SMC) is renowned for its robustness against
matched uncertainties and time delays
[
16
,
7
]
. We consider
discrete-time delayed systems of the form

(1.2)

\begin{cases}x(k+1)=Ax(k)+A_{d}x(k-\tau)+Bu(k)+D\delta(k),\\
s(k)=Cx(k),\\
u(k)=-Kx(k)-\rho\,\sat\bigl(s(k)/\phi\bigr),\end{cases}

where
x(k)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
u(k)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
,

D\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
,
\tau\in\mathbb{N}
is a known delay,

\delta(k)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is a bounded uncertainty satisfying

\|\delta(k)\|_{2}\leq\delta_{\max}
for all
k
, and
\sat(\cdot)
denotes the
componentwise saturation function

\sat(v/\phi)_{i}=\begin{cases}v_{i}/\phi&\text{if }|v_{i}|\leq\phi,\\
\mathrm{sgn}(v_{i})&\text{if }|v_{i}|>\phi,\end{cases}

with boundary layer width
\phi>0
to mitigate chattering in discrete time.

We assume throughout that
m=n
(the square case) and
p=m
, i.e.,

C\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is a square matrix. This ensures that

CB_{P}\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
is a square matrix, and its invertibility
(Assumption
4.1
) is well-defined in the ordinary sense without recourse to
pseudoinverses. The hypothesis
p=m
is adopted for notational convenience: it ensures
that the congruence transformation
\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}(Q,Q,I_{p},I_{n})
in
Remark
5.7
produces a well-structured LMI with consistent block
dimensions. Physically,
p=m
means the number of disturbance channels equals the
number of control inputs, a structural simplification that can be relaxed at the cost of
a more complex block structure in (
5.7
). The case
m<n
(rectangular

B
) or
p<m
(fat output map
C
) requires a separate treatment involving either
right-inverses or a reformulation of the equivalent control, and is deferred to future
work. The supplementary non-degenerate numerical example of Section
7.2

relaxes this assumption to
m=1
,
n=2
for purely illustrative purposes.

Assumption 1.2

(Bounded initial conditions)
.

The initial state and initial delay segment are bounded:

\sup_{k\geq 0}\|x(k)\|\leq r_{0}<\infty
and

\sup_{k\geq 0}\|x(k-\tau)\|\leq r_{d,0}<\infty
prior to closed-loop activation.
This holds whenever the open-loop system is BIBO stable and the initial data

x(0)
and
\{x(-\tau),\ldots,x(-1)\}
are finite. In practice,
r_{0}
and
r_{d,0}

are set conservatively from the known initial conditions; they are treated as design
parameters in the reaching analysis of Section
4
.

Important note on the design order.
The bound
r_{0}
is fixed from open-loop
data before the control gains
K
and
\rho
are selected. However, the validity of

r_{0}
as a uniform bound throughout the closed-loop reaching phase is a condition that
is
verified a posteriori
after
K
is chosen, via condition
(
4.5
). There is therefore no strict
a priori
non-circularity:
one must first fix
r_{0}
and
\rho_{\max}
from open-loop data, then choose
K

satisfying (
4.4
), and only then verify (
4.5
). If the
condition fails,
r_{0}
must be increased (conservatively) or
K
must be redesigned.
The full sequential procedure is spelled out in Remark
4.7
.

When the open-loop system is not BIBO stable, this assumption must be imposed explicitly
as a constraint on the initial data.

All operator norms appearing in the SMC analysis
(Sections
4
–
5
) are spectral (operator) norms

\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sigma_{\max}(\cdot)
, unless otherwise stated. The Frobenius

in

A non-standard norm requirement.
Throughout the SMC analysis, we impose
condition (
4.4
):
\|A_{P}-B_{P}K\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1
. This is
strictly
stronger
than Schur stability (spectral radius
\rho(A_{P}-B_{P}K)<1
), and is not
automatically satisfied even when
A_{P}-B_{P}K
is Schur stable. Its necessity is
discussed in Remark

; operator-specific guidance is given in

and
3.10
.

While the infinite-dimensional Toeplitz algebra inspires the structural properties of
memory operators (see Remark
1.1
), the control application is
carried out within the finite-dimensional matrix
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}=M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.
By applying a Rota-Baxter operator

, we obtain deformed dynamics

A_{P}=P(A)
,
A_{d,P}=P(A_{d})
,

. This deformation preserves the

\lambda
governs
the algebraic nature of the deformation:
\lambda=0
corresponds to integration-like
behavior (splitting of the algebra), while
\lambda\neq 0
models persistent algebraic
feedback.

Our contributions are threefold:

(1)

A structural characterization of Rota-Baxter operators on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})

(2)

A rigorous Lyapunov-based stability analysis for delayed sliding mode systems under

along with a fully linear reformulation via the congruence transformation
Q=X^{-1}
.

(3)

An algebraic characterization of sliding surface invariance and spectral properties
of the reduced dynamics, under explicit non-degeneracy and commutativity assumptions
on
P
, whose validity is discussed for the concrete examples provided.

2.
Preliminaries

This section introduces the fundamental algebraic and topological structures underlying
our study.

2.1.
Associative algebras and Lie structures

An associative algebra
\mathcal{A}
over a field
\mathbb{K}
is a vector space equipped
with a bilinear product
\cdot:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
satisfying

(x\cdot y)\cdot z=x\cdot(y\cdot z)
for all
x,y,z\in\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Given an associative algebra
\mathcal{A}
, the commutator

[x,y]:=xy-yx

endows
\mathcal{A}
with a Lie algebra structure
\mathfrak{g}
, satisfying bilinearity,
antisymmetry, and the Jacobi identity
[
12
]
.

Remark 2.2
.

Graded extensions of this framework yield Lie superalgebras

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
. While not
explicitly exploited in the finite-dimensional control synthesis below, this structure
provides a natural setting for future work on supersymmetric control and fermionic memory
models
[
14
]
.

2.2.
Topological and
C^{*}
-algebraic structures

Definition 2.3
.

A
C^{*}
-algebra is a complex Banach algebra
(\mathcal{A},\|\cdot\|)
with an involution

*
such that
\|x^{*}x\|=\|x\|^{2}
for all
x\in\mathcal{A}
.

In control applications, we primarily work with the finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra

M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
equipped with the operator norm and Hermitian adjoint. This algebra
naturally accommodates system matrices, Lyapunov inequalities, and Rota-Baxter
deformations.

Example 2.4
.

The discrete Toeplitz
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the unilateral shift on

\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
motivates the structural setting (see
Remark
1.1
), but its role is purely heuristic. For finite-dimensional
state spaces, we work with
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
directly. The Rota-Baxter identity
(
1.1
) in
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
reduces to a system of quadratic constraints on the
entries of the matrix representing the linear map
P
, which can be verified
computationally. We impose (
1.1
) directly on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
without appealing
to any infinite-dimensional reduction.

2.3.
Rota-Baxter operators and compatible derivations

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
. A
Rota-Baxter operator
of weight
\lambda
on
an algebra
\mathcal{A}
is a linear map
P:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
satisfying (
1.1
).

Such operators generalize integration and summation. For
\lambda=0
, the identity
resembles the integration by parts formula. For
\lambda\neq 0
, it relates to
summation operators; note that the scaling operator
P(X)=-\lambda X

(Example
3.5
(a)) gives a non-trivial instance for every
\lambda\neq 0
,
while the zero map
P=0
is the only Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
of this
scaling form.

Remark 2.6

(Rota-Baxter operators of weight zero)
.

For weight
\lambda=0
, the identity (
1.1
) reduces to

P(x)P(y)=P(xP(y))+P(P(x)y)
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
.
A standard family of non-trivial examples is provided by operators whose image is a
two-sided ideal. Specifically, if
P^{2}=P
is an idempotent whose image
P(\mathcal{A})
is
a two-sided ideal of
\mathcal{A}
satisfying
P(\mathcal{A})\cdot(I-P)(\mathcal{A})\subset\ker P
, then
P

is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
; see
[
4
]
for a proof.

We caution that not every idempotent is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero. In
particular, the Peirce corner map
P(X)=eXe
(for a minimal idempotent

e=e_{11}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
e^{2}=e
) is
not
a Rota-Baxter operator
of weight zero in general. Indeed, a direct computation gives:

P(x)P(y)=(exe)(eye)=exeye,

P(xP(y))+P(P(x)y)=e(x\cdot eye)e+e(exe\cdot y)e=exeye+exeye=2\,exeye,

so the identity
P(x)P(y)=P(xP(y))+P(P(x)y)
requires
exeye=0
for all

x,y\in\mathcal{A}
. For the minimal idempotent
e=e_{11}
, the corner algebra

e_{11}M_{n}(\mathbb{C})e_{11}=\mathbb{C}\,e_{11}\cong\mathbb{C}
, whose product
is non-zero (e.g.
e_{11}\cdot e_{11}=e_{11}\neq 0
), so the condition
exeye=0

fails for
x=y=I
. Thus
P(X)=eXe
is not a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero
for any non-zero minimal idempotent
e
in
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with
n\geq 1
.

The erroneous claim that
P(X)=eXe
always satisfies (
1.1
) for
\lambda=0

stems from an arithmetic oversight: each of the two right-hand side terms equals

exeye
, giving a sum of
2\,exeye\neq exeye
whenever
exeye\neq 0
.
The correct condition for
P(X)=eXe

to be a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero is that
e\mathcal{A}e
is a null algebra (every
product in
e\mathcal{A}e
is zero). For a minimal idempotent
e=e_{11}
in
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
the corner algebra
e\mathcal{A}e\cong\mathbb{C}
is not a null algebra, so this condition
is never satisfied for non-zero minimal idempotents.

A correct non-trivial example of a weight-zero Rota-Baxter operator in
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})

is given by any idempotent
P
whose image is a two-sided ideal, as described above and
in
[
4
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

A derivation
D:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
satisfies the Leibniz rule
D(xy)=D(x)y+xD(y)
.
We say
D
is
compatible
with
P
if the commutator

[D,P]:=D\circ P-P\circ D
satisfies a prescribed algebraic constraint
(e.g.,
[D,P]=0
).

Theorem 2.8

(Compatibility Criterion
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

If
P
is a Rota-Baxter operator and
D
a derivation such that

D\circ P=P\circ D
, then
D
preserves the Rota-Baxter identity and maps the
image of
P
into itself.

2.4.
Interrelations and motivation

The interplay between
C^{*}
-topology, Lie brackets, and Rota-Baxter deformations
provides a rigorous framework for modeling memory-dependent dynamics. In the
finite-dimensional setting
\mathcal{A}=M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, every linear map
P
is automatically
continuous (since all linear maps on finite-dimensional normed spaces are bounded), so no
additional boundedness hypothesis is required for the control synthesis below. The
Rota-Baxter identity reduces to a system of quadratic constraints on the entries of the
matrix representing
P

[
17
]
.

3.
Classification and Structural Properties

We now establish structural results for Rota-Baxter operators on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Lemma 3.1

(Boundedness and Continuity)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and
P:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
a Rota-Baxter operator of
weight
\lambda
.

(i)

If
\mathcal{A}
is infinite-dimensional, the Rota-Baxter identity (
1.1
) alone
does not force
P
to be bounded. Boundedness must be assumed separately; once
assumed, continuity follows immediately (equivalence of boundedness and continuity
for linear operators on normed spaces).

(ii)

If
\mathcal{A}=M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, then every linear map
P:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
is
automatically bounded and continuous, since all norms on a finite-dimensional
space are equivalent and every linear map on a finite-dimensional normed space
is continuous.

Lemma 3.2

(Induced Lie Structure)
.

Let
P
be a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
on an associative algebra

\mathcal{A}
. Define the bracket

(3.1)

[x,y]_{P}:=[P(x),y]+[x,P(y)]+\lambda[x,y].

Then
(\mathcal{A},[\cdot,\cdot]_{P})
is a Lie algebra.

Proof.

Antisymmetry.
Using antisymmetry of the ordinary commutator,

[y,x]_{P}=[P(y),x]+[y,P(x)]+\lambda[y,x]=-[x,P(y)]-[P(x),y]-\lambda[x,y]=-[x,y]_{P}.

Jacobi identity.
We verify

[[x,y]_{P},z]_{P}+[[y,z]_{P},x]_{P}+[[z,x]_{P},y]_{P}=0.

The key algebraic ingredient is obtained by writing the Rota-Baxter identity
(
1.1
) for the pair
(x,y)
and for the pair
(y,x)
:

\displaystyle P(x)P(y)

\displaystyle=P(xP(y))+P(P(x)y)+\lambda P(xy),

\displaystyle P(y)P(x)

\displaystyle=P(yP(x))+P(P(y)x)+\lambda P(yx).

Subtracting the second from the first and using
[a,b]=ab-ba
:

\displaystyle[P(x),P(y)]

\displaystyle=P(xP(y))-P(yP(x))+P(P(x)y)-P(P(y)x)+\lambda P(xy)-\lambda P(yx)

\displaystyle=P([x,P(y)])+P([P(x),y])+\lambda P([x,y]).

Rearranging yields the identity

(3.2)

P\bigl([P(x),y]\bigr)+P\bigl([x,P(y)]\bigr)=\bigl[P(x),P(y)\bigr]-\lambda P\bigl([x,y]\bigr),

which we use below. Set
u:=[x,y]_{P}=[P(x),y]+[x,P(y)]+\lambda[x,y]
.

Step 1: Compute
P(u)
.

By linearity of
P
and identity (
3.2
):

\displaystyle P(u)

\displaystyle=P([P(x),y])+P([x,P(y)])+\lambda P([x,y])

\displaystyle=\bigl([P(x),P(y)]-\lambda P([x,y])\bigr)+\lambda P([x,y])

\displaystyle=[P(x),P(y)].

Thus
P([x,y]_{P})=[P(x),P(y)]
, i.e.
P
maps the
[\cdot,\cdot]_{P}
-bracket to the
ordinary commutator of the
P
-images.

Step 2: Expand
[[x,y]_{P},z]_{P}
.

By definition (
3.1
) with
u=[x,y]_{P}
:

\displaystyle[[x,y]_{P},z]_{P}

\displaystyle=[P(u),z]+[u,P(z)]+\lambda[u,z]

\displaystyle=[[P(x),P(y)],z]+\bigl[[P(x),y]+[x,P(y)]+\lambda[x,y],\;P(z)\bigr]+\lambda\bigl[[P(x),y]+[x,P(y)]+\lambda[x,y],\;z\bigr].

Step 3: Cyclic sum.

The cyclic sum is taken over the three even permutations

(x,y,z)\to(y,z,x)\to(z,x,y)
, which we denote
\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}(x,y,z)}
.
Taking this cyclic sum and expanding, we obtain five families of terms:

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[x,y]_{P},z]_{P}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[P(x),P(y)],z]}_{S_{1}}+\underbrace{\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[P(x),y],P(z)]+\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[x,P(y)],P(z)]}_{S_{2}}+\underbrace{\lambda\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[x,y],P(z)]}_{G^{(2)}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\lambda\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[P(x),y],z]}_{G^{(1)}}+\underbrace{\lambda\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[x,P(y)],z]}_{H}+\underbrace{\lambda^{2}\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[x,y],z]}_{G^{(3)}}.

Step 4:
S_{1}+S_{2}=0
.

For any three elements
A,B,C
in an associative algebra,

(3.3)

[[A,B],C]+[[B,C],A]+[[C,A],B]=0.

The six terms of
S_{2}
are:

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{[[P(x),y],P(z)]}_{T_{1}}+\underbrace{[[P(y),z],P(x)]}_{T_{2}}+\underbrace{[[P(z),x],P(y)]}_{T_{3}}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{[[x,P(y)],P(z)]}_{T_{4}}+\underbrace{[[y,P(z)],P(x)]}_{T_{5}}+\underbrace{[[z,P(x)],P(y)]}_{T_{6}}.

Apply (
3.3
) to
(P(x),P(y),z)
:

[[P(x),P(y)],z]=-T_{2}-T_{6}
.

Apply (
3.3
) to
(P(y),P(z),x)
:

[[P(y),P(z)],x]=-T_{3}-T_{4}
.

Apply (
3.3
) to
(P(z),P(x),y)
:

[[P(z),P(x)],y]=-T_{1}-T_{5}
.

Summing:
S_{1}=-(T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}+T_{4}+T_{5}+T_{6})=-S_{2}
, so
S_{1}+S_{2}=0
.

Remark 3.3
.

Neither
S_{1}
nor
S_{2}
vanishes individually in general; their
joint

cancellation
S_{1}+S_{2}=0
is what matters.

Step 5:
G^{(1)}+H+G^{(2)}=0
.

Apply the ordinary Jacobi identity (
3.3
) to the triple

(P(x)+\lambda x,\,y,\,z)
:

[[P(x)+\lambda x,y],z]+[[y,z],P(x)+\lambda x]+[[z,P(x)+\lambda x],y]=0.

Expanding linearly and taking the cyclic sum
\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}(x,y,z)}
over the
three even permutations
(x,y,z)\to(y,z,x)\to(z,x,y)
:

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}\bigl([[P(x),y],z]+\lambda[[x,y],z]\bigr)+\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}\bigl([[y,z],P(x)]+\lambda[[y,z],x]\bigr)+\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}\bigl([[z,P(x)],y]+\lambda[[z,x],y]\bigr)=0.

Each
\lambda
-coefficient cyclic sum vanishes by (
3.3
)
(applied to
(x,y,z)
). Thus:

(3.5)

\underbrace{\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[P(x),y],z]}_{G^{(1)}/\lambda}+\underbrace{\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[y,z],P(x)]}_{(*)}+\underbrace{\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[z,P(x)],y]}_{(**)}=0.

We now identify the sums
(*)
and
(**)
with
G^{(2)}/\lambda
and
H/\lambda

respectively. For
(*)
: writing out the three terms of the cyclic sum over

(x,y,z)\to(y,z,x)\to(z,x,y)
gives

(*)=[[y,z],P(x)]+[[z,x],P(y)]+[[x,y],P(z)].

Under the relabelling
(x,y,z)\mapsto(z,x,y)
(the second even permutation applied
once more), the generic summand
[[x,y],P(z)]
maps to
[[z,x],P(y)]
, and cycling
again gives
[[y,z],P(x)]
. Hence the three terms of
(*)
coincide with the three
terms of
\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[x,y],P(z)]=G^{(2)}/\lambda
(same set of terms,
in a different order). Thus
(*)=G^{(2)}/\lambda
.

For
(**)
: writing out the cyclic sum gives

(**)=[[z,P(x)],y]+[[x,P(y)],z]+[[y,P(z)],x].

Relabelling
(x,y,z)\mapsto(y,z,x)
sends the generic summand
[[x,P(y)],z]
to

[[y,P(z)],x]
, and cycling again gives
[[z,P(x)],y]
. Hence the three terms of

(**)
coincide with the three terms of
\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[x,P(y)],z]=H/\lambda
.
Thus
(**)=H/\lambda
.

Multiplying (
3.5
) by
\lambda
gives
G^{(1)}+H+G^{(2)}=0
.

Remark 3.4

(Correct cancellation in Group 3 — explicit verification)
.

Neither
G^{(1)}
,
H
, nor
G^{(2)}
vanishes individually in general.
We verify this explicitly for
P=P_{+}
(
\lambda=-1
) in
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})

with
x=e_{12}
,
y=e_{21}
,
z=e_{11}
.

Recall
P_{+}(e_{12})=e_{12}
(upper triangular),
P_{+}(e_{21})=0
(strictly lower),

P_{+}(e_{11})=e_{11}
(diagonal, hence upper triangular).

Computation of
G^{(1)}

=\lambda\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[P(x),y],z]

=\lambda\bigl([[P(e_{12}),e_{21}],e_{11}]+[[P(e_{21}),e_{11}],e_{12}]+[[P(e_{11}),e_{12}],e_{21}]\bigr)
.

•

[[e_{12},e_{21}],e_{11}]=[e_{11}-e_{22},e_{11}]=0
(diagonal matrices commute).

•

[[0,e_{11}],e_{12}]=0
.

•

[e_{11},e_{12}]=e_{12}
, so
[[e_{11},e_{12}],e_{21}]=[e_{12},e_{21}]=e_{11}-e_{22}
.

Thus
G^{(1)}/\lambda=e_{11}-e_{22}
, giving
G^{(1)}=(-1)(e_{11}-e_{22})=e_{22}-e_{11}
.

Computation of
G^{(2)}

=\lambda\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[x,y],P(z)]

=\lambda\bigl([[e_{12},e_{21}],P(e_{11})]+[[e_{21},e_{11}],P(e_{12})]+[[e_{11},e_{12}],P(e_{21})]\bigr)
.

•

[[e_{12},e_{21}],e_{11}]=[e_{11}-e_{22},e_{11}]=0
.

•

[e_{21},e_{11}]=e_{21}
, so
[[e_{21},e_{11}],e_{12}]=[e_{21},e_{12}]=e_{22}-e_{11}
.

•

[[e_{11},e_{12}],0]=0
.

Thus
G^{(2)}/\lambda=e_{22}-e_{11}
, giving
G^{(2)}=(-1)(e_{22}-e_{11})=e_{11}-e_{22}
.

Computation of
H

=\lambda\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[x,P(y)],z]

=\lambda\bigl([[e_{12},0],e_{11}]+[[e_{21},e_{11}],e_{12}]+[[e_{11},e_{12}],e_{21}]\bigr)
.

•

[[e_{12},0],e_{11}]=0
.

•

[[e_{21},e_{11}],e_{12}]=[e_{21},e_{12}]=e_{22}-e_{11}
.

•

[e_{11},e_{12}]=e_{12}
, so
[[e_{11},e_{12}],e_{21}]=[e_{12},e_{21}]=e_{11}-e_{22}
.

Thus
H/\lambda=(e_{22}-e_{11})+(e_{11}-e_{22})=0
, giving
H=0
.

Final verification:

G^{(1)}+H+G^{(2)}=(e_{22}-e_{11})+0+(e_{11}-e_{22})=0.\quad\checkmark

This confirms: (i)
G^{(1)}/\lambda=e_{11}-e_{22}\neq 0
,
(ii)
G^{(2)}/\lambda=e_{22}-e_{11}\neq 0
,
(iii)
H=0
for this triple, and (iv) their joint sum
G^{(1)}+H+G^{(2)}=0
.
The identity is a
joint
cancellation, not three separate vanishings.
A fortiori, the invalid argument

\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[A,B],L(C)]=L(\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}[[A,B],C])
(which would
require
L
to act on the full expression, not only on
C
) cannot be used to simplify
these sums.

Step 6:
G^{(3)}=0
.

G^{(3)}=\lambda^{2}\bigl([[x,y],z]+[[y,z],x]+[[z,x],y]\bigr)=0

by (
3.3
) applied to
(A,B,C)=(x,y,z)
.

Conclusion.
Since
S_{1}+S_{2}=0
,
G^{(1)}+H+G^{(2)}=0
, and
G^{(3)}=0
,
the total cyclic sum
J=0
, establishing the Jacobi identity for
[\cdot,\cdot]_{P}
.
See also
[
4
, Theorem 3.3]
.
∎

Example 3.5

(Explicit Rota-Baxter Operators on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
)
.

Consider
\mathcal{A}=M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

(a) Scalar scaling.
Let
\lambda\neq 0
and
P(X)=-\lambda X
.
We verify (
1.1
) directly. The left-hand side is

P(X)P(Y)=(-\lambda X)(-\lambda Y)=\lambda^{2}XY.

The right-hand side is

\displaystyle P\bigl(X\,P(Y)\bigr)+P\bigl(P(X)\,Y\bigr)+\lambda P(XY)

\displaystyle=P(-\lambda XY)+P(-\lambda XY)+\lambda(-\lambda XY)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}XY+\lambda^{2}XY-\lambda^{2}XY=\lambda^{2}XY,

which equals the left-hand side. Thus
P(X)=-\lambda X
is a Rota-Baxter operator of
weight
\lambda\neq 0
, with operator norm
\|P\|_{\mathrm{op}}=|\lambda|
(with
respect to the spectral norm on
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
). The zero map
P=0
satisfies
(
1.1
) trivially for any weight
\lambda
, but is excluded from the control
synthesis since Assumption
4.1
requires
CP(B)
to be invertible.

Remark 3.6

(Structural degeneracy of scalar scaling in full-actuation)
.

For the scalar scaling operator
P(X)=-\lambda X
with
\lambda\neq 0
and any square
invertible matrices
B,C\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, the projection
\Pi
defined in
Section
5
satisfies

\Pi=I_{n}-B_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}C=I_{n}-(-\lambda B)((-\lambda)CB)^{-1}C=I_{n}-B(CB)^{-1}C.

More precisely,
(CB_{P})^{-1}=(-\lambda)^{-1}(CB)^{-1}
, so

B_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}=(-\lambda B)\cdot(-\lambda)^{-1}(CB)^{-1}=B(CB)^{-1}
,
and
\Pi=I_{n}-B(CB)^{-1}C
. When both
B
and
C
are invertible (
m=n
),

(CB)^{-1}=B^{-1}C^{-1}
and
B(CB)^{-1}C=I_{n}
, giving
\Pi=0
for every
choice of
\lambda
and every invertible
B
,
C
. This is an algebraic identity,
independent of the specific Rota-Baxter operator. Consequently, the reduced dynamics
(
5.1
) become
x(k+1)=0
(trivially stable), and the LMI conditions of
Theorem
5.4
are satisfied vacuously. To obtain non-trivial reduced
dynamics within the
m=n
framework, one must use a non-scalar Rota-Baxter operator
such as the triangular projection
P_{+}
combined with a non-invertible or rectangular
system configuration. Section
7.2
provides a genuine non-degenerate
example by relaxing the
m=n
assumption.

Remark 3.7

(On condition (
4.4
) for scalar scaling)
.

For
P(X)=-\lambda X
, the deformed closed-loop matrix is

A_{P}-B_{P}K=-\lambda(A-BK)
. Condition (
4.4
) requires

\sigma_{\max}(A_{P}-B_{P}K)=|\lambda|\,\sigma_{\max}(A-BK)<1
.
When
|\lambda|
is large (e.g.,
|\lambda|\geq 1
), this imposes a stronger
constraint on
K
than mere Schur stability of
A-BK
. In practice,
K
should be
chosen to satisfy
\sigma_{\max}(A-BK)<1/|\lambda|
, which can be achieved via
an LQR or
H_{\infty}
design on the original system, with the operator norm
constraint verified numerically by computing
\sigma_{\max}(A_{P}-B_{P}K)
.

(b) Triangular projection.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{+}
be the subalgebra of upper triangular matrices and
\mathcal{A}_{-}
the
subalgebra of strictly lower triangular matrices. We recall the well-known fact that

\mathcal{A}_{-}
is indeed a subalgebra (closed under multiplication): if
A,B
are strictly
lower triangular, then
(AB)_{ij}=\sum_{k}A_{ik}B_{kj}
; since
A_{ik}=0
whenever

k\leq i
and
B_{kj}=0
whenever
k\leq j
, every term vanishes when
i\leq j
, so

AB
is strictly lower triangular. In particular,
\mathcal{A}_{-}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{-}\subset\mathcal{A}_{-}
(and

\mathcal{A}_{-}
is nilpotent). Since
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{-}
as vector spaces, the
projection
P_{+}
onto
\mathcal{A}_{+}
along
\mathcal{A}_{-}
satisfies the Rota-Baxter identity
with weight
\lambda=-1

[
4
]
.

Remark 3.8

(Direct verification of the Rota-Baxter identity for
P_{+}
)
.

For completeness, we outline the verification of (
1.1
) for
P_{+}
with

\lambda=-1
. Write any
X=X_{+}+X_{-}
and
Y=Y_{+}+Y_{-}
with

X_{\pm},Y_{\pm}\in\mathcal{A}_{\pm}
. Then
P_{+}(X)=X_{+}
and
P_{+}(Y)=Y_{+}
. Using the
decomposition, the fact that
\mathcal{A}_{+}
is a subalgebra (products of upper triangular
matrices are upper triangular), and the inclusion
\mathcal{A}_{-}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{-}\subset\mathcal{A}_{-}

(established above), one computes:

P_{+}(X)P_{+}(Y)=X_{+}Y_{+}=P_{+}(X_{+}Y_{+}),

\displaystyle P_{+}(XP_{+}(Y))+P_{+}(P_{+}(X)Y)-P_{+}(XY)

\displaystyle=P_{+}\bigl((X_{+}+X_{-})Y_{+}\bigr)+P_{+}\bigl(X_{+}(Y_{+}+Y_{-})\bigr)-P_{+}(XY)

\displaystyle=P_{+}(X_{+}Y_{+})+P_{+}(X_{-}Y_{+})+P_{+}(X_{+}Y_{+})+P_{+}(X_{+}Y_{-})

\displaystyle\quad-P_{+}(X_{+}Y_{+})-P_{+}(X_{+}Y_{-})-P_{+}(X_{-}Y_{+})-P_{+}(X_{-}Y_{-})

\displaystyle=P_{+}(X_{+}Y_{+}),

where we used
P_{+}(X_{-}Y_{-})=0
since
X_{-}Y_{-}\in\mathcal{A}_{-}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{-}\subset\mathcal{A}_{-}
.
This confirms
P_{+}(X)P_{+}(Y)=P_{+}(XP_{+}(Y))+P_{+}(P_{+}(X)Y)-P_{+}(XY)
, i.e. (
1.1
) with
\lambda=-1
. For a complete proof by bases, see

[
11
, Example 1.1.14]
.

The Frobenius norm of
P_{+}
satisfies the following. Since
P_{+}^{2}=P_{+}
is a non-zero
idempotent on the Hilbert space
(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\|\cdot\|_{F})
, one has

\|P_{+}\|_{F}\geq 1
; equality holds when
P_{+}
is an orthogonal projection. For
n=2
,
the subspaces
\mathcal{A}_{+}
and
\mathcal{A}_{-}
are orthogonal in the Frobenius inner product (see
Remark
3.9
), so
P_{+}
is the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{A}_{+}
and

\|P_{+}\|_{F}=1
. Since any non-zero idempotent satisfies
\|P_{+}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\geq 1
,
and
\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\|X\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{n}\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}
for all
X
,
the estimate
\|P_{+}\|_{F}=1
gives
\|P_{+}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
for
n=2
, hence

\|P_{+}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1
when
n=2
. For
n\geq 3
,
\mathcal{A}_{+}
and
\mathcal{A}_{-}
are no
longer orthogonal (the diagonal matrices lie in
\mathcal{A}_{+}
but not in
\mathcal{A}_{-}
, and the
off-diagonal structure prevents orthogonality); the Friedrichs angle must be computed
explicitly, and both
\|P_{+}\|_{F}
and
\|P_{+}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
must be estimated
numerically for each
n
. In the stability analysis
(Sections
4
–
5
), only the spectral norm

\|P\|_{\mathrm{op}}
appears, and we use the generic bound

\|P\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa_{P}
without committing to a specific norm formula;
when
P_{+}
is used, the constant
\kappa_{P}
must be determined for each
n
.

Note: The Frobenius norm is used here explicitly in the structural analysis of

P_{+}
; all SMC norms remain spectral, as declared in Section
1
.

Remark 3.9

(Friedrichs angle for
n=2
)
.

For
n=2
, the subspaces
\mathcal{A}_{+}
(upper triangular) and
\mathcal{A}_{-}
(strictly lower
triangular, i.e. spanned by

) are orthogonal in the Frobenius inner product

\langle X,Y\rangle_{F}=\mathrm{tr}(Y^{*}X)
: indeed, any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{+}
has zero

(2,1)
-entry and any

has zero entries except the
(2,1)
-position, so

\langle A,B\rangle_{F}=\overline{A_{21}}B_{21}=0
. Hence
P_{+}
is the orthogonal
projection onto
\mathcal{A}_{+}
and
\|P_{+}\|_{F}=1
. For
n\geq 3
, the diagonal matrices
lie in
\mathcal{A}_{+}
but their off-diagonal lower entries are zero, while elements of
\mathcal{A}_{-}

have zero diagonal; the two subspaces are no longer orthogonal because both share the
ambient space of matrices with non-zero
(i,j)
-entries for
i>j
. The Friedrichs angle
must be computed explicitly from the principal angle between
\mathcal{A}_{+}
and
\mathcal{A}_{-}
in the
Frobenius inner product.

In control synthesis,
P_{+}
isolates causal (upper triangular) spectral components.
Admissibility (Assumption
4.1
) requires
CP_{+}(B)
to be invertible; this
holds when
B
is already upper triangular (so that
P_{+}(B)=B
) and
CB
is invertible.
The condition
CP(M)=P(CM)
required in Proposition
6.3
holds for
P_{+}

if and only if
C
maps
\mathcal{A}_{-}
to
\mathcal{A}_{-}
; a sufficient condition for this is that
C

is itself upper triangular, since then
CM_{-}\in\mathcal{A}_{-}
for all
M_{-}\in\mathcal{A}_{-}
.

Remark 3.10

(Condition (
4.4
) for the triangular projection
P_{+}
)
.

For

, the deformed closed-loop matrix is
A_{P}-B_{P}K=P_{+}(A)-P_{+}(B)K
.
Condition (

) requires
\sigma_{\max}(P_{+}(A)-P_{+}(B)K)<1
.
Since

. In applications where

Remark 3.11

(Admissibility of

for control synthesis)
.

A Rota-Baxter operator

is

for system (

) if

is invertible (Assumption

). Under the standing

(so that

4.
Sliding Mode Control Framework

We consider the discrete-time delayed system (
1.2
) with

\|\delta(k)\|_{2}\leq\delta_{\max}
for all
k
, and throughout this section we
maintain the assumption
m=n
and
p=m
so that
B\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,

C\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is square, and
P
is well-defined on all system matrices.
Assumption
1.2
is in force throughout.

The sliding surface is
s(k)=Cx(k)
. The control law

u(k)=-Kx(k)-\rho\,\sat\bigl(s(k)/\phi\bigr)

with
\rho>0
and boundary layer width
\phi>0
is designed to drive trajectories
toward the quasi-sliding mode (QSM) band
\|s(k)\|\leq\phi
in finite time, mitigating
chattering inherent to discrete-time implementations
[
16
,
7
]
.

We introduce a Rota-Baxter deformation
P:M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
of
weight
\lambda
. The deformed system matrices are

A_{P}:=P(A),\quad A_{d,P}:=P(A_{d}),\quad B_{P}:=P(B).

Assumption 4.1

(Non-degenerate Actuation under Deformation)
.

The matrix
CB_{P}=CP(B)
is invertible (as a square matrix in
M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
,
under the standing assumption
p=m
). Sufficient conditions are given in
Remark
3.11
and Example
3.5
.

The closed-loop dynamics under SMC become

(4.1)

x(k+1)=(A_{P}-B_{P}K)x(k)+A_{d,P}x(k-\tau)-\rho B_{P}\sat\bigl(Cx(k)/\phi\bigr)+D\delta(k).

The sliding surface dynamics are

(4.2)

s(k+1)=C(A_{P}-B_{P}K)x(k)+CA_{d,P}x(k-\tau)-\rho CB_{P}\sat\bigl(s(k)/\phi\bigr)+CD\delta(k).

Proposition 4.2

(QSM Reaching and Band Boundedness)
.

Under Assumptions
1.2
and
4.1
, the following hold.

(a) Reaching (Phase 1).

Let
r_{0}
and
r_{d,0}
be the bounds of Assumption
1.2
,
whose validity throughout the closed-loop reaching phase is guaranteed by
condition (
4.5
) (verified after
K
is chosen; see
Remark
4.3
).
All norms below are spectral (operator) norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}
. Define the
perturbation-plus-nominal bound

(4.3)

\alpha_{0}:=\|C(A_{P}-B_{P}K)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\,r_{0}+\|CA_{d,P}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\,r_{d,0}+\delta_{\max}\|CD\|_{\mathrm{op}}.

Remark 4.3

(Validity of
\alpha_{0}
as a uniform bound during the reaching phase)
.

The bound
\alpha_{0}
in (
4.3
) uses
r_{0}
and
r_{d,0}
as uniform upper
bounds on
\|x(k)\|
and
\|x(k-\tau)\|
for all
k\geq 0
during the reaching phase.
Assumption
1.2
guarantees these bounds prior to closed-loop
activation. Their use for
k\geq 1
requires the following.

Design order and conditional non-circularity.

Fix
r_{0}
and
r_{d,0}
from open-loop data (Assumption
1.2
) and fix
an
a priori
upper bound
\rho_{\max}
on the switching gain before any other
design parameter is chosen. Then select
K
so that

(4.4)

\|A_{P}-B_{P}K\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1,

which is a
strictly stronger
condition than Schur stability (spectral radius

<1
) and must be explicitly verified for the chosen
K
(e.g., by computing

\sigma_{\max}(A_{P}-B_{P}K)
numerically or by selecting
K
via an LQR or pole-placement
design; see Remarks
3.7
and
3.10
for
conditions specific to each operator). Once
K
satisfying (
4.4
) is
chosen, the sufficient condition on
r_{0}
is:

(4.5)

r_{0}\;\geq\;\frac{\|A_{d,P}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\,r_{d,0}+\rho_{\max}\,\|B_{P}\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\delta_{\max}\|D\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{1-\|A_{P}-B_{P}K\|_{\mathrm{op}}},

which depends on
K
(through the denominator) and on
\rho_{\max}
(not on the final
value of
\rho\leq\rho_{\max}
). The denominator
1-\|A_{P}-B_{P}K\|_{\mathrm{op}}>0

is guaranteed by (
4.4
).

Important.
Condition (
4.5
) must be
verified after

K
is chosen, not before. If it fails for the selected
K
and the initial
r_{0}
, two
remedies are available: (i) increase
r_{0}
conservatively (setting a larger a priori
bound on the initial state), or (ii) redesign
K
to increase the denominator

1-\|A_{P}-B_{P}K\|_{\mathrm{op}}
. The bound
r_{0}
is independent of
\rho
(which
appears only through
\rho_{\max}
, fixed before
K
), so no circularity between
r_{0}

and
\rho
arises. Once (
4.5
) is verified, the closed-loop state
satisfies
\|x(k)\|\leq r_{0}
for all
k\geq 0
, and
\alpha_{0}
remains a valid
uniform bound throughout the reaching phase.

Suppose
\phi>\alpha_{0}
(the boundary layer is wider than the open-loop disturbance
level). When the control gain satisfies

(4.6)

\rho\,\|CB_{P}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\;\leq\;\phi-\alpha_{0},

we have the contraction bound
\|s(k+1)\|_{2}\leq\alpha_{0}+\beta\|s(k)\|_{2}
with

\beta:=\rho\,\|CB_{P}\|_{\mathrm{op}}/\phi\in[0,1)
(see Remark
4.4
).
If
\|s(0)\|_{2}\leq\phi
, the trajectory is already in the band at
k=0
and the
reaching time is
T^{*}=0
. For
\|s(0)\|_{2}>\phi
, the fixed point of the map

t\mapsto\alpha_{0}+\beta t
is
s^{*}=\alpha_{0}/(1-\beta)\leq\phi
. The formula

(4.7)

T^{*}:=\left\lceil\frac{\ln\!\left(\dfrac{\|s(0)\|_{2}-s^{*}}{\phi-s^{*}}\right)}{\ln(1/\beta)}\right\rceil

provides an
upper bound
on the number of steps to enter the band

\{\|s\|\leq\phi\}
(valid when
\|s(0)\|_{2}>\phi>s^{*}
and
\beta\in(0,1)
;
if
\beta=0
then, since
\phi>\alpha_{0}=s^{*}
by assumption, one step suffices and

T^{*}=1
). The actual reaching time may be earlier; (
4.7
) bounds the first
k

for which the contraction estimate certifies entry into the band.

Remark 4.4

(Strict contractivity and validity of (
4.7
))
.

Condition (
4.6
) gives

Proof.

Part (a): Reaching condition.

Contraction argument.

5.
Stability Analysis via Lyapunov and Matrix Inequalities

Once
\|s(k)\|\leq\phi
, the equivalent control is

u_{\mathrm{eq}}(k)=-(CB_{P})^{-1}C\bigl[A_{P}x(k)+A_{d,P}x(k-\tau)+D\delta(k)\bigr].

Under the standing assumption
p=m
and Assumption
4.1
,
CB_{P}\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
is a square invertible matrix and
(CB_{P})^{-1}
denotes its ordinary
matrix inverse. The reduced-order dynamics on the manifold are

(5.1)

x(k+1)=\bar{A}x(k)+\bar{A}_{d}x(k-\tau)+\bar{D}\delta(k),

where

\bar{A}=\Pi A_{P},\quad\bar{A}_{d}=\Pi A_{d,P},\quad\bar{D}=\Pi D,\quad\Pi=I-B_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}C.

Remark 5.1

(Closed-loop dynamics vs. equivalent control form)
.

Theorem
5.4
below analyses the Lyapunov stability of the

equivalent control
system (
5.1
), which is the dynamics obtained
by substituting
u_{\mathrm{eq}}
into (
4.1
) on the sliding manifold

s(k)=0
. This is
distinct
from the actual closed-loop system (
4.1
)
in the linear regime
\|s\|\leq\phi
(
\sat(s/\phi)=s/\phi
), whose state matrix is

\hat{A}:=A_{P}-B_{P}\!\left(K+\tfrac{\rho}{\phi}C\right),

and which retains the delay and disturbance terms unmodified. The two descriptions
coincide exactly on the invariant manifold
\{x:Cx=0\}
: if
Cx(k)=0
then

u_{\mathrm{eq}}(k)=-(CB_{P})^{-1}C[A_{P}x(k)+A_{d,P}x(k{-}\tau)+D\delta(k)]
, and
substituting this into

x(k+1)=A_{P}x(k)+A_{d,P}x(k{-}\tau)+B_{P}u+D\delta(k)
recovers (
5.1
)
by the identity
\Pi=I-B_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}C
.

Theorem
5.4
therefore certifies asymptotic stability and bounded

\mathcal{L}_{2}
-gain
on the sliding manifold
. Band-invariance of the manifold
(i.e. that the actual closed-loop trajectories remain in
\|s\|\leq\phi
) is ensured
separately by the Phase 2 condition (
4.9
) of
Proposition
4.2
.

Remark 5.2

(Sliding surface invariance:
C\Pi=0
by construction)
.

The projection
\Pi=I-B_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}C
satisfies
C\Pi=0
for every admissible

P
(i.e., whenever Assumption
4.1
holds). Indeed, by direct computation:

C\Pi=C\bigl(I-B_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}C\bigr)=C-CB_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}C=C-I_{m}\cdot C=0.

This identity holds by construction and requires only the invertibility of
CB_{P}
; no
assumption on the specific form of
P
or on the system matrices
A
,
A_{d}
,
B
,
C

is needed beyond Assumption
4.1
. Note that
\Pi
is an oblique (not
necessarily orthogonal) projection: it satisfies
\Pi^{2}=\Pi
but is in general not
symmetric. As a consequence:

(i)

The reduced dynamics (
5.1
) are well-defined on the sliding manifold

\{x:s(k)=Cx(k)=0\}
: if
Cx(k)=0
then

Cx(k+1)=C\bar{A}\,x(k)+C\bar{A}_{d}\,x(k-\tau)+C\bar{D}\,\delta(k)=C\Pi A_{P}x(k)+C\Pi A_{d,P}x(k-\tau)+C\Pi D\,\delta(k)=0
,
so the manifold is positively invariant under the reduced dynamics.

(ii)

\Pi
is idempotent:
\Pi^{2}=\Pi
. To verify this, compute

\displaystyle\Pi^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigl(I-B_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}C\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle=I-2B_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}C+B_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}CB_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}C.

The last term simplifies as

B_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}(CB_{P})(CB_{P})^{-1}C=B_{P}\cdot I_{m}\cdot(CB_{P})^{-1}C=B_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}C
,
so
\Pi^{2}=I-2B_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}C+B_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}C=I-B_{P}(CB_{P})^{-1}C=\Pi
.

In the numerical example of Section
7.2
, both
C\Pi=0
and
\Pi^{2}=\Pi

are verified explicitly by direct matrix computation. The non-symmetry of
\Pi
in that
example (
\Pi_{12}=-0.25\neq\Pi_{21}=-1.00
) confirms that it is an oblique
projection, as expected.

Remark 5.3

(Telescoping of the delay Lyapunov functional)
.

The Lyapunov-Krasovskii functional

V_{k}=x(k)^{\top}Xx(k)+\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}x(k-i)^{\top}Yx(k-i)

involves all lags
i=1,\ldots,\tau
, but the reduced dynamics (
5.1
)
contain only the lag-
\tau
term. The forward difference is

\Delta V_{k}=x(k{+}1)^{\top}Xx(k{+}1)-x(k)^{\top}Xx(k)+\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\bigl[x(k{+}1{-}i)^{\top}Yx(k{+}1{-}i)-x(k{-}i)^{\top}Yx(k{-}i)\bigr].

The delay sum telescopes exactly:

\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}\bigl[x(k{+}1{-}i)^{\top}Yx(k{+}1{-}i)-x(k{-}i)^{\top}Yx(k{-}i)\bigr]=x(k)^{\top}Yx(k)-x(k-\tau)^{\top}Yx(k-\tau),

leaving only the boundary terms. This is why the augmented vector

\xi_{k}=[x(k)^{\top},x(k-\tau)^{\top},\delta(k)^{\top}]^{\top}
suffices for the quadratic
form in the proof of Theorem
5.4
.

Theorem 5.4

(Main Stability Result)
.

Consider the reduced dynamics (
5.1
) with a Rota-Baxter operator
P

satisfying (
1.1
) and Assumption
4.1
. Let
X=X^{\top}>0
,

Y=Y^{\top}>0
, and
\gamma>0
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The matrix inequality

(5.2)

\begin{bmatrix}-X+Y&0&0&\bar{A}^{\top}\\
0&-Y&0&\bar{A}_{d}^{\top}\\
0&0&-\gamma^{2}I&\bar{D}^{\top}\\
\bar{A}&\bar{A}_{d}&\bar{D}&-X^{-1}\end{bmatrix}<0.

(ii)

The block matrix

(5.3)

\mathcal{M}:=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{A}^{\top}X\bar{A}-X+Y&\bar{A}^{\top}X\bar{A}_{d}&\bar{A}^{\top}X\bar{D}\\
\bar{A}_{d}^{\top}X\bar{A}&\bar{A}_{d}^{\top}X\bar{A}_{d}-Y&\bar{A}_{d}^{\top}X\bar{D}\\
\bar{D}^{\top}X\bar{A}&\bar{D}^{\top}X\bar{A}_{d}&\bar{D}^{\top}X\bar{D}-\gamma^{2}I\end{bmatrix}<0.

If either condition holds, then:

(a)

The system (
5.1
) is asymptotically stable for
\delta(k)=0
.
More precisely, let
\mathcal{M}_{0}
denote the leading
2n\times 2n

principal submatrix of
\mathcal{M}
(obtained by deleting the last
block-row and block-column corresponding to
\delta
). Since

\mathcal{M}<0
implies
\mathcal{M}_{0}<0
: indeed, for every non-zero

v\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, letting

\tilde{v}=[v^{\top},\,0_{p}^{\top}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n+p}
gives

v^{\top}\mathcal{M}_{0}v=\tilde{v}^{\top}\mathcal{M}\tilde{v}<0
,
so
\mathcal{M}_{0}<0
. Hence there exists

\mu_{0}:=\lambda_{\min}(-\mathcal{M}_{0})>0
such that

\Delta V_{k}\leq-\mu_{0}\|x(k)\|^{2}
for all
k
when
\delta\equiv 0
.

(b)

The system has bounded
\mathcal{L}_{2}
-gain from
\delta
to
x
: for all

N\geq 0
and all bounded disturbances
\delta
,

(5.4)

\sum_{k=0}^{N}\|x(k)\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\mu}\left(V_{0}+\gamma^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{N}\|\delta(k)\|^{2}\right),

where
\mu:=\lambda_{\min}(-\mathcal{M})>0
. The effective

\mathcal{L}_{2}
-gain from
\delta
to
x
is
\gamma/\sqrt{\mu}
,
where
\mu=\lambda_{\min}(-\mathcal{M})
depends on the LMI solution

X=Q^{-1}
and is determined
a posteriori
once
(
5.7
) is solved.

Scope of the gain bound.
This gain characterization holds under

zero extended initial conditions
:
V_{0}=0
, i.e.
x(0)=0

and

x(-1)=\cdots=x(-\tau)=0
(since
V_{0}=x(0)^{\top}Xx(0)+\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}x(-i)^{\top}Yx(-i)
, all terms must
vanish). For
\tau>0
, this is a stronger requirement than merely

x(0)=0
. The bound (
5.4
) remains valid for
V_{0}>0

(non-zero initial conditions), but in that case
\gamma/\sqrt{\mu}
no
longer characterizes the gain from
\delta
to
x
in the standard

\ell_{2}/\ell_{2}
sense.

Consequently, minimizing
\gamma
in the LMI does not directly minimize
the effective gain
\gamma/\sqrt{\mu}
; one may instead minimize

\gamma^{2}/\mu
as a joint objective, noting that this ratio is not affine
in the LMI variables
(Q,\tilde{Y},\gamma)
and therefore requires a
dedicated nonlinear or iterative procedure (e.g. bisection on
\gamma

with inner LMI feasibility check) rather than a standard LMI solver.

Remark 5.5
.

The bound (
5.4
) is a
cumulative

\mathcal{L}_{2}

bound, not a pointwise Input-to-State Stability (ISS) bound in the
sense of
[

]
. A pointwise ISS bound is not established
here and is deferred to future work.

Remark 5.6

(Numerical computation of

Proof.

Equivalence of (i) and (ii).

Consider the Lyapunov-Krasovskii functional

V_{k}=x(k)^{\top}Xx(k)+\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}x(k-i)^{\top}Yx(k-i).

Set

and

By the telescoping argument of Remark

\displaystyle=x(k{+}1)^{\top}Xx(k{+}1)-x(k)^{\top}Xx(k)+x(k)^{\top}Yx(k)-x(k{-}\tau)^{\top}Yx(k{-}\tau)

\displaystyle=\xi_{k}^{\top}F^{\top}XF\,\xi_{k}+\xi_{k}^{\top}\mathrm{diag}(-X{+}Y,\;-Y,\;\mathbf{0})\,\xi_{k}.

Note that

has a zero block in the

\delta
to the telescoping difference of
V_{k}
. We now introduce
the auxiliary quantity

(5.5)

J_{k}:=\Delta V_{k}-\gamma^{2}\|\delta(k)\|^{2}=\xi_{k}^{\top}\underbrace{\bigl(F^{\top}XF+\mathrm{diag}(-X{+}Y,\;-Y,\;-\gamma^{2}I)\bigr)}_{=\,\mathcal{M}}\xi_{k}.

The
-\gamma^{2}I
block in
\mathcal{M}
arises from the external subtraction

-\gamma^{2}\|\delta\|^{2}
, not from
\Delta V_{k}
itself. Since
X>0
, the Schur
complement lemma gives

\mathcal{M}<0\penalty\ ifandonlyif\penalty\ \begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{diag}(-X{+}Y,\;-Y,\;-\gamma^{2}I)&F^{\top}\\
F&-X^{-1}\end{bmatrix}<0,

which is precisely (
5.2
) (expanding the diagonal block and
F
). This
establishes the equivalence (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii).

(a) Asymptotic stability for
\delta=0
.

When
\delta(k)\equiv 0
, set
F_{0}=[\bar{A}\ \ \bar{A}_{d}]
and

\xi_{k}^{0}=[x(k)^{\top},x(k-\tau)^{\top}]^{\top}
. Then

\Delta V_{k}=(\xi_{k}^{0})^{\top}\mathcal{M}_{0}\,\xi_{k}^{0}
, where

\mathcal{M}_{0}=F_{0}^{\top}XF_{0}+\mathrm{diag}(-X{+}Y,-Y)

is the leading
2n\times 2n
block principal submatrix of
\mathcal{M}
. Since

\mathcal{M}<0
implies
\mathcal{M}_{0}<0
(for every non-zero
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,
letting
\tilde{v}=[v^{\top},0_{p}^{\top}]^{\top}
gives

v^{\top}\mathcal{M}_{0}v=\tilde{v}^{\top}\mathcal{M}\tilde{v}<0
), there exists

\mu_{0}:=\lambda_{\min}(-\mathcal{M}_{0})>0
such that

\Delta V_{k}\leq-\mu_{0}\|\xi_{k}^{0}\|^{2}\leq-\mu_{0}\|x(k)\|^{2}
for all
k
.
Summing from
k=0
to
N-1
:

V_{N}\leq V_{0}-\mu_{0}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\|x(k)\|^{2}.

Since
V_{N}\geq 0
, we obtain
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|x(k)\|^{2}<\infty
, which implies

\|x(k)\|^{2}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Together with the monotone decrease of
V_{k}
,
this establishes asymptotic stability.

(b)
\mathcal{L}_{2}
-gain bound.

Since
\mathcal{M}<0
, let
\mu:=\lambda_{\min}(-\mathcal{M})>0
. From
(
5.5
),
\xi_{k}^{\top}\mathcal{M}\,\xi_{k}\leq-\mu\|\xi_{k}\|^{2}
, so

\Delta V_{k}=J_{k}+\gamma^{2}\|\delta(k)\|^{2}\leq-\mu\|\xi_{k}\|^{2}+\gamma^{2}\|\delta(k)\|^{2}.

Since
\|\xi_{k}\|^{2}=\|x(k)\|^{2}+\|x(k-\tau)\|^{2}+\|\delta(k)\|^{2}
, we have

\Delta V_{k}\leq-\mu\|x(k)\|^{2}-\underbrace{\mu\|x(k-\tau)\|^{2}}_{\geq\,0}+\underbrace{(\gamma^{2}-\mu)\|\delta(k)\|^{2}}_{\text{(absorbed below)}}.

Dropping the negative term
-\mu\|x(k-\tau)\|^{2}\leq 0
(which can only increase the
right-hand side) and bounding
-\mu\|\delta(k)\|^{2}
within
(\gamma^{2}-\mu)\|\delta(k)\|^{2}

by replacing it with
\gamma^{2}\|\delta(k)\|^{2}
(a valid upper bound since

-\mu\|\delta\|^{2}\leq 0
), we obtain the relaxed bound

(5.6)

\Delta V_{k}\leq-\mu\|x(k)\|^{2}+\gamma^{2}\|\delta(k)\|^{2}.

Summing from
k=0
to
N
and using
V_{N+1}\geq 0
gives (
5.4
).
The effective
\mathcal{L}_{2}
-gain
\gamma/\sqrt{\mu}
for zero extended initial
conditions (
V_{0}=x(0)^{\top}Xx(0)+\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}x(-i)^{\top}Yx(-i)=0
,
requiring
x(0)=0
and
x(-i)=0
for
i=1,\ldots,\tau
) then follows by taking

N\to\infty
with
V_{0}=0
.
∎

Remark 5.7

(Tractable LMI reformulation)
.

Condition (
5.2
) contains the term
-X^{-1}
in the
(4,4)
-block,
rendering it a
bilinear matrix inequality
(BMI) jointly in
(X,X^{-1})
. To
obtain a fully linear formulation, introduce
Q:=X^{-1}>0
and

\tilde{Y}:=QYQ
as new decision variables. Applying the congruence

\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}(Q,Q,I_{p},I_{n})
to condition (
5.2
) yields
the equivalent strict LMI

(5.7)

\begin{bmatrix}-Q+\tilde{Y}&0&0&Q\bar{A}^{\top}\\
0&-\tilde{Y}&0&Q\bar{A}_{d}^{\top}\\
0&0&-\gamma^{2}I_{p}&\bar{D}^{\top}\\
\bar{A}Q&\bar{A}_{d}Q&\bar{D}&-Q\end{bmatrix}<0,

to be solved in the variables
(Q,\tilde{Y},\gamma)
with
Q>0
,
\tilde{Y}>0
,

\gamma>0
.

Remark 5.8

(Role of
p=m
in the congruence)
.

The congruence
\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}(Q,Q,I_{p},I_{n})
acts on a matrix of size

(2n+p+n)\times(2n+p+n)=(3n+p)\times(3n+p)
. The condition
p=m=n
(square
case) ensures that the block sizes of
\Sigma
match those of (
5.2
)
without recourse to non-square identity blocks. Specifically, under
p=m
, the

(3,3)
block of (
5.2
) is
-\gamma^{2}I_{p}
with
p=m
, and

I_{p}\cdot(-\gamma^{2}I_{p})\cdot I_{p}=-\gamma^{2}I_{p}
under the congruence. For

p\neq m
, the block
\bar{D}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}
is rectangular, and
the congruence
\Sigma
must be adapted; this case is deferred to future work.

The congruence
\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}(Q,Q,I_{p},I_{n})
acts block-by-block
as follows (using
X=Q^{-1}
,
Y=Q^{-1}\tilde{Y}Q^{-1}
):

•

(1,1)
:
Q(-X+Y)Q=-QXQ+QYQ=-Q+\tilde{Y}
,
since
QXQ=Q\cdot Q^{-1}\cdot Q^{-1\top}\cdot Q
; more precisely,
with
Q=X^{-1}
symmetric,
QXQ=X^{-1}XX^{-1}=X^{-1}=Q
.

•

(2,2)
:
Q(-Y)Q=-\tilde{Y}
,

•

(3,3)
:
I_{p}\cdot(-\gamma^{2}I_{p})\cdot I_{p}=-\gamma^{2}I_{p}
,

•

(4,4)
:
I_{n}\cdot(-X^{-1})\cdot I_{n}=-Q
,

•

(1,4)
:
Q\cdot\bar{A}^{\top}\cdot I_{n}=Q\bar{A}^{\top}
, and symmetrically for
rows/columns
2
and
3
,

•

(3,4)
:
I_{p}\cdot\bar{D}^{\top}\cdot I_{n}=\bar{D}^{\top}
. This block has
dimensions
p\times n
and is left-multiplied by
I_{p}
and right-multiplied
by
I_{n}
under the congruence, so it is unchanged.

Since
\bar{A}
,
\bar{A}_{d}
,
\bar{D}
are fixed matrices, all blocks of
(
5.7
) are affine in
(Q,\tilde{Y})
, making (
5.7
)
a standard LMI solvable by interior-point solvers (e.g., SeDuMi, MOSEK via YALMIP).

Remark 5.9

(Sufficient condition for feasibility)
.

A sufficient condition for the feasibility of (
5.7
) can be obtained by
substituting the trial point
Q=I_{n}
and
\tilde{Y}=\varepsilon I_{n}
(with

\varepsilon\in(0,1)
). The LMI (
5.7
) is negative definite at this
trial point if and only if the Schur complement with respect to the
(4,4)
block

-I_{n}
is negative definite. For a given
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\gamma>0
, this
reduces to the joint condition

(5.8)

\sigma_{\max}(F)^{2}<\min(1-\varepsilon,\,\varepsilon,\,\gamma^{2}),

where
F=[\bar{A}\ \bar{A}_{d}\ \bar{D}]
. Specializing to
\varepsilon=1/2
and

\gamma>\sigma_{\max}(F)
(so that
\gamma^{2}>\sigma_{\max}(F)^{2}
) yields
the simplified conservative condition

(5.9)

\sigma_{\max}(F)^{2}<\tfrac{1}{2},\qquad\gamma>\sigma_{\max}(F),

as a small-gain-type sufficient condition for feasibility;
\sigma_{\max}(F)
denotes
the largest singular value of
F=[\bar{A}\ \bar{A}_{d}\ \bar{D}]
. For numerical
verification, computing
\sigma_{\max}(F)
directly is preferred over
triangle-inequality bounds, which may be overly conservative when cross terms
partially cancel. Note that (
5.9
) is a
sufficient

condition only; the actual LMI (
5.7
) may be feasible for larger values
of
\sigma_{\max}(F)
, as illustrated in Section
7.2
where feasibility is
established directly by the solver.

Once the LMI (
5.7
) is solved, the
\mathcal{L}_{2}
decay constants

\mu=\lambda_{\min}(-\mathcal{M})
and
\mu_{0}=\lambda_{\min}(-\mathcal{M}_{0})

are computable as described in Remark
5.6
.

Remark 5.10

(Conservatism of the Lyapunov functional)
.

The functional
V_{k}
uses a single matrix
Y
for all delay terms
x(k-i)
,

i=1,\ldots,\tau
. For
\tau>1
, this is conservative; a less conservative bound is
obtained by using distinct matrices
Y_{i}>0
for each lag. The resulting LMI retains
the same Schur-complement structure and is feasible under strictly weaker conditions

[
9
]
. We use the single-
Y
form for notational simplicity.

Spectral and Algebraic Properties of the Sliding Dynamics

Proposition 6.1

(Spectral Stability Condition)
.

The reduced sliding dynamics (
5.1
) with
\delta\equiv 0
are
asymptotically stable if and only if all roots
z\in\mathbb{C}
of the characteristic
equation

(6.1)

\det\!\left(z^{\tau+1}I-z^{\tau}\bar{A}-\bar{A}_{d}\right)=0

satisfy
|z|<1
.

Proof.

The system
x(k+1)=\bar{A}x(k)+\bar{A}_{d}x(k-\tau)
can be rewritten as a

(n(\tau+1))
-dimensional first-order system by augmenting the state: let

\mathbf{x}(k)=[x(k)^{\top},x(k-1)^{\top},\ldots,x(k-\tau)^{\top}]^{\top}
. Then

\mathbf{x}(k+1)=\mathcal{F}\,\mathbf{x}(k)
where the companion matrix is

\mathcal{F}=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{A}&0&\cdots&0&\bar{A}_{d}\\
I_{n}&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&I_{n}&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&I_{n}&0\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{n(\tau+1)\times n(\tau+1)}.

Asymptotic stability is equivalent to
\rho(\mathcal{F})<1
.

We establish
\det(zI-\mathcal{F})=\det(z^{\tau+1}I-z^{\tau}\bar{A}-\bar{A}_{d})

by induction on
\tau\geq 1
.

Base case
\tau=1
.
The companion matrix is

\mathcal{F}=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{A}&\bar{A}_{d}\\
I_{n}&0\end{bmatrix}
and

\det(zI-\mathcal{F})=\det\!\begin{bmatrix}zI-\bar{A}&-\bar{A}_{d}\\
-I_{n}&zI\end{bmatrix}.

Since
zI
is invertible for
z\neq 0
, the Schur complement of
zI
in the

(2,2)
block gives

\det(zI-\mathcal{F})=\det(zI)\cdot\det\!\bigl((zI-\bar{A})-(-\bar{A}_{d})(zI)^{-1}(-I_{n})\bigr)=z^{n}\det\!\bigl(zI-\bar{A}-z^{-1}\bar{A}_{d}\bigr).

Factoring
z^{-1}
from each of the
n
columns of the
n\times n
matrix

(zI-\bar{A}-z^{-1}\bar{A}_{d})
introduces a factor
z^{-n}
, giving

z^{n}\cdot\det(zI-\bar{A}-z^{-1}\bar{A}_{d})=z^{n}\cdot z^{-n}\cdot\det(z^{2}I-z\bar{A}-\bar{A}_{d})=\det(z^{2}I-z\bar{A}-\bar{A}_{d}),

confirming (
6.1
) for
\tau=1
.

Inductive step.
Assume the identity holds for delay
\tau-1
, i.e., for the
companion matrix
\mathcal{F}_{\tau-1}
of size
n\tau\times n\tau
(built from

\bar{A}
and
\bar{A}_{d}
with delay
\tau-1
), one has

\det(zI_{n\tau}-\mathcal{F}_{\tau-1})=\det(z^{\tau}I-z^{\tau-1}\bar{A}-\bar{A}_{d})
.

The companion matrix for delay
\tau
has size
n(\tau+1)\times n(\tau+1)
:

\mathcal{F}_{\tau}=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{A}&0&\cdots&0&\bar{A}_{d}\\
I_{n}&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&I_{n}&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&I_{n}&0\end{bmatrix}.

Partition
zI-\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
as

\begin{bmatrix}zI_{n}-\bar{A}&B_{12}\\
B_{21}&B_{22}\end{bmatrix},

where
B_{12}=[0,\ldots,0,-\bar{A}_{d}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\tau}
(with

-\bar{A}_{d}
in the last block column),
B_{21}=[-I_{n};0;\ldots;0]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\tau\times n}
(with
-I_{n}
in the first block row), and
B_{22}
is the
lower-right
n\tau\times n\tau
block. The matrix
B_{22}
is block lower-bidiagonal:
it has
zI_{n}
on each diagonal block and
-I_{n}
on the first block-subdiagonal, with
zeros elsewhere. Its determinant is
\det(B_{22})=z^{n\tau}
(product of diagonal
block determinants), so
B_{22}
is invertible for
z\neq 0
.

Explicit formula for
B_{22}^{-1}
.

By a standard inversion of block lower-bidiagonal matrices (verified by direct
block-multiplication), the
(i,j)
block of
B_{22}^{-1}
(for
1\leq i,j\leq\tau
)
is

(B_{22}^{-1})_{ij}=\begin{cases}z^{-(i-j+1)}I_{n}&\text{if }i\geq j,\\
0&\text{if }i<j.\end{cases}

Indeed, one can verify
(B_{22}\cdot B_{22}^{-1})_{ij}=\delta_{ij}I_{n}
directly:
for
i=j
,
(B_{22})_{ii}(B_{22}^{-1})_{ii}=(zI_{n})(z^{-1}I_{n})=I_{n}
; for
i>j
,

(B_{22})_{ii}(B_{22}^{-1})_{ij}+(B_{22})_{i,i-1}(B_{22}^{-1})_{i-1,j}=z\cdot z^{-(i-j+1)}I_{n}+(-I_{n})\cdot z^{-(i-1-j+1)}I_{n}=z^{-(i-j)}I_{n}-z^{-(i-j)}I_{n}=0
.

Computation of
B_{12}B_{22}^{-1}B_{21}
.

The matrix
B_{12}
has non-zero content only in its last block column (position

\tau
), equal to
-\bar{A}_{d}
. The matrix
B_{21}
has non-zero content only in its
first block row (position
1
), equal to
-I_{n}
. Therefore:

B_{12}B_{22}^{-1}B_{21}=(-\bar{A}_{d})\cdot(B_{22}^{-1})_{\tau,1}\cdot(-I_{n})=(-\bar{A}_{d})(z^{-\tau}I_{n})(-I_{n})=z^{-\tau}\bar{A}_{d}.

The Schur complement of
B_{22}
then gives:

\det(zI-\mathcal{F}_{\tau})=\det(B_{22})\cdot\det\!\bigl((zI_{n}-\bar{A})-z^{-\tau}\bar{A}_{d}\bigr)=z^{n\tau}\cdot\det(zI_{n}-\bar{A}-z^{-\tau}\bar{A}_{d}).

Factoring
z^{-\tau}
from each of the
n
columns of
(zI_{n}-\bar{A}-z^{-\tau}\bar{A}_{d})

introduces
z^{-n\tau}
, yielding

\det(zI-\mathcal{F}_{\tau})=z^{n\tau}\cdot z^{-n\tau}\cdot\det(z^{\tau+1}I_{n}-z^{\tau}\bar{A}-\bar{A}_{d})=\det(z^{\tau+1}I_{n}-z^{\tau}\bar{A}-\bar{A}_{d}),

completing the induction.

For a complete alternative proof using the companion matrix factorization, see

[
15
, Chapter 3, Proposition 3.2]
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

In earlier versions of this paper, the characteristic equation was written as

\det(zI-\bar{A}-z^{-\tau}\bar{A}_{d})=0
. This form mixes positive and negative

Proposition 6.3

(Algebraic Invariance of the Output Map)
.

Suppose that for all
M\in\mathrm{span}\{A,A_{d}\}
, the sliding surface matrix
C

and the Rota-Baxter operator
P
satisfy

(6.2)

CP(M)=P(CM).

Then:

(i)

The matrices
\bar{A}=\Pi A_{P}
and
\bar{A}_{d}=\Pi A_{d,P}
satisfy

C\bar{A}=0
and
C\bar{A}_{d}=0
, so the images of
\bar{A}
and
\bar{A}_{d}

lie in
\ker(C)
. This is an unconditional consequence of
C\Pi=0

(Remark
5.2
), holding under Assumption
4.1
alone,
independently of condition (
6.2
).

(ii)

Condition (
6.2
) is equivalent, for each concrete Rota-Baxter
operator, to an explicit structural constraint on
C
:

(a)

For
P(X)=-\lambda X
(
\lambda\neq 0
): condition (
6.2
)
holds for
every
matrix
C
and every
M
, since

CP(-\lambda M)=C(-\lambda M)=-\lambda CM=P(CM)
. No constraint on
C

is needed.

(b)

For the triangular projection
P=P_{+}
(weight
\lambda=-1
):
condition (
6.2
) is equivalent to
C
mapping
\mathcal{A}_{-}
to

\mathcal{A}_{-}
, i.e.,
CM_{-}\in\mathcal{A}_{-}
for all
M_{-}\in\mathcal{A}_{-}
. A sufficient
condition is that
C
is upper triangular.

Proof.

Write
M=M_{+}+M_{-}
with
M_{\pm}\in\mathcal{A}_{\pm}
. Then

P_{+}(CM)=P_{+}(CM_{+}+CM_{-})
. If
C
maps
\mathcal{A}_{-}
to
\mathcal{A}_{-}
, then

CM_{-}\in\mathcal{A}_{-}
and
CM_{+}
is upper triangular (since
C
upper
triangular and
M_{+}
upper triangular implies
CM_{+}
upper triangular),
so
P_{+}(CM)=CM_{+}
. On the other hand,

CP_{+}(M)=CM_{+}
. Hence
CP_{+}(M)=P_{+}(CM)
, confirming
(
6.2
). Conversely, if
CM_{0}\notin\mathcal{A}_{-}
for some

M_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{-}
, then
P_{+}(CM_{0})\neq 0
while
CP_{+}(M_{0})=C\cdot 0=0
,
so (
6.2
) fails. Hence the condition is also necessary.
∎

Remark 6.4

(Logical structure of Proposition
6.3
)
.

Parts (i) and (ii) are logically independent. Part (i) is a structural consequence of
the projection
\Pi
(holding under Assumption

(

By Assumption

Proposition 6.5

(Lie Compatibility of Reduced Dynamics)
.

Assume the state matrices belong to a Lie subalgebra

\mathfrak{g}\subset M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and that
P(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{g}

(assumed directly). Suppose
P
satisfies the condition

(6.3)

P([X,Y])=[X,P(Y)]\quad\text{for all }X,Y\in\mathfrak{g},

i.e.,
P
commutes with the right adjoint action of
\mathfrak{g}
, and that

[X,P(Y)]\in\mathfrak{g}
whenever
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Suppose further that

\Pi
preserves
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e.,
\Pi(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{g}
(a

and

both map

\mathfrak{g}
into itself). Under these hypotheses, the equivalent dynamics

remain in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Remark 6.7

Then
P_{+}([X,Y])=P_{+}(e_{11}-e_{22})=e_{11}-e_{22}
(both diagonal matrices are in

\mathcal{A}_{+}
), whereas
[X,P_{+}(Y)]=[e_{12},P_{+}(e_{21})]=[e_{12},0]=0
(since

e_{21}\in\mathcal{A}_{-}
and
P_{+}(e_{21})=0
). Thus condition (ii) fails. Therefore
Proposition
6.5
does not apply to
P_{+}
in general; a case-by-case
verification for the specific Lie subalgebra is required.

Proof of Proposition
6.5
.

By hypothesis,
P(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{g}
. Since
\Pi
preserves

\mathfrak{g}
by assumption, we have
\bar{A}=\Pi P(A)\in\Pi(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{g}
and similarly
\bar{A}_{d}=\Pi P(A_{d})\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Condition (
6.3
) is used to characterize the orbit structure: for

X\in\mathfrak{g}
and
Y=A
(or
A_{d}
), (
6.3
) gives

P([X,A])=[X,P(A)]
, showing that
P
maps the adjoint orbit of
A
in
\mathfrak{g}

into the adjoint orbit of
P(A)
.

For
P(X)=-\lambda X
:
P([X,Y])=-\lambda[X,Y]
and

[X,P(Y)]=-\lambda[X,Y]
, so (
6.3
) holds and

P(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{g}
trivially. For
P_{+}
, condition
(
6.3
) fails in general, as shown by the counterexample in
Remark
6.7
.
∎

Remark 7.4

Discussion.

The non-trivial structure
\Pi\neq 0
arises from the underactuated configuration

m=1<n=2
. The singularity
\det(\bar{A})=0
is a consequence of the rank-1 structure
of
\Pi
and does not affect the stability analysis. The Rota-Baxter weight

\lambda=0.5
compresses the effective state matrices by
0.5
, improving LMI
feasibility. The effective
\mathcal{L}_{2}
-gain (under zero extended initial conditions)
is
\gamma^{*}/\sqrt{\mu}\approx 0.693
, which is larger than the bare parameter

\gamma^{*}\approx 0.24
; this illustrates that
\gamma
alone does not characterize
the disturbance attenuation level. For non-zero initial conditions (
V_{0}>0
),
this ratio does not represent the standard
\ell_{2}/\ell_{2}
input-output gain.

8.
Conclusion

This paper established a rigorous framework linking Rota-Baxter operators, the matrix

C^{*}
-algebra
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
(motivated by the discrete Toeplitz algebra, whose
role is clarified in Remark
1.1
as purely heuristic—no rigorous
infinite-dimensional reduction is performed), and sliding mode control of delayed
systems. We provided structural characterizations of Rota-Baxter operators in

M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, derived Lyapunov-based matrix inequality conditions ensuring robust
asymptotic stability via the Schur complement lemma, and supplied a fully linear
reformulation via the congruence transformation
Q=X^{-1}

(Remark
5.7
). The algebraic invariance results
(Propositions
6.3
and
6.5
) were stated under explicit
hypotheses and verified on the concrete examples of Section
3
.
The finite-dimensional setting was justified by working directly within
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

The QSM analysis (Proposition
4.2
) distinguishes the reaching phase
(condition (
4.6
)) from band-invariance (condition
(
4.9
)). The formula (
4.7
) provides an upper bound on the
reaching time; the actual reaching time may be earlier. The validity of
\alpha_{0}
as a
uniform bound throughout the reaching phase is established in
Remark
4.3
under the design procedure of Remark
4.7
:

r_{0}
and
\rho_{\max}
are fixed from open-loop data before
K
and
\rho
are chosen;
condition (
4.5
) is then verified
after

K
is selected
(requiring
\|A_{P}-B_{P}K\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1
, condition (
4.4
), which is
strictly stronger than Schur stability; see
Remarks
3.7
–
7.2
). If
(
4.5
) fails,
r_{0}
is increased conservatively or
K
is redesigned.
This is verified numerically in Section
7.2
. The consistency of the
two-phase bounds is established in Remarks
4.6
and
4.5
.

The Jacobi identity for the induced Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{P}
(Lemma
3.2
)
is established via three cancellations:
S_{1}+S_{2}=0
(Groups 1–2),

G^{(1)}+H+G^{(2)}=0
(Group 3, joint cancellation via the ordinary Jacobi identity
applied to the triple
(P(x)+\lambda x,y,z)
, with cyclic sum over the three even
permutations
(x,y,z)\to(y,z,x)\to(z,x,y)
, and with the identifications of the cyclic
sums
(*)=G^{(2)}/\lambda
and
(**)=H/\lambda
made explicit by re-labelling the
dummy cyclic variable), and
G^{(3)}=0
(direct). The explicit computation of
Remark
3.4
confirms that none of the three Group-3 terms
vanishes individually in general (for
P_{+}
,
x=e_{12}
,
y=e_{21}
,
z=e_{11}
:

G^{(1)}/\lambda=e_{11}-e_{22}\neq 0
,
G^{(2)}/\lambda=e_{22}-e_{11}\neq 0
,

H=0
, with joint sum zero); only their combined sum vanishes.

The stability analysis of Theorem
5.4
applies to the equivalent control
system (
5.1
) on the sliding manifold; band-invariance of the manifold under
the actual closed-loop law (
4.1
) is certified separately by
condition (
4.9
) of Proposition
4.2
. The effective

\mathcal{L}_{2}
-gain
\gamma/\sqrt{\mu}
is valid under
zero extended initial
conditions
(
x(0)=0
and
x(-i)=0
for
i=1,\ldots,\tau
); this is clarified in
the statement of Theorem
5.4
(b) and its proof. Proposition
6.3
provides explicit characterizations (not merely reformulations)
of condition (
6.2
) for each operator: universally for scalar scaling,
and as a necessary and sufficient condition on
C
for the triangular projection
P_{+}
.
Proposition
6.5
applies in practice only to the scalar scaling
operator among the examples of this paper; its scope is now clearly delimited in
Remark
6.6
.

).

.

•

Providing a non-degenerate

P_{+}
with a suitably structured system), including explicit

) for
