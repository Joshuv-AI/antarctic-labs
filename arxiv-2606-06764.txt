Optimal Rates for Generalization of Gradient Descent Methods with Deep Neural Networks
Junyu Zhou 1 , Puyu Wang 2 , Yunwen Lei 3 , Yiming Ying 4∗ and Ding-Xuan Zhou 4

Abstract
Recent progress has been made in understanding the statistical generalization performance of gradient descent methods for overparameterized neural networks within the neural tangent kernel (NTK) regime. However, most of the existing work on regression problems is limited to shallow network architectures, leaving a notable gap in the theory of deep neural networks. This paper addresses this gap by presenting a comprehensive generalization analysis for deep ReLU networks trained using gradient descent (GD) and stochastic gradient descent (SGD). Specifically, we establish the first known minimax-optimal rates of excess population risk for both GD and SGD with deep ReLU networks, under the assumption that the network width scales polynomially with respect to the network depth and training sample size. Our results demonstrate that with sufficient width, gradient descent methods for deep ReLU networks can achieve optimal generalization rates on par with kernel methods.

Introduction

Deep neural networks (DNNs) trained with gradient descent methods have achieved a remarkable success across a wide range of applications, including computer vision, natural language processing, and speech recognition
[
4
,
24
,
28
,
44
]
.
Despite their highly nonconvex and overparameterized nature, DNNs can achieve a near-zero training error while still generalizing well to unseen data
[
57
]
.
To demystify this phenomenon, an extensive amount of work has been done to understand the generalization and optimization properties of gradient descent methods for training DNNs.

The neural tangent kernel (NTK), introduced by
[
26
]
, has emerged as a powerful framework for understanding the generalization performance of overparameterized neural networks trained using gradient descent methods.
It reveals that, in the infinite-width limit, the training trajectory of a neural network with random initialization closely mirrors the behavior of its counterpart in the reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) associated with the NTK.
This connection effectively bridges the gap between learning with DNNs and classical kernel methods, allowing insights from the kernel methods to inform our understanding of DNNs.

Following this perspective, the global convergence of gradient descent methods with DNNs has been extensively studied
[
1
,
19
,
59
]
, while their generalization properties have only been investigated in a few works
[
11
,
12
,
15
,
53
]
.
Specifically, the appealing work
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
developed algorithm-dependent misclassification error bounds for deep ReLU networks trained by gradient descent (GD) and stochastic gradient descent (SGD), respectively.

[
15
]
relaxed the requirement of their network width for both GD and SGD.
However, all of these works focused on classification problems under data separation assumptions.
Very recently, the work
[
53
]
studied one-pass SGD in the streaming (continuously coming) data setting with deep ReLU networks for regression problems and showed that the prediction error of one-pass SGD
for deep ReLU networks can converge to zero in expectation, provided that the width of the network scales exponentially with the number of layers.

On another important front, it is well-established in the kernel methods literature
[
18
,
35
,
54
,
56
]
that for least squares regression, GD and SGD in RKHS can achieve the minimax-optimal rates in the sense that the excess population risk is of the form
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+\gamma}})
, under standard regularity assumptions on the regression function and capacity assumptions associated with the RKHS
[
13
,
36
]
.
Here,
n
is the size of the training data,
\beta>0
is the smoothness of the target function
f_{\rho}
and
\gamma\in[0,1]
is a parameter that measures the capacity of the hypothesis space.

Motivation.
Since the NTK perspective provides a close connection between the two learning processes of neural networks and kernel methods trained by gradient descent methods, it is natural to expect that neural networks trained by GD and SGD exhibit generalization performance (measured by excess population risk) comparable to their kernel-based counterparts.
This conjecture has been partially validated for shallow neural networks when the width is large enough. In particular,
[
39
,
41
]
demonstrated that GD and one-pass SGD for two-layer neural networks with
smooth activation functions
can replicate the classical results in the kernel setting, achieving the excess risk rates
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+\gamma}})
.
However, a critical open question remains:

Can DNNs trained by GD and SGD achieve minimax-optimal excess risk rates on par with their kernel-based counterparts?

In this work, we provide an affirmative answer to this question, significantly advancing the theoretical understanding of generalization of GD and SGD for training DNNs.

Main contributions.
This paper extends the results for GD and SGD from shallow neural networks to DNNs while maintaining minimax-optimal excess risk rates under mild overparameterization conditions. Our contributions can be summarized as follows:

•

We provide a comprehensive generalization analysis for deep ReLU networks trained with gradient descent methods for regression problems.
For an
L
-layer ReLU network with a sufficiently large width
m
, we show that both GD and SGD can replicate the classical results in the kernel setting with the same gradient complexity under similar assumptions.
Here, gradient complexity is the number of times the algorithm calculates the gradient.
Specifically, we develop the minimax-optimal excess population risk rates
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+\gamma}})
for both GD and SGD with deep ReLU networks when
m
depends polynomially on
L,n
and
d
without imposing the commonly used assumptions on the Gram matrix of the NTK, where
d
is the dimension of the data.

•

We improve the requirement of the network width in
[
53
]
from exponential scaling in the number of layers
L
to polynomial scaling. This relaxation has been achieved in
[
60
,
15
]
for the classification setting by establishing favorable properties of the network at initialization. However, their methods cannot be directly applied to our setting, as we require these properties to hold uniformly over the entire input space, while their results are typically limited to the finite training dataset.

•

In particular, this is the first work to overcome the technical challenges of achieving the optimal excess risk rates from shallow to deep architectures within the NTK regime, demonstrating that deep ReLU networks trained with GD and SGD can achieve generalization performance on par with their kernel-based counterparts. Technical Novelty

The minimax-optimal excess risk rates for shallow neural networks trained with gradient descent methods have been established in
[
41
,
39
]
.
However, their approaches cannot extend directly to DNNs.
To analyze the excess population risk of
f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}
, which represents the performance of a network
f_{\mathbf{W}}
at the output of GD/SGD with
T
iterations,
[
41
,
39
]
employ the error decomposition

\big\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f_{\rho}\big\|^{2}_{\rho}\lesssim\big\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}\big\|^{2}_{\rho}+\big\|f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-g^{m}_{T}\big\|^{2}_{\rho}+\big\|g^{m}_{T}-h^{m}\big\|_{\rho}^{2}+\big\|h^{m}-f_{\rho}\big\|_{\rho}^{2},

where
f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}
is the linear approximation of
f_{\mathbf{W}}
at the Gaussian initialization
\mathbf{W}(0)
,
g^{m}_{T}
is GD/SGD associated with the finite-width NTK
K^{m}
within the RKHS
\mathcal{H}_{m}
,
h^{m}
is either the minimizer of the regularized population risk over
\mathcal{H}_{m}

[
41
]
or the GD for the population risk in
\mathcal{H}_{m}

[
39
]
, and
f_{\rho}
is the target function.
A critical step in their analysis is to control the term
\|g^{m}_{T}-h_{m}\|_{\rho}^{2}
by
n^{-\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+\tilde{\gamma}}}
, where
\tilde{\gamma}
is the effective dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{m}
.
To achieve minimax-optimal rates, it is essential to demonstrate that the effective dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{m}
matches that of
\mathcal{H}_{K}
, i.e.,
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma
.
For
\gamma=1
, this equivalence naturally holds since the integral operator associated with
K^{m}
is a trace-class operator.
For
\gamma<1
, the argument is established by treating
K^{m}
as a sum of i.i.d. random kernels with mean
K
(see Proposition B in
[
41
]
and Proposition A.18 in
[
38
]
).
Specifically, for a two-layer ReLU network
f_{\mathbf{W}}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\sum_{r=1}^{m}a_{r}\sigma(\mathbf{w}_{r}^{\top}\mathbf{x})
, a kernel has the structure
K^{m}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\langle\partial_{\mathbf{w}_{r}}f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x}),\partial_{\mathbf{w}_{r}}f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\rangle_{2}
.
Since
\partial_{\mathbf{w}_{r}}f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x})=m^{-\frac{1}{2}}a_{r}\sigma^{\prime}(\mathbf{w}_{r}(0)^{\top}\mathbf{x})
depends only on the initial i.i.d. Gaussian weight
\mathbf{w}_{r}(0)
,
K^{m}
is a sum of
i.i.d.
random kernels.
However, this structure is not valid for deep ReLU networks. In deeper architectures, the gradient
\partial_{\mathbf{w}^{\ell}_{r}}f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x})
is influenced not only by the weight
\mathbf{w}^{\ell}_{r}(0)
of the
\ell
-th layer but also by the weights of all preceding and former layers.
This interdependence makes a direct extension significantly more challenging.

To overcome this challenge and establish minimax-optimal rates for any
\gamma\in[0,1]
, we adopt a refined error decomposition by introducing a new GD iterate
g_{T}
associated with the infinite-width NTK
K
in the RKHS
\mathcal{H}_{K}
.
Specifically, we decompose the error as

\big\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f_{\rho}\big\|_{\rho}^{2}\lesssim\big\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}\big\|^{2}_{\rho}+\big\|f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-g^{m}_{T}\big\|^{2}_{\rho}+\big\|g^{m}_{T}-g_{T}\big\|^{2}_{\rho}+\big\|g_{T}-f_{\rho}\big\|_{\rho}^{2}.

First term:
The first term
\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}\|_{\rho}^{2}
depends critically on forward and backward propagation estimates at random initialization.
The work
[
53
]
provided such estimates with upper bounds that scale exponentially with the network depth
L
for deep ReLU networks.
Applying these estimates to our setting will lead to the unexpected bound
\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}\|_{\rho}^{2}\lesssim C^{L}m^{-\frac{1}{3}}\text{Poly}(\eta T)
valid when
m\gtrsim C^{L}\text{Poly}(\eta T,d)
.
Here,
C>1
is an absolute constant and
\eta>0
is the step size.
In contrast, by extending the estimates of forward and backward propagation in
[
60
,
15
]
from the finite training set
S
to the full input space
\mathcal{X}
,
we obtain the improved bound
\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}\|_{\rho}^{2}\lesssim m^{-\frac{1}{3}}\text{Poly}(L,\eta T)
with a relaxed requirement
m\gtrsim\text{Poly}(L,\eta T,d)
, reducing the dependence on the network depth from exponential to polynomial.

Second and fourth terms:
The second term
\|f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-g^{m}_{T}\|^{2}_{\rho}
can be controlled by the first term and the gap between the gradients of
f_{\mathbf{W}}
at initialization
\mathbf{W}(0)
and at
\mathbf{W}(T)
, while the final term,
\|g_{T}-f_{\rho}\|_{\rho}^{2}
, is bounded using standard results for kernel methods
[
35
]
.

Third term:
The
most challenging
term,
\|g^{m}_{T}-g_{T}\|_{\rho}^{2}
, requires a more nuanced analysis.
Since
g^{m}_{T}
and
g_{T}
lie in different RKHSs, directly controlling the
L_{2}
-norm of their difference is difficult. Then, we consider estimate their difference in the infinity norm by leveraging the uniform convergence properties of the NTK
K^{m}
.
A key insight here is that the infinity norm between
g^{m}_{T}
and
g_{T}
can be controlled by that of the corresponding kernels
K^{m}
and
K
, yielding
\|g^{m}_{T}-g_{T}\|_{\rho}^{2}\lesssim\|g^{m}_{T}-g_{T}\|_{\infty}^{2}\lesssim(\eta T)^{4}\|K^{m}-K\|_{\infty}^{2}
.

[
53
]
proved that
\|K^{m}-K\|_{\infty}\lesssim C^{L}m^{-\frac{1}{6}}\sqrt{d}
assuming exponential scaling of
m
with
L
.
By applying a more refined analysis (see Lemma
16
in Section Proof), we show
\|K^{m}-K\|_{\infty}\lesssim m^{-\frac{1}{6}}\sqrt{dL}
under a relaxed condition
m\gtrsim dL^{3}
.
This improvement significantly relaxes the overparameterization requirements, completing the estimation.
Further details can be found in Proposition
3
. Note that if
f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}
is produced by SGD, the estimate strategy for the other three terms remains unchanged. There will be an additional error in the third due to the discrepancy between the SGD and GD iterates in the RKHS
\mathcal{H}_{m}
, which can be estimated using the results in
[
34
]
.

The combination of the refined error decomposition and the key insights effectively extend the analysis from shallow to deep neural networks.

Problem Formulation

Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the input space,
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be the output space, and
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
.
For simplicity, for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
and
y\in\mathcal{Y}
, we assume
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}=1
and
|y|\leq 1
, where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
is the standard Euclidean norm.
Let
\rho
be a probability measure on
\mathcal{Z}
.
Denote by
S=\{z_{i}=(\mathbf{x}_{i},y_{i}):i=1,\ldots,n\}
a training dataset drawn from the unknown distribution
\rho
. Based on
S
, we aim to build a predictor
f:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, whose performance is measured by the expected risk

\mathcal{L}(f):=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x},y)\sim\rho}[(y-f(\mathbf{x}))^{2}].

Since the distribution
\rho
is unknown in practice, we instead minimize the empirical risk defined by

\mathcal{L}_{S}(f):=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i}-f(\mathbf{x}_{i}))^{2}
.
A minimizer of the expected risk over all measurable functions is the regression function
f_{\rho}(\mathbf{x}):=\mathbb{E}[y|\mathbf{x}]
, where
\mathbb{E}[\cdot|\mathbf{x}]
denotes
the conditional expectation given
\mathbf{x}
.

In this paper, we are interested in a prediction model
f
parameterized by
\mathbf{W}
in some parameter space
\mathcal{W}
with a neural network structure.
In particular, we focus on
L
-layer deep ReLU neural networks with width
m
of the form

\displaystyle f_{\mathbf{W}}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{a}^{\top}\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\sigma\,\Big(\,\mathbf{W}^{L}\cdots\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\,\sigma\,\big(\mathbf{W}^{1}\mathbf{x}\big)\,\Big),

(1)

where
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
is the input,
\sigma(\cdot)=\max\{\cdot,0\}
is the ReLU activation,
\mathbf{W}=\big(\mathbf{W}^{1},\ldots,\mathbf{W}^{L}\big)\in\mathcal{W}
with
\mathcal{W}:=\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}\times(\mathbb{R}^{m\times m})^{L-1}
denoting the collection of weight matrices for all layers, and
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the weight vector of the output layer.
In the above formulation,
\mathbf{W}^{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
and
\mathbf{W}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
for
\ell=2,\ldots,L
is the weight of the
\ell
-hidden layer.
We denote
(\mathbf{w}^{\ell}_{r})^{\top}
the
r
-th row of
\mathbf{W}^{\ell}
for
\ell\in[L]:=\{1,\ldots,L\}
. For the simplicity of argument, we assume
m
is even and
use the notations
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W})=\mathcal{L}(f_{\mathbf{W}})
,
\mathcal{L}_{S}(\mathbf{W})=\mathcal{L}_{S}(f_{\mathbf{W}})
, and the loss
l(\mathbf{W};z)=\frac{1}{2}(y-f_{\mathbf{W}}(\mathbf{x}))^{2}
.

In this paper, we are concerned with two notable algorithms to minimize the empirical risk, i.e., GD and SGD. We will consider symmetric initialization of GD and SGD, which are widely used in the theoretical analysis of neural networks
[
29
,
39
,
41
,
53
]
.
Especially, we adopt Gaussian initialization for all weights while the weights of the last layer are initialized additionally using the symmetric weights and uniform initialization for the output layer weight defined as follows:

\displaystyle\textit{for the first }L-1\textit{ layer: }\ \ \mathbf{w}_{r}^{1}(0)\overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{d})\textit{ and }\mathbf{w}_{r}^{\ell}(0)\overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{m})\textit{ for all }r\in[m],

\displaystyle\textit{for the last layer: }\ \ \mathbf{w}_{r}^{L}(0)\overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{m})\textit{ for }r\in\big\{1,\ldots,{m}/{2}\big\},\textit{ and }\mathbf{w}_{r+\frac{m}{2}}^{L}(0)=\mathbf{w}_{r}^{L}(0),

(2)

\displaystyle\textit{for the output layer:}\ \ a_{r}\overset{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\textit{Unif }(\{-1,1\})\textit{ for }r\in\big\{1,\ldots,{m}/{2}\big\},\textit{ and }a_{r+\frac{m}{2}}=-a_{r}.

Symmetric initialization is mainly used to ensure that the initial function
f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x})=0
for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
, which simplifies theoretical analysis. As noted in
[
39
,
41
]
,
this requirement can be relaxed by taking into account the additional error caused by non-symmetric initialization.
Moreover, this symmetric trick does not affect the concentration properties of the NTK for deep ReLU networks (see the discussion in Section
5
).
For a differentiable function
F
on
\mathcal{W}
, we denote

\partial F(\mathbf{W}_{0})=\frac{\partial F(\mathbf{W})}{\partial\mathbf{W}}\Big|_{\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{W}_{0}}\ \text{ and }\ \partial_{\ell}F(\mathbf{W}_{0})=\frac{\partial F(\mathbf{W})}{\partial\mathbf{W}^{\ell}}\Big|_{\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{W}_{0}}\ \text{ for all }\ \ell\in[L].

(Gradient Descent)
.

Let
\mathbf{W}(0)\in\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}(k+1)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{W}(k)-\eta\partial\mathcal{L}_{S}\big(\mathbf{W}(k)\big).

(3)

Definition 2

.

Let
\mathbf{W}(0)\in\mathcal{W}

We are interested in the generalization performance of a model
f_{\mathbf{W}}
trained by GD and SGD with
T

\varepsilon_{risk}(f_{\mathbf{W}(T)})=\mathcal{L}(f_{\mathbf{W}(T)})-\mathcal{L}(f_{\rho})
, i.e., the discrepancy between the expected risks of

and the target function

For the least square regression,

that
\varepsilon_{risk}(f_{\mathbf{W}(T)})

\varepsilon_{risk}\big(f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}\big)=\frac{1}{2}\big\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f_{\rho}\big\|_{\rho}^{2}.

Here,
\|\cdot\|_{\rho}
is the

-norm defined as
\|f\|_{\rho}=\big(\int_{\mathcal{X}}|f(\mathbf{x})|^{2}d\rho_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x})\big)^{1/2}
where

\mathcal{X}
.

In the remainder of the paper, we focus on studying
\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f_{\rho}\|_{\rho}^{2}
. The key idea of the analysis is to introduce kernel methods as a bridge between the neural network and the best model
f_{\rho}
.
To this end, we require the concept of the neural tangent kernel (NTK)
[
26
]
.
In our setting, the NTK
K:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
with symmetric initialization is defined, for any
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
, by

\displaystyle K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})={2}\mathbb{E}\big[\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}))\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))\big]q^{L}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}),

(5)

where
\big\{\big(U^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}),U^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\big)\big\}_{\ell=1}^{L-1}
are pairs of bivariate normal variables defined iteratively by

\big(U^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}),U^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\big)\sim\mathcal{N}\big(0,\Sigma^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\big)

with

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=2\left(\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}\big[\sigma^{2}(U^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x}))\big]&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{E}\big[\sigma(U^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x}))\sigma(U^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))\big]\\
\mathbb{E}\big[&\sigma(U^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x}))\sigma(U^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))\big]&&\ \ \mathbb{E}\big[\sigma^{2}(U^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))\big]\end{aligned}\right),\ \ \ \Sigma^{0}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=\left(\begin{aligned} &1&&\!\!\!\!\!\!\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\rangle_{2}\\
\langle\mathbf{x}&,\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\rangle_{2}&&1\end{aligned}\!\right)

and

\displaystyle q^{L}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=\frac{\pi-\arccos\big(p^{L-1}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\big)}{\pi}\ \text{ with }\ p^{L-1}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=\frac{\mathbb{E}\big[\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}))\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))\big]}{\big(\mathbb{E}\big[\sigma^{2}(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}))\big]\mathbb{E}\big[\sigma^{2}(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))\big]\big)^{{1}/{2}}}.

Note that for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\ell\in[L]
,
\mathbb{E}[\sigma(U^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}))\sigma(U^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))]
is deterministic, and does not involve any randomness.
Then, from the definition we know that the NTK
K
is also deterministic.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{K}
be the RKHS associated with the kernel
K
, with inner product and induced norm denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{K}
and
\|\cdot\|_{K}
, respectively. Let
\mathcal{L}_{\rho_{\mathbf{x}}}^{2}=\big\{f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}:\|f\|_{\rho}<\infty\big\}
be the space of square-integrable functions on
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\rho_{\mathbf{x}}
.
We introduce the integral operator
\mathbf{L}:\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\rho_{\mathbf{x}}}\to\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\rho_{\mathbf{x}}}
, defined by
\mathbf{L}f=\int_{\mathcal{X}}K(\cdot,\mathbf{x})f(\mathbf{x})d\rho_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x})
.
One can show
\int_{\mathcal{X}}K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x})d\rho_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x})\leq 1
(see Property
1
in Section
4.1
), hence
\mathbf{L}
is a compact,
self-adjoint and positive operator, which has the eigen-decomposition
\mathbf{L}f=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\langle f,\Phi_{i}\rangle_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\rho_{\mathbf{x}}}}\Phi_{i}
. Here,
\{(\lambda_{i},\Phi_{i})\}
are pairs of eigenvalues
and orthogonal eigenfunctions in
\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\rho_{\mathbf{x}}}
with
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq 0
, and
\{\Phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
forms an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\rho_{\mathbf{x}}}
. For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the power
\mathbf{L}^{s}
as, for any
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\rho_{\mathbf{x}}}
,
\mathbf{L}^{s}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}^{s}\langle f,\Phi_{i}\rangle_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\rho_{\mathbf{x}}}}\Phi_{i}
. For a bounded and positive linear operator
A
on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, the trace of
A
is defined by
tr(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle Ae_{i},e_{i}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
.

To analyze the performance of kernel methods, we impose the following standard assumptions on the capacity of the hypothesis space and the complexity of
f_{\rho}
.

Assumption 1

(Effective dimension)
.

For some
\gamma\in[0,1]
and
c_{\gamma}\geq 1
, there holds

tr\big(\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{L}+\lambda\mathbf{I})^{-1}\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i}+\lambda}\leq c_{\gamma}\lambda^{-\gamma}\text{ for all }\lambda>0.

In the above assumption, the quantity
tr\big(\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{L}+\lambda\mathbf{I})^{-1}\big)
is called as the effective dimension
[
13
]
or the degrees of freedom
[
58
]
.
Note that
\mathbf{L}
is a trace class operator satisfying
tr(\mathbf{L})=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=\int_{\mathcal{X}}K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x})d\rho_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x})\leq 1
, then Assumption
1
is always true for
\gamma=1
and
c_{\gamma}=1
.
In this sense, the case of
\gamma=1
is often referred to as the capacity independent setting
[
17
]
. Assumption
1
holds true if
\mathbf{L}
is of finite rank (corresponds to
\gamma=0
) or the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{i}\}
satisfy a polynomial decaying condition
\lambda_{i}\lesssim i^{-1/\gamma}
for
\gamma\in(0,1]
.
The specific decay rates of the eigenvalues have been studied for some specific settings
[
3
,
7
,
6
,
25
,
43
]
.
For example, under the assumption that the input
\mathbf{x}
is uniformly distributed on a unit sphere,
[
25
]
showed that the eigenvalues of the NTK associated with two-layer ReLU networks decay as
\lambda_{i}\asymp i^{-\frac{d}{d-1}}
.

Assumption 2

(Source condition)
.

There exist
\beta>0
and
B>0
, such that
\|\mathbf{L}^{-\beta}f_{\rho}\|_{\rho}\leq B
.

Assumption
2
is commonly used in nonparametric regression
[
16
]
, which quantifies the smoothness (regularity) of the regression function
f_{\rho}
. The larger the value of
\beta
, the smoother
f_{\rho}
becomes and, consequently, the more stringent the assumption. In particular, if
\beta=1/2
, then this assumption indicates the requirement of
f_{\rho}\in\mathcal{H}_{K}
, which implies that there exists at least one minimizer of population risk belonging to the RKHS
\mathcal{H}_{K}
.

3
Main Results

Before presenting our main results, we first introduce some necessary definitions and notations.
Given the initialization
\mathbf{W}(0)
, define the feature map
\Phi_{m}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{W}
by

\Phi_{m}(\mathbf{x})=\partial f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x})=\big(\partial_{1}f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,\partial_{L}f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x})\big).

With this feature map, we define a PSD kernel
K^{m}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle K^{m}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=\big\langle\Phi_{m}(\mathbf{x}),\Phi_{m}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\big\rangle_{2}=\sum\nolimits_{\ell=1}^{L}\big\langle\partial_{\ell}f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x}),\partial_{\ell}f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\big\rangle_{2},

(6)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{2}
is the inner product of a vector or a matrix. Here,
K^{m}
can be seen as a random feature approximation of the NTK
K
.
According to Theorem 4.21 in
[
45
]
, there exists a unique RKHS
\mathcal{H}_{m}
associated with the kernel
K^{m}
given by

\mathcal{H}_{m}=\big\{f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}\ :\ \exists\ \mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{W}\text{ such that }f(\mathbf{x})=\big\langle\mathbf{W},\Phi_{m}(\mathbf{x})\big\rangle_{2}\text{ for all }\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}\big\},

whose corresponding norm is defined, for any
f\in\mathcal{H}_{m}
, by

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{m}}=\inf\Big\{\big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\|\mathbf{W}^{\ell}\|_{2}^{2}\big)^{1/2}:\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{W}\text{ with }f(\mathbf{x})=\big\langle\mathbf{W},\Phi_{m}(\mathbf{x})\big\rangle_{2}\text{ for all }\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}\Big\}.

We further define the linear approximation of
f_{\mathbf{W}}
at the initialization
\mathbf{W}(0)
by

f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}}(\mathbf{x})=f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x})+\big\langle\partial f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{W}(0)\big\rangle_{2}.

Let
\mathbf{K}=\big(K(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j})\big)_{i,j=1}^{n}
and
\mathbf{K}^{m}=\big(K^{m}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j})\big)_{i,j=1}^{n}
be the Gram matrices with kernels
K
and
K^{m}
, respectively.
For a function
\psi
on an arbitrary space
\Omega
, we define the inifinty norm of
\psi
as
\|\psi\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}|\psi(\omega)|
.

3.1
Optimal Rates for Gradient Descent

Throughout the paper, we denote
M\lesssim M^{\prime}
if there exists a constant
c>0
such that
M\leq cM^{\prime}
, and denote
M\asymp M^{\prime}
if both
M\lesssim M^{\prime}
and
M^{\prime}\lesssim M
hold. Define the functions
K^{m}_{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{H}_{m},K_{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{H}_{K}
by
K^{m}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=K^{m}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
and
K_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
for any
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}.

If we regard the empirical risk
\mathcal{L}_{S}(\cdot)
as a functional on the RKHS
\mathcal{H}_{m}
and
\mathcal{H}_{K}
, the iteration of GD for least-square regression in
\mathcal{H}_{m}
and
\mathcal{H}_{K}
can be defined as

\displaystyle g^{m}_{k+1}=g^{m}_{k}-\frac{\eta}{n}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}\big(g^{m}_{k}(\mathbf{x}_{i})-y_{i}\big)K^{m}_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}\text{ for any $k\in\mathbb{N}$ with $g^{m}_{0}=0$},

(7)

\displaystyle g_{k+1}=g_{k}-\frac{\eta}{n}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}\big(g_{k}(\mathbf{x}_{i})-y_{i}\big)K_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}\text{ for any $k\in\mathbb{N}$ with $g_{0}=0.$}

(8)

Let
\mathbf{W}(T)
,
g^{m}_{T}
and
g_{T}
be produced by (
3
), (
7
) and (
8
) with
T
iterations, respectively.
Considering
f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}
,
g^{m}_{T}
and
g_{T}
as bridges connecting
f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}
and
f_{\rho}
, we introduce the following error decomposition

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{risk}\big(f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}\big)=\frac{1}{2}\big\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f_{\rho}\big\|_{\rho}^{2}\lesssim\big\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}\big\|_{\rho}^{2}+\big\|f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-\mathbf{S}_{m}g^{m}_{T}\big\|_{\rho}^{2}+\big\|\mathbf{S}_{m}g^{m}_{T}-\mathbf{S}g_{T}\big\|_{\rho}^{2}+\big\|\mathbf{S}g_{T}-f_{\rho}\big\|_{\rho}^{2},

(9)

where
\mathbf{S}_{m}:\mathcal{H}_{m}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\rho_{\mathbf{x}}}
and
\mathbf{S}:\mathcal{H}_{K}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\rho_{\mathbf{x}}}
are the inclusion mappings that map
g^{m}_{T}\in\mathcal{H}_{m}
to
\mathbf{S}_{m}g^{m}_{T}\in\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\rho_{\mathbf{x}}}
and
g_{T}\in\mathcal{H}_{K}
to
\mathbf{S}g_{T}\in\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\rho_{\mathbf{x}}}
, respectively.
We will state the estimates for the above four terms in the subsequent context respectively and present the upper bounds of
\varepsilon_{risk}(f_{\mathbf{W}(T)})
.
We assume
\eta T\geq 1
and denote
C\geq 1
as an absolute value which may differ from line to line.

We begin by estimating the term
\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}\|_{\rho}^{2}
on the right-hand side of (
9
).
Since the population distribution
\rho
is unknown, in the following proposition we employ the
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
-norm to control the
\|\cdot\|_{\rho}
-norm of
f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}
.
In this sense, the established bound is the worst-case one which holds true for any population distribution
\rho
.
The detailed proof is deferred to Section
4.3.1
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Assume
\eta\leq 1/5
and

\displaystyle m\gtrsim L^{22}d^{3}(\eta T)^{7}\log^{3}(m/\delta).

(10)

Then, with probability at least
1-L\exp\big(\mathcal{O}(dL\log(m))-\Omega(m^{\frac{1}{3}})\big)-\delta
over the initialization
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{W}(0))
, there holds

\big\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}\big\|_{\rho}^{2}\leq\big\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}\big\|_{\infty}^{2}\lesssim\frac{L^{\frac{14}{3}}(\eta T)^{\frac{4}{3}}}{m^{\frac{1}{3}}}.

Remark 1
.

The estimate of
\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}\|_{\rho}^{2}
is controlled by showing the trajectory of GD/SGD is always near the initialization, which critically depends on forward and backward propagation estimates at random initialization.
The work
[
53
]
provided such estimates with upper bounds depend exponentially on the number of layers
L
.
Applying these estimates to our approach leads to the unexpected bound
\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}\|_{\rho}^{2}\lesssim C^{L}m^{-\frac{1}{3}}\text{Poly}(\eta T)
valid when
m\gtrsim C^{L}\text{Poly}(\eta T,d)
.
Meanwhile,
[
60
,
15
]
conducted fine-grained analyses for forward and backward propagation for classification problems, significantly relaxing the required network width from an exponential to a polynomial scaling.
However, their approach cannot be directly applied to our setting, as we require these results to hold uniformly over the entire input space
\mathcal{X}
to control the
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
-norm, while their results are typically restricted to the training dataset
S
.
We extend their results from the finite training set
S
to the full input space
\mathcal{X}
, reducing the requirement of the width to a polynomial scaling. More details can be found in Lemmas
15
and
17
in Section
4
.

Proposition
2
presents the estimation of
\|f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-\mathbf{S}_{m}g^{m}_{T}\|_{\rho}^{2}
, whose proof can be found in Section
4.3.2
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Assume
\eta\leq 1/5
,
\eta T\leq n(36\log(2n/\delta))^{-1}
and (
10
).
Then, with probability at least
1-L\exp\big(\mathcal{O}(dL\log(m))-\Omega(m^{\frac{1}{3}})\big)-\delta
over the initialization
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{W}(0))
and sampling, there holds

\big\|f^{\text{lin}}_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-\mathbf{S}_{m}g^{m}_{T}\big\|_{\rho}^{2}\lesssim\frac{L^{\frac{14}{3}}(\eta T)^{\frac{7}{3}}}{m^{\frac{1}{3}}}.

Remark 2
.

Propositions
1
and
2
jointly demonstrate the almost equivalence of the GD trajectories for a deep ReLU network and for the corresponding NTK
K^{m}
under overparameterization, i.e.,
\|f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}-\mathbf{S}_{m}g^{m}_{T}\|_{\rho}^{2}\lesssim L^{\frac{14}{3}}(\eta T)^{\frac{7}{3}}m^{-\frac{1}{3}}
under the condition
m\gtrsim\text{Poly}(L,d,\eta T)
.
This implies the larger the width of the network
m
, the closer the two trajectories are and the more the behavior of
f_{\mathbf{W}(T)}
is similar to that of
g^{m}_{T}
.

[
41
]
established this estimate for the trajectory of the SGD average stream. They showed that these two trajectories behave almost the same when the network width
m
scales exponentially with
n
. Subsequently,
[
10
]
reduced the requirement of
m
to the polynomial degree.
However, both of these two works are limited to two-layer networks with smooth activation.
Our result demonstrates that even for deep networks with non-smooth ReLU activation, a polynomially large width is sufficient to ensure the alignment of the learning trajectories.

We estimate
\|\mathbf{S}_{m}g^{m}_{T}-\mathbf{S}g_{T}\|_{\rho}^{2}
in the following proposition.
The proof is deferred to Section
4.3.3
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Assume
\eta\leq 1/4
and (
10
).
Then, with probability at least
1-L\exp\big(\mathcal{O}(dL\log(m))-\Omega(m^{\frac{1}{3}})\big)-\delta
over the initialization
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{W}(0))
, there holds

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbf{S}_{m}g^{m}_{T}-\mathbf{S}g_{T}\big\|_{\rho}^{2}\leq\big\|g^{m}_{T}-g_{T}\big\|_{\infty}^{2}\lesssim(\eta T)^{4}\big\|K^{m}-K\big\|^{2}_{\infty}\lesssim\frac{L(\eta T)^{4}}{m^{\frac{1}{3}}}.

Remark 3
.

The above proposition shows that the distance between the GD iterates in
\mathcal{H}_{m}
and
\mathcal{H}_{K}
can be controlled by the discrepancy between their respective kernels,
K^{m}
and
K
.
In fact, this result can be extended to any pair of RKHS with bounded kernels.
Specifically, for arbitrary RKHS
\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}
with bounded kernels
K^{1},K^{2}
, let
g^{1}_{T}
and
g^{2}_{T}
denote the corresponding GD iterates (defined analogously to (
7
) with
K^{m}
replaced by
K^{1}
and
K^{2}
), respectively.
Then, it follows that
\|g^{1}_{T}-g^{2}_{T}\|_{\infty}\lesssim(\eta T)^{2}\|K^{1}-K^{2}\|_{\infty}
. In addition, the work
[
53
]
proved that
\|K^{m}-K\|_{\infty}\lesssim C^{L}m^{-\frac{1}{6}}\sqrt{d}
valid when
m
depends exponentially on
L
.
We improved their bound to
\|K^{m}-K\|_{\infty}\lesssim m^{-\frac{1}{6}}\sqrt{dL}
with the reduced condition
m\gtrsim\text{Poly}(L,n,d)
. More details can be found in Lemma
16
in Section
4
.

Finally, we provide an estimate for the last term,
\|\mathbf{S}g_{T}\!-\!f_{\rho}\|_{\rho}^{2}
, which captures the performance of GD within
\mathcal{H}_{K}
. The detailed proof is presented in Section
4.3.4
.

Proposition 4
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
and
2
hold.
Assume
\eta\leq 1
and
\eta T\leq n(9\log(2n/\delta))^{-1}
.
Then, with probability at least
1-\delta
over sampling, there holds

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbf{S}g_{T}-f_{\rho}\big\|_{\rho}^{2}\lesssim\Big(\frac{\eta T}{n^{2}}+\frac{(\eta T)^{\gamma}+(\eta T)^{1-2\beta}}{n}\Big)\log^{4}\big({T}/{\delta}\big)+\frac{1}{(\eta T)^{2\beta}}.

Remark 4
.

Propositions
3
and
4
together provide an estimate for
\|\mathbf{S}_{m}g^{m}_{T}-f_{\rho}\|_{\rho}^{2}
.
In this remark, we highlight the technical novelty of our approach.
For two-layer neural networks, previous work
[
14
,
40
]
estimated this term by introducing an intermediate term
h^{m}\in\mathcal{H}_{m}
, separately bounding
\|\mathbf{S}_{m}(g^{m}_{T}-h^{m})\|_{\rho}^{2}
and
\|\mathbf{S}_{m}h^{m}-f_{\rho}\|_{\rho}^{2}
.
Here,
h^{m}
is either the minimizer of the regularized population risk over
\mathcal{H}_{m}

[
41
]
or the GD for the population risk in
\mathcal{H}_{m}

[
39
]
.
One can show that
\|\mathbf{S}_{m}(g^{m}_{T}-h_{m})\|_{\rho}^{2}\lesssim n^{-\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+\tilde{\gamma}}}
with
\tilde{\gamma}
the effective dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{m}
.
Hence, to achieve optimal rates, it is essential to demonstrate that the effective dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{m}
matches that of
\mathcal{H}_{K}
, i.e.,
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma
.
As discussed in the introduction, this equivalence naturally holds for
\gamma=1
. When
\gamma<1
, it is established by treating
K^{m}
as a sum of i.i.d. random kernels with mean
K
.
However, this structure is not valid for deep architectures, as the gradient
\partial_{\mathbf{w}^{\ell}_{r}}f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x})
is influenced not only by the weights
\mathbf{w}^{\ell}_{r}(0)
of the
\ell
-th layer but also by the weights of all preceding layers.
In contrast, we introduce
g_{T}\in\mathcal{H}_{K}
as an intermediate term, and separately estimate
\|\mathbf{S}_{m}g^{m}_{T}\!-\!\mathbf{S}g_{T}\|_{\rho}^{2}
and
\|\mathbf{S}g_{T}\!-\!f_{\rho}\|_{\rho}^{2}
in Propositions
3
and
4
.

Combining the above four propositions, we now present our main result on the excess population risk of GD for deep ReLU networks. The detailed proof is deferred to Section
4.3.5
.

Theorem 5
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
and
2
hold.
For any
\delta\in(0,1)
, assume
\eta\leq 1/5
,
\eta T\leq n(36\log(8n/\delta))^{-1}
and (
10
) hold.
Then, with probability at least
1-L\exp\big(\mathcal{O}(dL\log(m))-\Omega(m^{\frac{1}{3}})\big)-\delta
over initialization
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{W}(0))
and sampling, there holds

\mathcal{L}(f_{\mathbf{W}(T)})-\mathcal{L}(f_{\rho})\lesssim\frac{L^{\frac{14}{3}}(\eta T)^{4}}{m^{\frac{1}{3}}}+\Big(\frac{\eta T}{n^{2}}+\frac{(\eta T)^{\gamma}+(\eta T)^{1-2\beta}}{n}\Big)\log^{4}\big({T}/{\delta}\big)+\frac{1}{(\eta T)^{2\beta}}.

We point out that our result does not need the widely adopted positivity assumption on the NTK Gram matrix
\mathbf{K}^{m}
to learn ReLU networks
[
2
,
20
]
, i.e., the smallest eigenvalue of
\mathbf{K}^{m}
is strictly larger than
0
.
Previous work
[
41
,
46
]
has shown that this assumption can be overly restrictive, as the smallest eigenvalue of
\mathbf{K}^{m}
tends to zero as the size of the training set increases.

The following corollary, derived from Theorem
5
, shows that when the network width scales polynomially with the sample size
n
, dimension
d
, and the number of layers
L
, GD with a deep ReLU network can achieve the optimal excess risk rate
\mathcal{O}\big(n^{-\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+\gamma}}\big)
, with a gradient complexity of
\mathcal{O}\big(n^{1+\frac{1}{2\beta+\gamma}}\big)
.

Corollary 6
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
and
2
hold and
2\beta+\gamma>1
.
For any
\delta\in(0,1)
, assume that
n\geq\big(36(2\beta+\gamma)\beta^{-1}\big)^{\frac{2\beta+\gamma}{\beta}}\frac{16}{\delta}
and
m\gtrsim L^{14}\max\big\{L^{8}d^{3}n^{\frac{7}{2\beta+\gamma}}\log^{3}(ndL/\delta),n^{\frac{6\beta+12}{2\beta+\gamma}}\big\}
.
Choosing
T=\lceil n^{\frac{1}{2\beta+\gamma}}\rceil
and
\eta\leq 1/5
as a constant yields that, with a probability of at least
1-\delta
over initialization
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{W}(0))
and sampling, there holds

\mathcal{L}(f_{\mathbf{W}(T)})-\mathcal{L}(f_{\rho})\lesssim n^{-\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+\gamma}}\log^{4}({n}/{\delta}).

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, the work
[
35
]
proved that the optimal excess risk rate
\mathcal{O}\big(n^{-\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+\gamma}}\big)
can be achieved by GD in the kernel setting, with a gradient complexity of
\mathcal{O}\big(n^{1+\frac{1}{2\beta+\gamma}}\big)
, when
2\beta+\gamma>1
.
Corollary
6
shows, provided that the network width scales polynomially with
n
,
d
, and
L
, GD with a deep ReLU network can replicate the classical results in the kernel setting.
It implies that the learning capability of GD with a deep ReLU network is competitive with that of the classical kernel regime.
Moreover, as
\beta
and
\gamma
increase, the required network width
m
and the gradient complexity become less restrictive. In particular, in the capacity independent case, that is,
\gamma=1
, the optimal rate
\mathcal{O}(n^{-{2\beta\over 2\beta+1}})
can be derived that matches the kernel setting
[
55
]
.

Discussion with the existing work.

The study most relevant to our work on GD is
[
39
]
, which provided the excess population risk bounds for two-layer neural networks with smooth activation.
Specifically,
[

]
established the optimal excess risk
\mathcal{O}\big(n^{-\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+\gamma}}\big)
under Assumptions

and

, assuming the network width

. In their analysis, the smoothness of the activation function plays a crucial role especially for ensuring the boundedness of the second partial derivatives of

at

.
In contrast, our work provides the first minimax-optimal excess risk rates for DNNs with non-smooth ReLU activation functions under the same assumptions, provided

.
Besides,
[
29
]
studied the generalization performance of GD for two-layer ReLU networks under the positive eigenvalue assumption of the NTK Gram matrix, focusing on learning target functions with additive noise that is uniformly bounded and Lipschitz.

[
30
]
showed that gradient flow in two-layer ReLU networks can achieve a generalization bound of
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{2}{3}})
when

and

.

In this subsection, we provide the uniform concentration of the NTK in our setting.
Denote by

the indicator function (i.e., taking the value 1 if the argument holds true, and 0 otherwise).
Given an input

\displaystyle f_{\mathbf{W}}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{a}^{\top}\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{L}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}^{L}\cdots\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{1}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}^{1}\mathbf{x},

(14)

where
\mathbf{D}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})
with

is the diagonal sign matrix defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})=\text{diag}\big(\mathbb{I}\{\langle\mathbf{w}^{\ell}_{r},o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}\geq 0\}\big)_{r=1}^{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}

(15)

with

and

\displaystyle o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})=\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}^{\ell-1}\cdots\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{1}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}^{1}\mathbf{x}\text{ for }\ell=2,\ldots,L.

(16)

Here,

can be regarded as the output of the
(\ell-1)
-th layer.
By further defining
\big(V^{L}_{L}(\mathbf{x})\big)^{\top}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{L}(\mathbf{x})
and

\displaystyle\big(\mathbf{V}^{\ell}_{L}(\mathbf{x})\big)^{\top}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{L}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}^{L}\cdots\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\text{ for }\ell\in[L-1],

\displaystyle f_{\mathbf{W}}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{a}^{\top}\big(\mathbf{V}^{\ell}_{L}(\mathbf{x})\big)^{\top}\mathbf{W}^{\ell}o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})=\big\langle\mathbf{V}^{\ell}_{L}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{a}\big(o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\big)^{\top},\mathbf{W}^{\ell}\big\rangle_{2}.

The above observation implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{\mathbf{W}}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{W}^{\ell}}=\mathbf{V}^{\ell}_{L}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{a}\big(o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\big)^{\top}.

Denote
\|\cdot\|_{op}
the operator norm of a matrix or an operator.
For any

Let
\mathbf{D}_{0}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})
,

and

15

16

for all

The following lemma shows that only the performance of the last layer plays a role in defining

under the symmetric initialization.

Lemma 12
.

For any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
, there holds

\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{D}^{L}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}^{L}(0)=0\ \text{ and }\ \frac{\partial f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{W}^{\ell}(0)}=0\;\text{ for any }\ell\in[L-1].

Further,

\displaystyle K^{m}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=\Big\langle\frac{\partial f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{W}^{L}(0)},\frac{\partial f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})}{\partial\mathbf{W}^{L}(0)}\Big\rangle_{2}\;\text{ for all }\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}.

Proof.

Note the
r
-th row of
\mathbf{D}_{0}^{L}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}^{L}(0)
is

Since

and
\mathbf{w}^{L}_{r}(0)=\mathbf{w}^{L}_{r+\frac{m}{2}}(0)

, there holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{D}^{L}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}^{L}(0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{m}a_{r}\mathbb{I}\{\langle\mathbf{w}^{L}_{r}(0),o^{L-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}\geq 0\}(\mathbf{w}^{L}_{r}(0))^{\top}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{m}{2}}a_{r}\mathbb{I}\{\langle\mathbf{w}^{L}_{r}(0),o^{L-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}\geq 0\}(\mathbf{w}^{L}_{r}(0))^{\top}+\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{m}{2}}a_{r+\frac{m}{2}}\mathbb{I}\{\langle\mathbf{w}^{L}_{r+\frac{m}{2}}(0),o^{L-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}\geq 0\}(\mathbf{w}^{L}_{r}(0))^{\top}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{m}{2}}a_{r}\mathbb{I}\{\langle\mathbf{w}^{L}_{r}(0),o^{L-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}\geq 0\}(\mathbf{w}^{L}_{r}(0))^{\top}-\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{m}{2}}a_{r}\mathbb{I}\{\langle\mathbf{w}^{L}_{r}(0),o^{L-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}\geq 0\}(\mathbf{w}^{L}_{r}(0))^{\top}=0.

It further implies

\displaystyle\big(\mathbf{V}_{L,0}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{a}\big)^{\top}=\mathbf{a}^{\top}\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{L}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}^{L}(0)\cdots\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}_{0}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})=0.

.
The first two results of the lemma are proved.

Finally,
from (

\displaystyle K^{m}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\Big\langle\frac{\partial f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{W}^{\ell}(0)},\frac{\partial f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})}{\partial\mathbf{W}^{\ell}(0)}\Big\rangle_{2}=\Big\langle\frac{\partial f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{W}^{L}(0)},\frac{\partial f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})}{\partial\mathbf{W}^{L}(0)}\Big\rangle_{2},

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{D}}\|\mathbf{V}^{\ell}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x})\|_{op}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{D}}\Big\|\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{L}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}^{L}(0)\cdots\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{\ell+1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}^{\ell+1}(0)\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{\ell}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\Big\|_{op}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{D}}\big\|\mathbf{D}^{L}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\big\|_{op}\Big\|\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{W}^{L}(0)\prod_{h=\ell+1}^{L}\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{h}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}^{h}(0)\Big\|_{op}\Big\|\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{\ell}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\Big\|_{op}\leq\frac{CL}{\sqrt{m}}.

Hence,

\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\|\mathbf{V}^{\ell}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x})\|_{op}\leq\frac{CL}{\sqrt{m}},

which completes the proof of part
(b)
.

It remains to prove the last part.
Note

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\Big\|\frac{\partial f_{\mathbf{W}(0)}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{W}^{L}(0)}\Big\|_{2}=\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\Big\|\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{a}\big(o^{L-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\big)^{\top}\Big\|_{2}=\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\Big\|\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{L}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{a}\big(o^{L-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\big)^{\top}\Big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\|o^{L-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{2}\big(\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\big|\|o^{L-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\|_{2}-1\big|+1\big)\leq\sqrt{2}\Big(CL\sqrt{\frac{dL\log(m/\delta)}{m}}+1\Big)\leq 2,

where the last second inequality follows from the first part of this lemma, and the last inequality used the condition
m\gtrsim dL^{3}\log(m/\delta)
.
The proof of the lemma is completed.
∎

The following property shows that
K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
is bounded for any
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
.

Property 1
.

For any
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
, there holds
|K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})|\leq 1.

Proof.

By the definition of
U^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})
, we know
\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(U^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}))]=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[(U^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}))^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(U^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x}))]
.
Recursively applying this equality, we have
\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(U^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}))]=\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(U^{1}(\mathbf{x}))]=\mathbb{E}_{w\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)}[\sigma^{2}(w)]=1/2
.
Then, according to Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\ell\in[L]
, there holds

\displaystyle\big|2\mathbb{E}[\sigma(U^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}))\sigma(U^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))]\big|\leq\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(U^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}))]}\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(U^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))]}=1.

(22)

Further, according to the definition of
q^{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
, there holds
|q^{\ell}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})|\leq 1
.
Then, for
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
,
K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
can be uniformly bounded by

\displaystyle|K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})|=\big|2\mathbb{E}[\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}))\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))]\big||q^{L}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})|\leq\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}))]}\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{2}(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))]}=1.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 16
.

Let

.
Assume

With probability at least

(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{W}(0))

\displaystyle\|K^{m}-K\|_{\infty}\lesssim\sqrt{L}m^{-\frac{1}{6}}+\sqrt{dL\log(m)m^{-1}}.

Proof.

Note (
22
) and
\big|(\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{a})^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\mathbf{a}\big|=\frac{1}{m}|\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{D}^{L}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{D}^{L}_{0}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\mathbf{a}|\leq 1

From the definitions of
K^{m}
and
K

\displaystyle\|K^{m}-K\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}\!\Big|\langle o^{L-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x}),\!o^{L-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\rangle_{2}(\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{a})^{\!\top}\!\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\mathbf{a}\!-\!2\mathbb{E}[\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}))\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))]q^{L}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}\big|\langle o^{L-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x}),o^{L-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\rangle_{2}-2\mathbb{E}[\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}))\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))]\big|\cdot\big|(\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{a})^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\mathbf{a}\big|

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}\big|2\mathbb{E}[\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}))\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))]\big|\cdot\Big|(\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{a})^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\mathbf{a}-tr\big(\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x})^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}\big|2\mathbb{E}[\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}))\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))]\big|\cdot\Big|tr\big(\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x})^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\big)-q^{L}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}\big|\langle o^{L-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x}),o^{L-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\rangle_{2}-2\mathbb{E}[\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}))\sigma(U^{L-1}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))]\big|

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}\Big|(\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{a})^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\mathbf{a}-tr\big(\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x})^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\big)\Big|+\sup_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}}\Big|tr\big(\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x})^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{L}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\big)-q^{L}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\Big|

\displaystyle\!=:\mathcal{E}_{1}+\mathcal{E}_{2}+\mathcal{E}_{3},

The estimates of the above three terms
\mathcal{E}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2},\mathcal{E}_{3}
are given as follows.

Estimate of
\mathcal{E}_{1}
: The estimate of
\mathcal{E}_{1}
follows the same proof steps as in Lemma 6 in
[
53
]
.
According to Lemma 6 in
[
53
]
, one can get that

We improve this estimate from

over initialization

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}\lesssim{L}{m^{-\frac{1}{3}}}.

In this subsection, we present some useful lemmas for proving main results of both GD and SGD with deep ReLU networks.

Recall that
\mathbf{D}_{0}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})
,

and
\mathbf{V}^{\ell}_{L,0}(\mathbf{x})
are defined as (

16

17

Lemma 17
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Assume
m\gtrsim L^{22}d^{3}R_{op}^{2}\log^{3}(m/\delta)
and
R_{op}\geq 1
.
Then, with probability at least
1-\delta
over initialization
\mathbf{W}(0)
, the following statement holds for any
\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{B}_{R_{op}}\big(\mathbf{W}(0)\big)
.

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\big\|o^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})-o^{\ell}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\big\|_{2}\lesssim\frac{\ell LR_{op}}{\sqrt{m}}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
\frac{1}{C^{L}\sqrt{m}}
-net of
\mathcal{X}
.
We know for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
, there exists
\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{D}
such that
\|\mathbf{x}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{C^{L}\sqrt{m}}.

Note the condition for
m
implies
R_{op}\leq\sqrt{m}
.
Then, similar to the proof of part
(a)
of Lemma
15
, we know
\|o^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})-o^{\ell}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\|_{2}\leq(\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{2}c_{0})^{\ell}\|\mathbf{x}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}
and
\|o^{\ell}_{0}(\mathbf{x})-o^{\ell}_{0}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}
.
Note
\mathcal{X}=S^{d-1}
is the unit sphere.
From Corollary 4.2.13 in
[
49
]
, it holds that
|\mathcal{D}|\leq(3\sqrt{m})^{d}C^{dL}.

Then, applying part
(d)
of Lemma
14
with
p=(3\sqrt{m})^{d}C^{dL}
, there holds

\displaystyle\big\|o^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})-o^{\ell}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|o^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})-o^{\ell}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\big\|_{2}+\big\|o^{\ell}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})-o^{\ell}_{0}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})\big\|_{2}+\big\|o^{\ell}_{0}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})-o^{\ell}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}+\frac{\ell LR_{op}}{\sqrt{m}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\lesssim\frac{\ell LR_{op}}{\sqrt{m}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 18
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Assume
m\gtrsim L^{22}d^{3}R_{op}^{2}\log^{3}(m/\delta)
and
R_{op}\geq 1
.
For any
\mathbf{W}\in\mathcal{B}_{R_{op}}(\mathbf{W}(0))
, with probability at least
1-L\exp(\mathcal{O}(dL\log(m))-\Omega(m^{\frac{1}{3}}))-\delta
over initialization
(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{W}(0))
, for any
\ell\in[L]
, there holds

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\big\|\mathbf{D}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{D}^{\ell}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\big\|_{0}\leq\big(LmR_{op}\big)^{\frac{2}{3}}.

Proof.

Let
R^{\prime}>0
which will be chosen later.
For
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\ell\in[L]
, define the diagonal matrix

\mathbf{E}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})=diag\big\{\mathbb{I}\big\{|\langle\mathbf{w}_{r}^{\ell}(0),o^{\ell-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}|\leq R^{\prime}\big\}\big\}_{r=1}^{m}\in\{0,1\}^{m\times m}.

Note
\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\|\mathbf{D}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{D}^{\ell}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\|_{0}\leq\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\|(\mathbf{D}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{D}^{\ell}_{0}(\mathbf{x}))(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{E}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}))\|_{0}+\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\|\mathbf{E}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\|_{0}
. We will estimate
\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\|(\mathbf{D}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{D}^{\ell}_{0}(\mathbf{x}))(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{E}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}))\|_{0}
and
\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\|\mathbf{E}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\|_{0}
separately in the following proof.

Estimate of
\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\big\|(\mathbf{D}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{D}^{\ell}_{0}(\mathbf{x}))(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{E}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}))\big\|_{0}
:

From the definition, if the absolute value of
(r,r)
-th entry of the diagonal matrix
(\mathbf{D}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{D}^{\ell}_{0}(\mathbf{x}))(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{E}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}))
is
1
, then
|\langle\mathbf{w}_{r}^{\ell}(0),o_{0}^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}|>R^{\prime}
and
\mathbb{I}\{\langle\mathbf{w}_{r}^{\ell},o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}\geq 0\}\neq\mathbb{I}\{\langle\mathbf{w}_{r}^{\ell}(0),o_{0}^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}\geq 0\}
.
Then, there holds

|\langle\mathbf{w}_{r}^{\ell},o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}-\langle\mathbf{w}_{r}^{\ell}(0),o_{0}^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}|^{2}\geq|\langle\mathbf{w}_{r}^{\ell}(0),o_{0}^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}|^{2}>(R^{\prime})^{2}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\big\|(\mathbf{D}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{D}^{\ell}_{0}(\mathbf{x}))(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{E}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}))\big\|_{0}\leq\frac{1}{(R^{\prime})^{2}}\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\big(\langle\mathbf{w}_{r}^{\ell},o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}-\langle\mathbf{w}_{r}^{\ell}(0),o_{0}^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(R^{\prime})^{2}}\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\big\|\mathbf{W}^{\ell}o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{W}^{\ell}(0)o^{\ell-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\big\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(R^{\prime})^{2}}\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\Big(\big\|\mathbf{W}^{\ell}-\mathbf{W}^{\ell}(0)\big\|_{op}\big\|o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})-o^{\ell-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})+o^{\ell-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\big\|_{2}+\big\|\mathbf{W}^{\ell}(0)\big\|_{op}\big\|o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})-o^{\ell-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\big\|_{2}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(R^{\prime})^{2}}\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\Big(R_{op}\big(\big\|o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})-o^{\ell-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\big\|_{2}+C\big)+c_{0}\sqrt{m}\big\|o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})-o^{\ell-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\big\|_{2}\Big)^{2},

where in the last inequality we have used
\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\|o^{\ell-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\|_{2}\leq C
implied by part
(a)
of Lemma
15
and the condition for
m
, and
\|\mathbf{W}^{\ell}(0)\|_{op}\leq c_{0}\sqrt{m}
.

Combining the above inequality with Lemma
17
and noting the condition
m\gtrsim L^{22}d^{3}R_{op}^{2}\log^{3}(m/\delta)
, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\big\|(\mathbf{D}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{D}^{\ell}_{0}(\mathbf{x}))(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{E}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x}))\big\|_{0}\lesssim\frac{1}{(R^{\prime})^{2}}\Big(R_{op}\Big(\frac{L^{2}R_{op}}{\sqrt{m}}+C\Big)+L^{2}R_{op}\Big)\lesssim\frac{L^{2}R_{op}^{2}}{(R^{\prime})^{2}}.

Estimates of
\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\big\|\mathbf{E}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\big\|_{0}
:

The proof is similar to that of Lemma 11 in
[
53
]
, we give the proof here for the sake of completeness.

Denoting the function class
\mathcal{F}=\{\mathbb{I}\{|\langle\ \cdot\ ,o^{\ell-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}|\leq R^{\prime}\}:\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}\},
there holds

\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\frac{1}{m}\big\|\mathbf{E}^{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\big\|_{0}=\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathbb{I}\Big\{\big|\langle\mathbf{w}_{r}^{\ell}(0),o_{0}^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}\big|\leq R^{\prime}\Big\}=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{r=1}^{m}f(\mathbf{w}^{\ell}_{r}(0)).

To control the right hand side of the above equality, we need to estimate the VC-dimension of
\mathcal{F}
.
We first fixed
(\mathbf{W}^{1}(0),\ldots,\mathbf{W}^{\ell-1}(0))
.
Denote
\mathcal{D}^{\ell-1}=\{(\mathbf{D}^{1}_{0}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,\mathbf{D}^{\ell-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})):\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{{(\ell-1)}\times m\times m}
.
From Lemma 32 in
[
53
]
we know the cardinality of
\mathcal{D}^{\ell-1}
is less than
m^{d(\ell-1)}
, i.e.,
|\mathcal{D}^{\ell-1}|\leq m^{d(\ell-1)}
.
Then, there exists a disjoint partition of
\mathcal{X}
such that
\mathcal{X}=\bigcup_{j\in[|\mathcal{D}^{\ell-1}|]}U_{j}
, where
U_{i}\cap U_{j}=\varnothing
for
i\neq j
and the tuple
(\mathbf{D}^{1}_{0}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,\mathbf{D}^{\ell-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x}))\in\mathbb{R}^{(\ell-1)\times m\times m}
is a fixed matrix sequence on each
U_{j}
.
Therefore,
o^{\ell-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})=\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{{\ell-1}}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}^{{\ell-1}}(0)\cdots\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}}\mathbf{D}^{1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}^{1}(0)\mathbf{x}
lies in a
d
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
on each
U_{j}
.
Let
V_{j}
and
V
be the VC-dimension of the classes
\mathcal{F}_{j}=\{\mathbb{I}\{|\langle\ \cdot\ ,o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}|\leq R^{\prime}\}:\mathbf{x}\in U_{j}\}
and
\mathcal{F}
, respectively.
By Theorem 9.5 in
[
23
]
, the VC-dimension of the class of indicators of half spaces in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
d+1
.
Further, note that
o^{\ell-1}_{0}(\mathbf{x})
lies in a
d
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
on each
U_{j}
and the indicator function
\mathbb{I}\{|\langle\mathbf{w}^{\ell},o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}|\leq R^{\prime}\}
can be written as the multiplication of two indicators of half space, i.e.,
\mathbb{I}\{|\langle\mathbf{w}^{\ell},o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}|\leq R^{\prime}\}=\mathbb{I}\{\langle\mathbf{w}^{\ell},o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}\leq R^{\prime}\}\mathbb{I}\{\langle\mathbf{w}^{\ell},o^{\ell-1}(\mathbf{x})\rangle_{2}\geq-R^{\prime}\}

Then, from Lemma 3.2.3 in
[
8

with

we know
V_{j}\leq 10(d+1)
for any

.
By further applying Lemma 23 in
[
53
]
with

, there holds
V\lesssim\max(d\log(d),\log(|\mathcal{D}^{\ell-1}|))\lesssim d\ell\log(m)
.

Now, we turn to control
\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{r=1}^{m}f(\mathbf{w}^{\ell}_{r}(0))
, which can be regarded as a function on
(\mathbf{w}^{\ell}_{1}(0),\ldots,\mathbf{w}^{\ell}_{m}(0))
.
One can check that the value of this function can change by at most
\frac{1}{m}
under an arbitrary change of the

-th coordinate.
Then, by McDiarmid’s inequality, we know with probability at least
1-\exp(-2m^{\frac{1}{3}})
over
\mathbf{W}^{\ell}(0)
, there holds

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{r=1}^{m}f(\mathbf{w}^{\ell}_{r}(0))\leq m^{-\frac{1}{3}}+\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{W}^{\ell}(0)}\Big[\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{r=1}^{m}f(\mathbf{w}^{\ell}_{r}(0))\Big].

Now, we estimate the right-hand side of the above inequality.
There holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{W}^{\ell}(0)}\Big[\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{r=1}^{m}f(\mathbf{w}^{\ell}_{r}(0))\Big]

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we prove that both GD and SGD with deep ReLU networks can achieve the minimax-optimal rates
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+\gamma}})
of the excess risk when the network width satisfies
m\gtrsim\text{Poly}(L,n,d)
. Our results indicate that gradient descent methods with deep ReLU networks can achieve generalization performance that is at least comparable to classical gradient methods in the kernel setting. Several directions for future study remain. First, extending our results to deep networks with smooth activation would be a valuable next step. It would also be interesting to broaden the analysis to other architectures, such as convolutional networks and residual networks.
