Toward Quantum-Enhanced ISAC: Active-RIS-Aided Integrated Sensing and Communication with Rydberg Atomic Receivers
Hong-Bae Jeon, Hyung-Joo Moon, Yonghwi Kim

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate an active-RIS (ARIS)-aided integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) system with Rydberg Atomic REceiver (RARE). Leveraging the magnitude-only and real-domain observation structure of RARE, we first derive a unified ISAC model, along with a closed-form Cramér-Rao bound (CRB) for direction-of-arrival (DoA) estimation. Based on this formulation, we propose a joint design of the base station (BS) beamforming and ARIS reflection coefficients to minimize the CRB under RARE-specific signal-to-interference-noise-ratio (SINR) and ARIS power constraints. To tackle the resulting highly non-convex problem, we develop an alternating optimization (AO) framework that combines semidefinite relaxation (SDR) for beamforming and a majorization-minimization (MM)-based approach for ARIS design. Numerical results demonstrate that the proposed RARE-aware framework significantly outperforms conventional RF-based designs and achieves performance close to the radar-only benchmark, highlighting the potential of RARE for quantum-enhanced ISAC with ARIS.

Meanwhile, conventional ISAC primarily rely on classical radio-frequency (RF) receivers, whose performance is fundamentally limited by the Johnson-Nyquist thermal noise (JNTN)

To overcome this fundamental limitation, Rydberg Atomic Receivers (RARE) have recently emerged as a promising quantum sensing technology

Unlike conventional RF antennas that rely on electronic circuitry for signal reception, RARE exploits the quantum-mechanical response of highly excited Rydberg atoms to incident RF electromagnetic (EM) fields. Specifically, by exciting atoms to high-lying energy states, strong dipole interactions are induced with external RF fields, allowing the incident RF signal to be directly transduced into optical-domain variations via phenomena such as electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) and Autler-Townes (AT) splitting

\mathrm{nVcm^{-1}Hz^{-1/2}}

,
32

Moreover, unlike conventional antennas that require physical dimensions comparable to the carrier wavelength for efficient electromagnetic coupling

,
23
]
, RARE operates based on photon-atom interactions and does not rely on resonant current induction. This fundamentally different reception mechanism allows RARE to flexibly operate across a wide range of frequencies by selecting appropriate atomic transitions, without requiring hardware reconfiguration

In parallel, quantum-enhanced spatial sensing techniques have also been investigated. For instance,
[

explored multi-band atomic sensing, demonstrating that RARE can leverage multiple atomic transitions to detect multi-frequency signals and estimate their spatial parameters via quantum probe measurements. In addition,

Complementing these RARE-based spatial sensing approaches,

[
29
]

[

,

]
, while its inherent broadband capability enables more adaptable and efficient ISAC designs. However, despite these promising features,

Although prior works have investigated RARE-enabled ISAC
[
6
,
14
]
, they primarily focus on receiver design and signal processing aspects, where the propagation environment is assumed to be fixed. Consequently, they cannot leverage propagation-domain DoF to enhance echo signals or suppress multi-user interference, which limits their effectiveness in challenging scenarios and motivates the integration of ARIS for joint propagation and receiver optimization.

Herein, the complementary gains can be achieved from both the propagation and receiver domains. Specifically, ARIS actively reshapes and amplifies the channel to mitigate severe path loss and multiplicative fading, while RARE enables high-sensitivity signal acquisition with a reduced noise floor. This complementary interaction establishes a powerful synergy, jointly enabling reliable ISAC beyond the capability of conventional RF-based systems. Motivated by this, this paper investigates an ARIS-ISAC framework with RARE that explicitly incorporates the RARE-specific signal model into a unified ISAC framework. This reveals new design insights arising from its real-domain observation structure and low-noise characteristics of RARE, which fundamentally reshape the signal model and require a rethinking of joint communication and sensing design. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

•

We establish a unified ARIS-ISAC framework incorporating RARE at multiple communication users and the sensing receiver at the BS. This dual deployment simultaneously mitigates receiver-side noise limitations and improves echo signal acquisition, thereby enhancing both communication reliability and sensing accuracy. Moreover, we characterize the unique magnitude-only real-domain observation structure and show that the RARE measurement can be equivalently represented as a structured real-valued projection of the effective channel, revealing that communication information is intrinsically embedded in the power-domain observations of RARE.

•

Based on the proposed RARE-aware signal model, we derive a closed-form CRB for target DoA estimation in an ARIS-ISAC with RARE at BS. It explicitly captures the impact of ARIS-induced channel shaping, amplified noise propagation, and RARE-specific measurement characteristics, thereby providing key insights into how receiver sensitivity and propagation control jointly influence sensing accuracy.

•

Leveraging the above analysis, we formulate a CRB-driven joint design problem, where the transmit beamforming and ARIS coefficients are jointly optimized under RARE-specific SINR and ARIS power constraints. To efficiently solve the resulting highly non-convex problem, we develop a structured alternating optimization (AO) framework:

1.

We first reformulate the transmit beamforming subproblem into a tractable semidefinite relaxation (SDR) by lifting the beamforming vector into a higher-dimensional positive semidefinite matrix. This converts the original non-convex quadratic problem into a convex semidefinite program (SDP), enabling efficient optimization under the QoS constraints. The beamforming solution is then recovered through rank-one approximation, yielding a near-optimal transmit strategy with manageable complexity.

2.

We then develop an ARIS update strategy based on the majorization-minimization (MM) framework to handle the non-convex unit-modulus and power-coupled constraints introduced by ARIS amplification. Specifically, a tight surrogate function is constructed to upper-bound the original objective while capturing both desired signal enhancement and amplified noise propagation. The resulting subproblem admits a low-complexity element-wise update with guaranteed monotonic improvement across iterations.

•

Extensive simulation demonstrates that the proposed RARE-enabled ARIS-ISAC framework consistently outperforms conventional RF-based designs across various operating conditions and approaches the radar-only performance bound. The results highlight the effectiveness of jointly exploiting ARIS-driven propagation enhancement and RARE-based quantum-enhanced reception, enabling robust echo recovery for accurate sensing while simultaneously improving communication reliability, thereby effectively enhancing overall ISAC performance.

\mathbf{Z}_{r}=\Re\left\{\mathbf{E}_{r}(\bm{\Phi},\theta)\mathbf{X}\right\}+\mathbf{W}_{r}
, where
\mathbf{X}=[\mathbf{x}[1]\cdots\mathbf{x}[L]]
and
\mathbf{W}_{r}=[\mathbf{w}_{r}[1]\cdots\mathbf{w}_{r}[L]]
. Finally, vectorizing it, we obtain the real-valued sensing observation model

\mathbf{z}_{e}\triangleq\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{Z}_{r})=\bm{\mu}(\bm{\xi})+\mathbf{w},

(13)

where
\bm{\mu}(\bm{\xi})\triangleq\Re\left\{\alpha\mathbf{a}(\theta)+\mathbf{b}\right\}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{t}L}
with
\mathbf{a}(\theta)\triangleq\mathrm{vec}\Big(\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\mathbf{Q}(\bm{\Phi},\theta)\mathbf{X}\Big)
and
\mathbf{b}\triangleq\mathrm{vec}\Big(\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{SI}}\mathbf{X}\Big)
, and
\mathbf{w}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{R}_{e}),~\mathbf{R}_{e}\triangleq\mathbf{I}_{L}\otimes\mathbf{R}_{w}
with Kronecker product
\otimes
.

II-D

CRB for DoA Estimation

We define the real-valued unknown parameter vector as
\bm{\xi}\triangleq[\theta~\alpha_{R}~\alpha_{I}]^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where
\alpha_{R}\triangleq\Re\{\alpha\}
and
\alpha_{I}\triangleq\Im\{\alpha\}
.
Let
\mathbf{S}=[\mathbf{s}[1]\cdots\mathbf{s}[L]]\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times L}
denote the probing symbol matrix, where
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{S}
. Since the BS is dual-functional,
\mathbf{X}
is perfectly known at the BS, and
L
is sufficiently large for estimation,
\mathbf{S}
is designed to satisfy
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{*}=L\mathbf{I}_{K}

[
43
,
68
]
:

\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{*}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{*}\mathbf{W}^{*}=L\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}.

(14)

Define
\dot{\mathbf{Q}}(\bm{\Phi},\theta)\triangleq\frac{\partial\mathbf{Q}(\bm{\Phi},\theta)}{\partial\theta}
and
\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)\triangleq\frac{\partial\mathbf{a}(\theta)}{\partial\theta}=\mathrm{vec}\Big(\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\dot{\mathbf{Q}}(\bm{\Phi},\theta)\mathbf{X}\Big)
. Since
\bm{\mu}(\bm{\xi})=\Re\{\alpha\mathbf{a}(\theta)+\mathbf{b}\}
, the partial derivatives are given by

\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}}{\partial\theta}=\Re\{\alpha\dot{\mathbf{a}}(\theta)\},~\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}}{\partial\alpha_{R}}=\Re\{\mathbf{a}(\theta)\},~\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}}{\partial\alpha_{I}}=\Re\{j\mathbf{a}(\theta)\}.

(15)

Noting that
\Re\{j\mathbf{a}\}=-\Im\{\mathbf{a}\}
, the above derivatives are all real-vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{N_{t}L}
, as required by the real Gaussian model.

With (
13
), the
(i,j)
th entry of the Fisher information matrix (FIM)
\mathbf{M}
is:
\mathbf{M}_{ij}=\left(\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}}{\partial\xi_{i}}\right)^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}}{\partial\xi_{j}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{R}_{e}}{\partial\xi_{i}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathbf{R}_{e}}{\partial\xi_{j}}\right\}
. Herein, since
\mathbf{R}_{e}\triangleq\mathbf{I}_{L}\otimes\mathbf{R}_{w}
is independent of
\bm{\xi}
, we have
\frac{\partial\mathbf{R}_{e}}{\partial\xi_{i}}=\mathbf{0}~(\forall i)
, and hence

\mathbf{M}_{ij}=\left(\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}}{\partial\xi_{i}}\right)^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}}{\partial\xi_{j}}\right).

(16)

For compactness, define
\mathbf{u}_{\theta}\triangleq\Re\{\alpha\dot{\mathbf{a}}\},~\mathbf{u}_{R}\triangleq\Re\{\mathbf{a}\},~\mathbf{u}_{I}\triangleq\Re\{j\mathbf{a}\}=-\Im\{\mathbf{a}\}
. Then the FIM can be written as

\mathbf{M}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}_{\theta}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{\theta}&\mathbf{u}_{\theta}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{R}&\mathbf{u}_{\theta}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{I}\\
\mathbf{u}_{R}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{\theta}&\mathbf{u}_{R}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{R}&\mathbf{u}_{R}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{I}\\
\mathbf{u}_{I}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{\theta}&\mathbf{u}_{I}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{R}&\mathbf{u}_{I}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{I}\end{bmatrix}.

(17)

Partition the FIM as
\mathbf{M}=\begin{bmatrix}M_{\theta\theta}&\mathbf{M}_{\theta\alpha}\\
\mathbf{M}_{\theta\alpha}^{\mathrm{T}}&\mathbf{M}_{\alpha\alpha}\end{bmatrix},~\bm{\alpha}\triangleq[\alpha_{R}~\alpha_{I}]^{\mathrm{T}}
, where
M_{\theta\theta}=\mathbf{u}_{\theta}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{\theta},~\mathbf{M}_{\theta\alpha}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}_{\theta}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{R}&\mathbf{u}_{\theta}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{I}\end{bmatrix},~\mathbf{M}_{\alpha\alpha}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}_{R}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{R}&\mathbf{u}_{R}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{I}\\
\mathbf{u}_{I}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{R}&\mathbf{u}_{I}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{I}\end{bmatrix}
. Therefore, the CRB of
\theta
is given by the Schur complement

\mathrm{CRB}_{\theta}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})=\left(M_{\theta\theta}-\mathbf{M}_{\theta\alpha}\mathbf{M}_{\alpha\alpha}^{-1}\mathbf{M}_{\theta\alpha}^{\mathrm{T}}\right)^{-1}.

(18)

For convenience, define

\displaystyle A\triangleq\mathbf{u}_{R}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{R},~B\triangleq\mathbf{u}_{I}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{I},~C\triangleq\mathbf{u}_{R}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{I},

(19)

\displaystyle p\triangleq\mathbf{u}_{\theta}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{R},~q\triangleq\mathbf{u}_{\theta}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{I},~r\triangleq\mathbf{u}_{\theta}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{\theta}.

Then
\mathbf{M}_{\alpha\alpha}=\begin{bmatrix}A&C\\
C&B\end{bmatrix}
and
\mathbf{M}_{\theta\alpha}=[p~q]
, and by substituting into (
18
), we obtain

\mathrm{CRB}_{\theta}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})=\left(r-\frac{Bp^{2}-2Cpq+Aq^{2}}{AB-C^{2}}\right)^{-1}.

(20)

To further express (
20
) in terms of
\mathbf{a}
and
\dot{\mathbf{a}}
, define

\displaystyle u\triangleq\mathbf{a}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{a},~\tilde{u}\triangleq\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{a},~t\triangleq\dot{\mathbf{a}}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{a},

(21)

\displaystyle\tilde{t}\triangleq\dot{\mathbf{a}}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\mathbf{a},~v\triangleq\dot{\mathbf{a}}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\dot{\mathbf{a}},~\tilde{v}\triangleq\dot{\mathbf{a}}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{e}^{-1}\dot{\mathbf{a}}.

By using
\mathbf{u}_{R}=\Re\{\mathbf{a}\}=\frac{\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{a}^{*}}{2},~\mathbf{u}_{I}=\Re\{j\mathbf{a}\}=-\Im\{\mathbf{a}\}=\frac{j(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{a}^{*})}{2},~\mathbf{u}_{\theta}=\Re\{\alpha\dot{\mathbf{a}}\}=\frac{\alpha\dot{\mathbf{a}}+\alpha^{*}\dot{\mathbf{a}}^{*}}{2}
, (
19
) becomes

\displaystyle

\displaystyle A=\frac{u+\Re\{\tilde{u}\}}{2},~B=\frac{u-\Re\{\tilde{u}\}}{2},~C=-\frac{\Im\{\tilde{u}\}}{2},

(22)

\displaystyle p=\frac{\Re\{\alpha^{*}t\}+\Re\{\alpha\tilde{t}\}}{2},~q=-\frac{\Im\{\alpha^{*}t\}+\Im\{\alpha\tilde{t}\}}{2},

\displaystyle r=\frac{|\alpha|^{2}v+\Re\{\alpha^{2}\tilde{v}\}}{2}.

In addition, using
\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{A})^{*}(\mathbf{I}_{L}\otimes\mathbf{B})\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{C})=\mathrm{tr}\{\mathbf{A}^{*}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{C}\}
, together with
\mathbf{R}_{e}=\mathbf{I}_{L}\otimes\mathbf{R}_{w}
and
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{*}=L\mathbf{I}_{K}
, we have

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{X}^{*}\mathbf{Q}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{X}\right\}

(23)

\displaystyle=L\mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{Q}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\right\},

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{X}^{*}\dot{\mathbf{Q}}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{X}\right\}

\displaystyle=L\mathrm{tr}\left\{\dot{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{Q}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\right\},

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{X}^{*}\dot{\mathbf{Q}}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\dot{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{X}\right\}

\displaystyle=L\mathrm{tr}\left\{\dot{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}\dot{\mathbf{Q}}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\right\},

and since
\{s_{i}\}
are unit-power proper signal:
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{T}}]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{s}[\ell]\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{T}}[\ell]]=L\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{s}\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{T}}]=\mathbf{0}
; for sufficiently large
L
,
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{T}}\approx\mathbf{0}
holds, which leads to

\displaystyle\tilde{u}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{X}\right\}

(24)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{T}}{\mathbf{W}}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{Q}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\right\}=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{t}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\dot{\mathbf{Q}}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{X}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{T}}{\mathbf{W}}^{\mathrm{T}}\dot{\mathbf{Q}}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\right\}=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{v}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\dot{\mathbf{Q}}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\dot{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{X}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left\{\dot{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{S}^{\mathrm{T}}{\mathbf{W}}^{\mathrm{T}}\dot{\mathbf{Q}}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\right\}=0.

Substituting
\tilde{u}=\tilde{t}=\tilde{v}=0
into (
22
), we obtain

\displaystyle A=\frac{u}{2},~B=\frac{u}{2},~C=0,

(25)

\displaystyle p=\frac{\Re\{\alpha^{*}t\}}{2},~q=-\frac{\Im\{\alpha^{*}t\}}{2},~r=\frac{|\alpha|^{2}v}{2}.

Accordingly, the denominator term in (
20
) becomes
AB-C^{2}=\frac{u^{2}}{4}
, while the numerator is simplified as

\displaystyle Bp^{2}-2Cpq+Aq^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{u}{2}\left(\frac{\Re\{\alpha^{*}t\}}{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{u}{2}\left(\frac{\Im\{\alpha^{*}t\}}{2}\right)^{2}

(26)

\displaystyle=\frac{u}{8}\left(\Re^{2}\{\alpha^{*}t\}+\Im^{2}\{\alpha^{*}t\}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{u}{8}|\alpha^{*}t|^{2}=\frac{u}{8}|\alpha|^{2}|t|^{2}.

Therefore,

\frac{Bp^{2}-2Cpq+Aq^{2}}{AB-C^{2}}=\frac{\frac{u}{8}|\alpha|^{2}|t|^{2}}{\frac{u^{2}}{4}}=\frac{|\alpha|^{2}|t|^{2}}{2u}.

(27)

Substituting (
25
) and (
27
) into (
20
) yields

\mathrm{CRB}_{\theta}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})=\frac{2}{|\alpha|^{2}}\left(v-\frac{|t|^{2}}{u}\right)^{-1}.

(28)

Finally, by substituting (
23
) into (
28
),
\mathrm{CRB}_{\theta}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})
becomes

\displaystyle\mathrm{CRB}_{\theta}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{L|\alpha|^{2}}\Big(\mathrm{tr}\Big\{\dot{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}\dot{\mathbf{Q}}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\Big\}

(29)

\displaystyle-\frac{\left|\mathrm{tr}\left\{\dot{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{Q}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\right\}\right|^{2}}{\mathrm{tr}\left\{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{Q}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\right\}}\Big)^{-1}.

III
Problem Formulation

III-A

SINR of RARE

Define the LO-phase-aligned effective channel
\mathbf{E}_{k}(\bm{\Phi})\triangleq\mathbf{D}_{b,k}\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{eff},k}(\bm{\Phi})\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N_{t}}
. Then (
5
) can be rewritten as

{\mathbf{z}}_{k}=\Re\left\{\mathbf{E}_{k}(\bm{\Phi})\mathbf{W}\mathbf{s}\right\}+\tilde{\mathbf{n}}_{k}+\bm{\eta}_{k},

(30)

where
\tilde{\mathbf{n}}_{k}\triangleq\Re\left\{\mathbf{D}_{b,k}\mathbf{H}_{RU,k}\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{z}_{0}\right\}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
. Define the effective field vectors
\mathbf{g}_{k,i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\triangleq\mathbf{E}_{k}(\bm{\Phi})\mathbf{w}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{M}~(i=1,\cdots,K)
, and stack them as
\mathbf{G}_{k}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\triangleq\mathbf{E}_{k}(\bm{\Phi})\mathbf{W}=\big[\mathbf{g}_{k,1}\cdots\mathbf{g}_{k,K}\big]\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times K}
. Hence, the observation becomes

{\mathbf{z}}_{k}=\Re\left\{\mathbf{g}_{k,k}s_{k}\right\}+\sum_{i\neq k}\Re\left\{\mathbf{g}_{k,i}s_{i}\right\}+\tilde{\mathbf{n}}_{k}+\bm{\eta}_{k}.

(31)

Since
\{s_{i}\}
are unit-power proper signal:
\mathbb{E}\left\{\big\|\Re\left\{\mathbf{g}_{k,k}s_{k}\right\}\big\|_{2}^{2}\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{g}_{k,k}\|_{2}^{2}
. Moreover, since
\mathbf{z}_{0}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\sigma_{z}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{N})
is circularly symmetric,
\tilde{\mathbf{n}}_{k}
satisfies
\mathbb{E}\left\{\big\|\tilde{\mathbf{n}}_{k}\big\|_{2}^{2}\right\}=\frac{\sigma_{z}^{2}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{D}_{b,k}\mathbf{H}_{RU,k}\bm{\Phi}\right\|_{F}^{2}
, and the readout noise contributes
\mathbb{E}\left\{\big\|\bm{\eta}_{k}\big\|_{2}^{2}\right\}=\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{\sigma_{q,k}^{2}}{2}\mathbf{I}_{M}\right)=\frac{M\sigma_{q,k}^{2}}{2}
. Motivated by these, we define an SINR that is consistent with the real-part detection model of RARE as

\mathrm{SINR}_{k}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\triangleq\frac{\|\mathbf{g}_{k,k}\|_{2}^{2}}{\sum_{i\neq k}\|\mathbf{g}_{k,i}\|_{2}^{2}+\sigma_{z}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{D}_{b,k}\mathbf{H}_{RU,k}\bm{\Phi}\right\|_{F}^{2}+M\sigma_{q,k}^{2}}.

(32)

III-B

ARIS Power Consumption Model

The ARIS power consumption consists of four parts: i) the amplified incident BS signal power, ii) the target-related re-amplified echo component, iii) the ARIS noise coupled through the target-related loop, and iv) the intrinsic amplifier-noise power, which can be respectively modeled as

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{RIS}}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})

(33)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bm{\Phi}(\mathbf{H}_{BR}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{z}_{0})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bm{\Phi}\big(\alpha\mathbf{h}_{r,t}\mathbf{h}_{r,t}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\Phi}(\mathbf{H}_{BR}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{z}_{0})+\mathbf{z}_{1}\big)\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\|\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{H}_{BR}\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}+\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}\left\|\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{h}_{r,t}(\theta)\mathbf{h}_{r,t}^{\mathrm{T}}(\theta)\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{H}_{BR}\mathbf{W}\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}\sigma_{z}^{2}\left\|\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{h}_{r,t}(\theta)\mathbf{h}_{r,t}^{\mathrm{T}}(\theta)\bm{\Phi}\right\|_{F}^{2}+2\sigma_{z}^{2}\|\bm{\Phi}\|_{F}^{2},

where
\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}\triangleq\mathbb{E}[|\alpha|^{2}]
denotes the power of the target reflection coefficient.

In addition, let
P_{c}
and
P_{\mathrm{DC}}
denote the circuit power consumption
[
45
]
: i)
P_{c}
, representing the logical control and switching power required for each element, and ii)
P_{\mathrm{DC}}
, representing the DC bias power required to support active reflection. Then, with
\xi_{e}=\frac{1}{v}
with efficiency
v\in(0,1)
, the total ARIS power consumption is expressed as
NP_{c}+NP_{\mathrm{DC}}+\xi_{e}P_{\mathrm{RIS}}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})
, constrained by the budget
P_{\max,a,t}
. Therefore, the constraint with respect to
P_{\mathrm{RIS}}
is

P_{\mathrm{RIS}}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\leq v(P_{\max,a,t}-NP_{c}-NP_{\mathrm{DC}})\triangleq P_{\max,a}.

(34)

III-C

CRB-Minimization Problem

We aim to minimize
\mathrm{CRB}_{\theta}
in (
29
) subject to the power budgets, user SINR, and ARIS amplitude constraints:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi}}~\mathrm{CRB}_{\theta}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})

(35)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*})\leq P_{\max},~P_{\mathrm{RIS}}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\leq P_{\max,a},

\displaystyle\mathrm{SINR}_{k}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\geq\gamma_{k}~(\forall k),~0<a_{n}\leq a_{\max}~(\forall n).

Problem (
35
) is highly non-convex due to the fractional CRB structure, the coupled SINR constraints, and the high-order dependence of the ARIS power on
(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})
. To address this challenge, we develop an AO framework that iteratively optimizes
\mathbf{W}
and
\bm{\Phi}
.

IV
Proposed Framework

IV-A

Design of
\mathbf{W}
for fixed
\bm{\Phi}

For any fixed
\bm{\Phi}
, we first reformulate the objective in an equivalent maximization form with respect to
\mathbf{W}
:

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{W})\triangleq

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\left(\dot{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}\dot{\mathbf{Q}}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\right)

(36)

\displaystyle-\frac{\left|\mathrm{tr}\left(\dot{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{Q}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\right)\right|^{2}}{\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}\mathbf{Q}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}\right)}.

To facilitate tractable optimization, we introduce the beamforming covariance matrices
\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\triangleq\mathbf{w}_{k}\mathbf{w}_{k}^{*}\}_{k=1}^{K}
, and define
\mathbf{R}_{W}\triangleq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{W}_{k}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}
. Then for
\mathbf{H}_{k}\triangleq\mathbf{E}_{k}^{*}\mathbf{E}_{k}
, the SINR constraint of user
k
in (
35
) becomes

\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k})\geq\gamma_{k}\left(\sum_{i\neq k}\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{i})+\sigma_{k}^{2}(\bm{\Phi})\right),

(37)

where
\sigma_{k}^{2}(\bm{\Phi})\triangleq\sigma_{q,k}^{2}M+\sigma_{z}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{D}_{b,k}\mathbf{H}_{RU,k}\bm{\Phi}\right\|_{F}^{2}
is the equivalent noise power. The transmit power constraint in (
35
) becomes
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}_{k})\leq P_{\max}
. Next, we introduce an auxiliary variable
t_{W}
and rewrite the objective in epigraph form as
f(\mathbf{W})\geq t_{W}
. The ARIS power constraint in (
35
) can be rewritten as

\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}\mathbf{R}_{W})+c_{\Phi}\leq P_{\max,a},

(38)

where
\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}\triangleq\mathbf{H}_{BR}^{*}\bm{\Phi}^{*}\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{H}_{BR}+\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}\mathbf{H}_{BR}^{*}\bm{\Phi}^{*}(\mathbf{h}_{r,t}\mathbf{h}_{r,t}^{\mathrm{T}})^{*}\bm{\Phi}^{*}\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{h}_{r,t}\mathbf{h}_{r,t}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{H}_{BR}
and
c_{\Phi}\triangleq\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}\sigma_{z}^{2}\left\|\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{h}_{r,t}\mathbf{h}_{r,t}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\Phi}\right\|_{F}^{2}+2\sigma_{z}^{2}\|\bm{\Phi}\|_{F}^{2}
. Therefore, the
\mathbf{W}
-subproblem can be formulated as

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\max_{\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\},t_{W}}~t_{W}

(39)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{W})\geq t_{W},~\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}_{k})\leq P_{\max},

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:sinr_linear},~\eqref{eq:aris_power_w},~\mathbf{W}_{k}\succeq\mathbf{0},~\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{W}_{k})=1~(\forall k).

Problem (
39
) is still non-convex due to the rank-one constraints and the fractional structure in
f(\mathbf{W})
. In the following, we apply SDR to drop the rank-one constraints and transform the fractional constraint into a linear matrix inequality (LMI) using the Schur complement, resulting in a convex SDP that can be efficiently solved
[
46
,
4
]
.

We first reformulate
f(\mathbf{W})\geq t_{W}
into an equivalent LMI. For convenience, define
A(\mathbf{W})\triangleq\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{A}_{\Phi}\mathbf{R}_{W}),~B(\mathbf{W})\triangleq\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{B}_{\Phi}\mathbf{R}_{W}),~C(\mathbf{W})\triangleq\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{C}_{\Phi}\mathbf{R}_{W})
, where

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{\Phi}\triangleq\dot{\mathbf{Q}}^{*}(\bm{\Phi},\theta)\mathbf{M}\dot{\mathbf{Q}}(\bm{\Phi},\theta),~\mathbf{B}_{\Phi}\triangleq\mathbf{Q}^{*}(\bm{\Phi},\theta)\mathbf{M}\dot{\mathbf{Q}}(\bm{\Phi},\theta),

(40)

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{\Phi}\triangleq\mathbf{Q}^{*}(\bm{\Phi},\theta)\mathbf{M}\mathbf{Q}(\bm{\Phi},\theta),

with
\mathbf{M}\triangleq\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}
. Then,
f(\mathbf{W})\geq t_{W}
can be equivalently written as

A(\mathbf{W})-\frac{|B(\mathbf{W})|^{2}}{C(\mathbf{W})}\geq t_{W}\leftrightarrow|B(\mathbf{W})|^{2}\leq(A(\mathbf{W})-t_{W})C(\mathbf{W}),

(41)

provided that
C(\mathbf{W})>0
. By applying the Schur complement, (
41
) is equivalently expressed as the following LMI:
\begin{bmatrix}C(\mathbf{W})&B(\mathbf{W})\\
B(\mathbf{W})^{*}&A(\mathbf{W})-t_{W}\end{bmatrix}\succeq\mathbf{0}
. Consequently, by dropping the non-convex rank-one constraints
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{W}_{k})=1
, the
\mathbf{W}
-subproblem can be relaxed into:

\displaystyle\max_{\{\mathbf{W}_{k}\},t_{W}}~t_{W}

(42)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}C(\mathbf{W})&B(\mathbf{W})\\
B(\mathbf{W})^{*}&A(\mathbf{W})-t_{W}\end{bmatrix}\succeq\mathbf{0},~\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}_{k})\leq P_{\max},

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{E}_{\Phi}\mathbf{R}_{W})+c_{\Phi}\leq P_{\max,a},~\mathbf{W}_{k}\succeq\mathbf{0}~(\forall k),

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{k})\geq\gamma_{k}\left(\sum_{i\neq k}\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{W}_{i})+\sigma_{k}^{2}(\bm{\Phi})\right)~(\forall k),

which is a convex SDP and can be efficiently solved using CVX
[
4
]
. Let
\{\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\star}\}
denote the optimal solution. For
k
with
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\star})=1
, the corresponding beamforming vectors can be directly obtained via eigenvalue decomposition as
\mathbf{w}_{k}^{\star}=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\star})}\mathbf{u}_{\max}(\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\star})

[
68
]
, where
\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\star})
denotes the maximum eigenvalue of
\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\star}
, and
\mathbf{u}_{\max}(\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\star})
is the corresponding unit-norm eigenvector. Otherwise, a feasible rank-one solution can be constructed from corresponding
\{\mathbf{W}_{k}^{\star}\}
using Gaussian randomization
[
46
]
.

IV-B

Design of
\bm{\Phi}
for Fixed
\mathbf{W}

For fixed
\mathbf{W}
, the optimization variable is the ARIS coefficient vector
\bm{\phi}\triangleq[\phi_{1}\cdots\phi_{N}]^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N},~\bm{\Phi}=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\phi})
. Recalling (
28
)-(
29
), minimizing
\mathrm{CRB}_{\theta}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})
is equivalent to maximizing
g(\bm{\phi})\triangleq v(\bm{\phi})-\frac{|t(\bm{\phi})|^{2}}{u(\bm{\phi})}
subject to the constraints.

IV-B
1
Lifted Representation of the Objective

Let
\mathbf{h}\triangleq\mathbf{h}_{r,t}(\theta),~\dot{\mathbf{h}}\triangleq\frac{\partial\mathbf{h}_{r,t}(\theta)}{\partial\theta}
, and define
\mathbf{B}_{0}\triangleq\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{h})\mathbf{H}_{BR}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N_{t}},~\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_{0}\triangleq\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{h})\mathbf{H}_{RB,r}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N_{t}}~\mathbf{B}_{1}\triangleq\mathrm{diag}(\dot{\mathbf{h}})\mathbf{H}_{BR}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N_{t}},~\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_{1}\triangleq\mathrm{diag}(\dot{\mathbf{h}})\mathbf{H}_{RB,r}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N_{t}}
. Then,
\mathbf{Q}(\bm{\Phi},\theta)
can be written as

\mathbf{Q}(\bm{\Phi},\theta)=\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\phi}\bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}_{0},

(43)

and its derivative with respect to
\theta
is

\dot{\mathbf{Q}}(\bm{\Phi},\theta)=\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_{1}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\phi}\bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}_{0}+\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\phi}\bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{B}_{1}.

(44)

Now define the lifted variable
\mathbf{x}\triangleq\mathrm{vec}(\bm{\phi}\bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{T}})=\bm{\phi}\otimes\bm{\phi}\in\mathbb{C}^{N^{2}}
, and let
\mathbf{M}_{r}\triangleq\mathbf{D}_{b,r}^{*}\mathbf{R}_{w}^{-1}\mathbf{D}_{b,r}
.
Then there exist
\mathbf{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{N^{2}\times N^{2}}
and positive definite matrices
\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{C}^{N^{2}\times N^{2}}
such that

u(\bm{\phi})=\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{x},~t(\bm{\phi})=\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{T}\mathbf{x},~v(\bm{\phi})=\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{V}\mathbf{x},

(45)

where
\mathbf{U}\triangleq L\mathbf{G}_{0}^{*}(\mathbf{M}_{r}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{R}_{W})\mathbf{G}_{0},~\mathbf{T}\triangleq L\mathbf{G}_{1}^{*}(\mathbf{M}_{r}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{R}_{W})\mathbf{G}_{0},~\mathbf{V}\triangleq L\mathbf{G}_{1}^{*}(\mathbf{M}_{r}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{R}_{W})\mathbf{G}_{1}
with
\mathbf{G}_{0}\triangleq{\mathbf{B}}_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_{0}^{\mathrm{T}},~\mathbf{G}_{1}\triangleq{\mathbf{B}}_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_{1}^{\mathrm{T}}+{\mathbf{B}}_{1}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\tilde{\mathbf{B}}_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}
. The derivation is given in Appendix
A
. Hence,
g(\bm{\phi})
can be equivalently written as

g(\bm{\phi})=\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{V}\mathbf{x}-\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{T}\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{x}}.

(46)

IV-B
2
Quadratic Transform of the Objective

Since
u>0
, the fractional term in (
46
) can be handled via the quadratic transform
[
56
]
:
-\frac{|t|^{2}}{u}=\min_{y\in\mathbb{C}}\left(u|y|^{2}-2\Re\{y^{*}t\}\right)
, whose minimizer is
y^{\star}=\frac{t}{u}
. Therefore, maximizing (
46
) is equivalent to maximizing

\min_{y\in\mathbb{C}}~\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{V}\mathbf{x}+|y|^{2}\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{x}-2\Re\left\{y^{*}\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{T}\mathbf{x}\right\}

(47)

with respect to
\bm{\phi}
satisfying the constraints. At the
s
th inner iteration, for fixed
\bm{\phi}^{(s)}
, the auxiliary scalar
y
is updated as:
y^{(s)}=\frac{\mathbf{x}^{(s)*}\mathbf{T}\mathbf{x}^{(s)}}{\mathbf{x}^{(s)*}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{x}^{(s)}},~\mathbf{x}^{(s)}=\bm{\phi}^{(s)}\otimes\bm{\phi}^{(s)}
. Then, with
y^{(s)}
fixed, the
\bm{\phi}
-update reduces to maximizing
f_{y}(\bm{\phi})\triangleq\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{y}^{(s)}\mathbf{x}
by (
47
), where
\mathbf{C}_{y}^{(s)}\triangleq\mathbf{V}+|y^{(s)}|^{2}\mathbf{U}-y^{(s)*}\mathbf{T}-y^{(s)}\mathbf{T}^{*}
.

IV-B
3
MM Surrogate of the Objective

At the
s
th MM iteration, let
\bm{\phi}^{(s)}
be the current feasible point and define
\mathbf{x}^{(s)}=\bm{\phi}^{(s)}\otimes\bm{\phi}^{(s)}
. For fixed
y^{(s)}
, maximizing
f_{y}(\bm{\phi})
is equivalent to minimizing
-\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{y}^{(s)}\mathbf{x}
. Define the Hermitian part
\mathbf{H}_{y}^{(s)}\triangleq\frac{\mathbf{C}_{y}^{(s)}+\mathbf{C}_{y}^{(s)*}}{2}
, so that
\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{y}^{(s)}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{H}_{y}^{(s)}\mathbf{x}
. Choose any constant
\lambda_{y}^{(s)}\geq\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{H}_{y}^{(s)})
. Then, by the standard quadratic majorization
[
58
,
18
]
,

\displaystyle-\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{H}_{y}^{(s)}\mathbf{x}\leq-\mathbf{x}^{(s)*}\mathbf{H}_{y}^{(s)}\mathbf{x}^{(s)}-2\Re\left\{\mathbf{x}^{(s)*}\mathbf{H}_{y}^{(s)}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{(s)})\right\}

(48)

\displaystyle+\lambda_{y}^{(s)}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{(s)}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow-\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{H}_{y}^{(s)}\mathbf{x}\leq\lambda_{y}^{(s)}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}-2\Re\left\{\mathbf{x}^{(s)*}\big(\mathbf{H}_{y}^{(s)}+\lambda_{y}^{(s)}\mathbf{I}\big)\mathbf{x}\right\}+c_{y}^{(s)},

where
c_{y}^{(s)}\triangleq\mathbf{x}^{(s)*}\mathbf{H}_{y}^{(s)}\mathbf{x}^{(s)}+\lambda_{y}^{(s)}\|\mathbf{x}^{(s)}\|_{2}^{2}
is independent of
\bm{\phi}
. Now define
\mathbf{q}_{y}^{(s)}\triangleq\big(\mathbf{H}_{y}^{(s)}+\lambda_{y}^{(s)}\mathbf{I}\big)\mathbf{x}^{(s)},~\mathbf{Q}_{y}^{(s)}\triangleq\mathrm{unvec}_{N\times N}\big(\mathbf{q}_{y}^{(s)}\big)
, where
\mathrm{unvec}_{N\times N}(\cdot)
reshapes a vector into an
N\times N
matrix. Then
\mathbf{q}_{y}^{(s)*}\mathbf{x}=\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{Q}_{y}^{(s)})^{*}\mathrm{vec}(\bm{\phi}\bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{T}})=\bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{Q}_{y}^{(s)*}\bm{\phi}
, and moreover,
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\bm{\phi}\otimes\bm{\phi}\|_{2}^{2}=(\bm{\phi}^{*}\bm{\phi})^{2}\leq Na_{\max}^{2}\bm{\phi}^{*}\bm{\phi}
. Substituting them into (
48
), we obtain

-\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{y}^{(s)}\mathbf{x}\leq\lambda_{y}^{(s)}Na_{\max}^{2}\bm{\phi}^{*}\bm{\phi}-2\Re\left\{\bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{Q}_{y}^{(s)*}\bm{\phi}\right\}+c_{y}^{(s)}.

(49)

The remaining term
\Re\{\bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{Q}_{y}^{(s)*}\bm{\phi}\}
in (
49
) is still non-convex in general. To handle it, introduce the real-valued embedding
\bar{\bm{\phi}}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}\Re\{\bm{\phi}\}\\
\Im\{\bm{\phi}\}\end{bmatrix},~\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{y}^{(s)}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}\Re\{\mathbf{Q}_{y}^{(s)}\}&-\Im\{\mathbf{Q}_{y}^{(s)}\}\\
\Im\{\mathbf{Q}_{y}^{(s)}\}&\Re\{\mathbf{Q}_{y}^{(s)}\}\end{bmatrix}
. Then
\Re\left\{\bm{\phi}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{Q}_{y}^{(s)*}\bm{\phi}\right\}=\bar{\bm{\phi}}^{\mathrm{T}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{y}^{(s)}\bar{\bm{\phi}}
. Choose any
\mu_{y}^{(s)}\geq\lambda_{\max}\big(\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{y}^{(s)}+\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{y}^{(s)\mathrm{T}}\big)
. Then, by the second-order upper-bound:

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{\phi}}^{\mathrm{T}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{y}^{(s)}\bar{\bm{\phi}}\leq

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{\phi}}^{(s)\mathrm{T}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{y}^{(s)}\bar{\bm{\phi}}^{(s)}+\bar{\bm{\phi}}^{(s)\mathrm{T}}\big(\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{y}^{(s)}+\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{y}^{(s)\mathrm{T}}\big)(\bar{\bm{\phi}}-\bar{\bm{\phi}}^{(s)})

(50)

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu_{y}^{(s)}}{2}\|\bar{\bm{\phi}}-\bar{\bm{\phi}}^{(s)}\|_{2}^{2}.

Substituting (
50
) into (
49
), we obtain

-\mathbf{x}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{y}^{(s)}\mathbf{x}\leq\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{A}_{y}^{(s)}\bm{\phi}+\Re\left\{\mathbf{b}_{y}^{(s)*}\bm{\phi}\right\}+c_{y,0}^{(s)},

(51)

where
\mathbf{A}_{y}^{(s)}\triangleq\left(\lambda_{y}^{(s)}Na_{\max}^{2}+\mu_{y}^{(s)}\right)\mathbf{I}_{N},~\mathbf{b}_{y}^{(s)}\triangleq-2\left(\bm{\ell}_{y,\mathrm{R}}^{(s)}+j\bm{\ell}_{y,\mathrm{I}}^{(s)}\right),~c_{y,0}^{(s)}\triangleq c_{y}^{(s)}+2\bar{\bm{\phi}}^{(s)\mathrm{T}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{y}^{(s)}\bar{\bm{\phi}}^{(s)}-\mu_{y}^{(s)}\|\bar{\bm{\phi}}^{(s)}\|_{2}^{2}
, with
\bm{\ell}_{y}^{(s)}\triangleq\left(\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{y}^{(s)}+\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{y}^{(s)\mathrm{T}}-\mu_{y}^{(s)}\mathbf{I}_{2N}\right)\bar{\bm{\phi}}^{(s)}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\ell}_{y,\mathrm{R}}^{(s)}\\
\bm{\ell}_{y,\mathrm{I}}^{(s)}\end{bmatrix}
. Herein,
\bm{\ell}_{y,\mathrm{R}}^{(s)}
and
\bm{\ell}_{y,\mathrm{I}}^{(s)}
denote the first and last
N
entries of
\bm{\ell}_{y}^{(s)}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}
, respectively.

IV-B
4
Reformulation of the Constraints

We next reformulate the constraints in terms of
\bm{\phi}
.

1) SINR Constraints

For fixed
\mathbf{W}
, define
\mathbf{c}_{k,i}\triangleq\mathbf{D}_{b,k}\mathbf{H}_{BU,k}\mathbf{w}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{M},~\mathbf{F}_{k,i}\triangleq\mathbf{D}_{b,k}\mathbf{H}_{RU,k}\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{H}_{BR}\mathbf{w}_{i})\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N}
. Then
\mathbf{g}_{k,i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})=\mathbf{E}_{k}(\bm{\Phi})\mathbf{w}_{i}=\mathbf{c}_{k,i}+\mathbf{F}_{k,i}\bm{\phi}
, which leads to
\|\mathbf{g}_{k,i}\|_{2}^{2}=\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{A}_{k,i}\bm{\phi}+2\Re\{\mathbf{b}_{k,i}^{*}\bm{\phi}\}+c_{k,i}
, where
\mathbf{A}_{k,i}\triangleq\mathbf{F}_{k,i}^{*}\mathbf{F}_{k,i},~\mathbf{b}_{k,i}\triangleq\mathbf{F}_{k,i}^{*}\mathbf{c}_{k,i},~c_{k,i}\triangleq\|\mathbf{c}_{k,i}\|_{2}^{2}
. Similarly,
\left\|\mathbf{D}_{b,k}\mathbf{H}_{RU,k}\bm{\Phi}\right\|_{F}^{2}=\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{Z}_{k}\bm{\phi},~\mathbf{Z}_{k}\triangleq\mathrm{diag}\left(\mathrm{diag}\big(\mathbf{H}_{RU,k}^{*}\mathbf{H}_{RU,k}\big)\right)
. Hence, the SINR constraint is equivalent to

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}^{*}\Big(\sum_{i\neq k}\mathbf{A}_{k,i}+\sigma_{z}^{2}\mathbf{Z}_{k}-\gamma_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{A}_{k,k}\Big)\bm{\phi}

(52)

\displaystyle+2\Re\left\{\Big(\sum_{i\neq k}\mathbf{b}_{k,i}-\gamma_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{b}_{k,k}\Big)^{*}\bm{\phi}\right\}+\sum_{i\neq k}c_{k,i}

\displaystyle-\gamma_{k}^{-1}c_{k,k}+M\sigma_{q,k}^{2}\leq 0.

For compactness, define
\mathbf{C}_{k}\triangleq\sum_{i\neq k}\mathbf{A}_{k,i}+\sigma_{z}^{2}\mathbf{Z}_{k}-\gamma_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{A}_{k,k},~\mathbf{d}_{k}\triangleq 2\left(\sum_{i\neq k}\mathbf{b}_{k,i}-\gamma_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{b}_{k,k}\right),~e_{k}\triangleq\sum_{i\neq k}c_{k,i}-\gamma_{k}^{-1}c_{k,k}+M\sigma_{q,k}^{2}
. Then (
52
) becomes

\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{k}\bm{\phi}+\Re\{\mathbf{d}_{k}^{*}\bm{\phi}\}+e_{k}\leq 0~(\forall k).

(53)

However,
\mathbf{C}_{k}
is not guaranteed to be positive semidefinite because of
-\gamma_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{A}_{k,k}
. Hence, (
53
) is generally non-convex. To expose its structure, we decompose
\mathbf{C}_{k}
as
\mathbf{C}_{k}=\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(+)}-\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(-)}
, where
\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(+)}\triangleq\sum_{i\neq k}\mathbf{A}_{k,i}+\sigma_{z}^{2}\mathbf{Z}_{k}\succeq\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(-)}\triangleq\gamma_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{A}_{k,k}\succeq\mathbf{0}
. Substituting it into (
53
) yields

\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(+)}\bm{\phi}-\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(-)}\bm{\phi}+\Re\{\mathbf{d}_{k}^{*}\bm{\phi}\}+e_{k}\leq 0.

(54)

Now, since
\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(-)}\succeq\mathbf{0}
, the quadratic function
f_{k}(\bm{\phi})\triangleq\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(-)}\bm{\phi}
is convex in
\bm{\phi}
. Therefore, its negative
-f_{k}(\bm{\phi})
is concave, and can be globally upper-bounded by its first-order expansion at
\bm{\phi}^{(s)}
:

\displaystyle-\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(-)}\bm{\phi}

\displaystyle\leq-\bm{\phi}^{(s)*}\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(-)}\bm{\phi}^{(s)}-2\Re\left\{\bm{\phi}^{(s)*}\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(-)}(\bm{\phi}-\bm{\phi}^{(s)})\right\}

(55)

\displaystyle=-2\Re\left\{\bm{\phi}^{(s)*}\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(-)}\bm{\phi}\right\}+\bm{\phi}^{(s)*}\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(-)}\bm{\phi}^{(s)}.

Substituting (
55
) into (
54
), we obtain

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(+)}\bm{\phi}+\Re\{\mathbf{d}_{k}^{*}\bm{\phi}\}

(56)

\displaystyle-2\Re\left\{\bm{\phi}^{(s)*}\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(-)}\bm{\phi}\right\}+\bm{\phi}^{(s)*}\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(-)}\bm{\phi}^{(s)}+e_{k}\leq 0.

Thus, defining
\tilde{\mathbf{d}}_{k}^{(s)}\triangleq\mathbf{d}_{k}-2\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(-)}\bm{\phi}^{(s)}
and
\tilde{e}_{k}^{(s)}\triangleq e_{k}+\bm{\phi}^{(s)*}\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(-)}\bm{\phi}^{(s)}
, the convex surrogate of the
k
th SINR constraint, modified from (
53
), is finally written as

\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{k}^{(+)}\bm{\phi}+\Re\{\tilde{\mathbf{d}}_{k}^{(s)*}\bm{\phi}\}+\tilde{e}_{k}^{(s)}\leq 0~(\forall k).

(57)

2) ARIS Power Constraint

Let
\mathbf{H}_{w}\triangleq\mathbf{H}_{BR}\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times K},~\mathbf{p}\triangleq\bm{\phi}\odot\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Then the first term of (
33
) becomes

\|\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{H}_{BR}\mathbf{W}\|_{F}^{2}=\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{K}_{1}\bm{\phi},~\mathbf{K}_{1}\triangleq\mathrm{diag}\big(\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{H}_{w}\mathbf{H}_{w}^{*})\big).

(58)

Next, since
\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{H}_{BR}\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{H}_{w}
, we have the second term:
\left\|\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{H}_{BR}\mathbf{W}\right\|_{F}^{2}=\|\mathbf{p}\|_{2}^{2}\mathbf{p}^{*}\mathbf{H}_{w}\mathbf{H}_{w}^{*}\mathbf{p}
. Herein, since
\mathbf{p}=\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{h})\bm{\phi}
, define
\mathbf{J}\triangleq\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{h})^{*}\mathbf{H}_{w}\mathbf{H}_{w}^{*}\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{h}),~\mathbf{D}_{h}\triangleq\mathrm{diag}(|h_{1}|^{2},\cdots,|h_{N}|^{2})
, then

\left\|\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{H}_{BR}\mathbf{W}\right\|_{F}^{2}=(\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{h}\bm{\phi})(\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{J}\bm{\phi}).

(59)

Likewise the third term becomes
\left\|\bm{\Phi}\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{\Phi}\right\|_{F}^{2}=(\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{h}\bm{\phi})^{2}
, and for the fourth term:
\|\bm{\Phi}\|_{F}^{2}=\bm{\phi}^{*}\bm{\phi}
. Substituting all into (
33
), the power constraint becomes

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{K}_{1}\bm{\phi}+\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}(\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{h}\bm{\phi})(\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{J}\bm{\phi})

(60)

\displaystyle+\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}\sigma_{z}^{2}(\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{h}\bm{\phi})^{2}+2\sigma_{z}^{2}\bm{\phi}^{*}\bm{\phi}\leq P_{\max,a}.

We consider the quartic part of (
60
), which breaks the convexity, given by
p_{4}(\bm{\phi})\triangleq\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}(\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{h}\bm{\phi})(\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{J}\bm{\phi})+\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}\sigma_{z}^{2}(\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{h}\bm{\phi})^{2}
. Define the lifted matrix and vector
\mathbf{X}_{p}\triangleq\bm{\phi}\bm{\phi}^{*},~\mathbf{y}\triangleq\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{X}_{p})=\overline{\bm{\phi}}\otimes\bm{\phi}
, and let
\mathbf{d}\triangleq\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{D}_{h}),~\mathbf{j}\triangleq\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{J})
. Then, using
\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}_{p})=\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{A})^{*}\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{X}_{p})
, we obtain
\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{D}_{h}\bm{\phi}=\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{D}_{h}\mathbf{X}_{p})=\mathbf{d}^{*}\mathbf{y}
and
\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{J}\bm{\phi}=\mathbf{j}^{*}\mathbf{y}
. Since
\mathbf{D}_{h}
and
\mathbf{J}
are Hermitian and
\mathbf{X}_{p}=\bm{\phi}\bm{\phi}^{*}
is Hermitian and positive semidefinite,
\mathbf{d}^{*}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{j}^{*}\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}
holds. Therefore,

(\mathbf{d}^{*}\mathbf{y})(\mathbf{j}^{*}\mathbf{y})=\mathbf{y}^{*}\left(\frac{\mathbf{d}\mathbf{j}^{*}+\mathbf{j}\mathbf{d}^{*}}{2}\right)\mathbf{y},~(\mathbf{d}^{*}\mathbf{y})^{2}=\mathbf{y}^{*}(\mathbf{d}\mathbf{d}^{*})\mathbf{y}.

(61)

Substituting (
61
) into
p_{4}(\bm{\phi})
, we obtain the quadratic form:
p_{4}(\bm{\phi})=\mathbf{y}^{*}\mathbf{M}_{p}\mathbf{y}
, where
\mathbf{M}_{p}\triangleq\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}\frac{\mathbf{d}\mathbf{j}^{*}+\mathbf{j}\mathbf{d}^{*}}{2}+\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}\sigma_{z}^{2}\mathbf{d}\mathbf{d}^{*}
, and it is readily verified that
\mathbf{M}_{p}
is Hermitian.

Now define for
\bm{\phi}^{(s)}
:
\mathbf{y}^{(s)}=\mathrm{vec}(\bm{\phi}^{(s)}\bm{\phi}^{(s)*})
. Choose any positive scalar
\lambda_{p}^{(s)}
satisfying
\lambda_{p}^{(s)}\geq\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{M}_{p})
. Then the following holds with standard quadratic majorization:

\mathbf{y}^{*}\mathbf{M}_{p}\mathbf{y}\leq\lambda_{p}^{(s)}\|\mathbf{y}\|_{2}^{2}+2\Re\left\{\mathbf{y}^{*}(\mathbf{M}_{p}-\lambda_{p}^{(s)}\mathbf{I})\mathbf{y}^{(s)}\right\}+c_{p}^{(s)},

(62)

where
c_{p}^{(s)}\triangleq\mathbf{y}^{(s)*}(\lambda_{p}^{(s)}\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{M}_{p})\mathbf{y}^{(s)}
is constant with respect to
\bm{\phi}
.

Now define
\mathbf{q}_{p}^{(s)}\triangleq(\mathbf{M}_{p}-\lambda_{p}^{(s)}\mathbf{I})\mathbf{y}^{(s)}
and
\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{(s)}\triangleq\mathrm{unvec}_{N\times N}(\mathbf{q}_{p}^{(s)})
. Using the identity
\mathrm{vec}(\bm{\phi}\bm{\phi}^{*})^{*}\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{Q})=\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{Q}\bm{\phi}
, we obtain
\mathbf{y}^{*}\mathbf{q}_{p}^{(s)}=\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{(s)}\bm{\phi}
and
\|\mathbf{y}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\bm{\phi}\bm{\phi}^{*}\|_{F}^{2}=(\bm{\phi}^{*}\bm{\phi})^{2}
. Hence, (
62
) becomes

p_{4}(\bm{\phi})\leq\lambda_{p}^{(s)}(\bm{\phi}^{*}\bm{\phi})^{2}+2\Re\{\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{(s)}\bm{\phi}\}+c_{p}^{(s)}.

(63)

However, the quadratic term
2\Re\{\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{(s)}\bm{\phi}\}
is not necessarily convex, since
\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{(s)}+\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{(s)*}
is not guaranteed to be positive semidefinite. To obtain a convex upper-bound, define
\mathbf{H}_{p}^{(s)}\triangleq\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{(s)}+\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{(s)*}
, which is Hermitian, and choose any positive scalar
\mu_{p}^{(s)}
satisfying
\mu_{p}^{(s)}\geq\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{H}_{p}^{(s)})
. Then, again by quadratic majorization,

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{H}_{p}^{(s)}\bm{\phi}\leq

\displaystyle\mu_{p}^{(s)}\bm{\phi}^{*}\bm{\phi}+2\Re\left\{\bm{\phi}^{*}(\mathbf{H}_{p}^{(s)}-\mu_{p}^{(s)}\mathbf{I})\bm{\phi}^{(s)}\right\}+d_{p}^{(s)},

(64)

where
d_{p}^{(s)}\triangleq\bm{\phi}^{(s)*}(\mu_{p}^{(s)}\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H}_{p}^{(s)})\bm{\phi}^{(s)}
is constant with respect to
\bm{\phi}
. Since
2\Re\{\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{(s)}\bm{\phi}\}=\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{H}_{p}^{(s)}\bm{\phi}
, substituting (
64
) into (
63
) yields

p_{4}(\bm{\phi})\leq\lambda_{p}^{(s)}(\bm{\phi}^{*}\bm{\phi})^{2}+\mu_{p}^{(s)}\bm{\phi}^{*}\bm{\phi}+2\Re\left\{\mathbf{b}_{p}^{(s)*}\bm{\phi}\right\}+\tilde{c}_{p}^{(s)},

(65)

where
\mathbf{b}_{p}^{(s)}\triangleq(\mathbf{H}_{p}^{(s)}-\mu_{p}^{(s)}\mathbf{I})\bm{\phi}^{(s)}
and
\tilde{c}_{p}^{(s)}\triangleq c_{p}^{(s)}+d_{p}^{(s)}
. Therefore, by substituting (
65
) into (
60
), we obtain the convex MM surrogate of the ARIS power constraint:

\displaystyle\lambda_{p}^{(s)}(\bm{\phi}^{*}\bm{\phi})^{2}+\bm{\phi}^{*}(\mathbf{K}_{1}+2\sigma_{z}^{2}\mathbf{I}+\mu_{p}^{(s)}\mathbf{I})\bm{\phi}

(66)

\displaystyle+2\Re\left\{\mathbf{b}_{p}^{(s)*}\bm{\phi}\right\}+\tilde{c}_{p}^{(s)}\leq P_{\max,a}.

IV-B
5
Convex MM
\bm{\Phi}
-Subproblem

Collecting (
51
), (
53
), and (
66
), the update of
\bm{\phi}
at iteration
s+1
, denoted by
\bm{\phi}^{(s+1)}
is obtained by solving

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{\phi}}~\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{A}_{y}^{(s)}\bm{\phi}+\Re\left\{\mathbf{b}_{y}^{(s)*}\bm{\phi}\right\}

IV-C

Overall AO Framework and Computational Complexity

According to the above derivations, the proposed joint transmit precoding and ARIS reflection design is summarized in Algorithm

At each iteration, the objective value is guaranteed to be non-decreasing, since each subproblem is optimally solved over its feasible set, and the MM-based surrogate function is tight at the current operating point. Consequently, the proposed AO framework produces a monotonically non-decreasing sequence and converges to a stationary point of the original problem.

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigated a RARE-enabled ARIS-assisted ISAC framework and developed a unified design that jointly optimized the BS beamforming and ARIS reflection coefficients under practical communication and sensing constraints. By explicitly incorporating the real-domain observation structure of RARE and the amplified-noise characteristics of ARIS, we established a CRB-driven optimization framework with an efficient AO-based solution. Numerical results demonstrated that the proposed RARE-aware design significantly outperformed conventional RF-based ISAC schemes and achieved performance close to the radar-only benchmark, while effectively balancing the sensing-communication trade-off. These findings highlighted the benefit of combining low-QSN RARE with ARIS-based amplification in mitigating multiplicative fading and preserving sensing accuracy, revealing the strong potential of RARE for quantum-enhanced ISAC.
