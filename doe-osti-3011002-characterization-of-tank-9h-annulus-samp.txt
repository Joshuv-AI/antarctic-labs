 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) was requested by Savannah River Mission
Completion (SRMC) to provide sample preparation and characterization of the Tank 9H annulus
sample in support of Residual Material Inventory Determinations. One Tank 9H sample in three
vials [HTF-9-25-13, HTF-9-25-14 and HTF-9-25-15], with each vial containing approximately
200 mL of the Tank 9H annulus salt solution, were delivered to the SRNL Shielded Cells for
sample preparation and characterizations in February 2025.

The density of the “as-received” solution contained in each of the three Tank 9H annulus sample
vials were determined followed by a solid-liquid separation on each one using 0.45-micron
Nalgene® nylon filter membranes. The resulting filtrates were combined to form the Tank 9H
annulus sample with a total volume of about 600 mL. The combined wet solid fractions, about a
total of 4.8 grams of salt material, remaining on the filter membranes were air-dried in the Shielded
Cells for 72 hours. The total weight of the air-dried solids was 2.1 grams. These air-dried solids
were washed with deionized water (DI water) at a phase ratio of 60 mL DI water/gram of solids to
recover insoluble solids, if any. No visible or measurable quantity of insoluble solids were
recovered after DI water washing of the air-dried solids because the air-dried solids completely
dissolved in the DI water. The solid fraction-wash water was not combined with the 600 mL of the
filtrate solution, and the resulting solution was not screened or analyzed for radionuclides.

Aliquot sample volumes of the undiluted Tank 9H annulus sample were sent to the SRNL
analytical services groups for radionuclides, elementals, anions and total mercury analysis by
various methods including radiochemical separations/counting methods, inductively coupled
plasma-atomic emission spectroscopy (ICP-AES), and Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass
Spectroscopy (ICP-MS) and special preparations. All sample analyses were performed in
triplicate.

This report presents the analytical characterization results for the Tank 9H annulus sample. The
results are also reported where analytical methods yielded additional analytes, other than those
requested by SRMC. In the characterization of the Tank 9H annulus sample, the detection limits
for all the analytes, as specified in the Technical Task Request (TTR) and Task Technical and
Quality Assurance Plan (TTQAP), were met.

1.0 Introduction
Radioactive waste from the processing of spent nuclear fuel has been stored in underground waste
tanks located on the Savannah River Site (SRS). The waste from these tanks must be removed to
the maximum extent practical before the tanks may be removed from service and grouted in place.
As part of this effort, residual material within the tanks must be analyzed to document radioactive
and chemical constituents remaining at the time of closure.

The Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) was contracted by Savannah River Mission
Completion (SRMC) to perform sample preparations and characterization of the Tank 9H annulus
sample, as detailed in the Technical Task Request (TTR)1 and Task Technical and Quality
Assurance Plan (TTQAP) 2 in support of residual material inventory determinations for Tank 9H.
Three Tank 9H annulus sample vials [HTF-9-25-13, HTF-9-25-14, and HTF-9-25-15], each
containing about 200 mL of the Tank 9H annulus salt solution, were delivered to the SRNL
Shielded Cells for sample preparation and characterizations in February 2025. The completed
chain-of-custody (COC) forms for receipt of these samples at SRNL are shown in Appendix A.
These Tank 9H annulus samples vials were opened in the SRNL Shielded Cell in March 2025.

2.0 Experimental Procedure/ Sample Preparations for Characterization
The sample vials from Tank 9H were opened in the SRNL Shielded Cells and the contents of each
vial were transferred into clear plastic poly-methyl pentane (PMP) bottle. The three Tank 9H
annulus sample vials [HTF-9-25-13, HTF-9-25-14, and HTF-9-25-15], as shown in Figure 1
(Insert A) were clear salt solutions with a few fine, visible, floating particles. These floating
particles failed to settle to the bottom of the containers even after 24 hours of settling time.
Appendix B lists the radionuclides and elemental constituents for analyses and requested target
detection limits (TDLs) for the Tank 9H annulus sample characterizations.

The density of the “as-received” salt solution in each of the three Tank 9H annulus sample vials
was determined in triplicate. After the density determinations, a solid-liquid separation was
performed on each of the three Tank 9H annulus vials using 0.45-micron Nalgene® nylon filter
membranes. The resulting filtrates were combined to form the Tank 9H annulus sample with a
total volume of about 600 mL. The wet solid fraction, about 4.8 grams of salt material, on the filter
membranes were air-dried in the Shielded Cells for 72 hours. The total weight of the air-dried solid
fraction was 2.1 grams. This air-dried solid fraction was washed with deionized (DI) water at a
phase ratio of 60 mL DI water/gram of solids to recover insoluble solids, if any. No measurable
quantity of insoluble solids was recovered after DI water washing of the air-dried solids because
the air-dried solids dissolved completely in the DI water. No characterizations were performed on
the resulting dissolved solid solution, and the solid fraction wash water was not combined with the
600 mL filtrate solution.

Aliquot sample volumes of the undiluted Tank 9H annulus filtrate sample, in the range of 2 to 30
mL portions, were collected for radionuclide, elemental, anions and total mercury analysis by
various methods including radiochemical separations /counting methods, inductively coupled
plasma-atomic emission spectroscopy (ICP-AES), and Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass
Spectroscopy (ICP-MS) analyses. A summary of the analytical methodologies and physical

  same precision for each individual measurement. The two-sigma analytical uncertainty (counting
  statistics for radionuclides only) reported in the tables are based on 2 times the counting analytical
  uncertainties. For non-radionuclide measurements such as ICP-AES for inorganic elements, anions
  by ion chromatography (IC), total mercury by direct mercury analysis and physical measurements,
  the 2-sigma analytical uncertainty is based on 2 times the one sigma analytical uncertainties from
  each analytical method. For example, the one-sigma instrument analytical uncertainties for ICP-

 Since the shielded Cells 45-minute sample preparation was not accompanied by in-Cell blank data for Cm-

 filtrate may be too high. It is worth mentioning again that the Tank 9H “as-received” sample filtrate was
 transferred in aliquots of 2-30 mL to the SRNL Analytical Group without sample dilutions in the Cells.
 Therefore, in addition to the regular analytical sample blanks that were prepared during analyses for Cm-
 244 in the laboratory hoods, it was suggested that a Shielded Cells blank be also prepared in the Shielded
 Cells and analyzed for Cm-244 (Am/Cm) to account for any cross contaminations in the Cells, if any.

 In the in-Cell “water blank” preparation, 10.9 g Na/100 mL solution was prepared with DI water (sodium
 concentration in the Tank 9H “as-received” sample is 1.09E+05 mg/L as presented in the ICP-AES data in
 Table 6 of this report). In the Shielded Cells, about 50 mL of the “water blank” was filtered through a 0.45-
 micron Nylon® filter membrane. About 20 mL of this “water blank” filtrate was put into a 60-mL capacity
 high density poly-bottle and exposed to the Cell environment for a total of 45 minutes, including filtration
 time. This blank sample was sent out of the Shielded Cells for Cm-244 (Am/Cm) analysis.

4.0 Conclusions
A Tank 9H annulus sample, derived from the combination of three “as received” Tank 9H annulus
samples [HTF-9-25-13, HTF-9-25-14, and HTF-9-25-15], were analyzed for select radionuclides

242m, Am-243 and cesium removed gamma scan analytes], inorganic elemental compositions
including I2 (total iodine), and total Hg.

Sufficient standards and blanks were utilized to demonstrate adequate quality assurance for the
characterization of the Tank 9H annulus sample, and the results are also reported where analytical
methods yielded additional analytes other than those requested by the customer.

APPENDIX C: Summary of Analytical Methods

Inductively Coupled Plasma – Atomic Emission Spectroscopy (ICP-AES)
Aliquots of the Tank 9H sample were diluted as necessary to bring analytes within the instrument
range. A scandium internal standard is added to all aliquot samples after dilution at a concentration
of 2 mg/L. The instrument is calibrated daily with a blank and two standards: 5 and 10 mg/L
National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) traceable multi-element standards in dilute
acid. Background and internal standard correction were applied to the results.

Aliquots of the Tank 9H sample were run concurrently with a laboratory control standard (LCS)

LCS provided a mass response covering most of the mass range of interest. The following

Cs-137
Aliquots of the Tank 9H sample were analyzed by coaxial high purity germanium gamma-ray
spectrophotometers with an appropriate efficiency calibration to quantify Cs-137 concentrations.
A laboratory-generated blank was included as an LCS.

Sr-90
Aliquots Tank 9H sample were spiked with an elemental strontium carrier. The strontium species
were extracted from the matrix using a crown-ether-based solid phase extractant. Sr-90
concentrations were measured by liquid scintillation analysis. Elemental strontium carrier yields
were measured ICP-AES and were used to correct the Sr-90 analyses for any strontium losses on
a sample-by-sample basis from the radiochemical separations. A laboratory blank, two Sr-90 spike
samples, and a serial dilution sample were included in the analysis.

 23

Pu-238, 239/240, 241
Aliquots of the Tank 9H sample were spiked with Pu-236 tracer. The plutonium was extracted
from the matrix using thenoyltrifluoroacetone (TTA) following a series of oxidation-state
adjustments. The TTA extracts were mounted on stainless steel counting plates and counted for
Pu-238 and Pu-239/240 using planar silicon (PIPS) detectors. Each separation was traced based
on the Pu-236 recovery. A laboratory blank sample, serial dilution sample, a traced Pu-238
standard, and Pu-236 standard sample were included with this preparation.

Pu-239, 240, 242, 244
The plutonium from aliquots of the Tank 9H sample were extracted using TEVA columns. The
Pu-containing extracts were then analyzed by ICP-MS to determine Pu-239, Pu-240, Pu-242, and
Pu-244 isotopics. This ICP-MS analysis was used to evaluate the Pu-239 fraction of the total Pu-

determined previously from the Pu-238, 239/240, 241 method, resulting in determination of a
recovery-corrected Pu-239 concentration. This Pu-239 concentration was then used in
combination with the ICP-MS Pu-239/240, Pu-239/242, and Pu-239/244 ratios to obtain recovery-
corrected Pu-isotopics. A laboratory reagent blank was used as a control in this method.

Aliquots of sample were also subjected to Cs-removal with Bio-Rad Ammonium
Molybdophosphate (AMP) resin and extracted using TEVA columns. The Pu-containing extracts
were measured by liquid scintillation analysis using an appropriate counting efficiency to
determine Pu-241 concentration. The Pu-241 concentration was yielded by combining the results
of the liquid scintillation counting and Pu-238 + Pu-239/240 from the alpha counting. A laboratory
blank sample and Pu-238 standard sample were included in the preparation.

Tc-99
Tank 9H sample aliquots were spiked with Tc-99m, and the technetium species were extracted

measured by liquid scintillation analysis. Tc-99m yields were measured with a high purity
germanium-well gamma spectrometer and were used to correct the Tc-99 analyses for any
technetium losses from the radiochemical separations. A laboratory blank and two Tc-99 standard-
spiked blanks were included in the preparation and analysis.

Am-242m, Am-243, Cm-243, 244, 245, 247, 248, Cf-249, Cf-251
The americium, curium and californium species were extracted from aliquots of Tank 9H sample
using a combination of Octylphenyl-N,N di-isobutyl carbamoylphosphine oxide/tri-n-butyl
phosphate (CMPO/TBP) commercial resin based solid phase extractant and purified further with
a proprietary commercial resin called di(2-ethylhexyl)orthophosphoric acid (HDEHP) based solid
phase extractant. Am-241, 243, Cm-243, 245, 247, Cf-249 and 251 concentrations were measured
using low energy photon/x-ray, thin-windowed, semi-planar high purity germanium
spectrometers. Am-242m, Cm-242, and 244 concentrations were measured using passivated,
implanted, PIPS alpha spectrometers. Am-241 quantities had been measured from the cesium
removed gamma analyses, Am, Cm, and Cf results were traced with the Am-241 present in the
sample matrix. A laboratory reagent blank and Am-241/243 spiked blank sample were also run as
controls.

Co-60, Am-241 (Cs-removed gamma analysis)
Aliquots of the Tank 9H sample were subjected to a Cs-removal process utilizing Bio Rad

I-129
Aliquots of Tank 9H sample were spiked with stable potassium iodide (KI) to act as an iodine
tracer/carrier. The traced samples were then rendered acidic. The samples were decontaminated
with a resin treatment to remove Cs-137 and the actinide elements. The solution was then treated
with AgNO3 to precipitate the iodide as AgI. The precipitate was analyzed by low energy
photon/x-ray, thin-windowed, semi-planar high purity germanium spectrometers to determine the
amount of I-129 present. I-129 is detected by its characteristic gamma and x-ray emissions. The
precipitate and an aliquot of the stable KI tracer were then neutron activated in a Cf-252 neutron
source to determine the total amount of elemental iodine present to calculate the recovery of I-129
in the radiochemical separation. A laboratory blank sample was included in this preparation.

U-233, U-234, U-235, U-236, 238
Uranium was extracted from aliquots of Tank 9H sample using a diamyl-amylphosphonate
(DAAP)-based solid phase extraction. The uranium extract was then analyzed by ICP-MS for U-

previously from a direct ICP-MS analysis. The U-233/238, U-234/238 U-235/238, and U-236/238
ratios measured from the ICP-MS analysis of the uranium extract was applied to the U-238
concentration quantified directly off the ICP-MS analysis to determine the sample U-233, U-234,
U-235, and U-236 concentrations. The sample U-238 concentrations were reported directly from
the non-separated ICP-MS analysis. A laboratory blank sample was included in this preparation
and analysis.

Cs-135
Cs-135 was extracted from aliquots of the Tank 9H sample using a spherical resorcinol

C-14
The Tank 9H sample was used for the C-14 separation and analysis. The material was added to a
mixture of sodium hydroxide, and sodium carbonate/sodium hydroxide. A series of oxidation and

selectively trapped the C-14 in a basic solution. The basic solutions were acidified and the C-14

Pa-231
Aliquots of Tank 9H sample were spiked with a Pa-233 tracer. Protactinium species were then
extracted from the traced matrix using TK400 Resin. Pa-233 tracer concentrations were measured
using coaxial high purity germanium gamma ray spectrometers to determine separation yields.

Cl-36
Aliquots of the Tank 9H sample were first spiked with HCl for tracing purposes. Spiked aliquots
were then subjected to numerous resin-based decontamination steps to primarily remove Cs-137,
Sr-90, and actinides. Chlorine was separated from the non-volatile components of the acidified
matrix via AgCl precipitation. The precipitate was counted using a gas flow proportional counter
(GFPC) analysis. The AgCl precipitate was then activated by neutron activation analysis to
determine Cl losses during the processes, and to correct Cl-36 results for those losses. The Cl in
the HCl was used to trace Cl-36 throughout the decontamination and processing. A laboratory
blank sample was included in the preparation and analysis.

Total mercury was analyzed by DMA.
The direct mercury analysis (DMA) method for mercury analysis is heat controlled in an
oxygenated decomposition furnace used to liberate mercury from solid and aqueous samples in the
instrument. The sample is dried and then thermally and chemically decomposed within the
decomposition furnace. The decomposition products are carried by flowing oxygen to the catalytic
section of the furnace. With the completion of oxidation, halogens and nitrogen/sulfur oxides are
trapped. The remaining decomposition products are then carried to an amalgamator that selectively
traps mercury. After the system is flushed with oxygen to remove any remaining gases or
decomposition products, the amalgamator is rapidly heated, releasing mercury vapor. Flowing
oxygen carries the mercury vapor through absorbance cells positioned in the light path of a single
wavelength atomic absorption spectrophotometer. Absorbance (peak height or peak area) is
measured at 253.7 nm as a function of mercury concentration.

The typical working range for this method is 0.05-600 ng. The mercury vapor is first carried
through a long path-length absorbance cell and then a short path-length absorbance cell. (The
lengths of the first cell and the second cell are in a ratio of 10:1 or another appropriate ratio.) The
same quantity of mercury is measured twice, using two different sensitivities, resulting in a
dynamic range that spans at least four orders of magnitude. The instrument detection limit for this
method is 0.01 ng of total mercury.

Total Iodine by Neutron Activation Analysis (NAA)
Three 30 mL aliquots of the Tank 9H sample, a blank, and a caustic-blank spiked with iodine were
treated with two strikes of hydrogen-form crystalline silico-titanate (CST) to reduce levels of Cs-
137 as well as several other radionuclide contaminants. The treatment was also designed to reduce
levels of sodium, an element that complicates NAA measurements. The cells treatment dropped
sample doses significantly, allowing for the treated aliquots to be removed from the Shielded
Cells. The aliquots were then screened by gamma spectrometry to determine what further

treatments were necessary prior to NAA. Low levels of Cs-137 and Co-60 were observed. The
samples were decontaminated a second time in the radiochemistry laboratories with two strikes of
CST. Two strikes of Chelex resin were also conducted to reduce levels of Co-60 specifically. The
samples were then analyzed by neutron activation analysis, activating I-127 to the short-lived,
gamma-emitting I-128 isotope. That isotope was measured by gamma spectrometry, activities
were compared to the measured I-128 activities of two activated elemental iodide standards (one
to calibrate, one to confirm) to determine elemental concentrations of iodine in the samples, blank,
and iodine-spiked caustic blank. No iodine above detection limits was observed in the samples or
in blank. The caustic-spiked blank’s iodine NAA measurement overlapped the expected iodine
concentrations and were within the analysis uncertainties.

K-40
Gamma analysis of the aliquots from the cesium-removed gamma preparation was sufficient to

were subjected to extended gamma counting via a coaxial high purity germanium spectrometry.
Detector backgrounds were sufficiently analyzed to ensure proper quantification of cosmic K-40

Pm-147/Sm-151
Aliquots of the Tank 9H sample were spiked with an elemental samarium carrier which was used
to trace the separation efficiency on a sample-by-sample basis. The promethium and samarium
species are extracted from the matrix using a combination of CMPO/TBP and di(2-
ethylhexyl)orthophosphoric acid (HDEHP) – based extractants. The separation is designed to
extract both Sm and Pm together. Sm-151 concentrations were measured by liquid scintillation
analysis with an appropriate quench curve/efficiency calibration to convert cpm to dpm. Pm-147
concentrations were also measured by liquid scintillation analysis using appropriate counting
efficiency. Elemental samarium carrier yields were measured by NAA and were used to correct
the analyses for any samarium and promethium losses from the radiochemical separations. The
quality of the tracing is indicated by the successful laboratory spikes containing both Sm-151 and
Pm-147, separately. In addition, a laboratory blank sample was included in the preparation and
analysis.

Gross Alpha/Beta
Aliquots of Tank 9H sample were added to liquid scintillation cocktail and analyzed for gross
alpha and gross beta activity using liquid scintillation analysis. Alpha/beta spillover is determined
for each sample by preparing two separate aliquots of each sample for analysis, one spiked with
plutonium (matrix spike) and one without the spike. The spillover value is determined by using
the matrix spike and is subsequently used for accurately determining alpha and beta activity.
Results were reported as disintegrations per minute (dpm) by converting counts per minute (cpm)
from the instrument to dpm using appropriate efficiencies. A laboratory blank sample was included
in this analysis.

Np-237

Aliquots of Tank 9H sample were first acidified and spiked with Am-243. Np-239, the short-lived
daughter of Am-243, acts as the tracer for this separation. A laboratory method blank, blank spike,
and Am-243/Np-239 standard sample were included in this preparation. The separated aliquots

Density Measurements and solid-Liquid separations
The bulk densities of the “as-received” Tank 9H annulus sample was determined as follows in the
SRNL Shielded Cells. A reference volume of 2.0 mL capacity glass flask was used to determine
the density of the “as-received” Tank 9H annulus samples or the liquid fraction resulting from
solid-liquid separation. The reference 2.0 mL capacity glass flask was filled to mark with the Tank
9H annulus samples at Shielded Cell temperature and the mass of the liquid determined by
difference using a 3-place balance. Knowing the mass of the liquid that is filled to the mark in the
reference 2.0 mL capacity glass flask, the density of the liquid was calculated. The measurements
were determined three times for each of the three sample vials containing Tank 9H sample and at
the end of the measurements the contents of the cup were put back into the original sample vial.
The density of the water was assumed to be 1.0 g/mL at the measuring temperature of
approximately 25 o C; the water mass was considered equal to cup volume.

A 0.45-micron Nylon® filter membrane and container cup were used to perform a solid-liquid
separation for each of the three Tank 9H annulus sample vials. The combined liquid fractions
formed the Tank 9H annulus sample.
