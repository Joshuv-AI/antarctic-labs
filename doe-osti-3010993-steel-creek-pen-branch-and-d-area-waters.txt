 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A network of stream gauging systems were installed in the Steel Creek, Pen Branch, D-Area Discharge
Canal, and the D006 Stream in support of the groundwater modeling efforts for the P-Area Groundwater
Operable Unit (OU); Chemical, Metals, and Pesticides (CMP) Pits OU; and the D-Area Watershed,
respectively. Each location is monitored by a MACE Floseries3 FloPro data logger and a MACE doppler
ultrasonic area/velocity sensor. Each stream gauging system is powered by an internal 12-volt battery
supplied by a solar panel with a trickle charger. Information collected by each data logger is logged
internally and telecommunicated via a cellular network to an online server for real time analysis and
monitoring. The MACE doppler ultrasonic area/velocity sensor can measure stream depth and velocities to
give output values of flow rates, total flow, net flow, and volumes. The water depth is measured by a
ceramic pressure transducer located on the top of the sensor. The velocity is measured by a continuous
wave doppler sensor to give an average velocity across the whole stream profile. This report discusses the
equipment and methods used to install continuous stream gauging stations and provides a summary of data
collected through FY2025.

1.0 Introduction
Groundwater flow and contaminant transport modeling is an important tool for contaminant plume
management and remediation. Groundwater models are used to analyze current conditions and to predict
plume behavior as function of time. Often, scientists make pertinent decisions based on these models
emphasizing the importance of consistent and accurate data. The creation of these models utilizes many
types of information (e.g., precipitation, stream data, infiltration, and contaminant levels). This project
focuses specifically on determining the baseflow of selected streams. Baseflow is the component of surface
water that is derived from groundwater that has discharged from the subsurface.

This report discusses a permanent method for measuring stream flow that will provide continuous stream
gauging information to ultimately determine baseflow of the monitored streams.

2.0 Stream Gauging Methods and Objectives
The objective of this project was to install stream gauging stations to provide continuous, accurate, and
telemetry capable field sensors for measuring stream flow for Steel Creek, Pen Branch, and the D-Area
Watershed. These objectives were met with the installation of the MACE Floseries3 FloPro data logger and
a MACE doppler ultrasonic area/velocity sensor. The following sections describe the equipment and
methods used to meet the projects objectives.

2.1 Stream Gauging Equipment
The equipment chosen to conduct continuous stream flow measurements was provided by In-Situ, Inc. The
data logging component of the stream gauging system is the MACE Floseries3 FloPro. The FloPro can
monitor water in full or partially full pipes, and open channels. The data logger is housed in a casing
constructed with ultraviolet (UV) resistant polycarbonate plastic. Power is supplied by a 12-volt 7.2Ah gel
cell battery connected to a solar panel for trickle charging.

The stream flow measurements are made using the MACE doppler ultrasonic area/velocity sensor. The
velocity component is measured using continuous doppler ultrasound to measure the speed of debris and/or
air bubbles flowing in the stream. The MACE sensor uses MACE Advanced Signal Processing (MASP) to
give true average velocities across the entire stream profile. For example, the velocities across the profile
of a stream are vastly different based on depth and distance from the center of the channel. The velocity
sensor takes velocities over the entire profile of the stream for a true average velocity. The sensor can be
placed to face up or down stream based on preference and can measure stream velocities ranging from 0.08
to 26 ft/s with a resolution of 0.04in. at 3.3ft/s and an accuracy of 1% up to 10ft/s. The depth component of
the sensor is a ceramic pressure sensor which lies flat on top of the sensor allowing for undeflected flow
and to create an area that prevents sediment build up. The depth sensor has a minimum range of 0.67in.

 1

with a maximum depth of 13ft. and has a resolution of 0.04in. and an accuracy of 0.2% at constant
temperature in a static stream. The MACE doppler ultrasonic area/velocity sensor comes factory calibrated;
however, field calibrations can be conducted and are found in the MACE FloPro user manual
(https://macemeters.com/pdf/FloPro_PDF.pdf)

The telemetry component of the sensor is supplied by a WebComm Card installed within the FloPro data
logger. The WebComm Card uploads data and run diagnostics uploaded to a web-based server called
HydroVu™ using a 4G cell network.

2.2 Stream Gauging Configuration and Locations
The stream gauging stations located along Steel Creek (SW-SC-03 and SW-SC-07) are located near surface
water sampling stations SC-03 and SC-07, respectively. SW-SC-03 is located close to the head waters of
Steel Creek and SW-SC-07 is located downstream (Figure A-1). Each stream gauging station along Steel
Creek was secured in a large, corrugated pipe to constrain the stream into a known area for more accurate
computation (see Figure B-1 for typical plastic corrugated pipe setup). The corrugated pipes were secured
to the streambed using T-posts and steel cabling with a threaded tensioner. The sensors were fastened at the
downstream portion of the corrugated pipe facing upstream using flexible plastic stripping and self-tapping
screws.

The stream gauging stations located along Pen Branch (CMP-SW-23A, CMP-SW-24, and CMP-SW-25A
are located at the intersection of C Road (upstream locations) and Pen Branch, and the intersection of
Youman Road (downstream location) and Pen Branch (Figure A-2). Each stream gauging station in Pen
Branch is located in preexisting structures. The upstream locations (CMP-SW-23A and CM-SW-25A) were
installed in a concrete culvert that runs beneath C Road (see Figure B-2 for typical concrete culvert setup).
The downstream location (CMP-SW-24) was installed in two adjacent plastic corrugated pipes that run
beneath Youman Road. Data is reported in tables and appendices separately for the two locations at CMP-
SW-24 as Location 1 and Location 2. The sensors located in the concrete culvert were secured using
concrete anchors mounted inside the pipe with flexible plastic stripping. The downstream location at
Youman Road was fastened with self-tapping screws mounted inside the corrugated pipe with flexible
plastic stripping.

The stream gauging stations located in D-Area are in the D006 Stream (DSWM-1A) and the Discharge
Canal (DSWM-8B) (Figure A-3). Both stream gauging stations were installed in corrugated plastic pipes.
The corrugated pipes were secured to the streambed using T-posts and steel cabling with a threaded
tensioner. The sensors were fastened with self-taping screws mounted inside the corrugated pipe with
flexible plastic stripping. The sensors were fastened at the downstream portion of the corrugated pipe facing
upstream.

At each stream gauging location, the MACE FloPro data loggers and solar panels were secured on T-posts
adjacent to each stream gauging station. The data logger wiring was protected using a rodent resistant mesh
and flexible plastic conduit. The solar panels were placed in locations to provide adequate sunlight to charge
the internal battery (see Figure B-3 for typical data logger and solar panel setup).

2.3 HydroVu
A HydroVu™ account is required to view the stream flow data remotely. HydroVu™ is a secure, web-
based platform for managing data from multiple devices. Warnings and timers can be integrated to alert the
user of certain conditions (e.g., battery life or velocities at certain peak stream flow periods). All of the
stream gauging systems are connected to the HydroVu™ platform and are programmed to upload at least
once per day.

2.4 System Maintenance
Monthly site maintenance was performed to ensure optimal operating conditions. Maintenance items varied
from station to station based on stream dynamics and exposure. Routine maintenance consisted of clearing
debris from the sensor, recalibration of the depth sensor, cleaning of the solar panel, and inspection of the
piping to verify its placement. Less common maintenance items were removing bacterial growth from
stagnant or low flowing streams and the removal of larger debris from beaver activity. At one location, a
10-foot PVC pipe was used to elevate the antenna for better cellular reception. No battery or charging
maintenance was required for any of the data loggers.

3.0 Results
Each stream gauging station was configured to record stream depth, velocity, and flow rate at 15-minute
intervals. For analysis purpose, the data was filtered to eliminate outliers (e.g., negative depth or velocity
recordings, or depth recordings over the diameter of the pipe). These data were not used in the below
calculations and will appears as missing data or gaps in figure in Appendix C. Table 3-1 shows a detailed
breakdown for each stream gauging station (with the exception to CMP-SW-25A as it was installed late
FY25). Depths, velocities, and flow rates were graphed for each station using a scatter plot layout. A best
fit line was placed to show the moving average over time (Appendix C). Battery life throughout the
monitoring period was recorded on HydroVu. No battery issues were noted during the monitoring period.

4.0 Recommendations
In the first year of service, each stream gauging station was relatively low maintenance and robust.
However, with the unpredictability of the field environment, it is recommended that routine maintenance
and data verification be conducted for each station. Common problems (e.g., debris and/or bacteria build
up on the sensors) can be remedied during monthly inspections. For problems like the one encountered at
DSWM-1A (beaver), additional work may be needed to block or deter any damming activity. Damming
activity will be monitored in FY26. SRNL will provide further recommendations for prevention if needed.

Baseflow calculations should be conducted once continuous data has been collected for a minimum of an
entire year. Using a year’s worth of data will account for seasonal variation. Baseflow calculations should
be conducted using similar methods provided by the United States Geological Survey USGS and should be
updated yearly.
