Research on precise segmentation model of thyroid nodules based on deep learning

Qingpeng Zhang, Qinqin Xiao, Jiacheng Wu, Guangming Shao

ABSTRACT
Thyroid nodules are a prevalent condition in clinical practice, typically identified via ultrasound imaging with visual assessment by physicians. However, the ambiguous features of malignant nodules, their proximity to surrounding tissues, image noise, and unclear boundaries can lead to variability in diagnostic outcomes among different practitioners. In this study, we initially employed nine established models to segment ultrasound images from two publicly available datasets, TN3K and DDTI. Evaluation metrics including IoU, F1-score, accuracy (Acc), specificity (Spec), and precision (Pre) were utilized to assess the segmentation performance of these models. The experimental results indicate that, given the current data scale, the overall segmentation performance of each model is relatively low. Analysis suggests this is primarily due to the limited number of samples in the medical imaging dataset. During training, the model struggles to fully learn the discriminative features of the lesion area. Particularly during the encoder’s down-sampling stage, the feature expression ability is constrained, affecting the model’s ability to accurately recognize lesion locations. To address this, we enhanced the classic model by incorporating the Residual Network ResNet34, constructing a new thyroid nodule segmentation model. The improved model demonstrated enhanced segmentation performance, with most IoU values on the TN3K dataset exceeding 0.8. However, models trained on the DDTI dataset rarely achieved high performance. Further analysis attributes this performance disparity to differences in the number of training samples. To further enhance model performance, we initialized the improved model with pre-trained ResNet34 weights from the ImageNet dataset, resulting in significant performance improvements. Notably, Res34-PAN performed best across both datasets. On the DDTI dataset, the model achieved an IoU of 0.8778, an F1-score of 0.9349, an accuracy of 0.9872, a specificity of 0.9928, and a precision of 0.9342. To further validate the model design’s rationale, we conducted a systematic ablation experiment. We selected commonly used network structures for encoders and used them as the encoders for nine segmentation models, comparing performance with and without the ImageNet pre-trained weights. The results further confirmed the effectiveness of the proposed method in the thyroid nodule segmentation task.

Introduction Thyroid nodules, resulting from the abnormal proliferation of thyroid cells [ 1 ], are a common condition, particularly among women and the elderly [ 2 ]. Research indicates that approximately 4% to 7% of adults have palpable thyroid nodules [ 3 ], but only about 8% to 16% of these cases progress to thyroid cancer [ 4 ]. The primary method for preoperative diagnosis is fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) [ 5 ], which boasts an accuracy rate of 97.5% [ 6 ]. Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) tests and ultrasound (US) are crucial in diagnosing and treating thyroid nodules, providing insights into thyroid function and the characteristics of malignant tumors. Technological advancements and the convenience of US have made it a popular diagnostic tool, capable of detecting ten times more nodules than traditional methods. However, despite its widespread use and ability to identify more nodules, US is less accurate than FNAC, leading to an increase in misdiagnoses in recent years [ 7 ]. In recent years, numerous scholars have dedicated themselves to researching accurate methods for diagnosing and treating thyroid nodules. A study by the Italian Minimally Invasive Thyroid Treatment Group suggested that thermal ablation could serve as a viable alternative to surgical intervention for patients with thyroid nodules [ 8 ]. In a comparative study conducted by Hisham Mehanna et al., it was observed that ultrasound elastography, when compared to traditional ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration cytology (US FNAC), marginally decreased the necessity for diagnostic thyroidectomies without significant additional benefits [ 9 ]. Luca Giovanella et al. proposed a modified diagnostic algorithm in thyroidology, leading to a reduction in FNAC procedures and diagnostic surgeries for patients with ambiguous thyroid nodules [ 10 ]. In computer science, the advancement of machine learning and deep learning has facilitated the convergence of medicine and engineering, paving the way for the advancement of precision medicine and intelligent healthcare. FCN [ 11 ], a variant of CNN [ 12 ], excels at recovering the category of each pixel in an image from abstract features, allowing for precise lesion localization. U-Net [ 13 ], with its “U”-shaped symmetric structure, effectively captures contextual information from medical images using an Encoder-Decoder framework. U-Net++ [ 14 ], a notable variant of U-Net, enhances segmentation performance by integrating multiple small U-Nets to fuse results from various depths. Since its introduction in 2015, ResNet [ 15 ] has significantly influenced the design of subsequent deep learning networks. Its distinctive residual learning and identity mapping techniques effectively mitigate gradient vanishing and explosion issues while deepening the model structure, thereby improving lesion localization. Numerous image segmentation models are commonly utilized for segmenting various disease images, including cell nucleus, lung, and breast cancer segmentation. Wan Siti Halimatul Munirah Wan Ahmad and colleagues (2015) proposed a Gaussian-response based automatic threshold generation algorithm that demonstrates strong cross-device generalization for lung segmentation. Their approach achieved consistently high performance on both the JSRT and SH datasets, with most evaluation metrics exceeding 0.90 on JSRT [ 16 ]. Goceri (2024) employed a hybrid attention-aware network and adversarial network for cell nucleus segmentation. The hybrid attention block enhanced global information extraction and interrelations between local features, resulting in a minimum 3.7% increase in segmentation accuracy compared to contemporaneous methods [ 17 ]. Na et al. (2024) introduced an automatic prompt generator based on SAC [ 18 ] to guide cell nucleus segmentation effectively. They integrated low-rank attention into the SAM model, enhancing SAC’s adaptability across diverse segmentation tasks. Rezvani et al. (2025) proposed a hybrid approach using the FusionLungNet network, amalgamating the ResNet50 encoder, CAA, MFF, SR, and multiple decoders. Lung segmentation on the self-compiled LungSegDB dataset achieved an IoU of 98.04 [ 19 ]. Hasan et al. (2024) employed DeepLabV3+ for lung segmentation in chest X-ray images [ 20 ], yielding 97.42% accuracy, 93.49% Jaccard index, and 96.63% Dice coefficient, outperforming other models. Sulaiman et al. (2024) integrated the attention mechanism with U-Net, proposing the Attention U-Net model for precise depiction of breast tumors and boundary separation. On the BUSI dataset, the model achieved 0.98 accuracy, 0.97 precision, 0.90 recall, and 0.92 Dice coefficient [ 21 ]. Samudrala et al. (2024) utilized a hybrid model of DenseNet-121 and Att-PSPnet with an Attention Gate to mitigate noise interference, enhancing multiple evaluation metrics and achieving 94.68% accuracy [ 22 ]. Despite extensive research on medical image segmentation, studies on thyroid nodule segmentation remain limited. This study evaluated nine classic models, including U-Net, U-Net++, DeepLabV3 [ 23 ], DeepLabV3+ [ 24 ], FPN [ 25 ], LinkNet [ 26 ], MANet [ 27 ], PSPNet [ 28 ], and PAN [ 29 ], for segmenting thyroid nodule ultrasound images. Nine classical models were found to perform poorly in recognizing thyroid nodule lesions within the small-sample dataset. PAN exhibited superior performance among the classic models. Subsequently, classic models were enhanced by incorporating the ResNet34 residual network. Performance comparison revealed enhancements in all nine models, with notable improvements in LinkNet and MANet, and the optimized model Res34-PAN demonstrated the best performance. Materials and methods TN3K dataset The dataset [ 30 ] comprising 3,493 2D ultrasound images of 2,421 patients from January 2016 to August 2020 was generously provided by Gong Haifan [ 31 , 32 ] and colleagues at Sun Yat-sen University. The dataset is divided into two segments: the training/validation set and the test set. The training/validation set comprises 2,879 ultrasound images, while the test set includes 614 ultrasound images. Sourced from real clinical cases, this dataset is highly regarded in the field of thyroid nodule segmentation. The official source provides a fixed division. For the experiments, the training/validation set is randomly split into a training set and a validation set at a 9:1 ratio, using a random seed of 42. DDTI dataset The dataset [ 33 ] comprising 637 thyroid nodule ultrasound images in PNG format with pixel labels was obtained from the MICCAI 2020 TN-SCUI Thyroid Nodule Ultrasound Image Segmentation Competition [ 34 ], as curated by Pedraza et al. Notably, all images originate from a singular device. In this experiment, to evaluate the model’s performance and generalization ability, the dataset was randomly split into a training set, validation set, and test set in a ratio of 8:1:1, using a random seed of 42. To address the inconsistent image sizes in the two datasets, OpenCV is employed to resize both the images and their corresponding annotations to 256 × 256. Once the images are read, if their pixel values fall within the range of [0, 255], normalization is applied, followed by standardization using ImageNet statistics. For the annotation masks, after reading, a single channel is selected for binary processing, where pixels with values ≥ 1 are set to 1, and all others are set to 0. ResNet34 residual network The original U-Net encoder comprises multiple layers of convolution and pooling operations, resembling the simple structure of VGG-like convolutional networks. Despite its ease of training, the U-Net model struggles with limited feature representation and decreased gradient propagation efficiency as network depth increases. This limitation is particularly evident with small samples, where it fails to effectively model complex lesion features. In neural networks, both model depth and width significantly impact performance, and enhancing either can potentially boost outcomes. The introduction of the Residual Network (ResNet) has profoundly addressed issues like the vanishing gradient problem associated with increased model depth. As shown in Fig 1 , ResNet employs multiple small convolution kernels instead of a single large one and connects outputs from upper and lower residual blocks to facilitate information reuse. This approach mitigates gradient dispersion and explosion issues caused by excessive convolution depth in traditional networks. The connection of residual blocks simplifies training and helps retain feature details in deeper convolutional layers. ResNet models are commonly categorized by their number of convolutional layers-152, 101, 50, 34, and 18-with those having 50 layers or more classified as deep residual networks. 10.1371/journal.pone.0358892.g001 Fig 1 Standard block and residual block. (a) A standard block composed of two stacked weight layers with activation functions. (b) A residual block that adds an identity (shortcut) connection to the block output, enabling residual learning and improving gradient propagation in deeper networks. In constructing the model, this paper employs the network parameters of ResNet34 as the encoder to enhance the classic model, taking into account both the number of model parameters and computational resources. As shown in Fig 2 , the ResNet34 architecture, with its layers of residual blocks, is employed to improve the feature extraction process. Additionally, it incorporates the weights of ResNet34, which were trained on the large-scale ImageNet database, to optimize the improved model. By leveraging low- and mid-level visual features learned from this extensive natural image dataset and fine-tuning on the thyroid nodule ultrasound image dataset, the model’s feature transfer capability and segmentation accuracy are effectively enhanced, even with limited sample sizes. This approach helps mitigate the performance bottleneck caused by insufficient data samples. 10.1371/journal.pone.0358892.g002 Fig 2 Schematic diagram of the ResNet34 architecture. ResNet34 is composed of an initial convolution and pooling layer followed by four stages of residual blocks (conv2_x–conv5_x). Each stage contains stacked residual blocks with identity (shortcut) connections, enabling deeper feature extraction and improved gradient propagation. In this study, ResNet34 (initialized with ImageNet-pretrained weights) is used as the encoder backbone for the improved segmentation models. PAN (Path Aggregation Network) The PAN model is an enhancement of the FPN model, which propagates high-level semantic features using a top-down pyramid structure. As shown in Fig 3 , PAN addresses the limitation of FPN by introducing a bottom-up pyramid structure following the FPN, facilitating the transfer of low-level localization features upwards. While FPN improves semantic information transfer, it lacks the ability to transfer localization information, necessitating further enhancement for improved model performance. This dual-path approach minimizes information loss, retains detailed information in feature maps, and enhances the model’s segmentation accuracy. 10.1371/journal.pone.0358892.g003 Fig 3 Architecture diagram of the PAN model. PAN (Path Aggregation Network) enhances the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) by adding a bottom-up path augmentation after the top-down pyramid. The top-down pathway propagates high-level semantic features to lower-resolution feature maps, while the bottom-up pathway further aggregates low-level localization information upward. This dual-path feature fusion reduces information loss and improves the transfer of both semantic and localization cues, thereby enhancing segmentation performance. Model optimization The research institute selected nine classic models in the semantic segmentation field, commonly applied in object detection and instance segmentation, all featuring Encoder-Decoder architectures. This study enhances these classic models by substituting the original Encoder component with ResNet34, while retaining the original Decoder structure. The construction of Res34-UNet and Res34-PAN serves as illustrative examples. Res34-UNet. The Res34-UNet model in this study follows a structure akin to the traditional U-Net, comprising an Encoder module and a Decoder module. Diverging from the U-Net architecture, ResNet34 is utilized for downsampling to capture features, supplanting the original downsampling mechanism. During upsampling, the Encoder of the conventional U-Net serves as the Decoder module, with Skip Connection facilitating the fusion of shallow and deep features between the Encoder and Decoder. As shown in Fig 4 , the Encoder module adopts the shallow residual network structure ResNet34 with 5 downsampling operations. Initially, the input image undergoes convolution processing, succeeded by a 3 × 3 max-pooling operation. Subsequently, it traverses four residual modules comprising varying numbers of residual blocks, processing the input feature vectors. These diverse residual modules effectively mitigate issues of gradient vanishing and network degradation encountered during downsampling in the conventional U-Net model. Lastly, a 2 × 2 max-pooling operation is applied to minimize noise interference in medical images. 10.1371/journal.pone.0358892.g004 Fig 4 Res34-UNet network framework. The proposed Res34-UNet follows an encoder–decoder structure. The encoder replaces the original U-Net downsampling path with a ResNet34 backbone (including an initial convolution and max-pooling, followed by four residual stages) to extract multi-level features. The decoder performs symmetric upsampling to recover the spatial resolution, and skip connections fuse shallow detailed features with deep semantic features to improve boundary localization and segmentation accuracy. The decoder of the traditional U-Net model employs the decoding module for upsampling, mirroring the number of upsampling iterations to match the downsampling steps in the encoder, both set at 5. Initially, the feature image undergoes two sequential processes involving the ReLu activation function and convolution. Subsequently, the U-Net model executes the upsampling operation to generate an output image identical in size to the original image. Res34-PAN. The Res34-PAN model introduced in this study retains the Encoder-Decoder structure of PAN, preserving both its Decoder and path aggregation mechanism, while exclusively modifying and reconstructing the Encoder of the original PAN. Like Res34-UNet, the primary design principle of Res34-PAN is to incorporate a residual network to improve feature extraction during the encoding phase, leaving the original decoding process intact. As shown in Fig 5 , during the encoding stage, Res34-PAN utilizes ResNet34 as the feature extraction network to conduct layer-by-layer downsampling and feature encoding on input thyroid nodule ultrasound images. ResNet34 is composed of several residual modules, each facilitating cross-layer feature transfer via residual connections. This design allows the network to maintain a stable information flow as its depth increases. The encoding method effectively extracts representative image features at various depths, offering multi-scale feature representations for the subsequent decoding stage. 10.1371/journal.pone.0358892.g005 Fig 5 Res34-PAN network framework. Res34-PAN inherits the original PAN encoder–decoder topology and keeps the PAN decoder and path aggregation mechanism unchanged, while reconstructing the encoder by replacing it with ResNet34. The ResNet34 encoder performs progressive downsampling and multi-level feature encoding through residual blocks and shortcut connections, providing richer multi-scale feature representations. The PAN decoder then aggregates features via bidirectional information flow (top-down and bottom-up), fusing high-level semantic features with low-level spatial localization cues to generate the final pixel-wise thyroid nodule segmentation. In the decoding stage, Res34-PAN retains the original PAN structure. By facilitating bidirectional information transfer both top-down and bottom-up, it effectively merges high-level semantic features with low-level spatial location information. Given that the features output during the encoding stage are richer in structural depth and semantic hierarchy, the decoder can achieve more comprehensive fusion and reconstruction of multi-scale features while adhering to the original design concept of PAN. The primary structural enhancement of Res34-PAN is evident in the encoder layer. By substituting the original encoder with ResNet34, a residual learning mechanism is incorporated, while the decoder and overall network topology remain unchanged. This modification improves the model’s capability to capture multi-level features during the encoding phase, offering a more robust structural foundation for subsequent feature fusion and pixel-level prediction. Evaluation indicators and loss functions To quantitatively assess the model’s performance in the thyroid nodule segmentation task, this study employs five common evaluation metrics: Intersection over Union (IoU), F1-score, Precision, Specificity, and Accuracy. IoU and F1-score gauge the overlap between the predicted segmentation region and the true label, serving as the primary metrics for evaluating segmentation effectiveness. Precision indicates the proportion of pixels correctly predicted as nodule regions, while Specificity assesses the model’s ability to identify non-nodule, or background, regions. Accuracy measures the proportion of correctly classified pixels relative to the total number of pixels, reflecting overall classification performance. All these metrics range from 0 to 1, with higher values signifying better segmentation performance. Thyroid nodule segmentation is fundamentally a binary classification task at the pixel level. In this experiment, Dice Loss serves as the optimization objective. The network outputs pixel-level logits, which are transformed into predicted probabilities p i through the sigmoid activation function. The corresponding ground-truth binary label is denoted by y i . For an image containing N pixels, the Dice coefficient is defined as follows: D i c e = 2 ∑ i = 1 N p i y i + ε ∑ i = 1 N p i + ∑ i = 1 N y i + ε (1) Accordingly, the Dice Loss is defined as: L D i c e = 1 − D i c e (2) where p i denotes the predicted probability of the i-th pixel, y i denotes the corresponding ground-truth binary label, N is the total number of pixels, and ε is a smoothing term used to avoid numerical instability when the denominator approaches zero. In this study, ε is set to 10 −6 . Throughout the training process, the batch size is maintained at 64, and the penalty coefficient is fixed at 0.000001, and iteration number interval of (60, 150) were specified. The optimal iteration number was determined based on the calculated loss function during training. By evaluating the model’s loss functions on the TN3K and DDTI datasets, an iteration number of 120 was chosen for the experiment. To mitigate sensitivity to the choice of the initial learning rate, we incorporated an adaptive learning-rate adjustment strategy during training. Specifically, we employed a ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler to monitor the validation loss; if no improvement was observed for 10 consecutive epochs, the learning rate was reduced by a factor of 0.5. Introducing an adaptive learning rate adjustment mechanism mitigates the impact of the initial learning rate configuration on experimental outcomes. Initially, a relatively high learning rate expedites the model’s convergence towards the optimal solution during training. As training progresses, the algorithm dynamically adjusts the step size of parameter updates based on gradient information, progressively decreasing the learning rate to avert convergence to local optima. Robustness test To further evaluate the generalization capability and robustness of the proposed model under different data distributions, a cross-dataset evaluation was conducted using Res34-PAN, which achieved the best segmentation performance on the TN3K dataset. Specifically, Res34-PAN was first trained on the TN3K dataset, and the model weights corresponding to the best validation performance were retained. The trained model was then directly applied to the entire DDTI dataset for segmentation without any additional training, fine-tuning, or parameter updating. Therefore, the DDTI dataset served as an independent external test dataset in this experiment to evaluate the transferability and generalization capability of the model across different data domains. Considering the potential differences between the TN3K and DDTI datasets in terms of image acquisition devices, imaging conditions, grayscale distributions, noise characteristics, and nodule morphology, this cross-dataset evaluation provides a more stringent assessment of model stability under distribution shifts. Unlike the previous evaluation based on a limited number of randomly selected images, the revised experiment performs inference on all images in the DDTI dataset. For each image, five evaluation metrics, including Intersection over Union (IoU), F1-score, Accuracy (Acc), Specificity (Spec), and Precision (Pre), are calculated, thereby providing a sample-level performance distribution over the entire external dataset. Conclusions Public datasets of 2D medical images of thyroid nodules are currently limited. Additionally, variations in annotation quality and dataset scale across these datasets hinder the model’s generalization ability, necessitating performance improvements. During model training, residual networks effectively train deep networks, circumvent gradient vanishing issues, enhance image feature learning, and capture both local and global image information, thereby boosting overall segmentation performance. Common residual networks include ResNet18, ResNet34, ResNet50, and ResNet101, with complexity increasing alongside the number of layers. Experimental comparisons indicate that ResNet34 excels in thyroid nodule segmentation tasks, making it the chosen residual network for this study. Compared to the original basic model, it enhances segmentation performance in accurate recognition, positioning ability, iteration speed, and generalization, offering a new direction for small-sample tasks like thyroid nodule lesion recognition. Furthermore, incorporating ResNet34 weights trained on ImageNet can further enhance model performance, partially offsetting the impact of limited data. In this experiment, while the Res34-PAN model demonstrates commendable segmentation performance, the next research phase aims to simultaneously enhance the learning of both regional and boundary features, establish their correlation, and achieve more precise segmentation to surpass current performance limits. Future efforts should focus on optimizing the model to boost recognition accuracy. Additionally, the model’s segmentation performance should be evaluated on a broader range of thyroid nodule test sets and diverse disease datasets to improve its generalization capabilities.
