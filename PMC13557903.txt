Osimertinib vs. erlotinib combined with an angiogenesis inhibitor in EGFR-mutated non-small cell lung cancer with malignant pleural effusion: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Tae Hata, Tadaaki Yamada, Koji Mikami, Toshihide Yokoyama, Tomohiro Oba, Yasuhiro Goto, Naoki Furuya, Shun Futamura, Hirokazu Taniguchi, Tamio Okimoto, Soichi Hirai, Sayaka Uda, Asuka Okada, Rie Watanabe, Naoko Ishizaki, Hayato Kawachi, Takashi Kijima, Koichi Takayama
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundOsimertinib is the standard first-line treatment for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) harboring an epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutation. Erlotinib plus an angiogenesis inhibitor (erlotinib+A) has demonstrated efficacy in controlling malignant pleural effusion (MPE) through reduction of vascular permeability; however, no clinical trials have directly compared its MPE control efficacy with that of osimertinib. This study aimed to compare the efficacy of osimertinib vs. erlotinib+A in controlling MPE in patients with EGFR-mutant NSCLC.MethodsThis multicenter retrospective cohort study analyzed longitudinal medical records of patients with advanced or recurrent EGFR-mutant NSCLC presenting with MPE at treatment initiation who received osimertinib or erlotinib+A at 14 institutions in Japan between November 2009 and October 2023. Patients with squamous cell carcinoma were excluded. The primary endpoint was time to MPE recurrence [MPE-progression-free survival (PFS)]. Secondary endpoints included PFS, overall survival (OS), and adverse events (AEs) graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE). Multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression served as the primary analysis, with propensity score matching (PSM) and inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) utilized for sensitivity analyses.ResultsAmong 421 patients (median age, 73 years; 65% female; 66% never-smokers; 99% adenocarcinoma), 373 received osimertinib and 48 received erlotinib+A. In the multivariable Cox regression analysis, no significant differences were observed between the osimertinib and erlotinib+A groups in MPE-PFS [adjusted hazard ratio (HR): 0.79; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.54–1.15; P=0.22], PFS (adjusted HR: 0.87; 95% CI: 0.60–1.25; P=0.45), or OS (adjusted HR: 0.95; 95% CI: 0.63–1.44; P=0.83). However, a significant interaction between the treatment regimen and EGFR mutation type was identified (P=0.004 for MPE-PFS). Subgroup analysis demonstrated that osimertinib was significantly superior to erlotinib+A in patients with EGFR exon 19 deletions (MPE-PFS: adjusted HR: 0.38; 95% CI: 0.21–0.67; P<0.001), whereas no difference was observed in those with L858R mutations. Grade ≥3 AEs were more frequent in the erlotinib+A group (38% vs. 21%, P=0.02), whereas osimertinib was associated with a higher incidence of all-grade interstitial pneumonia (21% vs. 6%, P=0.01).ConclusionsOsimertinib showed comparable efficacy to erlotinib+A in MPE control with a more favorable safety profile. Subgroup analysis suggested a particular benefit in patients harboring EGFR exon 19 deletions, highlighting the potential importance of mutation subtype-based treatment selection. Given the retrospective design and potential for residual confounding, prospective validation is warranted.

Highlight box

Key findings

• This study showed that osimertinib was comparable to the combination of erlotinib plus an anti-angiogenic agent in terms of pleural effusion control duration, progression-free survival, and overall survival in patients with epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-mutated non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) with pleural effusion. However, the combination therapy was associated with a higher incidence of grade ≥3 adverse events compared with osimertinib.

What is known and what is new?

• In EGFR-mutated lung cancer with pleural effusion, previous prospective trials have established that combining erlotinib with anti-angiogenic agents such as bevacizumab or ramucirumab improves pleural effusion control and prognosis, but data on osimertinib have been limited.

• This study provides the first comparative evidence of osimertinib vs. erlotinib and anti-angiogenic agent combination therapy in patients with EGFR mutations and pleural effusion.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Osimertinib may represent a less toxic yet similarly effective treatment option for patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC with pleural effusion.

• Subgroup findings suggest that patients with EGFR exon 19 deletion may derive particular benefit, highlighting the importance of molecular subtype-based treatment selection.

• These results support consideration of osimertinib as a preferred strategy in this population, especially when treatment tolerability is a concern.

Introduction

Lung cancer remains one of the most lethal malignant tumors worldwide and continues to be a disease with poor prognosis (1). Treatment strategies for lung cancer are significantly influenced by histological type and the presence of potentially treatable genetic mutations. In non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), which accounts for approximately 85% of lung cancers, epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations are detected in 13–50% of cases (2,3), and EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (EGFR-TKIs) form the core of treatment. In recent years, the introduction of third-generation EGFR-TKIs and combination therapy with EGFR-TKIs and angiogenesis inhibitors has significantly improved progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC (4-7).

EGFR-mutated NSCLC often complicates with malignant pleural effusion (MPE). Previous studies have demonstrated a positive correlation between MPE and EGFR mutation status (8), and MPE is considered one of the characteristic clinical features of EGFR-mutated NSCLC. MPE develops due to impaired lymphatic drainage caused by pleural metastasis, combined with increased permeability of tumor and pleural vessels (9). Clinically, patients with both EGFR mutations and MPE exhibit poorer overall survival (OS) compared to those with EGFR mutations alone, making MPE a significant adverse prognostic factor (10,11). This treatment resistance may be influenced by the tumor microenvironment, particularly vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), which is deeply involved in pleural effusion formation. Indeed, anti-angiogenic agents have been reported to be an independent factor associated with OS prolongation in patients with EGFR mutation and pleural effusion (8). Furthermore, subgroup analyses of randomized controlled trials demonstrated that combination therapy with erlotinib and bevacizumab significantly improved PFS in patients with pleural effusion compared to erlotinib monotherapy (12,13). These findings suggest that combination therapy with EGFR-TKIs and anti-angiogenic agents may represent a viable treatment strategy for EGFR-mutated NSCLC complicated by pleural effusion.

Within this evolving therapeutic landscape, prospective phase II trials evaluated the efficacy of adding bevacizumab to osimertinib in patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC. The SPIRAL II trial assessed this combination in the first-line setting (14), while the WJOG8715L trial examined it in patients with T790M-mutated NSCLC following progression on prior EGFR-TKI therapy (15). Neither trial demonstrated an additional clinical benefit from the addition of bevacizumab to the third-generation TKI. Notably, these studies lacked a direct comparator arm of first-generation EGFR-TKI/angiogenesis inhibitor combination therapy, thereby obfuscating whether third-generation TKIs require concurrent anti-VEGF therapy for optimal pleural effusion control. Furthermore, granular analyses of pleural effusion cases within these osimertinib-treated cohorts were historically limited, leaving the definitive efficacy of osimertinib in controlling pleural lesions and effusions poorly characterized. Current consensus guidelines, including the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) Clinical Practice Guidelines, recommend osimertinib as a preferred first-line treatment for EGFR-mutated NSCLC; however, they do not provide specific treatment recommendations stratified by the presence or absence of pleural effusion, reflecting the current paucity of high-quality comparative evidence.

To date, no study has directly compared osimertinib monotherapy with a first-generation EGFR-TKI/angiogenesis inhibitor combination in the clinically high-risk population of EGFR-mutated NSCLC with pleural effusion. Addressing this evidence gap could optimize initial treatment selection and improve outcomes for these patients. Therefore, this multicenter retrospective study aimed to compare the efficacy and safety of osimertinib monotherapy vs. erlotinib combined with an angiogenesis inhibitor in patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC complicated by pleural effusion. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://tlcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tlcr-2026-0343/rc).

Methods

Patients and study protocol

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Kyoto Prefectural University of Medicine (No. ERB-C-3377). All participating hospitals were informed of and agreed to the study. The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of this study, and an opt-out method was used to allow patients and families to decline participation.

This retrospective comparative cohort study included patients diagnosed with stage IVA, IVB, or recurrent NSCLC, as defined by the 8th edition of the tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) classification, at the following 14 participating institutions in Japan: Kyoto Prefectural University of Medicine, Hyogo Medical University, Kurashiki Central Hospital, Saitama Red Cross Hospital, Fujita Health University School of Medicine, St. Marianna University School of Medicine, Osaka International Cancer Institute, Nagasaki University Hospital, Shimane University Faculty of Medicine, Japanese Red Cross Kyoto Daini Hospital, Japanese Red Cross Kyoto Daiichi Hospital, Saiseikai Suita Hospital, Fukuchiyama City Hospital, and Uji-Tokushukai Medical Center.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: patients with EGFR mutations; patients who received osimertinib, erlotinib plus bevacizumab, or erlotinib plus ramucirumab as first- or second-line treatment between November 2009 and October 2023; and patients with pleural effusion at the start of treatment. Patients who underwent drainage or pleurodesis for pleural effusion were included, as well as those with a clinically diagnosed MPE whose malignancy was not confirmed by cytology. Patients with squamous cell carcinoma were excluded.

Assessments

Patients were identified at each participating institution through a retrospective review of electronic medical records, using diagnosis codes for NSCLC and prescription records for osimertinib or erlotinib combined with bevacizumab or ramucirumab. Clinical information—including age, sex, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS), smoking history, disease stage, histological subtype, EGFR mutation status, pleural effusion volume, pleurodesis, pathological diagnosis, and treatment details—was extracted from the electronic medical records at each site. Pleural effusion volume was classified based on chest computed tomography (CT) scans, with a depth of 10 mm or less defined as “small” and greater than 10 mm defined as “large” (16,17). As this was a retrospective study, no standardized follow-up protocol was mandated. In routine clinical practice at the participating institutions, chest and abdominal CT was generally performed every 2 to 3 months, with additional imaging as clinically indicated. Pleural effusion status was assessed at each CT evaluation. MPE-PFS was operationally defined as the time from the initiation of first-line therapy to either MPE recurrence (defined as the re-accumulation/increase in pleural effusion volume following initial control, or an increase beyond the baseline level on chest CT as determined by the attending physician) or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. Patients without documented MPE recurrence or death were censored at the date of the last imaging assessment. Systemic PFS was defined as the time from treatment initiation to the date of objective systemic disease progression determined per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 or death from any cause. OS was defined as the time from the start of treatment until death from any cause. Adverse events (AEs) were classified by physicians at each institution according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0, and all grade ≥3 AEs were recorded. The data cutoff for follow-up was July 2, 2025.

Statistical analyses

Categorical variables are presented as percentages, while continuous variables are presented as the median and interquartile range (IQR). Continuous variables were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test, and categorical variables were compared using Fisher’s exact test. MPE-PFS, PFS, and OS were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Median survival times are presented with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Unadjusted hazard ratios (HRs) were estimated using univariable Cox proportional hazards regression models.

Of the 421 patients evaluated, 16 patients presenting with minor or compound EGFR mutations were excluded to ensure cohort homogeneity. Consequently, for the primary analysis, a multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression model was applied to the remaining unmatched cohort (n=405) to compare MPE-PFS, PFS, and OS between the osimertinib group and the group receiving erlotinib plus an angiogenesis inhibitor (erlotinib+A). Adjustments were made for the following baseline covariates: age (continuous), sex, ECOG PS (0–1 vs. 2–4), smoking history (never-smoker vs. former/current smoker), disease stage (stage IV vs. recurrence), EGFR mutation type (exon 19 deletion vs. L858R), pleural effusion volume (small vs. large), and brain metastasis (present vs. absent). The proportional hazards assumption was assessed using Schoenfeld residuals. To evaluate whether treatment effects differed by EGFR mutation type, an interaction term between the treatment regimen and the EGFR mutation type was included in the model. Separate subgroup analyses were performed for patients with exon 19 deletions and those with L858R mutations.

To confirm the robustness of the primary analysis results, propensity score matching (PSM) and inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) were performed as prespecified sensitivity analyses. Covariates for propensity score estimation included age [dichotomized at 65 years based on previous literature (5)], sex, ECOG PS, stage IV disease or postoperative recurrence, EGFR exon 19 deletion or exon 21 L858R mutation, smoking history, and brain metastasis. PSM was performed at a 5:1 ratio between the osimertinib group and the erlotinib + an angiogenesis inhibitor combination group using the nearest-neighbor method without replacement, with a caliper width set at 0.2 times the standard deviation (SD) of the propensity score logit. Matching was considered appropriate if the standardized mean difference (SMD) for each covariate was less than 0.1. For IPTW, stabilized weights were applied, and post-weighting covariate balance was assessed using the SMD (values less than 0.1 were considered acceptable).

Patients without evidence of disease progression or those still alive at the last follow-up were censored. All statistical tests were two-sided, with significance defined as P<0.05. All analyses were performed using R (version 4.5.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) in RStudio (version 2024.12.0 +467; Posit, PBC, Boston, MA, USA).

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 421 patients were included in the study, with four patients excluded due to squamous cell carcinoma. The median age was 73 years [interquartile range (IQR), 65–78 years]; and 272 patients (65%) were female. Of the patients, 279 (66%) were never smokers, and 417 (99%) had adenocarcinoma. Among the patients, 373 (89%) received osimertinib, while 48 (11%) received erlotinib plus bevacizumab or ramucirumab (Table 1). In the osimertinib group, EGFR subtypes included exon 19 deletion in 207 patients (55%) and exon 21 L858R in 154 patients (41%). In the erlotinib+A group, EGFR exon 19 deletion was present in 16 patients (33%) and EGFR exon 21 L858R in 32 patients (67%) (P=0.005). There were no significant differences between the two groups in terms of chest fluid volume (P=0.66) or the percentage of patients undergoing pleurodesis (P=0.84).

Among the 421 patients evaluated, 373 received osimertinib monotherapy and 48 received erlotinib+A. This significant asymmetry in sample size reflects the historical real-world prescription trends during the study period, during which the Japan Lung Cancer Society guidelines recommended osimertinib as the preferred first-line EGFR-TKI (recommendation grade 1) and categorized the erlotinib plus anti-angiogenic combination as an alternative option (recommendation grade 2). Furthermore, the distribution of treatment regimens varied considerably across participating institutions; of the 48 patients who received the erlotinib combination, 15 (31.3%) were treated at a single institution, reflecting a distinct center-specific preference for this regimen in patients presenting with baseline brain metastases or severe pleural effusion. The median duration of follow-up was 56.5 months (95% CI: 12.9–133.2 months).

Treatment efficacy

The median MPE-PFS was 22.7 months (95% CI: 19.2–26.5 months) for the osimertinib group and 18.3 months (95% CI: 12.6–30.2 months) for the erlotinib+A group [HR: 0.79; 95% CI: 0.55–1.13; P=0.20] (Figure 1A). There was no statistically significant differences in PFS [median (95% CI), osimertinib vs. erlotinib+A: 13.9 (12.6–16.7) vs. 10.8 (8.6–19.3) months; HR: 0.76; 95% CI: 0.53–1.07; P=0.12] or OS [median (95% CI), osimertinib vs. erlotinib+A, 29.4 (26.0–32.6) vs. 28.4 (19.9–51.1) months; HR: 0.99; 95% CI: 0.67–1.46; P=0.95] between the two groups (Figure 1B,1C).

In the primary multivariable analysis adjusting for baseline confounders, no significant differences were observed between the osimertinib and erlotinib+A groups for MPE-PFS (adjusted HR: 0.79; 95% CI: 0.54–1.15; P=0.22), PFS (adjusted HR: 0.87; 95% CI: 0.60–1.25; P=0.45), or OS (adjusted HR: 0.95; 95% CI: 0.63–1.44; P=0.83). The proportional hazards assumption was satisfied for all models (global P>0.30) (Figure 2).

Subanalysis based on EGFR subtypes

In patients with EGFR exon 19 deletion, the osimertinib group had a significantly longer pleural effusion control period than the erlotinib+A group [median (95% CI), 27.1 (21.3–30.9) vs. 12.6 (9.3–not estimable) months; HR: 0.42; 95% CI: 0.24–0.74; P=0.002] (Figure 3A). In contrast, for patients with EGFR exon 21 L858R mutation, there was no significant difference in the duration of pleural effusion control between the osimertinib and the erlotinib+A groups [median (95% CI), osimertinib vs. erlotinib+A, 19.3 (15.0–23.9) vs. 23.2 (13.6–30.2) months; HR: 1.13; 95% CI: 0.70–1.83; P=0.61] (Figure 3B). In patients with EGFR exon 19 deletion, PFS was significantly longer in the osimertinib group than in the erlotinib+A group [median (95% CI), 17.0 (13.5–20.6) vs. 10.3 (8.6–not estimable) months; HR: 0.49; 95% CI: 0.27–0.87; P=0.01], although OS did not differ between the two groups (HR: 0.69; 95% CI: 0.37–1.29; P=0.24) (Figure S1). For patients with EGFR exon 21 L858R, no differences in PFS or OS were observed between the groups (PFS: HR: 1.08, 95% CI: 0.69–1.70, P=0.73; OS: HR: 1.31, 95% CI: 0.78–2.19, P=0.31) (Figure S1).

In the multivariable analysis, patients with EGFR exon 19 deletions in the osimertinib group had a significantly lower risk of MPE recurrence (adjusted HR: 0.38; 95% CI: 0.21–0.67; P<0.001) and disease progression (adjusted HR: 0.47; 95% CI: 0.26–0.84; P=0.01) compared with the erlotinib+A group. OS showed a trend favoring osimertinib but did not reach statistical significance (adjusted HR: 0.58; 95% CI: 0.31–1.09; P=0.09). In patients with EGFR L858R mutations, no significant differences were observed between the two treatment groups across all endpoints (MPE-PFS: adjusted HR: 1.09, 95% CI: 0.66–1.80, P=0.75; PFS: adjusted HR: 1.04, 95% CI: 0.64–1.68, P=0.87; OS: adjusted HR: 1.20, 95% CI: 0.70–2.05, P=0.50), suggesting comparable efficacy between osimertinib and erlotinib+A therapy in this subgroup (Figure 2).

Subanalysis based on type of angiogenesis inhibitors

We further investigated whether the type of antiangiogenic agent influenced the duration of pleural effusion control or survival. In the comparison of osimertinib vs. erlotinib/ramucirumab, no significant differences were observed in MPE-PFS, PFS, or OS (MPE-PFS: HR: 1.03, 95% CI: 0.62–1.71, P=0.91; PFS: HR: 0.77, 95% CI: 0.49–1.20, P=0.24; OS: HR: 1.40, 95% CI: 0.78–2.51, P=0.25) (Figure 3C, Figure S2). In the comparison of osimertinib vs. erlotinib/bevacizumab, MPE-PFS was significantly shorter in the erlotinib/bevacizumab group, while no difference in PFS or OS was observed between the two groups (MPE-PFS: HR: 0.58, 95% CI: 0.36–0.94, P=0.03; PFS: HR: 0.75, 95% CI: 0.44–1.26, P=0.27; OS: HR: 0.67, 95% CI: 0.41–1.12, P=0.13) (Figure 3D, Figure S2).

In the multivariable analysis, these findings were consistent. The erlotinib/ramucirumab group showed comparable outcomes to the osimertinib group across all endpoints (MPE-PFS: adjusted HR: 1.05; 95% CI: 0.62–1.77; P=0.86; PFS: adjusted HR: 0.88, 95% CI: 0.55–1.39, P=0.57; OS: adjusted HR: 1.35, 95% CI: 0.74–2.44, P=0.33). In the erlotinib/bevacizumab comparison, the osimertinib group demonstrated significantly longer MPE-PFS after adjustment (adjusted HR: 0.60; 95% CI: 0.37–0.99; P=0.045), while PFS and OS remained comparable between the groups (PFS: adjusted HR: 0.90, 95% CI: 0.52–1.55, P=0.70; OS: adjusted HR: 0.69, 95% CI: 0.41–1.16, P=0.17).

Sensitivity analysis

We performed sensitivity analyses using PSM and IPTW to validate the robustness of the primary multivariable Cox regression findings. Following 5:1 PSM, 201 patients in the osimertinib group and 47 patients in the erlotinib+A group were included in the analysis. The baseline characteristics were well-balanced, with all SMDs less than 0.1 (Table 1, Figure S3). In the PSM cohort, the osimertinib group showed comparable outcomes to the erlotinib+A group (MPE-PFS: adjusted HR: 0.82, 95% CI: 0.55–1.21, P=0.32; PFS: adjusted HR: 0.81, 95% CI: 0.55–1.18, P=0.27; OS: adjusted HR: 0.95, 95% CI: 0.62–1.45, P=0.80) (Figure S4). In the EGFR exon 19 deletion subgroup, the osimertinib group exhibited significantly longer MPE-PFS (adjusted HR: 0.41, 95% CI: 0.21–0.77, P=0.006) and PFS (adjusted HR: 0.51, 95% CI: 0.27–0.97, P=0.04) than the erlotinib+A group, whereas no significant difference was observed in the L858R subgroup (Figure S5). These findings were consistent with the primary analysis. In the comparison with individual erlotinib-based regimens within the PSM cohort, the erlotinib/bevacizumab group showed a trend toward shorter MPE-PFS compared with the osimertinib group (adjusted HR: 0.62; 95% CI: 0.37–1.05; P=0.08), while the erlotinib/ramucirumab group demonstrated comparable pleural effusion control and survival outcomes to the osimertinib group (Figure S6).

IPTW analysis yielded largely consistent results with greater statistical power, as this approach retains all patients in the analysis. In the overall cohort, IPTW demonstrated statistically significant improvements in MPE-PFS (adjusted HR: 0.63; 95% CI: 0.46–0.86; P=0.004) and PFS (adjusted HR: 0.73; 95% CI: 0.54–0.98; P=0.04) favoring the osimertinib group, which were not observed in the primary multivariable analysis or PSM (Figure S7). In the exon 19 deletion subgroup, the superiority of osimertinib was more pronounced, with significant differences observed not only in MPE-PFS and PFS but also in OS (adjusted HR: 0.56; 95% CI: 0.33–0.94; P=0.03). All other subgroup results, including the L858R subgroup and individual erlotinib-based regimen comparisons, were consistent across the three analytical approaches (Figures S8,S9). This isolated significance in the overall cohort via IPTW likely stems from the increased statistical power achieved by retaining the full weighted sample; however, these exploratory results should be interpreted with caution given the non-significant differences observed in the primary multivariable Cox model.

AEs

The incidence of all grade 3 or higher AEs was higher in the erlotinib+A group than in the osimertinib group [77 (21%) vs. 18 (38%), P=0.02]. Among the three treatment groups, the erlotinib/ramucirumab group had the highest incidence of grade 3 or higher AEs [osimertinib group, erlotinib/bevacizumab group, and erlotinib/ramucirumab group: 77 (21%) vs. 6 (33%) vs. 12 (41%), P=0.02] (Table 2). The erlotinib+A group had higher rates of liver damage and diarrhea than the other two groups, at 14% and 10%, respectively. The osimertinib group had the highest incidence of all grades of interstitial pneumonia [osimertinib group and erlotinib+A group: 78 (21%) vs. 3 (6%), P=0.01; osimertinib group, erlotinib/bevacizumab group, and erlotinib/ ramucirumab group: 78 (21%) vs. 1 (5%) vs. 2 (7%), P=0.06] (Figure S10).

Discussion

MPE is a common and challenging complication in patients with advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC. Clinical studies have reported that anti-angiogenic agents may help control pleural effusion (18,19). To our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate whether adding antiangiogenic therapy is necessary for pleural effusion control in patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC complicated by pleural effusion. The findings of the present study indicate that osimertinib monotherapy, the current standard of care for EGFR-mutated NSCLC, demonstrates comparable efficacy in terms of MPE-PFS, PFS, and OS relative to the combination regimen of erlotinib+A. Notably, treatment efficacy varied based on EGFR subtype, with osimertinib showing a significantly advantage in pleural effusion control and PFS in patients with EGFR exon19 deletions.

In cases of lung cancer, particularly metastatic tumors associated with pleural effusion, serum and pleural fluid concentrations of VEGF are significantly higher than in other conditions involving pleural effusion, such as heart failure, tuberculosis, and infectious diseases. VEGF is a key mediator in the formation of MPE (20,21). While historical prospective trials, such as JO25567 and NEJ026, demonstrated that combining bevacizumab with first-generation erlotinib significantly improved PFS in patients with pleural effusion, our study revealed that osimertinib monotherapy achieved comparable pleural effusion control and survival outcomes without direct anti-angiogenic co-administration.

A key molecular mechanism underlying this similar effectiveness is the indirect regulation of angiogenesis through the EGFR downstream pathway. In tumor cells harboring oncogenic EGFR mutations, constitutive hyperactivation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway directly drives the upregulation of VEGF expression. Preclinical data have demonstrated that while first-generation TKIs like gefitinib or erlotinib partially weaken VEGF production depending on the specific mutation phenotype (22-24), complete suppression of the angiogenic drive is frequently incomplete. Because osimertinib possesses substantially higher potency and selectivity for mutant EGFR than first-generation agents, it achieves a more thorough shutdown of the downstream PI3K/AKT/VEGF axis. This thorough cascade inhibition exerts a potent “indirect anti-angiogenic effect”, which may effectively replace direct extracellular VEGF blockade. From a clinical perspective, the SPIRAL II trial, a single-arm phase II study evaluating osimertinib plus bevacizumab in patients with malignant pleural or pericardial effusion, failed to meet its primary endpoint (1-year PFS rate: 32.1%; predetermined threshold: 43.5%), suggesting that the addition of bevacizumab does not provide meaningful additive benefit to osimertinib in this setting (14). Additionally, a retrospective study on osimertinib revealed that the presence of MPE had a minimal effect on the drug’s efficacy (25,26).

The median OS observed in this study was notably longer than the values reported in observational studies of first-generation EGFR-TKIs for EGFR-mutated NSCLC with pleural effusion (median OS: 17.4 and 19.5 months) (10,27). These findings are consistent with the results of randomized phase III trials showing that osimertinib monotherapy or the combination of erlotinib and an anti-angiogenic agent prolongs OS compared with first-generation TKI monotherapy (4,28,29). This suggests that treatment with osimertinib or erlotinib in combination with an anti-angiogenic agent prolongs OS compared with first-generation TKIs, even in patients with pleural effusion. In addition, the availability of multiple lines of subsequent therapy in EGFR-mutant NSCLC may have further contributed to the favorable OS observed in this cohort.

Our subgroup analysis showed that osimertinib monotherapy was associated with significantly longer MPE-PFS and PFS compared with the erlotinib plus anti-angiogenic combination regimen in patients harboring EGFR exon 19 deletions, whereas no significant difference was observed in patients with the L858R mutation. Such mutation-specific patterns are consistent with the findings of previous prospective trials. In the RELAY trial, adding ramucirumab to erlotinib improved PFS in both subgroups with similar HRs (ex19del: HR: 0.65; L858R: HR: 0.62), effectively eliminating the efficacy disparity typically seen with EGFR-TKI monotherapy (30). Similarly, the NEJ026 trial demonstrated improved PFS with the combination of bevacizumab and erlotinib; however, this effect was particularly notable in the L858R subgroup, while being limited in patients with exon 19 deletions. These findings suggest that anti-angiogenic agents may compensate for the lower sensitivity to EGFR-TKI monotherapy observed in L858R-mutant tumors compared with those with exon 19 deletions.

The molecular mechanisms underlying these differences in response are thought to reflect the distinct structural characteristics of these two mutation types. Exon 19 deletion-mutant EGFR maintains a more stable active conformation and binds to EGFR-TKIs with higher affinity than L858R-mutant EGFR, resulting in more complete inhibition of downstream signaling (31,32). Indeed, Hung et al. demonstrated that gefitinib inhibited VEGF production by 37.1% in L858R-mutant cells, compared to 56% in exon 19 deletion-mutant cells (22); this suggests that in tumors with the del19 mutation, the VEGF pathway is already markedly weakened by EGFR-TKI monotherapy, leaving limited room for additional benefit from anti-angiogenic agents. In contrast, L858R-mutant tumors retain VEGF pathway activation due to relatively lower EGFR inhibition efficiency, potentially allowing for greater benefit from combination therapy with anti-angiogenic agents. Importantly, the clear superiority of osimertinib observed within the del19 subgroup was not reflected in the primary analysis of the overall cohort (n=405, after excluding 16 patients with minor or co-occurring EGFR mutations). This discrepancy is mathematically logical, as the marked therapeutic benefit observed within the del19 subset was diluted by the prevalence of the L858R population, which showed no significant difference between treatment groups, within the full primary analysis population. Taken together, these findings suggest that the EGFR mutation subtype may be a crucial factor when selecting first-line treatment for EGFR-mutant NSCLC presenting with massive MPE. Specifically, osimertinib monotherapy may be preferable for patients with exon 19 deletions, whereas combination therapy involving an EGFR-TKI and an anti-angiogenic agent may be worth considering for patients with the L858R mutation.

Additionally, our findings suggest that ramucirumab may be more effective than bevacizumab in controlling pleural effusion. The differences observed between the two antiangiogenic agents may be partly attributed to their distinct mechanisms of antiangiogenic activity. A previous report observed increased expression of VEGF receptor (VEGFR)2 and VEGFA in human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) cultured with MPE, while changes in VEGFR1 expression were not significant (33). These findings suggest that VEGFR2 plays a more significant role than VEGFR1 in VEGF-induced cell proliferation and angiogenesis. Therefore, ramucirumab, a VEGFR2 inhibitor, may be a more effective treatment option for pleural effusion. However, given that these results are from a subanalysis, further validation in larger cohorts is warranted.

Regarding AEs, the incidence of grade 3 or higher events in this study was 21% for osimertinib, 33% for erlotinib/bevacizumab, and 41% for erlotinib/ramucirumab, which are lower than those reported in previous trials: 42% for osimertinib, 88% for erlotinib/bevacizumab, and 76% for erlotinib/ramucirumab (4,13,28). However, interstitial pneumonia was more frequent than in the FLAURA trial (all grades: 21% vs. 2%; grade ≥3: 8% vs. 1%), with grade ≥3 interstitial lung disease (ILD) developing in 29 patients (8%), including 4 fatal (grade 5) cases within the osimertinib cohort. This higher incidence may be attributable to the exclusively Japanese population in our study, which has a well-documented higher susceptibility to EGFR-TKI-induced ILD compared with non-Asian populations (34-36). Additionally, patients with MPE frequently present with concurrent pulmonary parenchymal abnormalities such as lymphangitic carcinomatosis, whose radiological features can overlap with those of drug-induced ILD, potentially leading to overdiagnosis (37,38). These findings highlight the need for careful risk-benefit assessment when selecting first-line therapy in this population, and prospective studies with systematic baseline ILD screening are warranted to clarify the true incidence and risk factors.

This study has several limitations. First, as a retrospective analysis, we cannot rule out the possibility that factors other than treatment efficacy influenced the results. Second, the erlotinib+A group had a smaller sample size the osimertinib group, which may have reduced statistical power. To address this, we implement rigorous statistical methods using PSM and IPTW sensitivity analyses to enhance the robustness of our research findings. Third, although this study was reliable based on the long follow-up period, the follow-up period for erlotinib/ramucirumab was shorter than that for the other regimens, which may have influenced the results. Fourth, this study did not systematically evaluate baseline interstitial pneumonia, a known risk factor for EGFR-TKI-induced ILD. This limitation makes it impossible to analyze whether pre-existing interstitial changes contributed to the high incidence of ILD observed in the osimertinib group and also restricts the interpretation of findings related to ILD. Fifth, the significant imbalance in sample sizes across the entire cohort (373 patients in the osimertinib group vs. 48 in the erlotinib+A group) reflects the preferential recommendation of osimertinib in Japanese guidelines. This inherent imbalance, driven by contemporary guideline recommendations and unmeasured institutional prescribing preferences, potentially introduces selection bias and limits the statistical power of the comparative analyses. Because guidelines and real-world treatment paradigms continuously evolve, these findings should be interpreted with caution, as this retrospective study design cannot fully eliminate residual confounding linked to the clinical rationale driving historical regimen selection.

Conclusions

In patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC complicated by pleural effusion, erlotinib combined with antiangiogenic therapy was not superior to osimertinib in MPE-PFS, PFS, and OS in patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC. Subanalyses suggested that osimertinib was superior erlotinib+A therapy in patients with EGFR exon 19 deletions, while erlotinib/bevacizumab showed inferior efficacy compared to osimertinib in controlling MPE.
