Poster 101. High Functional Recovery and Low Complication Rates After Percutaneous Knotless Bridge Achilles Repair in Athletes
Authors: Kyle Huntley, Kevin D Martin, Nicolas Kuttner, Maggie Wolodkevich, Aymin Bahhur, Adam Groth
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:Achilles tendon ruptures can be devastating injuries in the physically active, with both functional and psychological impacts. A key goal for this population is a return to pre-injury activity levels. Various repair techniques are utilized, including percutaneous methods. However, these typically rely on surgical knots for direct repair, which increases the risk of failure. The percutaneous knotless bridge technique for Achilles tendon repair has gained recent popularity. This method combines a minimally invasive approach with intraosseous fixation to reduce potential failure points, while preserving the paratenon and surrounding soft tissue (Figure 1). Although this technique has shown promise in biomechanical studies, patient outcome data remain limited - particularly with return to sport and daily function. The purpose of study is to analyze the return to sport status, patient satisfaction, clinical outcomes, and complications following this technique.Methods:This retrospective cohort study includes acute Achilles tendon ruptures repaired with a percutaneous knotless bridge technique at an academic medical center from 2020 - 2024. Postoperative clinic notes and physical therapy documentation were reviewed to assess rehabilitation progress, functional outcomes, and complications. Patient-reported outcomes were obtained via telephone interview. The primary outcomes include return to sports status, level of play compared to pre-injury status, Foot and Ankle Disability Index score (FADI), Achilles Tendon Total Rupture Score (ATRS), visual analogue scale (VAS) score, and complication rate. Secondary outcomes include activity at injury, time from injury to surgery, and incidence of heel pain. All repairs followed a standardized rehabilitation protocol.Results:101 acute Achilles tendon ruptures in 100 patients were included in the initial review, with 94.1% of tears occurring during a sporting event. The most common rupture mechanism was basketball (44.6%). Most patients were male (86.1%) with a mean age of 35.7 (range, 18-70) years and a BMI of 28.9 (range, 19.2-45.3) kg/m². Mean follow-up was 16.7 months (range, 6 weeks-53 months). Of the 49 patients with documented return-to-sport status, 85.1% resumed their preoperative sport or activity, with an average return time of 32.9 weeks (SD 18.8). Among these, 83.3% reported playing at the same or better level compared to their pre-injury status. On logistic regression, there was a trend toward lower return rates with higher BMI, although this did not reach statistical significance (OR = 0.93 per BMI unit, p = 0.11). Neither age (OR = 0.92; p = 0.41) nor time to surgery (OR = 1.01; p = 0.88) significantly impacted return to sport. However, nicotine use was significantly associated with a lower return rate (62.5% vs 90.2%; p = 0.0398). The mean ATRS was 95.3 and FADI 96.9%, both classified as highly functional. Pain at 3, 6, and 9 months was 1.3, 1.1, and 0.5, respectively (VAS; 1-10). 95.7% of patients reported a full return to daily activities without a walking boot at 12 weeks. Among patients with at least 3 months of follow-up, 14.6% (14/96) reported at least some level of heel pain at final assessment. The major complication rate was 3% (3/101), including infection with return to the operating room (n=1), DVT (n=1), and re-rupture (n=1). At final follow-up, patients reported an overall satisfaction rate of 96.1%.Conclusions:The percutaneous, knotless mid-substance bridge technique is effective and associated with minimal complications, including a low re-rupture rate of 1%. These findings indicate favorable clinical outcomes, with high rates of functional recovery and return to sport in an athletic population. Future research will aim to compare this percutaneous bridge technique with other commonly used repair methods.
