Embedded Trefftz DG method for reaction–diffusion problems on anisotropic meshes
Sergio Gómez, Chiara Perinati, Paul Stocker, Igor Voulis

Abstract
We present and analyze an embedded Trefftz discontinuous Galerkin method for reaction–diffusion problems on anisotropic meshes. The method is constructed by imposing a relaxed local Trefftz condition via an embedding into a tensor-product DG space, yielding a reduced global system while preserving the approximation properties of the underlying high-order discretization. We prove stability and quasi-optimality on anisotropic, possibly curved, quadrilateral elements, and derive anisotropic a priori error estimates. Numerical experiments for h h - and h ​ p hp -refinement, including curved-domain examples, validate the theoretical results.



Keywords.

Anisotropic meshes, embedded Trefftz methods, discontinuous Galerkin methods, error analysis.

Anisotropic mesh refinement is a classical tool for resolving directional features, layers, and strongly nonuniform solution behavior,

DG methods provide a flexible framework for the design of high-order methods on general meshes.
Trefftz DG methods
reduce the number of degrees of freedom by using local approximation spaces that are piecewise contained in the kernel of the underlying differential operator.
Trefftz DG methods have been widely studied in the context of wave propagation problems; cf.
[

,
16
,

For more general elliptic partial differential equations (PDEs), however, the construction of suitable Trefftz-type spaces is much less straightforward.
Some recent progress in this direction has been achieved by quasi-Trefftz techniques; see, e.g.,
[
17
,
24

.

The
embedded Trefftz
approach, introduced in
[
21
,
23
]
, overcomes this difficulty in a systematic way by constructing a suitable embedding, which embeds a subspace with a relaxed Trefftz property into a standard DG space.
It thereby provides a general framework for Trefftz-type DG methods that remains effective even when the kernel of the PDE operator is not explicitly known, or is trivial.
Such a technique has been successfully applied to several classes of PDEs, including elliptic problems, wave propagation, and fluid dynamics problems; see
[

,

,

Contributions.

In this work, we study an embedded Trefftz discontinuous Galerkin method for singularly perturbed reaction–diffusion problems on anisotropic quadrilateral meshes.
The method combines the approximation advantages of anisotropic DG discretizations with the reduction of globally coupled degrees of freedom provided by the embedded Trefftz construction.

The main novelty is the design of a
local test space compatible with tensor-product polynomial bases
.
This yields stability of the embedded Trefftz formulation in the anisotropic setting and
provides a
first systematic route to embedded Trefftz methods formulated in tensor-product polynomial spaces
, rather than in standard (simplicial) polynomial bases, thereby
opening the door to corresponding constructions on hexahedral meshes.

The analysis of the scheme combines tools from the embedded Trefftz DG abstract framework
[
20
]
and the anisotropic setting
[
6
]
.
We prove well-posedness and quasi-optimality of the method on anisotropic, possibly curved, quadrilateral elements, and derive anisotropic a priori error estimates.
Numerical experiments illustrate the performance of the method under
h
- and
hp
-refinement, including curved domain examples and comparisons with the standard DG scheme.

1.1
Model problem

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain with boundary
\partial\Omega
.
Given a diffusivity parameter
\varepsilon>0
, a source term
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, and a Dirichlet datum
g\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
, we consider the following reaction–diffusion boundary value problem: find
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{2}\Lap u+u=f

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(1.1)

\displaystyle u=g

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega.

Let
H^{1}_{g}(\Omega):=\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)\mid v_{|_{\partial\Omega}}=g\}
denote the convex set of
H^{1}(\Omega)
functions satisfying the Dirichlet condition in the trace sense.
The variational formulation of (
1.1
) reads as follows: find
u\in H^{1}_{g}(\Omega)
such that

a(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+uv\right)=\int_{\Omega}fv\qquad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

The bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is coercive and continuous with respect to the energy norm

(\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. By the Lax–Milgram theorem, the variational problem (
1.1
) admits a unique weak solution
u\in H^{1}_{g}(\Omega)
.

We are particularly interested in the singularly perturbed regime
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, where the diffusion term is dominated by the reaction term. In this case, the solution may exhibit sharp boundary layers and strongly anisotropic features. Standard isotropic meshes may therefore become inefficient, whereas anisotropic mesh refinement can resolve such localized features more effectively with fewer degrees of freedom.

\mathbb{V}_{h}(K):=\{v\in L^{2}(K)\mid(v\circ G_{K})\in\mathbb{P}^{p}_{\otimes}(\widetilde{K})\}.

\mathbb{V}_{\!h}:=\prod_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\mathbb{V}_{h}(K).

Here, and throughout the article, the subscript
h
is used to denote the discrete quantities for simplicity. The analysis is carried out in terms of the elemental mesh sizes
h_{1,K}
and
h_{2,K}
.

2.2
DG formulation

Let
\mathbb{V}_{*h}:=\mathbb{V}_{\!h}+H^{2}(\Omega)
.
We consider the following interior penalty DG formulation:

\text{Find }u_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{\!h}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=\ell_{h}(v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{\!h},

where the bilinear form
a_{h}:\mathbb{V}_{*h}\times\mathbb{V}_{\!h}\to\mathbb{R}
and the linear functional
\ell_{h}:\mathbb{V}_{\!h}\to\mathbb{R}
are given by

\displaystyle a_{h}(w,v_{h})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\int_{K}(\varepsilon^{2}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v_{h}+wv_{h})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{I}}}\int_{e}(-\{\!\!\{\varepsilon^{2}\nabla w\}\!\!\}_{h_{\perp}^{e}}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]-\{\!\!\{\varepsilon^{2}\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\}_{h_{\perp}^{e}}\cdot[\![w]\!]+\sigma\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{h^{+}_{\perp}+h^{-}_{\perp}}[\![w]\!]\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!])

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{D}}}\int_{e}(-\varepsilon^{2}\nabla w\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Omega}v_{h}-\varepsilon^{2}\nabla v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Omega}w+\sigma\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{h_{\perp}}wv_{h}),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\ell_{h}(v_{h})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\int_{K}fv_{h}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{D}}}\int_{e}g(-\varepsilon^{2}\nabla v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Omega}+\sigma\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{h_{\perp}}v_{h}),

(2.5)

with
\sigma>0
a “sufficiently large” dimensionless penalty parameter.

Remark 2.1

(Stability term and weighted averages)
.

Our choice of the stability term differs from the one used in
[
7
, Lemma 8.1]
. In that work, the
h_{\perp}
-dependent factor
(1/h_{\perp}^{+}+1/h_{\perp}^{-})
is used, which results in a strong dependence of the local contribution from a single element
to the energy norm on the adjacent edge lengths of both neighboring elements. That strong dependence would complicate the stability analysis of the proposed embedded Trefftz DG method, which relies on the study of such local contributions.

On the other hand, the
h_{\perp}
-dependent factor we employ can be easily bounded as

\frac{1}{h_{\perp}^{+}+h_{\perp}^{-}}\leq\frac{1}{h_{\perp}^{\pm}},

thus avoiding the issue mentioned above. Accordingly, the weighted-average parameters in (
2.3
) are defined to guarantee the stability of the global bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

2.3
Embedded Trefftz DG formulation

The embedded Trefftz DG method is obtained by restricting the
discrete solution to a subspace of
\mathbb{V}_{\!h}
whose elements satisfy,
in a weak sense,
equation (
1.1
) elementwise.
The construction of the embedded Trefftz space relies on two key ingredients on each element
K\in\mathcal{K}
: a local differential operator
A_{K}
and a suitably designed test space
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}
.
On the pullback element
\widetilde{K}=\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\widetilde{K}_{2}=G_{K}^{-1}(K)
, we define

\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K}):=\{\tilde{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}^{p}_{\otimes}(\widetilde{K})\mid\tilde{v}_{h}|_{\partial\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\widetilde{K}_{2}}=0\}.

(2.6)

This space consists of tensor-product polynomials on
\widetilde{K}
that vanish on the two longer edges, i.e., the edges parallel to the
\widetilde{x}_{2}
-axis.
We define the local test space on the physical element
K
as the pushforward of
\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
:

\mathbb{L}_{h}(K):=\{v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{\!h}(K)\mid(v_{h}\circ G_{K})\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})\}.

Remark 2.2

(Design of the test space)
.

The choice of the test space
\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
in (
2.6
) is motivated by the fact that functions in this space satisfy a Poincaré–Steklov inequality which involves only the shorter edge length of the element, see (
3.9
) below.
This is crucial for the local stability
analysis of
Section

3.1.1
.
In two dimensions the
H^{1}
-norm along the longer edge can be bounded by the
H^{1}
-seminorm along the shorter edge, in the following sense

\|\partial_{2}\tilde{u}\|_{\widetilde{K}}\lesssim\frac{h_{1,K}}{h_{2,K}}\|\partial_{1}\tilde{u}\|_{\widetilde{K}}
, for all
\tilde{u}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K}).

Details are given in
Lemma

3.2
.
We want to highlight, that in higher dimensions, this remains true, with the
H^{1}
-seminorm involving only the derivatives along the shorter edge.

Denoting by
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)^{\prime}

the dual space of
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)
, we define the local reaction–diffusion operator
{A_{K}}:\mathbb{V}_{*h}|_{K}\to\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)^{\prime}
as

A_{K}v:=-\varepsilon^{2}\Lap v+v\qquad\forall v\in\mathbb{V}_{*h}|_{K}.

Then the local embedded Trefftz space is defined by

\mathbb{T}_{\mspace{-1.5mu}h}(K):=\{v_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{\!h}(K)\mid({A_{K}}v_{h},q_{h})_{K}=0\quad\forall q_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)\},

and the global space is given by
\mathbb{T}_{\mspace{-1.5mu}h}:=\prod_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\mathbb{T}_{\mspace{-1.5mu}h}(K)
.
A straightforward dimension count gives

\operatorname{dim}\big(\mathbb{T}_{h}(K)\big)=\operatorname{dim}\big(\mathbb{P}^{p}_{\otimes}(K)\big)-\operatorname{dim}\big(\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)\big)=(p+1)^{2}-(p+1)(p-1)=2p+2.

Note that the dimension of the embedded Trefftz space grows only linearly
in
p
, in contrast to the
(p+1)^{2}
degrees of freedom of the full
tensor-product space
\mathbb{P}^{p}_{\otimes}(K)
. This leads to a significant reduction of the globally coupled
degrees of freedom, see Table
1
.

The embedded Trefftz DG method reads

\displaystyle\text{Find }u_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{\!h}\text{ such that }

\displaystyle\quad({A_{K}}u_{h},v_{\mathbb{L}})_{K}

\displaystyle=\ell_{K}(v_{\mathbb{L}})

\displaystyle\forall v_{\mathbb{L}}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(K),\ \forall K\in\mathcal{K},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\text{ and }

\displaystyle\quad a_{h}(u_{h},v_{\mathbb{T}})

\displaystyle=\ell_{h}(v_{\mathbb{T}})

\displaystyle\forall v_{\mathbb{T}}\in\mathbb{T}_{\mspace{-1.5mu}h},

where
\ell_{K}(v):=(f,v)_{K}
, and
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\ell_{h}(\cdot)
are defined in (
2.2
) and (
2.5
), respectively.

On the implementation.

The discrete formulation (
2.7
) is used for the analysis but not in the implementation.
In practice, the Trefftz space
\mathbb{T}_{\mspace{-1.5mu}h}
is never explicitly constructed.
Instead, we build an embedding based on the singular value decomposition (SVD) of the small matrices representing the local operators
A_{K}
. We explicitly construct the local test space
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)
and its pullback
\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
, as they are required to compute the SVD, and thus to construct the embedding.
We stress that the global degrees of freedom that remain after the embedding are only those associated with
the Trefftz space, leading to a significant reduction of the computational cost of the method.
Further implementation details are provided in
Section

4
, and we also refer to
[
21
]
.

3
Analysis of the embedded Trefftz DG method

The analysis proceeds in several steps.
In
Section

3.1
, we study stability and continuity of the local operator
A_{K}
, and in
Section

3.2
, we
prove coercivity and continuity of the global bilinear form

a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
.
Following the framework of
[
20
]
, these results imply well-posedness and quasi-optimality of the embedded Trefftz DG method (
2.7
); the corresponding statements are presented in
Section

3.3
.
Finally, in
Section

3.4
, we derive anisotropic a priori error estimates.

In the rest of the paper, we use the notation
\lesssim
for inequalities where we
omit
generic constants that are
independent of
\varepsilon
,
h_{1,K}
,
h_{2,K}
for all
K\in\mathcal{K}
, and of any functions involved in the estimate.

For all
v\in\mathbb{V}_{*h}=\mathbb{V}_{\!h}+H^{2}(\Omega)
, we define the following mesh-dependent norms:

\begin{split}&\left\|v\right\|_{\mathbb{V}_{\!h}}^{2}:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\left\|\nabla v\right\|^{2}_{K}+\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{K}\right)+\left|v\right|_{\mathrm{J}}^{2},\quad\left|v\right|_{\mathrm{J}}^{2}:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{I}}}\int_{e}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{h^{+}_{\perp}+h^{-}_{\perp}}[\![v]\!]^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathcal{D}}}\int_{e}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{h_{\perp}}v^{2},\\
&\left\|v\right\|_{\mathbb{V}_{*h}}^{2}:=\left\|v\right\|_{\mathbb{V}_{\!h}}^{2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\sum_{e\subset\partial K}h_{\perp}^{e,\widetilde{K}}\left\|\varepsilon\nabla v\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\|^{2}_{e}.\end{split}

(3.1)

For each
K\in\mathcal{K}
, we define a norm
in the pullback test space

\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
by

\|\tilde{v}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})}:=\left\|\tilde{v}\right\|^{2}_{\widetilde{K}}+\varepsilon^{2}\left\|\partial_{1}\tilde{v}\right\|^{2}_{\widetilde{K}}\qquad\forall\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K}),

(3.2)

and equip the test space
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)
with the norm

\|v\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)}:=\|v\circ G_{K}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})}
.
The dual space
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)^{\prime}
is endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)^{\prime}}:=\sup_{v\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle\cdot,v\rangle}{\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)}}
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality pairing between
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)^{\prime}
and
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)
.

We follow
the abstract framework of embedded Trefftz DG methods developed in
[
20
]
, and adapt it to the present setting.
Well-posedness of problem (
2.7
) holds under the following local and global properties:

Local properties.

There exist constants
c_{A},C_{A}>0
, independent of
h_{1,K}
and
h_{2,K}
for all
K\in\mathcal{K}
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|{A_{K}}v_{h}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq c_{A}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{\mathbb{V}_{\!h}}

\displaystyle\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(K),\ \forall K\in\mathcal{K},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\left\|{A_{K}}v\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{A}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathbb{V}_{*h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\forall v\in\mathbb{V}_{*h}.

(3.4)

Global properties.

There exist constants
c_{a},C_{a}>0
, independent of
h_{1,K}
and
h_{2,K}
for all
K\in\mathcal{K}
, such that

\displaystyle a_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\geq c_{a}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{\mathbb{V}_{\!h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{T}_{\mspace{-1.5mu}h},

(3.5)

\displaystyle a_{h}(v,v_{h})

\displaystyle\leq C_{a}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathbb{V}_{*h}}\,\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{\mathbb{V}_{\!h}}

\displaystyle\forall v\in\mathbb{V}_{*h},\ \forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{T}_{\mspace{-1.5mu}h}.

(3.6)

We recall the following anisotropic trace inequality from
[
6
, Corollary 3.49]
, which will be used in the analysis.

Lemma 3.1

(Anisotropic trace inequality)
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}
be a mesh element and let
\widetilde{K}=\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\widetilde{K}_{2}=G_{K}^{-1}(K)
be its pullback. Then

\left\|\tilde{v}\right\|_{\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\partial\widetilde{K}_{2}}^{2}\lesssim h_{2,K}^{-1}\left\|\tilde{v}\right\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \left\|\tilde{v}\right\|_{\partial\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\widetilde{K}_{2}}^{2}\lesssim h_{1,K}^{-1}\left\|\tilde{v}\right\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\quad\forall\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{P}^{p}_{\otimes}(\widetilde{K}).

(3.7)

3.1
Local problem

To establish the local stability (
3.3
) of the operator
A_{K}
on
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)
, we first prove in
Section

3.1.1
that (
3.3
)
holds for a prototype operator on
\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
.
Via the perturbation argument of Lemma
A.1
, we then show in
Section

3.1.2
that a pulled-back operator inherits
this stability, which then yields
stability of
A_{K}
on the physical element.
Finally, continuity of
A_{K}
is established in
Section

3.1.3
.

Before proceeding, we state two preliminary results that are used
throughout the analysis.

The specific construction of the test space
\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
allows us to prove the following anisotropic inverse inequality, which is crucial for the stability analysis of the method.

Lemma 3.2

(Anisotropic inverse inequality for
\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
)
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}
be a mesh element and let
\widetilde{K}=\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\widetilde{K}_{2}=G_{K}^{-1}(K)
be its pullback.
Then, there exists a constant
C_{\eqref{ainv}}>0
independent of
h_{1,K}
and
h_{2,K}
such that

\|\partial_{2}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}\leq C_{\eqref{ainv}}\frac{h_{1,K}}{h_{2,K}}\|\partial_{1}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}\quad\forall\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K}).

(3.8)

Proof.

Since
\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
vanishes
on
\partial\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\widetilde{K}_{2}
,
the Poincaré–Steklov inequality on the interval

\widetilde{K}_{1}
yields

\|\partial_{1}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}=\int_{\widetilde{K}_{2}}\|\partial_{1}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}_{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{x}_{2}\gtrsim h_{1,K}^{-2}\|\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}.

(3.9)

Moreover, by standard polynomial inverse estimates, we have

\|\partial_{2}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}=\int_{\widetilde{K}_{1}}\|\partial_{2}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}_{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{x}_{1}\lesssim h_{2,K}^{-2}\|\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}.

Combining these two inequalities gives the result.
∎

The following
lemma strengthens the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the second derivative of polynomials.
The constant
C_{p}
in (
3.10
) below depends adversely on the polynomial degree
p
, and it is expected to approach
0
as
p\to\infty
.
However, for any fixed
p
,

C_{p}
is strictly larger than
0
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Given a polynomial degree
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
we have

|(\phi^{\prime\prime},\phi)_{[0,1]}|\leq(1-C_{p})\,\left\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{[0,1]}\,\left\|\phi\right\|_{[0,1]}\qquad\forall\phi\in\mathbb{P}^{p}([0,1]),

(3.10)

for some constant
0<C_{p}<1
depending only on
p
.

Proof.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

|(\phi^{\prime\prime},\phi)_{[0,1]}|\;\leq\;\left\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{[0,1]}\,\left\|\phi\right\|_{[0,1]}\qquad\forall\phi\in\mathbb{P}^{p}([0,1]).

(3.11)

Equality holds if and only if
\phi
satisfies
\phi^{\prime\prime}=\lambda\phi
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e., if
\phi
is an eigenfunction of the differential operator
u\mapsto u^{\prime\prime}
. However, the only
eigenfunctions of such an operator are trigonometric or exponential functions (or affine functions for
\lambda=0
).
Apart from the trivial affine case, none of these are polynomials.
Hence, on the compact set

S:=\{\phi\in\mathbb{P}^{p}([0,1])\mid\left\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\right\|_{[0,1]}=1\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{p}([0,1]),

no
\phi\in S
achieves equality in (
3.11
).
Now, define the following maximum over
S
:

s_{p}:=\max_{\phi\in S}\frac{|(\phi^{\prime\prime},\phi)_{[0,1]}|}{\left\|\phi\right\|_{[0,1]}}<1.

Setting
C_{p}:=1-s_{p}
yields (
3.10
).
∎

3.1.1
Local stability on the pullback element

Given
K\in\mathcal{K}
, we introduce a prototype operator
{A_{\tilde{K},0}}
on the pullback element
\widetilde{K}=G_{K}^{-1}(K)
, defined as

{A_{\tilde{K},0}}\tilde{v}:=-\varepsilon^{2}\widetilde{\Delta}\tilde{v}+\tilde{v}\qquad\forall\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{P}^{p}_{\otimes}(\tilde{K}).

(3.12)

Recall that the norm on
\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
is given by (
3.2
), namely

\|\tilde{v}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})}:=\left\|\tilde{v}\right\|^{2}_{\widetilde{K}}+\varepsilon^{2}\left\|\partial_{1}\tilde{v}\right\|^{2}_{\widetilde{K}}
.
In the following lemma, we prove that
A_{\widetilde{K},0}
satisfies the stability estimate (
3.3
) on
\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
.
In fact, we show a stronger result, where the norm on the right-hand side exhibits the scaling of the operator with respect to the anisotropic mesh sizes.

Lemma 3.4

(Stability estimate for
A_{\widetilde{K},0}
)
.

There exists a constant
c_{A,0}>0
such that

\|{A_{\tilde{K},0}}\tilde{v}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})^{\prime}}\geq c_{A,0}\Big(\left\|\tilde{v}\right\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\left\|\partial_{1}\tilde{v}\right\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+\Big(C_{\eqref{ainv}}\frac{h_{1,K}}{h_{2,K}}\Big)^{-2}\varepsilon^{2}\left\|\partial_{2}\tilde{v}\right\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\forall\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K}),

and, in particular,
it holds

\|{A_{\tilde{K},0}}\tilde{v}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})^{\prime}}\geq c_{A,0}\left\|\tilde{v}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})}\quad\forall\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K}).

Proof.

For
p=1
, the space
\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
is trivial, so there is nothing to prove. Hence assume
p\geq 2
.

Let
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p-1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{p}(\widetilde{K}_{1})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\widetilde{K}_{1})
be a basis that is orthonormal in
L^{2}(\widetilde{K}_{1})
and orthogonal with respect to the inner product

(\cdot,\cdot)_{\widetilde{K}_{1}}+\varepsilon^{2}(\partial_{1}\cdot,\partial_{1}\cdot)_{\widetilde{K}_{1}}
, i.e., such that

(\psi_{i},\psi_{j})_{\widetilde{K}_{1}}+\varepsilon^{2}(\partial_{1}\psi_{i},\partial_{1}\psi_{j})_{\widetilde{K}_{1}}=\lambda_{i}\delta_{ij},\qquad i,j=1,\dots,p-1.

(3.13)

In particular, for
i\neq j
,

(\psi_{i},\psi_{j})_{\widetilde{K}_{1}}=0
, and

(\partial_{1}\psi_{i},\partial_{1}\psi_{j})_{\widetilde{K}_{1}}=0
, and there exist
\widehat{\lambda}_{i}>0
, depending only on
p
, such that

\lambda_{i}=1+\varepsilon^{2}h_{1,K}^{-2}\widehat{\lambda}_{i}.

Let
\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
and write

\tilde{v}(\widetilde{x}_{1},\widetilde{x}_{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\psi_{i}(\widetilde{x}_{1})\tilde{v}_{i}(\widetilde{x}_{2}),\quad\text{for }\tilde{v}_{i}\in\mathbb{P}^{p}(\widetilde{K}_{2}),i=1,\ldots,p-1.

The functions
\{\psi_{j}\tilde{v}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{p-1}

are orthogonal on
\widetilde{K}
with respect to several operators. More precisely, for all
i\neq j
, we have

\begin{cases}(\psi_{i}\tilde{v}_{i},\psi_{j}\tilde{v}_{j})_{\widetilde{K}}=0,\\
(\psi_{i}\partial_{2}^{2}\tilde{v}_{i},\psi_{j}\tilde{v}_{j})_{\widetilde{K}}=0,\\
(\partial_{1}^{2}\psi_{i}\tilde{v}_{i},\psi_{j}\tilde{v}_{j})_{\widetilde{K}}=0.\end{cases}

(3.14)

The first two equalities follow from the orthogonality of
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p-1}
in
L^{2}(\widetilde{K}_{1})
and the tensor-product structure of
\widetilde{K}
. For the last one, using integration by parts in the
\widetilde{x}_{1}
-variable and the fact that
\psi_{j}=0
on
\partial\widetilde{K}_{1}
, we obtain

(\partial_{1}^{2}\psi_{i}\,\tilde{v}_{i},\psi_{j}\,\tilde{v}_{j})_{\widetilde{K}}=-(\partial_{1}\psi_{i},\partial_{1}\psi_{j})_{\widetilde{K}_{1}}(\tilde{v}_{i},\tilde{v}_{j})_{\widetilde{K}_{2}}=0,

for
i\neq j
, where we used (
3.13
).
Using (
3.14
) and (
3.13
), we obtain the following expression for the norm of
\tilde{v}
:

\begin{split}\|\tilde{v}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})}^{2}=\|\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\partial_{1}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}&=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\left(\|\tilde{v}_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}_{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\partial_{1}\psi_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}_{1}}^{2}\|\tilde{v}_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}_{2}}^{2}\right)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\lambda_{i}\|\tilde{v}_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}_{2}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\lambda_{i}\|\psi_{i}\tilde{v}_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.15)

Now let
\tilde{u}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
and write

\tilde{u}(\widetilde{x}_{1},\widetilde{x}_{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\psi_{i}(\widetilde{x}_{1})\tilde{u}_{i}(\widetilde{x}_{2}),\quad\text{for }\tilde{u}_{i}\in\mathbb{P}^{p}(\widetilde{K}_{2}),\ i=1,\ldots,p-1.

For any
i\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
, set

u_{i}:=\psi_{i}\tilde{u}_{i},\qquad v_{i}:=u_{i}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\lambda_{i}}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{i}=\psi_{i}\Big(\tilde{u}_{i}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\lambda_{i}}\partial_{2}^{2}\tilde{u}_{i}\Big)\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K}).

By (
3.13
), these choices satisfy

(u_{i}-\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{1}^{2}u_{i},v_{i})_{\widetilde{K}}=\lambda_{i}(u_{i},v_{i})_{\widetilde{K}}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle({A_{\tilde{K},0}}u_{i},v_{i})_{\widetilde{K}}

\displaystyle=(u_{i}-\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{1}^{2}u_{i}-\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{i},v_{i})_{\widetilde{K}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{i}(u_{i},v_{i})_{\widetilde{K}}-(\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{i},v_{i})_{\widetilde{K}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{i}\|u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}-2(u_{i},\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{i})_{\widetilde{K}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\|\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2},

where we used integration by parts, the fact that
\psi_{i}
vanishes on the boundary
\partial\widetilde{K}_{1}
, and the
orthogonality relations (
3.14
).

Using Lemma
3.3
in the
\widetilde{x}_{2}
-variable and the weighted Young inequality, we get

\displaystyle 2\big|(u_{i},\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{i})_{\widetilde{K}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq 2(1-C_{p})\|u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}\|\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}

\displaystyle\leq(1-C_{p})\Big(\lambda_{i}\|u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\|\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\Big),

and we infer

({A_{\tilde{K},0}}u_{i},v_{i})_{\widetilde{K}}\geq C_{p}\Big(\lambda_{i}\|u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\|\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\Big).

(3.16)

Moreover, since (
3.13
) also gives
\|v_{i}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})}^{2}=\lambda_{i}\|v_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}
,
then by (
3.16
) and the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\frac{({A_{\tilde{K},0}}u_{i},v_{i})_{\widetilde{K}}}{\|v_{i}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})}}

\displaystyle\geq C_{p}\,\frac{\lambda_{i}\|u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+\lambda_{i}^{-1}\|\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}}{\lambda_{i}^{1/2}\|v_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}}

\displaystyle\geq C_{p}\,\frac{\lambda_{i}\|u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+\lambda_{i}^{-1}\|\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}}{\lambda_{i}^{1/2}\|u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}+\lambda_{i}^{-1/2}\|\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{2}^{2}u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}}\geq\frac{C_{p}}{2}\,\lambda_{i}^{1/2}\|u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}.

For every
i=1,\dots,p-1
, using again the orthogonality relations (
3.14
), we have

({A_{\tilde{K},0}}\tilde{u},v_{i})_{\widetilde{K}}=({A_{\tilde{K},0}}u_{i},v_{i})_{\widetilde{K}}

so that

\|{A_{\tilde{K},0}}\tilde{u}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})^{\prime}}=\sup_{q\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})}\frac{({A_{\tilde{K},0}}\tilde{u},q)_{\widetilde{K}}}{\|q\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})}}\geq\frac{({A_{\tilde{K},0}}\tilde{u},v_{i})_{\widetilde{K}}}{\|v_{i}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})}}\geq\frac{C_{p}}{2}\lambda_{i}^{1/2}\|u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}.

Since this holds for every
i
, taking the maximum over
i
and using

\max_{1\leq i\leq p-1}a_{i}\geq\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}a_{i}

for nonnegative numbers
a_{i}
, we get

\displaystyle\|{A_{\tilde{K},0}}\tilde{u}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{p}^{2}}{4}\max_{1\leq i\leq p-1}\lambda_{i}\|u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\geq\frac{C_{p}^{2}}{4(p-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\lambda_{i}\|u_{i}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}.

Using the anisotropic inverse estimate (
3.8
) and
identity (
3.15
), we finally obtain

\displaystyle\|{A_{\tilde{K},0}}\tilde{u}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\|\tilde{u}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\partial_{1}\tilde{u}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+\Big(C_{\eqref{ainv}}\frac{h_{1,K}}{h_{2,K}}\Big)^{-2}\varepsilon^{2}\|\partial_{2}\tilde{u}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}.

This proves the claim.
∎

3.1.2
Local stability on the physical element

In this section, we transfer
the stability of
A_{\widetilde{K},0}
to a pulled-back operator via the perturbation argument of Lemma
A.1
, and then to the physical operator
A_{K}
.

Given the
C^{1}
diffeomorphism
G_{K}:\widetilde{K}\to K
with Jacobian
J_{G_{K}}:\widetilde{K}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
satisfying (
2.1
), we set

\rho_{G_{K}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}):=\det(J_{G_{K}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})),\qquad\nu_{G_{K}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}):=\rho_{G_{K}}(\boldsymbol{x})J_{G_{K}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})^{-1}J_{G_{K}}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})^{-\top},\qquad\text{for }\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\widetilde{K}.

For
v\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)
, we write
\tilde{v}:=v\circ G_{K}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
and define the pulled-back operator

A_{\widetilde{K}}\tilde{v}:=-\varepsilon^{2}\widetilde{\Div}(\nu_{G_{K}}\widetilde{\nabla}\tilde{v})+\rho_{G_{K}}\tilde{v}.

(3.17)

The operator
A_{\widetilde{K}}
is the pullback of
A_{K}

to
\widetilde{K}
, in the sense that

(A_{K}v,\,q)_{K}=(A_{\widetilde{K}}\tilde{v},\,\tilde{q})_{\widetilde{K}}\qquad\forall v,q\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(K),\quad\tilde{v}=v\circ G_{K},\quad\tilde{q}=q\circ G_{K}.

Moreover,
A_{\widetilde{K}}
reduces to the prototype
A_{\widetilde{K},0}

when
G_{K}
is a rigid motion, i.e., when
\nu_{G_{K}}=I
and
\rho_{G_{K}}=1
.
Therefore,
A_{\widetilde{K}}
can be viewed as a
perturbation of
A_{\widetilde{K},0}
, with the perturbation controlled
by the deviation of
G_{K}
from a rigid motion.
To quantify this deviation, we write

\nu_{G_{K}}-I=\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{11}&\eta_{12}\\
\eta_{21}&\eta_{22}\end{pmatrix},

and define the quantity

\theta_{K}:=\|\rho_{G_{K}}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}+\|\eta_{11}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}+\frac{h_{1,K}}{h_{2,K}}\bigl(\|\eta_{12}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}+\|\eta_{21}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}\bigr)+\left(\frac{h_{1,K}}{h_{2,K}}\right)^{2}\|\eta_{22}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}.

(3.18)

Note that any relevant scaling of the element is done in the mapping
F_{K}
; therefore, in the definition of
\theta_{K}
, we compare
\nu_{G_{K}}
to the identity matrix
I
and
\rho_{G_{K}}
to
1
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}
and let
\widetilde{K}=\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\widetilde{K}_{2}=G_{K}^{-1}(K)
.
Let also
{A_{\tilde{K},0}}
and
\widetilde{A}_{K}
be the prototype and pulled-back operators from (
3.12
) and (
3.17
), respectively.
Assume that
\theta_{K}
defined in (
3.18
) satisfies

\theta_{K}<\frac{c_{A,0}}{C_{\rm pert}}
, with
c_{A,0}
as in Lemma
3.4
and
C_{\rm pert}>0

as in (
3.22
) below.
Then

\|\widetilde{A}_{K}\tilde{v}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})^{\prime}}\geq c_{A,0}(1-\gamma_{K})\|\tilde{v}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})}\qquad\forall\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K}),

with

\gamma_{K}:=\frac{C_{\rm pert}\,\theta_{K}}{c_{A,0}}<1.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{v},\tilde{q}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
.
Since functions in
\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
vanish on

\partial\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\widetilde{K}_{2}
, integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\bigl((\widetilde{A}_{K}-{A_{\tilde{K},0}})\tilde{v},\tilde{q}\bigr)_{\widetilde{K}}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\bigl((\nu_{G_{K}}-I)\widetilde{\nabla}\tilde{v},\widetilde{\nabla}\tilde{q}\bigr)_{\widetilde{K}}+\bigl((\rho_{G_{K}}-1)\tilde{v},\tilde{q}\bigr)_{\widetilde{K}}

\displaystyle\quad-\varepsilon^{2}((\nu_{G_{K}}-I)\widetilde{\nabla}\tilde{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\widetilde{K}},\tilde{q})_{\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\partial\widetilde{K}_{2}}.

By expanding the matrix-vector product componentwise and using the Hölder inequality, we get

\displaystyle\bigl|\big((\nu_{G_{K}}-I)\widetilde{\nabla}\tilde{v},\widetilde{\nabla}\tilde{q}\big)_{\widetilde{K}}\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta_{11}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}\|\partial_{1}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}\|\partial_{1}\tilde{q}\|_{\widetilde{K}}+\|\eta_{12}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}\|\partial_{2}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}\|\partial_{1}\tilde{q}\|_{\widetilde{K}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\eta_{21}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}\|\partial_{1}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}\|\partial_{2}\tilde{q}\|_{\widetilde{K}}+\|\eta_{22}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}\|\partial_{2}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}\|\partial_{2}\tilde{q}\|_{\widetilde{K}}.

Using the anisotropic inverse estimate (
3.8
) for both
\tilde{v}
and
\tilde{q}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\bigl|\big((\nu_{G_{K}}-I)\widetilde{\nabla}\tilde{v},\widetilde{\nabla}q\big)_{\widetilde{K}}\bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\Bigl(\|\eta_{11}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}+\frac{h_{1,K}}{h_{2,K}}\|\eta_{12}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{h_{1,K}}{h_{2,K}}\|\eta_{21}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}+\left(\frac{h_{1,K}}{h_{2,K}}\right)^{2}\|\eta_{22}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}\Bigr)\|\partial_{1}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}\|\partial_{1}\tilde{q}\|_{\widetilde{K}}.

The zeroth-order term is estimated
by

\bigl|\big((\rho_{G_{K}}-1)\tilde{v},\tilde{q}\big)_{\widetilde{K}}\bigr|\leq\|\rho_{G_{K}}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}\|\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}\|\tilde{q}\|_{\widetilde{K}}.

(3.20)

It remains to estimate the boundary term. Since the outward normal on

\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\partial\widetilde{K}_{2}
is

\mathbf{n}_{\widetilde{K}}=\pm(0,1)^{\top}
, we have

|(\nu_{G_{K}}-I)\widetilde{\nabla}\tilde{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\widetilde{K}}|=|\eta_{21}\partial_{1}\tilde{v}+\eta_{22}\partial_{2}\tilde{v}|\qquad\text{on }\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\partial\widetilde{K}_{2}.

By the anisotropic trace and inverse inequalities (
3.7
)–(
3.8
), together with the Poincaré–Steklov inequality (
3.9
), we obtain

(3.21)

,

Remark 3.6

(When is
\theta_{K}
small?)
.

The quantity
\theta_{K}
measures the deviation of the map
G_{K}
from a rigid motion,
resolved
in the anisotropic coordinates of the pullback element
\widetilde{K}
.
Recall that the element stretching is already encoded in the affine map
F_{K}
, while
G_{K}
describes only the remaining geometric deformation.
Therefore, the relevant regime for the analysis is that
G_{K}
is a uniformly mild bending of the tensor-product element
\widetilde{K}
, as expressed in (
2.1
).

In particular, if
G_{K}
is a rigid motion, i.e.,
G_{K}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}})=Q_{K}\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}+b
with
Q_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
an orthogonal matrix and
b_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then
\nu_{G_{K}}=I
and
\rho_{G_{K}}=1
.
Hence
\theta_{K}=0
, and the lemma applies without any smallness condition.

Moreover, the anisotropic weighting in
\theta_{K}
shows that the smallness condition becomes weaker on strongly stretched elements, whenever the deformation acts mainly through the long direction
\tilde{x}_{2}
.
The first term in
\theta_{K}
measures the deviation of the Jacobian determinant
\det(J_{G_{K}})
from a constant, i.e., the change in volume under the mapping
G_{K}
.
As

G_{K}
is only meant to bend the element without stretching, this term is expected to be small.
The remaining terms in
\theta_{K}
measure how the Laplacian transforms under the mapping
G_{K}
.
As we have seen, the dominant term in the Laplacian is
\partial_{1}^{2}
, hence the smallness condition on
\eta_{11}
is not weighted, whereas the other terms are weighted by powers of the anisotropic ratio
\frac{h_{1,K}}{h_{2,K}}
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Assume that
\theta_{K}
defined in (
3.18
) satisfies

\theta_{K}<\frac{c_{A,0}}{C_{\rm pert}}
, with
c_{A,0}
as in Lemma
3.4
and
C_{\rm pert}>0
as in (
3.22
).
Then there exists a constant
c_{A}>0
, independent of
h_{1,K}
,
h_{2,K}
, and
\varepsilon
, but depending on the uniform geometric constants in (
2.1
) and on
p
, such that

\|A_{K}v\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)^{\prime}}\geq c_{A}\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}_{\!h}}\qquad\forall v\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(K).

Proof.

Let
v\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)
and set
\tilde{v}:=v\circ G_{K}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
.
Using
Lemma

3.5
together with (
3.8
) and the anisotropy assumption (
2.2
), we have that

\|\widetilde{A}_{K}\tilde{v}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})^{\prime}}^{2}\gtrsim\|\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\widetilde{\nabla}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}.

For any
edge
\tilde{e}\subset\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\partial\widetilde{K}_{2}
, the anisotropic trace inequality (
3.7
)
and the Poincaré–Steklov inequality (
3.9
) yield

\|\tilde{v}\|_{\tilde{e}}^{2}\lesssim h_{2,K}^{-1}\|\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\lesssim h_{2,K}^{-1}h_{1,K}^{2}\|\partial_{1}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\lesssim h_{2,K}\|\partial_{1}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2},

since
h_{1,K}\leq h_{2,K}
.
Combining the two previous estimates and using that
\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})
vanishes on

\partial\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\widetilde{K}_{2}
gives

\|\widetilde{A}_{K}\tilde{v}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})^{\prime}}^{2}\gtrsim\|\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\widetilde{\nabla}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+\sum_{\tilde{e}\subset\partial\widetilde{K}}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{h_{2,K}}\|\tilde{v}\|_{\tilde{e}}^{2}.

(3.23)

Finally, the uniform bounds (

) imply the norm equivalences

\|v\|_{K}^{2}\lesssim\|\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2},\qquad\|\nabla v\|_{K}^{2}\lesssim\|\widetilde{\nabla}\tilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2},\qquad\|v\|_{e}^{2}\lesssim\|\tilde{v}\|_{\tilde{e}}^{2},

and its preimage

.
Using that
h_{2,K}\geq h_{\perp}^{\tilde{e},\widetilde{K}}
on

h_{1,K}\leq h_{2,K}
, and recalling that

\|A_{K}v\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)^{\prime}}=\|\widetilde{A}_{K}\tilde{v}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})^{\prime}}
(since
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)
is endowed with the pullback norm

\|v\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)}=\|\tilde{v}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(\widetilde{K})}
), estimate (

\|A_{K}v\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)^{\prime}}^{2}\gtrsim\|v\|_{K}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{K}^{2}+\sum_{e\subset\partial K}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{h_{\perp}^{e,\widetilde{K}}}\|v\|_{e}^{2},

which is the claim.
∎

3.1.3
Local continuity

Theorem 3.8

(Continuity of
A_{K}
on
\mathbb{V}_{*h}
)
.

There exists a constant
C_{A}>0
, depending only on the polynomial degree
p
and on the geometric constants in (
2.1
), such that

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\|{A_{K}}u\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)^{\prime}}^{2}\leq C_{A}^{2}\|u\|_{\mathbb{V}_{*h}}^{2}\qquad\forall u\in\mathbb{V}_{*h}.

Proof.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}
and let
v\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)
. Denote by
\widetilde{v}:=v\circ G_{K}
the pullback of
v
to
\widetilde{K}
.
Since
v
vanishes on
\mathcal{E}_{2}^{K}=G_{K}(\partial\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\widetilde{K}_{2})
,
Integration by parts on
K

(A_{K}u,v)_{K}=(\varepsilon^{2}\nabla u,\nabla v)_{K}+(u,v)_{K}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{K}_{1}}(\varepsilon^{2}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}_{K},v)_{e}.

(3.24)

We apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to each term on the right-hand side and estimate the resulting

v
-terms in the
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)
norm.

Using the properties (
2.1
) of
G_{K}
, Lemma
3.2
, and the anisotropy assumption (
2.2
) (
h_{1,K}\leq h_{2,K}
), we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{K}^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\|\widetilde{\nabla}\widetilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\|\partial_{1}\widetilde{v}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\leq\left\|v\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)}^{2}.

Let
e\in\mathcal{E}^{K}_{1}
and
\widetilde{e}:=G_{K}^{-1}(e)\subset\widetilde{K}_{1}\times\partial\widetilde{K}_{2}
.
Using again (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), together with the anisotropic trace inequality (
3.7
), and the Poincaré–Steklov inequality (

), we get

\left\|v\right\|_{e}^{2}\lesssim\left\|\widetilde{v}\right\|_{\widetilde{e}}^{2}\lesssim h_{2,K}^{-1}\left\|\widetilde{v}\right\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\lesssim h_{2,K}^{-1}h_{1,K}^{2}\left\|\partial_{1}\widetilde{v}\right\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\lesssim h_{2,K}\left\|\partial_{1}\widetilde{v}\right\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\lesssim h_{2,K}\varepsilon^{-2}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)}^{2}.

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\left\|\varepsilon\nabla u\right\|_{K}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{K}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{K}_{1}}h_{\perp}^{e,\widetilde{K}}\left\|\varepsilon\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}_{K}\right\|_{e}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)}.

as desired.
∎

Lemma 3.9

(Facet estimates)

For all

Moreover, for all

Let

For all

Theorem 3.10

(Coercivity and continuity of

)
.

For all
\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}

, with

\varepsilon

h_{1,K}
and
h_{2,K}
for all

.

, yield

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}h_{\bar{\imath}}\Bigg[h_{i}^{2s-1}\left(\frac{h_{i}}{h_{\bar{\imath}}}\|\partial_{i}^{s+1}\widetilde{u}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+\frac{h_{\bar{\imath}}}{h_{i}}\|\partial_{i}^{s}\partial_{\bar{\imath}}\widetilde{u}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+h_{\bar{\imath}}^{2s-1}\|\partial_{\bar{\imath}}^{s}\partial_{i}\widetilde{u}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+h_{\bar{\imath}}^{2s-1}\|\partial_{\bar{\imath}}^{s+1}\widetilde{u}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+\frac{h_{i}^{2s}}{h_{\bar{\imath}}}\|\partial_{i}^{s}\partial_{\bar{\imath}}\widetilde{u}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}h_{i}^{2s}\Bigg[\|\partial_{i}^{s+1}\widetilde{u}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}+\left(1+\left(\frac{h_{\bar{\imath}}}{h_{i}}\right)^{2}\right)\|\partial_{i}^{s}\partial_{\bar{\imath}}\widetilde{u}\|_{\widetilde{K}}^{2}\Bigg].

Collecting all contributions and using the definition of the anisotropic seminorm gives the result.
∎

We present numerical experiments to validate the theoretical results and illustrate additional features and limitations of the proposed method.

Replication data are available in

Implementational details of the embedded Trefftz DG method are given in

We briefly describe the construction of a basis for the special test space
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)
defined in (
2.6
), which can be easily implemented exploiting the tensor-product structure of the pullback elements.
We define

\psi_{j}(y):=y(1-y)\,L_{j}(y),\qquad j=0,\dots,p-2,

where
L_{j}
denotes the standard Legendre polynomials shifted and scaled to
[0,1]
.
Then
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{p-2}
spans the subspace of
\mathbb{P}^{p}([0,1])
with homogeneous endpoint conditions.
A basis for
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)
is obtained by tensorization with a standard polynomial
basis in the remaining variable.

We have also considered alternative choices for the test space
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)
, such as the image of the underlying space under the operator
A_{K}
, i.e.,
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)=A_{K}(\mathbb{V}_{\!h}(K))
, and also
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)=\Delta(\mathbb{V}_{\!h}(K))
.
These alternative choices are possible, however, they exhibit numerical instabilities on fine meshes or high polynomial degrees.
Moreover, on anisotropic elements, redefining
\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)
by swapping the roles of
x
and
y
, i.e., vanishing on the shorter edges, leads to suboptimal results, confirming the importance of the specific choice we make for
the test space.
In all the following experiments, we compare the standard DG method using the space
\mathbb{V}_{\!h}
to the embedded Trefftz DG method using
\mathbb{T}_{\mspace{-1.5mu}h}
.
The stabilization parameter
\sigma
is set to
\sigma=10
for all

The numerical results are compared in terms of

-convergence on a square geometry

Lemma A.1

(
[
20
, Lemma 4.2]
)
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}
and let
A_{K,0}:\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)\to\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)^{\prime}
be an invertible operator with

\left\|A_{K,0}u_{h}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)^{\prime}}\geq c_{A_{0}}\left\|u_{h}\right\|_{{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)}}\quad\forall u_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(K),

.
If there exists a constant
\gamma_{K}\in(0,1)
such that

\left\|{A_{K}}u_{h}-A_{K,0}u_{h}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)^{\prime}}\leq\gamma_{K}\left\|A_{K,0}u_{h}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{h}(K)^{\prime}}\quad\forall u_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}(K),

(Estimates for
