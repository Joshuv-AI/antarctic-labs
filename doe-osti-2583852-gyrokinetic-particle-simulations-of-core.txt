 Final report

1. Abstract summarizing the general topics addressed during the award.

 In this project, we have further developed GTC gyrokinetic particle simulations to study plasma
confinement properties in the stellarators and tokamaks with 3D magnetic fields, where both neoclassical
and turbulent transport are self-consistently treated on an equal footing.

2. Brief technical description of most relevant projects/publications.

 We have studied effects of kinetic electrons, impurities, isotopes, and ambipolar electric fields on
microturbulence in the LHD and W7-X stellarators, and collisional damping of zonal flows. We have also
performed LHD experiments on effects of fast ions on the ambipolar electric fields. Finally, we have
performed fully self-consistent simulations coupling neoclassical and turbulent transport by unifying the
formulation of neoclassical and turbulence simulations. The computational capability and physics insights
from this project are in support of fusion SciDAC HiFiStell stellarator project.

 • Trapped electron mode in a quasi-isodynamic stellarator, Javier H. Nicolau, Xishuo Wei, Pengfei
 Liu, Gyungjin Choi, and Zhihong Lin, Nuclear Fusion 65, 086049 (2025).

 Stellarators with 3-dimensional (3D) magnetic configurations are an attractive fusion reactor concept
thanks to their steady state operation and reduced risk of disruptions since no plasma current drive is needed.
The design of stellarator has gained intense research interest as private fusion companies focus on design
and optimization of stellarator reactors. To be a competitive reactor candidate, the optimized stellarators
need to demonstrate a turbulent transport level similar to or lower than an axisymmetric tokamak. Compared
with the axisymmetric tokamak, microturbulence in the non-axisymmetric stellarator is less well
understood.

 Our largest GTC global simulations by a UCI postdoc
H. Nicolau have discovered a new trapped electron mode
(TEM) instability in the optimized stellarator that can drive
a large particle transport. This TEM has an unconventional
eigenmode structure (Fig. 1) and was not found by previous
local simulations. Particle transport has thus been

overlooked in those local simulations, but is a key missing element in the transport studies of the fusion
reactor based on the optimized stellarator. The publication of our work could further put the spotlight on
the study of particle transport in the stellarator experiments and the need for large scale global simulations
of the stellarator transport at an exciting time of commercialization of fusion energy pursuing steady state
reactors based on the optimized stellarators.

 • Gyrokinetic simulations of effects of magnetic islands on microturbulence in KSTAR, Xishuo Wei,
 Javier H Nicolau, Gyungjin Choi, Zhihong Lin, Seong-Moo Yang, SangKyeun
 Kim, WooChang Lee, Chen Zhao, Tyler Cote, JongKyu Park, Dmitri Orlov, Nuclear Fusion 65,
        026026 (2025).

 The control of dangerous edge localized mode (ELM) using resonant magnetic perturbations (RMP)
has been demonstrated on several tokamaks, including DIII-D and KSTAR and is also predicted to be
effective in future devices like ITER. Understanding the physics mechanism of these RMP effects on
turbulent transport is crucial for optimizing RMP configurations to achieve better confinement and higher
fusion gain during steady-state operations. A key aspect of this is understanding the impact of magnetic
islands (MI) induced by the RMPs on turbulent transport.

 In this work, we use
GTC gyrokinetic
simulations to study the
effects of the MI on
turbulent transport using
realistic geometry and
profiles in an actual
experiment, KSTAR
long-pulse discharge
#19118, which features a
large MI with a width of
approximately 4 cm
generated by the RMP
that suppresses the ELMs. We performed global, self-consistent GTC electrostatic simulations using
gyrokinetic ions and drift kinetic electrons. Our simulations verified the presence of anisotropic turbulence
structures and turbulence spreading in the poloidal plane, regulated by nonlinear interactions between
turbulence, zonal flows, and vortex flows. MI greatly enhances turbulent transport of both particle and heat.
We also observed that turbulent transport exhibits variations in the toroidal direction, with transport through
the resonant layer near the X-point being enhanced when the X-point is located at the outer mid-plane (Fig.
2). Furthermore, we compared the features of turbulence between GTC simulations and experimental
observations, finding good agreement regarding the MI effect on time frequency and perpendicular
wavevector spectrum. The variation of density fluctuation at O-point and X-point are also similar to
previous DIII-D and TEXT experiments. This work enhances our understanding of turbulent transport in
the presence of MIs and provides insights in improving the confinement in RMP experiments.
