Valuative independence and metric SYZ conjecture
Yang Li

Abstract
Given a polarised maximal degeneration of compact Calabi-Yau manifolds, assuming there exists a canonical basis of the section ring for the polarisation line bundle, satisfying the valuative independence condition, we will prove the metric SYZ conjecture.



1
Introduction

Let
\pi:X\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
be a
meromorphic degeneration
of compact Calabi-Yau (CY) manifolds over the punctured disc with coordinate
t
, equipped with a relatively ample
polarisation
line bundle
L\to X
. We assume there is a nowhere vanishing holomorphic volume form
\Omega\in H^{0}(X,K_{X})
, which induces the fibrewise holomorphic volume form
\Omega_{t}
by
\Omega=\Omega_{t}\wedge dt
. The complexity of the degeneration family
\pi:X\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
is measured by a birational invariant called the
essential skeleton

Sk(X)
, which is a simplicial complex of dimension
0\leq\dim Sk(X)\leq n
. The case of
\dim Sk(X)=n
is known as
maximal degeneration
.

We consider the CY metric
\omega_{CY,t}
on
X_{t}
in the Kähler class
\frac{1}{|\log|t||}c_{1}(L)
, defined by the complex Monge-Ampère equation

\omega_{CY,t}^{n}=\frac{(L^{n})}{|\log|t||^{n}}\mu_{t},

where
\mu_{t}
is the normalised CY probability measure

\mu_{t}=\frac{\Omega_{t}\wedge\overline{\Omega}_{t}}{\int_{X_{t}}\Omega_{t}\wedge\overline{\Omega}_{t}}.

A central problem in CY geometry is the Strominger-Yau-Zaslow (SYZ) conjecture, which concerns the limiting behaviour of the CY metric
\omega_{CY,t}
as
t\to 0
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

(Metric SYZ conjecture) Let
\pi:(X,L)\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
be a polarised maximal degeneration of compact CY manifolds. Given any
0<\delta\ll 1
, for sufficiently small
t
depending on
\delta
, there exists a special Lagrangian
T^{n}
-fibration with respect to the Calabi-Yau structure
(X_{t},\omega_{CY,t},\Omega_{t})
, on an open subset of
X_{t}
whose normalised CY measure is at least
1-\delta
.

The metric SYZ conjecture is currently known for Abelian varieties, certain K3 surfaces, and a large class of hypersurface examples inside toric Fano manifolds
[
19
,
22
,
24
,
20
,
1
,
29
,
14
]
. A more general approach is based on
non-archimedean (NA) pluripotential theory

[
5
,
6
,
23
]
. Some key features are as follows (see also Section
2
for some quick recap):

1.

Given the polarised
meromorphic degeneration family of compact CY manifolds
\pi:X\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
, we can take the base change to a degeneration family over the formal disc
X_{K}\to\text{Spec}(K)
where
K=\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
, and then canonically
associate a NA object called the
Berkovich space

X_{K}^{an}
. There is a
hybrid topology
on
X\sqcup X_{K}^{an}
, so that
X_{K}^{an}
can be viewed as a tropical limit of
X_{t}
as
t\to 0
.

2.

The essential skeleton
Sk(X)
is canonically embedded as a subset of
X_{K}^{an}
. There is a canonical Lebesgue measure
\mu_{0}
supported on
Sk(X)\subset X_{K}^{an}
, which is the natural weak limit of normalised CY measures
\mu_{t}

on
X_{t}
in the hybrid topology. The central result of NA pluripotential theory due to Boucksom-Favre-Jonsson
[
5
]
is the solution of the NA Calabi conjecture. In this setting, it provides a unique (up to constant) continuous psh potential
\phi_{0}
on
(X_{K}^{an},c_{1}(L))
, which solves the
non-archimedean Monge-Ampère (NA MA) equation

\text{MA}(\phi_{0})=(L^{n})\mu_{0},

and is called the NA CY potential.
It is recently proved
[
27
]
that whenever
1\leq\dim Sk(X)\leq n
, up to suitable normalisation, the Kähler potential of
(X_{t},\omega_{t},\Omega_{t})
converges in the
C^{0}
-hybrid topology to the NA CY potential
\phi_{0}
.

3.

A choice of SNC model
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{D}
for the degeneration family
\pi:X\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
, induces a
retraction map

r_{\mathcal{X}}
from
X_{K}^{an}
to the dual complex
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
. The essential skeleton
Sk(X)
can be viewed as a subset of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
.

Definition 1.2
.

An SNC model satisfies the
weak comparison property
for the NA CY potential
\phi_{0}
, if there is an open subset
U\subset Sk(X)
with full Lebesgue measure
\mu_{0}(U)=1
, such that
\phi_{0}
factors through the retraction map
r_{\mathcal{X}}:X_{K}^{an}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
over the open subset
U\subset Sk(X)\subset\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
.

Remark 1.3
.

The name ‘comparison property’ comes from a result of C. Vilsmeier
[
32
]
, which allows one to compare the NA MA equation with the real Monge-Ampère equation when
this property is satisfied. The weak comparison property is preserved under smooth blow ups and blow downs, hence if it holds for one SNC model, then it will hold for all SNC models (
cf.
Remark
2.10
). As a small caveat, Def.
1.2
is similar but not identical to the comparison property in
[
23
]
, and we will give some exposition in Section
2.7
.

The arguments in
[
23
]
essentially proved the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\pi:X\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
be a polarised maximal degeneration of compact CY manifolds.
Suppose the weak comparison property is verified for some semistable SNC model, then the metric SYZ conjecture holds for the degeneration family
\pi:X\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
.

The upshot is that the metric SYZ conjecture, which is a priori a PDE problem in complex geometry, is reduced to a question about the NA CY potential, which one hopes to resolve using algebro-geometric inputs.

The key piece of algebraic geometry turns out to be a
canonical basis
in the graded vector space
\bigoplus_{l\geq 0}H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
over the field
K=\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
, satisfying
valuative independence
. Intuitively, such a basis
\{\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\}
behaves like the monomials on the affinoid torus, which makes the CY manifold resemble a toric variety, hence should be beneficial for producing the SYZ special Lagrangian
T^{n}
-fibration.

Definition 1.5
.

For any
l\geq 0
, we say a
K
-basis
\{\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\}\subset H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
satisfies
valuative independence
, if for every
x\in Sk(X)
viewed as the valuation
val_{x}
, we have

val_{x}(\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\theta^{l}_{\alpha})=\min_{\alpha:a_{\alpha}\neq 0}(val(a_{\alpha})+val_{x}(\theta^{l}_{\alpha})),\quad\forall a_{\alpha}\in K.

(Here we regard sections of
H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
as local meromorphic functions using some auxiliary local trivialisation of the line bundle, but the valuative independence condition is independent of the choice of trivialisation.)

The search for canonical basis is an active topic in algebraic geometry.

1.

In the Gross-Siebert programme
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
, given a polarised maximal degeneration of compact CY manifolds, one can build a graded ring from punctured Gromov-Witten invariants, and define the mirror CY family via the relative Proj construction of this graded ring. Consequently, the mirror family carries a polarisation line bundle, whose section ring has a canonical basis called
theta functions
. This is a vast generalisation for the classical theta functions on abelian varieties.

2.

It is unknown in general whether the Gross-Siebert theta functions satisfies the valuative independence condition for polarised maximal degeneration of compact CY manifolds. However, in an analogous context for cluster varieties, the Gross-Siebert construction produces a distinguished basis of theta functions for the coordinate ring on certain open Calabi-Yau manifolds, and the very recent work of Cheung-Magee-Mandel-Muller
[
11
]
verified the valuative independence condition.

3.

H. Blum and Y. Liu informed the author that they can prove the following in full generality (not necessarily assuming maximal degeneration).

Theorem 1.6
.

[
2
]
Let
(X_{K},L)\to\text{Spec}(K)
be any polarised degeneration of smooth projective CY varieties over the formal punctured disc, where
L
is relatively ample. Then there exists some integer
m_{0}\geq 1
, such that for any
l\geq 0
, there exists a
K
-basis
\{\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\}\subset H^{0}(X_{K},m_{0}lL)
satisfying valuative independence.

Remark 1.7
.

This relies on deep techniques from birational geometry concerning finite generation of section rings, and construction of multi-parameter test configurations. Blum-Liu’s general result also applies to projective log canonical Calabi-Yau pairs over
\text{Spec}(K)
.

Let
\pi:X\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
be a polarised maximal degeneration of compact CY manifolds. Up to finite base change, without loss of generality we suppose that there is some semistable model
\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{D}
.
Our main theorem is

Theorem 1.8
.

Assuming for any
l\geq 0
, the
K
-vector space
H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
admits a basis satisfying valuative independence, then the weak comparison property holds for this degeneration family
\pi:X\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
.

Together with Thm.
1.4
and the exciting upcoming work of Blum-Liu applied to
m_{0}L
instead of
L
, this would
prove the metric SYZ conjecture for all polarised maximal degeneration of compact CY manifolds
. Furthermore, we will interprete the NA MA equation, in terms of an
optimal transport problem
between
Sk(X)
and an auxiliary probability measure space
(B,\nu)
, which can be constructed via
Okounkov body
techniques.

Remark 1.9
.

Optimistically, one would hope that
(B,\nu)
can be identified with the essential skeleton of the mirror family, equipped with the Lebesgue measure. This would lead to an appealing metric version of mirror symmetry, see
[
27
, Section 6.3]
for more discussions.

Remark 1.10
.

J. Hultgren and M. S. Khalid inform the author that they have an upcoming preprint
[
21
]
, which defines a cost function explicitly computable from the monodromy of an affine structure. In the case of the Fermat family of cubic curves it agrees with the cost function attained from a valuative independent basis. They also derive a sufficient criterion for when the associated Kantorovich potential solves a real Monge-Ampère equation, and demonstrate that this is satisfied in some examples.

Organisation
. We recall some background from non-archimedean geometry in Section
2
, including the Boucksom-Favre-Jonsson solution to the NA Monge-Ampère equation, the relative volume interpretation for the NA Monge-Ampère energy due to Boucksom-Eriksson, and some exposition on how the weak comparison property implies the metric SYZ conjecture.

The new contents are contained in Section
3
,
4
.
In Section
3
we assume the existence of the valuative independent basis
\{\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\}
for
\bigoplus_{l\geq 0}H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
, and derive consequences on the NA semipositive potentials. We will extract a cost function
c(x,p):Sk(X)\times B\to\mathbb{R}
by taking suitable asymptotic limits for
\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|
, which allows us to introduce a distinguished class of functions
\mathcal{P}_{c}\subset C^{0}(Sk(X))
(Section
3.2
). The functions in
\mathcal{P}_{c}
can be canonically identified with continuous semipositive potentials on
(X,L)
satisfying a domination property (Section
3.3
). We use the tool of Okounkov bodies to extract more refined information for the cost function (Section
3.4
,
3.5
), leading to a criterion for the semipositive potential to factor through the retraction map
r_{\mathcal{X}}:X_{K}^{an}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
over local open neighbourhoods inside
Sk(X)
(Section
3.6
), and an alternative formula for the NA Monge-Ampère energy (Section
3.7
).

In Section
4
we draw consequences for a semipositive potential
\varphi
solving the NA MA equation

\text{MA}(\varphi)=(L^{n})\mu,

assuming that
\mu
is a probability measure supported on
Sk(X)
, and is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
Sk(X)
. The special case
\mu=\mu_{0}
corresponds to the NA CY potential. In this generality, we will prove that the weak comparison property holds for
\varphi
, and the potential
\varphi
admits an optimal transport interpretation. A crucial ingredient is that certain bad sets have null measure, and the proof involves a delicate application of the orthogonality property for psh envelops.

2.1
Models and dual complex

We denote
R=\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]
, and
K=\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
with the standard discrete valuation.

Given a polarised meromorphic degeneration family of
n
-dimensional smooth projective varieties
\pi:X\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
, upon base change to the formal punctured disc
\text{Spec}(K)
, we obtain a degeneration family
\pi:X_{K}\to\text{Spec}(K)
. Let
X_{K}^{an}
denote the
Berkovich space
. Given an affine variety over
K
, the Berkovich space is the space of semivaluations on the ring of functions extending the standard discrete valuation on
K
, and in general
X_{K}^{an}
is obtained by gluing the affine pieces. The semivaluations
v
are equivalently thought of as multiplicative seminorms
|\cdot|=e^{-v}
, satisfying the ultrametric property

|f+g|\leq\max\{|f|+|g|\},\quad|fg|=|f||g|.

A
model
of
X_{K}\to\text{Spec}(K)
(resp.
X\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
) is a normal projective scheme
\mathcal{X}
, flat and of finite type over
\text{Spec}(R)
(resp. the complex disc
\mathbb{D}
), together with an identification with
X_{K}\to\text{Spec}(K)
(resp.
X\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
) away from the special fibre. For any model
\mathcal{X}
and every irreducible component
E
of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
, we write the central fibre as
\mathcal{X}_{0}=\sum b_{i}E_{i}
, and
E_{J}=\cap_{i\in J}E_{i}
. We say
\mathcal{X}
is SNC if
\mathcal{X}
is regular and
\mathcal{X}_{0}
has SNC support, and an SNC model is semistable if all the divisor components on
\mathcal{X}_{0}
have multiplicity one. By Hironaka resolution and semistable reduction, up to finite base change, we shall without loss assume that there exists some semistable SNC model. A model of the line bundle
L\to X_{K}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on a proper model
\mathcal{X}
, together with an identification
\mathcal{L}|_{X_{K}}=L
.

The
dual complex

\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
of an SNC model
\mathcal{X}
is the simplicial complex whose vertices correspond to the irreducible components
E_{i}
of the central fibre, and whose faces correspond to the connected components of nonempty intersection strata
E_{J}
, and are identified as the simplex

\Delta_{J}\simeq\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{|J|}|\sum_{i\in J}b_{i}x_{i}=1\}.

Given an SNC model over
\text{Spec}(R)
, there are two natural comparison maps between its dual complex and the Berkovich space.

1.

There is a continuous
retraction map

r_{\mathcal{X}}:X_{K}^{an}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
.
Any point
e^{-v}\in X_{K}^{an}
admits
a centre on
\mathcal{X}
.
This is the unique scheme theoretic point

\xi\in X_{0}
such that

|f|_{x}\leq 1
for
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},\xi}

and
|f|_{x}<1
for

f\in m_{\mathcal{X},\xi}
.
Let
J\subset I

be the maximal subset such that

\xi\in E_{J}
. Then

r_{\mathcal{X}}(x)\in\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}

is determined by evaluating

-\log|z_{j}|_{x}
for
j\in J
.
These retraction maps form commutative diagrams under further blow ups of SNC models.
The tower of retraction maps defines a homeomorphism

X_{K}^{an}\simeq\varprojlim_{\text{snc models}}\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}.

2.

There is an
embedding map

emb_{\mathcal{X}}:\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}\to X_{K}^{an}
, which is a homeomorphism onto its image. Given
x\in\Delta_{J}
, we need to associate a quasi-monomial valuation
val_{x}
, regarded as a point in
X_{K}^{an}
. Given any local function
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},\eta}
where
\eta
is the generic point of
E_{J}
, we can Taylor expand

f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{|J|}}f_{\alpha}z_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}\ldots z_{|J|}^{\alpha_{|J|}},

where
z_{i}
are local equations of the divisors
E_{i}
, and
f_{\alpha}
is either a unit or zero. Then
val_{x}
is defined as the minimal weighted vanishing order:

val_{x}(f)=\min\{\langle x,\alpha\rangle|f_{\alpha}\neq 0\}.

In particular, the vertices of
\Delta_{J}
map to the divisorial valuations. The requirement
\sum b_{i}x_{i}=1
comes from
val(t)=1
, where
t\in R
is the uniformizer. The composition
r_{\mathcal{X}}\circ emb_{\mathcal{X}}=id_{\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}}
. Henceforth we identify the dual complex with its image in
X_{K}^{an}
.

Moreover, there is a natural topology on
X_{K}^{an}\cup X
, known as the
hybrid topology
, which describes
X_{K}^{an}
as a limiting object of the complex manifolds
X_{t}

[
4
, Appendix]
[
29
, Section 1.2]
; a one-page recap is in
[
25
, section 2.2]
.

2.2
Essential skeleton and volume form asymptote

Suppose
X
is a meromorphic degeneration family of smooth projective CY manifolds
X_{t}
over some punctured disc
\mathbb{D}^{*}
, and
X
is equipped with a holomorphic volume form
\Omega
, which induces the holomorphic volume forms
\Omega_{t}
on the
X_{t}
via
\Omega=\Omega_{t}\wedge dt
. The
essential skeleton
is the subset of
X_{K}^{an}
where the log discrepancy function takes the minimal value
\kappa_{0}

[
28
, Section 3.1.1]
. Up to passing to semistable reduction and multiplying
\Omega
by a suitable power of
t
, we may assume
\kappa_{0}=0
.

We follow
[
4
]
to sketch the computation of the CY volume form asymptote. Let
\mathcal{X}
be an SNC model of the degeneration family, whose dual complex is
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
. We write the central fibre as
\mathcal{X}_{0}=\sum b_{i}E_{i}
, and the relative log canonical divisor as
\sum a_{i}E_{i}
(so the log discrepancy function takes the value
\frac{a_{i}}{b_{i}}
at the divisorial point corresponding to
E_{i}
). We consider an intersection stratum
E_{J}=\cap_{i\in J}E_{i}
, and let
z_{0},\ldots z_{n}
be local coordinates on
E_{J}
bounded away from the deeper intersection strata, such that
z_{0},\ldots z_{k}
are the local defining equation for the
E_{i}
with
i\in J
. Locally there is a nowhere vanishing holomorphic function
u_{J}
such that

\Omega=u_{J}z_{0}^{a_{0}+b_{0}-1}\ldots z_{k}^{a_{k}+b_{k}-1}dz_{0}\wedge\ldots dz_{n},

and we can arrange

t=z_{0}^{b_{0}}\ldots z_{k}^{b_{k}}.

Then

\Omega_{t}=b_{0}^{-1}u_{J}z_{0}^{a_{0}}\ldots z_{k}^{a_{k}}d\log z_{1}\wedge\ldots d\log z_{k}\wedge dz_{k+1}\wedge\ldots dz_{n}.

(1)

By the non-negativity of the log discrepancy function, we have
a_{i}\geq 0
for
i=0,\ldots k
.

For
0<|t|\ll 1
, we have a logarithm map
\text{Log}_{\mathcal{X}}:X_{t}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, which can be chosen locally as

\text{Log}_{\mathcal{X}}(z)=(x_{0},\ldots x_{k}):=\frac{1}{\log|t|}(\log|z_{0}|,\ldots\log|z_{k}|),

so that
\sum_{0}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}=1
by
t=z_{0}^{b_{0}}\ldots z_{k}^{b_{k}}
. Passing to polar coordinates allows one to compute the volume asymptote.
If at least one exponent
a_{i}>0
, then the local contribution to the volume integral
\int_{X_{t}}\Omega_{t}\wedge\overline{\Omega}_{t}
is suppressed by a factor
O(\frac{1}{|\log|t||})
. The divisors with
a_{i}=0
correspond to the vertices of a subcomplex of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, which is naturally identified with the essential skeleton
Sk(X)\subset X_{K}^{an}
under the embedding map. The dimension
m=\dim Sk(X)
then equals the largest value of
k
such that
a_{0}=\ldots=a_{k}=0
, so the total volume integral
\int_{X_{t}}\Omega_{t}\wedge\overline{\Omega}_{t}=O(|\log|t||^{m})
. We denote the normalised CY measure as

\mu_{t}=\frac{\Omega_{t}\wedge\overline{\Omega}_{t}}{\int_{X_{t}}\Omega_{t}\wedge\overline{\Omega}_{t}},

which is a probability measure on
X_{t}
.
Boucksom-Jonsson
[
5
]
show that

Theorem 2.1
.

Under the hybrid topology, the measures
\mu_{t}
on
X_{t}
converge weakly to a probability measure
\mu_{0}
supported on
Sk(X)\subset X_{K}^{an}
, which puts no measure on the lower dimensional faces of
Sk(X)
, and agrees with a suitably normalized Lebesgue measure
\mu_{0}
on the
m
-dimensional faces of
Sk(X)
.
More concretely, for any continuous function
f
on the hybrid space
X_{K}^{an}\cup X
, we have

\int_{X_{t}}fd\mu_{t}\to\int_{X_{K}^{an}}fd\mu_{0}.

In the case of
maximal degeneration
,
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}Sk(\mathcal{X})=n
, and the measure convergence can be understood explicitly in a semistable SNC model. Near one of the deepest SNC intersection strata
E_{J}
corresponding to an
n
-dimensional simplex in
Sk(\mathcal{X})
, locally the CY volume form is proportional to

\sqrt{-1}^{n^{2}}\Omega_{t}\wedge\overline{\Omega}_{t}=|u_{J}|^{2}\prod_{1}^{n}\sqrt{-1}d\log z_{i}\wedge d\log\bar{z}_{i}.

(2)

Here
u_{J}
limits to its value
u_{J}(E_{J})
at the point stratum
E_{J}
, which is called the Poincaré residue of
\Omega
, and is easily seen to be independent of the choice of coordinates
z_{i}
. It is a consequence of the residue theorem on Riemann surfaces that
|u_{J}(E_{J})|^{2}
is independent of such
J

[
4
, Thm. 7.1]
. Thus the pushforward to
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
of the normalised CY measure
\mu_{t}
converges smoothly in the interior of
\Delta_{J}
to a constant multiple of the
Lebesgue measure
:

\text{Log}_{\mathcal{X}*}\mu_{t}=\text{Log}_{\mathcal{X}*}\frac{\Omega_{t}\wedge\overline{\Omega}_{t}}{\int_{X_{t}}\Omega_{t}\wedge\overline{\Omega}_{t}}\xrightarrow{t\to 0}\mu_{0}:=C_{0}dx_{1}\ldots dx_{n}.

(3)

Notice
dx_{1}\ldots dx_{n}
is canonically defined due to the presence of an integral affine structure on
\Delta_{J}
. The constant in (
3
) is independent of
J
and its sole purpose is to make
\mu_{0}
a probability measure.

2.3
Fubini-Study and semipositive metrics

We now discuss line bundles and metrics on
X_{K}^{an}
.
A GAGA principle says the line bundles on
X_{K}^{an}
correspond to the line bundles
L
on the scheme
X_{K}
. A
continuous metric
on
L
assigns to each local section
s
a nonnegative continuous local function
\left\lVert s\right\rVert
on open subsets of
X_{K}^{an}
, compatible with the sheaf structure, such that
\left\lVert fs\right\rVert(x)=|f|_{x}\left\lVert s\right\rVert(x)
, and
\left\lVert s\right\rVert>0
if
s
is a local frame of
L
.
Given a continuous metric, any other continuous metric on
L
is of the form
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert e^{-\phi}
for some
\phi\in C^{0}(X^{an})
. As such
\phi
is referred to as a
potential
function.

For any model
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{X}
of
L\to X
, we can associate a unique metric
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}}
on
L
with the following property: if
s
is a nowhere vanishing local section of the line bundle
\mathcal{L}^{\otimes k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, on an open set
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{X}
, then
\left\lVert s\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}}\equiv 1
on
\mathcal{U}\cap X_{K}
. Such metrics are called
model metrics
, which are dense within the continuous metrics on
L\to X_{K}^{an}
. In the special case of trivial
L=\mathcal{O}\to X
, then
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{X}
corresponds to vertical
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors, which
gives the
model functions
. These functions form a dense subset of
C^{0}(X_{K}^{an})
.

A norm on a finite dimensional
K
-vector space
V

\left\lVert x+y\right\rVert_{V}\leq\max(\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{V},\left\lVert y\right\rVert_{V})
. Concretely, one can select an orthogonal
K
-basis

, namely

\left\lVert a_{1}s_{1}+\ldots+a_{N}s_{N}\right\rVert_{V}=\max\{|a_{1}|\left\lVert s_{1}\right\rVert_{V},\ldots,|a_{N}|\left\lVert s_{N}\right\rVert_{V}\},\quad\forall a_{i}\in K.

Now let
L
be a relatively ample line bundle, and let

. Given an ultrametric norm on the
K
-vector space
V=H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
which generates the line bundle

, the
NA Fubini-Study metric
on the line bundle

is defined by

\left\lVert s\right\rVert_{FS,l}(x)=\inf_{\tilde{s}\in V,\tilde{s}(x)=s^{\otimes l}(x)}\left\lVert\tilde{s}\right\rVert_{V}^{1/l},\quad\forall x\in X_{K}^{an}.

Concretely, given an orthogonal
K
-basis
s_{1},\ldots s_{N}
for

, we can write

\left\lVert s_{j}\right\rVert_{V}=e^{c_{j}}
for

\left\lVert s\right\rVert_{FS,l}(x)=\frac{|s(x)|}{\max_{j}\{|s_{j}(x)|e^{-c_{j}}\}^{1/l}},\quad\forall x\in X^{an}.

This can be recast in terms of potentials

\phi_{FS,l}=\frac{1}{l}\max_{1\leq i\leq N}(\log|s_{i}|-c_{i}),

(4)

where to evaluate
|s_{i}|
, we implicitly use a local trivializing section of
L
coming from the choice of a model
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle.

A
continuous semipositive metric

\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
. These potentials satisfy an

Proposition 2.2
.

[
3
, Chapter 6]

Let
L\to X
be a relatively ample line bundle, and let
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{X}
be a fixed model
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle. Then there exists a constant depending only on

\phi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})

is convex and

\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
27
, Lemma 2.3]

(Uniform Lipschitz estimate)
Let

, and let

be a relatively ample line bundle. Given a model
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{X}

2.4
NA Monge-Ampère equation

The non-archimedean Monge-Ampère (NA MA) measure can be defined through
intersection theory
.
Given any model
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{X}
for
L\to X_{K}
, we write
\mathcal{X}_{0}=\sum_{i}b_{i}E_{i}
, then the NA MA measure for the model metric
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}}
is the signed atomic measure

\text{MA}(\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}})=\sum_{E_{i}}b_{i}(\mathcal{L}^{n}\cdot E_{i})\delta_{E_{i}},

where
\delta_{E_{i}}
denotes the delta measure supported at the divisorial point associated to
E_{i}
. If
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}}
is furthermore semipositive, then the intersection numbers are non-negative, so
\text{MA}(\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}})
is a measure, and the total measure is the intersection number
(L^{n})
.

Fixing a reference model
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{X}
, then any
\phi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
can be
C^{0}
-approximated by the potentials of a sequence of semipositive model metrics
[
3
, Cor. 8.8]
, and its NA MA measure
\text{MA}(\phi)
can be defined as the unique weak limit Radon measure of the NA MA measures for its approximation sequence
[
5
, Cor. 3.5]
.

The central result of NA pluripotential theory is the solution of the NA Calabi conjecture.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
6
,
5
]
Let
L\to X
be a relatively ample line bundle, and let
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{X}
be a fixed model
\mathbb{Q}
-line model. Let
\mu
be any Radon probability measure supported on some fixed dual complex inside
X_{K}^{an}
. Then there is a unique (up to an additive constant)
\phi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
solving the NA MA equation
\text{MA}(\phi)=(L^{n})\mu
.

In particular, when
(X,L)\to\mathbb{D}^{*}
is a polarised meromorphic degeneration of compact CY manifolds, we can take
\mu_{0}
to be the normalised Lebesgue measure on
Sk(X)\subset X_{K}^{an}
. The unique (up to constant) solution
\phi_{0}\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
to the NA MA equation

\text{MA}(\phi_{0})=(L^{n})\mu_{0},

(5)

is called the
NA CY potential
, and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}}e^{-\phi_{0}}
is called the NA CY metric.

The original proof by Boucksom-Favre-Jonsson uses a variational strategy. They introduce a concave functional
E:\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})\to\mathbb{R}
, called the
Monge-Ampère energy
, whose first variation is formally the NA MA measure. Then they consider the convex functional
F_{\mu}:\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})\to\mathbb{R}
,

F_{\mu}(\phi)=-\frac{1}{(L^{n})}E(\phi)+\int_{X_{K}^{an}}\phi d\mu=-\frac{1}{(L^{n})}E(\phi)+\int_{Sk(X)}\phi d\mu.

(6)

Upon enlarging the class of semipositive metrics, one can use compactness to extract a minimiser
\phi
, and prove that
\phi
in fact solves the NA MA equation by a subtle
differentiability property
for the Monge-Ampère energy, and furthermore
\phi
has
C^{0}
-regularity via capacity estimates
[
5
,
6
]
.

Remark 2.5
.

In our context of polarised degeneration for compact Calabi-Yau manifolds, one can show that up to suitable normalisation, the CY potentials on
(X_{t},\frac{1}{|\log|t||}c_{1}(L))
converge in the
C^{0}
-hybrid topology to the NA CY potential
\phi_{0}
on

.

The NA MA equation satisfies the following
domination principle
.

Lemma 2.6
.

[
5
, Lemma 8.4]
Let
\varphi,\psi\in CPSH(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
, suppose

2.5
Relative volume interpretation

Boucksom-Eriksson
[
7
]
interpretated
E(\phi)
in terms of relative volume as follows. Given two ultrametric norms
\left\lVert\right\rVert,\left\lVert\right\rVert^{\prime}
on the
K
-vector space
H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
, the relative volume is

vol(\left\lVert\right\rVert,\left\lVert\right\rVert^{\prime})=\log\frac{\det(\left\lVert\right\rVert^{\prime})}{\det(\left\lVert\right\rVert)}.

Concretely, if both
\left\lVert\right\rVert
and
\left\lVert\right\rVert^{\prime}
are diagonal with respect to some NA orthogonal basis
s_{1},\ldots s_{N}
over the
K
-vector space
V
, namely

\left\lVert\sum a_{i}s_{i}\right\rVert=\max_{i}|a_{i}|\left\lVert s_{i}\right\rVert,\quad\left\lVert\sum a_{i}s_{i}\right\rVert^{\prime}=\max_{i}|a_{i}|\left\lVert s_{i}\right\rVert^{\prime},\quad\forall a_{i}\in K,

and
\left\lVert s_{i}\right\rVert^{\prime}=\lambda_{i}\left\lVert s_{i}\right\rVert
, then
vol(\left\lVert\right\rVert,\left\lVert\right\rVert^{\prime})=\log\prod_{i}\lambda_{i}
.

Given a continuous psh potentials
\phi,\psi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
, we can define a sequence of NA norms on the
K
-vector spaces
H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
for
l
large enough,

\left\lVert s\right\rVert_{l\phi}=\sup_{X_{K}^{an}}|s|_{\mathcal{L}}e^{-l\phi},

and similarly we define
\left\lVert s\right\rVert_{l\psi}
.
By
[
7
, Theorem A]
, the
relative MA energy
admits the formula

E(\phi)-E(\psi)=E(\phi,\psi)=\lim_{l\to+\infty}\frac{n!}{l^{n+1}}vol(\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{l\phi},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{l\psi}).

This determines

up to the choice of an additive constant.

From henceforth

Remark 3.1

Many ideas here have been foreshadowed in

3.1
Valuative independence

Let
l\geq 0
, and let
\{\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\}\subset H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
be a basis over the discretely valued field
K=\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
. Using some local trivialisation
\tau

\mathcal{L}
, we can regard
\theta_{\alpha}^{l}
as local meromorphic functions
\theta_{\alpha}/\tau

as valuations. The valuative independence condition in Def.

reads

val_{x}(\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\theta^{l}_{\alpha}/\tau)=\min_{\alpha:a_{\alpha}\neq 0}(val(a_{\alpha})+val_{x}(\theta^{l}_{\alpha}/\tau)),\quad\forall a_{\alpha}\in K.

Notice that the valuative independence condition is unaffected if we change the trivialising section
\tau
to
\tau^{\prime}
, because
val_{x}(\tau^{\prime}/\tau)
would cancel out on both sides. Thus we will omit
\tau
from the notation.
Viewing valuations as norms, this is equivalent to

|\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)=\max_{\alpha}|a_{\alpha}||\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x).

(8)

We now explain the characterisation for valuative independence in
[
27
, Section 3.2]
, in the special case of maximal degenerations
\dim Sk(X)=n
. Let
E_{J}=\cap_{i\in J}E_{i}
be a depth
n+1
intersection of SNC divisor components
E_{i}
on the central fibre, such that
E_{i}
achieve the minimum value of the log discrepancy, hence the corresponding simplex
\Delta_{J}\subset\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
is an
n
-dimensional face of
Sk(X)\subset\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}

Let
z_{i}
be the local equations of the divisors
E_{i}
, and we can arrange

by the semistable SNC condition. Using the local trivialisation of
\mathcal{L}
, we can regard sections
s
of
L
as local meromorphic functions, and Taylor expand

s=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{m+1}}c_{\beta}z^{\beta},\quad c_{\beta}\in\mathbb{C},

where only finitely many terms contain negative exponents.
The monomial valuation of

at any point

is

-\log|s|(x)=val_{x}(s)=\min\{\langle x,\beta\rangle|c_{\beta}\neq 0\}.

Consequently, on any given face
\Delta_{J}

is the maximum of a finite collection of integral affine linear functions, and in particular is piecewise affine linear.

For any fixed
l\geq 1
, and any nonzero section

,
the valuation functions

has a uniform Lipschitz estimate independent of

\mathcal{S}_{l}\subset Sk(X)
which contains all the lower dimensional faces of
Sk(X)
, together with the corner locus of
\log|s|
for all such
s
. We denote the connected components of

as

, which are open polyhedral domains inside the
n
-dimensional open faces
\Delta_{J}^{0}=\text{Int}(\Delta_{J})
.

On each

are affine linear, and correspondingly only one monomial term
c_{\alpha,\beta}z^{\beta_{\alpha}}
dominates all the other terms in the Taylor expansion of

. We partition the exponents
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
into equivalence classes modulo integer multiples of
(1,1\ldots 1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}

Lemma 3.2
.

[

For fixed

Suppose for
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}
, their leading exponents
\beta,\beta^{\prime}
fall within the same equivalence class. The Taylor expansion for
\theta_{\alpha}^{l}
and

have leading terms
c_{\alpha,\beta}z^{\beta}
and

c_{\alpha,\beta},c_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
\beta^{\prime}-\beta=k(1,\ldots 1)

. Thus
in the Taylor expansion for the linear combination
c_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\theta_{\alpha}^{l}-c_{\alpha,\beta}t^{-k}\theta_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{l}

cancels, hence at any point

violating the valuative independence condition.

x\in Sk(X)
. Furthermore, there is an invertible diagonal matrix
\bar{A}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\in GL(N(l),\mathbb{C})
, such that the transition matrix
\bar{A}^{-1}A
is a perturbation of the identity that does not change the leading order Taylor expansion terms of
\{\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\}
.

We will sometimes refer to
\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|:Sk(X)\to\mathbb{R}
as
tropical theta functions
.
These are canonical up to additive constants (corresponding to multiplying
\theta_{\alpha}^{l}
by powers of
t
).

3.2
Cost function, c-transform

For each
l\geq 1
, let
\{\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\}
be a valuative independent
K
-basis of
H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
.
For any given parameter
y\in Sk(X)
, we consider the set of functions on
Sk(X)
,

\{\frac{1}{l}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\frac{1}{l}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)\}_{l,\alpha}.

By construction, these functions take the value zero at the point
y\in Sk(X)
, and restrict to convex functions on the faces of
Sk(X)
.
By the uniform Lipschitz estimate in Lemma
2.3
, this set is precompact in
C^{0}(Sk(X))
.

We define the set of
limiting tropical theta functions

B_{y}
to be the set of all subsequential
C^{0}
-limits of these functions, as
l\to+\infty
.
For any
p\in B_{y}
, we denote the corresponding function on
Sk(X)
as
c(x,p;y)
. The function
c(x,p;y):Sk(X)\times B_{y}\to\mathbb{R}
is called the
cost function
. It inherits the uniform Lipschitz estimate

\sup_{p\in B}|c(x,p;y)-c(x^{\prime},p;y)|\leq C|x-x^{\prime}|

(9)

for any
x,x^{\prime}
on the same face of
Sk(X)
. Hence the set of functions
B_{y}
is compact in
C^{0}
-topology.
On each
n
-dimensional face of
Sk(X)
, the cost function is convex in
x
. The dependence on
y
is very mild by the following simple observation, and we will sometimes drop
y
in the notation.

Lemma 3.4
.

Given any
y,y^{\prime}\in Sk(X)
, there is a canonical homeomorphism between
B_{y}
and
B_{y^{\prime}}
, by sending the function
c(\cdot,p;y):Sk(X)\to\mathbb{R}
to
c(\cdot,p;y)-c(y^{\prime},p;y):Sk(X)\to\mathbb{R}
.

Given the cost function
c(x,p)
, we can define the
c
-transform
for bounded functions
\phi:Sk(X)\to\mathbb{R}
and
\psi:B_{y}\to\mathbb{R}
:

\psi^{c}(x)=\sup_{p\in B_{y}}c(x,p)-\psi(p),\quad\phi^{c}(p)=\sup_{x\in Sk(X)}c(x,p)-\phi(x).

(10)

This can be viewed as a generalisation of the Legendre transform. Formal arguments imply that

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
\phi\in L^{\infty}(Sk(X))
and
\psi\in L^{\infty}(B_{y})
, we have
(\phi^{c})^{c}\leq\phi
and
(\psi^{c})^{c}\leq\psi
. For
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in L^{\infty}(Sk(X))
, we have
\left\lVert\phi_{1}^{c}-\phi_{2}^{c}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\lVert\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
, and likewise for functions in
L^{\infty}(B_{y})
.

Moreover, for
\phi,\psi
in the image of the
c
-transform, the
c
-transform is involutive:
(\phi^{c})^{c}=\phi
and
(\psi^{c})^{c}=\psi
. We shall denote this class of functions
\phi
as
\mathcal{P}_{c}
. By Lemma
3.4
, the function class
\mathcal{P}_{c}
is independent of the choice of
y\in Sk(X)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

The functions
\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
have uniform Lipschitz continuity on
Sk(X)
, and are convex when restricted on any face of
Sk(X)
.

Proof.

This follows formally from the uniform Lipschitz estimate (
9
), and the convexity of
c(x,p;y)
in the
x
-variable on any face of
Sk(X)
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

For any
\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
, we have

\begin{cases}\phi(x)=\max_{p\in B_{y}}c(x,p)-\phi^{c}(p)\\
\phi^{c}(p)=\max_{x\in Sk(X)}c(x,p)-\phi(x).\end{cases}

Proof.

The continuity of
c(x,p)
in
x
follows from the Lipschitz bound, while the continuity in
p
is tautological by the
C^{0}
-topology on
B_{y}
. Thus
\phi^{c}
and
\phi=(\phi^{c})^{c}
are both continuous. The sup must be achieved by the compactness of
Sk(X)
and
B_{y}
.
∎

3.3
Comparison with NA semipositive potentials

The functions in
\mathcal{P}_{c}
have unique
maximal psh extensions
to semipositive potentials in
\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
.

Proposition 3.8
.

For any potential
\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
defined on
Sk(X)\subset X_{K}^{an}
, there is a unique
\phi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
, whose restriction to
Sk(X)\subset X_{K}^{an}
agrees with the prescribed
\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
, and moreover satisfies the

domination property

\phi(x)=\max\{v(x):v\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L}),v|_{Sk(X)}\leq\phi|_{Sk(X)}\},\quad\forall x\in X_{K}^{an}.

(11)

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
, and let
\phi^{c}:B_{y}\to\mathbb{R}
be its
c
-transform. We define
\tilde{\phi}\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
as the psh envelop of
\phi\circ r_{\mathcal{X}}\in C^{0}(X_{K}^{an})
(
cf.
Section
2.6
), hence

\tilde{\phi}(x)=\max\{v(x):v\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L}),v|_{Sk(X)}\leq\phi|_{Sk(X)}\}.

We need to show that
\tilde{\phi}(x)=\phi(x)
for any
x\in Sk(X)
. Clearly
\tilde{\phi}(x)\leq\phi(x)
.

Let
\epsilon>0
be any given small constant. By Lemma
3.7
,
for any
x\in Sk(X)
, we can find some
p\in B_{y}
, such that

\phi(x)=c(x,p;y)-\phi^{c}(p).

Since
c(\cdot,p;y)
is a subsequential
C^{0}
-limit, we can find some
\theta_{\alpha}^{l}
with
l\gg 1
, such that for any
x\in Sk(X)
,

|\frac{1}{l}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\frac{1}{l}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)-c(x,p;y)|<\epsilon

We find some NA Fubini-Study potential
\phi_{l}
on
(X_{K}^{an},L)
, such that

\phi_{l}=l^{-1}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|-l^{-1}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)-\phi^{c}(p)-\epsilon\quad\text{on $Sk(X)$}.

Thus
\phi_{l}\leq\phi
on
Sk(X)
, so the domination property of
\tilde{\phi}
implies
\tilde{\phi}(x)\geq\phi_{l}(x)
, whence for any
x\in Sk(X)
,

\tilde{\phi}(x)\geq\phi_{l}(x)\geq c(x,p)-\phi^{c}(p)-2\epsilon\geq\phi(x)-2\epsilon.

Since
x,\epsilon
are arbitrary, this proves that
\tilde{\phi}(x)\geq\phi(x)
for any
x\in Sk(X)
. Hence
\tilde{\phi}=\phi
on
Sk(X)
, and
\tilde{\phi}\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
furnishes the maximal psh extension.
∎

In the converse direction,

Proposition 3.9
.

Suppose
\phi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
, then its restriction to
Sk(X)
lies in
\mathcal{P}_{c}
.

Proof.

Any
\phi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
can be
C^{0}
-approximated by Fubini-Study potentials. For any large
l\gg 1
, and any section
s\in H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
, upon writing
s=\sum a_{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha}^{l}
, by the valuative independence condition

\log|s|(x)=\max_{\alpha:a_{\alpha}\neq 0}(\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-val(a_{\alpha})),\quad\forall x\in Sk(X).

A general Fubini-Study potential is of the form

\phi_{FS}=l^{-1}\max_{i}(\log|s_{i}|-c_{i})

for some
s_{i}\in H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
,
hence on
Sk(X)
, the potential
\phi_{FS}
agrees with

\max_{\alpha}(l^{-1}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-l^{-1}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)-c_{\alpha})

for suitable choices of
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
.

By the equicontinuity estimate in Lemma
2.3
and the compactness of
B_{y}
, there are some uniform constants
\epsilon_{l}
depending only on
l
, such that for any
\theta_{\alpha}^{l}
, there is some
p\in B_{y}
such that

\sup_{x\in Sk(X)}|l^{-1}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-l^{-1}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)-c(x,p;y)|\leq\epsilon_{l}\to 0,\quad l\to+\infty.

Thus we have found a function
\varphi=\max_{p}(c(x,p;y)-c_{\alpha})
satisfying

|\phi_{FS}-\varphi|\leq\epsilon_{l}.

The function
\varphi
lies in
\mathcal{P}_{c}
. The upshot is that on
Sk(X)
, any
\phi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
can be
C^{0}
-approximated by functions in
\mathcal{P}_{c}
. But
\mathcal{P}_{c}
is closed under
C^{0}
-topology, hence
\phi|_{Sk(X)}
belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{c}
.
∎

In summary, the functions in
\mathcal{P}_{c}
can be identified with the NA semipositive potentials satisfying the domination property.

Corollary 3.10
.

\mathcal{P}_{c}
is a convex subset inside
C^{0}(Sk(X))
.

Proof.

Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
, and
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
. By Prop.
3.8
, we take the maximal psh extension of
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
to potentials in
\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
, denoted as
\tilde{\phi}_{1},\tilde{\phi}_{2}
. Since
\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
is convex, we see
\lambda\tilde{\phi}_{1}+(1-\lambda)\tilde{\phi}_{2}\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
. This function restricts to
\lambda\phi_{1}+(1-\lambda)\phi_{2}
, which belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{c}
by Prop.
3.9
.
∎

Corollary 3.11
.

The functions
l^{-1}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|:Sk(X)\to\mathbb{R}
belong to
\mathcal{P}_{c}
whenever
l\geq 1
.

Proof.

The function
l^{-1}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|:Sk(X)\to\mathbb{R}
can be extended to a Fubini-Study potential in
\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
, so the restriction to
Sk(X)
is a function in
\mathcal{P}_{c}
by Prop.
3.9
.
∎

3.4
Gradient convex hull and Okounkov body

The set of limiting tropical theta functions
B_{y}
is closely related to certain convex sets
\mathcal{C}_{y},\Delta_{y}
.

Recall that for any convex function
u
defined on an open convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
subgradient
at any point
x
is the set

\nabla u(x)=\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:u(x^{\prime})\geq u(x)+\langle x^{\prime}-x,p\rangle,\forall x^{\prime}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

When this set is a singleton, we often identify it with the gradient vector.
Recall that
\mathcal{P}_{c}
is defined independent of the parameter
y\in Sk(X)
.
Now for any
\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
, its restriction to any
n
-dimensional face
\Delta_{J}\subset Sk(X)
is a convex function by Lemma
3.6
. Thus at any
x\in\Delta_{J}^{0}=\text{Int}(\Delta_{J})
, we can define the
gradient convex hull

\mathcal{C}_{x}=\{p:p\in\nabla\phi(x)\text{ for some }\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}\}\subset T_{y}^{*}\Delta_{J}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(12)

Lemma 3.12
.

\mathcal{C}_{x}
is a closed, bounded and convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

The boundedness of
\mathcal{C}_{x}
follows from the uniform Lipschitz estimate for
\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
. If
p_{i}\in\nabla\phi_{i}
for some
\phi_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
with
i=1,2
, then any convex combination
\lambda p_{1}+(1-\lambda)p_{2}
for
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
is a subgradient for

\lambda\phi_{1}+(1-\lambda)\phi_{2}
, which belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{c}
. Thus
\mathcal{C}_{x}
is convex.

The function class
\mathcal{P}_{c}
is compact modulo an additive constant that does not affect the subgradients. Given any sequence
p_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{x}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
converging to
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we can find
\phi_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
which are uniformly bounded, such that
p_{i}\in\nabla\phi_{i}(x)
. Then we can take a subsequential
C^{0}
-limit
\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
, and we observe
p\in\nabla\phi(x)
. Thus
\mathcal{C}_{x}
is closed.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

We have abused the same notation
p
to denote gradient, and the elements in
B_{y}
. Their relationship will be more apparent in Section
3.5
.

Our next goal is to introduce an Okounkov body, associated to a semigroup constructed from the gradient of tropical theta functions.

On any open
n
-dimensional faces
\Delta_{J}^{0}
, there is a natural integral affine structure.
Recall from Section
3.1
that for any
l\geq 1
, there is a finite union
\mathcal{S}_{l}
of codimension one walls including all the lower dimensional faces of
Sk(X)
, such that on each connected component
O_{a}\subset Sk(X)\setminus\mathcal{S}_{l}
, the tropical theta functions
\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|=-\langle x,\beta_{\alpha}\rangle
are
integral affine linear
on
O_{a}
.

Notation
.

We say that a point
y
on an
n
-dimensional open face
\Delta_{J}^{0}\subset Sk(X)
is
sufficiently irrational
, if
y\notin\cup_{l\geq 1}\mathcal{S}_{l}
. Then at the point
y
, all the tropical theta functions
\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|
have a unique gradient

p(\alpha):=(\nabla\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|)(y)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\simeq T_{y}^{*}\Delta_{J}^{0}.

(13)

(As a caveat, the domain of affine linearity for
\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|
around
y
may become smaller as
l\to+\infty
, and the infinite intersection may not be open.) We will build a semigroup
\Gamma_{y}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
from the data of tropical theta functions. As a set,

\Gamma_{y,l}=\bigcup_{\alpha}\{(\nabla\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|)(y)\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n},\quad\Gamma_{y}=\bigcup_{l\geq 0}(\{l\}\times\Gamma_{y,l})\subset\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

(14)

The
Okounkov body

\Delta_{y}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the closure of the convex hull for
\bigcup_{l\geq 1}l^{-1}\Gamma_{y,l}
.
We denote
\Delta_{y}^{0}=\text{Int}(\Delta_{y})
.

Lemma 3.14
.

(Basic properties of
\Gamma_{y}
)

1.

For any given
l
, the gradients
p(\alpha)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
are all distinct for different
\alpha
.

2.

\Gamma_{y,l}
is the union of all
(\nabla\log|s|)(y)
for all nonzero
s\in H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
.

3.

The subset
\Gamma_{y}
is a
semigroup
under addition.

4.

The semigroup
\Gamma_{y}
generates
\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
as a group.

5.

Any
p\in\Gamma_{y,l}
satisfies the uniform linear growth bound
|p|\leq Cl
for some uniform constant independence of
l,y
. Consequently, the Okounkov body is bounded.

Proof.

The integral lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
inside
T_{y}^{*}\Delta_{J}^{0}
can be identified as
\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}/\mathbb{Z}(1,\ldots 1)
, where the Taylor exponents
\beta_{\alpha}
lie in
\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
, and the exponent of
t=z_{0}\ldots z_{n}
is
(1,\ldots 1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
. The gradient
p(\alpha)=(\nabla\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|)(y)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is then the image of
-\beta_{\alpha}
inside the quotient
\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}/\mathbb{Z}(1,\ldots 1)
. These are all distinct by Lemma
3.2
, which proves item 1.

Given any
s\in H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
, we can expand

s=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha}^{l},\quad a_{\alpha}\in K.

Since
y
is sufficiently irrational, we can find some domain of linearity
O_{a}\subset Sk(X)
containing
y
in its interior, where for some choice of
\alpha
,

\log|s|=\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|-val(a_{\alpha}),

hence
(\nabla\log|s|)(y)=(\nabla\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|)(y)\in\Gamma_{y,l}
. This proves item 2.

Now given
\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\in H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
and
\theta_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{l^{\prime}}\in H^{0}(X_{K},l^{\prime}L)
,
using item 2, we have

(\nabla\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|)(y)+(\nabla\log|\theta_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{l^{\prime}}|)(y)=(\nabla\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\theta_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{l^{\prime}}|)(y)\in\Gamma_{y,l+l^{\prime}},

so
\Gamma_{y}
is closed under addition. This proves item 3.

Item 4 follows from item 2 and the relative ampleness of
L
, by the argument in
[
8
, Prop. 3.3]
.

Item 5 follows from the uniform Lipschitz bound in Lemma
2.3
.
∎

The general theory of Okounkov bodies for semigroups then imply the following.

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be any fixed compact convex subset of
\Delta_{y}^{0}
.

1.

(Integer points) For all large
l\geq l_{0}(\mathcal{K})
, if
p\in\mathcal{K}\cap l^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then
lp\in\Gamma_{y,l}
.

2.

The subset
\bigcup_{l\geq 0}\{l\}\times(\Gamma_{y,l}\cap l\mathcal{K})\subset\Gamma_{y}
is a sub-semigroup. It is contained inside a finitely generated sub-semigroup of
\Gamma_{y}
.

3.

The Lebesgue volume of the Okounkov body
\text{Vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta_{y})=\frac{(L^{n})}{n!}
.

Proof.

Item 1 follows from the argument in
[
33
, Lemma 2.3]
.

For item 2, notice that the convexity of
\mathcal{K}
implies that
\bigcup_{l\geq 0}\{l\}\times(\Gamma_{y,l}\cap l\mathcal{K})
is closed under addition, hence defines a sub-semigroup. Now we can find a closed rational convex polyhedron
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
, such that
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\subset\Delta_{y}^{0}
. Then

\bigcup_{l\geq 0}\{l\}\times(\Gamma_{y,l}\cap l\mathcal{K})\subset\bigcup_{l\geq 0}\{l\}\times(\Gamma_{y,l}\cap l\mathcal{K}^{\prime})\subset\Gamma_{y}.

But by item 1, we have
\Gamma_{y,l}\cap l\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap l\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
for large enough
l
, whence
\bigcup_{l\geq 0}\{l\}\times(\Gamma_{y,l}\cap l\mathcal{K}^{\prime})
is a finitely generated sub-semigroup.

For item 3, we take an exhaustion of
\Delta_{y}^{0}
by compact convex subsets
\mathcal{K}
, so that the points in
l^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap(\Delta_{y}\setminus\mathcal{K})
are negligible. Using item 1 and Riemann-Roch, the Lebesgue volume of the bounded convex body
\Delta_{y}
is

\text{Vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta_{y})=\lim_{l\to+\infty}\frac{|\Delta_{y}^{0}\cap l^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{n}|}{l^{n}}=\lim_{l\to+\infty}\frac{|\Delta_{y,l}|}{l^{n}}=\lim_{l\to+\infty}\frac{\dim_{K}H^{0}(X_{K},lL)}{l^{n}}=\frac{(L^{n})}{n!}.

∎

We now compare the
gradient convex hull
with the
Okounkov body
.

Lemma 3.16
.

Suppose
y\in Sk(X)
is sufficiently irrational. Then for any
l\geq 1
,

1.

The Okounkov body
\Delta_{y}\subset\mathcal{C}_{y}
.

2.

Suppose there is some
\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
such that
\nabla\phi(y)
consists of a single point
p
, then
p\in\Delta_{y}
.

Proof.

For
l\geq 1
, the functions
l^{-1}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|:Sk(X)\to\mathbb{R}
belong to
\mathcal{P}_{c}
, hence their gradients lie inside
\mathcal{C}_{y}
. This proves
\Delta_{y}\subset\mathcal{C}_{y}
.

By the proof of Prop.
3.9
, any Fubini-Study potential restricted to
Sk(X)
is of the form

\phi_{FS}=\max_{\alpha}(l^{-1}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-l^{-1}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)-c_{\alpha}),

hence its gradient lies in the convex hull
\Delta_{y}
. Now any
\phi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
can be
C^{0}
-approximated by Fubini-Study potentials, hence any function
\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
can be
C^{0}
approximated by a sequence
\phi_{FS,l}
as
l\to+\infty
. Then there is a sequence of gradients
p_{l}\in\Delta_{y}
, such that

\phi_{FS,l}(x)-\phi_{FS,l}(y)\geq\langle p_{l},x-y\rangle,\quad\forall x\in\Delta_{J}^{0}.

Since
p_{l}\in\Delta_{y}\subset\mathcal{C}_{y}
are uniformly bounded, they converge to some
p_{\infty}\in\Delta_{y}
.
Taking the
C^{0}
-limit,

\phi(x)-\phi(y)\geq\langle p_{\infty},x-y\rangle,\quad\forall x\in\Delta_{J}^{0},

hence
p_{\infty}\in\nabla\phi(y)
. If
\nabla\phi(y)
consists of only one point
p
, then
p=p_{\infty}\in\Delta_{y}
.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

It is a curious question whether
\mathcal{C}_{y}=\Delta_{y}
for sufficiently irrational
y
. The
C^{0}
-approximation argument above is unfortunately not strong enough to prove this equality. As a toy case, on the real line
\mathbb{R}
one can find a sequence of convex functions
\max(|x|,\epsilon)
for
\epsilon>0
, which all have zero gradient at the origin, but the limit function
|x|
has many subgradients.

Remark 3.18
.

It would be interesting to give a more birational geometric interpretation for the gradient convex hull and the Okounkov body.

3.5
Okounkov body and cost function

Let
y\in Sk(X)
be a sufficiently irrational point.
The Okounkov body can be used to produce limiting tropical theta functions in
B_{y}
.
Recall that a real valued function on a semigroup
F:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
is called
subadditive
if

F(a)+F(b)\geq F(a+b).

For the semigroup
\Gamma_{y}
, by item 1 in Lemma
3.14
, for each given
l\geq 1
, there is a natural bijection between the indexing set for
\{\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\}
, and the gradients
p(\alpha):=(\nabla\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|)(y)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Now for any
x\in Sk(X)
, we can define the function

F_{x}:\Gamma_{y}\mapsto\mathbb{R},\quad(l,p(\alpha))\mapsto\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}|(y).

Lemma 3.19
.

For any choice of
x\in Sk(X)
, the function
F_{x}
is subadditive.

Proof.

We can expand the product

\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\theta_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{l^{\prime}}=\sum_{\gamma}a_{\gamma}\theta_{\gamma}^{l+l^{\prime}},\quad a_{\gamma}\in K.

By the valuative independence condition,

\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)+\log|\theta_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{l^{\prime}}|(x)=\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\theta_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{l^{\prime}}|(x)=\max_{\gamma}\log|\theta_{\gamma}^{l+l^{\prime}}|(x)-val(a_{\gamma}).

We pick
\gamma
so that
p(\alpha)+p(\alpha^{\prime})=p(\gamma)
. Then on the domain of affine linearity
O_{a}\subset Sk(X)\setminus\mathcal{S}_{l+l^{\prime}}
containing the point
y
, the dominant term in the expansion of
\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\theta_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{l^{\prime}}
has gradient
p(\gamma)
, so that on
O_{a}
we have

\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|+\log|\theta_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{l^{\prime}}|=\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\theta_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{l^{\prime}}|=\log|\theta_{\gamma}^{l+l^{\prime}}|-val(a_{\gamma}).

In particular,

\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)+\log|\theta_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{l^{\prime}}|(y)=\log|\theta_{\gamma}^{l+l^{\prime}}|(y)-val(a_{\gamma}),

\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)+\log|\theta_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{l^{\prime}}|(x)\geq\log|\theta_{\gamma}^{l+l^{\prime}}|(x)-val(a_{\gamma}),

hence

F_{x}(l,p(\alpha))+F_{x}(l^{\prime},p(\alpha^{\prime}))\geq F_{x}(l+l^{\prime},p(\alpha)+p(\alpha^{\prime}))

which means
F_{x}
is subadditive.
∎

Given any
p\in\Delta_{y}^{0}
,
the general theory for subadditive functions on semigroups allows us to define a function
c(\cdot,p;y)
in the class
B_{y}
.

Proposition 3.20
.

(Limiting tropical theta functions I) Let
y\in Sk(X)
be a sufficiently irrational point.

1.

Given
p\in\Delta_{y}^{0}
, for any sequence
(l,p(\alpha))\in\Gamma_{y,l}
such that
l^{-1}p(\alpha)\to p
as
l\to+\infty
, there is a well defined function in
B_{y}
,

c(x,p;y)=\lim_{l\to+\infty,l^{-1}p(\alpha)\to p}l^{-1}(\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)).

2.

As
p\in\Delta_{y}^{0}
varies, the function
c(x,p;y)
is convex in
p\in\Delta_{y}^{0}
.

3.

For
p
inside any given compact convex subset
\mathcal{K}\subset\Delta_{y}^{0}
, then
c(x,p;y)
is a bounded Lipschitz function in
p
.

4.

Inside any given compact subset
\mathcal{K}\subset\Delta_{y}^{0}
, there is a uniform convergence as
l\to+\infty
,

\sup_{x\in Sk(X),l^{-1}p(\alpha)\in\mathcal{K}}|l^{-1}(\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y))-c(x,l^{-1}p(\alpha);y)|\leq\epsilon_{l}\to 0.

Proof.

We divide the proof into a few steps.

1.

As in Section
3.2
, there is always some subsequential
C^{0}
-limit function for
l^{-1}(\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}|(y))
, which defines an element of
B_{y}
. We note

l^{-1}F_{x}(l,p(\alpha))=l^{-1}(\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)).

Since
|F_{x}|\leq Cl
, and
F_{x}
is subadditive on
\Gamma_{y}
, by
[
33
, Thm. 3.1]
the sequence
l^{-1}F(l,p(\alpha))
converges to a unique limit, depending on
p
but not on the details of the subsequence. Thus the function
c(x,p;y)
is well defined.

2.

Furthermore, by passing the subadditive property to the limit, we see
c(x,p;y)
is convex in
p\in\Delta_{y}^{0}

[
33
, Thm. 3.1]
.

3.

The function
c(x,p;y)
is bounded and convex in
p\in\Delta_{y}^{0}
, hence it is Lipschitz on any compact convex subset of
\Delta_{y}^{0}
.

4.

If the uniform convergence in item 4 fails, then we can extract some subsequence with
l\to+\infty
, and
l^{-1}\alpha(p)\in\mathcal{K}
,
x\in Sk(X)
depending on
l
, such that

|l^{-1}(\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y))-c(x,l^{-1}p(\alpha);y)|\geq C^{-1}.

By the compactness of
Sk(X)
and
\mathcal{K}
, we may assume
x
converges to
x_{0}\in Sk(X)
, and
l^{-1}p(\alpha)
converges to
p
. By the equicontinuity in
x
, for
l\gg 1
,

l^{-1}(\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x_{0})-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y))-c(x_{0},l^{-1}p(\alpha);y)|\geq\frac{1}{2C}.

But
l^{-1}(\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x_{0})-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y))\to c(x_{0},p;y)
by item 1, and

|c(x_{0},p;y)-c(x_{0},l^{-1}p(\alpha);y)|\leq C|p-l^{-1}p(\alpha)|\to 0

by item 3, so we get a contradiction.

∎

The limiting tropical theta functions arising from
p\in\Delta_{y}^{0}
enjoy some further nice properties, which makes the
c
-transform resemble the classical Legendre transform.

Proposition 3.21
.

(Limiting tropical theta functions II) Let
y\in Sk(X)
be a sufficiently irrational point, and let
\mathcal{K}\subset\Delta_{y}^{0}
be a fixed closed convex subset.

1.

(Domain of affine linearity) There is some open convex polyhedral neighbourhood
y\in U
depending on
\mathcal{K}
, contained in the open
n
-dimensional face
\Delta_{J}^{0}\subset Sk(X)
, such that for any
p\in\mathcal{K}\subset\Delta_{y}^{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the cost function is affine linear on
U
,

c(x,p;y)=\langle p,x-y\rangle,\quad\forall x\in U.

2.

In particular, for any
p\in\Delta_{y}^{0}
, the cost function
c(x,p;y)
has a unique gradient
p
at the point
y
.

3.

Suppose
\tilde{c}(\cdot,p;y)\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
is the maximal psh extension for
c(\cdot,p;y)\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
in the sense of Prop.
3.8
,

\tilde{c}(x,p;y)=\max\{v(x):v\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L}),v|_{Sk(X)}\leq c(\cdot,p;y)\},\quad\forall x\in X_{K}^{an}.

Then
\tilde{c}(\cdot,p;y)
factors through the retraction map
r_{\mathcal{X}}:X_{K}^{an}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
over the open set
U\subset Sk(X)\subset\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, namely

\tilde{c}(x,p;y)=c(r_{\mathcal{X}}(x),p;y),\quad\forall x\in r_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(U).

Proof.

We divide the proof into a few steps.

1.

By item 2 in Cor.
3.15
, we can find a finitely generated sub-semigroup for
\Gamma_{y}
containing
\bigcup_{l\geq 0}\{l\}\times(\Gamma_{y,l}\cap l\mathcal{K})\subset\Gamma_{y}
.
Each
\log|\theta_{\alpha_{i}}^{l_{i}}|
is affine linear on some open polyhedron
O_{a}
containing
y
. We take
U
to be an open convex polyhedral neighbourhood of
y
contained in the finite intersection of these
O_{a}
.

For any
l\geq 1
, any
(l,p(\alpha))\in\Gamma_{y,l}\cap l\mathcal{K}
can be written as

(l,p(\alpha))=\sum_{i}k_{i}(l_{i},p(\alpha_{i})),\quad k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\quad(l_{i},p(\alpha_{i}))\in\Gamma_{y,l_{i}}.

We consider the linear expansion of the product

\prod_{i}(\theta_{\alpha_{i}}^{l_{i}})^{\otimes k_{i}}=\sum_{\gamma}a_{\gamma}\theta_{\gamma}^{l}\in H^{0}(X_{K},lL),\quad a_{\gamma}\in K.

(15)

Thus on
Sk(X)
, the valuative independence condition implies

\sum_{i}k_{i}\log|\theta_{\alpha_{i}}^{l_{i}}|=\log|\prod_{i}(\theta_{\alpha_{i}}^{l_{i}})^{\otimes k_{i}}|=\max_{\gamma}\log|\theta_{\gamma}^{l}|-val(a_{\gamma}).

(16)

In particular,

\sum_{i}k_{i}\log|\theta_{\alpha_{i}}^{l_{i}}|\geq\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|-val(a_{\gamma}).

However, since
y
is a sufficiently irrational point, there is a unique dominant term in the linear expansion (
15
) on some smalll neighbourhood of
y
. The exponent of this term is determined by the gradient

(\nabla\log|\prod_{i}(\theta_{\alpha_{i}}^{l_{i}})^{\otimes k_{i}}|)(y)=\sum_{i}k_{i}(\nabla\log|\theta_{\alpha_{i}}^{l_{i}}|)(y)=\sum_{i}k_{i}p(\alpha_{i})=p(\alpha).

Thus in this small neighbourhood, the maximum in (
16
) is achieved by
\gamma=\alpha
. In particular

\sum_{i}k_{i}\log|\theta_{\alpha_{i}}^{l_{i}}|(y)=\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)-val(a_{\alpha}).

Combining the above, for any
x\in Sk(X)
,

\sum_{i}k_{i}\left(\log|\theta_{\alpha_{i}}^{l_{i}}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha_{i}}^{l_{i}}|(y)\right)\geq\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y).

On the open set
U
, all the finitely many functions
\log|\theta_{\alpha_{i}}^{l_{i}}|
are affine linear,

\log|\theta_{\alpha_{i}}^{l_{i}}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha_{i}}^{l_{i}}|(y)=\langle p(\alpha_{i}),x-y\rangle,

hence

\begin{split}&\langle p(\alpha),x-y\rangle=\sum_{i}k_{i}\langle p(\alpha_{i}),x-y\rangle\\
=&\sum_{i}k_{i}\left(\log|\theta_{\alpha_{i}}^{l_{i}}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha_{i}}^{l_{i}}|(y)\right)\geq\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y).\end{split}

But the convexity of
\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|
gives the reverse inequality

\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)\geq\langle p(\alpha),x-y\rangle.

We conclude that on the open neighbourhood
y\in U
,
the tropical theta function is affine linear
,

\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)=\langle p(\alpha),x-y\rangle,

(17)

whenever
l^{-1}\alpha(p)\in\mathcal{K}
. The key point here is that the set
U
is fixed independent of
l,\alpha
, but only depends on
\mathcal{K}
.

Since
\mathcal{K}
is a closed convex subset of
\Delta_{y}^{0}
,
for any
p\in\mathcal{K}
, we can find a sequence of
(l,p(\alpha))\in l\mathcal{K}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
such that
l^{-1}p(\alpha)\to p\in\mathcal{K}
as
l\to+\infty
. We note that for
l\gg 1
, then
l\mathcal{K}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}=\Gamma_{y,l}\cap l\mathcal{K}
by item 1 in Cor.
3.15
. Thus

\begin{split}c(x,p;y)=&\lim_{l\to+\infty,l^{-1}p(\alpha)\to p}l^{-1}(\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y))\\
=&\lim_{l\to+\infty,l^{-1}p(\alpha)\to p}l^{-1}\langle p(\alpha),x-y\rangle\\
=&\langle p,x-y\rangle\end{split}

for all
x\in U
. This proves item 1.

2.

Item 2 clearly follows from item 1.

3.

We return to
\theta_{\alpha}^{l}
for
l^{-1}p(\alpha)\in\mathcal{K}
and
l\gg 1
. Near the depth
(n+1)
intersection stratum
E_{J}
corresponding to the
n
-dimensional face
\Delta_{J}\subset Sk(X)
, we can Taylor expand

\theta_{\alpha}^{l}=\sum_{\gamma}c_{\gamma}z^{\gamma},\quad c_{\gamma}\in\mathbb{C}.

Since
U
is an open convex polyhedral subset of
\Delta_{J}^{0}\subset Sk(X)
, such that
\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|
is affine linear on
U
with gradient
p(\alpha)
by (
17
), we can
find a unique leading exponent
\beta_{\alpha}
in the Taylor expansion, and it satisfies
p(\alpha)=-\beta_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}/\mathbb{Z}(1,\ldots 1)\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Upon writing

\theta_{\alpha}^{l}=c_{\alpha,\beta}z^{\beta_{\alpha}}+\sum_{\gamma\neq\beta_{\alpha}}c_{\gamma}z^{\gamma}.

then

\log|\sum_{\gamma\neq\beta_{\alpha}}c_{\gamma}z^{\gamma}|(x)<\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)+\langle p(\alpha),x-y\rangle,\quad\forall x\in U\subset Sk(X),

hence

\log|\sum_{\gamma\neq\beta_{\alpha}}c_{\gamma}z^{\gamma}|(x)<\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)+\langle p(\alpha),x-y\rangle,\quad\forall x\in r_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(U).

The ultrametric property then implies that for any
x\in r_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(U)
,

\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)=\log|c_{\alpha,\beta}z^{\beta_{\alpha}}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)=\langle p(\alpha),r_{\mathcal{X}}(x)-y\rangle.

(18)

For any given
\epsilon>0
, similar to the proof of Prop.
3.8
, we find some
\theta_{\alpha}^{l}
with
l\gg 1
and
l^{-1}p(\alpha)\in\mathcal{K}
, such that

\sup_{x\in Sk(X)}|\frac{1}{l}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\frac{1}{l}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)-c(x,p;y)|<\epsilon.

Then the domination property of
\tilde{c}(x,p;y)
implies that for any
x\in X_{K}^{an}
,

\tilde{c}(x,p;y)\geq\frac{1}{l}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\frac{1}{l}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)-\epsilon.

Taking the limit as
l\to+\infty
, over
x\in r_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(U)
, we have

\tilde{c}(x,p;y)\geq\lim_{l\to+\infty,l^{-1}p(\alpha)\to p}l^{-1}\langle p(\alpha),r_{\mathcal{X}}(x)-y\rangle-\epsilon=\langle p,r_{\mathcal{X}}(x)-y\rangle-\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we obtain

\tilde{c}(x,p;y)\geq\langle p,r_{\mathcal{X}}(x)-y\rangle,\quad\forall x\in r_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(U).

(19)

In the reverse direction, recall that any
\phi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
satisfies
\phi\leq\phi\circ r_{\mathcal{X}}
, hence over

\tilde{c}(x,p;y)\leq c(r_{\mathcal{X}}(x),p;y)=\langle p,r_{\mathcal{X}}(x)-y\rangle.

Combining the above shows the equality

\tilde{c}(x,p;y)=c(r_{\mathcal{X}}(x),p;y)=\langle p,r_{\mathcal{X}}(x)-y\rangle.

∎

Remark 3.22
.

As a caveat, the convex domain
U
of affine linearity depends on
\mathcal{K}
, and may shrink to a point when
\mathcal{K}
increases to cover
\Delta_{y}^{0}
.

We can naturally embed
\Delta_{y}^{0}
as an open subset of
B_{y}
.

Proposition 3.23
.

(Embedding of
\Delta_{y}^{0}
)
The assignment

\Delta_{y}^{0}\to B_{y},\quad p\mapsto c(\cdot,p;y)

is a homeomorphism onto an open subset of
B_{y}
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into a few steps.

1.

By Prop.
3.20
, this assignment is well defined and depends continuously on
p\in\Delta_{y}^{0}
.

2.

We claim the image of this assignment is an open subset in
B_{y}
. Any function
c(x,p^{\prime};y)
in
B_{y}
is a
C^{0}
-subsequential limit for
l^{-1}(\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y))
as
l\to+\infty
. After passing to a further subsequence, without loss
l^{-1}p(\alpha)
converges to some
p_{\infty}\in\Delta_{y}
. If
p_{\infty}\in\Delta_{y}^{0}
, then
c(x,p^{\prime};y)=c(x,p_{\infty};y)
lies in the image. Otherwise
p_{\infty}\in\partial\Delta_{y}
. But since
l^{-1}(\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y))
has gradient
l^{-1}p(\alpha)
at
y
, upon passing to the
C^{0}
-limit for this sequence of convex functions, we deduce that
p_{\infty}
belongs to the subgradient set of
c(x,p^{\prime};y)
at the point
x=y
.

The upshot is that any function
c(x,p^{\prime};y)
in
B_{y}
lies in the image, if and only if its gradient at
y
is contained in
\Delta_{y}^{0}
. This is an open property under the
C^{0}
-topology for uniformly Lipschitz convex functions, so the image is indeed open.

3.

By Prop.
3.21
, the inverse map from the image back to
\Delta_{y}^{0}
, is given by taking the gradient at the point
y
, and this inverse is continuous with respect to the
C^{0}
-topology on
B_{y}
, because for a sequence of convex functions with uniform Lipschitz bounds, the limit of their gradients at
y
is a subgradient of the limiting convex function.

∎

Remark 3.24
.

From the definition of
B_{y}
as a subset of
C^{0}(Sk(X))
, it is only a compact topological space under the
C^{0}
-topology on functions, but has no a priori linear structure. On the other hand,
\Delta_{y}^{0}
is an open convex set, so one can take linear combinations. By Lemma
3.4
, one can canonically identify the
B_{y}
for different choices of sufficiently irrational

. It is curious what is the relationship between different
\Delta_{y}^{0}\subset B_{y}
when
y
varies. To answer this question, one presumably needs more information on the cost function
c(x,p;y)
.

Remark 3.25
.

As a caveat, the inclusion map
\Delta_{y}^{0}\to B_{y}
need not extend to a homeomorphism between the compact sets
\Delta_{y}\to B_{y}
. First, due to the ignorance of the uniform Lipschitz estimate in

near the boundary
\partial\Delta_{y}
, we do not know if item 1 in Prop.
3.20
still holds for

. Second, some functions in

(but not in the image of
\Delta_{y}^{0}
) may have non-unique subgradients at

. We expect

is in fact homeomorphic to the essential skeleton of some mirror CY family, which is topologically

in the case of strict CY manifolds (See
[
27
, Sections 5,6]

is a convex set, hence contractible.

Corollary 3.26
.

Let
y\in Sk(X)
be a sufficiently irrational point. Suppose a given function
\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
has a unique gradient
\nabla\phi(y)=p
, and
p\in\Delta_{y}^{0}
. Then

\phi(y)=-\phi^{c}(p)
, and
\phi(x)\geq\phi(y)+c(x,p;y)
for any
x\in Sk(X)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.7
, there must be some
p^{\prime}\in B_{y}
such that

\phi(y)=c(y,p^{\prime};y)-\phi^{c}(p^{\prime})=-\phi^{c}(p^{\prime}).

By the proof of Prop.
3.23
item 2, because the gradient
p=\nabla\phi(y)
is contained in
\Delta_{y}^{0}
, the function
c(x,p^{\prime};y)
in
B_{y}
must lie in the embedding image of
\Delta_{y}^{0}
, and
p=p^{\prime}
. This shows
\phi(y)=-\phi^{c}(p)
.

Now the definition of
c
-transform implies

\phi(x)\geq c(x,p;y)-\phi^{c}(p)=c(x,p;y)+\phi(y),

for any
x\in Sk(X)
.
∎

The interpretation is that if we prescribe the value
\phi(y)
and the gradient
p
at
y
, then the function
\phi(y)+c(x,p;y)
minimises among all
\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
subject to this prescription. Intuitively

3.6
A factorisation criterion for the retraction map

By Prop.
3.8
,
3.9
, we identify the functions in
\mathcal{P}_{c}
with the potentials in
\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
satisfying the domination property. We shall prove a criterion for a potential to factorise under the retraction map
r_{\mathcal{X}}:X_{K}^{an}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
over some open subset of
Sk(X)
.

Proposition 3.27
.

Suppose
\phi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
satisfies the domination property (
11
).
Suppose
y\in Sk(X)
is a sufficiently irrational point, such that its subgradient set
\nabla\phi(y)\subset\mathcal{C}_{y}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

is contained in the interior of the Okounkov body
\Delta_{y}^{0}
. Then there exists some open neighbourhood
y\in U_{y}\subset Sk(X)
, such that
\phi=\phi\circ r_{\mathcal{X}}
holds on
r_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(U_{y})
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into a few steps.

1.

We take a closed convex set
\mathcal{K}\subset\Delta_{y}^{0}
, which contains the subgradient set
\nabla\phi(y)
in its interior. (Here we do not need to assume
\nabla\phi(y)
is a singleton). We can find some open convex polyhedral neighbourhood
y\in U
depending on
\mathcal{K}
, such that the conclusions of Prop.
3.21
hold, and in particular

c(x,p;y)=\langle p,x-y\rangle,\quad\forall x\in U,\quad\forall p\in\mathcal{K}.

Since
\nabla\phi(y)\subset\text{Int}(\mathcal{K})
, and
\phi
is a convex function on
U
, we can find an open neighbourhood
y\in U_{y}\subset U
, such that

\nabla\phi(x)\subset\text{Int}(\mathcal{K}),\quad\forall x\in U_{y}.

2.

We consider any
y^{\prime}\in U_{y}
, which may not be sufficiently irrational. By Lemma
3.7
, there is some
p^{\prime}\in B_{y}
, such that

\phi(y^{\prime})=c(y^{\prime},p^{\prime};y)-\phi^{c}(p^{\prime}).

By the definition of
B_{y}
, the function
c(\cdot,p^{\prime};y)
is a
C^{0}
-limit for some sequence

\frac{1}{l}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\frac{1}{l}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y).

We focus on large
l\gg 1
, then Cor.
3.15

\Gamma_{y,l}\cap l\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap l\mathcal{K}
.

3.

Recall that if
p(\alpha)=(\nabla\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|)(y)\in\Gamma_{y,l}\cap l\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap l\mathcal{K}
, then by (
17
),

\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)=\langle p(\alpha),x-y\rangle,\forall x\in U_{y},

and in particular
(\nabla\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|)(y^{\prime})=p(\alpha)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap l\mathcal{K}
.
This construction exhausts all the
\theta_{\alpha}^{l}
such that

(\nabla\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|)(y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap l\mathcal{K}
.

4.

We claim that the function
c(\cdot,p^{\prime};y)
in fact lies in the image of the embedding
\mathcal{K}\subset\Delta_{y}^{0}\to B_{y}
(
cf.
Prop.
3.23
).

In the first case, suppose there is a subsequence with
p(\alpha)\in\Gamma_{y,l}\cap l\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap l\mathcal{K}
, then by taking the limit

, the claim would follow.

Otherwise,
p(\alpha)\notin\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap l\mathcal{K}
for all large enough
l
.
As a caveat, here we allow the possibility for
y^{\prime}
to lie on some codimension one wall in
\mathcal{S}_{l}
, in which case

may have non-unique subgradient at

.
Nontheless, if all its subgradients at

, then by the

3.2
), our

must coincide with one of those listed in Step 3, hence falls within the first case, contradiction.

Thus for
l\gg 1
, we
can find
p_{l}\in\nabla\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y^{\prime})
such that
p_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap l(\Delta_{y}\setminus\mathcal{K})
. Thus
l^{-1}p_{l}\in\Delta_{y}\setminus\text{Int}(\mathcal{K})
, and after passing to subsequence,

l^{-1}p_{l}\to p_{\infty}\in\Delta_{y}\setminus\text{Int}(\mathcal{K}),

by the closedness of
\Delta_{y}\setminus\text{Int}(\mathcal{K}).

Upon taking the
C^{0}
-limit for the convex functions

\frac{1}{l}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\frac{1}{l}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)\to c(x,p^{\prime};y),

we deduce that
p_{\infty}
is a subgradient for
c(x,p^{\prime};y)
at
x=y
.
Hence on the
n
-dimensional open face
\Delta_{J}^{0}\subset Sk(X)
, we have

c(x,p^{\prime};y)-c(y^{\prime},p^{\prime};y)\geq\langle p_{\infty},x-y^{\prime}\rangle.

By the definition of
c
-transform, for any
x\in\Delta_{J}^{0}\subset Sk(X)
,

\phi(x)\geq c(x,p^{\prime};y)-\phi^{c}(p^{\prime})\geq c(y^{\prime},p^{\prime};y)+\langle p_{\infty},x-y^{\prime}\rangle-\phi^{c}(p^{\prime}).

Using Step 2,

\phi(x)\geq c(y^{\prime},p^{\prime};y)+\langle p_{\infty},x-y^{\prime}\rangle-\phi^{c}(p^{\prime})=\phi(y^{\prime})+\langle p_{\infty},x-y^{\prime}\rangle.

The upshot is that
p_{\infty}\in\nabla\phi(y^{\prime})\notin\text{Int}(\mathcal{K})
, which contradicts Step 1. This concludes the proof for the claim. Thus we can regard
p^{\prime}
as an element in
\mathcal{K}\subset\Delta_{y}^{0}
.

5.

Consequently, by Prop.
3.21
, we must have

c(x,p^{\prime};y)=\langle p^{\prime},x-y\rangle,\quad\forall x\in U_{y},

and the maximal psh extension
\tilde{c}(x,p^{\prime};y)
satisfies

\tilde{c}(x,p^{\prime};y)=\langle p^{\prime},r_{\mathcal{X}}(x)-y\rangle,\quad\forall x\in r_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(U_{y})\subset X_{K}^{an}.

6.

By the definition of the
c
-transform,

\phi(x)\geq c(x,p^{\prime};y)-\phi^{c}(p^{\prime}),\quad\forall x\in Sk(X).

By the domination property of the potential
\phi
,

\phi(x)\geq\tilde{c}(x,p^{\prime};y)-\phi^{c}(p^{\prime}),\quad\forall x\in X_{K}^{an}.

For any
x\in r_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(U_{y})\subset X_{K}^{an}
, using Step 5, this implies

\phi(x)\geq c(r_{\mathcal{X}}(x),p^{\prime};y)-\phi^{c}(p^{\prime}).

In particular, if
r_{\mathcal{X}}(x)=y^{\prime}
, then using Step 2,

\phi(x)\geq c(y^{\prime},p^{\prime};y)-\phi^{c}(p^{\prime})=\phi(y^{\prime}).

Since this holds for any
y^{\prime}\in U_{y}
, we conclude that
\phi\geq\phi\circ r_{\mathcal{X}}
on
r_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(U_{y})\subset X_{K}^{an}.
The reverse inequality
\phi\leq\phi\circ r_{\mathcal{X}}
is automatic for any semipositive potentials, hence
\phi=\phi\circ r_{\mathcal{X}}
on
r_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(U_{y})\subset X_{K}^{an}.

This concludes the proof for the factorisation criterion.

∎

3.7
Relative volume and Monge-Ampère energy

We shall derive a formula for the Monge-Ampère energy via the relative volume (
cf.
Section
2.5
), similar to
[
27
, Section 5.4]
.
We begin with some formula for Fubini-Study norms. Let
y\in Sk(X)
be any given sufficiently irrational point.

Lemma 3.28
.

Suppose
\phi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
satisfies the domination property (
11
).
For
l
large enough so that the
K
-vector space
H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
generates the line bundle
L^{\otimes l}\to X_{K}
, we define the Fubini-Study approximation

\phi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)=\max_{x\in Sk(X)}(\frac{1}{l}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\frac{1}{l}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)-\phi(x)),\quad\forall\alpha.

(20)

Then the NA norms
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{l\phi}
on
H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
is given by (See section
2.5
)

\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\right\rVert_{l\phi}=\max_{\alpha}|a_{\alpha}|e^{l\phi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)+\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)},\quad\forall a_{\alpha}\in K.

Proof.

Let
s=\sum a_{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\in H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
, where
a_{\alpha}\in K
. By definition

\left\lVert s\right\rVert_{l\phi}=\sup_{X_{K}^{an}}|s|e^{-l\phi},

where
|s|
is defined by the model line bundle
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{X}
. The valuative independence condition implies that

|s|=\max_{\alpha}|a_{\alpha}||\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|\quad\text{on $Sk(X)$},

so by the definition of
\phi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)
, we have

\left\lVert s\right\rVert_{l\phi}\geq\max_{Sk(X)}|s|e^{-l\phi}=\max_{x\in Sk(X)}\max_{\alpha}|a_{\alpha}||\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|e^{-l\phi}=\max_{\alpha}|a_{\alpha}|e^{l\phi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)+\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)}.

We now show the reverse inequality. We define the Fubini-Study potential on
X_{K}^{an}
,

\phi_{l}=\max_{\alpha}l^{-1}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|-l^{-1}\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)-\phi^{c}_{l}(\alpha),

so
\phi_{l}\leq\phi
on
Sk(X)
, whence
\phi_{l}\leq\phi
on
X_{K}^{an}
by the domination property of
\phi
. Thus

\left\lVert\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\right\rVert_{l\phi}=\sup_{X_{K}^{an}}|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|e^{-l\phi}\leq\sup_{X_{K}^{an}}|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|e^{-l\phi_{l}}\leq e^{l\phi_{l}^{c}(\alpha)+\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)},

so the ultrametric inequality implies

\left\lVert s\right\rVert_{l\phi}\leq\max_{\alpha}|a_{\alpha}|\left\lVert\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\right\rVert_{l\phi}=\max_{\alpha}|a_{\alpha}|e^{l\phi_{l}^{c}(\alpha)+\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)},

completing the proof of the reverse inequality.
∎

Notation
.

Let
y
be any given sufficiently irrational point in
Sk(X)
.
The convex set
\Delta_{y}^{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
has a canonical Lebesgue measure determined by its integral structure.
We define the measure
\tilde{\nu}
on
B_{y}
as the pushforward of this Lebesgue measure via the embedding
\Delta_{y}^{0}\to B_{y}
in Prop.
3.23
, which in particular puts zero measure on the complement of the image of this embedding. We write
\tilde{\nu}=\frac{(L^{n})}{n!}\nu
. By Cor.
3.15
, the Lebesgue volume of the Okounkov body is
\frac{(L^{n})}{n!}
, hence
\nu
is a probability measure.

We now present the formula for the Monge-Ampère energy, following the approach of Boucksom-Eriksson
[
7
]
, which is inspired by the Chebyshev transform of Witt-Nyström
[
33
]
.

Proposition 3.29
.

Suppose
\phi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
satisfies the domination property (
11
).
Then up to an additive normalisation constant, the Monge-Ampère energy is

E(\phi)=-(L^{n})\int_{B_{y}}\phi^{c}(p)d\nu.

(21)

Proof.

We divide the proof into a few steps.

1.

Take any other potential
\psi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
with the domination property. Then by Lemma
3.28
, the NA norms
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{l\phi}
versus
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{l\psi}
have a simultaneous orthogonal basis
\theta_{\alpha}^{l}
, namely for any
s=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\theta_{\alpha}^{l}
with
a_{\alpha}\in K
,

\left\lVert s\right\rVert_{l\phi}=\max_{\alpha}|a_{\alpha}|e^{l\phi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)+\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)},\quad\left\lVert s\right\rVert_{l\psi}=\max_{\alpha}|a_{\alpha}|e^{l\psi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)+\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(y)}.

Thus the relative volume between the two NA norms is (see Section
2.5
)

vol(\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{l\phi},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{l\psi})=\log\prod_{\alpha}e^{-l\phi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)+l\psi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)}=l\sum_{\alpha}(-\phi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)+\psi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)).

(22)

By Section
2.5
and (
22
),

\begin{split}E(\phi)-E(\psi)=&\lim_{l\to+\infty}\frac{n!}{l^{n+1}}vol(\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{l\phi},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{l\psi})\\
=&n!\lim_{l\to+\infty}l^{-n}\sum_{\alpha}(-\phi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)+\psi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)).\end{split}

2.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subset\Delta_{y}^{0}
be any fixed closed convex polyhedral subset. We can partition
\{\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\}
according to whether
p(\alpha)=(\nabla\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|)(y)\in\Gamma_{y,l}\cap l\mathcal{K}
.

For all
p(\alpha)\in l\mathcal{K}\cap\Gamma_{y,l}
, by Prop.
3.21
item 4, we have the uniform convergence as
l\to+\infty
,

\sup_{x\in Sk(X),l^{-1}p(\alpha)\in\mathcal{K}}|l^{-1}(\log|\theta_{\alpha}^{l}|(x)-\log|\theta_{\alpha}|(y))-c(x,l^{-1}p(\alpha);y)|\leq\epsilon_{l}\to 0.

Thus we can compare the
c
-transforms,

|\phi_{l}^{c}(\alpha)-\phi^{c}(l^{-1}p(\alpha))|\leq\epsilon_{l}.

Consequently,

\sum_{\alpha:p(\alpha)\in l\mathcal{K}}(-\phi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)+\psi^{c}_{l}(\alpha))=\sum_{\alpha:p(\alpha)\in l\mathcal{K}\cap\Gamma_{y,l}}(-\phi^{c}(l^{-1}p(\alpha))+\psi^{c}(l^{-1}p(\alpha))+O(\epsilon_{l})).

The function
\phi^{c}(p)
is Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{K}
by Prop.
3.20
item 3. Moreover, for large enough
l
, then
l\mathcal{K}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}=l\mathcal{K}\cap\Gamma_{y,l}
by Cor.
3.15
item 1.

Thus the Riemann sum converges as
l\to+\infty
,

l^{-n}\sum_{p(\alpha)\in l\mathcal{K}}\phi^{c}(l^{-1}p(\alpha))\to\int_{\mathcal{K}}\phi^{c}(p)d\tilde{\nu}=\frac{(L^{n})}{n!}\int_{\mathcal{K}}\phi^{c}(p)d\nu.

Since the total number of integer points in
l\mathcal{K}
is
O(l^{n})
, the
O(\epsilon_{l})
term can be neglected, so

\lim_{l\to\infty}l^{-n}\sum_{\alpha:p(\alpha)\in l\mathcal{K}}(-\phi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)+\psi^{c}_{l}(\alpha))=-\frac{(L^{n})}{n!}\int_{\mathcal{K}}(\phi^{c}(p)-\psi^{c}(p))d\nu.

3.

The total number of integer points in
l\mathcal{K}
is
l^{n}\text{Vol}(\mathcal{K})(1+o(1))
, while the total number of
\theta_{\alpha}^{l}
is

\dim_{K}H^{0}(X_{K},lL)=\frac{(L^{n})}{n!}l^{n}(1+O(l^{-1}))=l^{n}\text{Vol}(\Delta_{y})(1+o(1)).

Since
\phi_{l}^{c}(\alpha),\psi_{l}^{c}(\alpha)
are uniformly bounded independent of
l,\alpha
, we can estimate

|l^{-n}\sum_{p(\alpha)\notin l\mathcal{K}}(-\phi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)+\psi^{c}_{l}(\alpha))|\leq Cl^{-n}|\{\alpha:p(\alpha)\notin l\mathcal{K}\}|\leq C\text{Vol}(\Delta_{y}\setminus\mathcal{K}).

4.

Combining Step 2,3, and taking an exhaustion of
\Delta_{y}^{0}
by an increasing sequence of closed convex polyhedral subsets
\mathcal{K}
, we deduce

\lim_{l\to\infty}l^{-n}\sum_{\alpha}(-\phi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)+\psi^{c}_{l}(\alpha))=-\frac{(L^{n})}{n!}\int_{B_{y}}(\phi^{c}(p)-\psi^{c}(p))d\nu.

By Step 1,

E(\phi)-E(\psi)=n!\lim_{l\to+\infty}l^{-n}\sum_{\alpha}(-\phi^{c}_{l}(\alpha)+\psi^{c}_{l}(\alpha))=-(L^{n})\int_{B_{y}}(\phi^{c}(p)-\psi^{c}(p))d\nu.

The Monge-Ampère energy formula follows.

∎

4
NA MA equation and optimal transport

We continue with the setup of Section
3
, and assume the existence of the valuative independent basis
\{\theta_{\alpha}^{l}\}\subset H^{0}(X_{K},lL)
for any
l\geq 0
. Throughout this Section, we suppose that
\varphi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
solves the NA MA equation

\text{MA}(\varphi)=(L^{n})\mu,

(23)

for some probability measure
\mu
supported on
Sk(X)\subset X_{K}^{an}
, such that
\mu

is
absolutely continuous
with respect to
the Lebesgue measure
\mu_{0}
on
Sk(X)
. In the special case
\mu=\mu_{0}
, then
\varphi=\phi_{0}
is the NA CY potential up to constant.
Our goal is to prove a version of the weak comparison property for
\varphi
, as well as deriving an optimal transport interpretation for the NA MA equation.

4.1
Weak comparison property

A key ingredient in proving the weak comparison property is that certain bad events can only happen on null
\mu
-measure sets. The proof subtlely uses the
orthogonality property
for psh envelops (
cf.
Section
2.6
).

Notation
.

Let
y\in Sk(X)
be fixed. The points
x\in Sk(X)
and
p\in B_{y}
are called
conjugate
points for the function
\varphi
, if
\varphi(x)=c(x,p;y)-\varphi^{c}(p)
.
We define the subsets

\begin{cases}E_{0}=\{x\in Sk(X):\text{$x$ is sufficiently irrational}\}=Sk(X)\setminus\bigcup_{l\geq 1}\mathcal{S}_{l},\\
E_{1}=\{x\in E_{0}:\nabla\varphi(x)\not\subset\text{Int}(\Delta_{x})\},\\
E_{2}=\{x\in E_{0}\setminus E_{1}:\text{$\exists x^{\prime}\neq x\in Sk(X),p\in B_{y}$, with $x,x^{\prime}$ both conjugate to $p$}\}.\end{cases}

Lemma 4.1
.

The sets
E_{1},E_{2}
have zero
\mu
-measure.

Proof.

We divide the proof into a few steps.

1.

Since
\varphi
is a convex function on each open
n
-dimensional face
\Delta_{J}^{0}\subset Sk(X)
, the set

E_{0}^{\prime}=\{x\in Sk(X):\text{the subgradient set $\nabla\varphi(x)$ is a singleton}\}

has full Lebesgue measure, hence full
\mu
-measure by absolute continuity. Let
E_{1}^{\prime}=E_{1}\cap E_{0}^{\prime}
and
E_{2}^{\prime}=E_{2}\cap E_{0}^{\prime}
, then it suffices to prove that
\mu(E_{1}^{\prime})=\mu(E_{2}^{\prime})=0
.

2.

Since
\varphi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
and
\mu=\text{MA}(\varphi)
, Cor.
2.8
implies the following result on psh envelops. Let
f:X_{K}^{an}\to\mathbb{R}
be any given model function, then for small
\tau>0
,

\mu(\{P(\varphi+\tau f)<\varphi+\tau f\})\leq C\tau,

(24)

for some constant
C
depending on
f,\varphi
, but independent of
\tau
.

Our argument will be based on testing various model functions
f
. These
f
come from
vertical
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors on all the models of the degeneration family, so forms a countable set.

3.

For any
x\in E_{1}^{\prime}
, then
x\in E_{0}\cap E_{0}^{\prime}
, so
x
is sufficiently irrational, and there is a unique gradient
p=\nabla\varphi(x)
. Since
\varphi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
, we know
p
belongs to the gradient convex hull
\mathcal{C}_{x}
(
cf.
Section
3.4
). Since
p
is the unique gradient, it belongs to the Okounkov body
\Delta_{y}\subset\mathcal{C}_{y}
by Lemma
3.16
item 2. Since
x\in E_{1}^{\prime}\subset E_{1}
, the gradient
p
is not in the interior
\Delta_{y}^{0}
, hence
p\in\partial\Delta_{y}
.

Given any model function
f
, let
E_{1}^{f}
be the subset consisting of the points
x\in E_{1}^{\prime}
, such that
f
is affine linear in some open neighbourhood of
x\in Sk(X)
, and
\nabla\varphi(x)+\tau\nabla f(x)\notin\Delta_{x}
at the point
x
for any
\tau>0
. This means that at the boundary point
p\in\partial\Delta_{x}
, the vector
\nabla f(x)
points outside of the convex Okounkov body
\Delta_{x}
.

We notice that at any
x\in E_{1}^{\prime}
, one can find such a model function
f
, so

E_{1}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{f}E_{1}^{f}.

4.

We claim that at any
x_{0}\in E_{1}^{f}
,

P(\varphi+\tau f)(x_{0})<(\varphi+\tau f)(x_{0}),\quad\forall\tau>0.

(25)

Suppose not, then
P(\varphi+\tau f)(x_{0})=\varphi(x_{0})+\tau f(x_{0})
. On
Sk(X)
, we have

P(\varphi+\tau f)\leq\varphi+\tau f.

The function
P(\varphi+\tau f)\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
restricts to a function in
\mathcal{P}_{c}
, which is in particular convex on the
n
-dimensional faces of
Sk(X)
. The above inequality shows that any subgradient
p^{\prime}\in(\nabla P(\varphi+\tau f))(x_{0})
is also a subgradient of the convex function
\varphi+\tau f
at
x_{0}
, which can only be

p^{\prime}=\nabla\varphi(x_{0})+\tau\nabla f(x_{0}).

In particular, this subgradient
p^{\prime}
is unique, while
P(\varphi+\tau f)\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
, so
p^{\prime}\in\Delta_{x_{0}}
by Lemma
3.16
. But by the definition of
E_{1}^{f}
,

\nabla\varphi(x_{0})+\tau(\nabla f)(x_{0})\notin\Delta_{x_{0}},\quad\forall\tau>0.

This contradiction proves the claim.

5.

By Step 4, the set
E_{1}^{f}\subset\{P(\varphi+\tau f)<\varphi+\tau f\}
for any
\tau>0
. Hence

\mu(E_{1}^{f})\leq\mu(\{P(\varphi+\tau f)<\varphi+\tau f\})\leq C\tau.

(26)

The constant
C
depends on
\varphi,f
, but not on
\tau
. Taking
\tau\to 0
, we obtain
\mu(E_{1}^{f})=0
. Since
E_{1}^{\prime}=\cup_{f}E_{1}^{f}
is a countable union, we deduce
\mu(E_{1}^{\prime})=0
.

6.

For any
x\in E_{2}^{\prime}
, then
x\in E_{0}\cap E_{0}^{\prime}
, so
x
is sufficiently irrational, and there is a unique gradient
p=\nabla\varphi(x)
. Since
x\in E_{2}^{\prime}\subset E_{2}
is disjoint from
E_{1}
, we deduce that
p\in\Delta_{x}^{0}
is in the interior of the Okounkov body.
Moreover, by the definition of
E_{2}
, there is some
x^{\prime}\neq x\in Sk(X)
, such that
x,x^{\prime}
are both conjugate to some
p^{\prime}\in B_{y}
, namely

\varphi(x)+\varphi^{c}(p^{\prime})=c(x,p^{\prime};y),\quad\varphi(x^{\prime})+\varphi^{c}(p^{\prime})=c(x^{\prime},p^{\prime};y).

Given any model function
f
, we let
E_{2}^{f}
to be the subset consisting of the points
x\in E_{2}^{\prime}
, such that

•

The function
f=0
on some open neighbourhood of
x\in Sk(X)
,

•

There is some
x^{\prime}\neq x\in Sk(X)
, and
p^{\prime}\in B_{y}
, such that
x,x^{\prime}
are both conjugate to the same
p^{\prime}\in B_{y}
, and
f(x^{\prime})<0
.

We notice that at any
x\in E_{2}^{\prime}
, we can always find such a model function
f
, so
E_{2}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{f}E_{2}^{f}
.

7.

We claim that at any
x_{0}\in E_{2}^{f}
,

P(\varphi+\tau f)(x_{0})<(\varphi+\tau f)(x_{0}),\quad\forall\tau>0.

Suppose not, then
P(\varphi+\tau f)(x_{0})=\varphi(x_{0})+\tau f(x_{0})
. On
Sk(X)
, we have

P(\varphi+\tau f)\leq(\varphi+\tau f).

We denote
p_{0}=\nabla\varphi(x_{0})
.
By the same argument as in Step 4, any subgradient
p_{0}^{\prime}\in(\nabla P(\varphi+\tau f))(x_{0})

is also a subgradient of the convex function
\varphi+\tau f
at
x_{0}
.
But
f=0
in an open neighbourhood of
x_{0}\in Sk(X)
, so

p_{0}^{\prime}=(\nabla(\varphi+\tau f))(x_{0})=\nabla\varphi(x_{0})=p_{0}.

(27)

The first part of Step 6 implies
p_{0}^{\prime}=p_{0}\in\Delta_{x_{0}}^{0}
.

By Prop.
3.23
,
\Delta_{x_{0}}^{0}

injects
into
B_{x_{0}}
, which determines a
unique limiting tropical theta function

c(\cdot,p_{0};x_{0})
in
B_{x_{0}}
whose gradient at
x_{0}
is
p_{0}
. On the other hand,
x_{0}
is conjugate to
p^{\prime}\in B_{y}
, so

\varphi\geq c(\cdot,p^{\prime};y)-\varphi^{c}(p^{\prime}),\quad\varphi(x_{0})=c(x_{0},p^{\prime};y)-\varphi^{c}(p^{\prime}),

hence the gradient of
c(\cdot,p^{\prime};y)
at the point
x_{0}
must agree with
\nabla\varphi(x_{0})=p_{0}
. Now by Lemma
3.4
which compares
B_{y}
with
B_{x_{0}}
, the function

c(\cdot,p^{\prime};y)-c(x_{0},p^{\prime};y)\in B_{x_{0}.}

The gradient of this function at
x_{0}
is still
p_{0}
. Hence by uniqueness,

c(\cdot,p_{0};x_{0})=c(\cdot,p^{\prime};y)-c(x_{0},p^{\prime};y).

(28)

Since
P(\varphi+\tau f)
lies in
\mathcal{P}_{c}
, by Lemma
3.7
there must be some
p^{\prime\prime}\in B_{y}
conjugate to
x_{0}
for the function
P(\varphi+\tau f)
. By running the same argument for
P(\varphi+\tau f)
as what we just did for
\varphi
, we deduce that

c(\cdot,p_{0}^{\prime};x_{0})=c(\cdot,p^{\prime\prime};y)-c(x_{0},p^{\prime\prime};y).

(29)

But
p_{0}=p_{0}^{\prime}\in\Delta_{x_{0}}^{0}
, so comparing the above gives

c(\cdot,p^{\prime};y)=c(\cdot,p^{\prime\prime};y)+\text{const}.

Since
c(y,p^{\prime};y)=c(y,p^{\prime\prime};y)=0
, this shows that
p^{\prime}=p^{\prime\prime}\in B_{y}
.

Since
x_{0}
and
p^{\prime}=p^{\prime\prime}\in B_{y}
are conjugate points for
g=P(\varphi+\tau f)
, by definition

g(x_{0})+g^{c}(p^{\prime})=c(x_{0},p^{\prime};y),

hence for any
x\in Sk(X)
,

\begin{split}(\varphi+\tau f)(x)\geq g(x)\geq&c(x,p^{\prime};y)-g^{c}(p^{\prime})\\
=&c(x,p^{\prime};y)-c(x_{0},p^{\prime};y)+g(x_{0})\\
=&c(x,p^{\prime};y)-c(x_{0},p^{\prime};y)+\varphi(x_{0}).\end{split}

(30)

where the last step used the contradiction hypothesis
P(\varphi+\tau f)(x_{0})=(\varphi+\tau f)(x_{0})=\varphi(x_{0})
.

Now by the definition of
E_{2}^{f}
, there is another point
x_{0}^{\prime}
with
f(x_{0}^{\prime})<0
, which is also conjugate to the same
p^{\prime}
for the function
\varphi
, hence

\varphi(x_{0}^{\prime})+\varphi^{c}(p^{\prime})=c(x_{0}^{\prime},p^{\prime};y),\quad\varphi(x_{0})+\varphi^{c}(p^{\prime})=c(x_{0},p^{\prime};y).

Therefore

\begin{split}(\varphi+\tau f)(x_{0}^{\prime})&\geq c(x_{0}^{\prime},p^{\prime};y)-c(x_{0},p^{\prime};y)+\varphi(x_{0})\\
&=\varphi(x_{0}^{\prime})+\varphi^{c}(p^{\prime})-c(x_{0},p^{\prime};y)+\varphi(x_{0})\\
&=\varphi(x_{0}^{\prime}),\end{split}

hence
f(x_{0}^{\prime})\geq 0
, contradicting the definition of
E_{2}^{f}
.
This proves the claim.

8.

By Step 7, the set
E_{1}^{f}\subset\{P(\varphi+\tau f)<\varphi+\tau f\}
for any
\tau>0
. Hence

\mu(E_{2}^{f})\leq\mu(\{P(\varphi+\tau f)<\varphi+\tau f\})\leq C\tau.

(31)

The constant
C
depends on
\varphi,f
, but not on
\tau
. Taking
\tau\to 0
, we obtain
\mu(E_{2}^{f})=0
. Since
E_{2}^{\prime}=\cup_{f}E_{2}^{f}
is a countable union, we deduce
\mu(E_{2}^{\prime})=0
.

We have proved
\mu(E_{1}^{\prime})=\mu(E_{2}^{\prime})=0
, hence
\mu(E_{1})=\mu(E_{2})=0
by Step 1.

∎

Theorem 4.2
.

(Weak comparison property for absolutely continuous NA MA
measures)

Suppose that
\varphi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
solves the NA MA equation

\text{MA}(\varphi)=(L^{n})\mu,

for some probability measure
\mu
supported on
Sk(X)\subset X_{K}^{an}
, such that
\mu

is
absolutely continuous
with respect to
the Lebesgue measure
\mu_{0}
on
Sk(X)
. Then there is some open subset
U
contained in the
n
-dimensional open faces of
Sk(X)
, with full measure
\mu(U)=1
, such that
\varphi=\varphi\circ r_{\mathcal{X}}
holds for the retraction map
r_{\mathcal{X}}:X_{K}^{an}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
on the set
r_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(U)
.

In particular, for the NA CY potential
\phi_{0}
solving
\text{MA}(\phi_{0})=(L^{n})\mu_{0}
, then
U\subset Sk(X)
is an open dense subset with full Lebesgue measure.

Proof.

Since
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, the set
E_{0}
has full measure
\mu(E_{0})=1
. Since
\mu(E_{1})=0
by Lemma
4.1
, we see
\mu(E_{0}\setminus E_{1})=1
. For every
x\in E_{0}\setminus E_{1}
, the point
x
is sufficiently irrational, and
\nabla\varphi(x)\subset\Delta_{x}^{0}
.

Since
\varphi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
has its NA MA measure supported on
Sk(X)
, Lemma
2.6
implies that
\varphi
satisfies the domination property (
11
). We can now apply the factorisation criterion Prop.
3.27
. At any
x\in E_{0}\setminus E_{1}
, there is some open neighbourhood
U_{x}\subset Sk(X)
, such that
\varphi=\varphi\circ r_{\mathcal{X}}
holds over
U_{x}
. Taking the union
U:=\bigcup_{x\in E_{0}\setminus E_{1}}U_{x}
, then
U\subset Sk(X)
is an open subset with full measure
\mu(U)=1
, and
\varphi=\varphi\circ r_{\mathcal{X}}
holds over
U
.

Finally, if
\mu
puts nontrivial measure on every open subset of
Sk(X)
, then the full measure open subset
U
must be dense in
Sk(X)
. This applies to the NA CY potential.
∎

On the open
n
-dimensional faces of
Sk(X)
, there is an integral affine structure, so we can make sense of the real Monge-Ampère measure
\text{MA}_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi)
.

Corollary 4.3
.

On the open set
U
, the potential
\varphi
solves the real Monge-Ampère equation

\text{MA}_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{n!}\mu.

Proof.

Since
\varphi=\varphi\circ r_{\mathcal{X}}
over
U
, by the result of Vilsmeier
[
32
]
,

\text{MA}_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi)|_{U}=\frac{1}{n!}r_{\mathcal{X}*}\text{MA}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{n!}\mu.

∎

Remark 4.4
.

If one wishes to directly study the real Monge-Ampère equation on
Sk(X)
, one would face the following basic difficulty. The structure of
Sk(X)
as a simplicial complex depends on the choice of the model
\mathcal{X}
, and
Sk(X)
has no canonical affine structure independent of models.
If the transition functions between local charts are not affine linear, then the notion of convex functions or the real Monge-Ampère equation is not invariantly defined. The affine structure (here the subset
U
) is determined by the solution
\varphi
, rather than given a priori.

Our arguments above does not yield any topological information on
U
. It is an interesting question for the NA CY potential
\phi_{0}
, whether there is some Hausdorff codimension two subset of
Sk(X)
, such that its complement admits some affine structure, wherein
\phi_{0}
solves the real MA equation.

4.2
Optimal transport formulation

The NA MA equation can be formulated as the Kontorovich dual of an optimal transport problem.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\mu
be any probability measure supported on
Sk(X)\subset X_{K}^{an}
.
The potential
\varphi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
solves the NA MA equation

\text{MA}(\varphi)=(L^{n})\mu,

if and only if its restriction to
Sk(X)
is the unique (up to constant) minimiser of the functional
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}:\mathcal{P}_{c}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\mathcal{F}_{\mu}(\phi)=\int_{B_{y}}\phi^{c}(p)d\nu+\int_{Sk(X)}\phi d\mu.

Proof.

We divide the proof into a few steps.

1.

By Prop.
3.9
, any
\phi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
restricts on
Sk(X)
to a function in
\mathcal{P}_{c}
. By Prop.
3.8
, each
\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
has a unique maximal psh extension to
\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
satifying the domination property (
11
). We can thus identify
\mathcal{P}_{c}
with the potentials in
\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
satisfying the domination property.

For any
\phi\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
satisfying the domination property,
the functional
F_{\mu}:\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})\to\mathbb{R}
(
cf.
Section
2.4
) is

F_{\mu}(\phi)=-\frac{1}{(L^{n})}E(\phi)+\int_{X_{K}^{an}}\phi d\mu=\int_{B_{y}}\phi^{c}(p)d\nu+\int_{Sk(X)}\phi d\mu=\mathcal{F}_{\mu}(\phi)

by Prop.
3.29
.

2.

Since the NA MA measure
\text{MA}(\varphi)
is supported on
Sk(X)
, it satisfies the domination property (
11
) by Lemma
2.6
. As discussed in Section
2.4
, the solution
\varphi
to the NA MA equation minimises
F_{\mu}
among
\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
, and in particular is the minimiser among those potentials satisfying the domination property. Hence
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
minimises
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
among
\mathcal{P}_{c}
.

3.

Conversely, suppose
\varphi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
minimises
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
. We take the maximal psh extension
\varphi^{\prime}\in\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})
, so

F_{\mu}(\varphi^{\prime})=\mathcal{F}_{\mu}(\varphi^{\prime})=\min_{\mathcal{P}_{c}}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}=\mathcal{F}_{\mu}(\varphi)=F_{\mu}(\varphi).

Thus
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}
are both minimisers of
F_{\mu}:\text{CPSH}(X_{K}^{an},\mathcal{L})\to\mathbb{R}
, hence both solve the same NA MA equation

\text{MA}(\varphi)=\text{MA}(\varphi^{\prime})=(L^{n})\mu.

But the solution to the NA MA equation is unique up to constant, hence
\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}+c
. This proves the uniqueness statement.

∎

4.3
A global real Monge-Ampère type equation

For motivation, recall that for a convex function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
on some open domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
the
Alexandrov weak solution to the real Monge-Ampère equation

\det(D^{2}u)=1
is defined by

|\nabla u(E)|=|E|,\quad\forall E\subset\Omega,

where
\nabla u(E)=\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:p\in\nabla u(x)\text{ for some }x\in E\}.

The notion of conjugate sets provides a global analogue for this weak formulation, for the solution
\varphi
to the NA MA equation.

Let
y
be any fixed sufficiently irrational point on
Sk(X)
.
Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
be the solution to the NA MA equation
\text{MA}(\varphi)=(L^{n})\mu
, where
\mu
is a probability measure supported on
Sk(X)
, which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Recall we have a probability measures
\nu
on
B_{y}
, coming from the Lebesgue measure on
\Delta_{y}^{0}
(
cf.
Section
3.7
).

Notation
.

For any Lebesgue measurable subsets
E\subset Sk(X)
(resp.
F\subset B_{y}
), we define the
conjugate sets

\begin{cases}\bar{\nabla}\varphi(E):=\{p\in B_{y}:\varphi(x)+\varphi^{c}(p)=c(x,p;y)\text{ for some }x\in E\},\\
\bar{\nabla}\varphi(F):=\{x\in Sk(X):\varphi(x)+\varphi^{c}(p)=c(x,p;y)\text{ for some }p\in F\}.\end{cases}

Lemma 4.6
.

For any measurable
E\subset Sk(X)
(resp.
F\subset B_{y}
), we have

\nu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(E))\geq\mu(E),\quad\mu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(F))\geq\nu(F).

Proof.

By Thm.
4.5
, the solution
\varphi
to the NA MA equation is the minimiser for the functional

\mathcal{F}_{\mu}(\phi)=\int_{B_{y}}\phi^{c}(p)d\nu+\int_{Sk(X)}\phi d\mu.

The rest of the argument is similar to
[
24
, Prop. 2.16]
.

We consider the function
\varphi-\tau 1_{E}
for
0<\tau\ll 1
, where
1_{E}
denotes the characteristic function of
E
. By elementary properties of the
c
-transform,

\varphi^{c}\leq(\varphi-\tau 1_{E})^{c}\leq\varphi^{c}+\tau.

Moreover
(\varphi-\tau 1_{E})^{c}>\varphi^{c}
at
p\in B_{y}
, only when

\sup_{x\in E}(c(x,p;y)-\varphi(x))\geq\varphi^{c}(p)-\tau.

Hence

\begin{split}\tau\nu(\{p:\sup_{x\in E}(c(x,p;y)-\varphi(x))\geq\varphi^{c}(p)-\tau\})\geq\int_{B_{y}}((\varphi-\tau 1_{E})^{c}-\varphi^{c})d\nu.\end{split}

Since
\varphi
is a minimiser of
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
, plugging in
(\varphi-\tau 1_{E})^{cc}\in\mathcal{P}_{c}
as a competitor, whose
c
-transform is
(\varphi-\tau 1_{E})^{c}
by Lemma
3.5
,
we get

\int_{B_{y}}((\varphi-\tau 1_{E})^{c}-\varphi^{c})d\nu\geq-\int_{Sk(X)}((\varphi-\tau 1_{E})^{cc}-\varphi)d\mu\geq-\int_{Sk(X)}(\varphi-\tau 1_{E})-\varphi=\tau\mu(E).

Here the first inequality follows by the minimisation property of
\varphi
, and the second inequality uses Lemma
3.5
. Combining the above and cancelling the
\tau
factor,

\nu(\{p:\sup_{x\in E}(c(x,p;y)-\varphi(x))\geq\varphi^{c}(p)-\tau\})\geq\mu(E),\quad\forall 0<\tau\ll 1.

Taking the
\tau\to 0
limit, and recalling
\varphi^{c}(p)\geq c(x,p;y)-\varphi(x)
, we get

\nu(\{p:\sup_{x\in E}(c(x,p;y)-\varphi(x))=\varphi^{c}(p)\})\geq\mu(E).

For closed subsets
E
, the sup can be replaced by max by the continuity of
\varphi
, so the LHS subset is the conjugate set
\bar{\nabla}\varphi(E)
. For general Borel subsets
E
, we take a compact exhaustion of
E
. For any compact subset
K\subset E
, we observe

\nu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(E))\geq\nu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(K))\geq\mu(K).

Since the measure
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, we obtain
\nu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(E))\geq\mu(E)
by taking the limit
K\uparrow E
.

The other inequality
\mu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(F))\geq\nu(F)
is entirely similar.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

(Global real Monge-Ampère type equation)
For any Lebesgue measurable
E\subset Sk(X)
(resp.
F\subset B_{y}
), we have

\nu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(E))=\mu(E),\quad\mu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(F))=\nu(F).

Proof.

Let
E^{\prime\prime}=\bar{\nabla}\varphi(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(E))\subset Sk(X)
.
By the definition of conjugate sets, clearly
E\subset E^{\prime\prime}
. Moreover, for any point
x\in E^{\prime\prime}\setminus E
, there is some
p\in B_{y}
, which is conjugate to both
x
and some
x^{\prime}\in E
. Thus recalling the sets
E_{0},E_{1},E_{2}
from Section
4.1
,

E^{\prime\prime}\setminus E\subset(Sk(X)\setminus E_{0})\cup E_{1}\cup E_{2}.

Since
\mu
is absolutely continuous,
\mu(Sk(X)\setminus E_{0})=0
. By Lemma
4.1
,
\mu(E_{1})=\mu(E_{2})=0
, hence
\mu(E^{\prime\prime}\setminus E)=0
.
But
by Lemma
4.6
,

\nu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(E))\geq\mu(E)=\mu(E^{\prime\prime})\geq\nu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(E)).

This forces the equality
\nu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(E))=\mu(E)
.

By Lemma
4.6
, we have
\mu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(F))\geq\nu(F)
. Let

F^{\prime}=\bar{\nabla}\varphi(F)\setminus((Sk(X)\setminus E_{0})\cup E_{1}\cup E_{2}),

then
\mu(F^{\prime})=\mu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(F))
, since the deleted subset has zero measure. Any
x\in F^{\prime}
has a unique conjugate point
p\in B_{y}
, since it avoids
(Sk(X)\setminus E_{0})\cup E_{1}\cup E_{2}
. But
F^{\prime}\subset\bar{\nabla}\varphi(F)
means that
x
must be conjugate to some point in
F
, hence
p\in F
. This shows
\bar{\nabla}\varphi(F^{\prime})\subset F
. By Lemma
4.6
again,

\mu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(F))=\mu(F^{\prime})\leq\nu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(F^{\prime}))\leq\nu(F)\leq\mu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(F)).

This forces the equality
\mu(\bar{\nabla}\varphi(F))=\nu(F)
.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

For different choices of sufficiently irrational
y\in Sk(X)
, we can canonically identify
B_{y}
using
3.4
. This identification is compatible with the notion of conjugate points, hence compatible with the map
\bar{\nabla}\varphi
.
Each
B_{y}
carries a measure
\nu
induced from the Lebsgue measure on
\Delta_{y}^{0}
. Since Thm.
4.7
applies to all sufficiently irrational
y
simultaneously,
a fortiori
the measure
\nu
is independent of
y
under the canonical identification of
B_{y}
. We can thus think of
\bar{\nabla}\phi
as a
measure preserving map
between two probability measure spaces
(Sk(X),\mu)
and
(B,\nu)
independent of
y
.

It is a curious question if one can give an a priori construction of
(B,\nu)
that is manifestly independent of the choice of the sufficiently irrational
y\in Sk(X)
. The proposal in
[
27
, Section 6.3]
suggests that
(B,\nu)
is the
normalised Lebesgue measure on the essential skeleton corresponding to the mirror family of Calabi-Yau manifolds
. This would be a very appealing metric version of mirror symmetry.
