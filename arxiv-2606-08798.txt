State-Feedback Control of Logistic-Based Gene Regulatory Networks: Closed-Form Lyapunov Certificates, Monostabilization, and Delay-Uniform Stability
Ismail Belgacem

Abstract
Gene regulatory networks (GRNs) are high-value targets for therapeutic and synthetic-biology intervention, yet the classical Hill-function models used to describe them carry a structural defect that is critical for control: the production term vanishes when the activator is absent, causing loss of controllability under multiplicative actuation and network decoupling under additive actuation—precisely the low-expression regime in which cells most often operate. Building on companion works that establish logistic functions as robust Hill alternatives [ 13 , 15 ] , we develop an additive state-feedback framework for logistic-based GRNs in which a feedforward-plus-proportional law renders any positive setpoint a closed-loop equilibrium, whether or not it is an equilibrium of the uncontrolled dynamics. We prove local exponential stability under an explicit Gershgorin gain bound and global exponential stability of the nonlinear closed loop—via a common quadratic Lyapunov function built on the logistic sector bound—under the gain condition ( γ 1 + K 1 ) ​ ( γ 2 + K 2 ) > κ 1 ​ κ 2 ​ λ 2 / 64 (\gamma_{1}+K_{1})(\gamma_{2}+K_{2})>\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\lambda^{2}/64 , with a closed-form rate. A diagonal Lyapunov certificate P = diag ⁡ ( B , A ) P=\operatorname{diag}(B,A) for the sign-structured closed-loop Jacobian supplies explicit settling-time and input-to-state-stability bounds, the rate min i ⁡ ( γ i + K i ) \min_{i}(\gamma_{i}+K_{i}) being arbitrarily accelerable by gain. Two complementary scalar results follow from the same universal Lipschitz bound λ / 4 \lambda/4 of the logistic derivative: a parameter-uniform monostabilization budget K ∗ = κ ​ λ / 4 − γ K^{*}=\kappa\lambda/4-\gamma (Theorem 3 ) for bistable self-activation switches, and a Halanay-type delay-uniform global exponential stability theorem (Theorem 4 ) under γ + K > κ ​ λ / 4 \gamma+K>\kappa\lambda/4 , with closed-form two-sided bounds on the delay-dependent rate. Introduction

The control of gene regulatory networks (GRNs) is a central challenge in systems
and synthetic biology, with applications spanning metabolic engineering, drug
discovery, and gene therapy
[
3
,
20
]
. Precise
regulation of gene expression requires both faithful mathematical models of the
underlying molecular interactions and rigorous control-theoretic frameworks capable
of driving network states to desired targets despite biological noise and parameter
uncertainty.

Hill functions, originally derived to model cooperative ligand binding to
hemoglobin
[
23
,
28
]
, have become the standard sigmoid
building blocks for GRN models
[
20
,
3
]
. Their
appeal lies in a compact parametric form:
h^{+}(x,\theta,n)=x^{n}/(x^{n}+\theta^{n})

interpolates smoothly between zero and one as the regulator concentration
x
passes
through the threshold
\theta
, with cooperativity controlled by the Hill coefficient

n
. However, as we established in our companion
papers
[
13
,
15
]
, Hill functions carry a
structural limitation that is particularly detrimental for control design:

h^{+}(0,\theta,n)=0
identically. When the activating regulator is absent, the
production term vanishes regardless of any control input, causing complete loss of
controllability precisely in the low-expression regimes most common in biological
practice—OFF states, post-perturbation recovery, and transitions between attractors.

Logistic functions
f^{+}(x,\theta,\lambda)=1/(1+e^{-\lambda(x-\theta)})
provide a
principled, biologically grounded alternative
[
13
,
15
,
16
]
. Their sigmoid shape closely approximates
Hill behavior for large steepness
\lambda
while maintaining a strictly positive
output at all finite concentrations, including
x=0
:

f^{+}(0,\theta,\lambda)=1/(1+e^{\lambda\theta})>0
. This non-zero basal response
is not an artifact but a faithful reflection of biological reality: promoter leakiness
sustains measurable transcription even in nominally repressed genes
[
24
]
,
and long-lived low-expression states are experimentally well-documented in systems
ranging from the yeast GAL network
[
1
]
to bacterial
operons
[
25
]
. Beyond biological fidelity, the smooth, bounded, and
analytically invertible nature of the logistic function makes it substantially more
tractable for control design than the power-law Hill function.

The present paper develops the additive state-feedback (Architecture B)
branch of this control framework, together with two complementary scalar
control results that share the same Lyapunov-based reasoning. It is part
of an ongoing series: Paper I

2
Logistic Gene Regulatory Network Model and Control Architecture

Definition 1

(Logistic sigmoid functions)
.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
, threshold
\theta>0
, and steepness
\lambda>0
, define the

activating
and
repressing
logistic sigmoids:

\displaystyle f^{+}(x;\theta,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda(x-\theta)}},

(1)

\displaystyle f^{-}(x;\theta,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+e^{\lambda(x-\theta)}}=1-f^{+}(x;\theta,\lambda).

Both functions map
\mathbb{R}
into
(0,1)
and satisfy

f^{+}(0;\theta,\lambda)=1/(1+e^{\lambda\theta})>0
and

f^{-}(0;\theta,\lambda)=1/(1+e^{-\lambda\theta})<1
.
Their derivatives satisfy
\tfrac{d}{dx}f^{\pm}=\pm\lambda f^{\pm}(1-f^{\pm})
,
with the universal bound
f(1-f)\leq 1/4
, attained at the threshold
x=\theta
.

Controlling biological networks is a frontier of substantial practical promise.
Our prior qualitative-control work on piecewise-affine and Hill-based
genetic networks
[
12
,
19
]

demonstrated that sliding-mode and qualitative feedback designs offer
rigorous frameworks for stabilising
gene expression. Optogenetic platforms such as the
Diya light-illumination system
[
26
]
are now mature enough to
implement closed-loop feedback at the single-cell or population level.
The control-theoretic difficulty with Hill functions is structural rather
than incidental: the activating factor

h^{+}(x,\theta,n)=x^{n}/(x^{n}+\theta^{n})
vanishes identically at
x=0
,
so when an activating regulator is absent, the production term is zero

independently of the control input
, and the system is uncontrollable
in the activator-absent regime. The logistic function

f^{+}(x,\theta,\lambda)
of Definition
1
satisfies

f^{+}(0;\theta,\lambda)=1/(1+e^{\lambda\theta})>0
, so the production
term never vanishes and the system remains controllable throughout the
strictly positive orthant. This is the structural property that drives every
comparison result in the present paper
[
13
,
15
]
.

2.1
Biological Foundations: Persistent Basal Expression

The non-zero basal output of the logistic function is not a mathematical
convenience: it is a faithful reflection of biological reality. In
activator-regulated systems, transcription proceeds at leaky basal rates even
when the activator is absent, and these low-expression states persist as long
as the activator is absent. The yeast GAL regulatory network is the canonical
example: cells maintain the OFF state with measurable expression across
multiple cell divisions without galactose
[
1
]
.
Quantitative single-molecule studies have shown that promoter leakage
stabilises low-expression states in auto-regulatory circuits while reducing
expression noise
[
24
]
. Long lifetimes of the basal
attractor follow from the rare-event statistics of binding/unbinding kinetics
in genetic switches
[
27
]
.

Repressor-free states are equally persistent. In bacterial operons such as
the
lac
system, knockout of the
lacI
repressor yields
constitutive expression of
lacZYA
for as long as the cell survives,
without inducer
[
25
]
. In phage
\lambda
lysogeny,
inactivation of the CI repressor switches the cell to the lytic programme
and dominates dynamics until lysis
[
2
]
.

The logistic framework captures both regimes intrinsically: the activator
basal
f^{+}(0;\theta,\lambda)=(1+e^{\lambda\theta})^{-1}
is positive but
small (engineerable via
\lambda\theta
), and the repressor-absent value

f^{-}(0;\theta,\lambda)=(1+e^{-\lambda\theta})^{-1}
is close to but less
than
1
. Hill functions force
h^{+}(0)=0
and
h^{-}(0)=1
identically,
which is incompatible with the leaky biology and—more importantly for the
present paper—incompatible with controllability at zero
input
[
13
,
15
]
.

2.2
Controlled Logistic-Based Network Model

A controlled logistic-based GRN can take several mathematical forms depending
on how the external input couples to the regulatory machinery. Two physically
motivated architectures are used in this paper, both preserving the
controllability property of the logistic framework:

Architecture A (argument-modulating).

The control input
u_{ij}\geq 0
scales the regulator concentration entering
the logistic argument, modelling situations where the input modulates the

effective
concentration of a regulator (e.g. optogenetic switching
that activates a fraction
u_{ij}
of regulator molecules):

\dot{x}_{i}\;=\;\kappa_{i}\,f_{i}\bigl(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{u}_{i}\bigr)-\gamma_{i}x_{i},\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,

(2)

where each logistic factor in
f_{i}
has the form

1/\bigl(1+e^{\mp\lambda(u_{ij}x_{j}-\theta_{ij})}\bigr)
.

Architecture B (additive).

The control input
u_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
is added directly to the production rate,
modelling externally supplied transcript or protein:

\dot{x}_{i}\;=\;\kappa_{i}\,f_{i}(\mathbf{x})-\gamma_{i}x_{i}+u_{i},\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,

(3)

where
f_{i}
is a product of plain logistic factors of
x_{j}
alone.

In both architectures
\kappa_{i}>0
is the maximal production rate,
\gamma_{i}>0

the degradation rate, and
f_{i}\in(0,1]
is a product of logistic factors

f^{\pm}
encoding the regulatory logic. For one activator

j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
and one repressor
k\in\mathcal{R}_{i}
in Architecture B:

f_{i}(\mathbf{x})\;=\;\frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda(x_{j}-\theta_{ij})}}\cdot\frac{1}{1+e^{\lambda(x_{k}-\theta_{ik})}}.

(4)

At
x_{j}=x_{k}=0
:

f_{i}(\mathbf{0})=(1+e^{\lambda\theta_{ij}})^{-1}(1+e^{-\lambda\theta_{ik}})^{-1}>0
,
so the logistic regulatory production is strictly positive. The Hill counterpart

x_{j}^{n}/(x_{j}^{n}+\theta_{ij}^{n})\cdot\theta_{ik}^{n}/(x_{k}^{n}+\theta_{ik}^{n})
vanishes
at
x_{j}=0
(the activator-absent regime), so the regulatory contribution
disappears entirely. The consequences differ by architecture. In
Architecture B the vanishing of
h_{i}
leaves the additive control alone to
supply the entire production flux of gene
i
. A sharper effect arises in the

output-multiplicative
variant

\dot{x}_{i}=\kappa_{i}u_{i}f_{i}(\mathbf{x})-\gamma_{i}x_{i}
, in which the input
enters as a multiplicative gain on the regulatory output: there

h_{i}(\mathbf{x})=0
annihilates all control authority over
\dot{x}_{i}
—an
outright loss of controllability—whereas the logistic
f_{i}^{\mathrm{log}}>0

retains it everywhere on the closed positive orthant. In Architecture A, by
contrast, the input acts through the product
u_{ij}x_{j}
, which vanishes at

x_{j}=0

independently of the regulatory function
: both the Hill and the
logistic models then lose controllability through regulator
j
at the
boundary
x_{j}=0
, and Architecture A is controllable only on the open orthant

x_{j}>0
. For an
n
-dimensional network with multiple parallel activators and
repressors,
f_{i}
generalises to

f_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}f^{+}(x_{j};\theta_{ij},\lambda)\cdot\prod_{k\in\mathcal{R}_{i}}f^{-}(x_{k};\theta_{ik},\lambda)
,
which remains strictly positive on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
for any choice of
control architecture.

Choice of architecture.

The two architectures call for different controller designs.
Architecture A, in which the control enters nonlinearly through the
logistic argument, is naturally suited to sliding-mode control: the equivalent
control can be solved in closed form by inverting the logistic, and
the boundary-layer linearisation yields a clean stability bound.
Architecture B, in which the control enters affinely, is naturally
suited to state-feedback: a feedforward-plus-proportional law makes
any positive setpoint a closed-loop equilibrium, and Gershgorin’s
theorem yields an explicit gain condition. The present paper develops
the Architecture B branch in detail.

Two-gene benchmark.

Throughout the rest of this paper we use the following two-gene
oscillator in Architecture B: gene 1 is repressed by gene 2, gene 2
is activated by gene 1, and each gene carries an additive control input

u_{i}
:

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \dot{x}_{1}&=\frac{\kappa_{1}}{1+e^{\lambda(x_{2}-\theta_{2})}}-\gamma_{1}x_{1}+u_{1},\\[2.0pt]
\dot{x}_{2}&=\frac{\kappa_{2}}{1+e^{-\lambda(x_{1}-\theta_{1})}}-\gamma_{2}x_{2}+u_{2}.\end{aligned}}

(5)

The uncontrolled limit
u_{1}=u_{2}=0
is the classical activator–repressor
oscillator. At
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
the regulatory productions evaluate
to
\kappa_{1}/(1+e^{-\lambda\theta_{2}})
and
\kappa_{2}/(1+e^{\lambda\theta_{1}})
,
both strictly positive, so the system has non-zero
“wake-up” drive even from a deep OFF state, regardless of
\mathbf{u}
.

3
State-Feedback Control Strategies

For Architecture B (additive control, (
3
)), we
develop a feedforward-plus-proportional-feedback design that makes any
positive setpoint
\mathbf{x}_{d}
a closed-loop equilibrium—not necessarily
an equilibrium of the uncontrolled system. The control law is

u_{i}\;=\;\underbrace{\gamma_{i}x_{d,i}-\kappa_{i}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{d})}_{u_{i}^{\mathrm{ff}}}\;-\;K_{i}(x_{i}-x_{d,i}),\qquad K_{i}>0.

(6)

The feedforward term
u_{i}^{\mathrm{ff}}
exactly cancels the steady-state
mismatch
\gamma_{i}x_{d,i}-\kappa_{i}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{d})
, while the feedback
term provides linear restoring action. Direct substitution
into (
3
) confirms that
\mathbf{x}_{d}
is an
equilibrium of the closed-loop system for every positive setpoint, removing
the implicit requirement (made in earlier formulations) that
\mathbf{x}_{d}

already be an equilibrium of the uncontrolled dynamics.

Linearizing around
\mathbf{x}_{d}
with
\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{d}
:

\dot{\mathbf{e}}\;=\;\bigl(J_{f}-\Gamma-K\bigr)\mathbf{e}+O(\|\mathbf{e}\|^{2}),

(7)

where
[J_{f}]_{ij}=\kappa_{i}\,(\partial f_{i}/\partial x_{j})|_{\mathbf{x}_{d}}
,

\Gamma=\mathrm{diag}(\gamma_{i})
, and
K=\mathrm{diag}(K_{i})
.

3.1
Local exponential stability: a Gershgorin gain bound

Theorem 1

(State-Feedback Exponential Stability)
.

For the additive system (
3
) under the law (
6
),

\mathbf{x}_{d}
is an equilibrium for any
K_{i}\geq 0
. A sufficient condition for
exponential stability of the linearization is the Gershgorin bound:

K_{i}\;>\;[J_{f}]_{ii}+\sum_{j\neq i}\bigl|[J_{f}]_{ij}\bigr|-\gamma_{i},\quad\forall i,

(8)

in which case every eigenvalue of
J_{f}-\Gamma-K
lies in the open left half-plane.
In particular, for the two-gene logistic activator–repressor network without
self-regulation,
[J_{f}]_{ii}=0
and the condition reduces to

K_{i}>|[J_{f}]_{ij}|-\gamma_{i}
for the single off-diagonal entry
j\neq i
, which is
always achievable for sufficiently large
K_{i}
.

Proof.

Substituting (
6
) at
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{d}
gives

\dot{x}_{i}\big|_{\mathbf{x}_{d}}=\kappa_{i}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{d})-\gamma_{i}x_{d,i}+\gamma_{i}x_{d,i}-\kappa_{i}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{d})=0
, so
\mathbf{x}_{d}
is an equilibrium.
The linearization (
7
) follows directly. By the Gershgorin
disc theorem, every eigenvalue of
J_{f}-\Gamma-K
lies in some disc centered at

[J_{f}]_{ii}-\gamma_{i}-K_{i}
with radius
\sum_{j\neq i}|[J_{f}]_{ij}|
.
Condition (
8
) places every disc strictly in the open left
half-plane, ensuring exponential stability.
∎

.

For the two-gene activator–repressor network at setpoint

3.2
Global exponential stability via a common quadratic Lyapunov function

Theorem
1
certifies exponential stability of the

linearisation
around
\mathbf{x}_{d}
; it is a local result. The
next theorem upgrades it to a
global, fully nonlinear
guarantee
by exploiting the one structural property that distinguishes the
logistic model—the global Lipschitz bound
\lambda/4
on the
regulatory derivative—and is the same bound that drives the scalar
results of Sections
5
–
6
.

Theorem 2

(Global Exponential Stability via a Common Quadratic Lyapunov Function)
.

Consider the two-gene benchmark (
5
) under the
state-feedback law (
6
), and write

\alpha_{i}:=\gamma_{i}+K_{i}
for the closed-loop decay rates and

c_{i}:=\kappa_{i}\lambda/4
for the maximal coupling rates. If

(\gamma_{1}+K_{1})(\gamma_{2}+K_{2})\;>\;\frac{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\lambda^{2}}{64},

(9)

then the diagonal quadratic form
V(\mathbf{e})=c_{2}e_{1}^{2}+c_{1}e_{2}^{2}
is a
global Lyapunov function for the error
\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{d}
,
and the setpoint
\mathbf{x}_{d}
is
globally exponentially stable
:
for every initial condition
\mathbf{x}(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\|\mathbf{x}(t)-\mathbf{x}_{d}\|_{2}\;\leq\;\sqrt{\frac{\max(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})}{\min(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})}}\;\|\mathbf{x}(0)-\mathbf{x}_{d}\|_{2}\;e^{-\rho_{\mathrm{glob}}\,t}

(10)

for all
t\geq 0
, with the explicit rate

\rho_{\mathrm{glob}}\;=\;\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl[(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})-\sqrt{(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})^{2}+c_{1}c_{2}}\Bigr]\;>\;0.

(11)

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{d}
. Substituting (
6
)
into (
5
) and using

\kappa_{i}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{d})+u_{i}^{\mathrm{ff}}=\gamma_{i}x_{d,i}
gives the
exact (un-linearised) error dynamics

\dot{e}_{1}=-\alpha_{1}e_{1}+\kappa_{1}g_{1}(e_{2}),\qquad\dot{e}_{2}=-\alpha_{2}e_{2}+\kappa_{2}g_{2}(e_{1}),

with
g_{1}(e_{2}):=f^{-}(x_{d,2}+e_{2})-f^{-}(x_{d,2})
and

g_{2}(e_{1}):=f^{+}(x_{d,1}+e_{1})-f^{+}(x_{d,1})
. Since
f^{-}
is strictly
decreasing,
f^{+}
strictly increasing, and both have derivative bounded
in magnitude by
\lambda/4
(Definition
1
), the
mean-value theorem yields the
sector bounds

g_{1}(e_{2})=-\sigma_{1}e_{2},\quad g_{2}(e_{1})=\sigma_{2}e_{1},\quad\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in[0,\lambda/4],

where
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
are state-dependent. Differentiating

V=c_{2}e_{1}^{2}+c_{1}e_{2}^{2}
along the trajectories,

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle=-2c_{2}\alpha_{1}e_{1}^{2}-2c_{1}\alpha_{2}e_{2}^{2}+2\bigl(c_{1}\kappa_{2}\sigma_{2}-c_{2}\kappa_{1}\sigma_{1}\bigr)e_{1}e_{2}

\displaystyle\leq-2c_{2}\alpha_{1}e_{1}^{2}-2c_{1}\alpha_{2}e_{2}^{2}+2c_{1}c_{2}\,|e_{1}|\,|e_{2}|\;=\;-\,\mathbf{z}^{\!\top}R\,\mathbf{z},

where
\mathbf{z}=(|e_{1}|,|e_{2}|)^{\!\top}
, the cross-term coefficient is
bounded via
\kappa_{1}\sigma_{1}\leq c_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}\sigma_{2}\leq c_{2}
(so

|c_{1}\kappa_{2}\sigma_{2}-c_{2}\kappa_{1}\sigma_{1}|\leq c_{1}c_{2}
), and

R=\begin{bmatrix}2c_{2}\alpha_{1}&-c_{1}c_{2}\\[2.0pt]
-c_{1}c_{2}&2c_{1}\alpha_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

Condition (
9
)—equivalently
4\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}>c_{1}c_{2}
,
since
c_{1}c_{2}=\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\lambda^{2}/16
—is exactly

\det R=c_{1}c_{2}(4\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}-c_{1}c_{2})>0
. With
R_{11}>0
this makes

R\succ 0
, so
V
strictly decreases off
\mathbf{x}_{d}
and is a global
Lyapunov function. Sharpening,
R-2\rho_{\mathrm{glob}}\operatorname{diag}(c_{2},c_{1})\succeq 0
for
\rho_{\mathrm{glob}}
as in (
11
) (its
positive-semidefiniteness reduces to

4(\alpha_{1}-\rho_{\mathrm{glob}})(\alpha_{2}-\rho_{\mathrm{glob}})\geq c_{1}c_{2}
),
hence
\dot{V}\leq-2\rho_{\mathrm{glob}}V
. Grönwall’s lemma gives

V(t)\leq V(0)e^{-2\rho_{\mathrm{glob}}t}
; combining with

\min(c_{1},c_{2})\|\mathbf{e}\|_{2}^{2}\leq V\leq\max(c_{1},c_{2})\|\mathbf{e}\|_{2}^{2}

and
\max(c_{1},c_{2})/\min(c_{1},c_{2})=\max(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})/\min(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})

yields (
10
).
∎

Corollary 1

(Global settling-time bound)
.

Under condition (
9
), for any tolerance

\delta\in(0,1)
the relative-to-initial settling time obeys

t_{s,\delta}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathrm{glob}}}\Bigl[\ln(1/\delta)+\tfrac{1}{2}\ln\tfrac{\max(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})}{\min(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})}\Bigr],

(12)

for
every
initial condition in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
—not only those in a
neighbourhood of
\mathbf{x}_{d}
. The bound follows by rearranging

\sqrt{\max(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})/\min(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})}\,e^{-\rho_{\mathrm{glob}}t}\leq\delta
in (
10
), exactly
as in Corollary
3
.

Corollary 2

(Tracking of time-varying references)
.

Suppose condition (
9
) holds and the regulation target
is a
C^{1}
time-varying reference
\mathbf{x}_{d}(t)
of bounded velocity,

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\dot{\mathbf{x}}_{d}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\nu
, the
feedforward in (
6
) being evaluated along

\mathbf{x}_{d}(t)
. Then the tracking error is globally ultimately bounded,

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|\mathbf{x}(t)-\mathbf{x}_{d}(t)\|_{2}\;\leq\;\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\rho_{\mathrm{glob}}}\,\frac{\max(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})}{\min(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})}\;\nu,

(13)

and this bound vanishes as
\nu\to 0
, recovering the exact regulation of
constant setpoints (Theorem
2
). The error is
gain-attenuable: larger
K_{i}
raise the rate
\rho_{\mathrm{glob}}
.

Proof.

With
\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{d}(t)
, the error dynamics in the
proof of Theorem
2
acquire the extra term

-\dot{x}_{d,i}(t)
. Differentiating
V=c_{2}e_{1}^{2}+c_{1}e_{2}^{2}
and using

\dot{V}\leq-2\rho_{\mathrm{glob}}V
for the homogeneous part,

\displaystyle\dot{V}\;

\displaystyle\leq\;-2\rho_{\mathrm{glob}}V+2\bigl|c_{2}e_{1}\dot{x}_{d,1}+c_{1}e_{2}\dot{x}_{d,2}\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\;-2\rho_{\mathrm{glob}}V+2\max(c_{1},c_{2})\,\|\mathbf{e}\|_{2}\,\|\dot{\mathbf{x}}_{d}\|_{2},

the last step by Cauchy–Schwarz. With

\|\dot{\mathbf{x}}_{d}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{2}\,\nu
and

\|\mathbf{e}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{V/\min(c_{1},c_{2})}
, the substitution
W=\sqrt{V}

gives the linear inequality
\dot{W}\leq-\rho_{\mathrm{glob}}W+\sqrt{2}\,\nu\,\max(c_{1},c_{2})/\sqrt{\min(c_{1},c_{2})}
. Its steady state,
divided once more by
\sqrt{\min(c_{1},c_{2})}
to return to

\|\mathbf{e}\|_{2}
, is the right-hand side of (
13
).
∎

Remark 2

(Interpretation, the local certificate, and the
n
-gene case)
.

Three points are worth noting. (i) The closed-loop Jacobian of the
two-gene benchmark has positive determinant and negative trace, so its

linearisation
is stable for every
K_{i}\geq 0
.
Theorem
2
is strictly stronger: local spectral
stability alone does not preclude large-amplitude limit cycles,
additional equilibria, or a non-trivial basin boundary, whereas
condition (
9

every
trajectory
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
converges exponentially to the unique equilibrium

\mathbf{x}_{d}
. This large-signal guarantee is the relevant one when the
closed loop must recover from deep-OFF states, or when feedback delays
threaten Hopf-type oscillations
[
16
]
.
(ii) The global Lyapunov function
V=c_{2}e_{1}^{2}+c_{1}e_{2}^{2}
is the
diagonal-weighting analogue of the closed-form
local
Lyapunov
certificate of Proposition
2
, with the
setpoint-specific Jacobian couplings replaced by their global suprema

c_{i}=\kappa_{i}\lambda/4
; the sign-cancellation that makes the local
certificate exact is exactly what makes the global one work. The cost is
mild: condition (
9
) constrains only the
product

\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}
, and is implied by—but far weaker than—the
setpoint-uniform Gershgorin requirement
\alpha_{i}>c_{i}
for each
i
.
(iii) Theorem
2
is dedicated to the two-gene
benchmark; for the general
n
-gene network (
3
) a
coarser global certificate follows from a comparison-principle argument.
Since every logistic factor lies in
(0,1)
,

|f_{i}(\mathbf{x})-f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{d})|\leq(\lambda/4)\sum_{j}|e_{j}|
over the
regulators
j
of gene
i
, so the upper Dini derivatives of
|e_{i}|
are
dominated by the Metzler matrix
C
with
C_{ii}=-(\gamma_{i}+K_{i})
and

C_{ij}=\kappa_{i}\lambda/4
. Global exponential stability holds whenever

C
is Hurwitz; a convenient sufficient condition is the row
diagonal-dominance bound

K_{i}\;>\;\frac{\kappa_{i}\lambda\,d_{i}}{4}-\gamma_{i},\qquad d_{i}:=\#\{\text{regulators of gene }i\},

(14)

the
global, nonlinear
counterpart of the local Gershgorin
condition (
8
).

3.3
The logistic structural advantage in additive control

Proposition 1

(Logistic Advantage in State-Feedback)
.

For the logistic model in Architecture B, the regulatory production term
is uniformly bounded:

0\;<\;\kappa_{i}f_{i}^{\mathrm{log}}(\mathbf{x}_{d})\;\leq\;\kappa_{i},

(15)

on the entire closed positive orthant
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, so the
feedforward satisfies

u_{i}^{\mathrm{ff}}\;=\;\gamma_{i}x_{d,i}-\kappa_{i}f_{i}^{\mathrm{log}}(\mathbf{x}_{d})\;\in\;\bigl[\gamma_{i}x_{d,i}-\kappa_{i},\;\gamma_{i}x_{d,i}\bigr]

(16)

for every positive setpoint, including the limit
\mathbf{x}_{d}\to\mathbf{0}
.
Moreover, for the two-gene activator–repressor case (where each
f_{i}

depends on a single coordinate via a single logistic factor),

|[J_{f}]_{ij}|\;=\;\kappa_{i}\lambda\,f^{\pm}(x_{d,j})\bigl(1-f^{\pm}(x_{d,j})\bigr)\;\leq\;\kappa_{i}\lambda/4,

(17)

and the off-diagonal Jacobian entries are
strictly positive
on
the entire positive orthant (since
0<f^{\pm}<1
there).
For an
n
-gene network with multi-factor
f_{i}
, an additional product of

f
-values appears in the chain-rule expansion; since each such factor lies
in
(0,1)
, the bound
\kappa_{i}\lambda/4
and the strict positivity are
both preserved.

For the Hill activation model with
n>1
,
h^{+}(\mathbf{x}_{d})\to 0

as any activating
x_{d,j}\to 0
. Hence the natural regulatory production

\kappa_{i}h^{+}_{i}
vanishes, forcing the additive control to
carry the entire degradation load
u_{i}^{\mathrm{ff}}\to\gamma_{i}x_{d,i}

unaided by any regulatory contribution. Simultaneously, the off-diagonal
Jacobian entry

[J_{f}^{\mathrm{Hill}}]_{ij}\;=\;\kappa_{i}\,n\theta^{n}\,x_{d,j}^{n-1}/(x_{d,j}^{n}+\theta^{n})^{2}\;=\;\Theta\bigl(x_{d,j}^{n-1}\bigr)\;\to\;0

(18)

as
x_{d,j}\to 0^{+}
, so the network
decouples
: the cross-axis
restoring action
[J_{f}]_{ij}\,e_{j}
becomes negligible and only the
diagonal gain
-\gamma_{i}-K_{i}
stabilises the network. In the alternative
output-multiplicative architecture

\dot{x}_{i}=\kappa_{i}u_{i}f_{i}(\mathbf{x})-\gamma_{i}x_{i}
in which the input
multiplies the regulatory output,
h^{+}_{i}(\mathbf{x})=0
would
make
u_{i}
have
no effect whatsoever
on
\dot{x}_{i}
—an exact loss
of controllability that the logistic structure prevents because

f_{i}^{\mathrm{log}}>0
everywhere on the positive orthant.

Proof.

For the logistic factor
f^{\pm}(x;\theta,\lambda)=1/(1+e^{\mp\lambda(x-\theta)})\in(0,1)

the bound
f(1-f)\leq 1/4
is attained at the threshold
x=\theta
and is
elementary. Equation (
17
) follows by chain rule

\partial f^{\pm}/\partial x=\pm\lambda f^{\pm}(1-f^{\pm})
. For the Hill
factor
h^{+}(x)=x^{n}/(x^{n}+\theta^{n})
with
n>1
, direct calculation gives

h^{+}(x)\sim(x/\theta)^{n}\to 0
and

\partial h^{+}/\partial x=n\theta^{n}x^{n-1}/(x^{n}+\theta^{n})^{2}\sim nx^{n-1}/\theta^{n}\to 0
as
x\to 0^{+}
, since the exponent
n-1>0
.
Equation (
18
) is direct differentiation.
∎

Remark 3

(Output-multiplicative control and the logistic advantage)
.

The logistic basal production
f_{i}^{\mathrm{log}}>0
keeps every positive
setpoint feasible even when the activator is absent. In the
output-multiplicative variant
\dot{x}_{i}=\kappa_{i}u_{i}f_{i}(\mathbf{x})-\gamma_{i}x_{i}
, driving a Hill-activated gene away from an activator-absent
state is infeasible because
f_{i}^{\mathrm{Hill}}=0
leaves
u_{i}
without
authority, whereas the logistic
f_{i}^{\mathrm{log}}>0
retains
controllability throughout the closed positive orthant

\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. In the additive Architecture B the Hill control
input remains effective, but the feedforward must then supply the full
degradation flux unaided. In the argument-modulating Architecture A the
input acts through
u_{ij}x_{j}
and is therefore effective only on the open
orthant
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
for
both
regulatory functions. The
logistic model thus combines controllability in the output-multiplicative
variant with bounded, basal-pre-loaded actuation in Architecture B.

4
Closed-Form Lyapunov Certificates

Theorem
1
establishes asymptotic stability of the
closed loop but does not by itself yield a
transient performance
certificate
—an explicit settling-time bound or a robustness margin to
disturbances. We now supply such certificates via a quadratic Lyapunov
function that, remarkably, has a closed-form expression in the very
entries
A,B
of the closed-loop Jacobian
J_{f}-\Gamma-K
. The result is
purely structural: it applies to
any

2\times 2
matrix sharing
the sign-pattern of the activator–repressor closed-loop Jacobian
(negative diagonal, off-diagonals of opposite sign), with positive
parameters
\alpha,\beta,A,B
. It therefore covers the
state-feedback closed loop developed in
Section
3
, and indeed any feedback architecture
whose closed loop has this sign structure, differing only in the values
of
A
,
B
,
\alpha
,
\beta
.

4.1
Closed-form Lyapunov function

Proposition 2

(Closed-form Lyapunov function for the closed-loop structural class)
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,A,B>0
and consider any matrix of structural form

M\;=\;\begin{bmatrix}-\alpha&-A\\
B&-\beta\end{bmatrix}.

(19)

Then the weighted quadratic function

V(\mathbf{e})\;=\;B\,e_{1}^{2}+A\,e_{2}^{2}\;=\;\mathbf{e}^{T}P\,\mathbf{e},\qquad P=\operatorname{diag}(B,A),

(20)

is a strict Lyapunov function for the linear system
\dot{\mathbf{e}}=M\mathbf{e}
:

\displaystyle M^{T}P+PM

\displaystyle\;=\;\operatorname{diag}(-2\alpha B,\;-2\beta A),

(21)

\displaystyle\dot{V}\;=\;-2\alpha B\,e_{1}^{2}-2\beta A\,e_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\;\leq\;-2\,\rho\,V,

where
\rho:=\min(\alpha,\beta)
. Consequently,

\displaystyle V(\mathbf{e}(t))

\displaystyle\;\leq\;V(\mathbf{e}(0))\,e^{-2\rho\,t},\qquad t\geq 0,

(22)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{e}(t)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\sqrt{\frac{\max(A,B)}{\min(A,B)}}\;\|\mathbf{e}(0)\|_{2}\;e^{-\rho\,t}.

(23)

Proof.

Direct computation:

M^{T}P=\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}-\alpha B&AB\\
-AB&-\beta A\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]

and

PM=\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}-\alpha B&-AB\\
AB&-\beta A\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]
,
so the off-diagonal entries of
M^{T}P+PM
cancel exactly, yielding

M^{T}P+PM=\operatorname{diag}(-2\alpha B,-2\beta A)
. Hence

\dot{V}=\mathbf{e}^{T}(M^{T}P+PM)\mathbf{e}=-2\alpha Be_{1}^{2}-2\beta Ae_{2}^{2}
,
and since
\alpha Be_{1}^{2}+\beta Ae_{2}^{2}\geq\min(\alpha,\beta)(Be_{1}^{2}+Ae_{2}^{2})=\rho V
,
the bound
\dot{V}\leq-2\rho V
follows. Inequality (
22
) is
Grönwall’s lemma applied to
\dot{V}\leq-2\rho V
. For (
23
),
note
\min(A,B)\|\mathbf{e}\|_{2}^{2}\leq V(\mathbf{e})\leq\max(A,B)\|\mathbf{e}\|_{2}^{2}
;
combining the upper bound at
t=0
with the lower bound at
t

and (
22
) yields the stated inequality.
∎

Remark 4

(Tightness and geometric interpretation)
.

Four observations clarify the result.
(i) When
\alpha=\beta
(matched degradation rates),

\alpha Be_{1}^{2}+\beta Ae_{2}^{2}=\alpha(Be_{1}^{2}+Ae_{2}^{2})=\alpha V

exactly
, so
\dot{V}=-2\alpha V
is an equality, not just a bound.
(ii) When additionally
A=B
, the prefactor
\sqrt{\max(A,B)/\min(A,B)}

in (
23
) equals
1
and the Euclidean norm contracts
strictly monotonically. For
A\neq B
, the sub-level sets of
V
are
ellipses with semi-axes
\sqrt{V/B}
along
e_{1}
and
\sqrt{V/A}
along

e_{2}
, so the Euclidean norm
\|\mathbf{e}\|
may transiently exceed its
initial value by up to the factor
\sqrt{\max(A,B)/\min(A,B)}
even while

V
decays monotonically: this is the price of using a weighted Lyapunov
function to bound an unweighted norm.
(iii) The Lyapunov matrix
P=\operatorname{diag}(B,A)
is not the only
positive-definite choice that certifies stability: the family

\{c\,\operatorname{diag}(B,A):c>0\}
is the entire set of positive diagonal

P
for which
M^{T}P+PM
is itself diagonal (the off-diagonal

Bp_{2}-Ap_{1}=0
forces
p_{1}/p_{2}=B/A
). The specific normalisation

P=\operatorname{diag}(B,A)
has no special role beyond convenience;
diagonality is what makes
P
explicit in
A,B
without solving a full
algebraic Lyapunov equation.
(iv) The Lyapunov rate
\rho=\min(\alpha,\beta)
is in general
conservative relative to the true spectral decay rate of
M
. The
eigenvalues of (
19
) are

\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[-(\alpha+\beta)\pm\sqrt{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}-4AB}\,\bigr]
;
when
(\alpha-\beta)^{2}<4AB
they form a complex-conjugate pair with real
part
-(\alpha+\beta)/2
, so the exact decay rate is

(\alpha+\beta)/2\geq\min(\alpha,\beta)
, the two coinciding precisely
when
\alpha=\beta
(cf. point (i)). The certificate therefore loses at
most
|\alpha-\beta|/2
in rate—nothing when the closed-loop decay
rates are matched.

4.2
Explicit settling-time bound

Corollary 3

(Explicit settling-time bound)
.

Under the conditions of Proposition
2
, for any tolerance

\delta\in(0,1)
, the time required for the state to satisfy

\|\mathbf{e}(t)\|_{2}\leq\delta\,\|\mathbf{e}(0)\|_{2}
obeys

t_{s,\delta}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{\rho}\left[\ln(1/\delta)+\tfrac{1}{2}\ln\!\frac{\max(A,B)}{\min(A,B)}\right].

(24)

The bound is dominated by the slowest physical mode
1/\rho
; the
coupling-condition-number correction

\tfrac{1}{2}\ln(\max(A,B)/\min(A,B))
is small when
A
and
B
are
comparable.

Proof.

By (
23
), the inequality

\|\mathbf{e}(t)\|_{2}\leq\delta\|\mathbf{e}(0)\|_{2}
is implied by

\sqrt{\max(A,B)/\min(A,B)}\;e^{-\rho t}\;\leq\;\delta,

which rearranges to

\rho t\geq\ln(1/\delta)+\tfrac{1}{2}\ln\bigl(\max(A,B)/\min(A,B)\bigr).

Dividing by
\rho>0
yields (
24
).
∎

4.3
Input-to-state stability and parametric robustness

The same Lyapunov function (
20
) certifies an
input-to-state-stability (ISS) bound under bounded disturbances:

Proposition 3

(ISS bound under additive disturbances)
.

Consider the perturbed dynamics
\dot{\mathbf{e}}=M\mathbf{e}+\boldsymbol{\eta}(t)
,
with
M
as in (
19
) and
\|\boldsymbol{\eta}(t)\|_{2}\leq D
for
all
t\geq 0
. Then
V
from (
20
) satisfies

\dot{V}\;\leq\;-2\rho\,V+2\max(A,B)\,D\,\sqrt{V/\min(A,B)},

(25)

and every trajectory converges
exponentially with rate
\rho
on

W=\sqrt{V}
to the ultimate-bound set

\Omega_{D}\;=\;\Bigl\{\mathbf{e}:\,\|\mathbf{e}\|_{2}\;\leq\;\frac{\max(A,B)}{\min(A,B)}\cdot\frac{D}{\rho}\Bigr\}.

(26)

Proof.

Direct computation gives

\displaystyle\dot{V}\;

\displaystyle=\;2\mathbf{e}^{T}P\dot{\mathbf{e}}

\displaystyle=\;-2\alpha Be_{1}^{2}-2\beta Ae_{2}^{2}+2(Be_{1}\eta_{1}+Ae_{2}\eta_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\;-2\rho V+2\sqrt{B^{2}e_{1}^{2}+A^{2}e_{2}^{2}}\,\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\;-2\rho V+2\max(A,B)\,D\,\|\mathbf{e}\|_{2}.

Using
\|\mathbf{e}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{V/\min(A,B)}
gives the displayed bound.
Setting
W=\sqrt{V}
turns this into the linear differential inequality

\dot{W}\leq-\rho W+c
with constant

c:=\max(A,B)\,D/\sqrt{\min(A,B)}
, whose steady state is
W_{\infty}=c/\rho
.
Since
\|\mathbf{e}\|_{2}\leq W/\sqrt{\min(A,B)}
, this is equivalent to

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|\mathbf{e}(t)\|_{2}\;\leq\;\frac{W_{\infty}}{\sqrt{\min(A,B)}}\;=\;\frac{\max(A,B)\,D}{\rho\,\min(A,B)}.

∎

Remark 6

(Application to state-feedback)
.

Proposition
2
applies verbatim to the state-feedback
closed-loop Jacobian of Theorem
1
. For the two-gene
activator–repressor network without self-regulation
(
[J_{f}]_{ii}=0
), the matrix
J_{f}-\Gamma-K
has structural
form (
19
) with

\alpha=\gamma_{1}+K_{1}
,
\beta=\gamma_{2}+K_{2}
,
A=|[J_{f}]_{12}|
,

B=|[J_{f}]_{21}|
. The convergence rate is therefore

\rho=\min(\gamma_{i}+K_{i})
, which can be increased arbitrarily by raising
the feedback gain
K_{i}
—a gain-rate trade-off unavailable to control
schemes whose effective decay rate is intrinsically bounded by the
open-loop degradation. By Proposition
3
, the
disturbance-rejection ultimate bound for state-feedback shrinks as

D/\min_{i}(\gamma_{i}+K_{i})
, providing an explicit design knob for noise
attenuation in noisy biological environments.

A noteworthy consequence: for this structural class,
\det(M)=\alpha\beta+AB>0
and
\mathrm{trace}(M)=-(\alpha+\beta)<0
, so Routh–Hurwitz
already certifies stability of the linearisation for
every

K_{i}\geq 0
. In particular, the Gershgorin
condition (
8
) of Theorem
1
,
while a valid sufficient condition for general
n
-dimensional networks,
is conservative for the two-gene activator–repressor network: even

K_{i}=0
(open loop) gives a
locally
asymptotically stable
linearisation, with local rate
\rho=\min(\gamma_{i})
. Note that this is
only a
local
guarantee: at
K_{i}=0
the global
condition (
9
) of Theorem
2
need not
hold (for the benchmark parameters of Section
8
it does
not), so global exponential stability of the nonlinear closed loop is
not certified at
K_{i}=0
.
Proposition
2
provides the explicit Lyapunov function
underlying the local observation. The Gershgorin gain bound remains
useful in higher dimensions, where the simple
2\times 2

sign-cancellation argument no longer applies.

Remark 7

(Robustness to parametric model mismatch)
.

Proposition
3
also quantifies robustness to errors in the
plant parameters. Suppose the feedforward
u_{i}^{\mathrm{ff}}

in (
6
) is computed from nominal parameters

(\kappa_{i},\gamma_{i},\lambda)
, while the true plant runs on perturbed
values
(\hat{\kappa}_{i},\hat{\gamma}_{i},\hat{\lambda})
. The mismatch enters
the error dynamics as an additive perturbation

\eta_{i}(t)=\bigl(\hat{\kappa}_{i}\hat{f}_{i}(\mathbf{x})-\kappa_{i}f_{i}(\mathbf{x})\bigr)-(\hat{\gamma}_{i}-\gamma_{i})x_{i}
. On any bounded operating region
\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq R
,

\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_{2}\leq D(R)
for a finite
D(R)
that vanishes as the
parameter mismatch tends to zero, since
\hat{f}_{i},f_{i}\in(0,1]
and the
linear term is bounded on
\{\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq R\}
.
Proposition
3
then bounds the resulting steady-state
setpoint offset by

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|\mathbf{x}(t)-\mathbf{x}_{d}\|_{2}\leq\tfrac{\max(A,B)}{\min(A,B)}\,D(R)/\rho
, with
\rho=\min_{i}(\gamma_{i}+K_{i})
.
For sufficiently small mismatch the perturbed trajectories remain in such
a bounded operating region (standard ISS bootstrap), and the designer
shrinks the offset by raising the feedback gain. The proportional term
thus converts an un-modelled parametric error into a bounded,
gain-attenuable tracking offset: exact knowledge of

(\kappa,\gamma,\lambda)
is not required for practical setpoint
regulation, only for exact (
D=0
) cancellation.

5
Feedback Monostabilization

of Scalar Bistable Switches

The state-feedback design of Section
3
targets
the smooth multi-gene oscillator. We now turn to a complementary
scalar problem:
suppressing
bistability in a single logistic
self-activation node by proportional feedback. A canonical bistability
theorem for the logistic self-activation

\dot{x}(t)\;=\;\kappa\,f^{+}(x;\theta,\lambda)\;-\;\gamma\,x,\qquad x\geq 0,

(27)

proved in the companion paper
[
18
]
, states
that in the dimensionless plane

(\eta,\phi)=(\kappa\lambda/\gamma,\lambda\theta)
bistability holds
exactly inside the cusp region
\eta>4,\,\phi_{-}(\eta)<\phi<\phi_{+}(\eta)

with explicit saddle-node curves
\phi_{\pm}(\eta)
meeting at

(\eta,\phi)=(4,2)
. Closing the loop with a proportional law

u(t)=-K(x(t)-x^{d})
steering toward a desired set-point
x^{d}\geq 0

yields the controlled scalar equation

\dot{x}(t)\;=\;\kappa\,f^{+}(x(t);\theta,\lambda)\;-\;(\gamma+K)\,x(t)\;+\;Kx^{d}.

(28)

The effective decay rate becomes
\gamma+K
, the effective forcing
acquires a constant
Kx^{d}
, and the resulting closed loop is itself
a logistic self-activation with input. The next theorem gives the
sharp gain threshold above which the closed loop is monostable for
every choice of set-point and threshold.

Theorem 3

(Feedback Monostabilization)
.

Suppose the open-loop system (
27
) is in the
bistable regime
\eta=\kappa\lambda/\gamma>4
and

\phi_{-}(\eta)<\phi<\phi_{+}(\eta)
. Apply proportional feedback

u(t)=-K(x(t)-x^{d})
with
K\geq 0
and
x^{d}\geq 0
, and define the
closed-loop dimensionless amplification

\eta_{K}\;:=\;\frac{\kappa\lambda}{\gamma+K}.

(i)

If
K\geq K^{*}:=\dfrac{\kappa\lambda}{4}-\gamma
,
then
\eta_{K}\leq 4
and (
28
) is

monostable
: it has a unique equilibrium

x_{K}^{*}\in\bigl(Kx^{d}/(\gamma+K),\;(Kx^{d}+\kappa)/(\gamma+K)\bigr)
,
globally asymptotically stable on
[0,\infty)
. If moreover

K>K^{*}
, the convergence is globally exponential with rate
at least
\gamma+K-\kappa\lambda/4>0
.

(ii)

If
K<K^{*}
, the closed loop may remain
bistable; the bistability persists iff

(\eta_{K},\tilde{\phi}_{K})
with

\tilde{\phi}_{K}:=\lambda\bigl(\theta-Kx^{d}/(\gamma+K)\bigr)

satisfies

\phi_{-}(\eta_{K})<\tilde{\phi}_{K}<\phi_{+}(\eta_{K})
.

(iii)

Hence
K=K^{*}=\kappa\lambda/4-\gamma
is the

minimal
gain that guarantees monostability irrespective
of
x^{d}
and
\theta
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{g}_{K}(x):=\kappa f^{+}(x)-(\gamma+K)x+Kx^{d}
. Equilibria
of (
28
) are roots of
\tilde{g}_{K}
.

(i)
Differentiating,

\tilde{g}_{K}^{\prime}(x)=\kappa\lambda f^{+}(x)(1-f^{+}(x))-(\gamma+K)\leq\kappa\lambda/4-(\gamma+K)\leq 0
when
K\geq K^{*}
, with equality
only at the isolated point
x=\theta
. Hence
\tilde{g}_{K}
is strictly
decreasing on
[0,\infty)
. Since
\tilde{g}_{K}(0)=\kappa f^{+}(0)+Kx^{d}>0

and
\tilde{g}_{K}(x)\to-\infty
as
x\to\infty
, there is a unique zero

x_{K}^{*}
, and every solution of the scalar autonomous
equation (
28
) converges monotonically to it: the
closed loop is globally asymptotically stable. The enclosure

x_{K}^{*}\in\bigl(Kx^{d}/(\gamma+K),\,(Kx^{d}+\kappa)/(\gamma+K)\bigr)

follows from
0<\kappa f^{+}(x_{K}^{*})=(\gamma+K)x_{K}^{*}-Kx^{d}<\kappa
.
If moreover
K>K^{*}
, then
\tilde{g}_{K}^{\prime}(x)\leq\kappa\lambda/4-(\gamma+K)<0

uniformly, and the Grönwall argument applied to
y:=x-x_{K}^{*}
gives

|y(t)|\leq|y(0)|\,e^{-(\gamma+K-\kappa\lambda/4)t}
, i.e. global
exponential stability with rate at least
\gamma+K-\kappa\lambda/4>0
.

(ii)
For
K<K^{*}
,
\eta_{K}>4
. Setting

y:=x-Kx^{d}/(\gamma+K)
, the equilibrium condition

\kappa f^{+}(x;\theta,\lambda)=(\gamma+K)y
becomes

\kappa f^{+}(y;\tilde{\theta}_{K},\lambda)=(\gamma+K)y
with

\tilde{\theta}_{K}:=\theta-Kx^{d}/(\gamma+K)
, by the identity

f^{+}(y+c;\theta,\lambda)=f^{+}(y;\theta-c,\lambda)
. This is the
form of the open-loop bistability problem with parameters

(\kappa,\gamma+K,\tilde{\theta}_{K},\lambda)
. Applying the cusp
condition of
[
18
]
in these closed-loop
coordinates gives the stated window.

(iii)
Sufficiency of
K\geq K^{*}
is (i). For necessity in the
worst case, for any
K<K^{*}
one has
\eta_{K}>4
, so the bistable
window has positive width. Choosing
x^{d}=0
and

\lambda\theta\in(\phi_{-}(\eta_{K}),\phi_{+}(\eta_{K}))
produces a
bistable closed loop. Hence no
K<K^{*}
guarantees monostability
for every
(\theta,x^{d})
.
∎

Remark 8

(Engineering interpretation)
.

Theorem
3
converts the open-loop cusp condition

\eta>4
into the closed-loop
monostabilization budget

K^{*}\;=\;\frac{\kappa\lambda}{4}-\gamma\;=\;\frac{\gamma(\eta-4)}{4},

(29)

the minimum feedback gain that suppresses bistability for every
choice of set-point
x^{d}
and threshold
\theta
. The bound depends
linearly on the open-loop amplification ratio
\eta
in excess of the
cusp value
4
, and collapses to
K^{*}=0
at the cusp. In
synthetic-biology parlance: the harder the open-loop switch (large

\eta
), the more “decay actuation” is required to suppress it.
The closed-form expression (
29
) is the analytical
dividend of the logistic representation: the logistic derivative has the
clean global maximum slope
\lambda/4
attained at
x=\theta
. The Hill
counterpart
h^{+}(x)=x^{n}/(x^{n}+\theta^{n})
instead has cooperativity-dependent
maximum slope

\max_{x\geq 0}\frac{dh^{+}}{dx}\;=\;\frac{(n+1)^{(n+1)/n}\,(n-1)^{(n-1)/n}}{4n\,\theta},

attained at the non-trivial location

x=\theta\,\bigl((n-1)/(n+1)\bigr)^{1/n}
, which depends on the
cooperativity
n
in a way far less amenable to closed-form control
synthesis than the parameter-free logistic slope
\lambda/4
.

6
Halanay-Type Delay-Uniform Global Exponential Stability

The state-feedback design of Section
3
treats
the delay-free 2-D oscillator; this section complements it
with a
delay-uniform
global exponential stability theorem for
the scalar feedback loop, useful when actuation (optogenetic
switching, inducer diffusion, signal transduction) introduces a
non-negligible delay. Consider the controlled scalar DDE

\dot{x}(t)\;=\;\kappa\,f^{\pm}\!\bigl(x(t-\tau);\theta,\lambda\bigr)\;-\;\gamma\,x(t)\;-\;K(x(t)-x^{d}),

(30)

with
x(t)\geq 0
,
\tau\geq 0
,
K\geq 0
,
x^{d}\geq 0
, and either choice of sign
f^{\pm}
.

Theorem 4

(Halanay-Type Global Exponential Convergence)
.

Let
x^{*}_{K}\geq 0
be an equilibrium of (
30
)
(which exists by Brouwer’s theorem applied to the compact invariant
interval
[
18
]
). If

\gamma+K\;>\;\frac{\kappa\lambda}{4},

(31)

then
x^{*}_{K}
is the unique equilibrium and is
globally
exponentially stable
on
[0,\infty)
,
uniformly in the delay

\tau\geq 0
. Explicitly, there exist
C,\beta>0
depending only on

(\kappa,\gamma,K,\lambda)
such that for every initial history

\varphi\in C([-\tau,0];[0,\infty))
and every
\tau\geq 0
,

|x(t)-x^{*}_{K}|\;\leq\;C\,e^{-\beta t}\,\sup_{-\tau\leq s\leq 0}|\varphi(s)-x^{*}_{K}|,\qquad t\geq 0,

where
\beta>0
is the unique positive solution of the transcendental
equation
\beta=(\gamma+K)-(\kappa\lambda/4)e^{\beta\tau}
.

Proof.

Let
y(t):=x(t)-x^{*}_{K}
. By the mean-value theorem applied to

f^{\pm}
between
x(t-\tau)
and
x^{*}_{K}
,

f^{\pm}(x(t-\tau))-f^{\pm}(x^{*}_{K})=\pm\lambda f^{\pm}(\xi_{t})(1-f^{\pm}(\xi_{t}))y(t-\tau)

for some
\xi_{t}
. Since
f^{\pm}(1-f^{\pm})\leq 1/4
pointwise,

\dot{y}(t)\;=\;-(\gamma+K)y(t)\;+\;r(t),\qquad|r(t)|\leq\frac{\kappa\lambda}{4}\,|y(t-\tau)|.

Defining
V(t):=|y(t)|
, the comparison principle gives

D^{+}V(t)\leq-(\gamma+K)V(t)+(\kappa\lambda/4)\sup_{t-\tau\leq s\leq t}V(s),

which is the hypothesis of the classical Halanay
inequality
[
22
]
. With
a:=\gamma+K
and

b:=\kappa\lambda/4
, condition (
31
) reads
a>b
,
and the Halanay inequality yields

V(t)\leq e^{-\beta t}\sup_{-\tau\leq s\leq 0}V(s),

with
\beta
the unique positive root of
\beta=a-be^{\beta\tau}
.
Existence of this
root for
a>b
follows from the elementary observation that

F(\beta):=\beta-a+be^{\beta\tau}
is strictly increasing on

[0,\infty)
(since
F^{\prime}(\beta)=1+b\tau e^{\beta\tau}>0
), with

F(0)=b-a<0
by hypothesis and
F(a-b)\geq 0
.
Uniqueness of
x^{*}_{K}
follows because

g_{K}(x):=\kappa f^{\pm}(x)-(\gamma+K)x+Kx^{d}
has

g_{K}^{\prime}(x)\leq\kappa\lambda/4-(\gamma+K)<0
under
(
31
).
∎

The rate
\beta
in Theorem
4
is defined implicitly by a
transcendental equation. The next corollary makes it concrete: it
brackets
\beta(\tau)
between two elementary closed-form expressions,
turning the delay-uniform guarantee into a quantitative,
design-usable rate.

Corollary 4

(Explicit closed-form bounds on the Halanay rate)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
4
, with
a:=\gamma+K
and

b:=\kappa\lambda/4
(so
a>b
), the convergence rate
\beta=\beta(\tau)

is continuous and strictly decreasing in
\tau
, with
\beta(0)=a-b
, and
satisfies the explicit two-sided estimate

\frac{a-b}{\,1+b\tau\,e^{(a-b)\tau}\,}\;\leq\;\beta(\tau)\;\leq\;\min\!\Bigl(a-b,\;\tfrac{1}{\tau}\ln\tfrac{a}{b}\Bigr),\quad\tau>0.

(32)

Both bounds tend to
a-b
as
\tau\to 0^{+}
and decay to
0
as

\tau\to\infty
, so
\beta(\tau)>0
for every finite delay—the
delay-uniform stability of Theorem
4
, now with an
explicit rate. The upper bound
\tfrac{1}{\tau}\ln(a/b)
is the
operative one at large delay, the lower bound is tight at small delay.

Proof.

By Theorem
4
,
\beta
is the unique positive root of

F(\beta):=\beta-a+be^{\beta\tau}
, and
F
is strictly increasing; hence

\beta\leq\beta_{0}\Leftrightarrow F(\beta_{0})\geq 0
and

\beta\geq\beta_{0}\Leftrightarrow F(\beta_{0})\leq 0
.
Upper bound:

be^{\beta\tau}=a-\beta<a
gives
\beta\tau<\ln(a/b)
, and
\beta\leq a-b

holds because the root lies in
(0,a-b]
.
Lower bound:
set

\beta_{0}=(a-b)/(1+b\tau e^{(a-b)\tau})\leq a-b
. The elementary
inequality
e^{x}\leq 1+x\,e^{x}
for
x\geq 0
gives

e^{\beta_{0}\tau}\leq 1+\beta_{0}\tau e^{\beta_{0}\tau}\leq 1+\beta_{0}\tau e^{(a-b)\tau}
, whence

F(\beta_{0})\leq\beta_{0}\bigl(1+b\tau e^{(a-b)\tau}\bigr)-(a-b)=0
, so

\beta\geq\beta_{0}
.
Monotonicity:
implicit differentiation of

F(\beta,\tau)=0
yields

d\beta/d\tau=-b\beta e^{\beta\tau}/(1+b\tau e^{\beta\tau})<0
.
∎

Remark 9

(Comparison with the delay-free state-feedback theorems)
.

Theorem
4
is the
global, nonlinear, delay-uniform

counterpart of the delay-free state-feedback theorems of
Section
3
: Theorem
1
gives

local
exponential stability of the 2-D linearisation under a
Gershgorin gain condition; Theorem
2
gives

global
exponential stability of the (delay-free) 2-D nonlinear
closed loop under the Lyapunov gain condition (
9
);
Theorem
4
gives
global
exponential stability of
the
scalar delayed
closed loop under the (delay-uniform)
condition
\gamma+K>\kappa\lambda/4
that the closed-loop decay rate
exceeds the maximum logistic slope. The three results share the global
Lipschitz bound
\lambda/4
as their structural cornerstone.
Condition (
31
) is sharp in the limit
\tau\to 0

(recovering the scalar global stability bound), and is decoupled from

\theta
,
x^{d}
, and the sign of
f^{\pm}
, making it directly usable for
design.

8.6
Discussion

Three regimes magnify the structural difference predicted by
Proposition
1
: (i) As
x_{d,1}\to 0
, the Hill
off-diagonal Jacobian collapses polynomially and the actuator must
absorb the entire degradation load, whereas the logistic coupling and
basal production both remain bounded away from zero. (ii) For large
Hill coefficients
n
, the production derivative scales as

\Theta(n/\theta)
near the threshold, creating numerical stiffness
that the logistic derivative, uniformly bounded by
\lambda/4
, does
not suffer from. (iii) For non-integer Hill coefficients, the Hill
production
h^{+}(x)=x^{n}/(\theta^{n}+x^{n})
becomes non-smooth at the origin
and ill-conditioned for fractional arguments, while the logistic
sigmoid
1/(1+e^{-\lambda(x-\theta)})
remains
C^{\infty}
everywhere.

The Lyapunov bound of Corollary
3
, instantiated for
the SF closed loop in Remark
5
, predicts a
relative-to-initial norm-settling time of

t_{s,5\%}^{\|\cdot\|}\leq 2.15
a.u. at the standard setpoint. Direct
ODE simulation of the
full nonlinear
closed loop from

\mathbf{x}(0)=(0.1,0.1)
gives empirical relative-to-initial norm
settling at
t_{s,5\%}^{\|\cdot\|}\approx 2.15
a.u., a
<0.5\%
gap
to the linearisation-based bound. The per-coordinate absolute-to-setpoint
times
t_{s,1}=2.18
and
t_{s,2}=1.88
reported in
Table
1
use a different convergence criterion
(5% of
x_{d,i}
, not 5% of
\|\mathbf{e}(0)\|_{2}
); the closeness
of
t_{s,1}
to the norm bound is incidental.

9
Conclusions

This paper has developed an analytical additive state-feedback framework for
logistic-based gene regulatory networks, together with a closed-form Lyapunov
certificate supplying quantitative performance bounds (settling-time and ISS
ultimate-bound estimates) and two complementary scalar control results
(parameter-uniform monostabilization and Halanay-type delay-uniform stability).
The work is motivated by the structural limitations of the classical Hill
function for control design and by our companion series on logistic
models
[
13
,
15
,
16
]
.

Main findings.
The principal contributions, each
accompanied by an explicit stability or performance result, are
summarised below.
(1)
Additive state-feedback control, local and global
: A
feedforward-plus-proportional
law (Equation (
6
)) makes any positive setpoint a closed-loop
equilibrium of the additive-input architecture, regardless of whether it is
an equilibrium of the uncontrolled dynamics. Theorem
1

gives an explicit Gershgorin-based gain bound for local exponential
stability; Theorem
2
upgrades this to
global

exponential stability of the nonlinear closed loop under the explicit
gain condition
(\gamma_{1}+K_{1})(\gamma_{2}+K_{2})>\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\lambda^{2}/64
,
via a common quadratic Lyapunov function built on the logistic sector
bound, with a closed-form rate and
a global settling-time bound (Corollary
1
), and
the same Lyapunov function yields ultimately bounded tracking of time-varying
references (Corollary
2
);
and Proposition
1
formalises the logistic advantage:
both the regulatory production and the off-diagonal Jacobian coupling
remain bounded away from zero on the entire positive orthant for the
logistic, whereas the Hill activation makes both vanish as the activator
setpoint approaches zero, causing network decoupling in additive control
and—in the output-multiplicative architecture—outright controllability loss.
(2)
Closed-form Lyapunov function and quantitative performance bounds
:
The closed-loop Jacobian falls into a structural class of
2\times 2

matrices admitting an exact diagonal Lyapunov weighting

P=\operatorname{diag}(B,A)
(Proposition
2
). This yields
an explicit settling-time bound matching direct ODE simulation to within

1\%
–
16\%
depending on initial-condition direction
(Corollary
3
; tight for IC aligned with the heavier
Lyapunov weight, conservative for IC aligned with the lighter weight),
and an ISS-type ultimate-bound certificate quantifying disturbance
rejection (Proposition
3
). The Lyapunov rate

\rho=\min(\gamma_{i}+K_{i})
is arbitrarily accelerable by the feedback
gain, and the disturbance ultimate bound shrinks correspondingly
(Remark
6
). The same matrix structure arises in any
closed loop of this sign form, so the
certificate transfers without modification between architectures.
(3)
Monostabilization of bistable switches
: For scalar
logistic self-activation switches in the bistable cusp region, the

parameter-uniform monostabilization budget

K^{*}=\kappa\lambda/4-\gamma
(Theorem
3
) is the
minimum proportional gain above which the closed loop is monostable for
every set-point and every threshold.
(4)
Delay-uniform global exponential stability
: For the scalar
delayed feedback loop, the condition
\gamma+K>\kappa\lambda/4

(Theorem
4
) yields
delay-uniform
global
exponential stability via a logistic–Lipschitz Halanay inequality, and
Corollary
4
brackets the delay-dependent
convergence rate between two elementary closed-form expressions. A
worked T-cell switch application (Section
7
,
Figure
1
) illustrates both scalar results.

Numerical evidence.
Side-by-side benchmark simulations
(Section
8
) under identical setpoints, gains, and parameters
show that the state-feedback law converges robustly to the prescribed
setpoint for both the logistic and Hill regulatory functions in the nominal
operating range, and that the simulated settling times match the Lyapunov
prediction of Remark
5
. The structural divergence
predicted by Proposition
1
manifests at the boundary
of the positive orthant, where the Hill off-diagonal Jacobian coupling

|[J_{f}^{\mathrm{Hill}}]_{21}|=\Theta(x_{d,1}^{n-1})
collapses to zero as

x_{d,1}\to 0
while the logistic counterpart converges to the strictly
positive limit
\kappa\lambda f^{+}(0)(1-f^{+}(0))
(Figure
5
).
This confirms the operational regime—small or vanishing activator
setpoints—where the logistic design retains network coupling that Hill
loses.

Key advantages of logistic-based models for closed-loop control.

•

Continuous controllability
: Non-zero production at all
expression levels prevents loss of controllability in OFF-states and
low-expression regimes where Hill-based models fail.

•

Biological realism
: Non-zero basal expression faithfully models
promoter leakiness and persistent low-expression states without
artificial offsets.

•

Analytical tractability
: Closed-form feedforward laws,
local (Gershgorin) and global (common quadratic Lyapunov)
stability conditions in explicit form
(Theorems
1
and
2
),
closed-form settling-time, tracking, and ISS certificates from
the explicit diagonal Lyapunov function
(Propositions
2
and
3
,
Corollaries
3
and
2
), and the parameter-uniform
monostabilization budget—all explicit, implementable formulas
that remain intractable for Hill functions with non-integer
exponents.

•

Computational robustness
: Smooth, bounded, and Lipschitz
responses prevent numerical stiffness in ODE solvers underlying
closed-loop simulation.

•

Experimental compatibility
: Parameters
\lambda
and
\theta

correspond directly to tunable molecular properties (cooperativity,
binding affinity), enabling systematic implementation via optogenetics
and directed evolution.

Future directions.

•

Extension of the closed-form
Lyapunov
framework
(Section
4
) to
n
-gene networks with general
topologies: the comparison-principle argument of
Remark
2
already yields a global stability
certificate for the
n
-gene case, but the exact diagonal Lyapunov
weighting
P=\operatorname{diag}(B,A)
of
Proposition
2
—and hence the sharp
settling-time and ISS certificates—rests on a
2\times 2

sign-cancellation that does not extend directly to higher
dimensions.

•

Extension to stochastic gene networks, incorporating intrinsic and
extrinsic noise relevant to low-copy-number regimes.

•

Integration of spatial dynamics and cell-to-cell communication in
multi-cellular systems.

•

Adaptive feedback gains combining logistic-based mechanistic models
with online identification.

•

Experimental validation in optogenetic platforms supporting millisecond
feedback resolution.
