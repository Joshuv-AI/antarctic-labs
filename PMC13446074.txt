Discovering potential key biomarkers and molecular mechanisms in Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease and rheumatoid arthritis using integrated bioinformatics and machine learning approaches
Authors: Wahia Tasnim, M. Rubaiyat Hossain Mondal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) and Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) are among the leading causes of illness and mortality globally. Many studies claim that these diseases are interconnected. However, the underlying molecular processes and shared biomarkers between RA and COPD are still undiscovered.Methods:This research is aimed at discovering the key potential biomarkers and mechanisms in RA and COPD, incorporating several Bioinformatics and Machine Learning (ML) approaches via R programming. Several validation methods are also applied to validate the key potential biomarkers.Results:By performing the differential expression analysis, we have discovered 90 common Differentially Expressed Genes (DEGs) between RA and COPD. From the Gene Set Enrichment analysis, we get the top molecular mechanisms related to the common DEGs. From the Protein Protein Interaction analysis, we get the candidate hub genes among the common DEGs. Among all the ML approaches, ElasticNet outperforms with the highest accuracy of 91.52% with a set of important genes. By intersecting the candidate hub genes and the ElasticNet important genes, we get four candidate genes, ETS1, YY1, CREBP5, and GTF2H1. Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve and Expression analysis show robust discriminatory efficacy for all four genes, while nomogram modeling further validates their predictive correlations with RA and COPD. Finally, the candidate transcription factors, microRNAs and drugs related to the potential biomarkers are also suggested.Conclusion:In conclusion, this research identifies the key potential molecular mechanisms and four potential biomarkers between RA and COPD. These findings could potentially improve our understanding of the molecular mechanisms behind COPD and RA by providing a preliminary basis for future experimental and clinical studies.

Introduction

Biomedical research has made enormous advances in recent decades, changing our comprehension of intricate disease systems and accelerating the creation of innovative treatment approaches. Although they served as the basis for early biomedical discoveries, traditional two-dimensional (2D) cell culture techniques and conventional animal models have shown considerable limitations in adequately reproducing the complex physiological and pathological circumstances of the human body. Disease modeling has been transformed by innovations like organ-on-a-chip systems and 3D organoids, which allow researchers to reproduce human physiological and pathological circumstances with uncommon precision.1 These state-of-the-art systems, which are produced from either stem cells or patient-specific materials, have shown a remarkable ability to replicate complex tissue structures, providing more physiologically accurate models for toxicity assessment, drug discovery, and disease inquiry. Furthermore, the shift to customized and precision medicine is being accelerated by the integration of biosensors, multi-omics profiling, and artificial intelligence (AI) with these cutting-edge platforms. These interdisciplinary developments have radically changed the field of modern medicine.

Biomarkers are essential for the development of customized therapy interventions, early diagnosis, and identification of disease. A gene, protein, substance present in body fluids like blood or tissue, or any other chemical entity that may predispose to a disease state, can all be considered as biomarkers.2 Nayak, SP Ramya Ranjan, et al.,3 in their study, found DKO3 as a potential therapeutic biomarker for the treatment of inflammatory diseases and diabetes. Packiapalavesam, Shakthi Devi, et al.,4 in their study, found compound S35 as a perfect candidate for selectively inhibiting CA IX. The discovery of reliable molecular biomarkers holds great promise for enhancing clinical outcomes through personalized medicine techniques in addition to facilitating a deeper comprehension of the underlying pathophysiological mechanisms of diseases. Finding shared molecular biomarkers is particularly crucial in complicated, multifactorial diseases like Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) and Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD), as it may reveal shared pathogenic pathways that could function as unified treatment targets.

The diverse pulmonary disorder, COPD is typified by anomalies in the lung system (bronchitis and bronchiolitis), which lead to obstruction of airflow and worsening breathing problems. A primary cause of illness, mortality, and health issues worldwide is COPD.5 COPD is predicted to rank as the third most fatal illness worldwide by 2030.6 According to estimates from the World Health Organization, 600 million individuals globally have COPD, making it the fourth leading cause of death.7 A growing number of people worldwide are suffering from the crippling symptoms of COPD, which include persistent coughing, sputum production, and dyspnea.8,9 Many cardiovascular conditions, such as heart failure, coronary atherosclerotic heart disease, diabetes, lung cancer, and arthritis, are frequently co-occurring with COPD in clinical practice.10

RA is the most prevalent kind of arthritis, which is characterized by symmetric, peripheral inflammation and extra-articular symptoms. RA often necessitates lifelong care and can result in chronic impairment.11 It is distinguished by invasive, symmetrical, multi-joint arthritis of the hand and foot’s tiny joints. The immune system attacks the joint tissue as RA worsens, often leading to extra-articular involvement of organs and serum rheumatic factor positivity. This can ultimately cause joint destruction, loss of function, and persistent disability.[12], [13], [14] The higher frequency of coronary atherosclerotic heart disease highlights how the extended inflammatory response greatly increases the RA population’s risk of cardiovascular disease.15 There is still no recognized cause of RA, which has a major effect on people and society.16 Even after controlling for lifestyle factors and post-diagnosis mediators like smoking, an increased incidence of COPD was associated with RA.17 A cohort research found that RA had a Hazard Ratio (HR) of 1.52 (95% CI: 1.17–1.97) and was related to a higher risk of COPD.17 According to a different cohort research, individuals with RA were more likely than the control group to develop COPD, with an HR of 2.11 (95% CI, 1.96–2.28).18 The study of Dhital R et al. 19 also showed that the odds of COPD are higher with RA, with an HR of 1.20 (95% CI: 1.17–1.22). The existence of shared biological and molecular foundations of these two diseases is clearly implied by this clinical overlap. However, the shared molecular pathways and biomarkers connecting these two disorders are still poorly understood and unexplored.

The vast quantities of genetic information publicly available in the rapidly developing field of bioinformatics can be used to identify numerous unknown pathophysiological pathways in disease genesis. The bioinformatics methods will help us better understand the genetic causes of these related health problems. However, for traditional analytical methods, analyzing and interpreting large amounts of transcriptome sequencing data present substantial challenges. Machine learning’s (ML) remarkable capacity to identify hierarchical and non-linear correlations in such data is exceptional. ML plays a key role in diagnosing diseases by utilizing genomic and epigenetic data to identify genes essential to biological processes.20,21 Sun, Z. et al., 22 in their study, discovered the shared processes and biomarkers between ischemic heart failure and RA using thorough bioinformatics with ML assistance. Liang, Q. et al.,23 in their study, determined the immunological and metabolic diagnostic biomarkers that are shared by COPD and diabetes using extensive bioinformatics and ML. Sun, Z. et al.,24 in their study, found shared processes and biomarkers of atrial fibrillation and COPD by combining bioinformatics and ML.

Therefore, in this research, we intend to use extensive bioinformatics and more advanced machine-learning approaches via R programming to get potential biomarkers shared by both RA and COPD. R programming has greatly enabled biomedical researchers to carry out complex data analysis, bioinformatics processes, and ML modeling with high repeatability and transparency. With the use of specific R tools like limma, DESeq2, ggplot2, and caret, transcriptomic data analysis, differential gene expression profiling, and predictive modeling can now be easily combined into a single analytical framework. In this study, common DEGs in RA and COPD were found by applying rigorous differential expression analysis to microarray gene expression datasets obtained from the NCBI-GEO database, utilizing R programming. Through sophisticated bioinformatics and ML techniques built in R, possible molecular mechanisms and key potential biomarkers were found for this study. The novelty of this work lies in its integrative, data-driven framework that not only identifies but also validates shared potential biomarkers through validation analysis, such as ROC curve and nomogram analyses. The results of this study are anticipated to reveal mechanistic insights into the shared pathology of RA and COPD, as well as prospective avenues for the creation of diagnostic tools and treatment approaches for both conditions.

Literature review

Despite the growing awareness of these interconnected conditions, most prior research has focused on identifying biomarkers for either RA or COPD alone, not together. Z. Zhu et al. in their study,25 identified the potential biomarkers and pathways related only to COPD. Mu, K.L. et al.,16 in their study, identified the diagnostic biomarkers related only to rheumatoid arthritis (RA). Zhang, L. et al. 26 concentrated on locating immune-related hub genes only in COPD. This limited strategy ignores the possible genetic risk factors that are shared by both illnesses. Also, most of the studies performed very limited bioinformatics and ML analyses to find the potential biomarkers related to these diseases. Sun, Z. et al.,22 in their study, only applied three ML models (RF, SVM, and Lasso) to find the potential biomarkers. Also, they did not suggest any possible drugs or therapeutic targets like transcription factors (TFs) and miRNAs, which are highly beneficial for this kind of study. Liang, Q. et al.,23 in their study, only used two ML models (RF and Lasso) to find the potential biomarkers. They also did not suggest any possible drugs or therapeutic targets, such as transcription factors (TFs) and miRNAs. Sun, Z. et al.,24 in their study, perform several ML algorithms, but they perform very limited bioinformatics analyses. Filling up these gaps is crucial to improving RA and COPD prognosis, diagnosis, and therapy, as well as to giving us a more thorough knowledge of this aggressive malignancy. Materials and methods

Workflow of the analytical approach

In this research, we first discovered significantly differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in RA and COPD. Then, we have found the common DEGs between COPD and RA. After that, we performed the Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) and protein–protein interaction (PPI) analysis on the found common DEGs. From GSEA, we have found the potential Gene Ontology (GO) terms and pathways related to the common DEGs. From protein–protein interaction analysis, we get the potential hub genes based on several topological algorithms like MCC, MNC, EPC, Degree, and BottleNeck. We also applied machine learning models like InfoGain, SVM-RFE, RF, Lasso, Boruta, DT, ElasticNet, GA, LightGBM, MLPerceptron, Pearson, Ridge, XGBoost, and Naive Bayes on the common DEGs to find potential genes by ML Analysis. From the ML analysis, we select the ML model with the highest accuracy to get the potential ML genes. By intersecting the Bioinformatics and ML analysis results, we will get the final potential key biomarkers (genes) for COPD and RA. Later on, we validated the expression of the key genes using test datasets by performing ROC curve and Nomogram analyses. Finally, we have suggested the candidate drugs, transcription factors (TFs), and miRNAs associated with the potential key biomarkers. The detailed workflow is depicted in Fig. 1.

Dataset description

This study consists of four microarray datasets, including two COPD and two RA datasets named GSE76925, GSE118468, GSE93777, and GSE294225. GSE76925 is the training dataset of COPD, and GSE118468 is the testing dataset of COPD. GSE93777 is the training dataset of RA, and GSE294225 is the testing dataset of RA. We get all the datasets from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The GSE76925 (training) dataset of COPD includes a total of 151 samples with 111 COPD cases and 40 control cases. Whereas the GSE118468 (testing) dataset of COPD includes a total of 18 samples, with 12 COPD and 6 normal samples. The GSE93777 (training) dataset of RA includes a total of 448 samples with 315 RA cases and 133 healthy controls. Whereas the GSE294225 (testing) dataset of COPD includes a total of 24 samples, with 9 RA and 15 healthy samples. Based on the sample size, the training and test datasets are chosen. Larger datasets were chosen for training and smaller ones for testing.

Differential expression analysis

One of the most important methods for determining potential genes is differential expression (DE) analysis. Using DE analysis, Differentially Expressed Genes (DEGs) can be found, which can act as potential biomarkers for the diseases. DEGs are found by comparing the disease samples and the non-disease samples. The Benjamini–Hochberg with False Discovery Rate (FDR) is used to modify p-values in this work. The R package ‘limma’ with the criteria |log2FC|>=1.0 and adj.P.Val<0.05 were applied to discover the significant DEGs in GSE76925 (COPD) and GSE93777 (RA). The genes with the values log2FC>=1 are known as upregulated genes, and the genes with the values log2FC<=−1 are known as downregulated genes, which are basically the significant DEGs found in this study. Common DEGs between the identified significant DEGs were taken for further studies.

Gene set enrichment analysis

To discover the potential gene ontology (GO) terms and pathways connected to the common DEGs, we applied the gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA). The GSEA analysis aids in comprehending the biological processes and functions related to the genes. The R package “clusterProfiler” was utilized via R programming to obtain the key GO terms under Biological Process (BP), Molecular Function (MF), Cellular Component, and the key pathways related to the common DEGs. To find the significant GO and pathway terms, a criterion of adjusted P value < 0.05 was taken into consideration.

Protein–protein interaction analysis

The protein–protein interaction (PPI) analysis is used to obtain the potential hub genes among the common genes. Hub genes are the most important genes that are densely connected to one another. The PPI analysis was carried out using the R packages “STRINGdb” and “igraph.” Later, Cytoscape software (https://cytoscape.org/) was used to visualize the PPI network for better representation. For improved biological network visualization and analysis, especially in systems biology and genomics research, Cytoscape is a reliable open-source software platform.27 Later, five topological algorithms named MCC, Degree, MNC, EPC, and Bottleneck via R programming are used for finding the potential hub genes in the network.

Machine learning analysis

We also ran a number of machine learning algorithms on the common genes, including Boruta, DT (Decision Tree), ElasticNet, GA (Genetic Algorithm), InfoGain, Lasso, LightGBM, Pearson, RF (Random Forest), RR (Ridge Regression), SVM-RFE, XGBoost, NB (Naive Bayes), and MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) via R programming to find the potential genes from ML analyses. These machine learning (ML) methods are used for feature selection in this research; this is done by estimating the relevance of genes to diseases. Boruta method identifies important features by comparing original genes with randomized shadow features by considering a Random Forest framework.28 In this context, the DT method can rank genes based on information gain,29 whereas ElasticNet combines L1 (LASSO) and L2 (Ridge) regularization for feature selection.30 Moreover, GA performs an evolutionary search to identify informative gene subsets, and InfoGain can rank genes.31,32 LightGBM and XGBoost may provide feature importance using gradient-boosting frameworks,33,34 while RF uses decision trees to derive feature rankings.35 Furthermore, Pearson Correlation uses the relationship between gene expression and disease, while SVM-RFE recursively eliminates less important features based on the weights of SVM.36 NB uses a probabilistic approach,37 whereas MLP captures complex and nonlinear gene interactions.38

ROC curve analysis

The ROC (Receiver Operating Characteristic) curve analysis was done on both the RA and COPD training and test datasets to examine the expression values of the key candidate genes. A gene’s ROC curve shows how well it distinguishes between healthy and disease samples. A higher AUC (Area Under the Curve) in ROC suggests that a gene is probably a better biomarker with greater predictive power. The pROC package through R programming and the logistic regression model were used to get the accuracy of the classification model.

Nomogram analysis

Using the rms package in R, a diagnostic nomogram model has been created to forecast the likelihood of key candidate genes. “Points” are used in the nomogram to represent model coefficients. In a nomogram, the length of each scale line represents the predictor’s relative significance. The nomogram model’s predictive power was evaluated using a calibration curve. A model that is correctly calibrated will follow the diagonal path y = x. If the slope is above the straight line, the risk is underestimated; if it is below the diagonal, the risk is overstated. Lastly, the model’s practical utility was evaluated using decision curve analysis (DCA).

Gene expression analysis

We conducted gene expression analysis on the key candidate genes using both the train and test datasets to compare the expression values in both normal and disease samples. The expression plot’s findings are visualized using the R programming library package ggplot2. This plot shows the significance of each gene in disease progression.

TFs-gene-mirna and drug interaction

Finally, we suggested the TFs-gene-miRNA and Gene–Drug interaction on the key potential biomarkers. The TFs-gene-miRNA network suggests the candidate transcription factors (TFs) and microRNAs (miRNAs) connected to the key potential biomarkers, and the Gene–Drug interaction suggests the candidate drugs related to the key potential biomarkers. TFs-gene-mirRNA were predicted using the Network Analyst platform (www.networkanalyst.ca). The NetworkAnalyst platform is a web tool designed to use biological network-based methodologies for thorough graphical analysis of genomics, gene regulation, and other genomics data. Related drug molecules were also suggested using the EnrichR web tool. With over 30 gene-set libraries, an alternative ranking system for enriched phrases, and a range of interactive visualization tools to display enrichment results, EnrichR is a comprehensive web-based program.39

Results

Identification of common differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

We found 618 significant DEGs in the GSE76925 (COPD), comprising 564 down-regulated and 54 up-regulated genes, which are represented using a volcano plot in Fig. 2A. We found 1812 significant DEGs in GSE93777 (RA), including 720 down-regulated and 1092 up-regulated genes, which are represented using a volcano plot in Fig. 2B. In the volcano plot, the blue dot represents the down-regulated genes, the red dot represents the up-regulated genes, and the black dot represents the non-significant genes. After taking the common of the 618 DEGs found in GSE76925 (COPD) and 1812 DEGs in GSE93777 (RA), we found 90 common DEGs between RA and COPD. A Venn diagram is used to represent the 90 common DEGs found in both the GSE76925 (COPD) and the GSE93777 (RA) (Fig. 2C).

Identification of GO terms and pathways

We conducted the gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) to discover potential GO and pathway terms associated with 90 common DEGs. The GO terms were derived from three different categories: cellular component (CC), molecular function (MF), and biological process (BP). The key potential CC terms are nuclear speck and spliceosomal complex. The key potential BP terms are RNA splicing, regulation of RNA splicing, and positive regulation of cytokine production. The key potential MF terms are NADP+ nucleosidase activity and NAD+ nucleotidase, cyclic ADP-ribose generating. Fig. 3A represents the key potential GO terms related to the common DEGs, and Fig. 3B represents the key potential pathways related to the common DEGs based on p-values.

Identification of hub genes

We conducted the protein–protein interaction analysis to discover the potential hub genes that are densely connected to each other. Fig. 4 shows the protein–protein interaction PPI network generated from the common DEGs. The PPI network demonstrates several protein interactions with the genes. Further, five topological algorithms, namely MCC, MNC, Degree, EPC, and Bottleneck, are applied on the PPI network to get the top candidate hub genes. Fig. 5 represents all five topological algorithms’ results. Finally, the top 7 genes named GTF2H1, YY1, RBBP6, ETS1, CREB5, EP300, and TP53 are found by taking the common of the five topological algorithms’ results. Fig. 6 represents the common hub genes found from the topological analysis.

Identification of ML genes

We applied several ML algorithms, including InfoGain, SVM-RFE, RF, Lasso, Boruta, DT, ElasticNet, GA, LightGBM, MLPerceptron, Pearson, Ridge, XGBoost, and Naive Bayes, on the common DEGs to identify the potential ML genes. Each ML model identifies a set of potential genes from the 90 common DEGs with a good model accuracy. Among all the ML models, ElasticNet shows the highest accuracy at 91.52%. Table 3 shows the accuracy of different ML models. For further analysis, we took the potential genes selected by the Elasticnet model, as it shows the highest accuracy among all ML models. Fig. 7 represents the potential genes selected by the Elasticnet model.

Identification of final potential biomarkers

To get the final potential biomarkers (genes), we took the intersection of bioinformatics and ML results. Finally, we found four candidate genes named ETS1, YY1, GTF2H1, and CREB5 by taking the intersection. Fig. 8 represents the intersected genes found from potential hub genes and ML genes.

Validation of potential diagnostic value and gene expression levels

We validated the expression levels of the four candidate genes found from intersecting the ML and hub genes in the RA and COPD train and validation datasets (Fig. 9). In the training set (GSE76925, GSE93777), GTF2H1 and CREB5 show increasing expression in the disease set compared to ETS1 and YY1. In the validation datasets (GSE118468, GSE294225), CREB5, ETS1, and YY1 show increasing expression in the disease set compared to GTF2H1. The ROC analysis of the COPD training set (GSE76925) revealed that the all four candidate genes’ AUC regions were higher than 0.5, with CREB5 exhibiting the highest AUC of 0.723 (Fig. 10A). The RA training set (GSE93777) ROC analysis revealed that all four candidate genes AUC regions were more than 0.6, with GTF2H1 exhibiting the highest AUC of 0.78 (Fig. 10B). The COPD validation set (GSE118468) ROC analysis revealed that all four candidate genes’ AUC regions were higher than 0.5, with CREB5 exhibiting the highest AUC of 0.811 (Fig. 10C). The COPD validation set (GSE294225) ROC analysis revealed that all four candidate genes’ AUC regions were higher than 0.5, with GTF2H1 exhibiting the highest AUC of 0.815 (Fig. 10D). Overall, all four candidate genes demonstrate a strong association with RA and COPD.

Results of nomogram analysis

In the nomogram model, higher expression levels of ETS1, YY1, GTF2H1, and CREB5 were linked to higher total points in the nomogram, suggesting that they positively contribute to the risk of illness (Fig. 11A). In the nomogram model, total points is set by taking the weighted sum of all the predictor genes’ individual points according to their regression coefficient. In the calibration curve (Fig. 11B), the ideal reference line (dashed line) is closely followed by the bias-corrected calibration line (solid black line), which was produced using 1000 bootstrap resamples. This shows a strong concordance between the observed and anticipated illness probability. The model has low variation throughout the whole probability range, indicating superior calibration performance. Additionally, the apparent curve (dotted line) stayed close to the ideal line, indicating that there is no significant overfitting in the model. The model’s reliability is supported by the confidence band (light gray), which constantly overlaps the ideal line despite being broader at the ends due to fewer samples. The predicted probability differed from the true probabilities by an average of just 1.8%, according to the overall mean absolute error of 0.018. This tiny error indicates the model’s ability to produce precise and clinically significant risk assessments. Overall, the calibration analysis shows that the generated nomogram has good model stability, high predictive accuracy, and great agreement between expected and actual results, supporting its use for individualized disease risk assessment.

Discussion

RA and COPD have been the subjects of a great number of separate studies,[22], [23], [24],26,40 but there is a lack of research on the two diseases together. In this study, we have performed combined research on RA and COPD to find the shared potential biomarkers between them using bioinformatics and machine learning approaches. Using the COPD training dataset (GSE76925) and the RA training dataset (GSE93777), we discovered 90 common DEGs between them. We have carried out further bioinformatics and ML analysis on these common DEGs to find the key potential biomarkers among these common DEGs.

The GSEA analysis was carried out on the common DEGs to find the potential pathways and GO terms under three categories: Biological Process (BP), Molecular Function (MF), and Cellular Component (CC). By providing fresh and validating information on the practical applications of the query genes set in molecular processes like binding or catalysis, as well as biological processes like development, differentiation, diseases, and pathways, the GSEA analysis results add value to GeneAnalytics.41 The nuclear speck and spliceosomal complex are the key potential GO terms under the cellular component category. RNA splicing, regulation of RNA Splicing, and positive regulation of cytokine production are the key potential GO terms under the biological process category. NADP+ nucleosidase activity and NAD+ nucleotidase, cyclic ADP-ribose generating are the key potential GO terms under molecular function found in this study.

To identify the potential hub genes, the shared DEGs were subjected to the PPI analysis. To discover the potential hub genes in the PPI network, five topological techniques are used: MCC, MNC, Degree, EPC, and Bottleneck. Ultimately, seven important genes were identified by combining the output of the five topological techniques. To find the possible ML genes, we used a number of ML algorithms on the common DEGs, such as InfoGain, SVM-RFE, RF, Lasso, Boruta, DT, ElasticNet, GA, LightGBM, MLPerceptron, Pearson, Ridge, XGBoost, and Naive Bayes. ElasticNet exhibits the highest accuracy of 91.52% among them, reflecting its better ability to balance normalization and feature selection by employing the combined L1 and L2 penalty framework, which is especially useful in high-dimensional genomic datasets where nonlinearity among gene features is frequent. Although ElasticNet’s exceptional predictive performance led to its selection as the final model, it is crucial to acknowledge that cross-validation techniques were used during model training to reduce the risk of overfitting and guarantee consistent and generalized performance across various data partitions. However, future validation in broader, independent, and clinically well-defined cohorts will be necessary to further confirm model reliability, as the comparatively small sample sizes of the publicly available microarray datasets may impose certain limitations on the ML model’s wider generalizability. Four high-confidence potential biomarkers—ETS1, YY1, GTF2H1, and CREB5—were found by connecting the seven hub genes found by PPI network analysis with the ElasticNet-derived ML candidate genes. By ensuring that the resulting biomarkers were supported by solid and complementary evidence from both network topology and a data-driven feature selection perspective, this convergent bioinformatics and ML approach greatly increased the biological reliability and diagnostic significance of the identified candidates for both RA and COPD. The ETS1 gene directly regulates the expression of chemokine and cytokine genes in numerous cellular settings and may also regulate lymphoid cell development, survival, and proliferation.42 The YY1 (Yin Yang 1) gene is involved in the activation or repression of a promoter by histone acetyltransferases and deacetylases.43 YY1 plays dual roles that depend on the setting in a variety of malignancies. On the one hand, it can decrease tumor-suppressor genes (like APC) by communicating directly and aiding the ubiquitination and instability of p53. At the same time, it can activate pathways like Wnt and c-Myc that promote cell survival and proliferation. Overexpression and improper regulation of YY1 have also been linked to higher oncogenic potential, improved cell growth, invasion, and metastasis, and worse clinical outcomes in breast, colon, prostate, and endometrial carcinomas.44 GTF2H1 (General transcription factor IIH subunit 1) is a protein-encoding gene that positively regulates DNA-templated transcription and transcription initiation at the RNA polymerase II promoter.45 CREB5 (CAMP Responsive Element Binding Protein 5) is a protein-coding gene that controls important gene networks in a variety of pathogenic contexts. CREB5 promotes cell invasion, migration, proliferation, and metastasis, all of which contribute to oncogenic characteristics. Mechanistically, CREB5 participates in regulatory networks influenced by non-coding RNAs and activates signaling pathways like the hepatocyte growth factor–MET cascade to increase the production of pro-metastatic effectors.46 We validate these potential biomarkers by performing ROC curve analysis and Nomogram analysis. Both analyses showed that these four genes have a strong association in RA and COPD. Finally, we suggested the candidate TFs, miRNAs, and Drug molecules related to the four potential biomarkers found in this study. MYC, CREBBP, EP300, E2F1, and EGR1 are found as the candidate transcription factors related to the four potential biomarkers. The MYC is a transcription factor that is frequently dysregulated in various cancers and is essential for cell cycle, proliferation, metabolism, and apoptosis.47 CREB-binding protein, also known as CREBBP, is essential for embryonic development, growth regulation, and homeostasis.48 It is regarded as an oncogene, or tumor suppressor, because of its crucial function in controlling cell division, growth, migration, and death.49 EP300 is an essential transcription factor that plays a key part in controlling gene expression via chromatin remodeling.50 This enzyme is crucial for controlling cell division and proliferation, which causes cells to develop and take on specific functions.51 The transcription factor E2F1 performs a vital part in the regulation of the cell cycle and the activity of tumor suppressor proteins and transforming small DNA tumor viruses.52 EGR1’s functions in the body include controlling the ratio of hematopoietic stem cells, causing some cardiovascular disorders, and impacting angiogenesis, migration, and cell proliferation, and affects how cancer develops.53 hsa-miR-181b, hsa-miR-130a, hsa-miR-548c-3p, hsa-miR-129-5p, hsa-miR-519b-3p, hsa-miR-1, hsa-miR-429, hsa-miR-338-5p, hsa-miR-200b, hsa-miR-410, and hsa-miR-568 are the candidate miRNAs related to the four potential biomarkers found in this study. hsa-miR-130a has been found to be among the miRNAs that are concentrated in melanoma cell extracellular vesicles (EVs) and are engaged in the post-transcriptional control of multicellular organism gene expression by influencing mRNA translation and stability.54,55 Numerous tumor processes also have been linked to hsa-miR-548c-3p.56 hsa-miR-1 is a muscle-specific microRNA that is mainly linked to the growth of skeletal muscle in animals. Additionally, this microRNA controls embryonic angiogenesis and heart contraction.57 hsa-miR-429 has been found to regulate cellular functions in a number of carcinomas, including those of the breast, prostate, and endometrial tissues.58 It has been demonstrated that exposure to tobacco carcinogens epigenetically silences hsa-miR-200b in lung epithelial cells and influences EMT phenotypic alterations.59 hsa-miR-410 has been linked to the control of apoptosis in human pulmonary artery endothelial cells (hPAECs). Furthermore, hsa-miR-410 has been found associated with the metabolism of glucose.60 This link to both glucose metabolism and apoptosis implies that it play a variety of roles in cellular processes and may play a major part in the control of disease states and cellular homeostasis. hydrogen peroxide CTD 00006118, Decitabine CTD 00000750, lycorine PC3 UP, Rosuvastatin CTD 00003899, salsolidin PC3 UP, Aluminum sulfate CTD 00001900, sisomicin HL60 DOWN, amprolium PC3 UP, nicotinic acid PC3 DOWN and Indeno[1,2,3-cd]pyrene CTD 00001895 are the top 10 candidate drugs found in this study related to the four potential biomarkers based on p-values. However, in order to establish the biological or therapeutic significance of the candidate drugs, transcription factors and microRNAs requires additional experimental validation, as these connections were produced through bioinformatics databases and network-based analysis.

Overall, our study has a number of benefits. Using datasets from publicly available databases, we are the first to screen for shared potential biomarkers between RA and COPD. Previous studies have focused only on biomarker identification on each disease separately. Mu, K.L. et al.,16 in their investigation found diagnostic biomarkers only specific to RA. Z. Zhu et al. 25 in their study identified biomarkers only related to COPD. The investigation of shared molecular biomarkers between RA and COPD remains unexplored. Second, we perform several bioinformatics and ML analyses to get more accurate results. Whereas, most of the studies performed very limited bioinformatics and ML analyses. In order to identify potential biomarkers, Sun, Z. et al. 22 only used three ML models (RF, SVM, and Lasso). Liang, Q. et al. 23 in their investigation only employed two ML models (RF and Lasso) to identify possible biomarkers. Sun, Z. et al. 24 in their study use a number of ML techniques, but perform relatively few bioinformatics analyses. We also validate the results by performing ROC and nomogram analyses. To better understand our findings, a comparison chart of the present study with previous studies is shown in Table 5.

Despite the study’s promising outcomes, the results are still required to undergo thorough experimental validation, such as in vitro and in vivo investigations, to validate the functional roles of ETS1, YY1, CREBP5, and GTF2H1 in the pathophysiology of RA and COPD. In the future, a more thorough and systems-level understanding of the molecular interaction between COPD and RA may be possible through the integration of multi-omics data, such as genomics, proteomics, metabolomics, and epigenomics.

Conclusions

In summary, this study combines bioinformatics and ML analyses to find the shared potential biomarkers between RA and COPD. We performed several bioinformatics analyses, like Differential Expression (DE) analysis, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA), and protein–protein Interaction (PPI) analysis. From the differential expression analysis, we find the significant DEGs of RA and COPD, from the GSEA analysis, we find the potential GO and pathway terms, and from the PPI analysis, we get the candidate hub genes related to RA and COPD. We also applied several ML analyses, including InfoGain, SVM-RFE, RF, Lasso, Boruta, DT, ElasticNet, GA, LightGBM, MLPerceptron, Pearson, Ridge, XGBoost, and Naive Bayes, to identify the potential ML genes. Among all ML models, Elasticnet shows the highest accuracy of 91.52%. By taking the common of the bioinformatics and ML results, a final set of four candidate genes, ETS1, YY1, GTF2H1, and CREB5, has been found for this study. This work also suggests potential Tfs, miRNAs, and small-molecule drugs for medical applications. Moreover, the computationally identified biomarkers in this study may offer initial leads for targeted therapy exploration and future clinical investigations, but extensive clinical trials and rigorous experimental validation are required before these outcomes can be interpreted into any clinically meaningful assessment or therapeutic uses for COPD and RA.
