On a conjecture on Romanoff type sumsets
Yuchen Ding and Liangxun Li

Abstract
. In this note, we generalize a 1950 result of P. Erdős on upper bounds of k k -th moment of Romanoff type representation functions. As an application, we give a conditional proof of a recent conjecture of Y.-G. Chen on Romanoff type sumsets under the assumption of the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11P32, 11A41, 11N36

1.
Introduction

Let

\mathcal{R}:=\{r_{1}<r_{2}<\cdots\}

be the set of odd integers which can be written as the sum of a prime and a power of two.
Let
\mathbb{N}_{\text{odd}}
be the set of odd numbers and

\mathcal{N}=\mathbb{N}_{\text{odd}}\setminus\mathcal{R}.

The old letters between Euler and Goldbach
[
19
]
as well as de Polignac’s conjecture
[
28
,
29
]
inspired Romanoff
[
30
]
to prove that there is an absolute constant
c_{1}>0
such that

\mathcal{R}(x)>c_{1}x

for all sufficiently large
x
, where
\mathcal{R}(x)
is the number of elements of
\mathcal{R}
not exceeding
x
.
Later, van der Corput
[
6
]
proved that there is an absolute constant
c_{2}>0
such that

\mathcal{N}(x)>c_{2}x

for all sufficiently large
x
.
Introducing concepts of the covering congruence systems Erdős
[
15
]
showed that
\mathcal{N}
contains an arithmetic progression. Erdős
[
17
]
asked whether the set
\mathcal{N}
is composed by the union of an arithmetic progression and a zero density set. Very recently, Chen’s remarkably interesting result
[
2
]
shows that the set
\mathcal{N}
is not composed by the union of a finite number of arithmetic progressions and a zero density set, thus answering negatively Erdős’ problem in a rather satisfactory way.

Erdős’ result above implies that the gaps between the elements of
\mathcal{N}
are bounded. In
[
1
]
, Chen proved that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}(r_{n+1}-r_{n})\geq 8.

Answering in the affirmative a 1977 problem of Erdős
[
16
, Page 51]
, very recently Elsholtz, Planitzer and Schlage-Puchta
[
13
]
(via private communications) proved that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}(r_{n+1}-r_{n})=\infty.

Actually, they gave a quantitative form involving the maximal order of
r_{n+1}-r_{n}
. The result of Elsholtz, Planitzer and Schlage-Puchta is rather surprising since the predicted lower density of
\mathcal{R}
is pretty high approximately
0.437...
, thanks to the article of Del Corso, Corso, Dvornicich and Romani
[
7
]
. At present, the best record of the lower density of
\mathcal{R}
is
0.110114
given by Elsholtz and Schlage-Puchta
[
14
]
.

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of natural numbers and
\mathbb{P}
the set of primes.
Improving the lower bound of Yang and Chen
[
33
]
, the first named author
[
9
]
proved

\#\left\{n\leq x:n=p+2^{m_{1}^{2}}+2^{m_{2}^{2}},m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{N},p\in\mathbb{P}\right\}\gg x.

Let
r_{1},r_{2},\cdots,r_{t}
be
t
given positive integers. Chen and Xu
[
4
]
proved that

\#\left\{n\leq x:n=p+2^{m_{1}^{r_{1}}}+\cdots+2^{m_{t}^{r_{t}}},m_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq r,p\in\mathbb{P}\right\}\gg x,

provided that
r_{1}^{-1}+\cdots+r_{t}^{-1}\geq 1
. Their result was extended by Zhai and the first named author
[
11
]
to real cases which means that all of
r_{i}
are allowed to be real numbers. In the extended real case, all the summands
2^{m_{i}^{r_{i}}}
in Chen-Xu’s result should be naturally adjusted to
2^{\lfloor m_{i}^{r_{i}}\rfloor}
. A shorter interval version of this representation was considered by Verwee and the first named author very recently
[
10
]
. The representations of odd integers of the form
p+2^{m_{1}^{2}}+2^{m_{2}^{2}}
were further investigated by Xu and Chen in two subsequent articles
[
31
,
32
]
.

Recently, in
[
3
]
Chen obtained some new results involving odd numbers of the representations

p+2^{m_{1}^{r_{1}}}+\cdots+2^{m_{t}^{r_{t}}}.

He proved that almost all odd integers of this form have unique representations, provided that
r_{1}^{-1}+\cdots+r_{t}^{-1}<1
. This phenomenon is not isolated. Actually, previously Chen
[
5
]
proved that almost all odd numbers of the form
p+2^{q}
have unique representations, where
p
and
q
are both primes.
From now on, let
r_{1},\cdots,r_{t}
be positive numbers with
r_{i}\geq 1~(1\leq i\leq t)
and
r_{1}^{-1}+\cdots+r_{t}^{-1}\geq 1
. Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\left\{2^{\left\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\right\rfloor}+\cdots+2^{\left\lfloor m_{t}^{r_{t}}\right\rfloor}:m_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq t\right\}

(1.1)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}=\mathbb{P}+\mathcal{A}:=\big\{p+a:p\in\mathbb{P},a\in\mathcal{A}\big\}.

(1.2)

Chen
[
3
, Conjecture 1.7]
made the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(Chen)
.

If
r_{1},\cdots,r_{t}
are positive integers with
r_{1}^{-1}+\cdots+r_{t}^{-1}\geq 1
, then
the set
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}\cap(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}+2)
has a positive lower asymptotic density, where

\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}+2=\big\{n+2:n\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}\big\}.

The purpose of this note is to provide a conditional proof of Conjecture
1.1
under the assumption of a uniform Hardy-Littlewood conjecture (see e.g.,
[
23
]
).

Conjecture 1.2

(Weak uniform Hardy-Littlewood Conjecture)
.

There is a fixed
\delta>0
such that the inequality

\#\big\{p\leq x:p,p+h\in\mathbb{P}\big\}\gg_{\delta}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}

holds uniformly for any even
h\leq x^{\delta}
, provided that
x
is sufficiently large.

Remark 1.1
.

The Hardy-Littlewood Conjecture on generalized twin primes was initially posed by Hardy and Littlewood
[
21
]
, which states for even
h

\#\big\{p\leq x:p,p+h\in\mathbb{P}\big\}\sim\mathfrak{S}_{h}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}

as
x\to\infty
, where

\mathfrak{S}_{h}:=\prod_{p\geq 3\atop p\nmid h}\Big(1-\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2}}\Big)\prod_{p\geq 2\atop p\mid h}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{-1},

which obeys the uniform lower bound
\mathfrak{S}_{h}\geq\mathfrak{S}_{2}\gg 1.

For the proof of our theorem, instead of the original asymptotic formula of generalized twin primes we need this weak uniform version of the Hardy-Littlewood Conjecture which only admits a lower bound. The uniformity of
h\leq x^{\delta}
for some small
\delta>0
is not too far-fetched. In fact, Montgomery and Soundararajan
[
26
]
investigated Cramér’s conjecture
[
8
]
on prime gaps assuming a variant of the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture under the uniformity of
h\leq x^{1-\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
. An even stronger conjecture was assumed in an article of Lemke Olivera and Soundararajan
[
23
]
. Maynard’s remarkable results on prime tuples
[
24
,
25
]
appear not to be applicable directly in this context (but we are not sure whether the method leading to the results therein could be used here). Actually, we do not even know how to prove unconditionally that the set
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}\cap(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}+2)
contains infinitely many odd numbers. For the particular case
\mathcal{A}=\big\{2^{m}:m\in\mathbb{N}\big\}
, it is clear that there are
\gg x/\log x
odd numbers
n=p+2
such that
n+2=p+2^{2}
are also the sums of a prime and a power of two.

We now state our main result as the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
r_{1},\cdots,r_{t}
are positive numbers with
r_{i}\geq 1~(1\leq i\leq t)
and

r_{1}^{-1}+\cdots+r_{t}^{-1}\geq 1.

Then
the set
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}\cap(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}+2)
has a positive lower asymptotic density under the assumption of Conjecture
1.2
.

It is worth emphasizing that we do not require
r
’s in Theorem
1.1
to be integers which covers a wider range than the original conjecture.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The proof of Theorem
1.1
, among other things, is based on an extended high moments of representation functions which generalizes a 1950 result of Erdős
[
15
]
.

For odd integer
n
, let

f(n)=\#\left\{(p,m)\in\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{N}:n=p+2^{m}\right\}.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the prime number theorem, Romanoff’s theorem is equivalent to

\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)^{2}\ll x.

Erdős
[
15
]
then took a further step, and established

\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)^{k}\ll_{k}x

for any fixed integer
k
. Now, for odd integer
n
let

f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)=\#\left\{(p,a)\in\mathbb{P}\times\mathcal{A}:n=p+a\right\},

where the set
\mathcal{A}
is defined by (
1.1
). Motivated by Erdős, we will prove the following.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any fixed positive integer
k
, we have

\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)^{k}\ll x(\log x)^{k\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}-1\right)},

where the implied constant depends at most on
\mathcal{A}
and
k
.

The arguments in
[
4
,
11
]
could essentially lead to the proof of Theorem
2.2
with
k=2
. But, for
k=2
these two articles are slightly different both in their forms of presentations and in their proofs. We believe that the generality and concise form of Theorem
2.2
have the potential for further applications in Romanoff type sumsets.

We postpone the proof of Theorem
2.2
to the last section, and give first the proof of Theorem
1.1
by Conjecture
1.2
and Theorem
2.2
.
Let

\mathcal{W}_{r}=\{j:r_{j}=r,1\leq j\leq t\},\ w_{r}=|\mathcal{W}_{r}|,\ \text{and}~w=\prod_{r=1}^{\infty}w_{r}!.

Chen
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
proved that

\mathcal{A}(x)\sim\frac{1}{w}\left(\frac{\log x}{\log 2}\right)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}},

provided that
r_{1},\cdots,r_{t}
are positive integers. For our purpose, a lower bound of
\mathcal{A}(x)
which allows
r
’s to be positive numbers will be applicable enough.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
r_{1},\cdots,r_{t}
be positive number. Then for sufficiently large
x
we have

\mathcal{A}(x)\gg(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}},

where the implied constant depends only on
r_{1},\cdots,r_{t}
.

Proof.

For any
1\leq i\leq t
, we consider the number of integers
m_{i}
such that

\displaystyle x^{\frac{i-1}{t+1}}\leq 2^{\left\lfloor m_{i}^{r_{i}}\right\rfloor}<x^{\frac{i}{t+1}}.

(2.1)

It is clear that the number of such
m_{i}
is

\gg(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{i}}}.

Moreover, for integers
m_{i}
in the range (
2.1
) it is easy to see that all the sums below

2^{\left\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\right\rfloor}+\cdots+2^{\left\lfloor m_{t}^{r_{t}}\right\rfloor}

are different because of the unique binary representation of integers. Therefore,

\mathcal{A}(x)\gg\prod_{1\leq i\leq t}(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{i}}}=(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}},

proving our lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A},2}=\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}\cap(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}+2)
for simplification. Using twice the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)f_{\mathcal{A}}(n+2)\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)f_{\mathcal{A}}(n+2)\geq 1\end{subarray}}1^{2}\Big)\Big(\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)^{2}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n+2)^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A},2}(x)\sqrt{\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)^{4}}\sqrt{\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n+2)^{4}}.

(2.2)

By Theorem
2.2
, we know

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)^{4}\ll x(\log x)^{4\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}-1\right)}

(2.3)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n+2)^{4}\leq\sum_{n\leq 2x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)^{4}\ll x(\log x)^{4\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}-1\right)}.

(2.4)

Inserting (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) into (
2
), we get

\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A},2}(x)\gg\frac{\Big(\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)f_{\mathcal{A}}(n+2)\Big)^{2}}{x(\log x)^{4\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}-1\right)}}.

Thus, to prove our theorem it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)f_{\mathcal{A}}(n+2)\gg x(\log x)^{2\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}-1\right)}.

(2.5)

From now on, the symbols
p
’s will always be primes. Clearly,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)f_{\mathcal{A}}(n+2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=p_{1}+a_{1}\\
n+2=p_{2}+a_{2}\\
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{p_{1}+a_{1}=p_{2}+a_{2}-2\leq x}1

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}\\
a_{2}<a_{1}\leq x^{\delta}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}\leq\frac{x}{2}\\
p_{2}=p_{1}+a_{1}-a_{2}+2\end{subarray}}1

where
\delta
is the one occurred in Conjecture
1.2
. By Conjecture
1.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}\leq\frac{x}{2}\\
p_{2}=p_{1}+a_{1}-a_{2}+2\end{subarray}}1\gg\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}.

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)f_{\mathcal{A}}(n+2)\gg\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathcal{A}\\
a_{2}<a_{1}\leq x^{\delta}\end{subarray}}1=\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\binom{\mathcal{A}\left(x^{\delta}\right)}{2}.

By Lemma
2.1
we know that

\mathcal{A}\left(x^{\delta}\right)\gg(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}},

from which we deduce that

\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)f_{\mathcal{A}}(n+2)\gg x(\log x)^{2\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}-1\right)},

proving (
2.5
).
∎

3.
k
-th moment of Romanoff type representation functions

In this section, we offer the proof of Theorem
2.2
.
We need a few more lemmas. The first one is a standard sieve result, see e.g.,
[
20
, Theorem 2.4, p. 76]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
g
be a natural number, and let
q_{i},b_{i}~(i=1,2,\cdots,g)
be integers satisfying

E:=\prod_{1\leq i\leq g}q_{i}\prod_{1\leq h<s\leq g}\left(q_{h}b_{s}-q_{s}b_{h}\right)\neq 0.

Let
\rho(p)
be the number of solutions in
n
modulo
p
of

\prod_{1\leq i\leq g}\big(q_{i}n+b_{i}\big)\equiv 0\ ({\rm{mod}}\ p).

Then

\#\big\{p\leq y:q_{i}p+b_{i}\in\mathbb{P},1\leq i\leq g\big\}\ll\prod_{p|E}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\rho(p)-g}\prod_{p|b_{1}\cdots b_{g}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\frac{y}{(\log y)^{g+1}},

where the implied constant depends at most on
g
.

Extending an old result of Erdős and Turán
[
18
]
as well a result of Erdős
[
15
, Eq. (14)]
, we give the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any odd integer
d
, let
e_{2}(d)
be the least positive integer
m
such that
2^{m}\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)
. Let
B
and
\varepsilon
be two given positive numbers. Then

\sum_{d=1,\ 2\nmid d}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}B^{\omega(d)}}{d\big(e_{2}(d)\big)^{\varepsilon}}<\infty.

Proof.

For
k=e_{2}(d)
, by the definition we have
2^{k}\equiv 1\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{d=1,\ 2\nmid d}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}B^{\omega(d)}}{d\big(e_{2}(d)\big)^{\varepsilon}}=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{\varepsilon}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1\\
e_{2}(d)=k\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}B^{\omega(d)}}{d}.

(3.1)

It is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
27
]
) that there is an absolute constant
c_{0}>e^{e}
such that

\omega(d)<c_{0}\log d,

provided that
d>1
. For any
t\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle g(t):=\sum_{k\leq t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1\\
e_{2}(d)=k\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}B^{\omega(d)}}{d}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1\\
d|\prod_{k\leq t}\left(2^{k}-1\right)\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}B^{\omega(d)}}{d}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq 2^{t^{2}}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}B^{\omega(d)}}{d}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{p\leq c_{0}t^{2}\log 2}\left(1+\frac{B}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(\sum_{p\leq c_{0}t^{2}\log 2}\log\Big(1+\frac{B}{p}\Big)\bigg)

\displaystyle<\exp\Big(B\sum_{p\leq c_{0}t^{2}\log 2}1/p\Big).

(3.2)

By Mertens formula (see, e.g.,
[
27
, Theorem 2.7]
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq c_{0}t^{2}\log 2}\frac{1}{p}=\log\log(c_{0}t^{2}\log 2)+\gamma+O(1/\log k)<\widetilde{c_{0}}\log\log t,

(3.3)

where
\gamma
is the Meissel–Mertens constant and
\widetilde{c_{0}}
is an absolute constant.
Combining (
3
) with (
3.3
), for any
t\geq 2
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ d|2^{k}-1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}B^{\omega(d)}}{d}\leq(\log t)^{B_{1}},

(3.4)

where
B_{1}=B\widetilde{c_{0}}
. Hence, by (
3.1
), (
3.4
) and partial summations we have

\displaystyle\sum_{d=1,\ 2\nmid d}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}B^{\omega(d)}}{d\big(e_{2}(d)\big)^{\varepsilon}}=\varepsilon\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{g(t)}{t^{1+\varepsilon}}{\rm d}t<\infty,

proving our lemma.
∎

The following technical lemma is quoted from
[
11
, inequality between (2.16) and (2.17), p. 171]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(\kappa,\lambda)
be an exponent pair and
d
an odd integer. Suppose that
r_{1}>1
is not an integer. Then

\max_{f}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}\equiv f\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\\
m_{1}\leq(\log 2x/\log 2)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\end{subarray}}1\ll\frac{(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}}{e_{2}(d)}+(\log x)^{\frac{r_{1}\kappa+\lambda}{r_{1}(1+\kappa)}}e_{2}(d)^{-\frac{\kappa}{1+\kappa}}+e_{2}(d)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}},

where the implied constant depends at most on
r_{1}
.

Let
\mu(d)
be the Möbius function,
\omega(d)
the number of different prime factors of
d
, and
P^{+}(d)
the largest prime factor of
d
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
k>0
be a given number. Suppose that
r_{1}>1
is not an integer. Then we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ 2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}}{d}\max_{f}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}\equiv f\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\\
m_{1}\leq(\log 2x/\log 2)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\end{subarray}}1\ll(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}},

provided that
x
is sufficiently large, where the implied constant depends at most on
r_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be the set of
d
such that
2\nmid d
and

\max_{f}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}\equiv f\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\\
m_{1}\leq(\log 2x/\log 2)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\end{subarray}}1\leq\frac{(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}}{(\log\log x)^{k^{3}}}.

By the Mertens estimate, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{p\leq\log x}\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{p\leq\log x}\log(1+k^{3}/p)\right)<\exp\left(k^{3}\sum_{p\leq\log x}1/p\right)\ll(\log\log x)^{k^{3}},

from which we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ d\in\mathcal{D}\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}}{d}\max_{f}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}\equiv f\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\\
m_{1}\leq(\log 2x/\log 2)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}}{(\log\log x)^{k^{3}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ d\in\mathcal{D}\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}}{d}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}}{(\log\log x)^{k^{3}}}\prod_{p\leq\log x}\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\ll(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}.

Now, for any
2\nmid d
with
d\not\in\mathcal{D}
, there is some
f_{d}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}\equiv f_{d}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\\
m_{1}\leq(\log 2x/\log 2)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\end{subarray}}1>\frac{(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}}{(\log\log x)^{k^{3}}}.

(3.5)

The congruence equation

2^{\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}\equiv f_{d}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)

implies that there is some
1\leq y_{d}<e_{2}(d)
such that

\displaystyle\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor\equiv y_{d}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ e_{2}(d)).

(3.6)

By (
3.5
), the number of
m_{1}
satisfying the congruence is more than
\frac{(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}}{(\log\log x)^{k^{3}}}
. In this situation, we are going to show

e_{2}(d)\ll(\log x)^{1-\frac{1}{r_{1}}}(\log\log x)^{k^{3}}.

In fact, let
M
be the least gap between those
m_{1}
’s with
m_{1}\geq(\log x)^{\frac{1}{2r_{1}}}
which satisfy congruence (
3.6
). Then, clearly

\frac{(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}}{(\log\log x)^{k^{3}}}\leq(\log x)^{\frac{1}{2r_{1}}}+\frac{(\log 2x/\log 2)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}}{M}+1,

which means

M\leq 2(\log\log x)^{k^{3}}.

Moreover, by the definition of
M
we have

\lfloor m_{1}+M\rfloor^{r_{1}}-\lfloor m_{1}\rfloor^{r_{1}}\geq e_{2}(d)

for any
m_{1}\geq(\log x)^{\frac{1}{2r_{1}}}
. Hence,

e_{2}(d)\leq(m_{1}+M)^{r_{1}}-m_{1}^{r_{1}}+1\ll_{r_{1}}m_{1}^{r_{1}-1}M\ll(\log x)^{1-\frac{1}{r_{1}}}(\log\log x)^{k^{3}}.

By the above analysis, we are left over to prove

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ 2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\\
e_{2}(d)\ll(\log x)^{1-r_{1}^{-1}}(\log\log x)^{k^{3}}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}}{d}\max_{f}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}\equiv f\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\\
m_{1}\leq(\log 2x/\log 2)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\end{subarray}}1\ll(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}.

(3.7)

We are in a position to use Lemma
3.4
. For any exponent pair
(\kappa,\lambda)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ 2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\\
e_{2}(d)\ll(\log x)^{1-r_{1}^{-1}}(\log\log x)^{k^{3}}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}}{d}\max_{f}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}\equiv f\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\\
m_{1}\leq(\log 2x/\log 2)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\end{subarray}}1\ll S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3},

where

S_{1}=(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ 2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}}{de_{2}(d)},

S_{2}=(\log x)^{\frac{r_{1}\kappa+\lambda}{r_{1}(1+\kappa)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ 2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}}{de_{2}(d)^{\frac{\kappa}{1+\kappa}}},

and

S_{3}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ 2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\\
e_{2}(d)\ll(\log x)^{1-r_{1}^{-1}}(\log\log x)^{k^{3}}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}e_{2}(d)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}}{d}.

By Lemma
3.3
with
B=k^{3}
and
\varepsilon=1
, clearly we have

S_{1}\ll(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}.

It is also easy to bound
S_{3}
as follows:

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle\ll\left((\log x)^{1-r_{1}^{-1}}(\log\log x)^{k^{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ 2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}}{d}

\displaystyle\leq(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}-\frac{1}{r_{1}^{2}}}(\log\log x)^{\frac{k^{3}}{r_{1}}}\prod_{p\leq\log x}\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\ll(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}-\frac{1}{r_{1}^{2}}}(\log\log x)^{\frac{k^{3}}{r_{1}}+k^{3}}

\displaystyle\ll(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}.

For the estimate of
S_{2}
, using Lemma
3.3
again we get

S_{2}\ll(\log x)^{\frac{r_{1}\kappa+\lambda}{r_{1}(1+\kappa)}}.

To complete the proof of (
3.7
), it remains to pick an exponent pair
(\kappa,\lambda)
such that

\frac{r_{1}\kappa+\lambda}{1+\kappa}<1.

For any
q\geq 2
and
Q=2^{q}
, we know from
[
22
, p. 60]
that

\left(\frac{1}{4Q-2},1-\frac{q+1}{4Q-2}\right)

is an exponent pair. We now choose
q=\lfloor r_{1}\rfloor+1
. Then

\frac{r_{1}\kappa+\lambda}{1+\kappa}<r_{1}\kappa+\lambda=\frac{r_{1}}{4Q-2}+1-\frac{q+1}{4Q-2}=1-\frac{2-(r_{1}-\lfloor r_{1}\rfloor)}{4Q-2}<1,

completing the proof of (
3.7
), and hence the whole lemma.
∎

The following technical lemma is quoted from Chen
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
N
and
r
be positive integers,
a
an integer,
K
a positive number and
\varepsilon>0
. Then

(i)
the number of solutions of
y^{r}\equiv a\ ({\rm{mod}}\ N)~(1\leq y\leq K)
is less than

c\left(KN^{-\frac{1}{r}}+N^{\varepsilon}\right),

where
c
is a positive constant depending only on
r
and
\varepsilon
;

(ii)
if
N\geq K^{r}
, the equation
y^{r}\equiv a\ ({\rm{mod}}\ N)~(1\leq y\leq K)
has at most one solution.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
k>0
be a given number. Suppose that
r_{1}
is a positive integer. Then we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ 2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}}{d}\max_{f}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{m_{1}^{r_{1}}}\equiv f\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\\
m_{1}\leq(\log 2x/\log 2)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\end{subarray}}1\ll(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}},

provided that
x
is sufficiently large, where the implied constant depends at most on
r_{1}
.

Proof.

For any given
f
, if there is at least one
m_{i}
satisfy the congruence

2^{m_{1}^{r_{1}}}\equiv f\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d),

then there exists some
1\leq a<e_{2}(d)
such that

\displaystyle m_{1}^{r_{1}}\equiv a\ ({\rm{mod}}\ e_{2}(d))

for all the solutions
m_{1}\leq(\log 2x/\log 2)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}
. By Lemma
3.6
we have

\displaystyle\max_{f}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{m_{1}^{r_{1}}}\equiv f\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\\
m_{1}\leq(\log 2x/\log 2)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\end{subarray}}1\leq c\left(\left(\frac{\log x}{e_{2}(d)}\right)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}+(\log x)^{\varepsilon}\right),

where
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrarily small and
c>0
is a constant depending on
r_{1}
and
\varepsilon
. Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ 2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\end{subarray}}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}}{d}\max_{f}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{m_{1}^{r_{1}}}\equiv f\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\\
m_{1}\leq(\log 2x/\log 2)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\ll(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ 2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}}{d\big(e_{2}(d)\big)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}}+(\log x)^{\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ 2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}}{d}.

(3.8)

By Lemma
3.3
with
B=k^{3}
and
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{r_{1}}
, the first term in the right-hand side of (
3
) is clearly
\ll(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}
. Using again the same argument as in Lemma
3.5
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ 2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}}{d}\leq\prod_{p\leq\log x}\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{p}\right)\ll(\log\log x)^{k^{3}},

giving the desired estimate of the second term in the right-hand side of (
3
).
∎

Now, we are ready to proceed with the proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

We prove the theorem by induction on
k
. First, for
k=1
by the prime number theorem we clearly have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+a\leq x\\
p\in\mathbb{P},\ a\in\mathcal{A}\end{subarray}}1\leq\Big(\sum_{p\leq x}1\Big)\Big(\sum_{a\leq x,\ a\in\mathcal{A}}1\Big)\ll\frac{x\mathcal{A}(x)}{\log x}.

By the definition (
1.1
) of
\mathcal{A}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}+\cdots+2^{\lfloor m_{t}^{r_{t}}\rfloor}\leq x\\
m_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\ 1\leq i\leq t\end{subarray}}1\leq\prod_{1\leq i\leq t}\sum_{2^{\lfloor m_{i}^{r_{i}}\rfloor}\leq x}1\ll(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}}.

(3.9)

Hence, we get

\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)\ll x(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}-1},

proving the case
k=1
.

Now, suppose that our theorem is true for all the integers
<k
. It suffices to prove the case
k
. In what follows,
p
’s will always be primes and
a
’s are the elements of
\mathcal{A}
. Expanding the
k
-th moment of the representation function we will get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)^{k}=\sum_{p_{1}+a_{1}=\cdots=p_{k}+a_{k}\leq x}1.

(3.10)

The primes above in the summation (
3.10
) can be separated into a few groups as follows:

\displaystyle p_{11}=p_{12}=\cdots=p_{1s_{1}},\ p_{21}=p_{22}=\cdots=p_{2s_{2}},\ \cdots,\ p_{\ell 1}=p_{\ell 2}=\cdots=p_{\ell s_{\ell}},

(3.11)

where
\ell\leq k
and
s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots+s_{\ell}=k
. For any
\ell<k
, the contribution of situation (
3.11
) in the sum (
3.10
) could be bounded as

\displaystyle\ell!\sum_{a_{11}<a_{21}<\cdots<a_{\ell 1}\leq x}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{\ell 1}<\cdots<p_{21}<p_{\ell 1}\\
a_{11}+p_{11}=\cdots=a_{\ell 1}+p_{\ell 1}\leq x\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\leq\ell!\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)^{\ell}

\displaystyle\ll\ell!\cdot x(\log x)^{\ell\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}-1\right)},

(3.12)

where the last inequality comes from the inductive hypothesis. In view of (
3.10
) and (
3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}f_{\mathcal{A}}(n)^{k}

\displaystyle\leq k!\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k}\leq x}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{k}<\cdots<p_{2}<p_{1}\\
a_{1}+p_{1}=\cdots=a_{k}+p_{k}\leq x\end{subarray}}1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\ell!\cdot x(\log x)^{\ell\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}-1\right)}

\displaystyle\leq k!\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k}\leq x}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{k}<\cdots<p_{2}<p_{1}\\
a_{1}+p_{1}=\cdots=a_{k}+p_{k}\leq x\end{subarray}}1+k!\cdot x(\log x)^{k\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}-1\right)},

(3.13)

where the last inequality is guaranteed by the condition
\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}\geq 1
.
Now, we see from (
3
) that to complete the proof of our theorem, it would suffice to show

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k}\leq x}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{k}<\cdots<p_{2}<p_{1}\\
a_{1}+p_{1}=\cdots=a_{k}+p_{k}\leq x\end{subarray}}1\ll x(\log x)^{k\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}-1\right)}.

(3.14)

It is clear that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{k}<\cdots<p_{2}<p_{1}\\
a_{1}+p_{1}=\cdots=a_{k}+p_{k}\leq x\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{k}\leq x\\
p_{i}=p_{k}+a_{k}-a_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq k-1\end{subarray}}1.

By Lemma
3.2
with
g=k-1
and
q_{i}=1
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{k}<\cdots<p_{2}<p_{1}\\
a_{1}+p_{1}=\cdots=a_{k}+p_{k}\leq x\end{subarray}}1\ll\prod_{p|E}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\rho(p)-k+1}\prod_{p|b_{1}\cdots b_{k-1}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\frac{x}{(\log x)^{k}},

where
b_{i}=a_{k}-a_{i}
and

E=\prod_{1\leq h<s\leq k-1}\big(b_{s}-b_{h}\big)=\prod_{1\leq h<s\leq k-1}\big(a_{h}-a_{s}\big).

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k}\leq x}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{k}<\cdots<p_{2}<p_{1}\\
a_{1}+p_{1}=\cdots=a_{k}+p_{k}\leq x\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{(\log x)^{k}}\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k}\leq x}\prod_{p|E_{k}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-k}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{(\log x)^{k}}\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k}\leq x}\prod_{p|E_{k}}\left(1+\frac{k}{p}\right),

(3.15)

where

E_{k}=Eb_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{k-1}=\prod_{1\leq h<s\leq k}\big(a_{s}-a_{h}\big).

Trivially, the right-hand side of (
3
) is
\ll x(\log x)^{k\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}-1\right)}(\log\log x)^{k}
since

\prod_{p|E_{k}}\left(1+\frac{k}{p}\right)\ll(\log\log x)^{k}.

Thus, we have to earn a saving of
(\log\log x)^{k}
factor in the estimate to fulfill our wish. Following Erdős’ idea
[
15
, Theorem 2]
, we employ the inequality of the geometric and power means

x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{m}\leq\frac{x_{1}^{m}+x_{2}^{m}+\cdots+x_{m}^{m}}{m}

to our case, and then we will get

\displaystyle\prod_{p|E_{k}}\left(1+\frac{k}{p}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\binom{k}{2}}\sum_{1\leq h<s\leq k}\prod_{p|(a_{s}-a_{h})}\left(1+\frac{k}{p}\right)^{\binom{k}{2}}\ll\sum_{1\leq h<s\leq k}\prod_{p|(a_{s}-a_{h})}\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{p}\right),

(3.16)

where the implied constant depends on
k
. Taking (
3.16
) into (
3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k}\leq x}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{k}<\cdots<p_{2}<p_{1}\\
a_{1}+p_{1}=\cdots=a_{k}+p_{k}\leq x\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{(\log x)^{k}}\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k}\leq x}~\sum_{1\leq h<s\leq k}\prod_{p|(a_{s}-a_{h})}\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{x}{(\log x)^{k}}\binom{k}{2}\mathcal{A}(x)^{k-2}\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}}\prod_{p|(a_{1}-a_{2})}\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{p}\right).

Now, using the bound (
3.9
) of
\mathcal{A}(x)
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k}\leq x}~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{k}<\cdots<p_{2}<p_{1}\\
a_{1}+p_{1}=\cdots=a_{k}+p_{k}\leq x\end{subarray}}1\ll x(\log x)^{(k-2)\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}\right)-k}\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}\leq x}\prod_{p|(a_{1}-a_{2})}\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{p}\right).

Hence, to complete the proof of (
3.14
) it remains to show

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}\leq x}\prod_{p|(a_{1}-a_{2})}\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{p}\right)\ll(\log x)^{2\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}\right)}.

We now make use of a trick of Elsholtz, Luca and Planitzer
[
12
]
. We separate the primes
p
in the product of (
3.17
) according to
p>\log x
or
p\leq\log x
. For
a_{1},a_{2}\leq x
, it is not hard to see

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|(a_{1}-a_{2})\\
p>\log x\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{p}\right)\leq\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{\log x}\right)^{\frac{\log x}{\log 2}}\ll 1.

Thus, we only need to prove

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}\leq x}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|(a_{1}-a_{2})\\
2<p\leq\log x\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{p}\right)\ll(\log x)^{2\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}\right)}.

(3.17)

Expanding the product and then exchanging the order of summations, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}\leq x}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|(a_{1}-a_{2})\\
2<p\leq\log x\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{p}\right)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\\
2\nmid d\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}}{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}<a_{2}\leq x\\
a_{1}\equiv a_{2}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\end{subarray}}1,

(3.18)

where
\mu(d)
is the Möbius function,
\omega(d)
is the number of different prime factors of
d
, and
P^{+}(d)
is the largest prime factor of
d
. By the definition of
\mathcal{A}
, we know

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}<a_{2}\leq x\\
a_{1}\equiv a_{2}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}+\cdots+2^{\lfloor m_{t}^{r_{t}}\rfloor}\equiv 2^{\lfloor n_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}+\cdots+2^{\lfloor n_{t}^{r_{t}}\rfloor}\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\\
2^{\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}+\cdots+2^{\lfloor m_{t}^{r_{t}}\rfloor}<2^{\lfloor n_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}+\cdots+2^{\lfloor n_{t}^{r_{t}}\rfloor}\leq x\\
m_{i},n_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\ 1\leq i\leq t\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\ll(\log x)^{2\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}\right)-\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\max_{f}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}\equiv f\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\\
m_{1}\leq(\log 2x/\log 2)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\end{subarray}}1,

which together with (
3.17
) implies

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}\leq x}

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|(a_{1}-a_{2})\\
2<p\leq\log x\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\ll(\log x)^{2\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}\right)-\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1,\ 2\nmid d\\
P^{+}(d)\leq\log x\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(d)^{2}k^{3\omega(d)}}{d}\max_{f}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{\lfloor m_{1}^{r_{1}}\rfloor}\equiv f\ ({\rm{mod}}\ d)\\
m_{1}\leq(\log 2x/\log 2)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\end{subarray}}1.

(3.19)

We will separate the remaining argument into two cases.

Case I.
At least one of
r_{i}
is not an integer. Changing the order of
r_{i}
’s if necessary, we may assume without loss of generality that
r_{1}
is not an integer. Then, by (
3
) and Lemma
3.5
,

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}\leq x}

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|(a_{1}-a_{2})\\
2<p\leq\log x\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{p}\right)\ll(\log x)^{2\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}\right)-\frac{1}{r_{1}}}(\log x)^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\ll(\log x)^{2\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}\right)},

establishing (
3.17
) in
Case I.

Case II.
All of the
r_{i}
’s are integers. By (
3
) and Lemma
3.7
, we also have

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1}<a_{2}\leq x}

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|(a_{1}-a_{2})\\
2<p\leq\log x\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{k^{3}}{p}\right)\ll(\log x)^{2\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r_{t}}\right)},

completing the proof of (
3.17
) in
Case II,
and hence the whole theorem.
∎
