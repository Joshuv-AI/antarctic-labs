Compositional Approximation Can Strictly Outperform Superpositional Approximation
Dennis Elbrächter and Philipp Petersen

Abstract
Many classically studied function classes are known to be approximated optimally by superpositional methods, i.e. with approximants constructed as the linear combination of elements in some dictionary. Here optimality means that the uniform approximation error viewed as a function of the number of parameters used has polynomial decay of the highest order achievable by any parametrized method whose parameters can be encoded as a bit string of length proportional, up to logarithmic factors, to the number of parameters. While compositional methods like neural networks are structurally different, their approximation rates can be made comparable by imposing constraints that ensure such a proportional bit string encoding. In this work we study function classes exhibiting structural properties that limit superpositional approximation rates to be strictly lower than compositional approximation rates. In particular, we construct explicit examples for which there is an arbitrarily large gap.

m\leq(\tfrac{M}{C_{k}})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
, that

\displaystyle H_{m,k,\alpha,\beta}\subseteq S_{M}=\left\{\mathcal{R}_{\rho}(\Theta)\colon\Theta\in\mathcal{N}_{d,d^{\prime}},\mathcal{M}(\Theta)\leq M,\mathcal{L}(\Theta)\leq\pi(\log(M)),\mathcal{B}(\Theta)\leq\pi(M)\right\},

where
\pi
is a polynomial of degree
\lceil\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\rceil(\tfrac{k}{2}(1-\alpha)-\mu)
. Thus, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in V_{m}}\infp_{h\in S_{M}}\|f-h\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}=0

for
m\leq(\tfrac{M}{C_{k}})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
as well as

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in V_{m}}\infp_{h\in S_{M}}\|f-h\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}\leq\sup_{f\in V_{m}}\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}\leq m^{-\mu}\leq(\tfrac{M}{C_{k}})^{-\frac{\mu}{\alpha}}

for
m>(\tfrac{M}{C_{k}})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
since
0\in S_{M}
.
Consequently we have

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}V_{m}}\infp_{h\in S_{M}}\|f-h\|_{L^{2}([0,1])}\in\mathcal{O}(M^{-\frac{\mu}{\alpha}}),

which means
\gamma^{\mathcal{N}}_{\rho}(V(k,\alpha,\beta,\mu))\geq\tfrac{\mu}{\alpha}
.
The upper bound on
\gamma^{\mathrm{orth}}(\mathcal{C})
follows from
Theorem

3.4
and
Lemma

4.4
.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

The proof simplifies for neural networks with the ReLU activation function, as they achieve exact representation up to a certain
m
.
It should, however, be straightforward to adapt the proof for neural networks with any activation function as long as they can efficiently approximate the basic hat function
f
.

Remark 4.8
.

Note that for the choice of
\mu=\sqrt{k}
, we obtain examples of function classes for which we get an arbitrarily large gap between compositional and Riesz approximation rates. More specifically,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\gamma^{\mathcal{N}}_{\rho}(V(k,\alpha,\beta,\sqrt{k}))=\infty

whereas

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\textbf{}\gamma^{\mathrm{Riesz}}(V(k,\alpha,\beta,\sqrt{k}))\leq\tfrac{1}{2}.

Another way to look at this is to consider the sets of all functions in
L^{2}([0,1])
, which can be approximated by ReLU neural networks with at least a given rate
\gamma
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\rho,\gamma}:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{C}\subseteq L^{2}([0,1])\\
\gamma^{\mathcal{N}}_{\rho}(\mathcal{C})\geq\gamma\end{subarray}}\mathcal{C}.

Due to
Proposition

4.6
we know that

\displaystyle V(k,\alpha,\beta,\mu)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\rho,\gamma}

if
\tfrac{\mu}{\alpha}\geq\gamma
. Thus

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mathrm{Riesz}}(\mathcal{C}_{\rho,\gamma})\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}),\beta\in(0,\alpha),\\
\mu\in(\gamma\alpha,\infty),k\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\gamma^{\mathrm{Riesz}}(V(k,\alpha,\beta,\mu))\leq\tfrac{1}{2}.

This is essentially due to the fact that the Riesz approximation rate decreases with the number of compositions
k
, whereas
k
does not affect the rate for neural networks, but only the implicit constants of the asymptotics.

Appendix A
Excessive Orthogonality Example

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, let
(e_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
be an orthonormal system,
(s_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be increasing,
(\delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be decreasing, let
\rho\colon x\mapsto\max\{0,x\}
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{s,\delta}:=\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha_{k}e_{k}\colon|\{i\leq k\colon\alpha_{i}\neq 0\}|\leq s_{k},|\alpha_{k}|\leq\delta_{k}\ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}\}.

If there exist
a,b,c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\in(0,\infty)
with
b>a
such that, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that
\delta_{k}\in(c_{1}\log(k)^{-b},c_{2}\log(k)^{-b})
and
s_{k}\in[1,c_{3}\log(k)^{a})
, then

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mathrm{super}}(\mathcal{C}_{s,\delta})=-\infty.

(11)

Furthermore, if there exist
d,d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
q,p\in(0,1)
,
c\in(0,\infty)
, and
\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{+}
, such that
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\{f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}\}
and that for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a neural network
\psi_{\varepsilon,k}\in\mathcal{N}_{d,d^{\prime}}
which satisfies

\|e_{k}-\mathcal{R}_{\rho}(\psi_{\varepsilon,k})\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\varepsilon
,
\mathcal{M}(\psi_{\varepsilon,k})\leq c\varepsilon^{-q}\log(k)^{p}
,
\mathcal{L}(\psi_{\varepsilon,k})\leq\pi(\log(\mathcal{M}(\psi_{\varepsilon,k})))
,
\mathcal{B}(\psi_{\varepsilon,k})\leq\pi(\mathcal{M}(\psi_{\varepsilon,k}))
, then

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mathcal{N}}_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{s,\delta})\geq\tfrac{b-a}{a+p+qb}.

Proof.

We first prove (
11
). Assume, towards a proof by contradiction, that there exists a dictionary
\mathcal{D}=(\varphi_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
,
C,r\in(0,\infty)
, and
\tau,M_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for every
M\geq M_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{M}(\mathcal{C}_{s,\delta}):=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{C}_{s,\delta}}\infp_{h\in\langle\mathcal{D}_{M}\rangle_{M}}\|f-h\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq CM^{-r},

where
\mathcal{D}_{M}=(\varphi_{i})_{i\in[M^{\tau}]}
.
We note that for
k,M\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{M}(\mathcal{C}_{s,\delta})

\displaystyle=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{C}_{s,\delta}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq[M^{\tau}],\\
|I|=M,\,|c_{i}|\leq\pi(M)\end{subarray}}\left\|f-\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}\varphi_{i}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{j\in[k]}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq[M^{\tau}],\\
|I|=M,\,c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\end{subarray}}\|\delta_{k}e_{j}-\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}\varphi_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\geq\max_{j\in[k]}\min_{h\in S_{M}}\|\delta_{k}e_{j}-h\|_{\mathcal{H}},

where
S_{M}
is the span of
\varphi_{1},\dots,\lfloor M^{\tau}\rfloor
. Applying Lemma
B.4
we obtain, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
M\geq M_{0}
that

\displaystyle\delta_{k}(1-\sqrt{\tfrac{\lfloor M^{\tau}\rfloor}{k}})\leq CM^{-r}.

Rearranging the inequality and plugging in
\delta_{k}\geq c_{1}\log(k)^{-b}
yields

\displaystyle\sqrt{\tfrac{M^{\tau}}{k}}\geq 1-\tfrac{C}{c_{1}}\log(k)^{b}M^{-r}.

In particular this must hold for every
M\geq M_{0}
and

\displaystyle k_{M}:=\lfloor 2^{((\frac{c_{1}M^{r}}{2C})^{\frac{1}{b}})}\rfloor,

in which case we get

\displaystyle\tfrac{C}{c_{1}}\log(k_{M})^{b}M^{-\gamma}\leq\tfrac{1}{2},

and consequently

\displaystyle M^{\tau}\geq\tfrac{k_{M}}{4}\geq 2^{((\frac{c_{1}M^{r}}{2C})^{\frac{1}{b}})-3}.

Since
M^{\tau}\in o(2^{((\frac{c_{1}M^{r}}{2C})^{\frac{1}{b}})-3})
we have established a contradiction and thus proved (
11
).

In order to prove (
12
), we first note that
\mathcal{C}_{s^{\prime},\delta}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{s,\delta}
if
s^{\prime}_{k}\leq s_{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and we can therefore w.l.o.g. assume that
s_{k}=c_{3}\log(k)^{a}
.
We proceed by estimating the truncation error for

\displaystyle f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}e_{k}\in\mathcal{C}_{s,\delta}.

Observe that, for
n\geq 3
, we have

\displaystyle T_{n}(f):=\|\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}e_{k}\|\leq\lceil s_{n}\rceil\delta_{n}+\sum_{s=\lceil s_{n}\rceil+1}^{\infty}\max_{j\colon s_{j}>s-1}\delta_{j},

where for the first
\lceil s_{n}\rceil
many non-zero coefficients in the above sum we only use that they cannot be larger than
\delta_{n}
, whereas for any further coefficient we use that the
s
-th non-zero coefficient must have an index
j
such that
s_{j}>s-1
.

Observe that
(\delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a decreasing sequence, and thus it holds for
s\in\mathbb{N}
that

\displaystyle\max_{j\colon s_{j}>s}\delta_{j}=\max_{j\colon j>2^{((c_{3}^{-1}s)^{\frac{1}{a}})}}\delta_{j}\leq c_{2}\log(2^{((c_{3}^{-1}s)^{\frac{1}{a}})})^{-b}=c_{2}c_{3}^{\frac{1}{a}}s^{-\frac{b}{a}}.

We further note that for
t\in\mathbb{N}
and
z>1
it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{s=t}^{\infty}s^{-z}\leq\sum_{s=t}^{\infty}\int_{s-1}^{s}x^{-z}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{t-1}^{\infty}x^{-z}\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{z-1}(t-1)^{-z+1}.

Combining the above we get

\displaystyle T_{n}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\lceil s_{n}\rceil\delta_{n}+\sum_{s=\lceil s_{n}\rceil+1}^{\infty}\max_{j\colon s_{j}>s-1}\delta_{j}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}c_{3}\lceil\log(n)^{a}\rceil\log(n)^{-b}+\sum_{s=\lceil s_{n}\rceil}^{\infty}\max_{j\colon s_{j}>s}\delta_{j}

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{2}c_{3}\log(n)^{a-b}+\sum_{s=\lceil s_{n}\rceil}^{\infty}c_{2}c_{3}^{\frac{1}{a}}s^{-\frac{b}{a}}

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{2}c_{3}\log(n)^{a-b}+c_{2}c_{3}^{\frac{1}{a}}\tfrac{1}{\frac{b}{a}-1}(\lceil s_{n}\rceil-1)^{-\frac{b}{a}+1}

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{2}c_{3}\log(n)^{a-b}+c_{2}c_{3}^{\frac{1}{a}}\tfrac{a}{b-a}(\tfrac{c_{3}}{2})^{-\frac{b}{a}+1}\log(n)^{a-b}

\displaystyle=C_{a,b}\log(n)^{a-b},

where
C_{a,b}:=2c_{2}c_{3}+c_{2}c_{3}^{\frac{1}{a}}\tfrac{a}{b-a}(\tfrac{c_{3}}{2})^{-\frac{b}{a}+1}
.
We can now employ
Lemma

B.2
to obtain, for every
f\in\mathcal{C}_{s,\delta}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, a network
\Psi_{f,\varepsilon}
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\rho}(\Psi_{f,\varepsilon})=\sum_{k\in[n],\alpha_{k}\neq 0}\alpha_{k}\mathcal{R}_{\rho}(\psi_{\eta,k})

with

\displaystyle n=n_{\varepsilon}:=\left\lceil 2^{((\frac{\varepsilon}{2C_{a,b}})^{\frac{1}{a-b}})}\right\rceil,\quad\eta=\eta_{\varepsilon}:=\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2s_{n}}.

Consequently we obtain

\displaystyle\|f-\mathcal{R}_{\rho}(\Psi_{f,\varepsilon})\|

\displaystyle\leq T_{n}+\|\sum_{k\in[n],\alpha_{k}\neq 0}\alpha_{k}(e_{k}-\mathcal{R}_{\rho}(\psi_{\eta,k}))\|\leq C_{a,b}\log(n)^{a-b}+s_{n}\eta\leq\varepsilon.

Next we estimate their connectivity as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\Psi_{f,\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{k\in[n],\alpha_{k}\neq 0}(\mathcal{M}(\psi_{\eta,k})+\mathcal{L}(\psi_{\eta,k}))

\displaystyle\leq 2s_{n}(c\eta^{-q}\log(n)^{p}+\pi(\log(c\eta^{-q}\log(n)^{p}))

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon\to 0}(\varepsilon^{-\frac{a+p+qb}{b-a}}).

Moreover, we have
\mathcal{L}(\Psi_{f,\varepsilon})\leq\max_{k\in[n]}\mathcal{L}(\psi_{\eta,k})
and
\mathcal{B}(\Psi_{f,\varepsilon})\leq\max_{k\in[n]}(|\alpha_{k}|\max\{1,\mathcal{B}(\psi_{\eta,k})\})
, and since
\max_{k\in[n]}\mathcal{M}(\psi_{\eta,k})\leq\mathcal{M}(\Psi_{f,\varepsilon})
, there clearly exists
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{+}
such that
\mathcal{L}(\Psi_{f,\varepsilon})\leq\pi^{\prime}(\log(\mathcal{M}(\Psi_{f,\varepsilon})))
and
\mathcal{B}(\Psi_{f,\varepsilon})\leq\pi^{\prime}(\mathcal{M}(\Psi_{f,\varepsilon}))
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mathcal{N}}_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{s,\delta})\geq\tfrac{b-a}{a+p+qb}.

(12)

∎

Lemma B.3
.

Let
T\subseteq\mathbb{N}
,
c\in(0,\infty)
,
M\in\mathbb{N}
, and

\mathcal{D}=(\varphi_{i})_{i\in T}\subset\mathcal{H}
be
c
-Riesz, and
x\in\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle_{M}
with
\|x\|=1
.
Then there exists
j\in T
such that

\displaystyle|\langle x,\varphi_{j}\rangle|\geq\sqrt{\frac{c}{M}}.

(13)

Proof.

As
x\in\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle_{M}
, there exists
I\subseteq T
with
|I|=M
such that
x=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\varphi_{i}
. Due to the
c
-Riesz assumption and the fact that
\|\cdot\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{M}\|\cdot\|_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{M}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}|\lambda_{i}|\leq\sqrt{M}\|\lambda\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{\tfrac{M}{c}}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle 1=|\langle x,x\rangle|=|\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\langle x,\varphi_{i}\rangle|\leq(\max_{i\in I}|\langle x,\varphi_{i}\rangle|)\sum_{i\in I}|\lambda_{i}|\leq\sqrt{\tfrac{M}{c}}\max_{i\in I}|\langle x,\varphi_{i}\rangle|.

This ensures that (
13
) is satisfied for
j\in\argmax_{i\in I}|\langle x,\varphi_{i}\rangle|.

∎

Lemma B.4
.

Let
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
,
d,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
d<k
, let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, let
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{k}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
be an orthogonal family with
\|f_{j}\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\delta
for
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, and let
S\subseteq\mathcal{H}
be a
d
-dimensional linear subspace. Then

\displaystyle\max_{j\in[k]}\min_{s\in S}\|f_{j}-s\|_{\mathcal{H}}\geq\delta(1-\sqrt{\tfrac{d}{k}}).

Proof.

We first consider the case where
S\subseteq\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\rangle
. Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{d}
be an orthonormal basis of
S
and note that there are
a_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i\in[d]
,
j\in[k]
, such that, for
i\in[d]
, we have

\displaystyle e_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{i,j}f_{j}.

(14)

Next, we define, for every
j\in[k]
,

\displaystyle s_{j}:=\argmin_{s\in S}\|f_{j}-s\|_{\mathcal{H}}

and note that it is given by the orthogonal projection of
f_{j}
into
S
, i.e. we have

\displaystyle s_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\langle f_{j},e_{i}\right\rangle e_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\langle f_{j},\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}a_{i,\ell}f_{\ell}\rangle e_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}a_{i,\ell}\left\langle f_{j},f_{\ell}\right\rangle\right)e_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|f_{j}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}a_{i,j}e_{i}.

Consequently we have, for every
j\in[k]
, that

\displaystyle\|f_{j}-s_{j}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\geq\|f_{j}\|_{\mathcal{H}}-\|f_{j}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\|\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i,j}e_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\delta-\delta^{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|a_{i,j}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}

We proceed by claiming that there must exist a
j\in[k]
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{d}|a_{i,j}|^{2}\leq\tfrac{d}{\delta^{2}k}
. Assume, towards a proof by contradiction, that
\sum_{i=1}^{d}|a_{i,j}|^{2}>\tfrac{d}{\delta^{2}k}
for every
j\in[k]
.
Note that (
14
) ensures
\sum_{j=1}^{k}|a_{i,j}|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{\delta^{2}}
. Thus we get

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{\delta^{2}}=k\tfrac{d}{\delta^{2}k}<\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{d}|a_{i,j}|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{k}|a_{i,j}|^{2}=d\sum_{j=1}^{k}|a_{i,j}|^{2}=\tfrac{d}{\delta^{2}},

which is a contradiction and thus proves our claim. As a consequence of this there exists
j\in[k]
for which

\displaystyle\|f_{j}-s_{j}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\geq\delta-\delta^{2}(\tfrac{d}{\delta^{2}k})^{\tfrac{1}{2}}=\delta(1-\sqrt{\tfrac{d}{k}}),

which concludes the proof in the case that
S\subseteq F:=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\rangle
.

In order to reduce the general case to the former, note that for an arbitrary
d
-dimensional linear subspace
S\subseteq\mathcal{H}
and every
s\in S
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|f_{j}-P_{F}(s)\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\|P_{F}(f_{j}-s)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\|P_{F}\|_{\text{op}}\|f_{j}-s\|\leq\|f_{j}-s\|,

where
P_{F}
is the orthogonal projection into
F
and
\|\cdot\|_{\text{op}}
the usual operator norm, which is bounded by
1
for orthogonal projections.
Thus we have that

\displaystyle\max_{j\in[k]}\min_{s\in S}\|f_{j}-s\|_{\mathcal{H}}\geq\max_{j\in[k]}\min_{s\in P_{F}(S)}\|f_{j}-s\|_{\mathcal{H}},

which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

3.1
.

Let
y
be the orthogonal projection of
x
into
\langle z_{1},\dots,z_{T}\rangle
and note that
\|y\|_{2}\leq\|x\|_{2}=1
and
|\langle y,z_{j}\rangle|=|\langle x,z_{j}\rangle|
for all
j\in[T]
. Since
y\in\langle z_{1},\dots,z_{T}\rangle
there exists
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{T})\in\mathbb{R}^{T}
such that

\displaystyle y=\sum_{j=1}^{T}\lambda_{j}z_{j}.

W.l.o.g. we assume that
|\lambda_{1}|\geq|\lambda_{2}|\geq\dots\geq|\lambda_{T}|
and note that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[T]\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}|=2\sum_{i=1}^{T-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{T}|\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}|\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{T-1}(T-i)|\lambda_{i}|^{2}\leq 2T\|\lambda\|^{2}_{2}.

Consequently we have

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\geq\|y\|_{2}^{2}=\langle\sum_{j=1}^{T}\lambda_{j}z_{j},\sum_{j=1}^{T}\lambda_{j}z_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{T}(\lambda_{j})^{2}\langle z_{j},z_{j}\rangle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[T]\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\langle z_{i},z_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{T}(\lambda_{j})^{2}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in[T]\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\langle z_{i},z_{j}\rangle|

\displaystyle\geq\|\lambda\|^{2}_{2}-2T^{1-\theta}\|\lambda\|^{2}_{2},

which implies

\displaystyle\|\lambda\|^{2}_{2}\leq\frac{1}{1-2T^{1-\theta}}.

(15)

Next we note that
|\lambda_{1}|\geq|\lambda_{2}|\geq\dots\geq|\lambda_{T}|
implies
|\lambda_{T}|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\lambda\|_{2}
. Denoting by
P
the orthogonal projection onto
\langle z_{T}\rangle
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\langle y,z_{T}\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\sum_{j=1}^{T}\lambda_{j}\langle z_{j},z_{T}\rangle|\leq|\lambda_{T}|+T^{-\theta}\sum_{j=1}^{T-1}|\lambda_{j}|\leq|\lambda_{T}|+T^{-\theta}\|\lambda\|_{1}\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\lambda\|_{2}+T^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}\|\lambda\|_{2},

where we used that
\|\lambda\|_{1}\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\lambda\|_{2}
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{T}
, and that
\|Pz_{j}\|_{2}=|\langle z_{T},z_{j}\rangle|\leq T^{-\theta}
, for
j\in[T-1]
, by assumption.
Combining this with (
15
) yields

\displaystyle|\langle y,z_{T}\rangle|\leq T^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1+T^{1-\theta}}{(1-2T^{1-\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq 4T^{-\frac{1}{2}},

since
1+T^{1-\theta}\leq 2
and
(1-2T^{1-\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
due to the assumption of
T\geq 4^{\frac{1}{\theta-1}}
.
∎
