 Abstract
 This report describes the Multiple Phase Screen (MPS) code module for the Diorama elec-
tromagnetic pulse propagation suite.

 1

1 introduction

Figure 1: Conditions and geometry for transionospheric signal scintillation, from [1] . The earth’s magnetic field is
in the y direction.

The phenomenon of random fluctuations in the amplitude and phase of received transionospheric
radio frequency (RF) signals is well known and documented [2].
 For wide band signals, sometimes a set of frequencies in the the band or even the whole band
can suffer degradation or complete drop out at the receiver. The former is known as frequency
selective scintillation where only a fraction of the signal bandwidth is affected, whereas the latter
is known as flat fading.
 As a physical process, scintillation is best described as random variations in the amplitude and
phase of a propagating EM wave caused by an irregular structure in the local electron density [1];
or equivalently, diffraction of the EM wave due to spatial irregularities in the electron content in
the ionosphere.
 In the ionosphere, irregular electron density structure is caused by plasma instabilities which
distribute the electron density randomly along local magnetic field lines. Thus, a striated plasma
forms with rods, or sheets, of increased (or decreased) plasma density relative to the surround-
ing background plasma (Fig. 1). These electron density fluctuations effectively create a stochastic
‘screen’ that causes the phase of the incident signal, at that location along the screen, to advance or

 2

retard in a fashion governed by the spatial statistics of the density fluctuations. Signal scintillation
can be characterized from one or more of these phase screens.
 This diffraction is different from the refraction experienced by the EM wave as it propagates
through the bulk magnetized plasma. Naturally, the scintillation suffered by a propagating EM wave
with polarization along or across the magnetic field will be different.
 For wide bandwidth signals, each frequency component experiences a different phase pertur-
bation through the same region due to the dispersive nature of EM wave propagation through a
plasma. The time variation in the plasma irregularities will also affect the imparted phase. This
module assumes that time scales of signals germane to this application are much smaller than time
scales involved with electron density fluctuations. Thus, a ‘time snapshot’ model of the ionosphere
is assumed.
 EM wave propagation through a ‘disturbed’ ionosphere, then, can be described in two parts. In
the first, wave refraction through the quiescent (bulk) plasma is calculated using standard techniques
such as full wave solutions or ray tracing techniques [3, 4, 5, 6]. The second part is the calculation
of the propagation characteristics through the stochastic structure component of the ionospheric
electron density [1, 7, 8, 9]. The solutions for the propagating wave fields in each part can then be
superposed.
 This report describes the MPS-based computer code module for implementation in the Diorama
EM code suite.

2 theory overview
The detailed theoretical model for this module is presented in a separate technical report [10], and
will only briefly be outlined here.
 Scintillation effects on propagating EM waves (also referred to herein as RF signals, or simply
signals) are best described as diffraction through a screen, or screens, with a stochastic grid(s)
created by instabilities that randomly distribute ionospheric plasma electrons with structure along
and across the local magnetic field (Fig. 1). The phase imparted to the signal will have different
statistical properties across the grid depending on the orientation of electric field with respect to
the local magnetic field.

 Figure 2: Conceptual approach to the MPS code.

 Fig. 2 shows the situation. The signal is represented as an EM Plane Wave (PW). It propagates
through a phase screen, whereupon its phase at each location along the phase screen is changed by
an amount driven by ionospheric electron density irregularity statistics. It then propagates through
free space to the next screen. This process continues until it propagates beyond the last phase screen
to the detector.
 Recall that the ionospheric electron density irregularities are stochastic and therefore must be
quantified statistically. The (smooth) background ionospheric electron density profile is a known
quantity. Thus the EMPW’s propagation through the ionosphere is calculated as a deterministic
refraction using, i.e., ray tracing techniques, whereas its diffractive characteristics are calculated by
imposing phase screens at discrete locations. These locations are separated by free space because
its propagation between the screens is assumed to be specified in the refractive solution.
 The main equations to solve for the MPS scintillation problem involve the EMPW’s propagation
through free space as well as a medium with a stochastic refractive index.
 The geometry of the problem separates naturally into directions parallel and perpendicular to the
magnetic field B0 . It is then appropriate to treat the stochastic part of the plasma as non-magnetized
with different statistical properties along, or across B0 . Electric field polarization can effectively

be separated in this manner without loss of generality. With this in mind, assume propagation in
the z direction with plasma gradients in x and y. Parallel to B0 in y (Fig. 1), it is expected that
gradients will be very gradual. Thus solve the problem in the z − x plane, where all gradients are
assumed across the magnetic field (x). Under this approximation, the statistics of the stochastic
plasma perpendicular to B0 will dominate.

3 code structure
The MPS code module requires two inputs. The first is a file containing a list of parameters and
their values, and the second is the input signal to be scintillated. There are also run time arguments
that can be added if desired. The input formats and run time arguments will be explained later.
 The input signal is first Fourier transformed from time to frequency and then copied to make
a 2D array with rows corresponding to frequency bins (positive and negative frequencies) and
columns corresponding to the number of locations along the phase screen. Thus, for a signal of
length j and a phase screen with N equally spaced grid points, a 2D EM Plane Wave Array (PWA)
with N columns and j rows is constructed (N copies of the signal frequency spectrum). It is then
propagated through a specified number of phase screens, separated by equal distances, and lastly to
the detector, as outlined in equations 1 - 7. This is shown in Fig. 4.

 Figure 4: The split step algorithm approach to solving equations 1 - 7.

 The statistics required to calculate parameters like S4 are taken from the N spectra after the
PWA has propagated a user-specified distance. This method to calculate statistical parameters is
valid under the assumption that all quantities are statistically stationary.
 The time delay suffered by the signal (in the form of a PWA) between screens and from the last
screen to the detector was pointed out as the boxed term in Eq. 7. The user can specify whether
or not to include it, but it is recommended that it not be employed as that delay is included in the
refractive solution for the signal’s propagation through the ionosphere to the detector (Ionospheric
Transfer Function).
 Overall, calculating the magnitude and phase of the propagating plane wave field at a discrete
number of points along a phase screen (or screens), and then propagating it to the next screen or to

4 compiling and running
The directory in which the module will reside must have two sub-directories: INPUT_FILES and
OUTPUT_FILES. All input files specified below must reside in the former, and all output files are
written to the latter.
 The MPS module MPS_CODE.c is written in the C programming language. It must be linked to
the FFTW3 [14] and OpenMP [15] libraries when compiling:
 gcc -g MPS_CODE.c -o MPS_CODE -lfftw3_omp -lfftw3 -lm -fopenmp

 where "MPS_CODE" can be replaced with a user specified filename for the executable code.

6 verification tests
6.1 zero σT EC phase screen
This is a simple test of the module whereupon a no scintillation condition should give the same
output waveforms as the input signal. Construct a signal with no DC component. A good exam-
ple is a linear frequency chirp transitioning smoothly from zero and back to zero. Set psig = 0
(σT EC = 0) in scint_MPS_inputs.in. This is effectively shutting off the phase screen. Now run
the command
 ./MPS_CODE -oXX -dn

where MPS_CODE is assumed to be the executable code name, XX is the number of time and frequency
waveforms to write out. All of the XX time waveforms should be exact copies of the original time
waveform.
 Note that the input waveform was specified to have no DC component. This is due to the fact
that the MPS module will not propagate zero-frequency signal components. Thus if the input wave-
form were a double exponential, the output waveforms from this particular test would look slightly
different because they would have their DC frequency components removed.
 As a second test, keep σT EC = 0 and run

 ./MPS_CODE -oXX - zYY

Here, the time delay in the Fresnel propagator (Eqn. 7) is not omitted, so the original waveform
should be delayed by YY/3e5 seconds (YY is km). Keep in mind that for large YY, the delay could
be greater than the total time window of the original waveform (numvalues × dt). In this case, the
XX waveforms will be aliased and wrap around the time axis. For this test, the MPS module does
not check for this particular Nyquist condition since there is effectively no phase screen involved.

7 validation example
The examples shown here will be calculated with the random number generator configured such
that each run will generate a different number sequence. That is, line 81 uncommented and line 87

8 specific needs/recommendations for implementation into Dio-
 rama
Typical input signals for the MPS module will be broad-band VHF (30M Hz − 300M Hz). The
three Nyquist conditions will constrain the applicability of the MPS module calculations based on
this bandwidth. The wave propagation Nyquist criterion, Eq. 9, is the most troublesome for low
frequencies in the VHF band. This will place limits on the propagation distance of the PW between
screens and after the last screen (dz_km and z0_km in scint_MPS_inputs.in respectively). The
input file listing given in Fig. 5 gives values of L0 , Lknee , Li , dx, dz, and z0 that will satisfy all the
Nyquist criteria over the VHF band-width.
 Note that the propagation distance z0 is set to 500km in scint_MPS_inputs.in (Fig. 5),
whereas the propagation distance to a typical satellite based detector is on the order of z0 ∼
20000km. The relevant scale length parameters of the ST EC profile, L0 , Lknee , Li , and dx are
the controlling factor here. The module can be run with many screens separated by a few hundred
km, but this would take an extremely long time.
 In order to make the MPS model tractable for implementation in the Diorama suite of codes, it
is necessary to relax requirements on the detector distance z0 as the PW will suffer the vast majority
of scintillation inside the ionosphere, whose thickness is usually taken to be approximately 500km.
 The particular ST EC (k⊥ ) profile and associated scale length parameters can be chosen for nat-
ural, or man made scintillation effects based on the appropriate literature [13, 2, 12, 17].
