Executive Summary of CRADA Work:
 This CRADA between LLNS and Longview Fusion Energy Systems utilized a modernized LLNL
 Integrated Process Model (IPM) to assess technoeconomic viability and power plant
 configurations for Inertial Fusion Energy (IFE). During the collaboration period, a streamlined
 IPM model was produced by LLNL, which consolidated a combination of 3 models under one
 file. Additionally, obsolete parameters were removed and parameters available for trade
 space analysis were organized in a user-friendly fashion on the front interface. Gain scaling
 curves were discussed and analyzed in the framework of the IPM. Additional updates were
 made to account for cost scaling to today's dollars using an average inflation rate. Heat
 transfer material costing was updated using a ground up approach with public vendor data.
 Further levelized cost of electricity (LCOE) methods and models were discussed and reviewed
 for applicability to the IPM. Prior published literature on optical scaling was also discussed.
 Moreover, as part of this CRADA, the Participant developed a fusion technology development
 roadmap and point designs.

 This research adds to the understanding of IFE plant design by providing an updated, self -

Summary of Research Results:

 Technical Objectives:

 • Original Objectives: Utilize and develop LLNL’s IPM (IPM-V1.0-DS-LLNLV1, CP02892) to
 conduct trade space analyses and evaluate Participant-proposed IFE plant designs.
 Update performance and cost bases to reflect contemporary technology data, vendor
 inputs, and expected maturation over approximately a 10-year horizon. Generate
 quantitative, self-consistent point designs for Participant’s MEP (Market Entry Plant) and
 NOAK (Nth of a Kind) plants. Produce a Technology Development Roadmap and conduct
 workshops to inform and engage the technical community.
 • Approaches Used:
 o Trade space exploration across a wide range of inputs, with sensitivity analyses to
 identify cost and performance drivers.
 o Incorporation of cost escalation factors.
 o Iterative point design development for MEP and NOAK plants using IPM outputs
 and engineering constraints from Participant and partners.
 o Discussions for knowledge transfer on laser architectures, target manufacturing,
 fusion engine materials, tritium handling, and integrated plant design.
 • Problems Encountered and Methodology Adjustments:
 o N/A.
 • Technical Data and Assumptions:
 o Laser subsystem assumptions reflecting modern DPSSL performance envelopes,
 optical architectures, and driver module integration constraints.
 o Balance-of-plant assumptions for power conversion efficiencies, cooling systems,
 tritium fuel cycle approaches, availability modeling, and maintenance factors.
 o Cost bases integrating vendor inputs, conventional equipment costs, indirect
 factors, escalation indices, and sensitivity ranges.

  Actual Accomplishments:

  • Task 1: Updated IPM datasets and conducted trade space analyses of IFE reactor
 designs, including Participant point designs.
 o Deliverable 1.1: Technology update integrating various aspects of performance
 and cost bases. LLNL provided historical LIFE documentation to Participant to
 facilitate collaborative updates, including LIFE Basis-of-Estimate, Delivery Plan,
 Pre-Conceptual Design Report, Scoping Assessment, LIFE plant CAD model, and
 misc. Parsons Corporation documents. Participant, in cooperation with Parsons
 Corporation, provided additional LIFE documents developed in collaboration with
 Parsons Corporation previously under the LIFE effort.
 Participant provided mentorship and support to the CRADA team during the
 development of code changes from IPM-V1.0-DS-LLNLV1 to IPM-V2.0-DS-LLNLV1.
 o Deliverable 1.2: Software update reflecting revised cost scaling. Cost scaling
 based on inflation rate, enabling cost estimate with same reference value
 throughout model. Cost models were traded, considering different cost of
 electricity modeling methods (LCOE in existing IPM model and verification/cross-
 reference with Lazard method using internal rate of return).
 Participant also made substantial coding changes beyond IPM-V2.0-DS-LLNLV1
 including changes to the gain scaling, combination of learning and technology
 maturation, calculation flow charts, and additional user interfaces. During the
 assessment of IPM, high-impact subsystems were identified where the original scaling
 rules required modification. These updates ensure that subsystem costs more
 accurately reflect variations in plant geometry, beamline count, and thermal power.
 Participant held discussions with industrial partner and received information for
 conventional facilities and support equipment. Some of this information was shared in
 a draft of the final report developed by the Participant and presented to LLNL on
 January 30, 2026. These strategies are and will be implemented into a Longview version
 of an advanced integrated model. This analysis identified key opportunities across five
 major domains—Tritium Processing, Power Conversion, Laser Cooling,
 Buildings/Structures, Electrical Systems, and Common Project Practices—to optimize
 design, reduce costs, and improve operational efficiency. The recommendations
 address regulatory alignment, layout improvements, modularization, structural
 simplification, equipment consolidation, and lifecycle cost savings.
 o Deliverable 1.3: General trade space and trend analysis discussed during
 technical discussions. No specific details were incorporated into LLNL IPM.
 Participant completed and performed extensive trade space analyses and sensitivity
 parameter studies. The data produced from these studies led to tools for the use of
 artificial intelligence and machine learning models to calculate plant costs, which is
