Abstract: The goal of this project is to build an ultrafast entangled photon spectrometer and use it
to measure theoretical predictions that photoexcited states are enhanced by spin-photon
interactions. Entanglement of two states describes a specific type of quantum superposition in
which measuring one state gives information about a second state. While entanglement is well
explored for quantum information and computing systems, its effects on photoexcited states are
less understood, especially in the ultrafast domains of molecular vibronic coupling. The grant
designs a frequency and temporally resolved entangled photon spectrometer and uses it to test
theoretical predictions like enhanced two photon absorption, non-reciprocal Fourier relations, and
coupling of photons to spin systems. The end goal of the project is an understanding of how, when,
and where entanglement is useful for spectroscopy.

Introduction

Entanglement describes a quantum superposition that is not factorable into a product of the
individual states, such that measuring one state gives information about the other. Entanglement
can be generated in a two-level quantum system such as electron spin. In the two-state case,
commonly referred to as a qubit, the total system can be created in a superposition where either
sub-state can be denoted as 0 or 1. Phonons have been shown to mediate entanglement between
states1. A spin-selective intersystem crossing allows for an optically controlled qubit2,3. How spin-
vibronic coupling decoheres a photoexcited entangled state is therefore a critical question 4–6. In a
molecular qubit, internal conversion and intersystem crossings can occur on femtosecond and
longer time scales. Interface and nearest neighbor interactions, which limit practical application,
also occur on these timescales.
The goal of this research is to measure the timescale in which a photoexcited, molecular spin state
can stay entangled with an incident photon. The entangled dynamics of a photon-spin state will be
measured in two molecular systems capable of supporting two-photon or intermediate state
mediated two photon absorption. Combined, the experiments will give insight into how electronic
and vibrational coupling can mediate molecular-entangled photon interactions, which is relevant
to solid-state qubits as well as non-photoexcited, molecular magnet and spintronic systems7–9. The
exploration of entanglement for ultrafast optical measurements is also explored, such as for
replicating time-resolved fluorescence or transient absorption measurements.
To achieve this goal requires the creation of an ultrafast entangled photon spectrometer. Two
challenges currently limit entangled photon spectroscopy: the photon counting bottleneck and the
need to vary the coherence time of entangled photons10–14. The photon-counting bottleneck is
overcome by multiplexing a sub-𝜇𝑊, broadband flux of entangled photons created in a waveguide
over a single-photon counting CCD camera. This experimental approach enables a >1000 times
increase in experiment speed versus photon counting with a single SPAD detector and allows
entangled correlations to be measured in frequency and time. The coherence time is varied by the
temperature of the waveguide. The resultant instrument can measure classical and entangled
interactions on femtosecond time scales, with few-photon and higher excitation densities, and with
a theorized non-Fourier reciprocal energy and time resolution.
Objectives of the Proposal
 1. Entangled Photon Spectrometer: An entangled photon spectrometer will be designed and
 built based on a phase-unlocked Hong-Ou-Mandel interferometry scheme15. This scheme
 allows for femtosecond and longer correlations to be measured between two entangled
 photons and a sample. A broadband, SPDC process in a waveguide and a multiplexed
 photon-counting scheme speed up the experiments. Dynamics will be measured as a
 function of time delay between and within the entangled photon pairs.
 2. Benchmarking Classical versus Entangled Spectroscopy: Amongst the predicted
 advantages of entangled light, the non-Fourier reciprocity of frequency and temporal
 resolution could have an outstanding spectroscopic impact16–20. The Soret-band transition
 in ZnTPP will be used to test this idea. The <500 kHz laser linewidth will initially be
 centered on a specific vibronic transition and the vibronic wave packet will be measured
 by the correlation of the entangled photons. The entangled photon flux will then be

 compressed from femtoseconds to picoseconds and will be varied in bandwidth from a few
 nanometers to 300 nanometers. If the vibronic wave packet remains unchanged, the non-

 3. Optically Excited Entanglement in Molecules: The entangled photon spectrometer will
 be used to measure if the photon-spin entanglement is preserved during optical excitation,
 of two molecules: R6G and ICG. The former supports a proposed two-photon absorption
 pathway while the latter supports an intermediate state mediated two-photon pathway
 where the intermediate states coherence lifetime should mediate the two photon transition.
 4. Replicating Ultrafast Classical Experiments with Entangled Photons: The time-energy
 entanglement of the created photons and their discrete nature should allow for classical
 pulsed laser experiments to be replicated at lower flux. We explore if time resolved
 fluorescence and pump-probe spectroscopy can be replicated in the single photon limit.

Successfully Meeting the Objective of the Project: We (1) designed the highest flux and lowest
wavelength range SPDC sources to enable entangled spectroscopy,21–23 (2) built the first spectrally
and temporally resolved quantum state spectrometer, (3) used this spectrometer to disprove
controversial findings on enhancements to two-photon absorption,24 (4) explored whether or not
an excited spin-photon state preserved entanglement,25 and (5) invented a single photon approach
to time-resolved fluorescence using entanglement.26

1. Entangled Photon Spectrometer:

An entangled photon spectrometer with both temporal and spectral resolution of the entangled
photon pairs was constructed. The spectrometer is outlined in Figure 1. The main components are
the entangled photon source, a HOM interferometer, and single-photon counting detectors for
temporal characterization, or an EMICCD for spectral characterization. In addition to building the
spectrometer for the first temporal/spectral characterization of entangled molecular processes,
significant advances were made in the entangled photon source itself. Previous experiments had
used bulk BBO crystals with efficiencies in the 10-12 range. We made advances in both flux and
wavelength tunability by employing lithium niobate and lithium tantalate.

4. Replicating Ultrafast Classical Experiments with Entangled Photons:

During the grant, we discovered (simultaneous to Fleming and Laurence29,30) that the temporal
correlations between entangled photons produced by a CW laser could be used to replicate classical
time resolved spectroscopy (Fig. 5).26 In particular, one entangled photon can be used as a pump,
and the other as a probe, as they are created simultaneously, effectively as two single-photon pulses
that can then be manipulated.

We took advantage of this in two ways. First, we use the probe photon to trigger a photon counting
detector and then measured the classical fluorescence emission following entangled photon
excitation using a second detector. In this way, time-resolved fluorescence measurements were
replicated. Advantages of this approach include the temperature-based wavelength tuning of the
entangled photon source, the fact that a CW laser diode can be used for time-resolved fluorescence
with <100 fs temporal correlations, and the compact size and nature of our developed nanophotonic
sources. Entangled time-resolved fluorescence using nanophotonic sources could therefore be
ideal for portable or distributable, cheap-to-produce sensors or microscopes. Additionally, the
ability to perform entangled pump-probe experiments in the single photon regime was explored.
Building on lessons from (3), we learned that to successfully do an entangled two-photon
experiment, a third entangled photon pair needs to be present to differentiate modulations in the
entangled photon wave packet from linear absorption events in multiple molecules. Efforts are
now underway to build entangled triplet sources.

Summary

A thorough investigation of two entangled photon molecular interactions was conducted. This
included building the necessary spectral/temporally resolved entangled photon spectrometer,
inventing new nanophotonic sources with record flux and wavelength ranges to enable the
spectroscopy, investigations of simultaneous and sequential entangled photon processes in
molecules, and the exploration of using entangled photon pairs to replicate ultrafast classical
approaches but now in the single photon regime. While strong signatures of entangled two photon
absorption or manipulation through a sequential state were not found, the spectroscopy advances
and new entangled time resolved fluorescence approaches set the foundation for new entangled
photon investigations of molecular processes to come.
