A Hecke algebra isomorphism over close local fields for metaplectic groups
Ritabrata Das

Abstract
. We establish the Kazhdan isomorphism for the n n -fold metaplectic cover of the reductive group SL 2 \mathrm{SL}_{2} over two sufficiently close non-archimedean local fields F F and F ′ F^{\prime} , both of which have residue characteristic coprime to n n and contain all distinct n n th roots of unity.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
22E50, 11F70

1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a local non-archimedean field with the ring of integers
\mathscr{O}
and the maximal ideal
\mathscr{P}\subset\mathscr{O}
. Let
F
contain the
n
-distinct
n
th roots of unity
\mu_{n}(F)
, and
n
is coprime to the residue characteristic. The group
G(F)=\mathrm{SL_{2}}(F)
has a unique nontrivial central extension
\widetilde{G}(F)
of degree
n
:

\displaystyle 1\longrightarrow\mu_{n}(F)\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}\widetilde{G}(F)\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}G(F)\longrightarrow 1.

The group
Mp_{2}(F):=\widetilde{G}(F)
is the
n
-fold metaplectic cover of
G(F)
. In this paper, we establish a Hecke algebra isomorphism over close local fields for
\widetilde{G}(F)
.

For an object
X
associated with the field
F
, we will use the notation
X^{\prime}
to denote the corresponding object over
F^{\prime}
. In
[
11
]
Kazhdan proved
that for
G
, being a split, connected reductive group defined over
\mathbb{Z}
and given
l\geq 1
, there exists
m\geq l
such that if
F
and
F^{\prime}
are

m
-close, then there is an isomorphism of Hecke algebras

\mathrm{Kaz}_{l}:\mathcal{H}(G(F),K_{l})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}(G(F^{\prime}),K^{\prime}_{l}),

where
K_{l}
is the
l
-th usual congruence subgroup of
G(\mathscr{O})
. Hence, when the fields
F

and
F^{\prime}
are sufficiently close, we have a bijection

\displaystyle\{\text{Irreducible admissible }\mathbb{C}\text{-representations }(\pi,V)\text{ of }G(F)\text{ such that }\pi^{K_{l}}\neq 0\}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\{\text{Irreducible admissible }\mathbb{C}\text{-representations }(\pi^{\prime},V^{\prime})\text{ of }G(F^{\prime})\text{ such that }\pi^{\prime K^{\prime}_{l}}\neq 0\}.

Ganapathy in
[
7
]
extended this result for general connected reductive groups. Kazhdan’s philosophy has been useful in studying the local Langlands correspondence for certain groups over local fields of positive characteristic (
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
).

There are three crucial steps in the proof of the Kazhdan isomorphism of Hecke algebras for reductive groups:

(1)

The group
G(F)
admits a Cartan decomposition.

(2)

If the fields
F
and
F^{\prime}
are
l
-close, then we obtain a natural isomorphism

\displaystyle G(\mathscr{O})/K_{l}\cong G(\mathscr{O^{\prime}})/K_{l}^{\prime}.

(3)

The Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(G(F),K_{l})
is finitely presented.

We write
\mathscr{O}
for the ring of integers of
F
. Let
\delta
denote the Kubota cocycle for our metaplectic cover
\widetilde{G}(F)
and let
S
denote the corresponding splitting of
G(\mathscr{O})
(see Section 2). For any subgroup
H
of
G(\mathscr{O})
, we denote its image under the splitting by
H^{*}:=S(H)
.

Fixing a faithful character
\varepsilon:\mu_{n}(F)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, we define the genuine Hecke algebra with respect to the congruence subgroup
K_{l}^{*}
for the covering group
\widetilde{G}(F)
as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F),K_{l}^{*}):=\left\{f:\widetilde{G}(F)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}\,:\,\begin{aligned} &\bullet\ \text{$f$ is locally constant and compactly supported},\\
&\bullet\ f(\zeta g)=\varepsilon(\zeta)f(g),\quad\forall\zeta\in\mu_{n}(F),\ g\in\widetilde{G}(F),\\
&\bullet\ f(k_{1}gk_{2})=f(g),\quad\forall k_{1},k_{2}\in K_{l}^{*},\ g\in\widetilde{G}(F).\end{aligned}\right\}.

The algebra structure is given by the convolution of functions:

\displaystyle(f*h)(x)=\int_{\widetilde{G}(F)}f(y)h(y^{-1}x)dy,

where
dy
is the Haar measure of the group
\widetilde{G}(F)
, which is normalized such that the measure of
\mu_{n}(F)\times K_{l}^{*}
is
1
.

In this paper, we establish an isomorphism between the genuine Hecke algebras over close local fields. Our proof relies on establishing analogs of the three crucial steps (1), (2), and (3) of Kazhdan’s proof for reductive groups. However, we do encounter several technical difficulties along the way.

Because the unipotent subgroups of
G(F)
split canonically in
\widetilde{G}(F)
(see
[
14
, Prop. 4.1]
,
[
15
, Appendix 1]
), we can fix preferred liftings of the elements in the diagonal torus. These are obtained via the canonical liftings of the root subgroups (see Section
2.4
).

We derive the Cartan decomposition for the
n
-fold covering group of
\mathrm{SL_{2}}
in Section 3. This result is implicit in Theorem 9.2 of
[
14
]
. Peskin explicitly worked out the special case for the
2
-fold metaplectic cover in
[
17
, Lemma 2.6]
.

A main difficulty in proving the isomorphism between the genuine Hecke algebras lies in understanding the behavior of Kubota’s cocycle on the
K_{l}^{*}
-double cosets in
\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})
. We carefully analyze the Kubota cocycle in Section 5 and provide technical lemmas in Section 7 to show that the natural vector space isomorphism between the genuine Hecke algebras respects convolution when the fields are sufficiently close.

Finally, we show that the genuine Hecke algebra is finitely presented, which is a key step in proving the Hecke algebra isomorphism. For reductive groups, this property is proved in
[
2
, §2]
, where the authors remarked that the proof should also extend to covers of reductive groups. The proof relies heavily on the Bernstein decomposition, which was established for covering groups in
[
10
]
. We then follow the framework laid out for reductive groups in
[
2
]
to complete the proof.

Our main result is stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

( For a precise version, see Theorem
7.1
)
Let
l
be an integer such that
l\geq 1
. Then, there exists an integer
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with
m\geq l
such that, for any non-archimedean local field
F^{\prime}
which is
m
-close to
F
, we have the following algebra isomorphism

\displaystyle\Phi_{l}:\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F),K_{l}^{*})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\widetilde{G}(F^{\prime}),K_{l}^{\prime*}).

By combining this genuine Hecke algebra isomorphism with the equivalence of categories established in Section 8, we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
F
and
F^{\prime}
be sufficiently close local fields, and let
l
be as in Theorem
1.1
. Then, we have the following bijection:

\displaystyle\{\text{ Irreducible genuine $\mathbb{C}$-representations }(\pi,V)\text{ of }\widetilde{G}(F)\text{ such that }\pi^{K_{l}^{*}}\neq 0\}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\{\text{ Irreducible genuine $\mathbb{C}$- representations }(\pi^{\prime},V^{\prime})\text{ of }\widetilde{G}(F^{\prime})\text{ such that }\pi^{\prime K_{l}^{\prime*}}\neq 0\}.

This result allows us to study the representations of the
n
-fold metaplectic cover of
\mathrm{SL_{2}}
over close local fields.

Notations

2.1.

Let us fix a uniformizer
t
of
F
, and the valuation
v
of
F
is normalized so that
v(t)=1
. The Hilbert symbol of degree
n
is denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{F}
. Let
p
be the residue characteristic of

and let

be the cardinality of the residue field. Since
(n,p)=1
, the Hilbert symbol for any

can be calculated using the following formula, given in

(2.1)

\displaystyle(a,b)_{F}=\omega\Big((-1)^{v(a)v(b)}\frac{b^{v(a)}}{a^{v(b)}}\Big)^{\frac{q-1}{n}},

where for
x\in\mathscr{O}^{\times}
with decomposition
x=\omega(x)x^{\prime}\in\mu_{q-1}(F)\times(1+\mathscr{P)}

\omega(x)
denotes the unique
\mu_{q-1}(F)
part of
x
.

2.2.

In
[
13
]
, Kubota provided an explicit construction of the metaplectic group as follows. As a set,

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}(F)=G(F)\times\mu_{n}(F),

and the group operation is defined by
(g,a)(h,b)=(gh,\delta(g,h)ab)
, where
\delta\in H^{2}(G(F),\mu_{n}(F))
is Kubota’s cocycle.

The cocycle is explicitly given by the formula

(2.2)

\delta(g,h)=\left(\frac{X(gh)}{X(g)},\frac{X(gh)}{X(h)}\right)_{F},

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{F}
denotes the
n
-th Hilbert symbol of
F
, and the function
X
is defined on matrices by

X\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{cases}c&\text{if }c\neq 0,\\
d&\text{if }c=0.\end{cases}

By
[
12
, §3, Thm. 2]
, the Kubota cocycle
\delta
allows for a splitting of
G(\mathscr{O})
. This splitting is a homomorphism
S:G(\mathscr{O})\longrightarrow\widetilde{G}(F)
defined by
g\longmapsto(g,\theta(g))
, where

\theta\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }c=0\text{ or }c\in\mathscr{O}^{\times},\\
(c,d)_{F}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

That is,
\theta(gh)=\theta(g)\theta(h)\delta(g,h)
. For any subgroup
H\subset G(\mathscr{O})
, we set
H^{*}:=S(H)
.
For
h\in H
, we denote the element
S(h)=(h,\theta(h))\in H^{*}
by
h^{*}
.

Consider the congruence subgroup
K_{m}=Ker(G(\mathscr{O})\longrightarrow G(\mathscr{O}/\mathscr{P}^{m}))
in
G(\mathscr{O})
. For
m\geq 1
, if
k=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in K_{m}
, then
d\in 1+\mathscr{P}^{m}
and by the formula (
2.1
), it is clear that
\theta(k)=1
. Hence,
K_{m}^{*}=\{(k,1):k\in K_{m}\}
for
m\geq 1
.

2.3.

The unipotent subgroups of
G(F)
split canonically ( see
[
14
, Prop. 4.1]
,
[
15
, Appendix 1]
) in
\widetilde{G}(F)
. Let
U_{+}
and
U_{-}
denote the upper triangular and lower triangular unipotent subgroups of
G(F)
, respectively.

The canonical splittings for
U_{+}
and
U_{-}
with respect to Kubota’s cocycle are as follows

(2.3)

\displaystyle U_{+}\rightarrow\widetilde{G}(F),\hskip 14.22636ptu_{+}(x):=\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\tilde{u}_{+}(x):=(u_{+}(x),1)

and,

(2.4)

\displaystyle U_{-}\rightarrow\widetilde{G}(F),\hskip 14.22636ptu_{-}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
x&1\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\tilde{u}_{-}(x):=(u_{-}(x),1).

It is straightforward to verify that (
2.3
) defines a splitting for the unipotent upper triangular subgroup
U_{+}
. Thus, it remains only to show that (
2.4
) yields a splitting for the unipotent lower triangular subgroup
U_{-}
. To prove this, it suffices to show that
\delta(u_{-}(x),u_{-}(y))=1
for all
x,y\in F
. If either
x=0
or
y=0
, the result is immediate. Hence, we may assume that both
x
and
y
are nonzero.
We have

u_{-}(x)u_{-}(y)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
x+y&1\end{pmatrix}.

First, suppose that
x+y=0
. Then, by the formula (
2.2
)

\delta(u_{-}(x),u_{-}(y))=\left(\frac{1}{x},\frac{1}{y}\right)_{F}=\left(\frac{1}{x},-\frac{1}{x}\right)_{F}=1.

The last equality follows from
[
16
, Prop. 3.2(v), §3, Ch. V]
.

Now assume that
x+y\neq 0
. In this case,

\delta(u_{-}(x),u_{-}(y))=\left(\frac{x+y}{x},\frac{x+y}{y}\right)_{F}=\left(\frac{x}{x+y},\frac{y}{x+y}\right)_{F}=\left(\frac{x}{x+y},1-\frac{x}{x+y}\right)_{F}=1.

Here, we have used the fact that
(a,b)_{F}=(a^{-1},b^{-1})_{F}
, which is clear from the formula (
2.1
) and the last equality again follows from
[
16
, Prop. 3.2(v), §3, Ch. V]
.
This completes the proof.

2.4.

For
x\in F^{*}
, we define the elements

\displaystyle w(x)=u_{+}(x)u_{-}(-x^{-1})u_{+}(x),

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}(x)=w(x)w(-1)=\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&x^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Using the canonical liftings of
U_{+}
and
U_{-}
from Section
2.3
, we choose the preferred liftings of
w(x)
and
h(x)
as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{w}(x)=\tilde{u}_{+}(x)\tilde{u}_{-}(-x^{-1})\tilde{u}_{+}(x)=(w(x),1),

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{0}(x)=\tilde{w}(x)\tilde{w}(-1)=(\lambda_{0}(x),(-1,x)_{F}).

Proof.

By definition, the element
\tilde{w}(x)
is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{w}(x)=\big(\omega(x),\delta(\tilde{u}_{+}(x),\tilde{u}_{-}(-x^{-1}))\,\delta(\tilde{u}_{+}(x)\tilde{u}_{-}(-x^{-1}),\tilde{u}_{+}(x))\big).

We first evaluate the individual cocycle terms. For the first factor, an immediate calculation yields

\delta\big(\tilde{u}_{+}(x),\tilde{u}_{-}(-x^{-1})\big)=(-x^{-1},1)_{F}=1.

For the second factor, expressing the arguments explicitly as matrices, we have

\delta\big(\tilde{u}_{+}(x)\tilde{u}_{-}(-x^{-1}),\tilde{u}_{+}(x)\big)=\delta\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&x\\
-x^{-1}&1\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)=(1,-x^{-1})_{F}=1.

Combining these evaluations, we obtain
\tilde{w}(x)=(\omega(x),1)
.

Similarly, for the element
\tilde{\lambda}_{0}(x)
, we have

\tilde{\lambda}_{0}(x)=\big(\lambda_{0}(x),\delta(\omega(x),\omega(-1))\big).

Evaluating this final cocycle term yields

\delta(\omega(x),\omega(-1))=\delta\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&x\\
-x^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\right)=(-1,x^{-1})_{F}=(-1,x)_{F},

where we used the fact that
(-1,x^{-1})_{F}=(-1,x)_{F}
. It follows that

\tilde{\lambda}_{0}(x)=\big(\lambda_{0}(x),(-1,x)_{F}\big),

which completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle=(-1,t)_{F}^{k}\,(t,t)_{F}\,(t,t)_{F}^{2}\cdots(t,t)_{F}^{k-1}

3.
Cartan Decomposition

Let
T\subset G(F)
be the maximal split torus consisting of diagonal matrices, and let
B\subset G(F)
be the Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices containing
T
. Denote the characters by
X^{*}(T)
and the cocharacters by
X_{*}(T)
. Then,
X^{*}(T)\cong\mathbb{Z}\chi_{{}_{0}}
, where
\chi_{{}_{0}}(\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&x^{-1}\end{pmatrix})=x
and
X_{*}(T)\cong\mathbb{Z}\lambda_{0}
, where
\lambda_{0}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}x&0\\
0&x^{-1}\end{pmatrix}
. The set of roots associated with
(T,B)
is
\phi=\{\pm 2\chi\}
. Since
\langle\chi_{{}_{0}},\lambda_{0}\rangle=1
, the set of dominant coweights is given by

\displaystyle\Lambda^{+}

\displaystyle=\{\lambda\in X_{*}(T):\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle\geq 0\hskip 5.69046pt\forall\alpha\in\phi^{+}\}

\displaystyle=\{k\cdot\lambda_{0}:k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}.

The group
G(F)
has the following Cartan decomposition:

\displaystyle G(F)

\displaystyle=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}}G(\mathscr{O})\lambda(t^{-1})G(\mathscr{O)}.

\displaystyle=\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}G(\mathscr{O})\lambda_{0}(t)^{-k}G(\mathscr{O)}.

From now on, we are going to denote
\tilde{\lambda}_{0}(t)^{-k}
by
\tilde{t}_{k}
.

Remark 3.1
.

\widetilde{G}(F)=\underset{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}{\bigsqcup}\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})\tilde{t}_{k}\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})
as observed in
[
17
, Section 2.3]
.

Proof. Let
(g,\zeta)\in\widetilde{G}(F)
. The Cartan Decomposition of
G(F)
yields
g=g_{1}t_{k}g_{2}
for some
g_{1},g_{2}\in G(\mathscr{O})
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Say,

\displaystyle(g_{1},1)\tilde{t}_{k}(g_{2},1)=(g,\eta),\hskip 5.69046pt\textit{ where}\hskip 5.69046pt\eta\in\mu_{n}(F).

Then,
(g,\zeta)=(g,\eta)(1,\eta^{-1}\zeta)=(g_{1},1)\tilde{t}_{k}(g_{2},\eta^{-1}\zeta)\in\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})\tilde{t}_{k}\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})
.

Suppose there exists an element
(g,\zeta)
such that
(g,\zeta)\in\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})\tilde{t}_{k}\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})\cap\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})\tilde{t}_{r}\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})
, for some
k\neq r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. That will give us that
g\in G(\mathscr{O})t_{k}G(\mathscr{O})\cap G(\mathscr{O})t_{r}G(\mathscr{O})
, a contradiction. Hence, the union is disjoint.

Proposition 3.2
.

\widetilde{G}(F)
has the Cartan decomposition

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}(F)=\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\\
\zeta\in\mu_{n}(F)\end{subarray}}G(\mathscr{O})^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}\zeta G(\mathscr{O})^{*}.

Proof.

The disjointness of the union is implicit in
[
14
, Theorem 9.2]
, and we adopt a similar approach here.

Any element in the double coset
\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})\tilde{t}_{k}\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})
can be expressed as
g_{1}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}\zeta g_{2}^{*}
for some
\zeta\in\mu_{n}(F)
and
g_{i}^{*}=(g_{i},\theta(g_{i}))\in G(\mathscr{O})^{*}
. This yields the decomposition

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})\tilde{t}_{k}\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})=\bigcup_{\zeta\in\mu_{n}(F)}G(\mathscr{O})^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}\zeta G(\mathscr{O})^{*}.

Consequently, by Remark
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}(F)=\bigsqcup_{k\geq 0}\left(\bigcup_{\zeta\in\mu_{n}(F)}G(\mathscr{O})^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}\zeta G(\mathscr{O})^{*}\right).

In the following steps, we prove that for a fixed coweight
k
, the union over
\mu_{n}(F)
is disjoint.

Step 1:

For a fixed
k
, consider the subgroup

(3.1)

\displaystyle S_{k}:=G(\mathscr{O})\cap t_{k}G(\mathscr{O})t_{k}^{-1}.

Every element
g\in S_{k}
can be written as
g=t_{k}g_{0}t_{k}^{-1}
for a unique
g_{0}\in G(\mathscr{O})
. We define a map

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}:S_{k}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mu_{n}(F)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\longmapsto\zeta,

where
\zeta
is the unique element in
\mu_{n}(F)
satisfying the relation
g^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}=\tilde{t}_{k}g_{0}^{*}\zeta
.

To see that
\Psi_{k}
is a group homomorphism, let
g,h\in S_{k}
with
\Psi_{k}(g)=\zeta_{1}
and
\Psi_{k}(h)=\zeta_{2}
. A direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle(gh)^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}

\displaystyle=g^{*}h^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}=g^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}h_{0}^{*}\zeta_{2}=\tilde{t}_{k}g_{0}^{*}h_{0}^{*}\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}=\tilde{t}_{k}(g_{0}h_{0})^{*}\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}.

This implies
\Psi_{k}(gh)=\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}
, confirming the homomorphism property.

Step 2:

The homomorphism
\Psi_{k}
is trivial if and only if the double cosets
G(\mathscr{O})^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}\zeta G(\mathscr{O})^{*}
and
G(\mathscr{O})^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}\zeta^{\prime}G(\mathscr{O})^{*}
are disjoint for all pairs of distinct central elements
\zeta\neq\zeta^{\prime}
in
\mu_{n}(F)
.

Suppose these double cosets are mutually disjoint for distinct central elements. For any
g=t_{k}g_{0}t_{k}^{-1}\in S_{k}
, the definition in (
3.2
) implies

\displaystyle g^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}\in G(\mathscr{O})^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}G(\mathscr{O})^{*}\cap G(\mathscr{O})^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}\Psi_{k}(g)G(\mathscr{O})^{*}.

The disjointness assumption forces
\Psi_{k}(g)=1
, meaning
\Psi_{k}
is trivial.

Conversely, assume
\Psi_{k}
is trivial, and suppose for contradiction that there exist distinct elements
\zeta\neq\zeta^{\prime}
such that

G(\mathscr{O})^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}\zeta G(\mathscr{O})^{*}\cap G(\mathscr{O})^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}\zeta^{\prime}G(\mathscr{O})^{*}\neq\varnothing.

Then there exist elements
g_{1},g_{2},h_{1},h_{2}\in G(\mathscr{O})
satisfying
g_{1}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}\zeta g_{2}^{*}=h_{1}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}\zeta^{\prime}h_{2}^{*}
, which can be rearranged into

\displaystyle g^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}=\tilde{t}_{k}h^{*}\zeta^{\prime}\zeta^{-1},

for some
g^{*},h^{*}\in G(\mathscr{O})^{*}
. It follows that
\Psi_{k}(g)=\zeta^{\prime}\zeta^{-1}\neq 1
, contradicting the triviality of
\Psi_{k}
.

Step 3:

We finally show that the homomorphism
\Psi_{k}
is identically trivial.

The group
S_{k}
is generated by its unipotent intersections, namely
U_{+}\cap S_{k}
and
U_{-}\cap S_{k}
. Therefore, it suffices to check that
\Psi_{k}
restricts to the identity on both
U_{+}\cap S_{k}
and
U_{-}\cap S_{k}
. This triviality follows directly from the relations established in Section
2.6
.
∎

4.
Hecke Algebra

Fix a faithful embedding
\varepsilon:\mu_{n}(F)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. We define the genuine Hecke algebra with respect to the congruence subgroup
K_{m}^{*}
for the covering group
\widetilde{G}(F)
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F),K_{m}^{*}):=\left\{f:\widetilde{G}(F)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}\,:\,\begin{aligned} &\bullet\ \text{$f$ is locally constant and compactly supported},\\
&\bullet\ f(\zeta g)=\varepsilon(\zeta)f(g),\quad\forall\zeta\in\mu_{n}(F),\ g\in\widetilde{G}(F),\\
&\bullet\ f(k_{1}gk_{2})=f(g),\quad\forall k_{1},k_{2}\in K_{m}^{*},\ g\in\widetilde{G}(F).\end{aligned}\right\}.

The multiplication is given by the convolution of functions; that is,

\displaystyle(f*h)(x)=\int_{\widetilde{G}(F)}f(y)h(y^{-1}x)dy,

where
dy
is the Haar measure of the group
\widetilde{G}(F)
, which is normalized such that the measure of
\mu_{n}(F)\times K_{m}^{*}
is
1
.

Define

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{o}:=\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 0\end{subarray}}G(\mathscr{O})^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}G(\mathscr{O})^{*}.

The union is disjoint by Proposition
3.2
. Let
X(m)
be the discrete set of
K_{m}^{*}
double cosets
K_{m}^{*}\backslash\widetilde{G}_{o}/K_{m}^{*}
. For each
K_{m}^{*}gK_{m}^{*}\in X(m)
, define the functions

(4.1)

\psi_{K_{m}^{*}gK_{m}^{*}}(h)=\begin{cases}\varepsilon(\zeta),&\text{ if }h\in K_{m}^{*}g\zeta K_{m}^{*},\text{ for some }\zeta\in\mu_{n}(F),\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proposition 4.1
.

The functions
\{\psi_{K_{m}^{*}gK_{m}^{*}}\}_{K_{m}^{*}gK_{m}^{*}\in X(m)}
form a basis for the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F),K_{m}^{*})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F),K_{m}^{*})
. Since
f
is
K_{m}^{*}
-bi-invariant, its support is stable under left and right multiplication by
K_{m}^{*}
; that is,

\displaystyle\text{Supp}(f)=K_{m}^{*}\text{Supp}(f)K_{m}^{*}=\cup_{x\in\text{Supp}(f)}K_{m}^{*}xK_{m}^{*}.

Since
\text{Supp}(f)
is compact, there are
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\in\text{Supp}(f)
such that
\text{Supp}(f)=\cup_{i=1}^{n}K_{m}^{*}x_{i}K_{m}^{*}
. We can choose these double cosets
K_{m}^{*}x_{i}K_{m}^{*}
to be distinct. Also, for any
\zeta\in\mu_{n}(F)
, we have
f(\zeta g)=\varepsilon(\zeta)f(g)
. Hence, if
K_{m}^{*}gK_{m}^{*}
belongs to
\text{Supp}(f)
, then
K_{m}^{*}gK_{m}^{*}\zeta
also belongs to
\text{Supp}(f)
for all
\zeta\in\mu_{n}(F)
. Therefore,
\text{Supp}(f)=\cup_{i=1}^{m}\cup_{\zeta\in\mu_{n}(F)}K_{m}^{*}y_{i}K_{m}^{*}\zeta
, where each
y_{i}
belongs to
\widetilde{G}_{o}
. Thus,

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(y_{i})\psi_{K_{m}^{*}y_{i}K_{m}^{*}}.

Now, since distinct double cosets in
X(m)
are disjoint, the supports of the functions
\psi_{K_{m}^{*}gK_{m}^{*}}
are disjoint. Therefore, the family
\{\psi_{K_{m}^{*}gK_{m}^{*}}\}_{K_{m}^{*}gK_{m}^{*}\in X(m)}
is linearly independent.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

The Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F),K_{m}^{*})
is a finitely presented
\mathbb{C}
-algebra.

It has been remarked in
[
2
, §2]
that the proof of the Hecke algebra being finitely presented for connected reductive groups will also work for its central extensions. We fill in the details here. Before providing a sketch of the proof, we introduce some preliminary notations and definitions.

Let
\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G}(F))
denote the category of smooth genuine representations of
\widetilde{G}(F)
, and let
\mathfrak{Z}
be the center of the abelian category
\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G}(F))
.

Parabolic subgroups

\widetilde{P}
in
\widetilde{G}(F)
are precisely the inverse images of parabolic subgroups
P
in
G
. Let
P=L\ltimes U
be a parabolic subgroup where
L
is the Levi subgroup and
U
is the unipotent radical. Then
U
splits canonically in
\widetilde{G}(F)
, yielding

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}=\widetilde{L}\ltimes U.

Let
\delta_{P}
be the modulus character of
P
. Then
\delta_{\widetilde{P}}:=\delta_{P}\circ\pi
is the modulus character of
\widetilde{P}
.

Following the formulation in
[
4
, Section 1.8]
(see also
[
10
, §3]
), we can define the normalized induction functor
\iota_{\widetilde{P}}^{\widetilde{G}(F)}
and the Jacquet functor
r_{\widetilde{P}}^{\widetilde{G}(F)}
for the covering group
\widetilde{G}(F)
. Additionally, these functors satisfy the necessary hypotheses for the geometric lemma
[
4
, Theorem 5.2]
. For a detailed study of these functors on coverings of the symplectic groups, one can refer to the work of Hanzer and Muić
[
8
]
.

Following the standard definition in the linear case
[
18
, §IV.2]
, a genuine representation
W
of
\widetilde{G}(F)
is said to be
supercuspidal
if its Jacquet module
W_{U}
vanishes for every proper parabolic subgroup
\widetilde{P}=\widetilde{L}U
of
\widetilde{G}(F)
.

Let
G(F)^{\circ}=\{g\in G(F):\text{val}_{F}(\chi(g))=0,\kern 5.0pt\forall\chi\in\text{Hom}_{F}(G,G_{m})\}
. Let
\widetilde{G}(F)^{\circ}
be the inverse image of
G(F)^{\circ}
. Then the group
X_{\mathrm{nr}}(\widetilde{G}(F))
of
unramified characters
of
\widetilde{G}(F)
is defined by

X_{\mathrm{nr}}(\widetilde{G}(F))=\mathrm{Hom}(\widetilde{G}(F)/\widetilde{G}(F)^{\circ},\mathbb{C}^{\times}).

As noted in
[
10
, §3]
, Harish-Chandra’s characterization theorem for supercuspidal representations
[
3
, 3.20–3.24]
also remains valid for
\widetilde{G}(F)
.

Let
\mathrm{Irr}(\widetilde{G}(F))
denote the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible representations of
\widetilde{G}(F)
, and let
\mathrm{Irr}(\widetilde{G}(F))_{\mathrm{sc}}\subset\mathrm{Irr}(\widetilde{G}(F))
denote the subset consisting of supercuspidal representations.

Let
\widetilde{L}
be a Levi subgroup of
\widetilde{G}(F)
(possibly
\widetilde{L}=\widetilde{G}(F)
), and let
\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathrm{Irr}(\widetilde{G}(F))_{\mathrm{sc}}
. The pair
(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})
is called a
supercuspidal pair
.

Now let
(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})
and
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{\tau})
be supercuspidal pairs. We say that
(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})
and
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{\tau})
are
conjugate
if

\widetilde{M}={}^{g}\widetilde{L}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{\tau}\cong\chi{\widetilde{\rho}}^{g},

for some
g\in\widetilde{G}(F)
and
\chi\in X_{nr}(\widetilde{G}(F))
.
This defines an equivalence relation on the set of all supercuspidal pairs. We denote the equivalence class of
(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})
by
[\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho}]
, and write
\mathfrak{B}(\widetilde{G}(F))
for the set of all equivalence classes of supercuspidal pairs.

The Bernstein block

\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G}(F)){(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})}
is the subcategory of
\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G}(F))
formed by representations
W
that embed into a direct sum of representations
\iota_{\widetilde{P}}^{\widetilde{G}(F)}(W_{\widetilde{P}})
taken over all parabolic subgroups
\widetilde{P}
with Levi component
\widetilde{L}
, where each irreducible subquotient of
W_{\widetilde{P}}
is isomorphic to
\chi\widetilde{\rho}
for some
\chi\in X_{nr}(\widetilde{G}(F))
.

We denote the center of the category
\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G}(F)){(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})}
by
\mathfrak{Z}{(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})}
.

Let
(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})
be a supercuspidal pair. For each element
w\in N(\widetilde{L})/\widetilde{L}
, choose a representative
n\in N(\widetilde{L})
. We define the conjugate representation
w\cdot\widetilde{\rho}
of
\widetilde{L}
by

(w\cdot\widetilde{\rho})(l)=\widetilde{\rho}(n^{-1}ln),\qquad l\in\widetilde{L}.

The relative Weyl group
W(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})
associated with the pair is defined as:

W(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})=\{\,w\in N(\widetilde{L})/\widetilde{L}\mid w\cdot\widetilde{\rho}=\widetilde{\rho}\,\}.

A representation
V
of
\widetilde{G}(F)
is called
admissible
if, for every open compact subgroup
\widetilde{K}\subset\widetilde{G}(F)
, the space of
\widetilde{K}
-fixed vectors

V^{\widetilde{K}}=\{\,v\in V:kv=v\text{ for all }k\in\widetilde{K}\,\}
is finite-dimensional. A representation
V
of
\widetilde{G}(F)
is said to be of finite type if there exists a finite collection of vectors
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset V
such that the set
\{\,gv_{i}:g\in\widetilde{G}(F),\ i\in I\,\}
spans
V
as a complex vector space.

Let
B
be a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, and let
V
be a
B
-module equipped with an action of
\widetilde{G}(F)
. The representation
V
is called
B
-admissible if, for every open compact subgroup
\widetilde{K}\subset\widetilde{G}(F)
, the space
V^{\widetilde{K}}
of
\widetilde{K}
-fixed vectors is a
B
-module of finite type. The representation
V
is said to be of
B
-finite type if there exists a finite family of vectors
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset V
such that
\{\,gv_{i}:g\in\widetilde{G}(F),\ i\in I\,\}
generates
V
as a
B
-module.

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
4.2

The proof relies on the following three results:

(i)

(Bernstein decomposition)
\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G}(F))=\underset{(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})\in\mathfrak{B}(\widetilde{G}(F))}{\bigoplus}\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G}(F)){(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})}.

(ii)

\mathfrak{Z}{(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})}\cong\text{ Ring of regular functions on }\widetilde{\rho}/W(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})
.

(iii)

Every representation of
\widetilde{G}(F)
of finite type is
\mathfrak{Z}
- admissible.

We briefly explain why these three results imply that the Hecke algebra is finitely presented.

By (i), we obtain the decomposition

\mathfrak{Z}=\bigoplus_{(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})\in\mathfrak{B}(\widetilde{G}(F))}\mathfrak{Z}{(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})}.

Let
L_{c}^{\infty}(\widetilde{G}(F)/K_{m}^{*})
denote the space of compactly supported genuine functions on
\widetilde{G}(F)
that are right
K_{m}^{*}
-invariant. The group
\widetilde{G}(F)
acts on
L_{c}^{\infty}(\widetilde{G}(F)/K_{m}^{*})
via the regular representation. Moreover, this representation is generated by a single element, namely the function
\psi_{K_{m}^{*}}
.

By (iii), the representation
L_{c}^{\infty}(\widetilde{G}(F)/K_{m}^{*})
is
\mathfrak{Z}
-admissible. Consequently,

L_{c}^{\infty}(\widetilde{G}(F)/K_{m}^{*})^{K_{m}^{*}}=\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F),K_{m}^{*})

is a finitely generated
\mathfrak{Z}
-module. Hence, only finitely many Bernstein blocks
\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G}(F)){(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})}
act nontrivially on
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F),K_{m}^{*})
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{Z}
acts on
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F),K_{m}^{*})
via the finite quotient
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{Z}{(\widetilde{L}_{i},\widetilde{\rho}_{i})}.

By (ii), each algebra
\mathfrak{Z}{(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})}
is the coordinate ring of an affine variety. In particular, every
\mathfrak{Z}{(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{C}
-algebra. It follows that
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{Z}{(\widetilde{L}_{i},\widetilde{\rho}_{i})}
is also a finitely generated
\mathbb{C}
-algebra. Hence, by Hilbert, this algebra is finitely presented over
\mathbb{C}
.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F),K_{m}^{*})
is a finitely generated module over the finitely presented algebra
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{Z}{(\widetilde{L}_{i},\widetilde{\rho}_{i})}
, we conclude that
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F),K_{m}^{*})
is itself a finitely presented
\mathbb{C}
-algebra.

The proof of (i) is established in
[
10
, Theorem 3.9]
. For (ii), the proof for reductive groups given in
[
2
, §2]
relies on the geometric lemma for induction and Jacquet functors, alongside technical results from
[
2
, §1]
. Since Section 1 of
[
2
]
holds for arbitrary locally compact topological groups, and the geometric lemma extends to central extensions of reductive groups, the same argument remains valid for central extensions. The proof of (iii) is identical to the reductive group case proved in
[
2
, §3]
.

Remark 5.6
.

For
m\geq 1
, the Kubota cocycle does not satisfy the property

\delta(K_{m}gK_{m},\,K_{m}hK_{m})=\delta(g,h)

for all
g,h\in\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that there exist
g\in\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})
and
k\in K_{m}
such that

\delta(g^{-1},gk)\neq\delta(g^{-1},g).

Suppose for some
g\in\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})
and
k\in K_{m}
, we have that
\delta(g^{-1},gk)=\delta(g^{-1},g)
. Then, from the
2
-cocycle relation,

\delta(g^{-1},g)\delta(1,k)=\delta(g,k)\delta(g^{-1},gk),

we obtain that

\displaystyle\delta(g,k)=\delta(1,k)=1.

Therefore, it is enough to show that there exist elements
g\in G(\mathscr{O})
and
k\in K_{m}
such that
\delta(g,k)\neq 1
.
Now consider the elements

\displaystyle g=\begin{pmatrix}u&0\\
0&u^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\,\quad\text{ and }\quad k=\begin{pmatrix}1+t^{m}x&t^{m}y\\
t^{m}w&1+t^{m}z\end{pmatrix},

with
u\in\mathscr{O}^{\times}
,
x,y,z,w\in\mathscr{O}
, and
w\neq 0.

For these elements, we have

X(g)=u^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad X(k)=t^{m}w.

Multiplying
g
and
k
gives

gk=\begin{pmatrix}*&*\\
u^{-1}t^{m}w&*\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore,
X(gk)=u^{-1}t^{m}w.
Hence,

\delta(g,k)=\left(\frac{u^{-1}t^{m}w}{u^{-1}},\frac{u^{-1}t^{m}w}{t^{m}w}\right)_{F}=(t^{m}w,u^{-1})_{F}.

Write
w=t^{r}v
with
v\in\mathscr{O}^{\times}
. Then

\displaystyle\delta(g,k)

\displaystyle=(t^{m+r}v,u^{-1})_{F}=(t,u^{-1})_{F}^{\,m+r}=\omega(u^{-1})^{\frac{(q-1)(m+r)}{n}}.

Finally, we can choose
r,u
in such a way that
\omega(u^{-1})^{\frac{(q-1)(m+r)}{n}}\neq 1
, which yields the desired result.
∎

6.
Linear Isomorphism of Hecke Algebras over two Close Local Fields

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and for the local field
F
with the ring of integers
\mathscr{O}
, we introduce the following notation:

(1)

\widetilde{G}_{k}:=\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})\tilde{t}_{k}\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})

(2)

discrete set of
K_{m}^{*}
double cosets of
\widetilde{G}_{k}

The group
\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})\times\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})
acts transitively on
\widetilde{G}_{k}
via

(g_{1},g_{2})\cdot h\mapsto g_{1}hg_{2}^{-1}.

Consequently, the group
\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})/K_{m}^{*}\times\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})/K_{m}^{*}
acts transitively on
X_{k}(m)
by

(K_{m}^{*}g_{1},K_{m}^{*}g_{2})\cdot K_{m}^{*}hK_{m}^{*}\mapsto K_{m}^{*}g_{1}hg_{2}^{-1}K_{m}^{*}.

We denote by
\Gamma_{k}(m)
the stabilizer of
K_{m}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}K_{m}^{*}
under this action.

Similarly, for the local field
F^{\prime}
which is
m
-close to
F
, we will use the notations
K_{m}^{\prime},\widetilde{G}_{k}^{\prime},X_{k}^{\prime}(m),\Gamma_{k}^{\prime}(m)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

The stabilizer of
K_{m}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}K_{m}^{*}
is

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}(m)=\Big\{(K_{m}^{*}g,K_{m}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}g\tilde{t}_{k}):g\in\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})\cap\tilde{t}_{k}\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}\Big/K_{m}^{*}\cap\tilde{t}_{k}K_{m}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}\Big\}.

Proof.

If
h\in\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})\cap\tilde{t}_{k}\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}
, then

\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}h\tilde{t}_{k}\in\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})
. Hence, it is immediate that

(K_{m}^{*}h,\,K_{m}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}h\tilde{t}_{k})\in\Gamma_{k}(m).

Conversely, suppose
(K_{m}^{*}g,K_{m}^{*}h)\in\Gamma_{k}(m)
. Then

K_{m}^{*}g\tilde{t}_{k}h^{-1}K_{m}^{*}=K_{m}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}K_{m}^{*}.

Therefore, there exist
k_{1},k_{2}\in K_{m}^{*}
such that

g\tilde{t}_{k}h^{-1}=k_{1}\tilde{t}_{k}k_{2}.

Equivalently,

x:=k_{1}^{-1}g=\tilde{t}_{k}k_{2}h\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}\in\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})\cap\tilde{t}_{k}\widetilde{G}(\mathscr{O})\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}.

It follows that

K_{m}^{*}g=K_{m}^{*}x\quad\text{and}\quad K_{m}^{*}h=K_{m}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}x\tilde{t}_{k},

where

.

∎

Lemma 6.2
.

\displaystyle\longmapsto(K_{m}^{\prime*}g^{\prime},K_{m}^{\prime*}(\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}g\tilde{t}_{k})^{\prime})

We have

Hence, for any

(6.1)

\displaystyle g=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{u}_{\alpha}(x_{i})\zeta,\quad\text{ with }\alpha\in\{\pm 1\},

and

(6.2)

\displaystyle\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}g\tilde{t}_{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{u}_{\alpha}(t^{2\alpha k}x_{i})\zeta.

5.1

\displaystyle g^{\prime}=k_{1}^{\prime}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{u}_{\alpha}(x_{i}^{\prime})\phi(\zeta)\quad\text{ and }\quad(\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}g\tilde{t}_{k})^{\prime}=k_{2}^{\prime}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{u}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime 2\alpha k}x_{i}^{\prime})\phi(\zeta),

for some

\displaystyle(\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}g\tilde{t}_{k})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=k_{2}^{\prime}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{u}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime 2\alpha k}x_{i}^{\prime})\phi(\zeta)=k_{2}^{\prime}\tilde{t^{\prime}_{k}}^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{u}_{\alpha}(x_{i}^{\prime})\tilde{t^{\prime}_{k}}\phi(\zeta)=k_{2}^{\prime}\tilde{t^{\prime}_{k}}^{-1}k_{1}^{\prime-1}g^{\prime}\tilde{t^{\prime}_{k}}.

\Phi_{m}\times\Phi_{m}

To prove surjectivity, consider an element

(K_{m}^{\prime*}h^{\prime},K_{m}^{\prime*}\tilde{t^{\prime}_{k}}^{-1}h^{\prime}\tilde{t^{\prime}_{k}})\in{\Gamma}_{k}^{\prime}(m).

Write

\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}h\tilde{t}_{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{u}_{\alpha}(t^{2\alpha k}y_{i})\zeta\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{t^{\prime}_{k}}^{-1}h^{\prime}\tilde{t^{\prime}_{k}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{u}_{\alpha}(t^{\prime 2\alpha k}y_{i}^{\prime})\eta.

7.
Kazhdan Isomorphism

Theorem 7.1

There exists an integer

is an isomorphism of algebras.
Here, the metaplectic groups

and

Lemma 7.2
.

Let

For a positive integer

\displaystyle K_{l}^{*}=\prod_{\alpha\in\{\pm 1\}}\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,l}\times\widetilde{T}_{l}.

Then,

\displaystyle\tilde{t}_{k}K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}=\underset{\alpha\in\{\pm 1\}}{\prod}\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l-2\alpha k}\times\widetilde{T_{l}}\quad\textit{and,}\quad\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}=\underset{\alpha\in\{\pm 1\}}{\prod}\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l+2\alpha k}\times\widetilde{T_{l}}.

\tilde{t}_{k}{K_{m_{k}}^{*}}\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1},\,\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}{K_{m_{k}}^{*}}\tilde{t}_{k}\subset K_{l}^{*}
. Finally, for

g{K_{{}_{m}}^{*}}g^{-1}\subset K_{l}^{*}
and
g^{-1}{K_{{}_{m}}^{*}}g\subset K_{l}^{*}
for all

.
∎

Lemma 7.7
.

If for any
g,h\in\widetilde{G}(F)
, we have

\displaystyle Vol(K_{l}^{*}gK_{l}^{*})Vol(K_{l}^{*}hK_{l}^{*})=Vol(K_{l}^{*}ghK_{l}^{*}),

\displaystyle\psi_{{}_{K_{l}^{*}gK_{l}^{*}}}*\psi_{{}_{K_{l}^{*}hK_{l}^{*}}}=\psi_{{}_{K_{l}^{*}ghK_{l}^{*}}}.

Proof.

Write
K_{m}^{*}gK_{m}^{*}
as a union of
K_{m}^{*}
-cosets

\displaystyle K_{m}^{*}gK_{m}^{*}=\underset{k}{\bigcup}kgK_{m}^{*}.

Suppose
\delta_{x}
is the point mass function at

\displaystyle\psi_{{}_{K_{m}^{*}gK_{m}^{*}}}=\underset{k}{\sum}\delta_{kg}*\psi_{{}_{K_{m}^{*}}}.

In a similar way, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{{}_{K_{m}^{*}hK_{m}^{*}}}=\underset{k^{\prime}}{\sum}\delta_{k^{\prime}h}*\psi_{{}_{K_{m}^{*}}}.

Now, in the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F),K_{m}^{*})

\psi_{{}_{K_{m}^{*}}}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{{}_{K_{m}^{*}gK_{m}^{*}}}*\psi_{{}_{K_{m}^{*}hK_{m}^{*}}}

\displaystyle=(\underset{k}{\sum}\delta_{kg}*\psi_{{}_{K_{m}^{*}}})*(\underset{k^{\prime}}{\sum}\delta_{k^{\prime}h}*\psi_{{}_{K_{m}^{*}}})

\displaystyle=\underset{k,k^{\prime}}{\sum}\delta_{kg}*\psi_{{}_{K_{m}^{*}}}*(\delta_{k^{\prime}h}*\psi_{{}_{K_{m}^{*}}})

\displaystyle=\underset{k,k^{\prime}}{\sum}\delta_{kgk^{\prime}h}*\psi_{{}_{K_{m}^{*}}}.

The rest of the proof then follows from

Lemma 7.8
.

For the congruence subgroup
K_{l}^{*}
of
G(\mathscr{O})^{*}
, we have the following:

i.

For
k,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have
\psi_{{}_{K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}K_{l}^{*}}}*\psi_{{}_{K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{r}K_{l}^{*}}}=\psi_{{}_{K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k+r}K_{l}^{*}}}
.

ii.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in G(\mathscr{O})^{*}
, we have
\psi_{{}_{K_{l}^{*}g_{1}\tilde{t}_{k}g_{2}K_{l}^{*}}}=\psi_{{}_{K_{l}^{*}g_{1}K_{l}^{*}}}*\psi_{{}_{K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}K_{l}^{*}}}*\psi_{{}_{K_{l}^{*}g_{2}K_{l}^{*}}}
.

Proof.

To compute the volume
\text{Vol}(K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}K_{l}^{*})
, we first note that it is determined by the index of the intersection subgroup:

\text{Vol}(K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}K_{l}^{*})=[K_{l}^{*}:\tilde{t}_{k}K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}\cap K_{l}^{*}].

For a positive integer
l
and
\alpha\in\{\pm 1\}
, let
\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l}:=\widetilde{U}_{\alpha}(\mathscr{P}^{l})
and
\widetilde{T}_{l}:=\tilde{\lambda}_{0}(1+\mathscr{P}^{l})
. Applying the Iwahori factorization yields:

K_{l}^{*}=\prod_{\alpha\in\{\pm 1\}}\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l}\times\widetilde{T}_{l}.

Then, we have

\tilde{t}_{k}K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}=\prod_{\alpha\in\{\pm 1\}}\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l-2\alpha k}\times\widetilde{T}_{l}.

This yields the quotient structure:

K_{l}^{*}/\left(\tilde{t}_{k}K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}\cap K_{l}^{*}\right)\cong\prod_{\alpha\in\{\pm 1\}}\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l}/\left(\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l}\cap\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l-2\alpha k}\right).

Taking the cardinality of this quotient yields the index:

[K_{l}^{*}:\tilde{t}_{k}K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}\cap K_{l}^{*}]=\prod_{\alpha\in\{\pm 1\}}[\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l}:\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l}\cap\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l-2\alpha k}].

For
\alpha=1
,
\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l}\subseteq\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l-2\alpha k}
, and for
\alpha=-1
,
\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l-2\alpha k}\subseteq\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l}
. This gives us

\displaystyle[K_{l}^{*}:\tilde{t}_{k}K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}\cap K_{l}^{*}]=[\widetilde{U}_{-1,\,l}:\widetilde{U}_{-1,\,l+2\alpha k}].

There is a bijection between the coset space
\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l}/\widetilde{U}_{\alpha,\,l+2\alpha k}
and
\mathscr{P}^{l}/\mathscr{P}^{l+2\alpha k}
. Hence,

\displaystyle Vol(K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}K_{l}^{*})=[K_{l}^{*}:\tilde{t}_{k}K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}^{-1}\cap K_{l}^{*}]=q^{2\alpha k},

where
q=[\mathscr{O}:\mathscr{P}]
. Therefore, for any
k,r\in\Lambda^{+}
, we have

\displaystyle Vol(K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k}K_{l}^{*})Vol(K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{r}K_{l}^{*})=Vol(K_{l}^{*}\tilde{t}_{k+r}K_{l}^{*}).

The parts
i.
and
ii.
then follow from Lemma
7.7

Corollary 8.3
.

The category
\mathcal{R}^{m}(\widetilde{G}(F))
is closed under sub-quotients and hence the functor

\displaystyle J_{m}:\mathcal{R}^{m}(\widetilde{G}(F))

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F),K_{m}^{*})\text{-mod},

\displaystyle(\sigma,V)

\displaystyle\longmapsto V^{K_{m}^{*}},

is an equivalence of categories with the left adjoint

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}_{m}:\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F),K_{m}^{*})\text{-mod}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{R}^{m}(\widetilde{G}(F)),

\displaystyle V^{K_{m}^{*}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F))\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{G}(F),K_{m}^{*})}V^{K_{m}^{*}}.

Proof.

By the Bernstein decomposition
[
10
, Theorem 3.9]
, the category
\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G}(F))
decomposes as the direct sum

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G}(F))=\bigoplus_{(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})\in\mathfrak{B}(\widetilde{G}(F))}\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G}(F)){(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})},

where
\mathfrak{B}(\widetilde{G}(F))
denotes the set of equivalence classes of supercuspidal pairs from Section

. Let
\Sigma_{m}\subset\mathfrak{B}(\widetilde{G}(F))
be the subset defined by

\displaystyle\Sigma_{m}=\left\{(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})\in\mathfrak{B}(\widetilde{G}(F))\mid\exists W\in\widetilde{\rho}\text{ such that }W^{(K_{m}^{*})_{\widetilde{L}}}\neq 0\right\}.

8.2

\mathcal{R}^{m}(\widetilde{G}(F))
consisting of representations generated by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{m}(\widetilde{G}(F))=\bigoplus_{(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})\in\Sigma_{m}}\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{G}(F)){(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{\rho})}.

According to
[
2
, §3]
, the Bernstein-Zelevinsky finiteness theorem for reductive groups
[
3
, §4]
extends naturally to finite central extensions. Hence, for the compact subgroup

Each Bernstein block
