Sectional Curvature for Kantorovich-Wasserstein and Hellinger-Kantorovich Geometries
Karl-Theodor Sturm

Abstract
. We derive an explicit formula for the sectional curvature of the space ℳ e ​ ( 𝖬 ) {{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}({\sf M}) of finite measures on a Riemannian manifold 𝖬 {\sf M} . The space ℳ e ​ ( 𝖬 ) {{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}({\sf M}) is equipped with the Hellinger-Kantorovich metric 𝖧 ​ 𝖪 {\sf H\!\!K} . Even in the case 𝖬 = ℝ n {\sf M}={\mathbb{R}}^{n} , the curvature is comprised of two parts: the ‘lifted part’ is negative, and the ‘twisted part’ is positive. It will be analyzed in detail for the multidimensional torus. Our general approach to sectional curvature in geodesic spaces also leads to new insights into the curvature of the space 𝒫 2 ​ ( 𝖬 ) \mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf M}) of probability measures on 𝖬 {\sf M} equipped with the Kantorovich-Wasserstein metric 𝖶 2 {\sf{W}}_{2} .



1.
Introduction and summary of main results

We will analyze in detail curvature properties of the space
{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}={{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}({\sf M})
of finite measures on a (Euclidean or Riemannian) space
{\sf M}
. Equipped with the Hellinger-Kantorovich metric
{\sf H\!\!K}
, the space of measures is a geodesic metric space of fundamental importance which has attracted a lot of attention in recent years, both from theoretical perspectives in unbalanced optimal transport problems and from applications, including
computational optimal transport, statistical optimal transport, applications to data science and machine learning,
modeling cellular dynamics, birth-death dynamics for sampling, and
interaction-force transport gradient flows.

Most importantly, the Hellinger-Kantorovich metric admits an intuitive interpretation as a relaxation of the Kantorovich-Wasserstein metric
{\sf{W}}_{2}
with optional creation and annihilation of mass, the cost of the latter being measured in terms of the Kakutani-Hellinger metric.
For a measure
\mu\in{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}
, the ‘analytic tangent space’ is given by the Sobolev space
{\sf T}_{\mu}{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}=H^{1}(\mu)
and the ‘extended tangent space’ is

{\sf T}^{\sf ext}_{\mu}{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}=H^{1}(\mu)\otimes{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}\big({{\sf M}\setminus B_{\pi/2}(\mathrm{supp}[\mu])}\big)
.

a

We will show that for every
\mu\in{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}({\sf M})
and every pair of tangent vectors
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}({\sf M})\subset H^{1}(\mu)
, the sectional curvature is given as

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{3}{\Lambda t^{4}}\Bigg[t^{2}|\varphi-\psi|_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}-{\sf H\!\!K}^{2}\Big(\exp_{\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(t\varphi),\exp_{\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(t\psi)\Big)\Bigg]

with

\Lambda:=|\varphi|_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}\cdot|\psi|_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}-\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}
, and
decomposes
into a lifted and a twisted part

\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi)=\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\uparrow}(\varphi,\psi)+\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\nabla}(\varphi,\psi).

b

The lifted part vanishes if
\dim{\sf M}=1
and otherwise it is given in terms of the sectional curvature of the underlying Riemannian manifold
{\sf M}
:

\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\uparrow}\big(\varphi,\psi\big)=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\int_{{\sf M}}\Big(\mathsf{Sec}^{\sf M}_{x}\big(\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi\big)-1\Big)\cdot\Big[|\nabla\varphi|_{x}^{2}\,|\nabla\psi|_{x}^{2}-\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi\rangle_{x}^{2}\Big]\,d\mu(x).

c

The twisted part is always nonnegative:

\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\uparrow}\big(\varphi,\psi\big)\geq 0.

In the Euclidean case with absolutely continuous
\mu
, it is explicitly given as

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\nabla}(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\Lambda}\,\inf\bigg\{\int_{\sf M}\Big[|F-\nabla\eta|^{2}+4\eta^{2}\Big]\,d\mu:\ \eta\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},\mu)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\frac{12}{\Lambda}\int\Big|\Big(-\Delta_{\mu}(4-\Delta_{\rho})\Big)^{-1/2}\mathrm{div}_{\mu}F\Big|^{2}\,d\mu

with

F:=\frac{1}{2}\big(\nabla^{2}\varphi\nabla\psi-\nabla^{2}\psi\nabla\varphi\big)
. Even for 1-dimensional spaces,

\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\nabla}
does not vanish.

d

In particular for
{\sf M}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, there will be a competition between the two parts:
\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\nabla}(\varphi,\psi)\geq 0
and
\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\uparrow}(\varphi,\psi)\leq 0
.
So far, it was only known that
({{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),{\sf H\!\!K})
for
n\geq 2
does not have nonnegative curvature in the sense of Alexandrov. We show that it also does not have nonpositive curvature in the sense of Alexandrov.

e

More detailed insights are obtained for the multi-dimensional torus
{\sf M}=\mathbb{T}^{n}
and for its normalized volume measure
\mu
. Let
(e_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}
denote the Fourier basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n},\mu)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(e_{k},e_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\,\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}S(k^{\sigma},\ell)

with
k^{\sigma}=(\sigma_{p}k_{p})_{p=1,\ldots,n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and

\displaystyle S(k,\ell)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4+|k|^{2})\,(4+|\ell|^{2})}\bigg(-|k|^{2}\,|\ell|^{2}+\langle k^{2},\ell^{2}\rangle+\frac{3}{4}\,\bigg[|k-\ell|^{2}-\frac{\big(|k|^{2}-|\ell|^{2}\big)^{2}}{4+|k+\ell|^{2}}\bigg]\,\langle k,\ell\rangle^{2}\bigg)

for any pair
k,\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
which is ‘generic’ in the sense that
k_{p}\not=\pm\ell_{p},k_{p}\ell_{p}\not=0
for all
p=1,\ldots,n
.
Moreover,

-\Big(\frac{3}{2}\Big)^{n}\leq\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}\big(e_{k},e_{\ell}\big)\leq\frac{3}{2}\,4^{n}\,\Big(|k|^{2}+|\ell|^{2}\Big)

for arbitrary
k,\ell
with
k\not=\ell
.
In particular, we prove that

•

there are plenty of cases with negative sectional curvature:
if
n\geq 2
,
k,\ell\not=0
, and
k_{p}\ell_{p}=0
(
\forall p=1,\ldots,n
), then

\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(e_{k},e_{\ell})=-\frac{|k|^{2}\,|\ell|^{2}}{(4+|k|^{2})\,(4+|\ell|^{2})}\ \in\ \bigg(-1,-\frac{1}{25}\bigg];

•

in average, the sectional curvature will be positive:
for all sufficiently large
C,L
and all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
with
|k_{1}|,|k_{2}|\geq C
,

\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2},|\ell|\leq L}\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(e_{k},e_{\ell})\geq\frac{1}{1000}\,C^{2}\,L^{2};

•

for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
with sufficiently large
|k_{1}|,|k_{2}|
, the Ricci curvature diverges

\mathsf{Ric}_{\mu}(e_{k},e_{k}):=\|e_{k}\|_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}\cdot\lim_{L\to\infty}\sum_{\ell\in\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2},|\ell|\leq L}\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(e_{k},e_{\ell})=+\infty;

•

for every
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and every
s>2+\frac{n}{2}
, the re-normalized Ricci curvature

\mathsf{Ric}^{(s)}_{\mu}(e_{k},e_{k})

exists and is finite.

f

Our approach to sectional curvature on the space of finite measures is based on the observation that
in the case of a smooth Riemannian manifold
({\sf M},{\sf g})
, the sectional curvature
\mathsf{Sec}^{\sf M}_{z}(v,w)
is given in purely metric terms as

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sec}^{\sf M}_{z}(v,w)

\displaystyle=3\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{t^{2}|v-w|^{2}-{\sf d}^{2}\big(\exp_{z}(tv),\exp_{z}(tw)\big)}{t^{4}\big(|v|^{2}|w|^{2}-\langle v,w\rangle^{2}\big)}

for every
z\in{\sf M}
and every linearly independent pair of tangent vectors
v,w\in{\sf T}_{z}{\sf M}
.
Inspired by this observation, therefore for each geodesic metric space
({\sf X},{\sf d})
, each
z\in{\sf X}
and each ‘independent’ pair of geodesics
\alpha,\beta
emanating from
z
we define

\mathsf{Sec}^{\sf X}_{z}(\alpha,\beta):=3\,\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{t^{2}{\sf d}_{z}^{2}(\alpha,\beta)-{\sf d}^{2}\big(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t}\big)}{t^{4}\big(|\dot{\alpha}|^{2}|\dot{\beta}|^{2}-\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{z}^{2}\big)}

where

{\sf d}_{z}(\alpha,\beta):=\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}{\sf d}(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t})
denotes the directional distance of the geodesics,

|\dot{\alpha}|,|\dot{\beta}|
their metric speed,
and

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{z}:=\frac{1}{2}\big(|\dot{\alpha}|^{2}+|\dot{\beta}|^{2}-{\sf d}_{z}^{2}(\alpha,\beta)\big)
.
One easily verifies that for every complete geodesic space
({\sf X},{\sf d})
,

•

\mathsf{Sec}({\sf X},{\sf d})\geq\kappa\text{ in Alexandrov sense}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\forall z\in{\sf X},\alpha,\beta\in\mathring{\sf T}^{\sf dir}_{z}{\sf X}:\ \mathsf{Sec}_{z}(\alpha,\beta)\geq\kappa,

•

\mathsf{Sec}({\sf X},{\sf d})\leq\lambda\text{ in Alexandrov sense}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\forall z\in{\sf X},\alpha,\beta\in\mathring{\sf T}^{\sf dir}_{z}{\sf X}:\ \mathsf{Sec}_{z}(\alpha,\beta)\leq\lambda.

g

This concept of sectional curvature for geodesic metric spaces will be analyzed in detail for two spaces: the Hellinger-Kantorovich space
({{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}({\sf M}),{\sf H\!\!K})
of finite measures on
{\sf M}

and the Kantorovich-Wasserstein space
(\mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf M}),{\sf{W}}_{2})
of probability measures on
{\sf M}
. In both cases, for convenience
{\sf M}
is assumed to be a smooth Riemannian manifold.

In the latter case, which is significantly easier than the former, we can indeed confirm and extend formal calculations of Lott
[
Lot08
]

who provided a geometric differential calculus approach to sectional curvature in the Kantorovich-Wasserstein space
(\mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf M}),{\sf{W}}_{2})
based on the so-called Otto calculus.
He could already deduce the formulas
for the lifted part

\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\uparrow}(\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi)=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\int_{\sf M}\mathsf{Sec}^{\sf M}_{x}(\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi)\cdot\big[|\nabla\varphi|_{x}^{2}\,|\nabla\psi|_{x}^{2}-\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi\rangle_{x}^{2}\big]\,d\mu(x)

with
\Lambda:=|\nabla\varphi|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}|\nabla\psi|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}-\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}
,
and for the twisted part

\mathsf{Sec}^{\nabla}_{\mu}(\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi)=\frac{3}{\Lambda}\cdot\inf\bigg\{\int_{{\sf M}}\big|\nabla^{2}\psi\nabla\varphi-\nabla\eta\big|^{2}d\mu:\ \eta\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\sf M})\bigg\}

of the sectional curvature on
(\mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf M}),{\sf{W}}_{2})
.

h

The PDE approach of the Otto calculus, however, is limited to absolutely continuous measures
\mu
and to directions
\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi
in the analytic tangent space (which correspond to Monge transport problems) whereas our OT approach also applies to general measures
\mu
and to general directions
P,Q
in the geometric tangent space (which correspond to Kantorovich transport problems).
This, for instance, allows us to prove that

{\mathsf{Sec}}^{\mathscr{P}_{2}}_{\delta_{z}}(P,Q)\leq 0

for all
z\in{\sf M}
and all linearly independent
P,Q\in\mathring{\sf T}^{\sf geo}_{\delta_{z}}\mathscr{P}_{2}

provided that
\mathsf{Sec}^{\sf M}_{x}\leq 0
for all
x\in{\sf M}
.
In particular,
{\mathsf{Sec}}^{\mathscr{P}_{2}}_{\delta_{z}}=0
for all
z\in{\sf M}
in the Euclidean case
{\sf M}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

i

On the space
({{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}({\sf M}),{\sf H\!\!K})
, anyway, so far there does not exist a sufficiently well-established Otto calculus
whereas our OT based, rigorous approach provides surprising deep insights.

2.
Sectional curvature in Riemannian and metric geometry

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
{\sf M}={\mathbf{M}}^{\kappa,n}
is the
n
-dimensional model space of constant curvature
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Consider two geodesics
(\alpha_{t})_{0\leq t\leq a}
and
(\beta_{t})_{0\leq t\leq b}
emanating from the same point
\alpha_{0}=\beta_{0}
. Then for
t\searrow 0
,

(1)

{\sf d}^{2}(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t})=t^{2}\big|\dot{\alpha}_{0}-\dot{\beta}_{0}|^{2}-\frac{\kappa}{3}t^{4}\Big[|\dot{\alpha}_{0}|^{2}\,|\dot{\beta}_{0}|^{2}-|\langle\dot{\alpha}_{0},\dot{\beta}_{0}\rangle|^{2}\Big]+\mathit{O}(t^{6}).

More generally, for every
C>0
,

(2)

{\sf d}^{2}(\alpha_{s},\beta_{t})=\big|s\dot{\alpha}_{0}-t\dot{\beta}_{0}|^{2}-\frac{\kappa}{3}s^{2}t^{2}\Big[|\dot{\alpha}_{0}|^{2}\,|\dot{\beta}_{0}|^{2}-|\langle\dot{\alpha}_{0},\dot{\beta}_{0}\rangle|^{2}\Big]+\mathit{O}(s^{3}t^{3})

as
s,t\to 0
with
\frac{s}{t}+\frac{t}{s}\leq C
.

Proof.

Let
\phi
denote the angle between
\dot{\alpha}_{0}
and
\dot{\beta}_{0}
and put
v:=|\dot{\alpha}_{0}|
,
w:=|\dot{\beta}_{0}|
,

\delta_{t}:={\sf d}(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t})
. Then in case
\kappa>0
by the law of cosines in spherical geometry,

\displaystyle\cos(\sqrt{\kappa}\delta_{t})=\cos(\sqrt{\kappa}tv)\cos(\sqrt{\kappa}tw)+\sin(\sqrt{\kappa}tv)\sin(\sqrt{\kappa}tw)\cos(\phi).

Thus

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa}{2}\delta_{t}^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{24}\delta_{t}^{4}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa}{2}t^{2}v^{2}-\frac{\kappa}{2}t^{2}w^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{4}t^{4}v^{2}w^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{24}t^{4}v^{4}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{24}t^{4}w^{4}

\displaystyle+\Big[\kappa t^{2}vw-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{6}t^{4}vw^{3}-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{6}t^{4}v^{3}w\Big]\cos(\phi)+\mathit{O}(t^{6}).

In particular,

\delta_{t}^{2}=t^{2}(v^{2}+w^{2}-2vw\cos\phi)+\mathit{O}(t^{4})
, and thus

\displaystyle\delta_{t}^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{12}\Big[t^{4}\big(v^{2}+w^{2}-2vw\cos\phi\big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle+t^{2}v^{2}+t^{2}w^{2}-\frac{\kappa}{2}t^{4}v^{2}w^{2}-\frac{\kappa}{12}t^{4}v^{4}-\frac{\kappa}{12}t^{4}w^{4}

\displaystyle-\Big[2t^{2}vw-\frac{\kappa}{3}t^{4}vw^{3}-\frac{\kappa}{3}t^{4}v^{3}w\Big]\cos(\phi)+\mathit{O}(t^{6})

\displaystyle=t^{2}\Big[v^{2}+w^{2}-2vw\cos\phi\Big]-\frac{\kappa}{3}t^{4}v^{2}w^{2}(1-\cos^{2}\phi)+\mathit{O}(t^{6}).

Analogous calculations prove the claim in the cases
\kappa=0
and
\kappa<0
.

To prove the second claim, let a sequence
(s_{i},t_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be given with
s_{i}/t_{i}+t_{i}/s_{i}\leq C
and put

\alpha^{(i)}_{t}:=\alpha_{ts_{i}/t_{i}}
.
Then the claim follows from the first claim applied to
\alpha^{(i)}
and
\beta
in the place of
\alpha
and
\beta
and with error term uniform in
i
,

\displaystyle{\sf d}^{2}(\alpha_{ts_{i}/t_{i}},\beta_{t})

\displaystyle=t^{2}\big|\frac{s_{i}}{t_{i}}\dot{\alpha}_{0}-\dot{\beta}_{0}|^{2}-\frac{\kappa}{3}t^{4}\Big[|\frac{s_{i}}{t_{i}}\dot{\alpha}_{0}|^{2}\,|\dot{\beta}_{0}|^{2}-|\langle\frac{s_{i}}{t_{i}}\dot{\alpha}_{0},\dot{\beta}_{0}\rangle|^{2}\Big]+\mathit{O}(t^{6}),

and thus

\displaystyle{\sf d}^{2}(\alpha_{s_{i}},\beta_{t_{i}})=\big|{s_{i}}\dot{\alpha}_{0}-t_{i}\dot{\beta}_{0}|^{2}-\frac{\kappa}{3}s_{i}^{2}\,t_{i}^{2}\Big[|\dot{\alpha}_{0}|^{2}\,|\dot{\beta}_{0}|^{2}-|\langle\dot{\alpha}_{0},\dot{\beta}_{0}\rangle|^{2}\Big]+\mathit{O}(s_{i}^{3}\,t_{i}^{3}).

∎

Definition 2.2
.

Given a geodesic space
({\sf X},{\sf d})
we define
{\sf T}^{\sf dir}_{z}{\sf X}
, its
directional tangent space
at
z\in{\sf X}
, as the completion of the metric space

\mathring{\sf T}^{\sf dir}_{z}{\sf X}:=\big\{\gamma=(\gamma_{t})_{0\leq t\leq\tau}\textrm{ geodesic in }{\sf X},\tau>0,\gamma_{0}=z\big\}\big/\sim

where

{\sf d}_{z}(\alpha,\beta):=\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}{\sf d}(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t})

and
\alpha\sim\beta
if
{\sf d}_{z}(\alpha,\beta)=0
.

\mathring{\sf T}^{\sf dir}_{z}{\sf X}
(as well as its completion) is a cone. Given
\alpha\in\mathring{\sf T}^{\sf dir}_{z}{\sf X}
and
r>0
, the tangent vector
r\alpha\in\mathring{\sf T}^{\sf dir}_{z}{\sf X}
is defined by
(r\alpha)_{t}:=\alpha_{rt}
.

For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathring{\sf T}^{\sf dir}_{z}{\sf X}
we define

\displaystyle\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{z}:=\frac{1}{2}\big(|\dot{\alpha}|^{2}+|\dot{\beta}|^{2}-{\sf d}_{z}^{2}(\alpha,\beta)\big)=|\dot{\alpha}|\,|\dot{\beta}|\,\cos\Big(\angle_{z}(\alpha,\beta)\Big)

with the upper angle

\angle_{z}(\alpha,\beta):=\limsup_{t\to 0}\angle_{z}(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t})

as considered in
[
BBI01
]
.
We say that
\alpha,\beta\in\mathring{\sf T}^{\sf dir}_{z}{\sf X}
are
linearly independent
if

\angle_{z}(\alpha,\beta)\not\in\{0,\pi\}.

The latter property is equivalent to

{\sf d}_{z}(\alpha,\beta)\not\in\big\{|\dot{\alpha}|+|\dot{\beta}|,\big||\dot{\alpha}|-|\dot{\beta}|\big|\big\},

as well as to

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{z}<|\dot{\alpha}|\,|\dot{\beta}|.

Given a function
F
on
(0,\infty)^{2}
we say that the limit

\lim_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}{s,t\to 0}\\
{t/s+s/t\textrm{ bdd}}\end{array}}\!\!\!\!\!\!F(s,t)\ \textrm{exists and equals }L\in{\mathbb{R}}

if

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}F(s_{\ell},t_{\ell})=L
for every sequence
(s_{\ell},t_{\ell})_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
(0,\infty)^{2}
with
\sup_{\ell}(s_{\ell}/t_{\ell}+t_{\ell}/s_{\ell})<\infty
and
\lim_{\ell\to\infty}s_{\ell}=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}t_{\ell}=0
.

Similarly with
\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}F(s_{\ell},t_{\ell})
and
\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}F(s_{\ell},t_{\ell})
in the place of
\lim_{\ell\to\infty}F(s_{\ell},t_{\ell})
.

Definition 2.3
.

Given linearly independent
\alpha,\beta\in\mathring{\sf T}^{\sf dir}_{z}{\sf X}
and
\kappa,\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we say that

\overline{\mathsf{Sec}}_{z}(\alpha,\beta)\leq\lambda\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad 3\!\!\!\limsup_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}{s,t\to 0}\\
{t/s+s/t\textrm{ bdd}}\end{array}}\!\!\!\frac{{\sf d}_{z}^{2}(s\alpha,t\beta)-{\sf d}^{2}\big(\alpha_{s},\beta_{t}\big)}{s^{2}\,t^{2}\big(|\dot{\alpha}|^{2}|\dot{\beta}|^{2}-\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{z}^{2}\big)}\leq\lambda

and, similarly,

\underline{\mathsf{Sec}}_{z}(\alpha,\beta)\geq\kappa\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad 3\!\!\!\liminf_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}{s,t\to 0}\\
{t/s+s/t\textrm{ bdd}}\end{array}}\!\!\!\frac{{\sf d}_{z}^{2}(s\alpha,t\beta)-{\sf d}^{2}\big(\alpha_{s},\beta_{t}\big)}{s^{2}\,t^{2}\big(|\dot{\alpha}|^{2}|\dot{\beta}|^{2}-\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{z}^{2}\big)}\geq\kappa.

We say that
\mathsf{Sec}_{z}(\alpha,\beta)=\lambda
if the limit

3\!\!\!\lim_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}{s,t\to 0}\\
{t/s+s/t\textrm{ bdd}}\end{array}}\!\!\!\frac{{\sf d}_{z}^{2}(s\alpha,t\beta)-{\sf d}^{2}\big(\alpha_{s},\beta_{t}\big)}{s^{2}\,t^{2}\big(|\dot{\alpha}|^{2}|\dot{\beta}|^{2}-\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{z}^{2}\big)}

exists and equals
\lambda
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume that
({\sf X},{\sf d})
is a complete geodesic space and consider any
z\in{\sf X}
.

(i)

If
({\sf X},{\sf d})
has curvature
\geq\kappa
in the sense of Alexandrov in a neighborhood of
z
then

\underline{\mathsf{Sec}}_{z}(\alpha,\beta)\geq\kappa

for all linearly independent
\alpha,\beta\in\mathring{\sf T}^{\sf dir}_{z}{\sf X}
.

(ii)

If
({\sf X},{\sf d})
has curvature
\leq\lambda
in the sense of Alexandrov in a neighborhood of
z
then

\overline{\mathsf{Sec}}_{z}(\alpha,\beta)\leq\lambda

for all linearly independent
\alpha,\beta\in\mathring{\sf T}^{\sf dir}_{z}{\sf X}
.

Proof.

In both cases, for any pair of geodesics
(\alpha_{s})_{s\in[0,a]}
and
(\beta_{t})_{t\in[0,b]}
emanating from
z
, the limit

{\mathsf{D}}_{z}(\alpha,\beta)=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}{\sf d}(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t})

exists
[
BBI01
]
. Assuming now that the curvature is
\geq\kappa
(or
\leq\kappa
, resp.), by triangle comparison

{\sf d}(\alpha_{s},\beta_{t})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\geq)}}{{\leq}}\Phi_{\kappa,s,t,|\dot{\alpha}|,|\dot{\beta}|}\Big({\sf d}_{z}(\alpha,\beta)\Big)

where

\Phi_{\kappa,\ldots}(.)
stands for the formula which provides the exact value in the model space of constant curvature
\kappa
, cf. Lemma
2.1
. The claim thus follows by comparison with the model case.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

For any geodesic space
({\sf X},{\sf d})
,

\textrm{NPC in sense of Alexandrov}\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\not\Leftarrow}}{{\Longrightarrow}}\quad\textrm{NPC in sense of Busemann}\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\not\Leftarrow}}{{\Longrightarrow}}\quad\textrm{NPC in above sense.}

The fact that the converse implications do not hold is illustrated by the following examples: 1) a Banach space which is not Hilbertian, and 2) the flat torus.

.

For every Riemannian manifold
({\sf M},{\sf g})
, every

.

{t/s+s/t\textrm{ bdd}}\end{array}}\!\!\!\frac{{\sf d}_{z}^{2}(s\alpha,t\beta)-{\sf d}^{2}\big(\alpha_{s},\beta_{t}\big)}{s^{2}\,t^{2}\big(|\dot{\alpha}|^{2}|\dot{\beta}|^{2}-\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{z}^{2}\big)}

exists, it coincides with the simpler expression

\dot{\mathsf{Sec}}_{z}(\alpha,\beta):=3\,\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{t^{2}{\sf d}_{z}^{2}(\alpha,\beta)-{\sf d}^{2}\big(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t}\big)}{t^{4}\big(|\dot{\alpha}|^{2}|\dot{\beta}|^{2}-\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{z}^{2}\big)}.

Thus the (existence of the) latter limit could be used for an alternative definition of sectional curvature. The only disadvantage of this simpler approach would be that scaling only holds true in the restricted form of
\dot{\mathsf{Sec}}_{z}(a\alpha,a\beta)=\dot{\mathsf{Sec}}_{z}(\alpha,\beta)
for all
a>0
whereas
\mathsf{Sec}_{z}(a\alpha,b\beta)=\mathsf{Sec}_{z}(\alpha,\beta)
for all
a,b>0
.

Remark 2.9
.

Another option for a synthetic definition of sectional curvature, again based on the observation of Lemma
2.1
, is to consider for every

and all linearly independent
\alpha,\beta\in\mathring{\sf T}^{\sf dir}_{z}{\sf X}

with

3.1.1.
The analytic tangent space

Let
\Gamma({\sf T}{\sf M})=\{V:{\sf M}\to{\sf T}{\sf M}\textrm{ Borel}:V(x)\in{\sf T}_{x}{\sf M}\ (\forall x)\}
and
let
L_{{\sf T}{\sf M}}^{2}(\mu):=\{V\in\Gamma({\sf T}{\sf M}):\int|V|^{2}d\mu<\infty\}
denote the space of

vector fields.

Definition 3.1
.

We define the
analytic (or deterministic) tangent space
of
(\mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf M}),{\sf{W}}_{2})
at
\mu
by

{\sf T}^{\sf ana}_{\mu}\mathscr{P}_{2}:=\overline{\big\{\nabla\varphi:\ \varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}({\sf M})\big\}}^{L^{2}(\mu)}\subset L_{{\sf T}{\sf M}}^{2}(\mu).

For

we put

and

with

.

Remark 3.2
.

{\sf T}^{\sf ana}_{\mu}\mathscr{P}_{2}=\overline{\big\{\nabla\varphi:\ \varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}({\sf M}),\ t\varphi\textrm{ is $\mathfrak{c}$-convex for some }t>0\big\}}^{L^{2}(\mu)}.

Example 3.3
.

Consider
\mu=\delta_{z}
. Then

{\sf T}^{\sf ana}_{\mu}\mathscr{P}_{2}=L^{2}_{{\sf T}{\sf M}}(\mu)\cong{\sf T}_{z}{\sf M}
.

3.1.2.
Tangent measures

Recall that

{\sf T}{\sf M}=\{(x,v):x\in{\sf M},v\in{\sf T}_{x}{\sf M}\}
.
Given
\mu\in\mathscr{P}({\sf M})
, denote by
\mathscr{P}({\sf T}{\sf M})_{\mu}
the set of
P\in\mathscr{P}({\sf T}{\sf M})
with first marginal
\mu
, that is,
({\rm{pr}}_{1})_{*}P=\mu
.
We define the multiplication with positive scalars by

t\,P:=(\theta_{t})_{*}P,\qquad\theta_{t}:(x,v)\mapsto(x,tv),

and the exponential map
\exp:\mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf T}{\sf M})_{\mu}\to\mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf M})
by

\exp(P)=\Theta_{*}P,\qquad\Theta:(x,v)\mapsto\exp_{x}v.

For
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf M})
we denote by
\mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf T}{\sf M})_{\mu}
the subset of
P\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf T}{\sf M})_{\mu}
with finite modulus

|P|_{\mu}:=\left(\int_{{\sf T}{\sf M}}|v|^{2}dP(v)\right)^{1/2}.

Then obviously
|tP|_{\mu}=t\,|P|_{\mu}
for all
t\geq 0
, and the family

p_{t}:=\exp(tP)
for
t\geq 0
obtained by pushing forward
P
with the maps
\Theta_{t}:(x,v)\mapsto\exp_{x}(tv)
defines a ‘pseudo geodesic’ (i.e. looks like a geodesic but is not necessarily minimizing) in
\mathscr{P}_{2}{\sf M}
.

Every
P\in\mathscr{P}({\sf T}{\sf M})_{\mu}
can be decomposed into
\mu
and the disintegration of
P
w.r.t.
\mu
such that

\int_{{\sf T}{\sf M}}f(v)dP=\int_{\sf M}\int_{{\sf T}_{x}{\sf M}}f(v)dP_{x}(v)d\mu(x).

We define a transport distance on
\mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf T}{\sf M})_{\mu}
by

{\sf{W}}_{\mu}(P,Q):=\inf\Bigg\{\int_{M}\int_{{\sf T}_{x}{\sf M}\times{\sf T}_{x}{\sf M}}|v-w|_{x}^{2}dN(x,v,w):\ N\in\Cpl_{\mu}(P,Q)\Bigg\}^{1/2}

where
\Cpl_{\mu}(P,Q)
denotes the set of all probability measures
N
on
\{(x,v,w):x\in{\sf M},v\in{\sf T}_{x}{\sf M},w\in{\sf T}_{x}{\sf M}\}
with marginals

({\rm{pr}}_{1})_{*}N=\mu,\quad({\rm{pr}}_{1},{\rm{pr}}_{2})_{*}N=P,\quad({\rm{pr}}_{1},{\rm{pr}}_{3})_{\mu}=Q.

As in the classical case, continuity and nonnegativity of the cost function imply that always a minimizer
N
exists. It is easy to see that
{\sf{W}}_{\mu}
defines a complete, separable metric on
\mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf T}{\sf M})_{\mu}
.
Moreover, it can equivalently be reformulated as

{\sf{W}}_{\mu}(P,Q):=\inf\Bigg\{\int_{M}\int_{{\sf T}_{x}{\sf M}\times{\sf T}_{x}{\sf M}}|v-w|_{x}^{2}dN_{x}(v,w)\,d\mu(x):\ N_{x}\in\Cpl(P_{x},Q_{x})\Bigg\}^{1/2}

for
P,Q\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf T}{\sf M})_{\mu}
(where
N_{x}
has to be chosen as a measurable function of
x
).
Indeed, the kernel
N_{x}\in\Cpl(P_{x},Q_{x})
can always be chosen as the disintegration w.r.t. the first marginal
d\mu(x)
of the minimizer
N\in\Cpl_{\mu}(P,Q)
in the previous formulation.

Definition 3.4
.

The
geometric (or probabilistic) tangent space

{\sf T}^{\sf geo}_{\mu}\mathscr{P}_{2}
is the closure of

\mathring{\sf T}^{\sf geo}_{\mu}\mathscr{P}_{2}:=\Big\{P\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf T}{\sf M})_{\mu}:\ {\exists C,t>0:|v|\leq C\textrm{ for $P$-a.e.~$v$ and }}{\sf{W}}_{2}(\mu,\exp(tP))=t|P|_{\mu}\Big\}

in
(\mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf T}{\sf M})_{\mu},{\sf{W}}_{\mu})
.

3.1.3.
Embeddings and identifications

Theorem 3.5

For every
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\sf M})

The

(or, more generally, if

Theorem 3.7

If

is bounded, then for each

the geometric tangent space

Remark 3.25
.

(i)
Given a vector field
F
, let

\delta(F):=\inf\{\int|F-\nabla\eta|^{2}d\mu:\eta\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},\mu)\}^{1/2}

denote its
L^{2}(\mu)
-distance from the space of gradients.
The Theorem then states that
\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi)=\frac{3}{\Lambda}\cdot\delta^{2}(\nabla^{2}\psi\nabla\varphi)
.
Note that the right hand side is symmetric in
\varphi
and
\psi
, i.e.

\delta(\nabla^{2}\psi\nabla\varphi)=\delta(\nabla^{2}\varphi\nabla\psi)

since
\nabla^{2}\varphi\nabla\psi+\nabla^{2}\psi\nabla\varphi=\nabla\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi\rangle
.
Also note that
\delta(F)=0
for all
F
in dimension
n=1
.

(ii)
The minimizer
\eta
for
\delta(F)
is explicitly given as

\eta=\Delta_{\rho}^{-1}\big(\mathrm{div}_{\rho}F\big).

Note that on the set of
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},\rho\mathcal{L}^{n})
with
\int u\rho dx=0
, the weighted Laplacian
\Delta_{\rho}
is invertible, and that the function
\nabla_{\rho}\cdot F
belongs to this set.

Here the weighted divergence operator is defined through

-\int u\,(\mathrm{div}_{\rho}F)\,\rho dx=\int\nabla u\,\cdot F\,\rho dx
for all compactly supported smooth vector fields
F
and functions
u
,
and the weighted Laplacian as
\Delta_{\rho}u:=\mathrm{div}_{\rho}\nabla u
.
Indeed, with
\eta
defined this way,

\displaystyle\int\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta\,\rho dx=-\int u\Delta_{\rho}\eta\,\rho dx=-\int u\,(\mathrm{div}_{\rho}F)\,\rho dx=\int\nabla u\,\cdot F\,\rho dx.

Thus
\nabla\eta-F
is orthogonal in
L^{2}(\rho dx)
to all vector fields of the form
\nabla u
.

(iii) By othogonality of the projection,

\displaystyle\delta^{2}(F)

\displaystyle=\|F\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\|\nabla\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int\bigg[|F|^{2}+\big(\mathrm{div}_{\rho}F\big)\Delta_{\rho}^{-1}\big(\mathrm{div}_{\rho}F\big)\bigg]\rho\,dx

for any vector field
F
and associated projection
\nabla\eta
.

Proof of the Theorem.

(i) The non-optimal transport map.

Put

p_{t}:=(\Id+t\nabla\varphi)_{*}\mu
and
q_{t}:=(\Id+t\nabla\psi)_{*}\mu
. Furthermore, put

U_{t}:=(\Id+t\nabla\psi)\circ(\Id+t\nabla\varphi)^{-1}
such that

(U_{t})_{*}p_{t}=q_{t}
. Then

U_{t}=\Id+t\nabla f+t^{2}F_{t}

with
f:=\psi-\varphi
,
F_{t}=F_{0}+tF_{0}^{\prime}+\mathit{O}(t^{2})
for

F_{0}:=\nabla^{2}(\varphi-\psi)\nabla\varphi
and some
F_{0}^{\prime}
.
Indeed,

(\Id+t\nabla\varphi)^{-1}=\Id-t\nabla\varphi+t^{2}\,\nabla^{2}\varphi\nabla\varphi+\mathit{O}(t^{3}).

Thus

U_{t}:=(\Id+t\nabla\psi)\circ(\Id+t\nabla\varphi)^{-1}=\Id+t(\nabla\psi-\nabla\varphi)+t^{2}\Big(\nabla^{2}\varphi-\nabla^{2}\psi\Big)\nabla\varphi+\mathit{O}(t^{3}).

(ii) The optimal transport map.
By polar factorization, there exist maps
T_{t},S_{t}
such that
U_{t}=T_{t}\circ S_{t}
where
T_{t}
is the optimal transport map from
p_{t}
to
q_{t}
and
S_{t}
is
p_{t}
-preserving.
T_{t}
is given in terms of a Kantorovich potential (which is smooth in
x
and smoothly depends on
t
). That is, there exists a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}
function

\theta_{t}
such that

T_{t}=\Id+\nabla\theta_{t}
. By elliptic regularity theory,
\theta_{t}
smoothly depends on
t
in a small neighborhood around 0. Furthermore,
\theta_{0}=0
and
\theta^{\prime}_{0}=f
. Thus there exists
a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}
function

\eta_{t}
with

\eta_{t}=\eta_{0}+t\eta_{0}^{\prime}+\mathit{O}(t^{2})
such that

T_{t}=\Id+t\nabla f+t^{2}\nabla\eta_{t}.

(iii) The projection onto gradients.

Since
S_{s,t}
is
p_{s}
-preserving,

\int u(U_{s,t})dp_{s}=\int u\big(T_{s,t}\circ S_{s,t}\big)dp_{s}=\int u(T_{s,t})dp_{s}

for every
u\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{c}({\sf M})
, that is,

(7)

\int u\Big(x+t\nabla f(x)+t^{2}F_{0}(x)+\mathit{O}(t^{3})\Big)dp_{t}(x)=\int u\Big(x+t\nabla f(x)+t^{2}\nabla\eta_{0}(x)+\mathit{O}(t^{3})\Big)dp_{t}(x).

Therefore, Taylor expansion of
u(x+\ldots)
around
x
yields

\int\langle\nabla u,F_{0}-\nabla\eta_{0}\rangle dp_{t}=O(t)

and thus in turn

\int\langle\nabla u,F_{0}-\nabla\eta_{0}\rangle dp_{0}=0

for all
u\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{c}({\sf M})
.
That is,
\nabla\eta_{0}
is the projection of
F_{0}
onto the space of gradients in
L^{2}(p_{0})
.

(iv) Several almost orthogonality results.

Now let us slightly modify the previous argument and consider the Taylor expansion of
u(x+t\nabla f(x)+t^{2}F_{t}(x))
around
x+t\nabla f(x)
. Then (
7
) yields

(8)

\int\langle\nabla u(\Id+t\nabla f),F_{t}-\nabla\eta_{t}\rangle dp_{t}=O(t^{2})

which of course immediately implies

(9)

\int\langle\nabla u,F_{t}-\nabla\eta_{t}\rangle dp_{t}=O(t^{1}).

For the particular choice
u=f
, (
8
), however, yields

(10)

\int\langle\nabla f,F_{t}-\nabla\eta_{t}\rangle dp_{t}=O(t^{2})

since
\nabla f(x+t\nabla f(x))=\nabla f_{t}(x)+O(t^{2})
with
f_{t}:=f+\frac{t}{2}|\nabla f|^{2}
and

\int\langle\nabla|\nabla f|^{2},F_{t}-\nabla\eta_{t}\rangle dp_{t}=O(t^{1})

according to (
9
).

(v) The transport cost estimate.

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\Big|(\Id+t\nabla\varphi)

\displaystyle-(\Id+t\nabla\psi)\Big|^{2}d\mu-{\sf{W}}^{2}_{2}(p_{t},q_{t})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\Big[\big|\Id-U_{t}\big|^{2}-\big|\Id-T_{t}\big|^{2}\Big]dp_{t}

\displaystyle=t^{2}\,\int\Big[\big|\nabla f+tF_{t}\big|^{2}-\big|\nabla f+t\nabla\eta_{t}\big|^{2}\Big]dp_{t}

\displaystyle=t^{4}\,\int\Big[|F_{t}|^{2}-|\nabla\eta_{t}|^{2}\Big]dp_{t}+2t^{3}\,\int\langle\nabla f,F_{t}-\nabla\eta_{t}\rangle dp_{t}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{3333}}}{{=}}t^{4}\,\int\Big[|F_{t}-\nabla\eta_{t}|^{2}+2\langle\nabla\eta_{t},F_{t}-\nabla\eta_{t}\rangle\Big]dp_{t}+O(t^{5})

\displaystyle=t^{4}\,\int\Big[|F_{t}-\nabla\eta_{t}|^{2}+2\langle\nabla\eta_{0},F_{t}-\nabla\eta_{t}\rangle\Big]dp_{t}+O(t^{5})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{2222}}}{{=}}t^{4}\,\int\big|F_{t}-\nabla\eta_{t}\big|^{2}dp_{t}+O(t^{5})

\displaystyle=t^{4}\,\int\big|F_{0}-\nabla\eta_{0}\big|^{2}dp_{0}+O(t^{5}).

(v) The conclusion: convergence of the 1-parameter functional

\displaystyle\dot{\mathsf{Sec}}_{\mu}^{\nabla}(\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{3}{\Lambda\,t^{4}}\bigg[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\Big|(\Id+t\nabla\varphi)-(\Id+t\nabla\psi)\Big|^{2}d\mu-{\sf{W}}^{2}_{2}(p_{t},q_{t})\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\Lambda}\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\big|F_{0}-\nabla\eta_{0}\big|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\Lambda}\,\inf\bigg\{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\big|F_{0}-\nabla\eta\big|^{2}d\mu:\ \eta\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\bigg\}

with
F_{0}:=\nabla^{2}(\varphi-\psi)\nabla\varphi
.

(vi) The final extension to the 2-parameter limit.

Let
(s_{\ell},t_{\ell})_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence with
\sup_{\ell}(s_{\ell}/t_{\ell}+t_{\ell}/s_{\ell})<\infty
and
\lim_{\ell}s_{\ell}=\lim_{\ell}t_{\ell}=0
such that

\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}K(s_{\ell},t_{\ell})=\!\!\!\limsup_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}{s,t\to 0}\\
{t/s+s/t\textrm{ bdd}}\end{array}}\!\!\!K(s,t)

where

K(s,t):=\frac{3}{\Lambda\,s^{2}\,t^{2}}\bigg[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\big|s\nabla\varphi-t\nabla\psi\big|^{2}\,d\mu-{\sf{W}}_{2}^{2}\big(\exp_{\mu}(s\nabla\varphi),\exp_{\mu}(t\nabla\psi)\big)\bigg].

Passing to a subsequence, we may assume that the

is indeed a
\lim_{\ell}
and that

exists. Replacing
\varphi
by

, we may assume that without restriction

in the place of

where

This is easily verified.
∎

4.
The Kantorovich-Hellinger geometry

4.1.
The space of finite measures

Throughout the sequel, we assume that

({\sf M},{\sf d})
is a Polish metric space (i.e. there exists a complete separable metric which generates the same topology).
Our prime examples are Riemannian manifolds (not necessarily complete).

With

{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}:={{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}({\sf M})

finite measures

{\sf M}
equipped with its Borel field (where “e” stands for “endlich”).
We will equip
{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}
with the
Hellinger-Kantorovich metric

{\sf H\!\!K}
, a metric of fundamental importance – both from theoretical perspectives and from applications.
It was introduced and analyzed in detail within the last decade by three independent teams: Liero-Mielke-Savaré,
Kondratyev-Monsaingeon-Vorotnikov, Chizat-Peyré-Schmitzer-Vialard, and it can be defined (at least in the Riemannian case) in three entirely different, equivalent ways:

•

as projection of the Kantorovich-Wasserstein metric on the cone
{\mathsf{C}}={\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\sf M}/\sim
over
{\sf M}
,

•

as
\inf
-convolution of the Kantorovich-Wasserstein and the Kakutani-Hellinger metrics,

•

in terms of the continuity equation with growth term,

cf.
[
LMS16
,
LMS18
,
LMS23
]
,
[
KMV16
]
,
[
CPSV18a
,
CPSV18b
]
,
[
DPST25
]
.
For related recent developments, see also

[
KV19
]
,

[

]
,
[
LM26
]
.

4.1.1.
The cone picture

Given the metric space
({\sf M},{\sf d})
we denote by
({\mathsf{C}},{\sf d}^{\mathsf{C}})
the metric space where

{\mathsf{C}}:={\sf M}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}/\sim
with
(x,r)\sim(x^{\prime},r^{\prime})\Leftrightarrow(x,r)=(x^{\prime},r^{\prime})
or
r=r^{\prime}=0
, and

\displaystyle{\sf d}^{\mathsf{C}}\big((x,r)),(x^{\prime},r^{\prime})\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=r^{2}+{r^{\prime}}^{2}-2rr^{\prime}\cos\big({\sf d}(x,x^{\prime})\wedge\pi\big)

\displaystyle=(r-r^{\prime})^{2}+rr^{\prime}\,{\sf d}_{\pi}(x,x^{\prime})^{2}

with
{\sf d}_{\pi}(x,x^{\prime}):=2\sin\big(\frac{1}{2}({\sf d}(x,x^{\prime})\wedge\pi)\big)
, cf.
[
BBI01
]
.
The (equivalence class of) point(s)

o=(x,0)
(with arbitrary
x\in{\sf M}
) is the
vertex
of the cone.

We define canonical maps between the space of finite measures on
{\sf M}
and the space of probability measures with finite second moments on
{\mathsf{C}}
:

•

Lifting:

{\sf Li}:{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}\to\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathsf{C}}),\quad\mu\mapsto\hat{\mu}:=\frac{1}{r^{2}_{\mu}}\mu\otimes\delta_{r_{\mu}}\ \textrm{with }r_{\mu}:=\sqrt{\mu({\sf M})}.

•

Projection:

\Pr:\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathsf{C}})\to{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}},\quad\nu\mapsto\underline{\nu}:=\bigg(A\mapsto\int_{A\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}r^{2}d\nu(x,r)\bigg).

In particular, note that

\underline{\nu}({\sf M})=\int_{{\mathsf{C}}}r^{2}d\nu(x,r)={\sf{W}}^{2}_{\mathsf{C}}(\nu,\delta_{o}).

The projection is the left inverse of the lifting, i.e.
\Pr\circ\,{\sf Li}={\sf Id}_{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}
, but not the other way round since the projection is not injective: for every
\mu\in{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}\setminus\{0\}
there exist plenty of
\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathsf{C}})
with
\Pr(\nu)=\mu
.

Definition 4.1
.

The Hellinger-Kantorovich metric
{\sf H\!\!K}
on
{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}
is defined as

\displaystyle{\sf H\!\!K}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})

\displaystyle:=\inf\Big\{{\sf{W}}_{2}^{{\mathsf{C}}}(\nu_{0},\nu_{1}):\ \nu_{0},\nu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathsf{C}}),\ \Pr(\nu_{0})=\mu_{0},\ \Pr(\nu_{1})=\mu_{1}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\inf\Big\{\int_{{\mathsf{C}}\times{\mathsf{C}}}{\sf d}^{2}_{\mathsf{C}}\,dq:\ q\in\mathscr{P}({\mathsf{C}}\times{\mathsf{C}}),\ \Pr({\pi_{0}}_{*}q)=\mu_{0},\ \Pr({\pi_{1}}_{*}q)=\mu_{1}\Big\}^{1/2}.

Here
\pi_{0}
and
\pi_{1}
denote the projections
{\mathsf{C}}\times{\mathsf{C}}\to{\mathsf{C}}
onto the first and second,
resp., factors.

This obviously defines a metric on
{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}
.
Let us state two basic properties of it.

Lemma 4.2

(Reduction Lemma)
.

Given
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}
, define

\displaystyle S_{i}:=\mathrm{supp}[\mu_{i}],\qquad S_{i}^{\prime}:=\big\{x\in S_{i}:{\sf d}(x,S_{1-i})<\pi/2\big\},\qquad S_{i}^{\prime\prime}:=S_{i}\setminus S_{i}^{\prime}

for
i=0,1
, and

\mu_{i}^{\prime}:={\mathbb{1}}_{S_{i}^{\prime}}\,\mu_{i},\qquad\mu_{i}^{\prime\prime}:={\mathbb{1}}_{S_{i}^{\prime\prime}}\,\mu_{i}.

Then the
{\sf H\!\!K}
-distance of the pair
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}
can be expressed in terms of the
{\sf H\!\!K}
-distance of the ‘reduced pair’
\mu_{0}^{\prime},\mu_{1}^{\prime}
and the total masses of the remainders
\mu_{0}^{\prime\prime}
and
\mu_{1}^{\prime\prime}
:

\displaystyle{\sf H\!\!K}^{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})

\displaystyle={\sf H\!\!K}^{2}(\mu^{\prime}_{0},\mu^{\prime}_{1})+{\sf H\!\!K}^{2}(\mu^{\prime\prime}_{0},\mu^{\prime\prime}_{1}),

\displaystyle{\sf H\!\!K}^{2}(\mu^{\prime\prime}_{0},\mu^{\prime\prime}_{1})

\displaystyle=\mu_{0}^{\prime\prime}({\sf M})+\mu_{1}^{\prime\prime}({\sf M}).

Proof.

[
LMS18
, Lemma 7.19]
and
[
LMS23
, Thm. 2.5]
, the latter dealing only with the case

, but the general case is completely analogously.
∎

Lemma 4.3

(Dilation Invariance)
.

Given any measurable function
\vartheta:{\mathsf{C}}\times{\mathsf{C}}\to(0,\infty)
, define the map
\widehat{\vartheta}:{\mathsf{C}}^{2}\to{\mathsf{C}}^{2}
by

\widehat{\vartheta}\Big((x,s),(y,t)\Big):=\bigg(\Big(x,\frac{s}{\vartheta\big((x,s),(y,t)\big)}\Big),\Big(y,\frac{t}{\vartheta\big((x,s),(y,t)\big)}\Big)\bigg).

Then for every
q\in\mathscr{P}({\mathsf{C}}\times{\mathsf{C}})
, the measure
q_{\vartheta}:=\vartheta^{2}\cdot(\widehat{\vartheta}_{*}q)

has the same projected marginals and the same transport cost, that is,

\Pr({\pi_{i}}_{*}q)=\Pr({\pi_{i}}_{*}q_{\vartheta})
for
i=0,1
and

\int{\sf d}_{\mathsf{C}}^{2}\,dq=\int{\sf d}_{\mathsf{C}}^{2}\,dq_{\vartheta}.

Proof.

Straightforward calculation, see
[
LMS18
]
.
∎

The dilation invariance in particular implies that,
if
q\in\mathscr{P}({\mathsf{C}}^{2})
is a minimizer in the definition of
{\sf H\!\!K}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})

q_{\vartheta}
for each dilation
\vartheta
as above.
Similarly, for each minimizing sequence.

This allows us to show that, in the definition of
{\sf H\!\!K}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
, one of the factors can be fixed a priori (or ‘pinned’) when choosing the minimizing pairs
\nu_{0},\nu_{1}
of liftings.

Theorem 4.4

(Pinning Theorem)
.

Let
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}
be a reduced pair and
let any
\rho_{0}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathsf{C}})
be given with
\Pr(\rho_{0})=\mu_{0}
. Then

{\sf H\!\!K}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}):=\inf\Big\{{\sf{W}}_{{\mathsf{C}}}(\rho_{0},\nu_{1}):\ \nu_{1}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathsf{C}}),\ \Pr(\nu_{1})=\mu_{1}\Big\}.

Proof.

(i)
Let us first consider the case
that

d\rho_{0}(x,r)=\frac{1}{r^{2}_{0}(x)}d\delta_{r_{0}(x)}(r)\,d\mu_{0}(x)

with some measurable function
r_{0}:{\sf M}\to(0,\infty)
.
By assumption, we may restrict ourselves to minimizers
q
in the definition of
{\sf H\!\!K}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
which are supported on
{\mathsf{C}}^{*}\times{\mathsf{C}}^{*}
.
Choose
\vartheta\big((x,s),(y,t)\big):=\frac{s}{r_{0}(x)}
provided
s>0
, and
:=1
otherwise. Then

{\pi_{0}}_{*}q_{\vartheta}=\rho_{0}
.
Indeed, with
\nu_{0}:={\pi_{0}}_{*}q
,

\displaystyle q_{\vartheta}(A\times{\mathsf{C}})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathsf{C}}^{2}}\vartheta^{2}((x,s),(y,t))\,{\mathbb{1}}_{A}(\widehat{\vartheta}((x,s),(y,t)))\,dq((x,s),(y,t))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathsf{C}}\frac{s^{2}}{r_{0}^{2}(x)}{\mathbb{1}}_{A}(x,r_{0}(x))\,d\nu_{0}(x,s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\sf M}\frac{1}{r_{0}^{2}(x)}{\mathbb{1}}_{A}(x,r_{0}(x))\,d\mu_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathsf{C}}{\mathbb{1}}_{A}(x,r)\,d\rho_{0}(x,r)

for every measurable subset
A\subset{\mathsf{C}}
.
Thus

{\sf H\!\!K}^{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})=\int{\sf d}^{2}_{\mathsf{C}}\,dq=\int{\sf d}^{2}_{\mathsf{C}}\,dq_{\vartheta}={\sf{W}}_{\mathsf{C}}^{2}(\rho_{0},\nu_{1})

with some
\nu_{1}
which projects to
\mu_{1}
, namely,
\nu_{1}:={\pi_{1}}_{*}q_{\vartheta}
.

(ii) Given an arbitrary
\rho_{0}
which projects to
\mu_{0}
, choose a sequence

(11)

d\rho_{0}^{n}(x,r)=\frac{1}{r^{2}_{n}(x)}d\delta_{r_{n}(x)}(r)\,d\mu_{n}(x)

of measures on
{\mathsf{C}}
with
{\sf{W}}_{\mathsf{C}}(\rho_{0}^{n},\rho_{0})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Note that the set of measures of type (
11
) with arbitrary choice of measures
\mu_{n}
and functions
r_{n}
is dense in
\mathscr{P}_{2}({\mathsf{C}})

Put

(iii) We claim that the family

(iv) With compactness at hands, we conclude that there exists an accumulation point

\displaystyle{\sf{W}}_{\mathsf{C}}(\rho_{0},\nu^{\infty}_{1})

∎

The inf-convolution

Definition 4.6
.

Given two length metrics
{\sf d}_{1},{\sf d}_{2}
on a space
{\sf M}
, their inf-convolution
{\sf d}_{*}:={\sf d}_{1}\,\triangledown\,{\sf d}_{2}
is the pseudo length metric on
{\sf M}
defined by

\displaystyle{\sf d}_{*}(x,y):=\lim_{N\to\infty}\,

\displaystyle\inf\bigg\{N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big({\sf d}_{1}^{2}(x_{2i-2},x_{2i-1})+{\sf d}_{2}^{2}(x_{2i-1},x_{2i})\Big):

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad x_{1},\ldots,x_{2N-1}\in{\sf M},\ x_{0}=x,x_{2N}=y\bigg\}^{1/2}

provided this limit exists for all
x,y\in{\sf M}
.

Obviously,
{\sf d}_{1}\,\triangledown\,{\sf d}_{2}\leq{\sf d}_{1}
and
{\sf d}_{1}\,\triangledown\,{\sf d}_{2}\leq{\sf d}_{2}
.
In general, it is not guaranteed that
{\sf d}_{1}\,\triangledown\,{\sf d}_{2}(x,y)\not=0
for
x\not=y
.
(Thus “pseudo” metric.)

Definition 4.7
.

Given a Polish space
{\sf M}
, the Kakutani-Hellinger metric
{\sf He}
on
{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}({\sf M})
is defined as

{\sf He}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\rho)}

where
\rho
is any measure on
{\sf M}
with
\mu_{1}\ll\rho,\mu_{2}\ll\rho
and
u_{i}:=\sqrt{\frac{d\mu_{i}}{d\rho}}
for
i=1,2
.

It is a geodesic metric. The space
({{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}({\sf M}),{\sf He})
has nonnegative and nonpositve curvature in the sense of Alexandrov.

If
{\sf M}
comes with a metric
{\sf d}
, then another natural pseudo metric on
{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}
is the Kantorovich-Wasserstein metric

\displaystyle{\sf{W}}_{2}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\int_{{\sf M}\times{\sf M}}{\sf d}^{2}(x,y)\,dq(x,y):\,q\in\Cpl(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\right\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\mu_{1}({\sf M})=\mu_{2}({\sf M})=0\\
r\,{\sf{W}}_{2}\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\mu_{1},\frac{1}{r^{2}}\mu_{2}\Big),\quad&\text{if }\mu_{1}({\sf M})=\mu_{2}({\sf M})=r^{2},r\in(0,\infty)\\
\infty,&\text{if }\mu_{1}({\sf M})=\mu_{2}({\sf M})=\infty\text{ or }\mu_{1}({\sf M})\not=\mu_{2}({\sf M}).\end{cases}

Theorem 4.8

(
[
DPST25
, Thm. 1.1]
)
.

Let

be a complete separable geodesic space. Then the Hellinger-Kantorovich metric on

is the inf-convolution of the Kantorovich-Wasserstein metric and the Kakutani-Hellinger distance:

{\sf H\!\!K}={\sf{W}}_{2}\,\triangledown\,{\sf He}.

4.2.
The geometry of the cone

To proceed, we will now restrict to the Riemannian case where
({\sf M},{\sf g})
is a smooth Riemannian manifold with uniformly bounded sectional curvature and without boundary (but not necessarily complete).

Let
{\mathsf{C}}
denote the cone over the base space
{\sf M}
and let

denote its vertex.
We write
\langle v,w\rangle_{x}
for the metric tensor
{\sf g}^{\sf M}(v,w)_{x}
on
{\sf M}
, and
analogously
\langle(v,s),(w,t)\rangle_{(x,r)}
for the metric tensor
{\sf g}^{\mathsf{C}}((v,s),(w,t))_{x}
on the punctured cone

. The cone
{\mathsf{C}}
is the ‘warped product’
={\sf M}\,{}_{r}\!\!\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with warping function
r
. Thus
for
(v,s)\in{\sf T}_{(x,r)}{\mathsf{C}}\cong{\sf T}_{x}{\sf M}\times{\mathbb{R}}

|(v,s)|_{(x,r)}^{2}=r^{2}|v|^{2}_{x}+s^{2}.

Lemma 4.11
.

The exponential map on
{\mathsf{C}}
is given by

\exp^{\mathsf{C}}_{(x,r)}(v,s):=\Bigg(\exp^{\sf M}_{x}\bigg(\arctan\bigg(\frac{r|v|}{r+s}\bigg)\frac{v}{|v|}\bigg),\sqrt{(r+s)^{2}+r^{2}|v|^{2}}\Bigg)

for

and

with

and

.
In particular, for suitable
t_{*}>0
, the map

[0,t_{*}]\ni t\mapsto\gamma_{t}:=\Bigg(\exp^{\sf M}_{x}\bigg(\arctan\bigg(\frac{rt|v|}{r+ts}\bigg)\frac{v}{|v|}\bigg),\sqrt{(r+ts)^{2}+r^{2}t^{2}|v|^{2}}\Bigg)\in{\sf M}

is a geodesic in
{\mathsf{C}}
with

\dot{\gamma}_{0}=(v,s)
and

|\dot{\gamma}_{0}|=\big|(v,s)\big|_{x,r}=r^{2}\,|v|^{2}+s^{2}

Proof.

Assume that there is a unique geodesic
(\alpha_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
in
{\sf M}
which connects
x
and
x^{\prime}
. This geodesic can be “lifted” to obtain the geodesic
(z_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
in
{\mathsf{C}}
which connects
(x,r)
and

[
BBI01
]
,
[
LMS16

This lifting only depends on the range

of the geodesic, or in other words, only on the metric space
({\sf M}_{\alpha},{\sf d})

Thus for this lifting construction we can assume without restriction that
({\sf M},{\sf d})=({\mathbb{R}}^{1},|.|)

The claim then follows from

[
LMS23
, equ. (2.7)]
with

replaced by
\frac{s}{r}
.
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

Assume that

According to B. O’Neill
[
O’N83
, Chapter 7, Prop. 42]

cf. A. Bisson
[
Bis23

{\mathsf{C}}
satisifes

\displaystyle R^{\mathsf{C}}_{V+S,W+T}(V+S)

\displaystyle=R^{\mathsf{C}}_{S,T}(S)+R^{\mathsf{C}}_{S,W}(S)+R^{\mathsf{C}}_{V,T}(S)+R^{\mathsf{C}}_{V,W}(S)

Lemma 4.22

\displaystyle V_{t}^{\sf hor}(x)

\displaystyle=x+\arctan\left(\frac{t|\nabla\varphi|}{1+2t\varphi}\right)\cdot\frac{\nabla\varphi}{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}

\displaystyle=x+\frac{t\nabla\varphi}{1+2t\varphi}-\frac{1}{3}t^{3}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\nabla\varphi+\mathit{O}(t^{4})

\displaystyle=x+t\nabla\varphi-2t^{2}\varphi\nabla\varphi+4t^{3}\varphi^{2}\nabla\varphi-\frac{1}{3}t^{3}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\nabla\varphi+\mathit{O}(t^{4}),

\displaystyle V_{t}^{\sf vert}(x,1)

\displaystyle=\Big((1+2t\varphi)^{2}+t^{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=(1+2t\varphi)\cdot\bigg(1+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{(1+2t\varphi)^{2}}\bigg)+\mathit{O}(t^{4})

\displaystyle=1+2t\varphi+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+t^{3}\Big(\varphi|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-\varphi|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\Big)+\mathit{O}(t^{4}).

∎

5.
Sectional curvature of
{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}({\sf M})

5.1.
Definition, decomposition, and scaling

Definition 5.1
.

For all
\mu\in{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}
and all
\varphi,\psi\in{\sf T}^{\sf ana}_{\mu}{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\lim_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}{s,t\to 0}\\
{t/s+s/t\textrm{ bdd}}\end{array}}\!\!\!\frac{3}{\Lambda s^{2}\,t^{2}}\Bigg[|s\varphi-t\psi|_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}-{\sf H\!\!K}^{2}\Big(\exp_{\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(s\varphi),\exp_{\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(t\psi)\Big)\Bigg]

with

\Lambda:=|\varphi|_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}\cdot|\psi|_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}-\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}
, provided the limit exists.

Theorem 5.2
.

(i)

The sectional curvature on
{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}

can be decomposed as

\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi)=\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\uparrow}(\varphi,\psi)+\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\nabla}(\varphi,\psi)

into its lifted part

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\uparrow}(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle={\!\!\!\lim_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}{s,t\to 0}\\
{t/s+s/t\textrm{ bdd}}\end{array}}\!\!\!}\frac{3}{\Lambda s^{2}\,t^{2}}\Bigg[|s\varphi-t\psi|_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{\mathsf{C}}{\sf d}_{\mathsf{C}}^{2}\Big(\exp^{\mathsf{C}}_{x,r}\big(s(\nabla\varphi,2r\varphi)\big),\exp^{\mathsf{C}}_{x,r}\big(t(\nabla\psi,2r\psi)\big)\Big)\,d\hat{\mu}(x,r)\Bigg]

and its twisted part

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\nabla}(\varphi,\psi)=\!\!\!{\lim_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}{s,t\to 0}\\
{t/s+s/t\textrm{ bdd}}\end{array}}\!\!\!}\frac{3}{\Lambda s^{2}\,t^{2}}\Bigg[\int_{\mathsf{C}}{\sf d}_{\mathsf{C}}^{2}\Big(\exp^{\mathsf{C}}_{x,r}\big(s(\nabla\varphi,2r\varphi)\big),

\displaystyle\exp^{\mathsf{C}}_{x,r}\big(t(\nabla\psi,2r\psi)\big)\Big)\,d\hat{\mu}(x,r)

\displaystyle-{\sf H\!\!K}^{2}\Big(\exp_{\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(s\varphi),\exp_{\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(t\psi)\Big)\Bigg]

provided these limits exist.

(ii)

The twisted part is always nonnegative:

\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\nabla}(\varphi,\psi)\geq 0.

(iii)

For all
a,b>0
,

\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(a\varphi,b\psi)=\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi).

(iv)

For any
a>0
,

\mathsf{Sec}_{a\mu}(\varphi,\psi)=\frac{1}{a}\,\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi).

Similarly,
\mathsf{Sec}_{a\mu}^{\uparrow}(\varphi,\psi)=\frac{1}{a}\,\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\uparrow}(\varphi,\psi)
and

\mathsf{Sec}_{a\mu}^{\nabla}(\varphi,\psi)=\frac{1}{a}\,\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\nabla}(\varphi,\psi)
.

Proof.

(i) and (iii) are obvious.

(ii) follows from the fact that the push forward of
\hat{\mu}
under the map

(x,r)\mapsto\Big(\Pr\Big[\exp^{\mathsf{C}}_{x,r}\big(s(\nabla\varphi,2r\varphi)\big)\Big],\Pr\Big[\exp^{\mathsf{C}}_{x,r}\big(t(\nabla\psi,2r\psi)\big)\Big]\Big)

is a (not necessarily optimal) coupling of the measures
\exp_{\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(s\varphi)
and
\exp_{\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(t\psi)
.

(iv)
Obviously

\Lambda_{a\mu}(\varphi,\psi)=a^{2}\,\Lambda_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi)
for
\Lambda_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi):=|\varphi|_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}\cdot|\psi|_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}-\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}
.
Furthermore,

|\varphi-\psi|_{H^{1}(a\mu)}^{2}=a\,|\varphi-\psi|_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}
and

{\sf H\!\!K}^{2}\Big(\exp_{a\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(s\varphi),\exp_{a\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(t\psi)\Big)=a\,{\sf H\!\!K}^{2}\Big(\exp_{\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(s\varphi),\exp_{\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(t\psi)\Big)

since
\exp_{a\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(s\varphi)=a\,\exp_{\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(s\varphi)
and

{\sf H\!\!K}(a\mu_{0},a\mu_{1})=\sqrt{a}\,{\sf H\!\!K}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
.

The scaling property of

\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\uparrow}\big(\varphi,\psi\big)
is obvious according to the explicit formula given in the next subsection.
This then also entails the scaling property of
\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\nabla}\big(\varphi,\psi\big)
.
∎

Remark 5.3

The scaling property (iv) of the sectional curvature reflects the fact that
({{\mathcal{M}}_{e}},{\sf H\!\!K})
is a cone and

{\sf H\!\!K}^{2}(a\mu,0)=a\,{\sf H\!\!K}^{2}(\mu,0)

For a further scaling property of the sectional curvature, see Theorem

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big[{\sf d}_{\mathsf{C}}^{2}\Big((x,r),U_{t}(x,r)\Big)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{C}}{\sf d}_{\mathsf{C}}^{2}\Big(V_{t}(x,r),

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\nabla}(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{3}{\Lambda\,t^{4}}\bigg[\int_{\mathsf{C}}{\sf d}_{\mathsf{C}}^{2}\Big(V_{t}(x,r),W_{t}(x,r)\Big)\,d\hat{\mu}(x,r)-{\sf H\!\!K}^{2}\Big(\exp_{\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(t\varphi),\exp_{\mu}^{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}(t\psi)\Big)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\Lambda}\,\inf\bigg\{\int_{\sf M}\Big[|F_{0}-\nabla u|^{2}+4|f_{0}-u|^{2}\Big]\,d\mu:\ u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\bigg\}.

Recall that

\displaystyle F_{0}=-2\nabla\big(\xi\varphi\big)-\nabla^{2}\xi\nabla\varphi,\qquad f_{0}=-2\xi\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\xi\nabla\varphi

and
note that

\inf_{u}\big\|(\nabla u,2u)-(F_{0},2f_{0})\|_{\mu}^{2}=\inf_{u}\big\|(\nabla u,2u)-(F^{*},2f^{*})\|_{\mu}^{2}

of the functional

and thus

\int_{\sf M}\Big[-\mathrm{div}_{\mu}F_{0}+4f_{0}+\Delta_{\mu}u_{0}-4u_{0}\Big]\,v\,d\mu=0

for all

Hence,
-\mathrm{div}_{\mu}F_{0}+4f_{0}+\Delta_{\mu}u_{0}-4u_{0}=0
a.e. and

u_{0}=\big(4-\Delta_{\mu}\big)^{-1}\big(-\mathrm{div}_{\mu}F_{0}+4f_{0}\big).

Finally, observe that
F=\nabla f
for

.

\displaystyle\int\big[|F|^{2}-(\mathrm{div}_{\rho}F)\,\big(4-\Delta_{\rho}\big)^{-1}(\mathrm{div}_{\rho}F)\Big]\,d\mu

\displaystyle=4\int(\mathrm{div}_{\rho}F)\Big(-\Delta_{\rho}(4-\Delta_{\rho})\Big)^{-1}(\mathrm{div}_{\rho}F)\,d\mu

\displaystyle=4\int\Big|\Big(-\Delta_{\rho}(4-\Delta_{\rho})\Big)^{-1/2}\mathrm{div}_{\rho}F\Big|^{2}\,d\mu.

,

5.4.

The explicit representation formulas for lifted and twisted parts of the sectional curvature on

Theorem 5.10

Given
\mu=\rho\mathcal{L}^{n}

\rho\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

Proof.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell^{8}}\,\Lambda_{\ell}

\ell\to\infty

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu_{\ell}}^{\uparrow}\big(\varphi_{\ell},\psi_{\ell}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\Lambda_{\ell}}\,\inf\bigg\{\int_{\sf M}\Big[|F_{\ell}-\nabla\eta|^{2}+4\eta^{2}\Big]\,\rho_{\ell}\,dx:\ \eta\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\bigg\}

6.1.
Hellinger-Kantorovich geometry for the torus

In
n=1
, for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
x\in\mathbb{T}
put
f_{k}(x)=e^{ikx}
and

e_{k}(x):=\begin{cases}\sqrt{2}\,\cos(kx),&k>0\\
\sqrt{2}\,\sin(kx),&k<0\\
1,&k=0.\end{cases}

Then

e_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}}s_{k}^{\sigma}\,f_{\sigma k}(x)

with complex unit numbers
s_{k}
for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
defined as

s_{k}:=\begin{cases}1,&k>0\\
-i,&k<0\\
\frac{1-i}{\sqrt{2}},&k=0\end{cases},\quad\textrm{and thus }\ s_{k}^{-1}=\overline{s}_{k}=\begin{cases}1,&k>0\\
i\,&k<0\\
\frac{1+i}{\sqrt{2}},&k=0\end{cases}.

In the multidimensional case, for parameters
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and points
x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
define

f_{k}(x):=\prod_{p=1}^{n}f_{p}(x_{p})=e^{i\langle k,x\rangle}

and

\displaystyle e_{k}(x):=\prod_{p=1}^{n}e_{p}(x_{p})

\displaystyle=2^{-n/2}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\prod_{p=1}^{n}s_{k_{p}}^{\sigma_{p}}\,f_{\sigma_{p}k_{p}}(x_{p})

\displaystyle=2^{-n/2}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}s_{k}^{\sigma}\,f_{k^{\sigma}}(x)

with

s_{k}^{\sigma}:=\prod_{p=1}^{n}s_{k_{p}}^{\sigma_{p}}\in\mathbb{C},|s_{k}^{\sigma}|=1
and
conjugate
parameters

k^{\sigma}:=(\sigma_{1}k_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}k_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

Note that

\Delta e_{k}=-|k|^{2}\,e_{k}

with
|k|:=\left(\sum_{p}k_{p}^{2}\right)^{1/2}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

The family

(e_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}

is a complete ON basis of
L^{2}({\sf M},\mu)
and a complete orthogonal basis of
H^{1}({\sf M},\mu)
. More precisely,

\int e_{k}\,e_{\ell}\,d\mu=0,\qquad\int\langle\nabla e_{k},\nabla e_{\ell}\rangle\,d\mu=0

for all
k\not=\ell
and

\int e_{k}^{2}d\mu=1,\qquad\int|\nabla e_{k}|^{2}d\mu=|k|^{2}.

Thus in particular,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k\ell}

\displaystyle:=|e_{k}|_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}\cdot|e_{\ell}|_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}-\langle e_{k},e_{\ell}\rangle_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}=(4+|k|^{2})\cdot(4+|\ell|^{2}).

for all
k\not=\ell
.

Proof.

Straightforward calculations yield

\nabla e_{k}=2^{-n/2}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}s_{k}^{\sigma}\,\nabla f_{k^{\sigma}}=i\,2^{-n/2}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}s_{k}^{\sigma}\,f_{k^{\sigma}}\,k^{\sigma}

and

\displaystyle\int|\nabla e_{k}|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle=|k|^{2},\qquad\int|e_{k}|^{2}d\mu=1.

Similarly,
\int|\nabla e_{\ell}|^{2}d\mu=|\ell|^{2}
,
\int|e_{\ell}|^{2}d\mu=1
.
Furthermore,

\int e_{k}\,e_{\ell}\,d\mu=0
and
\Delta e_{k}=-|k|^{2}e_{k}
. Thus

\displaystyle\int\langle\nabla e_{k},\nabla e_{\ell}\rangle d\mu=-\int\Delta e_{k}\,e_{\ell}\,d\mu=|k|^{2}\,\int e_{k}\,e_{\ell}\,d\mu=|k|^{2}\,{\mathbb{1}}_{k=\ell}.

∎

Lemma 6.2
.

In the case
n=1
,

\displaystyle s_{k}^{\sigma}\,s_{\ell}^{\tau}\,\overline{s_{k}^{\sigma^{\prime}}}\,\overline{s_{\ell}^{\tau^{\prime}}}\,{\mathbb{1}}_{k^{\sigma}+\ell^{\tau}=k^{\sigma^{\prime}}+\ell^{\tau^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{1}}_{\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma,\tau^{\prime}=\tau}+\Big({\mathbb{1}}_{k=-\ell\not=0}{\mathbb{1}}_{\sigma\tau=1}-{\mathbb{1}}_{k=\ell\not=0}{\mathbb{1}}_{\sigma\tau=-1}\Big)\,{\mathbb{1}}_{\sigma^{\prime}=-\sigma,\tau^{\prime}=-\tau}

\displaystyle-i\,\sigma{\mathbb{1}}_{k=0}{\mathbb{1}}_{\sigma^{\prime}=-\sigma,\tau^{\prime}=\tau}-i\,\tau{\mathbb{1}}_{\ell=0}{\mathbb{1}}_{\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma,\tau^{\prime}=-\tau}-\sigma\tau\,{\mathbb{1}}_{k=0,\ell=0}\,{\mathbb{1}}_{\sigma^{\prime}=-\sigma,\tau^{\prime}=-\tau}.

Proof.

We distinguish 4 cases.

Generic case
k\not=\pm\ell
and
k\ell\not=0
:

here
(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})k+(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\ell=0
implies
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime},\tau=\tau^{\prime}
and thus

s_{k}^{\sigma}\,,\overline{s_{k}^{\sigma^{\prime}}}=1,\qquad s_{\ell}^{\tau}\,\overline{s_{\ell}^{\tau^{\prime}}}=1.

Anticorrelated case
k=-\ell\not=0
:

here
(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})k+(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\ell=0
implies

\sigma+\tau=\sigma^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime}

and thus

s_{k}^{\sigma}\,s_{\ell}^{\tau}\,\overline{s_{k}^{\sigma^{\prime}}}\,\overline{s_{\ell}^{\tau^{\prime}}}=s_{k}^{\sigma+\tau-\sigma^{\prime}-\tau^{\prime}}=1

Correlated case
k=\ell\not=0
:

here
(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})k+(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\ell=0
implies

\sigma+\tau=-(\sigma^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime})

and thus

s_{k}^{\sigma}\,s_{\ell}^{\tau}\,\overline{s_{k}^{\sigma^{\prime}}}\,\overline{s_{\ell}^{\tau^{\prime}}}=s_{k}^{\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}}\,s_{-k}^{\tau-\tau^{\prime}}=\pm 1.

Degenerate case
k\ell=0
:

e.g. if
k=0
,
\ell\not=0
then
(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})k+(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\ell=0
implies

\tau=\tau^{\prime}
whereas
\sigma^{\prime}
is arbitrary.
Thus

s_{k}^{\sigma}\,s_{\ell}^{\tau}\,\overline{s_{k}^{\sigma^{\prime}}}\,\overline{s_{\ell}^{\tau^{\prime}}}=\Big(\frac{1-i}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)^{\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}}\in\{i,-i,1\}.

If
k=\ell=0
then

s_{k}^{\sigma}\,s_{\ell}^{\tau}\,\overline{s_{k}^{\sigma^{\prime}}}\,\overline{s_{\ell}^{\tau^{\prime}}}=\Big(\frac{1-i}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)^{\sigma+\tau-\sigma^{\prime}-\tau^{\prime}}\in\{i,-i,1,-1\}.

Then we verify the claim by considering all 16 possible choices for
\sigma,\tau,\sigma_{,}\tau^{\prime}
.
∎

implies that

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2^{2n+2}}\,\frac{1}{(4+|k|^{2})\cdot(4+|\ell|^{2})}

\displaystyle\cdot\sum_{\sigma,\tau,\sigma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\bigg[\big\langle k^{\sigma}-\ell^{\tau},k^{\sigma^{\prime}}-\ell^{\tau^{\prime}}\big\rangle-\frac{(|k|^{2}-|\ell|^{2})^{2}}{4+|k^{\sigma}+\ell^{\tau}|^{2}}\bigg]\langle k^{\sigma},\ell^{\tau}\rangle\,\langle k^{\sigma^{\prime}},\ell^{\tau^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\prod_{p=1}^{n}\bigg[{\mathbb{1}}_{\sigma^{\prime}_{p}=\sigma_{p},\tau^{\prime}_{p}=\tau_{p}}+\Big({\mathbb{1}}_{k_{p}=-\ell_{p}\not=0}{\mathbb{1}}_{\sigma_{p}\tau_{p}=1}-{\mathbb{1}}_{k_{p}=\ell_{p}\not=0}{\mathbb{1}}_{\sigma_{p}\tau_{p}=-1}\Big)\,{\mathbb{1}}_{\sigma^{\prime}_{p}=-\sigma_{p},\tau^{\prime}_{p}=-\tau_{p}}

6.5.
Renormalization

Already on the torus we see that “typically” the sectional curvature is not summable, that is,

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ric}_{\mu}(e_{k},e_{k})

\displaystyle:=\|e_{k}\|_{H^{1}(\mu)}^{2}\cdot\sum_{\ell\in\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\setminus\{k\}}\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(e_{k},e_{\ell})=+\infty

for most
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. To overcome this divergence, we can consider a renormalized version of the sectional curvature
\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi)
where we replace the normalizing factor
\Lambda_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi)
by its scaled version

\Lambda^{(s)}_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi):=\|\varphi\|^{2}_{H^{s}({\sf M},\mu)}\cdot\|\psi\|^{2}_{H^{s}({\sf M},\mu)}-\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle^{2}_{H^{s}({\sf M},\mu)}

with
\|u\|_{H^{s}({\sf M},\mu)}:=\|(4-\Delta_{\mu})^{s/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
.

Definition 6.18
.

For any Riemannian manifold
({\sf M},{\sf g})
, any
\mu\in{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}
, linearly independent
\varphi,\psi\in{\sf T}^{\sf ana}_{\mu}{{\mathcal{M}}_{e}}
,
and
\Lambda^{(s)}_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi)
for
s\geq 0
as above, we define

\mathsf{Sec}^{(s)}_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi):=\frac{1}{\Lambda^{(s)}_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi)}\,R_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi),\qquad R_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi):=\Lambda_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi)\cdot\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi)

provided
\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(\varphi,\psi)
exists. Furthermore, if
(\varphi_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
denotes an ON basis of
H^{1}({\sf M},\mu)
we define

\mathsf{Ric}^{(s)}_{\mu}(\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j}):=\sum_{i=0,i\not=j}^{\infty}\mathsf{Sec}^{(s)}_{\mu}(\varphi_{j},\varphi_{i})

provided all the involved quantities exist and the sum converges.

Proposition 6.19
.

Let
{\sf M}
be the
n
-dimensional torus,
\mu
its normalized volume measure, and
(e_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}
its eigenbasis for the Laplacian as considered above, and
\varphi_{k}:=(4+|k|^{2})^{-1/2}e_{k}
. Then for every
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and every
s>2+\frac{n}{2}
, the re-normalized Ricci curvature

\mathsf{Ric}^{(s)}_{\mu}(\varphi_{k},\varphi_{k})

exists and is finite.

Proof.

\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{Sec}^{(s)}_{\mu}(\varphi_{k},\varphi_{\ell})}{\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(\varphi_{k},\varphi_{\ell})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda_{\mu}(\varphi_{k},\varphi_{\ell})}{\Lambda^{(s)}_{\mu}(\varphi_{k},\varphi_{\ell})}=\frac{\|\varphi_{k}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\cdot\|\varphi_{\ell}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}}{\|\varphi_{k}\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\cdot\|\varphi_{\ell}\|^{2}_{H^{s}}}=\frac{1}{(4+|k|^{2})^{s-1}\cdot(4+|\ell|^{2})^{s-1}}.

Together with the both-sided estimate
for
\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(\varphi_{k},\varphi_{\ell})=\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(e_{k},e_{\ell})

in Theorem
6.11
this yields for each fixed
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and any
s>1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},\ell\not=k}\Big|\mathsf{Sec}^{(s)}_{\mu}(\varphi_{k},\varphi_{\ell})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},\ell\not=k}\frac{1}{(4+|k|^{2})^{s-1}\cdot(4+|\ell|^{2})^{s-1}}\cdot\Big|\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}(\varphi_{k},\varphi_{\ell})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\,4^{n}\,\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\frac{1}{(4+|k|^{2})^{s-1}\cdot(4+|\ell|^{2})^{s-1}}\cdot\Big(|k|^{2}+|\ell|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=C\,\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\frac{|k|^{2}+|\ell|^{2}}{(4+|\ell|^{2})^{s-1}}.

The latter is finite if and only if

s>\frac{n+4}{2}
.

k=(0,j)
and
\ell=(p,q)
with
p,q,j\not=0
and
q\not=\pm j
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\mathscr{P}}(e_{k},e_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2^{6}}\,\frac{1}{j^{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})}

\displaystyle\cdot\sum_{\sigma,\tau,\sigma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}\in\{-1,1\}^{2}}\bigg[\tau_{1}\tau^{\prime}_{1}p^{2}+\sigma_{2}\sigma_{2}^{\prime}j^{2}+\tau_{2}\tau_{2}^{\prime}q^{2}+(\sigma_{2}\tau_{2}^{\prime}+\tau_{2}\sigma_{2}^{\prime})jq

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\frac{(p^{2}+q^{2}-j^{2})^{2}}{p^{2}+(\sigma_{2}j+\tau_{2}q)^{2}}\bigg]\sigma_{2}\tau_{2}\sigma_{2}^{\prime}\tau_{2}^{\prime}\,j^{2}q^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\Big[{\mathbb{1}}_{\sigma^{\prime}_{1}=\sigma_{1},\tau^{\prime}_{1}=\tau_{1}}-i\sigma_{1}{\mathbb{1}}_{\sigma^{\prime}_{1}=-\sigma_{1},\tau^{\prime}_{1}=-\tau_{1}}\Big]\,{\mathbb{1}}_{\sigma^{\prime}_{2}=\sigma_{2},\tau^{\prime}_{2}=\tau_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}\,\frac{q^{2}}{p^{2}+q^{2}}\,\bigg[p^{2}+q^{2}+j^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma_{2}\in\{-1,1\}}\frac{(p^{2}+q^{2}-j^{2})^{2}}{p^{2}+(\sigma_{2}j+q)^{2}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=3\frac{p^{2}q^{2}j^{2}}{p^{2}+q^{2}}\cdot\frac{p^{2}+q^{2}+j^{2}}{(p^{2}+q^{2}+j^{2})^{2}-4q^{2}j^{2}}.

Thus with
\ell_{m}:=(mj,mj)
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{Sec}_{\mu}^{\mathscr{P}}(e_{k},e_{\ell})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{8}j^{2}\cdot\frac{4m^{4}+2m^{2}}{4m^{4}+1}\geq\frac{3}{8}j^{2}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{0,k\}}\mathsf{Sec}^{\mathscr{P}}_{\mu}(e_{k},e_{\ell})\geq\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\mathsf{Sec}^{\mathscr{P}}_{\mu}(e_{k},e_{\ell_{m}})=+\infty.

∎
