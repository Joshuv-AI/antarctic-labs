Auto-correlation Function Keying
Weijiang Zhao, Yifeng Xiong, Fan Liu, Shuangyang Li, Jianhua Zhang

Abstract
Communication-centric isac ( isac ) is a promising paradigm for future 6g ( 6g ) networks, in which data payload signals are expected to be reused for sensing to enhance time-frequency resource efficiency. For random payload signals, existing studies have mainly characterized the esl ( esl ) of the pacf ( pacf ). However, esl only captures the average sidelobe behavior and does not control large spurious sidelobe peaks in individual payload realizations, which may deteriorate weak-target detection performance. This motivates the design of information-bearing signals whose random pacf satisfies stringent psl ( psl ) constraints. In this paper, we formulate a mutual information maximization problem under psl constraints and a power budget. For quasi-static frequency-flat channels, we show that a continuous acf ( acf )-domain uniform construction provides an asymptotically optimal high- snr ( snr ) design principle. Motivated by this principle, we propose acfk ( acfk ), a finite-constellation modulation architecture that embeds data symbols directly onto the acf -domain sidelobes. acfk enables exact control of the nominal pacf , which coincides with the actual pacf when a spectral non-negativity constraint is met. When this is not the case, we quantify the non-negativity violation probability and bound the resulting pslr ( pslr ) degradation. We further provide a reference isac transceiver design for acfk over quasi-static multipath channels, together with high- snr approximate ber ( ber ) analysis. Numerical results validate the theoretical analysis and show that, compared with a generalized pas ( pas ) baseline, acfk provides substantially stronger pslr control and improved weak-target detection performance under comparable sensing and communication settings.

\displaystyle\hskip 8.53581pt+\log\int_{{\cal{P}}}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}|[\bm{h}_{\rm c}]_{i}|^{-2}}{\rm d}\bm{p}^{\prime}\!+\!\log 2\!+\!o(1).

\displaystyle I(\bm{\mathsfbr{x}};\bm{\mathsfbr{y}}_{\rm c})

\displaystyle=\frac{2N-1}{2}\log\Bigl(\frac{|\bar{h}|^{2}}{e\sigma_{\rm c}^{2}}\Bigr)+\log|{\cal{P}}|

\displaystyle\hskip 8.53581pt+\log(2\sqrt{\pi})+\frac{1}{4}\log N+o(1),

(18)

for which a detailed estimate of

In light of this, let us take a closer look at the structure of the feasible region
{\cal{M}}
. To elaborate, under the
\bm{\mathsfbr{u}}
-coordinate, it consists of the following constraints

\displaystyle\bm{\stheta}\in\mathbb{T}^{N},~\bm{\mathsfbr{p}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(20a)

\displaystyle\mathsfbr{p}_{i}\geqslant 0,~\forall i=1,\dotsc,N,

(20b)

\displaystyle|\bm{f}_{i}^{\rm H}\bm{\mathsfbr{p}}|^{2}\leqslant t,~\forall i\in{\cal{S}}_{\rm ACF},

(20c)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathsfbr{p}_{i}={N}.

In that case, the uniform distribution over the feasible region would factorize into a product of one-dimensional uniform distributions, thereby significantly simplifying the codebook. To this end, we consider the inverse

\bm{p}
to the

\displaystyle\bm{\stheta}\in\mathbb{T}^{N},

(22a)

\displaystyle\sqrt{[\bm{\mathsfbr{a}}]_{2i-1}^{2}+[\bm{\mathsfbr{a}}]_{2i}^{2}}\leqslant\sqrt{tN^{-1}},~\forall i=1,\dotsc,\Bigl\lfloor\frac{N-1}{2}\Bigr\rfloor,

\displaystyle\bm{A}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{\frac{N-1}{2}}\otimes[1,~i]\\

to

Transmitter Design

As can be observed from the top row of Fig.

{\cal{S}}_{\rm{s}}

\bm{\mathsfbr{x}}

\bm{\mathsfbr{x}}_{\rm{ACFK}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}

From a practical perspective,
acfk
can be implemented by modifying and amalgamating the existing
ofdm
and
sc

5

For the channel coding, we employ a joint coding scheme, in which the information bits modulated on amplitude and phase are jointly encoded using a single codebook. Naturally, an alternative approach is to modulate information bits separately on amplitude and phase, referred to as the “separate coding scheme” hereafter. As we shall see in Sec.
VII
, the joint coding scheme enables encoded bits under favorable channel conditions to assist the decoding of bits suffering from poor channel quality, thereby yielding a lower total

Remark 6

Finally, we would like to discuss the relationship between the non-negativity violation and
acfk
-induced deep fading. As shown in (
98

acf
symbol vector, follows a folded normal distribution

. Since the probability mass of the normal distribution over
(-\infty,0]
is folded onto
[0,\infty)

VIII
Conclusions

In this paper, we have investigated the payload-bearing waveform design problem, which imposes stringent constraints on the sidelobes of random
pacf
while maintaining a high achievable communication rate. Specifically, built upon the mutual information maximization framework under
psl
and power budget constraints, we have shown that the continuous
acf
-domain uniform construction serves as an asymptotically optimal high-
snr
design principle under
psl
constraints over quasi-static frequency-flat channels. In this context, we have proposed
acfk
, which can be interpreted as a structured implementation of this principle in practical finite-constellation systems. From the sensing perspective,
acfk
directly embeds data symbols into the
acf
-domain sidelobes, thereby enabling exact control of the nominal
pacf
and high-probability control of the actual
pacf
under potential violation of a power spectral non-negativity constraint. We have provided a reference
isac
transceiver design for
acfk
over quasi-static multipath channels, and analyzed the high-
snr
approximate
ber
. From a practical perspective,
acfk
can be implemented by modifying and amalgamating the existing
ofdm
and
sc
modulation schemes. Numerical results have demonstrated that
acfk
outperforms a generalized
pas
baseline in
pslr
control and weak-target detection under comparable sensing and communication settings. Future work will explore further enhancements in
acfk
system design, for example, developing practical coding strategies for data sequences satisfying the non-negativity constraint, as well as investigating the optimal communication receiver design and performance analysis.

Proof:
