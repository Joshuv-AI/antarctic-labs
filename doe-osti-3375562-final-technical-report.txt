Final Technical Report

 US Department of Energy, Office of Science, High Energy Physics

DOE Award Number: DE-SC0026193
Recipient Institution: Michigan State University
Project Title: Ensuring the Longevity of the DUNE Detectors through Contaminant Monitoring
Principal Investigator: Sophie Berkman
Project Dates: April 1, 2025 – March 31, 2026

Project Goals:
This project had two main goals:
 1. MicroBooNE physics analysis, including a new measurement of the electron neutrino
 interaction cross section on an argon target.
 2. Initial work to set up a facility for construction of X-Arapuca photon detectors for the
 DUNE vertical drift far detector.

Personnel funded by this award:
Principal Investigator (PI): Sophie Berkman
Postdoctoral researcher (PD): Lucile Mellet

Accomplishments:

MicroBooNE:
Through this award the PI and PD led a new measurement of the electron neutrino interaction
cross section on an argon target (product [1]). Measurements of electron neutrinos are
particularly critical for upcoming DUNE precision oscillation measurements. Current oscillation
measurements are limited by our understanding of neutrino-nucleus interactions and the
associated systematic uncertainties, and must be reduced for DUNE. This measurement is one of
only three measurements of electron neutrinos on an argon target (other measurements combine
electron neutrinos and anti-neutrinos in their signal definition). It expands on a previous
measurement of electron neutrinos (also led by the PI), to now use all of MicroBooNE’s data
from the Fermilab Booster Neutrino Beamline as well as other key analysis updates including the
treatment of detector systematic uncertainties and the addition of the cosmic ray tagger for
additional background rejection. This measurement provides comparisons to many modern
generators used to model neutrino interactions. It is complimentary to a similar measurement
using electron neutrinos from the NuMI beamline (product [2]) because the beam is on-axis,
which makes it possible to measure the electron and proton directions relative to the neutrino
direction, and also means the measurement has a smaller flux uncertainty. This measurement
provides critical electron neutrino data for future model development.

In addition, the PI serves as senior advisor to the MicroBooNE cross section group where she
provides review of analyses and support to the postdoc conveners of the group. This working
group had 6 papers (products [1]-[6]) submitted and subsequently accepted by journals during
the award term.

DUNE X-Arapuca Construction:
Through this award the PI and PD began the work to provide DUNE with an assembly factory
for X-Arapuca light detectors at Michigan State University. This involved meeting with project
management to determine the scope of the project and put the project funding in place through a
Fermilab subcontract that is now in its final stages. The space for the project was secured and
preparations for set-up have begun. In addition, the PD and PI traveled to Northern Illinois
University (supported by PI’s startup funds) to build a X-Arapuca frame and gain familiarity
with the process to use in the development of the MSU facility.

Project Impact and Outcomes:

This project provided a new measurement of electron neutrino interactions on an argon target
performed by the PI and PD. It also provided effort from the PI to serve in a leadership role on

the MicroBooNE experiment, resulting in 6 measurements published or accepted for upcoming
publication. Finally it provided the PI and PD’s time to prepare to set up an assembly facility for
X-Arapuca light detectors, now well underway.
