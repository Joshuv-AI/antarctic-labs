Cyclic phenomena for composition operators on weighted Bergman spaces
Artur Blois

Abstract
. In this article, we study cyclic phenomena on the full range of weighted Bergman spaces on the right half-plane 𝒜 α 2 ​ ( ℂ + ) . \mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}). We completely characterize cyclic and supercyclic composition operators induced by affine self-maps of ℂ + , \mathbb{C}_{+}, thereby completing the characterization of all hypercyclic, supercyclic and cyclic composition operators induced by affine symbols on 𝒜 α 2 ​ ( ℂ + ) . \mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 47A16, 47B33, Secundary: 30H20

1.
Introduction

The study of cyclic phenomena has been one of the central research topics in linear dynamics in recent decades, more specifically, hypercyclic, supercyclic and cyclic operators. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a separable Banach space. If
T
is a linear operator acting on
\mathcal{X},
we say that
T
is
hypercyclic
if there exists a non-zero vector
f\in\mathcal{X}
such that the orbit
\{T^{n}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is dense
\mathcal{X}.
The first example of hypercyclic operator on a Banach space is due to Rolewicz
[
Rolewicz
]
on the sequence space
\ell^{p},
for
1\leq p<\infty.
Prior to Rolewicz, the only examples of hypercyclic operators were on the Fréchet space of entire functions due to Birkhoff and MacLane
[
2
,
Maclane
]
. Since then, many advances have been made on the topic and we suggest to the reader the recent monograph
[
Bayart-Matheron
]
for a more in-depth view of hypercyclic operators.

A weaker concept of hypercyclic operators are the supercyclic operators. Recall that if
T
is linear operator on
\mathcal{X},
it is said to be
supercyclic
if there exist a non-zero vector
f\in\mathcal{X}
such that the projective orbit
\{\lambda T^{n}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\,,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\}
is dense in
\mathcal{X}.
It is easy to see that every hypercyclic operator is supercyclic, however the converse is not in general true. It was first introduced by Hilden and Wallen in
[
HW
]
and since then many researchers have been interested in the subject, for instance,
[
Ansari-Bourdon
,
1
,
GG-MR
,
Kumar
,
MR-Shkarin
]
. Another important definition is of
n
-
supercyclicity
, which instead of asking for the projective orbit of vector being dense, we ask for the projective orbit of a
n
-dimensional vector space, for
n\geq 2,
to be dense in
\mathcal{X}.
Is worth noting that every supercyclic operator is
n
-supercylic but there exists a
n
-supercyclic operator which is not supercyclic
[
Feldman
]
.

Also, a linear operator
T
is said to be
cyclic
on
\mathcal{X}
if there exists a non-zero vector
f
such that
{\rm span}\,\{T^{n}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is dense in
\mathcal{X}.
Is easy to see that every supercyclic operator is cyclic, but again the converse is not true in general.

In the setting of spaces of holomorphic functions, one of the most studied classes of operators is of composition operators. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a domain in the complex plane and let
\mathcal{S}(\Omega)
be a space of holomorphic functions on
\Omega
. We define the composition operator with holomorphic symbol
\phi:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega
by

C_{\phi}:\mathcal{S}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(\Omega),\quad f\mapsto f\circ\phi,\quad f\in\mathcal{S}(\Omega).

While studying spaces of holomorphic functions, one of the most studied symbols are the linear fractional symbols, that is, functions of the form

\eta(z)=\dfrac{az+b}{cz+d},

whose coefficients satisfy
ad-bc\neq 0
and
\eta(\Omega)\subseteq\Omega.
For the weighted Dirichlet space of the disk (which includes the classical Hardy, Bergman and Dirichlet spaces), the recent monograph by Gallardo-Gutiérrez and Montes-Rodríguez
[
GG-MRbook
]
completely characterize all cyclic phenomena for linear fractional symbols.

In
[
Noor-Severiano
]
, Noor and Severiano studied hypercyclicity and cyclicity for composition operators induced by linear fractional symbols on the Hardy space of right half-plane,
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),
and showed that no composition operator is hypercyclic but in some cases they are cyclic, leaving the question open if there were any supercyclic composition operators. Recently, Kumar
[
Kumar
]
showed that
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
supports no supercyclic composition operator induced by linear fractional symbols, however due to minor technical mistake the conclusion is incorrect. The aim of this paper is to further extend the study of composition operators on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
started in
[
Blois-Severiano
]
, to correct and extend the proof of supercyclicity given in
[
Kumar
]
and completely characterize
n
-supercyclicity and cyclicity among said operators.

This article is organized as follows: Section
2
will be dedicated to the preliminaries, Section
3
we characterize supercyclicity, Section
4
we characterize
n
-supercyclicity and Section
5
we characterize cyclicity.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper
\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of all non-negative integers,
\mathbb{C_{+}}
the open right-half plane
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\rm Re}\,(z)>0\},

\mathbb{D}
the open unit disk
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\},

\mathbb{R}
denotes the set of the real numbers and
\mathbb{R}^{+}
denotes the set of positive real numbers.

For
\alpha>-1,
we define the weighted Bergman space over the right-half plane, denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),
as the space of all holomorphic functions
f
on
\mathbb{C}_{+}
such that the norm

\|f\|=\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}_{+}}|f(x+iy)|^{2}x^{\alpha}dxdy\right)^{1/2}

is finite. In particular,
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, where the family
\{k^{\alpha}_{w}:w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}\}
given by

k^{\alpha}_{w}(s)=\dfrac{2^{\alpha}(\alpha+1)}{(s+\overline{w})^{(\alpha+2)}},\quad s\in\mathbb{C}_{+},

are the reproducing kernels. These kernels define a continuous evaluation functional for all
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
These spaces are closely related to the Hardy space over the right half-plane, in fact, it is well-known in the literature that the ordinary Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
can be seen as the “limit case” as
\alpha\rightarrow-1
(see for instance
[
Elliot-Wynn
]
). Similarly, we define the weighted Bergman space over the unit disk, denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}),
as the space of all holomorphic functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
such that the norm

\|f\|=\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z)\right)^{1/2}

is finite, where
dA
denotes the normalized area measure on
\mathbb{D}.

The weighted Bergman spaces have a canonical isomorphism between
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})
and
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
given by

(2.1)

J:\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),\quad(Jf)(w)=f\left(\dfrac{w-1}{w+1}\right)\dfrac{2^{\alpha+1}}{(w+1)^{\alpha+2}}.

There exists a version of the Paley-Wiener theorem for
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}):
Let
\mu_{\alpha}
be the weighted Lebesgue measure given by

d\mu_{\alpha}=\dfrac{\pi\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{2^{\alpha}t^{\alpha+1}}dt,

where
\Gamma
denotes the usual gamma function and
dt
is the usual Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{+}.
Let
\mathcal{L}
denote the Laplace transform given by

(\mathcal{L}F)(w)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(t)e^{-wt}dt,\quad w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

By
[
Elliot-Wynn
, Lemma 3.3]
,
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is isometrically isomorphic to
L^{2}_{\alpha}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},d\mu_{\alpha})
via the Laplace transform. So
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if there exists
F\in L^{2}_{\alpha}
such that

(2.2)

f(w)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(t)e^{-wt}dt,\quad w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

Our focus on this article are the composition operators, Elliot and Wynn
[
Elliot-Wynn
]
proved the following boundedness criterion for composition operators on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})

[
Elliot-Wynn
, Theorem 3.4]

Theorem 1
.

Let
\phi
be a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{C}_{+},
then
C_{\phi}
is bounded on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if
\phi
has finite angular derivative at
\infty,
that is,
\phi(\infty)=\infty
and the non-tangential limit

\phi^{\prime}(\infty)=\lim_{w\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{w}{\phi(w)}

exists and is finite.

In general, the norm of composition operators are not completely understood and we mainly have upper bounds for it, however, the norm of
C_{\phi}
is precisely given by
\|C_{\phi}\|=(\phi^{\prime}(\infty))^{(\alpha+2)/2}
on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
The only linear fractional symbols that induce bounded composition operators are the symbols of the form

(2.3)

\phi(w)=aw+b,\quad\text{where $a>0$ and ${\rm Re}\,(b)\geq 0$,}

which for simplicity we will call them
affine symbols
and operators induced by them
affine composition operators
. Among the affine symbols, we may categorize them in the following manner

Definition
.

Let
\phi
be an affine symbol:

•

If
a=1
we say that
\phi
is of parabolic type. If additionally
{\rm Re}\,(b)=0,
we say that
\phi
is a parabolic automorphism.

•

If
a\neq 1
we say that
\phi
is of hyperbolic type. If additionally
{\rm Re}\,(b)=0,
we say that
\phi
is a hyperbolic automorphism.

•

If
\phi
is of hyperbolic type, we say that
\phi
is of type I if
a\in(0,1)
and
\phi
is of type II if
a\in(1,\infty).

If
\phi
is as (
2.3
), we may compute the iterates of
\phi
as follows

(2.4)

\displaystyle\phi^{[n]}(w)=\begin{cases}w+nb,&\text{if}\ a=1,\\
a^{n}w+\frac{\left(1-a^{n}\right)}{1-a}b,&\text{if}\ a\neq 1,\end{cases}

hence
C^{n}_{\phi}=C_{\phi^{[n]}}.
By this iterate behavior we observe

Lemma 2
.

Let
\phi
be an affine symbol such that
a\in(0,1).
Then
\phi^{[n]}\rightarrow b/(1-a)
locally uniformly as
n\rightarrow\infty.

In
[
Blois-Severiano
, Lemma 3.7]
, the authors were able to show that the composition operator
C_{\phi}
is unitarily equivalent to the weighted composition operator
\widehat{C}_{\phi}
in
L^{2}_{\alpha}
given by

(2.5)

(\widehat{C}_{\phi}F)(t)=\dfrac{1}{a}e^{-bt}F(t/a),\quad F\in L^{2}_{\alpha}.

3.
Supercyclicity

We devote this section to the study of supercyclicity among affine composition operators on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
Here we correct the proof of
[
Kumar
]
and further extend the characterization of supercyclicity for the weighted Bergman space and we start the section by stating its main result.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\phi
be an affine symbol. Then
C_{\phi}
is supercyclic on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if
\phi
is hyperbolic of type II.

We divide the main result in a few steps, one of the key ingredients is the normality among said operators. By
[
Blois-Severiano
, Proposition 3.8]
, we know that the normality condition holds if and only if
a=1
or
{\rm Re}\,(b)=0.
In
[
HW
]
Hilden and Wallen proved that no normal operator can be supercyclic, so we are able to discard supercyclicity for some of our operators.

Proposition 4
.

If
\phi
is of parabolic type or is a hyperbolic automorphism, then
C_{\phi}
is not supercyclic on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

Proof.

If
\phi
is a hyperbolic automorphism, then we have
{\rm Re}\,(b)=0
which implies that
C_{\phi}
is normal and therefore is not supercyclic on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
On the other hand, if
\phi
is of parabolic type, then
a=1
which again implies that
C_{\phi}
is normal and the conclusion holds.
∎

Now we address the hyperbolic type symbols. Note that if
\phi
is of type I, we know that
b/(1-a)
is a fixed point of
\phi
and we will make use of Lemma
2

Proposition 5
.

If
\phi
is hyperbolic of type I, then
C_{\phi}
is not supercyclic on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a supercyclic vector
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
for
C_{\phi},
then given any function
g\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
there exists a sequence
(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}
such that

\|a_{k}C^{n_{k}}_{\phi}f-g\|\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as $k\rightarrow\infty$}.

In particular, since
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we get

|a_{k}f(\phi^{[n_{k}]}(w))-g(w)|\leq\|a_{k}C^{n_{k}}_{\phi}f-g\|\|k^{\alpha}_{w}\|,\quad w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

Hence we obtain a point-wise convergence

(3.1)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}a_{k}f(\phi^{[n_{k}]}(w))=g(w),\quad w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

By Lemma
2
, we have a local uniform convergence of
\phi^{[n_{k}]},
so up to a subsequence we note that the left-hand side of (
3.1
) is independent of
w,
thus the function
g
is constant and therefore it is the null function since
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
does not admit any other constant function. However, this is a contradiction to the assumption that
f
is a supercyclic vector and we conclude that
C_{\phi}
does not admit a supercyclic vector on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

∎

Note that if we take a non-automorphic self-map of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
with a fixed point in
\mathbb{C}_{+}
, the proof of Proposition
5
still holds. Now, we need only to prove that every hyperbolic of type II symbol induce a supercyclic composition operator on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
In
[
Kumar
, Theorem 3.20]
, it claims that on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
a composition operator induced by a hyperbolic of type II symbol is a scalar multiple of a composition operator induced by hyperbolic of type I, which is true to its adjoint as is stated in
[
Noor-Severiano
, Proposition 1]
.

Proposition 6
.

If
\phi
is hyperbolic of type II, then
C_{\phi}
is supercyclic on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

Proof.

It is known in the literature that the operator
C_{\phi}
is similar to
C_{\psi_{a}},
where
\psi_{a}(w)=aw+a(1-a^{-1}).
Recall the isomorphism given in (
2.1
), a straightforward calculation shows that

J(C_{\varphi_{a}f})(w)=\left(\dfrac{\psi_{a}(w)+1}{w+1}\right)^{\alpha+2}C_{\psi_{a}}(Jf)(w),

where
\varphi_{a}
is given by
\varphi_{a}(z)=a^{-1}z+1-a^{-1}.
Thus,
C_{\varphi_{a}}
is similar to a
a^{\alpha+2}C_{\psi_{a}}.
By
[
FRhyper
, Proposition 5.2]
, it follows that
C_{\varphi_{a}}
is hypercyclic on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}).
Via the isomorphism
J,
we get that
a^{\alpha+2}C_{\psi_{a}}
is hypercyclic on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
and in particular it is supercyclic. We use a known fact that if a linear operator is supercyclic, any non-null scalar multiple is supercyclic, from which we conclude that
C_{\psi_{a}}
is supercyclic and by similarity,
C_{\phi}
is supercyclic, completing the proof.
∎

The proofs above can be adapted to the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
setting by simply using the characterization of normality given in
[
Noor-Severiano
, Theorem 2]
, the fixed point argument also holds with the same arguments and finally by using the canonical isomorphism between
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
(see
[
Noor-Carmo
]
for a discussion in this subject) and the fact that
C_{\varphi_{a}}
is hypercyclic in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
(see
[
GG-MRbook
]
or
[
Shapiro
]
). Therefore, the classical cyclic phenomena for the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is now completely characterized for affine composition operators, completing the work of Noor and Severiano
[
Noor-Severiano
]
.

4.
n
-supercyclicity

In this section we completely characterize
n
-supercyclicity for affine composition operators on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
For this goal we first characterize hyponormality among these operators

Definition 7
.

Let
T
be a linear operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}.
We say that
T
is hyponormal if
T^{*}T\geq TT^{*}.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\phi
be an affine symbol. Then
C_{\phi}
is hypornormal if and only if
a\leq 1
or
{\rm Re}\,(b)=0.

Proof.

By the Paley-Wiener theorem,
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is isometrically isomorphic to
L^{2}_{\alpha}
and
C_{\phi}
is equivalent to a weighted composition operator
\widehat{C}_{\phi}
given by equation (
2.5
) and also by
[
Blois-Severiano
, Proposition 3.8]
we have the following

(\widehat{C}_{\phi}^{*}\widehat{C}_{\phi}F)(t)=a^{-(\alpha+2)}e^{-2{\rm Re}\,(b)t}F(t)\quad\text{and}\quad(\widehat{C}_{\phi}\widehat{C}_{\phi}^{*}F)(t)=a^{-(\alpha+2)}e^{-2{\rm Re}\,(b)t/a}F(t),

therefore the condition of
\widehat{C}_{\phi}^{*}\widehat{C}_{\phi}\geq\widehat{C}_{\phi}\widehat{C}_{\phi}^{*}
is equivalent to

e^{-2{\rm Re}\,(b)t}\geq e^{-2{\rm Re}\,(b)t/a}.

The above relation is valid if and only if
{\rm Re}\,(b)=0
or
a\leq 1.

∎

In
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
, Bayart and Matheron proved that no hyponormal operator can be
n
-supercyclic. Thus with the characterization of hyponormality, we are able to conclude the following

Theorem 9
.

Let
\phi
be an affine symbol. Then
C_{\phi}
is
n
-supercyclic on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if
\phi
is hyperbolic of type II.

Proof.

By Lemma
8
, every symbol except a hyperbolic of type II is hyponormal and therefore cannot be
n
-supercyclic. By Theorem
3
, only hyperbolic of type II symbols are
n
-supercyclic since they are supercyclic.
∎

5.
Cyclicity

In this section, we characterize cyclicity among affine composition operators. Some of the techniques used in this section are adapted arguments from
[
GG-MRAdjoints
,
Noor-Severiano
]
. We start this section by presenting an adjoint formula for the affine composition operators only in terms of operators in
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

Proposition 10
.

Let
\phi
be an affine symbol. Then the adjoint of
C_{\phi}
acting on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is given by

C^{*}_{\phi}=a^{-(\alpha+2)}C_{\psi},

where
\psi(w)=a^{-1}w+a^{-1}\overline{b}.

Proof.

We know that if
\phi
is an affine symbol, then
C_{\phi}
is unitarily equivalent to the operator

(\widehat{C}_{\phi}F)(t)=\dfrac{1}{a}e^{-bt/a}F(t/a)

acting on
L^{2}_{\alpha}.
Now the adjoint in
L^{2}_{\alpha}
is given by

(\widehat{C}^{*}_{\phi}F)(t)=a^{-(\alpha+1)}e^{-\overline{b}t}F(at).

A straightforward calculation shows that

\widehat{C}^{*}_{\phi}\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(a^{-(\alpha+2)}C_{\psi}),

where
\psi(w)=a^{-1}w+a^{-1}\overline{b}.

∎

Its clear that if
C_{\phi}
is induced by a hyperbolic of type I symbol, its adjoint is a scalar multiple of a composition operator induced by a hyperbolic of type II symbol and vice versa. We first characterize cyclicity for operators induced by hyperbolic type symbols. In
[
Bourdon-Shapiro
, Proposition 2.7]
, Bourdon and Shapiro proved that if the adjoint of a linear operator
T
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
has a multiple eigenvalue, then
T
is not cyclic.

Proposition 11
.

If
\phi
is hyperbolic of type I, then
C_{\phi}
is not cyclic on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

Proof.

Take the function

f_{\lambda}(w)=\left(w+\dfrac{b}{a-1}\right)^{\lambda}

where
a>1
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)>0.
Is easy to see that
f_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if
{\rm Re}\,(\lambda)<\dfrac{-(\alpha+2)}{2}.
We want to show that functions of the form
f_{\lambda}
are eigenvectors for a composition operator induced by a hyperbolic of type II symbol. Let
\psi(w)=aw+b
denote a hyperbolic of type II symbol, thus

(C_{\psi}f_{\lambda})(w)=\left(aw+b+\dfrac{b}{a-1}\right)^{\lambda}=a^{\lambda}f_{\lambda}(w).

Now we show that
a^{\lambda}
is an eigenvalue of infinite multiplicity. Let
\lambda_{n}=\lambda+\frac{2\pi n}{\log a}i
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and we get

C_{\psi}f_{\lambda_{n}}=a^{\lambda_{n}}f_{\lambda_{n}}=a^{\lambda}f_{\lambda_{n}},\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
(f_{\lambda_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an infinite family of functions, follows that
a^{\lambda}
is an eigenvalue of infinite multiplicity for
C_{\psi}.
Therefore the adjoint of
C_{\phi}
has a multiple eigenvalue and thus
C_{\phi}
cannot be cyclic.
∎

Now, from Proposition
6
, its clear that if
\phi
is hyperbolic of type II then
C_{\phi}
is cyclic on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
Now we address the automorphic case, which we adapt techniques used by Gallardo-Gutiérrez and Montes-Rodríguez in
[
GG-MRAdjoints
]
.

Proposition 12
.

If
\phi
is a hyperbolic automorphism, then
C_{\phi}
is not cyclic on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

Proof.

If
\phi
is a hyperbolic automorphism, then
a\neq 1
and
{\rm Re}\,(b)=0.
We consider the parabolic automorphism given by

\tau(w)=w+\dfrac{b}{1-a}

and we note that
C^{-1}_{\tau}C_{\phi}C_{\tau}=C_{\varphi},
where
\varphi(w)=aw.
Therefore
C_{\phi}
is similar to
C_{\varphi}
and by Laplace transform we obtain that
C_{\varphi}
is equivalent to
\widehat{C}_{\varphi}
as in (
2.5
).

Now define the linear operator given by

(TF)(t)=\left(\dfrac{\pi\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{2^{\alpha}t^{\alpha}}\right)^{1/2}F(t)

and we note that the operator
T
induces an isometry from
L^{2}_{\alpha}
onto
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dt/t).
In particular, one can show that

T\widehat{C}_{\varphi}=V_{a}T,

where
V_{a}
is a linear operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dt/t)
given by

(V_{a}F)(t)=a^{\frac{-(\alpha+2)}{2}}F(t/a).

If we consider the Fourier transform with respect to the multiplicative group of positive real numbers, namely

\hat{F}(t)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(x)x^{-it}\dfrac{dx}{x},

it defines an isometry from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dt/t)
onto
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},2\pi dt)
and the operator
V_{a}
is unitarily similar to the operator
a^{\frac{-(\alpha+2)}{2}}M_{\eta},
where
M_{\eta}
is the multiplication operator with symbol
\eta(t)=a^{-it},
acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},2\pi dt).
So far we showed that the operator
C_{\phi}
is unitarily similar to
a^{\frac{-(\alpha+2)}{2}}M_{\eta}
and now suffices to show that
M_{\eta}
is not cyclic. We may rewrite
\eta
in the following fashion

a^{-it}=e^{\log a^{-it}}=e^{-it\log a}.

In
[
Noor-Severiano
, Lemma 8]
, Noor and Severiano showed that a multiplication operator induced by a symbol of the form
e^{ist},
with
s\in\mathbb{R},
is not cyclic on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},2\pi dt)
and hence
C_{\phi}
cannot be cyclic on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

∎

Finally, we characterize cyclicity among composition operators induced by symbols of parabolic type

Proposition 13
.

If
\phi
is of parabolic type, then
C_{\phi}
is cyclic on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if
{\rm Re}\,(b)>0.

Proof.

By equation (
2.5
), we have that
C_{\phi}
in unitarily equivalent to
\widehat{C}_{\phi},
which is given by

(\widehat{C}_{\phi}F)(t)=e^{-bt}F(t)

and we may rewrite
e^{-bt}
as

e^{-{\rm Re}\,(b)t}e^{-i{\rm Im}\,(b)t}.

By
[
Seid
, Theorem 3.3]
,
\widehat{C}_{\phi}
is cyclic if and only
e^{-bt}
is essentially univalent. Is easy to see that
e^{-bt}
is essentially univalent if and only if
{\rm Re}\,(b)>0
(otherwise it would a periodic function), thus the result holds.
