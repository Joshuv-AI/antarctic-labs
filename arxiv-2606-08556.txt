Abstract
. We establish several improvements to the main results of [ 37 ] and [ 5 ] , refining the seminal self-improving method for generalized Poincaré inequalities from [ 12 , 27 ] . These results, together with various related applications, stem from a general self-improving property for functions satisfying the local inequality 1 | Q | ​ ∫ Q | f ⁡ ( x ) − f Q | ​ 𝑑 x ≤ a ⁡ ( Q ) \frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|\,dx\leq a(Q) for all cubes Q ⊂ ℝ n Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} . The functional a a is assumed to obey a specific discrete geometric summability condition. By restricting our focus to axis-parallel cubes in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , this geometric setting allows us to obtain sharper estimates than those available in more general metric measure spaces.

1.
Introduction and main results

The classical local
(1,1)
Poincaré inequality asserts that there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
such that for every
f\in W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and every cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
one has

(1.1)

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|\,dx\leq c_{n}\,\ell(Q)\,\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|\nabla f(x)|\,dx,

where
f_{Q}=\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}f(y)\,dy
denotes the average of
f
over
Q
, and
\ell(Q)
is the side length of
Q
.

This inequality enjoys a self-improving property that has been the focus of recent studies. Notably, this phenomenon does not stem from the presence of the gradient on the right-hand side of (
1.1
), but rather is a consequence of a discrete summation condition inherent to the functional therein. Motivated by this, we consider inequalities of the form

(1.2)

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|\,dx\leq a(Q),

valid for every cube
Q
, where
a
is a functional defined on the family of all cubes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes.
Inequalities of this type, featuring a local oscillation term on the left-hand side and a functional
a(Q)
on the right, will be referred to as generalized Poincaré inequalities.

Generalized Poincaré inequalities and their self-improving properties were first studied in a general framework by Franchi, the second author,
and Wheeden
[
12
]
; see also
[
27
,
25
]
for further refinements.
In this setting, the functional
a
is required to satisfy a suitable geometric discrete summation condition.
This approach provides a way to obtain weighted Poincaré–Sobolev inequalities without relying on classical representation formulas.

A major motivation for studying weighted Poincaré–Sobolev inequalities comes from the regularity theory of degenerate elliptic equations. In their seminal work, Fabes, Kenig, and Serapioni
[
11
]
used such inequalities as part of the Moser iteration scheme to prove local Hölder regularity for weak solutions of degenerate elliptic equations whose ellipticity is controlled by an
A_{2}
weight (see also
[
16
]
).

While the self-improving results in these earlier works hold in the more general setting of spaces of homogeneous type, the specific geometry of cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(with sides parallel to the axes) allows for a more delicate analysis. In the present work, we exploit this framework to obtain more precise estimates than those treated in
[
12
]
or
[
27
]
. Within this context, the discrete summation condition introduced in
[
12
]
can be stated as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
w
be any weight in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. We say that the functional
a
satisfies the weighted
D_{p}(w)
condition for
0<p<\infty
if there is a constant
C
such that, for any cube
Q
and any family
\{Q_{j}\}_{j}
of pairwise disjoint subcubes of
Q
, the following inequality holds:

\sum_{j}a(Q_{j})^{p}w(Q_{j})\leq C^{p}a(Q)^{p}w(Q),

where
w(E)=\int_{E}w(x)dx
. The best possible constant
C
above is denoted by
\left\|a\right\|_{D_{p}(w)}
, and in this case we write
a\in D_{p}(w)
.

In
[
12
]
it was proved that if
w\in A_{\infty}
and
a\in D_{p}(w)
, then the starting point inequality (
1.2
) implies a weighted weak
L^{p}
generalized Poincaré inequality. More recently, the dependence of the constants in the main result was improved in
[
5
]
, always in the context of cubes as a consequence of an extension of the John–Nirenberg theorem. For completeness, we state the latest version of this result below.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
12
,
5
]
)
.

Let
w\in A_{\infty}
and let
1<p<\infty
. Consider a functional
a
satisfying the
D_{p}(w)
condition with constant
\left\|a\right\|_{D_{p}(w)}
. Let
f
be a locally integrable function such that

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|dx\leq a(Q)

for every cube
Q
. Then, for every cube
Q
,

(1.3)

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n}p[w]_{A_{\infty}}\left\|a\right\|_{D_{p}(w)}a(Q).

As a consequence of the so-called Kolmogorov inequality, (
1.3
) yields the following strong estimate:

\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|^{q}w(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq c_{n}\left(\frac{p}{p-q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}p[w]_{A_{\infty}}\left\|a\right\|_{D_{p}(w)}\,a(Q),

for all
0<q<p
. Thus, the
D_{p}(w)
condition implies that (
1.2
) improves to a weighted
L^{q}
inequality for every
0<q<p
, although in general it does not yield the strong norm
L^{p}
.
In the case of functionals for which the truncation method (or a weak-implies-strong argument) is applicable, (
1.3
) does indeed imply the
L^{p}
estimate (see
[
23
]
). More recently, the condition introduced in Definition
1.1
was sharpened in
[
37
]
.
This more refined formulation is satisfied by the fundamental examples and was specifically designed to yield direct strong-type
L^{p}
estimates, in contrast to the weak-type
L^{p}
bounds obtained under the original
D_{p}
condition. The new condition, denoted by
SD_{p}^{s}(w)
, is stronger than
D_{p}(w)
, yet it is satisfied by a broad class of functionals; see
[
37
,
6
,
19
]
for examples in a variety of settings. The precise definition is the following.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
w
be any weight in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and let
s>0
. We say that the functional
a
satisfies the weighted
SD^{s}_{p}(w)
condition for
0<p<\infty
if there is a constant
C
such that, for any cube
Q
and any family
\{Q_{j}\}
of pairwise disjoint subcubes of
Q
, the following inequality holds:

\sum_{j}a(Q_{j})^{p}w(Q_{j})\leq C^{p}\left(\frac{\left|\bigcup_{j}Q_{j}\right|}{|Q|}\right)^{\frac{p}{s}}a(Q)^{p}w(Q).

The best possible constant
C
above is denoted by
\left\|a\right\|_{SD_{p}^{s}(w)}
, and in this case we write
a\in SD^{s}_{p}(w)
.

The constants
\|a\|_{D_{p}(w)}
and
\|a\|_{SD_{p}^{s}(w)}
are always greater than or equal to 1; we shall omit the subscript when there is no ambiguity; moreover, when working with Lebesgue measure (i.e.
w=1
) we will also drop the dependence on
w
and simply write
\|a\|_{D_{p}}
and
\|a\|_{SD_{p}^{s}}
. This condition may be viewed as a strengthened version of the
D_{p}(w)
condition, incorporating an additional smallness preservation factor on the right-hand side. A typical example of a functional satisfying this condition is

a(Q)=\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\nu(Q)}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\nu
is a locally finite measure and
\alpha,p>0
. It was shown in
[
37
, Lemma 3.2]
that
a\in SD_{p}^{n/\alpha}(w)
.

The main self-improving result of
[
37
]
states that if
w\in A_{\infty}
and
a\in SD^{s}_{p}(w)
, then the inequality (
1.2
) implies a weighted
L^{p}
generalized Poincaré inequality. In that paper, the second author
and Rela conjectured that the
A_{\infty}
assumption could be removed. A partial step in this direction was later obtained by Martínez-Perales
[
30
]
. More recently, the conjecture was fully resolved by Lerner, Lorist, and Ombrosi
[
24
]
. Their approach relies on sparse domination to derive a pointwise estimate and, moreover, yields an improved dependence on
s
in the resulting constant. One may ask if the assumption
w\in A_{\infty}
in the
D_{p}
-type self-improving result, Theorem
1.2
, can also be removed; the answer is no. We construct a counterexample in Proposition
3.2
to illustrate this phenomenon.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
37
,
24
]
)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Consider a functional
a
satisfying the
SD^{s}_{p}(w)
condition with
s>0
and constant
\left\|a\right\|
. Let
f
be a locally integrable function such that

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|dx\leq a(Q)

for every cube
Q
. Then there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}
such that, for any cube
Q
,

(1.4)

{}\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c_{n}(1+s)\left\|a\right\|a(Q).

The
SD_{p}^{s}(w)
condition is notably flexible and has found applications in several contexts, including the multi-parameter setting of rectangles
[
6
]
and fractional Poincaré–Sobolev inequalities
[
19
]
.

1.1.
Main self-improving results

In this work, we improve the self-improving result under the assumption
a\in SD_{p}^{s}(w)
and
w\in A_{r}
for some
1\leq r<\infty
. Moreover, we show that our result is sharp for a wide range of
r
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
,
1\leq s<\infty
, and let
a
be a functional over cubes. Let
w\in A_{r}
and let
f
be a locally integrable function satisfying

(1.5)

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|\,dx\leq a(Q)

for every cube
Q
.

•

Assume that
a\in SD_{p}^{s}(w)
and
p<rs
. Then there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
such that, for every cube
Q
,

(1.6)

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p_{r,s}^{*},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n}p_{r,s}^{*}\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{rs}}\,\|a\|\,a(Q),

where
p_{r,s}^{*}
is defined by the relation

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{r,s}^{*}}=\frac{1}{s\,r}.

•

Assume that
a\in SD_{p}^{s}(w)
and
p\geq rs
. Then there exists a constant
c_{n,p}>0
such that, for every cube
Q
,

(1.7)

\|f-f_{Q}\|_{\exp L\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n,p}\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\|a\|\,a(Q).

Here
\|\cdot\|_{\exp L(X,\mu)}
denotes the usual Luxemburg norm associated to the Young function
\Phi(t)=e^{t}-1
, namely

\|f\|_{\exp L(X,\mu)}:=\inf\Big\{\lambda>0:\ \int_{X}\Phi\!\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\,d\mu(x)\leq 1\Big\}.

Estimate (
1.6
) reflects the compatibility between the
D_{p}
condition and the
SD_{p}^{s}
condition. Using Hölder’s inequality, we know that
D_{p^{*}_{s}}\subseteq SD_{p}^{s}\subseteq SD_{1}^{(p^{*}_{s})^{\prime}}
with

\|a\|_{SD_{1}^{(p^{*}_{s})^{\prime}}}\leq\|a\|_{SD_{p}^{s}}\leq\|a\|_{D_{p^{*}_{s}}},

where
1\leq p<s
and
p^{*}_{s}=\frac{sp}{s-p}
. Applying estimate (
1.6
) with
r=1
, one can improve the self-improving result under the condition
a\in SD_{p}^{s}
to the sharp result of the
D_{p^{*}_{s}}
condition, namely the weak-
L^{p^{*}_{s}}
generalized Poincaré inequality. Comparing (
1.6
) with the estimate (
1.4
) presented in
[
37
,
24
]
, we significantly improve their result when
w
is an
A_{r}
weight.

Estimate (
1.7
) extends
[
37
, Corollary 1.9]
in two directions: we do not assume any monotonicity of the functional
a
, and we work in the weighted setting under the more general condition
a\in SD_{p}^{s}(w)
.

Remark 1.6
.

Consider the unweighted setting
w=1
, so that
w\in A_{1}
and we may take
r=1
in Theorem
1.5
. In the classical Poincaré inequality, we have

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|dx\leq c_{n}\ell(Q)\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|\nabla f(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and the corresponding functional belongs to
SD^{n}_{p}
; thus
s=n
is the natural “dimension” in this toy model situation. Then for
1\leq p<s=n
, the exponent
p_{1,n}^{*}
in (
1.6
) becomes the classical critical Sobolev exponent
p^{*}=\frac{np}{n-p}
. On the other hand, in the borderline and supercritical regime
p\geq n
(equivalently
p\geq rs
with
r=1
and
s=n
), Theorem
1.5
gives us the exponential endpoint (
1.7
), which is consistent with the expected classical Trudinger-type behavior when
p\geq n
.

Remark 1.7
.

The exponent
p_{r,s}^{*}
in (
1.6
) is also sharp in the range
1\leq r\leq p
and
s\geq n
. Indeed, in Section
3
we construct weights
w\in A_{r}
and smooth test functions for which the conclusion of (
1.6
) fails if one replaces
p_{r,s}^{*}
by any larger exponent in those cases; see Proposition
3.3
and Proposition
3.4
.

In Appendix
A
, we extend Theorem
1.5
to higher-order oscillations by replacing the average
f_{Q}
, in both the hypothesis and the conclusion, with an appropriate polynomial
P_{Q}^{m}f
. This polynomial denotes the projection of
f
onto the space of polynomials of degree
m
in
n
variables over
Q
, leading to corollaries of Poincaré-type inequalities involving higher-order derivatives. In Appendix
B
, we also extend the results of Theorem
1.5
to vector-valued generalized Poincaré inequalities. Specifically, we consider functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\ell_{q}
, where the oscillation in both the hypothesis and the conclusion is measured with respect to the
\ell_{q}
-norm, appearing as
\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\ell_{q}}
. Finally, in Appendix
C
, we extend the result to rectangles in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

For the
A_{\infty}
class of weights, we can prove the following result.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
w\in A_{\infty}
, let
1<p<\infty
and let
a
be a functional over cubes. Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a locally integrable function such that

(1.8)

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|dx\leq a(Q)

for every cube
Q
.
If
a\in SD_{p}^{s}(w)
for
s>0
, then for every cube
Q
,

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p\left(1+\frac{1}{s}\right),\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n}p\left(1+\frac{1}{s}\right)[w]_{A_{\infty}}\left\|a\right\|^{\frac{s}{s+1}}a(Q).

Remark 1.9
.

Formally, the condition
SD_{p}^{s}(w)
with
s=\infty
coincides with
D_{p}(w)
. Our result recovers the sharp
D_{p}
type self-improving result Theorem
1.2
, with the same constant.

Comparing with the estimate (
1.6
), Theorem
1.8
provides a better result under the assumption
r>p\left(1+\frac{1}{s}\right)
, in the sense that the norm on the left-hand side is larger.

We also provide a self-improving result for
general
weights.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and let
w
be any weight. Consider a functional
a
satisfying the
SD^{s}_{p}(w)
condition with
s>0
and constant
\left\|a\right\|
. Let
f
be a locally integrable function such that the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1.9)

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|dx\leq a(Q)

and,

(1.10)

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq Ka(Q),

for every cube
Q
, where
K
is a constant independent of
Q
. Then, there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
such that for any cube
Q
,

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p\left(1+\frac{1}{s}\right),\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n}\max\left\{1,K\left\|a\right\|\right\}^{\frac{s}{s+1}}a(Q).

Condition (
1.10
) is fairly mild and holds for a wide range of practical cases. Indeed, there are at least two standard ways to verify it. First, if
w\in A_{\infty}
, one may invoke Theorem
1.2
to obtain a weighted weak-
L^{p}
estimate. This yields a conclusion of the same qualitative nature as Theorem
1.8
, albeit with a better dependence on
[w]_{A_{\infty}}
(and, in contrast, a worse dependence on
\|a\|
). Second, for completely general weights, one may appeal to Theorem
1.4
, which provides a weighted strong
L^{p}
generalized Poincaré inequality. This immediately implies (
1.10
) with
K=c_{n}(1+s)\|a\|
. Therefore, Theorem
1.10
applies under very mild additional input, and as an immediate corollary, we recover and sharpen the main result of
[
37
]
and its recent refinement in
[
24
]
.

1.2.
Weighted Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities

As a first application of our main self-improving result, Theorem
1.5
, we recover the following weighted weak Poincaré-Sobolev estimate: for every
w\in A_{r}
with
1\leq r\leq p
and
1\leq p<nr
we have

(1.11)

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p_{r}^{*},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n}p^{*}_{r}[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{rn}}\,\ell(Q)\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|\nabla f(x)|^{p}\,w(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

for every cube
Q
, where
p^{*}_{r}
is defined by the relation

(1.12)

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{r}^{*}}=\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{r}.

Remark 1.11
.

The exponent
p_{r}^{*}
on the left-hand side of (
1.11
) is sharp within the class
A_{r}
. We prove this in Proposition
3.3
below; see also
[
8
, Theorem 2.17]
for related sharpness results.

Furthermore, the weak norm in the left-hand side of (
1.11
) can be strengthened to a Lorentz norm by means of a standard truncation argument (see Section
2
).

Corollary 1.12
.

Let
w\in A_{r}
with
1\leq r\leq p
,
1\leq p<nr
and consider
p_{r}^{*}
defined in (
1.12
). Then, there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
such that

(1.13)

\|f-f_{Q}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p_{r}^{*},p}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n}p^{*}_{r}[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{rn}}\,\ell(Q)\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|\nabla f(x)|^{p}\,w(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

for every cube
Q
.

Remark 1.13
.

The second Lorentz index
p
in the target space
L^{p_{r}^{*},p}
in (
1.13
) is natural from the viewpoint of Sobolev-Lorentz embeddings. Already in the unweighted case
w=1
(hence
r=1
and
p_{r}^{*}=p^{*}
), the endpoint embedding
W^{1,p}\hookrightarrow L^{p^{*},p}
is optimal along the Lorentz scale (and, more generally, within rearrangement-invariant spaces), in the sense that
W^{1,p}\not\hookrightarrow L^{p^{*},q}
for any
q<p
(see
[
10
]
). Consequently, we should not expect a general improvement of (
1.13
) with
L^{p_{r}^{*},q}
,
q<p
, even in the simplest situation.

Remark 1.14
.

We remark that we could bound
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{rn}}
by a multiple of
[w]_{A_{r}}^{1+\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{rn}}
.

As estimates (
1.11
) and (
1.13
) are derived from the self-improving argument under very general settings, they are not sharp in the
A_{r}
constant. The optimal
A_{r}
dependence in this scale was recently identified in
[
8
]
, where it is shown that the best possible exponent is
[w]_{A_{r}}^{1/p}
.

Furthermore, the
L^{p_{r}^{*},\infty}
norm on the left-hand side of (
1.11
) can be maintained while sharpening the right-hand side; this is achieved by replacing the strong
L^{p}
norm of the gradient with the smaller Lorentz quasi-norm
L^{p,p_{r}^{*}}
. This improvement is made precise in the next result.

Corollary 1.15
.

Let
w\in A_{r}
with
1\leq r\leq p
,
1<p<nr
and consider
p_{r}^{*}
defined in (
1.12
). Then, there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
such that

(1.14)

\|f-f_{Q}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p_{r}^{*},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n}p^{*}_{r}[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,[w]_{A_{p,p_{r}^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{rn}}\,\ell(Q)\,\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p,p_{r}^{*}}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)},

for every cube
Q
, where
[w]_{A_{p,p_{r}^{*}}}
denotes the characteristic constant associated with the class
A_{p,p_{r}^{*}}
of Chung, Hunt, and Kurtz, as defined in Definition
2.2
.

Remark 1.16
.

The truncation argument used to strengthen weak-type estimates into Lorentz space bounds (as in Corollary
1.12
) is intrinsically related to a summability condition on the right-hand side: it is applicable provided that the Lorentz index satisfies
q\leq p
(see Theorem
2.3
). This is consistent with the
L^{p}
norm of the gradient term in (
1.11
), but it does not apply to the smaller Lorentz norm
\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p,p_{r}^{*}}}
in (
1.14
), since
p_{r}^{*}>p
. Consequently, the same argument cannot strengthen (
1.14
) to an
L^{p_{r}^{*},p}
estimate on the left-hand side.

Remark 1.17
.

We observe that for
w\in A_{r}
with
1<r\leq p
, the preceding arguments in conjunction with the precise open property of the
A_{r}
class (cf.
[
20
,
35
]
) allow for a refinement of the above estimates. In particular, the target Sobolev exponent can be extended beyond
p_{r}^{*}
to
p_{r-\varepsilon}^{*}
for some
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,r,w)>0
, following the approach in
[
8
]
. We do not explore this direction further in the present work.

In the critical and supercritical range
p\geq rn
, the Sobolev exponent
p_{r}^{*}
is no longer finite, so the
L^{q}
estimate must be replaced by an endpoint estimate of exponential type. The next result provides such an

1.3.
Fractional Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities

In this section, we establish weighted Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities whose right-hand side is given by the local fractional Sobolev seminorm (also called Gagliardo seminorm). Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<\delta<1
. A classical fractional Poincaré-Sobolev inequality was obtained by Bourgain, Brezis, and Mironescu
[
4
]
(see also
[
3
]
), which shows that there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
such that, for every cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(1.16)

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|\,dx\leq c_{n}\,(1-\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\ell(Q)^{\delta}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\int_{Q}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,dy\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

For fixed
p
and
0<\delta<1
, the right-hand side of (
1.16
) is smaller than
\ell(Q)\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(Q,\frac{dx}{|Q|})}
(see for instance
[
18
]
). The BBM extra gain
(1-\delta)^{1/p}
here reflects the correct scaling of the fractional seminorm: in

[
3
]
it is proved that there exists
K_{n,p}>0
such that for every
f\in W^{1,p}(Q)
,

\lim_{\delta\to 1^{-}}(1-\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\int_{Q}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,dy\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=K_{n,p}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|\nabla f(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We will use as a starting point a weighted version of (
1.16
). More
precisely, we shall rely on a weighted estimate proved in
[
33
]
(see
Theorem
6.1
below), which is a weighted counterpart of (
1.16
) for
A_{p}
weights. Combining this starting inequality with Theorem
1.5
, we obtain fractional Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities and, in the critical regime, exponential integrability. The main result of this section is the following
corollary.

Corollary 1.20
.

Let
0<\delta<1
and
w\in A_{r}
with
1\leq r\leq p
.

•

Assume that

. Then there exists

such that, for every cube

\displaystyle\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p^{*}_{\delta,r},p}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq

\displaystyle\,c_{n}p^{*}_{\delta,r}[w]_{A_{\infty}}[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{\delta}{rn}}(1-\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}\cdot

\displaystyle\cdot\ell(Q)^{\delta}\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\int_{Q}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}dy\,w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the weighted fractional Sobolev exponent

is defined by the relation

(1.17)

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{*}_{\delta,r}}=\frac{\delta}{n}\frac{1}{r}.

•

Assume that

such that, for every cube

\displaystyle\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\exp L\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq

In the subcritical range

1.4.
Weighted
L^{p,\infty}
–
L^{p^{*},\infty}
Poincaré–Sobolev inequality and applications to
A_{1}
weights

In this subsection, we investigate the weighted Poincaré-Sobolev inequality of type

\|f-f_{Q}\|_{Y_{Q}}\lesssim\ell(Q)\|\nabla f\|_{X_{Q}},\quad\forall Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

where
X_{Q}
and
Y_{Q}
are Lorentz quasi-norms in
Q
induced by the measure
\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}
. We call it a weighted
X-Y
Poincaré-Sobolev inequality. In Subsection
1.2
, we first prove a weighted
L^{p}-L^{p_{r}^{*},\infty}
Poincaré-Sobolev inequality (
1.11
) and improve this result to a weighted
L^{p}-L^{p_{r}^{*},p}
Poincaré-Sobolev inequality (
1.13
) by a truncation argument. In the present subsection, we improve (
1.11
) from another perspective, replacing the right-hand side term with a smaller Lorentz norm. One result along this direction has already been shown in the previous subsections, namely (
1.14
). Here we show that, if
w\in A_{1}
, the right-hand side term can be replaced by the weak norm
L^{p,\infty}
. More precisely, we prove a weighted
L^{p,\infty}-L^{p^{*},\infty}
Poincaré-Sobolev inequality for every
w\in A_{1}
.

We first provide a Fefferman-Stein-type Poincaré-Sobolev inequality via an alternative approach, independent of the self-improving theory developed above. The estimate has weak norms on both sides and the maximal function of the weight on the right-hand side. We do not impose any assumptions on the weight.

Theorem 1.23
.

Let
w
be a weight in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
and let
1<p<n
. Suppose
f
is a Lipschitz function. Then, there exists a constant
c_{n,p}>0
such that

(1.19)

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p^{*},\infty}(Q,w)}\leq c_{n,p}\left\|\left|\nabla f\right|\frac{M^{c}(w\chi_{Q})^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}{w}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,\infty}(Q,w)}

for each cube
Q
, where
\frac{1}{p^{*}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{n}
and
M^{c}
denotes the centered Hardy-Littlewood maximal function.

The previous result is in the spirit of
[
37
, Theorem 1.21]
, which it improves for the range
p>1
in two significant ways. First, the right-hand side involves the weaker Lorentz quasi-norm
L^{p,\infty}
. Second, the oscillation on the left-hand side is measured with respect to the unweighted average
f_{Q}
, rather than the weighted version
f_{Q,w}
. Furthermore,
[
37
, Theorem 1.21]
can be recovered as a corollary by combining (
1.19
) with a standard truncation argument. We provide further details in Section
7
.

For
A_{1}
weights, we can derive the following weighted Poincaré-Sobolev inequality with weak norms.

Corollary 1.24
.

Let
w\in A_{1}
be a weight in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
and let
1<p<n
. Then there exists a constant
c_{n,p}>0
such that

(1.20)

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p^{*},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n,p}[w]_{A_{1}}\ell(Q)\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}

for each cube
Q
.

The unweighted global version of (
1.20
) is known for the Lebesgue measure
[
36
]
; see also
[
28
]
for a different approach.

Remark 1.25
.

Regarding the optimal power of the
A_{1}
constant in (
1.20
), we will show that
\frac{1}{p}
is a lower bound for (
1.20
) to hold. Indeed, if (
1.20
) were to hold with a smaller exponent
[w]_{A_{1}}^{\alpha}
,
\alpha<1/p
, then, since
\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p,\infty}\left(Q,w\right)}\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}\left(Q,w\right)}
, the same bound would follow with the strong
L^{p}
norm of the gradient on the right-hand side. Combining this with the truncation method would then yield an improvement of the sharp weighted Poincaré-Sobolev inequality obtained in
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
, and hence lead to a contradiction.

2.
Some preliminaries and known results

2.1.
Lorentz spaces

We briefly recall the definition and some basic properties of Lorentz spaces. Let
\mu
be a Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and let
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a
\mu
-measurable set. For
0<p<\infty
and
0<q\leq\infty
, the Lorentz space
L^{p,q}(E,\mu)
consists of all measurable functions
f
such that

(2.1)

\|f\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q}(E,\mu)}:=\begin{cases}\left(p\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q}\,\mu\big(\{x\in E:\ |f(x)|>t\}\big)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},&0<q<\infty,\\[11.00008pt]
\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\,t\,\mu\big(\{x\in E:\ |f(x)|>t\}\big)^{1/p},&q=\infty,\end{cases}

is finite. The functional
\|\,.\,\|_{L^{p,q}(E,\mu)}
is a norm if and only if either
1\leq q\leq p
or
p=q=\infty
; otherwise it is a quasi-norm. Moreover, if
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
(as well as in the endpoint cases
p=q=1
and
p=q=\infty
), the quasi-norm (
2.1
) is equivalent to a norm on
L^{p,q}(E,\mu)
; see
[
1
]
.

We will use the following form of Hölder’s inequality in Lorentz spaces (see
[
21
]
): if
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
f\in L^{p,q}(E,\mu)
,
g\in L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(E,\mu)
, then

(2.2)

\int_{E}|f(x)g(x)|\,d\mu(x)\leq\|f\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q}(E,\mu)}\,\|g\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(E,\mu)},

where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
is the usual conjugate exponent (with the convention
p^{\prime}=1
if
p=\infty
and
p^{\prime}=\infty
if
p=1
).

We also recall the nestedness of the scale with respect to the second index (see
[
1
]
): if
0<p\leq\infty
and
0<q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq\infty
, then

(2.3)

\|f\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q_{2}}(E,\mu)}\leq\left(\frac{q_{1}}{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}-\frac{1}{q_{2}}}\|f\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q_{1}}(E,\mu)}.

Finally, we will use the standard embedding between Lorentz spaces on a probability space (see
[
17
]
). Let
(X,\nu)
be a measure space with
\nu(X)=1
. If
0<p_{1}<p_{2}<\infty
and
0<q<\infty
,
then

(2.4)

\|f\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p_{1},q}(X,\nu)}\leq\Big(\frac{p_{1}p_{2}}{q(p_{2}-p_{1})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\|f\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p_{2},\infty}(X,\nu)}.

2.2.
Maximal operators and
A_{p}
theory of weights

In this section, we collect notation and basic properties of Muckenhoupt weights and maximal operators. A weight is a nonnegative, locally integrable function, usually denoted by
w
.

Given a locally integrable function
f\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, the (uncentered) Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator is defined by

Mf(x)=\sup_{x\in Q}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)|dy,

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes and such that
x\in Q
. The centered version of the maximal operator is defined by

M^{c}f(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|Q(x,r)|}\int_{Q(x,r)}|f(y)|dy,

where
Q(x,r)
denotes the cube centered at
x
with side length
r
. Since Lebesgue measure is doubling,
M
and
M^{c}
are pointwise comparable; in particular, any estimate for one operator implies the corresponding estimate for the other with dimensional constants.

Let
1<p<\infty
. A weight
w
belongs to
A_{p}
if

[w]_{A_{p}}:=\sup_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)\,dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)^{1-p^{\prime}}\,dx\right)^{p-1}<\infty,

where the supremum is over all cubes
Q
. For
p=1
we write
w\in A_{1}
if

[w]_{A_{1}}:=\sup_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)\,dx\right)\,\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\in Q}w(x)^{-1}<\infty.

Equivalently,

Mw(x)\leq[w]_{A_{1}}\,w(x)\qquad\text{for a.e. }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

The boundedness of
M
on weighted spaces is characterized by these classes (see
[
32
]
):

M:L^{p}(w)\longrightarrow L^{p}(w)\ \text{ if and only if }\ w\in A_{p},\qquad 1<p<\infty,

and,

M:L^{p}(w)\longrightarrow L^{p,\infty}(w)\ \text{ if and only if }\ w\in A_{p},\qquad 1\leq p<\infty.

We will frequently use the following property of
A_{p}
weights: a weight
w
belongs to the class
A_{p}
if and only if for every measurable function
f\geq 0
, we have

(2.5)

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}f(x)dx\leq[w]^{\frac{1}{p}}_{A_{p}}\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}f(x)^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for every cube
Q
. In particular, taking
f=\chi_{E}
for a measurable set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we obtain

(2.6)

\frac{|E|}{|Q|}\leq[w]^{\frac{1}{p}}_{A_{p}}\left(\frac{w(E)}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We will also consider the class of pairs of
A_{p}
weights.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(u,v)
be a pair of weights.

(1)

For
1<p<\infty
we say that
(u,v)\in A_{p}
if

[u,v]_{A_{p}}=\sup_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}u(x)dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}v(x)^{1-p^{\prime}}dx\right)^{p-1}<\infty.

(2)

We say that
(u,v)\in A_{1}
if

[u,v]_{A_{1}}=\sup_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}u(x)dx\right)\underset{Q}{\operatorname{ess}\sup}\left(v^{-1}\right)<\infty,

or equivalently, if
Mu(x)\leq[u,v]_{A_{1}}v(x)
a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

We write

A_{\infty}=\bigcup_{p\geq 1}A_{p}.

We will quantify membership of a weight in
A_{\infty}
using the Fujii–Wilson constant

[w]_{A_{\infty}}=\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}M(w\chi_{Q})(x)dx,

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

We will also use a weight class naturally adapted to Lorentz norms, introduced by Chung, Hunt, and Kurtz in
[
7
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
1\leq q<\infty
. We denote by
A_{p,q}
the class of weights
w
such that

[w]_{A_{p,q}}=\sup_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)dx\right)\left\|\frac{1}{w}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{|Q|}\right)}^{p}<\infty.

Using (
2.2
), one readily checks that
w\in A_{p,q}
implies the following weighted estimate analogous to (
2.5
): for every measurable
f\geq 0
we have,

(2.7)

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}f(x)dx\leq[w]^{\frac{1}{p}}_{A_{p,q}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p,q}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}

for every cube
Q
. In particular, for
1<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
one has
A_{p,q}=A_{p}
. At the endpoint
q=1
, the class
A_{p,1}
strictly contains
A_{p}
and is sometimes referred to as the restricted
A_{p}
class, and denoted by
A_{p}^{\mathcal{R}}
.

2.3.
Truncation method

The truncation method is a classical technique for upgrading weak-type Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities to stronger results along the Lorentz scale. In the first-order setting, this idea already appears in the work of Maz’ya
[
31
, p. 110]
; see also
[
15
,
23
]
for more details. We will use a non-standard version of this argument, based on a trick from
[
12
]
, which allows us to keep the unweighted average
f_{Q}
on the left-hand side. In order to do this, besides the weak-type Poincaré-Sobolev inequality, we require an additional unweighted
L^{1}
Poincaré inequality.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
,
1\leq r\leq p
, let
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a cube, and let
\mu
and
\nu
be positive Borel measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
\mu(Q)=1
. Assume that there exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
f\in Lip(Q)
,

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,\infty}(Q,\mu)}\leq C\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,r}(Q,\nu)}

and

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|\,dx\leq C\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,r}(Q,\nu)}.

Then, for every
f\in Lip(Q)
, we have

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,p}(Q,\mu)}\leq 10\,C\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,r}(Q,\nu)}.

The previous theorem can be proved by following the strategy in
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
and using the summability property of Lorentz quasi-norms stated in Lemma
5.1
below; this is also the point where the restriction
1\leq r\leq p
enters.

In
[
9
]
, it was proved that the truncation method is applicable to the fractional seminorm. We will use the following version of the truncation method; this version combines the result of
[
9
]
and the weak-implies-strong argument of
[
23
]
to improve it up to the Lorentz norm
L^{q,p}
. We also use the ideas of
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
in order to keep the unweighted average
f_{Q}
on the left-hand side. We refer to
[
26
, Proposition A.2]
for an explicit proof of a related result.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
, let
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a cube, let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
\mu(Q)=1
, and let
K:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
be a positive measurable function. Assume that there exists
C>0
such that for every
f\in Lip(Q)
,

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,\infty}(Q,\mu)}\leq C\left(\int_{Q}\int_{Q}|f(y)-f(z)|^{p}K(y,z)\,dy\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|\,dx\leq C\left(\int_{Q}\int_{Q}|f(y)-f(z)|^{p}K(y,z)\,dy\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Then, for every
f\in Lip(Q)
, we have

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,p}(Q,\mu)}\leq 130\,C\left(\int_{Q}\int_{Q}|f(y)-f(z)|^{p}K(y,z)\,dy\,dz\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Notation

As usual,
c
denotes a positive constant, possibly varying from line to line. We write
c_{\alpha,\beta,...}
to denote a constant depending only on
\alpha,\beta,...\,
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.5
and Sharpness

We begin this section by establishing a refinement of
[
5
, Proposition 2.3]
, which will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.5
to prove the exponential-type estimate.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a probability space and let
f\geq 0
be measurable. Assume that there exist constants
\gamma>0
and
p_{0}\geq 1
such that for every
p>p_{0}
,

(3.1)

\|f\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,\infty}(X,\mu)}\leq\gamma\,p.

Then
f\in\exp L(X,\mu)
, in the sense that

\mu(\{x\in X:\ f(x)>t\})\leq e^{p_{0}}\exp\Big(-\frac{t}{e\,\gamma}\Big),

for every
t>0
. Moreover, there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that

\|f\|_{\exp L(X,\mu)}\leq C\,p_{0}\,\gamma.

Proof.

Fix
t>e\gamma p_{0}
. Choose
p=p(t):=\frac{t}{e\gamma}>p_{0}
, so that
\gamma p/t=e^{-1}
. Applying (
3.1
) with this choice of
p
gives

\mu(\{x\in X:f(x)>t\})\leq\Big(\frac{1}{e}\Big)^{p(t)}=\exp\Big(-\frac{t}{e\gamma}\Big),

for every
t>e\gamma p_{0}
. If
0<t\leq e\gamma p_{0}
, then
\mu(\{x\in X:f(x)>t\})\leq 1
and hence

\mu(\{x\in X:f(x)>t\})\leq e^{p_{0}}\exp\Big(-\frac{t}{e\gamma}\Big),

since
\exp(-t/(e\gamma))\geq e^{-p_{0}}
in the range
0<t\leq e\gamma p_{0}
. Therefore, for every
t>0
,

\mu(\{x\in X:f(x)>t\})\leq e^{p_{0}}\exp\Big(-\frac{t}{e\gamma}\Big).

∎

With Proposition
3.1
in hand, we now turn to the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We first show that estimate (
1.7
) follows directly from estimate (
1.6
) and Proposition
3.1
. Suppose that
a\in SD_{p}^{s}(w)
with
p\geq rs
. Then, for every
s_{0}\geq s
,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j}a(Q_{j})^{p}w(Q_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\|a\|_{SD_{p}^{s}(w)}^{p}\left(\frac{\left|\bigcup_{j}Q_{j}\right|}{|Q|}\right)^{\frac{p}{s}}a(Q)^{p}w(Q)

\displaystyle\leq\|a\|_{SD_{p}^{s}(w)}^{p}\left(\frac{\left|\bigcup_{j}Q_{j}\right|}{|Q|}\right)^{\frac{p}{s_{0}}}a(Q)^{p}w(Q),

for any family
\{Q_{j}\}_{j}
of pairwise disjoint subcubes of the cube
Q
. Hence
\|a\|_{SD_{p}^{s_{0}}(w)}\leq\|a\|_{SD_{p}^{s}(w)}
. For each
p<q
, we choose
s_{0}
to be the constant satisfying the relation

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r{s}_{0}}.

Since
p<rs_{0}
, applying estimate (
1.6
) gives

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n}q[w]_{A_{\infty}}[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|a\right\|_{SD_{p}^{s}(w)}a(Q),

for every
q>p\geq 1
. We can apply Proposition
3.1
with

\gamma=c_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|a\right\|_{SD_{p}^{s}(w)}a(Q),

and we obtain the desired result (
1.7
).

We now prove the estimate (
1.6
). Fix a cube
Q
, our goal is to show that for every
t>0
,

(3.2)

t^{p_{r,s}^{*}}\,w(\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>t\})\leq\|a\|^{p_{r,s}^{*}}(c_{n}\,p_{r,s}^{*}\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{rs}}a(Q))^{p_{r,s}^{*}}\,\,w(Q).

Denote by
M_{Q}
the dyadic maximal operator localized in
Q
. Since

|f(x)-f_{Q}|\leq M_{Q}(f-f_{Q})(x),

we estimate the larger set

\Omega_{t}=\{x\in Q:M_{Q}(f-f_{Q})(x)>t\}.

Let
\{Q_{j}\}_{j}
be the maximal dyadic cubes forming
\Omega_{t}
. Then, either
\{Q_{j}\}_{j}=\{Q\}
or the cubes
Q_{j}
satisfy the condition

(3.3)

{}t<\frac{1}{|Q_{j}|}\int_{Q_{j}}{|f-f_{Q}|}\leq 2^{n}t

and for almost every
x\in Q\setminus\cup_{j}Q_{j}
,
|f(x)-f_{Q}|\leq t
. Let
\kappa=2^{n}+1
. We claim that

w(\Omega_{\kappa t})\leq\sum_{j}w(E_{Q_{j}}),

where

E_{Q_{j}}=\{x\in Q_{j}:M_{Q_{j}}(f-f_{Q_{j}})(x)>t\}.

When
\{Q_{j}\}_{j}=\{Q\}
, this claim holds trivially. In the other case, since
\kappa>1
, it is not difficult to show that, except for a set of null measure,

\Omega_{\kappa t}\subset\bigcup_{j}\{x\in Q_{j}:M_{Q_{j}}(f-f_{Q})(x)>\kappa t\}.

Moreover, for each
j
, by (
3.3
), we have
|f_{Q_{j}}-f_{Q}|\leq 2^{n}t
. Applying these two observations, we have, for every
x\in Q_{j}
satisfying
M_{Q_{j}}(f-f_{Q})(x)>\kappa t
,

M_{Q_{j}}(f-f_{Q_{j}})\geq|M_{Q_{j}}(f-f_{Q})-|f_{Q}-f_{Q_{j}}||>(\kappa-2^{n})t=t.

This proves the claim.

Note that for every
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle E_{Q_{j}}\subseteq

\displaystyle\{x\in Q_{j}:M_{Q_{j}}(f-f_{Q_{j}})(x)>t,\,M_{Q}^{\#}f(x)\leq\gamma t\}\cup\{x\in Q_{j}:M_{Q}^{\#}f(x)>\gamma t\}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle A_{j}\cup B_{j},

where

M_{Q}^{\#}f(x)=\sup_{x\in R\in\mathcal{D}(Q)}\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}|f(y)-f_{R}|dy.

Thus, we have

w(\Omega_{\kappa t})\leq\sum_{j}w(E_{Q_{j}})\leq\sum_{j}w(A_{j})+\sum_{j}w(B_{j}).

For the sets
A_{j}
, we use the good-lambda type inequality
[
5
, Corollary 1.4]
.

\sum_{j}w(A_{j})\leq c_{1}e^{-\frac{c_{2}}{[w]_{A_{\infty}}\gamma}}\sum_{j}w(Q_{j})=c_{1}e^{-\frac{c_{2}}{[w]_{A_{\infty}}\gamma}}w(\Omega_{t}),

where
c_{1},c_{2}>0
are dimensional constants. On the other hand, for
B_{j}
we can argue as follows. We have

\displaystyle\bigcup_{j}B_{j}\subseteq\{x\in Q:M_{Q}^{\#}f(x)>\gamma t\}:=\bigcup_{i}R_{i},

where
R_{i}
are the maximal dyadic subcubes of
Q
such that

\gamma t<\frac{1}{|R_{i}|}\int_{R_{i}}|f(x)-f_{R_{i}}|dx.

By hypothesis (
1.5
), we know that
\gamma t<a(R_{i})
. We now use the condition that
a
satisfies, namely
a\in SD_{p}^{s}(w)
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{j}w(B_{j})\leq

\displaystyle w\big(\{x\in Q:M_{Q}^{\#}f(x)>\gamma t\}\big)

(3.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w\left(\bigcup_{i}R_{i}\right){}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\gamma t}\right)^{p}\sum_{i}a(R_{i})^{p}w(R_{i})

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|a\|^{p}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma t}\right)^{p}\,\left(\frac{|\bigcup_{i}R_{i}|}{|Q|}\right)^{\frac{p}{s}}a(Q)^{p}w(Q).{}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|a\|^{p}[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{p}{rs}}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma t}\right)^{p}\,\left(\frac{w\left(\bigcup_{i}R_{i}\right)}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{p}{rs}}a(Q)^{p}w(Q).

Hence, combining this with (
3.4
) and recalling that

\frac{1}{p_{r,s}^{*}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{sr},

we have,

\left(\frac{w(\bigcup_{i}R_{i})}{w(Q)}\right)^{1-\frac{p}{rs}}\leq[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{p}{rs}}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma t}\right)^{p}\|a\|^{p}a(Q)^{p},

and hence

\frac{w(\bigcup_{i}R_{i})}{w(Q)}\leq[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{p_{r,s}^{*}}{rs}}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma t}\right)^{p_{r,s}^{*}}\|a\|^{p_{r,s}^{*}}a(Q)^{p_{r,s}^{*}}.

Combining these results, it follows that,

\displaystyle w(\Omega_{\kappa t})

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}e^{-\frac{c_{2}}{[w]_{A_{\infty}}\gamma}}w(\Omega_{t})+[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{p_{r,s}^{*}}{rs}}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma t}\right)^{p_{r,s}^{*}}\|a\|^{p_{r,s}^{*}}a(Q)^{p_{r,s}^{*}}w(Q)

and then

\displaystyle(\kappa t)^{p_{r,s}^{*}}w(\Omega_{\kappa t})

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}e^{-\frac{c_{2}}{[w]_{A_{\infty}}\gamma}}\,(\kappa t)^{p_{r,s}^{*}}w(\Omega_{t})+[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{p_{r,s}^{*}}{rs}}\left(\frac{\kappa}{\gamma}\right)^{p_{r,s}^{*}}\|a\|^{p_{r,s}^{*}}a(Q)^{p_{r,s}^{*}}w(Q).

We define

\varphi(N)=\sup_{0<t\leq N}t^{p_{r,s}^{*}}w(\Omega_{t}),

since this function is increasing, we have

\varphi(N)\leq\varphi(\kappa N)\leq c_{1}\kappa^{p_{r,s}^{*}}e^{-\frac{c_{2}}{[w]_{A_{\infty}}\gamma}}\varphi(N)+[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{p_{r,s}^{*}}{rs}}\left(\frac{\kappa}{\gamma}\right)^{p_{r,s}^{*}}\|a\|^{p_{r,s}^{*}}a(Q)^{p_{r,s}^{*}}w(Q).

Since the parameter
\gamma
remains free throughout the derivation, we may set it such that

c_{1}\kappa^{p_{r,s}^{*}}e^{-\frac{c_{2}}{[w]_{A_{\infty}}\gamma}}=\frac{1}{2},

then

\frac{1}{\gamma}\leq c_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}p_{r,s}^{*}.

This yields the estimate

\varphi(N)\leq c_{n}^{p_{r,s}^{*}}[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{p_{r,s}^{*}}[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{p_{r,s}^{*}}{rs}}({p_{r,s}^{*}})^{p_{r,s}^{*}}\|a\|^{p_{r,s}^{*}}a(Q)^{p_{r,s}^{*}}w(Q).

Passing to the limit
N\to\infty
in the above inequality yields (
3.2
), concluding the proof of the theorem.
∎

Before addressing the sharpness of Theorem
1.5
, we present a consequence of Proposition
3.1
. Specifically, the following result shows that the condition
w\in A_{\infty}
is necessary for the
D_{p}
-type self-improving property (Theorem
1.2
) to hold. More precisely, if the conclusion of Theorem
1.2
holds for every functional
a\in D_{p}(w)
, then
w
must belong to
A_{\infty}
, with quantitative control of
[w]_{A_{\infty}}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The assumption
w\in A_{\infty}
in Theorem
1.2
is necessary. More precisely, let
w
be a weight and assume that there exists a constant
C_{w}<\infty
such that, for every
1\leq p<\infty
, every functional
a\in D_{p}(w)
, and every locally integrable function
f
satisfying

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|\,dx\leq a(Q)

for every cube
Q
, we have

(3.6)

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq C_{w}p\,\left\|a\right\|_{D_{p}(w)}a(Q)

for every cube
Q
. Then
w\in A_{\infty}
with
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\leq c_{n}C_{w}
.

Proof.

Assume that (
3.6
) holds for a weight
w
. Let
f\in\operatorname{BMO}
and define
a(Q):=\|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}
for every cube
Q
. Then,

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|\,dx\leq a(Q)

for every cube
Q
. Moreover,
a\in D_{p}(w)
for every
1\leq p<\infty
and
\|a\|_{D_{p}(w)}=1
. Applying (
3.6
), we obtain, for every cube
Q
and every
1<p<\infty
,

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq C_{w}p\,\|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}.

By Proposition
3.1
, it follows that

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\exp L\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq C\,C_{w}\,\|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}.

Hence
f\in\operatorname{BMO}_{w,w}
and

(3.7)

\|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}_{w,w}}\leq C\,C_{w}\,\|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}},

where
\|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}_{w,w}}:=\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|w
. Thus we have proved
\operatorname{Id}:\operatorname{BMO}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{BMO}_{w,w}
. However, by the characterization proved in
[
34
, Theorem 1.2]
, the embedding
\operatorname{Id}:\operatorname{BMO}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{BMO}_{w,w}
holds if and only if
w\in A_{\infty}
, therefore
w\in A_{\infty}
. Moreover, taking the supremum in (
3.7
) over all
f\in\operatorname{BMO}
with
\|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}=1
, and using again
[
34
, Theorem 1.2]
, we obtain
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\leq c_{n}C_{w}
.
∎

Now we discuss the sharpness of (
1.6
) in Theorem
1.5
. In this context, sharpness means that the weak norm
L^{p_{r,s}^{*},\infty}
on the left-hand side cannot be replaced by an
L^{q,\infty}
norm for any
q>p_{r,s}^{*}
. We construct a counterexample showing that (
1.6
) is sharp in the case
s\geq n
and
w\in A_{r}
with
1\leq r\leq p
.

We start with the case
s=n
and
1\leq r\leq p<nr
. By using (
1.1
) and (
2.5
), we have that for
w\in A_{p}
,

(3.8)

{}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|dx\leq c_{n}[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\ell(Q)\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|\nabla f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}:=a(Q).

Moreover, from
[
37
, Lemma 3.2]
, we know that
a\in SD_{p}^{n}(w)
. Then, applying Theorem
1.5
, we obtain

(3.9)

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p_{r}^{*},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n}p^{*}_{r}[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{rn}}\,\ell(Q)\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|\nabla f(x)|^{p}\,w(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

for every cube
Q
, where
\frac{1}{p_{r}^{*}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{nr}
, providing the proof of (
1.11
).
The application of the truncation argument from Theorem
2.3
yields the proof of Corollary
1.12
. The proof of Corollary
1.19
is analogous and follows the same lines.

In the next proposition, we demonstrate that the exponent
p_{r}^{*}
on the left-hand side of (
3.9
) cannot be improved, thereby establishing the sharpness of Theorem
1.5
in the case
s=n
and
p<nr
.

For the sake of simplicity, in the following two propositions, we use the notation
A\lesssim B
to indicate that there exists a constant
C
, depending on
n
,
p
,
q
,
[w]_{A_{r}}
, and on any fixed auxiliary cut-off function chosen in the proof, such that
A\leq CB
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose
1\leq r\leq p
and
0<\frac{1}{q}<\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{nr}
. Let
Q=[-1,1]^{n}
. Then there exists
w\in A_{r}
such that

(3.10)

{}\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|\nabla f(x)|^{p}\,w(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

cannot
hold for every
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Proof.

Suppose (
3.10
) holds for every
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Let
B=B(0,1)
denote the open unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered at the origin. Then

B\subset Q\subset\sqrt{n}B=:\widetilde{B}.

Since an
A_{p}
weight satisfies the doubling condition
w(2Q)\lesssim w(Q)
, applying (
3.8
), for every
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\|f-f_{B}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,\infty}\left(B,\frac{wdx}{w(B)}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-f_{Q}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,\infty}\left(B,\frac{wdx}{w(B)}\right)}+|f_{B}-f_{Q}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f-f_{Q}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,\infty}\left(Q,\frac{wdx}{w(Q)}\right)}+\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|\nabla f|^{p}wdx\Big)^{1/p}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{w(\widetilde{B})}\int_{\widetilde{B}}|\nabla f|^{p}wdx\Big)^{1/p}.{}

Let
w(x)=|x|^{\delta-n}
with
0<\delta<nr
, it is well known that
w
is an
A_{r}
weight. Let
\varphi
be a smooth function supported in
B(0,2)
and taking the value
1
on
B(0,1)
. For
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
, we define
f_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varphi(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})
. Since
f_{\varepsilon}
vanishes on
E=B(0,1)\backslash B(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
q>1
, applying (
3.11
),

\displaystyle\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,\infty}\left(B,\frac{wdx}{w(B)}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{\varepsilon}-(f_{\varepsilon})_{B}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,\infty}\left(B,\frac{wdx}{w(B)}\right)}+|(f_{\varepsilon})_{B}|

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{\varepsilon}-(f_{\varepsilon})_{B}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,\infty}\left(B,\frac{wdx}{w(B)}\right)}+\|f_{\varepsilon}-(f_{\varepsilon})_{B}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,\infty}\left(E,\frac{wdx}{w(E)}\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{\varepsilon}-(f_{\varepsilon})_{B}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,\infty}\left(B,\frac{wdx}{w(B)}\right)}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{1}{w(\widetilde{B})}\int_{\widetilde{B}}|\nabla f_{\varepsilon}|^{p}wdx\Big)^{1/p},{}

where we use the fact that
w(B(0,1))\simeq\frac{1}{\delta}\simeq w(E)
. Note that
f_{\varepsilon}=1
on
B(0,\varepsilon)
and
|\nabla f_{\varepsilon}|\lesssim_{\varphi}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\chi_{B(0,2\varepsilon)}
, we have

\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,\infty}\left(B,\frac{wdx}{w(B)}\right)}\geq\Big(\frac{w(B(0,\varepsilon))}{w(B)}\Big)^{1/q}\simeq\varepsilon^{\delta/q}

and

(3.13)

{}\Big(\frac{1}{w(\widetilde{B})}\int_{\widetilde{B}}|\nabla f_{\varepsilon}|^{p}wdx\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{w(B(0,2\varepsilon))}{w(\widetilde{B})}\Big)^{1/p}\simeq\varepsilon^{\delta/p-1}.

Applying estimate (
3.12
), we have

(3.14)

{}\varepsilon^{\delta(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})+1}\lesssim 1.

Since
\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}<-\frac{1}{nr}
, we can choose
\delta
sufficiently close to

, such that
\delta(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})<-1
. In this case, estimate (
3.14
) fails if we let
\varepsilon
tend to
0
, and as a result, we arrive at a contradiction.
∎

Now, we are going to construct the counterexample for the case
s>n
and
1\leq r\leq p<rs
. Similarly to the previous case, applying the fractional Poincaré inequality (
1.16
) with
p=1
and (
2.6
), for
0<\alpha<1
and
w\in A_{p}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|dx\lesssim\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|\mathcal{D}_{Q}^{\alpha}f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}:=a(Q),

where

\mathcal{D}_{Q}^{\alpha}f(x):=\int_{Q}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha}}dy\qquad x\in Q

is the local fractional derivative of order
\alpha
. From
[
37
, Lemma 3.2]
, we know that
a\in SD_{p}^{s}(w)
with
s=\frac{n}{\alpha}>n
. Then, applying Theorem
1.5
, we obtain

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p_{r}^{*},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\lesssim\ell(Q)^{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|\mathcal{D}_{Q}^{\alpha}f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\frac{1}{p_{r}^{*}}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{sr}
. The following proposition yields the sharpness of the estimate (
1.6
) in this case.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose
1\leq r\leq p
and
0<\frac{1}{q}<\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{nr}
. Let
Q=[-1,1]^{n}
. Then there exists
w\in A_{r}
such that

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{q,\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|\mathcal{D}_{Q}^{\alpha}f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

cannot hold for every
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

Proof.

Analogously to the proof of Proposition
3.3
, we let
w(x)=|x|^{\delta-n}
with
0<\delta<nr
, and let
\varphi
be a smooth function supported in
B(0,2)
such that
\varphi\equiv 1
on
B(0,1)
. For
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
, we define
f_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varphi(x/\varepsilon)
. The desired result then follows by adapting the strategy used in that proposition; the only difference appears in the estimate (
3.13
). We decompose

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{B}}|\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\widetilde{B}}f_{\varepsilon}|^{p}|x|^{\delta-n}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,4\varepsilon)}|\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\widetilde{B}}f_{\varepsilon}|^{p}|x|^{\delta-n}dx+\sum\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}\int_{B(0,2^{k+1}\varepsilon)\backslash B(0,2^{k}\varepsilon)}|\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\widetilde{B}}f_{\varepsilon}|^{p}|x|^{\delta-n}dx.

For
x\in B(0,4\varepsilon)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\widetilde{B}}f_{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{B}}\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{B(x,4\varepsilon)}\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha}}dy+\int_{\widetilde{B}\backslash B(x,4\varepsilon)}\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\int_{B(x,4\varepsilon)}\frac{|x-y|}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha}}dy+\|\varphi\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash B(x,4\varepsilon)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\int_{B(0,4\varepsilon)}|y|^{-n-\alpha+1}dy+\|\varphi\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash B(0,4\varepsilon)}|y|^{-n-\alpha}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\alpha}.

For
x\in B(0,2^{k+1}\varepsilon)\backslash B(0,2^{k}\varepsilon)
with
k\geq 2
, we have
f_{\varepsilon}(x)=0
and
|x-y|\simeq|x|\simeq 2^{k}\varepsilon
for
y\in B(0,2\varepsilon)
. Then,

\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\widetilde{B}}f_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{B(0,2\varepsilon)}\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha}}dy\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}(2^{k}\varepsilon)^{-n-\alpha}(2\varepsilon)^{n}\lesssim(2^{k})^{-n-\alpha}\varepsilon^{-\alpha}.

Combining everything together,

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{w(\widetilde{B})}\int_{\widetilde{B}}|\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\widetilde{B}}f_{\varepsilon}|^{p}|x|^{\delta-n}dx\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\delta^{1/p}\Big(\varepsilon^{-\alpha p}\int_{B(0,4\varepsilon)}|x|^{\delta-n}dx+\varepsilon^{-\alpha p}\sum\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}(2^{k})^{-np-\alpha p}\int_{B(0,2^{k+1}\varepsilon)\backslash B(0,2^{k}\varepsilon)}|x|^{\delta-n}dx\Big)^{1/p}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\varepsilon^{-\alpha}\Big(\varepsilon^{\delta}+\varepsilon^{\delta}\sum\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}(2^{k})^{-np-\alpha p+\delta}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\varepsilon^{\delta/p-\alpha}.

With this estimate, we can arrive at a contradiction by following the argument in Proposition
3.3
.
∎

4.
Proofs of self-improving results to
L^{p(1+\frac{1}{s}),\infty}
generalized Poincaré-type inequality

In this section, we provide the proof of the two
L^{p(1+\frac{1}{s}),\infty}
generalized Poincaré-type inequalities with different assumptions. First, we provide the proof of Theorem
1.8
, assuming
w\in A_{\infty}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

The proof follows a similar strategy to that of Theorem
1.5
; hence, we focus only on the necessary modifications.
Starting from (
3.5
), using Minkowski’s integral inequality, we obtain

\gamma t<\frac{1}{|R_{i}|}\int_{R_{i}}|f-f_{R_{i}}|\leq\frac{1}{|R_{i}|}\int_{R_{i}}|f-f_{Q}|+|f_{Q}-f_{R_{i}}|\leq 2\frac{1}{|R_{i}|}\int_{R_{i}}|f-f_{Q}|.

Hence,

\left|\bigcup_{i}R_{i}\right|=\sum_{i}|R_{i}|<\frac{2}{\gamma t}\sum_{i}\int_{R_{i}}|f-f_{Q}|\leq\frac{2}{\gamma t}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|\leq\frac{2}{\gamma t}a(Q)|Q|.

Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{j}w(B_{j})\leq

\displaystyle\|a\|^{p}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma t}\right)^{p}\,\left(\frac{2}{\gamma t}a(Q)\right)^{\frac{p}{s}}w(Q)a(Q)^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|a\|^{p}2^{\frac{p}{s}}\,\left(\frac{1}{\gamma t}a(Q)\right)^{p+\frac{p}{s}}w(Q).

We proceed as before, combining everything together, and we get

\displaystyle w(\Omega_{\kappa t})

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}e^{-\frac{c_{2}}{[w]_{A_{\infty}}\gamma}}w(\Omega_{t})+\|a\|^{p}2^{\frac{p}{s}}\,\left(\frac{1}{\gamma t}a(Q)\right)^{p+\frac{p}{s}}w(Q),

and then

\displaystyle(\kappa t)^{p+\frac{p}{s}}w(\Omega_{\kappa t})

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}e^{-\frac{c_{2}}{[w]_{A_{\infty}}\gamma}}\,(\kappa t)^{p+\frac{p}{s}}w(\Omega_{t})+\|a\|^{p}\left(\frac{\kappa}{\gamma}\right)^{p+\frac{p}{s}}2^{\frac{p}{s}}w(Q)a(Q)^{p+\frac{p}{s}}.

Defining

\varphi(N)=\sup_{0<t\leq N}t^{p+\frac{p}{s}}w(\Omega_{t})

and choosing
\gamma
satisfying

c_{1}\kappa^{p+\frac{p}{s}}e^{-\frac{c_{2}}{\gamma[w]_{A_{\infty}}}}=\frac{1}{2},

we obtain that

\varphi(N)\leq c_{n}^{p+\frac{p}{s}}[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{p+\frac{p}{s}}\Big(p(1+\frac{1}{s})\Big)^{p+\frac{p}{s}}\|a\|^{p}w(Q)a(Q)^{p+\frac{p}{s}}.

Finally, letting
N\to\infty
completes the proof.
∎

Now, we turn to the proof of Theorem
1.10
, which covers the case of general weights.

Proof of Theorem
1.10
.

Let
0<p<\infty
. Fix a cube
Q
and let
t>a(Q)
. Observe that by (
1.9
) we have,

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|dx\leq a(Q)<t.

We consider the local Calderón–Zygmund decomposition of the function
|f-f_{Q}|
relative to the cube
Q
at height
t
. Then, there is a family of dyadic pairwise disjoint subcubes
\{Q_{j}\}_{j}
with respect to
Q
, which satisfy the following properties. For each
j
,

(4.1)

t<\frac{1}{|Q_{j}|}\int_{Q_{j}}|f(x)-f_{Q}|dx\leq 2^{n}t,

and for almost every
x\in Q\setminus\bigcup_{j}Q_{j}
,

|f(x)-f_{Q}|\leq t.

From the first initial hypothesis (
1.9
) and (
4.1
),

(4.2)

\frac{\left|\bigcup_{j}Q_{j}\right|}{|Q|}\leq\frac{1}{t|Q|}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|\,dx\leq\frac{a(Q)}{t}.

Let
\kappa=2^{n}+1
. Since (
4.1
) implies that
|f_{Q_{j}}-f_{Q}|\leq 2^{n}t
,
we have,

\displaystyle w\left(\left\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>\kappa t\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}w\left(\left\{x\in Q_{j}:|f(x)-f_{Q_{j}}|+|f_{Q_{j}}-f_{Q}|>\kappa t\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}w\left(\left\{x\in Q_{j}:|f(x)-f_{Q_{j}}|>t\right\}\right).

We can estimate the sum using the second hypothesis (
1.10
) at each cube
Q_{j}
and then the fact that the functional
a
satisfies the
SD_{p}^{s}(w)
condition,

\displaystyle w\left(\left\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>\kappa t\right\}\right)\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j}w\left(\left\{x\in Q_{j}:|f(x)-f_{Q_{j}}|>t\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K^{p}t^{-p}\sum_{j}a(Q_{j})^{p}w(Q_{j})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K^{p}t^{-p}\left\|a\right\|^{p}\left(\frac{\left|\bigcup_{j}Q_{j}\right|}{|Q|}\right)^{\frac{p}{s}}a(Q)^{p}w(Q)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K^{p}\left\|a\right\|^{p}\left(\frac{a(Q)}{t}\right)^{p+\frac{p}{s}}w(Q)

where in the last inequality, we used (
4.2
). Combining these estimates, we have proved

t\,w\left(\left\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>\kappa t\right\}\right)^{\frac{1}{p+\frac{p}{s}}}\leq\left(K\left\|a\right\|\right)^{\frac{s}{s+1}}w(Q)^{\frac{1}{p+\frac{p}{s}}}a(Q)

for all
t>a(Q)
. This is equivalent to saying that

(4.3)

\sup_{t>c_{n}a(Q)}t\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}w\left(\left\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>t\right\}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p+\frac{p}{s}}}\leq c_{n}\left(K\left\|a\right\|\right)^{\frac{s}{s+1}}a(Q).

On the other hand,

\sup_{t\leq c_{n}a(Q)}t\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}w\left(\left\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>t\right\}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p+\frac{p}{s}}}\leq c_{n}a(Q).

Hence,

\displaystyle\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{L^{p+\frac{p}{s},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}=

\displaystyle\max\left\{\sup_{t\leq c_{n}a(Q)}\cdots,\sup_{t>c_{n}a(Q)}\cdots\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{c_{n}a(Q),c_{n}\left(K\left\|a\right\|\right)^{\frac{s}{s+1}}a(Q)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{n}\max\left\{1,\left(K\left\|a\right\|\right)^{\frac{s}{s+1}}\right\}a(Q).

This concludes the proof.
∎

As an immediate consequence of the argument, one also obtains a self-improvement along the Lorentz scale: under the assumptions of Theorem
1.10
, using (
2.4
) we have

(4.4)

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\max\left\{1,\left(K\left\|a\right\|\right)^{\frac{s}{s+1}}\right\}(1+s)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,a(Q),

for every
1\leq q<\infty
. In particular, within this framework, the weak
estimate (
1.10
) is, up to quantitative constants, equivalent to the family of strong Lorentz estimates (
4.4
); this is noteworthy since the endpoint
q=1
is a priori much stronger than
q=\infty
.

5.
Applications to degenerate Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities

In this section, we investigate the Lorentz-type Poincaré–Sobolev inequalities stated in Section
1.2
. The proofs of strong-type results Corollary
1.12
and Corollary
1.19
have already been discussed before Proposition
3.3
. To prove Corollaries
1.15
and
1.18
, we will need two auxiliary lemmas. The first one is a geometric property satisfied by Lorentz quasi-norms; it is in the spirit of the well-known result from
[
7
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space. Let
1\leq p,q<\infty
and
\alpha\geq\max\{p,q\}
. Then we have

\sum_{j}\left\|f\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q}\left(E_{j},\mu\right)}^{\alpha}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q}\left(\cup_{j}E_{j},\mu\right)}^{\alpha}

for each pairwise disjoint family
\{E_{j}\}_{j}
of
\mu
-measurable sets.

Proof.

Let
\{E_{j}\}_{j}
be a pairwise disjoint family of
\mu
-measurable sets. Since
\frac{\alpha}{q}\geq 1
, we can use Minkowski’s integral inequality,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j}\left\|f\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q}\left(E_{j},\mu\right)}^{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{q}{\alpha}}=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j}\left(p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q}\mu\left(\{x\in E_{j}:|f(x)|>t\}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}\right)^{\frac{q}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\left\{p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q}\mu\left(\{x\in E_{j}:|f(x)|>t\}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\frac{dt}{t}\right\}_{j}\right\|_{\ell^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q}\left\|\left\{\mu\left(\{x\in E_{j}:|f(x)|>t\}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right\}_{j}\right\|_{\ell^{\frac{\alpha}{q}}}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q}\left\|\left\{\mu\left(\{x\in E_{j}:|f(x)|>t\}\right)\right\}_{j}\right\|_{\ell^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}}^{\frac{q}{p}}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q}\left\|\left\{\mu\left(\{x\in E_{j}:|f(x)|>t\}\right)\right\}_{j}\right\|_{\ell^{1}}^{\frac{q}{p}}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q}\mu\left(\left\{x\in\cup_{j}E_{j}:|f(x)|>t\right\}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q}\left(\cup_{j}E_{j},\mu\right)}^{q},

where, in the two inequalities above, we have used that
\frac{\alpha}{q}\geq 1
and
\frac{\alpha}{p}\geq 1
respectively.
∎

The next lemma shows that the functional arising in our Lorentz-type estimates satisfies the corresponding geometric condition under the natural assumptions.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
w\in A_{r}
with
1\leq r\leq p
. For
1\leq M\leq\infty
, we define
p_{M}^{*}
by the relation

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{M}^{*}}=\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{rM}.

We consider the following functional over cubes:

a(Q)=\ell(Q)\left\|g\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)},

where
q\leq p_{M}^{*}
. Then,

(1)

If
M>1
, then
a\in SD_{p_{M}^{*}}^{nM^{\prime}}(w)
with
\|a\|\leq[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{nrM}}
.

(2)

If
M=1
, then
p_{M}^{*}=p_{r}^{*}
and
a\in D_{p^{*}_{r}}(w)
with
\|a\|\leq[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{nr}}
.

Proof.

Let
Q
be a cube and let
\{Q_{j}\}_{j}
be a collection of pairwise disjoint subcubes of
Q
. Invoking (
2.6
), which is a consequence of the
A_{r}
condition, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j}a(Q_{j})^{p_{M}^{*}}w(Q_{j})=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\ell(Q_{j})^{p_{M}^{*}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p,q}\left(Q_{j},\frac{wdx}{w(Q_{j})}\right)}^{p_{M}^{*}}w(Q_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}|Q_{j}|^{\frac{p_{M}^{*}}{n}}\left(\frac{1}{w(Q_{j})}\right)^{\frac{p_{M}^{*}}{nrM}}\left\|g\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q}\left(Q_{j},w\right)}^{p_{M}^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}|Q_{j}|^{\frac{p_{M}^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{|Q_{j}|^{r}}{w(Q_{j})}\right)^{\frac{p_{M}^{*}}{nrM}}\left\|g\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q}\left(Q_{j},w\right)}^{p_{M}^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left([w]_{A_{r}}\frac{|Q|^{r}}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{p_{M}^{*}}{nrM}}\sum_{j}|Q_{j}|^{\frac{p_{M}^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}\left\|g\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q}\left(Q_{j},w\right)}^{p_{M}^{*}}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left([w]_{A_{r}}\frac{|Q|^{r}}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{p_{M}^{*}}{nrM}}\left(\sup_{j}|Q_{j}|^{\frac{p_{M}^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}\right)\sum_{j}\left\|g\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q}\left(Q_{j},w\right)}^{p_{M}^{*}}.

By hypothesis we have
q\leq p^{*}_{M}
, so we can use Lemma
5.1
with
\alpha=p^{*}_{M}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j}a(Q_{j})^{p_{M}^{*}}w(Q_{j})\leq

\displaystyle\left([w]_{A_{r}}\frac{|Q|^{r}}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{p_{M}^{*}}{nrM}}\left|\bigcup_{j}Q_{j}\right|^{\frac{p_{M}^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}\left\|g\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q}\left(Q,w\right)}^{p_{M}^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{p_{M}^{*}}{nrM}}\left(\frac{\left|\bigcup_{j}Q_{j}\right|}{|Q|}\right)^{\frac{p_{M}^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}\ell(Q)^{p_{M}^{*}}\left\|g\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q}\left(Q,\frac{wdx}{w(Q)}\right)}^{p_{M}^{*}}w(Q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{p_{M}^{*}}{nrM}}\left(\frac{\left|\bigcup_{j}Q_{j}\right|}{|Q|}\right)^{\frac{p_{M}^{*}}{nM^{\prime}}}a(Q)^{p_{M}^{*}}w(Q).

This proves the first assertion for
1<M\leq\infty
. In particular, when
M=\infty
, one has
p_{M}^{*}=p
,
M^{\prime}=1
, and
[w]_{A_{r}}^{1/(nrM)}=1
. When
M=1
, the same computation gives
p_{M}^{*}=p_{r}^{*}
and
1/M^{\prime}=0
, hence

\sum_{j}a(Q_{j})^{p_{r}^{*}}w(Q_{j})\leq[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{p_{r}^{*}}{nr}}a(Q)^{p_{r}^{*}}w(Q),

which is precisely the
D_{p_{r}^{*}}(w)
condition with
\|a\|\leq[w]_{A_{r}}^{1/(nr)}
.
∎

The weighted Poincaré-Sobolev estimate (
1.11
) provides a convenient example to compare the self-improvement phenomena associated with the conditions
a\in D_{p_{r}^{*}}(w)
and
a\in SD_{p}^{n}(w)
. Denote by
a(Q)
the right-hand side functional in (
1.11
), and for
M\geq 1
we consider
p_{M}^{*}
defined in Lemma
5.2
. We have proved that
a\in D_{p_{r}^{*}}(w)
with
\|a\|\leq[w]_{A_{r}}^{1/(nr)}
and
a\in SD_{p_{M}^{*}}^{nM^{\prime}}(w)
with
\|a\|\leq[w]_{A_{r}}^{1/(nrM)}
for every
M>1
. Consequently, whether one applies the self-improving result under the
D_{p_{r}^{*}}(w)
condition (Theorem
1.2
) or the one under the
SD_{p_{M}^{*}}^{nM^{\prime}}(w)
condition (Theorem
1.5
), one recovers the same conclusion, namely (
1.11
). This shows the compatibility between the
D_{p}(w)
and
SD_{p}^{s}(w)
conditions in this natural model example.

Now, we apply the previous result to provide the proof of Corollary
1.15
.

Proof of Corollary
1.15
.

Since
1\leq r\leq p
and
p_{r}^{*}>1
, we have
A_{r}\subset A_{p}=A_{p,p_{r}^{*}}
. Combining the
(1,1)
Poincaré inequality (
1.1
) with the characterization of the
A_{p,q}
condition given in (
2.7
), applied with
g=|\nabla f|
, we obtain

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|dx\leq c_{n}[w]_{A_{p,p_{r}^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\ell(Q)\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,p_{r}^{*}}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}

for each cube
Q
. Now we consider the functional

a(Q)=c_{n}[w]_{A_{p,p_{r}^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\ell(Q)\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,p_{r}^{*}}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}.

By Lemma
5.2
(with
M=1
) we have
a\in D_{p_{r}^{*}}(w)
and
\|a\|\leq[w]_{A_{r}}^{1/(nr)}
. Therefore, we can apply Theorem
1.2
and this yields (
1.14
).
∎

Let us emphasize that the previous argument is specific to the Lorentz norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p,p_{r}^{*}}}
on the right-hand side. Indeed, in this setting we have
p_{r}^{*}=p_{M}^{*}
when
M=1
, and this endpoint case corresponds to the condition
D_{p_{M}^{*}}(w)
rather than to an
SD_{p}^{s}(w)
-type condition. For this reason, in the proof of Corollary
1.15
, we have used Theorem
1.2
instead of Theorem
1.5
. By contrast, as we discussed before the proof of Corollary
1.15
, if we consider the self-improving result with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}
on the right-hand side, then Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.2
will lead to the same target exponent, since
(p_{M}^{*})_{r,nM^{\prime}}^{*}=p_{r}^{*}
.

To conclude this section, we will apply Theorem
1.5
to prove Corollary
1.18
, providing an
\exp L
-type Poincaré-Sobolev inequality in the case of
p\geq nr
.

Proof of Corollary
1.18
.

Fix
1\leq q<\infty
. Choose
M>1
so that
q\leq p_{M}^{*}
, where
p_{M}^{*}
is the exponent from Lemma
5.2
. Consider the cube functional

a(Q)=[w]_{A_{p,q}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\ell(Q)\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,q}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}.

By Lemma
5.2
we have
a\in SD_{p_{M}^{*}}^{\,nM^{\prime}}(w)
with

\|a\|\leq[w]_{A_{r}}^{1/(nrM)}
. Since
p\geq nr
, it follows that
p_{M}^{*}\geq rnM^{\prime}
. Therefore, the second part of Theorem
1.5
applies and yields (
1.15
).
∎

6.
Applications to fractional Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities

In this section, we investigate the fractional Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities. In order to consider two-weight
A_{p}
conditions, we need the following result adapted from
[
33
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
0<\delta<1
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
f\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, and let
(u,v)\in A_{p}
. Then there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
such that

(6.1)

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|\,dx\leq c_{n}[u,v]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}(1-\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}\ell(Q)^{\delta}\left(\frac{1}{u(Q)}\int_{Q}\int_{Q}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,dy\,v(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for every cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Proof.

Assume first that
0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{2}
. By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|Q|^{2}}\int_{Q}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f(y)|\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\ell(Q)^{\delta}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\left(\int_{Q}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}[u,v]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\ell(Q)^{\delta}\left(\frac{1}{u(Q)}\int_{Q}\int_{Q}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,dy\,v(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Since
(1-\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq 2^{-\frac{1}{p}}
in this range, this gives the claimed bound.

It remains to consider the case
\frac{1}{2}\leq\delta<1
. We follow the proof of the one-weight version in
[
33
, Corollary 5.2]
. Fix a cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and let
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\delta
. By (
1.16
) applied with parameter
\delta-\varepsilon
, we have

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|\,dx\leq C_{n}(1-\delta+\varepsilon)\ell(Q)^{\delta-\varepsilon}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\int_{Q}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+\delta-\varepsilon}}\,dy\,dx.

If
p=1
, the conclusion follows from this estimate with
\varepsilon=0
and the definition of the two-weight
A_{1}
condition. We may therefore assume that
p>1
. For
0<\varepsilon\leq\delta
, Hölder’s inequality in the
y
variable gives, for every
x\in Q
,

\int_{Q}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+\delta-\varepsilon}}\,dy\leq C_{n}\frac{\ell(Q)^{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}\left(\int_{Q}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Hence, applying Hölder’s inequality again, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\ell(Q)^{\delta}\frac{1-\delta+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\left(\int_{Q}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}[u,v]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\ell(Q)^{\delta}\frac{1-\delta+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}\left(\frac{1}{u(Q)}\int_{Q}\int_{Q}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\delta p}}\,dy\,v(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Choosing
\varepsilon=1-\delta
yields
\frac{1-\delta+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}=2(1-\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}
. This proves the desired estimate also for
\frac{1}{2}\leq\delta<1
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

In fact, the previous result is a small improvement of

, where an extra factor

separately to eliminate the factor

.

We will use the following result from

. Although it is stated in the product space setting, the argument for cubes is identical; therefore, we state it below in that form.

Lemma 6.3
.

[

Let

with

, let

7.
Weak Poincaré-Sobolev inequality and applications to
A_{1}
weights

We present the proof of Theorem
1.23
and Corollary
1.24
. We will use the following weak Fefferman–Stein-type isoperimetric inequality.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
\mu
be a non-negative Borel measure. Then, there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
such that for all Lipschitz functions
f
we have

(7.1)

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(Q,\mu)}\leq c_{n}\int_{Q}|\nabla f(x)|M^{c}(\chi_{Q}\mu)(x)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}dx,

for each cube
Q
.

The previous result was first established in
[
13
]
in a different and more general setting. It was later considered in the Euclidean setting and extended in
[
37
]
, while a different proof, avoiding Minkowski’s inequality, was given in
[
38
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.23
.

By the definition of the Lorentz quasi-norm and the pointwise equivalence of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
and the centered Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M^{c}
, it suffices to prove that there exists
c_{n,p}>0
such that

(7.2)

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p^{*},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n,p}w(Q)^{\frac{1}{n}}\left\|\left|\nabla f\right|\frac{M(w\chi_{Q})^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}{w}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}.

For simplicity, set

A:=w(Q)^{\frac{1}{n}}\left\|\left|\nabla f\right|\frac{M(w\chi_{Q})^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}{w}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}.

If
A=0
, the desired estimate is trivial. Hence, we may assume that
A>0
. Let
t_{0}=C_{n,p}A
, where
C_{n,p}>0
is a sufficiently large constant to be fixed below. Since

\displaystyle\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p^{*},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}=\max\bigg\{

\displaystyle\sup_{0<t\leq t_{0}}t\left(\frac{w(\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>t\})}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}},

\displaystyle\sup_{t_{0}\leq t}t\left(\frac{w(\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>t\})}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\bigg\},

we consider two cases.

Case 1:

\sup_{0<t\leq t_{0}}t\left(\frac{w(\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>t\})}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\geq\sup_{t_{0}\leq t}t\left(\frac{w(\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>t\})}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}.

In this case, we have

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p^{*},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq t_{0}=C_{n,p}A,

and the desired estimate follows.

Case 2:

\sup_{0<t\leq t_{0}}t\left(\frac{w(\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>t\})}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\leq\sup_{t_{0}\leq t}t\left(\frac{w(\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>t\})}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}.

If the last supremum is zero, then the desired estimate is immediate. Otherwise, by the definition of the supremum, there exists
t_{1}\geq t_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}}{2}\left(\frac{w\left(\left\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>\frac{t_{1}}{2}\right\}\right)}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p^{*},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t_{0}\leq t}t\left(\frac{w(\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>t\})}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2t_{1}\left(\frac{w\left(\left\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>t_{1}\right\}\right)}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}.

This implies that

(7.3)

w\left(\left\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>\frac{t_{1}}{2}\right\}\right)\leq c_{n,p}w\left(\left\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>t_{1}\right\}\right).

Consider the set defined by

E_{t_{1}}:=\{x\in Q:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>t_{1}\}.

We proceed by considering the truncation operator

\tau_{\lambda}
defined as follows:

\tau_{\lambda}(g)(x):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }g(x)\leq\lambda,\\
g(x)-\lambda,&\text{if }\lambda<g(x)\leq 2\lambda,\\
\lambda,&\text{if }g(x)>2\lambda.\end{cases}

Note that, for each
x\in E_{t_{1}}
, we have

\frac{t_{1}}{2}=\tau_{\frac{t_{1}}{2}}(|f-f_{Q}|)(x)\leq\left|\tau_{\frac{t_{1}}{2}}(|f-f_{Q}|)(x)-\left(\tau_{\frac{t_{1}}{2}}(|f-f_{Q}|)\right)_{Q}\right|+\left(\tau_{\frac{t_{1}}{2}}(|f-f_{Q}|)\right)_{Q}.

Using the fact that
\tau_{\lambda}(g)(x)\leq g(x)
and (
1.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\tau_{\frac{t_{1}}{2}}(|f-f_{Q}|)\right)_{Q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|dx

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}\ell(Q)\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|\nabla f(x)|dx

\displaystyle=c_{n}\frac{1}{|Q|^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}\int_{Q}|\nabla f(x)|dx.

We now insert the weight in the previous estimate. Since

\frac{w(Q)}{|Q|}\leq\inf_{x\in Q}M(w\chi_{Q})(x),

we have

\displaystyle\left(\tau_{\frac{t_{1}}{2}}(|f-f_{Q}|)\right)_{Q}

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}\frac{1}{w(Q)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}\inf_{x\in Q}M(w\chi_{Q})(x)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\int_{Q}|\nabla f(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}\frac{1}{w(Q)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}\int_{Q}|\nabla f(x)|M(w\chi_{Q})(x)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}dx

\displaystyle=c_{n}w(Q)^{\frac{1}{n}}\left\|\left|\nabla f\right|\frac{M(w\chi_{Q})^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}{w}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{1}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq c_{n,p}w(Q)^{\frac{1}{n}}\left\|\left|\nabla f\right|\frac{M(w\chi_{Q})^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}{w}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}

\displaystyle=c_{n,p}A.

In the last inequality, we used Kolmogorov’s inequality again. Choosing the constant
C_{n,p}=4c_{n,p}
in the definition of
t_{0}
, and recalling that
t_{1}\geq t_{0}
, we obtain

\left(\tau_{\frac{t_{1}}{2}}(|f-f_{Q}|)\right)_{Q}\leq\frac{t_{1}}{4}.

As a result,

E_{t_{1}}\subset\left\{x\in Q:\left|\tau_{\frac{t_{1}}{2}}(|f-f_{Q}|)(x)-\left(\tau_{\frac{t_{1}}{2}}(|f-f_{Q}|)\right)_{Q}\right|\geq\frac{t_{1}}{4}\right\}.

Applying Theorem
7.1
to
\tau_{\frac{t_{1}}{2}}(|f-f_{Q}|)
with the measure
w(x)dx
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}}{4}w(E_{t_{1}})^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t_{1}}{4}w\left(\left\{x\in Q:\left|\tau_{\frac{t_{1}}{2}}(|f-f_{Q}|)(x)-\left(\tau_{\frac{t_{1}}{2}}(|f-f_{Q}|)\right)_{Q}\right|\geq\frac{t_{1}}{4}\right\}\right)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\tau_{\frac{t_{1}}{2}}(|f-f_{Q}|)-\left(\tau_{\frac{t_{1}}{2}}(|f-f_{Q}|)\right)_{Q}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{n^{\prime},\infty}(Q,w)}

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}\int_{Q}\left|\nabla\big(\tau_{\frac{t_{1}}{2}}(|f-f_{Q}|)\big)(x)\right|M^{c}(w\chi_{Q})(x)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}dx

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}\int_{\left\{x\in Q:\frac{t_{1}}{2}<|f(x)-f_{Q}|<t_{1}\right\}}|\nabla f(x)|\frac{M(w\chi_{Q})(x)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}{w(x)}w(x)dx.

Using the Lorentz-space version of Hölder’s inequality (
2.2
), it follows that,

\frac{t_{1}}{4}w(E_{t_{1}})^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\leq c_{n,p}\left\|\chi_{\left\{x\in Q:\frac{t_{1}}{2}<|f(x)-f_{Q}|<t_{1}\right\}}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p^{\prime},1}(Q,w)}\left\|\left|\nabla f\right|\frac{M(w\chi_{Q})^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}{w}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,\infty}(Q,w)}.

Moreover, by (
7.3
),

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\left\{x\in Q:\frac{t_{1}}{2}<|f(x)-f_{Q}|<t_{1}\right\}}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p^{\prime},1}(Q,w)}

\displaystyle=c_{p}w\left(\left\{x\in Q:\frac{t_{1}}{2}<|f(x)-f_{Q}|<t_{1}\right\}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{n,p}w\left(E_{t_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

Therefore,

t_{1}w(E_{t_{1}})^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\leq c_{n,p}w(E_{t_{1}})^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left\|\left|\nabla f\right|\frac{M(w\chi_{Q})^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}{w}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,\infty}(Q,w)}.

Since
\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{p^{*}}
, we obtain

(7.4)

t_{1}w(E_{t_{1}})^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\leq c_{n,p}\left\|\left|\nabla f\right|\frac{M(w\chi_{Q})^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}{w}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,\infty}(Q,w)}.

Combining everything together, we have

\displaystyle\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{L^{p^{*},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq 2t_{1}\left(\frac{w(E_{t_{1}})}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{n,p}\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\left\|\left|\nabla f\right|\frac{M(w\chi_{Q})^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}{w}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,\infty}(Q,w)}

\displaystyle=c_{n,p}w(Q)^{\frac{1}{n}}\left\|\left|\nabla f\right|\frac{M(w\chi_{Q})^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}}{w}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}

\displaystyle=c_{n,p}A.

This proves (
7.2
), and concludes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Generalized Poincaré inequalities with polynomials

Fix a cube
Q
and an integer
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
. We consider the space
\mathcal{P}_{m}(Q)
of polynomials of degree at most
m
in
n
variables restricted to the cube
Q
. We denote by
P_{Q}^{m}f
the projection of a function
f
onto the space
\mathcal{P}_{m}(Q)
. We have the following property: there exists a constant
c_{n,m}>0
such that

(A.1)

\left\|P_{Q}^{m}f\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{\infty}(Q)}\leq c_{n,m}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)|dx.

We refer to
[
37
, Section 8]
for a detailed explanation of this setting. The polynomial framework here is an extension of the average we discussed in previous sections, as
P_{Q}^{0}f=f_{Q}
.

We state the main result of this section.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
,
1\leq s<\infty
, and let
a
be a functional over cubes. Let
w\in A_{r}
and let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a locally integrable function satisfying

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-P_{Q}^{m}f(x)|\,dx\leq a(Q)

for every cube
Q
.

•

Assume that
a\in SD_{p}^{s}(w)
and
p<rs
. Then there exists a constant
c_{n,m}>0
such that, for every cube
Q
,

(A.2)

\left\|f-P_{Q}^{m}f\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p_{r,s}^{*},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n,m}p_{r,s}^{*}\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{rs}}\,\|a\|\,a(Q),

where
p_{r,s}^{*}
is defined by the relation

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{r,s}^{*}}=\frac{1}{s\,r}.

•

Assume that
a\in SD_{p}^{s}(w)
and
p\geq rs
. Then there exists a constant
c_{n,m,p}>0
such that, for every cube
Q
,

(A.3)

\|f-P_{Q}^{m}f\|_{\exp L\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n,m,p}\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\|a\|\,a(Q).

Sketch of the proof.

We follow the proof of Theorem
1.5
, replacing averages by the projections
P_{Q}^{m}f
. Fix a cube
Q
and set
\Omega_{t}:=\{x\in Q:\ M_{Q}(f-P_{Q}^{m}f)(x)>t\}
. Let
\{Q_{j}\}_{j}
be the maximal dyadic subcubes forming
\Omega_{t}
. Then either
\Omega_{t}=Q
, or

t<\frac{1}{|Q_{j}|}\int_{Q_{j}}|f-P_{Q}^{m}f|\,dx\leq 2^{n}t.

Using
P_{Q_{j}}^{m}f-P_{Q}^{m}f=P_{Q_{j}}^{m}(f-P_{Q}^{m}f)
and (
A.1
), one gets
\|P_{Q_{j}}^{m}f-P_{Q}^{m}f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{j})}\leq c_{m}2^{n}t
, and hence for a suitable
\kappa=\kappa(n,m)>1
,

w(\Omega_{\kappa t})\leq\sum_{j}w(E_{Q_{j}}),\qquad E_{Q_{j}}:=\{x\in Q_{j}:\ M_{Q_{j}}(f-P_{Q_{j}}^{m}f)(x)>t\}.

Introduce the polynomial sharp maximal function

M_{Q}^{\#,m}f(x):=\sup_{x\in R\in\mathcal{D}(Q)}\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}|f-P_{R}^{m}f|.

For
\gamma>0
split each
E_{Q_{j}}
into the regions where
M_{Q}^{\#,m}f\leq\gamma t
and where
M_{Q}^{\#,m}f>\gamma t
. The polynomial good-lambda inequality (obtained from
[
5
, Theorem 7.1]
) yields

w(\Omega_{\kappa t})\leq c_{1}e^{-\frac{c_{2}}{[w]_{A_{\infty}}\gamma}}\,w(\Omega_{t})+w(\{x\in Q:M_{Q}^{\#,m}f(x)>\gamma t\}).

The second term is estimated exactly as in the proof of (
1.6
) using the hypothesis
a\in SD_{p}^{s}(w)
. The exponential estimate (
A.3
) follows from (
A.2
) via Proposition
3.1
.
∎

As a consequence of this result, and in view of the lack of a truncation method for higher-order derivatives, we obtain the following analogue of Corollary
1.15
.

Corollary A.2
.

Let
1\leq m<n
. Let
w\in A_{r}
with
1\leq r\leq p
,
1<p<\frac{n}{m}r
and consider
p_{m,r}^{*}
defined by

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{m,r}^{*}}=\frac{m}{n}\frac{1}{r}.

Then, there exists a constant
c_{n,m}>0
such that

\|f-P_{Q}^{m-1}f\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p_{m,r}^{*},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n,m}p^{*}_{m,r}[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,[w]_{A_{p,p_{m,r}^{*}}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{m}{rn}}\,\ell(Q)^{m}\,\|\nabla^{m}f\|_{L^{p,p_{m,r}^{*}}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)},

for every cube
Q
.

The proof of this result is essentially the same as that of Corollary
1.15
, but using the following starting inequality from
[
2
]
: there exists a constant
C>1
such that

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-P_{Q}^{m-1}f(x)|dx\leq C\ell(Q)^{m}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|\nabla^{m}f(x)|dx,

for every cube
Q
. The rest of the proof follows from the preceding arguments; we omit it here.

Appendix B
Self-improving of vector-valued Poincaré inequalities

In this appendix, we extend the main self-improving result of the paper to the vector-valued setting, where the oscillation is measured through the
\ell_{q}
-norm.

Theorem B.1
.

Let
q\geq 1
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
,
1\leq s<\infty
, and let
a
be a functional over cubes. Let
w\in A_{r}
and let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\longrightarrow\ell_{q}
be a locally integrable function satisfying

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\left\|f(x)-f_{Q}\right\|_{\ell_{q}}\,dx\leq a(Q)

for every cube
Q
.

•

Assume that
a\in SD_{p}^{s}(w)
and
p<rs
. Then there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
such that, for every cube
Q
,

\left\|\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\ell_{q}}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p_{r,s}^{*},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n}p_{r,s}^{*}\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{rs}}\,\|a\|\,a(Q),

where
p_{r,s}^{*}
is defined by the relation

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{r,s}^{*}}=\frac{1}{s\,r}.

•

Assume that
a\in SD_{p}^{s}(w)
and
p\geq rs
. Then there exists a constant
c_{n,p}>0
such that, for every cube
Q
,

\left\|\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\ell_{q}}\right\|_{\exp L\left(Q,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n,p}\,[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\|a\|\,a(Q).

Sketch of the proof.

This is a direct vector-valued adaptation of the scalar proof: one applies the whole argument to the scalar function
x\mapsto\|f(x)-f_{Q}\|_{\ell_{q}}
. The Calderón–Zygmund decomposition and the key estimate
w(\Omega_{\kappa t})\leq\sum_{j}w(E_{Q_{j}})
follow from the triangle inequality in
\ell_{q}
and Minkowski’s inequality. The good-lambda step applies to the corresponding sharp maximal function built from
\|f-f_{R}\|_{\ell_{q}}
, and the remaining estimate uses
a\in SD_{p}^{s}(w)
exactly as in the scalar case. The exponential endpoint follows from the weak bounds via Proposition
3.1
.
∎

We also remark that one can prove a vector-valued version of
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
. Such a vector-valued extension would yield the corresponding good-lambda inequality needed in the previous argument.

In order to derive concrete consequences of this vector-valued self-improving result, we shall use the following lemma from
[
29
]
.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
q\geq 1
. There exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
such that, for any cube
Q
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and for any vector-valued function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\longrightarrow\ell_{q}
with components in
C^{1}(Q)
, the representation formula

\left\|f(x)-f_{Q}\right\|_{\ell_{q}}\leq c_{n}I_{1}(\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\ell_{q}}\chi_{Q})(x)

holds for every
x\in Q
, where
I_{1}
is the Riesz potential defined by
I_{1}g(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{g(y)}{|z-y|^{n-1}}dy
.

Since
I_{1}
is self-adjoint, the previous result implies the following vector-valued
(1,1)
Poincaré inequality.

Proposition B.3
.

Let
q\geq 1
. There exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
such that, for any cube
Q
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and for any vector-valued function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\longrightarrow\ell_{q}
with components in
C^{1}(Q)
, we have

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\left\|f(x)-f_{Q}\right\|_{\ell_{q}}dx\leq c_{n}\ell(Q)\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\left\|\nabla f(x)\right\|_{\ell_{q}}dx.

Consequently, noting the fact that the truncation method is not known to work in the vector-valued setting (as noted in
[
29
, p. 2]
), we obtain the following analogue of Corollary
1.15
.

Corollary B.4
.

Let
q\geq 1
. Let
w\in A_{r}
with
1\leq r\leq p
,
1<p<nr
and consider
p_{r}^{*}
defined by

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{r}^{*}}=\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{r}.

Then, there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
such that

\left\|\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{\ell_{q}}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p^{*}_{r},\infty}\left(Q,\frac{wdx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n}p^{*}_{r}[w]_{A_{\infty}}\,[w]_{A_{p,p^{*}_{r}}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,[w]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{rn}}\ell(Q)\left\|\|\nabla f\|_{\ell_{q}}\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p,p^{*}_{r}}\left(Q,\frac{wdx}{w(Q)}\right)},

for every cube
Q
.

Appendix C
Further extensions: Rectangles

In this appendix, we generalize Theorems
1.5
and
A.1
to the context of rectangles in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. We borrow the terminology from
[
6
]
. We denote by
\mathcal{R}
the family of rectangles in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. By a rectangle, we mean the product of
n
intervals in
{\mathbb{R}}
. We will denote by
SD_{p,\mathcal{R}}^{s}(w)
the condition analogous to the
SD_{p}^{s}(w)
condition, considering rectangles instead of cubes. We will state the result in the context of polynomial approximation, with the same definitions as in the previous section.

Theorem C.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
,
1\leq s<\infty
, and let
a
be a functional over rectangles. Let
w\in A_{r,\mathcal{R}}
and let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a locally integrable function satisfying

\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}|f(x)-P_{R}^{m}f(x)|\,dx\leq a(R)

for every rectangle
R
.

•

Assume that
a\in SD_{p,\mathcal{R}}^{s}(w)
and
p<rs
. Then there exists a constant
c_{n,m}>0
such that, for every rectangle
R
,

\left\|f-P_{R}^{m}f\right\|_{\rule{0.0pt}{6.93195pt}L^{p_{r,s}^{*},\infty}\left(R,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(R)}\right)}\leq c_{n,m}p_{r,s}^{*}\,[w]_{A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}}\,[w]_{A_{r,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{rs}}\,\|a\|\,a(R),

where
p_{r,s}^{*}
is defined by the relation

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{r,s}^{*}}=\frac{1}{s\,r}.

•

Assume that
a\in SD_{p,\mathcal{R}}^{s}(w)
and
p\geq rs
. Then there exists a constant
c_{n,m,p}>0
such that, for every rectangle
R
,

\|f-P_{R}^{m}f\|_{\exp L\left(R,\frac{w(x)\,dx}{w(R)}\right)}\leq c_{n,m,p}\,[w]_{A_{\infty,\mathcal{R}}}\,[w]_{A_{r,\mathcal{R}}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\|a\|\,a(R).

The proof is the same as that of Theorem
A.1
but using the Rising Sun Lemma instead of Calderón–Zygmund decomposition. We state here the lemma for the sake of completeness.

Lemma C.2

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
R
be a rectangle in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and let
f
be a function such that

\frac{1}{|R|}\int_{R}f(x)dx\leq A.

Then, there exists a pairwise disjoint family of rectangles
\{R_{j}\}_{j}
such that

•

R_{j}\subset R
for all
j
.

•

\frac{1}{|R_{j}|}\int_{R_{j}}f(x)dx=A
for all
j
.

•

f(x)\leq A
a.e.
x\in R\setminus\bigcup_{j}R_{j}
.

Remark C.3
.

The preceding lemma does not introduce any dimensional constant. The dimensional dependence in the previous result comes instead from the polynomial projection estimate (
A.1
). In the non-polynomial case, that is, when the oscillation is measured with respect to the average
f_{Q}
, Theorem
C.1
does not require any dimensional constants.
