Eichler-Selberg relations for the third-order mock theta functions
Wei Wang

Abstract
. 1.
Statement of the results

In his famous letter to Hardy, Ramanujan introduced 17 new series that he
called mock theta functions, and he grouped these series by order.
Since then, mock theta functions have attracted considerable interest, see for example
[
1
,
2
,
6
,
16
]
.
Furthermore, since Zwegers’ systematic investigation of the modularity of mock theta functions in his doctoral thesis
[
20
]
, these series can be understood within the framework of harmonic Maass forms, leading to the proof of the well-known conjectures regarding mock theta functions (e.g.
[
4
,
10
]
). A comprehensive survey of this topic can be found in
[
3
]
.

An example of a third-order mock theta function is given by

(1)

f(q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{f}(n)q^{n}:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}}}{(-q;q)_{n}^{2}}.

Throughout this paper we use the standard notation

(z;q)_{n}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-zq^{j})~~\text{ if }n\in\mathbb{N},\\
&\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}(1-zq^{j})~~\text{ if }n=\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

where
q:=e^{2\pi i\tau}
for
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
the upper half plane. Watson
[
16
]
studied the behaviour of the third order functions under the modular group, and he found the following Appell-Lerch series representation of
f(q)
:

(2)

f(q)=\frac{2}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{3}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n}}{1+q^{n}}.

By Euler’s pentagonal number theorem

(q;q)_{\infty}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{k}q^{\frac{k(3k+1)}{2}},

Watson’s identity (
2
) can be written as

(3)

\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{k}c_{f}\left(n-\frac{k(3k+1)}{2}\right)q^{n}=2\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{3}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n}}{1+q^{n}}.

where we define
c_{f}(n)=0
for
n<0
. The aim of this paper is to provide a natural generalization of (
3
). To state our results, we first introduce some notation. Let
T_{n}(x)
denote the Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind, which are defined by the following recurrence relation:

\displaystyle T_{0}(x)=1,~T_{1}(x)=x,

\displaystyle T_{n+1}(x)=2xT_{n}(x)-T_{n-1}(x)\text{ for }n\geq 1.

They are given by the explicit formula:

(4)

T_{n}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\binom{n}{2j}(x^{2}-1)^{j}x^{n-2j}.

Let
E_{n}
denote the Euler numbers, which are defined by the following generating function:

\frac{2}{e^{t}+e^{-t}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}E_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

The following is one of the main results of this paper.

Theorem 1
.

For every
v\geq 1
, the
q
-series

(5)

\displaystyle 6^{v}\cdot\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}n^{v}T_{2v}\left(\frac{6k+1}{\sqrt{24n}}\right)(-1)^{k}c_{f}\left(n-\frac{k(3k+1)}{2}\right)q^{n}

\displaystyle-2\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{12}{2n+1}\right)\frac{n^{2v}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{6}}}{1+q^{n}}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{E_{2v}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}(2n-1)^{2v}q^{2n-1}}{1+q^{2n-1}}-\frac{2^{2v+1}}{E_{2v}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}n^{2v}q^{n}}{1+q^{2n}}

is a cusp form in
S_{2v+1}(\Gamma_{0}(8),\left(\tfrac{-1}{\cdot}\right))
. In particular, when
v=1
or
2
, this cusp form vanishes.

Our results are in the spirit of the Eichler-Selberg class number formula (see
[
18
, Chapter 6.3]
or
[
9
, (1.3)]
):

\mathrm{Tr}(n;2k)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}n^{k-1}U_{2k-2}\left(\frac{r}{2\sqrt{n}}\right)H(4n-r^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d\mid n}\min(d,n/d)^{k},

where
H(d)
denotes the Hurwitz–Kronecker class numbers and
U_{2k-2}(x)
denotes the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind.

The
q
-series in the second line of (
5
) can be regarded as a proper generalization of the right-hand side of (
3
). Indeed, we observe that

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{3}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n}}{1+q^{n}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{12}{2n+1}\right)\frac{q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{6}}}{1+q^{n}}.

For a proof of this identity, see Lemma
7
. The
q
-series in the third line is essentially the Lambert series representation of the Eisenstein series on
\Gamma_{0}(8)
with the character
\left(\tfrac{-1}{\cdot}\right)
. Remarkably, although our proof assumes
v\geq 1
, since the Eisenstein parts cancel out at weight one, the result remains consistent for
v=0
. Watson’s identity (
2
) can be viewed as a special case of our result at weight one, providing a hint for generalizing the approach to other mock theta functions.

For
v=1
, our result yields the identity

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left((6k+1)^{2}-12n\right)(-1)^{k}c_{f}\left(n-\frac{k(3k+1)}{2}\right)q^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{12}{2n+1}\right)\frac{n^{2}q^{n(n+1)/6}}{1+q^{n}}+4\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}(2n-1)^{2}q^{2n-1}}{1+q^{2n-1}}-16\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}n^{2}q^{n}}{1+q^{2n}},

and for
v=2
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[(6k+1)^{4}-24n(6k+1)^{2}+72n^{2}\right](-1)^{k}c_{f}\left(n-\frac{k(3k+1)}{2}\right)q^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{12}{2n+1}\right)\frac{n^{4}q^{n(n+1)/6}}{1+q^{n}}-\frac{4}{5}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}(2n-1)^{4}q^{2n-1}}{1+q^{2n-1}}+\frac{64}{5}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}n^{4}q^{n}}{1+q^{2n}}.

To the best of the author’s knowledge, the two identities presented above have not previously appeared in the literature. For
v=3
, the cusp form in Theorem
1
is non-vanishing. Numerical computations show that this cusp form is given by

-\frac{20736}{61}\eta^{4}(z)\eta^{10}(2z),

where

\eta(z)=q^{-1/24}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})=q^{-1/24}(q;q)_{\infty}

denotes the Dedekind eta function.

Let

\omega(q):=\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{\omega}(n)q^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2n(n+1)}}{(q;q^{2})_{n+1}^{2}}

be another third order mock theta function. We establish the following analogous result.

Theorem 2
.

For every
v\geq 1
, the
q
-series

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(12n+9)^{v}T_{2v}\left(\frac{6k+1}{\sqrt{12n+9}}\right)(-1)^{k}c_{\omega}(n-k(3k+1))q^{\frac{n}{2}+\frac{3}{8}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{12}{n+2}\right)\frac{n^{2v}q^{\frac{n(n+4)}{24}}}{1-q^{\frac{n}{2}}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2E_{2v}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(2n-1)^{2v}q^{\frac{n}{4}-\frac{1}{8}}}{1+q^{\frac{n}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}}+\frac{1}{2E_{2v}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}(2n-1)^{2v}q^{\frac{n}{4}-\frac{1}{8}}}{1-q^{\frac{n}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}}

is a cusp form in
S_{2v+1}(\Gamma^{0}(8),\chi_{-4})
. In particular, when
v=1
or 2, this cusp form vanishes.

This can be seen as a generalization of the classical identity given by Watson
[
16
]
:

\omega(q)=\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}q^{3n(n+1)}\frac{1+q^{2n+1}}{1-q^{2n+1}}.

The second line of the
q
-series in Theorem
2
also serves as the proper generalization of the summation on the right-hand side of the identity above, see Lemma
8
.

The primary strategy of this paper is outlined below. This approach is also applicable to a wide class of other mock theta functions. First, we identify the non-holomorphic part and the modular transformation properties of the given mock theta function. Second, we interpret the Appell-Lerch or Hecke-type representation (see
[
5
]
) of a mock theta function can be seen as the holomorphic projection of a weight 1/2 eta-quotient multiplied by the corresponding harmonic Maass form. Third, we consider the Rankin-Cohen brackets of this eta-quotient and the harmonic Maass form. Its holomorphic projection yields a modular form on a certain congruence subgroup with specific characters. The Eisenstein part is then determined by analyzing its expansions at various cusps.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations

In this section, we fix some standard notation. For
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
the upper half plane,
q:=e^{2\pi i\tau}
. For positive integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\zeta_{n}:=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{n}}
denote the primitive
n
-th root of unity. Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character. For any matrix
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}^{+}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, define
\chi(\gamma):=\chi(d)
. Let
k
be a positive integer. For a function
f(\tau)
defined on the upper half plane
\mathbb{H}
, the weight
k
slash operator is defined by

(f|_{k}\gamma)(\tau)=(ad-bc)^{k/2}j(\gamma,\tau)^{-k}f\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right),~j(\gamma,\tau):=c\tau+d.

The incomplete gamma function is defined by

\Gamma(t,x):=\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-z}z^{t-z}dz.

Let
\Gamma
be a subgroup of finite index of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and
f,g
be two modular forms of weight
k
. Then the Petersson scalar product of
f
and
g
with respect to the group
\Gamma
is defined by

\langle f,g\rangle=\frac{1}{[\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\Gamma]}\int_{\Gamma\setminus\mathbb{H}}f(\tau)\overline{g(\tau)}y^{k-2}dxdy,~\tau=x+iy,

provided this integral exists.

We recall the Rankin–Cohen brackets, which serve as a generalization of the standard multiplication of modular forms. Let
f
and
g
be two smooth functions defined on the upper half-plane and let
k
and
l
be two integers or half-integers. The
v
-th Rankin–Cohen bracket for
f
and
g
is defined as

[f,g]_{v}:=\sum_{0\leq j\leq v}(-1)^{j}\binom{k+v-1}{v-j}\binom{l+v-1}{j}\operatorname{D}^{j}f\operatorname{D}^{v-j}g,

where
\operatorname{D}f:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{df}{d\tau}
. The 0-th Rankin–Cohen bracket of two modular forms is the product of these two modular forms. The following result given by Cohen
[
7
, Theorem 7.1]
shows that Rankin-Cohen bracket, in a sense, is a generalization of the product map:

(6)

[f\big|_{k}\gamma,g\big|_{l}\gamma]_{v}=[f,g]_{v}\big|_{k+l+2v}\gamma,~\forall\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

2.2.
Mock theta functions

Zwegers generalized Watson’s work and provided new insights into the study of mock theta functions by demonstrating that these series, when augmented by a suitable non-holomorphic completion, yield functions that satisfy modular transformation properties. These functions are now known as harmonic Maass forms and have since been the subject of extensive research. We recall Zwegers’ completion of the series (
1
), defining

\displaystyle g_{0}(\tau):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}(n+1/3)q^{\tfrac{3}{2}(n+\tfrac{1}{3})^{2}},

\displaystyle g_{1}(\tau):=-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}(n+1/6)q^{\tfrac{3}{2}(n+\tfrac{1}{6})^{2}},

\displaystyle g_{2}(\tau):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(n+1/3)q^{\tfrac{3}{2}(n+\tfrac{1}{3})^{2}}.

Set

F(\tau):=\left(q^{-1/24}f(q),2q^{1/3}\omega(q^{1/2}),2q^{1/3}\omega(-q^{1/2})\right)^{T}\text{ and }G(\tau):=\left(g_{1},g_{0},-g_{2}\right)^{T}.

Zwegers
[
19
, Theorem 3.6]
showed that

H(\tau):=F(\tau)-2i\sqrt{3}\int_{-\overline{\tau}}^{i\infty}\frac{G(z)}{(-i(\tau+z))^{\tfrac{1}{2}}}dz

is a vector valued real-analytic modular form of weight 1/2, transforming as

(7)

H(\tau+1)=\begin{pmatrix}\zeta^{-1}_{24}&0&0\\
0&0&\zeta_{3}\\
0&\zeta_{3}&0\\
\end{pmatrix}H(\tau),~~H(-1/\tau)=\zeta_{8}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&-1\\
\end{pmatrix}H(\tau)\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The following result establishes the modular transformation law for the Rankin–Cohen bracket of the Dedekind eta function and the required harmonic Maass form.

Lemma 3
.

We have, for
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(8)
,

[H_{1}(\tau),\eta(\tau)]_{v}\big|_{1+2v}\gamma=\left(\frac{-4}{d_{\gamma}}\right)[H_{1}(\tau),\eta(\tau)]_{v},

where
d_{\gamma}
denotes the lower-right entry of the matrix
\gamma
, and
H_{1}(z)
denotes the first component of the vector
H
.

Proof.

We first recall the transformation properties property of the Dedekind eta function:

(8)

\eta(\tau+1)=\zeta_{24}\eta(\tau),~~~\eta(-1/\tau)=\zeta_{8}^{-1}\eta(\tau)\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(8)
is generated by the following four matrices:

-I=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad\gamma_{1/4}=\begin{pmatrix}5&-1\\
16&-3\end{pmatrix},\quad\gamma_{1/2}=\begin{pmatrix}5&-2\\
8&-3\end{pmatrix}.

The modular curve
X_{0}(8)
associated with the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(8)
has genus
g=0
and
c=4
cusps, represented by the equivalence classes of
\infty,0,1/2
, and
1/4
. Since
\Gamma_{0}(8)
contains no elliptic elements, its image in
\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is a free group of rank
2g+c-1=3
. Consequently,
\Gamma_{0}(8)
can be minimally generated by
-I
along with three independent parabolic elements stabilizing the cusps. The matrices
T
,
\gamma_{1/4}
, and
\gamma_{1/2}
act as the parabolic generators stabilizing the cusps
\infty
,
1/4
, and
1/2
, respectively.

Since the group
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is generated by
T
and
S:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

we express the matrix
\gamma_{1/4}
and
\gamma_{1/2}
as words in the generators
T
and
S
:

\gamma_{1/4}=-ST^{-3}ST^{5}S\text{ and }\gamma_{1/2}=-ST^{-2}ST^{3}S.

By the modular transformation properties of
H
(
7
), we find that

H_{1}(\tau)\big|_{1/2}\gamma_{1/4}=H_{1}(\tau)\big|_{1/2}(-ST^{-3}ST^{5}S)=\zeta_{4}^{-1}\zeta_{8}^{-1}\zeta_{3}^{-3}(-\zeta_{8})^{-1}\zeta_{3}^{5}\zeta_{8}^{-1}H_{1}(\tau)=\zeta_{24}^{13}H_{1}(\tau).

The modular transformation properties of the Dedekind eta function (
8
) implies that

\eta(\tau)\big|_{1/2}\gamma_{1/4}=\zeta_{4}^{-1}\zeta_{8}^{-3}\zeta_{24}^{2}\eta(\tau)=\zeta_{24}^{-13}\eta(\tau).

Using the properties of the Rankin–Cohen brackets in (
6
), we obtain

[H_{1}(\tau),\eta(\tau)]_{v}\big|_{1+2v}\gamma_{1/4}=[H_{1}(\tau)\big|_{1+2v}\gamma_{1/4},\eta(\tau)\big|_{1+2v}\gamma_{1/4}]_{v}=[H_{1}(\tau),\eta(\tau)]_{v}.

Following an analogous calculation, we have

\displaystyle[H_{1}(\tau),\eta(\tau)]_{v}\big|_{1+2v}(-I)=-[H_{1}(\tau),\eta(\tau)]_{v},~

\displaystyle[H_{1}(\tau),\eta(z)]_{v}\big|_{1+2v}T=[H_{1}(z),\eta(z)]_{v},

\displaystyle[H_{1}(\tau),\eta(\tau)]_{v}\big|_{1+2v}\gamma_{1/2}=[H_{1}(\tau),\eta(\tau)]_{v}.

These relations characterize the transformation properties of
[H_{1}(\tau),\eta(\tau)]_{v}
under the generators of
\Gamma_{0}(8)
, which completes the proof.
∎

2.3.
Holomorphic projection

In the previous section, we obtained a real-analytic modular form
[H_{1}(\tau),\eta(\tau)]_{v}
. To study it within the framework of holomorphic modular forms, we require the tool of holomorphic projection. The holomorphic projection was first introduced by Sturm
[
15
]
and later extensively utilized by Gross and Zagier
[
11
]
in their seminal work on Heegner points. Imamoğlu, Raum, and Richter
[
13
]
provided a spectral-theoretic account of holomorphic projection and applied it to vector-valued harmonic Maass forms, particularly to mock theta functions. It provides a direct analytical bridge between real-analytic modular transformations and the arithmetic data encoded in the Fourier coefficients of holomorphic cusp forms.

Let
k>2
be an integer and
F:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
be a smooth function that transforms with weight
k
under the congruence subgroup
\Gamma
with the multiplier system
\nu
and has the Fourier expansion

F(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Q}}a_{F}(n,y)q^{n},

where
y=\Im(\tau)
. For every cusp
\kappa
of
\Gamma
, fix
\gamma_{\kappa}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\gamma_{\kappa}(i\infty)=\kappa
. Assume that for some
\delta,\epsilon>0
, the function
F(z)
satisfies

(1)

\left(F\big|_{k}\gamma_{\kappa}\right)(z)=c_{0,\kappa}+O(y^{-\delta})
for each cusp
\kappa
of
\Gamma
,

(2)

a_{F}(n,y)=O(y^{1-k+\epsilon})
as
y\rightarrow 0
for all
n>0
.

The holomorphic projection operator, denoted by
\pi_{\mathrm{hol}}
, assigns to
F
a unique holomorphic modular form
f=\pi_{\mathrm{hol}}(F)\in M_{k}(\Gamma,\nu)
such that their Petersson inner products with any holomorphic cusp form coincide. Specifically, for all
g\in S_{k}(\Gamma)
, the projection satisfies the identity

(9)

\langle g,\pi_{\mathrm{hol}}(F)\rangle=\langle g,F\rangle.

From a computational perspective, the holomorphic projection can be determined explicitly via its Fourier coefficients. The
n
-th Fourier coefficient
a_{n}
of the resulting holomorphic cusp form
f(z)=a_{0}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}}a_{n}q^{n}
is given by the integral formula:

a_{n}=\frac{(4\pi n)^{k-1}}{(k-2)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}a_{F}(n,y)e^{-4\pi ny}y^{k-2}dy,

and the constant term
a_{0}
of
f(z)
coincides with the constant term of
F(z)
at the cusp
i\infty
.

We also require the following properties of the holomorphic projection operator, which facilitate the computation of its expansion at various cusps.

Proposition 4
.

With the above notation, we have

\pi_{\mathrm{hol}}(F\big|_{k}\gamma_{\kappa})=\pi_{\mathrm{hol}}(F)\big|_{k}\gamma_{\kappa}.

Proof.

Recall that the Poincaré series with respect to the cusp
\kappa
is defined by

P_{k}^{n}(\Gamma,\nu,\kappa;z)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{\kappa}\setminus\Gamma}\overline{\chi(g)}e^{2\pi i(n/w)\gamma_{\kappa}^{-1}gz}j(\gamma_{\kappa}^{-1}g,z)^{-k},

where
\Gamma_{\kappa}=\Gamma\cap\gamma_{\kappa}\Gamma_{\infty}\gamma_{\kappa}^{-1}
and
w
denotes the width of the cusp
\kappa
. It has the property (see
[
8
, Chapter 8.2]
):

(1)

For
f\in M_{k}(\Gamma,\nu)
and
f\big|_{k}\gamma_{\kappa}=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{\kappa}(n)q^{n/w}
,

a_{\kappa}(n)=\frac{(4\pi n)^{k-1}}{(k-2)!}\frac{w^{k}}{\left[\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\Gamma\right]}\langle f(z),P_{k}^{n}(\Gamma,\nu,\kappa;z)\rangle.

(2)

If
\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
P_{k}^{n}(\Gamma,\nu,\kappa;z)\big|_{k}\gamma=P_{k}^{n}(\gamma^{-1}\Gamma\gamma,\gamma\nu\gamma^{-1},\gamma^{-1}(\kappa);z)
.

Now by (
9
) and the property of the Poincaré series, we get

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{\mathrm{hol}}(F),P_{k}^{n}(\Gamma,\nu,\kappa)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle F,P_{k}^{n}(\gamma_{\kappa}^{-1}\Gamma\gamma_{\kappa},\gamma_{\kappa}\nu\gamma_{\kappa}^{-1},i\infty)\big|_{k}\gamma_{\kappa}^{-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\pi_{\mathrm{hol}}(F\big|_{k}\gamma_{\kappa}),P_{k}^{n}(\gamma_{\kappa}^{-1}\Gamma\gamma_{\kappa},\gamma_{\kappa}\nu\gamma_{\kappa}^{-1},i\infty)\rangle.

Up to a common constant factor, the first term in the above expression is the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of
\pi_{\mathrm{hol}}(F)\big|_{k}\gamma_{\kappa}
, while the last term is the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of
\pi_{\mathrm{hol}}(F\big|_{k}\gamma_{\kappa})
. This concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

The following result complements the results of Mertens
[
14
]
. Although this specific case was not addressed in
[
14
]
, the proof is analogous; we therefore provide only a brief sketch here. We fix an integer
w>0
and an integer
\rho
satisfying
0\leq\rho\leq w
. We assume that
f
is a mock theta function, i.e. its shadow is a (linear combination of) weight 3/2 unary theta series
\theta_{\psi}
and
g
is also a weight 1/2 unary theta series
\theta_{\chi}
. In other words, we assume that
f
and
g
have the Fourier expansions

f(\tau)=\sum_{n+\rho\geq 0}a_{f}^{+}(n)q^{\frac{n}{w}}+\sum_{n>0}\psi(n)n^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2},4\pi\frac{n^{2}}{w}y\right)q^{-\frac{tn^{2}}{w}},

and

g(\tau)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\chi(n)q^{\frac{\rho n^{2}}{w}},

respectively.

Theorem 5
.

With the above notation, the holomorphic projection of
[f,g]_{v}
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho/w\right)^{v}\binom{2v}{v}a_{f}^{+}(-\rho)+\binom{2v}{v}(4w)^{-v}\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}T_{2v}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{n}}m\right)n^{v}\chi(m)a_{f}^{+}(n-\rho m^{2})q^{\frac{n}{w}}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\pi}(4w)^{-v}\binom{2v}{v}\sum_{\rho m^{2}-tn^{2}=r}\chi(m)\psi(n)\left(\sqrt{\rho}m-\sqrt{t}n\right)^{2v}q^{\frac{r}{w}}.

Proof.

We decompose
f
into its holomorphic and non-holomorphic parts:
f=f^{+}+f^{-}
. Accordingly, the holomorphic decomposition of
[f,g]_{v}
is given by

\pi_{\mathrm{hol}}([f,g]_{v})=[f^{+},g]_{v}+\pi_{\mathrm{hol}}([f^{-},g]_{v}).

The first part follows directly from the definition of the Rankin–Cohen bracket (
6
):

[f^{+},g]_{v}=\sum_{j=0}^{v}(-1)^{j}\binom{v-\tfrac{1}{2}}{v-j}\binom{v-\tfrac{1}{2}}{j}\operatorname{D}^{j}f^{+}\operatorname{D}^{v-j}g.

To simplify the coefficients, we employ the following identity for binomial coefficients involving half-integers:

\binom{v-\tfrac{1}{2}}{v-j}\binom{v-\tfrac{1}{2}}{j}=2^{-2v}\binom{2v}{v}\binom{2v}{2j},

which is a consequence of the duplication formula for the Gamma function, and we obtain

\displaystyle[f^{+},g]_{v}

\displaystyle=2^{-2v}\binom{2v}{v}\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=0}^{v}(-1)^{j}\binom{2v}{2j}\left(\frac{n}{w}\right)^{j}\left(\frac{\rho m^{2}}{w}\right)^{v-j}\chi(m)a_{f}^{+}(n)q^{\frac{n+\rho m^{2}}{w}}

\displaystyle=2^{-2v}\binom{2v}{v}w^{-j}\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=0}^{v}\binom{2v}{2j}(\rho m^{2}-n)^{j}(\rho m^{2})^{v-j}\chi(m)a_{f}^{+}(n-\rho m^{2})q^{\frac{n}{w}}.

The preceding expression can be reformulated in terms of Chebyshev polynomials (
4
), consistent with the statement of our result.

Regarding the holomorphic projection of the non-holomorphic component, we invoke the results of Mertens
[
14
, Theorem 4.6]
with necessary modifications, as we treat the case of weight
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
not explicitly covered in
[
14
]
. If
f^{-}=\sum_{n>0}c^{-}_{f}(n)\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2},4\pi\tfrac{n^{2}}{w}y)q^{-\frac{tn^{2}}{w}}
and
g=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{g}(n)q^{\frac{n}{w}}
, then

\pi_{\mathrm{hol}}([f^{-},g]_{v})=\sqrt{\pi}(4w)^{-v}\binom{2v}{v}\sum_{r\geq 1}\sum_{M-N=r}a_{g}(M)c^{-}_{f}(N)N^{-1/2}(M^{\frac{1}{2}}-N^{\frac{1}{2}})q^{r}.

In our setting, we have
c_{f}^{-}(N)=\psi(n)n^{-1/2}
for
N=tn^{2}
and
c_{f}^{-}(N)=0
otherwise,
a_{g}(M)=\chi(m)
for
M=\rho m^{2}
and
a_{g}(M)=0
otherwise. Consequently, in this case,
\pi_{\mathrm{hol}}([f^{-},g]_{v})
is given by

\sqrt{\pi}(4w)^{-v}\binom{2v}{v}\sum_{r\geq 1}\sum_{\rho m^{2}-tn^{2}=r}\chi(m)\psi(n)\left(\sqrt{\rho}m-\sqrt{t}n\right)^{2v}q^{\frac{r}{w}},

and the proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 7
.

The following identity holds:

(15)

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{3n^{2}+n}{2}}}{1+q^{n}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{12}{2n+1}\right)\frac{q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{6}}}{1+q^{n}}.

Proof.

Let
L(q)
and
R(q)
denote the left- and right-hand sides of (
15
), respectively. We evaluate
R(q)
by trisecting the summation index modulo 3.
Observe that the Kronecker symbol
\left(\frac{12}{2n+1}\right)
vanishes for
n\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, reduces to
(-1)^{m}
for
n=3m
, and to
-(-1)^{m}
for
n=3m-1
. Decomposing
R(q)
over these residue classes yields:

(16)

R(q):=R_{1}+R_{2}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{m}q^{\frac{3m^{2}+m}{2}}}{1+q^{3m}}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{m}q^{\frac{3m^{2}-m}{2}}}{1+q^{3m-1}}.

For
L(q)
, we multiply the summand by
(1-q^{n}+q^{2n})/(1-q^{n}+q^{2n})
, which splits the series into three parts:
L(q)=L_{1}-L_{2}+L_{3}
, where
L_{1}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}\frac{q^{\frac{3n^{2}+n}{2}}}{1+q^{3n}}
. Note that
L_{1}
coincides with
R_{1}
. For
L_{3}
, applying the involution
n\mapsto-n
, we find that
L_{3}=L_{1}
. Thus, it suffices to show that
R_{1}-R_{2}=L_{2}
.

Following Hickerson and Mortenson
[
12
]
, we define

j(z;q):=(z;q)_{\infty}(q/z;q)_{\infty}(q;q)_{\infty}

and

(17)

m(x,q,z):=\frac{1}{j(z;q)}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}z^{n}}{1-q^{n-1}xz}.

With this notation, we denote the part to be proven as

(18)

j(q^{2};q^{3})m(-q,q^{3},q^{2})-j(q;q^{3})m(-q,q^{3},q)=j(q^{3};q^{3})m(-1,q^{3},q^{3}).

By the partial fraction expansion for the reciprocal of Jacobi’s theta product (see
[
12
, (1.3)]
)

(19)

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{n(n+1)/2}}{1-q^{n}z}=\frac{J_{1}^{3}}{j(z;q)},

where
J_{m}:=j(q^{m},q^{3m})
, the right-hand side of (
18
) is equal to

\frac{J_{3}^{3}}{j(-1;q^{3})}.

For the left-hand side of (
18
), we need the following result (see
[
12
, Theorem 2.3]
):

(20)

m(x,q,z_{1})-m(x,q,z_{0})=\frac{z_{0}J_{1}^{3}j(z_{1}/z_{0};q)j(xz_{0}z_{1};q)}{j(z_{0};q)j(z_{1};q)j(xz_{0};q)j(xz_{1};q)}.

Substituting
q\mapsto q^{3}
,
x=-q
,
z_{1}=q^{2}
, and
z_{0}=q
into (
20
), we obtain

(21)

m(-q,q^{3},q^{2})-m(-q,q^{3},q)=\frac{qJ_{3}^{3}j(q;q^{3})j(-q^{4};q^{3})}{j(q;q^{3})j(q^{2};q^{3})j(-q^{2};q^{3})j(-q^{3};q^{3})}.

Apply the standard quasi-periodicity
j(q^{3}z;q^{3})=-z^{-1}j(z;q^{3})
and symmetry
j(z;q^{3})=j(q^{3}/z;q^{3})
, the right-hand side of (
21
) simplifies to:

\frac{J_{3}^{3}}{J_{1}j(-1;q^{3})},

and this completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 8
.

The following identity holds:

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}q^{3n(n+1)}\frac{1+q^{2n+1}}{1-q^{2n+1}}=-q^{-\frac{3}{4}}\sum_{n\in 2\mathbb{Z}+1}\left(\frac{12}{n+2}\right)\frac{q^{\frac{n(n+4)}{12}}}{1-q^{n}}

Proof.

First, by setting
n=2k+1
, the right-hand side series becomes:

R(q):=-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{12}{2k+3}\right)\frac{q^{\frac{k^{2}+3k-1}{3}}}{1-q^{2k+1}}.

Setting
k=3m+1
and
k=3m-1
, we decompose
R(q)=R_{1}-R_{2}
, where:

R_{1}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{m}\frac{q^{3m^{2}+5m+1}}{1-q^{6m+3}},~~R_{2}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{m}\frac{q^{3m^{2}+m-1}}{1-q^{6m-1}}.

Using
\frac{1+q^{2n+1}}{1-q^{2n+1}}=\frac{2}{1-q^{2n+1}}-1
and
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}q^{3n(n+1)}=0
, we find that the left-hand side series becomes

L(q)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{3n^{2}+3n}}{1-q^{2n+1}}.

We express the denominator as
(1+q^{2n+1}+q^{4n+2})/(1-q^{6n+3})
, which splits
L(q)
into three sums:
L(q)=L_{1}+L_{2}+L_{3}
, where
L_{1}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}\frac{q^{3n^{2}+3n}}{1-q^{6n+3}}
.
Notice
L_{2}
identically matches
R_{2}
. Applying the involution
n\mapsto-n-1
to
L_{3}
precisely yields
L_{2}
. Thus,
L(q)=L_{1}+2R_{1}
. It then suffices to show
L_{1}+R_{1}+R_{2}=0
.

Using the
m
-function introduced in (
17
), we express
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
via the standard
m(x,q,z)
function:

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=-q^{-1}j(q^{2};q^{6})m(q,q^{6},q^{2}),

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=q^{-1}j(q^{4};q^{6})m(q,q^{6},q^{4})=q^{-1}j(q^{2};q^{6})m(q,q^{6},q^{4}).

Applying the pole-shifting identity (
20
) for
z_{1}=q^{4},z_{0}=q^{2}
:

m(q,q^{6},q^{4})-m(q,q^{6},q^{2})=\frac{q^{2}J_{6}^{3}j(q^{2};q^{6})j(q^{7};q^{6})}{j(q^{2};q^{6})j(q^{4};q^{6})j(q^{3};q^{6})j(q^{5};q^{6})}=\frac{-qJ_{6}^{3}}{j(q^{2};q^{6})j(q^{3};q^{6})}.

Substituting this back, we obtain

R_{1}+R_{2}=q^{-1}j(q^{2};q^{6})\left[\frac{-qJ_{6}^{3}}{j(q^{2};q^{6})j(q^{3};q^{6})}\right]=-\frac{J_{6}^{3}}{j(q^{3};q^{6})}=-\frac{J_{6}^{4}}{J_{3}^{2}}.

Finally, by Jacobi’s theta product (
19
), we evaluate
L_{1}
using the standard Lambert series evaluation:

L_{1}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{3n^{2}+3n}}{1-q^{6n+3}}=\frac{J_{6}^{4}}{J_{3}^{2}}.

Therefore,
R_{1}+R_{2}+L_{1}=\frac{J_{6}^{4}}{J_{3}^{2}}-\frac{J_{6}^{4}}{J_{3}^{2}}=0
, completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 9
.

For any integer
v\geq 0
, the following identity holds:

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{12}{2n+1}\right)\frac{n^{2v}q^{n(n+1)/6}}{1+q^{n}}=\sum_{m>n>0}\left(\frac{12}{m}\right)\left(\frac{-12}{n}\right)\left(\frac{m-n}{2}\right)^{2v}q^{\frac{m^{2}-n^{2}}{24}}.

Proof.

Let
L(q)
denote the left-hand side of the equation. We separate
L(q)
into positive and negative index domains:
L(q)=L_{+}+L_{-}
. For
n>0
, we expand the denominator as a geometric series
\frac{1}{1+q^{n}}=\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^{j}q^{jn}
, yielding:

L_{+}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^{j}\left(\frac{12}{2n+1}\right)n^{2v}q^{\frac{n(n+1+6j)}{6}}.

We introduce a diagonalization of the quadratic form by setting
m=2n+6j+1
and
n^{\prime}=6j+1
. Since
n\geq 1
and
j\geq 0
, this maps bijectively to the domain
m>n^{\prime}>0
with
n^{\prime}\equiv 1\pmod{6}
. Under this substitution, we have
n=\frac{m-n^{\prime}}{2}
and the exponent becomes
\frac{m^{2}-(n^{\prime})^{2}}{24}
.
Furthermore,
n^{\prime}\equiv 1\pmod{6}
implies
\left(\frac{-12}{n^{\prime}}\right)=1
. The periodicity of the Kronecker symbol gives
\left(\frac{12}{m}\right)=\left(\frac{12}{2n+1+6j}\right)=(-1)^{j}\left(\frac{12}{2n+1}\right)
. Substituting these into
L_{+}
yields:

L_{+}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m>n^{\prime}>0\\
n^{\prime}\equiv 1\pmod{6}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{12}{m}\right)\left(\frac{-12}{n^{\prime}}\right)\left(\frac{m-n^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{2v}q^{\frac{m^{2}-(n^{\prime})^{2}}{24}}.

For
n<0
, we substitute
n=-u
with
u>0
. Similarly, the sum becomes

L_{-}=\sum_{u\geq 1}\sum_{j\geq 1}(-1)^{j-1}\left(\frac{12}{2u-1}\right)u^{2v}q^{\frac{u(u-1+6j)}{6}}.

We apply the substitution
m=2u+6j-1
and
n^{\prime}=6j-1
. For
u,j\geq 1
, this maps to
m>n^{\prime}>0
with
n^{\prime}\equiv 5\pmod{6}
.
Here,
n^{\prime}\equiv 5\pmod{6}
dictates
\left(\frac{-12}{n^{\prime}}\right)=-1
. Thus, the negative index sum exactly generates the
n^{\prime}\equiv 5\pmod{6}
congruences:

L_{-}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m>n^{\prime}>0\\
n^{\prime}\equiv 5\pmod{6}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{12}{m}\right)\left(\frac{-12}{n^{\prime}}\right)\left(\frac{m-n^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{2v}q^{\frac{m^{2}-(n^{\prime})^{2}}{24}}.

Since
\left(\frac{-12}{n^{\prime}}\right)
is supported entirely on
\gcd(n^{\prime},6)=1
, the sum
L_{+}+L_{-}
perfectly reconstitutes the Hecke indefinite theta series over all valid coprime residue classes.
∎

Lemma 10
.

For every integer
v\geq 0
, the following identity holds:

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{12}{n+2}\right)\frac{n^{2v}q^{n(n+4)/24}}{1-q^{n/2}}=\sum_{m>n>0}\bigg(\frac{12}{m}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{n}{3}\bigg)(-1)^{n-1}\left(m-2n\right)^{2v}q^{\frac{m^{2}-4n^{2}}{24}}.

Proof.

The proof is entirely analogous to that of Lemma
9
. We establish the identity by expanding the denominator geometrically and splitting the summation into positive and negative domains of
n
. For
n>0
, expanding
(1-q^{n/2})^{-1}=\sum_{j\geq 0}q^{jn/2}
and applying the substitution
m=n+6j+2
and
n^{\prime}=3j+1
bijectively generates the terms of the Hecke double sum restricted to the residue class
n^{\prime}\equiv 1\pmod{3}
. For
n<0
, we set
n=-u
and expand
(1-q^{-u/2})^{-1}=-\sum_{j\geq 1}q^{ju/2}
. The substitution
m=u+6j-2
and
n^{\prime}=3j-1
similarly recovers exactly the terms corresponding to
n^{\prime}\equiv-1\pmod{3}
.

One can straightforwardly verify that the quadratic forms factorize as
m^{2}-4n^{\prime 2}=n(n+4)
in both domains. The remaining character evaluations follow from the periodicity of the Kronecker symbol exactly as demonstrated in Lemma
9
.
∎

Lemma 11
.

For every
v\geq 0
, the following identities hold:

\displaystyle E_{2v+1}^{1,\chi_{-4}}(\tau)-2E_{2v+1}^{1,\chi_{-4}}(2\tau)+\frac{E_{2v}}{2}=2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}(2n-1)^{2v}q^{2n-1}}{1+q^{2n-1}},

\displaystyle E_{2v+1}^{1,\chi_{-4}}(\tau)-E_{2v+1}^{1,\chi_{-4}}(2\tau)=2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}(2n-1)^{2v}q^{2n-1}}{1-q^{4n-2}},

\displaystyle E_{2v+1}^{\chi_{-4},1}(\tau)-2^{2v+1}E_{2v+1}^{\chi_{-4},1}(2\tau)=2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}n^{2v}q^{n}}{1+q^{n}},

\displaystyle E_{2v+1}^{\chi_{-4},1}(\tau)-2^{2v}E_{2v+1}^{\chi_{-4},1}(2\tau)=2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(2n-1)^{2v}q^{2n-1}}{1+q^{4n-2}}.

Proof.

It is well-known that

\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d>0\end{subarray}}\chi_{-4}(d)d^{s}q^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}(2n-1)^{s}q^{2n-1}}{1-q^{2n-1}}

and

\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d>0\end{subarray}}\chi_{-4}(n/d)d^{s}q^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{n^{s}q^{n}}{1+q^{2n}}.

The result follows from elementary
q
-series analysis. For instance, the first identity is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n-1}(2n-1)^{s}q^{2n-1}}{1-q^{2n-1}}-\frac{2(-1)^{n-1}(2n-1)^{s}q^{4n-2}}{1-q^{4n-2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}(2n-1)^{s}q^{2n-1}(1+q^{2n-1})}{(1-q^{2n-1})(1+q^{2n-1})}-\frac{2(-1)^{n-1}(2n-1)^{s}q^{4n-2}}{1-q^{4n-2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}(2n-1)^{2v}q^{2n-1}}{1+q^{2n-1}}

The proofs of the remaining identities are left to the reader.
∎
