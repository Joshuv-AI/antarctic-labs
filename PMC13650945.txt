Development
of Noval, Recyclable, and Tunable Aryl
Imidazolium Salts with a Dual Brønsted–Lewis Acid Character
as Catalysts for the Copper(I)-Catalyzed Azide–Alkyne Cycloaddition
Reaction (CuAAC)

Wen-Yueh Ho, Yu-Hsuan Yang, Jyun-Siao Chen, Ratnnadeep C. Sawant, Tzu-Hsin Shu, Pei-Yu Chen, Chia-Yun Chang, Shun-Yuan Luo

Abstract

The development of novel aryl imidazolium salts (ISs)
with dual
Brønsted–Lewis acid character represents a significant
advancement in the field of green chemistry and catalysis. These imidazolium
salts serve as efficient catalysts for the copper­(I)-catalyzed azide–alkyne
cycloaddition (CuAAC) reaction, a cornerstone of click chemistry.
The unique design of the aryl imidazolium framework, incorporating
both Brønsted and Lewis acid sites, enhances the catalytic efficiency
and selectivity of the CuAAC reaction. Furthermore, the use of these
imidazolium salts as catalysts eliminates the need for additional
phase-transfer catalysts or cosolvents, simplifying the reaction setup
and reducing environmental impact. This study demonstrates the synthesis,
characterization, and application of these novel imidazolium salts,
highlighting their potential as sustainable and efficient media for
CuAAC reactions. The developed catalyst is stable in water and air,
and the highest catalytic activity could still be maintained after
4 cycles. The results indicate that the novel, recyclable, and tunable
aryl imidazolium salts with dual Brønsted–Lewis acid properties
offer a promising platform for the development of greener and more
efficient catalytic processes in organic synthesis.

Introduction

In an uncatalyzed reaction, the azide–alkyne
Huisgen cycloaddition
of organic azides and alkynes is a slow, high-temperature, and nonselective
reaction, which produces an equimolar mixture of 1,4- and 1,5-disubstituted
triazoles ( A).
−

 However, since the copper­(I)-catalyzed azide–alkyne
cycloaddition (CuAAC) discovery in 2002 by the groups of Sharpless,
Forkin, and Meldal independently, which are highly efficient, straightforward,
and mild procedures for the regioselective synthesis of 1,4-disubstituted
triazoles (B). This technique has received wide
applications in such diverse fields as bioconjugation, polymer ligation,
organic and medicinal syntheses, and surface science.
−

Over the past few decades, a great variety of
copper precursors
have been used in the CuAAC reaction.
−

 Recently, transition-metal-based molten salts have caught the attention
of chemists.
−

 In this work, because the organic substrates and
the synthesized aryl imidazolium salt catalyst are insoluble or only
partially soluble in water, the system operates as a heterogeneous
“on-water” catalytic system. In comparison with other
reported catalysts, this heterogeneous mixture avoids the use of organic
cosolvents and offers notable advantages in ease of preparation and
direct recyclability via centrifugation, allowing the reaction to
proceed smoothly at the solid–liquid–liquid interface
under mild conditions.

While current research on imidazole-based
molten salts predominantly
focuses on platforms bearing sp3-hybridized alkyl substituents,
introducing aryl substituents with distinct electron-withdrawing or
electron-donating groups onto the imidazole core can significantly
modulate the physicochemical features of the resulting ionic matrix
via mesomeric effects. Considering the importance of these attractive
properties and in continuation of our experience in molten salt catalysis.
−

 Herein, we report the application of Cu­(I) in molten salts as a
reusable catalyst system. This study investigated catalyzed-CuAAC
reactions of different substituents in aryl and distinct anions as
a ligand with copper­(I) in molten salts. In comparison with other
reported catalysts, this catalyst offers notable advantages in ease
of preparation and recyclability. Additionally, it enables the reaction
to proceed smoothly under mild conditionsnamely, at room temperature
and ambient pressurewith water serving as an environmentally
friendly solvent.
−

 Besides, we also explored the structure–activity relationship
based on molten salts’ physical and chemical properties.

Further investigation, including evaluating the reusability
and
stability of catalyst IS-Br2 for the CuAAC reaction of
benzyl azide 1a with phenylacetylene 2,
was conducted under the optimum conditions obtained by the test as
mentioned above (at room temperature, a reaction time of 3 h, catalyst IS-Br2 amount 3.0 mol %, and a molar ratio of benzyl azide 1a to phenylacetylene 2 of 1.05:1) that is based
on the previous study of aryl imidazolium ionic liquid. IS-Br2 could be easily separated from the reaction mixture by centrifugation
with drying under a vacuum after washing with EtOAc 2–3 times.
To ensure that the observed catalytic performance was not biased by
mass loss during the process, the recovered catalyst was carefully
weighed and resupplemented to the initial catalytic load (3.0 mol
%) before each cycle. Using this process, three consecutive CuAAC
procedures were conducted, for which the results are summarized in . Despite the precise
resupplementation of the catalyst amount, a gradual decrease in product
yield was observed, suggesting that the decline in reaction outcome
is primarily attributed to the reduction in the intrinsic catalytic
activity of IS-Br2, possibly due to structural degradation,
poisoning, or active site leaching during the reaction cycles. To
fully evaluate the practical appropriateness of this catalytic system
for recycling applications, the nature of the handling process must
be addressed. Because the reaction runs under a very low initial catalyst
loading (3.0 mol %), the absolute mass of the recovered solid in our
bench-scale setup is extremely small. The trace resupplementation
of the catalyst before each run was a necessary measure to compensate
for the unavoidable mechanical transfer losses caused by solid adhesion
to centrifuge tubes and pipettes during the multistep washing sequence.
Remarkably, despite these physical handling constraints, the catalytic
system maintained excellent efficiency throughout the first three
consecutive runs (98%, 94%, and 93% yield), with the performance only
declining to 86% by the fourth cycle. This outcome underscores the
excellent intrinsic chemical stability and robustness of the IS-Br2
matrix. Furthermore, such operational mass losses are highly scale-dependent
and are significantly minimized upon scaling up (as demonstrated by
the successful gram-scale synthesis), confirming that this system
is highly appropriate for scalable and sustainable green chemistry
applications.

With the best reaction conditions in optimization
experiments (, entry 9), we extended
the investigation to various substrate scopes (). Substrates 1b–1e bearing para halogen-substituted benzyl groups were all
suitable for this reaction and gave the corresponding products 3b–3e in good to excellent yields. However,
the responses of ortho-substituted benzyl substrate 3f delivered the desired product in a relatively lower yield (75%)
due to steric hindrance. In contrast, electron-withdrawing substituted
of ester- and fully fluoride-benzyl substrates (1g-1h) and 2-naphthylmethyl (Nap)-substituted substrates 1i proceeded with lower but still acceptable yields (46–67%)
to the corresponding triazoles 3g–3i. The reason is that the electron-withdrawing-substituted substrates
reduce the coupling effectivity of the azide with copper during the
reaction, resulting in poor reactivity. We attempted the reactions
of hydrophobic substrates 1j–1l.
In the case of phenyl-substituted substrates 1j and substrates 1l bearing long-chain alkyl substitution, the desired products
were reacted in 90% and 82%, respectively. On the other hand, the
reaction of 1k afforded 3k with 40% isolated
yield. It is noteworthy that several specific substrates, such as 3i (46%) and 3k (40%), afforded lower isolated
yields compared to other derivatives. This decrease in isolation efficiency
is directly attributed to the poor chemical reactivity of the starting
materials 1i and 1k under the standard reaction
conditions. Due to the less favorable electronic or steric effects
of these specific substrates, the low catalyst loading of 0.03 eq.
proved to be insufficient to drive the click reaction to completion
within the designated time frame. As a result, an excessive amount
of unreacted starting materials remained in the reaction mixture,
directly leading to the lower isolated yields. This limitation suggests
that a higher catalyst loading or extended reaction time might be
required for these less reactive substrate variants.

The proposed mechanism (

−

) fits perfectly with the heterogeneous and “on-water”
nature of this catalytic system. Since the organic azide, terminal
alkyne, and the aryl imidazolium matrix are hydrophobic, they are
forced to aggregate at the aqueous interface due to the hydrophobic
effect. This interfacial aggregation drastically increases the local
concentration of reactants. The terminal alkyne reacts with the accessible
[CuX2]− active sites on the partially dissolved
or suspended catalyst surface to generate Complex A.
Subsequently, the organic azide easily accesses the localized copper
center to form Complex C. The water molecules at the
interface further stabilize the polar intermediates (Complexes A–E) via hydrogen bonding, promoting smooth
oxidative coupling, ring contraction, and final product dissociation.
This heterogeneous pathway not only drives high catalytic efficiency
at room temperature but also enables convenient catalyst recovery.

Conclusion

The use of CuAAC click chemistry is increasing
tremendously daily,
such as in organic synthesis, drug designation, material science,
and polymer application. We herein report a novel imidazolium salt
as the catalyst, which was designed based on a tunable aryl imidazolium
moiety with dual Brønsted and Lewis acidic sites (B-L MISs).
These B-L MISs exhibited good reactivity for synthetic 1,4-disubstituted
1,2,3-triazoles by CuAAc reaction. The catalyst showed high atom economy
and high thermal stability, besides the imidazolium salt could be
recovered and reused for click chemistry. This provides more possibilities
for the industrial process application of CuAAc reaction, which provides
another eco-friendly method with the potential for scaling up in the
field of CuAAC.

Procedure and Characterization of CuAAC Products

General Procedure for CuAAC Reaction

In a vial containing
a magnetic stir bar were added IS-Br2 (1.0 mol % for 1b–1c and 1l; 3.0 mol % for 1a, 1d–1f, and 1h–1k; 0.5 mol % for 1g), water (solvent, 1.0 mL per 100
mg of 1), followed by phenylacetylene 2 (1.0
equiv) and azide compounds 1a–1l (1.05
equiv). The reaction mixture was stirred for 3 h at room temperature.
The mixture was quenched with an ammonia solution and diluted with
EtOAc. After the mixture was extracted with brine. The organic layer
was dried over MgSO4, then filtered, and concentrated with
reduced pressure to remove the solvent. Afterward, the residue was
purified by column chromatography (eluent: ethyl acetate/n-hexane) to afford the desired 1,2,3-triazole products 3a-3l.

Gram-Scale Synthesis of 1,2,3-Triazole 3a

In a vial containing a magnetic stirrer bar were added IS-Br2 (3.0 mol %), water (solvent, 10 mL), followed by phenylacetylene 2 (0.73 g, 7.15 mmol, 1.0 equiv) and azide compounds 1a (1.0 g, 7.51 mmol, 1.05 equiv). The reaction mixture was
stirred for 3 h at room temperature. The mixture was quenched with
an ammonia solution and diluted with EtOAc. After that, the mixture
was extracted with brine. The organic layer was dried over MgSO4, then filtered, and concentrated with reduced pressure to
remove the solvent. Afterward, the residue was purified by column
chromatography (eluent: ethyl acetate/n-hexane =
5/95) to afford the desired 1,2,3-triazole product 3a as a white solid (2.3 g, 98% isolated yield).
