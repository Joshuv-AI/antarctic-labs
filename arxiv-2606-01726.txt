Space of prime congruences in tropical geometry
Kentaro Tanaka

Abstract
. We investigate spaces of prime congruences on tropical algebras and study their geometry, inspired by classical scheme theory. Our main strategy is to use tropical algebras associated to ordered monoids, which play the role of the monomial structure of these algebras. Using the space of prime congruences as local models, we introduce a tropical toric scheme which contains the usual tropical toric variety as a subspace. We show how the separatedness and properness of these schemes are captured by scheme-theoretic points. As an application of our framework, we obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for a prime congruence to be finitely generated.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Tropical geometry has found an increasing number of applications in
algebraic geometry, providing combinatorial shadows of algebro-geometric
objects. Its primary geometric objects are rational polyhedral complexes
called tropical varieties, which are often obtained via the
tropicalization of algebraic varieties.

Recently, attempts have been made to develop a theory of tropical geometry
from an algebraic perspective.
The basic algebras used in tropical geometry are the semifields

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}=(\{-\infty,0\},\operatorname{max},+),

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\},\operatorname{max},+),

and (semi)algebras over these semifields.
The set of functions on a tropical variety is considered to form such an algebra.
One of the main interests in algebraic tropical geometry is
what geometric data these algebras contain and how they can be extracted.

In
[
JM18
]
,
the authors defined prime congruences and studied their properties.
A
prime congruence
on a semiring
S
is an equivalence relation on
S

which gives a surjective semiring homomorphism from
S
to
a semiring with suitable properties (see
definition

2.3
for the details).
In particular, they proposed the definition of the Krull dimension of
an additively idempotent semiring
S

and showed that it works well for some semirings which appear in tropical geometry.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
JM18
, parts of Theorems 4.6, 4.9, 4.14]
)
.

The following equalities hold:

•

\dim\mathbb{B}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]=\dim\mathbb{B}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]=n
,

•

\dim\mathbb{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]=\dim\mathbb{T}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]=n+1
.

In order to compute the Krull dimension,
they showed that any prime congruence on
\mathbb{B}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
or
\mathbb{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]

can be represented by a matrix with coefficients in
\mathbb{R}
, called an
admissible matrix

Furthermore, in
[
SN24
]
and
[
JM25
]
,
the authors calculated the Krull dimension of semirings of the form
\mathbb{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]/C

where
C
is a congruence satisfying appropriate conditions, using these matrices.

Admissible matrices are useful for expressing prime congruences as numerical data.
However, for a given prime congruence, the corresponding matrix is not uniquely determined.
This non-uniqueness makes it difficult to decide inclusion relations between prime congruences.

1.2.

Our aim is to treat the set of prime congruences on semirings as a geometric object.

Semirings and prime congruences

We briefly recall some basic properties of semirings.

In this paper, a

A semiring

A semiring

\phi(a+b)=\phi(a)+\phi(b),\ \phi(a\cdot b)=\phi(a)\cdot\phi(b),\ \phi(0_{S_{1}})=0_{S_{2}},\text{ and }\phi(1_{S_{1}})=1_{S_{2}},

Recall that

We introduce the notion above because the dimension of semirings often exhibits undesirable behavior.

-algebra

gives a continuous map

(7)

\displaystyle\phi=\iota^{*}\colon\operatorname{CSpec}S\to\operatorname{CSpec}\mathbb{T}.

The base space
\operatorname{CSpec}\mathbb{T}
contains two points:
the diagonal
\Delta
, and the kernel
P_{0}
of the surjective map
\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{B}
.
Note that the singleton
\{\Delta\}
is an open subset of
\operatorname{CSpec}\mathbb{T}

whose complement is a closed subset
V(P_{0})=\{P_{0}\}
.

Proposition 2.17
.

For the continuous map of (
7
),
the subset
\operatorname{CSpec}_{\mathbb{T}}S
is equal to the fiber
\phi^{-1}(\Delta)
of the point
\Delta
.
In particular,
\operatorname{CSpec}_{\mathbb{T}}S
is an open subset of
\operatorname{CSpec}S
.

Proof.

For
P\in\operatorname{CSpec}S

S^{\prime}
be the image of
\mathbb{T}
by the quotient map
\pi_{P}
.
Then the pull-back
\iota^{*}P
induces the composition map

\mathbb{T}\xrightarrow{\iota}S\xrightarrow{\pi_{P}}S^{\prime}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\phi^{-1}(\Delta)

\displaystyle=\{P\in\operatorname{CSpec}S\mid\phi(P)=\iota^{*}P=\Delta\}

\displaystyle=\{P\in\operatorname{CSpec}S\mid\iota^{*}P\text{ induces }\mathbb{T}\cong S^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{CSpec}_{\mathbb{T}}S

The latter assertion holds since the singleton

∎

With respect to the fibration
\operatorname{CSpec}S\to\operatorname{CSpec}\mathbb{T}
,
the special fiber

corresponds to what is often called the
recession
in tropical geometry
(see also
proposition

3.15
).

Definition 2.18
.

Let
S
be a
\mathbb{T}
-algebra and
P\in\operatorname{CSpec}_{\mathbb{T}}S
.
Define a map

\lim_{P}\colon S\to\mathbb{T}\cup\{+\infty\}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}

as follows:
If
\pi_{P}(s)\in S/P
is larger than any element of
\mathbb{R}\subseteq S/P
, then define
\lim_{P}s
as
+\infty
.
If
\pi_{P}(s)\in S/P
is smaller than any element of
\mathbb{R}\subseteq S/P
, then define
\lim_{P}s
as
-\infty
.
Otherwise,
\lim_{P}s
is defined as the supremum of the subset

\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mid t\leq\pi_{P}(s)\text{ in }S/P\}
of
\mathbb{R}
.
Note that
\lim_{P}s
is also the infimum of the subset

\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mid t\geq\pi_{P}(s)\text{ in }S/P\}
of
\mathbb{R}
.
We call
\lim_{P}s
the
limit
of
s
under
P
.

Lemma 2.19
.

If
P_{1},P_{2}\in\operatorname{CSpec}_{\mathbb{T}}S
and
P_{1}\subseteq P_{2}
,
then
\lim_{P_{1}}s=\lim_{P_{2}}s
for any
s\in S
.

Proof.

Assume
P_{1}\subseteq P_{2}
. Then there is a surjective homomorphism
S/P_{1}\to S/P_{2}
.
Since these are totally ordered
\mathbb{T}
-algebras, then the following are equivalent:

•

t-\epsilon<\pi_{P_{1}}(s)<t+\epsilon
for all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,

•

t-\epsilon<\pi_{P_{2}}(s)<t+\epsilon
for all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

Thus
\lim_{P_{1}}s=\lim_{P_{2}}s
holds for any
s\in S
if these limits are finite.
The same holds if these limits are
+\infty
or
-\infty
.
∎

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
P
be a prime congruence on a
\mathbb{T}
-algebra
S
lying over
\mathbb{T}
.
Then there exists a geometric congruence on
S
containing
P

if and only if
\lim_{P}s\in\mathbb{T}
holds for any
s\in S
.

Proof.

Assume that there exists a geometric congruence
P_{0}
which contains
P
.
By the definition of the limit, we have
\lim_{P_{0}}s=\pi_{P_{0}}(s)\in\mathbb{T}
for any
s\in S
.
By
lemma

2.19
, we have
\lim_{P}s\in\mathbb{T}
for any
s\in S
.

Conversely, assume that
\lim_{P}s\in\mathbb{T}
holds for any
s\in S
.
Then the map
\lim_{P}:S\to\mathbb{T}
is a homomorphism of semirings
such that the composition with
\mathbb{T}\hookrightarrow S
is identity.
Since
\lim_{P}
factors through
S\to S/P
,
the kernel congruence of
\lim_{P}
is a geometric congruence containing
P
.
∎

We would like to call the property of
proposition

2.21

the
separatedness
of
\operatorname{CSpec}_{\mathbb{T}}S
.
In this paper, this is not defined in a precise way,
but it is motivated by the valuative criterion for separatedness of usual schemes
(see
[
Har13
, Theorem 4.3]
for the details).

Proposition 2.21
.

For a prime congruence
P
on a
\mathbb{T}
-algebra
S
,
there is at most one geometric congruence which contains
P
.

Proof.

Assume that there exists a geometric congruence
P_{0}
which contains
P
.
In particular,
P
is lying over
\mathbb{T}
.
Fix
s\in S
. By the definition of
\lim_{P}s
, we have

\lim_{P}s-\epsilon<\pi_{P}(s)<\lim_{P}s+\epsilon
for any
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Sending to
S/P_{0}
, we have
\lim_{P}s-\epsilon<\pi_{P_{0}}(s)<\lim_{P}s+\epsilon
for any

.
Since

\pi_{P_{0}}(s)=\lim_{P}s

Thus

is equal to the kernel congruence of

∎

Remark 2.22
.

Any geometric congruence is maximal in

.
However, as we will see in

,

-algebra which is maximal in

3.
Tropical algebras with monomial sets

We now proceed gradually toward a tropical setting.
When we consider tropical algebras, additive idempotency is
one of the minimal requirements which we should impose on algebras.
However, for simplicity,
we impose not only additive idempotency but also a
monomial structure
on algebras.

3.1.
Ordered monoids

Recall that a
(commutative) monoid
is
a set equipped with an associative and commutative binary operation
+
and an identity element 0.
We write the monoid operation additively,
although the operation
+
will be identified
with the product
\odot
of a
\mathbb{B}
-algebra in the subsequent discussion.

An
ordered monoid
is
a pair
(\mathsf{M},\geq)
consisting of a commutative monoid
\mathsf{M}=(\mathsf{M},+)

together with a partial order
\geq
satisfying

a\geq b\implies a+c\geq b+c,

for any
a,b,c\in\mathsf{M}
.
The pair
(\mathsf{M},\geq)
is often denoted simply by
\mathsf{M}
.
If every element of an ordered monoid
(\mathsf{M},\geq)
is invertible, then we call it a
ordered group
.
A monoid homomorphism
\phi:(\mathsf{M}_{1},\geq_{1})\to(\mathsf{M}_{2},\geq_{2})

between ordered monoids is an
o-homomorphism
if for any
a,b\in\mathsf{M}_{1}
,

a\geq_{1}b\implies\phi(a)\geq_{2}\phi(b).

If an o-homomorphism
\phi
is a bijective map whose inverse map is also an o-homomorphism,
then
\phi
is called an
o-isomorphism
.
If there exists an o-isomorphism between two ordered monoids
\mathsf{M}_{1}
and
\mathsf{M}_{2}
,
then
\mathsf{M}_{1}
and
\mathsf{M}_{2}
are said to be
o-isomorphic
.

The order on
\mathsf{M}
is said to be
trivial
if no two distinct elements of
\mathsf{M}
are comparable.
We often regard any monoid as an ordered monoid with the trivial order.

For an ordered monoid
(\mathsf{M},\geq)
,
every submonoid
\mathsf{M}^{\prime}
of
\mathsf{M}
inherits the induced order,
also denoted by
\geq
.
Unless otherwise stated,
we equip a submonoid of an ordered monoid
\mathsf{M}
,
such as the kernel or image of an o-homomorphism, with the order induced from
\mathsf{M}
.

We next define the
localization
of an ordered monoid,
in particular, the
group completion
of an ordered monoid.
Let
\mathsf{M}
be an ordered monoid and
T
be a multiplicatively closed subset of
\mathsf{M}
.
Recall that the
localization
of
\mathsf{M}
by
T
is a monoid
T^{-1}\mathsf{M}=(\mathsf{M}\times T)/\sim

where the relation
\sim
is given as follows:

(a,b)\sim(c,d)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{\iff}\exists t\in T,\ a+d+t=b+c+t.

We often denote by
a-b
the equivalent class of
(a,b)
.
Now we also endow
T^{-1}\mathsf{M}
with the order induced by
\mathsf{M}
:

a-b\geq c-d\overset{\mathrm{def}}{\iff}\exists t\in T,\ a+d+t\geq b+c+t.

Note that the natural homomorphism
\mathsf{M}\to T^{-1}\mathsf{M}
is an o-homomorphism.
In particular, we denote
\mathsf{M}^{-1}\mathsf{M}
by
\mathsf{M}^{\mathrm{gp}}
, called the
group completion
of
\mathsf{M}
.

Before giving examples, we introduce some terminology.
Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be formal variables.
Throughout this paper, we use the following notation.

(8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&M_{\geq 0}=\{u_{1}X_{1}+u_{2}X_{2}+\dots+u_{n}X_{n}\mid u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\},\\
&M=\{u_{1}X_{1}+u_{2}X_{2}+\dots+u_{n}X_{n}\mid u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\},\\
&\mathbb{R}\times M_{\geq 0}=\{(r,u_{1}X_{1}+u_{2}X_{2}+\dots+u_{n}X_{n})\mid r\in\mathbb{R},\ u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\},\\
&\mathbb{R}\times M=\{(r,u_{1}X_{1}+u_{2}X_{2}+\dots+u_{n}X_{n})\mid r\in\mathbb{R},\ u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\}.\end{split}

By abuse of notation, we often write
u_{1}X_{1}+\dots+u_{n}X_{n}
as
u\cdot X
or
X^{u}
.
Similarly, we write
(r,u_{1}X_{1}+\dots+u_{n}X_{n})
as
r+u\cdot X
or
rX^{u}
.
While we equip
M_{\geq 0}
and
M
with the trivial order,
we define orders on
\mathbb{R}\times M_{\geq 0}
and
\mathbb{R}\times M
as follows:

\displaystyle rX^{u}\geq r^{\prime}X^{u^{\prime}}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{\iff}r\geq r^{\prime}\text{ and }u=u^{\prime}.

These ordered monoids serve as the guiding examples throughout the paper.

Example 3.1
.

A real-valued function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
integral affine

if it has the form

f(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=c+u_{1}X_{1}+\dots+u_{n}X_{n}

where
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then
\mathbb{R}\times M
can be seen as the set of all integral affine functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We endow
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the Euclidean topology.
We say that a real-valued function
f
on an open subset
U
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is

locally integral affine

if for each point
x\in U
, there exists an open neighborhood
U_{x}\subseteq U
of
x

and an integral affine function
f_{x}
such that
f_{x}|_{U_{x}}=f|_{U_{x}}
.

We will denote by
\operatorname{Aff}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
the sheaf of locally integral affine functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
As a sheaf of groups, any section of the sheaf is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times M

and any restriction map is an isomorphism.
However, the sheaf
\operatorname{Aff}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
becomes nontrivial when regarded as a
sheaf of ordered groups

by equipping each section
\operatorname{Aff}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(U)
with the natural order: for
f,g\in\operatorname{Aff}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(U)
,

f\geq g\overset{\mathrm{def}}{\iff}f(x)\geq g(x)\text{ for all }x\in U.

In particular, the ordered group
\operatorname{Aff}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is o-isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times M

in the sense of
eq.

8
.

Example 3.2
.

For
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
define a subspace
H_{x}
of
\mathbb{R}\times M
by

H_{x}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}\{(-x_{1},X_{1}),\dots,(-x_{n},X_{n})\}.

Then
H_{x}
defines the surjective homomorphism

\pi_{x}:\mathbb{R}\times M\to(\mathbb{R}\times M)/H_{x}\cong\mathbb{R}
.
This map can be seen as the map that substitutes
X_{i}=x_{i}
for each
i
.

Now we define a partial order
\geq_{x}
on
\mathbb{R}\times M
.
For
f,g\in\mathbb{R}\times M
,

f\geq g\overset{\mathrm{def}}{\iff}\pi_{x}(f)>\pi_{x}(g)\text{ or }f=g

Then the ordered group
(\mathbb{R}\times M,\geq_{x})
is o-isomorphic to the stalk
\operatorname{Aff}_{\mathbb{R}^{n},x}
at
x
.

3.2.
\mathbb{B}
-algebras associated to ordered monoids

We work under the setting of
section

2.2
:
an additively idempotent semiring is called a
\mathbb{B}
-algebra and
its operations are denoted by
\oplus
and
\odot
.
By the following definition, we construct a
\mathbb{B}
-algebra from an ordered monoid
\mathsf{M}=(\mathsf{M},+)
,
identifying the monoid operation
+
with the product
\odot
of the
\mathbb{B}
-algebra.

Definition 3.3
.

For an ordered monoid
\mathsf{M}=(\mathsf{M},+,\geq)
,
we consider the following set:

\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]\coloneqq\{A\mid\text{$A$ is a finite subset of }\mathsf{M}\}/\sim,

where the relation
\sim
is given as follows:

A_{1}\sim A_{2}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{\iff}\text{the set of all maximal elements in $A_{1}$ is equal to that of $A_{2}$}.

We denote the equivalence class of
A
by
\sum_{m\in A}m
.
If
A=\{m_{0}\}
is a singleton, then we simply denote
\sum_{m\in A}m
by
m_{0}
.
One checks that the following operations are well-defined:

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in A_{1}}m\oplus\sum_{m\in A_{2}}m

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in A_{1}\cup A_{2}}m,

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in A_{1}}m\odot\sum_{m\in A_{2}}m

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{1}\in A_{1},m_{2}\in A_{2}}(m_{1}+m_{2}).

Then
\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]
is a semiring
with additive identity
-\infty\coloneqq\sum_{m\in\emptyset}m
and multiplicative identity
0=\sum_{m\in\{0\}}m
.

For an element
f=\sum_{m\in A}m\in\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]
,
the set of all maximal elements of
A
is called the
support
of
f
, denoted
\supp(f)
.
Then
\supp(f)
is uniquely determined, and two distinct elements of
\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]
have distinct supports.
In other words,
\supp
gives the following bijection:

(9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]\xrightarrow{1:1}\{A\subseteq\mathsf{M}\mid A\text{ is finite and any two elements in $A$ are not comparable}\},\,f\mapsto\supp(f).

In particular, there is an injective map
\mathsf{M}\to\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]
.
So we often treat
\mathsf{M}
as a submonoid of
\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]
.
Recall that a
\mathbb{B}
-algebra has a natural partial order (see (
6
)).
Note that the original order of
\mathsf{M}
and the order induced by that of
\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]
are equivalent.
We also note that any o-homomorphism
\mathsf{M}_{1}\to\mathsf{M}_{2}
induces a semiring homomorphism
\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}_{1}]\to\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}_{2}]
.

Example 3.4
.

Here we use the notation of (
8
).
For example, we consider the
\mathbb{B}
-algebra associated to
\mathbb{R}\times M
.
A map

\displaystyle\{A\mid\text{$A$ is a finite subset of }\mathsf{M}\}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}],

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{(r,u)\in A}r\odot X_{1}^{\odot u_{1}}\odot\dots\odot X_{n}^{\odot u_{n}}=\sum_{(r,u)\in A}(r+u_{1}X_{1}+\dots+u_{n}X_{n})

induces an isomorphism
\mathbb{B}[\mathbb{R}\times M]\cong\mathbb{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
by
proposition

2.1
.
Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}[M]\cong\mathbb{B}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}[M_{\geq 0}]\cong\mathbb{B}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}[\mathbb{R}\times M_{\geq 0}]\cong\mathbb{T}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]

In this way, we can treat some semirings arising in tropical geometry uniformly.

Recall that
\operatorname{Aff}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
in
example

3.1
is a sheaf of ordered groups.
Each restriction map
\operatorname{Aff}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\operatorname{Aff}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(U)

induces
\mathbb{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]\to\mathbb{B}[\operatorname{Aff}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(U)]
and a congruence on
\mathbb{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
.
This demonstrates that
\mathbb{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
admits many congruences.

Example 3.5
.

In
example

3.2
, for
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have seen two o-homomorphisms:

H_{x}\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times M\to\operatorname{Aff}_{\mathbb{R}^{n},x}.

These induce

\mathbb{B}[Z_{1}^{\pm},\dots,Z_{n}^{\pm}]\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathbb{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathbb{B}[\operatorname{Aff}_{\mathbb{R}^{n},x}],

where each
Z_{i}
is a formal variable that maps to
-x_{i}+X_{i}
by
\iota
.
Note that the image of
\iota
is the set of all integral affine functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

which take the value 0 at
x
, except for the constant function
-\infty
.

For an ideal
I
of
\mathbb{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
, let
I^{\prime}
be the ideal of
\mathbb{B}[\operatorname{Aff}_{\mathbb{R}^{n},x}]

generated by the image
\pi(I)
and

I^{\prime\prime}
be the pull-back
(\iota\circ\pi)^{-1}(I^{\prime})\subseteq\mathbb{B}[Z_{1}^{\pm},\dots,Z_{n}^{\pm}]
of

.
Then

\displaystyle I^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{u\colon a_{u}x^{u}=f(x)}a_{u}X^{u}\ \middle|\ f(X)=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{u}X^{u}\in I\right\}

\displaystyle I^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{u\colon a_{u}x^{u}=0}a_{u}X^{u}\ \middle|\ f(X)=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{u}X^{u}\in I,\ f(x)=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{u\colon a_{u}x^{u}=f(x)}Z^{u}\ \middle|\ f(X)=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{u}X^{u}\in I\right\}.

In
[

]
and related works,
the ideal

is called the
initial ideal
of

associated to

.

We next introduce a basic invariant of prime congruences on
\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]
,
which we call
the mobile face
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\mathsf{M}
be an ordered monoid.
A submonoid
\mathsf{F}\subseteq\mathsf{M}
is a
face

if it satisfies the following conditions: for any
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathsf{M}
,

•

If
m_{1}+m_{2}\in\mathsf{F}
, then
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathsf{F}
.

•

If
m_{1}\in\mathsf{F}
and
m_{1}\leq m_{2}
, then
m_{2}\in\mathsf{F}
.

For a face
\mathsf{F}\subseteq\mathsf{M}
, we denote by
\Gamma_{\mathsf{F}}

the congruence generated by
\{(m,-\infty)\mid m\in\mathsf{M}\setminus\mathsf{F}\}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathsf{F}
be a face of
\mathsf{M}
. Then a subset

I_{\mathsf{F}}=\left\{\sum_{m\in S}m\;\middle|\text{ $S\subseteq\mathsf{M}\setminus\mathsf{F}$ is a finite subset}\right\}

is an ideal of
\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]
.
Furthermore, the congruence
\Gamma_{\mathsf{F}}
is equal to

C_{0}=\left\{(i_{1}\oplus f,i_{2}\oplus f)\;\middle|\;i_{1},i_{2}\in I_{\mathsf{F}},f\in\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{F}]\right\}.

Proof.

By the definition of faces, it is immediate that the subset
I_{\mathsf{F}}
is an ideal.
Since
\{(m,-\infty)\mid m\in\mathsf{M}\setminus\mathsf{F}\}\subseteq C_{0}\subseteq\Gamma_{\mathsf{F}}
,
it is enough to show that
C_{0}
is actually a congruence.
Since
I_{\mathsf{F}}
is an ideal,
C_{0}
is a subsemiring of
\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]^{2}
.
By the second condition of
definition

3.6
, we have

C_{0}=\{(g,h)\mid\supp(g)\cap\mathsf{F}=\supp(h)\cap\mathsf{F}\},

which implies
C_{0}
is an equivalence relation.
Thus
C_{0}
is a congruence and
\Gamma_{\mathsf{F}}
is equal to
C_{0}
.
∎

Thus, if a face
\mathsf{F}\subseteq\mathsf{M}
is given,
then we obtain a closed subset
V(\Gamma_{\mathsf{F}})
of
\operatorname{CSpec}\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]
.
Note that for faces
\mathsf{F}_{1},\mathsf{F}_{2}
of
\mathsf{M}
, the following are equivalent:

•

\mathsf{F}_{1}\subseteq\mathsf{F}_{2}
,

•

\Gamma_{\mathsf{F}_{2}}\subseteq\Gamma_{\mathsf{F}_{1}}
,

•

V(\Gamma_{\mathsf{F}_{1}})\subseteq V(\Gamma_{\mathsf{F}_{2}})
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
C
be a congruence on
\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]
and
\pi_{C}
be the quotient map.
Then

\mathsf{F}(C)=\{m\in\mathsf{M}\mid\pi_{C}(m)\neq-\infty\}=\{m\in\mathsf{M}\mid(m,-\infty)\notin C\}

is a face of
\mathsf{M}
.
In particular, for a face
\mathsf{F}
of
\mathsf{M}
, we have
\mathsf{F}(\Gamma_{\mathsf{F}})=\mathsf{F}
.

Proof.

Let
m_{1},m_{2}
be elements of
\mathsf{M}
.
If
m_{1}\notin\mathsf{F}(C)
, then
\pi_{C}(m_{1}+m_{2})=-\infty\odot\pi_{C}(m_{2})=-\infty
.
Thus we have
m_{1}+m_{2}\notin\mathsf{F}(C)
.

Assume that
m_{1}\in\mathsf{F}(C)
and
m_{1}\leq m_{2}
.
If
m_{2}\notin\mathsf{F}(C)
, then
-\infty=\pi_{C}(m_{2})\geq\pi_{C}(m_{1})\geq-\infty
,
which contradicts the assumption that

3.3.

We define

Definition 3.13

For an ordered monoid

An o-homomorphism

\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{B}[\mathbb{R}\times\mathsf{M}]

Faces of

Lemma 3.14
.

For any face
\mathsf{F}
of an ordered monoid
\mathbb{R}\times\mathsf{M}
,

\mathsf{F}^{\prime}
of

\mathsf{F}=\mathbb{R}\times\mathsf{F}^{\prime}

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{F}^{\prime}
be the image of

by the projection
\mathbb{R}\times\mathsf{M}\to\mathsf{M}
.
It is easy to check that
\mathsf{F}^{\prime}
is a face of
\mathsf{M}
.
Since
(r,0)+(-r,0)=(0,0)\in\mathsf{F}
holds for any

\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}\subseteq\mathsf{F}

By the definition of faces, for any

and

(r,m)\in\mathsf{F}

∎

Proposition 3.15
.

For an ordered monoid
\mathsf{M}

the complement of an open subset

\operatorname{CSpec}\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]

Proof.

Let
\iota\colon\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}[\mathsf{M}]
be the
\mathbb{T}

the closed point.
The complement of

The projection

Lemma 3.16
.

Assume that the free monoid
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{k}
is equipped with the trivial order.
Then the map

\iota:\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{k},\mathbb{T})\to\operatorname{CSpec}_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{T}[\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{k}]

) is a topological embedding onto the subset
\operatorname{CSpec}^{\mathrm{geo}}\mathbb{T}[\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{k}]
.

Proof.

By
[
Rab12
, Proposition 3.4]
,
the natural bijection
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{k},\mathbb{T})\to\mathbb{T}^{k}
is a homeomorphism.
Thus it is enough to show that the Euclidean topology on
\mathbb{T}^{k}

is equal to the topology on
\mathbb{T}^{k}
whose closed sets are of the form

V_{\mathbb{T}^{k}}(C)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{k}\mid f(x)=g(x)\text{ for any }(f,g)\in C\}

as
C
runs over the set of all congruences on
\mathbb{T}[X_{1},\dots,X_{k}]
.
Since it is immediate that

is a closed subset under the Euclidean topology,
it suffices to show that
any open subset of
\mathbb{T}^{k}
under the Euclidean topology is the complement of

for some congruence

.

The Euclidean topology on
\mathbb{T}^{k}
is generated by the subbasis consisting of open subsets of the form

U_{i,a}^{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{k}\mid a<x_{i}\},\ U_{i,a}^{-}=\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{k}\mid x_{i}<a\},

and

∎

Prime congruences and submonoids

The aim of this section is to understand
\operatorname{CSpec}\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]
as a poset.
We mainly use
proposition

2.14
.
In
section

, we consider the problem in the case where
\mathsf{M}=G

In

4.3

4.1.
Convex congruences

In this and the following subsection, we assume that
\mathsf{M}=G
is an ordered group.
We first give some observations.
Let
C
be a congruence on
\mathbb{B}[G]
and
\pi_{C}:\mathbb{B}[G]\to\mathbb{B}[G]/C
be the quotient homomorphism.
Then

(12)

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}(C)\coloneqq\{m\in G\mid\pi_{C}(m)\geq 0\}

is a submonoid of
G
.
Since
\pi_{C}|_{G}:G\to\pi_{C}(G)
is an o-homomorphism,
the submonoid
\mathsf{L}=\mathsf{L}(C)
satisfies the following conditions.

(L1)

\displaystyle\{m\in\mathsf{M}\mid m\geq 0\}\subseteq\mathsf{L}.

Conversely, for an arbitrary subset
A
of
G
,
we can define a congruence
\langle A\geq 0\rangle
on
\mathbb{B}[G]
as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

For a subset
A
of
G
,
we define a congruence
\langle A\geq 0\rangle
on
\mathbb{B}[G]
as the congruence generated
by
\{(m\oplus 0,m)\mid m\in A\}
, i.e.,

\langle A\geq 0\rangle\coloneqq\langle\{(m\oplus 0,m)\mid m\in A\}\rangle.

A
convex congruence
is a congruence of the form
\langle A\geq 0\rangle
for a subset
A
of
G
.

For a subset
A
of
G
, let
\mathsf{L}
be a submonoid generated by

A
and
\{m\in G\mid m\geq 0\}
.
Then we have

(13)

\displaystyle\langle A\geq 0\rangle=\langle\mathsf{L}\geq 0\rangle.

Furthermore, for a family
\{A_{i}\}_{i}
of subsets of
G
, we have

(14)

\displaystyle\left\langle\bigcup_{i}A_{i}\geq 0\right\rangle=\sum_{i}\langle A_{i}\geq 0\rangle,

where
\sum_{i}\langle A_{i}\geq 0\rangle
is the congruence generated by
\bigcup_{i}\langle A_{i}\geq 0\rangle
.

On the other hand, for an arbitrary submonoid
\mathsf{L}
of
G
satisfying (
L1
),
We define an order of
G/(\mathsf{L}\cap-\mathsf{L})
as follows:

(15)

\displaystyle\overline{g_{1}}\geq\overline{g_{2}}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{\iff}g_{1}-g_{2}\in\mathsf{L},

where
\overline{g_{i}}
is the image of
g_{i}\in G
in
G/(\mathsf{L}\cap-\mathsf{L})
.
Then
G\to G/(\mathsf{L}\cap-\mathsf{L})
is an o-homomorphism and induces the homomorphism

(16)

\displaystyle\pi:\mathbb{B}[G]\to\mathbb{B}[G/(\mathsf{L}\cap-\mathsf{L})].

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
A
be a subset of
G
.
Then
\langle A\geq 0\rangle
is equal to the kernel of the homomorphism
\pi
defined in (
16
)
where
\mathsf{L}
is the submonoid generated by
A
and
\{m\in G\mid m\geq 0\}
.

Proof.

By (
13
), we may assume
A=\mathsf{L}
.
An inclusion
\langle\mathsf{L}\geq 0\rangle\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}\pi
is immediate from the definitions.
To show the opposite inclusion, we take an arbitrary element
(f_{1},f_{2})\in\operatorname{Ker}\pi
.
Define a subset

\displaystyle A_{(f_{1},f_{2})}\coloneqq\{m_{+}-m_{-}\mid m_{+},m_{-}\in\supp(f_{1})\cup\supp(f_{2})\text{ such that }\pi(m_{+})\geq\pi(m_{-})\}.

Since the o-homomorphism
G\to G/(\mathsf{L}\cap-\mathsf{L})
is a restriction of
\pi
,

A_{(f_{1},f_{2})}
is a subset of
\mathsf{L}
.
Hence we have
(f_{1},f_{2})\in\langle A_{(f_{1},f_{2})}\geq 0\rangle\subseteq\langle\mathsf{L}\geq 0\rangle
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For a submonoid
\mathsf{L}
of
G
satisfying (
L1
), we have
\mathsf{L}(\langle\mathsf{L}\geq 0\rangle)=\mathsf{L}
.
In particular, the map
\mathsf{L}\mapsto\langle\mathsf{L}\geq 0\rangle
is injective.

Proof.

We have
\mathsf{L}\subseteq\mathsf{L}(\langle\mathsf{L}\geq 0\rangle)
from the definition of
\langle\mathsf{L}\geq 0\rangle
.
To show the opposite inclusion, we take an arbitrary element
m\in\mathsf{L}(\langle\mathsf{L}\geq 0\rangle)
.
Then we have
\pi(m)\geq 0
in
\mathbb{B}[G/(\mathsf{L}\cap-\mathsf{L})]
.
By the definition of the order of
G/(\mathsf{L}\cap-\mathsf{L})
, we have
m\in\mathsf{L}
.
∎

Thus two maps
C\mapsto\mathsf{L}(C)
and
\mathsf{L}\mapsto\langle\mathsf{L}\geq 0\rangle
give a one-to-one correspondence:

(17)

\displaystyle\{C\mid C\text{ is a convex congruence on }\mathbb{B}[G]\}\leftrightarrow\{L\mid L\text{ is a submonoid of }G\text{ satisfying }\eqref{L1}\}.

Note that the correspondence preserves inclusions.

The following claim is crucial in
section

7
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
C
be a convex congruence on
\mathbb{B}[G]
.
Then the following are equivalent:

•

C
is finitely generated as a congruence,

•

there exists a finite subset
A
of
G
such that
the corresponding submonoid
\mathsf{L}(C)
is generated by
A
and
\{m\in G\mid m\geq 0\}
.

Proof.

By (
13
), it is immediate that the latter implies the former.

Assume that
C
is generated by a finite set
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{B}[G]^{2}
.
As in the proof of
lemma

4.2
, we define a finite subset of
G
by

A_{(f_{1},f_{2})}\coloneqq\{g_{+}-g_{-}\in G\mid g_{+},g_{-}\in\supp(f_{1})\cup\supp(f_{2}),\ \overline{g_{+}}\geq\overline{g_{-}}\text{ in }G/(\mathsf{L}(C)\cap-\mathsf{L}(C))\},

for a pair
(f_{1},f_{2})
.
Since
\langle A_{(f_{1},f_{2})}\geq 0\rangle
contains a pair
(f_{1},f_{2})
,
a finite subset
A\coloneqq\bigcup_{(f_{1},f_{2})\in\mathcal{A}}A_{(f_{1},f_{2})}
of
G
satisfies

\langle A\geq 0\rangle=\sum_{(f_{1},f_{2})\in\mathcal{A}}\langle A_{(f_{1},f_{2})}\geq 0\rangle\supseteq C.

The opposite inclusion is clear from the definition of
A
. Thus we have
C=\langle A\geq 0\rangle
.
By (
13
), the claim holds.
∎

Now we consider the case of prime congruences.

Lemma 4.5

A congruence

Proof.

See

Lemma 4.6
.

Prime congruences on
\mathbb{B}[G]
are convex.

Proof.

Let
P
be a prime congruence on
\mathbb{B}[G]
and
\pi_{P}:\mathbb{B}[G]\to\mathbb{B}[G]/P
be the quotient homomorphism.
By
lemma

3.12
, there is a natural isomorphism
\mathbb{B}[G]/P\cong\mathbb{B}[\pi_{P}(G)]
.

As we did in (

), we can define a submonoid
\mathsf{L}(P)
of
G
by

\mathsf{L}(P)=\{m\in G\mid\pi_{P}(m)\geq 0\}.

There is an isomorphism of groups:

(18)

\displaystyle G/(\mathsf{L}(P)\cap-\mathsf{L}(P))\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{P}(G),\,\overline{g}\mapsto\pi_{P}(g).

Since the order of
G/(\mathsf{L}(P)\cap-\mathsf{L}(P))
is defined by (

),

\overline{g_{1}}\geq\overline{g_{2}}\iff g_{1}-g_{2}\in\mathsf{L}(P)\iff\pi_{P}(g_{1}-g_{2})\geq 0\iff\pi_{P}(g_{1})\geq\pi_{P}(g_{2})

holds. Thus (

) is an o-isomorphism.
By
lemma

4.2
, we have
P=\langle\mathsf{L}(P)\geq 0\rangle
.
∎

If
P
is a prime congruence on
\mathbb{B}[G]
, then
\mathsf{L}(P)
satisfies not only (

)
but also the following condition:

(L2)

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}\cup-\mathsf{L}=G.

We denote by
\mathcal{L}_{G}
the set of all submonoids of

satisfying (
L1
) and (

).

Conversely, if a submonoid
\mathsf{L}
of
G
satisfies (
L1
) and (
L2
),
then the congruence
\langle\mathsf{L}\geq 0\rangle
is prime by
lemma

4.5
.
Thus we obtain a one-to-one correspondence as a restriction of (
17
).

Proposition 4.7
.

The one-to-one correspondence of (
17
) induces a one-to-one correspondence
between
\operatorname{CSpec}\mathbb{B}[G]
and
\mathcal{L}_{G}
.
Furthermore, it preserves inclusions.

As illustrated in
figs.

1
and
3
, for a typical ordered group,

) and (

) necessarily have a very restricted form.
See
section

5
for the details.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{B}[G_{1}]\to\mathbb{B}[G_{2}]
be the semiring homomorphism induced by an o-homomorphism
\psi:G_{1}\to G_{2}
.
For
P_{2}\in\operatorname{CSpec}\mathbb{B}[G_{2}]

\mathsf{L}(\phi^{*}P_{2})=\psi^{-1}(\mathsf{L}(P_{2}))
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle g\in\mathsf{L}(\phi^{*}P_{2})

\displaystyle\iff(g\oplus 0,g)\in\phi^{*}P_{2}

\displaystyle\iff(\phi(g)\oplus 0,\phi(g))=\phi((g\oplus 0,g))\in P_{2}

\displaystyle\iff\phi(g)\in\mathsf{L}(P_{2})

\displaystyle\iff g\in\phi^{-1}(\mathsf{L}(P_{2}))\cap G_{1}=\psi^{-1}(\mathsf{L}(P_{2})).

Thus the equality holds.

In this section, we construct tropical toric schemes using sets of prime congruences.

First we recall the usual setting in toric geometry.

A

&N^{\operatorname{cong}}(\tau)\xrightarrow{\cong}\operatorname{CSpec}_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{T}[\tau^{\perp}\cap M],\ P\mapsto\tilde{P}.\end{split}

Thus we obtain a stratification of
X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
analogous to that of
X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{trop}}
.
By (

),
X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
can also be seen as a set of flags.

Remark 6.8
.

In (
34
),
one should be careful that different points may corresponds to the same flags.
Indeed, for any maximal cone
\tau^{\prime}
,
the correspondence of (

) maps points on
N^{\operatorname{cong}}(\tau^{\prime})
to
\operatorname{CSpec}_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{T}
.
To avoid this ambiguity, we should record from which stratum each flag is mapped.
More precisely, for a point

on

, the corresponding objects must be a pair
(\tau,\tilde{P})
and a triple

(\tau,H(\tilde{P})^{\mathrm{out}}_{\bullet},H(\tilde{P})^{\mathrm{inn}}_{\bullet})
.
In this way, the correspondence (
34
) remains bijective,
although we often omit this additional data when it is clear from the context.

Recall the map (
11

\iota_{\sigma}\colon\operatorname{Hom}(\mathsf{M}_{\sigma},\mathbb{T})\to\operatorname{CSpec}_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{T}[\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}].

By

and

6.4
,
the image of
\iota_{\sigma}
is the set of points of height

.
The morphisms
\{\iota_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
glue together
to give an injective map
\iota:X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{trop}}\to X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
.
Recalling
definition

2.5

\iota
satisfies the following theorem.

Theorem 6.9
.

The inclusion
\iota\colon X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{trop}}\to X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
induces
a homeomorphism

X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{trop}}\cong\{P\in X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}\mid\dim_{P}X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}=n\}.

In what follows, we regard
X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{trop}}
as a subspace of
X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
.
A point
P
of
X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
is called a
closed point

if a singleton
\{P\}
is a closed subset.
Since
\dim X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
is equal to
n
, any point
P
in
X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{trop}}
is closed.
However, a closed point is not of height
n
in general.
Indeed, in
example

5.25
, a point

is closed in
\operatorname{CSpec}_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{T}[X^{\pm}]

Next, we examine when the two notions coincide, that is, when every closed point is of height
n
.

Lemma 6.10
.

For
i=1,2
, let
P_{i}
be a point in
N^{\operatorname{cong}}(\tau_{i})
.
If
P_{2}\in\overline{\{P_{1}\}}
, then
\tau_{1}
is a face of
\tau_{2}
.

Proof.

Assume
P_{2}\in\overline{\{P_{1}\}}
.
By the construction of
X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
, there exists a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
such that

P_{2}\in U_{\sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
. Since
U_{\sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
is an open subset of
X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}

and
P_{2}\in\overline{\{P_{1}\}}
,
then
P_{1}\in U_{\sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
.
For each
i=1,2
,
\mathbb{R}\times(\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}\cap\tau_{i}^{\perp})
be the mobile face
of
P_{i}
in
U_{\sigma}
.
By
P_{1}\subseteq P_{2}
and
proposition

4.20
,
we have
\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}\cap\tau_{2}^{\perp}\subseteq\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}\cap\tau_{1}^{\perp}
.
Thus
\tau_{1}\prec\tau_{2}
holds.
∎

For
m\in M
, we define
m^{\perp}=\{n\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\langle m,n\rangle=0\}
.

Lemma 6.11
.

[
CLS24
, Lemma 1.2.13]

Let
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
be cones in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then

\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2}=m^{\perp}\cap\sigma_{1}=m^{\perp}\cap\sigma_{2}

for any
m\in\operatorname{relint}(\sigma_{1}^{\vee}\cap-\sigma_{2}^{\vee})
.

Theorem 6.12
.

Let
\Sigma
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and
P
an arbitrary point in
X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
.
Then there is at most one point
P_{0}
in
X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{trop}}
contained
in
\overline{\{P\}}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\overline{\{P\}}\cap X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{trop}}
contains two points
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
.
Let
\tau,\tau_{0},
and
\tau_{1}
be the cones of
\Sigma
giving the strata containing these points:

P\in N^{\operatorname{cong}}(\tau),\ P_{0}\in N^{\operatorname{cong}}(\tau_{0}),\ P_{1}\in N^{\operatorname{cong}}(\tau_{1}).

By
lemma

6.10
, we have
\tau
is a face of
\tau_{0}
and
\tau_{1}
.
By
lemma

6.11
, for any
m\in\operatorname{relint}(\tau_{0}^{\vee}\cap-\tau_{1}^{\vee})
,

(35)

\displaystyle\tau_{0}\cap\tau_{1}=\tau_{0}\cap m^{\perp}=\tau_{1}\cap m^{\perp}

holds. Now we fix such
m
.

To derive a contradiction, we assume
\tau_{0}\neq\tau_{1}
.
From (
35
), we have
m\neq 0
.
In the chart
U_{\tau_{0}}=\operatorname{CSpec}_{\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{T}[\mathsf{M}_{\tau_{0}}]
,
P_{i}
is a point of
mobile face
\mathsf{F}_{i}=\mathsf{M}_{\tau_{0}}\cap\tau_{i}^{\perp}
for
i=0,1
.
Since
m\in\operatorname{relint}(\tau_{0}^{\vee}\cap-\tau_{1}^{\vee})
holds, we have
m\in\mathsf{F}_{0}\setminus\mathsf{F}_{1}
.
By the condition (ii) of
proposition

4.20
and
P\subseteq P_{0}
,
we have
m\notin\mathsf{L}(\tilde{P})
.
By applying the same argument to
U_{\tau_{1}}
, we have
-m\notin\mathsf{L}(\tilde{P})
.
This contradicts the condition (
L2
).
Thus we have
\tau_{0}=\tau_{1}
and hence
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
lie in the same chart
U_{\tau_{0}}=U_{\tau_{1}}
.
By
proposition

2.21
, we have
P_{0}=P_{1}
.
∎

Theorem 6.13
.

Let
\Sigma
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

for any
P\in X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
, there is a point
P_{0}
in
X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{trop}}
contained
in
\overline{\{P\}}
,

(2)

the fan
\Sigma
is complete.

Proof.

First we show that (2) implies (1).
By
proposition

6.6
, it is enough to show that
for a cone
\tau\in\Sigma
and a
(\tau^{\perp}\cap M)
-flag
H_{\bullet}
of length
l
,
there exists a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
containing
\tau
such that

(36)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\vee}\cap\tau^{\perp}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}-H_{i,+}\cup H_{l}.

We show the claim by induction on the codimension
k
of
\tau
.
If
k
is equal to
0
, then the claim clearly holds.
Assume that the claim holds if the codimension is smaller than
k
.
Note that the pairing
M\times N\to\mathbb{Z}
induces
a pairing
(\tau^{\perp}\cap M)\times N/(\mathbb{R}\tau\cap N)\to\mathbb{Z}
.
With this pairing, consider a normal vector
v\in N_{\mathbb{R}}/\mathbb{R}\tau
of
H_{1}
pointing
-H_{1,+}
.
Let
\pi\colon N_{\mathbb{R}}\to N_{\mathbb{R}}/\mathbb{R}\tau
be the projection.
Since the fan

\Sigma/\tau\coloneqq\{\pi(\tau^{\prime})\mid\tau^{\prime}\in\Sigma,\ \tau\subseteq\tau^{\prime}\}

is complete,
there is a unique cone
\tau_{1}
of
\Sigma
satisfying
\tau\prec\tau_{1}
and
v\in\operatorname{relint}\pi(\tau_{1})
.

Using the induction assumption
for a
(\tau_{1}^{\perp}\cap M)
-flag
H_{\bullet}\cap^{*}(\tau_{1}^{\perp}\cap M)
,
we obtain a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
satisfying
\tau_{1}\prec\sigma
and

(37)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\vee}\cap\tau_{1}^{\perp}\subseteq\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}-H_{i,+}\cup H_{l}\right)\cap\tau_{1}^{\perp}.

Since
\tau_{1}\prec\sigma
and
v\in\operatorname{relint}\pi(\tau_{1})
hold, we have

(38)

\displaystyle(\sigma^{\vee}\cap\tau^{\perp})\setminus\tau_{1}^{\perp}\subseteq\tau^{\perp}\cap\tau_{1}^{\vee}\setminus\tau_{1}^{\perp}\subseteq-H_{1,+}.

Combining (
37
) and (
38
), we obtain the desired inclusion (
36
).

Conversely we assume that the fan
\Sigma
is not complete.
Let
\Sigma^{\prime}
be a complete fan which contains
\Sigma
as a subfan.
Then the tropical toric scheme
X_{\Sigma^{\prime}}^{\operatorname{cong}}
contains
X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
as an open subset.

We fix a maximal cone
\tau^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}\setminus\Sigma
.
Since
\tau^{\prime}
is maximal,
\tau^{\prime\vee}
is a strictly convex cone of
M_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Hence we can take a hyperplane
H
of
M_{\mathbb{R}}
and its orientation
H_{+}

satisfying
H\cap\tau^{\prime\vee}=\{0\}
and
H_{+}\cap\tau^{\vee}=\emptyset
.
Define an
M
-flag
H^{\mathrm{out}}_{\bullet}
of length 1 by

\displaystyle H_{1}^{\mathrm{out}}\coloneqq H,

\displaystyle H_{1,+}^{\mathrm{out}}\coloneqq H_{+}.

Let
H_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{inn}}
be an arbitrary
\mathbb{R}\times(H\cap M)
-flag satisfying

\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}\not\subseteq H_{1}^{\mathrm{inn}}
.

By (
25
), we obtain a point
P
of
N(\{0\})\subseteq X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
.
Using
proposition

6.6
in the chart

,
there exists a point

which is contained in

.
As can be seen from the proof of

,

lies on a stratum

,
which is not contained in

.
By

, there is no point of

.
∎

Corollary 6.14
.

Let
\Sigma
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then the subset
X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{trop}}\subseteq X_{\Sigma}^{\operatorname{cong}}
is equal to the set of closed points
if and only if
\Sigma
is complete.

7.
Finitely generated prime congruences

Finally, we consider a purely algebraic problem: determining which prime congruences are finitely generated.
First we refer to a general result.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
S_{1}\to S_{2}
be a surjective homomorphism of semirings and

C
be a finitely generated congruence on
S_{1}
.
Then the push-out
C\cdot S_{2}
is also finitely generated.

Proof.

See
[
BE17
, Corollary 2.15]
.
∎

Let
\mathsf{M}
be an ordered monoid and
P
a point of
\operatorname{CSpec}\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}]
whose mobile face is
\mathsf{F}\subseteq\mathsf{M}
.
Recall the relation of
P
and
\tilde{P}
:

\displaystyle\tilde{P}=(P\cap\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{F}])\cdot\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{F}^{\mathrm{gp}}],

\displaystyle P=\langle(\tilde{P}\cap\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{F}])\cdot\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}],\Gamma_{\mathsf{F}}\rangle.

In general, the congruence
\Gamma_{\mathsf{F}}
is not finitely generated.
However, in the case of
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}
or
\mathbb{R}\times\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}
(see
section

6
),

\Gamma_{\mathsf{F}}
is finitely generated.
Indeed, the quotient of
\mathsf{M}
by any face is a finitely generated monoid.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
P
be a prime congruence on
\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}]
or
\mathbb{T}[\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}]
.
Then
P
is finitely generated if and only if
\tilde{P}
is finitely generated.

Proof.

We only show the assertion for
\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}]
since the other case is similar.
Let
\mathsf{F}
be the mobile face of
P
.
By the isomorphism
\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{F}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}]/\Gamma_{\mathsf{F}}
,

P\cap\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{F}]
is pushed forward to
P\cdot\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{F}^{\mathrm{gp}}]
.
Then by
lemma

7.1
,
if
P
is finitely generated, then
P\cap\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{F}]
and
\tilde{P}
are also finitely generated.

Conversely, assume that
\tilde{P}
is finitely generated.
Since
\Gamma_{\mathsf{F}}
is finitely generated,
it is enough to show that
\tilde{P}\cap\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{F}]
is finitely generated.
By
proposition

4.4
, the submonoid
\mathsf{L}=\mathsf{L}(\tilde{P})
is a finitely generated monoid.
Since
\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}
is torsion-free,
\mathsf{F}
can be seen as a submonoid of
\mathsf{F}^{\mathrm{gp}}
.
Since
\mathsf{F}^{\mathrm{gp}}
is a finitely generated abelian group,
and its submonoids
\mathsf{L}
and
\mathsf{F}
are also finitely generated,

\mathsf{L}\cap\mathsf{F}
and
-\mathsf{L}\cap\mathsf{F}
are also finitely generated.
Assume that these submonoids are generated by

\{m_{1}^{+},\dots,m_{k}^{+}\}
and
\{m_{1}^{-},\dots,m_{l}^{-}\}
, respectively.
We define a congruence
C
on
\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{F}]
generated by

\{(m_{i}^{+}\oplus 0,m_{i}^{+})\mid 1\leq i\leq k\}\cup\{(m_{j}^{-}\oplus 0,0)\mid 1\leq j\leq l\}.

Then
\tilde{P}\cap\mathbb{B}[\mathsf{F}]
is equal to
C
and hence finitely generated.
∎

By
lemma

7.2
, it is enough to consider the case of
\mathbb{B}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
and
\mathbb{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
.

Theorem 7.3
.

For a prime congruence
P
on
\mathbb{B}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

P
is finitely generated,

(2)

\mathsf{L}(P)
is a finitely generated monoid,

(3)

(\operatorname{ht}P,\operatorname{coht}P)
is equal to
(n,0)
or
(n-1,1)
,

(4)

P
is one of the following two: (i) the maximum congruence or (ii)
P
corresponds to an
M
-flag of length 1
where
H_{1}
is a rational hyperplane.

Proof.

The equivalence of (1) and (2) is already proven in
proposition

4.4
.
The equivalence of (3) and (4) is also immediate by
theorem

5.6
.

Then we will show the equivalence of (2) and (4).
Assume the condition (2).
Then
\operatorname{cone}(\mathsf{L}(P))
is a closed cone.
Let
\{H_{i}\}_{i=0}^{l}
be the
M
-flag corresponding to
P
.
If the length
l
is equal to
0
, then
P
is the maximum congruence.
If the length
l
is larger than
0
, by
theorem

5.6
, we have

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{cone}(\mathsf{L}(P))}\cap\overline{-\operatorname{cone}(\mathsf{L}(P))}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{cone}(\mathsf{L}(P))\cap-\operatorname{cone}(\mathsf{L}(P))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{cone}\left(\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}H_{i,+}\cup H_{l}\right)\cap M\right)\cap-\operatorname{cone}\left(\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}H_{i,+}\cup H_{l}\right)\cap M\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{R}(H_{l}\cap M),

which implies that
H_{1}
is rational and the length
l
is equal to
1
.
Thus we have one implication (2)
\implies
(4).

Finally we show the opposite implication.
For (i) the maximum congruence
P_{0}
,
the corresponding submonoid
\mathsf{L}(P_{0})
is equal to
M
, which is finitely generated.
Let
P
be (ii) a prime congruence whose
M
-flag is
M_{\mathbb{R}}=H_{0}\supsetneq H_{1}

where
H_{1}
is a rational hyperplane.
Then we can take a finite subset
\{m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\}
of
M
such that

•

m_{1}
generates
M/(H_{1}\cap M)\cong\mathbb{Z}
as a group, and

•

m_{2},\dots,m_{n}
generate
H_{1}\cap M
,

as groups.
Thus the submonoid
\mathsf{L}(P)
of
M
is generated by
\{m_{1},\pm m_{2},\dots,\pm m_{n}\}
.
∎

The situation is similar in the case of
G=\mathbb{R}\times M
.
Note that in this case,
\{m\in G\mid m\geq 0\}
is equal to
\{(r,0)\mid r\geq 0\}=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\langle(1,0)\rangle
.
Recall that a geometric congruence on
\mathbb{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
is a prime congruence associated to a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 7.4
.

For a prime congruence
P
on
\mathbb{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

P
is finitely generated,

(2)

\mathsf{L}(P)
is a submonoid of
\mathbb{R}\times M
generated by finitely many elements and
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\{0\}
,

(3)

(\operatorname{ht}P,\operatorname{coht}P)
is equal to
(n+1,0)
or
(n,1)
,

(4)

P
is one of the following three: (i) the maximum congruence, (ii) a geometric congruence,
or (iii) a congruence which corresponds to an
(\mathbb{R}\times M)
-flag
H_{\bullet}
of length 1
and
H_{1}=\mathbb{R}\times H
where
H
is a rational hyperplane of
M_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
theorem

7.3
.
∎

Note that congruences in (4) of
theorem

7.4

are not lying over
\mathbb{T}
except for geometric congruences.
Thus the following corollary is immediate.

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
P
be a prime congruence on
\mathbb{T}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{n}^{\pm}]
lying over
\mathbb{T}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

•

P
is finitely generated,

•

(\operatorname{ht}P,\operatorname{coht}P)
is equal to
(n,1)
, and

•

P
is geometric.

The following claim follows from
lemma

7.2
and
corollary

7.5
.

Corollary 7.6
.

Let
P
be a prime congruence on
\mathbb{T}[\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}]
lying over
\mathbb{T}
.
Then
P
is geometric if and only if
P
is finitely generated.

Thus we have obtained another characterization of the subset
U^{\operatorname{trop}}_{\sigma}
of
U^{\operatorname{cong}}_{\sigma}
.
