Early Career: Constraining the Electromagnetic Shower Energy Scale at
 LArTPC Neutrino Detectors Near and Far

 Final Scientific/Technical Report

 Principal Investigator: Prof. Michael Mooney, Associate Professor
 Principal Investigator’s Institution: Colorado State University
 DOE Award Number: DE-SC0021191

 Executive Summary:

The Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment (DUNE) at the Long-Baseline Neutrino Facility is part of a
multi-billion dollar international project that will be the largest particle physics experiment ever built in
North America. The DUNE project will use massive liquid argon time projection chambers (LArTPCs)
to answer fundamental questions about the universe, such as: what is the origin of the matter/antimatter
asymmetry in the universe? In order to answer such questions, DUNE will make measurements that probe
the nature of neutrino oscillation, the changing of neutrino flavor (electron, muon, or tau) during the flight
from source to detector. These measurements will be made with a beam of muon neutrinos originating
at Fermilab, located roughly 1300 km from the LArTPC “far detector” that will be constructed almost a
mile underground in Lead, South Dakota; the neutrino spectrum will also be characterized prior to any
measurable oscillation by a “near detector” that is located close to the beamline at Fermilab.
 While DUNE will start collecting its first neutrino beam events in the early 2030s, an experimental
neutrino program that is currently operating is the Short-Baseline Neutrino (SBN) Program. SBN will
make use of a LArTPC near detector (SBND or Short-Baseline Near Detector) and a LArTPC far detector
(ICARUS, short for Imaging Cosmic And Rare Underground Signals) in order to characterize the potential
anomalous phenomenon of neutrino oscillation over shorter distances that might be indicative of the existence
of one or more “sterile” neutrinos; evidence for such an anomaly has been previously observed at both the
Liquid Scintillator Neutrino Detector (LSND) and at MiniBooNE. Another experiment, MicroBooNE, which
operated in neutrino beam at Fermilab between 2015 and 2021, has already produced preliminary results
probing this anomaly in addition to enabling LArTPC R&D relevant for the DUNE physics program.
 Both DUNE and the SBN Program will utilize the near detector in estimating the neutrino spectrum
prior to oscillation, while the far detector(s) will characterize the oscillated neutrino spectrum; a multi-prong
measurement of this nature (with identical target material, liquid argon) leads to the minimization of cross
section and neutrino flux systematic uncertainties, which is key to the success of the physics programs of both
experiments. However, detector effects, such as attachment of drifting ionization electrons to electronegative
impurities in the argon and prompt electron-ion recombination after initial ionization of the liquid argon,
may interfere with the reconstruction of the energy of the various particle species in final states of interest.
A failure to pin down these detector effects (separately in each detector) could result in a significant smear-
ing and/or bias in reconstructed particle energy distributions, which would negatively impact the physics
programs of both DUNE and SBN. Of particular interest is constraining the energy scale of electromagnetic
showers in these experiments as they are the principal signals of interest, produced by electron neutrino
interactions in the detectors.
 The research enabled by this award addresses the data-driven constraint of the electromagnetic shower
energy scale in LArTPC neutrino detectors such as those used at DUNE and the SBN Program, benefiting fu-
ture neutrino oscillation physics measurements. The four primary thrusts of this research are (1) development
of calibration strategies for a variety of low-level detector effects that impacts the electromagnetic shower
energy scale, (2) use of reconstructed neutral pion decays to two photons in order to establish a high-level
calibration of the electromagnetic shower energy scale, (3) performing neutrino-argon cross section measure-
ments and neutrino oscillation physics measurements involving electromagnetic showers at MicroBooNE and
SBN, and (4) hardware R&D relevant for LArTPC detector calibrations at DUNE.

1 Project Goals
This original goals of this project include five different research topics that are closely connected:
 • LArTPC detector calibration for the DUNE detector prototypes and SBN Program detectors, including
 both calibrations using cosmic muon tracks and those employing low-energy radiological decays;
 • utilizing π 0 → γγ decays in the SBN Program detectors and DUNE detector prototypes in order to
 constrain the electromagnetic shower energy scale, for eventual use in neutrino oscillation measurements
 using electron neutrinos;
 • participating in the MicroBooNE low-energy excess analysis probing the potential existence of a sterile
 neutrino, specifically focusing on electron neutrino interactions without pions in the final state;
 • carrying out the sterile neutrino analysis at the SBN Program through an electron neutrino appearance
 measurement at ICARUS and intrinsic electron neutrino beam flux constraint at SBND; and
 • hardware R&D and leading of TPC module assembly and testing for the LArTPC component of the
 DUNE ND (ND-LAr), including prototype tests at the University of Bern and Fermilab.
These five research topics are synergistic in that they are all relevant for the analysis of electron neutrino
events at SBN and DUNE. The first two items are directly relevant for constraining the energy scale of elec-
tron neutrino events by focusing on the difficult problem of electromagnetic shower reconstruction, including
understanding low-level detector effects that impact the electromagnetic shower energy scale; the third and
fourth items are the flagship analyses at MicroBooNE and SBN that directly involve the reconstruction of
electron neutrino events. The last item, DUNE ND hardware R&D and TPC module assembly and testing,
is also relevant as part of this effort includes calibration of the developmental pixel-based charge readout that
will have implications on reconstructed particle energy scale, including for electron neutrino measurements
at the DUNE ND (necessary for intrinsic electron neutrino beam flux constraints for the DUNE accelerator
neutrino physics program).
 The above goals have largely been met through the research Mooney’s research group carried out during
the award period. Specific research accomplishments by Mooney’s research group are described in Section 2
for MicroBooNE, in Section 3 for the SBN Program, and in Section 4 for DUNE. Products associated with
these research activities are listed in Section 5, including published research papers as well as publications
that are currently in progress at the time of writing this report. While carrying out the project, some modest
changes to the project goals were needed, which are described below.
 • Given that machine-learning-based reconstruction tools were better developed at the SBN Program
 than DUNE, Mooney’s research group focused on using ICARUS and SBND data for studying con-
 straints of the electromagnetic energy scale using π 0 → γγ decays. These studies will be replicated for
 DUNE in the near future, benefiting from this formative work at the SBN Program.
 • Due to delays in the start of ICARUS and SBND operations that were a result of the COVID-19 pan-
 demic, the SBN Program neutrino oscillation physics program was pushed back, preventing substantial
 progress on an electron neutrino appearance measurement using data collected by both ICARUS and
 SBND. Instead, Mooney’s research group focused on carrying out neutrino-argon cross section mea-
 surements with electromagnetic showers in the final state at ICARUS, featuring neutrino selections
 that will be inputs to future SBN neutrino oscillation physics measurements.
 • It was determined by Mooney that the originally proposed calibration approach using a fixed 60 Co
 source would be of limited value at the DUNE far detector given the large size of the detector in
 comparison to the photon interaction length in argon (roughly 18 cm). Instead, Mooney’s research
 group focused on developing DUNE calibrations through injection of 220 Rn into the liquid argon,
 including both R&D in a small LArTPC at CSU and a preliminary test at the 2x2 prototype for the
 DUNE near detector.
None of the above changes in project goals significantly altered the original scope of the project.

2 MicroBooNE Research
The majority of this project has focused on research for the SBN Program and DUNE. However, one
significant research activity using MicroBooNE data was carried out by Mooney’s research group earlier on
in the award period, which is described below.
MicroBooNE Low-Energy Excess Analysis: The chief physics goal of MicroBooNE is understanding
the nature of the electron-neutrino-like low-energy excess (LEE) observed at MiniBooNE. Of particular
relevance to the MicroBooNE LEE analysis is the reconstruction of low-energy electrons that are produced
from electron neutrino interactions with argon nuclei in the detector, serving as the signal of interest for the
LEE analysis. Mooney’s research group was involved with a MicroBooNE LEE analysis that makes use of
the Pandora multi-algorithm approach for pattern recognition of both cosmic-ray muon activity and neutrino
events [1]. The specific contributions by Mooney’s research group included development and optimization of
the 1e0p0π signal channel (the other analysis signal channel being 1eN p0π) and contributions to the fitting
procedure used for hypothesis testing and signal extraction. One of Mooney’s graduate students defended
his PhD thesis on this research topic. The conclusion of the analysis was that no excess of low-energy νe
candidates was observed [2, 3], suggesting that further investigation is needed to explain the MiniBooNE
anomaly; this was consistent with the results of two additional analyses [4, 5] carried out in parallel with
this work as shown in Figure 1. This work seeded additional MicroBooNE analyses on similar topics that
have since led to published research results.

4 DUNE Research
Mooney’s research group has made several significant research contributions to DUNE over the award period,
including activities relevant to both the DUNE far detector (FD) and near detector (ND). These activities
are discussed below.
Continuation of DUNE Leadership Roles: Mooney has served as co-convener of the Calibration Work-
ing Group for DUNE since 2019, coordinating the detector calibration plan for the experiment. This calibra-
tion plan includes use of both cosmogenic activity and radiogenic activity in both the FD and ND for carrying
out various calibrations of interest. As part of this role, Mooney has been facilitating detector calibrations
at the DUNE prototype detectors, including ProtoDUNE-SP, ProtoDUNE-HD, and ProtoDUNE-VD for the
FD and the 2x2 prototype for the liquid argon component of the DUNE near detector (ND-LAr). In addition,
Mooney served as L3 Manager of TPC Module Assembly and Testing for DUNE ND-LAr between 2020 and
2024, supervising the design, prototyping, and testing of assembly fixtures for the ND-LAr TPC modules.
Mooney continues to play a leading role in TPC module assembly for ND-LAr through his supervision of
continued CSU contributions.
Detector Calibrations for DUNE Far Detector Prototypes: Mooney and his research group have
led the development of several key calibrations for the DUNE FD prototypes, including ProtoDUNE-SP,
ProtoDUNE-HD, and ProtoDUNE-VD. This work features several contributions from Mooney’s graduate
students, including simulation and calibration of space charge effects, the leading detector effect at these
prototypes given their operation near the surface [11, 12], and TPC ionization signal response tuning. The
calibration work for ProtoDUNE-SP was published as part of the ProtoDUNE-SP performance paper [13],
while preliminary results for ProtoDUNE-HD are shown in Figure 4. Additionally, Mooney’s graduate stu-
dents have developed novel calibrations making use of radiogenic activity in the DUNE FD prototypes; this
requires the matching of the ionization charge and scintillation light associated with low-energy activity in
the detector such as 39 Ar beta decays (see Figure 5 for first results at ProtoDUNE-HD). Using radiogenic
activity for calibrations is important developmental work for future DUNE FD calibrations given that the
underground DUNE FD modules will collect few cosmic muons for calibration purposes [14].
