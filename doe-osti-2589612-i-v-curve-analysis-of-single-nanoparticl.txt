I-V curve analysis of single-nanoparticle Al-doped SrTiO3

 (Dated: 15 September 2025)
 Strontium Titanate (SrTiO3 or STO) is an oxide-semiconductor material which shows promise for photocatalytic water
 splitting, but remains poorly understood. To better understand its carrier dynamics, STO nanoparticles deposited onto
 an Au substrate were placed inside of a Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM) and then contacted with an Au probe.
 Current-voltage (I-V) measurements were taken with and without electron beam illumination. Fitting to the curves, we
 estimate the doping density (ND ≈ 1022.1 ± 1.2 cm−3 ) and built-in potential (φbi ≈ 0.84 ± 0.45 eV ). We then used these
 I-V measurements to estimate the diffusion time (τ) of our samples and observed an exponential dependence of τ on
 sample size in the range of 350-550 nm.

I. INTRODUCTION

 Photocatalytic water splitting is a process by which water
is split into hydrogen and oxygen using light and a photocat-
alyst. Methods to utilize this process based on nanoparticle
photocatalyst suspensions have been investigated due to their
low cost, but suffer from a low solar-to-hydrogen conversion
efficiency. This efficiency is primarily determined by a photo-
catalyst’s ability to absorb light, transport excited charge car-
riers to the surface, and then react those charges with water
at the surface. In order for nanoparticle photocatalyst suspen-
sions to be economical, new materials with higher efficiencies
must be found.
