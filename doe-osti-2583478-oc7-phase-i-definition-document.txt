Executive Summary
The OC7 project is organized under International Energy Agency Wind Technology
Collaboration Programme Task 56 with an express objective to evaluate and enhance the
predictive accuracy of engineering-level modeling tools used in the design of floating wind
energy systems. Phase I of OC7 is focused on improving the models and modeling practices of
hydrodynamic viscous loads on floating platforms. In alignment with this goal, Work Package
1.1, the first among several work packages that constitute OC7 Phase I, is formulated to
investigate the modeling of hydrodynamic viscous loads on several different geometric
components commonly encountered in many platform designs, including cylindrical columns,
heave plates, and rectangular pontoons. Work Package 1.1 also explores the dependence of
hydrodynamic coefficients on the sea state to drive toward practical guidance on how these
coefficients can be selected or adjusted for different conditions.

To support Work Package 1.1, three experimental datasets are selected, with each dataset
forming the basis of one of the three subphases of Work Package 1.1, labeled Phase Ia, Ib, and
Ic. The first dataset for Phase Ia is derived from a component-level wave basin experimental
campaign on the nonlinear wave loads on a fixed structure. This campaign makes use of platform
geometry inspired by the DeepCwind design with cylindrical columns and heave plates. The
second dataset for Phase Ib is derived from the WINDMOOR campaign made available to OC7
courtesy of SINTEF Ocean. This dataset describes the motion response of the INO
WINDMOOR semisubmersible platform under nonoperational conditions and covers a wide
range of sea states. The third and final dataset for Phase Ic is derived from the FOCAL project,
focusing on the motion response of the VolturnUS-S semisubmersible platform under
nonoperational conditions.

This report outlines the motivation and objectives behind each subphase of OC7 Phase I Work
Package 1.1 along with the necessary technical specifications and load case definitions to guide
the project participants. It also serves as an important part of the project documentation for future
modelers who would like to reproduce this work or make use of the data and information
generated from this project. The results and findings from the OC7 Phase I project will be
presented in separate publications.

1 Introduction
This document defines Work Package (WP) 1.1 of the OC7 Phase I project organized under
International Energy Agency Wind Technology Collaboration Programme Task 56. WP 1.1 aims
to further develop the hydrodynamic modeling practices for floating platforms designed to
support wind turbines in engineering-level tools, particularly the modeling of viscous loads. The
overarching strategy to achieve this goal is to investigate suitable hydrodynamic model
formulations for common platform geometric components. Furthermore, the dependence of
optimal hydrodynamic coefficients (such as added mass, damping, and drag coefficients) for
these component types on flow conditions (such as different sea states) is studied to guide the
adaptation of existing hydrodynamic models to different environmental conditions.

In alignment with the objective and strategy, OC7 Phase I WP 1.1 is subdivided into three
subphases: Ia, Ib, and Ic. Each of the three subphases considers a different platform design (all
semisubmersibles with associated components) and investigates different questions relevant to
the modeling of hydrodynamic viscous loads. The three selected designs cover the most common
types of geometric components encountered in most platform designs: cylindrical surface-
piercing columns, heave plates, and rectangular pontoons. A different experimental dataset is
selected for each phase.

Phase Ia is concerned with the hydrodynamic excitation on a fixed structure. The component-
level load measurements from the wave basin of the University of Maine are used in this phase
(Robertson and Wang, 2021). The geometry of the physical model is derived and simplified from
the DeepCwind platform with cylindrical columns and circular heave plates/base columns. The
viscous contributions to the nonlinear low-frequency excitation are the main quantities of interest
in this investigation.

Phase Ib makes use of the wave basin measurements from the WINDMOOR project provided by
SINTEF Ocean (Thys et al., 2021). A subset of the WINDMOOR data focusing on the
hydrodynamic response of the INO WINDMOOR semisubmersible platform is selected for this
subphase. The platform design features three cylindrical columns and rectangular pontoons
forming a triangle. The selected data cover a wide range of irregular sea states, and the
dependence of optimal hydrodynamic model coefficients on the sea state is investigated. The
rectangular pontoons also pose unique challenges to the modeling of viscous loads that have not
been investigated in previous OC projects.

Finally, Phase Ic calls for modeling the hydrodynamic response of the VolturnUS-S reference
semisubmersible platform. The VolturnUS-S platform features four cylindrical columns with
rectangular pontoons. Unlike the INO WINDMOOR design, the pontoons are arranged in a Y-
shape connecting the central column to the outer columns. The measurements from the FOCAL
project conducted at the wave basin of the University of Maine are used (Lenfest and Fowler,
2023). This subphase is primarily intended as a validation exercise to evaluate the model
formulation and procedures developed in the two prior subphases. Furthermore, the possibility of
integrating computational fluid dynamics (CFD) data is also explored. The hydrodynamic model
developed in Phase Ic will serve as a reference in OC7 Phase II on the validation of internal
structural loads partly based on the same FOCAL dataset.

 1

Experimental measurements are shared with the participants in advance for Phase Ia and for a
subset of load cases in Phase Ib. The participants are encouraged to develop and tune their
models independently. Furthermore, they are encouraged to fine-tune the model coefficients for
each load case separately to obtain the best possible agreement with the measurements. The
intention of this exercise is to identify the most effective model formulation and understand how
the optimal model coefficients change with the environmental conditions (primarily the sea
state). This knowledge will provide guidance for future modeling efforts and for the a priori
determination of model coefficients based on environmental conditions, which is the express
goal of OC7 Phase I. The remaining experimental measurements of Phase Ib and Ic are withheld
from the participants and reserved for validation.

2 Phase Ia
OC7 Phase Ia is a model development and tuning exercise centered on the contribution of
viscous loads to the low-frequency hydrodynamic excitation on floating platform components.
The wave-frequency excitation is usually well modeled by potential-flow theory without having
to consider any viscous effects; however, the nonlinear low-frequency excitation, especially the
force in the horizontal surge and sway directions, can contain significant contributions from
viscous loads in addition to second-order difference-frequency potential-flow excitation. The
nonlinear low-frequency excitation and the resulting platform resonance motion were subject to
extensive investigation in OC6 Phase I (Robertson et al., 2020). The predictions of the low-
frequency loads and motion with engineering-level tools were generally found to be
unsatisfactory. Several subsequent developments have improved the low-frequency predictions,
at least for the DeepCwind platform and a limited set of sea states (Li and Bachynski-Polić,
2021a, 2021b; Wang et al., 2022). However, it is still of interest to further investigate this issue
considering the effects of different sea states and platform geometries to help guide model
development and tuning in the future.

Concurrent with OC6 Phase I, a component-level wave basin experimental campaign was carried
out in the Alfond wind-wave basin at the Advanced Structures and Composites Center of the
University of Maine (Robertson and Wang, 2021). This experimental campaign is unique with
the inclusion of separate testing of individual components that constitute a conventional
semisubmersible platform. The physical model configurations included (1) a single vertical
cylinder, (2) a vertical cylinder and heave plate with two different heave plate thicknesses, and
(3) three cylinders with heave plates in a typical semisubmersible arrangement. The campaign
also exclusively measured the wave diffraction loads on a fixed structure, allowing the viscous
load contribution to hydrodynamic excitation to be investigated separately from its contribution
to damping. The hydrodynamic excitation was measured separately for each column when three
columns were present to investigate any hydrodynamic interaction between the columns and
heave plates. Furthermore, experiments were conducted with the structure in both an even-keel
configuration and with a 5° pitch offset representative of generic conditions during turbine
operation with high rotor thrust. The complete dataset and relevant documentation are publicly
accessible. 1

A small subset of the data from this experiment involving bichromatic waves was previously
used to validate CFD simulations in OC6 Phase I (Wang et al., 2021). Only one configuration
with three columns and thick heave plates at even keel was considered in this validation exercise.
Therefore, it is valuable to revisit this experimental dataset with engineering models that rely on
potential-flow theory, Morison-equation-type strip-theory modeling, or a combination of the two.
The objective of the investigation is to obtain an improved understanding of the best approaches
to capture the nonlinear viscous contribution to hydrodynamic excitation in the low-frequency
region with engineering-level tools. It is also of interest to gain an understanding of if and how
the model coefficients should be adjusted for different wave conditions and platform geometry.

2.1 Objectives
Phase Ia investigates the contribution from viscous loads to low-frequency hydrodynamic
excitation on the cylindrical columns and heave plates. We aim to develop guidance on the most
effective model formulation, model tuning approach, and selection of hydrodynamic model
coefficients based on the sea state and geometry to accurately capture this viscous contribution
with engineering-level tools.

2.1.1 Viscous Loads on Vertical Columns
As demonstrated in the prior OC6 Phase I investigation, quadratic strip-theory drag force on the
vertical columns in the splash zone in conjunction with wave stretching can be a dominant
contributor to the slow-drift force (Wang et al., 2022). This contribution is commonly referred to
as the viscous drift force. It is of interest to investigate the effects of different wave-stretching
formulations and how the drag coefficients in the wave zone should be adjusted for different sea
states and different geometries.

In addition to wave stretching, participants can also explore other approaches to capture the
viscous drift force, such as the semiempirical model for viscous mean drift force in conjunction
with Newman’s approximation developed within the Exwave Joint Industry Project (Yang et al.,
2018).

2.1.2 Viscous Loads on Heave Plates
In addition to the second-order potential-flow excitation, the viscous load on the heave plates in
the vertical direction also contributes to the low-frequency heave force and pitch moment on the
structure. It is of interest to identify the best formulations for the heave plate viscous load and to
investigate how the optimally tuned model coefficients change with condition and geometry. The
results can be compared to existing literature data on the relation between the heave plate
hydrodynamic coefficients and the Keulegan-Carpenter (KC) number for different heave plate
geometries undergoing forced harmonic oscillation. This comparison allows us to investigate
how an equivalent KC number can be evaluated for irregular seas and to explore the possibility
of estimating the model coefficients based on existing data for harmonic oscillations.

2.1.3 Other Factors That Influence the Modeling of Viscous Loads
In addition to different sea states, the selected dataset also uniquely supports the investigation of
the following factors that potentially impact the modeling of viscous loads:
 • The hydrodynamic interaction between a column and an attached heave plate
 • The hydrodynamic interaction between neighboring columns and heave plates
 • Different heave plate thicknesses
 • A 5° static pitch offset such as that induced by rotor thrust.
The modeling guidance developed in Phase Ia, especially for the vertical columns, can
potentially be applied to the remaining phases, which also consider semisubmersible platforms
featuring cylindrical columns.

2.2 Experimental Setup
The wave basin experiment was conducted at 1:50 scale with a full-scale water depth of 250 m.
The experimental setup for a three-column configuration is shown in Figure 1 along with the

 4

suite of instrumentation. The three columns were arranged such that one of the columns, the
upstream column (UC), was placed on the center plane facing the waves. The other two columns
were placed downstream on the port side and starboard side symmetric about the center plane.
The three columns form an equilateral triangle with a full-scale center-to-center distance of 50
m.

The entire physical model was held captive at a fixed position (apart from any unintended
vibration in the support structure). The UC and the starboard column (SC) had independent load
cells so that the hydrodynamic load on each of the two columns can be measured separately. One
of the columns was also fitted with pressure taps, and the UC had a run-up wave probe in front.
However, for OC7 Phase Ia, we ignore these additional measurements and only consider the
hydrodynamic loads measured by the two load cells.

As shown in Figure 1, the origin of the global earth-fixed coordinate system is located at the still
water level (SWL) at the center of the waterplane area of the three-column configurations
without pitch offset. The 𝑥𝑥-axis points along the direction of wave propagation. The 𝑧𝑧-axis points
vertically upward, and the 𝑦𝑦-axis points toward the starboard direction following the right-hand
convention.

2.3 Formatting and Naming of Experimental Data Files
Two types of files from the experimental campaign are made available to the participants:

 • Wave measurements from the wave calibration experiments without the structure
 • Load, wave, and pressure measurements with the structure in water.
All experimental data are Froude-scaled to full scale following the model-scale ratio of 1:50.

The filenames for the wave calibration experiments indicate the wave condition and the
repetition number. For example, the file oc6.phase1b.experiment.wavecheck.waveJ1.repeat2.txt
contains the measurements from the second repeat of the wave calibration experiment for wave
J1 in Table 3. The filenames for experiments with the structure indicate the configuration of the
physical model (Figure 2), wave condition (Table 3), and the repetition number. As an example,
the measurements from the third repeat of Config. P in wave J1 can be found in the file
oc6.phase1b.experiment.configP.waveJ1.repeat3.txt.

The columns included in the files from the wave calibration experiments are summarized in
Table 4. The wave measurements from reference probes A through E and the wave calibration
probes F through J are available. Robertson and Wang (2021) provide the locations of the wave
probes in the basin. The measurements from the calibration probes are considered more reliable
(Robertson and Wang, 2021). If the selected modeling tool can make use of externally defined
incident wave time series as inputs, the measurements from WP_H can be approximately used as
the incident waves at the fixed origin in Figure 3.

2.6 Comments on Scaling Effects
As with prior OC projects, all properties of the physical model and experimental measurements
provided are Froude-scaled to full scale. Consistently, we also request simulation results from
the participants to be at full scale.

Viscous loads are known to be influenced by scaling (Reynolds number) effects depending on
the geometry. In the context of this investigation, we expect the transverse viscous loads on the
columns and the heave plates to be more sensitive to scaling effects due to the circular cross
section. Therefore, we intend to focus instead on the effects of the KC number related to the ratio
of the significant wave height, 𝐻𝐻𝑠𝑠 , to the column diameter. It is further acknowledged that the
hydrodynamic coefficients derived from this model tuning exercise are only applicable at model
scale. Their applicability to a full-scale structure cannot be evaluated. However, we believe the
model formulations explored in this project and the observed general dependence of the
hydrodynamic coefficients on the sea state remain applicable to a full-scale structure, although
different values might be required for the coefficients to account for the scaling effects.

In contrast to the transverse viscous loads on the columns and heave plates, the viscous loads in
the heave direction on the heave plates are known to be less sensitive to scaling effects and are
primarily controlled by the KC number (Bezunartea-Barrio et al., 2020). Therefore, the
associated results can be considered approximately applicable to a full-scale system.

3 Phase Ib
Phase Ib aims to develop approaches for tuning the hydrodynamic coefficients of a
semisubmersible platform with rectangular pontoons based on the sea state. This work is
conducted using model information and measurements from the WINDMOOR project, which
was funded by the Research Council of Norway and industry partners. Courtesy of SINTEF
Ocean, a subset of the WINDMOOR experimental data is made available to the OC7 team. The
selected subset of data is concerned with the hydrodynamic responses of a scaled INO
WINDMOOR semisubmersible and includes a wide range of wave conditions. Note that a
significant portion of the WINDMOOR project was on the incorporation of aerodynamic loads
and effects of turbine controls through a hybrid software-in-the-loop setup. However, this is
outside the scope of the OC7 Phase I investigation.

3.1 Objectives
It is generally recognized that the hydrodynamic coefficients, including added mass, damping,
and drag coefficients, can be sensitive to the sea state. This sensitivity poses challenges to more
predictive modeling because the model coefficients tuned to one sea state do not necessarily
yield accurate predictions with a different sea state, especially with the low-frequency resonance
response of the platform. To tackle this challenge and drive toward more predictive
hydrodynamic modeling, the overarching objective of Phase Ib is to investigate the dependence
of the hydrodynamic coefficients on the sea state and develop methods that allow the a priori
selection of hydrodynamic coefficients for different sea states for which no experimental
measurements are available.

This investigation is made possible by the fact that the WINDMOOR dataset covers a wide range
of sea states with different significant wave heights and wave peak periods. In alignment with the
overarching objective, a subset of experimental measurements is made available to the
participants for the fine-tuning of hydrodynamic coefficients. This exercise should help illustrate
the sensitivity and dependence of optimally tuned hydrodynamic coefficients on key sea state
parameters. Furthermore, it should allow us to develop suitable methods for adjusting the
hydrodynamic coefficients for different sea states not covered by the data for model tuning. The
developed methods will subsequently be put to the test in a validation exercise using a separate
set of experimental measurements with distinct sea states reserved for this purpose.

The INO WINDMOOR platform features three vertical cylindrical columns and rectangular
pontoons at the keel level (see Figure 6). Considering the geometry of the WINDMOOR
platform, we can formulate several more specific questions that can potentially be addressed
through this investigation.

 • First, it is of interest to determine whether the strip-theory drag coefficients for the
 vertical columns tuned in OC7 Phase Ia to capture the viscous drift force can, in some
 fashion, be applied to the WINDMOOR platform, thus demonstrating the transferability
 of coefficient tuning between similar designs.
 • Rectangular pontoons have not been considered in prior OC projects. Therefore, it is of
 interest to investigate how strip-theory hydrodynamic loads should be formulated for the
 pontoons to best capture the directionality of the pontoon loads.

 14

 • The pontoon hydrodynamic coefficients are expected to be strongly influenced by the KC
 number. Therefore, it is of interest to compare the tuned pontoon coefficients to, for
 example, results of regular-oscillation CFD simulations that provide the pontoon
 coefficients as functions of the KC number. This comparison can potentially shed light on
 how to characterize the KC number in realistic irregular seas and guide the a priori
 selection of pontoon hydrodynamic coefficients for different sea states.
 • In OC6 Phase I (Robertson et al., 2020), it was observed that model coefficients tuned to
 free-decay experiments do not necessarily yield good predictions of the platform
 response in waves. With OC7 Phase Ib, it is of interest to further compare the model
 coefficients tuned to free decay and tuned to wave response to investigate how the
 contradiction, if any, can be reconciled and how the two different tuning approaches can
 be integrated.

3.2 Experimental Setup
As shown in Figure 6, the INO WINDMOOR semisubmersible platform (Silva de Souza et al.,
2022) consists of three cylindrical columns connected at the keel level by three rectangular
pontoons forming a triangle. The origin of the global earth-fixed coordinate system is located on
the SWL at the center of the waterplane area of the nominal undisplaced system (even keel with

supporting the wind turbine tower is located on the +𝑥𝑥-axis. The body-fixed coordinate system
coincides with the global earth-fixed system when the structure is at the nominal undisplaced
position. The adopted global coordinate system and the convention for wave headings are shown

3.5 Modeling Approaches and Coefficient Tuning
As with Phase Ia, there is no prescribed modeling approach. Participants should model the
system based on their respective best practices. We encourage the participants to fine-tune the
model for each load case separately, especially the load cases listed in Table 18, to deduce the
dependence of the relevant model coefficients on the sea state, so that the model coefficients can
be adjusted accordingly for the reserved validation load cases in Table 19.

Participants can consider using different hydrodynamic coefficients on different members,
different coefficients along the same member, or different coefficients as a function of depth in
conjunction with wave stretching to improve predictions. Other model components that
potentially improve predictions include the semiempirical modeling of viscous drift force and
wave drift damping (Yang et al., 2018).

To facilitate the modeling effort, NREL will provide first-order potential-flow hydrodynamic
coefficients and second-order difference-frequency wave excitation quadratic transfer functions.
However, participants can also opt to generate their own potential-flow hydrodynamic database.

Finally, using CFD simulations, we plan to develop a database of hydrodynamic coefficients for
the rectangular pontoons featured in the WINDMOOR design. The relevant hydrodynamic
coefficients, primarily the added mass and drag coefficients in the heave and sway directions, as
functions of the KC number shall be provided to help guide model tuning. However, the pontoon
hydrodynamic coefficients will be computed with a simplified CFD setup where a section of the
pontoon (2.5-dimensional simulations) is forced to oscillate in an infinite fluid without a free
surface. Participants should therefore use discretion when referencing the CFD hydrodynamic
coefficients for modeling tuning.

4 Phase Ic
Phase Ic is on the validation of engineering-level models of the VolturnUS-S reference
semisubmersible platform. A subset of the experimental measurements is selected from
Campaign 4 of the FOCAL project (Lenfest and Fowler, 2023), supported by the U.S.
Department of Energy’s Advanced Research Projects Agency – Energy (ARPA-E).

4.1 Objectives
The main objective of Phase Ic is to validate and evaluate the modeling techniques and
coefficient tuning/selection procedures developed in Phase Ia and Phase Ib. Participants are
encouraged to leverage any new developments and experience gained from the two prior phases
in Phase Ic to generate a priori predictions of the motion response of the VolturnUS-S platform
in two distinct sea states without access to the corresponding experimental measurements.

Phase Ic is also designed to explore the possibility of directly integrating CFD data into the
development of engineering-level models. As part of OC7 WP 1.3, CFD simulations of a
selection of simplified and idealized scenarios will be performed with the VolturnUS-S platform,
including forced oscillation and a captive/fixed structure in regular waves. From these CFD
simulations, local hydrodynamic loads on various structural components of the platform, such as
columns and pontoon sections, will be extracted to estimate local hydrodynamic coefficients,
especially drag coefficients. These data will be made available to the participants at the start of
Phase Ic to support model development in lieu of experimental measurements.

Finally, the hydrodynamic models of the VolturnUS-S platform developed in Phase Ic will help
jump-start the OC7 Phase II effort on the validation of global structural load models utilizing the
same dataset from the FOCAL project. Accurate modeling of the hydrodynamic load distribution
on, and the motion response of, the platform is a prerequisite to successful validation of
structural load models.

4.2 Experimental Setup
The FOCAL experimental campaigns were also conducted in the Alfond wind-wave basin at the
Advanced Structures and Composites Center of the University of Maine. The FOCAL campaign
was developed to validate models of global structural loads, hull-based load-mitigation systems,
and advanced turbine controls. Therefore, the complete model-scale system included three tuned-
mass-damper systems with one located in each outer column of the platform, a performance-
matched and fully actuated model-scale wind turbine, and load cells at various critical locations
of the system, including in the rectangular pontoons. An umbilical cable bundle was attached to
the platform to provide power to and communicate with the various actuators and sensors.

With an emphasis on hydrodynamic modeling, OC7 Phase Ic only considers load cases without
any action from the tuned mass dampers and with an idling rotor and feathered blades. (OC7
Phase II will model additional load cases and make use of the measurements from the various
load cells on the model.) This simplification also allows us to exclusively select load cases
without the umbilical cable bundle attached to minimize the uncertainty in the measured
platform motion.

4.5 Modeling Approaches and Coefficient Selection
As with the two prior phases, no specific modeling approach is prescribed. Participants are
encouraged to model the system using their respective best practices, preferably leveraging any

 31

new development made or experience gained through Phase Ia and Phase Ib. Due to the
similarity between the two phases, any modeling approach successfully applied in Phase Ib is
likely also applicable to Phase Ic, assuming appropriate selection of hydrodynamic coefficients.

Note that the measured motion from free-decay experiments is primarily provided to verify
whether the numerical models correctly capture the free-decay periods of platform motion.
Participants can also opt to tune system damping to the measured free-decay motion. However, it
is generally expected that models tuned to free decay do not necessarily provide the correct
motion response in waves. Therefore, participants should also consider referencing their
experience from Phase Ia and Phase Ib and the CFD data generated from WP 1.3 when
predicting the motion response in waves for LC 3.1 and LC 3.2.

NREL will compute first-order potential-flow hydrodynamic coefficients and quadratic transfer
functions for second-order difference-frequency potential-flow wave excitation to support the
participants’ modeling effort. Participants can opt to use these as input to their models or
generate their own hydrodynamic coefficients.

4.6 Result Comparison and Analyses
Participants should simulate LC 1.0 and LC 2.x and independently check the results in
preparation for model validation based on LC 3.x. NREL will conduct validation of the
simulation results for LC 3.x primarily through comparing the PSDs of platform motion from the
simulations to those from the wave basin experiments. The initial portion of the time series up to
654.6 s will be discarded as startup transient, and the PSDs will be computed using the remainder
of the time series up to 9,294.6 s, covering 2.4 h of motion response. As in Phase Ib, square roots
of the PSD integrals of platform motion will also be computed for specific frequency ranges to
help compare the motion response near the resonance frequencies and in response to direct linear
wave excitation. The frequency ranges are provided in Table 30.

4.7 Simulation Outputs and Formatting
As shown in Table 31, the required outputs from the simulations are the full-scale wave elevation
at the global earth-fixed origin, the 6-DoF motion of the platform, and the mooring line tension
at the three fairleads. See Figure 10 and the accompanying description for the coordinate systems

adopted and the numbering of the mooring lines and fairleads. Note that for the translation of the
platform, we request the instantaneous position of the body-fixed origin in the global earth-fixed
coordinate system. For the rotational displacement, the order of rotation is deemed not critical
due to the relatively small rotation involved. Participants can report the roll, pitch, and yaw
angles following a convenient convention or based on a small/moderate angle approximation
independent of the order of rotation.

The second column for wave elevation should be retained for all load cases, even though it is
only relevant for LC 3.x. For all other load cases, the second column should be uniformly filled

the structure present and properly synchronized in time with the other output channels.

Note that the output file is slightly different for LC 1.0, hydrostatic equilibrium. For this case, the
simulation should run for as long as is necessary to reliably determine the equilibrium values of
platform offset and fairlead pretension. The result file for LC 1.0 should only contain one line of
data, either the values of the last time step if the relevant quantities in Table 31 have fully
stabilized or the averages of a suitable portion of the time series toward the end of the simulation
if small fluctuations in the relevant quantities persist.
