Genetic evaluation of encorafenib-associated kinases RAF1 and MAPK8 in pancreatic cancer: Exploratory mediation by circulating immune traits

Fen Lin, Wenwen Tu, Ronggui Lin, Han Shi, Shiyun Lu, Zongqi Weng

Abstract

•Genetically proxied RAF1 and MAPK8 expression was associated with pancreatic cancer risk.•A circulating CD4+ Treg-related phenotype showed a possible partial indirect contribution.•PheWAS detected no broad genetic pleiotropic signals at the prespecified threshold.•The findings support further study of RAF1- and MAPK8-related pathways in pancreatic cancer.
ObjectivesPancreatic cancer (PC) is a highly lethal malignancy, and identifying actionable therapeutic targets remains crucial. RAF1 and MAPK8 are kinases linked to the in vitro pharmacological profile of encorafenib. This study investigated the associations of genetically proxied RAF1 and MAPK8 expression with PC risk and explored the potential mediating role of circulating immune traits.MethodsWe implemented a two-sample, two-step Mendelian randomization framework integrating expression quantitative trait locus data for RAF1 and MAPK8 with genome-wide association study data for PC and 731 immune cell phenotypes. Causal estimates were primarily derived using inverse-variance weighting, and mediation analysis assessed possible immune cell-dependent effects. A phenome-wide association study (PheWAS) evaluated potential genetic pleiotropy.ResultsGenetically proxied RAF1 expression (OR = 0.748, 95% CI: 0.633–0.882, P = 0.001) and MAPK8 expression (OR = 0.829, 95% CI: 0.740–0.929, P = 0.001) were significantly associated with a lower risk of PC. An activated and resting CD4+ regulatory T-cell-related phenotype showed a possible partial mediating effect. The estimated mediation proportions were 16.202% for MAPK8 and 10.607% for RAF1, suggesting that Treg-related immune variation may account for part of these associations. In the PheWAS analysis, no association reached the prespecified significance threshold, and no broad genetic pleiotropic signal was detected.ConclusionsThis study provides genetic evidence linking RAF1 and MAPK8 to PC risk. A circulating regulatory T-cell-related phenotype showed exploratory evidence of a possible partial contribution. These findings support further functional and pharmacological investigation of RAF1- and MAPK8-related pathways in pancreatic cancer.

Introduction

Pancreatic cancer (PC) remains one of the most lethal malignancies worldwide, with a five-year survival rate stubbornly below 10% [, , ]. Its insidious onset and rapid progression often lead to late-stage diagnosis, severely limiting the efficacy of conventional therapies such as surgery and chemotherapy [,]. The projected doubling of PC-related deaths by 2060 underscores the urgent, unmet need for novel therapeutic strategies [,].

In this context, modulating the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) has emerged as a promising frontier []. PC is characterized by a profoundly immunosuppressive TME, where mechanisms such as immune checkpoint activation and infiltration of immunosuppressive cells, including regulatory T cells (Tregs), facilitate immune evasion and promote tumor progression [, , , ]. Consequently, strategies to overcome this immune barrier are of paramount importance.

The mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway is a cornerstone of PC pathogenesis, frequently activated by oncogenic KRAS mutations and driving uncontrolled proliferation and survival []. Beyond its cell-autonomous oncogenic role, the MAPK pathway critically shapes the immune landscape of the TME, influencing responses to both chemotherapy and immunotherapy []. This dual function makes it an attractive therapeutic target. Notably, MAPK pathway inhibitors, such as RAF kinase inhibitors, have revolutionized the treatment of cancers such as BRAF-mutant melanoma, where they not only directly inhibit tumor growth but also synergize with immunotherapy by modulating immune responses []. Beyond direct RAF inhibition, pharmacological targeting of upstream regulators of RAS–MAPK signaling, such as SHP2, has also emerged as a potential anticancer strategy, reflecting the broader therapeutic relevance of modulating this signaling network []. Similar principles of targeting MAPK to overcome resistance are being explored for other malignancies, such as EGFR-mutant non-small cell lung cancer []. These successes raise a pivotal, yet unexplored, question: Could therapeutic targeting of the MAPK pathway be harnessed in PC by leveraging its immunomodulatory effects?

Encorafenib, a selective RAF kinase inhibitor approved for BRAF-mutant melanoma, may represent a potential candidate for further investigation in PC []. Pharmacological evidence indicates that encorafenib can inhibit CRAF, encoded by RAF1, and bind JNK1, encoded by MAPK8, in vitro, linking these kinases to its broader pharmacological profile. Preliminary evidence suggests that encorafenib may affect PC through the modulation of MAPK-related signaling []. However, its mechanism of action in PC remains unclear, particularly whether immune regulation, including Treg-related responses, contributes to its potential effects. Clarifying these relationships may help define the biological relevance of RAF1- and MAPK8-related pathways and inform further experimental evaluation in PC.

To address this question, we employed Mendelian randomization (MR), an established genetic epidemiological method that uses genetic variants as instrumental variables to assess potential causal relationships while reducing the influence of confounding and reverse causation [,]. We hypothesized that genetically proxied perturbations of the encorafenib-associated kinases RAF1 and MAPK8 may be associated with a reduced risk of PC and that circulating immune cell phenotypes, including Treg-related traits, may partially mediate these associations. Therefore, this study aimed to systematically investigate the relationships among RAF1 and MAPK8, circulating immune cell subtypes, and PC risk. Our findings were intended to provide gene-level evidence on the associations of RAF1 and MAPK8 with PC risk and to explore the possible contribution of circulating immune traits.

Materials and methods

Study design

In this eQTL-based study, a two-sample, two-step MR framework was used to examine the associations of genetically proxied RAF1 and MAPK8 expression with circulating immune phenotypes and PC risk (). To ensure the validity of the IVs and the credibility of the study results, the following core assumptions were followed. First, single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) were significantly associated with the expression of RAF1 or MAPK8 (P < 1 × 10-5, F statistic > 10), indicating adequate instrument strength. Second, SNPs were independent of potential confounders, thereby minimizing the risk of bias. Furthermore, SNPs affect PC through genetically proxied RAF1/MAPK8 expression rather than directly, as verified by the MR‒Egger regression model, with an intercept P > 0.05, indicating no significant pleiotropy []. Additionally, adherence to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology using Mendelian Randomization (STROBE-MR) reporting guidelines was maintained to ensure transparency and standardization []. Because this study used cis-eQTL instruments to evaluate genetically proxied gene expression, a less stringent threshold than genome-wide significance thresholds used for complex trait GWAS was applied to obtain sufficient expression-associated instruments. Additional procedures, including cis-region restriction, linkage disequilibrium pruning, PhenoScanner-based filtering, and sensitivity analyses, were performed to minimize potential bias.Fig. 1Mendelian randomization analysis of the immune cell-mediated effect of genetic expression of RAF1/MAPK8 on pancreatic cancer.[gr1.webp]

Within this framework, we first estimated the associations of genetically proxied RAF1 and MAPK8 expression with PC risk and subsequently explored candidate circulating immune pathways using two-step MR analysis.

Data sources

Summary-level genetic data were obtained from the IEU OpenGWAS Project (https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/; accessed 13 October 2024), including eQTL data for nine candidate genes (CCND1, RAF1, STK36, MAPK9, MAPK8, LIMK2, MAP2K4, LIMK1, and BRAF) and GWAS datasets for 731 immune cell phenotypes, pancreatic cancer, and three comparison cancer outcomes. All the data were fully anonymized without access to individual identifiers. The original studies obtained participant consent and ethics approval. Selection of IVs

eQTL instruments for candidate genes

To ensure biological relevance, the IVs were restricted to the cis-acting regions of the target gene within a 100 kb range. Relevant SNPs were extracted from the GWAS summary data of the outcome variables (PC and immune cells). Palindromic SNPs and those with a minor allele frequency (MAF) > 0.42 were excluded. Outlier SNPs were further removed using Mendelian randomization pleiotropy residual sum and outlier (MR-PRESSO). Additionally, linkage disequilibrium (LD) was controlled by setting the LD coefficient (r2) to 0.3 and the LD region width to 100 kb, with an MAF > 0.01, to ensure independence between SNPs and eliminate the influence of LD on the results. SNPs associated with confounding factors or outcomes were excluded using the PhenoScanner tool (http://www.phenoscanner.medschl.cam.ac.uk/).

eQTL IVs for immune cells

To ensure the independence of the SNPs and minimize the influence of LD on the results, a more stringent threshold was applied. The LD threshold was set at r2 < 0.001, with a window size of 10,000 kb. The MAF threshold was set to > 0.01. The PhenoScanner tool was used to exclude SNPs associated with potential confounders or outcome variables (PC).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using the TwoSampleMR package in R (version 4.1.0). A nominal significance threshold of P < 0.05 was used for exploratory screening analyses to identify candidate associations. Given the number of candidate genes and circulating immune phenotypes evaluated, nominally significant secondary associations were interpreted cautiously and were considered hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

Causal relationships analysis

To investigate the causal relationships between the exposure, defined as genetically proxied RAF1 or MAPK8 expression and the outcome (PC), multiple regression models, including MR‒Egger regression, inverse-variance weighting (IVW), a weighted median estimator (WME), the weighted mode, and the simple mode, were employed in this study. For all regression models, eQTLs were utilized as IVs, and two-sample MR analysis was applied to evaluate the association of genetically proxied RAF1 or MAPK8 expression with PC risk. The IVW method served as the primary analytical approach, whereas the other methods were used for complementary analysis.

When the number of SNPs (N_snp) was ≤ 3, the Wald ratio was used to assess the effect of individual SNPs on the outcome, whereas the fixed-effects IVW method was applied in other cases. For N_snp > 3, random-effects IVW was employed. In cases where horizontal pleiotropy of SNPs was present, the results of MR‒Egger regression were used for reference.

Sensitivity analysis

The pleiotropy test was conducted using MR‒Egger regression. A nonsignificant MR-Egger intercept test (P > 0.05) was considered to indicate no evidence of directional horizontal pleiotropy.

Heterogeneity was assessed using Cochran's Q test, with P < 0.05 indicating the presence of heterogeneity. An I2 > 50% suggested significant heterogeneity. Furthermore, a leave-one-out sensitivity analysis was performed by sequentially removing each SNP to evaluate the impact of each SNP on the final result.

Mediation effect analysis

Given the potential role of immune cells in PC, a two-step MR approach was used to explore whether circulating immune phenotypes represented candidate indirect pathways in the associations between genetically proxied RAF1/MAPK8 expression and PC risk. First, the causal effect of RAF1 or MAPK8 expression on immune cell phenotypes was estimated using target gene expression-associated SNPs (β1). Second, the causal effect of immune cell phenotypes on PC was estimated using immune cell-associated SNPs (β2). The total effect of genetically proxied RAF1 or MAPK8 expression on PC was denoted as β3. The mediation proportion was calculated as follows:E(%)=(β1×β2)/β3where β3 represents the total effect of genetically proxied RAF1 or MAPK8 expression on PC. The mediation proportion was used to estimate the relative contribution of each circulating immune phenotype to the total association between genetically proxied RAF1 or MAPK8 expression and PC risk. Positive mediation proportions indicate indirect effects operating in the same direction as the total effect, whereas negative values indicate opposite directional effects and may reflect suppression rather than conventional mediation. Treg-related traits were of particular biological interest given their established relevance to immune regulation in cancer; however, no single circulating immune phenotype was designated as the sole mediator before the exploratory screening.

PheWAS analysis

To evaluate broad genetic phenotype associations and potential pleiotropic signals related to RAF1 and MAPK8, we performed a PheWAS via the AstraZeneca PheWAS portal, which provides gene–phenotype associations across 17,361 binary and 1,419 quantitative phenotypes. Genetic instruments for MAPK8 and RAF1 were tested across these phenotypes using logistic regression for binary phenotypes and linear regression for quantitative phenotypes, with a genome-wide significance threshold of P < 5 × 10-8. Manhattan plots were used to visualize the PheWAS results.

Results

Results of SNP selection

We identified 60 SNPs associated with PC (Supplementary Table S1) and 40,310 SNPs associated with immune cells (Supplementary Table S2) from eQTL data for RAF1 and MAPK8. Additionally, 10,936 PC-related SNPs were obtained from immune cell GWAS data (Supplementary Table S3), and 95 SNPs associated with the three comparison cancer outcomes (non-small cell lung cancer, malignant neoplasm of the thyroid gland, and non-Hodgkin lymphoma) were identified in the same eQTL data (Supplementary Table S4).

No significant heterogeneity for either expression was found in any sensitivity or pleiotropy analysis (Supplementary Figs. S1 and S2). Among the additional candidate genes evaluated, genetically proxied MAP2K4 expression showed a nominal IVW association with PC risk (OR = 1.533, 95% CI: 1.099–2.137, P = 0.012), whereas the remaining candidate genes showed no nominal evidence of association. Exploratory associations of RAF1 and MAPK8 expression with circulating immune phenotypes

At the nominal significance threshold of P < 0.05, exploratory IVW analysis identified candidate associations between genetically proxied MAPK8 expression and 196 circulating immune phenotypes (Supplementary Table S5). Of these phenotypes, 76 showed inverse associations and 120 showed positive associations. HLA-DR on HLA-DR+ T cells had the lowest OR (OR = 0.786, 95% CI: 0.734–0.842, P < 0.001), whereas CD45RA+ CD28− CD8br AC had the highest OR (OR = 22,428.702, 95% CI: 1.127–446,420,959.281, P = 0.047).

Similarly, exploratory analysis identified nominal associations between genetically proxied RAF1 expression and 207 circulating immune phenotypes, including 140 inverse associations and 67 positive associations. CD45 on CD33− HLA-DR− cells had the lowest OR (OR = 0.669, 95% CI: 0.568–0.789, P < 0.001), while CD45RA on naive CD8br cells had the highest OR (OR = 1.371, 95% CI: 1.215–1.548, P < 0.001).

Sensitivity analyses for these representative associations are presented in Supplementary Fig. S3–S6. These results were used to identify candidate circulating immune phenotypes for the subsequent two-step MR analyses.

PheWAS assessment of broad genetic associations

In a PheWAS evaluating RAF1 and MAPK8 genetic instruments across 17,361 binary and 1,419 quantitative traits in the UK Biobank, no associations for either instrument reached the genome-wide significance threshold (P > 5 × 10-8; Supplementary Fig. S12). The strongest nominal signal was observed for MAPK8 (lowest P = 3.2 × 10-6), but this did not meet the multiple-testing threshold. Global pleiotropy tests for RAF1 and MAPK8 also revealed nonsignificant differences (P = 0.17 and 0.06, respectively). Collectively, no broad genetic pleiotropic associations were detected for RAF1 or MAPK8 at the prespecified significance threshold.

Discussion

In this study, a two-step Mendelian randomization framework was used to examine the associations of genetically proxied RAF1 and MAPK8 expression with pancreatic cancer risk and to explore the possible involvement of circulating immune phenotypes. Higher genetically proxied expression of RAF1 and MAPK8 was associated with a lower risk of pancreatic cancer. Among the immune phenotypes evaluated, an activated and resting CD4+ regulatory T-cell-related trait showed a possible indirect contribution, with estimated mediation proportions of 16.202% for MAPK8 and 10.607% for RAF1. These observations suggest that systemic immune variation may explain a small proportion of the associations observed for RAF1 and MAPK8.

The MAPK signaling pathway plays a central role in pancreatic tumorigenesis, particularly in tumors harboring oncogenic KRAS alterations. Sustained activation of this pathway promotes tumor-cell proliferation, survival, therapeutic resistance, and interactions between malignant cells and the surrounding stroma [,]. RAF1 is a key component of RAF signaling, while MAPK8 encodes JNK1, a stress-responsive kinase involved in apoptosis, inflammation, and cellular adaptation. Altered expression or activity of these genes may therefore influence pancreatic cancer through several interconnected biological processes. Studies in colorectal cancer and melanoma have demonstrated the biological and therapeutic relevance of RAF pathway modulation and RAF1-related signaling [, , , ]. A recent clinical report involving molecularly selected pancreatic adenocarcinoma further supports continued investigation of RAF-directed therapeutic strategies in specific patient subgroups [].

The exploratory mediation analysis provided additional insight into the potential role of circulating immune regulation. Among the candidate immune phenotypes, the activated and resting CD4+ Treg-related trait was highlighted because it showed the largest positive estimated mediation proportion in both the MAPK8- and RAF1-related analyses. This finding suggests that circulating Treg-related variation may represent one of several pathways linking RAF1- and MAPK8-related biology to pancreatic cancer risk. The relatively modest mediation proportions also indicate that a considerable part of the observed associations may involve other mechanisms. These may include additional immune-cell populations, tumor-intrinsic MAPK signaling, stromal remodeling, angiogenesis, metabolic regulation, and inflammatory responses.

Regulatory T cells are essential for maintaining immune tolerance and limiting excessive immune activation. In cancer, however, increased Treg activity may contribute to an immunosuppressive environment and facilitate tumor immune escape. Cytokines and chemokines further influence the balance between immune activation and tolerance within malignant tissues [,]. Previous studies in BRAF-altered melanoma have shown that RAF pathway inhibition may be accompanied by changes in tumor antigen expression, T-cell infiltration, and immune signaling [,,]. These observations provide a biological context for potential interactions between MAPK-related pathways and immune regulation.

The immune phenotypes examined in the present study were derived from peripheral blood GWAS data and primarily reflect variation in circulating immune-cell abundance, relative proportions, and surface-marker expression. These measures do not directly assess the localization, infiltration, or functional state of Tregs within pancreatic tumor tissue. The Treg-related finding should therefore be interpreted as evidence of systemic immune variation associated with pancreatic cancer risk. Accordingly, the direction of association observed for circulating Treg-related variation may not directly reflect the biological role of Tregs within pancreatic tumor tissue. Circulating immune phenotypes and tumor-infiltrating immune populations represent related but distinct immunological compartments. It does not provide direct information on immune-cell behavior within the local tumor microenvironment. Circulating Treg-related traits may also capture broader changes in immune homeostasis, inflammatory signaling, and interactions between innate and adaptive immune compartments.

RAF1 and MAPK8 are associated with different components of the broader in vitro pharmacological profile of encorafenib. Encorafenib is primarily recognized as a BRAF inhibitor, although in vitro studies have also reported activity against CRAF and binding to JNK1 [,]. These observations provide a pharmacological basis for considering RAF1- and MAPK8-related pathways in the present study. However, the MR analysis reflects genetically proxied differences in gene expression over the life course, whereas pharmacological effects are additionally influenced by dose, treatment duration, tissue exposure, and target selectivity. The present findings therefore provide genetic support for the biological relevance of RAF1- and MAPK8-related pathways and warrant further pharmacological and functional investigation in pancreatic cancer.

RAF-directed therapies have shown clinical activity in selected BRAF-altered malignancies, particularly when combined with MEK inhibition, EGFR blockade, or immune checkpoint therapy [,,,,,]. These clinical findings support continued investigation of RAF-related pathways in molecularly defined cancers. Their application to pancreatic cancer is likely to depend on tumor genotype, pathway activity, and treatment context. The current results add genetic evidence supporting the biological relevance of RAF1- and MAPK8-related pathways in pancreatic cancer.

The PheWAS analysis identified no significant associations for the RAF1 or MAPK8 genetic instruments across the phenotypes examined. Although MAPK8 showed the lowest nominal P value, this association did not reach the prespecified multiple-testing threshold, and the global pleiotropy tests were also nonsignificant. Overall, the analysis did not reveal evidence of broad phenotypic effects associated with these instruments. More specific associations may still occur in particular tissues, biological pathways, or disease settings that are not fully represented in the available PheWAS data.

Several strengths of this study merit consideration. The use of genetic instruments reduced the potential influence of conventional confounding and reverse causation. The two-step MR framework also enabled the evaluation of gene-disease associations and candidate immune pathways within a unified analytical design. Multiple MR methods and sensitivity analyses were applied to assess the consistency and robustness of the findings. In addition, the integration of target-related eQTL data, a large pancreatic cancer GWAS dataset, 731 circulating immune phenotypes, and PheWAS information provided a broad basis for investigating the relationships among gene expression, systemic immune variation, and pancreatic cancer risk.

Several limitations should be considered. Genetically proxied expression reflects long-term biological variation and may not fully reproduce the acute, dose-dependent, and tissue-specific effects of pharmacological kinase inhibition. The available eQTL data may also provide limited information on gene regulation within pancreatic tumor cells, stromal cells, and local immune populations. In addition, the immune phenotypes were derived from peripheral blood flow cytometry datasets and therefore mainly represent systemic immune variation, without directly measuring Treg infiltration or functional activity in pancreatic tumor tissue. Given the large number of circulating immune phenotypes examined, the immune-related associations and estimated mediation proportions are best viewed within an exploratory screening framework. The absence of colocalization and pancreatic tissue-specific molecular analyses also limits the interpretation of the gene-level associations. Finally, the genetic datasets included in the present study were predominantly restricted to individuals of European ancestry. Differences in allele frequencies, linkage disequilibrium patterns, and immune-related genetic architecture across ancestral populations may limit the generalizability of the present findings, and validation in more diverse populations will be important in future studies. Future studies incorporating colocalization, tumor-specific eQTL data, single-cell analyses, and functional experiments may help clarify the biological relevance of RAF1- and MAPK8-related pathways in pancreatic cancer.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study provides genetic evidence supporting associations of RAF1 and MAPK8 expression with pancreatic cancer risk. The two-step MR analysis further suggested that a circulating activated and resting CD4+ Treg-related phenotype may contribute to a small proportion of these associations, indicating a possible involvement of systemic immune regulation. Further tissue-specific, functional, and pharmacological studies may help clarify the biological relevance of RAF1- and MAPK8-related pathways in pancreatic cancer and their pharmacological implications in the context of encorafenib.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

This study utilized publicly available summary-level data from previous genome-wide association studies (GWAS). No new data were collected, and all data were publicly accessible; therefore, no additional ethical approval was required. The original studies that generated these datasets obtained ethical approval from their respective institutional review boards, and all participants provided informed consent. Details of the ethical approvals can be found in the original publications.
