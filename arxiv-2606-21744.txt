Reactor operation induced thermal effects on neutron flux measurements using 3 He neutron detectors at a TRIGA Mk II research reactor
Sebastian Dorer, Clemens Trunner, Robert Bergmann, Dieter Hainz, Erwin Jericha, Stephan Sponar, Thomas Stummer, Mario Villa

Abstract
A variation of the measured neutron flux using 3 He detectors at a TRIGA Mk II nuclear research reactor during steady-state operation is reported in this article. The observed effect shows a statistically significant anti-correlation between the temperature in the reactor pool and recorded neutron counts. Following reactor start-up under nominal operating conditions, the effect occurs for a specific time period until thermal equilibrium and a constant neutron count rate are reached. Simultaneous neutron and temperature measurements are performed in different configurations around the reactor in order to gain qualitative insights about the temperature dependent count rate behavior. Possible origins of the effect are identified and further measurements for a more detailed investigation are suggested.

Introduction

During neutron count rate measurements over the period of a full reactor day,
7\text{\,}\mathrm{h}
, at the TRIGA Mk II nuclear research reactor at the Atominstitut of TU Wien (ATI) and the TRIGA Center, a statistically significant reduction of the count rate of around 1.5-3.0% at nominal
250\text{\,}\mathrm{kW}
operation over a duration of about
2\text{\,}\mathrm{h}2.5\text{\,}\mathrm{h}
after the reactor start-up was observed.
Following the first observation, neutron rate measurements at different beam tubes around the reactor were performed, in order to investigate the reduction effect in more detail.
Furthermore, after contemplating about plausible explanations for the behavior, temperature data inside the reactor pool was taken simultaneously, leading to the discovery of the apparent anti-correlation between the count rate and temperature, with the count rate decreasing with increasing temperature.

The aim of the paper is the description of the observed effect, to present already performed measurements and their results, as well as providing first conclusions and suggestions for future experiments in order to further investigate the root cause of the effect.
Our first results indicate the possible requirement of the correction of this effect for high precision experiments performed at TRIGA Mk II reactors (examples are provided in
Section

4
).

2
Experimental Setup

2.1
TRIGA Mk II Reactor

The TRIGA reactor was invented and designed by General Atomic. It originated with the TRIGA Mk I reactor, following the idea of building a reactor that
“could be given to a bunch of high school children to play with, without any fear that they would get hurt”
[
1
]
. This objective was achieved through the use of Uranium-zirconium hydride (
U-ZrH
) as nuclear fuel.
The inherent TRIGA safety derives from a strong negative temperature coefficient. This behavior results of the tight temperature coupling between uranium and hydrogen in the
U-ZrH
fuel, with hydrogen providing a substantial share of neutron moderation. Consequently, thermal power is directly tied to the system’s moderation capacity and self-limits as temperature rises. To improve neutron economy, the core is surrounded by a graphite reflector. Both the core and the reflector are immersed in a water pool, which not only removes heat but also acts as an additional moderator.
The TRIGA Mk II design is based on its predecessor and extends it by incorporating a graphite thermal column and neutron beam ports penetrating the graphite reflector. In the Mk II design, the graphite reflector fulfills several functions already present in the Mk I, primarily the reduction of neutron losses. This is particularly important with respect to the power density resulting from the special fuel type and the associated safety requirements. A further, newly introduced function of the graphite reflector in the TRIGA Mk II design is its use as an additional moderator for the beam tubes.
As shown in
Figure

1
, the four beam tubes, as well as the beam port for the dry irradiation room and the thermal column, terminate in or at the graphite reflector depending on the desired neutron spectrum. In particular, the tangential beam tube passing the core receives its neutrons via scattering processes in the graphite reflector.
To realize the concept of a reactor suitable for training, research, and isotope production, both the TRIGA Mk I and Mk II employ an open reactor pool with natural convection cooling through the core. The deposited thermal energy is removed by extracting pool water from the bottom of the tank, cooling the water, and reinserting it at approximately half the height of the pool
[
2
]
.

Measurements

To investigate the cause of the decreasing neutron count rate, measurements were performed with different experimental configurations.
The reactor was operated at power levels of
5\text{\,}\mathrm{kW}
,

During reactor operation, the primary water cooling circle was active for all measurements except for two specific runs at
5\text{\,}\mathrm{kW}
and

without cooling.
Measurements were performed at the tangential beam tube (

), therefore allowing to investigate if the observed reduction effect is specific to one beam tube or a global effect observable at all measurement positions.

For the runs at the tangential beam tube and irradiation room (except at
5\text{\,}\mathrm{kW}
), the initial neutron beam is reflected once on a highly oriented pyrolytic graphite (HOPG) crystal acting as a monochromator.
Therefore, only thermal neutrons of certain wavelengths fulfilling Bragg’s reflection condition
[
11
]
are reflected towards the neutron detector, e.g.
0.88\text{\,}\mathrm{\text{\AA}}1.32\text{\,}\mathrm{\text{\AA}}2.64\text{\,}\mathrm{\text{\AA}}

During each run, multiple consecutive neutron count rate measurements were taken, as well as additional temperature measurements for the runs at the tangential beam tube and one run at the irradiation room at
5\text{\,}\mathrm{kW}

Neutron counts were acquired for 60 seconds per measurement and the temperature sensors were also read out every 60 seconds.
Combining the results from different runs allows to draw first conclusions about the possible origin of the effect.

4
Results

The measurements under nominal reactor operation conditions (
250\text{\,}\mathrm{kW}
and active cooling) show that the reduction of the count rate occurs on a time scale of about
2\text{\,}\mathrm{h}2.5\text{\,}\mathrm{h}
after the reactor start-up.
This time scale introduces an upper limit to the duration of individual measurements during one run of about
10\text{\,}\mathrm{min}
, to still provide sufficient temporal resolution to observe the reduction effect.
However, in order to achieve the desired statistical uncertainty of less than 1%, the count rate per time interval must still be high enough.
Therefore, a tradeoff exists between the temporal resolution of the reduction effect and statistical uncertainty per time bin.
Ideally, for more precise investigations of the effect, these measurements should be performed with detectors with large active detection volumes, high detection efficiency and low deadtime.
The influence of the reduction effect on the integral of the counts over a whole reactor day is less than 1%, however, due to the percent-level change of the count rate from the reactor start-up to the plateau region over a span of less than
2.5\text{\,}\mathrm{h}
, it may have consequences for high precision experiments relying on stable conditions, e.g. scattering experiments using a scanning technique
[
13
]
or neutron interferometry experiments which require stability during the acquisition of the complete interference pattern
[
14
]
.

By comparing the temporal behavior of the neutron count rate and temperature measured by the sensor in the reflector groove for runs with and without cooling (
Figure

2
), the data does not only show a statistically significant decrease of the measured neutron count rate, but also the clear anti-correlation between the two measured quantities.
This hints to a temperature dependency of the count rate reduction.
Due to the high neutron count rates (at all measurement positions other than radial beam tube) in the order of
\mathcal{O}(10^{4})
per second and a measurement duration of 60 seconds, the statistical uncertainty from the underlying Poisson process is
\epsilon_{\text{stat.}}\approx 0.12\%
, and therefore one order of magnitude smaller than the observed count rate reduction of around
1\%-5\%
presented in this work.

Since the count rate reduction described above is observed for measurements at different locations at the reactor (see
Figures

2
,
4
and
3
), this strongly hints at an intrinsic reactor-wide effect and not an effect specific to only one beam tube.
The thermal column is the only position at which the effect was not observable at
250\text{\,}\mathrm{kW}
(see
Figure

5
) possibly due to its weak expression, which makes it invisible due to the uncertainties of the measurements. This can be explained to the significantly different construction of the thermal column compared with the other beam tubes. The difference lies in the fact that the thermal column is not a beam tube in the conventional sense. Instead, it consists of a graphite-brick assembly directly located at the reflector and has dimensions of approximately
$1\text{\,}\mathrm{m}$\times$1\text{\,}\mathrm{m}$\times$1.7\text{\,}\mathrm{m}$

[
2
]
. For the experiment described a collimator configuration was used, where the central graphite brick was replaced by a conical collimator
[
15
]
. Consequently, only about 35 cm of graphite remains along the beam direction. The combination of this graphite path length and the substantial dimensions of the thermal column enables the neutrons to reach near-thermal equilibrium with the ambient temperature before exiting the collimator, thereby considerably diminishing the effect.
In addition, the observation that the effect does not occur at the thermal column excludes the reactor instrumentation as an origin.

The measurements at the irradiation room with a reactor power of
5\text{\,}\mathrm{kW}
without cooling (see
Figure

3
b) show a constant (within the margins of error) neutron count rate and a slight temperature increase of
\Delta T<$1\text{\,}\mathrm{K}$

\left(\approx$0.0051(\pm 0.0002)\text{\,}\mathrm{K}\text{\,}{\mathrm{min}}^{-1}$\right)
, which is depicted in
Figure

3
b.
This observation additionally supports the theory of a temperature dependency of the measured neutron count rate, since the effect occurred at the same beam tube for a larger temperature increase (see
Figure

3
a).

The reactor design suggests that three distinct temperatures of the following components could induce these changes in the neutron count rate:

1.

Fuel elements

2.

Cooling water surrounding the reactor core

3.

Graphite reflector

The observed changes in the neutron count rate may not reflect actual variations in total neutron flux, but could instead result from a shift of the neutron energy spectrum toward higher energies due to a temperature increase.
This would influence the measured count rate, since the detection efficiency for thermal neutrons of the
3
He detectors used for this paper strongly depends on the neutron energy (
\propto 1/\sqrt{E}
)
[
16
]
.

Provided that the accumulated number of events is large enough to ensure the necessary statistical certainty required, in combination with a measurement duration short enough to allow for the temporal resolution of the reduction effect, a time of flight measurement would be a suitable experiment to investigate such a shift of the neutron energy distribution.

5
Conclusion and Outlook

In summary, utilizing
3
He neutron detectors, a neutron count rate reduction of
1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}3.0\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
over a duration of about
2\text{\,}\mathrm{h}2.5\text{\,}\mathrm{h}
following the reactor start-up was observed during nominal
250\text{\,}\mathrm{kW}
operation at the TRIGA Mk II research reactor at TRIGA Center Atominstitut, TU Wien, Austria.
Simultaneous temperature measurements inside the reactor pool provide a clear temperature dependence of the rate reduction, with the rate decreasing with increasing temperature.
Furthermore, by performing the measurements at different beam tubes around the reactor, the effect is shown to occur at all beam tubes reactor-wide, with the exception of the thermal column.

Since the reactor is a complex coupled thermal system, our current results and limitations do not allow the concrete determination of the effect’s origin.
Searching for options to decouple contributions of individual parts (e.g. cooling water and graphite reflector) to the temperature effect is critical to further progress.

If the observed behavior is present at all TRIGA Mk II reactors, it could have consequences for high-precision experiments relying on the measurement of relative count rates.
The investigations performed for this paper lead to the conclusion that the described behavior depends on the specific reactor design, characteristics of the installation, power level, operational mode and schedule.
Thus, the authors suggest to reproduce the current results presented in this paper at other TRIGA Mk II reactors.
This would also provide insights into possible contributions of the reactor instrumentation.
Additionally, the implementation of neutron monitors for high-precision experiments is recommended, in order to allow for a correction of the effect during data analysis.

The following suggestions for future measurements are proposed by the authors.
A detailed study of the long-time count rate and temperature behavior, which is not possible with the 7 hour per day, 5 days per week operating schedule at TRIGA Center Atominstitut.
Precise temperature measurements of the cooling water across multiple position inside the reactor pool, the fuel elements, the graphite reflector and the thermal column.
Different reactor operation modes with the goal of independently influencing the temperature of the fuel elements, cooling water and graphite reflector (e.g. by operating the reactor at a low power level of a few
\mathrm{kW}
and manually heating the water).
As described above, the apparent count rate reduction could also result from a shift of the thermal neutron energy spectrum towards slightly higher energies, reducing the detection efficiency of the
3
He detectors.
Thus, a time-dependent investigation of the thermal neutron energy spectrum at different beam tubes via time of flight experiments would be highly valuable.

If the described effect occurs at all TRIGA Mk II reactors, a detailed investigation and in-depth understanding of the effect would be of utmost importance, justifying the investment of additional research resources.
