Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

Road transportation accounts for 23% of our nation's total energy consumption, 59% of our
petroleum consumption and 22% of our total emissions. Electric vehicles (EVs) have much
higher well-to-wheel efficiency compared to gasoline vehicles and can reduce our dependence on
oil, minimize emissions, improve local air quality and provide a platform for technological
innovation and economic growth. While major progress has been made in the development of
electric vehicles, their penetration remains under 1 The main hurdles in their widespread
adoption are high cost, limited range and long charging times, due to limitations in battery
technology. An approach to overcome these hurdles is to substantially reduce the on-board
energy storage and instead deliver power wirelessly to the vehicle while it is in motion dynamic
wireless power transfer (WPT). For EV charging applications, researchers have traditionally
focused on inductive WPT. An issue with inductive WPT systems is that for magnetic flux
guidance and shielding, they require ferrite cores, making them expensive, bulky and difficult to
embed in the roadway. Under an ARPA-E IDEAS grant, the CU Boulder team has recently

 1

developed an alternative approach to WPT based on capacitive coupling that is much smaller,
lighter, less expensive, and easier to embed in the roadway than inductive WPT; and have
demonstrated a 1.2-kW 6.78-MHz version of this capacitive WPT system on a stationary
platform. Under this seed grant program, preliminary work will be carried out on a dynamic
capacitive WPT system to prepare a strong proposal for the ARPA-E OPEN 2018 FOA.

Summary of Research Results:

Task 1: Estimate the Market Potential for Dynamic Power Transfer

The Automotive Deployment Option Projection Tool (ADOPT) will be used to assess the
market potential for dynamic electric power transfer. It trades-off vehicle attributes of all the
existing vehicle options and additional hypothetical vehicles to estimate technology impacts on
future vehicle sales, petroleum use, and emissions. ADOPT will be used to estimate the sales
and benefits of dynamic roadway electrification.

While consumers may consider many different vehicle attributes when deciding to purchase a
new vehicle, ADOPT assumes that their ultimate decision is primarily driven by a much
smaller subset of key factors. The framework varies the importance of these attributes by
income to account for the greater sensitivity to cost at lower levels of income, the higher rates
of purchase at higher incomes, and the changing level of income over time. Their importance
was set to match historical sales. Most of the existing vehicle options were included to enable
extensive validation and provide a complete representation of the market. ADOPT's stock
model uses the sales to estimate fuel use and emissions. It captures key aspects, including the
changing vehicle miles traveled (VMT) as vehicles age, new model option creation, and scrap
rates. A simple graphical user interface (GUI) provides access to most inputs and assumptions
used by the model, manages scenario setups, and displays results.

ADOPT will model dynamic power transfer vehicles by adding PEVs that have improved range,
lower fuel cost, and higher purchase prices. Their attributes will be propagated to additional
platforms and adjusted for other technology improvements. The market share of dynamic power
transfer vehicles will be evaluated for several fuel prices and levels of technology improvements.

Evaluation Tool

NREL conducted market acceptance analysis to strengthen a proposal for additional funding.
NREL used its vehicle choice model, the Automotive Deployment Option Projection Tool
(ADOPT), to help evaluate the potential of new technologies.

Evaluation Scenarios

To evaluate the impacts of DPT technology, NREL set up two scenarios: (1) Base scenario, and
(2) DPT scenario. In both scenarios, the market share of different vehicle types was predicted
from the current year to the year of 2050. The vehicle types included in the evaluation are
conventional vehicles (CVs), plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PLEVs), electric vehicles (EVs),
hybrid vehicles (HVs) and fuel-cell vehicles (FCVs).

In the Base scenario, DPT technology is not available.

In the DPT scenario, DPT technology is assumed to be available starting from 2019. To utilize
DPT, vehicles need to be equipped with DPT charging components which can increase vehicle
cost. Researchers estimated the additional vehicle cost associated with DPT to range between
$25 per kilowatts and $43 per kilowatts (Colorado State University 2018). The conservative
assumption of $43 per kilowatts was used. Choosing the highest DPT vehicle cost is expected to
bring relative conservative evaluation results which provide the lower boundary of DPT benefits.
On the other hand, as more vehicles benefit from DPT technology, more travel is made with DPT
vehicles. Studies (Aaron Brooker, Eric Wood, Evan Burton, Jeff Gonder, Joann Wang, Mike
Simpson 2013) showed that when 1% of the roadway is covered with DPT facilities, 17% of
total VMTs would be made by DPT vehicles. Therefore, in this evaluation, the percentage of the
VMT made with DPT vehicles was assumed to gradually increase from 0% to 17% between
2025 and 2040 and remain at 17% until the end of evaluation period (year of 2050).

 3

In addition to the scenario settings, several assumptions were made. One of the key influencing
factors that affect people’s vehicle purchase choice is the fuel price. In the evaluation, gas and
electricity are two major fuels. Their prices were assumed to be the reference prices in 2018
Annual Energy Outlook projected by Energy Information Administration (EIA) (Energy
Information Administration 2018). The commercial use price was chosen to represent the
electricity price of both traditional charging approaches and the DPT technology. The electricity
emission rate was also assumed to gradually decrease to 17% of current year in 2050.

Evaluation Results

Two scenarios described above were implemented in ADOPT. The impacts of DPT technology
on future vehicle sales, petroleum use, and emissions were evaluated and discussed in this
section.

Annual sales by powertrain

The future annual sales of different vehicle types were predicted until the year of 2050. The sales
of each vehicle types in the Base scenario and the DPT scenario were shown in Figure 1.
Compared to the Base scenario, EVs have a much larger market share in the DPT scenario. That
means the availability of DPT technology has the potential to increase the market share of EVs.

Best selling vehicles

To have a better understanding of the impacts of DPT technology as well as the reason of
increased EV market share, the best-selling vehicle of each vehicle type were also examined.
Figure 2 and 3 show the information of the best-selling vehicle in each vehicle type. Each
narrow-stacked bar represents the vehicle’s generalized cost which is a weighted summation of a
group of vehicle features such as price, range, fuel cost, and other factors. The taller the bar is,
the less attractive the vehicle is. When comparing the relative height of bars, EVs are more
competitive in the DPT scenario than in the Base scenario.

When looking at the performance of specific EV features, as shown in Figure 2, EV’s price was
reduced from $33,404 in the Base scenario to $28,466 in the DPT scenario. On the other hand, as
shown in Figure 3, EV’s range was increased from 208 miles in the Base scenario to 411 miles in
the DPT scenario. The cheaper price and longer range brought by DPT technology explain the
increased annual sale of EVs. An additional benefit was reflected in EV’s battery size. In the
year of 2050, EV’s battery size in the Base scenario is 72 kilowatts-hour. The battery size was
decreased to 44 kilowatts-hour in the DPT scenario. DPT technology enables EVs to be charged
during traveling thus the battery size can be reduced.

Conclusions

NREL used ADOPT to estimate the sales and benefits of dynamic roadway electrification. A
base scenario and a DPT scenario were evaluated and compared to show the impacts of DPT
technology on future vehicle sales, petroleum use, and CO2e emissions.

Evaluation results show that DPT technology has the potential to increase sales of EVs with
additional benefits of EVs’ lower price, range increase and smaller battery size. In addition, as
more vehicles can benefit from DPT technology, the total petroleum consumption and total
CO2e emissions are also expected to decrease.
