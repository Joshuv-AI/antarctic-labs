1 Introduction
Accurate materials’ equations of state (EOS) are essential for understanding materials properties as well as for use in
multiphysics simulations. In particular, hydrodynamics simulations are based on three fundamental conservation laws
(mass, momentum and energy) that form an under-determined system of equations [12]. The equation of state serves as
an additional closure relation between thermodynamic variables for a given material that enables numerical hydrodynamics
simulation. In this report, we focus on the development of an ab initio EOS for the body centered cubic (BCC) phase of
Vanadium (V) for eventual integration into a multiphase EOS in the OpenSesame EOS database [21, 1].
 The fundamental quantity in OpenSesame is the Helmholtz free energy, which is decomposed into three parts [30],

                                              F (V, T ) = Fcold (V ) + Fvib (V, T ) + Fel (V, T )                        (1)
Fcold (V ) is the “cold curve”, or static lattice energy at T = 0. Fvib (V, T ) and Fel (V, T ) are both derived from thermal
excitations of the material: Fvib captures free energy associated with vibrational motion of atoms while Fel captures free
energy associated with electrons entering excited states. The quantized vibrational states within a material have relatively
small spacings between energy levels, and as such, vibrational excitations are important at intermediate temperatures such
as near the melting point. Electronic excited states, on the other hand, have a relatively large energy gap between states
and thus become dominant at very high temperatures near the plasma. In this work, we use density functional theory
(DFT) to generate ab initio estimates of each free energy term in equation (1) over a limited range of volumes and
temperatures for the BCC lattice.
 Although in theory one could create a fully ab initio equation of state for a material via DFT, these calculations
are too expensive to run over the entire region of thermodynamic space of interest. As such, it is necessary to fit ab
initio free energy calculations to a number of empirical models for interpolation and extrapolation beyond the sampled
points in phase space. These empirical models also provide a convenient model space in which a small number (∼ 10)
parameters can be fit to a combination of DFT and experimental data. Furthermore, these empirical models provide a
computationally tractable way to perform uncertainty quantification (UQ) on this parameter set via Markov Chain Monte
Carlo (MCMC) methods [15]. As such, after computing ab initio free energies, the fitting of this data to a number of
empirical models was investigated.
 This report is structured as follows: in section 2 the details of the DFT calculations will be described. Convergence
studies for DFT parameters was investigated for both the Perdew Burke Ernzerhof (PBE) [27] and Perdew Becke Ex-
change - Solids (PBS) functionals [28] as described in sections 2.2 and 2.3. Using these converged parameters, geometry
optimzation was then performed to find the ground state lattice geometry for each functional. As described in section
3, the lattice parameters of each ground state geometry were then scaled to create ab initio E(V ) curves. These curves
were fit to the Burch-Murnaghan model [2, 25]. The cold curve fit to PBE functional DFT calculations best fit the
experimental data. Section 5 explores the vibrational free energy. For the vibrational free energy, harmonic, second order
force constants were computed from DFT calculations. Additionally, the vibrational free energy was fit to a Debye model;
as the vibrational energies at more volumes and temperatures are computed, a quasiharmonic Grüniesen will be fit to the
data [30]. Finally, section 6 describes how Fermi smearing was used to compute the ab initio electronic free energies at a
variety of temperatures and volumes. Typically, in OpenSesame, Fel is computed using Thomas-Fermi-Dirac theory [5];
however, in this report the accuracy of the TFD electronic free energy is compared to the DFT electronic free energy.
It is found that TFD performs poorly compared to DFT and at high temperatures even does not show the qualitatively
correct dependence on volume.

2 Methods
2.1 Details on DFT calculations
DFT calculations were done using the Vienna ab-initio Simulation Package (VASP) version 5.4.4 [16, 17, 3, 18].The
Projector Augmented Wave (PAW) [16, 17, 3, 18] V sv GW potential in VASP was used which contains thirteen valence
electrons in a 3s2 3p6 3d5 configuration. To begin with, the convergence of the energy of a given lattice configuration with
respect to the plane wave energy cutoff and k-mesh density was investigated for both the PBESol and PBE functionals as
described in sections 2.2 and 2.3. The Methfessel-Paxton method [24] was used for interpolation of the electronic density.
After convergence, the equilibrium BCC lattice geometry was found for each functional by performing lattice relaxation.
Cold curves were then computed via changing the scaling of the unit scale to match desired volumes. Phonon calculations
were performed using the PBEsol functional using the Phonopy software [32] to extract forces from VASP and to compute
dispersions, phonon densities of states, and vibrational thermodynamics. Temperature dependent electronic excitations
were computed via changing the effective fermi smearing width.

2.2 PBE Convergence
The V sv GW PAW potential was used for the PBE calculations. Convergence calculations were performed and a k-mesh
of 44 × 44 × 44 and an energy cutoff of 1000 eV were selected. Graphs for the convergences are shown in Figure 1.

2.3 PBEsol Convergence
First, the convergence of the plane-wave energy cutoff was investigated fixing the k-mesh density to be 12 × 12 × 12. The
target convergence for the energy was 0.1 meV/atom, which was reached at a 1000 eV cutoff (Figure 2). After a 1000
eV planewave cutoff was selected, the convergence of the energy with respect to k mesh density was evaluated. Due to
the symmetry of the BCC unit cell, a symetric k mesh was selected to have the same number of k-mesh in along each
direction - e.g. 32 × 32 × 32. Due to the oscillatory nature of the k-mesh convergence, a 32 × 32 × 32 k-mesh was selected.
This large density of the k-mesh greatly increased calculation time, but is necessary for vanadium due to the large density
of electronic states at the fermi level as well as the Kohn anomaly present in the phonon dispersion spectra [22]. After
convergence of these parameters, the lattice was relaxed to find the equilibrium volume for the BCC unit cell with the
PBEsol functional.

After the enthalpy is calculated, different solid phase structures can be compared. The one with the lowest enthalpy
corresponds to the solid phase present at equilibrium. A rhombohedral phase was discovered to have a lower enthalpy at
high pressures. The literature is inconsistent with reported values from theoretical calculations and experimental results
as shown in Figure 6.
 Of particular note are the discoveries revealed in Stevenson et al. [31]. In that work DAC experiments were performed
for nonhydrostatic powder data and Single-crystal data. The nonhydrostatic powder data they measured are consistent
with previously published works and is very inconsistent with the rhombohedral phase structures predicted by theoretical
calculations. Their single-crystal data does have two rhombohedral phases at high pressures with the rhombohedral
angle decreasing then increasing with higher pressures which is opposite of the theoretical predictions. In addition the
rhombohedral angles are much smaller than that of the theoretical calculations. The opposite behavior of the rhombohedral
angle and the significantly smaller changes in magnitude of the rhombohedral angle can be seen in the first graph in Figure
6.

5 Phonons
As described in section 2, Phonopy was used in conjunction with VASP to compute force constants in a harmonic
approximation. Once again, convergence of the phonon density of states with respect to several parameters was examined:
planewave energy cutoff, supercell k-mesh density, and supercell size (Figure 7). First, planewave energy cutoff was
evaluated with the a 3 × 3 × 3 supercell with a 4 × 4 × 4 k-mesh density and reference calculations on the primitive unit cell
with 12 × 12 × 12 k-mesh density. Based on the convergence of the density of states, it was found that using a plane wave
cutoff of 700 eV was sufficient. With a 700 eV planewave cutoff, the k-mesh density of the supercell was investigated. Due
to the aforementioned Kohn anomaly, a 10 × 10 × 10 supercell k-mesh was required for a 3 × 3 × 3 supercell. Finally, fixing
the planewave cutoff to be 700 eV and using a supercell k-mesh density of ≥ 30 ( e.g. a 8 × 8 × 8 supercell k-mesh for a
4 × 4 × 4 supercell ), the convergence of the phonon DoS with respect to supercell size was investigated. Unfortunately,
convergence could not be achieved due to computational expense. Interestingly, imaginary frequency modes observed for
phonons calculated in the 4 × 4 × 4 and 5 × 5 × 5 supercells, but not for phonons with 3 × 3 × 3 or 6 × 6 × 6 supercells.

6 Electronic Contribution
6.1 Implementation
For the electronic contribution certain values in the VASP input files were changed. For the cold curve, a order 2 Methfessel-
Paxton method [24] was used with a smearing width of 0.1 eV. For the temperature dependence Fermi smearing was used.
The energy range between maximum and minimum energy values was set to increase resolution for the electronic density
of state. Volume scaling from V /V0 = 0.5 to V /V0 = 1.2 were used and the temperature ranged from 0 K to 10 000 K.
 The VASP output file has three energies reported: free energy, internal energy, and energy as temperature approaches
zero. The values that VASP gives for these settings automatically combine the cold curve contribution and the electronic
contribution for the free energy. To get the electronic contribution to the free energy by itself without the cold curve
the energy value from the VASP output file that reports energy as temperature approaches zero calculated at 300 K was
subtracted from each value with the corresponding volume. The values for energy as temperature approaches zero for 300
K and the free energy with the before mentioned Methfessel-Paxton method for the cold curve were very close but not
the same. Depending on the volume the difference between them is on the order of 0.00001 eV. The electronic density of
state was graphed from the VASP density of state output file for different volumes shown in Figure 10. The temperature
and volume dependence of the electronic contribution to the free energy is shown in Figure 11.

6.2 Comparison of Thomas-Fermi-Dirac and density functional theory
A comparison was done between the Thomas-Fermi-Dirac (TFD) [9] model with the PBE model. The comparison is
shown if Figure 12. The TFD model underpredicts the PBE functional by a significant amount (ranging from 30-60% for
the most part) as temperature and volume increase.

7 Conclusion
This work achieved several key objectives toward the development of an equation of state (EOS) for vanadium. First,
a comparison of exchange-correlation functionals was conducted, revealing that the PBE functional provides the best
agreement with experimental data based on the fit of the cold curve (Figure 3). Within each functional, convergence
studies were performed for key computational parameters, particularly the plane-wave cutoff energy and k -point mesh
density (Figures 2 and 1).
 The potential appearance of a rhombohedral distortion was also investigated. The distortion angle was found to be
very slight, suggesting that this phase can be effectively modeled as a body-centered cubic (BCC) structure without
introducing significant errors into the EOS.
 In addition, ab initio estimates of the three components of the SESAME free energy decomposition (Figure 1) were
obtained for a limited range of densities and temperatures. Phonon contributions to the vibrational free energy, Fvib , were
calculated within the harmonic approximation using Phonopy and the PBEsol functional. The resulting phonon dispersion
curves showed acceptable agreement with experimental X-ray scattering data, while the computed density of states (DoS)
demonstrated reasonable consistency with low-temperature inelastic neutron scattering measurements (Figure 8).
 Future work will include computing phonons with the PBE functional in the harmonic approximation and exploring
anharmonic effects using self-consistent stochastic temperature-dependent effective potential (sTDEP) methods [14].
 The electronic free energy for vanadium was computed as a function of temperature and volume using Fermi smearing
in VASP (Figures 11 and 10). This ab initio Fel was compared to the usual TFD model currently used in OpenSesame
(Figures 12). It was found that while TFD shows the correct qualitative, directional dependence on T and V, it severely
underestimates the electronic free energy by 30%. This suggests that a fast, analytic version of the TFD method will not
introduce significant error compared to the typical time-consuming self consistent scheme currently used for TFD. That
is, the error introduced by using an analytic approximation will not be great relative to the error between TFD and the
ab initio Fel .
 After completing the additional phonon calculations at more temperatures and volumes, this phonon data will be
fit to quasiharmonic debye model. After this, the parameters in the empirical models fit to ab initio data - the Burch
Murnaghan model for Fcold , the Debye model for Fion and the TFD model for Fel - will be further fit to experimental data.
As part of this fitting, uncertainty quanitfication will be performed in this model space to generate a finalized equation of
state for use in OpenSesame.
