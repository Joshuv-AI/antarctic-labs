1.
Introduction

The three-dimensional incompressible Euler equations

(1)

\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=0,\qquad\nabla\cdot u=0,

are the inviscid model for an ideal incompressible fluid. The possibility of finite-time singularity formation for smooth three-dimensional Euler flows has been a central problem in mathematical fluid dynamics. The Beale–Kato–Majda criterion
[
3
]
identifies the time-integrability of the maximum vorticity as a decisive breakdown criterion. This makes the geometry of vorticity amplification, vortex stretching, and nonlocal velocity recovery central to any proposed blow-up mechanism.

The axisymmetric-with-swirl class is one of the most important reduced three-dimensional geometries in which the Euler equations still retain a genuinely three-dimensional vortex-stretching mechanism. In cylindrical coordinates
(r,\theta,z)
, an axisymmetric velocity with swirl has the form

(2)

u(r,z,t)=u^{r}(r,z,t)e_{r}+u^{\theta}(r,z,t)e_{\theta}+u^{z}(r,z,t)e_{z}.

The angular momentum

(3)

\Gamma=ru^{\theta}

is transported by the meridional flow, while the vorticity-ratio variable

(4)

G=\frac{\omega^{\theta}}{r}

is forced by the axial derivative of
\Gamma^{2}
. Precisely,

(5)

D_{t}\Gamma=0,\qquad D_{t}G=r^{-4}\partial_{z}(\Gamma^{2}),

where

D_{t}=\partial_{t}+u^{r}\partial_{r}+u^{z}\partial_{z}.

Thus the swirl is transported, but its gradient can force growth of
G
. This is the fundamental source mechanism studied in this paper.

The boundary hyperbolic scenario for axisymmetric Euler with swirl was brought into sharp focus by the numerical work of Luo and Hou
[
12
,
13
]
, who studied the three-dimensional axisymmetric Euler equations in a periodic cylinder with solid boundary and reported strong numerical evidence for a potential finite-time singularity at the boundary. Related one-dimensional and reduced models inspired by this mechanism have been studied rigorously, including finite-time blow-up for Hou–Luo-type models
[
7
,
6
]
. The broader landscape also includes Elgindi’s finite-time singularity formation for
C^{1,\alpha}
Euler solutions
[
10
]
, boundary blow-up constructions in related settings
[
5
]
, and small-scale creation mechanisms for incompressible Euler in bounded domains
[
11
]
.

Recent work of Chen and Hou gives a rigorous boundary singularity result for smooth three-dimensional Euler flows by a different, computer-assisted, nearly self-similar framework
[
4
]
. The goal of the present paper is different and more structural: it isolates a direct kernel–packet mechanism in the original axisymmetric variables and develops a self-contained cluster-invariant proof of hyperbolic amplification.

1.1.
Relation with the companion Navier–Stokes manuscripts.

This paper is the first part of a two-paper Euler series on inviscid packet amplification. It is also meant to be read alongside two separate Navier–Stokes manuscripts of the author, currently under review, in which a five-dimensional lift and direct first-threshold method are developed for the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes global-existence problem with smooth data
[
1
,
2
]
. Those Navier–Stokes papers are cited here only for conceptual context. No theorem, lemma, or estimate in the present Euler series depends on them, and the Euler conclusions would stand or fail independently of the Navier–Stokes manuscripts.

The point of mentioning the Navier–Stokes companion work is structural rather than logical. The same packet language appears in both settings, but the mechanisms are opposite. In the Navier–Stokes papers, the five-dimensional lift, full-Dirichlet visibility, viscous coercivity, and first-threshold continuation mechanism are used to suppress the packet configurations that could otherwise concentrate. In the present inviscid Euler paper, the dissipative part is absent and the side-wall Dirichlet kernel creates a positive hyperbolic feedback loop for a swirl-gradient packet. Thus the two series are complementary: the Navier–Stokes manuscripts describe how the viscous equations prevent first-threshold packet amplification, while the Euler papers isolate inviscid geometries in which packet amplification can persist and lead to singularity formation.

The central object is an active boundary packet near the side wall of the periodic cylinder

(6)

\Omega=\{(r,\theta,z):0<r<1,\ z\in\mathbb{T}\},

with impermeable boundary condition

(7)

u^{r}(1,z,t)=0.

Near the side wall we set

(8)

x=1-r,\qquad y=z.

The candidate singular point is
(x,y)=(0,0)
, corresponding to
(r,z)=(1,0)
. The active packet is

(9)

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}=\{0<x<\lambda,\ |y|<\lambda\}.

The key elliptic fact is that the axisymmetric recovery can be written in a five-dimensional lifted form. If

(10)

u^{r}=-r\partial_{z}\phi,\qquad u^{z}=2\phi+r\partial_{r}\phi,

then

(11)

-\Delta_{5}\phi=G,\qquad\Delta_{5}=\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{3}{r}\partial_{r}+\partial_{z}^{2}.

The side-wall boundary condition implies that
\phi
is constant on
r=1
, and after normalization we take
\phi|_{r=1}=0
. The boundary compression rate is

(12)

\sigma(t)=-\partial_{z}u^{z}(1,0,t)=-\partial_{z}\partial_{r}\phi(1,0,t).

The side-wall Green-kernel expansion gives the leading effective two-variable kernel

(13)

K_{0}(x,y)=C_{0}\frac{xy}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}},\qquad C_{0}>0.

This kernel is positive for
x>0,y>0
and odd in
y
. Hence, for an odd sign-coherent packet

G(x,-y)=-G(x,y),\qquad G(x,y)\geq 0\quad(y>0),

the local packet contribution creates positive hyperbolic compression.

The rest of the paper turns this observation into a cluster-invariant theorem package. The package contains:

(i)

a side-wall Green-kernel sign/parity expansion;

(ii)

a boundary Biot–Savart sign lemma;

(iii)

construction of smooth exact-odd narrow diagonal initial data;

(iv)

over-compressed dyadic angular cluster scores;

(v)

Dini absorption of same-scale and near-scale fragments;

(vi)

dynamically separated far-tail and global exterior control;

(vii)

integrable affine Campanato control for the full cluster;

(viii)

packetwise shape-error persistence on the active cluster;

(ix)

selection of a uniformly coherent component;

(x)

an explosive Dini–ODE comparison for the coherent component.

The final normalized engine is

(14)

D^{+}A_{*}(t)\gtrsim B_{*}(t)^{2},\qquad D^{+}B_{*}(t)\gtrsim A_{*}(t)B_{*}(t),

where
A_{*}
and
B_{*}
are the aggregate score and source amplitudes of the selected uniformly coherent component. The dyadic cluster is used to absorb fragments and exterior errors; the coherent component is where the scalar blow-up ODE is run.

1.2.
Main theorem

The final result is the following smooth-data boundary singularity theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Smooth exact-odd side-wall Euler blow-up)
.

There exist smooth axisymmetric initial data with swirl in the periodic cylinder

\Omega=\{0<r<1,\ z\in\mathbb{T}\}

satisfying the impermeable boundary condition
u^{r}(1,z,0)=0
and the exact odd symmetries

\Gamma(r,-z,0)=-\Gamma(r,z,0),\qquad G(r,-z,0)=-G(r,z,0),

such that the associated classical Euler solution develops a finite-time boundary singularity. More precisely, for some finite
T_{*}<\infty
,

\limsup_{t\uparrow T_{*}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=\infty.

The singularity forms at the side-wall stagnation point
(r,z)=(1,0)
through the over-compressed dyadic-cluster hyperbolic amplification mechanism developed in this paper.

Proof.

The side-wall Dirichlet parametrix gives the leading positive compression kernel and the shear/strain estimates. Exact odd symmetry gives the identities

\Gamma(1,0,t)=0,\qquad b^{\prime}=\sigma b,\qquad a^{\prime}=\sigma a+2b^{2}.

The narrow diagonal maximal-score argument gives

D^{+}\mathfrak{M}_{\delta_{c}}\geq cB^{2},\qquad B^{\prime}\geq c\mathfrak{M}_{\delta_{c}}B.

The comparison ODE blows up in finite time, and
B(t)\to\infty
implies
\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\to\infty
. This is proved by combining the dyadic-cluster invariance theorem of
Section

12
with the side-wall parametrix estimates of
Appendix

A
.
∎

2.
Physical and geometric interpretation

The mechanism studied here is a boundary version of hyperbolic small-scale creation. The physical loop is

(15)

\boxed{\begin{gathered}\text{odd }G\text{ packet}\Rightarrow\text{boundary compression}\Rightarrow\partial_{z}\Gamma\text{ steepening}\\
\Rightarrow\partial_{z}(\Gamma^{2})\text{ source}\Rightarrow\text{larger }G\text{ packet}.\end{gathered}}

The side wall is essential. The impermeability condition
u^{r}=0
allows a hyperbolic stagnation structure on the wall:

(16)

u^{z}(1,y,t)\approx-\sigma(t)y,\qquad\sigma(t)>0.

This compresses material points toward
y=0
along the boundary. Since
\Gamma
is transported, axial compression amplifies
\partial_{z}\Gamma
. The amplified swirl gradient then feeds
G
through

D_{t}G=r^{-4}\partial_{z}(\Gamma^{2}).

The nonlocal Biot–Savart law converts the odd
G
-packet back into the compression
\sigma
. Thus the packet attempts to close a positive feedback loop.

The geometry differs sharply from viscous no-slip Navier–Stokes. In a no-slip viscous cylinder, the tangential velocity must vanish at the wall, producing Hardy-type boundary coercivity. In inviscid Euler, the natural boundary condition is impermeability, not no-slip. The tangential swirl is allowed at the wall. Consequently, the boundary does not impose the same two-wall vanishing structure. This is why the side-wall Euler packet is a more dangerous inviscid candidate than the no-slip viscous edge-ring packet.

The five-dimensional lift is also geometric. The operator

\Delta_{5}=\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{3}{r}\partial_{r}+\partial_{z}^{2}

is the radial Laplacian in four transverse dimensions plus the axial direction. Near the side wall
r=1
, the wall is locally flat in this lifted five-dimensional picture. The reflected 5D Dirichlet Green function produces, after integrating the three transverse tangential variables, the effective two-variable kernel

K_{0}(x,y)=C_{0}\frac{xy}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}.

This is the mathematical expression of hyperbolic boundary compression.

3.
Basic equations and notation

3.1.
Axisymmetric Euler with swirl

Let
u
be axisymmetric with swirl as in (
2
). The vorticity is

\omega=\nabla\times u=\omega^{r}e_{r}+\omega^{\theta}e_{\theta}+\omega^{z}e_{z},

where

(17)

\omega^{r}=-\partial_{z}u^{\theta},\qquad\omega^{\theta}=\partial_{z}u^{r}-\partial_{r}u^{z},\qquad\omega^{z}=\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(ru^{\theta}).

The incompressibility condition is

(18)

\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(ru^{r})+\partial_{z}u^{z}=0.

The meridional material derivative is

(19)

D_{t}=\partial_{t}+u^{r}\partial_{r}+u^{z}\partial_{z}.

The angular momentum and vorticity-ratio variables are

(20)

\Gamma=ru^{\theta},\qquad G=\frac{\omega^{\theta}}{r}.

They satisfy

(21)

D_{t}\Gamma=0,

and

(22)

D_{t}G=r^{-4}\partial_{z}(\Gamma^{2}).

3.2.
Five-dimensional stream-function recovery

Define
\phi
by

(23)

u^{r}=-r\partial_{z}\phi,\qquad u^{z}=2\phi+r\partial_{r}\phi.

Then (
18
) is automatic, and
\phi
solves

(24)

-\Delta_{5}\phi=G,\qquad\Delta_{5}=\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{3}{r}\partial_{r}+\partial_{z}^{2}.

The natural lifted measure is

(25)

d\mu_{5}=r^{3}drdz.

At the side wall
r=1
, impermeability gives

0=u^{r}(1,z)=-\partial_{z}\phi(1,z).

Thus
\phi(1,z)
is constant in
z
. We normalize this constant to zero:

(26)

\phi(1,z)=0.

3.3.
Flattened side-wall variables

Near
(r,z)=(1,0)
, set

(27)

x=1-r,\qquad y=z.

Then the side wall is
x=0
, the fluid lies in
x>0
, and a side-wall packet of scale
\lambda
is

(28)

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}=\{0<x<\lambda,\ |y|<\lambda\}.

The positive half-packet is

(29)

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{+}=\{0<x<\lambda,\ 0<y<\lambda\}.

The leading hyperbolic kernel is

(30)

K_{0}(x,y)=\frac{xy}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}.

The local hyperbolic mass is

(31)

\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}[G]=\int_{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{+}}K_{0}(x,y)G(1-x,y)\,dx\,dy.

4.
Side-wall Green kernel and hyperbolic parity

Theorem 4.1

(Side-wall Green-kernel sign and parity expansion)
.

Let
\phi
solve

-\Delta_{5}\phi=G,\qquad\phi|_{r=1}=0,

in the periodic cylinder. Let

\sigma=-\partial_{z}u^{z}(1,0)=-\partial_{z}\partial_{r}\phi(1,0).

Then, in flattened coordinates
x=1-r
,
y=z
, the recovery kernel for
\sigma
has the local expansion

(32)

K_{\Omega}(1-x,y)=C_{0}\frac{xy}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}+K_{\rm rem}^{\lambda}(x,y),\qquad C_{0}>0,

inside every sufficiently small side-wall packet
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
. On fixed cones
mx\leq|y|\leq Mx
, the remainder satisfies

(33)

|K_{\rm rem}^{\lambda}(x,y)|\leq C\lambda\frac{|xy|}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}+K_{\rm smooth}^{\lambda}(x,y),

where the smooth term is generated by periodic images, the axis, and global boundary corrections.

Proof.

The local model is the 5D half-space
\mathbb{R}^{5}_{+}=\{x>0\}
. The full-space fundamental solution of
-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}
is
c_{5}|X|^{-3}
. The Dirichlet half-space Green function is obtained by reflection:

G_{D}(X,\Xi)=c_{5}\bigl(|X-\Xi|^{-3}-|X-\Xi^{*}|^{-3}\bigr),

where
\Xi^{*}
is the reflection of
\Xi
across
x=0
. Differentiating at the boundary point produces

\partial_{x}\partial_{y}G_{D}(0;\xi,\zeta,\eta)=C\frac{\xi\zeta}{(\xi^{2}+\zeta^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{7/2}}.

Integrating the transverse variable
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\xi\zeta}{(\xi^{2}+\zeta^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{7/2}}\,d\eta=C_{0}\frac{\xi\zeta}{(\xi^{2}+\zeta^{2})^{2}}.

The difference between the true operator

\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}-\frac{3}{1-x}\partial_{x}

and the flat half-space model is perturbative on scale
\lambda
, producing the error term in (
33
). Periodic images and the axis are separated from the packet and contribute only smooth kernels.
∎

Corollary 4.2

(Boundary Biot–Savart sign)
.

Assume

G(1-x,-y)=-G(1-x,y),\qquad G(1-x,y)\geq 0\quad(y>0),

in
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
. Then

(34)

\sigma\geq c_{0}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}[G]-\operatorname{Tail}_{\lambda}[G]-\operatorname{Smooth}_{\lambda}[G]-C\lambda\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}[G].

In particular, if
\lambda
is sufficiently small and the tail and smooth terms are dominated by the local mass, then
\sigma>0
.

5.
Dyadic tail control and packet reselection

Definition 5.1

(Dyadic tail masses)
.

Let

A_{j}=\mathcal{P}_{2^{j+1}\lambda}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{2^{j}\lambda},\qquad j\geq 1.

Define

\mathcal{H}_{j}[G]=\left|\int_{A_{j}}K_{\Omega}G\,d\mu_{5}\right|.

The exterior tail is

\operatorname{Tail}_{\lambda}[G]\leq\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathcal{H}_{j}[G].

Theorem 5.2

(Hyperbolic tail dominance or reselection)
.

Suppose an active boundary packet
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
is selected by first-threshold maximality among all sign-coherent hyperbolic packets. Then either

(35)

\operatorname{Tail}_{\lambda}[G]\leq\eta\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}[G]

for a fixed small
\eta>0
, or there exists a dyadic shell containing a competing sign-coherent hyperbolic packet at scale comparable to
2^{j}\lambda
. Under first-threshold maximality, the second alternative is excluded.

Proof.

Assume (
35
) fails for the first time. Then some shell satisfies

\mathcal{H}_{j}\geq c_{\beta}\eta 2^{-\beta j}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}.

Cover the shell by a finite-overlap family of boundary packets at scale
2^{j}\lambda
. A covering pigeonhole argument extracts a packet with comparable signed hyperbolic mass unless the shell contribution is incoherent. Incoherent pieces cannot cancel the local sign contribution and are assigned to a defect. Thus any large dyadic tail produces a competing sign-coherent packet, contradicting first-threshold maximality.
∎

6.
Packet–core coupling

Odd symmetry implies
G(1,0,t)=0
. Therefore the relevant core amplitude is not
G(1,0,t)
, but the odd slope

(36)

a(t)=\partial_{z}G(1,0,t).

The normalized hyperbolic amplitude is

(37)

A(t)=\lambda(t)a(t).

Theorem 6.1

(Packet–core coupling for an odd hyperbolic packet)
.

Assume
G
is odd in
z
near
(r,z)=(1,0)
, nonnegative for
z>0
, and satisfies the cone nondegeneracy condition: for fixed constants
0<m<M
,
0<\kappa<1
, and
0<c_{0}<C_{0}
,

(38)

c_{0}a(t)y\leq G(1-x,y,t)\leq C_{0}a(t)y

on the cone

\mathcal{K}_{\kappa\lambda}=\{0<x<\kappa\lambda,\ mx<y<Mx\}.

Assume also that the outer annular contribution is controlled by the cone contribution. Then

(39)

c\lambda(t)a(t)\leq\mathcal{H}_{\lambda(t)}[G(t)]\leq C\lambda(t)a(t).

Equivalently,

\mathcal{H}_{\lambda(t)}[G(t)]\sim A(t).

Proof.

On the cone
y\sim x
,

K_{0}(x,y)\sim x^{-2},\qquad G(1-x,y,t)\sim a(t)y\sim a(t)x.

Thus
K_{0}G\sim a(t)/x
. The width of the cone section at radius
x
is comparable to
x
, so integrating in
y
gives a contribution comparable to
a(t)
. Integrating
x\in(0,\kappa\lambda)
yields
\lambda a(t)
. The upper bound follows in the same way, together with the assumed outer-annular control.
∎

7.
Off-diagonal shear suppression and anisotropy

The dangerous radial-swirl defect is controlled by the ratio

(40)

R=\frac{\partial_{r}\Gamma}{\partial_{z}\Gamma}.

Differentiating
D_{t}\Gamma=0
gives

(41)

D_{t}R=-\partial_{r}u^{z}+(\partial_{z}u^{z}-\partial_{r}u^{r})R+(\partial_{z}u^{r})R^{2}.

The term
\partial_{r}u^{z}
is therefore the leading source that can rotate the swirl gradient from the axial direction into the radial direction.

Theorem 7.1

(Off-diagonal shear suppression in the hyperbolic core)
.

Assume the odd hyperbolic sign class for
G
and the side-wall Green-kernel expansion of
Theorem

4.1
. Then, in the inner core

x+|y|\leq\kappa\lambda,

one has

(42)

|\partial_{r}u^{z}(1-x,y)|\leq C\kappa\sigma+\operatorname{Tail}_{\rm shear}[G]+\operatorname{Smooth}_{\rm shear}[G]+O(\lambda)\sigma.

Proof.

Represent
\partial_{r}u^{z}
by its Green kernel. At the exact center, the leading shear kernel has parity that cancels against odd
G
. Away from the center, Taylor expansion gives a gain
(x+|y|)/\lambda
relative to the compression kernel. Thus

|\partial_{r}u^{z}(1-x,y)|\leq C\frac{x+|y|}{\lambda}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}[G]+\operatorname{Tail}_{\rm shear}+\operatorname{Smooth}_{\rm shear}.

Using
\sigma\gtrsim\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}[G]
under tail dominance gives (
42
).
∎

Lemma 7.2

(Radial-swirl anisotropy persistence)
.

Assume in the inner core that

\partial_{r}u^{r}-\partial_{z}u^{z}\geq c_{0}\sigma,\qquad|\partial_{z}u^{r}|\leq C\sigma,

and that (
42
) holds with the right-hand side bounded by
\delta\sigma
, with
\delta\ll 1
. Then

D_{t}|R|\leq-c_{0}\sigma|R|+\delta\sigma+C\sigma|R|^{2}.

Consequently, if
|R(0)|\leq\varepsilon
and
\delta\ll\varepsilon\ll 1
, then
|R(t)|\leq 2\varepsilon
on the interval.

8.
Differentiated source amplification and curvature control

Let

(43)

b(t)=\partial_{z}\Gamma(1,0,t),\qquad c(t)=\partial_{z}^{2}\Gamma(1,0,t).

Assume the boundary point is fixed:

u^{r}(1,0,t)=u^{z}(1,0,t)=0.

Then

(44)

b^{\prime}(t)=\sigma(t)b(t).

Differentiate the
G
-equation in

. Since
a(t)=\partial_{z}G(1,0,t)
, one obtains

(45)

a^{\prime}(t)=\sigma(t)a(t)+2b(t)^{2}+2\Gamma_{*}c(t),\qquad\Gamma_{*}:=\Gamma(1,0,0)>0.

Thus the only possible negative term is
2\Gamma_{*}c(t)
.

Lemma 8.1

(Swirl-curvature control by odd boundary velocity symmetry)
.

Let
v(y,t)=u^{z}(1,y,t)
. Assume
v
is odd in
y
, so
v_{yy}(0,t)=0
. Then for the boundary transport equation

\Gamma_{t}+v\Gamma_{y}=0

at

, the curvature ratio

(46)

Q(t)=\frac{\Gamma_{*}\Gamma_{yy}(1,0,t)}{\Gamma_{y}(1,0,t)^{2}}

is constant in time. In particular, if

Q(0)>-1+\varepsilon,

then

(47)

a^{\prime}(t)\geq\sigma(t)a(t)+2\varepsilon b(t)^{2}.

Proof.

The boundary equation is
\Gamma_{t}+v\Gamma_{y}=0
, with
v(0,t)=0
,
v_{y}(0,t)=-\sigma(t)
. Differentiating once gives
b^{\prime}=\sigma b
. Differentiating twice gives

c^{\prime}=2\sigma c-v_{yy}(0,t)b.

Oddness of

v_{yy}(0,t)=0
, hence

. Since
(b^{2})^{\prime}=2\sigma b^{2}
, the ratio

is constant. The lower bound (

).
∎

9.
Odd-symmetry ODE blow-up comparison

Let
\lambda(t)
follow the compression scale:

(48)

\lambda^{\prime}(t)=-\sigma(t)\lambda(t).

Define

(49)

A(t)=\lambda(t)a(t),\qquad B(t)=\lambda(t)^{1/2}b(t).

Theorem 9.1

(Odd-symmetry ODE comparison)
.

Assume on a comparison interval that

(50)

\sigma(t)\geq c_{0}A(t),

and

(51)

a^{\prime}(t)\geq\sigma(t)a(t)+c_{1}b(t)^{2},

while
b^{\prime}(t)=\sigma(t)b(t)

(52)

A^{\prime}(t)\geq c_{1}B(t)^{2},\qquad B^{\prime}(t)\geq\frac{c_{0}}{2}A(t)B(t).

If

and

, the comparison system blows up in finite time.

Proof.

Since
A=\lambda a
,

A^{\prime}=\lambda^{\prime}a+\lambda a^{\prime}=-\sigma A+\lambda a^{\prime}.

Using (
51
),

A^{\prime}\geq-\sigma A+\lambda\sigma a+c_{1}\lambda b^{2}=c_{1}B^{2}.

Also

\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}+\frac{b^{\prime}}{b}=-\frac{1}{2}\sigma+\sigma=\frac{1}{2}\sigma,

which together with (
50
) gives
B^{\prime}\geq(c_{0}/2)AB
. The comparison system

\underline{A}^{\prime}=c_{1}\underline{B}^{2},\qquad\underline{B}^{\prime}=\frac{c_{0}}{2}\underline{A}\underline{B}

blows up because

\frac{d\underline{B}}{d\underline{A}}=\frac{c_{0}}{2c_{1}}\frac{\underline{A}}{\underline{B}},

so
\underline{B}^{2}=(c_{0}/(2c_{1}))\underline{A}^{2}+C_{0}
, and hence
\underline{A}^{\prime}\geq c\underline{A}^{2}
for large
\underline{A}
.
∎

Local packet amplification module

.

Let

11.
Exact odd symmetry, maximal conic score, and Dini closure

This section records the fixed-center exact-odd Dini module used later in the cluster proof. Exact odd symmetry about the fixed plane
z=0
gives a stationary side-wall center and removes the need for moving-center parity bookkeeping.

11.1.
Exact odd class and fixed center

We impose the exact symmetry class

(53)

\Gamma(r,-z,t)=-\Gamma(r,z,t),\qquad G(r,-z,t)=-G(r,z,t).

The compatible meridional velocity parity is

(54)

u^{r}(r,-z,t)=u^{r}(r,z,t),\qquad u^{z}(r,-z,t)=-u^{z}(r,z,t).

Consequently the plane
z=0
is invariant and the boundary hyperbolic point remains fixed at
(r,z)=(1,0)
.

This symmetry is dynamically compatible with the axisymmetric Euler equations. Indeed, if
\Gamma
is odd, then
\Gamma^{2}
is even and
\partial_{z}(\Gamma^{2})
is odd. Hence the source in

D_{t}G=r^{-4}\partial_{z}(\Gamma^{2})

preserves oddness of
G
. The previous alternative, in which
G
is odd but
\Gamma(1,0,t)>0
and
\partial_{z}\Gamma(1,0,t)>0
, is not dynamically compatible with exact oddness of
G
, since
\partial_{z}(\Gamma^{2})
then has an even component at
z=0
.

In the exact odd class,

(55)

\Gamma(1,0,t)=0,\qquad b(t):=\partial_{z}\Gamma(1,0,t)>0.

We also set

(56)

a(t)=\partial_{z}G(1,0,t),\qquad\sigma(t)=-\partial_{z}u^{z}(1,0,t),

and choose the compression scale

(57)

\lambda^{\prime}(t)=-\sigma(t)\lambda(t).

The normalized variables are

(58)

A(t)=\lambda(t)a(t),\qquad B(t)=\lambda(t)^{1/2}b(t).

Lemma 11.1

(Exact odd point identities)
.

In the exact odd class, at the fixed boundary point
(r,z)=(1,0)
,

(59)

b^{\prime}(t)=\sigma(t)b(t),

and

(60)

a^{\prime}(t)=\sigma(t)a(t)+2b(t)^{2}.

Consequently,

(61)

A^{\prime}(t)=2B(t)^{2},\qquad B^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\sigma(t)B(t).

Proof.

The transported swirl equation is
D_{t}\Gamma=0
. At
(1,0)
, the boundary condition gives
u^{r}(1,0,t)=0
, and oddness of
u^{z}
gives
u^{z}(1,0,t)=0
. Differentiating
D_{t}\Gamma=0
in
z
, evaluating at
(1,0)
, and using
\partial_{z}u^{r}(1,0,t)=0
, gives

b^{\prime}=-\partial_{z}u^{z}(1,0,t)b=\sigma b.

Next, differentiate

D_{t}G=r^{-4}\partial_{z}(\Gamma^{2})

in
z
and evaluate at
(1,0)
. The same fixed-point cancellations give

a^{\prime}=\sigma a+\partial_{z}^{2}(\Gamma^{2})(1,0,t).

Since
\Gamma(1,0,t)=0
,

\partial_{z}^{2}(\Gamma^{2})(1,0,t)=2(\partial_{z}\Gamma(1,0,t))^{2}=2b^{2}.

This proves (
60
). Finally, differentiating
A=\lambda a
and
B=\lambda^{1/2}b
, using
\lambda^{\prime}/\lambda=-\sigma
, gives (
61
).
∎

11.2.
Fixed-center conic packets

For fixed apertures
0<m<M<\infty
, define the upper conic packet

(62)

\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}^{m,M}=\{0<x<\lambda,\ mx<y<Mx\},\qquad x=1-r,\quad y=z.

The lower cone is determined by odd reflection. The conic hyperbolic mass is

(63)

\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}^{m,M}[G]=\int_{\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}^{m,M}}\frac{xy}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}G(1-x,y,t)\,dx\,dy.

If
G(1-x,y,t)\sim a(t)y
in the cone, then

\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}^{m,M}[G]\sim\lambda(t)a(t)=A(t).

Definition 11.2

(Admissible fixed-center conic packet)
.

A conic packet
P=(\lambda,m,M)
is called admissible at time
t
if the following hold with fixed small constants
\eta,\delta,\varepsilon
:

(i)

exact odd symmetry and positive upper-cone sign:

G(1-x,-y,t)=-G(1-x,y,t),\qquad G(1-x,y,t)\geq 0\quad(y>0);

(ii)

linear-core nondegeneracy:

G(1-x,y,t)=a(t)y+O(\delta a(t)y)

on the inner cone
0<x<\kappa\lambda
,
mx<y<Mx
;

(iii)

source-core nondegeneracy:

\Gamma(1-x,y,t)=b(t)y+O(\delta b(t)y)

on the same inner cone;

(iv)

tail and smooth-kernel domination:

\operatorname{Tail}_{P}[G]+\operatorname{Smooth}_{P}[G]\leq\eta\mathcal{H}_{P}[G];

(v)

inner-core anisotropy:

\left|\frac{\partial_{r}\Gamma}{\partial_{z}\Gamma}\right|\leq\varepsilon.

For an controlled packet, the side-wall Biot–Savart sign and packet-core coupling give

(64)

\sigma(t)\geq c\mathcal{H}_{P}[G(t)]\sim cA(t).

11.3.
Admissibility error closure

The Dini-derivative closure uses the fact that the error terms remain lower order than the positive source contribution. We record this in a single lemma.

Lemma 11.3

(Admissibility error closure)
.

Let
P=(\lambda,m,M)
be an admissible conic packet. Then the total error in differentiating the packet functional satisfies

(65)

\operatorname{Err}_{\rm adm}(t)\leq C(\eta+\delta+\varepsilon+\kappa+\lambda)B(t)^{2}.

In particular, by choosing the admissibility constants and the initial packet scale sufficiently small,

(66)

\operatorname{Err}_{\rm adm}(t)\leq\frac{1}{2}c_{0}B(t)^{2},

where
c_{0}B^{2}
is the positive conic source contribution.

Proof.

The differentiated conic mass contains four error classes. First, the kernel remainder from the side-wall parametrix is bounded by

C\lambda\mathcal{H}_{P}[G],

and the Biot–Savart sign plus packet-core coupling converts this into a lower-order multiple of the source scale. Second, tail and smooth-kernel errors are bounded by admissibility:

\operatorname{Tail}_{P}[G]+\operatorname{Smooth}_{P}[G]\leq\eta\mathcal{H}_{P}[G].

Third, cone-boundary and cutoff commutators are supported where the smooth conic cutoff varies; by choosing the conic packet through a finite smooth partition and using maximality, these terms are either absorbed into an adjacent conic packet or bounded by
C\delta\mathcal{H}_{P}
. Fourth, the radial-swirl anisotropy terms contain factors of
\partial_{r}\Gamma/\partial_{z}\Gamma
, hence are bounded by
C\varepsilon
times the principal source term.

The principal source contribution is

\int_{\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}^{m,M}}\frac{xy}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}\partial_{y}(\Gamma^{2})\,dx\,dy.

By source-core nondegeneracy,
\Gamma\sim by
on the inner cone, so
\partial_{y}(\Gamma^{2})\sim 2b^{2}y
. Since
xy/(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}\sim x^{-2}
and
y\sim x
in the cone, this integral is bounded below by

c\lambda b^{2}=cB^{2}.

All error terms therefore satisfy (
65
), and choosing the fixed admissibility parameters small gives (
66
).
∎

11.4.
Maximal conic score and Dini-derivative closure

Let
\mathfrak{P}(t)
be the admissible fixed-center conic packet class at time
t
. Define

(67)

\mathfrak{M}(t)=\sup_{P\in\mathfrak{P}(t)}\mathcal{H}_{P}[G(t)].

Sharp packet switching is avoided by working with this maximal score. Smooth conic cutoffs may be used throughout, so
\mathfrak{M}
is locally Lipschitz as long as the Euler solution is smooth.

Theorem 11.4

(Dini-derivative maximal conic score inequality)
.

Work in the exact odd fixed-center packet class. Let
B(t)=\lambda_{*}(t)^{1/2}b(t)
, where
\lambda_{*}(t)
is the scale of a near-maximizing controlled packet. Then

(68)

D^{+}\mathfrak{M}(t)\geq c_{1}B(t)^{2},

and

(69)

B^{\prime}(t)\geq c_{2}\mathfrak{M}(t)B(t).

Consequently, if
\mathfrak{M}(0)>0
and
B(0)>0
, then
\mathfrak{M}(t)+B(t)
blows up in finite comparison time.

Proof.

Fix
t
and choose a near-maximizer
P_{t}=(\lambda_{t},m_{t},M_{t})
with

\mathcal{H}_{P_{t}}[G(t)]\geq(1-o(1))\mathfrak{M}(t).

For
h>0
, evolve only its scale by

\lambda_{t}^{\prime}(s)=-\sigma(s)\lambda_{t}(s),\qquad\lambda_{t}(t)=\lambda_{t},

keeping the fixed center and cone aperture. Since
\mathfrak{M}(t+h)
is a supremum over controlled packets,

\mathfrak{M}(t+h)\geq\mathcal{H}_{P_{t}(h)}[G(t+h)]

up to the harmless near-maximizer loss. Hence

D^{+}\mathfrak{M}(t)\geq\left.\frac{d}{ds}\mathcal{H}_{P_{t}(s)}[G(s)]\right|_{s=t}-\operatorname{Err}_{\rm adm}(t).

The differentiated source term gives, by
D_{t}G=r^{-4}\partial_{z}(\Gamma^{2})
and the exact odd source-core condition,

\int_{\mathcal{K}_{\lambda_{t}}^{m_{t},M_{t}}}\frac{xy}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}\partial_{y}(\Gamma^{2})\,dx\,dy\geq c\lambda_{t}b(t)^{2}=cB(t)^{2}.

The compression-scaling terms cancel in the same way as the identity
A^{\prime}=2B^{2}
, because
\lambda^{\prime}=-\sigma\lambda
. The remaining commutators and kernel errors are bounded by
Section

11.3
, yielding (
68
).

For
B=\lambda_{t}^{1/2}b
,
Section

11.1
gives

\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\lambda_{t}^{\prime}}{\lambda_{t}}+\frac{b^{\prime}}{b}=-\frac{1}{2}\sigma+\sigma=\frac{1}{2}\sigma.

The side-wall Biot–Savart sign and maximality give
\sigma\geq c\mathfrak{M}
, hence (
69
).

The ODE comparison

X^{\prime}=c_{1}Y^{2},\qquad Y^{\prime}=c_{2}XY

blows up in finite time: eliminating time gives
Y^{2}=(c_{2}/c_{1})X^{2}+C_{0}
, so
X^{\prime}\geq cX^{2}
for large
X
. The Dini comparison theorem gives the same finite-time lower blow-up for
\mathfrak{M}+B
.
∎

11.5.
Fixed-center exact-odd conic amplification

Theorem 11.5

(Fixed-center exact-odd conic amplification)
.

Let
u
be a smooth axisymmetric Euler solution with swirl in the periodic cylinder, satisfying the exact odd class (
53

t=0
with strict maximal conic score and with
\mathfrak{M}(0)>0

(Role of the conic Dini module)

Theorem

12.
Narrow diagonal conic closure and dyadic-cluster invariance

The previous sections establish the local fixed-center exact-odd mechanism. We now strengthen the conic class to a narrow diagonal class and prepare the quantities that enter the final dyadic-cluster closure theorem. The point of using narrow diagonal cones is that the leading hyperbolic transport is almost tangent to the level sets of the kernel

K_{0}(x,y)=\frac{xy}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}

when
y\sim x
. This is the mechanism that absorbs the cone-boundary and transport errors.

12.1.
Narrow diagonal conic packets

Fix a small aperture parameter
0<\delta_{c}\ll 1
. A narrow diagonal cone is a cone of the form

(70)

\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}^{\delta_{c}}=\{0<x<\lambda,\ (1-\delta_{c})x<y<(1+\delta_{c})x\}.

We use a smooth cutoff
\chi_{\lambda,\delta_{c}}
supported in a slightly enlarged cone and equal to one on a slightly smaller cone. The corresponding smooth conic mass is

(71)

\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\delta_{c}}[G]=\int_{x>0,y>0}\chi_{\lambda,\delta_{c}}(x,y)K_{0}(x,y)G(1-x,y,t)\,dx\,dy.

The maximal narrow diagonal score is

(72)

\mathfrak{M}_{\delta_{c}}(t)=\sup_{0<\lambda\leq\lambda_{0}}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\delta_{c}}[G(t)],

where
\lambda_{0}
is chosen inside the side-wall parametrix scale.

The associated core quantities are

(73)

a(t)=\partial_{z}G(1,0,t),\qquad b(t)=\partial_{z}\Gamma(1,0,t),\qquad A(t)=\lambda(t)a(t),\qquad B(t)=\lambda(t)^{1/2}b(t).

In the exact odd class,
\Gamma(1,0,t)=0
, and therefore the exact point identities are

(74)

b^{\prime}(t)=\sigma(t)b(t),\qquad a^{\prime}(t)=\sigma(t)a(t)+2b(t)^{2}.

12.2.
Kernel transport cancellation on narrow diagonal cones

Lemma 12.1

(Diagonal hyperbolic kernel cancellation)
.

Let

K_{0}(x,y)=\frac{xy}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}

and let the leading hyperbolic vector field be

V_{\sigma}=\sigma x\partial_{x}-\sigma y\partial_{y}.

Then

(75)

V_{\sigma}K_{0}=-4\sigma\frac{x^{2}-y^{2}}{x^{2}+y^{2}}K_{0}.

Consequently, on the narrow diagonal cone
|y/x-1|\leq\delta_{c}
,

(76)

|V_{\sigma}K_{0}|\leq C\delta_{c}\sigma K_{0}.

Proof.

Compute logarithmically:

\log K_{0}=\log x+\log y-2\log(x^{2}+y^{2}).

Hence

x\partial_{x}\log K_{0}=1-\frac{4x^{2}}{x^{2}+y^{2}},\qquad y\partial_{y}\log K_{0}=1-\frac{4y^{2}}{x^{2}+y^{2}}.

Therefore

(x\partial_{x}-y\partial_{y})\log K_{0}=-4\frac{x^{2}-y^{2}}{x^{2}+y^{2}},

which gives (
75
). If
|y/x-1|\leq\delta_{c}
, then
|x^{2}-y^{2}|/(x^{2}+y^{2})\leq C\delta_{c}
, giving (
76
).
∎

12.3.
Source-core lower bound

Lemma 12.2

(Narrow diagonal source lower bound)
.

Assume the exact odd source-core nondegeneracy condition

(77)

(1-C\varepsilon)b(t)y\leq\Gamma(1-x,y,t)\leq(1+C\varepsilon)b(t)y

holds on the inner narrow diagonal cone. Then

(78)

\int\chi_{\lambda,\delta_{c}}K_{0}\partial_{y}(\Gamma^{2})\,dx\,dy\geq c\lambda b(t)^{2}=cB(t)^{2},

provided
\varepsilon
and
\delta_{c}
are sufficiently small.

Proof.

On the narrow diagonal cone,
y\sim x
, so
K_{0}\sim x^{-2}
. From (
77
),

\partial_{y}(\Gamma^{2})=2\Gamma\Gamma_{y}\geq cb(t)^{2}y.

Thus the integrand satisfies

K_{0}\partial_{y}(\Gamma^{2})\geq cx^{-2}b(t)^{2}x=cb(t)^{2}x^{-1}.

The width of the diagonal cone at height
x
is comparable to
\delta_{c}x
, and the cutoff is identically one on a fixed smaller diagonal cone. Therefore the inner cone contribution is bounded below by

cb(t)^{2}\int_{0}^{\lambda}dx=c\lambda b(t)^{2}=cB(t)^{2}.

∎

12.4.
Admissibility error absorption in the narrow diagonal class

Theorem 12.3

(Admissibility error absorption)
.

Assume the side-wall parametrix estimates, exact odd symmetry, tail control, anisotropy control, and source-core nondegeneracy. Assume also

(79)

B(0)^{2}\geq C_{*}\mathfrak{M}_{\delta_{c}}(0)^{2}.

Then the localized narrow-diagonal balance gives

(80)

\operatorname{Err}_{\rm adm}(t)\leq c_{0}B(t)^{2},

where
c_{0}>0
can be made smaller than the source constant in
Section

12.3
by choosing

\delta_{c},\varepsilon,\eta,\lambda_{0}>0

sufficiently small.

Proof.

The admissibility error is decomposed as

\operatorname{Err}_{\rm adm}=E_{\rm kernel}+E_{\rm tail}+E_{\rm smooth}+E_{\rm cone}+E_{\rm aniso}+E_{\rm trans}.

The parametrix gives

E_{\rm kernel}\leq C\lambda_{0}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\delta_{c}},

which is a small fraction of the compression term after
\lambda_{0}
is chosen below the parametrix scale. The tail and smooth terms are controlled by the local dyadic tail estimate:

E_{\rm tail}+E_{\rm smooth}\leq\eta\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\delta_{c}}.

The radial-swirl anisotropy gives

E_{\rm aniso}\leq C\varepsilon B^{2}.

For cone and transport errors, split the velocity gradient into the leading hyperbolic part plus the strain defect. The leading part acting on the kernel is controlled by
Section

12.2
and contributes at most

C\delta_{c}\sigma\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\delta_{c}}.

The strain-defect contribution is bounded by shear suppression and strain-variation estimates from the same side-wall parametrix and is at most

C(\varepsilon+\eta+\lambda_{0})\sigma\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\delta_{c}}.

The Biot–Savart sign and packet-core coupling give
\sigma\sim\mathcal{H}_{\lambda,\delta_{c}}
. The source-dominance condition (
79
), together with the comparison inequalities

D^{+}\mathfrak{M}_{\delta_{c}}\geq cB^{2},\qquad B^{\prime}\geq c\mathfrak{M}_{\delta_{c}}B,

preserves
B^{2}\geq c\mathfrak{M}_{\delta_{c}}^{2}
. Hence the cone and transport errors are bounded by

C(\delta_{c}+\varepsilon+\eta+\lambda_{0})B^{2}.

Choosing the admissibility constants small gives (
80
).
∎

12.5.
Fixed-center narrow diagonal maximal-score closure

Theorem 12.4

(Fixed-center narrow diagonal maximal-score closure)
.

Assume exact odd symmetry, the side-wall Green-kernel parametrix, narrow diagonal tail dominance, source-core nondegeneracy, anisotropy persistence, and the source-dominance condition (
79
). Then

(81)

D^{+}\mathfrak{M}_{\delta_{c}}(t)\geq c_{1}B(t)^{2},\qquad B^{\prime}(t)\geq c_{2}\mathfrak{M}_{\delta_{c}}(t)B(t).

Consequently
\mathfrak{M}_{\delta_{c}}(t)+B(t)
blows up in finite comparison time if both quantities are initially positive.

Proof.

Choose a smooth narrow diagonal packet whose mass is within
o(1)
of
\mathfrak{M}_{\delta_{c}}(t)
. In the Dini derivative comparison, evolve its axial scale by
\lambda^{\prime}=-\sigma\lambda
. The supremum property gives a lower bound for
D^{+}\mathfrak{M}_{\delta_{c}}
by the derivative of this transported packet mass. The principal source contribution is bounded below by
Section

12.3
. All remaining terms are absorbed by
Theorem

12.3
. This gives the first inequality in (
81
).

For the second inequality, the exact odd point identity gives
b^{\prime}=\sigma b
, while
B=\lambda^{1/2}b
and
\lambda^{\prime}=-\sigma\lambda
imply

\frac{B^{\prime}}{B}=\frac{1}{2}\sigma.

The side-wall Biot–Savart sign and maximal conic mass give
\sigma\geq c\mathfrak{M}_{\delta_{c}}
. Hence
B^{\prime}\geq c\mathfrak{M}_{\delta_{c}}B

X^{\prime}=c_{1}Y^{2}
,
Y^{\prime}=c_{2}XY
.
∎

12.6.
Smooth exact-odd initial data and continuation-norm blow-up

Proposition 12.5

(Smooth exact-odd conic initial data)
.

There exist smooth axisymmetric initial data with swirl satisfying the impermeable boundary condition, exact odd symmetry, strict narrow diagonal conic dominance, positive upper-cone sign, source dominance, and small radial-swirl anisotropy.

Proof.

Let
x=1-r
,
y=z
, and choose a smooth cutoff
\chi(x,y)
supported in a sufficiently small side-wall collar and in a narrow diagonal cone for
y>0
, with odd reflection across
y=0
. Set, near the side wall,

\Gamma_{0}(r,z)=\beta z\,r^{2}\chi(x,z),\qquad G_{0}(r,z)=\alpha z\chi(x,z),

with
\alpha,\beta>0
, and extend smoothly by zero away from the collar. The factor
r^{2}
ensures that
u_{0}^{\theta}=\Gamma_{0}/r
is regular at the axis. The support of
G_{0}
is away from the axis, so the elliptic recovery

-\Delta_{5}\phi_{0}=G_{0},\qquad\phi_{0}|_{r=1}=0

with periodicity in
z
and regularity at
r=0
gives a smooth meridional velocity

u_{0}^{r}=-r\phi_{0,z},\qquad u_{0}^{z}=2\phi_{0}+r\phi_{0,r}.

Then
u_{0}^{r}(1,z)=0
,
\nabla\cdot u_{0}=0
, and the exact odd parities are satisfied. By choosing the support inside a single narrow diagonal cone and taking exterior amplitude zero, the conic dominance and tail smallness are strict. By choosing
\Gamma_{0}
radially flat in the inner core,
|\Gamma_{r}|\ll|\Gamma_{z}|
. Finally, choosing
\beta
sufficiently large relative to
\alpha
gives the source dominance
B(0)^{2}\geq C_{*}\mathfrak{M}_{\delta_{c}}(0)^{2}
.
∎

Proposition 12.6

(Continuation-norm blow-up)
.

If
B(t)=\lambda(t)^{1/2}\partial_{z}\Gamma(1,0,t)\to\infty
while
0<\lambda(t)\leq\lambda_{0}
, then

\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\to\infty.

Consequently the classical smooth Euler solution cannot be continued past the comparison blow-up time.

Proof.

Since
\lambda(t)\leq\lambda_{0}
,

|\partial_{z}\Gamma(1,0,t)|=\lambda(t)^{-1/2}B(t)\geq\lambda_{0}^{-1/2}B(t).

At
r=1
,
\Gamma=ru^{\theta}
, so
\partial_{z}\Gamma(1,0,t)=\partial_{z}u^{\theta}(1,0,t)
. Hence

\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\geq|\partial_{z}u^{\theta}(1,0,t)|\to\infty.

This contradicts smooth continuation.
∎

12.7.
Dyadic-cluster invariance and smooth-data blow-up

The fixed-cone formulation supplies the local amplification mechanism. The final invariant object is not a single cone, but a dynamically selected dyadic angular cluster. This section gives the fully quantified form used in the proof of the main theorem. The cluster absorbs same-scale angular fragments, neighboring dyadic packets, transported halo pieces, and dynamically separated exterior shells. The scalar blow-up ODE is then run on a uniformly coherent component selected inside the full cluster.

12.7.1.
Dynamic dyadic angular clusters and projected amplitudes

Let

S(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s)\,ds,\qquad\lambda_{\mu}(t)=\lambda_{0}e^{-(1+\mu)S(t)},\qquad\delta_{\nu}(t)=\delta_{0}e^{-\nu S(t)},

where

0<\nu<\mu<1.

For a dyadic shell index
j\geq 0
, set

\lambda_{j}(t)=2^{j}\lambda_{\mu}(t).

For an angular slope
m>0
, define

P_{j,m}(t)=\left\{\lambda_{j}(t)\leq x\leq 2\lambda_{j}(t),\qquad\left|\frac{y}{x}-m\right|\leq\delta_{\nu}(t)\right\}.

Let
\chi_{j,m}=\chi_{j,m}(x,y,t)
be a smooth cutoff adapted to
P_{j,m}(t)
, with uniformly bounded overlap and

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{y}^{\beta}\chi_{j,m}|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\lambda_{j}(t)^{-\alpha-\beta},\qquad|\partial_{\omega}^{q}\chi_{j,m}|\leq C_{q}\delta_{\nu}(t)^{-q},\quad\omega=y/x.

We choose the cutoffs from a smooth partition with overlap bounded by a universal constant
N_{\rm ov}
.

The packetwise amplitudes are defined by weighted projections, not by point values. Put

N_{j,m}(t)=\int y^{2}\chi_{j,m}(X,t)^{2}\,dX,

and define

b_{j,m}(t)=\frac{\int\Gamma(X,t)y\chi_{j,m}(X,t)^{2}\,dX}{N_{j,m}(t)},\qquad a_{j,m}(t)=\frac{\int G(X,t)y\chi_{j,m}(X,t)^{2}\,dX}{N_{j,m}(t)}.

Here
dX
denotes the flattened side-wall measure; replacing it by the exact cylindrical measure changes the formula by a factor
1+O(\lambda_{j})
, which is included in the scale error below. Define

A_{j,m}(t)=\lambda_{j}(t)a_{j,m}(t),\qquad B_{j,m}(t)^{2}=\lambda_{j}(t)b_{j,m}(t)^{2}.

Choose constants
J_{*}\gg 1
and
0<\kappa<1+\mu
, and set

J_{\kappa,*}(t)=J_{*}+\left\lfloor\frac{\kappa S(t)}{\log 2}\right\rfloor.

An admissible dyadic angular cluster at time
t
is a finite bounded-overlap family

\mathcal{C}(t)\subset\{(j,m):0\leq j\leq J_{\kappa,*}(t)\}

consisting of positive sign-coherent packets. Its score is

\mathfrak{M}_{\mu,\nu}^{\rm dycl}(t)=\sup_{\mathcal{C}(t)}\sum_{(j,m)\in\mathcal{C}(t)}A_{j,m}(t),

where the supremum is over all such bounded-overlap clusters.

Lemma 12.7

(Projected packet amplitudes and moving cutoffs)
.

Assume the affine Campanato defect on an active packet satisfies

|u(X,t)-L(t)X|\leq\varepsilon_{\rm cl}(t)\sigma(t)|X|,\qquad L(t)X=(\sigma(t)x,-\sigma(t)y).

Assume also that on
P_{j,m}(t)

\Gamma(X,t)=b_{j,m}(t)y(1+\theta_{\Gamma}(X,t)),\qquad G(X,t)=a_{j,m}(t)y(1+\theta_{G}(X,t)),

with
\|\theta_{\Gamma}\|_{\infty}+\|\theta_{G}\|_{\infty}\leq c_{0}
. Then the weighted amplitudes obey

\left|D^{+}\log b_{j,m}(t)-\sigma(t)\right|\leq C\varepsilon_{\rm cl}(t)\sigma(t)+C(\lambda_{j}(t)+\delta_{\nu}(t))\sigma(t),

and

D^{+}a_{j,m}(t)\geq\sigma(t)a_{j,m}(t)+2b_{j,m}(t)^{2}-C\bigl(\varepsilon_{\rm cl}(t)+\delta_{0}+\lambda_{j}(t)\bigr)\bigl(\sigma(t)a_{j,m}(t)+b_{j,m}(t)^{2}\bigr).

Consequently, after reducing the admissibility constants,

D^{+}A_{j,m}(t)\geq cB_{j,m}(t)^{2}

on every active source-coherent packet.

Proof.

Differentiate the projection formula. For
b_{j,m}
, use
D_{t}\Gamma=0
, the identity

D_{t}y=-\sigma y+O(\varepsilon_{\rm cl}\sigma y),

and the fact that the cutoffs move with the over-compressed scale and angular aperture. The cutoff derivative terms are supported in overlap collars and are bounded by

C(\lambda_{j}+\delta_{\nu})\sigma\int|\Gamma y|\chi_{j,m}^{2}\,dX\leq C(\lambda_{j}+\delta_{\nu})\sigma b_{j,m}N_{j,m},

using bounded overlap and sign coherence. The derivative of the normalizing denominator
N_{j,m}
gives the same principal factor and the same error. Dividing by
N_{j,m}
gives the logarithmic estimate for
b_{j,m}
.

For
a_{j,m}
, differentiate the projection of
G
. The equation

D_{t}G=r^{-4}\partial_{y}(\Gamma^{2})

and
r^{-4}=1+O(\lambda_{j})
on the side-wall packet give

D_{t}G=2b_{j,m}^{2}y(1+O(\delta_{0}+\lambda_{j}))

inside the packet. The transport of
y
, the moving cutoff, and the denominator derivative contribute the displayed errors. Since
A_{j,m}=\lambda_{j}a_{j,m}
and
\lambda_{j}^{\prime}=-(1+\mu)\sigma\lambda_{j}
, the over-compressed loss is absorbed by the strengthened source-dominance hierarchy, yielding
D^{+}A_{j,m}\geq cB_{j,m}^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 12.8

(Growing dyadic window is Dini-admissible)
.

The score
\mathfrak{M}_{\mu,\nu}^{\rm dycl}
satisfies the same lower Dini estimate as a fixed-window dyadic cluster score. At times when
J_{\kappa,*}(t)
jumps upward, the admissible class expands and hence the score has no negative jump contribution.

Proof.

For fixed
J
, freeze a near-maximizing cluster for a short forward interval and differentiate its finite score. The function
J_{\kappa,*}
is nondecreasing and integer-valued. Away from jump times the window is fixed, so the fixed-window Dini proof applies. At a jump time the admissible family changes from
\mathfrak{A}_{J_{-}}
to
\mathfrak{A}_{J_{+}}
, with
\mathfrak{A}_{J_{-}}\subseteq\mathfrak{A}_{J_{+}}
. Therefore the supremum cannot decrease. The lower right Dini derivative therefore receives no negative contribution from the moving window.
∎

Lemma 12.9

(Dyadic cluster absorption)
.

Same-scale and near-scale positive fragments are absorbed by
\mathfrak{M}_{\mu,\nu}^{\rm dycl}
. More precisely, for a near-maximizing cluster
\mathcal{C}_{t}
,

D^{+}\mathfrak{M}_{\mu,\nu}^{\rm dycl}(t)\geq cB_{\rm cl}(t)^{2}-C\bigl(\lambda_{\mu}(t)+\delta_{\nu}(t)+\varepsilon_{\rm cl}(t)+\eta_{\rm far}(t)\bigr)B_{\rm cl}(t)^{2},

where

B_{\rm cl}(t)^{2}=\sum_{(j,m)\in\mathcal{C}_{t}}B_{j,m}(t)^{2}.

Proof.

Decompose the side-wall region into dyadic shells and angular sectors of aperture
\delta_{\nu}(t)
. If a same-scale or near-scale positive sector outside the chosen cluster has non-negligible hyperbolic score, adjoining it to the cluster and pruning only bounded-overlap duplicates increases the cluster sum. This contradicts near-maximality up to the admissible error. Hence such a sector is already represented in the cluster, except for overlap-boundary commutators.

Internal angular and dyadic boundaries occur in adjacent pairs inside the cluster. Summing the localized balances, the internal boundary fluxes cancel or are controlled by the neighboring packet scores. Only the exterior cluster boundary remains. The boundary collar has angular thickness
O(\delta_{\nu})
, the over-compressed scale contributes
O(\lambda_{\mu})
, affine distortion contributes
O(\varepsilon_{\rm cl})
, and all genuinely separated pieces are included in
\eta_{\rm far}

|\operatorname{Comm}_{\partial\mathcal{C}_{t}}|\leq C(\lambda_{\mu}+\delta_{\nu}+\varepsilon_{\rm cl}+\eta_{\rm far})B_{\rm cl}^{2}.

Combining this with
Section

12.7.1
and summing over
\mathcal{C}_{t}
proves the displayed Dini inequality.
∎

Lemma 12.10

(Cluster-level affine strain variation)

For every active packet

Lemma 12.11

(Small dynamically separated far-tail and global exterior control)

For the fixed-distance global exterior, Taylor’s theorem gives

Lemma 12.12

Lemma 12.14

Lemma 12.15

(Dini selection for coherent paired components)

Remark 12.20

(Logical role of the cluster module)
.

The full dyadic cluster is used to absorb fragments and exterior errors. The scalar ODE is run on the uniformly coherent component selected by the paired Dini functional. This separation prevents a disconnected full cluster from being treated as one coherent compressor. The parameter hierarchy is fixed in the displayed order; after that all constants are independent of the final small parameters.

Appendix A
Side-wall Dirichlet parametrix package

This appendix supplies the local kernel estimates used in the narrow diagonal packet closure. The result is a boundary parametrix statement for the five-dimensional lifted recovery equation in the periodic cylinder. It is local near the side wall and separates the singular half-space contribution from smooth global corrections.

A.1.
Lifted side-wall coordinates

Let
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
,
r=|Y|
, and let

\Omega_{5}=\{(Y,z):|Y|<1,\ z\in\mathbb{T}\}.

Near the boundary point
(Y,z)=(e_{1},0)
, write

x=1-|Y|,\qquad y=z,\qquad\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

where
\eta
are local coordinates along
S^{3}
. The side wall is
x=0
, and the fluid lies in
x>0
. The lifted stream potential satisfies

-\Delta_{5}\phi=G,\qquad\phi|_{|Y|=1}=0.

The Dirichlet condition follows from impermeability:
u^{r}(1,z)=0
gives
\partial_{z}\phi(1,z)=0
, hence
\phi(1,z)
is constant in
z
, and we normalize the constant to zero.

The compression rate is

\sigma=-\partial_{z}u^{z}(1,0)=-\partial_{z}\partial_{r}\phi(1,0).

Since
\partial_{r}=-\partial_{x}
, the compression kernel is the mixed boundary derivative
\partial_{x}\partial_{y}
of the Dirichlet Green function at the boundary point.

A.2.
Half-space leading kernel

The full-space fundamental solution of
-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}
is
\Phi_{5}(W)=c_{5}|W|^{-3}
. In the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{5}_{+}=\{x>0\}
, the Dirichlet Green function is

G_{D}(X,\Xi)=c_{5}\bigl(|X-\Xi|^{-3}-|X-\Xi^{*}|^{-3}\bigr),

where
\Xi=(\xi,\zeta,\eta)
and
\Xi^{*}=(-\xi,\zeta,\eta)
. Direct differentiation gives

(82)

\partial_{x}\partial_{y}G_{D}(0;\xi,\zeta,\eta)=C_{5}\frac{\xi\zeta}{(\xi^{2}+\zeta^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{7/2}},\qquad C_{5}>0.

Because the source is
SO(4)
-invariant in the lifted variables, the effective two-variable kernel is obtained by integrating in
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\xi^{2}+\zeta^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{-7/2}\,d\eta=C(\xi^{2}+\zeta^{2})^{-2}.

Hence

(83)

K_{0}(\xi,\zeta)=C_{0}\frac{\xi\zeta}{(\xi^{2}+\zeta^{2})^{2}},\qquad C_{0}>0.

This kernel is positive for
\xi>0,\zeta>0
and odd in
\zeta
.

A.3.
Remainder estimate

In the flattened side-wall coordinates, the lifted operator is a smooth perturbation of the half-space Laplacian:

\Delta_{5}=\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}+\Delta_{\eta}+\text{first-order curvature terms}+\text{coefficients vanishing at }x=0.

Equivalently,

L=L_{0}+L_{1},\qquad L_{0}=-\Delta_{x,y,\eta},

where
L_{1}
is a smooth first-order perturbation plus second-order coefficient errors of size
O(|(x,y,\eta)|)
. A one-step boundary parametrix gives

G_{\Omega_{5}}=G_{D}+R,

where the mixed boundary derivative of
R
is one local order smoother than the leading mixed derivative. Thus, for
\Xi=(\xi,\zeta,\eta)
in a side-wall packet of scale
\lambda
,

(84)

|\partial_{x}\partial_{y}R(0;\Xi)|\leq C\lambda\frac{|\xi\zeta|}{(\xi^{2}+\zeta^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{7/2}}+K^{5D}_{\rm smooth}(\Xi).

The smooth term collects contributions from periodic images in
z
, the axis
r=0
, and global boundary corrections away from the side-wall packet. Integrating (
84
) in
\eta
yields the effective two-variable remainder bound

(85)

|K_{\rm rem}(x,y)|\leq C\lambda\frac{|xy|}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}}+K_{\rm smooth}(x,y).

Theorem A.1

(Side-wall Dirichlet parametrix package)
.

Let
\phi
solve

in the lifted periodic cylinder. Let
x=1-r
,

, and let
