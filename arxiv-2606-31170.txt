1.
Introduction

The canonical map is a fundamental tool in the study of the explicit birational geometry of varieties of general type. For a smooth curve
C
of genus
g\geq 2
, it’s well-known that the canonical map of
C
is either an embedding or a finite morphism of degree
2
. Let
S
be a smooth surface of general type whose canonical map is generically finite. Beauville
[
1
]
proved that the degree of the canonical map of
S
is bounded. Xiao
[
14
]
showed that if
p_{g}(S)>132
, then the canonical map of
S
has degree at most
8
.

For a Gorenstein minimal
3
-fold of general type with generically finite canonical map, Meng Chen
[
4
, §2.10]
posted the following problem :

Problem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a Gorenstein minimal 3-fold with at worst locally factorial terminal singularities. Suppose the canonical map
\phi_{X}
of
X
is generically finite onto its image. Is the generic degree
d_{X}
of
\phi_{X}
universally upper bounded ?

An affirmative answer was given by Hacon
[
9
]
, who established the bound that
d_{X}\leq 576
. Subsequently, Du and Gao
[
5
]
improved this to
d_{X}\leq 360
. Later Du
[
6
]
obtained a further refinement under an additional assumption on the canonical image of
\phi_{X}
.

It is natural to expect that, as in the surface case, such an upper bound should become smaller when the geometric genus is large. In this direction, Cai
[
2
]
proved that
d_{X}\leq 72
whenever
p_{g}(X)>105411
.

Our first main result is the following

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a Gorenstein minimal threefold of general type with canonical divisor
K_{X}
. Suppose that
|K_{X}|
defines a generically finite map
\phi_{X}
of degree
d
. If
p_{g}(X)>243
, then
d\leq 72
.

Moreover, if
d=72
, then the general Albanese fibre
F
of
X
is a smooth minimal surface of general type satisfying
p_{g}(F)=3,q(F)=0
and
K_{F}^{2}=36
, and the canonical map of
F
has degree
36
.

Remark 1.3
.

(1)

When
p_{g}(X)
is small, examples of smooth minimal threefolds of general type with canonical map of degree
96
do exist (see
[
7
]
).

(2)

If
X
is not assumed to be Gorenstein minimal, Hacon
[
9
]
proved that the canonical degree
d
is unbounded.

Under the same assumption as in Theorem
1.2
, we have

Corollary 1.4
.

If
d>64
, then the general Albanese fibre of
X
is a regular surface.

1.1.
Notation and Conventions.

Throughout the paper, we work over the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
. We follow the standard definitions and notation in
[
10
]
.
All varieties are assumed to be projective.

Varities and divisors

For a normal variety
X
, we denote by
K_{X}
for the canonical divisor. We write
D\geq 0
to indicate that
D
is an effective divisor, and denote by
|M|
the linear system associated to a divisor
M
on
X
.
For two
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors
D_{1},D_{2}
, we write
D_{1}\geq D_{2}
if
D_{1}-D_{2}
is effective.

Irregular varieties

Let
X
be a normal variety with at worst rational singularities. We say that
X
is
irregular
, if
q(X):=h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})>0
. Note that
X
has a well-defined
Albanese map

a:X\to\mathrm{Alb}(X),

where
A:=\mathrm{Alb}(X)
is an abelian variety referred to as the Albanese variety of
X
. The number
\dim a(X)
, denoted by
\mathrm{alb}.\dim X
, is called the
Albanese dimension
of
X
. Let

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h}}{{\rightarrow}}A

be the Stein factorization of
a
. Then
f
is called the
Albanese fibration
of
X
, and a fibre of
f
is called an
Albanese fibre
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Image sheaf of the evaluation map

Let
C
be a smooth curve of genus
g
, and let
E
be a vector bundle of rank
r
on
C
. Consider the evaluation map

\mathrm{ev}\colon H^{0}(E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}\to E

Denote by
E_{\mathrm{ev}}
the image sheaf of
\mathrm{ev}
, which is a vector bundle, say of rank
l
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
14
, Proposition page 476]

Suppose that
E\otimes\omega_{C}
is nef, then

(g-1)(r-l)\leq l.

Moreover, when the equality holds,
E
is a semi-stable bundle of degree
(2g-2)r
.

2.2.
Miyaoka-Yau inequality

For minimal varieties of general type, Greb, Kebekus, Peternell and Taji (see
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
8
, line 12 in §1.1, page 1488]
) proved the following
\mathbb{Q}
-Miyaoka-Yau inequality:

Theorem 2.2

(
\mathbb{Q}
-Miyaoka-Yau inequality)
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional, minimal variety of general type. Then,

\bigl(2(n+1)\cdot c_{2}(X)-n\cdot c_{1}(X)^{2}\bigr)\cdot[K_{X}]^{n-2}\geq 0.

In particular, we have

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
X
be a Gorenstein minimal
3
-fold of general type, then

(2.1)

K_{X}^{3}\leq 64\chi(\omega_{X}).

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
, we have

8(K_{X}\cdot c_{2}(X))\geq 3K_{X}^{3}.

By
[
12
, Corollary 10.3]
, we have
(K_{X}\cdot c_{2}(X))=24\chi(\omega_{X})
. It follows easily that

K_{X}^{3}\leq 64\chi(\omega_{X}).

The proof is completed.
∎

3.
Proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.4

Let
X
be a Gorenstein minimal threefold of general type. Suppose that
|K_{X}|
defines a generically finite map
\phi_{X}\colon X\dashrightarrow\Sigma\subset\mathbb{P}^{p_{g}(X)-1}
, where
\Sigma=\mathrm{Im}(\phi_{X})
. Let
d
be the generic degree of
\phi_{X}
,
by the Miyaoka-Yau inequality (
2.1
), we have

d\deg(\Sigma)\leq K_{X}^{3}\leq 64\chi(\omega_{X}).

Since
\Sigma
is nondegenerate,
\deg(\Sigma)\geq p_{g}(X)-3
. It follows that

(3.1)

d\leq\frac{K_{X}^{3}}{\deg(\Sigma)}\leq\frac{K_{X}^{3}}{p_{g}(X)-3}\leq\frac{64\chi(\omega_{X})}{p_{g}(X)-3}.

The proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.4
follow directly from two propositions below.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
p_{g}(X)>243
. Then we have
d\leq 64
, unless the general Albanese fibre of
X
is a regular surface.

Proof.

By
[
3
, Proposition 2.1]
and our assumption, we have
\chi(\omega_{X})\leq p_{g}(X)
.
Since
p_{g}(X)>243
, by (
3.1
), we have

d\leq\frac{64\chi(\omega_{X})}{p_{g}(X)-3}\leq\frac{64p_{g}(X)}{p_{g}(X)-3}<65.

The proof is completed.

∎

By adopting the idea from the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 5]
, we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that
p_{g}(X)>243
and the general Albanese fibre
F
of
X
is regular. Then we have
d\leq 72
. In the case
d=72
, the general fibre
F
is a smooth minimal surface of general type with
p_{g}(F)=3,q(F)=0
and
K_{F}^{2}=36
whose canonical degree is
36
.

Proof.

Note that the Albanese fibration
f:X\to Y
is a fibration onto a smooth curve
Y
of genus
b=q(X)
. By
[
3
, Proposition 2.1]
, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\chi(\omega_{X})\leq p_{g}(X)(1+\frac{1}{p_{g}(F)}).

Remark that we have

p_{g}(X)=h^{0}(f_{\ast}\omega_{X})\geq\chi(f_{\ast}\omega_{X})\geq p_{g}(F)(q(X)-1).

We proceed with the proof by three cases:

Case 1.

p_{g}(F)\geq 9
.

We deduce that

d\leq\frac{64\chi(\omega_{X})}{p_{g}(X)-3}\leq\frac{64p_{g}(X)(1+\frac{1}{p_{g}(F)})}{p_{g}(X)-3}<72.

Case 2.

p_{g}(F)\leq 8
and
b=q(X)\leq 7
.

In this case, we have
\chi(\omega_{X})\leq p_{g}(X)+6
. We deduce that

d\leq\frac{64\chi(\omega_{X})}{p_{g}(X)-3}\leq\frac{64(p_{g}(X)+6)}{p_{g}(X)-3}<67.

Case 3.

p_{g}(F)\leq 8
and
b=q(X)\geq 8
.

Up to some birational modifications of
X
and
\Sigma
, we may assume that
\phi_{X}
is a morphism,
\Sigma
is smooth. Let
H
be a very ample divisor on
Y
. Choose two elements
D_{1},D_{2}\in|H|
with
\mathrm{supp}D_{1}\cap\mathrm{supp}D_{2}=\emptyset
. Put
\widetilde{D_{i}}=\phi_{X,\ast}f^{\ast}D_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Then the linear pencil spanned by
\widetilde{D_{1}}
and
\widetilde{D_{2}}
induces a rational map
\Sigma\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
. After taking some higher birational model, we may assume that the rational map
\Sigma\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a morphism. Let
h\colon\Sigma\to C
be a fibration obtained from the Stein factorization of
\Sigma\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then we have the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}

The image of the evaluation map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{ev}\colon H^{0}(f_{\ast}\omega_{X})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{\ast}\omega_{X}}

generates a rank
r
subsheaf
(f_{\ast}\omega_{X})_{\mathrm{ev}}
of
f_{\ast}\omega_{X}
. We consider separately the two subcases.

Subcase
r<p_{g}(F)

By Theorem
2.1
, we deduce that

b-1\leq(b-1)(p_{g}(F)-r)\leq r\leq p_{g}(F)-1.

It follows that
b\leq p_{g}(F)\leq 8
. Note that

\chi(\omega_{X})=\chi(f_{\ast}\omega_{X})+b-1\leq p_{g}(X)+b-1\leq p_{g}(X)+7.

Then we have

d\leq\frac{64\chi(\omega_{X})}{p_{g}(X)-3}\leq\frac{64(p_{g}(X)+7)}{p_{g}(X)-3}<67.

Subcase
r=p_{g}(F)

In this case, the natural restriction map

H^{0}(K_{X})\to H^{0}(K_{F})

is surjective. We conclude that

(3.3)

d=\deg(\phi_{F})\cdot\deg(\delta).

By adopting the idea in
[
1
]
, one can prove that
\deg(\phi_{F})\leq 36
(see
[
11
, Proposition 1.1]
for the proof).
We are left to bound
\deg(\delta)
. To this end, take a global section
t\in H^{0}(f_{\ast}\omega_{X})
such that the zero divisor
D_{t}
has maximal degree.

If
\deg(D_{t})>\frac{3}{2}b
, then by Riemann-Roch theorem, we deduce that

h^{0}(D_{t})\geq\deg(D_{t})+1-b>\frac{1}{3}\deg(D_{t})+1.

Consider the rational map induced by linear system
|D_{t}|
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{|D_{t}|}\colon Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma_{t}\subset\mathbb{P}^{h^{0}(D_{t})-1}}

then
\deg(D_{t})\geq\deg\phi_{|D_{t}|}\cdot\deg(\Sigma_{t})>\frac{1}{3}\deg(D_{t})\cdot\deg(\phi_{|D_{t}|})
. So we have
\deg(\phi_{|D_{t}|})\leq 2
. Since
f^{\ast}|D_{t}|
is a sub-linear system of
|K_{X}|
, the map
\phi_{|D_{t}|}
factors through
\delta
. It follows that
\deg(\delta)\leq\deg(\phi_{|D_{t}|})\leq 2
.

Now we consider the case where
\deg(D_{t})\leq\frac{3}{2}b
. Let
D
be a sum of
b-2
general points on
Y
. By Riemann-Roch theorem, we deduce that

\displaystyle h^{0}(f_{\ast}\omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-D))

\displaystyle\geq\chi(f_{\ast}\omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-D))

\displaystyle\geq\deg(f_{\ast}\omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-D))-p_{g}(F)(b-1)

\displaystyle\geq p_{g}(F)>0.

Let
s\in H^{0}(Y,f_{\ast}\omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-D))
be a nonzero section, then after twisting by
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(D)
yields a section

of
f_{\ast}\omega_{X}
, with the corresponding divisor
D_{1}
satisfying
D_{1}=D+div(s)\geq D
. Hence
\deg(\delta_{\ast}D_{1})\geq b-2
. The rational map
\phi_{|D_{1}|}
, defined by linear system
|D_{1}|
, factors through
\delta
, and
t\in H^{0}(f_{\ast}\omega_{X})
has maximal degree, we infer that

\frac{3}{2}b\geq\deg(D_{t})\geq\deg(D_{1})\geq\deg(\delta)\cdot(b-2),

which implies that
\deg(\delta)\leq 2
since
b=q(X)\geq 8
.

Thus we always have
\deg(\delta)\leq 2
. We conclude that
\deg(\phi_{X})\leq 72
by equality (
3.3
).

For the case
d=72
. By the above discussion, we know that
\deg(\phi_{F})=36
and
\deg(\delta)=2
. It follows that
F
is a smooth minimal surface of general type with
p_{g}(F)=3,q(F)=0
and
K_{F}^{2}=36
. The proof is completed.

]

Example 3.3
.

Let
S
be a minimal surface of general type with

and the canonical map of

36
. Let
C
be a hyperelliptic curve of genus

. Consider the product
