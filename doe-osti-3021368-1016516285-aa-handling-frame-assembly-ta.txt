1 System Scope Summary
Within the NIF (Na onal Igni on Facility) sustainment project, LLNL is replacing all 1,728 blast shields with new
ones. Before the new blast shield glass can be installed on NIF, it must pass through the AMOL (Ampliﬁer Main
Op cs Loop). The AMOL process involves cleaning and coa ng the glass, assembling it into a Line Replaceable
Unit (LRU), and performing tes ng and inspec on.

                                    ELM Product – 1016516285-AA                                     Page 3 of 94

This work will be conducted in the OPFX cleanroom facility (Building 391, Room 1250) and requires several pieces
of custom equipment. One key piece of equipment within the loop is the Handling Frame Assembly Table (see
Figure 1.1). Its purpose is to assist operators in installing the blast shield glass into handling frames
(1014442709) (see Figure 1.2). The table is also used to store handling frame assembly components. The primary
components stored are the L-frames (1015208271), which are kept in a drawer underneath the table (see Figure
1.3).

The purpose of this safety note is to ensure that the system does not pose a risk of injury to personnel during a
seismic event. The system owner is OMST Engineering & Maintenance.

2 Engineering Analysis
The analysis methodology used for the engineering analysis references the DSS, speciﬁcally the sec on 1.2
(Threaded Fasteners), sec on 5.2 (Seismic Analysis), and sec on 5.7 (Welded Assemblies). All DSS sec ons used
are men oned in sec on 6 of this document.
The evaluated scenario assumes the worst-case conﬁgura on of the system, that is, the highest center of gravity
and maximum weight. This occurs when the blast shield handling frame is assembled on top of the sta on.
The analysis began by modeling the system as a single rigid body, applying seismic loads at the center of gravity,
and calcula ng the reac ons at the connec on between the table and the ﬂoor (see Figure 2.1). A er
determining these reac ons, the table was divided into mul ple rigid bodies, and the forces and moments at
each interface were computed using the reac on forces solved for in the ﬁrst single rigid body model. Once all
forces and moments ac ng on each component were established, the stress within each component along the
load path was evaluated to assess poten al failure (see Appendix A). To validate the calcula ons, the resul ng
forces at the tabletop center of gravity from the mul ple rigid body model were compared to the seismic forces
ini ally applied to the table to show they are equivalent.
The key assump ons of the system are listed below to solve the reac ons and forces ac ng on the system during
a seismic event.
 1. The front legs connec on to the ﬂoor are modeled as rollers under seismic force in the X direc on, since
 there is no front bracing plate and s ﬀness is lower compared to the rear legs, allowing movement in
 that direc on. The front legs under seismic force in the remaining direc ons and the rear legs under
 load in all direc ons are modeled as pinned.
 2. Connec ons between bracing plates (Item 5 & 6) and legs are transla onally ﬁxed in all direc ons. The
 back bracing plate to leg connec ons is considered rota onally ﬁxed about the Y and the side bracing
 plate to leg connec ons is considered rota onally ﬁxed about the X. These assump ons are made
 because the bracing plates s ﬀness prevents rota on about speciﬁc axes.
 3. Connec ons between legs and tabletop are assumed to be pinned, as the s ﬀness of the tabletop to leg
 connec on is assumed to be less than the bracing plates below and allows rota on.
 4. Axial forces in the bracing plates are assumed to be zero, as bending stresses dominate.
 5. Axial forces in the bolts connec ng the bracing plates to the legs are assumed to be zero, since the
 bracing plate in the parallel direc on to axial loading is expected to carry the load due to its higher
 s ﬀness than the perpendicular bracing plate.

Since the direc on of seismic forces can vary, each component within the system may experience diﬀerent
combina ons of forces and, consequently, a diﬀerent worst-case SF on an individual component basis. For the
purposes of this note, only a single case is analyzed with respect to the direc ons of the applied forces. However,
all possible combina ons of seismic force direc ons were evaluated, and each component achieved a rare event
safety factor greater than 1. The scenario analyzed in this note is SDZ and SDY as posi ve, while SDX is nega ve.
This is the worst-case scenario as it results in the lowest safety factor for any individual component. The square
root of the sum of the squares (SRSS) method, discussed in the DSS, is used for combining forces ac ng in the
same direc on resul ng from seismic loading in diﬀerent direc ons.
The loading of the blast shield handling frame assembly on the Delrin ﬁxturing and its bolted connec on to the
table surface during a seismic event is considered acceptable based on engineering judgment and is therefore
not included in the load path discussion below. In the worst case, during a seismic event, the handling frame
assembly (weight approximately 100 lb) is expected to apply a total shear load of about 200 lb to the Delrin
guides. This load is shared by two guides, each with a cross sec on of 1/4 in by 3 in. Each Delrin guide is a ached
to the table with two M6 bolts, so each bolt is expected to carry approximately 50 lb of shear. Given the low
magnitude of the applied loads rela ve to the size and capacity of the Delrin guides and bolts, failure of these
components is not worth analyzing in a detailed calcula on.
