 Executive Summary
Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) analyzed samples from Tank 38H, Tank 43H and Tank 22H to
support the Enrichment Control Program (ECP) and Corrosion Control Program (CCP). The results indicate the
concentrations of most soluble species in the Tank 38H surface sample are ~ 76% of the concentrations in the
previous Tank 38H surface sample. The current Tank 38H subsurface sample is a clear solution with soluble
species that are ~ 70% of the concentrations in the previous Tank 38H subsurface. Significant differences in the
concentrations of major components between the current Tank 38H surface and subsurface samples indicate
stratification of solution species between these two locations within Tank 38H.

Both the recent March 2026 Tank 43H surface sample and Tank 43H subsurface sample show similar
composition to the previous respective Tank 43H samples that were pulled in October of 2025. Similar solution
compositions measured in the current Tank 43H surface and subsurface samples indicate a minimal stratification
within the tank.

Total uranium in the current Tank 38H surface sample (6.6 mg/L) is slightly less than the total uranium from the
previous Tank 38H surface analysis (8 mg/L). Plutonium isotopes are slightly higher than those of the previous
Tank 38H surface samples. The Tank 38H subsurface sample shows similar plutonium isotope concentrations
compared to the previous 38H subsurface sample. The total uranium concentration in the two Tank 43H samples
is slightly lower than the previous Tank 43H sample results with all total uranium values in the range of 20 mg/L
to 26 mg/L. The Pu-238 concentrations in the current Tank 43H surface sample and the Tank 43H subsurface
sample are similar, respectively, to previous Tank 43H surface and subsurface samples.

The sum of the major detectable cations (Na and K) versus the sum of the major detectable anions shows a
difference of ~1.2% or less for the relatively concentrated samples (Tank 38H subsurface and both Tank 43H
samples) providing indication of good data quality for the non-radioactive analytes in the samples. The current
average silicon concentration measured in the Tank 38H subsurface sample shows a slight increase (46 mg/L)
when compared with the previous Tank 38H subsurface sample results (40 mg/L). Silicon concentrations for the
Tank 38H surface sample and both Tank 43H samples in the range of 30 mg/L to 46 mg/L are slightly higher to
the respective previous samples.

All of the data for the Tank 22H surface and subsurface dark-colored samples indicate similar concentrations to
the previous Tank 22H data. Exceptions are the uranium and plutonium isotope Tank 22H subsurface
concentrations. For the Tank 22H subsurface sample, uranium isotope concentrations were only 31% and
plutonium isotopes were only 33% of the respective isotope concentrations in the previous Tank 22H subsurface
sample. These concentrations of U and Pu are likely associated with the trace observable insoluble solids that
were visible upon settling with the Tank 22H subsurface sample.

1.0 Introduction
Feed limits have been established for the 2H-Evaporator system to ensure nuclear criticality is
not possible and corrosion is minimized1. These limits are protected by the Enrichment Control
Program (ECP) and the Corrosion Control Program (CCP) that require periodic sampling and
analysis to confirm that the waste supernate composition stays within the limits2,3.

Savannah River Mission Completion (SRMC) obtained samples from two different heights within
each of the three waste tanks supporting the 2H-Evaporator operations. Tank 38H and Tank 43H
samples were pulled on March 6, 2026, and were received by the SRNL Shielded Cells Facility
(SCF) on March 9, 2026. Tank 22H samples are included in this study because of recent
significant transfers into Tk 38H. Tank 22H samples were pulled on March 9, 2026, and were
received by the SRNL SCF on March 10, 2026. Analysis of these samples provides information
necessary for determining compliance with the ECP and CCP. The sample characterization was
requested via a Technical Task Request (TTR)4 and conducted based on a Task Technical and
Quality Assurance Plan (TTQAP)5. Waste tank information provided by email communication
from SRMC to SRNL indicates that the 2H evaporator cumulatively operated for ~ 1132 hours
from early October of 2025 through early March of 2026. According to the SRMC Electronic
Morning Report Version v1.6.17.0 database, during this time Tank 38H received ~ 10,000 gallons
of rain/flush water, and ~ 447,000 gallons from Tank 22. Approximately 1,110,000 gallons were
transferred during this time from Tank 38H to Tank 43H in addition to the ~ 18,000 gallons of
various aqueous streams added to Tank 43H.

2.0 Experimental
2.1 Technical Details and Procedures

The March 2026 samples from Tank 38H, 43H and 22H were opened in the SRNL SCF and
poured into clear plastic 100-mL beakers. The beakers were photographed, and the masses of
the samples were determined. Table 2-1 provides the sampling height and mass of each sample.
Figure 2-1 shows photographs of the samples in clear beakers. The Tank 38H and Tank 43H
samples are mostly clear and show no visible undissolved solids when poured into the plastic
clear beakers. Both Tank 22H samples are dark-colored solutions. The Tank 22H surface and
subsurface samples remained a dark colored solution with overnight storage, similar to the
previous Tank 22H samples from October 2025.6 The current Tank 38H subsurface sample was
pulled at the same height from the tank bottom (255 inches) compared to the previous sample
(255 inches).6

All six samples received the analyses required by the ECP that includes determination of uranium
isotopes by uranium separation/inductively coupled plasma-mass spectrometry (S/ICP-MS) and
determination of plutonium isotopes by radiochemical separation and counting methods.
Additionally, the samples were also submitted for gamma spectroscopy to determine the Cs-137
concentration and inductively coupled plasma-optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES) to
determine Na, Al, Si, and other metals. All six samples from all three tanks also received the
characterizations required by the CCP. The CCP analysis suite includes determination of free
hydroxide by acid titration and anions by ion chromatography (IC). The total inorganic carbon

 1

(TIC) and total organic carbon (TOC) were also determined. The TIC value provides a
concentration for the carbonate anion in the samples. Density measurements were made on well-
mixed (unfiltered) aliquots of the samples using calibrated volumetric tubes at ambient cell
temperature (18 °C) as measured from an arbitrary K-type thermocouple that is typically used to
monitor drying oven temperatures within the SRNL SCF.

For the samples receiving the CCP analysis suite, de-ionized (DI) water dilutions were made in
triplicate from a well-mixed (unfiltered) sample and submitted to SRNL Analytical & Tank Farm
Characterization (A&TFC) for analysis. A blank of DI water was also prepared along with the
samples. The water diluted samples were analyzed by ion chromatography, TIC/TOC, and free
hydroxide methods. Triplicate aliquots were prepared from the well-mixed (unfiltered) samples
for the ECP analysis suite using the warm acid strike method that involved dissolving 2 mL of the
well-mixed sample into a total volume of 100 mL.7 A DI water reagent blank and three silicon
standard solutions were submitted for analysis with the tank samples. The samples prepared by
warm acid strike were submitted to A&TFC and Nuclear Measurements (NM) for analysis by ICP-
OES, uranium isotopics, plutonium isotopics, and gamma spectroscopy. During warm acid strike
preparation of the samples, no visible insoluble solids were observed after heating the samples
in nitric acid and dissolution to the final diluted volume of 100 mL.

3.0 Results and Discussion

Table 3-1 contains the results from the analyses of the 2H-Evaporator system samples. These
results were originally reported in SRNL memorandum SRNL-3130000-2026-00010, Rev. 0 in
April 202614. The table shows the average concentrations and the percent relative standard
deviation (%RSD) for the triplicate sample preparations unless otherwise noted. Results
preceded by “<” indicate the analyte concentration or activity was below the limits of quantification
for all three replicate aliquots of the sample. In this case, the less than value in the table results
from averaging the three less than values for the replicates. The %RSD presented in the table
only includes the uncertainty associated with subsampling/sample preparation in the SRNL SCF
and for the analytical measurements. Estimated one sigma analytical uncertainty provides an
indication of the uncertainty associated with the analytical method as reported by A&TFC and
NM. These estimated one sigma uncertainties are 5% for density, Cs-137 and ICP-OES, and
10% for free hydroxide; 7% for TIC/TOC; 10% to 17% for Uranium isotopics and IC-Anions; 15%
to 35% for Pu-241 liquid scintillation; 5% to 30% for Pu-239/240 alpha pulse height analysis; and
5% to 12% for Pu-238 alpha pulse height analysis. Neither of these measures of uncertainty
include the uncertainty associated with sampling a large waste tank. Previous investigations
indicate the uncertainty from taking a small sample from a large waste tank can be
significant.11,12,13

Results in Table 3-1 indicate the concentrations of most species in the Tank 38H surface sample
are only ~76% of the concentrations the previous ECP surface sample (HTF-38-25-58) reported
in October of 2025.6 TOC in the Tank 38H surface sample is similarly not detectable (<5.13E+02
mg C/L) compared to the previous sample (<5.07E+02 mg C/L). The Tank 38H surface sample
has a sum of anions (<9.9E-01 M) and sum of cations (7.4E-01 M). The sum of cations is ~76%
of the concentrations of the previous Tank 38H surface samples (sum of anions = 9.70E-01 M)
and (sum of cations = 9.74E-01 M).6

Total uranium in the current Tank 38H surface sample (6.6 mg/L) is ~ 82% of the total uranium
from the previous Tank 38H surface analysis (8 mg/L). Plutonium isotopes are slightly higher
than the Pu concentrations of the previous Tank 38H surface samples. The Tank 38H subsurface
sample shows similar plutonium isotope concentrations compared to the previous Tank 38H
subsurface sample.6 The weight fraction of U-235 to U-total in the Tank 38H surface sample, at

 4

1.25%, is similar to the previous Tank 38H surface sample at 1.00%. The U-235 to U-total weight
fraction of 0.85% in the current Tank 38H subsurface sample is slightly higher than the 0.82%
weight fraction in the previous Tank 38H subsurface sample.6 Uranium isotopes are based on
S/ICP-MS analyses. Significant differences in sodium and other major component ICP-OES
metals concentrations between the current Tank 38H surface (Na+ = 0.70 M) and Tank 38H
subsurface samples (Na+ = 6.5M) indicate obvious stratification of solution species within the tank
locations where these two different samples were pulled. The density of the Tank 38H subsurface
sample (1.29 g/mL) is slightly higher than the Tank 38H surface sample (1.27 g/mL).

The Tank 43H surface sample soluble species composition indicates that the current sample is
similar in concentration (~90%) to the previous Tank 43H surface sample (HTF-43-25-60).6 The
Tank 43H subsurface sample is similar to the previous Tank 43H subsurface sample (HTF-43-
25-61) for most of the highly soluble species analyzed. Sodium concentration in the current Tank
43H subsurface sample of 5.6 M is the same order of magnitude sodium concentration (5.7 M)
measured in the previous Tank 43H subsurface sample. Both current Tank 43H samples show
less than detectable concentrations of TOC. Both of the Tank 43H surface sample levels are
comparable to the respective below detection TOC concentrations of the previous Tank 43H
samples.6 Total uranium concentrations in the Tank 43H surface and subsurface samples are
slightly lower concentrations than those measured in the previous respective Tank 43H surface
and subsurface samples. Plutonium isotope concentrations in the current Tank 43H surface
sample and Tank 43H subsurface samples are significantly lower compared to the previous Tank
43H surface and subsurface sample results.6 The U-235 weight fraction of ~0.8% is similar to the
previous analysis in both the Tank 43H surface and subsurface samples. Current Tank 43H
subsurface species comparison to similar species in current Tank 43H surface sample indicates
the subsurface sample is ~ 40% more concentrated than the surface sample, which indicates
minimal stratification within the tank. The Pu-238 and Si concentrations are similar for both
current Tank 43H samples and the Tank 43H subsurface Cs-137 concentration is 70% of the
Tank 43H surface sample. The density of the current Tank 43H subsurface sample (1.26 g/mL)
is also less than 10% higher than the current Tank 43H surface sample (1.19 g/mL).

Results for most species, as presented in Table 3-1, along with the density measurements,
generally show small percent relative standard deviation (%RSD) values up to ~ 5% for replicate
analyses. Exceptions of higher %RSD values are reported for the various detectable Pu isotopes.
All reported TOC values are less than detectable for all samples. The magnitude for the detection
limits for TOC (~ 500 mg C/L for Tank 38H and Tank 43H) are similar to previous detectable
concentrations reported for Tank 38H and Tank 43H samples. The sum of the major detectable
cations (Na and K) versus the sum of the major detectable anions shows a difference of ~1.2%
or less for the relatively high concentration samples (Tk 38H subsurface and both Tank 43H
samples) providing indication of good data quality for the non-radioactive analytes in the samples.
The relatively lower concentration samples (Tank 38H surface and both Tank 22H samples) have
less than detectable free hydroxide concentrations which causes the sum of anions to be reported
as ‘less than’ values. The magnitude of these less than sum of anions concentrations exceed the
sum of cation concentrations by ~ 30% for Tank 38H surface sample and ~ 60% for both Tank
22H samples.

The Cs-137 results for the current Tank 38H samples indicate an increase of 1.06X and 1.56X in
the surface and subsurface samples, respectively, versus previous Tank 38H samples.6 The Cs-
137 results for the current Tank 43H samples indicates an increase of 1.4X and 1.9X in the
surface and subsurface samples, respectively, versus previous Tank 43H samples. Silicon
concentrations for the Tank 38H surface sample and both Tank 43H samples are similar to the
respective previous samples.6 The current average silicon concentration measured in the Tank
38H subsurface sample (4.59E+01 mg/L) is similar to the previous Tank 38H subsurface sample

Standards used for the silicon analysis (50 mg/L silicon in the solution prepared by warm acid
strike diluted to final concentrations of 0.5, 1.0, and 2.0 mg/L) were close to the target
concentrations with differences from the targeted concentrations within ± 5.8%. The silicon
concentration was below detectable levels in the process blank. The DI water blanks prepared
for the water dilutions contained no detectable analytes for the TIC/TOC, free hydroxide and anion
analyses. The DI water blank prepared for warm acid strike showed no measurable analytes for
the ICP-OES analysis and no detectable radionuclides except for Pu-238. The detectable amount
of Pu-238 in the water blank was ~ 4X to 19X lower than any of the measured Pu-238
concentrations in the actual tank samples.

All of the data for the Tank 22H surface and subsurface dark-colored samples indicate similar
concentrations to the previous Tank 22H data. The only exceptions are the uranium and plutonium
isotope Tank 22H subsurface concentrations. For the Tank 22H subsurface sample, uranium
isotope concentrations were only 31% and plutonium isotopes were only 33% of the respective
isotope concentrations in the previous Tank 22H subsurface sample. These concentrations of U
and Pu are likely associated with the trace observable insoluble solids that were visible upon
prolonged settling with the Tank 22H subsurface sample.

At the request of SRMC, total mercury via DMA and methyl mercury were run on the Tank 22H
subsurface sample as a single analysis from one of the triplicate warm acid strike dilutions. The
total mercury concentration and the methyl mercury concentration were reported as 2,123.8 mg/L
and 108.8 mg/L, respectively. Since only a single analysis was performed for each of these
mercury methods, there is no percent relative standard deviation associated with these results.
The 1 uncertainty reported for the analytical method is 10% for both the total mercury and methyl
mercury.

4.0 Conclusions
Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) analyzed samples from Tank 38H, Tank 43H and
Tank 22H to support the Enrichment Control Program (ECP) and Corrosion Control Program
(CCP). The results indicate the concentrations of most soluble species in the Tank 38H surface
sample are ~ 76% of the concentrations in the previous Tank 38H surface sample. The current
Tank 38H subsurface sample is a clear solution with soluble species that are ~ 70% of the
concentrations in the previous Tank 38H subsurface. Significant differences in the concentrations
of major components between the current Tank 38H surface and subsurface samples indicate
stratification of solution species between these two locations within the Tank 38H.

Both the recent March 2026 Tank 43H surface sample and Tank 43H subsurface sample show
similar composition to the previous respective Tank 43H samples that were pulled in October of
2025. Similar solution compositions measured in the current Tank 43H surface and subsurface
samples indicate a minimal stratification within the tank.

Total uranium in the current Tank 38H surface sample (6.6 mg/L) is slightly less than the total
uranium from the previous Tank 38H surface analysis (8 mg/L). Plutonium isotopes are slightly
higher than those of the previous Tank 38H surface samples. The Tank 38H subsurface sample
shows similar plutonium isotope concentrations compared to the previous Tank 38H subsurface
sample. The total uranium concentration in the two Tank 43H samples is slightly lower than the
previous Tank 43H sample results with all total uranium values in the range of 20 mg/L to 26
mg/L. The Pu-238 concentrations in the current Tank 43H surface sample and the Tank 43H
subsurface sample are similar, respectively, to previous Tank 43H surface and subsurface
samples.

The sum of the major detectable cations (Na and K) versus the sum of the major detectable anions
shows a difference of ~1.2% or less for the relatively concentrated samples (Tank 38H subsurface
and both Tank 43H samples) providing indication of good data quality for the non-radioactive
analytes in the samples. The current average silicon concentration measured in the Tank 38H
subsurface sample shows a slight increase (46 mg/L) when compared with the previous Tank
38H subsurface sample results (40 mg/L). Silicon concentrations for the Tank 38H surface
sample and both Tank 43H samples in the range of 30 mg/L to 46 mg/L are slightly higher to the
respective previous samples.

All of the data for the Tank 22H surface and subsurface dark-colored samples indicate similar
concentrations to the previous Tank 22H data. Exceptions are the uranium and plutonium isotope
Tank 22H subsurface concentrations. For the Tank 22H subsurface sample, uranium isotope
concentrations were only 31% and plutonium isotopes were only 33% of the respective isotope
concentrations in the previous Tank 22H subsurface sample. These concentrations of U and Pu
are likely associated with the trace observable insoluble solids that were visible upon settling with
the Tank 22H subsurface sample.
