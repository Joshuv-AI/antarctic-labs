Norming Approximate Orthogonality in Normed Linear Spaces
Saikat Roy

Abstract
. We introduce and study the notion of norming approximate orthogonality , a two-parameter generalization of Birkhoff–James orthogonality in normed linear spaces. For δ , ε ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) \delta,\varepsilon\in[0,1) with ε < ( 1 − δ ) 2 \varepsilon<(1-\delta)^{2} , we say x ⟂ δ ε y x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}y in X X if there exists f ∈ X ∗ f\in X^{*} with | f ⁡ ( x ) | ≥ ( 1 − δ ) ​ ‖ f ‖ ​ ‖ x ‖ |f(x)|\geq(1-\delta)\|f\|\|x\| and | f ⁡ ( y ) | ≤ ε 1 − δ ​ ‖ f ‖ ​ ‖ y ‖ |f(y)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|f\|\|y\| , simultaneously relaxing both the norming condition on x x and the vanishing condition on y y . It is proved that x ⟂ δ ε y ⇔ ∥ x + λ y ∥ ≥ ( 1 − δ ) ∥ x ∥ − ε 1 − δ ∥ λ y ∥ ∀ scalars λ . x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}y\iff\|x+\lambda y\|\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|\lambda y\|~\qquad\forall~\text{scalars}~\lambda. This framework interpolates between two notions of approximate orthogonality in normed linear spaces due to Chmieliński and Dragomir, and recovers three existing notions of orthogonality in extreme cases: exact Birkhoff–James orthogonality at δ = ε = 0 \delta=\varepsilon=0 , the approximate orthogonality of Chmieliński at δ = 0 \delta=0 , and the approximate orthogonality of Dragomir at ε = 0 \varepsilon=0 . In Hilbert spaces such an orthogonality is characterized by an inner product bound L ⁡ ( δ , ε ) < 1 L(\delta,\varepsilon)<1 , namely x ⟂ δ ε y ⇔ | ⟨ x , y ⟩ | ≤ L ( δ , ε ) ∥ x ∥ ∥ y ∥ . x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}y\iff|\langle x,y\rangle|\leq L(\delta,\varepsilon)\|x\|\|y\|. A two-parameter proximity result generalizing Chmlieński’s characterization of ⟂ B ε \perp_{B}^{\varepsilon} is established. The forward and converse implications are governed by the distinct thresholds ε ( 1 − δ ) 2 \frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\delta)^{2}} and ε 1 − δ \frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta} , which collapse to ε \varepsilon of Chmieliński precisely when δ = 0 \delta=0 , and the strictness of this gap is confirmed by counterexamples in ℓ ∞ 2 \ell_{\infty}^{2} . We apply our results to (vector-valued) continuous function spaces, which extends some earlier results and recovers few operator theoretical results with alternative proofs using measure theoretic techniques.

1.
Introduction

All normed linear spaces are over
\mathbb{C}
; results remain valid over

\mathbb{R}
with straightforward modifications. We write
X^{*}
for the (topological) dual of
X
,
and
B_{X}
,
S_{X}
for the closed unit ball and unit sphere of
X
, respectively. For any
\epsilon>0
,
B(0,\epsilon)
denotes the closed disc of radius
\epsilon
centred at
0
in the complex plane. For any non-zero
x
in a normed linear space
X
, we denote the collection of all support functionals at
x
, by
J(x)=\{f\in S_{X^{*}}:~f(x)=\|x\|\}
, which is non-empty by the Hahn-Banach Theorem.

1.1.
Motivation and Background

Orthogonality in a normed linear space is captured by the Birkhoff–James
relation
[
2
]
:
x\perp_{B}y
if
\|x+\lambda y\|\geq\|x\|

for all scalars
\lambda
, or equivalently, if there exists
f\in X^{*}
with

\|f\|=1
,
f(x)=\|x\|
and
f(y)=0
. Birkhoff-James orthogonality has a deep connection with the norm attainment set of functionals
[
13
,
14
,
17
]
, namely
x\perp_{B}y
if and only if there exists a continuous linear functional that attains
its norm at
x
and vanishes at
y
. Two independent lines of
relaxation have been pursued in the literature. Dragomir
[
12
]
introduced
the first approximate version, defining
x\perp^{\varepsilon}_{D}y
for

\varepsilon\in[0,1)
by the following norm inequality

\|x+\lambda y\|\geq(1-\varepsilon)\|x\|\quad\text{for all scalars }\lambda,

Another notion of approximate orthogonality is introduced by Chmieliński
[
5
]
defining
x\perp_{B}^{\varepsilon}y
by

\|x+\lambda y\|^{2}\geq\|x\|^{2}-2\varepsilon|\lambda|\,\|x\|\,\|\lambda y\|\quad\text{for all scalars }\lambda,

and this relation has since been studied
extensively
[
8
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
20
,
21
,
16
,
1
]
.
While
x\perp_{B}^{\varepsilon}y
possesses an equivalent form in terms of continuous linear functionals attaining norm at
x
and approximately vanishes at
y

[
10
,
20
]

x\perp_{B}^{\varepsilon}y\iff\exists\,f\in X^{*}:\|f\|=1,\ f(x)=\|x\|,\ |f(y)|\leq\varepsilon\|y\|,

the equivalent continuous linear functional version for
x\perp^{\varepsilon}_{D}y
is absent.
These two notions lacked a common parametric framework interpolating between them, and reduces to the Birkhoff–James orthogonality in the limiting case
\varepsilon=0
. The present paper addresses both simultaneously, asking what orthogonality is induced when one requires a functional that

approximately
attains its norm at
x
and
approximately

vanishes at
y
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space and
x,y\in X
. Let
\delta,\varepsilon\in[0,1)

satisfy
\varepsilon<(1-\delta)^{2}
. Then
x
is said to be

(\delta,\varepsilon)
-norming approximately orthogonal
to
y
, written

x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}y
, if

\exists\,f\in X^{*}:\quad|f(x)|\geq(1-\delta)\|f\|\|x\|,\quad|f(y)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|f\|\|y\|.

Remark 1.2
.

The relation
\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}
recovers all three classical notions: setting

\delta=\varepsilon=0
gives exact Birkhoff–James orthogonality;

\delta=0
gives Chmieliński’s
\perp_{B}^{\varepsilon}

[
5
,
10
]
;
and
\varepsilon=0
gives what we call
\delta
-norming orthogonality

\perp^{0}_{\delta}
, the functional analytic counterpart of Dragomir’s approximate
orthogonality
[
12
]
, as made precise by condition (iv) of
Theorem
1.3
below. The
\varepsilon=0
, specialization requiring
a functional that approximately attains its norm at
x
and exactly vanishes at

y
does not appear to have been studied from a functional analytic perspective,
and its characterization is a new contribution of the present work.

The condition
\varepsilon<(1-\delta)^{2}
ensures non-degeneracy:

x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}x
if and only if
x=0
. The relation is homogeneous and, when

x\neq 0
, forces linear independence of
\{x,y\}
. It is also monotone:

x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}y
implies
x\perp_{\alpha}^{\beta}y
for all
\alpha\geq\delta
,

\beta\geq\varepsilon
satisfying
\beta<(1-\alpha)^{2}
.

1.2.
Statement of Main Results

Our first main result gives a complete characterization of
\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}

through several equivalent conditions. In the spirit of
[
10
]
,
Theorem
1.3
extends the functional characterization of

\perp_{B}^{\varepsilon}
to the two-parameter setting and to complex spaces.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space,
x,y\in X
non-zero, and
\delta,\varepsilon\in[0,1)
with
\varepsilon<(1-\delta)^{2}
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}y
.

(ii)

\exists\,f\in X^{*}
with
f(x)\geq(1-\delta)\|f\|\|x\|

and
|f(y)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|f\|\|y\|
.

(iii)

\exists\,f\in X^{*}
with
\mathrm{Re}\,f(x)\geq(1-\delta)\|f\|\|x\|

and
|f(y)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|f\|\|y\|
.

(iv)

\|x+\lambda y\|\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}|\lambda|\|y\|
for all scalars
\lambda
.

Moreover, if
X
is a Hilbert space, then
(i)–(iv)
are further equivalent to:

(v)

|\langle x,y\rangle|\leq L(\delta,\varepsilon)\|x\|\|y\|
, where

L(\delta,\varepsilon)=\varepsilon+\frac{\sqrt{\delta(2-\delta)\left((1-\delta)^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}\right)}}{1-\delta}.

(vi)

\exists\,u\in S_{X}
with
\langle x,u\rangle\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|

and
|\langle y,u\rangle|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|y\|
.

Remark 1.4
.

Condition (iv) makes the unification explicit. Setting
\varepsilon=0

gives
\|x+\lambda y\|\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|
for all scalars
\lambda
,
which is precisely Dragomir’s approximate orthogonality
[
12
]
. Setting

\delta=0
gives
\|x+\lambda y\|\geq\|x\|-\varepsilon|\lambda|\|y\|
,
the equivalent formulation of Chmieliński’s
\perp_{B}^{\varepsilon}

[
8
,
1
]
in terms of norm inequality. Setting
\delta=\varepsilon=0
recovers
the Birkhoff–James inequality. Condition (iv) is thus the natural
interpolant between the Dragomir and Chmieliński formulations, with
\delta

and
\varepsilon
independently controlling the two relaxations. In the Hilbert
space case, the sharp constant
L(\delta,\varepsilon)
satisfies

L(0,\varepsilon)=\varepsilon
(Chmieliński) and

L(\delta,0)=\sqrt{\delta(2-\delta)}
(Dragomir), interpolating between
the two boundary cases.

The single-vector characterization extends uniformly over closed subspaces,
yielding a description of
\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}
relative to a subspace entirely in terms of
the restricted norm of a single functional.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
Y
be a closed subspace of
X
and
x\in X\setminus Y
. Then
x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}y

for every
y\in Y
if and only if there exists
f\in X^{*}
with
\|f\|=1
such that

|f(x)|\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|,\qquad\|f|_{Y}\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}.

The next result addresses the proximity structure of
\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}
. The following result proved in
[
10
]
serves as an alternative characterization of approximate Birkhoff-James orthogonality.

Theorem
.

[
10
, Theorem 2.2]

Let
X
be a real normed space. For
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
and
x,y\in X

x\perp^{\varepsilon}_{B}y\iff\exists~z\in span\{x,y\}:~x\perp_{B}y,~\|z-y\|\leq\varepsilon\|y\|

This result, later extended in the complex case in
[
21
]
. The following theorem generalizes this to the two-parameter setting. A notable feature is that the forward and converse implications involve different threshold constants
\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\delta)^{2}}
and
\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}
respectively, which are equal only when
\delta=0
, precisely the case in which the two implications become equivalent.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space and
\varepsilon,\delta\in[0,1)
be such that

\varepsilon<(1-\delta)^{2}
. Let
x,y\in X
be non-zero. Then the following hold:

(i)

If
x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}y
in
X
, then there exists
z\in\mathrm{span}\{x,y\}

such that
x\perp_{\delta}^{0}z
and
\|z-y\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\delta)^{2}}\|y\|
.

(ii)

If there exists
z\in X
such that
x\perp_{\delta}^{0}z
and

\|z-y\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\delta)}\|y\|
, then
x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}y
.

In particular, when

The converse of (i) and (ii) fails in general, as illustrated in
Examples
5.5
,
5.6
.

The following theorem characterizes norming approximate orthogonality in

X^{*}
through point evaluations in
X
, with the reflexive case yielding a
complete equivalence.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
f,g\in X^{*}
, and
\delta,\varepsilon\in[0,1)
with

\varepsilon<(1-\delta)^{2}
. Then
f\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}g
in
X^{*}
if there
exists
x\in S_{X}
such that

|f(x)|\geq(1-\delta)\|f\|,\qquad|g(x)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|g\|.

Building on Theorem
1.7
, we establish a two-sided estimate
for the orthogonality threshold, recovering at
\delta=\varepsilon=0

the classical characterization of best approximations from a subspace via
Birkhoff–James orthogonality.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
Y
be a closed proper non-trivial subspace of
X
,
x\in X\setminus\{0\}
,
and
\delta\in[0,1)
. Suppose

m_{\delta}(x,Y):=\inf\bigl\{\|f|_{Y}\|:f\in B_{X^{*}},\ |f(x)|\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|\bigr\}<(1-\delta)^{2}.

Then

(1-\delta)\,m_{\delta}(x,Y)\leq\varepsilon_{\min}\leq m_{\delta}(x,Y),

where

\varepsilon_{\min}:=\inf\bigl\{\varepsilon\in[0,(1-\delta)^{2}):x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}Y\bigr\}.

In particular, both infima are attained, and
x\perp_{\delta}^{m_{\delta}(x,Y)}Y
.

2.
Norming Approximate Orthogonality

In this Section we prove Theorem
1.3
, Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.6
, which mainly deals with equivalent criterion of norming approximate orthogonality between two vectors and between a vector and a subspace. We start with the proof of Theorem
1.3
. We begin with a proposition which we need to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\delta,\varepsilon\in[0,1)
with
\varepsilon<(1-\delta)^{2}
. Then
L(\delta,\varepsilon)<1
.

Proof.

Let
a=1-\delta\in(0,1)
. Then

L(\delta,\varepsilon)=\varepsilon+\frac{\sqrt{(1-a^{2})(a^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})}}{a}.

Write

L(\delta,\varepsilon)=\left(1,\frac{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}{a}\right)\cdot\left(\varepsilon,\sqrt{a^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}\right).

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

L(\delta,\varepsilon)\leq\left\|\left(1,\tfrac{\sqrt{1-a^{2}}}{a}\right)\right\|_{2}\left\|\left(\varepsilon,\sqrt{a^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\right\|_{2}=\frac{1}{a}\cdot a=1.

Equality holds if and only if the two vectors are linearly dependent, which yields
\varepsilon=a^{2}
. Since
\varepsilon<a^{2}
, equality cannot occur. Hence
L(\delta,\varepsilon)<1
.
∎

In the Hilbert space setting, the constant
L(\delta,\varepsilon)
determines the strength of the orthogonality relation
\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}
(see Theorem
1.3
). In order for this condition to be non-trivial, it is necessary that
L(\delta,\varepsilon)<1
. The above observation confirms that this is indeed the case whenever
\varepsilon<(1-\delta)^{2}
.

Now, we prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of theorem
1.3
.

Evidently,
(i)
,
(ii)
,
(iii)
are equivalent and they are equivalent to
(vi)
, through the Riesz representation Theorem, if additionally, we assume
X
is a Hilbert space. Therefore, it only remains to prove the equivalence of
(i)
and
(iv)
for general normed linear spaces, and equivalence of
(i)
and
(v)
under the additional assumption of
X
is a Hilbert space. Since norming approximate orthogonality is homogeneous, without loss of generality we assume that
\|x\|=\|y\|=1
.

(i)\implies(iv)
. Suppose there exists
f\in X^{*}

satisfying
(i)
. Then for any scalar
\lambda
,

\|f\|\,\|x+\lambda y\|\geq|f(x+\lambda y)|\geq|f(x)|-|\lambda||f(y)|\geq(1-\delta)\|f\|-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}|\lambda|\|f\|.

Dividing through by
\|f\|>0
yields
(iv)
.

(iv)\implies(i)
. Define a sublinear functional

p:X\to\mathbb{R}
by

p(v)=\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}\left(\|v+\lambda y\|+\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}|\lambda|\right),\quad v\in X.

Positive homogeneity is immediate. Subadditivity follows from the
triangle inequality: for any
v_{1},v_{2}\in X
and
\eta>0
, choosing

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
nearly optimal and setting
\lambda=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
,

p(v_{1}+v_{2})\leq\|v_{1}+\lambda_{1}y\|+\|v_{2}+\lambda_{2}y\|+\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}(|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|)\leq p(v_{1})+p(v_{2})+\eta.

Define
h:\mathrm{span}\{x\}\to\mathbb{C}
by
h(\alpha x)=(1-\delta)\alpha
.
For any
\alpha\neq 0
and
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
, condition
(iv)
gives

\|x+\mu y\|+\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}|\mu|\geq(1-\delta),

so that
p(\alpha x)\geq|\alpha|(1-\delta)=|h(\alpha x)|
. By the
Hahn–Banach theorem,
h
extends to
f\in X^{*}
with
|f(v)|\leq p(v)

for all
v\in X
. Taking
\lambda=0
gives
\|f\|\leq 1
, and taking

\lambda=-1
gives
|f(y)|\leq p(y)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}
.

Consider the set

\widetilde{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}=\left\{g\in X^{*}:\|g\|\leq 1,\;|g(y)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\right\}.

The functional
f
constructed above belongs to
\widetilde{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}
,
so it is nonempty. It is convex and weak
∗
-closed, hence
weak
∗
-compact by the Banach–Alaoglu theorem. The map
g\mapsto|g(x)|

is weak
∗
-continuous, so it attains its maximum on

\widetilde{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}
at some
f_{0}\in\widetilde{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}
.
Since
f\in\widetilde{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}
,

|f_{0}(x)|\geq|f(x)|=1-\delta>0.

We shall now prove
\|f_{0}\|=1
. Since
\varepsilon<(1-\delta)^{2}
, the
set
\{x,y\}
is linearly independent: indeed, if
x=cy
for some scalar

c
, then

\|x-cy\|\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|cy\|=\|x\|\left((1-\delta)-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\right)

which gives

(1-\delta)^{2}\leq\varepsilon,

a contradiction. By the Hahn–Banach Theorem there therefore exists

k\in X^{*}
with
k(x)\neq 0
,
k(y)=0
and
\|k\|=1
. If
\|f_{0}\|<1
, for sufficiently small
\zeta>0
with
\|f_{0}\|+\zeta\leq 1
, define

l=\mathrm{sgn}(f_{0}(x))f_{0}+\zeta k.

Then
\|l\|\leq\|f_{0}\|+\zeta\leq 1
and
|l(y)|=|f_{0}(y)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}
, so
l\in\widetilde{N}^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}
.
However,

|l(x)|=|f_{0}(x)|+\zeta|k(x)|>|f_{0}(x)|,

contradicting the maximality of
f_{0}
. Therefore
\|f_{0}\|=1
, and

|f_{0}(x)|\geq 1-\delta=(1-\delta)\|f_{0}\|\|x\|,\qquad|f_{0}(y)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}=\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|f_{0}\|\|y\|,

and
f_{0}
witnesses condition
(i)
.

For the rest of the proof, we now assume that
X
is a Hilbert space.

(iv)\iff(v)
. For any scalar
\lambda=te^{i\theta}
with
t=|\lambda|\geq 0
,

\|x+\lambda y\|^{2}=1+2t\,\mathrm{Re}(e^{i\theta}\langle x,y\rangle)+t^{2},

which is minimized over all
\lambda

|\lambda|=t

e^{i\theta}\langle x,y\rangle=-|\langle x,y\rangle|
, giving

\min_{|\lambda|=t}\|x+\lambda y\|^{2}=1-2t|\langle x,y\rangle|+t^{2}.

Since the right side of
(iv)
depends only on
t=|\lambda|
, condition
(iv)
holds for all scalars

if and only if

\forall~t\geq 0:\qquad\sqrt{1-2t|\langle x,y\rangle|+t^{2}}\geq(1-\delta)-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}t.

If the right side is negative the inequality holds trivially,
so we may assume that
(1-\delta)-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}t\geq 0
,
and squaring both sides, condition
(iv)
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\forall~t\geq 0:\qquad 1-2t|\langle x,y\rangle|+t^{2}\geq\left[(1-\delta)-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}t\right]^{2}.

(II)

Expanding and rearranging, (II) holds if and only if

\displaystyle\forall~t\geq 0:\qquad Q(t):=\left(\frac{(1-\delta)^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}{(1-\delta)^{2}}\right)t^{2}+2\left(\varepsilon-|\langle x,y\rangle|\right)t+\delta(2-\delta)\geq 0.

(III)

Q(t)=At^{2}+2Bt+C,\qquad t\geq 0.

Since

(1-\delta)^{2}<1-\delta

,

.

, that is

. Since

.
Therefore, the continuous convex function

Substituting the values of

Proof of theorem

The implication

. Define

p(v)=\inf_{w\in Y}\left(\|v+w\|+\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|w\|\right),\qquad v\in X.

Arguing exactly as in the proof of Theorem
1.3
,

p
is sublinear. Since
x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}y
for every
y\in Y
, Theorem
1.3
(iv)
implies

\|x+w\|+\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|w\|\geq 1-\delta\qquad\text{for all }w\in Y.

Hence
p(x)\geq 1-\delta
. Define
h:\mathrm{span}\{x\}\to\mathbb{C}
by

h(\alpha x)=(1-\delta)\alpha.

Then

|h(\alpha x)|=|\alpha|(1-\delta)\leq p(\alpha x).

By the Hahn–Banach theorem,
h
extends to

f\in X^{*}
satisfying
|f(v)|\leq p(v)
for all
v\in X.
By taking
w=0
, we have
\|f\|\leq 1
. Also, for every
z\in Y
,
|f(z)|\leq p(z)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|z\|,
and therefore

\|f|_{Y}\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}.

Now, consider

\widetilde{N}_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}(Y)=\left\{g\in X^{*}:\|g\|\leq 1,\ \|g|_{Y}\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\right\}.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, the weak
∗
-compactness
of
\widetilde{N}_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}(Y)
yields a functional

f_{0}\in\widetilde{N}_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}(Y)
maximizing
|g(x)|
.
Since
|f(x)|=1-\delta
, we have

|f_{0}(x)|\geq 1-\delta.

Using the fact that
x\notin Y
, and mimicking the same argument as in
Theorem
1.3
shows that
\|f_{0}\|=1
. Consequently,

|f_{0}(x)|\geq 1-\delta=(1-\delta)\|f_{0}\|\,\|x\|,

and

\|f_{0}|_{Y}\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}.

Thus
f_{0}
satisfies
(ii)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Theorem
1.5
reduces to Theorem
1.3
in the case
Y=\mathrm{span}\{y\}
, since
\|f|_{Y}\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}
is then equivalent to

|f(y)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|y\|
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

(i)
It follows from the definition that there exists
f\in S_{X^{*}}
such that
|f(x)|\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|,\qquad|f(y)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|y\|.
Let

z=y-x\frac{f(y)}{f(x)}.

Then we get
f(z)=0
, and therefore
x\perp_{\delta}^{0}z
. Also, since
|f(x)|\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|
, we have

\|z-y\|=\left|\frac{f(y)}{f(x)}\right|\|x\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\delta)^{2}}\|y\|.

(ii) If there exists
z\in X
satisfying the stated conditions, then there exists
g\in S_{X^{*}}
such that
|g(x)|\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|
and
g(z)=0
. Thus, by the hypothesis

|g(y)|=|g(y-z)|\leq\|y-z\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\delta)}\|y\|,

which shows
x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}y
.

Evidently,
(i)
and
(ii)
are equivalent, whenever
\delta=0
, since in that case
\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}=\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\delta)^{2}}=\varepsilon
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Dual formulation of Norming Approximate Orthogonality

We start with the proof of Theorem
1.7
which gives a sufficient condition for norming approximate orthogonality of continuous linear functionals.

Proof of theorem
1.7
.

Recall that
f\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}g
in
X^{*}
means there exists
\Gamma\in X^{**}

such that

|\Gamma(f)|\geq(1-\delta)\|\Gamma\|\|f\|,\qquad|\Gamma(g)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|\Gamma\|\|g\|.

Let
\pi:X\to X^{**}
denote the canonical isometric embedding, defined by

\pi(x)(h)=h(x)
for all
h\in X^{*}
. Suppose
x\in X
satisfies the two
conditions as in the statement of the theorem. Taking
\Gamma=\pi(x)
, we have
\|\Gamma\|=\|x\|
, and

|\Gamma(f)|=|f(x)|\geq(1-\delta)\|f\|\|x\|=(1-\delta)\|\Gamma\|\|f\|,

|\Gamma(g)|=|g(x)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|g\|\|x\|=\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|\Gamma\|\|g\|.

Thus
\pi(x)
witnesses
f\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}g
in
X^{*}
, and the proof is complete.
∎

In the reflexive case, the converse also holds.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
X
be a reflexive Banach space,
f,g\in X^{*}
, and
\delta,\varepsilon\in[0,1)
with
\varepsilon<(1-\delta)^{2}
. Then
f\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}g
in

X^{*}
if and only if there exists
x\in X
such that

|f(x)|\geq(1-\delta)\|f\|\|x\|,\qquad|g(x)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|g\|\|x\|.

Proof.

The sufficiency follows from Theorem
1.7
. For necessity, suppose

f\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}g
in
X^{*}
, so there exists
\Gamma\in X^{**}

witnessing this. Since
X
is reflexive, the canonical embedding

\pi:X\to X^{**}
is surjective, so
\Gamma=\pi(x)
for some
x\in X
, and the proof follows.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Therefore, in the reflexive case,
f\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}g
in
X^{*}
if and only if there exists
x\in S_{X}
such that
f
approximately attains its norm at
x
, that is,

|f(x)|\geq(1-\delta)\|f\|
, and
g
approximately vanishes at
x
, i.e.,

|g(x)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}\|g\|
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
X
be a reflexive Banach space and
f,g\in X^{*}
. Then
f\perp_{B}g
in

X^{*}
if and only if

M_{f}\cap\ker g\neq\emptyset,

where
M_{f}=\{x\in S_{X}:|f(x)|=\|f\|\}
is the norm-attainment set of
f
.

Proof.

Set
\delta=\varepsilon=0
in the corollary above. Then
f\perp_{B}g
in

X^{*}
if and only if there exists
x\in S_{X}
such that

|f(x)|=\|f\|,\qquad g(x)=0,

that is,
x\in M_{f}\cap\ker g
.
∎

We conclude this Section with the proof of Theorem
1.8
which recovers an the characterization of best approximation to a point out of a closed subspace.

Proof of theorem
1.8
.

Define
\mathscr{N}_{\delta}(x):=\{f\in B_{X^{*}}:|f(x)|\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|\}
.
This set is a weak
∗
-closed subset of
B_{X^{*}}
, hence weak
∗
-compact by the
Banach–Alaoglu theorem. Since the map
f\mapsto\|f|_{Y}\|
is
weak
∗
-lower semicontinuous, it attains its infimum on
\mathscr{N}_{\delta}(x)
,
so
m_{\delta}(x,Y)
is well-defined and the minimum is attained.

Let
f_{0}\in\mathscr{N}_{\delta}(x)
be the minimiser, so

\|f_{0}|_{Y}\|=m_{\delta}(x,Y)
. Since
|f_{0}(x)|\geq(1-\delta)\|f_{0}\|\|x\|

and
\|f_{0}\|\leq 1
, we have
\|f_{0}\|\geq 1-\delta>0
. Define

g_{0}:=f_{0}/\|f_{0}\|\in S_{X^{*}}
. Then

|g_{0}(x)|=\frac{|f_{0}(x)|}{\|f_{0}\|}\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|,

and for every
y\in Y
,

|g_{0}(y)|\leq\|g_{0}|_{Y}\|=\frac{\|f_{0}|_{Y}\|}{\|f_{0}\|}\leq\frac{m_{\delta}(x,Y)}{1-\delta}\|g_{0}\|\|y\|.

Since
m_{\delta}(x,Y)<(1-\delta)^{2}
by hypothesis,
g_{0}
witnesses

x\perp_{\delta}^{m_{\delta}(x,Y)}Y
, so the set

S:=\bigl\{\varepsilon\in[0,(1-\delta)^{2}):x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}Y\bigr\}

is non-empty and
\varepsilon_{\min}=\inf S
is well-defined. To see that the
infimum is attained, take any sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\searrow\varepsilon_{\min}

with each
\varepsilon_{n}\in S
. By the equivalence

(i)\Leftrightarrow(iv)
of Theorem
1.3
, for every scalar
\lambda

and every
y\in Y
,

\|x+\lambda y\|\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|-\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{1-\delta}|\lambda|\|y\|\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}(1-\delta)\|x\|-\frac{\varepsilon_{\min}}{1-\delta}|\lambda|\|y\|,

so condition
(iv)
holds at
\varepsilon_{\min}
for every
y\in Y
, and by

(iv)\Rightarrow(i)
of Theorem
1.3
we conclude

x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon_{\min}}Y
. Thus
\varepsilon_{\min}\in S
,
the minimum is attained, and since
m_{\delta}(x,Y)\in S
,

\varepsilon_{\min}\leq m_{\delta}(x,Y).

For the lower bound, since
\varepsilon_{\min}
is attained there exists

h\in S_{X^{*}}
such that
|h(x)|\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|
and

\|h|_{Y}\|\leq\varepsilon_{\min}/(1-\delta)
. Since

h\in\mathscr{N}_{\delta}(x)
, the minimality of
m_{\delta}(x,Y)
gives

m_{\delta}(x,Y)\leq\|h|_{Y}\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{\min}}{1-\delta},

and therefore
(1-\delta)\,m_{\delta}(x,Y)\leq\varepsilon_{\min}
.
Combining both estimates yields

(1-\delta)\,m_{\delta}(x,Y)\leq\varepsilon_{\min}\leq m_{\delta}(x,Y),

and the proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space,
Y
a closed proper non-trivial subspace
of
X
, and
x\in X\setminus Y
. Then
y_{0}\in Y
is a best approximation
to
x
from
Y
if and only if

m_{0}(x-y_{0},Y):=\min\left\{\|f|_{Y}\|:f\in S_{X^{*}},\ f(x-y_{0})=\|x-y_{0}\|\right\}=0.

Proof.

By the classical characterization of best approximations,
y_{0}\in Y
is
a best approximation to
x
from
Y
if and only if

x-y_{0}\perp_{B}y\quad\text{for every }y\in Y.

Setting
\delta=\varepsilon=0
in Theorem
1.5
applied
to
x-y_{0}\in X\setminus Y
, this holds if and only if there exists

f\in S_{X^{*}}
such that
f(x-y_{0})=\|x-y_{0}\|
and
f|_{Y}\equiv 0
,
which is precisely the condition
m_{0}(x-y_{0},Y)=0
.
∎

4.
Application to continuous function spaces

In this Section we provide some applications of the theory of norming approximate orthogonality developed so far, in case of continuous function spaces. Given any continuous linear functional
f
on a normed linear space
X
, we consider the
\delta
-norming set and absolute
\delta
-norming set of
f
as

M_{\delta}(f)=\{x\in S_{X}:~|f(x)|\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|\},~A_{\delta}(f)=\{x\in S_{X}:~f(x)\geq(1-\delta)\|x\|\}

for some
\delta\geq 0
. We note that
M_{0}(f)
is the exact norm attainment set of
f
, which can be empty in the non-reflexive case, however,
M_{\delta}(f)\neq\emptyset
in any normed linear space provided
\delta>0
. We have the following characterization of approximate norming orthogonality in terms of approximate norm attainment set which is a reformulation of Definition
1.1
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a normed linear space and let
\delta,\varepsilon\in[0,1)
with
\varepsilon<(1-\delta)^{2}
. Then
x\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}y
if and only if

\displaystyle\left\{f(y):~f\in X^{*},~\frac{x}{\|x\|}\in A_{\delta}(f)\right\}\cap B\left(0,\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\delta)}\|y\|\right)\neq\emptyset.

(1)

The above reformulation, although elementary, has deeper implications in case of
\delta=0
through the Krein-Milman Theorem. However, first we formulate norming approximate orthogonality in the continuous function space
C(K,X)
endowed with the supremum norm, where
K
is a compact Hausdorff space and
X
is a normed linear space. For any
\phi\in C(K,X)
, we define the norm attainment of
\phi
as

M_{\phi}=\{t\in K:~|\phi(t)|=\|\phi\|_{\infty}\}

which is always non-empty, since
K
is compact. The dual
(C(K,X))^{*}
of
C(K,X)
can be identified isometrically with
rcabv(K,X^{*})
, the space of regular countably additive
X^{*}
-valued Borel measures
of bounded variation on
K
with the dual pairing:

m(\phi)=\int_{K}\phi\,dm,\quad\phi\in C(K,X),\;m\in rcabv(K,X^{*}),

with
\|m\|=|m|(K)
. Thus, Proposition
4.1
takes the following from in
C(K,X)
:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\phi,\psi\in C(K,X)
and
\delta,\varepsilon\in[0,1)
with
\varepsilon<(1-\delta)^{2}
. Then
\phi\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}\psi
if and only if

\left\{\int_{K}\psi\,dm:~\int_{K}\phi\,dm\geq(1-\delta)\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|m\|\right\}\cap B\left(0,\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-\delta)}\|\psi\|\right)\neq\emptyset.

For
t\in K
, we consider the Dirac measure
\delta_{t}
on
\mathcal{B}(K)
, the
\sigma
-algebra of all Borel subsets of
K
, by

\delta_{t}(A)=\begin{cases}1,&t\in A\\
0,&t\notin A.\end{cases}

For any
t\in K
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, we consider the measure
\delta_{t}\otimes x^{*}
as a linear functional on
C(K,X)
, defined by

x^{*}\otimes\delta_{t}:C(K,X)\to\mathbb{C},\quad(x^{*}\otimes\delta_{t})(\phi)=x^{*}(\phi(t)).

Then for every
\phi\in C(K,X)
,

\int_{K}\phi\,d(x^{*}\otimes\delta_{t})=x^{*}(\phi(t)).

Let
\phi\in C(K,X)
and
\delta\in[0,1)
. Consider the collection of probability measures

F_{\delta}=\{m\in S_{(C(K,X))^{*}}:~m(\phi)=\int_{K}\phi\,dm\geq(1-\delta)\}.

When
\delta=0
, the collection
F_{0}
is a face of
B_{(C(K,X))^{*}}
and is weak
∗
-closed. Therefore, by the Krein–Milman theorem,
F_{0}
is the weak
∗
-closed convex hull of its extreme points. It follows from
[
3
,
19
]
that

\mathrm{ext}(B_{(C(K,X))^{*}})=\{x^{*}\otimes\delta_{t}:~x^{*}\in\mathrm{ext}(B_{X^{*}}),~t\in K\}.

Since
\mathrm{ext}(F_{0})\subset\mathrm{ext}(B_{(C(K,X))^{*}})
, and
x^{*}\otimes\delta_{t}\in F_{0}
if and only if
x^{*}(\phi(t))=\|\phi\|_{\infty}
, which together with
\|x^{*}\|=1
forces
t\in M_{\phi}
, we have

\displaystyle F_{0}

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathrm{conv}}^{w^{*}}\{x^{*}\otimes\delta_{t}:~x^{*}\in\mathrm{ext}(B_{X^{*}}),~t\in K,~x^{*}(\phi(t))=\|\phi\|_{\infty}\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathrm{conv}}^{w^{*}}\{x^{*}\otimes\delta_{t}:~x^{*}\in\mathrm{ext}(B_{X^{*}}),~t\in M_{\phi},~x^{*}(\phi(t))=\|\phi\|_{\infty}\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathrm{conv}}^{w^{*}}\{x^{*}\otimes\delta_{t}:~x^{*}\in S_{X^{*}},~t\in M_{\phi},~x^{*}(\phi(t))=\|\phi\|_{\infty}\}.

The first two equalities follow from the above observation. For the third equality, the inclusion
\subset
is clear. For
\supset
, fix
t\in M_{\phi}
and
x^{*}\in S_{X^{*}}
with
x^{*}(\phi(t))=\|\phi\|_{\infty}
. Then
x^{*}
belongs to the weak
∗
-closed face
\{y^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}:~y^{*}(\phi(t))=\|\phi\|_{\infty}\}
of
B_{X^{*}}
. Hence, by the Krein–Milman theorem,

x^{*}\in\overline{\mathrm{conv}}^{w^{*}}\{y^{*}\in\mathrm{ext}(B_{X^{*}}):~y^{*}(\phi(t))=\|\phi\|_{\infty}\}.

Applying the weak
∗
-continuous linear map
y^{*}\mapsto y^{*}\otimes\delta_{t}
, we obtain

x^{*}\otimes\delta_{t}\in\overline{\mathrm{conv}}^{w^{*}}\{y^{*}\otimes\delta_{t}:~y^{*}\in\mathrm{ext}(B_{X^{*}}),~y^{*}(\phi(t))=\|\phi\|_{\infty}\}.

Taking weak
∗
-closed convex hulls over all such
t
and
x^{*}
gives the reverse inclusion. Therefore, with
\delta=0
, we get the following result, as a corollary to Proposition
4.2
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\phi,\psi\in C(K,X)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then
\phi\perp^{\varepsilon}_{0}\psi
if and only if

\mathrm{conv}\{x^{*}(\psi(t)):\ t\in M_{\phi},\ x^{*}\in S_{X^{*}},\ x^{*}(\phi(t))=\|\phi\|_{\infty}\}\cap B(0,\varepsilon\|\psi\|)\neq\emptyset.

Proof.

In the case
\delta=0
, we have

F_{0}=\overline{\mathrm{conv}}^{w^{*}}\{x^{*}\otimes\delta_{t}:\ t\in M_{\phi},\ x^{*}\in S_{X^{*}},\ x^{*}(\phi(t))=\|\phi\|_{\infty}\}.

Applying the weak
∗
-continuous affine map
m\mapsto\int_{K}\psi\,dm
, it follows that

\left\{\int_{K}\psi\,dm:\ m\in F_{0}\right\}=\overline{\mathrm{conv}}\{x^{*}(\psi(t)):\ t\in M_{\phi},\ x^{*}\in S_{X^{*}},\ x^{*}(\phi(t))=\|\phi\|_{\infty}\}.

To remove the closure, consider the set

E=\{(t,x^{*})\in K\times B_{X^{*}}:\ x^{*}(\phi(t))=\|\phi\|_{\infty}\}.

Since
K
is compact and
B_{X^{*}}
is weak
∗
-compact, the product
K\times B_{X^{*}}
is compact. Moreover, the map
(t,x^{*})\mapsto x^{*}(\phi(t))
is continuous (norm in
t
, weak
∗
in
x^{*}
), so
E
is closed and hence compact. The map
(t,x^{*})\mapsto x^{*}(\psi(t))
is also continuous, and therefore the set

\{x^{*}(\psi(t)):\ t\in M_{\phi},\ x^{*}\in S_{X^{*}},\ x^{*}(\phi(t))=\|\phi\|_{\infty}\}

is a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}
. Consequently, by Carathéodory’s Theorem on convex hull, its convex hull is also a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}
, and in particular closed, so the closure may be omitted. The conclusion now follows from the Proposition
4.1
.
∎

The above corollary gives a shorter proof of
[
18
, Theorem 3.2]
(for
\varepsilon=0
) and an independent proof of
[
18
, Theorem 4.4]
and extends them by removing the finite dimensionality of
X
. Moreover, particular case of this corollary extends
[
18
, Theorem 4.4]
for compact operators, as we illustrate below. Let
X
be a reflexive Banach space. Then
B_{X}
equipped with the weak topology is a compact Hausdorff space, which we denote
B_{X}^{w}
. Since every member of
K(X)
, the space of all compact operators are weak to strong continuous, we may identify
K(X)
inside
C(B_{X}^{w},X)
through the isometric embedding

K(X)\hookrightarrow C(B_{X}^{w},X),\qquad T\mapsto T|_{B_{X}},

since
\|T\|=\sup_{x\in B_{X}}\|Tx\|=\|T|_{B_{X}}\|_{\infty}
. For
T\in K(X)
, define

M_{T}=\{x\in B_{X}:\|Tx\|=\|T\|\}.

Since
x\mapsto\|Tx\|
is weakly continuous (by complete continuity) and
B_{X}^{w}

is compact, the supremum is attained, so
M_{T}\neq\emptyset
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
X
be a reflexive Banach space,
T,A\in K(X)
, and
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
.
Then
T\perp_{B}^{\varepsilon}A
if and only if

\operatorname{conv}\{y^{*}(Ax):x\in M_{T},\;y^{*}\in J(Tx)\}\cap B(0,\varepsilon\|A\|)\neq\emptyset.

Proof.

Apply Corollary
4.3
with
K=B_{X}^{w}
,
f=T|_{B_{X}}
, and
g=A|_{B_{X}}
.
Under the isometric embedding
K(X)\hookrightarrow C(B_{X}^{w},X)
, we have

\|f\|_{\infty}=\|T\|
and

M_{0}(f)=\{x\in B_{X}:\|Tx\|=\|T\|\}=M_{T},\qquad J(f(x))=J(Tx),

so the conclusion follows immediately from Corollary
4.3
.
∎

5.
Symmetry, Remarks and counter examples

In this section, we examine geometric aspects of the proposed orthogonality, including its lack of symmetry in general Banach spaces, its symmetric behaviour in Hilbert spaces, and the failure of certain converse statements. We would like to mark that
\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}
is strictly a two-parameter orthogonality, as illustrated in the following example.

Example 5.1
.

Consider the two-dimensional real Banach space
X=\ell_{\infty}^{2}
. Consider the unit vectors

x_{0}=(1,0.6),~y_{0}=(1,0.4).

Let
\varepsilon=0.2
and
\delta=0.5
. Then
\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}=0.4.
Consider the norm one continuous linear functional
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
by

f(u,v)=v,\qquad(u,v)\in\ell_{\infty}^{2}.

Then

f(x_{0})=0.6\geq(1-\delta)=0.5,~f(y_{0})=0.4\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\delta}=0.4.

Therefore,
x_{0}\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}y_{0}
. However, it is easy to see that
x_{0}
is a smooth point in
X
and therefore, has a unique support functional given by

g_{0}(u,v)=u,\qquad(u,v)\in\ell_{\infty}^{2}.

However, since
g_{0}
also supports
y_{0}
,
g_{0}(y_{0})=1
, which shows
x_{0}\not\perp^{\gamma}_{B}y_{0}
for any
\gamma\in[0,1)
, i.e.,
x_{0}
is not approximately orthogonal to
y_{0}
in the sense of Chmieliński.

Remark 5.2
.

It is well known that Birkhoff–James orthogonality is symmetric in a normed linear space
X
with
\dim X\geq 3
if and only if
X
is an inner product space. This naturally raises the question of whether symmetry of the two-parameter orthogonality relation
\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}
imposes similar geometric restrictions on the underlying space. However, symmetry of
\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}
(for fixed
\delta,\varepsilon>0
) does not a priori imply symmetry of Birkhoff–James orthogonality, since the latter corresponds to the limiting case
(\delta,\varepsilon)=(0,0)
, where the defining conditions become exact rather than approximate. Thus, the behaviour of
\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}
may differ substantially from that of
\perp_{B}
.

The following example shows that
\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}
is not symmetric in general.

Example 5.3
.

We work with the same space
X
,
\varepsilon
,
\delta
as in Example
5.1
. We know that
x_{0}\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}y_{0}
. We show that
y_{0}\not\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}x_{0}
. The dual of
X
is
\ell_{1}^{2}
and the dual action is point-wise multiplication. Every norm-one functional has
g
can be identified with a unique vector tuple
(a,b)
with
|a|+|b|=1
.
For
y_{0}\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}x_{0}
we would need
g(y_{0})\geq 0.5

and
g(x_{0})\leq 0.4
. We show that no such
g
exists.

Case I
:
a\geq 0,\;b\geq 0
, so
a+b=1
.
The condition
g(y_{0})=a+0.4b\geq 0.5
gives, on substituting

a=1-b

1-0.6b\geq 0.5\implies b\leq\tfrac{5}{6}.

Then

g(x_{0})=(1-b)+0.6b=1-0.4b\;\geq\;1-0.4\cdot\tfrac{5}{6}=\tfrac{2}{3}>0.4.

Case II:

a\geq 0,\;b\leq 0
, so
a-b=1
,
i.e.,
a=1+b
with
b\in[-1,0]
.
The condition
g(y_{0})=(1+b)+0.4b=1+1.4b\geq 0.5
gives

b\geq-\frac{5}{14}
, so
b\in[-\frac{5}{14},0]
.
Then
g(x_{0})=(1+b)+0.6b=1+1.6b
is increasing in
b
,
with minimum

g(x_{0})\;\geq\;1+1.6\cdot\bigl(-\tfrac{5}{14}\bigr)=1-\tfrac{4}{7}=\tfrac{3}{7}>0.4.

Case III:

a\leq 0,\;b\geq 0
, so
b-a=1
,
i.e.,
a=b-1
with
b\in[0,1]
.
The condition
g(y_{0})=(b-1)+0.4b=1.4b-1\geq 0.5
requires

b\geq\frac{15}{14}>1
, which is impossible and therefore, such a case does not arise.

Case IV:

a\leq 0,\;b\leq 0
.
Then
g(y_{0})=a+0.4b\leq 0<0.5
, and such a case is also not feasible.

Therefore, we conclude that

|g(x_{0})|\geq\frac{3}{7}>0.4,\quad\forall g\in S_{X^{*}}:~g(y_{0})\geq 0.5

Consequently,

y_{0}\not\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}x_{0}.

Thus, norming approximate orthogonality is not symmetric in general.

Remark 5.4
.

In contrast to the general normed space setting, the situation is markedly different in Hilbert spaces. In this case, by Theorem
1.3
, the relation
\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}
admits a characterization in terms of the inner product:

|\langle x,y\rangle|\leq L(\delta,\varepsilon)\|x\|\|y\|,

where
\varepsilon<(1-\delta)^{2}
. This condition is clearly symmetric in
x
and
y
, and therefore
\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}
is symmetric in Hilbert spaces.

On the other hand, as seen in Example
5.3
,
\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}
fails to be symmetric in general normed spaces. This contrast naturally leads to the following question.

Open question
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with three or more dimensions. For fixed
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1)
satisfying
\varepsilon<(1-\delta)^{2}
, suppose that the norming approximate orthogonality relation
\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}
is symmetric. Does it follow that
X
is a Hilbert space?

We terminate the paper by considering the converse of (i) and (ii) of Theorem
1.6
. As noted earlier,
\perp_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}
-orthogonality gives us two proximity results in form of Theorem
1.6
which are equivalent when
\delta=0
, reduces to the proximity result due to Chmieliński’s
[
10
, Theorem 2.2]
. However, in general, converse of the both results can fail. We illustrate this fact with a couple of examples.
