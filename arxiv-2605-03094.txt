Combinatorial aspects of normal ordering of 3-dimensional skew polynomial rings Thanks: The second author was supported by Faculty of Science, Universidad Nacional de Colombia–Sede Bogotá, Colombia [grant number 65488].
Andrés Rubiano, Armando Reyes

Abstract
. In this paper, we discuss combinatorial aspects of normal ordering of 3-dimensional skew polynomial rings defined and classified by Bell and Smith [ 3 ] . With some help of the Mathematical software SageMath , we are able to reduce the length of computation of PBW forms and normal orderings appearing in commutation rules of these algebras.

1.
Introduction

Just as Schork
[
37
, Section 1]
asserted, “Maybe the first explicit results concerning normal ordering were derived by Scherk
[
35
]
in his dissertation from 1823.” In his thesis (and using the current terminology in the literature), he considered the action of two operators
X
and
D
on functions of a real variable defined by

(1.1)

(Xf)(x)=xf(x)\quad{\rm and}\quad(Df)(x)=\frac{df}{dx}(x),

respectively. As it can be seen, these two operators do not commute, and if
I
denotes the identity operator, they satisfy the
commutation relation
given by

(1.2)

DX-XD=I.

As is well-known, this algebra is called the
first Weyl algebra
and is defined over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
as the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
A_{1}(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}\{X,D\}/\langle XD-DX-I\rangle
. History and a detailed study of this noncommutative ring was made by Coutinho
[
10
]
.

If we consider the definition of an
arbitrary word
in the indeterminates
X
and
D
, then we mean an object
\omega
that can be written as
\omega=X^{r_{1}}D^{s_{1}}X^{r_{2}}D^{s_{2}}\dotsb X^{r_{n}}D^{s_{n}}
for some elements
r_{k},s_{k}
belonging to the set of natural numbers
\mathbb{N}
(we consider the zero element as a natural number). Having in mind the relation (
1.2
), it is necessary to respect the order of the operators when acting on a function
f
; this motivates the following definition: a word
\omega
in the letters
X
and
D
is said to be in
normal ordered form
if
\omega=a_{r,s}X^{r}D^{s}
for
r,s\in\mathbb{N}
(and arbitrary coefficients
a_{r,s}
in some kind of ring or field - not necessarily the field of complex numbers). With this, an expression that consists of a sum of words is called
normal ordered
if each of the summand is normal ordered. The process of expressing a word (or a sum of words) into a normal ordered form by using the commutation relation is called
normal ordering
, which is the key notion of the paper. In this procedure, we get expressions such as

(1.3)

\omega=\sum_{r,s\in\mathbb{N}}S_{r,s}(\omega)X^{r}D^{s},

with the uniquely determined coefficients
S_{r,s}(\omega)
known as
normal ordering coefficients
.

As a matter of fact, Scherk
[
35
]
also determined the normal ordered form of the words
(XD)^{n}
and
(X^{p}D)^{n}
and investigated combinatorial properties of the normal ordering coefficients. For instance, he proved that

(1.4)

(XD)^{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{1,1}(n,k)X^{k}D^{k},

where
S_{1,1}(n+1,k)=S_{1,1}(n,k-1)+kS_{1,1}(n,k)
. Today, these normal ordering coefficients are known today as the
Stirling numbers of the second kind

S(n,k)

[
38
, A008277]
(of course, Scherk did not recognize them). In the extensive literature about these numbers, we find the relations between them and the notion of
set partition
. In fact, Scherk also considered the analogous normal ordering coefficients
S_{p,1}(n,k)
- which are particular examples of the
generalized Stirling numbers
- These objects written as
S_{p,q}(n,k)
are defined by considering normal orderings in the expression
(X^{p}D^{q})^{n}
.

A major theme in subsequent work is that
changing the commutation rule
(or moving to deformations and variants of the Weyl algebra) produces new families of Stirling-type numbers with rich combinatorics. A striking example is the interpretation of normal order coefficients in terms of rook numbers on Ferrers boards, leading to explicit formulas and factorization phenomena as shown by Varvak
[
39
]
. Deformations also connect normal ordering to
q
-analogues and
q
-Stirling families
[
36
]
, while more refined operator strings yield generalized Bell-Stirling structures with graph-theoretic models and additional combinatorial interpretations
[
19
,
9
]
. These contributions establish normal ordering as a robust
combinatorial machine
that turns commutation relations into computable, structured coefficient systems.

In the literature, we can find a large number of works that have addressed the study of normal orders for various families of non-commutative algebras such as the
quantum plane
, the
q
-
deformed Weyl algebra
, the algebras in physical models of quantum theory defined by the
creation operator
and the
annihilation operator
, and other generalizations of the Weyl algebra (e.g.
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
12
,
17
,
18
,
20
,
37
]
, and references therein). Recently, Briand et al.
[
8
]
studied normally ordered forms for powers of differential operators, deriving generalized Stirling numbers and arithmetic properties of the coefficients, while Mansour and Schork
[
16
]
developed
Ore-Stirling numbers
as normal ordering coefficients in a more general setting than Patrias and Pylyavskyy
[
23
]
. As it can be seen in all these works, normal ordering becomes an efficient bridge between algebraic identities and concrete combinatorial classes (set partitions, contractions, graphs), yielding recurrences, generating functions and closed forms for the associated coefficient arrays. It is important to note that all structures considered above have only one nontrivial commutation relation.

The algebras of interest in this paper are the 3-dimensional skew polynomial rings introduced by Bell and Smith
[
3
]
as a class of noncommutative algebras on three generators admitting a PBW basis of standard monomials which are defined by three nontrivial commutation relations. Beyond their intrinsic ring-theoretic interest,
3
-dimensional skew polynomial algebras naturally capture familiar structures from Lie theory and quantum groups such as the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
, the dispin algebra
U(\mathfrak{osp}(1,2))
and Woronowicz-type algebras associated to noncommutative differential calculi appear within this framework
[
29
,
40
]
.

Our purpose in this paper is to develop the algebraic combinatorics underlying PBW normal forms in the class of
3
-dimensional skew polynomial algebras. Fixing the PBW order
x\prec y\prec z
, we regard PBW reduction as a rewriting process that eliminates the forbidden adjacent pairs
yx
,
zy
and
zx
via the affine relations (as expected, our ideas are based on Bergman’s paper
[
4
]
, see also
[
29
]
). This viewpoint turns the computation of products and powers into a systematic
normal ordering problem
: given a noncommutative expression, determine its unique expansion in the PBW basis consisting of the monomials
x^{i}y^{j}z^{l}
with
i,j,l\in\mathbb{N}
. Our approach is uniform and computable: we work in a parameterized
3
-skew family and then specialize the parameters to cover the
15
isomorphism types in the Bell and Smith’s classification. In our treatment, we keep in mind Schork’s words about algebras with more than two generators: “When considering algebras with three or more generators where none of the generators is central (hence, one has at least three nontrivial commutation relations) one can clearly define normal ordering in an analogous fashion but the considerations quickly become tedious”
[
37
, p. 4]
. These words motivated us to look for computational alternatives to reduce calculations, and it is the reason why we developed a code for it in the mathematical software
SageMath
.

The article is organized as follows. Section
2
presents the definition and classification of 3-dimensional skew polynomial rings. Next, Section
3
contains the original results of the paper. With some help of the Mathematical software
SageMath
, we are able to compute PBW forms and hence normal orderings appearing in commutation rules. More exactly, for each of the fifteen 3-dimensional skew polynomial rings (Proposition
2.2
), we establish the fundamental two letter normal ordering identities (such as
y^{n}x^{m}
,
z^{n}x^{m}
, and
z^{n}y^{m}
) that serve as basic rewriting rules. These identities are then used as building blocks to derive the higher-order expansions required in our combinatorial results, including powers of blocks and mixed words (e.g.
(x^{n}y^{m})^{s}
,
(xyz)^{s}
,
(x^{n}y^{m}z^{t})^{s}
) and the corresponding coefficient recursions. We want to remark that the software
SageMath
is used in an elementary way only to reduce the
length of the computation and all computation can be done by hand without assistance of it. Precisely, Section
4
contains the code we implemented in
SageMath
that allowed us to compute the normal ordered forms of every algebra. Finally, Section
5
presents some ideas for possible future work related to normal ordering issues.

Throughout the paper,
\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of natural numbers including the zero element. The term ring means an associative ring with identity, not necessarily commutative. All vector spaces and algebras (always associative and unital) are over a fixed field
\Bbbk
, which is assumed to have characteristic zero. As usual,
\Bbbk^{\times}:=\Bbbk\ \backslash\ \{0\}
.

2.
Definitions and Preliminaries

Definition 2.1

(
[
3
]
,
[
30
, Definition C4.3]
)
.

A 3-dimensional skew polynomial algebra

A
is a
\Bbbk
-algebra generated by the indeterminates
x,y,z
restricted to relations

yz-\alpha zy=\lambda,\quad zx-\beta xz=\mu\quad{\rm and}\quad xy-\gamma yx=\nu,

such that

(i)

\lambda,\mu,\nu\in\Bbbk+\Bbbk x+\Bbbk y+\Bbbk z
and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\Bbbk\ \backslash\ \{0\}
and

(ii)

standard monomials
\left\{x^{i}y^{j}z^{l}\mid i,j,l\geq 0\right\}
are a
\Bbbk
-basis of the algebra
A
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
30
, Theorem C.4.3.1]
)
.

If
A
is a 3-dimensional skew polynomial algebra, then
A

is one of the following algebras:

(1)

If
|\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}|=3
, then
A
is given by the relations

yz-\alpha zy=0,\quad zx-\beta xz=0\quad{\rm and}\quad xy-\gamma yx=0.

(2)

If
|\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}|=2
and
\beta\neq\alpha=\gamma=1
, then
A
is one of the following algebras:

(i)

yz-zy=z,\ zx-\beta xz=y
and
xy-yx=x

(
if
\beta=-1
, then we get the Dispin algebra
)
.

(ii)

yz-zy=z,\ zx-\beta xz=b
and
xy-yx=x
;

(iii)

yz-zy=0,\ zx-\beta xz=y
and
xy-yx=0
;

(iv)

yz-zy=0,\ zx-\beta xz=b
and
xy-yx=0
;

(v)

yz-zy=az,\ zx-\beta xz=0
and
xy-yx=x
;

(vi)

yz-zy=z,\ zx-\beta xz=0
and
xy-yx=0
,

where
a,b
are any elements of
\Bbbk
. All non-zero values of
b
give isomorphic algebras.

(3)

If
|\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}|=2
and
\beta\neq\alpha=\gamma\neq 1
, then
A
is one of the following algebras:

(i)

yz-\alpha zy=0,\ zx-\beta xz=y+b
and
xy-\alpha yx=0
;

(ii)

yz-\alpha zy=0,\ zx-\beta xz=b
and
xy-\alpha yx=0
.

In this case,
b
is an arbitrary element of
\Bbbk
. Again, any
non-zero values of
b
give isomorphic algebras.

(4)

If
\alpha=\beta=\gamma\neq 1
, then
A
is the algebra defined by the relations

\displaystyle yz-\alpha zy=

\displaystyle\ a_{1}x+b_{1},

\displaystyle zx-\alpha xz=

\displaystyle\ a_{2}y+b_{2},\ {\rm and}

\displaystyle xy-\alpha yx=

\displaystyle\ a_{3}z+b_{3}.

If
a_{i}=0
for all
i
, then all non-zero values of
b_{i}
give isomorphic
algebras.

(5)

If
\alpha=\beta=\gamma=1
, then
A
is isomorphic to one of the following algebras:

(i)

yz-zy=x,\ zx-xz=y
and
xy-yx=z
;

(ii)

yz-zy=0,\ zx-xz=0
and
xy-yx=z
;

(iii)

yz-zy=0,\ zx-xz=0
and
xy-yx=b
;

(iv)

yz-zy=-y,\ zx-xz=x+y
and
xy-yx=0
;

(v)

yz-zy=az,\ zx-xz=z
and
xy-yx=0
;

Parameters
a,b\in\Bbbk
are arbitrary, and all non-zero values of

b
generate isomorphic algebras.

Remark 2.3
.

In
[
30
, Theorem C4.3.1, case (e)(v), p. 101]
, the relation is stated as

yz-zy=az,\quad zx-xz=x\quad{\rm and}\quad xy-yx=0.

However, this is a typo. Indeed, Rosenberg later realized the same algebra as the Ore extension

A=\Bbbk[x,y][z;\varphi],

where
\varphi
is an automorphism of
\Bbbk[x,y]
given by
\varphi(x)=x+1
and
\varphi(y)=y-a

[
30
, p. 108]
. By the defining rule
za=\varphi(a)z
for all
a\in\Bbbk[x,y]
, we obtain that

\displaystyle zx=\varphi(x)z=(x+1)z

\displaystyle\quad{\rm whence}\quad zx-xz=z,

\displaystyle zy=\varphi(y)z=(y-a)z

\displaystyle\quad{\rm and\ so}\quad yz-zy=az.

Therefore, the correct presentation of case (e)(v) is

yz-zy=az,\quad zx-xz=z\quad{\rm and}\quad xy-yx=0.

3.
Normal ordering identities

This section contains the original results of the paper. We present explicit PBW computations for each one of the
3
-dimensional skew polynomial algebras characterized in Proposition
2.2
.

We start by recalling some key preliminary facts on
quantum calculus
. More details can be found in
[
1
,
14
,
17
]
.

•

Throughout this section, the PBW monomial order is fixed as
x\prec y\prec z
. Thus, a normal ordered monomial means a monomial of the form
x^{i}y^{j}z^{k}
, with
i,j,k\in\mathbb{N}
. Equivalently, the normal ordering procedure consists of rewriting the forbidden adjacent pairs
yx,\ zy,\ zx
until a finite
\Bbbk
-linear combination of PBW monomials is obtained.

•

We use the convention that empty sums are equal to
0
and empty products are equal to
1
. Moreover,
\delta_{i,j}
denotes the
Kronecker delta
. Unless otherwise stated, any coefficient family indexed outside its prescribed range is understood to be zero. In particular, for coefficient arrays indexed by powers of
\mathbb{N}
, any term with a negative index is taken to be zero.

•

For
s,t\in\mathbb{N}
, we use the standard binomial coefficient

\dbinom{s}{t}:=\frac{s!}{t!(s-t)!}\quad\text{ for }0\leq t\leq s,

with the convention that
\binom{s}{t}=0
if
t>s
. In particular,

\binom{s}{2}=\frac{s(s-1)}{2}.

Moreover, since
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\Bbbk^{\times}
, the expression
\frac{\beta}{\alpha\gamma}
denotes the scalar
\beta(\alpha\gamma)^{-1}
belonging to
\Bbbk^{\times}
. Hence,

\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha\gamma}\right)^{\binom{s}{2}}=\beta^{\binom{s}{2}}\alpha^{-\binom{s}{2}}\gamma^{-\binom{s}{2}}.

•

For
q\in\Bbbk^{\times}
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

[r]_{q}:=1+q+\cdots+q^{r-1}\quad{\rm with}\quad[0]_{q}:=0.

If
q\neq 1
, then

[r]_{q}=\frac{q^{r}-1}{q-1}.

The corresponding
q
-factorial is defined by

[r]_{q}!:=\prod_{j=1}^{r}[j]_{q}\quad{\rm and}\quad[0]_{q}!:=1.

•

For
0\leq k\leq r
, the
q
-
falling factorial
and the
Gaussian binomial coefficient
are denoted by

[r]^{k}_{q}:=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}[r-j]_{q}\quad{\rm with}\quad[r]^{0}_{q}:=1,

and

\begin{bmatrix}r\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{q}:=\frac{[r]_{q}!}{[r-k]_{q}![k]_{q}!}=\frac{[r]^{k}_{q}}{[k]_{q}!},

respectively. We use the convention

\begin{bmatrix}r\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{q}:=0\ \text{if }k>r.

•

For
\rho,\sigma\in\Bbbk
and
r\geq 1
, we define the mixed

(\rho,\sigma)
-number by

\langle r\rangle_{\rho,\sigma}:=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\rho^{r-1-j}\sigma^{j}.

In particular, the mixed number used in algebras of type
(3)
in Proposition
2.2
is given by

\langle r\rangle_{\beta,\alpha^{-1}}=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\beta^{r-1-j}\alpha^{-j}.

We also set

\langle r\rangle_{\rho,\sigma}!:=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\langle j\rangle_{\rho,\sigma},\ \langle 0\rangle_{\rho,\sigma}!:=1,

and

\begin{bmatrix}r\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{\rho,\sigma}:=\frac{\langle r\rangle_{\rho,\sigma}!}{\langle r-k\rangle_{\rho,\sigma}!\ \langle k\rangle_{\rho,\sigma}!}.

•

The symbols

W,\ U,\ V,\ R,\ C,\ E,\ S,\ T,\ \Theta,\ Q_{r},\ P_{r}

denote coefficient families arising from PBW normal ordering. They are not universal constants: in each proposition they are defined by the corresponding displayed PBW expansion or recurrence relation. For instance, coefficients of the form
W^{(m)}_{n,k}(y)
encode the normal ordering of
z^{n}x^{m}
, while coefficients of the form
U^{(n,m)}_{s,\ell}
,
V_{s,\ell}
,
R^{(n,m,t)}_{s,\ell}
,
C^{(n,m,t)}_{s,\ell}
, and
E_{n;i,k}
encode, respectively, the PBW expansions of powers of blocks and noncommutative binomial expressions appearing below.

Let us start.

3.1.
Algebras of type (1)

Proposition 3.1
.

For the algebra given by the relations

yz-\alpha zy=0,\ zx-\beta xz=0\ \,\,{\rm and}\,\,xy-\gamma yx=0\quad{\rm with}\,\,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\Bbbk^{\times},

the following identities hold.

(1)

For all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

y^{n}x^{m}=\gamma^{-mn}x^{m}y^{n},\quad z^{n}x^{m}=\beta^{mn}x^{m}z^{n}\quad{\rm and}\quad z^{n}y^{m}=\alpha^{-mn}y^{m}z^{n}.

(2)

For all
m,n,s\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\left(x^{n}y^{m}\right)^{s}=

\displaystyle\,\gamma^{-mn\binom{s}{2}}x^{ns}y^{ms},

\displaystyle(x^{n}z^{m})^{s}=

\displaystyle\,\beta^{mn\binom{s}{2}}x^{ns}z^{ms},

\displaystyle(y^{n}z^{m})^{s}=

\displaystyle\,\alpha^{-mn\binom{s}{2}}y^{ns}z^{ms}.

(3)

For all
s\in\mathbb{N}
,

(xyz)^{s}=\beta^{\binom{s}{2}}\alpha^{-\binom{s}{2}}\gamma^{-\binom{s}{2}}x^{s}y^{s}z^{s}=\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha\gamma}\right)^{\binom{s}{2}}x^{s}y^{s}z^{s}.

(4)

For all
m,n,t,s\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left(x^{n}y^{m}z^{t}\right)^{s}=\beta^{nt\binom{s}{2}}\gamma^{-mn\binom{s}{2}}\alpha^{-mt\binom{s}{2}}x^{ns}y^{ms}z^{ts}.

(5)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle(x+y)^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{\gamma^{-1}}x^{n-k}y^{k},

\displaystyle(x+z)^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{\beta}x^{n-k}z^{k}\quad{\rm and}

\displaystyle(y+z)^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{\alpha^{-1}}y^{n-k}z^{k}.

Proof.

(1)

By induction on
m
, from
yx=\gamma^{-1}xy
we get
yx^{m}=\gamma^{-m}x^{m}y
, and so
y^{n}x^{m}=\gamma^{-mn}x^{m}y^{n}
by induction on
n
. Similarly,
zx=\beta xz
implies that
zx^{m}=\beta^{m}x^{m}z
, whence
z^{n}x^{m}=\beta^{mn}x^{m}z^{n}
. The equality
yz=\alpha zy
is equivalent to
zy=\alpha^{-1}yz
, so that
z^{n}y^{m}=\alpha^{-mn}y^{m}z^{n}
by double induction.

(2)

If
vu=quv
for some
q\in\Bbbk\ \backslash\ \{0\}
, then

(uv)^{s}=q^{\binom{s}{2}}u^{s}v^{s},\quad{\rm for}\,\,s\in\mathbb{N},

which follows by induction using that

(uv)^{s+1}=(uv)^{s}(uv)=q^{\binom{s}{2}}u^{s}(v^{s}u)v\quad{\rm and}\quad v^{s}u=q^{s}uv^{s}.

Set
u:=x^{n}
and
v:=y^{m}
. From (1),

vu=y^{m}x^{n}=\gamma^{-mn}x^{n}y^{m}=\gamma^{-mn}uv,

whence
q=\gamma^{-mn}
, and the first identity follows. In a similar way, the other two follow by considering
q=\beta^{mn}
for
(x^{n},z^{m})
and
q=\alpha^{-mn}
for
(y^{n},z^{m})
, respectively.

(3)

Write
w:=xyz
. Using the general multiplication rule for PBW monomials given by

(x^{i}y^{j}z^{k})(x^{i^{\prime}}y^{j^{\prime}}z^{k^{\prime}})=\beta^{ki^{\prime}}\gamma^{-ji^{\prime}}\alpha^{-kj^{\prime}}x^{i+i^{\prime}}y^{j+j^{\prime}}z^{k+k^{\prime}},

we compute recursively

w^{r+1}=w^{r}w=\big(\beta^{r}\gamma^{-r}\alpha^{-r}\big)x^{r+1}y^{r+1}z^{r+1}.

Multiplying these scalars for
r=1,\dots,s-1
yields the claimed factor

\beta^{\binom{s}{2}}\alpha^{-\binom{s}{2}}\gamma^{-\binom{s}{2}}
.

(4)

Let
M:=x^{n}y^{m}z^{t}
. Using the PBW multiplication rule above with

(i,j,k)=(rn,rm,rt)
and
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime},k^{\prime})=(n,m,t)
gives

M^{r+1}=M^{r}M=\beta^{(rt)n}\gamma^{-(rm)n}\alpha^{-(rt)m}x^{(r+1)n}y^{(r+1)m}z^{(r+1)t}.

Multiplying over
r=1,\dots,s-1
yields the stated factor with exponent
\binom{s}{2}
.

(5)

If
vu=quv
, then

(u+v)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{q}u^{n-k}v^{k},

proved by induction using
(u+v)^{n+1}=(u+v)^{n}(u+v)
and the recurrence for Gaussian binomials.
Here,
yx=\gamma^{-1}xy
,
zx=\beta xz
and
zy=\alpha^{-1}yz
, so the three identities follow with

q=\gamma^{-1}
,
q=\beta
and
q=\alpha^{-1}
, respectively.

∎

3.2.
Algebras of type (2)

In what follows, we treat the six algebras of type (2) separately. We state the corresponding normal ordering identities and PBW expansions and provide complete proofs based on the defining relations and the fixed PBW order
x\prec y\prec z
. In particular, all formulas summarized in Tables
1
-
4
are derived explicitly and whenever coefficient families are defined recursively, we verify their initial conditions and recurrence relations in detail.

3.2.1.
Algebras of type (2)(i)

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
A
be the
\Bbbk
-algebra generated by the indeterminates
x,y,z
with defining relations given by

yz-zy=z,\quad zx-\beta xz=y\quad{\rm and}\quad xy-yx=x\quad{\rm with}\quad\beta\in\Bbbk^{\times}.

The following assertions hold:

(1)

For all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we get that

y^{n}x^{m}=x^{m}(y-m)^{n}\quad{\rm and}\quad z^{n}y^{m}=(y-n)^{m}z^{n}.

For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist uniquely polynomials
W^{(m)}_{n,k}(y)\in\Bbbk[y]
with
0\leq k\leq\min\{m,n\}
such that

(3.1)

z^{n}x^{m}=\sum_{k=0}^{\min\{m,n\}}x^{m-k}W^{(m)}_{n,k}(y)z^{n-k}.

These polynomials are given by
W^{(m)}_{0,0}(y):=1
and
W^{(m)}_{0,k}(y)=0
for
k\geq 1
, and the recurrence relation

(3.2)

W^{(m)}_{n+1,k}(y)=\beta^{m-k}W^{(m)}_{n,k}(y-1)+Q_{m-k+1}(y)W^{(m)}_{n,k-1}(y),

where
W^{(m)}_{n,-1}(y):=0
and

(3.3)

Q_{r}(y):=[r]_{\beta}y-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}[i]_{\beta}.

(2)

For all
m,n,s\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

(x^{n}y^{m})^{s}=x^{ns}\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(y-jn)^{m}\quad{\rm and}\quad(y^{n}z^{m})^{s}=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(y-jm)^{n}\right)z^{ms}.

Moreover,
(x^{n}z^{m})^{s}

(3.4)

(x^{n}z^{m})^{s}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{ns,ms\}}x^{ns-\ell}U^{(n,m)}_{s,\ell}(y)z^{ms-\ell}\quad{\rm where}\quad U^{(n,m)}_{s,\ell}(y)\in\Bbbk[y],

and

U^{(n,m)}_{1,0}(y)=1\quad{\rm and}\quad U^{(n,m)}_{1,\ell}(y)=0\text{ for }\ell\geq 1,

Besides, for every
s\geq 1
and every
\ell\geq 0
we obtain the explicit recursion

(3.5)

U^{(n,m)}_{s+1,\ell}(y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\min\{n,\ell\}}U^{(n,m)}_{s,\ell-k}\big(y-(n-k)\big)W^{(n)}_{ms-(\ell-k),k}(y),

where the polynomials
W^{(n)}_{r,k}(y)\in\Bbbk[y]
are the normal ordering coefficients in expression (
3.2
).

(3)

There exist uniquely determined polynomials
V_{s,\ell}(y)\in\Bbbk[y]

(0\leq\ell\leq s)

(3.6)

(xyz)^{s}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}x^{s-\ell}V_{s,\ell}(y)z^{s-\ell}\quad{\rm where}\quad V_{s,\ell}(y)\in\Bbbk[y],

with

V_{1,0}(y)=y\quad{\rm and}\quad V_{1,\ell}(y)=0\quad\text{for}\,\,\ell\geq 1,

Once again, for every
s\geq 1
and every
\ell\geq 0
, we get the explicit recursion

(3.7)

V_{s+1,\ell}(y)=(y-(s-\ell))\left(\beta^{s-\ell}V_{s,\ell}(y-1)+Q_{s-\ell+1}(y)V_{s,\ell-1}(y)\right),

where we set
V_{s,-1}(y):=0
.

(4)

For all
m,n,t,s\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq\ell\leq\min\{ns,ts\}
, there exist uniquely determined polynomials
R^{(n,m,t)}_{s,\ell}(y)\in\Bbbk[y]
, such that

(3.8)

(x^{n}y^{m}z^{t})^{s}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{ns,ts\}}x^{ns-\ell}R^{(n,m,t)}_{s,\ell}(y)z^{ts-\ell}\quad{\rm and}\quad R^{(n,m,t)}_{s,\ell}(y)\in\Bbbk[y],

with

R^{(n,m,t)}_{1,0}(y)=y^{m}\quad{\rm and}\quad R^{(n,m,t)}_{1,\ell}(y)=0\,\,\text{ for}\,\,\ell\geq 1,

For every
s\geq 1
and every
\ell\geq 0
, we obtain the recurrence relation given by

(3.9)

R^{(n,m,t)}_{s+1,\ell}(y)=\big(y-(ts-\ell)\big)^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{\min\{n,\ell\}}R^{(n,m,t)}_{s,\ell-k}\big(y-(n-k)\big)W^{(n)}_{ts-(\ell-k),k}(y),

where the polynomials
W^{(n)}_{r,k}(y)\in\Bbbk[y]
are the normal ordering coefficients in expression (
3.2
).

(5)

There exist uniquely determined polynomials
S_{n,k}(y),T_{n,k}(y)\in\Bbbk[y]
such that

(3.10)

(x+y)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}S_{n,k}(y),\quad(y+z)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{n,k}(y)z^{k},

and they are characterized by the Stirling/Whitney-type recursions

S_{0,0}(y)=1,\ S_{0,k}(y)=0\ (k\geq 1),\quad S_{n+1,k}(y)=S_{n,k-1}(y)+(y-k)S_{n,k}(y),

and

T_{0,0}(y)=1,\ T_{0,k}(y)=0\ (k\geq 1),\quad T_{n+1,k}(y)=yT_{n,k}(y)+T_{n,k-1}(y-1).

Furthermore,
(x+z)^{n}

(3.11)

(x+z)^{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\geq 0\\
i+k\leq n\end{subarray}}x^{i}E_{n;i,k}(y)z^{k}\quad{\rm where}\quad E_{n;i,k}(y)\in\Bbbk[y],

with the conventions
E_{n;i,k}(y)=0
if
i<0
or
k<0
or

E_{0;0,0}(y)=1,\quad E_{0;i,k}(y)=0\text{ for }(i,k)\neq(0,0),

and for every
n\geq 0
and all
i,k\geq 0

(3.12)

E_{n+1;i,k}(y)=E_{n;i,k-1}(y)+\beta^{k}E_{n;i-1,k}(y-1)+Q_{k+1}(y)E_{n;i,k+1}(y),

Q_{r}(y)

\beta
-dependent polynomial defined in relation (
3.3
).

Proof.

(1)

From
yx=xy-x
we get

, and so for any polynomial
f(y)\in\Bbbk[y]
,

(3.13)

f(y)x=xf(y-1).

Iterating (
3.13
) yields
f(y)x^{m}=x^{m}f(y-m)
and taking
f(y)=y^{n}
gives
y^{n}x^{m}=x^{m}(y-m)^{n}
.

Similarly,
yz-zy=z
is equivalent to
zy=(y-1)z
, whence for
f(y)\in\Bbbk[y]
,

(3.14)

zf(y)=f(y-1)z.

If we iterate (
3.14
), then we get
z^{n}y^{m}=(y-n)^{m}z^{n}
.

For
zx^{m}
, write
zx^{m}=(zx)x^{m-1}=(\beta xz+y)x^{m-1}=\beta x(zx^{m-1})+yx^{m-1}
and use
yx^{m-1}=x^{m-1}(y-(m-1))
from the first part. Induction on
m
gives

zx^{m}=\beta^{m}x^{m}z+x^{m-1}\left([m]_{\beta}y-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}[i]_{\beta}\right),

that is, the expression with
Q_{m}(y)
in (
3.3
).

To obtain (
3.1
), consider induction on
n
and write

z^{n+1}x^{m}=z\left(\sum_{k}x^{m-k}W^{(m)}_{n,k}(y)z^{n-k}\right).

Using that
zf(y)=f(y-1)z
and the already proved formula for
zx^{m-k}
, it follows the recursion (
3.2
) while the PBW uniqueness yields
W^{(m)}_{n,k}(y)
.

(2)

Since
f(y)x^{n}=x^{n}f(y-n)
with
f(y)=y^{m}
, then
y^{m}x^{n}=x^{n}(y-n)^{m}
, and so that

\displaystyle(x^{n}y^{m})^{s+1}=

\displaystyle\ (x^{n}y^{m})^{s}x^{n}y^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,x^{ns}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(y-jn)^{m}\right)x^{n}y^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,x^{n(s+1)}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{s}(y-jn)^{m}\right),

which proves the first identity by induction on
s
. The second identity is analogous using
z^{m}y^{n}=(y-m)^{n}z^{m}
.

For
(x^{n}z^{m})^{s}
, the existence and uniqueness of the PBW expansion (
3.4
) is immediate from PBW basis.

To obtain the recursion for
U^{(n,m)}_{s,\ell}(y)
, one multiplies (
3.4
) by
x^{n}z^{m}
on the right, then repeatedly normal orders each subword
z^{(\cdot)}x^{n}
using (
3.1
) and finally pushes any intermediate
x
-powers past polynomials in
y
via
f(y)x^{r}=x^{r}f(y-r)
. This produces an explicit recursive procedure expressed solely in terms of
W^{(n)}_{\bullet,\bullet}
.

(3)

This is the special case of (2) applied to the block
xyz

(xyz)^{s}
by iterated multiplication and repeatedly reducing the forbidden pairs

zx,zy,yx
produces a unique expression of the form (
3.6
). The same normal ordering step
zx=\beta xz+y
is responsible for the
[r]_{\beta}
-coefficients in the recursion.

(4)

Write
(x^{n}y^{m}z^{t})^{s}
as a product of
s
identical blocks and proceed as in (2): first commute the
y
-powers past the
x
-powers using that

y^{m}x^{n}=x^{n}(y-n)^{m}

to obtain the top term

x^{ns}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(y-jn)^{m}\right)z^{ts}.

All remaining corrections come from normal ordering subwords
z^{r}x^{n}
and thus are governed by the same coefficients
W^{(n)}_{\bullet,\bullet}
. This yields a unique PBW expansion of the form (
3.8
).

(5)

For
(x+y)^{n}
, write

(x+y)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}S_{n,k}(y)

and multiply on the left by
(x+y)
to obtain

\displaystyle(x+y)^{n+1}=

\displaystyle\,x\sum_{k}x^{k}S_{n,k}(y)+y\sum_{k}x^{k}S_{n,k}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{k}x^{k+1}S_{n,k}(y)+\sum_{k}x^{k}(y-k)S_{n,k}(y),

using that
yx^{k}=x^{k}(y-k)
. Comparing coefficients of
x^{k}
gives

S_{n+1,k}(y)=S_{n,k-1}(y)+(y-k)S_{n,k}(y)

and the stated initial conditions.

For

(y+z)^{n}=\sum_{k}T_{n,k}(y)z^{k}

multiply on the left by
(y+z)
and use
zf(y)=f(y-1)z
. Then

\displaystyle(y+z)^{n+1}=

\displaystyle\,y\sum_{k}T_{n,k}(y)z^{k}+z\sum_{k}T_{n,k}(y)z^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{k}(yT_{n,k}(y))z^{k}+\sum_{k}T_{n,k}(y-1)z^{k+1}

which yields

T_{n+1,k}(y)=yT_{n,k}(y)+T_{n,k-1}(y-1).

Finally, for
(x+z)^{n}
one argues exactly as in part (2): PBW gives the uniqueness of (
3.11
) and the recursion for
E_{n;i,k}(y)
is obtained by expanding
(x+z)^{n+1}=(x+z)^{n}(x+z)
and repeatedly normal ordering every subword
zx^{r}
using (
3.1
).

∎

(x+z)^{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\geq 0\\

(1)

\displaystyle\ =(zx)x^{m-1}=(\beta xz+b)x^{m-1}=\beta x(zx^{m-1})+bx^{m-1}

(2)

For all

(

(5)

Since

and

y
and

(x+z)^{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\geq 0\\
i+k\leq n\end{subarray}}x^{i}E_{n;i,k}(y)z^{k},\quad E_{n;i,k}(y)\in\Bbbk[y],

k<0

(4)

3.2.5.
Algebras of type (2)(v)

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
A
be the
\Bbbk
-algebra generated by
x,y,z
with relations

yz-zy=az,\quad zx-\beta xz=0\quad{\rm and}\quad xy-yx=x,

where
a\in\Bbbk
and
\beta\in\Bbbk^{\times}
. Then the following hold:

(1)

For all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,

y^{n}x^{m}=x^{m}(y-m)^{n},\quad z^{n}x^{m}=\beta^{mn}x^{m}z^{n},\quad z^{n}y^{m}=(y-an)^{m}z^{n}.

(2)

For all
m,n,s\in\mathbb{N}
,

(x^{n}y^{m})^{s}=x^{ns}\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(y-jn)^{m},\quad(x^{n}z^{m})^{s}=\beta^{nm\binom{s}{2}}x^{ns}z^{ms}.

Moreover,
(y^{n}z^{m})^{s}
has the explicit form

(3.53)

(y^{n}z^{m})^{s}=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(y-ajm)^{n}\right)z^{ms}.

(3)

For all
s\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.54)

(xyz)^{s}=\beta^{\binom{s}{2}}x^{s}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(y-aj)\right)z^{s}.

(4)

For all
n,m,t,s\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.55)

(x^{n}y^{m}z^{t})^{s}=\beta^{nt\binom{s}{2}}x^{ns}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(y-ajt)^{m}\right)z^{ts}.

(5)

There exist uniquely determined polynomials
S_{n,k}(y)\in\Bbbk[y]
such that

(x+y)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}S_{n,k}(y),

characterized by the Stirling-type recursion

S_{0,0}(y)=1,\quad S_{0,k}(y)=0\ (k\geq 1),\quad S_{n+1,k}(y)=S_{n,k-1}(y)+(y-k)S_{n,k}(y).

Moreover, since
zx=\beta xz
, the
q
-binomial theorem gives

(x+z)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{\beta}x^{n-k}z^{k}.

Finally,
(y+z)^{n}
satisfies the
a
-shift binomial formula

(y+z)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{n,k}(y)z^{k},

where the coefficients are characterized by

T_{0,0}(y)=1,\ T_{0,k}(y)=0\ (k\geq 1),\quad T_{n+1,k}(y)=yT_{n,k}(y)+T_{n,k-1}(y-a).

Proof.

(1)

From
yx=xy-x
we obtain
yx=x(y-1)
, hence
f(y)x^{m}=x^{m}f(y-m)
for all
f\in\Bbbk[y]
,
and thus
y^{n}x^{m}=x^{m}(y-m)^{n}
.
From
zx=\beta xz
we immediately get
z^{n}x^{m}=\beta^{mn}x^{m}z^{n}
.

Next,
yz-zy=az
is equivalent to
zy=(y-a)z
. Thus for
t\in\mathbb{N}
,

zy^{t}=(y-a)^{t}z,

and iterating gives
z^{n}y^{t}=(y-an)^{t}z^{n}
, i.e.
z^{n}y^{m}=(y-an)^{m}z^{n}
.

(2)

The identity for
(x^{n}y^{m})^{s}
is proved as in the Weyl-type case: from
y^{m}x^{n}=x^{n}(y-n)^{m}
one deduces

(x^{n}y^{m})^{s+1}=x^{n(s+1)}\prod_{j=0}^{s}(y-jn)^{m}.

For
(x^{n}z^{m})^{s}
, since
z^{m}x^{n}=\beta^{mn}x^{n}z^{m}
, the standard
q
-commuting lemma yields
(x^{n}z^{m})^{s}=\beta^{mn\binom{s}{2}}x^{ns}z^{ms}
.
For
(y^{n}z^{m})^{s}
, note that
z^{m}y^{n}=(y-am)^{n}z^{m}
, hence by induction

(y^{n}z^{m})^{s+1}=(y^{n}z^{m})^{s}y^{n}z^{m}=\Big(\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(y-ajm)^{n}\Big)z^{ms}y^{n}z^{m}=\Big(\prod_{j=0}^{s}(y-ajm)^{n}\Big)z^{m(s+1)}.

(3)

Write
(xyz)^{s+1}=(xyz)^{s}xyz
. Using
zx=\beta xz
we get a factor
\beta^{s}
at the
(s+1)
-st step, so the total contribution is
\beta^{\binom{s}{2}}
. Moreover, the
z
coming from the rightmost factor meets
y
-powers created
previously. Since
zy=(y-a)z
, at the
j
-th step we pick up the factor
(y-aj)
.

(4)

Set
B:=x^{n}y^{m}z^{t}
. Since
zx=\beta xz
we have
z^{t}x^{n}=\beta^{nt}x^{n}z^{t}
.
Also, from
zy=(y-a)z
we get
z^{t}y^{m}=(y-at)^{m}z^{t}
. Hence

\displaystyle B^{s+1}

\displaystyle\ =B^{s}x^{n}y^{m}z^{t}=\beta^{nts}x^{ns}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(y-ajt)^{m}\right)z^{ts}x^{n}y^{m}z^{t}

\displaystyle\ =\beta^{nt(s+1)s/2}x^{n(s+1)}\prod_{j=0}^{s}(y-ajt)^{m}z^{t(s+1)},

which is the expression (
3.55
).

(5)

The expansion of
(x+y)^{n}
is the same as in the Weyl relation
xy-yx=x
, giving the Stirling-type recursion for
S_{n,k}(y)
. Since
zx=\beta xz
, the
q
-binomial theorem yields the Gaussian expansion of
(x+z)^{n}
with parameter
q=\beta
.
Finally, from
zy=(y-a)z
one checks that for
f\in\Bbbk[y]
,
zf(y)=f(y-a)z
, so multiplying

(y+z)^{n}=\sum_{k}T_{n,k}(y)z^{k}

by
(y+z)
on the left yields
T_{n+1,k}(y)=yT_{n,k}(y)+T_{n,k-1}(y-a)
.

∎

(x^{n}y^{m}z^{t})^{s}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{ns,ms\}}x^{ns-\ell}R^{(n,m,t)}_{s,\ell}y^{ms-\ell}z^{ts+\ell}.

for all

.

(5)

Since
z
is central and commutes with
x
and
y
, one has the ordinary binomial formulas

Finally,

(3.149)

(x+y)^{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\geq 0\\
i+2k\leq n\end{subarray}}x^{i}E_{n;i,k}y^{n-i-2k}z^{k},\ E_{n;i,k}\in\Bbbk,

E_{n+1;i,k}=E_{n;i-1,k}+E_{n;i,k}-(n+2-i-2k)E_{n;i,k-1},

if

E_{n;i,k}=(-1)^{k}\frac{n!}{i!(n-i-2k)!k!2^{k}},\text{ for }i+2k\leq n.

Proof.

(1)

The identities

and

Write

and substitute (
3.153
); collecting the coefficients of

gives (
3.140
).

(2)

The formulas for
(x^{n}z^{m})^{s}
and
(y^{n}z^{m})^{s}
are immediate since
z
commutes with
x
and
y
. For (
3.142
), PBW gives the existence and uniqueness of the coefficients
U^{(n,m)}_{s,\ell}
.

3.142
) holds for some

on the right:

Normal order the subword

using (
3.139
) with

replaced by
n

Since
z
is central, this yields exactly (
3.143
) after reindexing
\ell\mapsto\ell-k
.

(3)

Since
z
is central,
\left(xyz\right)^{s}=(xy)^{s}z^{s}
. We claim that

(3.154)

(xy)^{s}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}x^{s-\ell}y^{s-\ell}V_{s,\ell}z^{\ell}.

For

. Assume (
3.154
) for
s

Using that

y^{r}x=xy^{r}-ry^{r-1}z\text{ for }r\geq 1,

(proved by induction from

\displaystyle x^{s-\ell}\big(y^{s-\ell}x\big)yz^{\ell}

\displaystyle\ =x^{s-\ell}\big(xy^{s-\ell}-(s-\ell)y^{s-\ell-1}z\big)yz^{\ell}

Collecting coefficients of
x^{(s+1)-\ell}y^{(s+1)-\ell}z^{\ell}

3.145
).
Multiplying (
3.154
) by

gives (
3.144
).
Finally, (
3.146
) follows from (
3.145
) together with the standard Stirling recursion

\left\{\begin{matrix}s+1\\
r\end{matrix}\right\}=r\left\{\begin{matrix}s\\
r\end{matrix}\right\}+\left\{\begin{matrix}s\\
r-1\end{matrix}\right\},

after the change

.

(4)

Since
z
is central,

(x^{n}y^{m}z^{t})^{s}=(x^{n}y^{m})^{s}z^{ts}.

Substituting (
3.142
) gives (
3.147

3.148

3.143

(5)

The expansions for

and

commutes with

and

and use (

-term:

Collecting coefficients of

3.150
).

3.156
)
(

(5)

is central and commutes with

and

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,x(x^{m-1}y-b(m-1)x^{m-2})-bx^{m-1},

3.172

3.5.5.
Algebras of type (5)(v)

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
A
be the
\Bbbk
-algebra generated by
x,y,z
with relations

yz-zy=az,\ zx-xz=z\quad{\rm and}\quad xy-yx=0,\quad{\rm where}\ a\in\Bbbk^{\times}.

For
s\in\mathbb{N}
, set the rising factorial

(x)^{\overline{s}}:=\prod_{r=0}^{s-1}(x+r)\in\Bbbk[x],

and the
a
-step falling factorial

\left(y\right)^{\underline{s}}_{a}:=\prod_{r=0}^{s-1}(y-ra)\in\Bbbk[y],\quad{\rm with}\,\,(x)^{\overline{0}}=(y)^{\underline{0}}_{a}:=1.

Then the following assertions hold:

(1)

For all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
y^{n}x^{m}=x^{m}y^{n}
. Moreover,

(3.192)

z^{n}x^{m}=(x+n)^{m}z^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}n^{k}x^{m-k}z^{n},

and

(3.193)

z^{n}y^{m}=\left(y-na\right)^{m}z^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(-na)^{k}y^{m-k}z^{n}.

Equivalently, the coefficients

W^{(m)}_{n,k}:=\binom{m}{k}n^{k}\quad{\rm and}\quad\widehat{W}^{(m)}_{n,k}:=\binom{m}{k}(-na)^{k}

are the unique scalars such that

z^{n}x^{m}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}x^{m-k}W^{(m)}_{n,k}z^{n}\quad{\rm and}\quad z^{n}y^{m}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}y^{m-k}\widehat{W}^{(m)}_{n,k}z^{n}.

(2)

For all
m,n,s\in\mathbb{N}
,
\left(x^{n}y^{m}\right)^{s}=x^{ns}y^{ms}
. Moreover, we get the product forms

(3.194)

\left(x^{n}z^{m}\right)^{s}=\left(\prod_{r=0}^{s-1}(x+rm)^{n}\right)z^{ms}\quad{\rm and}\quad(y^{n}z^{m})^{s}=\left(\prod_{r=0}^{s-1}(y-rma)^{n}\right)z^{ms}.

Equivalently, there exist uniquely determined scalars

U^{(n,m)}_{s,\ell},\ \widehat{U}^{(n,m)}_{s,\ell}\in\Bbbk
such that

(3.195)

\displaystyle\left(x^{n}z^{m}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{ns}x^{ns-\ell}U^{(n,m)}_{s,\ell}z^{ms},

(3.196)

\displaystyle\left(y^{n}z^{m}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{ns}y^{ns-\ell}\widehat{U}^{(n,m)}_{s,\ell}z^{ms},

with
U^{(n,m)}_{1,0}=\widehat{U}^{(n,m)}_{1,0}=1
and
U^{(n,m)}_{1,\ell}=\widehat{U}^{(n,m)}_{1,\ell}=0
for
\ell\geq 1
, and the explicit recursions

(3.197)

\displaystyle U^{(n,m)}_{s+1,\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\min\{n,\ell\}}\binom{n}{k}(sm)^{k}U^{(n,m)}_{s,\ell-k},\text{ for }s\geq 1,\ \ell\geq 0,

(3.198)

\displaystyle\widehat{U}^{(n,m)}_{s+1,\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\min\{n,\ell\}}\binom{n}{k}(-sma)^{k}\widehat{U}^{(n,m)}_{s,\ell-k},\text{ for }s\geq 1,\ \ell\geq 0.

(3)

One has the closed form

(3.199)

\left(xyz\right)^{s}=(x)^{\overline{s}}(y)^{\underline{s}}_{a}z^{s}.

Equivalently, there exist uniquely determined scalars
V_{s;i,j}\in\Bbbk
, for
0\leq i,j\leq s
such that

(3.200)

\left(xyz\right)^{s}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\ \sum_{j=0}^{s}x^{i}y^{j}V_{s;i,j}z^{s},

with
V_{1;1,1}=1
and
V_{1;i,j}=0
for
(i,j)\neq(1,1)
, and the explicit recursion

(3.201)

V_{s+1;i,j}=V_{s;i-1,j-1}+sV_{s;i-1,j}-saV_{s;i,j-1}-s^{2}aV_{s;i,j},\text{ for }s\geq 1,

where
V_{s;i,j}=0
if
i<0
or
j<0
or
i>s
or
j>s
.

(4)

For all
n,m,t,s\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the closed product form given by

(3.202)

\left(x^{n}y^{m}z^{t}\right)^{s}=\left(\prod_{r=0}^{s-1}(x+rt)^{n}(y-rta)^{m}\right)z^{ts}.

That is, there exist uniquely determined elements
R^{(n,m,t)}_{s;p,q}\in\Bbbk
such that

(3.203)

\left(x^{n}y^{m}z^{t}\right)^{s}=\sum_{p\geq 0}\sum_{q\geq 0}x^{p}y^{q}R^{(n,m,t)}_{s;p,q}z^{ts},

with

and all other
R^{(n,m,t)}_{1;p,q}=0
, and the explicit recursion

(3.204)

R^{(n,m,t)}_{s+1;p,q}=\sum_{u=0}^{n}\sum_{v=0}^{m}\binom{n}{u}\binom{m}{v}\left(st\right)^{u}\left(-sta\right)^{v}R^{(n,m,t)}_{s;p-(n-u),\ q-(m-v)},\text{ for }s\geq 1,

with the convention
R^{(n,m,t)}_{s;p,q}=0
if
p<0
or
q<0
.

(5)

Since

Moreover, there exist uniquely determined scalars

and
E_{0;i,k}=\widehat{E}_{0;i,k}=0

\displaystyle\ E_{n;i-1,k}+kE_{n;i,k}+E_{n;i,k-1}\quad{\rm and}

Proof.

(1)

Having in mind Definition

class ThreeSkewPBW:

def __init__(self, K, params):

"""

K: a field where alpha,beta,gamma are invertible.

params: dict with keys:

alpha,beta,gamma, A1,B1,C1,D1, A2,B2,C2,D2, A3,B3,C3,D3

"""

self.K = K

self.p = params

self.forbidden = {(’y’,’x’), (’z’,’y’), (’z’,’x’)}

def _rewrite_pair(self, pair):

K = self.K

p = self.p

alpha = p[’alpha’]; beta = p[’beta’]; gamma = p[’gamma’]

A1,B1,C1,D1 = p[’A1’],p[’B1’],p[’C1’],p[’D1’]

A2,B2,C2,D2 = p[’A2’],p[’B2’],p[’C2’],p[’D2’]

A3,B3,C3,D3 = p[’A3’],p[’B3’],p[’C3’],p[’D3’]

# yx -> gammaˆ{-1}(xy - (A3 x + B3 y + C3 z + D3))

if pair == (’y’,’x’):

invg = K(1)/gamma

return {

(’x’,’y’): invg,

(’x’,): -invg*A3,

(’y’,): -invg*B3,

(’z’,): -invg*C3,

(): -invg*D3

}

# zy -> alphaˆ{-1}(yz - (A1 x + B1 y + C1 z + D1))

if pair == (’z’,’y’):

inva = K(1)/alpha

return {

(’y’,’z’): inva,

(’x’,): -inva*A1,

(’y’,): -inva*B1,

(’z’,): -inva*C1,

(): -inva*D1

}

# zx -> beta*xz + (A2 x + B2 y + C2 z + D2)

if pair == (’z’,’x’):

return {

(’x’,’z’): K(beta),

(’x’,): K(A2),

(’y’,): K(B2),

(’z’,): K(C2),

(): K(D2)

}

return None

def reduce_lin(self, expr):

"""

expr: dict word(tuple of ’x’,’y’,’z’) -> coefficient in K.

"""

K = self.K

expr = {tuple(w): K(c) for w,c in expr.items() if c != 0}

changed = True

while changed:

changed = False

new = {}

for word, coef in expr.items():

idx = None

for i in range(len(word)-1):

if (word[i], word[i+1]) in self.forbidden:

idx = i

break

if idx is None:

new[word] = new.get(word, K(0)) + coef

continue

changed = True

pre = word[:idx]

suf = word[idx+2:]

repl = self._rewrite_pair((word[idx], word[i+1]))

repl = self._rewrite_pair((word[idx], word[idx+1]))

for w2, c2 in repl.items():

wfull = pre + w2 + suf

new[wfull] = new.get(wfull, K(0)) + coef*c2

expr = {w:c for w,c in new.items() if c != 0}

return expr

def NF_word(self, word):

return self.reduce_lin({tuple(word): self.K(1)})

def add(self, A, B, sA=None, sB=None):

K = self.K

if sA is None: sA = K(1)

if sB is None: sB = K(1)

C = {}

for w,c in A.items():

C[w] = C.get(w,K(0)) + sA*c

for w,c in B.items():

C[w] = C.get(w,K(0)) + sB*c

return {w:c for w,c in C.items() if c != 0}

def mul(self, A, B):

K = self.K

prod = {}

for w1,c1 in A.items():

for w2,c2 in B.items():

w = w1 + w2

prod[w] = prod.get(w, K(0)) + c1*c2

return self.reduce_lin(prod)

def pow(self, A, n):

K = self.K

E = {(): K(1)}

if n == 0:

return E

for _ in range(n):

E = self.mul(E, A)

return E

def X(self): return {(’x’,): self.K(1)}

def Y(self): return {(’y’,): self.K(1)}

def Z(self): return {(’z’,): self.K(1)}

def collect_PBW(self, NF):

out = {}

for w,c in NF.items():

i=0

while i<len(w) and w[i]==’x’: i+=1

j=0

t=i

while t<len(w) and w[t]==’y’:

j += 1

t += 1

k = len(w)-t

out[(i,j,k)] = out.get((i,j,k), self.K(0)) + c

return {e:v for e,v in out.items() if v != 0}
