 The ability to forecast instability and conflict with sufficient skill to deploy actionable responses remains an unsolved
 challenge for national security. Current models struggle to predict conflict emergence or cessation. Using previously
 unused, high-value datasets and state-of-the-art AI models alongside model validation with conflict case studies, we
 can enable more timely and accurate conflict forecasting. Navigating and visualizing our model input datasets using
 data analytics tools such as Python, Google Earth Engine, and Power BI, I attempt to find correlations between the
 data and a key case study, the 2024 Syrian rebel offensive. As the offensive occurred suddenly during a frozen state
 in the war, the objective was to uncover possible causation factors using novel datasets for the rapid collapse of the
 Assad regime. Correlations were found between U.S. sanctions and regime social services provisions, as well as VIIRS
 nighttime lights data with Syrian regime and rebel control.

I. INTRODUCTION

A. Current Conflict Forecasting

 Instability and conflict forecasts use advanced data analysis
and machine learning to predict the likelihood, intensity, and
location of armed conflicts and civil unrest worldwide. Fore-
casting regional conflict and instability is a national security
imperative needed to protect U.S. assets and operations.
 Current conflict early warning systems (CEWS) apply di-
verse machine learning and deep learning techniques. Data
inputs to current CEWS range from tens to hundreds of eco-
nomic, political, and environmental variables1 . However, the
United Nations Development Program’s crisis-risk unit, has
stated that “all models appear unreliable” in the task of pre-
dicting conflict onset2 . Few existing CEWS update inputs or
generate forecasts at the sub-annual time scales needed to sup-

emergence and identify specific conflict development indica-

war, and the 2024 Syrian rebel offensive. This report will
