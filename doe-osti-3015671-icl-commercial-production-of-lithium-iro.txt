Introduction
ICL was investing in producing cathode active material, Lithium Iron Phosphate (LFP), used
in the production of batteries for both electric vehicles and stationary storage. The demand
for LFP was increasing with the global focus on sustainable energy, and there was no
current manufacturer of this material in the United States. As such, imported LFP is subject
to the uncertainties around supply chain and control of a foreign supplier. This investment
would have allowed ICL to be a United States leader in LFP production. To gain a position in
this market, ICL licensed the technology from a firm that has the process technology and
has reliably supplied LFP into the battery market and currently has customers purchasing
their product.

This project intended to design and build an LFP cathode active materials production plant
with an output of 30,000 metric tons of material in St. Louis, MO that would use LFP
manufacturing technology from Taiwanese company Aleees.

United States Department of Energy (DOE) awarded ICL a financial grant of up to $197.4M
to offset the cost of building this facility. ICL engaged an Owner Engineer (WSP) and a
Design Engineer (Clayco) on the project.

Progress Toward Statement of Project Objectives (SOPO)
Budget Period 1
Task 1.1 – Plant Design
Subtask 1.1.1 – The Recipient will engage and contract external design engineering firm for
initial front end engineering services and conceptual plant and process design. The work
will include refactoring the licensed plant layout to US standards.
 Hargrove Engineering was contracted to work on design engineering. Layout
 and basic design completed with submittal of FEL-2 in November 2023. Cost
 of $650M was estimated at +-30%.

 Due to the change in the plant location, layout, and design, FEL-2 level
 design engineering will need to be repeated (in part). Additionally, ICL’s
 engagement with McCarthy (and therefore Hargrove) was terminated. A new
 contracting strategy was developed that reflected a more traditional design,
 bid, build approach using an EPC partner to complete the work.

Subtask 1.1.2 – The Recipient will perform initial front end design work for the plant, which
will include value engineering.
 Hargrove Engineering, McCarthy Construction and ICL Project Team
 completed initial Value Engineering in January 2024. A savings of $100M was
 identified through a combination of action items. Most important was the
 need to relocate the facility to an alternate site to save approximately $50M
 with no need to bring in dirt to get out of floodplain and extra foundation work
 to support a three-story building built on a former landfill. An additional
 $50M in savings was identified through process optimization and layout
 changes. ICL received Board approval to absorb the additional $50M in
 capital expense for this project while driving for continued cost savings
 through competitively bidding the General Contractor portion of this project.

Task 1.2 –– Research and Development
Subtask 1.2.1 –– The Recipient will initiate laboratory work to familiarize with LFP reaction
chemistry and conversion. This will include synthesis work and characterization to support
process improvements.
 ICL successfully synthesized LFP from iron powder to final product in the
 laboratory at 7kg scale according to the licensed technology method.
 Laboratory work reduced the production time of the LFP which significantly

 reduced capital for the LFP plant (>$20M) as part of the Value Engineering
 effort. All battery testing would have been completed at Webster Groves
 Technical Center.

Task 1.3 –– Equipment Specifications and Ordering
Subtask 1.3.1 –– The Recipient will qualify and write specifications for the primary
production equipment for the LFP Line.
Subtask 1.3.2 –– The Recipient will place the orders for all the QC and R&D equipment.
 Almost all laboratory equipment for LFP reaction chemistry and
 characterization was purchased and installed during the first budget period.
 Vertical and horizontal ball mill installation was completed which allowed for
 efficient milling prior to spray drying. A 3L lab spray drier was purchased and
 installed. Two nitrogen inerted sintering ovens were purchased and installed.
 A jet mill was purchased and installed along with cyclone collection unit. For
 characterization a glove box was purchased and installed along with
 calendaring equipment. A pellet density press was installed along with an 8-
 channel battery cell cycler. An ICP-OES unit with microwave digester was
 purchased and installed. A Carbon Analyzer was purchased and installed.

Project Cancellation
In Q2 2025, the project began to slow to allow stakeholders to evaluate the final production
process and begin marketing a product with improved specifications. ICL received notice in
October 2025 that the grant was terminated and ICL’s Board of Directors elected not to
proceed with the project shortly thereafter. The following SOPO tasks were not completed,
but are listed here for the sake of report completion.
