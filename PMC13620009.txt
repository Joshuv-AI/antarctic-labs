Bacilli the most important biocontrol agent in Brazilian agriculture: from mechanisms of action to market adoption

Wagner Bettiol, Roberto Lanna-Filho, Cristiano Limberger

Abstract

Bacilli have become one of the most commercially significant groups of microorganisms used in agricultural biocontrol products in Brazil. Their ability to form endospores and produce a wide range of metabolites allows them to control plant diseases, promote plant growth, and alleviate stress, supporting their extensive adoption in crop protection systems. This study analyzes the Brazilian market for Bacillus-based products used in plant disease management and summarizes their mechanisms of action. Data on commercial registrations, active strains, product formulations, and target diseases were compiled from Brazilian national regulatory databases. Market indicators, such as potential area treated (PAT) and turnover, were estimated using structured agricultural surveys conducted across Brazil during the 2019/2020 and 2024/2025 crop seasons, the latter encompassing more than 16,000 interviews with farmers and agricultural managers. By December/2025, 297 Bacillus-based products containing 126 strains were commercialized in Brazil, with Bacillus amyloliquefaciens, B. subtilis, B. velezensis, and B. pumilus being the most prominent species. These products are applied via seed treatment, soil drenching, furrow, and foliar spraying to manage fungal, bacterial, and nematode diseases. During the analyzed period, PAT increased from 7.5 to 37.2 million hectares (391% growth) and turnover increased from US$114.31 to US$403.17 million (252% growth). Currently, Bacillus-based products accounted for approximately 48% of the Brazilian biological control market, highlighting the prevalence of this genus in the sector. Overall, these results demonstrate how advances in microbial biotechnology, regulatory development, and farmer adoption have positioned Bacillus spp. as key components of the rapidly expanding biocontrol industry.

Introduction

Bacilli are renowned for their versatility in suppressing a wide range of plant pathogens, promoting plant growth, and alleviating abiotic stresses [–]. This multifunctionality is closely associated with their metabolic diversity and ecological adaptability.

Bacillus spp. produce endospores, which are extremely resistant structures able to withstand several adverse environmental conditions [, ]. This sporulation capacity enables these bacteria to survive, persist, and remain metabolically viable under unfavorable conditions, allowing these bacteria to thrive across a wide range of environments and enhancing their effectiveness as biocontrol and plant growth-promoting agents [, ].

As bioactive constituents of commercial formulations, endospore-based products offer enhanced flexibility of use by preserving bacterial cell viability under exposure to a broad range of chemical agents, thermal extremes, and water deficit conditions [–]. From a practical standpoint, endospore-based products exhibit wide compatibility with different synthetic fungicides, herbicides, and insecticides [–], which enables their co-application through tank mixing prior to field application. Moreover, Bacillus species are able to survive in crop debris by synthesizing enzymatic complexes capable of degrading plant straw and acting against pathogens [, –]. This ability makes Bacillus species highly competitive and well suited to establish in specific niches within intensive agricultural systems, where these bacteria can exert multiple mechanisms of action against pathogens [, ].

The mechanisms of action employed by Bacillus species in the control of plant pathogens include antibiosis, colonization and competition for ecological niches, quorum-quenching, and induced resistance [–]. In terms of plant growth promotion, Bacillus acts through both direct or indirect mechanisms. Direct mechanisms include the production of phytohormones (indole-3-acetic acid, cytokinin, gibberellic acid), solubilize phosphorus and other nutrients, and the production of siderophores, which stimulate plant growth [–]. Indirect mechanisms include changes in root system architecture, which influence the plant’s ability to exploit the soil resources by improving water and nutrient uptake [, ].

Several species of Bacillus have been shown to improve plant tolerance to abiotic stress, such as salinity stress [–], drought stress [], and thermal stress [, ]. Medeiros and Bettiol [] reported increased levels of peroxidase, polyphenol oxidase, phenylalanine ammonia lyase, and L-proline in tomato plants treated with Bacillus grown under saline stress when compared to those grown without salt stress. Fonseca et al. [] reported that sugarcane under drought stress inoculated with Bacillus subtilis improved plant nutrition and chlorophyll concentrations. Also, these authors reported that the gas exchange parameters, root growth, tillering, stalk weight, and higher sucrose concentration in the stalks were increased.

In Brazil, Bacillus-based formulations for biological control purposes account for approximately 80% of the number of products available on the market [, ]. Products for nematode control stand out, representing nearly 70% []. Among the 729 microbiological-based products registered for the control of fungi, bacteria, nematodes, and insects, the species B. amyloliquefaciens (7%), B. thuringiensis (6.2%), B. subtilis (4%), B. velezensis (2.2%), and B. licheniformis (1%) stand out as the principal active agents comprising commercial formulations [, ]. These findings reflect the global market scenario [–], which highlights the widespread adoption of formulations based on Bacillus strains for the biological control of pests and diseases across diverse crops of major economic importance worldwide [, ].

As a result of these well-documented physiological and biochemical benefits, Bacillus species have been widely commercialized for agriculture use, with numerous Bacillus-based products currently available worldwide. Given Brazil’s importance in the production and consumption of biocontrol agents [–], this review discusses the growth of the Brazilian market for Bacillus-based products in terms of both potential treated area and turnover. We also present data on the potential treated area and turnover of all commercial Bacillus-based products registered in Brazil between 2020 and 2025, in addition to outlining the mechanisms of action of Bacillus species in the control of plant diseases.

Mechanisms of action of Bacillus to control plant disease

Widely used as biocontrol agents, Bacillus species exhibit a broad range of mechanisms that act either directly or indirectly against plant pathogens [–]. In addition to conventional biocontrol strategies - such as antibiosis, competition, and the induction of systemic resistance - Bacillus species can also activate the quorum quenching system. This process inhibits pathogenesis by interrupting the mechanisms of pathogen attack [–].

The antagonistic activity exerted by Bacillus species occurs dynamically and simultaneously on different pathogens structures, resulting in a multi-site mode capable of repressing or suppressing the pathogenic activity. Understanding these mechanisms and their role in pathogen suppression is fundamental for optimizing the application of Bacillus-based biocontrol agents and maximizing their effectiveness against major diseases affecting economically important crops. The primary mechanisms of action include antibiosis, colonization and competition for ecological niches, quorum quenching, and induced resistance.

- Antibiosis

The biocontrol activity of Bacillus species is largely attributed to their ability to produce a wide range of chemically diverse compounds that act directly against pathogens. Bacilli genomes are particularly rich in biosynthetic gene clusters encoding bioactive secondary metabolites, such as toxins, bacteriocins, (lipo)peptides, polyketides, volatile organic compounds (VOCs), and siderophores [, ]. Furthermore, compounds that may be associated with primary metabolism, such as (hydro)lytic enzymes, also exhibit bioactive action against pathogens [–].

Up to 12% of the Bacillus genome is dedicated to the biosynthesis of metabolites [–], contributing to their survival and ecological fitness. In Bacillus subtilis strains, approximately 4 to 5% of their genome is dedicated to metabolite biosynthesis [], whereas Bacillus amyloliquefaciens strains may allocate about 8.5% [, , ]. For Bacillus velezensis strains, between 2.29 and 10% of the genome is devoted to the biosynthesis of antimicrobials [, ].

The primary antimicrobials produced by different Bacillus species are antibiotics [], which are (lipo)peptides of either ribosomal or non-ribosomal nature []. As ribosomal peptides, bacteriocins exhibit a broad antibacterial activity by inducing cell lysis, forming pores in the plasma membrane, or inhibiting cell wall biosynthesis [–].

Non-ribosomal (lipo)peptides are an extremely heterogeneous group of compounds which can be linear or cyclic in nature. Among Bacillus spp., the most studied compounds are the cyclic lipopeptides (CLPs), which exhibit amphiphilic nature [, ]. The primary mechanisms of action of cyclic lipopeptides involve interactions with the pathogen’s plasma membrane, leading to structural changes and increased permeability through rupture, solubilization, or the formation of ion-conducting pores. The most important CLPs are represented by the iturins, fengycins, and surfactins families [, , ]. Iturins display potent antifungal activity but are less active against bacteria []. The main mode of action of these compounds is the formation of ion-conducting transmembrane channels, which promote electrolyte leakage and disruption of the pathogen’s plasma membrane.

The antagonistic activity of Bacillus species can also be associated with the synthesis of volatile organic compounds (VOCs). In bacteria, VOCs - which belong to different chemical classes - are generally produced via catabolic pathways, including glycolysis, proteolysis, and lipolysis [, ]. In Bacillus, VOCs typically contain up to 20 carbon atoms and belong to the following chemical classes: alcohols, aldehydes, acids, benzenoids, ketones, esters, hydrocarbons, pyrazines, sulfur compounds, and terpenes [–]. Volatile organic compounds synthesized by Bacillus species have demonstrated bioactivity against fungi, bacteria, and nematodes.

Bacillus species are capable of synthesizing a diverse range of hydrolytic enzymes with the potential to collapse the cell wall of fungi and oomycetes [, ] and to degrade the cuticle of nematodes [–]. This enzymatic complex is formed by chitinases, β-glucanases, chitosanases, proteases, lipases, and cellulases, which act to hydrolyze specific components of the cell wall by breaking the glycosidic bonds that link polymeric structures. As a result, the target organism loses key protective and functional properties, such as selective permeability, tensile strength, and turgor-driven cell expansion, all of which are essential for growth and host infection [, ].

Iron is an essential element for various biological processes such as oxygen metabolism, DNA and RNA synthesis, electron transfer, and enzymatic processes []. Although iron is the fourth most abundant element on Earth, its bioavailability in aerobic soils is quite limited [, ]. This is because the predominant form in nature, the ferric ion (Fe3+), has low solubility, hindering its assimilation by both bacteria and plants. As a result, the amount of iron available to living organisms is extremely low. To survive with such a limited iron supply, Bacillus species synthesize non-ribosomal metal-chelating peptides (siderophores) of low molecular weight (∼400–1500 Da) [, ]. These peptides display a high affinity for Fe3+ (with association constants ranging from of 1023 a 1052) and form Fe-siderophore complexes which are capable of binding to membrane receptors (FeuA) [, ]. Since bacterial siderophores have a higher affinity for Fe3+ than the uptake systems of many pathogens, they sequester the available iron in the environment. This results in iron deprivation for the pathogen, leading to the inhibition of mycelial growth, reduced sporulation, or even cell death [, ]. On the other hand, plant growth is generally not affected by this depletion of iron in the rhizosphere caused by siderophores because most plants can thrive at much lower iron concentrations than most microorganisms [].

- b.Colonization and ecological niche competition

The success of biocontrol provided by Bacillus species is based on their ability to colonize and to establish themselves in specific plant niches. The synthesis of compounds toxic to pathogens directly depends on a sufficiently high bacterial population density on the host. In cellular aggregates (Fig. ), Bacillus stimulates the production of autoinducers which activate the expression of genes responsible for collective behaviors [–], including those responsible for the synthesis of bioactive molecules. In addition to creating an environment saturated with toxic compounds, a high bacterial population density also prevents pathogens from occupying the same space. Bacillus exhibits a faster growth rate compared to pathogens, providing a substantial competitive advantage in the occupation and colonization of specific niches. The radial growth (colonies) of Bacilli is around 2–5 mm after 24 h [, ], while pathogenic fungi grow on average 5–20 mm/day [–].

On the host surface, bacterial aggregates become embedded in a heterogeneous exopolymeric matrix (EPM), which protects the cells against adverse environmental conditions, such as toxic substances, desiccation, thermal extremes, pH changes, nutrient depletion, and solar irradiation [–]. Nutrients derived from the plant-microbe association accumulate within the biofilm, enabling the bacterial cells inside the exopolymeric matrix to remain viable for a long period. In addition, there is an accumulation of toxic compounds that permeate the aggregate, causing niche suppressiveness against phytopathogens. The occupation and colonization of spaces (anticlinal wall junctions, ostioles, substomatal chambers, and the base of trichomes) in the phylloplane prevent the establishment of pathogens and avert successful infection.

In the rhizoplane, Bacillus species form extensive regions of cellular aggregates, with the most intense accumulation occurring near micro-wounds and root elongation zones (e.g., behind the root cap) [–]. By forming a robust biofilm layer on the roots, Bacillus establishes a physico-toxic barrier - a biofilm-based defense that combines physical exclusion of phytopathogens with localized toxic effects mediated by antimicrobial compounds - that restricts the penetration and colonization of root phytopathogens. Seeds treated with Bacillus spp. show a relevant protective effect against pathogens due to the rapid multiplication rate and bacterial colonization on the seed surface. In addition to the topical action, Bacillus promotes a suppressive effect that extends to the spermosphere (Fig. ) and to the soil. At the moment of radicle protrusion, these bacteria form a dense biofilm in the rhizoplane, creating a persistent protective barrier against pathogens.

- c.Quorum-quenching

An important biocontrol mechanism triggered by Bacillus species involves interference with the intraspecific communication of bacteria and fungi. This process, known as quorum-quenching, interrupts the signaling mediated by quorum-sensing, leading to disturbances in physiological processes such as metabolism, virulence, sporulation, and biofilm formation [, ]. The quorum-quenching phenomenon was first reported in Bacillus thuringiensis, through the synthesis of the AiiA enzyme, which inactivated the quorum-sensing signal of Pectobacterium carotovorum (syn.: Erwinia carotovora), thereby attenuating the pathogen’s virulence [, ]. Quorum-sensing is a cell-cell communication system that enables microorganisms to detect population density and collectively coordinate gene expression. In bacterial and fungal pathogens, quorum-sensing plays a central role in both pathogenicity and survival within the host [, , ]. Consequently, the destruction of quorum-sensing signaling molecules causes drastic effects on pathogenesis and compromises essential processes required for successful host colonization.

- d.Induced resistance

The mechanism of induced resistance in plants can be triggered by cellular constituents or by compounds synthesized by Bacillus spp [–]. Plant defense responses, whether local or systemic, involve a complex network of interactions regulated by the pathways of salicylic acid (SA), jasmonic acid (JA), and ethylene (ET). The modulation of these pathways depends on proteins that exert synergistic, antagonistic, or cooperative effects among the mediators of Systemic Acquired Resistance (SAR) and Induced Systemic Resistance (ISR) [–]. The SAR pathway is SA-dependent, whereas the ISR pathway is JA/ET-dependent [, ]. Both defense mechanisms (SAR and ISR) can be activated by Bacillus spp., resulting in distal defense responses that provide plant protection in tissues spatially separated from the initial contact site of interaction [, ].

- e.From mechanisms to integrated crop protection

The multiple mechanisms described above should not be viewed as independent biological phenomena, but rather as interconnected biological processes that collectively determine the agronomic performance of Bacillus-based products under field conditions. Unlike conventional synthetic pesticides, whose efficacy generally depends on one or a few specific biochemical targets, Bacillus spp. simultaneously combine direct pathogen suppression through antibiosis, ecological exclusion by rapid colonization and niche competition, disruption of pathogen communication via quorum-quenching, activation of induced systemic resistance, and enhancement of plant vigor through growth promotion and improved tolerance to abiotic stresses. Consequently, field performance results from the integration of ecological, physiological, biochemical, and plant-mediated interactions operating throughout the crop cycle rather than from a single antimicrobial activity [–].

This multifunctional mode of action positions Bacillus-based products as components of Integrated Crop Protection (ICP), where biological control agents are incorporated into comprehensive disease management programs together with resistant cultivars, crop rotation, cultural practices, decision-support systems, and, when necessary, synthetic pesticides [, ]. Within this framework, Bacillus-based products are primarily deployed as preventive components of disease management to reduce primary inoculum, delay epidemic development, suppress pathogen establishment, and enhance crop resilience before severe disease outbreaks occur. Their broad compatibility with numerous fungicides and other crop protection products further facilitates integration into existing production systems without requiring major changes in growers’ management practices [–].

A practical example is the association of Bacillus-based products with demethylation inhibitor (DMI) fungicides, such as triazoles []. During plant colonization, Bacillus rapidly establishes biofilms on the phylloplane and rhizoplane, creating a stable microbial barrier that favors persistence, niche occupation, and the localized accumulation of bioactive metabolites, including cyclic lipopeptides, hydrolytic enzymes, siderophores, and volatile organic compounds [–]. These compounds directly interfere with fungal cell integrity, particularly through membrane disruption and inhibition of pathogen development. In contrast, triazoles inhibit sterol 14α-demethylase (CYP51), blocking ergosterol biosynthesis and compromising fungal membrane formation. Thus, biological and chemical agents target different physiological processes of the pathogen, providing distinct yet convergent modes of action of action that enhance disease suppression while simultaneously diversifying the selection pressures imposed on pathogen populations, thereby contributing to fungicide resistance management [–].

Another important aspect of the integration between Bacillus-based products and synthetic fungicides concerns the interaction between Bacillus-mediated induced resistance and the direct activity of systemic fungicides [, ]. Whereas fungicides predominantly protect plant tissues through direct activity against the pathogen after translocation within the plant, Bacillus-mediated induced resistance activates endogenous defense pathways regulated by salicylic acid, jasmonic acid, and ethylene, resulting in systemic defense responses in tissues spatially separated from the initial colonization site [–]. Consequently, chemical fungicides act directly on the pathogen, whereas Bacillus simultaneously enhances the defensive capacity of the host, generating a complementary protection strategy that combines direct antimicrobial activity with increased plant immune competence. This functional integration represents one of the principal reasons why Bacillus-based products have become increasingly incorporated into integrated crop protection programs worldwide.

An additional advantage of Bacillus-based products lies in their multisite biological activity. Because pathogen suppression results from the simultaneous action of several complementary mechanisms rather than from a single molecular target, these products impose substantially lower selection pressure for resistance than site-specific fungicides. Consequently, their incorporation into integrated disease management programs contributes not only to disease suppression but also to fungicide resistance management by diversifying selection pressures within pathogen populations [, ]. Taken together, these characteristics demonstrate that the value of Bacillus-based products extends well beyond their antimicrobial activity. Their multifunctionality, compatibility with conventional crop protection technologies, low selection pressure for resistance, and ability to simultaneously suppress pathogens, stimulate plant growth, and enhance crop resilience position them as biological components of modern ICP programs rather than as stand-alone alternatives to synthetic pesticides. Consequently, the continued expansion of Bacillus-based products is expected to be driven not only by advances in microbial biotechnology [, ], together with the increasing adoption of integrated and sustainable disease-management strategies aimed at maintaining crop productivity, reducing environmental impacts, and preserving fungicide efficacy over the long term [–].

Methodology to obtain information on the potential area treated and turnover of Bacillus-based products for plant disease control in Brazil in the 2024/2025 crop season

The market panel methodology involves structured, face-to-face interviews conducted with farmers and managers of agricultural properties. The survey covers the main crops in Brazilian agriculture, encompassing the most important regions in terms of cultivated area and production volume. Over the past six crop seasons, Kynetec has conducted approximately 16,000 interviews across different crops, with the sampling designed to consider both regional representativeness and landholding stratification within each region. The data obtained are analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics software for statistical analysis, data mining, and predictive modeling, which is widely used in the social sciences, market research, and academia (https://www.ibm.com/br-pt/products/spss-statistics).

During the 2019/2020, 2020/2021, 2021/2022, 2022/2023, 2023/2024, and 2024/2025 crop seasons, 15,404; 15,063; 14,839; 15,834; 15,903; and 15,708 interviews were conducted, respectively. Of this total number of interviewees, 30%, 36%, 39%, 43%, 48% and 52% adopted the use of biocontrol agents in the management of diseases and pests, respectively. Therefore, the surveys follow a rigorous statistical process for quantitative research, ensuring high levels of reliability of results and analyses. For each crop, the sample size and the corresponding margins of error are determined based on the indicators and the profile of the investigated universe. Table shows the sampling data and margins of error for the crops surveyed in Brazil during the 2024/2025 crop season.

During the interviews, respondents are questioned about the entire phytosanitary management system adopted for each specific crop, encompassing all stages, from seed and soil treatments, to foliar applications and post-harvest practices.

The primary objective of this study is to map the phytosanitary management practices implemented in each production system for each crop season. Based on these data, it is possible to consolidate and characterize the Brazilian market for crop protection products, including agrochemicals and biological agents, at the end-user level.

Growth trends in the potential area treated of Bacillus-based products for plant disease control in Brazil between 2020 and 2025

The total agricultural area in Brazil was 78,564,000; 82,860,000; 88,173,000; 91,391,000; 92,571,000 and 96,102,000 hectares, for the years 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025, respectively. These areas were primarily cultivated with soybean, corn, sugarcane, wheat, coffee, cotton, rice, common bean, citrus, eucalyptus, among other crops. The potential area treated (PAT) with crop protection products (both chemical and biological) was 1,511,064,000; 1,631,818,000; 1,770,877,000; 1,980,006,000; 2,200,268,000, and 2,480,015, 000 hectares from 2020 to 2025, respectively. This represents a 12% annual increase in the potential area treated between 2024 and 2025 (Table ).

The potential area treated with products containing biological control agents for crop protection in Brazil was 22,186,000; 26,632,000; 42,549,000; 52,056,000; 65,724,000; and 86,937,000 hectares, while the PAT with Bacillus-based products was 7,576,610; 9,591,760; 14,101,930; 18,097,660; 25,991,710; and 37,194,240 hectares, for the years 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025, respectively. Thus, the PAT with Bacillus-based products accounted for 34%, 36%, 34%, 35%, 40%, and 43% of the total PAT with products containing biological control agents in 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025, respectively (Table ). These figures indicate increases of 32%, and 35% in PAT between 2024 and 2025 for the products containing biocontrol agents and for Bacillus-based products, respectively.

The evolution of the potential area treated (PAT) with Bacillus-based products for plant disease control in Brazil between 2020 and 2025 was remarkable (Tables and ; Fig. A). The PAT surged dramatically from 7,576,610 hectares in 2020 to 37,194,240 hectares in 2025, representing a staggering increase of approximately 391% over this period.

The most commercially available species for plant disease control was B. amyloliquefaciens, with a PAT of 13,871,490 hectares in 2025, followed by B. subtilis, B. pumilus, C. firmus, B. velezensis, and B. methylotrophicus, with PAT of 3,352,440; 2,861,570; 1,624,020; 1,598,270; and 1,591,680 hectares, respectively. The PAT for Bacillus thuringiensis, used primarily for pest control but also as bionematicides, also increased during this period, reaching 4,200,320 hectares in 2025.

There is also a trend towards an expanding market for consortium-based products containing Bacillus species and Bacillus spp. plus antagonistic fungi between 2020 and 2025 (Table ). The most important consortium is that consisting of B. pumilus + B. subtilis + B. velezensis, covering 3,037,750 hectares in 2025. On the other hand, when considering consortium with fungi, the one consisting of B. amyloliquefaciens and Trichoderma reached 1.35 million hectares (Table ).

Another important consortium with fungi is: B. subtilis + Trichoderma sp. + P. lilacinus + Arthrobotrys oligospora + Pochonia chlamydosporia, with a PAT of 128 thousand hectares (Table ).

Discussion

In harvest season 2024/2025, the turnover for biological products in Brazil was US$855,680,000, and in the same period, the turnover for products containing Bacillus in their formulation was US$403,170,000. Thus, approximately 47% of the turnover of the Brazilian market for biological products consisted of Bacillus-based products. This number is extraordinary for a single genus (Table ; Fig. B). Over the past six years, the turnover for Bacillus-based products has hovered around 42% of total sales in the Brazilian market for biocontrol agents. These figures clearly confirm the importance of this group of microorganisms in the current Brazilian biocontrol market.

The increased importance of Bacillus-based products when considering the PAT is shown in Fig. A; Table . The PAT with biological products in 2025 was 86,937,000 ha, while the PAT considering only Bacillus-based products was 37,194,240 ha (Table ), which corresponds to approximately 43% of the total area treated with biological products. The percentage share of Bacillus-based products in the PAT was ranged from 34% to 43% in the years between 2020 and 2025, respectively.

Bacillus-based products are applied to a wide large of crops through seed treatment, drench, planting furrow, and foliar application.

The importance of Bacillus-based products in the Brazilian market is attributable to the biological and functional traits of this genus, as many Bacillus strains exhibit nematocidal, insecticidal, and fungicidal action, in addition to promoting plant growth and alleviating abiotic stresses. Moreover, Bacillus acts through highly diverse and complementary mechanisms of action. The ability to produce endospores enables the development of stable formulations with a shelf-life exceeding two years.

Considering the potential of Bacilli in not only controlling plant diseases but also promoting plant growth and alleviating water, saline, and thermal stresses, in addition to exhibiting a certain compatibility with chemical pesticides [–, , , , ], the number of Bacillus species to be investigated and explored is expected to increase in the coming years. Moreover, Bacilli are likely to play an increasing role in strategies aimed at reducing the use of nitrogen, phosphate, and potassium fertilizers; consequently, additional species and strains will be explored for this purpose.

A fundamental driver for expanding the exploration of new Bacillus species and strains lies in their capacity to produce metabolites that can be formulated into biochemical and, potentially, bioinspired synthetic fungicidal products. However, this strategy must be responsibly explored to avoid problems historically associated with synthetic chemical fungicides, such as the selection of resistant pathogens populations to their metabolites as well as ecotoxicological and toxicological problems.

We believe that the most effective way to fully harness Bacillus is not through the isolated application of their metabolites, but rather through formulations that combine these compounds with living cells, whether in vegetative form or as endospores. Under these conditions, Bacillus spp. can express their full biological and ecological potential, delivering durable benefits for both agriculture and the environment.

Conclusion

The Brazilian market analysis presented here demonstrates that these biological attributes have translated into rapid and sustained commercial adoption. The exponential growth in potential area treated (≈ 391%) and turnover (≈ 252%) between 2020 and 2025 positions Brazil as one of the most dynamic global hubs for Bacillus-based bioproducts. The predominance of species such as B. amyloliquefaciens, B. subtilis, B. velezensis, and B. pumilus reflects both their biological efficacy and the robustness of scientific evidence supporting their use. Moreover, the increasing adoption of multi-strain and multi-kingdom consortia signals a paradigm shift toward more ecologically informed formulations that more closely emulate natural suppressive microbiomes.

Market maturation reflects a broader regulatory refinement, characterized by stricter regulatory requirements for strain-level identification, improved taxonomic resolution, and higher-quality market surveillance. Together, these changes have contributed to greater transparency, consistency, and scientific rigor in product registration, aligning Brazil with international regulatory standards and enhancing farmer confidence and long-term market stability.

Looking ahead, the trajectory of Bacillus-based biocontrol is expected to accelerate further. Advances in genomics, metabolomics, and systems biology will enable more precise strain selection, optimization of metabolite profiles, and rational design of microbial consortia tailored to specific crops, pathogens, and environmental contexts. In parallel, the integration of Bacillus spp. into digital agriculture, predictive disease modeling, and climate-resilient production systems will expand their functional relevance beyond mere disease suppression, positioning Bacillus-based products as key biological components of Integrated Crop Protection systems and holistic crop health management.

In the context of global challenges - including climate change, pesticide resistance, regulatory restrictions on chemical inputs, and societal demand for sustainable food production - Bacillus-based products represent not merely an alternative, but a central pillar of future plant protection strategies.

The Brazilian experience documented in this study offers a compelling model for other agricultural regions, demonstrating how scientific knowledge, regulatory evolution, and market dynamics can converge to scale biologically based solutions without compromising productivity. As such, Bacillus spp. are poised to remain at the forefront of sustainable plant disease control in the coming decades.
