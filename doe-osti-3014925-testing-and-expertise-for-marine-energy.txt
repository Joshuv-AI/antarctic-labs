 ORIGINAL AGREEMENT / MODIFICATION 0

 Purpose:

 To provide validation infrastructure and conduct testing to explore the performance of the
 Virginia Tech AC-DC power converter designed for the Wave Energy Converter (WEC)

 Statement of Work:

 Virginia Tech (VT) had previously developed a 50 kW AC to DC power converter that is
 specifically designed to improve the performance and efficiency of wave energy converters
 (WECs). Through this CRADA, NREL will test the VT power converter via a coupled
 dynamometer and power electronics test platform. The NREL test platform will be comprised of
 (1) a rotary dynamometer that will drive a VT provided gearbox and generator, (2) one or more
 DC regenerative power supplies that provide input power to and take power from the VT
 supplied power electronics, and (3) a data acquisition system for measurement. NREL will work
 with VT to develop a test plan, set up the test platform and integrate the test article, perform the
 testing, and assist in the data analysis.

 Electrical Set-Up:

 The certain design limit for the power converter should not be exceeded due to safety concerns.
 Specifically, the input voltage should not exceed 566V, and the current should not exceed 107A.
 The temperature of the IGBT module should also be taken into consideration.

 The power electronics device should be assembled into the converter mode, which connects with the
 output from the 3-phase AC generator for measurement. The AC-DC power converter is operated under
 constant input impedance control with no DC bus voltage control. It is connected to an output DC source
 and programmable load to provide constant DC voltage. The DC programmable load and the DC source
 are set on constant voltage mode to emulate a 700 VDC battery.

 4

 Mechanical Set-Up:

 The actuator should be a rotary motor and drive the mechanical system in rotation motion.

 Preferred configuration of the mechanical set-up:

 The preferred set-up includes the bi-directional rotation input from the rotary actuator, the MMR
 gearbox and the generator. The MMR gearbox is driven by the actuator and can rectify the
 motion into uni-directional rotation of the generator. The output of the generator will be directed
 to the power converter for measurement.

 If the MMR gearbox breaks during the test due to unexpected reasons, another configuration
 should be investigated before continuing the test.

 Alternate configuration:

 In this configuration, the MMR gearbox is removed, the input motion towards the generator will
 be simulated so the test can continue.

 7

 Safety

 Since the power converter works with high voltage and large power, the regular electrical
 engineering safety protocols need to be followed. Since the mechanical components can also
 break during the testing, a safety distance and potential protection may be desired for the testing.

 Contingency Plans

 The system may encounter different levels of component failure during testing. If there is a
 mechanical component failure, then a backup test set-up should be investigated. If there is a
 power converter failure, the converter needs to be checked for potential damage and replace the
 broken components if the converter can still perform functionally.

 Data Management, Processing, and Analysis

 Data Management

 The data acquired will be stored on the cloud so it can be acquired through the internet for more
 simple access to everyone. All recorded data, including the reading from both the mechanical
 part and the power electronics part can be shared to the MHK DR in the required format.

 Data Processing

 The acquired data will be analyzed daily and compared against the simulated data from the
 model. Due to testing conditions, there may be some fluctuation of the data, which is acceptable.
 Once it is determined as noise, a numerical filter will be added when processing the data to
 eliminate the influence from these uncertainties.

 Data Analysis

 The data collected from the test will be used for two major purposes; one is to evaluate the
 efficiency of the energy conversion, and the other is to conduct longer term safety calculation for
 the system. Neither will require massive processing of the raw data. However, since there is
 noise coming from all sources of data collection, a numerical filter may be added to the data for
 post processing.

 The final outcome of the data should be the energy conversion efficiency of the system over
 different load conditions, and the head stress of the power converter under different load
 conditions.

 MODIFICATION 1

 This modification adds a new scope of work, funds and time to the agreement.

 Purpose:

 VT, Resolute Marine Energy and Stevens Institute of Technology are designing a 1:2 scale, 100-
 kW FOSWEC. Once this FOSWEC has been designed and built, it will be deployed and
 analyzed at the wave energy test site, PacWave South Site (“PacWave Site”), based at Oregon
 State University. The objective of this TEAMER project is to develop an anchor/mooring
 configuration for this FOSWEC design that is suitable for PacWave Site deployment at a
 reasonable cost.

 Statement of Work:

 There are three objectives with this TEAMER project:

 1) Review the preliminary mooring design configuration.

 2) Build a model coupling the mooring system and the floating platform interactions.

 3) Using the coupled model, perform a parameter optimization considering both technical
 and economic impacts.

 NREL shall complete the following tasks:

 Task 1:

 NREL will determine the anchor specifications for the FOSWEC at the PacWave Site. To
 accomplish this, NREL will acquire the local marine geology conditions (i.e., the seabed
 geological conditions and rock structures) for the PacWave Site. The preliminary anchor design,
 provided by VT, will be reviewed, and modified as necessary, accounting for the local marine
 geology, the desired connection points, and estimates of the maximum mooring loads and
 stresses. This task is estimated to be completed within 0.5 months of the execution of the
 agreement.

 The anchor specifications for the different mooring designs are shown in Tables 2 and 4 below. The
 catenary-chain mooring systems assume drag-embedment anchors (DEAs), which are easy to install,
 relatively non-expensive, but cannot allow for vertical forces on the anchor. This requires a certain
 amount of chain to always remain on the seabed over time as to not produce vertical forces, which would
 decrease the effectiveness of a DEA. The taut-polyester mooring systems assume suction pile anchors,
 which can also be easy to install, can handle both vertical and horizontal mooring loads, but are more
 expensive than DEAs. These anchors allow different styles of mooring systems, such as taut systems with
 large attachment angles, to be used with line types other than chain, which can decrease cost. However, as
 the taut designs increase the mooring line tension on the anchor, the larger the suction piles have to be,
 which will increase mooring system costs.

 10

 For this initial mooring system design exploration for the VT FOSWEC, specific anchor details
 were not investigated. Many mooring system designs were being iterated in quasi-static and
 dynamic design tools, and any other computation time to design specific anchors was deemed
 unnecessary. It was simply assumed that catenary-chain mooring system designs would use
 DEAs and the taut-polyester mooring system designs would use suction piles, and the hardware
 costs of each could be calculated based on the mooring load on the anchor point.

 Ideally, we would use specific anchor design tools (which are currently being developed at
 NREL at the time of this writing), in conjunction with the local seabed conditions and
 bathymetry, to parameterize the anchor specifications for a given mooring system. These tools
 would be able to describe the type, size, geometry, and holding capacity of specific anchors,
 depending on the maximum mooring line loads and the seabed conditions.

 The reference site of the VT FOSWEC was set as the PacWave South Test Site off the coast of
 Oregon, USA. The seabed conditions and bathymetry of the region are publicly available
 (https://tethys.pnnl.gov/project-sites/pacwave-south-test-site), with a map of the seabed
 bathymetry shown in Figure 1. The water depth in the test site ranges from 62-70 meters.

 Figure 1. Seabed bathymetry of the PacWave South test site, which is reference site for the VT
 FOSWEC. The seabed bathymetry and soil conditions should be used as input to the anchor and
 mooring system design process.

 11

 Again, these seabed properties would ideally be used as input to a specific anchor design tool,
 but that level of design detail was initially deemed unnecessary for the amount of mooring
 design iterations being done for the VT FOSWEC. This is also why the order of tasks was
 rearranged, so that the WEC design could be finalized first, then the mooring design, and then
 the anchor design.

 Task 2:

 NREL will finalize the mooring configuration for the FOSWEC at the PacWave Site. VT has
 developed a preliminary, 70m-deep mooring configuration, based on a simplified ANSYS-AQWA
 model of the FOWEC. NREL will review and modify, as necessary, VT’s preliminary mooring
 configuration, considering the anchor specifications and IEC standards (IEC TS 62600-10
 Assessment of Mooring System). This task is estimated to be completed within 1.5 months of the
 execution of the agreement.

 Power response curves were created for a range of different mooring stiffnesses using a linear
 mooring model. This enabled us to identify a target mooring stiffness of 70 kN/m for the
 mooring system (Figure 2).

 • Geometry Parameterization and Mesh Generation

 Python scripts were developed to take descriptions of the FOSWEC geometry (e.g. base
 length, flap width, draft) and convert them into .stl and .nemoh meshes using Gmsh and
 Meshmagick.

 Figure 5. Screen capture of mesh generation with Gmsh.

 • Hydrostatic Properties

 The open-source Python package Meshmagick was used to take the .nemoh mesh files and
 compute properties such as the hydrostatic stiffness matrix, center of buoyancy and shell
 inertia properties, which are necessary for the time-domain simulations. These properties
 were saved in .txt files.

 • Hydrodynamic Coefficient Computation

 The open-source Python package Capytaine was used to compute hydrodynamic
 coefficients (excitation, added mass and radiation damping) for each geometry across a
 range of frequencies. The coefficients were saved in .nc files.

 • Data Conversion

 The MATLAB tool BEMIO was used to convert the .txt and .nc files into .h5 files, making
 them compatible with WEC-Sim.

 • Time-domain simulations with WEC-Sim & MoorDyn

 The WEC-Sim model of the VT FOSWEC was developed with a simple linear mooring
 model (i.e. a linear spring-damper) and with a MoorDyn coupling for a fully nonlinear
 mooring analysis to assess the performance of the system with both catenary and taut
 mooring systems.
