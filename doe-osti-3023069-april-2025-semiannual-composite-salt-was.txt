 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The aqueous waste from the Salt Waste Processing Facility is sampled semiannually for transfers to the
Saltstone Production Facility. Salt solution is treated at the Saltstone Production Facility and disposed of in
the Saltstone Disposal Facility. Per request of customer, X-TTR-Z-00027, Revision 0, one Saltstone
Disposal Facility waste form was prepared at Savannah River National Laboratory using the Salt Waste
Processing Facility Decontaminated Salt Solution and Z-area premix material for the April 2025 semiannual
Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure sample. The sample contained 60:40 (by weight) of slag and
fly ash (referred to as the “Cement-Free grout sample”). Results from the technical report support Task 2:
‘Grout Leaching Analyses’ of the Task Technical Request prepared by Savannah River Mission Completion.
At 43 days cured, the Saltstone sample was collected and shipped to a certified laboratory for analysis using
the Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure. The April 2025 semiannual Cement-Free grout sample met
the South Carolina Code of Regulations for Hazardous Waste Management Regulations 61-79.261.24 and
61-79.268.48 requirements for a non-hazardous waste form with respect to the Resource Conservation and
Recovery Act metals and Underlying Hazardous Constituents, and met the Saltstone Production Facility
Waste Acceptance Criteria that was in effect at the time of the sample collection at the Salt Waste
Processing Facility.

1.0 Introduction
A composite sample from the Salt Waste Processing Facility (SWPF) Decontaminated Salt Solution (DSS)
was used to prepare one Saltstone Disposal Facility (SDF) waste form sample, referred to as a Cement-Free
grout sample1 per request of SWPF & End Stream Delivery (ESD) Engineering (S&E-E). The Cement-Free
sample is representative for 6 months of saltstone processing and is the sample used to determine that
saltstone produced by the Saltstone Production Facility (SPF) is a non-hazardous waste form. The April
2025 semiannual Cement-Free saltstone sample was prepared on June 23, 2025. The sample cured for 43
days, it was crushed, sieved, packaged, and deemed “collected”.2,3 The sample was then shipped to
Southwest Research Institute (SwRI®) to analyze for toxicity per the Toxicity Characteristic Leaching
Procedure (TCLP) method.1,4 The Cement-free saltstone sample determined whether the non-hazardous
nature of the grout meets the requirements of the South Carolina Code of Regulations 61-79.261.245 for
Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA) metals and 61-79.268.486 for inorganic/organic
Underlying Hazardous Constituents (UHCs) (for informational purposes only).1

2.0 Experimental Procedure

2.1 Technical
The April 2025 Semiannual sample of the SWPF DSS is a composite from the six months of SWPF
processing during the First Quarter Fiscal Year 2025 (1QFY2025) and the Second Quarter Fiscal Year 2025
(2QFY2025).7 Saltstone preparation was performed at Savannah River National (SRNL). S&E-E provided
SRNL with the saltstone grout recipe as well as the premix components.1 Table 2-1 shows the premix
components obtained for the last 2024 semiannual TCLP sample including specific LOT numbers and
similar information for the current April 2025 sample. Savannah River Mission Completion (SRMC)
provided SRNL personnel with the premix formulation for the April 2025 semiannual TCLP sample.

The saltstone sample was prepared with a water-to-premix ratio of 0.59 using the mixing method outlined

using the method outlined in SRNL work instructions.9 Material that passed through the 3/8-inch (0.375”)
sieve (9.252 mm) was subsequently screened through a No. 4 sieve (4.76 mm). The material retained on
the No. 4 sieve was packaged in a primary container (250 mL High Density Polyethylene bottle) and
shipped on the same day that it was prepared to SwRI® by the SRNL shipping group.

3.0 Results
Table 3-1 summarizes the analytical results provided by the vendor, SwRI®.13 The first eight rows show
data for the RCRA metals, and the next four rows show data for the UHC metals from the TCLP leachates.
The last four rows show results from solids analyses of the saltstone for benzene, phenol, total and amenable
cyanide. The entire vendor report is documented and included as a reference.13 Some of the data values are
flagged with qualifier letters (U, D, J, B) that are shown as footnotes to the table. Further explanation for
these qualifiers can be found in the vendor report. For comparison to the current Cement-Free results, the
previous quarter and four previous quarter average results for the Cement-Free sample are shown in the
final two columns. The four-quarter average values contain a qualifier (*, +, ^) if past values have been
reported as a non-detectable analyte (U’). The Regulatory Toxicity5 values and the Waste Acceptance
Criteria (WAC) Limits are from Table 7 of the WAC14 and reflect the requirements in the applicable version
of the document. Note that the vendor used a “modified” Method 1311 where sample mass was restricted
due to the elevated activity of the sample. This methodology is consistent with the joint guidance from the
Nuclear Regulatory Commission and Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) for mixed radioactive and
hazardous waste. For the April 2025 semiannual saltstone sample, the TCLP extraction was performed on
the Cement-Free sample using extraction fluid #2. Extraction fluid #1 is an aqueous acetic acid solution
with pH= 4.93 ± 0.05 prepared by diluting 5.7 mL of glacial acetic acid into 500 mL of reagent water, then
adding 64.3 mL of sodium hydroxide and diluting with reagent water for a total volume of 1 Liter.4
Extraction fluid #2 is an aqueous acetic acid solution with pH= 2.88 ± 0.05 prepared by diluting 5.7 mL of
glacial acetic acid into reagent water for a total volume of 1 Liter. Determination of the extract fluid used
in the TCLP uses Section 7.1.4 ‘Determination of Appropriate Extraction Fluid’ of the TCLP method.4 A
size reduced portion of the solid is mixed with reagent water and stirred for 5 minutes. If the resulting pH
of the contacted water is below pH 5.0, then extraction fluid #1 is used. However, if the resulting pH is
above 5.0, then an additional step using HCl and heating to 50°C for 10 minutes is performed. If the pH of
the cooled solution is below 5.0, then extraction fluid #1 is used and if the pH is above 5.0, then extraction
fluid #2 is used. The sample resulted in an initial pH above 5.0 of the contacted water and a second pH
above 5.0. Therefore, extraction fluid #2 was used. The final extract pH value resulting from the TCLP
extraction using fluid #2 showed a pH of 6.55 for the Cement-Free Sample.13

4.0 Conclusions
Analyses of the SDF Cement-Free waste form prepared from the April 2025 semiannual SWPF DSS sample
and premix material resulted in the following findings.
  The RCRA metal TCLP result concentrations met the South Carolina Code of Regulations 61-
 79.261.24 requirements for a nonhazardous waste form.5
  The measured concentrations of the TCLP RCRA metals and additional inorganic/organic UHCs
 met the South Carolina Code of Regulations 61-79.268.48 non-wastewater standards.6
  The measured concentrations of the TCLP RCRA metals met the SPF WAC.14
