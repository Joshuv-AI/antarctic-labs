Bayesian Best-Arm Identification with Abstention: A Polynomial-to-Exponential Phase Transition
Yuqi Huang, Yunlong Hou, Vincent Y. F. Tan

Abstract
We study the Bayesian fixed-budget best-arm identification problem in which a learner can abstain from making a terminal recommendation. Subject to an abstention budget α \alpha , we analyze the probability of undetected error–the risk of recommending a suboptimal arm without abstaining. Our central finding is that abstention induces a phase transition: without abstention, the error probability decays polynomially in the sampling budget T T ; in contrast, introducing any small positive abstention budget shifts this to an exponential decay. For Gaussian priors and rewards, in the regime T → ∞ T\to\infty followed by α ↓ 0 \alpha\downarrow 0 , we establish exact matching information-theoretic lower bounds and algorithmic upper bounds on the optimal error exponent, which takes the form exp ⁡ ( − α 2 ​ T 8 ​ κ ν 2 ) \exp(-\frac{\alpha^{2}T}{8\kappa_{\nu}^{2}}) . The hardness parameter κ ν \kappa_{\nu} represents the prior density of the top-two gap at zero, highlighting that nearly tied instances drive the fundamental error. We introduce an adaptive algorithm, PGWS , that successfully achieves this optimal exponent by expending its abstention budget on statistically ambiguous instances. We further demonstrate that this polynomial-to-exponential improvement is exclusively a Bayesian phenomenon–in the frequentist setting, abstention only affects lower-order exponent terms. We also extend our results beyond the Gaussian model.



1
Introduction

1.1
Problem Overview and Motivation

Fixed-Budget Best-Arm Identification
(FB-BAI)
(
3
;
16
;
6
)
is a canonical pure exploration problem in
sequential decision making. A learner is given a sampling budget, adaptively
collects noisy observations from a set of
K
arms with unknown mean rewards, and
finally recommends the arm believed to have the largest mean. This formulation
captures the statistical core of ranking and selection,
online experimentation, and treatment-selection problems, where samples are
costly and the objective is to make a reliable terminal recommendation rather
than to maximize reward during the experiment
(
3
;
17
;
26
)
.

Typical FB-BAI formulations require a firm terminal recommendation after the
sampling budget is exhausted. In many applications, however, this forced decision, in which the decision maker commits to one of the
K
arms, may pose undue risks. When the collected evidence is ambiguous, the decision maker may
defer the decision, run a follow-up experiment, escalate the case to an expert,
or choose a conservative default action. This motivates a FB-BAI model
with terminal
abstention
option: after sampling, the learner may either recommend an arm
or output an inconclusive decision.

Abstention changes the notion of error. A policy that always abstains
never makes an incorrect recommendation, but it is not useful. We therefore
treat abstention as a scarce resource: the learner must keep the probability of
abstention below a prescribed level
\alpha
. Subject to this constraint, the
performance criterion is the probability of an
undetected error
, namely the probability that the learner does not abstain but recommends a suboptimal arm. Informally, let
a^{\star}
denote the best arm,
?
be the abstention decision, and

\widehat{a}\in[K]\cup\{?\}
be the terminal output. The objective is to minimize

\mathbb{P}\big(\widehat{a}\notin\{a^{\star},?\}\big),\quad\textrm{subject to}\quad\mathbb{P}(\widehat{a}=?)\leq\alpha.

(1.1)

This paper studies the problem in a Bayesian fixed-budget setting, where the
unknown reward means are drawn from known priors. The Bayesian formulation is
natural when the same experimental protocol is repeatedly applied to related
instances, or when historical data provide a prior model for plausible reward
configurations. It is also the setting in which the effect of abstention is particularly
pronounced. Under a continuous prior, the Bayes error probability of forced-decision (i.e., without abstention) FB-BAI is
dominated by instances in which the two best arms are nearly tied. These instances
are rare, but they are also the hardest to resolve with a finite sampling budget.

The central observation of this paper is that a small abstention budget allows the learner to spend its inconclusive decisions precisely on these statistically ambiguous near-tie cases. We show that this fundamentally results in a phase transition of the behavior of Bayesian
BAI: even a small positive abstention budget substantially reduces the forced-decision

polynomially
small Bayes error probability into an
exponentially
small Bayes undetected error probability.
We derive this exponent and show that it is governed by the amount of prior probability near top-two ties.
The rest of the paper formalizes this principle, develops an adaptive algorithm
that attains the optimal exponent, and contrasts the Bayesian phenomenon with the frequentist setting.

1.2
Problem Formulation and Top-Two Gap Complexity

We now introduce the formal model. For an integer
K\geq 2
, let
[K]:=\{1,\ldots,K\}
. The learner is given a fixed sampling budget
T
and a collection of
K
arms.
Arm
i\in[K]
has an unknown mean reward
\mu_{i}
. We study a Bayesian model in which the mean vector
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{K})
is drawn from a known product Gaussian prior

P_{\nu}(\mathrm{d}\mu):=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{K}\mathcal{N}(\nu_{i},\sigma_{0}^{2})(\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}),

where the prior means
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{K}\in\mathbb{R}
and prior variance (which is common to all arms)

\sigma_{0}^{2}>0
are fixed and known.
We define
the best arm

a^{\star}=a^{\star}(\mu):=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{i\in[K]}\mu_{i}
which is unique almost surely since the prior
P_{\nu}
is continuous.
Conditional on
\mu
, the rewards from the arms are independent (across arms and time) and normally distributed:

Y_{i,s}\mid\mu_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{i},1),\qquad s\in\mathbb{N},~i\in[K].

Policies and Probability Laws.

A
policy

\pi
consists of a sequence of sampling rules and a terminal
decision rule. At each round
t=1,\ldots,T
, the policy selects an arm
A_{t}\in[K]
. Conditional on
A_{t}=i
and the mean vector
\mu
, the learner observes a random sample
X_{t}=Y_{i,N_{i}(t)}
where
N_{i}(t):=\sum_{s=1}^{t}\mathbbm{1}\{A_{s}=i\}

denotes the number of samples collected from arm
i
up to and including time
t
. The choice of the arm to be pulled
A_{t}
depends on the previous arm choices
\{A_{s}\}_{s=1}^{t-1}
and observations
\{X_{s}\}_{s=1}^{t-1}
. At the terminal time
T
, the policy recommends an arm from
[K]
as the estimated best arm or an abstention decision denoted as
?
. The class of all such policies is denoted as
\Pi_{T}
.

For a fixed mean vector
\mu
, we write
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\pi}
and

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}^{\pi}
for the probability and expectation over the reward
realizations and the potential randomization of the policy. The joint Bayesian law is the
mixture

\mathbb{P}_{\nu}^{\pi}(\cdot):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{K}}\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\pi}(\cdot)\,P_{\nu}(\mathrm{d}\mu),

and
\mathbb{E}_{\nu}^{\pi}
is defined analogously. When the policy (resp. probability law) is clear from context, we suppress the superscript
\pi
(resp. subscript
\nu
).

Terminal Decisions with Abstention.

At the end of the experiment, the learner outputs
\widehat{a}_{T}\in[K]\cup\{?\}
,
where
?
denotes abstention, an inconclusive decision. The learner makes an

undetected error
if it recommends an arm and the recommended arm is not
the true best arm, i.e., the event

\big\{\widehat{a}_{T}\notin\{a^{\star},?\}\big\}=\big\{\widehat{a}_{T}\in[K],\ \widehat{a}_{T}\neq a^{\star}\big\}
occurs.

For a policy
\pi\in\Pi_{T}
, define its
Bayes abstention probability
and
Bayes undetected error probability
respectively as

\mathcal{A}_{T}(\pi):=\mathbb{P}_{\nu}^{\pi}(\widehat{a}_{T}=?)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{T}(\pi):=\mathbb{P}_{\nu}^{\pi}\bigl(\widehat{a}_{T}\in[K],\ \widehat{a}_{T}\neq a^{\star}(\mu)\bigr).

Given an abstention budget
\alpha\in(0,1)
, let

\Pi_{T}(\alpha):=\{\pi\in\Pi_{T}:\mathcal{A}_{T}(\pi)\leq\alpha\}.

The
optimal Bayes undetected error probability
is

\mathfrak{E}_{T}(\alpha):=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{T}(\alpha)}\mathcal{E}_{T}(\pi).

(1.2)

For later comparison, we also define the
optimal forced-decision Bayes probability of
misidentification
(in which abstention is not allowed) as

\mathfrak{E}_{T}(0):=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{T}:\,\widehat{a}_{T}\in[K]\ {\rm a.s.}}\mathbb{P}_{\nu}^{\pi}(\widehat{a}_{T}\neq a^{\star})=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi_{T}(0)}\mathbb{P}_{\nu}^{\pi}(\widehat{a}_{T}\neq a^{\star})\ .

(1.3)

Top-Two Gap Complexity.

We define the
top-two gap

\Gamma:=\mu_{(1)}-\mu_{(2)},

(1.4)

where
\mu_{(1)}>\mu_{(2)}>\cdots>\mu_{(K)}
denote the means of the arms arranged in decreasing order. Theorems
2.1
and
3.1
identify the density of
\Gamma
at zero as the fundamental hardness parameter for the Bayesian BAI problem with abstention.

For each arm
i
, we denote the prior density and distribution function of
\mu_{i}
as

f_{i}(x):=\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}}\phi\left(\frac{x-\nu_{i}}{\sigma_{0}}\right),\quad\mbox{and}\quad F_{i}(x):=\Phi\left(\frac{x-\nu_{i}}{\sigma_{0}}\right),

where
\phi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}
and
\Phi(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\phi(u)\,\mathrm{d}u
are the standard normal density and cumulative distribution function, respectively.

For a pair of distinct arms
(i,j)\in[K]^{2}
, define their
average midpoint variable
as

X_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(\frac{\nu_{i}+\nu_{j}}{2},\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{2}\right)
,
and define the
top-relevance weight

w_{ij}:=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\prod_{k\neq i,j}\Phi\!\left(\frac{X_{ij}-\nu_{k}}{\sigma_{0}}\right)\right]
.
The quantity
w_{ij}
is the prior probability, averaged over a local tie value
of arms
i
and
j
, that this tied value lies above all remaining arms.
Define the
hardness parameter
for Bayesian BAI with abstention problem as

\kappa_{\nu}=\sum_{i\neq j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{i}(x)f_{j}(x)\prod_{k\neq i,j}F_{k}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq K}w_{ij}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(\nu_{i}-\nu_{j})^{2}}{4\sigma_{0}^{2}}\right).

In Lemma
A.1
, we show that the top-two gap
\Gamma
is related to the hardness parameter
\kappa_{\nu}
as follows:

\mathbb{P}_{\nu}(\Gamma\leq\varepsilon)=\kappa_{\nu}\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\downarrow 0.

(1.5)

In view of (
1.5
), we see that
\kappa_{\nu}
measures the amount of
prior probability assigned to locally ambiguous instances.
Specifically, pairs with similar prior means and a realistic chance of being the top two arms contribute more to
\kappa_{\nu}
, whereas pairs that are unlikely to be optimal contribute little.

1.3
Main Contributions and Results

We formulate the fixed-budget BAI with abstention problem under the Bayesian setting.
We study this problem under the large-budget and small-abstention probability regime through the iterated limit
T\to\infty
followed by
\alpha\downarrow 0
.

Our main result is a sharp characterization of the exponential rate of decay of the optimal Bayes
undetected error probability under abstention. In the Gaussian
prior and Gaussian reward model, we prove the information-theoretic lower bound

\liminf_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathfrak{E}_{T}(\alpha)\geq-\frac{1}{8\kappa_{\nu}^{2}}.

We also propose an adaptive algorithm,
Posterior Gap Weighted Sampling with Abstention
(
PGWS
(
\alpha
)), whose
abstention probability is calibrated exactly to
\alpha
and whose
undetected error probability
\mathcal{E}_{T}({\textnormal{{PGWS}}}(\alpha))
satisfies

\limsup_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathcal{E}_{T}({\textnormal{{PGWS}}}(\alpha))\leq-\frac{1}{8\kappa_{\nu}^{2}}.

Combining these results, we see that the fundamental hardness of the Bayesian BAI with abstention problem depends on the prior through
\kappa_{\nu}
, the density of top-two gap at zero. In other words, the complexity is intimately related to the prior probability of instances in which the means of two best arms are nearly the same.

Subsequently, we compare the abstention problem with ordinary (forced-decision) Bayesian BAI, where the learner is compelled to recommend an arm. We establish that the optimal forced-decision Bayes probability of misidentification satisfies

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\sqrt{T}\,\mathfrak{E}_{T}(0)\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,\kappa_{\nu}.

Therefore, allowing an
\alpha
fraction of abstentions transits the Bayes probability of error from being on the
polynomial scale to the exponential scale.

We further demonstrate that this phase transition is inherently Bayesian. In contrast, for any fixed frequentist instance with a positive gap, the error probability of conventional forced-decision FB-BAI already exhibits exponential decay in
T
; see, e.g.,
16
. As a result, terminal abstention cannot improve the leading error exponent and affects only lower-order terms.

Finally, the analysis is extended beyond the Gaussian model. For regular
one-parameter reward models, the same exponent takes the form

1/(8\kappa^{2})
, where
\kappa
is the top-two gap density evaluated in Fisher–Rao information coordinates; see Eqn. (
4.1
).

We also provide numerical experiments illustrating the benefit of abstention and the practical advantage of our adaptive algorithm which we call
PGWS
.

1.4
Related work

Best-arm Identification and Ranking and Selection.

Best-arm identification is a central pure-exploration problem in multi-armed
bandits. In the fixed-confidence setting, the objective is to identify the best
arm with prescribed confidence using as few samples as possible

(
20
;
10
;
17
)
. In the fixed-budget setting,
which is our focus, the learner is given a sampling budget and aims to minimize
the probability of an incorrect terminal recommendation

(
3
;
16
;
6
;
19
;
29
)
. For a fixed instance with a positive gap,
this probability typically decays exponentially in the budget, with the exponent
determined by instance-dependent complexity. Our work instead studies a Bayesian
average over instances and allows terminal abstention. The problem is also
related to ranking and selection and ordinal optimization, where the objective is
often to maximize the probability of correct selection under a simulation budget

(
7
;
12
;
14
)
. In contrast to most of
that literature, our performance criterion is an abstention-constrained Bayes undetected error probability.

Bayesian Best Arm Identification.

Bayesian BAI involves a prior distribution over the unknown reward means. Bayesian
algorithms such as Thompson sampling and top-two Thompson sampling have been
studied for posterior convergence and BAI

(
26
;
27
)
. Recent work develops prior-sensitive
guarantees for Bayesian BAI:
2
study Gaussian fixed-budget Bayesian BAI, while
18
characterize Bayesian simple-regret rates under continuity assumptions and identify near-tie instances as the leading source of error. Related work also studies prior-dependent
allocations in structured Bayesian fixed-budget BAI

(
24
)
and fixed-confidence BAI in the Bayesian setting

(
15
)
. Our contribution is to add a
terminal abstention option and provide a full characterization of the undetected error exponent
under an abstention budget.

Abstention and Reject-Option Decisions.

Abstention has a long history in statistical decision theory and classification.
Classical reject-option rules trade prediction error against rejection
(
8
)
, and selective prediction studies risk-coverage tradeoffs in supervised learning
(
4
;
9
)
.
These settings are typically passive, whereas our learner actively allocates a fixed sampling budget before deciding whether to recommend or abstain. Bandit models with abstention-like actions include selective sampling for online BAI
(
5
)
and regret minimization with per-round abstention
(
30
)
. In contrast, our model permits abstention only at the terminal recommendation stage.

Erasure Decoding and Undetected Error.

An essential motivation for considering this setup arises from erasure decoding in information theory. In
channel coding with an erasure option, the decoder may either output a message or declare an erasure; performance is then measured by the tradeoff between the total and undetected error probabilities.
This line of work dates back to Forney’s exponential bounds for erasure, list, and decision-feedback decoding
(
11
)
, with later refinements for erasure/list exponents,
exact random-coding exponents, and asymmetric erasure-error regimes

(
23
;
28
;
13
)
. Our
abstention option plays an analogous role to erasure, but the BAI setting is an
active sequential experiment, in contrast to the non-sequential nature of channel coding (without feedback).

Algorithm and Error Analysis

2.1
The Algorithm
PGWS

We now present
Posterior Gap Weighted Sampling with Abstention
(
PGWS
). This policy has two components, a sampling rule and a terminal decision rule. During the sampling phase,
it maintains Gaussian posterior distributions of the arm means and
samples arms according to posterior mean gaps. At the terminal time, it either
recommends the posterior mean leader or abstains, with the abstention rule
calibrated to the prescribed budget
\alpha
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\sigma(A_{1},X_{1},\ldots,A_{t},X_{t})
denote the history up to time
t
, with
\mathcal{F}_{0}:=\{\varnothing,\Omega\}
, augmented to include any external randomization used by the policy. Under the Gaussian prior and
Gaussian reward model, the posterior distributions after
t
samples are
independent across arms:

\mu_{i}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}\big(M_{i}(t),V_{i}(t)\big),\qquad i\in[K],

where the posterior variance
V_{i}(t)
and mean
M_{i}(t)
are, respectively,

V_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}^{-2}+N_{i}(t)},\quad\mbox{and}\quad M_{i}(t)=V_{i}(t)\left(\frac{\nu_{i}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}+\sum_{s=1}^{N_{i}(t)}Y_{i,s}\right).

(2.1)

At each time
t
, define the
posterior mean leader

\widehat{b}_{t}:=\arg\max_{i\in[K]}M_{i}(t)
.
For
i\neq\widehat{b}_{t}
, define the

posterior mean gap

\widehat{\Delta}_{i}(t):=M_{\widehat{b}_{t}}(t)-M_{i}(t),

with
\widehat{\Delta}_{\widehat{b}_{t}}(t):=\min_{j\neq\widehat{b}_{t}}\widehat{\Delta}_{j}(t)
.

Algorithm 1

Posterior Gap Weighted Sampling with Abstention
(
PGWS
(\alpha)
)

1:

Budget
T
, abstention level
\alpha
, prior parameters

(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{K})
and
\sigma_{0}^{2}
.

2:

Initialize
N_{i}(0)=0
,
M_{i}(0)=\nu_{i}
, and

V_{i}(0)=\sigma_{0}^{2}
for all
i\in[K]
.

3:

for

t=0,1,\ldots,T-1

do

4:

if
there exists
i\in[K]
such that
N_{i}(t)<\sqrt{t+1}

then

5:

Sample one such arm, using deterministic tie-breaking.

6:

else

7:

Compute
\widehat{b}_{t}
, the gaps

\{\widehat{\Delta}_{i}(t)\}_{i\in[K]}
, and the sampling probabilities

\{p_{i}(t)\}_{i\in[K]}
in (
2.2
).

8:

Sample arm
i
with probability
p_{i}(t)
.

9:

end

if

10:

Observe the reward and update
N_{i}(t+1)
,
M_{i}(t+1)
, and

V_{i}(t+1)
according to (
2.1
).

11:

end

for

12:

Set
\widehat{b}_{T}=\arg\max_{i\in[K]}M_{i}(T)
.

13:

Compute the terminal statistic
R_{T}
in
(
2.3
).

14:

Output
\widehat{a}_{T}
according to the abstention rule
(
2.6
).

The forced-exploration step in Lines 3–4 ensures that every arm is
sufficiently sampled to yield an accurate estimate of the mean.
On all
remaining rounds,

PGWS
samples arm
i
with
probability

p_{i}(t)=\frac{\widehat{\Delta}_{i}(t)^{-2}}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}\widehat{\Delta}_{\ell}(t)^{-2}},\qquad i\in[K].

(2.2)

Consequently, the leader and the closest
challenger receive equal sampling probability, while arms with larger
posterior mean gaps receive smaller probabilities.

At the end of the sampling phase,
PGWS
measures the strength of the
posterior evidence in favor of the final leader
\widehat{b}_{T}
. Define

R_{T}:=\min_{j\neq\widehat{b}_{T}}\frac{\bigl(M_{\widehat{b}_{T}}(T)-M_{j}(T)\bigr)^{2}}{2\bigl(V_{\widehat{b}_{T}}(T)+V_{j}(T)\bigr)},

(2.3)

which represents the smallest pairwise posterior Gaussian exponent
separating the final leader from its challengers. A small value of
R_{T}

indicates that at least one challenger remains statistically difficult
to be distinguished from the leader.

To calibrate abstention, let
r_{T,\alpha}
be the lower

\alpha
-quantile of
R_{T}
under the joint Bayesian law induced by the
sampling rule, i.e.,

r_{T,\alpha}:=\inf\bigl\{r\geq 0:\mathbb{P}(R_{T}\leq r)\geq\alpha\bigr\}.

(2.4)

If the distribution of
R_{T}
has an atom at the
threshold, define

\theta_{T,\alpha}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\alpha-\mathbb{P}(R_{T}<r_{T,\alpha})}{\mathbb{P}(R_{T}=r_{T,\alpha})},&\mathbb{P}(R_{T}=r_{T,\alpha})>0,\\
0,&\mathbb{P}(R_{T}=r_{T,\alpha})=0.\end{cases}

(2.5)

The terminal decision is

\widehat{a}_{T}=\begin{cases}?,&R_{T}<r_{T,\alpha},\\
?,&R_{T}=r_{T,\alpha}\text{ with probability }\theta_{T,\alpha},\\
\widehat{b}_{T},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.6)

Thus
PGWS
abstains on the least confident posterior histories, up to boundary randomization which is used only to obtain

exact
calibration when
R_{T}
has an atom at its
\alpha
-quantile.

Remark 2.1

(Computing the quantile)
.

The quantile
r_{T,\alpha}
is deterministic once the prior, the sampling
rule,
T
, and
\alpha
are fixed. For the purpose of implementation, it can be estimated
offline by Monte Carlo simulation from the prior. A
large-
T
, small-
\alpha
approximation is

r_{T,\alpha}^{\mathrm{asy}}=\frac{1}{8\kappa_{\nu}^{2}}\alpha^{2}T.

Upper Bound Analysis

We now derive theoretical guarantees for
PGWS
. In
this subsection, all probabilities are under the joint Bayesian law induced by
Algorithm
1
.

Theorem 2.1

(Upper Bound for
PGWS
)
.

For any
T
and any
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
PGWS
(
\alpha
) with the terminal rule (

) satisfies

\mathcal{A}_{T}({\textnormal{{PGWS}}}(\alpha))=\alpha

Furthermore, asymptotically,

\limsup_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathcal{E}_{T}({\textnormal{{PGWS}}}(\alpha))\leq-\frac{1}{8\kappa_{\nu}^{2}}.

(2.7)

The proof uses two key lemmas. In particular, the first lemma gives a finite-sample posterior

R_{T}

controls the posterior probability that the final leader is not the true best arm.

Lemma 2.1

(Posterior Certification Error Bound)
.

For every
T
and every
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\mathcal{E}_{T}({\textnormal{{PGWS}}}(\alpha))\leq(K-1)e^{-r_{T,\alpha}}.

Lemma 2.2

(Asymptotic Lower Bound on the Calibration Threshold)
.

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
\alpha_{0}>0
such that for every

0<\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}
, there exists

The proofs of both Lemmas are deferred to
Appendix

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

By the definition of the lower
\alpha
-quantile,

\mathbb{P}(R_{T}<r_{T,\alpha})\leq\alpha\leq\mathbb{P}(R_{T}\leq r_{T,\alpha}).

The boundary randomization in (

) is chosen so that

\mathbb{P}(\widehat{a}_{T}=?)=\mathbb{P}(R_{T}<r_{T,\alpha})+\theta_{T,\alpha}\mathbb{P}(R_{T}=r_{T,\alpha})=\alpha.

Thus
\mathcal{A}_{T}({\textnormal{{PGWS}}}(\alpha))=\alpha
.

For the error probability, Lemma

\mathcal{E}_{T}({\textnormal{{PGWS}}}(\alpha))\leq(K-1)e^{-r_{T,\alpha}}.

By Lemma

\varepsilon\in(0,1)

r_{T,\alpha}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\frac{1}{8\kappa_{\nu}^{2}}\alpha^{2}T.

Therefore

\mathcal{E}_{T}({\textnormal{{PGWS}}}(\alpha))\leq(K-1)\exp\left\{-(1-\varepsilon)\frac{1}{8\kappa_{\nu}^{2}}\alpha^{2}T\right\}.

This then yields the final result in (

3
Lower Bounds

This section presents three lower bounds. The first is the main impossibility result for
Bayesian BAI with abstention and matches the upper bound presented in
Section
2.2
. The second
shows that forced-decision Bayesian BAI has only polynomial decay in the error probability, which refines the lower bound established by
2
. The third considers the
frequentist (non-Bayesian) formulation and shows that, when the instance has a positive gap,
abstention improves only lower-order exponential terms. Together, these results
identify both the value of abstention and the role of the Bayesian formulation.

3.1
Bayesian BAI with Abstention

Theorem 3.1

(Lower Bound for Bayesian BAI with Abstention)
.

In the Gaussian prior and Gaussian reward model,

\liminf_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathfrak{E}_{T}(\alpha)\geq-\frac{1}{8\kappa_{\nu}^{2}}.

Theorem
3.1
applies to every adaptive sampling
and terminal decision rule satisfying the abstention constraint

\mathcal{A}_{T}(\pi)\leq\alpha
.
The proof, which is deferred to Appendix
C
, proceeds by decomposing the parameter space into disjoint regions
\Omega_{ij}
where arms
i
and
j
are the two arms with the largest means. Since the undetected error is dominated by near-tie instances, we focus on small neighborhoods of the tie surfaces inside these regions and introduce a locally flat subprior that is a submeasure of the original prior. This reduction transforms the problem into a two-arm hypothesis testing problem under a convenient approximately uniform prior. Combining a Neyman–Pearson-like argument with the aggregation over all pairs yields the exponent
1/(8\kappa_{\nu}^{2})
that matches that of the upper bound.

Combining Theorem
3.1
with the achievability
guarantee for
PGWS
gives the tight asymptotics on the exponential scale:

Corollary 3.2

(Tight Asymptotics and Optimality of
PGWS
)
.

The following holds:

\limsup_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathcal{E}_{T}({\textnormal{{PGWS}}}(\alpha))=\liminf_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathfrak{E}_{T}(\alpha)=-\frac{1}{8\kappa_{\nu}^{2}}.

(3.1)

Corollary
3.2
identifies
\kappa_{\nu}
, the density at zero of the prior top-two gap
\Gamma
,
as the
prior-dependent hardness parameter on the leading exponential scale.

3.2
Bayesian BAI without Abstention

To characterize the role of the abstention formulation, we next consider the forced-decision Bayesian BAI problem
(
2
)
, in which the learner must recommend
an arm at the end of the experiment. Recall that we defined the forced-decision Bayes error probability as in (
1.3
).
The following theorem shows that, without abstention, the Bayes probability of
misidentification cannot decay exponentially fast.

Theorem 3.3

(No-Abstention Lower Bound)
.

For the Gaussian prior and Gaussian reward model,

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\sqrt{T}\,\mathfrak{E}_{T}(0)\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,\kappa_{\nu}.

The proof is deferred to Appendix
D
.
Theorem
3.3
refines the two-arm Bayesian
fixed-budget BAI lower bound of
2
. They show that,
for
K=2
Gaussian arms with Gaussian priors, the Bayes probability of
misidentification is
\Omega((T\log T)^{-1/2})
, with the multiplicative factor depending on the prior as
\exp\big\{\!-\!\frac{(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})^{2}}{4\sigma_{0}^{2}}\big\}
.
For
K=2
, our hardness parameter is

\kappa_{\nu}=2\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}\sqrt{\pi}}\exp\big\{\!-\!\frac{(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})^{2}}{4\sigma_{0}^{2}}\big\}
.
Thus Theorem
3.3
improves the dependence on
T
, recovers the same prior-separation
dependence through
\kappa_{\nu}
, and extends the
result from two arms to general
K\geq 2
.
For
K>2
,
\kappa_{\nu}
represents a weighted sum (with weights
\{w_{ij}\}
) over all distinct pairs of arms
(i,j)
of the likelihood of
i
and
j
being equal.
Additionally, this theorem is also materially different from the lower bound analysis of the Bayesian simple regret in
18
.
That work studies the
simple regret
, rather than the probability of error, and establishes a decay rate of
\Theta(T^{-1})
.

Theorem
3.3
identifies the mechanism behind the
polynomial Bayes error probability in standard forced-decision Bayesian FB-BAI. The dominant error comes
from instances whose top-two gap,
\Gamma=\mu_{(1)}-\mu_{(2)}
, is of order
T^{-1/2}
. The prior probability of these instances is of order
\kappa_{\nu}/\sqrt{T}
, and on these instances, the two leading arms cannot be reliably separated with
T
samples. Thus the same local near-tie density
\kappa_{\nu}
that governs
the abstention exponent also determines the constant in
the forced-decision problem.

This contrasts with the results for Bayesian BAI with abstention in Theorem
3.1
. By allowing an
\alpha
-fraction of inconclusive decisions on the most ambiguous posterior
histories, the learner removes the near-tie region that dominates the
forced-decision error probability.
Consequently, the error probability exhibits a fundamental phase transition from
polynomial decay (i.e.,
\Omega(T^{-1/2})
)
to exponential decay (i.e.,
\exp(-\Theta(\alpha^{2}T))
).

3.3
Frequentist BAI with Abstention

The preceding results concern the Bayesian formulation, where the error probability is
averaged over a prior.
We now contrast this behavior with the frequentist (fixed-instance) setting, and we show that the polynomial-to-exponential improvement is specific to the Bayesian formulation.
In particular, for a fixed positive-gap instance, the error probability of ordinary frequentist FB-BAI decays exponentially with
T

(
3
;
6
;
17
)
.
We show that the terminal abstention can improve only lower-order (i.e., sublinear in
T
)
terms in the exponent.

We exemplify this point with a symmetric pair of two-arm Gaussian instances which we call the
mirror
experiments
. Pulling arm
i\in\{1,2\}
produces an independent reward from

\mathcal{N}(\mu_{i},1)
. A strategy
\pi
adaptively samples the arms for
T
rounds
and then outputs
\widehat{a}_{T}\in\{1,2,?\}
,
where
?
denotes abstention. For an instance
\mu
, let

a^{\star}(\mu)=\arg\max_{i\in\{1,2\}}\mu_{i}
be the optimal arm, and
define

\mathcal{E}_{T,\mu}(\pi):=\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\pi}\!\left(\widehat{a}_{T}\notin\{a^{\star}(\mu),?\}\right),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{A}_{T,\mu}(\pi):=\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\pi}(\widehat{a}_{T}=?).

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, let

\Pi_{T}^{\rm freq}(\alpha):=\left\{\pi:\sup_{\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{A}_{T,\mu}(\pi)\leq\alpha\right\}

be the class of strategies with abstention probability uniformly bounded above by
\alpha
.
Let
z_{\alpha}:=\Phi^{-1}\!\left(\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\right)
,
so that
\mathbb{P}(|Z|\leq z_{\alpha})=\alpha
for

Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\Delta>0
, and consider the following two
mirror instances

\mu^{+}:=\left(x+\frac{\Delta}{2},x-\frac{\Delta}{2}\right),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mu^{-}:=\left(x-\frac{\Delta}{2},x+\frac{\Delta}{2}\right).

Under
\mu^{+}
, arm
1
is optimal; under
\mu^{-}
, arm
2
is optimal.

Theorem 3.4

(Two-Arm Frequentist Lower Bound with Abstention)
.

Given
T\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\Delta>0
, and any strategy

\pi\in\Pi_{T}^{\rm freq}(\alpha)
, we have

\max\left\{\mathcal{E}_{T,\mu^{+}}(\pi),\mathcal{E}_{T,\mu^{-}}(\pi)\right\}\geq\Phi\!\left(-\frac{\Delta\sqrt{T}}{2}-z_{\alpha}\right).

The proof is deferred to Appendix
E
. The theorem is a minimax statement over the mirror instances
(\mu^{+},\mu^{-})
: any
strategy whose abstention probability is uniformly bounded by
\alpha

necessarily incurs the stated undetected error on at least one of the two instances.

This bound is sharp for the two-arm Gaussian mirror experiment: when
T
is even, sampling
each arm
T/2
times and abstaining when
|\widehat{\mu}_{1}-\widehat{\mu}_{2}|\leq\frac{2z_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{T}}

attains the lower bound.

Theorem
3.4
shows that abstention does not
change the leading fixed-gap exponential rate. Without abstention, the policy that pulls each arm an equal number of times results in the error probability

\Phi\!\left(-\frac{\Delta\sqrt{T}}{2}\right)=\exp\!\left\{-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{8}T+O(\log T)\right\}.

With an abstention budget
\alpha
, the undetected error probability becomes

\Phi\!\left(-\frac{\Delta\sqrt{T}}{2}-z_{\alpha}\right)=\exp\!\left\{-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{8}T-\frac{\Delta z_{\alpha}}{2}\sqrt{T}-\frac{z_{\alpha}^{2}}{2}+O(\log T)\right\}.

Hence, abstention only improves the exponent by a lower order term. For small
\alpha
,

z_{\alpha}=\sqrt{\pi/2}\,\alpha+O(\alpha^{3})
, so the logarithmic improvement is
of order
O(\alpha\sqrt{T})
.

In contrast to the Bayesian formulation, the error probability under the frequentist setup is already decaying exponentially without abstention, and
the additional option to abstain yields only a lower-order improvement in the error probability, characterized by a moderate-deviation term.

4
Extensions Beyond the Gaussian Model

In the preceding sections, we established the polynomial-to-exponential phase transition under the Gaussian model. In this section, we show that this transition extends beyond the Gaussian setting.
Specifically, the same exponent arises for regular one-parameter reward models after measuring
gaps in the Fisher–Rao information scale.

Let
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an open interval, called the
quality parameter space
. At the beginning of each experiment, the
quality parameters

\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{K}\in\mathcal{I}
are drawn from their prior distributions and remain fixed throughout the experiment. The best arm is
a^{\star}:=\arg\max_{i\in[K]}\mu_{i}
.
Conditional on
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{K}
, each pull of arm
i
produces an independent reward with distribution
P_{\mu_{i}}
. We assume that each
P_{\mu_{i}}
comes from a regular one-parameter exponential family, defined as follows.

Assumption 4.1

(Regular One-Parameter Exponential Family)
.

The family
\{P_{\mu}:\mu\in\mathcal{I}\}
is a regular full
one-parameter exponential family, possibly after a smooth monotone
reparameterization (which we call
\theta(\cdot)
); see, e.g.,
(
20
, Sec. 34.3.1)
. Specifically,
there exists an open natural parameter space

\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, a dominating measure
\lambda
, a natural
statistic
t(\cdot)
, a base distribution
h(\cdot)
, a
C^{4}
log-partition function

A:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}
with
A^{\prime\prime}(\theta)>0
, and a
C^{4}
map

\theta:\mathcal{I}\to\Theta
onto
\Theta
with
\theta^{\prime}(\mu)>0
such that
\theta(\mu)
is the natural
parameter corresponding to the quality parameter
\mu
, and

\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}(w)=h(w)\exp\big\{\theta(\mu)t(w)-A(\theta(\mu))\big\},\qquad\mu\in\mathcal{I}.

Moreover,
the image of every compact subset of
\mathcal{I}
under

\theta
is compact in
\Theta
.

Under Assumption
4.1
, the Fisher information for the
quality parameter
\mu
is

I(\mu):=A^{\prime\prime}(\theta(\mu))\big(\theta^{\prime}(\mu)\big)^{2}.

We then define the
Fisher–Rao information coordinate

s(\mu):=\int_{\mu_{\circ}}^{\mu}\sqrt{I(v)}\,\mathrm{d}v,

(4.1)

where
\mu_{\circ}\in\mathcal{I}
is arbitrary and all quantities and results below are independent of the choice of
\mu_{\circ}
, as changing
\mu_{\circ}
shifts
s(\cdot)
by an additive constant, leaving
\kappa
and gap-based results unchanged.
This is the arc-length coordinate induced by the
Fisher–Rao metric
(
25
;
1
)
.
Since
s(\cdot)
is strictly
increasing, the best arm is unchanged by the transformation

\mu_{i}\mapsto\eta_{i}:=s(\mu_{i})
.

Assumption 4.2

(Regular Prior in Information Coordinates)
.

The arms’ quality parameters
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{K}
are independent under the prior. In the information coordinate
\eta_{i}=s(\mu_{i})
, the induced prior distribution of arm
i
has density
\bar{f}_{i}
and distribution function
\bar{F}_{i}
on
\mathcal{H}:=s(\mathcal{I})
. For each
i\in[K]
,

\bar{f}_{i}\text{ is continuous and bounded on }\mathcal{H},\qquad\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)>0\quad\forall\,\eta\in\mathcal{H}.

Define the
information-scaled top-two tie density

\kappa:=\sum_{i\neq j}\int_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{f}_{i}(u)\bar{f}_{j}(u)\prod_{k\neq i,j}\bar{F}_{k}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

Equivalently, if
f_{i}
and
F_{i}
respectively denote the prior density and distribution function
of
\mu_{i}
in the original coordinate system, then

\kappa=\sum_{i\neq j}\int_{\mathcal{I}}\frac{f_{i}(\mu)f_{j}(\mu)\prod_{k\neq i,j}F_{k}(\mu)}{\sqrt{I(\mu)}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

Thus
\kappa
is the density at zero of the top-two gap after
transforming the quality parameters to Fisher–Rao coordinates via (
4.1
).
In the Gaussian model with unit variance,
\theta(\mu)\equiv\mu
and

I(\mu)\equiv 1
, so this reduces to the top-two gap density used in the preceding
sections.

For the achievability result we also assume a global quadratic lower bound on
the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence. Let

D(\mu,z):=D(P_{\mu}\,\|\,P_{z}).

Assumption 4.3

(Quadratic KL Growth in the Fisher–Rao Information Coordinate)
.

There exists
c_{\rm KL}>0
such that, for all
\mu,z\in\mathcal{I}
,

D(\mu,z)\geq c_{\rm KL}\big(s(\mu)-s(z)\big)^{2}.

(4.2)

Assumption
4.3
holds for several one-parameter exponential families when
s(\cdot)
is the Fisher–Rao information coordinate. In particular, Gaussians with unit variance, Bernoulli, and Poisson satisfy (
4.2
) with
c_{\rm KL}=1/4
. Assumption
4.3
can be viewed as a global version of the local quadratic approximation
D(\mu,z)\approx\frac{1}{2}\big(s(\mu)-s(z)\big)^{2}
where
|s(\mu)-s(z)|
is the Fisher–Rao distance
(
1
)
.

Theorem 4.1

(Extension to General Distributions)
.

Suppose Assumptions
4.1
–
4.3
hold, then

\limsup_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathcal{E}_{T}(\text{{IC-PGWS}}(\alpha))=\liminf_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathfrak{E}_{T}(\alpha)=-\frac{1}{8\kappa^{2}}.

The lower bound holds for all adaptive policies satisfying the abstention
constraint, and the upper bound is attained by an information-coordinate version
of
PGWS
, namely,
IC-PGWS
.

The proof is postponed to Appendix
F
. We now provide a concrete example of applying Theorem
4.1
to Beta-Bernoulli arms.

Example 4.2

(Beta–Bernoulli arms)
.

Consider Bernoulli rewards with unknown success probabilities

\mu_{i}\in(0,1)
that follow Beta distributions, i.e.,

Y_{i,s}\mid\mu_{i}\sim{\rm Bernoulli}(\mu_{i}),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mu_{i}\sim{\rm Beta}(a_{i},b_{i}),

independently across arms and time, where
a_{i},b_{i}\geq 1/2
for
i\in[K]
.

We show that the Beta–Bernoulli arms satisfy Assumptions
4.1
–
4.3
.
Firstly,
the Bernoulli family is a regular exponential family with

\theta(\mu)=\log\frac{\mu}{1-\mu},\quad A(\theta)=\log(1+e^{\theta}),\quad\mbox{and}\quad t(w)=w.

Its Fisher information is
I(\mu)=\frac{1}{\mu(1-\mu)}
. Hence,
s(\mu)=\int_{0}^{\mu}\sqrt{I(v)}\,\mathrm{d}v=2\arcsin\sqrt{\mu}
, and
\mathcal{H}=(0,\pi)
.

Secondly, if
\eta=s(\mu)
, then
\mu=\sin^{2}(\eta/2)
, and the prior density of
\eta_{i}
is

\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)=\frac{\sin^{2a_{i}-1}(\eta/2)\cos^{2b_{i}-1}(\eta/2)}{{\rm Beta}(a_{i},b_{i})},\qquad 0<\eta<\pi.

Because
a_{i},b_{i}\geq 1/2
, this density is bounded, continuous, and strictly
positive on
(0,\pi)
. Hence, Assumption
4.2
holds.

Lastly, a routine calculation shows that the Bernoulli KL divergence satisfies

D(\mu,z)\geq\frac{1}{4}\big(s(\mu)-s(z)\big)^{2},\qquad\mu,z\in(0,1),

so Assumption
4.3
also holds with
c_{\rm KL}=1/4
.

Therefore, the conditions of Theorem
4.1
hold and the theorem applies. Define

\kappa_{\rm BB}:=\sum_{i\neq j}\int_{0}^{\pi}\bar{f}_{i}(u)\bar{f}_{j}(u)\prod_{k\neq i,j}\bar{F}_{k}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u=\sum_{i\neq j}\int_{0}^{1}f_{i}(\mu)f_{j}(\mu)\prod_{k\neq i,j}F_{k}(\mu)\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}\,\mathrm{d}\mu

where
\bar{F}_{i}
is the cumulative distribution function of
2\arcsin\sqrt{\mu_{i}}
, and
f_{i}
and
F_{i}
are, respectively, the Beta
(a_{i},b_{i})
density and CDF. So by Theorem
4.1
, we have

\limsup_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathcal{E}_{T}^{\rm BB}(\text{{IC-PGWS}}(\alpha))=\liminf_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathfrak{E}_{T}^{\rm BB}(\alpha)=-\frac{1}{8\kappa^{2}_{\rm{BB}}}.

in the Beta-Bernoulli case.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied Bayesian FB-BAI with an abstention option. We characterized
the sharp small-abstention, large-budget exponent:

\mathfrak{E}_{T}(\alpha)=\exp\!\left\{-\frac{\alpha^{2}T}{8\kappa_{\nu}^{2}}+o(\alpha^{2}T)\right\}.

The exponent is governed by the density
\kappa_{\nu}
of the prior top-two gap at
zero.

We proposed
PGWS
, an adaptive algorithm that
allocates samples to the posterior mean leader and its challengers.

PGWS
samples arms according to the empirical allocation distribution which is proportional to the reciprocal of the squared posterior mean gaps.
It achieves the same exponent as the information-theoretic lower bound. Hence it is asymptotically optimal on the
\alpha^{2}T
logarithmic scale. Numerical experiments support the practicality of our theoretical findings.

Our results clarify the benefits of abstention. Without abstention, Bayesian BAI has only a polynomial error decay, driven by prior probability on instances with top-two gap of order
T^{-1/2}
.
With abstention, the learner can spend its abstention budget on these near-tie instances, leading to exponentially decaying undetected error. In contrast, in the frequentist fixed-gap setting, ordinary BAI is already exponentially accurate, and abstention improves only lower-order
terms in the exponent.

Finally, we showed that the same mechanism extends beyond the Gaussian model
under continuous prior, one parameter exponential family reward, and quadratic KL growth conditions, with the Gaussian top-two gap density replaced by an information-scaled top-two gap density.

Appendix A
Prior Density of the Top-Two Gap at Zero

Lemma A.1

(Prior Density of the Top-Two Gap at Zero)
.

The hardness parameter
\kappa_{\nu}
and the top-two gap
\Gamma
are related as follows:

\kappa_{\nu}=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\nu}(\Gamma\leq\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

For each arm
i\in[K]
, the event that arm
i
is the best and the top-two gap is at most
\varepsilon
is

\left\{\mu_{i}=\mu_{(1)},\ \mu_{(1)}-\mu_{(2)}\leq\varepsilon\right\}.

Conditional on
\{\mu_{i}=x\}
, this event says that the means of all the other arms are at most
x
and at least one other arm is in
[x-\varepsilon,x]
. Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Gamma\leq\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{P}(\mu_{i}=\mu_{(1)},\ \mu_{(1)}-\mu_{(2)}\leq\varepsilon)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{i}(x)\left[\prod_{k\neq i}F_{k}(x)-\prod_{k\neq i}F_{k}(x-\varepsilon)\right]\mathrm{d}x

Let

G_{i}(x):=\prod_{k\neq i}F_{k}(x)
, then
G_{i}^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{j\neq i}\big(f_{j}(x)\prod_{k\neq i,j}F_{k}(x)\big)
.
By the fundamental theorem of calculus,

G_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x-\varepsilon)=\int_{x-\varepsilon}^{x}G_{i}^{\prime}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

Additionally, by the dominated convergence theorem, as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{P}(\Gamma\leq\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{i}(x)\left[\frac{G_{i}(x)-G_{i}(x-\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\to\sum^{K}_{i=1}\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{i}(x)f_{j}(x)\prod_{k\neq i,j}F_{k}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

It remains to simplify the product of the Gaussian densities. For
i<j
,

\displaystyle f_{i}(x)f_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma_{0}^{2}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x-\nu_{i})^{2}+(x-\nu_{j})^{2}}{2\sigma_{0}^{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}\,\sigma_{0}}\exp\left(-\frac{(\nu_{i}-\nu_{j})^{2}}{4\sigma_{0}^{2}}\right)g_{ij}(x),

where

is the density of

\mathcal{N}\!\left(\frac{\nu_{i}+\nu_{j}}{2},\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{2}\right)

Therefore

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{i}(x)f_{j}(x)\prod_{k\neq i,j}F_{k}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}\sqrt{\pi}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(\nu_{i}-\nu_{j})^{2}}{4\sigma_{0}^{2}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}g_{ij}(x)\prod_{k\neq i,j}F_{k}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}\sqrt{\pi}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(\nu_{i}-\nu_{j})^{2}}{4\sigma_{0}^{2}}\right)w_{ij}.

Summing over

Proof of Lemma

\widehat{b}_{T}

Consequently, the law of the means is

\mathcal{L}(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{K}\mid\mathcal{F}_{T})=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{K}\mathcal{N}(M_{i}(T),V_{i}(T)),

\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{K}

\mathcal{F}_{T}

Furthermore,
M_{i}(T)
and
V_{i}(T)
are
\mathcal{F}_{T}

\widehat{b}_{T}=\arg\max_{i}M_{i}(T)

is also
\mathcal{F}_{T}

\mathcal{F}_{T}

\widehat{b}_{T}

j\neq\widehat{b}_{T}

\Phi(-x)\leq e^{-x^{2}/2}

B.2
Asymptotic Behavior of the Statistic

To analyze the (asymptotic) behavior of
r_{T,\alpha}
, we first analyze
R_{T}
.

For ease of notation, we introduce auxiliary unnormalized weights. At time
t
, define
\widehat{b}_{t}
and
\widehat{\Delta}_{i}(t)
from the
posterior means as in Algorithm
1
. We also define
\widehat{\Gamma}_{t}=\widehat{\Delta}_{\widehat{b}_{t}}(t)
. Set
w_{\widehat{b}_{t}}(t)=1
,
w_{j}(t)=\big(\frac{\widehat{\Gamma}_{t}}{\widehat{\Delta}_{j}(t)}\big)^{2}
for
j\neq\widehat{b}_{t}
,
and
W(t):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}w_{\ell}(t)
. Then, on every non-forced round, the sampling rule in (
2.2

can be written equivalently as
p_{i}(t)=\frac{w_{i}(t)}{W(t)}
for
i\in[K]
.

Let

b^{\star}:=\arg\max_{i\in[K]}\mu_{i}

be the true best arm which is unique almost surely.
For
j\neq b^{\star}
, define the true gap

\Delta_{j}:=\mu_{b^{\star}}-\mu_{j}>0
.
Recall that the true top-two gap is

\Gamma:=\min_{j\neq b^{\star}}\Delta_{j}=\mu_{(1)}-\mu_{(2)}
.

Define the (deterministic) unnormalized limiting sampling weights

w_{b^{\star}}^{\dagger}(\mu):=1,\quad\mbox{and}\quad w_{j}^{\dagger}(\mu):=\left(\frac{\Gamma}{\Delta_{j}}\right)^{2},\qquad j\neq b^{\star},

Also define their sum, which is the normalization constant for the sampling distribution

W^{\dagger}(\mu):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}w_{\ell}^{\dagger}(\mu)=1+\sum_{j\neq b^{\star}}\left(\frac{\Gamma}{\Delta_{j}}\right)^{2}.

(B.1)

Finally, define the limiting sampling proportions

p_{i}^{\dagger}(\mu):=\frac{w_{i}^{\dagger}(\mu)}{W^{\dagger}(\mu)}=\frac{w_{i}^{\dagger}(\mu)}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}w_{\ell}^{\dagger}(\mu)},

as well as the worst-case pairwise error exponent

C^{\dagger}(\mu):=\min_{j\neq b^{\star}}\frac{\Delta_{j}^{2}}{2\left(1/p_{b^{\star}}^{\dagger}(\mu)+1/p_{j}^{\dagger}(\mu)\right)}.

Next we simplify
C^{\dagger}(\mu)
.

Lemma B.1

(Explicit Form of the Limiting Exponent)
.

Almost surely,

C^{\dagger}(\mu)=\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{4W^{\dagger}(\mu)}.

In particular,

\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{4K}\leq C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{8}.

Proof.

By definition, the normalized sampling proportions are:

p_{b^{\star}}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{W^{\dagger}(\mu)},\quad\mbox{and}\quad p_{j}^{\dagger}=\frac{\Gamma^{2}/\Delta_{j}^{2}}{W^{\dagger}(\mu)}.

We substitute these proportions into the pairwise error exponent for a specific challenger
j
:

\frac{\Delta_{j}^{2}}{2\left(1/p_{b^{\star}}^{\dagger}+1/p_{j}^{\dagger}\right)}=\frac{\Delta_{j}^{2}}{2W^{\dagger}(\mu)\left(1+\Delta_{j}^{2}/\Gamma^{2}\right)}.

To isolate the effect of the gap, we factor out
\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{2W^{\dagger}(\mu)}
and express the remainder as a function of the relative gap
r_{j}:=\Delta_{j}/\Gamma
:

\frac{\Delta_{j}^{2}}{2\left(1/p_{b^{\star}}^{\dagger}+1/p_{j}^{\dagger}\right)}=\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{2W^{\dagger}(\mu)}\left[\frac{(\Delta_{j}/\Gamma)^{2}}{1+(\Delta_{j}/\Gamma)^{2}}\right]=\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{2W^{\dagger}(\mu)}\left(\frac{r_{j}^{2}}{1+r_{j}^{2}}\right).

Now, recall the limiting exponent
C^{\dagger}(\mu)
is defined as the minimum of this expression over all challengers
j\neq b^{\star}
. Observe that the function
f(r)=\frac{r^{2}}{1+r^{2}}
is increasing on
[1,\infty)
. Since
\Delta_{j}/\Gamma\geq 1
for all
j\neq b^{\star}
, the minimum is attained by
a second-best arm, for which
\Delta_{j}=\Gamma
:

C^{\dagger}(\mu)=\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{2W^{\dagger}(\mu)}\left(\frac{1^{2}}{1+1^{2}}\right)=\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{4W^{\dagger}(\mu)}.

Also, because
w_{b^{\star}}^{\dagger}=1
, the second-best arm has weight
1
, and all other
weights are at most
1
, we have

2\leq W^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq K
.
Substituting back gives

\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{4K}\leq C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{8}.

Next, we prove that the forced-exploration safeguard collects enough data to guarantee the posterior mean of every arm converges to its true mean.

Lemma B.2

(Posterior Consistency)
.

For every arm
i
,

M_{i}(t)\to\mu_{i}\qquad\text{almost surely}.

(B.2)

Proof.

First, recall that the algorithm’s forced-exploration samples arm

for every

Second, conditional on a specific realization of the true mean

Next, we show that the empirical allocation of arm pulls converges to the sampling proportions.

Lemma B.3

(Convergence of Empirical Sampling Proportions)
.

For every arm
i
,

\frac{N_{i}(T)}{T}\to p_{i}^{\dagger}(\mu)\qquad\text{almost surely}.

(B.3)

Proof.

Fix an arm
i
. We proceed in four steps:

Step 1: Forced exploration is negligible.

Let
F_{i}(T)
be the number of forced-exploration pulls of arm
i
up to time
T
.
If the
m
-th forced pull of arm
i
occurs before time
T
, then immediately before that pull,

N_{i}(t)\geq m-1
.
But a forced pull of arm
i
can occur only if

N_{i}(t)<\sqrt{t+1}\leq\sqrt{T+1}
.
Hence

m-1<\sqrt{T+1}
,
and therefore

F_{i}(T)\leq\lceil\sqrt{T+1}\rceil+1
.
Consequently,

\frac{F_{i}(T)}{T}\to 0.

Let
F(T)
be the total number of forced-exploration rounds up to time
T
. Since

F(T)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}F_{\ell}(T)
,
we also have

\frac{F(T)}{T}\leq\frac{K(\lceil\sqrt{T+1}\rceil+1)}{T}\to 0.

Step 2: Martingale concentration on non-forced rounds.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{t}
be the history before round
t+1
, and define

B_{t}:=\mathbbm{1}\{\text{round }t+1\text{ is non-forced}\}
.
The event
\{B_{t}=1\}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable. On non-forced rounds, the algorithm samples arm
i
with conditional probability
p_{i}(t)
.

Define

D_{i,t+1}:=B_{t}\bigl(\mathbbm{1}\{A_{t+1}=i\}-p_{i}(t)\bigr)
.
Then

\mathbb{E}[D_{i,t+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0
,
so
(D_{i,t+1})_{t\geq 0}
is a martingale difference sequence. Also,

|D_{i,t+1}|\leq 1
.

Therefore, by Azuma’s inequality, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\bigg|\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}D_{i,t+1}\bigg|\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}T}{2}\right).

The right-hand side is summable in
T
. Hence, by the Borel–Cantelli lemma,

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}D_{i,t+1}\to 0\qquad\text{almost surely}.

Step 3: Decomposition of the empirical pull count.

The total number of pulls of arm
i
can be decomposed as

N_{i}(T)=F_{i}(T)+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}B_{t}\mathbbm{1}\{A_{t+1}=i\}.

Using the definition of
D_{i,t+1}
, we have

B_{t}\mathbbm{1}\{A_{t+1}=i\}=D_{i,t+1}+B_{t}p_{i}(t)
.
Thus

\frac{N_{i}(T)}{T}=\frac{F_{i}(T)}{T}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}D_{i,t+1}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}B_{t}p_{i}(t).

By Steps 1 and 2,

\frac{N_{i}(T)}{T}-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}B_{t}p_{i}(t)\to 0\qquad\text{almost surely}.

Step 4: Cesàro convergence of the target probabilities.

By Lemma
B.2
,
M(t)\to\mu
almost surely.
Since the prior is continuous, the true best arm and true second-best arm are unique almost surely. On this probability-one event, the map
m\mapsto p(m)
is continuous at
m=\mu
. Therefore, as
t\to\infty
,

p_{i}(t)\to p_{i}^{\dagger}(\mu)\qquad\text{almost surely}.

Hence, the sequence
\{p_{i}(t)\}
is also Cesàro convergent, i.e.,

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}p_{i}(t)\to p_{i}^{\dagger}(\mu)\qquad\text{almost surely}.

Now write

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}B_{t}p_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}p_{i}(t)-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(1-B_{t})p_{i}(t).

Since
0\leq p_{i}(t)\leq 1
,

0\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(1-B_{t})p_{i}(t)\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(1-B_{t})=\frac{F(T)}{T}\to 0.

Hence

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}B_{t}p_{i}(t)\to p_{i}^{\dagger}(\mu)\qquad\text{almost surely}.

Combining this with Step 3 gives (
B.3
).
∎

By Lemma

B.3
Lower Tail of the Exponent

We now analyze the lower tail of
C^{\dagger}(\mu)
. We first bound the event that the means of three or more arms are
\varepsilon
-close.

Lemma B.5

(Triple Near-Tie Probability)
.

Let
\Gamma_{3}:=\mu_{(1)}-\mu_{(3)}
when
K\geq 3
. If
K=2
, set
\Gamma_{3}:=+\infty
. We have that as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
,

\mathbb{P}(\Gamma_{3}\leq\varepsilon)=O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Proof.

For
K=2
the statement is trivial. Assume
K\geq 3
.
If
\Gamma_{3}\leq\varepsilon
, then there exist three distinct arms
i,j,k

such that all three means lie within an interval of length
\varepsilon
around
the largest of the three. By a union bound over ordered triples,

\mathbb{P}(\Gamma_{3}\leq\varepsilon)\leq\sum_{i\neq j\neq k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{i}(x)\left[\int_{x-\varepsilon}^{x}f_{j}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right]\left[\int_{x-\varepsilon}^{x}f_{k}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right]\mathrm{d}x.

Gaussian densities are bounded uniformly by
(\sigma_{0}\sqrt{2\pi})^{-1}
, so each
inner integral is
O(\varepsilon)
uniformly in
x
. Since
\int f_{i}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1
,
the whole expression is
O(\varepsilon^{2})
.
∎

Remark B.1
.

This lemma reveals why asymptotically the leading-order complexity of the abstention problem depends only on pairwise gaps. While a two-arm near-tie forms the primary statistical bottleneck (occurring with probability
O(\varepsilon)
), the event that three or more arms are simultaneously tied is one order rarer (namely,
O(\varepsilon^{2})
). Consequently, in the small-abstention regime, the asymptotic error exponent is perfectly captured by the closest challenger alone; the statistical cost of separating third-best (and worse) arms vanishes.

Next, we show that
W^{\dagger}(\mu)
, defined in (
B.1
), is approximately
2
when
C^{\dagger}(\mu)
is small, because it is unlikely (by Lemma
B.5
) that many other arms are close to the optimal. Consequently,
\Gamma^{2}/8
and
C^{\dagger}(\mu)
behave similarly.

Lemma B.6

(Lower Tail of
C^{\dagger}(\mu)
)
.

As
x\downarrow 0
,

\mathbb{P}(C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq x)=\kappa_{\nu}\sqrt{8x}+o(\sqrt{x}).

(B.5)

Proof.

By Lemma
B.1
,

C^{\dagger}(\mu)=\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{4W^{\dagger}(\mu)}\leq\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{8}
.
Therefore

\{\Gamma\leq\sqrt{8x}\}\subseteq\{C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq x\}
.

Using Lemma
A.1
,

\mathbb{P}(C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq x)\geq\mathbb{P}(\Gamma\leq\sqrt{8x})=\kappa_{\nu}\sqrt{8x}+o(\sqrt{x}).

On the other hand, to derive an upper bound, set

\delta_{x}:=x^{3/8}
.
On the event
\{C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq x\}
, Lemma
B.1
yields

\Gamma^{2}\leq 4Kx
,
because
W^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq K
.
On the additional event
\{\Gamma_{3}>\delta_{x}\}
, every arm other than the top two
has gap at least
\delta_{x}
from the best arm. Hence, for every such arm

Thus, on

we have

W^{\dagger}(\mu)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}w_{\ell}^{\dagger}(\mu)=1+\sum_{j\neq b^{\star}}\left(\frac{\Gamma}{\Delta_{j}}\right)^{2}=2+o(1),

uniformly as

Consequently,

Appendix C
Proof of Theorem
3.1

We aim to lower bound the Bayes undetected error probability subject to an upper bound on the Bayes abstention probability. We begin by expressing these global probabilities over the full parameter (mean of the arms) space
\mathbb{R}^{K}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{A}_{T}(\pi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{K}}\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\pi}(\widehat{a}=?)\,P_{\nu}(\mathrm{d}\mu),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{T}(\pi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{K}}\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\pi}\bigl(\widehat{a}_{T}\notin\{a^{\star}(\mu),?\}\bigr)\,P_{\nu}(\mathrm{d}\mu).

Inspired by the form of the prior complexity
\kappa_{\nu}
, it is natural to partition the parameter space
\mathbb{R}^{K}
according to the top pair of arms. For any pair
i<j
, define the region
\Omega_{ij}
where arms
i
and
j
strictly dominate all the other arms, i.e.,

\Omega_{ij}:=\left\{\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{K}:\min\{\mu_{i},\mu_{j}\}>\max_{k\neq i,j}\mu_{k}\right\}.

Because the boundaries between these regions correspond to tie events and hence have
P_{\nu}
-measure zero, the sets
\Omega_{ij}
form a partition of the parameter space up to
P_{\nu}
-null sets. Thus, we can decompose the abstention and undetected error probability as:

\mathcal{A}_{T}(\pi)=\sum_{i<j}\int_{\Omega_{ij}}\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\pi}(\widehat{a}=?)\,P_{\nu}(\mathrm{d}\mu),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{T}(\pi)=\sum_{i<j}\int_{\Omega_{ij}}\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\pi}\bigl(\widehat{a}_{T}\notin\{a^{\star}(\mu),?\}\bigr)\,P_{\nu}(\mathrm{d}\mu).

Ultimately, we want to lower bound the undetected error
\mathcal{E}_{T}(\pi)
using a Neyman–Pearson lemma (cf.
21
) style rearrangement. However, working directly with the restricted true prior
\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{ij}}(\mu)P_{\nu}(\mathrm{d}\mu)
presents significant technical hurdles. This is overcome by the introduction of a submeasure, which we call a
flat local subprior
.

C.1
A Submeasure: Flat Local Subprior

To resolve the difficulties of integrating over
\Omega_{ij}
, we construct a mathematically cleaner measure
Q_{\rho}
that is a submeasure of the true Gaussian prior
P_{\nu}
, where
\rho\in(0,1)
is a fixed slack parameter.

Definition C.1

(Local Change of Variable)
.

For a fixed pair of arms
i<j
, we reparameterize the mean vector
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
into
(i,j,x,\delta,h,\zeta_{-ij})
, a “local” coordinate system defined by the top two arms, their gap, and the other arms. Specifically,

•

x\in\mathbb{R}
: the midpoint of the top two arms’ means.

•

\delta\geq 0
: the absolute gap between the top two arms.

•

h\in\{+1,-1\}
: the sign indicating which arm is better (
h=+1
for arm
i
,
h=-1
for arm
j
).

•

\zeta_{-ij}=(\zeta_{k})_{k\neq i,j}
: the means of the remaining
K-2
arms.

We consolidate the background variables,
i,j,x,\zeta_{-ij}
, into one tuple
\xi=(i,j,x,\zeta_{-ij})
. Given
\xi
,
\delta
, and
h
, the inverse mapping
\mu(\xi,\delta,h)\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
is defined as:

\mu_{i}=x+h\frac{\delta}{2},\quad\mu_{j}=x-h\frac{\delta}{2},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mu_{k}=\zeta_{k}\text{ for }k\neq i,j.

(C.1)

Note the absolute value of the determinant of the Jacobian of the map
(x,\delta)\mapsto(\mu_{i},\mu_{j})
is
1
.

Remark C.1
.

To see why the restricted true prior is difficult to work with, let
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{K}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be any non-negative measurable function. Applying the local coordinate transformation in (
C.1
) to the true prior over the partitioned space yields:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{K}}\psi(\mu)P_{\nu}(\mathrm{d}\mu)=\sum_{i<j}\int_{\Omega_{ij}}\psi(\mu)p_{\nu}(\mu)\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}\sum_{h\in\{+1,-1\}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{D}_{ij}(\delta)}\psi\bigl(\mu(\xi,\delta,h)\bigr)\bigg[f_{i}\Big(x+h\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)f_{j}\Big(x-h\frac{\delta}{2}\Big)\prod_{k\neq i,j}f_{k}(\zeta_{k})\bigg]\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\delta,

where
\mathrm{d}\xi=\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\zeta_{-ij}
, and the domain of integration

\mathcal{D}_{ij}(\delta):=\left\{(x,\zeta_{-ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{K-1}:\max_{k\neq i,j}\zeta_{k}<x-\frac{\delta}{2}\right\}

depends on the gap
\delta
.
The expansion of
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{K}}\psi(\mu)P_{\nu}(\mathrm{d}\mu)
above reveals the technical hurdles: the domain of integration
\mathcal{D}_{ij}(\delta)
shrinks as the gap
\delta
grows, and the densities
f_{i}
and
f_{j}
fluctuate with both the gap
\delta
and the sign
h
. To overcome these hurdles, we construct
Q_{\rho}
to eliminate these dependencies by restricting
(x,\zeta_{-ij})
to a smaller set, and by lower bounding
f_{i}\left(x+h\frac{\delta}{2}\right)f_{j}\left(x-h\frac{\delta}{2}\right)
with
(1-\rho)f_{i}(x)f_{j}(x)
, which depends on neither
\delta
nor
h
.

For
\ell>0
and
M>0
, and for each pair
1\leq i<j\leq K
, define the
(\ell,M)
-restricted set

\mathcal{C}_{ij}(\ell,M):=\left\{(x,\zeta_{-ij}):|x|\leq M,\ |\zeta_{k}|\leq M,\ \zeta_{k}\leq x-\ell\text{ for all }k\neq i,j\right\}.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}=\cup_{i<j}\mathcal{C}_{ij}(\ell,M)
denote the disjoint union of
\mathcal{C}_{ij}(\ell,M)
over all arm pairs
i<j
. Recall that we defined

\kappa_{\nu}=\sum_{i\neq j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{i}(x)f_{j}(x)\prod_{k\neq i,j}F_{k}(x)\ \mathrm{d}x=2\sum_{i<j}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{x}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{x}f_{i}(x)f_{j}(x)\prod_{k\neq i,j}f_{k}(\zeta_{k})\ \mathrm{d}\zeta_{-ij}\right]\ \mathrm{d}x.

By the monotone convergence theorem, for any
\rho>0
, we can choose
\ell>0
sufficiently small and
M>0
sufficiently large such that:

2\sum_{i<j}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{ij}(\ell,M)}f_{i}(x)f_{j}(x)\prod_{k\neq i,j}f_{k}(\zeta_{k})\,\mathrm{d}\zeta_{-ij}\,\mathrm{d}x\geq(1-\rho)\kappa_{\nu}.

We now define a measure
\Lambda_{\rho}
whose support is the set
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
. Since
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
is the disjoint union of
\mathcal{C}_{ij}(\ell,M)
, we only need to specify
\Lambda_{\rho}
for each pair
i<j
. For the pair
i<j
,
\Lambda_{\rho}
takes the form

\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi):=(1-\rho)f_{i}(x)f_{j}(x)\prod_{k\neq i,j}f_{k}(\zeta_{k})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\zeta_{-ij}

(C.2)

Let
c_{\rho}:=2\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{\rho})\geq(1-\rho)^{2}\kappa_{\nu}
.

Lemma C.1

(Uniform Lower Bound)
.

There exists a gap
\delta_{\rho}\in(0,2\ell)
such that, for all
\delta\in[0,\delta_{\rho}]
,
\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{ij}(\ell,M)
, and
h\in\{+1,-1\}
, the original densities satisfy the multiplicative bound:

(1-\rho)f_{i}(x)f_{j}(x)\leq f_{i}\!\left(x+h\frac{\delta}{2}\right)f_{j}\!\left(x-h\frac{\delta}{2}\right).

Furthermore, for any
\delta\in[0,\delta_{\rho}]
, arms
i
and
j
are the top two arms because
x-\frac{\delta}{2}>x-\ell\geq\zeta_{k}
.

Proof.

For each
(i,j)
, because
\mathcal{C}_{ij}(\ell,M)
is compact, the Gaussian densities defined on
\mathcal{C}_{ij}(\ell,M)
are (strictly) positive and uniformly continuous. Because
\ell>0
separates the top pair of arms
i,j
from the other arms
k\neq i,j
, we can choose a sufficiently small
\delta_{\rho}<2\ell
such that the continuity of
f_{i}
and
f_{j}
guarantees the multiplicative bound over the interval
[0,\delta_{\rho}]
. Since there are finitely many pairs, taking minimum of the pair-specific values of
\delta_{\rho}
yields the desired result.
∎

We now define a convenient submeasure to work with later.

Definition C.2

(Flat Local Subprior)
.

Define the flat local subprior
Q_{\rho}
as

Q_{\rho}(E):=\sum_{i<j}\sum_{h\in\{+1,-1\}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{ij}(\ell,M)}\int_{0}^{\delta_{\rho}}\mathbbm{1}_{E}\!\bigl(\mu(\xi,\delta,h)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\delta\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)\qquad\forall\,E\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{K}).

(C.3)

Lemma C.2

(Domination)
.

The constructed measure
Q_{\rho}
in (
C.3
) is a submeasure of the true Gaussian prior
P_{\nu}
, i.e.,
Q_{\rho}\leq P_{\nu}
everywhere.

Proof.

For each
i<j
, let

\mathcal{S}_{ij}:=\left\{\mu(\xi,\delta,h):\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{ij}(\ell,M),\ \delta\in[0,\delta_{\rho}],\ h\in\{+1,-1\}\right\},

and let
\mathcal{S}:=\bigcup_{i<j}\mathcal{S}_{ij}
. For any Borel set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{K}
, substituting the definition of

\Lambda_{\rho}
in (
C.2
) into (
C.3
) gives

Q_{\rho}(E)=\sum_{i<j}\sum_{h\in\{+1,-1\}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{ij}(\ell,M)}\int_{0}^{\delta_{\rho}}\mathbbm{1}_{E}\!\bigl(\mu(\xi,\delta,h)\bigr)(1-\rho)f_{i}(x)f_{j}(x)\prod_{k\neq i,j}f_{k}(\zeta_{k})\,\mathrm{d}\delta\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\zeta_{-ij}.

For each fixed
i<j
and
h
, the Jacobian determinant of the coordinate transformation in (
C.1
) has absolute value
1
.
Moreover, except on the null set
\{\delta=0\}
, the sign
h
is uniquely
determined by
\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}
, and the top-two pair
\{i,j\}
is uniquely
determined on
\mathcal{S}
. Hence
Q_{\rho}
has Lebesgue density

q_{\rho}\bigl(\mu(\xi,\delta,h)\bigr)=(1-\rho)f_{i}(x)f_{j}(x)\prod_{k\neq i,j}f_{k}(\zeta_{k})

for Lebesgue-almost every

\mu(\xi,\delta,h)\in\mathcal{S}_{ij}
. Per the definition in
(
C.3
),
Q_{\rho}
is supported on
\mathcal{S}
, hence we also have
q_{\rho}(\mu)=0
for Lebesgue-almost every
\mu\notin\mathcal{S}
.

For
\mu=\mu(\xi,\delta,h)\in\mathcal{S}_{ij}
, the true prior density is

p_{\nu}(\mu)=f_{i}\left(x+h\frac{\delta}{2}\right)f_{j}\left(x-h\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\prod_{k\neq i,j}f_{k}(\zeta_{k}).

Lemma
C.1
therefore gives

q_{\rho}(\mu)\leq p_{\nu}(\mu)
for Lebesgue-almost every

\mu\in\mathcal{S}
. The same inequality holds outside

\mathcal{S}
because
q_{\rho}=0
there. Consequently, for every
Borel set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{K}
,

Q_{\rho}(E)=\int_{E}q_{\rho}(\mu)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\int_{E}p_{\nu}(\mu)\,\mathrm{d}\mu=P_{\nu}(E).

Hence
Q_{\rho}\leq P_{\nu}
.
∎

Lemma C.3

(Properties of
Q_{\rho}
)
.

Let
\Xi
(background oracle),
\Delta
(gap), and
H
(sign) denote random quantities under
Q_{\rho}
. The construction of
Q_{\rho}
results in the following joint distribution:

Q_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\mu)=Q_{\rho}(\Xi\in\mathrm{d}\xi,\Delta\in\mathrm{d}\delta,H=h)=\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\delta,\qquad h\in\{+1,-1\}.

(C.4)

Because
Q_{\rho}
is not a probability measure in general, we refer to the
Q_{\rho}
measure of a set as its
Q_{\rho}
-mass in the following. This measure has two important properties:

1.

Fair Sign:
Conditional on any realization
(\Xi=\xi,\Delta=\delta)
, the two signs have equal
Q_{\rho}
-mass, i.e.,
Q_{\rho}(H=+1\mid\xi,\delta)=Q_{\rho}(H=-1\mid\xi,\delta)
.

2.

Flat Gap:
The marginal unnormalized density of the gap is uniform, i.e.,
Q_{\rho}(\Delta\in\mathrm{d}\delta)=c_{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\delta
for
\delta\in[0,\delta_{\rho}]
.

Proof.

By the definition of
Q_{\rho}
, the measure on the product space
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}\times[0,\delta_{\rho}]\times\{+1,-1\}
factors as
\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)\otimes\mathrm{d}\delta\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\{h\}}
. Because this is a product measure that does not depend on
h
, the sign
H
occurs with equal probability
1/2
regardless of
\Xi
or
\Delta
. Furthermore, integrating out
\Xi
and
H
leaves the measure of the gap
\Delta
as
2\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{\rho})\,\mathrm{d}\delta=c_{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\delta
, proving the flat gap property.

∎

Lemma C.4

(Probability Domination Under
Q_{\rho}
)
.

For any policy
\pi
, the Bayes probability evaluated under the subprior
Q_{\rho}
is a lower bound of the probability under the original prior
P_{\nu}
. Specifically, if a strategy
\pi
satisfies the abstention condition
\mathcal{A}_{T}(\pi)\leq\alpha
under
P_{\nu}
, it also satisfies:

\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\pi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{K}}\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\pi}(\widehat{a}_{T}=?)\,Q_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\mu)\leq\mathcal{A}_{T}(\pi)\leq\alpha.

In addition, the true undetected error is lower bounded by the error evaluated under the subprior:

\mathcal{E}_{T}(\pi)\geq\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\pi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{K}}\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\pi}(\widehat{a}_{T}\notin\{a^{\star},?\})\,Q_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\mu).

Proof.

This follows directly from Lemma
C.2
.
∎

This lemma explains how to use
Q_{\rho}
. Any strategy satisfying
\mathcal{A}_{T}(\pi)\leq\alpha
under the original prior also has
Q_{\rho}
-abstention mass at most
\alpha
. Consequently, to lower bound the original Bayes undetected error probability, it suffices to lower bound the error contributed by the flat subprior
Q_{\rho}
.

C.2
Oracle Reduction: Lower Bounding via a 2-Arm Hypothesis Testing Problem

Next, we evaluate the probabilities under the local subprior
Q_{\rho}
. To isolate the fundamental difficulty of the problem, we reduce the
K
-arm task to a
2
-arm hypothesis testing problem by giving the learner additional side (oracle) information.

Revealing Oracle Information.

Before sampling begins, we reveal the
background oracle

\Xi=(I,J,X,\zeta_{-IJ})
, which depends on the random means
\mu
, to the learner. For a specific realization
\Xi=\xi=(i,j,x,\zeta_{-ij})
, this means the learner knows the identities of the top two arms, their midpoint
x
, and the means
\zeta_{-ij}
of all remaining background arms.

Conditional on any realization of the background oracle
\Xi=\xi
, the only remaining random variables relevant to the decision under
Q_{\rho}
are the gap magnitude and the sign, i.e.,

\Delta\in[0,\delta_{\rho}],\quad\mbox{and}\quad H\in\{+1,-1\}.

For ease of notation, we define the unknown signed gap
\Theta:=H\Delta
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}
denote the probability law of the experiment conditional on the background oracle
\Xi=\xi
and the signed gap
\Theta=\theta
.

Since any policy in the original experiment can be implemented in the oracle-revealed setting by simply ignoring the side information
\Xi
, any lower bound derived for this oracle-revealed experiment provides a lower bound for the original problem. Furthermore, since the background means are known, querying any arm other than
i
or
j
provides no information about the unknown gap
\Theta
. We show that we can restrict our analysis to strategies that only sample the top two arms and output the decision of the sign; we call these
local pair strategies
.

Lemma C.5

(Sufficiency of Local Pair Strategies)
.

Fix a realization of the background oracle
\Xi=\xi=(i,j,x,\zeta_{-ij})
. For any adaptive strategy
\pi
, there exists a modified strategy
\pi^{\prime}
that samples only arms
i
and
j
, restricts its terminal recommendation to
\widehat{a}^{\prime}_{T}\in\{+,-,?\}
, and satisfies for every signed-gap realization
\theta\in[-\delta_{\rho},\delta_{\rho}]\setminus\{0\}
with true sign
h=\text{sgn}(\theta)\in\{+,-\}
, i.e.,

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}(\widehat{a}^{\prime}_{T}=?)=\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}(\widehat{a}_{T}=?),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}(\widehat{a}^{\prime}_{T}=-h)\leq\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}(\widehat{a}_{T}\notin\{a^{\star}(h),?\}),

(C.5)

where
a^{\star}(+)=i
and
a^{\star}(-)=j
. This says that the abstention probability of
\pi^{\prime}
is unchanged from that of
\pi
and the undetected error probability of
\pi^{\prime}
is less than or equal to that of
\pi
.

Proof.

Fix
\xi=(i,j,x,\zeta_{-ij})
. We construct a modified strategy
\pi^{\prime}
from
\pi
that possesses the desired properties in (
C.5
).

When
\pi
samples from an arm
k\in\{i,j\}
,
\pi^{\prime}
queries that same arm and feeds the observed reward back to
\pi
. Whenever
\pi
chooses to sample from an arm
k\notin\{i,j\}
,
\pi^{\prime}
instead queries arm
i
, discards the true reward, and feeds
\pi
an independent simulated reward drawn from
\mathcal{N}(\zeta_{k},1)
.

Because
\Xi=\xi
is known,
\zeta_{k}
is the true mean of arm
k
. Furthermore, for
k\neq i,j
, the reward distributions of arm
k
are independent of the unknown signed gap
\Theta
. Therefore, the simulated reward from
\pi^{\prime}
matches that from the true distribution of arm
k
. By induction, the internal history observed by
\pi
under
\pi^{\prime}
is identically distributed to the true history in the original experiment. Consequently, the terminal distribution of
\pi
is preserved for every
\theta
.

When
\pi
stops and outputs a recommendation
\widehat{a}_{T}
,
\pi^{\prime}
maps this to a sign decision
\widehat{a}^{\prime}_{T}\in\{+,-,?\}
as follows:
i\mapsto+
,
j\mapsto-
,
?\mapsto?
, and any
k\notin\{i,j,?\}
is mapped arbitrarily to
+
. This map preserves the abstention probability. For the undetected error, recall that on the support of
Q_{\rho}
, arms
i
and
j
are strictly the top two arms. Thus, any original output
k\notin\{i,j,?\}
is an error. If
h=-
, mapping
k
to
+
yields
\widehat{a}^{\prime}_{T}=+
, which is an incorrect sign, thereby preserving the error probability. If
h=+
, mapping
k
to
+
converts an original error into a correct guess, reducing the error probability. Thus, across all cases, the probability of an incorrect sign under
\pi^{\prime}
is less than or equal to the probability of an undetected error under
\pi
, showing (
C.5
).
∎

C.3
Transformed Observations and Sufficient Statistics:

To establish clean analytical expressions for the abstention and undetected error probabilities, we now demonstrate that any decision rule acting on the full history of this restricted two-arm game can be reduced to a randomized decision rule acting on a scalar sufficient statistic, without altering any terminal-output probability.

Fix the revealed background oracle realization
\Xi=\xi=(i,j,x,\zeta_{-ij})
, and consider strategies that sample only from arms
i
and
j
. Define the sign indicator for the arms:
\mathrm{sgn}(i):=+1
and
\mathrm{sgn}(j):=-1
. At time
t
, if the learner samples
A_{t}\in\{i,j\}
and observes the reward
X_{t}
, we define the
transformed observation
:
Z_{t}:=\mathrm{sgn}(A_{t})(X_{t}-x).

For any realization of the signed gap
\Theta=H\Delta=\theta
, the means of the top two arms are:

\mu_{i}=x+\frac{\theta}{2},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mu_{j}=x-\frac{\theta}{2}.

Let
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}
denote the probability law of the experiment conditional on
\Xi=\xi
and
\Theta=\theta
. Furthermore, let:

\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\xi}:=(\xi,A_{1},Z_{1},\dots,A_{t},Z_{t})

denote the
transformed oracle history
up to time
t
.

Lemma C.6

(Transformed Observations are i.i.d.)
.

Fix an oracle realization
\Xi=\xi
and a signed-gap realization
\theta\in[-\delta_{\rho},\delta_{\rho}]
. Under
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}
, conditionally on
\mathcal{H}_{t-1}^{\xi}
and the chosen arm
A_{t}\in\{i,j\}
, the transformed observation satisfies:

Z_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2},1\right).

i.e., under the full joint Bayesian law,

Z_{t}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t-1}^{\xi},A_{t}=i,\Theta=\theta,\Xi=\xi\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2},1\right).

(C.6)

Because this conditional law does not depend on the chosen arm
A_{t}
, the sequence
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{T}
is identically and independently distributed as
\mathcal{N}(\theta/2,1)
under
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}
.

Proof.

If
A_{t}=i
, the true observation follows

X_{t}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t-1}^{\xi},A_{t}=i,\Theta=\theta,\Xi=\xi\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(x+\frac{\theta}{2},1\right).

Applying the transformation
Z_{t}=X_{t}-x
yields (
C.6
).
While if
A_{t}=j
, the true observation follows
X_{t}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t-1}^{\xi},A_{t}=j,\Theta=\theta,\Xi=\xi\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(x-\frac{\theta}{2},1\right)
. Similarly, applying the transformation yields (
C.6
).
Thus, the conditional law of
Z_{t}
is
\mathcal{N}(\theta/2,1)
regardless of which of the two arms is chosen.

Next we prove independence. Let
\gamma_{\theta}
denote the measure corresponding to
\mathcal{N}(\theta/2,1)
. For any Borel set
B
, we have:

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}(Z_{t}\in B\mid\mathcal{H}_{t-1}^{\xi},A_{t})=\gamma_{\theta}(B).

Iterating this identity using the tower property shows that for any sequence of Borel sets
B_{1},\dots,B_{T}
:

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}(Z_{1}\in B_{1},\dots,Z_{T}\in B_{T})=\prod_{t=1}^{T}\gamma_{\theta}(B_{t}).

Hence, given
\xi
and
\theta
,
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{T}
are i.i.d. over the transformed history.
∎

We consolidate the sequence of observations into a sum and its time-scaled counterpart. Define:

W:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t},\quad\mbox{and}\quad V:=\frac{W}{\sqrt{T}}.

Lemma C.7

(Likelihood Ratios Depend Only on
W
)
.

For every
\xi
and every
\theta\in[-\delta_{\rho},\delta_{\rho}]
, the measure
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}
(i.e.,
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}\ll\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}
), and the likelihood ratio of the transformed oracle history
\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}
is:

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}}(\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi})=\exp\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2}W-\frac{T\theta^{2}}{8}\right)=\exp\!\left(\frac{\theta\sqrt{T}}{2}V-\frac{T\theta^{2}}{8}\right).

Consequently,
W
(and equivalently
V
) is a sufficient statistic for
\theta
.

Proof.

Let
h_{T}=(\xi,a_{1},z_{1},\dots,a_{T},z_{T})
be the realized transformed oracle history, and let
\phi
be the standard normal density as usual. Under
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}
, the density of
h_{T}
factors as:

p_{\theta}^{\xi}(h_{T})=\left[\prod_{t=1}^{T}\pi_{t}(a_{t}\mid\xi,a_{1},z_{1},\dots,a_{t-1},z_{t-1})\right]\prod_{t=1}^{T}\phi\!\left(z_{t}-\frac{\theta}{2}\right),

where
\pi_{t}
is the strategy’s sampling kernel at time
t
. Because the action probabilities are identical functions of the realized history under both
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}
and
\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}
, they cancel in the likelihood ratio, i.e.,

\frac{p_{\theta}^{\xi}(h_{T})}{p_{0}^{\xi}(h_{T})}=\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\phi(z_{t}-\theta/2)}{\phi(z_{t})}=\prod_{t=1}^{T}\exp\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2}z_{t}-\frac{\theta^{2}}{8}\right).

Aggregating the sum inside the exponential yields:

\frac{p_{\theta}^{\xi}(h_{T})}{p_{0}^{\xi}(h_{T})}=\exp\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}z_{t}-\frac{T\theta^{2}}{8}\right)=\exp\!\left(\frac{\theta}{2}W-\frac{T\theta^{2}}{8}\right).

∎

Lemma C.8

(Sufficiency of
V
for Terminal Decisions)
.

Fix the revealed background oracle realization
\Xi=\xi
. Let
\widetilde{\psi}^{\xi}=(\widetilde{\psi}_{+}^{\xi},\widetilde{\psi}_{-}^{\xi},\widetilde{\psi}_{?}^{\xi})
be any randomized terminal rule based on the transformed oracle history
\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}
, and let
\widehat{a}\in\{+,-,?\}
be its random output. There exists a modified terminal rule
\psi^{\xi}
depending on the history only through
V
, with random output
\widehat{a}^{V}\in\{+,-,?\}
, such that for every
\theta\in[-\delta_{\rho},\delta_{\rho}]
and every decision
d\in\{+,-,?\}
:

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}(\widehat{a}^{V}=d)=\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}(\widehat{a}=d).

Proof.

By Lemma
C.7
, the likelihood ratio
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}/\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}
is
\sigma(W)
-measurable. Since
\sigma(V)=\sigma(W)
, it is also
\sigma(V)
-measurable.

Let
\widetilde{\psi}_{d}^{\xi}(h_{T})
denote the probability that the original terminal rule outputs decision
d\in\{+,-,?\}
after observing transformed history
h_{T}
. Let
\mathbb{Q}^{\xi}(v,\mathrm{d}h_{T})
be a regular conditional distribution of
\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}
given
V=v
under
\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}
. i.e.,
\mathbb{Q}^{\xi}(V,B)
is a version of
\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}\left(\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}\in B\mid V\right)
.

By Lemma
C.7
,
\mathbb{P}^{\xi}_{\theta}(V\in dv)=\exp\!\left(\frac{\theta\sqrt{T}}{2}v-\frac{T\theta^{2}}{8}\right)\mathbb{P}^{\xi}_{0}(V\in dv)
. Therefore, for every measurable set
B
and
C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}\left(\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}\in B,V\in C\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{\xi}_{0}\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{\theta\sqrt{T}}{2}V-\frac{T\theta^{2}}{8}\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}\in B\}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{V\in C\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{C}\exp\!\left(\frac{\theta\sqrt{T}}{2}v-\frac{T\theta^{2}}{8}\right)Q^{\xi}(v,B)\mathbb{P}^{\xi}_{0}(V\in\mathrm{d}v)

\displaystyle=\int_{C}Q^{\xi}(v,B)\mathbb{P}^{\xi}_{\theta}(V\in\mathrm{d}v).

This implies that the kernel
\mathbb{Q}^{\xi}(v,\mathrm{d}h_{T})
is a conditional law under
\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}
. Hence, for every bounded measurable
f
,

\mathbb{E}^{\xi}_{\theta}\big[f(\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{T})\mid V\big]=\int f(h_{T})Q^{\xi}(V,\mathrm{d}h_{T})\quad\mathbb{P}^{\xi}_{\theta}\text{-a.s.,}

(C.7)

Define the terminal rule
\psi^{\xi}=(\psi_{+}^{\xi},\psi_{-}^{\xi},\psi_{?}^{\xi})
via the kernel
\mathbb{Q}^{\xi}(v,\mathrm{d}h_{T})
as follows:

\psi_{d}^{\xi}(v):=\int\widetilde{\psi}_{d}^{\xi}(h_{T})\,\mathbb{Q}^{\xi}(v,\mathrm{d}h_{T}),\qquad d\in\{+,-,?\}.

By definition,
\psi_{(\cdot)}^{\xi}(v)
is a probability distribution on
\{+,-,?\}
. For every signed-gap realization
\theta
and every decision
d\in\{+,-,?\}
, taking
f=\widetilde{\psi}_{d}^{\xi}
in (
C.7
), the tower property guarantees:

\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}(\widehat{a}^{V}=d)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}^{\xi}[\psi_{d}^{\xi}(V)]=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}^{\xi}\big[\mathbb{E}_{\theta}^{\xi}[\widetilde{\psi}_{d}^{\xi}(\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi})\mid V]\big]=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}^{\xi}[\widetilde{\psi}_{d}^{\xi}(\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi})]=\mathbb{P}_{\theta}^{\xi}(\widehat{a}=d).

Thus, the
V
-based rule preserves all probabilities under every realization of the signed-gap
\theta
.
∎

C.4
The Resulting Bayesian Flat Sign Experiment

We are now ready to express the abstention and undetected error probability by integrating over the local subprior
Q_{\rho}
.

Recall from Lemma
C.3
that on the local coordinate space, the subprior measure factors as in (
C.4
).
Under the measure
Q_{\rho}
, conditional on
(\Xi,\Delta)
, the two signs have equal mass. The unnormalized marginal measure of the gap is
Q_{\rho}(\Delta\in\mathrm{d}\delta)=c_{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\delta
, where
c_{\rho}=2\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{\rho})
.

Define the rescaled gaps:

G:=\frac{\Delta\sqrt{T}}{2}
and
L_{T}:=\frac{\delta_{\rho}\sqrt{T}}{2}
.
Under
Q_{\rho}
, the support of the rescaled gap
G
is
[0,L_{T}]
. For any realization
G=g
on the interval
[0,L_{T}]
, the corresponding absolute gap is
\delta=\frac{2g}{\sqrt{T}}
, yielding the differential change of variables as follows:

\mathrm{d}\delta=\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\,\mathrm{d}g.

Applying Lemma
C.6
, the conditional distribution of
V
given the rescaled gap and sign is Gaussian and independent of the background oracle
\Xi
, namely,

V\mid(G=g,H=h,\Xi=\xi)\sim\mathcal{N}(hg,1).

(C.8)

This reduces the given experiment to one that has the following properties under
Q_{\rho}
:

•

The unnormalized marginal density of
G
is constant on
[0,L_{T}]
(and zero elsewhere).

•

H\in\{+1,-1\}
is uniform.

•

V\mid(G=g,H=h)\sim\mathcal{N}(hg,1)
.

As established in Lemma
C.8
, any terminal decision can be represented by a
V
-based rule. Thus it suffices to consider rules that only depend on
V
. We represent this rule via the tuple of measurable functions
\psi^{\xi}=(\psi_{+}^{\xi},\psi_{-}^{\xi},\psi_{?}^{\xi})
, mapping the scalar
v
to the probability simplex on
\{+,-,?\}
. In other words,

\psi_{+}^{\xi}(v)+\psi_{-}^{\xi}(v)+\psi_{?}^{\xi}(v)=1.

The superscript
\xi
indicates that the decision rule is allowed to depend on the revealed background oracle
\xi
. It follows that we can write

\mathbb{P}^{\xi}_{h\delta}\left(\widehat{a}^{V}=?\right)=\mathbb{E}^{\xi}_{h\delta}\left[\psi^{\xi}_{?}(V)\right]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi^{\xi}_{?}(v)\phi\left(v-h\frac{\delta\sqrt{T}}{2}\right)\mathrm{d}v

(C.9)

For ease of notation, we define the unnormalized marginal density of
V
corresponding to (
H=+1
) and (
H=-1
) sign realizations as follows:

A_{T}(v):=\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\phi(v-g)\,\mathrm{d}g,\quad\mbox{and}\quad B_{T}(v):=\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\phi(v+g)\,\mathrm{d}g.

Then we can express the total
Q_{\rho}
-abstention mass of any rule
\psi
as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{K}}\mathbb{P}_{\mu}\left(\widehat{a}^{V}=?\right)Q_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\mu)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\sum_{h\in\{+1,-1\}}\int^{\delta_{\rho}}_{0}\mathbb{P}^{\xi}_{h\delta}\left(\widehat{a}^{V}=?\right)\,\mathrm{d}\delta\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\sum_{h\in\{+1,-1\}}\int^{\delta_{\rho}}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi^{\xi}_{?}(v)\phi\left(v-h\frac{\delta\sqrt{T}}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}\delta\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{=}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)\sum_{h\in\{+1,-1\}}\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{?}^{\xi}(v)\phi(v-hg)\,\mathrm{d}v\right)\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\,\mathrm{d}g

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{?}^{\xi}(v)\bigl[A_{T}(v)+B_{T}(v)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}v\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi),

where
(a)
is due to the fair sign property and factorization of Lemma
C.3
,
(b)
is due to (
C.9
) and
(c)
is due to change of variables with
\mathrm{d}\delta=\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\,\mathrm{d}g.

Similarly, an undetected error occurs when the rule outputs
-
when
H=+1
, or outputs
+
when
H=-1
. Hence the
Q_{\rho}
-undetected error mass can be expressed as:

\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\psi)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\psi_{-}^{\xi}(v)A_{T}(v)+\psi_{+}^{\xi}(v)B_{T}(v)\right]\,\mathrm{d}v\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi).

C.5
Solving the Bayesian Flat Sign Experiment

We are now ready to solve the core optimization problem: establishing a lower bound on the undetected error probability
\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\psi)
for any terminal decision rule
\psi
that satisfies the abstention budget
\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\psi)\leq\alpha
.

Lemma C.9

(Pointwise Optimal Sign Decision)
.

For every terminal decision rule
\psi
, the undetected error mass is bounded below as:

\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\psi)\geq\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(1-\psi_{?}^{\xi}(v)\bigr)\min\{A_{T}(v),B_{T}(v)\}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi).

Proof.

Fix
(\xi,v)
. Since
\psi_{+}^{\xi}(v)+\psi_{-}^{\xi}(v)=1-\psi_{?}^{\xi}(v)
, and both
\psi_{+}^{\xi}(v)
and
\psi_{-}^{\xi}(v)
are non-negative,

\psi_{-}^{\xi}(v)A_{T}(v)+\psi_{+}^{\xi}(v)B_{T}(v)\geq\bigl(\psi_{-}^{\xi}(v)+\psi_{+}^{\xi}(v)\bigr)\min\{A_{T}(v),B_{T}(v)\}=\bigl(1-\psi_{?}^{\xi}(v)\bigr)\min\{A_{T}(v),B_{T}(v)\}.

Integrating over
v
and
\xi
proves the claim.
∎

Lemma C.10

(Uniform Central Bound)
.

Fix
\eta\in(0,1)
. For all sufficiently large
T
, the following bounds hold for all
|v|\leq\frac{L_{T}}{2}
:

A_{T}(v)+B_{T}(v)\geq 1-\eta,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\min\{A_{T}(v),B_{T}(v)\}\geq(1-\eta)\Phi(-|v|).

Proof.

First, evaluating the integrals of the standard normal density
\phi
yields:

A_{T}(v)=\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\phi(v-g)\,\mathrm{d}g=\Phi(v)-\Phi(v-L_{T}),\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;B_{T}(v)=\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\phi(v+g)\,\mathrm{d}g=\Phi(v+L_{T})-\Phi(v).

Adding these yields:

A_{T}(v)+B_{T}(v)=\Phi(v+L_{T})-\Phi(v-L_{T}).

For
|v|\leq L_{T}/2
, this domain restriction implies
v+L_{T}\geq L_{T}/2
and
v-L_{T}\leq-L_{T}/2
. Since
\Phi
is strictly increasing, we can lower bound the sum by evaluating it at these extremes:

A_{T}(v)+B_{T}(v)\geq\Phi(L_{T}/2)-\Phi(-L_{T}/2)=1-2\Phi(-L_{T}/2).

As
T\to\infty
,
L_{T}\to\infty
, so
1-2\Phi(-L_{T}/2)\to 1
. Thus, for sufficiently large
T
, the sum is bounded below by
1-\eta
.

For the second inequality, observe that
\phi
is symmetric and strictly decreasing away from zero. Thus, the relationship between
A_{T}(v)
and
B_{T}(v)
depends only on the sign of
v
. For
v\geq 0
, the integration interval for
A_{T}(v)
is
[v-L_{T},v]
, which is closer to the origin than the interval
[v,v+L_{T}]
for
B_{T}(v)
. Hence,
A_{T}(v)\geq B_{T}(v)
, yielding:

\min\{A_{T}(v),B_{T}(v)\}=B_{T}(v)=\Phi(v+L_{T})-\Phi(v)=\Phi(-v)-\Phi(-(v+L_{T}))

since
\Phi(x)=1-\Phi(-x)
.
By identical symmetric reasoning for
v<0
, we find
\min\{A_{T}(v),B_{T}(v)\}=A_{T}(v)
. Consolidating both cases yields a single identity for all
v
:

\min\{A_{T}(v),B_{T}(v)\}=\Phi(-|v|)-\Phi(-(|v|+L_{T})).

(C.10)

We wish to show that
\Phi(-|v|)-\Phi(-(|v|+L_{T}))\geq(1-\eta)\Phi(-|v|)
, which is equivalent to bounding the ratio:

\frac{\Phi(-(|v|+L_{T}))}{\Phi(-|v|)}\leq\eta.

Since
0\leq|v|\leq L_{T}/2
, the numerator is bounded above by
\Phi(-L_{T})
and the denominator is bounded below by
\Phi(-L_{T}/2)
. Therefore:

\frac{\Phi(-(|v|+L_{T}))}{\Phi(-|v|)}\leq\frac{\Phi(-L_{T})}{\Phi(-L_{T}/2)}\to 0

uniformly as
T\to\infty
. Thus, the bound holds for sufficiently large
T
.
∎

To complete the lower bound, we use the following specialized consequence of
the
Bathtub Principle
(cf. Theorem 1.14 of
22
). The following lemma states the form of this principle needed for our subsequent analysis.

Lemma C.11

(Abstention Rearrangement / Bathtub Principle)
.

Let
B>0
, let
\Lambda
be a finite measure on a space
\mathcal{O}
, and let
w(v)\geq 0
be an even function that is non-increasing in
|v|
on
[-B,B]
. Let
r:\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be measurable. If there exists some
a\in[0,B]
such that:

\int_{\mathcal{O}}\int_{-B}^{B}r(\xi,v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\Lambda(\mathrm{d}\xi)\leq 2a\,\Lambda(\mathcal{O}),

then the complementary integral is bounded below as:

\int_{\mathcal{O}}\int_{-B}^{B}(1-r(\xi,v))w(v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\Lambda(\mathrm{d}\xi)\geq 2\,\Lambda(\mathcal{O})\,\int_{a}^{B}w(u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

Proof.

Define the central cylinder set
C:=\mathcal{O}\times\{v:|v|<a\}
. The measure of
C
is
2a\,\Lambda(\mathcal{O})
.
Because
w(v)
is non-increasing in
|v|
, we have
w(v)\geq w(a)
on
C
and
w(v)\leq w(a)
on
C^{c}
. Thus, the integrand
\bigl(\mathbbm{1}_{C}(\xi,v)-r(\xi,v)\bigr)\bigl(w(v)-w(a)\bigr)
is non-negative everywhere. Consequently:

0\leq\int_{\mathcal{O}\times(-B,B)}\bigl(\mathbbm{1}_{C}-r\bigr)\bigl(w-w(a)\bigr).

Expanding this inequality gives:

\int_{\mathcal{O}\times(-B,B)}rw\leq\int_{C}w-w(a)\left(\int_{C}1-\int_{\mathcal{O}\times(-B,B)}r\right)\leq\int_{C}w,

where the final step follows because the assumption
\int_{\mathcal{O}\times(-B,B)}r\leq 2a\,\Lambda(\mathcal{O})=\int_{C}1
forces the bracketed term to be non-negative. Therefore:

\int_{\mathcal{O}\times(-B,B)}(1-r)w\geq\int_{\mathcal{O}\times(-B,B)}w-\int_{C}w=\int_{C^{c}}w=2\ \Lambda(\mathcal{O})\ \int_{a}^{B}w(u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

∎

Lemma C.12

(Flat Bayesian Sign Lower Bound)
.

Fix
\eta\in(0,1)
and suppose
\alpha<\frac{(1-\eta)c_{\rho}\delta_{\rho}}{4}
. Define the threshold parameter:

a_{T,\eta}:=\frac{\alpha\sqrt{T}}{2(1-\eta)c_{\rho}}.

(C.11)

Then, for every terminal rule
\psi
satisfying
\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\psi)\leq\alpha
, we have that as
T\to\infty
:

\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\psi)\geq(1-o_{T}(1))\frac{2(1-\eta)c_{\rho}}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{a_{T,\eta}}^{\infty}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

Proof.

By Lemma
C.10
, for all sufficiently large
T
,
A_{T}(v)+B_{T}(v)\geq 1-\eta
on the interval
|v|\leq L_{T}/2
. Given the abstention constraint
\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\psi)\leq\alpha
, we can further restrict the integration domain to this interval
[-L_{T}/2,L_{T}/2]
to obtain:

\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{|v|\leq L_{T}/2}\psi_{?}^{\xi}(v)\bigl[A_{T}(v)+B_{T}(v)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}v\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)\leq\alpha.

Substituting the uniform central bound (first bound in Lemma
C.10
) yields:

\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{|v|\leq L_{T}/2}\psi_{?}^{\xi}(v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)\leq\frac{\alpha\sqrt{T}}{2(1-\eta)}.

Because
\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{\rho})=c_{\rho}/2
, the right-hand side is
2a_{T,\eta}\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{\rho})
.

Next, we evaluate the undetected error mass. Discarding the non-negative contribution from
|v|>L_{T}/2
and applying Lemma
C.9
and the second bound of Lemma
C.10
, we obtain:

\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\psi)\geq\frac{2(1-\eta)}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{|v|\leq L_{T}/2}\bigl(1-\psi_{?}^{\xi}(v)\bigr)\Phi(-|v|)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi).

We now apply the abstention rearrangement result from Lemma
C.11
with
B=L_{T}/2
,
w(v)=\Phi(-|v|)
, and
r(\xi,v)=\psi_{?}^{\xi}(v)
. Our assumption on
\alpha
ensures that
a_{T,\eta}<L_{T}/4<L_{T}/2
. This allows the rearrangement to yield:

\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{|v|\leq L_{T}/2}\bigl(1-\psi_{?}^{\xi}(v)\bigr)\Phi(-|v|)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)\geq\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{\rho})\,2\int_{a_{T,\eta}}^{L_{T}/2}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

Substituting
\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{\rho})=c_{\rho}/2
:

\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\psi)\geq\frac{2(1-\eta)c_{\rho}}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{a_{T,\eta}}^{L_{T}/2}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

Finally, because
a_{T,\eta}<L_{T}/4
, the omitted tail
\int_{L_{T}/2}^{\infty}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u
decays exponentially faster than
\int_{a_{T,\eta}}^{\infty}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u
. Thus

\int_{a_{T,\eta}}^{L_{T}/2}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u=(1-o_{T}(1))\int_{a_{T,\eta}}^{\infty}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u,

which concludes the proof.
∎

C.6
Proof of the Lower Bound Theorem

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
\pi
be any policy satisfying the global abstention budget
\mathcal{A}_{T}(\pi)\leq\alpha
. By Lemma
C.4
, the abstention and undetected error mass of this strategy under the local subprior
Q_{\rho}
are bounded above by their true global counterparts, i.e.,

\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\pi)\leq\mathcal{A}_{T}(\pi)\leq\alpha,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\pi)\leq\mathcal{E}_{T}(\pi).

By Lemma
C.5
and Lemma
C.8
, the original strategy
\pi
induces a
V
-based terminal rule
\psi^{\xi}
in the experiment with background oracle revealed. This reduction preserves the abstention probability and ensures the induced sign-rule error is no larger than the original undetected error. Therefore

\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\psi^{\xi})\leq\alpha.

Because the abstention constraint is satisfied, we may apply Lemma
C.12
. This yields:

\mathcal{E}_{T}(\pi)\geq\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\psi^{\xi})\geq(1-o_{T}(1))\frac{2(1-\eta)c_{\rho}}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{a_{T,\eta}}^{\infty}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u,

where the lower limit of the integral
a_{T,\eta}
is defined in (
C.11
).

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
and
\eta\in(0,1)
. The construction of the local subprior gives constants
c_{\rho}>0
and
\delta_{\rho}>0
. We define the critical abstention threshold

\alpha_{0}(\rho,\eta):=\frac{(1-\eta)c_{\rho}\delta_{\rho}}{4}.

For every fixed
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0}(\rho,\eta))
, Lemma
C.12
applies. Furthermore, as
T\to\infty
,
a_{T,\eta}=\frac{\alpha\sqrt{T}}{2(1-\eta)c_{\rho}}\to\infty.

Using
\int_{a}^{\infty}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u=\phi(a)-a\Phi(-a)
and the standard Gaussian tail asymptotics:

\log\left(\int_{a}^{\infty}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u\right)=-\frac{a^{2}}{2}+O(\log a)\qquad\text{as }a\to\infty,

we extract the exponential decay rate for any feasible strategy
\pi
:

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathcal{E}_{T}(\pi)\geq-\frac{1}{8(1-\eta)^{2}c_{\rho}^{2}}.

Taking the infimum over all feasible strategies yields the lower bound on the optimal undetected error probability
\mathfrak{E}_{T}(\alpha)
. Since this bound holds for all
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0}(\rho,\eta))
, taking the limit as
\alpha\downarrow 0
:

\liminf_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathfrak{E}_{T}(\alpha)\geq-\frac{1}{8(1-\eta)^{2}c_{\rho}^{2}}\geq-\frac{1}{8(1-\eta)^{2}(1-\rho)^{4}\kappa_{\nu}^{2}},

(C.12)

where the second inequality follows because our definition of the restricted set
\mathcal{C}_{ij}
ensures that the constant satisfies
c_{\rho}\geq(1-\rho)^{2}\kappa_{\nu}
.
Because (
C.12
) holds for arbitrary
\eta\in(0,1)
and
\rho\in(0,1)
, we may take
\eta\downarrow 0
and then
\rho\downarrow 0
to obtain the desired lower bound.
∎

Appendix D
Proof of Theorem
3.3

We prove the no-abstention (forced-decision) lower bound by reusing the local subprior and
oracle reduction developed in the proof of
Theorem
3.1
. The only difference is that the
terminal rule is not allowed to abstain. Thus, in the resulting one-dimensional
flat sign experiment, the rule must always choose one of the two signs.

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
. Let
Q_{\rho}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
,
\Lambda_{\rho}
,

\delta_{\rho}
, and
c_{\rho}
be the flat local subprior objects
constructed in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. We
recall the two properties of the construction of
Q_{\rho}
. First, by Lemma
C.2
,

Q_{\rho}\leq P_{\nu}
.
Second, by Lemma
C.3
, under
Q_{\rho}
, the local coordinates

(\Xi,\Delta,H)
satisfy (
C.4
)
and

c_{\rho}:=2\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{\rho})\geq(1-\rho)^{2}\kappa_{\nu}
.
On the support of
Q_{\rho}
, the two arms in
\Xi=(i,j,x,\zeta_{-ij})
are (strictly) the top two arms.

Let
\pi
be any forced-decision policy, so

\widehat{a}_{T}\in[K]
almost surely. Since abstention never occurs,
misidentification and undetected error coincide:

\{\widehat{a}_{T}\neq a^{\star}\}=\{\widehat{a}_{T}\notin\{a^{\star},?\}\}
.
So by
Q_{\rho}\leq P_{\nu}
,

\mathbb{P}_{\nu}^{\pi}(\widehat{a}_{T}\neq a^{\star})=\int\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\pi}(\widehat{a}_{T}\neq a^{\star})P_{\nu}(\mathrm{d}\mu)\geq\int\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\pi}(\widehat{a}_{T}\notin\{a^{\star},?\})Q_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\mu):=\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\pi)\geq\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\psi^{\xi}).

(D.1)

The lower bound of the error probability in the oracle-revealed experiment provides a lower bound for the original problem. Moreover, by Lemma
C.5
, after
revealing
\Xi=\xi
it is enough to consider a strategy that samples only the
two local top arms and outputs a sign in
\{+,-\}
. By
Lemma
C.8
, the terminal rule can further be taken to
depend on the history only through the sufficient statistic

V=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}
,
where
Z_{t}
denotes the transformed observation defined in the local reduction.

Let
G:=\frac{\Delta\sqrt{T}}{2}
and
L_{T}:=\frac{\delta_{\rho}\sqrt{T}}{2}
.
Then
G
has support
[0,L_{T}]
under
Q_{\rho}
, and the flat sign experiment satisfies

V\mid(G=g,H=h,\Xi=\xi)\sim\mathcal{N}(hg,1),\qquad h\in\{+1,-1\}.

Any forced-decision terminal rule
\psi^{\xi}
satisfies

\psi_{+}^{\xi}(v)+\psi_{-}^{\xi}(v)=1,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\psi_{+}^{\xi}(v),\psi_{-}^{\xi}(v)\geq 0.

Define

A_{T}(v):=\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\phi(v-g)\,\mathrm{d}g,\quad\mbox{and}\quad B_{T}(v):=\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\phi(v+g)\,\mathrm{d}g.

Using (
C.4
) and the change of variables

\mathrm{d}\delta=2\,\mathrm{d}g/\sqrt{T}
,

\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\psi)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\psi_{-}^{\xi}(v)A_{T}(v)+\psi_{+}^{\xi}(v)B_{T}(v)\right]\,\mathrm{d}v\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi).

Since
\psi_{+}^{\xi}(v)+\psi_{-}^{\xi}(v)=1
, we have pointwise

\psi_{-}^{\xi}(v)A_{T}(v)+\psi_{+}^{\xi}(v)B_{T}(v)\geq\min\{A_{T}(v),B_{T}(v)\}.

Therefore,

\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho}}(\psi)\geq\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{\rho})\int_{\mathbb{R}}\min\{A_{T}(v),B_{T}(v)\}\,\mathrm{d}v=\frac{c_{\rho}}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\min\{A_{T}(v),B_{T}(v)\}\,\mathrm{d}v.

(D.2)

It remains to evaluate the one-dimensional integral. Recall that (
C.10
) gives

\min\{A_{T}(v),B_{T}(v)\}=\Phi(-|v|)-\Phi(-(|v|+L_{T}))
.
Consequently,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\min\{A_{T}(v),B_{T}(v)\}\,\mathrm{d}v=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\Phi(-v)-\Phi(-(v+L_{T}))\right]\,\mathrm{d}v=2\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

Since
L_{T}\to\infty
as
T\to\infty
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\min\{A_{T}(v),B_{T}(v)\}\,\mathrm{d}v\to 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

Finally,

satisfying

\bar{q}_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}\bigl(\eta(\xi,\delta,h)\bigr)=\frac{(1-\rho)\bar{f}_{i}(u)\bar{f}_{j}(u)\displaystyle\prod_{k\neq i,j}\bar{f}_{k}(\zeta_{k})}{J_{ijh}(u,\delta)}

for Lebesgue-almost every

\eta(\xi,\delta,h)\in\mathcal{S}_{i,j}^{\bar{\delta}}
.

Furthermore, the definition
(
F.9
) implies

\bar{Q}_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}\bigl(\mathcal{H}^{K}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{\rho}^{\bar{\delta}}\bigr)=0
.
Hence

\bar{q}_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}(\eta)=0
for Lebesgue-almost every

\eta\notin\mathcal{S}_{\rho}^{\bar{\delta}}
.

For

\eta=\eta(\xi,\delta,h)\in\mathcal{S}_{i,j}^{\bar{\delta}}
,
Lemma
F.1
gives

(1-\rho)\bar{f}_{i}(u)\bar{f}_{j}(u)\leq\bar{f}_{i}(\eta_{i})\bar{f}_{j}(\eta_{j})J_{ijh}(u,\delta).

Since
J_{ijh}(u,\delta)>0
and
\eta_{k}=\zeta_{k}
for
k\neq i,j
,

\displaystyle\bar{q}_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}(\eta)\leq\bar{f}_{i}(\eta_{i})\bar{f}_{j}(\eta_{j})\prod_{k\neq i,j}\bar{f}_{k}(\eta_{k})=\bar{p}(\eta).

The same inequality holds outside
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}^{\bar{\delta}}
because

\bar{q}_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}=0
there. Thus

\bar{q}_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}\leq\bar{p}
Lebesgue-almost everywhere on

\mathcal{H}^{K}
.

Consequently, for every Borel set
B\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{K}
,

\bar{Q}_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}(B)=\int_{B}\bar{q}_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}(\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta\leq\int_{B}\bar{p}(\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta=\bar{P}(B).

Finally, for every Borel set

E\subseteq(\mathcal{I})^{K}
,

\displaystyle Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}(E)=\bar{Q}_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}\bigl(s(E)\bigr)\leq\bar{P}\bigl(s(E)\bigr)=P(E).

Hence
Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}\leq P
.
∎

Lemma F.3

(Properties of
Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}
)
.

Except on the
Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}
-null tie set
\{\Delta=0\}
, let

\Xi
,
\Delta
, and
H
denote the unique background oracle, local gap coordinate, and sign associated with the representation

\mu=\mu(\Xi,\Delta,H)
. Then

Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}\left(\Xi\in\mathrm{d}\xi,\,\Delta\in\mathrm{d}\delta,\,H=h\right)=\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\delta,\qquad h\in\{+1,-1\}.

(F.10)

Consequently,

Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}\bigl(H=+1\mid\Xi=\xi,\Delta=\delta\bigr)=Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}\bigl(H=-1\mid\Xi=\xi,\Delta=\delta\bigr)=\frac{1}{2},

and

Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}(\Delta\in\mathrm{d}\delta)=c_{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\delta,\qquad 0\leq\delta\leq\bar{\delta}.

In particular,

Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}((\mathcal{I})^{K})=c_{\rho}\bar{\delta}
.

Proof.

The uniqueness of the pair follows from the strict top-two property in
Lemma
F.1
. When
\delta>0
, the sign
is determined by
h=\operatorname{sign}(\eta_{i}-\eta_{j})
, and the center
and remaining coordinates are recovered from the inverse transformation
given above. The two sign representations coincide only when
\delta=0
,
which is a
Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}
-null set. Define the coordinates
arbitrarily on this null set.

For Borel sets
C\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
and

D\subseteq[0,\bar{\delta}]
, the definition
(
F.9
) gives

\displaystyle Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}\bigl(\Xi\in C,\,\Delta\in D,\,H=h\bigr)=\int_{C}\int_{D}\mathrm{d}\delta\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)=\Lambda_{\rho}(C)\operatorname{Leb}(D).

This proves
(
F.10
). Since the two signs
have the same mass conditional on
(\Xi,\Delta)
, integrating over
\xi
and summing over the
two signs gives

Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}(\Delta\in\mathrm{d}\delta)=2\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{\rho})\,\mathrm{d}\delta=c_{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\delta.

∎

Lemma F.4

(Probability Domination Under
Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}
)
.

For any policy
\pi
, define the abstention mass

\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}}(\pi):=\int\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\pi}(\widehat{a}_{T}=?)\,Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}(\mathrm{d}\mu)

and the undetected error mass

\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}}(\pi):=\int\mathbb{P}_{\mu}^{\pi}\bigl(\widehat{a}_{T}\notin\{a^{\star}(\mu),?\}\bigr)\,Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}(\mathrm{d}\mu).

Then

\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}}(\pi)\leq\mathcal{A}_{T}(\pi),\qquad\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}}(\pi)\leq\mathcal{E}_{T}(\pi).

(F.11)

In particular, any policy satisfying

\mathcal{A}_{T}(\pi)\leq\alpha
also satisfies

\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}}(\pi)\leq\alpha
.

Proof.

Follows directly from
Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}\leq P
.
∎

F.2.2
Step 2. Oracle Reduction

Before sampling begins, we reveal the
background oracle

\Xi=(I,J,U,Z_{-IJ})
, which depends on the random means
\mu
, to the learner. Conditional on

\Xi=\xi=(i,j,u,\zeta_{-ij})
, the learner knows the identities of the
local top-two arms, the local center
u
, and the information-coordinate
values
\zeta_{k}
of all background arms. Equivalently, the learner knows
that each background arm
k\neq i,j
has quality parameter

s^{-1}(\zeta_{k})
and reward distribution
P_{s^{-1}(\zeta_{k})}
.

The only unknown local coordinates under
Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}
are

\Delta\in[0,\bar{\delta}],\qquad H\in\{+1,-1\}.

Here
H=+1
means that arm
i
is better, whereas
H=-1
means that arm

j
is better.

For ease of notation, denote the probability law of the experiment conditional on
\Xi
,
H
and
\Delta

\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi,\pi}:=\mathbb{P}_{\mu(\xi,\delta,h)}^{\pi},\qquad h\in\{+1,-1\},\quad\delta\in[0,\bar{\delta}].

Since any policy in the original experiment can be implemented in the oracle-revealed setting by simply ignoring the side information
\Xi
, any lower bound derived for this oracle-revealed experiment provides a lower bound for the original problem. Furthermore, since the background means are known, querying any arm other than
i
or
j
provides no information about
\Delta
and
H
. We show that we can restrict our analysis to strategies that only sample the top two arms and output the decision of the sign; we call these
local pair strategies
.

Lemma F.5

(Sufficiency of Local Pair Strategies)
.

Fix a realization of the background oracle
\Xi=\xi=(i,j,u,\zeta_{-ij})\in\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
. For any adaptive strategy
\pi
, there exists a modified strategy
\pi^{\prime}
that samples only arms
i
and
j
, restricts its terminal recommendation to
\widehat{a}^{\prime}_{T}\in\{+,-,?\}
, and satisfies, for every
\delta\in(0,\bar{\delta}]
and
h\in\{+1,-1\}
,

\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi,\pi^{\prime}}(\widehat{a}^{\prime}_{T}=?)=\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi,\pi}(\widehat{a}_{T}=?),\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi,\pi^{\prime}}(\widehat{a}^{\prime}_{T}=-h)\leq\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi,\pi}(\widehat{a}_{T}\notin\{a^{\star}(h),?\}),

(F.12)

where
a^{\star}(+)=i
and
a^{\star}(-)=j
. This says that the abstention probability of
\pi^{\prime}
is unchanged from that of
\pi
and the undetected error probability of
\pi^{\prime}
is less than or equal to that of
\pi
.

Proof.

Fix
\xi=(i,j,u,\zeta_{-ij})\in\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
. We construct the
modified strategy
\pi^{\prime}
that satisfies (
F.12
) by internally simulating
\pi
.

Whenever the internal copy of
\pi
samples arm
k\in\{i,j\}
, the strategy

\pi^{\prime}
samples the same arm and feeds the observed reward to the internal
copy. Whenever the internal copy samples an arm
k\notin\{i,j\}
, the
strategy
\pi^{\prime}
instead samples arm
i
, discards the resulting reward, and
feeds the internal copy an independent simulated reward drawn from
P_{s^{-1}(\zeta_{k})}
.
Conditional on
\Xi=\xi
, the quality parameter of every background arm

k\notin\{i,j\}
is known to be
s^{-1}(\zeta_{k})
, and its reward
distribution is independent of
(H,\Delta)
. Therefore, for every fixed

(h,\delta)
, the simulated reward has the same distribution as the reward
that
\pi
would have observed by sampling arm
k
in the original
experiment.

It follows inductively over the sampling rounds that the internal history
observed by
\pi
under
\pi^{\prime}
has the same distribution as the history
observed by
\pi
under

\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi,\pi}
. Consequently, the terminal output of the
internal copy has the same distribution as
\widehat{a}_{T}
under the original
strategy.

At time
T
, the strategy
\pi^{\prime}
maps the terminal output of the internal
copy according to

i\mapsto+,\qquad j\mapsto-,\qquad?\mapsto?,

and maps every recommendation
k\notin\{i,j\}
arbitrarily to one of the
two signs, say
+
. This mapping preserves the abstention event, and hence

\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi,\pi^{\prime}}(\widehat{a}^{\prime}_{T}=?)=\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi,\pi}(\widehat{a}_{T}=?).

Moreover, on the support of
Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}}
, arms
i
and
j
are
the unique top-two arms, with
a^{\star}(+)=i
and
a^{\star}(-)=j
. Therefore,
any original recommendation
k\notin\{i,j\}
is already an undetected
error. Mapping such a recommendation to a sign either preserves that error
or converts it into a correct sign decision. Since the recommendations

i
and
j
are mapped to their corresponding signs, the mapping cannot
turn a correct original recommendation into an incorrect sign decision.
Thus

\left\{\widehat{a}^{\prime}_{T}=-h\right\}\subseteq\left\{\widehat{a}_{T}\notin\{a^{\star}(h),?\}\right\}

under the coupled construction, and consequently

\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi,\pi^{\prime}}(\widehat{a}^{\prime}_{T}=-h)\leq\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi,\pi}\bigl(\widehat{a}_{T}\notin\{a^{\star}(h),?\}\bigr).

This proves (
F.12
).
∎

F.2.3
Step 3. Log-Likelihood Ratio and MLE

Fix
D>0
, to be chosen later, and set

\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}:=D\alpha,\qquad L_{T}:=\frac{\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}\sqrt{T}}{2}=\frac{D\alpha\sqrt{T}}{2}.

For all sufficiently small
\alpha
, we have

\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}\leq\delta_{\rho}
.

Fix a realization of the background oracle

\Xi=\xi=(i,j,u,\zeta_{-ij})\in\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
and a local-pair strategy

\pi
. Recall that

\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi,\pi}:=\mathbb{P}_{\mu(\xi,\delta,h)}^{\pi},\qquad h\in\{+1,-1\},\quad\delta\in[0,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}].

At
\delta=0
, the two signs induce the same parameter vector. We denote
their common law by

\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi,\pi}:=\mathbb{P}_{+1,0}^{\xi,\pi}=\mathbb{P}_{-1,0}^{\xi,\pi}
.
When the strategy is clear, we suppress the superscript
\pi
.

Consider the experiment where only two arms
i
or
j
can be sampled, let

H_{t}^{\xi}:=(\xi,A_{1},X_{1},\ldots,A_{t},X_{t})

denote the
oracle history
up to time
t
.
Let
(\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi},\mathscr{H}_{T}^{\xi})
denote the measurable space of oracle histories for this two-arm experiment. We write

\omega=(\xi,a_{1},x_{1},\ldots,a_{T},x_{T})\in\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}

for a realization, and let

\omega_{t-1}:=(\xi,a_{1},x_{1},\ldots,a_{t-1},x_{t-1})

denote its restriction to the first
t-1
rounds.

Write

\theta_{0}:=\beta(u),\qquad a_{0}:=A^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{0}).

For
h\in\{+1,-1\}
and

\delta\in[0,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}]
, define the
signed rescaled local gap

r:=h\frac{\delta\sqrt{T}}{2}\in[-L_{T},L_{T}].

The natural parameters of the two local arms are then

\theta_{i}(r)=\theta_{0}+\frac{r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}},\qquad\theta_{j}(r)=\theta_{0}-\frac{r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}.

For a realized history
\omega\in\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}
, define

S_{t}(\omega):=\begin{cases}+1,&a_{t}=i,\\
-1,&a_{t}=j.\end{cases}

Thus, under the signed rescaled gap
r
, the natural parameter of the arm
sampled at time
t
is

\theta_{0}+\frac{S_{t}(\omega)r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}
.

For
h\in\{+1,-1\}
and

\delta\in[0,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}]
, define the log-likelihood ratio of the oracle history relative to the tie law:

\ell_{T,\xi}\left(r;\omega\right)=\ell_{T,\xi}\left(h\frac{\delta\sqrt{T}}{2};\omega\right):=\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}}(\omega).

(F.13)

This definition is unambiguous at
\delta=0
because

\mathbb{P}_{+1,0}^{\xi}=\mathbb{P}_{-1,0}^{\xi}
.

Lemma F.6

(Local Likelihood Factorization)
.

For every
h\in\{+1,-1\}
and

\delta\in[0,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}]
, let

r=h\delta\sqrt{T}/2
. Then

\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi}\ll\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}
, and for

\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}
-almost every

\omega\in\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}
,

\displaystyle\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left[\frac{S_{t}(\omega)r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}t(x_{t})-A\left(\theta_{0}+\frac{S_{t}(\omega)r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}\right)+A(\theta_{0})\right].

(F.14)

Proof.

Let
\pi_{t}(a_{t}\mid\xi,\omega_{t-1})
denote the sampling kernel used by
the strategy at time
t
, evaluated at the realized history

\omega_{t-1}
. This kernel is fixed by the strategy and is the same
measurable function of the realized past under every value of

(h,\delta)
.

For a natural parameter
\theta\in\Theta
, write

p_{\theta}(x):=h(x)\exp\{\theta t(x)-A(\theta)\}.

Under
\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi}
, where

r=h\delta\sqrt{T}/2
, the density of the realized history
\omega
is

\displaystyle p_{h,\delta}^{\xi}(\omega)=\prod_{t=1}^{T}\pi_{t}(a_{t}\mid\xi,\omega_{t-1})\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_{\theta_{0}+S_{t}(\omega)r/\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}(x_{t}).

Under the
tie law
(where
\delta=0
),

p_{0}^{\xi}(\omega)=\prod_{t=1}^{T}\pi_{t}(a_{t}\mid\xi,\omega_{t-1})\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_{\theta_{0}}(x_{t}).

The sampling kernels
\pi_{t}
and carrier densities
h
cancel in the likelihood ratio.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\log\frac{p_{h,\delta}^{\xi}(\omega)}{p_{0}^{\xi}(\omega)}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left[\frac{S_{t}(\omega)r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}t(x_{t})-A\left(\theta_{0}+\frac{S_{t}(\omega)r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}\right)+A(\theta_{0})\right],

which proves
(
F.14
).

∎

We use the likelihood function to define a scalar statistic that orders full histories. For
\omega\in\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}
, let

V_{T,\xi}(\omega):=\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{|r|\leq L_{T}}\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)

(F.15)

be the bounded local maximum-likelihood estimator of the signed rescaled gap
r=h\frac{\delta\sqrt{T}}{2}
.
Differentiating (
F.14
) gives

\displaystyle\ell_{T,\xi}^{\prime}(r;\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}S_{t}(\omega)\left[t(x_{t})-A^{\prime}\left(\theta_{0}+\frac{S_{t}(\omega)r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}\right)\right],

and

-\ell_{T,\xi}^{\prime\prime}(r;\omega)=\frac{1}{Ta_{0}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}A^{\prime\prime}\left(\theta_{0}+\frac{S_{t}(\omega)r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}\right)>0.

(F.16)

Hence
\ell_{T,\xi}(\cdot;\omega)
is strictly concave on

[-L_{T},L_{T}]
, and its maximizer is unique. Since

\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)
is measurable in
\omega
and continuous in
r
,
the map
\omega\mapsto V_{T,\xi}(\omega)
is measurable.

F.2.4
Step 4. The Resulting Bayesian Flat Sign Experiment

Recall that we defined

G:=\frac{\Delta\sqrt{T}}{2},\qquad L_{T}:=\frac{\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}\sqrt{T}}{2}.

By the coordinate factorization of
Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}
and the
change of variables

g=\frac{\delta\sqrt{T}}{2},\qquad\delta=\frac{2g}{\sqrt{T}},\qquad\mathrm{d}\delta=\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\,\mathrm{d}g,

we have

Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}\left(\Xi\in\mathrm{d}\xi,\,G\in\mathrm{d}g,\,H=h\right)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)\,\mathrm{d}g,\qquad h\in\{+1,-1\},

for
g\in[0,L_{T}]
.

Fix
\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
. Recall
(\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi},\mathscr{H}_{T}^{\xi})
is the oracle history space, and

\omega\in\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}
denotes a realization. For every

E\in\mathscr{H}_{T}^{\xi}
, define the
unnormalized marginal measures of the history
, corresponding to two values of the sign
h
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{T,\xi}(E):=\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\mathbb{P}_{+1,\,2g/\sqrt{T}}^{\xi}(E)\,\mathrm{d}g,\quad\mathbb{B}_{T,\xi}(E):=\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\mathbb{P}_{-1,\,2g/\sqrt{T}}^{\xi}(E)\,\mathrm{d}g.

(F.17)

Let

\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}:=\mathbb{A}_{T,\xi}+\mathbb{B}_{T,\xi}.

In particular,

\mathbb{A}_{T,\xi}(\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi})=\mathbb{B}_{T,\xi}(\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi})=L_{T},\qquad\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}(\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi})=2L_{T}.

and define their
overlap measure

\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}:=\mathbb{A}_{T,\xi}\wedge\mathbb{B}_{T,\xi}

by

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}}:=\min\left\{\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{A}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}},\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{B}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}}\right\}.

Recall that, from (
F.13
), for
g\in[0,L_{T}]
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{+1,\,2g/\sqrt{T}}^{\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}}(\omega)=\exp\{\ell_{T,\xi}(g;\omega)\},

whereas

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{-1,\,2g/\sqrt{T}}^{\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}}(\omega)=\exp\{\ell_{T,\xi}(-g;\omega)\}.

Tonelli’s theorem therefore gives

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{A}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\exp\{\ell_{T,\xi}(g;\omega)\}\,\mathrm{d}g=\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\exp\{\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)\}\,\mathrm{d}r

(F.18)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{B}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\exp\{\ell_{T,\xi}(-g;\omega)\}\,\mathrm{d}g=\int_{-L_{T}}^{0}\exp\{\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)\}\,\mathrm{d}r.

Consequently,

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}}(\omega)=\int_{-L_{T}}^{L_{T}}\exp\{\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)\}\,\mathrm{d}r,

and

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{0}^{\xi}}(\omega)=\min\left\{\int_{0}^{L_{T}}e^{\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}r,\,\int_{-L_{T}}^{0}e^{\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}r\right\}.

In particular, wherever the denominator is positive,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}}(\omega)=\frac{\min\left\{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L_{T}}e^{\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}r,\,\displaystyle\int_{-L_{T}}^{0}e^{\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}r\right\}}{\displaystyle\int_{-L_{T}}^{L_{T}}e^{\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}r}.

(F.19)

Let

\psi^{\xi}=\bigl(\psi_{+}^{\xi},\psi_{-}^{\xi},\psi_{?}^{\xi}\bigr)

denote the randomized terminal rule of an oracle local-pair strategy. Thus,
for every
\omega\in\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}
,

\psi_{+}^{\xi}(\omega)+\psi_{-}^{\xi}(\omega)+\psi_{?}^{\xi}(\omega)=1,

where
\psi_{+}^{\xi}(\omega)
,
\psi_{-}^{\xi}(\omega)
, and

\psi_{?}^{\xi}(\omega)
are the probabilities of recommending
+
,
recommending
-
, and abstaining after observing the history
\omega
.

Lemma F.7

(Risk Representation in the Flat Sign Experiment)
.

For every terminal rule
\psi
, its abstention mass
under
Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}
is

\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}}(\psi)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}}\psi_{?}^{\xi}(\omega)\,\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi).

(F.20)

Its undetected error mass is

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}}(\psi)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\bigg[\int_{\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}}\psi_{-}^{\xi}(\omega)\,\mathbb{A}_{T,\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)+\int_{\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}}\psi_{+}^{\xi}(\omega)\,\mathbb{B}_{T,\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\bigg]\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi).

(F.21)

Moreover,

\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}}(\psi)\geq\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}}\bigl(1-\psi_{?}^{\xi}(\omega)\bigr)\,\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi).

(F.22)

Proof.

For fixed
(\xi,\delta,h)
, the conditional abstention probability induced
by
\psi^{\xi}
is

\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi}(\widehat{a}_{T}=?)=\int_{\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}}\psi_{?}^{\xi}(\omega)\,\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega).

(F.23)

Hence we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathcal{I})^{K}}\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(\widehat{a}_{T}=?)Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}(\mathrm{d}\mu)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\sum_{h\in\{+1,-1\}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{0}^{\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi}(\widehat{a}_{T}=?)\,\mathrm{d}\delta\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}\sum_{h\in\{+1,-1\}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{0}^{\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}}\psi_{?}^{\xi}(\omega)\,\mathbb{P}_{h,\delta}^{\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\delta\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi).

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{=}}\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}}\psi_{?}^{\xi}(\omega)\,\mathbb{P}_{+1,\,2g/\sqrt{T}}^{\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\,\mathrm{d}g\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}}\psi_{?}^{\xi}(\omega)\,\mathbb{P}_{-1,\,2g/\sqrt{T}}^{\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\,\mathrm{d}g\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi).

where
(a)
is due to the coordinate factorization of
Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}
(
F.10
),
(b)
is due to (
F.23
) and
(c)
is due to the change of variable with
g=\delta\sqrt{T}/2
separately for the two signs. Hence by the definitions of
\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}
, this is (
F.20
).

An undetected sign error occurs when the rule recommends
-
under

H=+1
, or recommends
+
under
H=-1
. Repeating similar arguments as for (
F.20
) with the two error events gives (
F.21
). Note the tie case
\delta=0
does not
affect the calculation because it is null with respect to the

\delta
-integration.

To prove the lower bound, write

a_{T,\xi}:=\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{A}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}}
,
and

b_{T,\xi}:=\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{B}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}}
.
Since

\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}=\mathbb{A}_{T,\xi}+\mathbb{B}_{T,\xi}
,

a_{T,\xi}(\omega)+b_{T,\xi}(\omega)=1\qquad\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}\text{-a.e.}

For
\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}
-almost every
\omega
,

\displaystyle\psi_{-}^{\xi}(\omega)a_{T,\xi}(\omega)+\psi_{+}^{\xi}(\omega)b_{T,\xi}(\omega)

\displaystyle\geq\bigl(\psi_{-}^{\xi}(\omega)+\psi_{+}^{\xi}(\omega)\bigr)\min\{a_{T,\xi}(\omega),b_{T,\xi}(\omega)\}

\displaystyle=\bigl(1-\psi_{?}^{\xi}(\omega)\bigr)\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}}(\omega).

Integrating with respect to
\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}
, and then with respect to

\Lambda_{\rho}
, proves
(
F.22
).
∎

In particular, if

\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}}(\psi)\leq\alpha,

then (
F.20
) implies

\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}}\psi_{?}^{\xi}(\omega)\,\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)\leq\frac{\alpha\sqrt{T}}{2}.

(F.24)

Thus the remaining problem is to study, as functions of the full history

\omega
, the total unnormalized marginal measure of the history
\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}
and the overlap ratio

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}}(\omega)
.
Next, we compare this overlap ratio with

\Phi(-|V_{T,\xi}(\omega)|)
and use the local MLE

V_{T,\xi}(\omega)
in the bathtub argument.

F.2.5
Step 5. Solving the Bayesian Flat Sign Experiment

Intuitively, the measures
\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}
and
\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}
determine,
respectively, the available abstention mass and the unavoidable undetected error mass. We now compare these measures with their Gaussian analogues through the
local MLE
V_{T,\xi}
. We use the abbreviations

S_{t}:=S_{t}(H_{T}^{\xi}),\qquad\ell_{T,\xi}(r):=\ell_{T,\xi}(r;H_{T}^{\xi}),\qquad V_{T,\xi}:=V_{T,\xi}(H_{T}^{\xi}).

For
r\in[-L_{T},L_{T}]
, define

\mathrm{sgn}(r):=\begin{cases}+1,&r\geq 0,\\
-1,&r<0,\end{cases}\qquad\delta_{T}(r):=\frac{2|r|}{\sqrt{T}}.

At
r=0
, the choice of
\mathrm{sgn}(r)
is immaterial because both signs induce the
same tie law.

Lemma F.8

(Uniform Curvature and Local-MLE Tightness)
.

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
and
D<\infty
. There exist constants

\alpha_{0}>0
and
C,C_{1}<\infty
such that, for every

0<\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}
, the following statements hold uniformly over

\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
and all local pair strategies.

First, for every history realization
\omega
,

1-C\alpha\leq-\ell_{T,\xi}^{\prime\prime}(r;\omega)\leq 1+C\alpha,\qquad|r|\leq L_{T}.

(F.25)

In particular,
\ell_{T,\xi}(\cdot;\omega)
is strictly concave on

[-L_{T},L_{T}]
, and the local MLE
V_{T,\xi}(\omega)
is unique.

Second, for every
R>0
and every
T
such that
R\leq L_{T}/2
,

\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\sup_{|r|\leq L_{T}/2}\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left(|V_{T,\xi}-r|>R\right)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{R^{2}}.

(F.26)

Proof.

Fix
\xi=(i,j,u,\zeta_{-ij})\in\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
, and recall that

\theta_{0}=\beta(u)

and

a_{0}=A^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{0})
.
By (
F.16
),

-\ell_{T,\xi}^{\prime\prime}(r;\omega)=\frac{1}{Ta_{0}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}A^{\prime\prime}\left(\theta_{0}+\frac{S_{t}(\omega)r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}\right).

Because
\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
is compact, the centers
\theta_{0}=\beta(u)

range over a compact subset of
\Theta
, and
\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}a_{0}>0
.
Moreover, for
|r|\leq L_{T}
,

\left|\frac{r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}\right|\leq C_{D}\alpha

uniformly over
\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
. Thus, for all sufficiently small

\alpha
, every perturbed natural parameter

\theta_{0}+\frac{S_{t}(\omega)r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}

belongs to a common compact subset of
\Theta
. Since
A^{\prime\prime}
is locally
Lipschitz on this compact set, there exists
C<\infty
such that

\left|\frac{A^{\prime\prime}\left(\theta_{0}+\frac{S_{t}(\omega)r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}\right)}{A^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{0})}-1\right|\leq C\alpha.

Averaging over
t
proves
(
F.25
). By decreasing
\alpha_{0}
if
necessary, we may assume
C\alpha_{0}<1
, so the likelihood is strictly
concave.

We next prove (
F.26
). Fix

r\in[-L_{T}/2,L_{T}/2]
and consider the experiment under

\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}
. Define the score at the true signed
parameter by

U_{T,\xi}(r):=\ell_{T,\xi}^{\prime}(r)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}D_{t}(r),

where

D_{t}(r):=\frac{S_{t}}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}\left[t(X_{t})-A^{\prime}\left(\theta_{0}+\frac{S_{t}r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}\right)\right].

Let

\mathcal{G}_{t}^{\xi}:=\sigma\bigl(\xi,A_{1},X_{1},\ldots,A_{t-1},X_{t-1},A_{t}\bigr)

denote the information available after choosing
A_{t}
but before observing

X_{t}
. Conditional on
\mathcal{G}_{t}^{\xi}
, the reward has natural parameter

\theta_{0}+\frac{S_{t}r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}.

Therefore,

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left[D_{t}(r)\mid\mathcal{G}_{t}^{\xi}\right]=0

and

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left[D_{t}(r)^{2}\mid\mathcal{G}_{t}^{\xi}\right]=\frac{A^{\prime\prime}\left(\theta_{0}+\frac{S_{t}r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}\right)}{Ta_{0}}.

Moreover, for
s<t
, the variable
D_{s}(r)
is

\mathcal{G}_{t}^{\xi}
-measurable. Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left[D_{s}(r)D_{t}(r)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left[D_{s}(r)\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left[D_{t}(r)\mid\mathcal{G}_{t}^{\xi}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=0.

Thus the martingale increments are orthogonal in
L^{2}
, and therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left[U_{T,\xi}(r)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left[D_{t}(r)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left[D_{t}(r)^{2}\mid\mathcal{G}_{t}^{\xi}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left[\frac{1}{Ta_{0}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}A^{\prime\prime}\left(\theta_{0}+\frac{S_{t}r}{\sqrt{Ta_{0}}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left[-\ell_{T,\xi}^{\prime\prime}(r)\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}1+C\alpha,

where
(a)
follows from
(
F.16
), and
(b)
follows from (
F.25
).

Let
R\leq L_{T}/2
. If
V_{T,\xi}\geq r+R
, then concavity implies

\ell_{T,\xi}^{\prime}(r+R)\geq 0
. Hence,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{T,\xi}^{\prime}(r+R)

\displaystyle=\ell_{T,\xi}^{\prime}(r)+\int_{r}^{r+R}\ell_{T,\xi}^{\prime\prime}(v)\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leq U_{T,\xi}(r)-(1-C\alpha)R,

so that

U_{T,\xi}(r)\geq(1-C\alpha)R.

Similarly,

V_{T,\xi}\leq r-R\quad\Longrightarrow\quad U_{T,\xi}(r)\leq-(1-C\alpha)R.

Consequently,

|V_{T,\xi}-r|>R\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|U_{T,\xi}(r)|\geq(1-C\alpha)R.

Chebyshev’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left(|V_{T,\xi}-r|>R\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+C\alpha}{(1-C\alpha)^{2}R^{2}}.

Since
\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}
, the right-hand side is at most
C_{1}/R^{2}

for a constant
C_{1}<\infty
independent of
\xi
,
T
,
r
, and the
sampling strategy.
∎

Lemma F.9

(Uniform Local Likelihood Curve Comparison)
.

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
,
D<\infty
, and

\varepsilon\in(0,1/10)
. Let

a_{T}:=\frac{\alpha\sqrt{T}}{2(1-3\varepsilon)c_{\rho}}
,
and assume

D>\frac{4}{(1-3\varepsilon)c_{\rho}}
,
so that

L_{T}=\frac{D\alpha\sqrt{T}}{2}>4a_{T}
.

For all sufficiently small
\alpha
, and then all sufficiently large
T
,
the following statements hold uniformly over

\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
and all nonanticipating local-pair sampling
strategies.

First,

\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}\left(|V_{T,\xi}|\leq a_{T}\right)\geq 2(1-\varepsilon)a_{T}.

(F.27)

Second,

\displaystyle\int_{\{a_{T}<|V_{T,\xi}|\leq L_{T}/2\}}\Phi\bigl(-|V_{T,\xi}(\omega)|\bigr)\,\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\geq\exp\{-o_{T}(\alpha^{2}T)\}2(1-\varepsilon)\int_{a_{T}}^{L_{T}/2}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

(F.28)

Third, on the central history set

\{\omega:|V_{T,\xi}(\omega)|\leq L_{T}/2\}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}}(\omega)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\exp\{-\varepsilon\alpha^{2}T-o_{T}(\alpha^{2}T)\}\Phi\bigl(-|V_{T,\xi}(\omega)|\bigr),\qquad\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}\text{-a.e.}

(F.29)

The
o_{T}(\alpha^{2}T)
terms are uniform over

\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\rho}
and all nonanticipating local-pair sampling
strategies.

Proof.

Recall we have established that, for every measurable (history event)
E\in\mathscr{H}^{\xi}_{T}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}(E)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\mathbb{P}_{+1,\,2g/\sqrt{T}}^{\xi}(E)\,\mathrm{d}g+\int_{0}^{L_{T}}\mathbb{P}_{-1,\,2g/\sqrt{T}}^{\xi}(E)\,\mathrm{d}g

(F.30)

\displaystyle=\int_{-L_{T}}^{L_{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}(E)\,\mathrm{d}r.

Choose
R>0
sufficiently large that

\frac{C_{1}}{R^{2}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
,
where
C_{1}
is the constant in
Lemma
F.8
. For all sufficiently
large
T
, we have
R<a_{T}
and
R<L_{T}/2
. Also recall
L_{T}>4a_{T}
, hence
[-a_{T}+R,a_{T}-R]\subset[-L_{T},L_{T}]
. And on this interval
|r|\leq a_{T}-R
, hence
\{|V_{T,\xi}-r|\leq R\}\subset\{|V_{T,\xi}|\leq a_{T}\}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}\left(|V_{T,\xi}|\leq a_{T}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{-L_{T}}^{L_{T}}\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left(|V_{T,\xi}|\leq a_{T}\right)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\geq\int_{-a_{T}+R}^{a_{T}-R}\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left(|V_{T,\xi}-r|\leq R\right)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\geq(2a_{T}-2R)\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right).

Since
a_{T}\to\infty
for every fixed
\alpha>0
, the final expression is
at least
2(1-\varepsilon)a_{T}
for all sufficiently large
T
. This proves
(
F.27
).

For the second (weighted) bound, suppose first that
|V_{T,\xi}-r|\leq R
and
r\in\left[a_{T}+2R,\,\frac{L_{T}}{2}-R\right]
.
Then

a_{T}<V_{T,\xi}\leq\frac{L_{T}}{2}
,
and hence

\Phi(-|V_{T,\xi}|)\geq\Phi(-(r+R))
.
Similarly, if
|V_{T,\xi}-r|\leq R
and
r\in\left[-\frac{L_{T}}{2}+R,\,-a_{T}-2R\right]
,
then

a_{T}<|V_{T,\xi}|\leq\frac{L_{T}}{2}
,
and

\Phi(-|V_{T,\xi}|)\geq\Phi(-(|r|+R))
.
Using (
F.30
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\{a_{T}<|V_{T,\xi}|\leq L_{T}/2\}}\Phi\bigl(-|V_{T,\xi}(\omega)|\bigr)\,\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{T}}\mathbbm{1}\left\{a_{T}<|V_{T,\xi}(\omega)|\leq\frac{L_{T}}{2}\right\}\Phi\bigl(-|V_{T,\xi}(\omega)|\bigr)\int_{-L_{T}}^{L_{T}}\mathrm{d}r\,\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{-L_{T}}^{L_{T}}\int_{\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{T}}\mathbbm{1}\left\{a_{T}<|V_{T,\xi}(\omega)|\leq\frac{L_{T}}{2}\right\}\Phi\bigl(-|V_{T,\xi}(\omega)|\bigr)\,\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\geq\int_{a_{T}+2R}^{L_{T}/2-R}\int_{\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{T}}\mathbbm{1}\left\{|V_{T,\xi}(\omega)-r|\leq R\right\}\Phi(-(r+R))\,\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle+\int_{-L_{T}/2+R}^{-a_{T}-2R}\int_{\mathcal{H}^{\xi}_{T}}\mathbbm{1}\left\{|V_{T,\xi}(\omega)-r|\leq R\right\}\Phi(-(|r|+R))\,\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{a_{T}+2R}^{L_{T}/2-R}\Phi(-(r+R))\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left(|V_{T,\xi}-r|\leq R\right)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle+\int_{-L_{T}/2+R}^{-a_{T}-2R}\Phi(-(|r|+R))\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left(|V_{T,\xi}-r|\leq R\right)\,\mathrm{d}r.

By (
F.26
),

\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{sgn}(r),\,\delta_{T}(r)}^{\xi}\left(|V_{T,\xi}-r|\leq R\right)\geq 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}

throughout both integration intervals. Then, with
the change of variables
u=-r
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\{a_{T}<|V_{T,\xi}|\leq L_{T}/2\}}\Phi\bigl(-|V_{T,\xi}(\omega)|\bigr)\,\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)

\displaystyle\geq 2\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)\int_{a_{T}+2R}^{L_{T}/2-R}\Phi(-(r+R))\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=2\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)\int_{a_{T}+3R}^{L_{T}/2}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

Since
R
is fixed,
a_{T}\asymp\alpha\sqrt{T}
, and

L_{T}/2>2a_{T}
, Gaussian tail asymptotics (
\Phi(-x)=\frac{\phi(x)}{x}(1+O(x^{-2)})
and
\int^{\infty}_{x}\Phi(-u)\mathrm{d}u=\phi(x)-x\Phi(-x)
) imply

\log\frac{\displaystyle\int_{a_{T}+3R}^{L_{T}/2}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u}{\displaystyle\int_{a_{T}}^{L_{T}/2}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u}=-3Ra_{T}+O(1+\log a_{T})=-o_{T}(\alpha^{2}T)

for every fixed
\alpha>0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\{a_{T}<|V_{T,\xi}|\leq L_{T}/2\}}\Phi\bigl(-|V_{T,\xi}(\omega)|\bigr)\,\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\geq\exp\{-o_{T}(\alpha^{2}T)\}2(1-\varepsilon)\int_{a_{T}}^{L_{T}/2}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u,

which proves (
F.28
).

It remains to prove the third overlap bound. Fix a history realization
\omega
, and for ease of notation define
v:=V_{T,\xi}(\omega)
. Assume
|v|\leq L_{T}/2
.
Since
v
is an interior maximizer,

\ell_{T,\xi}^{\prime}(v;\omega)=0.

By (
F.25
) in
Lemma
F.8
, for every

r\in[-L_{T},L_{T}]
,

\displaystyle\ell_{T,\xi}(v;\omega)-\frac{1+C\alpha}{2}(r-v)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)

(F.31)

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{T,\xi}(v;\omega)-\frac{1-C\alpha}{2}(r-v)^{2}.

Recall from (
F.19
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}}(\omega)=\frac{\min\left\{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L_{T}}e^{\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}r,\,\displaystyle\int_{-L_{T}}^{0}e^{\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}r\right\}}{\displaystyle\int_{-L_{T}}^{L_{T}}e^{\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}r}.

Suppose first that
v\geq 0
. By the lower bound in
(
F.31
),

\displaystyle\min\left\{\int_{0}^{L_{T}}e^{\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}r,\,\int_{-L_{T}}^{0}e^{\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}r\right\}

\displaystyle\geq e^{\ell_{T,\xi}(v;\omega)}\min\left\{\int_{0}^{L_{T}}e^{-(1+C\alpha)(r-v)^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}r,\,\int_{-L_{T}}^{0}e^{-(1+C\alpha)(r-v)^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}r\right\}

\displaystyle\geq e^{\ell_{T,\xi}(v;\omega)}\int_{-L_{T}}^{0}\exp\left\{-\frac{1+C\alpha}{2}(r-v)^{2}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}r.

By the upper bound in
(
F.31
),

\displaystyle\int_{-L_{T}}^{L_{T}}e^{\ell_{T,\xi}(r;\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq e^{\ell_{T,\xi}(v;\omega)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{1-C\alpha}{2}(r-v)^{2}\right\}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=e^{\ell_{T,\xi}(v;\omega)}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{1-C\alpha}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}}(\omega)

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\frac{1-C\alpha}{1+C\alpha}}\left[\Phi\left(-\sqrt{1+C\alpha}\,v\right)-\Phi\left(-\sqrt{1+C\alpha}\,(L_{T}+v)\right)\right].

The case
v\leq 0
is symmetric and yields the same bound with
|v|
in
place of
v
.

Since
\Phi(-x)
is decreasing, uniformly over
|v|\leq L_{T}/2
,

\frac{\Phi\left(-\sqrt{1+C\alpha}(L_{T}+|v|)\right)}{\Phi\left(-\sqrt{1+C\alpha}|v|\right)}\leq\frac{\Phi\left(-\sqrt{1+C\alpha}L_{T}\right)}{\Phi\left(-\sqrt{1+C\alpha}L_{T}/2\right)}=o_{T}(1).

Moreover, note
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\log\Phi(-t)=-\frac{\phi(t)}{\Phi(-t)}
. By Mills ratio bound
\Phi(-t)\geq\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\phi(t)
, we have

\displaystyle\log\frac{\Phi\left(-\sqrt{1+C\alpha}\,v\right)}{\Phi\left(-v\right)}

\displaystyle=-\int^{\sqrt{1+C\alpha}\,v}_{v}\frac{\phi(t)}{\Phi(-t)}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq-\int^{\sqrt{1+C\alpha}\,v}_{v}(t+\frac{1}{t})\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\frac{1+C\alpha-1}{2}v^{2}-\log\left(\sqrt{1+C\alpha}\right)

And note
(1+C\alpha)^{-1/2}\geq 1-\frac{C}{2}\alpha
. Hence there exists
C_{2}=C/2<\infty
such that, for all sufficiently small

\alpha
and all
v>0
,

\Phi\left(-\sqrt{1+C\alpha}\,v\right)\geq(1-C_{2}\alpha)\exp\{-C_{2}\alpha v^{2}\}\Phi(-v).

Finally, uniformly over
|v|\leq L_{T}/2
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}}(\omega)

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\frac{1-C\alpha}{1+C\alpha}}\left[\Phi\left(-\sqrt{1+C\alpha}\,|v|\right)-\Phi\left(-\sqrt{1+C\alpha}\,(L_{T}+|v|)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1-C\alpha}{1+C\alpha}}\Phi\left(-\sqrt{1+C\alpha}\,|v|\right)\left[1-\frac{\Phi\left(-\sqrt{1+C\alpha}\,(L_{T}+|v|)\right)}{\Phi\left(-\sqrt{1+C\alpha}\,|v|\right)}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-o_{T}(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{1-C\alpha}{1+C\alpha}}(1-C_{2}\alpha)\exp\{-C_{2}\alpha v^{2}\}\Phi(-|v|).

Moreover, for all sufficiently small
\alpha
, there exists

C_{3}<\infty
such that

\sqrt{\frac{1-C\alpha}{1+C\alpha}}\geq 1-C_{3}\alpha
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}}(\omega)

\displaystyle\geq(1-o_{T}(1))(1-C_{2}\alpha)(1-C_{3}\alpha)\exp\left\{-C_{2}\alpha v^{2}\right\}\Phi\left(-|v|\right).

Since

|v|\leq\frac{L_{T}}{2}=\frac{D\alpha\sqrt{T}}{4}
,
there exists
C_{4}<\infty
such that

C_{2}\alpha v^{2}\leq C_{4}D^{2}\alpha^{3}T
.
Choose
\alpha
sufficiently small such that

C_{4}D^{2}\alpha\leq\varepsilon
.
Then

\exp\{-C_{2}\alpha v^{2}\}\geq\exp\left\{-\varepsilon\alpha^{2}T\right\}.

Shrinking
\alpha
further and then taking
T
sufficiently large to obtain
(1-o_{T}(1))(1-C_{2}\alpha)(1-C_{3}\alpha)>(1-\varepsilon)
. This proves
(
F.29
).
∎

Lemma F.10

(Bathtub Principle under an Arbitrary Measure)
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a finite measure, let
V
be a measurable real-valued
statistic, and let
w:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
be even and nonincreasing in

|v|
. Fix
0<a<B
, and let
\varphi
be measurable with

0\leq\varphi\leq 1
. If

\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\leq b\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{m}(|V|\leq a)\geq b,

then

\int_{\{|V|\leq B\}}(1-\varphi)w(V)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\geq\int_{\{a<|V|\leq B\}}w(V)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}.

(F.32)

Proof.

Let

C:=\{|V|\leq a\},\qquad D:=\{|V|\leq B\}.

Since
w(V)\leq w(a)
on
D\setminus C
,

\displaystyle\int_{D}\varphi w(V)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{C}\varphi w(V)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}+w(a)\int_{D\setminus C}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}.

The budget and central-mass assumptions imply

\displaystyle\int_{D\setminus C}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{C^{c}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{m}(C)-\int_{C}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{C}(1-\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}.

Since
w(V)\geq w(a)
on
C
,

w(a)\int_{C}(1-\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\leq\int_{C}(1-\varphi)w(V)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{D}\varphi w(V)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{C}\varphi w(V)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}+\int_{C}(1-\varphi)w(V)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{C}w(V)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{D}(1-\varphi)w(V)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{D}w(V)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}-\int_{D}\varphi w(V)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{D}w(V)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}-\int_{C}w(V)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{D\setminus C}w(V)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m},

which proves
(
F.32
).
∎

Lemma F.11

(Flat Local Sign Lower Bound)
.

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/10)
, and choose

D>\frac{4}{(1-3\varepsilon)c_{\rho}}
.
Set

\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}:=D\alpha,\qquad L_{T}:=\frac{D\alpha\sqrt{T}}{2},\qquad a_{T}:=\frac{\alpha\sqrt{T}}{2(1-3\varepsilon)c_{\rho}}.

For all sufficiently small
\alpha
, and then all sufficiently large
T
,
every oracle local-pair terminal rule
\psi
satisfying

\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}}(\psi)\leq\alpha

must satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}}(\psi)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\{-\varepsilon\alpha^{2}T-o_{T}(\alpha^{2}T)\}\frac{2(1-\varepsilon)^{2}c_{\rho}}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{a_{T}}^{\infty}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

(F.33)

Proof.

Define a finite measure
\mathfrak{m}_{T}
by

\displaystyle\int F(\xi,\omega)\,\mathfrak{m}_{T}(\mathrm{d}\xi,\mathrm{d}\omega)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}}F(\xi,\omega)\,\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)

for every nonnegative measurable function
F
. Let

V(\xi,\omega):=V_{T,\xi}(\omega),\qquad\varphi(\xi,\omega):=\psi_{?}^{\xi}(\omega).

By the abstention representation
(
F.20
), the constraint

\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}}(\psi)\leq\alpha
implies

\int\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{T}\leq\frac{\alpha\sqrt{T}}{2}.

On the other hand, by
(
F.27
) and

\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{\rho})=c_{\rho}/2
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{T}(|V|\leq a_{T})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}\left(|V_{T,\xi}|\leq a_{T}\right)\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle\geq 2(1-\varepsilon)a_{T}\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{\rho})

\displaystyle=(1-\varepsilon)c_{\rho}a_{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1-3\varepsilon}\frac{\alpha\sqrt{T}}{2}

\displaystyle>\frac{\alpha\sqrt{T}}{2}.

Intuitively, this means the central set
\{|V|\leq a_{T}\}
has enough

\mathfrak{m}_{T}
-mass to contain the entire abstention budget.

By the overlap lower bound
(
F.22
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}}(\psi)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{T}^{\xi}}\bigl(1-\psi_{?}^{\xi}(\omega)\bigr)\,\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi).

Restricting the integral to

\{|V_{T,\xi}|\leq L_{T}/2\}
and applying
(
F.29
) yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}}(\psi)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{\{|V_{T,\xi}|\leq L_{T}/2\}}\bigl(1-\psi_{?}^{\xi}(\omega)\bigr)\,\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{\{|V_{T,\xi}|\leq L_{T}/2\}}\bigl(1-\psi_{?}^{\xi}(\omega)\bigr)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{N}_{T,\xi}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}}\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2(1-\varepsilon)}{\sqrt{T}}\exp\{-\varepsilon\alpha^{2}T-o_{T}(\alpha^{2}T)\}\int_{\{|V|\leq L_{T}/2\}}(1-\varphi)\Phi(-|V|)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{T}.

Apply
Lemma
F.10
with

w(v):=\Phi(-|v|),\qquad a=a_{T},\qquad B=L_{T}/2,\qquad b=\frac{\alpha\sqrt{T}}{2}.

The budget and central-mass conditions were verified above. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\{|V|\leq L_{T}/2\}}(1-\varphi)\Phi(-|V|)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{T}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\{a_{T}<|V|\leq L_{T}/2\}}\Phi(-|V|)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{T}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\rho}}\int_{\{a_{T}<|V_{T,\xi}|\leq L_{T}/2\}}\Phi\bigl(-|V_{T,\xi}(\omega)|\bigr)\,\mathbb{M}_{T,\xi}(\mathrm{d}\omega)\,\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\xi)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\geq}}\exp\{-o_{T}(\alpha^{2}T)\}2(1-\varepsilon)\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{\rho})\int_{a_{T}}^{L_{T}/2}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\exp\{-o_{T}(\alpha^{2}T)\}(1-\varepsilon)c_{\rho}\int_{a_{T}}^{L_{T}/2}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

where
(a)
is by (
F.28
) and

\Lambda_{\rho}(\mathcal{C}_{\rho})=c_{\rho}/2
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}}(\psi)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\{-\varepsilon\alpha^{2}T-o_{T}(\alpha^{2}T)\}\frac{2(1-\varepsilon)^{2}c_{\rho}}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{a_{T}}^{L_{T}/2}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

Finally, the choice of
D
implies

\frac{L_{T}}{2}>2a_{T}
.
Since
a_{T}\to\infty
for every fixed
\alpha>0
, Gaussian tail asymptotics
give

\int_{a_{T}}^{L_{T}/2}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u=(1-o_{T}(1))\int_{a_{T}}^{\infty}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u.

Absorbing the factor
1-o_{T}(1)
into

\exp\{-o_{T}(\alpha^{2}T)\}
proves
(
F.33
).
∎

F.2.6
Step 6. Conclusion

Let
\pi
be any original adaptive policy satisfying

\mathcal{A}_{T}(\pi)\leq\alpha.

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/10)
. Choose
D>\frac{4}{(1-3\varepsilon)c_{\rho}}

and set
\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}:=D\alpha
.

For all sufficiently small
\alpha
,
\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}\leq\delta_{\rho}
,
so
Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}
is well-defined.

By (
F.11
),

\mathcal{A}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}}(\pi)\leq\mathcal{A}_{T}(\pi)\leq\alpha,

and

\mathcal{E}_{T}(\pi)\geq\mathcal{E}_{T}^{Q_{\rho,\bar{\delta}_{\alpha}}}(\pi).

After revealing the background oracle and applying
Lemma
F.5
, the original policy induces an
oracle local-pair sign rule whose abstention probability is at most
\alpha
, and
whose sign-error probability is no larger than the original undetected-error probability.
Therefore Lemma
F.11
implies

\mathcal{E}_{T}(\pi)\geq\exp\{-\varepsilon\alpha^{2}T-o_{T}(\alpha^{2}T)\}\frac{2(1-\varepsilon)^{2}c_{\rho}}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{a_{T}}^{\infty}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u,

where
a_{T}=\frac{\alpha\sqrt{T}}{2(1-3\varepsilon)c_{\rho}}
.

For fixed
\alpha>0
,
a_{T}\to\infty
. The identity

\int_{a}^{\infty}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u=\phi(a)-a\Phi(-a)

and the standard Gaussian tail expansion imply

\log\left(\int_{a}^{\infty}\Phi(-u)\,\mathrm{d}u\right)=-\frac{a^{2}}{2}+O(\log a),\qquad a\to\infty.

Thus, for every feasible policy
\pi
,

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathcal{E}_{T}(\pi)\geq-\varepsilon-\frac{1}{8(1-3\varepsilon)^{2}c_{\rho}^{2}}.

The bound is uniform over all policies satisfying

\mathcal{A}_{T}(\pi)\leq\alpha
. Taking the infimum over feasible policies gives

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathfrak{E}_{T}(\alpha)\geq-\varepsilon-\frac{1}{8(1-3\varepsilon)^{2}c_{\rho}^{2}}.

Now take
\alpha\downarrow 0
. Since
\rho
and
\varepsilon
are fixed,

\liminf_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathfrak{E}_{T}(\alpha)\geq-\varepsilon-\frac{1}{8(1-3\varepsilon)^{2}c_{\rho}^{2}}.

Using (
F.7
),

c_{\rho}\geq(1-\rho)^{2}\kappa,

so

\liminf_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathfrak{E}_{T}(\alpha)\geq-\varepsilon-\frac{1}{8(1-3\varepsilon)^{2}(1-\rho)^{4}\kappa^{2}}.

Finally let
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
and then
\rho\downarrow 0
. This gives

\liminf_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathfrak{E}_{T}(\alpha)\geq-\frac{1}{8\kappa^{2}}.

This proves the lower-bound part of Theorem
4.1
.

F.3
Algorithm and Upper Bound

Next we prove the matching upper bound.

Recall Assumption
4.3
assumes that there exists
c_{\rm KL}>0
such that, for all
\mu,z\in\mathcal{I}
,

D(\mu,z)\geq c_{\rm KL}\{s(\mu)-s(z)\}^{2}.

Take
\widetilde{c}:=\min\{c_{\rm KL},1/4\}
.
Then

D(\mu,z)\geq\widetilde{c}\,\{s(\mu)-s(z)\}^{2},\qquad\mu,z\in\mathcal{I}.

(F.34)

F.3.1
The Algorithm

We now define the information-coordinate version of PGWS,

IC-PGWS
. As in the Gaussian algorithm in the main paper, the policy
has two components. During the sampling phase, it forms empirical centers in
the information coordinate and allocates samples according to information-gap
weights. At the terminal time, it recommends the empirical information-coordinate leader unless the terminal certificate is below a calibrated
abstention threshold.

For each arm
i\in[K]
, define

N_{i}(t):=\sum_{r=1}^{t}\mathbbm{1}\{A_{r}=i\},\qquad S_{i}(t):=\sum_{r\leq t:A_{r}=i}t(X_{r}).

The sampling rule uses an empirical information-coordinate center. If

N_{i}(t)>0
, define

\widehat{t}_{i}(t):=\frac{S_{i}(t)}{N_{i}(t)}.

If
N_{i}(t)>0
and

\widehat{t}_{i}(t)\in A^{\prime}(\Theta)
, set

\widehat{\theta}_{i}(t):=(A^{\prime})^{-1}\!\left(\widehat{t}_{i}(t)\right),\qquad\widehat{\eta}_{i}(t):=\beta^{-1}\!\left(\widehat{\theta}_{i}(t)\right),\qquad\widehat{\mu}_{i}(t):=s^{-1}\!\left(\widehat{\eta}_{i}(t)\right).

(F.35)

If
N_{i}(t)=0
, or if

\widehat{t}_{i}(t)\notin A^{\prime}(\Theta)
, use the deterministic fallback

\widehat{\eta}_{i}(t):=\eta_{\circ}:=s(\mu_{\circ}),\qquad\widehat{\mu}_{i}(t):=\mu_{\circ}.

(F.36)

The fallback is only a finite-time safeguard. For example, in Bernoulli rewards,
the empirical natural statistic may equal
0
or
1
after only failures
or only successes. Since the true parameter lies in the interior and forced
exploration gives
N_{i}(t)\to\infty
, the strong law implies

\widehat{t}_{i}(t)\to A^{\prime}(\theta(\mu_{i}))\in A^{\prime}(\Theta)
, so the fallback
is used only finitely many times almost surely for each arm.

At time
t
, define the empirical information-coordinate leader

\widehat{b}_{t}:=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{i\in[K]}\widehat{\eta}_{i}(t),

(F.37)

with deterministic tie-breaking. For
j\neq\widehat{b}_{t}
, define the empirical
information-coordinate gap

\widehat{\Delta}_{j,s}(t):=\widehat{\eta}_{\widehat{b}_{t}}(t)-\widehat{\eta}_{j}(t),

and define the empirical top-two information gap

\widehat{\Gamma}_{s,t}:=\min_{j\neq\widehat{b}_{t}}\widehat{\Delta}_{j,s}(t).

When
\widehat{\Gamma}_{s,t}>0
, set

w_{\widehat{b}_{t}}(t):=1,\qquad w_{j}(t):=\min\left\{1,\,\frac{\widehat{\Gamma}_{s,t}^{\,2}}{4\widetilde{c}\,\widehat{\Delta}_{j,s}(t)^{2}}\right\},\qquad j\neq\widehat{b}_{t}.

(F.38)

The sampling probabilities are

p_{i}(t):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{w_{i}(t)}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}w_{\ell}(t)},&\widehat{\Gamma}_{s,t}>0,\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{1}{K},&\widehat{\Gamma}_{s,t}=0.\end{cases}

(F.39)

The constant
\widetilde{c}\leq 1/4
is the one in
(
F.34
). The cap at
1/4
ensures that the closest empirical challenger receives the same sampling weight (
1
) as the leader.

At the terminal time, set

\widehat{b}_{T}:=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{i\in[K]}\widehat{\eta}_{i}(T),

(F.40)

again with deterministic tie-breaking.
Given the terminal history, for ease of notation let

\varPi_{T}(\cdot):=\mathbb{P}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{F}_{T})

denote the posterior distribution induced by the prior and the observed data.
The
terminal certificate
is

R_{T}:=-\log\max_{j\neq\widehat{b}_{T}}\varPi_{T}(\mu_{j}\geq\mu_{\widehat{b}_{T}})=-\log\max_{j\neq\widehat{b}_{T}}\varPi_{T}(\eta_{j}\geq\eta_{\widehat{b}_{T}}),

(F.41)

where the equality follows because
s
is strictly increasing. Thus
R_{T}
is large when the final leader is
well separated from all challengers under the posterior, and small when at least
one challenger remains plausible.

To calibrate abstention, let
r_{T,\alpha}
be the lower
\alpha
-quantile of

R_{T}
under the joint Bayesian law induced by the sampling rule:

r_{T,\alpha}:=\inf\left\{r\geq 0:\mathbb{P}(R_{T}\leq r)\geq\alpha\right\}.

(F.42)

All probabilities in (
F.42
) are taken before the
final terminal randomization. If the distribution of
R_{T}
has an atom at the
threshold, define

\theta_{T,\alpha}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\alpha-\mathbb{P}(R_{T}<r_{T,\alpha})}{\mathbb{P}(R_{T}=r_{T,\alpha})},&\mathbb{P}(R_{T}=r_{T,\alpha})>0,\\[12.0pt]
0,&\mathbb{P}(R_{T}=r_{T,\alpha})=0.\end{cases}

(F.43)

The terminal decision is

\widehat{a}_{T}=\begin{cases}?,&R_{T}<r_{T,\alpha},\\[3.0pt]
?,&R_{T}=r_{T,\alpha}\text{ with probability }\theta_{T,\alpha},\\[3.0pt]
\widehat{b}_{T},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(F.44)

Theorem F.2

(Upper Bound for

Under Assumptions

F.3.3
Error Guarantee

Lemma F.12

(Error Guarantee)
.

For every
T
and every
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\mathcal{E}_{T}(\textsc{IC-PGWS}(\alpha))\leq(K-1)e^{-r_{T,\alpha}}.

Proof.

Condition on the terminal history
\mathcal{F}_{T}
. If the algorithm recommends

\widehat{b}_{T}
, then

\mathbb{P}(a^{\star}\neq\widehat{b}_{T}\mid\mathcal{F}_{T})\leq\sum_{j\neq\widehat{b}_{T}}\varPi_{T}(\mu_{j}\geq\mu_{\widehat{b}_{T}}).

By the definition of
R_{T}
,

\max_{j\neq\widehat{b}_{T}}\varPi_{T}(\mu_{j}\geq\mu_{\widehat{b}_{T}})=e^{-R_{T}}.

Therefore

\mathbb{P}(a^{\star}\neq\widehat{b}_{T}\mid\mathcal{F}_{T})\leq(K-1)e^{-R_{T}}.

On the event that the algorithm does not abstain, the terminal quantile rule
implies
R_{T}\geq r_{T,\alpha}
. Hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{T}(\textsc{IC-PGWS}(\alpha))=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbbm{1}\{\widehat{a}_{T}\neq?\}\mathbb{P}(a^{\star}\neq\widehat{b}_{T}\mid\mathcal{F}_{T})\right]\leq(K-1)e^{-r_{T,\alpha}}.

∎

F.3.4
Consistency and Allocation Convergence.

We next identify the almost-sure limit of
R_{T}/T
. We first introduce the
limiting quantities associated with a realized parameter vector. Let

b^{\star}:=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{i\in[K]}\eta_{i}=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{i\in[K]}\mu_{i}

be the true best arm. For each
j\neq b^{\star}
, define the information coordinate
gap

\Delta_{j,s}:=\eta_{b^{\star}}-\eta_{j}
,
and define the true top two information gap

\Gamma_{s}:=\min_{j\neq b^{\star}}\Delta_{j,s}=\eta_{(1)}-\eta_{(2)}
,
where
\eta_{(1)}>\eta_{(2)}>\cdots>\eta_{(K)}
are the ordered
information-coordinate qualities.

Define the
limiting unnormalized sampling weights
by

w_{b^{\star}}^{\dagger}(\eta):=1,\qquad w_{j}^{\dagger}(\eta):=\min\left\{1,\,\frac{\Gamma_{s}^{2}}{4\widetilde{c}\,\Delta_{j,s}^{2}}\right\},\qquad j\neq b^{\star}.

(F.45)

Let

W^{\dagger}(\eta):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}w_{\ell}^{\dagger}(\eta),\qquad p_{i}^{\dagger}(\eta):=\frac{w_{i}^{\dagger}(\eta)}{W^{\dagger}(\eta)}.

Because
\widetilde{c}\leq 1/4
, every second-best arm has limiting weight one.
Consequently,

2\leq W^{\dagger}(\eta)\leq K,\qquad p_{i}^{\dagger}(\eta)>0\quad\text{for every }i\in[K].

For each challenger
j\neq b^{\star}
, define its pairwise error exponent

C_{j}^{\dagger}(\mu):=\inf_{z\in[\mu_{j},\mu_{b^{\star}}]}\left\{p_{b^{\star}}^{\dagger}(\eta)D(\mu_{b^{\star}},z)+p_{j}^{\dagger}(\eta)D(\mu_{j},z)\right\}.

The worst-case pairwise error exponent is

C^{\dagger}(\mu):=\min_{j\neq b^{\star}}C_{j}^{\dagger}(\mu).

(F.46)

Equivalently,

C^{\dagger}(\mu)=\frac{1}{W^{\dagger}(\eta)}\min_{j\neq b^{\star}}\inf_{z\in[\mu_{j},\mu_{b^{\star}}]}\left\{D(\mu_{b^{\star}},z)+w_{j}^{\dagger}(\eta)D(\mu_{j},z)\right\}.

(F.47)

We now show that the empirical centers converge to the true qualities, the
empirical sampling proportions converge to
p^{\dagger}(\eta)
, and the
normalized terminal certificate converges to
C^{\dagger}(\mu)
.

Lemma F.13

(Information Coordinate Center Consistency)
.

Under Algorithm
2
, for every arm
i
,

\widehat{\eta}_{i}(t)\to\eta_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widehat{\mu}_{i}(t)\to\mu_{i}\qquad\text{a.s.}

Proof.

The forced-exploration rule implies

N_{i}(t)\to\infty\qquad\text{a.s. for every }i.

Conditional on
\mu_{i}
, the observations from arm
i
are i.i.d. from

P_{\mu_{i}}
. Since the log-partition function is finite on an open natural-parameter
space, the natural statistic has finite mean under
P_{\mu_{i}}
, and

\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{i}}[t(X)]=A^{\prime}(\theta(\mu_{i})).

Therefore, by the strong law of large numbers,

\widehat{t}_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{N_{i}(t)}\sum_{r\leq t:A_{r}=i}t(X_{r})\to A^{\prime}(\theta(\mu_{i}))\qquad\text{a.s.}

Because
\theta(\mu_{i})\in\Theta
and
\Theta
is open,

A^{\prime}(\theta(\mu_{i}))\in A^{\prime}(\Theta)
. Hence
\widehat{t}_{i}(t)\in A^{\prime}(\Theta)
eventually almost surely. On this eventual event,

\widehat{\theta}_{i}(t)=(A^{\prime})^{-1}\!\left(\widehat{t}_{i}(t)\right)\to\theta(\mu_{i}),

since
A^{\prime\prime}>0
implies that
A^{\prime}
is strictly increasing and continuous.
Thus

\widehat{\eta}_{i}(t)=\beta^{-1}(\widehat{\theta}_{i}(t))\to\beta^{-1}(\theta(\mu_{i}))=s(\mu_{i})=\eta_{i}.

Continuity of
s^{-1}
gives

\widehat{\mu}_{i}(t)=s^{-1}(\widehat{\eta}_{i}(t))\to\mu_{i}.

∎

Lemma F.14

(Sampling Proportions)
.

Under Algorithm
2
,

\frac{N_{i}(T)}{T}\to p_{i}^{\dagger}(\eta)\qquad\text{a.s. for every }i\in[K].

Proof.

By Lemma
F.13
,

\widehat{\eta}_{i}(t)\to\eta_{i}\qquad\text{for every }i.

Since the prior densities are continuous, ties have probability zero. Thus the
best arm and the second-best arm are unique almost surely. On this
probability-one event,
\widehat{b}_{t}=b^{\star}

eventually, and

\widehat{\Delta}_{j,s}(t)\to\Delta_{j,s},\qquad\widehat{\Gamma}_{s,t}\to\Gamma_{s}.

Therefore

w_{i}(t)\to w_{i}^{\dagger}(\eta),\qquad p_{i}(t)\to p_{i}^{\dagger}(\eta)\qquad\text{a.s. for every }i.

Now fix an arm
i
. We take four steps:

Step 1: Forced exploration is negligible.

Let
F_{i}(T)
be the number of forced-exploration pulls of arm
i
up to time
T
.
If the
m
-th forced pull of arm
i
occurs before time
T
, then immediately before that pull,

N_{i}(t)\geq m-1
.
But a forced pull of arm
i
can occur only if

N_{i}(t)<\sqrt{t+1}\leq\sqrt{T+1}
.
Hence

m-1<\sqrt{T+1}
,
and therefore

F_{i}(T)\leq\lceil\sqrt{T+1}\rceil+1
.
Consequently,

\frac{F_{i}(T)}{T}\to 0.

Let
F(T)
be the total number of forced-exploration rounds up to time
T
. Since

F(T)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}F_{\ell}(T)
,
we also have

\frac{F(T)}{T}\leq\frac{K(\lceil\sqrt{T+1}\rceil+1)}{T}\to 0.

Step 2: Martingale concentration on non-forced rounds.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{t}
be the history before round
t+1
, and define

B_{t}:=\mathbbm{1}\{\text{round }t+1\text{ is non-forced}\}
.
The event
\{B_{t}=1\}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable. On non-forced rounds, the algorithm samples arm
i
with conditional probability
p_{i}(t)
.

Define

D_{i,t+1}:=B_{t}\Bigl(\mathbbm{1}\{A_{t+1}=i\}-p_{i}(t)\Bigr)
.
Then

\mathbb{E}[D_{i,t+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0
,
so
(D_{i,t+1})_{t\geq 0}
is a martingale difference sequence. Also,

|D_{i,t+1}|\leq 1
.

Therefore, by Azuma’s inequality, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}D_{i,t+1}\right|\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}T}{2}\right).

The right-hand side is summable in
T
. Hence, by the Borel–Cantelli lemma,

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}D_{i,t+1}\to 0\qquad\text{almost surely}.

Step 3: Decomposition of the empirical pull count.

The total number of pulls of arm
i
can be decomposed as

N_{i}(T)=F_{i}(T)+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}B_{t}\mathbbm{1}\{A_{t+1}=i\}.

Using the definition of
D_{i,t+1}
, we have

B_{t}\mathbbm{1}\{A_{t+1}=i\}=D_{i,t+1}+B_{t}p_{i}(t)
.
Thus

\frac{N_{i}(T)}{T}=\frac{F_{i}(T)}{T}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}D_{i,t+1}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}B_{t}p_{i}(t).

By Steps 1 and 2,

\frac{N_{i}(T)}{T}-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}B_{t}p_{i}(t)\to 0\qquad\text{almost surely}.

Step 4: Cesàro convergence of the target probabilities.

Since

p_{i}(t)\to p_{i}^{\dagger}(\eta)\qquad\text{almost surely},

the sequence
\{p_{i}(t)\}
is Cesàro convergent, i.e.,

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}p_{i}(t)\to p_{i}^{\dagger}(\eta)\qquad\text{almost surely}.

Now write

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}B_{t}p_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}p_{i}(t)-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(1-B_{t})p_{i}(t).

Since
0\leq p_{i}(t)\leq 1
,

0\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(1-B_{t})p_{i}(t)\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}(1-B_{t})=\frac{F(T)}{T}\to 0.

Hence

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}B_{t}p_{i}(t)\to p_{i}^{\dagger}(\eta)\qquad\text{almost surely}.

Combining this with Step 3 gives the desired result.

∎

F.3.5
Posterior Ordering Large Deviations

Let
X_{i,1},\ldots,X_{i,n}
denote the first
n
rewards observed from
arm
i
, and let

\mathcal{G}_{i,n}:=\sigma(X_{i,1},\ldots,X_{i,n})
.
Define the
one-arm posterior

\varPi_{i,n}
as the conditional distribution
of
\eta_{i}=s(\mu_{i})
given
\mathcal{G}_{i,n}
:

\varPi_{i,n}(B):=\mathbb{P}(\eta_{i}\in B\mid\mathcal{G}_{i,n}),\qquad B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).

Intuitively, the next lemma proves that

\varPi_{i,n}(\mu_{i}^{\prime}\approx z)=\exp\{-nD(\mu_{i},z)+o(n)\}

Lemma F.15

(One-arm Posterior Estimates for Ordering)
.

Fix an arm
i
, and work on the event (that happens almost surely)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=1}^{n}t(X_{i,r})\to A^{\prime}(\theta(\mu_{i})).

Let
\varPi_{i,n}
be the one-arm posterior defined above. Define

I_{i}(\eta):=D(\mu_{i},s^{-1}(\eta)).

Then the following hold.

First, for every compact interval
K\Subset\mathcal{H}
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\varPi_{i,n}(K)\leq-\inf_{\eta\in K}I_{i}(\eta).

Second, for every nonempty open interval
G\subseteq\mathcal{H}
,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\varPi_{i,n}(G)\geq-\inf_{\eta\in G}I_{i}(\eta).

Third, for every
a,b\in\mathcal{H}
with
a<\eta_{i}<b
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\varPi_{i,n}(\eta\leq a)\leq-I_{i}(a),

and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\varPi_{i,n}(\eta\geq b)\leq-I_{i}(b).

Proof.

Set

\eta_{i}=s(\mu_{i}),\qquad\theta_{i}:=\theta(\mu_{i})=\beta(\eta_{i}),\qquad m_{i}:=A^{\prime}(\theta_{i}),

and write

\bar{m}_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=1}^{n}t(X_{i,r}).

For
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
, write

p_{\eta}(w):=\frac{\mathrm{d}P_{s^{-1}(\eta)}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}(w)=\exp\{\beta(\eta)t(w)-A(\beta(\eta))\}h(w).

Consider a realized full adaptive history
h_{t}=(a_{1},x_{1},\ldots,a_{t},x_{t})
,
For a candidate parameter vector

\bm{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{K})\in\mathcal{H}^{K}
,
the density of this realized history under the policy
\pi
, with respect to
counting measure on actions and
\lambda^{\otimes t}
on rewards, is

L_{t}^{\pi}(\bm{\eta};h_{t})=\prod_{r=1}^{t}\pi_{r}(a_{r}\mid h_{r-1})\prod_{r=1}^{t}p_{\eta_{a_{r}}}(x_{r}).

Here
\pi_{r}(a_{r}\mid h_{r-1})
is the sampling probability assigned by the
policy to the realized action
a_{r}
after the realized past
h_{r-1}
.

Using the product prior in information coordinates, the posterior density of

\bm{\eta}
is therefore proportional to

\prod_{\ell=1}^{K}\bar{f}_{\ell}(\eta_{\ell})\prod_{r:a_{r}=\ell}p_{\eta_{\ell}}(x_{r}).

Thus the posterior factorizes arm by arm. In particular, the marginal posterior
of arm
i
depends only on the rewards observed from arm
i
. If these
rewards are denoted

X_{i,1},\ldots,X_{i,n}
,
then, for every measurable set
B\subseteq\mathcal{H}
,

\varPi_{i,n}(B)=\frac{\int_{B}\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)\prod_{r=1}^{n}p_{\eta}(X_{i,r})\,\mathrm{d}\eta}{\int_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)\prod_{r=1}^{n}p_{\eta}(X_{i,r})\,\mathrm{d}\eta}.

Substituting the exponential-family density gives

\prod_{r=1}^{n}p_{\eta}(X_{i,r})=\exp\left\{\beta(\eta)\sum_{r=1}^{n}t(X_{i,r})-nA(\beta(\eta))\right\}\prod_{r=1}^{n}h(X_{i,r}).

\prod_{r=1}^{n}h(X_{i,r})
does not depend on
\eta
, so
it also cancels between numerator and denominator.
Bayes’ rule gives

\varPi_{i,n}(B)=\frac{\int_{B}\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)\exp\{n[\beta(\eta)\bar{m}_{n}-A(\beta(\eta))]\}\,\mathrm{d}\eta}{\int_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)\exp\{n[\beta(\eta)\bar{m}_{n}-A(\beta(\eta))]\}\,\mathrm{d}\eta}.

Multiplying both numerator and denominator by

\exp\{-n[\beta(\eta_{i})\bar{m}_{n}-A(\beta(\eta_{i}))]\}

allows us to write

\varPi_{i,n}(B)=\frac{\int_{B}\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)e^{nL_{n}(\eta)}\,\mathrm{d}\eta}{Z_{n}},

where

L_{n}(\eta):=(\beta(\eta)-\beta(\eta_{i}))\bar{m}_{n}-A(\beta(\eta))+A(\beta(\eta_{i}))

and

Z_{n}:=\int_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)e^{nL_{n}(\eta)}\,\mathrm{d}\eta.

Note
L_{n}(\eta_{i})=0
and

L_{n}(\eta)\to(\beta(\eta)-\beta(\eta_{i}))A^{\prime}(\beta(\eta_{i}))-A(\beta(\eta))+A(\beta(\eta_{i}))=-D(\mu_{i},s^{-1}(\eta))=-I_{i}(\eta)

on compact subsets of
\mathcal{H}
.

We first prove that

\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}\to 0.

(F.48)

For the lower bound on
Z_{n}
, fix
\tau>0
. Since
\bar{m}_{n}\to m_{i}
,
L_{n}(\eta)\to-I_{i}(\eta)
uniformly on compact neighborhoods of
\eta_{i}
.

Since
I_{i}(\eta_{i})=0
, choose a compact interval
J_{\tau}\Subset\mathcal{H}
containing
\eta_{i}
such that, for
all sufficiently large
n
,
\inf_{\eta\in J_{\tau}}L_{n}(\eta)\geq-\tau
.

By continuity and strict positivity of
\bar{f}_{i}
,

\int_{J_{\tau}}\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta>0.

Therefore

Z_{n}\geq\int_{J_{\tau}}\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)e^{nL_{n}(\eta)}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\geq e^{-n\tau}\int_{J_{\tau}}\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta.

Thus

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}\geq-\tau.

Letting
\tau\downarrow 0
gives

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}\geq 0.

For the upper bound on
Z_{n}
, for all large
n
we have

\bar{m}_{n}\in A^{\prime}(\Theta)
. Define the pseudo-MLE

\theta_{n}:=(A^{\prime})^{-1}(\bar{m}_{n}),\qquad\eta_{n}:=\beta^{-1}(\theta_{n}).

Then
\theta_{n}\to\theta_{i}
and
\eta_{n}\to\eta_{i}
.
Moreover, using
\bar{m}_{n}=A^{\prime}(\theta_{n})
,

\displaystyle L_{n}(\eta_{n})-L_{n}(\eta)

\displaystyle=(\theta_{n}-\beta(\eta))A^{\prime}(\theta_{n})-A(\theta_{n})+A(\beta(\eta))

\displaystyle=D(s^{-1}(\eta_{n}),s^{-1}(\eta)).

Therefore, by assumption (
F.34
),

L_{n}(\eta)\leq L_{n}(\eta_{n})-\widetilde{c}\,(\eta-\eta_{n})^{2}.

Since

L_{n}(\eta_{n})=D(s^{-1}(\eta_{n}),\mu_{i})\to 0,

boundedness of
\bar{f}_{i}
gives

\displaystyle Z_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bar{f}_{i}\|_{\infty}e^{nL_{n}(\eta_{n})}\int_{\mathcal{H}}e^{-n\widetilde{c}(\eta-\eta_{n})^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq\|\bar{f}_{i}\|_{\infty}e^{nL_{n}(\eta_{n})}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{n\widetilde{c}}}.

Hence

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}\leq 0.

This proves (
F.48
).

Now fix a compact interval
K\Subset\mathcal{H}
. Since
\bar{m}_{n}\to m_{i}
,

L_{n}(\eta)\to-D(\mu_{i},s^{-1}(\eta))=-I_{i}(\eta)

uniformly on
K
. Hence

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\int_{K}\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)e^{nL_{n}(\eta)}\,\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq-\inf_{\eta\in K}I_{i}(\eta).

Combining this numerator bound with
(
F.48
) gives the compact-interval
upper bound.

Next let
G\subseteq\mathcal{H}
be a nonempty open interval. Choose

\eta_{0}\in G
such that

I_{i}(\eta_{0})\leq\inf_{\eta\in G}I_{i}(\eta)+\tau.

Choose a compact interval
J\Subset G
containing
\eta_{0}
and small enough
that

\sup_{\eta\in J}I_{i}(\eta)\leq I_{i}(\eta_{0})+\tau.

By uniform convergence of
L_{n}
on
J
and strict positivity of

\bar{f}_{i}
on
J
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\int_{G}\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)e^{nL_{n}(\eta)}\,\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\int_{J}\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)e^{nL_{n}(\eta)}\,\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\geq-\sup_{\eta\in J}I_{i}(\eta)\geq-\inf_{\eta\in G}I_{i}(\eta)-2\tau.

Using again the denominator rate
(
F.48
), and then letting

\tau\downarrow 0
, gives the open-interval lower bound.

It remains to prove the one-sided estimates. Consider
a<\eta_{i}
. Since

\eta_{n}\to\eta_{i}
, eventually
a<\eta_{n}
. The function
\theta\mapsto\theta\bar{m}_{n}-A(\theta)

is strictly concave and maximized at
\theta_{n}
. Since
\beta
is strictly
increasing,
L_{n}(\eta)
is increasing on
\{\eta<\eta_{n}\}
. Therefore, for all large

n
,

\sup_{\eta\leq a}L_{n}(\eta)=L_{n}(a).

Thus

\varPi_{i,n}(\eta\leq a)\leq\frac{e^{nL_{n}(a)}}{Z_{n}},

because
\int_{\{\eta\leq a\}}\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\eta\leq 1
.
Since
L_{n}(a)\to-I_{i}(a)

and
n^{-1}\log Z_{n}\to 0
, we obtain

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\varPi_{i,n}(\eta\leq a)\leq-I_{i}(a).

The proof for
b>\eta_{i}
is identical: eventually
b>\eta_{n}
,
L_{n}
is
decreasing on
\{\eta>\eta_{n}\}
, and hence

\sup_{\eta\geq b}L_{n}(\eta)=L_{n}(b).

This gives the upper-tail estimate.

∎

The following lemma shows that, if arms
i
and
j

receive asymptotic sampling proportions
q_{i}
and
q_{j}
, then the posterior
probability of incorrectly ordering them decays with exponent

\inf_{z\in[\mu_{j},\mu_{i}]}\{q_{i}D(\mu_{i},z)+q_{j}D(\mu_{j},z)\}.

Lemma F.16

(Posterior Ordering Large Deviations)
.

Fix two arms
i
and
j
with
\mu_{i}>\mu_{j}
. Suppose that

\frac{N_{i}(T)}{T}\to q_{i}>0,\qquad\frac{N_{j}(T)}{T}\to q_{j}>0.

Then almost surely

-\frac{1}{T}\log\varPi_{T}(\mu_{j}\geq\mu_{i})\to\inf_{z\in[\mu_{j},\mu_{i}]}\{q_{i}D(\mu_{i},z)+q_{j}D(\mu_{j},z)\}.

(F.49)

Proof.

Write

\eta_{i}=s(\mu_{i}),\qquad\eta_{j}=s(\mu_{j}),\qquad\eta_{i}>\eta_{j}.

Recall from the proof of Lemma
F.15
that,
conditional on the realized history, the arm-wise posterior of

\eta_{i}=s(\mu_{i})
is

\varPi_{i,T}(\mathrm{d}\eta)=\frac{\bar{f}_{i}(\eta)\exp\{\beta(\eta)S_{i}(T)-N_{i}(T)A(\beta(\eta))\}}{Z_{i,T}}\,\mathrm{d}\eta,

where

Z_{i,T}:=\int_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{f}_{i}(u)\exp\{\beta(u)S_{i}(T)-N_{i}(T)A(\beta(u))\}\,\mathrm{d}u.

Because the prior is a product prior and the likelihood factors arm by arm, the
full posterior factorizes:

\varPi_{T}(\mathrm{d}\eta_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{d}\eta_{K})=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{K}\varPi_{\ell,T}(\mathrm{d}\eta_{\ell}).

Consequently, let
U\sim\varPi_{i,T}
and
V\sim\varPi_{j,T}
denote the posterior
information coordinate qualities (arm parameter) of arms
i
and
j
, then
U
and
V

are independent under the posterior, and for Borel sets
B,C\subseteq\mathcal{H}
,

\varPi_{T}(U\in B,\ V\in C)=\varPi_{i,T}(B)\varPi_{j,T}(C).

In particular,

\varPi_{T}(\mu_{j}\geq\mu_{i})=\varPi_{T}(\eta_{j}\geq\eta_{i})=\varPi_{T}(V\geq U).

Define

I_{i}(u):=D(\mu_{i},s^{-1}(u)),\qquad I_{j}(v):=D(\mu_{j},s^{-1}(v)),

and

C_{ij}:=\inf_{w\in[\eta_{j},\eta_{i}]}\{q_{i}I_{i}(w)+q_{j}I_{j}(w)\}.

The one-arm bounds on
\log\varPi_{i,n}
of Lemma
F.15
hold
along every subsequence
n\to\infty
on the arm-wise strong-law event.
Therefore they also hold along the random subsequences

N_{i}(T)
and
N_{j}(T)
. Multiplying the one-arm exponents by

N_{i}(T)/T\to q_{i}
and
N_{j}(T)/T\to q_{j}
gives the corresponding

T
-speed bounds: For intervals
B,C
,

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\{\varPi_{i,T}(B)\varPi_{j,T}(C)\}\leq-q_{i}\inf_{u\in B}I_{i}(u)-q_{j}\inf_{v\in C}I_{j}(v)

when
B,C
are compact, and the corresponding lower bound when
B,C
are
open.

We first prove the lower bound for (
F.49
). Let
w^{\star}\in[\eta_{j},\eta_{i}]
be a minimizer of

w\mapsto q_{i}I_{i}(w)+q_{j}I_{j}(w).

If
w^{\star}\in(\eta_{j},\eta_{i})
, choose
\delta>0
so small that

(w^{\star}-\delta,w^{\star})\times(w^{\star},w^{\star}+\delta)\subseteq\{(u,v):v\geq u\}.

Then, by posterior product decomposition and the open-interval lower bounds in
Lemma
F.15
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\varPi_{T}(V\geq U)

\displaystyle\geq-q_{i}\inf_{u\in(w^{\star}-\delta,w^{\star})}I_{i}(u)-q_{j}\inf_{v\in(w^{\star},w^{\star}+\delta)}I_{j}(v).

Letting
\delta\downarrow 0
gives

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\varPi_{T}(V\geq U)\geq-C_{ij}.

If
w^{\star}=\eta_{j}
, use instead the rectangle
U\in(\eta_{j}-\delta,\eta_{j})
and
V\in(\eta_{j},\eta_{j}+\delta)
.
Since

\inf_{u\in(\eta_{j}-\delta,\eta_{j})}I_{i}(u)\to I_{i}(\eta_{j}),\qquad\inf_{v\in(\eta_{j},\eta_{j}+\delta)}I_{j}(v)\to I_{j}(\eta_{j})=0,

the same lower bound follows.

Similarly if
w^{\star}=\eta_{i}
, use
U\in(\eta_{i}-\delta,\eta_{i})
and
V\in(\eta_{i},\eta_{i}+\delta)
.
Then

\inf_{u\in(\eta_{i}-\delta,\eta_{i})}I_{i}(u)\to I_{i}(\eta_{i})=0,\qquad\inf_{v\in(\eta_{i},\eta_{i}+\delta)}I_{j}(v)\to I_{j}(\eta_{i}),

and the same lower bound again follows. Thus

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\varPi_{T}(V\geq U)\geq-C_{ij}.

For the upper bound, decompose

\{V\geq U\}\subseteq E_{-}\cup E_{0}\cup E_{+},

where
E_{-}:=\{U\leq\eta_{j}\}
,
E_{+}:=\{V\geq\eta_{i}\}
and
E_{0}:=\{\eta_{j}\leq U\leq V\leq\eta_{i}\}
.

The one-sided estimates in Lemma
F.15
give

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\varPi_{T}(E_{-})\leq-q_{i}I_{i}(\eta_{j})=-q_{i}D(\mu_{i},\mu_{j}),

and

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\varPi_{T}(E_{+})\leq-q_{j}I_{j}(\eta_{i})=-q_{j}D(\mu_{j},\mu_{i}).

Since
C_{ij}\leq q_{i}D(\mu_{i},\mu_{j})
, by taking
w=\eta_{j}
, and

C_{ij}\leq q_{j}D(\mu_{j},\mu_{i})
, by taking
w=\eta_{i}
, both endpoint events have
exponent at least
C_{ij}
.

It remains to control
E_{0}
. The set
K_{0}:=\{(u,v):\eta_{j}\leq u\leq v\leq\eta_{i}\}
is compact. Fix
\tau>0
. By continuity of
(u,v)\mapsto q_{i}I_{i}(u)+q_{j}I_{j}(v)

on the compact square
[\eta_{j},\eta_{i}]^{2}
, there exist finitely many compact
rectangles

R_{m}=I_{m}\times J_{m},\qquad m=1,\ldots,M,

whose union covers
K_{0}
, such that each
R_{m}\subseteq[\eta_{j},\eta_{i}]^{2}
and

\inf_{(u,v)\in R_{m}}\{q_{i}I_{i}(u)+q_{j}I_{j}(v)\}\geq\inf_{(u,v)\in K_{0}}\{q_{i}I_{i}(u)+q_{j}I_{j}(v)\}-\tau.

By posterior product decomposition,

\varPi_{T}(E_{0})\leq\sum_{m=1}^{M}\varPi_{i,T}(I_{m})\varPi_{j,T}(J_{m}),

where
\varPi_{i,T}
and
\varPi_{j,T}
are the corresponding arm-wise posterior
marginals. Applying the compact-interval upper bound from
Lemma
F.15
along the subsequences

N_{i}(T)
and
N_{j}(T)
, we get, for each
m
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\{\varPi_{i,T}(I_{m})\varPi_{j,T}(J_{m})\}

\displaystyle\leq-q_{i}\inf_{u\in I_{m}}I_{i}(u)-q_{j}\inf_{v\in J_{m}}I_{j}(v)

\displaystyle=-\inf_{(u,v)\in R_{m}}\{q_{i}I_{i}(u)+q_{j}I_{j}(v)\}.

Since the cover is finite, the union bound yields

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\varPi_{T}(E_{0})

\displaystyle\leq-\inf_{(u,v)\in K_{0}}\{q_{i}I_{i}(u)+q_{j}I_{j}(v)\}+\tau.

Letting
\tau\downarrow 0
, we obtain

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\varPi_{T}(E_{0})\leq-\inf_{\eta_{j}\leq u\leq v\leq\eta_{i}}\{q_{i}I_{i}(u)+q_{j}I_{j}(v)\}.

We now reduce this infimum to the diagonal (
u=v
). Note the derivative

\frac{\partial}{\partial z}D(\mu,z)=\theta^{\prime}(z)\left[A^{\prime}(\theta(z))-A^{\prime}(\theta(\mu))\right],

is negative for
z<\mu
and positive for
z>\mu
. Hence, on the interval

[\eta_{j},\eta_{i}]
, the map
u\mapsto I_{i}(u)
is non-increasing whereas
v\mapsto I_{j}(v)
is non-decreasing.
Therefore, if
u\leq v
, then for every
w\in[u,v]
,
I_{i}(w)\leq I_{i}(u)
and
I_{j}(w)\leq I_{j}(v)
.

Thus every feasible off-diagonal point can be moved to a diagonal point with no
larger objective. Hence

\inf_{\eta_{j}\leq u\leq v\leq\eta_{i}}\{q_{i}I_{i}(u)+q_{j}I_{j}(v)\}=\inf_{w\in[\eta_{j},\eta_{i}]}\{q_{i}I_{i}(w)+q_{j}I_{j}(w)\}=C_{ij}.

Combining the bounds for
E_{-}
,
E_{0}
, and
E_{+}
by a union bound gives

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\varPi_{T}(V\geq U)\leq-C_{ij}.

Together with the lower bound,

-\frac{1}{T}\log\varPi_{T}(V\geq U)\to C_{ij}.

Returning to
z=s^{-1}(w)
proves the claimed formula.

∎

F.3.6
Asymptotic Behavior of the Certificate
R_{T}

Recall we defined

C^{\dagger}(\mu):=\min_{j\neq b^{\star}}\inf_{z\in[\mu_{j},\mu_{b^{\star}}]}\left\{p_{b^{\star}}^{\dagger}(\eta)D(\mu_{b^{\star}},z)+p_{j}^{\dagger}(\eta)D(\mu_{j},z)\right\}.

(F.50)

Lemma F.17

(Terminal Certificate Limit)
.

Under Algorithm
2
,

\frac{R_{T}}{T}\to C^{\dagger}(\mu)\qquad\text{a.s.}

Proof.

By Lemma
F.13
,
\widehat{b}_{T}=b^{\star}
eventually almost surely. By Lemma
F.14
,

\frac{N_{i}(T)}{T}\to p_{i}^{\dagger}(\eta)\qquad\text{for every }i.

Since
p_{i}^{\dagger}(\eta)>0
for every arm, applying
Lemma
F.16
with
i=b^{\star}
and each

j\neq b^{\star}
gives

-\frac{1}{T}\log\varPi_{T}(\mu_{j}\geq\mu_{b^{\star}})\to\inf_{z\in[\mu_{j},\mu_{b^{\star}}]}\left\{p_{b^{\star}}^{\dagger}(\eta)D(\mu_{b^{\star}},z)+p_{j}^{\dagger}(\eta)D(\mu_{j},z)\right\}.

Taking the minimum over the finite set
j\neq b^{\star}
yields

\frac{R_{T}}{T}=\min_{j\neq b^{\star}}\left\{-\frac{1}{T}\log\varPi_{T}(\mu_{j}\geq\mu_{b^{\star}})\right\}\to C^{\dagger}(\mu).

∎

F.3.7
Lower Tail of the Limiting Certificate.

We now prove that
C^{\dagger}(\mu)
has the same small lower tail as

\Gamma_{s}^{2}/8
.

Lemma F.18

(Top-Two Gap and Triple-Gap Expansions)
.

Under Assumption
4.2
,

\mathbb{P}(\Gamma_{s}\leq\varepsilon)=\kappa\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon),\qquad\varepsilon\downarrow 0.

Moreover, define

\Gamma_{3,s}:=\eta_{(1)}-\eta_{(3)}

for
K\geq 3
, and
\Gamma_{3,s}:=+\infty
for
K=2
, then

\mathbb{P}(\Gamma_{3,s}\leq\varepsilon)=O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Proof.

For the top-two gap expansion, condition on arm
i
having information coordinate
value
u
. The event that arm
i
is best and that the top-two gap is at most

\varepsilon
has conditional probability

\prod_{k\neq i}\bar{F}_{k}(u)-\prod_{k\neq i}\bar{F}_{k}(u-\varepsilon),

where
\bar{F}_{k}(t)
is understood to be
0
below the left endpoint of

\mathcal{H}
and
1
above the right endpoint. Hence

\mathbb{P}(\Gamma_{s}\leq\varepsilon)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\int_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{f}_{i}(u)\left[\prod_{k\neq i}\bar{F}_{k}(u)-\prod_{k\neq i}\bar{F}_{k}(u-\varepsilon)\right]\mathrm{d}u.

Let

G_{i}(u):=\prod_{k\neq i}\bar{F}_{k}(u).

Then

G_{i}^{\prime}(u)=\sum_{j\neq i}\bar{f}_{j}(u)\prod_{k\neq i,j}\bar{F}_{k}(u)

where the derivative exists, and
G_{i}
is Lipschitz because the densities are
bounded. Therefore

\frac{G_{i}(u)-G_{i}(u-\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\to G_{i}^{\prime}(u)

for almost every
u
, and the quotient is uniformly bounded. Dominated
convergence gives

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{P}(\Gamma_{s}\leq\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\to\sum_{i=1}^{K}\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{f}_{i}(u)\bar{f}_{j}(u)\prod_{k\neq i,j}\bar{F}_{k}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u=\kappa.

For the triple-gap bound, the case
K=2
is trivial. Assume
K\geq 3
, and
let

M_{\bar{f}}:=\max_{i\in[K]}\|\bar{f}_{i}\|_{\infty}<\infty.

If
\Gamma_{3,s}\leq\varepsilon
, then there exist three distinct arms

i,j,k
whose information coordinate values all lie in an interval of length

\varepsilon
below the largest of the three. Hence, by a union bound over
ordered triples,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Gamma_{3,s}\leq\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j,k\ {\rm distinct}}\int_{\mathcal{H}}\bar{f}_{i}(u)\left[\int_{u-\varepsilon}^{u}\bar{f}_{j}(v)\,\mathrm{d}v\right]\left[\int_{u-\varepsilon}^{u}\bar{f}_{k}(w)\,\mathrm{d}w\right]\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq K(K-1)(K-2)M_{\bar{f}}^{2}\varepsilon^{2}.

∎

Define the ordered top-pair tie manifold

\mathcal{T}^{\uparrow}:=\biguplus_{i\neq j}\left\{(i,j,u,\zeta_{-ij}):u\in\mathcal{H},\ \zeta_{k}\in\mathcal{H},\ \zeta_{k}<u\ \forall k\neq i,j\right\}.

Here
i
denotes the best arm and
j
denotes the second-best arm. For a
parameter vector
\eta
with unique best and second-best arms, define

\xi^{\uparrow}(\eta):=\bigl(b^{\star},b^{\star}_{2},\eta_{b^{\star}},(\eta_{k})_{k\neq b^{\star},b^{\star}_{2}}\bigr),

where
b^{\star}
is the best arm index, and
b^{\star}_{2}
is the second-best arm index. Note that it does not include
\eta_{b^{\star}_{2}}
, which is meant to be recovered by
\Gamma_{s}
.

On the component
(i,j)
, define the finite measure

\lambda(\mathrm{d}\xi):=\bar{f}_{i}(u)\bar{f}_{j}(u)\prod_{k\neq i,j}\bar{f}_{k}(\zeta_{k})\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}\zeta_{-ij}.

Then

\lambda(\mathcal{T}^{\uparrow})=\kappa
.

Lemma F.19

(Compact Localization of Top-Two Gap Density)
.

For every
\rho>0
, there exists a compact set

\mathcal{K}_{\rho}\subset\mathcal{T}^{\uparrow}

such that, for every fixed
R<\infty
,

\mathbb{P}\{\Gamma_{s}\leq R\varepsilon,\,\xi^{\uparrow}(\eta)\in\mathcal{K}_{\rho}\}\geq(\kappa-\rho)R\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon),

and

\mathbb{P}\{\Gamma_{s}\leq R\varepsilon,\,\xi^{\uparrow}(\eta)\notin\mathcal{K}_{\rho}\}\leq\rho R\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon),

as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{T}^{\uparrow}
is a finite disjoint union of open subsets of
Euclidean spaces and
\lambda
is a finite Borel measure with density with
respect to Lebesgue measure on each component,
\lambda
is a finite Radon
measure. In particular, it is inner regular. Choose a compact set
\mathcal{K}_{\rho}\subset\mathcal{T}^{\uparrow}

such that
\lambda(\mathcal{K}_{\rho})\geq\kappa-\rho
.

Because
\mathcal{K}_{\rho}
is compact and lies inside the open ordered tie
manifold, there exists
\ell_{\rho}>0
such that, for every

(i,j,u,\zeta_{-ij})\in\mathcal{K}_{\rho}
,
\zeta_{k}\leq u-\ell_{\rho}
for all
k\neq i,j
.

For all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, uniformly over

0\leq r\leq R\varepsilon
, the second-arm value
u-r
remains above all
background values on
\mathcal{K}_{\rho}
. Hence, restricted to

\mathcal{K}_{\rho}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\{\Gamma_{s}\leq R\varepsilon,\,\xi^{\uparrow}(\eta)\in\mathcal{K}_{\rho}\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}\int\mathbbm{1}_{\{(i,j,u,\zeta_{-ij})\in\mathcal{K}_{\rho}\}}\bar{f}_{i}(u)\prod_{k\neq i,j}\bar{f}_{k}(\zeta_{k})\left[\int_{u-R\varepsilon}^{u}\bar{f}_{j}(v)\,\mathrm{d}v\right]\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}\zeta_{-ij}.

By continuity of
\bar{f}_{j}
on the compact projection of

\mathcal{K}_{\rho}
,

\int_{u-R\varepsilon}^{u}\bar{f}_{j}(v)\,\mathrm{d}v=R\varepsilon\,\bar{f}_{j}(u)+o(\varepsilon)

uniformly over
\mathcal{K}_{\rho}
. Therefore

\mathbb{P}\{\Gamma_{s}\leq R\varepsilon,\,\xi^{\uparrow}(\eta)\in\mathcal{K}_{\rho}\}=R\varepsilon\,\lambda(\mathcal{K}_{\rho})+o(\varepsilon)\geq(\kappa-\rho)R\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon).

The complement bound follows by subtracting this lower bound from the total
top-two gap expansion:

\mathbb{P}(\Gamma_{s}\leq R\varepsilon)=\kappa R\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon).

∎

Lemma F.20

(Small Certificate Implies Small Information Gap)
.

There exists a constant
R^{\star}<\infty
, depending only on
K
and

\widetilde{c}
, such that

C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq u\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Gamma_{s}\leq R^{\star}\sqrt{u}.

Proof.

Fix
j\neq b^{\star}
, write

\Delta:=\Delta_{j,s}=\eta_{b^{\star}}-\eta_{j}
,
and

a:=w_{j}^{\dagger}(\eta)
.
For
z\in[\mu_{j},\mu_{b^{\star}}]
, write

r:=s(z)-\eta_{j}\in[0,\Delta]
.
By (
F.34
),

\displaystyle D(\mu_{b^{\star}},z)+aD(\mu_{j},z)

\displaystyle\geq\widetilde{c}\{(\Delta-r)^{2}+ar^{2}\}.

If
a=1
, then

\inf_{0\leq r\leq\Delta}\widetilde{c}\{(\Delta-r)^{2}+r^{2}\}=\frac{\widetilde{c}}{2}\Delta^{2}\geq\frac{\widetilde{c}}{2}\Gamma_{s}^{2}.

If
a<1
, then by (
F.45
),

a=\frac{\Gamma_{s}^{2}}{4\widetilde{c}\,\Delta^{2}}
.
Thus

\displaystyle\inf_{0\leq r\leq\Delta}\widetilde{c}\{(\Delta-r)^{2}+ar^{2}\}=\widetilde{c}\frac{a}{1+a}\Delta^{2}\geq\frac{\widetilde{c}a}{2}\Delta^{2}=\frac{\Gamma_{s}^{2}}{8},

where we used
a<1
. Therefore, for every challenger
j
,

\inf_{z\in[\mu_{j},\mu_{b^{\star}}]}\{D(\mu_{b^{\star}},z)+w_{j}^{\dagger}(\eta)D(\mu_{j},z)\}\geq c_{\rm gap}\Gamma_{s}^{2},

where
c_{\rm gap}:=\min\left\{\frac{\widetilde{c}}{2},\frac{1}{8}\right\}>0
.

Since
W^{\dagger}(\eta)\leq K
, (
F.47
) gives
C^{\dagger}(\mu)\geq\frac{c_{\rm gap}}{K}\Gamma_{s}^{2}
.
Hence
C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq u
implies

\Gamma_{s}\leq\sqrt{\frac{K}{c_{\rm gap}}}\sqrt{u}.

Taking
R^{\star}:=\sqrt{K/c_{\rm gap}}
gives the desired result.

∎

Lemma F.21

(Local Expansion of the Limiting Certificate)
.

Fix a compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{T}^{\uparrow}
. Uniformly over
parameter vectors satisfying

\xi^{\uparrow}(\eta)\in\mathcal{K}

and

\Gamma_{s}\downarrow 0
,
we have

C^{\dagger}(\mu)=\frac{\Gamma_{s}^{2}}{8}\{1+o(1)\}.

Proof.

Let
b^{\star}_{2}
denote the second-best arm and put
\gamma:=\Gamma_{s}
.

Because
\mathcal{K}
is compactly contained in

\mathcal{T}^{\uparrow}
, there exists
\ell>0
such that, whenever

\xi^{\uparrow}(\eta)\in\mathcal{K}
,

\eta_{b^{\star}}-\eta_{k}\geq\ell,\qquad k\notin\{b^{\star},b^{\star}_{2}\},

for all sufficiently small
\gamma
. Also, all relevant
information coordinate values remain in a compact subset of
\mathcal{H}
.

The KL divergence has the uniform local Fisher coordinate expansion

D(s^{-1}(\eta),s^{-1}(v))=\frac{1}{2}(\eta-v)^{2}+O(|\eta-v|^{3}),

(F.51)

for
\eta,v
in any compact subset of
\mathcal{H}
. This follows from a
Taylor expansion of the KL divergence and the definition of
s
.

For the top two arms,
w_{b^{\star}}^{\dagger}(\eta)=1
and
w_{b^{\star}_{2}}^{\dagger}(\eta)=\min\left\{1,\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4\widetilde{c}\,\gamma^{2}}\right\}=1,

Hence using
(
F.51
),

\displaystyle\inf_{z\in[\mu_{b^{\star}_{2}},\mu_{b^{\star}}]}\{D(\mu_{b^{\star}},z)+D(\mu_{b^{\star}_{2}},z)\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{w\in[\eta_{b^{\star}}-\gamma,\eta_{b^{\star}}]}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(\eta_{b^{\star}}-w)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(w-\eta_{b^{\star}}+\gamma)^{2}\right\}+o(\gamma^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}+o(\gamma^{2}).

For any other challenger
k\notin\{b^{\star},b^{\star}_{2}\}
, its information gap

\Delta_{k,s}:=\eta_{b^{\star}}-\eta_{k}
is bounded below by
\ell
. Therefore
w_{k}^{\dagger}(\eta)=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4\widetilde{c}\,\Delta_{k,s}^{2}}=O(\gamma^{2})
,
for all sufficiently small
\gamma
. Hence

W^{\dagger}(\eta)=1+w_{b^{\star}_{2}}^{\dagger}(\eta)+\sum_{k\notin\{b^{\star},b^{\star}_{2}\}}w_{k}^{\dagger}(\eta)=2+O(\gamma^{2}).

Furthermore, by the same coercivity calculation as in
Lemma
F.20
,

\displaystyle\inf_{z\in[\mu_{k},\mu_{b^{\star}}]}\{D(\mu_{b^{\star}},z)+w_{k}^{\dagger}(\eta)D(\mu_{k},z)\}\geq\widetilde{c}\frac{w_{k}^{\dagger}(\eta)}{1+w_{k}^{\dagger}(\eta)}\Delta_{k,s}^{2}=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\{1+o(1)\}.

Thus

\min_{j\neq b^{\star}}\inf_{z\in[\mu_{j},\mu_{b^{\star}}]}\{D(\mu_{b^{\star}},z)+w_{j}^{\dagger}(\eta)D(\mu_{j},z)\}=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\{1+o(1)\}.

Combining this with
W^{\dagger}(\eta)=2+o(1)
in (
F.47
) gives

C^{\dagger}(\mu)=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{8}\{1+o(1)\}.

The preceding estimates are uniform on
\mathcal{K}
, so the proof is complete.

∎

Lemma F.22

(Small Lower Tail of the Limiting Certificate)
.

As
u\downarrow 0
,

\mathbb{P}(C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq u)=\kappa\sqrt{8u}+o(\sqrt{u}).

Proof.

We prove matching lower and upper bounds.

For the lower bound, fix
\rho>0
and
\tau\in(0,1)
. Choose

\mathcal{K}_{\rho}
as in
Lemma
F.19
. By the uniform local
expansion in Lemma
F.21
, for all
sufficiently small
u
,

\left\{\Gamma_{s}\leq(1-\tau)\sqrt{8u},\ \xi^{\uparrow}(\eta)\in\mathcal{K}_{\rho}\right\}\subseteq\{C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq u\}.

Indeed, on
\mathcal{K}_{\rho}
,

C^{\dagger}(\mu)=\frac{\Gamma_{s}^{2}}{8}\{1+o(1)\},

uniformly as
\Gamma_{s}\downarrow 0
, and the factor

(1-\tau)^{2}
leaves enough slack. Therefore, using
Lemma
F.19
with

\varepsilon=\sqrt{u}
and
R=(1-\tau)\sqrt{8}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq u)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\Gamma_{s}\leq(1-\tau)\sqrt{8u},\ \xi^{\uparrow}(\eta)\in\mathcal{K}_{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle\geq(\kappa-\rho)(1-\tau)\sqrt{8u}+o(\sqrt{u}).

Letting
\rho\downarrow 0
and then
\tau\downarrow 0
yields

\liminf_{u\downarrow 0}\frac{\mathbb{P}(C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq u)}{\sqrt{u}}\geq\kappa\sqrt{8}.

For the upper bound, Lemma
F.20
gives

\{C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq u\}\subseteq\{\Gamma_{s}\leq R^{\star}\sqrt{u}\}.

Fix
\rho>0
, and again use the compact set
\mathcal{K}_{\rho}
. On

\{C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq u,\ \xi^{\uparrow}(\eta)\in\mathcal{K}_{\rho}\},

Lemma
F.20
implies

\Gamma_{s}\to 0
uniformly as
u\downarrow 0
. Hence the uniform local
expansion gives, for all sufficiently small
u
,

C^{\dagger}(\mu)\geq(1-\tau)\frac{\Gamma_{s}^{2}}{8}.

Consequently, on the same event,

\Gamma_{s}\leq\sqrt{\frac{8u}{1-\tau}}\leq(1+\tau)\sqrt{8u},

after decreasing
u
if necessary and replacing
\tau
by a smaller slack
constant. Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq u)\leq\mathbb{P}\{\Gamma_{s}\leq(1+\tau)\sqrt{8u}\}+\mathbb{P}\{\Gamma_{s}\leq R^{\star}\sqrt{u},\,\xi^{\uparrow}(\eta)\notin\mathcal{K}_{\rho}\}.

By Lemmas
F.18
and

F.19
,

\mathbb{P}(C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq u)\leq\kappa(1+\tau)\sqrt{8u}+\rho R^{\star}\sqrt{u}+o(\sqrt{u}).

Letting
u\downarrow 0
, then
\rho\downarrow 0
, and finally

\tau\downarrow 0
, gives

\limsup_{u\downarrow 0}\frac{\mathbb{P}(C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq u)}{\sqrt{u}}\leq\kappa\sqrt{8}.

Combining the two bounds proves the claim.

∎

F.3.8
Lower Bound on the Calibration Threshold

Lemma F.23

(Lower Bound on the Calibration Threshold)
.

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
\alpha_{0}>0
such that, for
every
0<\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}
, there exists

T_{0}(\alpha,\varepsilon)<\infty
such that, for all

T\geq T_{0}(\alpha,\varepsilon)
,

r_{T,\alpha}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\frac{\alpha^{2}T}{8\kappa^{2}}.

Proof.

Set
c:=\frac{1}{8\kappa^{2}}
and
u_{\alpha,\varepsilon}:=(1-\varepsilon)c\alpha^{2}
.
By Lemma
F.22
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq u_{\alpha,\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\kappa\sqrt{8(1-\varepsilon)c}\,\alpha+o(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}\,\alpha+o(\alpha).

Therefore, for all sufficiently small
\alpha
,

\mathbb{P}(C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq u_{\alpha,\varepsilon})<\alpha
.
By Lemma
F.17
,

\frac{R_{T}}{T}\to C^{\dagger}(\mu)\qquad\text{a.s.}

Thus
R_{T}/T
converges to
C^{\dagger}(\mu)
in distribution. Applying the
Portmanteau theorem to the closed set
(-\infty,u_{\alpha,\varepsilon}]

gives

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{R_{T}}{T}\leq u_{\alpha,\varepsilon}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}(C^{\dagger}(\mu)\leq u_{\alpha,\varepsilon})<\alpha.

Hence, for all sufficiently large
T
,

\mathbb{P}(R_{T}\leq u_{\alpha,\varepsilon}T)<\alpha
.
By the definition of
r_{T,\alpha}
as the lower
\alpha
-quantile of

R_{T}
,

r_{T,\alpha}\geq u_{\alpha,\varepsilon}T=(1-\varepsilon)\frac{\alpha^{2}T}{8\kappa^{2}}.

∎

F.3.9
Proof of Theorem
F.2
.

Proof.

We first prove abstention calibration. By the definition of the lower

\alpha
-quantile,

\mathbb{P}(R_{T}<r_{T,\alpha})\leq\alpha\leq\mathbb{P}(R_{T}\leq r_{T,\alpha}).

If
\mathbb{P}(R_{T}=r_{T,\alpha})>0
, the boundary randomization in
(
F.44
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\widehat{a}_{T}=?)=\mathbb{P}(R_{T}<r_{T,\alpha})+\theta_{T,\alpha}\mathbb{P}(R_{T}=r_{T,\alpha})=\alpha.

If
\mathbb{P}(R_{T}=r_{T,\alpha})=0
, then

\mathbb{P}(R_{T}<r_{T,\alpha})=\mathbb{P}(R_{T}\leq r_{T,\alpha})=\alpha,

by the defining property of the lower quantile and the absence of an atom at
the quantile. Since
\theta_{T,\alpha}=0
in this case, the same conclusion
holds. Therefore

\mathcal{A}_{T}(\textsc{IC-PGWS}(\alpha))=\alpha.

For the undetected error probability,
Lemma
F.12
gives

\mathcal{E}_{T}(\textsc{IC-PGWS}(\alpha))\leq(K-1)e^{-r_{T,\alpha}}.

By Lemma
F.23
, for every

\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, all sufficiently small
\alpha
, and all sufficiently
large
T
,

r_{T,\alpha}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\frac{\alpha^{2}T}{8\kappa^{2}}.

Hence

\mathcal{E}_{T}(\textsc{IC-PGWS}(\alpha))\leq(K-1)\exp\left\{-(1-\varepsilon)\frac{\alpha^{2}T}{8\kappa^{2}}\right\}.

Taking logarithms, dividing by
\alpha^{2}T
, taking

T\to\infty
, then
\alpha\downarrow 0
, and finally

\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, yields

\limsup_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}T}\log\mathcal{E}_{T}(\textsc{IC-PGWS}(\alpha))\leq-\frac{1}{8\kappa^{2}}.

∎
