Introduction
In the framework of the ongoing project "Design and Development of Complex Bend Magnet
System for Testing in Storage Ring Operations", a full set of Complex Bend magnets is being
designed with associated vacuum chambers and mechanical supports for temporary installation in
the NSLS-II storage ring replacing two existing dipole magnets as shown in Fig. 1. These CB
magnets will be fabricated, assembled and tested in preparation for commissioning with the 3-GeV
electron beam under normal operating conditions for an extended duration.
With the installed magnets, detailed beam dynamics studies are planned to quantify every aspect
of the CB’s influence on the beam performance and de-risk CB technology from unexpected
harmful effects imposed by beam operations.
For accurate measurement of the beam position in the CB, we plan to install two button-type beam
position monitors (BPMs) close to each magnet. We analyzed the BPM sensitivity and spatial
linearity; the results are summarized in this technical note.

Summary
We analyzed the performance of a button-type electrostatic BPM for the Complex Bend Test in
the NSLS-II Storage Ring in terms of the sensitivity, spatial resolution, and linearity. A Gaussian
bunch with a charge of 1.3 nC and an RMS length of 6 mm (20 ps) circulating in the NSLS-II
Storage Ring generates a signal of about 150 mV amplitude at the output of a bandpass filter with
a 500 MHz central frequency and 20 MHz bandwidth. For a train of 1000 bunches with a total
current of 500 mA (user operations), the signal amplitude is 1.5 V. For a train of 100 bunches with
a total current of 1.5 mA (beam studies), the signal amplitude is 50 mV. A 2D 5th-order
polynomial fit is quite efficient for correcting the BPM nonlinearity in the horizontal range of
±5 mm and the vertical range of ±2.5 mm.
