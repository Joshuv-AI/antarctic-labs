Faà di Bruno is Taylor Composition
Heinrich Hartmann

Abstract
We approach Faà di Bruno as a composition theorem for Taylor polynomials. For C k C^{k} maps ϕ : E → F \phi:E\to F and ψ : F → G \psi:F\to G between Banach spaces, let T ∗ k ​ ( ϕ , x ) T^{k}_{\ast}(\phi;x) denote the reduced Taylor polynomial of ϕ \phi at x x , obtained by removing the constant term. We show that T ∗ k ​ ( ψ ∘ ϕ , x ) = π ≤ k ​ ( T ∗ k ​ ( ψ , ϕ ⁡ ( x ) ) ∘ T ∗ k ​ ( ϕ , x ) ) . T^{k}_{\ast}(\psi\circ\phi;x)=\pi_{\leq k}\bigl(T^{k}_{\ast}(\psi;\phi(x))\circ T^{k}_{\ast}(\phi;x)\bigr). The proof is an elementary estimate of the Peano remainder and does not use partitions or combinatorial enumeration. Expanding this composition identity recovers the classical Faà di Bruno formulas. Polarization gives the multivariate partition formula (Lévy 2006), while coefficient extraction gives the multi-index formula (Constantine and Savits 1996). Our approach separates the functorial nature of Taylor approximation from the combinatorial bookkeeping of polarization and coefficient extraction. As an application, we give a general higher-order product rule.

1
Introduction

Faà di Bruno formulas describe the higher-order behavior of derivatives
under composition. In one variable, the formula expresses the
n
-th
derivative of
f\circ g
as a universal polynomial in the derivatives
of
f
and
g
. For
f,g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, this formula was first
derived in 1855 by Faà di Bruno
[
2
]
:

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\bigl(f\circ g\bigr)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f^{(k)}(g(x))\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ast)\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{n}!}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl(\frac{g^{(j)}(x)}{j!}\Bigr)^{m_{j}},

where
(\ast)
runs over all tuples
m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with

m_{1}+\dots+m_{n}=k
and
m_{1}+2m_{2}+\dots+nm_{n}=n
. The multivariate
generalization to maps
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}

was established by Constantine and Savits
[
1
]
, who gave a
complete explicit formula for
\partial^{\alpha}(f\circ\phi)
in full
multi-index form:

\frac{\partial^{\alpha}(f\circ\phi)(x)}{\alpha!}\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\\
\\
1\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|\end{subarray}}\partial^{\beta}f(y)\;\sum_{s\geq 1}\;\sum_{(\ast)}\;\prod_{r=1}^{s}\frac{1}{\beta_{r}!}\frac{\big(\partial^{\alpha_{r}}\phi(x)\big)^{\beta_{r}}}{\alpha_{r}!^{|\beta_{r}|}},

where
(\ast)
runs over tuples

(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s};\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{s})
with

\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,

\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,

\alpha_{1}<\cdots<\alpha_{s}
, subject to

\sum_{r}\alpha_{r}|\beta_{r}|=\alpha
and
\sum_{r}\beta_{r}=\beta
.

We give a functorial repackaging of these formulas, following the
philosophy that differentiable functions are functions well approximated
by polynomials, and the composition of differentiable functions should
be well approximated by the composition of their Taylor polynomials. Our
main result makes this precise:

(1) Theorem

(Taylor Composition
(13)
)
.

Let
E,F,G
be Banach spaces,
U_{E}\subset E
and

U_{F}\subset F
open. Let
\phi\in C^{k}(U_{E},F)
and

\psi\in C^{k}(U_{F},G)
with
y=\phi(x)\in U_{F}
. Then

\psi\circ\phi\in C^{k}(U_{E},G)
and the reduced Taylor polynomials
compose:

T_{\ast}^{k}(\psi\circ\phi;\,x)=\pi_{\leq k}\bigl(T_{\ast}^{k}(\psi;\,y)\circ T_{\ast}^{k}(\phi;\,x)\bigr).

The proof starts directly from the Peano remainder form of the Taylor
approximation and establishes the Peano bounds for the composition. This
is a straightforward argument that requires neither heavy combinatorics,
multi-index calculations, nor partition arguments.

The functoriality of Taylor approximation under composition is certainly
well known to experts and mentioned in passing, e.g. Malgrange
(
[
7
]
, p.16). However, we are not aware of a self-contained
treatment in this generality. Hernández Encinas and Muñoz Masqué

[
4
]
are close in spirit, but work in the finite-dimensional
smooth setting and employ coordinate algebra on truncated local rings,
instead of bounding the Taylor residual directly.

From this composition principle we derive independent proofs of both
combinatorial forms. The partition form (Theorem
(16)
,

[
6
]
) is obtained by polarization, extracting symmetric
multilinear forms from homogeneous polynomials:

D(\psi\circ\phi;\,x;\,u_{1},\dots,u_{k})=\sum_{\pi\vdash[k]}D\bigl(\psi;\,y;\,(D(\phi;\,x;\,u_{B}))_{B\in\pi}\bigr).

The multi-index form (Theorem
(19)
,
[
1
]
) is
obtained by monomial coefficient extraction from the composed Taylor
polynomials, recovering the formula above.

In summary, this note gives a short, self-contained account in the
general Banach space setting that presents all three forms of Faà di
Bruno in their natural relationship: Taylor composition as the analytic
core, the partition formula as its polarization, and the multi-index
formula as its coefficient extraction. As an application we derive a
higher-order product rule in both partition and multi-index form.

(2) Remark

(Notation)
.

In the following we will use the following notational conventions. We
generally prefer explicit arguments over subscript notation, writing
e.g.
\Delta(f;x;v)
instead of
\Delta_{v}f(x)
. Semicolons and
commas are both argument separators; we use semicolons as a visual hint
when arguments are of different kinds, as in

D(\phi;x;v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
. Dropping trailing arguments denotes
currying: for a function
f(x;y;z)
, we write
f(x;y)
for the
map
z\mapsto f(x;y;z)
. Thus
D(\phi;x;u_{1},\dots,u_{k})

denotes the
k
-th derivative evaluated on directions; dropping the
vector arguments,
D(\phi;x)
denotes the corresponding multilinear
map when
k
is clear from context. Similarly
T_{\ast}^{k}(\phi;x)

is a polynomial map and
T_{\ast}^{k}(\phi;x;v)
is its evaluation at

v
.

2
Polynomials in Banach
Spaces

In this section we develop the basic theory of polynomials in the
setting of Banach spaces, as found e.g. in Dineen
[
3
]
.
The reader who is only interested in the finite-dimensional setting can
safely skip this section.

Let
E,F
be Banach spaces. We write
\mathcal{L}(E,F)
for the Banach
space of bounded linear maps
E\to F
with operator norm

\|A\|=\sup_{\|v\|\leq 1}\|A(v)\|
, and more generally

\mathcal{L}(^{k}E,F)
for the Banach space of bounded
k
-fold multilinear
maps
E^{\times k}\to F
with norm

\|A_{k}\|=\sup_{\|v_{i}\|\leq 1}\|A_{k}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\|
. We write

\mathcal{L}^{s}(^{k}E,F)\subset\mathcal{L}(^{k}E,F)
for the subspace of symmetric
multilinear maps.

(3) Definition

(Polynomials)
.

A map
p:E\to F
is a
k
-homogeneous polynomial
if there
exists
A\in\mathcal{L}^{s}(^{k}E,F)
such that

p(x)=\frac{1}{k!}\,A[x,\dots,x]=\frac{1}{k!}\,A[x^{\otimes k}],\qquad x\in E.

We write
\mathcal{P}^{k}(E,F)
for the space of such maps. A

polynomial map

p:E\to F
is a finite sum of homogeneous
polynomials
p=\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{k}
with

p_{k}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(E,F)
. We write
\mathcal{P}(E,F)
for the
space of all polynomial maps,
\mathcal{P}_{\leq n}(E,F)
for those of
degree
\leq n
, and
\mathcal{P}(E):=\mathcal{P}(E,\mathbb{R})
.

(4) Definition

(Forward differences)
.

For
f:E\to F
and
x,v\in E
, define the
forward
difference

\Delta(f;x;v):=f(x+v)-f(x).

The
iterated forward difference
is defined recursively:

\Delta(f;x):=f(x)
for
k=0
, and

\Delta(f;x;v_{1},\dots,v_{k}):=\Delta(w\mapsto\Delta(f;x;v_{2},\dots,v_{k});\,x;\,v_{1})

for
k\geq 1
. Since the operators
\Delta_{v_{i}}
commute, this
is symmetric in
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
. Expanding the recursion gives the
alternating sum over vertices of the
k
-cube:

\Delta(f;x;v_{1},\dots,v_{k})=\sum_{S\subseteq[k]}(-1)^{k-|S|}\,f(x+\textstyle\sum_{i\in S}v_{i}).

(5) Lemma

(Polarization)
.

Let
p:E\to F
be a polynomial map,
represented as
p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}A_{k}[x^{\otimes k}]

with
A_{k}\in\mathcal{L}^{s}(^{k}E,F)
. Then the top-degree multi-linear form
is recovered as a forward difference:

A_{n}[v_{1},\dots,v_{n}]=\Delta(p;0;v_{1},\dots,v_{n}).

Furthermore, every polynomial map
p
has a unique representation

p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}A_{k}[x^{\otimes k}]
with

A_{k}\in\mathcal{L}^{s}(^{k}E,F)
.

Proof.

The polarization formula is Möbius inversion on the Boolean lattice

2^{[n]}
: By multilinearity,

p(\sum_{i\in I}v_{i})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\in I}\frac{1}{k!}A_{k}[v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{k}}]=\sum_{J\subset I}A_{|J|}[v_{J}]

where
A_{k}[v_{J}]
denotes evaluation on the
|J|
-tuple indexed by

J
. The alternating sum
\sum_{I\subset[n]}(-1)^{n-|I|}
inverts
this: all terms with
J\subsetneq[n]
cancel by the identity

\sum_{I\supset J}(-1)^{n-|I|}=(1-1)^{n-|J|}=0
, leaving only

J=[n]
. Uniqueness follows by induction: recover
A_{n}
via
polarization, subtract, and repeat.

∎

(6) Definition

(Degree Truncation)
.

Let
p:E\rightarrow F
be a polynomial map. By Lemma
(5)

the representation

p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}A_{k}[x^{\otimes k}]
is unique. We
can hence define:

•

\deg(p):=n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the degree of
p
. By convention

\deg(0)=-\infty
.

•

\pi_{k}(p):=p_{k}:E\rightarrow F
the degree-k part of
p
.

•

\Pol_{k}(p):=A_{k}\in\mathcal{L}^{s}(^{k}E,F)
the degree-k polarization of

p
.

•

\pi_{\leq k}(p)\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq k}(E,F)
the (lower) degree
truncation of
p
.

•

\pi_{>k}(p)\in\mathcal{P}_{>k}(E,F)
the upper degree
truncation of
p
.

We will make use of the following properties of polynomial maps:

(7) Lemma

(Polynomial Properties)
.

Let
p:E\rightarrow F
,
q:F\rightarrow G
be polynomial
maps.

1.

(Composition) The composition
q\circ p:E\rightarrow G
is a polynomial
with
\deg(q\circ p)\leq\deg(q)\cdot\deg(p)
.

2.

(Lipschitz) There exists
\varepsilon>0,C>0
such that

\|\Delta(p;0;x)\|\leq C\|x\|
for all
\|x\|<\varepsilon
.

3.

(Upper Vanishing) We have
\|\pi_{>k}(p)(x)\|/\|x\|^{k}\rightarrow 0

for
x\rightarrow 0
.

4.

(Lower Vanishing) If
\|p(x)\|/\|x\|^{k}\rightarrow 0
for
x\rightarrow 0
,
and
k\geq\deg(p)
then
p=0
.

Proof.

Ad 1) Expanding
q(p(x))
by multilinearity yields a sum of terms

B[A_{1}[x^{\otimes j_{1}}],\dots,A_{\ell}[x^{\otimes j_{\ell}}]]
where

B\in\mathcal{L}(^{\ell}F,G)
and
A_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(^{j_{i}}E,F)
are bounded
multilinear.

The composition

(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\mapsto B[A_{1}[u_{I_{1}}],\dots,A_{\ell}[u_{I_{\ell}}]]

is bounded multilinear, hence each term is a homogeneous polynomial of
degree
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{\ell}\leq\ell\cdot\deg(p)
. Thus

q\circ p
is a polynomial with

\deg(q\circ p)\leq\deg(q)\cdot\deg(p)
.

Ad 2) Write

\Delta(p;0;x)=p(x)-p(0)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{j!}A_{j}[x^{\otimes j}]

with
A_{j}\in\mathcal{L}^{s}(^{j}E,F)
bounded. For
\|x\|<1
we have

\|x\|^{j}\leq\|x\|
, hence

\|\Delta(p;0;x)\|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\|A_{j}\|}{j!}\|x\|^{j}\leq C\|x\|

with
C=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\|A_{j}\|}{j!}
.

Ad 3) Each homogeneous component
p_{j}
of degree
j>k
satisfies

\|p_{j}(x)\|\leq C_{j}\|x\|^{j}
, hence

\|p_{j}(x)\|/\|x\|^{k}=C_{j}\|x\|^{j-k}\to 0
as
x\to 0
.

Ad 4) If
p\neq 0
, let

j_{0}:=\min\{j:p_{j}\neq 0\}\leq\deg(p)\leq k
and choose

v\in E
with
p_{j_{0}}(v)\neq 0
. Then

p(tv)=t^{j_{0}}p_{j_{0}}(v)+O(t^{j_{0}+1})
. If
j_{0}<k
, then

\|p(tv)\|/t^{k}
diverges as
t\to 0^{+}
. If
j_{0}=k
, then

\|p(tv)\|/t^{k}\to\|p_{j_{0}}(v)\|\neq 0
. In either case

p=o(\|x\|^{k})
is impossible.

∎

3
Fréchet Differentiability

We summarize the classical notion of Fréchet differentiability following

[
5
]
.

(8) Definition

(Fréchet differentiability
[
5
]
XIII.6)
.

Let
E,F
be Banach spaces,
U_{E}\subset E
an open subset and

\phi:U_{E}\rightarrow F
a map.

•

(Fréchet differentiable) We say that
\phi
is Fréchet
differentiable at a point
x\in U_{E}
if there exists a bounded
linear map
L\in\mathcal{L}(E,F)
such that

\lim_{v\to 0}\frac{1}{\|v\|}\,\|\Delta(\phi;x;v)-L(v)\|=0.

The map
L
is uniquely determined and written
D(\phi;x)
,
so that
D(\phi;x;v)=L(v)
.

•

(
C^{1}
maps) We say that
\phi
is continuously Fréchet
differentiable (
C^{1}
) on
U_{E}
if
D(\phi;x)
exists for every

x\in U_{E}
and the map
x\mapsto D(\phi;x)\in\mathcal{L}(E,F)
is
continuous.

•

(
C^{k}
maps) Recursively,
\phi
is
k
-times continuously
Fréchet differentiable (
C^{k}
) if
\phi
is
C^{1}
and

x\mapsto D(\phi;x)
is
C^{k-1}
. We write
C^{k}(U_{E},F)
for
the space of such maps, and denote by

D^{k}(\phi;x;v_{1},\dots,v_{k}):=D(z\mapsto D^{k-1}(\phi;z;v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1});\,x;\,v_{k})

the
k
-th Fréchet differential. This is a symmetric

k
-linear form:
D^{k}(\phi;x)\in\mathcal{L}^{s}(^{k}E,F)
. When the order
is clear from context, we write
D(\phi;x;v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
for

D^{k}(\phi;x;v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
.

(9) Definition

(Taylor polynomials)
.

Let
\phi\in C^{k}(U_{E},F)
and
x\in U_{E}
. The

Taylor polynomial
of
\phi
at
x
is

T^{k}(\phi;x;v):=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{1}{\ell!}D^{\ell}(\phi;x;v,\dots,v),\quad T^{k}(\phi;x)\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq k}(E,F).

The
reduced Taylor polynomial
is

T_{\ast}^{k}(\phi;x;v):=T^{k}(\phi;x;v)-\phi(x)
, so that

T_{\ast}^{k}(\phi;x;0)=0
.

(10) Remark
.

The full Taylor polynomial
T^{k}
is the correct object for studying
the commutative algebra of functions under pointwise multiplication. The
reduced Taylor polynomial
T_{\ast}^{k}
is the correct object for
studying functions under composition. We will focus on the reduced
version in what follows, returning to the full version for the Leibniz
rule (
(21)
).

(11) Proposition

(Taylor Approximation
[
5
]
, XIII.6)
.

Let
\phi\in C^{k}(U_{E},F)
and
x\in U_{E}
.

1.

The Taylor remainder in Peano form

R^{k}(\phi;x):=\Delta(\phi;x)-T_{\ast}^{k}(\phi;x)
satisfies:

\Delta(\phi;x;v)=T_{\ast}^{k}(\phi;x;v)+R^{k}(\phi;x;v)

and
\|R^{k}(\phi;x;v)\|/\|v\|^{k}\to 0
as
v\to 0
.

2.

(Uniqueness) Let
\Delta(\phi;x)=T+R
be any other
decomposition with
T\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq k}(E,F)
,
T(0)=0
,
and
\|R(v)\|/\|v\|^{k}\rightarrow 0
as
v\rightarrow 0
. Then

T=T_{\ast}^{k}(\phi;x)
and
R=R^{k}(\phi;x)
.

Proof.

Part 1 is standard (
[
5
]
, XIII.6). For the uniqueness (part
2), assume
\Delta(\phi;x)=T+R=T^{\prime}+R^{\prime}
are two such
decompositions. Then
p=T-T^{\prime}=R-R^{\prime}
is a polynomial of degree

\leq k
with
\|p(v)\|/\|v\|^{k}\rightarrow 0
for
v\rightarrow 0
. By Lemma

(7)
we must have
p=0
.

∎

(12) Lemma

(Lipschitz Continuity)
.

If
\phi\in C^{k}(U_{E},F)
with
k\geq 1
,
then
\phi
is locally Lipschitz continuous: for every
x\in U_{E}

there exist
C>0,\varepsilon>0
such that

\|\Delta(\phi;x;v)\|\leq C\|v\|
for all
\|v\|<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

We may assume
x=0
. Write
T_{\ast}^{k}(\phi;0)=P
and

R^{k}(\phi;0)=R
. By Lemma
(7)
there exist

\varepsilon>0
and
C_{1}>0
such that
\|P(v)\|\leq C_{1}\|v\|
for

\|v\|<\varepsilon
. Since
R(v)=o(\|v\|^{k})
we have

\|R(v)\|\leq C_{2}\|v\|^{k}\leq C_{2}\|v\|
for

\|v\|<\min(\varepsilon,1)
. Thus

\|\Delta(\phi;0;v)\|\leq\|P(v)\|+\|R(v)\|\leq(C_{1}+C_{2})\|v\|

for
\|v\|
small.

∎

4
Taylor Composition

We now prove the main result: reduced Taylor polynomials compose, up to
degree truncation.

(13) Theorem

(Taylor Composition)
.

Let
E,F,G
be Banach spaces. Let

U_{E}\subset E
be open,
x\in U_{E}
, and
\phi\in C^{k}(U_{E},F)
.
Let
U_{F}\subset F
be open with
y=\phi(x)\in U_{F}
and

\psi\in C^{k}(U_{F},G)
.

Then
\psi\circ\phi\in C^{k}(U_{E},G)
and the reduced Taylor
polynomials compose:

T_{\ast}^{k}(\psi\circ\phi;\,x)=\pi_{\leq k}\bigl(T_{\ast}^{k}(\psi;\,y)\circ T_{\ast}^{k}(\phi;\,x)\bigr).

Proof.

By translation, we may assume
x=0
and
\phi(x)=y=0
as well as

\psi(y)=0
throughout the proof. In particular

\Delta(\phi;0)=\phi
,
\Delta(\psi;0)=\psi
.

Choose Taylor decompositions
\phi=P+R
and
\psi=Q+S
, with
polynomials
P,Q
of degree
\leq k
and remainders
R,S
vanishing
to order
k
at
0
. Now write

\psi\circ\phi=Q(\phi)+S(\phi)=\pi_{\leq k}(Q\circ P)+E
, with:

E:=Q(\phi)+S(\phi)-Q(P)+\pi_{>k}(Q\circ P).

We have to show that
\|E(x)\|/\|x\|^{k}\to 0
for
x\to 0
.

Clearly
\|\pi_{>k}(Q\circ P)\|/\|x\|^{k}\to 0
for
x\to 0
by
Lemma
(7)
.

To see that
\|S(\phi(x))\|/\|x\|^{k}\to 0
we argue as follows: By the
Lipschitz property of
\phi
(Lemma
(12)
), we find

\delta>0,C>0
so that
\|\phi(x)\|<C\|x\|
for all

\|x\|<\delta
. Since
S
is a Taylor residual we have

\|S(y)\|<\eta(y)\|y\|^{k}
with
\eta(y)\to 0
as
y\to 0
.
Hence
\|S(\phi(x))\|/\|x\|^{k}\leq\eta(\phi(x))C^{k}\to 0
as

x\to 0
.

For the term
Q(P+R)-Q(P)
, we decompose

Q(y)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}Q_{\ell}(y)=\sum_{\ell}\frac{1}{\ell!}B_{\ell}[y^{\otimes\ell}]

where
Q_{\ell}=\pi_{\ell}Q
and
B_{\ell}=\Pol_{\ell}Q
. Then

Q_{\ell}(P+R)-Q_{\ell}(P)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=\ell\\
j\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{i!\,j!}\,B_{\ell}\big(P^{\otimes i},R^{\otimes j}\big).

It suffices to show that each summand

B_{\ell}\big(P^{\otimes i},R^{\otimes j})
is in
o(\|x\|^{k})
. To
this end we note that:

•

\|B_{\ell}(P(x)^{\otimes i},R(x)^{\otimes j})\|\leq\|B_{\ell}\|\cdot\|P(x)\|^{i}\|R(x)\|^{j}

as
B_{\ell}\in\mathcal{L}^{s}(^{\ell}F,G)
bounded.

•

\|P(x)\|\leq C\|x\|
for
\|x\|<\delta
as
P
is Lipschitz
(Lemma
(7)
) and
P(0)=0
.

•

\|R(x)\|<\eta(x)\|x\|^{k}
with
\eta(x)\rightarrow 0
for
x\to 0

as
R
is a Taylor remainder.

So that:

\|B_{\ell}(P^{\otimes i}(x),R^{\otimes j}(x))\|/\|x\|^{k}\leq\|B_{\ell}\|\;C^{i}\;\eta(x)^{j}\;\|x\|^{i+(j-1)k}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad x\to 0

For the last step, note that
j\geq 1
so the factor

\eta(x)^{j}\to 0
as
x\to 0
, and
i+(j-1)k\geq 0
hence

\|x\|^{i+(j-1)k}
stays bounded (and even goes to zero for

j\geq 2
).

∎

(14) Remark

(Pointwise Taylor approximation)
.

The assumptions of this theorem can be slightly weakened. The proof does
not use the existence of derivatives in a neighborhood or the continuity
of
z\mapsto D^{j}(\phi;z)
. What is needed is only a decomposition

\Delta(\phi;x)=P+R
with
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\leq k}(E,F)
,

P(0)=0
, and
\|R(v)\|/\|v\|^{k}\to 0
as
v\to 0
(the Peano
remainder condition), and similarly for
\psi
at
y
. The theorem
therefore applies to a broader class of functions that are well
approximated by a polynomial at a single point.

5
Partition Form Faà di
Bruno

The partition form of Faà di Bruno is obtained by polarizing the Taylor
composition theorem: extracting the degree-
k
symmetric multilinear
part from both sides.

(15) Definition
.

Let
I
be a finite set.

•

An un-ordered (non-empty) partition
\pi
of
I
is a set

\pi=\{B_{1},\dots,B_{r}\}
of subsets
B_{i}\subset I

(“blocks”), so that
B_{i}
are disjoint,
I=\cup_{i=1}^{r}B_{i}

and
B_{i}\neq\emptyset
. We write
\pi\vdash I
in this case and
call
|\pi|=r
the rank of
\pi
.

•

An
ordered
r
-decomposition
of
I
is a tuple

(B_{1},\dots,B_{r})
of subsets
B_{i}\subset I
, so that
B_{i}
are
disjoint and
I=\cup_{i=1}^{r}B_{i}
. We write

(B_{1},\dots,B_{r})\models I
in this case.

Note that we allow empty blocks, so that this datum is equivalent to a
map
\sigma:I\rightarrow[r]
with
B_{i}=\sigma^{-1}\{i\}
.

•

For a set
X
, a tuple
v=(v_{i})_{i\in I}\in X^{I}
and a block

B\subset I
, we write
v_{B}:=(v_{b})_{b\in B}\in X^{B}
for the
restricted tuple.

(16) Theorem

(Multivariate Faà di Bruno - Partition Form, cf.
[
6
]
)
.

Let
x\in U_{E}\subset E
and set

y:=\phi(x)\in F
. Assume that
\phi\in C^{k}(U_{E},F)
and

\psi\in C^{k}(U_{F},G)
. For
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in E
we have:

D(\psi\circ\phi;\,x;\,u_{1},\dots,u_{k})=\sum_{\pi\vdash[k]}D\bigl(\psi;\,y;\,(D(\phi;\,x;\,u_{B}))_{B\in\pi}\bigr).

The order of the blocks is immaterial because higher derivatives are
symmetric.

Proof.

Fix
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in E
. By translation, we may again assume
x=0

and
y=0
throughout the proof. Fix Taylor decompositions

\phi=P+R
,
\psi=Q+S
with
P,Q
polynomial of degree

\leq k
. By
(13)
we have

T_{\ast}^{k}(\psi\circ\phi;0)=\pi_{\leq k}(Q\circ P)
, and hence

D(\psi\circ\phi;0)=\Pol_{k}(Q\circ P)
.

Write the polynomial maps in polarized form as

P(x)=\sum_{m=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m!}\,P_{m}[x^{\otimes m}],\qquad Q(y)=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\frac{1}{r!}\,Q_{r}[y^{\otimes r}].

Expanding
Q_{r}
by multilinearity and isolating the degree
k
term
gives:

\pi_{k}Q(P(x))=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\geq 1\\
\\
m_{1}+\cdots+m_{r}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{r!}Q_{r}\bigl[\frac{1}{m_{1}!}P_{m_{1}}[x^{\otimes m_{1}}],\dots,\frac{1}{m_{r}!}P_{m_{r}}[x^{\otimes m_{r}}]\bigr].

Applying
\Pol_{k}
on both sides using Lemma
(17)
below
gives:

D(\psi\circ\phi;0;u_{1},\dots,u_{k})=\sum_{r=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\geq 1\\
\\
m_{1}+\cdots+m_{r}=k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(B_{1},\dots,B_{r})\models[k]\\
\\
|B_{i}|=m_{i}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{r!}Q_{r}\bigl[P_{m_{1}}[u_{B_{1}}],\dots,P_{m_{r}}[u_{B_{r}}]\bigr].

Now observe that there are exactly
r!
re-orderings of each ordered
decomposition
(B_{1},\dots,B_{r})
, and these re-orderings yield the same
summand as
Q_{r}
is symmetric. Hence the sum over

m_{i},B\models[k]
can be replaced by a single sum over un-ordered
partitions
\pi\vdash[k]
with given block sizes
|B_{i}|=m_{i}
.
Furthermore we have
Q_{r}[v_{1},\dots,v_{r}]=D(\psi;0;v_{1},\dots,v_{r})

and
P_{m}[u_{1},\dots,u_{m}]=D(\phi;0;u_{1},\dots,u_{m})
by definition.
Substituting these into the above formulas yields the claim.

∎

It remains to prove the composition polarization lemma used above.

(17) Lemma

(Composition Polarization)
.

If
k=m_{1}+\dots+m_{r}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and

A_{i}\in\mathcal{L}^{s}(^{m_{i}}E,F)
, and
B\in\mathcal{L}^{s}(^{r}F,G)
. Let

H(x)=B[\frac{1}{m_{1}!}A_{1}[x^{\otimes m_{1}}],\dots,\frac{1}{m_{r}!}A_{r}[x^{\otimes m_{r}}]]

then:

\Pol_{k}(H)[u_{1},\dots,u_{k}]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(B_{1},\dots,B_{r})\models[k],\\
\\
|B_{i}|=m_{i}\end{subarray}}B[A_{1}[u_{B_{1}}],\dots,A_{r}[u_{B_{r}}]].

Proof.

Let
G[u_{1},\dots,u_{k}]
be the claimed expression for

\Pol_{k}(H)[u_{1},\dots,u_{k}]
. As both sides are symmetric, bounded

k
-multilinear forms in
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
we only have to validate
that
\frac{1}{k!}G[x^{\otimes k}]=H
. We find:

G[x^{\otimes k}]=\sum_{(B_{1},\dots,B_{r})\models[k],\\
\\
|B_{i}|=m_{i}}B[A_{1}[x^{\otimes m_{1}}],\dots,A_{r}[x^{\otimes m_{r}}]]=\frac{k!}{m_{1}!\dots m_{r}!}\cdot B[A_{1}[x^{\otimes m_{1}}],\dots,A_{r}[x^{\otimes m_{r}}]]

as there are
k!/m_{1}!\dots m_{r}!
ordered decompositions of

[k]
into subsets of sizes
m_{1},\dots,m_{r}
. Re-arranging the
factorials yields the claim.

∎

6
Multi-index Faà di Bruno

In coordinates, the Taylor composition theorem yields an explicit
formula for partial derivatives of a composition by monomial coefficient
extraction.

(18) Definition

(Multi-Indices)
.

•

Elements
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
are called
multi-indices of dimension
d
. We write
|\nu|=\sum_{i}\nu_{i}

for the degree and
\nu!:=\nu_{1}!\cdots\nu_{d}!
for the factorial.

•

If
A
is a commutative algebra and
v\in A^{d}
is a
d
-tuple,
then we write:
v^{\nu}=\prod_{i}v_{i}^{\nu_{i}}\in A
.

•

If
E=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, write
v^{1},\dots,v^{d}:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
for the
coordinate projections. The monomials
v^{\nu}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
,
form a basis of
\mathcal{P}(E)
.

•

If
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is open and
\phi\in C^{k}(U,F)
with

x\in U
. Let
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
k=|\nu|
. We define the
partial derivative
\partial^{\nu}\phi(x)\in F
via the basis expansion
of

T_{\ast}^{k}(\phi;x)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d},F)=\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\otimes F

as coefficient of
v^{\nu}/\nu!
:

T_{\ast}^{k}(\phi;x;v)=\sum_{1\leq|\nu|\leq k}\frac{v^{\nu}}{\nu!}\;\partial^{\nu}\phi(x).

(19) Theorem

(Multivariate Faà di Bruno - Multi-index Form, cf.
[
1
]
)
.

Let
x\in U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and set

y:=\phi(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that
\phi\in C^{k}(U,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

f\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
,
\alpha\neq 0
the partial derivatives of
the composition are given by:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{\alpha}(f\circ\phi)(x)}{\alpha!}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\partial^{\beta}f(y)\,\sum_{\rho\,(\ast)}\Bigl(\prod_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\frac{1}{\rho(\gamma)!}\frac{(\partial^{\gamma}\phi(x))^{\rho(\gamma)}}{(\gamma!)^{|\rho(\gamma)|}}\Bigr)

where
(\ast)
runs over all finitely supported maps

\rho:\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
satisfying

\sum_{\gamma}\rho(\gamma)=\beta
and

\sum_{\gamma}\gamma|\rho(\gamma)|=\alpha
.

This is equivalent to the tuple form stated in the introduction:
enumerating the support of
\rho
in any total order on
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}

recovers the Constantine–Savits formula.

Proof.

We may assume that
x=y=f(y)=0
, without loss of generality.

Step 1: Taylor Expansion) Write
v^{1},\dots,v^{d}
for the coordinate
functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
w^{1},\dots,w^{n}
for the coordinates on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Expand the Taylor polynomials of
\phi
and
f
as

P(v)=T_{\ast}^{k}(\phi;0;v)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
\\
1\leq|\alpha|\leq k\end{subarray}}\frac{v^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\;p_{\alpha},\qquad F(w)=T_{\ast}^{k}(f;0;w)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\\
\\
1\leq|\beta|\leq k\end{subarray}}\frac{w^{\beta}}{\beta!}\;f_{\beta},

with
p_{\alpha}=\partial^{\alpha}\phi(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

f_{\beta}=\partial^{\beta}f(0)\in\mathbb{R}
.

Step 2: Polynomial Composition) By Theorem
(13)
we have

\partial^{\nu}(f\circ\phi)(0)/\nu!=[v^{\nu}](F\circ P)
. We compute

[v^{\nu}](F\circ P)
as follows: Let
m\geq 0
. For each coordinate

w^{i}
we have:

(w^{i}\circ P)^{m}=\Bigl(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\frac{p_{\alpha}^{i}}{\alpha!}\;v^{\alpha}\Bigr)^{m}=\sum_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{p_{\alpha_{j}}^{i}}{\alpha_{j}!}v^{\alpha_{j}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho_{i}:\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}\\
\\
\sum_{\alpha}\rho_{i}(\alpha)=m\end{subarray}}\Bigl(\frac{m!}{\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\rho_{i}(\alpha)!}\Bigr)\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\Bigl(\frac{p_{\alpha}^{i}}{\alpha!}\,v^{\alpha}\Bigr)^{\rho_{i}(\alpha)}.

Note that there are only finitely many maps
\rho:\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}

with
\sum_{\alpha}\rho(\alpha)=n
, and that each of those maps is
finitely supported i.e.
\rho(\alpha)=0
except for finitely many

\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
. In the second step the map
\rho_{i}
counts
the number of occurrences of
\alpha
in a given tuple

(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m})
,
i.e.
\rho_{i}(\alpha)=\#\left\{\,j\;|\;\alpha_{j}=\alpha\,\right\}
. For each given
map
\rho_{i}
we have
m!/\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\rho_{i}(\alpha)!

tuples which give the same map.

Now let
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
and apply this formula to each

m=\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Write
\rho:\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,

\rho(\alpha)=(\rho_{1}(\alpha),\dots,\rho_{n}(\alpha))
, so that:

\displaystyle(w\circ P)^{\beta}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(w^{i}\circ P)^{\beta_{i}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho:\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\\
\\
\sum_{\alpha}\rho(\alpha)=\beta\end{subarray}}\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\beta_{i}!}{\prod_{\alpha}\rho_{i}(\alpha)!}\Bigr)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\Bigl(\frac{p_{\alpha}^{i}}{\alpha!}\,v^{\alpha}\Bigr)^{\rho_{i}(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\beta!\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho:\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\\
\\
\sum_{\alpha}\rho(\alpha)=\beta\end{subarray}}\Bigl(\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\frac{1}{\rho(\alpha)!}\frac{(p_{\alpha})^{\rho(\alpha)}}{(\alpha!)^{|\rho(\alpha)|}}\Bigr)\;v^{\sum_{\alpha}\alpha|\rho(\alpha)|}.

Substituting into
F(P(v))
and extracting the
v^{\alpha}

coefficient gives:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{\alpha}(f\circ\phi)(0)}{\alpha!}=[v^{\alpha}]\,(F\circ P)=[v^{\alpha}]\,\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{f_{\beta}}{\beta!}\,(w\circ P)^{\beta}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}f_{\beta}\,\sum_{\rho\,(*)}\Bigl(\prod_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\frac{1}{\rho(\gamma)!}\frac{(p_{\gamma})^{\rho(\gamma)}}{(\gamma!)^{|\rho(\gamma)|}}\Bigr)

Here
(\ast)
runs over all maps
\rho:\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}

satisfying
\sum_{\gamma}\rho(\gamma)=\beta
and

\sum_{\gamma}\gamma|\rho(\gamma)|=\alpha
. Since
P(0)=0
, only
maps with
\rho(0)=0
contribute, so we may restrict to

\rho:\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
. The constraint

\sum_{\gamma}\gamma|\rho(\gamma)|=\alpha
with
|\gamma|\geq 1

forces
1\leq|\beta|\leq|\alpha|
.

∎

(20) Remark

(Factorials and Symmetry)
.

Controlling the complexity of combinatorial expressions is a key
technical difficulty when working with Faà di Bruno. An important
insight in this presentation is that factorials are always associated
with symmetrization: passing from multilinear maps to polynomials via
diagonal evaluation, or quotienting out ordering information (e.g., from
ordered decompositions to unordered partitions). Formulas are often
simpler when symmetry is not yet imposed. We deliberately keep each
factorial adjacent to the corresponding symmetrized quantity to make the
combinatorics transparent.

7
Product Rule

As an application of the Taylor composition theorem, we derive a
higher-order product rule for pointwise multiplication.

(21) Proposition

(Leibniz rule)
.

Let
E
be a Banach space and let

f_{1},\dots,f_{n}:E\to\mathbb{R}
be
k
-times Fréchet differentiable at a
point
x\in E
. Recall the (unital) Taylor polynomial

T^{k}(f;x;v)=f(x)+T_{\ast}^{k}(f;x;v)
from
(9)
.
Then:

1.

(Taylor form).

T^{k}(f_{1}\cdots f_{n};\,x;\,v)=\pi_{\leq k}\bigl(T^{k}(f_{1};\,x;\,v)\cdots T^{k}(f_{n};\,x;\,v)\bigr).

2.

(Ordered-decomposition form.) For
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in E
,

D(f_{1}\cdots f_{n};\,x;\,u_{1},\dots,u_{k})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(B_{1},\dots,B_{n})\models[k]\end{subarray}}\ D(f_{1};\,x;\,u_{B_{1}})\cdots D(f_{n};\,x;\,u_{B_{n}})

where we use the convention
D(f_{i};x;u_{\emptyset})=f_{i}(x)
for
the empty block.

3.

(Multi-index form.) If
E=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with

|\nu|=k
, then

\frac{\partial^{\nu}}{\nu!}(f_{1}\cdots f_{n})(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
\\
\nu_{1}+\dots+\nu_{n}=\nu\end{subarray}}\ \frac{\partial^{\nu_{1}}f_{1}(x)}{\nu_{1}!}\cdots\frac{\partial^{\nu_{n}}f_{n}(x)}{\nu_{n}!}

where the sum ranges over all tuples

(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n})\in(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})^{n}
with
\nu_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with

\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{n}=\nu
.

Proof.

For
k=0
both expressions are trivially true. Hence we may assume

k\geq 1
.

Ad 1) Set
\phi=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n}):E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

\psi(y)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
c_{i}=f_{i}(x)
so that

\psi\circ\phi=f_{1}\cdots f_{n}
and
c=\phi(x)
.

As
\psi(y)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}
is a polynomial itself, the reduced
Taylor polynomial at
c
can be calculated by translation and degree
truncation:

T_{\ast}^{k}(\psi;c;v)=\pi_{\leq k}(\psi(c+v)-\psi(c))
.
Furthermore

T_{\ast}^{k}(\phi;x;v)=(T_{\ast}^{k}(f_{1};x;v),\dots,T_{\ast}^{k}(f_{n};x;v))\in\mathcal{P}(E,\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{P}(E)\otimes\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Now Faà di Bruno in composition form (Theorem
(13)
) yields

T_{\ast}^{k}(f_{1}\cdots f_{n};x;v)=\pi_{\leq k}(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(c_{i}+T_{\ast}^{k}(f_{i};x;v))-\prod_{i=1}^{n}c_{i})
.
Adding
\prod_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}
on both sides gives the claimed identity:

T^{k}(f_{1}\cdots f_{n};\,x;\,v)=\pi_{\leq k}\bigl(T^{k}(f_{1};\,x;\,v)\cdots T^{k}(f_{n};\,x;\,v)\bigr).

Ad 2) The decomposition formula is derived by applying
\Pol_{k}
to
both sides of (1). We hence find:

D(f_{1}\cdots f_{n};x)=\Pol_{k}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{k}\frac{1}{m!}D(f_{i};x;\underbrace{v,\dots,v}_{m})=\Pol_{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\dots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\\
\\
m_{1}+\dots+m_{n}=k\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m_{i}!}D(f_{i};x;\underbrace{v,\dots,v}_{m_{i}}).

The claim follows by the Polarization Lemma
(17)
applied
to
B[a_{1},\dots,a_{n}]=\prod a_{i}
.

Ad 3) Expanding the Taylor polynomial in multi-index form

T^{k}(f_{i};x;v)=\sum_{0\leq|\mu|\leq k}\frac{v^{\mu}}{\mu!}\partial^{\mu}f_{i}(x)
,
we find:

\frac{\partial^{\nu}F(x)}{\nu!}=[v^{\nu}]\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{0\leq|\mu|\leq k}\frac{v^{\mu}}{\mu!}\partial^{\mu}f_{i}(x)=\sum_{\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{n}=\nu}\frac{\partial^{\nu_{1}}f_{1}(x)}{\nu_{1}!}\cdots\frac{\partial^{\nu_{n}}f_{n}(x)}{\nu_{n}!}.

∎
