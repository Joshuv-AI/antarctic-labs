1.0 Executive Summary
 This project provided funding for the contracting and implementation of a 35.2kW rooftop solar
photovoltaic system installation located at the Coeur Center Family and Youth Center, operated by the
Coeur d’Alene Tribe’s Marimn Health and Wellness, in Worley, Idaho. The objectives of the project
were to:
 1) Increase use of renewable energy and decrease the Tribe’s carbon footprint at the Coeur
 Center,
 2) Reduce energy costs at the Coeur Center,
 3) Provide a solar energy educational opportunity for Tribal community members and Reservation
 residents, and
 4) Increase the number of Coeur d’Alene Reservation communities that have solar energy by
 installing the first solar project in Worley, Idaho, on the northern portion of the Reservation.

 The project was coordinated by the Tribe’s Environmental Programs Office (EPO), housed in the
Tribe’s Natural Resources Department, and was expected to reduce energy use at the center by 41,750
kWh per year, saving $88,000 over the lifespan of the system. This project was part of EPO’s efforts in
supporting the departmental goals of Natural Resources to preserve, protect, and enhance the natural
resources, improve quality of life, and provide social and economic benefits across the Reservation. To
date, the installation has provided 55,120 kWh of energy to the center, producing 38.2 MWh of energy
in calendar year 2025.

2.0 Background
 The Coeur d’Alene Tribe, known as the schitsu’umsh in their own language, have resided in their
homeland since Time Immemorial. This homeland stretches nearly five million acres from western
Montana to eastern Washington, encompassing Coeur d’Alene Lake, Lake Pend Oreille, the St. Joe, St.
Maries, Coeur d’Alene, and Spokane Rivers, and numerous smaller lakes and tributaries. The Tribe
today is headquartered on the Coeur d’Alene Reservation, a 345,000-acre reservation that is overlain
by Kootenai and Benewah Counties in northern Idaho. The Tribe has over 2,800 members, and the
Reservation population is just over 8,000 people. The Tribe is governed by a seven-member elected
Tribal Council elected by the Tribal membership, led by a Chairperson, Chief J. Allan, who is elected by
the Council. The Tribal Council oversees administration of its governed, facilitated by its Administrative
Director, Robert Matt, who supervises 13 departments, including Natural Resources, Facilities,
Education, Social Services, Law & Order, Culture, Legal, Human Resources, Finance, IT, Tribal
Employment Rights Ordinance, Enrollment, and Public Works. The Tribe also has a number of
additional enterprises, including Marimn Health and Wellness, which houses medical and dental
services as well as several recreation and wellness centers, the Tribal Housing Authority, the Economic
Development Corporation, and the Coeur d’Alene Casino and Resort.
Energy development is currently coordinated in the Tribe’s Natural Resources Department, which is led
by Director Caj Matheson and Deputy Director Ralph Allan, and facilitated by the Environmental

Programs Office (EPO) Manager, Dr. Laura Laumatia. EPO currently has a staff of 11, and is supported
in its energy work with two DOE-funded Energy Innovator Fellows who are assisting the Tribe in the
completion of its strategic energy plan (described below) and supporting code.

 The Tribe is in the process of finalizing a strategic energy plan, with the assistance of the Office of
Indian Energy and the National Lab of the Rockies. The draft vision that has been compiled (pending
Tribal Council approval) by a committee of Tribal leaders and staff is: The Coeur d’Alene Tribe shall

and economic and environmental leadership, so that the people, lands, and resources across the
Reservation and Tribal aboriginal lands shall thrive and prosper. Supporting the Tribe’s path to this
vision include its goals and objectives of capacity building through training and workforce
development, disaster preparedness, energy efficiency supported by codes and standards, energy-
related business development, expansion of energy outreach, and energy generation, and
establishment of a tribal utility.

3.0 Project Objectives
Per the Award Statement of Objectives, the Coeur d’Alene Tribe’s Project Goals for this award were:
 1) Increase use of renewable energy and decrease the Tribe’s carbon footprint on the Reservation
 and at the Coeur Center,
 2) Reduce energy costs at the Coeur Center,
 3) Provide a solar energy educational opportunity for Tribal community members and Reservation
 residents at the Coeur Center, and
 4) Increase the number of Coeur d’Alene Reservation communities that have solar energy by
 providing the first solar project in Worley on the Reservation.

The supporting tasks identified in this project to accomplish these goals included:
 1. Development of an agreement with Bonneville Environmental Foundation for technical and
 financial assistance, development of an interconnection agreement with Kootenai Electric
 Cooperative for the solar installation, and development of a contract with a subcontractor for
 installation.
 2. Development and review of a solar PV design for the Project Manager and BEF staff for review
 and finalization.
 3. Development of a construction permit for approval, including review for NEPA and NHPA
 compliance, in coordination with the Natural Resources and Culture Committees.
 4. Procurement of materials.
 5. Install the solar installation, with potential for training Tribal members.
 6. Inspect the installation with support from the State of Idaho.
 7. Conduct a walk-through and systems test with the vendor, project team, and Marimn Facilities.
 8. Obtain monthly energy and cost data from Marimn to analyze cost savings data.
 9. Compile a final report.

4.0 Description of Activities Performed
 The Coeur d’Alene Tribe’s Environmental Programs Office worked with Bonneville Environmental
Foundation (BEF) to develop a Request for Proposals for contractors and identify suitable contractors
within the region. In addition to this technical assistance, BEF also provided a generous amount of cost-
share for the project, awarding the Tribe $50,000 for the solar installation. Previous EPO Manager
Tiffany Allgood also successfully garnered additional matching funds from the Tribal Solar Accelerator
funds. The RFP was developed and released in April 2023. In response to questions from prospective
contractors, the Tribe and Marimn coordinated additional structural analysis of the Coeur Center roof
to see if there was additional capacity beyond the original 35.2 kW plans. Due to the type of roof
design, this was not possible. The RFP was sent to eight regional solar installers, three of which
conducted site visits and submitted final proposals. The project team, which included the EPO
Manager, an engineer serving as Marimn’s buyer’s representative, and the Marimn Strategic
Development Director, reviewed and selected Nimiipuu Energy, an entity of the Nez Perce Tribe, as the
preferred contractor based on its ability to not only perform the installation but its ability to provide
substantial training for up to ten Coeur d’Alene Tribal community members.

 The contract routing process was delayed by external factors that prevented the Tribal Council
from reviewing the contract until mid-Fall. The contract was finalized in October 2023. EPO worked
with the Tribe’s TERO Department to recruit ten community members, all of whom were Tribal
members, descendants, or members of other tribes, to attend 40 hours of solar installation training in
Lapwai, Idaho, led by Nimiipuu Energy, and its subcontractor, RevoluSun. All ten trainees attend the
entire training, which included classroom work, a residential, and a commercial installation, and safety
training, during the first full week of December 2023.

 After review of final design with the project team and Marimn Facilities, installation was then
scheduled for March 2024, and in February, the Tribe and Nimiipuu Energy applied for the
interconnection agreement with Kootenai Electric Company. The interconnection agreement was
finalized on April 1, 2024, and in late April, a Solar Panel Ribbon Cutting and commissioning were held,
attended by Tribal Council, Marimn Health leadership, Department of Energy – Office of Indian Energy
representatives Tweedie Doe and Jen Luna, the Nimiipuu Energy Executive Director, the Coeur d’Alene
Tribe’s solar installers, and staff and community members.

 After commissioning, there was a technical issue with one of the inverters, requiring repair. This
was quickly resolved by Nimiipuu Energy, and the system was fully functioning as of June 2024.

 The Coeur Center Solar Production met all of its goals, providing an excellent opportunity for
demonstration of solar capacity at a commercial facility while providing workforce training, education,
and capacity building on the Coeur d’Alene Reservation. The collaboration between EPO and Marimn
Health allowed staff to engage in co-learning on the needs, challenges, and opportunities for
renewable energy projects, from development of contracts to project implementation. The workforce
development component was a key success, and one installer went on to full-time employment with a
regional installation company following the completion of the project; another installed a solar PV
residential system at his own home. Several installers have moved into other full-time employment
with the Tribe.

6.0 Lessons Learned

 This project provided an excellent opportunity for EPO and Marimn Health to understand each
stage of project development for a solar project. The support provided by Bonneville Environmental
Foundation was critical in ensuring that the RFP was written in a way that met the Tribe’s stated
objectives, as many of the technical specifications were new to the internal project team. However,
Marimn Health had engaged a buyer’s representative to assist with building development of the Coeur
Center and related projects; this individual’s guidance during the RFP review and identifying gaps in
information related to the building structure were also critical. Over the course of the project, Marimn
also hired a new Facilities Director; he, too, proved invaluable in providing needed input on systems
operation, and he and his team helped immensely in their work with the subcontractor on placement
of systems controls and identifying project needs, such as scheduling power outages during system
installation so that community access was minimally impacted.

 Most of the challenges experienced over the course of the project were beyond control of the
project team, such as supply chain issues and external factors preventing the Tribal Council from
holding its regular Council meetings. There is no single manner to address these challenges, other than
regular communication with the project team, contractors, and the project officer. The support and
guidance form DOE-OIE staff was also critical, as they patiently assisted the EPO Program Manager with
review and guidance. One capacity challenge that remains is remaining capacity issues. The Tribe’s
Facilities Department employs just one electrician, who is close to retirement, forcing the Tribe to rely
on outside contractors for assistance. The Tribe is continuing to explore opportunities for
apprenticeships in hopes of Tribal members pursuing this and similar careers and meeting this ongoing
need.

 The workforce development component of the project was probably the most significant
success. The training provided by the subcontractor were not only technical, but encompassed the
values and goals of energy sovereignty shared by the Coeur d’Alene and Nez Perce Tribes. Trainees
spoke at length of how this resonated with them and motivated them to not only complete the
training, but return four months later for installation (eight of the 10 trainees returned to complete the
project). These installers shared their pride on social media, with family members, and friends, and the
sense of pride was shared by Tribal leadership and staff.
