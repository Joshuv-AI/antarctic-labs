1 Final Report: New Physics at Short and Long Baseline: Accel-
 erator Based Neutrino Experiments
Understanding the three neutrino mixing matrix, the mass hierarchy and the search for CP viola-
tion, the nature of neutrinos, and looking for new physics are among a host of pressing questions
at the forefront of neutrino physics and particle physics today. Coupled with advances in precision
neutrino detection, the US is addressing these questions from small scale experiments to the mas-
sive DUNE experiment. PI Fleming and her team play critical roles in accelerator based neutrino
physics at short and long baseline with participation on MicroBooNE [1], SBND [2], and with the
group’s participation in DUNE [3]. These experiments are at the heart of the US-based high energy
physics program.

 The Liquid Argon TPC detectors developed for these experiments have led to a paradigm shift
in neutrino physics enabling truly precision study of neutrino interactions and searches for new
physics. Imaging techniques taking advantage of machine learning continue to improve reconstruc-
tion algorithms to benefit from the huge amount of information available in each event both in
event topology and using calorimetry.
 This proposal has funded the PI and her Postdoctoral scholar, overseeing work on the Micro-
BooNE experiment. The outcome of this work is both the actual analyses from MicroBooNE and
what this adds to our body of knowledge in neutrino interactions, as well as how the MicroBooNE
experiment prepares the field for the flagship DUNE experiment.
 For neutrino interactions on MicroBooNE, the research funded has focussed on events that
produce a single photon that mimic the interaction of an electron neutrino that produces and
electron. The cross section, or probability that that interaction occurs, can then serve as a test for
whether or not a signal observed by a predecessor experiment was seeing an indication of a new
kind of neutrino, or just an interaction with a single photon at a frequency not predicted.
 PD Bhat mentored then graduate student Lee Hagaman who worked on events producing a
single gamma. Like in the electron appearance search when an outgoing electron is produced, in
the photon search, there are a number of topologies to differentiate. Differences in distributions
with different hadronic activity could be significant for beyond the standard model physics and is
certainly significant in cross section measurements. Hagaman worked on a search in single photon
mode using a reconstruction paradigm called ”wire cell”. Hagaman combined the data, nearly
orthogonal to those selected in the previous analysis, into one analysis with significantly improved
sensitivity. The publication produced from this work is [4]
 The outcome of this work is that there remains no indication for new physics beyond the
standard model either from neutrino interactions producing single photons, or neutrino interactions
producing electrons.
 In addition to this physics result, the development of analysis techniques, including those em-
ploying Artificial Intelligence, is impactful for the experiments that follow on to MicroBooNE. This
includes the SBND experiment as well as the flagship DUNE experiment.
 The DUNE experiment is the flagship of the US based program in high energy physics. DUNE
will address some of the most pressing questions in particle physics today most notably looking
for CP violation in the neutrino sector. The DUNE experiment is in the design and construction
phase for its near and far detector complexes at Fermilab and SURF respectively.
 On DUNE, PD Avinay mentored graduate student Angela White in her work on the DUNE 2x2
prorotype. The 2x2 is the prototype for the DUNE NDLAr near detector. It was deployed in the
NuMI beam at Fermilab and collected neutrino data both as a prototype and also to produce cross
