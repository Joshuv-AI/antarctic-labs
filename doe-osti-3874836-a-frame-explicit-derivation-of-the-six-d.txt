1 Introduction
The six degree-of-freedom (6DOF) equations of motion (EOMs) governing the coupled translational
and rotational dynamics of a rigid body are fundamental to vehicle and spacecraft dynamics, guid-
ance, and control. This report derives the 6DOF EOMs for reconfigurable rigid bodies of constant
mass from first principles, employing a notation in which the described object, its reference frame,
and its resolving frame are made explicit for every quantity. Existing treatments such as [1, 2, 3]
typically establish coordinate frame information primarily in prose rather than adopting a notation
that explicitly encodes all of the coordinate-frame information. When the object being described, its
reference, and its resolving basis are left implicit in the surrounding text, the EOMs and derivations
become a frequent source of ambiguity and error upon implementation or extension. The primary
objective of this report is to remove coordinate-frame ambiguity by presenting the equations in a
frame-explicit notation form suitable for direct implementation.
 Furthermore, although these equations are well established, references addressing the reconfig-
urable rigid body—one whose mass distribution varies in time while its total mass is conserved—are
comparatively scarce. Throughout this report, a reconfigurable rigid body denotes a family of rigid
bodies that represent a single vehicle across multiple physical configurations. Within any fixed
configuration, the inertia tensor resolved in the body frame is constant; during a transition between
configurations, the rotational equations of motion must additionally account for the time derivative
of the body-frame inertia tensor. Both cases are treated in the derivation that follows.

2 Notation
The derivation adopts the coordinate-frame notation convention of [4], which encodes frame in-
formation directly in each symbol. A vector xγβα denotes property x of object α with respect to
reference frame β, resolved in observer frame γ. For example, vab i is the velocity of body b with

respect to reference a, resolved in the inertial frame i.
 A change of resolving frame is effected by a direction cosine matrix (DCM). The DCM Cab rotates
a vector from resolving frame a to resolving frame b; it does not alter the reference frame. Consider
for example the velocity vib , whose object is the body frame b and whose reference is the inertial
frame i. The rotation Cbi vib
