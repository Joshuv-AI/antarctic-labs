Insufficiency of the algebraic Brauer–Manin obstruction for homogeneous spaces
Nguyễn Mạnh Linh

Abstract
Over any number field containing a root of unity of odd prime order, we construct a homogeneous space of SL n \mathrm{SL}_{n} with finite 2 2 -nilpotent geometric stabilizers, with a constant unramified algebraic Brauer group, which has no rational point but has local points in every completion of the ground field. This yields the first example of transcendental Brauer–Manin obstruction for homogeneous spaces of connected linear algebraic groups. Our method exploits a previous idea by Borovoi and Kunyavskii.



Keywords.
Brauer–Manin obstruction, Galois cohomology, Hasse principle, homogeneous spaces, nonabelian cohomology, rational points.

11R34, 11S90, 14G12.

Introduction

1.1
Context

A (smooth, geometrically integral) variety
X
over a number
k
(let us say,
k=\mathbf{Q}
) is said to be a
counterexample to the Hasse principle
if
X(k_{v})\neq\varnothing
for all place
v
of
k
but
X(k)=\varnothing
. The Hasse principle (or local–global principle) is known to hold for projective quadric hypersurfaces (Hasse–Minkowskii, see
[
23
, Theorem 27.3 (ii)]
) and torsors under simply connected semisimple linear algebraic groups (Kneser–Harder–Chernousov, see
[
19
, Theorems 6.4 et 6.6]
). Nevertheless, counterexamples over
k=\mathbf{Q}
, due to Selmer and Cassels–Guy already exist within the class of projective hypersurfaces such as
3x^{3}+4y^{3}+5z^{3}=0

[
21
]
and
5x^{3}+9y^{3}+10z^{3}+12t^{3}=0

[
4
]
.

Systematic studies on the failure of the Hasse principle were extensively developed in the last century. Remarkably, Manin’s foundational work
[
17
]
introduced the use of cohomological methods, namely, the Brauer group and global class field theory. More precisely, assuming
X
projective, the cohomological Brauer group
\operatorname{Br}(X):=\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,\mathbf{G}_{m})
(which is a stable birational invariant) may explain why the Hasse principle fails, as follows. Assume
\prod_{v}X(k_{v})\neq\varnothing
and consider the pairing

\operatorname{Br}(X)\times\prod_{v}X(k_{v})\to\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z},\quad(\alpha,(P_{v}))\mapsto\sum_{v}\operatorname{inv}_{v}(\alpha(P_{v})),

where
\operatorname{inv}_{v}:\operatorname{Br}(k_{v})\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}
are the invariant maps from local class field theory. By the local reciprocity law (Albert–Hasse–Brauer–Noether), the pairing vanishes on the constant subgroup

\operatorname{Br}_{0}(X):=\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Br}(k)\to\operatorname{Br}(X))

and on the diagonal image of
X(k)
in
\prod_{v}X(k_{v})
. Therefore, if there is no family
(P_{v})
orthogonal to
\operatorname{Br}(X)
, then
X(k)=\varnothing
. In this case, the failure of the Hasse principle is said to be
explained by the Brauer–Manin obstruction
. If
X
is not projective, we replace
\operatorname{Br}(X)
by its
unramified part

\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{Br}(X)
,
cf.

[
5
, §6.2]
.

In general, even this obstruction may fail to control the Hasse principle,
e.g.
, for Skorobogatov’s bielliptic surface
[
24
]
. A conjecture by Colliot-Thélène
[
5
, Conjecture 14.1.2]
predicts the sufficiency of the Brauer–Manin obstruction for varieties whose geometry is tame (geometrically “rationally connected”). These include geometrically unirational varieties such as homogeneous spaces of connected linear algebraic groups. This conjecture was established for principal homogeneous spaces by Sansuc
[
20
, Corollaire 8.7]
. More generally, Borovoi established the same conjecture for homogeneous spaces with connected stabilizers, or commutative stabilizers if the ambient group is simply connected semisimple
[
3
, Theorems 2.2 et 2.3]
. For finite nonabelian (geometric) stabilizers, Colliot-Thélène’s conjecture remains largely open (this is also the hardest case, by a reduction argument of Demarche–Lucchini Arteche
[
7
, Théorème 1.1]
).

As a matter of fact, Borovoi’s theorem asserts that the
algebraic part

\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr},1}(X):=\operatorname{Ker}(\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)\to\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X_{\bar{k}}))

of the unramified Brauer group is sufficient to control the Hasse principle on homogeneous spaces with connected or commutative stabilizers. In fact, the abelian nature of the stabilizers enables the machinery of arithmetic duality theorems
à la
Poitou–Tate. For finite noncommutative stabilizers, one expects the intervention of transcendental classes. Regarding a closely-related property of the Hasse principle, the so-called
weak approximation
, the insufficiency of the algebraic Brauer–Manin obstruction was established for finite constant metabelian stabilizers, independently by Demarche–Lucchini Arteche–Neftin
[
6
, Corollary 5.2, Example 5.3]
, Demeio
[
9
, Theorem 1.5]
, and Lagarde
[
14
, Theorem 1.5]
. As for the Hasse principle, the existence of such homogeneous spaces are unknown so far. This is the aim of the present article. Here is our main result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
k
a number field containing a primitive
p
-th root of unity. Then, there exist an integer
n\geq 1
and a homogeneous space
X
of
\mathbf{SL}_{n}
over
k
satisfying the following conditions.

(i).

The geometric stabilizers of
X
are finite
p
-groups of nilpotency class
2
.

(ii).

The unramified algebraic Brauer group of
X
is
\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr},1}(X)=\operatorname{Br}_{0}(X)
.

(iii).

X(k_{v})\neq\varnothing
for all places
v
of
k
.

(iv).

X(k)=\varnothing
.

1.2
Organization of the manuscript

Let us explain the idea of construction of the homogeneous space in Theorem
1
, which is the content of Section
2
. In
[
2
]
, Borovoi and Kunyavskii introduced a general machinery to construct finite
k
-group schemes
F
of nilpotency class
2
. This was studier further by
[
15
]
, where the author established the Hasse principle and weak approximation for a subclass of homogeneous spaces with geometric stabilizers
F
. Here is the idea (see the detail in
[
15
, §3]
). Let
M
and
Z
be finite Galois modules and

\phi:M\otimes M\to Z

a (Galois equivariant) homomorphism. We assume
\phi
is surjective and
nondegenerate
, that is

(i).

If
x\in M
such that
\phi(x\otimes y)=0
for all
y\in M
, then
x=0
.

(ii).

If
y\in M
such that
\phi(x\otimes y)=0
for all
x\in M
, then
y=0
.

From
\phi
, one constructs a central extension
F
of
M\oplus M
by
Z
with
Z(F)=[F,F]=Z
. For each
\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(k,Z)
, the construction of Demarche and Lucchini Arteche
[
7
, Corollaires 3.3 and 3.5]
yields a homogeneous space
X=X_{\beta}
of
\mathbf{SL}_{n}
(for some
n\geq 1
) with geometric stabilizers
F
. Furthermore, for any overfield
K\supseteq k
, one has
X(K)\neq\varnothing
if and only if there exist
x,y\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,M)
with
\beta|_{K}=\phi_{\ast}(x\cup y)
(see
[
15
, Proposition 3.3, Lemme 3.8]
).

In
[
15
, §5B]
, our choice of
(M,Z,\phi)
is as follows. Let
A
be a finite constant abelian group of exponent
e
such that
k
contains a primitive
e
-th root of unity. Let
M:=\operatorname{Ind}_{L}^{k}(A)
, let
Z
be the cokernel of the canonical inclusion
A\otimes A\hookrightarrow M\otimes M
, and
\phi:M\otimes M\to Z
the canonical projection. Using the Poitou–Tate exact sequence in Galois cohomology, the Hasse principle was established for the obtained homogeneous spaces
[
15
, Théorème 5.6]
. Quite surprisingly, a slight modification of this construction yields the counterexample in Theorem
1
. Namely, we take
A=\mathbf{F}_{p}
and
M=\mathbf{F}_{p}^{d}
for some sufficiently large integer
d
. We now consider a different kind of inclusion

A\otimes A=\mathbf{F}_{p}\hookrightarrow M\otimes M=\mathbf{M}_{d}(\mathbf{F}_{p}),\quad\lambda\mapsto\lambda\mathbf{I}_{d},

where
\mathbf{I}_{d}
denotes the identity matrix. Again, we take
Z
to be the cokernel of this inclusion and
\phi
the canonical projection. The obtained stabilizer
F
then has order
p^{d^{2}+2d-1}
. The minimal numerical example yielded from this construction is
p=3
and
d=p+2=5
over the Eisenstein cyclotomic field
k=\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{3})
, where
F
has order
3^{34}=16677181699666569
.

Next, decompose
\mathbf{I}_{d}
into a sum of rank one matrices

\mathbf{I}_{d}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}E_{i}

and then choose distinct finite places
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}
. Identify
\mathrm{H}^{2}(k_{v_{i}},M\otimes M)
to
M\otimes M
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}(k_{v_{i}},Z)
to
Z
via the local invariants (see (
1
) below). Global class field theory then produces a class
\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(k,Z)
that vanishes outside
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\}
and satisfies
\beta|_{k_{v_{i}}}=\phi(E_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,d
. Since each local invariant
\beta|_{k_{v}}
has rank
\leq 1
, it is easy to produce local classes
x_{v},y_{v}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},M)
with
\phi_{\ast}(x_{v}\cup y_{v})=\beta|_{k_{v}}
(Lemma
3
).

To show that
\beta
is not globally of the form
\beta=\phi_{\ast}(x\cup y)
, we consider the local invariant matrices
A_{v}=(x\cup y)|_{k_{v}}\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(k_{v},M\otimes M)\cong M\otimes M
. Then
\phi(A_{v_{i}})=\phi(E_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,d
and
\phi(A_{v})=0
for
v\notin\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\}
. The point here is that the dimension of the
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-vector space
\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},\mathbf{F}_{p})
is bounded above by a constant independent of
v
. This yields a uniform bound on the rank of
A_{v}
which forces
A_{v_{i}}=E_{i}
and
A_{v}=0
for
v\notin\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\}
once
d
is large enough. We then derive a contradiction using the global reciprocity law (Proposition
4
) for the finite constant Galois module
M\otimes M
. The homogeneous space
X
is therefore a counterexample to the Hasse principle.

In Section
3
, we show that the above counterexample cannot be explained by the algebraic Brauer–Manin obstruction. Computations of the unramified algebraic Brauer group for our construction was done in
[
15
, §4C]
using Demarche’s formula
[
8
, Théorème 1]
. This ensures
\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr},1}(X)=\operatorname{Br}_{0}(X)
, which contributes nothing to the Brauer–Manin pairing. Now, the stabilizers of our homogeneous space is
supersolvable
as finite groups equipped with an outer Galois action (in fact, this outer action is
trivial
). Thanks to the sophisticated result of Harpaz–Wittenberg
[
13
, Théorème B]
, the full Brauer–Manin obstruction suffices to explain the failure of the Hasse principle on
X
. This already proves the existence of a transcendental Brauer–Manin obstruction. As a matter of fact, an application of Bogomolov’s formula
[
1
]
, as in
[
15
, §4D]
, shows that the transcendental Brauer group
\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)
is
\mathbf{Z}/p
.

To produce explicit unramified transcendental Brauer classes that give a nonvanishing Brauer–Manin condition, we start by construction a geometrically unramified class
\bar{\mathcal{A}}\in\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X_{\bar{k}})
using the description of the Bogomolov multiplier in Subsection
3.1
. Next, we descend this class into a class
\mathcal{A}\in\operatorname{Br}(X)
and investigate its evaluation at local points in Subsection
3.2
. These procedure can be done by attaching such a class
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{L}
to
any
functional
L:M\otimes M\to\mathbf{F}_{p}
. We do not prove that
\mathcal{A}\in\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)
algebraically, instead we apply Harari’s criterion
[
11
]
to do so. A suitable choice of
L
then gives a
\bar{\mathcal{A}}\neq 0
, that is,
\mathcal{A}
is transcendental (Subsection
3.3
).

Construction of the counterexample

We fix an odd prime
p
and a number field
k
containing a primitive
p
-th root of unity (in particular,

\Omega

\mathbf{F}_{p}=\mathbf{Z}/p

In what follows, we make use of the following fact. Let

\hat{A}:=\operatorname{Hom}(A,\mu_{p})=\operatorname{Hom}(A,\mathbf{F}_{p})

\mathrm{H}^{2}(k_{v},A)\times\hat{A}\xrightarrow{\cup}\mathrm{H}^{2}(k_{v},\mathbf{F}_{p})\xrightarrow{\operatorname{inv}_{v}}\mathbf{Z}/p

is a perfect pairing of finite groups, that is, one has an isomorphism

\operatorname{Inv}_{v}^{A}:\mathrm{H}^{2}(k_{v},A)\xrightarrow{\cong}\operatorname{Hom}(\hat{A},\mathbf{Z}/p)=A

(1)

which is natural in the sense that for any homomorphism
f:A\to B
of finite constant
p
-elementary abelian groups, one has a commutative diagram

\textstyle{\mathrm{H}^{2}(k_{v},A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{Inv}_{v}^{A}}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{\mathrm{H}^{2}(k_{v},B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{Inv}_{v}^{B}}
\textstyle{B.}

(2)

We now apply Poitou–Tate duality
[
12
, Théorème 17.13]
to
A
and
\hat{A}
. The map

\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,\hat{A})\to\prod_{v\in\Omega}\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},\hat{A})

is injective by virtue of Chebotarev’s density theorem. Dually, the map

\mathrm{H}^{2}(k,A)\to\prod_{v\in\Omega}\mathrm{H}^{2}(k_{v},A)

is injective as well. In view of (

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathrm{H}^{0}(k,\hat{A}),\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})=A

(note that

The homogeneous space
X

Proof.

Since

3
Transcendental Brauer–Manin obstruction

We now show that the failure of the Hasse principle for the homogeneous space
X
from Section
2
cannot be explained by the algebraic part of the Brauer–Manin obstruction. Calculation of the unramified algebraic Brauer group
\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr},1}(X)
modulo its constant part
\operatorname{Br}_{0}(X)
of the constructed homogeneous space
X
was done in
[
15
, Proposition 4.6]
. We can apply this result because our group
F
is finite constant. Since the Cartier dual
\widehat{M\oplus M}:=\operatorname{Hom}(M\oplus M,\mu_{p})\cong\mathbf{F}_{p}^{2d}
is again finite constant, the cited proposition gives

\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr},1}(X)/\operatorname{Br}_{0}(X)=\{\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,\mathbf{F}_{p}^{2d}):\alpha|_{k_{v}}=0\text{ for all but finitely many }v\in\Omega\}.

By Chebotarev’s density theorem, the right hand side vanishes, so
\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr},1}(X)=\operatorname{Br}_{0}(X)
does not contribute to the Brauer–Manin pairing. This concludes the proof of Theorem
1
on the insufficiency of the algebraic Brauer–Manin obstruction on
X
.

As explained at the end of Subsection
1.2
, Théorème B of
[
13
]
implies the existence of a transcendental Brauer–Manin obstruction on
X
. In particular, we have
\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)\supsetneq\operatorname{Br}_{0}(X)
. According to
[
15
, Proposition 4.9]
, the unramified geometric Brauer group
\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X_{\bar{k}})
(
i.e.
, the Bogomolov multiplier of
F
) is given by

\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X_{\bar{k}})=B_{0}(F)=\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)/H,\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}),

where
H\subseteq M\otimes M
is generated by rank one matrices
x\otimes y
with
\phi(x\otimes y)=0
. As in the proof of Lemma
3
, this implies
x\otimes y=0
. Therefore,
H=0
and
\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X_{\bar{k}})=\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Ker}(\phi),\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{F}_{p},\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})=\mathbf{Z}/p
. It follows that

\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)/\operatorname{Br}_{0}(X)=\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)/\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr},1}(X)\cong\mathbf{Z}/p,

because the first term has been shown above to be nontrivial.

3.3
Explicit computation of Brauer–Manin obstruction

So far, we have constructed a class
\mathcal{A}_{L}\in\operatorname{Br}(X)
from any given linear form
L:M\otimes M\to\mathbf{F}_{p}
, but we have not established its unramified nature. The trick here is to not prove this algebraically, but arithmetically using Harari’s evaluative criterion for ramification.

We recall that the class
\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(k,Z)
and the places
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\in\Omega
were constructed such that
\beta|_{k_{v}}=0
for
v\neq v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}
and

\operatorname{Inv}_{v_{i}}^{Z}(\beta)=\phi(e_{i}\otimes e_{i})

for
1\leq i\leq d
, where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}
is the standard basis for
M=\mathbf{F}_{p}^{d}
,

Proposition 7
.

Let
[\mathcal{A}_{L}]\in\operatorname{Br}(X)
be the class constructed from any linear form
L:M\otimes M=\mathbf{M}_{d}(\mathbf{F}_{p})\to\mathbf{F}_{p}
as in Subsection
3.2
. Then, the following are true.

(i).

For any place
v\in\Omega
and any
P_{v}\in X(k_{v})
, one has

\operatorname{inv}_{v}([\mathcal{A}_{L}](P_{v}))=\begin{cases}L(e_{i}\otimes e_{i})&\text{if }v=v_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq d,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(here,
\operatorname{inv}_{v}
takes value in
\mathbf{Z}/p
).

(ii).

[\mathcal{A}_{L}]
is unramified.

Proof.

We prove
(i)
. By Proposition
6
, there exists
x^{\prime}_{v},y^{\prime}_{v}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(k_{v},\mathbf{F}_{p})
such that

[\mathcal{A}_{L}](P_{v})=L_{\ast}(x^{\prime}_{v}\cup y^{\prime}_{v})

and
\phi_{\ast}(x^{\prime}_{v}\cup y_{v}^{\prime})=\beta|_{k_{v}}
. If
v
is a finite place, let
A_{v}^{\prime}:=\operatorname{Inv}_{v}^{M\otimes M}(x^{\prime}_{v}\cup y^{\prime}_{v})\in M\otimes M
(the isomorphism
\operatorname{Inv}_{v}^{M\otimes M}
being defined from (
1
)). We can now repeat the rank-bounding argument from the proof of Proposition
4
. Namely,
A^{\prime}_{v}
has small rank (bounded uniformly in
v
). On the the hand, it follows from the functoriality (
2
) that

\phi(A^{\prime}_{v})=\phi(\operatorname{Inv}_{v}^{M\otimes M}(x^{\prime}_{v}\cup y^{\prime}_{v}))=\operatorname{Inv}_{v}^{Z}(\phi_{\ast}(x^{\prime}_{v}\cup y^{\prime}_{v}))=\operatorname{Inv}_{v}^{Z}(\beta)=\begin{cases}\phi(e_{i}\otimes e_{i})&\text{if }v=v_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq d,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For sufficiently large choice of
d
(
d=[k:\mathbf{Q}]+3
will do the trick), the rank bound on
A^{\prime}_{v}
forces

A^{\prime}_{v}=\begin{cases}e_{i}\otimes e_{i}&\text{if }v=v_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq d,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The claim
(i)
then follows from the functoriality condition (
2
). In particular, the evaluation map induced by
[\mathcal{A}_{L}]
is zero for all but finitely many places
v\in\Omega
. By
[
11
, Théorème 2.1.1]
, we have
[\mathcal{A}_{L}]\in\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)
, which proves
(ii)
.

With Proposition
7
at our disposal, we are now ready to produce unramified Brauer classes which obstructs the Brauer–Manin condition. Let
L:M\otimes M=\mathbf{M}_{d}(\mathbf{F}_{p})\to\mathbf{F}_{p}
be the
(1,1)
-entry and
[\mathcal{A}_{L}]\in\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)
the corresponding element. Then, for any faimly
(P_{v})\in\prod_{v\in\Omega}X(K_{v})
of local points, one has

\sum_{v\in\Omega}\operatorname{inv}_{v}([\mathcal{A}_{L}](P_{v}))=\sum_{i=1}^{d}L(e_{i}\otimes e_{i})=1\in\mathbf{Z}/p

In particular,
[\mathcal{A}_{L}]
is transcendental, because
\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr},1}(X)=\operatorname{Br}_{0}(X)
has no contribution to the Brauer–Manin condition.

Remarks 8
.

Here is an alternative way to see the transcendence of
[\mathcal{A}_{L}]
. Recall from Subsection
3.1
that the restriction of
[\mathcal{A}_{L}]
to
\operatorname{Br}(X_{\bar{k}})=\mathrm{H}^{2}(F,\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})
is represented by the cocycle

\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{L}:F\times F\to\mathbf{F}_{p},

which itself is the inflation of bilinear map

\Psi_{L}:(M\oplus M)\times(M\oplus M)\to\mathbf{F}_{p},\quad((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))\mapsto L(x\otimes y^{\prime}).

Under the isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{2}(M\oplus M,\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\bigwedge^{2}(M\oplus M),\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})=\operatorname{Hom}((\bigwedge^{2}M)\oplus(M\otimes M)\oplus(\bigwedge^{2}M),\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}),

the class of
\Psi_{L}
corresponds to that of

\tilde{\Psi}_{L}:(\bigwedge^{2}M)\oplus(M\otimes M)\oplus(\bigwedge^{2}M)\to\mathbf{F}_{p},\quad(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3})\mapsto L(\gamma_{2}).

Recall from (
7
) that the commutator map
\lambda:(\bigwedge^{2}M)\oplus(M\otimes M)\oplus(\bigwedge^{2}M)\to Z
has kernel

\operatorname{Ker}(\lambda)=(\bigwedge^{2}M)\oplus\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)\oplus(\bigwedge^{2}M),

and if
S_{\lambda}:=(\bigwedge^{2}M)\oplus\{0\}\oplus(\bigwedge^{2}M)
, then the Bogomolov multiplier of
F
is

B_{0}(F)=\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Ker}(\lambda)/S_{\lambda},\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})=\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Ker}(\phi),\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z})

cf.
the proof of
[
15
, Lemme 4.8 and Proposition 4.9]
. Now,
\mathbf{I}_{d}\in\operatorname{Ker}(\lambda)
but our choice of the functional
L
give
L(\mathbf{I}_{d})=1\neq 0
, so
\tilde{\Psi}_{L}
remains nonzero when restricted to the subgroup
\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)
of the second factor
M\otimes M
. Thus,
[\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{L}]
is a nonzero element of
\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X_{\bar{k}})
, that is,
[\mathcal{A}_{L}]
is a transcendental Brauer class.
