Power-law hypothesis and (un)fairness of PageRank on undirected multi-type PAMs
Christian Borgs, Florian Henning, Remco van der Hofstad, Nelly Litvak

Abstract
The preferential attachment model (PAM) describes the sequential growth of a network based on the “rich-get-richer” principle. Several versions of it have become established for modeling, e.g., citation networks, capturing a power-law degree distribution. Directed versions of the preferential attachment model where the edges are directed from the new to the old vertices have been the subject of extensive research. They have been shown to exhibit remarkable properties such as heavier tails for the limiting graph-normalized PageRank than for the in-degrees. By contrast, for the undirected version, we recently showed that PageRank has similar tails as the degree. In the present paper, we discuss the PageRank asymptotics for a multi-type version of the undirected PAM (here vertices have different colors), complementing previous results of Antunes, Bhamidi, Banerjee and Pipiras on the asymptotics of PageRank on similar directed multi-type or colored PAMs. Our studies are motivated by the aim to go beyond the rigid rule of edge orientation in directed preferential attachment models. As the main result, for the case of a finite set of colors, we show that the power-law hypothesis for PageRank is fulfilled also for the colored undirected PAM, where, by contrast to the directed case, the power-law exponent is color-dependent for some choices of the initial color distribution and the attractiveness function. For the specific case of a two-type model, we discuss implications of our results on fairness in sampling underrepresented nodes from the network.

1
Introduction

The preferential attachment model (PAM) was initially introduced by Barabási and Albert in
[
6
]
to model the structure and growth of the web graph and its particular property of being scale free.
In PAMs, at each time step, a new vertex is added to the existing graph. The core idea is that this new vertex has
m
half-edges to connect to the already existing vertices in the network, where high-degree vertices are more likely to be connected to (
preferential attachment
). In the initial formulation in
[
6
]
, the probability of the new vertex in the network to connect an edge to a specific vertex in the graph is
proportional
to the degree of that vertex (up to normalization), with the additional constraint of simplicity, which means that no two of the
m
half edges are allowed to connect to the same existing vertex in the network. Since
[
6
]
, several variants and generalizations of the original Barabási-Albert model have been studied, many of them describing multi-graphs instead of simple graphs (see also
[
15
, Ch. 8]
for a literature overview on common variations of the PAM).

In this paper, we are mainly interested in the case of undirected PAM with a finite number of vertex colors.
We start with a short summary of some of the landmarks in studying versions of the PAM that will become important for the present paper.

For the case of the undirected PAM with an arbitrary finite number of colors, Jordan
[
18
, Theorem 2.2]
provides the precise power-law asymptotics of the degree sequence. The proof is based on studying the discrete dynamical system of the proportion of edge ends that connect to a vertex with a specified color. Furthermore, in the case of colors in an arbitrary (possibly infinite) space (see
[
11
]
for an introduction to that case), and building upon the formerly mentioned result,
[
18
, Theorem 2.3]
gives power-law bounds on the limiting degree-sequence.
These result extend earlier results on the PAM with
fitness

[
9
]
, where the linear attachment rule is perturbed by a multiplicatively acting random attribute (
fitness
) of the target vertex.
Going beyond the degree sequence, Berger et al.
[
8
]
first provided a full description of the
local limit
of the undirected preferential attachment model without colors. Here, the proof in
[
8
]
is based on exploiting the close relation between the construction of the PAM and the update rule in a Pólya urn model by means of a
Pólya urn representation
of the PAM.

In short,
local
(weak) convergence of sequences of (random or deterministic)
undirected
graphs (cf.
[
1
,
7
]
) is a notion defined on the space of connected rooted graphs up to root-preserving isomorphisms, into which any graph can be embedded by choosing a vertex uniformly at random from the graph as a root, and restricting to the connected component around that vertex.
Note that the limiting behavior of (root-)PageRank is uniquely determined by the local limit (if it exists)
[
13
,
16
,
17
]
.
For sequences of
directed
(random or deterministic) graphs, similar notions of local convergence exist, where the notion used in
[
13
]
, which will be particularly relevant for the remainder of this paper, is based on the topology of
vertex-marked
incoming neighborhoods, with the vertex marks denoting the vertex out-degrees. Following
[
13
]
, any neighborhood of a vertex in the directed graph contains only vertices that are
explorable
by backtracking paths of
incoming
edges.
In the case of the PAM without colors, the known weak limit for the undirected case is sufficient to directly deduce the corresponding directed local limit (in the above sense) from it.
This observation was first used in
[
5
]
in the context of the PageRank asymptotics for the
directed
PAM without marks, where the local limit for
m\geq 2
was taken from the undirected case discussed in
[
8
]
and then extended by a new representation in terms of a stopped continuous-time branching process.
For the directed version of the preferential attachment model with marks in an arbitrary finite space,
[
3
, Theorem 4.3]
provides a full description of the local limit of in-components. Interestingly, in contrast to
[
8
]
, the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 4.3]
is not based on a Pólya urn representation, but on a generalization of the tree case (
m=1
), which is proved in
[
3
, Theorem 3.5]
employing the notion of
fringe convergence
. Fringe convergence is a tree-specific formulation of local convergence introduced by Aldous in
[
2
]
. This allows the authors of
[
3
]
to prove directed local convergence by means of an adaption of the dynamical system approach of
[
18
]
. However, making heavy use on the orientation of edges, the proof of
[
3
]
does not have an obvious adaption to the undirected set-up.
For the case of the
generalized
undirected preferential attachment model without colors, but with a random offspring distribution for the new vertices,
[
10
]
provides a description of the limiting behavior of the degree sequence, while, under a moment assumption on the offspring distribution of the new vertices,
[
12
]
extends this result to a full description of the local limit of the graph sequence.

Main contribution of this paper.

We prove power-law bounds for the PageRank at a uniformly chosen vertex from an undirected multi-type PAM conditioned on its color (see Theorem
2.1
below). In contrast to the corresponding known result for the directed model with edges directed from younger to older vertices, the power-law exponents for the PageRank in the undirected model can exhibit a dependence on the type of the vertex (see Corollary
2.2
below).
As an application to fairness in sampling nodes from an undirected two-type network, where vertices of one type form an underrepresented (regarding the proportion of vertices in the network) minority, we compare sampling by PageRank with uniform sampling and sampling by degree for large networks and small values of the damping factor
c
(see Proposition
3.3
below). In the case of a symmetric attractiveness kernel, we illustrate our results by simulations.

1.2
PageRank on undirected PAM with finite set of colors

In this subsection we establish the basic notions that we will employ and the definition of the model that is central to this paper.

Undirected PAM with a finite set of colors.

We adapt the notations in
[
18
,
3
,
14
]
to establish our formal framework.
Let
S=\{1,2,\ldots,q\}=:[q]
, where
q\in\mathbb{N}
, denote the finite set of colors, and let
\mu
be any strictly positive fixed probability measure on
(S,2^{S})
, where we abbreviate
\mu_{i}:=\mu(\{i\})
for the atoms.
Further, let
\kappa\colon S\times S\rightarrow(0,\infty)
denote the
attractiveness
function. Finally, let
\boldsymbol{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{q})^{T}\in\mathbb{N}^{q}
be a fixed deterministic vector, where as in the reminder of this paper, boldface fonts denote vectors or matrices.

We define a sequence
(G_{n})_{n\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})}
of undirected colored multi-graphs without self-loops that grows sequentially as follows:

Initially, at time
n=0
, there is a base graph
G_{0}
with
n_{0}
vertices, where each vertex
v
in
G_{0}
has a color
x_{v}\in S
.
For any time
n+1>0
, given the sequence
(G_{s})_{s\leq n}
, let
v_{n+1}
denote the new vertex that is added to the graph
G_{n}
.
First, the vertex
v_{n+1}
is assigned a color
X_{v_{n+1}}
according to
\mu
independently of everything else.
Second, conditionally on
X_{v_{n+1}}=k
, the vertex
v_{n+1}
is assigned
m_{k}
half-edges, which we denote by
v^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{n+1},\ldots,v^{\scriptscriptstyle(m_{k})}_{n+1}
.
Third, conditionally on
G_{n}
and
X_{v_{n+1}}=k
, the
m_{k}
half-edges of vertex
n+1
are independently attached to vertices in the existing graph
G_{n}
according to

\mathbb{P}(v^{\scriptscriptstyle(h)}_{n+1}\longrightarrow v\mid G_{n},\ X_{v_{n+1}}=k)=\frac{\kappa(X_{v},k)d_{v}(G_{n})}{\sum_{v^{\prime}\in G_{n}}\kappa(X_{v^{\prime}},k)d_{v^{\prime}}(G_{n})},

(1)

where
d_{v^{\prime}}(G_{n})
is the degree of vertex
v
in
G_{n}

before
any of the half-edges of
v_{n+1}
has been attached to the graph.
Note that due to the specific form of the attachment rule (
1
), the model is invariant under multiplication of
\kappa
by a positive constant.

Remark 1.1

(Attachment rule in non-tree case: independent vs. sequential model)
.

The attachment rule (
1
), which is the basis of all results taken from
[
18
]
and
[
3
]
, does not consider an intermediate update of the degrees in the course of subsequentially attaching the half-edges of the new vertex. In the terminology of
[
8
]
, this describes the
independent model
, where the results of
[
8
]
regarding the undirected PAM without colors refer to the
sequential model
, in which the degrees occurring in the attachment rule are updated after each half-edge attachment.

Graph-normalized PageRank.

For any undirected finite graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
, the
graph-normalized
(normalized by the number of vertices)
PageRank

(R_{v})_{v\in V}
with damping factor
c\in(0,1)
is defined as the unique solution to

R_{v}=R_{v}(G)=c\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in V\\
w\sim v\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d_{w}}R_{w}+1-c,

(2)

where we write
w\sim v
when
v
and
w
are vertices in
G
that are connected by an edge, i.e., the sum runs over all nearest neighbors of
v
. In case of multi-edges, the respective term is multiplied by the multiplicity of the edge.

2
Result: Color-dependent power-law for root PageRank

In order to state our first main result, we introduce some definitions and objects from
[
3
]
. The meaning and intuition behind these will be explained later on. Note that our notation partially differs from that in
[
3
]
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\boldsymbol{m}}=(\eta^{\boldsymbol{m}}_{1},\ldots,\eta^{\boldsymbol{m}}_{q})
be the unique minimizer for the
convex function

V^{\boldsymbol{m}}(y):=\sum_{i=1}^{q}y_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{q}m_{k}\mu_{k}\left[\log(y_{k})+\log\left(\sum_{l=1}^{q}y_{l}\kappa(l,k)\right)\right],\quad\boldsymbol{y}\in[0,\infty)^{q},

(3)

in the interior of the set

\Big\{\boldsymbol{y}\in[0,\infty)^{q}\mid\sum_{k=1}^{q}y_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{q}\mu_{k}m_{k}\Big\}.

The function
V^{\boldsymbol{m}}
first appeared in
[
18
]
for the case of
m_{k}=m_{1}
for all
k\in S
, i.e., when the number of half-edges of a new vertex does not depend on its color.
For
k,l\in S
, set

\varphi_{k,l}^{\boldsymbol{m}}:=\frac{m_{l}\kappa(k,l)\mu_{l}}{\sum_{l^{\prime}\in S}\kappa(l^{\prime},l)\eta^{\boldsymbol{m}}_{l^{\prime}}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}:=\sum_{l\in S}\varphi_{k,l}^{\boldsymbol{m}}.

(4)

For our next theorem, we only need
\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}
, but the definition of
\varphi_{k,l}^{\boldsymbol{m}}
is relevant in what follows.
Finally, for functions
f,g\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we write, for
a\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}
, that
f=o(g)
as
x
tends to
a
if and only if
\limsup_{x\rightarrow a}|\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}|=0
and
f=O(g)
as
x
tends to
a
if and only if
\limsup_{x\rightarrow a}|\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}|<\infty
.
With these definitions in hand, our main result is as follows:

Theorem 2.1

(Power-law bounds for PageRank in undirected multi-type PAM)
.

Consider the undirected multi-type PAM
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with finite set of colors
S=\{1,2,\ldots,q\}
with
q\in\mathbb{N}
. Further, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\phi_{n}
denote a vertex that is chosen uniformly at random from
G_{n}
, and let
c\in(0,1)
.
Then there exists a constant
\gamma\in(1,\infty)
that does not depend on the color
k
such that, as
r\rightarrow\infty
, the graph-normalized PageRank with damping factor
c
satisfies

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{\phi_{n}}(G_{n})>r\mid X_{\phi_{n}}=k)\geq(1+o(1))\frac{2\Gamma(m_{k}+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}})}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}\Gamma(m_{k})}\sum_{s=\lceil\gamma r\rceil}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}}+1)}

(5)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{\phi_{n}}(G_{n})>r\mid X_{\phi_{n}}=k)\leq\frac{2\Gamma(m_{k}+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}})}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}\Gamma(m_{k})}\sum_{s=\lceil r\rceil}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}}+1)}.

(6)

We will prove the upper and the lower power-law bound separately in Propositions
4.1
(upper bound) and
4.6
(lower bound) below.
By Stirling’s formula, as
s
tends to infinity,

\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(s+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}}+1)}=\left(1+O(s^{-1})\right)s^{-\lambda_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}},

where
\lambda_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}:=1+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}}.

In particular, by Theorem
2.1
and the Euler-Maclaurin formula, as
r
tends to infinity, we obtain the following power-law bounds for PageRank:

Corollary 2.2

(Exponents in power-law bounds)
.

For
k\in S
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{\phi_{n}}(G_{n})>r\mid X_{\phi_{n}}=k)\geq(1+o(1))\frac{\Gamma(m_{k}+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}})}{\Gamma(m_{k})}\gamma^{-(\lambda_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}-1)}r^{-(\lambda_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}-1)}

(7)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(R_{\phi_{n}}(G_{n})>r\mid X_{\phi_{n}}=k)\leq(1+o(1))\frac{\Gamma(m_{k}+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}})}{\Gamma(m_{k})}r^{-(\lambda_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}-1)}.

(8)

Thus, the exponent for the limiting power-law tails of the PageRank of a uniformly chosen vertex of color
k
is
\lambda_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}-1=\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}}
.

3.2
Fairness: Sampling uniformly, by PageRank, and by degree

Consider the graph-normalized total PageRank of the minority and majority vertices, respectively, given by

\begin{split}R_{\mathrm{mino}}(G_{n})&=\sum_{v\in V(G_{n})\colon X_{v}=1}R_{v}(G_{n})\qquad\text{ and}\qquad R_{\mathrm{majo}}(G_{n})=\sum_{v\in V(G_{n})\colon X_{v}=2}R_{v}(G_{n}).\end{split}

(14)

Small-
c
approximation of the total minority PageRank.

Iterating the PageRank equation once gives

\begin{split}R_{\mathrm{mino}}(G_{n})&=\sum_{v\in V(G_{n})\colon X_{v}=1}\left((1-c)+c\sum_{w\sim v}\frac{R_{w}(G_{n})}{d_{w}(G_{n})}\right)\cr&=\sum_{v\in V(G_{n})\colon X_{v}=1}\left((1-c)+c\sum_{w\sim v}\frac{1}{d_{w}(G_{n})}\left((1-c)+c\sum_{\ell\sim w}\frac{R_{\ell}(G_{n})}{d_{\ell}(G_{n})}\right)\right).\end{split}

(15)

With the notations

N_{\mathrm{mino}}(G_{n}):=\#\{v\in V(G_{n})\mid X_{v}=1\}\text{ and }d^{\mathrm{mino}}_{v}(G_{n}):=\#\{w\in V(G_{n})\mid w\sim v\text{ and }X_{w}=1\}

rearranging terms leads to

\begin{split}R_{\mathrm{mino}}(G_{n})&=(1-c)N_{\mathrm{mino}}(G_{n})+c(1-c)\sum_{v\in V(G_{n})}\frac{d_{v}^{\mathrm{mino}}(G_{n})}{d_{v}(G_{n})}+c^{2}\mathbbm{L}_{c}(G_{n})\cr&=(1-c)N_{\mathrm{mino}}(G_{n})+c(1-c)n\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{d_{\phi_{n}}^{\mathrm{mino}}}{d_{\phi_{n}}}\mid G_{n}\Big]+c^{2}\mathbbm{L}_{c}(G_{n}),\end{split}

(16)

where
\phi_{n}
is uniformly chosen from
V(G_{n})
, and the remainder term
\mathbbm{L}_{c}
is given by

\begin{split}\mathbb{L}_{c}&=\sum_{\ell\in V(G_{n})}R_{\ell}(G_{n})\sum_{v\in V(G_{n})\colon X_{v}=1}\left(P(G_{n})\right)^{2}_{\ell v}\end{split}

with
P_{\ell v}(G_{n})=\frac{1}{d_{\ell}}1_{\ell\sim v}
denoting the random walk transition matrix of
G_{n}

We will now compare the large-

A brief recap on local convergence.

Let us begin with a brief recap on local convergence. For a more detailed overview we refer to
[

First assume that
(\mathcal{G}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is any sequence of finite

and any deterministic rooted graph

\mathcal{N}_{(\mathcal{H},\rho),s}^{(n)}:=\{v\in V(\mathcal{G}_{n})\mid B_{s}(\mathcal{G}_{n},v)\cong(\mathcal{H},\rho)\}

of vertices

for which the closed ball of radius

around

(with respect to the usual graph distance), viewed as a rooted graph with root

under graph-isomorphisms that preserve the root.
Then we say that

converges

(weakly/in probability/almost surely) to a random locally finite connected rooted graph

\frac{\#\mathcal{N}_{(\mathcal{H},\rho),s}^{(n)}}{\#V(G_{n})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathbb{P}(B_{s}(\mathcal{G}^{\star},\rho^{\star})\cong(\mathcal{H},\rho))\quad\text{(weakly/in probability/almost surely).}

(25)

Next, for any sequence of finite
directed
graphs
(\vec{\mathcal{G}}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

with respect to

In (

denotes equivalence under isomorphisms that preserve both the root and the marks of all vertices.

In the framework of this paper, the above mentioned marks are the colors of the vertices, which determine the out-degrees of the respective vertices.

Proof of Lemma
4.9
.

The proof is based on calculating the first two moments of the random sum in (

\bar{\ell}_{k,\tilde{t}/2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(r)}:=\inf\{l\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}\mid\sum_{j=l}^{r}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\tau}_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(j)}]\leq\tilde{t}/2\}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}.

First,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{j=l}^{r}\tilde{\tau}_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(j)}\Big]=\frac{1}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}}\sum_{j=l}^{r}\frac{1}{j+m_{k}}=\frac{1}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}}\sum_{j=l+m_{k}}^{r+m_{k}}\frac{1}{j}.

Using
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j}=\log(n)+\gamma+\frac{1}{2n}-L_{n},

where
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant, and

and considering the boundary cases for

gives

\sum_{j=l+m_{k}}^{r+m_{k}}\frac{1}{j}\leq\log(r+m_{k})+\frac{1}{2(r+m_{k})}-\left(\log(l+m_{k}-1)+\frac{1}{2(l+m_{k}-1)}-\frac{1}{8(l+m_{k}-1)^{2}}\right),

so from (

\mathbb{E}[\sum_{j=l}^{r}\tilde{\tau}_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(j)}]\leq\tilde{t}/2
is guaranteed whenever the r.h.s. of (

\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}\tilde{t}/2

\begin{split}&\log(l+m_{k}-1)+\frac{1}{2(l+m_{k}-1)}-\frac{1}{8(l+m_{k}-1)^{2}}\geq\log(r+m_{k})+\frac{1}{2(r+m_{k})}-\varphi_{m_{k}}^{\boldsymbol{m}}\tilde{t}/2.\end{split}

(39)

In other words, the infimum of all
l\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
that satisfy (
39
) provides an upper bound on
\bar{\ell}_{k,\tilde{t}/2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(r)}.

Similarly,

\sum_{j=l+m_{k}}^{r+m_{k}}\frac{1}{j}\geq\log(r+m_{k})+\frac{1}{2(r+m_{k})}-\frac{1}{8(r+m_{k})^{2}}-\left(\log(l+m_{k}-1)+\frac{1}{2(l+m_{k}-1)}\right),

(40)

so from (
37

\mathbb{E}[\sum_{j=l}^{r}\tilde{\tau}_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(j)}]\leq\tilde{t}/2
then the r.h.s. of (
40
) is necessarily smaller than or equal to
\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}\tilde{t}/2
, which is equivalent to
l
being large enough to satisfy

\begin{split}&\log(l+m_{k}-1)+\frac{1}{2(l+m_{k}-1)}\geq\log(r+m_{k})+\frac{1}{2(r+m_{k})}-\frac{1}{8(r+m_{k})^{2}}-\varphi_{m_{k}}^{\boldsymbol{m}}\tilde{t}/2.\end{split}

(41)

In other words, the infimum of all
l\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
that satisfy (
41
) provides a lower bound on
\bar{\ell}_{k,\tilde{t}/2}^{\scriptscriptstyle(r)}.

Note that any
l
that is large enough to fulfill (
39
) will also fulfill (
41
).
Further, applying the inequalities

\phi

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{T}}_{k}(d_{\phi,d+1}^{-,\mathrm{mino}}=d_{\phi,d}^{-,\mathrm{mino}}+1\mid d_{\phi}^{-}=d+1+s)\cr&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{T}}_{k}(d_{\phi,d+1}^{-,\mathrm{mino}}=d_{\phi,d}^{-,\mathrm{mino}}+1\mid\tau_{d}=t)\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\tau_{d}}(dt)\cr&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\mathcal{T}}_{k}(\tau_{t,\mathrm{mino}}<\tau_{t,\mathrm{majo}})\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\tau_{d}}(dt)=\frac{\varphi^{m}_{k,1}}{\varphi^{m}_{k,1}+\varphi^{m}_{k,2}}.\end{split}

(59)

This proves the induction step. With the choice
s=0
this thus concludes the proof of the first statement of Lemma
4.11
.

For the second statement, we condition on the value of
d_{\phi}^{-}+m_{k}
to obtain

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{T}}_{k}\Big[\frac{d_{\phi}^{-,\mathrm{mino}}}{d_{\phi}^{-}+m_{k}}\Big]=\sum_{r=m_{k}}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}\Big[\frac{d_{\phi}^{-,\mathrm{mino}}}{r}\mid d_{\phi}^{-}+m_{k}=r\Big]\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}(d_{\phi}^{-}+m_{k}=r).

(60)

By the first statement of the lemma, conditionally on
d_{\phi}^{-}+m_{k}=r
and
X_{\phi}=k
, the random variable
d_{\phi}^{-,\mathrm{mino}}
is binomially distributed with parameters
r-m_{k}
and
\varphi^{m}_{k,1}/(\varphi^{m}_{k,1}+\varphi^{m}_{k,2})
. Thus,

\mathbb{E}_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}\Big[\frac{d_{\phi}^{-,\mathrm{mino}}}{r}\mid d_{\phi}^{-}+m_{k}=r\Big]=\frac{\varphi^{m}_{k,1}}{\varphi^{m}_{k,1}+\varphi^{m}_{k,2}}\left(1-\frac{m_{k}}{r}\right).

(61)

Inserting (
61
) into (
60
) finishes the proof of Lemma
4.11
.
∎

The degrees and minority-out degrees are asymptotically independent.

The behavior of
d_{\phi_{n}}^{+,\mathrm{mino}}/d_{\phi_{n}}
is not described by the directed local limit in Theorem
4.4
, which only provides information on finite incoming neighborhoods of
\phi_{n}
and its color. In particular, controlling the correlation between
d_{\phi_{n}}^{+,\mathrm{mino}}
and
d_{\phi_{n}}
needs an additional argument. In Lemma
4.13
below we will describe the asymptotic joint law of
(d_{\phi_{n}},d_{\phi_{n}}^{+,\mathrm{mino},(n)},X_{\phi_{n}})
. The proof of Lemma
4.13
is an extension of the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 2.2]
, which describes the distribution of
d_{\phi_{n}}
as

, and also serves as the basis of the proof of Theorem
4.4
.
It is based on the following stochastic recursion:

Lemma 4.12

(Lemma 3.3 in
[
18
]
)
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
be random variables taking non-negative values,
\xi_{n},R_{n}
random variables taking real values, and
a
and
b
positive constants such that

B_{n+1}-B_{n}=\frac{1}{n}(A_{n}-bB_{n}+\xi_{n})+R_{n+1},

where

1.

A_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\rightarrow}}a
almost surely;

2.

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}R_{n}<\infty;

3.

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{n}]=0
and
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded.

Then
B_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\rightarrow}}a/b
almost surely.

We start our extension by recalling and introducing some notation.
Recall that
M=\mu_{1}m_{1}+\mu_{2}m_{2}
and, for every
y\in(0,M)
, define

p_{y,k}=\frac{\kappa(1,k)y}{\kappa(1,k)y+\kappa(2,k)(M-y)}.

(62)

Then, the statement of Lemma
4.13
is as follows:

Lemma 4.13
.

For any
k\in\{1,2\}
, any
\omega\in\{0,1,\ldots,m_{k}\}
and any
d\in\{m_{k},m_{k}+1,\ldots\}
,
as
n\to\infty

\left(\mathbb{P}(d_{\phi_{n}}=d,d_{\phi_{n}}^{+,\mathrm{mino},(n)}=\omega,X_{\phi_{n}}=k\mid G_{n})\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

(63)

converges almost surely to

\mu_{k}\cdot\frac{2\Gamma(m_{k}+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}})}{\varphi_{k}\Gamma(m_{k})}\frac{\Gamma(d)}{\Gamma(d+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}}+1)}\cdot\binom{m_{k}}{\omega}p^{\omega}_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k}(1-p_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k})^{m_{k}-\omega}.

(64)

Proof of Lemma
4.13
.

The proof is an adaption of the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 2.2]
to cover the additional constraint on
d_{\phi_{n}}^{+,\mathrm{mino},(n)}
. First define

\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}:=\{v\in V(G_{n})\mid d_{v}=d,d_{v}^{+,\mathrm{mino},(n)}=\omega,X_{v}=k\}

(65)

and

Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}:=\frac{1}{n+n_{0}}\#\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}.

(66)

We want to prove almost sure convergence of
(Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to the quantity in (
64
).
First note that, for each
u\in G_{n}
with
d_{u}=d
and
X_{u}=k
, by the attachment rule (
21
) and Proposition
4.1
,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}(v_{n+1}\text{ does not connect to }u\mid G_{n})\cr&=\sum_{l=1}^{2}\mu_{l}\left(1-\frac{d\kappa(k,l)}{2(n+n_{0})\sum_{\tilde{l}=1}^{2}\kappa(\tilde{l},l)\tilde{Y}_{\tilde{l}}^{\boldsymbol{m},(n)}}\right)^{m_{l}}\cr&=\sum_{l=1}^{2}\mu_{l}\left(1-m_{l}\frac{d\kappa(k,l)}{2(n+n_{0})\sum_{\tilde{l}=1}^{2}\kappa(\tilde{l},l)\tilde{Y}_{\tilde{l}}^{\boldsymbol{m},(n)}}\right)+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1/n)\cr&=1-\frac{d}{2(n+n_{0})}\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1/n).\end{split}

(67)

Here, the second equality follows from a first-order Taylor expansion in combination with the assumption of strict positivity of
\kappa
.
Furthermore, for a sequence of random variables
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and a sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive numbers, we write
\xi_{n}=o_{\mathbb{P}}(a_{n})
when
(\xi_{n}/a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in probability to zero.
Similarly, for each such
u
,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}(v_{n+1}\text{ connects to }u\text{ via a single edge}\mid G_{n})=\frac{d}{2(n+n_{0})}\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1/n).\end{split}

(68)

From (
67
) and (
68
), we obtain, for every vertex
u\in G_{n}
with prescribed degree and color, that

\mathbb{P}(v_{n+1}\text{ and }u\text{ are connected by a multi-edge}\mid G_{n})=o_{\mathbb{P}}(1/n).

(69)

Next, (
67
), (
68
), (
69
), and linearity of the conditional expectation, give

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n+1)}\mid G_{n}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+n_{0}+1}\Big((n+n_{0})Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}(1-\frac{d}{2(n+n_{0})}\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1/n))

(70)

\displaystyle+Z_{k,d-1,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}(\frac{d-1}{2}\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1))+\mu_{k}\delta_{m_{k},d}\binom{m_{k}}{\omega}p^{\omega}_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k}(1-p_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k})^{m_{k}-\omega}+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1)\Big).

Here, in view of the independent attachment rule, the last term in (
70
) addresses the case that
v_{n+1}\in\mathcal{N}_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n+1)}
, where
\delta_{m_{k},d}=1
if
m_{k}=d
and
\delta_{m_{k},d}=0
, else.
We may reformulate (
70
) as

\begin{split}&Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n+1)}-Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}=\frac{1}{n+n_{0}+1}\Big(-Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}(1+\frac{d}{2}\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1))+Z_{k,d-1,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}(\frac{d-1}{2}\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1))\cr&+\mu_{k}\delta_{m_{k},d}\binom{m_{k}}{\omega}p^{\omega}_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k}(1-p_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k})^{m_{k}-\omega}+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1)+(n+n_{0}+1)(Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n+1)}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n+1)}\mid G_{n}])\Big).\end{split}

(71)

Note that the noise term
(n+n_{0}-1)(Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n+1)}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n+1)}\mid G_{n}])
in (
71
) satisfies

\displaystyle(n+n_{0}+1)(Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n+1)}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n+1)}\mid G_{n}])

\displaystyle\leq|(n+n_{0}+1)Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n+1)}-(n+n_{0})Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n+1)}|

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}[|(n+n_{0}+1)Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n+1)}-(n+n_{0}+1)Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|\mid G_{n}]

\displaystyle\leq 2\max\{m_{1},m_{2}\}.

(72)

We proceed to prove almost sure convergence of
(Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
by induction on
d\geq m_{k}
.
In the initial case
d=m_{k}
,
Z_{k,d-1,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}=0
almost surely, and we apply Lemma
4.12
with
B_{n}:=Z_{k,m_{k},\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}
,
A_{n}:=\mu_{k}\binom{m_{k}}{\omega}p^{\omega}_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k}(1-p_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k})^{m_{k}-\omega}+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
,
b=1+\frac{m_{k}}{2}\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}
,
\xi_{n}:=(n+n_{0}+1)(Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n+1)}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n+1)}\mid G_{n}])
and
R_{n}=0
, to obtain

Z_{k,m_{k},\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\rightarrow}}\mu_{k}\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}m_{k}+2}\binom{m_{k}}{\omega}p^{\omega}_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k}(1-p_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k})^{m_{k}-\omega},

(73)

as required, and this initializes the induction hypothesis.
Next, consider
d>m_{k}
and assume that the statement of Lemma

holds for

.
Then we apply Lemma
4.12
to (

) with
B_{n}:=Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}

A_{n}:=Z_{k,d-1,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}(\frac{d-1}{2}\varphi_{k}^{\scriptscriptstyle(m)}+o_{\mathbb{P}(1)})

and

, to obtain

\displaystyle Z_{k,d,\omega}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\rightarrow}}\frac{1}{1+\frac{d}{2}\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}}\frac{d-1}{2}\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}\mu_{k}\ \frac{2\Gamma(m_{k}+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}})}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}\Gamma(m_{k})}\frac{\Gamma(d-1)}{\Gamma(d+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}})}\binom{m_{k}}{\omega}p^{\omega}_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k}(1-p_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k})^{m_{k}-\omega}

\displaystyle=\mu_{k}\frac{2\Gamma(m_{k}+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}})}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}\Gamma(m_{k})}\frac{\Gamma(d)}{\Gamma(d+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}}+1)}\binom{m_{k}}{\omega}p^{\omega}_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k}(1-p_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k})^{m_{k}-\omega}.

(74)

The expected inverse degree of the root.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
\psi_{1}(z):=\frac{d^{2}}{dz^{2}}\log(\Gamma(z))
be the trigamma function. Then

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{T}}_{k}\Big[\frac{1}{d_{\phi}^{-}+m_{k}}\Big]=\frac{2\Gamma(m_{k}+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}})}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}\Gamma(m_{k})\Gamma(1+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}})}\left(\psi_{1}(1+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}})-\sum_{r=1}^{m_{k}-1}\frac{\beta(r,1+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}})}{r}\right).

(75)

Proof of Lemma
4.14
.

From the known distribution of
d_{\phi}
(see
[
3
, Theorem 4.4]
),
we obtain

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{T}}_{k}\Big[\frac{1}{d_{\phi}^{-}+m_{k}}\Big]&=\sum_{r=m_{k}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}(d_{\phi}^{-}+m_{k}=r)=\frac{2\Gamma(m_{k}+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}})}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}\Gamma(m_{k})}\sum_{r=m_{k}}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(r)}{r\Gamma(r+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}}+1)}\end{split}

(76)

Now recall the definition of the
\beta
-function:

\beta(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{\Gamma(z_{1})\Gamma(z_{2})}{\Gamma(z_{1}+z_{2})}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{z_{1}-1}(1-t)^{z_{2}-1}\text{d}t\text{ for all }z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}\text{ with }\text{Re}(z_{1})>0,\text{Re}(z_{2})>0.

Thus, we have for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
and any
a>2
:

\begin{split}\frac{\Gamma(r)}{r\Gamma(r+a)}=\frac{\beta(r,a)}{r\Gamma(a)}=\frac{1}{r\Gamma(a)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{r-1}(1-t)^{a-1}\,\text{d}t.\end{split}

(77)

In combination with monotone convergence, this allows us to rewrite

\begin{split}\sum_{r=m_{k}}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(r)}{r\Gamma(r+a)}&=\frac{1}{\Gamma(a)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-t)^{a-1}}{t}\sum_{r=m_{k}}^{\infty}\frac{t^{r}}{r}\ \text{d}t\cr&=\frac{1}{\Gamma(a)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-t)^{a-1}}{t}\left(-\log(1-t)-\sum_{r=1}^{m_{k}-1}\frac{t^{r}}{r}\right)\ \text{d}t\cr&=\frac{1}{\Gamma(a)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{z^{a-1}}{1-z}\left(-\log z-\sum_{r=1}^{m_{k}-1}\frac{(1-z)^{r}}{r}\right)\ \text{d}z\cr&=\frac{\psi_{1}(a)}{\Gamma(a)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(a)}\sum_{r=1}^{m_{k}-1}\frac{\beta(a,r)}{r}.\end{split}

(78)

Finally, inserting
a=\lambda_{k}=\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}}+1
(cf. Corollary
2.2
) completes the proof of Lemma
4.14
.
∎

Finalization of the proof of Proposition
3.3
.

Recalling that
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}(d_{\phi}^{-}+m_{k})=\frac{2\Gamma(m_{k}+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}})}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}\Gamma(m_{k})}\frac{\Gamma(d)}{\Gamma(d+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}}}+1)}
, by Lemma

,

\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{d_{\phi_{n}}^{+,\mathrm{mino},(n)}}{d_{\phi_{n}}}\boldsymbol{1}\{X_{\phi_{n}}=k\}\mid G_{n}\Big]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\rightarrow}}\mu_{k}\mathbb{E}_{k}^{\mathcal{T}}\Big[\frac{1}{d_{\phi}^{-}+m_{k}}\Big]m_{k}p_{\eta_{1}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k}\quad\text{almost surely,}

(79)

where, by (

) and (

To formally reason (

4.13
and Scheffé’s Lemma (e.g.,
[

, Lemma 5.10]
)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{2}\sum_{d=m_{k}}^{\infty}\sum_{\omega=1}^{m_{k}}\Big|

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(d_{\phi_{n}}=d,d_{\phi_{n}}^{+,\mathrm{mino},(n)}=\omega,X_{\phi_{n}}=k\mid G_{n})

(80)

\displaystyle\quad-\mu_{k}\cdot\frac{2\Gamma(m_{k}+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}})}{\varphi_{k}\Gamma(m_{k})}\frac{\Gamma(d)}{\Gamma(d+\frac{2}{\varphi_{k}}+1)}\cdot\binom{m_{k}}{\omega}p^{\omega}_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k}(1-p_{\eta_{k}^{\boldsymbol{m}},k})^{m_{k}-\omega}\Big|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\rightarrow}}0,

5
Discussion

Undirected local limit for the colored PAM.

In this paper we studied the PageRank in the undirected colored model; yet, we did not derive its local limit. Instead we based our analysis solely on the known local limit for the directed version. Then, a natural open problem is to derive the local limit for the undirected model with colors.
The approach of Antunes et al.
[
3
]
for obtaining the directed local limit by reducing the problem to discrete dynamical systems of the same type that had already been studied by Jordan
[
18
]
(cf. (
3
) and Lemma
4.12
above) is based on
fringe convergence
, which works particularly well for trees. Its structural strength lies in the fact that it provides local convergence almost surely, going beyond local convergence in probability, which often follows from a second-moment method.
Recall from
[
8
]
that the known local limit for the undirected PAM without colors is a multi-type branching process tree, where vertices carry a two-dimensional type in
[0,1]\times\{y,o\}
describing the absolute time of birth and the relative age (
y
ounger or
o
lder) compared to that of root in the underlying PAM.
We may conjecture that a potential candidate for the local limit in the undirected PAM with colors is a branching process tree where vertex types also include information on the relative age.
It is an interesting question whether the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 4.3]
has a natural adaption to undirected graphs when splitting the test-graph, which under the above conjecture can be assumed to be a tree, into a subgraph consisting of older vertices and a subgraph consisting of younger vertices. A first step in this direction can be seen in the proof of Lemma
4.13
, where we combine a constraint on the incoming neighborhood (the in-degree) with a constraint on the outgoing neighborhood (number of out-edges that connect to minority vertices).
Another approach to obtain an undirected local limit for the colored PAM is to obtain a Pólya-urn representation as established for the model without colors by Berger et al.
[
8
]
. Note that as in the case of the undirected PAM with i.i.d. random numbers of half-edges for the new vertices (cf.
[
12
]
for a study of various sequential PAMs with i.i.d. out-degrees satisfying a moment condition), implementing colors to the model destroys exchangeability, and calculations thus cannot be simplified by employing de Finetti’s Theorem.
In particular, following the strategy of proof employed in
[
12
]
, the steps to prove the undirected local convergence for the colored PAM include:

\rhd

Reduce problem to the tree-case (
m=1
) by identifying the model for
m>1
with a suitable
collapsed
tree-model.

\rhd

Find a candidate for the Pólya urn representation of the tree-model and verify by explicit calculation that it has the same distribution as the colored PAM at least on the level of neighborhoods around uniformly chosen vertices.

\rhd

Analyse the long-term behavior of the representation on the local level.

Precise asymptotics of PageRank in when undirected local limit is known.

Note that we were able to prove the power-law bounds for PageRank in Theorem
2.1
without knowing the local limit (and even more, without knowing that this local limit exists at all) for the undirected PAM with colors. Knowing the local limit gives rise to a convergence statement for PageRank beyond power-law bounds in the following sense:
For any locally finite, potentially infinite connected rooted graph
(G,\phi)
, we define

R_{\phi}(G)=(1-c)\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}c^{s}\sum_{j\in V(G)}(\boldsymbol{P}^{s})_{j\phi}.

(81)

Here,
\boldsymbol{P}=(p_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
is the (possibly infinite-dimensional) matrix which is defined by
p_{ij}=a_{ij}/d_{v_{i}}
, with
\boldsymbol{A}=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
denoting the adjacency matrix of
G
.
Then
[
16
, Theorem 9.2]
guarantees that for any sequence of directed random graphs that converges locally weakly in the marked backward sense with limit
(G_{\infty},\phi)
, the sequence of PageRanks at a uniformly chosen vertex from the respective graph converges weakly to the limiting PageRank
R_{\phi}(G_{\infty})
as defined in (
81
), where
\mathbb{E}[R_{\phi}(G_{\infty})]<\infty
.
This result also covers the case of undirected graphs that converge locally weakly.
