1.
Introduction

Let
n,m\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
\beta\geq 0
. Consider the degenerate elliptic operator

(1.1)

L=-\nabla_{x}\!\cdot\!\bigl(|x|^{2\alpha}\nabla_{x}\bigr)+|x|^{2\beta}\Delta_{y}\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},

where
\Delta_{y}
denotes the standard Laplacian in the
y
-variables. We refer to
[
10
]
for the precise definition and basic properties of
L
. Associated with
L
is the first-order differential operator

\nabla_{L}:=\bigl(|x|^{\alpha}\nabla_{x},\;|x|^{\beta}\nabla_{y}\bigr),

and the corresponding control distance
d
. We write
B(\xi,r)
for the
d
-ball centered at
\xi
with radius
r
, and
P(\Omega)
for the perimeter of a smooth set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
with respect to this geometry.

The purpose of this paper is to establish the isoperimetric inequality on Grushin spaces. More precisely, we prove that for every smooth bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
,

(1.2)

|\Omega|^{\frac{\mathcal{Q}-1}{\mathcal{Q}}}\leq C_{1}P(\Omega),

where
|\cdot|
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
,
P(\Omega)
denotes the perimeter of
\Omega
, and

(1.3)

\mathcal{Q}=\frac{n+m(\beta+1-\alpha)}{1-\alpha}

is the homogeneous dimension associated with
L
. By the co-area formula, (
1.2
) is equivalent to the Sobolev inequality

(
\mathrm{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{p}
)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|f(\xi)|^{\frac{\mathcal{Q}p}{\mathcal{Q}-p}}\,d\xi\right)^{\frac{\mathcal{Q}-p}{\mathcal{Q}}}\leq C_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|\nabla_{L}f(\xi)|^{p}\,d\xi,\qquad\forall f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m}),

in the case
p=1
.

We now state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
n,m\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
\beta\geq 0
. Then the isoperimetric inequality (
1.2
), or equivalently the Sobolev inequality (

S
Q

p

) with
p=1
, holds on
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
.

Since
\nabla_{L}
is local, the family (

S
Q

p

) enjoys a self-improvement property:

(\mathrm{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{p})\implies(\mathrm{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{q}),\qquad 1\leq p\leq q<\mathcal{Q}.

As a consequence, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose that
n,m\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
\beta\geq 0
. Then the Sobolev inequality
(\mathrm{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{p})
holds on
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
for all
1\leq p<\mathcal{Q}
.

In the Grushin plane, the isoperimetric inequality was established in
[
9
]
. In higher dimensions, Franceschi and Monti
[
5
]
proved it for
n=1
,
\alpha=0
, and
m\geq 1
. Moreover, for
n\geq 2
, they obtained the corresponding result for
x
-spherically symmetric sets. Finally, under the same assumptions as in Theorem
1.1
, Hauer and Sikora
[
6
, Theorem 6.3]
established the Sobolev inequality
(\mathrm{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{p})
for
2\leq p<\mathcal{Q}
.

Our argument is motivated by the following observation. Endow
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
with the metric

g=|x|^{-2\alpha}dx^{2}+|x|^{-2\beta}dy^{2}.

Passing to polar coordinates in the
x
-variable and introducing
\rho=(1-\alpha)^{-1}r^{1-\alpha}
, one sees that
g
can be written as the doubly warped product metric

g=d\rho^{2}+[(1-\alpha)\rho]^{2}\,d\theta^{2}+[(1-\alpha)\rho]^{-2\beta/(1-\alpha)}\,dy^{2}

on

(0,\infty)_{\rho}\times_{(1-\alpha)\rho}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times_{[(1-\alpha)\rho]^{-\beta/(1-\alpha)}}\mathbb{R}^{m}.

By the standard curvature formula for doubly warped products,

\textrm{Ric}_{\xi}\geq-\frac{C(n,m,\alpha,\beta)}{\rho^{2}}\,g_{\xi}=-\frac{C(n,m,\alpha,\beta)}{|x|^{2-2\alpha}}\,g_{\xi}.

Although our Grushin operator
L
is not the standard Laplace–Beltrami operator associated with
g
, this computation suggests a corresponding curvature-dimension inequality for
-L
. Combined with a remote ball argument from
[
2
]
, this yields a suitable gradient estimate for the heat semigroup. We then apply the method of
[
7
]
, based on a weak-type Sobolev inequality and a Hardy-type inequality, to obtain the desired isoperimetric estimate.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, we write
\xi=(x,y)
and
\eta=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
, where
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We denote by
B(\xi,r)
the Grushin ball
B(\xi,r):=\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}:d(\xi,\eta)<r\}
, and by
V(\xi,r):=|B(\xi,r)|
its volume with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
. We also write
B_{n}
and
B_{m}
for Euclidean balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, respectively.

The following lemma summarizes some basic geometric properties of Grushin spaces; see
[
10
, Proposition 5.1, Theorem 6.4]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
n,m\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\beta\geq 0
.

(1)

Distance estimate: there exists constant
C_{d}>0
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle C_{d}^{-1}D(\xi,\eta)\leq d(\xi,\eta)\leq C_{d}D(\xi,\eta),

where

D(\xi,\eta)=\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{\left(|x|+|x^{\prime}|\right)^{\alpha}}+\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{(|x|+|x^{\prime}|)^{\beta}+|y-y^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+1-\alpha}}}.

(2)

Volume estimate:

\displaystyle V(\xi,r)=V((x,y),r)\sim\begin{cases}r^{\mathcal{Q}},&r\geq|x|^{1-\alpha},\\
r^{n+m}|x|^{n\alpha+m\beta},&r\leq|x|^{1-\alpha}.\end{cases}

In addition, there exists constant
C_{D}>0
such that the volume doubling condition holds:

\displaystyle\frac{V(\xi,R)}{V(\xi,r)}\leq C_{D}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\mathcal{Q}}

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
and all
R\geq r>0
.

(3)

Gaussian upper bound for heat kernel:

e^{-tL}(\xi,\eta)\leq\frac{C}{V(\xi,\sqrt{t})}e^{-\frac{d(\xi,\eta)^{2}}{ct}}

for all
t>0
and almost every
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
.

With Lemma
2.1
, we develop the following property.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
n,m\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\beta\geq 0
. Let
\xi=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
. There exist
0<c_{1}<c_{2}
and
\epsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for all
r\leq\epsilon_{0}|x|^{1-\alpha}
, one has

(2.2)

B_{n}(x,c_{1}r|x|^{\alpha})\times B_{m}(y,c_{1}r|x|^{\beta})\subset B(\xi,r)\subset B_{n}(x,c_{2}r|x|^{\alpha})\times B_{m}(y,c_{2}r|x|^{\beta}).

Moreover, there exist
0<c_{3}<c_{4}
such that for all
r>0
, one has for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

(2.3)

B_{n}(0,c_{3}r)\times B_{m}(y,c_{3}r^{\beta+1-\alpha})\subset B((0,y),r^{1-\alpha})\subset B_{n}(0,c_{4}r)\times B_{m}(y,c_{4}r^{\beta+1-\alpha}).

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

We first show that

(2.4)

B(\xi,r)\subset B_{n}(x,c_{2}r|x|^{\alpha})\times B_{m}(y,c_{2}r|x|^{\beta}).

Take
\eta=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in B(\xi,r)
. Then by Lemma
2.1
,
D(\xi,\eta)\leq C_{d}r
. We claim that

(2.5)

|x-x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{|x|}{2},

provided
\epsilon_{0}
is small enough. Indeed, if not, then
|x|+|x^{\prime}|\leq|x-x^{\prime}|+2|x|\leq 3|x-x^{\prime}|
and thus

\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{\left(|x|+|x^{\prime}|\right)^{\alpha}}\geq 3^{-\alpha}|x-x^{\prime}|^{1-\alpha}\geq 3^{-\alpha}2^{\alpha-1}|x|^{1-\alpha},

which further implies that
D(\xi,\eta)\geq 3^{-\alpha}2^{\alpha-1}|x|^{1-\alpha}
. Pick
\epsilon_{0}<C_{d}^{-1}3^{-\alpha}2^{\alpha-1}
. Then the previous argument contradicts the fact that
D(\xi,\eta)\leq C_{d}r
and hence the claim (
2.5
) is verified. With (
2.5
), one gets

(2.6)

\frac{|x|}{2}\leq|x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{3}{2}|x|\quad\textrm{and}\quad|x|+|x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{5}{2}|x|.

Next, since
D(\xi,\eta)\leq C_{d}r
, we must have

\displaystyle\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{\left(|x|+|x^{\prime}|\right)^{\alpha}}\leq C_{d}r\implies|x-x^{\prime}|\leq C_{d}r\left(|x|+|x^{\prime}|\right)^{\alpha}\leq C_{d}\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^{\alpha}r|x|^{\alpha}.

While for the
y
-part of
D(\xi,\eta)
, if
\left(|x|+|x^{\prime}|\right)^{\beta+1-\alpha}\geq|y-y^{\prime}|
then one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{2\left(|x|+|x^{\prime}|\right)^{\beta}}\leq\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|}{\left(|x|+|x^{\prime}|\right)^{\beta}+|y-y^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+1-\alpha}}}\leq C_{d}r.

By (
2.6
), one yields

|y-y^{\prime}|\leq 2C_{d}r\left(|x|+|x^{\prime}|\right)^{\beta}\leq 2C_{d}\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^{\beta}r|x|^{\beta}.

If
\left(|x|+|x^{\prime}|\right)^{\beta+1-\alpha}\leq|y-y^{\prime}|
, then

\displaystyle|y-y^{\prime}|^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\beta+1-\alpha}}\leq 2C_{d}r\implies|y-y^{\prime}|\leq\left(2C_{d}\right)^{\frac{\beta+1-\alpha}{1-\alpha}}rr^{\frac{\beta}{1-\alpha}}\leq\left(2C_{d}\right)^{\frac{\beta+1-\alpha}{1-\alpha}}r|x|^{\beta}.

Setting
c_{2}=\max\left(C_{d}\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^{\alpha},2C_{d}\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^{\beta},\left(2C_{d}\right)^{\frac{\beta+1-\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\right)
, one concludes (
2.4
).

The proof of the converse direction:

(2.7)

B_{n}(x,c_{1}r|x|^{\alpha})\times B_{m}(y,c_{1}r|x|^{\beta})\subset B(\xi,r)

is obvious. Indeed, for
\eta=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
such that
|x-x^{\prime}|\leq c_{1}r|x|^{\alpha}
and
|y-y^{\prime}|\leq c_{1}r|x|^{\beta}
, one directly bounds

d(\xi,\eta)\leq C_{d}D(\xi,\eta)\leq C_{d}\frac{c_{1}r|x|^{\alpha}}{\left(|x|+|x^{\prime}|\right)^{\alpha}}+C_{d}\frac{c_{1}r|x|^{\beta}}{\left(|x|+|x^{\prime}|\right)^{\beta}+|y-y^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+1-\alpha}}}\leq 2C_{d}c_{1}r.

Choosing
c_{1}=\frac{1}{4C_{d}}
, one confirms
\eta\in B(\xi,r)
as desired.

The proof for (
2.3
) is similar and we omit it.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Note that the constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
depend only on
C_{d}
,
\alpha
, and
\beta
. Therefore, by choosing
\epsilon_{0}<\min\left(C_{d}^{-1}3^{-\alpha}2^{\alpha-1},\frac{1}{2c_{2}}\right)
, one sees that for every
r\leq\epsilon_{0}|x|^{1-\alpha}
and every
\eta=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in B_{n}(x,c_{2}r|x|^{\alpha})\times B_{m}(y,c_{2}r|x|^{\beta})
, we have

|x|\leq|x-x^{\prime}|+|x^{\prime}|\leq c_{2}r|x|^{\alpha}+|x^{\prime}|\leq c_{2}\epsilon_{0}|x|+|x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{|x|}{2}+|x^{\prime}|.

Hence
|x|\leq 2|x^{\prime}|\leq 3|x|
.

Definition 2.4
.

A ball
B=B(\xi,r)=B((x,y),r)
is said to be
remote
if

r\leq\epsilon_{0}|x|^{1-\alpha},

where
\epsilon_{0}
is given by Lemma
2.2
. For such a ball, every
\eta=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in B
satisfies

|x-x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{|x|}{2}\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad|x|\sim|x^{\prime}|.

Next, we recall the following result from
[
1
, Theorem 3.1, Proposition 3.5]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
r_{0},s_{0}\in(0,\infty]
and
\theta_{0}\in(0,1]
and that the parameter
q=q(r_{0},s_{0},\theta_{0})
defined by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r_{0}}=\frac{\theta_{0}}{q}+\frac{1-\theta_{0}}{s_{0}},

satisfies
p\leq q<\infty
. Suppose that

(2.8)

\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\left|\{|f|\geq\lambda\}\right|^{\frac{1}{r_{0}}}\leq C\|\nabla_{L}f\|_{p}^{\theta_{0}}\|f\|_{\infty}^{1-\theta_{0}}|\textrm{supp}(f)|^{\frac{1-\theta_{0}}{s_{0}}}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{q}\leq C\|\nabla_{L}f\|_{p},\quad q=\frac{r_{0}s_{0}\theta_{0}}{s_{0}-r_{0}(1-\theta_{0})}

for all
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})
.

Remark 2.6
.

In particular, if we take
p=1
and show that

(2.9)

\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda^{\frac{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}-1}}\left|\left\{|f|\geq\lambda\right\}\right|\leq C\|\nabla_{L}f\|_{1}\|f\|_{q}^{\frac{1}{\mathcal{Q}-1}},

then Lemma
2.5
yields
(\textrm{S}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{1})
and hence (
1.2
).

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Given a symmetric Markov generator
\mathcal{L}
with carré du champ
\Gamma
and iterate
\Gamma_{2}
, the inequality

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(f)\geq K\Gamma(f)+\frac{1}{N}\left(L(f)\right)^{2},\quad\forall f\in C^{\infty}

encodes both a ’Ricci curvature’ lower bound
K
and ’effective dimension’
N
. For conventional issues, we let
\mathcal{L}
be a
non-positive
self-adjoint diffusion operator. Formally, for smooth function
f,g
one defines bilinear form:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Gamma(f,g)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{L}(fg)-f\mathcal{L}(g)-g\mathcal{L}(f)\right).

Note that
\Gamma
is symmetric and
\Gamma(f)=\Gamma(f,f)
is the so-called
carre du champ
operator. Next, one defines the iterated bilinear form:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(f,g)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{L}\Gamma(f,g)-\Gamma(f,\mathcal{L}(g))-\Gamma(g,\mathcal{L}(f))\right),

and
\Gamma_{2}(f)=\Gamma_{2}(f,f)
is the second
carre du champ
operator.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n,m\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\beta\geq 0
. Let
\Gamma
(resp.
\Gamma_{2}
) be the carre du champ (resp. second carre du champ) of
L
. The following curvature dimension inequality holds

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(f)\geq-\frac{C(n,m,\alpha,\beta)}{|x|^{2(1-\alpha)}}\Gamma(f)+C^{\prime}(n,m,\alpha,\beta)\left(L(f)\right)^{2}

for all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Since
L
is non-negative, we set
\mathcal{L}=-L
and calculate (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) directly. Put
r=|x|
,
a=r^{2\alpha}
and
b=r^{2\beta}
. Then

\mathcal{L}=-a\Delta_{x}-b\Delta_{y}+\nabla a\cdot\nabla_{x}.

For simplicity, we use indices
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
\mu,\nu\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, one gets directly from the definition (
3.1
) that

(3.3)

\Gamma(f)=af_{i}^{2}+bf_{\mu}^{2}.

Next, by (
3.2
), a direct calculation gives

(3.4)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(f)={}

\displaystyle a^{2}f_{ij}^{2}+2abf_{i\mu}^{2}+b^{2}f_{\mu\nu}^{2}

\displaystyle+2aa_{i}f_{j}f_{ij}+aa_{j}f_{j}\Delta_{x}f

\displaystyle+2ab_{i}f_{\mu}f_{i\mu}+ab_{i}f_{i}\Delta_{y}f

\displaystyle+\Bigl(-\frac{a}{2}\Delta_{x}a+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla a|^{2}\Bigr)f_{i}^{2}-aa_{ij}f_{i}f_{j}

\displaystyle+\Bigl(-\frac{a}{2}\Delta_{x}b+\frac{1}{2}\nabla a\cdot\nabla b\Bigr)f_{\mu}^{2}.

Note that

\displaystyle a_{i}=2\alpha r^{2\alpha-2}x_{i},\quad|\nabla a|^{2}=4\alpha^{2}r^{4\alpha-2},\quad\Delta_{x}a=-2\alpha(n+2\alpha-2)r^{2\alpha-2},

a_{ij}=2\alpha r^{2\alpha-2}\delta_{ij}+4\alpha(\alpha-1)r^{2\alpha-4}x_{i}x_{j},

and

\displaystyle b_{i}=2\beta r^{2\beta-2}x_{i},\quad|\nabla b|^{2}=4\beta^{2}r^{4\beta-2},\quad\Delta_{x}b=-2\beta(n+2\beta-2)r^{2\beta-2}.

We estimate the lower-order terms in (
3.4
) by Young’s inequality.

First, using
|\Delta_{x}f|\leq\sqrt{n}|D_{x}^{2}f|
, where
|D_{x}^{2}f|^{2}=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}|\partial_{x_{i},x_{j}}f|^{2}
, and Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and then Young’s inequality, one obtains

(3.5)

2aa_{i}f_{j}f_{ij}+aa_{j}f_{j}\Delta_{x}f\geq-\frac{1}{4}a^{2}|D_{x}^{2}f|^{2}-C|\nabla a|^{2}|\nabla_{x}f|^{2}.

Also, since
\alpha<1
,

(3.6)

-aa_{ij}f_{i}f_{j}=-2\alpha r^{4\alpha-2}|\nabla_{x}f|^{2}+4\alpha(1-\alpha)r^{4\alpha-4}(x\cdot\nabla_{x}f)^{2}\geq-2\alpha r^{4\alpha-2}|\nabla_{x}f|^{2}.

Hence

(3.7)

\Bigl(-\frac{a}{2}\Delta_{x}a+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla a|^{2}\Bigr)f_{i}^{2}-aa_{ij}f_{i}f_{j}\geq-Cr^{4\alpha-2}|\nabla_{x}f|^{2}.

Next, it is clear that

(3.8)

2ab_{i}f_{\mu}f_{i\mu}\geq-abf_{i\mu}^{2}-\frac{a|\nabla b|^{2}}{b}|\nabla_{y}f|^{2},

and, using
|\Delta_{y}f|\leq\sqrt{m}|D_{y}^{2}f|
, where
|D_{y}^{2}f|^{2}=\sum_{1\leq\mu,\nu\leq m}|\partial_{y_{\mu},y_{\nu}}f|^{2}
,

(3.9)

ab_{i}f_{i}\Delta_{y}f\geq-\frac{1}{4}b^{2}|D_{y}^{2}f|^{2}-C\frac{a^{2}|\nabla b|^{2}}{b^{2}}|\nabla_{x}f|^{2}.

Finally,

(3.10)

\Bigl(-\frac{a}{2}\Delta_{x}b+\frac{1}{2}\nabla a\cdot\nabla b\Bigr)f_{\mu}^{2}\geq-Cr^{2\alpha+2\beta-2}|\nabla_{y}f|^{2}.

Insert (
3.5
)–(
3.10
) into (
3.4
).
We obtain

(3.11)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(f)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{4}a^{2}|D_{x}^{2}f|^{2}+ab|D_{xy}^{2}f|^{2}+\frac{3}{4}b^{2}|D_{y}^{2}f|^{2}-C\Bigl(r^{4\alpha-2}|\nabla_{x}f|^{2}+r^{2\alpha+2\beta-2}|\nabla_{y}f|^{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}a^{2}|D_{x}^{2}f|^{2}+ab|D_{xy}^{2}f|^{2}+\frac{3}{4}b^{2}|D_{y}^{2}f|^{2}-\frac{C}{|x|^{2-2\alpha}}\Gamma(f)

On the other hand, by trace inequality, with
C^{\prime}=12(n+m)
,

(3.12)

\displaystyle C^{\prime-1}(\mathcal{L}f)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime-1}3a^{2}(\Delta_{x}f)^{2}+C^{\prime-1}3b^{2}(\Delta_{y}f)^{2}+C^{\prime-1}3|\nabla a|^{2}|\nabla_{x}f|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime-1}3na^{2}|D_{x}^{2}f|^{2}+C^{\prime-1}3mb^{2}|D_{y}^{2}f|^{2}+C^{\prime-1}12\alpha^{2}r^{4\alpha-2}|\nabla_{x}f|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}a^{2}|D_{x}^{2}f|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}b^{2}|D_{y}^{2}f|^{2}+\frac{1}{|x|^{2-2\alpha}}\Gamma(f).

Combining (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), we conclude that

\Gamma_{2}(f)\geq-\frac{C}{|x|^{2-2\alpha}}\Gamma(f)+\frac{1}{C^{\prime}}(\mathcal{L}f)^{2}

for some
C=C(n,m,\alpha,\beta)>0
, as desired.

∎

Next, we establish a gradient estimate for the heat semigroup. The argument is inspired by
[
2
, Sections 3.2–3.3]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n,m\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\beta\geq 0
. The following gradient estimate holds

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{L}e^{-tL}(\xi,\eta)\right|\leq\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}+\frac{1}{|x|^{1-\alpha}}\right)\frac{C}{V(\xi,\sqrt{t})}e^{-\frac{d(\xi,\eta)^{2}}{ct}},

where
\xi=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Let
\xi=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and let
B=B(\xi,r)
be a remote ball with radius
r\leq\epsilon_{0}|x|^{1-\alpha}
. By Lemma
3.1
, one readily checks that for every
\xi^{\prime}\in B
, the curvature-dimension inequality
\mathrm{CD}(-c_{1}/r^{2},c_{2})
holds uniformly for some constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
. Therefore, by
[
13
, Theorem 1.4]
; see also
[
8
, Theorem 5.1]
, a local Li–Yau type estimate implies that for every
\xi^{\prime}\in B/2
, every
\gamma>1
, and every positive solution
u_{t}
of

\partial_{t}u_{t}=-Lu_{t},

one has

(3.13)

\frac{|\nabla_{L}u_{t}|^{2}}{u_{t}^{2}}-\gamma\frac{\partial_{t}u_{t}}{u_{t}}\leq C(\gamma,n,m)\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right).

Combining (
3.13
) with
[
12
, Theorem 2.6]
, namely

(3.14)

\left|\partial_{t}e^{-tL}(\xi,\eta)\right|\leq\frac{C}{t\,V(\xi,\sqrt{t})}e^{-\frac{d(\xi,\eta)^{2}}{ct}},

yields the desired gradient estimate.
∎

The next step is to establish the following Hardy type inequality.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
n,m\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\beta\geq 0
. Then, the following Hardy type inequality holds

(3.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}\frac{|u(\xi)|}{|x|^{1-\alpha}}d\xi\leq C_{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|\nabla_{L}u(\xi)|d\xi,

for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

We give a direct proof. Assume first that
n\geq 2
. Passing to polar coordinates in the
x
-variable, the left-hand side of (
3.15
) becomes

\displaystyle C(n)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|u((r,\theta);y)|}{r^{1-\alpha}}\,d\theta\,r^{n-1}\,dr\,dy.

Using the inequality

\displaystyle|u((r,\theta);y)|\leq\int_{r}^{\infty}|\partial_{s}u((s,\theta);y)|\,ds,

we deduce that the left-hand side of (
3.15
) is bounded by

\displaystyle C(n)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{r}^{\infty}|\partial_{s}u((s,\theta);y)|\,ds\,r^{n-2+\alpha}\,dr\,d\theta\,dy.

Since
r\leq s
in the region of integration, we have
r^{\alpha}\leq s^{\alpha}
. Moreover,

|s^{\alpha}\partial_{s}u|\leq|\nabla_{L}u|.

Therefore, the above expression is bounded by

\displaystyle C(n)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{s}|\nabla_{L}u((s,\theta);y)|\,r^{n-2}\,dr\,ds\,d\theta\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\nabla_{L}u((s,\theta);y)|\,s^{n-1}\,ds\,d\theta\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}|\nabla_{L}u(\xi)|\,d\xi.

The case
n=1
is similar, and we omit the details.
∎

The next lemma is a variant of the argument in
[
4
, Section 2.1]
. We include a proof for completeness.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
R>0
. There exists a sequence of balls
\{B_{n}^{j}=B(x_{j},r_{j})\}_{j\geq 0}
such that

(1)

\mathbb{R}^{n}=\cup_{j\geq 0}B_{n}^{j}
, where
B_{n}^{0}=B_{n}(0,R)
and
B_{j}
(
j\geq 1
) is
x
-remote in the sense
r_{j}\leq|x_{j}|/2
,

(2)

for all
j\geq 1
,
2^{-10}|x_{j}|\leq r_{j}\leq 2^{-9}|x_{j}|
,

(3)

\sum_{j}\mathbf{1}_{B_{j}}(x)\leq C_{L}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
C_{L}>0
does not depend on
R
.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Set
B_{n}^{0}=B_{n}(0,R)
and
A_{N}:=B_{n}(0,R2^{N})\setminus B_{n}(0,R2^{N-1})
for each
N\geq 1
. Apparently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}=B_{n}^{0}\cup\bigcup_{N\geq 1}A_{N},\quad A_{N}\subset\bigcup_{x\in A_{N}}B_{n}\big(x,R2^{N-13}\big).

It follows by Vitali’s covering lemma, that one can find an index set
I_{N}
and a collection of balls
\{B_{n}\big(x_{N,j},R2^{N-13}\big)\}_{j\in I_{N}}
with
x_{N,j}\in A_{N}
, pairwise disjoint and
A_{N}\subset\bigcup_{j\in I_{N}}B_{n}\big(x_{N,j},R2^{N-10}\big)
. One deduces the finiteness of
\#I_{N}
,

\displaystyle\#I_{N}|B_{n}(0,R2^{N+2})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in I_{N}}|B_{n}\big(x_{N,j},R2^{N+3}\big)|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{16n}\sum_{j\in I_{N}}|B_{n}(x_{N,j},R2^{N-13})|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{16n}|B_{n}\big(0,R2^{N+2}\big)|,

Note that by setting
B_{n}^{j}=B_{n}(x_{j},r_{j})
with
x_{j}=x_{N,j}
,
r_{j}=R2^{N-10}
and relabeling, we construct a sequence of balls
\{B_{n}^{j}\}_{j}
such that
\cup_{j\geq 0}B_{n}^{j}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, we have for
j\neq 0
(since
x_{j}\in A_{N}
for some
N
),

\displaystyle 2^{-10}|x_{j}|\leq r_{j}\leq 2^{-9}|x_{j}|,

and for all
x\in B_{n}^{j}
,

\displaystyle 2^{8}r_{j}\leq|x|\leq 2^{11}r_{j}.

Clearly, this construction guarantees that
B_{n}^{0}=B_{n}(0,R)
is the only ball in the collection which contains
0
. In addition, for
x\neq 0
, one sets
J_{x}=\{j;x\in B_{n}^{j}\}
. Observe that if
x\in A_{N}
for some
N\geq 1
(the case
x\in B_{n}^{0}
is similar), and
x\in B_{n}^{j}
for some
j
, then
j
is either in
I_{N}
,
I_{N-1}
or
I_{N+1}
. Therefore, if
x\in A_{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\#J_{x}

\displaystyle\big|B_{n}\big(x,2^{-1}|x|\big)\big|\leq\sum_{j\in J_{x}}\big|B_{n}\big(x_{j},2^{-1}|x|+2^{-8}|x|\big)\big|\leq 2^{20n}\sum_{j\in J_{x}}\big|B_{n}\big(x_{j},2^{-20}|x|\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{20n}\sum_{j\in J_{x}}\big|B_{n}\big(x_{j},2^{-3}r_{j}\big)\big|=2^{20n}\sum_{l\in\{-1,0,1\}}\sum_{j\in J_{x}\cap I_{N+l}}\big|B_{n}\big(x_{j},2^{-3}r_{j}\big)\big|

\displaystyle=2^{20n}\sum_{l\in\{-1,0,1\}}\left|\cup_{j\in J_{x}\cap I_{N+l}}B_{n}\big(x_{j},2^{-3}r_{j}\big)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 3\cdot 2^{20n}\big|B_{n}\big(x,2^{-1}|x|\big)\big|,

since for all
j\in J_{x}\cap I_{N+l}
, one has

B_{n}\big(x_{j},2^{-3}r_{j}\big)\subset B_{n}\big(x,9\cdot 2^{-3}r_{j}\big)\subset B_{n}\big(x,9\cdot 2^{-11}|x|\big)\subset B_{n}\big(x,2^{-1}|x|\big).

The case
x\in B_{n}^{0}
is similar. Hence, we conclude that there exists a constant
C_{L}>0
which does not depend on
R,N
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\mathbf{1}_{B_{j}}(x)\leq C_{L},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

completing the proof.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
1.1

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\lambda>0
,
q=\frac{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Q}-1}
and
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})
be fixed. Set
R=\left(\frac{\|f\|_{q}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{q}{\mathcal{Q}(1-\alpha)}}
. By Lemma
3.4
with parameter
R
, there exists a sequence of balls
\{B_{n}^{j}=B_{n}(x_{j},r_{j})\}_{j\geq 0}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}=B_{n}^{0}\cup\left(\cup_{j\geq 1}B_{n}^{j}\right).

Moreover, each
B_{n}^{j}
(
j\neq 0
) is
x
-remote and
B_{n}^{0}=B_{n}(0,R)
. Let
\{\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}\}
be a smooth partition of unity subordinated to
B_{n}^{j}
such that
0\leq\mathcal{X}_{j}\leq 1
and

(1)

\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathcal{X}_{j}+\mathcal{X}_{0}=1
,

(2)

\|\mathcal{X}_{j}\|_{\infty}+r_{j}\|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{X}_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq C
for all
j\geq 1
,

(3)

\textrm{supp}(\mathcal{X}_{j})\subset B_{n}^{j}
for all
j\geq 0
.

Next, we decompose

f(x,y)=\sum_{j\geq 1}f(x,y)\mathcal{X}_{j}(x)+f(x,y)\mathcal{X}_{0}(x):=\sum_{j\geq 1}f_{j}+f_{0}.

Since the covering has finite overlap property (and the finite overlap constant does not depend on
R
), one infers

(3.16)

\displaystyle\left|\{\mathbb{R}^{n+m};|f|\geq\lambda\}\right|\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathcal{F}_{j},

where

\mathcal{F}_{j}=\left|\{B_{n}^{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};|f_{j}|\geq C_{L}^{-1}\lambda\}\right|,\quad\forall j\geq 0.

Now, for each
j\geq 1
, we further split (for some
t>0
)

f_{j}=\left(f_{j}-e^{-tL}f_{j}\right)+e^{-tL}f_{j}.

Note that by
[
11
, Prop 3.1]
, we have
\|e^{-tL}\|_{1\to\infty}\lesssim t^{-\mathcal{Q}/2}
. It follows by interpolation that
\|e^{-tL}f_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq Ct^{-\frac{\mathcal{Q}}{2q}}\|f\|_{q}
. Therefore, by setting
t=\left(2C_{L}C\|f\|_{q}/\lambda\right)^{2q/\mathcal{Q}}
, we deduce that

\left|\{|e^{-tL}f_{j}|\geq(2C_{L})^{-1}\lambda\}\right|=0.

Thus we obtain for
j\geq 1

(3.17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{j}\leq\left|\left\{B_{n}^{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};|f_{j}-e^{-tL}f_{j}|\geq(2C_{L})^{-1}\lambda\right\}\right|.

To estimate (
3.17
), we write
f_{j}-e^{tL}f_{j}=\int_{0}^{t}Le^{-sL}f_{j}ds
. With Minkowski’s integral inequality,
\mathcal{F}_{j}
is bounded by

(3.18)

\displaystyle 2C_{L}\lambda^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\|Le^{-sL}f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(B_{n}^{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}ds.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}
supported in
B_{n}^{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
\|g\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. By integration by parts and Hölder’s inequality, it is clear that

(3.19)

\displaystyle\iint Le^{-sL}f_{j}(x,y)g(x,y)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{B_{n}^{j}}\nabla_{L}f_{j}(x,y)\cdot\nabla_{L}e^{-sL}g(x,y)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla_{L}f_{j}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})}\left\|\nabla_{L}e^{-tL}g\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{n}^{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}.

Claim.

(3.20)

\displaystyle\sup_{j\geq 1}\sup_{s>0}\|\sqrt{s}\nabla_{L}e^{-sL}g\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{n}^{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq C\|g\|_{\infty}

for all
g\in L^{\infty}
supported in
B_{n}^{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
C
depends only on
n,m,\alpha,\beta,C_{d},C_{D}
.

Proof of Claim..

Let
\xi=(x,y)\in B_{n}^{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\eta=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in B_{n}^{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
s>0
. Note that by Lemma
3.4
, one has
2^{8}r_{j}\leq|x|\leq 2^{11}r_{j}
. Then Lemma
3.2
implies that

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{L}e^{-sL}g(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\int_{B_{n}^{j}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}+\frac{1}{r_{j}^{1-\alpha}}\right)\frac{C}{V(\xi,\sqrt{s})}e^{-\frac{d(\xi,\eta)^{2}}{cs}}|g(\eta)|d\eta.

Case 1.
If
\sqrt{s}\leq 2^{11(1-\alpha)}r_{j}^{1-\alpha}
, then a standard argument (c.f.
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
) yields that

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{L}e^{-sL}g(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq s^{-1/2}\|g\|_{\infty}\frac{C}{V(\xi,\sqrt{s})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}e^{-\frac{d(\xi,\eta)^{2}}{cs}}d\eta\leq Cs^{-1/2}\|g\|_{\infty},

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
C_{D}
.

Case 2.
If
\sqrt{s}\geq 2^{11(1-\alpha)}r_{j}^{1-\alpha}\geq|x|^{1-\alpha}
, one deduces

(3.21)

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{L}e^{-sL}g(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq r_{j}^{\alpha-1}\|g\|_{\infty}\frac{C}{V(\xi,\sqrt{s})}\int_{B_{n}^{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-\frac{d(\xi,\eta)^{2}}{cs}}dy^{\prime}dx^{\prime}.

By Lemma
2.1
, one has volume estimate
V(\xi,\sqrt{s})=V((x,y),\sqrt{s})\sim s^{\mathcal{Q}/2}
. Also, the distance estimate gives

\displaystyle d((x,y);(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))^{2}\sim\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}}{\left(|x|+|x^{\prime}|\right)^{2\alpha}}+\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}}{(|x|+|x^{\prime}|)^{2\beta}+|y-y^{\prime}|^{\frac{2\beta}{\beta+1-\alpha}}}.

Transferring to polar coordinates system (set
\sigma=(|x|+|x^{\prime}|)^{2\beta}
), the inner integral of (
3.21
) is bounded by (up to a constant depending on
C_{d}
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\textrm{exp}\left(\frac{-cr^{2}s^{-1}}{\sigma+r^{2\beta/(\beta+1-\alpha)}}\right)r^{m-1}dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\sigma^{\frac{\beta+1-\alpha}{2\beta}}}\textrm{exp}\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2s\sigma}\right)r^{m-1}dr+\int_{\sigma^{\frac{\beta+1-\alpha}{2\beta}}}^{\infty}\textrm{exp}\left(-\frac{r^{\frac{2-2\alpha}{\beta+1-\alpha}}}{2s}\right)r^{m-1}dr

\displaystyle\leq C\max\left(s^{\frac{m}{2}}\sigma^{\frac{m}{2}},s^{\frac{m(\beta+1-\alpha)}{2(1-\alpha)}}\right)\leq Cs^{\frac{m(\beta+1-\alpha)}{2(1-\alpha)}},

where the last inequality follows by observing that
\sigma\sim|x|^{2\beta}\sim r_{j}^{2\beta}\leq Cs^{\frac{\beta}{1-\alpha}}
.

Combining this with (
3.21
), one infers

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{L}e^{-sL}g(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|g\|_{\infty}r_{j}^{\alpha-1}s^{-\frac{\mathcal{Q}}{2}}|B_{n}^{j}|s^{\frac{m(\beta+1-\alpha)}{2(1-\alpha)}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|g\|_{\infty}r_{j}^{n+\alpha-1}s^{-\frac{n}{2(1-\alpha)}}

\displaystyle\leq Cs^{\frac{n-(1-\alpha)}{2(1-\alpha)}-\frac{n}{2(1-\alpha)}}\|g\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=Cs^{-1/2}\|g\|_{\infty}

as desired. This proves the
Claim.

∎

By the
Claim
(
3.20
), (
3.18
) and (
3.17
), one obtains for
j\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{j}\leq CC_{L}\lambda^{-1}\|\nabla_{L}f_{j}\|_{1}t^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=CC_{L}\lambda^{-1}\|\nabla_{L}f_{j}\|_{1}\left(\frac{\|f\|_{q}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{q}{\mathcal{Q}}}

\displaystyle=CC_{L}\lambda^{-1-q/\mathcal{Q}}\|\nabla_{L}f_{j}\|_{1}\|f\|_{q}^{q/\mathcal{Q}}.

Note that for
x\in B_{n}^{j}

(3.22)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{L}f_{j}|

\displaystyle=\left|\left(|x|^{\alpha}\mathcal{X}_{j}\nabla_{x}f+|x|^{\alpha}f\nabla\mathcal{X}_{j},|x|^{\beta}\mathcal{X}_{j}\nabla_{y}f\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|\nabla_{L}f|+|x|^{\alpha}\frac{|f|}{r_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|\nabla_{L}f|+\frac{|f|}{|x|^{1-\alpha}}\right).

It then follows by finite overlap property and Lemma

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathcal{F}_{j}

\displaystyle\leq CC_{L}(1+C_{H})\lambda^{-1-q/\mathcal{Q}}\|f\|_{q}^{q/\mathcal{Q}}\|\nabla_{L}f\|_{1}.

To this end, one also needs to treat
\mathcal{F}_{0}
. By Lemma
3.3

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{0}

\displaystyle=\left|\{B_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};|f_{0}|\geq C_{L}^{-1}\lambda\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{L}\lambda^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{1}\left(B_{n}(0,R)\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)}\leq C_{L}\lambda^{-1}R^{1-\alpha}\left\|\frac{f}{|x|^{1-\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})}

Consequently, one concludes by (

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda^{1+q/\mathcal{Q}}\left|\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m};|f(x,y)|\geq\lambda\right\}\right|\leq C\|\nabla_{L}f\|_{1}\|f\|_{q}^{q/\mathcal{Q}},

The proof of Theorem
1.1

∎
