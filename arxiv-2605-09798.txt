The density of graphs with no ℓ \ell -path connecting equal-degree vertices: a short proof
Yamaan Attwa, Matías Azócar Carvajal, Simona Boyadzhiyska, Théo Pierron, Anusch Taraz

Abstract
. Addressing a question posed by Chen and Ma from an asymptotic point of view, we present a short proof for the edge density needed to guarantee that two vertices of the same degree are connected by a path of a fixed length. In particular, we show that for any sufficiently large graph, a density of at least 1 / 2 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) 1/2+o(1) enforces the existence of two such vertices. This bound is tight for paths of odd length.



1.
Introduction

It is a simple exercise to show that every graph contains a pair of vertices of the same degree. Being so, it is only natural to ask: under what conditions can we expect such vertices to satisfy additional properties?
Erdős and Hajnal
[
2
]

formulated the following question of this flavor:

Problem 1.1
.

Is it true that every
(2n+1)
-vertex graph with
n^{2}+n+1
edges contains two vertices of the same degree which are joined by a path of length three?

As usual, the length of a path is the number of edges it contains.

The bound of
n^{2}+n+1
is tight, as demonstrated by the complete bipartite graph
K_{n,n+1}
.
In a recent paper, Chen and Ma
[
1
]
resolved this problem, in a very strong sense, for large
n
. In fact, they also handled the case where the graph has an even number of vertices, characterizing the unique extremal construction in both situations.

Theorem 1.2
.

The following holds.

(
)

Let
n\geq 600
. The unique
(2n+1)
-vertex graph with at least
n^{2}+n
edges that does not contain two vertices of the same degree joined by a path of length three is the complete bipartite graph
K_{n,n+1}
.

(
)

There exists an integer
n_{0}>0
such that the following holds for all
n\geq n_{0}
. The unique
2n
-vertex graph with at least
n^{2}-1
edges that does not contain two vertices of the same degree joined by a path of length three is the complete bipartite graph
K_{n-1,n+1}
.

This result was later improved by Liu and Zeng
[
3
]
, who showed that part
( )
‣
1.2
holds for all
n\geq 2
and part
( )
‣
1.2
is true for all
n\geq 3
.

In their paper, Chen and Ma
[
1
]
defined the following extremal function, generalizing the Erdős–Hajnal problem.

Definition 1
.

For any positive integers
\ell
and
n
, let
p_{\ell}(n)
denote the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex graph
G
that contains no two vertices of equal degree connected by a path of length
\ell
.

The results in
[
1
,
3
]
thus show that
p_{3}(2n+1)=n^{2}+n
for all
n\geq 2
and
p_{3}(2n)=n^{2}-1
for all
n\geq 3
. Chen and Ma further showed that

\displaystyle p_{1}(n)=\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\frac{n\sqrt{2n}}{3}+O(n)\quad\text{and}\quad p_{2}(2n)=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}.

Additionally, they conjectured that the behavior of
p_{\ell}(2n+1)
should be the same for any odd
\ell\geq 3
.

Conjecture 1.3
.

For any odd integer
\ell\geq 3
and sufficiently large
n
, it holds that
{p_{\ell}(2n+1)=n^{2}+n}
.

In a very recent paper, Liu and Zeng
[
4
]
tackled the case
\ell=5
, once again in a strong sense, proving an analog of both parts of
1.2
(for
n\geq 11
in part
( )
‣
1.2
and
n\geq 13
in
( )
‣
1.2
). The conjecture was subsequently resolved in full by Zhao, Wang, and Lu
[
5
]
, who once again proved that
K_{n,n+1}
is the unique extremal example when the number of vertices is odd. The case of an even number of vertices was not addressed directly in
[
5
]
, even though the authors comment that their methods likely extend to this setting.

For even values of
\ell
, our knowledge is much more limited. The
half graph
on
2n
vertices is defined as the bipartite graph with vertex classes
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
and
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
, where
u_{i}v_{j}
is an edge if and only if
i\leq j
. It is not difficult to check that in the half graph no vertices of the same degree are connected by an even-length path, implying that

p_{\ell}(2n)\geq\frac{n(n+1)}{2}
for all even
\ell\geq 2
. As far as we are aware, nothing more is known about this function when
\ell
is even, though in
[
4
]
Liu and Zeng refer to upcoming work showing that
p_{4}(2n)\leq(1-\varepsilon+o(1))n^{2}
for some constant
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
and
sufficiently large
n
.

In all previous work on the problem, the arguments have been rather technical and involved.
The goal of this note is to provide a short proof of an asymptotic version of
1.3
. Our main result addresses the remaining cases by showing that the number of edges in a graph not containing a pair of vertices connected by a path of some fixed length
\ell\geq 6
, odd or even, is always at most
\frac{1}{2}\binom{n}{2}
asymptotically. Our analysis is somewhat reminiscent of that provided in
[
5
]
but it is much simpler.

Theorem 1.4
.

For every
\ell\geq 6
and every sufficiently large
n
, we have
p_{\ell}(n)\leq\left(\frac{1}{4}+o(1)\right)n^{2}
.

Clearly, this bound is tight when
\ell
is odd. We tend to believe that it should not be tight for even
\ell
and in that case the maximum number of edges should be closer to
\frac{1}{4}\binom{n}{2}
. We pose this as a question.

Question 1.5
.

Is it true that
p_{\ell}(n)=(\frac{1}{8}+o(1))n^{2}
for all even
\ell
?

Notation

Our notation is mostly standard. An
xy
-path is a path connecting vertices
x
and
y
. Let
G
be a graph and
A,B\subseteq V(G)
be any subsets (not necessarily disjoint). We write
G[A]
for the subgraph of
G
induced by
A
, that is, the subgraph obtained by deleting the vertices not in
A
. The neighborhood of
A
, denoted
N(A)
, refers to the set
\{v\in V(G)\,:\,va\in E(G)\text{ for some }a\in A\}
. We write
E(A,B)
for the set
\{ab\in E(G)\,:\,a\in A,b\in B\}
.

2.
The proof

This section is devoted to the proof of
1.4
. Throughout this section, we will always assume that
\ell\geq 2
is a fixed integer,
n
is sufficiently large with respect to
\ell
, and
G
is an
n
-vertex graph. The proof of
1.4
relies on the following technical lemma, which allows us to find long paths when
G
has too many vertices of large enough degree. We consider several cases depending on whether we look for paths of even or odd length.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
D\geq\frac{n}{2}+k+1
be an integer. Suppose
G
contains a set
B=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{t}\}
of
2\leq t\leq k
vertices of degree at least
D
, and let
x,y\notin B
be distinct vertices of degree at least
n-D+t+4
. Then:

(
)

There exists an
xy
-path of length
2t+2
.

(
)

If
G[B]
contains an edge, then there exists an
xy
-path of length
2t+1
.

(
)

If
E(N(b_{i}),N(b_{j}))\neq\emptyset
for some
i\neq j
, then there exists an
xy
-path of length
2t+3
.

Proof.

(
)

Set
b_{0}=x
and
b_{t+1}=y
. We iteratively find a family of pairwise distinct vertices
a_{1},\ldots,a_{t+1}\notin B\cup\{x,y\}
such that
a_{i}
is a common neighbor of
b_{i-1}
and
b_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq t
. Once this is done,
xa_{1}b_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{t}b_{t}a_{t+1}y
will form the desired
xy
-path of length
2t+2
.

We first choose
a_{1}
and
a_{t+1}
. Observe that
\deg(b_{i})+\deg(b_{i+1})\geq n+t+4
for
i\in\{0,t\}
. Hence
|N(b_{i})\cap N(b_{i+1})|\geq t+4
, and thus this set contains two distinct vertices not in
B\cup\{x,y\}
.

Now, for
1<i\leq t-1
, if
a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}
are already selected, we similarly find that
|N(b_{i})\cap N(b_{i+1})|\geq 2D-n\geq 2t+2
. So we can choose the next vertex
{a_{i+1}\in N(b_{i})\cap N(b_{i+1})}
outside of
B\cup\{x,y,a_{1},\ldots,a_{i},a_{t+1}\}
; indeed, since
{b_{i}\notin N(b_{i})}
and
b_{i+1}\notin N(b_{i+1})
, the latter set contains at most
2t
vertices of
N(b_{i})\cap N(b_{i+1})
.

(
)

Assume without loss of generality that
b_{1}b_{2}
is an edge. We choose
a_{1},\ldots,a_{t+1}
as in the previous case, but build a path using the edge
b_{1}b_{2}
instead of the vertex
a_{2}
.

(
)

Assume without loss of generality that there is an edge
a_{2}a_{2}^{\prime}
with
a_{2}\in N(b_{1})
and
a_{2}^{\prime}\in N(b_{2})
. In this case, we choose
a_{1}\in(N(b_{1})\cap N(x))\setminus(B\cup\{x,y,a_{2},a^{\prime}_{2}\})
, since this set contains at least
t+4-(t+2)\geq 2
vertices. Similarly, we choose
a_{t+1}\in(N(b_{t})\cap N(y))\setminus(B\cup\{x,y,a_{2},a^{\prime}_{2}\})
. Since, in the previous items, we had at least two choices for the vertices
a_{3},\dots,a_{t}
, we can also choose them distinct from
a^{\prime}_{2}
. We thus obtain the path
xa_{1}b_{1}a_{2}a_{2}^{\prime}b_{2}a_{3}\dots a_{t}b_{t}a_{t+1}y
of length
2t+3
. ∎

Using this lemma, we can complete the proof of
1.4
. The proof is split into two cases, depending on the parity of
t
. We start with the less involved even case.

2.1.
Even-length paths

Assume that
G
does not contain two vertices of the same degree connected by a path of length
\ell=2k
. Our goal is to show that
e(G)=n^{2}/4+O(n)
. Label the vertices of
G
as
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
in non-increasing degree order, and let
\Delta_{k}=\deg(v_{k-1})
. We consider two cases depending on whether
\Delta_{k}
is large or not.

Case 1:
\Delta_{k}<\frac{n}{2}+k+1
.
Summing up the degrees of the vertices, and upper-bounding
\deg(v_{i})
by
n
if
i<k-1
and by
\Delta_{k}
otherwise, we obtain

\displaystyle 2e(G)\leq(k-2)n+(n-k+2)\left(\frac{n}{2}+k\right)\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}+\frac{3}{2}kn,

as claimed.

Case 2:
\Delta_{k}\geq\frac{n}{2}+k+1
.
Our goal is to apply Lemma
2.1

( )
‣
2.1
, with
t=k-1
,
B=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k-1}\}
, and
D=\Delta_{k}
. If there is at most one vertex
x\notin B
of degree at least
n-\Delta_{k}+(k-1)+4=n-\Delta_{k}+k+3
, we are done by a similar argument as above. So we may assume there exist at least two of them, say
x
and
y
. By
2.1
, there exists an
xy
-path of length
2(k-1)+2=2k
, so we conclude that
x
and
y
must have distinct degrees.

Again, summing up the degrees of the vertices, we obtain that
2e(G)
is bounded above by

(1)

\displaystyle\underbrace{(k-1)n}_{v_{i}\text{ for }i<k}+\underbrace{\sum_{i=n-\Delta_{k}+k+3}^{\Delta_{k}}i}_{\text{at most one vertex of each degree }}+\underbrace{\left(n-\left(\Delta_{k}-(n-\Delta_{k}+k+3)\right)+1\right)(n-\Delta_{k}+k+2)}_{\text{remaining vertices}}

\displaystyle=(k-1)n+\binom{\Delta_{k}+1}{2}-\binom{n-\Delta_{k}+k+3}{2}+(2n-2\Delta_{k}+k+4)\left(n-\Delta_{k}+k+2\right)

\displaystyle\leq(k-1)n+\binom{\Delta_{k}+1}{2}-\binom{n-\Delta_{k}+k+3}{2}+(n-k+2)\left(n-\Delta_{k}+k+2\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta_{k}^{2}}{2}-\frac{(n-\Delta_{k})^{2}}{2}+n(n-\Delta_{k})+O(n)=\frac{n^{2}}{2}+O(n),

which concludes the even case.

2.2.
Odd-length paths

Assume now that
G
does not contain two vertices of the same degree connected by a path of length
\ell=2k+1
with
k\geq 3
. We again label the vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
by non-increasing degree and set
\Delta_{k}=\deg(v_{k})
.

Let
D=\frac{n}{2}+k+1
and
B=\{v\in V(G)\,:\,\deg(v)\geq D\}
. Note that
2e(G)\leq n|B|+D(n-|B|)
. If
|B|=o(n)
, then
e(G)\leq\frac{n^{2}}{4}+o(n^{2})
and we are done. So we may assume that
|B|=\omega(n)
and in particular that
\Delta_{k}\geq D
. Additionally, write
R=V(G)\setminus(B\cup N(B))
.

Case 1:
G[B]
contains an edge
uw
or
E(N(u),N(w))\neq\emptyset
for some distinct
u,w\in B
.

Suppose first that
G[B]
contains the edge
uw
. Let
x,y
be two vertices of degree at least
n-\Delta_{k}+k+4
. Select
k-2
vertices
b_{1},\dots,b_{k-2}\in B\setminus\{u,w,x,y\}
, and write
b_{k-1}=u
,
b_{k}=w
. By
2.1

( )
‣
2.1
,
G
must contain an
xy
-path of length
2k+1
, and hence
x
and
y
have distinct degrees. In particular, no two vertices of
B
(except possibly
u,w
) share the same degree.
Then proceeding similarly to (
1
), where we allow for the degrees of
u
and
w
to be as large as
n
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2e(G)

\displaystyle\leq(k+2)n+\sum_{i=n-\Delta_{k}+k+4}^{\Delta_{k}}i+\left(n-\left(\Delta_{k}-(n-\Delta_{k}+k+4)\right)+1\right)\cdot(n-\Delta_{k}+k+3)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}+O(n),

using that
n\geq\Delta_{k}\geq D\geq n/2
.

The latter case, where
E(N(u),N(w))\neq\emptyset
for some distinct
u,w\in B
, is similar, except we now take
b_{1},\dots,b_{k-3}\in B\setminus\{u,w,x,y\}
, and write
b_{k-2}=u
,
b_{k-1}=w
and apply
2.1

( )
‣
2.1
.

Case 2:
B
is an independent set and
E(N(u),N(w))=\emptyset
for any distinct
u,w\in B
.

In this case,
B\cap N(B)=\emptyset
. Partition
N(B)
into sets
X\cup\left(\bigcup_{v\in B}Y_{v}\right)
, where
X=\{w\in V(G)\,:\,|N(w)\cap B|\geq 2\}
and
Y_{v}=\{w\in V(G)\,:\,N(w)\cap B=\{v\}\}
for all
v\in B
. Note that the only edges within
N(B)
are fully contained in the sets
Y_{v}
. We now claim that
X
is relatively large.

Claim 2.2
.

|X|\geq\frac{|B|-2}{|B|-1}\cdot\frac{n}{2}
.

Proof.

We proceed by double counting the edges between
B
and
X
. On the one hand, each vertex in
X
is adjacent to at most
|B|
vertices in
B
, so
|E(B,X)|\leq|X|\cdot|B|
. On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle|E(B,X)|

\displaystyle=|E(B,N(B))|-\sum_{v\in B}|E(B,Y_{v})|\geq D|B|-\sum_{v\in B}|Y_{v}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{2}|B|-(n-|B|-|X|-|R|)\geq(|B|-2)\frac{n}{2}+|X|,

where in the first inequality we used that every vertex in
Y_{v}
has at most one edge to
B
.
Combining these two bounds proves the claim.
∎

We now conclude by estimating the number of edges of
G
. For this, recall that
B
is an independent set, the only edges within
N(B)
can occur inside the individual sets
Y_{v}
, and every vertex in
R
has degree at most
D
by definition. Hence

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle\leq|B|\cdot|X|+\sum_{v\in B}\left(|Y_{v}|+{|Y_{v}|\choose 2}\right)+D|R|

\displaystyle\leq|B|\cdot|X|+\binom{\sum_{v}|Y_{v}|}{2}+\frac{n}{2}\left(n-|B|-|N(B)|\right)+O(n)

(2)

\displaystyle\leq|B|\cdot|X|+\binom{|N(B)|-|X|}{2}+\frac{n}{2}\left(n-|B|-|N(B)|\right)+O(n),

where for the second inequality we used that
\sum_{v\in B}{|Y_{v}|\choose 2}\leq\binom{\sum_{v}|Y_{v}|}{2}
, and that
R=V(G)\setminus(B\cup N(B))
and
B\cap N(B)=\emptyset
.
Note that, with respect to
|X|
, the expression in (
2
) is an upward-opening parabola, so its value is maximized at some extreme point for
|X|
. By Claim
2.2
and the fact that
X\subseteq N(B)
, we know that
\frac{(|B|-2)}{(|B|-1)}\cdot\frac{n}{2}\leq|X|\leq|N(B)|
. We consider these two cases separately. Observe that
\frac{n}{2}\leq D\leq|N(B)|\leq n-|B|
, so in particular
|B|\leq n-D<\frac{n}{2}
.

Case 1:
|X|=|N(B)|
. Then (
2
) becomes

\displaystyle|B|\cdot|N(B)|+\frac{n}{2}\left(n-|B|-|N(B)|\right)+O(n)

\displaystyle=|N(B)|\left(|B|-\frac{n}{2}\right)+\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\frac{n}{2}|B|+O(n)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{2}\left(|B|-\frac{n}{2}\right)+\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\frac{n}{2}|B|+O(n)=\frac{n^{2}}{4}+O(n),

as required.

Case 2:
|X|=\frac{(|B|-2)}{(|B|-1)}\cdot\frac{n}{2}
. Note that in this case (
2
) is also an upward-opening parabola with respect to
|N(B)|
. Hence, it is maximized when either
|N(B)|=D
or
|N(B)|=n-|B|\leq n
. In the latter case, (
2
) is upper bounded by

\displaystyle|B|\cdot|X|+\binom{n-|B|-|X|}{2}+O(n)\leq|B|\cdot|X|+\frac{\left(n-|B|-|X|\right)^{2}}{2}+O(n)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|B|^{2}}{(|B|-1)^{2}}\cdot\frac{n^{2}}{8}+\frac{|B|^{2}}{2}+O(n)\leq\left(1+o(1)\right)\frac{n^{2}}{4},

since
|B|=\omega(n)
and
|B|\leq\frac{n}{2}
.

In the case
|N(B)|=D
, we bound (
2
) by

\displaystyle|B|\cdot|X|+\binom{n/2+k+1-|X|}{2}+\frac{n}{2}\left(n-|B|-|N(B)|\right)+O(n)

\displaystyle\leq|B|\cdot\frac{n}{2}+\frac{\left(\frac{n}{2(|B|-1)}\right)^{2}}{2}+\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{n}{2}-|B|\right)+O(n)=\left(1+o(1)\right)\frac{n^{2}}{4},

where we used that
|X|\leq\frac{n}{2}
and
|B|=\omega(n)
. This concludes the proof for the odd-length case.
