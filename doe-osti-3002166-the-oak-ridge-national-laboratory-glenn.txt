 ABSTRACT

The Glenn T. Seaborg Institute (GTSI) at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) serves as a dedicated
hub for advancing actinide R&D, aimed at strengthening the United States’ capabilities in actinide
science. With a focus on medical, industrial, and national security applications, the ORNL GTSI seeks to
foster the next generation of scientists and engineers. Collaborating closely with corresponding Seaborg
Institutes at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL), Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory
(LBNL), Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL), and Idaho National Laboratory, the ORNL GTSI
leverages its unique strengths. These include unparalleled expertise in isotope production, handling of
rare isotopes, and leadership in neutron and computational sciences.

Notably, the ORNL GTSI is positioned as the nation’s cornerstone for actinide R&D, capitalizing on the
exceptional capabilities of the High Flux Isotope Reactor—the premier reactor for isotope production—
and the Hazard Category 2 Radiochemical Engineering Development Center, which supports advanced
production, processing, and applications research. Furthermore, the GTSI is committed to serving as a
national center of excellence for training across all levels of actinide science education.

This report provides an overview of the activities, milestones, and accomplishments of the ORNL GTSI
over the past year.

 1. ACCOMPLISHMENTS

1.1 POSTDOCTORAL CALLS IN FY 2025

In FY 2025, the ORNL GTSI initiated five postdoctoral recruitment calls spanning three directorates at
the laboratory. Two of these calls were specifically aimed at rebuilding expertise in UF6 chemistry at
ORNL. Another two were focused on supporting the 238Pu Supply Program, and the final call targeted
research related to uranium fuels.

The pool of eligible applicants was highly competitive. From these five calls, the GTSI extended offers to
three postdoctoral candidates, two of whom accepted their positions (Carmen Chamberlain: Uranium
Chemistry, Applications, and Simulations; Kaitlyn Engle: Radiochemical Process Development). The
remaining two calls have since been reposted, and the GTSI is actively seeking qualified candidates to fill
these roles.

1.1 SEABORG SEMINAR SERIES

This summer, we launched the Summer Seaborg Seminar Series to engage students and staff members
from across the laboratory and spark interest in the exciting and innovative actinide science we conduct.
Additionally, we had the pleasure of inviting an external speaker, Prof. Rebecca Abergel from LBNL, to
contribute to the series.

The primary goal of this initiative was to foster a strong and collaborative community of actinide
scientists. In total, the series featured eight speakers (see Table 1). The seminars were met with
enthusiastic participation, drawing an average of 100 attendees per session-typically comprising about 35
in-person attendees and 65 online participants.

To ensure broader accessibility and lasting impact, each talk was recorded and will be made available
both on the Isotope Science and Enrichment Directorate (ISED) SharePoint and the GTSI website.

1.2 NEUTRON SCIENCE EFFORT

Over the past year, ORNL has worked in collaboration with scientists from across the nation to advance
the use of neutron beamlines for measuring materials containing actinides. Much of this progress has been
documented in ORNL/TM-2024/3525.1 To sustain this momentum, the ORNL GTSI successfully
established new operational limits for actinide quantities at the Spallation Neutron Source, ensuring
compliance with radiation safety guidelines and meeting the needs of experimental-scale research.

Additionally, the ORNL GTSI initiated regular meetings with collaborators at LANL, which recently
secured funding to reinvigorate their neutron research capabilities at the Los Alamos Neutron Science
Center. As LANL advances these efforts, the ORNL GTSI aims to maintain a strong collaborative
partnership, recognizing the critical role both facilities play in enabling actinide measurements.

 3. FUNDING PORTFOLIO

The management and outreach efforts of the ORNL GTSI have been internally funded by ISED since the
institute’s inception. Currently, funding for postdoctoral researchers and students is provided directly by
interested programs. Thus far, these programs have included the 238Pu Supply Program (US Department
of Energy [DOE] Office of Nuclear Energy), National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) Office of
Defense Nuclear Nonproliferation (NA-22), and NNSA NA-19. Starting next year, the ORNL GTSI
anticipates also receiving support from the 252Cf programs (DOE Office of Isotope R&D and Production).

Looking ahead, the ORNL GTSI aims to broaden its funding portfolio to include sources such as
Laboratory Directed Research and Development (LDRD) initiatives, additional programs, and the
multilab LDRD framework. The institute’s ultimate goal is to establish a balanced funding strategy for
postdoctoral researchers, with equal support from LDRD and programmatic funds. This approach would
enable postdocs to pursue innovative, high-impact scientific endeavors independently and simultaneously
gain valuable experience by contributing to programmatic work. Such dual-focus exposure allows them to
learn about program intricacies and perform impactful tasks, even if some outcomes are not suitable for
publication. This approach is modeled after the Los Alamos Seaborg Institute, which has successfully
hired over 130 postdocs over the last 11 years.

 4. FUTURE PLANS

Next year, the ORNL GTSI aims to further the institute’s growth, building on the progress achieved over
the past couple years. The institute’s plans include diversifying funding sources, hiring additional
postdocs and students, and strengthening the ORNL GTSI professional network. In collaboration with
LANL and LLNL, the ORNL GTSI hopes to provide the institute’s students with opportunities to tour
various national laboratories, broaden their networks, and gain insights into the exciting work conducted
across these complexes to further expand the nation’s nuclear workforce.
