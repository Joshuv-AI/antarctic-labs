Primordial Black Holes from Vector-Induced Curvature Perturbations Sourced by Primordial Magnetic Fields
Chang Han, Zu-Cheng Chen, Hongwei Yu, Puxun Wu

Abstract
Generating an appreciable abundance of primordial black holes (PBHs) requires a substantial enhancement of primordial curvature perturbations on small scales. In this work, we propose a new post-inflationary mechanism in which such an enhancement arises during a stiff, or kination, epoch. The mechanism is driven by metric vector perturbations sourced by the vector component of the electromagnetic stress-energy tensor associated with primordial magnetic fields (PMFs). Since these first-order vector modes remain approximately constant during kination, they act as persistent nonlinear sources for second-order scalar perturbations. We show that the resulting vector-induced curvature perturbations are amplified toward the infrared cutoff of the kination band and exhibit the characteristic scaling 𝒫 ℛ ​ ( k ) ∝ k − 5 \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)\propto k^{-5} . As a concrete realization, we consider PMFs generated in a Ratra-type magnetogenesis scenario and find that the induced curvature perturbations can produce PBHs with an abundance large enough to constitute a substantial fraction of the dark matter.

In standard radiation- or matter-dominated eras, vector metric perturbations decay with the cosmic expansion

In this paper, we demonstrate that metric vector perturbations sourced by the vector component of the PMF electromagnetic stress-energy tensor can induce enhanced second-order curvature perturbations during a post-inflationary kination era. These induced curvature perturbations create over-dense regions that may collapse gravitationally into black holes. This provides a new PBH production mechanism in which the relevant enhancement occurs not in the linear scalar sector, as in most conventional scenarios, but through second-order scalar perturbations induced by magnetically sourced vector modes.
We further show that the resulting curvature power spectrum is enhanced toward the infrared cutoff and exhibits a characteristic power-law behavior with spectral index exactly
-5
. Applying the mechanism to PMFs generated in a Ratra-type magnetogenesis scenario, we find that the resulting PBHs can account for a substantial fraction of the dark matter.

First-order vector perturbations

We first introduce the first-order vector mode sourced by the vector component of the electromagnetic stress-energy tensor (ESET). In a spatially flat FLRW background, vector perturbations

VI
Discussions and Conclusions

We have demonstrated that vector modes soured by the vector component of the electromagnetic stress-energy tensor of primordial magnetic fields can induce significant curvature perturbations at second order during a stiff post-inflationary epoch. Since the first-order vector mode remains approximately constant during kination, it acts as a persistent nonlinear source for scalar perturbations. The resulting curvature power spectrum is enhanced toward the infrared cutoff and follows the characteristic scaling
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}\propto k^{-5}
. When these enhanced curvature perturbations reenter the horizon during the kination phase, they can produce sufficiently large overdensities to gravitationally collapse into PBHs. Applying this mechanism to PMFs produced in a Ratra-type magnetogenesis scenario, we find that the resulting PBHs can account for a substantial fraction of the dark matter.

This work proposes a new mechanism for enhancing curvature perturbations and producing PBHs. In contrast to many conventional PBH scenarios, where the relevant enhancement occurs directly in the first-order scalar sector, the present mechanism relies on second-order scalar perturbations induced by magnetically sourced vector modes through nonlinear gravitational coupling. The characteristic infrared scaling of the induced curvature spectrum provides a distinctive feature of this scenario.

several issues deserve further investigation. First, the PBH abundance estimate presented here is based on the conventional Press-Schechter formalism. A more complete treatment should include the nonstandard subhorizon evolution of the vector-induced scalar perturbations, as well as the possible modification of the collapse threshold by magnetic pressure. Second, because the curvature perturbations are generated at second order from a quadratic vector source, their statistics are expected to be intrinsically non-Gaussian. A dedicated analysis of this non-Gaussianity is therefore essential for a more accurate prediction of the PBH abundance. Finally, the same enhanced curvature perturbations may source a stochastic gravitational-wave background, whose spectrum could differ from standard scalar-induced gravitational waves because the curvature perturbations obey a sourced second-order evolution equation during their amplification. We leave these questions for future work.

\Phi_{k}(\tau)=\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau}d\bar{\tau}\,G(\tau,\bar{\tau})\,S_{k}(\bar{\tau}).

(34)

During the kination era, which has
w=1
, we have
\nu=1
. Then Eq. (

)
can be reduced to

\Phi_{k}(\tau)=\frac{\pi}{2\tau}\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau}d\bar{\tau}\,\bar{\tau}^{2}\left[J_{1}(k\bar{\tau})Y_{1}(k\tau)-J_{1}(k\tau)Y_{1}(k\bar{\tau})\right]S_{k}(\bar{\tau}),

(35)

where

\displaystyle S_{\mathbf{k}}(\bar{\tau})=\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\Big[(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})^{2}+\mathbf{q}\!\cdot(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})-\frac{\bar{\tau}^{2}}{6}\mathbf{q}^{2}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})^{2}\Big]V_{i}(\mathbf{q})V_{i}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}),

(36)

as given in Eq. (
9

We choose the initial time such that the relevant modes are well outside the
horizon,
x_{i}=k\tau_{i}\ll 1

x_{i}\to 0

using standard identities of Bessel functions

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}d\bar{x}\,\bar{x}^{2}\left[J_{1}(\bar{x})Y_{1}(x)-J_{1}(x)Y_{1}(\bar{x})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\Big[x-2J_{1}(x)\Big],

(37)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}d\bar{x}\,\bar{x}^{4}\left[J_{1}(\bar{x})Y_{1}(x)-J_{1}(x)Y_{1}(\bar{x})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\Big[x^{3}-8x+16J_{1}(x)\Big],

we obtain that the Green-function solution can be re-expressed as

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}(x)=\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\left[\frac{(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})^{2}+\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})}{k^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2J_{1}(x)}{x}\right)-\frac{\mathbf{q}^{2}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})^{2}}{6k^{4}}\left(x^{2}-8+\frac{16J_{1}(x)}{x}\right)\right]V_{i}(\mathbf{q})V_{i}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}).

(38)

Here
x\equiv k\tau
.
By taking the symmetry under

\mathbf{q}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}
, the Green-function solution in Eq. (

) can be further simplified to:

\Phi_{k}(x)=\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{k}}\left(1-\frac{2J_{1}(x)}{x}\right)+\mathcal{B}_{\mathbf{k}}\left(8-x^{2}-\frac{16J_{1}(x)}{x}\right),

(39)

where

\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{k}}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\,V_{i}(\mathbf{q})\,V_{i}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}),

(40)

and

\mathcal{B}_{\mathbf{k}}\equiv\frac{1}{6k^{4}}\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\,\mathbf{q}^{2}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})^{2}\,V_{i}(\mathbf{q})\,V_{i}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}).

(41)

Appendix B
Power spectrum of scalar perturbations

The power spectrum of metric scalar perturbation
\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}
is defined as

\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}(k)\,\delta(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime})=\frac{k^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}\left\langle\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}\Phi_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right\rangle.

(42)

Here,
\left\langle\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}\Phi_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right\rangle
is the two-point correlation function of
\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}
.
Using the Green-function solution of
\Phi_{k}
obtained in Appendix
A
, we find that this two-point correlation function can be expressed as

\displaystyle\left\langle\Phi_{\mathbf{k}}\Phi_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right\rangle=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4\tau^{2}}\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau}d\bar{\tau}_{1}\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau}d\bar{\tau}_{2}\,\bar{\tau}_{1}^{2}\bar{\tau}_{2}^{2}\,\mathcal{G}(k;\bar{\tau}_{1},\tau)\,\mathcal{G}(k^{\prime};\bar{\tau}_{2},\tau)\,\left\langle S_{\mathbf{k}}(\bar{\tau}_{1})S_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}(\bar{\tau}_{2})\right\rangle,

(43)

where

\mathcal{G}(k;\bar{\tau},\tau)\equiv J_{1}(k\bar{\tau})Y_{1}(k\tau)-J_{1}(k\tau)Y_{1}(k\bar{\tau}).

(44)

Substituting the solution of the first-order vector perturbation,
V_{i}(\mathbf{k})=-\frac{16\pi CG}{k}\,\Pi_{i}^{(V)}(\mathbf{k})
,
into Eq. (
36
) yields the two-point correlation of source term appearing in Eq. (
43
)

\displaystyle\left\langle S_{\mathbf{k}}(\bar{\tau}_{1})\,S_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}(\bar{\tau}_{2})\right\rangle=\int\frac{d^{3}q_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\int\frac{d^{3}q_{2}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\Bigg[(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}_{1})^{2}+\mathbf{q}_{1}\!\cdot(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}_{1})-\frac{\bar{\tau}_{1}^{2}}{6}\,\mathbf{q}_{1}^{2}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}_{1})^{2}\Bigg]

(45)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Bigg[(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}_{2})^{2}+\mathbf{q}_{2}\!\cdot(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}_{2})-\frac{\bar{\tau}_{2}^{2}}{6}\,\mathbf{q}_{2}^{2}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}_{2})^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{(16\pi CG)^{4}}{|\mathbf{q}_{1}|\,|\mathbf{q}_{2}|\,|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}_{1}|\,|\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}_{2}|}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left\langle\Pi_{i_{1}}^{(V)}(\mathbf{q}_{1})\,\Pi_{i_{1}}^{(V)}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}_{1})\,\Pi_{i_{2}}^{(V)}(\mathbf{q}_{2})\,\Pi_{i_{2}}^{(V)}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}_{2})\right\rangle.

According to the Wick’s theorem,
Eq. (
45
) can be re-written as

\displaystyle\left\langle S_{\mathbf{k}}(\bar{\tau}_{1})S_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}(\bar{\tau}_{2})\right\rangle=\delta(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}

\displaystyle\Bigg[(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})^{2}+\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})-\frac{\bar{\tau}_{1}^{2}}{6}\,q^{2}|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|^{2}\Bigg]

(46)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Bigg[1+\frac{\bigl[\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})\bigr]^{2}}{q^{2}|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|^{2}}\Bigg]\frac{(16\pi CG)^{4}}{q\,|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|}|\Pi^{(V)}(q)|^{2}\,|\Pi^{(V)}(|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|)|^{2}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Bigg[\frac{k^{2}}{q\,|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|}-\frac{\bar{\tau}_{2}^{2}}{3}\,q\,|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|\Bigg].

Substituting Eq. (
46
) into Eq. (
43
) and using the definition of power spectrum given in Eq. (
42
), we get the expression of power spectrum
\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}(k)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}(k)=\frac{k^{3}}{8\tau^{2}}\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau}d\bar{\tau}_{1}\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau}d\bar{\tau}_{2}\,\bar{\tau}_{1}^{2}\bar{\tau}_{2}^{2}\,\mathcal{G}(k;\bar{\tau}_{1},\tau)\,\mathcal{G}(k;\bar{\tau}_{2},\tau)\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p};\bar{\tau}_{1},\bar{\tau}_{2}),

(47)

where

\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p};\bar{\tau}_{1},\bar{\tau}_{2})\equiv

\displaystyle\left[p^{2}+\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{p}-\frac{\bar{\tau}_{1}^{2}}{6}\,q^{2}p^{2}\right]\left[1+\frac{(\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{p})^{2}}{q^{2}p^{2}}\right]\left[\frac{k^{2}}{q^{2}p^{2}}-\frac{\bar{\tau}_{2}^{2}}{3}\right](16\pi CG)^{4}|\Pi^{(V)}(q)|^{2}|\Pi^{(V)}(p)|^{2}

(48)

is the kernel function.
From
\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{p}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{q})
, one can derive

\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{k}-q^{2}=\frac{k^{2}-q^{2}-p^{2}}{2},

which leads to
\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{k}=\frac{k^{2}+q^{2}-p^{2}}{2}
.
Without loss of generality, we can choose

\mathbf{k}
along the
z
axis and then obtain

\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{k}=qk\cos\theta.

Using the above relations, we have

\displaystyle d^{3}q=2\pi q^{2}dq\,d\cos\theta=\frac{2\pi qp}{k}\,dq\,dp

(49)

with
p\in[|k-q|,k+q]
. Substituting Eq. (
49
) into Eq. (
47
) gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}(k)=\frac{k^{3}}{8\tau^{2}}\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau}d\bar{\tau}_{1}\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau}d\bar{\tau}_{2}\,\bar{\tau}_{1}^{2}\bar{\tau}_{2}^{2}\,\mathcal{G}(k;\bar{\tau}_{1},\tau)\,\mathcal{G}(k;\bar{\tau}_{2},\tau)\,\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dq\int_{|k-q|}^{k+q}\frac{qp}{k}\,dp\,\mathcal{F}(k,q,p;\bar{\tau}_{1},\bar{\tau}_{2}),

(50)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(k,q,p;\bar{\tau}_{1},\bar{\tau}_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,\left[1+\frac{(k^{2}-q^{2}-p^{2})^{2}}{4q^{2}p^{2}}\right]

(53)

\displaystyle\times\left[\frac{k^{2}(k^{2}+p^{2}-q^{2})}{2p^{2}q^{2}}-\frac{k^{2}}{6}\bar{\tau}_{1}^{2}-\frac{k^{2}+p^{2}-q^{2}}{6}\bar{\tau}_{2}^{2}+\frac{p^{2}q^{2}}{18}\bar{\tau}_{1}^{2}\bar{\tau}_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\times(16\pi CG)^{4}|\Pi^{(V)}(q)|^{2}|\Pi^{(V)}(p)|^{2}.

Introducing the following dimensionless
variables,

u=\frac{q}{k},\hskip 18.49988ptv=\frac{p}{k},\hskip 18.49988pt\bar{x}_{1}=k\bar{\tau}_{1},\hskip 18.49988pt\bar{x}_{2}=k\bar{\tau}_{2},\hskip 18.49988ptx=k\tau,

(54)

we obtain that Eq. (
50
) can be re-expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}(k)=\frac{k^{2}}{8x^{2}}\int_{x_{i}}^{x}d\bar{x}_{1}\int_{x_{i}}^{x}d\bar{x}_{2}\,\bar{x}_{1}^{2}\bar{x}_{2}^{2}\,\mathcal{G}(\bar{x}_{1},x)\,\mathcal{G}(\bar{x}_{2},x)\,\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\int_{|1-u|}^{1+u}dv\,\bar{\mathcal{M}}(u,v;\bar{x}_{1},\bar{x}_{2}),

(55)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\bar{x},x)\equiv J_{1}(\bar{x})Y_{1}(x)-J_{1}(x)Y_{1}(\bar{x}),

(56)

and

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{M}}(u,v;\bar{x}_{1},\bar{x}_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle uv\left[1+\frac{(-1+u^{2}+v^{2})^{2}}{4u^{2}v^{2}}\right]\left[\frac{1+v^{2}-u^{2}}{2u^{2}v^{2}}-\frac{\bar{x}_{1}^{2}}{6}-\frac{1+v^{2}-u^{2}}{6}\bar{x}_{2}^{2}+\frac{u^{2}v^{2}}{18}\bar{x}_{1}^{2}\bar{x}_{2}^{2}\right]

(57)

\displaystyle\times(16\pi CG)^{4}|\Pi^{(V)}(uk)|^{2}|\Pi^{(V)}(vk)|^{2}

is the dimensionless kernel function.

Since the internal momenta
q
and
p=|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|
appear symmetrically in the original
convolution, the final momentum integral should remain
invariant under the exchange
u\leftrightarrow v
. To make this symmetry explicit, we define a symmetrized kernel as follows:

\mathcal{M}(u,v;\bar{x}_{1},\bar{x}_{2})\equiv\frac{1}{2}\Big[\bar{\mathcal{M}}(u,v;\bar{x}_{1},\bar{x}_{2})+\bar{\mathcal{M}}(v,u;\bar{x}_{1},\bar{x}_{2})\Big],

(58)

and then achieve

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(u,v;\bar{x}_{1},\bar{x}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle uv\left[1+\frac{(-1+u^{2}+v^{2})^{2}}{4u^{2}v^{2}}\right]\left[\frac{1}{2u^{2}v^{2}}-\frac{\bar{x}_{1}^{2}+\bar{x}_{2}^{2}}{6}+\frac{u^{2}v^{2}}{18}\bar{x}_{1}^{2}\bar{x}_{2}^{2}\right]

(60)

\displaystyle\times(16\pi CG)^{4}|\Pi^{(V)}(uk)|^{2}|\Pi^{(V)}(vk)|^{2}.

Thus, the power spectrum can be further re-written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}(k)=\frac{k^{2}}{8x^{2}}\int_{x_{i}}^{x}d\bar{x}_{1}\int_{x_{i}}^{x}d\bar{x}_{2}\,\bar{x}_{1}^{2}\bar{x}_{2}^{2}\,\mathcal{G}(\bar{x}_{1},x)\,\mathcal{G}(\bar{x}_{2},x)\,\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\int_{|1-u|}^{1+u}dv\,\mathcal{M}(u,v;\bar{x}_{1},\bar{x}_{2}).

(61)

Now, we consider the initial scalar mode to be well outside the horizon, which indicates
x_{i}\to 0
. We then take the
\bar{x}_{1}
and
\bar{x}_{2}
integrals in Eq. (
61
) to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}(k)=2(8CG)^{4}k^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\int_{|1-u|}^{1+u}dv\,|\Pi^{(V)}(uk)|^{2}|\Pi^{(V)}(vk)|^{2}\,\mathcal{K}(u,v;x),

(62)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(u,v;x)\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{uv}{x^{2}}\left[1+\frac{(-1+u^{2}+v^{2})^{2}}{4u^{2}v^{2}}\right]

(63)

\displaystyle\times\left[\frac{1}{2u^{2}v^{2}}\Big(x-2J_{1}(x)\Big)^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\Big(x-2J_{1}(x)\Big)\Big(x^{3}-8x+16J_{1}(x)\Big)+\frac{u^{2}v^{2}}{18}\Big(x^{3}-8x+16J_{1}(x)\Big)^{2}\right].

The induced scalar perturbation relevant for PBH formation is evaluated when the corresponding mode reenters the horizon. Since
\mathcal{H}=1/(2\tau)
during the stiff epoch,
the horizon-entry condition becomes

k/\mathcal{H}=1
, which means that

x=k\tau=\frac{k}{2\mathcal{H}}=\frac{1}{2}.

(64)

Considering this condition in Eq. (
62
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}(k)=\frac{64C^{4}G^{4}k^{2}}{9}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\int_{|1-u|}^{1+u}dv\,|\Pi^{(V)}(uk)|^{2}|\Pi^{(V)}(vk)|^{2}\,\mathcal{T}(u,v),

(65)

with

\mathcal{T}(u,v)\equiv\frac{1}{u^{3}v^{3}}\left[u^{4}+(v^{2}-1)^{2}+u^{2}(6v^{2}-2)\right]\left[12+31u^{2}v^{2}-16(3+8u^{2}v^{2})J_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\right]^{2}.

(66)

Finally, we convert the power spectrum of metric scalar perturbations
\Phi
to that of curvature
perturbations
\mathcal{R}
. In the Newtonian gauge, we assume that
\Phi
and
\mathcal{R}
satisfy the relation

\mathcal{R}\simeq\frac{5+3w}{3(1+w)}\Phi.

(67)

When
w=1
, one has

\mathcal{R}=\frac{4}{3}\Phi,

(68)

which indicates that
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)=\frac{16}{9}\mathcal{P}_{\Phi}(k)
. Thus, the power spectrum of curvature perturbations takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)=\frac{1024C^{4}G^{4}k^{2}}{81}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\int_{|1-u|}^{1+u}dv\,|\Pi^{(V)}(uk)|^{2}|\Pi^{(V)}(vk)|^{2}\,\mathcal{T}(u,v).

(69)

Appendix C
Infrared spectral index of the power spectrum

We consider a finite comoving band for
k
bounded by the ultraviolet cutoff (
k_{\rm UV}
) and the infrared cutoff (
k_{\rm IR}
). These cutoffs arise from the finite range of modes that reenter the horizon during the stiff epoch. The ultraviolet cutoff is associated with the shortest mode exiting the horizon at the end of inflation,
k_{\rm UV}\sim a_{\rm inf}H_{\rm inf}

Mack et al. 2002
;
Kosowsky et al. 2002
, while the infrared cutoff
k_{\rm IR}\sim a_{\rm kin}H_{\rm kin}
corresponds to the longest mode that reenters the horizon at the end of the kination epoch
Bhaumik et al. 2025
. Consequently, only modes within the interval
k_{\rm IR}<k<k_{\rm UV}
contribute to the source, and both internal momenta are restricted according to
k_{\rm IR}<q<k_{\rm UV}
and
k_{\rm IR}<|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}|<k_{\rm UV}
. Thus, the power spectrum of curvature perturbations derived in the Appendix B
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)={}

\displaystyle\frac{1024\,C^{4}G^{4}k^{2}}{81}\int_{k_{\rm IR}/k}^{k_{\rm UV}/k}du\int_{\max\left\{\lvert 1-u\rvert,k_{\rm IR}/k\right\}}^{\min\left\{1+u,k_{\rm UV}/k\right\}}dv\left|\Pi^{(V)}(uk)\right|^{2}\left|\Pi^{(V)}(vk)\right|^{2}\mathcal{T}(u,v).

(70)

Near the infrared cutoff,
k\sim k_{\rm IR}\ll k_{\rm UV}
,
one has
k_{\rm IR}/k\sim 1
and
k_{\rm UV}/k\gg 1
.
The integration domain of
u
is therefore dominated by the region

u\gtrsim 1
. Now, we assume that the integral of
u
is dominated by the region away from the infrared and ultraviolet
boundaries, which indicates that the integration interval of
v
reduces approximately to

u-1<v<u+1
. Then we can obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)\simeq{}

\displaystyle\frac{1024\,C^{4}G^{4}k^{2}}{81}\int_{1}^{k_{\rm UV}/k}du\int_{u-1}^{u+1}dv\left|\Pi^{(V)}(uk)\right|^{2}\left|\Pi^{(V)}(vk)\right|^{2}\mathcal{T}(u,v).

(71)

When
u\gg 1
, we have

\int_{u-1}^{u+1}dv\,f(u,v)\simeq 2f(u,u),

which indicates that Eq. (
71
) can be simplified to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2048\,C^{4}G^{4}k^{2}}{81}\int_{1}^{k_{\rm UV}/k}du\,|\Pi^{(V)}(uk)|^{4}\,\mathcal{T}(u,u)

(72)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2048\,C^{4}G^{4}k^{2}}{81}\int_{1}^{k_{\rm UV}/k}du\,u^{6}|\Pi^{(V)}(uk)|^{4}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2048\,C^{4}G^{4}k_{\rm IR}^{7}}{81\,k^{5}}\int_{1}^{k_{\rm UV}/k_{\rm IR}}dy\,y^{6}\left[|\Pi^{(V)}(yk_{\rm IR})|^{2}\right]^{2},

where
y\equiv\frac{uk}{k_{\rm IR}}
. Eq. (
72
) shows clearly that

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)\propto k^{-5}.

(73)

Thus, the spectral index of the power spectrum of curvature perturbations takes the form

n\equiv\frac{d\ln\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}}{d\ln k}\simeq-5.

(74)

Appendix D
Primordial Magnetic Fields

In Fourier space, the general ESET can be decomposed into
Mack et al. 2002

\displaystyle\Pi^{(B)}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}{\Pi}+\left(\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\right)\Pi^{(S)}(\mathbf{k})+\frac{k_{i}}{k}\Pi^{(V)}_{j}(\mathbf{k})+\frac{k_{j}}{k}\Pi^{(V)}_{i}(\mathbf{k})+\Pi^{(T)}_{ij}(\mathbf{k}),

(75)

where
\Pi
and
\Pi^{S}
are the isotropic and anisotropic scalar parts, respectively, and
\Pi^{(V)}
and
\Pi^{(T)}
are respectively the vector and tensor components, which satisfy
k^{i}\Pi^{(V)}_{i}=k^{i}\Pi^{(T)}_{ij}=\Pi^{(T)i}_{\ \ i}=0
.

The anisotropic scalar, vector and tensor components can be obtained through the following projections

\Pi^{(S)}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\right)\Pi^{(B)}_{ij}(\mathbf{k}),

(76)

\Pi^{(V)}_{i}(\mathbf{k})=P_{in}(\mathbf{k})\,\frac{k_{m}}{k}\,\Pi^{(B)}_{mn}(\mathbf{k}),

(77)

and

\Pi^{(T)}_{ij}(\mathbf{k})=\Lambda_{ijmn}(\mathbf{k})\Pi^{(B)}_{mn}(\mathbf{k}),

(78)

where

P_{ij}(\mathbf{k})\equiv\delta_{ij}-\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}},

(79)

and

\Lambda_{ijmn}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{2}\left[P_{im}(\mathbf{k})P_{jn}(\mathbf{k})+P_{in}(\mathbf{k})P_{jm}(\mathbf{k})\right]-\frac{1}{2}P_{ij}(\mathbf{k})P_{mn}(\mathbf{k}).

(80)

It is easy to see that the scalar, vector, and tensor parts are different irreducible
projections of the same ESET and they can source different
metric sectors at linear order.

If the ESET arises from the PMFs, it can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Pi^{(B)}_{mn}(\mathbf{k})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{32\pi^{4}}\int d^{3}q\Bigl[B_{m}(\mathbf{q})B_{n}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{mn}B_{l}(\mathbf{q})B_{l}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})\Bigr],

(81)

which shows explicitly that the magnetic stress-energy tensor is quadratic of the magnetic field.

For a stochastic PMF, its characteristics can be described by the power spectrum
P_{B}
, which is defined as

\left\langle B_{i}(\mathbf{k})B_{j}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}P_{ij}(\mathbf{k})P_{B}(k)\delta(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime}).

(82)

Since a power-law form of power spectrum offers a minimal parametrization of the magnetic power distributed over a continuous range of comoving scales, we parameterize the magnetic spectrum as a finite-band power-law spectrum,

P_{B}(k)=A_{B}k^{n_{B}}\Theta(k_{\rm UV}-k)\Theta(k-k_{\rm IR}),

(83)

where
k_{\rm UV}
and
k_{\rm IR}
denote the largest and smallest
comoving wavenumbers over which the magnetic field is supported. This
parametrization has been widely used in studies of PMF-induced cosmological
perturbations
Kosowsky et al. 2002
;
Mack et al. 2002
;
Paoletti et al. 2009
;
Ade et al. 2016
,
and is also naturally connected with inflationary magnetogenesis
scenarios
Ratra 1992
;
Martin and Yokoyama 2008
;
Subramanian 2016
;
Yokoyama 2015
.
For a magnetic field from the inflationary magnetogenesis, the ultraviolet cutoff can be set by the comoving
Hubble scale at the end of inflation,

k_{\rm UV}\sim a_{\rm inf}H_{\rm inf}
. The infrared cutoff

k_{\rm IR}\sim H_{\rm kin}a_{\rm kin}
corresponds to the longest mode that
reenters the horizon during the kination era
Bhaumik et al. 2025
.
