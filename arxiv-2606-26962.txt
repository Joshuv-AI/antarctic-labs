1.1
Glancing trajectories

Let us consider the

(x,t)=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{t},\hskip 9.24994pt(p,E)=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n},E)\in T^{\ast}_{(x,t)}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{t}.

The union

1.2
Lagrangian singularities

Let us consider the
energy-space

\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{E}\ni(x,E)
and the two natural projections

\displaystyle\pi_{E}:T^{\ast}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{t}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{E},\hskip 9.24994ptz=(p,x,t,E)\mapsto(x,E),

\displaystyle\pi_{t}:T^{\ast}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{t}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{t},\hskip 9.24994ptz=(p,x,t,E)\mapsto(x,t).

The fibres of the both projections are Lagrangian and so the latter define the following two Lagrangian mappings

\Lambda\hookrightarrow T^{\ast}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{E}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{E},\hskip 9.24994pt\Lambda\hookrightarrow T^{\ast}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{t}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{t}.

If the second Lagrangian mapping is degenerate at some point of
\Lambda

\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}{d\left[\pi_{t}|_{\Lambda}\right](z)}<n+1
, then the first one is degenerate as well because
t
is a coordinate on
\Lambda

If the first Lagrangian mapping is degenerate at some point outside of the glancing trajectories,
i. e.
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}{d\left[\pi_{E}|_{\Lambda}\right](z)}<n+1
, then the first one is degenerate as well because
E

is a local coordinate on
\Lambda
in this case. The first Lagrangian mapping is degenerate at all points of the glancing trajectories.
We investigate its simplest singularity when the second Lagrangian mapping is not degenerate, i. e. away from focal points.

Our first main result is Theorem
1.1
giving a normal form of this simplest Lagrangian singularity up to diffeomorphisms preserving the energy level.

3.4
Application to Bessel functions

We apply our considerations to Helmoltz operator with constant coefficients, thus retrieving integral formulas for Bessel functions.
Assume for instance we are given a non-linear
Helmholtz equation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of the type

-h^{2}\Delta u-u=F(\mu u),\hskip 9.24994ptF(0)=0,\hskip 9.24994ptF^{\prime}(0)=1

where
F
is a smooth function and
\mu
is a small parameter.
We expand

, and find at zero order in

(-h^{2}\Delta-1)u_{0}=0
.
Its radially symmetric solution is given by

where

and is microlocalized on Bessel cylinder

defined by (
3.17
) for

.
Here

J_{0}\bigl({|x|/h}\bigr)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{i\langle x,\omega(\psi)\rangle/h}\,d\psi=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{i|x|(\sin\psi)/h}\,d\psi

is Bessel function of order 0.

At first order in
\mu
we get the Helmholtz equation

(-h^{2}\Delta-1)u_{1}=f_{h},\hskip 9.24994ptH=p^{2}

where
u_{1}=u(x,1)
in our previous notation, and

u_{h}(x,1)=-(|x|/2h)J_{1}(|x|/h)=-2(|x|/2h)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}tJ_{0}(t|x|/h)\,dt

(where
J_{1}(z)=(2i\pi)^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{iz\cos\psi}e^{i\psi}\,d\psi
is Bessel function of order 1)
is its radially symmetric solution, which is again microlocalized on Bessel cylinder. We observe that all points of
\Lambda_{0}
turn out
to be glancing
because
\Lambda_{0}\subset\Sigma_{1}
and
H_{\Lambda_{0}}=1
.

In case of constant coefficients we need not replace
p^{2}
by
|p|
, and can use the generating family of Proposition 3.3.
Consider first the homegeneous equation
(-h^{2}\Delta-1)u_{h}(x)=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. This amounts to choose the constant amplitude
a=1
on
\Lambda_{0}
.
We have

P(t,\varphi,\psi)=\omega(\psi),\hskip 9.24994ptX(\varphi,\psi,t)=2t\omega(\psi)+\varphi\omega(\psi)

Choosing some local coordinates

on

, it is easy to see that

\Lambda
(up to sign) is given by

, and

(3.24)

So the solution of
(-h^{2}\Delta-E)u_{h}=f_{h}
is given by

u_{h}(x)=\frac{i}{h}\int_{0}^{\infty}\,dt[{\cal K}_{\Lambda}a](x,t)e^{iEt/h}.

Let
\Phi^{E}_{+}=\Phi+Et
, we compute the critical point

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\partial\Phi^{E}_{+}\over\partial\lambda}=\langle\omega^{\perp}(\psi),x-X(\varphi,\psi,t)\rangle=0,\hskip 9.24994pt{\partial\Phi^{E}_{+}\over\partial\varphi}=1-\lambda=0

(3.25)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\partial\Phi^{E}_{+}\over\partial\psi}=\lambda\langle\omega^{\perp}(\psi),x-X(\varphi,\psi,t)\rangle=\lambda\langle\omega^{\perp}(\psi),x\rangle=0

while
{\partial\Phi^{E}_{+}\over\partial t}=-2\lambda+E=-1

when

. Repeated integration by parts in (

) with respect to

gives

u_{h}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi h}\int e^{i\Phi^{E}_{+}(0,x,\theta)/h}\bigl(a+{h\over i}{\partial a\over\partial t}+\cdots\bigr)\,d\lambda\,d\varphi\,d\psi

Applying stationary phase to (

) with respect to
(\lambda,\varphi)

is
\langle\omega(\psi),x\rangle

readily shows that when

, we get only the first term, namely

Note that the same kind of argument works for equation
(-h^{2}\Delta-1)u_{h}(x)=J_{0}(|x|/h)
, which gives

u_{h}(x)=-{|x|\over h}J_{1}(|x|/h)
, etc…. In fact all these functions are microlocally supported on Bessel cylinder.
