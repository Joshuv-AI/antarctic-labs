Poster 120. Rising Achilles Tendon Ruptures in the NBA: The Role Of Playing Time And Experience
Authors: Vineet S Kumar, Luke L Sang, Wyatt David, Nirav K Pandya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:Achilles tendon ruptures (ATR) have long been a serious concern in the National Basketball Association (NBA), with an average of 1.36 cases per season from 1990 to 2023. Alarmingly, the 2024-2025 season saw a sharp rise to seven ATRs, the highest ever recorded in a single season. ATRs are considered one of the most debilitating soft tissue injuries in basketball, often requiring close to a year of recovery and frequently resulting in long-term declines in athletic performance. Beyond the physical challenges, players must also overcome significant psychological hurdles, including loss of confidence, fear of re-injury, and difficulty regaining pre-injury form. These consequences not only impact individual careers but also have major implications for team performance and roster stability. Identifying specific risk factors and workload patterns leading up to ATRs is therefore critical for developing strategies aimed at prevention and player health optimization. The objective of this study was to evaluate the characteristics, workload trends, and contextual factors surrounding NBA players who sustained ATRs. We hypothesized that players who suffered ATRs would demonstrate measurable increases in workload in the games leading up to injury, as well as identifiable patterns in schedule congestion and player usage, compared to their typical season averages.Methods:This was a retrospective review of publicly available NBA data from the 2014-2015 through the 2024-2025 seasons. Players were included if they sustained a basketball-related Achilles tendon rupture (ATR) during the regular season or playoffs after participating in at least one game that year. A total of 25 players were identified, six were excluded due to non-basketball or off-season injuries, leaving 19 cases for final analysis. Collected variables included player age, position (Guard, Forward, Center, Combo), role (starter vs bench), early sport specialization status (single- vs multi-sport participation in high school), games played in the 30 days preceding injury, number of back-to-back games (no rest day between two separate games), total rest days, and workload measures. Workload measures included meters run in the injured season versus career averages, total meters run in the injured season versus total career totals, and minutes played across 5-, 10-, and 15-game spans before injury. Workload and performance data were obtained from NBA Advanced Stats and StatMuse. Information on background and multisport participation was collected from ESPN, player interviews, publicly available biographies, and high school sports records. Statistical significance was set at p<0.05.Results:19 athletes were included in this study. There were no significant differences in age at time of ATR or schedule congestion metrics when stratified by player position (G vs F vs C vs Combo), role (Starter vs Bench), or early sports specialization status (Single vs Multi-Sport). There was a significant spike in minutes played observed in the 10 and 15 games preceding the Achilles rupture (p < 0.01, p <0.03) for all players. Additionally, a positive correlation was found between years of NBA experience and total minutes played in the injury season (p < 0.02, r = 0.53), suggesting that more veteran players tended to carry heavier workloads at the time of injury compared to their younger counterparts.Conclusions:Achilles tendon ruptures (ATRs) reached a record high in the 2024-2025 NBA season. In this retrospective study of 19 cases, players demonstrated significant increases in minutes played in the 10-15 games preceding injury, with veteran players carrying greater workloads in their injury season. No significant associations were identified with player position, early sport specialization, or schedule congestion. These findings emphasize the importance of proactive workload management and maintaining consistency in player usage to reduce rupture risk. Although limited by sample size and, this study highlights actionable recommendations for player health and team management. Larger and future longitudinal studies using advanced workload metrics may further clarify risk patterns and guide evidence-based strategies to protect the long-term health and careers of NBA athletes.
