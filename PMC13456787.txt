Risk analysis and warning model of post-noncardiac surgery acute kidney injury: A retrospective observational study
Authors: Yan Xu, Wen Li, Qi Pang, Leiyun Wu, Xingtong Dong, Wenjing Fu, Aihua Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Postoperative acute kidney injury (PO-AKI) following noncardiac surgery remains a major clinical challenge, for which effective early warning models are still lacking. This study aimed to identify risk factors for noncardiac PO-AKI and to develop and validate a practical early warning model based on readily available clinical parameters across multiple surgical subspecialties. In this study, large-scale data from noncardiac surgical patients were extracted from the electronic medical record system of Xuanwu Hospital to analyze risk factors. Subsequently, an early warning model for PO-AKI was developed by integrating logistic regression (LR) and deep learning (DL) approaches. Internal and external validations were performed to assess model performance. A total of 19,152 patients were included in the analysis. Eleven independent risk variables were identified using LR, including age, surgical department, prolonged operation duration, contrast agent exposure, elevated serum creatinine, proteinuria, hemoglobin reduction, elevated fasting blood glucose, serum potassium abnormalities, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, and D-dimer levels. A corresponding risk score was subsequently constructed. The receiver operating characteristic analysis demonstrated areas under the curve (AUCs) of 0.7679 for the LR model and 0.7578 for the derived risk score. In the validation cohort, the AUCs were 0.7856 and 0.7122, respectively. The DL model further improved predictive performance, achieving AUCs of 0.97 and 0.93 in the validation and test sets, respectively. Precision–recall analysis showed area under the precision–recall curve values of 0.96 and 0.74, with corresponding F1 scores of 0.945 and 0.897. Big data analysis revealed an overall incidence of PO-AKI after noncardiac surgery of 3.69%. A robust early warning model incorporating 11 clinically practical parameters was developed. The integration of LR and DL approaches improved predictive performance while maintaining interpretability.

1. Introduction

The global incidence of acute kidney injury (AKI) ranges from 0.99% to 18.4%.[1–3] Postoperative AKI (PO-AKI) following noncardiac surgery represents a major contributor to the overall burden of AKI.[3,4] PO-AKI is associated with a broad spectrum of adverse outcomes, including increased mortality, higher healthcare costs, prolonged hospital stay, and an elevated risk of acute kidney disease and chronic kidney disease. Importantly, even mild AKI or transient elevations in serum creatinine (Scr) that return to baseline before discharge may still be associated with poor long-term outcomes.[5] These findings highlight the urgent need for effective preventive strategies and early identification of high-risk patients, as current therapeutic options for AKI remain limited and are primarily supportive.[6]

Currently, serum creatinine and urine output are the most commonly used indicators for monitoring renal function; however, their ability to predict AKI at an early stage remains inadequate. Previous studies have demonstrated that these conventional biomarkers often fail to identify AKI before substantial renal injury has already occurred.[7] In addition, the furosemide stress test has shown inconsistent predictive performance across different patient populations.[8] Despite increasing recognition of these limitations, relatively few studies have focused on establishing reliable early warning systems for PO-AKI after noncardiac surgery.[9] This gap underscores the need for more comprehensive predictive models capable of integrating multiple clinical variables to improve risk stratification and facilitate timely clinical intervention.

Deep learning (DL) techniques have demonstrated considerable potential across multiple medical fields.[10,11] By analyzing large-scale datasets and identifying complex nonlinear relationships among clinical variables, DL methods may facilitatemore accurate early prediction of AKI risk. Nevertheless, the application of DL to PO-AKI prediction after noncardiac surgery remains limited, and additional studies are required to evaluate the feasibility and generalizability of such models across diverse surgical populations. Most currently available AKI prediction models were developed for patients undergoing cardiac surgery or for critically ill populations, whose pathophysiological characteristics and perioperative risk factors differ substantially from those of patients undergoing noncardiac surgery. Moreover, few existing models combine the interpretability of traditional statistical approaches with the adaptive learning capability of deep learning algorithms, thereby limiting their clinical applicability. Therefore, the objectives of the present study were to 1st identify risk factors associated with PO-AKI after noncardiac surgery using logistic regression (LR) analysis, and subsequently to develop and validate an early warning model based on routinely available clinical variables using the TabNet deep learning algorithm.[12]

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Study design and patients

In this single-center retrospective study, data from patients undergoing noncardiac surgery were collected from the electronic medical record system of Xuanwu Hospital, Capital Medical University. Data obtained between November 2015 and December 2020 were used for risk factor analysis and model development, whereas data collected between January and December 2021 were used for temporal validation and model testin. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Xuanwu Hospital, Capital Medical University (approval number: [2022]228). Ethical approval was granted on January 30, 2023. Owing to the retrospective nature of the study, the requirement for written informed consent was waived.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: patients aged 18 years or older who underwent noncardiac surgery were eligible for enrollment. The exclusion criteria were: preoperative end-stage kidney disease or receipt of kidney replacement therapy[12]; AKI occurring within 30 days before surgery; missing Scr data; and multiple surgical procedures during the study period, in which case only data from the 1st surgery were included in the analysis. Urological and obstetric surgeries were excluded because of their distinct pathophysiological mechanisms and nonuniform diagnostic criteria for PO-AKI. In addition, thoracic surgery cases were excluded because of the limited sample size.[13,14] Baseline Scr was defined as the value measured within 1 month before surgery, and when multiple measurements were available, the value closest to the operation was selected. Postoperative Scr levels were collected within 7 days after surgery.[12,13]

2.2. Definition of PO-AKI

Postoperative Scr levels were compared with preoperative baseline values to diagnose PO-AKI according to the 2012 Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes criteria.[15] PO-AKI was defined as an increase in Scr of ≥0.3 mg/dL (≥26.5 µmol/L) within 48 hours or an increase in Scr to ≥1.5 times the baseline value, known or presumed to have occurred within 7 days after surgery.

2.3. PO-AKI warning model research steps

Overall, the development of the warning model followed a 2-step approach. First, LR analysis was performed to identify risk factors and establish the initial warning model. Second, DL was applied to optimize the model.

In the 1st procedure, the study was conducted in several steps: data collected between November 2015 and December 2020 were used to establish a derivation cohort to determine the incidence of PO-AKI; potential risk variables associated with PO-AKI were initially screened using univariate logistic regression analysis; multivariate logistic regression analysis was subsequently performed to identify independent risk factors and evaluate their effects; based on the weights of the identified risk factors, an early warning scoring system was established; and data from January to December 2021 were used to construct a validation cohort, in which the scoring system underwent external temporal validation, a commonly used validation method.

The 2nd procedure involved deep learning. The TabNet neural network model, a DL algorithm, was introduced to optimize the warning model. Data collected between November 2015 and December 2020 were randomly divided into DL derivation and validation sets at a ratio of 8:2. The risk variables identified through LR analysis were used as input features, and the PO-AKI warning model was trained using the derivation set and validated in the validation set. In addition, data from January to December 2021 were used to establish an independent DL test set for temporal external validation and evaluation of the optimized warning model.

2.4. Data collection

Other laboratory parameters were categorized according to the reference ranges provided by the source hospital, and all variable cutoff values were determined based on these institution-specific reference ranges. This approach ensured consistency with local clinical practice; however, potential interlaboratory variability should be considered when generalizing the findings.

2.5. DL protocol

For model optimization, we employed TabNet, a neural network architecture introduced by Google Research in 2019. Data preprocessing included data cleaning, organization, and imputation of missing values using mean substitution. Risk factors identified through logistic regression analysis were incorporated into the TabNet model for training in the DL derivation set. The model was subsequently validated in the DL validation set and tested in the independent test set. Model performance was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), the F1 score, and the area under the precision–recall curve (AUPRC). In addition, feature importance was assessed using SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) values, which quantify the contribution of each variable to the predictions generated by the deep learning model. To address class imbalance in the derivation set, the Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique was applied to generate synthetic samples from the minority class and thereby reduce bias toward the majority class. An early stopping strategy was implemented during model training to prevent overfitting, with a maximum of 1000 iterations and a patience threshold of 80 epochs.

2.6. Statistical methods

All statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc.). Data cleaning procedures were conducted before analysis. Missing values were imputed with “0.”[16] Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median with interquartile range, depending on data distribution. The reference category was defined as either the normal range of the variable or the subgroup with the lower incidence rate. Logistic regression analysis was used for variable selection and model construction, with P < .05 considered statistically significant for variable screening. The performance of the warning model was evaluated using AUROC analysis, and the optimal cutoff values were determined based on Youden index.

3.2. Univariate LR analysis of risk factors for noncardiac postoperative AKI

Univariate LR analysis identified several potential risk factors associated with PO-AKI, as detailed in Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content 1.

3.3. Multiple LR analysis of risk factors for noncardiac postoperative AKI

Variables with P < .1 in the univariate analysis were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis to identify independent risk factors for AKI and determine their weights for inclusion in the scoring system. A total of 11 independent risk factors were identified for PO-AKI, including age, surgical department, prolonged operative duration, contrast agent administration, elevated Scr, proteinuria, reduced hemoglobin level, elevated fasting blood glucose (FBG), abnormal serum potassium level, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), and D-dimer level (P ≤ .05) (Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content 2). ROC curve was generated based on the multivariate LR model (Fig. 2), yielding an AUROC of 0.7679. The maximum Youden index was 0.392, corresponding to a sensitivity of 64.8% and a specificity of 74.4%. The regression coefficients and odds ratios for different intervals of each variable are presented in Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content 2.

3.4. Establishing an early alert score scale for noncardiac PO-AKI by statistical logistic analysis

To facilitate clinical application, a scoring scale (Table 2) was developed based on the LR model. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was used to determine the weights of the identified risk factors, and scores were assigned accordingly. The ROC curve of the scoring scale demonstrated an AUROC of 0.7578 (Fig. 3). When the Youden index reached its maximum value of 0.38, the sensitivity and specificity of the scoring scale were 69.9% and 68.1%, respectively. A score range of 2.5 to 3 was defined as the gray zone. Scores <2.5 indicated a low risk of PO-AKI, with an estimated prevalence of approximately 1%; scores between 2.5 and 3 indicated an intermediate risk, with a prevalence of approximately 3%; and scores >3 indicated a high risk of PO-AKI, with a prevalence exceeding 11%.

3.5. Validation of the early warning scoring scale derived from the LR model for noncardiac postoperative AKI

Temporal external validation of the LR-derived scoring scale was subsequently performed. In the validation set, the AUROC was 0.7856 for the multivariate LR model (Fig. 4) and 0.7122 for the scoring scale (Fig. 5). In this cohort, the maximum Youden index was 0.305, with a sensitivity of 63.1% and a specificity of 67.4%. The corresponding optimal score range was 2.1 to 2.5. Therefore, consistent with the derivation cohort, a cutoff value of 2.5 was ultimately selected.

3.6. Training, validation, and testing of the DL-based warning model for noncardiac PO-AKI

In the DL derivation set, all 11 risk variables identified in the LR model were entered into the TabNet neural network for model training and optimization. The optimized model was subsequently validated in the DL validation set and evaluated in the independent test set. ROC curve analysis demonstrated AUROCs of 0.97 in the validation set (Fig. 6) and 0.93 in the test set (Fig. 7), respectively. SHAP value plots were generated to illustrate the contribution of each variable to the prediction of noncardiac PO-AKI (Fig. 8). In descending order of contribution, the variables were ranked as follows: contrast agent administration, operative duration, hemoglobin level, preoperative serum creatinine, preoperative FBG level, advanced age, D-dimer level, surgical department, proteinuria, NLR, and serum potassium level. Precision–recall curves for the DL-based warning model were also generated in the validation and test sets (Figs. 9 and 10), yielding AUPRC values of 0.96 and 0.74, and F1 scores of 0.945 and 0.897, respectively.

4. Discussion

In a multinational study, the overall incidence of PO-AKI after major surgery was reported to be 18.4%, with 33.8% of affected patients showing persistent renal dysfunction.[3] However, relatively few studies have investigated early warning systems for PO-AKI following noncardiac surgery to date. In the present study, data from 19,152 patients undergoing noncardiac surgery were analyzed, revealing a PO-AKI incidence of 3.69%. Eleven independent risk factors were identified, including older age, prolonged operative duration, contrast agent exposure, and abnormalities in serum biomarkers. A logistic regression model and a simplified scoring system were subsequently developed, demonstrating moderate predictive performance. Furthermore, incorporating these variables into a DL model using the TabNet algorithm significantly improved discriminative ability, with an AUROC of up to 0.93 in the DL test set. Overall, the combined LR-DL framework provides a robust, interpretable, and accurate early warning tool for PO-AKI across multiple surgical specialties.

The incidence of PO-AKI in this study (3.69%) was lower than that reported in some previous studies. This discrepancy may be attributed to several factors. First, some patients lacked serum creatinine measurements within 7 days after surgery, which may have led to underestimation of AKI incidence. Second, the present study included non-ICU patients, who generally have a lower baseline risk of AKI. Notably, emergency procedures were not excluded, and inclusion criteria were not restricted to major surgeries, consistent with previous publications.[12,17] As a result, the study population covered a broader range of surgical types and techniques, which may also have influenced the observed incidence.

In this study, 11 independent risk factors for noncardiac PO-AKI were identified using the logistic regression model (Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content 2), which is largely consistent with previous studies.[18–21] However, for clinical convenience, preoperative FBG was used as a variable, which differs from some prior studies, and elevated preoperative FBG was confirmed as a significant risk factor. In addition, preoperative serum creatinine, rather than estimated glomerular filtration rate or blood urea nitrogen, was selected for inclusion in the scoring system as a key risk factor. The results also indicated that both hyperkalemia and hypokalemia were independent risk factors for PO-AKI. Although previous studies have primarily focused on the association between potassium levels and mortality or major cardiovascular events,[22,23] the underlying mechanism linking potassium abnormalities and PO-AKI remains insufficiently elucidated.

We also found that both elevated and reduced NLR were associated with increased risk of PO-AKI, whereas most previous studies have reported only elevated NLR as a risk factor.[24] The review by Kai Singbartl[25] highlighted the bidirectional interaction between AKI and the immune system, suggesting that immune responses play a key role in both injury and recovery processes. This immunomodulatory mechanism may partially explain our findings. In addition, we observed that both abnormally high and low D-dimer levels were associated with PO-AKI. D-dimer is well known as a marker of thromboembolic disease and has also been shown to be elevated in critically ill patients and in conditions such as contrast-induced AKI.[20,26,27] Previous studies have further demonstrated its association with systemic inflammation and infection,[28] which may promote inflammatory coagulation and fibrinolysis imbalance, ultimately contributing to AKI development in critically ill patients.[29] However, the mechanism underlying the association between low D-dimer levels and PO-AKI remains unclear and requires further investigation.

For potential clinical application, we used 11 risk variables independently associated with PO-AKI to develop an early warning scoring table, in which the scores were derived from the regression coefficients obtained in the logistic regression model. The primary purpose of constructing this scoring system was to enable rapid bedside or oral calculation in routine clinical practice. However, the PO-AKI early warning score derived from statistical modeling showed only moderate discriminative performance in temporal external validation, with an AUC of 0.7122. Therefore, a deep learning algorithm was subsequently introduced to further optimize model performance, representing one of the key innovations of this study.

DL has emerged as a powerful approach for solving a variety of complex medical problems.[30] However, its application in the development of PO-AKI prediction models remains limited.[31,32] A systematic review by Vagliano et al[33] in 2022, which analyzed 46 studies on machine learning for AKI risk prediction, highlighted limited progress in postoperative AKI prediction and emphasized the underuse of DL approaches. Accordingly, we applied a DL neural network to improve predictive performance.[34] In this study, an early warning model based on big data and the TabNet architecture was developed and validated, representing an innovative aspect of the present work. The TabNet model offers several advantages, including efficient processing of tabular clinical data, built-in feature selection, and improved interpretability compared with conventional deep learning models, making it particularly suitable for clinical risk prediction tasks.

The PO-AKI warning model developed using the TabNet DL algorithm demonstrated excellent performance. Temporal external validation using noncardiac surgical data from different time periods yielded an AUROC of 0.93 in the test set, indicating strong discrimination and generalizability. This performance exceeded that of a recently published multimodal DL model for moderate-to-severe AKI (AUROC = 0.88–0.93),[35] and was comparable to a causal DL model for cardiac surgery-associated AKI (internal AUROC about 0.93; external AUROC about 0.92),[36] although cardiac surgery-related AKI models are generally more mature and extensively validated. Importantly, the use of temporal external validation in this study strengthened the evaluation of model robustness, which remains uncommon in noncardiac PO-AKI prediction research. Given the high AUROC values during training (up to 0.97), potential overfitting was carefully considered. An early stopping strategy was implemented during model training to mitigate overfitting, and consistent performance in temporally independent external data (AUROC = 0.93) further supported the model’s stability and predictive reliability. Notably, the DL-based model outperformed the LR-based scoring system in predictive accuracy. As a binary classification model, we further evaluated performance using precision–recall analysis and calculated the AUPRC and F1 score. The F1 score was approximately 0.9 in both the validation and test sets, indicating a strong balance between precision and recall. In addition, the AUPRC reached 0.96 in the validation set and decreased to 0.74 in the test set, which still indicated acceptable performance. SHAP analysis further improved interpretability by identifying the most influential features, including contrast agent use, operative duration, hemoglobin level, preoperative creatinine, FBG, age, D-dimer, surgical department, proteinuria, NLR, and serum potassium, thereby providing clinically actionable insights for perioperative AKI prevention.

Moreover, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-center study, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, the retrospective design inevitably introduced limitations related to data completeness and quality. Third, external validation using multicenter prospective cohorts is required to further confirm the robustness of the proposed model. Fourth, after integration with the deep learning framework, the final model was implemented as a computational algorithm rather than a simple bedside scoring system. Finally, although the DL model provided overall feature importance, it did not fully capture risk variation across different value ranges. Future integration with electronic medical record systems may enable real-time risk alerts and further enhance clinical applicability.

5. Conclusions

Large-scale data analysis showed that the incidence of PO-AKI after noncardiac surgery was 3.69% in this study. We identified 11 independent risks factors, including older age, surgical department, prolonged operative duration, contrast agent exposure, elevated serum creatinine, proteinuria, reduced hemoglobin, elevated FBG, abnormal serum potassium, NLR, and D-dimer. Using these 11 clinically accessible variables, we 1st developed a PO-AKI early warning model applicable across multiple surgical subspecialties, rather than a single surgical type as in most previous studies. The integration of logistic regression analysis with the TabNet deep learning algorithm significantly improved model performance, providing both high predictive accuracy and strong interpretability. Temporal external validation further confirmed the robustness and generalizability of the model.
