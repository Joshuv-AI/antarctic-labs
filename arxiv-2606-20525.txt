The B + → K + ​ ν ​ ν ¯ B^{+}\to K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu} decay as a QCD axion search: comparing reinterpretation approaches Preprint: LAPTH-033/26
Merna Abumusabh, Giulio Dujany, Diego Guadagnoli, Méril Reboud, Claudio Toni

Abstract
Two recent independent analyses of Belle II B + → K + ​ ν ​ ν ¯ B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu} data yield limits on ℬ ⁡ ( B + → K + ​ a ) \mathcal{B}(B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}a) —the two-body mode to a light invisible particle such as the QCD axion—differing by a factor of roughly four; we trace this to the choice of kinematic variable space. The central figure of merit is the resolution in the reconstructed di-neutrino invariant mass q r ​ e ​ c 2 q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec} : fine-grained binning resolves the narrow axion signal, while coarse binning dilutes it into a background-dominated range. A BDT axis trained on B + → K + ​ ν ​ ν ¯ B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu} adds little discriminating power for B + → K + ​ a B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}a , as this axis is largely uncorrelated with q 2 q^{2} . These expectations are confirmed by a set of numerical tests. The subleading shape systematics omitted from our q r ​ e ​ c 2 q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec} -based approach lower , not raise, the B + → K + ​ a B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}a limit: by better accommodating the B + → K + ​ ν ​ ν ¯ B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu} shape, they leave less room for the axion signal, making our q r ​ e ​ c 2 q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec} -based bound conservative, if anything. A dedicated reanalysis confirms that the kinematic-axes choice alone accounts for the factor-of-four sensitivity difference, and that the B + → K + ​ a B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}a bound varies sizeably within the q r ​ e ​ c 2 × η ⁡ ( B ​ D ​ T 2 ) q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}\times\eta({\mathrm{B}DT}_{2}) space, depending on the SM-likeness of B + → K + ​ ν ​ ν ¯ B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu} , thus losing the dual-probe feature of our q r ​ e ​ c 2 q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec} -based approach. These results point to a broader consideration: likelihoods dominated by BDT variables are of limited use for reinterpretations when the signal shape differs appreciably from the BDT’s training signal. We therefore advocate that experimental collaborations publish likelihood projections in physical variable spaces alongside BDT-based likelihoods, to maximise the reinterpretability of their measurements.



I
Introduction

The reinterpretation of Belle II
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
data as a search for the two-body decay
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}a
, where
a
is a light invisible state such as the QCD axion or a generic axion-like particle (ALP), has recently been addressed by two independent groups.
The first approach
Abumusabh et al. 2026
introduces a model-independent framework using only public data, analytically reconstructing the mapping between true and reconstructed kinematic variables within the statistically dominant Inclusive Tagging Analysis (ITA). Applied to
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}a
, this yields the strongest existing bounds on
\mathcal{B}(B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}a)
and on the coupling-rescaled Peccei–Quinn scale
|(F_{V})_{sb}|
, improving previous limits by about one order of magnitude
Martin Camalich et al. 2020
;
Ferber et al. 2022
.
A second approach
Gärtner et al. 2026
is based on the published model-agnostic likelihood of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2025
. For the physically motivated QCD axion, the resulting limits on
\mathcal{B}(B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}a)
are weaker by a factor of roughly four than those of the first approach.

In this note we discuss the origins of this difference. Both approaches begin from
projections
of the same underlying Belle II data and simulation: neither has access to the full unpublished event-level information. The relevant difference lies in the granularity and the kinematic axes of these projections. The likelihood of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2025
is a function of two variables: a coarsely binned
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
(three bins over the full kinematically accessible range), and the transformed
{\mathrm{B}DT}_{2}
output
\eta({\mathrm{B}DT}_{2})
, a variable designed to classify events as signal-like for
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
. As we argue below, neither variable is well matched to a narrow invisible signal: the coarse
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
binning washes out the structured signal distribution, while
\eta({\mathrm{B}DT}_{2})
carries little or no discriminating power between
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}a
and background.
By contrast, Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
works directly with the fine-grained
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
distribution of Fig. 17 of Ref.
Adachi et al. 2024
, which retains approximately seven times more
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
resolution and is the natural variable for narrow-signal recasting.

The rest of this note is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
we develop the analytical basis for the expected sensitivity difference, discussing in detail the role of
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
granularity, the properties of the
\eta({\mathrm{B}DT}_{2})
axis, and a formal limitation of the reweighting method of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2025
for delta-function signals. In Sec.
III
we subject the approach of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
to a series of robustness tests, varying the background-normalization uncertainty and examining the profile likelihood for
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
; these tests simultaneously validate our uncertainty estimate and clarify the physics of the shape separation between the
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}a
and
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
components. In Sec.
IV
we construct an independent analysis in the
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}\times\eta({\mathrm{B}DT}_{2})
space, directly comparable to Ref.
Gärtner et al. 2026
, and use it to quantify the sensitivity loss attributable to the choice of kinematic axes. Sec.
V
collects our conclusions.

II
Origins of the sensitivity difference

The starting point of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
is Fig. 17 of Ref.
Adachi et al. 2024
, which displays the
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
distribution in the ITA signal region with approximately 21 bins over the range
[-1,25]~{\mathrm{G}eV}^{2}
, derived directly from Belle II data and simulation. The starting point of Ref.
Gärtner et al. 2026
is Fig. 1 of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2025
, which encodes the same observable in the binning of the published likelihood:
4\times 3
bins in
\eta({\mathrm{B}DT}_{2})\times q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
, with the three
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
bin boundaries at
\{-1,4,8,25\}~{\mathrm{G}eV}^{2}
.
Both starting points are projections of the same underlying Belle II data and simulation, which exist in a richer, higher-dimensional form than either figure captures. Fig. 17 of Ref.
Adachi et al. 2024
is a projection of actual data and internal simulation; Fig. 1 of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2025
is a projection of the same underlying data encoded in the published likelihood. Neither is the “ultimate truth”; the difference between them is one of
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
resolution, approximately seven times finer in our case.

Resolution in
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
: the key figure of merit for narrow signals
– This difference in resolution is consequential specifically for narrow invisible signals. For the QCD axion (
m_{a}\simeq 0
), the signal is monochromatic at
q^{2}=0
and maps, via the kinematic relation derived analytically in Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
, onto a distribution in
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
that is broadened by the unmeasured decay angle but remains structured and concentrated near
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}\simeq 0
. With the published likelihood’s three
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
bins, this signal is subsumed into the single bin
[-1,4]~{\mathrm{G}eV}^{2}
, where it competes with the
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
background that populates the same bin. With approximately 21 bins, the signal structure is resolved over several bins, each carrying a smaller background contribution, and sensitivity improves proportionally. For the
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
measurement itself—for which the three-bin structure was designed—the two approaches are equivalent: the
\nu\bar{\nu}
spectrum is smooth and broad, and its integral over any bin is well represented at either resolution. The asymmetry between the two cases is therefore not a limitation of the
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
analysis, but a structural feature of repurposing it for narrow signals.

The
\eta({\mathrm{B}DT}_{2})
axis: a variable optimized for
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
– The second axis of the published likelihood,
\eta({\mathrm{B}DT}_{2})
, raises an independent concern of the same nature. The variable
{\mathrm{B}DT}_{2}
was trained to separate
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
signal from background: a value
\eta\simeq 1
identifies events as signal-like for
\nu\bar{\nu}
, not for
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}a
. The two signals differ kinematically: the axion produces a monochromatic kaon in the
B
-meson rest frame, whereas
\nu\bar{\nu}
produces a continuous kaon spectrum. As a result, the
\eta({\mathrm{B}DT}_{2})
axis may carry little or no discriminating power between
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}a
and background, and may even preferentially suppress axion signal events that are not
\nu\bar{\nu}
-like. More precisely, the sole variable that truly discriminates
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}a
from
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
is
q^{2}
itself—and
\eta({\mathrm{B}DT}_{2})
is only loosely correlated with
q^{2}
, since the BDT was not trained to exploit this distinction. The 2D space of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2025
is therefore not a neutral basis for a model-independent recast of narrow signals: it is a space optimized for a specific, and different, signal topology.

The reweighting method in the delta-function limit
– The two limitations described above—coarse
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
binning and a BDT axis tuned to
\nu\bar{\nu}
kinematics—are both natural consequences of applying a likelihood designed for
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
to the qualitatively different case of a narrow two-body signal. They are also directly connected to a formal property of the reweighting method of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2025
. That method constructs alternative signal templates via
w(q^{2})=\sigma_{1}(q^{2})/\sigma_{0}(q^{2})
, where
\sigma_{0}
is the smooth SM
\nu\bar{\nu}
spectrum, and notes explicitly that it “performs best when the alternative theory remains close to the null distribution”, warning that large deviations produce “very large weights” and “potentially unreliable results”. For a narrow or zero-width invisible state,
\sigma_{1}(q^{2})\propto\delta(q^{2}-m_{a}^{2})
, which is by construction maximally far from the smooth null distribution. This is precisely the least favorable regime for the method. The resulting large weights, concentrated in a single
q^{2}
bin, compound the sensitivity loss from the coarse
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
binning: the signal information is already diluted at the level of the kinematic axes, and the reweighting step cannot recover it.

The role of systematic uncertainties
– A separate question is whether the factor-of-four difference in the
\mathcal{B}_{a}
limits between the two approaches might be driven, at least in part, by the more conservative treatment of uncertainties in Ref.
Gärtner et al. 2026
, which incorporates the full set of systematic uncertainties from the Belle II analysis, whereas Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
retains only the dominant background-normalization component. We argue that this is not the case. The background-normalization uncertainty accounts for approximately 50% of the total systematic uncertainty in the ITA
Adachi et al. 2024
, and the remaining components individually contribute at a level well below what would be needed to explain a factor-of-four degradation. Moreover, the inclusion of additional systematic components tends to
lower
the
\mathcal{B}_{a}
limit rather than raise it, because it gives the fit more freedom to accommodate the
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
shape and thereby leaves less room for the
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}a
signal. Therefore, the neglect of subleading shape systematics makes the
\mathcal{B}_{a}
bound from Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
, if anything, slightly conservative. These conclusions are verified numerically in Sec.
III
.

III
Robustness of the approach in Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026

The analysis of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
incorporates one dominant systematic uncertainty: the normalization of the total background, estimated at approximately 1% for the ITA. This estimate is derived from the pull distribution of Fig. 17 of Ref.
Adachi et al. 2024
. Identifying the pull in each
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
bin
i
as
|n^{i}_{\mathrm{o}bs}-n^{i}_{\mathrm{h}st}|/\sqrt{n^{i}_{\mathrm{o}bs}+(\sigma^{i}_{\mathrm{h}st})^{2}}
, where
n^{i}_{\mathrm{o}bs}
and
n^{i}_{\mathrm{h}st}
are the observed yield and the stacked-histogram prediction respectively, and inverting this relation for
\sigma^{i}_{\mathrm{h}st}
, one finds that the relative uncertainty
\sigma^{i}_{\mathrm{h}st}/n^{i}_{\mathrm{h}st}
is approximately constant across bins at the level of
\sim 1\%
. This motivates modeling the background uncertainty as a single overall normalization factor, with a relative uncertainty of 1%, common to all bins.

In this section we subject this choice to a series of tests by varying the background-normalization error over a wide range and examining the effect on two separate quantities: the
\mathcal{B}_{a}
limit and the profile likelihood for
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
. The two quantities respond differently to this variation, and the contrast between them is itself informative. The
\mathcal{B}_{a}
signal is concentrated near
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}\simeq 0
and has a qualitatively different shape from both the
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
signal and its backgrounds; the data in all bins but one therefore constrain the
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
component independently of the normalization prior, leaving the
\mathcal{B}_{a}
limit unaffected. The
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
profile likelihood, by contrast, is sensitive to the normalization error, because the
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
signal and its backgrounds have similar shapes in
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
and are therefore not shape-separable. These expectations are confirmed by the tests described below, which simultaneously validate the 1% error estimate and clarify the physical basis for the dual-probe property specific to the approach in Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
.

Throughout this paper, limits are quoted at 95% CL and restricted to the ITA-only analysis, to allow direct numerical comparison with Ref.
Gärtner et al. 2026
.
The definitive 90% CL bounds, following PDG convention
Navas et al. 2024
, are those of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
and are to be found therein.

IV
A reanalysis in the
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}\times\eta({\mathrm{B}DT}_{2})
space

This section probes the sensitivity difference between the two approaches through two complementary analyses. The first is a direct rerun of the published
pyhf
likelihood of Ref.
Gärtner et al. 2026
, which establishes a numerical baseline for the Ref.
Gärtner et al. 2026
result and quantifies the roles of
\mu_{\nu\bar{\nu}}
freedom and of the shape systematics within that framework. The second is a purpose-built analysis—referred to hereafter as the
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}\times{\mathrm{B}DT}
analysis—that applies the likelihood framework of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
to the same starting point used by Ref.
Gärtner et al. 2026

histogram of Fig. 15 of Ref.
Adachi et al. 2024
, rather than the fine-grained
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
distribution of Fig. 17. The statistical method, signal model, and treatment of the background normalization are otherwise identical to those of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
. Any difference in the
\mathcal{B}_{a}
limit between Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
and the
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}\times{\mathrm{B}DT}
analysis is therefore directly attributable to the choice of kinematic axes, while any residual difference between the
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}\times{\mathrm{B}DT}
analysis and Ref.
Gärtner et al. 2026
reflects the additional systematic components present in the latter but absent in both the former and Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
.

V
Conclusions

The choice of kinematic variable space in recasting
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
data as a search for narrow invisible signals has a decisive impact on sensitivity. This note has traced the origins of a factor-of-four difference in the
\mathcal{B}_{a}
limits between two recent independent analyses of the same Belle II data.

The central finding is that
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
resolution is the key figure of merit. The published model-agnostic likelihood of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2025
encodes only three
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
bins—sufficient for the broad
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
spectrum it was designed for, but inadequate for a narrow signal whose structured
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
distribution is subsumed into a single bin dominated by
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
background. Its second axis,
\eta({\mathrm{B}DT}_{2})
, was trained to separate
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
from background and carries little discriminating power for
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}a
, whose sole distinguishing kinematic variable is
q^{2}
itself. Both limitations reflect a more fundamental property of the reweighting method of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2025
: that method requires the alternative theory to remain close to the smooth null distribution. For a narrow or zero-width invisible state,
\sigma_{1}(q^{2})\propto\delta(q^{2}-m_{a}^{2})
, which is maximally far from the smooth null—precisely the least favorable regime.

These analytical expectations are confirmed through five numerical tests. Tests 1 and 2 establish the robustness of the Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
approach: the
\mathcal{B}_{a}
limit is insensitive to the background-normalization uncertainty, confirming strong shape separation between the axion signal and the
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
background in
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}
space; the
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
profile likelihood, by contrast, depends sensitively on this error—0% gives a profile that is too sharp, 50% one that is too broad, and 1% reproduces the experimental profile closely—independently validating the error estimate; the “dual-probe” feature—the mutual independence of the
\mathcal{B}_{a}
bound and the size of new physics in
\mathcal{B}_{\nu\bar{\nu}}
—is preserved throughout.

Test 3, a direct rerun of the Ref.
Gärtner et al. 2026
likelihood with its full set of 231 nuisance parameters, shows that shape systematics lower
\mathcal{B}_{a}
by
\sim 20\%
, confirming that omitting them from Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
makes its bound
conservative
. It further shows that freeing
\mu_{\nu\bar{\nu}}
entirely improves the limit by roughly a factor of two, confirming that the
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}\times\eta({\mathrm{B}DT}_{2})
space is sensitive to the SM-likeness of
B^{+}\!\to\!K^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}
—thus losing the dual-probe property of the approach in Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
.

Tests 4 and 5 use a dedicated
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}\times{\mathrm{B}DT}
analysis, which applies the Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
framework to the
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}\times\eta({\mathrm{B}DT}_{2})
space of Ref.
Gärtner et al. 2026
, while keeping the statistical method, signal model, and background treatment identical to those of Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
. This cleanly isolates the kinematic-axes effect: the
\sim
factor-of-five degradation in
\mathcal{B}_{a}
relative to Ref.
Abumusabh et al. 2026
is attributable solely to the change of kinematic axes. The direct rerun of Test 3 and the
q^{2}_{\mathrm{r}ec}\times{\mathrm{B}DT}
analysis of Tests 4 and 5 are therefore complementary: the former probes the full Ref.
Gärtner et al. 2026
likelihood; the latter isolates the kinematic-axes contribution.

The results of this comparison bring, we believe, insights that extend beyond the specific case studied here. Likelihoods defined in spaces dominated by BDT variables are of limited use for recasting measurements into signals whose shape differs appreciably from the signal the BDT was trained on. We therefore advocate that experimental collaborations publish likelihood projections in the space of physical variables alongside BDT-based likelihoods, so as to maximise the reinterpretability of their measurements for the broadest possible range of new-physics scenarios.
