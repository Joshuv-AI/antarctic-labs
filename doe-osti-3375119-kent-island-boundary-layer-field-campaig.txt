1.0 Summary
 Understanding the mechanisms governing the urban atmospheric environment is critical for informing
 urban populations about the impacts of climate change and associated mitigation and adaptation
 measures. The U.S. Department of Energy Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) User Facility’s
 Coast-Urban-Rural Atmospheric Gradient Experiment (CoURAGE) was conducted to study the
 interactions among the earth’s surface, the atmospheric boundary layer (ABL), aerosols, atmospheric
 composition, clouds, radiation, and precipitation at each experimental site, and to examine how the spatial
 gradients across the region interact to create the climate conditions in Baltimore.

 In this activity we performed weekly soundings, alternating between day and night from Kent Island, to
 get low-frequency, long-term observations of ABL/lower tropospheric thermodynamic structure.
 However, during the CoURAGE intensive operational period (IOP), we performed high-frequency
 sampling (2x/day), at times coinciding with CoURAGE IOP soundings at the main site. The short-term,
 high-frequency sounding information that was launched at 12Z and 18Z was to complement the
 longer-term data set.

 Figure 1. Windsonde launch at Kent Island during CoURAGE.

 The windsonde sounding system from Sparv Embedded consists of a balloon, radiosonde, receiver, and
 licensed software. The windsonde measures profiles of wind speed, wind direction, temperature, and
 humidity. Figure 2 shows an image of the windsonde system.

 1.1 Scientific Focus
 The specific aim of this activity was to profile Chesapeake Bay atmospheric thermodynamics, which are
 important for urban climate, air quality, and cloud development.

 1.2 Relevance to ARM
 The data from this study supported the CoURAGE field campaign. Bay atmosphere thermodynamics are
 not currently sampled. The substantial spatial heterogeneity in the Bay ABL properties complements
 Doppler lidar and surface flux measurements taken at Kent Island.

2.0 Results
 The data is currently published on the ARM data site https://iop.arm.gov/2025/crg/kibl/li-
 windsond/. Figure 3 reveal the percentage of point data received from each sensor. Out of about
 61,317 total data points for the whole period, the wind sensor lost about 21% of the data. Figure
 4 shows the profiles for temperature (red), relative humidity (blue), wind speed (green), and
 wind direction (purple) from Kent Island on February 22, 2025 at 8am local time. Within the
 PBL, the sonde flight heads southwards until above the planetary boundary layer (PBL), where it
 turns southeast. In the PBL, the temperature dropped from 2.5o C to -7.5o C with steady relative
 humidity of 50%-60% and wind speed staying around 5m/s. A temperature inversion occurring
 above the PBL at 1000m caused an increase in temperature from -7.5o C to -2o C with a sharp
 decrease in both relative humidity from 60% to 20% and wind speed from 5m/s to 1m/s.
