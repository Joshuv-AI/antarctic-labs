Abstract
Fusion energy promises virtually limitless, carbon-free power, yet its viability depends on overcoming a
critical fuel challenge. Tritium, one of the two principal fuels used in fusion reactions, is exceedingly scarce
in nature and must therefore be generated within the reactor itself for use as fuel in future reactions, a process
known as tritium breeding.
 We varied nine design parameters to quantify their effects on the tritium breeding ratio (TBR) of cermaic
breeders, identify the highest-performing configuration, and determine optimal parameter combinations that
maximize TBR using sensitivity analyses.
 The maximum TBR was 1.5242 ± 0.00420. Neutron multiplier material and thickness, along with solid
angle reduction, were the most influential parameters, while reflector properties and porosity had limited
effects. An optimized design maintained comparable high breeding performance with reduced blanket and
reflector material.
