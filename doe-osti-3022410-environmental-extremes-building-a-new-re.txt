Public Executive Summary
This project focused on strengthening research partnership and collaboration between the
University of Nevada, Reno (UNR) and the Pacific Northwest National laboratory (PNNL) in two
focused areas: wildfires and hydrology in the context of environmental extremes. The goal was to
overcome barriers and bring UNR university researchers, students, and postdocs up to speed and
entrained into DOE/SC/BER /Earth and Environmental System Science Division’s (EESSD)
environmental research enterprise.

UNR continues to work with the Earth and Biological Sciences Directorate (EBSD) at PNNL to create
a broad new collaborative research program connecting scientists and research faculty at the two
institutions. This research partnership has been designed to increase the capabilities and velocities
of both institutions by developing long-term relationships between researchers from each
institution, exposing university researchers to the deep capabilities in the DOE National
Laboratories and user facilities, and establishing a pipeline of skilled and experienced graduate
students and postdoctoral researchers ready to work alongside DOE scientists to take on grand
challenge science problems. These collaborations are intended to ultimately benefit the public by
advancing scientific discovery, unleashing the scientific potentials through collaboration and
connection, enriching the technical and scientific competitiveness of our nation, and enabling the
development of a skilled workforce to tackle the critical scientific challenges that face our nation’s
security, infrastructure, and prosperity.

Accomplishments and Objectives
The overall goal of this project was to develop new research capabilities addressing EESSD science
priorities by creating a sustained collaborative research partnership between the environmental
research programs at UNR and PNNL. Initially focused on understanding and predicting
environmental extremes in water and wildfires under climate and societal change, the specific
objectives of this partnership were to:

 1. Increase the research capabilities and velocities of both institutions to achieve shared
 goals;
 2. Create strong research partnerships between the two institutions;
 3. Expose UNR faculty researchers to the ARM and EMSL facilities;
 4. Establish a pipeline of skilled and experienced UNR graduate students and postdoctoral
 researchers ready to work on National Laboratory-class grand challenge science; and,
 5. Eliminate barriers keeping UNR faculty from participating in EESSD science programs.

This project enabled multiple opportunities to increase research capabilities and strong research
partnerships between the two institutions. As further detailed in the section below, UNR produced
a joint, virtual research lightning talk to explore collaborations around the DoE focus area
“Environmental Systems Science.”

This project successfully exposed UNR faculty researchers to the ARM and EMSL facilities by, as
further detailed in the section below, enabling a reverse site visit for UNR faculty to visit PNNL, tour
laboratory facilities, and meet in-person with PNNL scientists.

This project successfully established a pipeline of skilled and experienced UNR graduate students
and postdoctoral researchers ready to work on National Laboratory-class grand challenge science
by, through the formal Distinguished Graduate Fellowship Program, awarding a fellowship to one
graduate student to work on the River Corridor Project at PNNL for a two-year term.

This project successfully worked to eliminate barriers keeping UNR faculty from participating in
EESSD science programs by enrolling one faculty member in the joint appointment program and by
continued engagement among lab and university leadership.

A major accomplishment from this project was completing a US Forest Service modeling study of
post-forest restoration effects on hydrology. This project was part of a large interdisciplinary
collaboration that supported the Tahoe Central Sierra Initiative, one of the largest forest restoration
projects in the country. The project led to the development of an important publication Boardman
et al. (2025a) in Water Resources Research.

One of the major accomplishments of this project was a DOE-supported modeling system for
predicting streamflow response following megafire. This project introduces a new model calibration
technique that leverages wildfire as a natural experiment to reduce model uncertainty and improve
predictive performance. This project included a meeting in the Seattle PNNL facility and
culminated in the publication Boardman et al. (2025b) in Hydrology and Earth Systems Science.

Project Activities
Activities that increase research capabilities of both institutions and create strong research
partnerships between them:
 1. On April 1, 2022, UNR and PNNL produced a joint, virtual lightning talk seminar to learn
 corresponding expertise and explore collaboration opportunities around the DoE focus area
 “Environmental Systems Science.” Understanding the complex ecological and hydro-
 biogeochemical processes of the Earth and environmental systems is a focus of many
 researchers at both UNR and PNNL, and these research lightning talks provided a valuable
 forum for discussion. Five scientists from PNNL and five faculty from UNR engaged in this
 virtual event.
 2. On Nov. 21, 2022, Dr. Malin Young, PNNL Associate Lab Director for Earth and Biological
 Sciences visited campus to further explore UNR expertise and capabilities. During this visit,
 Dr. Young engaged with faculty in both formal and informal settings to hear about their
 collaboration experiences and needs, visited UNR laboratory space, and discussed
 collaboration strategies with leadership.
 3. On Sept. 18, 2023, PNNL scientists traveled to UNR for a day-long collaboration-building
 and proposal development workshop on the broad topics of wildfire resilience and
 mountain hydrology. Six PNNL scientists and 31 UNR faculty and graduate students
 gathered for structured presentations and conversations exploring environmental extremes.
 The workshop was followed by a networking social hosted by the Global Water Center. The
 next day, PNNL scientists were invited to visit the site of the 2021 Caldor Fire near South
 Lake Tahoe with co-PI Anne Nolin and see first hand the impacts of wildfire.

Activities that exposed UNR faculty researchers to the ARM and EMSL facilities:
 1. On Jan. 18, 2023, three faculty traveled to PNNL for a reverse site visit. Faculty who visited
 the EMBL facility found it extremely informative and sparked ideas on how they could
 incorporate the capabilities into their research. This in-person visit allowed for
 conversations that wouldn’t have happened otherwise, enabling future collaborations. Four
 other faculty and one graduate student were expected to participate in this reverse site visit,
 but weather impacted their travel and precluded the visit.)

Activities that established a pipeline of skilled and experienced UNR graduate students and
postdoctoral researchers ready to work on National Laboratory-class grand challenge science:
 1. Though initiated prior to submission of this grant, the joint UNR/PNNL Distinguished
 Graduate Research Fellowship Program was finalized through a signed MOU during this
 project period and UNR’s first graduate student was accepted into the program to work on
 the River Corridor Project. UNR is working to attract other strong candidates to this program
 that promises to enhance research capabilities at both institutions.

Activities that worked to eliminate barriers keeping UNR faculty from participating in EESSD science
programs:
 1. Though initiated prior to submission of this grant, the UNR/PNNL Joint Appointment
 program has continued to be an effective mechanism for enabling novel collaborations
 between the two institutions. UNR has continued to work throughout the terms of this
 award to promote and encourage faculty and scientists to explore use of the joint
 appointment mechanism. One additional joint appointment was development during the
 terms of this award.
 2. Beginning in 2024 and ongoing, UNR faculty and two PNNL scientists met regularly to
 discuss collaborative research involving post-fire watershed hydrology.
 3. UNR and PNNL leadership and personnel continue regular engagement even outside the
 terms of this award.
