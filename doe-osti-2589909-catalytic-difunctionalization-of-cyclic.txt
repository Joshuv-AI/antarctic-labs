DOE Project Award Number: DE-SC0023205

Sponsoring Program Office: Basic Energy Sciences

Project Title: Catalytic Difunctionalization of Cyclic Dienes: Direct Entry to Novel ROMP
Monomers

Principle Investigator: Keary M. Engle (Scripps Research); Will R. Gutekunst (Georgia
Tech)

Abstract: We developed a catalytic platform to convert simple hydrocarbon feedstocks
into valuable, tunable materials by leveraging nickel-catalyzed difunctionalization of cyclic
dienes to access a novel class of cyclic alkene monomers. These monomers undergo
ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) to yield sequence-controlled polymers
with defined stereochemistry. Through mechanistic studies and catalyst optimization, we
established a scalable, gram-level synthesis for selective diarylation, and expanded the
reaction scope to include arylalkylation through rationally tuning the organoboron coupling
partner. The resulting polymers were systematically studied to understand how steric,
electronic, and stereochemical features influence polymerization behavior and bulk
material properties. Functionalized derivatives bearing sulfonated groups were explored
as proton-exchange membranes, and chemical recycling pathways were developed to
recover monomers from the final materials. This work bridges small-molecule catalysis
and macromolecular design, enabling access to tunable, recyclable polymers from
abundant hydrocarbon starting materials.

Project Summary:

The overall goal of this project was establishing a reliable multi-catalytic platform to
streamline the functionalization of feedstock cyclic dienes to obtain novel cycloalkene
monomers for preparation of unique macromolecular structures via polymerization.
Initial efforts focused on developing and optimizing the nickel-catalyzed 5,6-diarylation
and 5,6-dialkenylation of 1,5-cyclooctene and established an efficient ruthenium-
catalyzed ring-opening metathesis polymerization protocol to access formal styrene–
butadiene copolymers. Notable findings from optimization of the diarylation was that the
reaction was sensitive to mass transfer effects due to the limited solubility of the base and
organoboron coupling partner. Hence, the optimized protocol required careful control of
rate of mixing to achieve reproducible and scalable monomer production to support bulk
material preparation.
The decorated monomers from the nickel-catalyzed diarylation were studied in ROMP.
The reaction conditions were optimized and found to provide targeted molecular weight
when varying monomer to catalyst ratios. The kinetic profile for the polymerization of

As compared to the diarylated cyclooctene monomers, their arylalkyl cyclooctene
(AACOE) counterparts react in ROMP at faster rates due to reduced 1,3-diaxial strain in
the preferred conformation of 8-membered ring in the metathesis transition state. All
AACOE monomers examined polymerized to high conversions (>95%) and gave
molecular weights in general agreement with the monomer:initiator ratios (~20 kDa to 40
kDa). The thermal properties of the AACOE polymers were found to dramatically change
based on the nature of alkyl substituent. Glass transition temperatures (Tg) measured by
differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) ranged from -1 ºC for the perfluoro alkyl P2 to 61
ºC for Boc-piperidine P5. For the arylalkylated polymers, the influence of alkyl chain on
hydrophobicity was studied via contact angle measurements on thin films. The
perfluorinated P2 was found to be the most hydrophobic material with a contact angle of
106.7°, and the piperidine-containing P5 was the least hydrophobic with a contact angle
of 75.6°. The hydrophilicity of P5 could readily be increased upon Boc deprotection with

TFA, P6 which further decrease the contact angle to 63.9°. Collectively, these studies
highlight the tailorable structure-property relationships of the AACOE materials, with
further application possibilities enabled through polymer post-functionalization.

The periodic nature of the DACOE polymers suggest potential use as precision ion
exchange membranes after polymer post-functionalization. Hydrogenation of the
backbone double bonds with diimide, exposure to sulfuric acid, and dialysis with lithium
hydroxide resulted in clean sulfonation in the para-positions of the diphenyl cyclooctene
polymer. Through a collaboration with the Winey Lab at UPenn, lithium polysulfonates
with molecular weights of 50 and 100 kDa were measured through electronic impedance
spectroscopy to reveal conductivities on the order of 10-7 and 10-8, respectively, and
activation energies of ~90 kJ/mol. These results compare favorably to other precision
sulfonate materials in the literature, which have seen considerable success in transport

applications.

Direct reaction of DACOE polymers with sulfuric acid indiscriminately para-sulfonates the
phenyl rings, and this limits the diversity of ion exchange materials that can be accessed.
To overcome this limitation, a new methodology for polymer post-sulfonation was
developed based on ipso-substitution of aryl silanes. 3-trimethylsilylphenyl boronate was
found to be a competent coupling partner in the directed 1,2-diarylation reaction to give a
meta-silylated DACOE, P7, which was polymerized and hydrogenated with diimide. Clean
ipso-sulfonation using trimethylsilyl chlorosulfonate at room temperature was observed to
give P7-SO3Na without any observable sulfonation of the unsilylated phenyl rings. Similar
protocols were employed to generate P8-SO3Na and P9-SO3Na with the additional
sulfonation patterns on the parent DACOE polymer that would be otherwise challenging
to access, and conductivity measurements on these materials are currently performed by
the Winey Lab. This strategy provides a means to control electrophilic reactions at non-
native positions that will likely find broader use in the field of polymer chemistry.

The presence of alkenes on the polymer backbone of the DACOE and AACOE polymers
presents an opportunity to depolymerize the materials to low molecular weight vinyl-
terminated molecules through ethenolysis with appropriate metathesis catalysts.
Preliminary ethenolysis experiments on diphenyl P1 (Mn = 32 kDa) using a 5-membered
CAAC-ligated ruthenium complex developed by Virgil, Bertrand, and Grubbs were
encouraging (Figure 7). When performed at 135 psi of ethylene in toluene (0.1M), 68%
conversion to the targeted small molecule diene is produced, along with low molecular
weight oligomers (< 3 kDa). The reduced catalytic activity on this substrate relative to
substances previously studied in the literature (e.g., methyl oleate) is likely due to the
greater steric hindrance of the backbone olefins. Future efforts to improve the TON and
overall conversion is currently being pursued by exchanging the phenyl in the CAAC
ligand for smaller substituents.
