A note on o-minimal tropicalizations
Lorenzo Baldi †, Máté L. Telek ‡

Abstract
. In this note, we provide several equivalent descriptions of the tropicalization of definable sets in a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of a real closed field. We show that the tropicalization can be described in multiple ways: as the image under the valuation map, as the image under the tropicalization map of the archimedean points in the o-minimal spectrum, in terms of initial degenerations of the definable set and initial nonnegativity cones, or via the real tropicalization of polynomials belonging to the corresponding nonnegativity cone. Moreover, we prove analogous results for the fine tropicalization of definable sets, where the valuation map is replaced by the RV sort map, which records not only the valuation of a point but also its angular component.

1.
Introduction

The origins of tropical geometry can be traced back to the work of Bergman
[
6
]
and Viro
[
64
]
. Since their pioneering work, the theory has developed in several directions and has found applications in both pure and applied mathematics. Tropical geometry studies algebraic varieties (or more generally, definable sets) via their degenerations, often referred to as their
polyhedral shadows
. These polyhedral shadows are typically easier to work with than their algebraic counterparts, while still retaining significant information about the original objects. For example, they have been used to compute Gromov–Witten invariants
[
40
]
, study toric compactifications of algebraic varieties
[
56
]
, and construct real algebraic varieties with prescribed topology
[
65
,
12
,
49
]
. On the applied side, tropical geometry has been used to determine the generic number of solutions of parametrized polynomial systems
[
26
,
22
]
, solve such systems via homotopy continuation methods
[
27
,
34
,
25
]
, and compute Feynman integrals
[
8
]
.

One of the ways to define the tropicalization
\Trop(X)
of a set
X\subset(\mathbb F\setminus\{0\})^{n}
, where
\mathbb F
is a valued field, is as the closure, in the euclidean topology of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, of the image of
X
under the coordinate-wise valuation map.
While this definition is straightforward, it is convenient to have other equivalent descriptions of the points in
\Trop(X)
, that are often better suited for understanding how properties of
X
translate to properties of
\Trop(X)
, and vice versa. When
\mathbb F
is algebraically closed and
X
is an algebraic set, the Fundamental Theorem of Tropical Algebraic Geometry gives these equivalent descriptions for
\Trop(X)
, which can be characterized in terms of the initial degenerations of
X
, of the Berkovich analytification of
X
, or of the tropicalizations of all polynomials vanishing on
X
(see
[
35
, Th. 3.2.3]
,
[
18
, Th. 4.2]
, and
[
20
]
).
These results rely on the algebraically closedness of the ground field, but can be adapted to give interesting information also for algebraic sets defined over a real closed field, see e.g.
[
54
,
10
]
.

However, this approach is not sufficient if one wants to take into account the
order
of the real closed field, which makes possible to define
semialgebraic
sets (or more generally, definable sets in an o-minimal structure) in addition to algebraic sets. This necessity led to the works
[
17
]
(for the special case of polyhedra) and
[
1
]
, where the tropicalization of semialgebraic sets is systematically studied for the first time.
Since then, this topic has attracted an increasing attention, see e.g.
[
2
,
55
,
7
]
. In particular, in their seminal paper, Jell, Scheiderer, and Yu established an analogue of the Fundamental Theorem in the semialgebraic setting
[
28
, Th. 6.9]
.

In this note, we show that this theorem can be generalized to definable sets in polynomially bounded o-minimal structures. While retaining the tameness properties of semialgebraic sets, definable sets in polynomially bounded o-minimal structures are considerably more general, as they include for instance globally subanalytic sets, see e.g.
[
60
]
, and allows the use of
real
exponents for monomial functions, with potential applications e.g. to the study of irrational toric varieties
[
46
]
.

Furthermore, in our fundamental theorem we prove a characterization of the tropicalization of a definable set in terms of
real
initial degenerations (see
Remark

3.3.5
for a comparison with the classical ones) , which is new already in the semialgebraic case. After the following theorem, which is our main result, we discuss the objects which appear in the statement, and compare the results obtained with the existing literature.

Theorem A

(Fundamental Theorem of Tropical O-minimal Geometry, see Theorem
4.5.1
)
.

Let
\mathfrak K\prec\mathfrak F\prec\mathfrak M
be elementary extensions of polynomially bounded o-minimal expansions of the real closed fields
\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb F\subset\mathbb{M}
. Furthermore, assume that
\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb F\subset\mathbb{M}
is an extension of valued real closed fields such that the valuation on
\mathbb F
is nontrivial and
\val(\mathbb{M}^{\times})=\mathbb{R}
. For any definable set
X\subset\mathbb{K}_{>0}^{n}
, the following subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are equal.

(i)

\Trop(X)
;

(ii)

\trop(\widetilde{XF}^{\mathrm{arch}})
;

(iii)

\overline{\{\,w\in\Gamma^{n}{F}\mid\initial_{w}(XF)\neq\emptyset\,\}}
;

(iv)

\val(XM)
;

(v)

\bigcapop\displaylimits_{f\in\mathcal{P}(X)}\{\,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\trop_{r}(f)(1,w)\geq 0\,\}
.

Moreover, the intersection in (v) can be taken to be finite.

As observed e.g. in
[
28
, Sec. 6]
, one must consider the intersection of a set
X
with each orthant in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
separately, in order to study its full tropicalization. The gluing of the tropicalizations of the intersections of
X
with different orthants leads to the definition of
real phase structures
in the algebraic case, see e.g.
[
49
]
. To simplify our notation, we restrict our attention to the positive orthant
\mathbb{K}_{>0}^{n}
only.

In (ii), the archimedean points in the o-minimal spectrum
\widetilde{X_{\mathfrak F}}
play the role of the real Berkovich analytification of
X
, cf.
[
28
, Th. 3.17]
and
Remark

4.2.2
. In our work, they are constructed as special points in the o-minimal spectrum of the extension
XF
of
X
to
\mathfrak F
, see
Section

4.2
. Those points admit a natural valuation extending the valuation of
\mathbb F
, see
Lemma

4.3.1
, which is used to define the set in point (ii) in Theorem
A
.

Part (iii) characterizes the points of
\Trop(X)
in terms of real initial degenerations, in analogy to the case of algebraic varieties. The initial degenerations
\initial_{w}(XF)
are closed definable subsets in the residue field of
\mathbb F
, see
Proposition

3.1.2
. Furthermore, we show that
\initial_{w}(X_{\mathfrak F})
can be described by the initial forms of polynomials contained in the nonnegativity cone
\mathcal{P}(X)
of
X
(Proposition
3.3.4
). Thus, in the definable setting, initial nonnegativity cones play a role analogous to that of initial ideals in algebraic geometry.

Part (v) further strengthens this analogy. There, we consider the real tropicalizations of polynomials that are nonnegative on
X
. These real tropical polynomials have coefficients in the real tropical hyperfield, which record both the sign and the valuation of the coefficients of the original polynomials. The idea of using such real tropicalizations was introduced in
[
28
]
. Indeed, in the special case of semialgebraic sets, the equivalence of conditions (i), (ii), (iv), and (v) was already established in
[
28
, Th. 6.9]
.

Our techniques for the proof of
A
combine the ideas from
[
28
]
with results in model theory and o-minimal geometry. The study in
[
59
,
62
]
of valued real closed fields is particularly important, together with the o-minimal spectrum, for which we refer to the many references in
Section

4.1
. The o-minimal spectrum plays the role of the real spectrum in semialgebraic geometry, see
[
14
,
57
]
.

Another useful ingredient for the proof of
A
(and for understanding tropicalizations of definable sets in general) is to enrich the tropicalization map to retain information beyond the valuation. This idea has already appeared in several forms in the literature. For example, the phase tropicalization map records not only the valuation of an element but also its phase
[
39
,
40
,
67
,
30
]
.
In the real setting, the phase reduces to the sign of an element, thus it does not provide any additional information, since we restrict our attention to the positive orthant.
To obtain a refinement of the tropicalization, we instead follow
[
38
]
and enrich the valuation by recording the full angular components. We rephrase the ideas of
[
38
]
to the real closed setting using the RV sort structure in model theory, see e.g.
[
23
]
.

We define the
fine tropicalization
of a definable set as its image under the coordinate-wise Residual Value sort map
\rv\colon\mathbb F^{\times}\to\mathbb F^{\times}/(1+\mathfrak{m}F)
. Here,
\mathfrak{m}F
denotes the maximal ideal of the valuation ring of
\mathbb F
. If we fix a splitting of
\mathbb F
, there is an associated notion of
angular component
, denoted
\ac
, and
\rv\cong\ac\times\val
(see
Section

2.1
).
In this note, we prove an analogue of the Fundamental Theorem in the fine tropical setting. The equality of (iii) and (iv) below in
B
is a real counterpart of
[
38
, Theorem 5.2]
. To make the analogy more explicit, we state here the theorem in the case
\mathbb F=\mathbb{R}((t{R}))
, the field of Hahn series with real exponents. Moreover, we describe the fine tropicalization in terms of archimedean points of the o-minimal spectrum, and by using the
\mathrm{RV}
-tropicalization of nonnegative polynomials, whose coefficients live in the
\mathrm{RV}
-tropical hyperfield.

Theorem B

(Fundamental Theorem of Fine Tropical O-minimal Geometry, see
Theorem

4.5.2
)
.

Let
\mathbb F=\mathbb{R}((t{R}))
be the field of Hahn series and let
\mathfrak F
be a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of
\mathbb F
.
Then, for any closed definable subset
X\subset\mathbb F_{>0}^{n}
, the following sets are equal:

(i)

\ftrop(\widetilde{XF}^{\mathrm{arch}})
;

(ii)

\bigcapop\displaylimits_{f\in\mathcal{P}(XF)}\{z\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\trop_{\rv}(f)(z)\geq 0\}
;

(iii)

\rv(XF)
;

(iv)

\bigcupop\displaylimits_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\initial_{w}(XF)\times\{w\}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some basic notions and set up the notation for valued real closed fields, hyperfields, and model theory. In Section
3.1
, we begin our investigation of the tropicalization of definable sets. Section
3.2
and
3.3
are devoted to the tropicalizations and initial forms of polynomials, and to their relationship with initial degenerations. In Section
3.4
, we firsty prove weak versions of Theorems
A
,
B
, without considering the o-minimal spectrum and the closure in
A
, (iii).

In Section
4
, we turn our attention to the closure and relate the points in the closure to the archimedean points of the o-minimal spectrum of the definable set. After discussing several characterizations of the o-minimal spectrum in Section
4.1
, we show that the residue fields corresponding to archimedean points admit a unique valuation, which we will use to define the tropicalization and
\rv
-tropicalization maps. This is the content of Sections
4.2
and
4.3
. In Section
4.4
, we prove the Strong Density Theorem (
Theorem

4.4.7
) and other key technical ingredients for the proofs of Theorems
A
and
B
, which is concluded in Section
4.5
.

2.1.
Real closed fields and valuations

A
real field
is a field
\mathbb{K}
that admits an
ordering

\leq
, that is, a total order
\leq
on
\mathbb{K}
that is compatible with the field operations: for all
a,b,c\in\mathbb{K}
, we have

\displaystyle a\leq b\Rightarrow a+c\leq b+c,\quad\text{ and }\quad a\leq b,\,0\leq c\Rightarrow ac\leq bc.

For an
ordered field

(\mathbb{K},\leq)
, we write
\mathbb{K}_{>0}
for the set of elements
a\in\mathbb{K}
with
a>0
, and define an
absolute value
as
\absolutevalue{a}\coloneqq\max\{a,-a\}
.
A field
\mathbb{K}
is
real closed
if it admits a unique ordering and every odd degree polynomial with coefficients in
\mathbb{K}
has a root in
\mathbb{K}
. We refer the reader to
[
5
, Sec. 2.2]
or
[
51
, Sec. 1.2]
for other equivalent characterizations. The inclusion
\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb{F}
is an
extension of real closed fields
if
\mathbb{K}
and
\mathbb F
are real closed fields and the ordering on
\mathbb{F}
, when restricted to
\mathbb{K}
, coincides with the ordering on
\mathbb{K}
.

In tropical geometry, we are interested in fields
\mathbb{F}
equipped with a
valuation map

\val\colon\mathbb{F}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
, i.e. a map satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\val(a)=\infty
if and only if
a=0
;

(2)

\val(ab)=\val(a)+\val(a)
; and

(3)

\val(a+b)\geq\min\{\,\val(a),\val(b)\,\}
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{F}
.

Condition (3) is called the
ultrametric inequality
.
We denote by
\Gamma_{\mathbb F}\coloneqq\val(\mathbb{F}^{\times})
the
value group
of
\mathbb{F}
, which is an additive subgroup of
(\mathbb{R},+)
.
If
\Gamma_{\mathbb F}\neq\{0\}
, the valuation is called
nontrivial
. The
valuation ring
and the
valuation ideal
are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}\coloneqq\{\,a\in\mathbb{F}\mid\val(a)\geq 0\,\},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{F}}\coloneqq\{\,a\in\mathbb{F}\mid\val(a)>0\,\}

respectively. The valuation ring is a local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbb{F}}
(see e.g.
[
31
, Proposition 2.4.5]
). We write
k_{\mathbb{F}}\coloneqq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}/\mathfrak{m}{F}
for the residue field and
\res_{\mathbb{F}}\colon\mathcal{O}{F}\to k{F}
for the natural projection map.

We call
\mathbb{F}
a
valued real closed field
, if the valuation is
compatible with the ordering
of
\mathbb{F}
, that is, if for all
a,b\in\mathbb{F}
we have

(1)

\displaystyle 0\leq a\leq b\Rightarrow\val(a)\geq\val(b).

This condition is satisfied if and only if the valuation ring
\mathcal{O}F
is a
convex subring
of
\mathbb F
. This means that, if
a\leq c\leq b
and
a,b\in\mathcal{O}F
, then
c\in\mathcal{O}F
.
In this case, the residue field
k{F}
is also a real closed field whose ordering is given by
\res_{\mathbb{F}}(a)\geq 0
whenever
a\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{\mathbb{F}}
and
a\geq 0

[
51
, Prop. 3.5.10]
. An
extension of valued real closed fields

\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb{F}
is an extension of real closed fields such that the valuation on
\mathbb{F}
restricts to the valuation on
\mathbb{K}
. We refer the reader to
[
31
,
51
]
for more details on order compatible valuations and convex subrings.

Example 2.1.1
.

Our primary example for a real closed field with a nontrivial order compatible valuation is the field of
Hahn series
with real coefficients and exponents, denoted
\mathbb{R}((t{R}))
, whose nonzero elements are formal series of the form

\displaystyle a=\sumop\displaylimits_{v\in\supp(a)}a_{v}t^{v},\qquad a_{v}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}

where

in
\mathbb{R}
. Then the valuation of

is given by

.
An element

The field of Hahn series

is a valued real closed extension of

is equipped with the trivial valuation, i.e.,
\val(a)=0
for all

. Although this case might seem "trivial", the study of tropicalizations over trivially valued real closed fields leads to interesting results and is worthwhile in its own right.

Assumption 2.1.2

Let
\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb F\subset\mathbb{M}
be an extension of valued real closed fields such that the valuation on
\mathbb F

\Gamma{M}=\mathbb{R}

2.2.
Hyperfields

Hyperfields are generalizations of fields, where addition can be a multivalued map. Their study goes back at least to the work of Krasner
[
32
,
33
]
, and their first appearance in tropical geometry occurred in works of Viro
[
66
,
67
]
. In this section, we recall some background on hyperfields that is necessary for Section
3.2
. Our exposition closely follows
[
38
]
.

Definition 2.2.1
.

A
hyperfield
is a tuple
(\mathbb{H},\oplus,\odot,0,1)
where
\mathbb{H}^{\times}\coloneqq\mathbb{H}\setminus\{0\}
,
(\mathbb{H}^{\times},\odot,1)
is an abelian group, and

\oplus\colon\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}\rightarrow 2^{\mathbb{H}}\setminus\{\emptyset\}

satisfies the following axioms:

•

Identity:
0\oplus x=\{x\}
for all
x\in\mathbb{H}
.

•

Unique Inverse: For all
x\in\mathbb{H}
, there is a unique
-x\in\mathbb{H}
such that
0\in x\oplus(-x)
.

•

Reversibility: For all
x,y,z\in\mathbb{H}
, we have
z\in x\oplus y
if and only if
y\in z\oplus(-x)
.

•

Commutativity:
x\oplus y=y\oplus x
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{H}
,

•

Associativity:
(x\oplus y)\oplus z=x\oplus(y\oplus z)
, where
(x\oplus y)\oplus z=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{u\in x\oplus y}u\oplus z
and
x\oplus(y\oplus z)=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{u\in y\oplus z}x\oplus u
.

•

Distributivity:
z\odot(x\oplus y)=z\odot x\oplus z\odot y
, where
z\odot(x\oplus y)=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{u\in x\oplus y}z\odot u
.

•

0\odot x=0
for all
x\in H
.

The simplest instance of hyperfields are ordinary fields. In this case, the hyperaddition
x\oplus y
always gives a singleton. In the next example, we present two additional examples that play an important role in tropical geometry.

Example 2.2.2
.

(a)

The
Krasner hyperfield
is the set
\mathbb{H}_{K}\coloneqq\{0,1\}
with multiplication
1\odot 1=1,\,0\odot 1=1\odot 0=0\odot 0=0
and hyperaddition defined as

0\oplus 0=\{0\},\qquad 0\oplus 1=1\oplus 0=\{1\},\qquad 1\oplus 1=\{0,1\}.

(b)

The
sign hyperfield
is the set
\mathbb{S}\coloneqq\{-1,0,1\}
with multiplicative group
\mathbb{S}^{\times}=\{-1,1\}
and hyperaddition defined as

0\oplus x=\{x\},\qquad x\oplus x=\{x\},\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{S}\qquad\text{ and }\quad 1\oplus-1=\{-1,0,1\}.

Following
[
38
, Def. 2.6]
, we define the
tropical extension of a hyperfield
as follows.

Definition 2.2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{H}
be a hyperfield and
(\Gamma,+)
an (additive) ordered abelian group. The
tropical extension of
\mathbb{H}
by
\Gamma
is the set

\mathbb{H}\rtimes\Gamma=\left\{\,(x,w)\mid x\in\mathbb{H}^{\times},w\in\Gamma\,\right\}\cup\{\,0\,\}

where multiplication is given by
\left(x_{1},w_{1}\right)\odot\left(x_{2},w_{2}\right)\coloneqq\left(x_{1}\odot_{\mathbb{H}}x_{2},w_{1}+w_{2}\right)
and addition is given by

\left(x_{1},w_{1}\right)\oplus\left(x_{2},w_{2}\right)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\left(x_{1},w_{1}\right)&w_{1}<w_{2},\\
\left(x_{2},w_{2}\right)&w_{2}<w_{1},\\
\left\{\,(x,w_{1})\mid x\in x_{1}\oplus_{\mathbb{H}}x_{2}\,\right\}&w_{1}=w_{2},\;0\notin x_{1}\oplus x_{2},\\
\left\{\,(x,w_{1})\mid x\in\left(x_{1}\oplus_{\mathbb{H}}x_{2}\right)\setminus\{0\}\,\right\}\cup\left\{\,(y,v)\mid v>w_{1},y\in\mathbb{H}^{\times}\,\right\}\cup\{0\}&w_{1}=w_{2},\;0\in x_{1}\oplus x_{2}.\end{cases}

Tropical extensions of hyperfields are again hyperfields. If
\mathbb{H}
is the Krasner hyperfield

, the sign hyperfield

or a field, then the tropical extension

is a

is multivalued if and only if

Example 2.2.4

Let

(a)

\left(x_{1},w_{1}\right)\oplus\left(x_{2},w_{2}\right)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\left(x_{1},w_{1}\right)&w_{1}<w_{2},\\

2.3.
Model theory and o-minimal geometry

Both of our main theorems (Theorems
A
and
B
) concern definable sets in a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of a real closed field. In this subsection, we recall the related necessary notions from model theory, following
[
58
,
37
]
.

Definition 2.3.1
.

A
structure
on a nonempty set
\mathcal{R}
is a sequence
S=(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

•

S_{n}
is a boolean algebra of subsets of
\mathcal{R}^{n}
, i.e. a collection of subsets of
\mathcal{R}^{n}
containing
\emptyset
such that, if
A,B\in S_{n}
, then
A\cup B\in S_{n}
and
\mathcal{R}^{n}\setminus A\in S_{n}
;

•

if
A\in S_{n}
, then
A\times\mathcal{R}\in S_{n+1}
and
\mathcal{R}\times A\in S_{n+1}
;

•

\{\,(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathcal{R}^{n}\mid a_{1}=a_{n}\,\}\in S_{n}
;

•

if
\pi\colon\mathcal{R}^{n+1}\to\mathcal{R}^{n}
is the projection onto the first
n
coordinates and
A\in S_{n+1}
, then
\pi(A)\in S_{n}
.

Given a structure
S
on
\mathcal{R}
, we say that
A\subset\mathcal{R}^{n}
is
definable
if
A\in S_{n}
. We say that a function is definable if its graph is definable.

We are interested in sets equipped with an ordering, such as real closed fields and, more generally, ordered groups. A (linearly) ordered set
(\mathcal{R},<)
is called
dense
if for all
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
with
a<b
, there exists
c\in\mathcal{R}
such that
a<c<b
. We say that
\mathcal{R}
has
no endpoints
if
\mathcal{R}
has no smallest or largest element.
Following the French tradition, we denote by
]a,b[
the open intervals in
\mathcal{R}
, and by
[a,b]
the closed intervals. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we equip
\mathcal{R}^{n}
with the box topology, i.e. the topology generated by the open sets of the form
]a_{1},b_{1}[\,\times\dots\times\,]a_{n},b_{n}[
. In case
\mathcal{R}
is a real closed field, we also call the box topology the
strong topology
, as usual.

Definition 2.3.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{R},<)
be a dense linearly ordered nonempty set without endpoints. We call a structure
S
on
(\mathcal{R},<)

order-minimal
(or
o-minimal
) if

•

\{\,(a,b)\in\mathcal{R}^{2}\mid a<b\,\}\in S_{2}
;

•

the sets in
S_{1}
are exactly finite unions of (possibly unbounded) intervals and points.

A structure
S
on a set
\mathcal{R}
is usually introduced specifying relations and functions that "generate"
S
. For instance, the structure over a real closed field
\mathbb{K}
generated by addition, multiplication, and by
\mathbb{K}
(identified with the set of functions from
\mathbb{K}^{0}=\{0\}
to
\mathbb{K}
) will consist exactly of semialgebraic sets.

Definition 2.3.3
.

A
model-theoretic structure

\mathfrak R=(\mathcal{R},(R_{i})_{i\in I},(f_{j})_{j\in J})
consists of a nonempty set
\mathcal{R}
, of relations
R_{i}\subset\mathcal{R}^{m(i)}
for
i\in I
and
m(i)\in\mathbb{N}
, and of functions
f_{j}\colon\mathcal{R}^{n(j)}\to\mathcal{R}
for
j\in J
and
n(j)\in\mathbb{N}
. Given a model-theoretic structure
\mathfrak R
on
\mathcal{R}
, we denote
\Def(\mathfrak R)
to be the smallest structure on
\mathcal{R}
that contains each relation and the graph of each function in
\mathfrak R
. Moreover, for a subset
C\subset\mathcal{R}
, we denote
\mathfrak R_{C}
the model-theoretic structure obtained by adding to the functions of
\mathfrak R
the constant functions
c\colon\mathcal{R}^{0}\to\mathcal{R}
for
c\in C
. A set
A
is
definable in
\mathfrak R
with parameters from
C
, or simply
C
-definable
, if it is definable in
\mathfrak R_{C}
.

Definition 2.3.4
.

(a)

Let
(\mathcal{R},<)
be a dense linearly ordered group without endpoints. A model-theoretic structure
\mathfrak R=(\mathcal{R},(R_{i})_{i\in I},(f_{j})_{j\in J})
is called
o-minimal
if

<
gives a relation
R_{i_{*}}
for some
i_{*}\in I
, and
\Def(\mathfrak R_{\mathcal{R}})
is an o-minimal structure.

(b)

An o-minimal structure
\mathfrak R=(\mathcal{R},(R_{i})_{i\in I},(f_{j})_{j\in J})

expands
an ordered abelian group
\mathcal{R}
(respectively an ordered abelian ring
\mathcal{R}
) if there are functions
0\colon\mathcal{R}^{0}\to\mathcal{R}
,
+\colon\mathcal{R}^{2}\to\mathcal{R}
,
-\colon\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{R}
(and
\cdot\colon\mathcal{R}^{2}\to\mathcal{R}
for an ordered abelian ring) that are definable and such that
(\mathcal{R},0,+,-)
is an abelian group (respectively such that
(\mathcal{R},0,+,-,\cdot)
is an ordered abelian ring).

Definable sets in o-minimal expansions of a real closed fields enjoy most of the properties that usual semialgebriac sets have. We refer the reader to
[
58
,
16
]
for detailed accounts of such properties.

Next, we recall an equivalent characterization of definable sets.
A

\mathcal{L}

and a set of

m_{R}

is
n_{f}
-ary).
0

\mathcal{L}_{C}
the extension of

with the constant symbols

.

If
\mathcal{L}
is a language with relation symbols

and function symbols

\mathcal{L}
-structure
is a model-theoretic structure
\mathfrak R=(\mathcal{R},(R_{i}^{\mathcal{R}})_{i\in I},(f_{j}^{\mathcal{R}})_{j\in J})

An
\mathcal{L}
-term

We will write

We conclude this subsection by discussing a crucial assumption on o-minimal structures needed to prove Theorem

Definition 2.3.7
.

Let
\mathfrak F
be an o-minimal expansion of a real closed field
(\mathbb F,<,+,\,\cdot\,)
. A
power function
of
\mathfrak F
is a definable function
f\colon\mathbb F_{>0}\to\mathbb F_{>0}
, not identically zero, such that
f(rs)=f(r)f(s)
for all
r,s\in\mathbb F_{>0}
. The set of power functions is a field with the operations of multiplication and composition.

The derivation map gives an embedding
f\mapsto f^{\prime}(1)
of the field of power functions into
\mathbb F
. The image of this map is called
field of exponents
, which we denote by
\Lambda=\Lambda(\mathfrak F)
.
For
\lambda=f^{\prime}(1)
, we write
f=x^{\lambda}
and
f(s)=s^{\lambda}
. The usual properties of monomial powers still hold true:
(rs)^{\lambda}=r^{\lambda}s^{\lambda}
,
(s^{\lambda_{1}})^{\lambda_{2}}=s^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}
,
s^{\lambda_{1}}s^{\lambda_{2}}=s^{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}
,. We say that
\mathfrak F
is
power bounded
if for every definable function
f\colon\mathbb F\to\mathbb F
there exists
\lambda\in\Lambda
such that
\absolutevalue{f(s)}\leq s^{\lambda}
for all
s\in\mathbb F
sufficiently large. We say that
\mathfrak F
is
polynomially bounded
if it is power bounded and the field of exponents is archimedean, i.e.,
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 2.3.8
.

Let
\mathfrak F
be a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of a real closed field
\mathbb F
, and equip
\mathbb F
with an order compatible nontrivial valuation.
After fixing a splitting and normalizing the valuation as in Assumption
2.1.2
, it follows that

\val(t^{\lambda})=\lambda\val(t)=\lambda.

In particular, the field of exponents
\Lambda(\mathfrak F)
is contained in the value group
\Gamma F
.

For a list of polynomially bounded o-minimal structures, we refer the reader to
[
60
, p. 505]
. Semialgebraic sets are the most elementary example. For an equivalent characterizations of polynomially bounded o-minimal structures, see
[
41
,
42
]
. We have the following explicit description of definable functions in polynomially bounded o-minimal structures.

Theorem 2.3.9

(Preparation theorem for polynomially bounded o-minimal structures
[

,
[

, p. 85]

.

Let
\mathfrak F

(or equivalently, a convex valuation ring
\mathcal{O}F
), then the residue field
kF
inherits an
\mathcal{L}
-structure
\mathfrak{k}F
from
\mathfrak F
, see
[
59
, (2.6) Rem.]
. The structure
\mathfrak{k}F
arises by identifying
k_{\mathbb F}
with any subfield of
\mathcal{O}F
maximal with respect to inclusion.

We can also induce a structure
\Gamma_{\mathbb F,\mathrm{vg}}
on the value group
\Gamma F
, by introducing a relation symbol for the image under the valuation map of every definable (without paramaters) function and relation in
\mathfrak F
, see
[
59
, (3.15)]
. The structure
\Gamma_{\mathbb F,\mathrm{vg}}
is o-minimal in the polynomially bounded case
[
59
, (4.5) Cor.]
. The value group
\Gamma_{\mathbb F}
is an ordered vector space over the field of exponents
\Lambda=\Lambda(\mathfrak F)
(which we identify as a subspace of
\Gamma F
as in
Remark

2.3.8
). It is proven in
[
62
, Th. B]
that the definable sets in
\Gamma_{\mathbb F,\mathrm{vg}}
coincide exactly with the definable subsets of
\Gamma F
, seen as an ordered vector space over
\Lambda
. Such sets are called
semilinear
, and we refer the reader to
[
58
, p. 25–28]
for their precise description.

Finally, we note that some of the results of
[
59
,
62
]
have been recently rediscovered in
[
2
, Sec. 3 and 4]
in the more restrictive semialgebraic setting.

3.
Tropicalization and initial degenerations

3.1.
Tropicalizing definable sets

In this section, we begin investigating the tropicalization of definable sets. From now on we will work under the following assumption.

Assumption 3.1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak K\prec\mathfrak F\prec\mathfrak M
be elementary extensions of polynomially bounded o-minimal expansions of the real closed fields
\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb F\subset\mathbb{M}
. Furthermore, assume that
\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb F\subset\mathbb{M}
is an extension of valued real closed fields as in Assumption
2.1.2
.

The
tropicalization
of a definable set
X\subset\mathbb{K}_{>0}^{n}
is defined as

(8)

\displaystyle\Trop(X)\coloneqq\overline{\val(X_{\mathfrak F})}

where the closure is taken in the euclidean topology of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In the following, we investigate which points are added to
\val(XF)
when taking its closure.

Proposition 3.1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak F
be a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of a real closed field
\mathbb F
, equipped with nontrivial order compatible valuation
\val\colon\mathbb F^{\times}\to\Gamma F
. Then, for
X\subset\mathbb F^{n}_{>0}
definable,
\val(X)
is a semilinear closed subset of
\Gamma^{n}F
, and
\val(X)=\val(\closure\left({X}\right))
(where the closure is the relative closure of
X
in
\mathbb F_{>0}^{n}
).

Proof.

From
Section

2.4
,
\val(X)
is a semilinear subset
\Gamma F^{n}
.
By
[
59
, (4.5) Cor.]
, the structure on
\Gamma F
induced by the valuation is o-minimal. Take
w
to be in the closure of
{\val(X)}
in
\Gamma F^{n}
. By the o-minimal curve selection lemma, see e.g.
[
16
, Th. 3.2]
, there exists a continuous definable curve

\phi\colon\Gamma F\supset[0,v]\longrightarrow\val(X)\subset\Gamma^{n}F

such that
\phi(]0,v])\subset X
and
\phi(0)=w
. Up to extending the language of
(\mathfrak F,\mathcal{O}F)
as in
[
62
, p. 21]
, we have definable Skolem functions
[
62
, Rem. 2.7]
, i.e. definable sections of maps always exist (see
[
37
, Ex. 2.5.25]
for the precise definition). Therefore there exists a continuous definable function

f\colon\mathbb F\supset[0,t^{v}]\longrightarrow\closure\left({X}\right)

(where the closure is taken inside
\mathbb F^{n}_{>0}
) such that
\val(f(a))=\phi(\val(a))
and
\phi(]0,t^{v}])\subset X
. Definable sets in the value group are precisely
\Gamma
-affine
\Lambda
-integral functions; hence there exists
\lambda\in\Lambda^{n}
and
b\in\Gamma F^{n}
such that, for all
\gamma\in[0,v]
small enough,
\phi(\gamma)=\gamma\lambda+b
. But then, for all
a
small enough:

\val(a)\lambda+b=\phi(\val(a))=\val(f(a))\xrightarrow{a\to 0}\val(f(0))\in\val(\closure\left({X}\right))\subset\Gamma F^{n}

Since
\val(a)\to\infty
as
a\to 0
, continuity of
\phi
forces
\lambda=0
.
This means that the valuation
\val(f(a))
is finally constant and equal to
w=\val(b)
. Hence there exists
a\in X
such that
\val(a)=w
, proving that
{\val(X)}
is closed.

For the last statement, recall that
\closure\left({X}\right)
is definable and that
\val\colon\mathbb F^{n}_{>0}\to\Gamma^{n}F
is continuous, and then
\val(X)=\overline{\val(X)}=\overline{\val(\closure\left({X}\right))}=\val(\closure\left({X}\right))
.
∎

We will need the following weak version of the strong density theorem, (c.f.
Theorem

4.4.7
), which follows from quantifier elimination.

Proposition 3.1.3
.

Let
\mathfrak F\prec\mathfrak M
and
\mathbb F\subset\mathbb{M}
be as in Assumption
3.1.1
. Then, for
X\subset\mathbb F^{n}
definable:

\displaystyle\val(XM)\cap\Gamma_{\mathbb F}^{n}=\val(X)

Proof.

If a structure is polynomially bounded, then its convex valuation ring is
T
-convex in the sense of
[
59
,
62
]
. Up to extending the language of valued real closed fields as in
[
59
, Rem. 3.11]
, the quantifier elimination theorem holds true
[
59
, Rem. 3.11]
, and then the set
\val(XF)\subset\Gamma F^{n}
is defined by a quantifier-free formula. By model completeness, which follows from quantifier elimination (see e.g.
[
47
, Sec. 3.3 and Th. 3.4.3]
), quantifier free formulas which are true over
\mathfrak M
are true over
\mathfrak F
. Hence, if
w\in\val(XM)\cap\Gamma F^{n}
then there exists
a\in X
such that
\val F(a_{i})=w_{i}
for
i\in[n]
.
∎

We finish this section by showing that no additional
\Gamma F
-valued points are added to the tropicalization in (
8
) when taking the closure.

Corollary 3.1.4
.

Let
\mathfrak F
be a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of a real closed field
\mathbb F
, equipped with a nontrivial order compatible valuation
\val\colon\mathbb F^{\times}\to\Gamma_{\mathbb F}
. For each definable set
X\subset\mathbb F^{n}_{>0}
, we have

\displaystyle\val(X)=\overline{\val(X)}\cap\Gamma{F}^{n}

where the closure is taken in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak F\prec\mathfrak M
be an elementary extension equipped with a valuation extending the valuation of
\mathbb F
such that
\val(\mathbb{M}^{\times})=\mathbb{R}
. Such an extension exists by
[
59
, (4.4) Prop.]
.
The claim follows by considering the following chain of inclusions

\displaystyle\val(X)\subset\overline{\val(X)}\cap\Gamma F^{n}\overset{(1)}{=}\overline{\val(XM)\cap\Gamma F^{n}}\cap\Gamma F^{n}\subset\overline{\val(XM)}\cap\overline{\Gamma F^{n}}\cap\Gamma F^{n}\overset{(2)}{\subset}\val(XM)\cap\Gamma F^{n}\overset{(3)}{=}\val(X),

where
(1)
and
(3)
follows from Proposition
3.1.3
and
(2)
holds by Proposition
3.1.2
.
∎

3.2.
Tropicalizations and the initial form of a polynomial

In this section, we assume that
\mathbb F
is a nontrivially valued real closed field and
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a field of exponents.
The
real tropicalization
and the
tropicalization
of a polynomial
f=\sumop\displaylimits_{\lambda}c_{\lambda}\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}\in\mathbb F[\mathbf{x}^{\Lambda}]
are respectively defined as

\displaystyle\trop_{r}(f)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\lambda}\left(\sign(c_{\lambda}),\val(c_{\lambda})\right)\odot\overline{\mathbf{w}}^{\lambda}\in\mathbb{RT}F[\overline{\mathbf{w}}^{\Lambda}]

\displaystyle\trop(f)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\lambda}\val(c_{\lambda})\odot\mathbf{w}^{\lambda}\in\mathbb{T}F[\mathbf{w}^{\Lambda}]

where the addition and multiplications are interpreted in the hyperfields
\mathbb{RT}F
and
\mathbb{T}F
respectively, cf. Section
2.2
.
Following
[
28
, Section 5.2]
, we write

(9)

\displaystyle\trop_{r}(f)(\overline{w})<0:\Longleftrightarrow\trop_{r}(f)(\overline{w})\cap(\{1\}\times\Gamma\cup\{\infty\})=\emptyset,

\displaystyle\trop_{r}(f)(\overline{w})\geq 0:\Longleftrightarrow\trop_{r}(f)(\overline{w})\cap(\{1\}\times\Gamma\cup\{\infty\})\neq\emptyset.

These conventions are chosen precisely so that
\trop_{r}(f)(\overline{w})\geq 0
and
\trop_{r}(f)(\overline{w})<0
are logical negations of each another.
In light of these definitions, it is natural to introduce the
RV-tropicalization
of
f
as

(10)

\displaystyle\trop_{\rv}(f)(z)\coloneqq\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\lambda}\rv(c_{\lambda})\odot z^{\lambda}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\lambda}(\ac(c_{\lambda}),\val(c_{\lambda}))\odot z^{\lambda}\in\mathrm{RV}F[\mathbf{z}^{\Lambda}]

where now we interpret the addition and the multiplication in the
\mathrm{RV}
-tropical hyperfield. Similarly to (
9
), using the ordering on
\mathrm{RV}F
discussed in Section
2.1
, for
z\in\mathrm{RV}F
we have

\displaystyle\trop_{\rv}(f)(z)<0\Longleftrightarrow\trop_{\rv}(f)(z)\cap(k_{\mathbb F,>0}\times\Gamma F\cup\{0\})=\emptyset,

\displaystyle\trop_{\rv}(f)(z)\geq 0\Longleftrightarrow\trop_{\rv}(f)(z)\cap(k_{\mathbb F,>0}\times\Gamma F\cup\{0\})\neq\emptyset.

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Consider the hyperfield morphism
\rv\colon\mathbb{F}\to\mathrm{RV}F

from Lemma
2.2.5
and extend this coordinate wise to
\rv\colon\mathbb{F}_{>0}^{n}\to\mathrm{RV}_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}
.
For a polynomial
f=\sumop\displaylimits_{\lambda}c_{\lambda}\mathbf{x}^{\lambda}\in\mathbb F[\mathbf{x}^{\Lambda}]
and for each
z\in\mathrm{RV}_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\rv\left(f\left(\rv^{-1}(z)\right)\right){\subset}\trop_{\rv}(f)(z).

Proof.

Let
u\in\rv(f(\rv^{-1}(z)))
. Hence there exists
a\in\rv^{-1}(z)
such that

u=\rv(f(a))=\rv(\sumop\displaylimits_{\lambda}c_{\lambda}a^{\lambda})\subset\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\lambda}\rv(c_{\lambda}a^{\lambda})=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\lambda}\rv(c_{\lambda})\odot\rv(a)^{\lambda}=\trop_{\rv}(z)

where the inclusion follows since
\rv
is a hyperfield morphism.
∎

One might be tempted to believe that the converse inclusion holds in Lemma
3.2.1
. The following example shows that this is not the case.

Example 3.2.2
.

Consider the polynomial
f=(x-1)^{2}=1-2x+x^{2}\in\mathbb F[x]
. Since
f(a)\geq 0
for all
a\in\mathbb F_{>0}^{n}
, we have
\rv(f(\rv^{-1}(z)))\subset\mathrm{RV}_{\mathbb F,>0}\cup\{0\}
for each
z\in\mathrm{RV}_{\mathbb F,>0}
. On the other hand, consider the RV-tropicalization

\trop_{\rv}(f)(z)=(1,0)\odot z^{0}\oplus(-2,0)\odot z\oplus(1,0)\odot z^{2}.

Evaluating this polynomial at
z=(1,0)
we have

\trop_{\rv}(f)(1,0)=(1,0)\oplus(-2,0)\oplus(1,0)=\left\{\,(y,v)\mid v\in\Gamma F,\,v>0,\,y\in k^{\times}F\,\right\}\cup\{0\},

which strictly contains the set
\mathrm{RV}_{\mathbb F,>0}\cup\{0\}=(k_{\mathbb F,>0}\times\Gamma F)\cup\{0\}
.

Definition 3.2.3
.

The
initial form
of a polynomial
f=\sumop\displaylimits_{\lambda}c_{\lambda}x^{\lambda}\in\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{x}^{\Lambda}]
is defined as

\displaystyle\initial_{w}(f)(y)\coloneqq\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Lambda^{n}\\
\val(c_{\lambda})+w\cdot\lambda=W\end{subarray}}\res_{\mathbb{F}}\big(c_{\lambda}t^{-\val(c_{\lambda})}\big)\;y^{\lambda}\in kF[\mathbf{y}^{\Lambda}],\quad\text{ where }W=\min\{\,\val(c_{\lambda})+w\cdot\lambda\mid\lambda\in\Lambda^{n},\,c_{\lambda}\neq 0\,\}.

To study initial forms and initial degenerations, it is convenient to consider the twist of an element
b\in\mathbb F^{n}_{>0}

by a vector
w\in\Gamma F^{n}
. To that end, we introduce the following notation.

t^{w}.b\coloneqq(t^{w_{1}}b_{1},\dots,t^{w_{n}}b_{n})\in\mathbb F^{n}_{>0}.

For a set
X\subset\mathbb F^{n}_{>0}
, we also write
t^{w}.X\coloneqq\{\,t^{w}.b\mid b\in X\,\}
.
In the next lemma, we give a description of initial forms using the angular component map. Switching between these two descriptions will be useful for relating initial forms to
\mathrm{RV}
-tropicalizations.

Lemma 3.2.4
.

Let
f=\sumop\displaylimits_{\lambda}c_{\lambda}x^{\lambda}\in\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{x}^{\Lambda}]
be a polynomial and
W\coloneqq\min\{\,\val(c_{\lambda})+w\cdot\lambda\mid\lambda\in\Lambda^{n},\,c_{\lambda}\neq 0\,\}
. For all
y\in k_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}
and
b\in\mathcal{O}^{n}_{\mathbb F}
with
\res F(b)=y
, we have

\displaystyle\initial_{w}(f)(y)\;=\;\res_{\mathbb{F}}\big(t^{-W}f(t^{w}.b)\big)\;=\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Lambda^{n}\\
\val(c_{\lambda})+w\cdot\lambda=W\end{subarray}}\ac F(c_{\lambda})\;y^{\lambda}.

Proof.

The first equality is well-known in the tropical geometry literature, and it follows directly from

\initial_{w}(f)(y)=\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Lambda^{n}\\
\val(c_{\lambda})+w\cdot\lambda=W\end{subarray}}\res_{\mathbb{F}}\big(c_{\lambda}t^{w\cdot\lambda-W}\big)\res_{\mathbb{F}}(\;b)^{\lambda}=\sumop\displaylimits_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{n}}\res_{\mathbb{F}}\big(c_{\lambda}t^{w\cdot\lambda-W}\big)\res_{\mathbb{F}}(\;b^{\lambda})=\res_{\mathbb{F}}\big(t^{-W}f(t^{w}.b)\big)

since
\res_{\mathbb{F}}\big(c_{\lambda}t^{w\cdot\lambda-W}\big)=0
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda^{n}
with
w\cdot\lambda-W>0
.

The second equality follows by observing that
\ac F(c_{\lambda})=\res F(t^{-\val(c_{\lambda})}c_{\lambda})
.
∎

The initial form and the
\mathrm{RV}
-tropicalization of a polynomial are related as follows.

Lemma 3.2.5
.

Let
f=\sumop\displaylimits_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{n}}c_{\lambda}x^{\lambda}\in\mathbb F[\mathbf{x}^{\Lambda}]
be a polynomial and
W\coloneqq\min\{\,\val(c_{\lambda})+w\cdot\lambda\mid\lambda\in\Lambda^{n},\,c_{\lambda}\neq 0\,\}
. For each
(y,w)\in\mathrm{RV}_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)=\begin{cases}\{\,(\initial_{w}(f)(y),W)\,\}\quad&\text{ if }\initial_{w}(f)(y)\neq 0\\
\{(s,v)\mid v\in\Gamma F,v>W,s\in k^{\times}F\}\cup\{0\}\quad&\text{ if }\initial_{w}(f)(y)=0.\end{cases}

Proof.

Using the definition of the hyperfield operations in
\mathrm{RV}F
from Example
2.2.4
, we rewrite
\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)
:

\displaystyle\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)

\displaystyle=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{n}}(\ac F(c_{\lambda}),\val(c_{\lambda}))\odot(y,w)^{\lambda}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{n}}(\ac F(c_{\lambda})y^{\lambda},\val(c_{\lambda})+\lambda\cdot w)

\displaystyle\overset{(*)}{=}\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Lambda^{n}\\
\val(c_{\lambda})+w\cdot\lambda=W\end{subarray}}(\ac F(c_{\lambda})y^{\lambda},W)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\{\,(\sumop\displaylimits_{\val(c_{\lambda})+w\cdot\lambda=W}\ac F(c_{\lambda})\;y^{\lambda},W)\,\}\quad&\text{ if }\sumop\displaylimits_{\val(c_{\lambda})+w\cdot\lambda=W}\ac F(c_{\lambda})\;y^{\lambda}\neq 0\\
\{(s,v)\mid v\in\Gamma F,v>W,s\in k^{\times}F\}\cup\{0\},\quad&\text{ if }\sumop\displaylimits_{\val(c_{\lambda})+w\cdot\lambda=W}\ac F(c_{\lambda})\;y^{\lambda}=0\end{cases}

The equality
(*)
follows from Lemma
2.2.6
. Using Lemma
3.2.4
, we conclude the proof by observing that
\initial_{w}(f)(y)=\sumop\displaylimits_{\val(c_{\lambda})+w\cdot\lambda=W}\ac F(c_{\lambda})\;y^{\lambda}
.
∎

We conclude this section with a technical result that will be used in the proofs of
Theorems

3.4.1
and
3.4.9
.

Proposition 3.2.6
.

Let
f=\sumop\displaylimits_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{n}}c_{\lambda}x^{\lambda}\in\mathbb F[\mathbf{x}^{\Lambda}]
be a polynomial and let
w\in\Gamma F^{n}
.

(a)

For all
(y,w)\in\mathrm{RV}_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}
, we have
\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)\geq 0
if and only if
\initial_{w}(f)(y)\geq 0
.

(b)

If
\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)\geq 0
for some
y\in k_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}
, then
\trop_{r}(f)(1,w)\geq 0
.

(c)

If
\trop_{r}(f)(1,w)<0
, then
\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)<0
for all
y\in k_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}
.

Proof.

We prove (a) by applying repeatedly
Lemma

3.2.5
. Let
W=\min\{\,\val(c_{\lambda})+w\cdot\lambda\mid\lambda\in\Lambda^{n},\,c_{\lambda}\neq 0\,\}
.
If
\initial_{w}(f)(y)>0
, then
\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)=\{\,(\initial_{w}(f)(y),W)\,\}
by
Lemma

3.2.5
, and
\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)>0
.
If
\initial_{w}(f)(y)=0
, using again
Lemma

3.2.5
we have that
\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)\cap(k_{\mathbb F,>0}\times\Gamma F\cup\{0\})\neq\emptyset
, which by definition is equivalent to
\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)\geq 0
.
Now assume that
\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)\geq 0
. If
0\in\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)
, then
Lemma

3.2.5
implies that
\initial_{w}(f)(y)=0
.
If
0\notin\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)
, then again by
Lemma

3.2.5
we have
\initial_{w}(f)(y)>0
.

To prove part (b), consider the hyperfield morphism
\phi\colon\mathrm{RV}F\to\mathbb{RT}F
from
Lemma

2.2.5
. Let
y\in k_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}
such that
\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)\geq 0
, that is,
\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)\cap(k_{\mathbb F,>0}\times\Gamma F\cup\{0\}))\neq\emptyset
. Therefore

\emptyset\neq\phi\big(\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)\cap(k_{\mathbb F,>0}\times\Gamma F\cup\{0\})\big)\subset\phi\big(\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)\big)\cap\big(\{1\}\times\Gamma F\cup\{0\}\big).

Since
\phi
is a hyperfield morphism and
\phi(y,w)=(1,w)
, we also have

\displaystyle\phi(\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w))=\phi\left(\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\lambda}(\ac F(c_{\lambda}),\val(c_{\lambda}))\odot(y,w)^{\lambda}\right)\subset\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\lambda}(\sign(c_{\lambda}),\val(c_{\lambda}))\odot(1,w)^{\lambda}=\trop_{r}(f)(1,w)

which implies that the intersection
\trop_{r}(f)(1,w)\cap(\{1\}\times\Gamma\cup\{0\})
is nonempty, showing that
\trop_{r}(f)(1,w)\geq 0
. Part (c) is simply the negation of (b).
∎

3.3.
Initial degenerations of definable sets

Initial degenerations of algebraic varieties have been studied extensively in tropical geometry
[
52
,
53
,
44
,
29
,
24
]
.
In this section, we extend the study of initial degenerations to definable sets.
Let
\mathbb F
be a nontrivially valued real closed field.
Inspired by
[
1
, Sec. 4.3]
, we call

(11)

\displaystyle\initial_{w}(X)\coloneqq\res F(t^{-w}.X\cap(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb F,>0}^{\times})^{n})\subset k_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}

the
initial degeneration
of a set
X\subset\mathbb F^{n}_{>0}
with respect to
w\in\Gamma_{\mathbb F}^{n}
.

Proposition 3.3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak F
be a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of a real closed field
\mathbb F
, equipped with a nontrivial order compatible valuation. Then, for any definable set
X\subset\mathbb F_{>0}^{n}
and
w\in\Gamma F
, the set
\initial_{w}(X)
is a closed subset of
k_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}
, which is definable in
\mathfrak{k}F
(see
Section

2.4
for the notation) and satisfies
\dim\initial_{w}(X)\leq\dim X
.

Proof.

For any
a\in\mathbb F_{>0}
, the set
a.X
is a definable subset of
\mathbb F_{>0}^{n}
of the same dimension of
X
. Then, by
[
62
, Th. A]
,
\res F(a.X\cap(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb F,>0}^{\times})^{n})
is a closed definable subset of
k_{>0}^{n}
of dimension at most
\dim a.X=\dim X
. The resul then follows by choosing
a=t^{-w}
.
∎

To our best knowledge,
Proposition

3.3.1
did not appear previously in the literature even for the semialgebraic setting.
We can describe initial degenerations using the angular component map, as follows.

Lemma 3.3.2
.

Let
\mathbb F
be a nontrivially valued real closed field and let
X\subset\mathbb F_{>0}^{n}
be a set. Then

\initial_{w}(X)=\ac F(X\cap\val^{-1}(w)).

Proof.

By definition (
11
),
y\in\initial_{w}(X)
if and only if there exists
a\in t^{-w}.X\cap(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb F,>0}^{\times})^{n}
such that
\res F(a)=y
. This is equivalent to
t^{w}.a\in X
,
\val(t^{w}.a)=w
and
\ac F(t^{w}.a)=\res F(t^{-\val(t^{w}.a)}.(t^{w}.a))=\res F(a)=y
.
∎

In the rest of this section, we describe the initial degeneration by an algebraic object, by the
initial nonnegativity cone
, that plays the same role as initial ideals in tropicalizations of algebraic varieties.

The
nonnegativity cone
of a set
X\subset\mathbb{F}^{n}_{>0}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(X)\coloneqq\{\,f\in\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{x}^{\Lambda}]\mid f(a)\geq 0\text{ for all }a\in X\,\}.

The nonnegativity cone
\mathcal{P}(X)
is closed under addition, multiplication and contains all the squares of polynomials in
\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{x}^{\Lambda}]
, that is, it is a preordering
[
51
, Def. 1.1.23]
. The following elementary fact about nonnegativity cones will be used repeatedly.

Lemma 3.3.3
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a real closed field and
X\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}_{>0}
be a set with nonnegativity cone
\mathcal{P}(X)\subset\mathbb{K}[\mathbf{x}^{\Lambda}]
. Then it holds that

(12)

\displaystyle X\subset\{\,a\in\mathbb{K}^{n}_{>0}\mid f(a)\geq 0\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{P}(X)\,\}.

Moreover, if
X
is closed in
\mathbb{K}^{n}_{>0}
, then equality holds in (
12
).

Proof.

Let
T=\{\,a\in\mathbb{K}^{n}_{>0}\mid f(a)\geq 0\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{P}(X)\,\}
. The inclusion
X\subset T
follows by definition. Now assume that
X
is closed and let
a\in\mathbb{K}^{n}_{>0}\setminus X
. Since
X
is closed and by the definition of strong topology, there exists
r\in\mathbb F_{>0}
such that for the closed ball with center
a
and radius
r
we have

\{x\in\mathbb{K}^{n}_{>0}\mid(x_{1}-a_{1})^{2}+\dots+(x_{n}-a_{n})^{2}-r^{2}\leq 0\}\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}_{>0}\setminus X

Thus,
(x_{1}-a_{1})^{2}+\dots+(x_{n}-a_{n})^{2}-r^{2}
is negative at
a
but positive on
X
, showing that
\mathbb{K}^{n}_{>0}\setminus X\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}_{>0}\setminus T
and concluding the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.3.4
.

Let
\mathbb F
be a nontrivially valued real closed field.
For any set
X\subset\mathbb F_{>0}^{n}
, we have that

(13)

\displaystyle\initial_{w}(X)\subset\{\,y\in k_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}\mid\initial_{w}(f)(y)\geq 0\text{ for all }f\in\pos(X)\,\}

Moreover, if
X
is closed in
\mathbb F^{n}_{>0}
, then we have equality in (
13
).

Proof.

The splitting
t>0
is positive by assumption, and the residue map
\res_{\mathbb{F}}
is compatible with the ordering on
\mathbb{F}
and
k{F}
. Then for all
f\in\mathbb{F}[\mathbf{x}^{\Lambda}]
and
b\in(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\times})^{n}
, using the description of initial forms from Lemma
3.2.4
we have that

(14)

\displaystyle f(t^{w}.b)\geq 0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\initial_{w}(f)(\res F(b))\geq 0.

By definition,
y\in\initial_{w}(X)
if and only if there exists
b\in(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\times})^{n}
such that
t^{w}.b\in X
and
\res_{\mathbb{F}}(b)=y
. From Lemma
3.3.3
, it follows that
f(t^{w}.b)\geq 0
for all
f\in\mathcal{P}(X)
, and using (
14
), we have that
y
is contained in the right-hand side of (
13
).

Now let
X
be closed and let
y\in k_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}
satisfy
\initial_{w}(f)(y)\geq 0
for all
f\in\mathcal{P}(X)
. By (
14
) we have
f(t^{w}.b)\geq 0
for any
b\in(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\times})^{n}
with
\res_{\mathbb{F}}(b)=y
, and using Lemma
3.3.3
we have
t^{w}.b\in X
, concluding the proof.
∎

Remark 3.3.5
.

In the special case where
X
is an algebraic variety intersected with

or

for more details.

3.4.
First version of the Fundamental Theorem

In this section, we prove a version of the Fundamental Theorem for both the tropicalization and the fine tropicalization of definable sets.
The first equality in (
15
) of
Theorem

3.4.1
is the real counterpart of
[
38
, Th. 5.2]
.

Theorem 3.4.1
.

Let
\mathbb F
be a nontrivially valued real closed field.
For any nonempty
X\subset\mathbb F_{>0}^{n}
, we have

(15)

\displaystyle\rv(X)=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{w\in\Gamma F^{n}}\initial_{w}(X)\times\{w\}\subset\bigcapop\displaylimits_{f\in\mathcal{P}(X)}\{\,z\in\mathrm{RV}_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}\mid\trop_{\rv}(f)(z)\geq 0\,\}.

If
X
is closed in
\mathbb F^{n}_{>0}
, then equality holds in (
15
).

Proof.

A pair
(y,w)
lies in
\rv(X)\subset k^{n}_{\mathbb F,>0}\times\Gamma^{n}F
if and only if there exists
a\in X
such that
\ac F(a)=y
and
\val(y)=w
, which is equivalent to
y\in\ac F(X\cap\val^{-1}(w))
. Thus, the first equality in (
15
) follows from Lemma
3.3.2
.

By Proposition
3.3.4
, we have
\initial_{w}(X)\subset\bigcapop\displaylimits_{f\in\mathcal{P}(X)}\{\,y\in k_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}\mid\initial_{w}(f)(y)\geq 0\,\}
with equality if
X
is closed. Therefore

\displaystyle\bigcupop\displaylimits_{w\in\Gamma F^{n}}\initial_{w}(X)\times\{w\}

\displaystyle\subset\bigcupop\displaylimits_{w\in\Gamma F^{n}}\left(\bigcapop\displaylimits_{f\in\mathcal{P}(X)}\{y\in k_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}\mid\initial_{w}(f)(y)\geq 0\}\right)\times\{w\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(y,w)\in\Gamma F^{n}\times k_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}\mid\initial_{w}(f)(y)\geq 0\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{P}(X)\right\}

\displaystyle\overset{(*)}{=}\bigcapop\displaylimits_{f\in\mathcal{P}(X)}\{\,(y,w)\in\mathrm{RV}_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}\mid\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)\geq 0\,\}

where in (*) we used that
\initial_{w}(f)(y)\geq 0
if and only if
\trop_{\rv}(f)(y,w)\geq 0
, see Lemma
3.2.6
.
∎

Corollary 3.4.2
.

Let
\mathbb F
be a nontrivially valued real closed field.
For any nonempty subset
X\subset\mathbb F_{>0}^{n}
, we have the following inclusions of sets

(16)

\displaystyle\val(X)=\{\,w\in\Gamma^{n}{F}\mid\initial_{w}(X)\neq\emptyset\,\}\subset\bigcapop\displaylimits_{f\in\mathcal{P}(X)}\{w\in\Gamma{F}^{n}\mid\trop_{r}(f)(1,w)\geq 0\}.

Proof.

Since the diagram (
3
) commutes we have
\val_{\rv}(\rv(X))=\val(X)
. The equality

\val_{\rv}\big(\bigcupop\displaylimits_{w\in\Gamma^{n}}\initial_{w}(X)\times\{w\}\big)=\{\,w\in\Gamma^{n}{F}\mid\initial_{w}(X)\neq\emptyset\,\}

is also immediate.
Thus, the equality in (
16
) follows from Theorem
3.4.1
.
To show the remaining inclusion, let
w\in\Gamma F^{n}
such that
\initial_{w}(X)\neq\emptyset
. By Theorem
3.4.1
, there exists
z=(y,w)\in\mathrm{RV}_{\mathbb F,>0}^{n}
such that
\trop_{\rv}(f)(z)\geq 0
for all
f\in\mathcal{P}(X)
. Lemma
3.2.6
implies that
\trop_{r}(f)(1,w)\geq 0
for all
f\in\mathcal{P}(X)
. Thus
w=\val_{\rv}(y,w)
is contained in the right-hand side of (
16
).
∎

In
Theorem

3.4.9
, we strengthen
Corollary

3.4.2
for definable sets in two ways. First, we consider the case where
X\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}_{>0}
and
\mathbb{K}
is a real closed field whose valuation may be trivial. Second, we show that the intersection may be taken over finitely many polynomials.

Our proof closely follows
[
28
, Sec. 6]
. However, for completeness, we include the necessary arguments here. To this end, we need the following results.

Definition 3.4.3
.

Let
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
be subgroups of
\mathbb{R}
. Generalizing
[
28
, Def. 6.5]
, we define a
\Gamma
-affine
\Lambda
-polyhedron
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as a set of the form

\left\{\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\lambda_{i}\cdot p+w_{i}\leq 0\text{ for }i\in[m]\,\right\},

and an
open
\Gamma
-affine
\Lambda
-polyhedron
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as a set of the form

\left\{\,p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\lambda_{i}\cdot p+w_{i}<0\text{ for }i\in[m]\,\right\}

where
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\in\Lambda^{n}
, and
w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}\in\Gamma
.

When
\Gamma
is the value group of a real closed field, which is an ordered vector space over
\Lambda
, the field of exponents of a polynomially bounded o-minimal structure, then open and closed
\Gamma
-affine
\Lambda
-polyhedra are extension to
\mathbb{R}
of semilinear sets (see
Section

2.4
).

Proposition 3.4.4
.

Let
\mathfrak K\prec\mathfrak F
and
\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb F
be as in Assumption
3.1.1
.
For any definable set
X\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}_{>0}
, the closure of
\val(X_{\mathfrak F})
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.
\Trop(X)
, is a finite union of
\Gamma_{\mathbb{K}}
-affine
\Lambda
-polyhedra.

Proof.

The proof follows by combining the polynomially bounded o-minimal preparation theorem (see
Theorem

2.3.9
) with the o-minimal cell decomposition theorem (see
[
58
, p. 52]
or
[
16
, Th. 2.10]
). A
cell
is a definable set inductively defined as follows. For
\ell=1
, a cell is simply a singleton or an interval in
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{K}^{1}
. For
\ell>1
, a cell in
\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
can be the graph of a continuous definable function
h\colon D\subset\mathbb{K}^{\ell-1}\to\mathbb F
, where
D
is a cell in
\mathbb{K}^{\ell-1}
, or the set of points lying between the graphs of two continuous definable function
f_{1},f_{2}\colon D\subset\mathbb{K}^{\ell-1}\to\mathbb F
satisfying
f_{1}<f_{2}
on the cell
D
. The cell decomposition theorem asserts that every definable set can be written as disjoint union of cells.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}
be definable functions as above appearing in a cell decomposition of
X\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}_{>0}
. Refining the cell decomposition if necessary, we can assume that
f_{1},f_{2}
can be written as in
Theorem

2.3.9
:

f_{i}(s,a)=\absolutevalue{s-r_i(a)}^{\mu_{i}}c_{i}(a)u_{i}(s,a)

for all
(s,a)\in D\subset\mathbb{K}^{n-1}
, where
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
is small,
\mu_{i}\in\Lambda=\Lambda(\mathfrak K)
,
r_{i},c_{i}
are continuous
(n-2)
-ary functions definable over
\mathbb{K}
, and
u_{i}
is a
(n-1)
-ary function definable over
\mathbb{K}
such that
1-\varepsilon<u<1+\varepsilon
on
D
. The cell decomposition is compatible with field extensions, and we use the same notation for the extension of the functions to
\mathfrak F
. Since
1-\varepsilon<u_{i}<1+\varepsilon
on
DF
, and we have that
\val F(u_{i}(s,a))=0
on
DF
. Hence:

\val F(f_{i}(s,a))=\mu_{i}\val F(s-r_{i}(a))+\val F(c_{i}(a))

on
DF
. We can proceed by induction on
n
(since
r_{i}
and
c_{i}
both depend on
n-2
variables), and get an expression of the form
\mu\cdot p+v
for some
\mu\in\Lambda^{n}
and
v\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
(notice that a
0
-ary function is just a constant, and since
c_{i}
is defined over
\mathbb{K}
, we eventually get
c_{i}\in\mathbb{K}
and
\val F(c_{i})=\val{K}(c_{i})\in\Gamma{K}
). By taking valuation on both sides in
f_{1}<f_{2}
, we thus get a formula of the form
\lambda\cdot p+w\leq 0
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda^{n}
and
w\in\Gamma{K}
. The same computation shows that cells defined as the graph of
h
as above give formulas of the form
\lambda\cdot p+w=0
. By taking unions of all such cells, we have that

\val(XF)
can be written as union of sets of the form

(17)

\displaystyle\left\{\,p\in\Gamma F^{n}\mid\lambda_{i}\cdot p+w_{i}=0\text{ for }i=1,2,\ldots,r\text{ and }\lambda_{j}\cdot p+w_{j}\leq 0\text{ for }j=r+1,2,\ldots,m\,\right\},

for some
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}\in\Lambda^{n}
and
w_{1},\dots,w_{m}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{K}}
.
Since
\Gamma F^{n}
is dense in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it follows that the closure of the sets in (
17
) are
\Gamma{K}
-affine
\Lambda
-polyhedra.
∎

Lemma 3.4.5
.

Let
T
be a finite union of
\Gamma
-affine
\Lambda
-polyhedra in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the complement of
T
is a finite union of open
\Gamma
-affine
\Lambda
-polyhedra.

Proof.

The proof is identical to the proof of
[
28
, Lem. 6.7]
. Since the argument there does not rely on the defining linear equations of
T
having coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
, one may replace them by coefficients from
\Lambda
.
∎

Lemma 3.4.6
.

Let
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
be subgroups of
\mathbb{R}
and consider the real tropical hyperfield
\mathbb{RT}=\mathbb{S}\rtimes\mathbb{R}
.
For any open
\Gamma
-affine
\Lambda
-polyhedron
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists a polynomial
F\in\mathbb{S}\rtimes\Gamma[\overline{\mathbf{w}}^{\Lambda}]\subset\mathbb{RT}[\overline{\mathbf{w}}^{\Lambda}]
with
P=\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid F(1,w)<0\}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\in\Lambda^{n}
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\in\Gamma
such that

P=\left\{\,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\lambda_{i}\cdot w+v_{i}<0\text{ for }i\in[m]\,\right\}=\{\,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\max_{i\in[m]}(\lambda_{i}\cdot w+v_{i})<0\,\}.

Set
v_{0}=0
,
\lambda_{0}=0,\varepsilon_{0}=-1
and
\varepsilon_{i}=1
for
i\in[m]
. Consider the polynomial
F=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{m}(\varepsilon_{i},-v_{i})\odot\overline{w}^{\;-\lambda_{i}}\in\mathbb{RT}[\overline{\mathbf{w}}^{\Lambda}]
. Lemma
2.2.6
implies that

\displaystyle F(1,w)=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{m}(\varepsilon_{i},-v_{i}-\lambda_{i}\cdot w)=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0,\dots,m\\
-v_{i}-\lambda_{i}\cdot w=\min_{j=0,\dots,m}(-v_{j}-\lambda_{j}\cdot w)\end{subarray}}(\varepsilon_{i},-v_{i}-\lambda_{i}\cdot w).

Therefore, we have
F(1,w)\cap(\{1\}\times\mathbb{R}\cup\{0\})=\emptyset
if and only if

-v_{0}-\lambda_{0}\cdot w=0<\min_{i\in[m]}(-v_{i}-\lambda_{i}\cdot w)=-\max_{i\in[m]}(\lambda_{i}\cdot w+v_{i}),

finishing the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4.7
.

Let
\mathfrak K\prec\mathfrak F
and
\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb F
be as in Assumption
3.1.1
, and let
\Lambda=\Lambda(\mathfrak K)
be the field of exponents.
Let
X\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}_{>0}
be a definable set and let
F\in\mathbb{RT}[\overline{w}^{\Lambda}]
be polynomial with coefficients in
\mathbb{S}\rtimes\Gamma{K}
such that
F(1,w)\geq 0
for all
w\in\val(XF)
. Then there exists a polynomial
f\in\mathcal{P}(X)\subset\mathbb{K}[\mathbf{x}^{\Lambda}]
such that
\trop_{r}(f)=F
.

Proof.

Distinguishing the signs of its coefficients, we write
F
as
\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}(1,v_{i})\odot\overline{w}^{\lambda_{i}}\oplus\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=r+1}^{m}(-1,u_{j})\odot\overline{w}^{\lambda_{j}}
for some
v_{1},\dots,v_{r},u_{r+1},\dots,u_{m}\in\Gamma{K}
and
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}\in\Lambda
. Choose
a_{1},\dots,a_{r},b_{r+1},\dots,b_{m}\in\mathbb{K}_{>0}
such that
\val(a_{i})=v_{i}
,
\val(b_{j})=u_{j}
for each
i=1,\dots,r,\,j=r+1,\dots m
.

Following the proof of
[
28
, Lem. 6.8]
, we consider the definable map

\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{K},\quad x\mapsto\tfrac{\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}x^{\lambda_{i}}}{\sumop\displaylimits_{j=r+1}^{m}b_{j}x^{\lambda_{j}}},

and its extension
\varphi F\colon XF\to\mathbb{F}
. Since
XF\subset\mathbb F_{>0}^{n}
and
a_{i},b_{j}>0
, we have
\varphi F(x)>0
for all
x\in XF
,

If for some
x\in XF
we have
0<\val(\varphi F(x))=\val\big(\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}x^{\lambda_{i}}\big)-\val\big(\sumop\displaylimits_{j=r+1}^{m}b_{j}x^{\lambda_{j}}\big)
, then the ultrametric inequality and Lemma
2.2.6
imply that

\displaystyle F(1,\val(x))

\displaystyle=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}(1,v_{i})\odot(1,\val(x))^{\lambda_{i}}\oplus\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=r+1}^{m}(-1,u_{j})\odot(1,\val(x))^{\lambda_{j}}

\displaystyle=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}\sval(a_{i}x^{\lambda_{i}})\oplus\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=r+1}^{m}\sval(-b_{j}x^{\lambda_{j}})=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j=r+1}^{m}\sval(-b_{j}x^{\lambda_{j}})<0,

which contradicts our assumption
F(1,\val(x))\geq 0
.

Thus, we have
0\geq\val(\varphi F(x))
for all
x\in XF
.
If
0>\val(\varphi F(x))
for all
x\in XF
, then by order compatibility of the valuation map, cf. (
1
), we have
\varphi F(x)>\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{K}_{>0}
with
\val(\varepsilon)=0
. Therefore the polynomial

(18)

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}x^{\lambda_{i}}-\varepsilon\sumop\displaylimits_{j=r+1}^{m}b_{j}x^{\lambda_{j}}\in\mathbb{K}[\mathbf{x}^{\Lambda}]

is nonnegative on
XF
and
\trop_{r}(f_{\varepsilon})=F
.

In the rest of the proof, we consider the case when there exists
x_{*}\in XF
with
\val(\varphi F(x_{*}))=0
.
Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{K}
be the infimum of
\varphi
on
X
.
Since the statement that
\alpha
is the infimum of
\varphi
on
X
is expressible using formulas in the language of
\mathfrak K
, and since
\mathfrak K\prec\mathfrak F
, it follows that
\alpha
is also the infimum of
\varphi F
over
XF
.

By order compatibility of the valuation map, from
\val(\varphi F(XF))\leq 0<1=\val(t)
it follows that
0<t<\varphi F(XF)
. In particular,
\alpha>0
. Moreover, we have that
\val(\alpha)=0
. To justify this claim, suppose that
\val(\alpha)\neq 0
.

If
\val(\alpha)<0=\val(\varphi F(x_{*}))
, then
\alpha>\varphi F(x_{*})
by order compatibility of the valuation map. Thus, we must have
\val(\alpha)>0
. But then for
\alpha^{\prime}\coloneqq t^{-\tfrac{1}{N}}\alpha
we have
\val(\alpha^{\prime})=\val(\alpha)-\tfrac{1}{N}>0
for some large enough
N\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular,
\alpha^{\prime}<\varphi F(x)
for all
x\in XF
and
\alpha<\alpha^{\prime}
, which is a contradiction.
Now, set
\varepsilon=\alpha
, and consider
f_{\varepsilon}
as in (
18
), for which we have that
f_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{P}(XF)\cap\mathbb{K}[\mathbf{x}^{\Lambda}]
and
\trop_{r}(f_{\varepsilon})=F
.
To finish the proof, we notice that
\mathcal{P}(XF)\cap\mathbb{K}[\mathbf{x}^{\Lambda}]=\mathcal{P}(X)
. Indeed, for
f\in\mathbb{K}[\mathbf{x}^{\Lambda}]
, we have
\mathfrak K\models\forall x\in X\colon f(x)\geq 0
if and only if
\mathfrak F\models\forall x\in XF\colon fF(x)\geq 0
by definition of elementary extension.
∎

The next lemma will be used not only in the proof of
Theorem

3.4.9
, but also in the proof of
Theorem

4.5.1
.

Lemma 3.4.8
.

Let
\mathfrak K\prec\mathfrak F
and
\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb F
be as in Assumption
3.1.1
. For any definable subset
X\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}_{>0}
, there exist polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
such that

\displaystyle\overline{\val(XF)}=\bigcapop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k}\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\trop_{r}(f_{i})(1,w)\geq 0\}.

Proof.

To prove the equality, we adapt the argument showing
(3)\subset(4)
in
[
28
, Th. 6.9]
.
By Proposition
3.4.4
,

\overline{\val(XF)}
is a finite union of
\Gamma{K}
-affine
\Lambda
-polyhedra in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
From Lemma
3.4.5
, it follows that

\overline{\val(XF)}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left(\cup_{i=1}^{k}P_{i}\right)

for some open
\Gamma{K}
-affine
\Lambda
-polyhedra
P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
By Lemma
3.4.6
there exist real tropical polynomials
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}\in\mathbb{S}\rtimes\Gamma{K}[\overline{\mathbf{w}}^{\Lambda}]
such that
P_{i}=\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid F_{i}(1,w)<0\}
for each

, which implies that

\overline{\val(XF)}=\bigcapop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k}\{\,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid F_{i}(1,w)\geq 0\,\}.

By Lemma
3.4.7
, there exist polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
with
\trop_{r}(f_{i})=F_{i}
for

, finishing the proof.
∎

With these technical lemmas in place, we are now ready to prove the main result of this section, which improves Corollary
3.4.2
.

Theorem 3.4.9
.

Let
\mathfrak K\prec\mathfrak F
and
\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb F
be as in Assumption
3.1.1
.
For a definable subset

, the following sets are equal

\displaystyle\val(XF)=\{\,w\in\Gamma^{n}{F}\mid\initial_{w}(XF)\neq\emptyset\,\}=\bigcapop\displaylimits_{f\in\mathcal{P}(X)}\{w\in\Gamma{F}^{n}\mid\trop_{r}(f)(1,w)\geq 0\},

Proof.

The first equality holds by Corollary
3.4.2
.
By Lemma
3.4.8
, there exist polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
such that

\displaystyle\val(XF)

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{=}\overline{\val(XF)}\cap\Gamma F^{n}=\bigcapop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k}\{w\in\Gamma F^{n}\mid\trop_{r}(f_{i})(1,w)\geq 0\}\supset\bigcapop\displaylimits_{f\in\mathcal{P}(X)}\{w\in\Gamma F^{n}\mid\trop_{r}(f)(1,w)\geq 0\}

\displaystyle\supset\bigcapop\displaylimits_{f\in\mathcal{P}(XF)}\{w\in\Gamma F^{n}\mid\trop_{r}(f)(1,w)\geq 0\}\supset\val(XF),

where
(1)
holds by Corollary
3.1.4
and the last inclusion follows from Corollary
3.4.2
.
∎

4.1.
The o-minimal spectrum

We recall in this section some fundamental properties of the o-minimal spectrum, following

,

,

,

,

,

Definition 4.1.1

Let

\mathcal{D}\subset 2^{Z}
be a Boolean algebra of subsets of

\alpha

is a nonempty collection of subsets in

(i)

;

(ii)

; and

(iii)

if

.

We say that a filter

if, in addition, it satisfies the following property:

(iv)

for each

, if
A\notin\alpha
, then
Z\setminus A\in\alpha
.

Definition 4.1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak K

\Def(X)
. We equip

,

We recall an alternative, more geometric characterization of the o-minimal spectrum in the polynomially bounded case for locally closed sets, which follows from the Łojasiewicz inequality. For a definable set

Definition 4.1.4

Definition 4.1.6
.

Let

Let

4.2.
Relatively archimedean points

We study relatively archimedean points in the o-minimal setting. This concept has been introduced in
[
28
]
to study tropicalizations of semialgebraic sets. In the following, we fix
\mathfrak K
a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of a real closed field
\mathbb{K}
and a definable set
X\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
.

Definition 4.2.1
.

We say that
\alpha\in\widetilde{X}
is
relatively archimedean
if
\mathbb{K}(\alpha)
is relatively archimedean over
\mathbb{K}
. We denote the set of relatively archimedean points as
\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}
, and equip it with the relative spectral topology.

Remark 4.2.2
.

It is shown in
[
28
, Theorem 3.17]
that
\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}
is homeomorphic, in the semialgebraic case, to the real Berkovich analytification of
X
. While an analog statement holds true also in our generalized setup, we prefer in the following to work directly inside the o-minimal spectrum, since our model-theoretic techniques are better expressed in this context.

We use (
19
) to characterize relatively archimedean points in terms of continuous semialgebraic functions.

Lemma 4.2.3
.

Let
X
be a definable locally closed set. Then
\alpha\in\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}
if and only if for every
D\in\alpha
and
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\definable}(X)
, there exists
D^{\prime}\in\alpha
and
c\in\mathbb{K}
such that
\absolutevalue{f(a)}\leq c
for all
a\in D\cap D^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Every element of
\mathbb{K}(\alpha)
can be represented as
f(\alpha)
for some
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\definable}(D)
and
D\in\alpha
. Then the result follows from (
19
).
∎

Example 4.2.4
.

For any polynomially bounded o-minimal structure
\mathfrak K
expanding a real closed field
\mathbb{K}
, we describe the o-minimal spectrum
\widetilde{\mathbb{K}_{>0}}
. Since the definable subsets of
\mathbb{K}
are finite unions of points and intervals, these are precisely the semialgebraic subsets of

. Thus, combining
[

A

of

is a pair

such that
\mathbb{K}_{>0}=I\cup J
and

. We say that a Dedekind cut is

or

for some

•

is equal to the union of
\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}
with

and

, where

(resp.

(resp.

•

\widetilde{\mathbb{K}_{>0}}

with

for

We denote

), and the structure

. The field

Notice that the inclusion

Let

Notice that

The equality

Proof.

The inclusion

\beta\cap\mathfrak F=\alpha\Longleftrightarrow\forall V\in\alpha,\ VM\in\beta

since for
D\in\Def(X)
we have
DM\cap\mathbb F^{n}=D
. Consider the family
\beta_{0}\coloneqq\{\,VM\in\Def(XM)\mid V\in\alpha\,\}
. This family has the finite intersection property, i.e., any finite intersection of sets in
\beta_{0}
is nonempty. As in
[
47
, Lem. 2.6.1]
, we can then construct an ultrafilter
\beta\in\widetilde{XM}
extending
\beta_{0}
. It follows from (
20
) that
\beta\cap\mathfrak F=\alpha
.

We now study the bottom horizontal map. We first need to show that it is well-defined, that is, for
\beta\in\widetilde{X_{\mathfrak M}}^{\mathrm{arch}}
, we have
\alpha\coloneqq\beta\cap\mathfrak F\in\widetilde{X_{\mathfrak F}}^{\mathrm{arch}}
. Consider the commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb F(\alpha)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{M}(\beta)}

where the bottom map follows from the inclusion
\mathcal{C}^{\definable}(X_{\mathfrak F})/\mathfrak p_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\definable}(X_{\mathfrak M})/\mathfrak p_{\beta}
. Since
\beta\in\widetilde{X_{\mathfrak M}}^{\mathrm{arch}}
,
\mathbb{M}(\beta)
is relatively archimedean over
\mathbb{M}
, and by hypothesis
\mathbb{M}
is relatively archimedean over
\mathbb F
. Therefore
\mathbb{M}(\beta)
is relatively archimedean over
\mathbb F
, which implies that
\mathbb F(\alpha)
is relatively archimedean over
\mathbb F

\alpha\in\widetilde{X_{\mathfrak F}}^{\mathrm{arch}}
.

Now we prove that the map
\widetilde{X_{\mathfrak M}}^{\mathrm{arch}}\to\widetilde{X_{\mathfrak F}}^{\mathrm{arch}}
is surjective. Let
\alpha\in\widetilde{X_{\mathfrak F}}^{\mathrm{arch}}
, and pick
\beta\in\widetilde{X_{\mathfrak M}}
such that
\beta\cap\mathfrak F=\alpha
. Let
\overline{\beta}
be the unique closed point in
\closure\left({\{\beta\}}\right)\cap\widetilde{X_{\mathfrak M}}^{\max}
(the uniqueness follows e.g. from
[

or
[

).
We first show that

. Notice that
\overline{\beta}\supset\beta
, and hence the inclusion
\overline{\beta}\cap\mathfrak F\supset\beta\cap\mathfrak F
follows. But
\alpha\in\widetilde{X_{\mathfrak F}}^{\mathrm{arch}}\subset\widetilde{X_{\mathfrak F}}^{\max}
is closed, and thus
\overline{\beta}\cap\mathfrak F

\beta\cap\mathfrak F=\alpha
, is itself equal to

.

Since
\alpha\in\widetilde{X_{\mathfrak F}}^{\mathrm{arch}}

for some

from (

c^{-1}\leq{x_{i}({\overline{\beta}})}\leq c

∎

4.3.
Valuations and relatively archimedean points

We now study natural valuations associated to the residue field of every relatively archimedean point, in the case the base field admits a nontrivial valuation. The next lemma is the analog to
[
28
, Lemma 3.13]
in our context.

Lemma 4.3.1
.

Assume that
\mathbb F\subset\mathbb{L}
is an extension of real closed fields, that
\mathbb F
has an order compatible (rank one) valuation
\val F\colon\mathbb F\to\Gamma F\cup\{\infty\}
, and that
\mathbb{L}
is archimedean over
\mathbb F
.
Then there is a unique order compatible (rank one) valuation
\val_{\mathbb{L}}
extending
\val_{\mathbb F}
, defined as:

(21)

\displaystyle\val{L}(x)=\sup\{\,\val F(a)\mid a\in\mathbb F,\ a\geq x\,\}=\inf\{\,\val F(a)\mid a\in\mathbb F,\ a\leq x\,\}

This is the valuation having as valuation ring the convex hull of
\mathcal{O}F
in
\mathbb{L}
, i.e. the elements in
\mathbb{L}
which are upper and lower bounded by elements in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb F}
.

Proof.

Denote
R
the convex hull of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb F}
in
\mathbb{L}
. Since
R
is a convex subring, then
R
is a valuation ring
[
31
, Prop. 2.2.4]
. In other words,
R
is the smallest convex valuation ring of
\mathbb{L}
containing
\mathcal{O}F
.
We show that the valuation
v_{R}\colon\mathbb{L}^{\times}\to\mathbb{L}^{\times}/R^{\times}\eqqcolon\Gamma_{R}
associated to
R
is of rank one, or equivalently that
\Gamma_{R}
is archimedean (i.e. relatively archimedean over
\mathbb{Q}
), or equivalently that
\Gamma_{R}
is a subfield of
\mathbb{R}

[
31
, Th. 2.1.10]
. Since
R\cap\mathbb F=\mathcal{O}F
, we can embed
\Gamma F=\mathbb F^{\times}/\mathcal{O}F^{\times}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\times}/R^{\times}=\Gamma_{R}
and
v_{R}|_{\mathbb F}=\val_{\mathbb F}
. Pick
\gamma=v_{R}(a)\in\Gamma_{R}
. Without loss of generality, assume
a>0
. By archimedeanity, there exists
b\in\mathbb F
such that
a^{-1}\leq b
, and hence
v_{R}(a)\leq v_{R}(b^{-1})=\val_{\mathbb F}(b^{-1})
. Therefore,
\Gamma_{R}
is archimedean over
\Gamma F\subset\mathbb{R}
, which implies that
\Gamma_{R}
is itself archimedean, and a subfield of
\mathbb{R}
.

Now let
\val{L}\colon\mathbb{L}\to\Gamma{L}\cup\{\infty\}
be any order compatible rank one valuation of
\mathbb{L}
extending
\val F
. For
x\in\mathbb{L}
, let
\mathrm{UB}F(x)
(resp.
\mathrm{LB}F(x)
) the set of upper bounds (resp. lower bounds) of
x
in
\mathbb F
. Since
\mathbb{L}
is archimedean over
\mathbb F
both
\mathrm{UB}F(x)
and
\mathrm{LB}F(x)
are nonempty. The valuations are compatible with the ordering, and since
\Gamma F\subset\mathbb{R}
, we have

\val_{\mathbb{L}}(x)=\sup\val_{\mathbb F}(\mathrm{UB}F(x))=\inf\val_{\mathbb F}(\mathrm{LB}F(x))\in\mathbb{R}

for
0\neq x\in\mathbb{L}
. Hence any such valuation is uniquely determined by (
21
), and in particular
\val{L}=v_{R}
.
∎

Let

4.4.
Tropicalization maps and strong density theorem

Let
\mathfrak F
be a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of a real closed field
\mathbb F
, equipped with a nontrivial order compatible valuation
\val\colon\mathbb F\to\Gamma F\cup\{\infty\}
, and let
X\subset\mathbb F_{>0}^{n}
be a definable set. In the following, we always assume that
\alpha\in\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}
.

Let
k=kF
be the residue field of
\mathbb F
and
k(\alpha)\coloneqq k_{\mathbb F(\alpha)}
be the residue field of
\mathbb F(\alpha)
. There exists a universal real closed field
\mathbb{L}
and
kF
-embeddings
k(\alpha)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}
. If
k
is archimedean, then
k(\alpha)
is archimedean as well, and we can simply take
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{R}
. In general, the field
\mathbb{L}
can be for instance constructed as follows.

Let
A=\bigotimesop\displaylimits_{k}k(\alpha)
be the coproduct of the family of
\mathbb F
-algebras
\{\,k(\alpha)\colon\alpha\in\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}\,\}
. This can also be interpreted as the colimit of all the possible finite tensor products of the
k(\alpha)
’s, see e.g.
[
21
, Prop. A6.7 b]
. This implies that, if
a\in A
, then there exists
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}
such that
a\in k(\alpha_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes k(\alpha_{m})
. We want to prove that
A
is a
real ring
, that is,
-1
is not a sum of squares in
A
, which we denote by
-1\notin\Sigma A^{2}
. Assume that
-1\in\Sigma A^{2}
. Then there exist
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}
such that, denoting
B\coloneqq k(\alpha_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes k(\alpha_{m})
, we have
-1\in\Sigma B^{2}
. By the amalgamation property for real closed fields, see e.g.
[
47
]
, there exists a real closed field
R
and
k
-embeddings
\iota_{\alpha}\colon k(\alpha)\to R
for every
\alpha
. Consider the map

\phi\colon B\longrightarrow R,\quad a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{m}\longmapsto\iota_{\alpha_{1}}(a_{1})\cdots\iota_{\alpha_{m}}(a_{m})

The field
\quot(B/\ker\phi)
is a subfield of
R
, and in particular it is a real field. Hence
B
is a real ideal
[
51
, Lem. 3.2.16]
: in particular,
-1\notin\Sigma B^{2}
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
-1\notin\Sigma A^{2}
. This implies that there exists a prime ideal
\mathfrak p\subset A
such that
\quot(A/\mathfrak p)
is a real field
[
51
, Lem. 3.2.16]
. Define
\mathbb{L}
to be the real closure of
\quot(A/\mathfrak p)
with respect to one of its field ordering.
The composition

k\hookrightarrow k(\alpha)\hookrightarrow A\twoheadrightarrow\quot(A/\mathfrak p)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}

gives the desired
k
-embeddings.

Now let
\Omega=\Omega(X)\coloneqq\mathbb{L}((t{R}))
be the field of Hahn series with coefficients in
\mathbb{L}
, which is real closed since
\mathbb{L}
is real closed and
\mathbb{R}
is divisible
[
3
, p. 219]
(see also
[
63
, Cor. 3.9]
). Notice that there is a canonical embedding
kF((t^{\Gamma F}))\hookrightarrow\Omega
, and a map

\mathbb F(\alpha)\longrightarrow\Omega,\quad a\longmapsto\ac_{\alpha}(a)t^{\val_{\alpha}(a)}

which makes the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb F(\alpha)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{RV}F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{RV}(\alpha)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{RV}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rv}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rv_{\alpha}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rv}

commute.

Definition 4.4.1
.

Using the notations from
Definition

4.3.2
and the previous conventions, we define the
fine tropicalization map
as

\ftrop\colon\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}\to\mathrm{RV}\cong\mathbb{L}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\alpha\longmapsto(\rv_{\alpha}(x_{1}(\alpha)),\dots,\rv_{\alpha}(x_{n}(\alpha)))

where we use the embddings
k(\alpha)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}
, and the
tropicalizaiton map

\trop\colon\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\alpha\longmapsto(\val_{\alpha}(x_{1}(\alpha)),\dots,\val_{\alpha}(x_{n}(\alpha)))

Moreover, we equip
\Omega
with the topology of open sets
U
, where
U\subset kF((t^{\Gamma F}))
is open in the strong topology and
U
is the field extension to
\Omega
. We equip
\mathrm{RV}^{\times}={}^{\times}/(1+\mathfrak m)
with the quotient topology, and refer to both of these topologies as the
\mathbb F
-topology
on
\Omega
resp. on
\mathrm{RV}^{\times}
.

Remark 4.4.2
.

The topology defined on
\Omega
can be strictly coarser than the strong topology induced by the ordering. For example, if
\mathbb F=\mathbb{R}\{t\}
the field of real algebraic Puiseux series, then
\Omega\cong\mathbb{R}((t{R}))
. Consider the set
]t^{\pi},2t^{\pi}[
, which is open in the strong topology of
\Omega
. However, any
\mathbb F
-open set containing an element with valuation
\pi
must contain the set
\mathbb{R}^{\times}\cdot t^{\pi}
, showing that
]t^{\pi},2t^{\pi}[
is not open in the
\mathbb F
-topology.

Lemma 4.4.3
.

Let
\mathfrak F
be a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of a real closed field
\mathbb F
, equipped with a nontrivial order compatible valuation
\val\colon\mathbb F\to\Gamma F\cup\{\infty\}
, and let
X\subset\mathbb F_{>0}^{n}
be a definable set. Equip
\Omega
as before with the
\mathbb F
-topology. The maps
\ftrop\colon\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}\to\mathrm{RV}
and
\trop\colon\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
are continuous.

Proof.

In the following, we identify every
\mathrm{RV}(\alpha)
with its isomorphic copy in
\mathrm{RV}
. Let
U\subset\mathrm{RV}
be
\mathbb F
-open. Then
\rv_{\alpha}^{-1}(U)=\rv_{\alpha}^{-1}(U\cap\mathrm{RV}(\alpha))
is an open subset of
X_{\mathfrak F(\alpha)}
defined over
\mathbb F
. Hence
\widetilde{\rv_{\alpha}^{-1}(U)}^{\mathrm{arch}}
is an open subset of
\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}
.
Now notice that

\ftrop^{-1}(U)=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{\alpha\in\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}}\widetilde{\rv_{\alpha}^{-1}(U)}^{\mathrm{arch}}

which is an open set in
\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}
, proving that
\ftrop
is continuous.

The map
\val_{\rv}\colon\mathrm{RV}_{\Omega,>0}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous: indeed, since
\Gamma F
is dense in
\mathbb{R}
, it is sufficient to check that
\val_{\rv}^{-1}(]w_{1},w_{1}[)
is
\mathbb F
-open for
w_{1},w_{2}\in\Gamma F
. But this is clear by the definition of
\mathbb F
-topology and the continuity of
\val_{\rv F}
. Since
\trop=\val_{\rv}\circ\ftrop
the tropicalization map is also continuous.
∎

Proposition 4.4.4
.

Let
\mathfrak F
be a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of a real closed field
\mathbb F
, equipped with a nontrivial order compatible valuation
\val\colon\mathbb F\to\Gamma F\cup\{\infty\}
, and let
X\subset\mathbb F_{>0}^{n}
be a definable locally closed set. The map
\trop\colon\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is proper.

Proof.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be compact. Then there exists
r\in\mathbb{R}
such that

K\subset\;]-r,\,r[^{n}\;\,=\;\,]\val(t^{-r}),\val(t^{r})[^{n}

It follows from the compatibility of the valuation that, if
\alpha\in\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}
is such that
\trop(\alpha)\in K
, we have
t^{r+1}\leq t^{r}<x_{i}(\alpha)<t^{-r}\leq t^{-r-1}
for every
i=1,\dots,n
(we are using the embedding
\mathbb F\subset\mathbb F(\alpha)
). This implies that

\trop^{-1}(K)\subset\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}\cap\widetilde{[t^{r+1},t^{-r-1}]^{n}}=\widetilde{X}^{\max}\cap\widetilde{[t^{r+1},t^{-r-1}]^{n}}^{\max}\cong\sper(\mathcal{C}^{\definable}(X\cap[t^{r+1},t^{-r-1}]^{n}))^{\max}

where the equality follows from
Lemmas

4.2.5
and
4.2.6
.
The set
\sper(\mathcal{C}(X\cap[t^{-r-1},t^{r+1}]^{n}))^{\max}
is a compact (Hausdorff) space, see e.g.
[
51
, Prop. 3.4.19]
. Since
\trop
is continuous from
Lemma

4.4.3
,
\trop^{-1}(K)
is a closed subset of a compact space, and hence compact.
∎

Remark 4.4.5
.

In
[
28
, Lem. 3.8]
the corresponding properness statement for the tropicalization map is proven in the case of real algebraic varieties, but it not extended to arbitrary semialgebraic sets. On the contrary, in
Proposition

4.4.4
we prove properness in the case of arbitrary (locally closed) definable sets: this will simplify the proof of
Theorem

4.5.1
, compared to the proof of
[
28
, Th. 6.9]
.

Lemma 4.4.6
.

Let
\mathfrak F
be a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of a real closed field
\mathbb F
, equipped with a nontrivial order compatible valuation, and let
X\subset\mathbb F_{>0}^{n}
be a definable set. Denote
\closure\left({X}\right)\subset\mathbb F^{n}_{>0}
the relative closure of
X
in
\mathbb F^{n}_{>0}
. Then we have

\trop(\widetilde{\closure\left({X}\right)}^{\mathrm{arch}})=\overline{\trop(\widetilde{{X}}^{\mathrm{arch}})}=\trop(\widetilde{{X}}^{\mathrm{arch}}).

Proof.

Let
\closure\left({\widetilde{{X}}^{\mathrm{arch}}}\right)
denote the relative closure of
\widetilde{{X}}^{\mathrm{arch}}
in
\widetilde{\mathbb F^{n}_{>0}}^{\mathrm{arch}}
. We have

\closure\left({\widetilde{{X}}^{\mathrm{arch}}}\right)=\closure\left({\widetilde{X}}\right)\cap\widetilde{\mathbb F_{>0}}^{\mathrm{arch}}=\widetilde{\closure\left({X}\right)}\cap\widetilde{\mathbb F_{>0}}^{\mathrm{arch}}=\widetilde{\closure\left({X}\right)}^{\mathrm{arch}}

where:

•

the first closure is taken in
\widetilde{\mathbb F_{>0}}^{\mathrm{arch}}
, the second one in
\widetilde{\mathbb F_{>0}}
, and the third and fourth ones in
\mathbb F_{>0}
;

•

the second equality follows since the tilde operator commutes with closures, see e.g.
[
19
, Rem. 2.3]
, and the third one from
Lemma

4.2.5
.

A proper map is a closed map, and hence the image of the maps
\trop\colon\widetilde{\closure\left({X}\right)}^{\mathrm{arch}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\trop\colon\widetilde{{X}}^{\mathrm{arch}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
are closed by
Proposition

4.4.4
. Therefore:

\trop(\widetilde{\closure\left({X}\right)}^{\mathrm{arch}})=\overline{\trop(\widetilde{\closure\left({X}\right)}^{\mathrm{arch}})}=\overline{\trop(\closure\left({\widetilde{{X}}^{\mathrm{arch}}}\right))}=\overline{\trop({\widetilde{{X}}^{\mathrm{arch}}})}=\trop(\widetilde{{X}}^{\mathrm{arch}})

where the third equality follows because
\trop
is continuous by
Lemma

4.4.3
.
∎

Theorem 4.4.7

(Strong density)
.

Let
\mathfrak F
be a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of a real closed field
\mathbb F
, equipped with a nontrivial order compatible valuation
\val\colon\mathbb F\to\Gamma F\cup\{\infty\}
, and let
X\subset\mathbb F^{n}_{>0}
be definable. Then

\trop(\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}})\cap\Gamma F^{n}=\val(X).

Proof.

The inclusion
\trop(\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}})\cap\Gamma F^{n}\supset\val(X)
follows from the embedding
X\hookrightarrow\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}
and the compatibility of the valuations
\val_{\alpha}
with the valuation of
\mathbb F
. For the converse inclusion, let
w\in\trop(\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}})\cap\Gamma F^{n}
and
\alpha\in\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{arch}}
such that
\val_{\alpha}(x_{i}(\alpha))=w_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,n
. The extension
\mathfrak F\prec\mathfrak F(\alpha)
is elementary, and
\alpha\in X_{\mathfrak F(\alpha)}
.
Proposition

3.1.3
(whose proof was based on
[
59
]
) implies that there exists
a\in X
with
\val(a)=w
.
∎

In this final section, we complete the proof of

and

.
Recall from (

\Trop(X)=\overline{\val F(XF)}

Theorem 4.5.1

(Fundamental Theorem of Tropical O-minimal Geometry)
.

Let
\mathfrak K\prec\mathfrak F\prec\mathfrak M
and
\mathbb{K}\subset\mathbb F\subset\mathbb{M}
be as in Assumption
3.1.1
, and let
X\subset\mathbb{K}_{>0}^{n}
be definable.
Then the following sets are equal:

(i)

\trop(\widetilde{XF}^{\mathrm{arch}})
;

(ii)

\Trop(X)
;

(iii)

\overline{\{\,w\in\Gamma^{n}{F}\mid\initial_{w}(XF)\neq\emptyset\,\}}
;

(iv)

\val{M}(XM)
;

(v)

\bigcapop\displaylimits_{f\in\mathcal{P}(X)}\{\,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\trop_{r}(f)(1,w)\geq 0\,\}
.

Proof.

We first notice that the equality of the sets in (ii), (iii) and in (iv), (v) is a consequence of
Theorem

3.4.9
. We proceed by showing the inclusions (iv) = (v)
\subset
(ii) = (iii)
\subset
(i)
\subset
(iv).

By Lemma
3.4.8
, there exist polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
such that

\displaystyle\Trop(X)=\overline{\val(XF)}=\bigcapop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{k}\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\trop_{r}(f_{i})(1,w)\geq 0\}\supset\bigcapop\displaylimits_{f\in\mathcal{P}(X)}\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\trop_{r}(f)(1,w)\geq 0\}.

We assume in the following that
X
is locally closed. We show that
\Trop(X)=\overline{\val F(XF)}\subset\trop(\widetilde{XF}^{\mathrm{arch}})
. This is true because

\overline{\val(XF)}\subset\overline{\trop(\widetilde{XF}^{\mathrm{arch}})}=\trop(\widetilde{XF}^{\mathrm{arch}})

where the inclusion follows from the embedding
XF\hookrightarrow\widetilde{XF}^{\mathrm{arch}}
, and the equality from
Lemma

4.4.6
.

We conclude by showing that
\trop(\widetilde{XF}^{\mathrm{arch}})\subset\val{M}(XM)
. The inclusion of rings
\mathcal{C}^{\definable}(X_{\mathfrak F})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\definable}(X_{\mathfrak M})
induces a surjective map
\widetilde{XM}^{\mathrm{arch}}\twoheadrightarrow\widetilde{XF}^{\mathrm{arch}}
, see
Proposition

4.2.8
.
Since this map is surjective and compatible with valuations, we have

\trop(\widetilde{XF}^{\mathrm{arch}})\subset\trop(\widetilde{XM}^{\mathrm{arch}})=\val_{\mathbb{M}}(X_{\mathfrak M})

where the equality follows from
Theorem

4.4.7
.

We now remove the assumption that
X
is locally closed.
Proposition

3.1.2
Implies that
\val{M}(XM)=\val{M}(\closure\left({XM}\right))=\val{M}(\closure\left({X}\right)M)
and
\Trop(X)=\Trop(\closure\left({X}\right))
. Notice also that
\trop(\widetilde{XF}^{\mathrm{arch}})=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{\alpha}\val_{\alpha}(X_{\mathfrak F(\alpha)})
. Applying again
Proposition

3.1.2
, to each
\val_{\alpha}(X_{\mathfrak F(\alpha)})
we have that
\trop(\widetilde{XF}^{\mathrm{arch}})=\trop(\widetilde{(\closure\left({X}\right)F)}^{\mathrm{arch}})
. Hence we can always replace
X
with
\closure\left({X}\right)
, which is a locally closed definable set, concluding the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.5.2

(Fundamental Theorem of Fine Tropical O-minimal Geometry)
.

Let
\mathfrak M
be a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of a real closed field
\mathbb{M}
. Assume that
\mathbb{M}
has a nontrival order compatible valuation s.t.

and assume further that the residue field of

is
\mathbb{R}
.

Then for any closed definable subset

, the following sets are equal:

(i)

\ftrop(\widetilde{XM}^{\mathrm{arch}})
