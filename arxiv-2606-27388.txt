Solar System and Atomic Clock Bounds on Locally Coupled Swampland Scalars
Suraj Gavhale, Maxim Khlopov, Oem Trivedi, Maxim Krasnov

Abstract




Abstract:
We study how local measurements can constrain light scalar fields that are relevant for late time cosmic acceleration and are often discussed in connection with swampland criteria. Starting from a scalar-tensor framework, we define the relevant swampland quantities in the canonically normalized Einstein frame and relate them to Solar System, Lunar Laser Ranging, equivalence principle, and atomic clock bounds. These probes do not constrain the scalar velocity by itself but rather products of the scalar motion with microscopic couplings to matter, gravity and atomic parameters. For coupling directions that are visible to local experiments, the scalar is driven into an ultra slow present regime and this leads to severely restricting the possibility of realizing an
\mathcal{O}(1)
de Sitter gradient through unscreened visible sector couplings alone. The refined de Sitter alternative remains viable only in restricted regions of parameter space, such as near a hilltop or under tuned dynamical evolution.

Introduction

The swampland conjectures are based on the idea that not every low energy effective field theory that looks consistent on its own can actually arise as the infrared limit of a quantum theory of gravity. This viewpoint goes back to Vafa’s original swampland proposal and to later reviews of quantum gravity constraints on apparently consistent low energy EFTs

[

M_{\rm Pl}\frac{|\nabla V|}{V}\geq c_{*},

(1)

O(1)

\min(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}V)\leq-c^{\prime}_{*}\frac{V}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}.

(2)

In this work, we ask what part of the usual swampland parameter space is actually probed when a late time scalar field is light, locally coupled to visible matter or to the effective gravitational coupling, and unscreened on laboratory and Solar System scales. Under these assumptions, the scalar is not only a cosmological field but it is also subject to local tests including Lunar Laser Ranging, post-Newtonian measurements, weak equivalence principle searches and atomic clock comparisons

Clock measurements constrain only those microscopic coupling directions to which a given clock comparison is sensitive. Lunar Laser Ranging constrains the time variation of the gravitational coupling, not the scalar velocity by itself. 5
Conclusion

We have formulated the Solar System and atomic clock swampland problem as a local allowed region map. For an unscreened light scalar relevant for dark energy and locally coupled to visible matter or to gravity, local measurements do not constrain the scalar velocity alone. What we have instead shown is that they constrain products of the scalar motion with microscopic couplings together with composition dependent and source dependent scalar charges. Representative local scales are
|\alpha_{M,0}|\lesssim 10^{-2}
and
|x|\lesssim 10^{-2}
for coupling directions that are visible to the relevant local probes. Precisely, the coupling consistent condition is direction dependent, wherein
|x|\leq x_{\rm max}(\mathbf{d})=\min[x_{\rm clk}(\mathbf{d}),\epsilon_{G}/|d_{F}|]
, supplemented by post-Newtonian and equivalence principle constraints on the microscopic coupling vector
\mathbf{d}
. Thus the bound on
x
should be understood as a representative ultra slow roll consequence for locally visible coupling directions.

For a canonical scalar supplying the present dark energy density,
1+w_{0}=x^{2}/(3\Omega_{{\rm DE},0})
which implies
1+w_{0}\lesssim 10^{-4}\text{-}10^{-5}
for standard
\Omega_{{\rm DE},0}
. For a noncanonical scalar, the clock relation to the field velocity is unchanged, while the equation of state relation acquires the factor
P_{X}(X_{0},\phi_{0})
. The local Hubble time excursion is also small,
\Delta\phi_{H_{0}}/M_{\rm Pl}\sim|x|\lesssim 10^{-2}\ll 1
, although this is not a full distance conjecture test. The global displacement cannot be inferred from
x_{0}
alone. The de Sitter gradient variable is constrained instead through the scalar equation of motion. At
z=0
, one has Eq. (
71
). Since the visible contribution is weighted by
\Omega_{b,0}/\Omega_{{\rm DE},0}\simeq 0.0715
, even an approximately universal visible scalar charge near the post-Newtonian limit contributes only at the few
10^{-4}
level to
c
, while composition dependent visible charges are restricted even more strongly by equivalence principle tests. Therefore, within the unscreened local theory considered here, visible sector matter coupling alone cannot naturally raise
c
to order unity.

In the slow acceleration limit, with small unscreened visible coupling and no dark matter coupling, this gives
c\lesssim O(10^{-2})
. Compatibility with an
O(1)
gradient criterion then requires a direction not fixed by visible local bounds such as a large acceleration contribution
y
, screening, a dark or hidden sector coupling, a locally invisible coupling direction, tuned cancellation, a smaller conjectural coefficient, or perhaps a departure from the single field assumptions.

The refined de Sitter option is also restrictive as we see that if
c\ll 1
is to be compensated by
\eta\sim-O(1)
, the scalar must lie close to a hilltop, with
|(\phi_{0}-\phi_{h})/M_{\rm Pl}|\lesssim x_{\rm max}(\mathbf{d})/\lambda_{+}(|\eta|)
and
\lambda_{+}(|\eta|)=(-3+\sqrt{9+12\Omega_{{\rm DE},0}|\eta|})/2
. For
|\eta|=1
and
x_{\rm max}=10^{-2}
, this gives
|\phi_{0}-\phi_{h}|/M_{\rm Pl}\lesssim 1.7\times 10^{-2}
, so the refined conjecture is compatible with local clock bounds only through hilltop proximity, tuned suppression of the growing mode, or by moving outside the unscreened single field setup. The main conclusion is therefore restricted but sharp, wherein Solar System, equivalence principle, Lunar Laser Ranging and clock probes severely limit the possibility that a locally unscreened, dark energy relevant scalar satisfies order one de Sitter like swampland criteria through visible sector matter coupling alone.
