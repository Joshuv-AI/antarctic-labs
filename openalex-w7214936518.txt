Nanocomposite-derived Ni/YSZ hydrogen electrode active layer
for enhancing performance of solid oxide electrolysis cells
Yurika Sone1,2, Kazuyoshi Sato1,³, Toshiaki Yamaguchi2,3, Katherine Develos-Bagarinao3 and
Haruo Kishimoto3
1

Division of Materials & Environments, Graduate School of Science and Technology, Gunma University,
1–5–1 Tenjin-cho, Kiryu, Gunma 376–8515, Japan

National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology, Energy Process Research Institute, AIST Tsukuba-West,

National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology, Global Zero Emission Research Center, AIST Tsukuba-West,

A Ni/YSZ (Yttria-stabilized zirconia; YSZ) hydrogen electrode active layer was employed to improve the
performance of solid oxide electrolysis cells (SOECs). The active layer was derived from pre-fabricated NiO/
YSZ nanocomposite particles grown by a co-precipitation method. The hydrogen electrode supported cells with
and without the active layer were prepared to investigate the eﬀect of the active layer on performance and
durability. The cell with the active layer exhibited a high current density of 1.93 A·cm¹2 at 750 °C and 1.3 V. The
performance was approximately 1.5 times higher than that of the cell without the active layer. Electrochemical
impedance spectroscopy (EIS) revealed that the higher performance can be attributed not only to a decrease in
polarization resistance but also to a reduction in ohmic resistance. Furthermore, the cell with the active layer
showed durability comparable to or higher than that of the cell without the active layer, despite being composed
of submicron-sized ﬁne grains and pores.
Keywords: Solid oxide electrolysis cells, Ni/YSZ cermet, Nanocomposite particles, Hydrogen electrode active
layer, Steam electrolysis
[Received May 8, 2026; Accepted July 25, 2026]

1. Introduction
Solid oxide electrolysis cells (SOECs) are regarded as a
promising electrochemical device for eﬃciently converting electric power into chemical energy.1) SOECs typically
operate at high temperatures of 700–800 °C, so the total
energy required for hydrogen production can be supplied
as not only electricity but also heat. Moreover, the high
temperature operation results in reducing the reaction
overpotential. For these reasons, the required operating
voltage for electrolysis in SOECs is signiﬁcantly lower
than other electrolyzers operating at lower temperatures
(e.g., alkaline electrolysis cells, proton-exchange-membrane
electrolysis cells) owing to the utilization of thermal energy at elevated temperatures. Consequently, the energy
conversion eﬃciency of SOECs is superior to those of
other electrolysis technologies.
When cell performance is improved (i.e., reaction
overpotential and ohmic resistivity of the cell are reduced),
higher current density can be obtained at the same operating voltage. This will lead to reduction of hydrogen

resulting in a reduction in the internal ohmic resistance.
For reduction of polarization resistance, it is necessary to
realize both good gas diﬀusivity in the hydrogen electrode
support and a higher density of reaction sites at the
electrode/electrolyte interfaces. To improve mass transportability through the hydrogen electrode support, a large
amount of pore formers is typically added to increase its
open porosity.2–6) In contrast, increasing the porosity of
hydrogen electrode leads to a decrease in the number of
reaction sites, known as triple phase boundaries (TPBs)
consisting of electrode material, electrolyte material and
gas phase. To increase the number of TPBs, an active layer
is generally introduced into the hydrogen electrode supported cells.7,8) A Ni-YSZ cermet is commonly used as the
hydrogen electrode active layer material in SOECs.

derived from the NiO/YSZ nanocomposite particles
synthesized via a liquid-phase method, exhibits superior
steam electrolysis performance compared with electrodes

In this study, a hydrogen electrode active layer containing a high density of reaction sites was fabricated using
the NiO/YSZ nanocomposite particles on a hydrogen
electrode-supported cell with high porosity. As a result,
good gas diﬀusivity and a high density of electrochemical
reaction sites were achieved. The eﬀect of introducing the
active layer on the polarization resistance (Rpol) and ohmic
resistance (Rohm) was electrochemically evaluated. In addition, the durability was also examined. It was revealed that
incorporation of the active layer signiﬁcantly enhanced the
SOEC performance without negatively aﬀecting durability.

2. Experimental
Coin-sized hydrogen electrode-supported cells were
adopted in this study. The cells consisted of a thin and
porous hydrogen electrode support layer designed in order
to promote gas diﬀusion and dense Ce0.9Gd0.1O2¹¤ (GDC)
interlayer. This GDC interlayer reduced ohmic resistance,
enabling accurate evaluation of the eﬀect of active layer.
These cells were prepared under identical fabrication conditions, except for the presence or absence of the active layer.
The detailed preparation procedure is described below.
Hydrogen-electrode supported SOECs with sizes of
³20 mm diameter were fabricated according to the following sequence. First, green sheet for hydrogen-electrode
support layer of approximately 750 ¯m thick was fabricated
by tape casting of a slurry containing commercial NiO
(Type-F, Nikko Rika Co., Japan) and YSZ (TZ-8Y, Tosoh
Co., Japan) powders with mass fraction of 0.656:0.344
(corresponding to a volume fraction of Ni and YSZ of
0.5:0.5 in cermet), polymethyl methacrylate powder

(PMMA, Soken Kagaku Co., Japan) as pore former, and organic substances including dispersant (polyethyleneimine,
Junsei Kagaku Co., Japan), binder (polyvinyl butyral,
Sekisui Chem., Co., Japan) and plasticizer (di-butyl phthalate, Kanto Chemicals Co., Japan). After drying, the sheet
was cut into disks with ³30 mm diameter. A slurry containing the NiO/YSZ nanocomposite particles grown via a
co-precipitation approach9) was spin-coated onto the supporting layer disk to form the hydrogen electrode active
layer. Then YSZ electrolyte layer was deposited onto the
active layer by spin coating. Afterwards, the laminated
layers were co-ﬁred at 1350 °C for 2 h in air. A dense GDC
interlayer of approximately 2 ¯m thick was fabricated onto
the YSZ electrolyte using pulsed laser deposition.8) Finally,
the air electrode with ³10 mm diameter was fabricated by
screen printing method using commercial La0.6Sr0.4Co0.2Fe0.8O3¹¤ (AGC Seimi Chemical Co., Japan) and GDC
(Shinetsu Chemical Co., Japan) powders with a mass
fraction of 0.7:0.3, followed by sintering at 1000 °C in air.
A cell without the hydrogen electrode active layer was also
prepared using identical conditions for systematic comparison. Hereafter, the cells with and without the active layer
are denoted as Cell A and Cell B, respectively.
The electrochemical performance was evaluated using
the pseudo four-terminal method with a ceramic holder
(CHINO Co., Japan) as schematically shown in Fig. 1.
The cell was set on the holder and then placed into an
electric furnace and heated up to 750 °C. The ﬂow rate of
synthetic air supplied to the air electrode side was 200
sccm and the gas to the hydrogen electrode side was set to
100 sccm of 3 %H2O-97 %H2 to perform NiO reduction.
After the completion of NiO reduction into Ni, the gas
composition of the hydrogen electrode side was changed
to 100 sccm of 30 %H2O-70 %H2, and this condition was
kept during the performance evaluation.

The performance evaluation was carried out at 750 °C
using an electrochemical interface (1287 Solartron analytical Inc., UK) with a frequency response analyzer
(1260A, Solartron analytical Inc., UK). Current densitycell voltage (I-V) characteristics were measured from
open-circuit voltage (OCV) up to 1.5 V. The short-term
durability of electrolysis performance was evaluated by
recording the time-dependent current density change at
potentiostatic conditions at 1.3 V. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was performed at OCV in the
frequency range 10¹1–105 Hz with an applied amplitude of
10 mV. The EIS spectra were deconvoluted and analyzed
using distribution of relaxation times (DRT) approach.10,11)
The microstructures of the post-test Ni/YSZ hydrogen
electrodes were observed using SEM (Helios 5 Hydra CX,
ThermoFisher). Prior to the observation, the fractured cells
were embedded in resin and mechanically polished.

Figure 5 shows the short-term durability testing results
of the SOECs with potentiostatic condition at 1.3 V and
750 °C. The initial current densities of Cell A and Cell B
were 1.97 and 1.33 A·cm¹2, respectively. After the durability testing, the current densities of Cell A and Cell B
decreased to 1.84 and 1.19 A cm¹2, respectively. The
increases in resistance of Cell A and Cell B per unit time
during durability testing were calculated assuming an
overpotential of 0.3 V, yielding values of 8 and 13
m³·cm2·h¹1, respectively.
Figure 6(a) shows Rpol before and after the short-term
durability test. Rpol of Cell A was lower than that of Cell B
before and after testing. Moreover, the rates of Rpol increase during the test were almost the same for Cell A and
Cell B, at 0.3 m³·cm2·h¹1. The DRT data for Cell A and
Cell B are shown in Figs. 6(b) and 6(c), respectively. The
DRT peaks corresponding to P2, P3, P4 and P5 increased
in both Cell A and Cell B. As discussed earlier, these
peaks were associated with hydrogen electrode reactions.

The second is that the O2¹ migration length of Cell B in
the hydrogen electrode is longer than that of Cell A. As
shown in Fig. 8(a), the active layer had a higher density of
TPBs so the electrochemically eﬀective electrode thickness will be thin. Moreover, the large pores originated
from PMMA existed everywhere in the hydrogen electrode of Cell B. Therefore, O2¹ generated from TPB of
Cell B traveled a tortuous route due to increased tortuosity
of O2¹ transport whereas O2¹ moved a relatively straight
path in Cell A. From these reasons, it caused increasing
O2¹ transport distance of Cell B and its Rohm became
larger than that of Cell A. Thus, the higher Rohm observed
for Cell B can be attributed to the lower connectivity of its
relatively sparse YSZ network, which limits ionic transport
pathways. In contrast, the denser and more continuous
YSZ network in Cell A facilitates O2¹ conduction, thereby
resulting in a lower Rohm. Furthermore, the slight increase
in Rohm after 150 h, suggests that the YSZ networks in the

hydrogen electrodes of both Cell A and Cell B remained
stable throughout the durability testing.

4. Conclusion
Introduction of a Ni/YSZ hydrogen electrode active
layer using the pre-fabricated NiO/YSZ nanocomposite
particles is a promising approach for remarkable enhancement of steam electrolysis performance of SOECs without
sacriﬁcing durability. The higher performance can be
attributed to the reduction of both Rohm and Rpol. The
nanocomposite active layer which contains many active
TPBs due to homogeneous distribution of ﬁne Ni particles
and pores can realize a decrease in the polarization resistance without impeding gas diﬀusion. Rohm of Cell A was
also improved compared to Cell B because it has many
contact interfaces between the electrolyte and hydrogen
activation layers, and the transport distance of O2¹ in
Cell A was shorter than that in Cell B. In consequence, the
current density of Cell A with the nanocomposite active
layer was 1.94 A·cm¹2 at 1.3 V and 750 °C, which is higher
than reported values including cells with an active layer.
In addition, the cell with active layer maintained high
performance without signiﬁcant degradation after 150 h.
