Abstract
. In this paper, based on the fast averaged L1 method, we present an error analysis for time-fractional advection-dispersion equations with a weak singularity at the initial time. An integrating-factor transformation is introduced to convert the tempered fractional derivative into the standard Caputo derivative, which is more suitable for discretization using the fast averaged L1 method. A sum-of-exponentials approximation is then incorporated into the averaged L1 method to reduce computational cost and storage while preserving the desired accuracy. By deriving error estimates for the discrete coefficients and the accumulated truncation errors, we establish the stability and H 1 H^{1} -norm convergence analysis, with a convergence order higher than those in the published literature. Numerical examples are tested to validate our theoretical results. The effects of the fractional parameters α \alpha and λ \lambda on the solution are discussed. The memory effect and long-time tail phenomenon, which are known to exist in real systems yet cannot be captured by classical integer-order equations, are again found in the current fractional case.

1.
Introduction

As seen in
[
4
,
8
,
21
,
29
,
32
]
, fractional partial differential equations (PDEs) have provided a powerful tool for simulating challenging phenomena such as anomalous diffusion, transport and memory effects. When the fractional derivative is introduced into advection-dispersion models, the core significance lies in overcoming the limitations of traditional integer-order models based on Brownian motion, thereby accurately characterizing the ubiquitous phenomenon of anomalous diffusion in nature and engineering. For integer-order diffusion models, the mean squared displacement scales linearly with time, indicating that these models fail to describe behaviors such as the heavy tail in pollutant transport through porous media, sub-diffusion in drug release from biological tissues, or the unexpectedly rapid super-diffusion jumps in turbulence and plasmas. While for fractional-order models, by virtue of their nonlocality and memory effects (i.e., the time-fractional derivative captures the system’s dependence on its historical states, and the space-fractional derivative accounts for long-range, Lévy-flight-type correlations), these models can provide a physically unified description spanning the entire spectrum from sub-diffusion to super-diffusion. Consequently, research on fractional-order models not only helps offer a more reliable theoretical foundation for applications such as groundwater contamination prediction, controlled drug delivery design, and reservoir engineering, but also drives the development of anomalous transport mechanisms in mathematical modeling, scientific computing, and inverse problems, establishing the fractional derivative as a key tool in advection-dispersion theory in complex media. So far, a substantial amount of work has been done on the qualitative analysis of fractional PDEs and the numerical methods for the computation of their solutions, e.g.,

,

,

,

,

,

.

In this paper, we investigate the stability and convergence of the fast averaged L1 method on nonuniform time meshes for a time-fractional advection-dispersion equation (TFADE):

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha,\lambda}u=\Delta u-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{j}}u+f(\bm{x},t),\quad&(\bm{x},t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
u(\bm{x},t)=0,\quad&(\bm{x},t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T],\\[5.0pt]
u(\bm{x},0)=\phi(\bm{x}),\quad&\bm{x}\in\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\lambda>0
,
0<\alpha<1
, and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

(d=1,2)
. As seen in
[
18
]
, the Caputo tempered fractional derivative is defined as follows:

(1.2)

{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha,\lambda}u=\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(e^{\lambda s}u(s))^{\prime}}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}\,ds,

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
denotes the gamma function. When
\lambda=0
, (
1.2
) becomes the standard Caputo fractional derivative, i.e.,

{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}u=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{u^{\prime}(s)}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}\,ds.

Compared with the model in
[
30
]
, the proposed time-fractional advection-dispersion equation (TFADE) presented in (
1.1
) exhibits several advantages. First, it incorporates the Caputo tempered fractional derivative, which is capable of describing the transition from power-law tailing to exponential tailing. Second, the inclusion of a first-order derivative advection term allows the model to better simulate transport phenomena in heterogeneous media. However, the first-order derivative term is spatially non-self-adjoint, which introduces additional nonsymmetric terms into the error equation. This property greatly complicates the stability and convergence analysis for (
1.1
). To address these difficulties, we propose an integrating-factor transformation that converts the tempered fractional derivative into the standard Caputo derivative and eliminates the first-order derivative term.

As mentioned in
[
21
]

This has motivated the development of numerical methods on nonuniform temporal meshes, with the aim of recovering optimal convergence rates in the presence of such initial weak singularity. σ

2.
An integrating-factor transformation for (
1.1
) and the fast temporal discretization

In this section, we introduce an integrating-factor transformation for (
1.1
) to facilitate the error estimates in the following sections. We then present a fast temporal discretization of (
1.1
) using the fast averaged L1 method.

For (
1.1
), the fast averaged L1 method cannot be applied directly to discrete
{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha,\lambda}u
due to the presence of the parameter
\lambda
. We therefore introduce an integrating-factor transformation:

(2.1)

v(\bm{x},t)=e^{\lambda t-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}}\,u(\bm{x},t).

As a result, the Caputo tempered derivative becomes

(2.2)

{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha,\lambda}u=e^{-\lambda t}\,{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}(e^{\lambda t}u)=e^{-\lambda t+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}}\,{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}v.

The standard Caputo derivative acting on
v
is then obtained. This allows us to construct the temporal discretization for
{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}v
rather than for
{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha,\lambda}u
. Based on (
2.1
), we further derive

(2.3)

\partial_{x_{j}}u=e^{-\lambda t+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}}\bigl(\partial_{x_{j}}v+\tfrac{v}{2}\bigr),\qquad\partial_{x_{j}x_{j}}u=e^{-\lambda t+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}}\bigl(\partial_{x_{j}x_{j}}v+\partial_{x_{j}}v+\tfrac{v}{4}\bigr).

Substituting (
2.2
)-(
2.3
) into (
1.1
) yields

(2.4)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}v=\Delta v+\mu v+\widetilde{f}(\bm{x},t),\quad&(\bm{x},t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\[8.53581pt]
v(\bm{x},t)=0,\quad&(\bm{x},t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T],\\
v(\bm{x},0)=\widetilde{\phi}(\bm{x}):=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}}\phi(\bm{x}),\quad&\bm{x}\in\Omega,\end{cases}

where

\mu:=\lambda-\frac{d}{4},\qquad\widetilde{f}(\bm{x},t):=e^{\lambda t-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}}f(\bm{x},t).\vskip-1.42271pt

Remark 2.1
.

Based on the integrating-factor transformation (
2.1
),
(
2.4
) has two significant advantages in comparison with (
1.1
): 1) the tempered fractional derivative
{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha,\lambda}u
is converted to the standard Caputo derivative
{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}v
, and it is more suitable for discretization using the fast averaged L1 method; 2) Compared with (
1.1
), the advection term in (
2.4
) is eliminated. This greatly facilitates the subsequent stability and convergence analysis.

Next we focus on the fast temporal discretization for (
2.4
). As noted in
[
2
, Chapter 6]
, the graded temporal mesh is known to be more effective than the uniform mesh for computing the time-fractional advection-dispersion equation. Motivated by this, we employ the following graded temporal mesh:

(2.5)

t_{n}=T(\frac{n}{M})^{r},\qquad n=0,1,\dots,M,\vskip 4.26773pt

where the grading parameter
r\geq 1
. To facilitate the subsequent derivation, we define

\tau_{n}:=t_{n}-t_{n-1},\qquad\nabla_{\tau}v^{n}:=\tfrac{1}{\tau_{n}}{(v^{n}-v^{n-1})},\qquad v^{n-\frac{1}{2}}:=\tfrac{1}{2}(v^{n}+v^{n-1}).\vskip 2.84544pt

Integrating both sides of (
2.4
) over
[t_{n-1},t_{n}]
and taking the average, we obtain

(2.6)

\nabla_{\tau}v^{n}+\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}v\,dt=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}(\Delta v+\mu v)\,dt+\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\widetilde{f}(\bm{x},t)\,dt,\quad 1\leq n\leq M.\vskip 2.84544pt

To discretize
\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}v\,dt
in (
2.6
), we use the fast averaged L1 approximation presented in
[
30
]
as follows:

(2.7)

\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}v\,dt\approx\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\widetilde{a}_{n,k}\nabla_{\tau}v^{k},

(2.8)

\begin{cases}\widetilde{a}_{n,k}=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{N_{\exp}}\varpi_{\ell}e^{-s_{\ell}(t-s)}\,ds\,dt,&1\leq k\leq n-2,\\[14.0pt]
\widetilde{a}_{n,k}=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{\min\{t,t_{k}\}}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\,ds\,dt,&k=n-1,n.\end{cases}\vskip 4.26773pt

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
denotes the gamma function. Moreover, as shown in
[
12
,
32
]
,

N_{\exp}
represents the number of exponential terms,
\varpi_{\ell}>0
and
s_{\ell}>0
(
\ell=1,2,\ldots
) are the SOE weights and exponential parameters, respectively. For (
2.7
), we denote
\bar{\partial}_{\tau,\varepsilon}^{\alpha}v^{n}:=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\widetilde{a}_{n,k}\nabla_{\tau}v^{k}
, and rewrite it as

(2.9)

\bar{\partial}_{\tau,\varepsilon}^{\alpha}v^{n}=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{N_{\exp}}\varpi_{\ell}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{n}(v^{n})+\sum\limits_{k=n-1}^{n}\widetilde{a}_{n,k}\nabla_{\tau}v^{k},

where

\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{n}(v^{n})=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-2}\nabla_{\tau}v^{k}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}e^{-s_{\ell}(t-s)}\,ds\,dt.\vskip 4.26773pt

It is worth noting that
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{n}(v^{n})
can be evaluated recursively via

\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{n}(v^{n})=\frac{\tau_{n-1}}{\tau_{n}}e^{-s_{\ell}\tau_{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{n-1}(v^{n-1})+\nabla_{\tau}v^{n-2}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{n-3}}^{t_{n-2}}e^{-s_{\ell}(t-s)}\,ds\,dt,\quad 4\leq n\leq M.

Remark 2.2
.

Compared with (
2.7
) or (
2.9
), the classical averaged L1 approximation, as shown in
[
11
]
, is

(2.10)

\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}v\,dt\approx\bar{\partial}_{\tau}^{\alpha}v^{n}:=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}a_{n,k}\nabla_{\tau}v^{k},

where

a_{n,k}=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{\min\{t,t_{k}\}}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\,ds\,dt,\quad 1\leq k\leq n.\vskip 4.26773pt

\bar{\partial}_{\tau,\varepsilon}^{\alpha}v^{n}
in (
2.9
) offers a fast advantage over
\bar{\partial}_{\tau}^{\alpha}v^{n}
in (
2.10
), i.e., only
\mathcal{O}(1)
operations are required to update
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{\,n}(v^{n})
given
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}^{\,n-1}(v^{n-1})
. Furthermore, as noted in
[
30
]
, for sufficiently large
M
, the storage requirement and computational cost for evaluating
\bar{\partial}_{\tau,\varepsilon}^{\alpha}v^{n}
are significantly lower than those for evaluating
\bar{\partial}_{\tau}^{\alpha}v^{n}
. Specifically, evaluating
\bar{\partial}_{\tau,\varepsilon}^{\alpha}v^{n}
requires
\mathcal{O}(\log M)
storage and
\mathcal{O}(M\log M)
operations, whereas evaluating
\bar{\partial}_{\tau}^{\alpha}v^{n}
requires
\mathcal{O}(M)
storage and
\mathcal{O}(M^{2})
operations.

Now we focus on local truncation errors (i.e.,
R_{1}^{n}
and
R_{2}^{n}
) from the fast averaged L1 approximation and the trapezoidal rule as follows:

(2.11)

R_{1}^{n}:=\bar{\partial}_{\tau,\varepsilon}^{\alpha}v^{n}-\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}v\,dt,

(2.12)

R_{2}^{n}:=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}(\Delta v+\mu v)\,dt-(\Delta v^{n-\frac{1}{2}}+\mu v^{n-\frac{1}{2}}).\vskip 2.84544pt

To facilitate the presentation of an estimate for
R_{1}^{n}
and
R_{2}^{n}
, as well as the analysis in the subsequent sections, the following preliminaries are provided.

•

The space
L^{2}(\Omega)
is defined by

L^{2}(\Omega):=\left\{u:u\text{ is measurable on }\Omega\text{ and }\|u\|<\infty\right\},

equipped with the norm and inner product

(2.13)

\|u\|=(u,u)^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}u({\bm{x}})v({\bm{x}})\,d{\bm{x}},\qquad\forall u,v\in L^{2}(\Omega).

•

The space
H^{m}(\Omega)
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
is the space of functions
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that all the distributional derivatives of order up to
m
can be represented by functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. That is,

H^{m}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):D^{\bm{\alpha}}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\;\;\mathrm{for}\;\;0\leq|\bm{\alpha}|\leq m\right\},

equipped with the norm

(2.14)

\|u\|_{m}=\left(\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=0}^{m}\left\|D^{\bm{\alpha}}u\right\|^{2}\right)^{1/2},\qquad D^{\bm{\alpha}}=\frac{\partial^{|\bm{\alpha}|}}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\partial x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}},

where
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})
is a multiindex of order
|\bm{\alpha}|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}
. Moreover,

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega):u|_{\partial\Omega}=0\right\}.

An estimate for
R_{1}^{n}
and
R_{2}^{n}
, given in (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), is presented in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
19
,
30
]
)
.

Suppose that
C>0
is a constant,
\delta\geq\alpha
and that for
0<t\leq T
,

\|\partial_{t}^{\ell}v(t)\|_{m}\leq C\bigl(1+t^{\delta-\ell}\bigr),\qquad\ell=0,1,2,\quad m\geq 1.

Then, for
1\leq n\leq M
, there exists a positive constant
C_{1}
, independent of
M
, such that

(2.15)

\|R_{1}^{n}\|_{m}\leq C_{1}\begin{cases}\varepsilon+M^{-2}t_{n}^{\delta-\alpha-\frac{2}{r}},&r(1+\delta)>2,\\[5.69054pt]
\varepsilon+M^{-r(1+\delta)}t_{n}^{-1-\alpha}\ln(n+1),&r(1+\delta)=2,\\[5.69054pt]
\varepsilon+M^{-r(1+\delta)}t_{n}^{-1-\alpha},&r(1+\delta)<2,\end{cases}\vskip 4.26773pt

(2.16)

\|R_{2}^{n}\|_{m}\leq C_{1}\begin{cases}M^{-r(\delta+1)},&n=1,\\[5.69054pt]
M^{-2}t_{n}^{\delta-\frac{2}{r}},&n\geq 2,\end{cases}\vskip 4.26773pt

where
\varepsilon>0
is a prescribed tolerance and
\|\cdot\|_{m}
is defined in (
2.14
).

3.
Stability and
H^{1}
-norm convergence analysis for a semidiscrete scheme

In this section, we focus on the stability and
H^{1}
-norm convergence analysis for a semidiscrete scheme. Based on Lemma
2.1
,
\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}v\,dt
and
\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}(\Delta v+\mu v)\,dt
given in (
2.11
)-(
2.12
) are well approximated by
\bar{\partial}_{\tau,\varepsilon}^{\alpha}v^{n}
and
\Delta v^{n-\frac{1}{2}}+\mu v^{n-\frac{1}{2}}
. Therefore, (
2.6
) becomes

(3.1)

\nabla_{\tau}v^{n}+\bar{\partial}_{\tau,\varepsilon}^{\alpha}v^{n}=\Delta v^{n-\frac{1}{2}}+\mu v^{n-\frac{1}{2}}+\bar{f}^{n-\frac{1}{2}},\quad 1\leq n\leq M,\vskip 2.84544pt

where
\bar{f}^{\,n-\frac{1}{2}}:=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\widetilde{f}(\bm{x},t)\,dt
.
Here (
3.1
) is called the semidiscrete scheme. We start with the following two lemmas, which will be used in the subsequent stability and
H^{1}
-norm convergence analysis for (
3.1
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
\varepsilon\leq c_{\alpha,r}T^{-\alpha}M^{-\alpha}
and

(3.2)

\frac{(2-\alpha)2^{1-\alpha}-1}{\Gamma(3-\alpha)}\left(\frac{rT}{M}\right)^{1-\alpha}+C^{*}\frac{rT}{M}\leq 1,\vskip 2.84544pt

then the following inequality holds:

(3.3)

\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,k}}{\tau_{k}}\leq\begin{cases}\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,k+1}}{\tau_{k+1}},\quad&1\leq k\leq n-2,\\[10.0pt]
\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}-C^{*}+\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,k+1}}{\tau_{k+1}},\quad&k=n-1,\end{cases}

where
\varepsilon>0
denotes a prescribed tolerance, the same as that in (
2.15
),
C^{*}
is an arbitrarily prescribed real number, and
c_{\alpha,r}
is a positive constant depending only on
\alpha
and
r
.

Proof.

To present our proof more clearly, the cases
1\leq k\leq n-3
,
k=n-2
and
k=n-1
are proved separately as follows:

Case 1 (
1\leq k\leq n-3
).
We denote
h(x)=\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{N_{\exp}}\varpi_{\ell}e^{-s_{\ell}x}/\Gamma(1-\alpha)
. Using (
2.8
) and the mean value theorem, we derive

(3.4)

\begin{split}\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,k}}{\tau_{k}}&=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}\tau_{k}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}h(t-s)\,ds\,dt=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}h(t-\xi_{k})\,dt,\vskip 4.26773pt\end{split}

where
\xi_{k}\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
. Since
h(x)
is monotonically nonincreasing, we obtain (
3.3
) from (
3.4
) directly.

Case 2 (
k=n-2
).

Based on (
2.8
) , we derive

(3.5)

\begin{split}\tfrac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n-2}}{\tau_{n-2}}=\;&\frac{1}{\tau_{n}\tau_{n-2}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{n-3}}^{t_{n-2}}\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{N_{\exp}}\varpi_{\ell}e^{-s_{\ell}(t-s)}\,ds\,dt\\[5.0pt]
=\;&\tfrac{1}{\tau_{n}\tau_{n-2}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{n-3}}^{t_{n-2}}(\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{N_{\exp}}\varpi_{\ell}e^{-s_{\ell}(t-s)}-(t-s)^{-\alpha})\,ds\,dt\\[5.0pt]
\;&+\frac{1}{\tau_{n}\tau_{n-2}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{n-3}}^{t_{n-2}}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\,ds\,dt.\\[3.0pt]
\end{split}

As shown in
[
12
,
32
]
, for a prescribed tolerance
\varepsilon>0
,
|\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{N_{\exp}}\varpi_{\ell}e^{-s_{\ell}(t-s)}-(t-s)^{-\alpha}|\leq\varepsilon
holds for
t-s\in[\tau_{1},T]
. Applying this to (
3.5
) yields

(3.6)

\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n-2}}{\tau_{n-2}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}+\frac{1}{\tau_{n}\tau_{n-2}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{n-3}}^{t_{n-2}}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\,ds\,dt.\vskip 4.26773pt

With (
2.8
) and (
3.6
), it holds

(3.7)

\begin{split}\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n-2}}{\tau_{n-2}}\leq\;&\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}+\frac{1}{\tau_{n}\tau_{n-2}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{n-3}}^{t_{n-2}}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\,ds\,dt\\[5.0pt]
&-\frac{1}{\tau_{n}\tau_{n-1}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{n-2}}^{t_{n-1}}(t-s)^{-\alpha}\,ds\,dt+\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n-1}}{\tau_{n-1}}\\[5.0pt]
\leq\;&\frac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{1}(\frac{1}{\tau_{n-2}}\int_{0}^{\tau_{n-2}}(t\tau_{n}+\tau_{n-1})^{-\alpha}\,ds.\\[5.0pt]
&-\int_{0}^{1}(t\tau_{n}+s\tau_{n-1})^{-\alpha}\,ds)\,dt+\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n-1}}{\tau_{n-1}}.\end{split}

Based on the mean value theorem, we can further derive from (
3.7
)

(3.8)

\tfrac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n-2}}{\tau_{n-2}}-\tfrac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n-1}}{\tau_{n-1}}\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}+\tfrac{\tau_{n-1}^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}((\xi+1)^{-\alpha}-\int_{0}^{1}(\xi+s)^{-\alpha}\,ds),

where
\xi\in[0,\frac{\tau_{n}}{\tau_{n-1}}]
. Based on (
2.5
), we have
\xi\leq\frac{\tau_{n}}{\tau_{n-1}}\leq 3^{r-1}
for
n\geq 3
. Consequently, for the last term on the right‑hand side of (
3.8
), there exists a positive constant
c_{\alpha,r}
, depending only on
\alpha
and
r
, such that

(3.9)

(\xi+1)^{-\alpha}-\int_{0}^{1}(\xi+s)^{-\alpha}\,ds\leq-c_{\alpha,r}.

With
\tau_{n-1}\leq rTM^{-1}
and
\varepsilon\leq c_{\alpha,r}T^{-\alpha}M^{-\alpha}
, we derive (
3.3
)
by substituting (
3.9
) into
(
3.8
).

Case 3 (
k=n-1
).

From (
2.8
), we have

(3.10)

\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n}}{\tau_{n}}=\frac{\tau_{n}^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(3-\alpha)}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n-1}}{\tau_{n-1}}=\frac{\left(\tau_{n}+\tau_{n-1}\right)^{2-\alpha}-\tau_{n}^{2-\alpha}-\tau_{n-1}^{2-\alpha}}{\tau_{n}\tau_{n-1}\Gamma(3-\alpha)}.

With the mean value theorem, we obtain from (
3.10
)

(3.11)

\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n-1}}{\tau_{n-1}}\leq\frac{(2-\alpha)2^{1-\alpha}}{\Gamma(3-\alpha)}\tau_{n}^{-\alpha}.\vskip 2.84544pt

Using (
3.10
)-(
3.11
) and
\tau_{n}\leq rTM^{-1}
, we derive

(3.12)

\begin{split}&\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n-1}}{\tau_{n-1}}-\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}+C^{*}-\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n}}{\tau_{n}}\\[5.0pt]
\leq\;&\frac{(2-\alpha)2^{1-\alpha}-1}{\Gamma(3-\alpha)}\tau_{n}^{-\alpha}-\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}+C^{*}\\[6.0pt]
\leq\;&\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\left(\tfrac{(2-\alpha)2^{1-\alpha}-1}{\Gamma(3-\alpha)}\left(\tfrac{rT}{M}\right)^{1-\alpha}-1+rTC^{*}M^{-1}\right).\end{split}

Under assumption (
3.2
), the quantity in the last line of (
3.12
) is nonpositive. Thus, (
3.3
) follows, completing the proof.
∎

An estimate for

R^{n}:=R_{1}^{n}+R_{2}^{n}
(see (

)-(
2.12

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Lemma
2.1
hold and
r\geq 4
. Then, we have

(3.13)

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{M}\tau_{n}\|R^{n}\|_{m}\leq C_{2}(\varepsilon+M^{-2})\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\sum\limits_{n=1}^{M}\tau_{n}\|R^{n}\|_{m}^{2}\leq C_{2}(\varepsilon+M^{-2})^{2},

where
C_{2}
is a constant independent of
M
and
\|\cdot\|_{m}
is defined in (
2.14
).

Proof.

Since
r\geq 4
and
\delta\geq\alpha>0
(see Lemma
2.1
), we have

r(1+\delta)>4
. It follows from Lemma
2.1
that

(3.14)

\|R^{n}\|_{m}\leq C_{1}\begin{cases}\varepsilon+M^{-2}t_{1}^{\delta-\alpha-\frac{2}{r}}+M^{-r(\delta+1)},&n=1,\\[5.69054pt]
\varepsilon+M^{-2}t_{n}^{\delta-\alpha-\frac{2}{r}}+M^{-2}t_{n}^{\delta-\frac{2}{r}},&n\geq 2.\end{cases}

When
n=1
, (
2.5
) gives
t_{1}=TM^{-r}
, indicating that

(3.15)

M^{-r(\delta+1)}\leq M^{-r(\delta+1)+r(1+\alpha)}=M^{-r(\delta-\alpha)}=T^{-\delta+\alpha+\frac{2}{r}}M^{-2}t_{1}^{\delta-\alpha-\frac{2}{r}}.

Similarly, we further derive

(3.16)

\begin{split}M^{-2}t_{n}^{\delta-\frac{2}{r}}\leq T^{\alpha}M^{-2}t_{n}^{\delta-\alpha-\frac{2}{r}},\quad\quad\forall\,n\geq 2.\end{split}

Consequently, substituting (
3.15
)–(
3.16
) into (
3.14
) yields

(3.17)

\|R^{n}\|_{m}\leq C_{2}(\varepsilon+M^{-2}t_{n}^{\delta-\alpha-\frac{2}{r}}),

where the constant
C_{2}
is independent of
M
. Based on (
2.5
), we have

(3.18)

\tau_{n}=t_{n}-t_{n-1}\leq CM^{-r}n^{r-1},\qquad 1\leq n\leq M.

where the constant
C
is independent of
M
. With (
3.18
) and
r(1+\delta-\alpha)\geq r\geq 4
, we derive

(3.19)

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{M}\tau_{n}M^{-2}t_{n}^{\delta-\alpha-\frac{2}{r}}\leq C_{2}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{M}M^{-r}n^{r-1}M^{-2}(\tfrac{n}{M})^{r(\delta-\alpha)-2}\leq C_{2}M^{-2}.

With (
3.17
) and (
3.19
), we obtain

(3.20)

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{M}\tau_{n}\|R^{n}\|_{m}\leq C_{2}(\varepsilon+M^{-2}).

Similarly, from (
3.17
), we derive

(3.21)

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{M}\tau_{n}\|R^{n}\|_{m}^{2}&\leq C_{2}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{M}\tau_{n}(\varepsilon^{2}+2\varepsilon M^{-2}t_{n}^{\delta-\alpha-\frac{2}{r}}+M^{-4}t_{n}^{2\delta-2\alpha-\frac{4}{r}})\\
&\leq C_{2}(T\varepsilon^{2}+2\varepsilon\sum\limits_{n=1}^{M}\tau_{n}M^{-2}t_{n}^{\delta-\alpha-\frac{2}{r}}+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{M}\tau_{n}M^{-4}t_{n}^{2\delta-2\alpha-\frac{4}{r}}).\end{split}

With (
3.18
) and
r+2r(\delta-\alpha)\geq 4
, it holds

(3.22)

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{M}\tau_{n}M^{-4}t_{n}^{2\delta-2\alpha-\frac{4}{r}}\leq C_{2}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{M}M^{-r}n^{r-1}M^{-4}t_{n}^{2(\delta-\alpha)-\frac{4}{r}}\leq C_{2}M^{-4}.

Substituting (
3.19
) and (
3.22

3.21
) yields

(3.23)

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{M}\tau_{n}\|R^{n}\|_{m}^{2}\leq C_{2}(\varepsilon+M^{-2})^{2}.\vskip 1.42271pt

Finally, (
3.13
) can be proved by using (
3.23
) and (
3.20
).
∎

Next, the stability and
H^{1}
-norm convergence analysis for (

) are presented in the Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
, respectively.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Lemma
3.1
hold and that
0\leq\rho<\rho_{1}
. Then, for
1\leq n\leq M
, the numerical solution of (
3.1
) satisfies

\|v^{n}\|\leq\begin{cases}e^{\tfrac{\rho\rho_{1}t_{n}}{\rho_{1}-\rho}}(\|v^{0}\|+\tfrac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}-\rho}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{j}\|\bar{f}^{\,j-\frac{1}{2}}\|),&0<\rho<\rho_{1},\\[15.0pt]
\|v^{0}\|+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{j}\|\bar{f}^{\,j-\frac{1}{2}}\|,&\rho=0,\end{cases}

where
\|\cdot\|
is defined in (
2.13
),
C_{P}
is the Poincaré constant,

\rho_{1}=\tfrac{2}{\max\limits_{1\leq n\leq M}\tau_{n}}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\rho=\max\{\mu-C_{P}^{-2},0\}.

Proof.

Let
a_{\mu}(u,w):=(\nabla u,\nabla w)-(\mu u,w)
. Multiplying (
3.1
) by a test function
\phi
yields

(3.24)

(\nabla_{\tau}v^{n},\phi)+(\bar{\partial}_{\tau,\varepsilon}^{\alpha}v^{n},\phi)+a_{\mu}(v^{n-\frac{1}{2}},\phi)=(\bar{f}^{\,n-\frac{1}{2}},\phi).\vskip 2.84544pt

Based on the Poincaré inequality, we have

a_{\mu}(w,w)=\|\nabla w\|^{2}-(\mu w,w)\geq-\rho\|w\|^{2},\qquad\forall\,w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

Let
\{\phi_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
be a
L^{2}
-orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions associated with
a_{\mu}
. Then we have

a_{\mu}(\phi_{m},w)=\lambda_{m}^{\mu}(\phi_{m},w),\qquad\forall w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\vskip 2.84544pt

where
\lambda_{m}^{\mu}\geq-\rho
. Let

(3.25)

v^{n}=\sum\limits_{m\geq 1}v_{m}^{n}\phi_{m}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\bar{f}^{\,n-\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\bar{f}_{m}^{\,n-\frac{1}{2}}\phi_{m},

indicating that (
3.24
) becomes

(3.26)

\frac{1}{{\tau_{n}}}{(v_{m}^{n}-v_{m}^{n-1})}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,k}}{\tau_{k}}(v_{m}^{k}-v_{m}^{k-1})+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{m}^{\mu}(v_{m}^{n}+v_{m}^{n-1})=\bar{f}_{m}^{\,n-\frac{1}{2}},

where
\{\widetilde{a}_{n,k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
are given by (
2.8
). We rewrite (
3.26
) as

(3.27)

D_{m,n}v_{m}^{n}=(D_{m,n}-\lambda_{m}^{\mu}-\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n-1}}{\tau_{n-1}})v_{m}^{n-1}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-2}(\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,k+1}}{\tau_{k+1}}-\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,k}}{\tau_{k}})v_{m}^{k}+\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,1}}{\tau_{1}}v_{m}^{0}+\bar{f}_{m}^{\,n-\frac{1}{2}},

where

D_{m,n}:=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}+\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n}}{\tau_{n}}+\frac{\lambda_{m}^{\mu}}{2}.\vskip 4.26773pt

Since
\lambda_{m}^{\mu}\geq-\rho>-\rho_{1}=\tfrac{2}{\max\limits_{1\leq n\leq M}\tau_{n}}
, we further derive

(3.28)

0\leq\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}-\frac{1}{\max\limits_{1\leq n\leq M}\tau_{n}}<\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}+\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n}}{\tau_{n}}-\frac{\rho}{2}\leq D_{m,n}.\vskip 2.84544pt

By Lemma
3.1
with
C^{*}=0
, we have

(3.29)

\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n-1}}{\tau_{n-1}}\leq\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}+\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n}}{\tau_{n}}\leq D_{m,n}-\frac{\lambda_{m}^{\mu}}{2}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,k}}{\tau_{k}}\leq\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,k+1}}{\tau_{k+1}}\,(1\leq k\leq n-2),\vskip 1.9919pt

indicating that
\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,k+1}}{\tau_{k+1}}-\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,k}}{\tau_{k}}
given in (
3.27
) are nonnegative. We define

(3.30)

M_{m}^{n}:=\max\limits_{0\leq j\leq n}|v_{m}^{j}|.

Taking absolute values in (
3.27
) and combining
-\lambda_{m}^{\mu}\leq\rho
with (
3.29
) yields

(3.31)

\begin{split}D_{m,n}|v_{m}^{n}|\leq\;&\bigl(\bigl|D_{m,n}-\lambda_{m}^{\mu}-\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n-1}}{\tau_{n-1}}\bigl|+\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n,n-1}}{\tau_{n-1}}\bigl)M_{m}^{n-1}+|\bar{f}_{m}^{\,n-\frac{1}{2}}|\\[3.0pt]
\leq\;&\left(D_{m,n}+\rho\right)M_{m}^{n-1}+|\bar{f}_{m}^{\,n-\frac{1}{2}}|.\\[3.0pt]
\end{split}

It follows from (
3.28
) that
D_{m,n}\geq\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}-\frac{\rho}{2}
, which, together with (
3.31
), implies

(3.32)

|v_{m}^{n}|\leq(1+\frac{2\rho\tau_{n}}{2-\rho\tau_{n}})M_{m}^{n-1}+\frac{2\tau_{n}}{2-\rho\tau_{n}}|\bar{f}_{m}^{\,n-\frac{1}{2}}|.\vskip 4.26773pt

Based on the notation
\rho_{1}=\frac{2}{\max\limits_{1\leq n\leq M}\tau_{n}}
, we derive from
(
3.30
) and (
3.32
)

M_{m}^{n}=\max\{v_{m}^{n},M_{m}^{n-1}\}\leq(1+\frac{\rho\rho_{1}\tau_{n}}{\rho_{1}-\rho})M_{m}^{n-1}+\frac{\rho_{1}\tau_{n}}{\rho_{1}-\rho}|\bar{f}_{m}^{\,n-\frac{1}{2}}|.\vskip 2.84544pt

Using the discrete Gronwall inequality and
|M_{m}^{0}|=|v_{m}^{0}|
, we further derive

(3.33)

v_{m}^{n}\leq M_{m}^{n}\leq e^{\tfrac{\rho\rho_{1}t_{n}}{\rho_{1}-\rho}}(|v_{m}^{0}|+\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}-\rho}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{j}|\bar{f}_{m}^{\,j-\frac{1}{2}}|).\vskip 2.84544pt

We apply the Parseval’s identity to (
3.25
). Together with (
3.33
), we have

(3.34)

\|v^{n}\|=(\sum\limits_{m\geq 1}|v_{m}^{n}|^{2})^{1/2}\leq e^{\tfrac{\rho\rho_{1}t_{n}}{\rho_{1}-\rho}}(\|v^{0}\|+\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}-\rho}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{j}\|\bar{f}^{\,j-\frac{1}{2}}\|).

Setting
\rho=0
in (
3.34
) gives

\|v^{n}\|\leq\|v^{0}\|+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{j}\|\bar{f}^{\,j-\frac{1}{2}}\|.

This completes the proof.
∎

The
H^{1}
-norm convergence analysis for (

) is now summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that the assumptions of Lemma
3.1
hold and
r\geq 4
. Then, we have

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\|e^{k}-e^{k-1}\|^{2}}{\tau_{k}}+\frac{\sigma(\alpha)}{\Gamma(3-\alpha)}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\|e^{k}-e^{k-1}\|^{2}}{\tau_{k}^{\alpha}}+\|e^{n}\|_{1}^{2}\leq C_{3}(\varepsilon+M^{-2})^{2},

where
v
is the exact solution of (
2.4
),
\{v^{k}\}_{k=1}^{M}
is the numerical solution of (
3.1
), and the norms
\|\cdot\|
and
\|\cdot\|_{1}
are defined in (
2.13
) and (
2.14
), respectively. The constant
C_{2}
is the same as that in (
3.13
).
Furthermore, we define

e^{k}:=v(t_{k})-v^{k},\quad\sigma(\alpha)=2\alpha(1-2^{-\alpha}),\quad C_{3}=C_{2}|\mu|t_{n}e^{\frac{\rho\rho_{1}t_{n}}{\rho_{1}-\rho}}(\tfrac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}-\rho})^{2}+1.

Proof.

The error equation for (
3.1
) is

(3.35)

\begin{cases}\nabla_{\tau}e^{n}+\bar{\partial}_{\tau,\varepsilon}^{\alpha}e^{n}=\Delta e^{n-\frac{1}{2}}+\mu e^{n-\frac{1}{2}}+R^{n},\quad\,\text{in }\Omega,\\[4.0pt]
e^{n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad\quad e^{0}|_{\overline{\Omega}}=0.\end{cases}

Multiplying (
3.35
) by
\phi:=e^{n}-e^{n-1}
yields

(3.36)

\begin{split}~&\tfrac{1}{\tau_{n}}\|e^{n}-e^{n-1}\|^{2}+\bigl(\bar{\partial}_{\tau,\varepsilon}^{\alpha}e^{n},e^{n}-e^{n-1}\bigr)+\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\|\nabla e^{n}\|^{2}-\|\nabla e^{n-1}\|^{2}\bigl)\\[5.0pt]
\leq\;&\tfrac{|\mu|}{2}\bigl(\|e^{n}\|^{2}-\|e^{n-1}\|^{2}\bigr)+\tfrac{1}{2}{\tau_{n}\|R^{n}\|^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{2\tau_{n}}\|e^{n}-e^{n-1}\|^{2}.\end{split}

As seen in
[
31
, Theorem 2.1]
, it holds

(3.37)

\frac{\sigma(\alpha)}{2\Gamma(3-\alpha)}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\tau_{k}^{\alpha}}{\|e^{k}-e^{k-1}\|^{2}}\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\bigl(\bar{\partial}_{\tau,\varepsilon}^{\alpha}e^{k},e^{k}-e^{k-1}\bigr).\vskip 2.84544pt

With (
3.37
), summing up both sides of (
3.36
) from
k=1
to
n
gives

(3.38)

\begin{split}~&\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\tfrac{1}{\tau_{k}}{\|e^{k}-e^{k-1}\|^{2}}+\tfrac{\sigma(\alpha)}{2\Gamma(3-\alpha)}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\tfrac{1}{\tau_{k}^{\alpha}}{\|e^{k}-e^{k-1}\|^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\big({\|e^{n}\|_{1}^{2}-\|e^{0}\|_{1}^{2}}\big)\\[4.0pt]
\leq\;&\tfrac{|\mu|}{2}\big({\|e^{n}\|^{2}-\|e^{0}\|^{2}}\big)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\tfrac{1}{2}{\tau_{k}\|R^{k}\|^{2}}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\tfrac{1}{2\tau_{k}}{\|e^{k}-e^{k-1}\|^{2}}.\end{split}

Since
e^{0}=0
, (
3.38
) reduces to

(3.39)

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2\tau_{k}}{\|e^{k}-e^{k-1}\|^{2}}+\frac{\sigma(\alpha)}{\Gamma(3-\alpha)}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\tau_{k}^{\alpha}}{\|e^{k}-e^{k-1}\|^{2}}+\|e^{n}\|_{1}^{2}\leq|\mu|\|e^{n}\|^{2}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\tau_{k}\|R^{k}\|^{2}.

Following the same argument as that of Theorem
3.1
, we further derive from (
3.35
) that

(3.40)

\|e^{n}\|^{2}\leq e^{\frac{\rho\rho_{1}t_{n}}{\rho_{1}-\rho}}(\tfrac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}-\rho}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\tau_{k}\|R^{k}\|)^{2}\leq t_{n}e^{\tfrac{\rho\rho_{1}t_{n}}{\rho_{1}-\rho}}(\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}-\rho})^{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\tau_{k}\|R^{k}\|^{2}.\vskip 4.26773pt

Substituting (
3.40
) into (
3.39
) yields

(3.41)

\begin{split}~&\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\|e^{k}-e^{k-1}\|^{2}}{2\tau_{k}}+\frac{\sigma(\alpha)}{\Gamma(3-\alpha)}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\|e^{k}-e^{k-1}\|^{2}}{\tau_{k}^{\alpha}}+\|e^{n}\|_{1}^{2}\\[4.0pt]
\leq\;&(|\mu|t_{n}e^{\tfrac{\rho\rho_{1}t_{n}}{\rho_{1}-\rho}}(\tfrac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}-\rho})^{2}+1)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\tau_{k}\|R^{k}\|^{2}.\end{split}

Combining (
3.41
) with Lemma
3.2
completes the proof.
∎

4.
H^{1}
-norm convergence analysis to a full discretization scheme

In this section, based on (
3.1
) and spectral collocation method in space,
a full discretization scheme for (
2.4
) is presented, and its convergence analysis is also provided.
Before presenting the full discretization scheme, the spectral collocation method given in
[
20
,
22
]
is introduced, where we take the two-dimensional case as an illustrative example.

(1)

(
Legendre-Gauss-Lobatto nodes and weights
) Consider the rectangular domain

\Omega:=(a,b)\times(c,d).

The collocation points along the
x-
axis are given by the affine transformation

(4.1)

x_{i}=\frac{b-a}{2}\xi_{i}+\frac{a+b}{2},\qquad 0\leq i\leq N,

where
\{\xi_{i}\}^{N}_{i=0}
represents the Legendre-Gauss-Lobatto (LGL) nodes along the
x-
axis. The weights are

(4.2)

\omega_{i}=\frac{2}{N(N+1)}\frac{1}{[L_{N}(\xi_{i})]^{2}},\qquad 0\leq i\leq N,

where
L_{N}
is the Legendre polynomial of degree
N
. Similarly, the collocation points and weights along the
y-
axis can be obtained following (
4.1
)-(
4.2
), i.e.,

y_{j}=\frac{d-c}{2}\xi_{j}+\frac{c+d}{2}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\omega_{j}=\frac{2}{N(N+1)}\frac{1}{[L_{N}(\xi_{j})]^{2}},

where
\{\xi_{j}\}^{N}_{j=0}
represents the Legendre-Gauss-Lobatto (LGL) nodes along the
y-
axis.

(2)

(
Differentiation matrix
) Let
D
be the LGL differentiation matrix on
[-1,1]
, whose entries are given by

(D)_{ij}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{L_{N}(\xi_{i})}{L_{N}(\xi_{j})}\frac{1}{\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}},&i\neq j,\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle-\frac{N(N+1)}{4},&i=j=0,\\[11.38109pt]
\displaystyle\frac{N(N+1)}{4},&i=j=N,\\[11.38109pt]
0,&i=j,\ 1\leq i\leq N-1.\end{cases}\vskip 1.42271pt

The corresponding second-order differentiation matrices on
\Omega
along
x-
and
y-
axes are

D_{x}^{(2)}=\left(\frac{2}{b-a}\right)^{2}D^{2}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad D_{y}^{(2)}=\left(\frac{2}{d-c}\right)^{2}D^{2}.

As a result, the Laplacian operator can be written as

\Delta_{N}:=I\otimes D_{x}^{(2)}+D_{y}^{(2)}\otimes I,

where
\otimes
represents the tensor-product and
I
represents the identity matrix.

(3)

(
Discrete
inner product
) Let
\mathbb{Q}_{N}(\Omega)
be the tensor-product polynomial space of degree at
most
N
in each variable. We denote

\mathbb{Q}_{N}^{0}(\Omega):=\mathbb{Q}_{N}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. For
\forall u,v\in\mathbb{Q}_{N}(\Omega)
,
the discrete inner product is

(4.3)

(u,v)_{N}:=\frac{(b-a)(d-c)}{4}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N}u(x_{i},y_{j})v(x_{i},y_{j})\omega_{i}\omega_{j}.

The reader is referred to
[
20
]
for more details.

Next, based on (
3.1
) and the spectral collocation method described above, a full discretization scheme for (
2.4
) is obtained as follows:

(4.4)

(\nabla_{\tau}v_{N}^{n},\phi_{N})_{N}+(\bar{\partial}_{\tau,\varepsilon}^{\alpha}v_{N}^{n},\phi_{N})_{N}+(\nabla v_{N}^{n-\frac{1}{2}},\nabla\phi_{N})_{N}=(\mu v_{N}^{n-\frac{1}{2}},\phi_{N})_{N}+(\bar{f}^{\,n-\frac{1}{2}},\phi_{N})_{N},\vskip 2.84544pt

where
\forall\phi_{N}\in\mathbb{Q}_{N}^{0}(\Omega)
. Before presenting
H^{1}
-norm convergence analysis for (
4.4
), the following preliminaries are provided.

•

(
Orthogonal projection operator
) Let
\pi_{N}^{1,0}:H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{Q}_{N}^{0}(\Omega)
be the

6.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we have presented a second-order
H^{1}
-norm error analysis for the time-fractional advection-dispersion equations (TFADE). An efficient integrating-factor transformation is introduced, and error estimates for the discrete coefficients and truncation errors of the fast averaged L1 method on a graded temporal mesh are established. Numerical examples confirm the theoretical results in Theorems
3.2
and
4.1
. Future work will extend this framework to more complex problems, including variable-order and nonlinear fractional equations.
