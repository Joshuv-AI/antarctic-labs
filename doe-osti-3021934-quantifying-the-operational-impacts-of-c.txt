Project Overview
The "Quantifying the Operational Impacts of Cyber Events on Enterprise Network
Environments" Feasibility Study addressed the question: Can an existing (novel) class of
network characterization and change detection algorithms previously developed at LLNL for
internal enterprise datasets, be effectively applied to assess and measure the analytic value of
features derived from datasets composed of passively collected RF and wireless network data?

This project addresses the feasibility question by presenting a robust and interpretable
clustering approach for characterizing wireless device behavior using a novel clustering
algorithm. The method demonstrates high efficacy in recovering known behavioral clusters and
provides clear interpretability through a small set of well-chosen features. Specifically, five
features were determined to most effectively capture the unique behaviors of wireless access
points, enabling accurate prediction of device type and function. The analysis highlights the
importance of sufficient data density and appropriate collection window length for effectively
determining cluster boundaries based on the observed behavior of wireless devices. When
applied to cybersecurity-focused datasets, this approach supports operational teams by
uncovering meaningful behavioral cohorts, facilitating the development of incident response
playbooks, and enabling continuous profiling, drift detection, and real-time anomaly alerts.

Mission Impact
The results of the "Quantifying the Operational Impacts of Cyber Events on Enterprise Network
Environments" Feasibility Study have had several noteworthy mission impacts. The findings are
helping to shape the research agenda for follow-on proposals, potentially leading to a more
expansive Exploratory Research (ER) effort in FY26. Insights and analyses from the feasibility
study have already been incorporated into proposal materials shared with potential future
sponsors, directly informing ongoing conversations and strategic planning. Additionally, the
outcomes of the experimental trials have been featured in cybersecurity research and
development presentations by the Network Analytics Group in Z Division/Global Security. The
developed code libraries have been integrated into the Network Mapping Tool (NEMS), which
provides foundational network monitoring capabilities for LLNL researchers. Finally, the
RF/Wireless datasets curated during the study have been archived and made accessible to
other research teams, supporting a wide range of adjacent research and programmatic
initiatives.
