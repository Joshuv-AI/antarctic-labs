 Public Executive Summary

In this project, a team of researchers from Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute developed high-
performance, low-cost alkaline conductors which can improve efficiency and lower cost of energy
storage and conversion technology. The Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute team has developed a
highly ion conductive, chemically stable, and mechanically durable alkaline membrane materials.
Such membranes will serve as a critical component in electrochemical devices when energy is
generated from renewable sources (e.g., sun and wind) or hydrogen fuel. The use of alkaline
membrane in electrochemical reactions allows us to replace expensive platinum catalysts with less
expensive, earth abundant metals as catalysts, offering huge economic advantages in renewable
energy technology.

 5

 Background and Objectives
This award allowed Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute (RPI) team to demonstrate a number of key
objectives in development and commercialization potential of anion exchange membranes (AEMs).
The focus of the project was on development of low cost, highly conductive, chemically stable,
mechanically robust hydrocarbon-based hydroxide ion conducting AEMs that could serve as a
standard membrane material in AEM water electrolyzers and/or AEM fuel cells with the following
works:
 • Develop next generation high-performance, low-cost AEMs
 • Synthesize a series of quaternary ammonium (QA)-functionalized cyclic olefin copolymers
 (COCs) having high anion conductivity, and high mechanical and chemical stabilities.
 • Prepare reinforced composite AEMs by impregnating QA-functionalized COCs into
 mechanically robust matrix.
 • Investigate the interfacial behavior of COC ionomer and catalysts using rotating disk
 electrode and microelectrode setups.

 Task 4.0: Ionomer-hydrogen oxidation catalyst interface: Commercial and in-house
 synthesized Pt-based and Ni-based catalysts will be used for measuring HOR activities in RDE
 and microelectrode. Milestone 4.0: Identification of more than one HOR catalysts for AEM fuel
 cell tests in Task 9.
 Actual Performance: This milestone of HOR activities characterization was 100% completed on
 March 31, 2021. From LANL’s alkaline membrane fuel cell project, we have learned that the
 catalyst-ionomer interaction at the AEM fuel cell anode may impact the long-term stability. Thus,
 we investigated to look at the possible interaction factor that may impact the AEM fuel cell
 performance and durability. We performed the rotating disk electrode tests in 0.5 M
 benzyltrimethyl ammonium hydroxide (BTMAOH) at 1.0 V vs. RHE. The ORR current slightly
 decreased from 0.06 to 0.003 μA over the 100-h test. The cyclic voltammogram (CV) after the
 chronoamperometry was similar, but slightly more oxidative current at > 0.8 V was observed after
 100-h chronoamperometry (Figure 3b). 1H NMR spectra of the BTMAOH solution taken at the

 9

initial, 1 h, 24 h, 48 h, 72 h, and 100 h. The 1H NMR spectra of the BTMAOH obtained from the
electrolyte during the chronoamperometry retained all characteristic peaks ca. ammonium C-H
bond (A) at 2.8 ppm, benzylic C–H bond (B) at 4.2 ppm, and aromatic C–H bond (Ar) at 7.4 ppm.
The 1H NMR spectra also show that a small and broad new peak centered at 5.68 ppm gradually
evolved while the peak corresponding to aromatic C–H gradually decreased (Figure 3d). The peak
at 5.68 ppm was assigned to the phenolic proton. The relatively high chemical shift of the phenolic
proton peak compared to that in non-substituted phenol suggests that the oxidation occurred on the
aryl protons in the two ortho-positions relative to the trimethylammonium substituent group as
shown in Figure 3d inset. In the previous study, the same analysis was conducted with 0.1 M
BTMAOH after exposing the electrolyte to Pt electrode at 1.6 V vs. RHE for 5 h.32 The 1H and
13C NMR analyses detected no phenolic compound but a relatively more highly oxidized
hydroquinone compound. This result suggests that at the ORR potential (1.0 V vs. RHE), acidic
phenol instead of hydroquinone may be formed as a result of phenyl group oxidation. We have
conducted the same experiments at 0.8 and 0.6 V vs. RHE and calculated the percent aryl proton
oxidation as a function of time (Figure 3e). For the BTMAOH exposed at 1.0 V vs. RHE, the
percent of aryl proton oxidation reached to 20% after 100 h chronoamperometry. The aryl proton
oxidation rate decreases as the cell potential decreases. As a result, at 0.6 V vs. RHE, negligible
oxidation occurred during 100-h chronoamperometry. We believe this phenol formation can
impact negatively on AEMFC performance and durability.

Actual Performance: This milestone of ORR activities was 100% completed on June 30, 2023.
LANL team investigated the interaction between the tetramethylammonium hydroxide (TMAOH)
and Pt catalyst using neutron reflectometry. The rotating disk electrode experiments showed that

 12

the current density of Pt rapidly dropped at hydrogen oxidation potentials due to
tetramethylammonium hydroxide (TMAOH)-water co-adsorption. Subsequent neutron
reflectometry in 0.1 M TMAOD/D2O reveals that the thickness of the co-adsorbed layer increased
to 18 Å after 10.5 hour-exposure at 0.1 V vs. RHE (Reverse Hydrogen Electrode). The scattering
length density analysis revealed that the TMAOD to water ratio in the co-adsorbed layer was 4.5,
which was significantly higher than the reported highest TMAOH concentration in aqueous
solution. In this quarter, we will keep continuing this adsorption behavior with bulkier cation, ca.
tetraethylammonium, to investigate the cationic structural impact on adsorption behavior.
Figures 6a and 6b show the reflectivity curves of the Pt electrode in TEAOD/D2O. Like the
TMAOD/D2O case, at 0.1 V, the reflectivity changes progressively from its highest value at 1.5-
hours, followed by the 4.5, 7.5, and 10.5-hour curves. The corresponding SLD profiles are given
in Figure 6c. However, the molecular ratio of TEAOD to D2O was calculated to 5.3 from the
average SLD of 0.62 ± 0.15 10-6 Å-2, which is similar to TMAOD. The mass of the co-ascorbates
(layer thickness × n) was plotted as a function of time at either 0.1 or 1.4 V vs. RHE (Figure 6d)
and compares TMA+, d-TMA+ and TEA+ cations. Note that the mass of the TEA+ in the co-
adsorbed layer was ~ 2-fold less than that of the TMA+. This result is consistent with previous
results that TEA+ has much lower adsorption energy on Pt surface due to the steric hindrance from
the bulky cation structure.

 Project Results and Discussion

 In this project, we have developed high-performance alkaline-stable anion exchange
membranes (AEMs) that could meet the requirements mentioned in Table 1 with (i) high ion
conductivity / low area specific resistance (ASR), (ii) robust chemical stability at high pH and
temperature (>80 °C), (iii) good mechanical properties (tensile strength and elongation), (iv)
conveniently scalable synthesis, and (v) low manufacturing cost. Although numerous AEMs have
been reported in literature, there is no AEM that satisfy all these criteria simultaneously until now.
Once promising polymer candidates are developed, their performance will be evaluated in
collaboration with team members at Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL).

1. Synthesis of functionalized COC
 Figure S1 shows the synthesis route of functionalized COCs that are used as AEM in this
project. The monomer PhNB was conveniently synthesized by the Diels-Alder reaction of
dicyclopentadiene and styrene. Figure S2 (a) shows the 1H NMR spectrum of PhNB. The NMR
data confirmed that PhNB is composed of 80 % endo-phenyl norbornene and 20 % exo-phenyl
norbornene isomers. The chemical shifts at 6.8-7.2 ppm (peak a) were assigned to the 5 protons of
the aromatic ring while peaks b-g confirm the attachment of a phenyl ring to the norbornene unit.
COC-Ph was prepared by copolymerization of PhNB and ethylene under inert conditions. The
ethylene unit provides flexibility to the resulting copolymer, while the PhNB unit gives rigidity.
PhNB incorporation ratio in the copolymer (i.e., n/(m + n) in COC-Ph) could be controlled from
16 to 45 mol% by changing the feed ratio of two monomers. Glass transition temperature (Tg) of
COC-Ph increases as PhNB content of the copolymer increases. Metallocene catalyst [(EBI)ZrCl2]
was used for the polymerization of PhNB and ethylene, and it was activated by modified
methylaluminoxane (MAO) co-catalyst. The catalytic activity was ~3.0 x 106 g/mol Zr·h. As
expected, the metallocene catalyst afforded COC-Ph with high molecular weights. For example,
weight average molecular weight (Mw) of COC-2Br with n/(m + n) = 34 mol% and Br content =
2.2 mmol/g was 733,000 g/mol.
 Once COC-Ph was prepared, its aromatic rings were functionalized by Friedel-Crafts alkylation

the integral ratio of the proton signal of -CH2Br at 3.2-3.3 ppm (peak c) and the signal of aromatic
protons at 6.8-7.2 ppm (peak a). For example, COC-2Br with 38 % degree of functionalization

4. Mechanical property and Thermal stability
 Good mechanical properties including tensile strength and elongation at break are important
parameters for durability of electrolyzers applications. In this study, reinforced COC membranes
were fabricated by incorporating ion-functionalized COC polymer to a thin, porous PE substrate
to enhance membrane toughness and decrease swelling in water. The lower thickness of reinforced
membrane can also contribute to decreasing membrane ohmic resistance. The stress-strain curves
of PE substrate, XL20-COC-2N-2.1 (non-reinforced, IEC = 2.1 mequiv./g), and R-XL-COC-2N-
PE membranes (reinforced, IEC = 2.2–2.8 mequiv./g) are depicted in Figure S5 (a). The tensile
strength and elongation at break of non-reinforced XL20-COC-2N-2.1 membrane were 23 MPa
and 104%, respectively. The PE substrate has weak mechanical properties (29 MPa of tensile
strength and 48% of elongation at break) because it consists of a porous inner structure with 83%
porosity. However, once those pores of PE substrate are completely filled with COC, the pore-
filled membranes result in significantly stronger and more tough materials; R-COC-2N-TMA-2.2-
PE reinforced membrane showed tensile strength of 49 MPa and elongation at break of 193%. All
other R-XL20-COC-2N-PE reinforced membranes with higher IECs also showed comparable
mechanical properties. Due to the synergy effect of dimensionally stable PE substrate and elastic
COC ionic polymer, the reinforced membranes showed good mechanical property (i.e., high
strength and large elongation at break) with good dimensional stability (i.e., low swelling in water)
while affording to reduce thickness. As summarized in Table S3, these reinforced COC AEMs
also achieved much better mechanical properties than the state-of-the-art AEMs based on
polynorbornenes. These results suggest that the impregnation of porous PE substrate with
functionalized COC is an effective method for improving mechanical properties and reducing
water swelling with a minimal impact on hydroxide conductivity.

5. Alkaline stability
 As AEM-based electrochemical energy conversion and storage technologies operate under high
pH environments, good alkaline stability of AEMs is important. We predicted that XL20-COC-
2N-2.2-PE sample exhibits good alkaline stability due to all-carbon backbone and the absence of
heteroatoms such as O or N that are prone to degradation under alkaline condition. The alkaline
stability test was performed by soaking XL20-COC-2N-2.2-PE sample in 1M NaOH solution at
95 oC for 1,000 hours and measuring the change in titration IEC. Figure S6 shows the results of
alkaline stability test; the measured IEC was virtually unchanged (2.16 vs. 2.14 mequiv./g after
1,000 hours). This demonstrates that the XL20-COC-2N-2.2-PE sample exhibits good alkaline
stability because COC polymer is made of all C-C backbone and the quaternary ammonium group
has good chemical resistance against nucleophilic attack or hydrolysis. The membrane sample
images of Figure S6 show that before alkaline stability testing, the membrane is translucent,
indicating good impregnation of the QA ionic polymer into the porous PE support. After the
alkaline stability test, the membrane remained transparent. Furthermore, the reinforced AEM did
not show any sign of delamination of QA polymer from the PE support. Unfortunately, alkaline
stability test by NMR analysis was not feasible, as the prepared crosslinked membrane is insoluble

6. Water electrolyzer performance and durability
 Several studies have reported high-performing durable AEMs for water electrolyzers. For
example, Li et al 40. investigated the application of AEMs functionalized with methylpyrrolidinium
cations and blended with poly(ether ketone-cardo). The electrodes comprised a NiFe layered
double hydroxide on nickel felt paper as the anode and a hydrothermally synthesized MoNi alloy
on nickel felt paper as the cathode. Their system demonstrated reasonable stability and
conductivity, achieving a current density of 0.5 A/cm2 at 2 V in 1 M KOH at 60 °C with an
estimated performance of 0.25 A/cm2 with an estimated performance of 0.25 A/cm2 at 1.8 V.
Similarly, Simari et al. developed composite AEMs based on quaternary ammonium polysulfone
and nanoscale ionic materials for alkaline water electrolysis. The membranes were mechanically
reinforced with silica-based trimethylammonium-functionalized nanoparticles to improve
dimensional stability. The anode consisted of a nickel foam substrate, while the cathode employed
a commercial Pt/C catalyst supported on carbon paper. The system demonstrated a current density
of 3.7 A/cm2 at 2.2 V and 80 °C with an estimated performance of 0.25 A/cm2 in 1 M KOH with
an estimated performance of 0.81 A/cm2 at 1.8 V 41. Soni et al.42 developed an innovative approach
for solid alkaline water electrolyzers using AEMs and ionomers based on wholly aromatic and
high-molecular-weight poly(fluorene-alt-tetrafluorophenylene) functionalized with
trimethylalkylammonium. The anode used IrO₂ catalysts deposited on pressed nickel foam, while
the cathode employed a Pt/C catalyst supported on carbon paper. Their system demonstrated a
current density of 1 A/cm2 at approximately 1.77 V (MEA-C6) and 1.79 V (MEA-C8) in 1 M
KOH at 80 °C with an estimated performance of 0.25 A/cm2 with an estimated performance of
1.11 A/cm2 at 1.8 V.
 Our R-XL20-COC-2N-2.2-PE membrane was evaluated in AEM water electrolyzer to
determine the membrane's performance and durability in water splitting reactions. As shown in
Figure S7 (a), the MEA with R-XL20-COC-2N-2.2-PE achieved a current density of 2.24 A/cm²
at 1.8 V, significantly outperforming commercial PiperION AEM (40 µm, Versogen) which
reached 1.50 A/cm² at the same voltage. In the Nyquist plots of Figure S7 (b), the MEA with R-
XL20-COC-2N-2.2-PE showed noticeably lower ohmic and charge transfer resistance than that
with PiperION AEM. The high ionic conductivity of R-XL20-COC-2N-2.2-PE membrane
contributed to its lower ohmic resistance. Furthermore, the reinforcement with a porous PE support
provides not only enhanced mechanical stability under water electrolyzer conditions but also plays
a key role in reducing resistance by lowering thickness of the membrane. These factors were
crucial in achieving high performance in AEM water electrolyzers.
 To assess stability of the reinforced COC AEM under device operating conditions, we
conducted a durability test of R-XL20-COC-2N-2.2-PE in AEM water electrolyzer MEAs by
applying a constant current test of 1 A/cm². The MEA exhibited a stable voltage profile over 100
hours with a voltage decay rate of 0.13 mV h-1. (Figure S7 (c)), demonstrating the robustness of
the membrane. A comparison of the polarization curves before and after the 100-hour durability
test revealed a notable current density loss immediately following the test, which was largely
recovered after a 24-hour rest period (Figure S7 (d)). This suggests that the performance loss is

results underscore not only excellent membrane properties of our reinforced COC AEM but also

electrolyzer systems.

 Significant accomplishments and Conclusions

In this study, quaternary ammonium-functionalized COCs that are comprised of an all carbon-
carbon backbone and a flexible alkyl sidechain are prepared as a low-cost, chemically stable AEM
material. To reduce swelling and enhance dimensional stability in water, reinforced composite
membranes are prepared by impregnating the functionalized COC into a mechanically robust
polyethylene matrix. XL20-COC-2N-2.2-PE, a reinforced COC membrane with 20% crosslinking
density and 2.2 mequiv./g IEC, gives high hydroxide conductivity of 127 mS/cm at 80 °C and low

 25

water swelling ratio of 8%. Its mechanical properties demonstrate features of a tough material with
tensile strength of 49 MPa and elongation at break of 205%. The reinforced COC membrane also
shows good alkaline stability when evaluated in 1M NaOH at 95 oC for 1000 h. The MEAs
incorporating the R-XL20-COC-2N-2.2-PE membrane exhibited promising performance in water
electrolyzers. In water electrolyzers, the MEA demonstrated exceptional performance due to its
low ohmic and charge transfer resistance. Overall, our results demonstrate that COC is a good
polymer backbone choice for AEM with advantages of convenient scalability, low-cost synthesis,
and excellent alkaline stability. Impregnation of functionalized COCs to a porous substrate yields
reinforced membranes that show greatly improved mechanical property and dimensional stability
under hydrated conditions. The pore-filled reinforced composite membranes result in low ohmic
resistance and good performance in AEM water electrolyzer performances with no sign of
degradation in a 100-hour durability test. This study demonstrates that the series of COC-based
AEMs and reinforce composite membrane offers valuable insight into development of AEMs for
water electrolyzer. Future work includes elimination or reduction of platinum-group-metal (PGM)
catalysts compared to their acidic counterpart proton exchange membrane-based fuel cells and
water electrolyzers.
