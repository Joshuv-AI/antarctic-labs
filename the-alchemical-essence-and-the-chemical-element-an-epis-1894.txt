Amid  the  rush  of  changing  appearances,  and  the

To  write  the  history  of  the  endeavours  that  men

these  phenomena  as  the  harmonious  outcome  of  a

the  history  of  natural  science.

The  chemists  form  one  of  the  bands.  The  path

Pliny  tells  us  that  some  Phoenician  merchants,
returning  by  sea  from  Egypt,  put  ashore  on  the
sandy  bank  of  a river,  and,  for  lack  of  better

came  in  its  place.  After  this  discovery  the  pro-

By  mercury  and  sulphur  the  alchemists  did  not
mean  the  two  kinds  of  matter  that  we  are  accustomed
to  designate  by  these  names.  The  terms  were  given
to  two  principles  supposed  to  be  present  in  metals ;
the  principle  of  malleability  and  lustre  was  called
mercury  or  quicksilver,  and  the  principle  of  change-
ability was  called  sulphur.  The  malleability  and
lustre  of  different  metals,  and  also  their  greater  or
less  readiness  to  change,  were  supposed  to  depend  on
the  quantities,  and  on  the  degrees  of  purity  and  of
fixation,  of  these  principles  present  in  the  metals.
The  mercury  and  sulphur  of  the  alchemists  were
intellectual  abstractions  clothed  in  material  garments,
which  fitted  very  loosely,  and  were  constantly  being
put  off  and  on.
