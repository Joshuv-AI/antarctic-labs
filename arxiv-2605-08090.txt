Residue Constraints in the Rank-Three Lifting Problem for Projective-Plane Incidence Matrices
Jaehwan Kim

Abstract
. We study the rank-three lifting problem for incidence matrices of finite projective planes through residue-level determinant constraints invisible to tropical valuations alone. In residue characteristic ≠ 3 \neq 3 , any rank- ≤ 3 \leq 3 lift of the incidence matrix of a projective plane of order q ≥ 3 q\geq 3 forces Ω ⁡ ( q 8 ) \Omega(q^{8}) distinct admissible 2 × 2 2\times 2 zero rectangles with nontrivial residue cross-ratio. We further prove that for q ≥ 6 q\geq 6 no monomial rank- ≤ 3 \leq 3 lift exists; in particular, any putative low-rank lift must already involve nontrivial first-order corrections on valuation- 0 0 entries. These results arise from a local analysis of 4 × 4 4\times 4 identity-pattern minors, where we derive the leading derangement equation together with its first-order companion and show that every vanished identity-pattern minor contains a cross-ratio-defective admissible rectangle. The unresolved part of the problem is therefore genuinely global: one must decide whether a rank- 3 3 residue model, together with a compatible first-order deformation, can satisfy the full overlapping system of local residue constraints.

Problem statement and motivation

We study Kapranov-rank asymptotics for incidence matrices of finite projective planes, in the sense of the problem highlighted by Guterman and Shitov
[
5
]
. The central difficulty is that Kapranov rank depends on cancellation among leading terms in lifts, whereas tropical rank records only the valuation-minimizing combinatorics
[

,

,

. The present paper does not resolve the full asymptotic problem. Instead, it isolates a residue-level obstruction package that any hypothetical rank-
\leq 3

2.
Background

2.1.
Finite projective planes and incidence matrices

Definition 2.1

(Projective plane of order
q
)
.

A finite projective plane of order
q
is an incidence structure
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{L},\in)
such that:
(i) any two distinct points lie on a unique line, (ii) any two distinct lines meet in a unique point,
(iii) there exist four points no three collinear, and (iv) each line contains
q+1
points (equivalently each point lies on
q+1
lines).
Consequently
|\mathcal{P}|=|\mathcal{L}|=q^{2}+q+1

[
2
,
6
]
.

Definition 2.2

(Combinatorial incidence matrix)
.

Fix enumerations of points
\mathcal{P}
and lines
\mathcal{L}
with
|\mathcal{P}|=|\mathcal{L}|=q^{2}+q+1
.
The incidence matrix
I(\Pi)\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{P}\times\mathcal{L}}
is defined by

I(\Pi)_{p,\ell}=1\iff p\in\ell.

Remark 2.3

(Tropical working matrix)
.

For determinant calculations we also use the complementary valuation pattern

M(\Pi):=\mathbf{1}-I(\Pi),

so that incident entries have tropical weight
0
and nonincident entries have tropical weight
1
.

Remark 2.4

(Convention used later)
.

The combinatorial object of interest is always the incidence matrix
I(\Pi)
. The auxiliary matrix
M(\Pi)=\mathbf{1}-I(\Pi)
is used only when tropical determinant bookkeeping is clearer in the complementary convention; all lift statements in Sections
3
–
5
are phrased back in terms of
I(\Pi)
.

2.2.
Tropical linear algebra: tropical rank and Kapranov rank

Definition 2.5

(Tropical semiring)
.

The tropical semiring in the min-plus convention is

\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{trop}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\},\qquad a\oplus b:=\min(a,b),\qquad a\odot b:=a+b.

Definition 2.6

(Tropical determinant, singularity, and tropical rank)
.

For a square matrix
M=(m_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{trop}}^{r\times r}
, its tropical determinant is

\operatorname{tropdet}(M):=\min_{\sigma\in S_{r}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i,\sigma(i)}.

The matrix
M
is
tropically singular
if the minimum is attained by at least two permutations; otherwise it is

tropically nonsingular
. For a rectangular matrix
A
, the tropical rank of
A
is the largest
r
for which
A
has a tropically nonsingular
r\times r
minor
[
3
,
7
]
.

Definition 2.7

(Kapranov rank via lifts)
.

Fix a valued field
(K,\mathrm{val})
over a ground field
F
. For
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{trop}}^{m\times n}
, the Kapranov rank over
F
is

\mathrm{Kapr}_{F}(A):=\min\bigl\{\mathrm{rank}(\widetilde{A}):\widetilde{A}\in K^{m\times n},\ \mathrm{val}(\widetilde{A}_{ij})=A_{ij}\ \text{entrywise}\bigr\}.

When the ground field is understood, we write
\mathrm{Kapr}(A)
.

Remark 2.8

(Equivalent geometric formulation)
.

By the determinantal-ideal description together with the Fundamental Theorem of tropical geometry, the condition
\mathrm{Kapr}(A)\leq r
is
equivalent to the existence of a rank-
\leq r
lift of
A
, and also to the existence of a classical linear space of dimension
r
whose
tropicalization contains the columns of
A

[
3
,
10
,
18
]
.

Proposition 2.9

(Initial-form criterion for tropical hypersurfaces)
.

Let

f=\sum_{u}c_{u}x^{u}\in K[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}],

and let
w\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{val}}^{n}
. If

m:=\min_{u}\bigl(\mathrm{val}(c_{u})+\langle w,u\rangle\bigr),

then the initial form
\operatorname{in}_{w}(f)
is the sum of those terms whose
w
-weight attains
m
, reduced to the residue field after
normalizing by valuation
m
. The tropical hypersurface
\operatorname{Trop}(V(f))
is exactly the set of
w
for which
\operatorname{in}_{w}(f)
is not a
monomial
[
10
]
.

Proposition 2.10

(Basic inequality and field sensitivity)
.

For every tropical matrix
A
, one has

\text{tropical rank}(A)\leq\mathrm{Kapr}(A).

Moreover, Kapranov rank can depend on the ground field
[
3
,
5
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
.

Remark 2.11

(Cancellation viewpoint)
.

Kapranov rank is controlled by whether the lowest-valuation determinant terms in a candidate lift can be made to cancel. Tropical data
records which permutations attain the minimum, but not whether the corresponding leading coefficients cancel in the residue field. This is
the first point where our cancellation-layer formalism enters.

2.3.
The asymptotic rank problem and known separations

Remark 2.12

(Guterman–Shitov Research Problem 5)
.

Fix a ground field
F
and let
(\Pi_{q_{k}})
be a sequence of projective planes of orders
q_{k}\to\infty
with incidence matrices

I_{q_{k}}\in\{0,1\}^{n_{k}\times n_{k}}
, where
n_{k}=q_{k}^{2}+q_{k}+1
. The problem is to determine the asymptotic growth of

\mathrm{Kapr}_{F}(I_{q_{k}})
as
k\to\infty

[
5
]
. In particular, one wants to know whether these ranks stay bounded,
grow polylogarithmically, or exhibit polynomial/linear growth in
q_{k}
or
n_{k}
.

Remark 2.13

(Weighted versus unweighted separation)
.

The known constructions showing a large gap between tropical rank and Kapranov rank rely on weighted or otherwise non-projective-plane patterns; they do not by themselves settle the unweighted
0
–
1
projective-plane incidence matrices considered here
[
9
,
14
,
15
,
17
,
5
]
.

Remark 2.14

(Field dependence as a structural warning)
.

Because Kapranov rank may depend on the ground field, any lower-bound mechanism for
I_{q}
must separate valuation-level data from
residue-level cancellation data. This is precisely the interface where a three-sign / cancellation-sensitive enrichment may add information
beyond ordinary tropicalization
[
10
,
7
]
.

2.4.
Cocircuit matrices and the representability gate

Definition 2.15

(Cocircuit matrix)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a matroid on ground set
E
, and let
\mathcal{C}^{*}(\mathcal{M})
denote its set of cocircuits.
The cocircuit matrix
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{M})
is the
\{0,1\}
-matrix with rows indexed by
E
, columns indexed by cocircuits,
and entries

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{M})_{e,C^{*}}=\begin{cases}0,&e\in C^{*},\\
1,&e\notin C^{*}.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.16

(Incidence matrix as cocircuit matrix)
.

Let
\Pi=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{L},\in)
be a projective plane, and let
\mathcal{M}(\Pi)
be the rank-
3
matroid on ground set

\mathcal{P}
whose rank-
2
flats are exactly the lines of
\Pi
. Then, up to column relabeling,

I(\Pi)=\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{M}(\Pi)).

Proof.

In a rank-
3
simple matroid, hyperplanes are exactly rank-
2
flats, hence here exactly the lines
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
.
Cocircuits are complements of hyperplanes, so they are the sets
\mathcal{P}\setminus\ell
. For a point
p
and a line
\ell
,
the cocircuit-matrix entry in the column indexed by
\mathcal{P}\setminus\ell
is
1
iff

p\notin\mathcal{P}\setminus\ell
, i.e. iff
p\in\ell
. This is precisely the defining incidence condition for
I(\Pi)
; see also the standard oriented-matroid background in
[
1
]
.
∎

Corollary 2.17

(Kapranov-rank-
3
gate via representability)
.

Let
\Pi
be a projective plane. Over an infinite field
k
,

\mathrm{Kapr}_{k}(I(\Pi))=3\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\mathcal{M}(\Pi)\ \text{is representable over }k.

In particular, if
\Pi
is not coordinatizable over
k
, then

\mathrm{Kapr}_{k}(I(\Pi))\geq 4.

Proof.

Combine the preceding lemma with the Develin–Santos–Sturmfels characterization of Kapranov rank for cocircuit matrices

[
3
]
.
∎

Remark 2.18

(Fano anchor case)
.

For the Fano plane, the associated rank-
3
matroid is representable if and only if
\operatorname{char}(k)=2
.
Hence over characteristic
0
one obtains the immediate lower bound

\mathrm{Kapr}_{k}(I(F_{7}))\geq 4

for every infinite field
k

[
12
]
.

Remark 2.19

(Asymptotic barrier)
.

The representability gate is a sharp first obstruction, but it does not yet address the asymptotic content of Research Problem 5:
it yields at best the fixed lower bound
4
once coordinatizability fails, whereas the open question asks how
\mathrm{Kapr}(I(\Pi_{q}))

grows with
q
.

3.
Valued-field setup and auxiliary residue tools

3.1.
Valued-field conventions, residues, and lift expansions

Definition 3.1

(Valued-field package and splitting)
.

Fix a non-archimedean valued field
(K,\mathrm{val})
with value group
\Gamma_{\mathrm{val}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, valuation ring

R_{K}:=\{x\in K:\mathrm{val}(x)\geq 0\},

maximal ideal

\mathfrak{m}_{K}:=\{x\in K:\mathrm{val}(x)>0\},

and residue field
k:=R_{K}/\mathfrak{m}_{K}
. When needed, we also fix a splitting

\phi:\Gamma_{\mathrm{val}}\to K^{*},\qquad w\mapsto t^{w},

so that one can factor out a chosen valuation level and then reduce modulo
\mathfrak{m}_{K}

[
10
]
.

Definition 3.2

(Leading residue)
.

Assume a splitting has been chosen. For
x\in K^{*}
with
\mathrm{val}(x)=\alpha
, define its leading residue by

\operatorname{lc}(x):=\overline{t^{-\alpha}x}\in k^{*}.

If
x=0
, we set
\operatorname{lc}(0):=0
.

Lemma 3.3

(Residue criterion for raising valuation)
.

Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\in K
and let

\alpha:=\min_{i}\mathrm{val}(x_{i}),\qquad I:=\{i:\mathrm{val}(x_{i})=\alpha\}.

Assume a splitting exists. Then

\mathrm{val}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\Bigr)>\alpha\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\sum_{i\in I}\operatorname{lc}(x_{i})=0\quad\text{in }k.

Proof.

Write
x_{i}=t^{\mathrm{val}(x_{i})}u_{i}
with
\mathrm{val}(u_{i})=0
whenever
x_{i}\neq 0
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}=t^{\alpha}\Bigl(\sum_{i\in I}u_{i}+\sum_{j\notin I}t^{\mathrm{val}(x_{j})-\alpha}u_{j}\Bigr).

Every term in the second sum has positive valuation inside the parentheses, hence lies in
\mathfrak{m}_{K}
. Therefore the residue of

t^{-\alpha}\sum_{i}x_{i}
is exactly
\sum_{i\in I}\overline{u_{i}}=\sum_{i\in I}\operatorname{lc}(x_{i})
, so the valuation rises above
\alpha

if and only if that residue vanishes.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(Leading-term cancellation criterion for minors)
.

Let
A=(a_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
be a tropical matrix, and let
F=(f_{ij})\in K^{k\times k}
be a lift with

\mathrm{val}(f_{ij})=a_{ij}\qquad\text{for all }i,j.

Assume a splitting has been fixed, so each nonzero entry admits an expansion

f_{ij}=c_{ij}t^{a_{ij}}+\text{(higher-valuation terms)}\qquad\text{with }c_{ij}=\operatorname{lc}(f_{ij})\in k^{*}.

For each permutation
\sigma\in S_{k}
, define its tropical weight and signed leading monomial coefficient by

w(\sigma):=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i,\sigma(i)},\qquad C(\sigma):=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{k}c_{i,\sigma(i)}\in k^{*}.

Let

m:=\min_{\sigma\in S_{k}}w(\sigma),\qquad S_{\min}:=\{\sigma\in S_{k}:w(\sigma)=m\}.

Then

\det(F)=t^{m}\Bigl(\sum_{\sigma\in S_{\min}}C(\sigma)\Bigr)+\text{(terms of valuation }>m\text{)}.

In particular,

\mathrm{val}(\det F)>m\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\sum_{\sigma\in S_{\min}}C(\sigma)=0\quad\text{in }k,

and if
|S_{\min}|=1
, then
\mathrm{val}(\det F)=m
and
\det(F)\neq 0
.

Proof.

Expand the determinant by the Leibniz formula,

\det(F)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i,\sigma(i)}.

Each summand has valuation
w(\sigma)
and leading term
C(\sigma)t^{w(\sigma)}
. Factoring out
t^{m}
, only the terms with

w(\sigma)=m
contribute to the coefficient of
t^{m}
, and every remaining term has strictly larger valuation. Applying

lemma

3.3
to the resulting sum proves the claimed criterion.
∎

Remark 3.5

(Kapranov bottleneck for incidence minors)
.

By the lift characterization of Kapranov rank
[
3
]
, the condition
\mathrm{Kapr}(A)\leq r
means that every

(r+1)\times(r+1)
minor of some lift must vanish.
Lemma

3.4
isolates the precise obstruction: for each
minor one must solve a residue-field cancellation equation among all valuation-minimal permutation monomials. In projective-plane
incidence matrices, the asymptotic difficulty of Research Problem 5 is therefore not visible at the valuation level alone; it sits in the
compatibility of these leading-term cancellation constraints across many overlapping minors
[
5
]
.

Lemma 3.6

(Two-term determinant cancellation criterion)
.

Let
A=(a_{ij})\in K^{r\times r}
and let
M=(m_{ij})
be its valuation matrix,
m_{ij}:=\mathrm{val}(a_{ij})
. For each permutation

\sigma\in S_{r}
, define

w(\sigma):=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i,\sigma(i)},\qquad w_{\min}:=\min_{\pi\in S_{r}}w(\pi).

Assume that the minimum is attained by exactly two permutations
\sigma,\tau
, and that a splitting exists so that

c_{ij}:=\operatorname{lc}(a_{ij})=\overline{t^{-m_{ij}}a_{ij}}\in k^{*}

is defined. Then

\mathrm{val}(\det A)>w_{\min}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{r}c_{i,\sigma(i)}+\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\prod_{i=1}^{r}c_{i,\tau(i)}=0\quad\text{in }k.

Proof.

Expand

\det A=\sum_{\pi\in S_{r}}\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\prod_{i=1}^{r}a_{i,\pi(i)}.

Each summand has valuation
w(\pi)
. By assumption, exactly two terms have valuation
w_{\min}
and all other terms have strictly larger
valuation. Applying
lemma

3.3
to this sum after factoring out
t^{w_{\min}}
gives the claim, because the leading residues
of the two minimal terms are precisely

\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i}c_{i,\sigma(i)}
and
\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\prod_{i}c_{i,\tau(i)}
.
∎

Corollary 3.7

(Initial form in the two-minimum determinant case)
.

Under the hypotheses of
lemma

3.6
, the determinant initial form at valuation level
w_{\min}
is

\operatorname{in}_{w_{\min}}(\det A)=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{r}c_{i,\sigma(i)}+\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\prod_{i=1}^{r}c_{i,\tau(i)}\quad\text{in }k.

Hence
\mathrm{val}(\det A)>w_{\min}
if and only if this binomial initial form vanishes.

Remark 3.8

(Connection to higher-order cancellation)
.

lemma

3.6
is the first exact interface between ordinary tropical data and our cancellation-sensitive layer. Tropicalization
records that two determinant terms tie at valuation level; the residue equation records whether those tied terms actually collapse into a
single
\Lambda
-type cancellation event rather than merely coexist.

Given a lift
F\in K^{m\times n}
, we write its expansion (entrywise) as

F=U+tV+O(t^{2}),

where
U:=F\bmod t
is the residue matrix and
V
is the first correction layer.

Definition 3.9

(Identity valuation pattern)
.

For
k\geq 3
, let
A^{(k)}\in\{0,1\}^{k\times k}
be the tropical matrix defined by

A^{(k)}_{ii}=1,\qquad A^{(k)}_{ij}=0\quad(i\neq j).

Thus diagonal entries have valuation
1
and off-diagonal entries have valuation
0
.

Definition 3.10

(Rank-
1
residue ansatz on the off-diagonal block)
.

Let
F=(f_{ij})
be a lift of
A^{(k)}
. For
i\neq j
, write

f_{ij}=u_{ij}+\text{(higher-valuation terms)},\qquad u_{ij}\in k^{*}.

We say that the off-diagonal residue data is
rank-
1
if there exist

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}\in k^{*}
and
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}\in k^{*}
such that

u_{ij}=\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}\qquad(i\neq j),

where
u_{ij}
denotes the valuation-
0
leading coefficient on the off-diagonal block.

Lemma 3.11

(Rank-
1
residues cannot cancel the leading term of an identity pattern)
.

Let
k\geq 3
and let
F
be a lift of
A^{(k)}
over a valued field with residue field
k_{0}
of characteristic not dividing
k-1
.
Assume the off-diagonal leading coefficients satisfy the rank-
1
ansatz

u_{ij}=\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}\qquad(i\neq j).

Then the leading coefficient of
\det(F)
at the tropical minimum cannot vanish. Equivalently,

\mathrm{val}(\det F)=0,

so this identity-pattern minor cannot be killed by any choice of higher-order terms.

Proof.

For the identity pattern, every fixed point contributes
1
to the tropical weight, so the valuation-minimal permutations are exactly the derangements
D_{k}\subset S_{k}
. Hence

\operatorname{LC}(\det F)=\sum_{\sigma\in D_{k}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^{k}u_{i,\sigma(i)}.

Under the rank-
1
ansatz,

\prod_{i=1}^{k}u_{i,\sigma(i)}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\alpha_{i}\beta_{\sigma(i)})=\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}\Bigr),

independently of
\sigma
. Therefore

\operatorname{LC}(\det F)=\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}\Bigr)\sum_{\sigma\in D_{k}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma).

Now let
B=J-I_{k}
, where
J
is the all-ones matrix. Expanding
\det(B)
by Leibniz shows

\det(B)=\sum_{\sigma\in D_{k}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma),

because the product contributes
1
exactly on derangements. Since
B
has eigenvalues
k-1
(once) and
-1
(multiplicity
k-1
),

\sum_{\sigma\in D_{k}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)=\det(B)=(k-1)(-1)^{k-1}.

By the characteristic assumption this scalar is nonzero in
k_{0}
, and the prefactor is nonzero because all
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}
are nonzero. Thus the leading coefficient cannot vanish, so
\det(F)\neq 0
and in particular
\mathrm{val}(\det F)=0
.
∎

Corollary 3.12

(
4\times 4
identity-pattern obstruction)
.

For
k=4
one has

\sum_{\sigma\in D_{4}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)=-3.

Hence over residue characteristic
\neq 3
(in particular over
\mathbb{C}
), rank-
1
residue factorization on the off-diagonal entries cannot produce the leading cancellation required to kill a
4\times 4
identity-pattern minor.

Remark 3.13

(Kapranov relevance of the rank-
1
obstruction)
.

If one can exhibit many
4\times 4
submatrices of an incidence matrix whose valuation pattern is
A^{(4)}
, then any hypothetical lift of Kapranov rank at most
3
must force every such minor to vanish. By
lemma

3.11
, this is impossible whenever the off-diagonal residue data on such a minor is rank-
1
. Thus each identity-pattern minor forces non-factorized residue behavior, providing a basic local obstruction to rank-
3
lifts.

3.2.
Three-minimizer patterns and overlap elimination

Lemma 3.14

(Three-minimum determinant initial form)
.

Let
B\in\{0,1\}^{4\times 4}
and let
\widetilde{B}
be a valued-field lift with
\mathrm{val}(\widetilde{B})=B
.
Assume that
\operatorname{tropdet}(B)=0
and that the minimum is attained by exactly three permutations

\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}\in S_{4}
.
For
j=1,2,3
, define

U_{\sigma_{j}}:=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma_{j})\prod_{i=1}^{4}\operatorname{lc}(\widetilde{B}_{i,\sigma_{j}(i)})\in k^{*}.

Then

\mathrm{val}(\det\widetilde{B})>0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad U_{\sigma_{1}}+U_{\sigma_{2}}+U_{\sigma_{3}}=0\quad\text{in }k.

Equivalently, the determinant initial form at valuation level
0
is the signed trimonial

\operatorname{in}_{0}(\det\widetilde{B})=U_{\sigma_{1}}+U_{\sigma_{2}}+U_{\sigma_{3}}.

Proof.

Expand the determinant as a signed sum over
S_{4}
. By assumption, exactly three summands have valuation
0
, and every other summand has
strictly larger valuation. Applying
lemma

3.3
to that expansion yields the stated equivalence, and the displayed

Definition 3.23

Proposition 3.57

Lemma 3.71

Lemma 3.82

(Connectivity and diameter of the nonincidence graph)
.

Let
\Pi_{q}
be a projective plane of order

. Then
G_{0}(I(\Pi_{q}))
is connected. More precisely:

(1)

any two distinct point-vertices are at distance
2
;

(2)

any two distinct line-vertices are at distance
2
;

(3)

any incident point-line pair is at distance
3
.

In particular,

\operatorname{diam}(G_{0}(I(\Pi_{q})))=3.

Proof.

Let
p\neq p^{\prime}
be distinct points. Since exactly
q+1
lines pass through each point and exactly one line passes through both,
the number of lines through
p
or
p^{\prime}
is

(q+1)+(q+1)-1=2q+1.

As the total number of lines is
q^{2}+q+1
, the number of lines through neither point is

(q^{2}+q+1)-(2q+1)=q(q-1)>0.

Choosing such a line
\ell
gives a path

in
G_{0}(I(\Pi_{q}))
, so every two distinct points are at distance
2
.
By point-line duality, the same holds for any two distinct lines.

Now let
p\in\ell
be incident. Choose any line
m
not through
p
; such lines exist because only
q+1
of the
q^{2}+q+1
lines
pass through
p
. The union
\ell\cup m
contains
(q+1)+(q+1)-1=2q+1

q^{2}+q+1
, so there exists a point

on neither
\ell
nor

. Then

is a path of length

in

. Because

is bipartite and

, hence it is exactly

. Therefore the graph is connected and has diameter

.

Lemma 3.83

(Holonomy-trivial

.

Assume

is connected. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

For every even cycle
C\subseteq G_{0}(B)
, one has
\operatorname{Hol}_{u}(C)=1
.

(2)

There exist functions
\alpha:R\to k^{*}
and
\beta:C\to k^{*}
such that

u_{r,c}=\alpha_{r}\beta_{c}

for every edge
(r,c)\in E(G_{0}(B))
.

Proof.

If
u_{r,c}=\alpha_{r}\beta_{c}
, then each cycle factor simplifies to

\frac{u_{p_{i},\ell_{i}}}{u_{p_{i+1},\ell_{i}}}=\frac{\alpha_{p_{i}}\beta_{\ell_{i}}}{\alpha_{p_{i+1}}\beta_{\ell_{i}}}=\frac{\alpha_{p_{i}}}{\alpha_{p_{i+1}}},

so the product telescopes around the cycle and gives
\operatorname{Hol}_{u}(C)=1
.

Conversely, choose a spanning tree
T
of
G_{0}(B)
and a root row-vertex
r_{\star}
. Set
\alpha_{r_{\star}}=1
. Traverse the tree as
follows: whenever
(r,c)\in T
and
\alpha_{r}
is known, define
\beta_{c}:=u_{r,c}/\alpha_{r}
; whenever
(r,c)\in T
and
\beta_{c}
is
known, define
\alpha_{r}:=u_{r,c}/\beta_{c}
. Because
T
is connected and acyclic, this assigns
\alpha
and
\beta
uniquely on all
vertices of
T
.

Now take any off-tree edge

. Adding

to

creates a unique cycle

. The condition

\operatorname{Hol}_{u}(C_{e})=1
is exactly the consistency statement that the tree-defined product along the path from

to

reconstructs the given label on

. Hence the same factorization holds on every edge of

.

Lemma 3.88

(Structure of
(0,2)
-type
4\times 4
zero patterns)
.

Let
B\in\{0,1\}^{4\times 4}
be of type
(0,2)
, and let
\sigma,\tau\in S_{4}
be its two weight-
0
permutations. Then:

(1)

the union of the two zero-matchings is a single even cycle of length
2k
with
k\in\{2,3,4\}
;

(2)

the permutation-sign ratio satisfies

\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)}{\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)}=(-1)^{k-1}.

Proof.

The symmetric difference of two perfect matchings in a bipartite graph is always a disjoint union of even cycles. If there were at least
two such cycles, one could flip the two matchings independently on each component and obtain at least four distinct perfect matchings in
the zero pattern, contradicting the assumption that
B
has exactly two weight-
0
permutations. Hence the symmetric difference is a
single even cycle. In a
4\times 4
bipartite graph, its length can only be
4
,
6
, or
8
, so it is of the form
2k
with

k\in\{2,3,4\}
.

On the support of that cycle, the permutation
\tau\sigma^{-1}
is a single
k
-cycle (with fixed points if
k<4
). A
k
-cycle has sign

(-1)^{k-1}
, so

\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)^{-1}=\operatorname{sgn}(\tau\sigma^{-1})=(-1)^{k-1}.

∎

Proposition 3.89

(Two-minimum
4\times 4
minors impose signed holonomy)
.

Let
B\in\{0,1\}^{4\times 4}
be of type
(0,2)
, and let
\widetilde{B}
be a valued-field lift with
\mathrm{val}(\widetilde{B})=B
.
Let
\sigma,\tau
be the two weight-
0
permutations, let

U_{\sigma}:=\prod_{i=1}^{4}\operatorname{lc}(\widetilde{B}_{i,\sigma(i)}),\qquad U_{\tau}:=\prod_{i=1}^{4}\operatorname{lc}(\widetilde{B}_{i,\tau(i)}),

and let
2k
be the length of the unique cycle from
lemma

3.88
. Then

\mathrm{val}(\det\widetilde{B})>0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\frac{U_{\sigma}}{U_{\tau}}=(-1)^{k}.

Equivalently, after ordering that cycle as

C:p_{1}-\ell_{1}-p_{2}-\ell_{2}-\cdots-p_{k}-\ell_{k}-p_{1}

so that one minimizing matching uses the edges
(p_{i},\ell_{i})
and the other uses
(p_{i+1},\ell_{i})
, one has

\operatorname{Hol}_{u}(C)=(-1)^{k}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\operatorname{sHol}_{u}(C)=1.

Proof.

By
lemma

3.6
,

\mathrm{val}(\det\widetilde{B})>0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)U_{\sigma}+\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)U_{\tau}=0.

Dividing by
\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)U_{\tau}
yields

\frac{U_{\sigma}}{U_{\tau}}=-\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)}{\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)}.

Now apply
lemma

3.88
to replace the sign ratio by
(-1)^{k-1}
and obtain

\frac{U_{\sigma}}{U_{\tau}}=(-1)^{k}.

With the displayed ordering of the cycle, the left-hand side is exactly
\operatorname{Hol}_{u}(C)
, so the signed-holonomy reformulation
follows directly from
definition

3.81
.
∎

Remark 3.90

(Characteristic-

If

+1=-1
in the residue field, so the signed-holonomy distinction collapses and every
(0,2)
-type

.

Definition 3.91

Lemma 3.92

(Transition scalar on a connected overlap)
.

Let
H\subseteq G_{0}(I(\Pi))
be a connected subgraph with at least one edge, and suppose that on
E(H)
one has two factorizations

u_{p,\ell}=\alpha_{p}\beta_{\ell}=\alpha^{\prime}_{p}\beta^{\prime}_{\ell}\qquad\text{for all }(p,\ell)\in E(H),

with all
\alpha,\beta,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\in k^{*}
. Then there exists a scalar
c\in k^{*}
such that

\alpha^{\prime}_{p}=c\alpha_{p}\quad(p\in P\cap V(H)),\qquad\beta^{\prime}_{\ell}=c^{-1}\beta_{\ell}\quad(\ell\in L\cap V(H)).

Proof.

Choose one edge
(p_{0},\ell_{0})\in E(H)
and define

c:=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}_{p_{0}}}{\alpha_{p_{0}}}=\frac{\beta_{\ell_{0}}}{\beta^{\prime}_{\ell_{0}}},

where the equality follows from
\alpha_{p_{0}}\beta_{\ell_{0}}=\alpha^{\prime}_{p_{0}}\beta^{\prime}_{\ell_{0}}
. If
(p_{0},\ell)
is any other edge of
H
, then

\alpha_{p_{0}}\beta_{\ell}=\alpha^{\prime}_{p_{0}}\beta^{\prime}_{\ell}=c\alpha_{p_{0}}\beta^{\prime}_{\ell},

so
\beta^{\prime}_{\ell}=c^{-1}\beta_{\ell}
. Similarly, from any edge
(p,\ell)
for which
\beta^{\prime}_{\ell}=c^{-1}\beta_{\ell}
is already known, one gets

\alpha_{p}\beta_{\ell}=\alpha^{\prime}_{p}\beta^{\prime}_{\ell}=\alpha^{\prime}_{p}c^{-1}\beta_{\ell},

so
\alpha^{\prime}_{p}=c\alpha_{p}
. Since
H
is connected, this propagates across all vertices of
H
.
∎

Definition 3.93

(Rectangle atlas and chart holonomy)
.

A
\Lambda
-inactive rectangle atlas
on a subgraph of
G_{0}(I(\Pi))
is a finite family of
\Lambda
-inactive degenerate charts

\mathcal{A}=\{R_{i}\}_{i\in I},

each equipped with one factorization
u=\alpha^{(i)}\beta^{(i)}
on
R_{i}
, such that every nonempty overlap
R_{i}\cap R_{j}
used by the atlas is connected. The
chart adjacency graph
has vertex set
I
and an edge
i\sim j
whenever
R_{i}\cap R_{j}\neq\varnothing
.

On each nonempty overlap,
lemma

3.92
gives a unique transition scalar
c_{ij}\in k^{*}
with

\alpha^{(j)}=c_{ij}\alpha^{(i)},\qquad\beta^{(j)}=c_{ij}^{-1}\beta^{(i)}

on
R_{i}\cap R_{j}
. For a cycle of charts

\gamma:i_{0}\to i_{1}\to\cdots\to i_{m}=i_{0}

in the adjacency graph, define its

by

\operatorname{Hol}_{\mathcal{A}}(\gamma):=\prod_{t=0}^{m-1}c_{i_{t}i_{t+1}}\in k^{*}.

If the local factorizations glue to one global gauge on the union of the charts, then necessarily
\operatorname{Hol}_{\mathcal{A}}(\gamma)=1
for every chart cycle
\gamma
.

Proposition 3.94

(Trivial atlas holonomy glues local gauges)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{R_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a
\Lambda
-inactive rectangle atlas whose chart adjacency graph is connected. Assume that every chart cycle
\gamma
satisfies

\operatorname{Hol}_{\mathcal{A}}(\gamma)=1.

Then there exist scalars
\alpha_{p},\beta_{\ell}\in k^{*}
such that

u_{p,\ell}=\alpha_{p}\beta_{\ell}\qquad\text{for every edge }(p,\ell)\text{ lying in the union of the charts.}

Proof.

Choose one root chart
R_{i_{0}}
and keep its factorization
\alpha^{(i_{0})},\beta^{(i_{0})}
. For any other chart
R_{j}
, pick a path

i_{0}\to i_{1}\to\cdots\to i_{t}=j

in the chart adjacency graph and define

\lambda_{j}:=\prod_{s=0}^{t-1}c_{i_{s}i_{s+1}}.

Because every chart cycle has holonomy
1
, this scalar is independent of the chosen path. Rescale the factorization on
R_{j}
by setting

\widehat{\alpha}^{(j)}:=\lambda_{j}^{-1}\alpha^{(j)},\qquad\widehat{\beta}^{(j)}:=\lambda_{j}\beta^{(j)}.

On an overlap
R_{i}\cap R_{j}
, the transition rule from
lemma

3.92
gives

\widehat{\alpha}^{(j)}=\widehat{\alpha}^{(i)},\qquad\widehat{\beta}^{(j)}=\widehat{\beta}^{(i)},

so the rescaled local gauges agree on every overlap. Hence they glue to one global pair
(\alpha_{p},\beta_{\ell})
on the union of the charts, and the displayed factorization follows.
∎

Lemma 3.96

(A concrete three-chart overlap cycle around a strict

.

Assume

Choose

∎

Lemma 3.99

(Transition scalar is observable under canonical gauges)

-inactive trimmed charts, canonically gauged by base columns

In the canonical gauge of
R_{i}
, the row factor at
p
is exactly

Remark 3.101

Definition 3.104

Lemma 3.107

Let

The inversion identities are immediate from

4.2.
Residue-rank reduction and the first-order deformation bottleneck

Lemma 4.8

(Reduction does not increase rank)
.

Let
F\in R_{K}^{m\times n}
, and let
\pi:R_{K}\to k=R_{K}/\mathfrak{m}_{K}
be the residue map.
Then

\mathrm{rank}_{k}(\pi(F))\leq\mathrm{rank}_{K}(F).

Proof.

If
\mathrm{rank}_{K}(F)\leq r
, then every
(r+1)\times(r+1)
minor determinant of
F
is zero.
Determinant is a polynomial in the matrix entries with integer coefficients, so reduction commutes with determinant evaluation:

\det\bigl(\pi(F)_{I,J}\bigr)=\pi\bigl(\det(F_{I,J})\bigr)=\pi(0)=0

for every choice of
(r+1)
rows
I
and
(r+1)
columns
J
.
Hence every
(r+1)\times(r+1)
minor of
\pi(F)
vanishes, proving
\mathrm{rank}_{k}(\pi(F))\leq r
.
∎

Corollary 4.9

(Low Kapranov rank gives a rank-

residue zero-pattern model)
.

Let
\Pi
be a projective plane with incidence matrix
I(\Pi)
.
Suppose
F
is a rank-
\leq 3
lift of
I(\Pi)
, so

\mathrm{val}(F_{p\ell})=\begin{cases}1,&p\in\ell,\\
0,&p\notin\ell.\end{cases}

Then the residue matrix

satisfies

\mathrm{rank}_{k}(U)\leq 3,

and

U_{p\ell}=0\iff p\in\ell,\qquad U_{p\ell}\neq 0\iff p\notin\ell.

Proof.

All entries of
F
lie in
R_{K}
because their valuations are
0
or
1
.
So the rank bound follows from
lemma

4.8
.
If
p\in\ell
, then
\mathrm{val}(F_{p\ell})=1
, hence
F_{p\ell}\in\mathfrak{m}_{K}
and

.
If
p\notin\ell

\mathrm{val}(F_{p\ell})=0
, so

is a unit in

and its residue is nonzero.
∎

Proposition 4.10

(First-order deformation directions for a rank-
3
residue chart)
.

Assume
U\in k^{m\times n}
has rank exactly
3
and factors as

U=A_{0}B_{0},\qquad A_{0}\in k^{m\times 3},\ B_{0}\in k^{3\times n}.

Fix a uniformizer
t
and consider first-order deformations

A(t)=A_{0}+tA_{1},\qquad B(t)=B_{0}+tB_{1}.

Then

F(t):=A(t)B(t)=U+tV+O(t^{2}),\qquad V=A_{1}B_{0}+A_{0}B_{1}.

In particular, the allowable first-order directions of rank-
3
deformations of
U
form the linear space

T_{U}:=\{A_{1}B_{0}+A_{0}B_{1}:\ A_{1}\in k^{m\times 3},\ B_{1}\in k^{3\times n}\}.

Proof.

Expand the product:

(A_{0}+tA_{1})(B_{0}+tB_{1})=A_{0}B_{0}+t(A_{1}B_{0}+A_{0}B_{1})+t^{2}A_{1}B_{1}.

Since
A_{0}B_{0}=U
, the claimed formula follows immediately.
The description of
T_{U}
is just the set of all possible first-order coefficients
V
arising from such factorizations.
∎

Remark 4.11

(Incidence valuation
1
is a first-order nonvanishing condition)
.

Under
corollary

4.9
, any uniformizer expansion of a rank-
\leq 3
lift has the form

For an incidence entry one needs valuation exactly
1
, so necessarily

U_{p\ell}=0,\qquad V_{p\ell}\neq 0\qquad\text{for every }p\in\ell.

Thus the hard part is not merely the existence of a rank-
3
residue matrix with the projective-plane zero pattern, but the existence of one tangent direction

that is nonzero on all incidence positions.

Corollary 4.12

(Rank bound for first-order coefficients)
.

Let
F(t)\in K^{m\times n}
have classical rank at most
3
, and write

F(t)=U+tV+O(t^{2})

entrywise in one uniformizer
t
.
Then

\mathrm{rank}_{k}(V)\leq 6.

Proof.

Choose a rank factorization

F(t)=A(t)B(t),\qquad A(t)\in K^{m\times 3},\ B(t)\in K^{3\times n}.

Expanding

A(t)=A_{0}+tA_{1}+O(t^{2}),\qquad B(t)=B_{0}+tB_{1}+O(t^{2}),

we get

Each summand has rank at most

, so
\mathrm{rank}_{k}(V)\leq 6
.
∎

Definition 4.13

(Monomial lift of the incidence matrix)
.

Let
\Pi
be a projective plane with incidence matrix

.
A rank-
\leq 3

Definition 4.14

(First-order expansion data on one identity block)
.

Let
M(t)=(m_{ij}(t))
be a
4\times 4
minor with identity valuation pattern.
We write

m_{ii}(t)=ta_{i}+O(t^{2})\qquad(a_{i}\in k^{*}),

and for
i\neq j

m_{ij}(t)=u_{ij}+tw_{ij}+O(t^{2})\qquad(u_{ij}\in k^{*},\;w_{ij}\in k).

We denote the diagonal first coefficients by

Thus

is the contribution of the two

Lemma 4.15

(Determinant expansion to first order on an identity block)
.

With the notation of Definition

, one has

\det M(t)=\Theta_{4}(U)+t\,\Psi_{4}(U,A,W)+O(t^{2}),

where

\Psi_{4}(U,A,W):=\sum_{i=1}^{4}a_{i}D_{i}(U)+\sum_{\sigma\in D_{4}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\Bigl(\prod_{r=1}^{4}u_{r,\sigma(r)}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{r=1}^{4}\frac{w_{r,\sigma(r)}}{u_{r,\sigma(r)}}\Bigr).

Proof.

Expand
\det M(t)
by the Leibniz formula.
The valuation-
0
terms are exactly the derangements, since every fixed point contributes one diagonal factor of valuation
1
.
This gives the leading coefficient
\Theta_{4}(U)
.

For the valuation-
1
coefficient there are two possibilities.
First, one may choose a permutation with exactly one fixed point
i
; then the remaining three indices are arranged by one of the two
3
-cycles, each of sign
+1
, producing the contribution
a_{i}D_{i}(U)
.
Second, one may choose a derangement and replace exactly one off-diagonal factor
u_{r,\sigma(r)}
by its first-order correction
w_{r,\sigma(r)}
.
Summing these contributions over all derangements gives the displayed formula for
\Psi_{4}(U,A,W)
.
All other Leibniz terms have valuation at least

Corollary 4.16

(Each vanished identity block forces two consecutive equations)
.

If

and the off-incidence first corrections

A rank-
\leq 3

∎

Remark 4.17

(Monomial specialization of the first-order equation)
.

If the off-diagonal entries of the chosen identity block carry no valuation-

Remark 4.18

(Location of the first-order obstruction)
.

The residue matrix
U
already satisfies many polynomial constraints: each identity-pattern block forces one equation
\Theta_{4}(U)=0
, and globally any rank-
\leq 3
lift must support
\Omega(q^{8})
cross-ratio-defective rectangles. The next obstruction layer is the search for a tangent direction in

that is nonzero on every incidence. The analysis above makes that layer algebraically explicit: on each identity block the same lift must satisfy both

Any forced vanishing

\Lambda

4.3.
Abundance of identity minors in projective-plane incidence matrices

Definition 4.19

(Ordered quadrangle)
.

Let
\Pi
be a projective plane. An ordered quadruple
(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3},P_{4})
of points is an

if no three of the four points are collinear.

Lemma 4.20

(Private-line identity blocks)
.

Let
\Pi
be a projective plane of order
q\geq 3
with combinatorial incidence matrix
I(\Pi)
.
Fix an ordered quadrangle
(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3},P_{4})
.
Then for each
i\in\{1,2,3,4\}
there are exactly
q-2
lines
L_{i}
such that

P_{i}\in L_{i},\qquad P_{j}\notin L_{i}\quad(j\neq i).

Choosing one such
L_{i}
for each
i
produces a
4\times 4
submatrix of
I(\Pi)
, with rows
(P_{1},\dots,P_{4})
and columns
(L_{1},\dots,L_{4})
, equal to
I_{4}
.
Equivalently, that block has valuation pattern
A^{(4)}
.

Proof.

Fix
i
. Exactly
q+1
lines pass through
P_{i}
.
For each
j\neq i
, there is a unique line through
P_{i}
and
P_{j}
, namely
\overline{P_{i}P_{j}}
.
Because
(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3},P_{4})
is a quadrangle, the three lines
\overline{P_{i}P_{j}}
for
j\neq i
are distinct.
Any line through
P_{i}
that also contains some other
P_{j}
must be one of those three forbidden lines.
Hence the number of lines through
P_{i}
that avoid the other three points is

(q+1)-3=q-2.

If we choose one such line
L_{i}
for each
i
, then
P_{i}\in L_{i}
by construction, while
P_{j}\notin L_{i}
for
j\neq i
.
So the induced
4\times 4
incidence submatrix is exactly

.
∎

Proposition 4.21

(Quantitative lower bound for identity-pattern minors)
.

Let
\Pi
be a projective plane of order
q\geq 3
, and set
v:=q^{2}+q+1
.
Let

denote the number of ordered
4\times 4
submatrices of
I(\Pi)
equal to

and obtained from

4.20
.
Then

In particular,

N_{\mathrm{id}}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\Pi)=\Omega(q^{12}).

After dividing by
(4!)^{2}
, the same construction still yields
\Omega(q^{12})
distinct unordered
4\times 4
identity-pattern minors.

Proof.

Choose
P_{1}
arbitrarily:
v
choices.
Choose
P_{2}\neq P_{1}
:
v-1
choices.
Now choose
P_{3}
off the unique line
\overline{P_{1}P_{2}}
.
Since each line has
q+1
points, this gives

v-(q+1)=q^{2}

choices.

At this stage

are noncollinear.
For
P_{4}
, exclude the three lines

\overline{P_{1}P_{2}},\qquad\overline{P_{1}P_{3}},\qquad\overline{P_{2}P_{3}}.

Each has
q+1

are noncollinear, the three lines meet pairwise exactly at

.
So their union has size

Hence the number of valid choices for

is at least

Any such choice makes
(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3},P_{4})
an ordered quadrangle.

Finally,
lemma

4.20
gives exactly
q-2
choices of
L_{i}
for each

, contributing a factor

.
Multiplying the choices yields

N_{\mathrm{id}}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\Pi)\geq v(v-1)\cdot q^{2}\cdot(q-1)^{2}\cdot(q-2)^{4},

which is (4.1).
Since

, the bound is
\Omega(q^{12})
.
The unordered estimate follows by dividing by at most
(4!)^{2}
orderings of the rows and columns.
∎

(Abundant identity blocks force multi-term residue cancellation)

Remark 4.23

(Scope and next bottleneck)
.

The private-line construction starts only at

; for

one has

, so no such blocks exist.
Also,

is a supply statement rather than an independence statement: many of the counted minors share rows, columns, and residue variables.
The first charging step is to show that each fixed admissible

\leq 3

The next bottleneck is therefore no longer to produce many witnesses, but to prove an upper bound on how many such cross-ratio defects a rank-

(Quadratic lower bound for first-order off-incidence support on a canonical line-choice block)

6.
Discussion and outlook

6.1.
Toward the Guterman–Shitov asymptotic problem

The results proved here sharpen the shape of the rank-
3
obstruction problem without claiming a full asymptotic resolution. By
lemmas

2.16
and
2.17
, non-representability already gives the uniform lower bound
\mathrm{Kapr}(I(\Pi))\geq 4
, but that representability gate is only the starting point of the Guterman–Shitov question. The main unconditional advances of the present paper are the following: vanished identity-pattern minors force local cross-ratio defects
corollaries

4.6
and
5.5
; counting those minors yields the global lower bound of
theorem

5.3
; and first-order analysis yields both the no-monomial-lift theorem
theorem

5.10
and the quadratic support bound of
theorem

5.12
. In particular, any hypothetical rank-
\leq 3
lift must already carry a large family of residue defects together with nontrivial first-order activity.

The remaining obstacle is therefore global rather than local. One still needs an independence principle strong enough to prevent a single rank-
3
residue realization and a single admissible first-order direction from absorbing all overlapping local constraints simultaneously. A natural next target is to globalize the first-order equations attached to identity-pattern minors. In the monomial specialization
w_{ij}=0
on off-incidence positions,
corollary

4.16
reduces the first-order condition to

\sum_{i=1}^{4}a_{i}D_{i}(U)=0.

A contradiction for this reduced system on a sufficiently independent family of identity-pattern minors would already eliminate the simplest surviving rank-
3
scenarios.

Conjecture 6.1

(First-order nonvanishing obstruction)
.

For sufficiently large
q
, no rank-
3
residue zero-pattern model
U
satisfying the identity-pattern minor constraints developed in Sections
3
–
4
admits a tangent direction
V\in T_{U}
with

V_{p\ell}\neq 0\qquad\text{for every incidence }p\in\ell.

Equivalently, any putative rank-
\leq 3
lift of
I(\Pi)
should force at least one incidence entry to acquire valuation at least
2
.

This conjecture is stated here, rather than in Section
5
, to emphasize that the core theorem package of the paper is complete at the local and counting level; what is missing is a globalization theorem, not an additional local computation.

Appendix A
Technical lemmas and normalization tools

A.1.
Rank bounds for first-order layers (details)

Lemma A.1

(First-order rank bound (detailed version))
.

If
F\in K^{m\times n}
has
\mathrm{rank}(F)\leq r
and
F=U+tV+O(t^{2})
, then
\mathrm{rank}(V)\leq 2r
.

Proof.

Choose a rank factorization

F=A(t)B(t),\qquad A(t)\in K^{m\times r},\quad B(t)\in K^{r\times n}.

Expand both factors to first order in the uniformizer
t
:

A(t)=A_{0}+tA_{1}+O(t^{2}),\qquad B(t)=B_{0}+tB_{1}+O(t^{2}).

Multiplying gives

F=A_{0}B_{0}+t(A_{1}B_{0}+A_{0}B_{1})+O(t^{2}).

Comparing the coefficient of
t
with the given expansion
F=U+tV+O(t^{2})
, we obtain

V=A_{1}B_{0}+A_{0}B_{1}.

Now
A_{1}B_{0}
has rank at most
r
, and
A_{0}B_{1}
has rank at most
r
. Therefore

\mathrm{rank}(V)\leq\mathrm{rank}(A_{1}B_{0})+\mathrm{rank}(A_{0}B_{1})\leq r+r=2r,

as claimed.
∎

A.2.
Counting identity-pattern minors in the incidence matrix (details)

Lemma A.2

(Detailed private-line count for identity-pattern minors)
.

Let
\Pi
be a projective plane of order
q\geq 3
with combinatorial incidence matrix
I(\Pi)
, and let
v:=q^{2}+q+1
.
The number of ordered
4\times 4
submatrices of
I(\Pi)
equal to
I_{4}
and obtained from ordered quadrangles with one private line through each vertex is at least

v(v-1)q^{2}(q-1)^{2}(q-2)^{4}.

Proof.

Choose an ordered quadrangle
(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3},P_{4})
first.

There are
v
choices for
P_{1}
and
v-1
choices for
P_{2}\neq P_{1}
.
The third point
P_{3}
must avoid the line
\overline{P_{1}P_{2}}
.
Because every line has
q+1
points, the number of available choices is

v-(q+1)=q^{2}.

Thus
(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})
is already noncollinear.

For
P_{4}
, exclude the three lines

\overline{P_{1}P_{2}},\qquad\overline{P_{1}P_{3}},\qquad\overline{P_{2}P_{3}}.

Each contains
q+1
points.
Since
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
are noncollinear, those lines are pairwise distinct and meet pairwise exactly at
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
.
Hence their union has size

3(q+1)-3=3q.

So the number of admissible choices for
P_{4}
is at least

v-3q=(q^{2}+q+1)-3q=(q-1)^{2}.

Any such
P_{4}
yields an ordered quadrangle.

Now fix such a quadrangle.
For each
i\in\{1,2,3,4\}
, exactly
q+1
lines pass through
P_{i}
.
Among them, precisely three also pass through one of the other vertices, namely

\overline{P_{i}P_{j}}\qquad(j\neq i).

These three lines are distinct because no three vertices are collinear.
Therefore exactly

(q+1)-3=q-2

lines through
P_{i}
avoid the other three vertices.
Choosing one such line
L_{i}
for each
i
produces a
4\times 4
incidence block with

P_{i}\in L_{i},\qquad P_{j}\notin L_{i}\quad(j\neq i),

hence the block is exactly
I_{4}
.

Multiplying the four point-selection factors and the four private-line factors gives

v(v-1)\cdot q^{2}\cdot(q-1)^{2}\cdot(q-2)^{4},

which is (A.1).
∎

A.3.
Multiplicity of one zero rectangle inside identity-pattern minors (details)

Lemma A.3

(Detailed multiplicity bound for a fixed
0
-rectangle)
.

Let
\Pi
be a projective plane of order
q\geq 3
, and let

R=(\{p,q\}\times\{\ell,m\})

be a
0
-rectangle in
I(\Pi)
, so
p,q\notin\ell\cup m
and
\ell\neq m
.
Then the number of ordered identity-pattern minors from the private-line construction of
lemma

4.20
that contain
R
as one of their admissible complement rectangles is at most

C(q+1)^{4}

for an absolute constant
C
.
One may take
C=16(4!)^{2}
.

Proof.

Fix one such minor
M
containing
R
.
Inside any ordered
4\times 4
identity-pattern block, the admissible
0
-rectangles are exactly the six complement rectangles obtained by choosing two point rows and the two line columns matched to the complementary two points.
Therefore, if
R
occurs inside
M
, then there exist two further points
r,s
in the block such that
\ell
and
m
are precisely the private lines matched to
r
and
s
.
In particular,

r\in\ell\setminus\{\ell\cap m\},\qquad s\in m\setminus\{\ell\cap m\}.

Each of the lines
\ell,m
contains exactly
q+1
points, so there are at most
(q+1)^{2}
choices for the ordered pair
(r,s)
.
(Indeed, one can sharpen this to
q^{2}
, but the coarser bound is sufficient.)

Once
(p,q,r,s)
are fixed, any compatible ordered identity minor must assign private lines to
p
and
q
as well.
For each of those two points there are at most
q+1
possible lines through the point, hence at most
(q+1)^{2}
choices for the ordered pair of private lines attached to
p
and
q
.
Some of these choices fail to produce an actual identity-pattern minor because they may meet one of the other three points, but counting them only enlarges the bound.

Finally, after the underlying four points and four lines are chosen, only constantly many orderings of the rows and columns realize the prescribed rectangle
R
as one of the six complement rectangles of the block.
A crude bound is obtained by freely ordering the four point rows and four line columns and then multiplying by an extra factor
16
for the possible identifications of the two rows and two columns appearing in
R
; this gives the explicit constant
16(4!)^{2}
.
Absorbing all such ordering choices into one constant
C
yields the claimed upper bound (A.2).
∎

Appendix B
Supplementary computational checks

B.1.
Recurring local configurations (supplementary)

This subsection records the three local configurations that recur most persistently in the later arguments.

Example B.1

(
4\times 4
identity pattern)
.

For the
4\times 4
identity valuation pattern, the valuation-minimal permutations are exactly the nine derangements of
\{1,2,3,4\}
. Thus a vanished identity-pattern minor forces a multi-term residue cancellation rather than a binomial relation. This is the local source of the derangement equation from Section
4
.

Example B.2

(Degenerate square-minimizer pattern)
.

A degenerate diamond may force four valuation-minimal determinant terms rather than three. In that regime the initial form factors as a sum of two binomial remainders, which is why the degenerate analysis naturally leads to transport laws and cancellation-depth considerations rather than to a single trimonial identity.

Example B.3

(Bridge-cycle holonomy)
.

For the skew rectangle
(A,B;L_{0},L_{1})
extracted from a strict
B_{*}
configuration, the bridge-cycle holonomy is exactly the residue cross-ratio

\rho_{AB}^{L_{0}L_{1}}=\frac{u_{A,L_{1}}}{u_{A,L_{0}}}\frac{u_{B,L_{0}}}{u_{B,L_{1}}}.

Equivalently, the associated skew determinant can be written as

\Delta_{AB}^{L_{0}L_{1}}=u_{A,L_{0}}u_{B,L_{1}}\bigl(1-\rho_{AB}^{L_{0}L_{1}}\bigr),

so the same local datum can be read either as a cross-ratio defect or as a holonomy defect.
