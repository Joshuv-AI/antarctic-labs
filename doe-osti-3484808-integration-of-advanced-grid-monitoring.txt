 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 This project will evaluate the impact of augmenting a utility situational awareness with additional
 supervisory sensors downstream of the substation to enhance the resiliency and reliability of the
 power delivery and management by improving the identification of a fault location.

 CRADA benefit to DOE, Participant, and US Taxpayer:

 • Assists laboratory in achieving programmatic scope
 • Uses the laboratory’s core competencies

 Summary of Research Results:

 This project aims to configure the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) Advanced
 Distribution Management System (ADMS) Test Bed so that it can be used to evaluate the impact
 of augmenting a utility's situational awareness with additional supervisory sensors downstream
 of the substation. It is envisioned that these sensors will enhance the resiliency and adaptability
 of power delivery with improved fault location information. Electrical Grid Monitoring (EGM)
 partnered with Israel Electric Corporation (IEC) and NLR for this effort as part of a Department
 of Energy (DOE) Advanced Research on Integrated Energy Systems (ARIES) user call. The
 intended outcome of this work is to enable expansion upon prior work that involved simulation-
 only evaluation of advanced sensor deployment for fault identification [1]. For this project, NLR
 will develop a controller hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) laboratory configuration to collaborate with
 EGM and assess the efficacy of its system fault location identification in a simulated IEC
 distribution feeder.

 This project encompasses the setup and configuration of the evaluation environment for the
 assessment of the EGM sensor and analytics platform with IEC’s feeder circuit data.

 Task 1.2 Configure the network model for the selected portion of the utility network in the
 Meta-Alert System

 • IEC to provide a portion of their utility distribution network model to EGM for Meta-
 Alert system integration. IEC may provide several complete feeders to simulate network
 events and have additional, connected feeders available for switching. The exact amount
 of network data will be determined collaboratively by the project team.
 • EGM to implement the provided distribution model from IEC into the latest distribution
 release of the Meta-Alert system, with all the analytic applications required to support the
 FLISR evaluation, such as accurate fault location, electrical quality analysis, and DER
 management used to acquire the real-time analytics during the evaluation period.

 Results

 IEC provided circuit data including feeder topology, connectivity, field equipment, and cable
 parameter information from their geographical information system (GIS) for three feeders in
 their service territory to be integrated into EGM’s Meta-Alert analytics platform. Once integrated
 into their platform, EGM containerizes the software, system configuration, and network details
 for deployment at NLR. The EGM system architecture is a containerized software solution that
 facilitates deployment in any environment and avoids any compatibility issues.

 The three feeders have representative topologies from urban or rural areas in IEC’s service
 territory. The feeders were all radial with varied lengths. For this project, the group decided to
 use a medium size rural feeder for the evaluation. The feeder has a peak demand of
 approximately 8.6 MW and a length of six miles. The substation is equipped with an arc
 suppression coil (Peterson Coil) grounding scheme. The feeder was integrated into EGM Meta-
 Alert analytics platform.

 Task 1.3 Develop utility network model for ADMS Test Bed integration

 • IEC to provide a portion of their utility network model to the Contractor in either
 Electrical Transient Analyzer Program (ETAP), CYME, Synergi, WindMil, or OpenDSS
 format. This model will be converted to the required format for ADMS Test Bed
 integration.
 • IEC to provide historical load profiles from selected network models to assist with the
 model validation
 • The Contractor to convert the provided utility network model from IEC using the
 Distribution Transformation Tool (DiTTo) for integration with the ADMS Test Bed
 • The Contractor to configure and integrate the converted model into the ADMS Test Bed

 Results

 The NLR team collaborated with IEC to acquire the selected feeder GIS model data and convert
 it to the format to be ingested by the RTDS [2] digital real-time simulator needed for the HIL
 evaluation. The NLR team utilize the open-source QGIS [3] software to visualize the feeder and
 extract the electrical network information necessary for the model conversion. The NLR team
 leveraged the Simple Synthetic Distribution Feeder Generation Tool (SHIFT) [4] to convert the
 GIS Data to an OpenDSS [5] intermediate model. The OpenDSS model is validated against the
 feeder's historical load data provided by IEC. After validation, the model is converted to CYME
 [6] format for easy import into the RTDS system. Once imported into RTDS, the NLR team
 performed several aggregation steps on the detailed model to reduce the number of nodes to run
 on a 10 cores RTDS simulator, preserving its attributes.

 4

 The high-level modeling approach and aggregation steps taken are as follows:

 1. Multiple overhead lines in series aggregated into a single overhead line with aggregated
 impedance. Distributed loads along these lines were also aggregated.
 2. Multiple underground cables in series aggregated into a single underground cable with
 aggregated impedance. Distributed loads along these lines were also aggregated.
 3. Loads were aggregated into areas around each sensor cluster.
 4. Laterals and other deviation points in the feeder were preserved to ensure the topology
 aligns with the original feeder.
 5. The substation was modeled as a 3-phase voltage source with variable series impedance
 (resistance and inductance) and a neutral point grounded via a variable impedance
 element. For direct grounded systems, the grounding impedance would be set to a
 minimal value (~1 milliohm), and for Peterson coil type grounding, an impedance value
 was set based on inputs from the utility engineers.
 6. Loads were modeled as constant power resistance and inductance (RL) loads, with a
 threshold of 0.8 per unit voltage below which the loads act as constant impedance loads.
 7. Once locations for EGM’s sensors were identified, three-phase current and voltage
 signals were monitored for each designated point in the model. These signals were then
 sent to EGM hardware sensors (model MSU-1) via RTDS analog output cards.

 A snapshot of the IEC feeder modeled in RSCAD, with sensor locations, is shown in Figure 2.

 Figure 2. Real-Time EMT model of IEC feeder as modeled in RSCAD

 EGM determined the location for the sensor placement to provide adequate coverage of the
 feeder and be able to triangulate the fault location within their Meta-Alert platform. The
 identified points were located and monitored for both three-phase voltage and current in the real-
 time model. Each signal from the model is routed to a corresponding RTDS analog output card
 channel. The EGM MSU-1 sensors are equipped with an interface card to connect current and
 voltage signals, and an external 24Vdc to provide power to each unit. A snapshot of the CHIL

 Task 1.4 Integration of data exchange between the Meta-Alert system with the ADMS Test
 Bed

 • EGM to develop a communications interface to support the fault detection data exchange
 and integration with the Meta-Alert system and the ADMS Test Bed with industrial
 protocols.
 • The Contractor to support the development of the communications interface between the
 Meta-Alert, and the ADMS Test Bed

 Results

 The NLR and EGM teams collaborated to successfully deploy the Meta-Alert analytics platform
 at NLR and integrate it with NLR’s ADMS Test Bed. The Meta-Alert platform is an Internet-of-
 Things (IoT) real-time monitoring and analytics software solution for electrical grid monitoring.
 The system collects data through its sensors to provide additional situation awareness and
 operational status. Meta-Alert is deployed as a containerized platform to ensure seamless
 integration and operation. The analytics platform hosts data security, analytics, data repository,
 communication, and visualization functionalities.

 6

 The sensors collect a wide variety of parameters, including voltage, current, and temperature,
 and they contain GPS antenna for post-processing time synchronization. The sensor data are
 transmitted wirelessly to a Communication Adapter Unit (CAU) that consolidates the
 information and then transmits it to the head end system via Ethernet or cellular communication.
 For our laboratory setup, we established a dedicated virtual local network for the data exchange
 between the CAU and the analytics platform. The sensors are capable of recording at 8kHz
 sampling rate during an event, allowing the system to capture transient event data for further
 analysis.

 For example, after a fault the sensors are triggered to capture a snapshot of the event at a high
 sampling rate and provide information from all the sensors to the Meta-Alert system for further
 analysis. The information is processed, and the fault details are displayed in the Meta-Alert user
 interface and the tabular alarm system. Meta-Alert information could be shared with other high-
 level controllers such as ADMS for their system operation and optimization through industrial
 communication protocols. However, integration with an external system using Distributed
 Network Protocol 3 (DNP3) was beyond the scope of this CRADA.

 Task 1.5 Deliver and deploy the Meta-Alert system and multi sensing units at the
 Contractor’s ESIF and establish a host environment at Contractor’s ESIF

 • EGM to deliver and deploy the Meta-Alert system and multi sensing units with the
 required analytical applications developed in subtask 1.2
 • EGM to provide installation and operation manuals for the Meta-Alert Management
 system and Multi Sensing Units
 • The Contractor to collaborate with EGM to establish a host environment for the Meta-
 Alert system at NLR’s ESIF

 Results

 EGM delivered twelve single-phase MSU-1 sensors and four CAUs communication gateways to
 NLR. The EGM team, with support from NLR’s ESIF Operations and Networking teams,
 deployed an isolated virtual environment in the ADMS Test Bed to host the Meta-Alert software
 platform as per their installation documentation. Communication between Meta-Alert and the
 CAU was established with a dedicated laboratory network connection with proper configuration
 and authorization. The MSUs automatically communicated with the CAUs wirelessly. The
 connection was confirmed when the twelve MSUs were detected and could transmit data to the
 Meta-Alert platform. A photo of the setup of MSUs and CAUs, along with the RTDS simulator,
 is shown in Figure 4 below.

 Task 1.6 Provide Operator and configuration training for Contractor’s researchers

 • EGM to provide a one-week hands-on or virtual training at NLR for Contractor’s
 researchers after the delivery of the Meta-Alert system
 • Contractor to collaborate with EGM on developing the training agenda

 Results

 Once the system setup and the hardware connection were completed, the EGM team provided an
 overview and training of their system. The EGM team walked through the procedure to update
 each sensor voltage and current gain, sensitivity constants, operational status, and other
 parameters in the Meta-Alert user interface. EGM provided instructions for the hardware
 mapping into their analytics platform and created dashboards for real-time status monitoring.
 This was validated by running basic simulations in the RTDS simulator and ensuring the steady-
 state voltages and currents seen in RTDS matched the values seen in the Meta-Alert platform.
 Figure 5 shows the Meta-Alert events dashboard for an L-G fault simulated in RTDS on one of
 the feeder laterals. Figure 6 shows the voltage and current trends on the Meta-Alert dashboard
 for one MSU location on the IEC feeder.

 The EGM team also provided the operating manuals of the Meta-Alert system in a digital format
 that served as a reference during this configuration step and subsequent simulations.

 Task 1.7 Integrate the Meta-Alert system at Contractor’s ESIF with simulated devices and
 hardware

 • Contractor to perform the integration of the Meta-Alert system with both simulated and
 hardware devices in the ADMS Test Bed through hardware-in-the loop techniques, with
 support from EGM as needed
 • EGM to assist on-site or virtually with technical or support services related to the
 integration of the Meta Alert System

 9

 Results

 The EGM team provided in-person support to set up the connection between the various sensors
 and the RTDS platform in the NLR facility. The feeder model in RTDS provided the analog
 signals proportional to the simulated voltages and currents as input to the MSUs. Both teams
 worked together to tune the parameters in the MSU to ensure the signal values seen in the RTDS
 model match, within tolerance, the values seen in the Meta-Alert.

 The EGM team made some modifications to the MSU sensors for the laboratory setup to directly
 integrate with the low-voltage outputs from the RTDS analog channels. Figure 7 shows the
 analog interface card from one MSU sensor that is used to interface with the output of the RTDS
 system. Voltages and currents from the RTDS simulation are sent to each MSU. The digital to
 analog (DAC) conversion on the RTDS analog I/O cards was set to work at a sampling rate of
 100 microseconds. With this setup, the RTDS simulation can simulate transients, such as load steps and
 short-circuit faults.

 Figure 7. MSU interface board to RTDS analog input

 Task 1.8 Set up data collection from the Meta-Alert system and its various analytic
 applications

 • EGM to configure the Meta-Alert system data collection and reporting process
 capabilities for the various analytical applications that will be used in the fault detection
 • EGM to provide training to Contractor’s personnel on the data collection and reporting
 capabilities of the Meta-Alert System
 • Contractor to develop a data collection process to acquire information during the
 evaluation period

 10

 Results

 Meta-Alert has its own integrated data storage capabilities. The team used Heidi SQL to extract
 and store event data locally from events collected by the EGM platform. The EGM team will use
 this data to understand how their algorithms perform with diverse types of transients in the
 feeder simulation.

 Additionally, the RTDS system is equipped to record the data generated during simulations. The
 model was configured to allow the extraction of high-sampling data to a flat file on a local
 system for each event. This data will also be provided to the EGM team for their own
 assessment.

 Task 1.9 Develop use cases for the evaluation of the fault detection performance with
 enhanced monitoring

 • Contractor to collaborate with EGM and IEC to develop use cases and a test plan for the
 various use cases that include the injection of random faults into the selected network
 without the use of smart devices or the EGM system.

 Results

 The EGM and NLR teams collaborated on the development of a use case to evaluate Meta-
 Alert’s fault detection performance. They decided to set up four clusters of MSUs, with each
 cluster consisting of three MSUs (one on each phase). Strategic placement of the MSUs in the
 IEC feeder was discussed with the EGM and IEC teams to achieve maximum feeder coverage.
 Figure 8 shows the location of each MSU in red. The use case was determined to simulate twelve
 low-impedance line-to-ground faults. High-fidelity analog fault current and voltage signals for
 these cases will be sent to MSUs. The simulated signal data will be recorded locally in a flat file
 format. These files will be shared with the EGM team for their analysis. These use case scenarios
 were discussed during our monthly meeting and incorporated into the final test plan.

 Figure 8. Feeder with fault locations and corresponding MSU sensor locations
