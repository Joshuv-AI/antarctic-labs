 SUMMARY
This report describes the pre-irradiation characterization and successful assembly
of a High Flux Isotope Reactor (HFIR) irradiation experiment designed to assess
radiation-induced lateral bowing of silicon carbide fiber–reinforced, silicon
carbide matrix composite (SiC/SiC) components under a radial fast neutron flux
gradient at representative light-water reactor (LWR) temperatures of
approximately 300 °C. The over-arching goal of this work is to validate
thermomechanical models of SiC/SiC composite fuel assembly distortion used to
predict the extent of lateral bowing and dose at which bowing reaches a maximum
value. Ultimately, these models are used to ensure that fuel assembly distortion
does not block coolant channels, interfere with control rod/blade movements,
and/or impact local reactivity. The experiment contains six cladding tube
specimens that are approximately 558.5 mm long with representative pressurized
water reactor (PWR) outer and inner diameters of 9.5 mm and 7.5 mm,
respectively. The specimens were thoroughly characterized prior to irradiation
using traditional dimensional inspection and surface profilometry so that these
measurements can later be compared with similar measurements that will be made
post-irradiation to determine radiation-induced deformations. Furthermore, fine
engraving markers were inscribed along the outer surfaces of the specimen and
mapped using analytic photography and a three-dimensional stage. This technique
allowed for accurate measurements of the marker spacings, which can be
compared with similar measurements that will be made post-irradiation to provide
local radiation-induced strain mapping. A light curtain optical micrometer was also
employed to measure the pre-irradiation bowing of each specimen. The experiment
was successfully assembled and inserted into position VXF-19 for HFIR cycle 516,
which began May 12, 2026. The experiment will be removed after cycle 519,
which is currently scheduled to end in December 2026.

 1. INTRODUCTION
Silicon carbide fiber–reinforced, silicon carbide (SiC/SiC) ceramic matrix composites are a promising
accident-tolerant fuel cladding concept for light-water reactors (LWRs). In addition to potential cladding
applications, SiC/SiC has also been considered for other LWR core structural components and boiling water
reactor channel boxes because of its high-temperature stability, oxidation resistance, and low neutron
absorption. Despite these advantages, several performance issues must be resolved before broad
deployment, including hermeticity retention, hydrothermal corrosion, joint and end cap performance, and
dimensional stability during irradiation [1-5].
One phenomenon of particular interest is radiation-induced lateral bowing caused by radial dose and
temperature gradients through SiC/SiC tubes or channel box components [6, 7]. Because swelling in SiC
depends strongly on both temperature and irradiation dose, differential swelling can develop radially across
a component, causing measurable bowing. Under radial neutron flux (or dose) gradients, swelling will
increase at a faster rate in regions of high flux, and this differential swelling will cause lateral bowing that
increases until the swelling approaches saturation, at which point the swelling in the lower-flux regions will
eventually equal the swelling in the higher-flux regions. If the magnitude of the transient swelling is too
high, then it could interfere with control rods/blades insertion, impact local reactivity, or restrict coolant
channels. A related concern is bowing in the presence of temperature variations. The strong temperature
dependence of SiC swelling results in greater swelling in the low-temperature regions than in the high-
temperature regions. If the temperature variations continue indefinitely—as in a circumferentially varying
heat flux caused by a lack of concentricity between the fuel pellets and cladding—then the differential
swelling will remain even after swelling saturation, and the bowing will be permanent.
A previous High Flux Isotope Reactor (HFIR) bowing experiment demonstrated that the bowing of SiC/SiC
cladding tubes and channel box specimens can be measured experimentally after irradiation in the
significant fast neutron flux gradients characteristic of the reflector positions of HFIR. This experiment
largely confirmed that simple analytical expressions for macroscopic bowing can reproduce the measured
bowing of both cladding tubes and a miniature channel box [7]. Those tests, however, were conducted in
direct contact with HFIR coolant at approximately 50 °C to 60 °C, which presents different swelling
behavior in SiC than is expected at representative LWR cladding temperatures near 300 °C. The present
experiment extends that work to a dry irradiation configuration intended to achieve cladding temperatures
near 300 °C while preserving a controlled radial neutron flux gradient.
This report documents a summary of supporting analyses, pre-irradiation characterization, and assembly of
an experiment containing six 558.5 mm long cladding tubes with representative pressurized water reactor
(PWR) outer and inner diameters of 9.5 mm and 7.5 mm, respectively. The tube length was maximized
within the constraints of the HFIR vertical experiment facility (VXF) irradiation facility length, but is
shortened compared with a prototypical PWR length of ~4 m. This experiment was inserted into HFIR for
cycle 516. The goal of the experiment is to measure the maximum bowing that occurs under a controlled
neutron flux gradient and the dose at which the maximum bowing occurs, which is predicted to be
approximately 0.1 displacements per atom (dpa) [7]. As a result, the experiment will be removed after cycle
519, when the cladding tubes are predicted to reach maximum doses from 0.09 to 0.12 dpa. The spread in
maximum doses among the cladding tubes provides a basis for evaluating the dose at which maximum
transient bowing occurs. A detailed description of the experiment design and analyses are documented in
previous works and are summarized here only as needed [8]. Ultimately, the results from this experiment
will help validate the underlying models used to predict bowing that will inform safety limits to ensure that
SiC/SiC bowing does not threaten the safe operating conditions of LWRs.

 2. EXPERIMENT OVERVIEW
The experiment described here is an irradiation vehicle intended to measure the bowing of SiC/SiC cladding
tubes irradiated at representative cladding temperatures of approximately 300 °C while exposed to the large
radial neutron flux gradients available in HFIR reflector experiment positions [8]. In contrast to the earlier
low-temperature, in-coolant bowing irradiations, the present experiment uses a dry, sealed capsule
configuration so that the specimens can be irradiated at temperatures representative of PWR operation. The
irradiation vehicle, shown in Figure 1, is designed to contain six SiC/SiC cladding tubes approximately
558.5 mm in length arranged in three radial pairs (denoted front, center, and back) within the experiment
holder. Each tube has a representative PWR outer diameter of 9.5 mm and an inner diameter of 7.5 mm.
The six tubes are positioned so that each pair experiences a different neutron flux level and a different radial
dose gradient relative to the HFIR core.

Figure 1. Overview of SiC/SiC bowing experiment capsule [8]: (a) experiment in the VXF sleeve, (b)
 axial cross section, (c) upper pinned region, and (d) radial cross section.

The cladding tubes are suspended within the aluminum holder by titanium pins oriented perpendicular to
the plane in which the maximum neutron flux variations are expected to permit essentially unconstrained
lateral deformation during irradiation. The holder geometry includes provisions for three axial stacks of
passive SiC thermometry so that post-irradiation examination (PIE) can be used to verify the irradiation
temperatures achieved. Titanium centering fixtures at each end of the holder locate the specimens, and a

 2

titanium tie rod holds the internal assembly together. This internal assembly is contained in an aluminum
experiment housing sealed in a fill gas specified to achieve the desired irradiation temperatures. A wave
spring is placed at the top of the internal assembly to provide compliance for thermal expansion of the
system while keeping the internal assembly seated in the housing bottom cap and preventing damage during
handling. The capsule is loaded into a basket suitable for irradiation in a HFIR large VXF facility. The
basket locates the capsule radially and axially in HFIR while also controlling the axial rotation of the
capsule to ensure that the cladding pairs and pins are oriented correctly relative to the core. The experiment
is intended for irradiation in a HFIR large VXF position, because it provides the large radial neutron flux
gradients required to drive differential swelling across the tube diameter, while also providing sufficient
experiment volume to irradiate multiple specimens of representative cladding diameters.

 3. SUMMARY OF SUPPORTING ANALYSES
The neutronic, thermal, and structural analyses supporting this high-temperature SiC cladding bowing
experiment are documented in Russell et al [8]. These analyses established the feasibility of irradiating
long-length SiC/SiC cladding tubes in a dry HFIR irradiation vehicle at temperatures representative of LWR
operation while maintaining the radial neutron flux gradients required to drive measurable differential
swelling and lateral bowing. Only a summary of the prior supporting analyses is provided here. Unless
otherwise noted, the assumptions and overall conclusions from the design milestone remain applicable to
the present experiment.

 3.1 Neutronic Analysis
The neutronics analysis documented previously was performed using Monte Carlo N-Particle (MCNP)
models of the experiment in the VXF-19 position of HFIR to determine heat generation rates (HGRs) and
displacement damage values to support subsequent thermal and bowing evaluations [8]. A reference HFIR
MCNP model was modified to represent the irradiation capsule, including the six cladding tubes, Al
housing, Al holder, Ti caps, Ti tie rod, internal gas gaps, Al VXF basket sleeve, and surrounding water
regions. A High Flux Isotope Reactor Controller (HFIRCON) simulation with 15 steps was used for single-
cycle fuel and experiment depletion and for calculation of time- and space-dependent neutron, gamma, and
total HGRs. Peak HGRs were found to occur at end of cycle (EOC), when HFIR’s control elements are
fully withdrawn, with average peak HGRs at EOC for the front cladding tube pair, center cladding tube
pair, back cladding tube pair, aluminum components, and titanium components of 1.89, 1.70, 1.56, 1.93,
and 2.67 W/g, respectively. These values were used in the design basis thermal models with Gaussian fits
of the HGRs with respect to axial position in HFIR.

Separate fixed-source calculations with MCNP and AutomateD VAriaNce reduction Generator
(ADVANTG) were used to determine dpa accumulation estimates based on an average HFIR cycle length
of 25 days. The analysis indicated that a four-cycle irradiation in HFIR should produce maximum specimen
doses on the order of 0.1 dpa, which was the target range identified for observing maximum transient
bowing in SiC/SiC cladding. More specifically, the two front tubes achieve a maximum dose of
approximately 0.12 dpa, whereas the middle and back tubes achieve maximum doses greater than 0.10 dpa
and 0.09 dpa, respectively. The three-pair arrangement provides a range of irradiation conditions in a single
experiment and is intended to support comparison of bowing behavior as a function of total dose. The
spatially dependent accumulated doses in the cladding tubes after four HFIR cycles are shown in Figure 2.
These doses were used in the subsequent bowing evaluations.

The previous neutronics analysis was conducted with 381 mm long cladding tubes, whereas the design basis

Accordingly, the previous neutronics analysis continues to provide the design basis for expected heat

 3.2 Thermal Analysis
Previous thermal analyses used HGRs from the neutronics analysis described in Section 3.1 together with
a 3D finite element model of the experiment [8]. Unlike the earlier low-temperature bowing tests, which
exposed specimens directly to reactor coolant, the present experiment uses a sealed housing and an internal
holder separated by insulating gas gaps. This configuration allows specimen temperature to be controlled
through the holder and housing diameters (gas gap size) and gas composition while preserving the radial
neutron flux gradients needed to drive differential swelling and bowing. The analyses demonstrated that
the dry irradiation vehicle could achieve SiC/SiC cladding temperatures of approximately 300 °C, which is
representative of LWR operation, while maintaining acceptable thermal gradients.
The optimization conducted in the previous analyses resulted in a holder outer diameter of 51.4 mm being
selected for the 53.74 mm housing inner diameter. However, the as-built components had diameters of
51.41 and 53.53 mm for the holder and housing, respectively. As a result, the as-built gas gap at room
temperature was 1.06 mm instead of the optimized 1.17 mm. To correct the specimen temperatures for the
smaller gas gap, the fill gas composition was altered in the ANSYS design model to determine the
composition that best matched the specimen temperatures to the previous optimized values. A composition
of 86% He, Ar balance was selected for the as-built capsule, in comparison with the 90% He, Ar balance
composition selected during the previous optimization. The optimized and as-built specimen and capsule
component temperatures predicted by the as-built thermal model are summarized in Table 1 and Table 2,
respectively. The 86% He, Ar balance gas composition maintains the 300 °C average specimen temperature
while preventing significant differences in the temperature gradients or component temperatures.

 3.3 Predicted Bowing
The predicted bowing is discussed in Russell et al. [8]. The resulting time evolution of the maximum dose
in each tube specimen and the corresponding bowing and differential volumetric swelling across each
cladding tube specimen at the axial midplane of the specimens are repeated here in Figure 4. The maximum
predicted bowing is in the range of 0.55–0.65 mm for all the specimens, with the center tubes expected to
experience the largest bowing. The experiment is planned to be removed after four HFIR cycles, which
correlates to the maximum bowing of the specimens.
The previous analysis is still expected to be valid for the as-built experiment because the predicted specimen
temperature distributions and neutron flux gradients for the as-built experiment are consistent with those
applied in the analysis. Furthermore, the as-built experiment assures that unconstrained bowing will be
permitted.

 4. SPECIMEN PRE-IRRADIATION CHARACTERIZATION
General Atomics (GA) completed fabrication and delivered a set of silicon carbide composite cladding tube
specimens to Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) in late 2025. Upon receipt, the specimens underwent
extensive pre-irradiation characterization to allow detailed analysis of the irradiation-induced specimen
deformation.

 4.1 Specimen Engraving
Prior to completing pre-irradiation characterization, each specimen was laser engraved with a specimen
identification (ID) and a series of markers to enable the determination of local displacements after
irradiation. The specimen IDs are 4-01, 4-02, 4-03, 5-01, 5-02, and 5-03, corresponding to the last characters
of the ID numbers given to each specimen by GA (2312FD004-01, 2312FD004-02, 2312FD004-03,
2312FD005-01, 2312FD005-02, and 2312FD005-03, respectively). The markers are arranged in eight
longitudinal rows that are equally spaced around the specimen circumference. Each row consists of an
alternating series of lines and small circles with an “X” at its center. Each row contains 18 markers separated
by approximately 31.75 mm, for a total of 144 per specimen, and a numeric ID (1–8) is engraved at the top
of each row. The intersection of each engraved “X” serves as the measurement location for subsequent
measurements, while the lines and circles are primarily included to more easily locate the “X’s”.
The specimen ID, row IDs, lines, and circles were first engraved on an Epilog laser engraver along the
length of each specimen using a 3D printed fixture that allowed each specimen to be rotated by 45° after
the engraving of each row. When row six on specimen 4-01 was engraved, the fixture was misaligned,
causing the row engraving to be misaligned with the axis of the specimen, as shown in Figure 23. However,
the engravings were still usable for subsequent pre-irradiation profilometry. Each specimen was then
transferred to an ACSYS Piranha laser engraver to engrave the “X” at the center of each circle due to its
increased precision and engraving quality, including the misaligned row six of 4-01. Examples of the
engravings are shown in Figure 5.

 Rod bowing in as-fabricated samples was measured by taking the axial profile along the length of
each specimen at each angle and coordinate shifting the profile to zero each end of the tube. A flatness

cladding tubes 4-01 and 5-01 were the front pair of specimens, 4-02 and 5-02 were the center pair, and 4-

closest to the core of HFIR.

Next, the pins were inserted into the cladding tubes and the upper centering fixture while ensuring that the

 6. PLANNED POST-IRRADIATION EVALUATIONS
Following removal from HFIR, the capsule will be shipped to hot cells on site at ORNL. The capsule will
be cut open to remove the holder assembly; this cut is recommended to be placed just below the top cap,
where the wave spring is located. The wire, locknut, tie rod, and lower centering fixture may then be
removed before sliding the top centering fixture and cladding tubes partially out of the holder. It is then
recommended to rotate the holder so that the pins are oriented vertically, which will allow them to fall out
of their respective holes. The top centering fixture and cladding tubes may then be removed. The cladding
tube specimens will then be measured with similar methods as described in Section 4.2.3. These post-
irradiation measurements will be compared with the pre-irradiation measurements reported here to quantify
the radiation-induced bowing. In addition, the thermometry will be removed from the holder to determine
the approximate irradiation temperature of the experiment.

 7. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
This report summarizes the pre-irradiation characterization and successful assembly of a HFIR irradiation
experiment that was inserted into position VXF-19 for HFIR cycle 516, which began May 12, 2026. The
experiment will be removed after cycle 519, which is currently scheduled to end in December 2026. The
overarching goal of the experiment is to validate thermomechanical models for radiation-induced bowing
of SiC/SiC composite components for LWR applications. These models will ultimately inform licensing
codes and methods to ensure that SiC/SiC bowing does not threaten the safe operating conditions of
LWRs. Previous calculations have predicted lateral bowing of SiC/SiC components during exposure to
either a differential radial fast neutron flux gradient or a significant radial temperature gradient during
irradiation [6, 7]. This experiment targets the former scenario, as HFIR’s reflector positions provide a
significant radial fast neutron flux gradient. Six SiC/SiC cladding tube specimens that are approximately
558.5 mm in length with representative PWR diameters are being irradiated. The specimens are contained
within a dry, sealed irradiation capsule designed to achieve specimen temperatures of 300 °C during
irradiation, which is representative of LWR cladding temperatures. The specimens are predicted to reach
maximum doses from 0.09 to 0.12 dpa after four irradiation cycles, and the maximum bowing is predicted
to be in the range of 0.55–0.65 mm for all the specimens, with the center tubes expected to experience the
largest bowing [8]. The spread in maximum doses among the cladding tubes provides a basis for
evaluating the dose at which maximum transient bowing occurs.
In addition to typical dimensional inspections (length, inner diameter, and outer diameter), the cladding
tube specimens were measured with a light curtain optical micrometer to characterize any bowing that
may have been present before irradiation. Bowing was found to be generally less than 0.15 mm in four of
the tubes but reached 0.25 mm in 4-02 and 0.45 mm in 4-03. Additionally, engraving markers were
inscribed along the outer surfaces of the specimens and mapped using analytical photography and a three-
dimensional stage. These measurements will be repeated post-irradiation to quantify radiation-induced
bowing. While the current experiment is intended to evaluate unconstrained bowing, future experiments
with this vehicle are being considered that would incorporate representative grid spacers to study the
effects of their constraint on the bowing of SiC/SiC cladding tubes, as their constraint is expected to
significantly reduce the bowing magnitude [3, 5, 8].
