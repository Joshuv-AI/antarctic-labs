Final report on DOE ASR project (DE-SC0022887)

Zhiming Kuang

The main research activities and findings on this project are the following:

In situ cloud measurements by aircrafts, an important part of the ASR program, are
expensive and are in our view underutilized. A major motivation for our ASR project is
to better use such data, especially those collected by ASR. The Paluch diagram has
served historically as a way of analyzing aircraft data to infer sources of air in the cloud.
However, Heus et al. (2008, JAS, Mixing in Shallow Cumulus Clouds Studied by
Lagrangian Particle Tracking) casted serious doubts on this method. In particular, their
Fig. 7 showed that, in a LES, results from the Paluch analysis were biased relative to
results from Lagrangian tracking; the latter is treated as the ground truth. Because it is not
possible to do Lagrangian tracking in observations, their results imply limited utility of
aircraft measurements without serious, yet to be developed, bias correction.

An important contribution from this project is that we have resolved this discrepancy and
traced it to incorrect statistical analysis. Using York regression, instead of the regular
regression, and with attention to the bi-modal characteristics of the distribution from the
Lagrangian tracking, we were able to reconcile the difference: the Paluch diagram and
Lagrangian tracking produce the same results for all heights. This result has important
implications: Paluch diagram analyses of in situ cloud measurements by aircrafts can
indeed be used to infer the source levels of air and to study cumulus mixing, at least in
steady oceanic cases like BOMEX. This work has been published in Lamraoui et al.
(2024). We have also made good process on extending the work to continental cases with
strong diurnal variations and spatial heterogeneity. The results are yet to be published.

After confirming the validation of the Paluch diagram analysis in oceanic cases, detailed
analyses of in situ aircraft measurements, both from the ASR ACE-ENA campaign and
from other campaigns such as EUREC4A have been carried out. Some data quality issues
were identified in the EUREC4A data and resolved. A variety of patterns have been
identified in the Paluch diagrams, including two-point mixing lines, samples along
density isopleths, and many cases in between. One new result is the presence of linear
mixing lines with a negatively buoyant cloudy end point. This has never been reported in
the literature and has not been seen before in our previous analysis, and may imply
rigorous mixing for cloudy parcels that overshoot their levels of neutral buoyancy.
However, obtaining sufficient contexts from observations to interpret these patterns
proved to be difficult. Large-domain large-eddy simulations (LES) were carried out to
allow for virtual field campaigns. Significant progresses were made in finding the LES
configurations that produce simulations most similar to the field campaigns, including
statistics such as source levels and the distribution in terms of mixing fractions between
the model and the aircraft measurements. However, a full synthesis of the LES simulation
and observation results was not completed before funding ran out; the pause and
resumption of the funding also had an effect on the project. We plan to seek additional
support to complete this work.

The grant also supported two additional papers that align with ASR’s goal of better
understanding and representing moist convection:

The first is that we have developed a theoretical framework based on zero-buoyancy
plumes for moist convection in radiative-convective equilibrium. This framework builds
upon previous work on zero-buoyancy plume and makes it into a complete framework.
This is a major step forward that will be of much interest to the ASR community. The
paper is now published (Hu et al., 2024).

The second paper is on data-driven parameterizations of moist convection (Kuang 2024),
which aligns well with the ultimate goal of our project and a major objective of the ASR
program. This was a multi-year undertaking with significant advances on the application
of state-space models to cumulus convection, which is a first in the literature, and newly
developed methods to quantify convection memory in a data-driven fashion, which is
quite nontrivial. This paper has been published, and an extension of the work into the
nonlinear regime is ongoing.
