Summary Page for the DOE SBIR Phase-1 Grant

The development of complex Deep Learning (DL) architectures, such as Large Language
Models (LLMs) and advanced Computer Vision (CV) models, demands significant computational
resources for pre-training, fine-tuning, and deployment. As these models grow in complexity,
efficiently managing workloads across various infrastructures has become increasingly
challenging. While distributed frameworks like X-ScaleAI excel at handling training and
deployment, they lack integrated Hyper Parameter Optimization (HPO) capabilities, which are
critical for achieving optimal model performance. The expanded search space in advanced
models significantly increases the computational demands of hyperparameter tuning. Although
tools like DeepHyper automate this tuning process, their lack of seamless integration with
training frameworks complicates their application to diverse workloads, making optimization a
cumbersome task.

X-ScaleSolutions proposes integrating DeepHyper, a state-of-the-art distributed hyperparameter
optimization tool (developed under the DOE ASCR program), into the X-ScaleAI framework,
resulting in a unified platform called X-ScaleAI-Hyper. DeepHyper supports established
optimization techniques such as Grid Search, Random Search, Bayesian Optimization, and
Aging Evolution, while X-ScaleAI enables efficient distributed DL training and inference covering
many parallelism schemes for a wide range of models defined in PyTorch or HuggingFace. This
integration will equip X-ScaleAI-Hyper with advanced hyperparameter optimization capabilities,
empowering users with a streamlined workflow for tuning and training models on diverse
infrastructures.

a. What are the major goals and objectives of this project? ​
​
Our goal in this project is to develop an integrated framework that combines the distributed DL
training and development capabilities of XScaleAI with scalable hyperparameter optimization
techniques of DeepHyper. We aim to develop a highly scalable, highly portable solution for
training and tuning models on diverse infrastructures. Figure. 1 represents our vision for the
proposed X-ScaleAI-Hyper framework. ​
​
We propose both containerized and non-containerized solutions that support tight integration of
model tuning and training into a streamlined workflow empowering organizations deploying AI
workloads at scale, such as enterprises in healthcare, finance, agriculture, and other industries. ​
The public benefit lies in democratizing AI capabilities, improving productivity, and expanding
the reach of AI-driven innovation across various industries.​

The specific tasks for this effort include: (1) Integration of DeepHyper’s Exhaustive Search
Methods; (2) Integration of Iterative Search Methods; (3) Developing a Unified X-ScaleAI-Hyper
API; (4) Containerization and Smart Allocation of On-premise and Cloud Resources; (5)

Codesigning a Two-Tier Scalability Scheme for HPO and Distributed DL; (6) Integration of
Distributed Decentralized Bayesian Optimization; (7) Co-designing Multi-Level Checkpointing
with SCR-Exa; (8) Co-designing an Automated HPO Selection Strategy; (9) Implementing
µTransfer for Large-Scale LLMs; and (10) Extensive Testing and Benchmarking of
X-ScaleAI-Hyper. Items 1, 2 and parts of items 3 and 10 will be carried out in Phase I of this
project. The remaining items will be completed in Phase II.

b.2) Specific Objectives

During the present reporting period of phase 1, the following specific objectives have been
concluded, or developed successfully to be integrated into objectives of the next reporting
phase.

 1.​ Deploying DeepHyper hyperparameter optimization (HPO) on scalable Ray clusters on
 Premise.
 1.1.​ We deployed HPO techniques on a scalable Ray Cluster. Ray is a popular
 communication framework used for DL training and deployment on-Premise and
 in cloud over distributed CPUs and GPUs. It is asynchronous and supports
 dynamic scale-up and scale-down. We validated its efficiency and scalability.
 On NERSC Perlmutter, we demonstrated scaling to 32 GPUs across 8 nodes
 with up to 16 concurrent HPO trials.

 2.​ Integrating DeepHyper’s optimization search methods with distributed training.
 2.1.​ We integrated multiple HPO techniques, including random search, grid search
 and centralized bayesian optimization search (CBO) with distributed Llama 3
 training (160M and 1B parameters) using FSDP. Our key findings are:
 ●​ Both random search and CBO identified similar optimal learning rates. We
 get an optimal rate of around 1.3e-3 for 160M and around 4.3e-4 for 1B.
 ●​ Optimal learning rate scales with model size, consistent with established
 scaling laws.
 ●​ CBO's batch-synchronous behavior limits its effectiveness with high
 parallelism. We plan to overcome this with our Phase II work on
 asynchronous BO.

 3.​ Containerization
 3.1.​ We containerized the full stack using the Apptainer container runtime on TACC
 Frontera and Shifter on NERSC PErlomutter. Apptainer is highly portable, runs
 on host without root privileges in many cases and has robust support for
 Message Passing Interface (MPI). Shifter is designed with high scalability and
 portability in mind on Slingshot based systems like NERSC Perlmutter.

 4.​ Implementation of a two-tier parallelization scheme for distributed training and HPO
 4.1.​ We devised a two-tier solution where HPO tasks are grouped and
 asynchronously launched in parallel and distributed training occurs under each
 HPO grouping. This decouples HPO scaling from training scaling and allows us
 to independently test scaling and efficiency for both workloads.

The tests were conducted on NERSC’s Perlmutter system using up to 8 GPU nodes. We used
two Llama3 LLMs (https://www.llama.com/models/llama-3/), scaled down to 160 million (160M)
and 1 billion (1M) model parameters. Llama3 models are autoregressive decoder-only
transformer models. All experiments trained on 300 million (∓ 3%) of FineWeb-Edu
(https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17557) tokens, with exact counts varying due to batch size divisibility
constraints. We selected optimizer learning rate (lr) as the hyperparameter to optimize and the
average of the last ten values of the model training loss as the objective scalar to be minimized.
Parallel HPO trials were scheduled and launched asynchronously using the ray collective
communication library, while the NVIDIA Collective Communication Library (NCCL) was used to
manage communication within a single trial. We observe that optimal learning rate decreases

with model scale; the 1b model achieves its best performance at lr=4.3e-4, a factor of 3 less
than 160M model's optimal lr=1.3e-3. This aligns with established scaling behavior (Kaplan et.
al. https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361, Hoffmann et. al. https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556). ​
​
Figure 2 compares the HPO results for the 160M model with those for the 1B model. We show
two HPO algorithms: Random search and centralized bayesian optimization (CBO). We used 16
parallel trials for a random search algorithm and 4 parallel trials for CBO, which learns from
previous trials. The larger model produces a better training loss.

Figure 2. HPO results for two different models, showing an optimum learning rate that minimizes training
loss. (a) Results for the 160M model. (b) results for the 1B model. The optimal training loss for the larger
 model is smaller than that for the smaller model. ​

In figure 3 we show the effect of model size on the value of the optimal learning rate. Larger
model sizes are known to train better with a smaller lr. The fit demonstrates established scaling
laws.

Figure 3. Effect of model size on optimal hyperparmeter and model training loss values. (a) Optimal lr for
 two model sizes, showing a lower value for the bigger model.(b) Best training loss at optimal lr.

3.1.1.3 Current Solutions
Large software companies often rely on internally developed tools to manage distributed training,
deployment, and model optimization. While these solutions can be effective, they are typically
proprietary, resource-intensive, and not broadly accessible.
While distributed frameworks like X-ScaleAI excel at handling training and deployment, they lack
integrated Hyper Parameter Optimization (HPO) capabilities, which are critical for achieving optimal
model performance. The expanded search space in advanced models significantly increases the
computational demands of hyperparameter tuning. Although tools like DeepHyper automate this tuning
process, their lack of seamless integration with training frameworks complicates their application to
diverse workloads, making optimization a cumbersome task.

3.1.1.5 Value Proposition
X-ScaleAI-Hyper delivers a unified, distributed platform for large-scale deep learning training and
automated hyperparameter optimization, enabling organizations to efficiently develop, tune, and deploy
high-performance AI models across cloud, on-premise, and high-performance computing environments.
By integrating DeepHyper—a DOE ASCR-developed state-of-the-art hyperparameter optimization
system—directly into the X-ScaleAI distributed framework, the platform combines advanced optimization
techniques such as Bayesian Optimization and Aging Evolution with scalable PyTorch and HuggingFace
training workflows. Unlike existing solutions that rely on disconnected toolchains or proprietary internal
systems, X-ScaleAI-Hyper provides a publicly available, industry-agnostic solution that streamlines AI
development while improving compute efficiency and reducing integration overhead.
Initial customers in healthcare, finance, agriculture, e-commerce, autonomous systems, and cloud/MLOps
platforms are expected to adopt this technology to accelerate model development, enhance predictive
performance, and optimize infrastructure utilization. Federal programs including DOE AI for Science, DoD
HPCMP, NIH BD2K, and NSF NAIRR represent additional transition pathways where scalable AI
optimization is critical. By delivering measurable reductions in tuning time, engineering effort, and
computational waste, X-ScaleAI-Hyper positions itself as a high-impact, cross-sector platform for next-
generation AI development and deployment.

of the software packages and libraries that can take advantage of this heterogeneity have remained solely
within the HPC ecosystem.

8.1.5.2 Introduction to the technology being developed
X-ScaleSolutions proposes integrating DeepHyper, a state-of-the-art distributed hyperparameter
optimization tool (developed under the DOE ASCR program), into the X-ScaleAI framework, resulting in a
unified platform called X-ScaleAI-Hyper. DeepHyper supports established optimization techniques such as
Grid Search, Random Search, Bayesian Optimization, and Aging Evolution, while X-ScaleAI enables efficient
distributed DL training and inference covering many parallelism schemes for a wide range of models
defined in PyTorch or HuggingFace. This integration will equip X-ScaleAI-Hyper with advanced

 and will your solution advance it? Can you be specific in performance expectations?
X-ScaleSolutions, LLC proposes integrating DeepHyper, a state-of-the-art distributed hyperparameter
optimization tool (developed under the DOE ASCR program), into the X-ScaleAI framework. The integration
with DeepHyper will enable X-ScaleAI to seamlessly incorporate hyperparameter tuning into its existing
framework, providing users with a powerful, efficient, and user-friendly tool for enhancing DL workflows
on on-premise and cloud systems.
While distributed frameworks like X-ScaleAI excel at handling training and deployment, they lack
integrated Hyper Parameter Optimization (HPO) capabilities, which are critical for achieving optimal
model performance. The expanded search space in advanced models significantly increases the
computational demands of hyperparameter tuning. Although tools like DeepHyper automate this tuning
process, their lack of seamless integration with training frameworks complicates their application to
diverse workloads, making optimization a cumbersome task.
