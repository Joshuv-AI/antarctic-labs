A remark on rational quartic curves in prime Fano threefolds of degree 22 22
Kiryong Chung, Jeong-Seop Kim

Abstract
. In this short note, using the Sarkisov link between a prime Fano threefold V 22 V_{22} of degree 22 22 and the quintic del Pezzo threefold V 5 V_{5} , we prove that the Hilbert scheme of rational quartic curves in V 22 V_{22} admits a generically 2 2 -to- 1 1 rational map onto the projective space ℙ 4 \mathbb{P}^{4} .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14J45, 14E15, 14M15, 14C05.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Previous works

The geometry of moduli spaces of rational curves in projective varieties has been extensively studied from various perspectives, including birational geometry and enumerative geometry.
Among these directions, the construction of compactifications and the investigation of their geometric invariants play a fundamental role in understanding the global structure of these moduli spaces.
In
[
CK11
,
CHK12
,
CM17
,
CHL18
,
Chu23
]
, the authors focused on the birational relation among several compactifications of moduli spaces of rational curves.
Through a systematic study of various compactifications, the authors established explicit birational relations by extending the moduli-theoretic birational maps. On the other hand, in
[
CKK25a
]
, by finding torus-invariant quartic curves in the Mukai–Umemura threefold (
[
MU83
]
), the authors proved the smoothness of the corresponding Hilbert scheme. These results subsequently enabled the application of various localization techniques to compute Donaldson–Thomas (DT) type invariants and verify several important conjectures.
For detailed descriptions of conjectures and their proof, see
[
CMT18
,
CT21
,
KP08
]
and
[
CLW21
,
CW26
]
.
Also, we obtained global descriptions of Hilbert schemes of rational curves for the quadric threefold and the quintic del Pezzo threefold (
[
CHY23
]
and
[
CKK25b
]
). Once such geometric descriptions are established, localization formulas (
[
GP99
]
) can be canonically applied to compute the associated DT-type invariants. On the other hand, for prime Fano threefolds
V_{22}
of degree
22
, including the Mukai–Umemura variety, the use of exceptional collections (and their mutations) of
V_{22}
allowed us to identify a component of the Hilbert scheme with a Grassmannian variety (
[
CL26
]
).
This approach depends heavily on the algebraic properties of rational curves in
V_{22}
.

1.2.
Result

In this short article, we propose a geometric property of the Hilbert scheme of rational quartic curves in
V_{22}
inspired by so-called
correlators
in quantum cohomology (
[
BM04
, Section 2.2]
).
Let
\mathbf{H}_{4}(X)
be the Hilbert scheme of curves
C
in a smooth projective variety
X
with Hilbert polynomial
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C}(m))=4m+1
.
Unless otherwise stated, we henceforth assume that
\mathbf{H}_{4}(X)
is irreducible to simplify the discussion.
Let
V_{22}\dashrightarrow V_{5}
be
the Sarkisov link
with centers: a line
L\subset V_{22}
and a rational quintic curve
\Gamma\subset V_{5}
. For the definition and geometric property of the Sarkisov link, see Section
2.2
and
[
KPS18
]
.
It is well known that the strict transform
\overline{C}
of a general rational quartic curve
C\subset V_{22}
, not meeting the line
L
, intersects
\Gamma
in four points (cf.
[
TZ12
, Section 1.3]
).
We prove that such correspondence provides a covering structure of the Hilbert scheme
\mathbf{H}_{4}(V_{22})
over a projective space
\mathbb{P}^{4}
.

Theorem 1.1

(
=
Theorem
3.5
)
.

Let
\mathbf{H}_{4}(V_{22})
be the Hilbert scheme of rational quartic curves in
V_{22}
.
Then there exists a
2
-to-
1
rational map

\Phi:\mathbf{H}_{4}(V_{22})\dashrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{4}\Gamma(\cong\mathbb{P}^{4})

which associates a general rational quartic curve to four points in a rational quintic curve
\Gamma\subset V_{5}
.

The outline of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is as follows.
For four general points in
V_{5}
, there exist three rational quartic curves (Lemma
3.2
).
One of them is contained in the strict transform of the flopping center of the Sarkisov link, and thus the inverse image of
\Phi
consists of two rational quartic curves in
V_{22}
.
Moreover, by analyzing the exceptional locus of a residual map, we can show that the rational map
\Phi
in Theorem
1.1
is branched over a hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
. For a geometric meaning of the branch locus of the map
\Phi
, see the proof of Theorem
3.5
.

2.1.
Rational quartic curves in
V_{5}

Let
V_{5}
be the quintic del Pezzo threefold.
In
[
CKK25b
]
, by extending the result of
[
FGP19
, Proposition 4.11]
, it is proved that the Hilbert scheme
\mathbf{H}_{4}(V_{5})
of rational quartic curves in
V_{5}
is isomorphic to a Grassmannian bundle over the Hilbert scheme
\mathbf{H}_{1}(V_{5})
of lines in
V_{5}
.
More precisely, a rational quartic curve in
V_{5}
arises as the residual curve of a linear section containing a line in
V_{5}
. In detail, let
l
be a line in
V_{5}
, and let
\Lambda
be a
4
-dimensional linear subspace of
\mathbb{P}^{6}
containing
l
.
Then
\Lambda\cap V_{5}=l\cup C
for a rational quartic curve
C
in
V_{5}
.
Moreover, such a residual line
l
is uniquely determined by
C
.
Indeed, if
C
admitted two distinct residual lines
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
, then the linear span
\Lambda
of
C
in
\mathbb{P}^{6}
would contain both
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
.
Consequently,

\Lambda\cap V_{5}\supseteq C\cup l_{1}\cup l_{2}

would have degree at least
6
, contradicting the fact that
\deg(V_{5}\cap\Lambda)=5
.

Note that the family of
4
-dimensional linear subspaces containing a fixed line in
\mathbb{P}^{6}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Gr}(3,5)
, and
\mathbf{H}_{1}(V_{5})\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Hence
\mathbf{H}_{4}(V_{5})
is isomorphic to a
\mathrm{Gr}(3,5)
-bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{2}
(
[
CKK25b
, Theorem 1.1]
).
A key technical step in the proof of this claim was to exclude the possibility that a non-Cohen–Macaulay curve
C
with Hilbert polynomial
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C}(m))=4m+1
is supported on an elliptic quartic curve (
[
CKK25b
, Section 4.2]
).
This was achieved by studying the geometry of surfaces arising as hyperplane sections of the quintic del Pezzo variety
V_{5}
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
FN89
, Corollary 2.1]
,
[
Ili94
, 1.2.1]
)
.

There exists a curve
\Sigma
and a surface
B
containing
\Sigma
on
V_{5}
which satisfy the following.

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle=\{x\in V_{5}\mid\text{$\exists$ only one line $\ell$ in $V_{5}$ such that $x\in\ell$}\},

\displaystyle B\setminus\Sigma

\displaystyle=\{x\in V_{5}\mid\text{$\exists$ exactly two lines $\ell$ in $V_{5}$ such that $x\in\ell$}\}

\displaystyle V_{5}\setminus B

\displaystyle=\{x\in V_{5}\mid\text{$\exists$ exactly three lines $\ell$ in $V_{5}$ such that $x\in\ell$}\}

Moreover,
B\in|-K_{V_{5}}|=|2H^{*}|
where
H^{*}=\cal{O}_{V_{5}}(1)
.

3.
Rational quartic curves in
V_{22}

The Sarkisov link between
V_{5}
and
V_{22}
induces a relationship between certain families of rational curves in these varieties.
We first study the geometry of the quintic del Pezzo threefold
V_{5}
arising from its Plücker embedding
V_{5}\subset\mathbb{P}^{6}
.
In this section, we will repeatedly denote by
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
p_{3}
, and
p_{4}
four points of
V_{5}
, which are also points of a rational quintic curve
\Gamma
in
V_{5}
. They correspond to a point of the fourth symmetric product of the curve
\Gamma
, and they span a linear subspace in
\mathbb{P}^{6}
.
We denote by

D=p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}+p_{4}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{4}\Gamma\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda_{D}=\langle p_{1},\,p_{2},\,p_{3},\,p_{4}\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{6}.

We say that the four points
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
p_{3}
, and
p_{4}
are general if
D\in\mathrm{Sym}^{4}\Gamma
is general and their linear span
\Lambda_{D}
has dimension
3
.

3.1.
Lemmas

We begin with several lemmas needed for the proof of Theorem
1.1
(
=
Theorem
3.5
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a rational quintic curve in
V_{5}
, and
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
p_{3}
, and
p_{4}
be four general points of
\Gamma
in
V_{5}
.
Then the linear subspace
\Lambda_{D}
spanned by these four points does not contain any line in
V_{5}
.

Proof.

Consider the incidence

\cal{I}=\{(D,\ell)\in\mathrm{Sym}^{4}\Gamma\times\mathbf{H}_{1}(V_{5})\mid\ell\subseteq\Lambda_{D}\}

together with the two projection maps:
\pi_{1}:\cal{I}\to\mathrm{Sym}^{4}\Gamma
and
\pi_{2}:\cal{I}\to\mathbf{H}_{1}(V_{5})
.
We claim that
\cal{I}
has dimension
3
.
Since
\mathbf{H}_{1}(V_{5})
has dimension
2
, it suffices to show that
\pi_{2}^{-1}(\ell)
has dimension at most
1
for all
\ell\in\mathbf{H}_{1}(V_{5})
.

Fix a line
\ell
in
V_{5}
, and consider a linear projection
P_{\ell}:\mathbb{P}^{6}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{4}
from
\ell
.
Then the condition
\ell\subseteq\Lambda_{D}
is equivalent to
\dim P_{\ell}(\Lambda_{D})=1
, that is, the points
P_{\ell}(x_{1})
,
P_{\ell}(x_{2})
,
P_{\ell}(x_{3})
,
P_{\ell}(x_{4})
are collinear in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
.
However, the dimension of the family of
4
-secant lines of the nondegenerate curve
P_{\ell}(\Gamma)
does not exceed
1
, so the proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
p_{3}
, and
p_{4}
be four general points of the curve
\Gamma
in
V_{5}
.
Then there exist exactly three rational quartic curves in
V_{5}
passing through these four points.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda_{D}
be the
3
-dimensional linear subspace of
\mathbb{P}^{6}
spanned by
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
p_{3}
, and
p_{4}
.
Then
\Lambda_{D}
intersects
V_{5}
in five points.
We denote by
p_{5}
the fifth point of
V_{5}\cap\Lambda_{D}
other than
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
p_{3}
, and
p_{4}
, and define the residual map

\rho:\mathrm{Sym}^{4}\Gamma\dashrightarrow V_{5};\ p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}+p_{4}\mapsto p_{5}.

Let
\ell
be a line in
V_{5}
passing through the point
p_{5}
.
By Lemma
3.1
,
\Lambda_{D}
does not contain
\ell
.
Let
\overline{\Lambda}_{D,\ell}=\langle\Lambda_{D},\ell\rangle
be the
4
-dimensional linear subspace of
\mathbb{P}^{6}
spanned by
\Lambda_{D}
and
\ell
.
Then the intersection
V_{5}\cap\overline{\Lambda}_{D,\ell}
is a reducible curve of degree
5
of the form
\ell\cup C
, and hence
C
is a curve of degree
4
.
By adjunction, the curve
\ell\cup C
has arithmetic genus
1
.
Since
\ell
is a secant line to
C
, it follows that
C
is a rational quartic curve.
Note that there are three distinct choices for the line
\ell=\ell_{j}
.
Consequently, there exist three distinct rational quartic curves
C=C_{j}
, each associated with a different line
\ell_{j}
for
j=1,2,3
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

,
p_{2}
,

, and

be four points of the curve

Then there exists a unique rational quartic curve contained in the hyperplane section

is non-normal, and its normalization

Let

Case 2.

is non-normal, and its normalization
\phi\colon S\to A
is isomorphic to the first Hirzebruch surface

.
Let
s
and

denote the exceptional section and a fiber, respectively, and set

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
V_{22}
be a prime Fano threefold of degree
22
.
Assume that the Hilbert scheme
\mathbf{H}_{4}(V_{22})
is irreducible.

Theorem 3.5
.

Under the above assumption, the Hilbert scheme
\mathbf{H}_{4}(V_{22})
is birational to a double cover of
\mathbb{P}^{4}(\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{4}\Gamma)
branched over a hypersurface.

Proof.

We denote by
\mathbf{H}_{4}(V_{5},\Gamma)
the Hilbert scheme of rational quartic curves
C
in
V_{5}
such that the
0
-dimensional subscheme
C\cap\Gamma
has length
4
.
By Lemma
3.2
, there exists a generically
3
-to-
1
rational map

\mathbf{H}_{4}(V_{5},\Gamma)\dashrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{4}\Gamma\cong\mathbb{P}^{4}.

Let
[C]\in\mathbf{H}_{4}(V_{5},\Gamma)
, and assume that
C
admits a strict transform
\overline{C}
on
V_{22}
under the Sarkisov link.
Then

\overline{C}.H=C.(3H^{*}-2\Gamma)=3\cdot 4-2\cdot 4=4,

so
\overline{C}
is a rational quartic curve in
V_{22}
.
Note that
C
admits a strict transform on
V_{22}
if and only if
C\not\subseteq A
.
Let us define

\mathbf{S}_{A}=\{[C]\in\mathbf{H}_{4}(V_{5},\Gamma)\mid C\subseteq A\},

which parametrizes the rational quartic curves
C
in
V_{5}
such that
C\cap\Gamma
is a
0
-dimensional subscheme of length
4
and the curve
C
is contracted by the Sarkisov link to
V_{22}
.
Then
\mathbf{S}_{A}
maps birationally onto
\mathrm{Sym}^{4}\Gamma
by Lemma
3.4
.
Consequently, the map

\mathbf{H}_{4}(V_{22})\dashrightarrow\mathbf{H}_{4}(V_{5},\Gamma)\setminus\mathbf{S}_{A}

is birational, and hence we obtain a generically
2
-to-
1
rational map

\Phi:\mathbf{H}_{4}(V_{22})\dashrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{4}\Gamma\cong\mathbb{P}^{4}.

We now describe the branch locus of
\Phi
.
Recall that exactly three lines pass through a general point
x\in V_{5}
.
Let
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
,
x_{3}
, and
x_{4}
be four general points of
\Gamma
, and let
\ell_{1}
,
\ell_{2}
, and
\ell_{3}
be the lines passing through the residual point
x_{5}=\rho(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4})\in V_{5}
.
As in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, define

\overline{\Lambda}_{i}=\langle x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},\ell_{i}\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{6}.

Then the residual curves
C_{i}
to
\ell_{i}
in
\overline{\Lambda}_{i}\cap V_{5}
are precisely the preimages of the map
\Phi
over the point
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{4}\Gamma
.
Also, the map
\Phi
branches precisely when two of the three associated quartic curves
C_{i}
coincide.
This occurs exactly when two of the lines among
\ell_{1}
,
\ell_{2}
, and
\ell_{3}
coincide and the remaining line is contained in
A
.
Equivalently, after relabeling,

\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ell_{3}\subseteq A.

Recall that the locus
B\subset V_{5}
consisting of points through which two of the three lines coincide is a divisor in the linear system
|\cal{O}_{V_{5}}(2)|
(see Proposition
2.1
).
Therefore, the inverse image
\rho^{-1}(B)
contains the branch divisor of
\Phi
. Since
\Phi
is generically two-to-one, this branch divisor is a hypersurface.
∎
