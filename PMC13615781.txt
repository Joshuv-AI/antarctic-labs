Network characteristics and driving factors of leaf trait networks along a gradient of rocky desertification
Authors: Huilian Deng, Shichu Liang, Wenxing Long, Miao Dong, Yong Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Rocky desertification severely constrains ecosystem functioning and vegetation in karst landscapes. Understanding how soil environmental changes influence plant functional coordination is therefore critical for ecological restoration. Here, we investigated soil physicochemical properties and leaf functional traits across a rocky desertification gradient in a karst ecosystem of southwest China. Using leaf trait networks (LTNs) to evaluate changes in trait coordination and identify key regulatory traits under different desertification intensities. We found that severely desertified (SRD) habitats were characterized by alkaline soils with lower bulk density, soil organic carbon (SOC), and total phosphorus (TP), but relatively higher total nitrogen (TN), total potassium (TK), and calcium (Ca) than lightly and moderately decertified habitats. Along the rocky desertification gradient, LTNs exhibited progressively lower edge density but higher average path length, diameter, and modularity, indicating reduced overall trait integration together with stronger functional compartmentalization under increasing environmental stress. Hub traits also shifted systematically across desertification stages. In lightly rocky desertified habitats, leaf water content, leaf thickness, and leaf nitrogen concentration acted as central traits, whereas specific leaf P and chlorophyll became dominant in moderately desertified habitats. In SRD habitats, leaf thickness and leaf tissue density emerged as the major hub traits associated with structurally conservative strategies. Redundancy analysis further showed that soil nutrient availability, pH, bulk density, and calcium jointly regulated changes in network topology across desertification stages. Overall, our results demonstrate that rocky desertification drives stage-dependent reorganization of leaf trait coordination, reflecting a transition from resource-acquisitive to structurally conservative adaptive strategies as environmental constraints intensify.

Introduction

Karst landscapes occupy approximately 12% of the global terrestrial surface (Liu, 2009; Yu et al., 2022). These landscapes are primarily formed through the dissolution of carbonate rocks, generating highly heterogeneous geomorphological features, including sinkholes, underground drainage systems, caves, and fractured bedrock structures (Geekiyanage et al., 2019). Such distinctive hydrological and geological characteristics create ecologically fragile environments that are highly sensitive to external disturbances (Zeng et al., 2024). In China, karst regions account for nearly 36% of the national land area (Jiang et al., 2014). Long-term agricultural cultivation, deforestation, and quarrying activities have substantially accelerated soil erosion and vegetation degradation in these regions (Guo et al., 2013; Li et al., 2021; Ran et al., 2023). Consequently, large areas have undergone severe rocky desertification, resulting in habitat fragmentation, biodiversity decline, and ecosystem functional deterioration (Li et al., 2020; D'Ettorre et al., 2024). Rocky desertification in Southwest China continues to expand at an annual rate of approximately 0.91%, which poses a major ecological challenge for regional sustainable development (Bai et al., 2013).

Considerable effort has been devoted to understanding how rocky desertification alters soil physicochemical properties in karst ecosystems (Wen et al., 2016; Sheng et al., 2016; Chen et al., 2018). Previous studies have shown that increasing rocky desertification generally reduces topsoil integrity, accelerates soil erosion, and alters soil organic matter, nitrogen availability, BD, and water-holding capacity (Wen et al., 2016; Sheng et al., 2016; Chen et al., 2018). However, these responses are not always consistent. For example, Ma et al. (2020) reported relatively limited variation in soil organic carbon (SOC), nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), potassium, and magnesium across desertification stages, with significant differences observed primarily for soil pH and calcium (Ca). These contrasting findings likely reflect differences in lithology, vegetation composition, microtopography, hydrological processes, and land-use history among karst regions. More importantly, they indicate that plants across rocky desertification gradients experience markedly different edaphic environments, which are expected to impose distinct selective pressures on plant functional strategies and trait coordination.

Leaves are the primary interfaces through which plants acquire carbon, regulate water loss, and exchange resources with the surrounding environment (Cornelissen et al., 2003). Consequently, variation in leaf functional traits reflects how plants adjust their resource-use strategies under changing environmental conditions (Zirbel et al., 2017). Because multiple physiological processes are tightly interconnected, plants typically respond to environmental stress through coordinated adjustments among multiple traits rather than through independent changes in individual traits. Such coordination arises from shared physiological constraints and functional trade-offs, resulting in characteristic patterns of covariation among structural, hydraulic, and nutrient-related traits (Reich et al., 1997; Liu and Ma, 2015). Understanding these coordinated responses therefore provides a more integrated perspective on plant adaptation than considering individual traits in isolation. Leaf trait networks (LTNs) offer a quantitative framework for describing this coordination by representing traits as interconnected components of an integrated functional system. For example, leaf thickness (LT), specific leaf area (SLA), and leaf nitrogen concentration often respond in a coordinated manner to nutrient limitation and drought stress, collectively reflecting the balance between resource acquisition and resource conservation. Although considerable attention has been devoted to variation in individual functional traits across environmental gradients, much less is known about whether environmental stress reorganizes trait coordination among traits at network level. In particular, it remains unclear how network topology, hub traits, and their relationships with soil conditions change along rocky desertification gradients.

To address these questions, we investigated changes in soil physicochemical properties, leaf functional traits across 36 plots distributes along a rocky desertification gradient in Guilin, Guangxi, China. We constructed leaf trait networks (LTNs) for each desertification stage and integrated network topology with soil physicochemical properties to determine how rocky desertification recognizes trait coordination and to identify the soil properties associated with these changes. Materials and methods

Study area and rocky desertification grading

This work was carried out in Guilin City, Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region, in southwestern China, with sampling plots located in Lingchuan, Lingui, and Yongfu counties (Fig. 1). Elevation across the study area ranges from 0 to 2100 m above sea level. This area is a typical karst landscape, diverse and complex terrain, low environmental carrying capacity, large surface area of exposed rock, generally shallow soil, and strong habitat heterogeneity. The region features abundant sunshine and a mild climate, and falls within the subtropical monsoon climate zone (He et al., 2021). Annual precipitation is 1856.7 mm, and the annual average relative humidity is 76% (Pan et al., 2023). Although the area experiences considerable annual rainfall, the precipitation is unevenly distributed, with the rainy season concentrated in spring and summer. The predominant soil type is limestone-derived soils, characterized by a clayey texture and pH range of 5∼8. According to the national forestry industry standard for rocky desertification classification (LY/T 1840–2020), the study area was categorized into three rocky desertification levels: lightly rocky desertified (LRD), moderately rocky desertified (MRD), and severely rocky desertified (SRD) habitats (State Forestry Administration, 2020; Jing et al., 2016). Climate data and validation

To minimize the potential confounding effects of climatic variation on LTNs analysis, monthly mean temperature and precipitation for sampling plots were extracted from the WorldClim database (https://www.worldclim.org/). One-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's HSD post-hoc tests were subsequently performed to compare climatic variables among the three counties (Lingchuan, Lingui, and Yongfu). No significant differences were detected in either temperature or precipitation among the three counties (p > 0.05), confirming a comparable macroclimatic background across the study region (Fig. S1) and minimizing the potential influence of climatic variation on subsequent analyses.

Data collection

Field survey

Field investigations were conducted from July to September 2024. Across the three rocky desertification gradients, three transect groups were established, each containing 12 plots, giving a total of 36 sampling plots. Each plot measured 20 m × 20 m. To facilitate vegetation surveys, each plot was further subdivided into four 10 m × 10 m subplots. Within each subplot, species identity, plant height, individual abundance, and diameter at breast height (DBH) were recorded for all woody individuals taller than 1.3 m. In addition, several critical habitat variables were measured for each plot, including GPS coordinates, elevation, slope gradient and rock exposure rate. A total of 57 plant species belonging to 51 genera and 26 families were recorded across the three rocky desertification gradients, comprising 3030 individuals in total. The dominant species in lightly rocky desertified (LRD) habitats included Pistacia chinensis, Celtis biondii, Rhaphiolepis ferruginea var. serrata, Clerodendrum cyrtophyllum, and Loropetalum chinense. In moderately rocky desertified (MRD) habitats, the dominant species were P. chinensis, R. ferruginea var. serrata, Quercus glauca, Boniodendron minus, and Bridelia retusa. In severely rocky desertified (SRD) habitats, the dominant species included Q. glauca, Zelkova schneideriana, Pteroceltis tatarinowii, B. minus, and Lindera megaphylla.

Leaf trait measurements

Measurements of 18 leaf functional traits were conducted for 57 plant species comprising more than 3000 individuals, following the standardized protocols proposed by Cornelissen et al. (2003). From each sampled individual, three healthy and fully expanded mature leaves without visible damage or disease symptoms were collected for trait measurements. Leaf chlorophyll content (Chl, %) was determined using a portable chlorophyll meter. Leaf thickness (LT, mm) was measured using a vernier caliper with a precision of 0.01 mm. Leaf area (LA, cm2) was determined using a digital foliar scanner. Leaf fresh weight (LFW, g) was measured using an analytical balance with a precision of 0.0001 g. Leaf dry weight (LDW, g) was measured after oven-drying the samples at 65 °C for 48 h until constant mass was achieved.

Leaf carbon (LC, mg/g) and leaf nitrogen (LN, mg/g) concentrations were determined using an elemental analyzer, whereas leaf phosphorus (LP, mg/g) concentration was quantified using the molybdenum–antimony colorimetric method. Based on these measurements, specific leaf carbon (SLC, mg/cm2), specific leaf nitrogen (SLN, mg/cm2), and specific leaf phosphorus (SLP, mg/cm2) were calculated as the elemental content per unit leaf area by multiplying elemental concentration by LDW and dividing by LA. In addition, the stoichiometric ratios C/N, C/P, and N/P were calculated following Dynarski et al. (2023).

Environmental variables measurement

Soil samples were collected from the 0–15 cm soil layer after removing surface litter in each of the 36 sampling plots (20 m × 20 m) distributed along the rocky desertification gradient. Within each plot, five replicate samples were collected using an S-shaped strategy and subsequently combined into a single composite sample. All soil samples were passed through a 2-mm sieve to remove gravel, roots, and other plant debris. Approximately 500 g of soil from each plot was sealed in sampling bags and transported to the laboratory for natural air-drying. After air-drying, soil samples were ground and passed through a 100-mesh sieve prior to physicochemical analyses. Soil pH was determined using the potentiometric method at a water-to-soil ratio of 2.5:1. Soil bulk density (BD, g/cm3) was measured using the ring-knife method. Soil Ca (mg/kg) concentration was quantified using atomic absorption spectrophotometry. Soil total phosphorus (TP, g/kg), total potassium (TK, g/kg), total nitrogen (TN, g/kg), and SOC (g/kg) were determined following the standardized analytical procedures described by Bao (2000).

Construction of the leaf trait networks

To account for the influence of phylogenetic history on leaf functional traits (Tables S3, S4 and S6), we incorporated a phylogenetic correction using phylogenetically independent contrasts (PICs). Closely related species may exhibit similar trait values because of shared evolutionary ancestry rather than ecological convergence alone; therefore, phylogenetic correction is necessary to avoid overestimating trait associations caused by common ancestry (Felsenstein, 1985). Accordingly, for each rocky desertification stage, including lightly rocky desertified (LRD), moderately rocky desertified (MRD), and severely rocky desertified (SRD) habitats, PIC analysis was first applied to the species-level mean values of all measured leaf traits. The resulting phylogenetically corrected trait values were subsequently used for the construction of LTNs. Each LTN consisted of 18 leaf functional traits treated as network nodes, whereas significant pairwise trait correlations were considered network edges. Trait association strength was quantified using the absolute Pearson correlation coefficient (|r|). To identify robust trait associations while minimizing spurious correlations, edges were retained only when |r| > 0.2 and p < 0.05 (Kleyer et al., 2019). Given the moderate sample size of this study (57 species in total, including 42, 49, and 41 species recorded in LRD, MRD, and SRD habitats, respectively, with some species occurring across multiple desertification stages), adopting a stricter threshold (e.g., |r| > 0.3) would substantially reduce the number of detectable correlations and potentially exclude biologically meaningful trait coordination patterns. Therefore, the selected threshold represented a compromise between statistical robustness and ecological interpretability while controlling false positives through significance testing (p < 0.05). LTNs were constructed and visualized using the ‘igraph’ package in R. Detailed descriptions and calculation formulae for all network parameters are provided in Table S2.

To characterize network topology, four key parameters were calculated, including edge density, average path length, network diameter, and modularity. Networks with higher edge density, shorter average path length, and smaller diameter generally indicate stronger trait integration and more efficient coordination among functional traits (Armbruster et al., 2014). In contrast, modularity reflects the degree of functional compartmentalization within the network. Higher modularity indicates that traits are organized into relatively independent functional modules, which may enhance ecological flexibility by allowing different trait groups to respond semi-independently to environmental stress (He et al., 2020). However, excessively high modularity may also reflect weakened overall trait integration under stressful environmental conditions.

To further evaluate the relative importance of individual traits within LTNs, three network node-level topological parameters were calculated: degree, closeness, and clustering coefficient (Deng et al., 2012). Traits with higher degree values are connected to a large number of other traits and therefore may exert stronger regulatory influences within the network. Traits with higher closeness occupy more positions within the network and can potentially influence information or functional coordination more efficiently across the entire trait system. The clustering coefficient quantifies the degree of interconnectivity among neighboring traits surrounding a focal node. Traits with high clustering coefficients may function as local hub traits that participate in tightly coordinated trait modules and contribute substantially to overall network organization (He et al., 2020).

Statistical analyses

Community-weighted mean calculation

To scale leaf trait values from the species level to the community level, we calculated the community-weighted mean (CWM) for 18 leaf functional traits within each rocky desertification stage (LRD, MRD, and SRD). The calculation involved three steps. First, a species-by- trait matrix was constructed using the data collected in the field surveys (Section 2.3.1) and leaf trait measurements (Section 2.3.2). The mean trait value (Xᵢ) was calculated by averaging measurements from all sampled individuals of 57 woody species. Second, a plot-by-species abundance matrix was constructed based on the vegetation survey conducted in each 20 m × 20 m plot (Section 2.3.1). Finally, the two matrices were integrated to calculate CWMs for 18 leaf functional traits in each plot using the following equation:(7)CWM=∑i=1SWi×Xiwhere S is the number of species in each plot, Wi is the relative abundance of species i, and Xi is the corresponding trait value of species i. All CWM calculations were performed using the dbFD function in the R package FD. The resulting CWMs for all 18 traits across the three rocky desertification stages (LRD, MRD, and SRD) are presented in Table S1.

Soil physicochemical properties analyais

Variation in soil physicochemical properties along the rocky desertification gradient was first evaluated by calculating variance inflation factors (VIFs) using the vif function in the R package car to assess multicollinearity among environmental variables. All VIF values were below 5 (Table S5), indicating that multicollinearity was not severe and that all selected variables were suitable for subsequent analyses. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was then performed using the princomp function in R to characterize overall variation in soil physicochemical properties among rocky desertification stages. Differences in soil properties among desertification levels were further assessed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by Tukey's honestly significant difference (HSD) post hoc tests at the p < 0.05 significance level.

Relationships between network topology and environmental variables

Relationships between LTN topology and soil physicochemical properties were examined using redundancy analysis (RDA) implemented with the rda function in the R package vegan (Legendre and Legendre, 2012). Two matrices were constructed for the analysis: (1) a response matrix consisting of network-level topological parameters derived from phylogenetically corrected LTNs, including edge density, modularity, average path length, and diameter; and (2) an explanatory matrix containing soil physicochemical properties, including soil BD, SOC, TN, TP, TK, Ca, and soil pH. All statistical analyses were performed in R v.4.5.2, and statistical significance was determined at p < 0.05 unless otherwise specified.

Redundancy analysis (RDA) revealed that the relationships between LTN topology and soil physicochemical properties differed among the three rocky desertification stages (Fig. 5). Discussion

Impacts of rocky desertification on soil physicochemical properties

Our results supported H1, demonstrating that soil physicochemical properties changed systematically along the rocky desertification gradient. The PCA results further demonstrated clear separation among the three rocky desertification stages, indicating progressive changes both soil nutrient availability and edaphic conditions with increasing desertification intensity (Fig. 2). SRD habitats were characterized by higher soil pH, Ca, TN, and TK, but lower BD, SOC, and TP than LRD habitats (Table 2). These patterns are consistent with the geochemical characteristics of karst ecosystems, where prolonged carbonate weathering progressively exposes calcium-rich parent material, thereby increasing soil Ca availability and alkalinity (Li et al., 2022). At the same time, intensified rock exposure and shallow fragmented soils accelerate runoff and erosion, reducing organic matter accumulation and phosphorus retention (Wang et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2023). Consequently, the progressive decline in SOC along the rocky desertification gradient most likely reflects the combined effects of reduced litter inputs and enhanced nutrient loss associated with vegetation degradation.

Despite severe degradation, TN remained relatively high in SRD habitats. This pattern may reflect altered nitrogen cycling processes under high-calcium rich karst soils. Previous studies have shown that elevated Ca availability can influence nitrogen mineralization and nitrification dynamics in karst soils (Yang et al., 2023). In addition, severe P limitation may reduce plant nitrogen utilization efficiency, thereby contributing to nitrogen accumulation within the soil system (Bai et al., 2013). Meanwhile, the relatively low BD observed in SRD habitats probably reflects the high proportion of exposed bedrock and gravel-rich substrates, which produce shallow discontinuous soil layers with weak structural stability (Peng et al., 2020).

Overall, these findings demonstrate the progressive rocky desertification reshapes both the physical and chemical properties of karst soils. Such stage-dependent changes in soil conditions provide the environmental context for the reorganization of LTN topology and the accompanying shift in plant resource-use strategies observed across the rocky ecological basis for the subsequent analyses of network reorganization.

Effects of rocky desertification intensity on leaf trait networks of the plant community

Our results supported H2, demonstrating that LTN topology was progressively recognized along the rocky desertification gradient, accompanied by shift in hub traits from resource-acquisition-related traits toward stress-tolerance-related traits. Increasing rocky desertification intensity was accompanied by pronounced changes in LTN architecture. Specifically, edge density declined overall, whereas both average path length and network diameter increased along the rocky desertification gradient (Table 3). These patterns suggest that increasing environmental stress progressively weakened whole-network trait integration and reduced the efficiency of functional coordination among leaf traits. In relatively less degraded habitats, high trait connectivity indicates that structural, and resource-allocation traits were tightly coordinated, thereby supporting an integrated resource-acquisitive strategy. Under such conditions, photosynthetic traits, water-regulation traits, and structural traits were strongly coupled, allowing plants to optimize resource acquisition and carbon assimilation under comparatively favorable environmental conditions. Network modularity exhibited a non-linear response to rocky desertification, increasing from LRD to MRD habitats before declining slightly in SRD habitats. In ecological networks, higher modularity generally indicates stronger compartmentalization of functional trait groups, characterized by tighter within-module interactions and weaker among-module connections (Zhu et al., 2023). In our study, we found modularity to peak at intermediate disturbance within the moderate rocky desertification level, consistent with previous reports (Li et al., 2024a; Yuan et al., 2021). However, modularity declined slightly in SRD habitats, indicating that this adaptive modular organization was not maintained under extreme rocky desertification. Under severe environmental filtering, the overall integrity of the trait coordination system may become increasingly constrained, such that neither highly integrated networks nor strongly differentiated modular structures can be effectively sustained (He et al., 2020; Yao et al., 2024). Simultaneously, the increases in average path length and network diameter, together with relatively low edge density, suggest that trait interactions became progressively weaker and more functionally isolated under severe stress conditions. Therefore, the observed changes in network topology across the rocky desertification gradient likely reflect a transition from highly integrated acquisitive coordination in LRD habitats, to adaptive modular reorganization in MRD habitats, and finally to structurally constrained conservative coordination under severe rocky desertification. This pattern provides strong support for H2 and highlights the dynamic reorganization of trait coordination as a key adaptive strategy of karst woody communities under increasing environmental stress.

Hub traits transformation and strategy shifts in leaf trait networks along the rocky desertification gradient

In the LRD habitats, hub traits were primarily associated with water regulation, leaf structural organization, and nitrogen metabolism. Specifically, LWC, LT, and LN occupied the most central positions within the LTN, indicating that these traits coordinated whole-network function under relatively favorable environmental conditions (Fig. 4A–D, G), Consistent with the leaf economics spectrum, relatively high CWM LN together with comparatively low CWM LT and CWM LWC suggests an acquisitive coordination strategy characterized by greater investment in photosynthetic metabolism than structural reinforcement (Table S1; Wright et al., 2004; Reich, 2014). Although soil water availability was relatively high, comparatively low community-level LWC indicates that plants were unlikely to rely on constitutive water storage. Instead, efficient water regulation was probably achieved through coordinated adjustment among leaf structure, transpiration, and photosynthetic metabolism (Prado and Maurel, 2013; Lawson and Vialet-Chabrand, 2019; Ratzmann et al., 2019). This interpretation is further supported by the highly integrated network topology observed in LRD, which exhibited the greatest edge density and the lowest path length, diameter, and modularity (Table 3). Together, these findings suggest that weak environmental filtering favored tightly coordinated acquisitive resource-use strategies.

A contrasting pattern was observed in the LRD stage. Although soil water availability was relatively high, the community-weighted mean LWC remained lower than that observed in the more desertified stages. This apparently paradoxical pattern may reflect coordinated regulation of plant water use rather than a simple linear relationship between soil moisture availability and leaf hydration status (Prado and Maurel, 2013; Ratzmann et al., 2019). Leaf water content influenced not only by external water supply, but also by the balance among leaf structural investment, transpiration regulation, and carbon assimilation dynamics (Lawson and Vialet-Chabrand, 2019). Under comparatively favorable soil moisture conditions, plants may experience weaker selective pressure to maintain constitutively high leaf water storage. Consequently, the relatively low community-weighted mean LWC in LRD habitats may reflect greater investment in metabolically active tissues and sustained gas exchange, rather than in water-retention capacity. This interpretation is further supported by the relatively high LN concentration and comparatively low LT observed in LRD communities, both of which are commonly associated with acquisitive resource-use strategies (Wright et al., 2004; Reich, 2014). Therefore, the central positions of LWC, LT, and LN within the LRD network likely reflect coordinated regulation of water balance, leaf structural organization, and photosynthetic metabolism under relatively low environmental stress, thereby supporting the highly integrated LTN topology observed in LRD habitats.

In MRD habitats, SLP showed both the highest degree and clustering coefficient whereas Chl exhibited the highest closeness (Fig. 4B–E, H), indicating that the functional organization of the LTNs shifted from the structure–nitrogen coordination observed in LRD toward a coordination strategy centered on phosphorus-allocation and photosynthetic–maintenance axis. Compared with LRD, MRD communities exhibited lower CWM values of LA, SLN, SLP, LP, and LN, together with higher CWM values of LT, LWC, LMA, C/N, and C/P, although most of these traits had not yet reached the strongly conservative values observed in SRD. This intermediate trait syndrome suggests that MRD represents a transitional stage in which the resources-acquisitive coordination pattern observed in LRD began to weaken, whereas a fully conservative structural strategy had not yet become dominant (Reich, 2014; Pan et al., 2020). Rather than representing a simple binary shift between acquisitive and conservative strategies, the MRD stage more likely reflects a process of adaptive resource reallocation under increasing environmental constraint. These trait-level adjustment were accompanied by corresponding changes in LTN topology. Compared with LRD, the MRD network became more modular and less integrated than that of LRD, indicating trait coordination may increasingly relied on selective coupling among specific functional dimensions rather than on highly integrated whole-network coordination.

Consistent with these topological changes, MRD habitats exhibited the lowest soil TN values, while CWM leaf N/P ratios remained below 14, indicating relatively strong nitrogen limitation but comparatively moderate P availability (Tables 2 and S1). Under such conditions, nitrogen limitation may restrict further enhancement of photosynthetic capacity through increased nitrogen investment (Luo et al., 2021). In contrast, P allocation may retain greater flexibility for coordinating multiple physiological processes under moderate environmental stress. P plays central roles in ATP synthesis, membrane integrity, and cellular metabolic regulation (Mo et al., 2018; Dynarski et al., 2023). Variation in SLP may therefore influence several interconnected functional dimensions, including energy metabolism, nitrogen-use efficiency, and stress-response regulation. The emergence of SLP as a hub trait in MRD most likely reflects its integrative role in mediating cross-functional coordination under transitional environmental conditions rather than identifying P as the sole limiting factor (Dynarski et al., 2023). Together, these findings suggests that plants in MRD habitats increasingly optimized internal nutrient allocation to maintain functional stability during the transitional reorganization of LTNs under progressively stronger environmental filtering.

In MRD, Chl exhibited the highest closeness (Fig. 4E), indicating that it occupied a central position linking multiple functional dimensions within the network. High closeness suggests that Chl may facilitate efficient coordination among traits associated with leaf economics, structural adjustment, and nutrient allocation. This pattern likely reflects the transitional ecological status of MRD habitats, where plants experience increasing environmental stress but have not fully shifted toward the strongly conservative strategies observed in SRD. The transitional coordination pattern also occurred under the characteristic soil conditions of MRD, including relatively low TN and SOC together with elevated Ca and pH, while TP and soil moisture remained at intermediate levels. Under these conditions, plants may face constraints on nitrogen-dependent photosynthetic investment while still retaining some flexibility in P allocation and structural adjustment. The observed associations between Chl and traits such as SLA, LMA, LT, SLC and SLP therefore suggest coordinated regulation among photochemical maintenance, leaf structural design, and nutrient-use strategies (Fig. 3B). Rather than representing a purely acquisitive strategy, the centrality of Chl in MRD more likely reflects an intermediate coordination mechanism that helps maintain functional stability under moderate rocky desertification stress (Reich, 2014; Ratzmann et al., 2019; He et al., 2020).

In SRD, the network center shifted again, with LT emerging as the dominant hub for both degree and clustering coefficient and LTD becoming the most central trait in terms of closeness. Structural leaf traits rather than nutrient-related traits became the principal axis of coordination under severe rocky desertification. Both thicker leaves and denser tissues are commonly associated with enhanced tissue robustness, prolonged leaf lifespan, and more conservative resource-use strategies (Wright et al., 2004; Xing et al., 2021). In severely desertified karst habitats, where soils are shallow, nutrient-poor, and highly heterogeneous, such traits likely enhance tolerance to dehydration, mechanical stress, and persistent nutrient limitation (Liu et al., 2021, 2024). Consistent with these trait-level shifts, SRD also exhibited greater network diameter and average path length together with reduced edge density compared with LRD, suggesting weaker overall trait coupling and greater functional independence among traits under intensified environmental filtering. Under such conditions, plants may no longer maintain highly integrated acquisitive coordination, but instead increasingly converge on structurally reinforced and conservative leaf construction strategies that prioritize long-term persistence over rapid resource acquisition. Collectively, the prominence of LT and LTD suggests that the SRD trait network was progressively reorganized around structural persistence and stress tolerance rather than metabolic flexibility and rapid growth.

Taken together, the replacement of hub traits across rocky desertification stages reveals a directional shift in community-level coordination strategies. LRD communities were primarily characterized by coordinated regulation of water balance, leaf structure, and nitrogen metabolism; MRD communities shifted toward P-associated modular coordination and photosynthetic maintenance; whereas SRD communities became increasingly dominated by structural reinforcement and conservative tissue construction. This sequential transition reflects the progressively stronger environmental filtering along the rocky desertification gradient and demonstrates that the reorganization of LTNs was accompanied by systematic replacement of hub traits, providing further support for H2 (He et al., 2020; Yao et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025).

Interaction mechanisms between leaf trait networks and soil properties along the rocky desertification gradient

Our RDA results fully supported H3, revealing that the relative importance of soil physicochemical drivers on LTN topology shifted systematically along the rocky desertification gradient, with each stage exhibiting a distinct driver-topology association pattern consistent with our predictions. Progressive changes in soil physicochemical conditions were therefore accompanied by corresponding reorganization of trait coordination patterns across the three rocky desertification stages. At the LRD stage, SOC and TN were positively correlated with edge density, TP and TK were positively correlated with modularity, whereas BD was negatively correlated with average path length and diameter. These relationships suggest that relatively high nutrient availability and favorable soil physical conditions alleviate resource constraints among traits, promoting stronger coordination among photosynthetic, hydraulic, and structural traits (Armbruster et al., 2014). The LRD soils in this study were characterized by the highest SOC, SWC, and TP together with relatively moderate TN and comparatively low Ca and TK levels (Table 1, Table 2), conditions that jointly mitigated multiple environmental stresses commonly observed in karst ecosystems. In particular, higher P availability likely alleviates one of the most important nutrient constraints in karst habitats, reducing functional trade-off between P acquisition and other physiological processes such as carbon fixation and structural investment. Simultaneously, favorable water conditions may have weakened the selective pressure for strict coordination among drought-resistance traits, thereby allowing greater flexibility in trait combinations. Although TK content was the lowest in the LRD stage, it does not appear to function as a dominant limiting factor, potentially because favorable soil moisture and relatively high SOC improve nutrient buffering capacity and reduce plant dependence on potassium-mediated osmotic adjustment (Nieves-Cordones et al., 2019). Moreover, relatively low Ca concentrations may have reduced the antagonistic effects of high-calcium environments on nutrient uptake. Consequently, under conditions where multiple environmental stresses are simultaneously alleviated, plants may have maintained globally coordinated trait relationships centered on relatively acquisitive resource-use strategies, resulting in the lowest modularity and strongest overall network integration observed in the LRD stage. In addition, the negative relationship between BD and network path metrics suggests that relatively loose soil structure may facilitate efficient resource transport, aeration, and root activity, thereby supporting tighter functional coordination among traits. This interpretation is consistent with previous studies showing that lower BD can improve soil pore structure, water retention, and SOC accumulation, ultimately promoting synergistic trait coordination and more compact ecological networks (Pan et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2023, 2025).

Compared with the LRD stage, the MRD stage exhibited decreased SOC, the lowest TN, and increased Ca, pH, TK, and BD (Table 2), indicating a shift from a relatively favorable soil conditions toward a more complex stress environment characterized by nutrient limitation and physical constraint. RDA results showed that pH and TN were positively correlated with average path length and diameter, whereas TK and BD were negatively correlated with edge density but positively correlated with modularity. Together, these associations suggest that the environmental drivers regulating LTN topology shifted from conditions favoring whole-network coordination to those promoting greater functional differentiation among trait groups. Previous studies have demonstrated that under nitrogen-limited conditions, plants often face stronger trade-offs among photosynthetic metabolism, structural construction, and water regulation, which may weaken whole-network connectivity and reduce overall trait integration (Onoda et al., 2017). Meanwhile, elevated soil pH in karst habitats is frequently associated with carbonate and calcium accumulation, both of which can influence nitrogen cycling through processes and potentially constrain nitrogen and P availability through altered microbial activity and nutrient fixation (Chen et al., 2018; Dai et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023). Consequently, the combined effects of nitrogen limitation and increasing alkalinity may contribute to weaker overall trait connectivity and longer coordination pathways, as reflected by the increased average path length and diameter observed in MRD. At the same time, increased BD may restrict root penetration, soil aeration, and nutrient mobility, thereby intensifying environmental heterogeneity within the plant community. Although TK content was not the lowest in the MRD, elevated BD and declining SOC may reduce effective potassium availability by limiting nutrient mobility and enhancing fixation processes (Ahmad et al., 2009; Dou et al., 2024). Under such conditions, plants may no longer maintain highly integrated whole-network coordination, but instead increasingly rely on modular coordination among specific functional trait groups. This shift toward greater modularity likely reflects adaptive functional differentiation under moderate rocky desertification stress, where plants balance nutrient acquisition, structural maintenance, and stress tolerance through more selective trait coupling rather than through globally coordinated resource-acquisitive strategy.

In the SRD stage, edge density was negatively correlated with soil Ca, while average path length and diameter were positively correlated with pH, indicating that calcium-rich and highly alkaline soils were associated with weaker network connectivity and reduced overall trait integration. This stage was characterized by the highest Ca and pH levels together with the lowest SOC, TP, and SWC (Table 1, Table 2), reflecting extremely stressful edaphic conditions. Although TN and TK contents remained relatively high, severe drought stress and excessive calcium accumulation may substantially constrain the effective utilization of these nutrients by plants (Zheng et al., 2023; Jiang et al., 2020). High Ca and elevated pH are widely known to reduce P availability in karst ecosystems, thereby intensifying P limitation and potentially suppressing vegetation recovery (Liu et al., 2021). In this study, SRD soils exhibited the lowest TP levels, suggesting that P deficiency may represent one of the key environmental constraints during the late stage of rocky desertification, reduced P availability may further influence carbon and nitrogen cycling processes, potentially limiting nutrient turnover and plant growth (Yang et al., 2023; Kajino et al., 2023). Under such intensified environmental filtering, the highly coordinated trait relationships observed in the less degraded habitats became progressively weakened, as reflected by the synchronous decrease in edge density and increase in average path length and diameter. Longer coordination pathways within the network suggest that trait interactions became increasingly indirect and functionally decoupled under severe environmental stress (Li et al., 2024b). Consequently, LTN topology in SRD habitats became increasingly dispersed and structurally fragmented, indicating that plants relied more heavily on relatively independent and conservative trait adjustments than on tightly integrated whole-specific driver–topology relationships demonstrate that the dominant soil factors regulating LTN organization changed systematically along the rocky desertification gradient, thereby providing further support for H3.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that karst rocky desertification drives stage-dependent reorganization of leaf trait networks (LTNs), resulting in coordinated shifts in network topology, hub trait identity, and their underlying environmental drivers. As rocky desertification intensified, soils became increasingly alkaline and calcium-rich, where SOC, TP, and effective soil buffering capacity declined progressively. Simultaneously, LTNs shifted from highly integrated resource-acquisitive coordination toward increasingly modular and structurally conservative coordination. Hub traits shifted sequentially from traits associated with water regulation and N metabolism in lightly rocky desertified (LRD) habitats, to P allocation and photosynthetic maintenance in moderately rocky desertified (MRD) habitats, and finally toward structural reinforcement and conservative tissue construction in severely rocky desertified (SRD) habitats. These shifts suggest that progressive environmental filtering reorganized plant functional coordination from metabolically flexible resource acquisition toward hydraulic safety, structural persistence, and conservative resource use under severe karst degradation. The relationships between LTNs and soil environmental factors also changed systematically along the rocky desertification gradient. In LRD habitats, network integration was primarily associated with SOC, TN, and soil physical permeability. In MRD habitats, increasing soil compaction, elevated pH, and altered nutrient balance promoted modular network reorganization. In SRD habitats, strong geochemical and hydrological filtering associated with high Ca, high pH, and shallow fragmented soil constrained trait coordination and favored structurally conservative strategies. These findings demonstrate that plant functional adaptation to karst rocky desertification is not characterized by a simple linear decline in network organization but by stage-dependent reorganization driven by changing environmental constraints. Overall, this study provides a trait-network-based conceptual framework for understanding how soil environmental change regulates multidimensional plant functional coordination during karst rocky desertification. These findings not only advance current understanding of plant adaptive strategies in karst ecosystems but also provide a theoretical basis for ecological restoration practices that simultaneously consider soil environmental regulation and multidimensional trait coordination.
