ably on no more than a 50-50 basis; that they believe that a suit-

3. Mr, Roger Jones, Chairman, Civil Service Commission,
presented a new attachment to the report on S. 94 which was only
delivered to the Committee yesterday. It outlines 18 principles
which should be taken into consideration in drafting a suitable bill.
These are diecuesed in brief. The attachment then comments on
S. 94 and then draws a comparison between S. 94 and the principles

more desirable. The Commieeion’e plan is very liberal and al-
though having a email deductible which applies per person per
year it covers all doctor's fees whether in or out of a hospital

4. Mr. Jones then gave the essentials of the FBI's plan
which is underwritten by Prudential, stating that the family rate
for agents was $13. 50, then outlined the Federal Deposit Insurance
Corporation's plan which costs $11. 48 for the family plan. The
employee pays the difference between the single plan and the
family plan. He also gave the essentials of the TVA plan and the
CIA plan. From hie rundown of the benefits it is my opinion that
we still have an excellent semi-major medical plan at the cheap-
est cost to the employee. Also, our Board of Directors is con-
sidering further liberalization of our present plan.

5. On what amounts should be paid by the Government and
what amounts should be paid by the employee it was noted that
General Electric, the Steel Industry, the Auto Industry, and
Dupont all have 50% participation,

6. Mr. Jones estimated that 4 1/2 million employees and
dependents would be covered and the estimated cost for the Civil
Service plan as they conceive it would be $5. 07 single, $15. 17
family.
