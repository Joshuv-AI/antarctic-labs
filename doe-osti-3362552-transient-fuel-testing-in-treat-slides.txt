Terminology

• Transient, accident, or upset condition refers to any plant condition that is outside window of
  normal and/or planned operations.

• Fuel Failures generally refers to a non catastrophic breach in the claddings hermiticity. Allows
  fission gases to be released into the reactor vessel

• Coolable Geometry generally refers to the maintenance of fuel geometry (e.g. rod-like, plate-
  like, etc.) with open flow channels.

• Safety Margin the difference between the fuel’s thermal-mechanical state in a transient or
  upset condition and the state leading to either fuel failure or loss of coolable geometry.

 TREAT Introduction
• The Transient Reactor Test (TREAT) facility was in operation from 1959 until 1994 and resumed in
  2017 to facilitate fuel safety testing and various transient science activities.
• TREAT utilizes Zircaloy-clad graphite/fuel blocks as its core, which is cooled by air blowers.
 • It operates at a steady state of 120 kW, with a peak of approximately 20 GW in pulse mode.
 • It can accommodate virtually any power history, with a maximum core transient energy of
 2500 MJ.
 • The absence of a reactor pressure vessel or containment allows for easier access to in-core
 instrumentation.
• Design of the Experiment
 • The reactor delivers short, often intense neutron flux histories, reaching
 up to 10^17 n·cm-2·s-1, to evaluate test specimens.
 • The experimental apparatus handles all other functions
 • Safety containment, specimen conditions, and instrumentation

 • The core center features 4 slots for viewing
 • 2 are currently utilized for fast neutron hodoscope and
 neutron radiography
 • Located at INL alongside other complementary facilities
 • ATR and HFEF
 • Fuel fabrication and characterization

 Example Test Program for Reactivity-initiated Accident
• Cr coated Zr claddings are being investigated due to their favorable resistance to cladding oxidation due to formation of stable Cr2O3 on
  outside surface. However, eutectic reactions can cause melting. Desirable to understand cladding behavior in a water environment, under RIA
  conditions with nuclear heating.
• Historically performed RIA testing from the 1950’s through 1980’s included:
 • Boiling Water Reactor Experiment (BORAX), Special Power Excursion Reactor Test Program (SPERT),
 Power Burst Facility (PBF) reactors
 • ~100 tests to simulate RIA conditions in the Transient Reactor Test Facility (TREAT) in water filled
 experiment devices were performed
 • Subsequently establish empirical fuel safety criteria for UO2/Zry based Radial Average Fuel Enthalpy.
 • Another criteria exists for cladding ductility based on equivalent cladding reacted criteria
