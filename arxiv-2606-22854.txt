Comparison Results for a class of Neumann Problems of the p p -Laplace Equation on Riemannian Manifolds

Comparison Results for a class of Neumann Problems of the
p
-Laplace Equation on Riemannian Manifolds

Wentao Liu and Anqiang Zhu

Abstract
:We consider Neumann boundary value problems for the
p
-Laplace equation on Riemannian manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature.
Using spherical symmetrization under appropriate constraints, we derive Talenti-type comparison results in Lorentz spaces.
We further show that, in contrast to the Robin case, the Neumann setting admits weaker constraints, which yields stronger comparison principles.

p
-Laplace equation

1
Introduction

For Neumann boundary value problems,
solutions are determined only up to additive constants.
Consequently, any meaningful comparison principle requires an additional normalization condition.
Unlike the Dirichlet case, where the boundary condition naturally fixes the solution,
the Neumann setting presents an intrinsic ambiguity, making comparison results substantially more delicate.

The origin of this circle of ideas can be traced back to the celebrated work of Talenti
[
24
]
,
who established a sharp comparison principle for elliptic equations with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions by means of Schwarz symmetrization.
More precisely, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary and consider the Poisson problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=f,&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0,&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.1)

Together with its symmetrized counterpart

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=f^{\sharp},&\text{in }\Omega^{\sharp},\\
v=0,&\text{on }\partial\Omega^{\sharp},\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
\Omega^{\sharp}
is a Euclidean ball satisfying

|\Omega^{\sharp}|=|\Omega|
and
f^{\sharp}
denotes the Schwarz symmetrization of
f
.

Talenti proved that the rearrangements of
u
and
v
satisfy a pointwise comparison, yielding a powerful method for deriving sharp functional inequalities.
Since then, symmetrization techniques have become a fundamental tool in the study of elliptic partial differential equations,
with applications ranging from Sobolev inequalities to Faber–Krahn type estimates.
The situation is considerably better for Dirichlet boundary conditions.
Starting from the seminal work of Talenti, symmetrization techniques
have led to a rich theory of comparison principles, often yielding
pointwise estimates between a solution and its symmetric counterpart.
We refer to the surveys

[
16
,
17
]

for a detailed overview of the subject.

The influence of these ideas extends far beyond the Euclidean setting.
Talenti-type comparison results have recently been established on
Riemannian manifolds with positive Ricci curvature by Colladay et al.

[
11
]
, and to nonnegative Ricci curvature by Chen et al.
[
9
]
.
Later it is generalized to

\mathrm{RCD}(K,N)
spaces by Mondino et al.

[
22
]
.

The comparison theorem for Robin boundary value problems was first considered by Alvino et al.
[
2
]
.
These results were generalized to Riemannian manifolds by Chen et al.
[
8
]
.
And also were then extended to the

p

-Laplacian by Amato et al.
[
3
]
.
Recently, Chen et al.
[
10
]
generalized the corresponding conclusions to the anisotropic

p

-Laplacian and proved rigidity properties for Talenti comparison inequalities.

Regarding Neumann boundary conditions, Maderna et al.
[
21
]
derived pointwise comparison results for weak solutions
under homogeneous Neumann conditions.
Ferone et al.
[
12
]
extended Talenti comparisons to cylindrical domains.
Volzone
[
25
]
studied fractional Neumann problems and obtained similar results.
For homogeneous Neumann problems on spherical shells,
Langford
[
20
]
established norm and oscillation comparisons under appropriate assumptions;
see also
[
19
]
.
More recently, Celentano et al.
[
7
]

considered nonhomogeneous Neumann problems and
obtained Lorentz norm comparison results under certain normalization assumptions.

Motivated by the above developments, we investigate
nonhomogeneous Neumann problems on Riemannian manifolds
with Ricci curvature bounded below.

Our framework includes both complete noncompact manifolds
with nonnegative Ricci curvature and positive asymptotic
volume ratio, and compact manifolds satisfying

\mathrm{Ric}\geq(n-1).

The corresponding comparison spaces are Euclidean balls
and spherical caps, respectively.
Motivated by the above developments,
we investigate nonhomogeneous Neumann problems on Riemannian manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete noncompact Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature,
and let
\Omega\subset M
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary.
We consider the boundary value problem

\begin{cases}-\operatorname{div}\bigl(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\bigr)=f,&\text{in }\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
|\nabla u|^{p-2}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta^{p-1}=0,&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.3)

Following Talenti’s philosophy,
we compare solutions of (
1.3
) with those of a suitable symmetrized problem posed on a model space.
Let
\Omega^{\sharp}
denote the symmetrized domain associated with
\Omega
(see
2.3
),
and let
f^{\sharp}
be the symmetrization of
f
. We consider the problem

\begin{cases}-\operatorname{div}\bigl(|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\bigr)=f^{\sharp},&\text{in }\Omega^{\sharp},\\[4.30554pt]
|\nabla v|^{p-2}\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}+(\beta^{\sharp})^{p-1}=0,&\text{on }\partial\Omega^{\sharp}.\end{cases}

(1.4)

Remark 1.1
.

Unless otherwise specified,
the symbol
|\cdot|
always denotes the corresponding Riemannian measure.
Likewise, all differential operators, gradients, volume forms,
and boundary measures are understood with respect to the ambient manifold under consideration.

To guarantee the solvability of the Neumann problems, the compatibility condition must be satisfied. Integrating the equations and applying the divergence theorem, we obtain

\beta^{p-1}=\frac{1}{|\partial\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}f\,dV,\qquad(\beta^{\sharp})^{p-1}=\frac{1}{|\partial\Omega^{\sharp}|}\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}f^{\sharp}\,dV.

(1.5)

We further assume that

f\geq 0,\int_{\Omega}f\,dV>0.

Under this assumption, we have

\beta^{p-1},\,(\beta^{\sharp})^{p-1}>0,

and therefore both
\beta
and
\beta^{\sharp}
can be taken to be positive.

We know that if
u
is a solution, then
u+C
is also a solution for any constant
C
.
Therefore, one may always normalize the solution by adding a suitable constant, and in particular,
we may assume that the solution is positive.
However, this normalization alone is not sufficient to establish a meaningful comparison principle between solutions on
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\sharp}
.
Indeed, due to the non-uniqueness up to constants, an additional normalization condition is required to ensure a one-to-one correspondence between the two solutions.
In the present paper, we impose the following compatibility (normalization) condition:

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{\sharp}}{\beta}\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{q}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\theta_{\kappa}\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}v^{q}\,d\sigma.

(1.6)

with
\theta_{\kappa}
given by (
2.2
).

To formulate the normalization conditions, we introduce the boundary
L^{q}
-moments

\alpha_{q}=\frac{1}{q}\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{q}\,d\sigma,\qquad\alpha_{q}^{\sharp}=\frac{1}{q}\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}v^{q}\,d\sigma.

In terms of these quantities, the above relations can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{\sharp}}{\beta}\,\alpha_{q}

\displaystyle=\theta_{\kappa}\,\alpha_{q}^{\sharp}.

(1.7)

We emphasize that the parameter
q
is completely independent of
p
,
and the normalization condition can be imposed for every
q\geq 1
.

One of the observations of the present paper is that the comparison argument is not restricted to particular choices of
q
.
This additional flexibility appears to be a characteristic feature of Neumann boundary value problems,
where the invariance of solutions under additive constants allows a broader class of admissible normalizations.

Such a relaxation is no longer available in the Robin case,
where the boundary condition imposes a much stronger constraint on the admissible normalizations; see
[
3
]
.

We are now ready to state our main result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
q\geq 1
, and let
u
be a positive weak solution to problem (
1.3
).

Suppose that
v
is the positive solution to problem (
1.4
)
satisfying condition (
1.7
). Then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\lambda,1}(\Omega)}\leq\theta_{\kappa}^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}\|v\|_{L^{\lambda,1}(\Omega^{\sharp})},\qquad\kappa=0,1,\quad 0<\lambda\leq\frac{n(p-1)}{p(n-1)},

(1.8)

and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q\lambda,q}(\Omega)}\leq\theta_{\kappa}^{\frac{1}{q\lambda}}\|v\|_{L^{q\lambda,q}(\Omega^{\sharp})},\qquad\kappa=0,\quad 0<\lambda\leq\frac{n(p-1)}{p(n-2)+n},

(1.9)

while

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q\lambda,q}(\Omega)}\leq\theta_{\kappa}^{\frac{1}{q\lambda}}\|v\|_{L^{q\lambda,q}(\Omega^{\sharp})},\qquad\kappa=1,\quad 0<\lambda\leq\frac{n(p-1)}{(p+1)(n-1)}.

(1.10)

In particular, if
n=2
,
\kappa=1
, and
0<\lambda\leq p-1
, then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q\lambda,q}(\Omega)}\leq\theta_{\kappa}^{\frac{1}{q\lambda}}\|v\|_{L^{q\lambda,q}(\Omega^{\sharp})}.

(1.11)

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
f\equiv 1
in
\Omega
.
Let
q\geq 1
, and let
u
be a positive weak solution to problem (
1.3
).

Suppose that
v
is the positive solution to problem (
1.4
)
satisfying condition (
1.7
). If

1\leq p\leq\frac{n}{n-1},

then

\displaystyle u^{\sharp}(x)\leq\theta_{\kappa}v(x),\qquad\forall\,x\in\Omega^{\sharp}.

(1.12)

If

p>\frac{n}{n-1},

then the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\lambda,1}(\Omega)}\leq\theta_{\kappa}^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}\|v\|_{L^{\lambda,1}(\Omega^{\sharp})},\qquad\kappa=0,1,\quad 0<\lambda\leq\frac{n(p-1)}{n(p-1)-p}.

(1.13)

Moreover, when
\kappa=0
, one has

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q\lambda,q}(\Omega)}\leq\theta_{\kappa}^{\frac{1}{q\lambda}}\|v\|_{L^{q\lambda,q}(\Omega^{\sharp})},\qquad 0<\lambda\leq\frac{n(p-1)}{n(p-1)-p}.

(1.14)

Remark 1.4
.

In order to make the above estimate precise,
we recall the definition of Lorentz spaces
L^{\lambda,q}(\Omega)
,
where
0<\lambda<\infty
and
0<q\leq\infty
.

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{\lambda,q}(\Omega)}=\begin{cases}\lambda^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(t\,|\{x\in\Omega:|g(x)|>t\}|^{1/\lambda}\big)^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/q},&0<q<\infty,\\[10.00002pt]
\sup_{t>0}t\,|\{x\in\Omega:|g(x)|>t\}|^{\frac{1}{\lambda}},&q=\infty.\end{cases}

For further discussions on related topics, we refer the reader to

[
15
,
5
]
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Manifolds and Isoperimetric Inequalities

Let
M
be a complete noncompact Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature. Assume that
M
has positive asymptotic volume ratio, namely,

\theta=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{|B(r)|}{|B^{n}|\,r^{n}}>0,

(2.1)

where
B(r)
denotes the geodesic ball of radius
r
in
M
, and
|B^{n}|
is the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In
[
4
]
, Brendle proved that for every bounded domain
\Omega\subset M
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
, the following isoperimetric inequality holds:
Let
\Omega^{\sharp}
denote the Euclidean ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

\theta|\Omega^{\sharp}|=|\Omega|.

Then

|\partial\Omega^{\sharp}|=n|B^{n}|^{\frac{1}{n}}|\Omega^{\sharp}|^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

and consequently

|\partial\Omega|\geq\theta|\partial\Omega^{\sharp}|.

For further developments on isoperimetric inequalities, we refer the reader to

[
1
,
6
,
18
]

and the references therein.
Now let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold satisfying

\operatorname{Ric}_{M}\geq(n-1)k,\qquad k>0.

Denote by
S_{k}^{n}
the standard sphere of radius
1/\sqrt{k}
, and let
\Omega^{\sharp}
be the geodesic ball in
S_{k}^{n}
such that

\frac{|M|}{|S_{k}^{n}|}|\Omega^{\sharp}|=|\Omega|.

By the Levy–Gromov isoperimetric inequality
[
13
]
, we have

|\partial\Omega|\geq\frac{|M|}{|S_{k}^{n}|}|\partial\Omega^{\sharp}|.

By a suitable scaling of the metric, it suffices to consider the case
k=1
. To unify the notation, we define

\displaystyle{}\theta_{\kappa}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{|B(r)|}{|B^{n}|r^{n}},&\kappa=0,\\[8.61108pt]
\displaystyle\frac{|M|}{|S^{n}|},&\kappa=1.\end{cases}

(2.2)

For convenience, we denote by
M_{\kappa}
the model space, namely,

M_{\kappa}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}^{n},&\kappa=0,\\
S^{n},&\kappa=1,\end{cases}

equipped with their standard metrics.

Throughout this paper,
M
denotes a Riemannian manifold satisfying

\operatorname{Ric}_{M}\geq(n-1)\kappa,\qquad\kappa\in\{0,1\}.

In the case
\kappa=0
, we additionally assume that
M
has positive asymptotic volume ratio.

Under these assumptions, for every measurable set

\Omega\subset M
, we denote by
\Omega^{\sharp}
the geodesic ball in the model space
M_{\kappa}
such that

|\Omega|=\theta_{\kappa}|\Omega^{\sharp}|.

(2.3)

With this normalization, the corresponding isoperimetric inequalities take the unified form

|\partial\Omega|\geq\theta_{\kappa}\,|\partial\Omega^{\sharp}|.

(2.4)

Moreover,
equality holds if and only if
M
is isometric to the model space
M_{\kappa}
and
\Omega
is a geodesic ball in
M_{\kappa}
.

2.2
Rearrangements

Talenti’s classical approach relies on spherical symmetrization, which is more commonly referred to as the rearrangement method in the modern literature.
In this section, we briefly recall the basic notation and several fundamental properties of rearrangements that will be used throughout the paper.
We note that these ideas also naturally arise in convex geometry. For further references, see
[
14
]
,
[
23
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
w:\Omega\to[0,\infty)
be a measurable function. For
t\geq 0
, define

\Omega_{w,t}=\{x\in\Omega:|w(x)|>t\},\qquad\mu_{w}(t)=|\Omega_{w,t}|.

The decreasing rearrangement
w^{*}
of
w
is defined by

\displaystyle w^{*}(s)=\begin{cases}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\in\Omega}w(x),&s=0,\\[3.00003pt]
\inf\{t\geq 0:\mu_{w}(t)<s\},&s>0,\end{cases}

(2.5)

for
s\in[0,|\Omega|]
.
The Schwarz decreasing rearrangement of
w
is the radially symmetric function
w^{\sharp}:\Omega^{\sharp}\to[0,\infty)
defined by

\displaystyle w^{\sharp}(x)=w^{*}\bigl(\theta_{\kappa}|B_{\kappa}(|x|)|\ \bigr),\qquad x\in\Omega^{\sharp},

(2.6)

where
B_{\kappa}(|x|)
denotes the geodesi ball in
{M}_{\kappa}
with radius
|x|
.

We emphasize that our definition of spherical rearrangement contains the normalization factor
\theta_{\kappa}
.
Consequently, compared with the classical Euclidean rearrangement,
the distribution function and several related identities acquire an additional factor
\theta_{\kappa}
.
We refer the reader to
[
9
]
for further discussion.
The distribution functions of
w
and its rearrangement
w^{\sharp}
satisfy

\mu_{w}(t)=\theta_{\kappa}\mu_{w^{\sharp}}(t).

(2.7)

As a consequence, the decreasing rearrangement
w^{*}
and the spherical rearrangement
w^{\sharp}
are related through the identity

\mu_{w}(t)=\mu_{w^{*}}(t)=\theta_{\kappa}\mu_{w^{\sharp}}(t).

The functions
w
,
w^{*}
and
w^{\sharp}
are equimeasurable in the sense that

\|w\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\|w^{*}\|_{L^{p}(0,|\Omega|)}=\theta_{\kappa}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|w^{\sharp}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\sharp})}.

(2.8)

Given measurable functions
u
,
v
on
\Omega
, the Hardy-Littlewood inequality holds,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|uv|\,dV\leq\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}u^{*}(s)v^{*}(s)\,ds.

(2.9)

Choosing
v=\chi_{\{|u|>t\}}
in (
2.9
), one has

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u|\,dV\leq\int_{0}^{\mu_{(}t)}u^{*}(s)\,ds

(2.10)

For
U\subset\Omega
, we define

\partial U^{i}:=\partial U\cap\Omega,\qquad\partial U^{e}:=\partial U\cap\partial\Omega.

Let
u
and
v
be solutions to problems (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), respectively. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

\displaystyle U_{t}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\Omega:|u(x)|>t\},\qquad\mu(t)=|U_{t}|,

\displaystyle V_{t}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\Omega^{\sharp}:|v(x)|>t\},\qquad\varphi(t)=|V_{t}|.

3
Proof of main result

For the sake of notation, we introduce several geometric quantities associated with the model space
M_{\kappa}
, involving only basic Riemannian geometric concepts.

We define the function
S_{\kappa}
by

S_{\kappa}(l)=\frac{dL_{\kappa}}{dr}\circ L_{\kappa}^{-1}(l),

where

L_{\kappa}(r)=n|B^{n}|\theta_{\kappa}\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{sn}_{\kappa}^{\,n-1}(s)\,ds,

and

\mathrm{sn}_{\kappa}(r)=\begin{cases}r,&\kappa=0,\\
\sin r,&\kappa=1.\end{cases}

The function
L_{\kappa}
represents the volume of a geodesic ball of radius
r
in the model space
M_{\kappa}
, while
S_{\kappa}
describes the corresponding surface area expressed as a function of the volume variable
l
. The factor
\theta_{\kappa}
is introduced for normalization and simplifies subsequent computations.

To establish our main results, the following lemma plays a crucial role.

Lemma 3.1

(Gronwall inequality)
.

Let
\zeta:[\tau_{0},\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuously differentiable function satisfying

\tau\,\zeta^{\prime}(\tau)\leq(q-1)\zeta(\tau)+C,\qquad\forall\,\tau\geq\tau_{0}>0,

for some constant
C\geq 0
.

Then, for every
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
,

\zeta(\tau)\leq\left(\zeta(\tau_{0})+\frac{C}{q-1}\right)\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)^{q-1}-\frac{C}{q-1}.

Consequently,

\zeta^{\prime}(\tau)\leq\frac{(q-1)\zeta(\tau_{0})+C}{\tau_{0}}\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)^{q-2}.

The following lemma is the main ingredient of this paper. Its idea originates from the work of Alvino et al.
[
2
]
,
and it also serves as a fundamental tool in many comparison results for Robin boundary value problems.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u
and
v
be positive solutions of the above problems. For
t>0
, we have the following result:

\displaystyle S_{\kappa}(\mu(t))^{\frac{p}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\mu(t)}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(-\mu^{\prime}(t)+\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{\partial U_{t}^{e}}d\sigma\right),

(3.1)

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{\kappa}(\varphi(t))^{\frac{p}{p-1}}

\displaystyle=\theta_{\kappa}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(\int_{0}^{\theta_{\kappa}\varphi(t)}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(-\varphi^{\prime}(t)+\frac{1}{\beta^{\sharp}}\int_{\partial V_{t}^{e}}d\sigma\right),

(3.2)

where
\tilde{S_{\kappa}}(l)=\theta_{\kappa}^{-1}S_{\kappa}(\theta_{\kappa}l)
.

Proof.

Let
t>0
and
c>0
. We introduce the standard truncation function associated with
u
at levels
t
and
t+c
,
defined by

\psi(x)=\begin{cases}0,&u(x)\leq t,\\[4.30554pt]
u(x)-t,&t<u(x)<t+c,\\[4.30554pt]
c,&u(x)\geq t+c.\end{cases}

This function is Lipschitz and belongs to
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
whenever
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
.
Choosing
\psi
as a test function in the weak formulation, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{U_{t}\setminus U_{t+c}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dV

\displaystyle+\beta^{p-1}c\int_{\partial U_{t+c}^{e}}d\sigma+\beta^{p-1}\int_{\partial U_{t}^{e}\setminus\partial U_{t+c}^{e}}(u-t)\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{U_{t}\setminus U_{t+c}}f(u-t)\,dV+c\int_{U_{t+c}}f\,dV.

Dividing by
c
and letting
c\to 0
, using the coarea formula

-\mu^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\partial U_{t}^{i}}\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\,d\sigma.

We obtain for a.e.
t>0
,

\int_{\partial U_{t}^{i}}|\nabla u|^{p-1}\,d\sigma+\beta\int_{\partial U_{t}^{e}}d\sigma=\int_{U_{t}}f\,dx.

Set

h(x)=\begin{cases}|\nabla u|^{p-1},&x\in\partial U_{t}^{i},\\
\beta^{p-1},&x\in\partial U_{t}^{e}.\end{cases}

Then Hölder’s inequality gives

\displaystyle|\partial U_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\partial U_{t}}h\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\partial U_{t}}\frac{1}{h^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\partial U_{t}}h\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\partial U_{t}^{i}}\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\,d\sigma+\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{\partial U_{t}^{e}}\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\mu(t)}f^{*}(s)ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(-\mu^{\prime}(t)+\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{\partial U_{t}^{e}}\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

Combining this with the isoperimetric inequality yields

S_{\kappa}(\mu(t))^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\leq|\partial U_{t}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{\mu(t)}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(-\mu^{\prime}(t)+\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{\partial U_{t}^{e}}d\sigma\right).

The argument for
v
is analogous. Indeed, since
v
is radial, nonincreasing and
\Omega^{\sharp}
is a geodesic ball,
all the above inequalities become equalities. In particular, we have

\tilde{S}_{\kappa}(\varphi(t))=|\partial V_{t}|,\qquad\int_{V_{t}}f^{\sharp}\,dV=\theta_{\kappa}^{-1}\int_{0}^{\theta_{\kappa}\varphi(t)}f^{*}(s)\,ds.

This completes the proof.

∎

We next observe an important property of the radial solution
v
and
u
. In particular,

v
and
u
both attains its minimum on the boundary, and moreover,
v

is constant on the boundary. Based on this observation,
we first establish a comparison result for minima under condition (
1.7
).
We define

u_{m}=\inf_{\Omega}u,\qquad v_{m}=\inf_{\Omega^{\sharp}}v.

We compute as follows:

\displaystyle\theta_{\kappa}v_{m}^{q}|\partial\Omega^{\sharp}|

\displaystyle=\theta_{\kappa}\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}v^{q}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{\sharp}}{\beta}\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{q}\,d\sigma\quad\text{ Condition \eqref{eq:1.7}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\beta^{\sharp}}{\beta}\,u_{m}^{q}|\partial\Omega|

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{|\partial\Omega|}{\theta_{\kappa}|\partial\Omega^{\sharp}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u_{m}^{q}|\partial\Omega|\quad\text{ Definition of }\beta\text{,}\beta^{\sharp}\text{ and equation}~\eqref{eq:2.8}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

consequently, we obtain

v_{m}^{q}\geq\left(\frac{|\partial\Omega|}{\theta_{\kappa}|\partial\Omega^{\sharp}|}\right)^{1+\frac{1}{p-1}}u_{m}^{q}.

Finally, by the isoperimetric inequality (
2.4
),

|\partial\Omega|\geq\theta_{\kappa}|\partial\Omega^{\sharp}|,

which yields

v_{m}\geq u_{m}.

Moreover, we observe that

\displaystyle\mu(t)\leq\theta_{\kappa}\varphi(t),\qquad\text{for all }0\leq t\leq v_{m}.

(3.3)

We therefore obtain the following result:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u
and
v
be positive solutions of (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), respectively. Then, for all
\tau\geq v_{m}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{q-1}\int_{\partial V_{t}^{e}}d\sigma dt=\frac{1}{q}\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}v^{q}\,d\sigma=\alpha_{q}^{\sharp}

while

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{q-1}\int_{\partial U_{t}^{e}}d\sigma dt\leq\frac{1}{q}\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{q}\,d\sigma=\alpha_{q}

Proof.

By Fubini’s theorem and the layer-cake representation, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q-1}\int_{\partial V_{t}^{e}}d\sigma dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}\int_{0}^{v(x)}t^{q-1}dt\,d\sigma=\frac{1}{q}\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}v^{q}\,d\sigma,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q-1}\int_{\partial U_{t}^{e}}d\sigma dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{0}^{u(x)}t^{q-1}dt\,d\sigma=\frac{1}{q}\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{q}\,d\sigma.

Since the integrands are nonnegative, we clearly have

\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{q-1}\int_{\partial U_{t}^{e}}d\sigma dt\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q-1}\int_{\partial U_{t}^{e}}d\sigma dt.

Moreover, for
\tau\geq v_{m}
, we have
\partial V_{\tau}^{e}\cap\partial\Omega^{\sharp}=\emptyset
for

Remark 3.4
.

One may ask whether the constraints imposed in the comparison procedure are optimal.
The Neumann case considered here indicates that weaker assumptions may still suffice to establish Talenti-type inequalities.
Identifying the minimal set of assumptions under which such comparison results remain valid appears to be an interesting open problem.

Appendix A
Proof of the monotonicity properties

Proof of the monotonicity of
l^{1/\lambda}S_{\kappa}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}
.

For
\kappa=0
, define

C_{0}:=n^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}|B^{n}|^{-\frac{p}{n(p-1)}}\theta_{0}^{-\frac{p}{n(p-1)}},

and we have

l^{1/\lambda}S_{0}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}=C_{0}\,l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}},

which is non-decreasing provided that
0<\lambda\leq\frac{n(p-1)}{p(n-1)}
.

For
\kappa=1
, we compute

\displaystyle\left(l^{1/\lambda}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}-\frac{p}{p-1}l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}S_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}-1}

(A.1)

\displaystyle=l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}-1}\left[\frac{1}{\lambda}S_{1}(l)-\frac{p}{p-1}lS_{1}^{\prime}(l)\right]

\displaystyle=l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}-1}\Biggl[\frac{1}{\lambda}n|B^{n}|\theta_{1}\sin^{n-1}r

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{p}{p-1}\Bigl(n|B^{n}|\theta_{1}\int_{0}^{r}\sin^{n-1}s\,ds\Bigr)\frac{(n-1)\cos r}{\sin r}\Biggr]

\displaystyle=l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}-1}\bigl(n|B^{n}|\theta_{1}\sin^{n-1}r\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{p(n-1)}{p-1}\frac{\cos r}{\sin^{n}r}\int_{0}^{r}\sin^{n-1}s\,ds\right].

Since for
0\leq r\leq\pi

\frac{\cos r}{\sin^{n}r}\int_{0}^{r}\sin^{n-1}s\,ds\leq\frac{1}{n},

we deduce that

\left(l^{1/\lambda}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\prime}\geq A(l)\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}\right),

where
A(l)>0
. Hence,

\left(l^{1/\lambda}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\prime}\geq 0

provided that

0<\lambda\leq\frac{n(p-1)}{p(n-1)}.

Therefore,

l^{1/\lambda}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}

is nondecreasing.
∎

Proof of the monotonicity of
F_{\kappa}(l)S_{\kappa}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}
.

Since

S_{0}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}=C_{0}\,l^{-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}},

and

F_{0}(l)=C_{0}\int_{0}^{l}w^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}}\left(\int_{0}^{w}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}dw,

it follows that

F_{0}(l)S_{0}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}=C_{0}^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{l}w^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}}\left(\int_{0}^{w}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}dw\right)l^{-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}}.

We compute that

\displaystyle\left(\left(\int_{0}^{l}w^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}}\left(\int_{0}^{w}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}dw\right)l^{-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}}\left(\int_{0}^{w}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}l^{-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left(\int_{0}^{l}w^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}}\left(\int_{0}^{w}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}dw\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\int_{0}^{w}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}l^{-1-\frac{2p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left[l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}+1}-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}l^{\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}}(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}+1)^{-1}l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}+1}\right]

\displaystyle\geq(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}+1)^{-1}\left(\int_{0}^{w}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{2p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}}\left[\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{2p(n-1)}{n(p-1)}+1\right].

Consequently, the above expression is non-decreasing when

0<\lambda<\frac{n(p-1)}{p(n-2)+n}.

For
\kappa=1
, by the definitions of
F_{1}
and
S_{1}
, a direct computation yields

\displaystyle\left(F_{1}(l)S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{1}(l)S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}-1}S_{1}^{\prime}(l)

\displaystyle=S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}-1}\left(F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)\right).

It therefore suffices to study the sign of

F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l).

Differentiating once more, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)+F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime\prime}(l)

\displaystyle=F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)-\frac{1}{p-1}F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime\prime}(l).

Since

S_{1}^{\prime\prime}(l)=\frac{d}{dl}\left(\frac{n-1}{\tan\bigl(L_{1}^{-1}(l)\bigr)}\right)\leq 0,

Consequently,

\left(F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)\right)^{\prime}\geq F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)-\frac{1}{p-1}F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l).

\displaystyle F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(l)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\left(\int_{0}^{l}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{p}{p-1}l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}-1}S_{1}^{\prime}(l)\left(\int_{0}^{l}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{p-1}l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\left(\int_{0}^{l}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}-1}f^{*}(l).

Substituting the above expression for
F_{1}^{\prime\prime}
into the previous inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)-\frac{1}{p-1}F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{l}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\Biggl[\frac{1}{\lambda}l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}+1}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{p}{p-1}l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}S_{1}^{\prime}(l)-\frac{1}{p-1}l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}S_{1}^{\prime}(l)\Biggr]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{p-1}l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}+1}\left(\int_{0}^{l}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}-1}f^{*}(l).

Since
f^{*}(l)\geq 0
, we deduce that

\displaystyle F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)-\frac{1}{p-1}F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)

\displaystyle\geq l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1}S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\left(\int_{0}^{l}f^{*}(s)\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\Biggl[\frac{1}{\lambda}S_{1}(l)-\frac{p+1}{p-1}lS_{1}^{\prime}(l)\Biggr].

Returning to (
A.1
), we proceed as in the previous argument. In view of

\frac{\cos r}{\sin^{n}r}\int_{0}^{r}\sin^{n-1}s\,ds\leq\frac{1}{n},

it follows that

\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{p+1}{p-1}(n-1)\frac{\cos r\int_{0}^{r}\sin^{n-1}s\,ds}{\sin^{n}r}\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{(p+1)(n-1)}{n(p-1)}.

Therefore,

\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{p+1}{p-1}(n-1)\frac{\cos r\int_{0}^{r}\sin^{n-1}s\,ds}{\sin^{n}r}\geq 0

whenever

0<\lambda\leq\frac{n(p-1)}{(p+1)(n-1)}.

Consequently, the function

l\mapsto F_{1}(l)S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}

is nondecreasing on its domain.

For the case
n=2
, we investigate the sign of

F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l).

Equivalently, it suffices to study the sign of

F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)^{2}-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{1}(l)S_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l).

We differentiate this quantity with respect to
l
, obtaining

\displaystyle\left(F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)^{2}-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{1}(l)S_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)^{2}+2F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{1}(l)\big(S_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)\big)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)^{2}+\frac{p-2}{p-1}F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{1}(l)\big(S_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)\big)^{\prime}.

In dimension
n=2
, we can directly verify that

\big(S_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)\big)^{\prime}=A(\cos r)^{\prime}\leq 0,

where
A>0
is a constant.
It follows that

\displaystyle\left(F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)^{2}-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{1}(l)S_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)\right)^{\prime}\geq F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)^{2}+\frac{p-2}{p-1}F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l).

We proceed as in the previous case and obtain

\displaystyle F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)^{2}+\frac{p-2}{p-1}F_{1}^{\prime}(l)S_{1}(l)S_{1}^{\prime}(l)\geq S_{1}(l)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}l^{\frac{1}{\lambda}-1}\left[\frac{1}{\lambda}S_{1}(l)-\frac{2}{p-1}lS_{1}^{\prime}(l)\right].

We now return to the previous argument. Consequently, we conclude that

F_{1}(l)S_{1}(l)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\ \text{is nondecreasing for }0<\lambda\leq p-1.

∎
