Multi-entropy in random tensor networks


Abstract
We study the evaluation of Rényi multi-entropies S n ( q ) S^{(q)}_{n} in Random Tensor Network (RTN) states in the large bond-dimension limit. For the case of Rényi index n = 2 n=2 and arbitrary number of parties q q , we prove that that multi-entropies are determined by minimal multiway cuts through the network. When the minimal multiway cut is degenerate, we characterize the full minimizer set via compatible families of minimal cuts and give a criterion for all minimizers to come from ordinary cut partitions. For n = 2 n=2 , this gives a natural generalization of the minimal cut description of bipartite entanglement to multipartite systems with arbitrarily many parties. For the case of integer n > 2 n>2 , we show that the minimal multiway cut conjecture is in general not true by providing explicit counter examples for both the single random tensor and for the network built from isometric tilings. We discuss the implication for our results on the multipartite entanglement structures in RTN and holography.

Preliminaries

We begin with a brief review of materials necessary for proving our main results.

2.1
Multi-entropy

Consider a quantum state
\ket{\psi}
in a finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
can be written as the tensor product of
q
different subspaces

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{q}.

(2.1)

We can decompose
\ket{\psi}
using

\ket{\psi}=\sum_{i_{1}}\sum_{i_{2}}\cdots\sum_{i_{q}}\psi_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{q}}\ket{e^{i_{1}}_{1}}\ket{e^{i_{2}}_{2}}\cdots\ket{e^{i_{q}}_{q}},

(2.2)

where
\ket{e^{i}_{a}}
represents a set of basis vectors in
\mathcal{H}_{a}
.
The coefficient
\psi_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}
can be viewed as a rank-
q
tensor that transforms contravariantly under the action of local unitaries (LU). In contrast, the complex conjugate
\bar{\psi}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{q}}
are the coefficient of the dual vector
\bra{\psi}
, which transforms covariantly under LU. One can build quantities known as
multi-invariants

[
30
]
that is LU-invariant by fully contracting an equal number of
\psi
and
\bar{\psi}
along their common indices.
Let
N
be the number of
\psi
copies (or equivalently, the number of
\bar{\psi}
copies). We can construct any contraction pattern by specifying how each individual subspaces are contracted, using an element in the permutation group
\Sym_{N}
(often called the
replica symmetry group
). There are
q
subsystems, and thus any multi-invariant is uniquely specified by
q
elements
g_{1},\cdots g_{q}\in\Sym_{N}
(often called the
twist operators
), as shown in the following definition:

Definition 1

(multi-invariants)
.

Let
\ket{\psi}
be a
q
-partite pure state with decomposition of (
2.2
) and
g_{1},\cdots g_{q}\in\Sym_{N}
be a list of permutations in the replica symmetry group. The multi-invariant
\mathcal{Z}
associated to the list
\{g_{a}\}
is defined as

\mathcal{Z}(g_{1},\cdots g_{q})=\Big(\psi_{i^{(1)}_{1}\cdots i^{(1)}_{q}}\cdots\psi_{i^{(N)}_{1}\cdots i^{(N)}_{q}}\Big)\Big(\bar{\psi}^{i^{(g_{1}\cdot 1)}_{1}\cdots i^{(g_{q}\cdot 1)}_{q}}\cdots\bar{\psi}^{i^{(g_{1}\cdot N)}_{1}\cdots i^{(g_{q}\cdot N)}_{q}}\Big),

(2.3)

where repeated indices are summed over.
Or equivalently, let
\rho_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{q}}=\psi_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}\bar{\psi}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{q}}
be the coefficients for the density matrix
\rho=\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}
, we have

\mathcal{Z}(g_{1},\cdots g_{q})=\rho_{i^{(1)}_{1}\cdots i^{(1)}_{q}}^{i^{(g_{1}\cdot 1)}_{1}\cdots i^{(g_{q}\cdot 1)}_{q}}\cdots\rho_{i^{(N)}_{1}\cdots i^{(N)}_{q}}^{i^{(g_{1}\cdot N)}_{1}\cdots i^{(g_{q}\cdot N)}_{q}}.

(2.4)

Definition
1
is a bit unwieldy to work with, so let us look at some examples.

Example 2

(Rényi entropy)
.

Let
\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
be a bipartite state. The bipartite Rényi entropy is
S_{n}(A)=\frac{1}{1-n}\ln(\tr\rho^{n}_{A})
where
\rho_{A}=\tr_{B}\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}
is the reduced density matrix. The moment invariant
\tr\rho^{n}_{A}
is an LU-invariant with twist operators

g_{A}=\tau_{n}=(12\cdots n),\hskip 10.00002ptg_{B}=\id.

(2.5)

The replica group for the moment is
\Sym_{n}
.

Example 3

(Entanglement negativity
[
62
]
)
.

Let
\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}
be a tripartite state and
\rho_{AB}=\tr_{C}\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}
be the reduced density matrix. The Rényi negativity is defined as (the logarithm of) the moments of the partial transpose
\rho^{T_{B}}_{AB}
. The twist operators for the Rényi negativity is

g_{A}=\tau_{n}=(12\cdots n),\hskip 10.00002ptg_{B}=\tau^{-1}_{n}=(n\cdots 21),\hskip 10.00002ptg_{C}=\id.

(2.6)

The replica group for the partial transpose moment is also
\Sym_{n}
.

Example 4

(Reflected entropy
[
26
]
)
.

Let
\ket{\psi}
and
\rho_{AB}
be the same as the previous example. For integer
n
and even integer
m
, the Rényi reflected entropy is the Rényi entropy
S_{n}(AA^{*})
for
\ket{\rho_{AB}^{m/2}}
, viewed as a vector in the doubled Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{AB}\otimes H_{A^{*}B^{*}}
via the usual state-operator correspondence. The twist operators for the Rényi reflected entropy is

\displaystyle\begin{split}g_{A}&=(1\cdots m)(m+1\cdots 2m)\cdots(nm-m+1\cdots mn),\\
g_{B}&=(\tfrac{m}{2}+1\cdots\tfrac{3m}{2})(\tfrac{3m}{2}+1\cdots\tfrac{5m}{2})\cdots(nm-\tfrac{m}{2}+1\cdots\tfrac{m}{2}),\\
g_{C}&=\id.\end{split}

(2.7)

The replica group for Rényi reflected entropy is
\Sym_{mn}
.

Before proceeding, let us introduce a diagrammatic notation for better visualization of the multi-invariants. Notice that
\mathcal{Z}
is invariant when we multiply all the twist operators by an arbitrary permutation
g_{a}\to hg_{a}
. We can use this freedom to fix one of them (say
g_{q}
) to be the identity permutation. In terms of the density matrix
\rho=\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}
, this corresponds to taking the partial trace over the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{q}
.
We denote the reduced density matrix
\rho_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q-1}}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{q-1}}
after tracing out
\mathcal{H}_{q}
as a vertex with
2(q-1)
legs. Each leg corresponds to one index
i_{a}
or
j_{a}
. The multi-variant
\mathcal{Z}
is constructed by first laying out
N
copies of vertices and then contracting the legs according to the order given by the twist operators. See Fig.
2
for an illustration.

Figure 2:
The diagrammatic notation for the reduced density matrix
\rho^{j_{1}\cdots j_{q}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}

The Rényi multi-entropy is a natural generalization for the bipartite Rényi entropy to higher partite systems
[
32
]
. The twist operators acts as translations on a periodic hypercube lattice of
(q-1)
dimensions.

Definition 5

(Rényi multi-entropy)
.

Let

X=\{0,\cdots,n-1\}^{q-1}=\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{q-1},\hskip 10.00002pt|X|=n^{q-1}

(2.8)

be the set of
(q-1)
-dimensional integer lattice points on a hypercube of length
n
. The
Rényi multi-entropy

S^{(q)}_{n}
is defined in terms of the multi-invariant
\mathcal{Z}(g_{1},\cdots,g_{q})
as

S^{(q)}_{n}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q}):=\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{1}{n^{q-2}}\ln\mathcal{Z}(g_{1},\cdots,g_{q}),

(2.9)

where the twist operators
g_{i}
act on
X
by translation along the
i
-th axis, i.e.

\displaystyle g_{i}\cdot(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i},\cdots,x_{q-1})=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i}+1,\cdots,x_{q-1}),\hskip 10.00002pti<q,

(2.10)

\displaystyle g_{q}=\id,

(2.11)

where the arithmetic operations on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
are understood to be up to modulo
n
.

The replica symmetry group for the Rényi multi-entropy is
\Sym(X)=\Sym_{n^{q-1}}
. The contraction patterns of
S^{(q)}_{n}
for some low
(q,n)
are illustrated in Fig.
2
. For
q=2
, it reduces back to the usual bipartite Rényi entropies
S_{n}
.

Rényi multi-entropies enjoy the following properties
[
32
,
31
]
:

•

(LU invariance).
S^{(q)}_{n}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q})
is invariant under LU operations.

•

(Symmetry).
S^{(q)}_{n}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q})
is invariant under permutations of the subsystems
R_{i}\to R_{\sigma\cdot i}
for any
\sigma\in S_{q}
.

•

(Additivity).
S^{(q)}_{n}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q})
is additive under tensor products: Let
\ket{\psi}\in H_{A_{1}\cdots A_{q}}
and
\ket{\phi}\in H_{B_{1}\cdots B_{q}}
be two
q
-partite states, then

S^{(q)}_{n}(A_{1}B_{1}:\cdots:A_{q}B_{q})_{\ket{\psi}\otimes\ket{\phi}}=S^{(q)}_{n}(A_{1}:\cdots:A_{q})_{\ket{\psi}}+S^{(q)}_{n}(B_{1}:\cdots:B_{q})_{\ket{\phi}}.

(2.12)

Just as bipartite Rényi entropy can be used to detect bipartite entanglement, Rényi multi-entropies can serve as signals for multi-partite entanglement. In particular, one can form linear combinations of
S^{(q)}_{n}
called
Genuine multi-entropy
that precisely detects genuine
q
-partite entanglement, which are states that cannot be written as tensor products of lower-partite entangled states. See Ref.
[
47
,
41
]
for more details on how to construct Genuine multi-entropies.

2.2
Random tensor networks

Definition 6

(Random tensor network states
[
37
]
)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with edge weight
\chi:E\to\mathbb{N}
. For each vertex
v\in V
we assign a Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}^{v}=\bigotimes_{e\in E(v)}\mathcal{H}^{(v)}_{e},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{H}^{(v)}_{e}=\mathbb{C}^{\chi(e)},

(2.13)

where
E(v)=\{(u,v):u\in N(v)\}
is the set of edges emanating from
v
.
We mark a subset
\partial\subset V
. We refer to
\partial
as the set of
boundary (terminal) vertices
and
V\setminus\partial
as the set of
bulk (interior) vertices
.
The random tensor network assigns
G
a pure state
\ket{\psi}_{\partial}
on its boundary vertex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\partial}=\bigotimes_{\mathit{v}\in\partial}\mathcal{H}_{v}
in the following manner: For each bulk vertex we sample a random vector
T(v)\in\mathcal{H}_{v}
according to the Haar measure, and construct

\ket{\psi}_{\partial}\propto\left(\bigotimes_{v\in V\setminus\partial}\bra{T(v)}\right)\left(\bigotimes_{e\in E}\ket{\Psi_{e}}\right),

(2.14)

where
\ket{\Psi_{e}}\in\mathcal{H}^{(u)}_{e}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{(v)}_{e}
is a maximally entangled state between
\mathcal{H}^{(u)}_{e}
and
\mathcal{H}^{(v)}_{e}
for an edge
e=(u,v)
.

In this paper we will work in the limit of
large bond dimensions
, i.e. we take
\chi(e)\to\infty
uniformly across all
e\in E
. For convenience we define the rescaled weight

w(e)=\frac{\ln\chi(e)}{\ln\chi}

(2.15)

which we hold finite as we take
\chi\to\infty
.

We are interested in the quantification of multi-partite entanglement in random tensor network states. To do this we divide the boundary vertices into
q
disjoint parties
\partial=R_{1}\sqcup R_{2}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup R_{q}
, and denote the resulting
q
-partite state
\ket{\psi_{12\cdots q}}
. We want to calculate the Rényi multi-entropies for this state. A remarkable result in Haar-random tensor networks is that the evaluation of multi-invariants for
\psi
can be mapped to an equivalent problem of evaluating the partition function on a associated “Ising model” on
G

[
37
]
.

\displaystyle S^{(q)}_{n}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\frac{1}{n^{q-2}}\ln\frac{Z^{(q)}_{n}}{(Z^{(q)}_{1})^{n^{q-1}}},

(2.16)

\displaystyle Z^{(q)}_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g}\exp\left(-\sum_{e=(u,v)\in E}d(g(u),g(v))\ln\chi(e)\right),

(2.17)

where the map
g:V\to\Sym(X)
assigns an element of the replica symmetry group
\Sym(X)
to each vertex. The “interaction” for this Ising model is given by the Cayley distance on
\Sym(X)
:

d(g,h)=|X|-\#(gh^{-1}),

(2.18)

where
\#(g)
counts the number of cycles of
g
(including unit cycles).
The Cayley distance measures the minimal number of transpositions one needs to apply to change
g
to
h
.
6
6

6

d(g,h)
is a distance function on
\Sym_{N}
, meaning that it satisfies: 1.
d(g,h)=d(h,g)
(symmetry), 2.
d(g,h)\geq 0
and is zero iff
g=h
(semi-positivity), and 3.
d(g,h)+d(h,k)\geq d(g,k)
(triangle inequality).
The boundary condition for this Ising model is fixed by the action of corresponding twist operators on the subregions. In our case we have

g(v)=g_{i},\hskip 10.00002ptv\in R_{i}

(2.19)

with
g_{i}
given in Definition
5
.

In the large bond-dimension limit
\chi\to\infty
, one can obtain a good approximation for the partition function by saddle point approximation, i.e. finding the configuration
g
that maximizes the exponential and dropping all the other terms:

\displaystyle\log Z^{(q)}_{n}

\displaystyle\approx\max_{g}\left(-\sum_{e=(u,v)\in E}d(g(u),g(v))\ln\chi(e)\right)

(2.20)

\displaystyle=-(\ln\chi)\min_{g}\left(F(g)\right),

(2.21)

where
g
is now the configuration that minimizes the “free energy”

F(g)=\sum_{e=\{x,y\}\in E}d(g(x),g(y))w(e).

(2.22)

This expression is the starting point of our main results in this paper. In Sec.
3
, we will prove that for
n=2
and arbitrary
q
, the minimizer of (
2.22
) is given by the solution of the multiway cut problem on the graph
G
, which in turn allows one to determine the multi-entropy of the RTN state
\ket{\psi}
through (
2.20
) and (
2.16
). We review multiway cut problem the following subsection.

2.3
Multiway cuts and minimal surfaces

Given any network
(G,w)
and set of boundary permutations
g_{i}
, finding the configuration
g(v)
that minimizes the free energy (
2.22
) is a highly non-trivial task.
There is however a simple solution that can be easily constructed: one simply divides
G
into
q
different “domains”
\Gamma_{i}
, each containing the corresponding boundary region
R_{i}
. One then assigns
g(v)=g_{i}
for all the vertices
v
inside the domain
\Gamma_{i}
. The contribution to the free energy for this configuration thus solely comes from the interface between these regions. We then pick the domain configurations
\{\Gamma_{i}\}
that minimizes the total interface area. This produces a candidate
g
for our problem.
The problem of finding the domains with minimal area is known as the
multiway cut problem
, which we now define:

Definition 7

(multiway partitions and cuts)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with edge weight
w:E\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
R_{1},\cdots,R_{q}\subset V
be a set of boundary vertices.
A
multiway partition
is a partition
V=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\Gamma_{q}
such that
R_{i}\subseteq\Gamma_{i}
for all
i=1,\cdots,q
.
A
multiway cut (or cut set)
, denoted
\mu(\{\Gamma_{k}\})
, is the set of edges that lies on the boundaries between any two subsets, i.e.

\mu(\{\Gamma_{k}\})=\{(u,v)\in E:u\in\Gamma_{i}\text{ and }v\in\Gamma_{j},\hskip 10.00002pti\neq j\}.

(2.23)

The
area
of a multiway cut, denoted
\mathcal{A}(\{\Gamma_{k}\})
, is the weighted sum of
\mu(\{\Gamma_{k}\})
over the edge weight
w
:

\mathcal{A}(\{\Gamma_{k}\})=\sum_{e\in\mu(\{\Gamma_{k}\})}w(e).

(2.24)

Definition 8

(minimal multiway cut)
.

The
minimal multiway cut
is a multiway cut that minimizes the cut area. Stated differently, the minimal multiway cut is the minimal number of edges (weighted by
w

R_{k}
.
We denote the the area of the minimal cut as

\mathcal{A}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q})=\min_{\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\Gamma_{q}=V}\mathcal{A}(\{\Gamma_{k}\}).

(2.25)

See Figure

\partial=A\sqcup B

g(v)=g_{i},\hskip 10.00002ptv\in\Gamma_{i},\hskip 10.00002pti=A,B

The minimal area prescription for bipartite quantities is what makes RTN states special (in the sense that it is always “maximally entangled” across any bipartite regions

For higher parties the situation is much more complicated. Even in tripartite settings
\partial=A\sqcup B\sqcup C

From the above examples, we see that, in order to hope for a simple description for the minimizer in terms of the multiway cut, the geodesic structure between the terminal permutations

must be “highly frustrated”. In particular, the geodesics between any pairs of the terminal permutations had better be as far apart from each other as possible (see Proposition

, by giving an explicit counter example.

3
A full description of the
n=2
case

We now prove our main result of this paper, that the multi-entropy of RTN states are given by the area of the multiway cut in the network. Our proof applies to
n=2
, but arbitrary
q
. The higher
n
cases are discussed in Section
5
.
Our proof rests on a simple estimate relating the Cayley distance and the number of moved points in
\Sym_{N}
. This estimate has previously appeared in the random matrix and quantum information theory literature, see e.g.
[
13
,
19
,
17
]
.

Lemma 9
.

For any permutation
g\in\Sym(X)
, let

m(g):=|\{x\in X:g(x)\neq x\}|

(3.1)

be the number of the moved points under the action of
g
. Then

d(g,\id)\geq\frac{m(g)}{2}.

(3.2)

Proof.

Write the disjoint cycle decomposition of
g
as fixed points together with nontrivial cycles of lengths

\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{r}\geq 2.

(3.3)

A cycle of length
\ell_{j}
has transposition length
\ell_{j}-1
, hence

d(g,\id)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\ell_{j}-1).

(3.4)

Also,

m(g)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\ell_{j}

(3.5)

because precisely the points lying in the nontrivial cycles are moved. Therefore

d(g,\id)-\frac{m(g)}{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left((\ell_{j}-1)-\frac{\ell_{j}}{2}\right)=\sum^{r}_{j=1}\left(\frac{\ell_{j}}{2}-1\right)\geq 0

(3.6)

since each
\ell_{j}\geq 2
.
∎

Remark 10
.

Note that the bound in the above lemma is not tight in general: for a full cycle
\tau
on
X
, we get

d(\tau,\id)=|X|-1\hskip 10.00002pt\text{while}\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{m(\tau)}{2}=\frac{|X|}{2},

(3.7)

but it is tight if every nontrivial cycle has length
2
, i.e. when
g
is an involution.

3.1
Single random tensor

We first consider the situation for the case of a single random tensor before moving on to RTNs defined on generic graphs. That is, we consider the minimization of the following target

\min_{g\in\Sym(X)}F(g)=\min_{g\in\Sym(X)}\sum_{i=1}^{q}w_{i}d(g,g_{i}),

(3.8)

where
w_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. See Figure
5
for an illustration. Let us first consider the case where
w_{i}
are all equal to each other.

Figure 5:
The
q
-partite single random tensor we consider in this subsection. The black node represents the random tensor and the white nodes represent the terminal vertices. The (logarithmic) bond dimensions of this random tensor are denoted by
w_{i}
, respectively.

Theorem 11
.

For
n=2
and for every integer
q
,

\min_{g\in\Sym(X)}\sum^{q}_{i=1}d(g,g_{i})=(q-1)2^{q-2}.

(3.9)

Moreover, the minimum is attained at
g=g_{q}=\id
.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary permutation
g\in\Sym(X)
. For each
i=1,\cdots,q
, define

h_{i}=g^{-1}g_{i}.

(3.10)

Then

d(g,g_{i})=d(g^{-1}g_{i},\id)=d(h_{i},\id).

(3.11)

By Lemma
9
,

d(h_{i},\id)\geq\frac{m(h_{i})}{2}.

(3.12)

Now

h_{i}(x)=x\Longleftrightarrow g^{-1}g_{i}(x)=x\Longleftrightarrow g_{i}(x)=g(x),

(3.13)

so

m(h_{i})=|\{x\in X:g(x)\neq g_{i}(x)\}|.

(3.14)

Summing over
i
, we obtain

\sum_{i}d(g,g_{i})\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}|x\in X:g(x)\neq g_{i}(x)|=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{i}\mathbf{1}_{g(x)\neq g_{i}(x)}

(3.15)

Fix
x\in X
. We claim that the
q
elements
g_{1}(x),\cdots,g_{q}(x)
are pairwise distinct. Indeed:

•

g_{q}(x)=x
;

•

for each
q>i\geq 1
,
g_{i}(x)
differs from
x
in exactly the
i
-th coordinate;

•

if
i\neq j
, then
g_{i}(x)
and
g_{j}(x)
differ in both the
i
-th and
j
-th coordinates.

Hence the single value
g(x)
can coincide with at most one of the values
g_{1}(x),\cdots,g_{q}(x)
. It follows that

\sum^{q}_{i=1}\mathbf{1}_{g(x)\neq g_{i}(x)}\geq q-1\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for every }x\in X.

(3.16)

Summing over all
x\in X
, we get

\sum_{i}d(g,g_{i})\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in X}(q-1)=\frac{|X|}{2}(q-1)=(q-1)2^{q-2}.

(3.17)

Since
g\in\Sym(X)
was arbitrary, this proves

\min_{g\in\Sym(X)}\sum_{i=1}^{q}d(g,g_{i})\geq(q-1)2^{q-2}.

(3.18)

It remains to show that the equality is attained. Take
g=g_{q}=\id
.
Then
d(g_{q},g_{q})=0
and
d(g_{q},g_{i})=2^{q-2}
for each
i=1,\cdots,q-1
. Therefore

\sum_{i}d(g_{q},g_{i})=(q-1)2^{q-2}.

(3.19)

Combining this with the lower bound yields

\min_{g\in\Sym(X)}\sum_{i=1}^{q}d(g,g_{i})=(q-1)2^{q-2}.

(3.20)

and thus completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 12
.

The set of minimizers in Theorem
11
is exactly

\{g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{q}\}.

(3.21)

Proof.

Let
g
be a minimizer. Then equality must hold at every step of the chain of inequalities in the proof of the theorem:

\sum_{i}d(g,g_{i})\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}m(g^{-1}g_{i})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{i}\mathbf{1}_{g(x)\neq g_{i}(x)}\geq(q-1)2^{q-2}.

(3.22)

Therefore, for every
x\in X
,

\sum_{i}\mathbf{1}_{g(x)\neq g_{i}(x)}=q.

(3.23)

Since the values
g_{1}(x),\cdots,g_{q}(x)
are pairwise distinct, it follows that
g(x)
coincides with exactly one of them.

Also, for every
i
, we have

d(g,g_{i})=\frac{1}{2}m(g^{-1}g_{i}).

(3.24)

From Remark
10
, equality occurs if and only if
g^{-1}g_{i}
is an involution. Taking
i=q
, we see that
g^{-1}
is an involution, so
g^{2}=\id
and therefore
g=g^{-1}
. For
i=1,\cdots,q-1
, the permutation
g^{-1}g_{i}=gg_{i}
is also an involution, therefore

g_{i}gg_{i}^{-1}=g,

(3.25)

since
g_{i}
are also involutions. This implies that
g
commutes with every translation: for any
v\in X
, then
g(\tau_{v}(x))=\tau_{v}(g(x))
where

\tau_{v}:X\to X,\hskip 10.00002pt\tau_{v}(x)=x+v.

(3.26)

Finally, from the pointwise condition above, we have
g(0)=g_{i}(0)
for some
i\in\{1,\cdots,q\}
. We have
For any
x\in X
,

g(x)=g(\tau_{x}(0))=\tau_{x}(g(0))=x+g_{i}(0)=g_{i}(x).

(3.27)

So
g=g_{i}
. This proves that the set of minimizers is exactly
\{g_{1},\cdots,g_{q}\}
.
∎

Remark 13
.

In the proof above it is crucial that the
g=g_{i}
saturates the estimate
d(g,g_{j})\geq\frac{1}{2}m(h_{j})=\frac{1}{2}m(g^{-1}g_{j})
.
This is only possible if
h_{j}
is an involution, i.e.
h_{j}^{2}=\id
, which happens to be true when
n=2
. For
n\geq 3
,
(g_{i}^{-1}g_{j})^{2}\neq\id
and the estimate is never tight for
g=g_{i}
. In fact we will see that, for
n\geq 3
there is a new element
\pi
where it attains a lower objective,
F(g)\geq F(\pi)
(with equality only at
(q,n)=(3,3)
).

We now deal with the case of arbitrary edge weights.

Theorem 14
.

Let
w_{1},\cdots,w_{q}>0
, and set

w_{\max}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq q}w_{i},\hskip 20.00003ptK_{\max}:=\{k\in\{1,\cdots,q\}:w_{k}=w_{\max}\}.

(3.28)

For

F(g)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}w_{i}d(g,g_{i})

(3.29)

one has

\min_{g\in\Sym(X)}F(g)=2^{q-2}\left(\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{q}w_{i}\Big)-w_{\max}\right),

(3.30)

and the minimizers are exactly

\{g_{k}:k\in K_{\max}\}.

(3.31)

Proof.

We follow the same proof as that of Theorem
11
up to (
3.14
). However instead of (
3.15
) we have

\sum_{i}w_{i}d(g,g_{i})\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}w_{i}|x\in X:g(x)\neq g_{i}(x)|=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{i}w_{i}\mathbf{1}_{g(x)\neq g_{i}(x)}.

(3.32)

Now since
g(x)
can only coincide with at most one of
g_{1}(x),\cdots,g_{q}(x)
, to minimize the RHS we must pick
g(x)=g_{k}(x)
for some
k\in K_{\max}
, and thus

\sum_{i}w_{i}\mathbf{1}_{g(x)\neq g_{i}(x)}\geq\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{q}w_{i}\Big)-w_{\max},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x\in X.

(3.33)

Summing over all
x\in X
we obtain

\sum_{i}w_{i}d(g,g_{i})\geq 2^{q-2}\left(\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{q}w_{i}\Big)-w_{\max}\right).

(3.34)

It is straightforward to check that plugging
g=g_{k}
into
F(g)
saturates this bound for every
k\in K_{\max}
.

Conversely, let
g
be a minimizer. Then equality holds in the pointwise bound above, so for every
x\in X
there is some
k(x)\in K_{\max}
such that

g(x)=g_{k(x)}(x).

(3.35)

Also equality holds in the bounds
d(g,g_{i})\geq\frac{1}{2}m(g^{-1}g_{i})
for all
i
, hence every
g^{-1}g_{i}
is an involution. As in the proof of the corollary to Theorem
11
, this implies that
g
commutes with every translation of
X
. Since
g(0)=g_{k}(0)
for some
k\in K_{\max}
, we have, for every
x\in X
,

g(x)=g(\tau_{x}(0))=\tau_{x}(g(0))=\tau_{x}(g_{k}(0))=g_{k}(x).

(3.36)

Thus
g=g_{k}
with
k\in K_{\max}
, which proves the claimed description of the minimizers.
∎

3.2
Random tensor networks

We now deal with the general optimization problem of multi-entropy on a RTN state:
Given a graph
G=(V,E)
and edge weight
w:E\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we would like to minimize

F(g)=\sum_{e=(u,v)\in E}w(e)d(g(u),g(v))

(3.37)

over all admissible
g:V\to\Sym(X)
satisfying the boundary condition
g(v)=g_{i}
for
v\in R_{i}
on the boundary (terminal) vertices.

We first state our main result of this subsection.

Theorem 15
.

For
n=2
and for every integer
q
, the minimum of (
3.37
) is given by the minimum multiway cut area over
G
:

\min_{g}F(g)=2^{q-2}\mathcal{A}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q}).

(3.38)

Our proof strategy will be similar to the single tensor case, by first applying Lemma
9
on the Cayley distances to obtain a bound for
F(g)
. We then show that, given a solution to the multiway cut problem, one can reconstruct a
g
that saturates the bound.

For each
x\in X
, we define the
slice constant

\mathcal{A}_{x}:=\min_{s}\left\{\sum_{e=(u,v)\in E}w(e)\mathbf{1}_{s(u)\neq s(v)}:s:V\to X,\hskip 10.00002pts(v)=g_{i}(x)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall v\in R_{i}\right\}.

(3.39)

Equivalently,
\mathcal{A}_{x}
is the (weighted) minimum number of edges one must cut in order to separate the terminal nodes whose
x
-images under
g_{i}
are different, while terminals with the same
x
-image are allowed to remain in the same component. Note that this quantity depends on the element
x\in X
.

Lemma 16

(Universal slice lower bound)
.

For every admissible
g
,

F(g)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in X}\mathcal{A}_{x}.

(3.40)

Proof.

Fix an admissible
g
. For each edge
(u,v)\in E
denote
g_{u}=g(u)
and
g_{v}=g(v)
. Lemma
9
gives

d(g_{u},g_{v})\geq\frac{1}{2}|\{x\in X:g_{u}(x)\neq g_{v}(x)\}|,

(3.41)

Summing over all edges and interchanging the order of summation, we obtain

\displaystyle F(g)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e=(u,v)\in E}w(e)|\{x\in X:g_{u}(x)\neq g_{v}(x)\}|

(3.42)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{e=(u,v)\in E}w(e)\mathbf{1}_{g_{u}(x)\neq g_{v}(x)}.

(3.43)

For a fixed
x\in X
, define

s_{x}(v):=g_{v}(x),\hskip 10.00002pt(v\in V).

(3.44)

Then
s_{x}:V\to X
is a labeling of
V
with boundary values

s_{x}(t_{i})=g_{t_{i}}(x)=g_{i}(x),\hskip 10.00002ptt_{i}\in R_{i}.

(3.45)

Therefore, by the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{x}
,

\sum_{e=(u,v)\in E}w(e)\mathbf{1}_{g_{u}(x)\neq g_{v}(x)}=\sum_{e=(u,v)\in E}w(e)\mathbf{1}_{s_{x}(u)\neq s_{x}(v)}\geq\mathcal{A}_{x}.

(3.46)

Substituting this into (
3.42
) and summing over
x
gives (
3.40
).
∎

We need the following technical lemma.

Lemma 17

(disjoint labelings are multiway cuts)
.

Let
Y
be any set, and let
y_{1},\cdots,y_{q}\in Y
be pairwise distinct. For a labeling
s:V\to Y
with

s(v)=y_{i},\hskip 10.00002ptv\in R_{i}.

(3.47)

Define

\mathcal{W}(s):=\sum_{e=(u,v)\in E}w(e)\mathbf{1}_{s(u)\neq s(v)}.

(3.48)

Then

\mathcal{W}(s)\geq\mathcal{A}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q}).

(3.49)

Moreover, equality is attained by the boundary-valued labeling: if
V=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\Gamma_{q}
is a minimum multiway partition, then the labeling

s(v)=y_{i},\hskip 10.00002ptv\in\Gamma_{i}

(3.50)

satisfies

\mathcal{W}(s)=\mathcal{A}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q}).

(3.51)

Proof.

Let

E_{\neq}(s):=\{(u,v)\in E:s(u)\neq s(v)\}.

(3.52)

Then
\mathcal{W}(s)=\sum_{e\in E_{\neq}(s)}w(e)
. Remove these edges and consider the spanning subgraph

G_{s}=(V,E\setminus E_{\neq}(s)).

(3.53)

Every connected component of
G_{s}
is monochromatic by construction. Since the terminal colors
y_{i}
are pairwise distinct, no connected component of
G_{s}
can contain boundary vertices of different indices.

For each
i
, let
\Gamma_{i}
be the union of all components of
G_{s}
that intersect
R_{i}
. Any remaining component of
G_{s}
contains no terminal vertices; assign each such component arbitrarily to one of the sets
\Gamma_{1},\cdots,\Gamma_{q}
. The produces a multiway partition

V=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\Gamma_{q}.

(3.54)

Every edge joining different parts of this partition must lie in
E_{\neq}(s)
, because edges outside
E_{\neq}(s)
stay inside components of
G_{s}
. Hence

\mu(\{\Gamma_{k}\})\subseteq E_{\neq}(s).

(3.55)

So

\mathcal{W}(s)=\sum_{e\in E_{\neq}(s)}w(e)\geq\sum_{e\in\mu(\{\Gamma_{k}\})}w(e)\geq\mathcal{A}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q}).

(3.56)

This proves (
3.49
).

Conversely, if
V=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\Gamma_{q}
is a minimum multiway partition and we set
s(v)=y_{i}
for
v\in\Gamma_{i}
, then an edge contributes
w(e)
to
\mathcal{W}(s)
if and only if it lies in
\mu(\{\Gamma_{k}\})
. Therefore

\mathcal{W}(s)=\sum_{e\in\mu(\{\Gamma_{k}\})}w(e)=\mathcal{A}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q}).

(3.57)

∎

Remark 18
.

Note that (
3.49
) is saturated
if and only if

s
is of the form of (
3.50
). This can be seen from (
3.56
): Saturating of the chain of inequalities requires
E_{\neq}(s)=\mu(\{\Gamma_{k}\})
where
\{\Gamma_{k}\}
is a minimal multiway partition. We call such
s
to be a labeling
associated
to the minimal multiway partition
\{\Gamma_{k}\}
.

We are now ready to prove the main result.

Proof of Theorem
15
.

Fix
x\in X
. The
q
elements
g_{1}(x),\cdots,g_{q}(x)
are pairwise distinct for
n=2
(see the proof of Theorem
11
).
Therefore Lemma
17
, applied to the colors
y_{i}=g_{i}(x)
shows that the minimum disagreement cost with these boundary values is exactly the minimum multiway cut:

\mathcal{A}_{x}\geq\mathcal{A}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q}).

(3.58)

From Lemma
16
we have

F(g)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in X}\mathcal{A}_{x}\geq\frac{|X|}{2}\mathcal{A}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q})=2^{q-2}\mathcal{A}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q}).

(3.59)

Next, let
V=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\Gamma_{q}
be a minimum multiway partition, and define

g_{*}(v)=g_{i},\hskip 10.00002ptv\in\Gamma_{i}.

(3.60)

It is clear that
g_{*}
is admissible. The only contribution to
F(g_{*})
comes from the cut set
\mu(\{\Gamma_{k}\})
, with each edge contributing
d(g_{i},g_{j})=2^{q-2}
. Therefore

F(g_{*})=2^{q-2}\sum_{e\in\mu(\{\Gamma_{k}\})}w(e)=2^{q-2}\mathcal{A}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q})

(3.61)

saturates the bound. This proves the theorem.
∎

In fact, we can state the previous result of single tensor as a simple corollary.

Example 19

(single random tensor)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
where
V
consists of a single internal vertex
v
and terminals
\{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}\}
, and edge weight
w(v,t_{i})=w_{i}
.
Let
k\in\{1,\cdots,q\}
such that
w_{k}=\max_{i}w_{i}
. The minimal multiway partition of this network is

\Gamma_{i\neq k}=\{t_{i}\},\hskip 10.00002pt\Gamma_{k}=\{v,t_{k}\}.

(3.62)

The area of the minimal multiway cut is

\mathcal{A}(t_{1}:\cdots:t_{q})=\sum_{i\neq k}w_{i}.

(3.63)

Applying Theorem
15
, we find

\min_{g}F(g)=2^{q-2}\mathcal{A}(t_{1}:\cdots:t_{q})=2^{q-2}\sum_{i\neq k}w_{i},

(3.64)

which reproduces the result of Theorem
14
.

The next result pertains to the uniqueness of the minimizer.

Corollary 20
.

If
G
admits a unique minimal multiway partition
V=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\Gamma_{q}
, then the minimizer of
F(g)
is unique and is given by

g(v)=g_{i},\hskip 10.00002ptv\in\Gamma_{i}

(3.65)

Proof.

Since
g
is a minimizer, it must saturate the chain of inequalities in the proof of Theorem
15
:

F(g)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{e=(u,v)\in E}w(e)\mathbf{1}_{g_{u}(x)\neq g_{v}(x)}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in X}\mathcal{A}_{x}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in X}\mathcal{A}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q})

(3.66)

In particular this chain of inequalities has to be tight for each
x\in X
.

Fix some
x\in X
. Let
s_{x}:V\to X
be a labeling defined by

s_{x}(v)=g_{v}(x).

(3.67)

By Remark
18
, saturation of (
3.66
) requires
s_{x}
to be the labeling associated to the unique minimal multiway partition
\{\Gamma_{k}\}
, i.e.

s_{x}(v)=g_{i}(x),\hskip 10.00002ptv\in\Gamma_{i},

(3.68)

which implies that for any two vertices
u,v
in the same part of the multiway partition
\{\Gamma_{k}\}

g_{u}(x)=g_{v}(x),\hskip 10.00002ptu,v\in\Gamma_{i}.

(3.69)

Since this is true for all
x
, we must have

g(u)=g(v),\hskip 10.00002ptu,v\in\Gamma_{i}.

(3.70)

Now take
u\in R_{i}
. Since
g
is admissible,
g(u)=g_{i}
. This proves that

g(v)=g_{i},\hskip 10.00002ptv\in\Gamma_{i}.

(3.71)

∎

When the minimal multiway partition is not unique, then each of such partition gives rise to a solution of the multi-entropy minimization problem. The question is whether these solutions constitute all the minimizers of the problem. This problem, as well as several examples, will be discussed in the next section.

4
Minimizer count and examples

In this section we count the number and study the nature of the minimizers of the function
F
. We discuss some simple examples that allow for minimizers which take values outside the set of terminal permutations. We emphasize that trees are easy to analyze and we study the simplest non-tree network with 3 terminals, the triangle network.

4.1
Counting minimizers in the general case

We now describe all minimizers in the connected case. Throughout this
subsection we keep
n=2
, assume that
G
is connected and that all edge
weights are strictly positive. We write

\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{A}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q}).

(4.1)

Let
e_{1},\cdots,e_{q-1}
be the standard generators of

X=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{q-1}
and put
e_{q}=0
, so that

g_{i}(x)=x+e_{i},\hskip 10.00002pti=1,\cdots,q,

(4.2)

with
g_{q}=\id
. We also write
\operatorname{Min}(F)
for the set of
admissible minimizers of (
3.37
); the goal of this
subsection is to provide a counting formula for the number of minimizers. We start with the precise necessary and sufficient conditions for equality in Thm.
15
.

Proposition 21
.

An admissible labeling
g:V\to\Sym(X)
minimizes (
3.37
) if
and only if the following two conditions hold.

(1)

For every edge
e=(u,v)\in E
, the relative permutation

g(u)^{-1}g(v)
satisfies

(g(u)^{-1}g(v))^{2}=\id.

(4.3)

(2)

For every
x\in X
, the slice

s_{x}(v)=g(v)(x)

(4.4)

is terminal-valued on a minimal multiway partition: there exists

V=\Gamma_{1}^{x}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\Gamma_{q}^{x}
with area
\mathcal{A}

such that

s_{x}(v)=g_{i}(x),\hskip 10.00002ptv\in\Gamma_{i}^{x}.

(4.5)

Proof.

Write
g_{u}=g(u)
and
g_{v}=g(v)
. For every edge
e=(u,v)
, Lemma

9
gives

d(g_{u},g_{v})\geq\frac{1}{2}|\{x\in X:g_{u}(x)\neq g_{v}(x)\}|.

(4.6)

Summing over all edges and exchanging the order of summation,

\displaystyle F(g)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e=(u,v)\in E}w(e)|\{x\in X:g_{u}(x)\neq g_{v}(x)\}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{e=(u,v)\in E}w(e)\mathbf{1}_{g_{u}(x)\neq g_{v}(x)}.

(4.7)

For fixed
x
, the boundary values of
s_{x}(v)=g(v)(x)
are

g_{i}(x)
on
R_{i}
. These
q
values are pairwise distinct for

n=2
. Applying Lemma
17
to the slice
s_{x}
, we get

\sum_{e=(u,v)\in E}w(e)\mathbf{1}_{g_{u}(x)\neq g_{v}(x)}\geq\mathcal{A}.

(4.8)

Therefore

F(g)\geq\frac{|X|}{2}\mathcal{A}=2^{q-2}\mathcal{A}.

(4.9)

By Theorem
15
, this is the minimum value.

If
g
is a minimizer, every nonnegative error in the two inequalities
above must vanish. Since all edge weights are positive, equality in
(
4.6
) holds for every edge. By the proof of
Lemma
9
, this is equivalent to every nontrivial cycle of

g(u)^{-1}g(v)
having length
2
, or equivalently

(g(u)^{-1}g(v))^{2}=\id
. This gives condition

(1)
.

Equality in the slice lower bound holds for every
x\in X
. Remark

18
then shows that each slice is
terminal-valued on a minimal multiway partition. This gives condition

(2)
.

Conversely, if conditions
(1)
and

(2)
hold, then every edge inequality
(
4.6
) is an equality and every slice has
disagreement cost
\mathcal{A}
. Hence

F(g)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in X}\mathcal{A}=2^{q-2}\mathcal{A},

(4.10)

so
g
is a minimizer.
∎

Since we are considering in this section the case of arbitrary tensor networks where the minimal multiway cut might not be unique, we introduce next the set of all such minimal cuts.

Definition 22
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
be the set of
minimal multiway cut labelings

\lambda:V\to\{1,\cdots,q\},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda(v)=i\hskip 10.00002pt(v\in R_{i}),

(4.11)

whose cut area is
\mathcal{A}
. Equivalently,
\lambda
records a
minimal multiway partition by

\Gamma_{i}=\lambda^{-1}(i).

(4.12)

For each pair
i\neq j
, define the terminal-geodesic matching

\mathcal{P}_{ij}:=\{\{x,x+e_{i}+e_{j}\}:x\in X\}.

(4.13)

This is exactly the set of transpositions appearing in the disjoint
cycle decomposition of
g_{i}^{-1}g_{j}
. Consequently the Cayley
geodesic interval
[g_{i},g_{j}]
consists of the permutations obtained
from
g_{i}
by choosing an arbitrary subcollection of the pairs in

\mathcal{P}_{ij}
and applying those transpositions.

The next theorem is the main result of this section, characterizing
exactly the set of minimizers in terms of two combinatorial conditions on
functions
X\to\mathfrak{M}
. Thus, for each
x\in X
, we choose a
minimal multiway cut labeling

\lambda_{x}:V\to\{1,\cdots,q\}.

(4.14)

Equivalently,
\lambda_{x}
determines a partition of
V
into
q
blocks

\Gamma_{i}(x):=\lambda_{x}^{-1}(i),\hskip 20.00003pti=1,\cdots,q,

(4.15)

where the block
\Gamma_{i}(x)
contains the terminal region
R_{i}
. We
write

\Lambda=(\lambda_{x})_{x\in X}\in\mathfrak{M}^{X}

(4.16)

for the resulting family of minimal multiway cut labelings.

Theorem 23

(Characterization of minimizers)
.

For a family
\Lambda=(\lambda_{x})_{x\in X}\in\mathfrak{M}^{X}
, define
pointwise maps

g_{v}^{\Lambda}(x):=g_{\lambda_{x}(v)}(x)=x+e_{\lambda_{x}(v)}.

(4.17)

Then the maps
g_{v}^{\Lambda}
define an admissible minimizer

g^{\Lambda}:V\to\Sym(X)
if and only if the following two combinatorial
conditions hold:

(V)

For every vertex
v
, the map

x\longmapsto x+e_{\lambda_{x}(v)}

(4.18)

is a bijection of
X
. Equivalently, for every
y\in X
,

|\{i\in\{1,\cdots,q\}:\lambda_{y+e_{i}}(v)=i\}|=1.

(4.19)

(E)

For every edge
e=(u,v)\in E
, every
x\in X
, and

i=\lambda_{x}(u),\hskip 10.00002ptj=\lambda_{x}(v),

(4.20)

one has

\lambda_{x+e_{i}+e_{j}}(u)=i,\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda_{x+e_{i}+e_{j}}(v)=j.

(4.21)

Equivalently, condition
(E)
says that, along
the graph edge
(u,v)
, the relative permutation

(g_{u}^{\Lambda})^{-1}g_{v}^{\Lambda}
is a product of selected
transpositions from the terminal geodesics
[g_{i},g_{j}]
.

Consequently the number of minimizers is given by

|\operatorname{Min}(F)|=|\{\Lambda\in\mathfrak{M}^{X}\,:\,\Lambda\text{ satisfies \ref{item:compatible-vertex} and \ref{item:compatible-edge}}\}|.

(4.22)

Proof.

First let
g\in\operatorname{Min}(F)
. By Proposition

21
, for every
x\in X
the slice

s_{x}(v)=g(v)(x)
is terminal-valued on a minimal multiway partition.
Since the values
g_{1}(x),\cdots,g_{q}(x)
are pairwise distinct, there
is a unique
\lambda_{x}\in\mathfrak{M}
such that

g(v)(x)=g_{\lambda_{x}(v)}(x),\hskip 10.00002ptv\in V.

(4.23)

Thus
g=g^{\Lambda}
for the family

\Lambda=(\lambda_{x})_{x\in X}
. Condition

(V)
holds because
each
g(v)
is a permutation of
X
.

We now derive condition
(E)
. Fix an edge

e=(u,v)
and put
f=g(u)
,
h=g(v)
. Proposition
21
gives
(f^{-1}h)^{2}=\id
. Fix
x\in X
and write
i=\lambda_{x}(u)
,

. If
i=j
, then (
4.21
) is immediate.
Suppose
i\neq j
, and let

. Then

(4.24)

Write
a=\lambda_{y}(u)
,

. Since

. Since

(4.25)

We now use the simplex property of

which is exactly (

).

Conversely, suppose
\Lambda\in\mathfrak{M}^{X}
satisfies
conditions
(V)
and

(E)
. By condition

, each

is a permutation of

X
. Since every

has

\lambda_{x}(v)=k
on

is admissible.
Each slice is terminal-valued on the minimal cut labeling

It remains to check condition

Fix an edge

and put

Since

Thus

The formula (

∎

4.2
Counting minimizers for three-terminal trees

The tree example above is an instance of a closed formula for weighted
three-terminal trees. In this subsection we specialize to
q=3
and
X=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
,
and assume that the terminal regions are singleton vertices
R_{i}=\{t_{i}\}
,
i=1,2,3
. Terminal-free branches attached to the minimal subtree spanning

t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}
never create choices in a minimizer: since all edge
weights are positive, the labels on such a branch must be constant and
equal to the label at the attachment point. Thus only the “terminal subtree” matters.

We first record the corresponding weighted one-vertex consequence of
Theorem
14
.

Corollary 25
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}>0
. For
h\in\Sym(X)
consider the quantity
F
from (
2.22
)

F(h)=a_{1}d(h,g_{1})+a_{2}d(h,g_{2})+a_{3}d(h,g_{3}).

(4.44)

If
a_{\max}=\max(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
, then

\min_{h\in\Sym(X)}F(h)=2(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}-a_{\max}),

(4.45)

and the minimizers are exactly
\{g_{k}\,:\,a_{k}=a_{\max}\}
.

We now consider the free energy to be minimized on an arm of the star graph.
The following lemma uses the term
geodesic subdivision
in the
following sense. If the path in the graph between two vertices
\sigma
and
\tau
consists of
\rho
edges, then a geodesic subdivision is a sequence

\sigma=\pi_{0}\to\pi_{1}\to\cdots\to\pi_{\rho}=\tau

(4.46)

whose total length is the distance between its endpoints:

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\rho}d(\pi_{\ell-1},\pi_{\ell})=d(\sigma,\tau).

(4.47)

Thus the intermediate labels subdivide a shortest path from
\sigma
to

\tau
in the Cayley graph of the symmetric group
\Sym_{n}
; some pieces may have length zero, corresponding to no label change across that edge. In what follows we shall consider the case where the permutation
\sigma^{-1}\tau
consists of a product of disjoint transpositions. The case where
\sigma
is the identity permutation and
\tau
is a full cycle in
\Sym_{n}
is very important in free probability theory, see
[
10
]
or
[
29
,
38
]
for some application to random tensor network theory.

Lemma 26
.

Let
P=(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})
be a path with positive edge weights, and fix endpoint labels

h(v_{0})=\sigma,\hskip 20.00003pth(v_{m})=\tau.

(4.48)

Consider the minimal weight on the path and its multiplicity:

\mu=\min_{1\leq r\leq m}w(v_{r-1},v_{r}),\hskip 20.00003pt\rho=|\{r\,:\,w(v_{r-1},v_{r})=\mu\}|.

(4.49)

Then the minimum possible path energy is

\mu\,d(\sigma,\tau).

(4.50)

The minimizing labelings are obtained as follows: labels are constant
across every edge of weight larger than
\mu
, and across the
\rho

minimum-weight edges they form a geodesic subdivision

\sigma=\pi_{0}\to\pi_{1}\to\cdots\to\pi_{\rho}=\tau

(4.51)

satisfying

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\rho}d(\pi_{\ell-1},\pi_{\ell})=d(\sigma,\tau).

(4.52)

Let
\mathsf{G}_{\rho}(\sigma,\tau)
denote the number of such geodesic
subdivisions. If
\sigma^{-1}\tau
is a product of
D
disjoint
transpositions, then

\mathsf{G}_{\rho}(\sigma,\tau)=\rho^{D}.

(4.53)

Proof.

For any labeling of the path,

\sum_{r=1}^{m}w(v_{r-1},v_{r})d(h(v_{r-1}),h(v_{r}))\geq\mu\sum_{r=1}^{m}d(h(v_{r-1}),h(v_{r}))\geq\mu\,d(\sigma,\tau),

minimizers.

If

a Cayley geodesic from

disjoint and commute, the intermediate labels are determined by these

∎

4.4
A sufficient condition for terminal-valued minimizers

We now record a simple condition under which all the minimizers are terminal-valued, and thus in bijection with the set
\mathfrak{M}
of minimal multiway cuts. Recall that

,

is connected and that all edge weights are strictly positive. Let us introduce the set of oriented graph edges

\overrightarrow{E}:=\{(u,v):(u,v)\in E\}.

(4.73)

For

and distinct terminal labels

\tau_{e_{b}-e_{a}}
are exactly the affine lines

x+\langle e_{b}-e_{a}\rangle,\hskip 20.00003ptx\in X.

(5.5)

The geodesic fact above therefore implies that
\beta
preserves each
such affine line. Equivalently, for every
x\in X
,

\alpha(x)-x\in e_{a}+\langle e_{b}-e_{a}\rangle=\{(1-t)e_{a}+te_{b}:t\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\}.

(5.6)

Now suppose first that the two unordered pairs share exactly one
terminal. Relabeling the three indices, it is enough to consider

\{i,j\}
and
\{i,k\}
, with
i,j,k
distinct. If

\alpha\in[g_{i},g_{j}]\cap[g_{i},g_{k}]
, then
(
5.6
) gives, for every
x
,

\alpha(x)-x\in\bigl(e_{i}+\langle e_{j}-e_{i}\rangle\bigr)\cap\bigl(e_{i}+\langle e_{k}-e_{i}\rangle\bigr).

(5.7)

This intersection is the single point
\{e_{i}\}
. To see this, write

(1-t)e_{i}+te_{j}=(1-s)e_{i}+se_{k},\hskip 20.00003pts,t\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}.

(5.8)

Since the vectors
e_{1},\ldots,e_{q-1},e_{q}=0
are the vertices of the
standard simplex, comparing the coordinates indexed by

\{i,j,k\}\setminus\{q\}
gives
s=t=0
. Thus

\alpha(x)=x+e_{i}=g_{i}(x)
for every
x
, and hence

\alpha=g_{i}
. This proves

[g_{i},g_{j}]\cap[g_{i},g_{k}]=\{g_{i}\}
, and the same argument gives the
stated singleton
\{g_{m}\}
for any common terminal
m
.

Finally suppose that
\{i,j\}\cap\{k,\ell\}=\varnothing
. If

\alpha
belonged to both intervals, then for every
x
the
displacement
\alpha(x)-x
would lie in

\bigl(e_{i}+\langle e_{j}-e_{i}\rangle\bigr)\cap\bigl(e_{k}+\langle e_{\ell}-e_{k}\rangle\bigr).

(5.9)

This intersection is empty. Indeed an equality

(1-t)e_{i}+te_{j}=(1-s)e_{k}+se_{\ell}

(5.10)

would give two affine combinations of the simplex vertices with total
coefficient
1
. Comparing the coordinates of

e_{1},\ldots,e_{q-1}
, and then using the total coefficient, gives
uniqueness of these affine coordinates. This is impossible because
the two unordered pairs of vertices are disjoint. Hence the two
intervals are disjoint.
∎

5.1
The single tensor minimizer

Recall that to find the saddle of a single random tensor, we need to optimize the following quantity

\min_{g\in\Sym(X)}F(g)=\min_{g\in\Sym(X)}\sum_{i=1}^{q}d(g,g_{i})

(5.11)

where we set all edge weights to be equal to 1 and we consider
X=\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{q-1}
for any
q
and
n>2
in this section.

If the multiway cut conjecture holds as in Section
3
, then
\{g_{1},\dots,g_{q}\}
is expected to be the set of minimizers for the free energy and one can obtain the following values for the free energy

F(g_{1})=\sum_{i=1}^{q}d(g_{1},g_{i})=(q-1)n^{q-2}(n-1)

(5.12)

However, the above value cannot always be the minimum value of the free energy for
n>2
. We propose the following counterexample.

Definition 36
.

Let the reflection permutation
\pi\in\Sym(X)
(see Fig.
8
right panel) be defined as

\pi(x)=-x\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x\in X

(5.13)

The Cayley distances from
\pi
to the terminal permutations depend on the parity of
n
. If
n
is odd,

d(\pi,g_{i})=\frac{n^{q-1}-1}{2},\hskip 20.00003pt1\leq i\leq q.

(5.14)

If
n
is even,

d(\pi,g_{i})=\frac{n^{q-1}}{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for }1\leq i<q,\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ and }\hskip 20.00003ptd(\pi,\id)=\frac{n^{q-1}-2^{q-1}}{2}.

(5.15)

Therefore

F(\pi)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}d(\pi,g_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{q(n^{q-1}-1)}{2}&\text{if $n$ is odd}\\
\dfrac{qn^{q-1}-2^{q-1}}{2}&\text{if $n$ is even}.\end{array}\right.

(5.16)

Remark 37
.

We emphasize that
F(\pi)\leq F(g_{1})
with the equality holding only when
q=2
(the bipartite case, corresponding to the Rényi entropy, discussed extensively in the literature),
n=2
(discussed in the previous sections), or
(q,n)=(3,3)
(can be shown by enumerating all possible permutations). Hence, the multiway cut conjecture is false for the other choices of
(q,n)
.

In fact, one can obtain the reflection permutation by solving for a set of conditions that force the bound suggested in Lemma
9
to be saturated.

Lemma 38
.

We have
d(g,g_{i})=\frac{1}{2}m(gg_{i}^{-1})
for all
i=1,\dots,q
if and only if

g=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{q-1}g_{i}^{k_{i}}\right)\pi\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for some integers $k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}$}.

(5.17)

Proof.

Using Lemma
9
, we find that
d(g,g_{i})=d(gg_{i}^{-1},\id)=\frac{1}{2}m(gg_{i}^{-1})
for all
i
if and only if

(gg_{1}^{-1})^{2}=\dots=(gg_{q-1}^{-1})^{2}=g^{2}=\id

(5.18)

where we use
g_{q}=\id
. Picking a particular
i\in\{1,\dots,q-1\}
, then

\begin{split}(gg_{i}^{-1})^{2}=\id,\quad g^{2}=\id\iff&gg_{i}^{-1}g^{-1}=g_{i},\quad g=g^{-1}\\
\iff&g(x-e_{i})=g(x)+e_{i}\quad\forall x\in X,\quad g=g^{-1}\end{split}

(5.19)

Note that from above, the
j
-th (
j\neq i
) component of
g(x)
is the same as that of
g(x-e_{i})
. Hence, the
j
-th component of
g(x)
is independent from the
i
-th component of
x
and the permutation
g
which satisfies Eq.(
5.18
) can be decomposed as follows:

g(x_{1},\dots,x_{q-1})=(\sigma_{1}(x_{1}),\dots,\sigma_{q-1}(x_{q-1}))\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where $\sigma_{i}\in\Sym_{n}$ for all $i=1,\dots,q-1$}

(5.20)

Eq.(
5.18
) is then equivalent to

\sigma_{i}^{2}=(\sigma_{i}\tau_{n}^{-1})^{2}=\id\hskip 10.00002pt\forall i=1,\dots,q-1

(5.21)

where
\tau_{n}=(1\dots n)
. Observe that

\begin{split}\sigma_{i}^{2}=(\sigma_{i}\tau_{n}^{-1})^{2}=\id\iff&\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i}^{-1},\quad\sigma_{i}\tau_{n}^{-1}\sigma_{i}^{-1}=\tau_{n}\\
\iff&\exists k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}:\sigma_{i}(x_{i})=k_{i}-x_{i}\quad\forall x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\\
\iff&\exists k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}:\sigma_{i}(x_{i})=\tau_{n}^{k_{i}}\pi(x_{i})\\
\iff&\sigma_{i}\in\{\tau_{n}^{k_{i}}\pi:k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\}\end{split}

(5.22)

Hence, it follows that

g=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{q-1}g_{i}^{k_{i}}\right)\pi\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for some integers $k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}$}

(5.23)

since
g_{i}^{k_{i}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i},\dots,x_{q-1})=(x_{1},\dots,\tau_{n}^{k_{i}}(x_{i}),\dots,x_{q-1})
.
∎

Among the set of solutions obtained above, the free energy may take different values but is always minimized when
g=\pi
. We have pointed out that the free energy
F(\pi)
cannot be greater than
F(g_{1})
, where the latter is the lower bound of the free energy suggested by the multiway cut conjecture, and, recalling Sec.
3.1
, we have shown that a minimizer for the free energy when
n=2
is obtained if and only if the lower bound in Lemma
9
is always saturated. Hence, it seems reasonable to say that:

Conjecture 39
.

The reflection permutation
\pi
gives the true minimum for all
(q,n)
, i.e.

\min_{g\in\Sym(X)}F(g)=F(\pi)

(5.24)

The structure of the minimizer from the conjecture above became apparent to us after running the
SkyDiscover EvoX
optimization pipeline for algorithmic discovery
[
52
]
.
As a consistency check, we have also numerically verified that Conjecture
39
is true for small
(q,n)
up to
(q,n)=(3,4)
.

5.2
Implication for RTNs

In this subsection, we touch on the implication of the existence of the replica-symmetry-breaking (RSB) element
\pi
on more general RTNs, along the lines of Ref.
[
1
]
.
We consider RTNs defined on an isometric tiling on a two-dimensional Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
(with boundary). We will also assume that the tiling is dense enough so that one can approximate the area of minimal cuts by lengths of the corresponding geodesics in
\mathcal{M}
.
The most interesting case is perhaps when one takes
\mathcal{M}
to be the hyperbolic Poincaré disk (e.g. Figure
1
), under which the RTN serves as a toy model for the standard AdS
3
/CFT
2
dictionary in the vacuum setting.
However we stress that our result in this section is valid for any two dimensional manifold
\mathcal{M}
, and we expect that it can be readily generalized to higher dimensions.

At first glance, the failure of the multiway cut conjecture for a single random tensor does not necessarily imply the failure of the multiway cut conjecture in arbitrary RTNs.
However, we will see that the existence of the element
\pi
allows one to construct solutions with lower energy than the minimal multiway cut configuration, provided that the Rényi index
n
is large enough and the multiway cut has non-trivial intersections.

In the case where there exists a nontrivial trivalent junction
p
for the minimal multiway cut, we consider two different configurations where they differ only near the vicinity of
p
: (left) The replica-symmetric saddle given by the minimal multiway cut. (right) The RSB saddle obtained by replacing a small triangular patch of elements near the junction to
\pi
.

To see how this works, consider first the tripartite case
q=3
. Suppose that we are given a minimal cut configuration
g
of
\mathcal{M}
such that the three domains meet at a Y junction for least one point
p
in the bulk.
The interface between different domains must intersect at an equal angle of
2\pi/3
, see Figure
9
(left).

To see why, note that the multiway problem can be thought equally as the problem of finding the lowest energy condition of a tensioned “soap film” supported on wireframe defined on the boundary partitions. The equal-angle condition then follows from the force balance condition of the film at point
p
.

Now zoom in around
p
. Locally, one can always approximate the metric in the neighborhood of
p
by the flat metric. We consider an alternative configuration of
g
where it agrees with the minimal cut configuration except for the region near
p
, where it contains a small triangular patch of the RSB element
\pi
, as shown in Figure
9
(right). The shape of the triangle depends on whether
n
is even or odd which we discuss separately below.

Suppose that
n
is odd. Then
d(\pi,g_{i})=(n^{2}-1)/2
for
i=1,2,3
. The
\pi
patch is an equilateral triangle because
\pi
is equidistant to all the terminal permutations. Suppose that the
\pi
triangle has side lengths of
1
, the local contribution to the free energy for the Y junction and the
\pi
solution can be worked out using simple trigonometry:

F^{(3)}_{Y}=\sqrt{3}(n^{2}-n),\hskip 10.00002ptF^{(3)}_{\pi}=\frac{3}{2}(n^{2}-1).

(5.25)

One can easily check that
F^{(3)}_{Y}>F^{(3)}_{\pi}
for any odd integer
n\geq 7
.

Now let
n
be even. Then
d(\pi,g_{i})=n^{2}/2
for
i=1,2
and
d(\pi,g_{3})=d(\pi,\id)=(n^{2}-4)/2
. Since the distances are not equal in this case, the shape of the
\pi
patch is an isosceles triangle instead. Suppose that the base of the
\pi
triangle has length
1
and the legs have length
a
. Then

F^{(3)}_{Y}(a)=\left(\sqrt{a^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)(n^{2}-n),\hskip 10.00002ptF^{(3)}_{\pi}(a)=\frac{1}{2}\left((1+2a)n^{2}-4\right).

(5.26)

Maximizing the difference over
a
one finds that
a_{\max}=\frac{n}{2\sqrt{2n-1}}
and

F^{(3)}_{Y}=\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2n-1}}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)(n^{2}-n),\hskip 10.00002ptF^{(3)}_{\pi}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(1+\frac{n}{\sqrt{2n-1}}\right)n^{2}-4\right).

(5.27)

Similarly, one can check that
F^{(3)}_{Y}>F^{(3)}_{\pi}
for any even integer
n\geq 6
. Therefore we conclude that for
q=3
, the RSB saddle dominates for all integer
n\geq 6
.

We now deal with the higher-partite cases
q>3
. Consider a configuration
g
associated to a multiway cut of
\mathcal{M}
. The minimal multiway cut for higher partite settings are given in terms of
Steiner trees

Consider any such trivalent vertex
p
in the tree (provided that
p

As before, we replace a small patch of permutations near
p
by
\pi
and denote the three neighboring permutations
g_{1},g_{2}
and
g_{3}
. Without loss of generality we can assume
g_{3}=\id
.

11

11

This is equivalent to translating
\pi^{\prime}\to\pi^{\prime}g_{3}^{-1}
in the computation of the free energy.
Consider the permutation element
\pi^{\prime}
where

\pi^{\prime}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\cdots,x_{q-1})=(-x_{1},-x_{2},x_{3},\cdots,x_{q-1}).

(5.28)

In other words,
\pi^{\prime}
only reflects the first two coordinates of
x
and keeps all the others invariant. Then

d(\pi^{\prime},g_{i})=n^{q-3}\frac{n^{2}-1}{2},\hskip 10.00002pt{i=1,2,3}

(5.29)

for odd

, and

\displaystyle d(\pi^{\prime},g_{1,2})=\frac{n^{q-1}}{2},\hskip 10.00002ptd(\pi^{\prime},g_{3})=d(\pi^{\prime},\id)=n^{q-3}\frac{n^{2}-4}{2}

(5.30)

for even

\pi^{\prime}
gives lower energy than the full reflector

q=3

\displaystyle F^{(q)}_{Y}=n^{q-3}F^{(3)}_{Y},\hskip 10.00002ptF^{(q)}_{\pi^{\prime}}=n^{q-3}F^{(3)}_{\pi},\hskip 10.00002ptq>3.

(5.31)

One immediately sees that
F^{(q)}_{Y}>F^{(q)}_{\pi^{\prime}}
for integer

.
Therefore we arrive at the following conclusion:

Proposition 40

(informal)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be two-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
\psi
be an RTN state associated to a (dense enough) isometric tiling on
\mathcal{M}
.
If
\partial\mathcal{M}=R_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup R_{q}
is a
q
-partite (
q>2
) partition of the boundary such that the minimal multiway cut has at least one trivalent junction, then the Rényi multi-entropy
S^{(q)}_{n}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{q})_{\psi}
does
not
equal to the area of the minimal multiway cut for any integer
n\geq 6
.

In the case where the underlying metric space is hyperbolic, the size of the
\pi
patch is finite and is controlled by the local curvature around point
p
. The appearance of the element
\pi
is thus local in nature, whose effect can be thought of as replacing the plain vertices in the minimal geodesic network by corresponding “dressed” vertices, each contributing an negative energy that depends on the local curvature.

We emphasis that the our results in this subsection only guarantees the existence for a RSB saddle for
n\geq 6
. For
2<n<6
, our construction does not give a more favorable solution, but this does not exclude the possibility for such saddles. For example, if Conjecture
39
happens to be false and there exists a lower energy minimizer (say
\sigma
) for the single random tensor, then one can replace
\pi^{\prime}\to\sigma
in our construction and we expect the bound can be further lowered. We also expect the existence of “global ” RSB solutions not considered here. The existence of such saddles depends on the underlying geometry and requires an individual analysis for each separate case.

6
Discussion

In this paper we started with the simple goal of understanding multi-entropy in random tensor networks. What we have found is nonetheless surprising: the nature of the solution is drastically different depending on the Rényi index
n
: For
n=2
the multi-entropy is given by the minimum multiway cut. For
n>2
this is not true — the existence of the special element
\pi
spoils the replica symmetry and renders the multiway cut saddle subdominant. Below we comment on several aspects related to our work.

The specialness of
n=2

The key property that makes our proof work for the
n=2
case is that the terminal permutations are all products of two-element transpositions. This allows the one to simultaneously saturate the estimates given by the number of moved points (Lemma
9
) and the incompatibility condition (i.e.
g(x)=g_{i}(x)
for at most one
i
).
For higher
n
it is not possible to simultaneously saturate both estimates, but both our Conjecture
39
and numerics seem to suggest that the saturation of Lemma
9
is more important.

It is also interesting to compare our results to the full gravitational counterpart
[
30
]
: In both RTN and gravity calculations, we see explicit breakdown of replica symmetry for higher Rényi indices. In both cases
n=2
seems to be special in the sense that it is the only Rényi index that admits full replica symmetric saddles (in the multipartite case
q\geq 3
). In RTN this saddle allows us to directly recast the
n=2
Rényi multi-entropy in terms of the minimal multiway cut area. In gravity the situation is more complicated, since the bulk solution involves cosmic branes
[
25
,
23
]
, and one must account for the backreaction to the background geometry.
However it seems that this is as close as one can get to a geometric dual in the same flavor of the RT formula.

Our result provides concrete evidence for the existence of genuine multi-partite entanglement for (holographic) RTN states. In Refs.
[
42
,
47
,
41
]
, it was argued that most holographic states necessarily contain large (
O(1/G_{N})
) amounts of genuine multi-partite entanglement. The argument typically involves a bulk evaluation of the
q
-partite
genuine multi-entropy

[
47
]
, a family of probes built from linear combinations of (Rényi) multi-entropies that are able to detect the existence of genuine multi-partite entanglement. The evaluation features extensive use of the multiway cut conjecture for the multi-entropy. In gravity, this requires the questionable
n\to 1
analytic continuation. One can instead work with
n=2
, but the calculation becomes difficult because of the back-reaction from the cosmic branes. However for RTN states, as the multiway cut conjecture has been proven for
n=2
, the argument simply goes through and it is possible to rigorously demonstrate that RTN states possess large amounts of genuine multipartite entanglement.

Other entanglement measures with multiway cut property

The multi-entropy quantity is formulated as a multi-invariant of a tensor defined via the terminal permutations
g_{1},\ldots,g_{q}
from Definition
5
. The permutations
g_{i}
are equidistant and satisfy a special property where for every
i\neq j
, the relative permutation
g^{-1}_{i}g_{j}
is a
fixed-point-free involution
on
X
.
In fact, it is possible to work with different families of twist operators
\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{p}
. As long as the fixed-point-free property is still satisfied by the new family
12
12

12

One can verify that the equidistant condition follows if one requires
\sigma_{i}^{-1}\sigma_{j}
to be a fixed-point-free involution for all pairs
i\neq j
.
, the conclusion of our main result, namely Theorem
15
, will still be valid. Stated more precisely:

Proposition 41
.

Let
p>2
be an integer and
\Sigma=(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{p}):\sigma_{i}\in\Sym(X)
be a family of permutations. Consider the problem of determining the multi-invariant
\mathcal{E}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{p})
on an RTN state defined on a graph
G=(V,E)
(with weight
w:V\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
) with boundary vertices
R_{i},\cdots,R_{p}\subset V
:

\displaystyle\min_{g:V\to\Sym(X)}F_{\Sigma}(g),\hskip 10.00002ptF_{\Sigma}(g):=\sum_{e=(u,v)\in E}w(e)d(g(u),g(v)),

(6.1)

subject to

g(v)=\sigma_{i},\hskip 10.00002ptv\in R_{i}.

(6.2)

If for every pair
i\neq j
, the relative permutation
\sigma_{i}^{-1}\sigma_{j}
is a
fixed-point-free involution
, i.e.

\sigma_{i}^{-1}\sigma_{j}(x)\neq x~\forall x\in X\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt(\sigma_{i}^{-1}\sigma_{j})^{2}=\id.

(6.3)

Then the minimal value of
F_{\Sigma}
is given by the multiway cut across
G
:

\displaystyle\min_{g}F_{\Sigma}(g)=\frac{|X|}{2}\mathcal{A}(R_{1}:\cdots:R_{p})

(6.4)

Moreover, a minimizer is obtained by taking any minimum multiway partition
V=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\Gamma_{p}
and setting

g(v)=\sigma_{i},\hskip 10.00002ptv\in\Gamma_{i}.

(6.5)

The proof of the proposition is left as an exercise for the reader.

As an illustration, one can consider the whole family of translations on
X=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{q-1}
: for all
z\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{q-1}
, define the permutation
\sigma_{z}(x)=x+z
. This family of
2^{q-1}
permutations satisfies the property above: for all
z\neq w
,
\sigma_{z}^{-1}\sigma_{w}=\sigma_{z+w}
, which is a fixed-point-free involution on
X
. The multi-entropy case corresponds to a subfamily of size
q
.

Lastly, we stress that the equidistant condition alone is not sufficient to guarantee the result. For example, the twist operators for the
n=3
Rényi negativity

X=\{1,2,3\},\hskip 10.00002ptg_{1}=(123),\hskip 10.00002ptg_{2}=(321),\hskip 10.00002ptg_{3}=\id

(6.6)

are equidistant since
d(g_{1},g_{2})=d(g_{2},g_{3})=d(g_{3},g_{1})=2
. However, the relative permutations
g_{i}^{-1}g_{j}
are all 3-cycles so the involutive condition fails. Indeed, the value of the Rényi negativity is not given by a multiway cut, see e.g. (
2.28
