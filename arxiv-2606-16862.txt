Quantum uniformity norms are pullbacks of matrix-valued uniformity norms
Asgar Jamneshan

Abstract
. We show that the quantum uniformity norms recently introduced by Bu, Gu, and Jaffe are the pullbacks, under the Weyl orbit embedding, of the matrix-valued uniformity norms of Gowers and Hatami. This identification yields the Gowers–Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the triangle inequality for the quantum uniformity norms, answering a question of Bu, Gu, and Jaffe. In the extremal regime, it describes the Clifford levels of Gottesman and Chuang in terms of certain unitary-valued Leibman polynomial maps on finite vector spaces.

The Clifford hierarchy was introduced by Gottesman and Chuang
[
4
]
and plays an important role in fault-tolerant quantum computation. It is the nested sequence
\mathcal{C}^{(1)}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{(2)}\subseteq\cdots
defined recursively by taking
\mathcal{C}^{(1)}
to be the Pauli group and, for
k\geq 2
,
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}=\{U:U\mathcal{C}^{(1)}U^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{(k-1)}\}
. Thus
\mathcal{C}^{(2)}
is the Clifford group, while already in the qubit case the higher levels need not be groups, starting at the third level
[
1
]
.
Bu, Gu, and Jaffe
[
3
]
introduced quantum higher-order Fourier analysis and the associated quantum uniformity measures, and showed that the Clifford hierarchy is characterized by their extremal values. We recall all definitions in Section
1
.

The purpose of this note is to record the simple observation that these quantum uniformity norms are precisely pullbacks under the Weyl orbit embedding of the matrix-valued uniformity norms introduced by Gowers and Hatami
[
5
]
and further studied by Thom and the author
[
7
]
. This observation transfers, among other properties, the Gowers–Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the triangle inequality from the matrix-valued theory to the quantum setting. Bu, Gu, and Jaffe proved these inequalities for the second and third quantum uniformity norms in
[
3
, §9]
; in
[
3
, Remark 43]
they asked whether they hold in higher orders.
Since the extremal regime of the matrix-valued uniformity norms is described by Leibman polynomial maps
[
8
]
, the
k
th level of the
Clifford hierarchy is identified with those Weyl orbit maps that are unitary-valued Leibman polynomial maps of degree at most
k
.

Bu, Gu, and Jaffe also formulated an interesting inverse conjecture for the quantum uniformity norms; see
[
3
, Conjecture 9]
. This conjecture can be interpreted as a Clifford-hierarchy testing problem. It asks, roughly, whether a unitary with large quantum uniformity norm must have nontrivial correlation with a unitary in the corresponding level of the Clifford hierarchy.
It is not difficult to prove a polynomial
1\%
-inverse theorem for the second quantum uniformity norm, see, e.g.,
[
2
, §3]
.
Hinsche et al.
[
6
, Theorem 1.3]
proved a polynomial
1\%
-inverse theorem for the third quantum uniformity norm in the
n
-qubit setting. Bao et al.
[
2
]
proved the
99\%
-inverse theorem for the fourth quantum uniformity norm in the general
n
-qudit setting. Using the correspondence established here with the matrix-valued uniformity norms, we hope to report progress on the
99\%
-inverse theorem for the
k
th quantum uniformity norm for arbitrary
k
in future work.

We follow the conventions of

\tau_{p}

The symplectic form on

[a,b]=[(u,v),(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})]:=u\cdot v^{\prime}-u^{\prime}\cdot v.

There is a map
\beta:A\times A\to\mathbb{Z}/D_{p}\mathbb{Z}
such that

(1)

W_{a}W_{b}=\tau_{p}^{\beta(a,b)}W_{a+b},\qquad W_{a}W_{b}=\omega^{[a,b]}W_{b}W_{a}.

The Pauli group is

\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{P}(n,p):=\{\tau_{p}^{t}W_{a}:t\in\mathbb{Z}/D_{p}\mathbb{Z},\ a\in A\}.

Equivalently,

\mathcal{C}^{(k)}:=\{U\in\mathrm{U}(N):U\mathcal{C}^{(1)}U^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{(k-1)}\}.

. Let

Define lists of cube words

c_{k}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{k})=\bigl(c_{1,k}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}),\ldots,c_{2^{k},k}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{k})\bigr)

recursively by

c_{0}=(1),\qquad c_{1}(h_{1})=(h_{1},1),

and

c_{k+1}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{k+1})=\bigl(c_{k}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{k})h_{k+1},\overline{c_{k}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{k})}\bigr),

where multiplication by
h_{k+1}
is applied to each entry of the list and

\overline{c_{k}}

For functions

\Lambda_{k,G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{2^{k}}):=\mathbb{E}_{x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\in G}\left\langle\mathbf{1},\prod_{i=1}^{2^{k}}F_{i}\bigl(c_{i,k}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{k})x\bigr)^{[i]}\right\rangle,

B^{[i]}:=\begin{cases}B,&i\ \mathrm{odd},\\

Then

\Lambda_{k,G}(F,\ldots,F)=\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{U^{k}(G)}^{2^{k}}.

When

is written additively, we write

c_{i,k}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{k})+x

c_{i,k}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{k})x
.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group.

(i)

Gowers–Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.

Let
k\geq 1
and let

F_{1},\ldots,F_{2^{k}}:G\to\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. Then

\left|\Lambda_{k,G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{2^{k}})\right|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{2^{k}}\left\lVert F_{i}\right\rVert_{U^{k}(G)}.

(ii)

Monotonicity
: Let
k\geq 1
and let
F:G\to\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. Then

\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{U^{k}(G)}\leq\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{U^{k+1}(G)}.

(iii)

Norm properties
: Let
k\geq 1
and let
F_{1},F_{2}:G\to\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. Then

\left\lVert F_{1}+F_{2}\right\rVert_{U^{k}(G)}\leq\left\lVert F_{1}\right\rVert_{U^{k}(G)}+\left\lVert F_{2}\right\rVert_{U^{k}(G)}.

Moreover, for
k\geq 2
, the map

F\longmapsto\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{U^{k}(G)}

defines a norm on the vector space of functions

G\to\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
.

2.
The pullback identity and some consequences

Define
\alpha\colon A\times\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
by

\alpha_{a}(S):=W_{a}SW_{a}^{*},\qquad a\in A,S\in\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C}).

Since phases disappear under conjugation, it follows from (
1
) that

(2)

\alpha_{a}\alpha_{b}=\alpha_{a+b}.

Thus
\alpha
defines a trace-preserving action of the additive group

A
on the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
by
*
-automorphisms.

For
S\in\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, we define its Weyl orbit map

(3)

F_{S}:A\to\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C}),\qquad F_{S}(a):=\alpha_{a}(S).

Using this map, we establish the following correspondence between the quantum uniformity norms and the matrix-valued uniformity norms.

Theorem 2.1
.

For every
S\in\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
and every
k\geq 1
,

\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{Q^{k}}=\left\lVert F_{S}\right\rVert_{U^{k}(A)}.

Proof.

Define

\mathcal{T}:\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\to\{A\to\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\},\qquad\mathcal{T}(S)(x):=\alpha_{x}(S).

Then
F_{S}=\mathcal{T}(S)
. For
h,x\in A
and
S\in\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
,

\displaystyle(\Delta_{h}\mathcal{T}(S))(x)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(S)(h+x)\mathcal{T}(S)(x)^{*}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{h+x}(S)\alpha_{x}(S)^{*}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{x}(\alpha_{h}(S)S^{*})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(\partial_{h}S)(x).

Thus

\Delta_{h}\mathcal{T}(S)=\mathcal{T}(\partial_{h}S).

Iterating gives

(\Delta_{h_{k},\ldots,h_{1}}F_{S})(x)=\alpha_{x}\!\left(\partial_{h_{k},\ldots,h_{1}}S\right).

Taking normalized traces removes the outer conjugation:

\left\langle\mathbf{1},(\Delta_{h_{k},\ldots,h_{1}}F_{S})(x)\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathbf{1},\partial_{h_{k},\ldots,h_{1}}S\right\rangle.

Averaging over
x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}
proves the identity.
∎

We first make explicit the relation between the quantum multilinear
functional of Bu, Gu, and Jaffe and the matrix-valued Gowers multilinear forms defined in Section
1

Define an ordering

\sigma^{(k)}=\bigl(\sigma^{(k)}_{1},\ldots,\sigma^{(k)}_{2^{k}}\bigr)

of
\{0,1\}^{k}
recursively by

\sigma^{(0)}=(\varnothing),\qquad\sigma^{(1)}=((0),(1)),

and

\sigma^{(j+1)}=\bigl((0,\sigma^{(j)}_{1}),\ldots,(0,\sigma^{(j)}_{2^{j}}),(1,\sigma^{(j)}_{2^{j}}),\ldots,(1,\sigma^{(j)}_{1})\bigr).

Let
(T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}}\subseteq\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
.
For
j\geq 0
, define
\mathcal{D}_{j}
recursively by

\mathcal{D}_{0}(T_{\varnothing}):=T_{\varnothing}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{j+1}\bigl((T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{j+1}};h_{j+1},\ldots,h_{1}\bigr)

\displaystyle:=\alpha_{h_{j+1}}\!\left(\mathcal{D}_{j}\bigl((T_{(0,\eta)})_{\eta\in\{0,1\}^{j}};h_{j},\ldots,h_{1}\bigr)\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\mathcal{D}_{j}\bigl((T_{(1,\eta)})_{\eta\in\{0,1\}^{j}};h_{j},\ldots,h_{1}\bigr)^{*}.

\Lambda_{k}^{Q}\bigl((T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}}\bigr):=\mathbb{E}_{h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\in A}\left\langle\mathbf{1},\mathcal{D}_{k}\bigl((T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}};h_{k},\ldots,h_{1}\bigr)\right\rangle.

Lemma 2.2
.

For every
k\geq 1
and every family
(T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}}\subseteq\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, we have

\Lambda_{k}^{Q}\bigl((T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}}\bigr)=\Lambda_{k,A}\bigl(F_{T_{\sigma^{(k)}_{1}}},\ldots,F_{T_{\sigma^{(k)}_{2^{k}}}}\bigr),

where
F_{T}
denotes the Weyl orbit map (
3
).

Proof.

We first introduce an auxiliary recursive multilinear expression. For
matrix-valued functions

(F_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{j}},\qquad F_{\varepsilon}:A\to\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C}),

set

\mathcal{Q}_{0}(F_{\varnothing};x):=F_{\varnothing}(x),

and define

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{j+1}\bigl((F_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{j+1}};x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{j+1}\bigr)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{Q}_{j}\bigl((F_{(0,\eta)})_{\eta\in\{0,1\}^{j}};h_{j+1}+x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{j}\bigr)

\displaystyle\cdot\mathcal{Q}_{j}\bigl((F_{(1,\eta)})_{\eta\in\{0,1\}^{j}};x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{j}\bigr)^{*}.

We claim first that

(4)

\mathcal{Q}_{j}\bigl((F_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{j}};x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{j}\bigr)=\prod_{i=1}^{2^{j}}F_{\sigma_{i}^{(j)}}\bigl(c_{i,j}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{j})+x\bigr)^{[i]}.

This is immediate for
j=0
. Assume it holds at level
j
, and put

m=2^{j}
. By the recursive definition of
\mathcal{Q}_{j+1}
, the first

\prod_{i=1}^{m}F_{(0,\sigma_{i}^{(j)})}\bigl(c_{i,j}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{j})+h_{j+1}+x\bigr)^{[i]}.

The second block, after taking the adjoint, becomes

\prod_{r=1}^{m}F_{(1,\sigma_{m+1-r}^{(j)})}\bigl(c_{m+1-r,j}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{j})+x\bigr)^{[m+r]}.

Here the adjoint reverses the order of the product and flips the parity of
the exponent
[\,\cdot\,]
. Hence the recursive definitions of

c_{j+1}
and
\sigma^{(j+1)}
give exactly

\mathcal{Q}_{j+1}\bigl((F_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon};x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{j+1}\bigr)=\prod_{i=1}^{2^{j+1}}F_{\sigma_{i}^{(j+1)}}\bigl(c_{i,j+1}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{j+1})+x\bigr)^{[i]}.

This proves (
4
) for all
j
.

We next prove the Weyl-orbit identity

(5)

\mathcal{Q}_{j}\bigl((F_{T_{\varepsilon}})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{j}};x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{j}\bigr)=\alpha_{x}\!\left(\mathcal{D}_{j}\bigl((T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{j}};h_{j},\ldots,h_{1}\bigr)\right).

For
j=0
, this is immediate:

\mathcal{Q}_{0}(F_{T_{\varnothing}};x)=F_{T_{\varnothing}}(x)=\alpha_{x}(T_{\varnothing})=\alpha_{x}(\mathcal{D}_{0}(T_{\varnothing})).

Assume (
5
) holds at level
j
. Put

D_{0}:=\mathcal{D}_{j}\bigl((T_{(0,\eta)})_{\eta\in\{0,1\}^{j}};h_{j},\ldots,h_{1}\bigr),\qquad D_{1}:=\mathcal{D}_{j}\bigl((T_{(1,\eta)})_{\eta\in\{0,1\}^{j}};h_{j},\ldots,h_{1}\bigr).

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{j+1}\bigl((F_{T_{\varepsilon}})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{j+1}};x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{j+1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}_{j}\bigl((F_{T_{(0,\eta)}})_{\eta};h_{j+1}+x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{j}\bigr)\mathcal{Q}_{j}\bigl((F_{T_{(1,\eta)}})_{\eta};x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{j}\bigr)^{*}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{h_{j+1}+x}(D_{0})\,\alpha_{x}(D_{1})^{*}.

Since
\alpha
is an action by
*
-automorphisms,

\displaystyle\alpha_{h_{j+1}+x}(D_{0})\,\alpha_{x}(D_{1})^{*}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{x}(\alpha_{h_{j+1}}(D_{0}))\,\alpha_{x}(D_{1}^{*})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{x}\!\left(\alpha_{h_{j+1}}(D_{0})D_{1}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{x}\!\left(\mathcal{D}_{j+1}\bigl((T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{j+1}};h_{j+1},h_{j},\ldots,h_{1}\bigr)\right).

This proves (

) for all

.

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k,A}\bigl(F_{T_{\sigma^{(k)}_{1}}},\ldots,F_{T_{\sigma^{(k)}_{2^{k}}}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\in A}\left\langle\mathbf{1},\mathcal{Q}_{k}\bigl((F_{T_{\varepsilon}})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}};x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\bigr)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\in A}\left\langle\mathbf{1},\alpha_{x}\!\left(\mathcal{D}_{k}\bigl((T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}};h_{k},\ldots,h_{1}\bigr)\right)\right\rangle.

Since
\alpha
is trace-preserving,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k,A}\bigl(F_{T_{\sigma^{(k)}_{1}}},\ldots,F_{T_{\sigma^{(k)}_{2^{k}}}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\in A}\left\langle\mathbf{1},\mathcal{D}_{k}\bigl((T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}};h_{k},\ldots,h_{1}\bigr)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{k}^{Q}\bigl((T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}}\bigr).

This proves the desired identity.

We are ready to deduce the basic analytic properties of the quantum uniformity norms from their matrix-valued analogues.

Proposition 2.3
.

(i)

Quantum Gowers–Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.

Let
k\geq 1
and let

(T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}}\subseteq\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C}).

Then

\left|\Lambda_{k}^{Q}\bigl((T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}}\bigr)\right|\leq\prod_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}}\left\lVert T_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{Q^{k}}.

(ii)

Monotonicity
: Let
k\geq 1
and let

S\in\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. Then

\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{Q^{k}}\leq\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{Q^{k+1}}.

(iii)

Norm properties
: Let
k\geq 1
and let

S,T\in\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. Then

\left\lVert S+T\right\rVert_{Q^{k}}\leq\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{Q^{k}}+\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{Q^{k}}.

Moreover, for
k\geq 2
, the map

\mathrm{M}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\to[0,\infty),\qquad S\mapsto\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{Q^{k}},

defines a norm.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, Theorem
2.1
, and
Proposition
1.2
(i), we have

\displaystyle\left|\Lambda_{k}^{Q}\bigl((T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}}\bigr)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\Lambda_{k,A}\bigl(F_{T_{\sigma^{(k)}_{1}}},\ldots,F_{T_{\sigma^{(k)}_{2^{k}}}}\bigr)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{2^{k}}\left\lVert F_{T_{\sigma^{(k)}_{i}}}\right\rVert_{U^{k}(A)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{k}}\left\lVert T_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{Q^{k}}.

Similarly, Theorem
2.1
and
Proposition
1.2
(ii) give

\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{Q^{k}}=\left\lVert F_{S}\right\rVert_{U^{k}(A)}\leq\left\lVert F_{S}\right\rVert_{U^{k+1}(A)}=\left\lVert S\right\rVert_{Q^{k+1}}.

Finally, the Weyl orbit map
S\longmapsto F_{S}
is linear and injective, since
F_{S}(0)=S
. Therefore the triangle inequality
and the norm properties follow from
Proposition
1.2
(iii).
∎

We recall Leibman’s definition of polynomial maps between
groups
[
8
]
. A map
\phi:G\to H
is polynomial of degree at most

-1
if it is identically
1_{H}
. For
k\geq 0
, it is polynomial of degree at
most
k
if, for every
h\in G
, the discrete derivative

(\Delta_{h}\phi)(g):=\phi(hg)\phi(g)^{-1}

is polynomial of degree at most
k-1
.

We have the following equivalence between the extremizers of quantum and matrix-valued uniformity norms.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
U\in\mathrm{U}(N)
and let
k\geq 1
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{Q^{k}}=1
;

(ii)

the Weyl orbit map
F_{U}:A\to\mathrm{U}(N)

has Leibman degree at most
k-1
.

Consequently, for
k\geq 2
, these conditions are equivalent to

U\in\mathcal{C}^{(k-1)}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
, we have

\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{Q^{k}}=\left\lVert F_{U}\right\rVert_{U^{k}(A)}.

Since
F_{U}
is unitary-valued, every
k
-fold discrete derivative

\Delta_{h_{k}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{1}}F_{U}(x)

is again unitary. Therefore, for every
x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\in A
,

\left\langle\mathbf{1},\Delta_{h_{k}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{1}}F_{U}(x)\right\rangle

lies in the closed unit disk. Moreover, if
V\in\mathrm{U}(N)
, then
\left\langle\mathbf{1},V\right\rangle=1
if and only if
V=\mathbf{1}
.
Since the average of finitely many points in the closed unit disk is equal to
1
only when all points are equal to
1
, the defining average for
\left\lVert F_{U}\right\rVert_{U^{k}(A)}^{2^{k}}
is equal to
1
if and only if

\Delta_{h_{k}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{1}}F_{U}(x)=\mathbf{1}

for every
x,h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\in A
. By Leibman’s definition,
this is equivalent to
F_{U}
being polynomial of degree at most
k-1
.
This proves the equivalence of (i) and (ii). For
k\geq 2
, applying Theorem
1.1
with
k-1
gives
the final assertion.
∎
