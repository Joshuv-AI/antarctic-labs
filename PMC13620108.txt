High-yield reactor production of terbium-161 using thin-layer gadolinium-160 targets, simplified chloride based separation and radiolabeling of DOTA based ligands
Authors: Shakera Khatoon, Amna Javed, Qaiser Khan, Kaleem Abbas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTerbium-161 (161 Tb) is an emerging therapeutic radionuclide combining β⁻ emission with a high yield of Auger electrons and conversion electrons, enabling enhanced localized dose deposition. However, its widespread clinical adoption is limited by inefficient target utilization and multistep post-irradiation processing. This study aimed to develop a simplified high-yield production and direct chloride-based purification workflow for clinical-grade terbium-161 using thin-layer enriched gadolinium-160 targets under limited-resource reactor conditions. Terbium-161 was produced by neutron irradiation of enriched Gadolinium-160 targets fabricated as conventional oxide powder and novel thin-layer chloride films at a thermal neutron flux of 1.5 × 1014 n cm⁻2 s⁻1 for 24 h in Pakistan Atomic Research Reactor-1 (PARR-I). Post-irradiation separation of terbium was carried out using LN resin employing HCl and HNO₃ media, and compared with α-hydroxyisobutyric acid (α-HIBA) cation-exchange chromatography. Radionuclidic purity was assessed by HPGe γ spectrometry, while radiochemical purity and labeling efficiency with DOTA conjugated Peptides were evaluated using ITLC and HPLC.ResultsThin-layer targets significantly enhanced neutron utilization, compared to oxide targets due to reduced self-shielding of target. The specific activities achieved across three production batches (34.25–79.01 MBq/mg) demonstrated an approximately 2.3-fold higher specific activity with 160GdCl3 layered targets than bulk Gd₂O₃ targets. LN–HCl separation provided superior recovery (91.4%) and enabled direct formulation of [161 Tb]TbCl₃, eliminating post-processing steps required in nitrate systems and cation exchange with α -HIBA. Radionuclidic purity exceeded 99.99%, with minimal Terbium-160 impurity and Radiolabeling yields with DOTA-conjugated peptides exceeded 95.0%.ConclusionTarget geometry plays a critical role in reactor-based radionuclide production. The integrated thin-layer target and chloride-based separation workflow provides a simplified, high-yield approach for production of terbium-161, enables efficient radiolabeling with DOTA-based ligands. These findings provide a foundation for further analytical qualification required for preclinical and clinical translations.Graphical abstract Keywords: Terbium-161, Radiolanthanides, Extraction chromatography, Thin-layer targets, Auger electron

Introduction

Terbium (Tb), often referred to as the “Swiss army knife” of nuclear medicine, comprises four clinically significant radionuclides: terbium-149 for targeted alpha therapy (TAT), terbium-152 for positron emission tomography (PET), terbium-155 for single photon emission computed tomography (SPECT), and terbium-161 for targeted radionuclide therapy. Among them, terbium-161 (T1/2 = 6.89 d) is particularly promising for β¯/auger electron therapy due to clustered emission of β¯particles (Eβ−max = 0.593 MeV), gamma radiation (Eγ = 75 keV, I = 10.2%), (Borgna et al. 2021) low-energy conversion (1.8), and Auger electrons (10.9) (Bolcaen et al. 2023) per decay as detailed in Fig. 1 (Desorgher et al. 2021) and structured in (Table 1“Nuclear National Data Center (Upton N.Y.) Additionally, its chemical similarity to lutetium-177 allows it to be stably coordinated with a macrocyclic chelators like 1,4,7,10-tetraazacyclododecane-1,4,7,10-tetraacetic acid (DOTA) and other targeting vectors currently used with lutetium-177 without altering established clinical protocols (Müller et al. 2018). It has been demonstrated that terbium-161 offers superior efficacy for single tumor cells and micro metastases, requiring 40% less absorbed dose, delivering over threefold higher cellular dose, and exhibiting a higher electron-to-photon dose ratio than lutetium-177 (Borgna et al. 2022; Schaefer-Schuler et al. 2024; Buteau et al. 2024; Tschan et al. 2023; Gracheva et al. 2019; Rizvi et al. 2025). Preclinical studies using [161 Tb][Tb-DOTATOC], [161 Tb][Tb-DOTA-LM3] ((Fricke et al. 2024; Borgna et al. 2022)), [161 Tb]Tb-PSMA-617(Schaefer-Schuler et al. 2024), [161 Tb][Tb-PSMA-I&T] (Buteau et al. 2024), [161 Tb][Tb-SibuDAB] (Tschan et al. 2023) showed excellent anti-tumor efficacy.

The reactor-based production through 160Gd(n,γ) 161Gd → 161 Tb reaction (160Gd σ = 1.5 b) is most efficient and scalable route for obtaining high-purity non-carrier-added (n.c.a.) terbium-161 (Gracheva et al. 2019). However, two major bottlenecks persist, namely neutron self-shielding in bulk targets that reduces effective activation, and complex post-separation processing. Specifically, traditional methods require an arduous, time-consuming evaporation phase to convert the isolated product from a nitrate form into a chloride matrix prior to radiolabeling, increasing processing time and radiation exposure to operators.

To address these hurdles, alternative separation systems continue to be developed. Recently, Furkina et al. reported extraction chromatographic separation of terbium-161 from neutron-irradiated gadolinium targets using LN resin systems, while Gizawy et al. demonstrated reactor-scale production and purification strategies using irradiated gadolinium targets, highlighting the continuing need for simplified separation approaches that facilitate downstream radiopharmaceutical applications (Furkina et al. 2026).

The present study focuses on the development of an integrated workflow for the production and radiopharmaceutical evaluation of terbium-161 under resource-limited conditions. A novel thin-layer gadolinium-160 target fabrication strategy was introduced to minimize neutron self-shielding and enhance effective burn-up compared to conventional oxide powder targets. The study further evaluates and compares two chromatographic separation systems using LN resin with nitric acid and hydrochloric acid eluents to optimize terbium-161 recovery, radionuclidic purity, and compatibility with subsequent radiolabeling using DOTA-based ligands.

Materials and methods

All the chemicals used in this work were analytical grade. Nitric acid (≥ 65% w/v), Hydrochloric acid (HCl), α-hydroxyisobutyric acid (α-HIBA Acros), Dowex 50W × 8 and HPLC grade acetonitrile (MeCN) were purchased from Sigma Aldrich. LN resin was procured from Eichrom Inc. (USA) and highly (98.04% Gd2O3) enriched gadolinium oxide target from Trace Sciences International (Canada). DOTA-Tyr3-Octreotate (DOTATTATE), and prostate-specific membrane antigen 617 (PSMA-617), were acquired from ABX Advanced Biochemical Compounds GmbH (Germany). All aqueous solutions were prepared using ultrapure water (derived from a Millipore Direct-Q 5 water purification system.

Radionuclidic purity, radiochemical purity, and radiolabeling yield were determined using a high-purity germanium (HPGe) co-axial gamma spectrometer (GCDX-60200, Baltic Scientific Instruments Latvia), a radio-thin-layer chromatography (radio-TLC)) scanning system (LabLogic Systems, Sheffield, UK) equipped with a 2.54 × 2.54 cm thallium-doped sodium iodide NaI(Tl) scintillation detector coupled to a digital multi-channel analyzer (MCA(, and an ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system (uHPLC Ultimate 3000, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).

Target fabrication and irradiation

Highly enriched gadolinium-160 oxide ([160Gd]Gd2O3 ,98.04% isotopic enrichment) was used as irradiation target. The target matrix contained 154Gd (0.04%,), 155Gd (0.24%), 156Gd (0.39%,), 157Gd (0.27%) and 158Gd trace. Two target configurations were engineered to investigate spatial flux attenuation:

A powdered target (Sample I) was prepared by directly sealing [160Gd]Gd2O3 in a quartz capsule of 5mm internal diameter. For the preparation of thin-film targets, Gd₂O₃ was dissolved in 4 M HCl, vacuum dried and reconstituted in ultrapure double-distilled water to obtain a stock solution of [160Gd]GdCl3 (~ 0.1 mg µL⁻1). Aliquots of this solution were transferred into quartz ampoules to form thin-film targets (~1.0 ± 0.03 mm thickness). The samples were irradiated at Pakistan Atomic Research Reactor –I (PARR-I) at thermal neutron flux of 1.5 × 1014 n cm−2sec−1 for 24 hours at maximum reactor power of 10 MW. Gold and Zirconium flux monitors were also irradiated for accurate flux measurements during the irradiation period. To ensure complete process validation and data traceability across the production campaigns, independent experimental runs were performed and tracked under validation batch identifiers: Batch Tb161-2025-01A (Sample I), Batch Tb161-2025-02B (Sample II), and Batch Tb161-2025-03C (Sample III). All production yields and specific activities were decay corrected to end of bombardment (EOB).

Chemical processing of irradiated target

Irradiated samples were processed for chemical separation following the decay cooling period of 24 h post irradataion to allow for the complete decay of short-lived intermediate activation products, the targets were transferred to a lead-shielded hot cell for chemical dissolution. The bulk oxide target (Sample I) was dissolved in 2.00 mL of 3.0 N HCl, followed by multiple cycles of drying and washing with double distilled water to neutralize the solution of [161 Tb]TbCl3 for subsequent use. The thin film targets (Samples II and III) were dissolved directly in ultra-pure double-distilled water with gentle thermal heating (45.00 °C) to form chloride solution without requiring post-dissolution acid conversion steps.

Radiochemical separation of terbium-161/gadolinium

The isolation of non-carrier-added (n.c.a.) terbium-161 from the macro-gadolinium target matrix was investigated using extraction chromatography (LN-3 resin) and cation exchange chromatography (Dowex 50W × 8 resin). All chromatographic workflows were executed within 72.00 h post-EoB. Due to resource limitations restricting access to routine offline inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry ICP-MS, co-activated gadolinium-159 (159Gd, T1/2 = 18.48 h) was successfully leveraged as an internal radiotracer to dynamically monitor, profile, and quantify the separation kinetics of the macro-target mass from the target terbium-161 fractions.

Cation exchange chromatography

A glass chromatographic column (ϕ:10 mm, l:170 mm) was packed with 1 g Dowex cation exchange resin (Dowex 50W × 8, (100–200 mesh H+ form) that has swollen overnight in 0.10 M NH4OH. The column was sequentially preconditioned with 5.00 mL of distilled water, 5.00 ml 0.50 M α-HIBA solution (pH = 4.5) and again with 10.00 mL of distilled water. A aliquot of crude [161 Tb]TbCl3 stock solution treated with 0.05 M NH4Cl solution (pH = 3) was gravity loaded onto the column at a flow rate of 0.1 mL/min.

Ammoniated α-HIBA solutions (titrated to pH = 4.5 using57.00 g L−1 ammonia) were evaluated as mobile-phase eluents. Gradient steps were evaluated at concentrations of 0.11 M, 0.12 M, 0.13 M, 0.15 M α-HIBA solutions using a peristaltic pump at constant velocity of 0.2 mL.min−1. Eluate radioactivity profiles were tracked in real-time. Upon full recovery of the terbium-161 fractions, the eluent concentration was increased to 0.50 M, α-HIBA to strip the retained gadolinium isotopes off the matrix. The activity level in the eluates were observed by the dose rate. As soon as, terbium—161 activity reached negligible level, the concentration of the ammoniated α-HIBA -solution was increased to 0.5 M to elute gadolinium isotopes.

Extraction chromatography

Two distinct extraction chromatography columns (ϕ:10 mm, l:170 mm) packed with 800.0 gm each of LN-3 resin (25–50 μm particle diameter) pre soaked overnight in 0.10 M HNO3.. Column 1(C1) was allocated for systematic elution studies utilizing nitric acid while column 2 (C2) was designated for elution studies with hydrochloric acid. Columns C1 and C2 were preconditioned with 5.00 mL of water followed by 5.00 mL of 0.15 M HNO3 and 0.15 M HCl, respectively, and washed with 10.00 mL of water. Equal activity fractions of the crude target solutions were loaded onto both column structures at a rate of 0.10 mL min−1. Stepwise gradient elutions were executed using progressive concentrations (0.05 M, 0.1 M, 0.2 M, 0.3 M, 0.4 M, upto 1 M) of either HNO3 (C1) or HCl (C2) driven at 0.20 mL min−1 using peristaltic pump. Fractions were isolated in 0.50 mL volumes and quantified on a dose calibrator and the coaxial HPGe spectrometer. The summary of the operational parameters is given in Table 2.

Radionuclidic purity of the [161 Tb]TbCl3 after purification

The radionuclidic purity of the isolated [161 Tb]TbCl3 product was verified via high-resolution gamma-ray spectrometry using a coaxial HPGe detector possessing a relative efficiency of 60\%. Energy calibrations were established using a certified multi-nuclide standard source covering a energy envelope from from 53.16 keV (Eγ of 133Ba) to 1457 keV Eluate fractions were measured dynamically until counting statistics yielded a 3σ confidence interval below 5%.. Terbium-161 was identified and quantified via its primary photopeaks at 25.7, 48.9, 57.2, 74.6, 87.9, 103.2, 106.1, and 292.4 keV. Co-extracted macro-gadolinium concentrations were tracked via the 348.0 keV and 363.0 keV gamma -lines of gadolinium-159. Traces of the activation impurity terbium-160 were monitored using its characteristic high-energy photopeaks at 298.00, 879.00, 966.00, 1177.00, and 1271.00 keV.

Radiolabeling

Radiolabeling of the macrocyclic DOTA-conjugated targeting vectors DOTATATE and PSMA-617 was executed in accordance with modified standard validation procedures. Briefly, n.c.a. [161 Tb]TbCl3 solution was added a in peptide solution containing either DOTATATE or PSMA-617 (1.00 mg mL−1) prepared in 10% (v/v) ethanol in water to achieve a final peptide concentration of 1.00 mg mL−1 to achieve a defined nuclide-to-peptide labeling ratio. Reactions were conducted inside 10.0 mL borosilicate glass vials sealed with chlorobutyl rubber stoppers that had been pre-sterilized via a 10% ethanol rinse. The reaction environment was buffered to a stable labeling pH of 5.50 utilizing a 0.2 M sodium ascorbate solution, which functioned as both a hydrogen ion buffer and an analytical radical scavenger to mitigate radiolytic peptide backbone degradation. The matrix was thoroughly homogenized via vortexing for 5.0 min and subsequently incubated at 95.0 °C for 20.0 min inside a calibrated digital dry heating block.(Rizvi et al. 2025).

Radiochemical yield of [.161 Tb]TbCl3,[161 Tb]Tb-DOTATATE and [161 Tb]Tb-PSMA-617

The radiochemical yield of the final product was determined by applying 10.0 μL drop of free [161 Tb]TbCl₃ solution, [161 Tb]Tb-DOTATATE and [161 Tb]Tb-PSMA-617on separate iTLC-SG plates. The plates were run in 0.1 M sodium citrate solution (pH 5.5). After development, the plates were dried and analyzed using a radio TLC scanner (Lablogic) equipped with Laura software.

Radiochemical purity of [161 Tb]Tb-DOTATATE and [161 Tb]Tb-PSMA-617

Radiochemical purity of the labeled peptides was assessed by radio-HPLC using a Thermo Fisher Scientific uHPLC system (Ultimate 3000) equipped with UV detection at 220 nm equipped with Chromeleon 7.0 software. Radioactivity was monitored using a Bioscan (Japan) system fitted with a NaI(Tl) scintillation detector. A 20 µL sample was injected onto an RP-18 column (LiChrospher® 100 RP-18 end capped, 5 µm). The method employed a binary solvent system consisting of (A) 0.1% TFA in water and (B) 0.1% TFA in acetonitrile, with a gradient elution from 5 to 100% B at a flow rate of 1.0 mL min−1 over 30 min.

Considering the hygroscopic nature of GdCl3, the target material was thoroughly dried before encapsulation and hermetically sealed in quartz ampoules under dry condition to minimize the moisture uptake before irradiation (Talip et al. 2020; International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA)). It is pertinent to mention that under the specified irradiation conditions (24 h, thermal neutron flux 1.5 × 1014 n m−2 s−1), the quartz ampoules remained intact, without obvious cracks, deformation, discoloration, or other signs of irradiation-induced damage. Furthermore, no gaseous discharge was observed during the opening of ampoule after irradiation, indicating absence of gaseous chlorine. These observations suggest that any radiolytic gas generation was insufficient to adversely affect target integrity. However, future studies employing analytical gas measurements or pressure monitoring will provide a more accurate evaluation of the radiation stability of GdCl3 targets during extended neutron exposure.

Cation exchange chromatography

Separation of terbium-161 from gadolinium-160 target using Dowex cation Exchange Resin was performed with increasing molarities of ammoniated α-HIBA as a complexing agent. The elution profile (Fig. 3a) showed that terbium-161 was released in the 0.12 M-0.14 M concentration of α-HIBA in the form of [Tb(IBA)]3+. Maximum activity of terbium-161 was released with 0.14 M concentration of eluent. Gadolinium-159 eluted later with 0.5 M α-HIBA (fractions 17–24). HPGe γ-spectrometry confirmed that terbium-161 fractions contained characteristic γ-lines at 25.7, 48.9 and 74.6 keV, whereas gadolinium-159 (226.0, 348.0, 363.0 keV was absent from the main terbium peak region. The total activity recovered was 8.65 MBq from 11.1 MBq loaded, corresponding to 77.93% recovery. Only trace terbium-160 impurity was observed.

Extraction chromatography

Nitric acid mobile phase system Within the nitric acid eluent configuration on the LN-3 extraction column matrix, the target macro-gadolinium ions eluted prior to the target radiometal. Quantitative gamma spectrometry profiling showed that the gadolinium-159 fraction cleared the column rapidly within a low-acid concentration threshold of 0.20 M HNO3 (encompassing fractions 1–5). Conversely, trivalent terbium-161 ions displayed intense retention on the resin bed at low acid strengths and were sharply recovered within an elevated gradient step of 0.75 M–1.00 M HNO3defining fractions 10–17 of the total elution volume (Fig. 3b). This system isolated 13.37 MBq of terbium-161 from a column loading of 15.22 MBq, corresponding to an 89.13% radiochemical extraction yield with trace co-eluted terbium-160 impurities.

Post separation radionuclidic purity of [.161 Tb]TbCl3

Following column purification, the final radionuclidic purity of the isolated [161 Tb]TbCl3 formulation was verified by analyzing the characteristic gamma emissions 25.7, 48.90, 57.2, 74.60, 87.90, 103.20, 106.10, and 292.40 keV using the calibrated HPGe spectrometer. The absolute radionuclidic purity was quantified at 99.99 ± 0.003%, as shown in Fig. 4. Long-lived activation contaminants, specifically terbium-160, were measured after allowing for ten half-lives of intermediate cooling, revealing an exposure level below 0.008% relative to the primary terbium-161 activity profile at the end of chemical separation.

Comparative HPGe gamma ray spectra of the target matrix recorded before and after extraction chromatography, illustrating the removal of radionuclidic impurities’

To evaluate possible gadolinium target contamination, the purified terbium-161 fraction was examined by high-resolution HPGe gamma-ray spectrometry for the characteristic gamma emissions of gadolinium-159. No detectable gadolinium-159 photopeaks were observed within the detection limits of the measurement, indicating negligible radioactive gadolinium activity in the final product also indicated by spectrum of purified terbium-161 shown in Fig. 4. Although a quantitative decontamination factor was not determined because residual gadolinium-159 activity was not quantified, the absence of detectable gadolinium-159 gamma emissions supports the high selectivity of the chromatographic separation.

Discussion

The experimental production yields of terbium-161 obtained from the neutron activation of highly enriched gadolinium-160 targets at the PARR-I facility demonstrated strong agreement with theoretical models derived from deterministic activation mathematics. The decay-corrected specific activities achieved across the validation batches (34.25–7 9.01 MBq/mg Gd, as detailed in Table 3, reveal that target macro-morphology governs initial activation kinetics.

The thin-layer [160Gd]GdCl3, matrices demonstrated an average 2.3-fold increase in specific activity compared to the bulk [160Gd]Gd2O3) powder pellet. This enhancement is directly attributed to the spatial mitigation of neutron self-shielding. Within the dense bulk oxide matrix, the high macroscopic thermal neutron capture cross-section of gadolinium induces severe local flux depression toward the center of the target volume, shielding internal atoms from core activation.

Conversely, the thin-layer configuration minimizes structural depth, enabling an unperturbed neutron flux envelope to penetrate uniformly across the target matrix. The minor yield variation observed between Sample II and Sample III is attributed to subtle micro-structural thickness non-uniformities occurring during manual chemical coating over the inner quartz walls. This variance can be readily minimized through automated automated film deposition technologies during scale-up. These bench-scale validation parameters provide a solid foundation for the engineering of automated radiochemical processing modules designed to safely isolate high-activity campaigns.

The raw radionuclidic composition profiles confirmed the dominance of terbium-161 across all targets, exceeding 90% of total target activity at early post-irradiation cooling points. Short-lived gadolinium-159 (T1/2 = 18.48 h initially presents distinct photopeaks, but it decays rapidly during the 24-h cooling window. The trace co-induced isotopic impurity terbium-160 T1/2 = 72.3 d cannot be accurately quantified via gamma spectrometry during initial timeframes due to intensive photopeak masking by the high-flux gamma emissions of terbium-161. However, following the decay of intermediate activation matrices over extended tracking intervals, the characteristic high-energy photopeaks of terbium-160 become detectable and can be cleanly quantified below a 3σ standard error threshold as shown in Fig. 2b This observed profile matches standard neutron activation pathways and cross-section parameters governing macro-lanthanide irradiation.

The evaluated Dowex 50W. × 0.8 cation exchange system successfully resolved terbium-161 from the macro-gadolinium target mass, leveraging differences in the thermodynamic stability of their respective α -hydroxyisobutyric acid (α -HIBA) coordination structures at pH 4.5. During mobile phase transition, both elements form trivalent species represented as [Tb-IBA]3+ and [Gd-IBA]3+. Trivalent terbium possesses a smaller ionic radius (lanthanide contraction), allowing it to exhibit a higher charge density and form a more stable, energetically favorable coordination chelate with α -HIBA than gadolinium. Consequently, terbium-161 is stripped from the sulfonic acid resin sites significantly earlier than the macro-gadolinium fractions, as displayed in Fig. 3a.

Although this classical pathway provides adequate resolution for analytical profiling, the observed.77.93% chemical recovery reflects localized mass retention during initial column loading and interstitial cell washing phases. Furthermore, this workflow has limited utility for routine therapeutic production because breaking down the stable α -HIBA complex requires challenging post-elution back-extraction steps to yield a carrier-free product.

In the LN-3 resin extraction system utilizing a nitric acid mobile phase, the opposite elution sequence was observed; the macro-gadolinium target mass cleared the column matrix first at a weak acid strength of 0.20 M HNO3 (Fig. 3b). Terbium-161 was strongly retained by the organophosphorus extractant layer and was recovered within a higher concentration step gradient of 0.75.–0.1.00 M HNO3\). This separation factor (alpha = 3.35\)) is consistent with values reported by Zowltoska et al. (2025).

However, the alternative hydrochloric acid mobile phase scheme on the LN-3 matrix exhibited superior chromatographic behavior. Terbium-161 was recovered as a sharp, high-intensity peak concentrated within a narrow 0.50 M HCl zone, while the macro-gadolinium mass remained strongly bound, eluting only when the acid threshold reached 0.75 M HC (Fig. 3c). This distinct behavior indicates that the choice of coordinating anion Cl− versus NO3− modifies the extraction equilibria and interfacial mass-transfer kinetics, as documented by Monroy-Guzman et al. (2020) and Furkina et al. (2026).

The LN-3/HCl process layout demonstrates clear advantages over recent literature methods. While both Gizawy et al. (2024) and Furkina et al. (2026) achieved high chemical recoveries approximately 85–90%, their elution profiles yielded a final product in a nitrate form, requiring an arduous evaporation-to-dryness step to reformulate the product into a chloride salt (Talip et al. 2020; Borgna et al. 2021) In contrast, our direct LN-3/HCl separation route achieves an excellent radiochemical recovery of 91.40 ± 1.30% directly in a high-purity chloride form [161 Tb]TbCl3This completely eliminates post-separation chemical conversion steps, reducing processing times and minimizing radiation doses to the laboratory technician. High-resolution gamma spectrometry confirmed that this process yields an absolute radionuclidic purity of 99.99 ± 0.003% with a long-lived terbium-160 fraction below 0.008% at the end of chemical processing.

Analytical radio-TLC and radio-HPLC quality control protocols confirmed the high chemical accessible purity of the final chloride solution. On the iTLC-SG matrix developed with a sodium citrate mobile phase, free uncomplexed [161 Tb]TbCl3 migrated rapidly with the solvent front Rf = 0.70–0.80\)), confirming a baseline radiochemical purity of 99.00 ± 0.10%. This high ionic accessibility enabled efficient downstream labeling of therapeutic peptides (DOTATATE and PSMA-617), consistently exceeding a radiochemical purity of 95.00 ± 1.20%

During radio-TLC monitoring of the labeling reactions, the fully chelated complexes remained at the origin (Rf = 0.0–0.30 while any unbound metal migrated to the front, showing a complete absence of unreacted radiometal peaks. These metrics were confirmed via analytical radio-HPLC, where free hydrophilic terbium eluted rapidly tR = 2.0.– 3.0 min while the highly symmetrical therapeutic product peaks resolved at longer retention intervals tR = 11.0.– 13.0 min for [161 Tb]Tb-DOTATATE tR = 15.0.—17.0.00 [161 Tb]Tb-PSMA-617. The complete absence of radiolytic degradation fragments confirms that the sodium ascorbate buffer effectively protects the peptide backbones from radiation damage during synthesis.

The high yield radiolabeling of [161 Tb]Tb-DOTATATE and [161 Tb]Tb-PSMA-617 demonstrates the practical suitability of the purified terbium-161 for radiopharmaceutical applications. Similar studies by Skalova et al. (2026) and Gracheva et al. (2023) demonstrated efficient chelation of reactor-produced terbium-161 without reporting comprehensive DOTA titration studies. Another study by Ntihabose et al. (2025) for evaluation of challenges associated with terbium-161 base radiopharmaceuticals also evaluated radionuclide quality through radiochemical purity, radiolabeling performance, and stability of the resulting radiopharmaceuticals without assessment of molar activity. Consistent with these studies, the high radiolabeling yields (> 95%) and radiochemical purities (> 99%) obtained in the present study, together with the absence of detectable 159Gd breakthrough, support the suitability of the purified terbium-161 produced by the proposed chloride-based separation method for efficient radiolabeling with DOTA-based ligands.

Conclusions

Terbium -161 is a promising therapeutic radionuclide for targeted radionuclide therapy owing to its favorable decay characteristics and coordination chemistry, which enable radiolabeling with established DOTA-based ligands. In this study, an integrated production workflow was developed to improve terbium-161 production and chemical processing under resource-limited conditions.

The proposed thin-layer gadolinium (III) chloride target configuration minimized neutron self-shielding, achieving specific activities of up to 79.10 MBq mg−1 Gd. In addition, the optimized hydrochloric acid elution protocol using LN3 resin provided a radiochemical recovery of 91.40 ± 1.30% while directly producing terbium-161 in chloride form, thereby eliminating the need for nitrate-to-chloride conversion prior to radiolabeling. The purified terbium-161 exhibited a radionuclidic purity of 99.99 ± 0.003%, with non detectable gadolinium-159 activity by HPGe gamma spectrometry, and demonstrated high radiolabeling efficiencies (> 95%) with DOTATATE and PSMA-617.

These findings demonstrate that the proposed production and separation methodology provides a reproducible approach for high yield production of terbium-161 suitable for radiolabeling with DOTA-based ligands.

Although further analytical characterization, including ICP-MS determination of elemental impurities, apparent molar activity measurements, and DOTA titration studies, is required before preclinical or clinical translation, the present work establishes a practical platform for terbium-161production and separation that may facilitate future radiopharmaceutical research, particularly in laboratories with limited radiochemical infrastructure.
