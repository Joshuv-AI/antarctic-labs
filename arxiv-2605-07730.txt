A Diffeological Construction of Singer’s Universal Connection
Dion Mann

Abstract
. We provide a rigorous construction of I.M. Singer’s universal connection , a natural connection on a bundle of paths associated to any manifold, using the theory of diffeology. Furthermore, we generalize the universal connection to the diffeological setting, which enables the reconstruction of diffeological principal bundles with connections from their holonomy representations. We show that any two diffeological bundle-connection pairs with conjugate holonomy representations must be equivalent in a certain sense. These constructions are functorial in that, ultimately, our results can be summarized as an equivalence of categories between the so-called holonomy category and the category of diffeological bundle-connection pairs.

An application of Singer’s universal connection is to prove a certain reconstruction-style theorem of Kobayashi:

Theorem

(
12
)
.

Let
(M,\bullet)
be a pointed smooth manifold which is path-connected and let
G
be a Lie group. Given a (continuous) homomorphism
H:\Omega_{\bullet}(M)\to G
, there exists a pointed principal
G
-bundle
\pi:(E,\xi_{\bullet})\to(M,\bullet)
and a connection
A
on
\pi:E\to M
such that the holonomy representation
H^{A}_{\xi_{\bullet}}:\Omega_{\bullet}(M)\to G
, defined implicitly by the equation

\bar{\gamma}_{\xi_{\bullet}}(1)=\xi_{\bullet}\cdot H^{A}_{\xi_{\bullet}}(\gamma),\quad\text{for all }\gamma\in\Omega_{\bullet}(M),

where
\bar{\gamma}_{\xi_{\bullet}}
is the horizontal lift of
\gamma
starting at
\xi_{\bullet}\in\pi^{-1}(\bullet)
, realizes
H
; that is,
H^{A}_{\xi_{\bullet}}=H
.

The bundle-connection pair in Kobayashi’s theorem is the associated bundle
E=\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(M)\times_{H}G
with connection induced by the universal connection.

Although the argument is convincing enough, Singer seems to admit the construction is a bit heuristic in
14
. We provide a fully rigorous and complete framework for Singer’s universal connection using the theory of
diffeology
. Diffeology is a theory of generalized smooth spaces which first appeared in
15
in the study of diffeomorphism groups. Such a framework appears most appropriate: the bundle
\tau:\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(M)\to M
is a genuine diffeological bundle (Proposition
3.8
), and the universal connection can be modified to give a diffeological connection (Proposition
3.11
) which gives rise to the original horizontal lift of Singer (Proposition
3.12
).

Furthermore, these notions generalize to the category of diffeological spaces and are not just restricted to smooth manifolds. We show:

Theorem

(Diffeological Bundle Reconstruction)
.

Let
(X,\bullet)
be a pointed, path-connected diffeological space and
G
a diffeological group. If
H:\Omega_{\bullet}(X)\to G
is a smooth group homomorphism, then there exists a diffeological principal
G
-bundle equipped over
X
with a diffeological connection
\Theta^{H}
whose holonomy representation coincides with
H
.

As a corollary, we have the following holonomy-based classification of diffeological bundles:

Theorem
.

Let
\pi:E\to X
and
\pi^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\to X
be diffeological principal
G
-bundles. If there exists diffeological connections
A
and
A^{\prime}
on the respective bundles
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
whose holonomy representations agree up to conjugation, then there is a
G
-bundle isomorphism from
\pi
to
\pi^{\prime}
that takes
A
to
A^{\prime}
.

We also show the diffeological bundle reconstruction is invertible in the following sense. For a fixed diffeological group
G
, the collection of all triples
(X,x_{0},H)
, where
H:\Omega_{x_{0}}(X)\to G
is a smooth group homomorphism, forms a category
\textsf{Hol}^{G}
which we can call the
holonomy category
. The diffeological bundle reconstruction theorem then defines a functor
\textsf{Hol}^{G}\to\textsf{B}_{\bullet}\mathcal{A}^{G}
, where
\textsf{B}_{\bullet}\mathcal{A}^{G}
is the category of diffeological principal bundle-connection pairs, which is inverse to a type of “forgetful functor”
\textsf{B}_{\bullet}\mathcal{A}^{G}\to\textsf{Hol}^{G}
. This means:

Theorem
.

There is an equivalence of categories

\textsf{Hol}^{G}\leftrightarrows\textsf{B}_{\bullet}\mathcal{A}^{G}.

Outline of paper.
In Section 2, we briefly provide the necessary background theory of diffeology, including diffeological fiber bundles and connections. We then carefully set up Singer’s path spaces and universal connection using the diffeological framework in Section 3, followed by its generalization to arbitrary diffeological spaces. Finally, we apply these constructions to prove our main results in Section 4.

History: Smooth Structures in Gauge Theory.
The general study of connections on principal bundles is the topic of
gauge theory
. Over time, the focus of gauge theory has shifted to include path structures, where one is interested in the parallel transport of not just points but paths. This notion is called
higher gauge theory
, for which there are a number of approaches. For example, categorical geometry is used in
2
;
5
, in comparison to more classical differential-geometric techniques in
4
;
6
.

In any case, the use of generalized smooth structures in place of classical manifolds naturally arises so as to provide a formal treatment of path spaces. There are several notions for generalized smooth spaces, for which many of them are explained in
1
. In particular, K.T. Chen introduced the notion of a “differentiable space” (now called
Chen spaces
) in
7
;
8
to study iterated path integrals. A few years later, J.M. Souriau introduced a closely-related type of smooth space (now called
diffeological spaces
) in
15
to study diffeomorphism groups. P. Iglesias-Zemmour then expanded on the theory of diffeology to include fibrations in his thesis
10
and more recently published the general textbook “Diffeology”
11
. Notably, the groundwork for diffeological connections on diffeological bundles has been laid in
11
, which we depend on for this work.

2.1.
Basics of Diffeology

Let
X
be any set (note we do not assume a priori any structure or topologies on
X
).

Definition 2.1
.

A
diffeology
on
X
is a collection
\mathcal{D}
consisting of maps
\psi:U\to X
, where
U\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
is an open subset (here
n\geq 0
is allowed to vary), subject to the following axioms:

(i)

Smooth Constants
: If
\psi:U\to X
is constant, then
\psi\in\mathcal{D}
.

(ii)

Locality
: If, given
\psi:U\to X
, there exists an open cover
U=\bigcup_{\alpha}U_{\alpha}
such that each
\psi\upharpoonright U_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{D}
, then
\psi\in\mathcal{D}
.

(iii)

Chain Rule
: If
\psi:U\to X
is a member of
\mathcal{D}
and
F:V\to U
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-map from an open subset
V\subseteq\mathds{R}^{k}
, then
\psi\circ F\in\mathcal{D}
.

We call the pair
(X,\mathcal{D})
a
diffeological space
. We will immediately be less formal and denote by
X
a diffeological space. Members of
\mathcal{D}
are called
plots for
X
, which we think of as “smooth parameterizations.”

Definition 2.2
.

A map
f:X\to Y
of diffeological spaces is called
smooth
if for each plot
\psi
for
X
, the composite
f\circ\psi
is a plot for
Y
. A
diffeomorphism
is a smooth map with a smooth inverse.

The set of smooth maps
f:X\to Y
is denoted by
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,Y)
. It is worth noting that a parameterization
\psi:U\to X
is a plot for
X
if and only if it is smooth, where
U
has the evident diffeology. Also, replacing the name “diffeological space” with “smooth manifold” yields all the expected results (smooth maps between diffeological spaces which are manifolds are the usual smooth maps, fiber bundles
\pi:E\to X
for diffeological spaces which are manifolds are the usual fiber bundles, etc.).

Definition 2.3

(Basic Constructions in Diffeology)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be diffeological spaces, and let
\{X_{\alpha}:\alpha\in J\}
be an arbitrary family of diffeological spaces.

(1)

Subspace Diffeology.
A subset
A\subseteq X
is a diffeological space whose plots
U\to A
are, when regarded as maps
U\to X
, plots for
X
.

(2)

Product Diffeology.
The cartesian product
\prod_{\alpha\in J}X_{\alpha}
is a diffeological space whose plots
\psi:U\to\prod_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}
, which have the form
\psi(r)=(\psi_{\alpha}(r))_{\alpha\in J}
for
\psi_{\alpha}:U\to X_{\alpha}
, satisfy that each
\psi_{\alpha}:U\to X_{\alpha}
is a plot.

(3)

Quotient Diffeology.
For an equivalence relation
\sim
on
X
, the quotient
X/\!\sim
is a diffeological space with plots
\psi:U\to X/\!\sim
which locally are lifts of plots for
X
. More explicitly: for each
r_{0}\in U
, there exists a neighborhood
V\subseteq U
of
r_{0}
and a plot
r\mapsto x_{r}
for
X
defined on
V
such that

(\psi\upharpoonright V)(r)=[x_{r}],

where
[\cdot]
denotes the equivalence class for
\sim
.

(4)

Functional Diffeology.
The space of smooth maps
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,Y)
is itself a diffeological space whose plots

\psi:U\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,Y),\quad r\mapsto\psi_{r}

are such that the induced evaluation map

\hat{\psi}:U\times X\to Y,\quad\hat{\psi}(r,x)=\psi_{r}(x)

is smooth.

Remark 2.4
.

There are diffeological co-products, but we have no need of them here.

The fact that the collection of smooth maps
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,Y)
is itself a diffeological space is one of the main reasons to consider diffeology as a framework for higher gauge theory. As an important application, we have:

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a diffeology on
X
. Then
\mathcal{D}
itself carries a natural diffeology called the
functional diffeology of a diffeology
. Namely, a parameterization
\psi:U\to\mathcal{D}
,
r\mapsto\psi_{r}
for
\mathcal{D}
is a plot if and only if for each
r_{0}\in U
and
s_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{r_{0}})
, there are open neighborhoods
V\subseteq U
of
r_{0}
and
W\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{r_{0}})
of
s_{0}
such that:

(i)

For all
r\in V
, one has
W\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(\psi_{r})
.

(ii)

The evaluation map
V\times W\to X
given by
(r,s)\mapsto\psi_{r}(s)
is a plot for
X
.

We will use the functional diffeology of a diffeology in our treatment of path spaces. In the functional diffeology, composition becomes a smooth operation, as is easy to verify:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
X,Y
, and
Z
be diffeological spaces. Then, the composition map

\displaystyle\circ:\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,Y)\times\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(Y,Z)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,Z)

\displaystyle(f,g)

\displaystyle\mapsto g\circ f

is smooth.

2.3.
Diffeological Connections and Holonomy Representations

One central object of study in principal bundles is the
connection
. A notion of a
diffeological connection
for diffeological principal
G
-bundles has been presented in the book
11
. It is interesting to note that this definition seems well-fitted for the constructions in this paper, in the sense that the
universal connection
—as we will see later (Definition
3.10
)—defines a diffeological connection (Proposition
3.11
).

Let us first establish some notation. For a diffeological space
(Y,\mathcal{D})
, denote by
\mathcal{P}Y
the subset of
\mathcal{D}
consisting of plots
\alpha:I\to Y
, where
I\subseteq\mathds{R}
is an open interval (possibly all of
\mathds{R}
). Also denote by
\hat{\mathcal{P}}Y
to be the following set:

\hat{\mathcal{P}}Y=\{(\alpha,t)\in\mathcal{P}Y\times\mathds{R}:t\in\mathrm{dom}(\alpha)\}

We endow both
\mathcal{P}Y\subseteq\mathcal{D}
and
\hat{\mathcal{P}}Y\subseteq\mathcal{P}Y\times\mathds{R}
with the subspace diffeologies, where
\mathcal{D}
has the functional diffeology of a diffeology (Definition
2.5
).

Definition 2.13
.

Let
Y
be a diffeological space.

(1)

The diffeological space
\mathcal{P}Y
is called the
path space
of
Y
. Members
\alpha
of
\mathcal{P}Y
are called
smooth paths
.

(2)

The diffeological space
\hat{\mathcal{P}}Y
is called the

of
Y
. Members
(\alpha,t)
of
\hat{\mathcal{P}}Y
are called

.

(3)

A

is an element
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}Y
such that

and
\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)
. If
y_{0}=\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)
, we say that
\gamma
is a

.

(4)

The diffeological subspace of
\mathcal{P}Y
consisting of smooth loops based at

is denoted by

.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
\pi:E\to X
be a diffeological principal

-bundle. By a

on

, we mean a smooth map

(i)

:
\mathrm{dom}(A_{t}\tilde{\alpha})=\mathrm{dom}(\tilde{\alpha}).

(ii)

:
\pi\circ A_{t}\tilde{\alpha}=\pi\circ\tilde{\alpha}
.

(iii)

:
A_{t}\tilde{\alpha}(t)=\tilde{\alpha}(t).

(iv)

A_{t}(\tilde{\alpha}\cdot\rho)=A_{t}\tilde{\alpha}\cdot\rho(t)

(v)

.

(vi)

:
A_{t}(A_{t}\tilde{\alpha})=A_{t}\tilde{\alpha}
.

We call the path
A_{t}\tilde{\alpha}
the

\tilde{\alpha}
at time

.

Definition 2.15
.

For a diffeological connection

Definition 2.17
.

Let

\alpha
and
\beta

\beta

3.
Diffeological Path Spaces and Singer’s Universal Connection

Throughout, we fix a pointed diffeological space
(X,\bullet)
which is path-connected, i.e. for every
x,y\in X
, there exists an
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}X
for which
x,y\in\image(\alpha)
. Note this fact implies there exists a smooth map
p:[0,1]\to X
with
p(0)=x
and
p(1)=y
. The goal of this section is to provide a complete and rigorous framework for Singer’s arguments in
14
and to generalize the notions to the diffeological setting.

3.1.
Path Concatenation and the Loop Space

We equip the set
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1],X)
with the functional diffeology. We take two quotients of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1],X)
by equivalence relations which will lead to the so-called
retrace equivalence
, which is an equivalence relation used in
12
and
14
.

First, put
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\sim}([0,1],X)=\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1],X)/\!\sim
, where
\alpha\sim\beta
if and only if there exists
f\in\textsf{Diff}([0,1])
with
f(0)=0
,
f(1)=1
, and
\alpha=\beta\circ f
. An element of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\sim}(X)
is written
\ulcorner\alpha\urcorner
, which may be thought of as an “unparameterized path.”

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\ulcorner\alpha\urcorner\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\sim}([0,1],X)
. Then the representative
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1],X)
can be chosen to be constant near its endpoints
0
and
1
.

Proof.

Take, for example, the smooth bump function
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
given by

f(t)=\frac{g(t)}{g(t)+g(1-t)},

where
g(t)=e^{-1/t}
if
t>0
and
g(t)=0
otherwise. Then
\alpha\circ f
is constant near
0
and
1
.

x

In particular, Lemma
3.1
implies that two unparameterized paths
\ulcorner\alpha\urcorner
and
\ulcorner\beta\urcorner
with
\beta(0)=\alpha(1)
for some (hence all) representatives
\beta
and
\alpha
can be concatenated into another unparameterized path
\ulcorner\beta\vee\alpha\urcorner
, as in Definition
2.17
. This means the following is well-defined:

Definition 3.2

(cf.
13
;
9
)
.

For each
\ulcorner\eta\urcorner\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\sim}([0,1],X)
, there is an operation on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\sim}([0,1],X)
given by

\ulcorner\beta\urcorner\vee\ulcorner\alpha\urcorner\mapsto\ulcorner\beta\urcorner\vee\ulcorner\eta\urcorner\vee\ulcorner\eta^{\leftarrow}\urcorner\vee\ulcorner\alpha\urcorner,

where
\eta^{\leftarrow}(t)=\eta(1-t)
, which generates an equivalence relation on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\sim}([0,1],X)
called
retrace equivalence
. Denote by
\mathcal{F}(X)
the quotient of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\sim}([0,1],X)
by retrace equivalence. A class of retrace-equivalent paths is denoted by
[\alpha]
, viewing
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1],X)
.

Note that
[\alpha]=[\beta]
if and only if
\alpha
and
\beta
differ by a finite number of reparameterizations and retraces. In light of Lemma
3.1
, let us now take the following convention:

Convention
.

If
[\alpha]\in\mathcal{F}(X)
is given, we assume the representative
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1],X)
is constant near
0
and
1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Path-concatenation (Definition
2.17
) defines a partial function

\mathcal{F}(X)\times\mathcal{F}(X)\rightharpoonup\mathcal{F}(X),\quad([\alpha],[\beta])\mapsto[\beta]\vee[\alpha]:=[\beta\vee\alpha],

which is smooth where it is defined—more precisely, on the diffeological subspace

\mathcal{F}^{\vee}(X)=\big\{([\alpha],[\beta])\in\mathcal{F}(X)\times\mathcal{F}(X):\beta(0)=\alpha(1)\big\}.

Proof.

Let
r\mapsto([\alpha_{r}],[\beta_{r}])
be a plot for the subspace
\mathcal{F}^{\vee}(X)
. Locally, by definition of the quotient diffeology, there are plots
r\mapsto\alpha_{r}
and
r\mapsto\beta_{r}
for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1],X)
with
\alpha_{r}(1)=\beta_{r}(0)
. We can assume
all
the
\alpha_{r}
and
\beta_{r}
are constant near their endpoints, for otherwise by smoothness of composition (Lemma
2.6
), we can consider the plots
r\mapsto\alpha_{r}\circ f
and
r\mapsto\beta_{r}\circ f
, where
f
is the smooth bump function in the proof of Lemma
3.1
. Now, the evaluation

(r,t)\mapsto(\beta_{r}\vee\alpha_{r})(t)

is locally
(r,t)\mapsto\alpha_{r}(2t)
, or
(r,t)\mapsto\beta_{r}(2t-1)
, or constant, all of which are smooth. This means
r\mapsto\beta_{r}\vee\alpha_{r}
is a plot for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1],X)
, hence
r\mapsto[\beta_{r}\vee\alpha_{r}]
is a plot for
\mathcal{F}(X)
. We’ve just shown that
([\alpha],[\beta])\mapsto[\beta\vee\alpha]
locally takes plots for
\mathcal{F}^{\vee}(X)
to plots for
\mathcal{F}(X)
, hence defines a smooth map.

Definition 3.4
.

The
loop space
of
(X,\bullet)
is the set

\Omega_{\bullet}(X):=\big\{[\gamma]\in\mathcal{F}(X):\gamma(0)=\bullet=\gamma(1)\big\}

endowed with the subspace diffeology from
\mathcal{F}(X)
.

Proposition 3.5
.

The loop space
\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
is a diffeological group whose group multiplication and inversion operations are
[\gamma]\vee[\sigma]:=[\gamma\vee\sigma]
and
[\gamma]^{-1}=[\gamma^{\leftarrow}]
.

Proof.

The fact that
\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
is a group is a standard fact of retrace equivalence. Smoothness of the group multiplication was Lemma
3.3
. We can show that inversion is smooth. Given a plot
r\mapsto[\gamma_{r}]
for
\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
, the composition

(r,t)\mapsto(r,1-t)\mapsto\gamma_{r}(1-t)=\gamma_{r}^{\leftarrow}(t)

is smooth, since the plot
r\mapsto[\gamma_{r}]
for the quotient locally lifts into a plot
r\mapsto\gamma_{r}
for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1],X)
. This means that
r\mapsto[\gamma_{r}]^{-1}=[\gamma_{r}^{\leftarrow}]
is a plot for
\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
, finishing the proof.

If
[\gamma]\in\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
, then the representative
\gamma
(being constant near its endpoints) admits a smooth extension to a loop defined on all of
\mathds{R}
. In particular, this implies:

Proposition 3.6
.

Let

be any holonomy representation for a diffeological group

and connection

. Then

induces a well-defined group homomorphism

Proof.

This follows from the reparameterization and concatenation properties in Proposition

is the reason for using retrace equivalence. In

3.2.
The Universal Connection

Adapting Singer’s construction in
14
, we show that every pointed diffeological space
(X,\bullet)
gives rise to a diffeological principal
G
-bundle over itself equipped with a diffeological connection called the
universal connection
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)=\{[\alpha]\in\mathcal{F}(X):\alpha(0)=\bullet\}
be endowed with the subspace diffeology from
\mathcal{F}(X)
. There is a natural projection

\tau:\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)\to X,\quad\tau([\alpha])=\alpha(1),

which is a well-defined smooth surjection. The map

\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)\times\Omega_{\bullet}(X)\to\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X),\quad([\alpha],[\gamma])\mapsto[\alpha]\vee[\gamma]:=[\alpha\vee\gamma],

defines a smooth right-action of
\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
on
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)
, thanks to Lemma
3.3
. To show
\tau:\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)\to X
is actually a diffeological principal
\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
-bundle, we will need the following result, which roughly says that plots for
X
determine locally a smooth family of radial paths in
X
:

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\psi:U\to X
be a plot and put
x_{r}:=\psi(r)
. Fix a point
r_{0}\in U
. Then there exists a neighborhood
V\subseteq U
of
r_{0}
and a plot
V\ni r\mapsto\alpha_{r}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1],X)
such that
\alpha_{r}(0)=x_{r_{0}}
and
\alpha_{r}(1)=x_{r}
for all
r\in V
.

Proof.

We can choose
V\subseteq U
to be any open ball containing
r_{0}
. For each
r\in V
, consider the straight-line path
\ell_{r}:[0,1]\to V
from
r_{0}
to
r
in
V
, i.e.
\ell_{r}
is given by

\ell_{r}(t)=(1-t)r_{0}+tr.

Then
r\mapsto\ell_{r}
is a plot for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1],V)
, since the evaluation
(r,t)\mapsto\ell_{r}(t)
is smooth. Define
\alpha_{r}:[0,1]\to X
by

\alpha_{r}(t)=\psi(\ell_{r}(t)).

The map
V\ni r\mapsto\alpha_{r}
defines the desired plot for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1],X)
, since the evaluation
(r,t)\mapsto\alpha_{r}(t)=\psi((1-t)r_{0}+tr)
is a composition of smooth maps.

x

Proposition 3.8
.

The projection
\tau:\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)\to X
is a diffeological principal
\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
-bundle.

Proof.

This amounts to showing two things (see Definition
2.8
):

(i)

The graph map
\Gamma:\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)\times\Omega_{\bullet}(X)\to\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)\times\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)
given by

\Gamma([\alpha],[\gamma])=([\alpha],[\alpha\vee\gamma])

is a diffeomorphism onto its image.

(ii)

There is a diffeomorphism
\Phi:\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)/\Omega_{\bullet}(X)\to X
such that

\Phi([\alpha]\vee\Omega_{\bullet}(X))=\tau([\alpha])=\alpha(1),

where
[\alpha]\vee\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
is the equivalence class

[\alpha]\vee\Omega_{\bullet}(X)=\big\{[\alpha\vee\gamma]:[\gamma]\in\Omega_{\bullet}(X)\big\}

in the quotient
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)/\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
.

For (i), the inverse of
\Gamma
, defined on
\image\Gamma
, is given by

\Gamma^{-1}([\alpha],[\beta])=([\alpha],[\alpha^{\leftarrow}\vee\beta]),\quad\text{where }\alpha^{\leftarrow}(t)=\alpha(1-t),

which is smooth by Lemma
3.3
. So,
\Gamma
is a diffeomorphism onto its image.

For (ii), we need to show that the map
\Phi:\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)/\Omega_{\bullet}(X)\to X
given by

\Phi([\alpha]\vee\Omega_{\bullet}(X))=\alpha(1)

is smooth with smooth inverse. By definition of the quotient diffeology, any plot for
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)/\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
locally has the form
r\mapsto[\alpha_{r}]\vee\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
for a plot
r\mapsto\alpha_{r}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1],X)
. Then
r\mapsto\Phi([\alpha_{r}]\vee\Omega_{\bullet}(X))=\alpha_{r}(1)
is just the evaluation map
(r,t)\mapsto\alpha_{r}(t)
restricted to
t=1
, which is smooth since
r\mapsto\alpha_{r}
is a plot for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1],X)
. This means that
\Phi
is smooth.

For each
x\in X
, let
\lambda_{x}:[0,1]\to X
be some path with
\lambda_{x}(0)=\bullet
and
\lambda_{x}(1)=x
, using the fact that
X
is path-connected. The inverse
\Phi^{-1}:X\to\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)/\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
is then

\Phi^{-1}(x)=[\lambda_{x}]\vee\Omega_{\bullet}(X).

If
[\lambda_{x}]=[\lambda^{\prime}_{x}]\in\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)
satisfies
\lambda_{x}(1)=x=\lambda^{\prime}_{x}(1)
, then
[\lambda_{x}]=[\lambda^{\prime}_{x}]\vee[(\lambda^{\prime}_{x})^{\leftarrow}\vee\lambda_{x}]
, hence
[\lambda_{x}]\vee\Omega_{\bullet}(X)=[\lambda^{\prime}_{x}]\vee\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
and
\Phi^{-1}
is well-defined. It remains to show
\Phi^{-1}
is smooth. Suppose a plot
r\mapsto x_{r}
is given. For a point
r_{0}
in its domain, there is (by Lemma
3.7
) a plot
r\mapsto\alpha_{r}
for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1],X)
, defined on an open neighborhood of
r_{0}
, such that
\alpha_{r}(0)=x_{r_{0}}
and
\alpha_{r}(1)=x_{r}
. Then

r\mapsto\Phi^{-1}(x_{r})=[\alpha_{r}\vee\lambda_{x_{r_{0}}}]\vee\Omega_{\bullet}(X)

locally defines a plot for
\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
(recall the convention that the representatives
\alpha_{r}
and
\lambda_{x_{r_{0}}}
are chosen to be constant near
0
and
1
). Since
r_{0}
was arbitrary, we’ve shown that
\Phi^{-1}
is smooth, hence a diffeomorphism.

Definition 3.9
.

For a path-connected, pointed diffeological space
(X,\bullet)
, the

associated to
(X,\bullet)
is the principal
\Omega_{\bullet}(X)
-bundle
\tau:\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)\to X
.

There is a natural notion of parallel transport on the pointed path bundle, which Singer calls the
universal connection

14
. We describe how to adapt Singer’s universal connection to the diffeological setting now.

We use the following notation: for any map
\alpha:(a,b)\to X
and points
r,s\in(a,b)

\alpha^{r\to s}
to be the map

In other words,
\alpha^{r\to s}
is the part of
\alpha

\alpha(r)
and ending at

Definition 3.10
.

The map
\Theta:\hat{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)\to\mathcal{P}\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)

denotes the smooth path

in
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)
.

It is worth spending some time to exercise caution:

is some open interval, and hence determines, for each

, an element
[\tilde{\alpha}(s)]
of
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)
, which is itself a class of paths
\tilde{\alpha}(s):[0,1]\to X
satisfying
\tilde{\alpha}(s)(0)=\bullet

[\tilde{\alpha}]\in\mathcal{P}\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)

\bullet

Theorem 3.11
.

The universal connection

\Theta:\hat{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)\to\mathcal{P}\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X),\quad\Theta_{r}[\tilde{\alpha}](s)=\big[(\tau\circ[\tilde{\alpha}])^{r\to s}\vee\tilde{\alpha}(r)\big]

\tau:\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)\to X

\Theta:\hat{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)\to\mathcal{P}\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)

\hat{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)

(u,s)\mapsto\Theta_{r_{u}}[\tilde{\alpha}_{u}](s)\in\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(X)

\Theta_{r_{u}}[\tilde{\alpha}_{u}](s)(t)=\tilde{\alpha}_{u}(st+(1-t)r_{u})(1)
smoothly depends on

, and

. Therefore,
\Theta
is a smooth. The proof concludes after verifying the conditions of Definition

:

(i)

Domain
: By definition of
\Theta

(ii)

(\tau\circ[\tilde{\alpha}])^{r\to s}(1)=\tau([\tilde{\alpha}(s)])

\tau(\Theta_{r}[\tilde{\alpha}](s))=\tau\big(\big[(\tau\circ[\tilde{\alpha}])^{r\to s}\vee\tilde{\alpha}(r)\big]\big)=\tau([\tilde{\alpha}(s)]).

\displaystyle=\Theta_{r}[\tilde{\alpha}](s)\vee\big[\rho(r)\big].

\displaystyle\Theta_{u}([\tilde{\alpha}]\circ f)(s)
