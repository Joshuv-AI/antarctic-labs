1.0 Summary
 The U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) User Facility’s
 Coast-Urban-Rural Atmospheric Gradient Experiment (CoURAGE; Davis et al. 2024) took place from
 December 2024 to November 2025 in Baltimore, Maryland, and surrounding areas. The ARM tethered
 balloon system (TBS) located at supplemental site S7 (39.2971° N, 76.1673° W, 12 m AMSL; see Figure
 1a) had a summer intensive operational period (IOP) from 14 to 28 July 2025.

 Figure 1. a) Map of the CoURAGE domain. CRGTBSO3 surface and TBS ozone measurements are
 from the TBS site (magenta triangle). Ozone lidar locations are shown in yellow circles.
 CoURAGE urban, bay, and rural sites are shown in magenta pentagons. The Baltimore
 Social-Environmental Collaborative (BSEC) Greenbelt site is shown in the green pentagon.
 Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) monitoring sites are shown in red. b) Data
 calendar for the ozone measurements at the TBS site for surface measurements (light green)
 and TBS profiles (dark green). c) Surface instrumentation at the TBS site including an ozone
 monitor in a yellow Pelican case and a weather station.

 1

 During the summer IOP, the small ARM field campaign CRGTBSO3 collected measurements to gain an
 improved understanding of differences between the atmospheric composition in and just above the marine
 layer at the CoURAGE TBS site near the eastern shore of Chesapeake Bay. CRGTBSO3 included guest
 instrumentation with ozone (O3) profile measurements on the TBS and surface O3 and meteorological
 measurements at the TBS site. An En-Sci 2Z electrochemical cell (ECC) ozonesonde (Komhyr 1969,
 1986, Witte et al. 2018) was included on the TBS. The ozonesonde was connected to an InterMet
 iMet-4RSB radiosonde, and the overall data collected included vertical profiles of ozone, relative
 humidity, temperature, pressure, and altitude. The CRGTBSO3 iMet-4 radiosonde data is identical to that
 of the iMet in the Tethered Balloon System Merged Data Product (TBSMERGED; Gaustad and
 Dexheimer 2025). The TBSMERGED data product also includes meteorological data from a different
 sensor, the iMet XQ2. In some cases, the iMet XQ2 relative humidity (RH) data may be more accurate
 than the iMet-4, such as for some instances when the iMet-4 RH data stays at 100% for an extended
 period throughout a profile.

 The approach of including an ozonesonde on the TBS mimics that of the Tracking Aerosol Convection
 ExpeRiment (TRACER)-Tethersonde ARM field campaign (Guagenti et al. 2025, Walter et al. 2023), but
 CRGTBSO3 also includes a continuous surface ozone measurement from a 2BTech Model 205
 dual-beam ozone monitor housed in a climate controlled Pelican case. An AIRMAR 220WX
 WeatherStation Instrument with the RH module was used for surface measurements of meteorological
 variables (i.e., pressure, temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, and wind direction). The weather
 station and the ozone inlet were both approximately 4-5 meters above ground level (Figure 1c). Figure 1b
 shows a calendar of dates when ozone data were collected at the surface (light green) and from the TBS
 (dark green).

 The ozonesonde measurements complement the aerosol and meteorological measurements from the TBS
 to help improve understanding of differences in atmospheric composition in and just above the marine
 layer near the eastern shore of Chesapeake Bay. The measurements also complement other vertical profile
 ozone measurements collected from the small mobile ozone lidars (SMOLs; Chouza et al. 2025) that are a
 part of the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) Tropospheric Ozone Lidar Network
 (TOLNet) at the CoURAGE M1 site in Baltimore and at Hart Miller Island as part of the ARM field
 campaign CoURAGE SWARM (SWARMO3; https://armgov.svcs.arm.gov/research/campaigns/
 amf2025swarmo3, last access: 30 June 2026). The locations of SMOL-1 at the CoURAGE M1 main site,
 SMOL-2 at Hart Miller Island, and the CRGTBSO3 ozonesonde profiles at the TBS site provide an
 east-west transect of vertical ozone profiles spanning from Baltimore across Chesapeake Bay during the
 summer IOP; however, there was little overlap in data coverage between the SMOLs and the TBS
 observations. The CRGTBSO3 data set also complements other surface ozone measurements collected at
 BSEC and MDE monitoring stations.

2.0 Results
 On the 13 days that included TBS measurements (Figure 1b), the TBS profiles included ozone
 measurements reached as high as 1.1 km above ground level (AGL). An example of the profiling on
 22 July 2025 is shown in Figure 2. The middle profile (18:00-19:30 UTC) shows higher ozone mixing
 ratios near the surface up to approximately 0.5 km AGL, which marks the top of the marine layer. Above
 that layer, the ozone concentrations are lower. Local time in Baltimore was UTC -4 hours during the
 deployment period.

 General trends observed in the comparison of surface measurements is that CRGTBSO3 surface RH
 measurement was consistently lower than the TBSGROUND measurement (e.g., Figure 3). From 24 to
 28 July 2025, the TBSGROUND temperature measurement showed increased variance compared to
 earlier in the summer IOP.

 Complementary ARM value-added products at the TBS site during the summer IOP include aerosol
 number concentration and size distribution measurements from a portable optical particle spectrometer
 (POPS; Gao et al. 2016) and meteorological variables onboard the TBS (TBSMERGED;
 Gaustad and Dexheimer 2025), near-surface-level meteorological variables (TBSGROUND;
 Shi et al. 2025), ceilometer-derived boundary-layer and cloud base heights (CEILPBLHT;
 Zhang and Shi 2025), and Doppler lidar vertically pointing radial velocities of the air and attenuated
 aerosol backscatter (DLFPT; Newsom et al. 2025).
