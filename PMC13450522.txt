Pathological lymph node response after immunotherapy-based total neoadjuvant therapy supports local excision for organ preservation in locally advanced rectal cancer: a propensity-score weighted analysis
Authors: Shujuan Zhou, Maoguang Ma, Siyuan Chen, Ting Zou, Yaqi Wang, Lijun Shen, Yan Wang, Fan Xia, Juefeng Wan, Zhen Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe immunotherapy-based total neoadjuvant therapy (iTNT) has improved response rates in locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC); however, a majority of patients do not achieve clinical complete response (cCR) and still require total mesorectal excision. High rates of ypT0–1 and particularly ypN0 in non-cCR patients suggest organ preservation via local excision (LE). This study aims to assess the feasibility of LE in LARC by comparing pathological lymph node responses after three neoadjuvant regimens.MethodsWe included 1568 LARC patients with pathological outcomes after neoadjuvant therapy in three cohorts: 238 received fluorouracil-based chemoradiotherapy (CRT); 1256 received total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT); and 74 received iTNT from the prospective TORCH trial (NCT04518280). Baseline characteristics among three cohorts were balanced using the stabilized inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) method.ResultsAfter IPTW adjustment, the overall rates of positive lymph nodes after neoadjuvant therapy (ypN+) were 44.1%, 34.0%, and 13.4% in the CRT, TNT, and iTNT cohorts, respectively. Notably, pathologic complete response of lymph nodes was achieved in both ypT0 and ypT1 tumors following iTNT. In contrast, the ypN+ rates for ypT0 and ypT1 tumors remained high at 16.8% and 26.4% with CRT and 11.2% and 19.2% with TNT. Univariate regression analysis identified that ypT2–4, positive ypMRF, and positive ypEMVI were associated with higher ypN+ risk, rather than baseline clinical characteristics.ConclusionsiTNT markedly improved lymph node regression in ypT0–1 tumors compared to CRT or TNT. LE, guided by tumor response but not by baseline clinical stages, may be considered for organ preservation in near-cCR patients with LARC following iTNT.

Introduction

The integration of immunotherapy into total neoadjuvant therapy (iTNT) for proficient mismatch repair or microsatellite stable (pMMR/MSS) locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC) has improved complete response (CR) rates, comprising both clinical CR (cCR) and pathological CR (pCR), to 45–67%. However, cCR rates vary markedly across centers from 16 to 50%, probably reflecting differences in experience and criteria for determining cCR. As a result, a majority of patients (50–85%) are assessed as non-cCR and subsequently require total mesorectal excision (TME) surgery. Among these patients, pCR is achieved at 37–50%, with an additional approximately 7% achieving ypTis/T1. Notably, ypN0 can reach as high as 83–100% [1], [2], [3], [4], [5]. Therefore, there still remains room to reduce surgical overtreatment and improve organ preservation for patients with non-cCR, especially near-cCR (ncCR).

An alternative strategy for organ preservation in patients with ncCR is local excision (LE). LE techniques such as conventional transanal local excision, transanal endoscopic microsurgery, and transanal minimally invasive surgery are now routinely recommended for baseline low-risk cT1N0 lesions. In neoadjuvant settings, LE following chemoradiotherapy has been assessed in relatively early-stage (cT1–3 and cN0–1) and small (≤4 cm) rectal cancer, which demonstrated superior organ preservation with no significant differences in local recurrence, metastasis, disease-free survival, or overall survival compared to TME. Moreover, LE alone was associated with higher quality of life and less morbidity and side effects than TME. The addition of completion TME does not necessarily compromise these advantages with optimal surgical timing and neoadjuvant regimens [6], [7], [8], [9].

A major concern in LE application after neoadjuvant therapy is the risk of persistent positive lymph nodes, which is reportedly correlated with multiple clinical characteristics such as baseline cT/N-stage, tumor size, grading, and CEA level. With the emergence of more intensive total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT) and iTNT regimens for LARC (cT3–4 and/or cN1–2), the overall ypN+ rates have declined from about 32% with fluorouracil-based chemoradiotherapy (CRT) to 16–29% with TNT therapy, and further to 0–17% with the addition of immunotherapy (iTNT) [10], [11], [12], [13]. Some studies have reported ypN+ rates stratified by ypT stages following CRT. In baseline cT3N0 disease, the ypN+ rates following CRT were 3%, 7%, 20%, and 36% for ypT0, ypT1, ypT2, and ypT3–4 tumors, respectively [14]. These rates rose to 5%, 8%, 26%, and 55% in cT3–4 disease [15]. It is crucial to update the corresponding rates following TNT and iTNT to re-evaluate the feasibility of LE as an organ-preserving strategy for LARC.

Here, we compared overall ypN+ rates and ypN+ rates across ypT0–4 tumors in patients with LARC following three regimens: CRT and TNT from our database, and iTNT from the TORCH trial (NCT04518280). We further analyzed factors associated with ypN+ status following iTNT to select candidates for whom LE may be considered for organ preservation.

Materials and methods

Study participants

This study included three cohorts: CRT, TNT, and iTNT. The retrospective CRT and TNT cohorts comprised 238 and 1256 LARC patients, respectively, who underwent TME following CRT or TNT at the Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center between March 2006 and February 2022. The iTNT cohort comprised 74 LARC patients from the multicenter, prospective, randomized TORCH trial who underwent TME following iTNT between May 2021 and September 2022. Patients with baseline stage-I disease (cT1–2 N0) or distant metastases before or during neoadjuvant treatment were excluded. This study was approved by the institutional review boards of all participating sites and was carried out in compliance with institutional guidelines. All patients provided broad consent for their data to be used for analyses.

Treatment

Patients in the CRT cohort received long-course radiotherapy of 50 Gy/25Fx over 5–6 weeks and concurrent capecitabine (825 mg/m2 twice daily). Patients in the TNT cohort received oxaliplatin or irinotecan-based chemoradiotherapy. The former consisted of the same long-course radiotherapy and concurrent capecitabine as in the CRT cohort, followed by 3–6 cycles of consolidation XELOX (oxaliplatin 130 mg/m2 on day 1 and capecitabine at 1000 mg/m2 twice daily on days 1–14, q3w) 2 weeks after the end of CRT. The latter comprised long-course radiotherapy of 50 Gy/25Fx with concurrent capecitabine (625 mg/m2 twice daily) and weekly irinotecan (80 mg/m2 for patients with UGT1A1*1*1 or 65 mg/m2 for patients with UGT1A1*1*28) followed by 1–3 cycles of consolidation XELIRI (irinotecan 200 mg/m2 on day 1 and capecitabine at 1000 mg/m2 twice daily on days 1–14, q3w) 2 weeks after the end of CRT. Patients in the iTNT cohort received short-course radiotherapy of 25 Gy/5Fx and 6 cycles of induction/consolidation immuno-chemotherapy, consisting of XELOX (oxaliplatin 130 mg/m2 on day 1 and capecitabine at 1000 mg/m2 twice daily on days 1–14, q3w) plus toripalimab (240 mg on day 1, q3w). TME surgery was performed generally 6–8 weeks after the completion of neoadjuvant treatment in three cohorts. Adjuvant chemotherapy was prescribed at the discretion of physicians.

Statistics

Baseline clinical characteristics of patients were described as mean and standardized difference (SD) for continuous variables and frequency and proportion for categorical variables. The standardized mean difference (SMD) and one-way ANOVA test, Chi-square test, or Fisher's exact test were used to compare differences in baseline characteristics among three cohorts. Univariate Firth's penalized-likelihood regression model was used to identify significant factors in relation to ypN+ status in the iTNT cohort.

To account for possible baseline confounding among three cohorts, a stabilized inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW)-adjusted analysis was performed. The propensity score (PS) for each patient was determined by fitting a multinomial logistic model based on clinical characteristics. Stabilized weights for each patient were calculated by multiplying the inverse of their PS by the marginal probability of their assigned treatment. Variables were considered to be balanced among cohorts if the SMD was <0.1. All statistical analyses were performed using R 4.3.2 (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A two-sided p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to report pCR of lymph nodes in patients with ypT0–1 LARC following iTNT. We further identified that post-treatment pathological factors, rather than baseline clinical stages, were significantly associated with ypN+ risk in the iTNT cohort. Our results supported LE as a promising strategy for organ preservation in ncCR patients with LARC following iTNT.

LE is now carefully recommended as a viable option for organ preservation in patients with baseline cT1–3aN0 rectal cancer who achieve ncCR after neoadjuvant therapy, based on organ preservation rates of 64%–91% and local recurrence rates ≤5% in studies including TREC, GRECCAR 2, and ACOSOG Z6041 [6], [8], [16], [17]. High risk of persistent positive lymph nodes after CRT and TNT, as well as correlations between persistence and baseline cT-and cN-stage, have impeded LE application in LARC (cT3–4 and/or cN1–2) [10]. Indeed, we found overall ypN+ rates at 44.1% and 34% following CRT and TNT, respectively. The ypN+ rates even in ypT0 tumors reached 16.8% and 11.2%, respectively. These higher rates, compared to published data (3–5%), are likely due to the high proportion of patients with baseline N+ disease (∼90%), particularly N2 (∼50%), in our study [15]. Surprisingly, the ypN+ rates dropped to 13.4% in the overall cohort and to 0% in ypT0 and ypT1 tumors following iTNT. Although we included only 74 patients in the iTNT cohort, similarly low total ypN+ rates (0–17%) seen in nearly 200 patients across several high-quality trials such as PRIME-RT, STELLAR II, and SPRING-01 suggest that ypN+ rates may also approach 0% in ypT0–1 tumors (Supplementary Table 1) [2], [3], [4], [5]. Moreover, in the iTNT cohort, the ypN+ risk was not associated with baseline clinical characteristics like cT-stage, cN-stage, cMRF, or cEMVI, but rather with post-treatment pathological factors. That is to say, patient selection for LE may be guided by responses to iTNT therapy rather than by baseline clinical stages. Specifically, LE may be restricted to ncCR and cCR patients after iTNT, with appropriately timed completion TME if needed [6]. More trials are needed to explore the feasibility of LE in LARC to translate high rates of ypT0–1 and ypN0 following iTNT into organ preservation, moving beyond early-stage rectal cancer.

Interpretation of our pathological findings should consider the full TORCH population. Of the 122 participants, 74 who did not achieve cCR underwent TME, while 48 were managed with watch-and-wait (WW) after being assessed as cCR by digital rectal examination, rectal MRI, endoscopic ultrasound with biopsy, and pelvic PET-CT. The WW group could not be included in our analysis, as they did not undergo TME surgery and thus had no pathological outcomes. Nevertheless, their exclusion is unlikely to weaken, and may even underestimate, the observed efficiency of iTNT on ypN0 rate and the association between local tumor response and ypN stage, as these WW patients meeting strict cCR criteria would most likely have achieved pCR (ypT0N0) if they underwent TME.

In addition to the absence of lymph node metastasis in ypT0–1 tumors, the immune function of tumor-draining lymph nodes (TDLNs) that become negative after iTNT warrants investigation. TDLNs are critical for antitumor immunity and ICB efficacy, but these findings largely derive from baseline negative TDLNs [18], [19], [20], [21], [22]. Whether metastatic TDLNs that convert to ypN0 regain similar immune function, or retain an immunosuppressive phenotype, remains unclear. It is also unknown whether systemic immunity can be maintained independently of TDLNs after neoadjuvant therapy. [23], [24]. These questions remain to be addressed in future studies.

Our study has three major limitations. First, as we only included 74 patients from the iTNT cohort and only 4 patients with ypT1 disease, the 0% ypN+ rate observed in ypT0–1 tumors in our study should be interpreted cautiously. Pooled analyses of larger prospective cohorts are needed to validate this finding and to identify clinical or pathological characteristics associated with ypN+ risk. Second, we did not report long-term survival after LE in ypT0–1 tumors following iTNT, as only three ncCR patients in the TORCH trial underwent LE, although all achieved ypT0. Of these 3 patients, 2 remained free of recurrence or metastasis throughout the 4-year follow-up period. The remaining patient developed one metastatic lesion in the left liver 1 year after LE, which was subsequently resected and treated with chemotherapy and targeted therapy. This patient remains disease-free to date. Long-term survival outcomes of LE in LARC are expected from TORCH, the ongoing OPRXX trial (Dutch trial register: NL75896.031.20), and future iTNT trials. Third, the retrospective CRT/TNT cohorts were accrued between 2006 and 2022, whereas the prospective iTNT cohort was accrued between 2021 and 2022. Although IPTW was used to balance measured baseline characteristics, differences in calendar period, radiologic staging, surgical techniques, and pathological assessment may have introduced residual confounding or treatment-selection bias.

In summary, we found complete pathologic lymph node response in ypT0–1 tumors and significant associations between post-treatment pathological factors and ypN+ risk. These results provide a rationale for considering the use of LE for organ preservation following iTNT in locally advanced rectal cancer.
