1. SUMMARY OF PROJECT ACTIVITIES

This Basic Energy Sciences (BES) award provided targeted support to early-career investigators to facilitate
their attendance and participation in the International Conference on Ionizing Processes (ICIP 2024),
organized and hosted at the University of Notre Dame campus between August 11th and 15th, 2024. The
support enabled young researchers to present their work through oral and poster presentations, engage with
senior leaders in the field, and strengthen national research capabilities in radiation chemistry and related
disciplines.

A total of 19 young investigators from U.S. academic institutions and national laboratories received registration
discounts, and 8 of them also received additional travel-offset awards. Four of the young investigators received
travel offset awards without registration discounts; hence, the total number of awardees was 23. Five awards
were given to young investigators from Brookhaven National Laboratory, five to Idaho National Laboratory,
one to Los Alamos National Laboratory, one to Michigan State University, one to Colorado School of Mines,
and 10 to Notre Dame Radiation Laboratory. Total DOE funding directly reduced financial barriers to
participation, enhancing U.S. early-career representation at this important international conference.

2. ACCOMPLISHMENTS RELATIVE TO THE PROJECT OBJECTIVES

The objectives of this BES award were as follows:

 1. Enhance early-career participation in ICIP 2024 by providing direct financial support.
 2. Enable dissemination of research findings by early-career investigators through oral and poster
 presentations.
 3. Strengthen the U.S. scientific pipeline in areas relevant to Basic Energy Sciences, particularly
 radiation chemistry, radiation physics, and charged-particle processes.
 4. Promote networking, collaboration, and professional development among early-career U.S.
 researchers.

All project objectives were fully met.
The number of supported investigators exceeded typical participation rates for this conference, demonstrating
that even modest DOE support can substantially improve early-career engagement.

3. TECHNICAL ACTIVITIES AND SCIENTIFIC PARTICIPATION

3.1 Participant Selection and Support Mechanism

Eligible participants were graduate students, postdoctoral scholars, and junior early-career investigators
affiliated with U.S. universities and national laboratories who were presenting at ICIP 2024.

Support included:

 • 19 registration fee discounts of $240 each, totaling $4560
 • 12 travel-support awards of $620 each, totaling $7440

Participants submitted abstracts and presentation confirmations as part of the internal review and selection
process.

3.2 Presentations and Scientific Contributions

The supported investigators collectively delivered a substantial number of oral and poster presentations. Topics
included:

 • Workshop for Young Investigators
 • Fundamental Advances in Radiation Chemistry
 • Theoretical and Computational Radiation Science
 • Radiation-driven Processes in Nuclear Environments
 • Radiation Chemistry and the Environment
 • Radiation-induced Materials Synthesis and Damage

These contributions directly align with BES goals of advancing the fundamental understanding of energy-
related chemical and physical processes.

4. IMPACTS AND BENEFITS

4.1 Scientific Impact

 • Increased representation of U.S. early-career researchers at ICIP 2024.
 • Broader dissemination of fundamental radiation-chemistry research conducted at U.S. institutions.
 • Enhanced cross-institutional visibility of emerging investigators.
 • Strengthening of scientific collaboration networks that further the BES mission.

4.2 Workforce Development Impact

 • Improved retention of young scientists in fields central to BES-supported research.
 • Expanded opportunities for early-career researchers to gain professional experience presenting their
 work on an international stage.
 • Stronger U.S. presence in a historically international-dominated conference series.

11. CONCLUSIONS

The DOE BES-supported participant program substantially increased the representation and scientific
engagement of early-career U.S. investigators at ICIP 2024. The project successfully met all objectives,
strengthened the early-career scientific community, and ensured broader dissemination of research aligned with
BES's mission areas in radiation chemistry and fundamental chemical sciences.

The project is complete, and all administrative and technical obligations under the award have been fulfilled.
