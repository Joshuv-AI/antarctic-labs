Exact 6 6 -cut rigidity and small-order superconnectivity for the 6 6 -regular case of Dirac’s k = 4 k=4 problem
Alper Ferudun

Abstract
Dirac asked in 1970 whether for every k ≥ 4 k\geq 4 there is a k k -vertex-critical graph without critical edges; Jensen settled all k ≥ 5 k\geq 5 , and only k = 4 k=4 remains open. Following Skottova and Steiner, call a graph G G a ( 4 , 1 ) (4,1) -graph if χ ⁡ ( G ) = 4 \chi(G)=4 , χ ⁡ ( G − v ) = 3 \chi(G-v)=3 for every vertex v v , and χ ⁡ ( G − e ) = 4 \chi(G-e)=4 for every edge e e ; they proved δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 6 \delta(G)\geq 6 and λ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 6 \lambda(G)\geq 6 for every ( 4 , 1 ) (4,1) -graph and asked whether a 6 6 -regular ( 4 , 1 ) (4,1) -graph exists. We prove three results about this 6 6 -regular case. Theorem A (computational): there is no 6 6 -regular 4 4 -vertex-critical graph on n ≤ 15 n\leq 15 vertices, except for a unique graph (up to isomorphism) on n = 13 n=13 vertices, whose 13 13 critical edges form a Hamilton cycle. Consequently any 6 6 -regular ( 4 , 1 ) (4,1) -graph has at least 16 16 vertices. Theorem B : in a 6 6 -regular ( 4 , 1 ) (4,1) -graph, every 6 6 -edge-cut is either the edge star of a vertex or has both shores of size at least 15 15 ; consequently every 6 6 -regular ( 4 , 1 ) (4,1) -graph on at most 29 29 vertices is super- 6 6 -edge-connected (every 6 6 -edge-cut is the edge star of a single vertex). Theorem C (all sizes): no shore of a nontrivial 6 6 -edge-cut in a 6 6 -regular ( 4 , 1 ) (4,1) -graph induces a bipartite graph; more generally, a shore whose deficiency is concentrated on two vertices forces them to receive equal colours in every proper 3 3 -colouring. The proof plays the cut-matrix classification against vertex-criticality and is sharp: the incidence graph of PG ⁡ ( 2 , 5 ) \mathrm{PG}(2,5) yields deficiency- 6 6 candidates that pass all one-sided counting filters. The proof of Theorem B combines an exact classification of the 3 × 3 3\times 3 cut matrices of 6 6 -edge-cuts in ( 4 , 1 ) (4,1) -graphs (exactly 21 21 matrices, five types up to row/column permutations) with four local necessary conditions on a hypothetical shore, the strongest being a boundary-shortfall lemma derived from vertex-deleted colourings; a finite, exhaustively enumerated candidate space then collapses. The unique near-miss is K 3 , 3 , 3 K_{3,3,3} minus a rainbow 3 3 -matching (one edge between each pair of parts, six distinct endpoints), which survives all counting filters and is excluded by the boundary-shortfall lemma alone. Several supporting lemmas are machine-checked in Lean 4/Mathlib (no native_decide ); the shore-exclusion counts are independently reproduced in a separate Python implementation for cut sizes a ≤ 11 a\leq 11 .

A

Take a

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

Exact rigidity of
6
-edge-cuts

Let

m_{ij}=|\{ab\in F:\alpha(a)=i,\ \beta(b)=j\}|

Lemma 3.1
.

In a target:
D_{\pi}(M)=0

(
[

Proof.

D_{\pi}=0
gives a
3
-colouring of
G
;
D_{\pi}=1
gives a
3
-colouring of
G-e

for the unique monochromatic cut edge

. Each cut edge lies on exactly two of
the six permutation diagonals, giving the average
|F|/3
; for

∎

Theorem 3.2

(
21
-matrix classification)
.

Let

be a

matrix with nonnegative integer entries, total sum
6
,
and

0&0&0\\
