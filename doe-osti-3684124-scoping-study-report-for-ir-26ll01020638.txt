1. Introduction
1.1 Overview
The report describes the scoping calculations performed as part of the DNCSH project IR-
26LL01020638, a proposal between Sandia National Laboratories and Lawrence Livermore National
Laboratory to perform chlorine salt experiments with the 7uPCX fuel at Sandia National Laboratory.
This proposal spawned from interest by TerraPower for their chlorine-based Generation IV reactor
design. However, due to the closure of their program, the project continued without their direct
involvement. As such, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory has taken over the experimental
design process and will work intimately with Sandia National Laboratories to execute and analyze
these experiments. The goal of this project is to perform critical experiments using NaCl and KCl salt
filled absorber rods within the Sandia 7uPCX framework at SPRF/CX. The experiments are aimed at
addressing water ingress scenarios for criticality safety for fuel load/storage and fuel transportation
stages in the nuclear fuel cycle for fissile salts that utilize HALEU. While this project may have the
opportunity to use the new HALEU fuel at Sandia, it is unlikely that the fuel will be fabricated with
sufficient time to perform experiments. As such, this project focuses on designs using the standard
7% enriched fuel rods with the anticipation that future experiments with the HALEU fuel could build
upon this work.
There is a notable lack of chlorine data sufficient for fissile salt validation. Within the ICSBEP1 there
are no KCl configurations and only thirteen NaCl configurations spanning all neutron energies and
fissile species. Additionally, two of the thirteen configurations were performed prior to 1985, which
indicates lower confidence in the evaluations for validation purposes. Within the available
benchmarks, only two benchmark cases provide validation for NaCl with H2O reflection, highlighting
a gap in the criticality safety validation basis for fissile chloride salts. This experiment set serves to
address this gap while also providing a fully decorrelated benchmark which poses a significant
benefit when considering the potential use of it for nuclear data validation.
The experiment will be designed based on previous 7uPCX benchmark evaluations (LEU-COMP-
THERM (LCT)-078, LCT-080, LCT-096, LCT-097, LCT-101, LCT-102, LCT-111, and LCT-112). These
experiments demonstrate a proven path for the design, execution, and analysis of critical
experiments that produce high-quality benchmark evaluations with experimental uncertainties near
or below 100 pcm. The information presented within this report documents the scoping study,
utilizing the designs from previous benchmark evaluations, of NaCl and KCl salt absorbers within the
7uPCX framework to address the needs of DNCSH, based on the Call #22 sensitivity profiles.

2. Intermixed Salt Configurations

2.1 Interspersed Chloride Salts
Scoping studies were performed using the Case 12 configuration of LCT-102. This configuration uses
the 0.800 cm grid and has 1.132 cm spacing by placing fuel rods in every other space, as shown in
Figure 1. In this configuration there are 471 fuel rods, which includes 12 control/safety elements. The
spacing between the fuel rods provides an optimal configuration to include equally interspersed
absorber rods. Three configurations of sodium chloride absorbers were tested, bunching them in the
center of the array, to identify the trend of total chlorine sensitivity as a function of the number of
absorber rods in the configuration. The three test configurations are shown in Figure 2, Figure 3, and
Figure 4.

The goal of this study was to identify the trend of total and capture 35Cl sensitivity in the configuration
with respect to the number of absorber rods. Given that the system will be deeply thermal, the
integral sensitivities were analyzed instead of the sensitivity profiles. The integral 35Cl total and
capture sensitivities for each configuration are provided in Table 1. The trend is shown visually in
Figure 5. Ultimately, this study highlighted that the number of absorber rods linearly affects the total
sensitivity and the greater number of rods, the greater the sensitivity. This study indicates that the
maximum possible number of absorber rods should be procured to achieve the greatest sensitivity.

3. Central Test Region Configurations
3.1 Central Test Region Design
The Central Test Region (CTR) is a grid-plate design with a centralized dry well in the center, hence
central test region, where test rods may be placed. This effectively creates a high-worth region with
minimal water-induced moderation to push the neutron spectrum faster than normal while also
increasing sensitivity to the test rod materials. A diagram of the CTR grid plate design is shown in
Figure 6.

 Figure 6: Grid plate example with Central Test Region without absorber rods.

3.2 Summary of CTR Designs
Additional configurations were tested within the CTR framework. It was found, which may be apparent from the
already presented results in this section, that the number of absorber rods and their orientation play little to no
role in the bulk sensitivity to chlorine. The NaCl 35Cl(n,p) sensitivity profiles for all tested configurations are
shown in Figure 14 (NaCl) and Figure 15 (KCl). However, we do observe an epithermal bump for these CTR
configurations which may be an advantageous feature to pursue, especially for reactor validation.

4. Conclusions
A scoping study was performed for the 7uPCX experimental campaign with NaCl and KCl absorber rods. Both
Central Test Region and intermixed configurations were tested and analyzed against the sensitivity profiles
from the DNCSH proposal call #2. This report highlights the work that was performed and summarizes the
conclusions to meet the scoping study milestone of IR-26LL01020638 prior to proceeding to the experimental
design phase of the project.

Using LCT-102, Case 12 as a design base, absorber rods were interspersed within an already critical design.
Given that the system will be deeply thermal, the integral 35Cl total and capture sensitivities were used, instead
of the sensitivity profiles, to present a path forward for the experimental design. It was found, which may be
obvious but needed to be verified, that the more absorber rods in the configuration, the greater the sensitivity
to the chlorine it is. This highlights the need to prioritize procuring the maximum number of absorber rods
possible. It is also important to highlight that the total sensitivity to 35Cl is dominated by the capture sensitivity,
which is the reaction channel of interest. The goal of this design is to maximize the thermal capture sensitivity
to provide validation for the thermal configuration in the DNCSH Call #2.

Multiple CTR configurations were testing spanning from grouping the absorber rods and creating more intricate
rings within the dry well. However, it was immediately apparent through quantitative and qualitative analysis
against the DNCSH Call #2 sensitivity profiles for the 35Cl(n,p) and 35Cl(n,total) reaction channels, that the
sensitivity achieved for these configurations were an order of magnitude too low to meet the needs of the call.
However, it should be noted that an interesting epithermal hump was produced for these experiments and may
be pursued, especially if this feature becomes interesting for other purposes.

Moving forward towards the experimental design, it is likely that at least one configuration should pull from
both design types to provide adequate thermal sensitivity but provide the epithermal bump which may be
important for industry.
