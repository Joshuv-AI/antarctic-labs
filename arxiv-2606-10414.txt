On superspecial hyperelliptic curves of Rosenhain forms
Ryo Ohashi

Abstract
Any genus- g g hyperelliptic curve C C defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p ≥ 3 p\geq 3 can be written in a Rosenhain form as y 2 = x ⁡ ( x − 1 ) ​ ∏ i = 1 2 ​ g − 1 ( x − λ i ) y^{2}=x(x-1)\prod_{i=1}^{2g-1}(x-\lambda_{i}) . In this paper, we first show that, if C C is superspecial, then each of λ i , 1 − λ i \lambda_{i},1-\lambda_{i} , and λ i − λ j \lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j} is a square in 𝔽 p 2 \mathbb{F}_{p^{2}} . As an application, we propose a new algorithm for enumerating superspecial hyperelliptic curves in small characteristic. By implementing our algorithm, we successfully computed the number of isomorphism classes of such curves of genera 4 4 and 5 5 in all characteristics p ≤ 41 p\leq 41 , and of genus 6 6 in all characteristics p ≤ 31 p\leq 31 .

Introduction

Throughout this paper, a
curve
always means a non-singular projective variety of dimension one defined over a field of characteristic
p>0
.
All
isomorphisms
are taken over the algebraic closure of the base field, unless otherwise stated.
A curve is said to be
superspecial
if its Jacobian is isomorphic to a product of supersingular elliptic curves (as unpolarized abelian varieties).
In recent years, superspecial curves have found applications in areas such as isogeny-based cryptography and algebraic geometry codes.
Consequently, understanding the properties of such curves has become an important research topic.
For a positive integer
g
and a prime
p
, it is known (cf.
[
5
, Corollary 1.2]
) that the number of isomorphism classes of superspecial curves of genus
g
in characteristic
p
is finite, and the following is a fundamental problem:

Problem 1.1
.

For a given pair
(g,p)
, determine the number of isomorphism classes of superspecial genus-
g
curves in characteristic
p
.

This problem has been solved for genera
g\leq 3
, but for
g\geq 4
, even the existence of such curves remains open in general
p
.
We refer to
[
2
]
for a survey of related works.

On the other hand, the focus of this paper is restricted to
hyperelliptic
curves.
More precisely, we consider the following problem:

Problem 1.2
.

For a given pair
(g,p)
, determine the number of isomorphism classes of superspecial genus-
g
hyperelliptic curves in characteristic
p
.

Since every genus-2 curve is hyperelliptic, Problem
1.2
is exactly the same as Problem
1.1
for the case
g=2
, and hence has already been solved, as mentioned above.
However, for every
\hskip-0.56905ptg\geq 3
, Problem
1.2
remains open in general
p
.
Nevertheless, for small characteristics
p
, the answers to Problem
1.2
are known in several cases, as summarized below:

•

Ekedahl showed in
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
that if there exists a superspecial hyperelliptic curve of genus
g\geq 2
in characteristic
p
, then
2g\leq p-1
holds.
Furthermore, Valentini proved in
[
9
, Theorem 1]
that there exists a unique isomorphism class of such curves, namely the curve
y^{2}=x^{p}-x
, when
2g=p-1
holds.
In other words, the answer to Problem
1.2
is
0
for
p\leq 2g-1
, and is
1
for
p=2g+1
.

•

In the case of genus
g=3
, the number of isomorphism classes for every characteristic
7<p<100
was determined in
[
8
]
by using the superspecial Richelot isogeny graph.

•

In the case of genus
g=4
, Kudo and Harashita determined in
[
3
,
4
]
the number of isomorphism classes for the characteristics
p=11,13,17
, and
19
by using Gröbner basis computations.

To the best of our knowledge, for genus
g\in\hskip-0.85358pt\{5,6\}
, the number of isomorphism classes has not been determined for any characteristic
p>2g+1
.

The main purpose of this paper is to extend the known results on Problem
1.2
by determining the number of isomorphism classes of superspecial hyperelliptic curves of genera
4,5
, and
6
in characteristics beyond the range previously covered in the literature.
To achieve this goal, we first establish the following useful theorem, which provides a necessary condition for hyperelliptic curves in
Rosenhain forms
to be superspecial:

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
p>2
. If a genus-
g
hyperelliptic curve

C:y^{2}=x(x-1)\prod_{i=1}^{2g-1}(x-\lambda_{i})\vskip-0.85358pt

in characteristic
p
is superspecial, then all of
\lambda_{i},1-\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}
are squares in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\hskip-0.56905pt
for all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,2g-1\}
.

This theorem can be seen as a generalization of
[
7
, Main Theorem A(1)]
, which corresponds to the case
g=2
.
As an application of this theorem, we propose a new algorithm (Algorithm
3.1
) for enumerating superspecial genus-
g
hyperelliptic curves in characteristic
p
for fixed
(g,p)
.
Essentially, the algorithm consists of collecting all sets
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}\}
satisfying the condition of Theorem
1.3
and then checking whether the Cartier-Manin matrix of the corresponding curve is zero (for details on the Cartier-Manin matrix, see
[
11
, Section 2]
).
Our approach has the advantage of avoiding Gröbner basis computations and allowing isomorphism testing to be performed relatively easily.
For small characteristics
p
, since the number of candidate sets
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}\}
is sufficiently small, our algorithm is computationally feasible.
By implementing and running the algorithm in
Magma
, we obtain the following main results:

.

(4)

There are precisely
23

.

(5)

There are precisely
34

.

.

2
Hyperelliptic curves of Rosenhain forms

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>2
.
In this section, we shall consider a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g\geq 2
defined by the affine equation

H:Y^{2}=c\prod_{i=1}^{2g+1}(X-a_{i})\eqqcolon f(X),\quad c\in K^{\times},\vskip-2.84526pt

(2.1)

where the
a_{i}
are distinct elements of
\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 13.0mu}}}{K}
(we also set
a_{2g+2}\coloneqq\infty
).
For any permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\ldots,2g+2\}
, the Möbius transformation

X\longmapsto\frac{X-a_{\sigma(2g)}}{X-a_{\sigma(2g+2)}}\cdot\frac{a_{\sigma(2g+1)}-a_{\sigma(2g+2)}}{a_{\sigma(2g+1)}-a_{\sigma(2g)}}\eqqcolon x

maps
a_{\sigma(2g)},a_{\sigma(2g+1)}
, and
a_{\sigma(2g+2)}
to
0,1
, and
\infty
, respectively.
After a suitable change of the
Y
-coordinate, it then induces an isomorphism over
\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 13.0mu}}}{K}
from
H
to its
Rosenhain form

y^{2}=x(x-1)\prod_{i=1}^{2g-1}(x-\lambda_{i}),\quad\lambda_{i}\coloneqq\frac{a_{\sigma(i)}-a_{\sigma(2g)}}{a_{\sigma(i)}-a_{\sigma(2g+2)}}\cdot\frac{a_{\sigma(2g+1)}-a_{\sigma(2g+2)}}{a_{\sigma(2g+1)}-a_{\sigma(2g)}}.\vskip-1.13809pt

Conversely, every Rosenhain form of
H
arises from a permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\ldots,2g+2\}
in this way.

Remark 2.1
.

Since the Rosenhain form obtained above depends only on the images of
2g,2g\hskip-0.28453pt+\hskip-0.28453pt1
, and
2g\hskip-0.28453pt+\hskip-0.28453pt2
under
\sigma
, the number of distinct Rosenhain forms of
H
is at most
(2g\hskip-0.28453pt+\hskip-0.28453pt2)\cdot(2g\hskip-0.28453pt+\hskip-0.28453pt1)\cdot(2g)=4g(g+1)(2g+1)
.

Next, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,2g+1\}
, let
P_{i}\coloneqq(a_{i},0)
be a Weierstrass point of
H
.
Then, the divisor class

D_{i}\coloneqq[P_{i}]-[\infty]\ \text{ with }\,i\in\{1,\ldots,2g+1\}

(2.2)

is a
2
-torsion point of the Jacobian
J
of
H
, and its Mumford representation is given by
[X-a_{i},0]
.
Moreover, the Mumford representations of the half-points of
D_{i}
are described as follows (in particular, when
c=1
, the resulting formula coincides with
[
12
, Theorem 3.2]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Fix
i\in\hskip-0.28453pt\{1,\ldots,2g+1\}
.
With the above notation, for each
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,2g+1\}\!\smallsetminus\!\{i\}
, let
r_{\ell}\in\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 13.0mu}}}{K}^{\times}\hskip-0.85358pt
be an arbitrarily chosen square root of
c(a_{i}-a_{\ell})
.
Define the tuple
\bm{r}\coloneqq(r_{\ell})_{\ell\neq i}
and

\displaystyle u_{i}(X)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{(-1)^{g}}{c^{g}}\sum_{k=0}^{g}e_{2k}(\bm{r})\bigl(-c(X-a_{i})\bigr)^{g-k},

(2.3)

\displaystyle v_{i}(X)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{c^{g}}\sum_{k=1}^{g}e_{2k-1}(\bm{r})\bigl(-c(X-a_{i})\bigr)^{g-k+1},

where
e_{k}
denotes the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial.
Then
[u_{i}(X),v_{i}(X)\bmod u_{i}(X)]
is a Mumford representation of a point
T_{i}\in J(\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 13.0mu}}}{K})
satisfying
2T_{i}=D_{i}
.

Proof.

Set
t\coloneqq-c(X-a_{i})
, and put

U(t)\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{g}e_{2k}(\bm{r})t^{g-k},\quad V(t)\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{g}e_{2k-1}(\bm{r})t^{g-k}.

Let
z
be a formal variable with
z^{2}=t
.
By definition of the elementary symmetric polynomials, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{\ell\neq i}(z+r_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{2g}e_{k}(\bm{r})z^{2g-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{g}e_{2k}(\bm{r})z^{2g-2k}+\sum_{k=1}^{g}e_{2k-1}(\bm{r})z^{2g-2k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{g}e_{2k}(\bm{r})t^{g-k}+z\sum_{k=1}^{g}e_{2k-1}(\bm{r})t^{g-k}=U(t)+zV(t).

This equation implies that

\displaystyle U(t)^{2}-tV(t)^{2}

\displaystyle=(U(t)+zV(t))(U(t)-zV(t))

\displaystyle=\prod_{\ell\neq i}(z+r_{\ell})\cdot\prod_{\ell\neq i}(-z+r_{\ell})=\prod_{\ell\neq i}(r_{\ell}^{2}-t).

On the other hand, since
r_{\ell}^{2}=c(a_{i}-a_{\ell})
, we obtain
r_{\ell}^{2}-t=c(a_{i}-a_{\ell})+c(X-a_{i})=c(X-a_{\ell})
, and hence, the above equation can be transformed into

U(t)^{2}-tV(t)^{2}=c^{2g}\prod_{\ell\neq i}(X-a_{\ell}).

Then, the right-hand side
f(X)
of (
2.1
) is written as

f(X)=c(X-a_{i})\prod_{\ell\neq i}(X-a_{\ell})=-\frac{t}{c^{2g}}(U(t)^{2}-tV(t)^{2}).\vskip 1.99168pt

It follows from the definition of
U(t)
and
V(t)
that

u_{i}(X)=\frac{(-1)^{g}}{c^{g}}U(t),\quad v_{i}(X)=\frac{t}{c^{g}}V(t),

which gives the key equation

\displaystyle f(X)-v_{i}(X)^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{t}{c^{2g}}(U(t)^{2}-tV(t)^{2})-\frac{t^{2}}{c^{2g}}V(t)^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{t}{c^{2g}}U(t)^{2}

\displaystyle=c(X-a_{i})u_{i}(X)^{2}.

In particular, we see that
f(X)-v_{i}(X)^{2}
is a multiple of
u_{i}(X)
.
Also, it is clear that

•

u_{i}(X)
is a monic polynomial of degree
g
,

•

v_{i}(X)\bmod{u_{i}(X)}
has degree less than
g
,

and thus
[u_{i}(X),v_{i}(X)\bmod{u_{i}(X)}]
is a valid Mumford representation (cf.
[
10
, Theorem 13.7]
).
Furthermore, by Cantor’s doubling algorithm (cf.
[
10
, Theorem 13.10]
), the
u
-polynomial of the double of the divisor class represented by
[u_{i}(X),v_{i}(X)\bmod{u_{i}(X)}]
is given by
X-a_{i}
from the key equation

\frac{f(X)-v_{i}(X)^{2}}{u_{i}(X)^{2}}=c(X-a_{i}).\vskip-1.13809pt

The corresponding
v
-polynomial is necessarily
0
since
\deg(X-a_{i})=1
and
f(a_{i})=0
(cf.
[
10
, Example 13.2]
).
Consequently, we finally obtain

2[u_{i}(X),v_{i}(X)\bmod{u_{i}(X)}]=[X-a_{i},0],\vskip 0.85358pt

that is,
[u_{i}(X),v_{i}(X)\bmod{u_{i}(X)}]
represents a point
T_{i}\in J(\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 13.0mu}}}{K})
satisfying
2T_{i}=D_{i}
.

∎

Remark 2.3
.

In Lemma
2.2
, each square root
r_{\ell}
may be chosen independently up to sign.
Hence, there are
2^{2g}
possible choices for the tuple
\bm{r}=(r_{\ell})_{\ell\neq i}
, yielding
2^{2g}
half-points of
D_{i}
.
This is consistent with the fact that
D_{i}
has exactly
2^{2g}
half points, since the
2
-torsion subgroup
J[2]
has order
2^{2g}
.

Lemma
2.2
provides explicit Mumford representations of certain 4-torsion points on the Jacobian
J
of
H
.
We now prove the following proposition, which plays a central role in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
J
be the Jacobian of the hyperelliptic curve
H
as in (
2.1
).
Suppose that the
4
-torsion subgroup
J[4]
is defined over
K
.
Then, for any pairwise distinct
i,j,k\in\{1,\ldots,2g+1\}
, the ratio

\frac{a_{i}-a_{j}}{a_{i}-a_{k}}

(2.4)

is a square in
K
.

Proof.

Since
J[4]
is defined over
K
, the
2
-torsion subgroup
J[2]
is also defined over
K
.
Hence, in particular, the divisor class
D_{i}
defined by (
2.2
) is
K
-rational for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,2g+1\}
.
Recalling that the Mumford representation of
D_{i}
is
[X-a_{i},0]
, we obtain

a_{i}\in K
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,2g+1\}
.

Fix pairwise distinct
i,j,k\in\{1,\ldots,2g+1\}
.
For each
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,2g+1\}\!\smallsetminus\!\{i\}
, choose an arbitrary square root
r_{\ell}\in\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 13.0mu}}}{K}^{\times}\hskip-0.85358pt
of
c(a_{i}-a_{\ell})
, and define
\bm{r}\coloneqq(r_{\ell})_{\ell\neq i}
.
Also, let
\bm{r}^{(j)},\bm{r}^{(k)}
, and
\bm{r}^{(jk)}
denote the tuples obtained from
\bm{r}
by changing the sign of
r_{j}
, the sign of
r_{k}
, and the signs of both
r_{j}
and
r_{k}
, respectively.
By Lemma
2.2
,

each of these choices determines a half-point of
D_{i}
.
Since
J[4]
is defined over
K
, all of these half-points are
K
-rational.
Hence, the corresponding
u
-polynomials have coefficients in
K
.
In particular, by considering the coefficients of
(X-a_{i})^{g-1}
in (
2.3
), we obtain

e_{2}(\bm{r}),\ e_{2}(\bm{r}^{(j)}),\ e_{2}(\bm{r}^{(k)}),\ e_{2}(\bm{r}^{(jk)})\in K\vskip-0.56905pt

(2.5)

since
c\in K^{\times}
.
On the other hand, a direct computation gives

e_{2}(\bm{r})-e_{2}(\bm{r}^{(j)})-e_{2}(\bm{r}^{(k)})+e_{2}(\bm{r}^{(jk)})=4r_{j}r_{k}.\vskip 0.85358pt

(2.6)

Indeed, for a monomial
r_{a}r_{b}
, its contribution to
e_{2}(\bm{r})-e_{2}(\bm{r}^{(j)})-e_{2}(\bm{r}^{(k)})+e_{2}(\bm{r}^{(jk)})
is as follows:

•

If
\{a,b\}=\{j,k\}
, then its contribution is
r_{j}r_{k}-(-r_{j})r_{k}-r_{j}(-r_{k})+(-r_{j})(-r_{k})=4r_{j}r_{k}
.

•

If exactly one of
a,b
belongs to
\{j,k\}
, then its contribution is

r_{j}r_{b}-(-r_{j})r_{b}-r_{j}r_{b}+(-r_{j})r_{b}=0\ \text{ or }\ r_{a}r_{k}-r_{a}r_{k}-r_{a}(-r_{k})+r_{a}(-r_{k})=0.\vskip-3.69885pt

•

Otherwise, its contribution is
r_{a}r_{b}-r_{a}r_{b}-r_{a}r_{b}+r_{a}r_{b}=0
.

Hence, all terms cancel except
4r_{j}r_{k}
, which proves (
2.6
).
By combining (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we obtain
r_{j}r_{k}\in K
. Since
r_{k}^{2}=c(a_{i}-a_{k})\in K^{\times}
, it follows that

\frac{a_{i}-a_{j}}{a_{i}-a_{k}}=\frac{r_{j}^{2}}{r_{k}^{2}}=\biggl(\frac{r_{j}r_{k}}{r_{k}^{2}}\biggr)^{\!2}\vskip-3.41432pt

is a square in
K
, as desired.
∎

At the end of this section, we give a proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

It is well-known (cf.
[
1
, p. 166]
) that
C
descends to a curve
H
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\hskip-0.56905pt
such that the Frobenius endomorphism
F
of its Jacobian
J
acts as
\pm p
.
Then, we choose
H
as follows:

—

If
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, we choose
H
such that
F
is equal to
-p
.

—

If
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, we choose
H
such that
F
is equal to
p
.

In either case, the Frobenius endomorphism
F
acts trivially on
J[4]
.
This shows that every point of
J[4]
is fixed by
F
, and thus
J[4]
is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.
As
H
is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, it admits a model of the form

H:\ Y^{2}=c\prod_{i=1}^{2g+2}(X-a_{i}),\qquad c\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\vskip-0.85358pt

where all the
a_{i}
are distinct elements of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
Let
P=(a_{2g},0),\hskip 1.99168ptQ=(a_{2g+1},0)
, and
R=(a_{2g+2},0)
, which are Weierstrass points of
H
.
Since
J[2]
is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, the divisor classes
[P]-[Q],\hskip 2.27621pt[Q]-[R]
, and
[R]-[P]
are
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
-rational.
Hence, their Mumford representations are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, so the corresponding
u
-polynomials

(X-a_{2g})(X-a_{2g+1}),\quad(X-a_{2g+1})(X-a_{2g+2}),\quad(X-a_{2g+2})(X-a_{2g})\vskip-0.28453pt

have coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.
It follows that

a_{2g+2}=\frac{(a_{2g+2}+a_{2g})-(a_{2g}+a_{2g+1})+(a_{2g+1}+a_{2g+2})}{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}.

After an
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
-rational Möbius transformation sending
a_{2g+2}
to
\infty
, we may therefore assume that
a_{2g+2}=\infty
.
Then
H
is given by an odd-degree model

H:\ Y^{2}=c\prod_{i=1}^{2g+1}(X-a_{i}),\quad c\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}.\vskip-12.80373pt

Recalling that
C
and
H
are isomorphic over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
(i.e.,
C
is a Rosenhain form of
H
), there exists a permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\ldots,2g+2\}
such that

\lambda_{i}=\frac{a_{\sigma(i)}-a_{\sigma(2g)}}{a_{\sigma(i)}-a_{\sigma(2g+2)}}\cdot\frac{a_{\sigma(2g+1)}-a_{\sigma(2g+2)}}{a_{\sigma(2g+1)}-a_{\sigma(2g)}}\vskip-1.13809pt

for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,2g-1\}
.
Since
a_{2g+2}=\infty
, the right-hand side is a product of factors, each of which is either
1
or a ratio of the form (
2.4
).
Therefore
\lambda_{i}
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\hskip-1.13809pt
by Proposition
2.4
.
In addition, a direct computation shows that, for every
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,2g-1\}
,

\displaystyle 1-\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{\sigma(i)}-a_{\sigma(2g+1)}}{a_{\sigma(i)}-a_{\sigma(2g+2)}}\cdot\frac{a_{\sigma(2g)}-a_{\sigma(2g+2)}}{a_{\sigma(2g)}-a_{\sigma(2g+1)}},

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{\sigma(i)}-a_{\sigma(j)}}{a_{\sigma(i)}-a_{\sigma(2g+2)}}\cdot\frac{a_{\sigma(2g+2)}-a_{\sigma(2g)}}{a_{\sigma(2g+2)}-a_{\sigma(j)}}\cdot\frac{a_{\sigma(2g+1)}-a_{\sigma(2g+2)}}{a_{\sigma(2g+1)}-a_{\sigma(2g)}},

whose right-hand sides are products of factors, each of which is either
1
or of the form (
2.4
).
Hence, applying Proposition
2.4
once again, we conclude that
1-\lambda_{i}
and
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}
are squares in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\hskip-1.13809pt
as well.
∎

3
Enumeration of superspecial hyperelliptic curves

In this section, we present an algorithm for enumerating superspecial genus-
g
hyperelliptic curves in characteristic
p
for fixed
(g,p)
, and report on the computational results obtained by implementing it.

3.1
Algorithm

The basic idea of our algorithm is simple.
We first enumerate all sets
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}\}
satisfying the condition of Theorem
1.3
.
For each such set
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}\}
, we then determine whether the corresponding hyperelliptic curve in Rosenhain form

C:y^{2}=x(x-1)\prod_{i=1}^{2g-1}(x-\lambda_{i})\eqqcolon f(x)\vskip 1.13809pt

is superspecial; equivalently, whether the Cartier-Manin matrix of
C
is zero (cf.
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
).
As is well known (cf.
[
11
, Section 2]
), the Cartier-Manin matrix of
C
is given by

\begin{bmatrix}\gamma_{p-1}&\!\gamma_{p-2}&\!\cdots&\!\gamma_{p-g}\\
\gamma_{2p-1}&\!\gamma_{2p-2}&\!\cdots&\!\gamma_{2p-g}\\
\vdots&\!\vdots&\!\ddots&\!\vdots\\
\gamma_{gp-1}&\!\gamma_{gp-2}&\!\cdots&\!\gamma_{gp-g}\end{bmatrix}\hskip-1.70717pt,

where
\gamma_{k}
denotes the coefficient of
x^{k}
in the polynomial
f(x)^{(p-1)/2}
.
Therefore, it suffices to check whether the coefficient of
x^{ip-j}
in
f(x)^{(p-1)/2}
vanishes for every
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,g\}
.

We note that, in order to avoid enumerating isomorphic hyperelliptic curves multiple times, it is necessary to detect whether
C
is isomorphic to a curve that has already been enumerated.
As explained at the beginning of Section
2
, this can be determined as follows.
Let

C^{\prime}:y^{2}=x(x-1)\prod_{i=1}^{2g-1}(x-\lambda^{\prime}_{i})\vskip-0.56905pt

be another hyperelliptic curve in Rosenhain form, and define
a_{1},\ldots,a_{2g+2}
such that

\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{2g+2}\}=\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}\}\sqcup\{0,1,\infty\}\vskip 0.28453pt

Then
C
and
C^{\prime}
are isomorphic to each other if and only if there exist pairwise distinct

i,j,k\in\hskip-0.56905pt\{1,\ldots,2g+2\}
such that

\left\{\frac{a_{\ell}-a_{i}}{a_{\ell}-a_{k}}\cdot\frac{a_{j}-a_{k}}{a_{j}-a_{i}}\ \middle|\ \ell\in\{1,\ldots,2g+2\}\!\smallsetminus\!\{i,j,k\}\right\}=\{\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{2g-1}\}.\vskip 1.42262pt

(3.1)

Summarizing the above discussion, our algorithm can be described as Algorithm
3.1
below.

Algorithm 3.1
.

Input: An integer
g\geq 2
and a prime
p>2
.

Output: A list of representatives for isomorphism classes of superspecial genus-
g
hyperelliptic curves over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

Step 1.

Compute the set

S\coloneqq\{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\hskip-0.56905pt\mid\hskip 0.85358pt\text{both $\lambda$ and $1-\lambda$ are squares in $\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}$}\}\!\smallsetminus\!\{0,1\}.\vskip-0.85358pt

Initialize three empty lists
\hskip 0.56905pt\mathcal{C},\hskip 0.56905pt\mathcal{T}\hskip-0.56905pt
, and
\mathcal{L}
.

Step 2.

Recursively choose distinct
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}\in S
such that
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\hskip-0.85358pt
for every
i<j
.
If the resulting set
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}\}
does not belong to
\mathcal{T}
, then add it to the list
\mathcal{C}
.
Moreover, for every choice of pairwise distinct
i,j,k\in\{1,\ldots,2g+2\}
, add the sets

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{a_{\ell}-a_{i}}{a_{\ell}-a_{k}}\cdot\frac{a_{j}-a_{k}}{a_{j}-a_{i}}\ \middle|\ \ell\in\{1,\ldots,2g+2\}\!\smallsetminus\!\{i,j,k\}\right\}

to the list
\mathcal{T}
, where
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{2g+2}\}=\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}\}\sqcup\{0,1,\infty\}
.

Step 3.

For each element
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2g-1}\}
of
\mathcal{C}
, let

\displaystyle C:y^{2}=x(x-1)\prod_{i=1}^{2g-1}(x-\lambda_{i})\eqqcolon f(x).

If the coefficient of
x^{ip-j}
in
f(x)^{(p-1)/2}
vanishes for every
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,g\}
, add
C
to the list
\mathcal{L}
.

Step 4.

Output
\mathcal{L}
.
