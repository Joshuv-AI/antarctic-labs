Invariant polynomials and Mukai’s models of moduli spaces of curves and K3 surfaces
David Swinarski

Abstract
. Beginning in the 1980s, Mukai introduced birational models of some moduli spaces of curves and some moduli spaces of K3 surfaces. They are defined as geometric invariant theory quotients. Little is known about the boundaries of these spaces. We describe an approach to efficiently evaluate certain invariant polynomials associated to these GIT problems. This allows us to show that several singular curves and surfaces are GIT semistable in Mukai’s models. In the appendix, we give a combinatorial formula for an SL n \operatorname{SL}_{n} -invariant in terms of the Gelfand-Tsetlin basis.

2.
The GIT problems associated to Mukai’s models

Each Mukai model is the GIT quotient of a Grassmannian variety by a
simple linear algebraic group. We use the following notation.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simple Lie algebra of rank
n
. Let

\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}
be the fundamental dominant weights for

\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\lambda=\sum c_{i}\omega_{i}
be a dominant
integral weight. We write
V(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
for the irreducible

\mathfrak{g}
-module with highest weight
\lambda
.

Apology.
We
apologize for the notation
G_{g}
used below. On the one hand, this is natural,
since we have a sequence of groups
G
in each genus
g=7,8,9,10
. On the
other hand, it is terrible because one of these groups is the
exceptional group
G_{2}
. We trust that the reader can muddle through.

3.
Invariant polynomials associated to Mukai’s
models

We have constructed one or more invariant polynomials for each of
Mukai’s models. Here, for each Mukai model
\mathrm{MX}^{k}_{g}
, we describe
an invariant polynomial
F_{g,k}
that establishes GIT semistability
for our selected examples. We repeatedly
exploit the fact that the representations
\mathsf{\Lambda}^{g}V_{g}^{*}
and

\mathsf{\Lambda}^{g+1}V_{g}^{*}
are reducible. This permits us to efficiently evaluate
polynomials that are suitably adapted to these decompositions.

3.1.
How we selected these polynomials

Suppose that
\mathsf{\Lambda}^{g}V_{g}^{*}
decomposes into irreducibles

\oplus V(\lambda_{i})^{m_{i}}
. We typically select the irreducible
V(\lambda_{i})
with smallest dimension. Then we seek the lowest degree
d
such that
\operatorname{Sym}^{d}V(\lambda_{i})
contains an invariant. If
d=4,6
we may seek trivial
G
-modules among the products of submodules of
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}V(\lambda_{i})
. When doing so, we typically select submodules of small dimension, but not necessarily the smallest ones.

Mukai’s model is

\mathrm{MX}^{1}_{7}=\operatorname{Gr}(7,V_{7})/\!\!/G_{7}=\operatorname{Gr}(7,V(0,0,0,1,0))/\!\!/\operatorname{Spin}(10).

Here
V_{7}

\operatorname{Spin}(10)

5.1.
Strategy

Consider the descriptions of the polynomials

These polynomials can be constructed by repeating the following task

\mathfrak{g}
-module

find a submodule

that is isomorphic to the irreducible representation

.

We can do this as follows.

(1)

Compute the explicit action of the lowering operators of

(2)

Each of these steps is implemented in the

5.5.
Comparison to earlier work

In
[
Swinarski2025
]
, a less efficient algorithm was used to evaluate the less strategically chosen
\operatorname{Spin}(10)
-invariant polynomial described there, and the calculations analogous to those described in the proof of
Theorem
1.1
required very large amounts of time and memory. They
were accomplished by parallel calculations on four AWS

r5.24xlarge
instances, each with 96 vCPUs and 768 GB
memory. This took approximately 36 hours, and cost over $1000.

In contrast, using the new approach described here, the calculations

Due to the amount of memory required by the approach used in

is saved when the

marginal cost of evaluating these polynomials on new examples is
trivial.

Appendix A
A combinatorial formula for an
\operatorname{SL}_{n}
-invariant

Let
G
be a simple algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
V(\lambda)
be an
irreducible representation with highest weight
\lambda
. Then the
space of invariants of the following form is one-dimensional.

\dim(V(\lambda)\otimes V(\lambda^{*}))^{G}=1

Many bases of the irreducible representations
V(\lambda)
have been
introduced in the literature, particularly when
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}
. In this
note we work with the Gelfand-Tsetlin basis for these representations. See

[
Molev2006
]
*Section 2 for an introduction.

Our goal is to prove the following formula. The notation will be
explained below in Section
A.1
.

Theorem A.1
.

Let

F=\sum_{P\in\operatorname{GT}(\lambda)}\frac{(-1)^{\ell(P)}}{\|P\|\|P^{*}\|}e_{P}\otimes e_{P^{*}}.

Then
F
is an
\operatorname{SL}_{n}
-invariant vector in
V(\lambda)\otimes V(\lambda^{*})
.

Algebraic combinatorics and invariant theory are research fields with
a long history and a large literature, and it would not surprise the
author if this formula is already known. Please contact
the author with relevant references.

Theorem
A.1
follows easily
from formulas for the action of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
in such works as

[
Molev2006
]
or
[
Z
]
. Unfortunately, we have not yet located a single
reference that has all the results we need, and it is not clear that
the various sources we draw on adopt the same sign conventions. Thus, we aim to give a
self-contained exposition that explains what we implemented in the

LieAlgebraRepresentations
package in
Macaulay2
.

The following discussion will be mostly
combinatorial in nature and develop only what we need. We adopted this
approach in the hope that it would be efficient and amenable to
formalization in the future. However, this
leaves out lots of beautiful mathematics. We warmly encourage interested
readers to explore the references cited here for more details.

We computed a number of examples in
Macaulay2
to
experimentally verify the formulas in this note, and wrote code to verify to illustrate
each of the formulas in this note. These files may be found at the author’s
webpage.

A.1.
Notation

A.1.1.
From weights to partitions

We mostly follow the definitions given by Molev in
[
Molev2006
]
*Section 2,
except that we use
\lambda
to denote a weight of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}

and denote the associated partition by
\pi(\lambda)
. (In
[
Molev2006
]
,

\lambda
is the partition.)

Let
\lambda=\sum a_{i}\omega_{i}
be a dominant integral weight for

\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
, written in the basis of fundamental
dominant weights
\omega_{i}
. The partition
\pi(\lambda)
associated
to
\lambda
has parts

\pi_{i}=\sum_{j=i}^{n-1}a_{i}

for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
, and we set
\pi_{n}=0
.

A.1.2.
The pattern
P

A
Gelfand-Tsetlin pattern of shape
\pi(\lambda)
is a triangular array of the following form.

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}x_{n,1}&&x_{n,2}&&x_{n,3}&&\cdots&&x_{n,n}\\
&x_{n-1,1}&&x_{n-1,2}&&\cdots&x_{n-1,n-1}&\\
&&\ddots\\
&&&x_{2,1}&&x_{2,2}\\
&&&&x_{1,1}\end{array}

Each entry
x_{i,j}
is a nonnegative integer, the top row
x_{n,i}
corresponds to
\pi(\lambda)
, and the entries satisfy the inequalities
x_{k,i}\geq x_{k-1,i}\geq x_{k,i+1}
.

We write
\operatorname{GT}(\lambda)
for the set of all Gelfand-Tsetlin patterns of
shape
\pi(\lambda)
. This set indexes a basis of
V(\lambda)
. We write

e_{P}
for the basis element corresponding to the pattern
P\in\operatorname{GT}(\lambda)
.

A.1.3.
The pattern
P^{*}

Let
P\in\operatorname{GT}(\lambda)
. We define the
dual pattern

P^{*}
by the formula

y_{i,j}=x_{n,1}-x_{j+1-i,j}.

A.1.4.
The numerator
(-1)^{\ell(P)}

The
level

\ell(P)
is defined as the level of the
weight
\operatorname{wt}(P)
. We briefly recall these definitions.

Let
P\in\operatorname{GT}(\lambda)
. Then we define the
content
of
P
as

(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
, where

c_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i+1}x_{i+1,j}-\sum_{j=1}^{i}x_{i,j}.

The
weight
of
P
is

\displaystyle\operatorname{wt}(P)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(c_{i}-c_{i+1})\omega_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}x_{i-1,j}+2\sum_{j=1}^{i}x_{i,j}-\sum_{j=1}^{i+1}x_{i+1,j}\right)\omega_{i}.

Let
\mu
be a weight occuring in the representation
V(\lambda)
. Then we may write
\mu=\lambda-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}k_{i}\alpha_{i}
, where the
\alpha_{i}
are the simple roots for
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
. Then we define the
level
of
\mu
as
\ell(\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}k_{i}
. See for instance
[
deGraaf2001
]
*Section 4.

A.1.5.
The norm
\|P\|

Write
l_{i,j}=x_{i,j}-j+1
.

Following
[
Molev2006
]
*Proposition 2.4, we define
\|P\|
by

\|P\|^{2}=\displaystyle\prod_{t=2}^{n}\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq p\leq q<t}\frac{(l_{t,p}-l_{t-1,q})!}{(l_{t-1,p}-l_{t-1,q})!}\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq p<q\leq t}\frac{(l_{t,p}-l_{t,q}-1)!}{(l_{t-1,p}-l_{t,q}-1)!}

We will frequently refer to the four products appearing in this formula as
N_{1}
,
D_{1}
,
N_{2}
, and
D_{2}
. That is,

\displaystyle N_{1}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\prod_{t=2}^{n}\prod_{1\leq p\leq q<t}(l_{t,p}-l_{t-1,q})!

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\prod_{t=2}^{n}\prod_{1\leq p\leq q<t}(l_{t-1,p}-l_{t-1,q})!

\displaystyle N_{2}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\prod_{t=2}^{n}\prod_{1\leq p<q\leq t}(l_{t,p}-l_{t,q}-1)!

\displaystyle D_{2}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\prod_{t=2}^{n}\prod_{1\leq p<q\leq t}(l_{t-1,p}-l_{t,q}-1)!

A.1

First, we state an easy lemma for obtaining invariant polynomials from
dual bases. Once again, the author welcomes references.

Let
\lambda
be a dominant integral weight, and let
\lambda^{*}
be its dual. Let
V(\lambda)
and
V(\lambda^{*})
be
\mathfrak{g}
-modules with these highest weights, and let
\rho_{\lambda}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V(\lambda))
and
\rho_{\lambda^{*}}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V(\lambda^{*}))
be the associated Lie algebra representations.

Definition A.2
.

We say that bases
\mathscr{B}
and
\mathscr{B}^{*}
of
V(\lambda)
and

V(\lambda^{*})

support negative transpose actions
if for any
X\in\mathfrak{g}
,
the matrices

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=[\rho_{\lambda}(X)]_{\mathscr{B}\leftarrow\mathscr{B}}

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=[\rho_{\lambda^{*}}(X)]_{\mathscr{B}^{*}\leftarrow\mathscr{B}^{*}}

satisfy
B=-A^{t}
.

The point of this definition is that we want to say
\mathscr{B}
and
\mathscr{B}^{*}

are dual bases, but we haven’t given an explicit isomorphism

V(\lambda^{*})\cong(V(\lambda))^{*}
yet. So instead we find bases that have the property that dual bases ought to have, and use them to define the isomorphism
V(\lambda^{*})\cong(V(\lambda))^{*}
.

Lemma A.3
.

Suppose that
\mathscr{B}
and
\mathscr{B}^{*}
support
negative transpose actions. Then

F=\sum_{i=1}^{N}e_{i}\otimes e_{i}^{*}

is a
G
-invariant vector.

Proof.

Let
X\in\mathfrak{g}
. Let
A
and
B
be the matrices shown in
Definition
A.2
. Then we may compute as follows.

\displaystyle X(F)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}X(e_{i}\otimes e_{i}^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}X(e_{i})\otimes e_{i}^{*}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}e_{j}\otimes X(e_{j}^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k,i}e_{k})\otimes e_{i}^{*}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}e_{j}\otimes(\sum_{l=1}^{N}b_{l,j}e_{l}^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k,i}e_{k}\otimes e_{i}^{*}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{l=1}^{N}b_{l,j}e_{j}\otimes e_{l}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k,i}e_{k}\otimes e_{i}^{*}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{l=1}^{N}(-a_{j,l})e_{j}\otimes e_{l}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k,i}e_{k}\otimes e_{i}^{*}+\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-a_{j,l})e_{j}\otimes e_{l}^{*}

\displaystyle=0.

This implies
F
is a highest weight vector of weight 0, hence an invariant.

q\neq i\end{subarray}}^{k}(l_{k,i}-l_{k,q})}}.

Now we are ready to prove Proposition
A.4
.

Proof of Prop.
A.4
.

We prove Part 1. Part 2 is similar.

Let
\mathscr{B}=\{\frac{1}{\|P\|}e_{P}\}
and let

\mathscr{B}^{*}=\{\frac{(-1)^{\ell(P^{*})}}{\|P^{*}\|}e_{P*}\}
. As in Definition
A.2
we write

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=[\rho_{\lambda}(X_{\alpha_{k}})]_{\mathscr{B}\leftarrow\mathscr{B}}

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=[\rho_{\lambda^{*}}(X_{\alpha_{k}})]_{\mathscr{B}^{*}\leftarrow\mathscr{B}^{*}}

We introduce one more basis and matrix. Let
\mathscr{B}_{+}^{*}=\{\frac{1}{\|P^{*}\|}e_{P*}\}
, and let

B^{+}=[\rho_{\lambda^{*}}(X_{\alpha_{k}})]_{\mathscr{B}_{+}^{*}\leftarrow\mathscr{B}_{+}^{*}}

Suppose
P
and
P+\delta_{k,i}
are valid patterns. Write
P^{\prime}=(P+\delta_{k,i})^{*}
. This prime notation will only be used in this
proof. Then
P^{\prime}
is related to
P^{*}
by
P^{\prime}+\delta_{k,k+1-i}=P^{*}
. Write
i^{\prime}=k+1-i
. Suppose
P
has index
I
in
GT(\lambda)
, and
P+\delta_{k,i}
has index
J
. Then the matrix entry
a_{I,J}
is the coefficient of
\frac{1}{\|P+\delta_{k,i}\|}e_{P+\delta_{k,i}}
in
X_{\alpha_{k}}\frac{1}{\|P\|}e_{P}
, and the matrix entry
b_{J,I}^{+}
is the coefficient of
\frac{1}{\|P^{*}\|}e_{P^{*}}
in
X_{\alpha_{k}}\frac{1}{\|P^{\prime}\|}e_{P^{\prime}}
. We show first that
a_{I,J}=b_{J,I}^{+}
. Then, at the end, we show that changing from the basis
\mathscr{B}_{+}^{*}
to the basis
\mathscr{B}^{*}
gives the extra minus sign needed to get the negative transpose.

By Proposition
A.7
, we have

\displaystyle a_{I,J}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{(-1)\displaystyle\prod_{q=1}^{k-1}(l_{k,i}-l_{k-1,q}+1)\displaystyle\prod_{q=1}^{k+1}(l_{k,i}-l_{k+1,q})}{\displaystyle\prod_{q=1}^{k}(l_{k,i}-l_{k,q}+1)\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1,\\
q\neq i\end{subarray}}^{k}(l_{k,i}-l_{k,q})}}

\displaystyle b_{J,I}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{(-1)\displaystyle\prod_{q^{\prime}=1}^{k-1}(l^{\prime}_{k,i^{\prime}}-l^{\prime}_{k-1,q^{\prime}}+1)\displaystyle\prod_{q^{\prime}=1}^{k+1}(l^{\prime}_{k,i^{\prime}}-l^{\prime}_{k+1,q^{\prime}})}{\displaystyle\prod_{q^{\prime}=1}^{k}(l^{\prime}_{k,i^{\prime}}-l^{\prime}_{k,q^{\prime}}+1)\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{\prime}=1,\\
q^{\prime}\neq i\end{subarray}}^{k}(l^{\prime}_{k,i^{\prime}}-l^{\prime}_{k,q})}}

First, we show that

(A.1)

\prod_{q=1}^{k-1}(l_{k,i}-l_{k-1,q}+1)=(-1)^{k-1}\prod_{q^{\prime}=1}^{k-1}(l^{\prime}_{k,i^{\prime}}-l^{\prime}_{k-1,q^{\prime}}+1)

via the map
q^{\prime}\mapsto k-q
.

We rewrite the left hand side of (
A.1
) as follows.

\displaystyle\prod_{q=1}^{k-1}(l_{k,i}-l_{k-1,q}+1)

\displaystyle=\prod_{q=1}^{k-1}((x_{k,i}-i+1)-(x_{k-1,q}-q+1)+1)

\displaystyle=\prod_{q=1}^{k-1}(x_{k,i}-x_{k-1,q}+q-i+1).

Next, we rewrite the right hand side of (
A.1
).

\displaystyle\prod_{q^{\prime}=1}^{k-1}(l^{\prime}_{k,i^{\prime}}-l^{\prime}_{k-1,q^{\prime}}+1)

\displaystyle=\prod_{q^{\prime}=1}^{k-1}((y_{k,i^{\prime}}-i^{\prime}+1)-(y_{k-1,q^{\prime}}-q^{\prime}+1)+1)

\displaystyle=\prod_{q^{\prime}=1}^{k-1}(y_{k,i^{\prime}}-y_{k-1,q^{\prime}}+(q^{\prime}-i^{\prime})+1)

\displaystyle=\prod_{q^{\prime}=1}^{k-1}((x_{n,1}-(x_{k,k+1-i^{\prime}}+1))-(x_{n,1}-x_{k-1,k-q^{\prime}})+(q^{\prime}-i^{\prime})+1)

\displaystyle=\prod_{q^{\prime}=1}^{k-1}(-x_{k,k+1-i^{\prime}}+x_{k-1,k-q^{\prime}}+(q^{\prime}-i^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\prod_{q=1}^{k-1}(-x_{k,i}+x_{k-1,q}-q+i-1).

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k-1}\prod_{q=1}^{k-1}(x_{k,i}-x_{k-1,q}+q-i+1).

This proves (
A.1
).

Similar arguments lead to the following equations.

\displaystyle\prod_{q=1}^{k+1}(l_{k,i}-l_{k+1,q})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k+1}\prod_{q^{\prime}=1}^{k+1}(l^{\prime}_{k,i^{\prime}}-l^{\prime}_{k+1,q^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\text{ via }q^{\prime}\mapsto k+2-q;

\displaystyle\prod_{q=1}^{k}(l_{k,i}-l_{k,q}+1)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{\prime}=1,\\
q^{\prime}\neq i\end{subarray}}^{k}(l^{\prime}_{k,i^{\prime}}-l^{\prime}_{k,q})

\displaystyle\text{ via }q^{\prime}\mapsto k+1-q;

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=1,\\
q\neq i\end{subarray}}^{k}(l_{k,i}-l_{k,q})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k-1}\prod_{q^{\prime}=1}^{k}(l^{\prime}_{k,i^{\prime}}-l^{\prime}_{k,q^{\prime}}+1)

\displaystyle\text{ via }q^{\prime}\mapsto k+1-q.

Combining these formulas, we have
a_{I,J}=b_{J,I}^{+}
.

Finally, we argue that
b_{J,I}=-b_{J,I}^{+}.
The patterns
P^{\prime}
and
P*
satisfy
wt(P^{*})=wt(P^{\prime})+\alpha_{k}
. Thus the levels
\ell(P^{*})
and
\ell(P^{\prime})
have opposite parity, and hence
b_{J,I}=-b_{J,I}^{+}.

∎

A.3.
Rationality of the coefficients

It turns out that the expressions
\|P\|\|P^{*}\|
appearing in
the formula for the invariant are rational numbers. This is useful
when computing, as it allows us to do exact calculations over

\mathbb{Q}
rather than approximate calculations over
\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition A.8
.

\|P\|\|P^{*}\|=\prod_{t=2}^{n}\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq p<q\leq t}(l_{t,p}-l_{t,q}-1)!}{\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq p\leq q<t}(l_{t-1,p}-l_{t-1,q})!}

Proof.

Write
\|P\|=\frac{N_{1}N_{2}}{D_{1}D_{2}}
and
\|P^{*}\|=\frac{N_{1}^{*}N_{2}^{*}}{D_{1}^{*}D_{2}^{*}}
. We will show that
N_{1}^{*}=D_{2}
,
D_{1}^{*}=D_{1}
,
N_{2}^{*}=N_{2}
, and
D_{2}^{*}=N_{1}
. Thus

\|P\|\|P^{*}\|=\sqrt{\frac{N_{2}^{2}}{D_{1}^{2}}}=\left|\frac{N_{2}}{D_{1}}\right|=\frac{N_{2}}{D_{1}}.

Consider

D_{2}=\displaystyle\prod_{t=2}^{n}\prod_{1\leq p<q\leq t}(l_{t-1,p}-l_{t,q}-1)!

and

N_{1}^{*}=\displaystyle\prod_{t=2}^{n}\prod_{1\leq p^{*}\leq q^{*}<t}(l^{*}_{t,p^{*}}-l^{*}_{t-1,q^{*}})!

We have

\displaystyle(l_{t-1,p}-l_{t,q}-1)

\displaystyle=x_{t-1,p}-p+1-(x_{t,q}-q+1)-1

\displaystyle=x_{t-1,p}-x_{t,q}-p+q-1.

Applying the map
p=t-q^{*}
and
q=t+1-p^{*}
we have

\displaystyle l^{*}_{t,p^{*}}-l^{*}_{t-1,q^{*}}

\displaystyle=l^{*}_{t,p^{*}}-l^{*}_{t-1,q^{*}}

\displaystyle=y_{t,p^{*}}-p^{*}+1-(y_{t-1,q^{*}}-q^{*}+1)

\displaystyle=(x_{n,1}-x_{t,t+1-p^{*}})-(x_{n,1}-x_{t-1,t-q^{*}})+(q^{*}-p^{*})

\displaystyle=-x_{t,q}+x_{t-1,p}+q-p-1.

Thus,
N_{1}^{*}=D_{2}
.

Similar arguments establish the remaining equations
D_{1}^{*}=D_{1}
,
N_{2}^{*}=N_{2}
, and
D_{2}^{*}=N_{1}
.

∎
