 ABSTRACT

Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) Manufacturing Demonstration Facility (MDF) entered a User
Proposal with Perseus Materials in Knoxville, TN. By reinventing how composites are made, we unlock
a new era of structural materials: faster to produce, easier to assemble, and strong enough for real-world
scale. Perseus Materials was targeting the wind turbine industry to make large adhesive joints for
composite turbine blades without the limitations of cure time and room temperature cure without the
needs to have work times. The triggerable aspect would increase manufacturing rates for composite wind
turbine blades.

Project title: Triggerable adhesives with infinite working life for large area application

This phase 1 user agreement project (MDF- UP-2023-277/NN-24-1485) began on October 07, 2024, and
concluded on December 29, 2025. The collaboration partner Perseus Materials is a small business.

1.1 BACKGROUND

 Perseus Materials builds the world’s fastest manufacturing system for large-scale composite parts
without molds, tooling, or delays. The goal of partnering with ORNL MDF was to develop an epoxy
adhesive that did not counter the catalyst to polymerize the resin. Previous epoxies used a dimethyl
amino phenol accelerant to cure the epoxy resin. This dimethyl amino phenol curative hindered the
catalyst activity, slowing the reaction. ORNL had proposed using Teramine epoxy curatives to bypass the
prior curative. The Teramine used ketimine which is different from dimethyl amino phenols.

1.3 IMPACTS

 Impacts for the project was to demonstrate the ability of ORNL MSD to make adhesive formulations
with on demand cure. The test results shows that the DCPD Perseus B formulation had higher adhesive
strength than a commercial epoxy. The ketimine curative was not compatible with the Huntsman epoxy,
and cure profiles were developed for Epon 828. The use of ketimine curative open ups new possible
curing schemes that are more compatible with the Perseus catalyst. Using on demand cure would simplify
all composite curing and could be applied to aerospace in addition to wind turbine blades. The possibility
needs to be scaled and further investigation is warranted.

1.5 CONCLUSIONS

 Perseus chose to cancel the user agreement in the first quarter of 2025, citing lack of resources.
 John Feist left Perseus Materials around this time. Without access to the proprietary Perseus
 proprietary formula as shown above in Table 1. Dicyclopentadiene (DCPD) was used but did not
 crosslink like the Perseus formula. The Perseus formula has fillers that prevent sagging after
 DCPD after it melts. After Perseus cancelled the user agreement, there was little progress made. If
 there is future interest with pursuing this type of on-demand cure, Perseus should provide the
 formulation, since there was an NDA between Perseus Materials and ORNL.
