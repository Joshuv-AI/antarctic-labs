Cauchy Aggregation of Ridge-Regularized Hotelling Tests for High-Dimensional Change-Point Detection
Ping Zhao, Le Zhou, Long Feng

Abstract
Ridge-regularized Hotelling-type (RHT) change-point tests depend on a ridge parameter λ \lambda , but the power-optimal value is determined by the unknown covariance structure and the unknown mean shift. We avoid selecting a single ridge value by computing fixed-ridge p-values on a finite deterministic grid and aggregating them with the Cauchy combination rule. Under the standard random-matrix conditions for fixed-ridge RHT statistics, we establish finite-grid joint weak convergence of the ridge processes. This leads to fixed-level validity under joint-limit calibration and small-tail validity for the analytic Cauchy p-value. Monte Carlo experiments show that deterministic-grid Cauchy aggregation has stable size behavior and achieves power close to the best stable fixed ridge choice across a range of covariance and signal configurations.



Keywords:
Cauchy combination; change point; deterministic ridge grid; high-dimensional mean; random matrix theory; ridge-regularized Hotelling test.

MSC 2020:
62H15, 62M10, 60B20, 62G10.

1
Introduction

Ridge regularization has long been used to stabilize inverse problems and covariance-based multivariate procedures. In the classical low-dimensional setting, Tikhonov regularization and ridge regression add a positive multiple of the identity matrix to an otherwise unstable inverse; see Tikhonov
[
1
]
and Hoerl and Kennard
[
2
]
. The associated tuning-parameter literature was primarily estimation-oriented. Cross-validation, generalized cross-validation and related risk criteria select the regularization level by balancing bias and variance; see Craven and Wahba
[
3
]
. Similar shrinkage ideas were later used in regularized discriminant analysis and covariance estimation, where the tuning parameter interpolates between an empirical covariance matrix and a stable target; see Friedman
[
4
]
and Ledoit and Wolf
[
5
]
.

In high-dimensional testing, ridge regularization plays a more structural role. When
p
is comparable to
n
, the sample covariance matrix is singular or ill-conditioned, and Hotelling-type tests based on
\mathbf{S}_{n}^{-1}
are no longer available without strong structural assumptions on
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}
. Ridge-regularized Hotelling statistics replace
\mathbf{S}_{n}^{-1}
by
(\mathbf{S}_{n}+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{p})^{-1}
, producing a stable covariance normalization while retaining spectral information about the population covariance matrix. This idea has been developed for high-dimensional mean testing by Chen et al.
[
6
]
and Li et al.
[
7
]
, and it is closely connected to deterministic-equivalent arguments from random matrix theory; see Bai and Silverstein
[
8
]
. For high-dimensional change-point inference, self-normalized and covariance-free methods have also been proposed; see Zhang and Lavitas
[
9
]
and Wang et al.
[
10
]
. More recently, Li and Xu
[
11
]
introduced ridge-regularized CUSUM statistics for high-dimensional mean change-point detection and showed that, for each fixed deterministic
\lambda>0
, the resulting RHT scan statistic has a pivotal Gaussian-process null limit.

The remaining difficulty is the choice of
\lambda
. A large ridge value moves the statistic toward an unnormalized
\ell_{2}
CUSUM statistic, whereas a small ridge value uses covariance information more aggressively. Power calculations for RHT-type tests show that the optimal value depends on the covariance structure of the mean shift and on its alignment with the eigenspaces of
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}
. Such information is unavailable in a change-point problem, where the location, direction, sparsity pattern and covariance alignment of the jump are all unknown under the alternative. Consequently, choosing a single ridge value can lead to substantial power loss under misspecified alternatives.

This paper takes an aggregation approach. We fix a finite deterministic grid
\Lambda_{n}=\{\lambda_{1,n},\ldots,\lambda_{L,n}\}
, compute the fixed-ridge RHT p-value at each grid point, and combine the p-values by the Cauchy combination rule of Liu and Xie
[
12
]
. The method avoids estimating a power-optimal ridge value and is directly compatible with existing fixed-ridge RHT implementations. On the theoretical side, finite-grid aggregation requires a joint null result across ridge values rather than only marginal fixed-
\lambda
limits. We derive this finite-grid joint convergence under the standard RHT random-matrix assumptions. The marginal limiting processes remain pivotal, while the cross-ridge covariance may depend on the limiting spectral structure. This distinction yields two calibration statements: joint-limit calibration gives asymptotically exact fixed-level size, and the analytic Cauchy p-value gives the usual small-tail validity without estimating cross-ridge dependence. Simulations indicate that the proposed aggregation maintains reasonable size and tracks the best stable fixed ridge choice across covariance and signal designs.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 formulates the change-point testing problem, reviews the fixed-ridge RHT scan statistic from Li and Xu
[
11
]
, and introduces the deterministic-grid Cauchy aggregation rule. Section 3 establishes the finite-grid joint null limit, size validity and conditional adaptive consistency. Section 4 reports size and power simulations, and Section 5 concludes with a brief discussion. Technical proofs are collected in the Appendix.

2
Problem formulation, fixed-ridge statistics and Cauchy aggregation

2.1
Problem formulation and fixed-ridge statistics

The model, scan statistic and fixed-ridge marginal calibration in this subsection follow Li and Xu
[
11
]
. We restate them in a self-contained form because the proposed Cauchy aggregation starts from their fixed-ridge RHT p-values. Let

\bm{X}_{j}=\bm{\mu}_{j}+\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}^{1/2}\bm{Z}_{j},\qquad j=1,\ldots,n,

where
\bm{X}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
\bm{\mu}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, the covariance matrix
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}
is common over time, and the entries of
\bm{Z}_{j}
are independent with mean zero and variance one. Fix a trimming constant
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
. The null hypothesis is

H_{0}:\quad\bm{\mu}_{1}=\cdots=\bm{\mu}_{n}=\bm{\mu},

for some unknown constant vector
\bm{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
. The alternative is that the mean sequence is piecewise constant with at least one abrupt change:

H_{1}:\quad\exists\ m\geq 1,\quad 0=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\cdots<\tau_{m}<\tau_{m+1}=1,\qquad\min_{0\leq r\leq m}(\tau_{r+1}-\tau_{r})\geq\varepsilon,

with vectors
\bm{\mu}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{\mu}^{(m+1)}
such that

\bm{\mu}_{j}=\bm{\mu}^{(r)},\qquad\lfloor n\tau_{r-1}\rfloor<j\leq\lfloor n\tau_{r}\rfloor,\qquad r=1,\ldots,m+1,

where at least one adjacent pair satisfies
\bm{\mu}^{(r)}\neq\bm{\mu}^{(r+1)}
. In the single-change case, this reduces to

H_{1,\rm sc}:\quad\bm{\mu}_{j}=\bm{\mu}^{(1)}1\{j\leq\lfloor n\tau_{\star}\rfloor\}+\bm{\mu}^{(2)}1\{j>\lfloor n\tau_{\star}\rfloor\},\qquad\bm{\delta}_{p}=\bm{\mu}^{(2)}-\bm{\mu}^{(1)}\neq 0,

for some unknown
\tau_{\star}\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]
. Thus the covariance matrix is not allowed to change; the testing problem is to detect changes in the high-dimensional mean sequence.

Let

\mathbf{S}_{n}=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\bm{X}_{j}-\bar{\bm{X}})(\bm{X}_{j}-\bar{\bm{X}})^{T},\qquad\bar{\bm{X}}=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{j}.

For an ordered triple
s=(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
with
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}\leq 1
, put
k(t)=\lfloor nt\rfloor+1
and define the adjacent-segment contrast

\bm{\Delta}(s)=\frac{1}{k(t_{3})-k(t_{2})}\sum_{j=k(t_{2})}^{k(t_{3})-1}\bm{X}_{j}-\frac{1}{k(t_{2})-k(t_{1})}\sum_{j=k(t_{1})}^{k(t_{2})-1}\bm{X}_{j}.

The effective sample size is

N(s)=\frac{\{k(t_{2})-k(t_{1})\}\{k(t_{3})-k(t_{2})\}}{k(t_{3})-k(t_{1})}.

For a fixed deterministic ridge parameter
\lambda>0
, define

V_{\lambda}(s)=N(s)\bm{\Delta}(s)^{T}(\mathbf{S}_{n}+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{p})^{-1}\bm{\Delta}(s).

Let
\gamma_{n}=p/(n-1)
, and let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}
be the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{S}_{n}
.
Set

\widehat{m}_{\lambda}=p^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left\{\frac{n}{n-1}\alpha_{j}+\lambda\right\}^{-1},\quad\widehat{m}^{\prime}_{\lambda}=p^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left\{\frac{n}{n-1}\alpha_{j}+\lambda\right\}^{-2},

\widehat{\Theta}_{\lambda}=1-\lambda\widehat{m}_{\lambda},

\widehat{\Gamma}_{\lambda}=2(1-\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{n}\lambda\widehat{m}_{\lambda})(1-\lambda\widehat{m}_{\lambda})-2(\lambda\widehat{m}_{\lambda}-\lambda^{2}\widehat{m}^{\prime}_{\lambda}).

The standardized local statistic is

D_{\lambda}(s)=\sqrt{p}\,\frac{p^{-1}V_{\lambda}(s)-\widehat{\Theta}_{\lambda}}{\widehat{\Gamma}_{\lambda}^{1/2}}.

For a single change-point alternative we use

\mathcal{S}_{\rm sc}(\varepsilon)=\{(0,t,1):\varepsilon\leq t\leq 1-\varepsilon\},\qquad T_{\lambda,{\rm sc}}=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm sc}(\varepsilon)}D_{\lambda}(s).

For multiple changes one may use either the continuous set

\mathcal{S}_{\rm mc}(\varepsilon)=\{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}):t_{2}-t_{1}\geq\varepsilon,\ t_{3}-t_{2}\geq\varepsilon\}

or a deterministic discretized version. We write
\mathcal{S}
for the selected scan set and

T_{\lambda}=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}D_{\lambda}(s),

where
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{\rm sc}(\varepsilon)
in the single-change case and
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{\rm mc}(\varepsilon)
in the multiple-change case. The theoretical result below is proved for the continuous single-change scan and for any fixed deterministic discretization of the multiple-change scan. This is the version used in the simulations and avoids imposing additional entropy conditions on the three-dimensional continuous scan.

Under standard random-matrix assumptions, for each fixed deterministic
\lambda>0
,

T_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{d}T_{0}=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}G(s),

where
G
is a centered Gaussian process whose covariance is determined only by the overlap of the segment-contrast functions.
For the single-change scan, if
s_{t}=(0,t,1)
, then

\operatorname{cov}\{G(s_{t}),G(s_{u})\}=\frac{\min(t,u)\{1-\max(t,u)\}}{\max(t,u)\{1-\min(t,u)\}}.

Thus the marginal p-value

P_{\lambda}=1-F_{\mathcal{S}}(T_{\lambda}),

where
F_{\mathcal{S}}
is the distribution function of
T_{0}
, is asymptotically valid and is free of
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}
.
The distribution
F_{\mathcal{S}}
is continuous for the nondegenerate Gaussian suprema considered here; for a finite scan this follows from nonsingularity of the Gaussian vector, and for the single-change scan it follows from standard anti-concentration results for Gaussian processes. In practice
F_{\mathcal{S}}
is simulated once from the limiting Gaussian process.

2.2
Cauchy combination over a deterministic ridge grid

The fixed-ridge RHT statistic provides a valid test once
\lambda
is fixed, but the ridge value that maximizes power is not universal. A large ridge value moves the statistic toward an unnormalized
\ell_{2}
CUSUM statistic, whereas a small ridge value uses covariance information more aggressively. The favorable choice depends on the spectrum of
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}
, the location of the change, and the alignment of the unknown mean shift with the eigenspaces of
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}
. These quantities are unavailable under
H_{1}
. We therefore avoid selecting a single ridge parameter and instead aggregate several fixed-ridge p-values. The Cauchy rule is used because its heavy-tailed transformation makes the combined statistic sensitive to the most significant ridge value while avoiding estimation of the cross-ridge dependence matrix.

Fix a finite deterministic grid

\Lambda_{n}=\{\lambda_{1,n},\ldots,\lambda_{L,n}\}\subset(0,\infty).

Throughout the theory,
L
is fixed and the grid is bounded away from zero and infinity.
For notational simplicity we write
\lambda_{\ell}
for
\lambda_{\ell,n}
.
A convenient implementation after trace standardization is

\lambda_{\ell}=a_{\ell}\gamma_{n},\qquad 0<a_{1}<\cdots<a_{L}<\infty,

which is deterministic because
\gamma_{n}=p/(n-1)
is fixed by the sample dimensions.
The simulations below use the deterministic grid
a_{\ell}\in\{0.05,0.10,\ldots,0.50\}
for the size study. The power plots display two representative fixed choices,
a=0.10
and
a=0.20
, the Cauchy rule over a size-filtered deterministic subgrid, and the oracle best fixed ridge value over the same subgrid.

For each
\lambda_{\ell}
, compute
T_{\lambda_{\ell}}
and its marginal p-value
P_{\lambda_{\ell}}
.
Let
w_{\ell}>0
be fixed weights satisfying
\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}w_{\ell}=1
; equal weights are used in the simulations.
The Cauchy statistic and analytic Cauchy p-value are

C_{n}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}w_{\ell}\tan\{\pi(1/2-P_{\lambda_{\ell}})\},\qquad P_{\operatorname{CCT}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan(C_{n}).

The analytic implementation rejects when
P_{\operatorname{CCT}}\leq\alpha
, equivalently when
C_{n}\geq\cot(\pi\alpha)
.
This implementation does not estimate the dependence among the ridge-specific p-values.

For formal fixed-level calibration, define the critical value from the joint limit of the vector
(P_{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,P_{\lambda_{L}})
and reject for large
C_{n}
.
This version has exact asymptotic size at any fixed level, but its joint critical value depends on the limiting cross-ridge dependence.
The analytic Cauchy p-value avoids this joint calibration and is justified by the usual CCT small-tail argument.
The two calibrations therefore have distinct roles: joint-limit calibration gives fixed-level exactness, while the analytic CCT gives a simple dependence-robust tail approximation.

Remark 1
.

The Cauchy rule is not a power-weighted average.
For small p-values,
\tan\{\pi(1/2-p)\}\sim(\pi p)^{-1}
, so the combined statistic is dominated by the most significant ridge value.
Hence the method behaves like a smooth adaptive minimum-p rule, but with a simple heavy-tailed reference distribution.

3
Asymptotic validity

We state the theory for a fixed finite deterministic grid. The regularity condition below is the same high-dimensional random-matrix condition used for the fixed-ridge RHT change-point theorem of Li and Xu
[
11
]
. Under the null hypothesis the change-point-location part of their condition is vacuous.

Assumption 1

(RHT regularity)
.

The entries of
\bm{Z}_{j}
are independent, have mean zero, unit variance and finite fourth moments. Moreover
p/(n-1)\to\gamma\in(0,\infty)
,
\|\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\|_{\operatorname{op}}
is uniformly bounded, and the empirical spectral distribution
H_{p}(x)=p^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p}1\{\tau_{j,p}\leq x\}
of
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}
, where
\tau_{1,p},\ldots,\tau_{p,p}
are the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}
, converges weakly to a nondegenerate compactly supported limit. The trimming parameter
\varepsilon
is fixed. The ridge grid is deterministic and contains no data-dependent element:

\Lambda_{n}=\{\lambda_{1,n},\ldots,\lambda_{L,n}\},\qquad L<\infty.

There are constants
0<\underline{\lambda}<\overline{\lambda}<\infty
such that

\underline{\lambda}\leq\min_{1\leq\ell\leq L}\lambda_{\ell,n}\leq\max_{1\leq\ell\leq L}\lambda_{\ell,n}\leq\overline{\lambda},

for all sufficiently large
n
, and
\lambda_{\ell,n}\to\lambda_{\ell,0}\in(0,\infty)
for each
\ell
. Repeated grid points are merged, so the limiting values
\lambda_{1,0},\ldots,\lambda_{L,0}
are distinct. The scanning set
\mathcal{S}
is either the single-change set
\mathcal{S}_{\rm sc}(\varepsilon)
or a deterministic discretized multiple-change set contained in
\mathcal{S}_{\rm mc}(\varepsilon)
; the grid version used in the RHT paper is included.

For
s=(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
, define the population contrast function

u_{s}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&0\leq x<t_{1},\\
-(t_{2}-t_{1})^{-1},&t_{1}\leq x<t_{2},\\
(t_{3}-t_{2})^{-1},&t_{2}\leq x<t_{3},\\
0,&t_{3}\leq x\leq 1,\end{cases}

and write
\kappa(s,r)=\int_{0}^{1}u_{s}(x)u_{r}(x)\,dx
and
\kappa(s)=\kappa(s,s)
. Put

K_{0}(s,r)=\frac{\kappa(s,r)^{2}}{\kappa(s)\kappa(r)}.

The next theorem derives the finite-grid joint null limit from the fixed-ridge RHT conditions. The marginal covariance is pivotal, while the cross-ridge correlation is a deterministic spectral quantity.

Theorem 1

(Finite-grid joint RHT null limit)
.

Suppose Assumption
1
holds and
H_{0}
is true. Then there exist constants
\rho_{\ell m}\in[-1,1]
,
\rho_{\ell\ell}=1
, and a centered Gaussian vector process

\bm{G}^{\Lambda}(s)=\{G_{1}(s),\ldots,G_{L}(s)\}^{T},\qquad s\in\mathcal{S},

with almost surely continuous sample paths such that

\{D_{\lambda_{\ell,n}}(s):s\in\mathcal{S},\ell=1,\ldots,L\}\xrightarrow{d}\{G_{\ell}(s):s\in\mathcal{S},\ell=1,\ldots,L\}

in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{S})^{L}
. Its covariance kernel is

\operatorname{Cov}\{G_{\ell}(s),G_{m}(r)\}=\rho_{\ell m}K_{0}(s,r),\qquad s,r\in\mathcal{S},\qquad 1\leq\ell,m\leq L.

For each fixed
\ell
,
G_{\ell}
has the usual fixed-ridge RHT null law. In general
\rho_{\ell m}
,
\ell\neq m
, is determined by cross-products of deterministic equivalents of the two ridge resolvents and may depend on the limiting spectral distribution of
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}
. If
F_{\mathcal{S}}
denotes the continuous distribution function of
\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}G_{\ell}(s)
, then

\bm{P}_{n}=(P_{\lambda_{1,n}},\ldots,P_{\lambda_{L,n}})^{T}\xrightarrow{d}\bm{P}_{\infty}=(P_{\infty,1},\ldots,P_{\infty,L})^{T},

where
P_{\infty,\ell}=1-F_{\mathcal{S}}\{\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}G_{\ell}(s)\}
.

Let

C(\bm{p})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}w_{\ell}\tan\{\pi(1/2-p_{\ell})\},\qquad w_{\ell}>0,\qquad\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}w_{\ell}=1.

Let
c_{\alpha,L}
be a continuity point satisfying

\mathbb{P}\{C(\bm{P}_{\infty})>c_{\alpha,L}\}=\alpha.

Theorem 2

(Size validity)
.

Under Assumption
1
and
H_{0}
,

\mathbb{P}\{C(\bm{P}_{n})>c_{\alpha,L}\}\to\alpha.

For the analytic Cauchy p-value, for every fixed
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
such that
\cot(\pi\alpha)
is a continuity point of
C(\bm{P}_{\infty})
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(P_{\operatorname{CCT}}\leq\alpha)=\mathbb{P}\{C(\bm{P}_{\infty})\geq\cot(\pi\alpha)\}.

Thus the analytic Cauchy p-value is exactly size-
\alpha
at a fixed level only when the last probability equals
\alpha
. If, in addition,

\mathbb{P}\{C(\bm{P}_{\infty})>t\}\sim\frac{1}{\pi t},\qquad t\to\infty,

then

\lim_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(P_{\operatorname{CCT}}\leq\alpha)}{\alpha}=1.

Theorem
2
is stated in two parts. Joint-limit calibration gives fixed-level exactness. The analytic Cauchy p-value gives the usual CCT small-tail approximation of Liu and Xie
[
12
]
; it does not by itself imply exact size at a fixed conventional level such as
0.05
unless the fixed-level identity above holds.

Before stating the adaptive power result, we spell out the quantities used under
H_{1}
. Let
H_{p}
be the empirical spectral distribution in Assumption
1
, and let
\phi_{n}(z)=\phi(z;\gamma_{n},H_{p})
denote the solution of the Marčenko–Pastur equation associated with
(\gamma_{n},H_{p})
. On the negative real axis define

\Theta_{n}(\lambda)=1-\lambda\phi_{n}(-\lambda),

\Gamma_{n}(\lambda)=2\{1-\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{n}\lambda\phi_{n}(-\lambda)\}\{1-\lambda\phi_{n}(-\lambda)\}-2\{\lambda\phi_{n}(-\lambda)-\lambda^{2}\phi^{\prime}_{n}(-\lambda)\},

and

\mathbf{D}_{p}(\lambda)=\left[\{1-\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{n}\lambda\phi_{n}(-\lambda)\}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{p}\right]^{-1}.

The matrix
\mathbf{D}_{p}(\lambda)
is the deterministic equivalent of
(\mathbf{S}_{n}+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{p})^{-1}
, and
\Gamma_{n}(\lambda)
is the deterministic counterpart of
\widehat{\Gamma}_{\lambda}
.

We use the probability-alternative formulation of Li and Xu
[
11
]
. In the single-change case, the mean jump is written as

\bm{\delta}_{p}=p^{-3/4}\mathbf{B}_{p}\bm{w},

where
\mathbf{B}_{p}
is a deterministic
p\times p
matrix with bounded operator norm and
\bm{w}
has independent mean-zero, unit-variance entries with uniformly controlled fourth moments. The ridge-specific signal strength is

q_{p}(\lambda,\mathbf{B}_{p})=p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\{\mathbf{D}_{p}(\lambda)\mathbf{B}_{p}\mathbf{B}_{p}^{T}\}.

If the true single change occurs at
\tau_{\star}\in(0,1)
and
s=(0,t,1)
, the location-shape function is

h_{\rm sc}(t;\tau_{\star})=\begin{cases}t(1-t)^{-1}(1-\tau_{\star})^{2},&0\leq t<\tau_{\star},\\
t^{-1}(1-t)\tau_{\star}^{2},&\tau_{\star}\leq t\leq 1.\end{cases}

For multiple changes with true fractions
0=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\cdots<\tau_{m}<\tau_{m+1}=1
, define
v_{r}(x)=1\{\tau_{r-1}\leq x<\tau_{r}\}
and

\psi_{r}(s)=\kappa(s)^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{1}u_{s}(x)v_{r}(x)\,dx,\qquad r=1,\ldots,m+1,

where
u_{s}
and
\kappa(s)
are defined above. Let
\bm{\psi}(s)=(\psi_{1}(s),\ldots,\psi_{m+1}(s))^{T}
and collect the segment mean vectors in
\mathbf{U}=(\bm{\mu}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{\mu}^{(m+1)})\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times(m+1)}
. Under a separable probability alternative,
\mathbf{U}=p^{-3/4}\mathbf{B}_{p}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{\Omega}_{\rm alt}^{T}
, where
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times r}
has independent mean-zero, unit-variance entries and
\mathbf{\Omega}_{\rm alt}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m+1)\times r}
is a fixed matrix for some fixed
r
. The corresponding location-shape function is

h_{\rm mc}(s)=\|\mathbf{\Omega}_{\rm alt}^{T}\bm{\psi}(s)\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus, for either the single-change scan with
h(s)=h_{\rm sc}(t;\tau_{\star})
or the multiple-change scan with
h(s)=h_{\rm mc}(s)
, the deterministic drift of the
\ell
th fixed-ridge process has the form

R_{n,\ell}(s)=\frac{q_{p}(\lambda_{\ell,n},\mathbf{B}_{p})}{\gamma_{n}\Gamma_{n}(\lambda_{\ell,n})^{1/2}}h(s)+o(1),

with the
o(1)
term interpreted uniformly over the selected scan set in the local alternatives considered by Li and Xu
[
11
]
. The next theorem states the aggregation consequence in a form that only requires the existence of such a drift expansion.

Theorem 3

(Conditional adaptive consistency)
.

Let

Y_{n,\ell}=\tan\{\pi(1/2-P_{\lambda_{\ell,n}})\}.

Suppose that, under a sequence of alternatives, there exist stochastic processes
Z_{n,\ell}
and deterministic drifts
R_{n,\ell}
satisfying

\max_{1\leq\ell\leq L}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}|D_{\lambda_{\ell,n}}(s)-Z_{n,\ell}(s)-R_{n,\ell}(s)|\leq a_{n},\qquad a_{n}\to 0,

and

\max_{1\leq\ell\leq L}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}|Z_{n,\ell}(s)|=O_{p}(1).

If there exists a grid point
\ell_{0}
such that

R_{n,\ell_{0}}^{*}:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}R_{n,\ell_{0}}(s)\to\infty,

and if the remaining Cauchy terms do not asymptotically cancel the diverging positive term,

\left[\sum_{\ell\neq\ell_{0}}w_{\ell}Y_{n,\ell}\right]_{-}=o_{p}\{w_{\ell_{0}}Y_{n,\ell_{0}}\},\qquad[x]_{-}=\max(-x,0),

then
P_{\operatorname{CCT}}\to 0
in probability. Consequently, the analytic Cauchy-combined RHT test has power tending to one. The no-cancellation condition is automatic when
\sum_{\ell\neq\ell_{0}}w_{\ell}Y_{n,\ell}=O_{p}(1)
.

4
Simulation

We conduct Monte Carlo experiments under the model

\bm{X}_{j}=\bm{\mu}_{j}+\mathbf{\Sigma}^{1/2}\bm{Z}_{j},

where every covariance matrix is scaled so that
\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{\Sigma})=p
. Four primary covariance structures are considered: (i)
ID
,
\mathbf{\Sigma}=\mathbf{I}_{p}
; (ii)
Toeplitz(.3)
, where
\mathbf{\Sigma}
is Toeplitz with
(i,j)
entry
0.3^{|i-j|}
; (iii)
Poly Decay
, where the raw eigenvalues are
\tau_{j}=0.01+(p-j+0.1)^{2}
; and (iv)
Exp Decay
, where the raw eigenvalues are
\tau_{j}=\exp(-3j/p)
. Two additional dependence designs are included:
Toeplitz(.6)
, with
(i,j)
entry
0.6^{|i-j|}
, and
CS(.6)
, with all off-diagonal correlations equal to
0.6
.

The trimming parameter is
\varepsilon=0.1
and the nominal level is
\alpha=0.05
. Marginal p-values are computed from
100,000
simulations of the limiting single-change Gaussian-process null distribution. The deterministic ridge grid for the size study is

\lambda/\gamma_{n}\in\{0.05,0.10,0.15,0.20,0.25,0.30,0.35,0.40,0.45,0.50\}.

Under
H_{0}
, the Cauchy statistic combines all ten fixed-ridge p-values with equal weights. We take
n\in\{200,400\}
and
p\in\{100,200,400\}
; each configuration is based on
1,000
replications under either Gaussian innovations or standardized
t_{5}
innovations.

5
Discussion

This paper studies ridge tuning in high-dimensional RHT change-point tests from an aggregation perspective. Rather than estimating a single power-optimal ridge value, the proposed procedure evaluates fixed-ridge statistics on a finite deterministic grid and combines their marginal p-values by the Cauchy rule. The main theoretical contribution is a finite-grid joint null limit for the ridge processes. This result justifies fixed-level joint-limit calibration and clarifies the scope of the analytic Cauchy p-value, which provides a simple small-tail approximation without estimating cross-ridge dependence.

The simulations support the proposed use of deterministic-grid aggregation. Moderate ridge values lead to stable marginal size, while the Cauchy statistic protects power against selecting a suboptimal fixed ridge value. The results also show that very small ridge values can be unstable in finite samples when
p/n
is large. In applications, the deterministic grid should therefore be chosen away from zero unless additional calibration is used.

Two extensions are natural. First, one could develop fixed-level calibration for the analytic Cauchy statistic by estimating the finite-grid joint limit or by constructing a valid bootstrap for the cross-ridge p-value vector. Second, the deterministic finite-grid theory could be extended to growing or data-adaptive ridge grids, where uniform stochastic equicontinuity in
\lambda
becomes essential.

\tilde{\bm{u}}_{n}(s)^{T}\tilde{\bm{u}}_{n}(r)=\int_{0}^{1}u_{s}(x)u_{r}(x)\,dx+O(n^{-1}).

The map
(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\mapsto u_{s}
is Lipschitz in
L^{2}[0,1]
on the trimmed set except for moving endpoints; the endpoint contribution is bounded by the length of the symmetric difference. Therefore

\int_{0}^{1}\{u_{s}(x)-u_{r}(x)\}^{2}dx\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|s-r\|_{1},

which together with the discretization error gives the displayed bound.

Lemma A.2

(Finite-grid cross-resolvent deterministic equivalents)
.

Under Assumption
1
, for every pair
\lambda_{a,n},\lambda_{b,n}\in\Lambda_{n}
there exist deterministic quantities

\mathcal{K}^{\Sigma\Sigma}_{ab,n},\qquad\mathcal{K}^{\Sigma}_{ab,n},\qquad\mathcal{K}^{I}_{ab,n},

with finite limits such that

\displaystyle p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\{\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{a,n}}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{b,n}}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\}-\mathcal{K}^{\Sigma\Sigma}_{ab,n}=o_{p}(1),

\displaystyle p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\{\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{a,n}}\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{b,n}}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\}-\mathcal{K}^{\Sigma}_{ab,n}=o_{p}(1),

\displaystyle p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\{\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{a,n}}\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{b,n}}\}-\mathcal{K}^{I}_{ab,n}=o_{p}(1).

For
\mathbf{A}\in\{\mathbf{I}_{p},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\}
, define

\vartheta_{\mathbf{A},n}(\lambda)=p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\{\mathbf{D}_{n}(\lambda)\mathbf{A}\}.

Then the deterministic equivalents without a middle population covariance are

\mathcal{K}^{\mathbf{A}}_{ab,n}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\vartheta_{\mathbf{A},n}(\lambda_{a,n})-\vartheta_{\mathbf{A},n}(\lambda_{b,n})}{\lambda_{b,n}-\lambda_{a,n}},&\lambda_{a,n}\neq\lambda_{b,n},\\[8.61108pt]
\displaystyle-\partial_{\lambda}\vartheta_{\mathbf{A},n}(\lambda)\big|_{\lambda=\lambda_{a,n}},&\lambda_{a,n}=\lambda_{b,n},\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{K}^{I}_{ab,n}=\mathcal{K}^{\mathbf{A}}_{ab,n}
with
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{I}_{p}
and

\mathcal{K}^{\Sigma}_{ab,n}=\mathcal{K}^{\mathbf{A}}_{ab,n}
with
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}
. The quantity

\mathcal{K}^{\Sigma\Sigma}_{ab,n}
is the two-sided deterministic equivalent for the product

\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{a,n}}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{b,n}}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}
. It is not, in general, equal to

p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\{\mathbf{D}_{n}(\lambda_{a,n})\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\mathbf{D}_{n}(\lambda_{b,n})\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\}
.

Proof.

For
\mathbf{A}\in\{\mathbf{I}_{p},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\}
and
\lambda\in[\underline{\lambda},\overline{\lambda}]
, Lemma 3.1 of Li and Xu
[
11
]
gives

p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\{\mathbf{R}_{\lambda}\mathbf{A}\}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{A},n}(\lambda)=O_{p}(n^{-1/2}).

If
\lambda_{a,n}\neq\lambda_{b,n}
, then

\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{a,n}}\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{b,n}}=\frac{\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{a,n}}-\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{b,n}}}{\lambda_{b,n}-\lambda_{a,n}},

and hence

\displaystyle p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\{\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{a,n}}\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{b,n}}\mathbf{A}\}-\mathcal{K}^{\mathbf{A}}_{ab,n}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\{\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{a,n}}\mathbf{A}\}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{A},n}(\lambda_{a,n})-\big[p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\{\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{b,n}}\mathbf{A}\}-\vartheta_{\mathbf{A},n}(\lambda_{b,n})\big]}{\lambda_{b,n}-\lambda_{a,n}}.

Since
\lambda_{a,n}\to\lambda_{a,0}
,
\lambda_{b,n}\to\lambda_{b,0}
and the finite grid has distinct limiting points after duplicate values are removed, the denominator is bounded away from zero for
a\neq b
. Thus the last display is
O_{p}(n^{-1/2})
. If
\lambda_{a,n}=\lambda_{b,n}
, which can occur only for
a=b
after repeated grid values have been merged, the preceding difference quotient is replaced by the two-resolvent deterministic equivalent at coincident spectral parameters. Equivalently, for
\mathbf{A}\in\{\mathbf{I}_{p},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\}
,

p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\{\mathbf{R}_{\lambda}^{2}\mathbf{A}\}+\partial_{\lambda}\vartheta_{\mathbf{A},n}(\lambda)=o_{p}(1),

with the derivative taken on the negative real axis. This derivative form is the coincident-parameter limit of the deterministic-equivalent system; it is justified by the uniform resolvent bound

\|\mathbf{R}_{\lambda}\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq\underline{\lambda}^{-1},\qquad\|\mathbf{R}_{\lambda+h}-\mathbf{R}_{\lambda}\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq|h|\underline{\lambda}^{-2},

and the locally uniform deterministic-equivalent convergence on compact subsets of
(0,\infty)
.
The two-sided trace

p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\{\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{a,n}}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{b,n}}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\}

is a standard bilinear-resolvent deterministic equivalent for sample covariance matrices. Under the separable covariance model
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}^{1/2}\mathbf{Z}
, the negative-real-axis version follows from the deterministic-equivalent theory of Hachem, Loubaton and Najim
[
13
]
and the bilinear resolvent bounds of Hachem et al.
[
14
]
; the finite-fourth-moment reduction is obtained by the truncation argument used in Bai and Silverstein
[
15
]
and in Li and Xu
[
11
]
. Therefore there is a deterministic
\mathcal{K}^{\Sigma\Sigma}_{ab,n}
such that

p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\{\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{a,n}}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{b,n}}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\}-\mathcal{K}^{\Sigma\Sigma}_{ab,n}=o_{p}(1).

Because
\|\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq C
and the ridge parameters lie in a compact subset of
(0,\infty)
,

|\mathcal{K}^{\Sigma\Sigma}_{ab,n}|+|\mathcal{K}^{\Sigma}_{ab,n}|+|\mathcal{K}^{I}_{ab,n}|\leq C.

The weak convergence of
H_{p}
and the convergence
\lambda_{a,n}\to\lambda_{a,0}
imply convergence of
\mathcal{K}^{I}_{ab,n}
and
\mathcal{K}^{\Sigma}_{ab,n}
through the displayed formulas. The deterministic-equivalent system in Hachem, Loubaton and Najim
[
13
]
is continuous on the negative real axis, so
\mathcal{K}^{\Sigma\Sigma}_{ab,n}
also has a finite limit.
∎

Lemma A.3

(Finite-grid regular-vector CLT)
.

Let
\bm{u}_{q},\bm{v}_{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
q=1,\ldots,Q
, be deterministic regular vectors satisfying

\max_{q}(\|\bm{u}_{q}\|_{2}+\|\bm{v}_{q}\|_{2})\leq C,\qquad\sqrt{n}\max_{q}(\|\bm{u}_{q}\|_{\infty}+\|\bm{v}_{q}\|_{\infty})\leq C.

Let
\lambda_{a_{q},n}\in\Lambda_{n}
and
b_{q}\in\mathbb{R}
be fixed. Define

H_{n,q}=p^{-1}\bm{u}_{q}^{T}\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{a_{q},n}}\mathbf{X}\bm{v}_{q},\qquad L_{n}=\sum_{q=1}^{Q}b_{q}\sqrt{p}\{H_{n,q}-EH_{n,q}\}.

Then
L_{n}\xrightarrow{d}N(0,\sigma_{L}^{2})
, where

\sigma_{L}^{2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{q_{1}=1}^{Q}\sum_{q_{2}=1}^{Q}b_{q_{1}}b_{q_{2}}\mathcal{V}_{q_{1}q_{2},n},

\mathcal{V}_{q_{1}q_{2},n}=\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{a_{q_{1}}a_{q_{2}},n}\{(\bm{u}_{q_{1}}^{T}\bm{u}_{q_{2}})(\bm{v}_{q_{1}}^{T}\bm{v}_{q_{2}})+(\bm{u}_{q_{1}}^{T}\bm{v}_{q_{2}})(\bm{v}_{q_{1}}^{T}\bm{u}_{q_{2}})\}+O(n^{-1/2}),

where
\Gamma_{ab,n}
is deterministic,
\Gamma_{aa,n}=\Gamma_{n}(\lambda_{a,n})
, and
\Gamma_{ab,n}\to\Gamma_{ab}
. The numbers
\Gamma_{ab}
are finite and are functions of the limiting cross-resolvent traces in Lemma
A.2
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{j}=\sigma(\bm{X}_{1},\ldots,\bm{X}_{j})
,
E_{j}(\cdot)=E(\cdot\mid\mathcal{F}_{j})
, and

\xi_{n,j}=(E_{j}-E_{j-1})L_{n},\qquad L_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{n,j}.

Then

E_{j-1}\xi_{n,j}=0,\qquad E\xi_{n,j}=0.

For
\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}=(\mathbf{\Omega}_{n}^{(j)}+\lambda\mathbf{I}_{p})^{-1}
and

\mathbf{\Omega}_{n}^{(j)}=n^{-1}\sum_{i\neq j}\bm{X}_{i}\bm{X}_{i}^{T},

\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}
is independent of
\bm{X}_{j}
but is not
\mathcal{F}_{j-1}
-measurable. The proof uses only this independence. Woodbury’s identity gives

\mathbf{R}_{\lambda}=\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}-\frac{n^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\bm{X}_{j}\bm{X}_{j}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}}{1+n^{-1}\bm{X}_{j}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\bm{X}_{j}}.

Writing
\mathbf{X}^{(j)}
for
\mathbf{X}
with the
j
th column replaced by zero,

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{T}\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{\lambda}\mathbf{X}\bm{v}

\displaystyle=\bm{u}^{T}(\mathbf{X}^{(j)})^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\mathbf{X}^{(j)}\bm{v}+B_{j,\lambda}(\bm{u},\bm{v}),

\displaystyle B_{j,\lambda}(\bm{u},\bm{v})

\displaystyle=v_{j}\bm{u}^{T}(\mathbf{X}^{(j)})^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\bm{X}_{j}+u_{j}\bm{X}_{j}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\mathbf{X}^{(j)}\bm{v}+u_{j}v_{j}\bm{X}_{j}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\bm{X}_{j}

\displaystyle-\frac{n^{-1}\{\bm{u}^{T}(\mathbf{X}^{(j)})^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\bm{X}_{j}+u_{j}\bm{X}_{j}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\bm{X}_{j}\}\{\bm{X}_{j}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\mathbf{X}^{(j)}\bm{v}+v_{j}\bm{X}_{j}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\bm{X}_{j}\}}{1+n^{-1}\bm{X}_{j}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\bm{X}_{j}}.

Therefore

\xi_{n,j}=\sum_{q=1}^{Q}b_{q}p^{-1/2}(E_{j}-E_{j-1})B_{j,\lambda_{a_{q},n}}(\bm{u}_{q},\bm{v}_{q}).

The finite-fourth-moment assumption is handled by the usual truncation and recentralization device. Choose
\eta_{n}\downarrow 0
such that

\eta_{n}^{-4}E\{Z_{11}^{4}I(|Z_{11}|>\eta_{n}\sqrt{n})\}\to 0,

put

Z_{ij}^{(0)}=Z_{ij}I(|Z_{ij}|\leq\eta_{n}\sqrt{n}),\qquad\widetilde{Z}_{ij}=\frac{Z_{ij}^{(0)}-EZ_{ij}^{(0)}}{\{\operatorname{Var}(Z_{ij}^{(0)})\}^{1/2}}.

Since
p/n\to\gamma
,

\displaystyle P\{Z_{ij}\neq Z_{ij}^{(0)}\text{ for some }i,j\}

\displaystyle\leq np\,P(|Z_{11}|>\eta_{n}\sqrt{n})

\displaystyle\leq C\eta_{n}^{-4}E\{Z_{11}^{4}I(|Z_{11}|>\eta_{n}\sqrt{n})\}=o(1),

while

|EZ_{11}^{(0)}|\leq(\eta_{n}\sqrt{n})^{-3}E\{|Z_{11}|^{4}I(|Z_{11}|>\eta_{n}\sqrt{n})\}=o(n^{-3/2}),

|\operatorname{Var}(Z_{11}^{(0)})-1|\leq E\{Z_{11}^{2}I(|Z_{11}|>\eta_{n}\sqrt{n})\}+|EZ_{11}^{(0)}|^{2}=o(1).

Thus the centering and rescaling modify
L_{n}
by
o_{p}(1)
. The difference between the original and truncated versions is therefore
o_{p}(1)
; see the truncation argument of Bai and Silverstein
[
15
]
and Li and Xu
[
11
]
. For the truncated variables the quadratic-form inequalities below hold uniformly in
j
and
\lambda
. Since
\|\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq\underline{\lambda}^{-1}
and
\|\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq C
,

\displaystyle E|\bm{X}_{j}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\mathbf{X}^{(j)}\bm{v}|^{4}\leq Cp^{2}\|v\|_{2}^{4},

\displaystyle E|\bm{u}^{T}(\mathbf{X}^{(j)})^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\bm{X}_{j}|^{4}\leq Cp^{2}\|u\|_{2}^{4},

\displaystyle E|\bm{X}_{j}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\bm{X}_{j}-\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p})|^{4}\leq Cp^{2},

\displaystyle 0\leq n^{-1}\bm{X}_{j}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\bm{X}_{j},\qquad(1+n^{-1}\bm{X}_{j}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{j,\lambda}\bm{X}_{j})^{-1}\leq 1.

The third inequality is the quadratic-form bound of Bai and Silverstein
[
16
]
; it is also Lemma S.1.4 in Li and Xu
[
11
]
. With

|u_{j}|+|v_{j}|\leq Cn^{-1/2},\qquad p/n\to\gamma,

these inequalities imply

E|p^{-1/2}B_{j,\lambda}(\bm{u},\bm{v})|^{4}\leq Cn^{-2},\qquad E_{j-1}|p^{-1/2}(E_{j}-E_{j-1})B_{j,\lambda}(\bm{u},\bm{v})|^{4}\leq Cn^{-2}+r_{n,j},

where

\sum_{j=1}^{n}Er_{n,j}=O(n^{-1}).

Hence

\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j-1}|\xi_{n,j}|^{4}=O_{p}(n^{-1}).

For every
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j-1}\{\xi_{n,j}^{2}I(|\xi_{n,j}|>\eta)\}

\displaystyle\leq\eta^{-2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j-1}|\xi_{n,j}|^{4}=O_{p}(n^{-1})=o_{p}(1).

For the conditional variance,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j-1}\xi_{n,j}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}}b_{q_{1}}b_{q_{2}}p^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j-1}\Big[(E_{j}-E_{j-1})B_{j,\lambda_{a_{q_{1}},n}}(\bm{u}_{q_{1}},\bm{v}_{q_{1}})

\displaystyle\times(E_{j}-E_{j-1})B_{j,\lambda_{a_{q_{2}},n}}(\bm{u}_{q_{2}},\bm{v}_{q_{2}})\Big].

Expanding
B_{j,\lambda}
and using

\displaystyle E\{(\bm{X}_{j}^{T}\mathbf{A}\bm{X}_{j}-\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}))(\bm{X}_{j}^{T}\mathbf{B}\bm{X}_{j}-\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}))\mid\mathbf{X}^{(j)}\}

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p})+O\left[\{\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p})\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p}\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{p})\}^{1/2}\delta_{n}\right],

where the truncation error
\delta_{n}\to 0
under the finite fourth moment assumption, yields

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_{j-1}\xi_{n,j}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{1},q_{2}}b_{q_{1}}b_{q_{2}}\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{a_{q_{1}}a_{q_{2}},n}\{(\bm{u}_{q_{1}}^{T}\bm{u}_{q_{2}})(\bm{v}_{q_{1}}^{T}\bm{v}_{q_{2}})+(\bm{u}_{q_{1}}^{T}\bm{v}_{q_{2}})(\bm{v}_{q_{1}}^{T}\bm{u}_{q_{2}})\}+O_{p}(n^{-1/2}).

All terms in
\Gamma_{ab,n}
are finite linear combinations of traces of the form displayed in Lemma
A.2
. Thus
\Gamma_{ab,n}\to\Gamma_{ab}
and the conditional variance converges in probability to
\sigma_{L}^{2}
. The martingale central limit theorem of Hall and Heyde
[
17
]
gives

Lemma A.4

(Uniform increment bound)
.

For

Z_{n,\ell}^{\Omega}(s)=\sqrt{p}\,\frac{(N(s)/n)p^{-1}\tilde{\bm{u}}_{n}(s)^{T}\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{\lambda_{\ell,n}}\mathbf{X}\tilde{\bm{u}}_{n}(s)-\Theta_{n}(\lambda_{\ell,n})}{\Gamma_{n}(\lambda_{\ell,n})^{1/2}},

\ell

If
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{\rm sc}(\varepsilon)

E\{|Z_{n,\ell}^{\Omega}(t)-Z_{n,\ell}^{\Omega}(t_{1})|^{2}|Z_{n,\ell}^{\Omega}(t_{2})-Z_{n,\ell}^{\Omega}(t)|^{2}\}\leq C|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{2}+Cn^{-2}

holds for
\varepsilon\leq t_{1}\leq t\leq t_{2}\leq 1-\varepsilon
. Consequently

\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{S})^{L}
. For deterministic finite
\mathcal{S}
, tightness is finite-dimensional.

By Lemma
A.1
,

\|\tilde{\bm{u}}_{n}(s)-\tilde{\bm{u}}_{n}(r)\|_{2}^{2}\leq C\|s-r\|_{1}+Cn^{-1}.

For regular
\bm{u}_{1},\bm{u}_{2}
,

\displaystyle E\Big|\sqrt{p}\{p^{-1}\bm{u}_{1}^{T}\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{\lambda}\mathbf{X}\bm{u}_{1}-Ep^{-1}\bm{u}_{1}^{T}\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{\lambda}\mathbf{X}\bm{u}_{1}-p^{-1}\bm{u}_{2}^{T}\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{\lambda}\mathbf{X}\bm{u}_{2}+Ep^{-1}\bm{u}_{2}^{T}\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{\lambda}\mathbf{X}\bm{u}_{2}\}\Big|^{2}

This inequality is Theorem S.2.3 of Li and Xu
[
11
]
, with constants uniform for

. Multiplication by
N(s)/n=\kappa(s)^{-1}+O(n^{-1})
preserves the rate because

c_{\varepsilon}\leq\kappa(s)^{-1}\leq C_{\varepsilon}.

The first display follows. The adjacent-increment bound is obtained by applying the Burkholder inequality to the martingale decomposition in Lemma
A.3

The fourth-moment inequality above is the truncated-variable extension used in Bai and Silverstein
[
15
]
and in the proof of Theorem S.2.4 of Li and Xu
[
11
]

[
18
]
gives tightness on

uniformly in

and
\ell

b_{\ell m}
,

\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\sum_{m=1}^{M}b_{\ell m}Z_{n,\ell}^{\Omega}(s_{m})\xrightarrow{d}N\left(0,\sum_{\ell,a=1}^{L}\sum_{m,r=1}^{M}b_{\ell m}b_{ar}\rho_{\ell a}K_{0}(s_{m},s_{r})\right),

where

\rho_{\ell a}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\Gamma_{\ell a,n}}{\{\Gamma_{n}(\lambda_{\ell,n})\Gamma_{n}(\lambda_{a,n})\}^{1/2}},\qquad\rho_{\ell\ell}=1.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cov}\{Z_{n,\ell}^{\Omega}(s),Z_{n,a}^{\Omega}(r)\}

\displaystyle=\frac{(N(s)/n)(N(r)/n)\Gamma_{\ell a,n}\{\tilde{\bm{u}}_{n}(s)^{T}\tilde{\bm{u}}_{n}(r)\}^{2}}{\{\Gamma_{n}(\lambda_{\ell,n})\Gamma_{n}(\lambda_{a,n})\}^{1/2}}+O(n^{-1/2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma_{\ell a,n}}{\{\Gamma_{n}(\lambda_{\ell,n})\Gamma_{n}(\lambda_{a,n})\}^{1/2}}\frac{\kappa(s,r)^{2}}{\kappa(s)\kappa(r)}+O(n^{-1/2}).

The Cramer–Wold device gives finite-dimensional convergence, and Lemma
A.4
gives tightness. Hence

\{Z_{n,\ell}^{\Omega}(s):s\in\mathcal{S},\ell=1,\ldots,L\}\xrightarrow{d}\{G_{\ell}^{\Omega}(s):s\in\mathcal{S},\ell=1,\ldots,L\}.

It remains to replace
\mathbf{\Omega}_{n}
by the centered covariance
\mathbf{S}_{n}=\mathbf{\Omega}_{n}-\bar{\bm{X}}\bar{\bm{X}}^{T}
. Lemma
A.5
gives

\max_{1\leq\ell\leq L}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}|Z_{n,\ell}^{S}(s)-Z_{n,\ell}^{\Omega}(s)|=O_{p}(p^{-1/2}),

where
Z_{n,\ell}^{S}
denotes the deterministically standardized process based on
S_{n}
.

The empirical centering and scaling errors are controlled by Lemma 3.1 of Li and Xu
[
11
]
:

\sup_{\lambda\in[\underline{\lambda},\overline{\lambda}]}\sqrt{n}|\widehat{\Theta}_{\lambda}-\Theta_{n}(\lambda)|=o_{p}(1),\qquad\sup_{\lambda\in[\underline{\lambda},\overline{\lambda}]}\sqrt{n}|\widehat{\Gamma}_{\lambda}-\Gamma_{n}(\lambda)|=o_{p}(1).

Because
p/n\to\gamma
,

\sqrt{p}\max_{\ell}|\widehat{\Theta}_{\lambda_{\ell,n}}-\Theta_{n}(\lambda_{\ell,n})|=o_{p}(1).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\max_{\ell}\left|\widehat{\Gamma}_{\lambda_{\ell,n}}^{-1/2}-\Gamma_{n}(\lambda_{\ell,n})^{-1/2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\max_{\ell}|\widehat{\Gamma}_{\lambda_{\ell,n}}-\Gamma_{n}(\lambda_{\ell,n})|=o_{p}(n^{-1/2}),

and Lemmas
A.3
–
A.4
give

\max_{\ell}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\left|\sqrt{p}\{p^{-1}V_{\lambda_{\ell,n}}(s)-\Theta_{n}(\lambda_{\ell,n})\}\right|=O_{p}(1).

Consequently,

\max_{1\leq\ell\leq L}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}|D_{\lambda_{\ell,n}}(s)-Z_{n,\ell}^{S}(s)|=o_{p}(1).

Combining the three displays,

\{D_{\lambda_{\ell,n}}(s):s\in\mathcal{S},\ell=1,\ldots,L\}\xrightarrow{d}\{G_{\ell}(s):s\in\mathcal{S},\ell=1,\ldots,L\}.

For
\ell=a
,
\rho_{\ell\ell}=1
, so the marginal covariance is
K_{0}(s,r)
. The map

(f_{1},\ldots,f_{L})\mapsto(\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}f_{1}(s),\ldots,\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}f_{L}(s))

is continuous on
\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{S})^{L}
at continuous sample paths. Therefore

(T_{\lambda_{1,n}},\ldots,T_{\lambda_{L,n}})\xrightarrow{d}(T_{\infty,1},\ldots,T_{\infty,L}),\qquad T_{\infty,\ell}=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}G_{\ell}(s).

Since
F_{\mathcal{S}}
is continuous,

\bm{P}_{n}=\{1-F_{\mathcal{S}}(T_{\lambda_{1,n}}),\ldots,1-F_{\mathcal{S}}(T_{\lambda_{L,n}})\}^{T}\xrightarrow{d}\{1-F_{\mathcal{S}}(T_{\infty,1}),\ldots,1-F_{\mathcal{S}}(T_{\infty,L})\}^{T}.

∎

A.2
Proof of Theorem
2

Proof.

By Theorem
1
,

\bm{P}_{n}\xrightarrow{d}\bm{P}_{\infty}.

For every
\delta\in(0,1/2)
, the map

C_{\delta}(\bm{p})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}w_{\ell}\tan\{\pi(1/2-p_{\ell})\}1\{\delta\leq p_{\ell}\leq 1-\delta\}

is continuous. Since
P_{\infty,\ell}\in(0,1)
almost surely,

\mathbb{P}\{\min_{\ell}P_{\infty,\ell}\leq\delta\text{ or }\max_{\ell}P_{\infty,\ell}\geq 1-\delta\}\downarrow 0.

Thus

C(\bm{P}_{n})\xrightarrow{d}C(\bm{P}_{\infty}).

At every continuity point
c_{\alpha,L}
,

\mathbb{P}\{C(\bm{P}_{n})>c_{\alpha,L}\}\to\mathbb{P}\{C(\bm{P}_{\infty})>c_{\alpha,L}\}=\alpha.

For every fixed
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
,

P_{\operatorname{CCT}}\leq\alpha\Longleftrightarrow\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan C(\bm{P}_{n})\leq\alpha\Longleftrightarrow C(\bm{P}_{n})\geq\cot(\pi\alpha).

If
\cot(\pi\alpha)
is a continuity point,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(P_{\operatorname{CCT}}\leq\alpha)=\mathbb{P}\{C(\bm{P}_{\infty})\geq\cot(\pi\alpha)\}.

If

\mathbb{P}\{C(\bm{P}_{\infty})>t\}\sim(\pi t)^{-1},\qquad t\to\infty,

then, since

\cot(\pi\alpha)=\frac{1}{\pi\alpha}\{1+O(\alpha^{2})\},\qquad\alpha\downarrow 0,

we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(P_{\operatorname{CCT}}\leq\alpha)}{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\frac{\mathbb{P}\{C(\bm{P}_{\infty})\geq\cot(\pi\alpha)\}}{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\alpha\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\pi\alpha\cot(\pi\alpha)}=1.

∎

A.3
Proof of Theorem
3

Proof.

Let

M_{n}=\max_{1\leq\ell\leq L}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}|Z_{n,\ell}(s)|.

The assumption
M_{n}=O_{p}(1)
means that, for each
K\to\infty
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>K)\to 0.

For the favorable grid point
\ell_{0}
,

\displaystyle T_{\lambda_{\ell_{0},n}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}D_{\lambda_{\ell_{0},n}}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}R_{n,\ell_{0}}(s)-\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}|Z_{n,\ell_{0}}(s)|-a_{n}

\displaystyle\geq R_{n,\ell_{0}}^{*}-M_{n}-a_{n}.

For every fixed
K>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(T_{\lambda_{\ell_{0},n}}\leq K)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(R_{n,\ell_{0}}^{*}-M_{n}-a_{n}\leq K)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq R_{n,\ell_{0}}^{*}-K-a_{n}).

Since
R_{n,\ell_{0}}^{*}\to\infty
,
a_{n}\to 0
, and
M_{n}=O_{p}(1)
,

\mathbb{P}(T_{\lambda_{\ell_{0},n}}\leq K)\to 0,\qquad K<\infty.

Thus

T_{\lambda_{\ell_{0},n}}\to\infty\quad\text{in probability}.

Because
F_{\mathcal{S}}(x)\uparrow 1
as
x\to\infty
,

P_{\lambda_{\ell_{0},n}}=1-F_{\mathcal{S}}(T_{\lambda_{\ell_{0},n}})\to 0\quad\text{in probability}.

For
p\downarrow 0
,

\tan\{\pi(1/2-p)\}=\cot(\pi p)=\frac{1}{\pi p}+O(p).

Hence

Y_{n,\ell_{0}}\to\infty\quad\text{in probability},\qquad w_{\ell_{0}}Y_{n,\ell_{0}}\to\infty\quad\text{in probability}.

Let

B_{n}=\sum_{\ell\neq\ell_{0}}w_{\ell}Y_{n,\ell}.

Then

C_{n}=w_{\ell_{0}}Y_{n,\ell_{0}}+B_{n}\geq w_{\ell_{0}}Y_{n,\ell_{0}}-[B_{n}]_{-}.

The no-cancellation condition gives

\frac{[B_{n}]_{-}}{w_{\ell_{0}}Y_{n,\ell_{0}}}\to 0\quad\text{in probability},

and therefore

C_{n}\geq w_{\ell_{0}}Y_{n,\ell_{0}}\left(1-\frac{[B_{n}]_{-}}{w_{\ell_{0}}Y_{n,\ell_{0}}}\right)\to\infty\quad\text{in probability}.

Since

P_{\operatorname{CCT}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan(C_{n}),\qquad\arctan x\uparrow\frac{\pi}{2}\quad(x\to\infty),

we obtain

P_{\operatorname{CCT}}\to 0\quad\text{in probability}.

For any fixed
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\mathbb{P}(P_{\operatorname{CCT}}\leq\alpha)\to 1.

∎
