Newton-type inequalities for the class of modified hyperbolic p-convex functions via Riemann-Liouville fractional integrals with numerical justifications

Iqra Iqra, Ammara Nosheen, Khuram Ali Khan, Saima Riaz, Michael Kikomba Kahungu

ABSTRACT
In this paper, an identity involving Riemann-Liouville fractional integrals is established which serves as the basis for several new Newton-type inequalities involving the modified class of hyperbolic p-convex property of differentiable functions. Some new inequalities are also derived for functions of bounded variation. Examples along with graphs are provided to demonstrate the validity of the newly established inequalities, and the comparison with earlier results highlights their enhanced applicability and significance.

1 Introduction Convex functions are one of the cornerstones of mathematical analysis, with their high level of applicability to a broad class of problems, including optimization theory [ 1 ], economic models [ 2 ], machine learning algorithms [ 3 ], and functional analysis [ 4 ]. The basic property that guarantees the uniqueness of the minima for functions is of vital importance for the stability of the methods. The classical concept of convexity has been extremely fruitful, giving rise to a broad class of generalizations, which greatly enrich the mathematical world [ 5 ]. Several types of extended convexity notions have been introduced, namely, s -convex functions [ 6 ], m -convex functions [ 7 ], hyperbolic p -convex functions of a modified class [ 8 ], and generalized exponential type convex functions [ 9 ]. This idea has been seen to take the most profound form in the theory of inequalities, where results such as Simpson-type inequalities, Newton-type inequalities [ 10 ], trapezoidal and midpoint type inequalities, and Giaccardi inequality [ 11 ] have been instrumental in solving many scientific and mathematical challenges. For instance, multi-output quantile regression neural networks [ 12 ] use convex optimization algorithms that depend on improved inequalities. Likewise, homogeneity identification in functional coefficient quantile regression with panel data [ 13 ] uses convex analysis to accommodate censored data. Quantum physics research involving stabilizer testing and central limit theorems [ 14 ] applies convexity in state estimation. Astronomical studies like the detection of new gamma-ray blazars [ 15 ] also apply fractional calculus in signal processing. Fractional calculus has developed into an important mathematical idea with the power to describe the intricate behavior of complex systems, especially those showing memory effects. The increasing use of fractional calculus is an evidence of its power to move beyond the limitations of classical approaches. The connection between fractional calculus and inequality theory has led to the derivation of several important fractional integral inequalities, which have significant value in the context of approximation theory. These inequalities have important value for the derivation of error estimates for numerical integration schemes, such as trapezoidal, Hermite-Hadamard, Simpson, mid-point, and Ostrowski-type inequalities. Further developments in this area are the works of Sarikaya and Yildirim [ 16 ], who introduced novel Hermite-Hadamard type and midpoint type inequalities for differentiable convex functions in terms of Riemann-Liouville fractional integrals. Sarikaya et al. [ 17 ] derived generalized Simpson-type inequalities for differentiable s -convex functions. Each of the fractional operators has unique properties that make them useful in modeling and analyzing problems in different fields of science and engineering. The fractional order α in the Riemann-Liouville integrals provides additional flexibility, enabling the inequalities to capture memory effects and non-local behavior inherent in many physical and engineering systems. Recently, several studies have advanced fractional Newton-type inequalities. Meftah et al. [ 18 ] established new local fractional Newton-type inequalities. Hezenci and Budak [ 19 ] derived fractional Newton-type integral inequalities for various function classes. Özdemir and Yıldız [ 20 ] obtained integral inequalities via Caputo and Liouville fractional operators for m -convex functions. Karagözoğlu et al. [ 21 ] extended Newton-type inequalities to co-ordinated convex functions. Our work complements these by focusing on modified hyperbolic p -convex functions. The research of Simpson-type inequalities within the framework of fractional calculus has gained significant scholarly interest. The authors of [ 22 ] derived Simpson-type inequalities for general convex functions in terms of Riemann-Liouville fractional integrals. Later, Chen and Huang [ 23 ] provided alternative forms of the inequalities for differentiable s -convex functions. The study of Newton-type inequalities for fractional integrals is not merely a mathematical generalization but has practical implications in numerical analysis, error estimation, and signal processing. Modified hyperbolic p -convex functions, introduced recently in [ 8 ], arise naturally in problems involving hyperbolic geometries and certain nonlinear models where classical convexity fails. However, no fractional Newton-type inequalities have been derived for this class. Our work fills this gap. Furthermore, compared to classical convexity, the additional parameters m , s , p allow finer control over function growth, making the inequalities adaptable to a wider range of applications, such as fractional-order system identification and machine learning regularization. The numerical comparisons with [ 25 ] demonstrate that our bounds are often tighter, providing better error estimates for quadrature rules. Motivated by our ongoing research, we derive Newton-type inequalities for differentiable modified hyperbolic p -convex functions and functions of bounded variation in terms of Riemann-Liouville fractional integrals. Moreover, we illustrate their validity with graphical examples. The paper is organized as follows: section 2 includes some fundamental definitions of fractional calculus and some preliminary important results. One important integral identity which plays a central role to prove our main results is established in section 3 and we present new Newton-type inequalities for Riemann-Liouville fractional integrals of modified hyperbolic p -convex functions. Using Riemann-Liouville fractional integrals, we establish fractional Newton-type inequalities for functions of bounded variation in section 4 . Discussion on parameters The parameters m , s , p , and α provide options to control the derived inequalities. The first parameter m ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] adjusts the weighting at the right end of an interval; the smaller m , the more accurate the upper/lower bound, however, narrower the set of functions. The asymmetry factor s ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] determines how much weight to assign each curvature κ 1 and κ 2 . For s = 1, a symmetric formulation is achieved. The exponent parameter p > 0 defines the geometry of convexity; in particular, p = 1, m = s = 1, results in a conventional convexity. The fractional order α > 0 controls the memory effect in the Riemann-Liouville integration operator; high values produce a smoothing effect and enlarge the upper/lower bounds. Some refined results are obtained by applying power-mean and Hölder inequalities. Numerical examples and 2D and 3D graphical representations support the findings, and a comparative analysis with existing work illustrates that our findings generalize and enhance already existing inequalities. Future research areas may include the application of these findings to additional fractional derivatives such as Caputo and Atangana-Baleanu derivatives, proving error estimates for numerical methods of fractional integrals, the study of similar inequalities in quantum calculus, and further applications in machine learning.
