Hypergraph and Latent ODE Learning for Multimodal Root Cause Localization in Microservices
Xin Liu*, Yuhang He, Sichen Zhao, Kejian Tong, Xingyu Zhang

Abstract
Root cause localization in cloud native microservice systems requires modeling complex service dependencies, irregular temporal dynamics, and heterogeneous observability data. We present HyperODE RCA, a unified framework that combines hypergraph attention learning, latent ordinary differential equations, and multimodal cross attention fusion for fine grained root cause analysis. The method learns higher order service interactions through differentiable hyperedge construction, captures continuous anomaly evolution from irregular observations with an ODE RNN encoder, and adaptively fuses logs, traces, metrics, entities, and events using context aware modality routing. We further improve robustness with a variational information bottleneck, temporal causal regularization, and invariant risk constraints. Experiments on the Tianchi AIOps benchmark show clear gains over strong baselines in ranking and classification performance, while preserving interpretability through learned hypergraph attention.

III
Methodology

We propose HyperODE-RCA for root cause localization in cloud-native microservices. Unlike prior methods based on pairwise dependencies and discrete-time assumptions, it models higher-order fault propagation and continuous anomaly evolution. The framework combines HyperGAT, which learns multi-service dependencies through differentiable hyperedge generation with Gumbel-Softmax relaxation, and a Latent ODE encoder, which captures continuous-time dynamics, supports interpolation at arbitrary times, and derives anomaly propagation velocity. Logs, traces, metrics, and events are fused by Transformer-based cross-attention with learned modality routing, where asymmetric inter-modal attention improves causal inference. The model further incorporates a variational information bottleneck, invariant-risk-minimization-based causal sufficiency constraints, temporal causal masking, contrastive log template clustering, and median-absolute-deviation normalization. On the Tianchi AIOps benchmark, HyperODE-RCA outperforms MicroRCA, CloudRanger, and recent causal discovery methods, while providing interpretable explanations through attention visualization over the learned hypergraph. IV
Algorithm and Model

IV-A

HyperGAT: Hypergraph Attention Network

Pairwise graph models are insufficient for microservice failures caused by coordinated multi-service interactions. HyperGAT addresses this with learned hyperedges and causal message passing, as illustrated in Fig.
2
.

Fig. 2:
HyperGAT module. (Center) Learned hypergraph with overlapping hyperedges capturing multi-service failure patterns. (Top-left) Differentiable hyperedge learning via Gumbel-Softmax with temperature annealing. (Top-right) Two-stage message passing: vertex-to-hyperedge aggregation followed by attention-weighted hyperedge-to-vertex distribution. (Bottom) Temporal causal regularization penalizing backward-in-time attention weights.

IV-A
1
Hypergraph Representation

We model the service topology as a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}_{h})
with incidence matrix
\mathbf{H}\in\{0,1\}^{|\mathcal{V}|\times|\mathcal{E}_{h}|}
:

H_{v,e}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }v\in e\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(1)

Hyperedges are inferred from data rather than predefined.

IV-A
2
Differentiable Hyperedge Learning

To enable gradient-based learning, hyperedge assignment is relaxed by Gumbel-Softmax. For candidate hyperedge
e_{k}
over service subset
S_{k}
,

\ell_{k}=\mathbf{w}_{e}^{T}\cdot\text{MLP}_{\phi}\left(\frac{1}{|S_{k}|}\sum_{v_{i}\in S_{k}}\mathbf{h}_{v_{i}}\right)

(2)

and the soft incidence value is

\tilde{H}_{v,e_{k}}=\frac{\exp((\ell_{k}+g_{1})/\tau)}{\exp((\ell_{k}+g_{1})/\tau)+\exp((-\ell_{k}+g_{2})/\tau)}

(3)

where
g_{1},g_{2}\sim\text{Gumbel}(0,1)
and
\tau>0
. We anneal
\tau
from 1.0 to 0.1 and restrict candidate hyperedges using trace call graphs and log co-occurrence.

IV-A
3
Hypergraph Convolution with Causal Attention

With
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}
, HyperGAT performs two-stage message passing. Degree matrices are

\tilde{D}_{v}=\text{diag}\left(\sum_{e}\tilde{H}_{v,e}\right),\quad\tilde{D}_{e}=\text{diag}\left(\sum_{v}\tilde{H}_{v,e}\right)

(4)

Hyperedge messages are

\mathbf{m}_{e}=\sum_{u\in e}\frac{\tilde{H}_{u,e}}{\tilde{D}_{e}[e,e]}\cdot\mathbf{W}_{v}\mathbf{h}_{u}

(5)

and vertex updates are

\mathbf{h}_{v}^{(l+1)}=\sigma\left(\sum_{e\ni v}\alpha_{e\rightarrow v}\cdot\mathbf{W}_{e}\mathbf{m}_{e}\right)

(6)

with attention

\alpha_{e\rightarrow v}=\frac{\exp\left(\text{LeakyReLU}\left(\mathbf{a}^{T}[\mathbf{W}_{q}\mathbf{h}_{v}\|\mathbf{m}_{e}]\right)\right)}{\sum_{e^{\prime}\ni v}\exp\left(\text{LeakyReLU}\left(\mathbf{a}^{T}[\mathbf{W}_{q}\mathbf{h}_{v}\|\mathbf{m}_{e^{\prime}}]\right)\right)}

(7)

We use
L=3
layers with residual connections.

IV-A
4
Temporal Causal Regularization

Temporal precedence is encouraged by penalizing backward attention:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{temp}}=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\sum_{(u,v)\in e\times e}\tilde{H}_{u,e}\cdot\tilde{H}_{v,e}\cdot\alpha_{u\rightarrow v}\cdot\max(0,\bar{t}_{u}-\bar{t}_{v})

(8)

where
\bar{t}_{v}
is the mean anomaly onset time estimated from metric deviations.

IV-B

Latent ODE Encoder for Continuous Dynamics

Because observability signals are irregularly sampled while fault propagation is continuous, we model latent dynamics with a neural ODE and ODE-RNN encoder, shown in Fig.
3
.

Fig. 3:
Latent ODE Encoder. Irregularly sampled observations (orange dots) are integrated into a continuous latent trajectory
\mathbf{z}(t)
via neural ODE dynamics. At each observation time, a GRU cell updates the state by incorporating new evidence. The continuous formulation enables extraction of propagation velocity
v(t)
and acceleration
a(t)
as diagnostic features, which spike at fault onset.

IV-B
1
Neural ODE Formulation

The latent state
\mathbf{z}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
evolves as

\frac{d\mathbf{z}(t)}{dt}=f_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}(t),t)

(9)

with vector field

f_{\theta}(\mathbf{z},t)=\mathbf{W}_{2}\cdot\text{SiLU}(\mathbf{W}_{1}[\mathbf{z}\|\gamma(t)]+\mathbf{b}_{1})+\mathbf{b}_{2}

(10)

and time encoding

\gamma(t)=[\cos(2\pi\mathbf{B}t),\sin(2\pi\mathbf{B}t)]

(11)

where
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\gamma}/2}
is fixed after sampling.

IV-B
2
ODE-RNN for Irregular Observations

For observations
\{(t_{i},\mathbf{x}_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
, latent dynamics between timestamps follow

\mathbf{z}(t_{i}^{-})=\mathbf{z}(t_{i-1}^{+})+\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}f_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}(\tau),\tau)d\tau

(12)

solved by a Dormand-Prince adaptive solver. At each observation, a GRU updates the state:

\mathbf{r}_{i}=\sigma(\mathbf{W}_{xr}\mathbf{x}_{i}+\mathbf{W}_{hr}\mathbf{z}(t_{i}^{-})+\mathbf{b}_{r})

(13)

\mathbf{u}_{i}=\sigma(\mathbf{W}_{xu}\mathbf{x}_{i}+\mathbf{W}_{hu}\mathbf{z}(t_{i}^{-})+\mathbf{b}_{u})

(14)

\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_{i}=\tanh(\mathbf{W}_{xz}\mathbf{x}_{i}+\mathbf{W}_{hz}(\mathbf{r}_{i}\odot\mathbf{z}(t_{i}^{-}))+\mathbf{b}_{z})

(15)

\mathbf{z}(t_{i}^{+})=(1-\mathbf{u}_{i})\odot\mathbf{z}(t_{i}^{-})+\mathbf{u}_{i}\odot\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_{i}

(16)

Gradients are computed by the adjoint sensitivity method:

\frac{d\mathbf{a}(t)}{dt}=-\mathbf{a}(t)^{T}\frac{\partial f_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}(t),t)}{\partial\mathbf{z}}

(17)

where
\mathbf{a}(t)=\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\mathbf{z}(t)
.

IV-B
3
Propagation Velocity Features

Continuous dynamics also provide derivative-based diagnostics:

v(t)=\left\|f_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}(t),t)\right\|_{2}

(18)

a(t)=\frac{dv(t)}{dt}=\frac{f_{\theta}^{T}\cdot J_{f_{\theta}}\cdot f_{\theta}}{\|f_{\theta}\|_{2}}

(19)

where
J_{f_{\theta}}=\partial f_{\theta}/\partial\mathbf{z}
. Velocity and acceleration are appended to the final representation.

IV-C

Multi-Modal Fusion via Cross-Attention Transformer

Logs, traces, metrics, entities, and events are first encoded separately and then fused through structured cross-attention with adaptive routing, as shown in Fig.
4
.

Fig. 4:
Multi-modal cross-attention fusion architecture. Each modality is first refined through intra-modal self-attention, then cross-modal attention with adaptive routing weights
\beta_{m,m^{\prime}}
enables modalities to selectively attend to each other based on incident context. Hierarchical attention pooling aggregates token-level representations into a unified embedding
\mathbf{z}^{(\text{final})}
.

IV-C
1
Modality-Specific Encoding

Logs are encoded by pre-trained RoBERTa after contrastive template clustering:

p(j|l_{i})=\frac{\exp(\text{sim}(\mathbf{h}_{l_{i}},\mathbf{p}_{j})/\tau_{c})}{\sum_{j^{\prime}}\exp(\text{sim}(\mathbf{h}_{l_{i}},\mathbf{p}_{j^{\prime}})/\tau_{c})}

(20)

where
\text{sim}(\cdot,\cdot)
is cosine similarity and
\tau_{c}=0.07
.

Trace spans are encoded by a 2-layer GAT:

\mathbf{h}_{i}^{(l+1)}=\sigma\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}\alpha_{ij}\mathbf{W}^{(l)}\mathbf{h}_{j}^{(l)}\right)

(21)

Metric sequences are modeled by dilated causal convolutions:

\mathbf{y}^{(l)}=\text{ReLU}(\text{Conv1D}(\mathbf{y}^{(l-1)};\text{dilation}=2^{l}))

(22)

IV-C
2
Cross-Modal Transformer

Given modality token sequences
\{\mathbf{Z}^{(m)}\}_{m\in\mathcal{M}}
, intra-modal self-attention is

\hat{\mathbf{Z}}^{(m)}=\text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{Z}^{(m)}+\text{MultiHead}(\mathbf{Z}^{(m)},\mathbf{Z}^{(m)},\mathbf{Z}^{(m)}))

(23)

Cross-modal attention from
m^{\prime}
to
m
is

\mathbf{C}^{(m\leftarrow m^{\prime})}=\text{MultiHead}(\hat{\mathbf{Z}}^{(m)},\hat{\mathbf{Z}}^{(m^{\prime})},\hat{\mathbf{Z}}^{(m^{\prime})})

(24)

with

\text{MultiHead}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V})=\text{Concat}(\text{head}_{1},\ldots,\text{head}_{h})\mathbf{W}^{O}

(25)

\text{head}_{i}=\text{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}_{i}^{Q}(\mathbf{K}\mathbf{W}_{i}^{K})^{T}}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}\right)\mathbf{V}\mathbf{W}_{i}^{V}

(26)

Routing weights depend on incident context:

\beta_{m,m^{\prime}}=\frac{\exp(s_{m,m^{\prime}})}{\sum_{m^{\prime\prime}}\exp(s_{m,m^{\prime\prime}})},\quad s_{m,m^{\prime}}=\mathbf{w}^{T}[\bar{\mathbf{z}}^{(m)}\|\bar{\mathbf{z}}^{(m^{\prime})}\|\mathbf{a}]

(27)

and fused modality representations are

\mathbf{Z}^{(m)}_{\text{fused}}=\hat{\mathbf{Z}}^{(m)}+\sum_{m^{\prime}\neq m}\beta_{m,m^{\prime}}\cdot\mathbf{C}^{(m\leftarrow m^{\prime})}

(28)

IV-C
3
Hierarchical Aggregation

Each modality is pooled by attention:

\mathbf{z}^{(m)}=\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathbf{Z}^{(m)}|}\frac{\exp(\mathbf{w}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{z}_{i}^{(m)})}{\sum_{j}\exp(\mathbf{w}_{p}^{T}\mathbf{z}_{j}^{(m)})}\mathbf{z}_{i}^{(m)}

(29)

The final fused representation is

\mathbf{z}^{(\text{final})}=\text{MLP}([\mathbf{z}^{(\text{log})}\|\mathbf{z}^{(\text{trace})}\|\mathbf{z}^{(\text{metric})}\|\mathbf{z}^{(\text{entity})}\|\mathbf{z}^{(\text{event})}])

(30)

IV-D

Variational Information Bottleneck with Causal Constraints

To suppress spurious correlations, we combine information bottleneck learning with invariance constraints.

IV-D
1
Information Bottleneck Principle

Let
\mathbf{X}
denote observations,
\mathbf{Z}
the representation, and
\mathbf{Y}
the root-cause labels. The IB objective is

\min_{\theta}I(\mathbf{X};\mathbf{Z})-\beta I(\mathbf{Z};\mathbf{Y})

(31)

We optimize a variational form by sampling

\mathbf{z}=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x})+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\phi}(\mathbf{x})\odot\boldsymbol{\epsilon},\quad\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})

(32)

and using the regularizer

D_{\text{KL}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\mu_{j}^{2}+\sigma_{j}^{2}-\log\sigma_{j}^{2}-1\right)

(33)

IV-D
2
Causal Sufficiency Constraint

For environments
\mathcal{E}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{E}\}
defined by time periods or service subsets, we enforce invariant performance:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{causal}}=\text{Var}_{e\in\mathcal{E}}[\mathcal{L}_{e}(\theta)]+\lambda_{\text{grad}}\|\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{e}(\theta)\|_{2}^{2}

(34)

IV-E

Root Cause Classification

Each candidate
c_{k}=\langle\text{service}_{k},\text{fault\_type}_{k}\rangle
is embedded as

\mathbf{e}_{c_{k}}=\mathbf{E}_{\text{svc}}[\text{service}_{k}]+\mathbf{E}_{\text{fault}}[\text{fault\_type}_{k}]

(35)

and scored by

s_{k}=\mathbf{z}^{(\text{final})T}\mathbf{W}_{\text{bil}}\mathbf{e}_{c_{k}}+\mathbf{w}_{z}^{T}\mathbf{z}^{(\text{final})}+\mathbf{w}_{c}^{T}\mathbf{e}_{c_{k}}+b,\quad\hat{y}_{k}=\sigma(s_{k})

(36)

where
\mathbf{W}_{\text{bil}}
is factorized as
\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}^{T}
with rank 64.

IV-F

Training Objective

The full objective is

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}}+\alpha_{1}\mathcal{L}_{\text{IB}}+\alpha_{2}\mathcal{L}_{\text{temp}}+\alpha_{3}\mathcal{L}_{\text{causal}}+\alpha_{4}\mathcal{L}_{\text{sparse}}

(37)

with classification loss

\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}}=-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}[\tilde{y}_{k}\log\hat{y}_{k}+(1-\tilde{y}_{k})\log(1-\hat{y}_{k})]

(38)

where
\tilde{y}_{k}=(1-\epsilon)y_{k}+\epsilon/K
. The sparsity term
\mathcal{L}_{\text{sparse}}=\|\tilde{\mathbf{H}}\|_{1}/(|\mathcal{V}|\cdot|\mathcal{E}_{h}|)
encourages interpretable hypergraphs. Training uses AdamW with learning rate
3\times 10^{-4}
, weight decay 0.01, and cosine annealing with 10% warmup.

IV-G

Data Preprocessing

IV-G
1
Metric Normalization

Heavy-tailed production metrics are normalized by median absolute deviation:

\tilde{m}_{t,k}=\frac{m_{t,k}-\text{median}_{k}}{1.4826\cdot\text{MAD}_{k}+\epsilon}

(39)

where
\text{MAD}_{k}=\text{median}(|m_{t,k}-\text{median}_{k}|)
.

IV-G
2
Missing Value Handling

Short gaps are linearly interpolated, and longer gaps use exponential decay:

\hat{m}_{t}=m_{t_{\text{last}}}\cdot\exp(-\lambda(t-t_{\text{last}}))

(40)

with
\lambda=0.1
per minute.

V
Evaluation Metrics

We adopt five metrics to comprehensively evaluate model performance.

V-A

F1-Score

The primary metric balancing precision and recall:

\text{Precision}=\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP}+\text{FP}},\quad\text{Recall}=\frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP}+\text{FN}}

(41)

\text{F1}=\frac{2\cdot\text{Precision}\cdot\text{Recall}}{\text{Precision}+\text{Recall}}=\frac{2\text{TP}}{2\text{TP}+\text{FP}+\text{FN}}

(42)

V-B

Mean Reciprocal Rank

MRR evaluates ranking quality:

\text{MRR}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\text{rank}_{i}}

(43)

where
\text{rank}_{i}
is the position of the first correct prediction for incident
i
.

V-C

Matthews Correlation Coefficient

MCC provides balanced evaluation for imbalanced classification:

\text{MCC}=\frac{\text{TP}\cdot\text{TN}-\text{FP}\cdot\text{FN}}{\sqrt{(\text{TP}+\text{FP})(\text{TP}+\text{FN})(\text{TN}+\text{FP})(\text{TN}+\text{FN})}}

(44)

V-D

Area Under ROC Curve

AUC measures discrimination capability across all thresholds:

\text{AUC}=\int_{0}^{1}\text{TPR}(t)\,d\text{FPR}(t)=P(\hat{y}_{\text{pos}}>\hat{y}_{\text{neg}})

(45)
