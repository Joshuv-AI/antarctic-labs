 ABSTRACT

Fish passage facilities are used to mitigate the impacts of hydropower dams on migratory fish in rivers,
but information on the location, types, and characteristics of this infrastructure is incomplete at a national
scale. Researchers at Oak Ridge National Laboratory partnered with federal agency and industry
stakeholders to create the first national-scale database of fish passage infrastructure at U.S. hydropower
developments. This database contains information of great value to a broad range of stakeholders; it
includes fish passage facility engineering characteristics, targeted fish species, operational schedules, and
costs. This data resource addresses a large gap in knowledge of the deployment of fish passage
technology and is freely available to members of the hydropower community, including federal and state
regulators and resource agencies, non-governmental organizations, industry, and other user groups to
support project planning and regulatory (re)licensing activities. This database supports the U.S.
Department of Energy Water Power Technologies Office objective to develop decision support tools and
data resources that improve environmental performance and ensure hydropower’s long-term value to the
American public.

 1. BACKGROUND

Hydropower plays an important role in ensuring that homes and businesses across the United States have
access to reliable, affordable, and secure electricity when they need it. By providing electricity generation
on demand, hydropower plants significantly contribute to both the stability and flexibility of the
electricity grid. Hydropower provides 26.8% of the electricity produced by renewable energy and 6% of
the total utility-scale electricity generation in the United States, and pumped storage hydropower
represents 96% of the U.S. energy storage capacity in 2022 (Uria-Martinez et al. 2023).

Infrastructure used to generate hydropower, including dams, power plants, and other water diversion
mechanisms, may negatively impact aquatic ecosystems and organisms. Hydropower infrastructure can
act as a barrier to the longitudinal movement of migratory fish within river systems, reducing population
connectivity and restricting access to habitats that fish need to complete complex life history stages.
Furthermore, the operations of hydropower facilities, particularly during downstream migration, may
cause injury or mortality to fish when they pass through energy generation turbines, from impingement on
barrier screens, or while passing over large spillways. To mitigate these negative impacts on migratory
fish health and population persistence, the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) may require
that hydropower owners construct fish passage infrastructure or implement adaptive management plans
that allow targeted fish species to move past hydropower dams in a safe, timely, and effective manner.
However, FERC-mandated fish passage requirements are not uniformly applied across the U.S.
hydropower fleet. The fish species that these requirements are intended to benefit are only found in
certain regions of the country. Additionally, hydropower licenses typically span periods of 30 to 50 years,
and some active licenses may have been issued before fish passage became a known or pressing issue at
some hydropower developments. New requirements for fish passage or other mitigations are frequently
delayed until the relicensing period, which must begin between 5 and 5.5 years before the expiration date
of the current license and typically require 4 to 11 years to complete (range of one standard deviation
around the mean) based on a sample of 62 FERC projects relicensed after October 2005 (Pracheil et al.
2022).

Fish passage can be accomplished using a wide variety of infrastructure types and technologies, which
must be designed and implemented in the context of numerous hydropower development and target
species-specific parameters. Many fish passage technologies were designed based on the specific
characteristics of the targeted species’ swimming performance (e.g., upstream passage for strong
swimming salmon versus undulating juvenile eels), the direction of passage (e.g., upstream versus
downstream), and the physical characteristics of the dam and proximate river channel (e.g., high-head
versus low-head dam), among others (see review by Katopodis and Williams, 2012). This variety in
technologies, combined with the site-specific nature of passage design and our limited understanding of
the built and natural conditions needed to achieve fish passage (Williams et al. 2012), has also limited our
understanding of the deployment and performance of the various fish passage technologies and their
associated construction, operation, and maintenance costs. The current state of knowledge of fish passage
capabilities at U.S. hydropower developments is incomplete at the national scale and was last censused
over 30 years ago (Čada and Sale, 1993). Existing data resources are limited to particular regions
(compiled by state or federal resource management agencies), time periods (e.g., 1998 to 2013 in Oak
Ridge National Laboratory’s [ORNL’s] Non-Federal Hydropower Mitigation database [Schramm et al.
2016], 1996 to 2018 in ORNL’s Cost of Mitigating the Environmental Impacts of Hydropower Projects
[Werble et al. 2021]), or industry-related interest groups (e.g., compiled by the Low Impact Hydropower
Institute). General information is available from the FERC eLibrary (https://www.ferc.gov/ferc-
online/elibrary), but compilation of this data can be labor-intensive, requiring manual extraction of
information from lengthy regulatory documents. Furthermore, fish passage requirements for hydropower
are becoming more common and stricter, yet the costs associated with constructing and operating fish

passage at hydropower facilities are not well known across the fleet (Oladosu et al. 2021). When
available, information on fish passage costs is often limited to the planning stage instead of the
implementation and operation stages, and that information is usually aggregated, which limits our ability
to identify drivers of costs.

The National Hydropower Fish Passage Database is the first national-scale, geo-referenced database of
fish passage infrastructure at U.S. hydropower developments (Matson et al., 2025a). This database was
developed through collaborative partnerships with fish passage engineers at both the U.S. Fish and
Wildlife Service (USFWS) and the National Marine Fisheries Service (NMFS), and hydropower experts
at the Low Impact Hydropower Institute (LIHI). A subset of these data have been used to describe the
distribution of fish passage facilities across the conterminous United States (CONUS) for the first time in
over 30 years (Matson et al., 2025b). This database is freely available to stakeholders and represents a
valuable resource that will support hydropower permitting, including relicensing, original licenses, and
other decision-making processes in evaluating the U.S. hydropower fleet.

 2. DATABASE PHILOSOPHY

This database contains information that describes the location and characteristics of fish passage facilities
at hydropower developments in CONUS. The database is supported by information solicited from a wide
range of hydropower stakeholders, including but not limited to hydropower owners and operators, state
and federal agency personnel, and non-governmental organizations. This database is intended to be a
living record, expanding as new fish passage facilities are built at new or existing hydropower
developments and shrinking as hydropower developments are retired or associated barriers are removed.
Moreover, the database creates a temporal record by documenting the source and date provided with each
data entry, allowing users to trace the history of fish passage capabilities at a project or within a region or
watershed.

 3. DATA OVERVIEW

The database contains many different types of information related to hydropower developments and fish
passage, beginning with whether passage infrastructure is present or absent at a given hydropower
development. For developments where fish passage infrastructure exists, the database reports information
on the type of passage infrastructure, including the direction of passage as well as the type of technology
(e.g., upstream ice harbor fishway, downstream bypass, bidirectional nature-like fishway, etc.). When
available, the database also reports technical, operational, and cost information, including the physical
attributes of the passage infrastructure, operational scheduling, passage capabilities, and capital costs of
construction and operations and maintenance (O&M). The database also attributes passage performance
studies, fish distributions, and development characteristics to specific developments, fish passage
infrastructure, or hydrologic sub-basins as available.

3.1 TERMINOLOGY

The terminology used to describe hydropower and associated fish passage infrastructure is complicated
and variable. Generally, hydropower dams, powerhouses, and related infrastructure are called
developments, and developments that are managed collectively are called projects (McManamay et al.
2016). This database focuses on infrastructure intentionally used to accomplish fish passage and is
therefore fish-centric in its characterization of potential barriers to fish passage. The terms hydropower
features and fish passage facilities are used to describe infrastructural elements associated with
hydropower production and fish passage within river networks.

 2

Hydropower features are dams or other discrete pieces of infrastructure (e.g., powerhouses, conduits,
etc.) that control or augment flows at a specific location within a river system for the purpose of
hydropower production and potentially impede upstream or downstream movement of fish within the
river system. Multiple hydropower features may belong to the same hydropower project or development
(or share a FERC docket number) but function as multiple distinct barriers to fish movement in a river
system. Dams, structures, or barriers unrelated to hydropower generation are not currently within the
scope of this database and accordingly are not captured in the set of features.

Fish passage facilities are combinations of infrastructure, technology, and operation implemented at
hydropower features for the express purpose of facilitating fish movement upstream, downstream, or
bidirectionally across the barrier. Such facilities include (but are not limited to) upstream fishways, lifts,
or locks, downstream open or pressurized bypass conduits, bidirectional nature-like fishways, and non-
volitional forms of passage such as trap and transport programs. Turbines designed to improve fish
survival by reducing injury and mortality of downstream entrained fish, here referred to as fish-friendlier
turbines (e.g., known as fish-friendly turbines, advanced turbines, ecologically improved turbines; see
Nielsen & Szabo-Meszaros, 2022, Watson et al. 2022, EPRI 2011) and other hydropower infrastructure
for which the primary purpose is not to pass fish (e.g., navigation locks) were not included. This omission
does not imply that these routes of passage cannot provide safe, timely, and effective passage; rather, they
are not within this project’s scope.

3.2 DATA DICTIONARY

ORNL staff engaged in an iterative process with project partners from USFWS, NMFS, and LIHI to
delineate the specific hydropower feature and fish passage facility characteristics to be included in the
database. This suite of database variables was selected based on partner expertise in fish passage facility
design and the types of information that are readily available in existing fish passage datasets or familiar
to hydropower stakeholders. These variables were selected to allow the database to capture a high level of
feature or facility detail while not being so specific as to pertain to only a handful of circumstances.

The full list of database variables—including variable name, description, units, and format—is described
in a data dictionary (Table 3.1). Database variables can be categorized by the characteristics of the
hydropower feature or fish passage facility they describe. These categories include development
characteristics, passage facility infrastructure, passage facility capabilities, passage facility operational
schedule, and passage facility estimated cost. These characteristic groups are stored in separate database
components—called dimensions—and can be accessed and related to the hydropower features, fish
passage facilities, or information sources—called facts—to which they pertain by feature-, facility-, or
source-specific identifiers (see Section 4 for more detail).

3.3 TYPES OF PASSAGE

Fish passage facilities are frequently designed to facilitate the passage of specific migratory fish species
or groups (e.g., salmonids or alosines) at specific hydropower features. This high degree of situation
specificity has resulted in the development of many different types of fish passage infrastructure to meet
site- and species-specific needs. Additionally, regional fish passage datasets and state and federal agency
personnel frequently use different terminology to refer to the same type of passage infrastructure. There is
no encompassing “industry standard” set of fish passage facility infrastructure options or terms used to
describe those options.

Accordingly, ORNL staff worked with project partners to develop a list of general fish passage facility
types that reflects the range of descriptions found in source data. These raw descriptions were then
grouped into simplified type groups (Table 3.2). The simplified type categories allow for assessing
whether multiple data sources describe unique or redundant fish passage facilities at a given hydropower
feature, and thus unique fish passage facility identifiers can be assigned to each unique fish passage
facility in the database. Two facility type groups, downstream bypasses and upstream fish ladders, were
further divided into sub-types because there are multiple common variants of each, and these variants
were reported in sufficient detail to warrant sub-classification.

3.4 DATABASE LIMITATIONS

The database is an important advancement in our understanding of fish passage capabilities at U.S.
hydropower developments and may support the hydropower community in making decisions that benefit
the U.S. power grid and the environment. However, it is important that database users understand the
limitations of the data provided in the database. Importantly, there are three key things the database does
not do. First, the database does not contain information, implicitly or explicitly, regarding the
effectiveness of fish passage facilities reported therein. The existence of a passage facility does not confer
any assumption of successful performance in passing fish. The information presented in this database is
limited to whether passage facilities have been constructed and have been reported as operational; it does
not currently include up-to-date information on whether the facilities have remained operational or at
what levels they are passing fish. Second, the database only considers infrastructure, technology, and
operations that are intentionally designed and implemented to pass fish in a safe, timely, and effective
manner. Consequently, downstream passage through the turbine is not included in the database. Lastly,
the database exclusively reports information on passage capabilities at hydropower-related infrastructure
and does not provide information on potential barriers that may exist elsewhere within river networks.
Accordingly, the database should not be used in isolation to infer river system connectivity for migratory
fish.

 4. DATABASE SCHEMA

The database is organized in a data schematic format. Three fact tables list all distinct hydropower
features, fish passage facilities, and information sources in the database, and dimension tables describe
specific components (e.g., passage existence, type, slope, planning cost, etc.) of facts.

Essentially, the fact tables are lists of database elements (features, facilities, information sources), and
dimension tables describe those elements.

4.1 FACT TABLES

Fact tables are critical components of the database schematic because they contain the universe of
elements contained in the database along with unique identifying keys that link those elements to the data
dimensions. There are three fact tables in the fish passage database: 1) the feature fact table, 2) the
passage facility fact table, and 3) the information source fact table. Each fact table contains a list of
unique elements (features, passage facilities, or information sources) that link to—and are described by—
other dimensions of the database via element-specific unique identifiers (Table 4.1). For instance, the
encompassing list of hydropower features in the database is contained in the feature fact table. The unique
identifiers for the hydropower features in our database, called the Fish Passage Database ID (FPD ID), are
present in dimension tables to link information therein to the appropriate feature.

Updates to fact tables are reflected in all other dimensions of the database without requiring redundant
updates in each dimension individually. Each fact table essentially functions as an entry point to the
database. Users can access relevant information (dimensions) by searching for specific features, facilities,
or information sources.

Note that unique identifiers in the fact tables link elements in the fact tables to one another and also
provide links to hydropower infrastructure information in external data resources. Unique identifiers
FPD_ID, Fish_passage_facility_ID, Information_source FERC_ID, and HUC_08 all provide links
between two or more fact and dimension tables. Unique identifiers HILARRI_ID, NID_ID,
Complex_ID_EHA, and Plant_ID_EHA link entities in the Fish Passage Database to ORNL’s HILARRI
dataset (https://hydrosource.ornl.gov/data/datasets/hilarri-v2/) (Hansen and Matson, 2023), the U.S. Army
Corps of Engineers’ National Inventory of Dams (https://nid.sec.usace.army.mil/#/), and ORNL’s
Existing Hydropower Assets (EHA) plant database (https://hydrosource.ornl.gov/data/datasets/eha-plant-
2025/) (Johnson et al., 2023), respectively (Table 4.1).

4.1.1 Feature Fact Table

The feature fact table contains all identifying information pertaining to a distinct hydropower feature in
the database. This includes all spatial and network information (e.g., geographical coordinates, hydrologic
unit codes, stream and reservoir name, state, etc.) along with other hydropower fleet-related identifiers
(e.g., HILARRI ID, National Inventory of Dams [NID] ID, and FERC docket number) and dam and/or
power plant names. These hydropower feature characteristics collectively allow users to delineate the
complete list of CONUS hydropower features to be described in the database, and those features can thus
be matched with entities described in fish passage data provided by project partners and other hydropower
stakeholders.

The feature fact table contains one row per hydropower feature in the database, and each feature is
assigned a unique Fish Passage Database ID (FPD ID) numeric value that links features in the fact table to
feature-relevant dimension tables. The inclusion of other hydropower dataset identifiers (i.e., HILARRI,
NID, FERC) also allows users to reference hydropower features in the database with existing datasets that
describe other elements of hydropower features not related to fish passage. The feature fact table was
created based on distinct, active hydropower features from the HILARRI dataset (see Section 5.1.1).

4.1.2 Fish Passage Facility Fact Table

The fish passage facility fact table is a complete list of all unique fish passage facilities at hydropower
features in the database. This fact table includes the FPD ID of the hydropower feature at which a given
passage facility is installed. It also includes the passage facility type and sub-type, the direction of passage
that is facilitated, the information source that provided passage information, and any additional narrative
comments about the passage facility from the original data source.

The facility fact table contains one row per report of a fish passage facility by an information source (i.e.,
one to several rows per distinct passage facility if the same facility is described by multiple sources). Each
distinct fish passage facility is assigned a unique Fish Passage Facility ID, which is a unique
alphanumeric code formatted to include the FPD ID of the feature where the facility is located followed
by the facility number at that feature. For instance, hydropower feature FPD_ID 23 might have 3 distinct
passage facilities with Fish Passage Facility IDs of 23_Pass_01, 23_Pass_02, and 23_Pass_03. The Fish
Passage Facility ID values link distinct passage facilities in the facility fact table to the information that
describes them in the facility-relevant dimension tables.

4.1.3 Information Source Fact Table

The information source fact table functions as a directory that links information sources listed in the
facility fact table and passage existence, infrastructure, capabilities, operation, cost, studies, and
comments dimensions to a specific organization. Unlike the other two fact tables, which are unique lists
of features and facilities described by dimensions, the information source fact table provides the unique
list of data sources along with links to their webpages or data download portals.

4.2 DATA DIMENSIONS

The database dimension tables contain descriptive information about hydropower features or fish passage
facilities identified in the fact tables and are linked to those entities via the distinct feature, facility, FERC
docket (passage studies dimension only), or HUC08 (migratory species distribution dimension only)

 14

identifier values delineated in the fact tables. The dimension tables are organized by the category of
information they contain, and they include development characteristics, passage existence, passage
infrastructure, passage capabilities, passage operation, passage costs, passage comments, passage studies,
and migratory species distributions. The purpose of database dimension tables is to allow users to access
specific database elements of interest and to streamline database updates and corrections over time. Each
database variable that is not a unique identifier in a fact table is present in only one dimension.
Additionally, each dimension contains an accession date column, which references the latest date that a
given entry was updated. The dimensions and their constituent database variables are listed below.

4.2.1 Passage Existence

The passage existence dimension is feature-specific and contains information designating whether the
feature has at least one fish passage facility (values: No, Yes, Unknown). This dimension also contains
feature FPD_ID and information source values that link features described in this dimension to features
and sources in the feature and information source fact tables, respectively. Lastly, this dimension contains
a variable describing whether there was conflicting information regarding the existence of fish passage at
a given feature from two or more information sources.

4.2.2 Passage Infrastructure

The passage infrastructure dimension is passage facility-specific. It contains information related to the
physical and engineered infrastructure of passage facilities, including entrance, conveyance, exit, and
protection types, slope, entrance gate width and protection rack spacing, entrance gate tailwater
fluctuation tracking capabilities, hydraulic capacity, percent of total feature hydraulic capacity in passage
facility, and whether the tailwater has been modified to facilitate fish approach/access. Fish passage
facility ID values link facilities referenced in this dimension to the facility fact table, FPD_ID values link
to features in the feature fact table, and information source values link to sources in the information
source fact table.

4.2.3 Passage Capabilities

The passage capabilities dimension is passage facility–specific. It contains information related to the
species-specific design and performance assessment capabilities of a given passage facility. These
variables include target species, other (non-targeted species) known to use the passage facility, whether
the facility has selective mechanisms to prevent the passage of invasive species (and, if so, which
species), whether the facility has passage performance standards, and whether the facility has counting
capabilities (and, if so, which technology, maximum count, and year of observed maximum count). Fish
passage facility ID values link facilities referenced in this dimension to the facility fact table, FPD_ID
values link to features in the feature fact table, and information source values link to sources in the
information source fact table.

4.2.4 Passage Operation

The passage operation dimension is passage facility–specific. It contains information on passage facility
owner and operator and whether the facility operation schedule is continuous or periodic. If passage
operation is periodic, it records the type of periodic operation (e.g., seasonal, daily, etc.), the temporal
window of operation (e.g., specific months or dates when the facility is operating), and the cue that
initiates facility operation (e.g., Julian date, temperature, weather, fish biology, etc.). Fish passage facility
ID values link facilities referenced in this dimension to the facility fact table, FPD_ID values link to
features in the feature fact table, and information source values link to sources in the information source
fact table.

4.2.5 Passage Costs

The passage cost dimension is passage facility–specific. It contains information related to estimated and
actual capital and operational and maintenance costs and the years of those cost estimates and incursions.
It also includes information on average annual monitoring, planning, and generation loss costs, as well as
facility construction year and construction duration. Fish passage facility ID values link facilities
referenced in this dimension to the facility fact table, FPD_ID values link to features in the feature fact
table, and information source values link to sources in the information source fact table.

4.2.6 Migratory Species Distributions

The migratory species distribution dimension is hydropower feature–specific. It uses data from the
HydroBio Dataset (https://hydrosource.ornl.gov/data/datasets/hydrobio/) to summarize, per hydrologic
sub-basin (8-digit hydrologic unit code), the number of migratory fish species of particular interest to the
hydropower community (Table 4.2), and the total number of migratory fish species per taxonomic group
of particular interest to the hydropower community. This dimension can be linked to the feature fact table
by HUC08 value.

4.2.7 Passage Studies

The passage studies dimension is FERC project–specific. This dimension lists the FERC accession
number of passage studies conducted at FERC-licensed features in the database, the date the documents
were filed and appeared in the eLibrary, the passage study description, the study class type, and the
information source that identified the study (e.g., the FERC eLibrary). Individual passage studies are
linked to hydropower features in the feature fact table by shared FERC_ID values. These studies were
identified using a Boolean search within the FERC eLibrary.

4.2.8 Development Characteristics

The development characteristics dimension is hydropower feature–specific. It contains information
relevant to hydropower features in the database that is not present in the feature fact table, including dam
owner and operator, development installed capacity, dam height, turbine type, distance to ocean, number
of upstream and downstream hydropower features, dates of license issue and expiration for FERC-
licensed features, and development operating mode (e.g., run-of-river, peaking, etc.). This dimension also
contains the complex and plant ID values used in ORNL’s EHA Plant Database
(https://hydrosource.ornl.gov/data/datasets/eha-plant-2025/) to allow users to reference the Fish Passage
Database to EHA. This dimension is linked to the feature fact table via shared FPD_ID values.

4.2.9 Passage Comments

The passage comments dimension is hydropower feature–specific, though it also sometimes contains
information pertaining to specific fish passage facilities at hydropower features. It contains any additional
narrative information regarding hydropower features and/or fish passage facilities provided by shared
datasets, but which did not fit cleanly into database variables. This dimension is linked to hydropower
features in the feature fact table via FPD_ID.

 5. BUILDING THE DATABASE

Building the fish passage database was a multi-step process that involved: 1) identifying all distinct
hydropower features in CONUS; 2) collecting fish passage facility information from project partners,
other hydropower stakeholders, online datasets available for download, or the stakeholder questionnaire;
3) matching this information to its corresponding hydropower feature(s); 4) converting the relevant
variables in the obtained data to database format; and 5) stitching all converted data together in the
database schematic. Each time new or updated fish passage data were received from an information
source, the last three steps were repeated to incorporate the updates into the database. In this way, the Fish
Passage Database collated and bundled all fish passage information for a given hydropower feature from
any source(s) that provided it, with each source attributed to the specific information it provided. This
process, including methods for addressing conflicting information from multiple data sources regarding
fish passage facilities and capabilities at a given hydropower feature, is described below.

5.1 IDENTIFYING HYDROPOWER FEATURES

The second version of ORNL’s (HILARRI, https://hydrosource.ornl.gov/dataset/hilarri-v2) (Hansen and
Matson, 2023) was used to identify the complete list of hydropower infrastructure in CONUS to be
included in the database for fish passage information attribution. HILARRI is the optimal resource for this
because it is regularly updated, provides useful spatial, network, and other identifying information (e.g.,
dam and power plant name), and is connected to other hydropower-related data sources, including the
NID and FERC projects. Additionally, because HILARRI is waterbody-focused, it delineates hydropower

 18

infrastructure elements that potentially function as discrete barriers to fish movement within river
networks, which is the focus of the database.

The HILARRI dataset was pruned to retain only features that were active (i.e., had not been removed or
retired or were not in the development pipeline) and removed redundant entries that were double-listed
due to the confluence of two waterbodies (with a HILARRI entry for each), state boundaries, or other
issues. This produced a list of distinct, active hydropower features in the conterminous U.S. to which to
attribute fish passage information. The list contained one row per distinct hydropower feature and
included information on dam and plant name, geographic coordinates, NID ID, FERC docket number, and
network information (stream name, watershed, etc.). Each of these hydropower features was assigned a
unique database ID (FPD_ID), and this became the database feature fact table.

5.2 INFORMATION SOLICITATION

Given that centralized knowledge on fish passage infrastructure is lacking, effort was focused on
identifying diverse and distributed sources from across CONUS to solicit information. This approach
leveraged three different options: 1) existing regional datasets, 2) direct communication with regional
experts (via email, online meetings, or web-based stakeholder questionnaire [Bozeman et al. 2025]), and
3) extracting information from published reports and regulatory documents within the FERC eLibrary.
Direct communication efforts targeted federal and state resource managers focused on hydropower and
fish passage within a given jurisdiction but also included hydropower developers and non-governmental
organizations.

5.3 DATA ATTRIBUTION

Fish passage information was attributed from the above sources to the database using data entry
templates, which contained database variables formatted to our database standards per the data dictionary
(Table 3.1). The exact method of converting information sources to data entry template format differed
based on the source and original format of the data.

5.3.1 Data Entry Templates

Fish passage data provided at direct request from state agency personnel (e.g., Michigan DNR) or
obtained from stakeholder questionnaire responses were already present in the correct template format
and matched to the appropriate hydropower feature FPD_ID. When fish passage information was solicited
for hydropower features in a given jurisdiction (e.g., state) from the relevant agency personnel or similar
contact, a blank data entry template was shared with them in which every hydropower feature in their
jurisdiction was listed. They provided fish passage information for that collection of features as able and
returned the completed data entry template form. Returned data entry template forms were then
incorporated into the database.

5.3.2 Templatizing Existing Datasets

Preexisting datasets describing fish passage facilities at U.S. hydropower features that were shared by
project partners or available for download online (e.g., USGS Fishway Structure Dataset, Gressler et al.,
2022) were not natively formatted for database attribution and needed to be converted to data entry
template format. This was done by first matching the information provided by the data source to the
corresponding hydropower feature in the database using geographic proximity (if coordinates were
provided by the data source) or combinations of NID ID values, FERC docket numbers, and dam and/or
plant names. Features that were matched via naming or dataset ID schemes (and not via spatial proximity)
were manually checked to confirm correct matches. Once the appropriate feature FPD_ID was assigned to

 19

the feature(s) described in the data source, all relevant information in the dataset was converted to the
format of the data entry template and then incorporated into the database.

5.3.3 Resolving Conflicting Information from Multiple Sources

All fish passage information was collated and bundled from all data sources, and that information was
attributed to the appropriate hydropower features in the database. Consequently, there were occasionally
situations where there was conflicting information about fish passage facilities at a given hydropower
feature (e.g., one source reported passage exists and another reported passage does not exist). Despite
attempts to resolve these discrepancies in determining whether passage does or does not exist, all
information pertaining to a given hydropower feature from all data sources was retained, and instances of
conflicting information regarding fish passage existence were denoted in a conflict variable. Future
updates to a given data source from the provider may resolve these conflicts and will be reflected in the
database.

5.3.4 Updating Information

New hydropower features can be added to the feature fact table at any time and assigned the next
available sequential FPD ID value. Similarly, features can be removed from the feature fact table if the
feature no longer produces hydropower or is removed from the river entirely. Removing a feature from
the feature fact table will remove all information associated with that feature from database dimensions
where it appears. However, in order to trace fish passage capabilities over time, an archive of removed
features, a justification for removal from the database, and all original affiliated records are maintained.
New fish passage facilities can be added to the facility fact table by assigning a new Fish Passage Facility
ID value to the new feature after confirming that the facility has not already been described by an existing
data source.

The information source fact table will be updated any time fish passage information is received from a
new source. This change will automatically be reflected in the relevant data schematic dimensions as the
fish passage information from the new source is attributed to the database. In the unlikely event that an
information source is removed from the source information fact table, this change would be reflected
throughout the database via the removal of all records provided from the affected information source.

New information pertaining to features or facilities already present in the database will be attributed to the
dimension(s) whose variable(s) are affected; unaffected dimensions remain unchanged. Each element of
the data schematic contains an accession date column that reflects the date that information was attributed
to the database; these dates will provide a record of updates for any affected features or facilities.

 6. DATA STORAGE AND ACCESS

All database files (fact and dimension tables) are publicly available as comma-separated value (.csv) files
on ORNL’s HydroSource website (https://hydrosource.ornl.gov/). Users have two options for accessing
information from the database: 1) directly from the database or 2) using the Hydropower Fish Passage
Webmap application (https://hydrosource.ornl.gov/tools/hydropower-fish-passage-webmap/). The
webmap application provides an environment that allows users to visualize a subset of database
information on fish passage infrastructure, including passage existence, type, and direction (Matson et al.,
2025c). Metadata for the database are available in the data dictionary, which is stored as a .csv file on
ORNL’s HydroSource website.
