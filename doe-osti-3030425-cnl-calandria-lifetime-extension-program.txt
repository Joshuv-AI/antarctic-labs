 ABSTRACT

This report provides documentation and a summary of all 18 capsules assembled for the Canadian
Nuclear Laboratories (CNL) Calandria Lifetime Extension Program. This is the first set of capsules in a
series of two irradiation campaigns to provide data relevant to weldments in the calandria of Canada
deuterium uranium (CANDU) reactors. This report provides documentation on capsule IDs, specimen
IDs, and capsule locations.

 1. INTRODUCTION

Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) and Canadian Nuclear Laboratories (CNL) worked together to
design an irradiation experiment in the High Flux Isotope Reactor (HFIR) intended to produce test
specimens for a calandria lifetime extension program for Canada deuterium uranium (CANDU) reactors.
This irradiation experiment is being conducted in the large removable beryllium (RB*) position, which
has a similar fast/thermal neutron flux as the calandria specimens are expected to experience during
service. The high flux of the HFIR will lead to accelerated neutron damage and helium generation, which
will produce steel specimens of interest for the calandria weldments. The RB* experiment was modeled
after HFIR flux trap (FT) capsule (typically contained within target rod rabbit holders [TRRHs]). The
initial irradiation fielded 18 perforated rabbit capsules, which will be followed by an additional
18 capsules, all containing steel specimens of interest to CANDU reactors.

 2. IRRADIATION

A new irradiation facility was developed to allow the irradiation of perforated capsules in the RB*
position within the HFIR. The facility, named the removable beryllium-target rod rabbit holder facility
(RB*-TRRH), is a trefoil-shaped aluminum facility designed to hold shortened target rod rabbit holders
(sTRRHs) in the RB* position. This allows the irradiation of the same style of capsule that is typically
inserted into the FT of HFIR. RB*-TRRH holds 3 sTRRHs, each of which is capable of holding 6
capsules for a total of 18 capsules in the facility. For this experiment, perforated capsules (drawing
X3E020977A542) were utilized, as this allows the specimens to be in direct contact with the coolant and
keeps the specimen temperature at just above the HFIR coolant temperature of ~ 54°C. The location of
each capsule is documented in the form RB##. The first number, as shown in Figure 2-1, indicates the
radial location around the facility, and the second number, as shown in Figure 2-2, indicates the axial
location within an sTRRH. The capsules are listed with IDs of CTS100–CTS117. The figures below give
a visual representation of capsule locations in the facility. A 40 mm spacer (ID of D##) was inserted into
the bottom of each sTRRH to make an adjustment in the axial flux profile. A design analysis of this
facility is published in ORNL/TM-2025/4103 [1]. The locations of the capsules are shown in Table 2-1,
additionally five capsules contain flux monitors, which are indicated with an asterisks.

 3. ASSEMBLY

The perforated capsules follow assembly procedure AP-RNSD-20977-022. However, the configuration of
specimens within the capsules was determined in discussions between ORNL and CNL. The included
specimens consist of round Charpy toughness (RCT), four-point bend (4PB), tensile, rods, and flux
monitors. The RCT specimens are held together with 304 base metal rings and pins, which allow coolant
to move between the RCT specimens and keep them oriented. The pins run through the RCT holes and
are clamped flat to prevent the RCT specimens from separating from the stack. The tensile and 4PB are
wrapped in Al-1100 foil to prevent interaction with and damage of the surface of the specimens as well as
to orient the specimens in the perforated housing. The flux monitors consist of a sealed vanadium capsule
with small wire segments containing Fe, Ti, Nb, Cu, and 1%Co-V to act as dosimeters in post-irradiation
examination. The masses of the individual flux monitor constituents are listed in Table 3-1, and their
capsule locations are listed in Table 4-1. These flux monitors were also contained in Al foil to prevent
their escape from the capsule. Each capsule has documented photos (below) and a fabrication request
sheet (FRS), which indicates exactly which specimen ID and location. Material certifications and critical
dimensions (such as mass) are also tracked via the FRS.

A general outline of the assembly procedure is given below:

1. Parts Fabrication
 a. Parts are fabricated and inspected against their respective drawings
 b. Materials composition is verified and tracked
 c. If possible, parts have part IDs engraved via laser engraving (for this program, CNL provided
 pre-engraved parts)
2. Cleaning
 a. Parts are cleaned in accordance with RRD-JS-31
 b. For these capsules, parts were sonicated in acetone and ethanol for 10 min each and air dried
 c. During and after cleaning, parts are only handled with clean gloves or tweezers
3. Parts Identification and Layout
 a. Clean parts are laid out on a clean tray for ease of identification
 b. An independent review ensures that parts match the IDs and parts on the FRS
4. Capsule Assembly
 a. RCT specimen stacks are assembled with a pin and ring spacers
 b. 4PB and tensile specimens are wrapped in aluminum foil
 c. Flux monitors are wrapped in aluminum foil
 d. Parts are inserted into the perforated capsule housing
 e. End caps are crimped into place
 f. End caps are tested for security
 g. Final capsule dimensions are inspected

5. Fabrication Package and Acceptance
 a. All documents supporting the irradiation are reviewed and signed (where applicable)
 i. Generally, this consists of the experiment authorization basis document (EABD), C-HFIR,
 FRS, material verifications, inspections, and assembly procedures
 b. Fabrication package is reviewed by the Research Reactors Division and Quality Assurance
 c. Approved package and experiment are delivered to the Research Reactors Division
 d. Experiment is installed

be irradiated for six cycles, at which point another set of capsules will be inserted in the sTRRHs.
