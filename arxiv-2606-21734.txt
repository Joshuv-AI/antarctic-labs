HPP: Hierarchical Programmatic Probing for Long Video Understanding by Decoupling Perception and Reasoning
Awais Rauf, Ahmed Hasssan, Greg Slabaugh, AwaisRauf.com/HPP

Abstract
Understanding long videos requires fine-grained perception and multi-step, higher-order reasoning over complex, long-range spatio-temporal dynamics. Vision-language models (VLMs) encode video frames into visual tokens and attempt to perform both perception and multi-step planning latently, within a single forward pass. This coupled formulation, however, is bottlenecked by the LLM’s limited capacity to discover and execute multi-step strategies in its latent representations. To address this bottleneck, we propose Hierarchical Programmatic Probing (HPP), a framework that decouples semantic perception from higher-order temporal reasoning by reformulating long video understanding as iterative, programmatic exploration of a hierarchically segmented video. Specifically, a coding-capable LLM plans and executes a multi-step strategy in an interactive coding environment, probing the video for information and invoking a VLM for localized perception on demand. To make probing tractable over long videos, we introduce three components: information-density-aware hierarchical segmentation, late-interaction semantic retrieval, and structured probing functions for coarse-to-fine temporal localization. We validate HPP on LongVideoBench, which requires both fine-grained perception and long-range relational reasoning, and show that decoupling the two via iterative programmatic probing yields substantial gains. Further results on EgoSchema, VideoMME, and MLVU demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach across diverse long-video benchmarks. Relevant code and supporting materials are available at: https:awaisrauf.com/hpp .

Introduction

Long video understanding has become increasingly critical as video content accounts for a growing share of global internet traffic across diverse domains.
The dominant paradigm extends Large Language Models (LLMs) to video by projecting frames into the LLM’s embedding space via a visual encoder and performing perception and reasoning jointly over the resulting token sequence within a single forward pass

This monolithic formulation of VLMs is structurally mismatched for long video understanding.
Unlike short clips, long videos require both localized perceptual grounding to extract sparse visual information from thousands of redundant frames and multi-step planning over long temporal horizons to reason about complex spatio-temporal interactions. However, recent work has shown that latent reasoning within a single forward pass is ineffective for resolving temporal dependencies and executing multi-step planning
Li et al. 2025c
;
Xu et al. 2026

Google 2026

This reliance on dense captioning and manually designed heuristics imposes rigid workflows that constrain adaptive exploration, limiting the ability to fully exploit both the sparse nature of video data and the iterative, dynamic problem-solving capabilities of modern LLMs. Meanwhile, these capabilities of LLMs have advanced rapidly recently

Building on these programmatic and multi-step planning capabilities, we ask: can long-video understanding be reformulated by decoupling semantic perception from higher-order temporal reasoning, allowing a coding-capable LLM to plan and execute structured exploration while a VLMs handles event recognition and fine-grained perceptual extraction within frames or short segments?
To this end, we cast long video understanding as iterative, programmatic probing over structured video segmentation.
We propose Hierarchical Programmatic Probing for long video understanding (HPP), a framework that enables a coding-capable LLM to perform high-level reasoning while interactively exploring long videos within a coding environment. Within this framework, the LLM is responsible for strategy formulation, planning, and relational inference through programmatic interaction, whereas a lightweight VLM is invoked for targeted semantic perception over selected frames or short segments.

Video navigation through code, however, presents unique challenges compared to text: videos consist of massive, highly redundant frame sequences with varying information density, making naive frame-level processing computationally prohibitive.
To enable efficient exploration, our framework provides the LLM with a set of structured probing functions inside the programming environment.
Specifically, we introduce (1) an information-density-aware, computationally efficient hierarchical structuring method that converts raw frames into multi-scale segments by exploiting temporal redundancy; (2) a motion-adaptive late-interaction semantic retrieval module for localizing query-relevant segments; (3) structure-aware video sampling functions for fine-grained temporal navigation; and (4) a small VLM for targeted spatio-temporal understanding of segments.

To demonstrate the effectiveness of HPP, we first evaluate our approach on LongVideoBench
Wu et al. 2024
, a large-scale long-video benchmark that stratifies videos by duration and task complexity across perception and relational reasoning. Consistent with our central hypothesis, decoupling semantic perception from programmatic reasoning enables strong long-horizon performance even with a smaller VLM.
We further validate our approach on general long video understanding benchmarks, including EgoSchema, VideoMME, MLVU
Mangalam et al. 2023
;
Fu et al. 2024
;
Zhou et al. 2024
, confirming the effectiveness of our proposed HPP across diverse long-video settings.

3
Method

Our method consist of an LLM navigating video through code execution and a set of efficient video probing functions.
To fully exploit the LLM’s reasoning and coding abilities without imposing a rigid task formulation, we initialize the LLM with minimal inputs: a system prompt for context, video metadata, and the user query. The model is given access to (i) an interactive Python environment (REPL) initalized with system variables, (ii) a set of video probing functions for structured navigation, and (iii) a small vision-language model (VLM) for targeted spatio-temporal understanding.

Given a query, the reasoning-and-navigation LLM first interprets the task and formulates a high-level strategy. It then executes this strategy by writing and executing Python code, selectively invoking probing functions to navigate the video and querying the VLM when fine-grained visual understanding is required. This iterative loop of reasoning, code execution, and video interaction enables explicit symbolic reasoning while efficiently exploring long videos, as illustrated in Figure
1
.

Interactive Hierarchical Programmatic Probing Environment

The core of our framework is an interactive probing environment that enables a coding-capable LLM to iteratively navigate long videos, retrieve query-relevant evidence, and refine its reasoning based on intermediate execution feedback. Inspired by Recursive Language Models
Zhang et al. 2025a
, we adopt a REPL-based interaction paradigm in which the LLM executes code within an isolated container and receives structured, terminal-style outputs, forming a tight reasoning–execution loop.

Unlike natural language, videos consist of dense and highly redundant frame sequences, making naive frame-level exploration computationally prohibitive. To support efficient and structured probing, we design four key modules within the REPL environment. First, we introduce a computationally efficient hierarchical structuring mechanism that leverages codec information to transform raw videos into multi-scale segments. Second, we design a semantic retrieval module that allows the LLM to localize query-relevant segments using both global embeddings and fine-grained token–frame late-interaction similarity. Third, we expose structured video sampling functions that enable fine-grained temporal navigation while constraining exploration to a controlled action space. Finally, we integrate a lightweight VLM that can be queried for targeted spatio-temporal understanding over selected frames or clips, while higher-level reasoning remains within the LLM.

1) Hierarchical Structure for Efficient Probing.

Most VLMs implicitly assume that videos consist of static information and downsample frames at uniform intervals. However, this uniform sampling is suboptimal as videos exhibit significant temporal redundancy and substantial variation in information density: some segments contain critical events, while others are largely repetitive. To enable the LLM to navigate video more efficiently in the interactive coding environment, we propose a hierarchical module that decomposes long videos into progressively coarser, perceptually and temporally coherent segments as needed.

The first part is to efficiently partition large number of video frames (
\{f_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
) into shots (
\{s_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
) with similar content and small motion.
The temporal motion can be extracted by handcrafted
Beauchemin and Barron 1995
or deep learning-based optical flow
Teed and Deng 2020
. However the expensive computation of optical flow methods makes them unsuitable for our purpose.
To perform this step efficiently, we leverage compressed representation used for storage and transmission and can be extracted at high speed
Wu et al. 2018
;
Jin et al. 2024
.
Specifically, most modern video codecs (e.g., MPEG-4, H.264 etc.) exploit temporal redundancy of successive frames by only storing few frames that are least compressible (I-frames) and reconstruct rest of the frames from motion vectors and residuals
Musmann et al. 2005
.
From an information-theoretic perspective, compression aims to minimize the expected code length of a video sequence by reducing the conditional entropy,

\sum_{t=1}^{T}H(f_{t}\mid\hat{f}_{t}),

where
\hat{f}_{t}
is motion-compensated prediction of frame
f_{t}
and stores motion vector
\mathbf{m}_{t}
and residuals
\mathbf{r}_{t}
, such that

\hat{f}_{t}\approx\mathcal{W}(f_{t-1},\mathbf{m}_{t},\mathbf{r}_{t})

where
\mathcal{W}(\cdot)
denotes motion warping.

s_{i}=\{f_{t}\mid t_{k}\leq t<t_{k+1}\},

where
f_{t_{k}}

To further reduce redundancy, we merge adjacent shots whose keyframes are visually similar (
\text{merge}_{s_{k},s_{k+1}}\text{if }\|f_{t_{k}}-f_{t_{k+1}}\|<\tau_{s}
).
For each shot
s_{k}
, we compute a shot energy using motion and residual vectors,

\gamma_{i}=\sum_{t\in s_{i}}(\|\mathbf{m}_{t}\|_{1}+\lambda\|\mathbf{r}_{t}\|_{1}),

(1)

which serves as a proxy for information change, as video codecs allocate fewer bits to more redundant content while preserving essential temporal context.
Lu et al. 2019
This proxy is used in subsequent steps for motion-adaptive frame selection.

For longer videos, shot-level segmentation alone is insufficient for effective navigation. To address this, shots can be further grouped into spatio-temporally coherent scenes and globally semantically consistent clusters using a lightweight distilled visual encoder. We defer the details to Section

2) Fine-grained , Motion-aware Semantic Search for Query-based Localization.

After dividing a video into manageable segments, the next step is to enable the LLM to retrieve query-relevant content via text. Video retrieval methods typically employ a bi-encoder architecture, in which frames are uniformly sampled from a video segment, encoded using a vision encoder, and aggregated into a single global embedding
Shu et al. 2024a
;
Xu et al. 2021
. This global representation, together with a text embedding, is then used to compute similarity and retrieve relevant results.

However, unlike general video retrieval, text-based localization within a single video requires a more fine-grained approach, as different segments may share substantial visual similarity. For example, localizing a segment corresponding to the query “bird flying over water” in a nature documentary requires distinguishing it from visually similar scenes of birds flying over a desert. Moreover, motion dynamics can vary significantly across short segments, making uniform frame sampling suboptimal. To address these challenges, we design a video localization method that adaptively samples frames based on the magnitude of motion within each shot and computes similarity using late interaction between independently encoded shot and query embeddings
Khattab and Zaharia 2020
;
Reddy et al. 2025
.

The localization is performed at the segment level (e.g., shots). Let
\{s_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
denote the set of shots for a video, where each shot
s_{i}
contains
T_{s_{i}}
frames
\{f_{i,t}\}_{t=1}^{T_{s_{i}}}
and a keyframe. We sample frames proportionally to the shot’s motion energy
\gamma_{i}
computed using Eq.
1
. The number of sampled frames for the
i
-th shot is given by

\eta_{i}=\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\sum_{m=1}^{N}\gamma_{m}}\,B_{\text{total}},

where
B_{\text{total}}
is total frame budget.
For each shot
s_{i}
,
\eta_{i}
frames
\{f_{i,k}\}_{k=1}^{\eta_{i}}
are sampled and encoded using a CLIP-like vision encoder
\phi_{v}
to obtain frame-level embeddings

\mathbf{z}_{i,t}=\phi_{v}(f_{i,t})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Similarly, a textual query consisting of
n_{t}
tokens
{q_{l}}_{l=1}^{n_{t}}
is encoded using a text encoder
\phi_{t}
to obtain token-level embeddings

\mathbf{q_{l}}=\phi_{t}(q_{l})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

To capture fine-grained token–frame interactions within each shot while maintaining the efficiency of dot-product similarity, we compute a weighted late-interaction MaxSim score:

\text{MaxSim}(q,s_{j})=\frac{1}{\eta_{i}}\sum_{k=1}^{\eta_{i}}w_{j,k}\cdot\max_{i}\left(\mathbf{q}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{j,k}\right),

where
w_{j,k}
is the weight of the
k
-th frame in shot
s_{i}
, assigning higher importance to keyframes. This per-token motion-adaptive per-frame interaction captures fine-grained and dynamic spatio-temporal information.
To further capture shot-level global information, we aggregate frame and query embeddings via mean pooling:

\mathbf{z}_{i}=\frac{1}{\eta_{i}}\sum_{t=1}^{\eta_{i}}\mathbf{z}_{j,t},

and
\mathbf{q}=\frac{1}{n_{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{t}}\mathbf{q}_{i},

and compute a shot-level similarity with the query:

\text{Sim}(q,s_{j})=\mathbf{q}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{i}.

The final similarity score combines fine-grained and global similarities:

\text{score}(q,s_{j})=\alpha_{s}\cdot\text{MaxSim}(q,s_{j})+(1-\alpha_{s})\cdot\text{Sim}(q,s_{j}),

where
\alpha_{s}
is a weighting hyperparameter.

By modeling both global shot-level semantics and fine-grained token–frame interactions, the search module captures static spatial context as well as motion-driven action cues, leading to more precise and reliable localization.
Top-
k
candidate shots are returned along with their similarity scores, which LLM can use to decide which shots to further probe.
Scenes are not embedded separately; instead, a scene representation is computed by using the embeddings of its constituent shots, reducing computation while preserving semantic structure.

3) Effective Sampling Interface and Perception.

After localizing relevant temporal regions, the next step is to enable the LLM to efficiently sample relevant segments for effective perception with the VLM. We provide a set of structured functions that support multi-scale temporal sampling. These functions include retrieving a frame at a specific timestamp, extracting frames over a time interval, hierarchy-aware frame sampling, temporal zoom-in within a selected segment, and segment clipping. More details of these functions are provided in Section
A
. These structured functions provide a predictable and well-defined interface within the REPL, enabling the LLM to systematically explore the video at the segment level with greater reliability and control.

Effectiveness of Decoupled Perception and Reasoning

To evaluate effectiveness of our central thesis, that decoupling perception from reasoning and enabling multi-step code-based planning improves long-video understanding, we first compare HPP against VLMs on LongVideoBench (LVB)
Wu et al. 2024
, which stratifies questions into perceptual (L1) and relational (L2) categories. While L1 questions primarily test perceptual grounding such as recognizing objects, actions, and events within local segments,
L2 demands relational reasoning over temporally distributed evidence, requiring both fine-grained perception and long-horizon multi-step reasoning across long videos
. LVB consist of 3600 questions spanning four duration groups from short to longer.

The model decomposes the query into sub-goals and executes its strategy in the REPL using compositional code (e.g., parallel search across paraphrased queries). HPP probes the video efficiently despite its 34k frames: structuring it into 169 scenes, retrieving and zooming into localized regions for targeted inspection, and iteratively refining its evidence before answering.

Wu et al. 2024

However, our method’s gains are most pronounced on longer videos (600s and 3600s subset) and L2 relational tasks that require multi-step planning and multimodal temporal information retrieval. For example, TAA (attribute change of an object between two subtitle-anchored moments) and T3O (identifying an object appearing before/after a subtitle) both demand cross-modal temporal anchoring followed by fine-grained perception at distant segments, precisely the kind of reasoning that monolithic VLMs struggle with, as they must integrate information across thousands of frames in a single pass. Similarly, strong improvements on E3E (temporal ordering of events) and SOS (re-identifying an object across different scenes) demonstrate that our hierarchical localization, programmatic probing and multi-step planning enable the model to first localize relevant moments and then invoke targeted perception, reducing distraction from redundant frames and enabling structured long-range understanding.

Emergent Coding-based Strategies.
A notable property of HPP is that the reasoning LLM spontaneously develops effective coding strategies to efficiently navigate the video, without being explicitly instructed to do so. For instance, Listing

Conclusion

We presented HPP, a programmatic framework for long-form video understanding that explicitly decouples semantic perception from higher-order temporal reasoning. Instead of treating videos as having uniformly distributed information, our approach reformulates video understanding as iterative code-driven probing over hierarchically structured video representations within a coding environment. In this setting, a coding-capable LLM performs planning, strategy execution, and relational reasoning, while a lightweight VLM is invoked only for targeted perceptual understanding. This design enables efficient exploration of long videos and better leverages the emerging reasoning capabilities of modern LLMs.

7
Limitations

While HPP is effective across a range of long-video benchmarks, several limitations remain.
Firstly, HPP’s effectiveness is fundamentally tied to the coding LLM that drives probing. Our scaling experiments (Table
1
) show that performance scales monotonically with the LLM’s coding ability: HPP inherits future gains from stronger LLMs but is also bounded by the LLM’s current capacity to decompose queries, formulate adaptive strategies, and recover from failed probes.

Although externalizing reasoning into code circumvents the depth limit of latent multi-step reasoning, each individual probing decision still relies on the LLM’s intrinsic planning ability within a single turn. Weaker LLMs may issue ill-formed retrieval queries, terminate prematurely without sufficient evidence, or fail to chain probes coherently. More subtly, HPP also inherits the LLM’s biases: query interpretation, the choice of paraphrases for semantic retrieval, and the prompts issued to the VLM all reflect the LLM’s pretraining priors, which can systematically privilege some types of evidence or framings over others.

Another limitation arises from HPP’s autonomous, multi-turn nature. Because the LLM plans and executes its own probing strategy, trajectories occasionally go awry in ways that are difficult to anticipate from the question alone. For instance, the model may latch onto an incorrect hypothesis early and confirm it through biased follow-up probes, repeatedly issue near-duplicate retrieval queries, or commit to a final answer before sufficient evidence has been gathered. Another failure mode observed in with some smaller LLMs is model’s inability to return final answer and instead keep looping for more and more confirmations.

A related failure mode is context filling: across multiple turns, intermediate reasoning, VLM responses and especially verbose code outputs accumulate in the context window, and we occasionally observe the LLM weighting earlier (sometimes weakly supported) evidence over more recent and more reliable findings, or losing track of the original query under accumulated context. These failures are characteristic of autonomous agentic systems and are not fully mitigated by the structured probing API.
Finally, our evaluation focuses on multiple-choice long-video QA benchmarks (LongVideoBench, EgoSchema, VideoMME, MLVU). Performance on open-ended generation, dense captioning, fine-grained temporal grounding, or interactive video tasks remains to be characterized.

Extended Method Details

In this section, we briefly expand on three key aspects of the REPL environment: hierarchical segmentation, VLM-based perception, and the various probing functions exposed by the environment.

From Shots to Scenes and Clusters.

Shot-level decomposition enables the LLM to efficiently inspect a video at a fine-grained level. However, videos often contain semantically similar scenes, including temporally distant segments that convey the same underlying narratives.
To this end, hierarchical structure module can group shots into temporally consistent scenes (
\{c_{l}\}_{l=1}^{L}
) and semantically similar clusters (
\{\mathcal{C}_{r}\}_{r=1}^{R}
), as shown in Figure
6
.

Figure 6:
Overview of our method for constructing hierarchical video units from shots to scenes, and clusters using a lightweight image encoder.

For scenes, each shot
s_{i}
with keyframe
\{f_{t_{k}}\}
is encoded using a small distilled visual encoder
\Phi(\cdot)
Ranzinger et al. 2024
to obtain spatio-temporal and semantic features

\mathbf{z}_{t_{k}},\mathbf{e}_{t_{k}}=\Phi(f_{t_{k}})\in\mathbb{R}^{D}.

Given the sequence of shot-level features
\{\mathbf{z}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, we compute pairwise cosine distances between adjacent shots:

\mathcal{D}_{s}(i,i+1)=1-\frac{\mathbf{z}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{z}_{i+1}}{\|\mathbf{z}_{i}\|\|\mathbf{z}_{i+1}\|},

and contiguous shots with high similarity (
\mathcal{D}(i,i+1)\leq\tau_{s}
) are grouped into scenes.

Next, non-contiguous and semantically similar scenes are grouped into clusters. Given global semantic embeddings for a shot calculated in previous step, we pool to form scene level embeddings

\mathbf{h}_{l}=\dfrac{1}{c_{l}}\sum_{i\in c_{l}}\mathbf{e}_{i}
and compute a pairwise similarity matrix using cosine similarity:

\mathbf{S}(i,j)=1-\frac{\mathbf{h}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{h}_{j}}{\|\mathbf{h}_{i}\|_{2}\|\mathbf{h}_{j}\|_{2}}.

The resulting
L\times L
similarity matrix is used as input to agglomerative clustering to group semantically related scenes into clusters
\{\mathcal{C}_{r}\}_{r=1}^{R}
. Each cluster is represented by the union of keyframes from its constituent scenes and forms the highest level of the hierarchy, enabling coarse-to-fine navigation of long videos.

Segment-level Perception with Small VLM.

To perform localized spatio–temporal understanding over the extracted segments, we employ a lightweight VLM (e.g., Qwen3VL-2B
Bai et al. 2023
) as a perception module. The LLM generates task-conditioned textual prompts and submits them, together with the sampled frame(s) or short video clip, to the VLM in order to extract query-relevant visual and temporal evidence.
The VLM is invoked without additional system prompts or external contextual information; it functions purely as a visual–temporal encoder and interpreter. Higher-level reasoning, cross-segment aggregation, and decision-making remain entirely within the LLM.

REPL Environment and Structured Probing Functions

HPP exposes the video to the coding LLM through a stateful Python Read–Evaluate–Print Loop (REPL), in which the LLM emits code, observes printed output, and continues reasoning across turns. Each session is initialized with a small set of pre-loaded variables that anchor the LLM in the task without consuming context budget: a
subtitles
list of {start, end, text} dictionaries (or
None
when unavailable), and video metadata exposed via
get_video_info()
(duration, resolution, frame rate, shot count). These initial variables let the LLM scope the problem before issuing any query and act as a free, always-available textual information about the video. Crucially, the REPL is persistent across turns: the LLM can accumulate evidence in arbitrary Python variables (e.g.,
evidence_by_option = {}
), revisit and refine retrieved segments, and compose primitives into multi-step strategies that span many iterations. This persistence is what makes adaptive, code-driven probing tractable. Specifically, intermediate findings do not need to be re-derived, and the LLM’s reasoning state is externalized into inspectable variables rather than carried latently inside a single forward pass.

Furthermore, HPP exposes the video to the coding LLM as system functions in REPL, organized into five families that span the cost: information trade-off and together support coarse-to-fine temporal localization.

Visualization of Reasoning Traces

Our framework answers queries by iteratively interacting with the video through a programmatic interface. Appendix D
Extended Related Work

D.1
Exploiting Temporal Redundancy and Structured Video Representations

Long videos contain substantial temporal redundancy, making direct frame-level processing inefficient. Video segmentation methods aim to partition videos into semantically or temporally coherent units such as shots or scenes, which serve as higher-level structural representations for downstream tasks
Sun et al. 2025
. These segment-level abstractions enable more efficient indexing, retrieval, and reasoning compared to raw frame sequences.

A closely related area is video compression, which explicitly exploits temporal redundancy to reduce storage and transmission costs. Early compression techniques focused primarily on frame-level encoding, whereas modern video codecs incorporate motion estimation and motion compensation to model temporal dependencies across frames
Musmann et al. 2005
. In typical compression pipelines, a subset of frames known as intra-coded frames (I-frames) are stored independently, while intermediate frames are reconstructed through motion vectors and residual information relative to reference frames. From an information-theoretic perspective, this process reduces redundancy by encoding only the incremental information necessary to reconstruct successive frames.
Although designed for compression, these representations implicitly capture important structural cues. For instance, motion vectors have been used as efficient approximations of optical flow, while I-frames often correlate with visually salient keyframes
Ai et al. 2023
. Prior work has leveraged such compression signals to accelerate video processing pipelines or approximate motion dynamics
Wu et al. 2018
;
Jin et al. 2024
.
In contrast to prior work that primarily uses compression artifacts for computational acceleration, we leverage temporal redundancy as a structural prior for hierarchical video decomposition. Our approach treats redundancy-aware segmentation as a first-stage abstraction layer that enables efficient downstream retrieval and reasoning over long videos.

D.2
Moment Localization and Retrievalin Videos

Video moment localization aims to identify temporal segments within long videos that correspond to a natural language query. Early approaches train task-specific architectures that align frame-level visual representations with textual embeddings through cross-modal objectives
Anne Hendricks et al. 2017
;
Gao et al. 2017a
;
Wu and Han 2018
;
Liu et al. 2018
;
Wang et al. 2020
;
Liu et al. 2021
. These methods typically rely on densely sampled frames and specialized supervision signals designed for benchmark datasets.
More recent research has explored generalized retrieval frameworks that reduce reliance on task-specific training and improve cross-domain transferability
Lin et al. 2023b
;
Lei et al. 2021
;
Ren et al. 2023
. These approaches aim to unify retrieval and localization tasks by learning more flexible video–text representations.

A closely related problem is text-based video retrieval, where the goal is to retrieve relevant videos given a natural language query. Early works extend contrastive image–text pretraining methods such as CLIP
Radford et al. 2021
to the video domain by aggregating frame-level representations
Zhang et al. 2024a
;
Xu et al. 2021
;
Wang et al. 2023b
. Subsequent models train large-scale video–text alignment systems specifically designed for video retrieval, achieving substantially stronger performance
Wang et al. 2024a
;
Halbe et al. 2026
;
Wang et al. 2024c
.
Existing methods for language-based video localization often rely on dense captioning pipelines or classifier-based feature extraction
Gao et al. 2017b
;
Huang et al. 2024
. While effective for narrow tasks, these approaches typically require exhaustive frame-level encoding and predefined alignment procedures.

In contrast, our framework employs pretrained video–text retrieval models as semantic search modules within a hierarchical video representation. Rather than performing exhaustive frame-level matching, retrieval operates over structured temporal segments derived from our hierarchy, enabling scalable and query-adaptive localization within long videos.

D.3
Long Video Understanding with Multimodal LLMs

Understanding long-form videos remains a major challenge for multimodal large language models (MLLMs)
Li et al. 2024a
;
Li et al. 2023a
;
Li et al. 2023c
;
Shen et al. 2024
. Unlike short clips, long videos contain complex temporal dependencies, variable information density, and massive input sizes that strain current architectures.

One common strategy involves token compression techniques that reduce redundancy in visual representations
Li et al.
;
Hu et al. 2025
;
Yang et al. 2025a
;
Li et al. 2024c
;
Jiang et al. 2025
. These methods merge similar visual tokens or apply aggressive compression ratios to fit long videos within limited context windows
Shen et al. 2024
;
Zhang et al. 2023
. However, such compression often discards fine-grained visual details, leading to degraded performance on tasks requiring precise temporal reasoning.

Another direction focuses on extending the context windows of large language models to support longer multimodal inputs
Zhang et al. 2024c
. While larger context windows enable models to process longer sequences, they do not inherently solve the challenge of efficiently locating relevant information within long videos.
Retrieval-based approaches attempt to address this limitation by selectively identifying informative segments. For example, VideoTree
Wang et al. 2025c
constructs a hierarchical tree over frame-level representations to guide search through long videos. However, building such structures requires expensive frame-level clustering and captioning. Similarly, VCA
Yang et al. 2025c
formulates video exploration as a reinforcement learning problem where an agent navigates a graph representation of the video to identify relevant segments.

Appendix E
Emergent Coding and Probing Functions-based Strategy Patterns

An important aspect of our method, HPP, is its ability to flexibly combine probing functions with a code execution environment (REPL) to analyze videos for efficient information extraction. This integration enables our method to iteratively query visual information and programmatically process intermediate results, leading to more structured and interpretable reasoning over complex video data.

In practice, we observe that HPP leverages these components in several unique and creative ways, forming distinct reasoning patterns during inference. To better understand these behaviors, we analyze the reasoning traces generated by our method and document representative samples here.

To facilitate further inspection and reproducibility, we provide an interactive visualization tool in the attached code of supplementary material that allows readers to explore the reasoning traces in detail.

E.1
Emergent Pattern 1: Hierarchical Coarse-to-Fine Localization

As shown in Listing
2
, our method is able to utilize hierarchical structure functions for coarse-to-fine localization. Rather than searching the full video at a uniform granularity, our method independently discovers a three-stage pyramid: (1) semantic search at the scene level to identify candidate temporal regions; (2) refinement at the shot level within those regions; (3) dense frame sampling within the top-scoring shots for VLM verification. This mirrors coarse-to-fine search strategies in classical computer vision
Burt and Adelson 1987
but is constructed entirely from probing function compositions in the code. The VLM calls only on the most promising temporal regions. This emergent efficiency is never instructed.

E.4
Pattern 4: Temporal Non-Maximum Suppression for Event Counting

In counting tasks, our method (HPP) implements an explicit temporal clustering algorithm: (1) collect candidate event timestamps via multi-query search; (2) sort chronologically; (3) apply a gap-threshold merge (typically 1-2 s) to collapse adjacent shots that belong to the same event occurrence into a single cluster; (4) verify each cluster with VLM; (5) count verified clusters as the answer. This constitutes a spontaneous re-implementation of temporal non-maximum suppression. The 1-second gap threshold is chosen heuristically and varies across questions (0.5–2s depending on action type), suggesting it reasons about action granularity. This is a spontaneous re-implementation of temporal non-maximum suppression
Brown et al. 2014
.

System Prompt.

In this section, we briefly discuss the system prompt used in our framework. As discussed in the main paper, the prompt is built upon the Recursive Language Model (RLM) framework
Zhang et al. 2025a
and is adapted for long-form video understanding. Specifically, we extend the original formulation with video-specific instructions and structured guidance that help the model plan exploration strategies and efficiently interact with the video through the REPL.

The prompt begins with a high-level description of the task and the overall reasoning setting. It then introduces the REPL environment, including the accessible variables and the available probing functions that the model can invoke to inspect different parts of the video. In addition, the prompt provides several strategy hints and operational guidelines that encourage the model to reason about the video structure, progressively refine its search, and avoid unnecessary visual queries. Finally, the prompt includes a set of explicit rules together with a short example illustrating how the probing functions can be combined during reasoning. These components guide the LLM to formulate structured exploration strategies and effectively navigate long videos during inference.
