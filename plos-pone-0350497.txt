Modeling spatially dependent count time series data using COM-poisson INGARCH Models

Stephanie Colwell, Prabhakar Chalise, Byron Gajewski, Isuru Panduka Ratnayake, Dinesh Pal Mudaranthakam

ABSTRACT
Count time series frequently exhibit serial dependence and either overdispersion or underdispersion relative to the Poisson equidispersion assumption. When such counts are observed across multiple locations, spatial dependence often arises, resulting in complex spatio-temporal dependency structures that are not adequately accommodated by standard integer-valued generalized autoregressive conditional heteroskedasticity (INGARCH) models. To address these challenges, this study proposes a spatial Conway–Maxwell–Poisson INGARCH (SP-COM-INGARCH) framework that jointly models temporal dependence, spatial interaction, and varying dispersion. The proposed framework incorporates the Conway-Maxwell-Poisson (COM-Poisson) distribution, which accommodates both overdispersion and underdispersion through an additional dispersion parameter, while a spatially weighted lag structure captures dependence across neighboring locations. We further introduce covariate-adjusted identity-link and softplus-link extensions. In particular, the softplus formulation allows covariates to have negative effects while ensuring positivity of the conditional COM-Poisson centering parameter. For the SP-COM-INGARCH (1,1) process sufficient conditions for stationarity and ergodicity are established, and expressions for the conditional first- and second-order moments are derived. The finite-sample performance of conditional maximum likelihood estimation is evaluated through simulations under several overdispersion and underdispersion scenarios. Parameter recovery is generally satisfactory, with accuracy tending to improve as the length of the time-series increases. We illustrate the framework using weekly influenza-like illness counts from Arkansas, Kansas, Missouri, and Oklahoma with absolute humidity as a time-varying covariate. Across six competing COM-Poisson and generalized Poisson models, the softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX model achieved the lowest joint out-of-sample RMSE, while the baseline SP-COM-INGARCH model achieved the lowest joint out-of-sample MAE, although the differences among several models were small. Overall, the proposed SP-COM-INGARCH framework offers a flexible extension for modeling spatiotemporal count data with covariate effects and flexible dispersion while providing competitive out-of-sample forecasting performance.

1. Introduction Count time series record the number of events occurring over time and arise frequently in epidemiology, medicine, economics, and environmental science [ 1 – 7 ]. Accurately modeling such data is essential because estimates of temporal dynamics, prediction uncertainty, and future counts often inform surveillance, planning, and decision-making. Poisson integer-valued generalized autoregressive conditional heteroscedasticity (INGARCH) models provide a natural observation-driven framework by allowing the current conditional intensity to depend on previous observations and conditional intensities [ 8 ]. However, the standard Poisson INGARCH model assumes conditional equidispersion, whereby the conditional variance equals the conditional mean. This assumption is often violated in practice. Clustering, unobserved heterogeneity, epidemic outbreaks, and reporting variability can produce overdispersion, whereas more regular or constrained count-generating mechanisms can produce underdispersion. Failure to account for these dispersion patterns can result in inadequate representation of variability, inaccurate uncertainty estimates, and diminished model-fitting and forecasting performance. Consequently, there is a need for INGARCH models that preserve temporal dependence while accommodating both overdispersed and underdispersed counts. A further challenge arises when count time series are observed across multiple geographic locations. Many natural phenomena, including influenza incidence, exhibit both temporal and spatial variations. Weekly influenza-like illness (ILI) counts are recorded for specific geographic units, but disease activity in neighboring regions may be associated through geographic proximity, population mobility, shared environmental conditions, and disease transmission dynamics [ 9 – 13 ]. As a result, count series observed across locations should not be assumed independent. Models that account for temporal dependence and dispersion but ignore spatial interaction may inadequately characterize regional transmission patterns and yield misleading inference, uncertainty assessments, or forecasts [ 14 ]. These considerations motivate the development of spatial count time-series models capable of jointly capturing temporal dependence, spatial interaction, flexible dispersion, and covariate effects. The Conway–Maxwell–Poisson (COM-Poisson) distribution provides a flexible basis for modeling dispersion because its additional parameter allows overdispersed, equidispersed, and underdispersed counts to be modeled within a unified family [ 15 , 16 ]. Zhu [ 17 ] embedded the COM-Poisson distribution in an INGARCH framework, providing a temporal framework for count series that depart from the Poisson equidispersion assumption. However, this formulation does not incorporate spatial interaction across locations. The COM-Poisson distribution is not universally preferable to alternative count distributions: the negative-binomial distribution is well suited to overdispersed data but does not ordinarily accommodate underdispersion, whereas the generalized Poisson distribution can represent departures from equidispersion with a different mean–variance relationship and parameter constraints [ 16 , 17 ]. Consequently, the COM-Poisson distribution offers a useful alternative when the direction or degree of dispersion is uncertain, although its normalizing constant adds computational complexity. Related developments have emerged in the spatial count modeling literature. Chen and Chen [ 18 ] proposed spatio-temporal hurdle and spatial zero-inflated generalized-Poisson models for weekly dengue counts, incorporating distance-based spatial weights and meteorological covariates. These models were designed to address spatial dependence, overdispersion, and excess zeros. This work demonstrates the value of combining spatial interaction, count dynamics, and environmental information, but its focus differs from a COM-Poisson framework intended to accommodate both overdispersion and underdispersion. Several other spatial and network extensions of INGARCH and related count autoregressive models have been proposed. Jahn et al. [ 19 ] developed approximately linear spatio-temporal INGARCH models using softplus response functions, permitting negative dependence parameters while preserving positivity. Armillotta and Fokianos [ 20 ] proposed count network autoregressive models based on specified neighborhood structures, with stability and inference results for fixed and increasing network dimensions. Maletz et al. [ 21 ] developed parsimonious spatio-temporal count autoregressive models that incorporate neighborhood effects and covariates while controlling the number of parameters. Guo and Zhu [ 22 , 23 ] introduced negative-binomial network autoregressive models for overdispersed multivariate counts, including softplus and community-based formulations. Chen et al. [ 24 ] developed an empirical Bayes spatial hurdle INGARCH model for spatial count time series with excess zeros. Collectively, these approaches address spatial or network dependence, nonlinear effects, overdispersion, excess zeros, and covariate adjustment. Comparatively limited attention has been given to integrating the COM-Poisson distribution into a spatial INGARCH framework that jointly accommodates temporal dependence, distance-based spatial interaction, covariates, and both overdispersion and underdispersion. To address this gap, we propose a spatial COM-Poisson INGARCH (SP-COM-INGARCH) framework that integrates INGARCH temporal dynamics, distance-based spatially weighted lags, and flexible dispersion modeling. We further develop SP-COM-INGARCHX and softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX extensions to incorporate spatial-unit-specific and time-varying covariates. The identity-link formulation provides an approximately linear interpretation when the conditional COM-Poisson centering parameter remains positive, whereas the softplus formulation permits negative covariate effects while ensuring positivity [ 19 ]. Corresponding spatial generalized-Poisson models are fitted under the same temporal, spatial, and covariate structures to provide a benchmark. Thus, the principal contribution of this study is not the spatial structure or the COM-Poisson distribution in isolation, but their integration within a unified INGARCH framework for spatially dependent count time series. The study makes four main contributions. First, it formulates the SP-COM-INGARCH, SP-COM-INGARCHX, and softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX models for spatially dependent count time series. Second, for the SP-COM-INGARCH (1,1) process, it establishes sufficient conditions for stationarity and ergodicity and derives expressions for the conditional first- and second-order moments. Third, finite-sample performance of conditional maximum likelihood estimation is evaluated through simulation studies under overdispersion and underdispersion scenarios, including scenarios with negative covariate effects. Finally, the proposed models and their generalized Poisson counterparts are applied to weekly ILI counts from Arkansas, Kansas, Missouri, and Oklahoma. Absolute humidity is incorporated as a time-varying exogenous covariate because low absolute humidity has been linked to influenza virus survival, transmission, and seasonal epidemic onset [ 25 , 26 ]. Model performance is evaluated using in-sample goodness-of-fit and prediction measures and rolling-origin one-step-ahead forecasts over a 52-week out-of-sample period. 2. Methods 2.1. Proposed models 2.1.1. Conway-maxwell-poisson distribution. This section provides a brief overview of the Conway–Maxwell–Poisson (COM-Poisson) distribution, which is used later in the proposed modeling framework. The COM-Poisson distribution was originally proposed by Conway and Maxwell [ 15 ] as a model for a queuing system with state-dependent service rates. Shmueli et al. [ 16 ] later studied its probabilistic and statistical properties and demonstrated its usefulness for modeling count data with both overdispersion and underdispersion. Sellers et al. [ 27 ] reviewed the methods and applications of COM-Poisson models. Sellers and Shmueli [ 28 ] compared COM-Poisson regression with Poisson, negative-binomial, and restricted generalized-Poisson regression models, illustrating its flexibility for count regression. More recently, Hawa et al. [ 29 ] proposed a bootstrap Liu-type estimator for COM-Poisson regression to address multicollinearity among predictors. In this study, the COM-Poisson distribution allows the proposed spatial count time-series framework to accommodate both overdispersed and underdispersed scenarios. Therefore, the COM-Poisson distribution is considered as a flexible modeling choice rather than as a universally superior count distribution. The boundary case ν = 0 is permitted only when 0 < λ < 1 , in which case the COM-Poisson distribution reduces to a geometric distribution. Here, λ is a rate parameter and ν is the dispersion parameter. The distribution represents overdispersion when ν < 1 , equidispersion when ν = 1 , and underdispersion when ν > 1 . Thus, the COM-Poisson distribution can accommodate overdispersed, equidispersed, and underdispersed count data [ 16 ]. The COM-Poisson distribution includes several standard count distributions as special or limiting cases. When ν = 1 , the COM-Poisson distribution reduces to the Poisson distribution. When ν = 0 and 0 < λ < 1 , it reduces to the geometric distribution. For fixed λ , as ν → ∞ , the COM-Poisson distribution converges to the Bernoulli distribution with probability parameter λ / ( 1 + λ ) [ 16 ]. The COM-Poisson distribution is also a two-parameter exponential-family distribution [ 16 ]. For Y ~ COM-Poisson ( λ , ν ), higher-order moments can be expressed recursively. An interpretative limitation of the original COM-Poisson formulation is that λ is not generally equal to the mean. Although λ corresponds to the mean in the special case ν = 1 , it can differ substantially from the mean when ν ≠ 1 . This can make interpretation difficult, especially in regression and time series modeling. Under this parameterization, 0 < ν < 1 corresponding to overdispersion, ν = 1 to equidispersion, and ν > 1 to underdispersion [ 31 ]. In the remainder of this paper, the reparametrized COM-Poisson distribution in Equation (6) is used. Accordingly, μ is interpreted as the conditional COM-Poisson intensity parameter or centering parameter rather than the exact conditional mean when ν ≠ 1 [ 31 ]. This parameterization is used in the proposed INGARCH-type model because it improves interpretability while retaining the ability to model overdispersion and underdispersion. In the next section, the reparametrized COM-Poisson distribution is embedded within the INGARCH framework to accommodate temporal dependence while retaining dispersion flexibility. Building on this temporal structure, the model is then extended to incorporate spatial dependence, allowing neighboring locations to interact through a spatially weighted lag structure. The resulting spatial Conway–Maxwell–Poisson INGARCH (SP-COM-INGARCH) formulation provides a unified framework for modeling spatially dependent count time series. 2.1.2 Spatial COM-Poisson INGARCH Models. The integer-valued generalized autoregressive conditional heteroscedasticity (INGARCH) framework provides the temporal foundation of the proposed model. It is an observation-driven approach in which a time-varying conditional mean or intensity depends on past observations and past conditional means or intensities [ 8 ]. A general INGARCH model of orders p and q (INGARCH ( p , q ) ) can be written as: η t = ω + ∑ r = 1 p α r Y t − r + ∑ m = 1 q β m η t − m , where, ω > 0 ; α r ≥ 0 , r = 1 , … , p , and β m ≥ 0 , m = 1 , … , q . In the conventional mean-parameterized formulation η t = E ( Y t | F t − 1 ) . The parameter ω represents the baseline level of the conditional mean or intensity η t . The coefficients α r measure the effects of the previous observed counts, whereas the coefficients β m capture dependence through the previous conditional means or intensities. For the standard mean-parameterized INGARCH ( p , q ) process, a commonly used sufficient condition for stationarity is ∑ r = 1 p α r + ∑ m = 1 q β m < 1 . Based on the COM-Poisson INGARCH model of Zhu [17] the proposed spatial extension is defined as follows. Consider the observed count Y i , t observed at a spatial unit i and time t , where i = 1 , … , K and t = 1 , … , N . Here K is the number of spatial units and N is the number of temporal observations. Let F t denote the σ − field generated by the observed history from all spatial units up to time t , that is, F t = σ { Y i , r : i = 1 , … , K , r ≤ t } . Conditional on the past information F t − 1 , the observations Y i , k , … , Y K , t are assumed to be conditionally independent, with spatial dependence introduced through their conditional centering parameters. Specifically, Y i , t | F t − 1 ~ C O M − P o i s s o n ( μ i , t , ν i ) . Here, μ i , t > 0 denotes the conditional COM-Poisson centering parameter for spatial unit i at time t , and ν i > 0 denotes the location-specific dispersion parameter. The parameter μ i , t controls the center or level of the conditional distribution, but it is not generally equal to the conditional mean when ν i ≠ 1 . The conditional mean is determined jointly by μ i , t and ν i ,is given by: E ( Y i , t | F t − 1 ) = m ν i ( μ i , t ) , where the exact conditional mean function is m ν i ( μ i , t ) = ∑ r = 0 ∞ r ( μ i , t r r ! ) ν i S ( μ i , t , ν i ) and S ( μ i , t ν i ) = ∑ r = 0 ∞ ( μ i , t r r ! ) ν i . In its general form, the SP-COM-INGARCH ( p , q ) model specifies the conditional centering parameter as μ i , t = ω i + ∑ r = 1 p α i , r ∑ j = 1 K γ j i Y j , t − r + ∑ m = 1 q β i , m μ i , t − m , (10) where ω i > 0 , α i , r ≥ 0 , and β i , m ≥ 0 , and γ j i > 0 denotes the spatial weight from unit j to unit i as defined below. The order p determines the number of lagged spatial-count terms included in the model, whereas q determines the number of lagged conditional centering parameters. Equation (10) demonstrates that the proposed framework is not restricted to first-order dependence. However, an SP-COM-INGARCH ( p , q ) model contains k p + k q location specific dynamic coefficients, in addition to its spatial and distributional parameters. Increasing p or q may therefore complicate estimation and weaken parameter identifiability. Therefore, the present study focuses on the parsimonious (1,1) specification. Setting p = q = 1 , the SP-COM- INGARCH (1,1) model is μ i , t = ω i + α i ∑ j = 1 K γ j i Y j , t − 1 + β i μ i , t − 1 , (11) where α i = α i , 1 ≥ 0 , and β i = β i , 1 ≥ 0 . The parameter ω i represents the baseline level of the conditional centering parameter for spatial unit i . Because the spatial weights are normalized, α i controls the overall magnitude of the lagged-count effect. The component with j = i represents feedback from the unit’s own lagged count, whereas the component with j ≠ i represent lagged cross-location effects. The parameter β i measures persistence through the previous conditional centering parameter. The equation (11) combined temporal persistence and spatial interaction within a single dynamic framework. Rather than using a binary neighborhood matrix based only on shared spatial boundaries, this study follows the continuous distance-based weighting approach of Chen and Chen [ 18 ]. Let s i = ( s i , 1 , s i , 2 ) T , denote the geographical coordinates of the i t h spatial unit, where i = 1 , … , K . The coordinates must be represented on a common spatial scale so that the distances are comparable. The spatial weight from unit j to unit i is γ j i = d j i ∑ l = 1 K d l i , where, d j i = exp [ − ( ‖ s j − s i ‖ κ ) τ ] , (12) and ‖ s j − s i ‖ = ( s j , 1 − s i , 1 ) 2 + ( s j , 2 − s i , 2 ) 2 . For each receiving spatial unit i , the weights satisfy ∑ j = 1 K γ j i = 1 . Here, 0 < τ < 2 and κ > 0 are spatial decay parameters, and ‖ s j − s i ‖ denotes the Euclidean distance between spatial units i and j . The parameter κ controls the spatial range: smaller values concentrate the normalized weights on nearby units, whereas larger values assign relatively more weight to distant units. The parameter τ controls the shape of the distance-decay curve. Consequently, spatial influence decreases smoothly with distance rather than being restricted to binary adjacency relationships [ 18 ]. For clarity, the SP-COM-INGARCH model without exogenous covariates is referred to throughout this manuscript as the baseline SP-COM-INGARCH model, and the two terms are used interchangeably. This model serves as the reference specification for comparison with the identity-link and softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX extensions discussed in section 2.1.3. 2.1.3 SP-COM-INGARCHX model with covariates and softplus extension. The baseline SP-COM-INGARCH (1,1) model can be extended to incorporate spatial-unit-specific and time-varying covariates. Let X i , t ϵ R d denote the vector of d covariates observed for spatial unit i at time t , and define X t = ( X 1 , t T , … , X K , t T ) T . For the covariate models, let H t denote the augmented information set containing the count history through time t − 1 and the covariates observed through time t . Specifically, H t is defined as H t = σ ( Y i , s : i = 1 , … , K , s ≤ t − 1 ; X s : s ≤ t ) . The resulting model, denoted the SP-COM-INGARCHX (1 1) model, is defined as Y i , t | H t ~ COM-Poisson ( μ i , t , ν i ) , with μ i , t = ω i + α i ∑ j = 1 K γ j i Y j , t − 1 + β i μ i , t − 1 + ϕ i T X i , t . Here, X i , t denotes the vector of covariates observed for spatial unit i at time t , and ϕ i ϵ R d denotes the corresponding vector of location-specific covariate coefficients. The covariate vector X i , t is assumed to be observed and available when specifying the conditional distribution of Y i , t . If X i , t is not available at time t, it must be forecast or otherwise estimated using only the information available at that time, alternatively a lagged covariate vector may be used. The constraints on ω i , α i , and β i remain as specified in Section 2.1.2. This extension allows the conditional COM-Poisson centering parameter μ i , t to incorporate external spatial or temporal information in addition to spatially weighted lagged counts and the previous conditional centering parameter. More generally, the higher-order SP-COM-INGARCHX ( p , q ) model is obtained by adding covariate component to the general SP-COM-INGARCH ( p , q ) specification in Equation (10) : μ i , t = ω i + ∑ r = 1 p α i , r ∑ j = 1 K γ j i Y j , t − r + ∑ m = 1 q β i , m μ i , t − m + ϕ i T X i , t . (14) In the identity-link SP-COM-INGARCHX model in Equation (13), the conditional COM-Poisson centering parameter is specified directly by the linear predictor. Although this formulation is simple and interpretable, it may not guarantee positivity when covariates are included. Specifically, a negative covariate coefficient, or a sufficiently large covariate value associated with a negative coefficient, can produce a μ i , t ≤ 0 . This is problematic because the COM-Poisson distribution requires μ i , t > 0 . Therefore, the identity-link formulation is valid only when the entire linear predictor is constrained to remain strictly positive at every time point. A natural way to address this limitation is to replace the identity response function with a softplus response function. Softplus INGARCH models were introduced in the univariate INGARCH framework to preserve positivity while allowing approximately linear dependence structures [ 32 ]. Ratnayake and Samaranayake [ 33 ] extended the softplus INGARCH framework to incorporate exogenous variables in a zero-inflated INGARCHX model. The softplus formulation has also been applied to spatio-temporal INGARCH models, where it preserves positivity while retaining approximately linear dynamics when the linear predictor is sufficiently positive [ 19 ]. Motivated by these developments, a softplus version of the SP-COM-INGARCHX model is considered when negative covariate effects are permitted. For the general softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX ( p , q ) model, let ψ i , t denote the unconstrained linear predictor. The softplus model is defined by ψ i , t = ω i + ∑ r = 1 p α i , r ∑ j = 1 K γ j i Y j , t − r + ∑ m = 1 q β i , m μ i , t − m + ϕ i T X i , t (15) The conditional COM-Poisson centering parameter is then defined using the softplus response function: μ i , t = s o f t p l u s c ( ψ i , t ) = c l o g ( 1 + exp ( ψ i , t c ) ) , c > 0. Setting p = q = 1 gives the softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX model evaluated in this study. The parameter c controls the scale and smoothness of the softplus response function. In the present formulation, c is treated as an additional positive scale parameter and is estimated jointly with the other model parameters. Because s o f t p l u s c ( z ) = c l o g ( 1 + exp ( z c ) ) > 0 , ∀ z ∈ R , the softplus formulation guarantees that μ i , t > 0 , for every spatial unit i and time point t , even when some covariate coefficients are negative. Thus, the conditional COM-Poisson distribution remains well-defined. Moreover, because s o f t p l u s c ( z ) ≈ z when z c is sufficiently large and positive, the formulation retains an approximately linear interpretation while ensuring positivity [ 19 , 32 ]. In this study, the identity-link SP-COM-INGARCHX model is used under the restriction that its linear predictor remains strictly positive. The softplus formulation is used as a positivity-preserving alternative when negative covariate effects are included or when positivity of the identity-link predictor cannot otherwise be guaranteed. 2.1.4 Stationarity and Ergodicity. Data sources Weekly counts of outpatient visits for influenza-like illness (ILI) were obtained for four U.S. states: Arkansas, Kansas, Missouri, and Oklahoma from the U.S. Outpatient Influenza-like Illness Surveillance Network (ILINet), available through the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) FluView system [ 35 ]. The weekly ILI count reported in the ILITOTAL field was used as the outcome, and all observations were indexed by Morbidity and Mortality Weekly Report (MMWR) epidemiological year and week. The analytic series contained 751 consecutive epidemiological weeks, from MMWR week 40 of 2010 through week 8 of 2025, corresponding approximately to October 2010 through February 2025. For model development, the first 699 weeks (MMWR week 40 of 2010 through MMWR week 8 of 2024), were used as the in-sample estimation period. The subsequent 52 weeks (MMWR week 9 of 2024 through MMWR week 8 of 2025), were reserved for out-of-sample forecasting. The latitude and longitude coordinates of the 2020 state mean centers of population, obtained from the U.S. Census Bureau [ 36 ], were used only to calculate the pairwise interstate distances required to construct the spatial weight matrix. A point-based estimate of absolute humidity was used as the state-specific exogenous covariate. Daily mean 2-m air temperature and relative humidity were obtained from the NASA POWER database for one prespecified location in each of the four states [ 37 ]. Daily absolute humidity (g/m 3 ) was calculated using Bolton’s saturation vapor pressure approximation [ 38 ]. Each daily value was assigned to its corresponding MMWR epidemiological week, and the daily values were averaged within each week to obtain the weekly absolute humidity covariate. All sources were publicly available and contained only aggregated surveillance or environmental data. The authors had no access to information that could identify individual participants during or after data collection. 3. Simulation study A Monte Carlo simulation study was conducted to evaluate the finite-sample performance of the conditional maximum likelihood estimators for the proposed spatial COM-Poisson models. Three model families were examined: the SP-COM-INGARCH (1,1) model, the identity-link SP-COM-INGARCHX (1,1) model with positive covariate effects, and the softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX (1,1) model with negative covariate effects. Both overdispersed and underdispersed data-generating processes were considered for each model family. All simulation settings involved K = 4 spatial units arranged on a fixed 2 × 2 grid with coordinates ( 1 , 1 ) , ( 1 , 2 ) , ( 2 , 1 ) , and ( 2 , 2 ) . Euclidean distances between the spatial units were used to construct the spatial weights. The simulation scenarios covered different baseline count levels, degrees of temporal persistence, spatial dependence, covariate effects, and dispersion levels. For the identity-link SP-COM-INGARCHX (1,1) model, the parameter and covariate values were selected so that the COM-Poisson centering parameter ( μ i , t ) remained positive. In contrast, the softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX (1,1) model was evaluated under negative covariate effects to assess its ability to accommodate such effects while preserving positivity of the μ i , t . For each simulation setting and each time series length, R = 1 0 0 0 independent datasets were generated for time-series lengths N = 5 0 0 and N = 1 0 0 0 . The process was initialized by setting μ i , 1 = ω i , i = 1 , … , K , after which the initial counts were generated from the corresponding COM-Poisson distributions. The values of μ i , t and the counts were then generated recursively until a complete time series of length N was obtained. Conditional on the past and the covariates, where applicable, counts were generated independently across spatial units from the reparametrized COM-Poisson distribution using the COMPoissonReg package in R [ 39 ]. At each time point, including initialization, the model recursion was first used to obtain the conditional COM-Poisson centering parameter μ i , t . Because the rcmp [ 39 ] function uses the conventional rate parameter, the following conversion was applied: λ i , t = μ i , t ν i . All simulations were performed in R using the COMPoissonReg [ 39 ] and nloptr [ 40 ] packages. For each generated dataset, the conditional log-likelihood was optimized using the derivative-free Constrained Optimization BY Linear Approximations (COBYLA) algorithm implemented in nloptr [ 40 , 41 ]. The maximum number of objective-function evaluations was set to 5000, and the relative parameter and objective-function tolerances were both set to 1 0 − 6 . Estimation was performed subject to the admissible parameter bounds and persistence restrictions specified in the simulation programs. For each replication, the complete and finite parameter estimates returned by the optimization procedure were recorded. Let θ h denote the true value of the h th parameter and let θ ^ h ( r ) denote its estimate from the r th replication, where r = 1 , … , R . The mean estimate of the h th parameter was calculated as θ ^ ― h = 1 R ∑ r = 1 R θ ^ h ( r ) , R = 1 0 0 0 . The empirical bias was calculated as B i a s ( θ ^ h ) = θ ^ ― h − θ h , and mean absolute error (MAE) were calculated as M A E ( θ ^ h ) = 1 R ∑ r = 1 R | θ ^ h ( r ) − θ h | . In Scenario O1 ( S2 Appendix Table S1), the temporal parameters α i and β i were estimated with relatively small biases, whereas the baseline, spatial, dispersion, and covariate-effect parameters generally exhibited positive biases. Increasing the time-series length from N = 5 0 0 to N = 1 0 0 0 generally reduced the MAEs, although the empirical biases did not decrease uniformly across all parameters. Scenario O2 ( S2 Appendix Table S2) demonstrated strong overall estimation performance, with most mean estimates close to their true values, relatively small biases in the temporal, spatial, and dispersion parameters, and consistent reductions in MAE as the time-series length increased. In Scenario O3 ( S2 Appendix Table S3), the temporal parameters α i and β i , spatial parameters τ and κ , and dispersion parameters νᵢ were estimated with relatively small errors. The larger absolute MAEs observed for the baseline parameters ω i partly reflect the substantially larger true values considered in this scenario. However, the covariate-effect parameters ϕ i remained positively biased at both time-series lengths. Moreover, increasing the time-series length did not consistently reduce the MAEs of the baseline and covariate-effect parameters. Across the three overdispersion scenarios, the temporal parameters were generally estimated accurately under the different parameter configurations. Increasing the time-series length produced the clearest and most consistent improvements in Scenarios O1 and O2, particularly through reductions in MAE. The improvement was less uniform in Scenario O3, which involved substantially larger baseline and covariate-effect parameters. Overall, the conditional maximum likelihood estimator demonstrated satisfactory finite-sample performance for the identity-link SP-COM-INGARCHX (1 1) model under overdispersion, although estimation accuracy varied across parameters and scenarios. 3.2.2 SP-COM-INGARCHX (1 1) underdispersion scenarios. Three underdispersion scenarios were examined, with dispersion parameters ν i > 1 , ranging from 1.20 to 1.80. Scenario U1 combined small baseline and positive covariate-effect parameters with low dynamic persistence and spatial parameters τ = 0 . 7 5 and κ = 0 . 3 0 . Scenario U2 used moderate baseline and positive covariate-effect parameters, moderate dynamic persistence, and spatial parameters τ = 1 . 5 0 and κ = 0 . 5 0 . Scenario U3 is considered substantially larger baseline and positive covariate-effect parameters, moderate dynamic persistence, and spatial parameters τ = 1 . 2 0 and κ = 0 . 8 0 . S2 Appendix (Tables S4–S6) presents the true parameter values, mean estimates, empirical biases, and mean absolute errors (MAEs) for Scenarios U1–U3 at time-series lengths N = 5 0 0 and N = 1 0 0 0 . The underdispersion results revealed three distinct estimation patterns. In Scenario U1 ( S2 Appendix Table S4), the baseline, temporal, and covariate-effect estimates closely tracked their true values, but τ and the dispersion parameters ν i were systematically overestimated. Nevertheless, extending the series from N = 5 0 0 to N = 1 0 0 0 reduced the MAE of every parameter, indicating improved precision despite the remaining positive biases. Scenario U2 ( S2 Appendix Table S5) provided the most balanced results: the temporal, spatial, and dispersion parameters showed little systematic bias, while every parameter exhibited a lower MAE at N = 1 0 0 0 . The covariate-effect estimates were somewhat less precise than the dynamic and spatial estimates but still benefited from the longer series. Scenario U3 ( S2 Appendix Table S6) presented a different pattern. The temporal, spatial, and dispersion components remained accurately estimated, but the substantially larger baseline and covariate-effect values were associated with larger absolute errors. In particular, the covariate effects were consistently overestimated, and the additional observations produced parameter-specific rather than uniform improvements. Taken together, the results indicate that the temporal dynamics were estimated reliably across all three underdispersion settings. The gain from increasing the time-series length was especially evident in U1 and U2, whereas the large-scale parameter configuration in U3 created greater difficulty in estimating the baseline and covariate effects. Thus, the conditional maximum likelihood estimator showed satisfactory finite-sample performance for the identity-link SP-COM-INGARCHX (1,1) model under underdispersion, with its strongest performance observed for the temporal parameters and for the small-to-moderate parameter configurations. 3.3. Real data example To evaluate their empirical performance, the proposed SP-COM-INGARCH, SP-COM-INGARCHX, and softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX models were applied to weekly influenza-like illness (ILI) counts from Arkansas (AR), Kansas (KS), Missouri (MO), and Oklahoma (OK). The corresponding 2020 mean centers of population, reported as latitude and longitude, were AR (35.199251, −92.713212), KS (38.480984, −96.409286), MO (38.432921, −92.234929), and OK (35.606866, −96.854171). Weekly absolute humidity, obtained as described in Section 2.2, was included as an exogenous covariate in the INGARCHX models. Absolute humidity was selected because previous studies have shown that low absolute humidity enhances influenza virus survival and transmission and is associated with the seasonal onset of influenza epidemics [ 25 , 26 ]. For comparison, the corresponding spatial generalized Poisson models were fitted to the same data: SP-COM-INGARCH was compared with SP-GP-INGARCH, SP-COM-INGARCHX with SP-GP-INGARCHX, and softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX with softplus SP-GP-INGARCHX. The initial in-sample period comprised 699 weeks, from MMWR week 40 of 2010 through MMWR week 8 of 2024. In this study, the COVID-19 period was operationally defined as MMWR week 11 of 2020 through MMWR week 19 of 2023. These boundaries correspond to the weeks containing the WHO’s characterization of COVID-19 as a pandemic on March 11, 2020, and the expiration of the U.S. COVID-19 public health emergency on May 11, 2023, respectively [ 42 , 43 ]. In-sample model fit was evaluated using the log-likelihood (LLF), Akaike information criterion (AIC), and Bayesian information criterion (BIC), with higher LLF and lower AIC and BIC values indicating better fit. In-sample prediction accuracy was evaluated using the root mean squared error (RMSE) and mean absolute error (MAE), calculated for both the overall in-sample period and the COVID-19 period. Prediction-interval calibration was assessed using the percentage of observed counts falling outside the 95% prediction intervals. The subsequent 52 weeks, from MMWR week 9 of 2024 through MMWR week 8 of 2025, were reserved for rolling one-step-ahead out-of-sample forecasting. This forecasting window allowed predictive performance to be evaluated over a full annual seasonal cycle, including both influenza-season and off-season activity. Out-of-sample forecast performance was evaluated using RMSE, MAE, and the percentage of observed counts falling outside the 95% prediction intervals. Discussion The primary motivation for this study was to develop a unified spatiotemporal framework for count data that simultaneously accommodates temporal dependence, spatial association, and deviations from the Poisson equidispersion assumption. Such features commonly arise in infectious-disease surveillance, where counts observed in one region may depend on both its previous activity and disease activity in neighboring regions. To address these challenges, we proposed a spatial Conway-Maxwell-Poisson INGARCH (SP-COM-INGARCH) framework in which the conditional distribution follows a COM-Poisson distribution capable of modeling both overdispersion and underdispersion. Three model formulations were considered: the baseline SP-COM-INGARCH model, the identity-link SP-COM-INGARCHX model, and the softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX model. The softplus extension was specifically designed to accommodate covariate effects of either sign while ensuring positivity of the conditional centering parameter. Model parameters were estimated using conditional maximum likelihood estimation and simulation studies demonstrated generally satisfactory finite-sample performance. Estimation accuracy improved as the time-series length increased, particularly for the INGARCH parameters that govern temporal dependence. Some spatial and dispersion parameters were estimated less precisely under low-count or strongly dispersed conditions. In addition, very large negative covariate effects tended to be estimated closer to zero, although their signs were recovered correctly. These findings suggest that estimates of spatial, dispersion parameters, and large-magnitude covariate-effect should be interpreted cautiously when the available time series is short or contains limited information regarding these components. The empirical analysis compared the proposed COM-Poisson formulations with analogous spatial generalized-Poisson INGARCH models. Within the COM-Poisson family, incorporating absolute humidity through the identity-link formulation produced only limited improvement over the baseline model. The softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX model, in contrast, achieved the lowest AIC and BIC, the lowest overall in-sample MAE, and prediction-interval coverage close to the nominal level. However, the baseline SP-COM-INGARCH model retained the lowest overall and COVID-period RMSE and the lowest COVID-period MAE. The estimated humidity effects were negative across all four states, demonstrating the practical value of a formulation that accommodates negative covariate effects while preserving positivity. Within the generalized-Poisson family, the baseline SP-GP-INGARCH model was favored by AIC and BIC, while its covariate extensions produced only modest improvements in predictive performance. The softplus formulation therefore provided a clearer empirical benefit within the COM-Poisson family than within the generalized-Poisson family. All fitted models experienced noticeable difficulty capturing influenza patterns during the COVID-19 period. This likely reflects abrupt changes in disease activity, health-care utilization, population behavior, and public-health interventions that were not fully represented by the spatial, temporal, and humidity components of the fitted models. In the 52-week rolling-origin out-of-sample evaluation, the softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX model achieved the lowest joint RMSE, whereas the baseline SP-COM-INGARCH model achieved the lowest joint MAE. The differences among several models were relatively small and should therefore not be overstated. Nevertheless, when considered together with its likelihood-based fit and ability to incorporate negative humidity effects, the results support the softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX model as the preferred predictive specification. The baseline SP-GP-INGARCH model remains a competitive and more parsimonious alternative. This comparison highlights an important trade-off: the COM-Poisson framework provides greater flexibility for modeling dispersion and covariate effects, whereas the generalized-Poisson framework is computationally simpler. Overall, the proposed SP-COM-INGARCH framework extends the spatial count time-series literature by integrating temporal dependence, distance-based spatial interaction, flexible dispersion modeling, and covariate effects within a single INGARCH framework. Theoretical results, simulation evidence, and the influenza application collectively demonstrate its usefulness for analyzing and forecasting spatially correlated count processes, particularly when both overdispersion and underdispersion are plausible features of the data. 6. Limitations Despite the strengths of the proposed framework, several limitations should be acknowledged. The empirical application was limited to four neighboring states and used a point-based estimate of absolute humidity from one prespecified location within each state. Consequently, these measurements may not fully represent environmental conditions across each state. Although absolute humidity varied across time and space, its effect was assumed to remain constant over the study period. Other factors, including vaccination, mobility, health-care utilization, public-health interventions, and additional meteorological variables, may account for some of the remaining variation. Future studies could evaluate the framework in larger spatial systems and consider additional covariates, alternative lag specifications, different spatial-weight structures, and time-varying covariate or intervention effects. Model estimation relies on numerical optimization and may therefore be sensitive to the choice of initial values, particularly because of the model’s dimensionality and dependence structure. Careful convergence diagnostics and, when necessary, multiple starting values are important for obtaining reliable estimates. The present study also focused on the parsimonious first-order specification. Increasing the autoregressive or moving-average orders would introduce additional spatial-count and conditional-parameter lag coefficients, increasing the computational burden and the potential for weak identifiability and convergence difficulties. The empirical performance of such higher-order specifications was not evaluated and remains an area for future research. Finally, the dispersion parameter was allowed to vary across states but was assumed to remain constant over time. Because influenza exhibits strong seasonality, its dispersion may change across seasons or epidemic periods, potentially shifting between overdispersion and underdispersion. A time-invariant dispersion parameter may not fully capture these changes. Future extensions could therefore incorporate time-varying dispersion and examine comparable softplus covariate formulations for other count distributions. 7. Conclusions This study proposed a flexible framework for modeling spatiotemporal count data by combining a conditional Conway–Maxwell–Poisson distribution with a spatial INGARCH structure. Three formulations were developed, and their parameters were estimated using conditional maximum likelihood. The simulation study demonstrated generally satisfactory finite-sample estimation accuracy, with performance typically improving as the time series length increased, although some spatial and dispersion parameters required more cautious interpretation under challenging conditions. The empirical analysis of weekly influenza-like illnesses counts across four neighboring states further demonstrated the practical applicability of the proposed framework. In particular, the softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX model accommodated negative covariate effects while preserving positivity and achieved the lowest joint out-of-sample RMSE. Although differences in forecasting performance among several models were small, the baseline SP-COM-INGARCH model achieved the lowest joint MAE, and the generalized Poisson models remained competitive as computationally simpler alternatives. Overall, the softplus SP-COM-INGARCHX model provided the most favorable balance of model fit, forecasting performance, and covariate flexibility. These findings support the proposed framework as a useful and interpretable approach for jointly capturing temporal dependence, spatial association, covariate effects, and flexible dispersion in count time series data.
