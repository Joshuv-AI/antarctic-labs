Goal: Enterprise and project team members (Project Partners) developed and
implemented a protocol to ensure that residents in multifamily buildings who can most
benefit from electrification and weatherization were prioritized to receive those services.

Expected outcomes for this project included:

 • Reduced greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions in 150-200 dwelling units of multifamily
 affordable housing through the implementation of comprehensive weatherization
 and electrification services. By the end of the period of performance, our team
 achieved this outcome, reducing GHG emissions in 5 buildings and 154
 dwelling units. Two of the five buildings, with 77 dwelling units, completed
 weatherization upgrades only during the grant period; construction for
 domestic hot water electrification is expected to be complete by Q4 2026, post
 grant period.
 • Greater adoption of this comprehensive retrofit approach throughout the
 multifamily sector, by making available our replicable protocol for prioritizing
 multifamily buildings to apply for and receive deep energy retrofits, electrification
 services, and solar services. By the end of the period of performance, our team
 achieved this outcome by producing the prioritization protocol and delivering
 trainings that we expect will lead to greater adoption.
 • Greater adoption of this comprehensive retrofit approach throughout the
 multifamily sector, by making available our electrification audit protocol which
 pairs deep energy retrofit opportunities, electrification services, solar feasibility,

Abstract/executive summary:
Enterprise Community Partners (Enterprise) joined forces with the Association for Energy
Affordability (AEA), with support from the New York City Department of Housing
Preservation and Development (HPD) and New York State Homes and Community
Renewal (HCR). Through this 3-year pilot project, the project partners collaborated to
develop tools and processes that efficiently utilize New York State Weatherization
Assistance Program (WAP) funding to both improve energy efficiency through
weatherization and to convert to clean heating systems through electrification. We
developed and piloted this approach on five multifamily dwelling buildings, totaling 154
units in New York City, using the energy audit protocol that was developed as part of this
project. Our goal was to develop a basis of knowledge for pursuing similar retrofits
nationwide.

Project Partners reviewed and conducted outreach to 140 eligible properties. Owners of
eighteen buildings indicated interest and five properties totaling 154 units were ultimately
selected for a comprehensive retrofit. The properties all had regulatory agreements
governing affordability and were selected based on the following characteristics.
Characteristics included:
 1. Met New York State Weatherization Assistance Program (WAP) income eligibility
 requirements.
 2. Located in an area with high health vulnerability (high asthma hospitalization rates
 and/or poor air quality) or climate vulnerability (high heat index and/or flood zone).

 Recent studies have shown certain air pollution hotspots likely contribute to
 elevated Particulate Matter (PM2.5) levels, such as local facilities and expressways,
 traffic congestion and poorly planned and sited transportation infrastructure, and
 hyperlocal mobile monitoring can contribute to 20x higher PM2.5 levels since acute
 short-term exposures to high concentrations of PM2.5 are associated with negative
 cardiorespiratory health outcomes. Health vulnerability was included as part of our
 property prioritization protocol, because weatherization and upgrades have been
 shown to improve resident health conditions through creation of tighter building
 envelopes, improved home ventilation rates, and reduction of combustion
 byproducts and other contaminants within the home. Project Partners used the
 NYC-EJA’s Community Air Mapping Project for Environmental Justice and the NYS
 Department of Environmental Conservation’s 2022-23 Statewide Community Air
 Monitoring Initiative findings to assess and prioritize properties for the pilot.

 Additionally, certain appliances such as natural gas stoves and natural gas or oil
 burning boilers release nitrogen dioxide, carbon monoxide and PM2.5 directly into
 the kitchen and throughout the building. These contaminants can cause airway
 inflammation, irritate the lungs, and trigger or worsen asthma symptoms such as
 wheezing or coughing. Project Partners used the Asthma Dashboard through the
 NYS Department of Health that tracks asthma emergency department visits to
 assess and prioritize properties.

 Electrification of gas and oil burning systems significantly reduces building-level air
 contamination. Better sealed windows and improved ventilation systems provide
 cleaner air to residents in neighborhoods with polluting external infrastructure,

 making properties in health vulnerable locations particularly well suited to benefit
 from weatherization and property upgrades.

 3. Had additional building characteristics such as vulnerable tenant populations
 including the presence of older adults, a history of utility blackouts, and heating and
 hot water system equipment that had reached the end of its useful life.
 4. Included additional physical or economic characteristics that would increase the
 likelihood of successful outcomes such as master metered electricity (to
 adequately incentivize owners to undertake the work), and existing hydronic heating
 systems (which have more options for cost-effective electrification).

Through this E&I grant, we successfully paired weatherization with electrification in these
pilot buildings. Project Partners completed WAP and electrification retrofits in pilot
buildings and created resources and tools that can be scaled and used for future projects
outside of the pilot.

Resources and tools created:
 1. Property Prioritization Framework
 The goal of the prioritization framework is intended to be used as a guide to help
 identify buildings that can most benefit from incorporating electrification upgrades
 into the standard Weatherization Assistance Program scope. In this pilot, Project
 Partners included additional criteria outside of the standard Weatherization
 Assistance Program to prioritize buildings.

 2. Energy Audit Protocol: Multifamily Electrification
 Project Partners developed the electrification audit protocol to complement the
 traditional WAP audit protocol with additional steps necessary to audit and plan for
 electrification of space heating and cooling systems in multifamily buildings, as
 well as domestic hot water generation, and installation of solar photovoltaic.

154 households are benefitting from weatherization and electrification upgrades, resulting
in improved residents and neighborhood health, safety, and comfort while reducing
building greenhouse gas emissions.
