Abstract
. Let p p be a prime. For every field F F of characteristic p p we exhibit pairs of non-isomorphic finite-dimensional p p -nilpotent restricted Lie algebras L L and H H over F F whose restricted enveloping algebras u ⁡ ( L ) u(L) and u ⁡ ( H ) u(H) are isomorphic as F F -algebras. Such pairs exist in every dimension at least p + 5 p+5 , with dim L ′ = p \dim L^{\prime}=p and dim H ′ = p + 1 \dim H^{\prime}=p+1 . Thus, the restricted isomorphism problem has a negative answer over every field of positive characteristic, even for p p -nilpotent algebras over perfect fields.

1.
Introduction

Whether a finite group is determined by its group algebra is one of the
classical questions of representation theory. In general, the answer
is negative: the dihedral and the quaternion group of order eight have
isomorphic complex group algebras, and even over the integers the answer is
negative, by Hertweck’s celebrated counterexample
[
5
]
. A particularly delicate form of the question is the modular one:
if
G
and
H
are finite

p
-groups and
F
is a field of characteristic
p
, does
FG\cong FH
imply
G\cong H
? This is the
modular isomorphism problem
, which appears in Brauer’s 1963 problem list
[
1
]
, and for half a century it
accumulated some positive results: Deskins proved it for abelian
p
-groups

[
3
]
, Passi and Sehgal for groups of class two and exponent
p

[
9
]
, and many further families followed (we refer to the
surveys of Sandling
[
12
]
and Margolis
[
8
]
). However, in the
article
[
4
]
, García-Lucas, Margolis and del Río found a negative solution:
there exist non-isomorphic
2
-groups of order

2^{9}
with isomorphic group algebras over every field of characteristic two.

Modular group algebras are tied to restricted Lie algebras in a precise way.
Jennings
[
7
]
showed that the dimension subgroups of a finite

p
-group
G
assemble into a graded restricted Lie algebra, and Quillen

[
10
]
identified the associated graded algebra of
FG
with respect
to the augmentation filtration as its restricted enveloping algebra.
This leads to the following restricted analogue of the isomorphism problem:

Restricted Isomorphism Problem:

Let
L
and
H
be two restricted Lie algebras over a field
F
of positive characteristic.
If
u(L)\cong u(H)
as
F
-algebras, must
L\cong H
as restricted Lie algebras?

Usefi’s survey
[
17
]
raises it twice among its open problems:
Problem 6 asks for a counterexample, and Problem 9 asks whether the answer is
positive at least for
p
-nilpotent restricted Lie algebras over perfect
fields.

The known results are closely related to the group case. In the article
[
15
]
,
Usefi proved that
u(\mathfrak{g})
determines
\mathfrak{g}
when it is
p
-nilpotent and metacyclic
over a perfect field. In
[
16
]
, he proved that
u(\mathfrak{g})
determines

\mathfrak{g}
when it is
p
-nilpotent and abelian over a perfect field, as well as when

\mathfrak{g}
is abelian and finite-dimensional over an algebraically closed field. In the
same article he also established that
u(\mathfrak{g})
determines the
dimension-subalgebra quotients
D_{i}(\mathfrak{g})/D_{i+1}(\mathfrak{g})
, hence the associated
graded restricted Lie algebra
\operatorname{gr}\mathfrak{g}
, and, for
p
-nilpotent algebras,
the nilpotency class up to an error of one, and that
the quotient

\mathfrak{g}/({\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}^{p}+\gamma_{3}(\mathfrak{g}))
is determined as well. On the other hand, for the ordinary universal
enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
the answer in prime characteristic
has long been known to be negative. Examples of non-isomorphic Lie algebras
with isomorphic universal enveloping algebras have been known at least since
Riley and Usefi
[
11
, Example A]
. Schneider and Usefi

[
13
]
proved that nilpotent Lie algebras of dimension at most
six are determined by
U(\mathfrak{g})
whenever the characteristic of the field is not
2
or
3
, exhibiting at the same time nilpotent
counterexamples of dimensions five and six in characteristics two and three,
and for every prime
p
there is a pair of non-isomorphic metabelian
nilpotent Lie algebras of dimension
p+3
whose universal enveloping algebras
are isomorphic even as Hopf algebras (see
[
13
]
and

[
17
, Example 6.1]
).

Furthermore, in characteristic zero, the answer for
nilpotent Lie algebras is positive: Problem 5 of
[
17
]
was
recently settled, affirmatively, by Campos, Petersen, Robert-Nicoud and
Wierstra
[
2
]
, who showed that a nilpotent Lie algebra over a field of
characteristic zero is determined by its universal enveloping algebra. The preceding results,
however, concern the infinite-dimensional algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
and
say nothing about the restricted enveloping algebra
u(\mathfrak{g})
: the
restricted isomorphism problem remained open in every characteristic.

The aim of this article is to settle the problem by constructing such
examples over every field of positive characteristic.

Theorem
5.2
.

Let
F
be a field of characteristic
p>0
. For every integer
n\geq p+5

there exist
p
-nilpotent restricted Lie algebras
L
and
H
over
F
with

\dim L=\dim H=n
,

u(L)\cong u(H)\ \text{ as $F$-algebras},\qquad\dim L^{\prime}=p\neq p+1=\dim H^{\prime}.

As a consequence, the restricted isomorphism problem has a negative
answer over every field of positive characteristic, already within the class
of
p
-nilpotent restricted Lie algebras over perfect fields.
Neither the dimension of the derived subalgebra nor the lower central series
of a restricted Lie algebra is determined by its restricted enveloping
algebra. In particular, the answers to Problems 6 and 9 of

[
17
]
are negative.

The manuscript is organised as follows. After some short preliminaries, in Section
3
we construct a parametric family of examples
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Since this construction does not carry over verbatim to odd characteristic, in Section
4
we present a new family of examples for any
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
In Section
5
we put everything together and prove the main
theorem over an arbitrary field of positive characteristic.
We conclude in Section
6
with remarks on how the examples sit
among the determined invariants, on the non-restricted enveloping algebra, and on the computer search that produced the first
counterexample.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the article
p
is a prime and
F
a field of characteristic
p
.
All the results in this section can be found in
[
6
]
and in
[
14
]
.
A
restricted Lie algebra
over
F
is a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with a
map
x\mapsto x^{[p]}
, usually called
p
-map
, such that

\operatorname{ad}(x^{[p]})=(\operatorname{ad}x)^{p}
,
(\lambda x)^{[p]}=\lambda^{p}x^{[p]}
and

(1)

(x+y)^{[p]}=x^{[p]}+y^{[p]}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}(x,y),

where the
s_{i}
are the universal Lie polynomials of Jacobson’s formula:

i\,s_{i}(x,y)
is the coefficient of
t^{i-1}
in
\operatorname{ad}(tx+y)^{p-1}(x)
. All we
shall use about them is that each
s_{i}(x,y)
is a sum of iterated brackets of

x
and
y
. For
p=2

formula (
1
) is
(x+y)^{[2]}=x^{[2]}+y^{[2]}+[x,y]
.
Jacobson’s formula also holds for the
p
-th power map of any associative

F
-algebra, with
[r,r^{\prime}]\coloneqq rr^{\prime}-r^{\prime}r
. In particular, in any associative

F
-algebra,

(2)

(r+r^{\prime})^{p}=r^{p}+r^{\prime p}\text{ whenever }[r,r^{\prime}]=0,\text{ and, for }p=2,\Bigl(\sum_{i}r_{i}\Bigr)^{2}=\sum_{i}r_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i<j}\,[r_{i},r_{j}].

We shall use throughout, without further mention, that the commutator is a
derivation in each variable:
[r,r^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime}]=[r,r^{\prime}]\,r^{\prime\prime}+r^{\prime}\,[r,r^{\prime\prime}]
and

[rr^{\prime},r^{\prime\prime}]=r\,[r^{\prime},r^{\prime\prime}]+[r,r^{\prime\prime}]\,r^{\prime}
.

We say that
\mathfrak{g}
is
p
-nilpotent
if for every
x\in\mathfrak{g}
there is an
N

with
x^{[p]^{N}}=0
, where
x^{[p]^{N}}
denotes the
N
-fold iterate of the

p
-map. A basic tool for constructing restricted structures is

Jacobson’s theorem
: if
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra over
F
with basis
\{e_{i}\}

and
h_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}
are vectors satisfying
\operatorname{ad}h_{i}=(\operatorname{ad}e_{i})^{p}
for every
i
,
then
\mathfrak{g}
carries a unique
p
-map with
e_{i}^{[p]}=h_{i}
.

The
restricted enveloping algebra
of
\mathfrak{g}
is

u(\mathfrak{g})=U(\mathfrak{g})/\bigl(x^{p}-x^{[p]}:x\in\mathfrak{g}\bigr)
. By the
Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem for restricted enveloping algebras, if
\mathfrak{g}
has basis

e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
then the
ordered restricted monomials

e_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots e_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
with
0\leq\alpha_{i}\leq p-1
form a basis
of
u(\mathfrak{g})
. In particular
\dim u(\mathfrak{g})=p^{\,n}
and
\mathfrak{g}
embeds into
u(\mathfrak{g})
. The
algebra
u(\mathfrak{g})
has the expected universal property: every homomorphism of
restricted Lie algebras from
\mathfrak{g}
to an associative
F
-algebra
A
, viewed as
a restricted Lie algebra under the commutator and the
p
-map,
extends uniquely to an algebra homomorphism
u(\mathfrak{g})\to A
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a restricted Lie algebra with basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
. Then, as an
associative algebra,

(P)

u(\mathfrak{g})\;=\;F\langle e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\rangle\Big/\bigl(\,e_{i}e_{j}-e_{j}e_{i}-[e_{i},e_{j}],\quad e_{i}^{\,p}-e_{i}^{[p]}\,\bigr)_{i,j}\,.

3.
Characteristic two

Throughout this section we work over the field
\mathbb{F}_{2}
of two elements. We fix an integer
k\geq 3
and set

q\coloneqq 2^{k-1}\ \ (\text{so }q\geq 4),\qquad n\coloneqq k+4.

Let
L_{k}
be the
n
-dimensional restricted Lie algebra over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with basis

a,\ b,\ b_{2},\ a_{2},\ v,\ b_{4},\ b_{8},\ \dots,\ b_{q},\ d\qquad(\text{the }b_{2^{j}}\text{ for }2\leq j\leq k-1,\ \text{and }d),

2
-map

\displaystyle a^{[2]}=a_{2},\quad a_{2}^{[2]}=d,\quad b^{[2]}=b_{2},\quad b_{2}^{[2]}=b_{4},

\displaystyle b_{2^{j}}^{[2]}=b_{2^{j+1}}\ (2\leq j\leq k-2),\quad b_{q}^{[2]}=d,\quad v^{[2]}=d^{[2]}=0,

with nonzero brackets

[a,b]=v,\qquad[a_{2},b]=b_{4},\qquad[a,v]=b_{4}.

Let
H_{k}
be the
n
-dimensional restricted Lie algebra over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with basis

x,\ y,\ y_{2},\ x_{2},\ w,\ y_{4},\ y_{8},\ \dots,\ y_{q},\ c,

2
-map

\displaystyle x^{[2]}=x_{2},\quad x_{2}^{[2]}=c,\quad y^{[2]}=y_{2},\quad y_{2}^{[2]}=y_{4},

\displaystyle y_{2^{j}}^{[2]}=y_{2^{j+1}}\ (2\leq j\leq k-2),\quad y_{q}^{[2]}=c,\quad w^{[2]}=c^{[2]}=0,

and nonzero brackets

[x,y]=w,\qquad[x,y_{2}]=y_{4},\qquad[y,w]=y_{4},\qquad[x_{2},y]=y_{4}+c,\qquad[x,w]=y_{4}+c.

Each algebra is generated by its first two basis vectors, the two
2
-power towers
merge at the top, and the eighth power of the first generator vanishes.

Lemma 3.1
.

L_{k}
and
H_{k}
are restricted Lie algebras of dimension
n=k+4
, generated
by
a,b
and by
x,y
respectively. Both have nilpotency class
3
, with
lower central dimension sequences
(n,2,1,0)
for
L_{k}
and
(n,3,2,0)
for

H_{k}
. In particular

L_{k}^{\prime}=\langle v,b_{4}\rangle,\qquad H_{k}^{\prime}=\langle w,y_{4},c\rangle.

Proof.

By Jacobson’s theorem it suffices to check the Jacobi identity and

(\operatorname{ad}e)^{2}=\operatorname{ad}(e^{[2]})
for each basis vector
e
.

Let us begin with
L_{k}
. The nonzero adjoint values are

\operatorname{ad}a\colon\ b\mapsto v,\ v\mapsto b_{4},\qquad\operatorname{ad}b\colon\ a\mapsto v,\ a_{2}\mapsto b_{4},\qquad\operatorname{ad}a_{2}\colon\ b\mapsto b_{4},\qquad\operatorname{ad}v\colon\ a\mapsto b_{4}.

Then
(\operatorname{ad}a)^{2}
maps
b\mapsto v\mapsto b_{4}
and kills every other basis
vector, which is
\operatorname{ad}a_{2}=\operatorname{ad}(a^{[2]})
. Each of
\operatorname{ad}b,\operatorname{ad}a_{2},\operatorname{ad}v
maps
the basis into
\langle v,b_{4}\rangle
, which it kills, so its square is zero,
matching
\operatorname{ad}(b^{[2]})=\operatorname{ad}b_{2}=0
,
\operatorname{ad}(a_{2}^{[2]})=\operatorname{ad}d=0
,

\operatorname{ad}(v^{[2]})=0
. All remaining basis vectors are central with central
squares. For the Jacobi identity: every bracket lies in
\langle v,b_{4}\rangle
, the
vector
b_{4}
is central, and
[v,e]
is nonzero only for
e=a
. Since
v

occurs in
[e_{i},e_{j}]
only for
\{e_{i},e_{j}\}=\{a,b\}
, a nonzero summand

[[e_{i},e_{j}],e_{l}]
of a Jacobiator with distinct entries would need

\{e_{i},e_{j}\}=\{a,b\}
and
e_{l}=a
, which is impossible.

Let us consider now
H_{k}
. The nonzero adjoint values are

\operatorname{ad}x\colon\ y\mapsto w,\ y_{2}\mapsto y_{4},\ w\mapsto y_{4}+c,\qquad\operatorname{ad}y\colon\ x\mapsto w,\ x_{2}\mapsto y_{4}+c,\ w\mapsto y_{4},

\operatorname{ad}y_{2}\colon\ x\mapsto y_{4},\qquad\operatorname{ad}x_{2}\colon\ y\mapsto y_{4}+c,\qquad\operatorname{ad}w\colon\ x\mapsto y_{4}+c,\ y\mapsto y_{4}.

Then
(\operatorname{ad}x)^{2}\colon y\mapsto y_{4}+c
(all other basis vectors
\mapsto 0
)
equals
\operatorname{ad}x_{2}
, and
(\operatorname{ad}y)^{2}\colon x\mapsto y_{4}
equals
\operatorname{ad}y_{2}
. Each of

\operatorname{ad}y_{2},\operatorname{ad}x_{2},\operatorname{ad}w
maps the basis into the central space

\langle y_{4},c\rangle
, so its square is zero, matching
\operatorname{ad}y_{4}=\operatorname{ad}c=\operatorname{ad}0=0
. For
the Jacobi identity: every bracket lies in
\langle w,y_{4},c\rangle
with

\langle y_{4},c\rangle
central,
[w,e]\neq 0
only for
e\in\{x,y\}
, and
w
occurs
only in
[x,y]
. A nonzero Jacobiator summand with distinct entries would need
inner pair
\{x,y\}
and outer entry in
\{x,y\}
, which is impossible.

Generation is clear in both cases by the
2
-map and the brackets. The lower
central series are

\displaystyle L_{k}

\displaystyle\supset\langle v,b_{4}\rangle\supset\langle b_{4}\rangle\supset 0,

\displaystyle H_{k}

\displaystyle\supset\langle w,y_{4},c\rangle\supset\langle y_{4},c\rangle\supset 0.

∎

Corollary 3.2
.

The restricted Lie algebras
L_{k}
and
H_{k}
are not isomorphic. In fact they
are not isomorphic even as Lie algebras, since

\dim L_{k}^{\prime}=2\neq 3=\dim H_{k}^{\prime}
.

By Lemma
2.1
, the relations (
P
) hold in
u(H_{k})
.
Inside

we may therefore write

x^{2}=x_{2},\qquad y^{2^{j}}=y_{2^{j}},\qquad x^{4}=x_{2}^{\,2}=c,\qquad w=xy+yx,

and we use this exponent notation from now on. All computations below take

Lemma 3.3
.

In
u(H_{k})
the following hold:

(1)

[x,y^{2}]=y^{4}
,
\ [x^{2},y]=\delta
,
\ w^{2}=0

\ x^{4}=y^{2^{k}}
,

\ x^{8}=0

y^{4}

(3)

[x^{2},y^{2}]=[x^{2},w]=[y^{2},w]=0
,
\ [x,w]=\delta

(4)

g\,\delta=x^{4}
and

.

Proof.

(1) These are immediate from the defining relations (

) of

.

(2)

[x,y^{4}]=[x,y^{2}]\,y^{2}+y^{2}\,[x,y^{2}]=y^{4}y^{2}+y^{2}y^{4}=0
, so

is central, since

Theorem 3.4
.

For every

\varphi(a)=X\coloneqq x,\qquad\varphi(b)=Y\coloneqq x+y+x\,g=x+y+x\bigl(y^{2^{k}-4}+y^{2^{k+1}-8}\bigr),

\displaystyle=[x,w]=\delta=f_{b_{4}}=f_{[a,v]},

\displaystyle[f_{b},f_{v}]

\displaystyle=[Y,w]=[x,w]+[y,w]+g\,[x,w]=\delta+y^{4}+g\delta=\delta+y^{4}+x^{4}=\delta+\delta=0=f_{[b,v]},

\displaystyle[f_{b_{2}},f_{a_{2}}]

\displaystyle=[Y^{2},x^{2}]=0,\qquad[f_{b_{2}},f_{v}]=[Y^{2},w]=0,\qquad[f_{a_{2}},f_{v}]=[x^{2},w]=0.

In each case the value agrees with
the image of the corresponding bracket of
L_{k}
. All relations (
P
) of
u(L_{k})

hold, so
\varphi
is a well-defined algebra homomorphism.

To see the bijectivity, by Lemma
3.1
and the
Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem the two algebras have dimension
2^{\,n}
,
so it suffices to show that
\varphi
is surjective. As
L_{k}
is generated by
a,b
,
so is
u(L_{k})
as an associative algebra, hence the image of
\varphi

is the subalgebra
B
generated by
X=x
and
Y
, and it is enough to show

x,y\in B
. Certainly
x=X\in B
, and, the characteristic being two,

\eta\coloneqq X+Y=y+xg\in B.

Moreover,
\eta^{4}=y^{4}
. Indeed
X^{2}=x^{2}
and
[X,Y]=w
, so (
2
) gives
\eta^{2}=X^{2}+Y^{2}+[X,Y]=x^{2}+Y^{2}+w
. Squaring
once more, the cross terms vanish, since
w^{2}=0
,

[x^{2},w]=0
, and
[x^{2},Y^{2}]=[Y^{2},w]=0
were shown
among the bracket relations above, whence

\eta^{4}=X^{4}+Y^{4}=x^{4}+\delta=y^{4},

using
Y^{4}=\delta=x^{4}+y^{4}
. Now
g=(y^{4})^{m}+(y^{4})^{2m}
with

m\coloneqq 2^{k-2}-1
(because
2q-4=4m
and

), so

g=(\eta^{4})^{m}+(\eta^{4})^{2m}\in B
. Therefore
xg\in B
and

. Thus

, and
\varphi
is an isomorphism.
∎

4.
Odd characteristic

Throughout this section

Lemma 4.1
.

Proof.

The nonzero adjoint values are

To check the Jacobi identity, let us note that every bracket lies in

To conclude, we have

Corollary 4.2
.

L\not\cong H
as restricted Lie algebras, since

We now produce the isomorphism. All computations take place in

y_{p^{2}}^{\,q}=c,\qquad y_{p^{2}}^{\,pq}=c^{\,p}=0,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad y_{p^{2}}^{\,m}=0\ \text{ for every }m\geq pq.

Lemma 4.3
.

In

, with
\ t\coloneqq x_{p}y_{p^{2}}^{\,q-1}

(2)

(2) By direct computations using centrality,

Theorem 4.4

For every odd prime

5.
Arbitrary fields

In order to generalise the examples to any field, we need only the restricted-Lie analogue of the classical
isomorphisms
U(\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{k}K)\cong U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{k}K

(see
[
14
]
). It follows from the universal
property of

together with the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
M
be a finite-dimensional restricted Lie algebra over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
F
a
field of characteristic
p
. Then
M\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}F
carries a unique
restricted structure extending that of
M
, the natural map

u(M)\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}F\to u(M\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}F)
is an isomorphism of
F

and
(M\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}F)^{\prime}=M^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}F
.

We are now ready to prove the main theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
F
be a field of characteristic

. For every integer
n\geq p+5

there exist
p
-nilpotent restricted Lie algebras

and

over

with

, such that:

u(L)\cong u(H)\ \text{ as $F$-algebras},\qquad\dim L^{\prime}=p\neq p+1=\dim H^{\prime}.

Proof.

Given

, set

if
p=2
and
k\coloneqq n-p-2
if
p
is odd, so that

, and let
(L_{0},H_{0})
be the pair constructed above over the prime field,
namely
(L_{k},H_{k})
if
p=2
and
(L_{p,k},H_{p,k})
if

is odd. Put

and

. By

or

and Proposition

while Lemma

or

,
together with Proposition

These algebras are

and that of

and every power

6.
Concluding remarks

The examples among the determined invariants

We first situate the counterexamples against the determined invariants.
If
u(L)\cong u(H)
, then by
[
15
,
17
]
the
dimension-subalgebra quotients
D_{i}(L)/D_{i+1}(L)
and
D_{i}(H)/D_{i+1}(H)

are isomorphic, so the associated graded restricted Lie algebras
\operatorname{gr}L

and
\operatorname{gr}H
are isomorphic, the nilpotency classes of
p
-nilpotent
algebras differ by at most one, and by
[
16
]
the quotients

L/({L^{\prime}}^{p}+\gamma_{3}(L))
and
H/({H^{\prime}}^{p}+\gamma_{3}(H))
are isomorphic. All
of this necessarily holds for our pairs. What the present examples show is
that the determinacy stops there: the dimension of the derived subalgebra,
the most basic invariant not captured by the graded data, is already lost in

u(L)
, in every positive characteristic.

The non-restricted enveloping algebra

The phenomenon is genuinely restricted. Forgetting the
p
-map,
L
and
H

are ordinary Lie algebras, and one may ask whether
U(L)\cong U(H)
. In prime
characteristic this problem is itself known to have a negative answer

[
11
,
13
]
, but our pairs are not of that kind. This is
immediate from the standard fact that, for any Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the
abelianisation of its enveloping algebra is

U(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong U(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})=S(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})
,
a polynomial algebra in
\dim(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})
variables. As both
the abelianisation and its number of variables are isomorphism invariants,

U(\mathfrak{g})
determines
\dim(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})
. The members of
each of our pairs have equal dimension but derived subalgebras of dimensions

p
and
p+1
, so
\dim(L/L^{\prime})\neq\dim(H/H^{\prime})
and hence
U(L)\not\cong U(H)
.

In the restricted case the same computation reads instead

u(L)^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong u(L/I)
, where
I
is the
p
-ideal generated by
L^{\prime}
:
the relations
x^{p}=x^{[p]}
collapse the whole
p
-closure of
L^{\prime}
in the
commutative quotient. For our pairs
L^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime}
differ in dimension, yet the

p
-ideals they generate have the same dimension. This is exactly what allows

u(L)\cong u(H)
while
U(L)\not\cong U(H)
.

How the examples were found

The examples of this article were discovered by a computer search guided by
the determined invariants.
The code used for this search was developed with the assistance of
Claude Code
.
Let us describe the process briefly. The mathematical arguments and final verification are independent of the
computer search.

The determined invariants dictate the shape of the search space. Since the
dimension-subalgebra quotients are determined by

u(L)
, the two members of a counterexample pair must share
all their graded invariants, and the search must therefore take place among

filtered deformations
: we fixed, for each small dimension, a graded
restricted Lie algebra over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
(an assignment of weights to a basis,
compatible with the towers of the
2
-map and with a generating
bracket), and parametrised its deformations, allowing each entry of the
bracket table and of the
2
-map to be perturbed by terms of strictly
larger weight, subject to the Jacobi identity and Jacobson’s criterion.

Candidate pairs
(L,H)
of deformations of one graded algebra were then
screened by invariants: a pair is of interest only if
L\not\cong H
. In
practice we started by using the cheap certificate
\dim L^{\prime}\neq\dim H^{\prime}
, while the determined invariants, such
as the dimension-subalgebra quotients, agree. For each surviving pair we
searched for an isomorphism
u(L)\to u(H)
directly. In dimension 7 the
sampling produced the pair
(L_{3},H_{3})
of Section
3
.

After checking that the counterexample did not generalise easily to odd characteristic, we proceeded in a similar fashion,
setting
p=3
and dimension 8, where we found the first pair that led us to the full generalisation.
