Technology Fundamentals

 Marine Energy Overview
 Marine energy is the energy contained in ocean tides, currents, waves, and temperature
 fluctuations, as well as river currents. It’s one of the world’s largest untapped renewable energy
 resources.
 Marine energy is:
 • Reliable and predictable:
 • Generates energy day and night and year-round, often producing
 more when other renewable energy sources produce less.
 • Complements intermittent sources, like solar and wind energy, for
 a reliable grid.
 • Affordable and resilient:
 • Locally-available energy for remote communities that rely on
 expensive shipments of diesel fuel.
 • Helps achieve energy security and resilience.
 • Power for the grid or blue economy:
 • Generates grid-scale electricity or power for blue economy
 industries, such as offshore fish farming, autonomous underwater
 vehicles for marine research, and ocean monitoring.

 Marine Energy: Key Opportunities for Impact

Powering communities and enhancing coastal resilience:
Marine energy can power electric microgrids in coastal,
remote, and islanded communities, enhancing energy and
coastal resilience and sustaining marine ecosystems.
Accelerating technology development timescales through
demonstration and deployment: Marine energy can meet
the needs of many blue economy markets in a variety of
ways, such as producing fresh water through desalination,
servicing the power demands for aquaculture
and ocean sensing, and deploying marine energy for
coastal and ocean-based applications to accelerate marine
energy technology development for the grid.

Case Studies

 About the Community

  • Igiugig is a remote southwestern Alaskan community of 70 people.
  • Located where Lake Iliamna is swallowed by the Kvichak River.
  • The Kvichak River is vital to the Igyararmiut (people of Igiugig) for drinking water and for salmon.
  • For more than 8,000 years, the Igyararmiut have lived sustainably on their land.
  • The community has relied on salmon as a primary staple to their subsistence lifestyle for
 generations.
