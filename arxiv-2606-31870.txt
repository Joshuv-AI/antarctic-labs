Intuitionistic Monotone Modal Logic: Proof Theory and Semantics
Tiziano Dalmonte, Jim de Groot

Abstract
We study the intuitionistic monotone modal logic 𝖨𝖬 \mathsf{IM} recently introduced in [ 28 ] . We first provide a semantic characterisation for a family of natural extensions of 𝖨𝖬 \mathsf{IM} in terms of constructive neighbourhood models. We then present a calculus for 𝖨𝖬 \mathsf{IM} and its extensions, obtained by adapting a structured calculus for the classical monotone modal logic 𝖬 \mathsf{M} . Based on the calculus, we prove some preliminary results for 𝖨𝖬 \mathsf{IM} , including its decidability. Our calculus also reveals an interesting analogy between constructive and intuitionistic variants of 𝖬 \mathsf{M} and the corresponding variants of 𝖪 \mathsf{K} , thereby further justifying 𝖨𝖬 \mathsf{IM} as a faithful intuitionistic variant of 𝖬 \mathsf{M} .

A peculiar feature of intuitionistic modal logics is that,
similar to the intuitionistic connectives, the modal operators
need not be interdefinable. This disconnection often gives rise to
a wide spectrum of intuitionistic counterparts of the same classical

On one side of this spectrum sit for example logics studied by

[

At the other extremity, we have systems

The study of constructive analogues of classical normal modal logics,

First,

Second,

The study of these in an intuitionistic context dates back to

Discarding the S4 axioms yields Goldblatt’s intuitionistic logic

However, compared to the wealth of research on intuitionistic analogues of

This is surprising, because classical monotone modal logic has been

The study of intuitionistic monotone modal logics with two modalities is

The analogy with the normal modal case gives three ways to decide on the level of

2.1
The logics

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the language generated by the grammar

\varphi::=p\mid\bot\mid\varphi\land\varphi\mid\varphi\lor\varphi\mid\varphi\to\varphi\mid\Box\varphi\mid\Diamond\varphi,

where

As usual, we abbreviate

and

Besides, the

c(p)=c(\bot)=0

c(\varphi\wedge\psi)=c(\varphi\vee\psi)=c(\varphi\to\psi)=c(\varphi)+c(\psi)+1

and
c(\Box\varphi)=c(\Diamond\varphi)=c(\varphi)+1

Intuitionistic analogues of monotone modal logics

2.2
Constructive neighbourhood semantics

Let
(X,\leq)
be a preordered set. For
a\subseteq X
we define

{\uparrow}a=\{y\in X\mid x\leq y\text{ for some }x\in a\}
.
Then
a
is called an
upset
if
{\uparrow}a=a
,
and we write
\mathrm{up}(X,\leq)
for the collection of upsets of
(X,\leq)
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
constructive neighbourhood frame
is a tuple
\mathfrak{F}=(W,\leq,N)

consisting of a set
W
, a preorder
\leq
on
W
, and a
function
N:W\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(W))
called a
neighbourhood function
.
A
constructive neighbourhood model
is a pair
\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},V)

comprising an constructive neighbourhood frame
\mathfrak{F}
and a
valuation
V:\mathrm{Prop}\to\mathrm{up}(X,\leq)
that assigns an upset of
(X,\leq)

to each proposition letter
p
.

The interpretation of
\mathcal{L}
-formulas at a world
w
of such a model
\mathfrak{M}

is defined recursively by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash p

\displaystyle\quad\text{iff}\quad w\in V(p)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\bot

never

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\varphi\wedge\psi

\displaystyle\quad\text{iff}\quad\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\varphi\text{ and }\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\psi

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\varphi\vee\psi

\displaystyle\quad\text{iff}\quad\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\varphi\text{ or }\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\psi

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\varphi\to\psi

\displaystyle\quad\text{iff}\quad\text{for all }v\geq w,\;\mathfrak{M},v\Vdash\varphi\text{ implies }\mathfrak{M},v\Vdash\psi

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\Box\varphi

\displaystyle\quad\text{iff}\quad\text{for all }v\geq w\text{ there exists }a\in N(v)\text{ such that for all }u\in a\text{ we have }\mathfrak{M},u\Vdash\varphi

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},w\Vdash\Diamond\varphi

\displaystyle\quad\text{iff}\quad\text{for all }v\geq w\text{ and all }a\in N(v)\text{ there exists }u\in a\text{ such that }\mathfrak{M},u\Vdash\varphi

Let
\Phi\cup\{\psi\}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
.
A constructive neighbourhood frame
\mathfrak{F}

validates

\psi

if
(\mathfrak{F},V),w\Vdash\psi
for every valuation
V
for
\mathfrak{F}

and every world
w
in
\mathfrak{F}
,
and it
validates

\Phi
if it validates every
formula in
\Phi
, notation:
\mathfrak{F}\Vdash\psi
and
\mathfrak{F}\Vdash\Phi
.
If
\Ax
is a set of axioms and
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}
,
then we write
\Vdash_{\Ax}\varphi
if every constructive neighbourhood
frame that validates the formulas in
\Ax
also validates
\varphi
.

It was shown in
[
12
]
that constructive neighbourhood frames provide sound and complete
semantics with respect to various extensions of
\mathsf{WM}
.
They can also be used for (extensions of)
\mathsf{IM}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}=(W,\leq,N)
be a constructive neighbourhood frame.

1.

If
N(w)\neq\emptyset
for all
w\in W
,
then
\mathfrak{F}
validates
\mathsf{N}
;

2.

If
\emptyset\notin N(w)
for all
w\in W
,
then
\mathfrak{F}
validates
\mathsf{P}
;

3.

If
w\in U
for all
U\in N(w)
and
w\in W
,
then
\mathfrak{F}
validates
\mathsf{T}
;

4.

If
U_{1}\cap U_{2}\neq\emptyset
for all
U_{1},U_{2}\in N(w)

and
w\in W
, then
\mathfrak{F}
validates
\mathsf{D}
;

5.

If
N(w)
is closed under binary intersections for all
w\in W
,
then
\mathfrak{F}
validates
\mathsf{K}
;

6.

If (
N(w)\neq\emptyset
and
w\leq v
) implies
N(v)\neq\emptyset
,
for all
w,v\in W
, then
\mathfrak{F}
validates
\mathsf{I}_{\Diamond}
.

Proof.

The first five items can be found in
[
12
]
.
For the last one, suppose
\mathfrak{F}
satisfies the given condition,
and let
\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},V)
be a model based on
\mathfrak{F}
.
Let
w\in W
and suppose
w\Vdash\Box\top\to\Diamond\varphi
.
We show that any neighbourhood
U
of any
v\geq w
contains an element
that satisfies
\varphi
. Let
v\geq w
. Then
v\Vdash\Box\top\to\Diamond\varphi
.
If
N(v)\neq\emptyset
, then by the frame condition we have

v\Vdash\Box\top
, so that
v\Vdash\Diamond\varphi
, which implies that
every neighbourhood of
v
contains an element satisfying
\varphi
.
If, on the other hand,
N(v)=\emptyset
, then it is trivially true
that every neighbourhood of
v
contains a world satisfying
\varphi
.
Therefore
w\Vdash\Diamond\varphi
. Since
w
was arbitrary, this proves
that the frame validates
\mathsf{I}_{\Diamond}
.
∎

) above are called

2.3
Canonical model constructions

Throughout this subsection, let

\Ax\subseteq\{\mathsf{N},\mathsf{P},\mathsf{T},\mathsf{D},\mathsf{K}\}
.
We prove that
\mathsf{IM}\oplus\Ax
is sound and complete with
respect to a suitable class of continual constructive neighbourhood frames.
Akin to
[
12
,
59
]
, we use so-called segments, rather than prime theories,
to construct a canonical model.
The case for
\mathsf{IM}
without additional axioms was proven (indirectly)
in
[
28

.

Definition 2.6
.

An
\Ax
-prime theory
is a subset
\Phi\subseteq\mathcal{L}
that is

consistent
(
\Phi\not\vdash_{\Ax}\bot
),

deductively closed
(
\Phi\vdash_{\Ax}\varphi
implies
\varphi\in\Phi
)
and
prime
(
\varphi\vee\psi\in\Phi
implies
\varphi\in\Phi
or
\psi\in\Phi
).

We write
\mathrm{PF}_{\Ax}
for the set of
\Ax
-prime theories.
We can prove the Lindenbaum lemma as usual:

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\Phi\cup\{\psi\}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
be such that
\Phi\not\vdash_{\Ax}\psi
.
Then there exists an
\Ax
-prime theory
\Phi^{\prime}
such that

\Phi\subseteq\Phi^{\prime}
and

Lemma 2.9

Let

\Ax

Proof.

The proof proceeds by induction on the structure of

.
The propositional cases are routine.

Case

Conversely, if

If

3.2
Structural properties and cut elimination

We prove standard structural properties of the calculus
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{IM}}
, most importantly
admissibility of cut.
As a consequence, we obtain that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{IM}}
is complete with respect to
\mathsf{IM}
and that

\mathsf{IM}
is decidable.
As usual, a rule is called
admissible
in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{IM}}
if derivability of
the premises implies derivability of the conclusion.
A one-premiss rule is
height-preserving admissible
(hp-admissible)
if derivability of the premiss via a derivation of height
h

implies derivability of the conclusion with a derivation of height
\leq h
.
Finally, a rule
R=(\Gamma_{1},...,\Gamma_{n}/\Gamma)
is
height-preserving invertible
(hp-invertible)
if the rule
R_{in}=(\Gamma/\Gamma_{k})
is hp-admissible for all
1\leq k\leq n
.

As is often the case, one can infer important properties of a logic based on its analytic cut-free calculus. As a first application of the calculus, we prove here that

Theorem 3.14

(Decidability)

By adapting the decidability argument for

in
[

To this end, consider that

of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{IM}}

the premiss(es) of

only contain subfomulas
of formulas in the conclusion;
(2) with the exception of

, the backward application
of all rules of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{IM}}
reduces the sequent complexity
according to the following measure

C(\Gamma)=\sum_{x\in\Gamma}c^{\prime}(x)

for

\mathbin{\scalebox{.75}{\text{\faIconFromMacro{faStarO}}}}\in\{\wedge,\vee,\to\}

4.2

So far, blocks contained at most one formula.
While not strictly necessary, it allowed us to simplify the calculus.
To formulate calculi for logics with
\mathsf{K}

where blocks contain finite
multisets
of marked formulas.
We sketch the required modifications.

We now call
input blocks

(or no formula at all),
and
output blocks
those containing an output formula
(possibly together with some input formulas).
As before, sequents are single-succedent, meaning that their only output component is either an output formula,
or an output block, or is not present.
We extend the formula interpretation from Definition

\iota(\{\ \})=\Box\top,\quad\iota(\{\varphi^{\bullet}_{1},\ldots,\varphi^{\bullet}_{k}\})=\Box(\varphi_{1}\land\dots\land\varphi_{k}),\quad\iota(\{\varphi^{\bullet}_{1},\ldots,\varphi^{\bullet}_{k},{\psi}^{\circ}\})=\Diamond(\varphi_{1}\land\dots\land\varphi_{k}\to\psi)

5
Conclusion

We studied the intuitionistic monotone modal logic
\mathsf{IM}
and several
extensions, providing a semantics by means of constructive neighbourhood
frames as well as cut-free proof systems.
The proof-theoretical analysis reveals the following analogy between
constructive and intuitionistic versions of
\mathsf{M}
and
\mathsf{K}
:

Constructive modal logics are single-succedent restrictions
of Genzten calculi;

Intuitionistic modal logics are single-succedent restrictions
of structured sequent calculi.

This immediately raises the question whether this pattern restricts to
constructive and intuitionistic versions of
\mathsf{M}
and
\mathsf{K}
, or if
it applies more generally.

Furthermore, Arisaka, Das and Strassburger
[
4
]
provide a nested
calculus for
\mathsf{CK}
which requires a more radical form of pruning than that
employed for
\mathsf{IK}
.
Similarly, we turned the calculus for
\mathsf{IM}
into one for
\mathsf{WM}
by replacing
context pruning with context thinning.
Again, it would be interesting to investigate if this is merely a coincidence
or part of a pattern.

Other interesting questions include:
Does the semantic reading from Remark
3.6
allow one to extract
countermodels for failed derivations in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{IM}}
?
What other variants of context thinning and pruning can be defined,
and what logics do they give rise to?
What kind of intuitionistic variants of
\mathsf{M}
arise from the
single-succedent restriction of other structured calculi, such as
the nested calculus from
[
38
]
?
We hope to address some of these in future research.

Finally, we would like to study the complexity of the derivability problem in
\mathsf{IM}
and its extensions by either extending an optimal calculus for
\mathsf{IPL}
like the one from
[
35
]
, or considering reductions to classical multi-modal logics
similarly to
[
13
,
20
]
,
[
23
, Chapter 10.2]
.
