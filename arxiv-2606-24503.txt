Asymptotics for Toeplitz operators with symbol an indicator function
Razvan Apredoaei

Abstract
. We prove an off-diagonal expansion of the kernel of the Toeplitz operator whose symbol is the indicator function of a compact domain with smooth boundary in a complete symplectic manifold of bounded geometry. Using our approach, we extend two results to the non-compact setting: the first concerns the asymptotics of the trace of polynomials in this operator, and the second establishes a Weyl law for this Toeplitz operator.



1.
Introduction

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold and
L
a holomorphic Hermitian line bundle over
X
such that
L
is prequantum, i.e., the curvature of the Chern connexion of
L
coincides with the symplectic form
\omega
(see (
1.2
)). Let
P_{p}
be the projection on the space
\mathcal{H}_{p}
of holomorphic sections of
L^{p}
. The projector
P_{p}
has a smooth kernel known as the
Bergman kernel
. Let
f
be a bounded function on
X
. The Toeplitz operator with symbol
f
is the family of operators defined by
T_{f,p}:=P_{p}fP_{p}
. In the particular case where
f=\mathbf{1}_{W}
is the indicator function of a compact domain
W
with smooth boundary
V=\partial W
, the operator
T_{\mathbf{1}_{W},p}
behaves semiclassically as an almost-projection. Its spectrum concentrates near
0
and
1
with order
p^{n}
and the number of eigenvalues contained in
[a,b]\subset]0,1[
is given by the following Weyl law:

(1.1)

\Big|\spec(T_{W,p})\cap[a,b]\Big|=p^{n-1/2}\frac{\mathrm{vol}(V)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}(\mathrm{erfc}^{-1}(a)-\mathrm{erfc}^{-1}(b))+o(p^{n-1/2}),

where
\mathrm{erfc}
denotes the complementary error function (see (
1.18
)). This behavior has been uncovered by Charles–Estienne in
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
and comes from the full asymptotic expansion in
p\rightarrow\infty
of the trace of
g(T_{\mathbf{1}_{W},p})
for
g
polynomial, which they prove in
[
5
, Theorem 1.2]
. They find an application of their results in the study of entanglement entropy for free Fermions
[
5
, §7]
. As another application, Polterovich remarked in
[
20
, §6]
that Toeplitz operators with piecewise constant symbol may be a relevant tool to infer the homotopy type of a quantum phase space.

In this paper we are interested in the more general setting where
(X,\omega)
is a symplectic manifold of bounded geometry endowed with a prequantum Hermitian line bundle
L
and a Hermitian vector bundle
E
over
X
. In this setting, the operators
T_{f,p}=P_{p}fP_{p}
act on sections of
L^{p}\otimes E
, and
P_{p}
denotes the projection on a closed subspace
\mathcal{H}_{p}
of the space of square-integrable sections
L^{2}(X,L^{p}\otimes E)
for the metrics on
L
and
E
. In our case
\mathcal{H}_{p}
is a spectral space associated to a renormalisation of the Bochner Laplacian which was also used in
[
11
]
.

We prove asymptotics for the Toeplitz operator with symbol an indicator function of a compact domain
W
inside
X
with smooth boundary
V
. The novelty of this paper is fourfold:

(1)

In Theorem
1.1
, we obtain full on-diagonal kernel asymptotics for the Toeplitz operator
T_{W,p}
and show that the transition across the boundary is governed by a universal error-function profile;

(2)

In Theorem
1.3
, we get on-diagonal kernel asymptotics for
g(T_{W,p})
with
g
a polynomial;

(3)

Using (2), we extend the trace asymptotics of Charles–Estienne
[
5
]
to complete symplectic manifolds of bounded geometry as stated in Corollaries
1.4
and
1.5
;

(4)

We also extend the Weyl law (
1.1
) for
T_{W,p}
to the non-compact setting (see Corollary
1.5
).

The case of an indicator function of a compact domain with smooth boundary is arguably the simplest example of a Toeplitz operator with singular symbol. The asymptotics we derive emphasize that its discontinuity creates a boundary layer phenomenon that is invisible for smooth symbols and leads to half-integer powers in the semiclassical expansions.

Two key results are essential to our proofs. The first one is the asymptotic expansion of the Bergman kernel in a neighborhood of the diagonal established by Dai–Liu–Ma
[
7
, Theorem 4.18]
, Ma–Marinescu
[
16
, Chapter 8]
and Kordyukov–Ma–Marinescu
[
12
, Theorem 4.3]
. The second one is the exponential decay property of the Bergman kernel outside the diagonal established by Ma–Marinescu
[
19
, Theorem 1]
, and Kordyukov–Ma–Marinescu
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Let us mention that previous known results on kernel asymptotics for the operators
T_{f,p}
were obtained with stronger regularity assumptions on
f
. On a symplectic manifold and in the case where
\mathcal{H}_{p}
is the kernel of the
\mathrm{spin}^{c}
-Dirac operator, Ma–Marinescu
[
16
, Lemma 7.2.4, §8.1.2]
, and
[
18
, §4]
proved a full off-diagonal expansion for the kernel of Toeplitz operators with symbol
f
when
f
is smooth. In
[
1
, §3]
, Barron, Ma, Marinescu and Pinssonnault prove kernel asymptotics for Toeplitz operators when
f
is
\mathscr{C}^{k}
and
\mathcal{H}_{p}
the kernel of the
\mathrm{spin}^{c}
-Dirac operator. In
[
6
]
, Charles and Polterovich also studied the Berezin–Toeplitz quantization of
\mathscr{C}^{k}
functions.

We now state our results. Let
(X,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold of dimension
2n

(L,h^{L})
be a Hermitian line bundle over
X

(E,h^{E})
be a Hermitian vector bundle over
X

\nabla^{L}
and
\nabla^{E}
, with curvatures
R^{L}:=(\nabla^{L})^{2}

(1.2)

\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}R^{L}=\omega.

We endow
(X,\omega)
with a
complete Riemannian metric

g^{TX}
. Let
J:TX\rightarrow TX
be an almost-complex structure on X compatible with
\omega
and
g^{TX}
. Let
J_{0}:TX\rightarrow TX
be the skew-adjoint linear map which satisfies

(1.3)

\omega(u,v)=g^{TX}(J_{0}u,v),\quad u,v\in TX,

then
J=J_{0}(-J_{0}^{2})^{-1/2}
. We suppose that
(X,L,E)
has
bounded geometry
: the injectivity radius
\inj^{X}
of
(X,g^{TX})
is positive and the derivatives of any order of
R^{L},R^{E},J,g^{TX}
are uniformly bounded on
X
. Let
\Delta^{L^{p}\otimes E}=(\nabla^{L^{p}\otimes E})^{*}\nabla^{L^{p}\otimes E}
denote the Bochner Laplacian (see (

\nabla^{L}
,
\nabla^{E}
and

(\nabla^{L^{p}\otimes E})^{*}

\nabla^{L^{p}\otimes E}

(1.4)

\Delta_{p}:=\Delta^{L^{p}\otimes E}-p\tau,\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\tau(x):=-\pi\tr\left[(JJ_{0})(x)\right],\quad x\in X,

the
renormalized Bochner Laplacian
acting on
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X,L^{p}\otimes E)

[

(1.5)

\mu_{0}:=\underset{x\in X,u\in T_{x}X\setminus\{0\}}{\inf}{\frac{\sqrt{-1}R_{x}^{L}(u,Ju)}{|u|_{g_{x}^{TX}}^{2}}}>0.

By
[

\spec(\Delta_{p})\subset[-C_{L},C_{L}]\cup[2p\mu_{0}-C_{L},+\infty[.

For

By the Schwartz kernel theorem,

has a distribution kernel with respect to the Riemannian volume form

In the above inequality the norm

,

(1.9)

\left|P_{p}(x,x)-\sum_{r=0}^{k}b_{r}(x)p^{n-r}\right|_{{\mathscr{C}^{l}}(X)}\leq C_{k,l}p^{n-k-1},

moreover if we denote by

As a particular case of Lemma
4.2
and Theorem
4.3
, our first result states as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
(X,g^{TX})
is complete,
(X,L,E)
has bounded geometry, i.e.,
\inj^{X}>0
and the derivatives of any order of
R^{L},R^{E},J,g^{TX}
are uniformly bounded on
X
, and assume (
1.5
). Let
W
be a compact domain of
X
with smooth boundary
V
and
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
a tubular neighborhood of
V
. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

There exist
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,
c>0
such that for
l\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
C_{l,W},C_{l,W}^{\prime},C_{l,W}^{\prime\prime}>0
such that for any
p\geq p_{0}
:

(a)

If
x,x^{\prime}\in X
,

(1.14)

|T_{W,p}(x,x^{\prime})|_{\mathscr{C}^{l}(X\times X)}\leq C_{l,W}p^{n+l/2}e^{-c\sqrt{p}d(x,x^{\prime})}.

(b)

If
x\in W\setminus V
,

(1.15)

|T_{W,p}(x,x)-P_{p}(x,x)|_{\mathscr{C}^{l}(X)}\leq C^{\prime}_{l,W}p^{n+l/2}e^{-c\sqrt{p}d(x,V)}.

(c)

If
x\in X\setminus W
,

(1.16)

|T_{W,p}(x,x)|_{\mathscr{C}^{l}(X)}\leq C^{\prime\prime}_{l,W}p^{n+l/2}e^{-c\sqrt{p}d(x,V)}.

(2)

There exist
\mathrm{End}(E)
-valued polynomials
q_{r,x}
with smooth coefficients in
x
such that for
x\in\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
, if
x\in V_{s}
for some
s\in]-\eta/2,\eta/2[
,

(1.17)

T_{W,p}(x,x)=P_{p}(x,x)\mathrm{erfc}\left(-s\sqrt{p\pi\alpha_{x}}\right)+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}p^{n-r/2}q_{r,x}(p^{1/2}s)\exp(-s^{2}p\pi\alpha_{x})+\mathcal{O}(p^{-\infty}),

with
\alpha_{x}:=\omega(e_{\mathfrak{n}},Je_{\mathfrak{n}})(x)
and
P_{p}(x,x)
has the asymptotics (
1.9
). In the particular case where
J=J_{0}
, we have
\alpha_{x}=1
.

The
complementary error function

\mathrm{erfc}
appearing in (
1.17
) is defined for
x\in\mathbb{R}
by the formulas

(1.18)

\mathrm{erfc}(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{x}^{+\infty}e^{-t^{2}}dt=\frac{1}{2}-\mathrm{erf}(x),\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\mathrm{erf}(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-t^{2}}dt,

where the function
\mathrm{erf}(x)
is called the error function.
The asymptotics we derive provide the following interpretation: in the semiclassical limit, the kernel
T_{W,p}(x,x)
on the diagonal mimics the behavior of the Bergman kernel for
x
in the interior of
W
. Conversely, on the complement
X\setminus W
,
T_{W,p}(x,x)
exhibits exponential decay in
\sqrt{p}
times the distance to the boundary. Formula (
1.17
) shows that the transition across the interface is universal. After rescaling by
p^{-1/2}
, the leading profile is always given by the complementary error function, independently of the geometry of
W
. The geometry only enters through the local coefficient
\alpha_{x}
. Furthermore, when
s\neq 0
, the sum in the right-hand side of (
1.17
) behaves as
\mathcal{O}(p^{-\infty})
and at
s=0
(where
x\in V
), half-integer powers of
p
emerge in the asymptotics of
T_{W,p}(x,x)
.

Remarks 1.2
.

We make the following observations:

(1)

For convenience, we intentionally left out a factor
2
in the definition of the functions
\mathrm{erf}
and
\mathrm{erfc}
in (
1.18
).

(2)

In our proof we do not really use the compactness assumption on
W
, and the statement of Theorem
1.1
should hold if we assume that the boundary
V
admits a tubular neighborhood
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
, and has bounded geometry.

(3)

The kernel asymptotics we derive exhibit behavior similar to that of the partial Bergman kernels as established by Zelditch and Zhou
[
24
]
, with "
\mathrm{erfc}
" asymptotics along the boundary
V
(see
[
24
, Main Theorem]
for comparison).

We also obtain the following kernel asymptotics for
g(T_{W,p})
when

is a polynomial vanishing at
0
and
1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
(X,g^{TX})
is complete,
(X,L,E)
has bounded geometry, i.e.,
\inj^{X}>0
and the derivatives of any order of
R^{L},R^{E},J,g^{TX}
are uniformly bounded on
X
, and assume (
1.5
). Let
W
be a compact domain of
X
with smooth boundary
V
and
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
a tubular neighborhood of
V
. Let
g
be a polynomial such that
g(0)=g(1)=0
.

(1)

There exist
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,
c>0
such that for
l\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
C_{l,g,W}>0
such that for any
p\geq p_{0}
, if
x\in X\setminus V
,

(1.19)

\left|g(T_{W,p})(x,x)\right|_{\mathscr{C}^{l}(X)}\leq C_{l,g,W}p^{n+l/2}e^{-c\sqrt{p}d(x,V)}.

(2)

There exist smooth
\mathrm{End}(E)
-valued functions
I_{r,g,x}(s)
, with fast decay of the form
\mathcal{O}((1+|s|)^{M_{r,g}}e^{-c^{\prime}s^{2}})
as
|s|\rightarrow\infty
, which depend smoothly on
x
and such that for
x\in\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
, if
x\in V_{s}
for some
s\in]-\eta/2,\eta/2[
,

(1.20)

g(T_{W,p})(x,x)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}p^{n-r/2}I_{r,g,x}(p^{1/2}s)+\mathcal{O}(p^{-\infty}),

and the above expansion is uniform on
\mathcal{U}_{\eta}
. Moreover we have

(1.21)

I_{0,g,x}(s)=\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(\mathrm{erfc}(y))\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\left(y+s\sqrt{2\pi\alpha_{x}}\right)^{2}}dy\right]\det\left({\dot{R}^{L}/2\pi}\right)\mathrm{Id}_{\mathrm{End}(E)},

and
\alpha_{x}=\omega(e_{\mathfrak{n}}(x),Je_{\mathfrak{n}}(x))
. If
s=0
, i.e. if
x\in V
in (
1.20
), we get

(1.22)

I_{0,g,x}(0)=\left[\int_{0}^{1}g(y)dy\right]\det\left({\dot{R}^{L}/2\pi}\right)\mathrm{Id}_{\mathrm{End}(E)}.

In the particular case where
J=J_{0}
, we have
\alpha_{x}=1
and
\det({\dot{R}^{L}/2\pi})=1
.

Note that the integral term in (
1.21
) is the convolution of
g\circ\mathrm{erfc}
and a Gaussian. Also note that by combining Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
1.3
and (
1.9
) we can get the on-diagonal expansion of
g(T_{W,p})
for

any polynomial.

From the above Theorem, we deduce asymptotics for the trace of
T_{W,p}
. For
g:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, let

(1.23)

\tilde{g}(x):=g(x)-g(0)(1-x)-g(1)x.

We define
I(g)
by

(1.24)

I(g):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tilde{g}\left(\mathrm{erfc}(x)\right)dx.

Let
C(V)
be the quantity defined by

(1.25)

C(V):=\int_{V}\bigg[{\det}(J_{0,x_{0}}|_{HV})\alpha_{x_{0}}\bigg]^{1/2}dv_{V}(x_{0}),

with
H_{x_{0}}V=T_{x_{0}}V\cap J_{x_{0}}T_{x_{0}}V
the maximal complex subspace of
T_{x_{0}}V
, and
\alpha_{x_{0}}=\omega(e_{\mathfrak{n}},Je_{\mathfrak{n}})(x_{0})
. As a corollary of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
, the third result of this paper states as follows.

Corollary 1.4
.

Assume
(X,g^{TX})
is complete,
(X,L,E)
has bounded geometry, i.e.,
\inj^{X}>0
and the derivatives of any order of
R^{L},R^{E},J,g^{TX}
are uniformly bounded on
X
, and assume (
1.5
). Let
W
be a compact domain of
X
with smooth boundary
V
. Let
g:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a polynomial vanishing at
0
.
We have the expansion

(1.26)

\displaystyle\tr(g(T_{W,p}))=\sum_{k\geq 0}p^{n-k/2}a_{k}(g)+\mathcal{O}(p^{-\infty}),\quad\mathrm{with}

\displaystyle a_{0}(g)=g(1)\rank(E)\int_{W}\det\left(\dot{R}^{L}/2\pi\right)dv_{X},\quad a_{1}(g)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\rank(E)C(V)I(g).

In particular if
J_{0}=J
, then
\alpha_{x_{0}}=1
,
C(V)=\vol(V)
,
a_{0}(g)=g(1)\rank(E)\vol(W)
and
a_{1}(g)=\rank(E)\vol(V)I(g)
.

As a consequence of Corollary
1.4
we obtain the following Weyl law for the Toeplitz operator
T_{W,p}
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Assume
(X,g^{TX})
is complete,
(X,L,E)
has bounded geometry, i.e.,
\inj^{X}>0
and the derivatives of any order of
R^{L},R^{E},J,g^{TX}
are uniformly bounded on
X
, and assume (
1.5
). Let
W
be a compact domain of
X
with smooth boundary
V
. If
g:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous, Hölder continuous at
0
and
1
and
g(0)=0
, we have

(1.27)

\tr(g(T_{W,p}))=a_{0}(g)p^{n}+a_{1}(g)p^{n-1/2}+o(p^{n-1/2}),

and for
0<a<b<1
, the concentration of eigenvalues of
T_{W,p}
in
[a,b]
verifies

(1.28)

\Big|\spec(T_{W,p})\cap[a,b]\Big|=p^{n-1/2}\frac{\rank(E)C(V)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}(\mathrm{erfc}^{-1}(a)-\mathrm{erfc}^{-1}(b))+o(p^{n-1/2}).

Moreover, we have, for
0<\varepsilon<1/2
,

(1.29)

\Big|\spec(T_{W,p})\cap[1-\varepsilon,1]\Big|=p^{n}\rank(E)\int_{W}\det\left(\dot{R}^{L}/2\pi\right)dv_{X}+o(p^{n}).

In particular if
J_{0}=J
, then
C(V)=\vol(V)
and
\det(\dot{R}^{L}/2\pi)=1
.

The above corollary shows that the operator
T_{W,p}
asymptotically behaves like a projection: the spectrum of
T_{W,p}
concentrates near
0
and

Lemma 2.1
.

For

T_{\alpha}^{s}(z,z^{\prime})=P_{\alpha}(z,z^{\prime})\mathrm{erfc}\left(\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\left(s-\frac{z-\overline{z}^{\prime}}{2i}\right)\right),

(2.8)

T_{\alpha}^{s,c}(z,z^{\prime})=P_{\alpha}(z,z^{\prime})\mathrm{erfc}\left(-\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\left(s-\frac{z-\overline{z}^{\prime}}{2i}\right)\right).

Proof.

Let
F_{a}
and
G

(2.9)

F_{a}(z):=\int_{a}^{+\infty}\exp(-\pi\alpha y^{2}-\pi\alpha yz)dy,\quad G(z):=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\exp(-\pi\alpha x^{2}-\pi\alpha xz)dx.

Notice

(2.10)

F_{a}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\exp\left(\pi\alpha t^{2}/4\right)\mathrm{erfc}\left(\sqrt{\pi\alpha}(a+t/2)\right),\quad G(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\exp\left(\pi\alpha t^{2}/4\right).

By the identity theorem, for

we have

(2.11)

F_{a}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\exp\left(\pi\alpha z^{2}/4\right)\mathrm{erfc}\left(\sqrt{\pi\alpha}(a+z/2)\right),\quad G(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\exp\left(\pi\alpha z^{2}/4\right).

By definition we have

(2.12)

T_{\alpha}^{s}(z,z^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{H}_{s}}P_{\alpha}(z,z^{\prime\prime})P_{\alpha}(z^{\prime\prime},z^{\prime})d\lambda(z^{\prime\prime}),

where

(2.13)

T_{\alpha}^{s}(z,z^{\prime})=\alpha^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}(|z|^{2}+|z^{\prime}|^{2})\right)\\
\times\int_{\mathrm{Im}(z^{\prime\prime})>s}\exp\left(-\pi\alpha|z^{\prime\prime}|^{2}+\pi\alpha z\overline{z}^{\prime\prime}+\pi\alpha z^{\prime\prime}\overline{z}^{\prime}\right)d\lambda(z^{\prime\prime}).

By writing

By (
2.11

G(-(z+\overline{z}^{\prime}))F_{s}(i(z-\overline{z}^{\prime}))=\alpha^{-1}\exp(\pi\alpha z\overline{z}^{\prime})\mathrm{erfc}\left(\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\left(s-\frac{z-\overline{z}^{\prime}}{2i}\right)\right),

\mathrm{erfc}(-z)=1-\mathrm{erfc}(z)

Corollary 2.3
.

Let

be a polynomial in
\sqrt{\pi\alpha}z,\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\overline{z}
and

and

of degree less than

(2.21)

T_{\alpha,f}(z,z^{\prime})=f(\sqrt{\pi\alpha}z,\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\overline{z}^{\prime})P_{\alpha}(z,z^{\prime}),

(2.22)

T^{s}_{\alpha,f}(z,z^{\prime})=T_{\alpha,f}(z,z^{\prime})\mathrm{erfc}\left(\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\left(s-\frac{z-\overline{z}^{\prime}}{2i}\right)\right)\\
+Q(f)(\sqrt{\pi\alpha}z,\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\overline{z}^{\prime},\sqrt{\pi\alpha}s)P_{\alpha}(z,z^{\prime})\exp\left(-\pi\alpha\left(s-\frac{z-\overline{z}^{\prime}}{2i}\right)^{2}\right),

(2.23)

T^{s,c}_{\alpha,f}(z,z^{\prime})=T_{\alpha,f}(z,z^{\prime})\mathrm{erfc}\left(-\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\left(s-\frac{z-\overline{z}^{\prime}}{2i}\right)\right)\\
-Q(f)(\sqrt{\pi\alpha}z,\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\overline{z}^{\prime},\sqrt{\pi\alpha}s)P_{\alpha}(z,z^{\prime})\exp\left(-\pi\alpha\left(s-\frac{z-\overline{z}^{\prime}}{2i}\right)^{2}\right).

Remark 2.4
.

The formula (
2.21
) is a particular case of the functional calculus developed by Ma–Marinescu as in
[

, §7.1]
. In our case we will use the formulas (

) and (

). The error function term
\mathrm{erfc}
appearing after integration over the half plane

poses a challenge when trying to compute the kernel of the composition

, with

polynomials in

,
\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\overline{z}

2.2.
Exponential decay in
s

From the previous computations, we notice that the kernels of the Toeplitz operators

have a form of exponential decay in

(0,0)

As in

By (

(2.25)

\mathrm{erfc}(z)=\mathrm{erfc}(x)-e^{-x^{2}}\int_{0}^{y}e^{u^{2}}\sin(2xu)du-ie^{-x^{2}}\int_{0}^{y}e^{u^{2}}\cos(2xu)du.

For

we have the inequality

(2.26)

|\mathrm{erfc(z)}|\leq e^{-x^{2}}\left(1+2|y|e^{y^{2}}\right),

which gives if

\left|\mathrm{erfc}\left(\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\left(s-\frac{z-\overline{z}^{\prime}}{2i}\right)\right)\right|\\
\leq\left[1+\sqrt{\pi\alpha}|x-x^{\prime}|\exp\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{4}(x-x^{\prime})^{2}\right)\right]\exp\left(-\pi\alpha\left(s-\frac{y+y^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{2}\right),

on the other hand if
x\leq 0
,
\mathrm{erfc}(-x)\leq e^{-x^{2}}
, therefore if
y+y^{\prime}\geq 2s
,

(2.28)

\left|\mathrm{erfc}\left(-\sqrt{\pi\alpha}\left(s-\frac{z-\overline{z}^{\prime}}{2i}\right)\right)\right|\\
\leq\left[1+\sqrt{\pi\alpha}|x-x^{\prime}|\exp\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{4}(x-x^{\prime})^{2}\right)\right]\exp\left(-\pi\alpha\left(s-\frac{y+y^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{2}\right).

We also have the following inequality on the Bergman kernel

(2.29)

|P_{\alpha}(z,z^{\prime})|\leq\alpha\exp\left(-\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}(x-x^{\prime})^{2}-\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}(y-y^{\prime})^{2}\right),

which gets us if

|T_{\alpha}^{s}(z,z^{\prime})|\leq\alpha\left(1+\sqrt{\pi\alpha}|z-z^{\prime}|\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\pi\alpha}{4}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}\right)\exp\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{4}\left(s-y+s-y^{\prime}\right)^{2}\right),

and if
y+y^{\prime}\geq 2s
,

(2.31)

|T_{\alpha}^{s,c}(z,z^{\prime})|\leq\alpha\left(1+\sqrt{\pi\alpha}|z-z^{\prime}|\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\pi\alpha}{4}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\pi\alpha}{4}\left(s-y+s-y^{\prime}\right)^{2}\right).

In the same way by Corollary
2.3
we get the following result.

.

For
f
polynomial there exist

,

such that for

\times\exp\left(-\frac{\pi\alpha}{4}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\pi\alpha}{4}(s-y+s-y^{\prime})^{2}\right),

If

,

(2.33)

\left|T_{\alpha,f}^{s,c}(z,z^{\prime})\right|\leq\alpha C\left(1+\sqrt{\pi\alpha}|z|+\sqrt{\pi\alpha}|z^{\prime}|+\sqrt{\pi\alpha}|s|\right)^{M_{f}}\\

\left|T_{\alpha,f}^{s,c}(z,z^{\prime})\right|\leq\alpha Ce^{-\pi\alpha s^{2}}\left(1+\sqrt{\pi\alpha}|z|+\sqrt{\pi\alpha}|z^{\prime}|+\sqrt{\pi\alpha}|s|\right)^{M_{f}}\exp\left(-\frac{\pi\alpha}{4}|z-z^{\prime}|^{2}\right).

We could still get a version of Corollary

This section is organized as follows. In Sect.

We quickly recall the context of
[

as in the introduction. Let

and

satisfies the prequantization condition (

We endow

with a Riemannian metric

with Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{TX}
, and curvature
R^{TX}:=(\nabla^{TX})^{2}
. Let
J:TX\rightarrow TX
be an almost-complex structure on

compatible with
\omega
and

, that is
\omega(J\cdot,J\cdot)=\omega(\cdot,\cdot)

commutes with

(see

has

\inj^{X}>0
and the derivatives of any order of

(3.1)

\mu_{0}:=\underset{x\in X,u\in T_{x}X\setminus\{0\}}{\inf}{\frac{\sqrt{-1}R_{x}^{L}(u,Ju)}{|u|_{g_{x}^{TX}}^{2}}}>0.

Let
\Delta^{L^{p}\otimes E}

\Delta^{L^{p}\otimes E}:=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[(\nabla^{L^{p}\otimes E}_{e_{i}})^{2}-\nabla^{L^{p}\otimes E}_{\nabla^{TX}_{e_{i}}e_{i}}\right],

defined as in (

1.6

Remarks 3.1
.

The asymptotic expansion of the Bergman kernel has a long history:

(ii)

3.1.
Off-diagonal asymptotics of the generalized Bergman kernel

In
[
7
, Theorem 5]
, Dai–Liu–Ma established a full off-diagonal expansion for the Bergman kernel of the
\mathrm{spin}^{c}
-Dirac operator. In
[
17
, Theorem 1.19]
, Ma–Marinescu obtained an expansion for the generalized Bergman kernel in a neighborhood of size
\varepsilon/\sqrt{p}
of the diagonal, called near off-diagonal expansion. This result has been improved by Kordyukov–Ma–Marinescu in
[

to an off-diagonal expansion similar to the one of Dai–Liu–Ma. We recall the result of Kordyukov–Ma–Marinescu in this paragraph.

Let

. For

,

,

and their duals, tensor products and exterior products are identified to

and

by parallel transport along the curve

for the connections
\nabla^{E}
,
\nabla^{L}
and
\nabla^{TX}
and the connections they induce. Under this identification there exists a function

such that the localized volume form

and where

denotes the Riemannian volume form of

. Still under this identification, the localized Bergman kernel

becomes a smooth function mapping

to an element of
(L^{p}\otimes E)_{x_{0}}\otimes(L^{p}\otimes E)^{*}_{x_{0}}\simeq\mathbb{C}\otimes\End(E)_{x_{0}}
.
Let
\mathcal{J}_{x_{0}}
be the endomorphism

(3.4)

\mathcal{J}_{x_{0}}=-2\pi\sqrt{-1}J_{0}(x_{0}),

then
\mathcal{J}_{x_{0}}:T^{(1,0)}_{x_{0}}X\rightarrow T^{(1,0)}_{x_{0}}X

(3.5)

\mathcal{P}_{x_{0}}(Z,Z^{\prime}):=\frac{\det_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{J}_{x_{0}}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4}\left\langle{(\mathcal{J}_{x_{0}}^{2})^{1/2}(Z-Z^{\prime})},{(Z-Z^{\prime})}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle{\mathcal{J}_{x_{0}}Z},{Z^{\prime}}\right\rangle\right).

\mathcal{L}
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. By
[
12
, Theorem 4.3]
, we have the following off-diagonal expansion for the generalized Bergman kernel, as an analogue of
[
7
, Theorem 4.18]
.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
12
, Theorem 4.3]
)
.

There exists
\varepsilon\in]0,\inj^{X}[
such that for any

(3.6)

\sup_{|\alpha|+|\alpha^{\prime}|\leq l}\left|\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|+|\alpha^{\prime}|}}{\partial Z^{\alpha}\partial Z^{\prime\alpha^{\prime}}}\left(p^{-n}P_{p,x_{0}}(Z,Z^{\prime})-\sum_{r=0}^{j}\mathcal{F}_{r,x_{0}}(\sqrt{p}Z,\sqrt{p}Z^{\prime})\kappa_{x_{0}}^{-1/2}(Z)\kappa_{x_{0}}^{-1/2}(Z^{\prime})p^{-r/2}\right)\right|_{\mathscr{C}^{l^{\prime}}(X)}\\

We integrate (

In this paragraph we prove the technical result of Lemma

(4.66)

J_{q}(s):=\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\rm Im}(z_{1}),\ldots,{\rm Im}(z_{q})>-s\\
{\rm Im}(z_{q+1})<-s\end{subarray}}\exp\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\left\langle{MZ},{Z}\right\rangle\right)d\lambda(z_{1})\ldots d\lambda(z_{q+1}),

Let
A^{\prime}
,
B^{\prime}
be the
(q+1)\times(q+1)
matrices defined by

(4.67)

A^{\prime}:=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&&0\\
-1&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&-1\\
0&&-1&2\\
\end{pmatrix},\qquad B^{\prime}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&&0\\
-1&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&1\\

Notice

X=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{q+1})

Hence the equality

(4.69)

\displaystyle J_{q}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}y_{1},\ldots,y_{q}>-s\\
y_{q+1}<-s\end{subarray}}\int_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{q+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{q+1}}\exp\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}(\left\langle{A^{\prime}X},{X}\right\rangle+\left\langle{A^{\prime}Y},{Y}\right\rangle)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{q+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{q+1}}\exp\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}\left\langle{A^{\prime}X},{X}\right\rangle\right]\exp\left[i\pi\left\langle{X},{B^{\prime}Y}\right\rangle\right]d\lambda(X)
