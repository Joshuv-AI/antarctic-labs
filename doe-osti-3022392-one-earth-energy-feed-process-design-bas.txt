EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This report establishes the process design basis for the front-end engineering design (FEED) of a
carbon capture and injection facility at One Earth Energy's (OEE) ethanol production plant in Gibson
City, Illinois, developed as part of the Illinois Storage Corridor CarbonSAFE Phase III project.
The facility is designed to compress and dehydrate up to approximately 458,000 metric tonnes of CO2
per year, sourced directly from OEE's ethanol fermenters, for permanent injection into a saline aquifer
approximately four miles from the plant. At normal operating conditions, the system will process
1,290 metric tonnes of CO2 per day, assuming 355 operating days per year and an ethanol production
rate of 160 million gallons per year.
The proposed process trains a multistage centrifugal blower with a five-stage reciprocating
compressor, delivering CO2 to the injection wellhead at up to 1,500 psig. Triethylene glycol (TEG)
dehydration, applied after the fourth compression stage, reduces water content to a target of 10
lb/MMscf, well within the 30 lb/MMscf injection limit. Beyond dehydration, no additional treatment
is required; trace impurities including oxygen and nitrogen will remain in the injected stream.
Key design considerations include the absence of spare cooling tower capacity at the site,
necessitating new cooling infrastructure, and the need for a new electrical substation to support large
motor loads. The facility is designed for continuous, largely unattended operation, monitored around
the clock by existing OEE operations staff.
This document serves as the foundational reference for all subsequent detailed engineering activities
associated with the OEE CO2 injection facility.

INTRODUCTION
This document defines the overall functional design requirements of the surface facilities for
compressing and dehydrating up to ~458,000 metric tons (tonnes) per year of carbon dioxide (CO2)
from the One Earth Energy, LLC (OEE) ethanol production facility in Gibson City, IL. This also
equates to 1,290 tonnes/day of CO2 based on 355 operating days/year. It is based on an ethanol
production rate of 160 MM gallons/year. The CO2 will be injected into a local saline aquifer
formation, located approximately 4 miles from the ethanol plant. Sampling data from the CO2 stream
leaving the ethanol plant (August 2019) show that the oxygen (O2) concentration ranges from 14
ppmv to 1,300 ppmv. The plant will be designed to meet injection-grade CO2 specifications without
the removal of oxygen from the source CO2. For this reason, the basis for the OEE facility is to use
compression to boost the pressure to injection conditions and triethylene glycol (TEG) dehydration
to remove water from the CO2 prior to injection.
This document serves as the starting point for the front end engineering design (FEED) of the CO2
injection plant and contains information on the CO2 stream properties from the OEE ethanol facility
and injection site that are known at the time of writing.
This document is organized into the following sections:

General Facility Design
This design utilizes a multistage centrifugal blower located near the fermenter that compresses the
feed gas up to approximately 15 psig. The feed gas is further compressed by a five-stage reciprocating
compressor to the required injection pressure. Between stages of the reciprocating compressor, the
gas stream is cooled and condensed water is removed from the gas stream. After the 4th stage of
compression, the gas stream flows through a packed tower that contacts the gas with triethylene glycol
(TEG). At this pressure, the water content in CO2 is relatively low, which reduces capital and
operating costs of the dehydration unit, while the solubility of TEG in CO2 is still low enough for
economic operation. TEG losses would be too high in the CO2 at the outlet of the 5th stage. The TEG
absorbs water vapor present in the gas stream and dehydrates the gas to an extent that it is suitable
for downstream equipment to be constructed from carbon steel.

 2

The CO2 produced in this process is not suitable for further use beyond injection and other than water,
it retains most of the impurities present in the source CO2 including any entrained O2.
The major rotating equipment and associated equipment (such as separators and heat exchangers) will
be located indoors. Dehydration equipment may be located indoors or outdoors, depending upon
OEE’s preference, but all liquid containing vessels and outdoor lines will require heat tracing and
insulation. The low pressure blower and associated knockouts will be located close to the fermenters
so that condensed water can be reused in the ethanol plant. The compression trains will be located on
the northwest corner of the facility, just north of the peaking plant. The facility will be located in a
non-classified area.

CO2 Production Specifications
The production of injection grade CO2 only requires the removal of water prior to injection. The water
limit in the injected CO2 is 30 lb/MMscf (633 ppmv), but a typical treatment specification of 10
lb/MMscf (211 ppmv) will be used. Other impurities in the source CO2, including oxygen, are not
removed at the facility.

Process Capacity and Delivery Requirements
The injection and dehydration system will be sized to process the entire feed stream from the OEE
plant fermenters, but this does not equate to recovering 100% of the CO2 fed to the facility. There
will be minor CO2 losses throughout the process. Some CO2 losses to atmosphere that may take place
in the facility include: the dehydration unit flash gas and regeneration streams that will be vented to
the atmosphere and the CO2 dissolved in the water that is removed in the compressor inter-stages;
however, the quantity of CO2 in these areas should be low. A small amount of CO2 will also be
required for blower seal gas.

Process Control and Monitoring Requirements
The CO2 processing and injection facility will be controlled by the existing control system in the OEE
facility and monitored by operations staff around the clock. It is likely that a custody transfer quality
flow meter will be required on the injection outlet of the facility. Other instrumentation will be
standard pressure, temperature, and flow instruments.
There is no need to control the flow, temperature, or pressure of the compressed and dehydrated CO2
to specifications outside of the values listed in Table 5. In other words, flow or pressure tests at certain
conditions will not be required and the facility will just compress and dehydrate the CO2 available
from the fermenters during normal operations.

Reliability and Operability Requirements
Major rotating equipment will not be spared as part of this project. Minor higher-maintenance
equipment like cooling water pumps and filters that are regularly changed out will be spared. Critical
instrumentation may also be spared as required but should be a minor cost for this project and will
not be included at this early phase of design.
The CO2 processing and injection equipment will be designed to operate unattended and should only
require monitoring by OEE personnel and occasional intervention from plant personnel. Routine
maintenance items may include filter change outs on rotating equipment and the dehydration unit,
addition of oil to compression systems, and periodic sampling.
