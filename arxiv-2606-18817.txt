Simultaneous determination of an unknown bending moment and shear force in the Euler–Bernoulli cantilever beam from measured boundary deflection and slope Journal: Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing
Alemdar Hasanov, Onur Baysal

Abstract

1
Introduction

Atomic force microscopy (AFM) is an indispensable tool in crucial processes such as imaging, manipulation, and lithography at the nanometer scale. Invented by Binning and coworkers in 1986
[
1
]
, this instrument has been subsequently improved through various enhancements
[
2
,
3
]
. In scanning processes for measuring surface topography and material properties with AFM, the most fundamental element is a sharp tip that moves across the surface of the sample. To understand the interaction forces between the tip and the sample, adequate mathematical modeling with complete vibration analysis of the AFM cantilever is necessary
[
4
]
. In this context, investigating the moment and shear force acting on the micro-cantilever tip is among the primary problems in studying the dynamic operation of AFM
[
5
]
.

The tip is located at the free end of an AFM micro-cantilever, and vibration begins as a result of the dynamic interaction forces between this tip and the sample. This vibration creates a transverse shear force and a bending moment on the tip of the cantilever
[
6
]
. Estimating the unknown shear force and bending moment allows us to have a better interpretation and understanding of the scan results. However, these forces can only be measured indirectly through a laser-based sensor system. For this reason, the problem of determining the unknown bending moment and shear force from experimentally measured deflection or/and slope, based on a suitable mathematical model, has been one of the most challenging inverse problems that has been continuously studied for over a quarter of a century (see
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
and references therein).

A common feature of the previous studies is that almost all of them address inverse problems involving the determination of a single input, such as the shear force, from a single observed output, such as the measured deflection at the tip of a cantilever. These problems are classified as inverse problems that are governed by the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map, according to the commonly used terminology. These types of problems can also be specified as inverse problems based on one unknown input and one measured output. The inverse problem of determining both the bending moment
M(t)
and the shear force
g(t)
from the measured deflection at the end of the AFM cantilever is considered in
[
12
]
. However, since a cone-shaped cantilever type is involved here, there is the relationship
M(t)=-\left(2h\cos\theta/\pi\right)\,g(t)
between the inputs
M(t)
and
g(t)
, where
h,\,\theta>0
are the tip length and half-conic angle, respectively. Hence, the bending moment
M(t)
here cannot be considered an independent unknown input.

In this study, a mathematical model within the Euler-Bernoulli theory is proposed for the simultaneous identification of two fundamental inputs, the bending moment and the shear force, affecting the tip of an AFM cantilever.
The most feasible data, the measured deflection and slope at the tip of the cantilever, are assumed as measured outputs. Since the deflection and slope at the tip of the AFM cantilever are experimentally measurable data, it is evident that the proposed model has a wide range of applications. In particular, the cantilever beam, examined in this article, whose free end is subjected to bending moment and shear force, has been the subject of many studies because it is an important part of numerous machine systems (see, for instance,
[
13
]
and references therein). This inverse problem was first examined in
[
14
]
for a simple beam model with constant coefficients and without damping term, where detailed numerical solution steps based on the Conjugate Gradient Algorithm (CGA) were presented. On the other hand, no further study has been reported in the literature for a more general beam model or mathematical analysis of the considered inverse problem. Our aim is to fill this gap. Therefore, the present study serves as a continuation of the work in
[
14
]
.

Note that the Euler-Bernoulli theory is the most appropriate model to predict the cantilever kinematics during snap-to-contact event
[
15
]
.

The paper is structured as follows. The mathematical model of dynamic vibration resulting from the effect of the bending moment and the shear force at the tip of the AFM cantilever is described in Section 2. A priori estimates for the weak and regular weak solutions of the forward problem are derived in Section 3. In Section 4, the input-output operators corresponding to inverse problem are introduced. Here, some properties of these operators are also analyzed. In Section 5 the Tikhonov functional is introduced and existence of a quasi solution is proved. The Fréchet derivative in vector form of the Tikhonov functional through a suitable adjoint problem is derived in Section 6.
In Section 7, the Lipschitz continuity of the Fréchet gradient is proved.
Some conclusions are discussed in the final Section 8.

\mathcal{G}(0,T)=\{g\in H^{2}(0,T):~\|g\|_{H^{2}(0,T)}\leq C_{\mathcal{Q}}\},\end{array}\right.

where
C_{\mathcal{Q}}>0
is a constant independent of
M(t)
and
g(t)
. Further, we define set
\mathcal{Q}(0,T)\subset H^{2}(0,T)\times H^{2}(0,T)
consisting of pairs of sets of admissible inputs:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(0,T):=\{q(t):=\langle M(t),g(t)\rangle:\,M\in\mathcal{M}(0,T),\,g\in\mathcal{G}(0,T)\},

(48)

where
\mathcal{M}(0,T)
and
\mathcal{G}(0,T)
are the sets of admissible inputs introduced in (20).

Denote by
u(x,t;q)
the unique weak solution of the forward problem (
2
) corresponding to this input
q\in\mathcal{Q}(0,T)
. Then
u(\ell,t;q)
and
u_{x}(\ell,t;q)
are the outputs. This naturally leads to introducing the following input-output operators:

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi(q)(t):=u(\ell,t;q),~q\in\mathcal{Q}(0,T),~\Phi:q\mapsto L^{2}(0,T);\\[5.0pt]
\Psi(q)(t):=u_{x}(\ell,t;q),~q\in\mathcal{Q}(0,T),~\Psi:q\mapsto L^{2}(0,T),\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{Q}(0,T)
is the set introduced in (
48
).

According to the widely used terminology of inverse problems, these input-output operators (
4
) can alternatively be described as Neumann-to-Dirichlet maps.

In view of the above input-output operators, the inverse problem (
2
)-(
2
) can be reformulated as the following system of operator equations:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi(q)(t)=w_{\ell}(t),~w_{\ell}\in L^{2}(0,T),\\[4.0pt]
\Psi(q)(t)=\theta_{\ell}(t),~\theta_{\ell}\in L^{2}(0,T),~q\in\mathcal{Q}(0,T).\end{array}\right.

From the following lemma it follows that the considered inverse problem is ill-posed.

Lemma 1

Assume that conditions of Theorem
2
are satisfied. Then the input-output operators introduced in (
4
) are compact operators.

Proof.
Let
\{q^{(m)}\}
be a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{Q}(0,T)
. Therefore, inputs
\{M^{(m)}\}\subset\mathcal{M}(0,T)
and
\{g^{(m)}\}\subset\mathcal{G}(0,T)
are bounded sequences in
H^{2}(0,T)
. Denote by
\{\Phi(q^{(m)})\}
and
\{\Psi(q^{(m)})\}
the sequences of outputs:
\Phi(q^{(m)})(t)=u(\ell,t;q^{(m)})
and
\Psi(q^{(m)})(t)=u_{x}(\ell,t;q^{(m)})
,
m=1,2,3,\,...~
.
From the estimates (
1
) and (
1
), it follows that these sequences are bounded in the norm of the Sobolev space
H^{1}(0,T)
, and hence compact in
L^{2}(0,T)
due to Rellich-Kandrachov compactness theorem
[
18
]
. This means that the input-output operators transform bounded sequences
\{M^{(m)}\}
and
\{g^{(m)}\}
in
H^{2}(0,T)
to the compact sequences
\{\Phi(M^{(m)})\}
, and
\{\Psi(g^{(m)})\}
in
L^{2}(0,T)
, respectively. Hence, these operators are compact.
\Box

Next lemma shows that the input-output operators are also Lipschitz continuous.

Lemma 2

Under the conditions of Theorem
1
, the input-output operators introduced in (
48
) are Lipschitz continuous, that is,

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\|\Phi(q_{1})-\Phi(q_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq L_{1}\|q_{1}-q_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(0,T)},\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\|\Psi(q_{1})-\Psi(q_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq L_{2}\|q_{1}-q_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(0,T)},~\forall q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}(0,T),\end{array}\right.

where
L_{1}=\ell\sqrt{\ell}\,C_{1}/\sqrt{2},\,L_{2}=\sqrt{\ell}\,C_{1}>0
are the Lipschitz constants, and
C_{1}>0
is the constant introduced in (
3
).

Proof.
By the definition (
4
) of the input-output operators,

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Phi(q_{1})(t)-\Phi(q_{2})(t)=\delta u(\ell,t),\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\Psi(q_{1})(t)-\Psi(q_{2})(t)=\delta u_{x}(\ell,t),\end{array}\right.

where
\delta u(\ell,t)=u(\ell,t;q_{1})-u(\ell,t;q_{2})
, with
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}(0,T)
, is the solution of the following initial boundary value problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho(x)\delta u_{tt}+\mu(x)\delta u_{t}+\left(r(x)\delta u_{xx}\right)_{xx}=0,~(x,t)\in\Omega_{T};\\[4.0pt]
\delta u(x,0)=\delta u_{t}(x,0)=0,\,x\in(0,\ell)\,;\\[4.0pt]
\delta u(0,t)=\delta u_{x}(0,t)=0,~\left(r(x)\delta u_{xx}\right)_{x=\ell}=\delta M(t),~\left(-(r(x)\delta u_{xx})_{x}\right)_{x=\ell}=\delta g(t),\end{array}\right.

with the inputs
\delta M(t)=M_{1}(t)-M_{2}(t)
and
\delta g(t)=g_{1}(t)-g_{2}(t)
.

We employ the trace inequalities (
1
), applied to the weak solution of problem (
4
), to estimate the norms:

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\|\Phi(q_{1})-\Phi(q_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}=\|\delta u(\ell,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,T)},\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\|\Psi(q_{1})-\Psi(q_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}=\|\delta u_{x}(\ell,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\end{array}\right.

We have:

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\|\Phi(q_{1})-\Phi(q_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq\frac{\ell\sqrt{\ell}}{\sqrt{2}}\,C_{1}\,\|\delta q\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(0,T)},\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\|\Psi(q_{1})-\Psi(q_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq\sqrt{\ell}\,C_{1}\,\|\delta q\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(0,T)}.\end{array}\right.

These estimates lead to the required inequalities (
2
).
\Box

5
The Tikhonov functional and existence of a quasi-solution

In practice, exact equalities in the system of equations (
4
) are not achievable due to random noise in the measured outputs
w_{\ell}(t)
and
\theta_{\ell}(t)
. As a consequence, one needs to introduce the Tikhonov functional

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(q):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left[\Phi(q)(t)-w_{\ell}(t)\right]^{2}dt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left[\Psi(q)(t)-\theta_{\ell}(t)\right]^{2}dt\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\qquad\equiv\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left[u(\ell,t;q)-w_{\ell}(t)\right]^{2}dt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left[u_{x}(\ell,t;q)-\theta_{\ell}(t)\right]^{2}dt\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{J}_{1}(q)+\mathcal{J}_{2}(q),~q\in\mathcal{Q}(0,T),\qquad\quad

(65)

assuming that the measured outputs satisfy the following conditions:

\displaystyle w_{\ell},\theta_{\ell}\in L^{2}(0,T).

(66)

Consider the following minimization problem for this functional:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(q_{*})=\inf_{q\in\mathcal{Q}(0,T)}\mathcal{J}(q).

(67)

A solution of the minimization problem (
67
) is called a quasi-solution of the inverse source problem (
2
)-(
2
), due to
[
19
]
.

The lemma below shows that the Lipschitz continuity property of the input-output operators yields the same property of the Tikhonov functional.

Lemma 3

Under the conditions of Theorem
1
, the Tikhonov functional (
5
) is Lipschitz continuous, that is,

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\mathcal{J}(q_{1})-\mathcal{J}(q_{2})|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq L_{\mathcal{J}}\,\|q_{1}-q_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(0,T)},~\forall q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}(0,T),

(68)

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{J}}=\sqrt{2}\,C_{\mathcal{Q}}\,\left(L_{1}^{2}+L_{2}^{2}\right)+L_{1}\|w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+L_{2}\|\theta_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)},

is the Lipschitz constant,
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are the positive constants introduced in Lemma
2
.

Proof.
The following inequalities are obtained using the method employed in the proof of Lemma 11.2.3 in
[
16
]
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\mathcal{J}_{1}(q_{1})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1}(q_{2})|\quad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\left(u(\ell,t;q_{1})-w_{\ell}(t)\right)^{2}dt-\int_{0}^{T}\left(u(\ell,t;q_{2})-w_{\ell}(t)\right)^{2}dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left|\Phi(q_{1})-\Phi(q_{2})\right|\cdot\left|\Phi(q_{1})+\Phi(q_{2})-2\,w_{\ell}\right|dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left|\Phi(q_{1})-\Phi(q_{2})\right|^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\,\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left|\Phi(q_{1})+\Phi(q_{2})-2\,w_{\ell}\right|^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\Phi(q_{1})-\Phi(q_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\,\left(\|\Phi(q_{1})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|\Phi(q_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+2\,\|w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\left(\|\Phi(q_{1})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|\Phi(q_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+2\,\|w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\right)\,\|\Phi(q_{1})-\Phi(q_{1})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

Due to (
1
),

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\mathcal{J}_{1}(q_{1})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1}(q_{2})|\quad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\left[\frac{\ell\sqrt{\ell}}{\sqrt{2}}C_{1}\left(\|q_{1}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(0,T)}+\|q_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(0,T)}\right)+2\,\|w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\right]\,\|\Phi(q_{1})-\Phi(q_{1})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

Since
\|q_{i}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(0,T)}=\left(\|M\|^{2}_{H^{1}(0,T)}+\|g\|^{2}_{H^{1}(0,T)}\right)^{1/2}\leq\sqrt{2}\,C_{\mathcal{Q}}
for
i=1,2
and (
2
),

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\mathcal{J}_{1}(q_{1})-\mathcal{J}_{1}(q_{2})|\leq\left[L_{1}^{2}\,\sqrt{2}\,C_{\mathcal{Q}}+L_{1}\,\|w_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\right]\,\|q_{1}-q_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(0,T)}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\mathcal{J}_{2}(q_{1})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{2}(q_{2})|\quad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\left[\|\Psi(q_{1})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|\Psi(q_{2})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+2\|\theta_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\right]\|\Psi(q_{1})-\Psi(q_{1})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[L_{2}^{2}\,\sqrt{2}\,C_{\mathcal{Q}}+L_{2}\,\|\theta_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\right]\,\|q_{1}-q_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(0,T)}.

Finally, using (
5
), proof is completed.
\Box

This property of the Tikhonov functional provides the fundamental basis for the existence of a quasi-solution to the inverse problem (
2
)-(
2
).

Theorem 3

Assume that the conditions of Theorem
1
ensuring the existence of a weak solution of the forward problem hold. Suppose, in addition, that the measured outputs satisfy the conditions (
66
). Then the minimization problem (
67
) for the Tikhonov functional (
5
) has at least one solution in the set of admissible inputs
\mathcal{Q}(0,T)
. Hence, the inverse source problem (

)-(

The proof of this theorem follows the same procedure as that of Theorem 10.1.11 in
[

]
.

6
Fréchet differentiability of the Tikhonov functional

In this section, we introduce first the formal Fréchet gradient formula for the Tikhonov
functional through a weak solution of the related (unique) adjoint problem corresponding to the inverse problem (
2
)-(
2
). Then, we will establish the sufficient conditions, including the conditions imposed on the measured outputs
w_{\ell}(t)
and
\theta_{\ell}(t)
, for the existence of this gradient. Thus, the existence of the Fréchet gradient of the Tikhonov functional will be justified.

First, we derive an integral identity establishing a relationship between the inputs
M(t)
and
g(t)
and outputs
u(\ell,t;q)
and
u_{x}(\ell,t;q)
, including the measured outputs
w_{\ell}(t)
and
\theta_{\ell}(t)
, through the weak solution of the related adjoint problem.

Lemma 4

Assume that the conditions of Theorem
1
are satisfied. Then between the inputs and outputs the following integral relationship holds:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\widehat{g}(t)\delta u(\ell,t)+\widehat{M}(t)\delta u_{x}(\ell,t)\right\}dt=\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\delta M(t)\phi_{x}(\ell,t)+\delta g(t)\phi(\ell,t)\right\}dt,

(69)

for all
\widehat{g},\widehat{M}\in H^{1}(0,T)
, where
\delta M(t)
and
\delta g(t)
are the Neumann inputs introduced in (
4
), and the function
\phi(x,t)
is the weak solution of the following backward problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho(x)\phi_{tt}-\mu(x)\phi_{t}+(r(x)\phi_{xx})_{xx}=0,~(x,t)\in\Omega_{T};\\[5.0pt]
\phi(x,T)=\phi_{t}(x,T)=0,~x\in(0,\ell);\\[5.0pt]
\phi(0,t)=\phi_{x}(0,t)=0,~\left(r(x)\phi_{xx}\right)_{x=\ell}=\widehat{M}(t),\,(-\left(r(x)\phi_{xx}\right)_{x})_{x=l}=\widehat{g}(t),\,t\in[0,T],\end{array}\right.

with the inputs
\widehat{g},\widehat{M}\in H^{1}(0,T)
, and
\delta u(x,t)
is the weak solution of the forward problem (
2
).

Proof.
Multiply both sides of equation (
4
) for
\delta u(x,t)
by an arbitrary function
\phi(x,t)
, integrate over
\Omega_{T}
and apply the integration by parts formula several times. Next, we get:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{\ell}\left[\rho(x)\phi_{tt}-\mu(x)\phi_{t}+\left(r(x)\phi_{xx}\right)_{xx}\right]\delta u\,dxdt\qquad~\qquad\qquad~\qquad\qquad~\qquad

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\ell}\left[\rho(x)\delta u_{t}\phi-\rho(x)\delta u\phi_{t}+\mu(x)\delta u\phi\right]_{t=0}^{t=T}\,dx\qquad~\qquad\qquad~\qquad\qquad~\qquad

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\left[\left(r(x)\delta u_{xx}\right)_{x}\phi-r(x)\delta u_{xx}\phi_{x}+r(x)\phi_{xx}\delta u_{x}-\left(r(x)\phi_{xx}\right)_{x}\delta u\right]_{x=0}^{x=\ell}\,dt=0.~

(74)

The expressions in parentheses under the integrals in (
6
) suggest to us how to choose an arbitrary function
\phi(x,t)
. Namely, the first of these expressions shows that this function must satisfy the adjoint equation (
4
), and the other two expressions show that this function must satisfy the limit and final conditions in (
4
). Thus, we assume that the function
\phi(x,t)
solves the backward problem (
4
). Then (
6

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left[-\delta g(t)\phi(\ell,t)-\delta M(t)\phi_{x}(\ell,t)+\widehat{M}(t)\delta u_{x}(\ell,t)+\widehat{g}(t)\delta u(\ell,t)\right]\,dt=0,~

the required integral relationship (
69
) is obtained.
\Box

Corollary 2

Assume that the inputs
\widehat{M}(t)
and
\widehat{g}(t)
in the backward problem (
4
) are defined as follows

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\widehat{M}(t)=u_{x}(\ell,t;q)-\theta_{\ell}(t),\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\widehat{g}(t)=u(\ell,t;q)-w_{\ell}(t),\,t\in(0,T).\end{array}\right.

The backward problem (
4
) with the inputs given by (
2
), i.e. the problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho(x)\varphi_{tt}-\mu(x)\varphi_{t}+(r(x)\varphi_{xx})_{xx}=0,~(x,t)\in\Omega_{T};\\[5.0pt]
\varphi(x,T)=\varphi_{t}(x,T)=0,~x\in(0,\ell);\\[5.0pt]
\varphi(0,t)=\varphi_{x}(0,t)=0,~\left(r(x)\varphi_{xx}\right)_{x=\ell}=u_{x}(\ell,t;q)-\theta_{\ell}(t),\\[3.0pt]
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left(-(r(x)\varphi_{xx}\right)_{x})_{x=\ell}=u(\ell,t;q)-w_{\ell}(t),\,t\in[0,T],\end{array}\right.

is defined as the adjoint problem corresponding to the inverse problem (

)-(

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\left[u(\ell,t;q)-w_{\ell}(t)\right]\delta u(\ell,t)+\left[u_{x}(\ell,t;q)-\theta_{\ell}(t)\right]\delta u_{x}(\ell,t)\right\}dt\qquad\quad

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left[\delta M(t)\varphi_{x}(\ell,t;q)dt+\delta g(t)\varphi(\ell,t;q)\right]dt,~q\in\mathcal{Q}(0,T)\qquad

(83)

which is defined as the input-output relationship corresponding to the inverse problem (

the increment of the Tikhonov functional introduced in (

\displaystyle\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{J}(q)=\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\left[u(\ell,t;q)-w_{\ell}(t)\right]\delta u(\ell,t;q)+\left[u_{x}(\ell,t;q)-\theta_{\ell}(t)\right]\delta u_{x}(\ell,t;q)\right\}dt\qquad

Theorem 4

Assume that conditions of Theorem

7
The Lipschitz continuity of the Fréchet gradient

It is well known that functionals with a Lipschitz continuous Fréchet gradient exhibit monotonicity when using gradient-based iteration algorithms. Therefore, the fact that the Fréchet gradient of the Tikhonov functional corresponding to any inverse problem is Lipschitz continuous is, in a sense, a natural regularizer.

Theorem 5

If conditions of Theorem
2
are satisfied, then the Fréchet gradient of the Tikhonov functional
\mathcal{J}(q)
corresponding to the inverse problem (
2
)-(
2
) is Lipschitz continuous, that is

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{J}(q_{1})-\nabla\mathcal{J}(q_{2})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(0,T)}\leq L_{\nabla}\,\|q_{1}-q_{2}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(0,T)},~\forall q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}(0,T),

(92)

where
\displaystyle L_{\nabla}=\left(\ell+\frac{\ell^{3}}{2}\right)C_{1}^{2}
and

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{J}(q_{1})-\nabla\mathcal{J}(q_{2})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(0,T)}^{2}:=\|\delta\varphi_{x}(\ell,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|\delta\varphi(\ell,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

is the Lipschitz constant,
C_{1}>0
is the constant introduced in Theorem
1
.

Proof.
Denote by
\varphi(x,t;q_{k})
the weak solution of the adjoint problem (
2
), corresponding to the admissible input
q_{k}\in\mathcal{Q}(0,T)
,
k=1,2
. Then the function
\delta\varphi(x,t):=\varphi(x,t;q_{1})-\varphi(x,t;q_{2})
is the weak solution of the following problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho(x)\delta\varphi_{tt}-\mu(x)\delta\varphi_{t}+(r(x)\delta\varphi_{xx})_{xx}=0,~(x,t)\in\Omega_{T};\\[5.0pt]
\delta\varphi(x,T)=\delta\varphi_{t}(x,T)=0,~x\in(0,\ell);\\[5.0pt]
\delta\varphi(0,t)=\delta\varphi_{x}(0,t)=0,~\left(r(x)\delta\varphi_{xx}\right)_{x=\ell}=\delta u_{x}(\ell,t),\\[3.0pt]
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad~(-\left(r(x)\delta\varphi_{xx}\right)_{x})_{x=\ell}=\delta u(\ell,t),\,t\in[0,T],\end{array}\right.

where
\delta u(\ell,t)=u(x,t;q_{1})-u(x,t;q_{2})
and
\delta u(x,t)
is the regular weak solution of problem (
4
). Furthermore,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{J}(q_{1})-\nabla\mathcal{J}(q_{2})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(0,T)}=\|\delta\varphi_{x}(\ell,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|\delta\varphi(\ell,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)},~q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}(0,T).

To estimate the right-hand-side norms, we employ the trace estimates (
1
) to get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{J}(q_{1})-\nabla\mathcal{J}(q_{2})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(0,T)}\leq\left(\ell+\frac{\ell^{3}}{2}\right)\,\|\delta\varphi_{xx}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(0,\ell))},

and then use the first estimate in (
1
) applied to the weak solution of problem (
7
), to obtain the following inequality:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{J}(q_{1})-\nabla\mathcal{J}(q_{2})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(0,T)}\leq\left(\ell+\frac{\ell^{3}}{2}\right)C_{1}^{2}\left[\|\delta u_{x}(\ell,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\|\delta u(\ell,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T)}\right],

In view of the trace inequalities (
1
) applied to the function
\delta u(x,t)
of problem (
4
) this yields:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{J}(q_{1})-\nabla\mathcal{J}(q_{2})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(0,T)}\leq\left(\ell+\frac{\ell^{3}}{2}\right)^{2}C_{1}^{2}\,\|\delta u_{xx}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(0,\ell))}.

With the first estimate in (
1
) applied to the solution
\delta u(x,t)
of problem (
4
) we arrive at the required result (

) with
\delta q(t)=q_{1}(t)-q_{2}(t)
.

8
Conclusion

Based on previous studies, a new mathematical model has been developed that considers the influence of not only the shear force at the tip of the micro-cantilever but also the moment in a dynamic vibration in a micro-cantilever-sample system. Apart from Atomic Force Microscopy, these types of oscillating systems are indispensable components in the operation of various devices. Within this model, a new inverse problem has been formulated for determining the unknown shear force and the moment. This inverse problem assumes use of only the feasible and measurable outputs: deflection and slope at the tip of a micro-cantilever. This model allows for a more realistic simulation of vibration in micro-cantilever-sample contact than was previously possible. Within the weak solution theory for the forward problem, and the quasi-solution approach for the inverse problem combined with the Tikhonov functional and adjoint method, the theoretical basis for the proposed mathematical model has been developed. In this context, the vector form gradient formula has been derived which verifies the gradient formula given for the simplified model
[
14
]
, and it has been proven that this gradient is Lipschitz continuous. The applications of these results, which form the basis for the numerical solution of the problem, will be discussed in the next study.
