The representations of the Lie superalgebra 𝔭 ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathfrak{p}(3) in characteristic 3
Ye Ren

Abstract
. Let 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} be the Lie superalgebra 𝔭 ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathfrak{p}(3) of rank 2 over an algebraically closed field 𝕂 \mathbb{K} of characteristic p = 3 p=3 . We classify all irreducible modules of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , and give the character formulae for irreducible modules.

Introduction

In the 1970s, Kac classified the finite-dimensional simple Lie superalgebras over the field of complex numbers (cf.
[
4
]
). Since then, the representation theory of Lie superalgebras has attracted increasing attention (cf.
[
1
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
14
]
, etc.). However, their irreducible modules have not been fully understood. This paper classifies all the irreducible modules of
\mathfrak{p}(3)
in characteristic
p=3
, which is a sequel to
[
6
]
, where all the irreducible modules of
\mathfrak{p}(3)
in characteristic
p>3
were classified. The method for classifying the irreducible modules of
\mathfrak{p}(3)
in characteristic
3
is derived from
[
12
,
13
]
. It is highly dependent on the representations of the even part of
\mathfrak{p}(3)
, that is, the structure of the irreducible modules of the special linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(3)
. The structure of the irreducible modules of
\mathfrak{sl}(3)
in characteristic
3
is given in
[
11
]
, and they are more complex than those of
\mathfrak{sl}(3)
in characteristic
p>3
.

This article is divided into two parts. The first part gives the general setup for the restricted Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{p}(3)
. The second part gives the multiplicities of simple
\mathfrak{p}(3)
-modules in the Kac modules
K_{\chi}(\lambda)

(\lambda\in\Lambda_{\chi})
. Up to isomorphism of the Kac modules
K_{\chi}(\lambda)
, there are six different types of
p
-characters. We provide specific calculations and proofs for four
p
-characters. Due to the similarities in their calculations and proofs, we omit the proofs for the other two.

1.
Preliminaries

1.1.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p=3
. In this paper, it is the base field of the theory.
The standard matrix realization of the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{p}(3)=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
is given as follows:

\mathfrak{p}(3)=\left\{X=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\\
\end{pmatrix}|A=-D^{t},B^{t}=B,C^{t}=-C,\text{tr}(A)=\text{tr}(D)=0\right\}.

Here,
A,B,C,D
are
3\times 3
matrices. The Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{p}(3)
is a restricted Lie superalgebra (cf.
[
9
]
).
Denote by
U(\mathfrak{g})
the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. There is a
[p]
-map such that
x^{p}-x^{[p]}
are in the center of
U(\mathfrak{g})
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
. For every irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
, there is a
p
-character
\chi
such that

(x^{p}-x^{[p]})_{|M}=\chi(x)^{p}\text{id}_{|M}.

Let
I_{\chi}
be the ideal of
U(\mathfrak{g})
generated by
x^{p}-x^{[p]}-\chi(x)^{p},x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
. We call
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g})=U(\mathfrak{g})/I_{\chi}
the reduced enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
E_{i,j}
be the matrix of order
6\times 6
where the entry in the
i
-th row and
j
-th column equals
1
, with all other entries zero,
1\leq i,j\leq 6
. Let
\epsilon_{i}
be the linear map defined by
\epsilon_{i}(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{3}a_{k}E_{k,k})=a_{i},a_{i}\in\mathbb{K}
. Let

H_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}=E_{i,i}-E_{j,j}-E_{3+i,3+i}+E_{3+j,3+j},1\leq i<j\leq 3,

X_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}=E_{i,j}-E_{3+j,3+i},1\leq i,j\leq 3,i\neq j,

X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}=E_{3+j,i}-E_{3+i,j},1\leq i<j\leq 3,

X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}=E_{i,3+j}+E_{j,3+i},1\leq i<j\leq 3.

X_{2\epsilon_{i}}=E_{i,3+i},1\leq i\leq 3.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be the Cardan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
spanned by
H_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}}(1\leq i\leq 2)
. With respect to
\mathfrak{h}
, there is a standard triangular decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
be the subalgebra generated by
X_{-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}(1\leq i<j\leq 3)
and

\mathfrak{g}_{+1}
be the subalgebra generated by
X_{\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}}(1\leq i\leq j\leq 3)
. Let
\mathfrak{n}^{-}=\mathfrak{n}_{0}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
,
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{+1}
.
So
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}=\mathfrak{n}_{0}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{0}
, and it is isomorphic to the special linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(3)
. Let
\mathfrak{b}_{0}=\mathfrak{n}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
,
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{n}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
.
Denote by
\Phi
the root system. Let
\Phi^{+}

(\Phi^{-})
be the positive (negative) root system. Let
\Phi_{0}
consist of even roots.
Then

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}

is the root space decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Denote by
\rho_{0}
the half sum of the roots of
\mathfrak{n}_{0}
. Denote by
W
the Weyl group of
\Phi_{0}
. Let
s_{\alpha}\in W
be the reflection corresponding to
\alpha\in\Phi_{0}
. For
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, the dot action
s_{\alpha}\cdot\lambda=s_{\alpha}(\lambda+\rho_{0})-\rho_{0}
.

Since
\mathfrak{h}
is commutative, for any
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, there is a one-dimensional
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{h})
-module
\mathbb{K}_{\lambda}
. For any
\chi
, define

\Lambda_{\chi}=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}|\lambda(h)^{p}-\lambda(h^{[p]})=\chi^{p}(h),\forall h\in\mathfrak{h}\}.

Write
\lambda=(r,s)\in\Lambda_{\chi}
if
\lambda(H_{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}})=r,\lambda(H_{\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{3}})=s
. If
\chi=0
, then
r,s\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

For
\chi\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}^{*}
, if
\gamma\in Aut_{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})
(the group of automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
as a restricted Lie algebra), then

U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g})\cong U_{\gamma\cdot\chi}(\mathfrak{g})
. Note that
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\cong\mathfrak{sl}(3)
, and there is a natural surjective homomorphism
\phi:\mathfrak{gl}(3)^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}^{*}
. So we only need to consider the
\text{GL}(3)
-orbit of
\chi\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{*}
.
There is a
\text{GL}(3)
-invariant non-degenerate bilinear form on
\mathfrak{gl}(3)
induced by trace, so
\mathfrak{gl}(3)\cong\mathfrak{gl}(3)^{*}
. For each
\chi\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}^{*}
, there exists
g\in\text{GL}(3)
with
g\cdot\chi
corresponds to a Jordan matrix. Then we can assume
\chi(\mathfrak{n}_{0})=0
. The
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{h})
-module
\mathbb{K}_{\lambda}
naturally becomes the
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{b}_{0})
-module with trivial
\mathfrak{n}_{0}
action. Define the induced
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{0})
-module£º

Z_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)=\text{Ind}_{\mathfrak{b}_{0}}^{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}(\mathbb{K}_{\lambda}).

According to
[
2
, 10.2]
,
Z_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
has a unique maximal submodule. Denote by
L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
the simple quotient of
Z_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
.
Let
v
be a maximal vector of
Z_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
. Then

\{X_{-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{3}}^{c}X_{-\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{3}}^{b}X_{-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}}^{a}v|0\leq a,b,c\leq 2\}

form a basis of
Z_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
. We still use
X_{-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{3}}^{c}X_{-\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{3}}^{b}X_{-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}}^{a}v
to represent elements of
L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}_{+1}
act trivially on the simple
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})
-module
L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
. Then
L_{\chi}^{0}(\lambda)
becomes a
U_{\chi}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{+1})

According to
[
6
, Proposition 1.4]
, there is a
\chi
in its
\text{GL}(3)
-orbit such that
K_{\chi}(\lambda)
has a unique maximal submodule. Denote by

the simple quotient of
K_{\chi}(\lambda)

For

, set

\delta(\lambda)=rs(r+s+1)\in\mathbb{K}.

If

, then

is called a typical weight. Otherwise, it is called an atypical weight. We have the following proposition:

Proposition 1.1
.

(See
[

)
Suppose

is an irreducible
