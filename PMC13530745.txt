Poster 207. Clinical Outcomes Following Split Anconeus Fascia Transfer for Lateral Ulnar Collateral Reconstruction
Authors: Aman Chopra, Brett D. Haislup, Linda Zhang, Nader Mujtaba, Anand Murthi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Posterolateral rotatory instability (PLRI) of the elbow occurs from chronic Lateral Ulnar Collateral Ligament (LUCL) injury leading to subluxation of the ulnohumeral joint and radial head.1 Initial injury occurs due to a valgus, supination force, but the mechanism of injury can include trauma or iatrogenic injury such as chronic injections leading to LUCL degeneration.2 PLRI has been traditionally treated with primary or revision Lateral Ulnar Collateral Ligament reconstruction (LUCLR) with an allograft or autografts such as Semitendinosus, Achilles, and Palmaris.3 However, these grafts require fixation of both the humerus and ulna, and may not represent the natural LUCL kinematics as a native tendon. Split Anconeus Fascia Transfer (SAFT) is a surgical treatment option for PLRI that does not require ulnar drilling or graft site morbidity. The purpose of this study is to compare patients with chronic PLRI that have undergone SAFT or LUCLR using allograft or autograft.Methods:This retrospective case series included adult patients with chronic PLRI who underwent SAFT or LUCLR using allograft or autograft by a shoulder and elbow fellowship-trained orthopedic surgeon at an academic medical center between 2010 and 2023. Patient demographics, follow-up periods, and postoperative complications were collected. Preoperative and postoperative range of motion and pronosupination were analyzed for both cohorts. Normality of the data was tested using the Shapiro-Wilk test, and Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for group comparisons following. Fisher's exact test was used for analyzing categorical variables. Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used to compare preoperative and postoperative measurements.Results:A total of 41 patients underwent operative management for PLRI, including SAFT (n=11) and LUCLR (n=30). The SAFT cohort has a mean age of 48.5±11.7 years, mean BMI of 28.1±8.8, and is 63.6% (n=7/11) female. The mean follow-up period was 5.9±5.5 months and 18.2% (n=2/11) of the SAFT cohort had a prior procedure performed. Only 9.1% (n=1/11) of patients in the SAFT cohort experienced a complication (chronic, non-specific elbow pain) and reinjury, while no patients experienced recurrent instability in the postoperative period. All patients were able to return to work and 36.4% (n=4/11) of patients were able to return to sport in the SAFT cohort. The LUCLR cohort has a mean age of 39.7±13.9 years, mean BMI of 31.7±9.1, and is 50.0% (n=15/30) female. The mean follow-up period was 8.3±12.2 months and 23.3% (n=7/30) of the LUCLR cohort had a prior procedure performed. In the LUCLR cohort, 26.7% (n=8/30) of patients experienced a complication. No patients in the LUCLR cohort experienced reinjury, but 13.3% (n=4/30) of patients experienced recurrent instability. Sixty percent (n=18/30) of patients were able to return to work and 16.7% (n=5/30) of patients were able to return to sport in the LUCLR cohort. When analyzing preoperative and postoperative range of motion and pronosupination, the SAFT cohort demonstrated similar, noninferior outcomes compared to the LUCLR cohort.Conclusions:SAFT is a surgical treatment option for chronic PLRI that does not require ulnar drilling or graft site morbidity. When comparing complications, range of motion, return to sport, and return to work, SAFT can provide similar, noninferior outcomes compared to LUCLR using an allograft or autograft.
