Building Codes and Hot Water Delivery
 Key Requirements (Title 24, 2019):
 • Demand-controlled recirculation systems are
 mandatory for systems where the furthest fixture is
 more than 50 feet from the water heater (Section
                        150.0(n)).
 • All recirculation piping must be insulated to minimize
 heat loss, in line with the California Plumbing Code
                        (Section 5.3.5).
 • Recirculation loops must be designed to reduce
 energy loss, with demand controls minimizing pump
 operation when hot water is not in use.

 California Energy Commission, 2019 Residential Compliance
 Manual, Title 24, Part 6, Section 150.0(n), Chapter 5.

Parts of a Well-Designed Recirculation System
Piping layout: Efficiently routes water to minimize volume and
reduce wait times.

Recirculation loop: A continuous pathway of supply and return
lines carrying hot water from the heater to fixtures and back.

 • Supply line: Delivers hot water from the heater toward fixtures.

 • Return line: Circulates unused hot water back to the heater,
 maintaining availability.

Insulation: Minimizes heat loss along hot water lines for energy
efficiency.

Pump: Circulates hot water through the system on-demand or
continuously.

Check valves: Ensures one-way water flow to prevent backflow.

Controls: Regulates pump operation with timers, sensors, or
thermostats.

Actuators: Buttons or motion sensors that trigger the pump,
initiating hot water delivery when needed.

Temperature-Controlled Systems

These systems maintain the hot
water loop at a preset temperature,
cycling the pump as needed to keep
the water hot. Due to frequent
cycling, they also result in high
energy consumption, often running
for several hours per day.
