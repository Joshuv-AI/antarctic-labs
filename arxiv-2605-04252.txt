Abstract
. Configuration polynomials generalize the Kirchhoff polynomial of a graph, as well as the Symanzik polynomials that appear in the denominators of Feynman integrands. The configuration hypersurfaces cut out by such polynomials are typically highly singular, which poses a challenge for the evaluation of Feynman integrals even in simplified settings. In this paper, we provide a two-step recipe for a resolution of singularities of any irreducible configuration hypersurface. We first consider the normalization of the Nash blow-up, which we identify with an incidence variety introduced by Bloch [ 10 , 11 ] . This variety is typically still not smooth, but it is the closure of a smooth subvariety of a torus. The latter then admits a smooth, tropical compactification, using work of Tevelev. We construct explicitly such a compactification and a morphism to the normalized Nash blow-up for every configuration, described in terms of bipermutohedral matroid combinatorics introduced by Ardila, Denham and Huh [ 3 ] . Along the way, we find that the normalized Nash blow-up of the configuration hypersurface has strongly F F -regular singularities in positive characteristic. We deduce this by certifying F F -rationality of its biprojective cone, and infer from it that the normalized Nash blow-up has rational singularities over the complex numbers.

Introduction

Feynman diagrams encode particle interactions in high-energy physics, and
Feynman integrals can be used to compute the probabilities of specific
interactions. Even in simplified settings that neglect mass and momenta,
evaluation of Feynman integrals poses serious challenges due to singularities and issues with convergence.

The Feynman integrand is the square root of a rational function, the denominator of
which involves the
(first) Symanzik polynomial

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{B}(G)}\prod_{e\not\in T}x_{e}
,
where
\mathcal{B}(G)
denotes the set of spanning trees in
the Feynman graph
G
.
The convergence properties and evaluation techniques for the Feynman integral,
then, depend on the geometry of the
graph hypersurface
cut out by
the Symanzik polynomial. This hypersurface is typically highly singular, and
the main focus of this paper is to provide an explicit resolution of
its singularities, expressed entirely in terms of combinatorial data from
the Feynman graph.

The search for a resolution of singularities for Feynman integrands was initiated by Bloch, Esnault and Kreimer
[
9
]
, and one of their key insights was that the construction of
Symanzik polynomials generalizes naturally from graphs to realizable matroids. More precisely, they found that the
first and second Symanzik polynomials are both instances of
configuration
polynomials

\psi_{W}

W
inside a vector space
V
with distinguished basis—compare Proposition
2.5

planar graphs. Our methods and results apply to this more general setting and make use of the added features.

From now on, let
V\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i\in E}\mathds{K}\cdot x_{i}
denote the vector space spanned by all edge variables

in the Feynman integral, and consider the Nash blow-up of the (projective) configuration hypersurface

This approach was pioneered in

Alas, this approach rarely provides a resolution of singularities.

Assumptions and reference texts

If the denominator in a Feynman diagram factors, one can decompose the Feynman integral into factors as well. This happens precisely when the underlying matroid is not connected. Our theorems thus focus on the connected case, although in some of our constructions we do need to pass through disconnected matroids.
Similarly, the basic definitions can be made over any field, but we often require geometric scenarios. For simplicity, we will assume throughout that our base field
\mathds{K}
is always perfect. Further assumptions about the field or matroid are indicated at the at the start of sections/subsections when stronger hypotheses are appropriate.

Our reference for matroid theory is the book of Oxley
[
43
]
.
For toric geometry we refer the reader to the book of Cox, Little and Schenck
[
15
]
, and for tropical geometry to that of Maclagan and Sturmfels
[
40
]
. For matters on the Frobenius morphism we refer to the notes by Ma and Polstra

2.2.
Round matroids

The following type of matroid considered by Geelen, Gerards and Whittle (see
[
23
]

Definition 2.0
.

A matroid
{\mathsf{M}}
is said to be

if every cocircuit (a minimal set that meets every basis of
{\mathsf{M}}
) spans
{\mathsf{M}}
.

Proposition 2.0
.

A matroid
\mathsf{M}
on
E
is round if and only if
\rank(\mathsf{M}\backslash F)=\rank\mathsf{M}
for all proper flats
F
.
Equivalently, there is no partition
E=E_{1}\sqcup E_{2}
for which neither
E_{1}
nor
E_{2}
span.
In particular, round matroids are connected.

Proof.

The maximal proper flats are precisely the complements of the cocircuits, since no basis can be contained in a proper flat (see
[
43
, 2.1.6.(iii)]
).
Any flat
F
is an intersection of maximal proper flats;
see
[
43
, Prop. 1.7.8]
.
The complement
E\backslash F
of
F
is thus a union of cocircuits.

If
\mathsf{M}
is round and
F
proper, it follows that
E\backslash F
spans and
\rank_{\mathsf{M}}(E\backslash F)=\rank_{\mathsf{M}}E
.
Conversely, let
C
be a cocircuit: then
C
is the complement of a maximal proper flat

, so the rank condition implies that
C
spans in
\mathsf{M}
.

This proves the equivalence and the remaining claims are obvious.
∎

2.5.

Consider a configuration

Proposition 2.0

3.2.
Stratification by flats and irreducibility

The first projection in (

by flats.
For any flat

\mathsf{M}_{W}

Lemma 3.0
.

Let
W\subseteq V
be a configuration.
For any flat
F
of
\mathsf{M}_{W}
and
\left|\mathds{K}\right|\gg 0
,

\dim U_{F}=\rank\mathsf{M}-\rank(F)-1

U_{E}=\varnothing

As a direct consequence of the description of

in (

.

For any flat

of
\mathsf{M}_{W}

.

Bloch
[
11
, §4]
shows that

is a vector bundle on the strata defined by flats.

Proposition 3.0
.

For any configuration
W\subseteq V
and any flat
F
of
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}_{W}
, there is an isomorphism of schemes

\Lambda_{W}|_{U_{F}}\cong U_{F}\times\mathds{P}^{n_{F}-1},\qquad n_{F}\coloneqq\left|E\right|-\rank(\mathsf{M}\backslash F).

If
\mathsf{M}
is round, then
\Lambda_{W}
is a bundle over
\mathds{P}W
whose fibre is
\mathds{P}^{\left|E\right|-\rank\mathsf{M}-1}
.

Proof.

Choose a basis
B=\left\{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\right\}
of
\mathsf{M}\backslash F
and vectors
w^{1},\dots,w^{m}\in W
that project under
\pi_{E\backslash F}
to a basis of
W\backslash F
such that
w^{j}_{i_{k}}=\delta_{j,k}
.
Note that
U_{F}
lies in the affine chart of
\mathds{P}(W/F)
defined by
\ell_{k}=1
, where
k\in E\backslash F
.
This identifies

\mathscr{O}_{U_{F}}(1)\cong\mathscr{O}_{U_{F}},\quad\ell_{i}\mapsto\frac{\ell_{i}}{\ell_{k}}.

Since
\frac{\ell_{i}}{\ell_{k}}|_{U_{F}}=0
for

, we find that

\varphi(1\otimes w)|_{U_{F}}=\sum_{i\in E\backslash F}w_{i}\frac{\ell_{i}}{\ell_{k}}|_{U_{F}}\otimes x_{i}.

In particular,
\varphi(1\otimes(W\cap\mathds{K}^{F}))|_{U_{F}}=0
where
\mathds{K}^{F}=\ker\pi_{E\backslash F}
.
Restricted to
U_{F}
, the image of
\varphi
is thus generated of
\mathscr{O}_{U_{F}}
by

\varphi(1\otimes w^{j})|_{U_{F}}\equiv\frac{\ell_{i_{j}}}{\ell_{k}}\otimes x_{i_{j}}\mod\left\langle 1\otimes x_{i}\mid i\in E\backslash B\right\rangle,\quad j=1,\dots,m.

Since
\frac{\ell_{i}}{\ell_{k}}\in\mathds{K}[U_{F}]^{\times}
is a unit for each

, this shows that
\mathscr{F}|_{U_{F}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{U_{F}}^{n_{F}}
.
Applying

yields
\Lambda_{W}|_{U_{F}}\cong\mathds{P}_{U_{F}}^{n_{F}-1}=U_{F}\times\mathds{P}^{n_{F}-1}
as claimed.

Suppose now that
\mathsf{M}
is round and hence
n_{F}=\left|E\right|-\rank\mathsf{M}
for all flats
F
.
Then
B
and
w^{1},\dots,w^{m}\in W
are bases of, respectively,
\mathsf{M}
and
W
, and
\frac{\ell_{i}}{\ell_{k}}\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathds{P}W,w}^{\times}
is a unit for each

and

.
As above, we find that
\mathscr{F}_{w}\cong\mathscr{O}_{\mathds{P}W,w}^{n_{F}}
for all

.
It follows that
\Lambda_{W}
is a projective bundle of rank

Bloch
[
11
, Prop. 3.5.(ii)]
shows that
\Lambda_{W}
is an irreducible complete intersection.

Corollary 3.0
.

Let
W\subseteq V
be a configuration with matroid
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}_{W}
.
Then
\Lambda_{W}
is a complete intersection of codimension
\rank\mathsf{M}
in
\mathds{P}W\times\mathds{P}V^{\star}
.
If
\mathsf{M}
is connected and
\left|\mathds{K}\right|\gg 0
, then
\Lambda_{W}
is an integral scheme, the closure of the open stratum
\Lambda_{W}|_{U_{\varnothing}}
.
Conversely, if
\Lambda_{W}
is irreducible and
\mathsf{M}
is loopless, then
\mathsf{M}
must be connected.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
and Proposition
3.2
the stratum of
\Lambda_{W}
indexed by any proper flat
F
of
\mathsf{M}
has dimension

\displaystyle\dim(\Lambda_{W}|_{U_{F}})

\displaystyle=\dim(W/F)-1+\left|E\right|-\rank(\mathsf{M}\backslash F)-1

\displaystyle=\left|E\right|+\rank(\mathsf{M}/F)-\rank(\mathsf{M}\backslash F)-2.

Its codimension in
\mathds{P}W\times\mathds{P}V^{\star}
therefore equals (see
[
43
, Prop. 3.1.6]
)

\displaystyle\codim_{\mathds{P}W\times\mathds{P}V^{\star}}(\Lambda_{W}|_{U_{F}})

\displaystyle=\rank\mathsf{M}-\rank(\mathsf{M}/F)+\rank(\mathsf{M}\backslash F)

(
3.0
)

\displaystyle=\rank(F)+\rank(\mathsf{M}\backslash F)\geq\rank\mathsf{M}.

In particular,
\codim_{\mathds{P}W\times\mathds{P}V^{\star}}\Lambda_{W}\geq\rank\mathsf{M}
.
Since
\Lambda_{W}
can be defined by
\rank\mathsf{M}
equations, it follows
that it is a complete intersection of codimension
\rank\mathsf{M}
.
In particular, it is equidimensional and has no embedded components.

Again by Lemma
3.2
and Proposition
3.2
, the open stratum
\Lambda_{W}|_{U_{\varnothing}}
indexed by the empty flat is nonempty, reduced and irreducible.
Its closure is thus a reduced irreducible component of
\Lambda_{W}
.
If
\Lambda_{W}
is irreducible, it is therefore reduced.

Irreducibility of
\Lambda_{W}
occurs exactly if the other strata do not contribute additional components, which is to say that the inequality (
3.0
) be strict for all nonempty proper flats.
This strictness is equivalent to no such flat being a separator of
\mathsf{M}
(see
[
43
, Prop. 4.2.1]
).
Thus,
\Lambda_{W}
is irreducible if and only if
{\mathsf{M}}
only allows the trivial separators
\varnothing
and
{\mathsf{M}}
, which is equivalent to
{\mathsf{M}}
being connected since separators are by definition unions of components.

For the converse, note that the complement of a loop is a separator but not a flat, whereas all separators of a loopless matroid are flats (see
[
43
, §4.1, Ex. 2]
).
∎

3.3.
Normality and smoothness

We now describe the Jacobian matrix of Bloch’s incidence variety and find smooth points over the strata by flats.
These are then used to establish normality using Serre’s criterion and to relate smoothness to roundness of the matroid.

Lemma 3.0
.

Let
W\subseteq V
be a configuration with matroid
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}_{W}
.
Then the (transposed) Jacobian matrix
J_{W}(w,\beta)
at
(w,\beta)\in W\times V^{\star}
obtained from the equations for
\hat{\Lambda}_{W}
in (
3.0
) reads

J_{W}(w,\beta)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}AD_{[\beta]}A^{\intercal}&AD_{A^{\intercal}[w]}\end{array}\right).

Its column space equals that of

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\sum_{i\in F}\beta_{i}[\ell_{i}][\ell_{i}]^{\intercal}&A_{E\backslash F}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}[\beta_{F}\circ Q_{W|_{F}}]&A_{E\backslash F}\end{array}\right),

where
F=F(w)
and the matrix
A_{E\backslash F}
is obtained from
A
by removing the columns indexed by
F
.
In particular,

\rank(\mathsf{M}\backslash F)\leq\rank J_{W}(w,\beta)\leq\rank({\left\{i\in F\mid\beta_{i}\neq 0\right\}\cup E\backslash F}),

and when
\beta_{F}\not\in\hat{X}_{W|_{F}}
the matrix
J_{W}(w,\beta)
has full rank:
\rank J_{W}(w,\beta)=\rank\mathsf{M}
.

Proof.

The block matrix expression for
J_{W}(w,\beta)
is immediate from (
3.0
).
Note that

AD_{[\beta]}A^{\intercal}=\sum_{i\in E}\beta_{i}[\ell_{i}][\ell_{i}]^{\intercal}

is a sum of square matrices of size
\rank\mathsf{M}
.
The
j
th column of
\beta_{i}[\ell_{i}][\ell_{i}]^{\intercal}
is a scaling of
[\ell_{i}]
by
\beta_{i}[\ell_{i}]_{j}
. If
F=F(w)
, then
(A^{\intercal}[w])_{j}=\ell_{j}(w)=0
if and only if
j\in F
(by (
2.0
)). That is, the nonzero columns of

AD_{A^{\intercal}[w]}
are indexed by
E\backslash F
are nonzero and agree with the corresponding ones of
A
up to
\mathds{K}^{\times}
-rescaling.
It follows that the column space of the second block of
J_{W}(w,\beta)
agrees with that of
A_{E\backslash F}
.
Using column operations, the summands with indices in
E\backslash F
in the first block can then be eliminated.
This gives the equality

\sum_{i\in F}\beta_{i}[\ell_{i}][\ell_{i}]^{\intercal}=[\beta_{F}\circ Q_{W|_{F}}],

and the claimed inequalities follow.
For all
\beta_{F}\not\in\hat{X}_{W|_{F}}
, the latter matrix has (full) rank equal to
\rank\mathsf{M}|_{F}=\rank A_{F}
,
so
J_{W}(w,\beta)
does too.
∎

Lemma 3.0
.

Let
W\subseteq V
be a configuration with matroid
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}_{W}
.
Then
\Lambda_{W}
has smooth points over

.

Proof.

Lemma

.
Since

and
\left|\mathds{K}\right|\gg 0
, there is an element
\beta^{\prime}\not\in\hat{X}_{W|_{F}}
in the complement of
\hat{X}_{W|_{F}}
.
Define
\beta\in\mathds{P}V^{\star}
by
\beta_{F}\coloneqq\beta^{\prime}
and
\beta_{E\backslash F}\coloneqq 0
.
Due to Lemmas

(w,\beta)\in\hat{\Lambda}_{W}

It follows that

∎

Corollary 3.0
.

Let
W\subseteq V
be a configuration with connected matroid
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}_{W}
.
Then
\Lambda_{W}
is an integral normal scheme.

Proof.

By hypotheses on
\mathsf{M}
and Corollary

\Lambda_{W}
is a reduced complete intersection integral scheme.
By using Serre’s criteria for normality and reducedness (applied in affine charts), it is enough to check that
\Lambda_{W}
is regular in codimension one.
By way of contradiction, suppose that
\Lambda_{W}
has an irreducible subvariety
Z
of codimension one with generic point in
\Lambda_{W}^{\mathrm{nsm}}
.
By Proposition
3.2
,
\Lambda_{W}|_{U_{\varnothing}}
is smooth and hence

for some proper flat

is irreducible.
The strict inequality (

shows that

It follows that

Finally, by base change, we may assume that

is infinite, since

).
∎

Proposition

is smooth if

is round.
We now establish a refined converse statement: the non-smooth strata arise exactly from flats that violate roundness.

Theorem 3.0
.

Let
W\subseteq V
be a configuration with connected matroid
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}_{W}
.
For any proper flat
F
of
\mathsf{M}
and
\left|\mathds{K}\right|\gg 0
,
\Lambda_{W}^{\mathrm{nsm}}|_{U_{F}}\neq\varnothing
if and only if
\rank(\mathsf{M}\backslash F)<\rank\mathsf{M}
. In particular,
\Lambda_{W}
is smooth if and only if
\mathsf{M}
is round.

Proof.

Lemma
3.2
yields a
w\in W
with projective image in
U_{F}
.

First, suppose that
\rank(\mathsf{M}\backslash F)<\rank\mathsf{M}
.
Since
\mathsf{M}
is connected,
E\backslash F
is not a flat and hence
\rank(E\backslash F)=\rank(\left\{j\right\}\cup(E\backslash F))
for some
j\in F
.
Define
\beta\in V^{\star}
by
\beta_{i}=\delta_{i,j}
.
By Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
,
(w,\beta)\in\hat{\Lambda}_{W}|_{U_{F}}

Definition 3.0

The resulting identification of co-character lattices then reads

(
4.0
)

N_{E,{\mathds{Z}}}^{\star}=N_{E,{\mathds{Z}}},\quad e_{i}^{\star}=-e_{i}.

The purpose of this identification will become clear in the following Lemma
4.2
and Proposition
4.2
.
We write

N_{(E,E),{\mathds{Z}}}\coloneqq N_{E,{\mathds{Z}}}\oplus N_{E,{\mathds{Z}}}\quad\text{and}\quad N_{E,E}\coloneqq N_{(E,E),{\mathds{Z}}}\otimes_{\mathds{Z}}\mathds{R}=N_{E}\oplus N_{E}

for the the co-character lattices and associated real vector spaces of both
{\mathds{T}}_{E}\times{\mathds{T}}_{E}
and
{\mathds{T}}_{E,E^{\star}}
.
The indicator vectors in the two summands of
N_{(E,E),{\mathds{Z}}}
will be denoted by
e_{S}
and
f_{S}
, respectively.
We consider the maps

\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\colon N_{E,E}\to N_{E}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta\colon N_{E,E}\to N_{E}

defined over
{\mathds{Z}}
, the
two projection maps
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
given by
\pi_{1}(x,y)=x
and
\pi_{2}(x,y)=y
, respectively, and the addition map
\delta
given by
\delta(x,y)=x+y
.

For a rational, polyhedral fan
\Sigma
, we denote by
\left|\Sigma\right|
its support and by
\mathds{P}(\Sigma)
its toric variety.
The simplicial, unimodular fans are called smooth because they correspond to smooth toric varieties.

Example 4.0
.

We collect the examples of fans that we will relevant for us.

(a)

The fan
\Gamma_{E}
is the smooth complete fan in
N_{E}
whose cones are spanned by all proper subsets of the coordinate vectors
\left\{e_{i}\mid i\in E\right\}
.
The associated toric variety
\mathds{P}(\Gamma_{E})\cong\mathds{P}V
is the projective space of
V
and
\mathds{P}(-\Gamma_{E})\cong\mathds{P}V^{\star}
its dual by (
4.0
).

(b)

The
permutohedral fan

\Sigma_{E}
is a smooth complete fan in
N_{E}
.
Its rays are spanned by indicator vectors
e_{S}
for which
S\subseteq E
is a nonempty, proper subset.
The cones

\sigma_{{\mathbf{S}}}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot e_{S_{i}}\subseteq N_{E}

are indexed by strict flags of
k
distinct, nonempty proper subsets

{\mathbf{S}}=(\varnothing\subsetneq S_{1}\subsetneq S_{2}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq S_{k}\subsetneq E),

where
k\geq 0
.
Since
-e_{S}=e_{E\backslash S}
,
\Sigma_{E}
is stable under negation, that is,
-\Sigma_{E}=\Sigma_{E}
.

(c)

For a matroid
\mathsf{M}
on
E
without loops, the
Bergman fan

\Sigma_{\mathsf{M}}
(see
[
4
,
20
]
) is an induced and hence smooth subfan of
\Sigma_{E}
of dimension
\rank\mathsf{M}-1
.
Its rays are spanned by indicator vectors
e_{F}
for which
F
is a nonempty, proper flat of
\mathsf{M}
.
The same holds for the subfan
-\Sigma_{\mathsf{M}}
of
-\Sigma_{E}=\Sigma_{E}
.
In particular,
-\Sigma_{\mathsf{M}}\times\Sigma_{\mathsf{M}^{\perp}}
is a (smooth) induced subfan of
\Sigma_{E}\times\Sigma_{E}
.

(d)

The
bipermutohedral fan

\Sigma_{E,E}
is a smooth, complete fan in
N_{E,E}
, which refines the product fan
\Sigma_{E}\times\Sigma_{E}
and admits maps of fans (see
[
3
, Props. 2.2, 2.11]
)

\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\colon\Sigma_{E,E}\to\Sigma_{E}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta\colon\Sigma_{E,E}\to\Gamma_{E}.

Its rays are generated by vectors
e_{S}+f_{T}
indexed by
bisubsets

S|T
.
These are, by definition, pairs of nonempty sets
S,T\subseteq E
for which
S\cup T=E
and
S\cap T\neq E
.
Its cones

\sigma_{{\mathbf{S}}|{\mathbf{T}}}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot e_{S_{i}|T_{i}}\subseteq N_{E,E}

are indexed by all
biflags

{\mathbf{S}}|{\mathbf{T}}
.
These are pairs of flags

{\mathbf{S}}=(S_{1}\subseteq S_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq S_{k})\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathbf{T}}=(T_{1}\supseteq T_{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq T_{k}),

regarded as collections of bisubsets
\left\{S_{i}|T_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq k\right\}
, for which
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}(S_{i}\cap T_{i})\neq E
.

Lemma 4.0
.

The tropicalization of the automorphism (
4.0
) is a lattice automorphism

\mu\coloneqq\trop(m)=\id_{N_{E}}\oplus\,\delta\colon N_{E,E}\to N_{E,E}

which satisfies
\pi_{2}\circ\mu=\delta
.

Proof.

According to Remark
4.1
, the automorphism
m^{\sharp}
corresponding to (
4.0
) on coordinate rings is a monomial map defined on exponent vectors by
(x,y)\mapsto(x,y-x)
.
The contragredient action from (
4.0
) negates the second coordinate, giving the map (see
[
40
, p. 87]
)

(x,y)=(x,-(-y))\mapsto(x,x-(-y))=(x,x+y).\qed

Notation 4.0
.

For any loopless matroid
\mathsf{M}
on
E
, consider the smooth fan in
N_{E,E}

\Delta_{\mathsf{M}}\coloneqq-\mu((-\Sigma_{\mathsf{M}})\times\Sigma_{\mathsf{M}^{\perp}}).

Proposition 4.0
.

Let
W
be a configuration whose matroid
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}_{W}
has no loops or coloops.
Then
\trop(\Lambda_{W}^{\circ})=\left|\Delta_{\mathsf{M}}\right|
with all weights equal to
1
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
, we have (see
[
40
, Cor. 3.2.13, (5.5.7)]
)

\displaystyle\trop(\Lambda_{W}^{\circ})

\displaystyle=\trop(m)\big(\trop((\mathds{P}W)^{\circ}\times(\mathds{P}W^{\perp})^{\circ})\big)

\displaystyle=\mu\big(\trop((\mathds{P}W)^{\circ})\times\trop((\mathds{P}W^{\perp})^{\circ})\big).

The set
\trop((\mathds{P}W)^{\circ})\subseteq N_{E}
is the support of the Bergman fan
\Sigma_{\mathsf{M}}
(see
[
20
, Lem. 6.5]
,
[
4
, Thm. 1]
).
Similarly, using (
4.0
), we identify
\trop((\mathds{P}W^{\perp})^{\circ})\subseteq N_{E}^{\star}
with the support of the fan
-\Sigma_{\mathsf{M}^{\perp}}
in
N_{E}
.
The weights on Bergman fans all equal
1
(see
[
1
, Prop. 5.2]
).
This property is preserved under products of fans (see
[
22
, Ex. 2.9.(iii)]
) and under lattice automorphisms.
The claim follows.
∎

4.3.
Tropical compactifications

We recall the following notions introduced by Tevelev
[
53
]
.

Definition 4.0
.

A compactification
\overline{Y}
of a very affine variety
Y\subseteq{\mathds{T}}
in a
{\mathds{T}}
-toric variety
\mathds{P}
is called
tropical
/
schön
if
\overline{Y}
is proper and the torus multiplication map
{\mathds{T}}\times\overline{Y}\to\mathds{P}
is surjective and flat/smooth.
If such a compactification exists, then also
Y\subseteq{\mathds{T}}
is called
tropical
/
schön
.

Remark 4.0
.

Hacking
[
27
, Lem. 2.7]
observed that
\overline{Y}
being schön is equivalent to smoothness of
\overline{Y}\cap O
for all orbits
O
in
\mathds{P}
. In particular,
Y=\overline{Y}\cap{\mathds{T}}
must be smooth in order for
\overline{Y}
to be schön.

Tevelev proved the following fundamental results.

Theorem 4.0

(
[
53
, Thms. 1.2, 1.4, Prop. 2.5]
)
.

Any very affine variety
Y\subseteq{\mathds{T}}
tropically compactifies in some
{\mathds{T}}
-toric variety
\mathds{P}
.
If it does so in
\mathds{P}=\mathds{P}(\Sigma)
for some rational, polyhedral fan
\Sigma
, then
\left|\Sigma\right|=\trop(Y)
, and it also does so in
\mathds{P}(\Sigma^{\prime})
for any refinement
\Sigma^{\prime}
of
\Sigma
.
In particular, there is always a tropical compactification in a smooth
\mathds{P}
.
If one tropical compactification of
Y
is schön, then all are.

\Box

Our starting point is the following observation.

Proposition 4.0
.

For any configuration
W\subseteq V
, the very affine variety
\Lambda_{W}^{\circ}\subseteq{\mathds{T}}_{E,E^{\star}}
is schön.

Proof.

Any hyperplane arrangement complement
(\mathds{P}W)^{\circ}\subseteq{\mathds{T}}_{E}
is schön due to
[
53
, Thm. 1.5]
.
By Proposition
4.1
,
\Lambda_{W}^{\circ}\subseteq{\mathds{T}}_{E,E^{\star}}
is isomorphic to a product of such under the torus automorphism in (
4.0
).
Schön compactifications are preserved under products and torus automorphisms.
The claim follows.
∎

A key result due to Luxton and Qu makes it easy to find schön compactifications.

Theorem 4.0

(
[
38
, Thm. 1.5]
)
.

Let
Y\subseteq{\mathds{T}}
be a schön very affine variety.
Then any rational, polyhedral fan
\Sigma
with
\left|\Sigma\right|=\trop(Y)
gives rise to a schön compactification
\overline{Y}
in
\mathds{P}(\Sigma)
.
\Box

For our purpose, the following well-known consequence is useful.

Corollary 4.0
.

Let
Y\subseteq{\mathds{T}}
be a schön very affine variety.
Then any smooth rational, polyhedral fan
\Sigma
with
\left|\Sigma\right|=\trop(Y)
gives rise to a smooth schön compactification
\overline{Y}
in
\mathds{P}(\Sigma)
.

Proof.

By hypothesis and Theorem
4.3
, both
\mathds{P}\coloneqq\mathds{P}(\Sigma)\to\Spec\mathds{K}
and
{\mathds{T}}\times\overline{Y}\to\mathds{P}
are smooth, hence so is the composite
{\mathds{T}}\times\overline{Y}\to\Spec\mathds{K}
.
Base change of the smooth map
{\mathds{T}}\to\Spec\mathds{K}
along
\overline{Y}\to\Spec\mathds{K}
shows that
{\mathds{T}}\times\overline{Y}\to\overline{Y}
is surjective and smooth.
Then it follows from the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathds{T}}\times\overline{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec\mathds{K}}

that
\overline{Y}\to\Spec\mathds{K}
is too (see
[
51
,
Lemma 02K5
]
).
∎

In order to obtain a smooth model of the configuration hypersurface
X_{W}
for general configurations
W
, we now replace
\mathds{P}V\times\mathds{P}V^{\star}
in the definition of
\Lambda_{W}^{\mathrm{irr}}
with a general
{\mathds{T}}_{E,E^{\star}}
-toric variety (see §
4.1
).

Notation 4.0
.

For any configuration
W\subseteq V
and any rational, polyhedral fan
\Sigma
in
N_{E,E}
, we denote by

\Lambda_{W}(\Sigma)\coloneqq\Lambda_{W}(\mathds{P}(\Sigma))\coloneqq\overline{\Lambda_{W}^{\circ}}\subseteq\mathds{P}(\Sigma)

the irreducible subvariety obtained as the closure of
\Lambda_{W}^{\circ}
in the
{\mathds{T}}_{E,E^{\star}}
-toric variety
\mathds{P}(\Sigma)
(see Proposition
4.1
).

Remark 4.0
.

For any connected configuration
W\subseteq V
, we have

\Lambda_{W}=\Lambda_{W}(\mathds{P}V\times\mathds{P}V^{\star})=\Lambda_{W}(\Gamma_{E}\times(-\Gamma_{E})),

due to Proposition
4.1
(see Example
4.0
.(
a
)).

We are interested in fans
\Sigma
for which
\Lambda_{W}(\Sigma)
is not only
smooth, but also maps to
X_{W}
. The correct notion is the following.

Definition 4.0
.

We will say that a smooth fan
\Delta
in
N_{E,E}
defines a
tropical resolution
for a matroid
\mathsf{M}
, or for a configuration
W\subseteq V
with matroid
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{M}_{W}
, if
\left|\Delta\right|=\left|\Delta_{\mathsf{M}}\right|
and the projection
-\pi_{2}\colon N_{E,E}\to N_{E}
defines a map of fans
\Delta\to\Gamma_{E}
.
We call it
biprojective
, if also
\pi_{1}
defines such a map of fans.
Equivalently, this means that the identity of
N_{E,E}
defines a map of fans

\Delta\to\Gamma_{E}\times(-\Gamma_{E})
.

We note that the smooth fan
\Delta_{\mathsf{M}}
itself is not in general a tropical
resolution, because it fails the condition on
\pi_{2}
.
In §
4.4
, we rectify the defect by producing a suitable refinement.

Theorem 4.0
.

Let
W\subseteq V
be a connected configuration.
If
\Delta
defines a tropical resolution for
W
, then
\Lambda_{W}(\Delta)
is a smooth schön compactification of
\Lambda_{W}^{\circ}\subseteq{\mathds{T}}_{E,E^{\star}}
and
\pi_{2}\colon N_{E,E}\to N_{E}
induces a birational surjection
p\colon\Lambda_{W}(\Delta)\to X_{W}
.

Proof.

By Propositions
4.2
,
4.3
and Corollary
4.3
,
\Lambda_{W}^{\circ}\subseteq{\mathds{T}}_{E,E^{\star}}
is schön, and the smooth
{\mathds{T}}_{E,E^{\star}}
-toric variety
\mathds{P}(\Delta)
makes
\Lambda_{W}(\Delta)
a smooth tropical compactification.

By hypothesis,
\pi_{2}
defines a toric morphism
p\colon\mathds{P}(\Delta)\to\mathds{P}V^{\star}
(see Example
4.0
.(
a
) and
[
15
, Thm. 3.4.11]
), which fits into a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{W}^{\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{W}(\Delta)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathds{P}(\Delta)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{W}^{\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{W}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathds{P}V^{\star}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

Each row contains a very affine variety (left) and its closure (middle) in a toric variety (right) (see Proposition
2.5
).
The left vertical map is birational (see Corollary
4.1
).
Since
p
is continuous, it induces the dashed map, which is closed because

\Lambda_{W}(\Delta)
is proper.
The image of
\Lambda_{W}(\Delta)
is then closed in
X_{W}
, and contains a dense open subset.
It follows that the dashed map is a birational surjection.
∎

The construction of
\Lambda_{W}(\Sigma)
is functorial in the following sense.

Proposition 4.0
.

Let
W\subseteq V
be a connected configuration.
Suppose that the identity of
N_{E,E}
defines a map
\Sigma\to\Sigma^{\prime}
of rational, polyhedral fans such that
\pi_{2}
defines a map of fans
\Sigma^{\prime}\to-\Gamma_{E}
.
Then there is an induced commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{W}(\Sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{W}(\Sigma^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{W}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{\prime}}

If
\left|\Delta_{\mathsf{M}}\right|\subseteq\left|\Sigma\right|
, then
f
and

are birational surjections.

Proof.

We argue as in the proof of Theorem

.
Since

is the identity on
\Lambda_{W}^{\circ}
, the maps exist by continuity of the induced toric morphisms, and

.

By a result of Tevelev (see
[

), the condition on supports

(see Proposition

\Lambda_{W}(\Sigma)
proper and thus

and

surjective.
∎

Corollary 4.0
.

Let
W\subseteq V
be a connected configuration.
If
\Delta
defines a biprojective tropical resolution for

, then the morphism

induced by

factors through

Apply Proposition
4.3
to

and

(see Remark

The required map of fans is given by definition of a biprojective tropical resolution.
∎

4.4.
Square conormal fans

Now we give a combinatorial recipe to construct a tropical resolution for
any matroid.
Our construction uses the bipermutohedral fan (see Example
4.0
.(
d
)).

Definition 4.0
.

For a matroid
\mathsf{M}
on
E
without loops or coloops, we define the
square conormal fan
of
\mathsf{M}
as the (cone-wise) intersection of fans

\Sigma_{-\mathsf{M},\mathsf{M}^{\perp}}\coloneqq((-\Sigma_{\mathsf{M}})\times\Sigma_{\mathsf{M}^{\perp}})\cap\Sigma_{E,E}.

Remark 4.0
.

The definition of the square conormal fan closely resembles that of the
conormal fan

\Sigma_{\mathsf{M},\mathsf{M}^{\perp}}=(\Sigma_{\mathsf{M}}\times\Sigma_{\mathsf{M}^{\perp}})\cap\Sigma_{E,E}
, which plays an important role in
[
3

;
however, these two fans are not isomorphic. They are tropicalizations of
incidence varieties coming from the Hadamard square immersion (
3.0
)
in the former case, and from the logarithmic immersion of
\mathds{P}W
given by
x\mapsto\log\left|x\right|
in the latter.

Definition 4.0
.

By a
square biflat

F\subseteq G
of a matroid
\mathsf{M}
on
E
we mean a pair
(F,G)
of flats
F\in\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{M}}
and
G\in\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{M}^{\perp}}
, such that and
(E\backslash F)|G
is a bisubset: that is,
F\subseteq G
, and if
F=\varnothing
, then
\varnothing\neq G\subsetneq E
.

Proposition 4.0
.

The square conormal fan
\Sigma_{-\mathsf{M},\mathsf{M}^{\perp}}
is the induced subfan of
\Sigma_{E,E}
whose rays are indexed by square biflats
F\subseteq G
.
In particular,
\Sigma_{-\mathsf{M},\mathsf{M}^{\perp}}
is smooth.

Proof.

The fan
-\Sigma_{\mathsf{M}}
is an induced subfan of
\Sigma_{E}
(see Example
4.0
.(
c
)).
Combined with the identity
-e_{F}=e_{E\backslash F}
for

, the following Lemma
4.4
yields the claim.
∎

The next lemma makes use of the fact that any nonzero incidence vector

in

spans a ray of

.

For any two induced subfans

induced by the rays indexed by bisubsets

for which

and
f_{S}\in\left|\Sigma_{2}\right|
.

Proof.

By hypothesis,
\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2}
is an induced subfan of
\Sigma_{E}\times\Sigma_{E}
.
In particular,
\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2}
consists of cones of
\Sigma_{E}\times\Sigma_{E}
, and the latter is refined
by
\Sigma_{E,E}
.
Each cone of

is thus a cone of
\Sigma_{E,E}
.

Suppose that

is a bisubset such that

spans a ray in

.
Then

lies in a cone

of

as in Example
4.0
.(

Since
\Sigma_{E}
is smooth,

and hence

.
Thus,

is zero or spans a ray of
\Sigma_{1}
.

Let now
\sigma=\sigma_{{\mathbf{S}}|{\mathbf{T}}}
be any cone of
\Sigma_{E,E}
as in Example
4.0
.(

\left|\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2}\right|

Then

span a cone
\sigma_{1}
of
\Sigma_{E}
.
By the above, it is a cone of the induced subfan
\Sigma_{1}
.
With a similar cone

of

\sigma_{1}\times\sigma_{2}
of
\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2}
then contains
\sigma
.
This makes
\sigma
a cone of
(\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2})\cap\Sigma_{E,E}
and the claim follows.
∎

Under the schön hypothesis, the multiplication map in the toric variety is

.

For any point

Proof.

We may work affinely, so let

\mathsf{M}
