 1 Purpose
This environmental calculation file (ECF) summarizes the groundwater pathway exposure assumptions
and methodologies used to revise radionuclide-specific unit dose factors (UDFs) published in CP-64491,
Hanford Site Composite Analysis Data Package – Exposure Scenarios and Radionuclide Specific Dose
Conversion Factors, and ECF-HANFORD-22-0091, Hanford Site Composite Analysis Special Studies:
Updates to the Groundwater Pathway Radiological Dose. Specifically, this ECF describes the updates to
the radiological dose coefficients published in DOE-STD-1196-2022, Derived Concentration Technical
Standard.
The Hanford Site Composite Analysis (CA) is based on the All-Pathways Representative Person
groundwater exposure scenario presented in DOE/RL-2019-52, Composite Analysis for Low-Level Waste
Disposal in the Hanford Site Central Plateau (FY 2020). Updated dose coefficients will supersede UDFs
published in the current Hanford Site CA, DOE/RL-2019-52 (Rev. 2, at the time of this ECF).

 2 Background
Since the completion of the CA, information has been updated that impacts the dose calculations for the
All-Pathways Representative Person scenario presented in CP-64491. The revised exposure-related
parameters include the following:
• Dose coefficients for environmental radionuclides published in DOE-STD-1196-2011, Derived
 Concentration Technical Standard, have been revised and published in DOE-STD-1196-2022, which
 provides per capita dose coefficients that reflect the current state of knowledge and practice in
 radiation protection, as well as updated demographic data for the United States.

This ECF includes the updated exposure-related parameters used to calculate radionuclide-specific UDFs
based on recommendations from the Low-Level Waste Disposal Facility Federal Review Group (LFRG).
These updates include the use of central tendency instead of the 95th percentile for ingestion intake rates
and the exclusion of iodine-129 water vapor inhalation pathway from the total dose calculation (per email
January 24, 2022, provided in Appendix A of ECF-HANFORD-22-0091).

In addition to the recommendations from LFRG, external dose conversion factors were revised in 2019 in
accordance with EPA-402-R-25-001, Federal Guidance Report No. 15: External Exposure to
Radionuclides in Air, Water, and Soil (hereafter referred to FGR No. 15). FGR No. 15 contributes to a
series designed to offer technical information for implementing radiation protection programs. FGR No. 15
tabulates age-specific, reference person effective dose rate coefficients for 1,252 radionuclides based on
external exposure to radionuclides distributed in air, water, and soil. These updates were included in the
updated UDFs calculated in ECF-HANFORD-22-0091 and are included in this ECF.

DOE-STD-1196-2022 supports the implementation of the DOE radiation protection programs and
contains DOE-approved effective dose coefficients that are used in estimating doses, supporting pathway
modeling, and comparing measurements to criteria provided in DOE O 458.1, Radiation Protection of the
Public and Environment. For each radionuclide and environmental medium addressed in DOE-STD-1196-
2022, a per capita dose coefficient or Dose Conversion Factor (DCF) is provided and used to estimate a
reference dose to the public. This reference dose determines compliance with DOE dose limits and
constraints. A per capita dose coefficient or DCF for a radionuclide is a weighted sum of age-specific
effective dose coefficients, where the weight takes account of the fractions of each subgroup in the United
States population, represented by an age-specific effective dose coefficient and the level of exposure to
the radionuclide for that age group. The per capita dose coefficients or DCFs may be useful in
calculations supporting demonstrations of compliance with public dose limits without having to assess
doses to individual age-specific categories.

Major changes to DOE-STD-1196-2022 include the following:
• Census Data – the revised DOE technical standard incorporates information from the U.S. Census
 Bureau from 2010 (U.S. Census Bureau, 2011, Age and sex composition: 2010, which details the

 1

 distribution of the United States population by age and sex. The 2010 data replace the age and sex
 distribution defined by the 2000 U.S. Census Bureau data.
• Effective Dose Rate Coefficients – age-independent effective dose rate coefficients from FGR No. 15
 replaced the age-specific effective dose rate coefficients of FGR No. 12 (EPA-402-R-93-081, External
 Exposure to Radionuclides in Air, Water, and Soil).
• Age-specific committed effective inhalation and ingestion dose coefficients for radionuclides are
 based on updated International Commission on Radiological Protection (ICRP) biokinetic and
 dosimetric models and ICRP-103, The 2007 Recommendations of the International Commission on
 Radiological Protection, tissue-weighting factors.
• Separate per capita dose coefficients for water and milk ingestion are provided.
• Updates have been made to reference age- and sex-specific usage of environmental media
 according to U.S. Census Bureau, 2011.
• Nuclear decay data from ICRP-107, Nuclear Decay Data for Dosimetric Calculations, are used.

 3 Methodology
Equations used to calculate the radionuclide-specific groundwater pathway total UDFs for the All-
Pathways Representative Person scenario are presented in CP-64491. Total UDFs were calculated by
summing each radionuclide’s exposure route-specific dose.

Section 3.1 of CP-64491 presents the equations used to calculate media-specific concentrations for each
exposure route. Equations, exposure assumptions, and methodologies used to calculate exposure route-
specific dose are provided in Section 3.2 of CP-64491. The exposure parameters, equations, and
methodologies applied in this ECF are consistent with CP-64491 Section 3.2 except for (1) the inhalation
and ingestion rates use central tendency values instead of 95th percentile, (2) use of the revised external
exposure DCFs recently published in FGR 15, (3) use of the revised dose rate coefficients published in
DOE-STD-1196-2022, and (4) exclusion of iodine-129 water vapor inhalation exposure pathway from total
UDF calculation.

For each of the six Hanford soil types, radionuclide-specific total UDFs were calculated as follows:
1. Dose equations published in CP-64491, along with the associated exposure assumptions and
 parameters, were the foundation upon which all above-mentioned updates are based.
2. External DCF values for each radionuclide were updated from FGR 12 to FGR 15 values. Note these
 were previously updated (see ECF-HANFORD-22-0091 for specific details).
3. All ingestion and the water vapor inhalation rates were updated using central tendency values. 95th
 Upper Confidence Limit was provided for reference only.
4. All DCF values for each radionuclide were updated using the revised dose rate coefficients published
   in DOE-STD-1196-2022.
5. Once the above-mentioned updates were incorporated, exposure route-specific doses were
 calculated for each radionuclide and each soil type.
6. Radionuclide total UDFs were calculated by summing the exposure route-specific doses.

 4 Assumptions and Inputs
This chapter provides key assumptions and revised exposure parameters used to calculate route-specific
doses and total UDFs for the All-Pathways Representative Person groundwater pathway exposure
scenario as described in Chapter 3 of this ECF.

4.1 Soil Type
The radionuclide and exposure route-specific doses were calculated for the following six soil types that
cover the bulk of the Hanford Site Central Plateau—three sand soils (Rupert Sand, Hezel/Koehler Sand,

 2

and Dunesand), one loamy sand soil (Burbank), one sandy loam soil (Ephrata), and one silt loam soil.
The silt loam type combines the Esquatzel, Kiona, Pasco, Scooteney Stoney, and Warden Silt Loam
soils. Appendix A of CP-64491 presents more detailed information related to the site-specific soil types
based on the soil map created for the site. Table 3-1 of CP-64491 presents the bulk density and moisture
content values for each soil type. Table 3-2 of CP-64491 presents the assigned values for non-site-
specific parameters used to calculate media-specific radionuclide concentrations.

• Ingestion of homegrown crops (fruits and vegetables)
• Ingestion of beef
• Ingestion of milk
• Ingestion of eggs
• Ingestion of poultry
• Incidental ingestion of soil
• Inhalation of soil particulates
• Inhalation of water vapor

4.5 Central Tendency
The 95 percentile intake assumptions were used during the base case dose assessment for the All-
 th

Pathways Representative Person, which is described as an age- and gender-weighted average person
receiving a dose that is representative of the more highly exposed individuals in the population.

Radiation Protection of the Public, and ICRP-103 further define a “Representative Person” by six age
subgroups including newborn, 1-year, 5-year, 10-year, 15-year, and adult. EPA/600/R-09/052F, Exposure
Factors Handbook 2011 Edition (Final Report), publishes the mean and percentiles intake rates for

 7 Results/Conclusions
Table 3 through Table 8 present the radionuclide-specific total UDFs for the All-Pathway Representative
Person scenario for the six relevant Hanford soil types. In addition to the radionuclide-specific total UDFs,
Table 9 shows the decrease in dose fold change for each radionuclide exposure route-specific pathway
for each soil type. Revised radionuclide total UDFs reported in this ECF had approximately a 0.698 fold
decrease in dose compared to previously reported UDFs regardless of radionuclide or soil type. In
contrast, two radionuclides, chlorine-36 and strontium-90, showed increasing dose fold changes of 1.35
and 1.10, respectively.
