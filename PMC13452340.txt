Dissolving microneedles for inflammatory diseases: Advances in formulation strategies and therapeutic applications
Authors: Maya Nurul Rahma, Arif Budiman, Lusi Nurdianti, Khaled M. Elamin, Ahmed Fouad Abdelwahab Mohammed, Safwat A. Mahmoud, Nasrul Wathoni
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Inflammatory diseases affecting various organs, such as arthritis in the joints, psoriasis of the skin, and keratitis in the eyes, often require long-term anti-inflammatory therapy, necessitating the development of innovative treatment strategies. Dissolving microneedles (DMNs) have emerged as promising transdermal, transmucosal, and transocular drug delivery systems for treating inflammatory diseases. DMNs are composed of biocompatible polymers that completely dissolve upon administration, thereby eliminating the risk of sharp waste. As a minimally invasive therapeutic approach, DMNs allow for direct application to the site of inflammation, enhancing drug bioavailability and reducing systemic toxicity compared with conventional oral administration routes. This review summarizes recent advances in DMN formulations for inflammatory disease therapy, including production methods and innovative DMN designs, such as hybrid, bilayer, core-shell, and stimuli-responsive systems. The integration of additional technologies, including drug polymer-based encapsulation, nanoparticles, nanocarriers, and mesoporous silica, has been shown to enhance the therapeutic potential of active compounds in in vivo studies using inflammatory disease models, with superior outcomes reported in clinical evaluations. Nevertheless, further research is required to analyze application-related factors, such as the location and condition of the administration area, drug release kinetics, and dosage loading, to fully elucidate the underlying mechanisms. Moreover, a more in-depth investigation of the regulatory challenges and clinical monitoring is necessary to optimize the clinical potential of DMNs. Despite these challenges, DMNs remain a highly promising platform for targeted and effective drug delivery in the management of inflammatory diseases.

Introduction

Inflammatory diseases are a major cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. The incidence of inflammatory diseases, such as arthritis, synovitis, psoriasis, and keratitis, continues to increase annually due to lifestyle habits, environmental exposure, and aging (Furman et al., 2019). Inflammatory conditions often cause pain, tissue damage, and long-term disability in patients (Seifert and Baerwald, 2021; Yacine et al., 2025). The clinical treatment of inflammatory diseases often involves the oral administration of corticosteroids and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) orally (James et al., 2025; Song et al., 2023). However, the use of oral NSAIDs is limited due to a 3–5-fold increased risk of upper gastrointestinal complications such as gastrointestinal erosions and ulcerations (Castellsague et al., 2012). Oral drug use also has limitations due to low bioavailability resulting from first-pass metabolism and increased systemic toxicity (Alqahtani et al., 2021; Aungst, 2017). Furthermore, the parenteral administration of drugs for the treatment of inflammatory diseases can lead to thrombus formation, hypersensitivity reactions, pain during administration, and high costs. The parenteral route is also ineffective because systemic administration can reduce drug availability in affected organs, such as joints and eyes(K. Rajendran et al., 2023). Treatment of inflammatory diseases requires effective local administration. Transdermal drug delivery systems (TDDS) are an option for local drug delivery, as they can reduce the risk of systemic side effects. However, the presence of a skin barrier layer can hinder permeability (Bakhrushina et al., 2025). Conventional topical preparations, such as gels or creams, cannot overcome this limitation (Raina et al., 2023). Microneedle (MNs) technology has been developed as an advanced transdermal drug delivery system that combines minimally invasive percutaneous administration with the therapeutic advantages of subcutaneous delivery (Gowda et al., 2022). MNs are designed to penetrate the stratum corneum painlessly, making them suitable for application to inflamed areas of the body because of their comfort (Sen et al., 2025).

(MNs)-based drug delivery offers significant pharmacokinetic advantages over conventional oral administration by creating microscale channels across the stratum corneum and delivering therapeutic agents directly into the viable epidermis or dermis, where they are subsequently absorbed into the dermal microcirculation and systemic circulation. Consequently, the administered drug bypasses the gastrointestinal tract and hepatic first-pass metabolism, thereby avoiding degradation caused by gastric acid, digestive enzymes, and cytochrome P450-mediated metabolism, which are major contributors to the low and variable oral bioavailability (Khalid et al., 2023; Moawad et al., 2025). Furthermore, unlike immediate-release oral formulations, which often produce rapid absorption followed by a sharp decline in plasma concentration, MN-based systems enable controlled and sustained drug release from the microneedle matrix or transdermal drug reservoirs. This sustained diffusion across the skin maintains a more consistent drug input into the systemic circulation, thereby reducing peak-to-trough fluctuations in plasma drug levels, prolonging therapeutic exposure, and minimizing concentration-dependent adverse effects (Khalid et al., 2023). In addition, the minimally invasive nature of MNs eliminates the pain and inconvenience associated with repeated injections while reducing the dosing frequency, which may improve patient adherence, particularly in chronic diseases requiring long-term pharmacotherapy (X. Xie et al., 2026).

MN technology is strongly justified for application in global health across several dimensions, particularly in terms of drug administration and distribution. In low- and middle-income countries, the delivery of parenteral therapies remains constrained by the limited availability of trained healthcare personnel, inadequate cold-chain distribution infrastructure, and low patient adherence to treatment regimens. As a transdermal drug delivery platform, microneedles enable self-administration, thereby reducing dependence on healthcare professionals and the use of sterile injection devices (Gattu et al., 2026). Furthermore, encapsulation-based modification of active pharmaceutical ingredients can enhance and preserve their biological activities over extended periods at room temperature. This approach reduces the reliance on costly refrigerated supply chains, which are often associated with significant logistical failures in developing countries. Consequently, microneedle-based systems offer a promising strategy for improving the stability, accessibility, and efficiency of drug delivery in resource-limited healthcare settings (Rodgers et al., 2019).

Several types of MNs have been developed to address these issues. Compared to other types of MNs, dissolving microneedles (DMNs) offer several advantages in terms of fabrication, drug delivery efficiency, and user convenience (Fig. 1). Solid microneedles generally induce a higher level of pain during application because they are typically fabricated from non-biodegradable materials, such as silicon or metals, which also pose a risk of generating sharps waste. In addition, solid MNs may result in dose variability because of the rapid closure of microchannels after needle removal. Their two-step application procedure further increases procedural complexity (Kim et al., 2012). In contrast, DMNs dissolve directly within the skin and release the encapsulated drug without requiring additional steps, while simultaneously eliminating the risk of sharps waste. Coated MNs are limited by their drug-loading capacity, which is highly dependent on coating thickness and needle dimensions, and they often suffer from drug accumulation on the baseplate. Conversely, DMNs enable homogeneous drug distribution throughout the entire polymeric needle matrix, thereby allowing higher drug-loading capacity (Avcil and Çelik, 2021a). Hollow MNs are constrained by mechanical strength issues, as the presence of a lumen can render the needles fragile. Furthermore, the lumen may become obstructed by skin tissue during penetration and disrupt drug flow (Verbist et al., 2026). Hydrogel-forming MNs involve more complex fabrication processes because of the need for a separate drug reservoir, and their relatively slow swelling behavior may delay the onset of drug release (Mohite et al., 2024). The use of DMNs in the treatment of inflammatory diseases has several advantages, including increasing drug concentration at the site of inflammation, compatibility with inflamed tissues, and reduced risk of systemic effects, making them suitable for long-term use (Guo et al., 2023). In recent studies, DMNs have been integrated with the formulation of active substances before incorporation, such as nanotechnology, encapsulation, and mesoporous silica, allowing for the modification of release kinetics, penetration ability, and increased stability of the active substance. DMNs are increasingly recognized as a promising platform for inflammatory disease drug delivery because they enable localized, minimally invasive, and controlled drug administration, providing an alternative to conventional systemic therapy (Avcil and Çelik, 2021a). By delivering drugs across the skin, DMNs bypass gastrointestinal degradation and hepatic first-pass metabolism, which may enhance drug bioavailability while reducing variability associated with conventional administration routes (Nguyen and Banga, 2025). Recent advances in DMN formulation, including the incorporation of nanocarrier-based delivery systems and controlled-release strategies, have further expanded their therapeutic potential by improving drug stability, facilitating drug transport across the skin barrier, and enabling sustained drug release (Avcil and Çelik, 2021a, Avcil and Çelik, 2021b; Nguyen and Banga, 2025). The success of DMNs drug delivery systems is influenced by several key attributes, including the biocompatibility and biodegradability of the polymer, the mechanical strength of the needles, drug loading capacity, compatibility between the drug and the polymer matrix, drug release characteristics, and the microneedle geometry, which determines the ability to penetrate target tissues (Peng et al., 2025).

Several review articles have discussed microneedle technologies, focusing on fabrication methods, drug delivery mechanisms, and biomedical applications (Avcil and Çelik, 2021b; Dave et al., 2024; Rodgers et al., 2019). Although DMNs have gained considerable attention as minimally invasive drug delivery systems, reviews specifically addressing their application in inflammatory diseases are limited. In particular, the integration of advanced formulation strategies, such as nanoparticles, nanocarriers, microemulsions, and polymer-based encapsulation systems, with emerging DMN designs for inflammatory disease therapy has not been comprehensively discussed. Therefore, this review provides an integrated overview of DMNs for the treatment of inflammatory diseases affecting the skin, joints, eyes, and oral mucosa of the human body. Unlike previous reviews that primarily emphasize microneedle fabrication and general biomedical applications, this review highlights recent advances in formulation strategies, innovative DMN designs, therapeutic applications, clinical progress, and translational challenges. Accordingly, this review summarizes the current developments and future perspectives of DMN-based systems for inflammatory disease management and clinical translation.

Advantages of Dissloving Microneedle (DMNs)

DMNs have emerged as one of the most promising transdermal drug delivery systems for inflammatory diseases because they can bypass the stratum corneum barrier and deliver therapeutic agents directly into the epidermis and dermis, where inflammatory processes predominantly occur (Gowda et al., 2022). Unlike conventional topical formulations, which are often limited by poor skin penetration, DMNs improve intradermal drug delivery and enhance local drug availability in diseased tissues (Mohite et al., 2024). In addition, the incorporation of drugs within biodegradable polymer matrices enables more homogeneous drug distribution and higher loading efficiency than that of coated microneedles, while avoiding the mechanical limitations and lumen blockage associated with hollow microneedles. Another important advantage of DMNs is their ability to modulate drug release profiles through formulation design, allowing either rapid drug release or sustained therapeutic delivery depending on clinical requirements. Furthermore, the integration of nanocarriers, nanosuspensions, and mesoporous materials into DMN matrices can improve drug stability, loading capacity, and retention within skin tissues, which are essential for maintaining therapeutic concentrations during the treatment of chronic inflammatory diseases. These characteristics make DMNs particularly attractive for long-term inflammatory disease management and improved patient adherence to treatment (Gowda et al., 2022).

In addition to improving drug deposition within inflamed tissues, DMNs provide significant advantages in maintaining therapeutic efficacy through enhanced drug stability and controlled release behavior. The biodegradable polymer matrix can protect encapsulated therapeutic agents from premature degradation while enabling sustained drug retention within the skin, thereby maintaining therapeutic concentrations for extended periods (Liu et al., 2024). Furthermore, advances in microneedle formulation have enabled the development of stimuli-responsive and controlled-release systems that regulate drug release according to physiological conditions, improving treatment precision and reducing dosing frequency (Gowda et al., 2022). Controlled release offers several therapeutic benefits, including improved bioavailability, reduced side effects, and enhanced patient adherence, particularly in chronic diseases that require long-term treatment (Mohite et al., 2024). In addition, prolonged drug retention within skin tissues helps maintain therapeutic drug levels and minimize fluctuations in drug concentration, thereby enhancing therapeutic outcomes and reducing the likelihood of disease recurrence (Song et al., 2023). These capabilities make DMNs a highly versatile platform that combines efficient transdermal delivery with long-term therapeutic control, which is particularly advantageous for the management of chronic inflammatory diseases (Liu et al., 2024).

The therapeutic advantages of DMNs have been demonstrated in several models of inflammatory diseases. In a knee synovitis model, DMNs containing a cannabidiol nanosuspension improved drug bioavailability, prolonged therapeutic activity, and significantly reduced the levels of the pro-inflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-1β compared to conventional subcutaneous injections (Cheng et al., 2024). Furthermore, biodegradable polymer-based microneedle systems enable controlled and sustained drug release according to the inflammatory conditions. For example, inflammation-responsive microneedle patches for atopic dermatitis can gradually release vitamin D3 based on matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) activity in the lesion area. This mechanism provides a more targeted therapeutic effect, reduces dosing frequency, improves patient compliance, and has the potential to prevent recurrence of chronic inflammatory diseases (Song et al., 2023). Therefore, DMNs are considered a promising drug delivery platform for the treatment of various inflammatory diseases compared to other microneedle types (Mohite et al., 2024; Parameshwar, 2024).

Furthermore, sustained drug release and minimally invasive administration may reduce the treatment burden, improve patient convenience, and enhance long-term treatment adherence, particularly for chronic inflammatory diseases requiring prolonged therapy (Nguyen and Banga, 2025).Collectively, these advances highlight the potential of DMNs to support the continued evolution of inflammatory disease drug delivery toward more effective, localized, and patient-centered therapeutic strategies.

Polymeric materials

Natural polymers

Natural polymers commonly employed in the fabrication of DMNs for inflammatory therapy include gelatin, chitosan, and hyaluronic acid. These polymers are biocompatible and exhibit excellent drug delivery capabilities (Chen et al., 2018a) Hyaluronic acid (HA) is a naturally occurring polymer that is widely used in the formulation of DMNs for transdermal drug delivery systems. The selection of the HA molecular weight is a critical aspect in the preformulation stage of DMNs. HA with a molecular weight of 10–50 kDa enables rapid needle dissolution, thereby enhancing permeability. In contrast, HA with a molecular weight of 50–900 kDa provides superior mechanical strength, while HA with higher molecular weights further contributes to improved structural integrity and penetration capability of the microneedles (Miura et al., 2025). In a study conducted by Hua et al., hyaluronic acid demonstrated several advantages as a base material for DMNs in rheumatoid arthritis therapy. It possesses sufficient mechanical strength to effectively penetrate the stratum corneum while maintaining rapid solubility, thereby enabling efficient drug release. Moreover, hyaluronic acid functions as a stimuli-responsive polymer under inflammatory conditions, such as mildly acidic pH and elevated levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) commonly present in inflamed tissues, thereby enabling targeted and controlled drug release (Hua et al., 2024).

Gelatin is a cost-effective natural polymer commonly used as a base material for dissolving microneedles (DMNs) because of its rapid solubility. In practical applications, gelatin is often chemically modified or carboxylated at concentrations of 10–20% (w/v) to enhance structural stability and mechanical strength (J. Rajendran et al., 2025). In this context, both gelatin and hyaluronic acid possess superior skin elasticity-enhancing properties, with ratios of 17.3% and 12.6%, respectively, which are advantageous for treating inflammatory skin conditions such as psoriasis and dermatitis (D. S. Kim et al., 2022).

Chitosan is an ideal natural polymer for use as a base material in DMNs. Chitosan is derived from chitin, which is safe for application in human tissues because of its biocompatibility and biodegradability. Structurally, chitosan can form physical crosslinks, which play a crucial role in governing drug release mechanisms. However, chitosan exhibits limited solubility at neutral pH, necessitating formulation modifications, such as the use of water-soluble chitosan (Chandrasekharan et al., 2019). In addition, it has been reported that chitosan-based DMN systems can function through various beneficial mechanisms in the treatment of inflammatory diseases by controlling inflammation, collagen deposition, and supporting wound healing mechanisms (Chi et al., 2020).

Synthetic polymers

Synthetic polymers offer advantages in the formulation of DMNs for transdermal and transocular drug delivery systems. Polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) exhibits tunable release kinetics by adjusting its molecular weight. In the case of PVA, an increase in molecular weight has been shown to reduce drug absorption. This phenomenon is not observed in natural polymers such as hyaluronic acid and chitosan (Chen et al., 2018a). In a study conducted by Li et al., PVA-based DMNs were employed for the delivery of tofacitinib in the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis and were evaluated in vivo using a rat model. The fabricated DMNs exhibited a needle height of 1160 ± 43 μm, a base width of 139 ± 17 μm, and an interspacing of approximately 32 ± 3 μm. The drug content coated onto the needle surface was 87.59 ± 9.36%. The results demonstrated a rapid drug release profile, with approximately 95% of tofacitinib released within 4 h. Furthermore, the PVA-based DMNs exhibited effective skin insertion capability, achieving a penetration depth of up to 710 μm in an animal skin model (Li et al., 2021).

Polyvinyl pyrrolidone (PVP) has advantages in terms of solubility, as it can increase permease, based on in vitro studies (Cole et al., 2017). PVP is well-tolerated and non-toxic, making it suitable for transdermal and ocular delivery systems (Cole et al., 2017). The study conducted by Zheng et al. demonstrated that the use of polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) as a base material for DMNs containing sodium diclofenac for rheumatoid arthritis therapy did not induce any allergic reactions, and no polymer accumulation was observed in major organs based on pathological evaluation using H&E staining (Zheng et al., 2024). These findings indicate that synthetic polymers, such as PVP, exhibit good biocompatibility for use in biomedical applications. In a study by Wu et al., poly(vinyl pyrrolidone) (PVP)-based DMNs were developed for the delivery of polydopamine/manganese dioxide (PDA/MnO₂) and methotrexate for the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis. The In vivo degradation of DMNs within the microsynovial region enabled the effective release of active agents, resulting in a significant reduction in inflammation, which was associated with decreased levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) (Wu et al., 2021).

Poly (acrylic acid) (PAA) possesses tunable mechanical strength, excellent hydrophilicity, and responsiveness to external stimuli, such as pH and light. These characteristics allow for efficient skin penetration, rapid needle dissolution, and controlled drug release (Tian et al., 2019). In a study conducted by Rajendran et al., poly(acrylic acid) (PAA) with a molecular weight of 450,000 was employed to fabricate DMNs as a transdermal delivery system for methotrexate in the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis. Continuous application of the PAA-based DMNs for 72 h exhibited a biphasic drug release profile, characterized by an initial rapid release attributed to the diffusion of drug molecules from the penetrated needle tips, followed by a sustained release phase governed by the gradual dissolution of the microneedles (K. Rajendran et al., 2023).

In practice, the combination of PVA-PVP allows for the control of the diffusion rate by adjusting the polymer ratio, thus allowing sustained drug release and producing good mechanical strength. (I.-C. Lee et al., 2015). These synthetic polymers offer several advantages, including minimally invasive application and painless administration. The ability to fabricate mechanically robust and functional microneedles with controlled release properties renders synthetic polymers, such as PVA, PVP, and PAA, particularly valuable for drug delivery applications targeting inflammatory conditions.

Natural and synthetic polymer combination

In addition to the individual use of natural and synthetic polymers, combinations of both are frequently employed in DMN formulations to achieve the desired physicochemical and mechanical characteristics. As in the study by Shi et al., the combination of hyaluronic acid (HA) and methoxy poly(ethylene glycol)–poly(ε-caprolactone) (MPEG-PCL) in the formulation of DMNs reduced intraocular inflation, strengthened the structure of DMNs, and increased penetration power into the corneal tissue, while HA provided easy dissolving properties (Tay et al., 2023). By adjusting the ratio of MPEG-PCL/HA, a synergy between the mechanical strength and dissolution rate can be achieved (Shi et al., 2023).

Song et al.used a combination of gelatin methacryloyl (GelMA) and hyaluronic acid (HA) as the polymeric base for DMNs in the treatment of dermatitis (Ohn et al., 2021). The incorporation of GelMA contributed to the formation of a stable and mechanically robust structure owing to the crosslinking of methacrylate groups (Basara et al., 2019), whereas HA functioned as a solubility-enhancing agent by increasing the water absorption capacity. This combination demonstrates excellent biocompatibility, no cytotoxic effects, and strong adhesion to the skin surface (Song et al., 2023).

A combination of hyaluronic acid (HA) and polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) is a highly effective and biocompatible base for DMNs, offering excellent mechanical strength for skin penetration and rapid, moisture-triggered dissolution of the microneedles. HA provides biocompatibility and rapid biodegradation in the skin, whereas PVP provides mechanical strength and controls the drug-release rate (Albadr et al., 2022). The combination of HA and PVP was employed by Oh et al. to load triamcinolone acetonide (TA) for the treatment of chronic inflammatory skin diseases. The developed DMNs successfully delivered TA uniformly throughout the skin tissue, thereby enabling a targeted drug delivery system.

Fabrication techniques for DMNs

Micromolding is the most widely used method compared to other methods. This technique is usually accompanied by photopolymerization using UV light and high temperatures. However, solvent-molding techniques are more often used to maintain the stability of the polymer load or certain active substances (Oliveira et al., 2024). In this technique, the DMN matrix, a polymer solution containing the active substance, is poured into a mold, and the matrix fills the microcavities with assistance, such as pressure, vacuum, or centrifugation. The fabrication technique using centrifugal lithography (Fig. 2b) is a DMN manufacturing approach that employs centrifugal force to transform polymer solutions from a flat surface into microneedle structures through a series of processing steps (Moore et al., 2022). DMNs fabricated using this method exhibit high mechanical strength and good reproducibility; however, the technique is only suitable for polymers with specific mechanical properties, such as hyaluronic acid, and is less ideal for large-scale industrial production (Chang et al., 2020).

The 3D printing method (Fig. 2c) employs an additive and highly precise manufacturing system in which microneedles are fabricated using a layer-by-layer deposition process. This technique offers several advantages, including excellent reproducibility, high-dimensional accuracy, and customizable DMN geometries. However, its limitations include a restricted selection of printable materials, mainly polymers such as PVA and PVP, and the relatively high production costs associated with the process (Detamornrat et al., 2022).

The - droplet-born air blowing method (Fig. 2d) is a fabrication technique for DMNs that does not require silicon molds. This method eliminates the need for thermal processing or ultraviolet (UV) irradiation by utilizing controlled airflow to shape the microneedles from polymer droplets. During fabrication, polymer solution droplets are first arranged on a plate and subsequently exposed to a precisely regulated air stream, causing the polymer to stretch and solidify into a microneedle structure. The use of a single polymer droplet per microneedle enables precise control over the droplet size and concentration, thereby facilitating controlled drug loading without loss of the active compound (Jamaledin et al., 2020).

Nanoparticle and nanocarrier

The integration of nanotechnology with DMNs in the development of drug delivery systems for the treatment of inflammatory diseases, such as gout arthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, and synovitis, has been shown to enhance therapeutic efficacy through improved solubility and controlled drug release mechanisms (Liu et al., 2024c). Several nanotechnology-based systems have been successfully integrated into DMNs, including nanocarriers such as liposomes (Xiao et al., 2024) and ethosomes (Li et al., 2024), as well as nanoparticles such as mesoporous silica (Du et al., 2024), magnetic nanoparticles (Chen et al., 2024), and nanosuspensions (Cheng et al., 2024).

Ethosomes integrated with DMNs have been shown to enable stable drug release, maintain consistent drug bioavailability, and provide controlled release profiles. The integration of both systems provides a dual advantage, whereby DMNs create microchannels in the skin, while ethosomes enhance drug penetration across the stratum corneum. For example, Li et al. modified a combination of Iguratimod and Colchicine into ethosomes, which are phospholipid vesicles containing ethanol (Li et al., 2024). The advantage of ethosomes in the treatment of gouty arthritis lies in their ability to increase the fluidity of the skin membrane (Chourasia et al., 2011), allowing faster diffusion into inflamed joint tissues. Moreover, the bilayer membrane structure of ethosomes enables the sustained release of iguratimod and colchicine from the DMNs formulation (Almuqbil and Aldhubiab, 2025), effectively suppressing the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α and IL-6, in gout arthritis conditions.

The integration of magnetic nanoparticles with DMNs offers significant advantages in terms of penetration capabilities. The application of an external magnetic field enhances drug penetration and accumulation within the target tissue, thereby improving drug distribution and enabling a more directed and efficient release profile. Furthermore, the presence of magnetic nanoparticles increases drug retention at the site of administration, which can enhance therapeutic efficacy while potentially reducing the required systemic dose and minimizing adverse side effects. In a study by Chen et al, a multifunctional therapeutic system based on exosomes, folic acid, lidocaine, and magnetic nanoparticles (Fe₃O₄) was developed for the treatment of recurrent oral ulcers (F. Chen et al., 2024). The magnetic nanoparticles (Fe₃O₄) acted as responsive agents that supported localized and controlled drug delivery (Nguyen et al., 2021). The application of a magnetic nanoparticle field retains the DMNs system for a longer period at the lesion site, thereby enhancing membrane permeability and accelerating the penetration and diffusion of active substances, such as exosomes, folic acid, and lidocaine into the damaged mucosal tissue. This finding contributes to the wound closure rate observed in in vivo studies, in which an 82.11 ± 8.75% increase in wound closure was achieved in the recurrent oral ulcer animal model compared with the control group (Rarokar et al., 2024).

Du et al. designed a psoriasis therapy using methotrexate (MTX) combined with mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNs) and DMNs. Based on in vitro evaluations, MSNs can provide sustained MTX release for up to seven days and effectively inhibit keratinocyte proliferation, thereby enhancing the anti-inflammatory effect. The slow-release profile of MTX also helps prevent rapid drug diffusion into the blood vessels, thereby reducing systemic toxicity (Du et al., 2024). Overall, the combination of MSNs and DMNs improves drug availability at the target tissue and reduces dosing frequency, indicating a promising approach for sustained anti-inflammatory therapy in patients with psoriasis.

Microemulsion

Microemulsions are drug delivery systems often used for hydrophilic drugs to increase their bioavailability, solubility, and therapeutic efficacy. Hydrophilic drugs have low solubility and require high doses to achieve their therapeutic effects (Yadav et al., 2023). However, the inherent limitation of microemulsions lies in their restricted penetration capability across the stratum corneum, which has motivated their integration with DMNs. DMNs create microchannels at the application site, enabling the direct and efficient delivery of microemulsions into the epidermal layers. Overall, the combination of these two systems provides synergistic advantages, namely enhanced drug solubility and permeability afforded by the microemulsion, together with significantly improved skin penetration facilitated by DMNs.

In a study by Li et al., osteoarthritis was treated by modifying celecoxib and α-linolenic acid into a microemulsion form and incorporating it into DMNs (Li et al., 2023). Celecoxib is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) with low water solubility (Motallae et al., 2018), whereas α-linolenic acid is an essential fatty acid that is easily degraded (Hénon et al., 1997). Microencapsulation modifications increased celecoxib solubility and protected α-linolenic acid stability before the incorporation of DMN. Within an 8-h period, the cumulative amount of celecoxib that permeated from the DMN-integrated microemulsion system reached 89.2 ± 5.1 μg/cm2, which was approximately 2.98-fold higher than that obtained from the microemulsion system without DMNs. This enhancement indicates that the formation of microchannels during DMN application markedly increased drug flux and skin permeation capability. Furthermore, the incorporation of α-linolenic acid into the microemulsion system significantly reduced serum prostaglandin E-2 (PGE-2) levels, a key biomarker of inflammation (Li et al., 2023). These two modifications can improve drug penetration and epidermal retention, resulting in a more optimal therapeutic effect for osteoarthritis treatment.

Polymer-based encapsulation

Polymer-based encapsulation is commonly employed to modify active compounds that are prone to degradation, including oxidative, hydrolytic, or photolytic degradation, as is frequently observed in drugs with antioxidant activity (Carvalho et al., 2022; Řepka et al., 2023). Furthermore, encapsulation provides a controlled and sustained drug release profile (Rezagholizade-shirvan et al., 2024). The integration of polymer-based encapsulation with DMNs is particularly advantageous because it prevents drug concentration fluctuations after DMN penetration into the skin, thereby reducing the risk of local irritation and toxicity.

Chiu et al. demonstrated this by encapsulating epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG) and l-ascorbic acid (AA) using poly-γ-glutamate (γ-PGA) within DMNs to treat dermatitis. EGCG is inherently prone to oxidation and degradation when exposed to light and heat, which limits its physicochemical stability. Encapsulation within a γ-PGA matrix, combined with the incorporation of l-ascorbic acid (AA) as a stabilizing agent, preserved approximately 95% of its structural integrity and 93% of its antioxidant activity after four weeks of storage. Furthermore, incorporation into dissolving microneedle (DMN) formulations enabled efficient transdermal delivery, with 63 ± 5% of the loaded EGCG successfully delivered into the skin within 3 min of application. The dissolved γ-PGA was retained in the skin, forming a viscous gel capable of releasing the encapsulated drugs for up to six days after application. The results demonstrated that a once-weekly microneedle regimen was able to produce therapeutic effects comparable to those of daily topical application, despite requiring a lower frequency and reduced dosage. The prolonged drug release and local retention of γ-PGA led to improved therapeutic outcomes in the treatment (Chiu et al., 2021).

Design modification for DMNs in inflammatory diseases treatment

Conventional DMNs

Conventional DMNs represent a classical type of microneedle system consisting solely of a drug dissolved or dispersed within a polymeric matrix and subsequently molded (Fig. 3a) into a microneedle structure. Two main approaches are typically employed for micromolding. The single-step casting method involves the simultaneous formation of the entire patch, which may lead to a decrease in drug-loading efficiency because the active compound is distributed throughout the entire patch, including the baseplate. In contrast, the two-step casting technique addresses this limitation by loading the drug into the needle tips, followed by the addition of a supporting layer, thereby allowing drug localization specifically within the needle region (Sabri et al., 2020),(Zhang et al., 2021). Conventional DMNs are generally easier to fabricate and exhibit good reproducibility. However, they are not suitable for hydrophobic drugs without further formulation modification (Maia et al., 2025).

In a study by Du et al., melittin, a water-soluble component of bee venom, was formulated into conventional DMNs using hyaluronic acid as the polymeric matrix for rheumatoid arthritis (RA) therapy. HA-based DMNs successfully delivered the entire melittin payload within 10 min. In contrast, HA-based DMNs modified with cross-linkable groups exhibited an initial burst release of approximately 56% within the first 10 min, followed by sustained drug release over a period of up to 48 h. Melittin loading into the DMN system effectively inhibited the progression of adjuvant-induced rheumatoid arthritis (RA), as evidenced by the reduced levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including IL-17 and TNF-α. The results demonstrated that hyaluronic acid–based DMNs containing melittin exhibited a controlled and sustained drug release profile, highlighting the potential of conventional DMNs for delivering peptide-based anti-inflammatory agents (G. Du et al., 2021).

Multidrug DMNs

Multidrug DMNs are a type of microneedle system that combines two or more therapeutic agents to achieve synergistic effects, enhance therapeutic efficacy, and reduce systemic dosage (Fig. 3b) (Maia et al., 2025). By facilitating the concurrent delivery of multiple therapeutic agents within a single microneedle platform, this strategy enables simultaneous intervention across various pathophysiological pathways involved in complex inflammatory diseases. Key considerations in developing multi-drug DMNs include drug compatibility, formulation stability, and optimization of the drug ratio within the microneedle matrix (Janczura et al., 2022). Moreover, the spatial distribution of the drug within the microneedle structure, as well as variations in its release kinetics, must be carefully designed to ensure controlled therapeutic effects and more predictable clinical outcomes. For the treatment of inflammatory diseases, multidrug DMNs commonly incorporate combinations of anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory agents to achieve complementary pharmacological actions (Janczura et al., 2022).

Ge et al. developed a multi-drug DMN system for treating recurrent oral ulcers. The microneedle tips were loaded with epidermal growth factor (EGF) and triamcinolone acetonide (TA), which synergistically suppress inflammation and promote angiogenesis. The base layer of the DMN was embedded with zeolitic imidazolate framework-8 (ZIF-8), which can release Zn2+ ions with antibacterial properties. In addition, hyaluronic acid, used as a DMN matrix, provides a protective and moisturizing effect on the oral mucosa (Ge et al., 2023). This multidrug DMN system demonstrates a synergistic therapeutic mechanism, offering simultaneous anti-inflammatory, regenerative, and antimicrobial effects for the effective management of recurrent oral ulcerations.

Core-Shell DMNs

Core–shell DMNs are a type of microneedle system composed of an inner core and an outer shell layer (Fig. 3c). This design enables the sequential release of therapeutic agents directly into inflamed tissues (Tang et al., 2024). Compared with multi-drug DMNs, the core–shell configuration offers improved drug stability by maintaining physical separation between the layers, thereby minimizing potential drug incompatibility. However, this architecture requires high fabrication precision, which poses a significant challenge during the manufacturing process (Q. L. Wang et al., 2018). Moreover, this modification enables the spatial separation of distinct therapeutic agents within the DMNs structure. The pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic profiles of drugs can be strategically tailored using appropriate loading approaches. The outer shell layer is engineered to provide immediate drug release, thereby inducing a rapid therapeutic effect, whereas the inner core is designed to facilitate delayed or sustained drug release. This modification also supports the incorporation of therapeutic agents with diverse physicochemical properties (Y. Xie et al., 2025). A critical consideration in this approach is ensuring adequate interlayer adhesion strength as well as the mechanical robustness of the resulting DMNs, as reliable penetration performance must be consistently maintained.

Jiang et al., developed a core–shell DMN system for the treatment of keratitis, an inflammatory condition of the cornea caused by bacterial infection that can lead to blindness (Yu et al., 2025). The core layer of the DMNs contained Ag@ZIF-8 nanoparticles, whereas the outer shell consisted of rapamycin incorporated into a biodegradable polymer matrix. Upon insertion into the corneal tissue, rapamycin is rapidly released from the outer shell, exerting antiangiogenic and anti-inflammatory effects. Subsequently, the Ag@ZIF-8 nanoparticles in the core gradually released Ag + ions in response to the mildly acidic environment of the infected tissue, thereby providing sustained antibacterial activity without inducing cytotoxicity (Jiang et al., 2024). This dual-release mechanism produces a synergistic therapeutic effect, combining anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial actions for the effective management of bacterial keratitis.

Hybrid DMNs

Hybrid DMNs are a type of microneedle system specifically designed for the delivery of hydrophobic compounds and are often integrated with modified drug systems, such as lipid nanocarriers or encapsulated formulations (Fig. 3d). This modification aims to regulate the drug release rate, enhance solubility, and improve bioavailability. Moreover, this modification can protect degradation-prone drugs, thereby enhancing their stability during storage and administration (Hidayatullah et al., 2023). Furthermore, such an approach enables controlled and sustained drug release, which may reduce the frequency of DMN application and consequently improve patient adherence. From a formulation perspective, critical parameters in the development of hybrid DMNs include matrix–carrier compatibility, mechanical strength, and drug loading capacity.

Shi et al. demonstrated the application of hybrid DMNs in their study, in which curcumin, a hydrophobic compound with potent anti-inflammatory activity, was used as a model drug for intraocular inflammation therapy (Shi et al., 2023). To overcome the limitations of solubility and bioavailability, curcumin was encapsulated in amphiphilic block copolymer micelles, specifically, methoxy poly(ethylene glycol)-poly(ε-caprolactone) (MPEG-PCL) micelles. The amphiphilic structure of MPEG-PCL forms a hydrophobic core capable of solubilizing curcumin, whereas its hydrophilic shell maintains dispersion stability within the DMN matrix, which is composed of hyaluronic acid. The combination of MPEG-PCL and hyaluronic acid produced a hybrid DMN structure with sufficient mechanical strength to penetrate the corneal epithelium, enabling the efficient and sustained delivery of curcumin to ocular tissues (Shi et al., 2023).

Responsive DMNs

Responsive DMNs are engineered to release therapeutic agents selectively in response to specific stimuli, either originating from internal physiological conditions or external environmental triggers. In contrast to conventional DMNs, which primarily rely on passive dissolution mechanisms for drug release, responsive DMNs can modulate their release profiles according to variations in pH, temperature, and enzymatic activity. This modification enables a more targeted drug delivery system, as the release of the active compound can be spatially and temporally controlled at the intended site of action. Consequently, this approach has the potential to enhance therapeutic efficacy and provide adaptive responses to specific physiological conditions.

In inflammatory therapy, responsive DMNs are microneedle systems designed to react to the physiological conditions of inflamed tissues (Fig. 4). Inflammatory sites, such as those observed in rheumatoid arthritis, dermatitis, and psoriasis, are characterized by a slightly acidic pH and elevated levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS). These pathological conditions trigger structural changes in responsive polymers, such as swelling or cleavage of chemical bonds, leading to controlled drug release at the target site. The main advantage of responsive DMNs is their selective release behavior, which minimizes off-target effects and reduces adverse reactions in healthy tissues.Fig. 4Mechanism of Responsive DMNs in Rheumatoid Arthritis Therapy.Fig. 4

Mechanism of Responsive DMNs in Rheumatoid Arthritis Therapy.

In a study by Liu et al., an ROS-responsive DMN system was developed for the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis (Fig. 5). The DMN-incorporated FTL@SIN nanoparticles were composed of fucoidan as an anti-inflammatory agent, sinomenine as an analgesic and immunomodulator, and luteolin together with thioketal linkers that provide ROS sensitivity. Upon insertion into the skin, the DMNs dissolved and released FTL@SIN nanoparticles into the inflamed joint tissue. Under the pathological conditions of rheumatoid arthritis, elevated ROS levels trigger the cleavage of thioketal bonds, leading to the controlled release of fucoidan and sinomenine at inflamed sites. This system promotes the repolarization of macrophages from the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype to the anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype, reduces the expression of inflammatory mediators, including IL-6, TNF-α, and NO, and contributes to the repair of damaged joint tissue (Liu et al., 2024b).

Therapeutic applications of DMNs in inflammatory diseases

Inflammatory diseases can affect various organs, including the skin, eyes, joints, and oral mucosa. Although they arise through different pathological mechanisms, they share characteristic features, such as increased immune overactivity, oxidative stress, and increased cytokine production. Conventional therapies often have limitations, such as difficulty in achieving effective local drug concentrations. In this context, DMNs have emerged as transdermal and transmucosal drug delivery systems that provide noninvasive treatment and increase the efficacy of inflammatory disease treatment.

Inflammation in skin

Several inflammatory skin disorders, including chronic inflammatory skin diseases, psoriasis, dermatitis, and acne, can be triggered by various factors, such as bacterial infection, oxidative stress, autoimmune responses, and environmental influences, such as ultraviolet (UV) radiation exposure (Abdolmaleky and Zhou, 2025; Ansary et al., 2021; Estadt et al., 2022). These conditions stimulate the activation of immune cells, particularly macrophages and T cells, and the subsequent release of proinflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α, leading to tissue damage and persistent inflammation (Brancewicz et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2018b). The physiological structure of the skin, particularly the presence of the stratum corneum, poses a major barrier to conventional topical therapies, such as creams and ointments, which often fail to deliver adequate drug concentrations to deeper layers (Brito et al., 2024).

DMNs are a promising alternative that physically breaches the stratum corneum and enables the efficient transdermal delivery of therapeutic agents (Sartawi et al., 2022), thereby improving the efficacy of anti-inflammatory treatments for skin diseases. Du et al. developed DMNs as a percutaneous drug delivery system containing methotrexate (MTX) for psoriasis treatment. The DMN-based MTX system demonstrated superior therapeutic performance compared with oral MTX administration in a mouse model of psoriasis. Oral MTX exhibits limited bioavailability due to first-pass metabolism, whereas DMN-based delivery significantly increases the local drug concentration at inflamed sites (Kuroda et al., 2000). Histological analysis revealed that MTX-loaded DMNs reduced the epidermal thickness to approximately ±65 μm, compared to ±97 μm with oral MTX. Moreover, enhanced antiproliferative and anti-inflammatory effects were observed, accompanied by significant suppression of Ki-67+, IL-17 A+, and T-cell expression, underscoring the therapeutic advantage of DMN-mediated MTX delivery in the management of psoriasis (H. Du et al., 2024).

Inflammation in eye

The treatment of ocular inflammatory diseases, such as intraocular inflammation and keratitis, remains a major challenge owing to the complex anatomy and dynamic physiological barriers of the eye (Agrahari et al., 2016). The presence of the tear film, corneal epithelium, and lacrimal drainage system limits the absorption and retention of topically administered drugs, such as eye drops, resulting in rapid drug clearance and low bioavailability (Kayal, 2022). In addition, the physicochemical properties of drugs, such as large molecular weight and short drug half-life, influence their ability to penetrate and distribute within ocular tissues. This presents a significant challenge for drug delivery, particularly to the retina and posterior segments of the eye. In general, the treatment of inflammatory conditions in the posterior segment commonly employs intravitreal injection as the gold standard. However, this approach is considered invasive and may lead to complications, such as endophthalmitis and retinal detachment. Moreover, the requirement for repeated injections can reduce patient adherence to therapy because of decreased comfort during the injection procedure (Glover et al., 2023). DMNs offer a promising strategy for overcoming these limitations by enabling minimally invasive penetration of the cornea, enhancing drug retention, and facilitating sustained intraocular drug release (Glover et al., 2023; J. Lee et al., 2025).

The advantages of DMNs over conventional formulations, such as eye drops, have been demonstrated in a study by Shi et al., who used curcumin as a therapeutic agent for uveitis. In vivo experiments conducted in an endotoxin-induced uveitis rabbit model compared the efficacy of curcumin-loaded DMNs with that of curcumin eye drops. According to in vitro release studies, the DMN system achieved sustained drug release for up to 8 h and extended the precorneal retention time by approximately 3.5 h compared to eye drops. Furthermore, DMNs can reversibly penetrate the corneal epithelium, improving ocular bioavailability. This enhanced drug delivery translated into superior therapeutic outcomes, as evidenced by a significantly greater reduction in the infiltration of inflammatory cells, including CD68 macrophages and CD45 leukocytes, in the DMN-treated group than in those treated with curcumin eye drops (Shi et al., 2023).

Joint Inflammation and localized DMNss-based therapy

Inflammatory joint disorders, such as osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis, exhibit high incidence rates and often require long-term therapeutic management (Evans et al., 2014). The presence of the synovial membrane, which restricts drug diffusion into the joint cavity, poses a significant challenge to effective treatment (Tao et al., 2025). Conventional oral administration of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) is frequently associated with gastrointestinal complications and systemic side effects, making localized drug delivery a promising therapeutic approach (Sohail et al., 2023). DMNs have been investigated as innovative local drug delivery systems for joint inflammation because of their ability to deliver therapeutic agents directly to synovial tissues, thereby reducing systemic toxicity and enhancing bioavailability. Compared with intra-articular injection, DMNs can provide sustained drug release, preventing high local drug concentrations typically associated with injection therapy and minimizing the risk of local toxicity (Guo et al., 2023).

Rajendran et al. compared the therapeutic efficacy of methotrexate (MTX) administered via DMNs with that of conventional intra-articular injections for the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis. In in vivo experiments, MTX-loaded DMNs demonstrated 1.84-fold higher bioavailability compared to injection and exhibited a prolonged half-life of 72–96 h, approximately twice that of the injected formulation. MTX-DMNs significantly reduced joint swelling by 45%, improved stride length by up to 80%, and markedly suppressed inflammatory biomarkers, including TNF-α, IL-1β, and anti-CCP antibodies (p < 0.001). Overall, MTX-DMNs achieved superior anti-inflammatory and osteoprotective effects compared with conventional methotrexate injections, highlighting their potential as an effective and safer alternative for the long-term management of rheumatoid arthritis (K. Rajendran et al., 2023).

Oral cavity inflammation and DMNs-based drug delivery

The physiological environment of the oral cavity, characterized by a moist mucosal surface and continuous salivary flow, presents a significant challenge in maintaining adequate drug retention, thereby limiting the therapeutic effectiveness of conventional treatments (Špiljak et al., 2025). Traditional dosage forms, such as gels or ointments, are easily washed away by saliva, preventing drugs from achieving a sufficient local concentration in the mouth. In contrast, dissolving microneedle (DMN) systems offer improved drug retention time in the oral mucosa, enabling localized and sustained release at the site of inflammation. The biocompatible polymer matrix of DMNs ensures safe application without causing mucosal irritation or exacerbating existing lesions.

In a study by Wang et al., a DMN formulation for recurrent oral ulcers (ROUMN), containing dexamethasone acetate, vitamin C, and tetracaine hydrochloride, demonstrated significant advantages over conventional flat patches. In in vitro assays, ROUMN reduced interleukin-6 (IL-6) expression by over 30% more effectively than dexamethasone acetate alone, while enhancing RAEC viability and migration by approximately 1.5-fold compared to vitamin C alone, indicating its stronger anti-inflammatory and regenerative activity. In in vivo studies, the ROUMN system accelerated oral ulcer healing within five days, whereas the flat patch required seven days to achieve comparable recovery, confirming the superior therapeutic efficacy and mucosal compatibility of the DMN-based delivery system.

The subsequent clinical trial (NCT07085520) is a Phase I study designed to evaluate the safety of DMNs containing denosumab in healthy participants, as well as individuals with osteoarthritis and osteoporosis. This study adopted a single-center interventional design involving approximately 12 participants aged 18–70 years. Participants were allocated into four dose groups, receiving DMN patches containing 0.24, 1.2, or 6 mg of denosumab, while the control group received DMNs containing saline buffer. Each participant applied four DMN patches to their knee. The safety responses assessed included skin erythema, pain, and swelling, which were evaluated at 15 min, 24 h, 48 h, and 2 weeks after application. This study aimed to assess the local reactions and tolerability of the patch as preliminary safety data for a microneedle-based drug delivery system.

Another clinical study focused on young patients with acne. Thirty participants received treatment with triamcinolone acetonide-loaded DMNs (TA-DMNs) applied once daily to acne-prone areas for 28 days. Efficacy was assessed based on acne volume (using a skin imaging system), pigmentation (using a Mexameter device), and skin irritation. The results showed that TA-DMNs reduced acne volume by 12.34% after 3 days and an additional 10.01% after 7 days compared to the baseline. After 28 days, skin pigmentation improved, with melanin and hemoglobin levels decreasing by 5.88% and 7.83%, respectively. No adverse effects or skin irritation were reported, confirming the safety and therapeutic potential of TA-DMNs for acne management (Tai et al., 2022).

Challenges and future perspective

The limitations of DMNs arise from various factors related to their active ingredients, design, and application. The efficacy of a drug in DMN preparation depends heavily on its physicochemical characteristics and stability, loading quantity, and compatibility with the DMN matrix (Chu and Prausnitz, 2011; Hiraishi et al., 2013). In terms of design, the treatment effectiveness is influenced by the dimensions, density, and composition of the DMN base (He et al., 2020; Li et al., 2020). Application factors, including application location, skin thickness, and differences between healthy and inflamed skin conditions, can also affect drug efficacy. In terms of drug concentration (Benítez and Montáns, 2017), DMN development must ensure bioavailability, systemic stability, and regulated release kinetics to avoid dose fluctuations and minimize systemic toxicity. These limitations may directly influence the therapeutic performance of DMNs. Drugs with poor physicochemical stability or limited compatibility with the polymer matrix may exhibit reduced loading efficiency and inconsistent release behavior, potentially compromising therapeutic outcomes. Likewise, microneedle design parameters, including needle geometry, dimensions, density, and mechanical strength, play critical roles in insertion capability, penetration depth, and drug delivery efficiency. Inappropriate design characteristics may reduce skin penetration and limit drug deposition at the target sites. An important limitation of DMNs is their relatively limited drug-loading capacity. Because drug incorporation is restricted by the small volume of the microneedle matrix, DMNs may not be suitable for therapies requiring high drug doses (Sartawi et al., 2022). Achieving adequate drug delivery for high-dose therapies therefore remains a major unresolved challenge, especially because effective therapeutic concentrations and dose precision are still difficult to guarantee consistently (Gopal et al., 2026).

The scalability of dissolvable microneedles (DMNs) is a critical factor for their successful clinical translation and commercialization. As DMN formulations become increasingly sophisticated through the incorporation of multiple drugs, nanocarriers, or compartmentalized structures, manufacturing processes become more challenging owing to increased formulation heterogeneity and additional processing requirements (Shriky et al., 2023). These complexities may influence mold filling, drying behavior, drug distribution, and batch-to-batch reproducibility, potentially affecting product consistency during large-scale production. Furthermore, scalable manufacturing requires rigorous quality control to ensure uniform needle geometry, mechanical strength, drug content, insertion performance, and dissolution characteristics (Weimer et al., 2021). The lack of standardized quality assessment methods and regulatory guidelines remains a significant barrier to industrial implementation, highlighting the need for harmonized testing strategies to support product development and regulatory approval (Weimer et al., 2021). Therefore, future DMN development should not only focus on therapeutic performance but also consider manufacturability and quality assurance to facilitate broader clinical adoption. (Dave et al., 2024).

The development of DMNs for therapeutics faces challenges in reaching the market because of concerns related to their drug efficacy. Therefore, clinical trials need to be explored more thoroughly, focusing on the evaluation and efficacy of drug absorption, and appropriate participant selection. Determining the optimal dosage of active ingredients is crucial for achieving therapeutic potential and ensuring participant safety (Sartawi et al., 2022). The materials used had the lowest potential for adverse reactions. Comprehensive data collection and analytical techniques are necessary to obtain accurate results. In the future, the development of DMNs is expected to focus on improving system performance, including optimizing needle geometry, polymer composition, and fabrication techniques to enhance mechanical strength, drug loading capacity, and delivery efficiency without compromising the dissolution capability (Peng et al., 2025).

Regulatory strategies for the clinical translation of DMNs

Despite the considerable potential of DMNs for transdermal drug delivery, their clinical translation remains challenging because of the lack of globally harmonized regulatory guidelines. Currently, the regulatory classification of microneedle-based products is largely determined by their design and intended applications. Drug-loaded DMNs are generally regulated as drug-device combination products because both the incorporated drug and the microneedle platform contribute to the therapeutic effect. Similarly, vaccine-loaded microneedles may require additional evaluation under biologic regulations, whereas hydrogel-forming microneedles integrated with drug reservoirs necessitate the assessment of both the delivery platform and reservoir system as an integrated combination product. In contrast, microneedles developed for diagnostic or biosensing applications are typically regulated as medical devices, whereas cosmetic microneedles without pharmacologically active ingredients may follow medical device or cosmetic product regulations, depending on regional regulatory frameworks (Gandhat et al., 2025). From a translational perspective, regulatory approval requires comprehensive evidence of product quality, safety, and efficacy. Regulatory agencies emphasize the importance of manufacturing consistency, sterility assurance, stability, dose reproducibility, and appropriate quality control strategies to ensure reliable clinical outcomes. However, the absence of standardized regulatory frameworks, along with challenges related to large-scale manufacturing, dose consistency, and quality control, continues to limit the commercialization of microneedle-based products (Gandhat et al., 2025; Moawad et al., 2025).

Recent advances in DMNs research

Recent advances in DMNs research have increasingly focused on developing intelligent, personalized, and multifunctional delivery systems. One notable trend is the integration of three-dimensional (3D) printing technologies, which enable precise control of microneedle geometry, customizable architectures, and patient-specific designs, while improving manufacturing flexibility compared with conventional molding approaches (Prabhu et al., 2025). In addition, DMNs are evolving beyond traditional drug delivery applications toward theranostic platforms capable of combining therapeutic administration with biomarker monitoring through interstitial fluid analysis. Such systems offer opportunities for real-time disease monitoring and personalized treatment strategies (G. Kim et al., 2024). Furthermore, the application of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) has emerged as a promising approach for optimizing microneedle design, predicting mechanical performance, and accelerating formulation development, thereby enhancing the efficiency of DMN research and translation (W. He et al., 2024). These developments demonstrate a shift from conventional DMN-based drug delivery to integrated biomedical platforms with enhanced precision, functionality, and clinical potential. In addition to therapeutic applications, microneedles have been explored for diagnostic and biosensing purposes through the collection of interstitial fluid and biomarker monitoring. These developments demonstrate the versatility of microneedle technology beyond conventional transdermal administration and highlight its potential as a minimally invasive platform for site-specific drug delivery and biomedical applications (Gandhat et al., 2025).

Conclusion

DMNs represent a significant advancement in transdermal and transmucosal drug delivery systems for the treatment of inflammatory diseases. The selection of a biocompatible polymer base and its integration with nanotechnology, polymer encapsulation, and microemulsions have resulted in an effective and safe delivery system. Recent advances in DMNs modification, such as controlled release using stimuli-responsive techniques, have expanded the potential of DMNs to deliver a variety of anti-inflammatory agents to organs such as the skin, eyes, joints, and oral mucosa. Despite their promising prospects, several challenges must be addressed to support the clinical translation of DMNs. These challenges include dose consistency, formulation stability, long-term safety evaluation, and the development of sterile and economical large-scale production processes for these vaccines. Furthermore, standardization of testing methods and clear regulations are needed to ensure product quality, safety, and effectiveness. With innovative support in biomaterials and drug delivery technology, DMNs have the potential to become the next generation transdermal therapeutic platform for more effective and patient-centered inflammatory disease management.
