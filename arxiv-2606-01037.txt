A further study of polynomial g n , q g_{n,q} over finite fields
Neranga Fernando, Bhitali Kousik

Abstract
. Let n ≥ 0 n\geq 0 be an integer and q q a prime power. The polynomial g n , q g_{n,q} was introduced in [ 10 ] with the purpose of finding new classes of permutation polynomials over finite fields. We investigate the permutation behaviour of the polynomial g n , q ​ ( 𝚇 ) g_{n,q}({\tt X}) over finite fields of even characteristic. We introduce the multivariate case of the polynomial g n , q g_{n,q} , and study the permutation polynomials in several variables and local permutation polynomials resulting from the polynomials g n , q ​ ( 𝚇 1 , 𝚇 2 , … , 𝚇 k ) g_{n,q}({\tt X}_{1},{\tt X}_{2},\ldots,{\tt X}_{k}) . We also present several new identities of g n , q ​ ( 𝚇 ) g_{n,q}({\tt X}) , and present some open questions on the permutation property of g n , q ​ ( 𝚇 ) g_{n,q}({\tt X}) .



1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime,
e
and
m
be positive integers,
q=p^{m}
, and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
the finite field with
q
elements. A polynomial
f({\tt X})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[{\tt X}]
is a called a
permutation polynomial
if the associated mapping from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a permutation. It is a well-known fact that every mapping from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can be uniquely represented by a polynomial of degree lees than
q
, and the unique polynomial can be found by Lagrange Interpolation. Finding new classes of permutation polynomials over finite fields has been a hot topic since the 1970s due to their applications in the areas of cryptography, coding theory, combinatorics, finite geometry, and computer science.

The study of permutation polynomials over finite fields has a long history. It was Charles Hermite who studied permutation polynomials over finite prime fields in the 1860s. Leonard Eugene Dickson studied permutation polynomials over finite arbitrary fields while he was a PhD student in the 1890s. Issai Schur later named these polynomials in Dickson’s honor. Dickson polynomials have played a pivotal role in the area of permutation polynomials over finite fields, and the permutation property of the Dickson polynomials is completely known.

In 2009, Xiang-dong Hou, Gary Mullen, James Sellers and Joseph Yucas introduced the twin of Dickson polynomials, reversed Dickson polynomials, by interchanging the roles of the variable and the parameter of Dickson polynomials. Polynomial
g_{n,q}
was introduced by Xiang-dong Hou in
[
10
]
as a
q
-ary version of the reversed Dickson polynomial, but its permutation behaviour was first studied in
[
11
]
. In
[
10
]
, the author showed that for each integer
n\geq 0
, there exists a unique polynomial
g_{n,q}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[{\tt X}]
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{a\hskip 2.84544pt\in\hskip 2.84544pt\mathbb{F}_{q}}({{\tt X}}+a)^{n}=g_{n,q}({{\tt X}}^{q}-{{\tt X}}).

The explicit form of
g_{n,q}
is given by Waring’s formula

(1.2)

g_{n,q}({{\tt X}})=\sum_{\frac{n}{q}\leq l\leq\frac{n}{q-1}}\frac{n}{l}\dbinom{l}{n-l(q-1)}{{\tt X}}^{n-l(q-1)}.

If
g_{n,q}
is a PP of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
, we say that the triple
(n,e;q)
is
desirable
. Since 2011, several articles have appeared in the literature on polynomial
g_{n,q}
:
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
, and
[
12
]
, to name a few. The authors in
[
20
]
showed that the generalized almost perfect nonlinear (GAPN) functions are related to the polynomial
g_{n,q}
. In fact, they showed that If
g_{n,p}({\tt X})
is a permutation of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{e}}
, then
f({\tt X})={\tt X}^{n}
is a GAPN function.

Even though the permutation behaviour of the polynomial
g_{n,q}
has been studied extensively, multivariate case of the polynomial has not appeared in the literature. In this paper, we introduce the multivariate case of the polynomial
g_{n,q}
and derive an explicit formula for the polynomial in several variables. We first show that

g_{n,q}({\tt X},{\tt Y})=g_{n,q}({\tt X})\circ e_{1}({\tt X},{\tt Y}),

where
e_{1}({\tt X},{\tt Y})
is the elementary symmetric polynomials in two variables, which is immediately followed by the generalization to several variables:

g_{n,q}({\tt X}_{1},{\tt X}_{2},\ldots,{\tt X}_{k})=g_{n,q}({\tt X})\circ e_{1}({\tt X}_{1},{\tt X}_{2},\ldots,{\tt X}_{k}),

where
e_{1}({\tt X}_{1},{\tt X}_{2},\ldots,{\tt X}_{k})
is the elementary symmetric polynomial in
k
variables.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
be the
k
-fold cartesian product of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
k\geq 1
is an integer. Let
\overline{{\tt X}}=({\tt X}_{1},\ldots,{\tt X}_{k})
,
\overline{{\tt X}}_{i}=({\tt X}_{1},\ldots,{\tt X}_{i-1},{\tt X}_{i+1},\ldots,{\tt X}_{k})
, and
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\overline{{\tt X}}]
denote the ring of polynomials in
k
variables over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. It is well-known that any map from
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can be uniquely represented as a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[\overline{{\tt X}}]
such that
\text{deg}_{{\tt X}_{i}}(f)<q
for all
i=1,\ldots,k
, where
\text{deg}_{{\tt X}_{i}}(f)
is the degree of
f
as a polynomial in the variable
{\tt X}_{i}
with coefficients in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\overline{{\tt X}}_{i}]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[{\tt X}_{1},\ldots,{\tt X}_{k}]
is a
permutation polynomial
in
k
variables over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if the equation
f({\tt X}_{1},\ldots,{\tt X}_{k})=\alpha
has exactly
q^{k-1}
solutions in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Definition 1.2
.

A polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[\overline{{\tt X}}]
is called a
local permutation polynomial
(or LPP) if for each
i
,
1\leq i\leq k
, the polynomial
f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},{\tt X}_{i},a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{k})
is a permutation polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[{\tt X}_{i}]
, for all choices of
\overline{a}_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k-1}
.

Local permutation polynomials have been studied by many due to their applications in the areas of cryptography, coding theory and latin squares. Any LPP is a permutation polynomial, but the converse is not true in general. For example, the polynomial
{\tt X}_{1}^{q-1}+{\tt X}_{2}
is a permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, but not a local permutation polynomial. To the best of our knowledge, local permutation polynomials were first studied by Gary L. Mullen in
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
. He gave necessary and sufficient conditions for polynomials in two and three variables to be local permutations polynomials over a finite prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
in
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
, respectively. Those conditions were expressed in terms of the coefficients of the polynomial.

For two variables, it was shown in
[
2
]
that the degree of an LLP in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x,y]
is bounded above by
2(q-2)
. In
[
7
]
, the authors showed that the result is true for several variables. We refer the reader to
[
2
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
16
]
, and
[
17
]
for detailed studies on local permutation polynomials.

In order to investigate the permutation polynomials in several variables and local permutation polynomials arising from
g_{n,q}({\tt X}_{1},{\tt X}_{2},\ldots,{\tt X}_{k})
, we first derive some results that are analogous to that of the univariate case. We then show that studying the permutation behaviour or the local permutation behaviour of the multivarite
g
-polynomial is equivalent to studying the permutation behaviour of its univariate case. Because of this reason, we take a new approach and investigate the permutation and the local permutation behaviour of the polynomial

g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}({\tt X}_{1},\,{\tt X}_{2},\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k})=g_{n,q}(x)\circ\sigma_{\ell}({\tt X}_{1},\,{\tt X}_{2},\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k}),

where
\sigma_{\ell}=\sigma_{\ell}({\tt X}_{1},\,{\tt X}_{2},\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k})={\tt X}_{1}^{\ell}+{\tt X}_{2}^{\ell}+\cdots+{\tt X}_{k}^{\ell},

where
\ell\geq 1.

We first show that if
\gcd(\ell,\,q^{e}-1)=1
, then
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}[\bar{{\tt X}}]
is a (local) permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
if and only if
g_{n,q}({\tt X})
is a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
. We then show that
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}
can never be an LPP over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
whenever
\gcd(\ell,\,q^{e}-1)\neq 1
. We also conjecture that the polynomial
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}[{\tt X}_{1},{\tt X}_{2},\ldots,{\tt X}_{k}]
is a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
if and only if
\text{gcd}(\ell,q^{e}-1)=1
and
g_{n,q}
is a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
.

We would like to point out to the reader that even though the multivariate polynomials resulting from
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}({\tt X}_{1},\,{\tt X}_{2},\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k})
may not necessarily belong to the family of polynomial
g_{n,q}({\tt X}_{1},\,{\tt X}_{2},\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k})
, we believe that our results on the permutation property and the local permutation property of the polynomials
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}({\tt X}_{1},\,{\tt X}_{2},\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k})
would complement the area of LPPs and PPs in several variables over finite fields.

2.
Some new identities of the polynomial
g_{n,q}({\tt X})

Let
k\geq 0
be an integer. For a fixed
q,
we define
S_{k}={\tt X}+{\tt X}^{q}+{\tt X}^{q^{2}}+\cdots+{\tt X}^{q^{k-1}}
for
k\geq 1
and
S_{0}=0.
Note that,
\text{Tr}_{q^{e}/q}=S_{e},
where
\text{Tr}_{q^{e}/q}
is the trace function from
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q}.

The following three results from the literature relate the polynomial
g_{n,q}
to
S_{k}
, and they will be useful in deriving further identities of
g_{n,q}.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[
11
, Eqn 4.1]
)
For integers
l,\,k\geq 0,
we have

g_{l+q^{k},q}=g_{l+1,q}+S_{k}\,g_{l,q}.

In Section 4, we will explain the multivariate case of Proposition
2.1
.

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
5
, Theorem 6.1]
)

Let
q\geq 4
be even, and let

n=1+q^{a_{1}}+q^{b_{1}}+\cdots+q^{a_{q/2}}+q^{b_{q/2}},

where
a_{i},b_{i}\geq 0
are integers.
Then

g_{n,q}=\sum_{i}S_{a_{i}}S_{b_{i}}+\sum_{i<j}(S_{a_{i}}+S_{b_{i}})(S_{a_{j}}+S_{b_{j}}).

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
5
, Lemma 6.5]
)

Let
n=(q-1)q^{a}+(q-1)q^{b}
, where
a,b\geq 0
. Then

g_{n,q}=-1-(S_{b}-S_{a})^{q-1}.

For the rest of the section, we list twelve new identities of the polynomial
g_{n,q}(x)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
q>2
be even and
n=1\cdot q^{0}+(q-1)\cdot q^{a}+(q-1)\cdot q^{b}
, where
a,b\geq 0
. Then

g_{1\cdot q^{0}+(q-1)\cdot q^{a}+(q-1)\cdot q^{b}}=S_{b-a+1}^{q^{a}}+S_{b}+S_{b}\,(S_{b}-S_{a})^{q-1}.

Proof.

We have

g_{(q-1)\cdot q^{a}+q^{b+1}}=g_{1\cdot q^{0}+(q-1)\cdot q^{a}+(q-1)\cdot q^{b}}+S_{b}\cdot g_{(q-1)\cdot q^{a}+(q-1)\cdot q^{b}},

which implies

{\tt X}^{q^{a}}+{\tt X}^{q^{a+1}}+\cdots+{\tt X}^{q^{b-1}}+{\tt X}^{q^{b}}=g_{1\cdot q^{0}+(q-1)\cdot q^{a}+(q-1)\cdot q^{b}}+S_{b}\,(-1-(S_{b}-S_{a})^{q-1}).

Thus we have

\begin{split}g_{1\cdot q^{0}+(q-1)\cdot q^{a}+(q-1)\cdot q^{b}}&=({\tt X}+{\tt X}^{q}+{\tt X}^{q^{2}}+\cdots+{\tt X}^{q^{b-a}})^{q^{a}}-S_{b}\,(-1-(S_{b}-S_{a})^{q-1})\cr&=S_{b-a+1}^{q^{a}}+S_{b}+S_{b}\,(S_{b}-S_{a})^{q-1}\end{split}

This completes the proof.

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
a,b\geq 0
be integers. Then

g_{(q-1)\cdot q^{a}+(q-2)\cdot q^{b}}=-(S_{b}-S_{a})^{q-2}.

Proof.

\begin{split}(S_{b}-S_{a})g_{(q-1)\cdot q^{a}+(q-2)\cdot q^{b}}&=g_{(q-1)\cdot q^{a}+(q-2)\cdot q^{b}+q^{b}}-g_{(q-1)\cdot q^{a}+(q-2)\cdot q^{b}+q^{a}}\cr&=g_{(q-1)\cdot q^{a}+(q-1)\cdot q^{b}}-g_{q^{a+1}+(q-2)\cdot q^{b}}\cr&=-1-(S_{b}-S_{a})^{q-1}-(-1)\cr\end{split}

This completes the proof.

∎

In the following lemma, we list seven more idendities of the polynomial
g_{n,q}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
a,b\geq 0
be integers. Then

(1)

g_{(q-2)\cdot q^{a}+(q-1)\cdot q^{b}}=(S_{b}-S_{a})^{q-2},

(2)

g_{(q-2)q^{a}+(q-2)q^{b}}=2(S_{b}-S_{a})^{q-3},

(3)

g_{(q-1)q^{a}+(q-3)q^{b}}=-(S_{b}-S_{a})^{q-3},

(4)

g_{(q-3)q^{a}+(q-1)q^{b}}=-(S_{b}-S_{a})^{q-3},

(5)

g_{(q-3)q^{a}+(q-2)q^{b}}=-3(S_{b}-S_{a})^{q-4},

(6)

g_{(q-3)q^{a}+(q-2)q^{b}}=3(S_{b}-S_{a})^{q-4},

(7)

g_{(q-3)q^{a}+(q-3)q^{b}}=-6(S_{b}-S_{a})^{q-5}.

We omit the proofs of the identities of Lemma
2.6
as they are similar to that of Lemma
2.5

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
l=(q-3)q^{0}+(q-3)q^{1}.
Then

g_{l+q^{2}}=3S_{1}^{q-4}-6S_{2}S_{1}^{q-5},

and

g_{l+2q^{2}}=-S_{1}^{q-3}+6S_{2}S_{1}^{q-4}-6S_{2}^{2}S_{1}^{q-5}.

Proof.

The result follows by applying the identities obtained in the previous lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
n=(q-3)q^{0}+(q-3)q^{1}+3q^{2}.
Then

g_{n,q}=-3S_{2}S_{1}^{q-3}+9S_{2}^{2}S_{1}^{q-4}-6S_{2}^{3}S_{1}^{q-5}.

Proof.

Let
l=(q-3)q^{0}+(q-3)q^{1}.
We have

\displaystyle g_{l+3q^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{l+2q^{2}+1}+S_{2}g_{l+2q^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{l+2q^{2}+q}-S_{1}g_{l+2q^{2}}+S_{2}g_{l+2q^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{l+2q^{2}}(S_{2}-S_{1})+g_{(q-3)q^{0}+(q-2)q^{1}+2q^{2}}.

We now compute
g_{j+2q^{2}},
where
j=(q-3)q^{0}+(q-2)q^{1}.
To do so, we first determine
g_{j+q^{2}}.

g_{j+q^{2}}=g_{j+1}+S_{2}g_{j}=2S_{1}^{q-3}-3S_{2}S_{1}^{q-4}.

Now,

\begin{split}g_{j+2q^{2}}&=g_{j+q^{2}+1}+S_{2}g_{j+q^{2}}=g_{j+2}+S_{2}g_{j+1}+S_{2}g_{j+q^{2}}\cr&=-S_{1}^{q-2}+2S_{2}S_{1}^{q-3}+2S_{2}S_{1}^{q-3}-3S_{2}^{2}S_{1}^{q-4}\cr&=-S_{1}^{q-2}+4S_{2}S_{1}^{q-3}-3S_{2}^{2}S_{1}^{q-4}.\end{split}

Thus by using the value of
g_{l+2q^{2}}
from Lemma
2.7
, we have,

\begin{split}g_{l+3q^{2}}&=(-S_{1}^{q-3}+6S_{2}S_{1}^{q-4}-6S_{2}^{2}S_{1}^{q-5})(S_{2}-S_{1})-S_{1}^{q-2}+4S_{2}S_{1}^{q-3}-3S_{2}^{2}S_{1}^{q-4}\cr&=-3S_{2}S_{1}^{q-3}+9S_{2}^{2}S_{1}^{q-4}-6S_{2}^{3}S_{1}^{q-5}.\end{split}

∎

For convenience, we write
A=g_{j+2q^{2}}
and
B=g_{l+3q^{2}}
, with these expressions coming from the proof of the preceding lemma.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
n=(q-3)q^{0}+(q-3)q^{1}+4q^{2}.
Then
g_{n,q}=-1+6S_{2}^{2}S_{1}^{q-5}(S_{2}^{2}+2S_{2}S_{1}-S_{1}^{2}).

Proof.

Let
l=(q-3)q^{0}+(q-3)q^{1}.
We have

\begin{split}g_{l+4q^{2}}&=g_{l+3q^{2}+1}+S_{2}g_{l+3q^{2}}\cr&=g_{l+3q^{2}+q^{1}}-S_{1}g_{l+3q^{2}}+S_{2}g_{l+3q^{2}}\cr&=g_{l+q^{1}+2q^{2}+1}+S_{2}g_{l+q^{1}+2q^{2}}+(S_{2}-S_{1})g_{l+3q^{2}}\cr&=g_{(q-3)q^{0}+(q-1)q^{1}+2q^{2}}+(S_{2}-S_{1})(A+B)\end{split}

Next, we determine
g_{j+q^{1}}
and
g_{j+q^{2}},
where
j=(q-3)q^{0}+(q-1)q^{1}.

g_{j+q^{2}}=g_{j+1}+S_{2}g_{j}=S_{1}^{q-2}-S_{2}S_{1}^{q-3},

and

\begin{split}g_{j+2q^{2}}&=g_{j+q^{2}+1}+S_{2}g_{j+q^{2}}=g_{j+2}+S_{2}g_{j+1}+S_{2}g_{j+q^{2}}\cr&=-1-S_{1}^{q-1}+2S_{2}S_{1}^{q-2}-S_{2}^{2}S_{1}^{q-3}.\end{split}

Also,
A+B=-S_{1}^{q-2}+S_{2}S_{1}^{q-3}+6S_{2}^{2}S_{1}^{q-4}-6S_{2}^{3}S_{1}^{q-5}.
Hence, after simplification, we have

\begin{split}g_{l+4q^{2}}&=-1-S_{1}^{q-1}+2S_{2}S_{1}^{q-2}-S_{2}^{2}S_{1}^{q-3}\cr&+(S_{2}-S_{1})(-S_{1}^{q-2}+S_{2}S_{1}^{q-3}+6S_{2}^{2}S_{1}^{q-4}-6S_{2}^{3}S_{1}^{q-5})\cr&=-1+6S_{2}^{2}S_{1}^{q-5}(S_{2}^{2}+2S_{2}S_{1}-S_{1}^{2}).\end{split}

Applying
S_{2}={\tt X}+{\tt X}^{q}
and
S_{1}={\tt X}
to the above expression, we obtain

g_{l+4q^{2},q}=-1-6{\tt X}^{5q-5}-12{\tt X}^{4q-4}-6{\tt X}^{3q-3}.

∎

4.
Multivariate Case

In this section, we introduce the bivariate case of the polynomial
g_{n,q}
.

4.1.
Bivariate case

In
\mathbb{F}_{q}[{\tt X},{\tt Y}]
, we have

\displaystyle{({\tt X}+{\tt Y})^{q}-({\tt X}+{\tt Y})=\prod_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}({\tt X}+{\tt Y}+a)}.

Let
{\tt t}
be another indeterminate and replace
{\tt X}+{\tt Y}
by
{\tt t}+{\tt X}+{\tt Y}
to obtain

\displaystyle{({\tt t}+{\tt X}+{\tt Y})^{q}-({\tt t}+{\tt X}+{\tt Y})=\prod_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}({\tt t}+{\tt X}+{\tt Y}+a)}.

The left-hand side is equal to
{\tt t}^{q}-{\tt t}+({\tt X}^{q}-{\tt X})+({\tt Y}^{q}-{\tt Y})
.

The product
\displaystyle{\prod_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}({\tt t}+{\tt X}+{\tt Y}+a)}
can be written in terms of the elementary symmetric polynomials (as described in Preliminaries) as follows:

\displaystyle{\prod_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}({\tt t}+{\tt X}+{\tt Y}+a)}=\sum_{k=0}^{q}\,\Big(\sigma_{k}({\tt X}+{\tt Y}+a)_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\Big){\tt t}^{q-k}.

We have

{\tt t}^{q}-{\tt t}+({\tt X}^{q}-{\tt X})+({\tt Y}^{q}-{\tt Y})=\sum_{k=0}^{q}\,\sigma_{k}\Big(({\tt X}+{\tt Y}+a)_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\Big)\,{\tt t}^{q-k}.

By comparing the coefficients of
{\tt t}
, we get

(4.1)

\sigma_{k}\Big(({\tt X}+{\tt Y}+a)_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\Big)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\,\,k=0,\\
-1&\text{if}\,\,k=q-1,\\
({\tt X}^{q}-{\tt X})+({\tt Y}^{q}-{\tt Y})&\text{if}\,\,k=q,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
n\geq 0
be an integer. Then by Waring’s formula, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}({\tt X}+{\tt Y}+a)^{n}&=\sum_{\alpha(q-1)+\beta q=n}\,(-1)^{\alpha}\frac{(\alpha+\beta-1)!n}{\alpha!\beta!}(-1)^{\alpha}({\tt X}^{q}-{\tt X}+{\tt Y}^{q}-{\tt Y})^{\beta}\cr&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l=\alpha+\beta}}{{=}}\sum_{\frac{n}{q}\leq l\leq\frac{n}{q-1}}\,(-1)^{\alpha}\frac{(l-1)!n}{(lq-n)!(n-l(q-1))!}(-1)^{\alpha}({\tt X}^{q}-{\tt X}+{\tt Y}^{q}-{\tt Y})^{n-l(q-1)}\cr&=\sum_{\frac{n}{q}\leq l\leq\frac{n}{q-1}}\,\frac{n}{l}\,\binom{l}{n-l(q-1)}\,({\tt X}^{q}-{\tt X}+{\tt Y}^{q}-{\tt Y})^{n-l(q-1)}\end{split}

Set

g_{n,q}({\tt X},{\tt Y})=\sum_{\frac{n}{q}\leq l\leq\frac{n}{q-1}}\,\frac{n}{l}\,\binom{l}{n-l(q-1)}\,({\tt X}+{\tt Y})^{n-l(q-1)}\,\,\in\mathbb{Z}[{\tt X},{\tt Y}]

Equivalently,

g_{n,q}({\tt X},{\tt Y})=\sum_{\frac{n}{q}\leq l\leq\frac{n}{q-1}}\,\frac{n}{l}\,\binom{l}{n-l(q-1)}\,(e_{1}({\tt X},{\tt Y}))^{n-l(q-1)}\,\,\in\mathbb{Z}[{\tt X},{\tt Y}]

The coefficients of the polynomial are integers because the coefficients in the Waring’s formula are integers. In
\mathbb{F}_{q}[{\tt X},{\tt Y}]
, we have

\displaystyle{\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}({\tt X}+{\tt Y}+a)^{n}=g_{n,q}({\tt X}^{q}-{\tt X},{\tt Y}^{q}-{\tt Y})}.

Remark 4.1
.

We point out to the reader that the bivariate polynomial
g_{n,q}({\tt X},{\tt Y})
is the composition of the univariate polynomial
g_{n,q}({\tt X})
and the elementary symmetric polynomial
e_{1}({\tt X},{\tt Y})
:

\begin{split}g_{n,q}({\tt X},{\tt Y})&=\sum_{\frac{n}{q}\leq l\leq\frac{n}{q-1}}\,\frac{n}{l}\,\binom{l}{n-l(q-1)}\,(e_{1}({\tt X},{\tt Y}))^{n-l(q-1)}\cr&=\sum_{\frac{n}{q}\leq l\leq\frac{n}{q-1}}\,\frac{n}{l}\,\binom{l}{n-l(q-1)}\,{\tt X}^{n-l(q-1)}\circ e_{1}({\tt X},{\tt Y})\cr&=g_{n,q}({\tt X})\circ e_{1}({\tt X},{\tt Y})\end{split}

Proposition 5.5
.

The polynomial

Conversely, suppose that

\mathbb{F}_{Q}.
As

is a PP over

be

As
e_{1}({\tt X},\,{\tt Y})
is a permutation polynomial, for each
z\in\mathbb{F}_{Q},\,e_{1}({\tt X},\,{\tt Y})={\tt Z}
has
Q
solutions in
\mathbb{F}_{Q}^{2}.
That is, each
{\tt Z}
contributes
Q
pairs of
({\tt X},\,{\tt Y})
to the equation
e_{1}({\tt X},\,{\tt Y})={\tt Z}
. Therefore, we have
NQ
pairs of
({\tt X},\,{\tt Y})\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}^{2}
such that
g_{n,q}(e_{1}({\tt X},\,{\tt Y}))=a
for each
a\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}.
But it is given that
g_{n,q}({\tt X},\,{\tt Y})
is a permutation polynomial. Hence,
NQ=Q,
which implies
N=1.
Thus we have the desired result.
∎

We obtain the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 5.7
.

The polynomial
g_{n,q}({\tt X},\,{\tt Y})
is an LPP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
if and only if it is a permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}.

As mentioned in the introduction, every local permutation polynomial is a permutation polynomial but the converse is not true in general. However, Corollary
5.7
says that converse also holds in the case of polynomial
g_{n,q}({\tt X},\,{\tt Y})
.

If we replace
g_{n,q}({\tt X})
by any non constant polynomial
f({\tt X})\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}
and
g_{n,q}({\tt X},\,{\tt Y})
by
f(h({\tt X},\,{\tt Y}))\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}[{\tt X},\,{\tt Y}],
where
h({\tt X},\,{\tt Y})
is an LPP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
, then the Proposition
5.5
, Proposition
5.6
and Corollary
5.7
still hold. We can rewrite them as follows.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
f({\tt X})\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}[x]
be any non constant polynomial and
g({\tt X},\,{\tt Y})=f(h({\tt X},\,{\tt Y})),
where
h({\tt X},\,{\tt Y})\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}[{\tt X},{\tt Y}]
be any LPP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

f({\tt X})
is a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
.

(2)

g({\tt X},\,{\tt Y})
is a local permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
.

(3)

g({\tt X},\,{\tt Y})
is a permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
(1)\implies(2)
follows from Lemma
5.4
. The implication
(2)\implies(3)
is obvious, and
(3)\implies(1)
follows from the proof of second part of Proposition
5.6
. Hence, all three statements are equivalent.
∎

We point out to the reader that Theorem
5.8
also holds in multivariate case.

Since the symmetric polynomial
e_{1}
with
k
variables is an LPP for each
k\geq 2
, studying the local permutation or permutation behaviour of the multivarite
g
-polynomial is equivalent to studying the permutation behaviour of its univariate case. Because the permutation beahviour of the univariate case has well been studied, for the rest of the paper, we will be investigating the permutation and the local permutation behaviour of the polynomial

g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}({\tt X}_{1},\,{\tt X}_{2},\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k})=g_{n,q}({\tt X})\circ\sigma_{\ell}({\tt X}_{1},\,{\tt X}_{2},\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k}),

where
\sigma_{\ell}=\sigma_{\ell}({\tt X}_{1},\,{\tt X}_{2},\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k})={\tt X}_{1}^{\ell}+{\tt X}_{2}^{\ell}+\cdots+{\tt X}_{k}^{\ell},

where
\ell\geq 1.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
\gcd(\ell,\,Q-1)=1.
Then
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}[\bar{{\tt X}}]
is a (local) permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
if and only if
g_{n,q}({\tt X})
is a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}.

Proof.

We have
\sigma_{\ell}({\tt X}_{1},\,{\tt X}_{2},\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k})=e_{1}({\tt X}_{1}^{\ell},\,{\tt X}_{2}^{\ell},\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k}^{\ell})
and
e_{1}
is always an LPP. Therefore by (2) of Lemma
5.4
,
\sigma_{\ell}
is a local permutation polynomial when
\gcd(\ell,\,Q-1)=1.
Thus by Theorem
5.8
,
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}
is an LPP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
if and only if
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}
is a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
if and only if
g_{n,q}({\tt X})
is a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

We point out to the reader that Corollary
5.7
is a particular case of Theorem
5.9
with
\ell=1
and
k=2
. Due to Theorem
5.9
, from now on, we study the case where
\gcd(\ell,\,Q-1)\neq 1
.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
\gcd(\ell,\,Q-1)\neq 1.
Then
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}
can never be an LPP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
5.2
that,
\sigma_{\ell}
is not an LPP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q},
whenever
\gcd(\ell,\,Q-1)\neq 1.
The proof follows from the fact that the composition of two univariate polynomials is not a PP whenever one of them is not a PP.
∎

Next, we will investigate the permutation behaviour of
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)},
when
\gcd(\ell,\,q^{e}-1)\neq 1.

Let
g_{n,q}
be a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
and
N
be the number of solutions of
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}(\bar{{\tt X}})=a,
where
a=g_{n,q}(b)
for some
b\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}.
As
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}(\bar{{\tt X}})=g_{n,q}(\sigma_{\ell}(\bar{{\tt X}}))
and
g_{n,q}
is a PP, we must have
\sigma_{\ell}(\bar{{\tt X}})=b.
This means that
\sigma_{\ell}(\bar{{\tt X}})=b
has exactly
N
solutions. If
N\neq Q^{k-1},
for some
b\in\mathbb{F}_{Q},
then
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}
is not a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
.

Thus, the problem reduces to counting the number of solutions of
\sigma_{l}({\tt X}_{1},\,{\tt X}_{2},\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k})=b
, a special class of diagonal equations over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
where all coefficients are equal to
1
and all exponents equal to
\ell
. A considerable amount of research has been devoted to the study of solutions of diagonal equations over finite fields. As a particular case of
[
14
, Theorem 6.33, Theorem 6.34]
, we have the following result.

Lemma 5.12
.

(
[
14
]
)
Let
b\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}.
The number of solutions
N
of
\sigma_{l}({\tt X}_{1},\,{\tt X}_{2},\,\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k})=b
in
\mathbb{F}_{Q}^{n}
is given by

N=Q^{k-1}+\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{d-1}\cdots\sum_{i_{k}=1}^{d-1}J_{0}(\lambda_{1}^{i_{1}},\dots,\lambda_{k}^{i_{k}}),

where
\lambda_{i},\,1\leq i\leq k
is a multiplicative character of
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
of order
d=\gcd(k,\,Q-1)
and

J_{b}(\lambda_{1}^{i_{1}},\dots,\lambda_{k}^{i_{k}})=\sum_{c_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}=b}\lambda_{1}^{i_{1}}(c_{1})\cdots\lambda_{k}^{i_{k}}(c_{k})

is the Jacobi sum.

Remark 5.13
.

Since
N
does not depend directly on the exponent
\ell,
but only on the greatest common divisor
d
of
\ell
and
Q-1
, we have
N(\sigma_{\ell}=b)=N(\sigma_{d}=b).

Proposition 5.14
.

If
\gcd(\ell,\,Q-1)=2,
then
\sigma_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}[{\tt X}_{1},\,\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k}]
is not a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}.

Proof.

By the result of Lidl and Niederreiter
[
14
, p. 284-285]
, the following explicit formula is obtained for the number of solutions
N
of
\sigma_{2}({\tt X}_{1},\,\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k})=b,
where
b\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}^{*}.

\begin{split}N&=\begin{cases}Q^{k-1}-Q^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\eta((-1)^{\frac{k}{2}})&\text{if}~k~\text{is even},\cr Q^{k-1}+Q^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\eta((-1)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}b)&\text{if}~k~\text{is odd},\end{cases}\end{split}

where
\eta
is the quadratic character of
\mathbb{F}_{Q}.
As
\eta(c)\neq 0,
for any
c\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}^{*},

N\neq Q^{k-1}.
Now it follows from Remark
5.13
that for any
b\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}^{*},
the number of solutions of
\sigma_{l}({\tt X}_{1},\,\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k})=b,
whenever
\gcd(\ell,\,Q-1)=2
, is never
Q^{k-1}.

∎

The following definition of
(p,\,r)
-
admissible
integers first appeared in
[
19
]
to the best of our knowledge.

Definition 5.15
.

Let
r>0
be an integer. An integer
d
is said to be
(p,\,r)
-
admissible
if
r
is the smallest integer such that
d\mid(p^{r}+1)
.

Lemma 5.16
.

[
19
, Theorem 2.14, Remark 7.3]

Let
N
denote the number of solutions of
\sigma_{d}({\tt X}_{1},\,\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k})=0
in
\mathbb{F}_{Q}^{n},
where
d>2
is a divisor of
(Q-1)
and
k>2.
Also assume that
(k,\,d)\neq(4,\,3).
Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

|N-Q^{k-1}|=M_{k}(d)(Q-1)Q^{\frac{k-2}{2}},
where
M_{k}(d)
is the cardinality of the set of all
(j_{1},\,j_{2},\,\dots,\,j_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
such that
1\leq j_{i}\leq d-1
and
j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{k}\equiv 0\pmod{d}.

(2)

Q=p^{s}
, where
s
is even, and
d
is
(p,\,r)
- admissible.

Moreover, if
k=2
, then
N=Q+(Q-1)(d-1)
.

The case
(k,\,d)=(4,\,3)
is explained in the following lemma that appeared in
[
1
]
.

Lemma 5.17
.

(
[
1
]
)
Let
Q=p^{s},
where
s
is even and a divisor
d\geq 3
of
(Q-1)
be
(p,\,r)
-admissible. Then the number of roots
N^{*}
of
\sigma_{d}({\tt X}_{1},\,\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k})
in
(\mathbb{F}_{Q}^{*})^{k}
is given by

(5.1)

N^{*}=\frac{1}{Q}\left((Q-1)^{k}+\frac{Q-1}{d}A(d)^{k}+\frac{(Q-1)(d-1)}{d}B(d)^{k}\right),

where

\begin{split}A(d)&=-1-(-1)^{\frac{s}{2r}}(d-1)Q^{\frac{1}{2}},\cr B(d)&=-1+(-1)^{\frac{s}{2r}}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

Proposition 5.18
.

\sigma_{\ell}({\tt X}_{1},\,{\tt X}_{2})
is a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
if and only if
\gcd(\ell,\,Q-1)=1.

Proof.

The necessity follows from Theorem
5.1
.

Assume to the contrary that
\gcd(\ell,\,Q-1)=d>1.
In fact we must have
d>2
because of Proposition
5.14
. Lemma
5.16
says that the number of solutions
N
of
\sigma_{d}(x_{1},\,x_{2})=0
in
\mathbb{F}_{Q}^{2}
is
Q+(Q-1)(d-1)\neq Q.
The desired result follows from Remark
5.13
.
∎

Remark 5.19
.

The following formula for
M_{k}(d)
appears in
[
14
, p. 293]
.

M_{k}(d)=(-1)^{k}+\sum_{r=1}^{k}(-1)^{k-r}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq k}\frac{d^{r}}{\text{lcm}~(\underbrace{d,\,d,\,\dots,\,d}_{r})}.

We simplify the expression to obtain

\begin{split}M_{k}(d)&=\begin{cases}1+\frac{(d-1)^{k}-1}{d}&\text{if}~k~\text{is even},\cr-1+\frac{(d-1)^{k}+1}{d}&\text{if}~k~\text{is odd},\end{cases}\end{split}

Clearly,
M_{k}(d)=0
if and only if
k
is odd and
d=2.

Proposition 5.20
.

Let
Q=p^{s},
where
s\equiv 0\pmod{2}
and
d=\gcd(\ell,\,Q-1)
be
(p,\,r)
-admissible. Then
\sigma_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}[{\tt X}_{1},\,\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k}],\,k>2
is never a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}.

Proof.

Case I:
(k,d)\neq(4,3).

By Lemma
5.16
, we have
|N-Q^{k-1}|=M_{k}(d)(Q-1)Q^{\frac{k-2}{k}},
where
N
denotes the number of solutions of
\sigma_{d}({\tt X}_{1},,\,\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k})=0
in
\mathbb{F}_{Q}^{k}.
Since
d>2,
Remark
5.19
implies that
M_{k}(d)\neq 0.
Consequently,
N\neq Q^{k-1},
which implies that
\sigma_{d}
is not a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
, and therefore the desired result follows from Remark
5.13
.

Case II:
(k,d)=(4,3).

Substituting this value in Equation
5.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle N^{*}(\sigma_{d})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{Q}\left[(Q-1)^{4}+\frac{Q-1}{3}(1+2Q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{4}+\frac{2(Q-1)}{3}(Q^{\frac{1}{2}}-1)^{4}\right]&\text{if}~\frac{s}{2r}~\text{is even},\cr\frac{1}{Q}\left[(Q-1)^{4}+\frac{Q-1}{3}(-1+2Q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{4}+\frac{2(Q-1)}{3}(Q^{\frac{1}{2}}+1)^{4}\right]&\text{if}~\frac{s}{2r}~\text{is odd},\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}Q^{3}+C_{1}&\text{if}~\frac{s}{2r}~\text{is even},\cr Q^{3}+C_{2}&\text{if}~\frac{s}{2r}~\text{is odd},\end{cases}\qquad\text{where}

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{-4Q^{3}+6Q^{2}-4Q+1}{Q}+\frac{Q-1}{3Q}(1+2Q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{4}+\frac{2(Q-1)}{3Q}(Q^{\frac{1}{2}}-1)^{4},

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{-4Q^{3}+6Q^{2}-4Q+1}{Q}+\frac{Q-1}{3Q}(-1+2Q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{4}+\frac{2(Q-1)}{3Q}(Q^{\frac{1}{2}}+1)^{4},

and
N^{*}(\sigma_{d})
denotes the number of solutions of
\sigma_{d}(x_{1},\,\dots,\,x_{k})=0
in
(\mathbb{F}_{Q}^{k})^{*}.

We claim that
C_{1}+1\neq 0
and
C_{2}+1\neq 0.
Let
Q^{1/2}=t,
where
t
is a positive integer. Then after simplification, we have

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=2t^{4}+8t^{3}+12t^{2}-8t-15,\,\text{and}

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle=2t^{4}-8t^{3}+12t^{2}+8t-15.

Now
C_{1}+1=0
if and only if
g_{1}(t)=2t^{4}+8t^{3}+12t^{2}-8t-14=0
and
C_{2}+1=0
if and only if
g_{2}(t)=2t^{4}-8t^{3}+12t^{2}+8t-14=0.
Since
t\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, the Rational Root Theorem implies that, any integer root of
g_{1}(t)
or
g_{2}(t)
must divide
14.
Hence, it suffices to check for only
t=2
and
t=7
since
t=Q^{1/2}
and
Q
is a square of a prime. A direct computation shows that
g_{i}(2)\neq 0
and
g_{i}(7)\neq 0
for any
i\in\{1,\,2\}.

Thus,
N=N^{*}(\sigma_{d})+1\neq Q^{3}
and it follows that
\sigma_{d}
is not a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q},
and therefore
\sigma_{\ell}
is not a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
by Remark
5.13
.
∎

Combining Proposition
5.14
, Proposition
5.18
and Proposition
5.20

Theorem 5.21
.

Let
q=p^{m},\,Q=p^{e},\,g_{n,q}
be a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
and assume that
\gcd(\ell,\,Q-1)=d>1.
Then the following statements hold.

(1)

g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}({\tt X}_{1},\,{\tt X}_{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}[{\tt X}_{1},\,{\tt X}_{2}]
is not a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}.

(2)

If
d=2,
then
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}
is not a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}.

(3)

If
d>2
is
(p,\,r)
-admissible and
me
is even then
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}
is not a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}.

Remark 5.22
.

If
g_{n,q}
is not a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q},
then there exists
a\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}
such that
a\neq g_{n,q}(b)
for any
b\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}.
If
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}
is a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q},
then
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}=a
has exactly
Q^{k-1}
solutions in
\mathbb{F}_{Q}^{k}.
It implies that
g_{n,q}(\sigma_{\ell}({\tt X}_{1},\,\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k}))=a
has exactly
Q^{k-1}
solutions in
\mathbb{F}_{Q}^{k}.
Consequently, we can write
a=g_{n,q}(b)
for some
b\in\mathbb{F}_{Q},
which is a contradiction.

Therefore, whenever univariate
g
-polynomial is not a PP, the multivariate
g
-polynomial
g^{(\ell)},\,\ell\geq 1
can never be a PP irrespective of the gcd condition.

Conjecture 5.23
.

Let
q=p^{m}
and
Q=p^{e}.
The polynomial
g_{n,q}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}[{\tt X}_{1},\,\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k}]
is a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}
if and only if
\gcd(\ell,\,Q-1)=1
and
g_{n,q}({\tt X})
is a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q}.

Remark 5.24
.

To prove the conjecture one only needs to show that if
\sigma_{\ell}\in\mathbb{F}_{Q}[{\tt X}_{1},\,\dots,\,{\tt X}_{k}]
is a PP over
\mathbb{F}_{Q},
then
\gcd(\ell,\,Q-1)=1
.
