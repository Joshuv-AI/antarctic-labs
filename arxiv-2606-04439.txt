Size Ramsey minimal graphs for uniform star forests
Pingting Fu, Zhidan Luo, Zhenyu Ni

Abstract
For given graphs G 1 , G 2 , … , G t G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{t} and G G , let G → ( G 1 , G 2 , … , G t ) G\rightarrow(G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{t}) denote that each t t -coloring of E ⁡ ( G ) E(G) yields a monochromatic copy of G i G_{i} in color i i for some i ∈ [ t ] i\in[t] . The size Ramsey number r ^ ​ ( G 1 , G 2 , … , G t ) \hat{r}(G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{t}) is the minimum size of G G such that G → ( G 1 , G 2 , … , G t ) G\rightarrow(G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{t}) . A graph G G is a size Ramsey minimal graph for ( G 1 , G 2 , … , G t ) (G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{t}) if G → ( G 1 , G 2 , … , G t ) G\rightarrow(G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{t}) and e ⁡ ( G ) = r ^ ​ ( G 1 , G 2 , … , G t ) e(G)=\hat{r}(G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{t}) . A star forest is a vertex-disjoint union of stars, and a uniform star forest is a star forest with the same size of each component. In 1978, Burr, Erdős, Faudree, Rousseau and Schelp, and in 2025, Davoodi, Javadi, Kamranian and Raeisi completely characterized the size minimal graphs for uniform star forests. In this paper, we completely characterize the size Ramsey minimal graphs for uniform star forests in multicolors. Preliminaries

For a given positive integer
t
, let
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{t},b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{t}
be positive integers and let
a=\sum_{s=1}^{t}a_{s}
. Moreover, Let
G
be a graph such that
G\rightarrow\left(a_{1}K_{1,b_{1}},a_{2}K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,b_{t}}\right)
. In the following, we firstly show that
\Delta(G)
is large.

Lemma 2.1

Let
G^{\prime}
be a graph with
\Delta(G^{\prime})\leq\sum_{s=1}^{t}n_{s}-t-1
. Then

G^{\prime}\not\rightarrow(K_{1,n_{1}},K_{1,n_{2}},\dots,K_{1,n_{t}}).

Proof:

Note that
\chi^{\prime}(G^{\prime})\leq\Delta(G^{\prime})+1\leq\sum_{s=1}^{t}n_{s}-t
by the Vizing Theorem
[
12
]
. Thus, there is a proper edge coloring of
G^{\prime}
with
\sum_{s=1}^{t}n_{s}-t
colors. In other words,
E(G^{\prime})
can be decomposed into
\sum_{s=1}^{t}n_{s}-t
edge-disjoint matchings. Color
n_{s}-1
matchings by color
s
for each
s\in[t]
. Consequently,
G\not\rightarrow\left(K_{1,n_{1}},K_{1,n_{2}},\dots,K_{1,n_{t}}\right)
.
\Box

We also need the following.

Lemma 2.2

Let
v\in V(G)
be a vertex. For each
s\in[t]
, if
a_{s}\geq 2
, then

G-\{v\}\rightarrow\left(a_{1}K_{1,b_{1}},\dots,a_{s-1}K_{1,b_{s-1}},(a_{s}-1)K_{1,b_{s}},a_{s+1}K_{1,b_{s+1}},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,b_{t}}\right).

Proof:

Otherwise, there is a
t
-coloring of
E(G-\{v\})
such that there is no monochromatic copy of
a_{j}K_{1,b_{j}}
in color
j
for each
j\in[t]\backslash\{s\}
and no monochromatic copy of
(a_{s}-1)K_{1,b_{s}}
in color
s
. Under this coloring of
G-\{v\}
, color all edges adjacent to
v
by color
s
. To create a monochromatic copy of
a_{s}K_{1,b_{s}}
in color
s
, we need at least two vertices that are not in
G-\{v\}
. Consequently,
G\not\rightarrow\left(a_{1}K_{1,b_{1}},a_{2}K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,b_{t}}\right)
. This is a contradiction.
\Box

3
Size Ramsey minimal graphs for uniform star forests

In this section, we characterize the size Ramsey minimal graphs for uniform star forests. For a given positive integer
t
, let
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{t}
and
b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{t}
be positive integers. Moreover, let
a=\sum_{s=1}^{t}a_{s}
and
b=\sum_{s=1}^{t}b_{s}-t+1
. Let
G
be a size Ramsey minimal graph for
(a_{1}K_{1,b_{1}},a_{2}K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,b_{t}})
. Thus,
e(G)=(a-t+1)b
by Theorem
1.5
. We firstly show that
\Delta(G)
is bounded.

Lemma 3.1

b-1\leq\Delta(G)\leq b
.

Proof:

If
\Delta(G)\leq b-2=\sum_{s=1}^{t}b_{s}-t-1
, then
G\not\rightarrow(K_{1,b_{1}},K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,K_{1,b_{t}})
by Lemma
2.1
, and thus
G\not\rightarrow(a_{1}K_{1,b_{1}},a_{2}K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,b_{t}})
since
K_{1,b_{s}}\subset a_{s}K_{1,b_{s}}
for each
s\in[t]
. This is a contradiction. Consequently,
\Delta(G)\geq b-1
.

If
a_{1}=a_{2}=\cdots=a_{t}=1
, then
e(G)=b
. Thus
\Delta(G)\leq b
, and we are done. Without loss of generality, suppose that
a_{1}\geq 2
. Let
v\in V(G)
be such that
d(v)=\Delta(G)
. By Lemma
2.2
,
G-\{v\}\rightarrow((a_{1}-1)K_{1,b_{1}},a_{2}K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,b_{t}})
. Thus,
e(G-\{v\})\geq(a-t)b
by Theorem
1.5
. Note that

(a-t+1)b=e(G)=d(v)+e(G-\{v\})\geq d(v)+(a-t)b.

Consequently,
d(v)\leq b
, and we are done.
\Box

For the convenience in the discussion, we need the following.

Lemma 3.2

Let
c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{t}
and
c
be positive integers. Let
G^{\prime}
be a graph, and let
E\subset E(G)
with
|E|=c
. If
G-E\not\rightarrow(c_{1}K_{1,b_{1}},c_{2}K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,c_{t}K_{1,b_{t}})
, then

G\not\rightarrow((c_{1}+p_{1})K_{1,b_{1}},(c_{2}+p_{2})K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,(c_{t}+p_{t})K_{1,b_{t}}),

where
p_{s}
is a nonnegative integer for each
s\in[t]
and
\sum_{s=1}^{t}p_{s}=c
.

Proof:

Note that an edge in color
s
creates at most a monochromatic copy of
K_{1,b_{s}}
in color
s
. Color
p_{s}
edges of
E
by color
s
for each
s\in[t]
. Thus,

G\not\rightarrow((c_{1}+p_{1})K_{1,b_{1}},(c_{2}+p_{2})K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,(c_{t}+p_{t})K_{1,b_{t}})

since
G-E\not\rightarrow(c_{1}K_{1,b_{1}},c_{2}K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,c_{t}K_{1,b_{t}})
.
\Box

Now, we are ready to proof Theorem
1.6
. In the following, we divide Theorem
1.6
into Lemma
3.3
, Theorem
3.4
, Theorem
3.5
, Theorem
3.6
and Theorem
3.7
.

Lemma 3.3

Suppose that
b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\cdots\geq b_{t}\geq 1
. If
G
is a size Ramsey minimal graph for
\left(K_{1,b_{1}},K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,K_{1,b_{t}}\right)
, then
G=K_{1,b}
, where
b=\sum_{s=1}^{t}b_{s}-t+1
. Moreover, if
b_{1}=b_{2}=2
and
b_{3}=1
, then also
G=K_{3}
.

Proof:

Note that
e(G)=b
by Theorem
1.5
. If
b=1
, then
b_{1}=b_{2}=\cdots=b_{t}=1
and
e(G)=1
. Thus,
G=K_{1,1}
, and we are done. Suppose that
b\geq 2
, and thus
b_{1}\geq 2
by our assumption. Let
C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{c}
be components of
G
, and then
c\geq 1
. Moreover, suppose that
e(C_{1})\geq e(C_{2})\geq\cdots\geq e(C_{c})
. Note that
\Delta(G)\geq b-1
by Lemma
3.1
. Thus,
e(C_{1})\geq b-1
. Furthermore,
c\leq 2
since
e(G)=b
.

If
c=2
, then
C_{2}=K_{1,1}
and
C_{1}=K_{1,b-1}
since
\Delta(G)\geq b-1
. Color the edge of
C_{2}
by color
1
, and color
b_{s}-1
edges of
C_{1}
by color
s
for each
s\in[t]
. Thus,
G\not\rightarrow\left(K_{1,b_{1}},K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,K_{1,b_{t}}\right)
since
b_{1}\geq 2
. This is a contradiction.

Consequently,
c=1
, and thus
G
is connected. If
\Delta(G)=b
, then
G=K_{1,b}
since
e(G)=b
, and we are done. Thus, suppose that
\Delta(G)=b-1
since
\Delta(G)\geq b-1
. Moreover,
G
is the union of
K_{1,b-1}
and
K_{1,1}
(they have at least one common vertex). If
b_{1}\geq 3
, then color the edge not in
K_{1,b-1}
by color
1
, and color
b_{s}-1
edges of
K_{1,b-1}
by color
s
for each
s\in[t]
. Thus,
G\not\rightarrow\left(K_{1,b_{1}},K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,K_{1,b_{t}}\right)
since
b_{1}\geq 3
. This is a contradiction.

Consequently,
b_{1}=2
. Suppose that
2=b_{1}=b_{2}=\cdots=b_{q}>b_{q+1}=b_{q+2}=\cdots=b_{t}=1
for some
q\in[t]
. If
q\geq 3
, then
b=q+1\geq 4
. Thus,
G
contains a copy of
2K_{1,1}
. Color the copy of
2K_{1,1}
by color
1
. Note that there are
b-2=q-1
uncolored edges. Color one of them by color
s
for each
s\in[q]\backslash[1]
. Thus,
G\not\rightarrow\left(K_{1,b_{1}},K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,K_{1,b_{t}}\right)
. This is a contradiction.

Consequently,
q\leq 2
. If
q=1
, then
e(G)=b=q+1=2
. It is impossible since
G
is connected and
\Delta(G)=b-1=1
. Consequently,
q=2
, and thus
b=q+1=3
. Therefore,
G=P_{4}
or
G=K_{3}
since
G
is connected,
e(G)=b=3
and
\Delta(G)=b-1=2
. Note that
P_{4}\not\rightarrow(K_{1,2},K_{1,2})
, and thus
P_{4}\not\rightarrow(K_{1,b_{1}},K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,K_{1,b_{t}})
. Consequently,
G=K_{3}
, and we are done.
\Box

Theorem 3.4

Suppose that
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{t}\geq 1
. If
G
is a size Ramsey minimal graph for
\left(a_{1}K_{1,1},a_{2}K_{1,1},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,1}\right)
, then
G=(a-t+1)K_{1,1}
, where
a=\sum_{s=1}^{t}a_{s}
.

Proof:

The assertion holds for
a=t
by Lemma
3.3
. Suppose that
a\geq t+1
, and thus
a_{1}\geq 2
. Note that
e(G)=a-t+1
by Theorem
1.5
. Let
C_{1},C_{2},\cdots,C_{c}
be components of
G
, and then
c\geq 1
. Moreover, suppose that
e(C_{1})\geq e(C_{2})\geq\cdots\geq e(C_{c})
. If
e(C_{1})\geq 2
, then color two incident edges in
C_{1}
by color
1
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph induced by colored edges. Note that
G^{\prime}\not\rightarrow(2K_{1,1},K_{1,1},\dots,K_{1,1})
. Moreover, there are
a-t+1-2=a-t-1
uncolored edges. Thus,
G\not\rightarrow\left(a_{1}K_{1,1},a_{2}K_{1,1},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,1}\right)
by Lemma
3.2
. This is a contradiction. Consequently,
e(C_{1})=1
. Furthermore,
G=(a-t+1)K_{1,1}
since
e(G)=a-t+1
, and we are done.
\Box

Theorem 3.5

Suppose that
b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\cdots\geq b_{t}\geq 1
. Moreover, suppose that
b_{1}\geq 3
, or
b_{1}=b_{2}=\cdots=b_{\ell}=2
and
b_{\ell+1}=b_{\ell+2}=\cdots=b_{t}=1
for some
\ell\in[t]\backslash[2]
. If
G
is a size Ramsey minimal graph for
(a_{1}K_{1,b_{1}},a_{2}K_{1,b_{2}},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,b_{t}})
, then
G=(a-t+1)K_{1,b}
, where
a=\sum_{s=1}^{t}a_{s}
and
b=\sum_{s=1}^{t}b_{s}-t+1
.

Proof:

Note that
b\geq 3
by our assumption. We use induction in
a
. The assertion holds for
a=t
by Lemma
3.3
. Suppose that the assertion holds for
a-1
and
a\geq t+1
. Thus, there is an
i\in[t]
such that
a_{i}\geq 2
. Moreover,
e(G)=(a-t+1)b
by Theorem
1.5
, and
b-1\leq\Delta(G)\leq b
by Lemma
3.1
. Let
v_{1}\in V(G)
be a vertex such that
d_{G}(v_{1})=\Delta(G)
.

Case 1
.
d_{G}(v_{1})=b
.

Note that
e(G-\{v_{1}\})=e(G)-d(v_{1})=(a-t+1)b-b=(a-t)b
. Moreover,
G\rightarrow(a_{1}K_{1,b_{1}},\dots,a_{i-1}K_{1,b_{i-1}},(a_{i}-1)K_{1,b_{i}},a_{i+1}K_{1,b_{i+1}},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,b_{t}})
by Lemma

c=a-t+1
. Furthermore,
G=(a-t+1)K_{3}
, and we are done.

Suppose that
\Delta(G)=3
. Let
v\in V(G)
be a vertex such that
d(v)=\Delta(G)=3
. Note that
e(G-\{v\})=e(G)-d(v)=3(a-t+1)-3=3(a-t)
. Moreover,

G-\{v\}\rightarrow\left((a_{1}-1)K_{1,2},a_{2}K_{1,2},a_{3}K_{1,1},a_{4}K_{1,1},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,1}\right),

or

G-\{v\}\rightarrow\left(a_{1}K_{1,2},a_{2}K_{1,2},(a_{3}-1)K_{1,1},a_{4}K_{1,1},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,1}\right)

by Lemma
2.2
since
a_{1}\geq 2
or
a_{3}\geq 2
. Thus,
G-\{v\}
is a size Ramsey minimal graph by Theorem
1.5
. Moreover, by the induction hypothesis,
G-\{v\}=cK_{3}\bigsqcup(a-t-c)K_{1,3}
or
G-\{v\}=cK_{3}\bigsqcup c^{\prime}K_{4}\bigsqcup(a-t-c-2c^{\prime})K_{1,3}
if
a_{1}=1
. Delete all isolated vertices in
G-\{v\}
and denote the resulting graph by
H
. If
N(v)\cap V(H)=\emptyset
, then
G=cK_{3}\bigsqcup(a-t+1-c)K_{1,3}
or
G=cK_{3}\bigsqcup c^{\prime}K_{4}\bigsqcup(a-t+1-c-2c^{\prime})K_{1,3}
if
a_{1}=1
, and we are done. Suppose that
N(v)\cap V(H)\neq\emptyset
. In the following, we divide the discussion into two parts.

Case 1
. There is a copy of
K_{1,3}
that contains at least one vertex of
N(v)
.

Denote the copy of
K_{1,3}
by
A
and let
w
be one of the common vertices. Note that
w
is not the center of
A
since
\Delta(G)=3
.

Subcase 1.1
.
a_{1}\geq 2
.

Note that
G-\{v\}=cK_{3}\bigsqcup(a-t-c)K_{1,3}
. Color edges incident to
w
by color
1
. Note that the uncolored edges of
G[N[v]\cup A]
is a union of two edge-disjoint matchings and denote them by
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
. Note that
G-(N[v]\cup A)=cK_{3}\bigsqcup(a-t-c-1)K_{1,3}
. Color
M_{1}
and a maximum matching of
G-(N[v]\cup A)
by color
1
. Moreover, color
M_{2}
and another maximum matching of
G-(N[v]\cup A)
by color
2
. Let
G^{\prime}_{2}
be the graph induced by colored edges. Note that
G^{\prime}_{2}\not\rightarrow(2K_{1,2},K_{1,2},K_{1,1},\dots,K_{1,1})
. Moreover, there are
c+(a-t-c-1)=a-t-1
uncolored edges. Thus,
G\not\rightarrow\left(a_{1}K_{1,2},a_{2}K_{1,2},a_{3}K_{1,1},a_{4}K_{1,1},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,1}\right)
by Lemma
3.2
. This is a contradiction.

Subcase 1.2
.
a_{1}=1
.

Note that
a_{3}\geq 2
and
G-\{v\}=cK_{3}\bigsqcup c^{\prime}K_{4}\bigsqcup(a-t-c-2c^{\prime})K_{1,3}
. Color all edges incident to
w
by color
3
. Note that the uncolored edges of
G[N[v]\cup A]
is a union of two edge-disjoint matchings and denote them by
M_{3}
and
M_{4}
. Note that
G-(N[v]\cup A)=cK_{3}\bigsqcup c^{\prime}K_{4}\bigsqcup(a-t-c-2c^{\prime}-1)K_{1,3}
. Color
M_{3}
and a maximum matching of
G-(N[v]\cup A)
by color
1
. Moreover, color
M_{4}
and another maximum matching of
G-(N[v]\cup A)
by color
2
. Let
G^{\prime}_{3}
be the graph induced by colored edges. Note that
G^{\prime}_{3}\not\rightarrow(K_{1,2},K_{1,2},2K_{1,1},K_{1,1}\dots,K_{1,1})
. Moreover, there are
c+2c^{\prime}+(a-t-c-2c^{\prime}-1)=a-t-1
uncolored edges. Thus,
G\not\rightarrow\left(a_{1}K_{1,2},a_{2}K_{1,2},a_{3}K_{1,1},a_{4}K_{1,1},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,1}\right)
by Lemma
3.2
. This is a contradiction.

Case 2
. There is a copy of
K_{3}
that contains at least one vertex of
N(v)
.

Denote the copy of
K_{3}
by
B
. Let
U=N(v)\cap V(B)
and let
u\in U
.

Subcase 2.1
.
a_{1}\geq 2
.

Note that
G-\{v\}=cK_{3}\bigsqcup(a-t-c)K_{1,3}
. If
|U|\geq 2
, then
v(G[N[v]\cup B])\leq 5
and color edges of
G[N[v]\cup B]
by color 1. If
|U|=1
, then color edges incident to
u
by color
1
. Note that the uncolored edges of
G[N[v]\cup B]
contain a copy of
2K_{1,1}
. Color it by color
2
. Moreover, color the remaining uncolored edge of
G[N[v]\cup B]
by color
1
. Note that
G-(N[v]\cup A)=(c-1)K_{3}\bigsqcup(a-t-c)K_{1,3}
. Color a maximum matching of
G-(N[v]\cup A)
by color
1
. Moreover, color another maximum matching of
G-(N[v]\cup A)
by color
2
. Let
G^{\prime}_{4}
be the graph induced by colored edges. Note that
G^{\prime}_{4}\not\rightarrow(2K_{1,2},K_{1,2},K_{1,1},\dots,K_{1,1})
. Moreover, there are
(c-1)+a-t-c=a-t-1
uncolored edges. Thus,
G\not\rightarrow\left(a_{1}K_{1,2},a_{2}K_{1,2},a_{3}K_{1,1},a_{4}K_{1,1},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,1}\right)
by Lemma
3.2
. This is a contradiction.

Subcase 2.2
.
a_{1}=1
.

Note that
a_{3}\geq 2
and
G-\{v\}=cK_{3}\bigsqcup c^{\prime}K_{4}\bigsqcup(a-t-c-2c^{\prime})K_{1,3}
. If
|U|=3
, then
G[N[u]\cup A]=K_{4}
. Thus,
G=(c-1)K_{3}\bigsqcup(c^{\prime}+1)K_{4}\bigsqcup(a-t-c-2c^{\prime})K_{1,3}
, and we are done. Suppose that
|U|\leq 2
. Color all edges incident to
u
by color
3
. Note that the uncolored edges of
G[N[u]\cup A]
is a union of two edge-disjoint matchings and denote them by
M_{5}
and
M_{6}
. Note that
G-(N[v]\cup A)=(c-1)K_{3}\bigsqcup c^{\prime}K_{4}\bigsqcup(a-t-c-2c^{\prime})K_{1,3}
. Color
M_{5}
and a maximum matching of
G-(N[v]\cup A)
by color
1
. Moreover, color
M_{6}
and another maximum matching of
G-(N[v]\cup A)
by color
2
. Let
G^{\prime}_{5}
be the graph induced by colored edges. Note that
G^{\prime}_{5}\not\rightarrow(K_{1,2},K_{1,2},2K_{1,1},K_{1,1},\dots,K_{1,1})
. Moreover, there are
(c-1)+2c^{\prime}+a-t-c-2c^{\prime}=a-t-1
uncolored edges. Thus,
G\not\rightarrow\left(a_{1}K_{1,2},a_{2}K_{1,2},a_{3}K_{1,1},a_{4}K_{1,1},\dots,a_{t}K_{1,1}\right)
by Lemma
3.2
. This is a contradiction.
\Box
