Spectral correspondence for cyclic Higgs bundles
JIA CHOON LEE and Ana Peón-Nieto

Abstract
. In this paper, we describe the spectral correspondence for cyclic Higgs bundles from the viewpoint of quiver bundles. Under this framework, we establish a one-to-one correspondence between cyclic Higgs bundles on a curve and sheaves on a noncommutative surface whose noncommutative structure originates from the path algebra associated to the cyclic quiver. As applications, this correspondence generalizes the known spectral correspondence for U ⁡ ( p , p ) U(p,p) -Higgs bundles and establish a connection between the spectral data for U ⁡ ( p , q ) U(p,q) -Higgs bundles and modules over the sheaf of even Clifford algebras of a conic fibration.



1.
Introduction

A cyclic Higgs bundle on a smooth projective curve
C
is a
GL(r,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle consisting of a vector bundle
E=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}E_{i}
and a Higgs field
\Phi:E\to E\otimes K_{C}
of the form

(1)

\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&\phi_{m-1}\\
\phi_{0}&0&&&0\\
0&\phi_{1}&&&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&&\phi_{m-2}&0\end{pmatrix}

where
\phi_{i}:E_{i}\to E_{i+1}\otimes K_{C}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
.

This notion arose originally in the work of Simpson
[
30
]
(called cyclotomic Higgs bundles) and was defined as the fixed points for the action of
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
on the Higgs field. From the perspective of moduli spaces, the moduli space of cyclic Higgs bundles can be viewed as the fixed point locus of an
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
-action on the moduli space of
GL(r,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle. Cyclic Higgs bundles have also been investigated in other contexts. When
m=2
, they are Higgs bundles for real Lie group
U(p,q)
and the topology of their moduli spaces have been studied extensively using Morse theory by Bradlow, García-Prada and Gothen
[
4
]
. Analytically, the work of Baraglia
[
2
]
establishes a correspondence between cyclic Higgs bundles and solutions of the affine Toda equation. This is later generalized to non-compact surfaces by Li and Mochizuki
[
22
]
. More recently, the concept of cyclic Higgs bundles has been generalized to arbitrary reductive groups through the Lie-theorectic perspective of Vinberg pairs by García-Prada and Gonzáles
[
11
,
8
]
(see also
[
10
]
).

In this paper, we are interested in studying the spectral correspondence for cyclic Higgs bundles. The classical spectral correspondence for
GL(r,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundles is originally established by Hitchin
[
13
]
, and subsequently generalized by Beauville–Narasimhan–Ramanan (BNR)
[
3
]
and Simpson
[
29
]
. Roughly speaking, the original viewpoint of Hitchin converts a Higgs bundle into a line bundle on its corresponding spectral curve (when smooth) by taking a fiberwise eigenspace decomposition. On the other hand, the viewpoint of BNR treats Higgs bundles as modules over the symmetric algebra
S^{\bullet}K_{C}^{-1}
which then lift to torsion-free sheaves on the spectral curve (when integral). Simpson further interprets these modules as pure dimension one coherent sheaves on the surface
X=\underline{\textrm{Spec}}(S^{\bullet}K_{C}^{-1})
. The two approaches of Hitchin and BNR/Simpson parallel an elementary equivalence in linear algebra: a vector space equipped with an endomorphism is equivalent to a module over the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[t]
. This can be naturally generalized to an equivalence in the theory of quiver that we will use: a representation of a quiver is equivalent to a module over its path algebra.

A cyclic Higgs bundle can be seen as a quiver bundle for the cyclic quiver
Q(m)
, or equivalently, a representation of
Q(m)
in the category of vector bundles.

We will adopt the module-theoretic viewpoint which is more flexible when the spectral curves become singular. This means that we first treat a cyclic Higgs bundle as a module over the sheaf of the associated path algebra
A(m)
(generalizing the symmetric algebra
S^{\bullet}K_{C}^{-1}
) on
C
. This path algebra
A(m)
is always noncommutative and of infinite rank. By performing a central reduction of
A(m)
, we obtain a sheaf of noncommutative algebras
\widehat{A}(m)
of finite rank on the surface
Y=\underline{\textrm{Spec}}(S^{\bullet}K_{C}^{-m})
. Then we can lift the
A(m)
-modules on
C
to
\widehat{A}(m)
-modules on
Y
. By rewriting the
\widehat{A}(m)
-modules in terms of quiver sheaves, we arrive at the following version of spectral correspondence for cyclic Higgs bundles.

Theorem 1.1

(Corollary
3.11
)
.

Fix a dimension vector
\bm{p}=(p_{0},\dots,p_{m-1})
and a degree vector
\bm{d}=(d_{0},\dots,d_{m-1})
.
Let
(\widetilde{C}_{0},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{m-1})\in B(\bm{p})
be a fixed collection of spectral curves in
Y
. The following two categories are equivalent:

(1)

(Higgs data) The category of cyclic Higgs bundles
(\{E_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}},\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}})
on
C
with dimension vector
\bm{p}
and degree vector
\bm{d}
, such that the spectral curve of
\Phi_{i}:=\phi_{i-1}\circ\dots\circ\phi_{i+1}\circ\phi_{i}
is
\widetilde{C}_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

(Spectral data) The category of
M_{Y}
-twisted
Q(m)
-quiver sheaves
(\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}},\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}})
on
Y
satisfying:

(a)

For each
i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
,
F_{i}
is a pure dimension one sheaf on
Y
that is scheme-theoretically supported on
\widetilde{C}_{i}
(i.e. an
\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C}_{i}}
-module) with Euler characteristic
\chi(F_{i})=d_{i}+p_{i}(1-g)
.

(b)

The relation
\psi_{i-1}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_{i}=\operatorname{Id}_{F_{i}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\lambda
holds in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}(F_{i}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-m},F_{i})
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 1.2
.

As we will establish in Proposition
3.9
, the spectral data above is equivalent to an
\widehat{A}(m)
-modules. Therefore, Theorem
1.1
can be stated as a one-to-one correspondence between cyclic Higgs bundles on
C
and coherent sheaves on the noncommutative surface
(Y,\widehat{A}(m))
.

This equivalence can be seen as a natural generalization of the works of BNR/Simpson in the case of
GL(r,\mathbb{C})
, Schaposnik
[
26
]
in the case of
U(p,p)
(which parallels the approach of Hitchin) and Peón-Nieto
[
25
]
in the case of
SU(p+1,p)
. There is also a general approach to abelianization of Higgs bundles for arbitrary quasi-split real groups via cameral cover that holds for regular locus by the work of Peón-Nieto and García-Prada
[
9
]
. In Section
4
, we study some cases where a further reduction of the spectral data is posssible.

A further advantage of working on the noncommutative surface
(Y,\widehat{A}(m))
is that it reveals a surprising relation to another noncommutative surface studied in the context of the derived category of a conic fibration. More precisely, we show that the sheaf of algebras
\widehat{A}(m)
is isomorphic to the sheaf of even Clifford algebras associated to a conic fibration over
Y
(Proposition
4.1
). The noncommutative algebraic varieties with the sheaf of even Clifford algebras
\mathcal{C}\ell_{0}
as structure sheaf appear originally in the work of Kuznetsov on homological projective duality
[
17
]
. For example, for a vector space
W
, one of the main results in loc. cit. is that the homological projective dual of
\mathbb{P}(W)
in the double Veronese embedding
\mathbb{P}(W)\to\mathbb{P}(S^{2}W)
is the noncommutative algebraic variety
(\mathbb{P}(S^{2}W),\mathcal{C}\ell_{0})
.

The idea of encoding spectral data in terms of sheaves on surfaces with extra structure has also been explored for other variants of Higgs bundles.
Notably, the use of Koszul duality and noncommutative geometry on some surfaces to establish spectral correspondence was developed by Katzarkov, Orlov and Pantev for twisted and curved Higgs bundles
[
14
]
. On the other hand, in the context of parabolic Higgs bundles, one can establish a spectral correspondence in terms of blow-up of ruled surface (see
[
20
]
). Further blow-up patterns are required if one is interested in parabolic Higgs bundles with irregular singularities
[
Kontsevich-Soibelman
,
32
,
7
]
. Alternatively, one can encode the parabolic structure in terms of root stacks and orbifold surfaces
[
5
]
.

Finally, we conclude the paper in Section
5
by exploring several future directions and open questions stemming from this spectral correspondence.

1.1.
Conventions

Throughout this paper, we work over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
. Moreover, all modules over an algebra are assumed to be right modules unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Quiver sheaves and modules over the sheaf of path algebras

2.1.
Quiver Sheaves

In this section, we recall the framework of quiver sheaves and modules over the associated sheaf of path algebras, following
[
1
]
. While we focus entirely in the case of curves, this framework is naturally defined over any algebraic variety.

Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a fixed quiver where
Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
are the sets of vertices and arrows together with the head and tail maps:

h,t:Q_{1}\to Q_{0}.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve and
M=\{M_{a}\}_{a\in Q_{1}}
be a collection of line bundles on
C
. An
M
-twisted
Q
-quiver sheaf is a pair
(\mathcal{E},\Phi)
consisting of a collection of coherent
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-modules
\mathcal{E}=\{E_{i}\}_{i\in Q_{0}}
and a collection of morphisms
\Phi=\{\phi_{a}\}_{a\in Q_{1}}
where

\phi_{a}:E_{t(a)}\otimes M_{a}\to E_{h(a)}.

When
E_{i}
are all locally free, we will call it an
M
-twisted
Q
-quiver bundle and denote by
\bm{p}=(\textrm{dim}(E_{i}))_{i\in Q_{1}}
the dimension vector of the quiver bundle.

2.2.
Path algebras

Recall that for each quiver
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
, a path (of length
m
) is a sequence
a_{1}\cdots a_{k}
of arrows
a_{i}\in Q_{1}
such that
t(a_{i})=h(a_{i+1})
for
i=1,\dots,k-1
. For each vertex
i\in Q_{0}
, we define the trivial path
e_{i}
(of length 0).

Let
S=\bigoplus_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathcal{O}_{C}\cdot e_{i}
be a commutative
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-algebra generated by the formal symbols
e_{i}
with relations

\begin{cases}e_{i}\cdot e_{i^{\prime}}=e_{i},\quad\textrm{if }i=i^{\prime}\\
e_{i}\cdot e_{i^{\prime}}=0,\quad\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

for each
i,i^{\prime}\in Q_{0}
.
Moreover, let
\widetilde{M}=\bigoplus_{a\in Q_{1}}M_{a}
and define a
S
-bimodule structure on it as follows: the left module structure is given by

\begin{cases}e_{i}\cdot m=m,\quad\textrm{if }m\in M_{a}\textrm{ and }i=h(a)\\
e_{i}\cdot m=0,\quad\textrm{otherwise}\end{cases}

for all
i\in Q_{0},a\in Q_{1},m\in M_{a}
, and likewise for the right module structure. Following
[
1
]
, we define the sheaf of
M
-twisted path algebra
A
of
Q
as the tensor
S
-algebra of the
S
-bimodule
\widetilde{M}
:

A=\bigoplus_{\ell\geq 0}\widetilde{M}^{\otimes_{S}\ell}

where we view
\widetilde{M}^{\otimes 0}:=S
.

Equivalently, one can directly define an
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-algebra structure on

MQ:=\bigoplus_{\textrm{all paths }p}M_{p}

where
M_{p}=M_{a_{1}}\otimes...\otimes M_{a_{k}}
and the relation is given by

\begin{cases}m_{p}\cdot m_{p^{\prime}}=m_{p}\otimes m_{p^{\prime}},\quad\textrm{if }t(p)=h(p^{\prime})\\
m_{p}\cdot m_{p^{\prime}}=0,\quad\textrm{otherwise }\end{cases}

where
m_{p}\in M_{p},m_{p^{\prime}}\in M_{p^{\prime}}
for all paths
p,p^{\prime}
. Then it is straightforward to see that the
A\cong MQ
as
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-algebra e.g. the algebra
A
has relations such as
m_{a}\otimes m_{a^{\prime}}=m_{a}\otimes e_{h(a^{\prime})}m_{a^{\prime}}=m_{a}\cdot e_{h(a^{\prime})}\otimes m_{a}
which vanishes when
t(a)\neq h(a^{\prime})
. The unit element in
A
admits a decomposition into orthogonal idempotents

(2)

1_{A}=\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}e_{i}.

Just as the category of quiver representations is equivalent to the category of modules over the associated path algebra, there is an analogue for quiver sheaves. Fix a quiver
Q
, a coherent right module over the associated sheaf of path algebra
A
(or simply,
A
-modules) is an
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-module
E
equipped with a right
A
-module structure given by an
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-module morphism
E\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}A\to E
satisfying the axioms of right modules over an algebra.

Proposition 2.4
.

There is an equivalence between the category of
M
-twisted
Q
-quiver bundles on
C
and the category of coherent right
A
-modules
\operatorname{mod}_{C}\!\text{-}A
.

Proof.

Given a
M
-twisted
Q
-quiver bundles
(\{E_{i}\}_{i\in Q_{0}},\{\phi_{a}\}_{a\in Q_{1}})
, we associate to it
E=\bigoplus_{i\in Q_{0}}E_{i}
with a right module structure
E\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}A\to E
induced by the maps
\phi_{a}
which are generators of the path algebra
A
. This construction defines a functor in the forward direction. Conversely, given a right
A
-module
\mathcal{E}
equipped with an
A
-module structure
\mu_{A}:\mathcal{E}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}A\to\mathcal{E}
, the decomposition of the unit
1_{A}
induces a decomposition of
\mathcal{E}=\oplus_{i\in Q_{0}}E_{i}
. Restricting
\mu_{A}
to
\mathcal{E}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}\widetilde{M}
induces the arrow morphisms
\phi_{a}:M_{a}\otimes E_{t(a)}\to E_{h(a)}
for
a\in Q_{1}
. For details, we refer the readers to
[
1
, Proposition 5.2]
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Clearly, a cyclic Higgs bundle in the definition is equivalent to a
GL(\sum p_{i},\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundles whose Higgs field is of the form
1
stated in the introduction.

Let
A(m)
be the sheaf of
M
-twisted path algebras on
C
associated to the quiver
Q(m)
. As
A(m)
is noncommutative, we begin by studying its central subalgebra
Z(A(m))
which is commutative. Note that we have the natural direct sum decomposition

(3)

A(m)=\bigoplus_{i,j\in Q_{0}}A_{i\leftarrow j}

where
A_{i\leftarrow j}:=e_{i}A(m)e_{j}
corresponds to paths from
v_{j}
to
v_{i}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Z(A(m))\subset\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}A_{i\leftarrow i}\subset A(m)
.

Proof.

Indeed, if
z\in Z(A(m))
and we write
z=\sum_{i,j\in Q_{0}}z_{i\leftarrow j}
according to the decomposition
3
, then the commutativity
e_{k}\cdot z=z\cdot e_{k}
implies that

\sum_{j\in Q_{0}}z_{k\leftarrow j}=\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}z_{i\leftarrow k}

It follows that only
z_{k\leftarrow j}=0
for
k\neq j
.
∎

For each vertex
i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
, we define the path (loop) of length
m

c_{i}=a_{i-1}\cdot a_{i-2}\dots a_{i+1}\cdot a_{i}

where all subscripts are understood modulo
m
. Clearly, we have

A_{i\leftarrow i}=\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{\infty}M_{c_{i}^{\ell}}=\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{\infty}M_{c_{i}}^{\otimes\ell}

as all paths starting and ending at the vertex
v_{i}
must be a composition of the loop
c_{i}
. Since
M_{c_{i}}=K_{C}^{-m}
, it induces a canonical isomorphism
\iota_{i}:S^{\bullet}(K_{C}^{-m})\xrightarrow{\sim}A_{i\leftarrow i}
. Let

(4)

\displaystyle\Delta:S^{\bullet}(K_{C}^{-m})

\displaystyle\to\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}A_{i\leftarrow i}\subset A(m),

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\iota_{i}(s)

be the diagonal map and denote its image by
A_{\Delta}\subset A(m)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

The central subalgebra
Z(A(m))
is equal to
A_{\Delta}
. Consequently,

Z(A(m))\cong S^{\bullet}(K_{C}^{-m})

as
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-algebras.

Proof.

Let
z=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}z_{i}\in Z(A(m))
where
z_{i}\in A_{i\leftarrow i}
by Lemma
3.3
. It remains to impose that
z
commutes with all local sections
\alpha_{i}\in M_{a_{i}}
. By matching the idempotents (heads and tails), the condition
z\cdot\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i}\cdot z
implies that

(5)

z_{i+1}\cdot\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i}\cdot z_{i}

which holds in
A_{i+1\leftarrow i}
. If
z_{i}\in M_{c_{i}^{\ell}}
, then the equality forces
z_{i+1}\in M_{c_{i+1}^{\ell}}.
As paths, we have
c_{i+1}^{\ell}\cdot a_{i}=a_{i}\cdot c_{i}^{\ell}
which induces a canonical isomorphism
M_{c_{i+1}}^{\otimes\ell}\otimes M_{a_{i}}\cong M_{a_{i}}\otimes M_{c_{i}}^{\otimes\ell}
. Since
M_{a_{i}}
is a line bundle (locally isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{C}
), the equality
5
holds for all local sections
\alpha_{j}
implies that the local sections
z_{i+1}
and
z_{i}
are identified under the isomorphism
\iota_{i}\circ\iota_{i+1}^{-1}
. Since this holds for all
i
, all components
z_{i}
are the image of a single section
s\in S^{\bullet}(K_{C}^{-m})
, so
Z(A(m))\subset A_{\Delta}
.

Conversely, it is straightforward to verify that any diagonal element
s\in A_{\Delta}
commutes with the idempotents
e_{i}
and the arrow generators of
A(m)
to establish the reverse direction.
∎

By Proposition
3.4
, we know
A(m)
is a
S^{\bullet}(K_{C}^{-m})
-module. It is then natural to consider the total space of
K_{C}^{m}
:

\pi:Y:=\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}(S^{\bullet}(K_{C}^{-m}))\to C

Let

A_{Y}(m)=\pi^{*}A(m)

be the usual pullback of a coherent module which is also the sheaf of
M_{Y}
-twisted path algebras on
Y
of the quiver
Q
with twisting line bundles
M_{Y}:=\{M_{Y,a_{i}}=\pi^{*}(K_{C}^{-1})\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}
. Consider the two morphisms of sheaves:

•

The composition
K_{C}^{-m}\hookrightarrow S^{\bullet}K_{C}^{-m}\xrightarrow{\Delta}A(m)
is a morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-modules. We denote its pullback by
\nu_{1}:=\pi^{*}(\Delta|_{K_{C}^{-m}})
. Locally, for a section
s\in K_{C}^{-m}
, this acts as:

\nu_{1}(\pi^{*}s)=\pi^{*}\left(\Delta(s)\right)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\pi^{*}(\iota_{i}(s))

•

Let
\lambda:\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-m}\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}
be the morphism induced by the tautological section which lies in
H^{0}(Y,\pi^{*}K_{C}^{m})
. We define

\nu_{2}:\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-m}\xrightarrow{\lambda}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to A_{Y}(m),\quad z\mapsto\lambda(z)\cdot 1_{A_{Y}(m)}

Then we define the two-sided ideal
I
in
A_{Y}
generated by the difference of
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
:

\displaystyle I:=\left\langle Im(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\pi^{*}(\Delta(s))-\lambda(\pi^{*}(s))\cdot 1_{A_{Y}(m)}|s\in K_{C}^{-m}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\pi^{*}(\iota_{i}(s))-\lambda(\pi^{*}(s))\cdot e_{Y,i}|i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},s\in K_{C}^{-m}\right\rangle

where the last equality is obtained by the decomposition of the unit
1_{A_{Y}(m)}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}e_{Y,i}
.
We define the sheaf of algebras on
Y
:

\widehat{A}(m):=A_{Y}(m)/I.

Hence, the idea of the ideal
I
is to identify the actions corresponding to loops and scalar multiplication by the tautological section
\lambda
.

Proposition 3.5
.

(1)

As an
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-module,
\widehat{A}(m)
is locally free of finite rank of
m^{2}
.

(2)

Let
Y_{0}\subset Y
denote the zero section of
K_{C}^{m}
over
C
. For any point
y\in Y\setminus Y_{0}
, the fiber
\widehat{A}(m)|_{y}
is isomorphic to the matrix algebra
\operatorname{Mat}_{m\times m}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

(1) The
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-algebra
A_{Y}(m)
is generated by sections corresponding to all paths. Any path
p
starting from
v_{j}
and ending at
v_{i}
can be factored as
p=c_{i}^{\ell}\cdot p_{ij}
, where
p_{ij}
is the unique path of length
<m
from
v_{j}
to
v_{i}
. Recall that taking the quotient of
A_{Y}(m)
by
I
means that we are identifying the action of the loop
c_{i}
with multiplication by the tautological section
\lambda
. Therefore, in the quotient
\widehat{A}(m)=A_{Y}(m)/I
, the loop
c_{i}^{\ell}
just acts as scalar multiplication. It follows that
\widehat{A}(m)
is generated as an
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-module by the sections corresponding to paths of length
<m
. Since the number of paths
p
of length
<m
is
m^{2}
and all the component
M_{p}
in
A_{Y}(m)
is locally free of rank 1, the quotient
A_{Y}(m)/I
is locally free of rank
m^{2}
.

(2) Let
y\in Y\setminus Y_{0}
. By the Wedderburn-Artin theorem, it suffices to check that
\widehat{A}(m)|_{y}
is a simple
\mathbb{C}
-algebra. Let
J
be a non-zero two-sided ideal of
\widehat{A}(m)|_{y}
. For simplicity, we will denote by
e_{i}
the restriction of the section
e_{i}
of
\widehat{A}(m)|_{y}
as well. Take any non-zero element
x\in J
. Write
x=\sum_{i,j\in Q_{0}}x_{i\leftarrow j}
according to the direct sum decomposition
3
(which holds for the quotient
\widehat{A}(m)
). Since
x\neq 0
, there must exist a nonzero component
x_{i\leftarrow j}=e_{i}xe_{j}
which also lies in
J
because
J
is a two-sided ideal.

Take a nonzero element
u_{j\leftarrow i}\in\widehat{A}(m)|_{y}
corresponding to the path from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
(which exists because the component of
\widehat{A}(m)|_{y}
for any path of length
<m
is a 1-dimensional vector space). The product
u_{j\leftarrow i}\cdot x_{i\leftarrow j}
lies in
e_{j}\widehat{A}(m)|_{y}e_{j}\subset J
which correpsonds to the loop starting and ending at
v_{j}
. By the definition of the ideal
I
of relations, the action corresponding to a loop evaluates to the tautological section
\lambda(y)
which is non-zero because
y\not\in Y_{0}
. Thus,
u_{j\leftarrow i}\cdot x_{i\leftarrow j}=b_{j}e_{j}
for some non-zero scalar
b_{j}
and it follows that
e_{j}\in J
.

Once we get
e_{j}\in J
, we can show
e_{k}\in J
for all vertices
v_{k}
. By choosing a nonzero element
u_{k\leftarrow j}
and
u_{j\leftarrow k}
corresponding to the path from
v_{j}
to
v_{k}
and
v_{k}
to
v_{j}
respectively. The product
u_{k\leftarrow j}\cdot e_{j}\cdot u_{j\leftarrow k}\in J
equals to
b_{k}e_{k}
for some non-zero
b_{k}
, which implies that
e_{k}\in J
. Hence, we obtain all the idempotents
e_{k}
and the identity element
1=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}e_{k}
. It follows that
J=\widehat{A}(m)|_{y}
.

∎

Remark 3.6
.

We can view the pair
(Y,\widehat{A}(m))
as a (midly) noncommutative surface in the sense that the category
\operatorname{Mod}_{Y}\!\text{-}\widehat{A}(m)
of quasicoherent right
\widehat{A}(m)
-modules on
Y
is interpreted as the category
\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\widehat{A}(m))
of coherent sheaves on
(Y,\widehat{A}(m))
. Note that while the original sheaf of path algebras
A(m)
is not a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-module (being of infinite rank), its central reduction
\widehat{A}(m)
on
Y
has finite rank by Proposition
3.5
. Consequently, the category
\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\widehat{A}(m))
and its derived category are more well-behaved. We refer the readers to
[
17
, Section 2]
[
16
, Appendix D]
for a detailed account of noncommutative algebraic geometry from this point of view.

Proposition 3.7
.

We have an isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-algebras:

\pi_{*}\widehat{A}(m)\cong A(m)

Proof.

Since
\pi
is affine, we have
\pi_{*}\widehat{A}(m)\cong\pi_{*}A_{Y}(m)/\pi_{*}I
. By the projection formula, we have a natural isomorphism:

\pi_{*}A_{Y}(m)\cong\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}A(m)

Recall that
Y=\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}(S^{\bullet}(K_{C}^{-m}))
. This implies that
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}=S^{\bullet}K_{C}^{-m}
which is isomorphic to the central subalgebra
Z(A(m))
by Proposition
3.4
.

Let
m_{C}:\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}A(m)\to A(m)
be the natural multiplication map. Since
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
maps into the center of
A(m)
, this multiplication is compatible with the algebra structure and
m_{C}
is a well-defined homomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-algebras. Furthermore,
m_{C}
is clearly surjective since
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
contains the identity element. It remains to show that the kernel of
m_{C}
is the ideal
\pi_{*}I
.

We can verify this locally over an affine open subset
U=\textrm{Spec}(R)\subset C
over which the line bundle
K_{C}^{-m}
is trivial. Let
A_{R}:=A(m)(U)
. Over
U
, the central subalgebra
Z(A(m))\cong S^{\bullet}K_{C}^{-m}
restricts to
Z_{R}\cong R[t]
where
t
corresponds to a trivializing section of
K_{C}^{-m}(U)
. The restriction of
\pi_{*}A_{Y}(m)
to
U
is simply the
R
-algebra
R[t]\otimes_{R}A_{R}
. Locally, the multiplication map
m_{C}
becomes

m_{R}:R[t]\otimes_{R}A_{R}\to A_{R}

defined by
1\otimes a\mapsto a
and
t\otimes 1\mapsto\Delta(t)
where
\Delta
is the diagonal map defined in
4
.

By definition, the ideal
\pi_{*}I
is generated by elements of the form
\pi^{*}(\Delta(s))-\lambda(\pi^{*}(s))\cdot 1_{A_{Y}(m)}
. On the local chart
U
, since any section
s|_{U}
is represented as
r\cdot t
for some
r\in R
. So, the ideal
\pi_{*}I(U)
is determined by the relation on
t
, which is the element
1\otimes\Delta(t)-t\otimes 1
. By standard properties of tensor products of algebras, the kernel of the
m_{R}
is generated exactly by the two-sided ideal
\langle 1\otimes\Delta(t)-t\otimes 1\rangle
. Therefore,
\textrm{ker}(m_{R})=\pi_{*}I(U)
. Since this holds for all affine open subset, we conclude that
\textrm{ker}(m_{C})=\pi_{*}I
.

∎

Corollary 3.8
.

There is an equivalence between
\operatorname{Mod}_{Y}\!\text{-}\widehat{A}(m)
and
\operatorname{Mod}_{C}\!\text{-}A(m)
.

Proof.

Since
\pi:Y\to C
is an affine morphism, the pushforward functor
\pi_{*}:\operatorname{Mod}_{Y}\!\text{-}\widehat{A}(m)\to\operatorname{Mod}_{C}\!\text{-}\pi_{*}\widehat{A}(m)
is an equivalence of categories, just as in the commutative analogue
[
31
,
Tag 01SB
]
. Then the result follows by Proposition
3.7
. ∎

Proposition 3.9
.

There is an equivalence between the category
\operatorname{mod}_{Y}\!\text{-}\widehat{A}(m)
of coherent right
\widehat{A}(m)
-modules on
Y
and the category of
M_{Y}
-twisted
Q(m)
-quiver sheaves
(\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}},\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}})
with the additional condition that for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
, the composition of arrows around the loop (suppressing the twist by the identity on
\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-1}
) yields:

\psi_{i-1}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_{i}=\operatorname{Id}_{F_{i}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\lambda\in Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}(F_{i}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-m},F_{i})

where the subcript of
\psi_{i}
is understood modulo
m
.

Proof.

The category
\operatorname{mod}_{Y}\!\text{-}\widehat{A}(m)
is a full subcategory of
\operatorname{mod}_{Y}\!\text{-}A_{Y}(m)
consisting of
A_{Y}(m)
-modules that annihilates the ideal
I
. By Proposition
2.4
, an object
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{mod}_{Y}\!\text{-}A_{Y}(m)
corresponds to a
M_{Y}
-twisted
Q(m)
-quiver sheaf
(\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}},\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}})
. It remains to determine what condition is imposed on this quiver sheaf by requiring it to annihilate
I
.

Recall that
I=Im(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})
.
The map
\nu_{1}:\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-m}\to A_{Y}(m)
corresponding to loops in the path algebra induces

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-m}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}A_{Y}(m)\to\mathcal{F}

By restricting this map to the component
F_{i}
, we get

\psi_{i-1}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_{i}:F_{i}\otimes\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-m}\to F_{i}

On the other hand, the map
\nu_{2}:\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-m}\to A_{Y}(m)
is defined by multiplication by the tautological section
\lambda\in H^{0}(Y,\pi^{*}K_{C}^{m})
.
Its induced action on each component
F_{i}
maps a section
f\otimes z\in F_{i}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-m}
to
f\cdot\lambda(z)\in F_{i}
, that is,

\operatorname{Id}_{F_{i}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\lambda:F_{i}\otimes\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-m}\to F_{i}.

Therefore, in order for
\mathcal{F}
to annihilates the ideal
I
, each component
F_{i}
must satisfy

\psi_{i-1}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_{i}=\operatorname{Id}_{F_{i}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\lambda,\quad\textrm{for }i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}

as desired.
∎

.

.

(2)

.

(3)

.

The category of

(b)

The equivalence (1)
\Leftrightarrow

Let
(\mathcal{E},\Phi)=(\{E_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}},\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}})
be a cyclic Higgs bundle. For each
i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
, the composite map for the loop at
v_{i}
,

\Phi_{i}:=\phi_{i-1}\circ\dots\circ\phi_{i}:E_{i}\otimes K_{C}^{-m}\to E_{i}

is an
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-linear map which defines a spectral curve
\widetilde{C}_{i}=\{\det(\lambda Id-\pi^{*}\Phi_{i})=0\}\subset Y
where
\lambda\in H^{0}(Y,\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-m})
is the tautological section. The restriction
\pi|_{\widetilde{C}_{i}}:\widetilde{C}_{i}\to C
is a
p_{i}
-sheeted cover of
C
, where
p_{i}=\operatorname{rank}(E_{i})
. The space of spectral curves is parameterized by the coefficients of the characteristic polynomials which lie in the vector space

B(p_{i})=\bigoplus_{\mu=1}^{p_{i}}H^{0}(C,K_{C}^{m\mu})

By a slight abuse of notation, we will write
\widetilde{C}_{i}\in B(p_{i})
to denote the spectral curve
\widetilde{C}_{i}
corresponding to that point in the parameter space
B(p_{i})
. For a given dimension vector
\bm{p}=(p_{0},\dots,p_{m-1})
, let
B(\bm{p})=B(p_{0})\times\cdots\times B(p_{m-1})
.

Corollary 3.11
.

Fix a dimension vector
\bm{p}=(p_{0},\dots,p_{m-1})
and a degree vector
\bm{d}=(d_{0},\dots,d_{m-1})
.
Let
(\widetilde{C}_{0},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{m-1})\in B(\bm{p})
be a fixed collection of spectral curves in
Y
. The following two categories are equivalent:

(1)

(Higgs data) The category of cyclic Higgs bundles
(\{E_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}},\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}})
on
C
with dimension vector
\bm{p}
and degree vector
\bm{d}
, such that the spectral curve of
\Phi_{i}
is
\widetilde{C}_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

(Spectral data) The category of
M_{Y}
-twisted
Q(m)
-quiver sheaves
(\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}},\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}})
on
Y
satisfying:

(a)

For each
i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
,
F_{i}
is a pure dimension one sheaf on
Y
that is scheme-theoretically supported on
\widetilde{C}_{i}
(i.e. an
\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C}_{i}}
-module) with Euler characteristic
\chi(F_{i})=d_{i}+p_{i}(1-g)
.

(b)

The relation
\psi_{i-1}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_{i}=\operatorname{Id}_{F_{i}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\lambda
holds in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}(F_{i}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-m},F_{i})
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

The correspondence between the cyclic Higgs bundles
(\{E_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}},\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}})
and quiver sheaves
(\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}},\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}})
is the equivalence (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(4) in Theorem
3.10
. In particular, under this equivalence, we have
E_{i}=\pi_{*}F_{i}
and the Higgs field
\Phi_{i}
is recovered by pushing forward
\psi_{i-1}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_{i}=\operatorname{Id}_{F_{i}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\lambda
to
C
. For each
i
, this reduces precisely to the classical spectral correspondence for
GL(p_{i},\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundles (
[
3
,
29
]
). Thus, the condition that
E_{i}
is locally free of rank
p_{i}
and degree
d_{i}
with spectral curve
\widetilde{C}_{i}
translates directly to
F_{i}
being pure dimension one with support
\widetilde{C}_{i}
and Euler characteristic
d_{i}+p_{i}(1-g)
.

∎

Remark 3.12

(Constraints on spectral curves)
.

Suppose
(\widetilde{C}_{0},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{m-1})\in B(\bm{p})
is a fixed collection of spectral curves in
Y
that comes from a cyclic Higgs bundle. Then the equivalence in Corollary
3.11
imposes a constraint on the spectral curves. Indeed, the relations

(\psi_{i-1}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_{i+1})\circ\psi_{i}=\operatorname{Id}_{F_{i}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\lambda,\quad\psi_{i}\circ(\psi_{i-1}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_{i+1})=\operatorname{Id}_{F_{i+1}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\lambda

implies that
\psi_{i}:F_{i}\otimes\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-1}|_{Y\setminus Y_{0}}\to F_{i+1}|_{Y\setminus Y_{0}}
is an isomorphism since
\operatorname{Id}_{F_{i}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\lambda
is an isomorphism on
Y\setminus Y_{0}
. Moreover, taking the tensor product with the line bundle
\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-1}
does not change the support of
F_{i}
, so
F_{i}
and
F_{i+1}
share the same support away from
Y_{0}
. In particular, there exists a common curve
\widetilde{C}\subset Y
such that
(\widetilde{C}_{0},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{m-1})\in B(\bm{p})
is of the form

(6)

(\widetilde{C}+q_{0}Y_{0},\dots,\widetilde{C}+q_{m-1}Y_{0}),\quad\textrm{ where }q_{0},\dots,q_{m-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Z(A(1))\cong A(1)
and

Y

4.2.
U(p_{0},p_{1})
-Higgs bundles and even Clifford algebras

Consider
m=2
and
\bm{p}=(p_{0},p_{1})
. In this case, we will show that the noncommutative surface
(Y,\widehat{A}(2))
can be identified with another noncommutative surface derived from a conic fibration over
Y
with ramification locus
Y_{0}
.

Let us first recall the definition of conic fibration and its associated sheaf of even Clifford algebras. Let
S
be a smooth variety.
Fix a rank
3
vector bundle
F
on
S
and a quadratic form on
F
which is an embedding of a line bundle
q:L\to S^{2}F^{\vee}
. Let
f^{\prime}:\mathbb{P}(F):=\operatorname{Proj}(S^{\bullet}F^{\vee})\to S
be the projection map. Since the quadratic form
q
is equivalent to a section

s_{q}\in H^{0}(S,S^{2}F^{\vee}\otimes L^{-1})=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}(F),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(F)/S}(2)\otimes f^{\prime*}L^{-1}),

the zero locus
\mathcal{X}
of
s_{q}
defines a conic fibration
f=f^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathcal{X}\to S
whose fibers are plane conics. The discriminant locus of
f
is the locus where the rank of the quadratic form drops.

It is known by the work of Kuznetsov
[
17
]
that the bounded derived category
D^{b}(\mathcal{X})
of the conic fibration
\mathcal{X}
over
S
admits a semiorthogonal decomposition

D^{b}(\mathcal{X})=\langle D^{b}(S,\mathcal{C}\ell_{0}(q)),f^{*}D^{b}(S)\rangle

where the first component is the bounded derived category of right modules over the sheaf of even Clifford algebra
\mathcal{C}\ell_{0}(q)
. Just as a quadratic form on a vector space defines the Clifford algebra which decomposes into the even and odd parts, the sheaf of algebras
\mathcal{C}\ell_{0}(q)
is obtained as a relative version of this construction: it is the sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebras whose fiber at any point
x\in S
is the even part of the Clifford algebra defined by the quadratic form
q_{x}
on the fiber
F_{x}
(see the original paper of Kuznetsov
[
17
]
for more details).

The following proposition identifies the sheaf of algebras
\widehat{A}(2)
as the sheaf of even Clifford algebras of a conic fibration over
Y
.

Proposition 4.1
.

There exists a rank 3 vector bundle
F
over
Y
and a quadratic form
q
on
F
with discriminant locus
Y_{0}
such that the associated sheaf of even Clifford algebras
\mathcal{C}\ell_{0}(q)
is isomorphic to
\widehat{A}(2)
.

Proof.

It is possible to explicitly write down the quadratic form and construct an isomorphism between the two sheaves of algebras. Instead, we will apply a useful criterion of Kuznetsov which essentially reduces to checking that
\widehat{A}(2)
is an even Clifford algebra over each point
y\in Y
. The idea is to show that
\widehat{A}(2)
is a pointwise Clifford algebra in the sense of Kuznetsov which implies the existence of the desired quadratic form by
[
18
, Proposition 2.7]
. We will focus on checking the required condition of pointwise Clifford algebra and refer the readers to
[
18
, Definition 2.5]
for a precise definition.

By the analysis in Proposition
3.5
,
\widehat{A}(2)
admits a direct sum decomposition according to the length of paths. More specifically, we have
\widehat{A}(2)\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}e_{0}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Y}e_{1}\oplus M_{0\leftarrow 1}\oplus M_{1\leftarrow 0}
as
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-modules where
M_{1\leftarrow 0}
and
M_{0\leftarrow 1}
are both isomorphic to
\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-1}
. We can define an
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-linear trace map via:

\operatorname{Tr}:\widehat{A}(2)\to\mathcal{O}_{Y},\quad\alpha=\alpha_{0}e_{0}+\alpha_{1}e_{1}+\alpha_{0\leftarrow 1}+\alpha_{1\leftarrow 0}\mapsto\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}

By the definition of
\widehat{A}(2)
, the sections corresponding to loops are identified with
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-scalar multiplication by the tautological section
\lambda
. So, for any
\alpha_{0\leftarrow 1}\in M_{0\leftarrow 1}
and
\alpha^{\prime}_{1\leftarrow 0}\in M_{1\leftarrow 0}
, their products yield
\alpha_{0\leftarrow 1}\alpha^{\prime}_{1\leftarrow 0}=\lambda e_{0}
and
\alpha^{\prime}_{1\leftarrow 0}\alpha_{0\leftarrow 1}=\lambda e_{1}
. The trace of their commutator

\operatorname{Tr}([\alpha_{0\leftarrow 1},\alpha^{\prime}_{1\leftarrow 0}])=\operatorname{Tr}(\lambda e_{0}-\lambda e_{1})=\lambda-\lambda=0.

This implies that the trace map also vanishes on
[\widehat{A}(2),\widehat{A}(2)]
.

Moreover,
\operatorname{Tr}
is clearly surjective and the section
\frac{1}{2}(e_{0}+e_{1}):O_{Y}\to\widehat{A}(2)
provides a splitting. Hence, we have a direct sum decomposition

(7)

\widehat{A}(2)=\mathcal{O}_{Y}(e_{0}+e_{1})\oplus\widehat{A}(2)^{0}

where
\widehat{A}(2)^{0}=\textrm{ker}(\operatorname{Tr})
contains the commutator subsheaf
[\widehat{A}(2),\widehat{A}(2)]
. This verifies the first half of the definition of a pointwise Clifford algebra.

For any point
y\in Y_{0}
, the fiber
\widehat{A}(2)|_{y}
is a path algebra of the quiver
Q(2)
with relations. It is observed in
[
21
, Proposition 4.1]
that
\widehat{A}(2)|_{y}
is isomorphic to the even Clifford algebra associated to a degenerate quadratic form. Moreover, the explicit isomorphism provided in
[
21
, Eq. (19)]
identifies the direct sum decomposition
7
with the natural decomposition of the even Clifford algebra.

For any point
y\in Y\setminus Y_{0}
, the fiber
\widehat{A}(2)|_{y}
is a matrix algebra by Proposition
3.5
(2) which is the even Clifford algebra associated to a nondegenerate quadratic form (e.g. see
[
18
, Remark 2.4]
). Moreover, the trace map on a matrix algebra is unique, and so is the direct sum decomposition
7
. Hence, the isomorphism between
\widehat{A}(2)|_{y}
and
\operatorname{Mat}_{2\times 2}(\mathbb{C})
identifies the the decompositions.

Therefore,
\widehat{A}(2)
is a pointwise Clifford algebra and the result follows from applying
[
18
, Proposition 2.7]
.
Moreover, by its construction, the vector bundle
F
is given by
(\widehat{A}(2)^{0})^{\vee}
.
∎

From the perspective of even Clifford algebras, there is a further central reduction of the sheaf of algebras
\widehat{A}(2)\cong\mathcal{C}\ell_{0}(q)
into a sheaf of Azumaya algebras
\widehat{\mathcal{C}\ell_{0}(q)}
on the
2
-nd root stack
\widehat{Y}
of
Y
along
Y_{0}

[
17
, Section 3.6]
(see
[
21
, Section 3.1-3.2]
for a summary). In particular, we have

(8)

\operatorname{Coh}(\widehat{Y},\widehat{\mathcal{C}\ell_{0}(q)})\cong\operatorname{Coh}(Y,\mathcal{C}\ell_{0}(q))

Note that there is a natural morphism
S^{\bullet}K_{C}^{-2}\to S^{\bullet}K_{C}^{-1}
of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-algebras which induces a (2-sheeted) cyclic cover
\eta:\widetilde{Y}=\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}(S^{\bullet}K_{C}^{-1})\to\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}(S^{\bullet}K_{C}^{-2})=Y
with a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-action and ramified at
Y_{0}
. So,
\widehat{Y}
is the quotient stack
\left[\widetilde{Y}/(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\right]
. If we restrict the equivalence
8
to a smooth curve
\widetilde{C}\subset Y
which intersects
Y_{0}
transversally (which happens for the case in Section
4.3
), then the sheaf of Azumaya algebras splits
[
17
, Corollary 3.16]
and the equivalence
8
becomes

\operatorname{Coh}\left(\left[\Sigma/(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\right]\right)\cong\operatorname{Coh}(\widetilde{C},\mathcal{C}\ell_{0}(q)|_{\widetilde{C}})

where
\Sigma\subset\tilde{Y}
is the double cover of
\widetilde{C}
. When
\widetilde{C}\subset Y
is a smooth spectral curve for cyclic Higgs bundles of dimension vector
(p,p)
, the double cover
\Sigma\subset\widetilde{Y}
is exactly the spectral curve of the associated
GL(2p,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle. Hence, the equivalence implies that cyclic Higgs bundles of dimension vector
(p,p)
with spectral curve
\widetilde{C}
corresponds to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-equivariant line bundles on
\Sigma
. This description is consistent with the characterization of
U(p,p)
-Higgs bundles as fixed points of an involution (see
[
26
, Proposition 3]
).

Remark 4.2
.

On a related note, we remark that conic fibrations also arise in the context of

-Hitchin systems. By the work of Diaconescu–Donagi–Pantev
[

(see also
[

there is a family of (Calabi-Yau) affine conic fibrations whose intermediate Jacobians are isomorphic to the Hitchin fibers of

4.3.
Spectral data for cyclic Higgs bundles of dimension vector
(p,\dots,p)

Consider
\bm{p}=(p,\dots,p)
and fix
(\widetilde{C}_{0},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{m-1})\in B(\bm{p})
. By Remark
3.12
, the degree of
\widetilde{C}_{i}
over
C
is
p
and so
q_{0}=\dots=q_{m-1}=0
in
6
. This means that the associated spectral curves
\widetilde{C}_{0}=\dots=\widetilde{C}_{m-1}=\widetilde{C}
coincide.

Suppose the common spectral curve
\widetilde{C}
is smooth and not equal to
Y_{0}
. The spectral data
(\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}},\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}})
in Corollary
3.11
can be further simplified as follows. For
i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
, the pure dimension one sheaf
F_{i}
on
Y
is a line bundle
L_{i}
on
\widetilde{C}
. Note that

\psi_{i}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\widetilde{C}}(L_{i}\otimes\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-1},L_{i+1})\cong H^{0}(\widetilde{C},L_{i}^{-1}\otimes L_{i+1}\otimes\pi^{*}K_{C}).

Since
\psi_{i}
is an isomorphism away from

, we see that
\psi_{i}
is a non-zero section of
L_{i}^{-1}\otimes L_{i+1}\otimes\pi^{*}K_{C}
and determines an effective divisor
D_{i}=\operatorname{div}(\psi_{i})
. Conversely, given
L_{i}
and an effective divisor

, we can recover

as
\mathcal{O}(D_{i})\otimes L_{i}\otimes\pi^{*}K_{C}^{-1}
and
\psi_{i}
as the unique section that vanishes on

. The relation
\psi_{i-1}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_{i}=\operatorname{Id}_{F_{i}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\lambda
translates into the relation that

(9)

D_{0}+\dots+D_{m-1}=\operatorname{div}(\lambda|_{\widetilde{C}})

By starting with a line bundle
L_{0}
on
\widetilde{C}
and running the argument above, we conclude that the spectral data
(\{F_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}},\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}})
in Corollary

is equivalent to the data of
(L_{0},D_{0},\dots,D_{m-1})
where

is a line bundle on
\widetilde{C}
and each

is an effective divisor on
\widetilde{C}
satisfying the relation
9
.

For
\bm{p}=(p,p)
, the description above recovers the spectral data for

-Higgs bundles studied in the work of Schaposnik
[
26
]
.

5.
Future Directions

5.1.
Spectral data for cyclic Higgs bundles of arbitrary dimension vector
(p_{0},\dots,p_{m-1}
)

The primary reason that the spectral data in the case of
(p,\dots,p)
admits a simplification to
(L_{0},D_{0},\dots,D_{m-1})
is that the associated spectral curves
\widetilde{C}_{0}=\dots=\widetilde{C}_{m-1}
coincide. However, as soon as one of the components in the dimension vector is different from the others, at least one of the spectral curves will be reducible.

For example, consider
\bm{p}=(p+1,\dots,p)
and fix
(\widetilde{C}_{0},\dots,\widetilde{C}_{m-1})\in B(\bm{p})
. As noted in Remark
3.12
, we must have
q_{0}=1
and
q_{i}=0
for
i\neq 0
in
6
. This implies that the associated spectral curves are given by
\widetilde{C}_{0}=\widetilde{C}\cup Y_{0}
and
\widetilde{C}_{1}=\dots=\widetilde{C}_{m-1}=\widetilde{C}
, where
\widetilde{C}
is a degree
p
cover over
C
. Then it is possible to reduce the spectral data in Corollary
3.11
in terms of spectral data on
\widetilde{C}
for dimension vector
(p,\dots,p)
, a line bundle on
Y_{0}\cong C
and a gluing data (such as
[
25
, Corollary 5.10]
). However, the complexity of this kind of analysis grows as the difference between the components in the dimension vector increases.

5.2.
Moduli spaces of cyclic Higgs bundles

As mentioned in the introduction, the moduli space of cyclic Higgs bundles can be viewed as the fixed point locus of an
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
-action on the moduli space of
GL(r,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle. By the spectral corresondence established in Corollary
3.11
and Proposition
3.9
, we can interpret this as the moduli space of pure dimension one sheaves on the noncommutative surface
(Y,\widehat{A}(m))
. One can argue the existence of its coarse moduli space by defining the appropriate stability conditions and show that the sheaf
\widehat{A}(m)
of algebras is a sheaf of rings of differential operators in the sense of Simpson
[
28
]
. Then the general theory of Simpson guarantees the existence of moduli space of semistable
\widehat{A}(m)
-modules on
Y
.

A potential application of this reinterpretation is an algebraic approach to study their connected components. Recall from Section
4.2
that
\widehat{A}(m)
is isomorphic to the sheaf of even Clifford algebras of a conic fibration on
Y
. It is proven in the work of Lahoz–Macrì–Stellari
[
19
, Theorem 2.12]
that the moduli space of modules over even Clifford algebras which arises from a cubic threefold is irreducible and hence connected. Their proof is a variant of the classical argument of Mukai that originally establishes the connectedness of the moduli space of sheaves on a K3 surface. Therefore, we expect a variant of Mukai’s argument can be used to prove the connectedness of the moduli space of
\widehat{A}(m)
-modules on
Y
(after imposing a suitable numerical invariant such as the Toledo invariant). This would provide an algebraic approach that complements the Morse-theoretic approach of Bradlow–García-Prada–Gothen
[
4
]
to study the connected components of the moduli space of
U(p,q)
-Higgs bundles.

5.3.
Generalized Hitchin fibration for quiver bundles

From the quiver theoretic viewpoint, cyclic Higgs bundles represent the quiver bundles of a specific quiver
Q(m)
. It would be interesting to study the spectral correspondence for quiver bundles of other types of quivers. A quick observation is that the reason that the case of the quiver
Q(m)
works well is that the center of the associated path algebra is exactly
S^{\bullet}K_{C}^{-m}
(Proposition
3.4
). It is known from the general theory of quiver that a quiver without oriented loop has trivial center. So, the natural first step to analyze the case for other quivers will be to look at quivers with oriented loops and then study the center subalgebra of their path algebras.

From the moduli viewpoint, the spectral correspondence for quiver bundles is equivalent to the study of the fibers of the corresponding generalized Hitchin fibration (originally defined by Schmitt in
[
27
]
). To give a brief description of the generalized Hitchin fibration, it is more convenient to use the language of stacks.

Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a general quiver with head and tail maps
h,t:Q_{1}\to Q_{0}
. For a fixed dimension vector
\bm{p}=(p_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}
, we associate to each vertex
i\in Q_{0}
a complex vector space
V_{i}
of dimension
p_{i}
. The space of representations of
Q
with dimension vector
\bm{p}
is the affine space

\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\bm{p})=\bigoplus_{a\in Q_{1}}\operatorname{Hom}(V_{t(a)},V_{h(a)}).

The change of basis group is defined as the product

G_{\bm{p}}:=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}GL(V_{i})

which acts naturally on
\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\bm{p})
by conjugation. Given this action, there is the associated morphism from the quotient stack to the GIT quotient:

\chi:\left[\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\bm{p})/G_{\bm{p}}\right]\to\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\bm{p})/\!/G_{\bm{p}}

Just as the original Hitchin fibration for
G
-Higgs bundles is induced by
\left[\mathfrak{g}/G\right]\to\mathfrak{g}/\!/G
, by following the general construction as in
[
23
,
24
]
, the morphism
\chi
induces a generalized Hitchin fibration from the moduli stack of quiver bundles to an affine Hitchin base.

In fact, Ngô has recently initiated a general framework to study the generalized Hitchin fibration induced from the action of a reductive group on an affine normal scheme (see the related work
[
12
]
). The action of
G_{\bm{p}}
on
\operatorname{Rep}(Q,\bm{p})
can be seen as an example of this general framework. His approach is closer to the original cameral cover approach that works well over the regular locus. Our emphasis here is on the quiver structure and a description over the whole Hitchin base. It will be interesting to study the relation between the different approaches.
