Volume of maximal representations into SO 0 ​ ( 2 , 3 ) \mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,3)
Timothé Lemistre

Abstract
. We study the volume of maximal representations from a surface group into SO 0 ​ ( 2 , 3 ) \mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,3) . We show that it is bounded from above, uniformly in the genus of the surface. On the Gothen components, we prove that it is bounded from below by a strictly positive constant.



Introduction

Teichmüller space parametrises marked hyperbolic structures on the closed oriented surface
\Sigma_{g}
of genus
g\geqslant 2
. By the uniformisation theorem and the work of Goldman (see
[
Gol88
]
), it is also a connected component of the representation variety
\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g}),G)=\Hom(\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g}),G)/G
for
G=\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,1)=\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. This connected component is homeomorphic to a ball and composed only of faithful and discrete representations. The work of Hitchin in the 1990s (see
[
Hit92
]
) has given further examples of connected components homeomorphic to balls for other simple Lie groups
G
. Those connected components have been interpreted geometrically by Labourie (see
[
Lab06
]
), who also showed that they contain only faithful and discrete representations. The latter property defines what Wienhard calls higher-rank Teichmüller space
[
Wie18
]
.

These generalisations of Teichmüller space rely on different classes of representations. The main examples are Hitchin representations (see
[
Hit92
]
), maximal representations (
[
BIW10
]
), positive representations (see
[
FG06
]
and
[
BIW10
]
),
\mathbb{H}^{p,q}
-convex cocompact representations (see
[
BK25
]
) and
\Theta
-positive representations (see
[
GW18
]
). We study here representations at the intersection of these notions : maximal representations in
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,3)
.

Maximal representations of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
into
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,q+1)
(
q\geqslant 1
) admit a geometric interpretation using the pseudo-hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}^{2,q}
, which are pseudo-Riemannian generalisations of the hyperbolic spaces. Indeed, they leave invariant particular submanifolds of
\mathbb{H}^{2,q}
(see
[
CTT19
]
), which allows associating them geometric quantities (see for example Mazzoli-Viaggi
[
MV25
]
).

We study here such a geometric quantity: the
volume
of a maximal representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})\to\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,q+1)
, defined as follows. By the work Danciger-Guéritaud-Kassel (see
[
DGK18
]
),
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
preserves properly convex closed subsets of
\mathbb{H}^{2,q}
on which it acts properly discontinuously and cocompactly. Among these, there exists a unique minimal convex, which we denote by
\cal{C}_{\rho}
. We call
volume
of
\rho
the volume of the quotient :

\Vol(\rho)=\Vol(\cal{C}_{\rho}/\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})).

This notion of volume has been studied by Bonsante-Seppi-Tamburelli for
q=1
. Since
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,2)
is a double cover of
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,1)^{2}
, a maximal representation of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
into
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,2)
corresponds to a pair of maximal representations into
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,1)
and a representation
\varepsilon
into
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, thus its conjugacy class corresponds to a pair of points
(h,h^{\prime})
in Teichmüller space of
\Sigma
and
\varepsilon
: we denote it
[\rho_{h,h^{\prime},\varepsilon}]\in\chi(\Gamma,\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,2))
. They then show (see
[
BST17
]
) :

\Vol(\rho_{h,h^{\prime},\varepsilon})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d(h,h^{\prime})\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}+\infty,

where
d(h,h^{\prime})
is the Weil-Petersson distance between
h
and
h^{\prime}
. We show an opposite result for
n=2
.

Theorem A
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for every integer
g\geqslant 2
and every maximal representation
\rho
of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
into
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,3)
, we have

\Vol(\rho)\leqslant C\,g.

Theorem
A
is a particular case of Theorem
C
, which is the technical core of this article and concerns maximal surfaces. Let us denote by
\cal{M}^{*}_{2,2}
the space of pointed complete maximal surfaces of
\mathbb{H}^{2,2}
(see Definition
1.20
). We use the following property, which appears in many similar contexts (see
[
CT90
]
,
[
CL17
]
or
[
OT23
]
). A continuous function
f
from
\cal{M}^{*}_{2,2}
to
\mathbb{R}
is said to be
exponentially decaying
(see Definition
3.7
) if there exist constants
C,\alpha>0
such that for every complete maximal surface
S
of
\mathbb{H}^{2,2}
and every ball
B(x,r)
of
S
on which the sectional curvature
K
satisfies
\left|K\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{4}
, we have
\left|f(S,x)\right|\leqslant Ce^{-\alpha r}
.

Theorem
C
.
Let
f:\cal{M}_{2,2}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous and exponentially decaying function. There exists a constant
C_{f}
satisfying the following property. Let
\Gamma
be a subgroup of
\mathrm{PO}(2,3)
preserving a complete maximal surface
S
of
\mathbb{H}^{2,2}
and whose action on
S
is properly discontinuous and free. Suppose that the sectional curvature
K
of
S/\Gamma
tends to

at infinity. We have

\int_{S/\Gamma}\left|f\right|\leqslant C_{f}\int_{S/\Gamma}\left|K\right|.

Theorem
A
follows from Theorem
C
together with differential-geometric considerations (Section
4.1
). Here are other applications of Theorem
C
.

Corollary
4.15
.
Moriani shows that a complete maximal surface
S
of
\mathbb{H}^{2,2}
has finite total curvature if, and only if, the boundary of
S
in
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2,3})
is the union of a finite number
N
of projective segments. We then have
\int_{S}K=-\frac{\pi}{2}(N-4)

[
Mor25
, Main Theorem]
. Theorem
C
proves that there exists a constant
C_{\Vol}
independent of
S
and
N
satisfying

\Vol(\Conv(S))\leqslant C_{\Vol}(N-4).

Corollary
4.16
.
By
[
CTT19
]
, every maximal representation
\rho
from
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
to
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,3)
defines a
\rho
-invariant maximal surface of
\mathbb{H}^{2,2}
denoted by
S_{\rho}
(see Section
1.6
). The quotient
S_{\rho}/\rho(\Gamma)
is then a compact Riemannian manifold whose sectional curvature we denote by
K_{S_{\rho}/\rho(\Gamma)}
. Theorem
C
proves that for every real
\alpha>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}
such that for any maximal representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})\to\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,3)
, we have

\int_{S/\rho(\Gamma)}\left|K_{S/\rho(\Gamma)}\right|^{\alpha}\leqslant C_{\alpha}\,g.

Let us turn back to representations. In
[
Got01
]
, Gothen discovered unexpected connected components of the space of maximal representations into
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,3)
. On the latter, called Gothen components, the volume
\Vol
does never vanish. These components are classified by an integer topological invariant denoted by
\deg(\rho)
, satisfying
0<\deg(\rho)\leqslant 4g-4
.

Theorem
B
.
There exists a constant
D>0
such that for every integer
g\geqslant 2
and every Gothen representation
\rho
from
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})
into
\mathrm{SO}_{0}(2,3)
, we have

\Vol(\rho)\geqslant D\,\deg(\rho).

Hitchin representations, defined by
\deg(\rho)=4g-4
, thus satisfy bounds of the type
Dg\leqslant\Vol(\rho)\leqslant Cg
. The bounds of Theorems
A
and
B
are therefore optimal in
g
.

Strategy of the proof of Theorem
A
.

By the work of
[
CTT19
]
, to each maximal representation
\rho
of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
into
\mathrm{SO}(2,3)
is associated a maximal surface
S_{\rho}
in
\mathbb{H}^{2,2}
(see Section
1.6
). The convex hull of this surface is equal to the convex
\cal{C}_{\rho}
used to define
\Vol(\rho)
. This allows reducing the study of the volume
\Vol(\rho)
to a problem of analytical nature on
S_{\rho}
to which Theorem
C
gives an answer, as follows. First, we define functions on
S_{\rho}
reflecting its geometry and satisfying elliptic differential equations (Section
3.1
). Using elliptic estimates, we then show the rapid decay of these functions when the curvature is small (
3.2
). We define a domain
D
where the sectional curvature is far from
0
. This definition allows controlling the volume of a slice of
\cal{C}_{\rho}
above a point of
S_{\rho}
, by a function of the distance to
D
(Section
4.1
). We then integrate this estimate (Section
4.2
and appendix
B
). We thus prove Theorem
C
, hence Theorem
A
(Section
4.2
).

Organisation.

We recall the classical results and techniques in Section
1
. Section
2
is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
B
: one finds there in germ the main ideas of the proof of Theorem
C
. In Section
3
, we prove the exponential decay of functions depending only on the germ of the considered surface. We then infer global results in Section
4
(Theorem
C

A

1.
Preliminaries

1.1.
Pseudo-hyperbolic space

A pseudo-Riemannian metric on a smooth manifold
M
is a section of the bundle
\Sym^{2}(\Tan^{*}M)
of symmetric bilinear forms on
M
, nowhere degenerate and of constant signature. It allows defining, as for a Riemannian metric, the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{M}
: it is the unique connection
\nabla
which is metric (
\nabla g=0
) and torsion-free. We denote by
\courb\in\Omega^{2}(\Hom(\Tan M)
its curvature tensor, defined by
\courb(a,b)X=\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}X-\nabla_{b}\nabla_{a}X-\nabla_{[a,b]}X
. Given a non-degenerate plane
P
for which we choose an orthonormal basis
(e_{1},e_{2})
, we define the sectional curvature
\kappa(P)=\frac{\left\langle\courb(e_{1},e_{2})e_{2},e_{1}\right\rangle}{\left\langle e_{1},e_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle e_{2},e_{2}\right\rangle}
. If
M
is oriented, we can define a volume form
\vol^{M}
, which is equal to
1
on any direct orthonormal basis. The corresponding measure and volume functional, denoted by
\mu_{M}
and
\Vol^{M}
, are defined independently of the orientation.

Pseudo-hyperbolic space.

The study of pseudo-hyperbolic spaces was initiated by the pioneering work of Mess
[
Mes07
]
linking geometry of
\mathbb{H}^{2,1}
and Teichmüller theory (in this direction, see also
[
BS10
]
and
[
BS20
]
). Those spaces allow interpreting geometrically a large class of representations into
\mathrm{PO}(p,q+1)
, called
\mathbb{H}^{p,q}
-convex-cocompact (see
[
Mes07
]
,
[
Bar15
]
,
[
BM12
]
and
[
DGK18
]
).

Let henceforth be integers
p>0
and
q\geqslant 0
. We construct the pseudo-hyperbolic space of signature
(p,q)
, which generalises the usual hyperbolic space (obtained with
q=0
) and the anti-de Sitter space (obtained with
q=1
). Let us denote by
\mathbb{R}^{p,q+1}
the oriented vector space
\mathbb{R}^{p+q+1}
endowed with the quadratic form of signature
(p,q+1)
defined by

\quadra(x_{1},\dots,x_{p+q+1})=x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{p}^{2}-x_{p+1}^{2}-\dots-x_{p+q+1}^{2}.

We denote by
(x,y)\mapsto\left\langle x,y\right\rangle
the associated bilinear form, which defines a pseudo-Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{R}^{p,q+1}
, and by
\mathrm{O}(p,q+1)
its orthogonal group. The group
\mathrm{PO}(p,q+1)=\mathrm{O}(p,q+1)/\{\pm\Id\}
acts on the projective space
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{R}^{p,q+1})
preserving an open set, the
pseudo-hyperbolic space
, defined by

\mathbb{H}^{p,q}=\{[x]\in\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{R}^{p,q+1})\mid\quadra(x)<0\}.

Let us define a double covering of
\mathbb{H}^{p,q}
by

\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p,q+1}\mid\quadra(x)=-1\}.

We have
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}/\{\pm\Id\}=\mathbb{H}^{p,q}
. A tangent space
\Tan_{x}\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}
is identified with
\mathrm{Span}(x)^{\perp}
, thus inherits the metric of
\mathbb{R}^{p,q+1}
. This endows
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}
with a metric of signature
(p,q)
which descends to a metric on
\mathbb{H}^{p,q}
. The metrics of
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}
and
\mathbb{H}^{p,q}
have constant curvature
-1
and respective isometry groups
\mathrm{O}(p,q+1)
and
\mathrm{PO}(p,q+1)
. Their Levi-Civita connections are geodesically complete, the geodesics of
\mathbb{H}^{p,q}
being its intersections with projective lines.

Connection.

The submanifold
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p,q+1}
is umbilic : given a vector
\mathsf{u}
in
\Tan_{x}\mathbb{H}^{p,q}
and a vector field
\mathsf{V}
on
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}
, denoting by
\nabla^{\mathbb{R}}
and
\nabla^{\mathbb{H}}
the connections of
\mathbb{R}^{p,q+1}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}
respectively, we have

(1.1)

\nabla^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathsf{u}}\mathsf{V}=\nabla^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathsf{u}}\mathsf{V}+\left\langle\mathsf{u},\mathsf{V}\right\rangle x.

Geodesics.

Consider a maximal geodesic of
\mathbb{H}^{p,q}
. Its image is the intersection of
\mathbb{H}^{p,q}
with a projective line. More precisely, it can be lifted to a complete geodesic of
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}
, i.e. of the form
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}
. If
\dot{\gamma}(0)=\mathsf{v}\in\Tan_{x}\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}
, then

•

\gamma(t)=\cosh(t)x+\sinh(t)\mathsf{v}
if
\left\langle\mathsf{v},\mathsf{v}\right\rangle>0
(
\gamma
is said to be spacelike) ;

•

\gamma(t)=x+t\mathsf{v}
if
\left\langle\mathsf{v},\mathsf{v}\right\rangle=0
(
\gamma
is said to be lightlike) ;

•

\gamma(t)=\cos(t)x+\sin(t)\mathsf{v}
if
\left\langle\mathsf{v},\mathsf{v}\right\rangle<0
(
\gamma
is said to be timelike).

Two distinct points
u
and
v
in
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}
are joined by a geodesic if, and only if,
\left\langle u,v\right\rangle<1
. If it exists, this geodesic is unique.

•

If
\left\langle u,v\right\rangle<-1
, it is spacelike, of length
\cosh^{-1}(-{\left\langle u,v\right\rangle})
.

•

If
\left\langle u,v\right\rangle=-1
, it is lightlike.

•

If
-1<\left\langle u,v\right\rangle<1
, it is timelike, of length
\cos^{-1}{\left\langle u,v\right\rangle}
.

We deduce that two distinct points
u,v\in\mathbb{H}^{p,q}
are joined by a geodesic, unique up to reparametrisation.

1.2.
Pseudo-Riemannian geometry

Let
M
be a pseudo-Riemannian manifold of signature
(p,q)
and
\nabla
its Levi-Civita connection. Let
S
be a submanifold of
M
whose induced metric is non-degenerate. Let us consider the restriction of the affine bundle
(\Tan M,\nabla)
to
S
: it decomposes as the orthogonal sum
\Tan S\oplus\Nor S
. The second fundamental form of
S
is a bundle morphism
\II:\Tan S\otimes\Tan S\to\Nor S
, defined by
\II(\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V})=(\nabla_{\mathsf{U}}\mathsf{V})^{\Nor S}
(normal component of
\nabla_{\mathsf{U}}\mathsf{V}
). The shape operator is a bundle morphism
B:\Tan S\otimes\Nor S\to\Tan S
, defined by
B(\mathsf{U},\mathsf{N})=(\nabla_{\mathsf{U}}\mathsf{N})^{\Tan S}
(tangent component of
\nabla_{\mathsf{U}}\mathsf{N}
). Therefore, we have
\left\langle\II(\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V}),\mathsf{N}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathsf{V},B(\mathsf{U},\mathsf{N})\right\rangle=0
and the restriction of
\nabla
to
(\Tan M)_{|S}
decomposes as

\nabla=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla^{\Tan}&-B\\
\II&\nabla^{\Nor}\end{pmatrix}.

S
is said to be spacelike if its induced metric is Riemannian. Let us now suppose that
S
is spacelike, of dimension
p
. Let
x
be a point of
S
and
(e_{i})
(
1\leqslant i\leqslant p
) an orthonormal frame of
\Tan_{x}S
. We define the norm
\left\|\II\right\|_{2}
and the mean curvature
H
of
S
by

and

\displaystyle\left\|\II\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j}\left\langle\II(e_{i},e_{j}),\II(e_{i},e_{j})\right\rangle

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i}\II(e_{i},e_{i}).

S
is said to be maximal if
H=0
. If
M
is Riemannian, this corresponds to the classical notion of minimal submanifold. Note that the metrics of
(\Tan S)^{2}
and
\Nor S
allow defining the adjoint
\II^{*}:\Nor S\to\Tan S\otimes\Tan S
and that we have
\left\|\II\right\|_{2}^{2}=-\tr[\II^{*}\II]
.

Sasaki metric.

Given a pseudo-Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, let us construct a metric
g^{\Tan M}
on the tangent space
\Tan M
seen as a manifold, called the Sasaki metric, which will be used in Section
2.2
and appendix
A
. We have two distributions on the total space
\Tan M
of the bundle
\pi:\Tan M\to M
: the vertical distribution
\mathbf{vert}=\ker(\mathrm{d}\pi)
and the horizontal distribution
\mathbf{hor}
given by the Levi-Civita connection (seen as an Ehresmann connection). At a point
v_{x}
pf
\Tan_{x}M
, we define
g^{\Tan M}_{v_{x}}
as follows.

Given
\mathsf{u}\in\Tan_{x}M
, let us denote by
t\mapsto\psi_{\mathsf{u},v_{x}}(t)
the path in
\Tan M
defined by parallel transporting
v_{x}
in
\Tan M
along the path
t\mapsto\exp^{M}_{x}(t\mathsf{u})
(defined for
t
close to
0
). We define the Sasaki metric
g^{\Tan M}
by imposing that
\mathbf{vert}_{v_{x}}
and
\mathbf{hor}_{v_{x}}
are orthogonal (
g^{\Tan M}(\mathsf{v},\mathsf{h})=0
for
\mathsf{v}\in\mathbf{vert}_{v_{x}}
,
\mathsf{h}\in\mathbf{hor}_{v_{x}}
), and that the following maps are isometries:

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\iota_{\mathbf{hor}}:&\Tan_{x}M&\to&\mathbf{hor}_{v_{x}}\\
&\mathsf{u}&\mapsto&\frac{\mathrm{d}\ }{\mathrm{d}t}\big|_{t=0}\psi_{\mathsf{u},v_{x}}(t),\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\iota_{\mathbf{vert}}:&\Tan_{x}M&\to&\mathbf{vert}_{v_{x}}\\
&\mathsf{u}&\mapsto&\frac{\mathrm{d}\ }{\mathrm{d}t}\big|_{t=0}v_{x}+t\mathsf{u}.\end{array}

We deduce the following lemma, which justifies the use of the Sasaki metric in this article.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a pseudo-Riemannian manifold and
S
a submanifold of
M
in restriction to which the metric
g
is non-degenerate. Let us restrict the Sasaki metric to the normal bundle
\Nor S
. Let
E
be a measurable subset of
\Nor S
. We have

\Vol^{\Nor S}(E)=\int_{S}\left(\Vol^{\Nor_{x}S}(E\cap\Nor_{x}S)\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{S}(x).

Proof.

Let us choose a simply connected open set
U
of
S
and orientations of
U
and of the bundle
\Nor U=(\Nor S)_{|U}
. The volume form
\vol^{\Nor U}
of the total space
\Nor U
satisfies

\vol^{\Nor U}=\pi^{*}(\vol^{S})\wedge\phi,

where the restriction of the differential form
\phi
to a fibre
\Nor_{x}U
is the volume form
\vol^{\Nor_{x}U}
of this fibre. We have, for
\chi
the indicator function of
E
:

\int_{\Nor U}\chi\ \mathrm{d}\!\vol^{\Nor U}=\int_{U}\left(\int_{\Nor_{x}U}\chi\mathrm{d}\!\vol^{\Nor_{x}S}\right)\mathrm{d}\!\vol^{S}(x),

whence the result if
E
is contained in
\Nor U
, thus in general.
∎

Proof.

Proposition

Definition 1.11

and

Lemma 1.13

(decomposition of an oriented metric vector bundle of rank
2
)

We have a canonical decomposition

, where

\cal{L}^{-1}
and
\ker(J+i\Id)

Proof.

All the geometric data of

and
\cal{E}=P[\mathbb{C}^{2}]
depend on the action of
\mathrm{SO}(2,\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathbb{C}^{2}
respectively : the lemma thus follows from the properties of this action.
The canonical orientation of

and its eigenspaces

are the isotropic lines of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
for the canonical quadratic form, denoted by

\mathbb{C}^{2}
preserves each of these two lines, acting on

as holomorphic vector bundles.

The lines

are orthogonal for the canonical hermitian metric of

deduced from

Lemma 1.15

1.6.

and volume

Let

allow defining particular connected components of the space of representations of

into this group.
For

is simple and hermitian : this allows defining maximal representations. These have been studied from the point of view of Higgs bundles (by García-Prada, Bradlow and Gothen, see

]
and

-convex cocompact representations (see

]

). Hitchin representations are maximal and maximal representations are

We will mainly use the geometric interpretation of a maximal representation given by the following theorem.

(

For every maximal representation

The set of maximal representations of

We now define the volume of a maximal representation

We call

Note that a representation

. On the contrary, the Gothen components contain only irreducible representations, hence of non-zero volume. These are therefore the only connected components of the

Let

Exponential decay

Remark 3.3

A change of orientation of

Proposition 3.4
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists

such that for every pointed complete maximal surface

\left|u(S,x)-\ln\frac{1}{2}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon

\left|v(S,x)-\ln\frac{1}{2}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon

Proof.

For every
(S,x)
in the space
\mathcal{M}_{2,2}^{*}
of pointed complete maximal surfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{2,2}
(see section

), according to remark

\phi(S,x)=(\min(\left\|\alpha^{S}_{x}\right\|^{2},\left\|\beta^{S}_{x}\right\|^{2}),\max(\left\|\alpha^{S}_{x}\right\|^{2},\left\|\beta^{S}_{x}\right\|^{2})).

Define
\kappa:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R},\ (x,y)\mapsto x+y-1
(compare with the formula

). The map

The aim of this section is to explicitly control the function

appearing in the statement of Proposition

; to this end, let us prove the following technical lemma (compare
[

, Lemma 2.3]
). Suppose
\mu_{1}
bounded on a ball

: the formulas (

) give an inequality of the type
\Delta\left|\mu_{2}\right|\geqslant C\sinh(\left|\mu_{2}\right|)
on this ball, which is the hypothesis of the following lemma. Note that in the points where

cancels, this inequality is only true in the sense of distributions but trivial.

Lemma 3.5

(exponential decay)
.

Let
\kappa,c>0
be real numbers,
M
a complete simply connected Riemannian
n
-manifold satisfying
\Ric\geqslant-(n-1)\kappa
, a ball
B(x,r)
and a function
f:B(x,r)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
of class
\cal{C}^{2}
, bounded and satisfying the following equation :

(\Delta-c)f\geqslant 0.

There exists
\alpha>0
, depending only and explicitly on
(n,\kappa,c)
, such that

f(x)\leqslant 2e^{-\alpha r}\ \sup_{B(x,r)}f.

Proof.

We may suppose
\sup_{B(x,r)}f=1
. The function
\rho=d(x,\cdot)
satisfies (
[
Pet16
, Lemma 7.1.2]
) :

\Delta\rho\leqslant(n-1)\sqrt{\kappa}\coth(\sqrt{\kappa}\rho).

We define a barrier function
b=\frac{\cosh(\alpha\rho)}{\cosh(\alpha r)}
,
\alpha>0
remaining to be chosen. Since
b=1
on
\partial B(x,r)
, we only need to have
(\Delta-c)b\leqslant 0
in order to conclude that
b\geqslant f
on
B(x,r)
. Now we have

\displaystyle(\Delta-c)b

\displaystyle=\left(\alpha^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{\coth(\alpha\rho)}\Delta\rho-c\right)b

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\alpha^{2}+\alpha(n-1)\sqrt{\kappa}\frac{\coth(\sqrt{\kappa}\rho)}{\coth(\alpha\rho)}-c\right)b.

By imposing
\alpha\leqslant\sqrt{\kappa}
, we obtain as soon as
\rho>0
:

(\Delta-c)b\leqslant\left(\alpha^{2}+\alpha(n-1)\sqrt{\kappa}-c\right)b.

Let us choose
\alpha>0
small enough so that the right-hand term is negative : we obtain
(\Delta-c)b\leqslant 0
, as required. The function
b
is therefore a barrier : we have
f(x)\leqslant b(x)=\frac{1}{\cosh(\alpha r)}\leqslant 2e^{-\alpha r}
.
∎

We now wish to apply Lemma

Lemma 3.6
.

We have

\varepsilon\geqslant 6e^{\sup u-\varepsilon}-4.

Letting

\displaystyle\geqslant\Delta\mu_{1}

We now wish to quantify the convergence of

Definition 3.7

(exponential decay)
.

Fix a real number

\left|f(S,x)\right|\leqslant Ce^{-\alpha\rho}.

Remark 3.8
.

With the notations of Corollary
1.22
, suppose that

is a continuous function from
\mathcal{M}_{2,2}^{*}
to
\mathbb{R}
,

is bounded by Corollary

thus up to modifying

without the hypothesis

.

Remark 3.9

If

is empty (that it

Remark 3.10
.

We will see (see proof of Corollary

is exponentially decaying. This shows that Definition

is independent of the choice of

.

Proposition 3.11

On the ball

4.1.

The proof of Proposition

Let us begin by defining this unit vector. We can consider the second fundamental form

The axes

Let us note that the image of the unit circle by

\displaystyle 2\left\|\II(\mathsf{t}_{u},\mathsf{t}_{u})\right\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left\|u^{2}\II(\partial_{z},\partial_{z})+\overline{u}^{2}\II(\partial_{\overline{z}},\partial_{\overline{z}})\right\|^{2}

and

, then

(minor axis at

) around

still denoted by

(this is possible according to Lemma

For any definite vector subspace
E
of
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
, let us denote
\varepsilon(E)=+1
if
E
is positive,
\varepsilon(E)=-1
if
E
is negative. Let
E
and

be two definite vector subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
and
(e_{i})
an orthonormal basis of

, we define its adjoint

by
\left\langle f(u),v\right\rangle=\left\langle u,f^{*}(v)\right\rangle
, as well as its norm :

\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i}\left\|f(e_{i})\right\|^{2}=\varepsilon(E)\varepsilon(F)\tr(f\circ f^{*}).

The second equality shows
\left\|f\right\|_{2}=\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{2}
. Let us denote by
\pi_{E\to F}:E\to F
the orthogonal projection. We define the
angle

\theta(E,F)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
between

and

by

\theta(E,F)^{2}=\left\|\pi_{E\to F}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

We have
\pi_{E\to F}^{*}=\pi_{F\to E}
, hence
\theta(E,F)=\theta(F,E)
. Given two definite vector subspaces
E_{1},E_{2}
of
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
and vectors
v_{1}\in E_{1}
,
v_{2}\in E_{2}
, we have by definition:

(4.2)

\left|\left\langle\pi_{E_{1}\to F}(v_{1}),\pi_{E_{2}\to F}(v_{2})\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\theta(E_{1},F)\theta(E_{2},F)\left\|v_{1}\right\|\left\|v_{2}\right\|.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{n}
and
F
be definite subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
such that the
E_{i}
are pairwise orthogonal and satisfy
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}=\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
. We have

(4.3)

\varepsilon(F)\dim(F)=\sum_{i}\varepsilon(E_{i})\,\theta(E_{i},F)^{2}.

Proof.

Let
(f_{i})
be an orthonormal basis of
F
. We have

\displaystyle\varepsilon(F)\dim(F)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left\langle f_{i},f_{i}\right\rangle

since
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\perp}}{{\bigoplus}}E_{j}=\mathbb{R}^{p,q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\left\langle\pi_{F\to E_{j}}(f_{i}),\pi_{F\to E_{j}}(f_{i})\right\rangle

by definition,

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\varepsilon(E_{j})\theta(F,E_{j})^{2}

whence the result given the symmetry
\theta(E_{i},F)=\theta(F,E_{i})
.
∎

Let us fix from now on

, equal to

\left\{\begin{aligned} \theta_{\gamma}(t)&=\theta(F,\mathrm{Span}(\gamma(t)))\\

\theta_{A}(t)&=\theta(F,\mathrm{Span}(A_{\gamma(t)}))\\
\theta_{a}(t)&=\theta(F,\mathrm{Span}(JA_{\gamma(t)}))\end{aligned}\right.

Lemma 4.4
.

There exist constants
R_{0},C,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
(depending only on
k
in Definition
3.7
) such that if
R\geqslant R_{0}
, then we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left|\theta_{\gamma}^{\prime}\right|\leqslant\theta_{\Tan}&&\text{when }\theta_{\gamma}>0\\
&\left|\theta_{\Tan}^{\prime}\right|\leqslant\theta_{\gamma}+\left\|A\right\|\theta_{A}+\left\|a\right\|\theta_{a}&&\text{when }\theta_{\Tan}>0\\
&\left|\theta_{A}^{\prime}\right|\leqslant\theta_{\Tan}+(\theta_{a}+\theta_{A})Ce^{-\alpha R}&&\text{when }\theta_{A}>0\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

Recall (see section
1.3
) that for
\mathsf{v}\in\Tan_{x}\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}
and
X
a vector field on
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}
in a neighbourhood of
x
, the connections
\nabla^{\mathbb{R}}
of
\mathbb{R}^{p,q+1}
and
\nabla^{\mathbb{H}}
of
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}
satisfy
\nabla^{\mathbb{R}}_{\mathsf{v}}X=\left\langle\mathsf{v},X\right\rangle x+\nabla^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathsf{v}}X
(equation (
1.1
)). If moreover
x\in S
and
v\in\Tan_{x}S
, then in the decomposition
\Tan_{x}\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{p,q}=\Tan_{x}S\oplus\Nor_{x}S
, we have

\nabla^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathsf{v}}=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla^{\Tan}_{\mathsf{v}}&B(\mathsf{v},\cdot)\\
\II(\mathsf{v},\cdot)&\nabla^{\Nor}_{\mathsf{v}}\end{pmatrix}.

By definition, we have
(\theta_{\gamma})^{2}=-\left\langle\pi(\gamma),\pi(\gamma)\right\rangle
(where
\pi
still denotes the orthogonal projection on
F
). Differentiating this expression gives

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}(\theta_{\gamma})^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=-2\left\langle\pi(\nabla^{\mathbb{R}}_{\dot{\gamma}}\gamma),\pi(\gamma)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\left\langle\pi(\dot{\gamma}),\pi(\gamma)\right\rangle

thus formula (
4.2
) gives the desired inequality. For
\theta_{\Tan}^{\prime}
, same idea: fix an orthonormal basis of
\Tan_{\gamma(0)}S
, then transport it along
\gamma
thanks to the connection
\nabla^{\Tan}
on the bundle
\Tan S
. We obtain a basis
(\mathrm{t}_{i}(t))
of
\Tan_{\gamma(t)}S
, which allows writing the definition
\theta_{\Tan}^{2}=-\sum_{i}\left\langle\pi(\mathrm{t}_{i}),\pi(\mathrm{t}_{i})\right\rangle
. From now on, we omit the indices : for exemple,
A_{\gamma(t)}
will be simply denoted by
A
. Let us differentiate, taking into account
\nabla^{\mathbb{R}}_{\dot{\gamma}}\mathsf{t}=\left\langle\dot{\gamma},\mathsf{t}\right\rangle\gamma+\II(\mathsf{t},\dot{\gamma})
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}(\theta_{\Tan}^{2})}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{i}\left\langle\pi(\nabla^{\mathbb{R}}_{\dot{\gamma}}\mathrm{t}_{i}),\pi(\mathrm{t}_{i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{i}\left\langle\dot{\gamma},\mathrm{t}_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle\pi(\gamma),\pi(\mathrm{t}_{i})\right\rangle-2\sum_{i}\left\langle\pi(\II(\dot{\gamma},\mathrm{t}_{i})),\pi(\mathrm{t}_{i})\right\rangle

(4.4)

\displaystyle=-2\left\langle\pi(\gamma),\pi(\dot{\gamma})\right\rangle-2\sum_{i}\left\langle\pi(\II(\dot{\gamma},\mathrm{t}_{i})),\pi(\mathrm{t}_{i})\right\rangle

To control the second term, set
\II(\mathsf{t}_{i},\dot{\gamma})=u_{i}\frac{A}{\left\|A\right\|}+v_{i}\frac{a}{\left\|a\right\|}
, where
\left|u_{i}\right|\leqslant\left\|A\right\|
and
\left|v_{i}\right|\leqslant\left\|a\right\|
, so that

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left\langle\pi(\II(\dot{\gamma},\mathrm{t}_{i})),\pi(\mathrm{t}_{i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left\langle\pi(u_{i}\frac{A}{\left\|A\right\|}),\pi(\mathrm{t}_{i})\right\rangle+\sum_{i}\left\langle\pi(v_{i}\frac{a}{\left\|a\right\|}),\pi(\mathrm{t}_{i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}u_{i}\left\langle\pi(\frac{A}{\left\|A\right\|}),\pi(\mathrm{t}_{i})\right\rangle+\sum_{i}v_{i}\left\langle\pi(\frac{a}{\left\|a\right\|}),\pi(\mathrm{t}_{i})\right\rangle.

Taking into account that
\left|u_{i}\right|\leqslant\left\|A\right\|
,
\left|v_{i}\right|\leqslant\left\|a\right\|
and using inequality (
4.2
), we find :

\left|\sum_{i}\left\langle\pi(\II(\dot{\gamma},\mathrm{t}_{i})),\pi(\mathrm{t}_{i})\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\left\|A\right\|\theta_{A}\theta_{\Tan}+\left\|a\right\|\theta_{a}\theta_{\Tan}.

Rewrite equation (
4.4
) using this upper bound, controlling the first term with inequality (
4.2
) : we obtain

\left|\theta_{\Tan}\theta_{\Tan}^{\prime}\right|\leqslant\theta_{\Tan}\theta_{\gamma}+\left\|A\right\|\theta_{A}\theta_{\Tan}+\left\|a\right\|\theta_{a}\theta_{\Tan},

hence the announced inequality on
\theta_{\Tan}^{\prime}
. We bound
\theta_{A}^{\prime}
and
\theta_{a}^{\prime}
using the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

The function
\left\|\nabla^{\Tan}\mathsf{V}\right\|
is exponentially decaying.

Proof.

Recall that the axes are given by
\II(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{V})=A
(major axis) and
\II(\mathsf{V},J\mathsf{V})=a
(minor axis). Let
\nabla^{\Tan}\mathsf{V}=\omega\otimes J(\mathsf{V})
with
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(U)
. We have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\nabla\left\langle\II(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{V}),\II(\mathsf{V},J\mathsf{V})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle(\nabla\II)(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{V}),a\right\rangle+2\left\langle\II(\mathsf{V},\nabla\mathsf{V}),a\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle A,(\nabla\II)(\mathsf{V},J\mathsf{V})\right\rangle+\left\langle A,\II(\nabla\mathsf{V},J\mathsf{V})\right\rangle+\left\langle A,\II(\mathsf{V},\nabla(J\mathsf{V}))\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle(\nabla\II)(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{V}),a\right\rangle+2\omega\left\langle a,a\right\rangle

since
\nabla J=0
and
\nabla\mathsf{V}=\omega\cdot J\mathsf{V}

\displaystyle+\left\langle A,(\nabla\II)(\mathsf{V},J\mathsf{V})\right\rangle+\omega\left\langle A,\II(J\mathsf{V},J\mathsf{V})\right\rangle+\omega\left\langle A,\II(\mathsf{V},J^{2}\mathsf{V})\right\rangle

since
J^{2}=-\Id
and
\II(J\mathsf{u},\mathsf{v})=-\II(\mathsf{u},J\mathsf{v})
, hence

\displaystyle=\left\langle(\nabla\II)(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{V}),A\right\rangle+\left\langle A,(\nabla\II)(\mathsf{V},J\mathsf{V})\right\rangle+2\omega\left\|a\right\|^{2}-2\omega\left\|A\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\langle(\nabla\II)(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{V}),a\right\rangle+\left\langle A,(\nabla\II)(\mathsf{V},J\mathsf{V})\right\rangle}{\left\|A\right\|^{2}-\left\|a\right\|^{2}}.

Taking into account
\left\|A\right\|^{2}-\left\|a\right\|^{2}=2e^{u+v}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\mathsf{V}\right\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\left\|\nabla\II\right\|_{\infty}(\left\|A\right\|+\left\|a\right\|)}{2e^{u+v}}.

Corollary
3.13
asserts that
\nabla\II
is exponentially decaying. We also control
e^{u+v}
(which tends to
\frac{1}{4}
for large
\rho
) and by equation (
4.1
), we have
\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\leqslant\left\|A\right\|\leqslant\left\|A\right\|+\left\|a\right\|\leqslant 2
, which suffices to conclude that
\nabla\mathsf{V}
is exponentially decaying.
∎

From the previous lemma, Corollary
3.13
(exponential decay of
\left\|\nabla\II\right\|
) and the fact that the image of
\gamma
is contained in the ball
B(x_{0},\frac{R}{2})
(thus remains at distance at least
\frac{R}{2}
from
D^{k}
), we deduces that there exist constants
R_{0},C,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
(depending only on
k
in Definition
3.7
) such that if
R\geqslant R_{0}
, then we have at the point
\gamma(t)
(for any
t
) :

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{\Nor}A\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\nabla^{\Nor}(\II(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{V}))\right\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|\nabla\II\right\|+2\left\|\II\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\nabla^{\Tan}\mathsf{V}\right\|

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leqslant Ce^{-\alpha R}.

We now turn to the control of

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\left\langle\pi(A),\pi(A)\right\rangle}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=2\left\langle\pi(B(\dot{\gamma},A)),\pi(A)\right\rangle+2\left\langle\pi(\nabla^{\Nor}_{\dot{\gamma}}A),\pi(A)\right\rangle.

By Theorem
1.23
and the correspondence between
\II
and
B
, we have
\left\|B\right\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
. As moreover
\left\|\dot{\gamma}\right\|=1
, inequality (
4.2
) gives:

\left|\left\langle\pi(B(\dot{\gamma},A)),\pi(A)\right\rangle\right|\leqslant\theta_{\Tan}\theta_{A}\left\|A\right\|^{2}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\pi(\nabla^{\Nor}_{\dot{\gamma}}A),\pi(A)\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\theta_{A}\theta_{a}\left\|\nabla^{\Nor}_{\dot{\gamma}}A\right\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\theta_{A}\theta_{a}Ce^{-\alpha R}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}\left\langle\pi(A),\pi(A)\right\rangle}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\theta_{A}[\theta_{\Tan}\left\|A\right\|+\theta_{a}Ce^{-\alpha R}].

Finally control
\theta_{A}
: by definition,
\theta_{A}^{2}=\frac{\left\langle\pi(A),\pi(A)\right\rangle}{\left\langle A,A\right\rangle}
, thus

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}(\theta_{A}^{2})}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left\|A\right\|^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\left\langle\pi(A),\pi(A)\right\rangle}{\mathrm{d}t}-2\left\langle\pi(A),\pi(A)\right\rangle\frac{\left\langle\nabla^{\Nor}A,A\right\rangle}{\left\|A\right\|^{4}}.

On the ball

, we have seen (equation (

)) that we have
\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\leqslant\left\|A\right\|\leqslant 1
, thus
\frac{\left\langle\nabla^{\Nor}A,A\right\rangle}{\left\|A\right\|^{4}}
is exponentially decaying by (
4.5
). Equation (
4.6
) controls the first term, which gives the expected control on
\theta_{A}
. This concludes the proof of Lemma
4.4
.
∎

We now deduce an upper bound on the angles
\theta_{A}
. Let
u=\theta_{\gamma}+\theta_{\Tan}+\theta_{A}
,
\varepsilon=2Ce^{-\alpha R}
(which should be thought of as close to

Lemma 4.6
.

There exists a constant
R_{0}
(depending only on
k
in Definition
3.7
) such that if

, then for any

u(t)\leqslant\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{e^{2dt}-1}{\frac{1}{1-d}-\frac{e^{2dt}}{1+d}}.

Proof.

Lemma
4.1
(which expresses
\left\|A\right\|\leqslant 1
and
\left\|a\right\|
in terms of the
\mu_{k}
) and Proposition
3.11
(which states that the
\mu_{k}
decay exponentially) show that
\left\|a\right\|
decays exponentially: on the ball
B(x_{0},\frac{R}{2})
, we have

\left\|a\right\|\leqslant Ce^{-\alpha R}.

Take
R_{0}
large, so that for
R\geqslant R_{0}
we have
\varepsilon<1
. The previous lemma (Lemma
4.4
) then allows controlling the derivative of
u
:

\left|u^{\prime}\right|\leqslant 2u+\varepsilon\theta_{a}.

Note furthermore that by Lemma
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle(1+\theta_{a})\left|1-\theta_{a}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|1-\theta_{a}^{2}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\theta_{\Tan}^{2}-\theta_{\gamma}^{2}-\theta_{A}^{2}\right|

thus as
1+\theta_{a}\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\leqslant u^{2}

\displaystyle\left|1-\theta_{a}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant u^{2}.

We finally obtains an inequality only in terms of
u
:
\left|u^{\prime}\right|\leqslant 2u+\varepsilon(1+u^{2})
. Since
u(0)=0
,
u
is bounded, for
t\geqslant 0
, by the function
u_{m}
satisfying:

u_{m}(0)=0,\quad u_{m}^{\prime}=2u_{m}+\varepsilon(1+u_{m}^{2}).

This is a Riccati differential equation, which we now recall how to solve.
v=\varepsilon u_{m}
satisfies
v^{\prime}=2v+\varepsilon^{2}+v^{2}
. Chooses a function
\phi
such that
v=-\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{\phi}
. We obtain
v^{\prime}=-\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}+v^{2}
, so that

\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi}=v^{2}-v^{\prime}=-2v+\varepsilon^{2}=2\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{\phi}+\varepsilon^{2},

which gives a linear differential equation:

\phi^{\prime\prime}-2\phi^{\prime}+\varepsilon^{2}\phi=0.

The solutions of this equation are given by the functions

\phi:t\mapsto\lambda e^{(1+d)t}+\mu e^{(1-d)t},

for all real
\lambda
and
\mu
. Taking into account the condition

, one can express

explicitly. The inequality
\varepsilon u\leqslant v
then rewrites as the inequality of Lemma
4.6
.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

\overline{\mathbf{V}}
is exponentially decaying.

Proof.

Given Definition
3.7
, we must show that there exist constants
R_{0},C,\alpha
(depending only on
k
in Definition

B(x,R)
not intersecting

large. The vector

a_{x}
; for any real

, let
a^{1}_{s}=\sin(s)\,x+\cos(s)\,a^{1}_{0}

\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{2,2}

E_{s}=\{y\in\widehat{\mathbb{H}}^{2,2}\mid\left\langle y,a^{1}_{s}\right\rangle\leqslant 0\}.

We shall show (step

) that for a suitably chosen

(roughly equal to

for some

), all

at distance

E_{s_{1}}\cap E_{\pi-s_{1}}
, for some

verifying

We shall then show (step
4.1
) that for a suitably chosen
s_{2}>0
(roughly equal to
e^{-\alpha^{\prime}R}
for some
\alpha^{\prime}>0
), all
y\in S
at distance

from
x
lie in
E_{s_{2}}\cap E_{\pi-s_{2}}

s\mapsto E_{s}\cap E_{\pi-s}
is increasing for inclusion (for

we have

\displaystyle y\in E_{s}\cap E_{\pi-s}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left\langle y,\pm\cos(s)\,a^{1}_{0}+\sin(s)\,x\right\rangle\leqslant 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle y,a^{1}_{0}\right\rangle\right|\leqslant-\tan(s)\left\langle y,x\right\rangle,

and this condition indeed becomes less restrictive as
s
increases. Combining steps
4.1
and
4.1
and setting

S\subseteq E_{s}\cap E_{\pi-s}.

The convex hull
\Conv(S)
is hence also contained in the intersection of half-spaces
E_{s}\cap E_{\pi-s}
. Let now a unit normal vector
\mathsf{n}\in\Nor_{x}S
and
d\geqslant 0
satisfying

(hence
\exp_{x}(d\mathsf{n})\in\Conv(S)
, see Definition
3.7
). By definition of

, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\langle\exp_{x}(d\mathsf{n}),a^{1}_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\cos(d)\,x+\sin(d)\,n,\cos(s)\,a^{1}_{0}+\sin(s)\,x\right\rangle

thus

\displaystyle=-\cos(d)\sin(s)+\sin(d)\cos(s)\left\langle\mathsf{n},a^{1}_{0}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathsf{n},a^{1}_{0}\right\rangle\tan(d)\leqslant\tan(s).

Replacing
a^{1}_{s}
by
a^{1}_{\pi-s}
, we similarly obtain :

\left\langle\mathsf{n},a^{1}_{0}\right\rangle\tan(d)\leqslant-\tan(s).

The two preceding inequalities show that if we write

then we have

. Moreover, since

is contained in the ball centred at

\left|u_{1}\right|\leqslant\frac{\pi}{2}
: thus

\overline{\mathbf{V}}_{S}(x)=\Vol\Nor_{x}^{0}S\leqslant\frac{\pi}{2}s.

Since we have an inequality of the type
s=\max(s_{1},s_{2})\leqslant Ce^{-\alpha R}
, this concludes the proof of Proposition
4.7
.

(with

. Let us therefore find such

. We can thus assume

\displaystyle\left\langle a^{1}_{s},y\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\cos(s)\left\langle\frac{JA_{x}}{\left\|A_{x}\right\|},y\right\rangle+\sin(s)\left\langle x,y\right\rangle.

of

Definition 4.8

Proof.

is a closed subset of
U
. The
\cal{I}_{k}
being countable,
\pi(\cal{I})
also is. This countable closed set is the set of
t\in U
such that
\gamma_{t}
has a self-intersection at a focal point. We now replace
U
by
U\setminus\pi(\cal{I})
, which is still an open subset of
\mathbb{R}
with negligible complement.

Step (iv) : Non-transverse intersections.

It remains to show that for generic
t\in U
,
\gamma_{t}
self-intersects transversely. We have already eliminated the case of a self-intersection at focal points. As
\gamma_{t}
is
\cal{C}^{\infty}
and the normal vector to
\gamma_{t}
at
\gamma(x,t)
is
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\exp(s\mathsf{n}_{x})
, it suffices to show that for generic
t
, one cannot find distinct
x,y\in\partial D
such that
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\exp(s\mathsf{n}_{x})\big|_{t}=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\exp(s\mathsf{n}_{y})\big|_{t}
(in other words, such that
\gamma_{t}
self-intersects non-transversely at
\gamma(x,t)
and
\gamma(y,t)
). Let us consider

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}G:&(\partial D)^{(2)}\times\mathbb{R}&\longrightarrow&S^{2},\\
&(x,y,t)&\longmapsto&(\gamma(x,t),\gamma(y,t)).\end{array}

As in the previous step, we consider the submanifold
\Delta=\{(x,x)\mid x\in S\}\subseteq S^{2}
(of codimension 2) and we verify that for
(x,y,t)\in G^{-1}(\Delta)
, the image of
\mathrm{d}G_{(x,y,t)}
intersects
\Tan\Delta
transversely. Indeed, thanks to the previous step, neither
(x,t)
nor
(y,t)
is a focal point, so that
G^{-1}(\Delta)
is a submanifold
V
(of dimension
1
) of
(\partial D)^{(2)}\times\mathbb{R}
, which does not accumulate on
\Delta\times U
(as in the previous step) thus is even a submanifold of
(\partial D)^{2}\times U
. The map
\pi_{t}:(\partial D)^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},(x,y,t)\mapsto t
restricts to
V
and we verify that its critical points are the
(x,y,t)
such that
t\in U
and
\gamma_{t}
has a non-transverse intersection at
\gamma(x,t)=\gamma(y,t)
. The set of its critical values, as previously, is a measure zero closed subset of
U
, which we subtract from
U
.

We obtain that for
t\in U
,
\gamma_{t}
has only non-degenerate focal points, no intersections at focal points and only transverse intersections. One can moreover show (as previously) that the set of
t\in U
such that
\gamma_{t}
self-intersects at three points is discrete ; we subtract this set from
U
.

For all
t
in
U
,
\partial D_{t}
therefore satisfies the properties of Definition
B.1
. This shows that
D_{t}
has piecewise
\cal{C}^{\infty}
boundary, thus completes the proof of Proposition
B.2
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
B.3
.

Given a relatively compact open subset
D
of
S
with
\cal{C}^{\infty}
boundary, we have defined the family
(\theta_{i})_{i\in I}
,
-\pi<\theta_{i}<\pi
, of its exterior angles (see Definition
B.1
). At every smooth point of a regular path used to parametrise the boundary of
D
, we can define the geodesic curvature
k
of
\partial D
.

Fact B.4

(Gauß-bonnet formula with angles, see
[
GHL04
]
)
.

We have
\sum_{I}\theta_{i}+\int_{\partial D}k=2\pi\chi(D)-\int_{D}K
.

Remark B.5
.

Note that
t\mapsto\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\Vol\{x\in S\mid\mathrm{d}^{S}(x,\partial D_{t})\leqslant\varepsilon\}}{2\varepsilon}
, called Minkowski content of
\partial D_{t}
, extends
t\mapsto\Vol(\partial D_{t})
to
\mathbb{R}
.

The function which to
t
in
U

\Vol(\partial D_{t})

with
(\theta_{i})_{i\in I_{t}}
the exterior angles of
D_{t}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Vol(\partial D_{s})}{\mathrm{d}s}\bigg|_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{I_{t}}\theta_{i}+\int_{\partial D_{t}}k

\displaystyle=2\pi\chi(\overline{D_{t}})-\int_{D}K

since

Moreover, the function

.
