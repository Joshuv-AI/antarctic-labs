3. Executive Summary

 Solar@Scale addresses large-scale solar soft costs by developing tools and
 resources related to planning, siting, permitting and inspection in support of local
 governments, special districts, and other authorities that have jurisdiction over large-
 scale solar projects.
 Solar photovoltaic (PV) deployment in the United States has grown rapidly, by an
 average of 28% each year over the past 10 years (Solar Energy Industry Association
 (SEIA), 2025). This means an increasing number of communities are managing a
 new type of development, often without existing planning, zoning, or permitting
 experience specific to large-scale solar and the challenges and opportunities that it
 presents. For many communities, large-scale solar can offer benefits and enhance
 other community goals related to economic development, energy resilience, and
 affordability. Solar@Scale focuses on developing strategies and improving local
 decision-making to maximize local benefits from large-scale solar development and
 manage trade-offs.
 To help communities better prepare and improve local outcomes related to this
 development, International City/County Management Association (ICMA), in
 partnership with American Planning Association (APA), implemented the
 Solar@Scale program to develop and share actionable practices, tools, and
 decision-making criteria to stakeholders in the siting, permitting, inspection, and
 realization of large-scale solar. Solar@Scale convened a national advisory
 committee comprised of stakeholders representing local governments, solar industry
 experts, planners, and related stakeholders to develop the Solar@Scale Guidebook,
 a comprehensive resource for local governments related to large-scale solar.
 Supplementary conference sessions, workshops, webinars and resources were
 Page 2 of 27

 developed to support local governments in their work to integrate large-scale solar
 energy into their plans and procedures and ultimately work toward projects that
 delivered greater local benefits.
 Throughout the program’s duration (August 2020 – July 2025), workshops,
 conference sessions, webinars, other programming, resources, and best practices
 were delivered to 3,677 local government staff. The Guidebook has been
 downloaded 2,900 times across three editions and is considered a leading resource
 for local governments on this topic. In the last two quarters of the project alone, 736
 professionals attended a Solar@Scale webinar. User feedback demonstrates the
 high value of content and demand for this content continues to grow as an
 increasing number of local governments are navigating large-scale solar
 development. Resources continue to be publicly available on the Solar@Scale
 webpage hosted by ICMA.

5. Background

 Solar@Scale has been an important and timely initiative to improve outcomes of
 large-scale solar development for local governments and their residents. Over the
 last 10 years, solar deployment has grown by an average of 28% annually, with
 most of this growth in utility, commercial, and community solar (Solar Energy
 Industry Association (SEIA), 2025). However, there has also been growing
 uncertainty about, or opposition to, these types of developments. Given the size of

 Page 3 of 27

these developments and the land required, former farmland and land in rural areas
are often siting candidates. Rural governments, in particular, may not have the
necessary staff or existing planning and zoning documents to manage new
developments of this type (Daniels and Wagner, 2022). Additionally, rural residents
may hold a perception of bearing a higher burden than urban energy users, and that
hosting solar installations in their community may not come with commensurate
compensation (Nilson and Stedman, 2023). Research also shows an increase in
moratoria and other efforts to slow down these developments as local communities
get their bearings with these new demands on local land (Eisenson et al., 2025;
Weise and Bhat, 2024).
Large-scale solar development does, however, offer a myriad of potential benefits to
local communities including opportunities to generate tax revenue or lease land,
redevelop brownfield sites or other underutilized properties, improve grid reliability
and reduce or stabilize local energy costs. Local governments can proactively
engage with solar developers to ensure that projects maximize local benefits and
mitigate concerns across a range of issues (Eisenson and Webb, 2023). Local
governments can also encourage and support “dual use” projects including
ecovoltaics and agrivoltaics. There is a growing body of work demonstrating the
potential for these practices and “the opportunity to harvest the sun twice” via solar
panels and co-located vegetation (U.S. Department of Energy, N.D.).
To help communities realize solar benefits and manage trade-offs, Solar@Scale
drew heavily on the resources and tools developed by organizations across the
country to distill a national-scale guide and trainings for local governments to
improve their planning and zoning ahead of large-scale solar development in their
area. To leverage the best available research and emerging knowledge, ICMA and
APA convened a national advisory committee to shape and inform the development
of the guide. This advisory committee included prominent researchers on this topic,
including representing from the Pace Land Use Law Center and National Renewable
Energy Laboratory, as well as practitioners working in local government, private
sector consulting, and solar industry representatives, all with direct knowledge and
experience “on the ground’ in planning and developing large-scale solar projects.
The guidebook formed the basis of the materials distributed through workshops,
webinars, trainings, presentations, and blogs and other publications, and the
practice of getting research and case studies to local governments was a hallmark of
Solar@Scale’s work throughout the program.
Solar@Scale provided a unique and important resource that did not previously exist:
a national guidebook specifically designed for local government leaders. The
guidebook drew on the best available resources to create a comprehensive resource
that supports practitioners to make decisions about policies and processes related to
large-scale solar permitting and implementation. The guidebook provides specific
use cases and information designed to help users take concrete steps toward policy
or programmatic goals.
Solar@Scale is complementary to other solar and energy programs, including the
following:

 Page 4 of 27

 • The SolSmart program offers free technical assistance to local governments
 to adopt solar development best practices – with an emphasis on residential
 solar but also including larger scale solar – and receive recognition of their
 achievements via designation on one of four levels: bronze, silver, gold, or
 platinum (SolSmart, 2025).
 • U.S. EPA’s Re-Powering America’s Land initiative provides resources and
 examples of renewable energy development on landfills, former mine sites,
 and other contaminated properties (United States Environmental Protection
          Agency (EPA), 2025).
 • U.S. DOE’s Reliable Energy Siting through Technical Engagement and
 Planning (R-STEP) program aims to ‘expand the decision-making capacity
 and expertise of state and local governments around large-scale renewable
 energy planning, siting, and permitting.’ This is pursued through state-level
 programs that act as resources for the local governments in that state (United
 States Department of Energy (DOE), 2025).
 • A growing number of resources that help local and state leaders work with the
 community to determine ideal outcomes from solar developments, like
 NYSERDA’s resources on clean energy siting, which cover a range of project
 types, scales, and resource types (New York State Energy Research and
 Development Authority (NYSERDA), 2025), and research institutions like the
 Graham Sustainability Institute’s growing body of work (Bessette and White,
 2024).

 Solar@Scale is an important addition to this universe of programming as it provides
 high-level resources applicable to many locations, with a national distribution model
 and specific focus on large-scale solar.

6. Project Objectives
 Through Solar@Scale, ICMA, in partnership with APA, developed and shared
 actionable practices, tools, and strategies to support local government stakeholders
 in the siting, permitting, inspection, and development of large-scale solar. The
 following describes the specific program goals and objectives and summarizes the
 tasks and milestones for the duration of the project:

 Project Goals, Outcomes, and Innovations
 Solar@Scale sought to reduce large-scale solar soft costs by bringing together
 public and private sector stakeholders to identify best practices for local
 governments, special districts, and other authorities that have jurisdiction to install
 large-scale solar projects. The program developed tools and resources for
 procedures, analysis, and communications related to permitting, zoning, regulations,
 and planning for these installations, especially on public and municipal lands,
 brownfields, and in rural areas. This newly developed content was disseminated

 Page 5 of 27

through workshops, trainings, conference sessions, webinars, and other
programming.
Solar@Scale served communities of all sizes across the U.S. by providing access to
best practices and training to support improved implementation of large-scale solar
energy facilities. This served dual purposes. First, Solar@Scale provided local
governments with the ability to integrate large-scale solar energy into their plans and
procedures and facilitate and improve existing processes. Second, it provided
relevant stakeholders with better guidance, opportunities, and options.

7. Project Results and Discussion
 The Solar@Scale project sought to improve outcomes of large-scale solar
 development for local governments. The final achievements of the Program
 included:
 • A National Solar@Scale steering committee was convened and met regularly
 over the course of four years. This steering committee shaped and informed
 the development of the Solar@Scale Guidebook and related resources.
 • The Solar@Scale Guidebook was developed and updated in three
 subsequent editions. It has been downloaded at least 2,900 times and is
 considered a “go to” resource for local government leaders trying to improve
 local planning, permitting and project development in their communities.
 • Solar@Scale trained 3,677 local government officials, including nine
 workshops and nine conference presentations.
 • 105 local governments attested to the value of the program through
 testimonials, pledges, and letters of support.

 The Solar@Scale project achieved all intended outcomes and deliverables. The
 original project timeline had to be adjusted due to the COVID-19 pandemic and the

 challenges of conducting in-person meetings and workshops, which were common
 deliverables in the Solar@Scale SOPO. The project team worked with DOE to
 develop a modified approach and timeline that ensured we met the project goals and
 objectives while recognizing the challenges facing our target audience.

8. Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions
  The Solar@Scale program succeeded in educating local government professionals
  on opportunities to plan for and realize the potential benefits of large-scale solar
  development. The project sought to educate at least 1,200 participants, and
  ultimately reach 3,677 professionals through conference presentations, workshops,
  webinars, guidebook downloads, and other resource delivery methods.
  Solar@Scale reached professionals working in a wide variety of geographic areas,
  including areas perceived as less than solar-friendly. The demand for this knowledge
  and these resources has only grown since the program began. This is evidenced by
  the extreme growth in webinar interest and attendance. Webinars in the final
  quarters of the project consistently had 200+ attendees. There is a large and
  growing appetite for these resources as an increasing number of local governments
  are experiencing large-scale solar development.
  The COVID-19 pandemic affected the duration of this project: originally, the project
  was planned to be three, 12-month budget periods, with a large proportion of the
  tasks in the form of in-person trainings and events. The project remained three
  budget periods, but the duration of these budget periods was adjusted given needed
  project changes. This change proved advantageous for the program and allowed
  Solar@Scale to reach a greater number of local governments and stakeholders
  through our virtual convenings and extended project timeline.

9. Path Forward
 ICMA and APA will continue to support best practices in energy planning and
 development. There are no immediate plans for continuation of the Solar@Scale
 program, despite the high demand for resources in this area. Additional funding for
 this work would be essential for continued success.
 It's clear local governments continue to need support around all types of energy
 infrastructure planning and siting, especially as electricity demand continues to
 increase in the United States (U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), 2025).
 Communities are often faced with inquiries from energy companies before they have
 had a chance to prepare their planning, zoning, permitting, fee schedule, and other
 important development procedures. Programs like Solar@Scale help communities
 do this work preemptively, so they are ready when there is development pressure in
 their community. When prepared, communities can better act as partners and work
 with developers to agree upon project conditions that work for both parties.
 ICMA has a long history of being a trusted messenger for local governments and
 their needs, goals, and interests, and the organization will continue this work across
 the range of topics in our portfolio (International City/County Management
 Association (ICMA), 2025). Ideally, this would also include energy development, as
 communities are regularly faced with development opportunities, and benefit greatly
 from reliable resources and best practices.
