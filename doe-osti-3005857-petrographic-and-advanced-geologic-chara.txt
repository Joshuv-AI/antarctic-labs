EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The One Earth Energy #1 (OEE1, API 1211325373) well was drilled to a depth of 7,099 feet from
the Pennsylvanian bedrock to the Precambrian granite. In total, 99 thin sections were taken from
Rotary Sidewall Core (RSWC) from 2,275 feet to 6,903 feet; 59 thin sections were taken from
Whole Core (WC) from 4,311.5 to 6,519.2 feet for this report.
This report details specifically thin section point-counting analysis that includes mineralogical and
pore space analysis, including grain size analysis annotated thin section photomicrographs,
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) with energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), and
statistics of grain size analysis on Mount Simon thin sections from OEE1. Characterized units
include the St. Peter Sandstone, Eminence Formation, Potosi Dolomite, Franconia Formation,
Davis Member, Ironton Sandstone, Galesville Sandstone, Eau Claire Formation, Elmhurst
Sandstone, Mt. Simon Sandstone, and Argenta Formation.

METHODOLOGY
All 158 thin sections were digitized by Zeiss Axioscan under 10x objective lens with one channel
of Plain Polarized Light (PPL), six channels of Cross Polarized Light (XPL) at 0°, 15°, 30°, 45°,
60°, and 75°, and one channel of Circular Polarized Light (CPL). Point counting analysis was
performed on 9 thin sections of St. Peter Sandstone, with a Petrog automated stage installed on a
Zeiss AxioImager Mkm.2 microscope. Each slide was analyzed with 400 points for mineralogy
and 100 points for texture analysis. scanning electron microscopy (SEM) analysis was conducted
using a Hitachi Flex SEM SU1000 equipped with a Brucker SCU X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) unit.

PETROGRAPHIC DESCRIPTIONS
St. Peter Sandstone
Formation Depth: 2,053 – 2,217 feet
Description: The St. Peter Sandstone is 164 feet thick in OEE1. Only one thin section was taken
from 2,275 feet. The thin section is very homogenous, with medium grained, well sorted, quartz
arenite. The authigenic clay is mostly illite and makes up to less than 3% of the total volume. The
St. Peter Sandstone at this depth shows high porosity (> 10%) and good conductivity between
pores. There is very limited quartz overgrowth on the grains. Some clusters of quartz show
evidence of compaction at earlier stage, and the oversized pore space is likely secondary porosity
from the dissolution of feldspar minerals.

Eminence Formation
Formation Depth: 3,090 – 3,326 feet
Description: The Eminence Formation is 236 feet thick in OEE1, and two thin sections were taken
from 3,219.88 feet and 3,270 feet. At 3,219.88 feet, two layers of terrigenous clays crosscut the
dolomite, and subangular to sub-rounded fine quartz grains suspended in the lime mud are
clustered at the corner (Figure 1.b). The thin section at 3,270 feet is a homogenous dolomitic
mudstone with 20% to 30% subrounded quartz grains suspended in the lime mud (Figure 1.c).

Potosi Dolomite
Formation Depth: 3,326 – 3,468 feet
Description: The Potosi Dolomite is 142 feet thick in OEE1, and two thin sections were taken from
3,382 feet and 3,420 feet. The dolomite at 3,382 feet is bimodal with fine and coarse dolomite
grains (Figure 2.a), and dolomite at 3,420 feet is vuggy (Figure 2.b). The infill of chalcedony and
authigenic quartz in the vug shows multiple stages of diagenetic alteration at this depth.

Franconia Formation
Formation Depth: 3,468 – 3,674 feet
Description: The Franconia Formation is 206 feet thick in OEE1, and three thin sections were

Davis Member
Formation Depth: 3,674 to 3,732 feet
Description: The Davis Member is 58 feet thick in OEE1, and five thin sections were taken from
this formation. From the thin sections made, the formation appears to be fining upward. The
limestone taken from the top of the formation (3,694 feet) has up to 10% plagioclase, 5% hematite,
and 2% glauconite (Figure 4.a).
The bottom of the Davis Member (3,725 feet) is mostly siliceous, with limestone lenses and
globular siliceous clusters interbedded with detrital clays (Figure 4.b). The siliceous part is arkose
sandstone with 40% quartz, 30% feldspar, and 15% glauconite (Figure 4.c). Porosity through the
formation is less than 2%.

Ironton Sandstone
Formation Depth: 3,732 to 3,853 feet
Description: The Ironton Sandstone is 121 feet thick in OEE1, and 15 thin sections were taken
from this formation. Ironton Sandstone is heterogeneous from lithology to sorting. The top of the
formation is fine to medium grained quartz arenite with <5 % feldspar and >10% porosity. 3,749
to 3,767 are cemented by carbonate, with that part of 3,759 argillaceous dolomite at 3,759 (Figure
5.a). A couple feet below the interval from 3,749 to 3,767, it is interbedded with poorly sorted to
very well sorted quartz arenite, and argillaceous sandstone (Figure 5.b). At the bottom of the
formation, the boundary between the Ironton and Galesville sandstones, is poorly sorted,
argillaceous sandstone with a ferroan rich clay (Figure 5.c).

Eau Claire Formation
Formation Depth : 3,908 – 4,368 feet
Description: The Eau Claire Formation is 460 feet thick in OEE1, and 11 thin sections were taken

Most of the Eau Claire Formation in OEE1 is dolomitic sandstone (Figure 7.a), and argillaceous

Elmhurst Sandstone
Formation Depth: 4,368 – 4,455 feet
Description: The Elmhurst Sandstone is 87 feet thick in OEE1, and four thin sections were taken
from this formation. The top of the thin section has heterogenous bedding of dolomitic siltstone,
arkose siltstone and fossiliferous limestone, like the Eau Claire formation at 4,366 feet, and the
bottom of the Elmhurst Sandstone is coarse, well sorted, quartz arenite. The authigenic quartz on
each grain completely covers the original grain and makes them euhedral. Bioclasts are commonly
seen at this depth (4,442 feet) (Figure 8).

Mt. Simon Sandstone
Formation Depth: 4,455 to 6,469 feet
Description: The Mt. Simon Sandstone is 2,014 feet thick in OEE1, and 92 thin sections were
taken from this formation. Mt. Simon Sandstone is further broken down into Upper (4,455 to 5,222
feet), Middle (5,222 to 5,969 feet), Lower (5,969 to 6,262 feet), and Arkose (6,262 to 6,469 feet)
sections.
Mt. Simon formation, which serves as the injection zone, is of particular interest in this report.
Point counting is a quantitative tool used to objectively analyze the crucial properties of
mineralogy and texture of each thin section. Point counting analysis has been done on fifty-eight
thin sections taken from Mt. Simon from 4,407.98 to 6,465.98 feet (

Table , Table ).
For quality assurance, the point counting porosity result is compared to that of the routine analysis,
and found the trend closely matches (Figure 9). Quantitative textural analysis combined with point
counting indicates that the ranges between the 1st and 3rd quartile major axis sizes show the Mt.
Simon samples collected from OEE1 are overall moderately sorted, with a few poorly sorted
outliers, particularly those from the lower portions of the Upper and Middle Mt. Simon formations
(Figure 10). More than 90% of these samples have a mean major axis smaller than 0.5 mm,
classifying them as fine to medium sand. (Figure 10 & Table 2).
The Upper Mt. Simon is quartz arenite to subarkose sandstone (Error! Reference source not
found..a). It has the highest total porosity and oversized porosity of all the sections of Mt. Simon
up to 15.7% (4,885.11 feet). A typical Upper Mt. Simon sandstone is moderately sorted, sub-
rounded, has around 5-10% feldspar, and porosity >10%. Pore spaces are interconnected. Quartz
overgrowth is commonly seen at the top of the section, with most of the sections having less than
10% authigenic quartz.
The Middle Mt. Simon is entirely quartz arenite. It has the highest partial dissolution porosity of
all Mt. Simon sections. These partial dissolution pores are commonly seen in lithic granitic pieces
(Error! Reference source not found..b), where the feldspar part of the grain is dissolved, leaving
the quartz and oxide part of it behind (Error! Reference source not found..c). Middle Mt. Simon
is coarser and less sorted compared to the Upper Mt. Simon. The seven thin sections point counted
for Middle Mt. Simon are all quartz arenites.
One of the thin sections found in Middle Mt. Simon at 5,530.08 feet has a quartz grain with long
fibrous mineral inclusions inside the quartz grain. These black colored minerals are growing in
bundles and were thought to be ilmenite (Error! Reference source not found..d). However, SEM
and EDS analysis were not able to confirm the mineralogy of this inclusion (Error! Reference
source not found..e), likely due to the nature of how the mineral was well sealed by the quartz
and SEM can only give us information of the surface of the mineral.
The Lower Mt. Simon has very limited primary porosity. Its overall porosity is comparable to any
of the sections above. The dominant porosity type here is the oversized porosity, the type of
porosity that is over 3rd percentile of the grain size at given depth (Error! Reference source not
found..f). This type of porosity is the result of total grain dissolution, and it differs from moldic
porosity in that oversized porosity provides good conductivity, thus high permeability for the
reservoir rock.
The arkose is 207 feet thick in OEE1 as determined by wire logging and core analysis. It was
previously categorized as part of the Lower Mt. Simon. Figure 11 and Figure 12 show that this
section has a distinctive lithology and texture from the Lower Mt. Simon, which is corroborated
by routine analysis that shows couple 0 porosity at multiple depths within this section.
As the name suggests, sandstone from this section/formation can be very arkosic, that it could have
up to 38% of feldspar content. However, the amount of feldspar varies greatly inside this formation
(Figure 12, Figure 14.a, b the yellow color is from the staining showing the feldspar).
Most of the porosity in Arkose is secondary porosity, likely correlated to its high feldspar content.
In the Arkose section, most porosity is secondary, likely resulting from the high feldspar content.
In some instances, these pores are partially or completely cemented by authigenic alkali feldspar
(Figure 14 c, d, e).

Argenta Formation
Formation Depth: 6,469 to 6,918 feet
Description: Argenta Formation is 449 feet thick in OEE1, and eighteen thin sections were taken
from this formation. Argenta is a recently recognized Cambrian unit (Freiburg, 2015). The Argenta
section is distinct from the Arkose section because it has very little to no feldspar. Quartz and clays
(detrital and authigenic) made up this unit. Most of the Argenta rock is quartz arenite, with very
little amount of argillaceous sandstone. Quartz grains in this unit are angular to subangular, poorly
sorted from fine to very coarse (Figure 15.a). The top of the section has less detrital clay, and more
feldspar (<5%, Figure 15b, red arrows). In some depth, the intergranular pore space is sealed by
fine crystalline authigenic quartz (Figure 15c, red arrows). The lower part of the section has more
clays (detrital and authigenic, Figure 15d) and less feldspar grains.
