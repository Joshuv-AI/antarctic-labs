A Zalcman–Pang type rescaling result for φ \varphi -normal harmonic mappings and applications Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author: Nikhil Bharti
Kuldeep Singh Charak, Pratiksha, Nikhil Bharti ∗

Abstract
. We investigate normality and φ \varphi -normality criteria for harmonic mappings in the unit disk. A new sufficient condition for normality involving extended spherical derivatives is established. We further prove a Zalcman–Pang type rescaling lemma for φ \varphi -normal harmonic mappings and derive new φ \varphi -normality criteria as applications. In addition, we introduce φ \varphi -normal families of harmonic mappings and obtain corresponding Lappan-type characterizations. In particular, we show that for sense-preserving harmonic mappings the relevant test set may be taken to consist of only three points.

1.
Introduction and main results

The theory of normal functions and normal families occupies a central position in
geometric function theory and complex analysis. Originating in the work of Montel

[
17
]
, normality has become one of the most fundamental compactness principles in
the study of analytic and meromorphic functions. A family of meromorphic functions
defined in a domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is said to be
normal
if every sequence in the
family contains a subsequence which converges locally uniformly, with respect to the
spherical metric, to a meromorphic function or to the point at infinity. One of the most
important characterizations of normality is Marty’s theorem
[
16
, Theorem 4]
, which
asserts that a family of meromorphic functions is normal if and only if the corresponding
spherical derivatives are locally uniformly bounded. Recall that the spherical derivative
of a meromorphic function
f
is

(1)

f^{\#}(z)=\frac{|f^{\prime}(z)|}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}.

Closely related to Marty’s criterion is the notion of a
normal function
, introduced
implicitly by Yosida
[
19
]
and Noshiro
[
18
]
, and later formalized by
Lehto and Virtanen
[
13
]
. A meromorphic function on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
is
called normal if its post-composition with conformal automorphisms of
\mathbb{D}
forms a normal
family. This admits the following equivalent quantitative characterization.

Definition 1.1
.

A meromorphic function
f
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
is called
normal
if

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\,(1-|z|^{2})\,f^{\#}(z)<\infty.

In recent decades considerable efforts have been devoted to extending classical results from
analytic and meromorphic function theory to harmonic mappings driven by the enormous applicability potential of harmonic mappings (see
[
1
,
8
]
). Recall that a complex-valued harmonic mapping defined in a simply connected domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
admits a canonical
decomposition
f=h+\bar{g}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic in
D
(see
[
10
]
).
Throughout this paper we use the decomposition
f=h+\bar{g}
.

Motivated by the work of Colonna on Bloch harmonic mappings
[
7
]
, Arbeláez,
Hernández and Sierra
[
2
]
introduced the notion of normality for harmonic mappings.

Definition 1.2
.

A harmonic mapping
f=h+\bar{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
is said to be
normal
in
\mathbb{D}
if it satisfies
the Lipschitz-type condition

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,w\in\mathbb{D}\\
z\neq w\end{subarray}}\frac{\chi(f(z),f(w))}{\rho_{h}(z,w)}<\infty,

where
\chi(f(z),f(w))
is the chordal distance, i.e. the Euclidean distance between the
stereographic projections of
f(z)
and
f(w)
on the Riemann sphere,

\chi(f(z),f(w))=\begin{cases}\dfrac{|f(z)-f(w)|}{\sqrt{1+|f(z)|^{2}}\,\sqrt{1+|f(w)|^{2}}},&f(z),f(w)\in\mathbb{C},\\[9.47217pt]
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+|f(z)|^{2}}},&f(w)=\infty,\\[9.47217pt]
0,&f(z)=f(w)=\infty,\end{cases}

and
\rho_{h}(z,w)
is the hyperbolic distance between
z
and
w
,

\rho_{h}(z,w)=\frac{1}{2}\log\!\left(\frac{1+t}{1-t}\right),\qquad t=\left|\frac{z-w}{1-\bar{w}z}\right|.

The following equivalent characterization is often more convenient.

Definition 1.3

(
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

A harmonic mapping
f=h+\bar{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
is
normal
in
\mathbb{D}
if

(2)

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\,(1-|z|^{2})\,\frac{|h^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}<\infty.

The theory of normal harmonic mappings has attracted significant attention. Deng,
Ponnusamy and Qiao
[
9
]
established harmonic analogues of several classical theorems
on normal meromorphic functions, including versions of Marty’s theorem and the
Lohwater–Pommerenke rescaling theorem. Bharti and Thin
[
4
]
established a
Zalcman–Pang type rescaling result in the same setting. Following
[
4
]
, the

spherical derivative
of a harmonic mapping
f=h+\bar{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
is

f^{\#}(z)=\frac{|h^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|}{1+|f(z)|^{2}},

and, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the
extended spherical derivative of order
k
is

(3)

f^{\#(k)}(z):=\frac{|h^{(k)}(z)|+|g^{(k)}(z)|}{1+|f(z)|^{k+1}}.

Motivated by the generalized Marty-type theorem of Li and Xie
[
14
]
and by the
normality criteria of Chen, Nevo and Pang
[
6
]
involving lower bounds of differential
expressions, we first establish a new criterion for a harmonic mapping to be normal.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
k
be a positive integer and let
M>0
. Suppose that
f=h+\bar{g}
is a non-constant
harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
satisfying

(4)

f^{\#(k)}(z)>M,\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then
f
is a normal harmonic mapping.

Choosing
k=1
in Theorem
1.4
recovers
[
4
, Theorem 1.4]
. Moreover
Theorem
1.4
may be regarded as a harmonic analogue, for higher-order
derivatives, of the result of Grahl and Nevo
[
11
]
.

We next study weighted versions of normality. Our work is motivated by Aulaskari and
Rättyä
[
3
]
, who introduced
\varphi
-normal meromorphic functions. A function

\varphi\colon[0,1)\to(0,\infty)
is said to be
smoothly increasing
if

\varphi(r)(1-r)\longrightarrow\infty\quad(r\to 1^{-}),\qquad\frac{\varphi\!\left(\left|a+\dfrac{z}{\varphi(|a|)}\right|\right)}{\varphi(|a|)}\longrightarrow 1\quad(|a|\to 1^{-}),

the latter converges uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
. A typical example is

\varphi(r)=(1-r)^{-\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
.

If
\varphi
is smoothly increasing then, without loss of generality, we shall assume
throughout that

(5)

\varphi(r)(1-r)\geq 1,\qquad 0\leq r<1.

Indeed, replacing
\varphi
by
\max\{\varphi,1\}
changes neither the smoothly increasing
property nor the notion of
\varphi
-normality below, since the two functions agree near
the boundary, where the relevant estimates take place.

Given such a
\varphi
, a meromorphic function in
\mathbb{D}
is
\varphi
-normal
if

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{f^{\#}(z)}{\varphi(|z|)}<\infty.

Following
[
3
]
, Bohra, Datt and Pal
[
5
]
extended
\varphi
-normality to planar
harmonic mappings: a harmonic mapping
f=h+\bar{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
is
\varphi
-normal
if

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{f^{\#}(z)}{\varphi(|z|)}<\infty,

where
f^{\#}
is as in (
3
) with
k=1
.

The theory of
\varphi
-normal harmonic mappings is largely unexplored. Our central tool is
the following Zalcman–Pang type rescaling lemma. Only the implication stated is required in
the sequel; see Remark
3.1
for a discussion of the converse.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\varphi\colon[0,1)\to(0,\infty)
be a continuous smoothly increasing function and let

\beta\in(-1,\infty)
. If a non-constant harmonic mapping
f=h+\bar{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
is

not

\varphi
-normal, then there exist sequences
\{w_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
and

\{\rho_{n}\}\subset(0,1)
, with
\rho_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, such that the functions

F_{n}(\eta)=\left(\frac{\rho_{n}}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\right)^{\beta}f\!\left(w_{n}+\frac{\rho_{n}\eta}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\right)

converge locally uniformly in
\mathbb{C}
to a non-constant harmonic mapping
F
satisfying

F^{\#}(\eta)\leq F^{\#}(0)=1
.

As a consequence we obtain the following sufficient condition for
\varphi
-normality.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
k
be a positive integer and let
\alpha>1
,
M>0
. Let

\varphi\colon[0,1)\to(0,\infty)
be a continuous smoothly increasing function. Suppose that

f=h+\bar{g}
is a non-constant harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
satisfying

(6)

\frac{|h^{(k)}(z)|+|g^{(k)}(z)|}{1+|f(z)|^{\alpha}}>\frac{M}{\varphi(|z|)^{\alpha-1}},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then
f
is a
\varphi
-normal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
.

Example 1.7
.

Let
\varphi(r)=(1-r)^{-2}
,
r\in[0,1)
, and let
f(z)=z+\tfrac{1}{2}\bar{z}
, so that

h(z)=z
and
g(z)=\tfrac{1}{2}z
. Take
\alpha=\tfrac{3}{2}
and

M=\tfrac{3}{2}\big/\big(1+(\tfrac{3}{2})^{3/2}\big)
. Since
|h^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|=\tfrac{3}{2}
and

|f(z)|\leq\tfrac{3}{2}|z|<\tfrac{3}{2}
, we have for all
z\in\mathbb{D}

\frac{|h^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|}{1+|f(z)|^{3/2}}=\frac{3/2}{1+|f(z)|^{3/2}}>\frac{3/2}{1+(3/2)^{3/2}}=M\geq M(1-|z|)=\frac{M}{\varphi(|z|)^{1/2}},

so
f
satisfies (
6
). Moreover

\frac{f^{\#}(z)}{\varphi(|z|)}=\frac{3/2}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}\,(1-|z|)^{2}\leq\frac{3}{2},

so
\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}f^{\#}(z)/\varphi(|z|)<\infty
and
f
is
\varphi
-normal, as predicted by
Theorem
1.6
.

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
k
be a positive integer,
M>0
, and let
\varphi\colon[0,1)\to(0,\infty)
be a
continuous smoothly increasing function. If
f=h+\bar{g}
is a non-constant harmonic mapping
in
\mathbb{D}
satisfying

(7)

f^{\#(k)}(z)>\frac{M}{\varphi(|z|)^{k}},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

then
f
is
\varphi
-normal.

We next pass to families. Following Lohwater and Pommerenke

[
15
, Theorem 1]
, Zalcman
[
20
]
established his rescaling lemma
for normal families of meromorphic functions, since then one of the most powerful tools in
the theory. We introduce the corresponding notion in the present setting.

Definition 1.9
.

A family
\mathcal{F}
of harmonic mappings in
\mathbb{D}
is said to be
\varphi
-normal
in

\mathbb{D}
if for each compact set
K\subset\mathbb{D}
there exists
M>0
such that

\sup\left\{\frac{f^{\#}(z)}{\varphi(|z|)}:z\in K,\ f\in\mathcal{F}\right\}<M.

Example 1.10
.

Let
\varphi(|z|)=(1-|z|)^{-2}
and

\mathcal{F}=\big\{f_{n}(z)=z+\tfrac{1}{n}\,\overline{z^{\,n}}:z\in\mathbb{D}\big\}
. Then

h(z)=z
,
g_{n}(z)=\tfrac{1}{n}z^{n}
, so
|h^{\prime}(z)|+|g_{n}^{\prime}(z)|=1+|z|^{n-1}
and

f_{n}^{\#}(z)=\frac{1+|z|^{\,n-1}}{1+|f_{n}(z)|^{2}}.

Hence for every compact
K\subset\mathbb{D}
,

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in K\\
f_{n}\in\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}\frac{f_{n}^{\#}(z)}{\varphi(|z|)}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in K\\
f_{n}\in\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}\frac{(1+|z|^{\,n-1})(1-|z|)^{2}}{1+|f_{n}(z)|^{2}}<2,

so
\mathcal{F}
is a
\varphi
-normal family.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
\varphi\colon[0,1)\to(0,\infty)
be a continuous smoothly increasing function and let

\beta\in(-1,\infty)
. If a family
\mathcal{F}
of non-constant harmonic mappings in
\mathbb{D}
is

not

\varphi
-normal, then there exist sequences
\{w_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
,

\{f_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{F}
and
\{\rho_{n}\}\subset(0,1)
, with
\rho_{n}\to 0
, such that

F_{n}(\eta)=\left(\frac{\rho_{n}}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\right)^{\beta}f_{n}\!\left(w_{n}+\frac{\rho_{n}\eta}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\right)

converge locally uniformly in
\mathbb{C}
to a non-constant harmonic mapping
F
satisfying

F^{\#}(\eta)\leq F^{\#}(0)=1
.

As an application we obtain a Lappan-type theorem. The novelty is that the test set
E
may
be taken to have only
three
points (rather than five), a saving made possible by
Lemma
2.7
below.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
\varphi\colon[0,1)\to(0,\infty)
be a continuous smoothly increasing function. Let

\mathcal{F}
be a family of sense-preserving harmonic mappings in
\mathbb{D}
and let
E
be a set
of three distinct complex numbers such that

\sup\left\{\frac{f^{\#}(w)}{\varphi(|w|)}:w\in f^{-1}(E),\ f\in\mathcal{F}\right\}<\infty.

Then
\mathcal{F}
is
\varphi
-normal in
\mathbb{D}
.

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
f=h+\bar{g}
be a non-constant sense-preserving harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
and let
E
be
a set of three distinct complex numbers such that

\sup\left\{\frac{f^{\#}(w)}{\varphi(|w|)}:w\in f^{-1}(E)\right\}<\infty.

Then
f
is a
\varphi
-normal harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
.

Remark 1.14
.

Theorem
1.13
improves
[
5
, Theorem 1.6]
by reducing the cardinality
of the test set from five to three. By the same argument, the cardinality of the set
E
in
the Lappan-type criterion
[
4
, Theorem 1.6]
can likewise be reduced to three.

2.
Preliminary lemmas

We collect the auxiliary results used in the proofs. The following is the harmonic analogue of Marty’s theorem, due to Deng, Ponnusamy and Qiao
[
9
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
9
, Lemma 1]
)
.

A family
\mathcal{F}
of harmonic mappings
f=h+\bar{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
is normal if

\{f^{\#}(z):f\in\mathcal{F}\}
is locally uniformly bounded.

Remark 2.2
.

A partial converse holds under the additional assumption
\operatorname{Re}\big(h(z)\overline{g(z)}\big)>0
;
see
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

A non-constant harmonic mapping
f
in
\mathbb{D}
is normal if and only if there do
not

exist sequences
\{w_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
and
\{\rho_{n}\}\subset(0,\infty)
with
\rho_{n}\to 0

such that

F_{n}(\eta):=\rho_{n}^{\,\beta}\,f(w_{n}+\rho_{n}\eta)

converges locally uniformly in
\mathbb{C}
to a non-constant harmonic mapping
F
satisfying

F^{\#}(\eta)\leq F^{\#}(0)=1
, where
\beta\in(-1,\infty)
is arbitrary but fixed.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
5
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Let
\varphi\colon[0,1)\to(0,\infty)
be smoothly increasing. A non-constant harmonic
mapping
f=h+\bar{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
is
not

\varphi
-normal if and only if there exist
sequences
\{w_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
and
\{\rho_{n}\}\subset(0,1)
, with
\rho_{n}\to 0
, such that

F_{n}(\eta)=f\!\left(w_{n}+\frac{\rho_{n}\eta}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\right)

converges locally uniformly in
\mathbb{C}
to a non-constant harmonic mapping
F
.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
10
, p. 10]
)
.

Let
\{f_{n}\}
be a sequence of sense-preserving harmonic mappings in
\mathbb{D}
converging
locally uniformly to a sense-preserving harmonic mapping
f
. Then
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
is a zero of

f
if and only if
z_{0}
is a cluster point of the zeros of
f_{n}
,
n\geq 1
.

The next lemma is classical (see
[
12
]
); we include a proof for completeness.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
f
be a non-constant entire function. Then there are at most two values
a
for which
all zeros of
f-a
are multiple.

Proof.

We use the second fundamental theorem of Nevanlinna. Suppose, to the contrary, that there
exist three distinct values
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in\mathbb{C}
such that all zeros of
f-a_{j}

(
j=1,2,3
) have multiplicity at least
2
. Since
f
is entire,
\overline{N}(r,f)=0
, and
since every zero of
f-a_{j}
is multiple,

\overline{N}\!\big(r,\tfrac{1}{f-a_{j}}\big)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}N\!\big(r,\tfrac{1}{f-a_{j}}\big)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}T(r,f)+O(1).

Hence

2T(r,f)\leq\overline{N}(r,f)+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\overline{N}\!\left(r,\frac{1}{f-a_{j}}\right)+S(r,f)\leq\frac{3}{2}\,T(r,f)+S(r,f),

so that
\tfrac{1}{2}T(r,f)\leq S(r,f)
, which is impossible. Hence the proof.
∎

The following sharpens
[
9
, Lemma 3]
, where four values appear in place of two.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
f=h+\bar{g}
be a sense-preserving harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{C}
with
g(0)=0
. Then there
are at most two values
a
for which all zeros of
f-a
are multiple.

Proof.

Since
f
is sense-preserving in
\mathbb{C}
, we have
|g^{\prime}(z)/h^{\prime}(z)|<1
in
\mathbb{C}
; as
g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
is then
a bounded entire function, Liouville’s theorem gives
\omega(z)=g^{\prime}(z)/h^{\prime}(z)\equiv c
with

c\in\mathbb{C}
,
|c|<1
. Integrating
g^{\prime}=c\,h^{\prime}
with
g(0)=0
yields
g(z)=c\big(h(z)-h(0)\big)
,
whence

f(z)=h(z)+\overline{c\,h(z)-c\,h(0)}.

As in
[
5
, Lemma 2.5]
, for any
a\in\mathbb{C}
the equation
f(z)=a
is equivalent to

(8)

h(z)=a^{*}:=\frac{a-\overline{c\,a}+\overline{c\,h(0)}-|c|^{2}h(0)}{1-|c|^{2}}.

Moreover
f(z)-a=u(z)+\bar{c}\,\overline{u(z)}
with
u=h-a^{*}
, so
z_{0}
is a zero of

f-a
of order
p
if and only if it is a zero of
h-a^{*}
of order
p
; in particular the
multiplicities coincide. Since
a\mapsto a^{*}
is a bijection of
\mathbb{C}
, and since by
Lemma
2.6
there are at most two values
a^{*}
for which every zero of

h-a^{*}
is multiple, there are at most two values
a
for which every zero of
f-a
is
multiple.
∎

3.
Proofs of the main results

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Suppose, to the contrary, that
f
is not normal. By Lemma
2.3
(with

\beta=2
), there exist sequences
\{w_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
and
\{\rho_{n}\}\subset(0,1)
with

|w_{n}|\to 1^{-}
and
\rho_{n}\to 0
such that

F_{n}(\eta)=\rho_{n}^{2}f(w_{n}+\rho_{n}\eta)=\rho_{n}^{2}\big(h(w_{n}+\rho_{n}\eta)+\overline{g(w_{n}+\rho_{n}\eta)}\big)

converges locally uniformly in
\mathbb{C}
to a non-constant harmonic mapping

F(\eta)=H(\eta)+\overline{G(\eta)}
with
F^{\#}(\eta)\leq 1
and
F^{\#}(0)=1
. Write

H_{n}(\eta):=\rho_{n}^{2}h(w_{n}+\rho_{n}\eta)
and

G_{n}(\eta):=\rho_{n}^{2}g(w_{n}+\rho_{n}\eta)
. Since
F_{n}\to F
locally uniformly, for
every positive integer
k
we have
H_{n}^{(k)}\to H^{(k)}
and
G_{n}^{(k)}\to G^{(k)}

locally uniformly. As
F
is non-constant there is
\eta_{0}
with
F(\eta_{0})\neq 0
.

Put
\zeta_{n}=w_{n}+\rho_{n}\eta_{0}
. Using

H_{n}^{(k)}(\eta_{0})=\rho_{n}^{k+2}h^{(k)}(\zeta_{n})
,

G_{n}^{(k)}(\eta_{0})=\rho_{n}^{k+2}g^{(k)}(\zeta_{n})
and

|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{k+1}=\rho_{n}^{2(k+1)}|f(\zeta_{n})|^{k+1}
, we estimate

\displaystyle|H_{n}^{(k)}(\eta_{0})|+|G_{n}^{(k)}(\eta_{0})|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|H_{n}^{(k)}(\eta_{0})|+|G_{n}^{(k)}(\eta_{0})|}{1+|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{k+1}}=\frac{\rho_{n}^{k+2}\big(|h^{(k)}(\zeta_{n})|+|g^{(k)}(\zeta_{n})|\big)}{1+\rho_{n}^{2(k+1)}|f(\zeta_{n})|^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|h^{(k)}(\zeta_{n})|+|g^{(k)}(\zeta_{n})|}{\rho_{n}^{k}\,|f(\zeta_{n})|^{k+1}}\cdot\frac{|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{k+1}}{1+|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{k+1}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|h^{(k)}(\zeta_{n})|+|g^{(k)}(\zeta_{n})|}{\rho_{n}^{k}\big(1+|f(\zeta_{n})|^{k+1}\big)}\cdot\frac{|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{k+1}}{1+|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{k+1}}

\displaystyle>\frac{M}{\rho_{n}^{k}}\cdot\frac{|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{k+1}}{1+|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{k+1}},

where the last inequality uses the hypothesis (
4
), i.e.
\big(|h^{(k)}|+|g^{(k)}|\big)/(1+|f|^{k+1})>M
. Since
\rho_{n}\to 0
and

|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|\to|F(\eta_{0})|\neq 0
, the right-hand side tends to
\infty
. But the
left-hand side converges to
|H^{(k)}(\eta_{0})|+|G^{(k)}(\eta_{0})|<\infty
, a
contradiction. Hence
f
is normal.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Suppose
f
is not
\varphi
-normal. Then there is a sequence
\{w_{n}^{\prime}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
with

|w_{n}^{\prime}|\to 1^{-}
and

(9)

\frac{f^{\#}(w_{n}^{\prime})}{\varphi(|w_{n}^{\prime}|)}\longrightarrow\infty.

By the continuity of
\varphi
we may choose
r_{n}\in(0,1)
with
|w_{n}^{\prime}|<r_{n}<1
and

(10)

\frac{f^{\#}(w_{n}^{\prime})}{\varphi\big(|w_{n}^{\prime}|/r_{n}\big)}\longrightarrow\infty.

For
t\in(0,1]
and
|w|<r_{n}
define

\Gamma_{n}(t,w):=\frac{1}{\varphi\big(|w|/r_{n}\big)^{\,1+\beta}}\cdot\frac{t^{\,1+\beta}\big(1+|f(w)|^{2}\big)f^{\#}(w)}{1+\dfrac{t^{2\beta}|f(w)|^{2}}{\varphi(|w|/r_{n})^{2\beta}}}.

Then
\Gamma_{n}
is continuous on
(0,1]\times\{|w|<r_{n}\}
, and since
\beta>-1
,

(11)

\lim_{t\to 0}\Gamma_{n}(t,w)=0.

Claim 1.

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}(t,w)\geq\frac{t^{\,1+|\beta|}f^{\#}(w)}{\varphi(|w|/r_{n})^{\,1+|\beta|}}.

Case
\beta\geq 0
.
By (
5
) we have
\varphi(|w|/r_{n})\geq 1
,
and since
t\leq 1
this gives
t^{2\beta}/\varphi(|w|/r_{n})^{2\beta}\leq 1
. Hence the
denominator is at most
1+|f(w)|^{2}
, and

\Gamma_{n}(t,w)\geq\frac{1}{\varphi(|w|/r_{n})^{1+\beta}}\cdot\frac{t^{1+\beta}\big(1+|f(w)|^{2}\big)f^{\#}(w)}{1+|f(w)|^{2}}=\frac{t^{1+\beta}f^{\#}(w)}{\varphi(|w|/r_{n})^{1+\beta}}.

Case
\beta<0
.
Then
|\beta|=-\beta>0
, and the same computation with
|\beta|
in
place of
\beta
yields the claim. This proves Claim 1.

By (
10
) and Claim 1,

(12)

\Gamma_{n}(1,w_{n}^{\prime})\geq\frac{f^{\#}(w_{n}^{\prime})}{\varphi(|w_{n}^{\prime}|/r_{n})^{\,1+|\beta|}}\longrightarrow\infty.

Thus
\sup_{|w|<r_{n}}\Gamma_{n}(1,w)\geq\Gamma_{n}(1,w_{n}^{\prime})>1
for large
n
, while by
(
11
)
\sup_{|w|<r_{n}}\Gamma_{n}(t,w)<1
for
t
small. By continuity there exist

t_{n}\in(0,1)
and
w_{n}
with
|w_{n}|<r_{n}
such that

(13)

\sup_{|w|<r_{n}}\Gamma_{n}(t_{n},w)=\Gamma_{n}(t_{n},w_{n})=1.

Combining (
13
) with Claim 1,

1=\Gamma_{n}(t_{n},w_{n})\geq\Gamma_{n}(t_{n},w_{n}^{\prime})\geq\frac{t_{n}^{\,1+|\beta|}f^{\#}(w_{n}^{\prime})}{\varphi(|w_{n}^{\prime}|/r_{n})^{\,1+|\beta|}},

which together with (
12
) forces
t_{n}\to 0
.

Set
\rho_{n}=\dfrac{t_{n}\varphi(|w_{n}|)}{\varphi(|w_{n}|/r_{n})}
. Since
\varphi
is
increasing and
|w_{n}|<|w_{n}|/r_{n}
, we have
\rho_{n}<t_{n}\to 0
. Define

F_{n}(\eta)=\left(\frac{\rho_{n}}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\right)^{\beta}f\!\left(w_{n}+\frac{\rho_{n}\eta}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\right),\qquad|\eta|<R_{n}=\frac{(1-|w_{n}|)\varphi(|w_{n}|)}{\rho_{n}}.

A direct computation gives

(14)

F_{n}^{\#}(\eta)=\left(\frac{\rho_{n}}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\right)^{1+\beta}\frac{\Big(1+\big|f(w_{n}+\tfrac{\rho_{n}\eta}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)})\big|^{2}\Big)f^{\#}\!\big(w_{n}+\tfrac{\rho_{n}\eta}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\big)}{1+\big(\tfrac{\rho_{n}}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\big)^{2\beta}\big|f(w_{n}+\tfrac{\rho_{n}\eta}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)})\big|^{2}},

and in particular, by (
13
),

(15)

F_{n}^{\#}(0)=\left(\frac{\rho_{n}}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\right)^{1+\beta}\frac{(1+|f(w_{n})|^{2})f^{\#}(w_{n})}{1+\big(\tfrac{\rho_{n}}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\big)^{2\beta}|f(w_{n})|^{2}}=\Gamma_{n}(t_{n},w_{n})=1.

Claim 2.

\{F_{n}\}
is normal.

Fix
R>0
and let
|\eta|\leq R
. By the smoothly increasing property,

\frac{\varphi\!\Big(\big|\tfrac{w_{n}}{r_{n}}+\tfrac{\rho_{n}\eta}{\varphi(|w_{n}|/r_{n})}\big|\Big)}{\varphi(|w_{n}|/r_{n})}\longrightarrow 1

uniformly for
|\eta|\leq R
, so there is a sequence
\delta_{n}\to 0^{+}
with, for all large

n
,

(16)

\frac{(1-\delta_{n})t_{n}}{\varphi\!\big(\big|\tfrac{w_{n}}{r_{n}}+\tfrac{\rho_{n}\eta}{\varphi(|w_{n}|/r_{n})}\big|\big)}\leq\frac{\rho_{n}}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\leq\frac{(1+\delta_{n})t_{n}}{\varphi\!\big(\big|\tfrac{w_{n}}{r_{n}}+\tfrac{\rho_{n}\eta}{\varphi(|w_{n}|/r_{n})}\big|\big)}.

Substituting (
16
) into (
14
) and bounding the denominator from below,

F_{n}^{\#}(\eta)\leq\frac{(1+\delta_{n})^{1+\beta}}{(1-\delta_{n})^{2\beta}}\longrightarrow 1\qquad(n\to\infty),

uniformly for
|\eta|\leq R
. Hence
\{F_{n}^{\#}\}
is locally uniformly bounded and, by
Lemma
2.1
, the family
\{F_{n}\}
is normal. This proves Claim 2.

Passing to a subsequence,
F_{n}\to F
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{C}
for some harmonic mapping

F
. By (
15
) and Claim 2,
F^{\#}(\eta)\leq 1
and
F^{\#}(0)=1
; in particular

F^{\#}(0)\neq 0
, so
F
is non-constant. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Only the implication proved in Theorem
1.5
is used below. The converse
(“existence of a non-constant rescaled limit implies non-
\varphi
-normality”) holds for

\beta=0
and is precisely Lemma
2.4
; for
\beta\geq 1
it may fail, since the bound of the rescaled spherical derivative carries a factor of order

\rho_{n}^{\,1-\beta}\varphi(|w_{n}|)^{\beta}
which may tend to
\infty.
However, the converse holds for
\beta\leq 0.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Suppose, to the contrary, that
f
is not
\varphi
-normal. Applying
Theorem
1.5
with
\beta=\dfrac{k}{\alpha-1}+1\;(>1)
, there exist sequences

\{w_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
and
\{\rho_{n}\}\subset(0,1)
with
\rho_{n}\to 0
such that

F_{n}(\eta)=\left(\frac{\rho_{n}}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\right)^{\frac{k}{\alpha-1}+1}f\!\left(w_{n}+\frac{\rho_{n}\eta}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\right)=H_{n}(\eta)+\overline{G_{n}(\eta)}

converges locally uniformly in
\mathbb{C}
to a non-constant harmonic mapping

F=H+\overline{G}
with
F^{\#}\leq 1
and
F^{\#}(0)=1
, where

H_{n}(\eta)=\left(\frac{\rho_{n}}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\right)^{\frac{k}{\alpha-1}+1}h\!\left(w_{n}+\frac{\rho_{n}\eta}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\right),\quad G_{n}(\eta)=\left(\frac{\rho_{n}}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\right)^{\frac{k}{\alpha-1}+1}g\!\left(w_{n}+\frac{\rho_{n}\eta}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\right).

Then
H_{n}^{(k)}\to H^{(k)}
and
G_{n}^{(k)}\to G^{(k)}
locally uniformly, and there is

\eta_{0}
with
F(\eta_{0})\neq 0
. Put
w_{n}^{*}=w_{n}+\tfrac{\rho_{n}}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\eta_{0}
.
Writing
c_{n}=\rho_{n}/\varphi(|w_{n}|)
and using

H_{n}^{(k)}(\eta_{0})=c_{n}^{\,\frac{k}{\alpha-1}+1+k}h^{(k)}(w_{n}^{*})
(similarly for

G_{n}
) and
|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{\alpha}=c_{n}^{\,(\frac{k}{\alpha-1}+1)\alpha}|f(w_{n}^{*})|^{\alpha}
,

\displaystyle|H_{n}^{(k)}(\eta_{0})|+|G_{n}^{(k)}(\eta_{0})|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{n}^{\,\frac{k}{\alpha-1}+1+k}\big(|h^{(k)}(w_{n}^{*})|+|g^{(k)}(w_{n}^{*})|\big)}{1+|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|h^{(k)}(w_{n}^{*})|+|g^{(k)}(w_{n}^{*})|}{c_{n}^{\,\alpha-1}\,|f(w_{n}^{*})|^{\alpha}}\cdot\frac{|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{\alpha}}{1+|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|h^{(k)}(w_{n}^{*})|+|g^{(k)}(w_{n}^{*})|}{c_{n}^{\,\alpha-1}\big(1+|f(w_{n}^{*})|^{\alpha}\big)}\cdot\frac{|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{\alpha}}{1+|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle>\frac{M}{\varphi(|w_{n}^{*}|)^{\alpha-1}\,c_{n}^{\,\alpha-1}}\cdot\frac{|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{\alpha}}{1+|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{M}{\big(\varphi(|w_{n}^{*}|)/\varphi(|w_{n}|)\big)^{\alpha-1}\rho_{n}^{\,\alpha-1}}\cdot\frac{|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{\alpha}}{1+|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|^{\alpha}},

where we used the hypothesis (
6
) at
z=w_{n}^{*}
and

c_{n}^{\,\alpha-1}\varphi(|w_{n}^{*}|)^{\alpha-1}=\rho_{n}^{\,\alpha-1}\big(\varphi(|w_{n}^{*}|)/\varphi(|w_{n}|)\big)^{\alpha-1}
. Since

\varphi(|w_{n}^{*}|)/\varphi(|w_{n}|)\to 1
,
\rho_{n}\to 0
and

|F_{n}(\eta_{0})|\to|F(\eta_{0})|\neq 0
, the right-hand side tends to
\infty
, while the
left-hand side tends to
|H^{(k)}(\eta_{0})|+|G^{(k)}(\eta_{0})|<\infty
, a contradiction.
Hence
f
is
\varphi
-normal.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.12
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is not
\varphi
-normal in
\mathbb{D}
. By Theorem
1.11

(with
\beta=0
) there exist
\{w_{n}\}
,
\{\rho_{n}\}\subset(0,1)
with
\rho_{n}\to 0
and

\{f_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{F}
such that

F_{n}(\eta)=f_{n}\!\left(w_{n}+\frac{\rho_{n}}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\eta\right)\longrightarrow F(\eta)

locally uniformly in
\mathbb{C}
, where
F
is a non-constant sense-preserving harmonic mapping;
since
g(0)=0
is a value-independent normalization, Lemma
2.7
applies to
F
.

Let
b\in\mathbb{C}
be such that
F-b
has a simple zero at some
\eta_{0}
, i.e.
F^{\#}(\eta_{0})\neq 0
. By Lemma
2.5
there exist
\eta_{n}\to\eta_{0}
with

F_{n}(\eta_{n})=f_{n}(w_{n}^{*})=b
for all large
n
, where

w_{n}^{*}=w_{n}+\tfrac{\rho_{n}}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\eta_{n}
. Since
F_{n}\to F
locally
uniformly,

F_{n}^{\#}(\eta_{n})=\frac{\rho_{n}}{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}\,f_{n}^{\#}(w_{n}^{*})\longrightarrow F^{\#}(\eta_{0})\neq 0,

and therefore, using
\varphi(|w_{n}^{*}|)/\varphi(|w_{n}|)\to 1
and
\rho_{n}\to 0
,

\frac{f_{n}^{\#}(w_{n}^{*})}{\varphi(|w_{n}^{*}|)}=\frac{\varphi(|w_{n}|)}{\rho_{n}}\cdot\frac{F_{n}^{\#}(\eta_{n})}{\varphi(|w_{n}^{*}|)}\longrightarrow\infty.

Thus, whenever
b
is a simple value of
F
,

(17)

\sup_{w\in f_{n}^{-1}(\{b\})}\frac{f_{n}^{\#}(w)}{\varphi(|w|)}=\infty.

By Lemma
2.7
there are at most two values
b
for which every zero of
F-b
is
multiple. Hence, among the three distinct numbers of
E
, at least one, say
b_{0}
, is a
simple value of
F
, and (
17
) for
b_{0}
contradicts the hypothesis

\sup\left\{\frac{f^{\#}(w)}{\varphi(|w|)}:w\in f^{-1}(E),\ f\in\mathcal{F}\right\}<\infty.

Therefore
\mathcal{F}
is
\varphi
-normal in
\mathbb{D}
.
∎
