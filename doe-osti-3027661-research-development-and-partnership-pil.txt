 Executive Summary/Abstract

The goals for this Research Development and Partnership Pilot (RDPP) proposal were to i) learn
about Department of Energy (DOE) research and use the PI’s expertise on patterns and
environmental drivers of tree reproduction as a basis to make connections with individuals and
research groups at DOE National Laboratories, and ii) to develop plans and form partnerships that will
both enhance the PI’s research. The objectives of the proj ect were for the PI to i) participate in the
Department of Energy's Office of Science program in Biological and Environmental Research training
and outreach activities, ii) conduct directed fact-finding on Earth and Environmental Systems
Sciences Division (EESSD) research projects and new partnerships with individuals and groups at
National Laboratories, ii) conduct meetings with potential collaborators at National Laboratories to
discuss EESSD-relevant research ideas, and iv) to develop a plan for future research efforts. During
the period of the award, the objectives of the proposal were met, as the PI attended Environmental
System Sciences meetings, American Geophysical Union meetings, SPRUCE experiment meetings,
and visited the Oak Ridge National Lab. These activities led to increased understanding of the
research being conducted by DOE scientists and the PI had meetings with potential collaborators to
develop future research plans that leverage expertise at DOE and the research interests of the PI to
increase understanding of the role of tree reproduction in future carbon allocation and in tree
regeneration models in boreal ecosystems.

  a. What was the impact on the development of the principal discipline(s) of the project?

  From this project, PI LaMontagne participated in a number of Department of Energy activities
  and interacted with a large number of DOE scientists across a range of meetings and
  conferences. During these interactions, different perspectives were connected that have the
  potential to lead to advancements in knowledge, theory, and research in various fields of

  biology. PI LaMontagne’s expertise is on the patterns and the environmental drivers of tree
  reproduction over time and space, as trees vary in the quantity of seeds that they produce. In
  the past, allocation of trees to reproduction has not been included in models to understand
  where resources go. Instead, research on tree allocation has focused primarily on tree growth
  aboveground, including to woody growth and leaves, as well as roots. While visiting ORNL, PI
  LaMontagne gave a talk about her research program to DOE scientists and described the
  concept of ‘mast seeding’ where trees can have orders of magnitude variation in reproductive
  output between years, and that this is a common reproductive strategy in tree species around
  the planet. This provides potential for these research ideas to be integrated into theory and
  mathematical models of allocation in the future. At the same time, PI LaMontagne has learned
  from DOE scientists across disciplines that can be integrated into her future research program.
  Additionally, there are discussions for future collaborations of the PI with DOE scientists.
