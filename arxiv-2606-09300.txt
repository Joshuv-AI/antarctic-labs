Chirp-Induced Non-Separable Gabor Windows on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d}
Lorenzo De Leonardis, Alessandro Mazzoccoli, Pierluigi Vellucci

Abstract
We construct an explicit class of non-separable Gabor windows on L 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) by applying chirp deformations to tensor-product dual pairs on separable lattices. Starting from one-dimensional dual Gabor frames, we first obtain separable higher-dimensional dual pairs by tensorization. We then transport these systems through the unitary chirp operator U C ​ f ​ ( x ) = e π ​ i ​ x T ​ C ​ x ​ f ​ ( x ) U_{C}f(x)=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}f(x) and the associated phase-space shear, obtaining Gabor systems on lower block-triangular lattices of the form Λ A , B , C = { ( A k , C A k + B ℓ ) : k , ℓ ∈ ℤ d } . \Lambda_{A,B,C}=\{(Ak,CAk+B\ell):k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}. The construction preserves compact support, smoothness, frame bounds, exact duality, canonical duals, and approximate-duality errors. We also quantify chirp-induced coupling through a phase-space covariance defect and show that non-separability changes the geometry of the atoms without deteriorating deterministic or finite-section statistical robustness bounds.



1
Introduction

The construction of explicit Gabor frames in higher dimension involves two competing requirements.
On the one hand, tensor-product constructions on separable lattices provide a tractable way to lift
one-dimensional windows and dual windows to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. On the other hand, many genuinely
multivariate signals exhibit geometric features that are not aligned with the coordinate axes, and
therefore call for time-frequency atoms with cross-coordinate coupling. The central question addressed
in this paper is whether one can pass from separable Gabor systems to genuinely non-separable
higher-dimensional systems while retaining explicit dual windows, compact support, smoothness, and
quantitative reconstruction control.

The answer developed below is constructive. Starting from dual Gabor pairs on separable lattices, we
apply a chirp deformation and the associated shear of phase space. This produces non-separable windows
on lower block-triangular lattices, while preserving the frame bounds, exact duality, approximate duality,
and the reconstruction estimates inherited from the separable model.

1.1
Why non-separable windows? Geometry versus noise and computational baselines

A natural question is why one should introduce
non-separable
Gabor windows in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and whether
non-separability is expected to improve (or worsen) reconstruction in the presence of noise.
The viewpoint adopted in this paper is deliberately
structural
: our chirp-based construction is
designed to change the
geometry
of the time-frequency atoms (their orientation and coupling across
coordinates) while
not
deteriorating the standard stability and noise-propagation constants
attached to the underlying frame reconstruction.

Separable windows of the form
g(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}(x_{j})
on separable lattices

A\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times B\mathbb{Z}^{d}
provide a robust and highly tractable baseline.
They allow one to lift one-dimensional constructions to higher dimension by tensorization, yielding explicit
dual pairs with controllable regularity (e.g. compact support and smoothness) and transparent proofs.
From a practical standpoint, separability also simplifies implementation: many operations factor into
lower-dimensional components, leading to reduced computational complexity and memory footprint.

Despite these advantages, separability imposes a rigid axis-aligned structure.
In multivariate signals and data, relevant features are often
anisotropic
and
coupled
across
coordinates (e.g. oriented patterns, oblique structures, and correlated variations). For such phenomena,
axis-aligned atoms may require many coefficients to represent a single geometric feature, whereas atoms that
incorporate cross-coordinate coupling can yield representations that are more faithful to the intrinsic
geometry of the signal. In this sense, non-separability is not introduced here to claim a generic improvement
of global stability constants, but to provide a controlled way to “tilt” and couple time-frequency atoms in

\mathbb{R}^{d}
while retaining full analytical tractability.

It is important to separate two distinct issues:

(i)

Linear reconstruction stability
(in the sense of frames), which governs how coefficient perturbations
propagate into the reconstructed signal;

(ii)

Nonlinear denoising performance
(e.g. thresholding/shrinkage), which depends on sparsity and on how the
signal energy concentrates in a few coefficients.

For linear reconstruction from noisy coefficients, separability by itself does not provide an intrinsic
noise-reduction mechanism. The quantities that control noise amplification are the frame bounds (or,
equivalently, the conditioning of the frame operator) and the Bessel bound of the synthesis window used in the
reconstruction. Likewise, in the approximately dual setting, the key bias term is the operator deviation

\|I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda}\|
, which quantifies how close the mixed reconstruction operator is to the identity.
These constants are
global
and are not determined solely by separability/non-separability.

The central feature of the present chirp-based approach is that it preserves the global stability constants and
the reconstruction error estimates inherited from the separable model.
More precisely, starting from a separable dual pair

(\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda),\mathcal{G}(\gamma,\Lambda))
on a separable lattice, we apply a unitary chirp deformation

U_{C}
and the associated shear of the lattice. This use of a chirp should be understood as a concrete,
support-preserving instance of the broader metaplectic viewpoint in time-frequency analysis. In particular,
recent work on metaplectic Gabor frames shows that certain metaplectic time-frequency representations can be
represented, up to chirps and linear changes of variables, as rescaled short-time Fourier transforms

(
5
)
. In contrast to that general program, our aim is deliberately narrower and constructive:
we restrict to the shear

S_{C}(x,\omega)=(x,\omega+Cx)

and use it to transport explicit dual windows from separable to non-separable lattices.

This produces a non-separable window

g_{C}(x)=U_{C}g(x)=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,g(x),

supported and smooth whenever
g
is, and defined on the sheared lattice
\Lambda_{C}=S_{C}\Lambda
.
Because the deformation is unitary and intertwines time-frequency shifts in a precise covariance sense, the
frame bounds are preserved. Moreover, in the approximately dual regime, the reconstruction error is invariant:

\|I-\Theta_{g_{C},\gamma_{C},\Lambda_{C}}\|=\|I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda}\|,\qquad\gamma_{C}:=U_{C}\gamma.

As a consequence, the deterministic error bounds for noisy coefficients, and the corresponding finite-section
mean-square error decompositions, carry over to the non-separable setting
without any deterioration of the
constants
. In short: in this paper non-separability is introduced to change the
geometry
of the atoms,
not to trade stability for flexibility.

While the global stability and noise-propagation constants remain unchanged under our deformation, the
time-frequency
geometry
can change substantially (the atoms become tilted/coupled).
This provides a principled, fully explicit testbed for studying how geometric coupling interacts with
representation properties that are crucial in applications, such as sparsity for anisotropic features and
the performance of nonlinear denoising procedures.
The construction therefore isolates a clean separation of roles: separable windows supply explicit baselines
with controlled duals, and the chirp deformation generates non-separable higher-dimensional windows that inherit
the same reconstruction guarantees while potentially offering a better geometric match to non-axis-aligned
structures.

The main contributions of the paper may be summarized as follows. First, we recall and use a
tensorization principle showing how one-dimensional dual Gabor pairs generate dual pairs on separable
lattices in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Second, we prove that chirp multiplication transports frames, exact duals,
canonical duals, and approximate duals from a lattice
\Lambda
to the sheared lattice
S_{C}\Lambda
.
Third, we obtain an explicit class of compactly supported smooth non-separable windows on lower
block-triangular lattices of the form

\Lambda_{A,B,C}=\left\{(Ak,CAk+B\ell):k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right\}=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
CA&B\end{pmatrix}\mathbb{Z}^{2d},\qquad C=C^{T},

where
A=\operatorname{diag}(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})
and
B=\operatorname{diag}(b_{1},\dots,b_{d})
are positive
diagonal matrices. Thus the time component is sampled on the rectangular lattice
A\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, while the
frequency component is sheared by the term
CAk
before adding the rectangular frequency sampling

B\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. When
C=0
, one recovers the separable lattice
A\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times B\mathbb{Z}^{d}
; when
C\neq 0
, the
time and frequency coordinates are coupled.
Fourth, we quantify the effect of the chirp deformation on time-frequency covariance and show that the
resulting non-separability changes the geometry of the atoms without changing the frame stability
constants or the deterministic and statistical robustness bounds.

1.2
Position of the present paper within the literature

The present work is motivated by several complementary strands in the Gabor-frame literature.

The first strand is the constructive theory of Gabor frames and explicit window design. The constructive
philosophy goes back to the classical paper of Daubechies, Grossmann, and Meyer
(
6
)
, where explicit
and implementable nonorthogonal expansions were emphasized. For the general Hilbert-space frame background,
including frame operators and canonical duals, we refer to
4
. In higher dimension,
explicit smooth compactly supported windows for multivariate Gabor frames were constructed by Pfander,
Rashkov, and Wang
(
10
)
. Their geometric viewpoint is one of the main motivations for the present
paper. Our construction is different in scope: instead of building windows directly from fundamental domains,
we start from separable dual pairs and transport them through a unitary chirp deformation.

A second relevant strand is the symplectic and metaplectic viewpoint in time-frequency analysis.
The chirp factor

e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}

is one of the elementary metaplectic building blocks, and the associated shear

(x,\omega)\mapsto(x,\omega+Cx)

is a simple symplectic transformation. Recent work on metaplectic Gabor frames provides a broad framework in
which Gabor-type systems are generated from metaplectic time-frequency representations
(
5
)
.
The present paper may be viewed as a concrete and explicitly computable subcase of this general philosophy:
we do not develop a full metaplectic theory, but we exploit one chirp shear to obtain non-separable windows
while preserving compact support, smoothness, frame bounds, and reconstruction errors.

A third strand concerns the control of dual windows. Exact compactly supported dual windows were studied in
detail by Stoeva
(
11
)
, while approximately dual Gabor systems and almost perfect reconstruction were
developed by Christensen, Janssen, Kim, and Kim
(
3
)
. From a more computational perspective,
Werther, Eldar, and Subbanna
(
12
)
studied dual Gabor frames different from the canonical dual and
emphasized the role of alternative duals in reducing computational complexity and improving conditioning.
Our approach is complementary to these works: we do not construct a new dual by solving an additional
optimization or inversion problem, but rather show that exact and approximate duality can be transported
unitarily from a separable model to a non-separable one.

A closely related finite-dimensional perspective has recently been developed by

1
, who study finite Gabor systems whose frame-operator matrices are unitarily equivalent, through explicit and computationally efficient transformations, to block-diagonal matrices. In particular, they show that certain finite Gabor systems indexed by Cartesian products of subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
admit such a block structure, which makes the inversion of the frame operator substantially more tractable. Although our setting is the continuous Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, their results provide a useful finite-dimensional counterpart to the computational question of how frame-operator structure can simplify reconstruction. In contrast, the present paper does not diagonalize the frame operator directly; instead, it uses chirp covariance to transport duality and frame-operator properties from a separable lattice to a sheared, non-separable one.

A further relevant perspective is provided by multi-window and Banach-space theories. The work of Balan,
Christensen, Krishtal, Okoudjou, and Romero
(
2
)
establishes stability and reconstruction results
for multi-window Gabor frames in Wiener amalgam spaces, showing that operator-algebra methods preserve
localization properties of dual systems. Rational and mixed rational subspace multi-window Gabor frames were
studied in
(
14
;
13
)
, while Gabor frame sets for subspaces were analyzed in
(
9
)
. These works
are not used directly in the proofs below, but they indicate a broader framework in which explicit dual
constructions and stability of reconstruction remain highly relevant.

Finally, for Gaussian windows in several variables, Gröchenig
(
8
)
showed that the multivariate frame
problem is closely related to sampling of entire functions in Bargmann–Fock spaces of several complex variables.
This Gaussian program is conceptually different from the present compactly supported constructive approach.
For the optimization of Gaussian frame bounds on separable lattices, see also
(
7
)
. A natural long-term
direction is to understand whether the chirp-transport philosophy developed here can interact with Gaussian
and Bargmann–Fock methods, especially in the study of non-separable lattices and lattice-shape optimization.

2
Preliminaries and notation

Throughout the paper, for
x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote by

T_{x}f(t):=f(t-x),\qquad M_{\omega}f(t):=e^{2\pi i\omega\cdot t}f(t),

and we write

\pi(x,\omega):=M_{\omega}T_{x}.

Given a lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
and a window
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we consider the Gabor system

\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda):=\{\pi(\lambda)g:\lambda\in\Lambda\}.

We say that
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
is a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if there exist constants

0<A\leq B<\infty
such that

A\|f\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|\langle f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|f\|_{2}^{2},\qquad\forall f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

If
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
is a frame, its frame operator is

S_{g,\Lambda}f:=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle\,\pi(\lambda)g,\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,.

Since
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
is a frame,
S_{g,\Lambda}
is bounded, positive, self-adjoint and invertible
(
4
)
. The canonical dual window is
\tilde{g}:=S_{g,\Lambda}^{-1}g
.

Definition 1
.

Let
g,\gamma\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
be a lattice.
We say that
\mathcal{G}(\gamma,\Lambda)
is a
dual Gabor frame
of

\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
if

f=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle f,\pi(\lambda)\gamma\rangle\,\pi(\lambda)g=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle\,\pi(\lambda)\gamma,\qquad\forall f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

with convergence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Definition 2
.

Let
g,\gamma\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
be a lattice.
We define the mixed reconstruction operator

\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda}f:=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle\,\pi(\lambda)\gamma.

The pair
(\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda),\mathcal{G}(\gamma,\Lambda))
is called an

approximately dual pair
if

\|I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda}\|<1\,,

where
\|\cdot\|
is the operator norm on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Remark 3

(Operator norm and reconstruction error)
.

For an approximately dual pair
(\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda),\mathcal{G}(\gamma,\Lambda))
, the quantity

\|I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda}\|

will be referred to as the
reconstruction error
(or
approximation error
), since it controls the
deviation of the mixed reconstruction operator
\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda}
from the identity.

The class of non-separable lattices considered in this paper is generated by lower block-triangular matrices. Let

A=\operatorname{diag}(a_{1},\dots,a_{d}),\qquad B=\operatorname{diag}(b_{1},\dots,b_{d}),\qquad a_{j},b_{j}>0,

and let
C\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be symmetric. We define

\Lambda_{A,B,C}:=\left\{(Ak,\,CAk+B\ell):k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right\}=\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
CA&B\end{pmatrix}\mathbb{Z}^{2d}.

When
C=0
, the lattice is separable:

\Lambda_{A,B,0}=A\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times B\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Definition 4
.

A function
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
is called
separable
if there exist one-variable functions

g_{1},\dots,g_{d}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
such that such that

g(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=\prod_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}(x_{j}).

Otherwise
g
is called
non-separable
.

3
Tensor-product dual pairs on separable lattices

In this section we use the standard Hilbert tensor product

\bigotimes_{j=1}^{d}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

defined as the completion of finite linear combinations of simple tensors with respect to the inner product

\left\langle\bigotimes_{j=1}^{d}f_{j},\bigotimes_{j=1}^{d}h_{j}\right\rangle:=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\langle f_{j},h_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

Thus, for a simple tensor one has

\left\|\bigotimes_{j=1}^{d}f_{j}\right\|^{2}_{\bigotimes_{j=1}^{d}L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

We shall use the canonical unitary identification

\bigotimes_{j=1}^{d}L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cong L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

under which

f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d}\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\left[(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\mapsto\prod_{j=1}^{d}f_{j}(x_{j})\right].

Indeed, by Fubini’s theorem,

\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{d}f_{j}(x_{j})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}|f_{j}(x_{j})|^{2}\,dx_{1}\cdots dx_{d}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

By density, this identification extends from simple tensors to the completed Hilbert tensor product.
Throughout the paper we use this identification without further comment.

We first record the elementary tensorization principle that will serve as the starting point
for our constructions.

Proposition 5
.

For each
j=1,\dots,d
, let
a_{j},b_{j}>0
and let
g_{j},\gamma_{j}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be such that

\mathcal{G}(g_{j},a_{j}\mathbb{Z}\times b_{j}\mathbb{Z})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{G}(\gamma_{j},a_{j}\mathbb{Z}\times b_{j}\mathbb{Z})

are dual Gabor frames for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Define
g
and
\gamma
in
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{d}L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
as a simple tensors (tensor product of d functions):

g:=g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{d},\qquad\gamma:=\gamma_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\gamma_{d}.

Then
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda_{A,B,0})
and
\mathcal{G}(\gamma,\Lambda_{A,B,0})
are dual Gabor frames for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the sense that:
g(x_{1},...,x_{d})=g_{1}(x_{1})\cdot\cdot\cdot g_{d}(x_{d})
. For simple tensor the norm assume this form:

\left\|g\right\|^{2}_{\bigotimes_{i=1}^{d}L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left\|g_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\left\|g\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(1)

If, in addition, each
g_{j},\gamma_{j}
belongs to
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, then

g,\gamma\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

For
\lambda=(Ak,B\ell)\in\Lambda_{A,B,0}
, one has

\pi(\lambda)g=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{d}\big(M_{\ell_{j}b_{j}}T_{k_{j}a_{j}}g_{j}\big),\qquad\pi(\lambda)\gamma=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{d}\big(M_{\ell_{j}b_{j}}T_{k_{j}a_{j}}\gamma_{j}\big).

Let
f=f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d}
be a simple tensor. Then

\langle f,\pi(Ak,B\ell)\gamma\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\langle f_{j},M_{\ell_{j}b_{j}}T_{k_{j}a_{j}}\gamma_{j}\rangle.

Hence, for rectangular partial sums,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|k_{j}|\leq N\\
|\ell_{j}|\leq N\end{subarray}}\langle f,\pi(Ak,B\ell)\gamma\rangle\,\pi(Ak,B\ell)g=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{d}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|m|\leq N\\
|n|\leq N\end{subarray}}\langle f_{j},M_{nb_{j}}T_{ma_{j}}\gamma_{j}\rangle\,M_{nb_{j}}T_{ma_{j}}g_{j}\right).

Passing to the limit as
N\to\infty
and using the one-dimensional duality for each factor, we obtain

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{A,B,0}}\langle f,\pi(\lambda)\gamma\rangle\,\pi(\lambda)g=f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d}=f.

The same argument yields the reverse reconstruction formula. Since finite linear combinations of simple tensors are dense in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it remains to justify
that the reconstruction operators are bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, so that the identities extend by continuity.
Let

\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda_{A,B,0}}f:=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{A,B,0}}\langle f,\pi(\lambda)\gamma\rangle\,\pi(\lambda)g,\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and define analogously
\Theta_{\gamma,g,\Lambda_{A,B,0}}
with the roles of
g
and
\gamma
interchanged.
Because
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda_{A,B,0})
and
\mathcal{G}(\gamma,\Lambda_{A,B,0})
are Bessel systems,
there exist constants
B_{g},B_{\gamma}<\infty
such that

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{A,B,0}}|\langle f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle|^{2}\leq B_{g}\|f\|_{2}^{2},\qquad\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{A,B,0}}|\langle f,\pi(\lambda)\gamma\rangle|^{2}\leq B_{\gamma}\|f\|_{2}^{2},\qquad\forall f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Fix
f,h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, by Cauchy–Schwarz in
\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{A,B,0})
,

\displaystyle\big|\langle\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda_{A,B,0}}f,h\rangle\big|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{A,B,0}}\langle f,\pi(\lambda)\gamma\rangle\,\langle\pi(\lambda)g,h\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{A,B,0}}|\langle f,\pi(\lambda)\gamma\rangle|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{A,B,0}}|\langle h,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{B_{\gamma}}\,\|f\|_{2}\;\sqrt{B_{g}}\,\|h\|_{2}.

Taking the supremum over
\|h\|_{2}=1
yields

\|\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda_{A,B,0}}f\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{B_{g}B_{\gamma}}\,\|f\|_{2},\qquad\forall f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

hence
\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda_{A,B,0}}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The same argument shows that

\Theta_{\gamma,g,\Lambda_{A,B,0}}
is bounded. We have proved on simple tensors that
\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda_{A,B,0}}f=f
and

\Theta_{\gamma,g,\Lambda_{A,B,0}}f=f
. By density of finite linear combinations of simple tensors and
boundedness of the reconstruction operators, both identities extend to all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The regularity statement is immediate because finite tensor products of compactly supported
smooth functions are again compactly supported and smooth.
∎

4
Chirp deformations and sheared lattices

The use of chirp and metaplectic deformations is consistent with the symplectic-geometric viewpoint that already plays an important role in higher-dimensional Gabor analysis; compare in particular
(
10
)
. In the present paper we restrict ourselves to a concrete chirp model because it allows a fully explicit transfer of support, smoothness, duality, and reconstruction estimates.

We now pass from separable lattices to non-separable ones through a simple metaplectic model,
namely chirp multiplication.

Definition 7
.

Let
C\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be symmetric. The associated chirp operator

U_{C}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by

(U_{C}f)(t):=e^{\pi i\,t^{T}Ct}f(t).

Since
|e^{\pi i\,t^{T}Ct}|=1
, the operator
U_{C}
is unitary on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Moreover,
U_{C}
preserves compact support, smoothness, and the Schwartz class.

Lemma 8

(Covariance under chirp)
.

Let
C\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be symmetric, and define

S_{C}(x,\omega):=(x,\omega+Cx),\qquad(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

Then, for all
x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\pi(S_{C}(x,\omega))\,U_{C}=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,U_{C}\,\pi(x,\omega).

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

\displaystyle\big(\pi(S_{C}(x,\omega))U_{C}f\big)(t)

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi i(\omega+Cx)\cdot t}\,(U_{C}f)(t-x)

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi i(\omega+Cx)\cdot t}\,e^{\pi i(t-x)^{T}C(t-x)}f(t-x).

Because
C
is symmetric,

(t-x)^{T}C(t-x)=t^{T}Ct-2x^{T}Ct+x^{T}Cx.

Therefore

\displaystyle\big(\pi(S_{C}(x,\omega))U_{C}f\big)(t)

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi i\omega\cdot t}e^{2\pi ix^{T}Ct}e^{\pi it^{T}Ct}e^{-2\pi ix^{T}Ct}e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}f(t-x)

\displaystyle=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}e^{\pi it^{T}Ct}e^{2\pi i\omega\cdot t}f(t-x)

\displaystyle=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,(U_{C}\pi(x,\omega)f)(t).

This proves the claim.
∎

Proposition 9

(Transport of frame property and duality)
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
be a lattice, let
C
be symmetric, and put

S_{C}\Lambda:=\{S_{C}\lambda:\lambda\in\Lambda\}.

If
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
is a Gabor frame with frame bounds
A,B
, then

\mathcal{G}(U_{C}g,S_{C}\Lambda)
is a Gabor frame with the same bounds.

If
\mathcal{G}(\gamma,\Lambda)
is a dual Gabor frame of
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
, then

\mathcal{G}(U_{C}\gamma,S_{C}\Lambda)
is a dual Gabor frame of
\mathcal{G}(U_{C}g,S_{C}\Lambda)
.

Proof.

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\lambda=(x,\omega)\in\Lambda
, Lemma
8
implies

\langle f,\pi(S_{C}\lambda)U_{C}g\rangle=e^{-\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,\langle U_{C}^{-1}f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle.

Hence

\sum_{\mu\in S_{C}\Lambda}|\langle f,\pi(\mu)U_{C}g\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|\langle U_{C}^{-1}f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle|^{2}.

Since
U_{C}
is unitary,
\|U_{C}^{-1}f\|_{2}=\|f\|_{2}
, and the frame inequalities for

\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
immediately yield the same inequalities for

\mathcal{G}(U_{C}g,S_{C}\Lambda)
.

Assume now that
\mathcal{G}(\gamma,\Lambda)
is dual to
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
.
For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\in S_{C}\Lambda}\langle f,\pi(\mu)U_{C}\gamma\rangle\,\pi(\mu)U_{C}g

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle f,\pi(S_{C}\lambda)U_{C}\gamma\rangle\,\pi(S_{C}\lambda)U_{C}g

\displaystyle=U_{C}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle U_{C}^{-1}f,\pi(\lambda)\gamma\rangle\,\pi(\lambda)g\right),

where the unimodular phase factors cancel. Since the inner sum equals
U_{C}^{-1}f
by duality on
\Lambda
,
the whole expression equals
f
. The reverse reconstruction identity is analogous.
∎

Corollary 10
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
9
,

S_{U_{C}g,S_{C}\Lambda}=U_{C}\,S_{g,\Lambda}\,U_{C}^{-1}.

Consequently,

\widetilde{(U_{C}g)}=U_{C}\,\widetilde{g}.

Proof.

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the proof of Proposition
9
yields

S_{U_{C}g,S_{C}\Lambda}f=U_{C}\big(S_{g,\Lambda}(U_{C}^{-1}f)\big).

Thus

S_{U_{C}g,S_{C}\Lambda}=U_{C}\,S_{g,\Lambda}\,U_{C}^{-1}.

Taking inverses and applying the resulting identity to
U_{C}g
, we obtain

S_{U_{C}g,S_{C}\Lambda}^{-1}(U_{C}g)=U_{C}S_{g,\Lambda}^{-1}g=U_{C}\widetilde{g}.

∎

5
Explicit compactly supported dual pairs on non-separable lattices

We now combine the tensor-product construction with the chirp transport principle.
This yields an explicit family of compactly supported smooth windows on non-separable lattices.

Theorem 11
.

For each
j=1,\dots,d
, let
a_{j},b_{j}>0
and let
g_{j},\gamma_{j}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be such that

\mathcal{G}(g_{j},a_{j}\mathbb{Z}\times b_{j}\mathbb{Z})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{G}(\gamma_{j},a_{j}\mathbb{Z}\times b_{j}\mathbb{Z})

are dual Gabor frames for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Let
A=\operatorname{diag}(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})
,
B=\operatorname{diag}(b_{1},\dots,b_{d})
,
and let
C\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be symmetric.

Define

g(x):=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}\prod_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}(x_{j}),\qquad\gamma(x):=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}(x_{j}),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then the Gabor systems
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda_{A,B,C})
and

\mathcal{G}(\gamma,\Lambda_{A,B,C})
are dual Gabor frames for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Moreover,

g,\gamma\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proof.

Set

g^{(0)}:=g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{d},\qquad\gamma^{(0)}:=\gamma_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\gamma_{d}.

By Proposition
5
, the Gabor systems

\mathcal{G}(g^{(0)},\Lambda_{A,B,0})
and
\mathcal{G}(\gamma^{(0)},\Lambda_{A,B,0})

are dual Gabor frames for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Next observe that

\Lambda_{A,B,C}=S_{C}(\Lambda_{A,B,0}),

because

S_{C}(Ak,B\ell)=(Ak,\,CAk+B\ell).

Also, by definition,

U_{C}g^{(0)}(x)=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}\prod_{j=1}^{d}g_{j}(x_{j})=g(x),

and similarly
U_{C}\gamma^{(0)}=\gamma
.

Applying Proposition
9
with
\Lambda=\Lambda_{A,B,0}
,
we conclude that
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda_{A,B,C})
and

\mathcal{G}(\gamma,\Lambda_{A,B,C})
are dual Gabor frames.

Finally, multiplication by the smooth unimodular factor
e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}
preserves
compact support and smoothness, hence
g,\gamma\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
∎

The previous theorem already yields a wide class of explicit higher-dimensional non-separable windows.
The next proposition shows that the coupling matrix
C
genuinely creates non-separability.

Proposition 12
.

Assume that
C=(c_{jk})_{j,k=1}^{d}
is symmetric and that
c_{jk}\neq 0
for some
j\neq k
.
Assume moreover that each one-dimensional factor
g_{m}
is nonzero on some nonempty open interval
I_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}
.
Then the function

g(x)=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}\prod_{m=1}^{d}g_{m}(x_{m}),\qquad x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

cannot be written in the form

g(x)=\prod_{m=1}^{d}h_{m}(x_{m})

for one-variable functions
h_{m}
. In particular,
g
is non-separable.

Proof.

Suppose, by contradiction, that there exist one-variable functions
h_{1},\dots,h_{d}
such that

g(x)=\prod_{m=1}^{d}h_{m}(x_{m}),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Choose indices
j\neq k
such that
c_{jk}\neq 0
.
By assumption, for each
m=1,\dots,d
there exists a nonempty open interval
I_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}
on which
g_{m}
does not vanish.
Fix arbitrary points
\xi_{m}\in I_{m}
for all
m\neq j,k
.

For
u\in I_{j}
and
v\in I_{k}
, define

x(u,v):=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

by setting

x_{j}=u,\qquad x_{k}=v,\qquad x_{m}=\xi_{m}\quad\text{for }m\neq j,k.

Since
g
is assumed separable, the two-variable function

F(u,v):=g(x(u,v))

must have the form

F(u,v)=A\,h_{j}(u)h_{k}(v),\qquad A:=\prod_{m\neq j,k}h_{m}(\xi_{m}),

and therefore it satisfies the multiplicative identity

F(u_{1},v_{1})F(u_{2},v_{2})=F(u_{1},v_{2})F(u_{2},v_{1})\qquad\text{for all }u_{1},u_{2}\in I_{j},\ v_{1},v_{2}\in I_{k}.

We now compute the same quantity using the explicit formula for
g
.

Set

G_{0}:=\prod_{m\neq j,k}g_{m}(\xi_{m}).

Then, for
u\in I_{j}
and
v\in I_{k}
,

F(u,v)=e^{\pi i\,x(u,v)^{T}Cx(u,v)}\,G_{0}\,g_{j}(u)g_{k}(v).

Hence

\displaystyle F(u_{1},v_{1})F(u_{2},v_{2})-F(u_{1},v_{2})F(u_{2},v_{1})

\displaystyle=G_{0}^{2}\,g_{j}(u_{1})g_{j}(u_{2})g_{k}(v_{1})g_{k}(v_{2})\Big(e^{\pi i\,[x(u_{1},v_{1})^{T}Cx(u_{1},v_{1})+x(u_{2},v_{2})^{T}Cx(u_{2},v_{2})]}

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0001pt-e^{\pi i\,[x(u_{1},v_{2})^{T}Cx(u_{1},v_{2})+x(u_{2},v_{1})^{T}Cx(u_{2},v_{1})]}\Big).

Since each
g_{m}
is nonzero on
I_{m}
, the prefactor

G_{0}^{2}\,g_{j}(u_{1})g_{j}(u_{2})g_{k}(v_{1})g_{k}(v_{2})

is nonzero. Therefore the multiplicative identity would force

e^{\pi i\,[x(u_{1},v_{1})^{T}Cx(u_{1},v_{1})+x(u_{2},v_{2})^{T}Cx(u_{2},v_{2})]}=e^{\pi i\,[x(u_{1},v_{2})^{T}Cx(u_{1},v_{2})+x(u_{2},v_{1})^{T}Cx(u_{2},v_{1})]}.

Equivalently,

e^{\pi i\Delta}=1,

where

\Delta:=x(u_{1},v_{1})^{T}Cx(u_{1},v_{1})+x(u_{2},v_{2})^{T}Cx(u_{2},v_{2})-x(u_{1},v_{2})^{T}Cx(u_{1},v_{2})-x(u_{2},v_{1})^{T}Cx(u_{2},v_{1}).

Because

x^{T}Cx=\sum_{r=1}^{d}c_{rr}x_{r}^{2}+2\sum_{1\leq r<s\leq d}c_{rs}x_{r}x_{s},

all terms depending only on one coordinate cancel in the alternating sum defining
\Delta
, and so do all mixed terms except the
(j,k)
-term. Thus

\Delta=2c_{jk}\big(u_{1}v_{1}+u_{2}v_{2}-u_{1}v_{2}-u_{2}v_{1}\big)=2c_{jk}(u_{1}-u_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2}).

Therefore separability would imply

e^{2\pi i\,c_{jk}(u_{1}-u_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2})}=1\qquad\text{for all }u_{1},u_{2}\in I_{j},\ v_{1},v_{2}\in I_{k}.

We now choose
u_{1},u_{2}\in I_{j}
and
v_{1},v_{2}\in I_{k}
such that

0<\big|c_{jk}(u_{1}-u_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2})\big|<1.

This is possible because
I_{j}
and
I_{k}
are open intervals and
c_{jk}\neq 0
.
For such a choice, the real number
c_{jk}(u_{1}-u_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2})
is nonzero and cannot be an integer, hence

e^{2\pi i\,c_{jk}(u_{1}-u_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2})}\neq 1,

a contradiction. Therefore
g
cannot be written as a product of one-variable functions. In particular,
g
is non-separable.
∎

Remark 13
.

Theorem

should be viewed as a constructive complement to the

Its strength is that once an exact dual pair is available on a separable lattice, the passage to the

6
Approximate duals and approximation of the canonical dual

This section is motivated by two complementary ideas from the literature. First, exact compactly supported dual windows may be approximated in a controlled way, while remaining within the class of exact duals, as shown in
(
11
)
. Second, one may also work with approximately dual systems and quantify the resulting reconstruction error, as in
(
3
)
. The point of the next results is that chirp transport preserves both mechanisms without loss.

We now show that the same chirp transport mechanism preserves not only exact duality,
but also the quality of approximate reconstruction.

Proposition 14

(Transport of approximate duals)
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
be a lattice and let
C
be symmetric.
If
(\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda),\mathcal{G}(\gamma,\Lambda))
is an approximately dual pair, then

(\mathcal{G}(U_{C}g,S_{C}\Lambda),\mathcal{G}(U_{C}\gamma,S_{C}\Lambda))

is also an approximately dual pair, with exactly the same reconstruction error:

\|I-\Theta_{U_{C}g,U_{C}\gamma,S_{C}\Lambda}\|=\|I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda}\|\,,

according to Remark
3
.

Proof.

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, arguing as in the proof of Proposition
9
, one finds

\Theta_{U_{C}g,U_{C}\gamma,S_{C}\Lambda}f=U_{C}\,\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda}\,U_{C}^{-1}f.

Therefore

I-\Theta_{U_{C}g,U_{C}\gamma,S_{C}\Lambda}=U_{C}\,(I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda})\,U_{C}^{-1}.

Since
U_{C}
is unitary, operator norm is preserved under conjugation, and the claim follows.
∎

The preceding proposition shows that any explicitly constructed approximate dual on a separable lattice
can be transferred to the corresponding sheared lattice without deterioration of the reconstruction error.

We next state a canonical-dual approximation result in the exact-dual regime.

Theorem 15

(Transport of exact-dual iterations)
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
be a lattice, let
C
be symmetric, and let

g,\gamma^{(0)}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
\mathcal{G}(\gamma^{(0)},\Lambda)
is a dual Gabor frame of

\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
.
Assume that
\rho>0
is such that

\|I-\rho S_{g,\Lambda}\|<1.

Define recursively

\gamma^{(n+1)}:=\rho g+(I-\rho S_{g,\Lambda})\gamma^{(n)},\qquad n\geq 0.

Set

g_{C}:=U_{C}g,\qquad\Gamma_{C}^{(n)}:=U_{C}\gamma^{(n)},\qquad\Lambda_{C}:=S_{C}\Lambda.

Then, for every
n\geq 0
,

\Gamma_{C}^{(n+1)}=\rho g_{C}+(I-\rho S_{g_{C},\Lambda_{C}})\Gamma_{C}^{(n)}.

Moreover, if each
\gamma^{(n)}
is an exact dual window of
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
, then each

\Gamma_{C}^{(n)}
is an exact dual window of
\mathcal{G}(g_{C},\Lambda_{C})
.
Moreover,

\gamma^{(n)}\to\widetilde{g}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Gamma_{C}^{(n)}\to\widetilde{g_{C}}\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Remark 16

(Neumann-series interpretation of the iteration)
.

The assumption
\|I-\rho S_{g,\Lambda}\|<1
is a standard sufficient condition ensuring that

S_{g,\Lambda}
is invertible and that its inverse admits a Neumann-series representation. Indeed,
set
R:=I-\rho S_{g,\Lambda}
. Then
\|R\|<1
implies that the series

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}R^{n}

converges in operator norm and satisfies
(I-R)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}R^{n}\right)=I
.
Since
I-R=\rho S_{g,\Lambda}
, it follows that

S_{g,\Lambda}^{-1}=\rho\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(I-\rho S_{g,\Lambda})^{n}\qquad\text{(convergence in operator norm).}

In particular, this representation identifies the expected fixed point of the iteration as the canonical
dual window
\widetilde{g}=S_{g,\Lambda}^{-1}g
. The convergence of the iteration to this fixed point will be
proved explicitly in the proof below.

Proof.

By Corollary
10
,

S_{g_{C},\Lambda_{C}}=U_{C}S_{g,\Lambda}U_{C}^{-1}.

Hence

\displaystyle\rho g_{C}+(I-\rho S_{g_{C},\Lambda_{C}})\Gamma_{C}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\rho U_{C}g+\big(I-\rho U_{C}S_{g,\Lambda}U_{C}^{-1}\big)U_{C}\gamma^{(n)}

\displaystyle=U_{C}\big(\rho g+(I-\rho S_{g,\Lambda})\gamma^{(n)}\big)

\displaystyle=U_{C}\gamma^{(n+1)}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{C}^{(n+1)}.

This proves the recursive identity.

If each
\gamma^{(n)}
is an exact dual of
g
on
\Lambda
, then Proposition

9
implies that each
U_{C}\gamma^{(n)}=\Gamma_{C}^{(n)}

is an exact dual of
U_{C}g=g_{C}
on
\Lambda_{C}
.

We show now that
\gamma^{(n)}
converge in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\tilde{g}
and
\tilde{g}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Rather than invoking the Banach-Caccioppoli fixed point theorem, we exploit the linear structure of the recursion and write the iterates explicitly. This gives a direct proof of convergence to the canonical dual window through the Neumann series. We prove directly the both things.

First we observe that the fixed point for the recursively is
\tilde{g}
, indeed if the limit exist

\lim\gamma^{(n+1)}=\lim\gamma^{(n)}=\bar{\gamma}

(2)

is the fixed point:

\bar{\gamma}=\rho g+(I-\rho S_{g,\Lambda})\bar{\gamma}=\rho g+\bar{\gamma}-\rho S_{g,\Lambda}\bar{\gamma}

(3)

that is equivalent to:

\rho g-\rho S_{g,\Lambda}\bar{\gamma}=0\iff S_{g,\Lambda}\bar{\gamma}=g

(4)

We observe that the error
R_{g,\Lambda}:=I-\rho S_{g,\Lambda}
for hypotesis have
\left\|R_{g,\Lambda}\right\|<1
, so as in Remark
16

S_{g,\Lambda}^{-1}=\rho(I-R_{g,\Lambda})^{-1}=\rho\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}R_{g,\Lambda}^{n}.

(5)

Since the inverse of
S_{g,\Lambda}
exists and
\bar{\gamma}=S^{-1}_{g,\Lambda}g
, the fixed point is exactly the dual
\tilde{g}=S^{-1}_{g,\Lambda}g=\rho\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}R_{g,\Lambda}^{n}g
with
R^{(0)}_{g,\Lambda}=I
.

Now we show the exact closed formula for the iteration if we start from a
\gamma^{(0)}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We define
Z_{n}(g):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}R_{g,\Lambda}^{k}g
the partial sum of the Neumann series.
The closed formula is:

\gamma^{(n)}=R^{n}_{g,\Lambda}\gamma^{(0)}+\rho Z_{n-1}(g)\;\;\forall n\geq 1

(6)

that tends to
\tilde{g}
for

since it is the fixed point, as said previously. The closed formula follows by a straightforward induction on

(equivalently, by solving first the
homogeneous recursion and then adding a particular solution through the usual variation-of-constants argument).
We now prove that
\gamma^{(n)}
converges to
\widetilde{g}
in

\begin{split}\left\|\gamma^{(n)}-\tilde{g}\right\|_{2}=\left\|R_{g,\Lambda}^{n}\gamma^{(0)}+\rho Z_{n-1}(g)-S_{g,\Lambda}^{-1}g\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|R_{g,\Lambda}^{n}\gamma^{(0)}\right\|_{2}+\rho\left\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}R_{g,\Lambda}^{k}g\right\|_{2}\\
\leq\left\|R_{g,\Lambda}^{n}\right\|\left\|\gamma^{(0)}\right\|_{2}+\rho\left\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}R_{g,\Lambda}^{k}g\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|R_{g,\Lambda}\right\|^{n}\left\|\gamma^{(0)}\right\|_{2}+\rho\left\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}R_{g,\Lambda}^{k}g\right\|_{2}\end{split}

(7)

and because

then for

\left\|\gamma^{(n)}-\tilde{g}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|R_{g,\Lambda}\right\|^{n}\left\|\gamma^{(0)}\right\|_{2}+\rho\left\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}R_{g,\Lambda}^{k}g\right\|_{2}\to 0

for

(

Finally, since

\Gamma^{(n)}_{C}=U_{C}\gamma^{(n)}=U_{C}R^{n}_{g,\Lambda}\gamma^{(0)}+\rho U_{C}Z_{n-1}(g),\;\;\forall n\geq 1

and tend to

7
Further structural remarks

The results above isolate a class of non-separable higher-dimensional Gabor systems for which one can
retain three desirable features simultaneously:

(i)

explicit formulas for the generating window and a dual window;

(ii)

preservation of compact support and smoothness;

(iii)

direct transfer of exact and approximate reconstruction properties from a separable model.

From a geometric viewpoint, the lattice
\Lambda_{A,B,C}
is already outside the purely tensor-product
setting as soon as
C\neq 0
, while the associated window becomes genuinely non-separable as soon as

C
has a nonzero off-diagonal entry. At the same time, the proofs remain transparent because the deformation
is implemented by a concrete unitary operator.

It is also worth emphasizing that the present strategy is compatible with several possible sources for the
initial separable dual pair:

•

one-dimensional compactly supported exact dual windows;

•

tensor products of such pairs;

•

higher-dimensional separable windows obtained by geometric constructions on product lattices.

Thus the method is not tied to a single explicit family of windows.

Although the present paper is formulated entirely on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the constructive control of dual windows obtained here is also compatible with broader stability questions in function spaces adapted to time-frequency analysis. In this direction, the Banach-space and operator-algebra approach of
(
2
)
suggests that explicit dual constructions may have consequences beyond the Hilbert-space level.

8
Quantitative effects of chirp-induced non-separability:
time–frequency coupling, stability, and robustness

This section complements the constructive results above by providing three quantitative
viewpoints on chirp-generated non-separable windows:
(i) a phase-space covariance formalism that makes non-separability
measurable
;
(ii) a precise comparison between atom-level localization and frame-level stability within
our transport class;
(iii) deterministic and statistical reconstruction error bounds under coefficient noise,
in both exact-dual and approximately-dual regimes.

Throughout,
d\geq 1
is fixed. We work with the time–frequency shifts

\pi(x,\omega)=M_{\omega}T_{x}
and the chirp operator

(U_{C}f)(t)=e^{\pi i\,t^{T}Ct}f(t),\qquad C=C^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},

as in Section
4
.
We denote by
S_{C}:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}^{2d}
the associated shear map

S_{C}(x,\omega)=(x,\omega+Cx),\qquad S_{C}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
C&I\end{pmatrix},\qquad S_{C}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
-C&I\end{pmatrix}.

8.1
A phase-space covariance matrix and a separability defect

8.1.1
Short-time Fourier transform and the Moyal identity

Let
f,\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\phi\neq 0
. We define the short-time Fourier transform (STFT)

V_{\phi}f(x,\omega):=\langle f,\pi(x,\omega)\phi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t)\,\overline{\phi(t-x)}\,e^{-2\pi i\omega\cdot t}\,dt,\qquad(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

We now prove the Moyal identity in the present normalization:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|V_{\phi}f(x,\omega)|^{2}\,dx\,d\omega=\|f\|_{2}^{2}\,\|\phi\|_{2}^{2}.

(10)

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and consider
V_{\phi}f(x,\omega)
as a function of
\omega
. By the definition above,

\omega\mapsto V_{\phi}f(x,\omega)
is the Fourier transform (in the
\omega
-variable, with
2\pi
-convention)
of the function
t\mapsto f(t)\overline{\phi(t-x)}
. Hence, by Plancherel in
\omega
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V_{\phi}f(x,\omega)|^{2}\,d\omega=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(t)|^{2}\,|\phi(t-x)|^{2}\,dt.

Integrating in
x
and applying Fubini,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|V_{\phi}f(x,\omega)|^{2}\,dx\,d\omega

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(t)|^{2}\,|\phi(t-x)|^{2}\,dt\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(t)|^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\phi(t-x)|^{2}\,dx\right)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(t)|^{2}\,dt\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\phi(u)|^{2}\,du=\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|\phi\|_{2}^{2},

which is (
10
).
∎

8.1.2
A normalized phase-space energy distribution

Assume
\|f\|_{2}=\|\phi\|_{2}=1
(this is without loss of generality in this subsection, since the normalization
will be explicit). Define the nonnegative function

p_{f,\phi}(x,\omega):=|V_{\phi}f(x,\omega)|^{2}.

By (
10
),
p_{f,\phi}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}p_{f,\phi}=1
; hence

p_{f,\phi}
is a probability density on phase space.

We define the associated
phase-space mean
and
phase-space covariance matrix

whenever the second moments are finite:

m_{f,\phi}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}z\,p_{f,\phi}(z)\,dz\in\mathbb{R}^{2d},\qquad\Sigma_{f,\phi}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}(z-m_{f,\phi})(z-m_{f,\phi})^{T}\,p_{f,\phi}(z)\,dz\in\mathbb{R}^{2d\times 2d}.

(Here we write
z=(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
and
dz=dx\,d\omega
.)

Finite second moments in our compactly supported smooth setting.

In the present paper,
f
and
\phi
are typically in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
In that case,
x\mapsto V_{\phi}f(x,\omega)
has compact support uniformly in
\omega
:
indeed,
V_{\phi}f(x,\omega)
can be nonzero only if
\operatorname{supp}f\cap(x+\operatorname{supp}\phi)\neq\varnothing
,
i.e.
x\in\operatorname{supp}f-\operatorname{supp}\phi
, a compact set. Hence all
x
-moments are finite.
Moreover, for each fixed
x
, the function
t\mapsto f(t)\overline{\phi(t-x)}
is in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
so its Fourier transform in
\omega
decays faster than any polynomial; therefore all
\omega
-moments are finite.
Consequently
\Sigma_{f,\phi}
is well-defined for our windows.

8.1.3
Exact shear covariance of the STFT energy under chirp

We now show that the chirp deformation transports the phase-space density by the shear
S_{C}
.

Proposition 19

(STFT covariance under chirp)
.

Let
C=C^{T}
. For all
f,\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and all
(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
,

V_{U_{C}\phi}(U_{C}f)(x,\omega)=e^{-\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,V_{\phi}f\!\big(S^{-1}_{C}(x,\omega)\big)=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,V_{\phi}f(x,\omega-Cx).

(11)

In particular,

|V_{U_{C}\phi}(U_{C}f)(x,\omega)|^{2}=|V_{\phi}f(x,\omega-Cx)|^{2}=p_{f,\phi}(x,\omega-Cx).

(12)

Proof.

By definition of the STFT and unitarity of
U_{C}
,

V_{U_{C}\phi}(U_{C}f)(x,\omega)=\langle U_{C}f,\pi(x,\omega)U_{C}\phi\rangle=\langle f,U_{C}^{-1}\pi(x,\omega)U_{C}\,\phi\rangle.

From Lemma
8
we have, for all
(x,\omega)
,

\pi(S_{C}(x,\omega))\,U_{C}=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,U_{C}\,\pi(x,\omega).

Multiplying on the left by
U_{C}^{-1}
and on the right by
U_{C}^{-1}
yields

U_{C}^{-1}\pi(S_{C}(x,\omega))U_{C}=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,\pi(x,\omega).

Replacing
(x,\omega)
with
S_{C}^{-1}(x,\omega)
, we obtain

U_{C}^{-1}\pi(x,\omega)U_{C}=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,\pi(S_{C}^{-1}(x,\omega)).

Since
S_{C}^{-1}(x,\omega)=(x,\omega-Cx)
, this reads

U_{C}^{-1}\pi(x,\omega)U_{C}=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,\pi(x,\omega-Cx).

Substituting into the STFT expression gives

V_{U_{C}\phi}(U_{C}f)(x,\omega)=e^{-\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,V_{\phi}f(S^{-1}_{C}(x,\omega)),

which is (
11
). Taking moduli yields (
12
).
∎

8.1.4
How the phase-space covariance transforms, and a defect of separability

Assume
\|f\|_{2}=\|\phi\|_{2}=1
so that
p_{f,\phi}
is a probability density.
By (
12
), for the chirped pair
(U_{C}f,U_{C}\phi)
we have

p_{U_{C}f,U_{C}\phi}(z)=p_{f,\phi}(S_{C}^{-1}z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

(13)

Since
\det S_{C}=1
, the change of variables
z^{\prime}=S_{C}z
has Jacobian
1
.

Proposition 20

(Transformation rule for mean and covariance)
.

Assume
m_{f,\phi}
and
\Sigma_{f,\phi}
are well-defined. Then
m_{U_{C}f,U_{C}\phi}
and

\Sigma_{U_{C}f,U_{C}\phi}
are well-defined and satisfy

m_{U_{C}f,U_{C}\phi}=S_{C}m_{f,\phi},

(14)

and

\Sigma_{U_{C}f,U_{C}\phi}=S_{C}\,\Sigma_{f,\phi}\,S_{C}^{T}.

(15)

Proof.

From
p_{U_{C}f,U_{C}\phi}(z)=p_{f,\phi}(S_{C}^{-1}z)
, since
\det S_{C}=1
, also
\det S_{C}^{-1}=1
, therefore

m_{U_{C}f,U_{C}\phi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}z\,p_{f,\phi}(S_{C}^{-1}z)\,dz.

Set
z^{\prime}=S_{C}^{-1}z
, so that
z=S_{C}z^{\prime}
and
dz=dz^{\prime}
. Then

m_{U_{C}f,U_{C}\phi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}S_{C}z^{\prime}\,p_{f,\phi}(z^{\prime})\,dz^{\prime}=S_{C}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}z^{\prime}\,p_{f,\phi}(z^{\prime})\,dz^{\prime}=S_{C}m_{f,\phi},

which proves (
14
).
For the covariance, write
m:=m_{f,\phi}
and
m_{C}:=m_{U_{C}f,U_{C}\phi}=S_{C}m
.
Then

\Sigma_{U_{C}f,U_{C}\phi}=\int(z-m_{C})(z-m_{C})^{T}\,p_{f,\phi}(S_{C}^{-1}z)\,dz.

Apply the same change of variables
z^{\prime}=S_{C}^{-1}z
, so
z=S_{C}z^{\prime}
:

z-m_{C}=S_{C}z^{\prime}-S_{C}m=S_{C}(z^{\prime}-m),

hence

(z-m_{C})(z-m_{C})^{T}=S_{C}(z^{\prime}-m)(z^{\prime}-m)^{T}S_{C}^{T}.

Therefore,

\Sigma_{U_{C}f,U_{C}\phi}=\int S_{C}(z^{\prime}-m)(z^{\prime}-m)^{T}S_{C}^{T}\,p_{f,\phi}(z^{\prime})\,dz^{\prime}

=S_{C}\left(\int(z^{\prime}-m)(z^{\prime}-m)^{T}p_{f,\phi}(z^{\prime})\,dz^{\prime}\right)S_{C}^{T}=S_{C}\,\Sigma_{f,\phi}\,S_{C}^{T},

which proves (
15
).
∎

To highlight how chirp produces
coupling
, we block-decompose the covariance matrix as

\Sigma_{f,\phi}=\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{xx}&\Sigma_{x\omega}\\
\Sigma_{\omega x}&\Sigma_{\omega\omega}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\Sigma_{xx},\Sigma_{x\omega},\Sigma_{\omega x},\Sigma_{\omega\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}.

Using
S_{C}=\big(\!\begin{smallmatrix}I&0\\
C&I\end{smallmatrix}\!\big)
, a direct block-matrix multiplication yields:

\Sigma_{U_{C}f,U_{C}\phi}=\begin{pmatrix}\Sigma_{xx}&\Sigma_{x\omega}+\Sigma_{xx}C^{T}\\[2.84526pt]
\Sigma_{\omega x}+C\Sigma_{xx}&\Sigma_{\omega\omega}+C\Sigma_{x\omega}+\Sigma_{\omega x}C^{T}+C\Sigma_{xx}C^{T}\end{pmatrix}.

(16)

Definition 21

(A separability/coupling defect)
.

Assume
\Sigma_{f,\phi}
exists. We define the
time–frequency coupling defect
of
(f,\phi)
as

\mathsf{D}(f,\phi):=\|\Sigma_{x\omega}\|_{\mathrm{F}},

where
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{F}}
is the Frobenius norm.
When
\mathsf{D}(f,\phi)=0
, the phase-space covariance has no
x
–
\omega
cross-terms
(with respect to the chosen STFT window
\phi
), which is a natural covariance-level notion of

no time–frequency tilt
.

Corollary 22

(Chirp creates coupling from a decoupled baseline)
.

Assume
\Sigma_{f,\phi}
exists and
\Sigma_{x\omega}=0
.
Then for every symmetric
C
,

\mathsf{D}(U_{C}f,U_{C}\phi)=\|\Sigma_{xx}C^{T}\|_{\mathrm{F}}.

In particular, if
\Sigma_{xx}
is positive definite and
C\neq 0
, then
\mathsf{D}(U_{C}f,U_{C}\phi)>0
,
so the chirped pair exhibits genuine time–frequency coupling at the covariance level.

Proof.

If
\Sigma_{x\omega}=0
, then by (
16
) the new cross-block is

\Sigma_{x\omega}^{(C)}=\Sigma_{xx}C^{T}
. Taking Frobenius norms gives the claim.
If
\Sigma_{xx}
is positive definite, then
\Sigma_{xx}C^{T}=0
implies
C=0
, hence for
C\neq 0

we have
\|\Sigma_{xx}C^{T}\|_{\mathrm{F}}>0
.
∎

Remark 23

(Interpretation for separable windows)
.

If
f
and
\phi
are separable (tensor products) and are centered/symmetric so that the cross-moments vanish,
then
\Sigma_{x\omega}=0
is natural: the corresponding phase-space distribution has axes aligned with the coordinate
directions. Corollary
22
shows that the chirp factor
e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}
produces a
tilt

controlled explicitly by
C
, making non-separability measurable by
\mathsf{D}
.

8.2
Localization versus frame stability: invariants and non-implications

8.2.1
Frame bounds are invariant under chirp transport

We rephrase the invariance established earlier in a form that will be used below.
Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
be a lattice. Define the analysis and synthesis operators

C_{g,\Lambda}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\ell^{2}(\Lambda),\qquad C_{g,\Lambda}f:=\big(\langle f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle\big)_{\lambda\in\Lambda},

D_{g,\Lambda}:\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\qquad D_{g,\Lambda}c:=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}c_{\lambda}\,\pi(\lambda)g,

(where the synthesis sum converges unconditionally in
L^{2}
when
c\in\ell^{2}
and
g
is Bessel).
Then the frame operator is
S_{g,\Lambda}=D_{g,\Lambda}C_{g,\Lambda}
and the mixed reconstruction operator is

\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda}=D_{\gamma,\Lambda}C_{g,\Lambda}
.

Proposition 24

(Conjugacy of analysis/synthesis and stability constants)
.

Let
C=C^{T}
and
\Lambda_{C}:=S_{C}\Lambda
. Then

\|C_{U_{C}g,\Lambda_{C}}\|=\|C_{g,\Lambda}\|,\qquad\|D_{U_{C}g,\Lambda_{C}}\|=\|D_{g,\Lambda}\|.

In particular, the optimal Bessel bound of
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
equals that of
\mathcal{G}(U_{C}g,\Lambda_{C})
.
Moreover, if
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
is a frame with bounds
A,B
, then
\mathcal{G}(U_{C}g,\Lambda_{C})
is a frame with the same bounds.

Proof.

Fix
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\lambda=(x,\omega)\in\Lambda
. By Lemma
8
,

\pi(S_{C}\lambda)\,U_{C}=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,U_{C}\,\pi(\lambda).

Taking adjoints and using unitarity gives

U_{C}^{-1}\pi(S_{C}\lambda)^{*}=e^{-\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,\pi(\lambda)^{*}U_{C}^{-1}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\langle f,\pi(S_{C}\lambda)U_{C}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\pi(S_{C}\lambda)^{*}f,U_{C}g\rangle=\langle U_{C}^{-1}\pi(S_{C}\lambda)^{*}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,\langle\pi(\lambda)^{*}U_{C}^{-1}f,g\rangle=e^{-\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,\langle U_{C}^{-1}f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle.

Taking moduli,

|\langle f,\pi(S_{C}\lambda)U_{C}g\rangle|=|\langle U_{C}^{-1}f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle|.

Therefore,

\|C_{U_{C}g,\Lambda_{C}}f\|_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{C})}^{2}=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda_{C}}|\langle f,\pi(\mu)U_{C}g\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|\langle U_{C}^{-1}f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle|^{2}=\|C_{g,\Lambda}(U_{C}^{-1}f)\|_{\ell^{2}(\Lambda)}^{2}.

Taking suprema over
\|f\|_{2}=1
and using
\|U_{C}^{-1}f\|_{2}=\|f\|_{2}
, we obtain

\|C_{U_{C}g,\Lambda_{C}}\|=\|C_{g,\Lambda}\|.

For the synthesis operator, let
c\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
and define
(c_{C})_{\mu}:=c_{\lambda}
when
\mu=S_{C}\lambda
.
Then
c\mapsto c_{C}
is an isometric identification
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\cong\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{C})
and

D_{U_{C}g,\Lambda_{C}}c_{C}=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda_{C}}(c_{C})_{\mu}\,\pi(\mu)U_{C}g=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}c_{\lambda}\,\pi(S_{C}\lambda)U_{C}g.

Using Lemma
8
again,

\pi(S_{C}\lambda)U_{C}g=e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,U_{C}\pi(\lambda)g,

hence

D_{U_{C}g,\Lambda_{C}}c_{C}=U_{C}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}c_{\lambda}\,e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}\,\pi(\lambda)g\right).

The unimodular factors
e^{\pi ix^{T}Cx}
define a diagonal unitary on
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
, so

\|D_{U_{C}g,\Lambda_{C}}\|=\|D_{g,\Lambda}\|
follows by taking operator norms and using unitarity of
U_{C}
.
Finally, frame bounds are equivalent to bounds for
\|C_{g,\Lambda}f\|^{2}
in terms of
\|f\|^{2}
, so they are preserved.
∎

8.2.2
A concrete non-implication: coupling can vary arbitrarily at fixed frame bounds

The previous proposition shows that, within our chirp-transport class,
frame stability constants do not see the coupling parameter
C
.
In contrast, the covariance-level defect
\mathsf{D}

does
see
C
explicitly.
This yields a precise statement: within families with identical frame bounds, one may generate arbitrarily strong
time–frequency coupling.

Proposition 25

(Arbitrarily large coupling at fixed stability)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a lattice and let
g,\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|g\|_{2}=\|\phi\|_{2}=1
.
Assume
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
is a frame with bounds
A,B
and assume
\Sigma_{g,\phi}
exists with
\Sigma_{x\omega}=0
and
\Sigma_{xx}
positive definite.
For
t>0
set
C_{t}:=tC_{0}
for some fixed symmetric matrix
C_{0}\neq 0
, and define

g_{t}:=U_{C_{t}}g,\qquad\phi_{t}:=U_{C_{t}}\phi,\qquad\Lambda_{t}:=S_{C_{t}}\Lambda.

Then:

(i)

\mathcal{G}(g_{t},\Lambda_{t})
is a frame with the same bounds
A,B
for every
t>0
.

(ii)

The coupling defect satisfies
\mathsf{D}(g_{t},\phi_{t})=t\,\|\Sigma_{xx}C_{0}^{T}\|_{\mathrm{F}}
, hence
\mathsf{D}(g_{t},\phi_{t})\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

Point (i) is exactly Proposition
24
.
For (ii), Corollary
22
applied to
C=C_{t}=tC_{0}
gives

\mathsf{D}(g_{t},\phi_{t})=\|\Sigma_{xx}(tC_{0})^{T}\|_{\mathrm{F}}=t\,\|\Sigma_{xx}C_{0}^{T}\|_{\mathrm{F}},

and the factor
\|\Sigma_{xx}C_{0}^{T}\|_{\mathrm{F}}
is strictly positive because
\Sigma_{xx}
is positive definite and
C_{0}\neq 0
.
∎

Remark 26

(Implication for “optimal atoms versus optimal frames”)
.

Proposition
25
shows that, at least within our explicit class,
atom-level coupling/localization descriptors and frame-level stability constants can be
decoupled
:
one may vary coupling arbitrarily without changing
(A,B)
.
This provides a concrete mechanism behind the general question of whether minimizing an atom-level
uncertainty functional necessarily yields the “best” frame in global stability terms:
even when the frame bounds are fixed, the geometric structure of phase-space concentration may change substantially.

8.3
Robust reconstruction under coefficient noise: deterministic and statistical bounds

8.3.1
A general noisy-coefficient model

Let
g,\gamma\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
\Lambda
be a lattice.
Assume that
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
is a Bessel system, not necessarily a frame, so that
the analysis operator
C_{g,\Lambda}
is bounded. Assume also that
\mathcal{G}(\gamma,\Lambda)

is a Bessel system, so that the synthesis operator
D_{\gamma,\Lambda}
is bounded.
We consider the observation model

y=C_{g,\Lambda}f+\eta\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda),

where
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the unknown signal and
\eta\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
is an additive noise sequence.
The reconstruction with synthesis window
\gamma
is

\widehat{f}:=D_{\gamma,\Lambda}y=D_{\gamma,\Lambda}C_{g,\Lambda}f+D_{\gamma,\Lambda}\eta=\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda}f+D_{\gamma,\Lambda}\eta.

8.3.2
Deterministic error bounds (exact dual and approximate dual)

Proposition 27

(Deterministic robustness bound)
.

Assume that
\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
and
\mathcal{G}(\gamma,\Lambda)
are Bessel systems, and let

B_{\gamma}
be a Bessel bound for
\mathcal{G}(\gamma,\Lambda)
, so that

\|D_{\gamma,\Lambda}\|\leq\sqrt{B_{\gamma}}.

Then, for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and all
\eta\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
,

\|f-\widehat{f}\|_{2}\leq\|(I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda})f\|_{2}+\sqrt{B_{\gamma}}\,\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}.

(17)

In particular, if the two Bessel systems form an approximately dual pair, namely

\delta:=\|I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda}\|<1,

then

\|f-\widehat{f}\|_{2}\leq\delta\,\|f\|_{2}+\sqrt{B_{\gamma}}\,\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}.

(18)

If
\gamma
is an exact dual window of
g
on
\Lambda
, then

\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda}=I
and (
17
) reduces to

\|f-\widehat{f}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{B_{\gamma}}\,\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}.

If, moreover,
\gamma=\widetilde{g}=S_{g,\Lambda}^{-1}g
is the canonical dual window and

\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)
has lower frame bound
A_{g}
, then
B_{\widetilde{g}}=A_{g}^{-1}
and hence

\|f-\widehat{f}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{A_{g}}}\,\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}.

(19)

Proof.

From
\widehat{f}=\Theta f+D_{\gamma,\Lambda}\eta
we have

f-\widehat{f}=(I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda})f-D_{\gamma,\Lambda}\eta.

Hence, by the triangle inequality and the operator norm bound for
D_{\gamma,\Lambda}
,

\|f-\widehat{f}\|_{2}\leq\|(I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda})f\|_{2}+\|D_{\gamma,\Lambda}\eta\|_{2}\leq\|(I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda})f\|_{2}+\|D_{\gamma,\Lambda}\|\,\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq\|(I-\Theta)f\|_{2}+\sqrt{B_{\gamma}}\,\|\eta\|_{\ell^{2}},

which is (
17
). If
\|I-\Theta\|=\delta
, then

\|(I-\Theta)f\|_{2}\leq\delta\|f\|_{2}
, giving (
18
).
If
\gamma
is an exact dual, then
\Theta=I
by definition, and the bias term vanishes.
∎

8.3.3
Mean-square error under finite noisy measurements

White noise on an infinite lattice is not an
\ell^{2}
random element, so we work with a standard
finite-measurement model.
Let
\Lambda_{N}\subset\Lambda
be a finite index set (e.g. a box truncation) and consider noisy
measurements

y_{\lambda}=\langle f,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle+\eta_{\lambda},\qquad\lambda\in\Lambda_{N},

where
\{\eta_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{N}}
are complex random variables with

\mathbb{E}[\eta_{\lambda}]=0,\qquad Cov(\eta_{\lambda}\eta_{\mu})=\mathbb{E}[\eta_{\lambda}\overline{\eta_{\mu}}]=\sigma^{2}\,\delta_{\lambda\mu}.

Define the truncated synthesis operator

D_{\gamma,\Lambda_{N}}c:=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{N}}c_{\lambda}\,\pi(\lambda)\gamma,

and the estimator

\widehat{f}_{N}:=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{N}}y_{\lambda}\,\pi(\lambda)\gamma=\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda_{N}}f+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{N}}\eta_{\lambda}\,\pi(\lambda)\gamma,

where
\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda_{N}}:=D_{\gamma,\Lambda_{N}}C_{g,\Lambda_{N}}
is the finite-section mixed operator.

Proposition 28

(Exact MSE decomposition for uncorrelated coefficient noise)
.

Assume
\mathbb{E}[\eta_{\lambda}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[\eta_{\lambda}\overline{\eta_{\mu}}]=\sigma^{2}\delta_{\lambda\mu}
on
\Lambda_{N}
.
Then

\mathbb{E}\|f-\widehat{f}_{N}\|_{2}^{2}=\|(I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda_{N}})f\|_{2}^{2}+\sigma^{2}\,|\Lambda_{N}|\,\|\gamma\|_{2}^{2}.

(20)

In particular, if
\gamma
is an exact dual for the finite-section model (i.e.
\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda_{N}}=I
on the relevant subspace),
then
\mathbb{E}\|f-\widehat{f}_{N}\|_{2}^{2}=\sigma^{2}|\Lambda_{N}|\|\gamma\|_{2}^{2}
.

Proof.

Write

f-\widehat{f}_{N}=(I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda_{N}})f-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{N}}\eta_{\lambda}\,\pi(\lambda)\gamma=:b-n,

where
b
is deterministic and
n
is random with
\mathbb{E}[n]=0
.
Then

\mathbb{E}\|b-n\|_{2}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\big(\|b\|_{2}^{2}+\|n\|_{2}^{2}-2\Rea\langle b,n\rangle\big)=\|b\|_{2}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|n\|_{2}^{2}-2\Rea\langle b,\mathbb{E}[n]\rangle,

so the cross term vanishes and

\mathbb{E}\|f-\widehat{f}_{N}\|_{2}^{2}=\|(I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda_{N}})f\|_{2}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|n\|_{2}^{2}.

Now expand
\|n\|_{2}^{2}
:

\|n\|_{2}^{2}=\left\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{N}}\eta_{\lambda}\,\pi(\lambda)\gamma\right\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda_{N}}\eta_{\lambda}\overline{\eta_{\mu}}\,\langle\pi(\lambda)\gamma,\pi(\mu)\gamma\rangle.

Taking expectations and using
\mathbb{E}[\eta_{\lambda}\overline{\eta_{\mu}}]=\sigma^{2}\delta_{\lambda\mu}
gives

\mathbb{E}\|n\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{N}}\sigma^{2}\,\langle\pi(\lambda)\gamma,\pi(\lambda)\gamma\rangle=\sigma^{2}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{N}}\|\gamma\|_{2}^{2}=\sigma^{2}\,|\Lambda_{N}|\,\|\gamma\|_{2}^{2},

since
\pi(\lambda)
is unitary. Substituting into the previous identity yields (
20
).
∎

8.3.4
Invariance of robustness bounds under chirp transport

We finally show that the deterministic and statistical robustness bounds above are
invariant

under the chirp deformation used in this paper.

Proposition 29

(Robustness is preserved under chirp transport)
.

Let
C=C^{T}
,
\Lambda_{C}=S_{C}\Lambda
,
g_{C}=U_{C}g
,
\gamma_{C}=U_{C}\gamma
.

(i)

(Approximate-dual error)
\|I-\Theta_{g_{C},\gamma_{C},\Lambda_{C}}\|=\|I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda}\|
.

(ii)

(Deterministic bound) The Bessel bound of
\gamma
equals the Bessel bound of
\gamma_{C}
, hence
the constant
\sqrt{B_{\gamma}}
in (
17
) is unchanged.

(iii)

(Finite-section MSE) If
\Lambda_{N}\subset\Lambda
is finite and
\Lambda_{C,N}:=S_{C}\Lambda_{N}
,
then the MSE formula (
20
) for
(g,\gamma,\Lambda_{N})
coincides with the MSE
formula for
(g_{C},\gamma_{C},\Lambda_{C,N})
(same bias term norm, same noise term).

Proof.

(i) is exactly Proposition
14
proved earlier.

(ii) By Proposition
24
,
\|D_{\gamma_{C},\Lambda_{C}}\|=\|D_{\gamma,\Lambda}\|
,
hence the optimal Bessel bound is preserved, so
\sqrt{B_{\gamma_{C}}}=\sqrt{B_{\gamma}}
.

(iii) For the finite section, note that
\|\gamma_{C}\|_{2}=\|\gamma\|_{2}
because
U_{C}
is unitary and

|\Lambda_{C,N}|=|\Lambda_{N}|
because
S_{C}
is a bijection between the finite sets.
Moreover, by the same conjugacy argument used in Proposition
14
,

\Theta_{g_{C},\gamma_{C},\Lambda_{C,N}}=U_{C}\,\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda_{N}}\,U_{C}^{-1},

hence
\|(I-\Theta_{g_{C},\gamma_{C},\Lambda_{C,N}})f\|_{2}=\|(I-\Theta_{g,\gamma,\Lambda_{N}})U_{C}^{-1}f\|_{2}
.
Taking
f
fixed and using
\|U_{C}^{-1}f\|_{2}=\|f\|_{2}
yields equality of the bias norms in (
20
)
after identifying the corresponding signals. The noise term is identical because it depends only on
|\Lambda_{N}|
and
\|\gamma\|_{2}
.
∎

Remark 30

(Summary)
.

Within the explicit chirp-transport class of this paper, non-separability can be quantified by a covariance-level defect

\mathsf{D}
that grows linearly with the coupling parameter
C
, while frame bounds and robustness constants remain invariant.
This isolates a precise sense in which non-separability changes the geometry of time–frequency concentration without changing
the global stability/robustness constants inherited from the separable model.

Concluding remarks

In this paper we proposed a constructive route to non-separable Gabor windows on

Several directions remain open.

First, it would be desirable to replace the purely chirp-type coupling by more general metaplectic transformations while still retaining a usable control on support, smoothness, or anisotropic decay. This would enlarge the lattice classes accessible by explicit constructions and would move the present approach closer to the geometric flexibility already visible in
(
10
)
.

Second, one would like to understand to what extent the present transport principle can be combined with higher-dimensional geometric constructions of smooth compactly supported windows in order to obtain dual pairs beyond the current lower block-triangular setting. It would also be interesting to investigate whether corresponding dual systems enjoy stability properties in modulation or amalgam spaces, in the direction suggested by
(
2
)
.

Third, the constructive program developed here should eventually be compared with the genuinely different Gaussian program. For Gaussian windows, already in dimension
d\geq 2
the frame property is tied to sampling in Bargmann–Fock spaces of several complex variables and is no longer governed by density alone
(
8
)
. A natural long-term objective is therefore to investigate whether some part of the present dual-transport philosophy can be merged with the multivariate Gaussian/sampling viewpoint. From a complementary perspective, one may also ask whether lattice-shape optimization phenomena, such as those studied for separable Gaussian lattices in
(
7
)
, admit constructive counterparts in the present non-separable setting.
