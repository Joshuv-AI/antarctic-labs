What Is Carbon Capture, Use and Storage (CCUS)?

 Carbon Capture Terminology

▸Carbon Reduction: a technology that reduces
  the amount of CO2 emitted from a point source

▸Carbon Removal: a technology that removes
  CO2 from the atmosphere

▸Pre-Combustion CO2 Capture: a process that
  breaks down a hydrocarbon before combustion
  to reduce CO2 emissions

▸ Post-Combustion CO2 Capture: a process
  that removes CO2 from flue gas from hydrocarbon
  combustion​

▸Oxy-Combustion: oxygen replaces air to
  produce pure CO2 stream

Reservoir and Seals
Reservoir
• Sufficient depth, pressure, and temperature for CO₂ to
 remain supercritical What is porosity and permeability?
• Adequate thickness and lateral continuity
• High porosity and permeability for injectivity and
 storage capacity
• Geochemical compatibility with CO₂ (low reactivity
 with formation fluids/minerals)
• Examples: Sandstone, limestones, basalts and
 fractured igneous rocks

Seal (Caprock)
• Laterally continuous, thick, and ductile lithologies
 (e.g., shales, evaporites)
• Very low permeability to prevent vertical migration
• High capillary entry pressure relative to reservoir rock
• Absence of transmissive faults, fractures, or wellbores
• Multiple/sequential sealing units provide additional
 security Credit: Louisiana DEQ
• Examples: Shales, evaporites (halite), and tight
 carbonates

An underground source of drinking water (USDW) is defined in the
Code of Federal Regulations (40 CFR 144.3) as:

 an aquifer or its portion:
  (a)(1) Which supplies any public water system; or
  (2) Which contains a sufficient quantity of ground
water to supply a public water system; and (i) currently
supplies drinking water for human consumption; or (ii)
 contains fewer than 10,000 mg/l total dissolved
  solids; and (b) Which is not an exempted aquifer.

 • Salinity can be calculated using logs as a proxy for TDS
 • Sample must be collected to prove over 10,000 mg/l
 • Class VI does not allow Aquifer Exemptions

 Injection Wells-Planning Considerations
Completion Installation
 • Make sure you have enough space. Monitoring equipment
 requires extra clearance between casing/tubing and casing/open
 hole.
 • Permanent monitoring equipment cannot be replaced. Use
 redundant systems when possible or have a contingency.
 • If perforating near fiber optic cables, make sure you can identify
 its location in the well.
Operations
 • Make sure the well size accounts for future logging and
 inspection operations. Some logging tools are too large in
 diameter.
Workovers
 • Account for workovers during the well design phase. Have
 packers that allow for the tubing to be pulled while the packer
 isolates the injection zone.
 • Have a back up injection packer if possible to reduce lead-times.

Ownership of Pore-Space Storage Rights
  • Section 9-17-161, “The ownership of pore space in all strata below the
 surface lands and waters of this state is vested in the owners of the
 surface rights above the underlying strata where the pore space exists,
 unless the ownership interest in the pore space has previously been
 severed from the surface ownership or is explicitly excluded or reserved
 in a conveyance.”

  • A conveyance of the surface ownership of real property shall be a
 conveyance of the pore space in all strata below the surface unless the
 ownership interest in the subsurface pore space has been previously
 severed.

  • Existing law- Most state appellate courts have held that the pore space-
 storage rights are owned by the surface owners.

  • Section 9-17-162: exception where the property is in coal mining areas

  • “Nothing in this division shall be construed to change, alter, diminish, or in
 any way affect the statutory or common law . . .”

Amalgamation of Rights of the Owners
• “A storage operator shall make a good faith effort to obtain the consent of all persons
  that own a storage facility’s pore space or storage rights.”

• In 2022, a stratigraphic test borehole was drilled to explore an
  otherwise untested region of Central Alabama. (SECARB USA)
• Based on regional structural trends, the Cambrian-Ordovician
  formations were thought to occur at <5,000 ft.

 scale tectonic thickening of thick, weak shales.

Conclusions

• The Alabama Valley and Ridge Province is a geologically complex area that requires a multi-faceted
 approach to characterize includes seismic data, stratigraphic wells with robust logging and
 sampling programs, and extensive modeling to delineate storage intervals and confining zones.
• The area around the Project OASIS site is composed of very tight rock with low porosity and
 permeability that will be challenging for carbon storage development.
• Deeper formations may allow for more opportunities, but these will need further characterization to
 fully understand the lateral connectivity.

• There are numerous carbon emitters in this region that are currently not served by a regional
 CO2 disposal option outside of long pipelines to the Coastal Plain. This is why it is important to
 define the subsurface in areas such as the Valley and Ridge that are not historically chosen or
 preferred.

Concluding Thoughts
✓ Project OASIS near Plant Gaston and the National Carbon Capture Center in the Coosa Deformed
  Belt, which is among the most structurally complex parts of the Appalachian foreland.

✓ Project area overlies the SE margin of the Birmingham Graben, which is an Iapetan rift.

✓ Alleghanian inversion of the Iapetan graben margin localized thrust ramps, resulting in an
  Appalachian-type triangle zone that inlcudes the OASIS anticline.

✓ Cambrian-Mississippian section dominated by tight rocks, including anhydrite, shale, dolomite, and
  limestone.

✓ Intense fracturing in Copper Ridge duplex includes numerous large-aperture fractures with high
  transmissivity; most viable CO2 objective in region.

✓ Overlying tight carbonates in OASIS Anticline interpreted as primary reservoir seal; deformed shale in
