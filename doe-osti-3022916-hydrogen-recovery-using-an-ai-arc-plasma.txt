 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

In the US, onshore oil and gas extraction operations generate nearly a trillion gallons of produced
water annually. Produced water is the largest waste stream associated with upstream development
of petroleum hydrocarbons. Managing produced water is especially challenging in locations such
as the Middle East, Africa and parts of America that have minimal water resources and high-water
purification costs. This has resulted in interest in finding alternative ways to dispose of, treat and
reuse produced water.

Oceanit has conceived of a novel concept to minimize volume of produced water and generating
hydrogen. This concept, known as Hydrogen-Recovery Using an AI-Arc-Plasma Learning
Operational System For Produced Water or HALO, involved treating produced water with
electricity to generate hydrogen and minimize produced water volume. Oceanit gathered a team
including experts in high power microwaves (Sem-Sol) and high-fidelity physics simulation
(ARA) to explore the HALO concept and design a pilot system.

The Oceanit team developed an experimental prototype HALO Pathfinder system to explore the
performance limits of the concept to determine engineering requirements for a pilot field system.
The Oceanit team demonstrated that achieving hydrogen producing arc-plasma discharge is
possible in extremely high salinity produced water. Cost share partners in the oil and gas water
treatment area have been adversely affected by the recent O&G market conditions which limited
field demonstration opportunities. Despite this, efforts to produce value added products from the
massive Produced water waste streams will continue.

Problem Background

Produced water is the largest waste stream associated with the upstream development of petroleum
hydrocarbons. As per Sustainable Produced Water Policy, Regulatory Framework, and
Management in Texas, it was estimated that in 2017 the total volume of produced water generated
in Texas, the leading oil and gas producing state in the US, was more than 357 billion gallons.
That figure is expected to grow to more than 630 billion gallons per year by 2023.i Produced water
is a global problem that significantly impacts oil and gas wells' overall production and operation.
Managing produced water is especially challenging in locations such as the Middle East, Africa
and parts of America that have minimal water resources and high purification costs. This has
resulted in interest in finding alternative ways to dispose of, treat and reuse produced water.
Historically, there has not been much consideration given to contaminants in produced water as
most is injected into sealed, underground wells. But as volumes increase and challenges arise with
existing disposal options (i.e., contamination, earthquakes, etc.), new options are needed. The
major constituents in produced water are salt, oil and grease, inorganic and organic chemicals, and
Naturally Occurring Radioactive Materials (NORM), leading to extremely low reuse of produced
water. The cost of managing produced water could be as high as $10/bbl, including:
 • Constructing treatment and disposal facilities, including equipment acquisitions
 • Operating those facilities, including chemical additives and utilities
 • Managing any residuals or byproducts resulting from the treatment of produced water
 • Permitting, monitoring, and reporting costs
 • Transportation costs.
The current strategy for managing produced water is a filtration treatment followed by reuse for
agricultural or municipal purposes or disposal using discharge, injection, removal to an offsite
disposal facility, and evaporation. However, it is imperative to identify other processes to help
manage produced water and develop strategies for repurposing water or extracting usable
chemicals/energy safely and economically.

environment, of an arc-plasma reactor utility in creating hydrogen as a treatment and disposal
method for toxic produced water. The following tasks were proposed below.

Budget Period 1
Task 2.0: Pilot HALO System Design and Fabrication
The Recipient will first model multiple design options for the P&ID of the HALO System. Options
will be considered that improve energy efficiency, maximizing heat streams generated from the
plasma torches. Routing product streams for recycling and dual use would be beneficial and
determined based on desired conversion rates weighted by energy consumption.
Subtask 2.1: Modeling Validation and System Design
The Recipient will conduct relevant thermodynamic and kinetic modeling using appropriate
software, that will allow for rapid screening of conditions and outputs, which take into
consideration heat, temperature and pressure generated from the plasma arc and chamber
dimensions to covert the produced water into steam and gas products.
Subtask 2.2: Materials and Component Sourcing and Fabrication
The Recipient will select materials and components to maximize hydrogen conversion efficiency
and other beneficial products, such as metals from the slag.
Subtask 2.3: Industry Partner Site Survey
The Recipient will perform a site survey of industry partners to determine the equipment and
personnel needed to support the deployment of the HALO system.
The site survey will be completed as part of Task 5.1 before a field trial is allowed in BP2.
Task 3.0: Pilot HALO System Validation and Testing
The Recipient will conduct initial trials on the fabricated HALO Pilot System. Each control and
operation will be validated, ensuring leak-proof connections and safety settings are in place.
Subtask 3.1: System Operation
The Recipient will use both municipal tap water and produced water as the test fluids for initial
validation tests. The Recipient will use guidance from industry partners, where available, to
determine the flow rates and pretreatment requirements, if any. The produced water will be fed
into the reactor and the resultant gas and solids generated will be collected and analyzed.
Analysis approach for the gas phase will include, but not be limited to, gas chromatography with
a thermal conductivity detector which will be used to identify which gases are the primary
constituents and track how changing process parameters will alter the composition of the gas
product.

management plan (PMP), technology maturation plan (TMP), Economic Revitalization and Job
Creation (ERJC) Questionnaire and DEI documentation. A high-level overview of Oceanit’s
PMP and TMP is provided in this section.

Project Management Plan (PMP)
Oceanit submitted original PMP to DOE on November 1st 2023, with subsequent updates to the
PMP on September 30th 2024 and June 30th, 2025. This includes updating the milestones and
deadlines for each task (Table 2, Table 4).
The organizational structure used in HALO was based upon past proven project experience with
produced water management, plasma reactors design and fabrication, gas separation and
transport, and oil and gas industry project management. The structure was designed to meet
aggressive schedules with open and frequent communication between all members to sustain a
constant awareness of current stakeholder input and project goals. The joint responsibility of
business and technical leads was to synchronize all creation, design and test actions with market
and sponsor input to minimize design iterations and provide a faster pathway to a usable product.
The technical lead will then direct the technical team to the appropriate tasking, with the project
manager tracking budget and schedule and serving as reinforcing lead for the team. Key
stakeholders include parties from the DOE NETL, Oceanit, Sem-Sol, industry partners and two
major produced water service company partners.

Roles and Responsibilities of Participants
 • Business Lead – Liaise with DOE sponsor and Industry Partners (produced water service
 companies) POCs to identify the best business case for technical implementation in the
 project and cost share development. BL will be the primary manager of sponsor and Industry
 Partner relations and build a commercialization strategy and pathway for HALO technology.
 • Technical Lead – Responsible for HALO process development, testing, and effectiveness
 in meeting the operational requirements that support the business case. TL will interact
 upward with DOE and Industry Partners on an as-needs basis for input, orchestrated by BL,
 and direct and lead the technical team in all tasks.
 • HALO Core Teams – Responsible for designing and fabricating the complete HALO
 system, starting from the input of the produced water to the plasma gasification and finally,
 gas conditioning, storage, and transportation.
 o Produced Water Group – Responsible for everything related to produced water

 • Testing & Field Trials Team – Responsible for pilot-scale testing, deployment and data

 collected from field trials will guide the design of the commercial-scale HALO system.
Risk Management Plan:
Risks to the project were minimized through detailed schedules, metrics, and the assignment of
specific responsibilities. The Project team held weekly meetings to assess progress and potential
problems and to develop appropriate actions. Oceanit performed periodic reviews with the
Department of Energy's Office of Fossil Energy (FE), including a Project Kick-Off Meeting and
additional briefings at key project milestones and/or budget period transitions. Oceanit delivered
quarterly Research Performance Progress Reports and SF-425 Federal Financial Reports, Annual
Special Status Reports, Indirect Cost Proposals and Conference Papers/Proceedings, Final
Closeout Reporting documents including Patent Certification, SF-428 & 428B Final Property

Conclusions

Under this proposed effort, Oceanit collaborated with oil and gas operators, and produced water
management partners to conduct in depth technology development using an innovative submerged
plasma arc-based treatment process for efficient produced water management in oil and gas
production. Oceanit was successful in advancing HALO technology from a lab scale concept to a
pilot scale design through experimentation and optimization of components in the HALO.
