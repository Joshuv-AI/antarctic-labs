Piatetski-Shapiro Primes in short intervals
Lingyu Guo, Victor Zhenyu Guo

Abstract
. The existence of primes in a short interval, which asks if there are prime numbers in the interval [ x , x + x θ ] [x,x+x^{\theta}] , is a core problem in number theory. Guth and Maynard proved the best known result for this problem with an asymptotic formula while Baker, Harman and Pintz proved the best lower bound result. In this article, we focus on Piatetski-Shapiro primes in a short interval. The study of Piatetski-Shapiro primes of the form ⌊ n c ⌋ \lfloor n^{c}\rfloor is an approximation of the well-known conjecture that there exist infinitely many primes of the form n 2 + 1 n^{2}+1 . We prove the existence of such primes under restrictions on θ \theta and c c with an asymptotic formula and a lower bound, respectively.

11B83; 11N05; 11L07.

Keywords:
Piatetski-Shapiro sequence; prime; exponential sum; short interval.

1.
Introduction

The existence of primes in a short interval, which asks if there is a prime number in the interval
[x,x+x^{\theta}]
, is a core problem in number theory. Hoheisel
[
11
]
firstly proved that
\theta=1-1/33000
is admissible. A remarkable result is by Huxley
[
12
]
, who proved that

(1.1)

\pi(x+x^{\theta})-\pi(x)\sim\frac{x^{\theta}}{\log x},

where
\pi(x)
is the prime counting function and
\theta>7/12
. Guth and Maynard
[
7
]
proved the best range of this result with
\theta>17/30
. If one considers a lower bound result of (
1.1
) instead of an asymptotic formula, Baker, Harman and Pintz
[
1
]
gave the current best result that for
\theta>0.525
and all large enough
x
it follows that

\pi(x+x^{\theta})-\pi(x)\gg\frac{x^{\theta}}{\log x}.

The Piatetski-Shapiro sequences are sequences of the form

\mathcal{N}_{c}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\lfloor n^{c}\rfloor)_{n=1}^{\infty},

where
\left\lfloor\cdot\right\rfloor
is the integer part. Piatetski-Shapiro
[
14
]
proved the Piatetski-Shapiro prime number theorem stating that for
1<c<12/11
the counting function

\pi_{c}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\#\big\{\text{\rm prime~}p\leqslant x:p\in\mathcal{N}_{c}\big\}

satisfies the asymptotic relation

\pi_{c}(x)=(1+o(1))\frac{x^{1/c}}{\log x}\qquad\text{~\rm as }x\to\infty.

The admissible range for
c
of the above formula has been extended many times and is currently known to hold for all
1<c<2817/2426
thanks to Rivat and Sargos
[
15
]
. Rivat and Wu
[
16
]
also showed that there are infinitely many Piatetski-Shapiro primes for
1<c<243/205
without an asymptotic formula. The estimation of Piatetski-Shapiro primes is an approximation of the well-known conjecture that there exist infinitely many primes of the form
n^{2}+1
. We refer the readers to
[
6
]
for a survey on the investigations of admissible ranges for
c
in this problem.

We are interested in counting Piatetski-Shapiro primes in a short interval. For a result with an asymptotic formula, we prove the following theorem; also see Figure
1
about the admissible range.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\theta\in(2/3,1)
and
\gamma\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=c^{-1}<1
. We have

\displaystyle\pi_{c}(x+x^{\theta})-\pi_{c}(x)=\frac{\gamma x^{\theta+\gamma-1}}{\log x}+O\Big(\frac{x^{\theta+\gamma-1}}{\log^{2}x}\Big)

provided that

(1.2)

\gamma>\begin{cases}\dfrac{20-3\theta}{18},&\text{if}\quad\dfrac{2}{3}<\theta\leqslant\dfrac{40}{51};\\[10.00002pt]
\max\left(\dfrac{1+\theta}{2},\;\dfrac{10-4\theta}{7},\;\dfrac{17-3\theta}{15}\right),&\text{if}\quad\dfrac{40}{51}<\theta\leqslant\dfrac{10}{11};\\[10.00002pt]
\max\left(\dfrac{15-6\theta}{10},\;\dfrac{17-3\theta}{15}\right),&\text{if}\quad\dfrac{10}{11}<\theta<1.\end{cases}

A key part is the estimation of the following exponential sum

(1.3)

\sum_{h\sim H}\delta_{h}\sum_{n\sim N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\\
mn\in I\end{subarray}}a_{n}b_{m}\mathbf{e}(h(mn)^{\gamma}),

where
|\delta_{h}|\leqslant 1
and
H\leqslant x^{1-\gamma+\epsilon}
. The interval
I=(x,x+y]
for
y\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=x^{\theta}
. If the coefficients
a_{n}
and
b_{m}
satisfy the conditions

|a_{n}|\ll x^{\varepsilon},\quad b_{m}=1\quad\text{or}\quad b_{m}=\log m,

the sum is a type I sum and denoted by
S_{I}
; if they satisfy the conditions

|a_{n}|\ll x^{\varepsilon},\quad|b_{m}|\ll x^{\varepsilon},

the sum is a type II sum and denoted by
S_{II}
in this paper.

We mention that the exponential sum of the form

(1.4)

\sum_{h\sim H}\delta_{h}\sum_{n\sim N}\sum_{m\sim M}a_{n}b_{m}\mathbf{e}(h(mn)^{\gamma})

was well investigated during the research of Piatetski-Shapiro primes; see Robert and Sargos
[
17
]
for a type I sum and Heath-Brown
[
10
]
for a type II sum. However, to bound the exponential sum as (
1.3
), we need to employ the short interval information
mn\in I
, so methods for (
1.4
) may not provide the best bound here.

For type I sums (Proposition
3.1
), a normal treatment is to consider the cancellation of
h,m,n
via a double large sieve technique. However, we start by estimating the inner sum of
m
by Lemma
2.5
, since the condition of the short interval entangles the relationship between
m
and
n
, which makes it hard to use other methods. Another reason is that in this question we try to make
\theta
as small as possible instead of enlarging the range of
\gamma
which leads that other normal methods do not work well. We mention that if
\theta>0.87
, combining double large sieve techique and our methods may work better, but as mentioned before, we do not focus on this case.

For type II sums, we apply two different methods, which lead to Proposition
3.2
and Proposition
3.3
. The first method of type II sum is by switching it into an estimation of a type I sum via the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and A-process, which fits the best to short interval information. To grab more type II information for sieves, we also apply Heath-Brown’s idea in
[
10
]
with counting rational points as a second method.

We prove the upper bounds of exponential sums in Section
3
. By the bounds, we provide an asymptotic formula for Piatetski-Shapiro primes in short intervals in Section
4
. The lower bound result (Theorem
1.2
) and the set-up of Harman sieve are described in Section
5
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations

We denote by
\left\lfloor t\right\rfloor
and
\{t\}
the integer part and the fractional part of
t
, respectively. As is customary, we put
\mathbf{e}(t)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=e^{2\pi it}
. We make considerable use of the sawtooth function defined by

\psi(t)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=t-\left\lfloor t\right\rfloor-\frac{1}{2}=\{t\}-\frac{1}{2}\qquad(t\in\mathbb{R}).

The letter
p
always denotes a prime. The interval
I
always denotes
(x,x+y]
and
y
is defined as
x^{\theta}
where
1/2<\theta\leqslant 1
. For the Piatetski-Shapiro sequence
(\left\lfloor n^{c}\right\rfloor)_{n=1}^{\infty}
, we denote
\gamma\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=c^{-1}
.
We use notation of the form
m\sim M
as an abbreviation for
M<m\leqslant 2M
.
\varepsilon
is always a sufficiently small positive number. For an arbitrary set
\mathcal{S}
, we use
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}}
to denote its indicator function:

\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}}(n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\begin{cases}1&\quad\hbox{if $n\in\mathcal{S}$,}\\
0&\quad\hbox{if $n\not\in\mathcal{S}$.}\\
\end{cases}

Throughout the paper, implied constants in symbols
O
,
\ll
and
\gg
may depend (where obvious) on the parameters
c,\varepsilon
but are absolute otherwise. For given functions
F
and
G
, the notations
F\ll G
,
G\gg F
and
F=O(G)
are all equivalent to the statement that the inequality
|F|\leqslant C|G|
holds with some constant
C>0
.
F\asymp G
means that
F\ll G\ll F
.

2.2.
Technical lemmas

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
H\geqslant 1
, there exist numbers
a_{h},b_{h}
such that

\bigg|\psi(t)-\sum_{0<|h|\leqslant H}a_{h}\,\mathbf{e}(th)\bigg|\leqslant\sum_{|h|\leqslant H}b_{h}\,\mathbf{e}(th),\quad a_{h}\ll 1/|h|,\quad b_{h}\ll 1/H.

Proof.

See
[
19
]
by Vaaler.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

A natural number
m
has the form
\left\lfloor n^{c}\right\rfloor
if and only if
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}_{c}}(m)=1
, where

\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}_{c}}(m)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\left\lfloor-m^{\gamma}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor-(m+1)^{\gamma}\right\rfloor
. Moreover,

\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}_{c}}(m)=\gamma m^{\gamma-1}+\psi(-(m+1)^{\gamma})-\psi(-m^{\gamma})+O(m^{\gamma-2}).

Proof.

The equality
m=\lfloor n^{c}\rfloor
holds precisely when
m\leqslant n^{c}<m+1
, or equivalently, when
-(m+1)^{\gamma}\leqslant-n<-m^{\gamma}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}_{c}}(m)

\displaystyle=\lfloor-m^{\gamma}\rfloor-\lfloor-(m+1)^{\gamma}\rfloor=(m+1)^{\gamma}-m^{\gamma}+\psi(-(m+1)^{\gamma})-\psi(-m^{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\gamma m^{\gamma-1}+\psi(-(m+1)^{\gamma})-\psi(-m^{\gamma})+O(m^{\gamma-2}).

∎

The following lemma is the famous Weyl-van der Corput inequality, also called the A-process; see
[
5
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
f(n)
is a complex valued function and
I
is an interval such that
f(n)=0
if
n\notin I
. If
H
is a positive integer then

|\sum_{n\in I}f(n)|^{2}\leqslant\frac{|I|+H}{H}\sum_{|h|<H}\big(1-\frac{h}{H}\big)\sum_{n\in I}f(n)\overline{f(n-h)}.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
f
is a real valued function with two continuous derivatives on
I
. Suppose also that there is some
\lambda>0
and some
\alpha\geqslant 1
such that

\lambda\leqslant|f^{\prime\prime}(x)|\leqslant\alpha\lambda

on
I
. Then

\sum_{n\in I}e(f(n))\ll\alpha|I|\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

See
[
5
, Theorem 2.2]
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
q
be a positive integer. Suppose that
f
is a real valued function with
q+2
continuous derivatives on
I
. Suppose also that for some
\lambda>0
and for some
\alpha\geqslant 1
,

\lambda\leqslant\left|f^{(q+2)}(x)\right|\leqslant\alpha\lambda

on

. Let
Q\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=2^{q}
. Then

\sum_{n\in I}e(f(n))\ll|I|(\alpha^{2}\lambda)^{1/(4Q-2)}+|I|^{1-1/2Q}\alpha^{1/2Q}+|I|^{1-2/Q+1/Q^{2}}\lambda^{-1/2Q}.

The implied constant is absolute.

Proof.

See
[
5
, Theorem 2.8]
.
∎

L(E)=\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant u}A_{i}E^{a_{i}}+\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant v}B_{j}E^{-b_{j}},

Proof.

See
[
5
, Lemma 2.4]
.
∎

3.
Bounds on exponential sums

Recall that the corresponding type I sum of the form (
1.3
) is

(3.1)

S_{I}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{h\sim H}\delta_{h}\sum_{n\sim N}a_{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\\
mn\in I\end{subarray}}\mathbf{e}(h(mn)^{\gamma})

and
the corresponding type II sum is

(3.2)

S_{II}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{h\sim H}\delta_{h}\sum_{n\sim N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\\
mn\in I\end{subarray}}a_{n}b_{m}\mathbf{e}(h(mn)^{\gamma})

where

|\delta_{h}|\ll 1,|a_{n}|,|b_{m}|\ll x^{\varepsilon}.

We prove the following bounds for type I and type II sums.

Proposition 3.1

(Type I)
.

Let
\theta\in(2/3,1]
and
N
satisfies the condition

\displaystyle N\ll x^{2\gamma-\frac{4}{3}-\varepsilon},

and

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{8-3\theta}{6}<\gamma<1,

then

S_{I}\ll x^{\theta-\varepsilon}.

Proposition 3.2

(Type II)
.

Let
\theta\in(2/3,1]
and
N
satisfies the condition

\displaystyle x^{3-\theta-2\gamma+\varepsilon}\ll N\ll x^{\theta+4\gamma-4-\varepsilon}\quad\text{or}\quad x^{5-\theta-4\gamma+\varepsilon}\ll N\ll x^{\theta+2\gamma-2-\varepsilon},

and

(3.4)

\displaystyle\max\Big(\frac{1+\theta}{2},\frac{6-3\theta}{4}\Big)<\gamma<1,

then

S_{II}\ll x^{\theta-\varepsilon}.

Proposition 3.3

(Type II)
.

Let
\theta\in(3/4,1]
and
N
satisfies the condition

\displaystyle x^{2-\theta-\gamma+\varepsilon}\ll N\ll x^{3\theta+5\gamma-7-\varepsilon}\quad\text{or}\quad x^{8-3\theta-5\gamma+\varepsilon}\ll N\ll x^{\theta+\gamma-1-\varepsilon},

and

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{9-4\theta}{6}<\gamma<1,

then

S_{II}\ll x^{\theta-\varepsilon}.

3.1.
Estimate of Type I Sum: Proof of Proposition
3.1

In this part, we shall bound the type I sum defined as (
3.1
). Unlike an usual approach, we start by estimating the inner sum of
m
by Lemma
2.5
for the sake of restriction that
mn\in I
, which gives that

x^{-\varepsilon}S_{I}\ll\sum_{h\sim H}\sum_{n\sim N}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\\
mn\in I\end{subarray}}\mathbf{e}(h(mn)^{\gamma})\Big|.

Let
f(m)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=h(mn)^{\gamma}
. The
(k+2)
-th derivative is

f^{(k+2)}(m)=\gamma\cdots(\gamma-k-1)hm^{\gamma-k-2}n^{\gamma}\asymp h\big(\frac{x}{n}\big)^{\gamma-k-2}n^{\gamma}=\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}\lambda.

By Lemma
2.5
, we derive that

x^{-\varepsilon}S_{I}\ll\sum_{h\sim H}\sum_{n\sim N}\bigg(\frac{y}{n}\lambda^{1/(4K-2)}+\big(\frac{y}{n}\big)^{1-1/2K}+\big(\frac{y}{n}\big)^{1-2/K+1/K^{2}}\lambda^{-1/2K}\bigg),

where
K=2^{k}
. Sum over
h
and
n
to obtain that

\displaystyle x^{-\varepsilon}S_{I}\ll

\displaystyle x^{\theta-\frac{4K-1}{4K-2}\gamma+\frac{\gamma+4K-k-3}{4K-2}}N^{\frac{k+2}{4K-2}}+x^{1+\theta-\gamma-\frac{\theta}{2K}}N^{\frac{1}{2k}}

\displaystyle+x^{1+\theta-\gamma+\frac{k+1-4\theta}{2K}+\frac{\theta}{K^{2}}}N^{\frac{1}{K}-\frac{k}{2K}-\frac{1}{K^{2}}}.

Let the upper bound of
S_{I}\ll x^{\theta-\varepsilon}
and
k=1
. We achieve that Type I sum holds if

\displaystyle N\ll x^{2\gamma-\frac{4}{3}}

with

\gamma>\frac{8-3\theta}{6}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma>\frac{6-3\theta}{4}.

Noting that for
\theta\in(2/3,1]

\frac{8-3\theta}{6}>\frac{6-3\theta}{4}

all the time, we finish the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

3.2.
Estimate of Type II Sum: Proof of Proposition
3.2

In this part, we estimate type II sum defined as (
3.2
). Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle x^{-\varepsilon}S_{II}^{2}\ll HN\sum_{h\sim H}\sum_{n\sim N}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\\
mn\in I\end{subarray}}b_{m}\mathbf{e}(h(mn)^{\gamma})\Big|^{2}.

By the A-process, namely, Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle x^{-\varepsilon}S_{II}^{2}

\displaystyle\ll HN\sum_{h\sim H}\sum_{n\sim N}\frac{y/n+Q}{Q}\sum_{0\leqslant|q|\leqslant Q}\big(1-\frac{|q|}{Q}\big)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\\
mn\in I\end{subarray}}b_{m+q}\overline{b_{m}}\mathbf{e}(hn^{\gamma}((m+q)^{\gamma}-m^{\gamma}))

\displaystyle\ll\frac{H^{2}y^{2}}{Q}+\frac{Hy}{Q}\sum_{h\sim H}\sum_{1\leqslant|q|\leqslant Q}\sum_{m\asymp x/N}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
mn\in I\end{subarray}}\mathbf{e}(hn^{\gamma}((m+q)^{\gamma}-m^{\gamma}))\Big|

provided
1\leqslant Q\leqslant y/(2N).
Applying Lemma
2.4
to the inner sum of variable
n
we obtain that

\displaystyle x^{-\varepsilon}S_{II}^{2}\ll\frac{H^{2}y^{2}}{Q}+\frac{Hy}{Q}\sum_{h\sim H}\sum_{1\leqslant|q|\leqslant Q}\sum_{m\asymp x/N}\bigg(yh^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{1}{2}}m^{\frac{\gamma-3}{2}}N^{\frac{\gamma}{2}-1}+h^{-\frac{1}{2}}q^{-\frac{1}{2}}m^{\frac{-\gamma+1}{2}}N^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}+1}\bigg).

Recalling that
H\leqslant x^{1-\gamma+\varepsilon}
and
y=x^{\theta}
, we have

\displaystyle x^{-\varepsilon}|S_{II}|^{2}

\displaystyle\ll Q\frac{x}{N}\sum_{1\leqslant q\leqslant Q}\sum_{m\asymp x/N}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\sim H\ mn\in I\\
