A Fletcher’s Augmented Lagrangian-Based Stochastic First-Order Method for Nonconvex Equality-Constrained Optimization
Yawen Cui, Qiankun Shi, Xiao Wang, Xiantao Xiao

Abstract
In this paper, we study nonconvex equality-constrained optimization problems in which only stochastic first-order approximations of the objective and constraint functions are available. Owing to the stochasticity in both objective and constraints, most existing stochastic first-order methods incur relatively high oracle complexity, particularly in terms of stochastic constraint function evaluations. To address this issue, we develop a stochastic first-order method based on a decomposed stochastic search direction, and employ Fletcher’s augmented Lagrangian as a smooth merit function for step-size selection. To cope with the possible loss of uniform nondegeneracy of the stochastic Jacobian, we introduce an event decomposition based on the smallest singular value, which enables us to control perturbations in the stochastic search direction. Under an additional Lipschitz continuity assumption on the second-order derivatives of the objective and constraint functions, we show that the proposed algorithm attains a stochastic ϵ \epsilon -KKT point with an expected total oracle complexity of 𝒪 ⁡ ( ϵ − 3 ) \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3}) in terms of stochastic gradient and stochastic constraint function evaluations. Finally, we present numerical experiments to demonstrate the performance of the proposed method.



Keywords
nonconvex constrained optimization, Fletcher’s augmented Lagrangian, stochastic approximation, oracle complexity

Motivation.
Reducing the oracle complexity regarding constraint value evaluations within a tractable stochastic first-order framework is a central challenge. Existing methods that employ smooth merit/penalty functions, such as quadratic penalty function or augmented Lagrangian function, typically require
\epsilon^{-1}
-dependent penalty parameters, thus own higher oracle complexity bounds
[
1
,
8
,
18
]
. Alternatively, methods based on nonsmooth merit/penalty functions, such as
f(x)+\rho\|c(x)\|
, admit exactness once
\rho

[
7
,
19
,
27
,
29
]
. Therefore, to better control the total oracle complexity bounds, we are motivated to search for a merit function that unifies smoothness and exactness. Fletcher’s augmented Lagrangian
[
13
]
serves this purpose by replacing the seperately updated dual variable with a continuous function of the primal variable, thereby providing a suitable choice to help design a stochastic approximation method for solving (

1.1
Contributions

This paper develops a single-loop stochastic first-order method based on Fletcher’s augmented Lagrangian for fully-stochastic nonconvex equality-constrained optimization. At each iteration, the algorithm employs truncated SPIDER-type recursive estimators to generate stochastic approximations, from which a decomposed direction is computed to balance objective decrease and feasibility improvement, with the Fletcher’s augmented Lagrangian serving as a smooth merit function and to determine the step size. To deal with the possible degeneracy of stochastic constraint Jacobians and to control the perturbation in the stochastic search direction, we separate the event that whether or not the sampled Jacobian satisfies a prescribed singular-value bound. We prove that the proposed method reaches a stochastic
\epsilon
-KKT point within
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
iterations and establish the corresponding expected oracle complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
to evaluate stochastic gradient and constraint function values under second-order smoothness of objective and constraint functions (Assumption
1.3
) and the strong LICQ condition (Assumption
1.4
). This complexity bound improves the state-of-the-art methods for fully-stochastic problems, particularly regarding the computation of stochastic constraint function values. We also demonstrate the promising numerical performance of the proposed method on test problems.

Algorithm Framework

In this section, we present a single-loop stochastic first-order method for solving (
1
). The framework is built on a decomposition strategy for constructing the search direction, together with adaptive updates of the merit parameter and the stepsize. We begin with the deterministic counterpart of problem (
1
), where both the objective and the constraints are deterministic, and consider the computation of a search direction. The main idea is to construct, at each iterate, a search direction through a decomposition into a tangential component for objective reduction and a normal component for feasibility improvement. More precisely, define the tangent space at a point
x
by

V(x):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\nabla c(x)^{\top}v=0\}.

Projecting
\nabla f(x)
onto
V(x)
gives the tangential direction that reduces the objective while remaining in the tangent space of the constraint manifold. Under Assumption
1.4
, the projection is given by

\displaystyle{\rm P}_{V(x)}\nabla f(x)=\mathop{\textrm{argmin}}_{v\in V(x)}\frac{1}{2}\|v-\nabla f(x)\|^{2}=\nabla f(x)-(\nabla c(x)^{\top})^{\dagger}\nabla c(x)^{\top}\nabla f(x).

(2.1)

With the definition of the mapping
\lambda(x)
in (
1.2
), it is easy to obtain
\nabla f(x)+\nabla c(x)\lambda(x)={\rm P}_{V(x)}\nabla f(x)
. To improve feasibility, we use the correction direction
-\nabla c(x)c(x)
, which is the steepest descent direction for the squared constraint violation
\frac{1}{2}\|c(x)\|^{2}
. Combining this component with the tangential direction gives

s(x)=-{\rm P}_{V(x)}\nabla f(x)-w\nabla c(x)c(x),

where
w\in(0,1)
.

For problem (
1
), since the exact information is computationally intractable in stochastic settings, we instead work with the stochastic estimators
\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}
,
\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}
, and
\tilde{c}_{k}
at iteration
k
to approximate the true objective gradient
\nabla f(x_{k})
, constraint gradient
\nabla c(x_{k})
, and constraint value
c(x_{k})
, respectively. For notational simplicity, we abbreviate
\nabla f(x_{k}),\nabla c(x_{k}),c(x_{k})
as
\nabla f_{k},\nabla c_{k},c_{k}
throughout the remainder of this paper. The search direction is then defined as

\tilde{s}_{k}=-{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}-w\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k},

(2.2)

where
w\in(0,1)
is a fixed parameter and
\widetilde{V}_{k}:=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top}v=0\}
represents the null space associated with the stochastic Jacobian. The operator

{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}:=\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}-(\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top})^{\dagger}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}

denotes the orthogonal projection of
\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}

\tilde{s}_{k}

{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\perp\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}

This decomposition shares a similar structure with stochastic SQP-type directions, as both combine a tangential component for objective reduction with a normal component for feasibility improvement. Specifically, there exists
y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
(\tilde{s}_{k},y_{k})
satisfies the following linear system:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}I_{n}&\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\\
\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{s}_{k}\\
y_{k}\end{bmatrix}=-\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\\
w\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}\end{bmatrix}.

(2.3)

In standard SQP methods, the matrix in place of

Theoretical Analysis

3.1
Directional Perturbation Bounds

In this subsection, we derive a perturbation bound for the stochastic search direction
\tilde{s}_{k}
with respect to its deterministic counterpart
s_{k}
. The main difficulty is that, although the true Jacobian satisfies a uniform lower singular value bound under Assumption
1.4
, the stochastic Jacobian may not remain uniformly nondegenerate at every iteration. To handle this issue, we partition the analysis into the good event and the bad event according to whether the stochastic Jacobian preserves a uniform lower singular value. Specifically, we define the good event

\mathcal{G}_{k}:=\left\{\mu_{\min}(\tilde{\nabla}c_{k})\geq\frac{\nu}{2}\right\}.

The following lemma provides a conditional probability bound for the corresponding bad event
\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}
.

Lemma
3.1
.

Under Assumptions
1.4
and
2.1
, it holds that

\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{k}(\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c})\leq\frac{4}{\nu^{2}}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}.

Proof.

It follows from Weyl’s inequality (Lemma
A.2
) that

\mu_{\min}(\tilde{\nabla}c_{k})\geq\mu_{\min}(\nabla c_{k})-\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}-\nabla c_{k}\|.

Then using Assumption
1.4
, we have
\mu_{\min}(\tilde{\nabla}c_{k})\geq\nu-\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}-\nabla c_{k}\|
. Therefore, it holds that

\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}=\left\{\mu_{\min}(\tilde{\nabla}c_{k})<\frac{\nu}{2}\right\}\subseteq\left\{\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}-\nabla c_{k}\|\geq\frac{\nu}{2}\right\}.

Applying Markov’s inequality and Assumption
2.1
yields

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{k}(\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c})\leq\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{k}\left(\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}-\nabla c_{k}\|^{2}\geq\frac{\nu^{2}}{4}\right)\leq\frac{4}{\nu^{2}}\,\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}-\nabla c_{k}\|^{2}]\leq\frac{4}{\nu^{2}}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We now proceed to establish a perturbation bound between the stochastic search direction
\tilde{s}_{k}
and the true direction
s_{k}
.

Lemma
3.2
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1.1
-
1.2
,
1.4
, and
2.1
hold. Then for any
k\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|s_{k}-\tilde{s}_{k}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{s}^{2}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2},

(3.1)

where
C_{s}:=[2C_{p}^{2}+4w^{2}(M^{2}+L_{c}^{2})+32(G^{2}+w^{2}M^{2}L_{c}^{2})/{\nu^{2}}]^{\frac{1}{2}}
with
C_{p}:=[5(1+(17G^{2}L_{c}^{2}+16L_{c}^{4})/\nu^{4}+64G^{2}L_{c}^{6}/\nu^{8})]^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

On the good event
\mathcal{G}_{k}
, after localization by the indicator
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}
, the Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury formula (Lemma
A.1
) implies that

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|(\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k})^{-1}-(\nabla c_{k}^{\top}\nabla c_{k})^{-1}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\Big[\big\|(\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k})^{-1}\big[\nabla c_{k}^{\top}\nabla c_{k}-\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\big](\nabla c_{k}^{\top}\nabla c_{k})^{-1}\big\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\Big[\big\|(\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k})^{-1}\big\|^{2}\big\|\nabla c_{k}^{\top}\nabla c_{k}-\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\big\|^{2}\big\|(\nabla c_{k}^{\top}\nabla c_{k})^{-1}\big\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{32}{\nu^{8}}\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\big[(\|\nabla c_{k}\|^{2}+\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\|^{2})\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}-\nabla c_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}\big],

where the last inequality comes from the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{k}
and Assumption
1.4
.
Under Assumptions
1.1
-
1.2
and
2.1
, together with the truncation bound
\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\|\leq L_{c}
, we obtain

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}-{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\nabla f_{k}-\nabla c_{k}(\nabla c_{k}^{\top}\nabla c_{k})^{-1}\nabla c_{k}^{\top}\nabla f_{k}-\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}+\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}(\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k})^{-1}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle\leq 5\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\nabla f_{k}-\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]+5\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\nabla c_{k}-\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\|^{2}\|(\nabla c_{k}^{\top}\nabla c_{k})^{-1}\nabla c_{k}^{\top}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle+5\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\|^{2}\|(\nabla c_{k}^{\top}\nabla c_{k})^{-1}-(\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k})^{-1}\|^{2}\|\nabla c_{k}^{\top}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle+5\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}(\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k})^{-1}\|^{2}\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}-\nabla c_{k}\|^{2}\|\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle+5\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}(\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k})^{-1}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}^{\top}\|^{2}\|\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}-\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle\leq 5\Big(1+\frac{G^{2}L_{c}^{2}}{\nu^{4}}+\frac{64G^{2}L_{c}^{6}}{\nu^{8}}+\frac{16G^{2}L_{c}^{2}}{\nu^{4}}+\frac{16L_{c}^{4}}{\nu^{4}}\Big)\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}:=C_{p}^{2}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}.

Then appealing to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the truncation bound
\|\tilde{c}_{k}\|\leq M
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\nabla c_{k}c_{k}-\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle\leq 2\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\nabla c_{k}\|^{2}\|\tilde{c}_{k}-c_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]+2\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}-\nabla c_{k}\|^{2}\|\tilde{c}_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]\leq 2(M^{2}+L_{c}^{2})\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}.

The contribution from the good event is therefore bounded by

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|s_{k}-\tilde{s}_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle\leq 2\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}-{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]+2w^{2}\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\nabla c_{k}c_{k}-\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle\leq(2C_{p}^{2}+4w^{2}M^{2}+4w^{2}L_{c}^{2})\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}.

On the bad event
\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}
, it follows from Assumptions
1.1
-
1.2
and the truncation bounds that

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|s_{k}-\tilde{s}_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}]

\displaystyle\leq 2\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}-{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}]+2w^{2}\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\nabla c_{k}c_{k}-\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}]

\displaystyle\leq(8G^{2}+8w^{2}M^{2}L_{c}^{2})\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{k}(\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}).

(3.3)

Combining the bounds derived on
\mathcal{G}_{k}
and
\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}
with the probability estimate in Lemma
3.1
, we derive

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\tilde{s}_{k}-s_{k}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\!\left[\|\tilde{s}_{k}-s_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}\right]+\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\!\left[\|\tilde{s}_{k}-s_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(2C_{p}^{2}+4w^{2}M^{2}+4w^{2}L_{c}^{2})\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}+\frac{32(G^{2}+w^{2}M^{2}L_{c}^{2})}{\nu^{2}}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}:=C_{s}^{2}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

3.2
Convergence analysis

In this subsection, we study the convergence properties of Algorithm
2.1
. We first observe that the update rule (
2.6
) guarantees a uniform upper bound on the merit parameter. On the good event
\mathcal{G}_{k}=\{\mu_{\min}(\tilde{\nabla}c_{k})\geq\nu/2\}
, we have
\mu_{\min}(\tilde{\nabla}c_{k})^{2}\geq\nu^{2}/4
. On the complementary event
\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}
, the denominator is trivially bounded below by the constant
2w\nu^{2}
. Consequently, the candidate update is always bounded above by
4\bigl(4L_{\lambda}^{2}+2(w^{2}L_{c}^{2}+1)\bigr)/(w\nu^{2})
. Since the sequence
\{\rho_{k}\}
is non-decreasing, it follows immediately by induction that

\displaystyle{\rho_{0}\leq\,}\rho_{k}\leq\rho_{\max}:=\max\left\{\rho_{0},\frac{4\bigl(4L_{\lambda}^{2}+2(w^{2}L_{c}^{2}+1)\bigr)}{w\nu^{2}}\right\},\quad\forall k\geq 1.

As a direct consequence,
L_{k}
is uniformly bounded from below and above. More precisely, let

L_{\min}:=L_{\rho_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad L_{\max}:=L_{\rho_{\max}},

with
L_{\rho}
defined in Proposition
2.1
.
Given that
\rho_{k}\in[\rho_{0},\rho_{\max}]
, the update rule (
2.5
) directly yields

\eta_{\min}:=\frac{1}{4L_{\max}}\leq\eta_{k}\leq\frac{1}{4L_{\min}}=:\eta_{\max},\quad\forall k\geq 1.

Equipped with these uniform bounds, we are now ready to characterize the convergence behavior of the sequence generated by Algorithm
2.1
. We first establish a descent property of
\mathcal{L}(x,\rho)
, which plays a central role in the subsequent analysis.

Lemma
3.3
.

Suppose Assumptions
1.1
-
1.4
and
2.1
hold. Then for any
k\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(x_{k+1},\rho_{k})]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k})]-\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}(\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}+(w^{2}L_{c}^{2}+1)\|c_{k}\|^{2})]

(3.4)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\eta_{k}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|+L_{c}\eta_{k}\rho_{k}\|c_{k}\|\big)(\|\Delta P_{k}\|+w\|\Delta c_{k}\|)\big]

\displaystyle+wM\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}\|c_{k}\|\|\Delta J_{k}\|^{2}]+\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}[(2\eta_{k}^{2}L_{k}+\eta_{k})\|\tilde{s}_{k}\|^{2}],

where
\Delta P_{k}:={\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}-{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}
,
\Delta c_{k}:=\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}-\nabla c_{k}c_{k}
and
\Delta J_{k}:=\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}-\nabla c_{k}
.

Proof.

It follows from the Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla\mathcal{L}(x,\rho)
in
x
established in Proposition
2.1
and
x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\eta_{k}\tilde{s}_{k}
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(x_{k+1},\rho_{k})]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k})]+\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[{\eta_{k}^{2}L_{k}}\|\tilde{s}_{k}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k})]+\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k}),\eta_{k}\tilde{s}_{k}\rangle]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[{\eta_{k}^{2}L_{k}}\|\tilde{s}_{k}\|^{2}],

(3.5)

For the second term, one has

-\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k}),\eta_{k}\tilde{s}_{k}\rangle]=\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k}),{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}+w\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}\rangle]

from (
2.2
). Recalling the definition of
\Delta P_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k}),{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\nabla}f_{k},{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\rangle+\langle\nabla\lambda_{k}c_{k},{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\rangle+\langle\lambda_{k},\nabla c_{k}^{\top}{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\rangle+\rho_{k}\langle\nabla c_{k}c_{k},{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\nabla}f_{k},{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\rangle+\langle\nabla\lambda_{k}c_{k},{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\rangle+\langle\lambda_{k},\nabla c_{k}^{\top}\Delta P_{k}\rangle+\rho_{k}\langle\nabla c_{k}c_{k},\Delta P_{k}\rangle,

where the second equality is due to the fact that
{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}
is orthogonal to the column space of
\nabla c_{k}
. With the notation
\Delta c_{k}:=\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}-\nabla c_{k}c_{k}
, it is easy to show that

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k}),w\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\nabla}f_{k},w\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}\rangle+\langle\nabla\lambda_{k}c_{k},w\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}\rangle+\langle\nabla c_{k}\lambda_{k},w\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}\rangle+\rho_{k}\langle\nabla c_{k}c_{k},w\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\nabla}f_{k}-\nabla c_{k}(\nabla c_{k}^{\top}\nabla c_{k})^{-1}\nabla c_{k}^{\top}\nabla f_{k},w\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}\rangle+\rho_{k}w\langle\nabla c_{k}c_{k},\Delta c_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+w\langle\nabla\lambda_{k}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k},c_{k}\rangle+\rho_{k}w\|\nabla c_{k}c_{k}\|^{2}.

Using the two preceding relations, we deduce that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k}),\eta_{k}\tilde{s}_{k}\rangle]

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\langle{\nabla}f_{k},{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\rangle+\eta_{k}\langle\nabla\lambda_{k}c_{k},{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\rangle+\eta_{k}\langle\nabla c_{k}(\lambda_{k}+\rho_{k}c_{k}),\Delta P_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle+\eta_{k}\rho_{k}w\|\nabla c_{k}c_{k}\|^{2}+\eta_{k}w\langle\nabla\lambda_{k}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k},c_{k}\rangle+\eta_{k}\langle\nabla f_{k}+\nabla c_{k}\lambda_{k}+\rho_{k}\nabla c_{k}c_{k},w\Delta c_{k}\rangle]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\langle{\nabla}f_{k},{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\rangle+\eta_{k}\langle\nabla\lambda_{k}^{\top}{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}+w\nabla\lambda_{k}\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k},c_{k}\rangle+\eta_{k}\rho_{k}w\|\nabla c_{k}c_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\eta_{k}\langle\nabla f_{k}+\nabla c_{k}\lambda_{k}+\rho_{k}\nabla c_{k}c_{k},\Delta P_{k}\rangle+\eta_{k}\langle\nabla f_{k}+\nabla c_{k}\lambda_{k}+\rho_{k}\nabla c_{k}c_{k},w\Delta c_{k}\rangle]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}]-L_{\lambda}\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}(\|{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\|+w\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}\|)\|c_{k}\|]+\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}w\|\nabla c_{k}c_{k}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|(\|\Delta P_{k}\|+w\|\Delta c_{k}\|)]-L_{c}\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}\|c_{k}\|(\|\Delta P_{k}\|+w\|\Delta c_{k}\|)],

where the first equality holds by the orthogonality of the two components of
\tilde{s}_{k}
, and the inequality relies on the properties of the orthogonal projection
\mathrm{P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}
combined with the bound
\|\nabla\lambda(x)\|\leq L_{\lambda}
from Proposition
2.1
.

On the good event
\mathcal{G}_{k}
, for the third item in R.H.S. of (
3.6
), it is easy to attain from
\|a+b\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|a\|^{2}-\|b\|^{2}
and
\Delta J_{k}:=\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}-\nabla c_{k}
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}w\|\nabla c_{k}c_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}w\|\nabla c_{k}c_{k}-\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}c_{k}+\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}c_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}w\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}c_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]-\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}w\|c_{k}\|^{2}\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}-\nabla c_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}w\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}c_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]-M\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}w\|c_{k}\|\|\Delta J_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}].

Recalling the update rule for
\rho_{k}
, we observe that on the good event
\mathcal{G}_{k}
, the merit parameter satisfies
\rho_{k}\geq(4L_{\lambda}^{2}+2w^{2}L_{c}^{2}+2)/(w\mu_{\min}(\tilde{\nabla}c_{k})^{2})
, thereby implying that
\frac{1}{2}\rho_{k}w\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}c_{k}\|^{2}-2L_{\lambda}^{2}\|c_{k}\|^{2}\geq(w^{2}L_{c}^{2}+1)\|c_{k}\|^{2}
. It thus together with Young’s inequality and the definition of
\tilde{s}_{k}
indicates that

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k}),\eta_{k}\tilde{s}_{k}\rangle\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

(3.7)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}w\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}c_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]-M\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}w\|c_{k}\|\|\Delta J_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle\quad-L_{\lambda}\mathbb{E}\Big[\eta_{k}\Big(\frac{1}{4L_{\lambda}}\|{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4L_{\lambda}}w^{2}\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}\|^{2}+2L_{\lambda}\|c_{k}\|^{2}\Big)\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\big(\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|+L_{c}\rho_{k}\|c_{k}\|\big)(\|\Delta P_{k}\|+w\|\Delta c_{k}\|)\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]+\mathbb{E}\big[\eta_{k}\big(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{k}w\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}c_{k}\|^{2}-2L_{\lambda}^{2}\|c_{k}\|^{2}\big)\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}\big]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\|\tilde{s}_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]-M\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}w\|c_{k}\|\|\Delta J_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\big(\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|+L_{c}\rho_{k}\|c_{k}\|\big)(\|\Delta P_{k}\|+w\|\Delta c_{k}\|)\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}(\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}+(w^{2}L_{c}^{2}+1)\|c_{k}\|^{2})\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]-M\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}w\|c_{k}\|\|\Delta J_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\|\tilde{s}_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]-\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\big(\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|+L_{c}\rho_{k}\|c_{k}\|\big)(\|\Delta P_{k}\|+w\|\Delta c_{k}\|)\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}].

On the other hand, on the complementary event
\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}
, the merit parameter satisfies
\rho_{k}\geq(2L_{\lambda}^{2}+1+w^{2}L_{c}^{2})/(w\nu^{2})
. Then it holds from (
3.6
) that

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k}),\eta_{k}\tilde{s}_{k}\rangle\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}]+\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}w\|\nabla c_{k}c_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}]

\displaystyle\quad-L_{\lambda}\mathbb{E}\Big[\eta_{k}\Big(\frac{1}{4L_{\lambda}}\|{\rm P}_{\widetilde{V}_{k}}\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4L_{\lambda}}w^{2}\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}\tilde{c}_{k}\|^{2}+2L_{\lambda}\|c_{k}\|^{2}\Big)\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\big(\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|+L_{c}\rho_{k}\|c_{k}\|\big)(\|\Delta P_{k}\|+w\|\Delta c_{k}\|)\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}]+\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}(\rho_{k}w\nu^{2}-2L_{\lambda}^{2})\|c_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}]-\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\|\tilde{s}_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}]

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\big(\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|+L_{c}\rho_{k}\|c_{k}\|\big)(\|\Delta P_{k}\|+w\|\Delta c_{k}\|)\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}(\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}+(w^{2}L_{c}^{2}+1)\|c_{k}\|^{2})\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}]-\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\|\tilde{s}_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}]

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\big(\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|+L_{c}\rho_{k}\|c_{k}\|\big)(\|\Delta P_{k}\|+w\|\Delta c_{k}\|)\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}],

where the second inequality comes from Assumption
1.4
. Combining the bounds obtained on
\mathcal{G}_{k}
and
\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k}),\eta_{k}\tilde{s}_{k}\rangle]=-\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k}),\eta_{k}\tilde{s}_{k}\rangle\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]-\mathbb{E}[\langle\nabla\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k}),\eta_{k}\tilde{s}_{k}\rangle\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}]

(3.8)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}(\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|^{2}+(w^{2}L_{c}^{2}+1)\|c_{k}\|^{2})]-\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\|\tilde{s}_{k}\|^{2}]-wM\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}\|c_{k}\|\|\Delta J_{k}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\eta_{k}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}{\nabla}f_{k}\|+L_{c}\eta_{k}\rho_{k}\|c_{k}\|\big)(\|\Delta P_{k}\|+w\|\Delta c_{k}\|)\big].

Substituting (
3.8
) into (
3.2
) yields the conclusion.
∎

The next lemma establishes an average bound on the stationarity and feasibility residuals along the sequence generated by Algorithm
2.1
.

Lemma
3.4
.

Under Assumptions
1.1
-
1.4
and
2.1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}+\|c_{k}\|^{2}\right]

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(x_{1},\rho_{1})-\mathcal{L}(x_{K+1},\rho_{K+1})]}{\eta_{\min}K}+\frac{2M^{2}\rho_{\max}}{\eta_{\min}K}+\frac{\bar{\eta}C_{s}^{2}}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}(4\eta_{\max}L_{\max}+2)\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+4\bar{\eta}\tilde{C}\max\{1,\rho_{\max}L_{c}\}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}\left((\mathbb{E}[\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}])^{1/2}+(\mathbb{E}[\|c_{k}\|^{2}])^{1/2}\right),

where
\tilde{C}:=(C_{p}^{2}+16G^{2}/\nu^{2})^{1/2}+w(2M^{2}+2L_{c}^{2})^{1/2}+2wM
and
\bar{\eta}:=\eta_{\max}/\eta_{\min}
.

Proof.

First, recall from (
3.4
) the error terms involving
\Delta P_{k}
,
\Delta c_{k}
, and
\Delta J_{k}
. It follows from Cauchy–Schwarz inequality that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|(\|\Delta P_{k}\|+w\|\Delta c_{k}\|)]

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{1/2}\big(\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta P_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{1/2}+w\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{1/2}\big(\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta c_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{1/2}\Big(\big(\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta P_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{1/2}+w\big(\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta c_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{1/2}\Big).

The tower property, together with (
3.2
) and the estimate on
\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}
in (
3.1
) yields

\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta P_{k}\|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\Delta P_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}}]+\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\Delta P_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}}]\right]\leq\big(C_{p}^{2}+\frac{16G^{2}}{\nu^{2}}\big)\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}:=\widehat{C}_{p}^{2}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}.

Moreover, it follows from Assumption
2.1
that, for the term
\|\Delta c_{k}\|
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta c_{k}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\Delta c_{k}\|^{2}]]\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}-\nabla c_{k}\|^{2}\|\tilde{c}_{k}\|^{2}]+\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\nabla c_{k}\|^{2}\|\tilde{c}_{k}-c_{k}\|^{2}]\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2(M^{2}+L_{c}^{2})\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}:=C_{v}^{2}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}.

Plugging the above estimates into (
3.10
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|(\|\Delta P_{k}\|+w\|\Delta c_{k}\|)]\leq(\widehat{C}_{p}+wC_{v})\tilde{\sigma}_{k}\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{1/2}.

(3.11)

An analogous argument for the other term involving

(\|\Delta P_{k}\|+w\|\Delta c_{k}\|)
gives

\displaystyle

\displaystyle L_{c}\,\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}\|c_{k}\|(\|\Delta P_{k}\|+w\|\Delta c_{k}\|)]

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{\max}L_{c}\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|c_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{1/2}\Big((\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta P_{k}\|^{2}])^{1/2}+w(\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta c_{k}\|^{2}])^{1/2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{\max}L_{c}(\widehat{C}_{p}+wC_{v})\tilde{\sigma}_{k}\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|c_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{1/2}.

In view of the bound
\|\Delta J_{k}\|^{2}\leq 4L_{c}^{2}
and
\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta J_{k}\|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\|\Delta J_{k}\|^{2}]\leq\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}
provided by Assumption
2.1
, the term involving
\Delta J_{k}
in (
3.4
) satisfies

\displaystyle

\displaystyle wM\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\rho_{k}\|c_{k}\|\|\Delta J_{k}\|^{2}]

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq wM\rho_{\max}\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|c_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{1/2}\big(\mathbb{E}[\|\Delta J_{k}\|^{4}]\big)^{1/2}\leq 2wM\rho_{\max}L_{c}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|c_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{1/2}.

For notational convenience, we define
\tilde{C}:=\widehat{C}_{p}+wC_{v}+2wM
. Incorporating (
3.11
)-(
3.13
) into (
3.4
) results in

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(x_{k+1},\rho_{k})]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k})]-\mathbb{E}\left[\eta_{k}\left(\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}+(w^{2}L_{c}^{2}+1)\|c_{k}\|^{2}\right)\right]+\tilde{C}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\rho_{\max}L_{c}\tilde{C}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|c_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}[(2\eta_{k}^{2}L_{k}+\eta_{k})\|\tilde{s}_{k}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k})]-\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\left(\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}+(w^{2}L_{c}^{2}+1)\|c_{k}\|^{2}\right)]+\tilde{C}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\rho_{\max}L_{c}\tilde{C}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|c_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\eta_{k}^{2}L_{k}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\right)(\|{s}_{k}\|^{2}+\|\tilde{s}_{k}-{s}_{k}\|^{2})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k})]-\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}\left(\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}+(w^{2}L_{c}^{2}+1)\|c_{k}\|^{2}\right)]+\tilde{C}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\rho_{\max}L_{c}\tilde{C}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|c_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\eta_{k}^{2}L_{k}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}\right)\|\tilde{s}_{k}-{s}_{k}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[(\eta_{k}^{2}L_{k}+\frac{\eta_{k}}{2})\left(\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}+w^{2}L_{c}^{2}\|c_{k}\|^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k})]-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}(1-2\eta_{k}L_{k})\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}+\left(\frac{\eta_{k}}{2}w^{2}L_{c}^{2}(1-2\eta_{k}L_{k})+\eta_{k}\right)\|c_{k}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{C}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}\left[\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\rho_{\max}L_{c}\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|c_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]+(\eta_{\max}^{2}L_{\max}+\frac{\eta_{\max}}{2})C_{s}^{2}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k})]-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\eta_{k}}{4}\left(\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}+\|c_{k}\|^{2}\right)\right]+(\eta_{\max}^{2}L_{\max}+\frac{\eta_{\max}}{2})C_{s}^{2}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{C}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|{\rm P}_{V_{k}}\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\rho_{\max}L_{c}\tilde{C}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}\big(\mathbb{E}[\eta_{k}^{2}\|c_{k}\|^{2}]\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the last inequality follows from

given by (
2.5
). Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x_{k+1},\rho_{k+1})-\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(x_{k+1},\rho_{k+1})-\mathcal{L}(x_{k+1},\rho_{k})+\mathcal{L}(x_{k+1},\rho_{k})-\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{k+1}-\rho_{k}}{2}\|c(x_{k+1})\|^{2}+\mathcal{L}(x_{k+1},\rho_{k})-\mathcal{L}(x_{k},\rho_{k}).

(3.14)

Summing the above inequality over
k=1,\ldots,K
and rearranging the terms leads to (
3.9
).
The proof is thus completed.
∎

Lemma
4.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1.1
-
1.2
holds. Then for any
k\geq 1

it holds that

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\!\left[\|\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}-\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma_{f}^{2}}{B_{q(k)}^{f}}+\sum_{p=q(k)+1}^{k}\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\!\bigg[\frac{L_{f}^{2}}{B_{p}^{f}}\|x_{p}-x_{p-1}\|^{2}\bigg],

(4.1)

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\!\left[\|\tilde{\nabla}c_{k}-\nabla c_{k}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m\sigma_{J}^{2}}{B_{q(k)}^{J}}+\sum_{p=q(k)+1}^{k}\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\!\left[\frac{mL_{J}^{2}}{B_{p}^{J}}\|x_{p}-x_{p-1}\|^{2}\right],

(4.2)

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\!\left[\|\tilde{c}_{k}-c_{k}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma_{c}^{2}}{B_{q(k)}^{c}}+\sum_{p=q(k)+1}^{k}\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\!\left[\frac{L_{c}^{2}}{B_{p}^{c}}\|x_{p}-x_{p-1}\|^{2}\right].

(4.3)

Proof.

For any fixed
k\geq 1
, we first consider the case
k=q(k)
. Since
\nabla f_{k}\in\mathbb{B}(G)
by Assumption
1.1
, the nonexpansiveness of the projection operator implies

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}-\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big\|{\rm P}_{\mathbb{B}(G)}\big(\frac{1}{B_{k}^{f}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}_{k}^{f}}\nabla F(x_{k},\xi)\big)-{\rm P}_{\mathbb{B}(G)}(\nabla f_{k})\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\frac{1}{B_{k}^{f}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}_{k}^{f}}\big(\nabla F(x_{k},\xi)-\nabla f_{k}\big)\Big\|^{2}.

Taking conditional expectation with respect to
\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{k}=\mathcal{F}_{k}
and using Assumption
1.2

\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}[\|\tilde{\nabla}f_{k}-\nabla f_{k}\|^{2}]\leq\frac{\sigma_{f}^{2}}{B_{k}^{f}}.

(4.4)

Next, let

2
) and the nonexpansiveness of the projection operator that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\nabla}f_{p}-\nabla f_{p}\|^{2}\leq\Big\|\tilde{\nabla}f_{p-1}+\frac{1}{B_{p}^{f}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}_{p}^{f}}\big(\nabla F(x_{p},\xi)-\nabla F(x_{p-1},\xi)\big)-\nabla f_{p}\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\tilde{\nabla}f_{p-1}-\nabla f_{p-1}+\frac{1}{B_{p}^{f}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}_{p}^{f}}\big[\nabla F(x_{p},\xi)-\nabla F(x_{p-1},\xi)-(\nabla f_{p}-\nabla f_{p-1})\big]\Big\|^{2}.

For brevity, define
\delta_{p}^{f}(\xi):=\nabla F(x_{p},\xi)-\nabla F(x_{p-1},\xi)-(\nabla f_{p}-\nabla f_{p-1})
. Taking conditional expectation with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{p}
in the above inequality, and using the fact that
\tilde{\nabla}f_{p-1}
,
\nabla f_{p-1}
,
x_{p}
, and
x_{p-1}
are
\mathcal{F}_{p}
-measurable, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p}[\|\tilde{\nabla}f_{p}-\nabla f_{p}\|^{2}]=\|\tilde{\nabla}f_{p-1}-\nabla f_{p-1}\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{p}\Big[\big\|\frac{1}{B_{p}^{f}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}_{p}^{f}}\delta_{p}^{f}(\xi)\big\|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{\nabla}f_{p-1}-\nabla f_{p-1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{(B_{p}^{f})^{2}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}_{p}^{f}}\mathbb{E}_{p}[\left\|\nabla F(x_{p},\xi)-\nabla F(x_{p-1},\xi)\right\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{\nabla}f_{p-1}-\nabla f_{p-1}\|^{2}+\frac{L_{f}^{2}}{B_{p}^{f}}\|x_{p}-x_{p-1}\|^{2},

where the equality uses the relations
\mathbb{E}_{p}\!\left[\delta_{p}^{f}(\xi)\right]=0
,
the first inequality comes from

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}_{p}[\langle\frac{1}{B_{p}^{f}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}_{p}^{f}}\left(\nabla F(x_{p};\xi)-\nabla F(x_{p-1};\xi)\right),\nabla{f}_{p}-\nabla{f}_{p-1}\rangle]

and the last inequality is due to Assumption
1.2
. Then taking the conditional expectation

on both sides of the preceding inequality, and invoking the tower property together with the inclusion

\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\!\big[\|\tilde{\nabla}f_{p}-\nabla f_{p}\|^{2}\big]\leq\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\!\big[\|\tilde{\nabla}f_{p-1}-\nabla f_{p-1}\|^{2}\big]+\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\!\bigg[\frac{L_{f}^{2}}{B_{p}^{f}}\|x_{p}-x_{p-1}\|^{2}\bigg].

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma_{f}^{2}}{B_{q}^{f}}+\sum_{p=q+1}^{k}\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{k}\!\bigg[\frac{L_{f}^{2}}{B_{p}^{f}}\|x_{p}-x_{p-1}\|^{2}\bigg].

Similarly, the upper bounds for stochastic variance of

Remark 4.1
.

The oracle complexity result is established in expectation, which is consistent with the present analysis framework. The argument based on the good/bad event decomposition requires per-iteration control of the bad-event probability, as shown in Lemma
3.1
. Hence, the analysis relies on conditional second-moment bounds for the stochastic estimators at each iteration, rather than only an averaged error bound along the trajectory. For the SPIDER-type recursion, such a requirement naturally leads to batch sizes depending on the local increment
\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}
, as reflected in Corollary
4.1
. Since these local increments are controlled only after summing over the iterates and taking expectations, the resulting oracle complexity statement is obtained in expectation.

6
Conclusion

This paper studies nonconvex stochastic equality-constrained optimization and proposes a stochastic first-order method. At each iteration, the method computes a search direction consisting of a tangential descent component and a normal component for feasibility improvement using truncated SPIDER-type recursive estimators, and employ the Fletcher’s augmented Lagrangian as a smooth merit function to updates the step size.
Under the additional Lipschitz continuity of the second order derivatives of the objective and constraint functions, together with a smallest singular value-based event decomposition, we derive that the method attains a stochastic
\epsilon
-KKT point within
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
iterations with an expected oracle complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
. This matches the best known stochastic gradient complexity and improves the known order for stochastic constraint value evaluations in the fully-stochastic setting. We finally report numerical results to illustrate the performance of the proposed method.

Proof of Proposition

For simplicity, we denote

and

\displaystyle\|A(x)-A(y)\|\leq\|A(x)\|\|\nabla c(x)^{\top}\nabla c(x)-\nabla c(y)^{\top}\nabla c(y)\|\|A(y)\|.

Since

\displaystyle\|\lambda(x)-\lambda(y)\|=\|A(x)b(x)-A(y)b(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|A(x)\nabla c(x)^{\top}-A(y)\nabla c(y)^{\top}\|\|\nabla f(y)\|+\|A(x)\nabla c(x)^{\top}(\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y))\|

\displaystyle\leq\|A(x)-A(y)\|\|\nabla c(x)\|\|\nabla f(y)\|+\|A(y)\|\|\nabla c(x)-\nabla c(y)\|\|\nabla f(y)\|

\displaystyle\quad+\|A(x)\|\|\nabla c(x)\|\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|A(x)\|\|\nabla c(x)^{\top}\nabla c(x)-\nabla c(y)^{\top}\nabla c(y)\|\|A(y)\|\|\nabla c(x)\|\|\nabla f(y)\|

\displaystyle\quad+\|A(y)\|\|\nabla c(x)-\nabla c(y)\|\|\nabla f(y)\|+\|A(x)\|\|\nabla c(x)\|\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2GL_{c}^{2}L_{J}}{\nu^{4}}\|x-y\|+\frac{GL_{J}}{\nu^{2}}\|x-y\|+\frac{L_{c}L_{f}}{\nu^{2}}\|x-y\|:=L_{\lambda}\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y,

where the third inequality follows from (
B.1
), thereby establishing the first part of the result. Attention is now turned to the second part. It follows from Assumptions
1.2
-
1.3
that

\displaystyle\|\nabla B(x)-\nabla B(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\nabla^{2}c(x)\|\|\nabla c(x)-\nabla c(y)\|+2\|\nabla c(y)\|\|\nabla^{2}c(x)-\nabla^{2}c(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq 2(L_{J}^{2}+L_{c}L_{h}^{c})\|x-y\|,

which in turn implies

\displaystyle\|\nabla A(x)-\nabla A(y)\|=\|A(x)\nabla B(x)A(x)-A(y)\nabla B(y)A(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|A(x)-A(y)\|\|\nabla B(x)\|\|A(x)\|+\|A(y)\|\|\nabla B(x)-\nabla B(y)\|A(x)\|

\displaystyle\quad+\|A(y)\|\|\nabla B(y)\|\|A(x)-A(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4L_{J}L_{c}}{\nu^{2}}\|A(x)-A(y)\|+\frac{1}{\nu^{4}}\|\nabla B(x)-\nabla B(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8L_{J}^{2}L_{c}^{2}}{\nu^{6}}\|x-y\|+\frac{2(L_{J}^{2}+L_{c}L_{h}^{c})}{\nu^{4}}\|x-y\|.

In addition, one has

\displaystyle\|\nabla b(x)-\nabla b(y)\|

\displaystyle=\|\nabla^{2}c(x)\nabla f(x)+\nabla c(x)^{\top}\nabla^{2}f(x)-\nabla^{2}c(y)\nabla f(y)-\nabla c(y)^{\top}\nabla^{2}f(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla^{2}c(x)\|\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|+\|\nabla^{2}c(x)-\nabla^{2}c(y)\|\|\nabla f(y)\|

\displaystyle\quad+\|\nabla c(x)\|\|\nabla^{2}f(x)-\nabla^{2}f(y)\|+\|\nabla c(x)-\nabla c(y)\|\|\nabla^{2}f(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{J}L_{f}\|x-y\|+L_{h}^{c}G\|x-y\|+L_{c}L_{h}^{f}\|x-y\|+L_{J}L_{f}\|x-y\|

\displaystyle=(2L_{J}L_{f}+L_{h}^{c}G+L_{c}L_{h}^{f})\|x-y\|.

Hence, together with

\displaystyle\|b(x)-b(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla c(x)\|\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|+\|\nabla c(x)-\nabla c(y)\|\|\nabla f(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq(L_{c}L_{f}+GL_{J})\|x-y\|,

we can derive

\displaystyle\|\nabla\lambda(x)-\nabla\lambda(y)\|

\displaystyle=\|\nabla A(x)b(x)+A(x)\nabla b(x)-\nabla A(y)b(y)-A(y)\nabla b(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla A(x)\|\|b(x)-b(y)\|+\|\nabla A(x)-\nabla A(y)\|\|b(y)\|+\|A(x)\|\|\nabla b(x)-\nabla b(y)\|

\displaystyle\quad+\|A(x)-A(y)\|\|\nabla b(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2L_{J}L_{c}(L_{c}L_{f}+GL_{J})}{\nu^{4}}\|x-y\|+GL_{c}\left(\frac{8L_{J}^{2}L_{c}^{2}}{\nu^{6}}+\frac{2(L_{J}^{2}+L_{c}L_{h}^{c})}{\nu^{4}}\right)\|x-y\|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2L_{J}L_{f}+L_{h}^{c}G+L_{c}L_{h}^{f}}{\nu^{2}}\|x-y\|+\frac{2L_{J}L_{c}(GL_{J}+L_{c}L_{f})}{\nu^{4}}\|x-y\|:=L_{\lambda}^{1}\|x-y\|,

which yields the conclusion.

To prove the Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla\mathcal{L}(x,\rho)
in
x
, for notational convenience we define

\psi(x,\rho):=\langle\lambda(x),c(x)\rangle+{\frac{\rho}{2}}\|c(x)\|^{2}
.
It then follows that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi(y,\rho)-\nabla\psi(x,\rho)\|

\displaystyle=\|\nabla\lambda(y)c(y)+\nabla c(y)\lambda(y)+\rho\nabla c(y)c(y)-\nabla\lambda(x)c(x)

\displaystyle\qquad-\nabla c(x)\lambda(x)-\rho\nabla c(x)c(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\lambda(y)\|\|c(y)-c(x_{k})\|+\|\nabla\lambda(y)-\nabla\lambda(x)\|\|c(x)\|

\displaystyle\quad+\|\lambda(y)-\lambda(x)\|\|\nabla c(y)\|+\|\lambda(x)\|\|\nabla c(y)-\nabla c(x)\|

\displaystyle\quad+\rho\|\nabla c(y)\|\|c(y)-c(x)\|+\rho\|\nabla c(y)-\nabla c(x)\|\|c(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(2L_{\lambda}L_{c}+ML_{\lambda}^{1}+\frac{GL_{c}L_{J}}{\nu^{2}}+\rho(L_{c}^{2}+ML_{J})\Big)\|y-x\|,

which further indicates

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(y,\rho)-\nabla\mathcal{L}(x,\rho)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x)\|+\|\nabla\psi(y,\rho)-\nabla\psi(x,\rho)\|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(L_{f}+2L_{\lambda}L_{c}+ML_{\lambda}^{1}+\frac{GL_{c}L_{J}}{\nu^{2}}+\rho(L_{c}^{2}+ML_{J})\Big)\|y-x\|,
