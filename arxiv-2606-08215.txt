Structure of Clifford groups of composite finite quantum systems
Miroslav Korbelář, Jiří Tolar

Abstract
. In this paper the Clifford groups of general multipartite quantum systems with configuration space ℤ n 1 ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ ℤ n k \mathbb{Z}_{n_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{n_{k}} , k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 are studied. It is known that the Clifford group is a natural semidirect product provided the dimension N = n 1 ⋯ n k N=n_{1}\cdots n_{k} of the corresponding Hilbert space is an odd number. For even N N special results on the Clifford groups are scattered in the mathematical literature, but they do not concern the semidirect product structure. Using the relation of generators of the associated symplectic group Sp [ n 1 , … , n k ] \mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]} we prove that for even N = n 1 ⋯ n k N=n_{1}\cdots n_{k} both Clifford group and the projective Clifford group are natural semidirect products if and only if N N is not divisible by four.



Keywords: finite-dimensional quantum mechanics, composite quantum system,
generalized Pauli matrices, automorphisms, (projective) Clifford group, semidirect product

2.
Clifford and symplectic groups for the multipartite systems

Let us briefly recall the definition of Clifford group and its associated symplectic group.

Let
P_{n}
and
Q_{n}
be the generalized unitary Pauli matrices of the operators of momentum and position. They fulfill the equality
Q_{n}P_{n}=\omega_{n}P_{n}Q_{n}
where
\omega_{n}=e^{\frac{2\pi\mathbf{i}}{n}}\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
\mathrm{U}(n)
be the group of all unitary
n\times n
complex matrices and
I_{n}
denote the identity
n\times n
matrix.

For a multipartite system with the the abelian group
\mathbb{Z}_{n_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{n_{k}}
as the configuration space, where
k\geq 1
and
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\geq 2
are positive integers, we define the
Heisenberg group

\mathcal{H}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}=\{\lambda\cdot P_{n_{1}}^{i_{1}}Q_{n_{1}}^{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes P_{n_{k}}^{i_{k}}Q_{n_{k}}^{j_{k}}|\ i_{1},j_{1},\dots,i_{k},j_{k}\in\mathbb{Z},\lambda\in\mathrm{U}(1)\}

of Kronecker product of generalized Pauli matrices and the
Clifford group

\mathcal{C}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}=N_{\mathrm{U}(N)}(\mathcal{H}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})})

as the normalizer of the Heiseberg group
\mathcal{H}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
in the unitary group
\mathrm{U}(N)
, where
N=n_{1}\cdots n_{k}
.

An element
U\in\mathcal{C}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
has a natural action on
\mathcal{H}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
as an automorphism of the form
X\mapsto UXU^{-1}
for
X\in\mathcal{H}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
. More generally, for a global linear group
GL(n,\mathbb{C})
we denote by
Ad_{M}
its
inner automorphism
associated to
M\in GL(n,\mathbb{C})
, i.e.
Ad_{M}(X)=MXM^{-1}
for every
X\in GL(n,\mathbb{C})
.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
contains an (ortonormal) basis of the vector space of all complex
N\times N
matrices (see e.g.
[
2
]
), it follows that for
U,U^{\prime}\in GL(N,\mathbb{C})
the maps
Ad_{U}
and
Ad_{U^{\prime}}
coincide on
\mathcal{H}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
if and only if
U=\lambda U^{\prime}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
(we denote this as
U\sim U^{\prime}
), in other words if
U
and
U^{\prime}
are equal as projective linear transformations (of the projective space
P(\mathbb{C}^{N})
).

Since these Clifford operations, i.e. the corresponding inner automorphisms,
are not affected by phase factors, it is natural to study the

projective Heisenberg group

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}=\{Ad_{M}|\ M\in\mathcal{H}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}\}

and the
projective Clifford group

\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}=\{Ad_{M}|\ M\in\mathcal{C}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}\}.

We can easily see that
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
is the normalizer of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
in the group
\overline{U}(N)=\{Ad_{M}|\ M\in\mathrm{U}(N)\}
, i.e.
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}=N_{\overline{U}(N)}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})})
, and that there is an isomorphism of the factor-groups
\mathcal{C}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}/\mathcal{H}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}\cong\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}/\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
of the form
M\cdot\mathcal{H}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}\mapsto Ad_{M}\cdot\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Let us consider the projective global linear group
\overline{GL}(N,\mathbb{C}))=\{Ad_{M}|\ M\in GL(N,\mathbb{C})\}
. In
[
13
]
a more general normalizer than
N_{\overline{U}(N)}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})})

was studied, namely the normalizer
N_{\overline{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})})
(see
[
13
, Definition 5.1]
). However, it turned out that

N_{\overline{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})})\subseteq\overline{U}(N)

(this follows from the generating set of this normalizer, see the results
[
13
, Theorem 5.9]
and
[
10
, Corollary 11]
) and that means that both these two normalizers are, in fact, equal. Hence

\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}=N_{\overline{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})})

and we may therefore apply the results from
[
13
]
for the projective Clifford group defined above.

Let us now recall the main theorem from
[
13
]
that states that the factor-group
\mathcal{C}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}/\mathcal{H}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}\cong\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}/\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
is isomorphic to a certain symplectic group
\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}
(see Definition
2.3
below).

Theorem 2.2
.

[
13
, Theorem 5.9]

Let
k\geq 1
and
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\geq 2
be positive integes. Then:

For every
Ad_{U}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
, with
U\in\mathcal{C}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
, there is a unique (matrix equivalence class)
[\mathbb{H}]\in\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}
such that for all
i_{1},j_{1},\dots,i_{k},j_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
there holds

Ad_{U}\left(P_{n_{1}}^{i_{1}}Q_{n_{1}}^{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes P_{n_{k}}^{i_{k}}Q_{n_{k}}^{j_{k}}\right)\sim P_{n_{1}}^{i^{\prime}_{1}}Q_{n_{1}}^{j^{\prime}_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes P_{n_{k}}^{i^{\prime}_{k}}Q_{n_{k}}^{j^{\prime}_{k}}

where
\begin{pmatrix}i^{\prime}_{1}\\
j^{\prime}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
i^{\prime}_{k}\\
j^{\prime}_{k}\end{pmatrix}=\mathbb{H}\begin{pmatrix}i_{1}\\
j_{1}\\
\vdots\\
i_{k}\\
j_{k}\end{pmatrix}
.

The map
\overline{\pi}:\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}\to\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}
,
Ad_{U}\mapsto[\mathbb{H}]
is a group epimorphism with
\ker(\overline{\pi})=\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
.

In particular, there is a group isomorphism

\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}/\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}\cong\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}.

Definition 2.3
.

(
of the symplectic group
\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}
)

Let
\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}
be a monoid consisting of

k\times k
matrices
\mathbb{H}
composed of
2\times 2
blocks, where the block on position
(i,j)
,
i,j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, has a form

(\mathbb{H})_{ij}=\tfrac{n_{i}}{\gcd(n_{i},n_{j})}A_{ij}

with
A_{ij}
beeing
2\times 2
matrices over
\mathbb{Z}
. We consider
\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}
as a monoid with the usual multiplication of matrices.

An adjoint
\mathbb{H}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}
of

\mathbb{H}\in\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}

is then defined by

(\mathbb{H}^{\ast})_{ij}=\tfrac{n_{i}}{\gcd(n_{i},n_{j})}A_{ji}^{T}

for
i,j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
.

Using a diagonal matrix

\mathbb{D}=\mathrm{diag}\Big(\tfrac{\mathrm{lcm}(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}{n_{1}}I_{2},\dots,\tfrac{\mathrm{lcm}(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}{n_{k}}I_{2}\Big)\in\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}

we define a congruence
\equiv
on

\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}
with respect to multiplication and the adjoint operation as

\mathbb{H}\equiv\mathbb{G}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \mathbb{D}\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{D}\mathbb{G}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \mathrm{lcm}(n_{1},\dots,n_{k}))\quad\textrm{for}\quad\mathbb{H},\mathbb{G}\in\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}

and denote
[\mathbb{H}]
the equivalence class of
\mathbb{H}
.

The
symplectic group

\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}
is now defined as

(1)

\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}=\{[\mathbb{H}]\ |\ \mathbb{H}\in\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]},\ \ \mathbb{H}^{\ast}\mathbb{J}\mathbb{H}\equiv\mathbb{J}\}.

where
\mathbb{J}=\mathrm{diag}(J_{2},\dots,J_{2})\in\mathcal{S}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}

and
J_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right)
.

3.
Clifford groups and exact sequences

The short exact sequences

1\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{C}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}\rightarrow 1.

and

1\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{\nu}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{\pi}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}\rightarrow 1.

comming from Theorem
2.2
, where
\nu
and
\overline{\nu}
are inclusions and
\pi(U)=\overline{\pi}(Ad_{U})
for
U\in\mathcal{C}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
,
will be called
natural
.

As a basic fact for our further considerations we will use the following well known property of exact sequences from the group theory:

The Clifford group (the projective Clifford group, resp.) has a structure of a semidirect product related to its natural exact sequence if and only this sequence is right splitting.

The right splitting of the natural exact sequence for the Clifford group
\mathcal{C}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
means that there is a group homomorphism
\vartheta:\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}\to\mathcal{C}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
such that
\pi\circ\vartheta=id_{\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}}
(and similarly for the projective Clifford group).

The next statement recalls the fact that two multipartite systems with isomorphic configuration space have also isomorphic natural exact sequences of their Clifford groups (projective Clifford groups, resp.). An interesting fact is that the isomorphism is provided via a permutation matrix. For completeness, we provide a short proof.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
k,\ell\geq 1
and
n_{1},\dots,n_{k},m_{1}\dots,m_{\ell}\geq 2
be positive integers such that
\mathbb{Z}_{n_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{n_{k}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{m_{\ell}}
. Then:

(1)

The natural exact sequences of
\mathcal{C}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
and
\mathcal{C}_{(m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell})}
are isomorphic via a permutation matrix. In particular,
\mathcal{C}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
is a natural semidirect product if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{(m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell})}
is a natural semidirect product.

(2)

The natural exact sequences of
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
and
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell})}
are isomorphic. In particular,
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
is a natural semidirect product if and only if
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell})}
is a natural semidirect product.

Proof.

First, let
a
and
b
be positive integers with
\gcd(a,b)=1
. Then
\mathbb{Z}_{ab}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{a}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{b}
and by the Chinese remainer theorem (see e.g.
[
3
]
) there is a permutation matrix
R
such that
R\mathcal{H}_{(n,m)}R^{-1}=\mathcal{H}_{(nm)}
. In this way we can decompose
n_{i}
into product of power of primes. Hence we obtain a permutation matrix
R_{1}
such that
R_{1}\mathcal{H}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}R_{1}^{-1}=\mathcal{H}_{(q_{1},\dots,q_{p})}
, where
q_{1},\dots,q_{p}
are powers of primes and
\mathbb{Z}_{n_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{n_{k}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{q_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{q_{p}}
. Since every finite abelian group has (up to an ordering) a unique decomposition into a direct sum of cyclic groups of prime power order, we obtain similarly a permutation matrix
R_{2}
such that
R_{2}\mathcal{H}_{(m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell})}R_{2}^{-1}=\mathcal{H}_{(q_{\sigma(1)},\dots,q_{\sigma(p)})}
for some permutation
\sigma
on
p
elements. Now, there is a permutation matrix that permutes the order of matrices in the Kronecker product (see e.g.
[
11
]
). Hence
R_{3}\mathcal{H}_{(q_{1},\dots,q_{p})}R_{3}^{-1}=\mathcal{H}_{(q_{\sigma(1)},\dots,q_{\sigma(p)})}
for some permutation matrix
R_{3}
. Consequently,
U\mathcal{H}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}U^{-1}=\mathcal{H}_{(m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell})}
for the permutation matrix
U=R_{2}^{-1}R_{3}R_{1}
.

We obtain in this way the following commutative diagram that provides the isomorphism of exact sequences (where the last vertical arrow is uniquely determined by the others):

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\geq 2
be positive integers. If the Clifford group
\mathcal{C}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
is a natural semidirect product, then the projective Clifford group
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
is a natural semidirect product as well.

Proof.

Consider the commutative diagram of natural short exact sequences

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A,A^{\prime}
be complex regular matrices
n\times n
and
B,B^{\prime}
be complex regular matrices
m\times m
. If
A\otimes B=A^{\prime}\otimes B^{\prime}
, then there is
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
such that
A^{\prime}=\lambda A
and
B^{\prime}=\lambda^{-1}B
.

Proof.

Regular matrices are invertible, so from
A\otimes B=A^{\prime}\otimes B^{\prime}
it follows that
I_{nm}=(A^{\prime}\otimes B^{\prime})(A\otimes B)^{-1}=(A^{\prime}\otimes B^{\prime})(A^{-1}\otimes B^{-1})=(A^{\prime}A^{-1}\otimes B^{\prime}B^{-1})
. As
I_{nm}
is diagonal, it follows that
A^{\prime}A^{-1}
and
B^{\prime}B^{-1}
have to be diagonal also and, in fact, there is
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
such that
A^{\prime}A^{-1}=\lambda I_{n}
and
B^{\prime}B^{-1}=\lambda^{-1}I_{m}
. Hence
A^{\prime}=\lambda A
and
B^{\prime}=\lambda^{-1}B
.
∎

Ad_{V_{1}}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})}
and
\overline{\pi^{\prime}}(Ad_{U_{1}})=[\mathbb{H}]
. By the acting of
Ad_{V_{1}}
on
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})}
, as inner automorphism, it immediately follows, by Theorem
2.2
, that

\overline{\pi}\left(Ad_{V_{1}\otimes I_{n_{\ell+1}\cdots n_{k}}}\right)=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbb{H}&0\\
0&I_{2(k-\ell)}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}.

Now, as
\overline{\pi}(Ad_{U})=\overline{\pi}(Ad_{V_{1}\otimes I_{n_{\ell+1}\cdots n_{k}}})
, the elements
Ad_{U}
and
Ad_{V_{1}\otimes I_{n_{\ell+1}\cdots n_{k}}}
differ only up to an element from
\ker(\overline{\pi})=\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
. In particular, there are
i_{1},\dots,i_{n},j_{1},\dots,j_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
W_{1}=P_{n_{1}}^{i_{1}}Q_{n_{1}}^{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes P_{n_{\ell}}^{i_{\ell}}Q_{n_{\ell}}^{j_{\ell}}
and
W_{2}=P_{n_{\ell+1}}^{i_{\ell+1}}Q_{n_{\ell+1}}^{j_{\ell+1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes P_{n_{k}}^{i_{k}}Q_{n_{k}}^{j_{k}}
such that
Ad_{U}=Ad_{V_{1}\otimes I_{n_{\ell+1}\cdots n_{k}}}\cdot Ad_{W_{1}\otimes W_{2}}
. Hence, there is
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
such that

U=\lambda(V_{1}\otimes I_{n_{\ell+1}\cdots n_{k}})\cdot(W_{1}\otimes W_{2})=\lambda(V_{1}W_{1}\otimes W_{2}).

As both
V_{1}W_{1}
and
W_{2}
are unitary matrices, so is
V_{1}W_{1}\otimes W_{2}
. And since
U
is also a unitary matrix, it follows that
|\lambda|=1
and the matrices
U_{1}=V_{1}W_{1}
and
U_{2}=\lambda W_{2}
are therefore unitary as well. We have thus
U=U_{1}\otimes U_{2}
and, by Lemma
3.3
, the matrices
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are unique up a non-zero scalar multiple. Finally,
Ad_{U_{1}}=Ad_{V_{1}}Ad_{W_{1}}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})}
.
∎

The next lemma is a key argument how to reduce the problem of the non-existence of the natural semidirect structure of Clifford group.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
k\geq 2
,
1\leq\ell<k
and
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\geq 2
be positive integers.
If the projective Clifford group
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
is a natural semidirect product, then the projective Clifford group
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})}
is also a natural semidirect product.

Proof.

Consider the natural exact sequences for the corresponding projective Clifford groups:

1\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{\nu}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{\pi}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}\rightarrow 1.

1\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{\nu^{\prime}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{\pi^{\prime}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell}]}\rightarrow 1.

Assume that there is a right splitting homomorphisms
\vartheta:\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}

\to\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
such that
\overline{\pi}\circ\vartheta=id_{\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}}
. We construct a splitting homomorphism
\vartheta^{\prime}:\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell}]}\to\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})}
such that
\overline{\pi^{\prime}}\circ\vartheta^{\prime}=id_{\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell}]}}
.

According the Definition
2.3
, the map
\psi:\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell}]}\to\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{k}]}
,
\psi\left([\mathbb{H}]\right)=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbb{H}&0\\
0&I_{2(k-\ell)}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
is clearly a group monomorphisms. As
\overline{\pi}
is an epimorphism, for given
[\mathbb{H}]\in\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell}]}
there is
U\in\mathrm{U}(n_{1}\cdots n_{k})
such that
\overline{\pi}(Ad_{U})=\psi\left([\mathbb{H}]\right)
.
By Lemma
3.4
, there are, up to a non-zero scalar multiples, matrices
U_{1}\in\mathrm{U}(n_{1}\cdots n_{\ell})
and
U_{2}\in\mathrm{U}(n_{\ell+1}\cdots n_{k})
such that
Ad_{U_{1}}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})}
,
U=U_{1}\otimes U_{2}
and
[\mathbb{H}]=\overline{\pi^{\prime}}(Ad_{U_{1}})
.
Now we set
\vartheta^{\prime}\left([\mathbb{H}]\right)=Ad_{U_{1}}
. As
U_{1}
is unique, up to a scalar multiple, the map
\vartheta^{\prime}
is well defined. Moreover,
\overline{\pi^{\prime}}(\vartheta^{\prime}([\mathbb{H}]))=\overline{\pi^{\prime}}(Ad_{U_{1}})=[\mathbb{H}]
and, therefore,
\overline{\pi^{\prime}}\circ\vartheta^{\prime}=id_{\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\cdots,n_{\ell}]}}
.

Finally, similarly as above, for an arbitrary
[\mathbb{G}]\in\mathrm{Sp}_{[n_{1},\cdots,n_{\ell}]}
, there is
V\in\mathrm{U}(n_{1}\cdots n_{k})
such that
\overline{\pi}(Ad_{V})=\psi\left([\mathbb{G}]\right)
and matrices
V_{1}\in\mathrm{U}(n_{1}\cdots n_{\ell})
and
V_{2}\in\mathrm{U}(n_{\ell+1}\cdots n_{k})
such that
Ad_{V_{1}}\in\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})}
,
V=V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
and
[\mathbb{G}]=\overline{\pi^{\prime}}(Ad_{V_{1}})
. For the matrix
UV\in\mathrm{U}(n_{1}\cdots n_{k})
there holds that
\overline{\pi}(Ad_{UV})=\overline{\pi}(Ad_{U})\overline{\pi}(Ad_{V})=\psi([\mathbb{H}])\psi([\mathbb{G}])=\psi([\mathbb{H}]\cdot[\mathbb{G}])
,
UV=(U_{1}V_{1})\otimes(U_{2}V_{2})
and
[\mathbb{H}]\cdot[\mathbb{G}]=\overline{\pi^{\prime}}(Ad_{U_{1}})\overline{\pi^{\prime}}(Ad_{V_{1}})=\overline{\pi^{\prime}}(Ad_{U_{1}V_{1}})
. By the construction of the map
\vartheta^{\prime}
, we obtain that
\vartheta^{\prime}\left([\mathbb{H}]\cdot[\mathbb{G}]\right)=Ad_{U_{1}V_{1}}
. Therefore
\vartheta^{\prime}\left([\mathbb{H}]\cdot[\mathbb{G}]\right)=Ad_{U_{1}V_{1}}=Ad_{U_{1}}Ad_{V_{1}}=\vartheta^{\prime}([\mathbb{H}])\cdot\vartheta^{\prime}([\mathbb{G}])
and the map
\vartheta^{\prime}
is thus a group homomorphism.

This proves that the exact sequences for the group
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})}
is right splitting, hence the projective Clifford group
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{\ell})}
is a natural semidirect product.
∎

i_{0}
or
n_{j_{0}}=2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
and
n_{j_{1}}=2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
for some
j_{0}\neq j_{1}
. In view of Proposition
3.1
, we may therefore assume without loss of generality that:

either
n_{1}=0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
and then our assertion holds by Theorem
5.1
and Proposition
3.5
,

or
n_{1}=2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
and
n_{2}=2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
and then our assertion follows from Propositions
4.10
and
3.5
.
∎

6.
The case of even dimension not divisible by four

In this section we show that if the dimension of a multipartite system with configation space
A=\mathbb{Z}_{n_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{n_{k}}
is divisible by
2
but not by
4
, then the correspoding Clifford group (as well as the projective one) are indeed natural semidirect products. This fact seems to be not (broadly) known. The approach is simply to consider the decomposition
A\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus B
, where
B
is an abelian group of odd order
m
. For
B
it is known that the corresponding Clifford groups are semidirect product, i.e. their short exact sequences are right splitting (see e.g.
[
6
]
). For
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
we write an explicit form of the right splitting homomorphism.

Proposition 6.1
.

The Clifford group
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is a natural semidirect product.

Proof.

Consider the natural short exact sequence

1\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{C}_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}SL(2,\mathbb{Z}_{2})\rightarrow 1

We show that it is is right splitting.

The group
SL(2,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is isomorphic to the permutation group
S_{3}
and has a presentation with two generators
s=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)
and
t=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
0&1\end{array}\right)
fulfilling the defining relations
s^{2}=t^{2}=(st)^{3}=id
.

Set two complex matrices
S=\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
1&-1\end{array}\right)
and
T=\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-1&-\mathbf{i}\\
\mathbf{i}&1\end{array}\right)
. It is easy to verify that
S,T\in\mathrm{U}(2)
. Further, it holds that

SP_{2}S^{-1}=Q_{2},\ \ SQ_{2}S^{-1}=P_{2}

TP_{2}T^{-1}=-P_{2},\ \ TQ_{2}T^{-1}=-\mathbf{i}\cdot P_{2}Q_{2}\ .

This implies that
S,T\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
and that the corresponding matrices of automorphisms inducing by
S
and
T
are
s
and
t
, i.e.
\pi(S)=s
and
\pi(T)=t
.

Now, as we can easily check,
S^{2}=T^{2}=(ST)^{3}=I_{2}
, and therefore there is a (unique) homomorphism

\vartheta:SL(2,\mathbb{Z}_{2})\to\mathcal{C}_{2}

such that
\vartheta(s)=S
and
\vartheta(t)=T
. Since
\pi(\vartheta(s))=s
and
\pi(\vartheta(t))=t
and
s,t
are generators of
SL(2,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, we obtain that
\pi\circ\vartheta=id_{SL(2,\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
.
Hence
\vartheta
is the right splitting homomorphisms and, consequently,
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is a natural semidirect product.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\geq 2
be positive integers and
N=n_{1}\cdots n_{k}
. If
N=2
(mod
4
), then both Clifford group
\mathcal{C}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
and the projective Clifford group
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}
are natural semidirect products.

Proof.

If
N=2
, then the assertion follows from Propositions
3.1
and
6.1
.

If
N>2
, then
N=2m
for some odd integer
m\geq 3
. Then there are positive integers
\ell\geq 1
and
m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell}\geq 2
such that
\mathbb{Z}_{n_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{n_{k}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus B
where
B=\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{m_{\ell}}
an the order of
B
is odd. According to Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
, it is enough to show that the exact sequence

(6)

1\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{(2,m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{C}_{(2,m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathrm{Sp}_{[2,m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell}]}\rightarrow 1.

is right splitting. Since
\gcd(2,n_{i})=1
for every
i=1,\dots,k
, it follows, according to the Definition
2.3
of
\mathrm{Sp}_{[2,m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell}]}
, that every element of this group is of the form of block matrices
\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{H}&0\\
0&\mathbb{G}\end{array}\right]
where

[\mathbb{H}]\in\mathrm{Sp}_{2}=SL(2,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
and
[\mathbb{G}]\in\mathrm{Sp}_{[m_{1},\dots,m_{k}]}
. The abelian groups of odd order provide right splitting of the natural exact sequences for their Clifford groups (for the proof see e.g.
[
6
]
). Hence, as
B
is of odd order, for the exact sequence

1\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{(m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu^{\prime}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{C}_{(m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{\prime}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathrm{Sp}_{[m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell}]}\rightarrow 1.

there is a right splitting homomorphism
\vartheta_{1}:\mathrm{Sp}_{[m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell}]}\to\mathcal{C}_{(m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell})}
such that
\pi^{\prime}\circ\vartheta_{1}=id_{\mathrm{Sp}_{[m_{1},\dots,m_{\ell}]}}
. Further, by Proposition
6.1
, there is a right splitting homomorphism
\vartheta_{2}:SL(2,\mathbb{Z}_{2})\to\mathcal{C}_{2}
such that
\pi^{\prime\prime}\circ\vartheta_{2}=id_{SL(2,\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
for the exact sequence

1\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu^{\prime\prime}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{C}_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{\prime\prime}}}{{\longrightarrow}}SL(2,\mathbb{Z}_{2})\rightarrow 1.

Now, we set

\vartheta\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{H}&0\\
0&\mathbb{G}\end{array}\right]\right)=\vartheta_{2}([\mathbb{H}])\otimes\vartheta_{1}([\mathbb{G}])

and it can be easily checked that
\vartheta
is a right splitting homomorphism for the exact sequence (
6
).
∎

7.
Conclusions

In this paper we have fully answered a natural question coming already from classical mechanics:
is the Clifford group a semidirect product?

The answer (see Theorems
5.2
and
6.2
) is positive if and only if the dimension of the underlying Hilbert space is
not
divisible by four. Although in even dimensions the metaplectic group is usually considered (as a covering of the symplectic group) and half-integer lattice states are used, the case when these are
not
needed (i.e. the case of dimension
2m
with
m
beeing odd) seemed to be not known so far in full generality. In applications, usually higher power of two appear, so then the metaplectic group is necessary indeed, but the special single case of the dimension
2m
,
m
odd, sugests a question what of theoretical relations and applications it may allow.
