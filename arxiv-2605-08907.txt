The Status of Gravitational Vector Perturbations with Recent CMB Data
Cyril Pitrou

Abstract
We present new constraints on gravitational vector perturbations ( 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -modes) using Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) data, including temperature and E E -mode polarization from SPT-3G D1 , ACT-DR6 , and Planck , as well as B B -mode data from BICEP/Keck and SPTpol, which provide the strongest constraints on 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -modes. We consider three initial conditions (ICs) that source 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -modes: neutrino isocurvature (ISO), neutrino octupole (OCT), and a sourced mode (SMD) generated by an anisotropic stress before matter–radiation equality. We also consider including tensor modes along with 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -modes for each of these ICs. Combining all datasets, we obtain 95% confidence level upper limits of r v < 1.3 × 10 − 4 r_{\mathrm{v}}<1.3\times 10^{-4} (ISO), r v < 6.8 r_{\mathrm{v}}<6.8 (OCT), and r v < 4.2 r_{\mathrm{v}}<4.2 (SMD), with slightly tighter bounds when tensors are included, at a pivot scale k p = 0.05 k_{p}\ =\ 0.05 Mpc -1 . Interestingly, for SMD without tensors, using SPTpol B B -modes alone yields r v = 4.7 ± 2.1 r_{\mathrm{v}}=4.7\pm 2.1 , consistent with zero at 2.2 ​ σ 2.2\sigma . Similar result is found for SMD when including tensor perturbations. No statistically significant deviation from Λ \Lambda CDM is found. However, 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -modes are not fully excluded by current B B -mode data and should be considered when interpreting primordial signals.

2
Review of
\mathcal{V}
-modes and their Initial Conditions

In this section, we make a short summary of the theory of
\mathcal{V}
-modes and of the three ICs we consider (for more details, see
[
32
]

2.1
\mathcal{V}
-modes

We consider a spatially flat Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric of the form

g_{\mu\nu}=a^{2}(\eta)\big(\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}\big),

(2.1)

where
\eta_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}[-1,1,1,1]
(the Minkowski metric),
h_{\mu\nu}
is the metric perturbation and
a(\eta)
is the scale factor as a function of conformal time,
\eta
. Focusing only on
\mathcal{V}
-modes, we choose the gauge such that the perturbed metric takes the form

h_{00}=0;\ h_{0i}=-\Phi_{i};\ h_{ij}=0,

(2.2)

where
\Phi_{i}
are the (divergenceless)
\mathcal{V}
-modes. After expanding all the relevant quantities using the normal expansion technique (see Section 2.2 of
[
32
]
), we get the following equations of motion for
\mathcal{V}
-modes:

\Phi^{(m)}{}^{\prime}+2{\cal H}\Phi^{(m)}=-\frac{8\pi Ga^{2}}{k}\sum_{s}\bar{p}_{s}\pi_{s}^{(m)},

(2.3)

\Phi^{(m)}=\frac{16\pi Ga^{2}}{k^{2}}\sum_{s}(\bar{\rho}_{s}+\bar{p}_{s})\left(v_{s}^{(m)}-\Phi^{(m)}\right).

(2.4)

where
\Phi^{(m)}
(with
m=\pm\ 1
) are the normal modes of
\Phi_{i}
,
′
denotes a derivative with respect to (w.r.t)
\eta
,
\mathcal{H}={a}^{\prime}/a
,
G
is Newton’s constant,
k
is the wavenumber,
\bar{\rho}_{s}
(
\bar{p}_{s}
) is the background energy density (pressure) of species
s
,
\pi_{s}^{(m)}
is its dimensionless anisotropic stress and
v_{s}^{(m)}
is its (divergenceless) velocity.

The presence of
\mathcal{V}
-modes will alter the evolution equations of CMB anisotropies. For instance, the evolution equation of the CMB temperature anisotropies takes the form

(\Theta_{\ell}^{(m)}){}^{\prime}=k\left[\frac{{}_{0}\kappa_{\ell}^{m}}{2{\ell}-1}\Theta_{{\ell}-1}^{(m)}-\frac{{}_{0}\kappa_{{\ell}+1}^{m}}{2{\ell}+3}\Theta_{{\ell}+1}^{(m)}\right]+{}^{\Theta}{\cal C}_{\ell}^{m}-\tau^{\prime}\Theta_{\ell}^{(m)}\,,\ (\ell\geq m)\,,

(2.5)

where
\Theta_{\ell}^{(m)}
are the temperature multipoles in the Boltzmann hierarchy,
\tau
is the optical depth,

{}_{s}\kappa_{\ell}^{m}\equiv\sqrt{\frac{(\ell^{2}-m^{2})(\ell^{2}-s^{2})}{\ell^{2}}},

(2.6)

and

{}^{\Theta}{\cal C}_{\ell}^{m}=\left(\tau^{\prime}v_{b}^{(m)}+\Phi^{(m)}{}^{\prime}\right)\delta_{\ell 1}+\tau^{\prime}P^{(m)}\delta_{\ell 2};\ P^{(m)}\equiv\frac{1}{10}\left(\Theta_{2}^{(m)}-\sqrt{6}E_{2}^{(m)}\right).

(2.7)

Here,
v_{b}^{(m)}
is the normal mode of the baryon velocity,
\delta_{ij}
the Kronecker delta and
E_{2}^{(m)}
is the quadrupole (
\ell=2
) of the
E
-mode polarization of the CMB. Moreover, the angular power spectra of the CMB will now have an additional contribution due to the presence of
\mathcal{V}
-modes of the form
2
2

2

There will be a similar expression for scalar and tensor perturbations. Eventually, the total CMB spectrum is the sum of the three spectra from each type of perturbations.

C_{\ell}^{XY}=\frac{2}{\pi(2\ell+1)^{2}}\int{\rm d}kk^{2}{\cal T}_{X}(k,\eta){\cal T}{}^{\star}_{Y}(k,\eta)P_{\Phi}(k)

(2.8)

where
X,Y=\{\Theta,E,B\}
,
{\cal T}_{X}(k,\eta)
is the corresponding transfer function (
{\cal T}_{X}{}^{\star}
its complex conjugate) and

P_{\Phi}=r_{\mathrm{v}}A_{s}\bigg(\frac{k}{k_{*}}\bigg)^{n_{v}}

(2.9)

is the primordial
\mathcal{V}
-modes power spectrum. Here,
r_{\mathrm{v}}=A_{\mathrm{v}}/A_{s}
is the amplitude of the
\mathcal{V}
-modes’ power spectrum (
A_{\mathrm{v}}
) relative to that of scalars (
A_{s}
),
n_{v}
is its spectral index and
k_{*}\ =\ 0.05
Mpc
-1
is a pivot scale. Note that this is the same expression as for the
\mathcal{T}
-modes power spectrum except that, assuming a slowly rolling single field inflation, the spectral index (
n_{t}
) and amplitude of
\mathcal{T}
-modes’ power spectrum are related by the self consistency condition (scc)
[
36
]
:

n_{\mathrm{t,scc}}=-\frac{r}{8}\left(2-\frac{r}{8}-n_{s}\right),

(2.10)

where
r=A_{T}/A_{s}
is the amplitude of the
\mathcal{T}
-modes’ power spectrum (
A_{T}
) relative to
A_{s}
, and
n_{s}
is the spectral index of the scalar perturbations’ power spectrum.

2.2
The Three Initial Conditions

We now give a qualitative description of the neutrino isocurvature (ISO)
[
35
,
30
,
47
,
54
,
53
]
, neutrino octupole (OCT) and sourced mode (SMD) initial conditions (see Section 3 of
[
32
]
for more details). The mechanism in common between these three ICs is the need for an initial anisotropy (
\pi^{(m)}_{s}
) to source the
\mathcal{V}
-modes, otherwise they decay as
a^{-2}
and will not survive long enough to make an impact on the CMB (see eq. (
2.3
)).

•

ISO
: Consider the situation where the early Universe is described by a theory beyond the Standard Model (SM) of particle physics. In this case, in order to source the
\mathcal{V}
-modes through eq. (
2.3
), one must have modifications to the weak nuclear interactions such that neutrinos could decouple with a velocity different from that of photons. This velocity difference will create a neutrino anisotropic stress (
\pi^{(m)}_{\nu}
) that sources the
\mathcal{V}
-modes.

•

OCT
: In the primordial Universe, all particles are in equilibrium and tightly coupled to each other. This means that all particles, specifically photons and neutrinos, have their Boltzmann hierarchy truncated at the quadrupole (
\ell=2
) level. However, if there are mechanisms beyond the SM that can break this tight coupling, this would be compatible with a non-vanishing value of the higher multipoles, in particular the neutrino octupole (
\ell=3
). In this case, the neutrino octupole will source its quadrupole, which will then source the
\mathcal{V}
-modes, allowing them (in principle) to survive long enough to have an imprint on the CMB.

•

SMD
: In this IC, we consider a toy model in which the
\mathcal{V}
-modes are directly sourced, at a redshift
z_{\mathrm{start}}
by an anisotropic stress of agnostic origin (this could be from topological defects
[
21
]
, for instance). More specifically, we consider an anisotropic stress of the form:

\pi_{s}=\pi_{*}\delta(z-z_{\mathrm{start}}),

(2.11)

where
\pi_{*}
is a constant and
\delta(z-z_{\mathrm{start}})
is the Dirac delta function. We then compute the amplitude of
\mathcal{V}
-modes relative to that at an arbitrary reference redshift, which we set to be
z_{\mathrm{ref}}=10^{4}
. Although this IC is more phenomenological than the previous two, it has a better chance of being explained by additional primordial physics, which is a topic that we will consider in a future work.

We finish this section by showing the shape of the CMB spectra,

{\cal D}_{\ell}^{XY}=\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{2\pi}C_{\ell}^{XY}

(2.12)

due to the different types of perturbations in Figure
1
. We set the amplitudes and spectral indices of all the perturbations equal to each other in order to focus on the intrinsic difference between these spectra.

CMB spectra,
{\cal D}_{\ell}
(eq. (
2.12
)), for the three different
\mathcal{V}
-modes considered in this work compared to those of Scalar and Tensor modes. For illustration, we set the

z_{*}=10^{7}

5
Conclusion

In this work, we present novel constraints on gravitational Vector modes (
\mathcal{V}
-modes) sourced with three different initial conditions (ICs). The first, called neutrino isocurvature (ISO), stems from a velocity difference between photons and neutrinos in the primordial universe. This velocity difference creates an anisotropic stress that sources
\mathcal{V}
-modes. The second IC, dubbed neutrino octupole (OCT), sources
\mathcal{V}
-modes by assuming a non-vanishing primordial value of the neutrino octupole. The latter will source the neutrino quadrupole, which then sources the
\mathcal{V}
-modes. The last IC, named sourced mode (SMD), assumes the presence of an anisotropic stress that sharply sources
\mathcal{V}
-modes at a specific redshift (
z_{\mathrm{start}}
) before matter-radiation equality. Our baseline SMD model has
z_{\mathrm{start}}=10^{4}
, but we also consider the situation where this is a free parameter. Finally, we also consider the presence of tensor perturbations (
\mathcal{T}
-modes), either alone or with
\mathcal{V}
-modes for each of these ICs.

Using CMB
TT
,
TE
and
EE
data from
SPT-3G D1
,
ACT-DR6
and
Planck
(collectively abbreviated as SPA), along with
BB
data from BICEP/Keck (BK18) and SPTpol, we find no statistically significant evidence for
\mathcal{V}
-modes. However, their presence is not definitely excluded, and the most up to date upper limits on their amplitude with SPA + SPTpolBB + BK18 are
r_{\mathrm{v}}<1.3\times 10^{-4}
,
r_{\mathrm{v}}<6.8
, and
r_{\mathrm{v}}<4.2
for the three ICs, respectively. We also find that ISO and SMD ICs have, respectively, a slightly blue-tilted and red-tilted spectra, though they are consistent with scale invariance. On the other hand, the OCT IC shows a highly blue-tilted spectrum, deviating from scale invariance at
\sim\ 7\sigma
. When adding
\mathcal{T}
-modes, we find a slightly lower upper limits on
r_{\mathrm{v}}
and
r
(the amplitude of
\mathcal{T}
-modes) for each IC, and no appreciable change in the spectral index of
\mathcal{V}
-modes,
n_{v}
.

When constraining SMD with SPA + SPTpolBB, we find
r_{\mathrm{v}}\ =\ 4.7\pm 2.1
(
4.8\pm 2.2
when
\mathcal{T}
-modes are included), which is the only case considered here that shows such a constraint. However, this is consistent with
r_{\mathrm{v}}=0
at the
\sim 2\sigma
level, i.e. consistent with being a statistical fluctuation above 0, and when BK18 is included such a constraint goes back to being just an upper limit
r_{\mathrm{v}}<4.2
(
<4.0
when
\mathcal{T}
-modes are included).

Our results have two important implications. First, they reinforce the importance of CMB
B
-mode measurements in constraining the fundamental properties of the Universe, further motivating upcoming measurements by LiteBIRD
[
25
,
6
]
, BICEP Array
[
43
,
2
]
, and the South Pole Telescope. Second, they indicate that
\mathcal{V}
-modes remain an interesting subject of investigation, particularly in the context of fundamental theories, such as modified gravity, loop quantum gravity or brane-world cosmology
[
11
,
13
,
64
,
41
,
38
,
23
,
24
]
. This, in turn, opens the possibility of testing these theories through the imprint of their
\mathcal{V}
-modes on the CMB.
