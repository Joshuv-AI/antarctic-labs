On the classification of indecomposable Ekedahl-Oort strata in unitary Shimura varieties, and related Newton polygons
Emerald Andrews, Deewang Bhamidipati, Maria Fox, Heidi Goodson, Steven R. Groen, Sandra Nair

Abstract
. In this paper, we give a complete classification of indecomposable Ekedahl-Oort strata of Shimura varieties associated to the unitary group 𝖦𝖴 ⁡ ( a , b ) \mathsf{GU}(a,b) over an odd inert prime. We show that each indecomposable stratum is one of four types: unitary unicycle, unitary bicycle, Serre unicycle, or Serre bicycle; the latter two types are named for a tensor construction of abelian varieties developed by Serre. We provide an algorithm that translates the description of a stratum in terms of words in the alphabet { f , v } \{\textnormal{{{f}}},\textnormal{{{v}}}\} to the corresponding Weyl group coset representative. Finally, using a p p -adic lift, we construct a ‘tautological’ point in each Ekedahl-Oort stratum, and compute its Newton polygon. As an application, we show that the indecomposable Ekedahl-Oort strata corresponding to unitary unicycles and Serre unicycles always intersect the supersingular locus.

1.
Introduction

Let
A
be an abelian variety of dimension
q
defined over
\mathbx k
, an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
. In this positive characteristic setting, an interesting invariant is its
p
-torsion group subscheme
A[p]
, which is a finite commutative
\mathbx k
-group scheme of rank
p^{2q}
. Such group schemes may be studied using linear algebraic methods via Dieudonné theory, which associates to a finite commutative
\mathbx k
-group scheme annihilated by
p
a finite-dimensional
\mathbx k
-vector space equipped with operators
F
and
V
(Frobenius and Verschiebung), called a mod-
p
Dieudonné module.

In an unpublished manuscript
[
17
]
, Kraft establishes a structure theorem for mod-
p
Dieudonné modules by describing
indecomposable
mod-
p
Dieudonné modules, those that cannot be decomposed as a direct sum of (lower-dimensional) mod-
p
Dieudonné modules. This allows for an elegant description of the (finitely many) possible isomorphism types of mod-
p
Dieudonné modules of a fixed
\mathbx k
-dimension.

The mod-
p
Dieudonné modules that arise from
p
-torsion group schemes of abelian varieties possess a constraint in terms of the operators
F
and
V
; they therefore belong to a sub-class of mod-
p
Dieudonné modules called
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules. Beginning with the work of Ekedahl and Oort
[
21
]
, isomorphism types of
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules have been used to establish a stratification, called the Ekedahl-Oort stratification, of characteristic
p
fibers of Shimura varieties of PEL type, which have a moduli interpretation as moduli spaces of abelian varieties over
\mathbx k
with additional structure.

The additional structure on an abelian variety parameterized by a Shimura varieties of PEL type imparts analogous additional structure on the associated
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules. For example, in the case of the characteristic
p
Siegel modular variety
\mathcal{A}_{q}
, which parameterizes polarized abelian varieties over
\mathbx k
of dimension
q
, the associated
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules admit a symplectic form and are called
polarized

\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules.

In this paper, we are interested in the
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules arising from characteristic
p
fibers of unitary Shimura varieties for
p>2
. The characteristic
p
unitary Shimura variety
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
is the moduli space of
(a+b)
-dimensional abelian varieties over
\mathbx k
with a prime-to-
p
quasi-polarization, an action by an order in an imaginary quadratic field wherein
p
is inert, and level structure. The action satisfies a “signature
(a,b)
” condition. The associated
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules admit additional structure and are named
unitary

\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules.

One of the main results of the present paper is the classification of
indecomposable
unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules in arbitrary signature (see
Theorem

4.21
).
Geometrically, an indecomposable unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module corresponds to an Ekedahl-Oort stratum of a unitary Shimura variety
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
which is disjoint from the images of the
product maps

\mathcal{M}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\times\mathcal{M}(a-a^{\prime},b-b^{\prime})\to\mathcal{M}(a,b),

from unitary Shimura varieties of lower signatures, given by taking the product of the parameterized abelian varieties and their additional structure.

For the case of unitary Shimura varieties with signature
(q-1,1)
,
[
6
]
gives a complete description of the structure of indecomposable Ekedahl-Oort strata, using mod-
p
Dieudonné modules and a “braid construction”. This is further expanded in
[
27
]
. In
[
1
, Section 4]
, we give a way to generate all decomposable Ekedahl-Oort strata of unitary Shimura varieties with signature
(q-2,2)
; we identify decomposable strata by exhibiting that they are in the images of product maps from unitary Shimura varieties of lower signature.

In this paper, we use the classical construction of Kraft diagrams
[
17
]
and words in the letters
\left\{\text{{f}, {v}}\right\}
to classify the indecomposable unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules. As a starting point, we expand on results of
[
21
,
23
]
through a study of indecomposable polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules (see
Section

3
). In
Section

4
, we turn our attention to unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules and show that there are four indecomposable types: unitary unicycles and bicycles (named for the number of “wheels” in the Kraft diagrams) and Serre unicycles and bicycles (named for their relation to the Serre tensor construction for abelian varieties). Moreover, every unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module decomposes uniquely as a direct sum of these indecomposable types.

Results of
[
20
]
establish a correspondence between Ekedahl-Oort strata of unitary Shimura varieties and cosets in certain Weyl groups. One advantage of this description is that the topological ordering between Ekedahl-Oort strata based on closure relations is captured directly by the partial ordering first developed by Bruhat and later generalized by He (see, for example,
[
1
,
5
,
14
,
24
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
).
In this present work, we give a method for going from words
in the letters
\left\{\text{{f}, {v}}\right\}

to Weyl group coset representatives expressed using the index set defined in
[
1
, Section 3]
. This process is described in
Section

5.2
.

Another invariant of interest for an abelian variety
A
over
\mathbx k
is its
p
-divisible group
A[p^{\infty}]
. The isogeny-types of
p
-divisible groups yield a different stratification of Shimura varieties of PEL type, called the Newton stratification. In addition to studying the Ekedahl-Oort stratification by itself, one may also study how it interacts with the Newton stratification. More specifically, one can ask when a given Ekedahl-Oort stratum and a given Newton stratum intersect.

This is studied for general PEL type Shimura varieties in
[
28
]
, but more can be said if one restricts to a smaller class of Shimura varieties. Such interactions between the stratifications are well-known for Shimura varieties of Coxeter type
[
10
]
. This includes unitary Shimura varieties in signature
(q,0)
and
(q-1,1)

[
6
,
27
]
, as well as the unitary Shimura variety in signature
(2,2)

[
10
,
11
,
15
]
. In these cases, every Newton stratum is a union of Ekedahl-Oort strata.
Beyond these signatures, the situation is more nuanced since the varieties are no longer of Coxeter type, making it challenging to explicitly describe the intersection behavior.

The two stratifications for signature
(q-2,2)
unitary Shimura varieties were studied in
[
1
,
25
]
, with the techniques in
[
25
]
primarily focusing on the interaction of the supersingular locus, the unique closed stratum of the Newton stratification, with the Ekedahl-Oort stratification. Studying Newton strata beyond the supersingular locus provides additional challenges, but has recently been done in some specific cases. For example, complete descriptions of the interactions between the two stratifications were given for the unitary Shimura variety with signature
(3,2)

[
2
]
and the Siegel modular variety
\mathcal{A}_{5}

[
12
]
, which both parameterize certain abelian varieties of dimension
5
.

In
Section

6
of this paper, we expand on the literature through a study of the interaction between the Ekedahl-Oort and Newton stratifications of unitary Shimura varieties in arbitrary signature. Using an extension of the tautological lift construction developed in
[
22
]
, we construct a point in each Ekedahl-Oort stratum and compute its Newton polygon (see
Theorem

6.10
), implying that the given Ekedahl-Oort stratum and Newton stratum intersect. As a special case, we show that Ekedahl-Oort strata that decompose into unitary unicycles and Serre unicycles always intersect the supersingular locus of the Newton stratification (see
Corollaries

6.12
and
6.13
).

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
\mathbx k
is an algebraically closed field of prime characteristic
p>2
. We study invariants associated with abelian varieties parameterized by certain Shimura varieties.

2.1.
Shimura varieties

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field such that
p
is inert in
K
, with residue field
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}
at
p
. The unitary Shimura variety
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
arises as the characteristic
p
fiber of an integral unitary Shimura variety associated to a PEL datum, constructed by Kottwitz
[
16
]
; see
[
26
]
or
[
1
, Section 2.1]
for a detailed definition. It is a quasi-projective variety over
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}
of dimension
ab
.

The
\mathbx k
-points of
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
correspond to isomorphism classes of tuples
(A,\lambda,\iota,\xi)
, where

\bullet

A
is an abelian variety over
\mathbx k
of dimension
a+b
;

\bullet

\lambda:A\overset{\sim}{\to}A^{\vee}
is a prime-to-
p
quasi-polarization;

\bullet

\iota:\mathcal{O}_{K}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{End}(A)
is an action of signature
(a,b)
satisfying the Rosati involution condition; and

\bullet

\xi:A[N](\mathbx k)\to(\mathbx Z/N\mathbx Z)^{2(a+b)}
is a level structure on
A
for some
N
coprime to
p
;

subject to some compatibility conditions.

The characteristic
p
Siegel modular variety, denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{q}
, is a quasi-projective variety over
\mathbx F_{p}
of dimension
q(q+1)/{2}
. Its
\mathbx k
-points correspond to isomorphism classes of
(A,\lambda,\xi)
, where

\bullet

A
is an abelian variety over
\mathbx k
of dimension
q
;

\bullet

\lambda:A\overset{\sim}{\to}A^{\vee}
is a prime-to-
p
quasi-polarization; and

\bullet

\xi:A[N](\mathbx k)\to(\mathbx Z/N\mathbx Z)^{2q}
is a level structure on
A
for some
N
coprime to
p
,

subject to some compatibility conditions.

2.2.
The Ekedahl-Oort and Newton stratification

An effective method of studying unitary Shimura varieties and Siegel modular varieties or, equivalently, the abelian varieties they parameterize, is via the
Ekedahl-Oort
and
Newton stratifications
of these Shimura varieties. We briefly give the moduli description of these stratifications for the unitary Shimura variety
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
. These can be analogously described for the Siegel modular variety
\mathcal{A}_{q}
.

The Ekedahl-Oort stratification is based on the isomorphism class of the
p
-torsion group scheme of the parameterized abelian varieties. Two
\mathbx k
-points
(A,\lambda,\iota,\xi)
and
(A^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime},\iota^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})
of
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
are in the same
Ekedahl-Oort stratum
if and only if the
p
-torsion group schemes equipped with induced action and polarization,
(A[p],\lambda,\iota)
and
(A^{\prime}[p],\lambda^{\prime},\iota^{\prime})
, are isomorphic as group schemes over
\mathbx k
. The Ekedahl-Oort strata are locally closed, and the closure of each Ekedahl-Oort stratum is a union of Ekedahl-Oort strata. For more details on Ekedahl-Oort stratifications, see
[
1
,
28
]
.

The Newton stratification is based on the isogeny class of the
p
-divisible group of the parameterized abelian varieties. Two
\mathbx k
-points
(A,\lambda,\iota,\xi)
and
(A^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime},\iota^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})
of
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
are in the same
Newton stratum
if and only if the
p
-divisible groups equipped with induced action and polarization,
(A[p^{\infty}],\lambda,\iota)
and
(A^{\prime}[p^{\infty}],\lambda^{\prime},\iota^{\prime})
, are isogenous over
\mathbx k
. The Newton strata are also locally closed, and the closure of each Newton stratum is a union of Newton strata. The unique closed Newton stratum of a Shimura variety is called the
basic locus
; in the case of
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
this is the supersingular locus, which we denote as
\mathcal{M}(a,b)^{\textnormal{ss}}
. A
\mathbx k
-point
(A,\lambda,\iota,\xi)
of
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
lies in
\mathcal{M}(a,b)^{\textnormal{ss}}
if and only if
A
is a supersingular abelian variety.

2.3.
Dieudonné Theory

The Ekedahl-Oort and Newton stratifications are defined in terms of finite commutative
p
-torsion group schemes and
p
-divisible groups. There is an equivalence between these objects and certain modules over
\mathbx{W}\coloneqq\mathbx{W}(\mathbx k)
, the ring of Witt vectors of
\mathbx k
, by Dieudonné theory. We briefly recall this equivalence (more detail can be found in
[
9
]
).

Define the (non-commutative)
Dieudonné ring
to be the following quotient of the free
\mathbx{W}
-algebra generated by letters
\mathbx{F}
and
\mathbx{V}
:

\mathbx{W}\left\{\mathbx{F},\mathbx{V}\right\}\coloneqq\mathbx{W}\langle\mathbx{F},\mathbx{V}\rangle/\langle\mathbx{F}a-a^{\sigma}\mathbx{F},\,\mathbx{V}a^{\sigma}-a\mathbx{V},\mathbx{F}\mathbx{V}-p,\mathbx{V}\mathbx{F}-p\ :\ a\in\mathbx{W}\rangle,

where
\sigma=\mathbx{W}(\mathrm{\mathsf{Frob}})
is the lift of the Frobenius automorphism
\mathrm{\mathsf{Frob}}\colon x\mapsto x^{p}
on
\mathbx k
to
\mathbx{W}
.

A Dieudonné module over
\mathbx k
is a left
\mathbx{W}\left\{\mathbx{F},\mathbx{V}\right\}
-module which is finitely generated as a
\mathbx{W}
-module. There is a canonical categorical anti-equivalence, called the
Dieudonné equivalence
and denoted
\mathbx{D}
, from the category of finite commutative
p
-torsion group schemes (resp.
p
-divisible groups) over
\mathbx k
to the category of Dieudonné modules over
\mathbx k
that are of finite
\mathbx{W}
-length and annihilated by
p
(resp. free and of finite rank as
\mathbx{W}
-modules). Note that this is the statement of
contravariant Dieudonné theory
; in this we differ here from
[
9
]
, which discusses
covariant Dieudonné theory
.

In practice, it is useful to work with the following equivalent descriptions of the objects in the essential image(s) of
\mathbx{D}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
p
-adic Dieudonné module
over
\mathbx k
is a triple
\overline{\mathbx{M}}=(\mathbx{M},\mathbx{F},\mathbx{V})
such that

(1)

\mathbx{M}
is a free
\mathbx{W}
-module with finite rank;

(2)

\mathbx{F}:\mathbx{M}\to\mathbx{M}
is a
\sigma
-linear map;

(3)

\mathbx{V}:\mathbx{M}\to\mathbx{M}
is a
\sigma^{-1}
-linear map; and

(4)

\mathbx{F}\mathbx{V}=\mathbx{V}\mathbx{F}=p
.

A
p
-adic Dieudonné module over
\mathbx k
is equivalent to a Dieudonné module over
\mathbx k
that is free and of finite rank as a
\mathbx{W}
-module. Therefore,
p
-adic Dieudonné modules are precisely those
\mathbx{W}
-modules that arise from
p
-divisible groups.

Reducing the Dieudonné ring modulo
p
, one obtains the
\mathbx k
-algebra
\mathbx k\left\{F,V\right\}=\mathbx{W}\left\{\mathbx{F},\mathbx{V}\right\}/p\mathbx{W}\left\{\mathbx{F},\mathbx{V}\right\}
; here
F
and
V
are the images of
\mathbx{F}
and
\mathbx{V}
in the quotient respectively. Dieudonné modules over
\mathbx k
that are annihilated by
p
are canonically modules over
\mathbx k\left\{F,V\right\}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
mod-
p
Dieudonné module
over
\mathbx k
is a triple
(M,F,V)
such that

(1)

M
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbx k
-vector space;

(2)

F:M\to M
is a
\mathrm{\mathsf{Frob}}
-linear map;

(3)

V:M\to M
is a
\mathrm{\mathsf{Frob}}^{-1}
-linear map; and

(4)

FV=VF=0
.

A mod-
p
Dieudonné module over
\mathbx k
is equivalent to a Dieudonné module over
\mathbx k
which is of finite
\mathbx{W}
-length and annihilated by
p
. Therefore, mod-
p
Dieudonné modules are precisely those
\mathbx{W}
-modules that arise from finite commutative
p
-torsion group schemes.

\mathrm{BT}_{1}
group schemes are those finite commutative
p
-torsion group schemes that arise as
p
-kernels of
p
-divisible groups. The name “
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
” stands for Barsotti-Tate groups truncated at level 1, where “Barsotti-Tate groups” is another name for
p
-divisible groups. Under the Dieudonné equivalence, the
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
group schemes carve out the full subcategory of
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules in the category of mod-
p
Dieudonné modules.

Definition 2.3
.

A
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
over
\mathbx k
is a triple
\overline{M}=(M,F,V)
such that

(1)

(M,F,V)
is a mod-
p
Dieudonné module; and

(2)

\ker(F)=\operatorname{im}(V)
and
\ker(V)=\operatorname{im}(F)
.

Via the Dieudonné equivalence,
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules are precisely the reductions modulo
p
of
p
-adic Dieudonné modules.
In the case of an abelian variety
A
over
\mathbx k
, its
p
-torsion group scheme
A[p]
is the
p
-kernel of its
p
-divisible group
A[p^{\infty}]
. Therefore
\mathbx{D}(A[p])
is a
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module. Hence the Ekedahl-Oort stratification is determined by isomorphism types of
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules.

The additional structures on the abelian varieties parameterized by the unitary Shimura variety and the Siegel modular variety impart additional structures on the associated
p
-adic Dieudonné modules and thereby on the
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules, as their reductions modulo
p
.

The
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules equipped with additional structure arising from the Siegel modular variety are called
polarized

\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules; these are discussed in
Section

3.2
. Those arising from the unitary Shimura variety are called
unitary

\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules. These modules, explored in
Section

4.1
, will be our primary objects of study. In
Section

6.1
, we work with unitary
p
-adic Dieudonné modules and investigate certain lifts of unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules to
unitary

p
-adic Dieudonné modules, which are
p
-adic Dieudonné modules with additional structure arising from the unitary Shimura variety.

2.4.
Weyl group cosets

Moonen
[
20
]
relates Ekedahl-Oort strata of Shimura varieties of PEL type to cosets in certain Weyl groups. The Weyl group that is relevant for the study of
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
is
\mathfrak{S}_{q}
, the symmetric group on
q=a+b
elements. We consider
\mathfrak{S}_{q}
as a Coxeter group with a set of
simple reflections

S=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{q-1}\},\text{where }s_{i}=(i,\ i+1).

Let
\mathbf{W}_{(a,b)}
denote the subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{q}
generated by the subset
\{s_{1},...,s_{q-1}\}\setminus\{s_{b}\}
of
S
, and let
\mathbf{W}(a,b)
denote the set of minimal-length coset representatives of
\mathbf{W}_{(a,b)}\setminus\mathfrak{S}_{q}
. The following theorem is paraphrased from Theorem 6.7 of
[
20
]
:

Theorem 2.4

(Moonen)
.

There is a bijection of sets:

\left\{\text{Ekedahl-Oort Strata of $\mathcal{M}(a,b)$}\right\}\longleftrightarrow\mathbf{W}(a,b).

We expand on Weyl group cosets associated to unitary Shimura varieties in
Section

5
. Such a correspondence also exists for Ekedahl-Oort strata of Siegel modular varieties
\mathcal{A}_{q}
, where the Weyl group is a certain subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{2q}
. C.f.
[
20
, Section 3]
or
[
1
, Section 5]
for details.

Let

For a word

Remark 3.2
.

For later purposes, it is useful to make the isomorphism

explicit. A precise description of the correspondence relies on the fact that

and

are semilinear operators. Let

and let

be spanned by

, we have

F(a_{j+i\ell})=\begin{cases}a_{j+1+i\ell}&\hbox{if $u_{j}=\textnormal{{{f}}}$,}\\
0&\hbox{if $u_{j}=\textnormal{{{v}}}$,}\end{cases}\hskip 56.9055ptV(a_{j+1+i\ell})=\begin{cases}a_{j+i\ell}&\hbox{if $u_{j}=\textnormal{{{v}}}$,}\\
0&\hbox{if $u_{j}=\textnormal{{{f}}}$.}\end{cases}

Now let

be elements of

that are linearly independent over
\mathbx F_{p}
.
We define the following change of basis: for

b_{k,j}\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\alpha_{k}^{p^{j+i\ell}}a_{j+i\ell}.

We claim that, for each

is isomorphic to

\displaystyle F(b_{k,j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\alpha_{k}^{p^{j+1+i\ell}}a_{j+1+i\ell}=b_{k,j+1}

\displaystyle V(b_{k,j+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\alpha_{k}^{p^{j+i\ell}}a_{j+i\ell}=b_{k,j}

and both equal 0 otherwise.
Crucially, we have

since

An isomorphism in the opposite direction can be obtained in a similar fashion. We may pick

that are linearly independent over

and define

3.2.
Polarized
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{BT}_{1}}
modules

Our next step is to consider
polarized

\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules. These are
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules that can occur as the Dieudonné module of the
p
-torsion group scheme of an abelian variety that admits a prime-to-
p
polarization. For this reason, isomorphism classes of polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules of
\mathbx k
-dimension
2q
correspond to Ekedahl-Oort strata of
\mathcal{A}_{q}
. In what follows, we work towards stating Oort’s structure theorem (
Proposition

3.11
) and include additional details that will be used later in the paper.

Definition 3.5
.

A
polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
is a quadruple
\widehat{M}=(M,F,V,\lambda)
such that

(1)

(M,F,V)
is a
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module;

(2)

\lambda:M\times M\to k
is a symplectic (bilinear, alternating, non-degenerate) pairing; and

(3)

\lambda(F(x),y)=\lambda(x,V(y))^{p}
for every
x,y\in M
.

We say a
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
(M,F,V)
is
polarizable
if there exists a polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
(M,F,V,\lambda)
. By
[
23
, Corollary 4.2]
, the pairing
\lambda
is unique, up to isomorphism, if it exists. Two polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules are isomorphic if and only if their underlying
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules are isomorphic. It is a consequence of Dieudonné theory that a
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\overline{M}
is polarizable if and only if it is isomorphic to its dual
\mathbx k\left\{F,V\right\}
-module.

Given a word
w=u_{\ell-1}\cdots u_{0}
, we define its
dual word

w^{\star}
to be the word obtained from
w
by changing every
f
to
v
and vice versa. A word
w
is called
self-dual
if
w^{\star}=\tau^{n}(w)
for some
n
.

For example,
(\textnormal{{{ffffv}}})^{\star}=\textnormal{{{vvvvf}}}
, and
fffvvv
is self-dual, since
(\textnormal{{{fffvvv}}})^{\star}=\textnormal{{{vvvfff}}}=\tau^{3}({\textnormal{{{fffvvv}}}})
.

Since the dual module of
\overline{M}(w)
is
\overline{M}(w^{\star})
by Dieudonné theory, it follows that
\overline{M}(w)\cong\overline{M}(w^{\star})
if and only if
w
is self-dual.

3.2.1.
Unicycles

We now focus on
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules
\overline{M}(w)
that are self-dual (thus polarizable). This is equivalent to the word
w
being self-dual. We begin by classifying what such words look like.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
w
be a self-dual word. Then
w=(yy^{\star})^{n}
for some word
y
such that
yy^{\star}
is primitive, and for some integer
n>0
.

Proof.

First, note that
w=z^{n}
for some primitive word
z
and some integer
n>0
, and
w
is self-dual if and only if
z
is as well. Thus, it suffices to show that
z
is of the desired form.

Since
z=u_{\ell-1}\cdots u_{0}
is self-dual, for some
0<m<\ell
, we have that
\tau^{m}(z)=z^{\star}
. Then for every
0\leqslant i<\ell-m
, we have that
u_{i+m}=u_{i}^{\star}
and for
\ell-m\leqslant i\leqslant\ell-1
, we have that
u_{i+m-\ell}=u_{i}^{\star}
. As a consequence, we have
\tau^{2m}(z)=z
. Now, since
z
is primitive, this implies
2m=\ell
. Thus the lemma follows by letting
y
be the first
m
letters of
z
.
∎

Note that the word
y
here need not itself be primitive. For instance, taking
y=\textnormal{{{ff}}}
results in the primitive word
w=\textnormal{{{ffvv}}}
.

We now describe explicitly the pairing
\lambda
on
\overline{M}(w)
when
w
is self-dual.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
w=(u_{\ell-1}\cdots u_{0}u_{\ell-1}^{\star}\cdots u_{0}^{\star})^{n}
be a self-dual word and consider the
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\overline{M}(w)=(M,F,V)
with basis
\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{2n\ell-1}\}
. Fix
c\in\mathbx k^{\times}
satisfying
c^{p^{\ell}}=-c
. Define a bilinear pairing by

\lambda(a_{i},a_{j})=\begin{cases}c^{p^{i}}&\hbox{if $j\equiv i+\ell\pmod{2n\ell}$,}\\
0&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then
\widehat{M}=(M,F,V,\lambda)
is a polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module.

Proof.

By construction,
(M,F,V)
is a
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module and
\lambda
is a non-degenerate bilinear pairing. We first show
\lambda
is alternating. If
j=i+\ell
, then

(3.A)

\lambda(a_{j},a_{i})=c^{p^{j}}=c^{p^{i+\ell}}=\left(c^{p^{\ell}}\right)^{p^{i}}=(-c)^{p^{i}}=-c^{p^{i}}=-\lambda(a_{i},a_{j}).

Furthermore, if
j=i+\ell-2n\ell,
note that
c^{p^{2\ell}}=c
, so that

\lambda(a_{j},a_{i})=c^{p^{j}}=c^{p^{i+\ell-2n\ell}}=\left(c^{p^{-2n\ell}}\right)^{p^{i+\ell}}=c^{p^{i+\ell}}=-\lambda(a_{i},a_{j}),

where the last equality holds by Equation (
3.A
).

It remains to check the condition

(3.B)

\lambda(F(x),y)=\lambda(x,V(y))^{p}.

It suffices to check this on the basis
\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{2n\ell-1}\}
, so let
x=a_{i}
and
y=a_{j}
. By the definition of
\lambda
, both sides of Equation (
3.B
) vanish unless
j=i+1+\ell
or
j=i+1+\ell-2n\ell
, both of which imply
u_{i}=u_{j-1}^{\star}
. We consider the two options for
u_{i}
.

(1)

Suppose
u_{i}=\textnormal{{{f}}}
. Then
u_{j-1}=\textnormal{{{v}}}
, and the construction of
\overline{M}(w)
yields
F(a_{i})=a_{i+1}
and
V(a_{j})=a_{j-1}
. Hence we obtain

\lambda(F(a_{i}),a_{j})=\lambda(a_{i+1},a_{j})=c^{p^{i+1}}=\lambda(a_{i},a_{j-1})^{p}=\lambda(a_{i},V(a_{j}))^{p},

as desired. Note that this also holds when
i=2\ell-1
, since
c^{p^{2\ell}}=c
.

(2)

Suppose
u_{i}=\textnormal{{{v}}}
. Then
u_{j-1}=\textnormal{{{f}}}
, so
F(a_{i})=V(a_{j})=0
.∎

Definition 3.8
.

For a primitive, self-dual word
w
, the polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widehat{M}(w)
in
Lemma

3.7
is called a
unicycle
. The underlying
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module of
\widehat{M}(w)
is
\overline{M}(w)
.

Observe that unicycles are invariant under rotation:
\widehat{M}(\tau^{n}(w))\cong\widehat{M}(w)
for every
n
.

3.2.2.
Bicycles

Note that, for any word
w
, the
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\overline{M}(w)\oplus\overline{M}(w^{\star})
is self-dual, as the summands are each other’s dual. We now detail the pairing on a
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module of this form.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
w=u_{\ell-1}\cdots u_{0}
be a word and consider the
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules
\overline{M}(w)
, with basis
\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{\ell-1}\}
, and
\overline{M}(w^{\star})
, with basis
\{b_{0},\ldots,b_{\ell-1}\}
. Let
(M,F,V)=\overline{M}(w)\oplus\overline{M}(w^{\star})
. Define the pairing

\lambda(a_{i},b_{j})=\delta_{i,j},

where
\delta_{i,j}
is the Kronecker delta function, and extend this symplectically. Then
\widehat{M}=(M,F,V,\lambda)
is a polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module.

Proof.

It suffices to check that
\lambda(F(a_{i}),b_{j})=\lambda(a_{i},V(b_{j}))^{p}
for each
i,j
. Note that both sides of this equation vanish unless
j=i+1
. For
j=i+1
, there are two possibilities:

(1)

Suppose
u_{i}=\textnormal{{{f}}}
, so that
F(a_{i})=a_{i+1}
. In this case, we have
u_{i}^{\star}=\textnormal{{{v}}}
, which implies that
V(b_{i+1})=b_{i}
. Hence,

\lambda(F(a_{i}),b_{i+1})=\lambda(a_{i+1},b_{i+1})=1=\lambda(a_{i},b_{i})^{p}=\lambda(a_{i},V(b_{i+1}))^{p}.

(2)

Suppose
u_{i}=\textnormal{{{v}}}
, so that
F(a_{i})=V(b_{i+1})=0
. Then

\lambda(F(a_{i}),b_{i+1})=\lambda(a_{i},V(b_{i+1}))^{p}=0.\qed

Definition 3.10
.

For a primitive, non-self-dual word
w
, the polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widehat{M}(w,w^{\star})
in
Lemma

3.9
is called a
bicycle
. The underlying
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module of
\widehat{M}(w,w^{\star})
is
\overline{M}(w)\oplus\overline{M}(w^{\star})
.

Observe that bicycles are invariant under rotation:
\widehat{M}(w,w^{\star})\cong\widehat{M}(\tau(w),\tau(w)^{\star})
. Bicycles are also invariant under dualizing
w
: we have
\widehat{M}(w,w^{\star})\cong\widehat{M}(w^{\star},w)
.

3.2.3.
Structure theorem

We can now state the structure theorem for polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules.

Proposition 3.11

(
21
, 9.9)
.

Any polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widehat{M}=(M,F,V,\lambda)
decomposes as follows

\widehat{M}=\bigoplus_{w\in W_{1}}\widehat{M}(w)\oplus\bigoplus_{w\in W_{2}}\widehat{M}(w,w^{\star}).

Here,
W_{1}
is a multiset of primitive self-dual words and
W_{2}
is a multiset of primitive non-self-dual words. The decomposition is unique up to rotation, dualizing the words in
W_{2}
, and permuting the summands.

Corollary 3.12
.

If
\widehat{M}=(M,F,V,\lambda)
is indecomposable as a polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module, then
\widehat{M}
is isomorphic to either a unicycle or a bicycle.

Remark 3.13
.

For our purposes, a Kraft-style decomposition theorem is essential as it allows us to identify the indecomposable polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules. But one may also prove a weaker semisimplicity result for polarized

Let
\widehat{M}=(M,F,V,\lambda)
be a polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module. If
\widehat{N}=(N,F|_{N},V|_{N},\lambda|_{N\times N})
is a polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module, for a
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
submodule
N
of
M
, we call
\widehat{N}
a
polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
submodule
of
M
. The following lemma is a reframing of
[
4
, Lemma 3.4]
in this language.

Lemma 3.14

(
4
, Lemma 3.4)
.

Let
P=\left\{m\in M\ \middle|\ \lambda_{M}(m,N)=0\right\}
be the symplectic complement
of
N
in
M
. Then
\widehat{P}=(P,F_{M}|_{P},V_{M}|_{P},\lambda_{M}|_{P\times P})
is a polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}

4.
Decomposing Unitary
{\mathrm{BT}_{1}}
modules

The goal of this section is to establish an analogue
of
Propositions

3.3
and
3.11
for
unitary

\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules. This is achieved in

.

4.1.
Unitary
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{BT}_{1}}
modules

In what follows, if
A,B
are
\mathbx k
-vector spaces,
h:A\to B
is a linear map, and
C
is a subspace of
A
, then we define
C[h]\coloneqq\ker(h|_{C}).

Definition 4.1
.

A
unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
is a quintuple
\widetilde{M}=(M,F,V,\lambda,M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1})
such that

(1)

(M,F,V,\lambda)
is a polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module;

(2)

F
and
V
are homogeneous of degree
1
with respect to the decomposition
M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1}
(i.e. they exchange
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
); and

(3)

M_{0}
and
M_{1}
are Lagrangian with respect to
\lambda

(this is called the
Rosati involution condition
).

Following
[
20
, (4.6)]
, we define
(a,b)\coloneqq(\text{dim}_{\mathbx k}(M_{1}[F]),\text{dim}_{\mathbx k}(M_{0}[F]))

to be the
signature
of the unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
(M,F,V,\lambda,M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1})
. We will write
q\coloneqq a+b
, so that
\dim kM=2q
.

Remark 4.2
.

Properties (
2
) and (
3
) in
Definition

4.1
are equivalent to equipping
M
with an action
\iota:\mathbx F_{p^{2}}\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathbx k}(M)
of
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}
such that it satisfies the
Rosati involution condition

\lambda(\iota(\alpha)m,m^{\prime})=\lambda(m,\iota(\alpha^{p})m^{\prime})

for
\alpha\in\mathbx F_{p^{2}}
and
m,m^{\prime}\in M
. With this condition we have

M_{0}=\left\{m\in M\ \middle|\ \iota(\alpha)m=\alpha m\right\}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{and}\hskip 28.45274ptM_{1}=\left\{m\in M\ \middle|\ \iota(\alpha)m=\alpha^{p}m\right\}.

Therefore, a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widetilde{M}=(M,F,V,\lambda,M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1})
may equivalently be expressed as the quintuple
\widetilde{M}=(M,F,V,\lambda,\iota)
, where
\iota
is the action of
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}
corresponding to the decomposition
M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1}
.

Just like how isomorphism classes of polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules of
\mathbx k
-dimension
2q
correspond to Ekedahl-Oort strata of
\mathcal{A}_{q}
, isomorphism classes of unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules of signature
(a,b)
correspond to Ekedahl-Oort strata of the unitary Shimura variety
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
.

We say that a
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
(M,F,V)
, or a polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
(M,F,V,\lambda)
,
admits unitary structure
if there exists a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
(M,F,V,\lambda,M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1})
. Note that two different unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules can have the same underlying (polarized)
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module. For instance, there are superspecial unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules of every signature. There are also pairs of non-isomorphic unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules with the same signature and the same underlying polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module.

Given a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widetilde{M}=(M,F,V,\lambda,M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1})
, we can form its
conjugate unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module

\widetilde{M}^{*}
by exchanging
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
. That is, define

\widetilde{M}^{*}\coloneqq(M,F,V,\lambda,M=M_{1}\oplus M_{0}).

From the definition, it is clear that
\widetilde{M}^{**}=\widetilde{M}
and
\widetilde{M}^{*}
has signature
(b,a)
.

In this section, we will prove a Kraft-style decomposition theorem for unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules, similar to Oort’s result for polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules (
Proposition

3.11
). This will allow us to identify the indecomposable unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules. But one may state and prove a weaker semisimplicity result for unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules as well, similar to
Remark

3.13
for polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules.

Remark 4.3
.

Let
\widetilde{M}=(M,F,V,\lambda,M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1})
be a unitary

4.2.
Unitary unicycles

Lemma 4.4

Let

of odd length.

Proof.

We begin with the “only if” statement. Note that
w
must be self-dual for
\overline{M}(w)
to be polarizable.
Hence
Lemma

3.6
yields that
w=yy^{\star}
for some word
y
. It remains to be shown that
q\coloneqq\ell(y)
is odd.
This comes essentially from the Rosati involution condition. More precisely, let
e_{0,1}\in B_{0}
. Because of the condition
\lambda(F(x),y)=\lambda(x,V(y))^{p}
and the assumption that
yy^{\star}
is primitive, it follows that
e_{0,1}
can only pair non-trivially with the element of

that is ‘antipodal’ to

steps away from

in the Kraft diagram. However, any element of

that is an even number of steps away from
e_{0,1}
is an element of
M_{0}
, and the Rosati involution condition prescribes that
M_{0}
is Lagrangian with respect to
\lambda
. Thus
q
must be odd.

For the “if” statement, let
w=yy^{\star}
with
y
of odd length
q
. Form
\widehat{M}(w)
as in
Lemma

3.7
. Let

M_{0}\coloneqq\text{span}_{\mathbx k}\{a_{j}\;|\;j\text{ even}\}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{and}\hskip 28.45274ptM_{1}\coloneqq\text{span}_{\mathbx k}\{a_{j}\;|\;j\text{ odd}\}.

By construction,
F
and
V
interchange the summands
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
. Finally, the fact that
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
are Lagrangian with respect to
\lambda
follows from the fact that
q
is odd, which ensures that
a_{j}
lies in a different summand than
a_{j\pm q}
. ∎

Note that, since
q
is odd in the context of
Lemma

4.4
, when defining the pairing

, one may pick the constant

in

3.7
to simply satisfy

. This then implies that
c^{p^{q}}=-c
.

Assume

is primitive and
y
has odd length. For any
n>0
, the construction in
Lemma

4.4
, combined with
Remark

3.2
, allows one to equip
\overline{M}((yy^{\star})^{n})
with unitary structure.

Definition 4.5
.

For a word
w=(yy^{\star})^{n}
with
y
a word of odd length and
yy^{\star}
primitive, we let
\widetilde{M}(w)
denote the unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module constructed in
Lemma

4.4
. In particular, the underlying
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module of
\widetilde{M}(w)
is
\overline{M}(w)
. When

is primitive, we call
\widetilde{M}(w)
a

.

Note that unitary unicycles are indecomposable as unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules, since they are indecomposable as
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules.

Remark 4.6
.

It is important to note that
\widetilde{M}(w)
is
not
invariant under rotation, because this exchanges
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
. In fact, rotating the word
w
amounts to conjugating
\widetilde{M}(w)
:

\widetilde{M}(\tau(w))\cong\widetilde{M}(w)^{*}.

Since rotating twice preserves
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
, we have that
\widetilde{M}(\tau^{2n}(w))\cong\widetilde{M}(w)
for every

.

Remark 4.7
.

Let

be primitive and assume

4.3.
Unitary bicycles and Serre tensor constructions

In an analogous fashion to the preceding subsection, we now construct unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules whose underlying polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module is of the form
\widehat{M}(w,w^{\star})
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
w
be a word of even length. Then
(M,F,V)=\overline{M}(w)\oplus\overline{M}(w^{\star})
admits unitary structure.

Proof.

Write
w=u_{q-1}\ldots u_{0}
, where
q
is even. As in
Lemma

3.9
, let
\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{q-1}\}
be the basis of
\overline{M}(w)
and let
\{b_{0},\ldots,b_{q-1}\}
be the basis of
\overline{M}(w^{\star})
. Let
\lambda
be as defined in
Lemma

3.9
and set

\displaystyle M_{0}

\displaystyle=\text{span}_{\mathbx k}\{a_{j}\mid j\text{ even}\}\oplus\text{span}_{\mathbx k}\{b_{j}\mid j\text{ odd}\},

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle=\text{span}_{\mathbx k}\{a_{j}\mid j\text{ odd}\}\oplus\text{span}_{\mathbx k}\{b_{j}\mid j\text{ even}\}.

Then one readily verifies that
F
and
V
exchange
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
and moreover
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
are Lagrangian with respect to
\lambda
.
∎

Definition 4.10
.

For a word
w
of even length, we let
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
denote the unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module constructed in
Lemma

4.9
. In particular, the underlying
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module of
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
is
\overline{M}(w)\oplus\overline{M}(w^{\star})
.

The following special cases of
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
will be of primary interest:

Definition 4.11
.

Let
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
be a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module. Then

\mathrlap{\oplus}\otimes

if
w
is a non-self-dual primitive word of even length, we refer to
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
as a
unitary bicycle
;

\mathrlap{\oplus}\otimes

if
w
is a self-dual primitive word whose length is divisible by
4
, we refer to
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
as a
Serre unicycle
; and

\mathrlap{\oplus}\otimes

if
w=zz
for a primitive word
z
of odd length (necessarily non-self-dual) we refer to
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
as a
Serre bicycle
.

See
Example

4.16
for a demonstration of each type of unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module described above.

Remark 4.12
.

If
w
is a self-dual primitive word whose length is divisible by
4
, then
\widehat{M}(w)
is a unicycle that does not admit unitary structure (see
Lemma

4.4
). In that case
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
is isomorphic to the
Serre tensor construction
of the unicycle
\widehat{M}(w)
; see
Proposition

4.27
.

Similarly, if
w=zz
and
z
is a non-self-dual primitive word of odd length, then
\widehat{M}(z,z^{\star})
is a bicycle that does not admit unitary structure (since the Kraft diagram has a loop of odd length), and
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
is the Serre tensor construction of the bicycle
\widehat{M}(z,z^{\star})
. See
Section

4.5
for details on the Serre tensor construction and how it applies to our setting.

Lemma 4.13
.

In all cases of
Definition

4.11
,
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
is indecomposable as a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module.

Proof.

First note that unitary bicycles are indecomposable as polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules, so in particular they are indecomposable as unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules.

Now, let
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
be a Serre unicycle. Then the underlying
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module of
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
is isomorphic to
\overline{M}(w)\oplus\overline{M}(w)
. Since
w
is primitive, any non-trivial factor of
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
would be isomorphic to
\overline{M}(w)
as a
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module. But such a
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module does not admit unitary structure by
Lemma

4.4
.

Finally, we consider the case when
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
is a Serre bicycle. Then the underlying polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module of
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
is isomorphic to
\widehat{M}(z,z^{\star})\oplus\widehat{M}(z,z^{\star})
and both summands are indecomposable as polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules. Hence any non-trivial factor of
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
would be isomorphic to
\widehat{M}(z,z^{\star})
as a polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module. But this is not possible, since the loop
\overline{M}(z)
has odd length and so we cannot form subspaces
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
that are exchanged by
F
and
V
.
∎

Remark 4.14
.

Similar to
Remark

4.6
, unitary bicycles are also not invariant under rotation, but are invariant under double rotation. Moreover, dualizing
w
has, up to isomorphism, the same effect as rotation:

\widetilde{M}(\tau(w),\tau(w)^{\star})\cong\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})^{*}\cong\widetilde{M}(w^{\star},w),

so that
\widetilde{M}(\tau(w)^{\star},\tau(w))\cong\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
. Note the distinction between
\star
(dual) and
*
(conjugate) in the previous equation.

On the other hand, if
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
is a Serre unicycle or a Serre bicycle, then it is self-conjugate and, therefore, invariant under rotation and under dualizing
w
.

Remark 4.15
.

We compute the signature of a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module of the form
\overline{M}(w)\oplus\overline{M}(w^{\star})
with

equals the number of

and

, which implies

. We conclude

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\#\{1\leqslant j\leqslant q/2\;|\;u_{2j-1}=\textnormal{{{v}}}\}+\#\{0\leqslant j<q/2\;|\;u_{2j}=\textnormal{{{f}}}\},

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\#\{0\leqslant j<q/2\;|\;u_{2j}=\textnormal{{{v}}}\}+\#\{1\leqslant j\leqslant q/2\;|\;u_{2j-1}=\textnormal{{{f}}}\}.

If

4.4.
A structure theorem for unitary
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{BT}_{1}}
modules

The goal of this section is to prove a structure theorem for unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules, akin to
Propositions

3.3
and
3.11
. More precisely, we will prove that unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules decompose as products of unitary unicycles, unitary bicycles, Serre unicycles, and Serre bicycles. See
Theorem

4.21
for the precise statement.

Recall that
[
20
, (4.9)]
gives a basis
B=B_{0}\cup B_{1}
that is preserved by
F
and
V
and such that
M_{i}=\text{span}_{\mathbx k}B_{i}
for
i\in\{0,1\}
. This gives a decomposition of
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules

(4.A)

(M,F,V)\cong\bigoplus_{w\in W}\overline{M}(w),

where each
\overline{M}(w)
is spanned by elements of
B
. Note that the words
w
in this decomposition need not be primitive and the decomposition need not be unique. Observe that all words
w\in W
must have even length, since the elements of
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
alternate in the Kraft diagram.

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
(M,F,V,\lambda,M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1})
decompose as in Equation (
4.A
). Then we may assume the words
w\in W
are either primitive or of the form
w=zz
, where
z
is primitive of odd length.

Proof.

Consider the isomorphism
\varphi:\overline{M}(w^{n})\overset{\sim}{\to}\overline{M}(w)^{n}
as is detailed in
Remark

3.2
. When
w
has even length,
\varphi
preserves the summands
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
. Therefore it provides bases
B^{\prime}_{i}
of
M_{i}
such that
B^{\prime}=B^{\prime}_{0}\cup B^{\prime}_{1}
is preserved by
F
and
V
. As for the pairing,
\varphi
maps
\lambda
to the pairing
\lambda_{\varphi}(-,-)=\lambda(\varphi^{-1}(-),\varphi^{-1}(-))
. Since
\varphi
is an isomorphism and
\lambda
is a symplectic pairing,
\lambda_{\varphi}
is a symplectic pairing. Furthermore, since
\varphi
preserves
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
, they are Lagrangian with respect to
\lambda_{\varphi}
. We conclude that we can apply
\varphi
without affecting the unitary structure.

Repeatedly applying isomorphisms like
\varphi
to
w\in W
with even length eventually leads to a basis
B^{\prime}
and decomposition as in Equation (
4.A
) where every word
w
is either primitive or of the form
w=zz
, where
z
is primitive of odd length. In the latter case, applying
\varphi
would not preserve the unitary structure and hence we cannot simplify further. ∎

We assume from here on out that all the words
w
in Equation (
4.A
) are either primitive or of the form
w=zz
, where
z
is primitive of odd length.

Lemma 4.18
.

Let
w
be a word such that
\overline{M}(w)
does not admit unitary structure and suppose that
\overline{M}(w)
occurs in Equation (
4.A
) with multiplicity
m
. Then
\overline{M}(w^{\star})
also occurs with multiplicity
m
.

Proof.

Since
\overline{M}(w)
does not admit unitary structure, it must be that any pairing
\lambda
that makes
(M,F,V,\lambda,M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1})
a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module is degenerate when restricted to
\overline{M}(w)
. Let
\lambda
be any such pairing and let
e_{i,j}\in\overline{M}(w)
be an element that pairs trivially with all of
\overline{M}(w)
. Let
e_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\in M
be such that
\lambda(e_{i,j},e_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}})\neq 0
. Then it follows from the condition
\lambda(F(x),y)=\lambda(x,V(y))^{p}
that
e_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
must lie on a copy of
\overline{M}(w^{\star})
.
∎

Remark 4.19
.

If
w
is non-self-dual, then
Lemma

4.18
follows immediately from
Proposition

3.11
. However,
Lemma

4.18
also covers the case when
w=yy^{\star}
for
y
of even length, so that
\overline{M}(w)
is polarizable but does not admit unitary structure.

Observe that the summands
\overline{M}(zz)
in Equation (
4.A
) are not polarizable, since
z
has odd length and is therefore non-self-dual. Moreover,
Lemma

4.4
shows that, for
w
primitive,
\overline{M}(w)
admits unitary structure exactly when
w=yy^{\star}
with
\ell(y)
odd.

We conclude that there is a decomposition of
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules

(4.B)

(M,F,V)\cong\bigoplus_{w\in W^{\prime}_{1}}\overline{M}(w)\oplus\bigoplus_{w\in W^{\prime}_{2}}\left(\overline{M}(w)\oplus\overline{M}(w^{\star})\right),

where each
\overline{M}(w)
is spanned by elements of
B
. Here
W^{\prime}_{1}
is a multiset of primitive words
w=yy^{\star}
with
\ell(y)
odd, and
W^{\prime}_{2}
is a multiset of words that either are primitive, but not of the form
w=yy^{\star}
with
\ell(y)
odd, or are of the form
w=zz
, with
z
primitive of odd length.

A priori, Equation (
4.B
) only gives a decomposition of
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules and does not take the pairing into account. When
(M,F,V,\lambda,M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1})
is a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module, the pairing
\lambda
is unique up to isomorphism of unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules (see
[
20
, (6.7)]
). Thus, it suffices to exhibit one suitable pairing.

Lemma 4.20
.

Let
(M,F,V,\lambda,M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1})
decompose as in Equation (
4.B
). Then we may assume
\lambda
is of the following form:

(1)

For
w\in W^{\prime}_{1}
, the pairing
\lambda
on
\overline{M}(w)
is as described in
Lemma

3.7
;

(2)

For
w\in W^{\prime}_{2}
, the pairing
\lambda
on
\overline{M}(w)\oplus\overline{M}(w^{\star})
is as described in
Lemma

3.9
.

Proof.

Since the pairing is unique up to isomorphism of unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules, it suffices to show that
\lambda
can be of the given form. It follows from
Lemmas

3.7
and
3.9
that
(M,F,V,\lambda)
is a polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module. Moreover,
Lemmas

4.4
and
4.9
provide that
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
are Lagrangian with respect to
\lambda
.
∎

We can now state and prove our structure theorem for unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules.

Theorem 4.21
.

Any unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widetilde{M}=(M,F,V,\lambda,M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1})
decomposes as a product of unitary unicycles, unitary bicycles, Serre unicycles, and Serre bicycles. Formally, we have

\widetilde{M}\cong\bigoplus_{w\in W_{1}}\widetilde{M}(w)\oplus\bigoplus_{w\in W_{2}}\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})\oplus\bigoplus_{w\in W_{3}}\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})\oplus\bigoplus_{w\in W_{4}}\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star}),

where

\mathrlap{\oplus}\otimes

W_{1}
is a multiset of primitive self-dual words
w=yy^{\star}
where
y
has odd length (unitary unicycles);

\mathrlap{\oplus}\otimes

W_{2}
is a multiset of primitive non-self-dual words of even length (unitary bicycles);

\mathrlap{\oplus}\otimes

W_{3}
is a multiset of primitive self-dual words
w=yy^{\star}
where
y
has even length (Serre unicycles);

\mathrlap{\oplus}\otimes

W_{4}
is a multiset of words
w=zz
where
z
is primitive of odd length (Serre bicycles).

The decomposition is unique up to the action of
\tau^{2}
on words in
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
, the action of
w\mapsto\tau(w)^{\star}
on words in
W_{2}
, the action of
\tau
on
W_{3}
and
W_{4}
, and the action of
w\mapsto w^{\star}
on
W_{4}
.

Proof.

Applying
Lemma

4.20
to Equation (
4.B
) gives the decomposition

\widetilde{M}\cong\bigoplus_{w\in W^{\prime}_{1}}\widetilde{M}(w)\oplus\bigoplus_{w\in W^{\prime}_{2}}\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star}).

By definition
W_{1}^{\prime}=W_{1}
. On the other hand,
W^{\prime}_{2}
is a multiset of words that are either primitive, but not of the form
w=yy^{\star}
with
\ell(y)
odd, or of the form
w=zz
, with
z
primitive of odd length.
It is clear that the case
w=zz
corresponds to the multiset
W_{4}
. Furthermore, if
w\in W_{2}^{\prime}
is primitive and self-dual, it must be of the form
w=yy^{\star}
by
Lemma

3.6
and
\ell(y)
must be even, so that
w\in W_{3}
. The remaining
w\in W_{2}^{\prime}
lie in
W_{2}
.

For the uniqueness statement, see
Remark

4.6
for
W_{1}
and
Remark

4.14
for
W_{2}
,
W_{3}
, and
W_{4}
.
∎

Corollary 4.22
.

If
\widetilde{M}
is indecomposable as a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module, then
\widetilde{M}
is isomorphic to a unitary unicycle, a unitary bicycle, a Serre unicycle, or a Serre bicycle.

Remark 4.23
.

Theorem

4.21
allows us to list the indecomposable types occurring for a given signature
(a,b)
. This depends on the parity of
q=a+b
.

Case I.
q
is odd
.

In this case, the indecomposable unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules of signature
(a,b)
are of the form
\widetilde{M}(w)
, where
w
is a primitive self-dual word of length
2q
in which
v
occurs in
b
places with even index. In other words, when
q
is odd, the only indecomposable type possible is that of a unitary unicycle.

Case II.
q
is even
.

In this case, the indecomposable unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules of signature
(a,b)
are of the form
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
with
b
equaling the sum of the number of
v
’s in even places and the number of
f
’s in odd places of
w
. There are three indecomposable types for
q
even.

For any even
q>2
, the indecomposable type of a unitary bicycle occurs. In this case, the word
w
is a non-self-dual primitive word of even length. The other two indecomposable types occur in the case of parallel signature
(a,a)
, depending on the parity of
a
.

\rhd

When
a
is even, the indecomposable type of a Serre unicycle occurs. In this case, the word
w
is a self-dual primitive word whose length is divisible by
4
.

\rhd

When
a
is odd, the indecomposable type of a Serre bicycle occurs. In this case, the word
w=zz
for a primitive word
z
of odd length.

See
Examples

4.8
and
4.16
for specific examples of each indecomposable type.

When
q=2
, indecomposables only appear in signature
(1,1)
, where there is a unique one with the indecomposable type of a Serre bicycle.

4.5.
Epilogue: The Serre Tensor Construction

In this section, we discuss the Serre tensor construction of an abelian variety (and of a
p
-divisible group) and its relation to
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules. For more details, see
[
3
,
7
,
8
,
18
]
.

Consider a polarized abelian variety
(A,\lambda)
over
\mathbx k
, and let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field such that
p
is inert in
K
. Then, the
Serre tensor construction
of
A
, denoted
\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbx Z}A
, is an abelian variety over
\mathbx k
with a natural action
\iota
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
; see
[
18
, Appendix]
,
[
3
, Section 1]
or
[
7
, Proposition 1.7.4.5]
.

Let
h
be the trace pairing
(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}(\alpha\overline{\beta})
, which is a positive definite hermitian form on
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Then
[
3
, Theorem A]
allows us to conclude that
(\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbx Z}A,h\otimes\lambda,\xi)
is a polarized abelian variety with an action
\iota
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Additionally, by
[
8
, Corollary 7.9]
, we have an isomorphism of
p
-torsion group schemes
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbx F_{p}}A[p]\cong(\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbx Z}A)[p]
.

Let
\widehat{D}=(D,F,V,\lambda)
be the polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module corresponding to
A[p]
under the Dieudonné equivalence. Then the isomorphism
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbx F_{p}}A[p]\cong(\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbx Z}A)[p]
, under the Dieudonné equivalence, produces the following unitary Dieudonné module (in the sense of
Remark

4.2
):

\mathbx F_{p^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbx F_{p}}\widehat{D}\coloneqq(\mathbx F_{p^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbx F_{p}}D,\operatorname{id}\otimes F,\operatorname{id}\otimes V,\widetilde{\lambda},\iota),

where

(1)

\iota
is the natural action of
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}
on
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbx F_{p}}D
; and

(2)

\widetilde{\lambda}\coloneqq\widetilde{\mathrm{Tr}}\otimes\lambda

is a pairing on
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbx F_{p}}D
, where
\widetilde{\mathrm{Tr}}
is the trace pairing on
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}
defined as

\widetilde{\mathrm{Tr}}(\alpha,\beta)=\mathrm{Tr}(\alpha\beta^{p}).

Definition 4.24
.

Consider
\widehat{N}=(N,F,V,\lambda)
, a polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module. Let
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}:\mathbx F_{p^{2}}\hookrightarrow\mathbx k
be the canonical
\mathbx F_{p}
-linear embeddings. For
i=0,1
, let
N_{i}
be the
\mathbx k
-vector space
N
equipped with an action of
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}
via
\varphi_{i}
.

Define operators
F_{\mathsf{STC}}
and
V_{\mathsf{STC}}
on
\mathsf{STC}(N)\coloneqq N_{0}\oplus N_{1}
as follows:

F_{\mathsf{STC}}(n_{0},n_{1})=(F(n_{1}),F(n_{0}))\quad\text{and}\quad V_{\mathsf{STC}}(n_{0},n_{1})=(V(n_{1}),V(n_{0})),

define an action
\iota_{\mathsf{STC}}
of
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}
on
N_{0}\oplus N_{1}
as follows:

\iota_{\mathsf{STC}}:\mathbx F_{p^{2}}\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathbx k}(N_{0}\oplus N_{1}),\;\alpha\mapsto\left[(n_{0},n_{1})\mapsto(\varphi_{0}(\alpha)n_{0},\varphi_{1}(\alpha)n_{1})\right],

and finally define a pairing
\lambda_{\mathsf{STC}}
on
N_{0}\oplus N_{1}
as follows:

\lambda_{\mathsf{STC}}:(N_{0}\oplus N_{1})\times(N_{0}\oplus N_{1})\to\mathbx k,\;((n_{0},n_{1}),(n_{0}^{\prime},n_{1}^{\prime}))\mapsto\lambda(n_{0},n_{1}^{\prime})-\lambda(n_{0}^{\prime},n_{1}).

The quintuple

\mathsf{STC}(\widehat{N})\coloneqq(\mathsf{STC}(N),F_{\mathsf{STC}},V_{\mathsf{STC}},\lambda_{\mathsf{STC}},\mathsf{STC}(N)=N_{0}\oplus N_{1})

is called the
Serre tensor construction
of
\widehat{N}
.

Proposition 4.25
.

Consider a
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widehat{N}=(N,F,V,\lambda)
. Then its Serre tensor construction
\mathsf{STC}(\widehat{N})
is a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module of signature
(q,q)
, where
q=\dim_{\mathbx k}N
.

Proof.

This is clear from the definition.
∎

The following proposition justifies calling
\mathsf{STC}(\widehat{N})
a Serre tensor construction.

Proposition 4.26
.

Consider a
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widehat{N}=(N,F,V,\lambda)
and its Serre tensor construction
\mathsf{STC}(\widehat{N})
.

Given the quintuple
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbx F_{p}}\widehat{N}\coloneqq(\mathbx F_{p^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbx F_{p}}N,\operatorname{id}\otimes F,\operatorname{id}\otimes V,\widetilde{\lambda},\iota)
, where

\iota
is the natural action of
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}
on
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbx F_{p}}N
and
\widetilde{\lambda}=\widetilde{\mathrm{Tr}}\otimes\lambda
, the following isomorphism of vector spaces

\mathbx F_{p^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbx F_{p}}N\to N_{0}\oplus N_{1}:\alpha\otimes n\mapsto(\varphi_{0}(\alpha)n,\varphi_{1}(\alpha)n)

commutes with the additional structures
(\operatorname{id}\otimes F,\operatorname{id}\otimes V,\widetilde{\lambda},\iota)
and
(F_{\mathsf{STC}},V_{\mathsf{STC}},\lambda_{\mathsf{STC}},\iota_{\mathsf{STC}})
on
\mathbx F_{p^{2}}\otimes_{\mathbx F_{p}}N
and
N_{0}\oplus N_{1}
respectively.

Proof.

This may be verified directly, using the details in
Remark

4.2
to translate between the
\iota
and the
\oplus
descriptions.
∎

The following two propositions explain the terminology
Serre unicycle
and
Serre bicycle
, respectively.

Proposition 4.27
.

Let
w
be a primitive, self-dual word, of length divisible by
4
. Then
\mathsf{STC}(\widehat{M}(w))\cong\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})
. That is, Serre unicycles arise as Serre tensor constructions of (polarized) unicycles.

Proof.

We apply
Definition

4.24
to the polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widehat{N}=\widehat{M}(w)
. The word
w
can be written as

w=u_{2q-1}\cdots u_{0}=\tau^{q}(w^{\star})=u_{q-1}^{\star}\cdots u_{0}^{\star}u_{2q-1}^{\star}\cdots u_{q}^{\star}

with
2q=\ell(w)
and
q
even. Given
0\leqslant j\leqslant 2q-1
, we let
0\leqslant\hat{\jmath}\leqslant 2q-1
be the integer satisfying
\hat{\jmath}\equiv j+q\pmod{2q}
, i.e.,
\hat{\jmath}
is either
j+q
or
j-q
.

Following
Definition

3.8
, we have
\widehat{N}=(N,F,V,\lambda)
, where
N=\mathrm{span}k\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{2q-1}\}
,

F(a_{j})=\begin{cases}a_{j+1}&\hbox{if $u_{j}=\textnormal{{{f}}},$}\\
0&\hbox{if $u_{j}=\textnormal{{{v}}},$}\end{cases}\hskip 56.9055ptV(a_{j+1})=\begin{cases}a_{j}&\hbox{if $u_{j}=\textnormal{{{v}}},$}\\
0&\hbox{if $u_{j}=\textnormal{{{f}}}$,}\end{cases}

and
\lambda
is as in
Lemma

3.7
.

Let
\{i,\hat{\imath}\}=\{0,1\}
. Now, applying
Definition

4.24
, we begin by formally defining the
\mathbx k
-vector spaces
N_{i}=\mathrm{span}k\{n_{i,0},\ldots,n_{i,2q-1}\}
and set
\mathsf{STC}(N)=N_{0}\oplus N_{1}
, the underlying vector space of
\mathsf{STC}(\widehat{N})
.

By
Definition

4.24
, the actions of
F_{\mathsf{STC}}
and
V_{\mathsf{STC}}
are:

F_{\mathsf{STC}}(n_{i,j})=\begin{cases}n_{\hat{\imath},j+1}&\hbox{if $u_{j}=\textnormal{{{f}}},$}\\
0&\hbox{if $u_{j}=\textnormal{{{v}}},$}\end{cases}\hskip 56.9055ptV_{\mathsf{STC}}(n_{i,j+1})=\begin{cases}n_{\hat{\imath},j}&\hbox{if $u_{j}=\textnormal{{{v}}},$}\\
0&\hbox{if $u_{j}=\textnormal{{{f}}}$.}\end{cases}

Together with the polarization
\lambda_{\mathsf{STC}}
, this construction yields the unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module

\mathsf{STC}(\widehat{M}(w))=(\mathsf{STC}(N),F_{\mathsf{STC}},V_{\mathsf{STC}},\lambda_{\mathsf{STC}},\mathsf{STC}(N)=N_{0}\oplus N_{1}).

On the other hand, consider the construction
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})=(M,\widetilde{F},\widetilde{V},\widetilde{\lambda},M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1})
from Definition
4.10
(see also
Lemma

4.9
). The underlying vector space is given by

M\coloneqq\mathrm{span}k\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{2q-1},b_{0},\ldots,b_{2q-1}\}.

The actions of the morphisms
\widetilde{F}
and
\widetilde{V}
on
\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{2q-1}\}
are the same as the actions of
F
and
V
on
N=\mathrm{span}k\{a_{0},\ldots,a_{2q-1}\}
, whereas their actions on
\{b_{0},\ldots,b_{2q-1}\}
are given by

\widetilde{F}(b_{j})=\begin{cases}b_{j+1}&\hbox{if $u_{j}^{\star}=\textnormal{{{f}}},$}\\
0&\hbox{if $u_{j}^{\star}=\textnormal{{{v}}},$}\end{cases}\hskip 56.9055pt\widetilde{V}(b_{j+1})=\begin{cases}b_{j}&\hbox{if $u_{j}^{\star}=\textnormal{{{v}}},$}\\

Note that

since

is self-dual and primitive. Following
Lemma

4.9
, we define subspaces

\displaystyle M_{0}

\displaystyle=\text{span}_{\mathbx k}\{a_{j}\mid j\text{ even}\}\oplus\text{span}_{\mathbx k}\{b_{j}\mid j\text{ odd}\},

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle=\text{span}_{\mathbx k}\{a_{j}\mid j\text{ odd}\}\oplus\text{span}_{\mathbx k}\{b_{j}\mid j\text{ even}\}.

Together with the polarization
\widetilde{\lambda}
from
Lemma

3.9
, this construction yields the unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module

\widetilde{M}(w)=(M,\widetilde{F},\widetilde{V},\widetilde{\lambda},M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1}).

We now exhibit an isomorphism between the constructions
\mathsf{STC}(\widehat{M}(w))
and
\widetilde{M}(w)
. For this, we define the following isomorphism of the underlying vector spaces:

\displaystyle\psi:\mathsf{STC}(N)

\displaystyle\to M

\displaystyle n_{i,j}

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}a_{j}&\hbox{if $i+j$ is even,}\\
b_{\hat{\jmath}}&\hbox{if $i+j$ is odd}.\end{cases}

One then verifies, using

and

.

gives rise to an isomorphism between

for

since

Finally, since the polarization is unique, up to isomorphism (see

\mathrm{BT}_{1}

Remark 4.29
.

We contextualize the results of this section, specifically the indecomposable types introduced so far, in terms of the forgetful functors

\displaystyle\widetilde{N}=(N,F,V,\lambda,M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1})

\displaystyle\mapsto(N,F,V,\lambda)\eqcolon\widehat{U}(\widetilde{N})

\displaystyle\widehat{L}=(L,F,V,\lambda)

\displaystyle\mapsto(L,F,V)\eqcolon\overline{U}(\widehat{L})

which, respectively, takes a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widetilde{N}
to its underlying polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widehat{U}(\widetilde{N})
, and takes a polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widehat{L}
to its underlying (undecorated)
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\overline{U}(\widehat{L})
.

Let the composition of these functor still be denoted as
\overline{U}
, i.e., the underlying (undecorated)
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module of a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widetilde{N}
is denoted as
\overline{U}(\widetilde{N})
.

Given an indecomposable unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widetilde{N}
, either the polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widehat{U}(\widetilde{N})
remains indecomposable, or it is decomposable.

When
\widehat{U}(\widetilde{N})
is indecomposable, either
\overline{U}(\widetilde{N})
is also indecomposable, in which case
\widehat{U}(\widetilde{N})
is a
unicycle
and
\widetilde{N}
is a
unitary unicycle
, or
\overline{U}(\widetilde{N})
is decomposable, in which case
\widehat{U}(\widetilde{N})
is a
bicycle
and
\widetilde{N}
is a
unitary bicycle
.

When
\widehat{U}(\widetilde{N})
is decomposable, it follows from
Theorems

4.21
,
4.27
and
4.28
that
\widetilde{N}\cong\mathsf{STC}(\widehat{L})
for some indecomposable polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widehat{L}
. Either
\overline{U}(\widehat{L})
is also indecomposable, in which case
\widetilde{N}
is a
Serre unicycle
, or
\overline{U}(\widehat{L})
is decomposable, in which case
\widetilde{N}
is a
Serre bicycle
.

5.
Words and Weyl group cosets

In
Theorem

4.21
, Ekedahl-Oort strata of
\mathcal{M}(a,b)
are described in terms of their associated words in the alphabet
\{\textnormal{{{f}}},\textnormal{{{v}}}\}
. In this section, we provide an algorithm, much like a dictionary, that describes these Ekedahl-Oort strata in terms of Weyl group cosets, as was first outlined in
[
20
]
. While the word construction is a natural way to describe and understand the decompositions of
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
modules, the Weyl group coset characterization has the benefit of making geometric properties of the strata, such as the dimension and the topological closure, more apparent.

5.1.
Preliminaries

Let
q=a+b
. Recall from
Section

2.4
that
\mathbf{W}(a,b)
denotes the set of minimal length representatives of the Weyl group cosets in
\mathbf{W}_{(a,b)}\setminus\mathfrak{S}_{q}
.

By the work of
[
20
, (3.5)]
and
[
1
, Section 3]
, there is a procedure to compute the
\gamma\in\mathbf{W}(a,b)
, corresponding to a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widetilde{M}=(M,F,V,\lambda,M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1})
of signature
(a,b)
:

\bullet

Let
W_{\bullet}
be a final filtration of
M
, i.e. a filtration stable under
F
and
V^{-1}
that contains a space of each dimension between
1
and
2(a+b)=2q
;

\bullet

Define
C_{\bullet}=W_{\bullet}\cap M_{0}
and define, for
s\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
, the function
\eta(s)\coloneqq\dim_{k}(C_{1,s}[F])
;

\bullet

Let
z_{1}<\cdots<z_{b}
be the integers where
\eta
jumps, i.e.
\eta(z_{m})=\eta(z_{m}-1)+1
for every
1\leqslant m\leqslant b
;

\bullet

Then
\gamma_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{b}}\coloneqq(b,b+1,\ldots,z_{b})\cdots(2,3,\ldots,z_{2})(1,2,\ldots,z_{1})\in\mathbf{W}(a,b)
corresponds to
\widetilde{M}
.

The goal of
Section

5.2
is to describe the indices
z_{1}<\cdots<z_{b}
in terms of the words in the alphabet
\{\textnormal{{{f,v}}}\}
that are used in
Theorem

4.21
.

5.2.
The “Word to Weyl” algorithm

Let
\widetilde{M}=(M,F,V,\lambda,M_{0}\oplus M_{1})
be a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module of signature
(a,b)
and let
\overline{M}=(M,F,V)
be its underlying
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module.

For
i\in\{0,1\}
, let
B_{i}=\{e_{i,j}\;|\;1\leqslant j\leqslant q\}
be a basis of
M_{i}
such that
F
and
V
preserve
B=B_{0}\cup B_{1}
.

By
Lemma

4.17
, there is a decomposition

\overline{M}=\bigoplus_{w\in W}\overline{M}(w)

with the following properties:

(1)

Every factor
\overline{M}(w)
is spanned by elements of
B
(which implies that every
w\in W
must have even length);

(2)

Every word
w\in W
is either primitive or of the form
w=zz
, where
z
is primitive of odd length.

In addition, we may and do assume, after rotating words if necessary, the following condition:

(3)

For each
w=u_{\ell-1}\cdots u_{0}\in W
, the letter
u_{0}
signifies either
F(e_{0,j})=e_{1,j^{\prime}}
or
V(e_{1,j^{\prime}})=e_{0,j}
for some
j,j^{\prime}
.

Under these assumptions, we associate a permutation
\gamma(W)\in\mathbf{W}(a,b)
to the multiset
W
. Recall the rotation operator
\tau
defined by
\tau(u_{\ell-1}\cdots u_{0})=u_{0}u_{\ell-1}\cdots u_{1}
. We denote by
\preccurlyeq
the lexicographical order that can compare any two words of equal length in the alphabet
\{\textnormal{{{f}}},\textnormal{{{v}}}\}
using the rule
\textnormal{{{f}}}\preccurlyeq\textnormal{{{v}}}
. Note that when we order the words lexicographically, we work left-to-right, but when we are reading the words as maps, we apply the functions right-to-left.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
W
be a multiset of words with
L_{W}\coloneqq\text{LCM}_{w\in W}\{\ell(w)\}
. Define the multiset

\widetilde{W}\coloneqq\left\{\left(\tau^{2j}(w)\right)^{L_{W}/\ell(w)}\;|\;w\in W,0\leqslant j<\ell(w)/2\right\}

and set
q_{W}\coloneqq\#\widetilde{W}
. Let
\widetilde{\omega}_{1}\preccurlyeq\cdots\preccurlyeq\widetilde{\omega}_{q_{W}}
be a lexicographical ordering of the elements (with multiplicity)
of
\widetilde{W}
. Then define

\displaystyle\left\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{b}\ \middle|\ z_{1}<\cdots<z_{b}\right\}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{s\ \middle|\ \widetilde{\omega}_{s}\text{ has $\textnormal{{{v}}}$ as its right-most letter}\right\};\text{ and}

\displaystyle\gamma(W)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\gamma_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{b}}=(b,b+1,\ldots,z_{b})\cdots(2,3,\ldots,z_{2})(1,2,\ldots,z_{1})\in\mathbf{W}(a,b).

Remark 5.2
.

The multiset
\widetilde{W}
records all the double rotations of words in
W
, with multiplicity. The words are raised to the power
L_{W}/\ell(w)
to arrange that all elements of
\widetilde{W}
are of equal length, so that the lexicographical order can compare any two distinct elements of
\widetilde{W}
. When
W
contains multiple elements that are in the same
\tau^{2}
-orbit, then
\widetilde{W}
is not a set and therefore the lexicographical ordering of
\widetilde{W}
is not canonical. However, this amounts to the fact that we may permute words that are equal, so it is clear that the definition of
\gamma(W)
is nonetheless canonical.

See
Example

5.5
for the process of computing
\widetilde{W}
and ordering the elements of this multiset.

The following theorem is a “word to Weyl” algorithm, as it converts the word-based description from
Theorem

4.21
to the Weyl group coset representative of a given Ekedahl-Oort stratum.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\widetilde{M}
be a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module of signature
(a,b)
whose underlying
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module admits the decomposition
\overline{M}=\bigoplus_{w\in W}\overline{M}(w)
satisfying Properties (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
) above. Let
\gamma(W)
be as in
Definition

5.1
. Then
\gamma(W)
is the minimal length representative of the coset of
(\mathfrak{S}_{a}\times\mathfrak{S}_{b})\setminus\mathfrak{S}_{q}
, where
q=q_{W}
, that corresponds to the Ekedahl-Oort stratum of
\widetilde{M}
via
[
20
, (6.7)]
.

Proof.

We follow the steps outlined in
Section

5.1
for computing
z_{1},\ldots,z_{b}
. The first step is to form a final filtration of
M
, for which we use
[
23
, Section 3.5]
. Let
n
be the number of distinct words. Following their notation, we write

\overline{M}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\overline{M}(w_{i})^{m_{i}},

where
\ell_{i}
is the length of the word
w_{i}=u_{i,\ell_{i}-1}\ldots u_{i,0}

and
m_{i}
is its multiplicity.
The vector space
M
has a basis
\{e_{i,j,k}\;|\;1\leqslant i\leqslant n,\;0\leqslant j<\ell_{i},1\leqslant k\leqslant m_{i}\}
, on which
F
and
V
act as follows:

\displaystyle F(e_{i,j,k})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}e_{i,j+1,k}&\hbox{if $u_{i,j}=\textnormal{{{f}}}$,}\\
0&\hbox{if $u_{i,j}=\textnormal{{{v}}}$,}\end{cases}\hskip 56.9055ptV(e_{i,j+1,k})=\begin{cases}e_{i,j,k}&\hbox{if $u_{i,j}=\textnormal{{{v}}}$,}\\
0&\hbox{if $u_{i,j}=\textnormal{{{f}}}$}.\end{cases}

Now, for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
, define
w_{i,0}\coloneqq w_{i}^{L_{W}/\ell_{i}}
and

w_{i,j}\coloneqq(\tau^{j}(w_{i}))^{L_{W}/\ell_{i}}=(u_{i,j-1}\cdots u_{i,0}u_{i,\ell_{i}-1}\cdots u_{i,j})^{L_{W}/\ell_{i}}

for
0<j<\ell_{i}
. Note that, by construction, every
w_{i,j}
has the length
L_{W}
. Let
\Omega
be the set of all
w_{i,j}
. For each
\omega\in\Omega
, we now define

N_{\omega}\coloneqq\text{span}\left\{e_{i,j,k}\;|\;w_{i,j}\preccurlyeq\omega\right\}.

By
[
23
, Section 3.5]
, the canonical filtration of
M
is given by
\{N_{\omega}\;|\;\omega\in\Omega\}
. From the construction it is clear that
N_{\omega}\subseteq N_{\omega^{\prime}}
if and only if
\omega\preccurlyeq\omega^{\prime}
.

The next step in
Section

5.1
is to intersect the final filtration with
M_{0}
. It suffices to intersect the canonical filtration with
M_{0}
. To this end, note that Property (
3
) above ensures that
e_{i,0,k}\in M_{0}
. Furthermore, since
F
and
V
interchange
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
, we see that
e_{i,j,k}\in M_{0}
if and only if
j
is even. Hence,

N_{\omega}^{0}\coloneqq N_{\omega}\cap M_{0}=\text{span}\{e_{i,j,k}\;|\;w_{i,j}\preccurlyeq\omega\text{ and }2\mid j\}.

Let
q=q_{W}
from
Definition

5.1
. The function
\eta:\{1,\ldots,q\}\to\{1,\ldots,q\}
will now be determined by its values at the numbers
s_{\omega}:=\dim(N_{\omega}^{0})
since it is irrelevant how the canonical filtration is refined to a final filtration. For this, recall the elements
\widetilde{\omega}_{1}\preccurlyeq\cdots\preccurlyeq\widetilde{\omega}_{q}
of
\widetilde{W}
in
Definition

5.1
. Note that these elements correspond to the elements
\omega_{i,j}\in\Omega
with
j
even. We then observe:

\displaystyle\dim\left(\text{span}\{e_{i,j,k}\;|\;w_{i,j}=\omega\text{ and }2\mid j\}\right)

\displaystyle=\#\{1\leqslant s\leqslant q\;|\;\widetilde{\omega}_{s}=\omega\},

\displaystyle s_{\omega}=\dim\left(N_{\omega}^{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\#\{1\leqslant s\leqslant q\;|\;\widetilde{\omega}_{s}\preccurlyeq\omega\}.

Equivalently,
s_{\omega}=\max\{s\;|\;\widetilde{\omega}_{s}\preccurlyeq\omega\}
.

The last step is to compute the dimension of
N_{\omega}^{0}[F]
for each
\omega\in\Omega
. For this, we note that
F(e_{i,j,k})=0
is equivalent to
u_{i,j}=\textnormal{{{v}}}
, which is in turn equivalent to
w_{i,j}
ending with
v
. Thus we conclude that, for each
\omega\in\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle\eta(s_{\omega})=\dim(N_{\omega}^{0}[F])

\displaystyle=\#\{1\leqslant s\leqslant q\;|\;\widetilde{\omega}_{s}\preccurlyeq\omega\text{ and }\widetilde{\omega}_{s}\text{ ends with $\textnormal{{{v}}}$}\}

\displaystyle=\#\{1\leqslant s\leqslant s_{\omega}\;|\;\widetilde{\omega}_{s}\text{ ends with $\textnormal{{{v}}}$}\}.

Finally, the property
\eta(s)\leqslant\eta(s+1)\leqslant\eta(s)+1
dictates the further behavior of the function
\eta
:

\eta(s)=\#\{1\leqslant t\leqslant s\;|\;\widetilde{\omega}_{t}\text{ ends with $\textnormal{{{v}}}$}\}.

Thus,
\eta
jumps at the values
\left\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{b}\ \middle|\ z_{1}<\cdots<z_{b}\right\}=\{1\leqslant s\leqslant q\;|\;\widetilde{\omega}_{s}\text{ ends with $\textnormal{{{v}}}$}\}.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

By

4.21
, every unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}

We now outline how to derive the multiset

used for

5.3
from this decomposition. From the definitions of
\widetilde{M}(w)
and
\widetilde{M}(w,w^{\star})

4.9

) above. To this end, we replace them by

). We conclude that

-divisible group of the parameterized abelian varieties. The unique closed Newton stratum of

is the supersingular locus, and a
\mathbx k

is contained in the supersingular locus if and only if

is a supersingular abelian variety.

The additional structure we consider on the abelian varieties parameterized by

imposes additional structure on the corresponding

-divisible groups, and thereby the corresponding p-adic Dieudonné modules via the Dieudonné equivalence; this gives us the notion of a

-divisible group and

p
-adic Dieudonné module respectively.

Theorem

-adic Dieudonné module called the

of

Definition 6.1

-divisible groups coming from

Recall that

Definition 6.2
.

A

Since the

6.3.
Newton polygons

Let
\widetilde{M}=(M,F,V,\lambda,M=M_{0}\oplus M_{1})
be a unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}

\mathrm{TL}(\widetilde{M})
be its tautological lift. For the purposes of

of

p

\overline{\mathbx{M}}\otimes_{\mathbx{W}}K
decomposes as a

\overline{\mathbx{M}}\otimes_{\mathbx{W}}K=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\overline{\mathbx{M}}_{m_{i},n_{i}}^{\lambda_{i}}.

Here
\overline{\mathbx{M}}_{m_{i},n_{i}}
is the simple
K[\mathbx{F}]
-module on which
\mathbx{F}^{m_{i}+n_{i}}=p^{n_{i}}

\alpha_{i}=\frac{m_{i}}{m_{i}+n_{i}}
the

of
\overline{\mathbx{M}}_{m_{i},n_{i}}

\lambda_{i}
the

of
\alpha_{i}

\mu_{i}=\lambda_{i}(m_{i}+n_{i})
the

of the slope
\alpha_{i}
. We assume
\alpha_{1}<\cdots<\alpha_{r}
. Then the

is the polygon from
(0,0)
to
(2q,q)
obtained by concatenating the line segments of slope
\alpha_{i}
and horizontal length
\mu_{i}
.

Definition 6.5
.

Let

be a word in the alphabet

Now, let

with length

Definition 6.7

We shall say a word

\mathcal{M}(a,b)_{\gamma(W)}\cap\mathcal{M}(a,b)^{\textnormal{ss}}
is non-empty if
\gamma(W)
corresponds to a unitary unicycle or Serre unicycle.

Remark 6.14
.

The converse of
Corollary

6.12
is not true. In
[
12
, Lemma 43]
, the authors prove the existence of a supersingular principally polarized abelian variety
A
of dimension
5
whose
p
-torsion group scheme corresponds to the polarized
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widehat{M}(\textnormal{{{fffvv}}},\textnormal{{{vvvff}}})
. Applying the Serre tensor construction described in
Section

4.5
to
A
yields an abelian variety
\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes_{\mathbx Z}A
, which admits an action by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of signature
(5,5)
.
This construction gives rise to a point in
\mathcal{M}(5,5)
. Since the Serre tensor construction of a supersingular abelian variety is supersingular, this point lies in
\mathcal{M}(5,5)^{\textnormal{ss}}
. On the other hand, by
Proposition

4.28
, its
p
-torsion group scheme corresponds to the unitary
\mathrm{BT}_{1}
module
\widetilde{M}(\textnormal{{{fffvvfffvv}}},\textnormal{{{vvvffvvvff}}})
. By Equation (
6.C
), we have
W_{1}=\varnothing
and
W_{2}=\{\textnormal{{{fffvvfffvv}}}\}
. Then Equation (
6.B
) yields

W=\{\textnormal{{{fffvvfffvv}}},\textnormal{{{vvffvvvffv}}}\}.

Note that using
W_{2}=\{\textnormal{{{vvvffvvvff}}}\}
gives the same result. It is clear that the word
fffvvfffvv
is not balanced and
Definition

6.5
prescribes that the Newton polygon of the tautological lift has slopes
2/5
and
3/5
, both with length
10
.
Thus we have constructed a point in
\mathcal{M}(a,b)_{\gamma(W)}\cap\mathcal{M}(a,b)^{\textnormal{ss}}
even though the words in
W_{2}
are not balanced.

We can use the “word to Weyl” algorithm of
Section

5.2
to identify the Weyl group coset representative that this corresponds to. To do this, we lexicographically order the double rotations of each component
\{\textnormal{{{fffvvfffvv}}},\textnormal{{{vvffvvvffv}}}\}
, which yields the ordered list
