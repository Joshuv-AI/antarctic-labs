1. Executive Summary
Quantum Information Science (QIS) promises transformative advances in sensing and computa-
tion, yet progress hinges on tools capable of probing quantum materials at atomic precision. Con-
ventional approaches struggle with chemically sensitive environments and small sample volumes,
limiting access to phenomena such as magnetic ordering, ferroelectricity, and exotic quasiparticles.
Current sensing technologies lack the selectivity and tunability required for interrogating strongly
correlated systems. This gap impedes efforts to understand emergent phases and interactions in
two-dimensional magnets, ferroelectrics, and multiferroics—critical knowledge for next-genera-
tion quantum devices.
To overcome these challenges, this project united leading researchers from MIT, Northwestern
University, University of Chicago, and University of California Berkeley, combining expertise in
synthetic chemistry, condensed matter physics, quantum spectroscopies, materials science, and
electronic structure theory. This interdisciplinary collaboration enabled the design and integration
of molecular qubits as programmable quantum sensors, delivering a platform with unprecedented
atomic-level control and tunability. Over the award, this team advanced a chemically driven ap-
proach to quantum information science by (1) creating optically addressable molecular qubits with
room-temperature relevant coherence, (2) scaling qubits into frameworks and thin films that retain
quantum functionality, and (3) building and validating quantum sensing modalities (magnetic and
electric) for emerging 2D quantum materials. These advances open experimental access to previ-
ously intractable quantum phenomena and lay the foundation for scalable quantum sensing.
Across the award period, we created, controlled, and integrated designer molecular qubits, which
ranged from Cr4+ color centers and radical frameworks to lanthanide complexes, and validated
thin-film and framework routes that maintain spin/optical functionality. We built computational
design tools (systems charts, Stark modeling, exchange/phonon analyses) and metrology (ODMR,
EPR, out‑of‑plane ESEEM, photocurrent spectroscopy) that bridge molecular sensors to 2D mag-
nets and hybrid devices. We extended these capabilities to telecom‑band spin‑photonics, bilayer
multiferroic excitonics, and substrate‑aware device engineering, establishing clear trajectories to
scalable quantum sensing and integrated molecular quantum technologies. More than 40 peer‑re-
viewed outputs in leading venues (e.g., Adv. Sci., Dalton Trans., Appl. Phys. Lett., Chem. Sci., ACS
Appl. Mater. Interfaces, Nat. Electron., Phys. Rev. Res.) document these advances.
The project delivered hands‑on training for 33 graduate students and 8 postdoctoral fellows, in-
cluding 10 PhD recipients, building a talent pipeline skilled in quantum materials synthesis, de-
vice‑relevant thin‑film fabrication, and state‑of‑the‑art spectroscopies that map directly to DOE’s
quantum information science mission needs. This training advances the national goal to “build the
S&T workforce of the future” and supports the priority area to “ensure unrivaled American lead-
ership in critical and emerging technologies,” including quantum information science and enabling
microelectronics. We also developed sharable datasets, testable design rules, and lab‑to‑device
practices that strengthen world‑class research infrastructure and position trainees to become lead-
ers in national labs, academia, and industry consortia.

2. Research Accomplishments
2.1 Foundational Advances in Molecular Qubit Arrays
Controlling Vibrational Modes for Room-Temperature Coherence. One of the most pressing
challenges in molecular quantum information science is achieving long coherence times at ele-
vated temperatures. Our team tackled this by engineering ligand environments to suppress detri-
mental vibrational modes. Through systematic design of square-planar versus tetrahedral com-
plexes, we achieved nearly two orders of magnitude improvement in spin-lattice relaxation times
(T1) above 200 K. Computational modeling revealed that low-energy vibrational modes strongly
influence relaxation, enabling predictive design of new candidates. This breakthrough establishes
foundational principles for creating molecular qubits that operate at or near room temperature,
marking a critical milestone for scalable quantum sensing.
The First Optically Addressable Molecular Qubit. Another major milestone was the creation of
the first optically addressable molecular qubits. Optical initialization and readout are essential for
integrating molecular qubits into hybrid quantum architectures. We synthesized Cr(IV) tetrahedral
complexes that exhibit optical spin-state initialization and readout, combined with microwave con-
trol. Complementary Ni(II) complexes demonstrated emission and EPR addressability, validating
our design strategy for optical interfaces. These molecules represent a new class of molecular color
centers, bridging the gap between molecular chemistry and solid-state quantum technologies such
as NV centers in diamond.
Radical Qubits in Covalent Organic Frameworks. To scale from single qubits to arrays, we em-
bedded spin-active sites into covalent organic frameworks (COFs) using naphthalenediimide
(NDI) linkers. By tuning radical density, we achieved coherence times (T1 = 740 μs, T2 = 1.5 μs at
30 K) sufficient for quantum gate operations. Increasing radical concentration reduced coherence
by an order of magnitude, confirming the role of spin-spin interactions. This work demonstrates
modular control of qubit density in extended frameworks, paving the way for chemically tunable
quantum architectures.
Spectrally Addressable Bimetallic Qubit Dyads. Understanding qubit-qubit interactions is vital
for multi-qubit systems. We designed heterobimetallic complexes containing Cu(II) and Ti(III)
centers with distinct ESR frequencies, enabling spectral addressability under a single magnetic
field. Experiments revealed minimal decoherence from neighboring spins but uncovered weak ex-
change coupling at longer separations through quantum state tomography. These insights inform
strategies for deterministic design of interactions in multi-qubit networks.
Design Principles for Coherence in 3D Networks. Scaling qubits into three-dimensional frame-
works introduces new decoherence pathways. We explored the impact of linker rigidity and net-
work topology in porphyrinic MOFs. Extended frameworks with longer linkers suppressed spin-
spin flip-flops at low temperatures, improving coherence, while added phonon modes reduced per-
formance at higher temperatures. These findings highlight the delicate balance between structural
flexibility and vibrational dynamics, guiding future design of robust qubit arrays.

Lanthanide-Based Frameworks for Optical Qubits. Lanthanide ions offer unique optical and
magnetic properties for quantum applications. We synthesized Ce3+-based MOFs with tunable co-
ordination environments to mimic garnet structures. EPR studies indicated promising relaxation
dynamics, opening a pathway for lanthanide-based molecular qubits that combine optical address-
ability with chemical tunability.
Organic Radical Qubits for Multi-Qubit Logic. Organic radicals provide lightweight, versatile
platforms for quantum logic. We demonstrated photoinduced spin-state control in NDI-based
diradicals and stabilized radicals within cyclophane hosts. Using pulse ENDOR, we executed two-
qubit CNOT gate operations in triplet states, validating the potential of organic radicals for quan-
tum photonics and logic operations.
Magnetic Substrates and Data-Driven Design. Integration with magnetic substrates and predic-
tive modeling accelerates qubit development. We achieved ambient-stable CrI3 flakes via atomic-
layer encapsulation and exfoliated RuCl3 for hybrid assembly.

2.2 Device-Relevant Thin Films and Novel Systems
Highly Ordered Thin Films of Molecular Qubits. Building on molecular design, we advanced
toward device integration by fabricating highly ordered thin films of molecular qubits on mono-
layer graphene. Using molecules designed and synthesized by Freedman, Hersam fabricated highly
ordered thin films on monolayer graphene. These films retained optical and spin properties com-
parable to single crystals, with spin-lattice relaxation times significantly longer than optical life-
times, satisfying the critical requirement for optical initialization. This advance demonstrates that
molecular qubits can be engineered into scalable thin-film platforms without sacrificing quantum
performance, opening pathways for hybrid quantum devices.
Quantum Magnetic Field Sensing with Molecular Color Centers. We formulated and validated
a magnetic field sensing modality using optically addressable molecular color centers. Through
computational modeling and experimental validation, they showed that these molecular sensors
can resolve proximity-exchange versus magnetostatic effects from two-dimensional magnets with
high fidelity across varying distances. This capability positions molecular qubits as precision
probes for nanoscale magnetic phenomena, surpassing conventional approaches in tunability and
spatial resolution.
Water-Stable Molecular Color Centers. We introduced a new generation of molecular color cen-
ters engineered for environmental stability. By leveraging sterically bulky ligand frameworks, they
synthesized Cr(IV) complexes that maintain optical addressability even after prolonged water ex-
posure. This achievement addresses a key barrier to deploying molecular qubits in real-world con-
ditions, enabling robust sensing in diverse environments.
Novel Molecular Qubits for Biological Transparency. Our team also expanded the molecular
qubit toolkit by synthesizing vanadium and molybdenum complexes with tunable near-infrared
emission, enabling access to the biological transparency window and new sensing modalities. We

achieved coherent spin control in vanadium and molybdenum complexes, refining design strate-
gies for optical addressability in transition-metal systems. Investigations into substrate-induced
spin–phonon coupling highlighted the importance of surface engineering for preserving coherence
in device-integrated qubits. High-field EPR and magneto-photoluminescence measurements re-
vealed design constraints and opportunities for optical initialization in these systems.
New Materials as Quantum Sensing Targets. Hersam expanded the library of quantum sensing
targets by synthesizing and characterizing novel two-dimensional magnetic materials, including
transition metal halides and chalcogen-based van der Waals magnets. These materials exhibit un-
conventional magnetism and multiferroic behavior, providing ideal testbeds for molecular qubit
sensors. Their integration will enable direct comparison between molecular sensing and traditional
magneto-optical techniques.
Lanthanide-Based Molecular Color Centers. Freedman, Awschalom, and Long introduced lan-
thanide complexes as a new class of molecular color centers. These systems exploit symmetry-
allowed f-d transitions to deliver high fluorescence quantum yields and nanosecond-scale life-
times, essential for optical initialization and readout. Cerium complexes exhibited nanosecond-
scale optical lifetimes and symmetry-controlled transitions, offering potential for single-spin de-
tection and optically initialized dynamic nuclear polarization. Parallel efforts incorporated these
qubits into porous frameworks for nuclear sensing, demonstrating localized readout of proton spins
in confined environments. Early optical and EPR studies confirm their potential for single-spin
detection, positioning lanthanide-based molecules as promising candidates for next-generation
quantum sensors.
Systems-Chart Approach to Design Long Spin Relaxation Times. We built the first dataset of
more than 100 molecular qubit candidates and identified coordination environment as a key pre-
dictor of coherence. By curating a broad dataset for metal-complex qubits, we built systems design
charts linking chemical variables (ligand field strength, coordination geometry, heavy-atom con-
tent) to T1/T2 via known physical processes. These charts reduce de novo design complexity by
highlighting interdependencies and priority features for synthesis, accelerating the rational optimi-
zation of spin relaxation. Our systems-chart approach provided a framework for rational molecular
qubit design, mapping chemical structure to spin relaxation behavior and accelerating the discov-
ery of high-coherence candidates.

2.3 Quantum Sensing
Development of Molecular Electric Field Sensors. Wasielewski advanced the design of molecular
systems capable of electric field sensing, while Rondinelli provided theoretical insight into their
sensitivity. By modeling Stark shifts and vibrational mode coupling in molecular color centers, the
team established predictive frameworks for optimizing electric-field responsiveness. The study
forecasts that polar, heteroleptic molecular color centers can achieve NV-comparable electric-field
sensing, validating molecular electrometry as a strong complement to solid-state platforms. Com-
plementary studies quantified electric-field sensitivity in molecular color centers, identifying

 5

structural motifs that maximize responsiveness and validating molecular platforms for quantum-
enabled electrometry.
Conjugates for Single-Molecule Magnet Sensing. Long, Freedman, and Awschalom pioneered
the chemical integration of molecular qubits with single-molecule magnets (SMMs), creating con-
jugates that serve as nanoscale magnetic field sensors. These hybrid systems combine the tunabil-
ity of molecular qubits with the extreme magnetic anisotropy of SMMs, offering a platform for
mapping magnetic fields at the molecular scale. Magnetic characterization confirms open hystere-
sis at accessible temperatures, validating the concept for future quantum sensing applications.
Telecom-Band Molecular Spin–Photon Interface. We demonstrated a high-resolution molecular
spin-photon interface operating at telecommunications wavelengths, leveraging erbium-based
complexes to achieve MHz-scale optical linewidths and spin-selective readout. Crucially, opera-
tion at telecommunications wavelengths aligns molecular qubits with mature fiber-photonics and
microwave electronics, enabling optical polarization and readout and enabling a path to scalable,
integrated molecular spin–optical technologies.
Dark Magnetic Excitons in 2D NiI2. We introduced photocurrent spectroscopy as a powerful tool
for probing optically dark magnetic excitons in two-dimensional multiferroics. This technique re-
vealed excitonic resonances linked to antiferromagnetic order and multiferroicity down to the bi-
layer limit, establishing a new paradigm for opto-spintronic sensing in 2D quantum materials.
