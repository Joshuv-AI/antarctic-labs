Final Technical Report
Grant Number: SC0024462
Title: Testing the robustness of CMB-S4 inflation measurements to foreground models
PI: Kevin M. Huffenberger
Recipient Institution: Florida State University
Reporting Period:      07/01/2023 – 03/31/2025 (Active work 07/01/2023 – 03/31/2024)

This final technical report summarizes the research activities and outcomes for the Department of
Energy (DOE) award SC0024462, “Testing the robustness of CMB-S4 inflation measurements to
foreground models.” The primary objective of this project was to characterize and mitigate the impact
of Galactic foreground emissions on the detection of faint, odd-parity B-mode polarization signals in
the Cosmic Microwave Background. While the CMB-S4 project was ultimately canceled, the
methodologies and results produced under this work remain directly applicable to current and future
CMB projects, including the BICEP/Keck Array, the South Pole Telescope, and the Simons
Observatory. Due to the Lead PI’s move from Florida State University to Texas A&M University in the
summer of 2024 without opportunity to transfer the award, this report covers the initial year of the
planned project, during which we made progress on physical foreground modeling and the
development of robust analysis pipelines.

A major focus of the research during this period involved grounding foreground models in the physical
processes of the Interstellar Medium (ISM). In Stalpes, Collins, & Huffenberger (2024) (DOI:
10.3847/1538-4357/ad571b), the research team investigated the statistical properties of Galactic dust
polarization as observed by the Planck mission. By comparing observational data with a suite of
magnetohydrodynamical numerical simulations that we created, the study demonstrated that Planck
dust polarization power spectra are consistent with a regime of strongly supersonic turbulence. This
finding provides a critical physical foundation for the small-scale foreground models required to
evaluate the sensitivity of current telescopes at the South Pole and in the Atacama Desert to primordial
gravitational waves and other fundamental physics.

The project also addressed the emerging challenge of parity-violating signals in Galactic foregrounds,
which can potentially mimic or bias searches for cosmic birefringence—a possible signature of new
physics. In Hervías-Caimapo et al. (2025) (DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevD.111.083532), the team developed
a modeling framework using filamentary structures to represent Galactic dust polarization. This work
specifically aimed at characterizing the EB and TB power spectra induced by the ISM. By providing a
realistic model for these parity-odd signals, the project has enhanced the ability of experiments to
distinguish between instrumental or foreground effects and genuine cosmological signals.

Beyond these theoretical and modeling achievements, the grant provided essential support for the
technical training of graduate student Bai-Chiang Chiang. During the reporting period, Chiang focused
on the implementation and testing of Needlet Internal Linear Combination (NILC) techniques for
foreground removal. This work is an important tool for extracting the B-mode power spectrum in the
presence of complex foreground contamination.

While the PI's relocation has necessitated the conclusion of this specific award at FSU, the results
published and the methodologies developed have moved the field toward the rigorous foreground
mitigation required for the success of the current and future generations of cosmic frontier CMB
experiments that probe inflation and other beyond-the-standard model physics.
