Abstract
. We investigate the Cauchy problem for a heat equation driven by the mixed local-nonlocal operator ℒ := − Δ + ( − Δ ) s \mathcal{L}:=-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{s} , s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) , with exponential nonlinearity ∂ t u ⁡ ( x , t ) + ℒ ​ u ​ ( x , t ) = f ⁡ ( u ⁡ ( x , t ) ) , ( x , t ) ∈ ℝ d × ( 0 , ∞ ) , \partial_{t}u(x,t)+\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=f(u(x,t)),\qquad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty), where f : ℝ → ℝ f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R} exhibits exponential growth at infinity and satisfies f ⁡ ( 0 ) = 0 f(0)=0 . We establish local well-posedness in a suitable Orlicz space in the case where f ⁡ ( u ) ∼ e | u | p f(u)\sim e^{|u|^{p}} as | u | → ∞ |u|\to\infty , with p > 1 p>1 . Moreover, we derive large-time decay estimates in Lebesgue spaces, showing that the behavior of the nonlinearity near the origin determines the decay rate of solutions and highlights a unique asymptotic transition that bridges local and non-local diffusion theories.

1.
Introduction, main results and discussion

The main aim of this paper is to study the following local-nonlocal semilinear heat equation:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\,\,\displaystyle{\partial_{t}u(x,t)+\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=f(u(x,t)),}&\displaystyle{t>0,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},}\\
\\
\displaystyle{u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\qquad\qquad}&\displaystyle{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},}\end{array}\right.

where
d\geq 1
,
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a nonlinear function with exponential growth at infinity (
f(u)\sim e^{|u|^{p}}
,
p>1
, for large
u
) and satisfying
f(0)=0.
The operator

\mathcal{L}=-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{s},

is the mixed local-nonlocal operator with the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
being the (nonlocal) fractional Laplacian of order
s\in(0,1)
defined by

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}v(x)

\displaystyle:=C_{d,s}\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x-y|^{d+2s}}\,dy,

where P.V. stands for the Cauchy principal value,
C_{d,s}:=\frac{s4^{s}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+2s}{2}\right)}{\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(1-s)}
is a normalization constant, and
\Gamma
denotes the Gamma function.

It is worth emphasizing that the mixed local-nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}
consists of the superposition of the classical Laplacian
\Delta
, a local second-order differential operator, and the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
, which is inherently nonlocal. While the classical Laplacian models standard diffusion mechanisms such as Brownian motion, the fractional Laplacian describes anomalous diffusion processes driven by long-range interactions and jump-type dynamics, as observed in Lévy flight processes
[
11
,
37
]
. As a result, the operator
\mathcal{L}
embodies the interplay between local and nonlocal diffusion effects and provides a suitable framework for the mathematical modeling of phenomena in which short-range and long-range interactions occur simultaneously; we cite
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
and references therein for more detail. Consequently, problem (
1.1
)
serves as a framework for reaction-diffusion models with mixed diffusion.

Before delving into the subject of this paper, let us recall some known results in the classical polynomial case.

1.1.
The polynomial case

The Cauchy problem (
1.1
) has been extensively studied for
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta
in the framework of Lebesgue spaces, particularly when
f
has polynomial growth, that is,
f(u)=|u|^{p-1}u,\,\,p>1.
In this case, problem (
1.1
) becomes the classical semi-linear heat equation:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\,\,\displaystyle{\partial_{t}u(x,t)-\Delta u(x,t)=f(u(x,t)),}&\displaystyle{t>0,\,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},}\\
\\
\displaystyle{u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\qquad\qquad}&\displaystyle{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}.}\end{array}\right.

It is well established that if the initial datum
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the nonlinear term
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies
f(0)=0
, then there exists a maximal time
T=T(u_{0})>0
for which problem (
1.2
) admits a unique local-in-time solution in
L^{\infty}\!\big(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)
. On the other hand, the first local well-posedness results for problem (
1.2
) with the nonlinearity

f(u)=|u|^{p-1}u
,
p>1,
and initial data
u_{0}
which do not belong to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, were established by Weissler
[
38
,
39
]
. He showed that there always exists a Lebesgue space
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with
1\leq q<\infty
, in which problem (
1.2
) is locally well-posed. The scaling invariance property is a key feature in the analysis of power-type nonlinearities. A simple calculation shows that if
u(t,x)
is a solution to problem (
1.2
), then so is
u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{2/(p-1)}u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t),\,\lambda>0
. Moreover, the
L^{q}
-norm is invariant under this scaling if and only if
q=q_{c}:=d(p-1)/2
, equivalently
p=p_{h}^{*}:=1+2q/d
. The exponent
p_{h}^{*}
plays a crucial role in the existence theory for the Cauchy problem (
1.2
). In addition, Weissler showed that the Lebesgue space
L^{q_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
serves as the role of “critical space” for the well-posedness of problem (
1.2
).
With respect to this critical exponent, the existence and uniqueness theory for equation (
1.2
) with nonlinearity

f(u)=|u|^{p-1}u
,
p>1,
can be divided into the following two cases.

Case 1.

If
q\geq q_{c}
with
q>1
, or
q>q_{c}
with
q\geq 1
, then Weissler
[
39
]
and Brezis-Cazenave
[
6
]
proved that for any initial data
u_{0}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a positive time
T=T(u_{0})
and a unique solution

u\in C([0,T],L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

to problem (1.2).

Case 2.

If
q<q_{c}
, Weissler
[
39
]
and Brezis-Cazenave
[
6
]
showed that no local solution exists in any suitable weak sense. Furthermore, Haraux-Weissler
[
21
]
proved that uniqueness fails for the trivial initial data
u_{0}=0
when
1+2/d<p<(d+2)/(d-2).

Now, we turn our attention towards the global existence of the solution of (
1.2
). In his seminal work
[
17
]
, Fujita investigated problem (
1.1
) in the particular case of
f(u)=u^{p}
and
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta

to characterize the conditions under which global solutions exist and those under which they do not. He showed that for any nonnegative initial data
u_{0}
, there are no global positive solutions if
1<p<p_{F}:=1+2/d.
In contrast, when
p>p_{F}
, the problem admits a global positive solution provided that the initial datum
u_{0}\geq 0
is sufficiently small. Later, Hayakawa
[
20
]
and Sugitani
[
35
]
proved that the critical case
p=p_{F}
also leads to blow-up for all nonnegative initial data. The quantity
p_{F}
is therefore referred to as the
Fujita (critical) exponent
. In the critical case
q=q_{c}
and
d\geq 3
, Weissler
[
40
]
proved a global existence result for problem (
1.2
) under a smallness assumption on the initial data from
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
1<q<\infty
.

More recently, several authors have considered problem (
1.1
) with
f(u)=u^{p}
and the fractional Laplacian

\mathcal{L}=(-\Delta)^{s},\,\,\,s\in(0,1).

We refer to
[
15
,
19
,
35
]
and the references therein for a detailed account of these works. It has been observed that, in the sense of Fujita, the corresponding critical exponent in this fractional setting is given by

p_{c}=1+2s/d.

Finally, we turn our attention to the local and global well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) for polynomial type nonlinearities. In this case, the global existence and blow-up of solutions have been investigated in recent works by Biagi, Punzo, and Vecchi
[
4
]
, as well as by Kumar and Torebek
[
28
]
. Similar results were obtained by Del Pezzo and Ferreira in
[
10
]
for the operator
\mathcal{L}_{a,b}=-a\Delta+b(-\Delta)^{s}
, where
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
In these studies, it was observed that the critical Fujita exponent is
p_{c}=1+2s/d,

which marks the threshold between global existence and finite-time blow-up of positive solutions to (
1.1
) for
u_{0}\geq 0
. Interestingly, this critical exponent coincides with the Fujita exponent for the heat equation (
1.1
) associated with the pure fractional operator
\mathcal{L}=(-\Delta)^{s}
with power-type nonlinearity. This shows that the presence of a local diffusion term does not modify the fundamental global existence behavior dictated by the fractional component. In particular, the mixed local-nonlocal operator retains the same criticality as the fractional Laplacian in determining the long-time dynamics of solutions. An interesting phenomenon observed in
[
26
]
concerns the heat kernel estimates for mixed local–nonlocal operators (see Lemma
2.4
). Specifically, the local component dominates the behavior in the small-time regime, whereas the nonlocal component dominates at large times. This interplay yields sharper and novel results that do not arise when considering purely local or purely nonlocal operators separately. We also cite
[
3
,
7
,
26
,
27
]
and references therein for further developments in this line of research.

1.2.
The exponential case, main results and discussion

A natural question that arises in this context is the following:
when the nonlinearity exhibits exponential growth, can one identify functional spaces that contain unbounded initial data and still allow for the development of a well-posed existence theory?
This question has attracted considerable attention and has been investigated by several authors.

Cazenave and Weissler
[
9
]
pointed out a strong connection between the
L^{q}
-theory for the nonlinear heat equation and the
H^{s}
-theory for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation

(1.3)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=|u|^{p-1}u,&t>0,\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{cases}

They showed that for
0\leq s<d/2
there exists a critical exponent
p_{s}^{*}=1+4/(d-2s),
which is determined by the scaling invariance of (
1.3
) in the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In particular, whenever
1<p\leq p_{s}^{*}
and
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a time
T=T(u_{0})>0
such that the Cauchy problem (
1.3
) possesses a unique solution
u\in C([0,T);H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})).
Furthermore, in the critical case
p=p_{s}^{*}
, global well-posedness can be obtained for sufficiently small initial data in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by applying a fixed point argument. When
s=d/2
, the corresponding critical exponent becomes infinite, that is
p_{s}^{*}=\infty
, which indicates that the critical nonlinearity grows faster than any power-type nonlinearity. In this regime, Nakamura and Ozawa
[
32
]
established global existence for small initial data in
H^{d/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with an exponential-type nonlinearity satisfying
f(u)\sim e^{|u|^{2}}
.

Cazenave and Weissler
[
9
]
also observed that the critical exponent
p_{h}^{*}=1+2q/d
, associated with the heat equation (
1.2
) with initial data
u_{0}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and the critical exponent
p_{s}^{*}
corresponding to the Schrödinger equation (
1.3
), are linked via the Sobolev embedding
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
0\leq s<d/2
and the parameters satisfy

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{s}{d}.

A direct computation then shows that this embedding yields

p_{h}^{*}=1+\frac{2q}{d}=1+\frac{4}{d-2s}=p_{s}^{*},

so that the functional spaces associated with the critical exponents of both equations coincide through this Sobolev embedding. Inspired by this relationship, Ruf and Terraneo
[
34
]
suggested an analogue of this correspondence for problem (
1.1
) with
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta
and
u_{0}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, in the presence of an exponential nonlinearity satisfying

f(u)\sim e^{|u|^{2}},\,|u|\to\infty
. They indicated that global solutions exist for sufficiently small initial data in a function space that contains
H^{d/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, by virtue of the Sobolev embedding. However, it is known that no Lebesgue space containing
H^{d/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
provides an appropriate framework for studying (
1.1
) with
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta
and such exponential nonlinearities. Instead, the suitable functional setting for their analysis of the heat equation with exponential type nonlinearity was given by the Orlicz space
\exp L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It is an appropriate place to introduce the Orlicz space
\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),1\leq p<\infty
, as follows

\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left\{u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\lambda^{p}}\right)-1\right)\,dx<\infty,\,\textnormal{for some}\,\,\lambda>0\right\},

which is a Banach space when endowed with the Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\lambda^{p}}\right)-1\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\}.

It follows from the Trudinger-Moser inequality that the Orlicz space

\exp L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
enjoys the continuous embeddings

H^{\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\exp L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\text{for every}\,\,2\leq r<\infty.

Furthermore, in the scale of Orlicz spaces,
\exp L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be regarded as
the smallest space that contains
H^{d/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Ruf and Terraneo
[
34
]
investigated the heat equation (
1.1
) with a nonlinear term
f

exhibiting exponential growth
e^{|u|^{2}}
at infinity. They obtained the local-in-time existence of
solutions for sufficiently small initial data in the Orlicz space

\exp L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Later, Ioku
[
25
]
proved the global-in-time existence
of solutions to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) for small initial data belonging to

\exp L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It has further been established that when the nonlinearity satisfies

f(u)\sim e^{|u|^{s}}
as
|u|\to\infty
with
s>2
, the existence of
solutions can no longer be guaranteed. In fact, in this regime one obtains
a nonexistence result in the Orlicz space
\exp L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;
see, for instance,
[
24
]
. The two-dimensional case of the heat equation (
1.1
) was investigated by
Ibrahim, Jrad, Majdoub, and Saanouni
[
23
]
. This setting is critical
from the viewpoint of the energy method as well as with respect to the
critical Trudinger embedding. They established a local existence and uniqueness
result for the corresponding problem. In addition, they obtained global
existence in the defocusing case and derived a blow-up result in the focusing case. Majdoub and Tayachi
[
30
,
31
]
extended the results of

[
24
,
25
]
to nonlinearities of the type

f(u)\sim e^{|u|^{q}}
, as
|u|\to\infty
, with
q>1
.
They studied the problem in the Orlicz framework

\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
p\geq q>1
,
establishing local well-posedness, global existence for small initial data,
and large-time decay estimates of solutions. The second author and Kirane
[
16
]
considered (
1.1
) for
\mathcal{L}=(-\Delta)^{s}
extending the results of
[
30
,
31
]
into the nonlocal setting.

The main aim of this paper is to study problem (
1.1
) with a nonlinearity
f
exhibiting exponential growth at infinity. As discussed earlier, to handle nonlinearities with exponential growth, one needs to work in the framework of Orlicz spaces, in analogy with the case of power-type nonlinearities, where Lebesgue spaces are the natural setting. Let
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
denote the linear semigroup generated by the mixed local-nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}
defined in (
2.3
). Motivated by
[
24
,
29
,
30
]
, we study the local-wellposedness of the equation (
1.1
) in the space
\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
which is defined by

\displaystyle\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\Big\{u\in\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\textnormal{there exists $\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$}\qquad

\displaystyle\quad\textnormal{such that $\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{n}-u\|_{\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0$}\Big\}.

This space turns out to be an appropriate setting for the study of (
1.1
),
since
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is not dense in

\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This lack of density influences the mapping
properties of the heat semigroup
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
. In particular,

e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
is bounded on
\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(see Proposition
2
), whereas such a continuity property is not
generally expected to hold on the whole space
\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(cf.
[
24
]
for the case
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta
). It is also known
[
24
]
that

\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left\{u\in L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\exp\left(\alpha|u(x)|^{p}\right)-1\right)\,dx<\infty,\,\textnormal{for every}\,\,\alpha>0\right\}.

In order to state our main results, we introduce two notions of mild solutions:
The standard mild solution formulated in the space

\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
and the weak-mild solution formulated in the larger space

\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
T>0
. A function

u\in C([0,T],\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is called a
mild solution
of the Cauchy problem
(
1.1
)
if it satisfies

(1.4)

u(t)=e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\mathcal{L}}f(u(s))\,ds,\qquad t\in[0,T].

Definition 1.2
.

Given
u_{0}\in\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
T>0
. A function
u\in L^{\infty}\big((0,T),\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)
is called a
weak-mild solution
of the Cauchy problem
(
1.1
)
if satisfies the corresponding integral equation (
1.4
) in
\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for almost every
t\in(0,T)
, and

u(t)\rightarrow u_{0}
in the weak
∗
topology as
t\to 0
.

Recall that
u(t)\to u_{0}
in the weak
∗
sense if and only if

\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(u(t,x)-u_{0}(x)\big)\varphi(x)\,dx=0,\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in L^{1}(\ln L)^{1/p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Here, the space
L^{1}(\ln L)^{1/p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by

L^{1}(\ln L)^{1/p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\;:\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|\,\ln^{1/p}\!\big(2+|f(x)|\big)\,dx<\infty\right\},

which serves as a predual of
\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
; we refer to excellent books
[
2
,
33
]
for details and further references.

We are now prepared to present the first main result of this paper, which addresses the local well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.3
.

(Local well-posedness)
. Let
d\geq 1
,
p>1
, and
s\in(0,1)
. Suppose that the nonlinearity
f
satisfies

(1.5)

f(0)=0,\qquad|f(u)-f(v)|\leq C|u-v|(e^{\lambda|u|^{p}}+e^{\lambda|v|^{p}}),\quad\hbox{for all}\,\,u,v\in\mathbb{R},

for some constants,
C>0
and
\lambda>0
. Then, for any initial data

u_{0}\in\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a time
T=T(u_{0})>0
such that the Cauchy problem
(
1.1
)
admits a unique mild solution

u\in C\big([0,T],\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big).

Condition (
1.5
) is fulfilled, for instance, by
f(u)=\pm ue^{|u|^{p}}
. In addition, by the uniqueness result of Theorem
1.3
, the local solution
u
of (
1.1
)
can be extended to a maximal interval
[0,T_{\max})

by a standard continuation argument
(see, e.g., Cazenave and Haraux
[
8
]
), where

T_{\max}:=\sup\left\{T>0:\;\text{there exists a mild solution }u\in C([0,T],\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\text{ to (\ref{eq1})}\right\}.

Moreover, if
T_{\max}<\infty
, then the blow-up alternative holds:

\lim_{t\to T_{\max}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\infty.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
relies on semigroup methods combined with a decomposition technique based on the density of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in
\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see, e.g.,
[
22
,
24
,
29
,
30
]
). This approach enables us to establish both existence and uniqueness of solutions to (
1.1
) in the
\exp L^{p}
framework. Given
u_{0}\in\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we decompose
u_{0}=v_{0}+w_{0}
, where
v_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
w_{0}\in\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is chosen to be sufficiently small in the corresponding norm. We first solve (
1.1
) with smooth initial data
v_{0}
via classical semigroup arguments, obtaining a local-in-time bounded solution
v
. Setting
w:=u-v
, we are led to a perturbed problem with small initial data. The latter is handled via the Banach fixed-point theorem applied to the integral formulation (
1.4
) in a suitable Banach space adapted to
\exp L^{p}
. The argument relies crucially on semigroup estimates associated with the mixed local-nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}
(see (
2.4
) and Proposition
1
), together with the growth condition (
1.5
). This procedure yields the existence of the desired solution
u=v+w
. Uniqueness follows from similar estimates in the same functional framework. Further details and related techniques can be found in the literature (see, e.g.,
[
16
,
24
,
30
]
).

Remark 1
.

The solution obtained in Theorem
1.3
belongs to

L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. Indeed, let

u\in C([0,T],\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

be a mild solution of (
1.1
), that is, a solution of the integral equation (
1.4
).
Using the
L^{p}-L^{\infty}
estimate (
2.4
) together with
Lemma
2.2
-(3), we obtain

\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ct_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sp}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\leq Ct_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sp}}\|u_{0}\|_{\exp L^{p}},\qquad 0<t<T.

Hence
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It remains to estimate the nonlinear term. Fix
r>\max\{p,\frac{d}{2}\}
.
By the
L^{r}-L^{\infty}
estimate (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\|_{L^{\infty}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sr}}\|f(u(\tau))\|_{L^{r}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\,t^{1-\frac{d}{2r}}\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq T}\|f(u(\tau))\|_{L^{r}}<\infty,

in view of Corollary
4.2
. Consequently,
u\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
Furthermore, if
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, then the additional regularity of the solution implies

u\in C([0,T],\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

satisfies (
1.1
) in the classical sense, namely,

C^{1}
in time and
C^{2}
in space.

We now examine the global existence and decay behavior of solutions to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
). In this context, the behavior of the nonlinearity
f(u)
in a neighborhood of the origin is of fundamental importance. To this end, we allow
f
to satisfy the following growth condition near zero:

|f(u)|\sim|u|^{m},

where the parameters are such that
d(m-1)/(2s)>1
. The reason of this assumption is to reach the Fujita exponent
1+2s/d
as obtained in
[
28
]
. More precisely, we assume that

(1.6)

f(0)=0,\qquad|f(u)-f(v)|\leq C\,|u-v|\left(|u|^{m-1}e^{\lambda|u|^{p}}+|v|^{m-1}e^{\lambda|v|^{p}}\right),

for all
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
, where
C>0
,
\lambda>0
, and the exponents satisfy
d(m-1)/(2s)\geq p>1
. A canonical example of a nonlinearity satisfying (
1.6
) is
f(u)=\pm|u|^{m-1}u\,e^{|u|^{p}}
, with
m\geq 1+2sp/d
. We state our global existence result in the form of the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

(Global existence).
Let
d\geq 1
and
s\in(0,1)
.
Assume that the nonlinearity
f
satisfies (
1.6
) for some
m\geq p
and

1<p\leq d(m-1)/(2s)
with
C
and
\lambda
being positive constants.
Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that, for every initial data

u_{0}\in\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|u_{0}\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq\varepsilon,
problem (
1.1
) admits a global weak-mild solution

u\in L^{\infty}\big((0,\infty),\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big).

Moreover, the solution satisfies

(1.7)

\lim_{t\to 0}\|u(t)-e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\|_{\exp L^{p}}=0.

In addition, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(1.8)

\|u(t)\|_{q}\leq C\,t_{s}^{-(\frac{1}{m-1}-\frac{d}{2sq})},\qquad\text{for all }t>0,

where
t_{s}:=\max\{t^{s},t\}
and the exponent
q
satisfies:

•

If
s<\dfrac{d(p-1)}{2p}
, then
\dfrac{d(m-1)}{s}<2q<\dfrac{d(m-1)}{s}\dfrac{1}{(2-m)_{+}}
.

•

If
s=\dfrac{d(p-1)}{2p}
, then
\dfrac{d(m-1)}{s}<2q<\dfrac{d(m-1)}{s}\dfrac{1}{(2-m)_{+}}
.

•

If
s>\dfrac{d(p-1)}{2p}
and
(2-m)_{+}<\dfrac{d(p-1)}{2ps}
, then

\dfrac{2(m-1)p}{p-1}<2q<\dfrac{d(m-1)}{s}\dfrac{1}{(2-m)_{+}}.

Here,
(\cdot)_{+}
denotes the positive part.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
is based on refined mapping estimates of the semigroup
\{e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\}_{t\geq 0}
(see Proposition
1
), obtained via heat kernel bounds and interpolation arguments in Orlicz spaces. We then implement a fixed-point argument in a complete metric space defined through time-weighted
\exp L^{p}
norms. This framework allows us to derive suitable a priori estimates and control the nonlinear term, leading to the global existence result for small initial data, as well as estimate (
1.8
).

Remark 2
.

The decay estimate (
1.8
) highlights a unique asymptotic transition that bridges local and non-local diffusion theories. Its primary novelty lies in the short-time regime (
t\to 0
), where the solution mimics the decay behavior of the local heat equation through the effective power
\tilde{m}
such that
\tilde{m}-1:=(m-1)/s,
that is,
\tilde{m}:=(m-(1-s))/s
, aligning with the local smoothing effects discussed in
[
30
,
31
]
. As time progresses (
t\to\infty
), the estimate naturally transitions to recover the purely non-local asymptotics of the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
established in
[
16
]
. By unifying these two scales into a single estimate, (
1.8
) demonstrates that non-local effects are not instantaneous but emerge over time, providing a continuous interpolation between singular local dynamics and heavy-tailed fractional dissipation.

Remark 3
.

For the analysis of Theorem
1.4
, we distinguish three cases according to the relationship between
s
and the critical threshold
d(p-1)/(2p)
, namely
s<d(p-1)/(2p)
,
s>d(p-1)/(2p)
, and
s=d(p-1)/(2p)
.
This distinction reflects the balance between the smoothing effect of the semigroup
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
and the growth
of exponential-type nonlinearity. In particular, the threshold
~d(p-1)/(2p)
arises naturally from the time-integrability condition required in the estimate of the nonlinear term in the mild formulation.

Moreover, in the case
s>d(p-1)/(2p)
, it is necessary to choose
m>p
. Indeed, taking
m=p
would yield
s>d(m-1)/(2m)
, while condition
d(m-1)/s\geq 2p
implies
s\leq d(m-1)/(2m),
leading to a contradiction. Therefore, the choice
m=p
is not admissible in this regime.

2.1.
Orlicz space and its properties

Definition 2.1

(Orlicz space and subspaces)
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a convex and increasing function satisfying

\phi(0)=0,\qquad\lim_{\tau\to 0^{+}}\phi(\tau)=0,\qquad\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\phi(\tau)=\infty.

Then the Orlicz space
L^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by

L^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left\{u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\!\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\right)dx<\infty,\text{ for some }\lambda>0\right\}.

The space is endowed with the Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{L^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\!\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\right)dx\leq 1\right\}.

We also introduce the subspace

L^{\phi}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left\{u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\!\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\right)dx<\infty,\text{ for every }\lambda>0\right\}.

Clearly,
L^{\phi}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
It is known (as in Ioku et al.
[
24
]
) that

L^{\phi}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\overline{C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\|\cdot\|_{L^{\phi}}},
. Moreover, both

(L^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\|\cdot\|_{L^{\phi}})

and

(L^{\phi}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\|\cdot\|_{L^{\phi}})

are Banach spaces.

Special cases.

•

If
\phi(s)=s^{p}
with
1\leq p<\infty
, then

L^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=L^{\phi}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
and the Luxemburg norm coincides with the usual
L^{p}
norm.

•

If
\phi(s)=e^{s^{p}}-1
with
1\leq p<\infty
, then

L^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
coincides with the exponential Orlicz space

\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, while

L^{\phi}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
corresponds to

\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

A basic property.

Let
u\in L^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and set

K=\|u\|_{L^{\phi}}>0.

By definition,

K=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\!\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\right)dx\leq 1\right\}.

Since
\phi
is increasing, if
\lambda
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\right)dx\leq 1,

then the same inequality holds for every
\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda
.
Hence, the admissible set of
\lambda
is of the form
[K,\infty)
, and in particular,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\!\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{\|u\|_{L^{\phi}}}\right)dx\leq 1.

The following lemma summarizes some embedding (inclusion) properties between Orlicz and Lebesgue spaces.

Lemma 2.2

(Inclusion properties)
.

We have the following inclusion relations:

(1)

[
30
, Lemma 2.3]

For every
1\leq q\leq p
, we have

L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

More precisely, we have

(2.1)

\|u\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{(\ln 2)^{1/p}}(\|u\|_{q}+\|u\|_{\infty}).

(2)

[
16
, Lemma 2.3]

Let
\phi(\tau)=e^{\tau^{p}}-1-\tau^{p}
,
p>1
. For every
q\leq 2p
, we have
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{\phi}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, more precisely

\|u\|_{L^{\phi}}\leq C(p)(\|u\|_{q}+\|u\|_{\infty}).

(3)

[
30
, Lemma 2.4]

For every
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
, we have
\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, more precisely

(2.2)

\|u\|_{q}\leq\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{q}{p}+1\right)\right)^{1/q}\|u\|_{\exp L^{p}}.

2.2.
Heat semigroup of
\mathcal{L}
and its properties

For the material of this subsection, we cite
[
4
,
7
,
26
]
and references therein. We begin with the following result regarding the integral representation of the heat semigroup associated with the mixed local-nonlocal operator.

Lemma 2.3

(Heat kernel representation)
.

Let
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
be the heat semigroup on
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(q\geq 1)
generated by
-\mathcal{L}
. Then

(2.3)

e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\phi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P^{s}_{t}(x-y)\,\phi(y)\,dy,\quad\phi\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where

P^{s}_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{|x-\xi|^{2}}{4t}}H_{t}^{s}(\xi)\,d\xi,\quad t>0,\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

is the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}
. Here,
H_{t}^{s}
denotes the heat kernel of the operator
-(-\Delta)^{s}
.

We have the following
L^{q}-L^{r}
estimate of the heat semigroup associated with the mixed local-nonlocal operator.

Lemma 2.4

(
L^{q}-L^{r}
estimate)
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
1\leq q\leq r\leq\infty
and any

\varphi\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(2.4)

\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\|_{r}\leq C\,t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2s}(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r})}\|\varphi\|_{q},\quad t>0,

where
t_{s}:=\max\{t^{s},t\}
.

3.
Some technical results

This subsection aims to derive some important results that will be useful throughout the paper. Our first result concerns estimates for
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
on
\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proposition 1
.

Let
1\leq q\leq p
,
1\leq r\leq\infty
, and
0<s<1
. Define
t_{s}:=\max\{t^{s},t\}
. Then the heat semigroup
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
satisfies the following mapping properties:

(1)

\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}
,     for all
t>0,

\varphi\in{\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.

(2)

\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\lesssim t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sq}}\left(\ln(t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2s}}+1)\right)^{-1/p}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{q}
,     for all
t>0,

\varphi\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(3)

\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(\ln 2)^{1/p}}\left[t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sr}}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{r}+\left\|\varphi\right\|_{q}\right]
,   for all
t>0,

\varphi\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

First, we establish (1). By the definition of the Luxemburg norm in the Orlicz space
\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

(3.1)

\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi|^{p}}{\lambda^{p}}\right)-1\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\}.

Now, we first estimate the integral in (
3.1
) by using (
2.4
) and the Taylor formula as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi|^{p}}{\lambda^{p}}\right)-1\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi|^{p}}{\lambda^{p}}\right)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\,dx=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\right\|_{pk}^{pk}}{k!\lambda^{pk}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left\|\varphi\right\|_{pk}^{pk}}{k!\lambda^{pk}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!\lambda^{pk}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi|^{pk}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{|\varphi|^{p}}{\lambda^{p}}\right)-1\right)\,dx,

for any
\lambda>0
. Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\{\lambda>0:\,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{|\varphi|^{p}}{\lambda^{p}}\right)-1\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\subseteq\left\{\lambda>0:\,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi|^{p}}{\lambda^{p}}\right)-1\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\},

which further implies, using (
3.1
), that

\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{|\varphi|^{p}}{\lambda^{p}}\right)-1\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\}=\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}.

Next, we prove (2). We first notice, using (
2.4
) and the Taylor expansion, that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi|^{p}}{\lambda^{p}}\right)-1\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\right\|_{pk}^{pk}}{k!\lambda^{pk}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{C^{pk}t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2s}(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{pk})pk}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{q}^{pk}}{k!\lambda^{pk}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{s}^{\frac{d}{2s}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{Ct_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sq}}\|\varphi\|_{q}}{\lambda}\right)^{p}-1\right),

for any
\lambda>0
. Moreover, it is easy to see that

t_{s}^{\frac{d}{2s}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{Ct_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sq}}\|\varphi\|_{q}}{\lambda}\right)^{p}-1\right)\leq 1\Longleftrightarrow\lambda\geq C\,t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sq}}\left(\ln(t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2s}}+1)\right)^{-1/p}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{q},

which shows that

\displaystyle\left\{\lambda>0:\,\,\lambda\in\Big[C\,t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sq}}\left(\ln(t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2s}}+1)\right)^{-1/p}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{q},\,\infty\Big)\right\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\lambda>0:\,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi|^{p}}{\lambda^{p}}\right)-1\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\}.

Therefore, using the Luxembourg norm (
3.1
) and the above inclusion, we have

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\,\,\lambda\in\Big[C\,t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sq}}\left(\ln(t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2s}}+1)\right)^{-1/p}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{q},\,\infty\Big)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\,t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sq}}\left(\ln(t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2s}}+1)\right)^{-1/p}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{q}.

Finally, we derive (3). Applying Lemma
2.2
-(1) and then using (
2.4
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\ln 2)^{1/p}}\left(\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\right\|_{q}+\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\right\|_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\ln 2)^{1/p}}\left(\left\|\varphi\right\|_{q}+C\,t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sr}}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{r}\right).

This completes the proof.
\square

We also require the following smoothing result. Although its proof follows along the same lines as that of
[
29
, Proposition 3.7]
, we provide a detailed argument here for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 2
.

For any
\varphi\in\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the map
t\mapsto e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi
belongs to
C([0,\infty),\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})).

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By Proposition
1
-(1) and the definition of
\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it follows that
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\in\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
t>0
. By the linearity of the semigroup
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
, it is sufficient to prove the continuity at
t=0
, that is,

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi-\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}=0.

Since
\varphi\in\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a sequence
(\varphi_{n})_{n}\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\varphi_{n}-\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}=0.

By virtue of (
2.1
) and Proposition
1
-(1), we infer that

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi-\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}(\varphi-\varphi_{n})\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}+\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{n}\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}+\left\|\varphi_{n}-\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\ln 2)^{1/p}}\left(\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{n}\|_{p}+\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{n}\|_{\infty}\right)+2\left\|\varphi_{n}-\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}.

Since
\varphi_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the semigroup
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
is strongly continuous on
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
1<r\leq\infty
, we obtain

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\left(\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{n}\|_{p}+\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{n}\|_{\infty}\right)=0.

Therefore,

\limsup_{t\rightarrow 0}\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi-\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq 2\left\|\varphi_{n}-\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}},

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This completes the proof of the proposition.

\square

Since
e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
is a
C^{0}
-semigroup on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
using Proposition
2
we obtain the following result.

Corollary 1
.

The semigroup
\{e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a
C^{0}
-semigroup on
\exp L^{p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We state the following result (see
[
8
, Lemma 4.1.5]
), adapted to the mixed local-nonlocal heat semigroup.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
g\in L^{1}(0,T;X)
, then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}g(\tau)\,d\tau\in C([0,T],X).

Moreover, we have

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}g(\tau)\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;X)}\leq\|g\|_{L^{1}(0,T;X)}.

The following lemmas are essential for the proof of the global existence (Theorem
1.4
).

Lemma 3.2
.

[
30
, Lemma 2.6]

Let
\lambda>0
,
1\leq q<\infty
and
K>0
be such that
\lambda qK^{p}\leq 1
. Assume that
u\in\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies

\|u\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq K,

then

\exp\left(\frac{|u|^{p}}{\lambda^{p}}\right)-1\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|e^{\lambda|u|^{p}}-1\right\|_{q}\leq\left(\lambda qK^{p}\right)^{1/q}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p>1
,
s\in(0,1)
be such that
s<d(p-1)/(2p)
. Then, for every
r>\max\{d/2,1\}
, there exists
C=C(d,p,\tau,r)
such that

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}g(\tau)\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\,\exp L^{p})}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\,L^{1}\cap L^{r})},

for every
g\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\,L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

Proof.

By Proposition
1
, we have

(3.2)

\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq C\,t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2s}}\left(\ln(t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2s}}+1)\right)^{-1/p}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{1},

and

(3.3)

\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq C\,(t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sr}}+1)\left[\left\|\varphi\right\|_{r}+\left\|\varphi\right\|_{1}\right].

for all
t>0
,
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
t_{s}:=\max\{t^{s},t\}
. Combining (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we get

\left\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\lesssim\kappa(t)\left[\left\|\varphi\right\|_{r}+\left\|\varphi\right\|_{1}\right],

where

\kappa(t):=\min\left\{(t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sr}}+1),t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2s}}\left(\ln(t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2s}}+1)\right)^{-1/p}\right\}.

Due to the assumptions
s<d(p-1)/(2p)
and
r>d/2
, we see that

We note that

Lemma 3.4

for every

Proof.

On the one hand, by

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\left(\frac{|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi|}{\lambda}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{C^{pk}t_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2s}(1-\frac{1}{pk})pk}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{1}^{pk}}{k!\lambda^{pk}}

\displaystyle t_{s}^{\frac{d}{2s}}\phi\left(\frac{Ct_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2s}}\|\varphi\|_{1}}{\lambda}\right)

for all

,

t>0,

\displaystyle Ct^{-\frac{d(p-1)}{4sp}}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{2p^{\prime}}+\left\|\varphi\right\|_{2p}

\displaystyle(t_{s}^{-\frac{1}{2}}+1)\left(\left\|\varphi\right\|_{2p^{\prime}}+\left\|\varphi\right\|_{2p}\right),

for all
t>0

s=d(p-1)/(2p)

Combining the above estimates, we obtain

(4.9)

\sup_{0<\tau<t}\|\tilde{u}(\tau)-\tilde{v}(\tau)\|_{p}\leq C(T,t_{0},\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\,t\,\sup_{0<\tau<t}\|\tilde{u}(\tau)-\tilde{v}(\tau)\|_{\exp L^{p}}.

Using the
L^{r}-L^{\infty}
estimate (
2.4
), we similarly obtain, for
r>\max\{p,d/2\}
,

\|\tilde{u}(t)-\tilde{v}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sr}}\|f(\tilde{u}(\tau))-f(\tilde{v}(\tau))\|_{r}\,d\tau.

Proceeding as above and using Lemma
2.2
-(3) and Proposition
4.1
, we get

(4.10)

\sup_{0<\tau<t}\|\tilde{u}(\tau)-\tilde{v}(\tau)\|_{\infty}\leq C(T,t_{0},\tilde{u},\tilde{v})t^{1-\frac{d}{2r}}\sup_{0<\tau<t}\|\tilde{u}(\tau)-\tilde{v}(\tau)\|_{\exp L^{p}}.

Finally, combining (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) and using Lemma
2.2
-(1), we deduce

\sup_{0<\tau<t}\|\tilde{u}(\tau)-\tilde{v}(\tau)\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq C(T,t_{0},\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\left(t+t^{1-\frac{d}{2r}}\right)\sup_{0<\tau<t}\|\tilde{u}(\tau)-\tilde{v}(\tau)\|_{\exp L^{p}}.

By choosing
t>0
sufficiently small, we obtain (
4.8
). This completes the proof.

\square

5.
Global Well-posedness: Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section, we prove the global well-posedness and decay estimates for problem (
1.1
) with initial data in the space
\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.
We prove the theorem by distinguishing two cases: the first case corresponds to
s<d(p-1)/(2p),
while the second corresponds to
s\geq d(p-1)/(2p).

(i) Case
s<d(p-1)/(2p)
.
We prove the result by means of the Banach fixed-point theorem. To this end, we introduce the complete metric space

\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}=\left\{u\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\;:\;\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq 2\varepsilon\right\},

and define the integral operator
\Phi
by

\Phi(u):=e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau.

Here
\varepsilon>0
is a sufficiently small constant,
which will be chosen later so that

\|u_{0}\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq\varepsilon.

To establish the result, we show that
\Phi
is a contraction
mapping on
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
for a suitable choice of

\varepsilon
. We begin by proving that
\Phi
is well-defined on
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
, that is,
\Phi:\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
. For
u\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
, using Proposition
1
-(1) and Lemma
3.3
, we have for every
r>\max\{d/2,1\}
,

\left\|\Phi(u)\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq\varepsilon+C\left\|f(u)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{1}\cap L^{r})}.

We now estimate
f(u)
in the
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm for cases
q=1
and
q=r
.
From assumption (
1.6
), we have

|f(u)|\leq C|u|^{m}\big(e^{\lambda|u|^{p}}-1\big)+C|u|^{m}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\|f(u)\|_{q}\leq C\|u\|_{2mq}^{m}\|e^{\lambda|u|^{p}}-1\|_{2q}+C\|u\|_{mq}^{m}.

Using Lemma
2.2
-(3) and the fact that
m\geq p
, we deduce

\|f(u)\|_{q}\leq C\|u\|_{\exp L^{p}}^{m}\|e^{\lambda|u|^{p}}-1\|_{2q}+C\|u\|_{\exp L^{p}}^{m}.

In addition, by Lemma
3.2
and the fact that
u\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

(5.1)

\|f(u)\|_{q}\leq C(2\varepsilon)^{m}\left(1+(2\lambda q(2\varepsilon)^{p})^{1/q}\right)\leq C(2\varepsilon)^{m}\left(1+(2\lambda q(2\varepsilon)^{p})^{1/r}\right).

Consequently, choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we arrive at

\|\Phi(u)\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq 2\varepsilon
, that is,
\Phi(u)\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
.

We now establish the contraction property of
\Phi
on
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
. Let
u,v\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
. For every
r>\max\{d/2,1\}
, Lemma
3.3
yields

\left\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq C\left\|f(u)-f(v)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{1}\cap L^{r})}.

To estimate
f(u)-f(v)
in the
L^{q}
-norm for
q=1
and
q=r
, we proceed as follows. Using assumption (
1.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle|f(u)-f(v)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|u-v|\left(|u|^{m-1}(e^{\lambda|u|^{p}}-1)+|v|^{m-1}(e^{\lambda|v|^{p}}-1)\right)

\displaystyle+\,C|u-v|\left(|u|^{m-1}+|v|^{m-1}\right).

Applying Hölder’s inequality with the conjugate exponent
m^{\prime}=m/(m-1)
, we deduce

\displaystyle C\left\|f(u)-f(v)\right\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{C\left\|u-v\right\|_{mq}\left\||u|^{m-1}(e^{\lambda|u|^{p}}-1)+|v|^{m-1}(e^{\lambda|v|^{p}}-1)\right\|_{qm^{\prime}}}_{I}

\displaystyle\underbrace{+C\left\|u-v\right\|_{mq}\left\||u|^{m-1}+|v|^{m-1}\right\|_{qm^{\prime}}}_{J}.

Using Hölder’s inequality once more, together with
Lemma
2.2
-(3) and the assumption
m\geq p
, we estimate
I
as follows

I\leq C\left\|u-v\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\left(\|u\|^{m-1}_{\exp L^{p}}\left\|e^{\lambda|u|^{p}}-1\right\|_{2qm^{\prime}}+\|v\|^{m-1}_{\exp L^{p}}\left\|e^{\lambda|v|^{p}}-1\right\|_{2qm^{\prime}}\right).

Invoking Lemma
3.2
and the fact that

u,v\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
, we infer that

I\leq C2^{m}\varepsilon^{m-1}\left(\frac{2\lambda mq}{m-1}(2\varepsilon)^{p}\right)^{1/2qm^{\prime}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}}\leq\frac{1}{8}\left\|u-v\right\|_{\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}},

for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small.
Similarly, we estimate
J
as

J\leq C2^{m}\varepsilon^{m-1}\left\|u-v\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{8}\left\|u-v\right\|_{\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}},

again for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small. Consequently,

C\left\|f(u)-f(v)\right\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{r}}\leq 2(I+J),

and therefore

\left\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|u-v\right\|_{\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}}.

This concludes the proof of the global existence result stated in
Theorem
1.4
for the case
s<d(p-1)/(2p)
. To derive the decay estimate (
1.8
), we proceed along the same lines as in the contraction argument for the case
s\geq d(p-1)/(2p)
below, with a slight modification of the functional framework. Instead of working in
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
, we consider the complete metric space

\left\{u\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}));\,\,\sup_{t>0}t_{s}^{\sigma}\|u(t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq M\varepsilon\right\},

equipped with the metric
d(u,v):=\sup_{t>0}t_{s}^{\sigma}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{q}
, where
M>0
is a sufficiently large constant and
\varepsilon>0
is chosen small enough so that
\|u_{0}\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq\varepsilon
. The parameters
\sigma
and
q
are selected to satisfy

\sigma=\frac{1}{m-1}-\frac{d}{2sq}>0\quad\hbox{and}\quad\frac{d(m-1)}{s}<2q<\frac{d(m-1)}{s}\frac{1}{(2-m)_{+}}.

(ii) Case
s\geq d(p-1)/(2p)
.

We now turn to the proof of the global existence result stated in Theorem
1.4
for the regime
s\geq d(p-1)/(2p)
. The argument follows the same approach as in
[
16
,
30
]
, based on a contraction mapping principle in a suitable complete metric space. We introduce the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}=\left\{u\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\;:\;\sup_{t>0}t_{s}^{\sigma}\|u(t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq M\varepsilon\right\},

where
t_{s}:=\max\{t^{s},t\}
, and
\sigma=1/(m-1)-d/(2sq)>0
. Here,
M>0
is a sufficiently large constant and
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
is chosen small enough so that

\|u_{0}\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq\varepsilon.

By
[
30
, Proposition 2.2]
,
it follows that
(\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon},d)
is a complete metric space endowed with the distance
d(u,v)=\sup_{t>0}t^{\sigma}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{q}.

For any
u\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
, we define the operator
\Phi
by

\Phi(u)=e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau.

The aim is to show that
\Phi
is a contraction map on
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
. We begin by showing that
\Phi
maps
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
into itself. Let
u\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
. By Proposition
1
, we have

\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq\varepsilon.

Next, we estimate the
L^{q}
-norm. Choosing

\sigma=\frac{d}{2s}\left(\frac{2s}{d(m-1)}-\frac{1}{q}\right)=\frac{1}{m-1}-\frac{d}{2sq}>0,

for
q>d(m-1)/(2s)\geq p
, and applying (
2.4
) together with Lemma
2.2
-(3), we obtain

t_{s}^{\sigma}\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\,\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{d(m-1)}{2s}}}\leq C\,\|u_{0}\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq C\varepsilon.

To estimate the second term of
\Phi(u)
in the space
\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we first consider the case
s=d(p-1)/(2p)
. Recall from (
3.4
) that

\|u\|_{L^{p}}+\|u\|_{L^{\phi}}\approx\|u\|_{\exp L^{p}},

where
\phi(u)=e^{|u|^{p}}-1-|u|^{p}
. Hence, it suffices to establish the following two estimates:

(5.2)

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}=O(\varepsilon),

and

(5.3)

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}=O(\varepsilon).

We now proceed with the proof of (
5.2
). Since

|f(u)|\leq C|u|^{m}e^{\lambda|u|^{p}}=C|u|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}|u|^{kp+m-1},

using (
2.4
) and Hölder’s inequality, with
1\leq r\leq p
, and
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{a}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sa}}\|f(u(\tau))\|_{r}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sa}}\|u(\tau)\|_{p}\|u(\tau)\|^{kp+m-1}_{{(kp+m-1)a}}\,d\tau,

Next, applying Hölder’s interpolation inequality together with Lemma
2.2
-(3), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sa}}\|u(\tau)\|_{p}\|u(\tau)\|^{(kp+m-1)\theta}_{q}\|u(\tau)\|^{(kp+m-1)(1-\theta)}_{\rho}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{\rho}{p}+1\right)\right)^{\frac{(kp+m-1)(1-\theta)}{\rho}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sa}}\tau_{s}^{-\sigma(kp+m-1)\theta}\left(\tau_{s}^{\sigma}\|u(\tau)\|\right)^{(kp+m-1)\theta}_{q}\|u(\tau)\|^{(kp+m-1)(1-\theta)+1}_{\exp L^{p}}\,d\tau,\qquad

where

(5.4)

\frac{1}{a(kp+m-1)}=\frac{\theta}{q}+\frac{1-\theta}{\rho},\quad 0\leq\theta\leq 1,\quad\hbox{and}\quad p\leq\rho<\infty.

Therefore, since
u\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{\rho}{p}+1\right)\right)^{\frac{(kp+m-1)(1-\theta)}{\rho}}(M\varepsilon)^{kp+m}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{d}{2sa}}\tau^{-\sigma(kp+m-1)\theta}\,d\tau

(5.5)

\displaystyle=C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{\rho}{p}+1\right)\right)^{\frac{(kp+m-1)(1-\theta)}{\rho}}(M\varepsilon)^{kp+m}\mathcal{B}\left(1-\frac{d}{2sa},1-\sigma(kp+m-1)\theta\right),

where we have used the fact that
t_{s}\geq t
for all
t>0
, and where
\mathcal{B}
denotes the beta function, under the following assumptions:

\frac{d}{2sa}<1,\qquad\sigma(kp+m-1)\theta<1,\quad\hbox{and}\quad 1-\frac{d}{2sa}-\sigma(kp+m-1)\theta=0.

It remains to establish the existence of the parameters
\theta=\theta_{k}
,
\rho=\rho_{k}
,
k\geq 0
, and
a
. Since
q>(m-1)p/(p-1)
, we can select the parameters as follows. First we choose

\frac{1-\frac{d(p-1)}{2sp}}{\sigma(pk+m-1)}<\theta_{k}<\frac{1}{pk+m-1}\min(m-1,\frac{1}{\sigma})=\frac{m-1}{pk+m-1},

since
\sigma=1/(m-1)-d/(2sq)<1/(m-1)
. The lower bound is imposed to ensure compatibility with the condition
r>1
. Next, we construct
\rho_{k}
. We require the relation
1-d/(2sa)-\sigma(kp+m-1)\theta_{k}=0
. Using the identity in (
5.4
), we choose
\rho_{k}
so that

\frac{1-\theta_{k}}{\rho_{k}}=\frac{2s}{d(kp+m-1)}-\frac{2s\theta_{k}}{d(m-1)}.

This in particular yields
\rho_{k}\geq d(m-1)/(2s)\geq p
. Finally, we fix
a>0
so that the remaining balance condition in (
5.4
) is satisfied, thereby completing the selection of parameters. With these choices, using the identity
\mathcal{B}(x,y)=\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)/\Gamma(x+y)
, for all
x,y>0
, we obtain

(5.6)

\mathcal{B}\left(1-\frac{d}{2sa},1-\sigma(kp+m-1)\theta\right)=\Gamma\left(1-\frac{d}{2sa}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2sa}\right)\leq C,

We also observe that
\theta_{k}\longrightarrow 0
and
\rho_{k}\longrightarrow\infty
as
k\rightarrow\infty,
which implies

\frac{(kp+m-1)(1-\theta_{k})}{p\rho_{k}}(1+\rho_{k})\leq k,\quad\hbox{for all}\,\,k\geq 1.

This, together with the estimate
\Gamma(x+1)\leq C\,x^{x+1/2}
for all
x\geq 1
, yields

(5.7)

\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{\rho_{k}}{p}+1\right)\right)^{\frac{(kp+m-1)(1-\theta_{k})}{\rho_{k}}}\leq C^{k}\,k!.

Combining (
5
), (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), we obtain

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau\right\|_{p}\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(C\,\lambda)^{k}(M\varepsilon)^{kp+m}\leq C(M\varepsilon)^{m},

for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. This concludes the proof of (
5.2
). We now turn to the proof of (
5.3
). Using the relation
s=d(p-1)/(2p)
along with Lemma
3.4
, and
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
, we deduce

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{\phi})}\leq C\|f(u(\tau))\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{1}\cap L^{2p}\cap L^{2p^{\prime}})}.

Arguing as in the case of
s<n(p-1)/(2p)
(see (
5.1
)), we deduce
\left\|f(u(t))\right\|_{r}\leq C(M\varepsilon)^{m}
, for
r=1,2p,2p^{\prime}
, and all
t>0
. This completes the proof of (
5.3
).

To estimate the second term of
\Phi(u)
in
\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the case
s>n(p-1)/(2p)
, let
b>0
be the positive number satisfying
b=2\ln(b+1)
. In fact,
2<b<3
and

2\ln(z+1)-z\geq 0,\qquad\hbox{for all}\,\,0\leq z\leq b.

Consequently, one verifies that

(5.8)

\left(\ln\left((t-\tau)_{s}^{-d/2s}+1\right)\right)^{-1/p}\leq 2^{1/p}(t-\tau)_{s}^{d/2sp},\quad\hbox{for all}\,\,0\leq\tau\leq t-b^{-2s/d}.

If
t\leq b^{-2s/d}
, then by Proposition
1
, for any
r\geq 1
, we obtain

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\left((t-\tau)_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sr}}+1\right)\left(\|f(u(\tau))\|_{r}+\|f(u(\tau))\|_{1}\right)\,d\tau.

Taking
r=p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
and using the assumption
s>d(p-1)/(2p)
, together with the inequality
(t-\tau)_{s}\geq(t-\tau)
, it follows that

(5.9)

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq C\|f(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{1}\cap L^{p^{\prime}})}.

Next, since
m>p
, and as in (
5.1
), one obtains
\|f(u(t))\|_{r}\leq C(M\varepsilon)^{m}
, for
r=1,p^{\prime}
, and all
t>0
. Therefore,

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}=O(\varepsilon).

For
t>b^{-2s/d}
, we split the integral into two parts

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t-b^{-2s/d}}\left\|e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{t-b^{-2s/d}}^{t}\left\|e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I+J.

In the same spirit as in (
5.9
), where the integration is performed over
[0,b^{-s/2d}]
, we argue similarly on the interval
[t-b^{-s/2d},t]
to obtain
J\leq C(M\varepsilon)^{m}.

On the other hand, using Proposition
1
-(2) together with (
5.8
), we estimate
I
as follows:

I\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2s}\left(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|f(u(\tau))\|_{a}\,d\tau,

where
1\leq a\leq p
, Applying the same argument used to derive (
5.2
) (under the same assumptions on
s
and
m
), we deduce that

I=O(\varepsilon).

Combining the estimates for
I
and
J
, we conclude that, for
t>b^{-2s/d}
,

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau\right\|_{\exp L^{p}}=O(\varepsilon).

Since the above bound is uniform in time, it follows that

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\exp L^{p})}=O(\varepsilon),\qquad\text{for all }t>0.

It remains to show that

t_{s}^{\sigma}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\,d\tau\right\|_{q}=O(\varepsilon),\qquad\text{for every }t>0,

which guarantees that
\Phi(u)\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
. This follows by an argument analogous to (
5.11
), together with the fact that
f(0)=0
.

We now proceed to prove that
\Phi
is a contraction on
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
. Let
u,v\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
. By (
2.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2s}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\|f(u(\tau))-f(v(\tau))\|_{r}\,d\tau,

for every
1\leq r\leq q
. Moreover, by invoking assumption (
1.6
) together with the power-series expansion of the exponential function, we obtain the pointwise estimate

|f(u)-f(v)|\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}|u-v|\left(|u|^{kp+m-1}+|v|^{kp+m-1}\right).

Applying Hölder’s inequality then yields

\|f(u)-f(v)\|_{r}\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\|u-v\|_{q}\left(\|u\|_{{a(kp+m-1)}}^{kp+m-1}+\|v\|_{{a(kp+m-1)}}^{kp+m-1}\right).

Next, we use Hölder interpolation to further estimate the norms, which gives

\displaystyle\|f(u)-f(v)\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\|u-v\|_{q}\Big(\|u\|_{q}^{(kp+m-1)\theta}\|u\|_{\rho}^{(kp+m-1)(1-\theta)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\|v\|_{q}^{(kp+m-1)\theta}\|v\|_{\rho}^{(kp+m-1)(1-\theta)}\Big),

where the parameters satisfy

\frac{1}{a(kp+m-1)}=\frac{\theta}{q}+\frac{1-\theta}{\rho},\qquad\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{a},

with
0\leq\theta\leq 1
. Consequently, using Lemma
2.2
-(3) and assuming that
p\leq\rho<\infty
, we infer that

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\left(\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\rho}{p}+1\right)\right)^{\frac{(kp+m-1)(1-\theta)}{\rho}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sa}}\tau_{s}^{-\sigma(1+(kp+m-1)\theta)}

\displaystyle\times\Big((\tau_{s}^{\sigma}\|u\|_{q})^{(kp+m-1)\theta}\|u\|_{\exp L^{p}}^{(kp+m-1)(1-\theta)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(\tau_{s}^{\sigma}\|v\|_{q})^{(kp+m-1)\theta}\|v\|_{\exp L^{p}}^{(kp+m-1)(1-\theta)}\Big)\,d\tau.

Since
u,v\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
, this further yields

(5.10)

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,d(u,v)\,(\varepsilon M)^{m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\varepsilon M)^{kp}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\left(\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\rho}{p}+1\right)\right)^{\frac{(kp+m-1)(1-\theta)}{\rho}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sa}}\tau_{s}^{-\sigma(1+(kp+m-1)\theta)}\,d\tau.

To estimate the last integral term on the right-hand side of (
5.10
), we use a Beta-function type argument. In particular, we have

\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)_{s}^{-\alpha}\tau_{s}^{-\beta}\,d\tau\leq t_{s}^{-\sigma}\mathcal{B}(1-\alpha,1-\beta),

where
\alpha=d/(2sa)
and
\beta=\sigma(1+(kp+m-1)\theta)
, provided that

1-\alpha-\beta=-\sigma
and
\alpha,\beta<1
.
Indeed, if
t\geq 1
, then

\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)_{s}^{-\alpha}\tau_{s}^{-\beta}\,d\tau\leq\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\alpha}\tau^{-\beta}\,d\tau=t^{-\sigma}\mathcal{B}(1-\alpha,1-\beta)=t_{s}^{-\sigma}\mathcal{B}(1-\alpha,1-\beta).

If
t\leq 1
, we similarly obtain

\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)_{s}^{-\alpha}\tau_{s}^{-\beta}\,d\tau=\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-s\alpha}\tau^{-s\beta}\,d\tau=t^{1-s\alpha-s\beta}\mathcal{B}(1-s\alpha,1-s\beta)\leq t_{s}^{-\sigma}\mathcal{B}(1-\alpha,1-\beta).

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle t_{s}^{\sigma}\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,d(u,v)\,(\varepsilon M)^{m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\varepsilon M)^{kp}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle\times\left(\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\rho}{p}+1\right)\right)^{\frac{(kp+m-1)(1-\theta)}{\rho}}\mathcal{B}(1-\alpha,1-\beta),

As in the previous argument, for each
k\geq 0
we first fix a parameter
\theta=\theta_{k}\geq 0
such that

\frac{1-\frac{d(q-1)}{2sq}}{\sigma(pk+m-1)}<\theta_{k}<\frac{1}{pk+m-1}\min\left\{m-1,\frac{1-\sigma}{\sigma}\right\},

where we have also used that
q>(m-1)p/(p-1)\geq m.

Next, we choose
\rho=\rho_{k}
satisfying

\frac{1-\theta_{k}}{\rho_{k}}=\frac{2s}{d(kp+m-1)}-\frac{2s\theta_{k}}{d(m-1)},

and we finally select
a>0
such that

\frac{1}{a(kp+m-1)}=\frac{\theta_{k}}{q}+\frac{1-\theta_{k}}{\rho_{k}}.

In order to guarantee that
\sigma<1
, we further assume
q<d(m-1)/(2s(2-m)_{+})
, where
(\cdot)_{+}
denotes the positive part.
With these choices of parameters, and arguing as in the previous case, the Beta-function term remains uniformly bounded, namely
\mathcal{B}\!\left(1-\alpha,\,1-\beta\right)=\Gamma\!\left(1-\alpha\right)\Gamma\!\left(1-\beta\right)/\Gamma\!\left(2-\alpha-\beta\right)\leq C
, and moreover,

\left(\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\rho_{k}}{p}+1\right)\right)^{\frac{(kp+m-1)(1-\theta_{k})}{\rho_{k}}}\leq C^{k}\,k!.

As a result, we arrive at

(5.11)

t_{s}^{\sigma}\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_{q}\leq C\,d(u,v)\,(M\varepsilon)^{m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(C\lambda)^{k}(M\varepsilon)^{kp}.

By choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, it follows that

t_{s}^{\sigma}\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_{q}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,d(u,v),

which shows that
\Phi
is a contraction on
\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
.
This completes the proof of the existence of a global solution in Theorem
1.4
in the case

s\geq d(p-1)/(2p)
. The estimate (
1.8
) follows directly from the fact that

u\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
.

We now turn to the proof of (
1.7
), namely the continuity of
u
at
t=0
in
\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
From the mild formulation, we have

\|u(t)-e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\|_{\exp L^{p}}\,d\tau.

Applying Lemma
2.2
-(1), we obtain

\|u(t)-e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\|_{\exp L^{p}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\|_{p}\,d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{-(t-\tau)\mathcal{L}}f(u(\tau))\|_{\infty}\,d\tau.

Let
q>\max\{d/(2s),1\}
.
Using (
2.4
), this yields

(5.12)

\displaystyle\|u(t)-e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\|_{\exp L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|f(u(\tau))\|_{p}\,d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)_{s}^{-\frac{d}{2sq}}\|f(u(\tau))\|_{q}\,d\tau.

For
r=p
or
r=q
, using

|f(u)|\leq C|u|^{m}e^{\lambda|u|^{p}}
together with Hölder’s inequality, we infer

\|f(u)\|_{r}\leq C\|u\|_{2mr}^{m}\|e^{\lambda|u|^{p}}-1\|_{2r}+C\|u\|_{mr}^{m}.

Since
2mr\geq mr\geq m\geq p
, Lemma
2.2
-(3) ensures that

\|u\|_{2mr}+\|u\|_{mr}\leq C\|u\|_{\exp L^{p}}.

Combining this with Lemma
3.2
and the fact that
u\in\mathcal{U}_{\varepsilon}
, we deduce

(5.13)

\|f(u)\|_{r}\leq C\|u\|_{\exp L^{p}}^{m}.

Substituting (
5.13
) into (
5.12
) and using that
u\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, we obtain

\|u(t)-e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\|_{\exp L^{p}}\leq Ct\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\exp L^{p})}^{m}+Ct^{1-\frac{d}{2sq}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\exp L^{p})}^{m},

which completes the proof of continuity at
t=0
.

To conclude the proof of Theorem
1.4
, it remains to verify that
u(t)\rightarrow u_{0}

in
\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
t\to 0,
in the weak
∗
sense. Let
X=L^{1}(\ln L)^{1/p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the pre-dual space of

\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
It is well known that
X
is a Banach space and that
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
X

(see
[
1
]
). For any
\varphi\in X
, using the duality between
\exp L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
X
, together with (
2.3
) and Hölder’s inequality in Orlicz spaces, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigl(e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(x)\bigr)\varphi(x)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{0}(x)\bigl(e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi(x)-\varphi(x)\bigr)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{\exp L^{p}}\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi-\varphi\|_{X}.

Since
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
X
and the semigroup

e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
is strongly continuous on
X

(see the argument in the proof of Proposition
2
), it follows that

\lim_{t\to 0}\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\varphi-\varphi\|_{X}=0.

Consequently,

\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigl(e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}-u_{0}\bigr)\varphi\,dx=0\qquad\text{for all }\varphi\in X,

which establishes the weak
∗
convergence. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

\square
