A Comprehensive Semi-Analytical Model for Predicting Gas–Water Two-Phase Productivity of Fractured Horizontal Wells in Deep Coalbed Methane Reservoirs
Authors: Pin Jia, Haozhe Dong, Gaojiaxiang Zhang, Cong Peng, Langyu Niu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The development of deep coalbed methane is becoming increasingly important in the energy sector. Faced with deep coal seam conditions, there is a lack of a comprehensive productivity evaluation model that considers the triple media of matrix, cleat fractures, and hydraulic fractures, as well as matrix shrinkage and stress sensitivity. This paper analyzes the geological characteristics and flow mechanisms of coalbed methane (CBM), including two-dimensional flow in cleat fractures, considering stress sensitivity and matrix shrinkage, and one-dimensional flow in hydraulic fractures, also considering stress sensitivity. A comprehensive CBM productivity evaluation model is established that incorporates the complexities of dynamic fracture permeability and matrix shrinkage. The model is based on the interactions among hydraulic fractures, cleat fractures, and matrix flow. The triple-medium-flow model is solved by coupling analytical solutions for matrix flow and cleat fracture flow with numerical solutions for hydraulic fracture flow. Through the analysis of typical wells, the influence and controlling factors of geological parameters and development factors on deep CBM productivity are elucidated. The results demonstrate that the proposed CBM productivity evaluation model effectively explains the seepage characteristics of triple media under complex conditions. The daily gas production at the current time can be determined on the basis of the average formation pressure and average gas saturation at a certain moment. For example, when the average pressure of deep coal seams is 26 MPa, the average gas saturation is 0.6, and the stress sensitivity index of cleat fractures increases from 0.1 to 0.5, the gas production rate decreases by 61%. When the stress sensitivity coefficient of hydraulic fractures increases from 0.02 to 0.08, the peak gas production rate of the inflow performance relationship (IPR) curve decreases by 49%. The model can be used to predict the total capacity of a certain production stage and the capacity of each fracturing cluster and provide guidance for the next step of production. The model proposed in this paper considers the complex fracture network morphology under the triple-medium condition of hydraulic fracture-cleat fractures-matrix, and the focus is on distinguishing the different effects of deep coal seam cleat fractures and hydraulic fractures on production, which enhances the understanding of the stress-sensitive mechanism under deep conditions and provides a basis for the study of coalbed methane productivity changes, controlling factors, and efficient coalbed methane exploitation at the production site.

Introduction

Despite the abundant deep coalbed methane (CBM) resources in China, which account for a significant proportion of overall CBM resources, deep CBM differs from shallow CBM because of its high reservoir pressure, poor permeability, and high gas content. Research indicates that deep CBM reservoir properties are influenced by stress sensitivity. As development progresses, the pressure within the coal reservoir decreases, leading to a decrease in permeability due to stress sensitivity. Given the low permeability of deep coal reservoirs, horizontal well-staged fracturing technology is essential for effective gas mobilization. The flow of deep CBM is governed by a triple medium comprising the hydraulic fracture network, coal cleats, and coal matrix, making the productivity evaluation of fractured CBM wells challenging.

Currently, the primary methods for assessing deep CBM productivity, considering complex fracture networks, include analytical, semi-analytical, and numerical simulation methods. Analytical methods for calculating the bottom-hole pressure and production of fractured horizontal wells date back to the 1960s. However, due to the complex structure of coal reservoirs and unique seepage mechanisms, there has been limited research on using analytical methods to evaluate CBM well productivity both domestically and internationally. Airey established the first partial differential equation predicting CBM release using an analytical method to solve it and creating a one-dimensional, single-porosity, gas-phase production prediction model. Bumb and McKee incorporated the effects of desorption processes on CBM well productivity into the gas compressibility factor, deriving productivity during the single-phase gas flow stage, validated against numerical models. Lu developed hydraulic models for vertical and horizontal wellbores, formulating methods for calculating gas production from fractured wells. Liu et al. , developed productivity formulas for the single-phase water flow and gas–water two-phase flow stages of CBM wells. Cong et al. utilized parallel plate theory to research the inflow performance relationship (IPR) model of CBM wells. Despite their utility, analytical methods often struggle to account for the complexity of fracture networks and interference between fractures, leading to significant errors.

The semianalytical method combines the derivation and solution of complex formulas with the superposition of discrete elements. Larsen and Hegre used image response theory and the semianalytical method to solve for bottom-hole pressure of a fractured horizontal well under infinite reservoir boundary conditions. Subsequent researchers , used Green’s functions and source-sink functions to solve for bottom-hole pressure and flow rate in multistage fractured horizontal wells. Many studies have employed semianalytical methods to investigate the seepage models of fractured horizontal wells with infinite or finite conductivity. Zhou et al. used the semianalytical method to study transient well-test dynamics of volumetrically fractured wells. Chen et al. developed a model reflecting fracture morphology using the semianalytical method, discretizing fractures into multiple elements and using point source functions and the superposition principle to obtain pressure solutions for each fracture element. Zhong et al. derived a semianalytical model for CBM wells under linear flow, while Jiang et al. developed a model for multibranch horizontal wells in dual-porosity composite CBM reservoirs, considering permeability stress sensitivity. Liu et al. derived a semianalytical solution for pressure distribution in CBM reservoirs considering low water production. Semianalytical methods can account for fracture interference but require inversion to real space using the Stehfest algorithm. Most semianalytical methods currently used do not adequately consider the complexities of fracture networks and stress sensitivity under matrix shrinkage and fracture closure.

Numerical methods are also widely used to study transient pressure dynamics of multistage fractured horizontal wells. Herge created a high-conductivity small grid near the wellbore for numerical simulation of bottom-hole pressure dynamics. Wan et al. established a three-dimensional fracture model, improving simulation accuracy. Al-Kobaisi et al. developed a numerical model for transient pressure response in fractured horizontal wells. Subsequent researchers − used numerical methods to solve for transient pressure in complex fracture networks and conducted sensitivity analyses on related parameters. However, numerical models often require extensive data, are slow to construct, and lack accurate descriptions of fractures and horizontal wellbores, resulting in less accurate productivity predictions and evaluations.

Existing semi-analytical productivity models for CBM reservoirs have provided valuable tools for evaluating fractured horizontal wells. However, most available models focus on only part of the governing mechanisms. Some models describe gas–water two-phase flow in dual-porosity systems but neglect the explicit contribution of hydraulic fractures and complex fracture networks. Other studies incorporate hydraulic fractures but assume constant permeability and ignore the effects of matrix shrinkage and stress-sensitive fracture closure under deep reservoir conditions. Furthermore, the coupled flow behavior among the matrix, cleat fractures, and hydraulic fractures is rarely considered within a unified framework.

To address these limitations, this study develops a comprehensive semi-analytical productivity model for deep CBM reservoirs. The main novelties of the proposed model are summarized as follows:A triple-medium flow system consisting of matrix, cleat fractures, and hydraulic fractures is established, and the fluid exchange among different media is coupled through source–sink terms.Dynamic permeability evolution caused by matrix shrinkage and stress-sensitive closure of both cleat fractures and hydraulic fractures is incorporated, enabling the model to characterize deep reservoir conditions more realistically.A complex fracture network morphology generated by multistage hydraulic fracturing is represented explicitly, allowing the productivity contribution of individual fracture clusters and fracture-network configurations to be evaluated.A unified semianalytical framework is developed to predict gas and water productivity, inflow performance relationships (IPR), and production contributions from different fracture clusters with high computational efficiency.

Physical Model

Through the analysis of deep coalbed methane (CBM) characteristics, it is essential to consider specific geological and seepage properties, including the reservoir features and the stress sensitivity of hydraulic and cleat fractures. Deep CBM is characterized by low permeability and high stress sensitivity under high pressure and stress conditions, which significantly influence development methods. Therefore, the seepage behavior of hydraulic and cleat fractures must account for permeability changes induced by stress sensitivity features. Figure shows the coalbed methane horizontal well after fracturing.

Schematic diagram of a fractured horizontal well in a coal reservoir.

Figure shows that the primary structure of the original coal reservoir consists of two systems: the coal matrix and cleat fractures within the matrix. Cleat fractures are divided into two main types: face and butt cleats. The cleat fractures in the original coalbed methane (CBM) reservoir store a significant amount of water and serve as crucial flow channels for the gas and water phases. The coal matrix surface adsorbs a substantial amount of CBM. Within the coal reservoir, gas and water exhibit two-phase seepage, but the water content in the matrix is minimal and can be disregarded. The naturally developed cleat fractures in CBM reservoirs have low permeability, making gas and water flow relatively difficult. Consequently, hydraulic fracturing is commonly performed on coal reservoirs to create a complex network of hydraulic fractures, enhancing the flow capacity of gas and water channels and ultimately improving the recovery rate.

Schematic diagram of the coalbed methane desorption-diffusion-seepage model.

Coalbed methane migrates from the matrix to the cleat fractures through desorption and diffusion, then flows through the cleat fractures in a two-dimensional manner to the hydraulic fracture network, and finally reaches the horizontal wellbore in a one-dimensional seepage manner. Similarly, coal fracture water migrates from the cleat fractures to the hydraulic fracture network in a two-dimensional manner and finally reaches the horizontal wellbore in a one-dimensional manner. This continuous process ensures that the source term of the matrix becomes the sink term of the cleat fractures, and the sink term of the cleat fractures becomes the source term of the hydraulic fractures. In the subsequent solving process, the relationship between the source and sink terms of each medium is used to couple and solve the flow equations. Mathematical Model

In this section, according to the assumptions of the established physical model, the gas–water flow in the three regions of matrix-cleat fracture-hydraulic fracture is further clarified. On the basis of the principle of material balance, and considering the dynamic permeability caused by stress sensitivity, a mathematical model of gas–water two-phase flow in a triple medium is constructed.

Stress Sensitivity of Cleat Fracture and Hydraulic Fracture

Some studies − have shown that the stress sensitivity of coal reservoirs show that the permeability of the reservoir decreases exponentially with the increase of effective stress of coal fractures. Under conditions of large effective stress, the seepage channel of the coal fracture becomes narrow or even closed, resulting in a sharp decrease in permeability. Aiming at the coupling problem of permeability and effective stress of fractures with different occurrences, Chen et al. established a permeability model considering both the fracture compression coefficient and effective stress anisotropy. This model uses complex polynomial and exponential models for the cleat fractures and hydraulic fractures, respectively.

(1) Cleat fracture stress sensitivity.

Because the cleat fracture is a gas–water channel, in the cleat fracture system, gas and water are mainly two-dimensional seepage, mainly in the x and y directions. It is worth noting that, on the basis of high pressure and high stress in deep coalbed methane, it is necessary to comprehensively consider the change in cleat fracture permeability caused by stress sensitivity in coal fractures. (2) Hydraulic fracture stress sensitivity:

The conditions of deep coal reservoirs are complex, and relying solely on cleat fractures with low permeability yields minimal gas production, lacking industrial development value. Consequently, hydraulic fracturing is essential for enhancing the conductivity of the near-wellbore zone and increasing gas production. The hydraulic fracture network created by fracturing comprises multiple segments and clusters, with each hydraulic fracture oriented differently. Gas and water within this network exhibit one-dimensional flow, moving along the direction of the distribution of the fracture network.

Gas–Water Two-Phase Flow Equation of Hydraulic Fracture

The hydraulic fractures formed by the fracturing process contain proppant. As the pressure within the hydraulic fracture system decreases, these fractures can close, reducing their conductivity, which is referred to as the stress sensitivity of the hydraulic fractures. Therefore, when constructing the model, it is essential to consider the permeability changes due to pressure variations. In the hydraulic fractures, both gas and water phases flow together, necessitating consideration of the changes in relative permeability caused by gas saturation. The gas and water phases follow Darcy’s Law in the hydraulic fractures. The hydraulic fracture area must account for the gas–water two-phase flow from the cleat fracture area into the hydraulic fracture area, as well as the gas–water two-phase flow from the hydraulic fracture area into the wellbore. Thus, both the gas–water two-phase flow from the cleat fractures, acting as the source term, and the gas–water two-phase flow into the wellbore, acting as the sink term, need to be incorporated into the model.

Semianalytical Solution

The traditional analytical method cannot accurately solve the complex situation in multiple media. Although the numerical simulation method can be solved, its solution process is complex, and progress is slow. The semianalytical solution method used in this paper can quickly and accurately solve the gas and water production of fractured horizontal wells in deep coalbed methane reservoirs, and its advantages are obvious.

The discrete method is used to solve the hydraulic fracture by numerical flow, and the analytical solution is used to solve the cleat fracture and the matrix. Finally, the gas and water production are obtained by coupling solution.

Numerical Flow Solution of Hydraulic Fracture

The hydraulic fracture area was solved by numerical flow. On the basis of the finite volume method, the hydraulic fracture is discretized to obtain n fracture elements. The internal pressure of each element is consistent, but the pressure between each element and its adjacent elements is different. The fracture microelement relies on the pressure difference to carry out gas–water two-phase seepage, and there is conductivity between each fracture microelement and two adjacent microelements. For the formed complex fracture network, the intersection was processed by the star-triangle transformation method.

The Basic Principle and Application of Star Triangle Transformation Method

In order to eliminate the intersection unit generated by the intersection of fracture microelements, this paper uses the star-triangle transformation method shown in Figure to process. This method utilizes the similarity principle between the seepage flow in porous media and the current in a circuit network. The fracture grids are directly adjacent through the intersection unit, and the flow state of the formation fluid at the fracture intersection can be directly determined by the pressure and conductivity of the fracture grids, without the information on the intersection unit. King used this method to calculate the effective permeability of reservoirs in heterogeneous reservoirs for the first time. Karimi et al. applied it to the numerical simulation of discrete fracture flow and eliminated the control volume formed by the fracture intersection.

Results and Discussion

Analysis of Productivity

The productivity of deep coalbed methane reservoirs is closely related to geological characteristics, fluid parameters, and working systems. Therefore, this paper uses the constructed semianalytical model to analyze the above influencing factors, following Dou et al. The following results are based on assumption 5 of the model, which is the gas production rate at a specific time point. The sensitivity analysis of this study was conducted on the basis of actual production well cases, and the average reservoir pressure and average gas saturation were based on the reasonable production range of the actual production wells. The discussion on fracture permeability and drainage capacity is based on the reasonable range of actual fracturing in this block, while the stress sensitivity coefficient is based on actual coal samples from the production area.

Influence of Productivity

(1) Influence of Hydraulic Fracture Stress Sensitivity Coefficient

Under the influence of different stress sensitivity coefficients of hydraulic fractures, the gas production rate will also be different. Figure shows that with the increase of stress sensitivity coefficient, the gas production rate decreased in varying degrees. When C F < 0.6 MPa–1, the gas production decreased quickly. When C F > 0.6 MPa–1, the gas production decreased slowly. Under the same stress sensitivity coefficient, different average reservoir pressure has a great influence on the gas production rate. When p ave is 21 MPa, the influence of the stress sensitivity coefficient of hydraulic fractures on the gas production rate is not obvious; only a decrease of 2 × 104m3/d can be seen. When p ave is 26 MPa, the influence of the stress sensitivity coefficient of hydraulic fractures on the gas production rate is obvious. A decrease of 16 × 104m3/d can be seen. In actual production, better proppants can be used during fracturing to reduce fracture closure caused by stress sensitivity, thereby increasing gas production.

Gas production rate under different stress sensitivity coefficients of hydraulic fractures.

(2) Influence of Cleat Fracture Stress-Sensitivity Coefficient

Under the influence of different stress sensitivity coefficients of cleat fractures, the gas production rate will also be different. Figure shows that with the increase of the stress sensitivity coefficient, the gas production rate decreased in varying degrees, and the gas production rate decreased rapidly in the early stage and decreased slowly in the later stage. Under the same stress sensitivity coefficient, the gas production rate increases with the increase of average reservoir pressure. When the average reservoir pressure is low, the influence of the hydraulic fracture stress sensitivity coefficient on the gas production rate is not significant, showing only a decrease of 1 × 104m3/d. When the average reservoir pressure is high, the influence of the hydraulic fracture stress sensitivity coefficient on the gas production rate becomes pronounced, showing a decrease of 9 × 104m3/d. The higher the average reservoir pressure, the greater the influence range of the cleat fracture stress sensitivity coefficient on the gas production rate. When p ave is 21 MPa, the cleat fracture stress sensitivity coefficient reaches 0.3, and the gas production rate becomes 0. When p ave is 26 MPa, the cleat fracture stress sensitivity coefficient reaches 0.9, and the gas production rate becomes 0.

Gas production rate under different stress sensitivity coefficients of cleat fractures.

(3) Influence of Hydraulic Fracture Conductivity

Figure shows that with the increase of hydraulic fracture conductivity, the gas production rate increases to varying degrees. When p ave is 26 MPa and the conductivity kw F < 200 μm2 ·cm, the gas production rate increased rapidly, up to 15 × 104m3/d. When the conductivity kw F > 200 μm2·cm, the gas production rate increases slowly. Finally, the gas production rate only increased to 25 × 104m3/d. When the conductivity of the hydraulic fracture is small, it has a great influence on gas production, but when it exceeds a certain value, the gas production rate stabilizes at a certain yield, indicating that its influence on gas production is not significant at this time. Therefore, the diversion capacity can be stabilized at a certain value, and it is not necessary to excessively pursue an increase in diversion capacity; otherwise, the benefit of output may not be able to make up for the cost of input.

Gas production rate under different hydraulic fracture conductivity.

(4) Influence of cleat fracture permeability.

Figure shows that with the increase in cleat fracture permeability, the gas production rate also increases. When p ave is 26 MPa and the cleat fracture permeability k f < 0.2 μm2, the trend of the gas production rate with the increase of cleat fracture permeability is gradually gentle, up to 17.5 × 104m3/d. When the cleat fracture permeability k f > 0.2 μm2, gas production hardly increases with the increase of cleat fracture permeability. Under the same cleat fracture permeability, as the average reservoir pressure increases, the gas production rate also increases. When p ave is 21 MPa, the maximum gas production rate can reach 2 × 104m3/d. When p ave is 26 MPa, the maximum gas production rate can reach 17.5 × 104m3/d. Therefore, the better the degree of cleat fractures formed by the natural development of coal reservoirs, the higher the permeability and the greater the gas production rate in the later development.

Gas production rate of different cleat permeability.

Influence of Inflow Performance Relationship Curve (IPR)

(1) IPR curves of different stress-sensitivity coefficients.

It can be seen from Figure that different fracture stress sensitivity coefficients have an effect on the maximum gas production rate. The larger the fracture stress sensitivity coefficient, the smaller the maximum gas production rate. This phenomenon is particularly obvious when the hydraulic fracture stress sensitivity coefficient changes. It can be observed that the stress sensitivity coefficient of hydraulic fractures changes relatively little, whereas the change in daily gas production is relatively large. The change in joint fractures also affects daily gas production, but its impact is not as significant as that of hydraulic fractures. Therefore, in the production process, the main consideration is artificial hydraulic fractures.

(2) IPR curves of different production stages.

The model predicts productivity at a specific time point, but in different production stages, the average pressures and average gas saturations of typical wells will also change. Therefore, the productivity of typical wells in different production stages is studied by setting different average pressures and different gas saturations. Figure shows the IPR curve at different production stages.

IPR curves of different production stages. (a) Different average formation pressures. (b) Different average gas saturations.

The analysis of the following diagram shows that the influence of average pressure on the maximum gas production rate is similar to the stress sensitivity of cleat cracks, but the influence of average pressure is greater. As the bottom-hole pressure decreases, the difference between the gas production rates at two different average pressures becomes larger and larger. The lowest production is 4.8 × 104m3/d, and the highest production can reach 9.5 × 104m3/d. The difference between the two is 4.7 × 104m3/d.

It can be seen that the impact of average gas saturation on the productivity trend is similar to the average pressure. However, compared with the maximum and minimum gas production, the average gas saturation has a greater impact. The minimum yield is 2.2 × 104m3/d, and the maximum yield can reach 13.5 × 104m3/d. The difference between the two is 11.3 × 104m3/d.

During the production process, the productivity of typical wells can be predicted based on this chart and adjusted in time.

Conclusions

In this paper, a triple-medium model for deep coalbed methane, considering the complex environment and dual stress sensitivity of cleats and hydraulic fractures, as well as the impact of matrix shrinkage, was established. The model integrates the coal matrix, cleat fractures, and hydraulic fractures, with each medium solved using analytical and numerical methods to predict coalbed methane productivity under deep, complex conditions. The productivity charts produced include IPR curves for various conditions, gas production rates across different development stages, and the effects of stress sensitivity coefficients and seepage capacities. Key conclusions and insights are as follows:This production evaluation model is applicable to steady-state flow conditions and can predict the coalbed methane reservoir under a given instantaneous state. It can predict the production at multiple pressures and obtain the production that varies with the pressure during the production process. However, such predictions do not take into account the influence of gas–water two-phase systems and the changes under material balance. To achieve a more comprehensive prediction, future work can introduce time-dependent factors into the model to assess the production performance that changes over time.Through the coupling matrix method, the productivity evaluation model established in this paper can be solved in one step according to the characteristics of deep CBM multimedia, which greatly speeds up the solving speed.The model allows for monitoring and evaluation of each fracturing section of a well. It is identified that the production supply varies along the wellbore, with end sections typically supplying more than the middle. This is beneficial for accurately locating the hydraulic fracture section with low production, so as to provide a basis for improving this section.The productivity map obtained by using this model can be used to evaluate the productivity of typical wells in deep, complex formation conditions. It accurately identifies the main controlling factor that has the greatest impact on production capacity among multiple factors. You can understand the impact trend and the specific quantity of each influencing factor on production capacity. For example, under certain conditions, as the diversion capacity of hydraulic fractures increases, the trend of daily gas production first increases rapidly and then slowly increases. When p ave is 26 MPa and the conductivity kw F = 500 μm2 ·cm, the gas production rate increased rapidly, up to 20 × 104m3/d.
