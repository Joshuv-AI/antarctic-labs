ABSTRACT: Modernizing the manufacturing of high-performance polymer foams such as amino-poly(oxadiazole) bis-
maleimide (APO-BMI), a bismaleimide resin with superior thermal and compressive strength that incorporates additive
manufacturing (AM) techniques, is crucial for its applications, but the parameters for AM can be challenging based on the
physical properties of the associated monomer. For our applications, selective laser sintering (SLS) is typically used. SLS is
a 3D printing technique that allows for complex shapes and geometries without structural supports while also providing
high resolution material. However, printing thermosets like APO-BMI with SLS is challenging due to the complex melting
and curing considerations required when selecting parameters. Additionally, the temperature difference between melting
and curing of APO-BMI is over a hundred ºC, which makes selecting a sintering window especially difficult. This work
explores structural modifications of APO-BMI that may be more amenable for selective laser sintering. The effects of how
different structural changes such as substitution pattern, heteroatom identity in the bridge, and bridge length affect the
thermal properties of the material were also compared. All APO variants were found to have a smaller temperature window
between the melting and curing peaks based on differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) which is advantageous for SLS.
Small structural changes significantly altered the melting and curing properties of APO. Additionally, DSC revealed signif-
icant polymorphisms in APO-BMI and other APO variants which could be attributed to differences in thermal history and
would need to be considered when adapting for SLS.

Currently, APO-BMI production occurs at an off-site pro-
duction facility (Kansas City Nuclear Security Complex,
KCNSC) while small batch synthesis occurs at Los Alamos
National Lab (LANL). APO-BMI polymorphisms can occur
due to various differing factors in synthesis and purifica-
tion procedures. Polymorphisms are undesirable for SLS as
they significantly broaden the melting curve which makes
selecting a sintering window more difficult. To explore po-
tential polymorphisms in APO-BMI we compared samples
from both facilities and the effects of establishing different
thermal history.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The 10 APO variants depicted in Figure 1 were synthesized,
and the thermal properties of 1-2 and 4-10 were compared.
Variants with optimized synthesis were scaled up so that
parts could be molded.
One goal of this work was to determine how changing the
bridge or the substitution pattern of APO variants affects
its thermal properties. Interestingly, every APO variant has
a smaller gap between the melt peak and the cure peak
compared to APO-BMI (Figure 2). The red box in Figure 2
highlights how the temperature window changes for two
APO variants that differ by substitution pattern. The blue
boxes in Figure 2 highlight how the temperature window
changes for three APO variants that differ by heteroatom
in the bridge.
Changing either the substitution pattern, bridge rigidity,
or heteroatom in the bridge all resulted in smaller gaps be-
tween melting and curing. This highlights not only how
impactful small structural changes are on thermal proper-
ties, but also how uniquely suited APO-BMI is for molding
with its large gap between melting and curing.
