PSPACE-Hardness of Existential Presburger Arithmetic with Divisibility
Ignacio Barros, Michaël Cadilhac, Guillermo A. Pérez

Abstract
We study satisfiability for existential Presburger arithmetic extended with divisibility predicates, EPAD. This problem has long been known to be 𝐍𝐏 {\mathbf{NP}} -hard and has often been expected to lie in 𝐍𝐏 {\mathbf{NP}} . We prove that it is 𝐏𝐒𝐏𝐀𝐂𝐄 {\mathbf{PSPACE}} -hard. We define functional EPAD, 𝐅𝐄𝐏𝐀𝐃 {\mathbf{FEPAD}} , using EPAD formulas with Boolean free variables and one distinguished output variable, required to define a total function of the Boolean inputs. We then introduce truncate–shift arithmetic circuits ( TSAC s). For each input length n n , a TSAC has n n Boolean input gates and one output gate. Each Boolean assignment to the input gates determines a nonnegative integer at the output gate. Its arithmetic gates are addition, multiplication, truncation modulo powers of two, and binary left and right shifts. The class 𝐓𝐒𝐀𝐂 {\mathbf{TSAC}} of functions computed by uniform polynomial-size TSAC s is shown to be exactly 𝐅𝐏𝐒𝐏𝐀𝐂𝐄 {\mathbf{FPSPACE}} , with the convention that the write-only output tape is not counted in the space bound. Then, the inclusion 𝐓𝐒𝐀𝐂 ⊆ 𝐅𝐄𝐏𝐀𝐃 {\mathbf{TSAC}}\subseteq{\mathbf{FEPAD}} is proved by evaluating TSAC s gate by gate in EPAD: addition is affine, multiplication uses an EPAD product formula, powers of two use positive homogeneous divisibility systems, and shifts and truncations use quotient–remainder decompositions. Applying this to characteristic functions of 𝐏𝐒𝐏𝐀𝐂𝐄 {\mathbf{PSPACE}} languages gives the 𝐏𝐒𝐏𝐀𝐂𝐄 {\mathbf{PSPACE}} -hardness of EPAD formula satisfiability. We also analyze a normalization step for divisibility atoms. When the surrounding side conditions force the quotient in an atom L | M L\mid M to belong to a finite set, the atom can be replaced by finitely many equations M = c ​ L M=cL . We introduce a polynomial-time recognizable fragment, called merge-absorptive, in which these replacements can be iterated until no divisibility atoms remain, yet satisfiability is already 𝐏𝐒𝐏𝐀𝐂𝐄 {\mathbf{PSPACE}} -hard on this fragment. This identifies a barrier in improving the complexity of Lipshitz’s original decision procedure for EPAD: the replacement steps can produce large coefficients. The same systems used to evaluate TSAC s yield such equations: a polynomial-size family forces relations r = ( 2 2 j − 1 ) ​ x r=(2^{2^{j}}-1)x , and the replacement steps produce equations with coefficients of bit length Θ ⁡ ( 2 j ) \Theta(2^{j}) .

1
Introduction

Existential Presburger arithmetic is
\mathbf{NP}
-complete
[
15
]
. Adding
divisibility predicates gives existential Presburger arithmetic with
divisibility, or EPAD, and allows multiplication-like constraints inside linear
arithmetic. A divisibility atom
L\mid M
relates two affine-linear
terms by requiring
M=qL
for some integer multiplier
q
. Such atoms
remain elementary number-theoretic constraints, but they can express relations
not available in ordinary linear arithmetic.

The decidability of the arithmetic of addition and divisibility goes back to
work of Lipshitz
[
26
]
and, independently,
Beltyukov
[
1
]
. The precise complexity of the existential
fragment has remained open. Lechner et al.
[
22
]

point out a folklore belief that EPAD formula satisfiability is in
{\mathbf{NP}}
,
citing assertions in Bundala and
Ouaknine
[
6
, Sec. 2.1]
and Haase’s
thesis
[
14
, Thm. 2.6.3]
, both of which cite
Lipshitz
[
27
]
as source. We show that EPAD formula
satisfiability is
{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
-hard.

Function classes.

An EPAD formula with Boolean free
variables and a distinguished output variable defines a function when, for every
Boolean input, there is a unique output satisfying the formula. We call such a
formula a functional EPAD formula, and we write
{\mathbf{FEPAD}}
for the resulting
class of functions. We also use truncate–shift arithmetic circuits
(
TSAC
s) as
a model for integer-valued functions. For each input length
n
, a
circuit
C_{n}
has Boolean input gates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
and one
output gate. Assigning values in
\{0,1\}
to the input gates
determines a value for every gate, and hence a function

\{0,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{N}
. The arithmetic gates are addition,
multiplication, truncation modulo powers of two, and binary shifts. We
write
{\mathbf{TSAC}}
for the class of functions computed by
polynomial-time uniform families of polynomial-size
TSAC
s.

We prove
{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}={\mathbf{TSAC}}\subseteq{\mathbf{FEPAD}}
. Here
{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
is the
standard space-bounded function class: the output tape is write-only and is not
counted in the space bound. To show
{\mathbf{TSAC}}\subseteq{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
, a
polynomial-space machine writes the binary expansion of the circuit value
sequentially. It first computes a polynomial-bit length bound for the output
gate, then scans the output positions and computes each requested bit by a
recursive evaluation procedure. For the converse, let
B(\boldsymbol{x},i)
be the
predicate saying that the
i
-th bit of an
{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
output on input
\boldsymbol{x}

is
1
. The construction builds one
TSAC
output whose binary
expansion contains all values of
B(\boldsymbol{x},i)
. For every intermediate Boolean
predicate, it keeps two truth tables encoded as integers, one for the
satisfying assignments and one for the falsifying assignments. Boolean
connectives and quantifiers are implemented by pointwise products of truth
tables and sums over disjoint cases, so all truth-table entries remain
0
or

1
.

The equality
{\mathbf{TSAC}}={\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
also gives a simple robustness principle.
A fixed operation may be added as a gate when its binary output length grows at
most linearly and its bit graph is in
{\mathbf{NL}}
.
This covers bitwise AND, OR, and XOR, as well as integer division.

The inclusion
{\mathbf{TSAC}}\subseteq{\mathbf{FEPAD}}
is proved by translating

TSAC
s into functional EPAD formulas. Addition is affine.
Multiplication is enforced by an EPAD formula that forces a variable to
be the product of two earlier values. Exact multiplication by powers of
two is supplied by polynomial-size divisibility systems, and right shifts
and truncations are expressed by ordinary quotient–remainder
decompositions. If
L\in{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
, then the characteristic function

\chi_{L}
lies in
{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
. Hence
\chi_{L}
has a functional EPAD
formula that computes
\chi_{L}
. Substituting a concrete input and
requiring the output to be
1
gives an EPAD formula satisfiable
exactly on inputs in
L
. This gives the
{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
-hardness of EPAD
formula satisfiability.

Normalization.

The lower bound also holds for a fragment, called
merge-absorptive, whose divisibility atoms can be removed by repeatedly applying a
finite-quotient replacement step. This step applies to an atom
L\mid M
when
the current linear constraints force every nonzero quotient
M/L
to belong
to a finite set, and the atom is then replaced by the corresponding finite
family of equations
M=cL
. There is an equisatisfiable reduction from
EPAD formula satisfiability to this fragment, so the
{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
lower bound
also holds for a class whose divisibility atoms can be eliminated in this way.

Lipshitz’s decidability proof repeatedly applies finite-quotient replacements
before reaching a divisibility-free normal form
[
26
]
, and
satisfiability of the final normal forms is in

{\mathbf{NP}}

[
11
]
. The same

{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
-hard systems inside
merge-absorptive
show that this replacement step
can result in coefficients of exponential bit length. Thus finite-quotient
replacement can produce large equations before the final normal form is
reached.

Contributions.

•

We define
{\mathbf{FEPAD}}
, the class of functions computed by
functional EPAD formulas with Boolean free variables and one output variable.

•

We introduce
TSAC
s, a nonnegative arithmetic circuit model
with addition, multiplication, truncations, and binary left and right shifts.

•

We prove that the corresponding function class
{\mathbf{TSAC}}
is exactly

{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
.

•

We show that EPAD evaluates
TSAC
s gate by gate, and therefore

{\mathbf{TSAC}}\subseteq{\mathbf{FEPAD}}
. This gives
{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
-hardness of EPAD
formula satisfiability.

•

We define the merge-absorptive fragment, prove that it is polynomial-time
recognizable and reducible by repeated finite-quotient replacement steps to
systems with no remaining divisibility atoms, and show that satisfiability for
this fragment is already
{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
-hard.

•

We exhibit polynomial-size merge-absorptive systems forcing

r=(2^{2^{j}}-1)x
, and show that finite-quotient replacement
produces equations with coefficients of bit length
\Theta(2^{j})
.

The function-theoretic part of the reduction is

{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}={\mathbf{TSAC}}\subseteq{\mathbf{FEPAD}}
,
and the hardness consequence is obtained by applying this to characteristic
functions of
{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
languages. The later reduction to merge-absorptive is
satisfiability-preserving: every EPAD instance can be transformed in
polynomial time into an equisatisfiable merge-absorptive system.

EPAD in the literature.

EPAD also appears as a tool in
verification and automata-theoretic applications. Presburger arithmetic is
standard in formal methods for counter-based systems, model checking, and
related verification problems
[
15
]
. Bozga and Iosif introduced
decidable fragments of arithmetic with divisibility motivated by quantitative
reasoning about memory shapes
[
3
]
. Lechner connected related
fragments to one-counter automata and synthesis
problems
[
23
,
24
]
, a line later revisited
by Pérez and Raha
[
33
]
. Another source of interest comes
from string constraints: Lin and Majumdar used EPAD in the analysis of quadratic
word equations with length constraints
[
25
]
, followed by
recent work on string solving, word equations with counting constraints, and
length
abstractions
[
7
,
8
,
10
]
.
Combining their reduction with our lower bound gives the following consequence:
nonerasing satisfiability for positive Boolean combinations of word equations
and quantifier-free Presburger length constraints over the fixed alphabet

\{a,b\}
is
{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
-hard (see
\Cref
app:word-equations).

Related computation models.

Packed computation over large
integers has a long history in unit-cost RAM and vector-machine models. In that
setting, polynomial-time computations over unbounded registers with arithmetic
and shift operations capture
{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
, with shifts serving as a way to
manipulate packed data inside large
words
[
16
,
34
,
35
,
2
,
38
,
4
]
.
These are machine models with control flow, tests, register addressing, and
often Boolean operations on words.
TSAC
s use a more restricted
format: the circuits have polynomial size, the truncation and shift amounts are
binary constants in the circuit description, and the gates are addition,
multiplication, truncations modulo powers of two, and shifts.

Closer in format are circuit-like descriptions of polynomial-space computation.
Cook and Soltys’s Boolean programs are straight-line definitions of Boolean
functions with arguments, where later functions may call earlier ones. They
characterize
{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
, and Skelley compares this presentation with quantified
Boolean formulas
[
9
,
37
]
. Galota and Vollmer
characterize
{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
using arithmetic circuits, straight-line programs, and
matrix programs, but the arithmetic descriptions have exponential size in the
input length
[
13
]
. Integer circuits over sets of natural
numbers give another
{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
-complete evaluation setting, with gates
computing sets rather than integer
values
[
39
,
30
]
. The
TSAC
model used here
is instead a polynomial-size arithmetic circuit model whose gates compute
nonnegative integers.

Other compressed-integer and word-level models are close at the level of
operations. Power circuits represent very large integers by DAGs and support
operations such as addition, comparison, and
x\cdot 2^{y}
on compressed
values
[
31
,
29
]
. Their operation set is
different from that of
TSAC
: power circuits have variable exponent shifts,
while
TSAC
has only constant shifts but allows arbitrary multiplication and
truncation modulo powers of two as circuit gates. Arithmetic circuits with
truncation study how floor or truncation operations affect arithmetic
computation, for instance in fast
exponentiation
[
5
]
. Fixed-size bit-vector
logics with binary-encoded widths provide a logical counterpart in which
succinct word sizes and shifts raise the complexity of satisfiability
problems
[
19
,
12
,
20
,
18
]
.

Paper organization.

The next section gives the basic
definitions. The following section proves
{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}={\mathbf{TSAC}}
, with one
subsection for each inclusion. The second inclusion represents truth tables of
Boolean functions by binary expansions of integers. We then translate

TSAC
s into EPAD. The last technical section proves the lower bound for
the merge-absorptive fragment and the coefficient blow-up caused by finite-quotient
replacements.

2
Preliminaries

All integers in inputs are binary encoded, and input size includes coefficient
bit length. An affine-linear term has the form
c+\sum_{i}a_{i}x_{i}
, with integer
coefficients. A homogeneous linear form is the special case with no constant
term.

Definition 1

(EPAD formula satisfiability)
.

An EPAD formula is an existential first-order formula over integer variables
using Boolean combinations of affine-linear equalities, affine-linear
inequalities, and divisibility atoms
L\mid M
, where
L
and
M
are
affine-linear integer terms. EPAD formula satisfiability asks, given an EPAD
formula, whether it is satisfiable.

A divisibility atom
L\mid M
means that there exists
q\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

M=qL
. Thus
0\mid 0
is true and
0\mid M
is false for
M\neq 0
.

Example 2

(A small EPAD system)
.

The following EPAD formula already forces a large multiplier. Take

0<x<y<r,\quad 3r\mid 2r+3y,\quad y+x\mid r+3y,

together with

x\mid y,\quad y-x\mid r+y,\quad y-2x\mid r,\quad y-3x\mid r-y.

This is the
t=2
instance of the scaling systems in

\Cref
lem:mersenne-multiplier. Systems of this kind are one source of the
large-solution phenomena studied by Defossez et
al.
[
11
]
. The constraints force

y=5x
and
r=15x
. Adding the affine equality
v=r+x
then gives

v=16x
. Thus divisibility atoms can describe large fixed multiples
without writing those multiples as coefficients.
\triangleleft

Definition 3

(Functional EPAD)
.

An
n
-input functional EPAD formula is an EPAD formula

\Phi_{n}(\boldsymbol{x},y)\equiv\exists\boldsymbol{z}\,\theta_{n}(\boldsymbol{x},y,\boldsymbol{z}),

where
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
are Boolean inputs and
y
is the output
variable. The formula is evaluated on assignments

\boldsymbol{a}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. A family
(\Phi_{n})_{n}
computes

f_{n}\colon\{0,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{N}
if, for every
\boldsymbol{a}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, there is a unique

with
\Phi_{n}(\boldsymbol{a},y)
, and this value is
f_{n}(\boldsymbol{a})
. The class

{\mathbf{FEPAD}}
consists of the functions computed by polynomial-time uniform
families of functional EPAD formulas.

The constructions below often use positive integer variables with linear side
conditions. In the EPAD instances themselves, these are ordinary Presburger
constraints over
\mathbb{Z}
.

We use an arithmetic circuit model over nonnegative integers. A
truncate–shift arithmetic circuit, abbreviated
TSAC
, is a finite acyclic
circuit with Boolean input gates, binary constants, addition
u+v
,
multiplication
uv
, truncations
\operatorname{trunc}_{d}(u)=u\bmod 2^{d}
, binary left shifts

\operatorname{lshift}_{s}(u)=2^{s}u
, and binary right shifts

\operatorname{rshift}_{e}(u)=\lfloor u/2^{e}\rfloor
. The nonnegative integers
d,s,e
are
binary parameters in the circuit description. They are not values produced by
gates. All gate values are nonnegative.

An
n
-input
TSAC
has distinguished Boolean input gates

x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
and one output gate. For an input assignment

\boldsymbol{a}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
and a gate
g
, let
\operatorname{Val}(C,\boldsymbol{a},g)
be the

g
under
\boldsymbol{a}
. If
o

\operatorname{Val}(C,\boldsymbol{a})=\operatorname{Val}(C,\boldsymbol{a},o)
. Thus a circuit
C
with
n

inputs denotes a function
\{0,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{N}
.

A polynomial-time uniform family
(C_{n})_{n}
, where
C_{n}
has
n
Boolean
input gates, computes the function
f_{n}\colon\{0,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{N}
given by

f_{n}(\boldsymbol{a})=\operatorname{Val}(C_{n},\boldsymbol{a})
. We write
{\mathbf{TSAC}}
for the class of functions
computed by such families when the circuits have polynomial size. In the proof
of
{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}\subseteq{\mathbf{TSAC}}
, binary expansions of gate values are used
to encode truth tables of Boolean functions.

Definition 5

(
{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
functions)
.

A family
f_{n}\colon\{0,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{N}
is in
{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
if it is computed by a
deterministic Turing machine using polynomial space on its work tapes, with a
one-way write-only output tape whose cells are not counted in the space bound.
The output word is interpreted as a binary integer. Leading zeros are
immaterial. Throughout the paper, bit position
i
means the coefficient of

2^{i}
, with position
0
the least significant bit. Length bounds are
positive upper bounds on the number of bits needed to represent a value.

Equivalently, there is a polynomial
p
and a
{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
predicate
B
such
that, for every
\boldsymbol{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
,
f_{n}(\boldsymbol{x})<2^{2^{p(n)}}
, and,
for every
0\leq i<2^{p(n)}
,
B(\boldsymbol{x},i)=1
iff the
i
-th bit of

f_{n}(\boldsymbol{x})
is
1
, where
i
is given in binary.

This bit formulation is the one used by Ladner
for polynomial-space computable functions
[
21
]
. We recall why it
agrees with the output-tape definition. In one direction, before halting, a
deterministic polynomial-space machine cannot repeat a configuration consisting
of its state, input-head positions, and work tapes. The output tape is
write-only, so such a repetition would make the computation loop. Hence the
machine writes at most exponentially many symbols. In the other direction, a
machine can write the output by scanning the binary addresses
i
and
computing
B(\boldsymbol{x},i)
for each of them.

3
Truncate–Shift Arithmetic Circuits and FPSPACE

This section proves
{\mathbf{TSAC}}={\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
. In this equality, a
TSAC

family
(C_{n})_{n}
computes a function by evaluating
C_{n}
on its
n

Boolean input gates and reading the integer at the output gate. The two
subsections prove the two inclusions separately.

3.1
From Truncate–Shift Arithmetic Circuits to FPSPACE

Proposition 6

(Truncate–shift arithmetic circuits yield

{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
)
.

Every polynomial-time uniform family of polynomial-size
TSAC
s
computes a function in
{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
.

Proof.

Let
(C_{n})_{n}
be such a family, where
C_{n}
has
n
Boolean input gates.
On input
\boldsymbol{x}
, a polynomial-space machine constructs
C_{|\boldsymbol{x}|}
and
computes binary length bounds
L_{g}
for all gate values in topological
order: inputs have bound
1
, constants use their binary length, addition
uses
\max(L_{u},L_{v})+1
, multiplication uses
L_{u}+L_{v}
, left shifts use

L_{u}+s
, right shifts use
L_{u}
, and truncations use
\max(1,d)
.
Here
s,e,d
denote the numeric parameters, not their bit lengths. Let
R

be the maximum of
1
, the binary lengths of constants, and the numeric values
of the shift offsets and truncation widths appearing in
C_{n}
. The parameters are written in binary, so
R
may be exponential in
n
, but
\log R
is polynomial.
A depth-
h
gate has value length at most
2^{h}R
; hence every bound

L_{g}
has polynomially many bits.

The machine then writes the value of the output gate in binary, from most
significant to least significant bit. For each requested position
i
, it
computes the
i
-th bit of a gate by the following recursive procedure

\operatorname{BIT}(g,i)
. The procedure returns
0
if
i<0
or
i\geq L_{g}
. Inputs and constants are read directly. A left shift changes the
request to
i-s
, a right shift changes it to
e+i
, and a truncation
returns
0
when
i\geq d
. Along a recursion stack, right-shift offsets are
added at most once per gate, so every requested address has polynomially many
bits.

For addition, the procedure scans columns
0,\ldots,i
with the usual one-bit
carry. For multiplication, it computes the convolution column

w_{t}=\sum_{j=0}^{t}\operatorname{BIT}(u,j)\operatorname{BIT}(v,t-j)

sequentially and propagates the carry. Here
w_{t}\leq t+1
. If
c_{t}
is
the carry entering column
t
, with
c_{0}=0
, then

c_{t+1}=\lfloor(c_{t}+w_{t})/2\rfloor
; induction gives
c_{t}\leq t
.
Thus the carry uses
O(\log(t+2))
bits. Each stack frame stores a gate
identifier, polynomial-bit addresses and counters, and one carry or running sum.
The stack depth is at most the number of gates, hence polynomial.

The output loop uses a polynomial-bit counter, and each invocation of

\operatorname{BIT}
uses polynomial space. The output tape is write-only and
is not counted in the space bound. Therefore the function computed by

(C_{n})_{n}
lies in
{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
.
∎

3.2
From FPSPACE to Truncate–Shift Arithmetic Circuits

Let
V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
be a set of Boolean variables. Put

\lambda_{i}=5^{i-1},\qquad C(\boldsymbol{a})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\lambda_{i}\quad(\boldsymbol{a}\in\{0,1\}^{k}),\qquad\Lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}.

The number
C(\boldsymbol{a})
is the position assigned to the Boolean assignment
\boldsymbol{a}
,
before multiplication by a scaling factor. The truth table of a Boolean
function
F\colon\{0,1\}^{k}\to\{0,1\}
is encoded, at scale
R
, by the
integer

\langle F\rangle_{R}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{a}\in\{0,1\}^{k}}F(\boldsymbol{a})2^{RC(\boldsymbol{a})}.

Thus
F(\boldsymbol{a})
is stored as the binary digit at position
RC(\boldsymbol{a})
. All other
positions are unused. The powers
1,5,5^{2},\ldots
are used because sums of
two such positions can be read one variable at a time: if
\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b}\in\{0,1\}^{k}
,
then each base-
5
digit of
C(\boldsymbol{a})+C(\boldsymbol{b})
is one of
0,1,2
, with no carries
between digits.

Example 7

(A packed truth table)
.

Let
V=\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
. Then
\lambda_{1}=1
,
\lambda_{2}=5
, and

(v_{1},v_{2})

Corollary 14

(Safe additional gates)
.

Let
h\colon\mathbb{N}^{k}\to\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that the binary length of

h(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})
is bounded linearly in the total binary length of

u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
. Suppose also that the bit graph

\{(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k},i):\text{the $i$-th bit of }h(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})\text{ is }1\}

is in
{\mathbf{NL}}
, with numbers written in binary and leading zeros ignored.
Then adding

as a gate type does not change
{\mathbf{TSAC}}
. This applies, for example, to bitwise AND, OR, and XOR, and to
integer division
[
17
]
.

Proof.

The length condition ensures that, throughout a polynomial-size extended

TSAC
, every gate value still has exponentially many bits in the number of
Boolean inputs. To compute one bit of an
h
-gate, simulate the
{\mathbf{NL}}

machine for the bit graph on the virtual binary encodings of the predecessor
values and of the requested bit position, allowing leading zero padding. These
encodings may be exponentially long, but the simulated input-head positions use
only logarithmic space in their length, hence polynomial space in the original
input length. Whenever the simulated machine reads a bit of a predecessor
value, compute that bit recursively from the predecessor gate. The recursion
depth is at most the circuit size, so the whole bit computation remains in
polynomial space.
Thus every extended circuit family computes an
{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
function. By

\Cref

y_{t}=(2^{2^{t-(t-1)}}-1)y_{t-1}
.

Now consider a non-base stage

, and assume the invariant

y_{t}=(2^{2^{t-i}}-1)y_{i}
. Put
B=2^{2^{t-i}}
. We show that the

y_{i}=(B+1)y_{i-1}
. From
y_{i-1}\mid y_{i}
, write

y_{i}=qy_{i-1}
, with
q\geq 2
. The atom

y_{i}-y_{i-1}\mid y_{t}+y_{i}
gives
q-1\mid Bq
. Since
\gcd(q-1,q)=1
, this
yields
q-1\mid B
, so
q=2^{s}+1
for some
s\geq 0
. The case
s=0
is
impossible because
y_{i}-2y_{i-1}=0
would have to divide the positive number

y_{t}
. The atom
y_{i}-2y_{i-1}\mid y_{t}
then gives

2^{s}-1\mid 2^{2^{t-i}}-1
, because
2^{s}-1
is coprime to

q=2^{s}+1
. By
\Cref
lem:mersenne,
s\mid 2^{t-i}
, so
s=2^{a}

for some
0\leq a\leq t-i
, and
q=2^{2^{a}}+1
.

It remains to eliminate
a<t-i
. Put
h=t-i
. If
a\leq D_{h}
, the atom
with
\ell=a
has zero left-hand side and positive right-hand side, which is
impossible. So assume

. We use the following consequence of the
exclusion atoms. Fix
0\leq\ell<a
. Under
q=2^{2^{a}}+1
, the atom with
index
\ell
implies
a-\ell\mid h-a
. Indeed, the standard Fermat-number
coprimality argument shows that
2^{2^{a}}-2^{2^{\ell}}
is coprime to

2^{2^{a}}+1
: any common odd prime would make the order of
2
modulo that
prime both
2^{a+1}
and a divisor of
2^{a}-2^{\ell}
. Using this coprimality,
the atom with index
\ell
implies

2^{2^{a}}-2^{2^{\ell}}\mid 2^{2^{h}}-2^{2^{\ell}}
. Cancelling the common factor

2^{2^{\ell}}
gives

2^{2^{a}-2^{\ell}}-1\mid 2^{2^{h}-2^{\ell}}-1
. By
\Cref
lem:mersenne,

2^{a}-2^{\ell}\mid 2^{h}-2^{\ell}
. Dividing by
2^{\ell}
gives

2^{a-\ell}-1\mid 2^{h-\ell}-1
, and applying
\Cref
lem:mersenne again gives

a-\ell\mid h-\ell
, hence
a-\ell\mid h-a
.

Apply this to every
0\leq\ell\leq D_{h}
. Then every integer in the interval

a-D_{h},\ldots,a
divides the positive integer
h-a
, so the lcm of this
interval is at most
h-a<h
. Since
a<h
and
a>D_{h}
, the cap in

D_{h}=\min\{m_{h},h-1\}
is not active, so
D_{h}=m_{h}
. This contradicts

\Cref
lem:lcm-interval, which makes the lcm at least
2^{m_{h}+1}/(m_{h}+2)>h
.
Hence
a=t-i
, and
q=2^{2^{t-i}}+1=B+1
.

The invariant updates to

y_{t}=(B-1)y_{i}=(B^{2}-1)y_{i-1}=(2^{2^{t-i+1}}-1)y_{i-1}
.
After the last stage
i=1
, we get
y_{t}=(2^{2^{t}}-1)y_{0}=(2^{2^{t}}-1)x
.

Completeness and size.

Conversely, fix any
x=y_{0}>0
and set

y_{i}=(2^{2^{t-i}}+1)y_{i-1}
for
1\leq i\leq t
.
The Fermat product identity
\prod_{r=0}^{m}(2^{2^{r}}+1)=2^{2^{m+1}}-1
shows
that, before stage
i
,
y_{t}=(2^{2^{t-i}}-1)y_{i}
. With this assignment the
base atom has quotient
1
, and each atom in a non-base stage
i
has
quotient
2^{2^{t-i}}+1
. The side conditions hold because every ratio is
greater than
1
. Hence every positive

extends to a
solution, and the projection is exactly the required set of pairs.

Finally, adding the equality

gives projection

. The number of constraints and the bit length of
all written coefficients are polynomial in

, since
D_{h}=O(\log h)
and

2^{D_{h}}=O(h\log h)
.
∎

Lemma 18

(Succinct power scaling)
.

Given
e\geq 0
in binary, there are positive homogeneous divisibility systems,
of size polynomial in
\log(e+2)
, whose
(u,v)
-projections are,
respectively,

\{(u,2^{e}u):u\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\},\qquad\{(u,(2^{e}+1)u):u\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\}.

Proof.

For
e=0
, the first projection is enforced by the equality
v=u
,
written as
0\mid v-u
. The second projection is enforced by the equality

v=2u
, written as
0\mid v-2u
.

Suppose
e>0
. Let
J
be the set of bit positions at which
e
has
value
1
, so
e=\sum\nolimits_{j\in J}2^{j}
. Enumerate

J=\{j_{0},\ldots,j_{m-1}\}
, in any order. We construct variables

u=z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}=v
. For each
\ell=0,\ldots,m-1
, add the side
condition
z_{\ell}<z_{\ell+1}
, introduce an intermediate variable

r_{\ell}
, use
\Cref
lem:mersenne-multiplier to force

r_{\ell}=(2^{2^{j_{\ell}}}-1)z_{\ell}
, and add the homogeneous equality

z_{\ell+1}=r_{\ell}+z_{\ell}
. It follows that

z_{\ell+1}=2^{2^{j_{\ell}}}z_{\ell}
. By induction over
\ell

projection to

is exactly

.

For the second projection, first construct a system with variables

, and then add the equality

. The projection is
therefore exactly

.
∎

Example 19

(Scaling by

4.4
The EPAD Translation

We translate
TSAC
s into functional EPAD formulas. The construction
evaluates the circuit over nonnegative integers. Sums are affine, products use
the two divisibility atoms from
\Cref
lem:signed-product, left shifts use exact
multiplication by powers of two, and right shifts and truncations use
quotient–remainder decompositions.

Theorem 26

(EPAD evaluates truncate–shift arithmetic circuits)
.

Every
n
-input
TSAC

C
has a polynomial-time constructible
functional EPAD formula
\operatorname{Eval}_{C}(\boldsymbol{x},y)
defining the
function
\boldsymbol{a}\mapsto\operatorname{Val}(C,\boldsymbol{a})
.

Proof.

Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
be the inputs of
C
, and let
o
be its output gate.
For every Boolean input
\boldsymbol{a}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
and every
y\in\mathbb{N}
, the intended
formula satisfies
\operatorname{Eval}_{C}(\boldsymbol{a},y)
iff
y=\operatorname{Val}(C,\boldsymbol{a},o)
.

Compute the binary length bounds
L_{g}
as in
\Cref
prop:tsac-pspace-upper.
For each gate
g
, introduce a nonnegative variable
Y_{g}
and a fresh bound
variable
M_{g}
. Add
\operatorname{Scale}_{L_{g}}(1,M_{g})
and
0\leq Y_{g}<M_{g}
. Thus

M_{g}
is forced to the fixed value
2^{L_{g}}
, without writing this power of
two as a coefficient; the scaling formula is polynomial-size because
L_{g}
is
available in binary with polynomially many bits. For every Boolean input
variable
x_{i}
, add
0\leq x_{i}\leq 1
, and set the corresponding gate variable
equal to
x_{i}
. Constant gates are affine equalities.

The formula contains the following constraints for the gates.

•

addition gates use the affine equality
Y_{g}=Y_{u}+Y_{v}
.

•

multiplication gates use the product formula of

\Cref
lem:signed-product.

•

left-shift gates use exact power-of-two scaling
\operatorname{Scale}_{s}(Y_{u},Y_{g})
.

•

right-shift gates use the quotient–remainder formula with the given
offset.

•

truncation gates use the quotient–remainder formula with offset
0
.

For a product gate
g=uv
, apply
\Cref
lem:signed-product with
U=Y_{u}
,

V=Y_{v}
, and
W=Y_{g}
. The represented bounds give

|Y_{u}|<2^{L_{u}}
and
|Y_{g}|<2^{L_{g}}
. Choose the separating exponent

\lambda=\max(L_{u},L_{g})+2
, so

2^{\lambda}>2^{L_{u}}+2^{L_{g}}+1
. This exponent has polynomial bit length
because the bounds
L_{u},L_{g}
do.

For a right-shift gate
\operatorname{rshift}_{e}(u)
, use
\Cref
lem:epad-block-extraction
with width
L_{g}
, which is large enough not to discard any bit of the shifted
value. For a truncation gate
\operatorname{trunc}_{d}(u)
, use the same lemma with offset

0
and width
d
. Finally add
y=Y_{o}
. Induction over the circuit
forces every
Y_{g}
to be the intended nonnegative gate value, and every actual
circuit evaluation extends to the auxiliary variables of the product and
block-extraction formulas. The auxiliary witnesses need not be unique;
functionality only requires uniqueness of the distinguished output variable

y
, which follows from the uniqueness of all gate values.
∎

(Truncate–shift arithmetic circuits yield
{\mathbf{FEPAD}}
)
.

Every function computed by a polynomial-time uniform family of polynomial-size

Proof.

Apply
\Cref
thm:tsac-epad-evaluation to each circuit in the uniform family. The

The theorem gives uniqueness of the output variable.

Theorem 28
.

EPAD formula satisfiability is
{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
-hard under polynomial-time many-one
reductions.

Proof.

Let
L\in{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
. Its characteristic function
\chi_{L}
is in
{\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
.
By
\Cref
thm:fpspace-tsac,cor:tsac-fepad, there is a polynomial-time uniform
family of functional EPAD formulas
\Phi_{n}(\boldsymbol{x},y)
computing

\chi_{L}
. Given
an input
\boldsymbol{a}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, substitute the Boolean constants from

\boldsymbol{a}
for the free variables and add the affine constraint

.
The resulting EPAD formula
\exists y\,(\Phi_{n}(\boldsymbol{a},y)\wedge y=1)

5
Coefficient Blow-Up in Normalization

Finite-quotient simplification can be viewed as a normalization search tree. A
branch is a node labeled by affine equations
\mathcal{E}
, a Presburger side
condition
\Phi
, and the divisibility atoms that remain to be simplified.
Affine forms on a branch are compared modulo
\mathcal{E}
, so the notation
does not depend on a particular order of substitutions.

At a branch, a finite-quotient replacement chooses an atom
L\mid M
and a
finite set of possible quotients, creating one child for each quotient. A child
closes if the new equation is inconsistent with the current side conditions.
Otherwise, the equation is added to the branch and may make further quotients
finite. Seen from this perspective,
\Cref
thm:epad-pspace-hard rules out a
polynomial-size normalization to branches with polynomial-time verifiable
certificates, unless
{\mathbf{NP}}={\mathbf{PSPACE}}
. The last subsection identifies a concrete
barrier: writing the relation forced by the scaling gadgets as
finite-quotient replacement equations can require coefficients with
exponentially many bits.

We now tighten the hardness result from the previous section, which proves

{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
-hardness for unrestricted EPAD. We define a restricted class in
which finite-quotient simplification removes all divisibility atoms. The
construction below transforms every EPAD instance into an equisatisfiable
merge-absorptive system.

5.1
Finite-Quotient Normalization

During normalization, a branch consists of the affine equations and Presburger
side conditions accumulated so far, together with the divisibility atoms that
remain to be simplified. A child branch is obtained by adding one equation and
closes if that equation is inconsistent with the current side conditions.

Definition 29

(Finite-quotient replacement)
.

For an atom
L\mid M
on a branch, a finite-quotient replacement with quotient
set
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is valid if the constraints present on the
current branch, including the remaining divisibility atoms, together with

L\mid M
force either
L=M=0
, or
L\neq 0
and
M/L\in\mathcal{Q}
.
It creates one child for each equation
M=cL
, with
c\in\mathcal{Q}
.
In the normalization procedures below, the finite quotient sets are computed
from the current branch constraints.

The case
L=M=0
satisfies every child equation. When we need a primitive
equation for a child, we obtain it from
M-cL=0
by dividing the integer
coefficients by their greatest common divisor. A finite-quotient
simplification is a sequence of finite-quotient replacements. A system reduces
to divisibility-free branches if such a sequence leaves every branch either
closed or without remaining divisibility atoms.

A normalization procedure that repeatedly applies such replacements forms a
search tree. If every satisfiable EPAD instance had a polynomial-size tree of
branches with polynomial-time verifiable leaves, then

\Cref
thm:epad-pspace-hard would imply
{\mathbf{NP}}={\mathbf{PSPACE}}

bound at the end of this section shows where large coefficients already arise
in the systems from

\Cref
lem:mersenne-multiplier,lem:power-scaling.

5.2

We next define the polynomial-time recognizable fragment used by the restricted
lower bound. We work with positive homogeneous divisibility systems of the kind
used in the systems from
\Cref

The support of a linear form is the set of variables with nonzero coefficient.
A form is supported on

Proposition 34

(Merge-absorptive recognition and normalization)
.

For positive homogeneous divisibility systems,
merge-absorptive
recognition is
decidable in polynomial time, and every
merge-absorptive
system reduces to
divisibility-free branches.

Proof.

For a component
C=\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\}
, positivity and nonvanishing of a form

F
are linear-programming tests. Fix the scale by adding

z_{1}+\cdots+z_{r}=1
, which selects one representative from each positive
scaling class. Test strict feasibility by adding a common slack
\sigma>0

and requiring every strict side condition supported on
C
, as well as every
constraint
z_{i}>0
, to hold with margin
\sigma
. Homogeneous equalities
are kept as equalities. Then
F
is nonvanishing iff
F=0
is infeasible,
and positive iff
F\leq 0
is infeasible. Homogeneity lets rational feasible
points be scaled to positive integer valuations, so the rational and integer
tests agree.

The side-condition boundedness test in the third merge condition is also a
linear-programming test: for each variable
y
in the support of
R_{B}
, add

\sum_{x\in A}x=1
and the side conditions supported on
A\cup B
, then
check whether
y
has finite supremum over the resulting polyhedron.

The recognizer uses union-find for the current partition. It repeatedly scans
all atoms; when an atom mentions exactly two current components, it tests the
three merge conditions in both orientations and unions the components after a
successful test. If a scan performs no union, the fixed point has been reached:
accept if one component remains, and reject otherwise. There are at most
linearly many successful unions, and each scan uses polynomially many
linear-programming tests.

The greedy merge procedure is complete because merge eligibility is monotone
under coarsening. Positivity and nonvanishing persist when components are
merged: admissible valuations of the larger component project to a subset of the
old admissible valuations. Side-condition boundedness is monotone for the same
reason. If
y\leq M\sum_{x\in A}x
, then after enlarging
A
the same
valuation satisfies
y\leq M\sum_{x\in A^{\prime}}x
, and added side conditions only
restrict the feasible set. Thus performing one available merge never destroys
another available merge.

To reach divisibility-free branches, maintain the invariant that, on every
branch not closed by inconsistency, all variables in each current component are
rational multiples of one positive representative for that component. This is
clear for singleton
components. A merge atom of the first kind gives a nontrivial linear equation
between the two representatives, and either closes the branch or expresses one
representative as a rational multiple of the other.

For the second kind, write on the current branch
P_{A}=pt_{A}
,
R_{B}=rt_{B}
,
and
Q_{A}=qt_{A}
. Positivity of
P_{A}+R_{B}
gives
p,r\neq 0
with the same
sign, and nonvanishing of
Q_{A}
gives
q\neq 0
. Then the quotient

Q_{A}/(P_{A}+R_{B})
has absolute value at most
|q/p|
. Hence there are only
finitely many possible integer quotients
c
, and this finite set is obtained
by listing the integers in that interval. Branching over them and adding

c(P_{A}+R_{B})=Q_{A}
either closes the branch or gives a nontrivial linear
equation between
t_{A}
and
t_{B}
, thereby preserving the rational-multiple
invariant after the two components are merged.
This is the first merge of
\Cref
ex:merge-sequence, where
c=2
and the added
equality is
a=2b
.

For the third kind, write
P_{A}=pt_{A}
,
T_{A}=st_{A}
, and
R_{B}=rt_{B}
, with

p,r\neq 0
. Side-condition boundedness gives a constant
M
such that

R_{B}
is bounded in absolute value by a fixed rational multiple of
Mt_{A}

on the current branch. Hence
(T_{A}+R_{B})/P_{A}
has only finitely many possible
integer values; the relevant finite set is computed from this rational bound.
Branching over these values again either closes the branch or adds a nontrivial
linear equation between

and
t_{B}
, so the merged component again has
one positive representative. This is the second merge of
\Cref
ex:merge-sequence,
where the bound gives

and the added equality is

.

After all successful merges, every branch not closed by inconsistency has one
component. Every remaining atom is then of the form

\lambda t\mid\mu t
for rational constants
\lambda,\mu
, with
t>0

on the branch. Cancellation by

is legitimate. Although
\lambda

\mu
may be rational after solving branch equations, the original forms are
integer-valued on integer solutions, so the atom is equivalent on the branch to
a tautology, to an inconsistent

with
\mu\neq 0
, or to the
constant divisibility test

. Thus the branch reaches
either inconsistency or an empty divisibility system.
∎

Proposition 35

(EPAD to affine divisibility)
.

Every EPAD formula has a polynomial-time equisatisfiable translation to affine
divisibility systems over integer variables.

Proof.

Put the quantifier-free matrix in negation normal form. Negated affine atoms
become positive Boolean combinations of affine equalities and strict
affine inequalities. Negated divisibility atoms can also be rewritten locally following
[
22
, Sec. IV.A]
. Thus, after adding only polynomially many arithmetic variables, the formula is a
positive Boolean combination of affine equalities, strict affine inequalities,
and unnegated affine divisibility atoms.

We first bound all arithmetic variables. We use the following effective
small-solution consequence for existential linear arithmetic with divisibility

[
22
]
: after Boolean structure has been put in
positive form, for conjunctions of affine equalities, strict affine
inequalities, and affine divisibility atoms over integer variables, every
satisfiable instance of size
n
has a satisfying assignment whose variables
have bit length at most
2^{p(n)}
, for a computable polynomial
p
. The
divisibility convention is
L\mid M
iff
M=qL
for some
q\in\mathbb{Z}
; if
a cited statement uses a nonzero-divisor convention, the case
L=M=0
is
separated by a Presburger disjunct before applying the bound. Introduce a bound variable
B
and
force
B=2^{2^{p(n)}+1}
by
\operatorname{Scale}_{2^{p(n)}+1}(1,B)
. Add

-B<x_{i}<B
for every original and auxiliary arithmetic variable. For every
affine term and every product variable introduced below, add a similar
represented power-of-two bound. The exponents have polynomial bit length, so
all scaling systems are polynomial-size conjunctions of affine divisibility
constraints and strict affine inequalities.

It remains to remove the Boolean structure. Give each subformula
\varphi
a
Boolean variable
b_{\varphi}
, constrained by the strict inequalities

b_{\varphi}+1>0
and
2-b_{\varphi}>0
. For conjunctions and disjunctions,
introduce a product variable
p=b_{\psi}b_{\theta}
, enforced by the signed
product formula of
\Cref
lem:signed-product, and add
b_{\psi\wedge\theta}=p

or
b_{\psi\vee\theta}=b_{\psi}+b_{\theta}-p
. For an atomic formula
\alpha
,
it is enough to enforce
b_{\alpha}=1\Rightarrow\alpha
. Because the syntax
tree is positive and the variable of the whole matrix is forced to be
1
,
these one-way implications force a satisfying assignment for the original
formula; reverse implications are unnecessary for equisatisfiability. If

\alpha
is
h=0
, introduce
T_{\alpha}=b_{\alpha}h
and require
T_{\alpha}=0
. If
\alpha
is
h>0
, introduce

T_{\alpha}=b_{\alpha}h
and require
T_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}+1>0
. If

\alpha
is
L\mid M

and

M_{\alpha}=b_{\alpha}M
, and require
L_{\alpha}\mid M_{\alpha}
. Each such
product is again implemented by

\Cref
lem:signed-product. Finally require the variable of the whole matrix to
be
1
.

If the original EPAD formula is satisfiable, choose a bounded satisfying
assignment, set each Boolean variable to the truth value of its subformula, and
interpret the product variables as the intended products. All constraints above
are then satisfied. Conversely, a satisfying assignment of the
affine-divisibility conjunction makes the Boolean output
1
. Since every
atom marked true is actually true, the original positive Boolean matrix is true
under the arithmetic assignment. Hence the construction is equisatisfiable.
∎

The fragment used in the next proposition is homogeneous. Constants in the

Proposition 36

(EPAD to merge-absorptive systems)
.

Every EPAD formula has a polynomial-time equisatisfiable translation to

merge-absorptive
positive homogeneous divisibility systems.

Proof.

By
\Cref
prop:epad-affdivz, reduce an affine divisibility system over integer
variables. Replace each

, with

. Write

as

, and

Write the atoms of

as

[
22

We first check that the constructed system is merge-absorptive. By

\mathcal{I}
has a
satisfying assignment

with

6
Conclusion

We introduced truncate–shift arithmetic circuits (
TSAC
s), as
arithmetic circuits with Boolean inputs and one nonnegative integer output. The
main characterization is
{\mathbf{TSAC}}={\mathbf{FPSPACE}}
: a polynomial-time uniform
family of polynomial-size
TSAC
s computes exactly the
polynomial-space computable functions, using the standard convention that the
write-only output tape is not counted in the space bound. This
characterization also gives a simple robustness principle: a fixed operation may
be added as a gate when its binary output length grows at most linearly and its
bit graph is in
{\mathbf{NL}}
. In particular, bitwise AND, OR, and XOR, as well as integer division,
are derived gates.

We then showed that
TSAC
s can be implemented by existential
Presburger arithmetic with divisibility. More precisely, every
TSAC
has
a functional EPAD formula for its output. Applying this to characteristic
functions of
{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
languages gives the
{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
-hardness of EPAD
formula satisfiability.

We used the same construction to study normalization. We identified the
merge-absorptive fragment and showed that EPAD formula satisfiability reduces to
that fragment. Thus the
{\mathbf{PSPACE}}
lower bound already holds for systems whose
divisibility atoms can be removed by finite-quotient simplification. We also
exhibited polynomial-size systems forcing multipliers
2^{2^{j}}-1
. Any
primitive equation over the distinguished variables expressing this relation has
a coefficient of bit length
\Theta(2^{j})
, and the finite-quotient
simplification following the merge sequence produces primitive equations of
that size.

The exact complexity of EPAD formula satisfiability remains open. The results
above rule out the expected
{\mathbf{NP}}
upper bound unless
{\mathbf{NP}}={\mathbf{PSPACE}}
, but they
do not give a matching upper bound. Closing this gap is the main remaining
complexity question. Another open question concerns the robustness of
TSAC

under arithmetic operations. The safe-gate criterion above applies to division,
but it does not settle whether adding a gcd gate changes the class: the length
condition is immediate, but the
{\mathbf{NL}}
bit-graph condition is not known. This
matches the state of the art in parallel algorithms for gcd; Sedjelmaci writes
that “The computation of the GCD of two integers is not known to be in the NC
class, nor [is] it known to be P-complete”
[
36
]
.

We first put the EPAD input into the form used by the reduction. Replace every

and

It remains to translate unnegated divisibility atoms. First consider positive
lengths. For word variables
X,Y
, define

\operatorname{Div}_{+}(X,Y):=XY=YX\ \wedge\ \bigl(X=b\ \vee\ (Za=aZ\wedge X=Zb)\bigr),

where
Z
is a fresh auxiliary word variable. For nonempty word values,

\operatorname{Div}_{+}(X,Y)
enforces exactly the relation
|X|\mid|Y|
on positive
lengths. If
X=b
, then
XY=YX
forces
Y\in b^{+}

Za=aZ

with

, so
X=a^{k}b
. This word is primitive, and
the commutation equation
XY=YX
therefore forces

to be a positive power
of
X
. Conversely, if
x\mid y
with
x,y>0

X=b
,
Y=b^{y}

when
x=1

X=a^{x-1}b

Y=X^{y/x}

Z=a^{x-1}
when
x>1

To handle zero, define the following formula for shifted lengths:

\operatorname{Div}_{0}(X,Y):=|Y|=1\ \vee\ \bigl(|U|+1=|X|\wedge|V|+1=|Y|\wedge\operatorname{Div}_{+}(U,V)\bigr),

with fresh auxiliary word variables
U,V
and the auxiliary variable used
inside
\operatorname{Div}_{+}
. For nonempty
X,Y

\operatorname{Div}_{0}(X,Y)
if and only if
\operatorname{val}(X)\mid\operatorname{val}(Y)
.
If
|Y|=1

\operatorname{val}(Y)=0

0

the second disjunct,

and
V

\operatorname{val}(X)

\operatorname{Div}_{+}

\operatorname{val}(X)\mid\operatorname{val}(Y)

\operatorname{val}(Y)=0

\operatorname{val}(Y)>0

\operatorname{val}(X)>0

\operatorname{Div}_{+}
gives suitable

following behavior: a positive divisor uses the second disjunct and
\operatorname{Div}_{+}
;
the case
0\mid 0

m>0

rejected because neither disjunct can apply.

For each unnegated divisibility atom
\alpha=L\mid M

A_{\alpha},B_{\alpha}

\operatorname{val}(A_{\alpha})=L

\operatorname{val}(B_{\alpha})=M

\operatorname{Div}_{0}(A_{\alpha},B_{\alpha})

existentially by the target satisfiability problem.

The construction is equisatisfiable. From an arithmetic satisfying assignment,
choose nonempty words of the prescribed shifted lengths. The divisibility
formulas are satisfied by the previous paragraphs. Conversely, a nonerasing
solution assigns each arithmetic variable
x
the value
|X|-1
. The
translated Presburger constraints have the same truth values as their arithmetic
originals, and each
\operatorname{Div}_{0}
formula enforces the corresponding divisibility
atom. The initial handling of signs and negated divisibility was logically
equivalent, so the recovered arithmetic assignment satisfies the original EPAD
formula.

The construction is polynomial. Each transformation is local, each affine term
is encoded by a linear-size length constraint, and each divisibility atom
contributes only constantly many word-equation atoms and length constraints,
apart from the size of the terms it mentions. The only terminal letters used
are

b
