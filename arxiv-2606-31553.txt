Spreading speeds for Fisher-KPP equations with slowly decaying initial data in an almost periodic setting
Xing Liang, Linfeng Xu, Tao Zhou

Abstract




Abstract
This paper investigates the long-time behavior of the Fisher-KPP equation with slowly decaying initial data in an almost periodic medium.
We mainly focus on two classes of initial data: exponentially decaying initial data and initial data that decay more slowly than any exponential function.
Employing the Hamilton–Jacobi approach, we provide a unified framework for analyzing the Cauchy problems with initial data in both cases.
We demonstrate that the level sets of the solution can be estimated by the generalized principal eigenvalue of the linearized operator and the decay rate of the initial data.

Key words
reaction-diffusion equations, almost periodic media, spreading speeds

Introduction and main results

In this paper, we study the spreading speeds associated with the following equation:

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\partial_{x}(a(x)\partial_{x}u)+b(x)\partial_{x}u+f(x,u),\ &t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\in[0,1],\ &x\in\mathbb{R}.\\
\end{cases}

(1)

We impose the following assumptions throughout the paper:

(
A1
) The coefficients
a\in C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\text{ and }b\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
are uniformly Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Moreover,
a\text{ and }b
are almost periodic with
\inf\limits_{x}a(x)>0.

The reaction term
f
is assumed to satisfy the following conditions:

(
F1
)

is of class
C^{1}
in
s
,
\partial_{s}f(\cdot,0)\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\text{ is almost periodic with }\inf\limits_{x}\partial_{s}f(x,0)>0.

(
F2
)

is assumed to be of the Fisher–KPP type, that is

f(x,0)=f(x,1)=0,\ 0<f(x,s)\leq\partial_{s}f(x,0)s\text{ for any }s\in(0,1).

(2)

(
F3
) there exist
\beta>0,s_{0}\in(0,1)
and
C\geq 0
such that

f(x,s)\geq\partial_{s}f(x,0)s-Cs^{1+\beta}\text{ for any }s\in(0,s_{0}).

(3)

For example, a concave function

uniformly in
x
, which is positive in
(0,1)
and vanishes at
0
and
1
, satisfies the conditions above.

Equation (

u_{t}=u_{xx}+f(u),

x_{\theta}^{+}(t)=\max E_{\theta}(t)\text{ and }x_{\theta}^{-}(t)=\min E_{\theta}(t),

E_{\theta}(t):=\{x|u(t,x)=\theta\}
is the so-called level set of

of value
\theta
at time

Consider the solution of equation (
1
) with compactly supported initial data. Existing results show that, under appropriate conditions, the limit

\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}\frac{x_{\theta}^{\pm}(t)}{t}

The existence and characterization of the spreading speed have already been proved.
As the present paper is concerned with propagation under initial data exhibiting different decay rates, we introduce the following definition of the spreading speed associated with initial data:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a set of functions defined on
\mathbb{R}
and
u
be a solution to (
1
) with initial datum
u_{0}\in\mathcal{U}
.
If there exist two constants
\omega^{+}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
\omega^{-}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
, which are independent of

, such that

\omega^{-}+\omega^{+}>0
, and

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}}\sup\limits_{x\in(-\infty,(-\omega^{-}-\epsilon)t]\cup[(\omega^{+}+\epsilon)t,+\infty)}|u(t,x)|=0\text{ for every sufficiently small }\epsilon>0,\\
\displaystyle{\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}}\sup\limits_{x\in[(-\omega^{-}+\epsilon)t,(\omega^{+}-\epsilon)t]}|u(t,x)-1|=0\text{ for every sufficiently small }\epsilon>0.\\
\end{cases}

(4)

then
\omega^{\pm}
are called the spreading speeds of (
1
) with respect to
\mathcal{U}
in the positive and negative directions, respectively.

Here we agree that if
\omega^{+}=+\infty
, then the interval
[(\omega^{+}+\epsilon)t,+\infty)
in (

[(-\omega^{-}+\epsilon)t,(\omega^{+}-\epsilon)t]
is replaced by
[(-\omega^{-}+\epsilon)t,+\infty)
.
Similarly, if
\omega^{-}=-\infty
, then the interval
(-\infty,(-\omega^{-}-\epsilon)t]
is understood as the empty set, and the interval
[(-\omega^{-}+\epsilon)t,(\omega^{+}-\epsilon)t]
is replaced by
(-\infty,(\omega^{+}-\epsilon)t]

When the coefficients are almost periodic functions, Berestycki and Nadin
[

\mathcal{U}=\{\phi(x)\in[0,1]\text{ with compact support}\}
exist.
The characterization of the spreading speeds are closely related to the generalized principal eigenvalues of the linearized operator, which are defined as follows. Let

\mathcal{L}\phi(x):=(a(x)\phi^{\prime}(x))^{\prime}+b(x)\phi^{\prime}(x)+c(x)\phi(x),

and,
\text{ for }p\in\mathbb{R}
,

\begin{split}&\quad L_{p}\phi(x)=e^{-px}\mathcal{L}(e^{p\cdot}\phi)(x)\\
&=(a(x)\phi^{\prime}(x))^{\prime}+(b(x)+2pa(x))\phi^{\prime}(x)+\big(p^{2}a(x)+p(b(x)+a^{\prime}(x))+c(x)\big)\phi(x).\\
\end{split}

Definition 1.2
.

(
[
8
, Definition 2.1]
)
The generalized principal eigenvalues associated with operator
L_{p}
are:

\underline{\lambda}(p):=\sup\{\lambda\mid\exists\phi\in\mathcal{A}_{-\infty}\text{ such that }L_{p}\phi\geq\lambda\phi\text{ on }\mathbb{R}\},

\overline{\lambda}(p):=\inf\{\lambda\mid\exists\phi\in\mathcal{A}_{-\infty}\text{ such that }L_{p}\phi\leq\lambda\phi\text{ on }\mathbb{R}\},

where
\mathcal{A}_{-\infty}
is the set of admissible test-functions over
\mathbb{R}
:

\mathcal{A}_{-\infty}:=\left\{\phi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\ \phi^{\prime}/\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\ \phi>0\text{ in }\mathbb{R},\ \lim_{|x|\to+\infty}\frac{\ln\phi(x)}{x}=0\right\}.

Generalized principal eigenvalues play an important role in the study of the propagation speed for problems with exponentially decaying initial data, which will be illustrated later.
We now recall the relevant definition and basic properties.
It was proved by Berestycki and Nadin
[
8
]
that
\overline{\lambda}(p)\geq\underline{\lambda}(p)\text{ for any }p\in\mathbb{R}

if
a,b
and

are only assumed to be uniformly continuous and bounded in

. Moreover, they showed that if
a,b
and

are almost periodic functions, then

\overline{\lambda}(p)=\underline{\lambda}(p)\text{ for any }p\in\mathbb{R}.

In fact, they showed that, for any
\kappa>0
and
p\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a unique solution
u_{\kappa,p}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to

L_{p}e^{u_{\kappa,p}}(x)=\kappa u_{\kappa,p}(x)e^{u_{\kappa,p}(x)}

(5)

and the limit

\lim\limits_{\kappa\to 0}\kappa u_{\kappa,p}(x)\text{ exists in }L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

(6)

with

-\|ap^{2}+(b+a^{\prime})p+c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\kappa u_{\kappa,p}(x)\leq\|ap^{2}+(b+a^{\prime})p+c\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

(7)

Taking
e^{u_{\kappa,p}(x)}
as a test function in
\mathcal{A}_{-\infty}
, one thus deduces that
\overline{\lambda}(p)\leq\lim\limits_{\kappa\to 0}\kappa u_{\kappa,p}(x)\leq\underline{\lambda}(p)
by the definition of
\overline{\lambda}(p)
and
\underline{\lambda}(p)
.
Therefore,
\overline{\lambda}(p)=\underline{\lambda}(p)=\lim\limits_{\kappa\to 0}\kappa u_{\kappa,p}(x)
and we thus denote it by
\lambda(p)
.
It was known that the function
\lambda(p)
is convex, and

\begin{aligned} \lambda(p)=O(p^{2})\end{aligned}

as
p\to\infty
(
[
8
,
9
]
).
Then there exists
p_{\pm}>0
, such that

\begin{aligned} \inf\limits_{p>0}\frac{\lambda(-p)}{p}=\frac{\lambda(-p_{+})}{p_{+}}\text{ and }\inf\limits_{p>0}\frac{\lambda(p)}{p}=\frac{\lambda(p_{-})}{p_{-}}.\end{aligned}

Furthermore, the spreading speeds of (
1
) with respect to compactly supported initial data are
\frac{\lambda(-p_{+})}{p_{+}}
and
\frac{\lambda(p_{-})}{p_{-}}
(
[
8
,
24
]
).

Homogenization of the equation

Recall the equation we will consider is

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\partial_{x}(a(x)\partial_{x}u)+b(x)\partial_{x}u+f(x,u),\ &x\in\mathbb{R},\ t>0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)=e^{-h(x)}\in\mathcal{U}_{p_{0}^{-},p_{0}^{+}},\ &x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

(19)

In this section, we proceed as follows for equation (
19
). First, we construct a suitable scaling that depends on the initial data.
Second, we determine the equation satisfied by the homogenized limit function of the solution under this scaling.
It is worth noting that we only consider the case
x\geq R_{0}
; the case
x\leq R_{0}
can be treated analogously.
After a translation, we may assume without loss of generality that
h

h(x)\in C^{2}([0,+\infty)),\ h(x)\geq 0\text{ and }h^{\prime}(x)>0\ \ \text{for any }x\geq 0.

(20)

Furthermore, we define the following scaling transformation:

\psi_{\epsilon}(x):=h^{-1}\left(\frac{h(x)-h(0)}{\epsilon}+h(0)\right),\ \text{for any }x\geq 0.

(21)

Set

u_{\epsilon}(t,x):=u\left(\frac{t}{\epsilon},\psi_{\epsilon}(x)\right),\ t\geq 0,x\geq 0,

(22)

and

Z_{\epsilon}(t,x):=\epsilon\ln u_{\epsilon}(t,x),\ t\geq 0,x\geq 0.

(23)

Then one can check that, on
\{(t,x)|t>0,x\geq 0\}

.

and

are well-defined,
and

Proof.

Define

and
\underline{u}(t,x)=\underline{c}^{-1}\exp(-h(x)-\underline{c}t)
,

and

-\underline{c}\leq\min\big\{-1,\inf_{x\geq 0}\{a(x)(h^{\prime}(x))^{2}-a(x)h^{\prime\prime}(x)-(b(x)+a^{\prime}(x))h^{\prime}(x)\big\}

are constants to be determined later.
Then we deduce that
\overline{u}
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\overline{u}

\displaystyle=\overline{c}\overline{u}\geq(a(x)(h^{\prime}(x))^{2}-a(x)h^{\prime\prime}(x)-(b(x)+a^{\prime}(x))h^{\prime}(x)+\partial_{s}f(x,0))\overline{u}

(26)

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}(a(x)\partial_{x}\overline{u})+b(x)\partial_{x}\overline{u}+\partial_{s}f(x,0)\overline{u}

\displaystyle\geq\partial_{x}(a(x)\partial_{x}\overline{u})+b(x)\partial_{x}\overline{u}+f(x,\overline{u})

in
(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)

with
\overline{u}(0,x)=\overline{c}e^{-h(x)}
for any

,
and
\underline{u}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\underline{u}

\displaystyle=-\underline{c}\underline{u}\leq(a(x)(h^{\prime}(x))^{2}-a(x)h^{\prime\prime}(x)-(b(x)+a^{\prime}(x))h^{\prime}(x))\underline{u}

for any

.
Here, we have used the fact that

Note that

Therefore, we can take

and

(i) can be obtained by the definition of

Proof of (ii): Since

is positive and increasing on

Proof of (iii): From

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\psi_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}(x)}{\epsilon(\psi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(x))^{3}}\right|\leq\frac{\sup\limits_{x\in[a,+\infty)}\epsilon|h^{\prime\prime}(x)||h^{\prime}(\psi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(x))|^{2}}{\inf\limits_{x\in[a,+\infty)}(h^{\prime}(x))^{3}}+\frac{|h^{\prime\prime}(\psi_{\epsilon}(x))|}{\inf\limits_{x\in[a,+\infty)}h^{\prime}(x)}\to 0,

-\zeta(t_{n},x_{n})\leq r\lambda^{*}(q)-\zeta(t_{n}-r,x_{n}+qr).

Because

\zeta(t_{n},x_{n})-\psi(t_{n},x_{n})\geq\psi(t_{n}-r,x_{n}+qr)-\psi(t_{n},x_{n})-r\lambda^{*}(q).

The choice of

Letting

gives a contradiction.
Hence
\underline{\zeta}
is a viscosity supersolution of (

It remains to verify the boundary and initial values.
For

and
(s,y)\to(t,0)

take the path

\gamma(r)=y-r.

When

is sufficiently close to
(t,0)

\tau(y)=y<s
.
Thus

0\leq-\zeta(s,y)\leq\max\{0,y\lambda^{*}(-1)\}\to 0,

and consequently

\lim\limits_{(s,y)\to(t,0)}Z(s,y)=0.

For the initial condition,

(s,y)\to(0,x)
with
x\geq 0

Taking the constant path
\gamma\equiv y
gives

-\zeta(s,y)\leq\max\{0,s\lambda^{*}(0)+py\}.

(46)

On the other hand,

for any admissible path
\gamma\in\mathcal{B}

\tau(\gamma)\geq s
,

\int_{0}^{s}\lambda^{*}(\gamma^{\prime}(r))dr+p\gamma(s)\geq py-s\lambda(-p).

If

\int_{0}^{\tau(\gamma)}\lambda^{*}(\gamma^{\prime}(r))dr\geq py-\tau(\gamma)\lambda(-p)\geq py-s\lambda(-p),

because
\lambda(-p)>0

Taking the supremum in time and then the infimum in
\gamma

-\zeta(s,y)\geq py-s\lambda(-p).

(47)

Combing (

47

\lim\limits_{(s,y)\to(0,x)}\zeta(s,y)=-px.

The above boundary limits give the same parabolic boundary values for
\overline{\zeta}
and
\underline{\zeta}
.

By the comparison principle for (

\overline{\zeta}\leq\underline{\zeta}.

Since always

Next, we give four types of typical examples for the case where
p^{+}_{0}=0
, which were considered by Hamel and Roques
[
18
]
.
We always assume that the initial data
u_{0}
satisfies the condition in Theorem
1.3
.
Then

\min E_{\theta}(t)\sim\max E_{\theta}(t)\sim h^{-1}(\lambda(0)t)\text{ as }t\to+\infty,

or, in other words,

h(\min E_{\theta}(t))\sim h(\max E_{\theta}(t))\sim(\lambda(0)t)\text{ as }t\to+\infty.

If

u_{0}(x)\sim C(\ln x)^{-\alpha}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }x\to+\infty

for some
\alpha,C>0
, then

\ln(\ln(\min E_{\theta}(t)))\sim\ln(\ln(\max E_{\theta}(t)))\sim\frac{\lambda(0)t}{\alpha}\text{ as }t\to+\infty.

If

u_{0}(x)\sim Cx^{-\alpha}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }x\to+\infty

for some
\alpha,C>0
, then
\min E_{\theta}(t)\sim\max E_{\theta}(t)\sim e^{\frac{\lambda(0)t}{\alpha}}\text{ as }t\to+\infty.

If

u_{0}(x)\sim Ce^{-\beta x^{\alpha}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }x\to+\infty

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\beta,C>0
, then

\min E_{\theta}(t)\sim\max E_{\theta}(t)\sim\lambda(0)^{1/\alpha}\left(\frac{t}{\beta}\right)^{1/\alpha}\text{ as }t\to+\infty.

If

u_{0}(x)\sim Ce^{-\alpha x/\ln x}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }x\to+\infty

for some
\alpha,C>0
, then
\frac{\alpha\min E_{\theta}(t)}{\ln\min E_{\theta}(t)}\sim\frac{\alpha\max E_{\theta}(t)}{\ln\max E_{\theta}(t)}\sim\lambda(0)t\text{ as }t\to+\infty
.
Hence

\min E_{\theta}(t)\sim\max E_{\theta}(t)\sim\frac{\lambda(0)t\ln t}{\alpha}\text{ as }t\to+\infty,

which is consistent with the result in the homogeneous case
[
18
]
.

An interesting question is whether, for an initial condition with sub-exponential decay, the expansion speed of its level set exhibits lower-order corrections analogous to those in Bramson’s results—and if so, how to determine them.
In the context of linear propagation speeds, it is known that such corrections are of order
\ln t
(
[
1
,
3
,
16
,
17
,
33
]
).
However, the four examples presented above indicate that any potential lower-order corrections, if they exist, are highly intricate and inevitably vary depending on the specific type of sub-exponential decay of the initial data. We leave this issue for future investigation.
