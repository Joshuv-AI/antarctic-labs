Extension of Sobolev functions on balls in infinite dimensions
Zhouzhe Wang, Xu Zhang, Shiliang Zhao

Abstract
We prove the existence of a bounded Sobolev extension operator E : W p , 1 ​ ( B , P ) → W p , 1 ​ ( ℓ 2 , P ) E:W^{p,1}\left(B,P\right)\rightarrow W^{p,1}\left(\ell^{2},P\right) using a completely new method, where B ⊂ ℓ 2 B\subset\ell^{2} is the unit ball and P P is any non-trivial centered Gaussian measure on ℓ 2 \ell^{2} . This solves an open problem posed in [ 1 , 5 , 7 ] .



1
Introduction

Of great significance to modern analysis, Sobolev spaces in finite dimensions (e.g.,
[
2
,
16
]
) are particularly crucial to partial differential equation theory and its applications in mathematical physics. Additionally, they are indispensable to approximation theory, control theory, differential geometry and beyond.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
m
be an integer. Denote by
W^{p,m}(\Omega)
the Sobolev space on
\Omega
. It consists of locally integrable functions whose distributional partial derivatives of all orders up to
m
are
L^{p}-
integrable. This is a Banach space with norm

\|f\|_{W^{p,m}(\Omega)}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|D^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

An operator
E
is called the bounded extension operator if there exists
C>0
such that

\|Ef\|_{W^{p,m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{p,m}(\Omega)},\qquad Ef|_{\Omega}(x)=f(x),\quad\forall f\in W^{p,m}(\Omega).

Then we say
\Omega
is a
W^{p,m}
-extension domain
.

Since the existence of bounded extension operator for
\Omega
guarantees that
W^{p,m}(\Omega)
inherits many properties of
W^{p,m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, lots of efforts have been made to give the criteria of the
W^{p,m}
-extension domains. In
[
6
]
, Calderón proved that Lipschitz domains are
W^{p,m}
-extension domains for
1<p<\infty,m\geq 1
. In
[
20
, Chapter VI]
, Stein showed that Lipschitz domains are
W^{p,m}
-extension domains for
p=1,\infty
. In
[
12
]
, Jones introduced the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-domains and proved that they are
W^{p,m}
-extension domain for
1\leq p\leq\infty,m\geq 1
. To show that
\Omega
is a
W^{p,m}
-extension domain, a natural approach is explicitly constructing a bounded extension operator. Indeed, Calderón in
[
6
]
defined the extension operator locally and using the partition of unity, constructed the bounded extension operator. Similarly, Stein’s extension operator relies on Whitney’s decomposition, and he also employed a partition of unity to assemble the global extension operator from locally defined extensions(
[
20
, Chapter VI]
). Jones in
[
12
]
also adopted Whitney’s decomposition, and by incorporating polynomial fitting on the cubes, he constructed a bounded extension operator with the aid of a partition of unity.

Sobolev spaces in infinite dimensions also play an important role in various areas of mathematics including Malliavin analysis and infinite-dimensional real and complex analysis (e.g.,
[
4
,
10
,
14
,
18
,
19
,
23
]
). However, compared to the finite dimensions, much less is known about the extension problems in infinite dimension. According to
[
5
]
, this problem has been discussed by V.I. Bogachev first with G. Da Prato and P. Malliavin in the 1990s, and later also with A. Lunardi, but only a trivial positive result was known about extension from half-spaces by reflection.

This problem remains open for several decades partially due to the fact that many basic tools – such as convolutions, mollifiers, and standard covering arguments– are not directly avaible in the infinite-dimensional setting.
In this paper, we prove the existence of a bounded Sobolev extension operator
E:W^{p,1}\left(B,P\right)\rightarrow W^{p,1}\left(\ell^{2},P\right)
using a completely new method, where
B\subset\ell^{2}
is the unit ball and
P
is any non-trivial centered Gaussian measure on
\ell^{2}
. This solves an open problem posed in
[
1
,
5
,
7
]
.

Existence of Sobolev Extension for the Unit Ball

Lemma 4
.

There exist bounded linear operators

E_{m}:W^{p,1}\left(B_{m},\mathcal{N}^{m}\right)\rightarrow W^{p,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathcal{N}^{m}\right),

such that

\left(E_{m}f\right)|_{B_{m}}=f,a.e,\forall f\in W^{p,1}\left(B_{m},\mathcal{N}^{m}\right),

Similarly,
E^{2}-2Q\widetilde{P}^{T}
is the Jacobian matrix of the mapping
\textbf{x}\mapsto\textbf{y}
and it satisfies

\displaystyle\left|\left|E^{2}-2Q\widetilde{P}^{T}\right|\right|\leqslant 1+2\left|\left|Q\right|\right|\cdot\left|\left|\widetilde{P}^{T}\right|\right|\leqslant 1+\frac{2}{\sqrt{\lambda}}.

(11)

Since

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left|\frac{\partial\vartheta}{\partial\xi_{i}}\left(\xi\right)\right|^{2}\leqslant 4\sup_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\theta^{\prime}\right|^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}^{4}\xi_{i}^{2}\leqslant 4\sup_{i\geqslant 1}a_{i}^{2}\cdot\sup_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\theta^{\prime}\right|^{2},\forall\xi\in\partial D_{m},

by (
10
) and the chain rule we obtain

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left|\frac{\partial\vartheta\left(\xi\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{2}\leqslant 4\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)^{2}\cdot\sup_{i\geqslant 1}a_{i}^{2}\cdot\sup_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\theta^{\prime}\right|^{2},\forall\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\left\{\textbf{0}\right\}.

(12)

Combining (
11
) together with the chain rule yields

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left|\frac{\partial f\left(\textbf{y}\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{2}\leqslant\left(1+\frac{2}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial y_{i}}\left(\textbf{y}\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)\right|^{2},\forall\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\overline{D_{m}}.

(13)

Set

D\triangleq\max\left\{\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)^{2}\cdot\sup_{i\geqslant 1}a_{i}^{2}\cdot\sup_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\theta^{\prime}\right|^{2},2\left(1+\frac{2}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)^{2}\right\}>0.

For
\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\overline{D_{m}}
, using (
12
) and (
13
), we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\left|\partial_{k}F_{2}\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right|^{2}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\left|\frac{\partial\vartheta\left(\xi\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)}{\partial x_{k}}\cdot f\left(\textbf{y}\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)+\vartheta\left(\xi\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}\left(\textbf{y}\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left|f\left(\textbf{y}\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)\right|^{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\left|\frac{\partial\vartheta\left(\xi\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)}{\partial x_{k}}\right|^{2}+2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\left|\frac{\partial f\left(\textbf{y}\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)}{\partial x_{k}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 8\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)^{2}\cdot\sup_{i\geqslant 1}a_{i}^{2}\cdot\sup_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\theta^{\prime}\right|^{2}\cdot\left|f\left(\textbf{y}\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)\right|^{2}+2\left(1+\frac{2}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial y_{i}}\left(\textbf{y}\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant D\left(\left|f\left(\textbf{y}\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)\right|^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial y_{i}}\left(\textbf{y}\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)\right|^{2}\right).

Making use of (
8
) and the above inequality, following the same reasoning as in the proof of (
9
), one arrives at

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\overline{D_{m}}}\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\left|\partial_{k}F_{2}\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{2}}\mathrm{d}\textbf{x}

\displaystyle\leqslant D^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\overline{D_{m}}}\left(\left|f\left(\textbf{y}\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)\right|^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial y_{i}}\left(\textbf{y}\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{2}}\mathrm{d}\textbf{x}

\displaystyle\leqslant D^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{D_{m}}\left(\left|f\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right|^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left|\partial_{i}f\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{2}}\mathrm{d}\textbf{x}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(2D\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{D_{m}}\left|f\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right|^{p}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{2}}\mathrm{d}\textbf{x}+\int_{D_{m}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left|\partial_{i}f\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{2}}\mathrm{d}\textbf{x}\right),

that is,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\overline{D_{m}}}\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\left|\partial_{k}F_{2}\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{2}}\mathrm{d}\textbf{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{2D}\left[\left(\int_{D_{m}}\left|f\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right|^{p}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{2}}\mathrm{d}\textbf{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\int_{D_{m}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left|\partial_{i}f\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{2}}\mathrm{d}\textbf{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right].

Now we have

\displaystyle\left|\left|F_{2}\right|\right|_{p,1,\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\overline{D_{m}}}\leqslant\left(\sqrt{2D}+1\right)\cdot\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{p,1,D_{m}}.

(14)

Step 3
We are now in a position to define the extension operator.

Set
F\left(\textbf{x}\right)\triangleq F_{1}\left(\textbf{x}\right)+F_{2}\left(\textbf{x}\right)
. It follows that

F(\textbf{x})=\left(1-\vartheta\left(\textbf{x}\right)\right)f\left(\textbf{x}\right)+\vartheta\left(\textbf{x}\right)f\left(\textbf{x}\right)=f(\textbf{x}),~\forall\textbf{x}\in\partial D_{m}.

Then from (
7
) and (
14
) we obtain that
F\left(\textbf{x}\right)
satisfies (
4
) with
C\triangleq\sqrt{2D}+1+\sum_{i=1}^{2N}\widetilde{D_{i}}
.

Set

E_{m}f(\textbf{x})=\begin{cases}f(\textbf{x}),&\textbf{x}\in D_{m},\\
F(\textbf{x}),&\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\backslash\overline{D_{m}}.\end{cases}

As a result, from (
4
), we obtain

\left|\left|E_{m}f\right|\right|_{p,1,\mathbb{R}^{m}}\leqslant\left|\left|F\right|\right|_{p,1,\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\overline{D_{m}}}+\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{p,1,D_{m}}\leqslant\left(1+C\right)\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{p,1,D_{m}}.

Clearly, since
D,\widetilde{D_{i}}
are independent of
m
,
C+1
is independent of
m
, hence we complete the proof of Lemma
4
.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Suppose
\left\{f_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\right\}_{k,n=1}\subset L^{p}\left(\ell^{2},P\right)
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\ell^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}\left|f_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\mathrm{d}P<\infty.

(15)

If for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, as
n\rightarrow+\infty
,
f_{n}^{\left(k\right)}
converges weakly to
f^{\left(k\right)}
in
L^{p}\left(\ell^{2},P\right)
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\ell^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}\left|f^{\left(k\right)}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\mathrm{d}P\leqslant\varliminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\ell^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}\left|f_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\mathrm{d}P.

(16)

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\ell^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}\left|f^{\left(k\right)}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\mathrm{d}P=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\ell^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}^{2}\left|f^{\left(k\right)}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\mathrm{d}P

\displaystyle\leqslant\varlimsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\varliminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\ell^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}^{2}\left|f_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\mathrm{d}P\leqslant\varliminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\ell^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}\left|f_{n}^{\left(k\right)}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\mathrm{d}P,

where the first equality follows from Levi’s theorem, and the second inequality can be obtained by considering the Banach space naturally defined by the Cartesian product of
m
copies of
L^{p}\left(\ell^{2},P\right)
and then using the weak lower semicontinuity of the norm.

This completes the proof of Lemma
6
.
∎

Theorem 7
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. There exist bounded linear operators

E:W^{p,1}\left(B,P\right)\rightarrow W^{p,1}\left(\ell^{2},P\right),

such that

Ef|_{B}=f,~a.e,~\forall f\in W^{p,1}\left(B,P\right).

Remark 8
.

Clearly, Theorem
7
can be extended to
B\left(\textbf{0},r\right),r>0
, since one can consider constructing the measure
P_{r}
using
ra_{1},ra_{2},\cdots
. Then consider the change of variables
\textbf{x}=r\textbf{y}
.

Proof.

For given
n\in\mathbb{N},f\in C_{b}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, by Lemma
4
, we know that for any positive integer
m>\max\left\{n,N\right\}
, there exist
F_{m}\in\bigcap_{q=1}^{\infty}W^{q,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathcal{N}^{m}\right)
such that

F_{m}|_{B_{m}}=f|_{B_{m}},a.e,

and for every
q\geqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|F_{m}\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{N}^{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{2}\left|\partial_{i}F_{m}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{N}^{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{q}\left[\left(\int_{B_{m}}\left|f\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{N}^{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\left(\int_{B_{m}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\left|\partial_{i}f\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{N}^{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right].

This is exactly

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\ell^{2}}\left|F_{m}\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}P\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\left(\int_{\ell^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}^{2}\left|\partial_{i}F_{m}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathrm{d}P\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{q}\left[\left(\int_{\ell^{2}}\left|f\right|^{q}\chi_{B_{m}\times\left(\ell^{2}\setminus\left\{1,2,\cdots,m\right\}\right)}\mathrm{d}P\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\left(\int_{\ell^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}\left|\partial_{i}f\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}\chi_{B_{m}\times\left(\ell^{2}\setminus\left\{1,2,\cdots,m\right\}\right)}\mathrm{d}P\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{q}\left[\left(\int_{\ell^{2}}\left|f\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}P\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\left(\int_{\ell^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}^{2}\left|\partial_{i}f\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathrm{d}P\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right]<\infty.

By the dominated convergence theorem we have

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{m\rightarrow+\infty}\left|\left|F_{m}\right|\right|_{q,1,\ell^{2}}\leqslant C_{q}\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{q,1,B},\forall q\geqslant 1.

(17)

By
[
8
, Theorem 4.6.2, p.251]
, we know that
\left\{F_{m}\right\}_{m=1}^{\infty},\left\{\partial_{1}F_{m}\right\}_{m=1}^{\infty},\left\{\partial_{2}F_{m}\right\}_{m=1}^{\infty},\cdots
are uniformly integrable and form a bounded sequence in
L^{p}\left(\ell^{2},P\right)
. Hence by
[
9
, Corollary 4, p.289]
and
[
9
, Corollary 11, p.294]
, we can use a diagonal argument to obtain a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers
\left\{m_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that
F_{m_{k}},\partial_{1}F_{m_{k}},\cdots
converge weakly in
L^{p}\left(\ell^{2},P\right)
to
F,F^{1},\cdots
.

By the weak lower semicontinuity of the norm and Lemma
6
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\ell^{2}}\left|F\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}P\leqslant\varliminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\ell^{2}}\left|F_{m_{k}}\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}P,\quad\int_{\ell^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}^{2}\left|F^{i}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\mathrm{d}P\leqslant\varliminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\ell^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}^{2}\left|\partial_{i}F_{m_{k}}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\mathrm{d}P.

(18)

For
j=1,2,\cdots,\phi\in\mathscr{C}_{b}^{\infty}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\ell^{2}}F\cdot\delta_{j}\phi\mathrm{d}P=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\ell^{2}}F_{m_{k}}\cdot\delta_{j}\phi\mathrm{d}P=-\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\ell^{2}}\partial_{j}F_{m_{k}}\cdot\phi\mathrm{d}P=-\int_{\ell^{2}}F^{j}\cdot\phi\mathrm{d}P.

(19)

From (
17
), (
18
), (
19
) we obtain
F\in W^{p,1}\left(\ell^{2},P\right)
and

\left|\left|F\right|\right|_{p,1,\ell^{2}}\leqslant C_{p}\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{p,1,B}.

From

F_{m_{k}}|_{B_{m_{k}}}=f|_{B_{m_{k}}},\text{a.e.},B\subset B_{m_{k}}\times\ell^{2}\left(\mathbb{N}\setminus\left\{1,2,\cdots,m_{k}\right\}\right),k=1,2,\cdots

we obtain
F_{m_{k}}|_{B}=f|_{B},\text{a.e},k=1,2,\cdots
. Moreover, for any
\phi\in C_{0,F^{\infty}}^{\infty}\left(B\right)
, we have

\int_{B}F\phi\mathrm{d}P=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B}F_{m_{k}}\phi\mathrm{d}P=\int_{B}f\phi\mathrm{d}P,

then by
[
23
, Lemma 3.1, p.17]
we get
F|_{B}=f|_{B},\text{a.e.}
.

Since
W^{p,1}\left(B,P\right)
is separable and the restrictions to
B
of all functions in
\mathscr{C}_{b}^{\infty}
form a dense subset of
W^{p,1}\left(B,P\right)
, there exist linearly independent
\left\{f_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathscr{C}_{b}^{\infty}|_{B}
such that
\mathrm{span}\left\{f_{1}|_{B},f_{2}|_{B},\cdots\right\}
is dense in
W^{p,1}\left(B,P\right)
. For each
f_{i}
we obtain an extension
F_{i}\in W^{p,1}\left(\ell^{2},P\right)
by the above procedure. The diagonal argument ensures that the subsequence
\left\{m_{k}\right\}
we extract can be chosen to be the same for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Define a linear operator

E:\mathrm{span}\left\{f_{1}|_{B},f_{2}|_{B},\cdots\right\}\rightarrow W^{p,1}\left(\ell^{2},P\right),

by

Ef_{i}=F_{i},\quad i=1,2,\cdots.

Note that for
f\in\mathrm{span}\left\{f_{1}|_{B},f_{2}|_{B},\cdots\right\}
,
Ef
can be obtained by the same method, hence we also have

\left|\left|Ef\right|\right|_{p,1,\ell^{2}}\leqslant C_{p}\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{p,1,B}.

Thus we can naturally extend
E
to

E:W^{p,1}\left(B,P\right)\rightarrow W^{p,1}\left(\ell^{2},P\right)

such that

\left|\left|Ef\right|\right|_{p,1,\ell^{2}}\leqslant C_{p}\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{p,1,B},\quad\forall f\in W^{p,1}\left(B,P\right).

This completes the proof of Theorem
7
.
∎
