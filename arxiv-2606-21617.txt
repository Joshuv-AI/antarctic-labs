Hyperonic equation of state for neutron stars: A systematic Bayesian comparison of density-dependent and non-linear relativistic mean-field models
Pedro Sanson, Tuhin Malik, Constança Providência

Abstract
A systematic Bayesian inference study of the equation of state (EOS) of dense matter with strangeness is presented extending five relativistic mean-field (RMF) models with both constant and density dependent couplings to include the full baryon octet. The hyperon-nucleon couplings in the scalar channel are varied within ranges informed by hypernuclear data, while vector isoscalar couplings are fixed by the SU(6) symmetry quark model. Observational constraints from NICER (PSR J0030, J0437, J0740) and GW170817, theoretical constraints from chiral effective field theory ( χ \chi EFT) and perturbative QCD (pQCD), and experimental constraints from nuclear saturation properties are imposed simultaneously. We find that the inclusion of hyperons systematically reduces the maximum neutron star mass by 0.05 0.05 – 0.10 ​ M ⊙ 0.10\,M_{\odot} across all models while increasing the radius at 1.4 ​ M ⊙ 1.4\,M_{\odot} by 0.5 0.5 – 0.8 0.8 km. The speed of sound exhibits a characteristic softening at densities 2 2 – 3 ​ ρ 0 3\,\rho_{0} coinciding with hyperon onset. All hyperonic models remain consistent with the 2 ​ M ⊙ 2\,M_{\odot} constraint. Models with a more flexible isovector channel span a larger proton fraction when only nucleons are included. However, the extra flexibility is visibly suppressed by hyperons, meaning that the average proton distribution is independent of model flexibility when hyperons are included. Less flexible models show comparable or slightly increased proton fractions due to EOS stiffening when hyperons are included. Only a residual number of hyperonic EOS give rise to a mass-radius curve with a negative slope at low masses. A 1.8 ​ M ⊙ 1.8M_{\odot} neutron star with a radius larger than or similar to the radius of a 1.2 ​ M ⊙ 1.2M_{\odot} star would give a strong indication that the star contains other baryonic degrees of freedom in addition to nucleons.



I
Introduction

Neutron stars (NSs), which are remnants of massive stellar explosions, are among the most extreme objects in the Universe. The equation of state (EOS) of dense matter is a central ingredient in modeling their structure and observable properties such as mass, radius, and tidal deformability
28
;
39
;
7
. While purely nucleonic models have been extensively constrained by multi-messenger astrophysical observations, the appearance of heavier baryons – hyperons (
\Lambda,\Sigma,\Xi
) – inside neutron stars is still debated. The onset of hyperons at densities of
2
–
3
times nuclear saturation density
\rho_{0}
is a robust prediction of relativistic mean-field (RMF) theory
20
;
36
.

While relativistic mean-field models including hyperons are able to describe two solar mass stars
42
;
5
;
32
;
27
;
14
, microscopic approaches based on many-body descriptions do not describe massive stars
41
. If hyperonic degrees of freedom are included, maximum masses of 1.5
M_{\odot}
or below have been obtained
37
, see
8
for a review. The inclusion of three-body forces may solve this tension
26
;
25
;
19
. The recent calculations carried out with a chiral hyperon-nucleon interaction tuned to the
p\Lambda
femtoscopic data from the ALICE Collaboration predicted a maximum mass below 1.4
M_{\odot}
, though hyperonic three-body forces were not included
40
.

The so-called “hyperon puzzle” refers to the tension between the softening of the EOS caused by hyperons, which tends to reduce the maximum NS mass below
2\,M_{\odot}
, and the observation of massive pulsars such as PSR J0740+6620 (
M\approx 2.07\,M_{\odot}
)
12
and PSR J0348+0432 (
M\approx 2.01\,M_{\odot}
)
4
. The inclusion of repulsive hyperon-hyperon interactions mediated by the hidden-strangeness
\phi
meson has been shown to stiffen the EOS sufficiently to accommodate massive NSs even with hyperons present
5
;
43
, within a RMF description.

In this work, we perform a systematic Bayesian comparison of five RMF model families — each in both nucleonic and hyperonic versions — constrained simultaneously by nuclear saturation properties,
\chi
EFT predictions for pure neutron matter, pQCD at high density, NICER mass-radius measurements, and GW170817 tidal deformability data. Information from hypernuclei
18
is used to constrain the hyperon-meson couplings. In particular, the couplings determined by reproducing binding energies of hypernuclei within RMF models are taken as reference
13
;
31
;
15
.

The possibility of using neutron stars to extract information on the hyperon content has been considered in references
38
;
22
. However, the authors concluded that the present uncertainties on the mass and radius of observed neutron stars are still too large and do not allow for drawing any conclusions on the hyperon content.

In
29
, Bayesian inference was performed within an RMF model with density dependent couplings, to determine under which conditions two solar mass neutron stars would contain hyperons. While it was shown that it is possible to build EOS that contain hyperons and predict maximum neutron star masses above 2
M_{\odot}
, it was also shown that this requires a quite stiff nuclear EOS, leading to 1.4
M_{\odot}
NS with a radius of
\sim 13
km, a conclusion similar to the one drawn in
16
. An extension of this study to four other RMF formulations, imposing the same constraints on all models, allows us to draw conclusions that are independent of the RMF parametrization/description considered. We hope that the present study will positively contribute to the discussion of whether hyperons are present inside neutron stars.

The paper is organized as follows: after a brief introduction of models in Sec.
II
, the results are presented and discussed in Sec.
IV
and some conclusions are drawn in Sec.
V
.

II
The Models

The EOS of hadronic matter is determined from the Lagrangian density that describes the hadron system. The degrees of freedom included are: the nucleons
N=(p,n)
; the hyperons
Y=\Lambda,\Sigma^{+,0,-},\Xi^{0,-}
in the hyperonic case; the meson fields that describe the nuclear interaction, the scalar isoscalar
\sigma
field, the vector isoscalar
\omega
field, and the vector isovector
\varrho
field, with masses
m_{i}
,
i=\sigma,\omega,\varrho
; for the hyperonic extension, we additionally include the hidden-strangeness vector meson
\phi
(
m_{\phi}=1020
MeV), which is important to describe the hyperon-hyperon interaction; in addition, electrons and muons are included for charge neutrality.

The Lagrangian density is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\bar{\Psi}[\gamma^{\mu}(i\partial_{\mu}-\Gamma_{\omega}\omega_{\mu}-\Gamma_{\varrho}\,\boldsymbol{t}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varrho}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\quad-\Gamma_{\phi}\phi_{\mu})-(m-\Gamma_{\sigma}\sigma-\Gamma_{\sigma^{*}}\sigma^{*})]\Psi

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\sigma\,\partial^{\mu}\sigma-m_{\sigma}^{2}\sigma^{2})

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}m_{\omega}^{2}\omega_{\mu}\omega^{\mu}-\tfrac{1}{4}F^{(\omega)}_{\mu\nu}F^{(\omega)\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}m_{\varrho}^{2}\boldsymbol{\varrho}_{\mu}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varrho}^{\mu}-\tfrac{1}{4}\boldsymbol{F}^{(\varrho)}_{\mu\nu}\cdot\boldsymbol{F}^{(\varrho)\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\sigma^{*}\partial^{\mu}\sigma^{*}-m_{\sigma^{*}}^{2}{\sigma^{*}}^{2})

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}m_{\phi}^{2}\phi_{\mu}\phi^{\mu}-\tfrac{1}{4}F^{(\phi)}_{\mu\nu}F^{(\phi)\mu\nu}+\mathcal{L}_{\rm NL},

(1)

where the parameters
\Gamma_{i}
or
g_{i}
(for constant couplings),
i=\sigma,\omega,\varrho,\phi,\sigma^{*}
designate the couplings of the mesons to the baryons. For models with constant couplings, the density-dependent couplings
\Gamma_{i}
in Eq. (
1
) are replaced by couplings
g_{i}
. The term
\mathcal{L}_{\rm NL}
is absent for DDH models and includes self-interacting and mixed meson terms in models with constant couplings
g_{i}
. This contribution to the Lagrangian density is important for defining the density dependence of the EOS when the coupling parameters are constants.

II.1
Density-dependent models

The density-dependent models include meson–nucleon couplings
\Gamma_{i}
that depend on the total baryon density
\rho
:

\Gamma_{i}(\rho)=\Gamma_{i,0}\,h_{i}(x),\quad x=\rho/n_{0},\quad i=\sigma,\omega,\varrho,

(2)

with
\Gamma_{i,0}
the couplings at saturation density
n_{0}
.
Two parametrizations for
h_{i},\,i=\sigma,\,\omega
are considered:
For the DDB (Malik22) model
28
:

h_{i}(x)=\exp[-(x^{a_{i}}-1)],

(3)

and for the DDME (Typel99) model
39
:

h_{i}(x)=a_{i}\,\frac{1+b_{i}(x+d_{i})^{2}}{1+c_{i}(x+d_{i})^{2}}.

(4)

The
\varrho
-meson coupling includes an optional
y
parameter:

h_{\varrho}(x)=y\exp[-a_{\varrho}(x-1)]+(1-y),\quad 0<y\leqslant 1.

(5)

Models with this extra parameter are designated by “model”
y

(DDBy, DDMEy). The density dependence of the couplings ensures
that they never increase with density.

II.2
Non-linear meson terms

The NL models employ constant couplings
g_{i}
and include nonlinear meson terms in the Lagrangian density:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm NL}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\kappa}{3!}(g_{\sigma}\sigma)^{3}-\tfrac{\lambda_{0}}{4!}(g_{\sigma}\sigma)^{4}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{\zeta}{4!}(g_{\omega}^{2}\omega_{\mu}\omega^{\mu})^{2}+\Lambda_{w}\,g_{\varrho}^{2}\,\boldsymbol{\varrho}_{\mu}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varrho}^{\mu}\,g_{\omega}^{2}\,\omega_{\nu}\omega^{\nu}.

(6)

The parameters
\kappa
,
\lambda_{0}
,
\zeta
, and
\Lambda_{w}
control various aspects of nuclear matter:
\kappa
and
\lambda_{0}
affect the incompressibility at saturation,
\zeta
modulates the high-density EOS behavior, and the
\omega
–
\varrho
term determines the density dependence of the symmetry energy.

II.3
Hyperonic extension

The full baryon octet (
n,p,\Lambda,\Sigma^{+},\Sigma^{0},\Sigma^{-},\Xi^{0},\Xi^{-}
) is included in beta equilibrium with electrons and muons. The hyperon couplings are expressed as ratios relative to the nucleon couplings:

g_{iY}=x_{iY}\cdot g_{iN},\quad\Gamma_{iY}(\rho)=x_{iY}\cdot\Gamma_{iN}(\rho)

(7)

where
i=\sigma,\omega,\varrho,\phi
and
Y=\Lambda,\Sigma,\Xi
. In the models with density-dependent couplings, the density dependence is the same for hyperons and for nucleons.

The
vector couplings
-isoscalar are fixed by SU(6) quark-model symmetry:

\displaystyle x_{\omega\Lambda}

\displaystyle=x_{\omega\Sigma}=\tfrac{2}{3},

\displaystyle x_{\omega\Xi}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{3},

\displaystyle x_{\phi\Lambda}

\displaystyle=x_{\phi\Sigma}=-\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{3},

\displaystyle x_{\phi\Xi}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3},

\displaystyle x_{\phi N}

\displaystyle=0,

(8)

The
\varrho
-meson coupling ratio
x_{\varrho Y}=1
for all
Y
is the same for all baryons; the isospin projection
I_{3B}
enters the field equation and defines the strength of the interaction. The
scalar couplings
(
x_{s\Lambda},x_{s\Sigma},x_{s\Xi}
) are three additional free parameters sampled in the Bayesian inference, with prior ranges informed by hypernuclear data (Table
2
)
13
;
31
;
15
.

In DDH models, the density dependence of the
\phi
coupling is taken to be the same as that of the
\omega
nucleon coupling. Although the
\sigma^{*}
meson (
m_{\sigma^{*}}=975

\sigma^{*}

x_{\sigma^{*}B}=0

II.4
Equations of motion

In the mean-field approximation, the meson field equations for the hyperonic case are:

\displaystyle m_{\sigma,{\rm eff}}^{2}\bar{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\in N\cup Y}g_{\sigma B}\,\rho^{s}_{B},

(9)

\displaystyle m_{\omega,{\rm eff}}^{2}\bar{\omega}

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\in N\cup Y}g_{\omega B}\,\rho_{B},

(10)

\displaystyle m_{\varrho,{\rm eff}}^{2}\bar{\varrho}_{03}

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\in N\cup Y}g_{\varrho B}\,I_{3B}\,\rho_{B},

(11)

\displaystyle m_{\phi}^{2}\bar{\phi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\in N\cup Y}g_{\phi B}\,\rho_{B},

(12)

\displaystyle m_{\sigma^{*}}^{2}\bar{\sigma}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\in N\cup Y}g_{\sigma^{*}B}\,\rho^{s}_{B},

(13)

where
\rho_{B}=k_{FB}^{3}/(3\pi^{2})
is the baryon number density,

\rho^{s}_{B}
the scalar density,
m^{2}_{i,{\rm eff}}=m^{2}_{i}
for DDH models, and for NL models are given by

\displaystyle m_{\sigma,{\rm eff}}^{2}

\displaystyle=m_{\sigma}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}g_{\sigma}^{3}\sigma+\frac{\lambda}{6}g_{\sigma}^{4}\sigma^{2}

(14)

\displaystyle m_{\omega,{\rm eff}}^{2}

\displaystyle=m_{\omega}^{2}+\frac{\xi}{3!}g_{\omega}^{4}\omega^{2}+2\Lambda_{\omega}g_{\varrho}^{2}g_{\omega}^{2}\varrho^{2}

(15)

\displaystyle m_{\varrho,{\rm eff}}^{2}

\displaystyle=m_{\varrho}^{2}+2\Lambda_{\omega}g_{\omega}^{2}g_{\varrho}^{2}\omega^{2}.

(16)

The effective baryon mass is
m^{*}_{B}=m_{B}-g_{\sigma B}\bar{\sigma}-g_{\sigma^{*}B}\bar{\sigma}^{*}
. For DDH models, a rearrangement self-energy
\Sigma_{0}^{R}
arises from the density dependence of the couplings and enters the chemical potential of every baryon.

III
Bayesian Inference Framework

The posterior is sampled using
PyMultiNest

6
with 4500 live points. The total log-likelihood combines the following constraints:

Nuclear matter properties (NMP).

Gaussian constraints at saturation — binding energy
e_{0}=-16.0\pm 0.2
MeV, incompressibility
K_{0}=230\pm 40
MeV, symmetry energy
J_{\rm sym}=32.5\pm 2.5
MeV. Computed from the
nucleonic
EOS.

\chi
EFT constraints.

Super-Gaussian constraints on pure neutron matter energy density at sub-saturation densities, with 5% enlargement
23
. Computed from the
nucleonic
EOS.

NICER mass-radius.

KDE likelihoods from posterior samples of PSR J0030+0451
33
, PSR J0437
-
4715
9
, and PSR J0740+6620
34
. Evaluated on the
hyperonic
EOS for hyperonic models.

GW170817.

Tidal deformability likelihood from the binary NS merger
1
;
2
.

pQCD.

Constraint at
\rho\approx 8.5\,\rho_{0}
using the Komoltsev-Kurkela framework
24
.

The key design choice is that NMP and
\chi
EFT use the nucleonic code (hyperons do not change saturation or PNM properties), while all astrophysical constraints use the hyperonic EOS.

), maintaining a large symmetry energy and therefore yielding high proton fractions; large
y
values cause
h_{\varrho}
to drop, reducing the symmetry energy and
x_{p}
. In the nucleonic posterior
y
is essentially unconstrained:
y=0.58^{+0.37}_{-0.31}
(DDBy),
0.49^{+0.43}_{-0.32}
(DDMEy) at 90% CI. This freedom produces a broad
x_{p}
distribution whose 90% CI width at
\rho_{B}=0.8
fm
-3
reaches
\Delta x_{p}\simeq 0.09
(DDBy) and
0.10
(DDMEy), with upper bounds as high as
x_{p}\sim 0.20
–
0.22
.

When hyperons are included, the
y
posterior collapses to
y\simeq 0.47\pm 0.04
in both models: the hyperon–meson couplings, including the isovector channel, control the amount of negatively charged hyperons
\Sigma^{-}
and
\Xi^{-}
. At the same time, electrons are suppressed. As a result, the fraction of protons is controlled by charge neutrality, which is now reached with massive negatively charged baryons. The narrowing of the
y
posterior eliminates the low-
y
tail that produced the high-
x_{p}
solutions.
The net effect is a clear suppression of the proton fraction, with the median shifting by
\Delta x_{p}^{\rm med}\simeq-0.04
(DDMEy) and
-0.01
(DDBy) at
\rho_{B}=0.8
fm
-3
.

For the models without
y
(DDB, DDME, RMFNL), the
\varrho
-meson coupling is constant (RMFNL) or follows a fixed functional form — pure exponential (DDB) or rational (DDME) — with no free mixing parameter. The nucleonic
x_{p}
is consequently narrow (
\Delta x_{p}\lesssim 0.04
at
0.8
fm
-3
). When hyperons are added, the system has more freedom to attain electric neutrality. In addition, the
2\,M_{\odot}
constraint requires a stiffer EOS to compensate for the softening from additional baryonic degrees of freedom. The posterior shifts to higher incompressibility (
\Delta K_{0}\simeq+14
to
+39
MeV) and less negative symmetry energy curvature (
\Delta K_{\rm sym}\simeq+14
to
+46
MeV), while
L_{\rm sym}
changes only modestly (
\lesssim 3
MeV for DDB and RMFNL). The combined effect of the higher-order symmetry energy terms enhances the symmetry energy at supra-saturation density, which
increases

x_{p}
in beta equilibrium. This stiffening-driven increase competes with the charge-neutrality reduction from
\Sigma^{-}
and
\Xi^{-}
. In DDB and RMFNL the stiffening dominates (
\Delta x_{p}^{\rm med}\simeq+0.01
to
+0.02
), while in DDME the two effects largely cancel (
\Delta x_{p}^{\rm med}\approx 0
), because
L_{\rm sym}
decreases by
\sim 6
MeV partially offsetting the
K_{\rm sym}
increase of
+37
MeV.

The fraction of protons is smaller for the RMFNL model because it predicts the smallest amount of negatively charged hyperons. On the other hand, models DDB and DDBy predict the highest amount of negatively charged hyperons at high densities, which explains the larger proton fractions.

IV.4
Hyperon optical potentials in symmetric nuclear matter

A key observable that connects the hyperon coupling ratios to experimentally accessible quantities is the single-particle potential (optical potential) of each hyperon species in symmetric nuclear matter at saturation density:

U_{Y_{i}}(n_{0})=x_{\omega i}\!\left(\Gamma_{\omega}+\frac{\partial\Gamma_{\omega}}{\partial\rho}\,n_{0}\right)\!\omega_{0}-x_{\sigma i}\!\left(\Gamma_{\sigma}+\frac{\partial\Gamma_{\sigma}}{\partial\rho}\,n_{0}^{s}\right)\!\sigma_{0},

(17)

where
\sigma_{0}
and
\omega_{0}
are the meson mean fields in symmetric nuclear matter at
\rho=n_{0}
, and
n_{0}^{s}
is the corresponding scalar density. For the RMFNL model with constant couplings, the derivative terms vanish:

U_{Y_{i}}(n_{0})=x_{\omega i}\,g_{\omega}\,\omega_{0}-x_{\sigma i}\,g_{\sigma}\,\sigma_{0},

(18)

The optical potential
U_{Y}
encodes the net attraction or repulsion experienced by a hyperon
Y
immersed in nuclear matter at saturation and is directly constrained by hypernuclear experiments.

Figure
6
shows the posterior distributions of
U_{\Lambda}
,
U_{\Sigma}
, and
U_{\Xi}
for all five hyperonic models, computed from the full posteriors by solving symmetric nuclear matter at
\rho_{0}
for each parameter sample.

V
Discussion and Conclusions

We have performed a systematic Bayesian comparison of hyperonic NS matter across five RMF model families, all constrained by the same observational and theoretical data. Our main findings:

1.

Hyperon puzzle alleviated:
All models produce

M_{\rm max}\gtrsim 1.9\,M_{\odot}
(90% CI). The repulsive
\phi
-meson interaction plays an important role in sustaining massive hyperonic stars.

2.

Radius increase:

R_{1.4}
increases by
0.5
–
0.8
km
with hyperons, due to the stiffer low-density EOS required to
support
2\,M_{\odot}
when the high-density EOS is softened.

3.

Model dependence:
RMFNL shows the strongest effect
(
\Delta M_{\rm max}\approx-0.10\,M_{\odot}
); DDH models show

-0.05
to
-0.09\,M_{\odot}
.

4.

c_{s}^{2}
signature:
Universal softening at

2
–
3\,\rho_{0}
in all hyperonic models.

5.

NMP stability:
Saturation properties are practically insensitive
to hyperons, except for
K_{0}
and
K_{\rm sym}
that increase if hyperons are included in order to stiffen the EOS.

6.

Mass-radius slope:
A positive slope of the mass-radius curve at low mass could be an indication of the presence of hyperons inside the NS. A positive slope at
1.8\,M_{\odot}
indicates that non-nucleonic degrees of freedom should be present inside the star.

7.

Proton fraction:
The response of
x_{p}
to hyperons depends on the flexibility of the isovector channel. In models with the
y
parameter (DDBy, DDMEy), the proton fraction spans a wide range that is narrowed by hyperonic constraints, resulting in net suppression. In models without
y
(DDB, DDME, RMFNL), the EOS stiffening required to satisfy the
2\,M_{\odot}
constraint enhances the symmetry energy at high density, leading to comparable or slightly increased
x_{p}
, compared to nucleonic stars. In summary, the extra flexibility of nucleonic models is visibly suppressed by hyperons, meaning that the average proton distribution is independent of model flexibility when hyperons are included, i.e. that the proton fraction is determined by the hyperon content.
