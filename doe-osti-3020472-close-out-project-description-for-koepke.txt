Close-Out Project Description for Koepke's Dept of Energy grant DE-
SC0021405 (Decomposing Turbulence: Nonmodal vs. Modal Approach), which started on
1 September 2020

There’s no distribution limitation on this close-out report..

Overall goal, Scientific Rationale, and Fundamental question or problem to be
addressed 2020-2024:

The project objective, was to document the signatures of non-modal behavior in plasma
turbulence in relation to the inhomogeneities in plasma-flow shear and to enhance the
current understanding of the classical plasma stability and transport theory which de facto
overlooks nonmodality, was not achieved. A primary objective was to determine if transient
turbulence generation correlates with steep shear. None of the nonmodal theories had been
previously tested in magnetic equilibria. Factor-of-2 changes anticipated in the
excitation/suppression of waves and in the influence of parameter profiles.

A. Primary Objective:
To establish, for lab & space conditions, EM-IEDDI’s (electromagnetic shear-driven
instability's) dispersion relation, unstable range, instability threshold, and mode
characteristics, we need LAPD’s Alfven-wave-favorable electromagnetic-style conditions,
including higher "beta" (0.001 < beta ≤ 0.3) and low-collisionality. Also, we attempted to
intentionally launch or spontaneously destabilize compressional and shear Alfven waves
in the strong, localized, perpendicular-velocity-shear region at the interface between
coaxial plasmas (one plasma cylinder inside an outer, otherwise hollow, tube, each having
a different, controllable, value of plasma electrostatic potential, i.e., “space” potential
(not to be confused with the temperature-dependent “floating” potential of an object
immersed in the plasma).

Nonlinear wave-wave interactions between same-family (EM-IEDD or Alfven) and cross-
family (EM-IEDD-with-Alfven) fluctuations were targeted for documentation over a range of
spectral overlap.

Previous experiments conducted at WVU (beta << m_e/m_i), NRL (0.07m_e/m_i < beta <
3.7m_e/m_i), and Auburn Univ (same range as NRL) have demonstrated that electrostatic
shear-driven waves in the intermediate frequency range, having a wave vector almost
perpendicular to B, can be scattered into obliquely propagating EM waves. These lab
experiments have explored the lower-beta regime (beta < 10^-4 ) of these instabilities,
hinting the effect of beta and agreeing with theory, but LAPD’s combination of Alfven-wave-
favorable conditions, including higher beta (0.001 < beta ≤ 0.3) and low-collisionality, will
much better establish the initiation, saturation, and propagation of EM-IEDD waves for
space conditions.

B. Background, Scientific Rationale, and Fundamental question or problem being
addressed:
Inhomogeneity in particle perpendicular-velocity or parallel-velocity can contribute to
either growth or damping of various plasma modes associated with localized flow shear
and/or localized current filaments or, if sufficiently strong, can itself destabilize
instabilities. These instabilities can have significant consequences in redefining the
plasma equilibrium and can lead to energization and transport [Amatucci, 1999; Koepke et
al., 1999]. Compared to the electrostatic (ES) Current-Driven Electrostatic Ion-Cyclotron
(CDEIC) mode [Rasmussen and Schrittweiser, 1991], the electrostatic (ES)
Inhomogeneous Energy-Density-Driven (IEDD) mode [Koepke et al., 1995; Amatucci, 1998]
can be destabilized at smaller values of field-aligned drift values, can remain unstable over
a significantly wider range of temperature-ratio values (T_i/T_e) and can remain unstable in
a significantly wider range of relative concentration values of different ion species in a
multicomponent plasma. Compared to the electrostatic (ES) IEDD mode, the
electromagnetic (EM) IEDD mode [Tejero et al., 2011] requires higher plasma beta beta,
propagates in the ExB direction rather than the electron diamagnetic direction, exhibits EM
signatures over a radial extent that far exceeds the radially narrow, cylindrical-source
region, and grows faster for increasing ExB flow.

Fundamental question or problem being addressed: This project was intended to advance
the understanding and interpretation of space observations of waves able to affect broader
ionospheric and magnetospheric dynamics by heating ions that transfer energy, especially
along magnetic field lines, to far beyond the local ionospheric/magnetospheric wave-
particle interaction heating-source region. Plasma waves in the intermediate frequency
range, between the ion and electron gyrofrequencies, are important to the dynamics of
space plasmas and are vital to the evolution of the radiation belts, reconnection
processes, and boundary layers.

Scientific rationale: The term broadband extremely low frequency (BB-ELF) waves refers to
the electric-field fluctuations in the range 10 Hz to 3 kHz observed in the auroral
ionosphere by sounding rockets and satellites at altitudes in the range 850-1700 km
[Kintner et al., 1996; André et al., 1998]. Evidence suggests that these fluctuations are
gyroresonant with the unheated ionospheric ion population and are thereby responsible
for the perpendicular energization and subsequent adiabatic outflow of ions from the
auroral ionosphere into the magnetosphere [Kintner et al.,1996] and, consequently, for the
ionospheric origin of a substantial fraction of the magnetospheric plasma [Pollock et al.,
1990].

Facility novelty of existing/new LAPD capabilities needed for success: (Why LAPD?):
Extreme-length of plasma column, high-beta, low-collisionality, and excellent
reproducibility. Previous IEDD experiments (non-LAPD) were conducted at β ≤ 0.00005.
LAPD can now achieve 0.001 < β ≤ 0.3. Necessary plasma parameters: 2x10^12 < n <
3x10^13 , Helium plasma 0.001 < β ≤ 0.3, 500 G < B < 2500 G, 1 eV < Te < 10 eV, 0.1 eV < Ti
< 5 eV.

C. Timetable of activities, milestones, and deliverables:

Step #0: In advance of the 18-24 October 2022 experiments that were conducted,
analytical theory were to be examined, used to compare with and guide the experiment,
and relied upon for the interpretation of the plasma physics and the ramifications of the
observed space-related processes.

Step #1 (weeks #1,#2): Utilize both LaB6 sources to achieve the newly standard highest-
beta LAPD plasma and to form a coaxial annular interface having controllable ExB-drifting
in the presence of perpendicular-velocity shear. Optimize plasma conditions for opposing
LaB6 sources.

Step #2 (weeks #3,#4): Launch propagating Alfven wave into sheared plasma. Observe
propagating EM-IEDD wave & propagating Alfven wave interacting. This milestone was
never delivered, but attempts continued until reserved run-time was exhausted.

Step #3 (weeks #5,#6): Ensure high-beta experimental objectives have been
accomplished. Observe EM-IEDD wave at lower density and observe at higher magnetic
field (both conditions represent lower beta) in order to document beta-dependence of EM-
IEDDI dispersion relation. This milestone was never delivered.

Not Delivered: Frequency spectra, phase velocities, excitation thresholds, and ion heating,
associated with IEDD unstable, multi-harmonic, EMIC (electromagnetic ion-cyclotron)
waves were not observed and thus were not documented.

We strove to see (a) how EM-IEDDI shows ES-IEDDI-analogous plots (time series, spectral
location and breadth, radial profile location and breadth of wave amplitude, and
beta/FAC/shear sensitivity of the instability threshold) for, at least, theory and, eventually,
experiment and simulation, (b) if ES-IEDDI could manifest itself in LAPD, and (c) whether
the effects of electron temperature, neutral collisions, magnitude of ExB-speed difference
[i.e., "inhomogeneity"], and derivative of ExB-speed magnitude [i.e., "shear"] could impact
the EM-IEDD wave properties.

Anticipated, but unseen, outcome: The abruptly inhomogeneous interface between coaxial
plasma columns having different plasma beta, density, temperature, potential, and
diameters did generate a localized, transverse, dc electric field E(r); but not strong enough
to form a strong ExB flow inhomogeneity which would have been responsible for triggering
intense, broadband, intermediate-frequency waves that had been expected to propagate
in many directions well beyond the source region. As beta increases, the EMIC signatures
and the radial extent of the wave-function had been predicted to increase, manifesting very
differently compared to the electrostatic limit of the IEDD instability. Spectral broadening
of over 100% and transverse ion heating of factors of 2–4 have been previously observed as
a result of these waves in the electrostatic limit and we had expected to document much
higher factors in the series of LAPD experiments.

D. Research methods aimed to address hypotheses:
Diagnostics:

B(r,t) was measured, using a 3-axis magnetic-flux probe, to characterize the wave modes
and their evolution in terms of magnetic-field amplitude, wave vector, and the wave
frequency.

T_i and T_e were measured using swept Langmuir probes.

Mach probes measured ion-flow profile (ExB flow).
