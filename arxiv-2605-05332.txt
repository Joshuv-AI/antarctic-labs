The Heegaard Floer d-invariant for more rational homology spheres
Isabella Khan

Abstract
. The Heegaard Floer d d -invariant for a rational homology sphere Y Y and spin c -structure 𝔰 \mathfrak{s} is defined as the minimal absolute grading of a generator of H ​ F + ​ ( Y , 𝔰 ) HF^{+}(Y;\mathfrak{s}) . In 2005, Némethi used lattice homology to compute the d-invariant for a particular class of negative-definite plumbed rational homology spheres, and conjectured that his formula should hold for all negative-definite plumbed rational homology spheres. In this paper, we use Zemke’s isomorphism between lattice and Heegaard Floer homology to prove Némethi’s conjecture.

1.
Introduction

Heegaard Floer homology is a powerful 3-manifold invariant first constructed by Ozsváth and Szábo in
[
14
]
. To any closed 3-manifold
Y
, the Heegaard Floer package associates a certain type of module
\mathrm{HF}^{\circ}(Y)
; while the type of module varies based on the particular flavor of Heegaard Floer homology which is being computed, each flavor splits over the spin
c
-structures of
Y
, so that
\mathrm{HF}^{\circ}(Y)
is a direct sum of submodules
\mathrm{HF}^{\circ}(Y;\mathfrak{s})
for each
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathrm{Spin}^{c}(Y)
. When the spin
c
-structure
\mathfrak{s}
is torsion,
\mathrm{HF}^{\circ}(Y;\mathfrak{s})
admits an absolute
\mathbb{Q}
-grading,
\widetilde{\mathrm{gr}}
. In this paper, we concentrate on rational homology spheres, on which all spin
c
-structures are torsion, so that we can define
\widetilde{\mathrm{gr}}
on
\mathrm{HF}^{\circ}(Y;\mathfrak{s})
for each spin
c
-structure of
Y
. In these cases, we have a fundamental invariant of the 3-manifold
Y
called the
Heegaard Floer
d
-invariant
. For each (torsion)
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathrm{Spin}^{c}(Y)
,
d(Y,\mathfrak{s})
is the minimal absolute grading of a non-torsion element of
HF^{+}(Y;\mathfrak{s})
. We can also define
d(Y,\mathfrak{s})
as the maximal grading of a non-torsion element of
\mathrm{HF}^{-}(Y;\mathfrak{s})
, which is the approach we will use in this paper.

In 2005, Némethi used lattice homology to prove in
[
9
]
that for certain rational homology spheres
Y
– those which admitted a particular kind of negative-definite plumbing called an
almost rational plumbing
— the
d
-invariant can be computed for each
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathrm{Spin}^{c}(Y)
, as:

(1)

d(Y,\mathfrak{s})=\max_{k\in\mathfrak{s}}\frac{k^{2}+|V|}{4}

where
|V|
is the number of vertices of the plumbing graph,
k
ranges across the characteristic vectors of the associated 4-manifold
X
which represent
\mathfrak{s}
, and the “
2
” in
k^{2}
indicates the intersection form of
X
. See Theorem 8.3 and Proposition 4.7 of
[
9
]
for an exact statement of this result.

In
[
10
]
, Némethi conjectured further that (
1
) should hold for all negative-definite plumbed rational homology spheres. In this paper, we verify Némethi’s conjecture. The main result is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Y
be a negative-definite plumbed 3-manifold which is a rational homology sphere, and let
[k]
be a spin
c
structure for
Y
. Then

(2)

d(Y,[k])=\max_{k^{\prime}\in[k]}\frac{(k^{\prime})^{2}+s}{4},

where
s
denotes the number of vertices of the plumbing graph for
Y
, and
(k^{\prime})^{2}=(k^{\prime})^{T}M(k^{\prime})
, where
M
is the plumbing matrix corresponding to
Y
.

This result is interesting for several reasons. First, the
d
-invariant is relevant on its own merits as a topological invariant of rational homology spheres. Also, given the work of Kutluhan-Lee-Taubes in
[
5
]
(and its sequels) identifying the Heegaard Floer invariants with Seiberg-Witten invariants, the
d
-invariant should coincide with the Frøyshov
h
-invariant, so Theorem
1.1
gives new information about the Frøyshov invariant as well.

The author’s particular interest in the
d
-invariant stems from quantum topology, specifically, the conjectural connections between the Gukov-Pei-Putrov-Vafa (GPPV) invariant and Floer theory. The GPPV invariant associates to a 3-manifold
Y
, with spin
c
-structure
[k]
, a
q
-series
\hat{Z}_{[k]}
, which Murakami showed in
[
8
]
converges to the Witten-Reshitikhin-Turaev invariants in a certain sense. The
\hat{Z}
-invariant was computed Gukov-Manolescu for Brieskorn spheres in
[
3
]
. In
[
1
]
, Akhmechet-Johnson-Krushkal used Némethi’s graded root construction from lattice homology to give an algorithm for computing
\hat{Z}
for negative-definite plumbed 3-manifolds, and in
[
6
]
, Liles used this algorithm to compute the
\hat{Z}
for Brieskorn spheres.

Given the existing connections between lattice homology and the
\hat{Z}
-invariant, as well as Zemke’s isomorphism between Heegaard Floer and lattice homology from
[
18
]
, it seems plausible to expect some meaningful connection between Heegaard Floer theory and the
\hat{Z}
-series. The
d
-invariant could provide one basis for such a connection: the minimal
q
-degree of terms in the
\hat{Z}
-series is a rational number usually denoted
\Delta
in the literature, which was conjectured in
[
1
]
to be equal to the
d
-invariant for rational homology spheres. In
[
4
]
, Harichurn-Némethi-Svoboda show that this conjecture is false; however, the broader question of whether, and how, the
d
-invariant and
\Delta
might in general be related, remains open. The author hopes that this new, calculation of the
d
-invariant might provide some insight into this question.

This paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we give review terminology for plumbed 3-manifolds, and establish notation which will be used in the rest of the paper. In Section
3
, we give a brief introduction to the Heegaard Floer invariants, and in Section
4
, we give the definition of Manolescu-Ozsváth’s link surgery complex of
[
7
]
, the reformulation of Heegaard Floer homology which will be used in proving Theorem
1.1
. In Section
5
, we define lattice homology and discuss the various results connecting lattice homology with Heegaard Floer homology, from
[
11
]
and
[
18
]
. Finally, in Section
6
, we give a proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Plumbed 3-manifolds

All manifolds considered in this paper will be negative-definite plumbed 3-manifolds. In this section, we therefore provide a brief review of definitions and terminology for these manifolds. A more complete exposition of this topic can be found in any number of sources, for instance
[
2
]
.

The data for a plumbed manifold is a
plumbing graph

G
which has finitely many vertices and no cycles. In this paper, we will also assume all graphs are connected. We write
V(G)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}\}
to denote the vertices of
G
, and
E(G)
to denote the edges of
G
; we say that
[v_{i},v_{j}]\in E(G)
if and only if
v_{i}
,
v_{j}
are connected by an edge of
G
. Each vertex
v_{i}\in V(G)
is decorated with an integer weight
m_{i}
; we write
\vec{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{s})
for this weight vector. Later, we will usually suppress the
G
from the notation for
V(G)
and
E(G)
. The plumbing graph and weight vector naturally determine a framed link of unknots
L=\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}K_{i}\subseteq S^{3}
, with framing given by
\vec{m}
, which we call the
plumbing link
. Define the
plumbing matrix

M=(a_{ij})
corresponding to
G
as

a_{ij}=\begin{cases}m_{i}&\text{ if }i=j;\\
1&\text{ if }i\neq j\text{ and }[v_{i},v_{j}]\in E(G);\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

To obtain a 3-manifold
Y
from this set-up, we do
\vec{m}
-framed Dehn surgery along
L\subseteq S^{3}
. The corresponding 4-manifold
X
is constructed by attaching an
m_{i}
-framed
2
-handle to
B^{4}
along each component
K_{i}
of
L
. Then we have
Y=\partial X
.

Next, we discuss characteristic vectors and spin
c
structures. There exists a canonical basis
\{[v_{i}]\}_{i=1}^{s}
for
H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})
. When
b_{1}(Y)=0
, we have
H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathrm{Hom}(H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Q}),\mathbb{Q})
, so we have a dual basis
\{[v_{i}]^{*}\}_{i=1}^{s}
for
H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})
. In this case, we write

k\cdot x=\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}x_{i},

for each
k=\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}[v_{i}]^{*}\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})
and
x=\sum_{i=1}^{s}x_{i}[v_{i}]\in H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})
. We write
(x,y)=x^{T}My
in this basis, so that
M
determines the intersection form of
X
. We will often write
x^{2}
for
(x,x)
, as we do in the statement of Theorem
1.1
. The 3-manifold
Y
is a
negative definite
plumbed 3-manifold precisely when
M
determines a negative definite intersection form on
X
. The
characteristic vectors of
X
are defined as

\mathrm{Char}(X):=\{k\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q}):k\cdot x+(x,x)=0\text{ for each }x\in H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})\}.

Notice that in the canonical basis
\{[v_{i}]^{*}\}
for
H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})
, we have

\mathrm{Char}(X)\cong\vec{m}+2\mathbb{Z}^{s}.

Recall that taking the first Chern class defines a bijection
c_{1}:\mathrm{Spin}^{c}(X)\to\mathrm{Char}(X)
. When we restrict our spin
c
structures to
Y
, this determines an isomorphism

(3)

\mathrm{Spin}^{c}(Y)\cong\frac{\mathrm{Char}(X)}{2M\mathbb{Z}^{s}}=\frac{\vec{m}+2\mathbb{Z}^{s}}{2M\mathbb{Z}^{s}}

in the canonical basis; that is
k,k^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Char}(X)
are representatives of the same element of
\mathrm{Spin}^{c}(Y)
if and only if
k-k^{\prime}\in 2M\mathbb{Z}^{s}
in the canonical basis.

3.
Heegaard Floer homology

Heegaard Floer homology is an invariant of closed 3-manifolds with wide applications to problems in low-dimensional topology. The goal in this section is not to give a full introduction either to the construction or the applications of this invariant, but to provide just enough information to understand the sections that follow and the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The reader interested in a more thorough treatment is directed to
[
14
]
,
[
13
]
, or
[
15
]
, among many excellent sources.

3.1.
Definitions

The Heegaard Floer homology of a closed 3-manifold
Y
is constructed as follows. A
Heegaard diagram

(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta})
consists of a genus
g
surface
\Sigma
and families
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}\}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}=\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{g}\}
of closed curves in
\Sigma
, such that
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\cup\boldsymbol{\beta}
generates
H_{1}(\Sigma)
. Let
U_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
denote the handlebody with boundary
\Sigma
determined by attaching 2-handles along the
\alpha
-circles, and
U_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
denote the handlebody, also with boundary
\Sigma
, determined by attaching 2-handles along the
\beta
-circles. We also choose a basepoint
z\in\Sigma
in the complement of the
\alpha
- and
\beta
-arcs. Then
(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},z)
is a
Heegaard splitting
of
Y
if
Y=U_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\cup_{\Sigma}U_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
; it is a well known fact that every closed 3-manifold
Y
admits a Heegaard splitting.

Given a Heegaard splitting
(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},z)
for
Y
, let
\mathbb{T}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
and
\mathbb{T}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
denote the
g
-dimensional Lagrangian tori in the
2g
-dimensional symplectic manifold
\mathrm{Sym}^{g}(\Sigma)
. Then
\mathrm{CF}^{\circ}(Y)
is generated by intersection points
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
. (The “
\circ
” in
\mathrm{CF}^{\circ}
denotes one of the flavors of Heegaard Floer homology, which we describe in slightly more detail in the next paragraph.) Letting
\pi_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
denote the set of holomorphic disks in
\Sigma
with boundary along
\mathbb{T}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\cup\mathbb{T}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
and corners at
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
, the differential counts rigid moduli spaces of holomorphic disks
\pi_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
. More specifically, Heegaard Floer homology is equipped with a relative
\mathbb{Z}
-grading
\mu
, called the
Maslov grading
, defined by assigning a
\mathbb{Z}
-value to each element of
\pi_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
and extended as a relative grading to generators as

\mu(\mathbf{y})-\mu(\mathbf{x})=\mu(\phi)

for each
\phi\in\pi_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
.

There are several flavors of Heegaard Floer homology –
\widehat{\mathrm{HF}}
,
\mathrm{HF}^{+}
,
\mathrm{HF}^{-}
,
\mathrm{HF}^{\infty}
, and
\mathbf{HF}^{-}
– which are determined by the ground ring and number of base-points in
\Sigma\smallsetminus(\bigcup_{i}\alpha_{i})\cup(\bigcup_{i}\beta_{i})
involved in the construction. Here and in what follows, we will be using
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-coefficients, so
\mathbb{F}
will always denote
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. All flavors of Heegaard Floer homology are generated by the intersections
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
in
\mathrm{Sym}^{g}(\Sigma)
. Having said this,
\mathrm{CF}^{\infty}(Y)
is generated over
\mathbb{F}[U,U^{-1}]
, so that generators are written as
[\mathbf{x},i]
, where
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, and the
U
-action raises
i
by
1
and
U^{-1}
-action drops
i
by 1. The complex
\mathrm{CF}^{-}(Y)
is generated over
\mathbb{F}[U]
by
[\mathbf{x},i]
with
i<0
and the stipulation that
U\cdot[\mathbf{x},0]=0
for each
\mathbf{x}
;
\mathrm{CF}^{+}(Y)
is generated over
\mathbb{F}[U]
by
[\mathbf{x},i]
with
i>0
, and
\widehat{\mathrm{HF}}(Y)
is generated over
\mathbb{Z}
by
[\mathbf{x},0]
. Finally,
\mathbf{CF}^{-}(Y)
is the completion of
\mathrm{CF}^{-}(Y)
over ground ring
\mathbb{F}[[U]]
. The reader interested in a more thorough discussion of the basepoints how they affect the differentials in these various cases is directed to e.g.
[
15
]
.

All flavors of Heegaard Floer homology split over spin
c
-structures of
Y
, as

\mathrm{HF}^{\circ}(Y)=\bigoplus_{[k]\in\mathrm{Spin}^{c}(Y)}\mathrm{HF}(Y;[k]).

In this paper, we will primarily be concerned with the completed version
\mathbf{HF}^{-}(Y)
, which appears in Zemke’s isomorphism with lattice homology.

3.2.
Cobordisms and gradings

If our 3-manifold
Y
is equipped with a torsion spin
c
-structure
\mathfrak{s}
, then there is also an absolute
\mathbb{Q}
-grading
\widetilde{\mathrm{gr}}
which lifts the relative
\mathbb{Z}
-grading
\mu
in the sense that for any pair of generators
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}
and
\phi\in\pi_{2}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
,

\widetilde{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathbf{y})-\widetilde{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathbf{x})=\mu(\phi).

This grading is constructed manually on
\mathrm{HF}^{\infty}(S^{3},\mathfrak{s}_{0})
in
[
16
]
(where
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
denotes the canonical spin
c
-structure on
S^{3}
), and has the property that the minimal grading element of
HF^{+}(Y;\mathfrak{s})
has
\widetilde{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathbf{x}_{0})=0
. For an arbitrary closed 3-manifold
Y
with torsion spin
c
-structure
\mathfrak{s}
, the absolute grading on
\mathrm{HF}^{\infty}(Y;\mathfrak{s})
is constructed using the grading on
\mathrm{HF}^{\infty}(S^{3})
, together with
cobordism maps
for Heegaard Floer homology. In order to discuss the absolute grading in more detail in what follows, we now give an overview of the construction of these maps. In this paper, we will only be interested in cobordisms between
S^{3}
and a closed manifold
Y
, so we describe only those maps here.

Any 3-manifold
Y
can be obtained via Dehn surgery along some framed link in
S^{3}
. For a link

,
a bouquet
B(L)
for the link
L
is a 1-complex in
S^{3}
which is the union of
L
with a collection of paths joining each component to a particular basepoint in
S^{3}
. Let

be a link in
S^{3}
with framing
\vec{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{s})
, such that
Y
is obtained by
\vec{m}
-framed Dehn surgery along

. A

is a triple
(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\gamma},z)
, with
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g},\boldsymbol{\beta}=\{\beta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g},
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\{\gamma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g}

\Sigma
and

in the complement of the
\alpha
-,
\beta

\gamma

•

(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta})

;

•

\gamma_{s+1},\ldots\gamma_{g}
are small isotopies of the

•

For each

•

\displaystyle Y_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=S^{3}

\displaystyle=\#^{g-s}(S^{1}\times S^{2})

\displaystyle Y_{\alpha\gamma}

Let

(4)

4.
The link surgery formula

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we will use an identification between a reformulation of Heegaard Floer homology due to Manolescu and Ozsváth, called the
link surgery complex
, and lattice homology. This identification will be discussed below, in Section

, and in order to understand it, we need to give the definition of the link surgery complex here.

The link surgery complex is a reformulation of Heegaard Floer homology defined in the case of plumbed 3-manifolds. For more details beyond the treatment given here, see
[
7
]
, in which the link surgery complex was originally defined, or
[
11
]
or
[
17
]
for slightly later discussions.

We first need to give the definitions of several types of Heegaard diagrams for links. This treatment follows Section 3.1 of
[
7
]
. First, a
multipointed Heegaard diagram
is
\mathcal{H}=(\Sigma,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})
such that

•

(Surface)
\Sigma
is a genus
g
surface;

•

(Circles) For some
k\geq 0
,
\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}
are each collections of
(g+k-1)
simple closed curves spanning a
g
-dimensional subspace of
H_{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})
;

•

(Basepoints) Let
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
and
\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be the connected components of
\Sigma\smallsetminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{g+k-1}\alpha_{i}
and
\Sigma\smallsetminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{g+k-1}\beta_{i}
, respectively. Then for some
m\leq k
, the basepoints are
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k})
and
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})
, with the property that there is some permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\ldots,m\}
such that

\displaystyle w_{i}\in A_{i}\cap B_{i}

\displaystyle\text{ for each }1\leq i\leq k

\displaystyle z_{i}\in A_{i}\cap B_{\sigma(i)}

\displaystyle\text{ for each }1\leq i\leq m

Any multi-pointed diagram
\mathcal{H}
specifies an oriented link
\vec{L}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\vec{K}_{i}\subseteq S^{3}
according to the following procedure. Let
U_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
and
U_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
denote the
\alpha
-and
\beta
-handlebodies discussed at the beginning of Section
3
. Then for each
1\leq i\leq m
, join
w_{i}
to
z_{i}
via an arc in
A_{i}

The link surgery complex is constructed in the following way. Let
Y
be a plumbed 3-manifold with corresponding plumbing link

\mathbb{H}(L)=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\mathrm{lk}(L\smallsetminus K_{i},L)}{2}+\mathbb{Z}\right).

The link surgery complex is defined as the direct sum over sublinks
M
of

\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}(L)=\bigoplus_{M\subseteq L}\prod_{\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbb{H}(L)}\mathfrak{A}^{-}(\mathcal{H}^{L\smallsetminus M},\psi^{M}(s)),

where
\mathcal{H}^{L\smallsetminus M}

\mathcal{H}^{L}
by deleting the basepoint

for each component

which is included in M, and

is a restriction map which deletes the components of
\mathfrak{s}
corresponding to components of
M
. The space
\mathfrak{A}^{-}(\mathcal{H}^{L\smallsetminus M},\psi^{M}(s))
is defined as the
\mathbb{F}[[U_{1},\ldots,U_{\ell}]]
-module generated by terms of the form

U_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots U_{\ell}^{i_{\ell}}\cdot\mathbf{x},

where
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
is a generator of the link Floer complex,
i_{j}\geq 0
for each
j
, we place a further lower bounds on each

based on the component

of

. For more details on the exact conditions, see e.g. Section of
[

or Section 6.1 of
[

have the same action, so we can view

The complex

\mathcal{D}=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}^{r},

where

r

(\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}(L),\mathcal{D}^{0})
denote the chain complex obtained by applying only the length-0 hypercube map on the link surgery complex, and let
H_{*}(\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}(L))
denote the homology of this chain complex. In Section

-module isomorphism between the chain complex

[Special case of Theorem 9.6 of

Moreover the authors of

Remark 4.2
.

For a rational homology sphere, are two ways to view the absolute grading on the link surgery formula. The first is by noting that all generators
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}(L)
are elements of

The other way to view the grading on

5.
Lattice homology

The goal of this section is to give the definitions from lattice homology sufficient to understand the main result of
[
11
]
(Theorem 1.1 part 4 and Propositions 4.4 and 4.8 of
[
11

5.1.
Definitions

Lattice homology is an invariant of negative definite plumbed 3-manifolds originally defined by Némethi in
[
9
]
, to give a purely combinatorial means of computing Heegaard Floer homology. For such a 3-manifold
Y
, the invariant
\mathbb{HF}^{*}(Y)
is a hypercube of
\mathbb{F}[U]
-chain complexes equipped with a filtration. In Theorem 8.3 of
[
9
]
, Némethi proves that for
Y
equipped with a particular type of plumbing graph called an
almost rational plumbing
, the zero-th degree of the lattice homology complex
\mathbb{HF}^{0}(Y)
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{HF}^{+}(Y)
; the result result (
1
) calculating the
d
-invariant for almost rational plumbings follows as a corollary.

In
[
11
]
, the authors use a different version of the lattice homology complex, which is equivalent to Némethi’s original complex when
Y
is a negative-definite plumbed 3-manifold. It is this reformulated version we will use here. Let
Y
be a plumbed 3-manifold, with all notation as in Section
2
. In the conventions of
[
11
]
, the lattice homology of
Y
is a hypercube of chain complexes
\mathbb{CF}^{-}(Y)
which splits over spin
c
-structures, as

\mathbb{CF}^{-}(Y)=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{s}\in\mathrm{Spin}^{c}(Y)}\mathbb{CF}^{-}(Y;\mathfrak{s}).

The generators of
\mathbb{CF}(Y)
over
\mathbb{F}[[U]]
are pairs
[K,E]
, where
K\in\mathrm{Char}(X)
and
E\in P(V)
, that is,
E
is some subset of the set of vertices of the plumbing graph
G
. . The complex has an filtration
\delta
given by
\delta([K,E])=|E|
, the number of elements in
E
. One should think of each
E\subseteq V
as a sublink
M
of the plumbing link
L
, analogous to the filtration for the link surgery complex above. The generator
[K,E]
is in spin
c
-structure
\mathfrak{s}
precisely when
K
is a representative of
\mathfrak{s}
according to the isomorphism (
3
).

This complex is equipped with hypercube maps and a differential, but in this paper, we will only be concerned with generators of
\mathbb{CF}^{-}(Y)
, so we will not discuss the formal definitions of these maps here. The interested reader may consult
[
9
]
for the original formulation,
[
11
]
for the reformulation, or Zemke’s paper
[
18
]
for a more recent discussion. It is also possible to define other flavors of lattice homology, particularly
\mathbb{CF}^{+}
or
\mathbb{CF}^{\infty}
, by a construction analogous to the one for Heegaard Floer homology. See e.g. Remark 3.4 of
[
11
]
, for more details. However, we are only concerned with the “
-
” version of lattice homology here.

When we are dealing with a torsion spin
c
-structure
\mathfrak{s}
, the lattice complex is also equipped with an absolute
\mathbb{Q}
-grading, which is defined in the following way. First, for a given
I\in P(V)
, define

2f(K,I)=\sum_{v\in I}K(v)+\sum_{v,v^{\prime}\in I}v\cdot v^{\prime},

where in the first sum, we are viewing
K
as an element of
\mathrm{Hom}(H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z})
, and evaluating on

, and in the second, the dot product denotes the intersection form. Since
K

is clearly an integer for each

the minimal G-weight
of a generator

of
\mathbb{CF}^{-}(Y)
as

g([K,E])=\min_{I\subseteq E}f(K,I)

Then we define the absolute Maslov grading

as

(9)

\mathrm{gr}(U^{i}\otimes[K,E])=-2i+2g([K,E])+|E|+\frac{K^{2}-2\chi(W)-3\sigma(W)}{4},

where

denotes the cobordism between

and

obtained by cutting out a small

from

Lemma 5.1

When
Y
is a negative-definite plumbed homology sphere with all notation as above, then

\mathrm{gr}([K,\varnothing])=\frac{K^{2}-2\chi(W)-3\sigma(W)}{4}=\frac{K^{2}+s}{4}.

6.
Proof of the main result

In this section, we give a proof of Theorem
1.1
. This proof consists of a detailed analysis of the grading shifts in the isomorphisms from Section
5.2
.

Proof.

Let
[k]
be a spin
c
structure of the negative definite plumbed 3-manifold
Y
, which we are assuming is a rational homology sphere. The goal here is to show that the maximal grading of a torsion-free element of
\mathbf{HF}^{-}(Y;[k])
is

(10)

\max_{K\in[k]}\frac{K^{2}+s}{4}

where all the
K
are characteristic vectors of
X
, and as usual,
s
is the number of vertices of the plumbing graph for
Y
.

First, look at
\mathbb{HF}^{-}(Y;[k])
. There is a single non-torsion module
\mathbb{F}[[U]]
in
\mathbb{HF}^{-}(Y;[k])
, which lives in
\delta
-filtration zero. (See e.g. the discussion following the statement of Theorem 2.8 in
[
12
]
for a proof of this fact.) This means that non-torsion generators of
\mathbb{HF}^{-}(Y,[k])
will all be of the form
[K,\varnothing]
where
K\in\mathrm{Char}(X)
is a representative of
[k]
. By Lemma
5.1
, the maximal degree of a non-torsion element of
\mathbb{HF}^{-}(Y;[k])
is as in (
10
).

As noted in Lemma
5.7
, the combination of Proposition
5.2
and Proposition
5.6
gives us an identification between elements of
\mathbb{C}_{\Lambda}(L,[k])
which descend to generators of
\mathbf{HF}^{-}(Y;[k])
on homology, and elements of
\mathbb{CF}^{-}(Y;[k])
which descend to generators of
\mathbf{HF}^{-}(Y;[k])
on homology. This identification respects spin
c
structures and preserves absolute gradings. In particular, the induced map on homology identifies non-torsion elements of
\mathbf{HF}^{-}(Y;[k])
with non-torsion elements of
\mathbb{HF}^{-}(Y;[k])
in a grading preserving way.

This means that any maximal degree non-torsion element of
\mathbf{HF}^{-}(Y;[k])
maps to a maximal degree non-torsion element of
\mathbb{HF}^{-}(Y;[k])
, and has the same grading. This means that the main result now follows from Lemma
5.1
and the previous paragraphs.
