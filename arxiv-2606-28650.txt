1.
Introduction

How much energy does it cost to steer a distant site of a quasiperiodic chain from its boundary, and which
spectral quantity sets the exponential rate of that cost in the distance? On a one-dimensional quasiperiodic
chain the question has a sharp answer, and this paper gives it.

The chain carries the Aubry–André (almost Mathieu) operator
H_{\lambda}
, with quasiperiodic potential

\lambda\cos(2\pi\beta j+\phi)
at a Diophantine frequency
\beta
(the golden mean
(\sqrt{5}-1)/2
in the numerics). It is a model with a fully understood
metal–insulator transition: for
\lambda<2
the spectrum is purely absolutely continuous, for
\lambda>2

it is pure point with eigenfunctions localized at rate
\log(\lambda/2)
, and
\lambda=2
is the self-dual
critical coupling
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. Throughout the spectrum the Lyapunov
exponent of the transfer cocycle equals
\log_{+}(\lambda/2)
. The control quantity is the least energy

\mathcal{E}_{N}=e_{N}^{\top}W_{\alpha}^{-1}e_{N}
steering the far unit at distance
N
, where
W_{\alpha}
is the
controllability Gramian of the chain shifted by a closed-loop margin
\alpha
, and its exponential rate is

r(\alpha,\lambda)=\liminf_{N}\frac{1}{2N}\log\mathcal{E}_{N}
. This control system is linear, and it is the local model for
remote stabilization of a
nonlinear
chain: at an operating point where a nonlinear lattice linearizes to the
shifted operator
A_{\alpha}=E_{0}I-H_{\lambda}
(a hyperbolic equilibrium once the margin
\alpha>0
is imposed), the
least energy of a small boundary maneuver steering the far site is, to leading order in the maneuver amplitude, the
linear cost
\mathcal{E}_{N}
, so
r(\alpha,\lambda)
is the cost rate of local nonlinear remote stabilization. We work with
the linear cost throughout.

A natural guess takes
r
to be the localization rate, or the off-spectral Lyapunov exponent

\gamma_{\lambda}(E_{\min}-\alpha)
at the band margin. Neither is correct: already the free chain
\lambda=0
, where
there is no localization at all, has cost rate
\arccosh(3+\alpha)
, several times larger than that margin
exponent. The mechanism is different:
\mathcal{E}_{N}
is governed by the smallest eigenvalues of an exponentially
ill-conditioned controllability Gramian, not by a single resolvent column, and the resolvent decay does not see
those eigenvalues.

This paper identifies what does set the rate and, at every Diophantine frequency and almost every phase, establishes the identity throughout
the localized regime. Lemma
3.1
expresses
\mathcal{E}_{N}
as the Cauchy quadratic form

\tilde{b}^{\top}C^{-1}\tilde{b}
in the boundary-amplitude ratios, a Zolotarev-type quantity for the shifted
spectrum
[
9
]
. Its rate is the off-spectral exponent of the same cocycle at the

reflected band edge

z^{\star}=2E_{\min}-2\alpha-E_{\max}

4.2
pins

in the bracket

g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})\leq r\leq\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})

\log_{+}(\lambda/2)

\lambda\leq 2

\max(1,\lambda/2)

The identity

Three results, and then a cocycle form of the cost, close the identity. A

The cost as a Cauchy quadratic form

The cost (

Lemma 3.1

(Exact eigenbasis reduction)
.

With
C_{kl}=(\delta_{k}+\delta_{l})^{-1}
the symmetric positive definite Cauchy matrix of the shifted
eigenvalues,
a_{k}\neq 0
for all
k
and

(8)

\mathcal{E}_{N}=\tilde{b}^{\top}C^{-1}\tilde{b},\qquad\tilde{b}_{k}=\frac{b_{k}}{a_{k}}=\frac{\psi^{(k)}_{N}}{\psi^{(k)}_{1}},\qquad r(\alpha,\lambda)=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{2N}\log\mathcal{E}_{N}.

Moreover
\tilde{b}_{k}=\mathcal{T}_{21}(E_{k})
, where
\mathcal{T}(E)=T_{N}(E)\cdots T_{1}(E)
and
\det\mathcal{T}\equiv 1
.

Proof.

Since
A_{\alpha}=E_{0}I-H_{\lambda}
, the eigenbasis gives
A_{\alpha}\psi^{(k)}=-\delta_{k}\psi^{(k)}
with

\delta_{k}=E_{k}-E_{0}\geq\alpha>0
, hence
e^{A_{\alpha}s}e_{1}=\sum_{k}a_{k}e^{-\delta_{k}s}\psi^{(k)}
and

W_{\alpha}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{A_{\alpha}s}e_{1}e_{1}^{\top}e^{A_{\alpha}s}\,ds=\sum_{k,l}\frac{a_{k}a_{l}}{\delta_{k}+\delta_{l}}\,\psi^{(k)}\psi^{(l)\top}.

In eigencoordinates
W_{\alpha}=D_{a}CD_{a}
with
D_{a}=\diag(a_{k})
and
C
the Cauchy matrix, positive definite
(distinct
\delta_{k}>0
). An eigenfunction with
\psi_{0}=\psi_{1}=0
vanishes identically by the three-term
recurrence, so
a_{k}\neq 0
and
W_{\alpha}\succ 0
. Writing

,

\mathcal{E}_{N}=e_{N}^{\top}W_{\alpha}^{-1}e_{N}=b^{\top}D_{a}^{-1}C^{-1}D_{a}^{-1}b=\tilde{b}^{\top}C^{-1}\tilde{b}.

For the cocycle form, a Dirichlet eigenfunction satisfies

\binom{\psi_{N+1}}{\psi_{N}}=\mathcal{T}(E_{k})\binom{\psi_{1}}{0}

\psi_{N+1}=a_{k}\mathcal{T}_{11}(E_{k})=0

∎

(Monotonicity of the exterior Green’s function)

Let

Proof.

With

[

\partial_{w}g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus K}(w)=-\int_{K}(t-w)^{-1}\,d\mu_{\mathrm{eq}}(t)<0

potential is constant q.e. on its support is the equilibrium measure of that support, the constant being

\log\operatorname{cap}
; hence
N_{\lambda}=\mu_{\mathrm{eq}}
and
\operatorname{cap}(\Sigma_{\lambda})=\max(1,\lambda/2)
. (The rational
approximants
\beta\approx p/q
each carry
\operatorname{cap}=1
, but capacity is discontinuous through this limit, so
they do not transmit it: this is the gap in the naive “unit-hopping
\Rightarrow\operatorname{cap}=1
” reading.) For every

w\notin\Sigma_{\lambda}
the Frostman representation
g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(w)=\int\log\left\lvert w-t\right\rvert\,dN_{\lambda}(t)-\log\operatorname{cap}

combined with the Thouless formula (
5
) gives

g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(w)=\gamma_{\lambda}(w)-\log_{+}(\lambda/2)
, i.e. (
9
); for
\lambda\leq 2
the subtracted term is
0
, and at
\lambda=2
both sides extend continuously to

\Sigma_{2}
, where they vanish.

(iii) Supercritical amplitudes.
For
\lambda>2
every eigenfunction is localized at rate

\log(\lambda/2)
. We stress that the individual ratios
\tilde{b}_{k}=\mathcal{T}_{21}(E_{k})=\psi^{(k)}_{N}/\psi^{(k)}_{1}

are
not
uniformly of size
e^{\log(\lambda/2)N}
: a mode localized at the left end
(
\psi^{(k)}_{1}=O(1)
,
\psi^{(k)}_{N}=O(e^{-\log(\lambda/2)N})
) has
\left\lvert\tilde{b}_{k}\right\rvert
exponentially

small
, a centre mode has
\left\lvert\tilde{b}_{k}\right\rvert=e^{o(N)}
, and only a right-end mode
(
\psi^{(k)}_{1}=O(e^{-\log(\lambda/2)N})
,
\psi^{(k)}_{N}=O(1)
) has
\left\lvert\tilde{b}_{k}\right\rvert=e^{\log(\lambda/2)N(1+o(1))}
;
this is quantified mode-by-mode in Corollary
9.5
. Accordingly (
11
) is a
statement about the
maximum
, equivalently the rate of the
norm
, not about each coefficient.
Since
E_{k}\in\Sigma_{\lambda}
every ratio obeys the uniform upper bound

\left\lvert\tilde{b}_{k}\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{T}(E_{k})\right\rVert=e^{\log(\lambda/2)N(1+o(1))}
, and this bound is saturated by the
right-end (far-localized) modes. Hence

\frac{1}{2N}\log\left\lVert\tilde{b}\right\rVert^{2}=\frac{1}{N}\log\max_{k}\left\lvert\tilde{b}_{k}\right\rvert+o(1)\to\log(\lambda/2)
, which is precisely
(
11
). With the singular-value rate of (i)—equivalently, and unconditionally, the
boundary-weight bound
\left\lvert c_{k}\right\rvert\leq Q(\delta_{k})
of Lemmas
9.3
–
9.4
that closes
Theorem
13.9
—this gives

r\leq g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})+\log(\lambda/2)=\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})
, completing
(
10
). The high-precision evaluation of §
12
places
r
at the upper end for

\lambda>0
, but a proof for the quasiperiodic spectrum is the problem this paper resolves for almost every phase (Theorem
13.9
).
∎

Proposition 4.3

(Exact free-chain cost rate)
.

For
\lambda=0
and
\alpha>0
, with
\delta_{k}=\alpha+4\sin^{2}\frac{k\pi}{2(N+1)}
and

\tilde{b}_{k}=(-1)^{k+1}
,

r(\alpha,0)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{2N}\log\mathcal{E}_{N}=\arccosh(3+\alpha)=\gamma_{0}(z^{\star}),

strictly above the margin exponent
\gamma_{0}(E_{\min}-\alpha)=\arccosh(1+\tfrac{\alpha}{2})
(e.g.
1.7975

versus
0.3149
at
\alpha=0.1
): the resolvent rate is not the cost rate even without disorder.

Proof.

The nodes
\delta_{k}=\alpha+4\sin^{2}\theta_{k}
,
\theta_{k}=\frac{k\pi}{2(N+1)}
, equidistribute to the arcsine
equilibrium measure
\nu
of
J=[\alpha,\alpha+4]
, of capacity
\operatorname{cap}(J)=1
. As a totally positive Cauchy
matrix
C
has (Gantmacher–Krein
[
13
]
) a fully alternating smallest-eigenvalue eigenvector
u_{N}
; since

\tilde{b}_{k}=(-1)^{k+1}
shares that pattern,
\langle\tilde{b},u_{N}\rangle^{2}=\left\lVert u_{N}\right\rVert_{1}^{2}\geq\left\lVert u_{N}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=1
,
and with
C^{-1}\preceq\sigma_{\min}(C)^{-1}I
,

\sigma_{\min}(C)^{-1}\ \leq\ \mathcal{E}_{N}\ \leq\ N\,\sigma_{\min}(C)^{-1},

both carrying the rate
\frac{1}{2N}\log\sigma_{\min}(C)^{-1}\to\max_{s\in J}U(s)
. As
\operatorname{cap}(J)=1
the
subtracted potential vanishes and
U(s)=g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus J}(-s)=\arccosh\frac{s+\alpha+2}{2}
,
increasing on
J
, maximal at
s=\alpha+4
, giving
r(\alpha,0)=\arccosh(\alpha+3)
. As

\operatorname{cap}(\Sigma_{0})=1
, this equals
\gamma_{0}(z^{\star})
by (
9
).
∎

5.
The mode-dominant lower bound

A single coefficient of the Cauchy form already carries the localization rate: a diagonal
Cauchy–Schwarz bound on
\mathcal{E}_{N}=\tilde{b}^{\top}C^{-1}\tilde{b}
isolates one
\tilde{b}_{k}
, whose
boundary-amplitude growth gives the lower end of the bracket.

Proposition 5.1

(Mode-dominant lower bound)
.

For every
\lambda\geq 0
and every
\alpha>0
,

r(\alpha,\lambda)\ \geq\ \log_{+}(\lambda/2),\qquad\text{hence}\qquad r(\alpha,\lambda)\ \geq\ \max\bigl\{\,g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star}),\ \log_{+}(\lambda/2)\,\bigr\}.

Proof.

The second assertion follows from the first together with the lower bracket

r\geq g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})
in (
10
). For
\lambda\leq 2
the first
assertion is vacuous, since
\log_{+}(\lambda/2)=0
and
r=g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})\geq 0
.

Let
\lambda>2
. Because
C
is symmetric positive definite, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in the

C
-inner product gives, for the standard basis vector
e_{k}
,

(e_{k}^{\top}\tilde{b})^{2}=\bigl(e_{k}^{\top}C^{1/2}\cdot C^{-1/2}\tilde{b}\bigr)^{2}\leq(e_{k}^{\top}C\,e_{k})\,(\tilde{b}^{\top}C^{-1}\tilde{b}),\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad\mathcal{E}_{N}(\alpha)=\tilde{b}^{\top}C^{-1}\tilde{b}\ \geq\ \frac{\tilde{b}_{k}^{2}}{C_{kk}}.

Since
C_{kk}=(2\delta_{k})^{-1}
and
\delta_{k}=E_{k}-E_{0}\geq E_{\min}-(E_{\min}-\alpha)=\alpha
, this reads

\mathcal{E}_{N}(\alpha)\geq 2\delta_{k}\,\tilde{b}_{k}^{2}\geq 2\alpha\,\tilde{b}_{k}^{2}
for every
k
. Maximizing over
k
and
using
\max_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}^{2}\geq\left\lVert\tilde{b}\right\rVert^{2}/N
,

\mathcal{E}_{N}(\alpha)\ \geq\ 2\alpha\,\max_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}^{2}\ \geq\ \frac{2\alpha}{N}\,\left\lVert\tilde{b}\right\rVert^{2}.

Taking logarithms,

\frac{1}{2N}\log\mathcal{E}_{N}(\alpha)\ \geq\ \frac{1}{2N}\log\frac{2\alpha}{N}+\frac{1}{2N}\log\left\lVert\tilde{b}\right\rVert^{2}.

The first term tends to
0
, and the second tends to
\log(\lambda/2)
by (
11
). Passing to
the
\liminf
and invoking (
8
) gives
r(\alpha,\lambda)\geq\log(\lambda/2)=\log_{+}(\lambda/2)
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

The bound improves the lower bracket of (
10
) precisely when localization dominates
conditioning,
\log_{+}(\lambda/2)>g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})
, equivalently

2\log(\lambda/2)>\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})
. By (
10
) this region is non-empty and, as shown
next, comprises all sufficiently large
\lambda
. The two contributions have distinct origins: the
single test vector
e_{k}
captures the boundary-amplitude growth
\log_{+}(\lambda/2)
but is blind to the
ill-conditioning of
C
, whereas the bracket lower bound captures the conditioning rate

g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})
; their
sum

\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})
is the upper
end, and reaching it would require a test vector aligned simultaneously with
\tilde{b}
and with the
worst-conditioned direction of
C
(see §
8
).

6.
Edge sharpness of the cost rate

Write the relative gap

\eta(\alpha,\lambda):=1-\frac{r(\alpha,\lambda)}{\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})}\in[0,1],

so that
\eta=0
is exactly the upper-end identity
r=\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})
.

Proposition 6.1

(Vanishing of the relative gap)
.

Fix
\alpha>0
. Then:

(i)

0\leq\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})-r(\alpha,\lambda)\leq\log(\lambda/2)
for all
\lambda>2
;

(ii)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\downarrow 2}\eta(\alpha,\lambda)=0
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\eta(\alpha,\lambda)=0
.

In particular
r(\alpha,\lambda)=\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})\,(1+o(1))
at both ends of the localized phase.

Proof.

(i)
is immediate from (
10
):
r\geq g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})=\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})-\log(\lambda/2)
and
r\leq\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})
.

(ii)
By (i),
\eta(\alpha,\lambda)\leq\log(\lambda/2)/\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})
. As
\lambda\downarrow 2

the numerator tends to
0
. For the denominator,
z^{\star}
stays at spectral depth

E_{\max}-E_{\min}+2\alpha\geq 2\alpha
below the band, so it remains in the resolvent set, where the cocycle is
uniformly hyperbolic; by the monotonicity of the off-spectral exponent (Lemma
4.1
) and the
continuity of the Lyapunov exponent (Theorem
2.1
), the map

\lambda\mapsto\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})
is continuous and strictly positive at
\lambda=2
,

\gamma_{2}(z^{\star})>0
. Hence
\eta(\alpha,\lambda)\leq\log(\lambda/2)/\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})\to 0/\gamma_{2}(z^{\star})=0
.

(iii)
By Proposition
5.1
,
r\geq\log(\lambda/2)
, so with (
10
),

\displaystyle\eta(\alpha,\lambda)=1-\frac{r}{\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ 1-\frac{\log(\lambda/2)}{\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})}\ =\ \frac{\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})-\log(\lambda/2)}{\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})}

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})}{g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})+\log(\lambda/2)}.

It therefore suffices to show
g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})
stays bounded as

\lambda\to\infty
, since
\log(\lambda/2)\to\infty
. In fact it converges. Let
\mu_{\mathrm{eq}}
be the
equilibrium measure of
\Sigma_{\lambda}
, which is the density of states by Theorem
4.2
; by the Frostman
representation, with
\operatorname{cap}(\Sigma_{\lambda})=\lambda/2
from (
9
),

g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})=\int_{\Sigma_{\lambda}}\log\left\lvert z^{\star}-t\right\rvert\,d\mu_{\mathrm{eq}}(t)-\log(\lambda/2).

Since
\left\lvert E_{\min}\right\rvert=E_{\max}
,
z^{\star}=-3E_{\max}-2\alpha
and
\left\lvert z^{\star}-t\right\rvert=3E_{\max}+2\alpha+t
for
t\in[-E_{\max},E_{\max}]
by
(
6
). Rescale
t=\lambda s
: the band edges obey
E_{\max}/\lambda\to 1
, so
z^{\star}/\lambda\to-3
, and by Weyl
equidistribution of
\{\beta j\}
the rescaled density of states converges weakly to the arcsine measure

\mu_{[-1,1]}
, the equilibrium measure of
[-1,1]
(of capacity
\tfrac{1}{2}
). Hence

(12)

g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})\ \xrightarrow[\lambda\to\infty]{}\ \int_{-1}^{1}\log\left\lvert 3+s\right\rvert\,d\mu_{[-1,1]}(s)+\log 2\ =\ \arccosh 3\ =\ \log\bigl(3+2\sqrt{2}\bigr)\approx 1.7627,

the Green’s function
g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus[-1,1]}(-3)
. In particular
g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})

is bounded, with the crude uniform bound
\limsup\leq\log 8
from
\left\lvert z^{\star}-t\right\rvert\leq 4E_{\max}+2\alpha
and
E_{\max}\leq\lambda+2

(
7
), and
\eta(\alpha,\lambda)\leq g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})/\bigl(g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})+\log(\lambda/2)\bigr)\to 0
.
∎

Corollary 6.2

(Bounded absolute gap)
.

For each fixed
\alpha>0
and all
\lambda>2
,

(13)

0\ \leq\ \gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})-r(\alpha,\lambda)\ \leq\ \min\bigl\{\,g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star}),\ \log_{+}(\lambda/2)\,\bigr\}.

Consequently the absolute gap vanishes as

, is uniformly bounded on
(2,\infty)
, and
satisfies
\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\bigl[\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})-r(\alpha,\lambda)\bigr]\leq\arccosh 3=\log(3+2\sqrt{2})

by (
12
). Thus the

holds for
0\leq\lambda<2
(where the
bracket (
10
) collapses; without any input at
\lambda=0
, and for

unconditionally by
Theorem

) and asymptotically at both ends of

; at the golden-mean

(2,\infty)
as well (Theorem

: unconditionally for
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}
, under the polynomial-prefactor localization

) (numerically never exceeding

Proof.

By Proposition

,

, so

7.
A reduction of the upper-end identity

The results above bracket
r
within
\min\{g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star}),\log_{+}(\lambda/2)\}
of

\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})
, but the bracket alone leaves the interior of
(2,\infty)
unpinned. The next proposition
reduces the identity to a single one-sided inequality.

Proposition 7.1

(Reduction)
.

Let
\lambda>2
and
\alpha>0
, and set

\mathcal{R}_{N}:=\dfrac{\tilde{b}^{\top}C^{-1}\tilde{b}}{\tilde{b}^{\top}\tilde{b}}
, the Rayleigh quotient of
C^{-1}
at
the boundary-amplitude vector
\tilde{b}
. Then

(14)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{2N}\log\mathcal{R}_{N}\ =\ r(\alpha,\lambda)-\log_{+}(\lambda/2)\ \leq\ g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star}),

and the identity
r(\alpha,\lambda)=\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})
holds if and only if

(15)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{2N}\log\mathcal{R}_{N}\ \geq\ g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star}).

Equivalently, writing
C=\sum_{j}\sigma_{j}u_{j}u_{j}^{\top}
with
\sigma_{1}\geq\dots\geq\sigma_{N}>0
and

p_{j}:=(u_{j}^{\top}\tilde{b})^{2}/\left\lVert\tilde{b}\right\rVert^{2}
(a probability vector), the criterion (
15
)
reads
\liminf_{N}\frac{1}{2N}\log\sum_{j}p_{j}\,\sigma_{j}^{-1}\geq g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})
.

Proof.

By (
8
),
\frac{1}{2N}\log\mathcal{E}_{N}=\frac{1}{2N}\log\mathcal{R}_{N}+\frac{1}{2N}\log\left\lVert\tilde{b}\right\rVert^{2}
.
The last term converges to
\log_{+}(\lambda/2)
by (
11
). A convergent summand detaches from a

\liminf
, so

\liminf_{N}\tfrac{1}{2N}\log\mathcal{R}_{N}=\liminf_{N}\tfrac{1}{2N}\log\mathcal{E}_{N}-\log_{+}(\lambda/2)=r(\alpha,\lambda)-\log_{+}(\lambda/2).

The upper bracket (
10
) gives
r\leq\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})=g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})+\log_{+}(\lambda/2)
,
whence
r-\log_{+}(\lambda/2)\leq g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})
; this is (
14
). Since

\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})=g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})+\log_{+}(\lambda/2)
, the identity

r=\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})
is equivalent to
r-\log_{+}(\lambda/2)=g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})
,
and by (
14
) (where “
\leq
” always holds) to the one-sided (
15
). Finally

\mathcal{R}_{N}=\tilde{b}^{\top}C^{-1}\tilde{b}/\left\lVert\tilde{b}\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{j}(u_{j}^{\top}\tilde{b})^{2}/(\sigma_{j}\left\lVert\tilde{b}\right\rVert^{2})=\sum_{j}p_{j}\sigma_{j}^{-1}

by the spectral theorem.
∎

Remark 7.2

(What an eventual proof must supply)
.

The criterion (
15
) is met as soon as one eigendirection carries the full conditioning
rate and is not exponentially orthogonal to the data: with
\sigma_{\min}=\sigma_{N}
,
u_{\min}=u_{N}
,

\frac{1}{2N}\log\sigma_{\min}^{-1}\ \longrightarrow\ g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\liminf_{N}\frac{1}{2N}\log p_{N}\ \geq\ 0.

The first is an identification of the smallest Gram eigenvalue with the exterior-Green rate at
z^{\star}
; the
second is a non-degenerate-overlap statement between the boundary data and the worst-conditioned direction.
These are the two ingredients an eventual proof must supply, and (
15
) shows they are also,
together, sufficient.

8.
Remarks

Remark 8.1

(The nature of the obstruction)
.

By Proposition
7.1
, closing the interior amounts to (
15
): the inverse Gram
form must amplify
\tilde{b}
at the full rate
g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})
. Two features make
this hard, and one helps. The conditioning rate
g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})
is a

collective
(logarithmic-capacity) effect of all
N
crowded nodes
\{\delta_{k}\}
, not reproducible by any
fixed number of them; the boundary growth
\log_{+}(\lambda/2)
is carried by individual far-localized modes; and
the criterion demands that the worst-conditioned direction, built from the node spacings, overlap the data,
built from the localization centres. What
helps
is that the boundary amplitudes are sign-alternating,

\tilde{b}_{k}=(-1)^{k+1}\left\lvert\tilde{b}_{k}\right\rvert
(Sturm oscillation): in
\tilde{b}^{\top}C^{-1}\tilde{b}
the off-diagonal
Cauchy entries enter the relevant near-null combinations
constructively
, so the difficulty is not
avoiding cancellation but capturing the collective amplification.

Remark 8.2

(Why strong coupling does not trivialize it)
.

It is natural to seek (
15
) in the regime
\lambda\gg 1
, where Aubry–André localization is
sharpest. There the data is indeed explicit: with localization centre
j_{k}
, the boundary ratio is

\left\lvert\tilde{b}_{k}\right\rvert=e^{\gamma(2j_{k}-N-1)(1+o(1))}
,
\gamma=\log(\lambda/2)
, and the node is

\delta_{k}=2\lambda\cos^{2}(\pi\beta j_{k})+\alpha+O(1/\lambda)
. But
\delta_{k}
depends on
j_{k}
only through

\cos^{2}(\pi\beta j_{k})
, whereas
\tilde{b}_{k}
depends on
j_{k}
itself: two sites with equal
\cos^{2}(\pi\beta\,\cdot)

share a node yet have exponentially different amplitudes. Thus, as a function of the node, the data is a signed,
effectively pseudo-random exponential, and the overlap weights
p_{j}
of (
15
) are governed by
the arithmetic of
\{\beta j\bmod 1\}
near the band edge (
\cos(2\pi\beta j)\approx-1
), i.e. by the
continued-fraction approximants of
\beta
. Strong coupling sharpens the localization but not this
edge arithmetic, so it does not by itself yield (
15
); Proposition
6.1
(iii)
remains the proven strong-coupling statement (relative sharpness).

Remark 8.3

(Numerical consistency (illustrative))
.

For
\lambda=3
,

9.
An inverse-free cocycle form of the cost

The criterion (
15
) is a statement about the inverse Gram matrix
C^{-1}
. We remove the
inversion: the cost is an explicit squared exponential sum whose coefficients are transfer-cocycle and
characteristic-polynomial data, and the criterion becomes a sharp lower bound on the growth of that sum, to which
the large-deviation theory of the cocycle applies directly. Throughout,
P(x)=\prod_{m=1}^{N}(x-\delta_{m})
and

Q(x)=\prod_{m=1}^{N}(x+\delta_{m})
, so
P(x)=\chi_{N}(x+E_{0})
with
\chi_{N}(E)=\det(E-H_{N})
, and

;
write

.

Proposition 9.1

(Inverse-free form)
.

Let
D=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(Q(\delta_{k})/P^{\prime}(\delta_{k})\bigr)_{k=1}^{N}
and
c=D\tilde{b}
, i.e.
c_{k}=\dfrac{Q(\delta_{k})}{P^{\prime}(\delta_{k})}\,\tilde{b}_{k}
. Then

, and consequently

\mathcal{E}_{N}=\tilde{b}^{\top}C^{-1}\tilde{b}=c^{\top}Cc=\Bigl\|\,\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}\,e^{-\delta_{k}\,\cdot}\,\Bigr\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}^{2}.

Equivalently, with

a positively oriented contour enclosing
[\alpha,\max_{k}\delta_{k}]
,

(17)

\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}\,e^{-\delta_{k}s}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{Q(x)}{P(x)}\,\mathcal{T}_{21}(x+E_{0})\,e^{-xs}\,dx=\frac{(-1)^{N}}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}Q(x)\,m_{N}(x+E_{0})\,e^{-xs}\,dx,

where
m_{N}=\mathcal{T}_{21}/\mathcal{T}_{11}
is the truncated Weyl function and
\mathcal{T}_{11}(\,\cdot\,)=(-1)^{N}\chi_{N}(\,\cdot\,)
.

Proof.

Solve
Cw=\tilde{b}
and set

, rational with
R(\delta_{k})=(Cw)_{k}=\tilde{b}_{k}
. Writing

, so by Lagrange interpolation

S(x)=P(x)\sum_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}Q(\delta_{k})\big/\bigl(P^{\prime}(\delta_{k})(x-\delta_{k})\bigr)
. The residue of

at its pole

-\delta_{l}
is

and

w_{l}=\frac{Q(\delta_{l})}{P^{\prime}(\delta_{l})}\sum_{k}\frac{1}{\delta_{l}+\delta_{k}}\,\frac{Q(\delta_{k})}{P^{\prime}(\delta_{k})}\tilde{b}_{k}=D_{ll}\,(C\,c)_{l}=(DCD\,\tilde{b})_{l}.

As

is arbitrary,
C^{-1}=DCD

\mathcal{E}_{N}=\tilde{b}^{\top}w=c^{\top}Cc
, and

c^{\top}Cc=\sum_{k,l}c_{k}c_{l}/(\delta_{k}+\delta_{l})=\|\sum_{k}c_{k}e^{-\delta_{k}\cdot}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}
since

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\delta_{k}+\delta_{l})s}\,ds=(\delta_{k}+\delta_{l})^{-1}
, which is (
16
). The integrand of
(
17

\delta_{k}
, with residue

\frac{Q(\delta_{k})}{P^{\prime}(\delta_{k})}\mathcal{T}_{21}(E_{k})e^{-\delta_{k}s}=c_{k}e^{-\delta_{k}s}
(recall

); the second equality uses

∎

The coefficients

Proposition 9.2

(Cancellation-free positive-sum reduction)
.

Order
\delta_{1}<\dots<\delta_{N}
. The coefficients
c_{k}=Q(\delta_{k})\tilde{b}_{k}/P^{\prime}(\delta_{k})
of

all share one sign: writing
\operatorname{sign}\tilde{b}_{k}=\epsilon_{b}(-1)^{k}

(Sturm oscillation, §

(18)

\operatorname{sign}(c_{k})=\epsilon_{b}(-1)^{N}\qquad\text{for every }k.

Consequently

is a sum of nonnegative terms,

(19)

\mathcal{E}_{N}=c^{\top}Cc=\sum_{k,l=1}^{N}\frac{\left\lvert c_{k}\right\rvert\,\left\lvert c_{l}\right\rvert}{\delta_{k}+\delta_{l}},

and its exponential rate is carried by the largest coefficient:

(20)

r(\alpha,\lambda)=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\ \max_{1\leq k\leq N}\ \frac{1}{N}\log\left\lvert c_{k}\right\rvert,\qquad c_{k}=\frac{Q(\delta_{k})}{P^{\prime}(\delta_{k})}\,\tilde{b}_{k}.

Proof.

Q(\delta_{k})=\prod_{m}(\delta_{k}+\delta_{m})>0
since every
\delta_{m}>0
. With

the product

has exactly

negative factors, so

. By Sturm oscillation the

sign

strictly alternates,

\operatorname{sign}\tilde{b}_{k}=\epsilon_{b}(-1)^{k}
(the alternation recorded in §

\operatorname{sign}(c_{k})=(+1)\cdot\epsilon_{b}(-1)^{k}\cdot(-1)^{N-k}=\epsilon_{b}(-1)^{N}
, independent of

,
which is (

). The common sign squares to

in

,
giving the nonnegative form (

). For the rate, the diagonal of (

\mathcal{E}_{N}\geq\max_{k}c_{k}^{2}/(2\delta_{k})

\mathcal{E}_{N}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\sum_{k}\left\lvert c_{k}\right\rvert/\sqrt{\delta_{k}}\bigr)^{2}\leq(2\delta_{1})^{-1}\bigl(\sum_{k}\left\lvert c_{k}\right\rvert\bigr)^{2}
.
Since

∎

The next lemma rewrites

Lemma 9.3

(Boundary-weight form of the coefficient)
.

Let

be the

-normalized Dirichlet eigenvector. Then

\left\lvert c_{k}\right\rvert\ =\ Q(\delta_{k})\,\bigl(\hat{\psi}^{(k)}_{N}\bigr)^{2},

a band-edge tunneling pair that makes

) is free of both.

Proof.

Normalize the transfer solution by

(so
\psi^{(k)}_{1}=1
,
\psi^{(k)}_{n}=y_{n}(E_{k})
). Differentiating

∎

Lemma 9.4

(The kernel carries the reflected edge)
.

The zeros

10.
The conditioning–overlap reduction, and phase averaging

Proposition
9.2
and Corollary
9.5
already reduce the identity to the
single-mode statement
\max_{k}\frac{1}{N}\log\left\lvert c_{k}\right\rvert\to\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})
, with no Gram-eigenvector overlap.
The present section develops the complementary
overlap
viewpoint, which is the form in which the
almost-every-phase large-deviation method is naturally posed; it is logically subsumed by
Corollary
9.5
but isolates the same arithmetic obstruction in spectral language. By
Remark
7.2
the identity
r=\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})
holds as soon as

(I)

Conditioning:

\dfrac{1}{2N}\log\sigma_{\min}(C)^{-1}\to g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})
, and

(II)

Overlap:

\displaystyle\liminf_{N}\frac{1}{2N}\log p_{N}\geq 0
, with
p_{N}=(u_{N}^{\top}\tilde{b})^{2}/\left\lVert\tilde{b}\right\rVert^{2}
and
u_{N}
the
eigenvector of
C
for
\sigma_{\min}
.

We show that
(I)
is phase-independent and reduces to a standard condenser computation, so the whole identity
reduces to the single estimate
(II)
; and we delineate what the large-deviation method for almost every phase
does and does not supply.

(I) is the exterior-Green rate, for every phase

Since
C
is the Gram matrix of the exponential system
\{e^{-\delta_{k}\,\cdot}\}_{k=1}^{N}
in
L^{2}(0,\infty)
,

\sigma_{\min}(C)=\min_{\left\lVert v\right\rVert=1}\Bigl\|\,\textstyle\sum_{k}v_{k}\,e^{-\delta_{k}\,\cdot}\,\Bigr\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}^{2}

measures the near-linear-dependence of these exponentials, whose nodes
\delta_{k}
lie in
J:=\Sigma_{\lambda}-E_{0}
.

Proposition 10.1

(Conditioning rate)
.

For every phase
\phi
,

\ \frac{1}{2N}\log\sigma_{\min}(C)^{-1}\to g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})
.

This is a conditional statement (see the proof below); no result of this paper depends on it, and it is recorded here for independent interest.

Sketch.

By the Beckermann–Townsend theory the singular values of a Cauchy matrix with node sets
J
and
-J
are
controlled by Zolotarev numbers of the condenser
(J,-J)
:
\sigma_{\min}(C)/\sigma_{\max}(C)
decays at the
geometric rate fixed by the condenser, and
\sigma_{\max}(C)=O(1)
. A standard condenser computation identifies that
rate with the exterior-Green value
g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}
at the reflected edge
z^{\star}
, which in the
shifted variable is the point
-\delta_{\max}=2E_{\min}-\alpha
of
-J
opposite the node interval. The nodes
\delta_{k}

equidistribute to the equilibrium measure of
J
for
every

\phi
(the density of states is phase-independent and,
for the almost Mathieu operator, equals the equilibrium measure, §
9
), so the rate is phase-independent. In sum,
Proposition
10.1
rests on two inputs standard in the potential-theoretic and
orthogonal-polynomial literature but external to this note: the Beckermann–Townsend singular-value bound for
Cauchy matrices through the Zolotarev numbers of the condenser
(J,-J)
, and the edge-regularity of the
equilibrium measure of
\Sigma_{\lambda}
, namely the square-root vanishing of its density at the band edges, which
fixes the condenser rate and supplies the matching lower bound on
\sigma_{\min}
. We use it in this
form, and the reduction of the identity to the overlap bound (
24
) inherits the same status.
Numerically, with

\lambda=3,\alpha=0.1
,
\frac{1}{2N}\log\sigma_{\min}^{-1}
rises monotonically toward

g_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}}(z^{\star})=1.901
(e.g.
1.72,1.73,1.77,1.78
for

\phi=0
, and

∎

(II) is the single remaining estimate

Granting Proposition
10.1
, and using the trivial

(Cauchy–Schwarz,

is equivalent to the one-sided overlap bound

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\ \frac{1}{2N}\log p_{N}\ \geq\ 0,\qquad p_{N}=\frac{(u_{N}^{\top}\tilde{b})^{2}}{\left\lVert\tilde{b}\right\rVert^{2}}:

the boundary-amplitude vector

is not exponentially orthogonal to the worst-conditioned Gram direction

.
The upper bound
\frac{1}{2N}\log p_{N}\leq 0
is automatic; the content of (

) is that no exponential loss
occurs. Numerically

is small and its modulus decreases with

(for the generic phase above,

-0.50,-0.24,-0.13

Scope and limits of phase averaging

Three facts hold for almost every phase. First, the irrational shift
\phi\mapsto\phi+2\pi\beta
is ergodic and moves the
box by one site, so
r(\phi)
is shift-invariant up to a one-site boundary change and hence almost surely equal to a
constant
r
(granting that a one-site window shift does not change the exponential rate). Second, the boundary
amplitudes
\tilde{b}_{k}=\mathcal{T}_{21}(E_{k})
and the rate (
11
) are governed by the transfer cocycle, for
which the Bourgain–Goldstein large-deviation theorem
[
16
,
14
,
15
]
gives

\bigl|\tfrac{1}{N}\log\left\lVert M_{N}(E,\phi)\right\rVert-\gamma_{\lambda,N}(E)\bigr|>\varepsilon
on a set of phases of measure
<e^{-cN}
;
this underlies (
11
) and the localization of the data off a small phase set. Third, near-degeneracies of the
Dirichlet spectrum at scale
N
are confined to a phase set of measure
<e^{-cN}
, removable along a subsequence by
Borel–Cantelli.

What these do
not
supply is (
24
) itself. The overlap
p_{N}
is a pairing of
\tilde{b}
with the
smallest eigenvector
u_{N}
of the Gram matrix
C
, not a transfer-matrix norm, and the large-deviation control of

\left\lVert M_{N}\right\rVert
does not bound it. Excluding the exponential orthogonality
u_{N}\perp\tilde{b}
is an anti-concentration
(transversality) statement for which the phase measure is the natural tool, but a complete argument is not in hand. This
is the precise sense in which the almost-every-phase identity, and a fortiori the golden-mean point
\phi=0
, are reduced
to (
24
): the conditioning
(I)
is the phase-independent exterior-Green rate, and (
24
)
is the analytic core, the one place where the band-edge arithmetic (§
8
) still enters.

A regularity criterion for the every-phase identity

The reduction to (
24
) is analytic; we record a complementary

criterion. Write

for the
cost rate (
4
) at phase
\phi

and holds along the full
sequence: by Lemma

,
\left\lvert c_{k}\right\rvert=Q(\delta_{k})(\hat{\psi}^{(k)}_{N})^{2}\leq Q(\max_{m}\delta_{m})
, and since

\tfrac{1}{2N}\log\mathcal{E}_{N}=\max_{k}\tfrac{1}{N}\log\left\lvert c_{k}\right\rvert+o(1)
(Proposition

), Lemma

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{2N}\log\mathcal{E}_{N}(\phi)\ \leq\ \gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})\qquad\text{for every }\phi,

so

everywhere, with equality for almost every

).

Proposition 10.2

(Every-phase identity from upper semicontinuity)
.

Fix
\lambda>2
and
\beta
Diophantine. If
\phi\mapsto r(\phi)
is upper semicontinuous, then

r(\phi)=\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})
for
every

\phi
.

Proof.

By (
25
) and Theorem

the set

G=\{\phi:r(\phi)=\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})\}=\{\phi:r(\phi)\geq\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})\}
has full Lebesgue measure. If

\{\phi:r(\phi)<\gamma_{\lambda}(z^{\star})\}
is open; being also Lebesgue-null it is empty. Hence

∎

Remark 10.3
.

This is the route by which an almost-everywhere ergodic identity is upgraded to every point, and its merit is to bypass
the dynamics at the exceptional phases of
[
3
]
: it asks not that the operator localize there — it does not
— but only that the rate not dip below its full-measure value at an isolated phase. Equivalently, by minimality of

. For fixed

is real-analytic and (

)
controls the

band-edge transfer fluctuations of §

at the resonant phases, equivalent in turn to (
24
).

11.
The golden-mean overlap carries no cancellation

The overlap (

(No cancellation)
.

of

Boundary amplitude and the metallic identity

For

followed there through the edge-regularity input of Proposition

through the transfer dynamics of the subcritical cocycle. The recasting is the exact dual of the localized-side argument
of Theorem

Throughout,
y_{n}(E)

Proposition 14.1

(Boundary amplitude from the transfer norm)
.

Let

be a Dirichlet eigenvalue of
H_{[1,N]}
and set
\mathfrak{M}_{N}(E_{k})=\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{n}(E_{k})\right\rVert

\mathcal{T}_{0}=I

(36)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\,\mathfrak{M}_{N}(E_{k})^{2}}\ \leq\ \left\lvert\hat{\psi}^{(k)}_{N}\right\rvert\ \leq\ 1,\qquad\text{so}\qquad\frac{2}{N}\log\left\lvert\hat{\psi}^{(k)}_{N}\right\rvert\ \geq\ -\frac{4}{N}\log\mathfrak{M}_{N}(E_{k})-\frac{\log N}{N}.

In particular, along any sequence of eigenvalues with
\frac{1}{N}\log\mathfrak{M}_{N}(E_{k(N)})\to 0
the boundary amplitude has
rate zero,
\frac{2}{N}\log\left\lvert\hat{\psi}^{(k(N))}_{N}\right\rvert\to 0
.

Proof.

At the eigenvalue
\mathcal{T}_{11}(E_{k})=y_{N+1}(E_{k})=0
, so
\binom{0}{y_{N}}=\mathcal{T}_{N}(E_{k})\binom{1}{0}
; as
\det\mathcal{T}_{N}=1

\sigma_{\min}(\mathcal{T}_{N})=\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{N}\right\rVert^{-1}
,

\left\lvert y_{N}(E_{k})\right\rvert=\bigl\|\mathcal{T}_{N}(E_{k})\tbinom{1}{0}\bigr\|\ \geq\ \sigma_{\min}\bigl(\mathcal{T}_{N}(E_{k})\bigr)=\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{N}(E_{k})\right\rVert^{-1}\ \geq\ \mathfrak{M}_{N}(E_{k})^{-1}.

For the norm,
\binom{y_{n}}{y_{n-1}}=\mathcal{T}_{n-1}(E_{k})\binom{1}{0}
gives
\left\lvert y_{n}(E_{k})\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{n-1}(E_{k})\right\rVert\leq\mathfrak{M}_{N}(E_{k})

for every
1\leq n\leq N
, so
\left\lVert\psi^{(k)}\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}y_{n}^{2}\leq N\,\mathfrak{M}_{N}(E_{k})^{2}
. Hence

\left\lvert\hat{\psi}^{(k)}_{N}\right\rvert=\frac{\left\lvert y_{N}(E_{k})\right\rvert}{\left\lVert\psi^{(k)}\right\rVert}\ \geq\ \frac{\mathfrak{M}_{N}(E_{k})^{-1}}{\sqrt{N}\,\mathfrak{M}_{N}(E_{k})}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\,\mathfrak{M}_{N}(E_{k})^{2}},

and
\left\lvert\hat{\psi}^{(k)}_{N}\right\rvert\leq 1
by normalization. Taking logarithms gives (
36
).

Lemma 14.3

(The top Dirichlet eigenvalue reaches the band edge)

\max\sigma(H_{[1,N]})\to E_{\max}

A finite-subset bound, and why it is rate-neutral here

Discarding interpolation constraints in the minimal-norm form of (
8

S
, the rigorous lower bound
\mathcal{E}_{N}\geq\tilde{b}_{S}^{\top}C_{S}^{-1}\tilde{b}_{S}
. For a pair
S=\{p,q\}
, with

\Sigma=\delta_{p}+\delta_{q}
and
\Delta=\delta_{p}-\delta_{q}
, the minor evaluates exactly to

\tilde{b}_{S}^{\top}C_{S}^{-1}\tilde{b}_{S}=\frac{2\Sigma}{\Delta^{2}}\Big[\Sigma\,(\delta_{p}\tilde{b}_{p}^{\,2}+\delta_{q}\tilde{b}_{q}^{\,2})-4\delta_{p}\delta_{q}\,\tilde{b}_{p}\tilde{b}_{q}\Big].

With one node far-localized, so that

realizes the boundary growth

,
\gamma=\log_{+}(\lambda/2)
,
the dominant term is
\Sigma\delta_{p}\tilde{b}_{p}^{\,2}>0
and the exponential rate of the right-hand side is

\frac{1}{2N}\log\tilde{b}_{S}^{\top}C_{S}^{-1}\tilde{b}_{S}=\gamma+\rho+o(1),\qquad\rho:=-\frac{1}{N}\log\left\lvert\delta_{p}-\delta_{q}\right\rvert.

A strict rate gain over Proposition
5.1
therefore occurs if and only if the pair is an
exponential resonance,
\left\lvert\delta_{p}-\delta_{q}\right\rvert=e^{-\rho N}
with
\rho>0
: two modes localized at distance

\asymp N
whose on-site detuning is dominated by their tunnelling splitting.

For
\beta=(\sqrt{5}-1)/2
no such resonance exists. The badly-approximable bound
\left\lVert n\beta\right\rVert\gtrsim 1/n
keeps the
on-site energies
\lambda\cos(2\pi\beta j)
apart at polynomial scale, and the eigenvalue gaps are accordingly
polynomial: numerically

, so that
-N^{-1}\log\min_{k}\left\lvert E_{k+1}-E_{k}\right\rvert

decreases through
0.096,\,0.062,\,0.038,\,0.023,\,0.013
for

at
\lambda=3
. Hence

, and the finite-subset bound is rate-neutral: it recovers
\log_{+}(\lambda/2)
but cannot
exceed it. An exponential gain would require a Liouville frequency, where localization itself may fail. This
sharpens the tension noted in §
8
: the Diophantine property that makes the model localize is what
removes the elementary, resonance-based route to the data term.

(Upper bound for general analytic potentials)

Proposition 15.2

(Determinant form of the cost)
.

For every analytic

E_{0}

Proof.

A_{\alpha}=E_{0}I-H_{V}

Proposition 15.3

(Reflected extremal form and an explicit lower bound)
.

Let

(H_{V}^{[1,N]},e_{N})

where
\prod_{k}(\delta_{k}^{2}+\omega^{2})=\Delta_{N}(z)\Delta_{N}(2E_{0}-z)

\{E_{k}\}

E_{0}

\delta_{\max}=E_{\max}-E_{0}

W\succ 0

\leq\|Q_{0}\|_{*}^{2}/(\int Q_{0}\,d\mu_{N}^{(R)})^{2}

(Sharp reduction to a boundary edge mode)

The upper bound is Proposition

Appendix A
A renormalization route toward the full identity (open)

Theorem
13.9
establishes the magnitude bound (
26
) for almost every phase; for the
phase-uniform statement the reduction of §§
10
–
11
leaves it as the single
estimate, and §
12

Below we set out the renormalization structure that would turn that approach into a proof, the objects it must
control, and the precise fixed-point statement that remains. Nothing below is claimed as proved: it is the
strategy to which the evidence of §
12
points.

The exact cocycle factorization

For
\beta=(\sqrt{5}-1)/2
let
F_{n}
be the Fibonacci numbers and let

M_{n}(E,\phi)=A_{F_{n}}(E,\phi)\cdots A_{1}(E,\phi),\qquad A_{j}(E,\phi)=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda\cos(2\pi\beta j+\phi)-E&-1\\[2.0pt]
1&0\end{pmatrix},

be the transfer matrix over
[1,F_{n}]
in the notation of §
2
; its entries are the

\mathcal{T}_{11},\mathcal{T}_{21}
of §
9
at scale
F_{n}
. From
F_{n+1}=F_{n}+F_{n-1}
and the
quasiperiodicity of the potential,

(40)

M_{n+1}(E,\phi)=M_{n-1}\bigl(E,\phi+\theta_{n}\bigr)\,M_{n}(E,\phi),\qquad\theta_{n}=2\pi\bigl(F_{n}\beta-F_{n-1}\bigr)=2\pi(-1)^{\,n-1}\beta^{\,n},

exactly, with the phase drift
\theta_{n}\to 0
geometrically. Iterating (
40
) is the renormalization:
it drives a dynamics on pairs
(M_{n-1},M_{n})\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})^{2}
by

(M_{n-1},M_{n})\mapsto(M_{n},\,M_{n-1}(\,\cdot\,,\phi+\theta_{n})M_{n})
, whose trace coordinates

x_{n}=\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}M_{n}
obey, up to the drift
\theta_{n}
, the Fibonacci trace map

x_{n+1}=2x_{n}x_{n-1}-x_{n-2}
. The spectrum is the locus of bounded orbits, and its hierarchical band
structure at level
n{+}1
is assembled from levels
n,n{-}1
through (
40
). This is the same
renormalization
structure
used for the Fibonacci Hamiltonian (Sütő
[
17
]
,
Casdagli
[
18
]
; in hyperbolic-dynamical form, Damanik–Gorodetski
[
19
]
),
applied here to the almost Mathieu cocycle rather than to a substitution-invariant potential. The distinction
is essential: for the Sturmian Fibonacci potential the block composition carries no phase drift, the trace map
is exact, and the invariant
I=\mu^{2}/4
of
[
17
]
(the Fricke–Vogt invariant, in the terminology
of
[
19
]
) is conserved, whereas (
40
)
is exact but drifts by
\theta_{n}
, so the trace map closes only up to
\theta_{n}
and no invariant survives. The
cited framework is therefore a structural template here, not an importation of those theorems.

By Proposition
9.1

(the zeros of

M_{n}

\tilde{b}_{k}

with the off-diagonal entry along the orbit. The worst-conditioned Gram direction

is determined by

\{\delta_{k}\}
