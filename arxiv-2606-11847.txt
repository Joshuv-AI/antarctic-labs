Degree of tensor train varieties via integral geometry
Andrea Rosana Otto T.P. Schmidt

Abstract
In this work we consider tensor train varieties. These are varieties of tensors arising in a range of fields, including quantum many-body physics and machine learning. Using methods from integral geometry, we obtain a combinatorial expression for their degrees. We provide the ready-to-use julia package TTVarietyDegree.jl .



1
Introduction

Tensor decompositions provide algebraic models for high-dimensional data with hidden
low-dimensional structure. Next to (hierarchical) Tucker decomposition
[
kolda_2009
,
cheng_2019
]
, the tensor train (TT) decomposition is one of
the most widely used formats
[
Oseledets_2011
]
. Consider a projective tensor
[T]\in\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}\right)

and fix a vector of positive integers

D=(D_{0},D_{1},\dots,D_{N-1},D_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N+1},\qquad D_{0}=D_{N}=1.

We refer to
d=(d_{1},\dots,d_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
as the
local dimensions
, while the integers
D=(D_{1},\dots,D_{N-1})
are called the tensor train ranks or
bond dimensions
.

A
tensor train (TT) variety
can be defined via rank restrictions on flattenings. The
i
-th flattening of a tensor
T\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}
is the representation of
T
as the linear map

T^{(i)}:(\mathbb{C}^{d_{i+1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}})^{*}\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{i}}).

(1)

The tensor train variety of
signature

(D,d)
is the projective algebraic variety defined by
[
Borovik_2025
, Lemma 2.2]

V_{D,d}=\{[T]\in\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}\right)\,|\,\rank(T^{(r)})\leq D_{r}\,,\ r=1,...,N-1\}\,.

(2)

The bond dimensions
D
are called
admissible
for
d
if

D_{r}\leq D_{r-1}d_{r}\qquad\text{and}\qquad D_{r}\leq d_{r+1}D_{r+1},\qquad r=1,\dots,N-1.

We denote
V^{=}_{D,d}
the full-rank stratum of
V_{D,d}
, where each rank condition in (
2
) is satisfied with equality.

The dimension of
V_{D,d}
is computed in
[
Bernardi_2022
]
. It is natural to study other invariants, such as the degree. In this paper, we derive an exact procedure for computing the degree of TT varieties. Drawing ideas from
[
Breiding_2024
]
, where the authors derive a formula for the degree of tensor subspace varieties
[
landsberg_2012
]
, we base our approach on integral geometry. Although the Tucker decomposition considered in
[
Breiding_2024
]
is similar, their results are not applicable to tensor trains. Instead, the specific format of tensor trains forces us to introduce a recursive construction of the varieties (see Section
).

We will use the following result, which is a consequence of the projective kinematic formula
[
LerarioNotes
,
howard_1993
]
. It links the degree of a projective
m
-dimensional algebraic variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{c}
to its volume (with respect to the Fubini-Study metric; see Section
2
):

\deg(X)=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(X)}{\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{P}^{m})}.

(3)

We denote by
{\rm Gr}(k,n)
the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional linear spaces in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Its degree is given in (
) (see Section
2
). Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1

(Degree of tensor train varieties)
.

Let
V_{D,d}\subset\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}\right)
be the tensor train variety of signature
(D,d)
and define
m_{r}:=D_{r-1}d_{r}-D_{r}
.
The degree of the tensor train variety is

\deg(V_{D,d})=\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{r=1}^{N-1}\deg\bigl(\Gr(D_{r},D_{r-1}d_{r})\bigr)}{\displaystyle\prod_{r=1}^{N-1}(D_{r}m_{r})!}\,f(P_{D,d}),

where
f(P_{D,d})
is a purely combinatorial factor depending only the signature
(D,d)
(see (
)).

The main result in Theorem
1
allows an algorithmic implementation. We provide the registered and ready-to-use software package
TTVarietyDegree.jl
in
julia
. All details, a user manual and instructions for importing the package are provided in the
GitHub
repository

\texttt{https://github.com/OTPS3141/TTVarietyDegree.jl}.

We benchmarked the results of our algorithm comparing it with available numerical methods in
HomotopyContinuation.jl
and symbolic ones in
Macaulay2
. For all the examples using reasonable computational resources, we were able to verify the correctness of our result (see Section
).

Tensor train decompositions of tensors are also known in quantum many-body physics as
matrix product states (MPS) with open boundary conditions
. MPS are central in the study of one-dimensional quantum many-body
systems, for instance in density matrix renormalization group (DMRG) routines
[
White1992
,
Schollwoeck2011
,
Orus2014
]
, but also in fields like machine learning
[
cheng_2019
]
.

Information about the degree of TT varieties is both computationally and
geometrically relevant. In physics, one may view it as an algebraic measure for the complexity
of reconstructing a quantum state from linear observables under a fixed
tensor train signature. Consider an unknown state
\eta\in V_{D,d}
that we want to characterize. To do so, we perform generic measurements, that is we project the unknown state onto known test states
\phi_{i}
,

\langle\phi_{i},\eta\rangle=\ell_{i}.

Each projection imposes a linear constraint
\langle\phi_{i},\eta\rangle-\ell_{i}=0
on the unknown state
\eta
. After
\dim(V_{D,d})
-many measurements, the number of states satisfying these projection constraints is exactly the degree of
V_{D,d}
. Thus, the degree quantifies the identifiability of a state under linear measurements.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the used language and notation and discuss the main tool we use to compute volumes, the smooth coarea formula. In Section
we define a recursive parametrization by introducing tail varieties and their associated one-step maps. Section
establishes key results about metric-related properties of one-step maps. In Section
, we apply the smooth coarea formula to derive a recursive expression for the volume of TT varieties. In Section
we evaluate the volume and conclude the degree computation via the kinematic formula. Finally, we illustrate the algorithmic implementation of our result and compare it to existing methods (Section
).

2
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, whenever a vector space
V
is endowed with an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, orthogonality is understood with respect to it. If
U\subset V
is a subspace, its orthogonal complement is denoted by
U^{\perp}=\{v\in V\ |\ \langle v,u\rangle=0\ \ \forall u\in U\}
.

Denote by
\U(c+1)
the unitary group of size
c+1
. It acts transitively on the (complex) projective space
\mathbb{P}^{c}
. There is a unique, up to scaling,
\U(c+1)
-invariant Riemannian metric for this action (
[
Cartan_1952
]
), defined as follows. Fix a representative
x\in\mathbb{C}^{c+1}
for
[x]\in\mathbb{P}^{c}
. The tangent space to
\mathbb{P}^{c}
at
[x]
can be identified with the orthogonal
x^{\perp}=\{y\in\mathbb{C}^{c+1}\ |\ \langle x,y\rangle=0\}\cong T_{[x]}\mathbb{P}^{c}
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the standard Hermitian inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{c+1}
. The
Fubini-Study inner product
on
T_{[x]}\mathbb{P}^{c}
is given by

\langle v,w\rangle_{FS}=\frac{{\rm Re}(\langle v,w\rangle)}{\|x\|},\quad\forall v,w\in x^{\perp}\cong T_{[x]}\mathbb{P}^{c}\,.

(4)

We refer to the corresponding Riemannian metric as the
Fubini-Study metric
on
\mathbb{P}^{c}
and denote it by
g_{FS}
. The induced volume measure
\mathrm{vol}(\cdot)
inherits the
\U(c+1)
-invariance. We recall that the Fubini-Study volume of the projective space is given by

\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{P}^{c})=\frac{\pi^{c}}{c!}\,.

(5)

Given a smooth manifold
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{c}
, we endow
X
with the Riemannian metric induced by the Fubini-Study metric, i.e., for
[x]\in X
we restrict the inner product (
4
) to the tangent space
T_{[x]}X\subset T_{[x]}\mathbb{P}^{c}
. We denote the induced volume measure on
X
by
\mathrm{vol}_{X}(\cdot)
.

The
Stiefel manifold
of orthonormal
k
-frames of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is denoted by

\mathrm{St}(k,n)=\{M\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times k}\ |\ M^{*}M=I\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n\times k}.

In other words, the matrices
M\in\mathrm{St}(k,n)
have orthonormal columns with respect to the standard Hermitian product. The unitary group
\U(n)
acts on
\mathrm{St}(k,n)
via left-multiplication, with isotropy subgroup given by
\U(n-k)
. This action realizes
\mathrm{St}(k,n)
as the smooth homogeneous manifold
\U(n)/\U(n-k)
, hence its real dimension is given by

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathrm{St}(k,n))=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\U(n))-\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\U(n-k))=2nk-k^{2}.

(6)

We consider the inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{n\times k}
defined by taking the real part of the Frobenius product:

\langle A,B\rangle_{F}:={\rm Re}(\rm tr(A^{*}B)).

(7)

It induces the
\U(n)
-invariant Riemannian metric
g_{F}
on
\mathrm{St}(k,n)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n\times k}
.
Recall that the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional linear spaces in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is related to the Stiefel manifold by the quotient
\Gr(k,n)\cong\mathrm{St}(k,n)/\U(k)
, since for any
U\in\U(k)
and
M\in\mathrm{St}(k,n)
the column span of
M
and
MU
are the same. Seen as a projective variety in
\mathbb{P}(\Lambda^{k}\mathbb{C}^{n})
via the Plücker embedding,
\Gr(k,n)
inherits a Riemannian metric. With respect to it, the quotient map
\mathrm{St}(k,n)\rightarrow\mathrm{St}(k,n)/\U(k)\cong\Gr(k,n)
is a Riemannian submersion, hence

\mathrm{vol}(\Gr(k,n))=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{St}(k,n))}{\mathrm{vol}(\U(k))}\,,

(8)

where
\mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{St}(k,n))
and
\mathrm{vol}(\U(k))
both refer to the volume measures induced by (
7
) on
\mathrm{St}(k,n)
and
\U(k)
respectively. We use the shorthand notation
dM
to denote
d\mathrm{vol}_{\mathrm{St}(k,n)}(M)
. The normalized volume form on
\mathrm{St}(k,n)
is instead denoted by
\mu
and we refer to
d\mu(M)
as the
normalized Haar measure
.

Finally, given a product of Riemannian manifolds
(P_{1},g_{1})\times\dots\times(P_{s},g_{s})
, we endow it with the product Riemannian metric
g=g_{1}\times\dots\times g_{s}
. Denoting
P=P_{1}\times\dots\times P_{s}
and
x=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{s}\in P
, recall that
T_{x}P=T_{x_{1}}P_{1}\times\dots\times T_{x_{s}}P_{s}
. Then, given
v=(v_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
and
w=(w_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
in
T_{x}P
, we have

g_{x}(v,w):=(g_{1})_{x_{1}}(v_{1},w_{1})+\cdots+(g_{s})_{x_{s}}(v_{s},w_{s})\,.

2.1  The smooth coarea formula

Given a smooth map
\varphi:X\rightarrow Y
between smooth manifolds, we denote by
D_{x}\varphi:T_{x}X\rightarrow T_{\varphi(x)}Y
the differential of
\varphi
at
x\in X
. The map
\varphi
is a
submersion
if the differential
D_{x}\varphi
is surjective for every
x\in X
; in particular
\dim(X)\geq\dim(Y)
and for every
y\in Y
the fiber
\varphi^{-1}(y)
is either a smooth submanifold of
X
of dimension
\dim(X)-\dim(Y)
or empty.

If
\varphi:X\rightarrow Y
is a smooth submersion between Riemannian manifolds, the restriction of
D_{x}\varphi
to
\mathcal{K}_{x}^{\perp}:=(\ker(D_{x}\varphi))^{\perp}
is an isomorphism. We define the
normal Jacobian
of
\varphi
at
x
as its determinant

\mathrm{NJ}(\varphi,x):=|\det(D_x\varphi|_{\mathcal K_x^{\perp}})|\,,

computed with respect to
orthonormal
bases. Given
any
basis
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{l}\}
of
\mathcal{K}_{x}^{\perp}
, denote by
G
the Gram matrix of the basis elements and by
R
the Gram matrix of their images
\{D_{x}\varphi(v_{1}),\dots,D_{x}\varphi(v_{l})\}
under the differential. Then the normal Jacobian is equivalently given by

\mathrm{NJ}(\varphi,x)=\sqrt{\frac{\det(R)}{\det(G)}}\,.

(9)

We now state the smooth coarea formula. Its most general version for Lipschitz maps can be found in
[
Federer_1969
]
. For a proof in this simpler setting we refer to
[
howard_1993
, Section A-2]
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\varphi:X\rightarrow Y
be a smooth submersion between Riemannian manifolds and let
h:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be measurable. Then

\displaystyle\int_{X}h(x)\,\mathrm{NJ}(\varphi,x)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{X}(x)=\int_{Y}\left(\int_{\varphi^{-1}(y)}h(x)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{\varphi^{-1}(y)}(x)\right)d\mathrm{vol}_{Y}(y).

Now let
\varphi:X\rightarrow Y
be as above and let instead
h:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be measurable and such that the composition
\tilde{h}:=h\circ\varphi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is measurable. Applying Theorem
3
to
\tilde{h}
, one obtains as a corollary

\int_{X}(h\circ\varphi)(x)\,\mathrm{NJ}(\varphi,x)\,d\mathrm{vol}_{X}(x)=\int_{Y}h(y)\mathrm{vol}_{X}(\varphi^{-1}(y))\,d\mathrm{vol}_{Y}(y)\,.

(10)

We will use formula (
10
) to compute volumes of smoothly parametrized manifolds.

2.2  Parametrization of tensor train varieties

We define a parametrization for the tensor train variety as follows. For
r=1,\dots,N-1
define
n_{r}:=d_{r}D_{r-1}
and let

\mathcal{P}:=\prod_{r=1}^{N-1}\mathrm{St}(D_{r},n_{r})\times\mathbb{P}^{=}\!\left(\mathbb{C}^{D_{N-1}\times d_{N}}\right),

where
\mathbb{P}^{=}\!\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{D_{N-1}\times d_{N}}\bigr)
is the projectivization of the space of full rank
D_{N-1}\times d_{N}
complex matrices. Note that
\mathcal{P}
is a smooth Riemannian manifold.
We decompose
M^{r}\in\mathrm{St}(D_{r},n_{r})\subset\mathbb{C}^{n_{r}\times D_{r}}
in
d_{r}
blocks of size
D_{r-1}\times D_{r}
as

M^{r}=\begin{bmatrix}M^{r}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
M^{r}_{d_{r}}\end{bmatrix},\qquad M^{r}_{j_{r}}\in\mathbb{C}^{D_{r-1}\times D_{r}}.

(11)

Similarly we think of
C\in\mathbb{P}^{=}(\mathbb{C}^{D_{N-1}\times d_{N}})
as a block matrix

C=\begin{bmatrix}C_{1}&\cdots&C_{d_{N}}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{P}^{=}\!\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{D_{N-1}\times d_{N}}\bigr),\qquad C_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{D_{N-1}\times 1}.

As introduced earlier, we define the full-rank stratum
V^{=}_{D,d}
of the TT variety
V_{D,d}
as

V^{=}_{D,d}:=\left\{[T]\in V_{D,d}:\rank(T^{(r)})=D_{r}\text{ for }r=1,\dots,N-1\right\}.

It is a smooth Riemannian manifold with the metric induced by the ambient space
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}})
. We define the
tensor train map
as the smooth surjective submersion between Riemannian manifolds

\Phi:\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow V^{=}_{D,d}\subset\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathbb{C}^{d_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{N}}\right),\quad(M^{1},\dots,M^{N-1},[C])\longmapsto[T],

where the tensor is given as the matrix contraction
T_{j_{1},\dots,j_{N}}=M^{1}_{j_{1}}M^{2}_{j_{2}}\cdots M^{N-1}_{j_{N-1}}C_{j_{N}}
(see Figure

).
