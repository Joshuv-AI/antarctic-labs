Final report on Soft X-ray/VUV diagnostic upgrades and installation on NSTX-U

FINAL REPORT

GRANT NAME - THE JOHNS HOPKINS UNIVERSITY

GRANT NUMBER – DE-SC0021207

PERIOD OF PERFORMANCE 09/01/20-08/31/25

TITLE OF GRANT - Soft X-ray measurements of transport and MHD activity in the core and
edge NSTX plasma

PI’S NAME – Kevin Tritz

Due to repeated delays in the schedule for the NSTX-U upgrade as well as the emergence
of COVID and subsequent personnel loss during the granting period, the scope of the
award was reduced to completing the refurbishment and upgrade of the JHU soft X-ray
(SXR) and vacuum ultraviolet (VUV) diagnostics for the NSTX-U experiment.

The two poloidal SXR arrays were completely refurbished with new X-ray diode sensors
from Optodiode, Inc. Additionally, the filters on the pinhole selection slide were replaced
with new 5-, 15-, 50-, and 150-micron beryllium filters from Lebow Company. After
refurbishment, the diodes were bench tested to confirm operation and mounted on the
NSTX-U experiment.

The Multi-Energy Soft X-ray system, composed of 5 individually filtered, horizontally-
viewing soft X-ray diode arrays were refurbished with new sensors, filters (open, 5-, 15-, 50-
, 150-micron beryllium from Lebow), tested, and mounted on the NSTX-U experiment.

The VUV instrument is a transmission grating imaging spectrometer (TGIS), which was
modified to adjust the viewing geometry. TGIS operation was bench validated in the lab at
Johns Hopkins, then transported and handed to personnel at the Princeton Plasma Physics
Laboratory (PPPL) for installation. Installation may be deferred until after the initial
operations pending construction of a shielded enclosure and setup of the imaging transfer
system.

Overall, the JHU diagnostics systems were refurbished, operationally validated, and either
installed on the machine, or waiting for further installation work by the PPPL crew.
