Intratumoral and peritumoral-based radiomics for assessment of lymphovascular invasion in invasive breast cancer: model development and validation
Authors: Jiale Li, Di Zhang, Xukai Yang, Dalin Zhu, Xiaoxia Zheng, Yanlong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPreoperative non-invasive assessment of lymphovascular invasion (LVI) is critical for optimizing surgical strategy and risk stratification in breast cancer patients, yet reliable preoperative predictive tools remain insufficient. Current radiomics studies for breast cancer LVI prediction predominantly focus on intratumoral region (ITR) features, while emerging evidence indicates that the peritumoral region (PTR) harbors tumor microenvironmental alterations closely related to tumor invasion and metastasis and possesses unique diagnostic potential. However, existing studies rarely explore the predictive value of magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)-based radiomics features from different peritumoral subregions for breast cancer LVI, and there is no unified consensus on the optimal effective peritumoral expansion range for LVI prediction. This study aimed to explore the predictive value of multi‑range peritumoral MRI radiomics features for LVI in breast cancer and identify the optimal peritumoral margin.MethodsA total of 291 patients with invasive breast carcinoma who received preoperative dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI (DCE-MRI) at Gansu Provincial Maternity and Child-Care Hospital (Gansu Provincial Central Hospital) between December 2019 and August 2023 were enrolled and divided into training (n=204) and testing (n=87) cohorts. Radiomics features were extracted from ITR, PTR-5 mm, PTR-10 mm, combined ITR and PTR-5 mm (ITR+PTR-5 mm), and combined ITR and PTR-10 mm (ITR+PTR-10 mm) on preoperative DCE-MRI images. Feature consistency screening, dimensionality reduction, and optimization were sequentially performed via intra-class correlation coefficients (ICCs), Spearman correlation analysis, and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression with 10-fold cross-validation. Five radiomics prediction models based on the random forest (RF) algorithm were constructed corresponding to the five feature regions. The predictive performance of each model was comprehensively assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, from which the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy were calculated, calibration curve with the Hosmer-Lemeshow test, and decision curve analysis (DCA). The clinically meaningful threshold ranges of model efficacy indicators were further clarified for clinical decision-making reference.ResultsThe ITR+PTR-5 mm radiomics model yielded the optimal and most stable predictive efficacy with AUC values of 0.920 (training cohort) and 0.794 (test cohort). The calibration curve of this model showed excellent consistency between predicted probabilities and actual observed LVI status. DCA verified that this model provided the highest clinical net benefit across a wide range of threshold probabilities compared with other models.ConclusionsThe DCE-MRI-based ITR+PTR-5 mm radiomics model can serve as a potential noninvasive auxiliary tool for preoperative LVI risk assessment, providing tentative evidence for individualized clinical treatment decision-making.Keywords: Breast cancer, intratumoral, peritumoral, lymphovascular invasion (LVI), prognosis, dynamic contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (DCE-MRI)

Highlight box

Key findings

• Five dynamic contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging radiomics models were constructed for preoperative breast cancer lymphovascular invasion (LVI) prediction. The combined intratumoral region and peritumoral region-5 mm (ITR+PTR‑5 mm) model achieved the optimal and most stable predictive performance, with good calibration and the highest clinical net benefit. Multi‑range peritumoral features provided extra predictive value for LVI.

What is known and what is new?

• Reliable non‑invasive preoperative tools for breast cancer LVI assessment are insufficient. Previous radiomics studies only adopted intratumoral or single‑zone peritumoral features, and no consensus exists on the optimal peritumoral expansion range.

• This study innovatively combined intratumoral and multi‑width peritumoral radiomics features. Head‑to‑head comparison verified that the ITR+PTR‑5 mm model was the optimal predictive scheme.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The ITR+PTR‑5 mm model may act as a non‑invasive auxiliary tool for preoperative LVI risk stratification. Peritumoral biopsy sampling can provide supplementary pathological clues. Given its single‑centre design without external validation, multi‑centre prospective trials are required before clinical promotion.

Introduction

Globally, breast cancer currently represents the most commonly diagnosed malignancy and serves as the primary cause of cancer-related deaths in women (1). Notably, tumor recurrence and distant metastasis constitute the primary drivers of mortality in the majority of breast cancer patients (2). LVI in breast cancer refers to the histologic evidence of tumor cell clusters within the lymphatic channels or blood vessels surrounding an invasive carcinoma (3). As a pivotal step in the metastatic cascade, LVI has been consistently linked to an elevated risk of cancer metastasis and locoregional recurrence (LRR). Consequently, it is widely recognized as a clinically significant prognostic factor for patients with breast cancer (4,5). In clinical practice, LVI status serves as a direct guide for the selection of therapeutic strategies. Following breast-conserving surgery, patients with LVI-positive tumors exhibit markedly elevated risks of distant metastasis and LRR compared with LVI-negative patients, with hazard ratios (HRs) of 2.08 and 2.00, respectively (6). This finding indicates that extended surgical resection or intensified adjuvant chemotherapy may be required for LVI-positive patients in clinical practice. Furthermore, LVI acts as a potential biological biomarker for identifying chemoresistance during neoadjuvant chemotherapy (7), providing evidence for early modification of treatment regimens. In addition, LVI represents a critical risk factor for non-sentinel lymph node metastasis in patients with positive sentinel lymph nodes (8), which is of great significance for determining whether axillary lymph node dissection is necessary. Currently, the definitive evaluation of LVI is exclusively dependent on postoperative pathological examination. From a clinical perspective, preoperative prediction of LVI status in breast cancer facilitates the development of individualized optimal treatment strategies and achieves refined clinical management for patients.

Dynamic contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (DCE-MRI) can reflect the volume and permeability of tumor microvessels as well as neoangiogenesis, effectively indicating the malignant proliferation, invasive and metastatic potential of tumors; it is regarded as the most sensitive modality for breast cancer detection (9). Previous studies have identified several MRI radiological features strongly correlated with LVI status, yet their evaluation and measurement are subject to substantial inter-observer subjectivity and variability (10-12).Radiomics has emerged as a promising tool for characterizing malignant lesions via high-throughput extraction and quantitative analysis of imaging information (13,14). To date, MRI-based radiomics studies focusing on the preoperative prediction of LVI in breast cancer have yielded encouraging outcomes (15-17). Nevertheless, most existing research has solely focused on intra-tumoral heterogeneity while overlooking the transitional zone between tumor parenchyma and normal tissue, namely the peritumoral region (PTR). As an early event of tumor metastasis, LVI involves tumor cells breaking through the basement membrane and invading adjacent lymphatic or blood vessels, an invasive process predominantly occurring at the tumor-normal tissue interface—the PTR (18).

The PTR constitutes the most active interface for crosstalk between tumor cells and the host microenvironment (19), which is abundant in cancer-associated fibroblasts, nascent microvessels, immune cells, and remodeled extracellular matrix (ECM). These components exert critical regulatory effects on tumor cell proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and angiogenesis by secreting signaling molecules including cytokines, chemokines, and growth factors (20,21). For instance, ECM remodeling and collagen fiber rearrangement establish physical tracks facilitating tumor cell migration, and such alterations are particularly prominent within the peritumoral zone (21). Several studies have demonstrated that peritumoral radiomics features exhibit equivalent or even superior predictive performance to intratumoral features for the diagnosis and prognosis of breast cancer (22,23).

In recent years, numerous studies have attempted to construct non-invasive LVI prediction models based on clinical data, conventional imaging signs, and single-region radiomics features (10-12,24). Traditional clinical prediction models relying on tumor size, histological grade, and immunohistochemical indicators show limited predictive accuracy and poor individual discrimination. Single-intratumoral radiomics models based on DCE-MRI can extract intratumoral heterogeneity features, but they ignore the tumor microenvironment changes closely related to tumor invasion, resulting in insufficient model robustness and generalizability. Most existing imaging models only focus on fixed single peritumoral ranges (25-28), lacking comparative analysis of different peritumoral expansion margins, and there is no unified standard for the optimal PTR for breast cancer LVI prediction.

Given the above research status and existing limitations, this study further supplements and optimizes previous research systems: we innovatively compared the predictive efficacy of radiomics models based on 5- and 10-mm peritumoral expansion ranges, and integrated intratumoral and peritumoral multi-region features to construct optimized LVI prediction models. We hypothesized that the combined intratumoral-peritumoral radiomics model can significantly improve preoperative LVI predictive accuracy and stability, and clarify the optimal peritumoral margin for breast cancer LVI prediction. This study aims to provide preliminary empirical evidence for the standardized construction of peritumoral radiomics models and non-invasive preoperative LVI risk stratification, and lay a foundation for subsequent multi-center prospective verification and clinical transformation. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-0592/rc).

Methods

Study population

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the ethics committee of Gansu Provincial Maternity and Child-Care Hospital (approval No. 2022-GSFY-05). Informed consent was waived as the study was retrospective. A total of 291 patients diagnosed with invasive breast cancer at Gansu Provincial Maternity and Child-Care Hospital (Gansu Provincial Central Hospital) from December 2019 to August 2023 were enrolled in this retrospective study. The sample size of this study is moderately matched with the number of candidate predictive features. According to the empirical principle of radiomics prediction model research, the sample size of more than 200 cases can effectively support the screening of dozens of high-dimensional imaging features and avoid serious overfitting (29). Patients were randomly divided into a training cohort (n=204, 70%) and a test cohort (n=87, 30%) using simple random sampling.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) imaging findings indicated a mass-like primary lesion, with no prior invasive procedures or treatments conducted before MRI examination; (II) patients underwent DCE-MRI of the breast preoperatively, with the interval between MRI examination and surgery ≤2 weeks; and (III) patients were included only if LVI status was confirmed by postoperative pathological examination. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) received chemotherapy or breast biopsy before MRI scans; (II) a history of other malignant tumors; and (III) poor image quality or incomplete clinical data.

LVI assessment criteria

Histological specimens obtained from surgery were evaluated using double immunohistochemistry (dIHC) with CD31 + CKP and D2-40 + CKP staining. LVI was definitively diagnosed when CKP-positive tumor cells were identified within luminal structures positive for D2-40 or CD31.Specifically, positivity for either CKP + CD31 (+) or CKP + D2-40 (+) indicated LVI (+), whereas concurrent negativity for both CKP + CD31 (−) and CKP + D2-40 (−) indicated LVI (−).

MRI protocol

Axial fat-suppressed T2-weighted imaging (T2WI), axial non-fat T1-weighted imaging (T1WI), diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), apparent diffusion coefficient, and DCE-MRI were performed on patients in the prone position. A 3.0-T MRI scanner (Siemens Healthcare, Erlangen, Germany) with a dedicated 18-channel surface breast coil was used. Images were obtained using the following sequences: axial T1WI: repetition time (TR) 4.50 ms, echo time (TE) 1.61 ms, field of view (FOV) 36 cm × 36 cm, slice thickness 1.3 mm, voxel size 0.9 mm × 0.9 mm × 1.3 mm; axial fat-suppressed T2WI: TR 3,860 ms, TE 69.0 ms, FOV 36 cm × 36 cm, slice thickness 4.0 mm, voxel size 0.9 mm × 0.9 mm × 4.0 mm; DWI: TR 4,700 ms, TE 62.0 ms, FOV 38 cm × 38 cm, slice thickness 4.0 mm, voxel size 2.2 mm× 2.2 mm × 4.0 mm. For the dynamic contrast-enhanced scan, gadoterate meglumine contrast agent (specification: 5.654 g per 15 mL vial) was administered intravenously at a dose of 0.2 mL per kilogram of body weight and a flow rate of 2.2 mL/s. This was followed by a 20 mL saline flush administered at the same flow rate. A pre-contrast fat-suppressed T1WI sequence was first performed with a slice thickness of 1.2 mm and no interslice gap. Image acquisition commenced 20 seconds after the contrast injection, encompassing a total of 5 phases, with each phase having an acquisition time of 60 seconds. Digital subtraction images were generated simultaneously.

Radiomics analysis

Image region of interest (ROI) segmentation

All early-phase DCE-MRI images were exported in standard DICOM format from the hospital’s Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS). Unified image preprocessing was performed before segmentation, including resampling to uniform voxel size, gray value normalization, and noise reduction processing to eliminate imaging parameter differences and improve data repeatability. Tumor segmentation was performed using 3D Slicer software. Image preprocessing was standardized by resampling. Subsequently, two radiologists with 3 and 10 years of breast imaging experience, respectively, performed segmentation of the ROI on DCE-MRI images while being blinded to the pathological results. During segmentation, lesions were delineated slice by slice along the tumor margins. For multicentric or multifocal lesions, the largest tumor was selected as the target for delineation. The delineation range covered the entire lesion completely, while normal glandular tissue, necrosis, hemorrhage, and cystic components were excluded. Slice-by-slice ROIs were fused to generate a three-dimensional volume of interest (VOI).

The intratumoral region (ITR) was automatically expanded annularly by 5 and 10 mm from the tumor margin to form dilated regions. The PTR-5 mm and PTR-10 mm were then obtained by subtracting the ITR from the corresponding dilated regions. The detailed workflow of lesion segmentation is illustrated in Figure 1. If the maximum distance between the peritumoral zone and the breast skin or chest wall was less than the predefined expansion distance, the PTR was defined according to this actual distance.

Radiomics feature extraction and standardization

Radiomics features of ITR and PTR were extracted using the PyRadiomics toolkit. A total of 999 radiomics features were extracted from the ITR, PTR-5 mm, and PTR-10 mm for each patient, respectively. Additionally, 999 radiomics features were extracted from the combined ITR and PTR-5 mm (ITR+PTR-5 mm) and combined ITR and PTR-10 mm (ITR+PTR-10 mm) via early feature fusion, respectively. The extracted radiomics features included: (I) diagnostic meta-features (n=13); (II) shape features (n=17); (III) first-order features (n=57); (IV) texture features, comprising gray level size zone matrix (GLSZM; n=48), gray level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM; n=72), gray level run length matrix (GLRLM; n=48), neighborhood gray tone difference matrix (NGTDM; n=15), and gray level dependence matrix (GLDM; n=42); and (V) wavelet features (n=687). Notably, diagnostic meta-features were excluded from model construction. Prior to analysis, the Z-score standardization method was applied to normalize the data. This process addresses issues arising from data of varying scales and enhances comparability. Furthermore, it accelerates the convergence of gradient descent algorithms when seeking optimal solutions and improves the robustness of the predictive model.

Imageomics feature selection

Thirty patients were randomly selected for MRI image analysis. Two radiologists performed ROI segmentation synchronously and independently on the selected images. One radiologist repeated the segmentation on the same 30 images after an interval to calculate inter- and intra-class correlation coefficients (ICCs). Features with ICC >0.80 were considered to have good consistency and were included in subsequent analysis. The correlation between each pair of features was calculated using Spearman’s correlation coefficient. If the correlation coefficient between two features exceeded 0.9, only one of them was retained to ensure mutual independence among features. Finally, LASSO regression combined with 10-fold cross-validation was adopted to screen the optimal feature subset, which effectively reduces feature dimensionality, eliminates overfitting caused by high-dimensional redundant features, and retains the most representative LVI-related imaging features.

Model construction and performance evaluation

In this study, random forest (RF) algorithm was selected for model construction with clear theoretical basis: compared with traditional logistic regression and linear discriminant analysis algorithms, RF has significant advantages in processing high-dimensional radiomics feature data, can effectively adapt to the nonlinear correlation between imaging features and pathological LVI status, has strong resistance to data overfitting, and is suitable for small and medium sample size medical imaging data modeling (30). A total of five radiomics models were established to predict LVI status: the ITR model, PTR-5 mm model, PTR-10 mm model, ITR+PTR-5 mm model, and ITR+PTR-10 mm model.

The models were evaluated using the validation set. For each model, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated and metrics including the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy were computed to assess their performance in predicting LVI in breast cancer. Calibration of the models was evaluated by plotting a calibration curve and applying the Hosmer-Lemeshow test to quantify its agreement with observed outcomes. Additionally, decision curve analysis (DCA) was conducted to estimate the clinical net benefit and utility of the predictive model across various probability thresholds. Based on the diagnostic characteristics of breast cancer LVI prediction and previous clinical research consensus, we defined that AUC ≥0.75, sensitivity ≥0.70, and specificity ≥0.65 are the clinically meaningful efficacy thresholds for preoperative auxiliary prediction models, which can provide effective reference for clinical risk stratification.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 25.0, Python 3.7, and R software. Patients were randomly divided into the training cohort and test cohort at a 7:3 ratio. Radiomics feature reproducibility was assessed using intra- and inter-observer ICCs, and features with ICC >0.80 were retained. Spearman correlation analysis and LASSO regression with 10-fold cross-validation were used for feature redundancy elimination and dimensionality reduction. Model diagnostic performance was evaluated using ROC curves, and the AUC, sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy were calculated for each model. Calibration curves and the Hosmer-Lemeshow test were adopted to evaluate model fitting, and decision curve analysis was performed to assess the clinical net benefit of the predictive models. A two-tailed P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

This study enrolled a total of 291 patients with invasive breast cancer. The cohort was randomly divided into a training set (n=204) and a test set (n=87) at a ratio of 7:3. The training set comprised 123 LVI-negative and 81 LVI-positive patients, while the test set included 50 LVI-negative and 37 LVI-positive patients. Following the aforementioned radiomics feature selection, the optimal radiomics features associated with LVI were finally incorporated into the construction of the five prediction models. Specifically, 8 features were included for both ITR and PTR-5 mm, 9 features for PTR-10 mm, and 7 features for both ITR+PTR-5 mm and ITR+PTR-10 mm. Collectively, the combined intratumoral-peritumoral models outperformed single intratumoral or peritumoral models, and the ITR+PTR-5 mm model showed the optimal and most stable predictive performance with clinically meaningful efficacy indicators (AUCtest =0.794, sensitivity exceeding 0.70 and specificity exceeding 0.65). As a preliminary exploratory model, the ITR+PTR-5 mm model can be used for non-invasive preoperative auxiliary prediction of breast cancer LVI risk. In clinical practice, clinicians can input standardized preoperative DCE-MRI imaging data into the model to obtain individual LVI positive probability, so as to formulate individualized stratified treatment plans for high- and low-risk patients and reduce unnecessary overtreatment. It should be emphasized that this model is only an auxiliary diagnostic tool and cannot replace postoperative pathological diagnosis.

Discussion

The findings of this study provide compelling evidence that the combined intratumoral + peritumoral model exhibits superior performance relative to the intratumoral-only model. Aligned with the consensus from contemporary literature, this observation supports the notion that the integration of ITR and PTR serves as an effective strategy to enhance the diagnostic utility of MRI sequences (31).

To contextualize these results, Luo et al. (32) previously developed a radiomics model leveraging preoperative DCE-MRI data, which incorporated both ITR and PTR-4 mm. This model was designed to predict the presence of intraductal components in invasive breast cancer. Notably, their combined intratumoral + peritumoral model achieved higher AUC values in both the training and independent testing cohorts (training: 0.82; testing: 0.82) when compared to two single-region counterparts: the intratumoral-only model (training: AUC =0.78; testing: AUC =0.76) and the peritumoral-only model (training: AUC =0.70; testing: AUC =0.81) (31). This prior work, alongside the results of the present study, collectively reinforces the value of multi-regional feature integration in optimizing radiomics-based diagnostic models.

Within the framework of this study, further stratification of peritumoral models by regional thickness revealed critical insights into model stability. Specifically, the peritumoral 10 mm region model demonstrated the highest AUC value (0.926) in the training cohort; however, its performance was substantially attenuated in the validation cohort (AUC =0.710). This marked discrepancy between training and validation performance is highly suggestive of potential overfitting—a common limitation in radiomics studies, wherein models may inadvertently learn noise or cohort-specific artifacts rather than generalizable biological or imaging features.

In stark contrast, the ITR+PTR models—with the ITR+PTR-5 mm model standing out as the most robust—exhibited minimal differences in performance between the training and validation cohorts. This consistent performance across independent datasets is indicative of enhanced model stability.

The superior stability of the combined models can be attributed to the complementary nature of the information encapsulated within ITR and PTR. By integrating multi-regional imaging features, the combined model not only capitalizes on the unique biological insights from each region but also mitigates the spurious chance correlations that are inherently associated with single-region analyses. Consequently, this integration enhances the model’s robustness, reducing its susceptibility to overfitting and improving its potential for clinical translation.

Contemporary research has predominantly centered on the robust predictive utility of radiomic features extracted from a single PTR in the context of breast cancer. Specifically, such features have shown significant value for predicting breast cancer molecular subtypes, expression patterns of key immunohistochemical markers such as Ki67 proliferation index, and responsiveness to chemotherapy (25-28). Despite these advances, critical knowledge gaps remain. First, few studies have explored the use of MRI-derived radiomic features from PTR of varying thickness for predicting LVI in breast cancer patients. Second, no consensus has been established regarding the optimal spatial extent of PTR that harbor biologically meaningful information relevant to breast cancer pathogenesis or clinical outcomes.

In the present study, the ITR+PTR-5 mm model achieved high AUC values across both the training and validation cohorts, with values of 0.920 and 0.794, respectively. This favorable performance may be biologically attributed to the proximity of the PTR-5 mm to the primary tumor boundary: this zone is directly impacted by tumor cell invasion, stromal remodeling, and early microenvironmental changes—all of which are pathologically relevant to LVI. In contrast, the PTR-10 mm may inadvertently incorporate excessive noise from normal, non-pathological breast parenchyma or adipose tissue, which could dilute the signal of biologically meaningful radiomic features and compromise model performance.

These observations align with the work of Huang et al. (23), who emphasized the critical role of peritumoral zones in distinguishing between benign and malignant breast lesions and identified a 5-mm ROI as the most clinically appropriate size for capturing relevant pathological signals. However, Lin et al. reported that the 10-mm peritumoral combined model had better diagnostic efficacy (33), indicating that the optimal peritumoral range may be affected by research endpoints, imaging sequences, and tumor types. This study fills the research gap of optimal peritumoral margin screening for breast cancer LVI prediction, and provides preliminary data support for the standardization of peritumoral ROI segmentation in breast cancer radiomics research.

It is necessary to clearly acknowledge the limitations of this study to avoid overstatement of clinical application value. First, this is a single-center retrospective study without an independent external validation cohort, which limits the generalizability and clinical transformation potential of the model. The model performance may decrease when applied to different imaging equipment, medical centers, and patient populations. Second, the sample size is limited, and the model needs further large-sample verification. Third, the optimal 5-mm peritumoral range is determined based on statistical predictive efficacy, and the underlying molecular biological mechanism needs further in-depth exploration.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this single-center preliminary study verifies that the DCE-MRI-based ITR+PTR-5 mm radiomics model has favorable preoperative predictive potential for breast cancer LVI, with stable diagnostic efficacy and good clinical net benefit. This study mainly proves the feasibility and hypothesis-generating value of multi-region radiomics models for LVI prediction. Subsequent multi-center prospective external validation and mechanism exploration are required to further optimize the model and promote its clinical practical application.
