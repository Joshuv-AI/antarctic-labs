Abstract
In this work we propose, by linearizing the equations of fully nonlinear poroelasticity, a consistent model in which only the solid phase is incompressible. This reformulation circumvents some inconsistency issues encountered in standard primal formulations of nonlinear poroelasticity while still retaining its key physical coupling mechanisms. We show a well-posed and consistent discretization strategy and also formulate scalable solvers based on a Schur complement formalism. A distinctive feature of the model is that it allows for a lowest order, inf-sup stable family of fe ( fe ) spaces. Numerical tests in two and three dimensions are provided to validate the proposed method and solver framework.



Keywords and phrases:
Flow in deformable porous media, finite element methods, stability analysis, scalable preconditioning.

Introduction

Context and main contributions.

Poroelastic media refer to a deformable elastic body with a complex network through which fluid passes. This framework has a wide variety of applications, such as CO2 sequestration, hydrogels, and soft tissue
[
7
]
. The latter is the main motivation of this work, with the additional hypothesis that the solid phase is incompressible
[
15
]
, as soft tissues are made mostly of water. This hypothesis is seldom used in both linear and nonlinear settings despite its clear interpretation, and instead other options are typically adopted, such as total incompressibility (
J=1
) or quasi-incompressibility
[
14
,
21
]
. We highlight works on edema modeling
[
5
]
,
scale and feather growth
[
4
]
, and heart perfusion
[
20
]
where solid incompressibility was considered.

The equations of nonlinear poroelasticity, albeit being very general, have remained understudied as they pose several practical difficulties, mainly lack of convergence of the nonlinear solvers involved. One fundamental difficulty was recently detected
[
6
]
, where primal formulations of nonlinear poroelasticity were found to be always inconsistent if the pressure depends on deformation, which is one of the defining physical features of poroelasticity. Two novel mixed formulations were proposed in
[
6
]
, which avoid this problem, and in this work we will focus on the simpler one, consisting in defining a total pressure variable.

Total pressure has already been considered as a variable in related linearized models
[
23
]
, and has proved to be a reliable reformulation of the problem with much better theoretical properties. It has been used for developing parameter robust methods that do not require an interface Lagrange multiplier
[
9
]
, to study nonlinear problems
[
9
,
13
]
, and to study well-posedness of novel discretization techniques
[
13
,
19
]
. Our formulation, despite being different as it does not consider pressure as a variable, depends on a similar idea in which a total pressure is defined as an auxiliary variable.

In this work, we present a linearization of the fully nonlinear poroelasticity equations with solid incompressibility and a modified total pressure variable. The mathematical structure of the resulting model allows for a simple lowest order
fe
approximation, and we leverage the well-established fixed-stress preconditioner
[
26
,
12
]
to obtain a scalable solver for the proposed model.

Structure.

This work is structured as follows: Section
2
reviews how a general poroelastic material is characterized in order to have an incompressible solid phase and show how to obtain the sought linearized system. In Section
3
we show how the problem can be discretized in a stable manner both in space and time. Here, we propose a Schur complement based preconditioner for the linearized model. In Section
4
we validate our theoretical claims with convergence tests for the steady and time-dependent versions of the scheme, and also address a benchmark 3D problem. For this, we study separately the optimality and the scalability of the proposed overall solution strategy. We conclude with a brief discussion of the developments of the paper, in Section

.

2
Model formulation

In this section we present the fully nonlinear model with solid incompressibility in pressure-mixed form. We then compute the linearized model and study the mathematical structure of its weak formulation. We further consider an additional section that shows how the parameters of this linearized model are related to a standard Biot model. This helps position this model within the landscape of other poroelastic models.

2.1
Nonlinear model with solid incompressibility

For a thorough explanation and derivation of the field equations, we redirect the reader to the excellent book by Coussy
[
15
]
. Given a domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\in\{2,3\}
) with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
, nonlinear poroelasticity consists in finding a displacement field
\mathbfit{d}
and a Lagrangian porosity
\varphi
such that the conservation of linear momentum and mass hold, i.e.

\displaystyle\rho_{s,0}\ddot{\mathbfit{d}}-\mathbfit{\dive}\widetilde{\mathsfbfit P}(\mathsfbfit F,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\mathbfit{f},

(2.1a)

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}-\dive\mathsfbfit K\grad\widetilde{p}(\mathsfbfit F,\varphi)

\displaystyle=g,

(2.1b)

where
\mathsfbfit F=\mathsfbfit I+\grad\mathbfit{d}
is the strain tensor,
\rho_{s,0}
is the reference solid density,
\mathbfit{f}
is a given volume load,
g
is a mass source or sink, and
\mathsfbfit K
is a permeability tensor assumed symmetric, bounded, and uniformly positive definite, i.e., there exist constants
0<\mathsfbfit K^{\mathrm{min}}\leq\mathsfbfit\mathsfbfit K^{\mathrm{max}}<\infty
such that

{\mathsfbfit{K}^{\mathrm{min}}|\mathbfit{\xi}|^{2}\leq\mathbfit{\xi}^{\tt t}\mathsfbfit K\mathbfit{\xi}\leq\mathsfbfit K^{\mathrm{max}}|\mathbfit{\xi}|^{2}\hskip 11.49994pt\forall\mathbfit{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.}

The material characterization is given through a Helmholtz potential
\Psi
such that

\mathsfbfit P=\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\mathsfbfit F},\hskip 11.49994ptp=\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\varphi}.

Solid incompressibility states that the Lagrangian solid porosity, given by
\varphi_{s}=J-\varphi
with
J=\det\mathsfbfit F
, is constant in time. In particular, we have that

J-\varphi=J_{0}-\varphi_{0}:=\varphi_{s,0},

(2.2)

for a given porosity
\varphi_{0}
and reference Jacobian
J_{0}
. Enforcing such a constraint is done through the Helmholtz potential, thus we consider a Langrange multiplier
\lambda
and modify the Helmholtz potential as

\widetilde{\Psi}=\Psi+\lambda(J-\varphi-\varphi_{s,0}).

This yields the modified Piola stress tensor

\widetilde{\mathsfbfit P}=\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\mathsfbfit F}+\lambda\cof\mathsfbfit F=\mathsfbfit P+\lambda\cof\mathsfbfit F,

where
\cof\mathsfbfit F=J\mathsfbfit F^{-T}
, and the modified pressure

\tilde{p}=\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\varphi}-\lambda=p-\lambda.

We highlight that this problem, as observed in
[
6
]
, is not consistent due to the presence of third order terms. In addition, the multiplier
\lambda
is an
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
function, but requires the computation of its Laplacian in (
2.1b
). To circumvent this, we use the mixed in pressure formulation proposed in
[
6
]
, which results in the following problem: find
(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\lambda,\varphi)
such that

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\ddot{\mathbfit{d}}-\dive\left(\mathsfbfit P(\mathsfbfit F,\varphi)+\lambda J\mathsfbfit F^{-T}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbfit{f}

\displaystyle\quad\text{in}\,\Omega,

(2.3a)

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}-\dive\mathsfbfit K\grad\mu

\displaystyle=g

\displaystyle\quad\text{in}\,\Omega,

(2.3b)

\displaystyle J-\varphi

\displaystyle=\varphi_{s,0}

\displaystyle\quad\text{in}\,\Omega,

(2.3c)

\displaystyle\mu-p(\mathsfbfit F,\varphi)+\lambda

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{in}\,\Omega,

(2.3d)

for some given initial data
\mathbfit{d}(0)=\mathbfit{d}^{0}
,
\dot{\mathbfit{d}}(0)=\mathbfit{v}^{0}
,
\varphi(0)=\varphi^{0}
and full Dirichlet boundary conditions given by

\mathbfit{d}=\mathbf{0}
,
\mu=0
on
\partial\Omega
.

We firstly note that the nonlinear terms in (
2.3
) are
\mathsfbfit P(\mathsfbfit F,\varphi)
,
\lambda J\mathsfbfit F^{-T}
,
J
, and
p(\mathsfbfit F,\varphi)
. Next, we consider all variables to be perturbed with respect to an equilibrium point as
\zeta=\zeta_{0}+\delta\zeta
for
\zeta\in\{\mathbfit{d},\mu,\lambda,\varphi\}
, from which we will compute a model for the increments
\delta\zeta
. We obtain the following relations by using a first order approximation of all nonlinear terms:

\displaystyle\mathsfbfit P(\mathsfbfit F,\varphi)

\displaystyle\approx\mathsfbfit P(\mathsfbfit F_{0},\varphi_{0})+\frac{\partial\mathsfbfit P}{\partial\mathsfbfit F}:\delta\mathsfbfit F+\frac{\partial\mathsfbfit P}{\partial\varphi}\delta\varphi,

\displaystyle\lambda J\mathsfbfit F^{-T}

\displaystyle\approx\lambda_{0}J_{0}\mathsfbfit F_{0}^{-T}+\delta\lambda J_{0}\mathsfbfit F_{0}^{-T}+\lambda_{0}\delta(J\mathsfbfit F^{-T}),

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\approx J_{0}+J_{0}\mathsfbfit F_{0}^{-T}:\delta\mathsfbfit F,

\displaystyle p(\mathsfbfit F,\varphi)

\displaystyle\approx p(\mathsfbfit F_{0},\varphi_{0})+\frac{\partial p}{\partial\mathsfbfit F}:\delta\mathsfbfit F+\frac{\partial p}{\partial\varphi}\delta\varphi,

where all omitted arguments in the partial derivatives are the reference equilibrium points. We make the following hypotheses on the linearization terms:

•

\mathbfit{d}_{0}=\mathbf{0}
, i.e., small displacements, and
\lambda_{0}=0
. Note that this yields
J_{0}=1
.

•

\frac{\partial\mathsfbfit P}{\partial\mathsfbfit F}:\delta\mathsfbfit F
yields
\mathsfbfit\sigma(\grad\mathbfit{d})=\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{Hooke}}:\mathsfbfit\varepsilon(\mathbfit{d})
, where
\mathsfbfit\varepsilon
is the symmetric part of the gradient and
\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{Hooke}}
is the well-known linear fourth-order Hooke tensor, assumed symmetric, bounded, and uniformly positive definite. Thus, there exist constants
0<\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{min}}\leq\mathsfbfit\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{max}}<\infty
such that

\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{min}}|\mathsfbfit\tau|^{2}\leq\mathsfbfit\tau^{\tt t}\mathbb{C}\mathsfbfit\tau\leq\mathbb{C}^{\mathrm{max}}|\mathsfbfit\tau|^{2}\hskip 11.49994pt\forall\mathsfbfit\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}.

This is not strictly a hypothesis but more of an algebraic convenience because the Piola stress tensor is not symmetric, and deriving a linearization that yields the Hooke tensor requires reparameterizing the Piola in terms of the second Piola tensor as a function of the Cauchy strain
\mathsfbfit C=\mathsfbfit F^{T}\mathsfbfit F
. We avoid these nuances and simply assume the linearized symmetric form for simplicity.

•

\frac{\partial\mathsfbfit P}{\partial\varphi}=\frac{\partial p}{\partial\mathsfbfit F}=\beta\mathsfbfit I
for some positive constant
\beta=\alpha M
, where
M
is the Biot modulus and
\alpha
is the Biot coefficient.

•

The pressure function is monotone, so
\frac{\partial p}{\partial\varphi}=M\geq 0
, with
M
the Biot modulus.

The parameter choices are motivated by the standard Biot model; details are given in Section
2.2
. Using these hypotheses, we obtain the linearized model that we propose, written in terms of displacement
\mathbfit{d}
, total pore pressure
\mu
, porosity
\varphi
, and incompressibility Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
:

\displaystyle\rho_{s,0}\ddot{\mathbfit{d}}-\dive\left(\mathsfbfit\sigma(\grad\mathbfit{d})+\beta\varphi\mathsfbfit I+\lambda\mathsfbfit I\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbfit{f}

\displaystyle\quad\text{in}\,\Omega,

(2.4a)

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}-\dive\mathsfbfit K\grad\mu

\displaystyle=g

\displaystyle\quad\text{in}\,\Omega,

(2.4b)

\displaystyle\mu-\beta\dive\mathbfit{d}-M\varphi+\lambda

\displaystyle=p_{0}

\displaystyle\quad\text{in}\,\Omega,

(2.4c)

\displaystyle\dive\mathbfit{d}-\varphi

\displaystyle=-\varphi_{0}

\displaystyle\quad\text{in}\,\Omega,

(2.4d)

together with the boundary and initial conditions specified at the end of Section
2.1
. In contrast to the standard Biot model, the proposed model yields both pressure and porosity as primary variables. In addition, solid incompressibility is considered by rigorously deriving it from the original nonlinear problem, and not through a limit process on a material specific constant. Naturally, both processes yield equivalent models, and we establish this in detail in Section
2.2
with respect to the well-known incompressible Biot.

2.2
Equivalence with Biot’s model

To obtain the parameters, we look at how they can be compared with a classic Biot system. In such models, the volume fraction
\varphi
is given by the same linearized law we obtained, given by

\varphi=\alpha\dive\mathbfit{d}+\frac{1}{M}p,

where
\alpha
is the Biot coefficient and
M
is the Biot modulus. If we consider that the function
\varphi\mapsto p(\mathsfbfit F,\varphi)
can be inverted for all
\mathsfbfit F
, then we can linearize the porosity as

\varphi=\varphi(\mathsfbfit F,p)\approx\varphi_{0}+\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\mathsfbfit F}:\mathsfbfit F+\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial p}p.

Through the linearization, we can then consider
\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial\varphi}\right)^{-1}=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial p}=\frac{1}{M}
and
\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\mathsfbfit F}=\alpha\mathsfbfit I
, from which we can readily compute that

\alpha\mathsfbfit I=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial p}\frac{\partial p}{\partial\mathsfbfit F}=\frac{1}{M}\frac{\partial p}{\partial\mathsfbfit F},

so that the linearization hypothesis yields
\frac{\partial p}{\partial\mathsfbfit F}=\alpha M\mathsfbfit I
. To further contextualize the proposed model, we demonstrate its exact equivalence to a standard two-field Biot model in the fully incompressible limit. Starting from the steady state of the continuous formulation defined in (
2.4
), we can eliminate the auxiliary variables
\lambda
and
\varphi
to recover a pure displacement-pressure system. From the solid incompressibility constraint (
2.4d
), we have:

\varphi=\dive\mathbfit{d}+\varphi_{0}.

(2.5)

Substituting (
2.5
) into the total pressure definition (
2.4c
), we can express the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
exclusively in terms of the displacement
\mathbfit{d}
and the pore pressure
\mu
:

\lambda=p_{0}-\mu+\beta\dive\mathbfit{d}+M(\dive\mathbfit{d}+\varphi_{0})=p_{0}-\mu+(\beta+M)\dive\mathbfit{d}+M\varphi_{0}.

(2.6)

Next, we define the total stress tensor of our formulation as
\mathsfbfit\sigma_{\text{tot}}=\mathsfbfit\sigma(\nabla d)+\beta\varphi I+\lambda I
. Inserting (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) into this stress tensor yields:

\displaystyle\mathsfbfit\sigma_{\text{tot}}

\displaystyle=2\mu_{s}\mathsfbfit\varepsilon(\mathbfit{d})+\lambda_{s}(\dive\mathbfit{d})\mathsfbfit I+\beta(\dive\mathbfit{d}+\varphi_{0})\mathsfbfit I+\big(p_{0}-\mu+(\beta+M)\dive\mathbfit{d}+M\varphi_{0}\big)\mathsfbfit I

\displaystyle=2\mu_{s}\mathsfbfit\varepsilon(\mathbfit{d})+\big(\lambda_{s}+2\beta+M\big)(\dive\mathbfit{d})\mathsfbfit I-\mu\mathsfbfit I+C_{0}\mathsfbfit I,

(2.7)

where
C_{0}=\beta\varphi_{0}+p_{0}+M\varphi_{0}
is a constant initial stress state. Equation (
2.7
) is exactly the stress tensor of a standard Biot model with a fluid pressure
\alpha_{\text{Biot}}p=\mu
, with the Biot–Willis coefficient
\alpha_{\text{Biot}}
, and a modified volumetric Lamé parameter defined as

\tilde{\lambda}=\lambda_{s}+2\beta+M.

(2.8)

Furthermore, substituting (
2.5
) into the mass conservation equation (
2.4b
) yields:

\dive\dot{\mathbfit{d}}-\dive(\mathsfbfit K\nabla\mu)=g,

(2.9)

which matches the standard Biot mass equation in the limit where both the solid grains and the fluid are fully incompressible (i.e.,
1/M_{\text{Biot}}=0
). This reduction confirms that while the proposed four-field system introduces additional variables to rigorously impose solid incompressibility and handle nonlinear stress-free configurations, its linearized form governs the exact same physics as a standard incompressible Biot model subject to an augmented volumetric stiffness.

Semi-discrete weak formulation

We start by considering a time partition

(2.10a)

2.4
Semi-discrete well-posedness analysis

In this section, we establish the well-posedness of the semi-discrete scheme in operator form (
2.11
), which is equivalent to the weak formulation (
2.10

\mathcal{A}:\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{V}^{\prime}

\mathcal{B}:\mathbf{V}\to\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}

\widetilde{F}:\mathbf{V}\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}(\mathbfit{d}^{n},\mu^{n},\varphi^{n}),(\mathbfit{d}^{*},\mu^{*},\varphi^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbfit{d}^{*},\mu^{*},\varphi^{*})

We now focus on checking the Ladyzhenskaya–Babuška–Brezzi conditions
[
8

17

2.12
). The following result shows the boundedness of the associated operators.

Lemma 2.1

(Boundedness)
.

The following bounds hold

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\left\{\frac{\rho_{0}}{\tau^{2}},\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{Hooke}}^{\mathrm{max}},2\beta,\tau\mathsfbfit K^{\mathrm{max}},M\right\}\|(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)\|_{\mathbf{V}},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)\|_{\mathbf{V}},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbfit{f}^{n}\|_{0,\Omega}+\tau\|g^{n}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{0}\|_{0,\Omega},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\varphi_{0}\|_{0,\Omega}.

Proof.

The bounds readily follow from the definition of dual norms, together with the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the assumptions over
\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{Hooke}}
and
\mathsfbfit K
(cf. Section
2.1
).
∎

The result below establishes the inf-sup condition, and thus the surjectivity of
\mathcal{B}
.

Lemma 2.2

(Inf-sup condition)
.

The following bound holds

\displaystyle\sup_{(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)\in\mathbf{V}\setminus\{\mathbf{0},0,0\}}\frac{|\langle\mathcal{B}(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi),\lambda^{*}\rangle|}{\|(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)\|_{\mathbf{V}}}\gtrsim\|\lambda^{*}\|_{\mathrm{Q}}\quad\forall\lambda^{*}\in\mathrm{Q}.

Proof.

From the definition of the operator
\mathcal{B}
, we readily see that

\mathcal{B}(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)=(\lambda^{*},\dive\mathbfit{d})-(\varphi,\lambda^{*}).

Since it does not depend on
\mu
, we choose
\mathbfit{d}=\mathbf{0}
and
\varphi=-\lambda^{*}
to obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)\in\mathbf{V}\setminus\{\mathbf{0},0,0\}}\frac{|\langle\mathcal{B}(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi),\lambda^{*}\rangle|}{\|(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)\|_{\mathbf{V}}}\geq\frac{|(\lambda^{*},\dive\mathbf{0})-(-\lambda^{*},\lambda^{*})|}{\|(\mathbf{0},-\lambda^{*})\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\mathbfit{d}}\times\mathrm{V}^{\varphi}}}=\|\lambda^{*}\|_{\mathrm{Q}}.

Thus, the result holds with the hidden constant equal to 1.
∎

We highlight that the proof hinges on the fact that
V^{\lambda}
is contained in
V^{\varphi}
. This will be the only assumption required for the discrete inf-sup. Now, we characterize the kernel of the operator
\mathcal{B}
, denoted as
\ker(\mathcal{B})
.

Lemma 2.3

(Kernel characterization)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
defined as in Section
2.4
. Then,

\ker(\mathcal{B})=\{(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)\in\mathbf{V}:\dive\mathbfit{d}=\varphi\}.

Proof.

From the definition of
\ker(\mathcal{B})
, we readily see that

\displaystyle\ker(\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle=\{(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)\in\mathbf{V}:(\lambda^{*},\dive\mathbfit{d})-(\varphi,\lambda^{*})=0\quad\forall\lambda^{*}\in\mathrm{Q}\}

\displaystyle=\{(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)\in\mathbf{V}:(\dive\mathbfit{d}-\varphi,\lambda^{*})=0\quad\forall\lambda^{*}\in\mathrm{Q}\}.

Since
\dive\mathbfit{d}
,
\varphi
, and
\lambda^{*}
are functions in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, the result holds.
∎

The coercivity of the operator
\mathcal{A}
in
\ker(\mathcal{B})
is provided next.

Lemma 2.4

(Coercivity of
\mathcal{A}
)
.

The following bound holds

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi),(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)\rangle\gtrsim\min\left\{\frac{\rho_{0}}{\tau^{2}},\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{Hooke}}^{\min},2\beta,\tau\mathsfbfit K^{\mathrm{min}},M\right\}\|(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)\|_{\mathbf{V}}^{2}

for all
(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)
in
\ker(\mathcal{B})
.

Proof.

From the definitions of the operator
\mathcal{A}
and
\ker(\mathcal{B})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi),(\mathbfit{d},\mu,\varphi)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{0}}{\tau^{2}}(\mathbfit{d},\mathbfit{d})+(\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{Hooke}}:\mathsfbfit\varepsilon(\mathbfit{d}),\mathsfbfit\varepsilon(\mathbfit{d}))+2\beta(\varphi,\varphi)

\displaystyle\quad+\tau(\mathsfbfit K\grad\mu,\grad\mu)+M(\varphi,\varphi).

Note that the off-diagonal terms given by
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{*}
canceled out because of the structure of
\ker(\mathcal{B})
. The result follows by using the definition of the norm
\|\bullet\|_{\mathbf{V}}
in the equation above.
∎

We finalise this section by establishing the well-posedness of the semi-discrete formulation as a direct consequence of Lemmas
2.1
-
2.4
(see also
[
8
, Theorem 4.2.3]
).

Theorem 2.5
.

Given
\mathbfit{f}^{n}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
and
g^{n},p_{0},\varphi_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, the saddle-point formulation (
2.12
) admits a unique solution
((\mathbfit{d}^{n},\mu^{n},\varphi^{n}),\lambda^{n})\in\mathbf{V}\times\mathrm{Q}
at each time instant
n\in\{1,...,N\}
. Moreover, the continuous dependence on data holds, i.e.,

\|((\mathbfit{d}^{n},\mu^{n},\varphi^{n}),\lambda^{n})\|_{\mathbf{V}\times\mathrm{Q}}\lesssim\max\left\{\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}},\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha_{1}}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{0}}},\alpha_{1}\right\}\left(\|\mathbfit{f}^{n}\|_{0,\Omega}+\tau\|g^{n}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{0}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\varphi_{0}\|_{0,\Omega}\right),

with
\alpha_{0}=\min\left\{\frac{\rho_{0}}{\tau^{2}},\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{Hooke}}^{\min},2\beta,\tau\mathsfbfit K^{\mathrm{min}},M\right\}
and
\alpha_{1}=\max\left\{\frac{\rho_{0}}{\tau^{2}},\mathbb{C}_{\mathrm{Hooke}}^{\mathrm{max}},2\beta,\tau\mathsfbfit K^{\mathrm{max}},M\right\}.

Remark 2.1
.

The loss of robustness as
C_{\mathrm{Hooke}}^{\mathrm{max}}
tends to infinity is an expected limitation of the proposed model, due to the deterioration of the conditioning in the nearly incompressible (or nearly rigid) regime and the consequent reduced control of the stress tensor. This behavior is well known in elasticity-type models with extreme parameter values. Such effects may be mitigated by adopting mixed formulations involving an independent approximation of the stress variable.

In this section, we show stable choices of conforming
fe

We propose using a block partitioned preconditioner for this problem, and in particular we use the Schur complement approximation produced by the fixed stress method
[
24
]

[

which can be used as a preconditioner
[

-\dive\left(\sigma(\bullet)\right)^{-1}\grad\approx\beta_{FS}I,

for some problem dependent constant
\beta_{FS}
(see
[
11

\beta_{FS}=2\mu_{s}/\text{dim}+\lambda_{s}

[
11
]

\mu_{s},\lambda_{s}
are the Lamè parameters. Note that the constant
\beta_{FS}
is necessarily positive, see
[
10
]
for an example with a negative stabilization constant. Considering the partition of variables into
\mathrm{V}^{d}
and
\mathrm{V}^{\mu}\times\mathrm{V}^{\varphi}\times\mathrm{V}^{\lambda}

\beta\mathcal{B}\\
\mathcal{B}\end{bmatrix}(\mathcal{A}_{1})^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}0&\beta\mathcal{B}^{*}&\mathcal{B}^{*}\end{bmatrix},

where we have defined
\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\mathcal{B}_{2}
and used that
\mathcal{B}_{1}=\beta\mathcal{B}_{2}
. We focus on the second term now, which is where we will obtain the blocks for the fixed-stress approximation

\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\beta\mathcal{B}\\
\mathcal{B}\end{bmatrix}(\mathcal{A}_{1})^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}0&\beta\mathcal{B}^{*}&\mathcal{B}^{*}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\

\mathcal{B}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0&\beta(\mathcal{A}_{1})^{-1}\mathcal{B}^{*}&(\mathcal{A}_{1})^{-1}\mathcal{B}^{*}\end{bmatrix}\\
=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0\\

for some constant

5
Discussion

In this work we formulate a linear poroelasticity model which accounts for solid phase incompressibility. One important feature of this model is that it is directly derived from a fully nonlinear poroelastic model, which yields a consistent formulation with both the porosity and the total pressure as variables, in addition to the expected displacement and Lagrange multiplier. This model presents a simple saddle-point structure which allows for a lowest order
fe
approximation, with only a very mild inf-sup condition which requires
\mathrm{L}^{2}
functions to belong to related spaces. Finally, the presence of the variable
\mu
as a total pressure suggests a connection to better established
total-pressure
formulations in the context of Biot’s equations, but a rigorous proof does not yet exist.

In terms of finding a scalable solver for this model, we have leveraged the interpretation of the fixed-stress iterative method as an Uzawa method to obtain a robust preconditioner. The preconditioner obtained relies on a spectrally equivalent block for the Schur complement which is much easier to precondition. For this operator, we have derived efficient options to use for a block-partitioned preconditioner depending ultimately on
amg
and Jacobi. The overall solution strategy was tested on a 3D benchmark problem, where its optimality and scalability were verified numerically.

For future work, the fixed-stress preconditioner has some well-established weaknesses depending on certain parameter regimes, for which it would be interesting to obtain a similar formulation based on the
undrained
iterative scheme. An alternative route would be developing an operator preconditioner based on the continuous spaces. In addition, the solution strategy was tested only on the linearized problem, so it will be important to validate this strategy also on the fully nonlinear case, as well on other systems coupled with poromechanics (such as thermo-poroelasticity
[
18
]
, stress-assisted diffusion, or interface poroelastic-fluidic problems
[
3
]
).
