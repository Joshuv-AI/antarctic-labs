Universal Assembly and Cellular Loop Spaces on Regular CW Complexes Thanks: This work was supported by grants VEGA 1/0425/25, UK 1162/2024, UK UK/304/2023. Parts of this work will be included in the PhD thesis of S.D
S. Dylda, T. Macko

Abstract
. We develop a regular CW analogue of the classical assembly formalism for chain complexes appearing in algebraic surgery theory. From the cell poset, we construct combinatorial path and loop objects using fences of comparable cells and prove that their classifying spaces recover the homotopy types of the ordinary based path and loop spaces. The resulting loop object carries a natural monoid structure, giving rise to a DG algebra defined directly from the cellular structure. For complexes of cellular cosheaves, we introduce a universal assembly functor to modules over the group ring of the fundamental group and study the localization determined by global equivalences. The associated homotopy category is identified with a Verdier quotient of the derived category of cellular cosheaves, and its fibrant objects are precisely the homotopy locally constant complexes. A single elementary cosheaf becomes a compact generator after localization, and its derived endomorphism DG algebra is identified with singular chains on the cellular loop space. Consequently, the localized theory admits a Morita description in terms of DG modules over the loop DG algebra. The formalism provides a regular CW counterpart of the classical delta-set approach to assembly in algebraic surgery theory due to Ranicki and Weiss.

1.
Introduction

The topological applications of algebraic
L
-theory involve a local-to-global procedure that assembles algebraic objects carried over a space into algebra over its fundamental group. This passage is used to encode geometric problems, such as the comparison of normal maps, surgery obstructions, and structure sets, in terms of chain level algebra equipped with the so-called
chain duality
. This program, together with many applications, is developed in detail in the book
[
11
]
by Ranicki. The local objects reflect the fact that the geometry is built piece by piece, while the assembled object records how these local pieces are glued in the presence of a nontrivial fundamental group.

A key ingredient of
[
11
]
is laid out in the paper
[
12
]
by Ranicki and Weiss, where a theory of chain complexes over delta-sets is first studied without the chain duality. One starts with a local system (alias “(co)sheaf”) of chain complexes over a delta-set
X
, performs assembly, and obtains a chain complex over the group ring
R[\pi_{1}(X,x)]
. In this formalism, the assembly functor is factored through a suitable localized category. In
[
11
]
the quadratic
L
-groups of the localized category and the assembled category were shown to be isomorphic; this result is known as the
algebraic
\pi
-
\pi
theorem

[
11
, §10]
. For its proof, it is important that in
[
12
]
the localized category is identified through the endomorphisms of a distinguished generator. In particular, a delta-set model of the based loop space of
X
enters as the object controlling these endomorphisms.

The purpose of the present paper is to carry out an analogous program for regular CW complexes. Regular CW complexes arise naturally in geometric topology, and one often wants to work with the given cell structure rather than introduce an auxiliary delta-set model. This becomes especially important in multiplicative questions. After the full theory is developed, as in
[
11
]
, we expect the product formulas for surgery-theoretic invariants, such as the total surgery obstruction, to acquire straightforward proofs in the regular CW setting because the cartesian product of regular CW complexes carries a simple canonical product cell structure, see also
[
1
]
. This happens to be in contrast with the more elaborate delta-set models described in
[
11
, Appendix B]
, which require diagonal approximations. A direct regular CW formalism therefore avoids auxiliary choices and is better adapted to product constructions.

In a regular CW complex, the local geometric pieces are the cells themselves, together with their incidence relations, so the natural way to record algebraic data over the space is to assign objects to cells and structure maps along face relations. This leads directly to functors on the cell poset
\mathcal{X}
, and hence to the notion of a cellular sheaf or cosheaf. The use of sheaf-theoretic language is justified by results of Curry and Shepard; see
[
4
, Theorem 4.2.10]
: for the Alexandrov topology associated with the cell poset, functor categories on
\mathcal{X}
are equivalent to categories of cellular sheaves and cosheaves on the corresponding topological space. This makes it natural to ask whether the assembly picture can be formulated entirely in terms of the cell poset, without passing through an auxiliary delta-set presentation.

One expects the localized category to be controlled by chains on a based loop object. In the delta-set setting, such loop and path objects are already available through Kan’s combinatorial constructions, see
[
7
, §9]
. But in the regular CW setting, one would like a combinatorial model defined directly from the incidence data of cells. The present paper tackles this question by introducing a
path poset
and a
loop poset
associated with the cell poset. These posets are built from suitably comparable zig-zags in
\mathcal{X}
, which are called
fences
. Their classifying spaces recover the homotopy type of the corresponding based path and loop spaces, and they provide an explicit DG-algebra model for the derived endomorphisms of the generator of the localized category, whose objects we call
homotopy local systems
. In this way, the regular CW assembly formalism acquires the same local-to-global shape as in
[
12
]
, but with the loop-space object described directly in cellular terms.

Main Theorems

Historically, the appearance of loop-space algebras was motivated by algebraic
K
-theory. In Waldhausen’s
K
-theory, a connected based space is studied via modules over the loop-space ring spectrum
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Omega X
. The loop DG algebra appearing in
L
-theory should be viewed as the chain-level linear analogue of a structure that was first exploited on the
K
-theoretic side
[
14
]
. Hence, we first identify the loop-space object that controls the localized theory:

Theorem A

(Cellular loop-space model)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{X})
be a connected locally finite regular CW complex and let
x
be a
0
-cell. There are posets
\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}
and
\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}
, defined from fences in
\mathcal{X}
, together with an endpoint map
\mathrm{ev}_{1}\colon\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
such that
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}
is contractible and

\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}\xrightarrow{\;\mathbfcal{B}\mathrm{ev}_{1}\;}\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{X}

is a homotopy fiber sequence. In particular,

\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}\Omega_{x}X.

Moreover,
\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}
is a monoid in the category
\mathbfsf{Pos}
of small posets and order-preserving maps, so
C_{*}^{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X};R)
carries a natural DG-algebra structure.

The main motivating point of considering the localized theory is the question of whether it is possible to suitably “disassemble” or “fragment over
X
” an
R[\pi]
-chain complex. In the delta-set setting, this was studied in
[
12
]
. It turns out that for the desired algebraic
\pi
-
\pi
-theorem, only the properties of certain specific complexes obtained in this way inside the localized category are needed; see
[
11
, §10, page 110]
. The localization itself was described in
[
12
]
by explicit constructions via categories of fractions.

Our next result gives the regular CW analogue of the localization. To describe homotopy-theoretic properties of cellular (co)sheaves we use the language of model categories and derived categories. We assume slight familiarity with the standard notions and constructions of the theory. For background and terminology, we refer the reader to the monographs of Hovey and Hirschhorn
[
5
,
6
]
. Other necessary definitions are presented in Sections 2 and 3.

Theorem B

(Homotopy-local-system localization)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{X})
be a connected finite regular CW complex and let the abelian category of chain complexes of cellular cosheaves

\mathcal{M}:=\mathbfsf{Ch}(\mathbfsf{CoSh}(\mathcal{X},R))

be equipped with the projective model structure. Then the left Bousfield localization
L_{S}\mathcal{M}
of
\mathcal{M}
at global equivalences exists, and its fibrant objects are precisely the homotopy locally constant complexes of cellular cosheaves. If
D_{\mathrm{gc}}(\mathcal{X})\subset D(\mathcal{X})
denotes the full triangulated subcategory of globally contractible objects, then there is an equivalence of triangulated categories

D_{\mathrm{hlc}}(\mathcal{X})\cong D(\mathcal{X})/D_{\mathrm{gc}}(\mathcal{X}).

where
D_{\mathrm{hlc}}(\mathcal{X}):=\mathrm{Ho}(L_{S}\mathcal{M})
is the homotopy category and
D(\mathcal{X})/D_{\mathrm{gc}}(\mathcal{X})
is the Verdier quotient of triangulated categories. Moreover, for any
0
-cell
x
, the image of the elementary cosheaf
\{\widehat{x}\}_{R}
is a compact generator of
D_{\mathrm{hlc}}(\mathcal{X})
.

Remark
.

Unlike
[
12
]
, where the
local systems
name is used for arbitrary cellular cosheaves, we use the name
(homotopy) local systems
or
(homotopy) locally constant cosheaves
to denote those cosheaves whose structure maps are (homotopy) equivalences, which is closer to the standard conventions from the sheaf theory.

Theorem
B
identifies the localized category as the quotient. Factorization of the assembly functor through this quotient yields a canonical functor from
D_{\mathrm{hlc}}(\mathcal{X})
to
D(R[\pi])
. The next theorem makes the description of this functor easier by allowing us to present the localized category as the category of modules over the loop DG-algebra and prescribing the functor by its action on the generators. It is an analogue of
[
12
, Theorem 4.2]
:

Theorem C

(DG-algebra model for homotopy local systems)
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{X})
be a connected finite regular CW complex, let
x
be a
0
-cell and
\pi=\pi_{1}(X,x)
be the fundamental group. There is a canonical homotopy local system
E_{x}
such that
E_{x}
is the fibrant replacement of
\{\widehat{x}\}_{R}
and

\uHom_{D_{\mathrm{hlc}}(\mathcal{X})}(\{\widehat{x}\}_{R},\{\widehat{x}\}_{R})\cong\uHom_{D(\mathcal{X})}(\{\widehat{x}\}_{R},E_{x})\cong C_{*}^{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}};R)

in
D(R)
. Consequently the model category of homotopy local systems is Quillen equivalent to the model category of DG-modules over the loop DG algebra

C_{*}^{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}};R).

This DG algebra admits an augmentation

C_{*}^{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}};R)\longrightarrow R[\pi]

which induces an isomorphism on
H_{0}
.

Remark
.

In the notation of
[
11
, §10]
the system
E_{x}
is an analogue of the
homogeneous envelope

V^{\infty}\Gamma
.

Taken together, these results show that the part of the homotopy theory of cellular cosheaves detected by localization is governed by a single generator whose endomorphisms are computed by chains on a cellular model of the based loop space. The augmentation to
R[\pi]
recovers the usual passage from loop-space algebra to the group ring, and hence places the construction in the expected surgery-theoretic framework.

This paper develops a part of the technical framework for algebraic surgery over regular CW complexes. In the followup paper, we develop the corresponding chain duality, which allows us to construct
L
-theory in this setting. The results of the present paper are then used to prove the analogue of the algebraic
\pi
-
\pi
theorem. In this way, we obtain the complete algebraic surgery exact sequence for finite regular CW complexes, see
[
11
, §14]
, as well as a significant simplification of definitions and proofs of multiplicative formulas for surgery obstruction invariants, such as
[
11
, Proposition 21.1]
. On the
L
-homology term of the sequence such formulas were already obtained in
[
1
]
.

It should be mentioned, that there exists a treatment of the present theory for finite polyhedra in the setup of stable
\infty
-categories, presented mostly in
[
8
]
and partially in
[
3
]
. The present paper attempts to achieve the middle ground between Ranicki’s explicit approach and such abstract
\infty
-categorical treatment, where we both generalize to regular CW-complexes and utilize model-categorical tools to enjoy the advantages of abstract
1
-categorical reasoning, while retaining connections and freedom to use explicit formulas.

2.
Cellular loop spaces

The geometric input needed for Section
5
is a poset-level model for the based path fibration. In ordinary topology, the endomorphisms of a based fiber are controlled by the based loop space; here the analogous role will be played by the generator coming from Section
4
. For this section we only need the standing connected regular CW hypotheses together with local finiteness, and we denote the chosen base
0
-cell simply by
x
. The aim is to construct from the poset alone a combinatorial analogue of the based path fibration

\Omega_{x}X\longrightarrow{\mathsf{P}}_{x}X\xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{ev}_{1}\;}X.

The basic objects are zig-zags of comparable cells. They should be thought of as
cellular
paths in a CW complex: one is allowed to move from a cell to a face or coface, and the order relation on such zig-zags is generated by elementary homotopies that refine, shift, or extend the zig-zag.

2.1.
Preliminaries

Throughout, a regular CW complex means a CW complex in which each attaching map is a homeomorphism onto its image. If
(X,\mathcal{X})
is a regular CW complex, we write
\mathcal{X}
for its cell poset, ordered by

x\leq y\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad x\subseteq\overline{y}.

For a cell
x\in\mathcal{X}
we write

\mathrm{st}(x):=\{\,y\in\mathcal{X}\mid x\leq y\,\},\qquad\mathrm{cl}(x):=\{\,y\in\mathcal{X}\mid y\leq x\,\}.

We use the classifying-space notation
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{A}:=|\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{A})|
for any small category
\mathcal{A}
.

The combinatorics of a regular CW complex are encoded faithfully by its cell poset. In particular,
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{X}
identifies with the barycentric subdivision of
X
, so there is a canonical homeomorphism

\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{X}\cong X.

This is the main reason that functor categories on
\mathcal{X}
can be used as a genuinely cellular model for sheaf-theoretic constructions on
X
.

2.2.
Fences and path posets

Definition 2.1
.

A
based fence at
x
is a finite sequence

z=(x=x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

of cells of
\mathcal{X}
such that each consecutive pair
x_{i},x_{i+1}
is comparable. When convenient we display the relevant order symbols between consecutive entries, for example

z=(x=x_{0}\leq x_{1}\geq x_{2}\leq\cdots\geq x_{n}).

The integer
n
is called the
length
of the fence.

Two consecutive equal entries carry no geometric information, so they are removed.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{x}
be the set of based fences at
x
. Define an equivalence relation on
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{x}
generated by deleting a repeated adjacent entry,

(x_{0},\dots,x_{i},x_{i},\dots,x_{n})\sim(x_{0},\dots,x_{i},\dots,x_{n}).

A fence is
reduced
if no two consecutive entries coincide.

We next define the order relation. Intuitively, a fence becomes
smaller
when an ascending step is refined or an endpoint is pushed downward, and becomes
larger
under the dual descending operations. Peaks may move upward and valleys may move downward.

Definition 2.3
.

On
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{x}
define a relation generated by the following elementary moves.

(1)

Internal refinements.

(\cdots a\leq b\cdots)\geq(\cdots a\leq y\leq b\cdots)\qquad\text{whenever }a\leq y\leq b,

and dually

(\cdots a\geq b\cdots)\leq(\cdots a\geq y\geq b\cdots)\qquad\text{whenever }a\geq y\geq b.

(2)

Internal shifts.

(\cdots a\leq b\geq c\cdots)\leq(\cdots a\leq y\geq c\cdots)\qquad\text{whenever }b\leq y,

and dually

(\cdots a\geq b\leq c\cdots)\geq(\cdots a\geq y\leq c\cdots)\qquad\text{whenever }b\geq y.

(3)

Endpoint extension.

(\cdots a)\leq(\cdots a\leq y)\qquad\text{whenever }a\leq y,

and dually

(\cdots a)\geq(\cdots a\geq y)\qquad\text{whenever }a\geq y.

The
cellular path poset

\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}
is the quotient of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{x}
equipped with the induced relation, given by the reflexive and transitive closure of the elementary relations above.

The endpoint of a based fence depends only on its equivalence class, so there is a well-defined endpoint map

\mathrm{ev}_{1}\colon\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X},\qquad\mathrm{ev}_{1}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=x_{n}.

For each
y\in\mathcal{X}
define the fiber poset

\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X}:=\mathrm{ev}_{1}^{-1}(y)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X},\qquad\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}:=\mathcal{P}_{x,x}.

Thus
\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X}
is the poset of cellular paths from
x
to
y
, and
\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}
is the poset of cellular loops based at
x
.

Proposition 2.4
.

The induced relation on
\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}
is a partial order.

Proof.

Reflexivity and transitivity are built into the definition, so only antisymmetry requires proof. Let
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}
satisfy
z\leq z^{\prime}
and
z^{\prime}\leq z
. Choose reduced representatives, still denoted
z
and
z^{\prime}
, having minimal possible length in their respective equivalence classes.

An elementary move of type (1) or (3) changes the length by one unless it inserts a repeated adjacent entry; the latter possibility is invisible in
\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}
because we have already passed to the degeneracy quotient. Consequently, no zig-zag of inequalities from
z
to
z^{\prime}
and back can contain a genuine move of type (1) or (3): such a move would strictly change the minimal length and could only be undone by passing through a degenerate fence, contrary to the choice of reduced representatives.

Hence every step in both chains is of type (2). Consider one such step,

(\cdots a\leq b\geq c\cdots)\leq(\cdots a\leq y\geq c\cdots).

If the reverse inequality also occurs along the chain back, then necessarily
b\leq y
and
y\leq b
. Since
\mathcal{X}
is a poset,
b=y
, so the move is trivial. The same argument applies to downward shifts of valleys. Therefore every elementary step appearing in both directions is trivial, and we conclude
z=z^{\prime}
. This proves antisymmetry.
∎

Example 2.5

(The interval)
.

Let
X=[0,1]
with regular CW structure consisting of vertices
v_{0},v_{1}
and one edge
e
. If the basepoint is
x=v_{0}
, then every reduced fence from
v_{0}
to
v_{1}
is comparable to

(v_{0}\leq e\geq v_{1}).

In particular
\mathcal{P}_{v_{0},v_{1}}
is contractible, and the loop poset
\varOmega_{v_{0}}\mathcal{X}
is also contractible. This matches the fact that both the path space from
0
to
1
and the based loop space of an interval are contractible.

Example 2.6

(A cellular circle)
.

Let
X=S^{1}
with two vertices
v_{0},v_{1}
and two edges
e_{+},e_{-}
joining them. With basepoint
x=v_{0}
there are two basic reduced loop fences

\ell_{+}=(v_{0}\leq e_{+}\geq v_{1}\leq e_{-}\geq v_{0}),\qquad\ell_{-}=(v_{0}\leq e_{-}\geq v_{1}\leq e_{+}\geq v_{0}).

They represent the two directions around the circle, and concatenations of these fences give combinatorial representatives of all winding classes. Theorem
2.11
below identifies

\pi_{0}(\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{v_{0}}\mathcal{X})\cong\pi_{1}(S^{1},v_{0})\cong\mathbb{Z}.

2.3.
Path objects and loop fibers

We begin by showing that
\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}
is contractible. The argument is completely combinatorial and uses a filtration by fence length.

Proposition 2.7
.

For every cell
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the classifying space
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}
is contractible.

Proof.

For
n\geq 0
let
\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n)}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}
be the full subposet spanned by fences of length at most
n
. Then

\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(0)}=\{(x)\}

and

\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}=\colim_{n}\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n)}

as a filtered union.

For each
n\geq 0
define a truncation map

p_{n}\colon\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n+1)}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n)}

by deleting the last entry of a fence of length exactly
n+1
and acting as the identity on shorter fences. We first verify that
p_{n}
is order preserving. Because the order on
\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}
is generated by elementary moves, it is enough to check compatibility with those moves that involve the last step of a fence. The only possibilities are:

•

an ascending refinement at the end,

(\cdots a\leq b)\geq(\cdots a\leq y\leq b)\quad\text{truncates to}\quad(\cdots a\leq b)\geq(\cdots a\leq y)\quad\text{because}\quad y\leq b

•

a descending refinement at the end,

(\cdots a\geq b)\leq(\cdots a\geq y\geq b)\quad\text{truncates to}\quad(\cdots a\geq b)\leq(\cdots a\geq y)\quad\text{because}\quad y\geq b

•

an endpoint extension,

(\cdots a)\leq(\cdots a\leq y)\quad\text{or}\quad(\cdots a)\geq(\cdots a\geq y)\quad\text{becomes identity after truncation}

All other elementary moves take place away from the endpoint and are unaffected by truncation. Thus
p_{n}
is order preserving.

For every
z\in\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n+1)}
one has either
p_{n}(z)\leq z
or
z\leq p_{n}(z)
, according to whether the final step of
z
is descending or ascending. Let

A_{n}:=\{\,z\in\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n+1)}\mid p_{n}(z)\leq z\,\}.

Define

r_{n}\colon\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n+1)}\longrightarrow A_{n},\qquad r_{n}(z):=\begin{cases}z,&z\in A_{n},\\
p_{n}(z),&z\notin A_{n}.\end{cases}

We claim that
r_{n}
is order preserving. Suppose
z\leq z^{\prime}
. There are four cases.

•

If
z,z^{\prime}\in A_{n}
, then
r_{n}(z)=z\leq z^{\prime}=r_{n}(z^{\prime})
.

•

If
z,z^{\prime}\notin A_{n}
, then
r_{n}(z)=p_{n}(z)\leq p_{n}(z^{\prime})=r_{n}(z^{\prime})
because
p_{n}
is order preserving.

•

If
z\in A_{n}
and
z^{\prime}\notin A_{n}
, then
r_{n}(z)=z\leq z^{\prime}\leq p_{n}(z^{\prime})=r_{n}(z^{\prime})
.

•

If
z\notin A_{n}
and
z^{\prime}\in A_{n}
, then
r_{n}(z)=p_{n}(z)\leq p_{n}(z^{\prime})\leq z^{\prime}=r_{n}(z^{\prime})
.

Hence
r_{n}
is a functor. Let
j_{n}\colon A_{n}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n+1)}
be the inclusion. Then
r_{n}j_{n}=\mathrm{id}_{A_{n}}
and there is a natural transformation

\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n+1)}}\Longrightarrow j_{n}r_{n},\qquad z\longmapsto z\leq r_{n}(z),

where the inequality is equality on
A_{n}
and is
z\leq p_{n}(z)
off
A_{n}
. Therefore
\mathbfcal{B}A_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n+1)}
.

Now consider the inclusion
i_{n}\colon\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n)}\hookrightarrow A_{n}
. Since
p_{n}i_{n}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n)}}
and every
z\in A_{n}
satisfies
i_{n}p_{n}(z)=p_{n}(z)\leq z
, we also obtain a natural transformation

i_{n}p_{n}\Longrightarrow\mathrm{id}_{A_{n}}.

Thus
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n)}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}\mathbfcal{B}A_{n}
. Combining the two equivalences gives

\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n)}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n+1)}\qquad\text{for all }n\geq 0.

Since
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(0)}\cong*
, every
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n)}
is contractible.

Finally,

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X})=\colim_{n}\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}^{(n)})

because every simplex in the nerve uses only finitely many fences and therefore lies in some finite-length stage. The transition maps are monomorphisms of simplicial sets, hence cofibrations. A filtered colimit of contractible simplicial sets along cofibrations is contractible, so
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X})
is contractible. Geometric realization therefore gives
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}*
.
∎

The next two results identify the under-categories of the endpoint map and compare path posets with different targets.

Proposition 2.8
.

For every cell
y\in\mathcal{X}
, let

(y\downarrow\mathrm{ev}_{1}):=\{\,z\in\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}\mid y\leq\mathrm{ev}_{1}(z)\,\}

be the usual under-category, viewed here as a full subposet of
\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}
. Then the inclusion

\iota_{y}\colon\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X}\hookrightarrow(y\downarrow\mathrm{ev}_{1})

exhibits
\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X}
as a deformation retract of
(y\downarrow\mathrm{ev}_{1})
. In particular,

\mathbfcal{B}(y\downarrow\mathrm{ev}_{1})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X}.

Proof.

Define a retraction

s_{y}\colon(y\downarrow\mathrm{ev}_{1})\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X}

by appending
y
at the endpoint:

s_{y}(x=x_{0},\dots,x_{n}):=(x=x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\geq y).

This is well defined because
y\leq x_{n}=\mathrm{ev}_{1}(z)
for every object of
(y\downarrow\mathrm{ev}_{1})
. It is order preserving because each elementary move relating two fences remains valid after the same endpoint extension is appended to both sides.

For
z\in\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X}
one has
s_{y}(z)=z
in the quotient, since the appended final step is
(y\geq y)
and is removed by degeneracy. Thus
s_{y}\iota_{y}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X}}
. On the other hand every
z\in(y\downarrow\mathrm{ev}_{1})
satisfies

s_{y}(z)\leq z

by the endpoint-extension relation, so there is a natural transformation

\iota_{y}s_{y}\Longrightarrow\mathrm{id}_{(y\downarrow\mathrm{ev}_{1})}.

Hence
\iota_{y}
induces a homotopy equivalence on classifying spaces.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

If
a,b\in\mathcal{X}
are comparable, then

\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,a}\mathcal{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,b}\mathcal{X}.

Proof.

Assume first that
a\leq b
. Define functors

s_{a,b}\colon\mathcal{P}_{x,a}\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}_{x,b}\mathcal{X},\qquad r_{b,a}\colon\mathcal{P}_{x,b}\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}_{x,a}\mathcal{X}

by endpoint extension,

s_{a,b}(x=x_{0},\dots,a):=(x=x_{0},\dots,a\leq b),\qquad r_{b,a}(x=x_{0},\dots,b):=(x=x_{0},\dots,b\geq a).

Both are order preserving for the same reason as in Proposition
2.8
.

For
z\in\mathcal{P}_{x,a}\mathcal{X}
we have

r_{b,a}s_{a,b}(z)=(\cdots a\leq b\geq a)\geq(\cdots a)=z,

so there is a natural transformation

\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}_{x,a}\mathcal{X}}\Longrightarrow r_{b,a}s_{a,b}.

For
w\in\mathcal{P}_{x,b}\mathcal{X}
we similarly have

s_{a,b}r_{b,a}(w)=(\cdots b\geq a\leq b)\leq(\cdots b)=w,

which gives a natural transformation

s_{a,b}r_{b,a}\Longrightarrow\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{P}_{x,b}\mathcal{X}}.

Therefore
s_{a,b}
and
r_{b,a}
induce inverse homotopy equivalences on classifying spaces.

If instead
a\geq b
, the same construction with the order signs reversed gives natural transformations in the opposite directions. The conclusion is again that
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,a}\mathcal{X}
and
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,b}\mathcal{X}
are homotopy equivalent.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

For every
y\in\mathcal{X}
there is a homotopy equivalence

\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}.

Proof.

Because
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{X}\cong X
is connected, the comparability graph of
\mathcal{X}
is connected. Hence there exists a fence

(y=s_{0},s_{1},\dots,s_{m}=x)

from
y
to
x
. Successive pairs
s_{i},s_{i+1}
are comparable, so repeated application of Proposition
2.9
yields

\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,s_{1}}\mathcal{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}\cdots\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,s_{m}}\mathcal{X}=\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,x}\mathcal{X}=\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}.

∎

2.4.
Comparison with based loops

We now identify the cellular path object with the usual topological one after passing to classifying spaces.

Theorem 2.11
.

For every
y\in\mathcal{X}
the classifying space
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X}
is naturally homotopy equivalent to the homotopy fiber of
\mathbfcal{B}\mathrm{ev}_{1}
, hence the sequence

\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}\xrightarrow{\;\mathbfcal{B}\mathrm{ev}_{1}\;}\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{X}

is a homotopy fiber sequence.

Proof.

Fix a relation
a\leq b
in
\mathcal{X}
. The induced functor between under-categories is the inclusion

(b\downarrow\mathrm{ev}_{1})\hookrightarrow(a\downarrow\mathrm{ev}_{1}),

because
b\leq\mathrm{ev}_{1}(z)
implies
a\leq\mathrm{ev}_{1}(z)
. By Proposition
2.8
and Proposition
2.9
we have a chain of homotopy equivalences

\mathbfcal{B}(b\downarrow\mathrm{ev}_{1})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,b}\mathcal{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,a}\mathcal{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}\mathbfcal{B}(a\downarrow\mathrm{ev}_{1}).

Thus every morphism of
\mathcal{X}
induces a homotopy equivalence on the corresponding under-categories of
\mathrm{ev}_{1}
.

Quillen’s Theorem B
[
9
]
therefore applies to the functor
\mathrm{ev}_{1}\colon\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
. It implies that for each object
y\in\mathcal{X}
the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbfcal{B}(y\downarrow\mathrm{ev}_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces*}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbfcal{B}\mathrm{ev}_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}

is homotopy cartesian. By Proposition
2.8
, the upper-left corner is homotopy equivalent to
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X}
. This proves the claim.
∎

Corollary 2.12
.

Statement of
2.11
is the generalized version of Theorem A: specializing to
y=x
gives that
\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}
is the homotopy fiber of
\mathbfcal{B}\mathrm{ev}_{1}
over
x
. Since
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}
is contractible by Proposition
2.7
, the homotopy fiber is homotopy equivalent to the based loop space
\Omega_{x}\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{X}
. Finally
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{X}\cong X
for a regular CW complex, so
\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}\Omega_{x}X
.

2.5.
Monoid structure

The cellular loop poset carries a strictly associative concatenation operation.

Proposition 2.13
.

Concatenation of fences defines an order-preserving map

*\colon\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}\times\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X},

(x=x_{0},\dots,x_{n}=x)*(x=y_{0},\dots,y_{m}=x):=(x=x_{0},\dots,x_{n}=x=y_{0},\dots,y_{m}=x),

and makes
\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}
a monoid object in
\mathbfsf{Pos}
.

Proof.

The concatenated sequence is again a loop fence based at
x
, because the last entry of the first fence and the first entry of the second fence are both
x
. Degeneracies remove the repeated middle occurrence of
x
, so the operation is well defined on equivalence classes.

To show monotonicity, let
z\leq z^{\prime}
and
w\leq w^{\prime}
in
\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}
. Any elementary move relating
z
to
z^{\prime}
takes place entirely inside the first factor, and any elementary move relating
w
to
w^{\prime}
takes place entirely inside the second factor. Performing those same moves after concatenation shows

z*w\leq z^{\prime}*w^{\prime}.

The unit is the length-zero fence
(x)
. Indeed,

(x)*z=z=z*(x)

after deleting the repeated middle entry
x
. Associativity is strict at the level of concatenation of sequences, hence also after passing to the quotient. Therefore
\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}
is a monoid in
\mathbfsf{Pos}
.
∎

Corollary 2.14
.

The nerve
\mathcal{N}(\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X})
is a simplicial monoid, the realization
\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}
is a topological monoid, and for every commutative ring
R
the singular chains

C_{*}^{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X};R)

carry a natural DG-algebra structure.

Proof.

The nerve functor preserves products, so Proposition
2.13
makes
\mathcal{N}(\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X})
into a simplicial monoid. Geometric realization preserves finite products of simplicial sets, hence
\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}
is a topological monoid. Applying singular chains and the Eilenberg–Zilber shuffle map yields an associative product on chains with unit induced by the constant loop.
∎

Proof of Theorem A.

It follows from Corollary
2.12
and Corollary
2.14
.
∎

3.
Cellular cosheaves and assembly

Definition 3.1
.

A
cellular cosheaf
of
R
-modules on
(X,\mathcal{X})
is a functor

{\mathsf{\widehat{F}}}\colon\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\mathbfsf{Mod}_{R}.

Equivalently, one may view
{\mathsf{\widehat{F}}}
as a
\mathcal{X}
-constructible cosheaf on
X
, or as a cosheaf on the Alexandrov topology associated to the cell poset
\mathcal{X}
; by the Curry–Shepard correspondence, see
[
4
, Theorem 4.2.10]
, these descriptions determine equivalent categories. The category of such objects is denoted by

\mathbfsf{CoSh}(\mathcal{X},R):=\mathbfsf{Fun}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbfsf{Mod}_{R}).

Similarly one has cellular sheaves
\mathbfsf{Sh}(\mathcal{X},R)=\mathbfsf{Fun}(\mathcal{X},R)
, but after this point we only use sheaves when a comparison is needed. These categories are abelian (and even Grothendieck) categories.

For any
M\in\mathbfsf{Mod}_{R}
we write
\underline{M}
for the constant cosheaf with value
M
and identity structure maps.

3.1.
Elementary objects and model structure

For each cell
x\in\mathcal{X}
there is an evaluation functor

\mathrm{ev}_{x}\colon\mathbfsf{CoSh}(\mathcal{X},R)\longrightarrow\mathbfsf{Mod}_{R},\qquad\mathrm{ev}_{x}({\mathsf{\widehat{F}}})={\mathsf{\widehat{F}}}(x).

Its left adjoint is the elementary cosheaf supported on the closure of
x
.

Definition 3.2
.

For
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
M\in\mathbfsf{Mod}_{R}
, the
elementary cosheaf

\{\widehat{x}\}_{M}\in\mathbfsf{CoSh}(\mathcal{X},R)

is defined by

\{\widehat{x}\}_{M}(y):=\begin{cases}M,&y\in\mathrm{cl}(x),\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

with identity structure maps on the full subposet
\mathrm{cl}(x)
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For every
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
M\in\mathbfsf{Mod}_{R}
, and
{\mathsf{\widehat{F}}}\in\mathbfsf{CoSh}(\mathcal{X},R)
there is a natural isomorphism

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbfsf{CoSh}(\mathcal{X},R)}(\{\widehat{x}\}_{M},{\mathsf{\widehat{F}}})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,{\mathsf{\widehat{F}}}(x)).

In particular, if
P
is projective over
R
, then
\{\widehat{x}\}_{P}
is projective in
\mathbfsf{CoSh}(\mathcal{X},R)
.

Proof.

This is the usual Kan-extension adjunction for evaluation at a point. The last statement follows because
\mathrm{ev}_{x}
is exact.
∎

We use the following notation

\mathcal{M}:=\mathbfsf{Ch}(\mathbfsf{CoSh}(\mathcal{X},R))

for the abelian category of chain complexes of cellular cosheaves. It carries the standard projective model structure:

Proposition 3.4
.

The category
\mathcal{M}
admits a cofibrantly generated projective model structure in which

•

weak equivalences are objectwise quasi-isomorphisms,

•

fibrations are objectwise epimorphisms,

•

cofibrations are monomorphisms with objectwise projective and K-projective cokernel.

We denote the corresponding homotopy category by

D(\mathcal{X}):=\mathrm{Ho}(\mathcal{M}).

In particular, bounded-below complexes of coproducts of elementary projective cosheaves are cofibrant.

Proof.

This is the projective model structure on complexes in a functor category. Since the elementary cosheaves
\{\widehat{x}\}_{P}
are projective, bounded-below complexes built from them are K-projective and hence cofibrant.
∎

For finite
\mathcal{X}
, bounded complexes of finitely generated projective cellular cosheaves are perfect, hence compact in
D(\mathcal{X})
. This is the class of objects relevant to the later generator statements.

3.2.
Derived pushforwards

Let
f\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be an order-preserving map of posets. Precomposition gives a pullback functor

f^{*}\colon\mathbfsf{CoSh}(\mathcal{Y},R)\longrightarrow\mathbfsf{CoSh}(\mathcal{X},R),\qquad(f^{*}{\mathsf{\widehat{G}}})(x)={\mathsf{\widehat{G}}}(f(x)).

Its left adjoint is the cellular pushforward, defined by left Kan extension.

Definition 3.5
.

For a cellular cosheaf
{\mathsf{\widehat{F}}}\in\mathbfsf{CoSh}(\mathcal{X},R)
, define

f_{*}{\mathsf{\widehat{F}}}:=\mathrm{Lan}_{f^{\mathrm{op}}}({\mathsf{\widehat{F}}})\in\mathbfsf{CoSh}(\mathcal{Y},R).

Pointwise,

(f_{*}{\mathsf{\widehat{F}}})(y)\cong\colim_{f(x)\geq y}{\mathsf{\widehat{F}}}(x).

For the terminal map
p\colon\mathcal{X}\to*
, the object
p_{*}{\mathsf{\widehat{F}}}
is the cosheaf of global cosections:

p_{*}{\mathsf{\widehat{F}}}\cong\colim_{x\in\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}}}{\mathsf{\widehat{F}}}(x).

Because the projective model structure is objectwise, the functor
f_{*}
is left Quillen and therefore admits a total left derived functor

\mathbb{L}f_{*}\colon D(\mathcal{X})\longrightarrow D(\mathcal{Y}).

The key case is the terminal map, where derived pushforward is just homotopy colimit.

Proposition 3.6
.

For every
C\in D(\mathcal{X})
and every
y\in\mathcal{Y}
there is a natural identification in
D(\mathcal{Y})

(\mathbb{L}f_{*}C)(y)\cong\hocolim_{f(x)\geq y}C(x).

In particular, for the terminal map
p\colon\mathcal{X}\to*
one has

\mathbb{L}p_{*}C\cong\hocolim_{\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}}}C.

Proof.

The under-category defining the Kan extension is computed pointwise, and cofibrant replacement in the projective model structure is objectwise. Thus the derived left Kan extension is obtained by replacing the ordinary colimit with the corresponding homotopy colimit.
∎

Applying this to the constant cosheaf recovers the usual chain complex of the underlying space.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
p\colon\mathcal{X}\to*
be the terminal map. Then there is a natural isomorphism in
D(R)

\mathbb{L}p_{*}(\underline{R})\cong C_{*}^{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{X};R).

If
(X,\mathcal{X})
is a regular CW complex, this further identifies with

\mathbb{L}p_{*}(\underline{R})\cong C_{*}^{\mathrm{sing}}(X;R).

Proof.

One computes the homotopy colimit of the constant diagram by the usual bar construction. The resulting simplicial
R
-module is the simplicial chain complex of the nerve of
\mathcal{X}
, hence its normalization is quasi-isomorphic to
C_{*}^{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{X};R)
. For regular CW complexes,
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{X}
is the barycentric subdivision of
X
.
∎

3.3.
Assembly and representations

From this point onward, fix a connected regular CW complex
(X,\mathcal{X})
together with a chosen base
0
-cell. Also fix a universal covering

q\colon(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\mathcal{X}})\longrightarrow(X,\mathcal{X}),

and write
\pi:=\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})
. Additional finiteness hypotheses will be imposed explicitly when they are needed.

We write

\mathbfsf{Mod}_{R}^{\pi}:=\mathbfsf{Fun}(\mathcal{B}\pi,\mathbfsf{Mod}_{R})

for the category of
R
-linear representations of
\pi
. Equivalently, this is the category of left modules over the group ring
R[\pi]
. For
M\in\mathbfsf{Mod}_{R}
we denote by

\mathrm{Ind}_{\pi}(M):=R[\pi]\otimes_{R}M

the regular representation.

If
C\in\mathcal{M}
is a complex of cellular cosheaves, then its pullback
q^{*}C\in\mathbfsf{Ch}(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}})
carries a natural deck-transformation action of
\pi
. Passing to colimits or homotopy colimits over
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathrm{op}}
therefore produces objects of
\mathbfsf{Ch}(R[\pi])
.

Definition 3.8
.

The
universal assembly
of a cellular cosheaf complex
C
is

\mathrm{Asm}(C):=\colim_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathrm{op}}}q^{*}C\in\mathbfsf{Ch}(R[\pi]).

Its total left derived functor is denoted by

\mathbb{L}\mathrm{Asm}\colon D(\mathcal{X})\longrightarrow D(R[\pi]),\qquad\mathbb{L}\mathrm{Asm}(C)\cong\hocolim_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathrm{op}}}q^{*}C.

This description is equivalent to the left-Kan-extension construction through the covering translation category; the universal cover formula is the one most convenient for the arguments used in the following sections.

3.4.
Global equivalences

The assembly functor measures which objects become invisible after passage from local data on
X
to
\pi
-representations.

Definition 3.9
.

A morphism
f\colon C\to D
in
D(\mathcal{X})
is called a
global equivalence
if the image

\mathbb{L}\mathrm{Asm}(f)\colon\mathbb{L}\mathrm{Asm}(C)\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\mathbb{L}\mathrm{Asm}(D)

is an isomorphism in
D(R[\pi])
.
An object
C\in D(\mathcal{X})
is
globally contractible
if

\mathbb{L}\mathrm{Asm}(C)\cong 0.

We write

D_{\mathrm{gc}}(\mathcal{X}):=\ker(\mathbb{L}\mathrm{Asm})

for the full subcategory of globally contractible objects.

We will also say that morphism
f\in\mathcal{M}
is a global equivalence if its canonical image in
D(\mathcal{X})
is. The first important fact is that elementary cosheaves already detect the basic behavior of assembly:

Proposition 3.10
.

For every cell
x\in\mathcal{X}
and every
M\in\mathbfsf{Mod}_{R}
there is a natural isomorphism

\mathbb{L}\mathrm{Asm}(\{\widehat{x}\}_{M})\cong\mathrm{Ind}_{\pi}(M)

in
D(R[\pi])
. In particular, if
x\leq y
in
\mathcal{X}
, then the canonical morphism

\varphi_{xy}\colon\{\widehat{x}\}_{M}\longrightarrow\{\widehat{y}\}_{M}

is a global equivalence.

Proof.

Choose a lift
\widetilde{x}
of
x
. The pullback
q^{*}\{\widehat{x}\}_{M}
decomposes over the deck translates of
\widetilde{x}
, and each summand is supported on the opposite of the closure of a lift. Since indexing poset has the initial object
x
, its classifying space is contractible, hence the homotopy colimit over it is just the value at
x
, that is
M
. Summing over the
\pi
-orbit of lifts gives the regular representation
R[\pi]\otimes_{R}M
.

For
x\leq y
, choose compatible lifts
\widetilde{x}\leq\widetilde{y}
in each deck-translated sheet. On each such sheet the map
q^{*}\varphi_{xy}
induces the identity map on the corresponding copy of
M
, hence the induced map on assembly is an isomorphism.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

A morphism
f\colon C\to D
in
D(\mathcal{X})
is a global equivalence if and only if its cofiber belongs to
D_{\mathrm{gc}}(\mathcal{X})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{L}\mathrm{Asm}
is triangulated, it sends a distinguished triangle

C\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow\mathrm{cofib}(f)\longrightarrow\Sigma C

to a distinguished triangle in
D(R[\pi])
. It follows that
\mathbb{L}\mathrm{Asm}(f)
is an isomorphism precisely when the third term is zero, i.e. when
\mathbb{L}\mathrm{Asm}(\mathrm{cofib}(f))\cong 0
.
∎

The category
D_{\mathrm{gc}}(\mathcal{X})
is the subcategory that will be inverted in the construction of homotopy local systems.

3.5.
Local systems

A cellular sheaf or cosheaf is
locally constant
if every structure map is an isomorphism. For locally constant objects, passing from sheaves to cosheaves simply amounts to inverting the structure maps, so we may speak unambiguously of
local systems
on
\mathcal{X}
.

The point is that a functor on
\mathcal{X}
is locally constant precisely when it factors through the groupoid completion of
\mathcal{X}
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{X})
be a connected regular CW complex. Then there are natural equivalences of categories

\mathbfsf{Sh}_{\mathrm{lc}}(\mathcal{X},R)\cong\mathbfsf{CoSh}_{\mathrm{lc}}(\mathcal{X},R)\cong\mathbfsf{Fun}(\Pi_{1}(X),R)\cong\mathbfsf{Mod}_{R}^{\pi}\cong\mathbfsf{Mod}_{R[\pi]}.

Consequently,

D(\mathbfsf{CoSh}_{\mathrm{lc}}(\mathcal{X},R))\cong D(R[\pi]).

Proof.

A locally constant functor sends every morphism in
\mathcal{X}
to an isomorphism; hence, it factors uniquely through the localization
\mathcal{X}[\mathcal{X}^{-1}]
. For any small category
\mathcal{A}
, the groupoid completion
\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{A}^{-1}]
is equivalent to the fundamental groupoid of
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{A}
. Since
\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{X}\cong X
and
X
is connected, we obtain

\mathcal{X}[\mathcal{X}^{-1}]\cong\Pi_{1}(X)\cong\mathcal{B}\pi.

The remaining identifications are the standard equivalences between representations of
\pi
and modules over
R[\pi]
, together with passage to derived categories.
∎

Thus, the target of assembly is the classical category in which locally constant data on
X
are encoded by modules over the group ring. In the next section we describe the larger localization
D_{\mathrm{hlc}}(\mathcal{X})
by similarly explicit algebraic means.

4.
Homotopy local systems

We now use assembly to isolate the correct local objects. Assume for the rest of the discussion that
(X,\mathcal{X})
is finite.

4.1.
Localization and fibrant objects

Definition 4.1
.

A complex
C\in\mathcal{M}
is
homotopy locally constant
or a
homotopy local system
if for every relation
x\leq y
in
\mathcal{X}
the corresponding structure map

C(y)\longrightarrow C(x)

is a quasi-isomorphism of chain complexes of
R
-modules.

Thus a notion of a homotopy local system is obtained from the standard notion of a local system by replacing isomorphisms with quasi-isomorphisms.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
C\in D(\mathcal{X})
be homotopy locally constant. If
\mathbb{L}\mathrm{Asm}(C)\cong 0
, then
C\cong 0
in
D(\mathcal{X})
.

Proof.

Pull
C
back to the universal cover. Because every structure map of
C
is a quasi-isomorphism, the induced functor

\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow D(R)

inverts every morphism and therefore factors through the groupoid completion of
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
. Since
\mathbfcal{B}\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}\cong\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{X}
is simply connected, that groupoid completion is equivalent to a point. Hence
q^{*}C
is constant up to isomorphism in
D(R)
; choose a lift
\widetilde{x}_{0}
of the basepoint and write
M\cong C(\widetilde{x}_{0})
for the common value.

The derived assembly is then the homotopy colimit of a constant diagram,

\mathbb{L}\mathrm{Asm}(C)\cong C_{*}^{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbfcal{B}\widetilde{\mathcal{X}};R)\otimes_{R}^{\mathbb{L}}M.

The augmentation
C_{*}^{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbfcal{B}\widetilde{\mathcal{X}};R)\to R
and the
0
-simplex determined by
\widetilde{x}_{0}
split
M
off as a retract of the right-hand side. Therefore
\mathbb{L}\mathrm{Asm}(C)\cong 0
implies
M\cong 0
. Since
C
is homotopy locally constant, every value of
C
is isomorphic to
M
in
D(R)
, so
C\cong 0
.
∎

Theorem 4.3

(Characterization of the localized model structure)
.

Choose a set
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gc}}
of cofibrant complexes whose images generate
D_{\mathrm{gc}}(\mathcal{X})
as a localizing subcategory, and let

S:=\{\,0\to K\mid K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gc}}\,\}.

Equivalently, one may express this formally as

S=\{\,0\to K\mid K\in D_{\mathrm{gc}}(\mathcal{X})\,\}.

Then the left Bousfield localization

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hlc}}:=L_{S}\mathcal{M}

exists, see
[
6
, Chapter 4]
. Its weak equivalences are exactly the global equivalences, and its fibrant objects are exactly the homotopy locally constant complexes. In particular,
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hlc}}
is the left Bousfield localization of
\mathcal{M}
at global equivalences.

Proof.

The category
\mathcal{M}
is a left proper combinatorial stable model category, so localization at the set
S
exists
[
2
, 2.11]
.

An object
Z\in\mathcal{M}
is
S
-local precisely when

\uHom(K,Z)\cong 0\qquad\text{for every }K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gc}}.

Because
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{gc}}
generates
D_{\mathrm{gc}}(\mathcal{X})
as a localizing subcategory and
\uHom(-,Z)
sends triangles to triangles and coproducts to products, this is equivalent to

\uHom(K,Z)\cong 0\qquad\text{for every }K\in D_{\mathrm{gc}}(\mathcal{X}).

Let
f\colon A\to B
be a morphism in
\mathcal{M}
, and write
C:=\mathrm{cofib}(f)
. For every
S
-local object
Z
, applying
\uHom(-,Z)
to the distinguished triangle

A\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow\Sigma A

shows that
\uHom(B,Z)\to\uHom(A,Z)
is a quasi-isomorphism if and only if
\uHom(C,Z)\cong 0
. Hence
f
is an
S
-equivalence if and only if
C
is
S
-acyclic. In a stable left Bousfield localization, the
S
-acyclic objects form the smallest localizing subcategory containing the cofibers of the maps in
S
, so in the present case they are exactly the objects of
D_{\mathrm{gc}}(\mathcal{X})
. By Lemma
3.11
, it follows that the weak equivalences in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hlc}}
are precisely global equivalences.

Since every object of the projective model structure on
\mathcal{M}
is fibrant, the fibrant objects of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hlc}}
are exactly the
S
-local objects. Suppose first that
Z
is
S
-local. If
x\leq y
in
\mathcal{X}
, then Proposition
3.10
shows that the cofiber of

\varphi_{xy}\colon\{\widehat{x}\}_{R}\longrightarrow\{\widehat{y}\}_{R}

belongs to
D_{\mathrm{gc}}(\mathcal{X})
. Therefore

\uHom(\mathrm{cofib}(\varphi_{xy}),Z)\cong 0,

so the induced map

\uHom(\{\widehat{y}\}_{R},Z)\longrightarrow\uHom(\{\widehat{x}\}_{R},Z)

is a quasi-isomorphism. Using Proposition
3.3
, this is exactly the structure map
Z(y)\to Z(x)
. Hence
Z
is homotopy locally constant.

Conversely, let
C
be homotopy locally constant and choose a fibrant replacement

u\colon C\longrightarrow LC

in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hlc}}
. By the first part of the proof,
u
is a global equivalence, so
\mathrm{cofib}(u)
belongs to
D_{\mathrm{gc}}(\mathcal{X})
. We claim that
\mathrm{cofib}(u)
is again homotopy locally constant. Indeed, for each relation
x\leq y
, evaluation on the distinguished triangle

C\longrightarrow LC\longrightarrow\mathrm{cofib}(u)\longrightarrow\Sigma C

produces a morphism of distinguished triangles in
D(R)
, and since the vertical maps induced by
C
and
LC
are isomorphisms, the same holds for the vertical map induced by
\mathrm{cofib}(u)
. Thus
\mathrm{cofib}(u)
is homotopy locally constant. Lemma
4.2
now implies that
\mathrm{cofib}(u)\cong 0
.

Finally, let
K\in D_{\mathrm{gc}}(\mathcal{X})
. Applying
\uHom(K,-)
to the triangle above and using that
LC
is
S
-local gives

\uHom(K,C)\cong 0.

Hence
C
is
S
-local. Therefore the fibrant objects of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hlc}}
are exactly the homotopy locally constant complexes.
∎

4.2.
Quotients and generators

Let

D_{\mathrm{hlc}}(\mathcal{X}):=\mathrm{Ho}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hlc}})

denote the homotopy category of the localized model structure. Also, recall the notion of a Verdier quotient of triangulated categories

Proposition 4.5
.

There is a natural equivalence of triangulated categories

D_{\mathrm{hlc}}(\mathcal{X})\cong D(\mathcal{X})/D_{\mathrm{gc}}(\mathcal{X}).

where the quotient on the right is the Verdier quotient.

Proof.

Stable left Bousfield localization identifies the localized homotopy category with the Verdier quotient by its acyclic objects. By Theorem

This quotient description makes precise the slogan that homotopy local systems are obtained from all cellular cosheaf complexes by killing the part invisible to universal assembly.

5.
Loop DG algebra

Return now to the finite case, and keep the notation

p:=\mathrm{ev}_{1}\colon\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}.

In Section
2
we constructed the missing geometric ingredient: a combinatorial loop object whose classifying space models
\Omega_{x}X
. We now show that the same construction controls the endomorphisms of the compact generator of
D_{\mathrm{hlc}}(\mathcal{X})
. To match the order of composition of endomorphisms, it is convenient to write formulas using the opposite poset
\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}}
. Since a category and its opposite have canonically homeomorphic classifying spaces, this changes only the bookkeeping of multiplication, not the underlying homotopy type.

5.1.
A canonical homotopy local system

Definition 5.1
.

With
p=\mathrm{ev}_{1}
as above, define the
canonical homotopy local system

E_{x}:=\mathbb{L}p_{*}(\underline{R})\in D(\mathcal{X}),

where
\underline{R}
denotes the constant cellular cosheaf of
R

\mathcal{E}_{x}\mathcal{X}

Proposition 5.2
.

For every cell
y\in\mathcal{X}
there is a natural isomorphism in

E_{x}(y)\cong C_{*}^{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X};R).

In particular,

E_{x}(x)\cong C_{*}^{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X};R)\cong C_{*}^{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}};R).

Proof.

By Proposition
3.6
,

E_{x}(y)\,\cong\,\hocolim_{p(z)\geq y}\underline{R}(z).

The indexing poset is precisely the under-category
(y\downarrow p)
, so the homotopy colimit of the constant diagram is the singular chain complex of its classifying space:

E_{x}(y)\cong C_{*}^{\mathrm{sing}}(\mathbfcal{B}(y\downarrow p);R).

By Proposition
2.8
,
\mathbfcal{B}(y\downarrow p)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.1}{$\displaystyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\textstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}{\scalebox{1.1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\simeq$}}}\mathbfcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X}
, and this gives the first claim. When
y=x
we have
\mathcal{P}_{x,x}=\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}
. The final identification uses the canonical homeomorphism between the realizations of the nerve of a category and of its opposite.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

The object
E_{x}
is homotopy locally constant.

Proof.

Let
y\leq z
in
\mathcal{X}
. By Proposition
5.2
, the corresponding structure map of

is represented by the map on chains induced from the functor between under-categories

(z\downarrow p)\longrightarrow(y\downarrow p).

After identifying these under-categories with
\mathcal{P}_{x,z}\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{P}_{x,y}\mathcal{X}
via Proposition
2.8
, this becomes the map between path posets induced by the relation
y\leq z
. Proposition
2.9
shows that the induced map on classifying spaces is a homotopy equivalence. Therefore, the associated map on singular chains is a quasi-isomorphism. Since this holds for every relation

, the object

is homotopy locally constant.
∎

Analogous to the homogeneous envelope

Proof.

Under Proposition
5.2

is the chain complex of the loop space

Proposition 5.6

(Augmentation to the group ring)
.

There is a morphism of DG algebras

\varepsilon\colon A_{x}\longrightarrow R[\pi]

which induces an isomorphism on
H_{0}
.

Proof.

The target is regarded as a DG algebra concentrated in degree
0
. We define
\varepsilon
on singular simplices. If

\sigma\colon\Delta^{0}\longrightarrow\mathbfcal{B}\varOmega_{x}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{op}}

\sigma
determines a path component of

We send
\sigma
to the basis vector of

This is a chain map because the boundary of a
1

\sigma

\sigma
and
\tau

Finally,

H_{0}(A_{x})\cong H_{0}(\Omega_{x}X;R)\cong R[\pi_{0}(\Omega_{x}X)]\cong R[\pi_{1}(X,x)],

so
