3. Project Summary: The project addressed an inability to monitor avian interactions
 with photovoltaic (PV) solar energy facilities necessary for understanding PV solar
 impacts on birds. In the project, machine-vision technology that continuously
 monitors avian activities at PV solar facilities was developed. The technology
 includes four machine-learning (ML) models, each of which accomplishes a specific
 task in detecting birds and classifying their activities in live or recorded videos—
 detecting and tracking moving objects, differentiating birds from other objects,
 detecting bird collisions with solar panels, and classifying non-collision bird activities
 around PV facilities. Major project outcomes include adoption by two of DOE
 SETO’s SolWEB projects, providing novel observational data on birds to promote
 co-location of PV solar development and habitat conservation, known as ecovoltaics.
4. Project Objectives and Outcomes: The goal of this project was to develop a
 technology for automated monitoring of avian-solar interactions using a ML
 approach in conjunction with a high-definition video camera and edge computing.
 Avian interactions of interest included fly over above, fly through, perch on panel,
 land on ground, perch in background, and collide with panel. The goal was
 accomplished by meeting three objectives: (1) assembling camera systems that
 enable continuous video collection and execution of ML models to perform avian
 monitoring, (2) developing ML models that detect birds and classify their interactions
 with PV solar infrastructure in videos, and (3) validating the system at a PV site for
 its bird detection and activity classification capability in a real-world environment.
 By meeting the above objectives, this project produced an AI-enabled avian-PV
 solar interactions monitoring technology. The technology consists of four ML models,
 each of which performs specific tasks to detect birds and classify their activities with
 a minimum accuracy of 94.6% and 83.4%, respectively, well surpassing the target
 accuracy of 80% and 70%, respectively. These models can be executed on an
 edge-computing camera for near real-time monitoring or on recorded videos off-
 camera using high-performance computing resources, which is one of the changes
 we made during the project in response to stakeholder feedback. The project also

produced >20,000 hours of daytime video footage collected at PV solar facilities and
>200,000 tracks of moving objects, which included 68,646 bird tracks, extracted from
the footage. More than ten graduate students at local universities and experts from
an AI camera company participated in the development of these ML models and
software integration. Upon the project completion, the technology was adopted by
two DOE-funded projects under the DOE SETO SolWEB opportunity. Through these
studies, the technology is being deployed at PV solar energy facilities across seven
U.S. regions—Pacific Southwest, Mountain-Prairie, Midwest, Northeast, Mid-Atlantic,
Southwest, and Southeast—to provide continuous bird observations to help improve
an understanding of potential risks and benefits of large-scale PV solar energy
facilities on wildlife. A full specification of the machine-vision technology and findings
from video analysis were published in peer-reviewed journals, conference
proceedings/presentations to disseminate information across the solar industry,
governmental agencies, and conservation organizations to be used as reference for
designing and implementing wildlife-conscious large-scale PV solar facility siting.
The significance of this project includes the development of technology that fills
critical data gaps to address outstanding questions on avian-PV solar interactions. It
was developed with experts of machine vision, solar energy, and avian and wildlife
monitoring from private industry, governmental agencies, and conservation
organizations. Fundamental advances of this project are (1) realizing continuous
observations of birds’ natural movements around PV solar energy facility
infrastructure, which no existing methods could accomplish, (2) enabling collection of
large volume of data on avian-PV solar interactions via standardized, simultaneous
observations at multiple PV facilities across the U.S., and (3) increasing the
possibility of observing causes of avian mortality at PV solar energy facilities, such
as predations by wildlife and collisions with panels and stranding inside the facilities.
Innovations that made these advances possible include a suite of ML models
applying novel architectures, each of which accomplishes a specific task. For
example, the algorithms utilize a multi-step process for moving object detection,
tracking and classification. Moving object detection and tracking are done as a first
pass to find objects to classify and then group the individual objects into a sequence
of data as the object moves across the field of view of the camera. Novel neural
network architectures were then developed that are capable of performing object,
activity and collision classification based on this entire sequence of both image and
metadata of each moving object. The final models were custom architectures based
on Multiple Instance Learning and Bi-directional Long Short-Term Memory. The
ability to perform classification on the sequence or object track data significantly
outperformed similar classifications using a single image or datapoint input.

stakeholder’s avian-PV solar monitoring priority, accomplishing critical milestones
while negotiating several unforeseen events during the project.

5. Path Forward: According to solar stakeholders and experts, understanding avian-
 solar collisions, preferably by type (e.g., waterfowl, raptor, and passerine, at
 minimum), remains priority for the Southwest and Great Plain regions; thus,
 additional field validation at the proper geographic region would be beneficial. The
 importance of nighttime monitoring, which our current technology does not support,
 is mentioned by multiple stakeholders. In contrast, for other regions, the
 stakeholders expressed a greater interest in understanding birds’ post-construction
 site use and nesting activities than in understanding collisions or stranding. Interests
 in bird nesting activities vary by region (e.g., woodpeckers in the Mid-Atlantic, and
 kestrels in the Northeast, and ground-nesting species in the Midwest). One
 consensus among the stakeholders and experts was that continuous videos provide
 data that are unattainable from other sources. They overwhelmingly support the
 value of video data—visual observations with motion. Current barriers that prevent
 use of the technology are lack of comparative understanding of the nature of data
 produced by videos and ML models relative to field observations.
