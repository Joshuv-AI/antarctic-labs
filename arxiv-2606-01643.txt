Conditional Collapse in Sign Language Production: A Diagnostic and a Scaling Argument
Rui Hong, Jana Košecká

Abstract
Sign Language Production (SLP) is the task of generating avatar sign language motion from natural language text. The quality of the generated motion is typically evaluated by a motion-space Fréchet distance (FID) and back-translation (BT) BLEU score on benchmarks such as How2Sign. Both metrics can improve substantially while the underlying generator fails to faithfully represent the sign language gestures. In this work we propose to evaluate the generated motion at three independent levels: ( τ 1 \tau_{1} ) initial-pose conditioning, ( τ 2 \tau_{2} ) output diversity, and ( τ 3 \tau_{3} ) target faithfulness. We compute these as pairwise-distance ratios using latent representations of a frozen motion autoencoder (MoAE). We evaluate 14 14 SLP model checkpoints on How2Sign dataset ( Duarte et al. 2021 ) , including a re-implemented Neural Sign Actors (NSA) ( Baltatzis et al. 2024 ) , and show that τ 3 \tau_{3} faithfulness is never attained, while FID varies by nearly two orders of magnitude and is uncorrelated with faithfulness. We show that on the isolated gloss dataset ASL3DWord ( Dong et al. 2024a ) favorable τ 3 \tau_{3} can be attained, hence isolating the size of the sentence-level paired-dataset as the bottleneck.



1
Introduction

Text-to-motion systems report steady improvements on standard quantitative benchmarks.
Reliable evaluation of text-to-motion generation requires metrics
that respond to whether each output is faithful to its specific input,
not merely to whether outputs look like plausible samples from the
training distribution. We study this requirement in sentence-level
Sign Language Production (SLP), where recent systems on
How2Sign
(
Duarte et al. 2021
)
report steadily improving
Fréchet distance (FID), computed over motion features, and
back-translation (BT) BLEU score, and declare progress toward usable
text-to-sign generation. We argue that both headline metrics can rise while the underlying
generator remains essentially text-independent. FID rewards an
anatomically valid mean motion that ignores the input over a model
that follows the input but produces slightly noisier motion. It
can thus prefer a collapsed generator over a faithful one, as we
observe on How2Sign (§
5.2
); back-translation
BLEU is bounded by a BT model that itself collapses
to high-frequency boilerplate, so it reflects token-frequency overlap
rather than motion-text correspondence.
In this work, we propose novel evaluation and diagnostic metrics that
reveal conditional collapse in Sign Language Production on
state-of-the-art datasets.

Contributions.

(i)
Given any text-to-motion model and a held-out test set, we
encode both ground-truth motions and model generations through a
frozen motion autoencoder MoAE (a small transformer)
trained once on the target dataset’s ground-truth motions, compressing
each motion into a compact latent vector and reconstructing it.
We then compute three pairwise-distance ratios in that space, varying
the sentence conditioning and comparing the generated and ground-truth
motions.

(ii)
We identify
three indicators
of conditional collapse
at different levels: an initial-pose level (
\tau_{1}
), an output
diversity level (
\tau_{2}
), and a faithfulness level (
\tau_{3}
) that can
be attained
independently
, with visual and quantitative
evidence (§
4
).

(iii)
We transfer the same generative diffusion model backbone to the gloss-level
ASL3DWord benchmark, showing that it attains significantly better
\tau_{3}
,
suggesting that sentence-level paired-data scale is a major bottleneck for How2Sign within the architecture space we tested
(§
5.4
, Figure
3
).

The failures we document are diagnosable and motivate three concrete changes. The field should
(a) report the diagnostic ratios alongside FID and back-translation BLEU;
(b) be transparent about the paired-data budget and any auxiliary
pretraining corpora; and (c) treat the faithfulness level (
\tau_{3}
) as an important criterion
when designing future models
(§
6
).

FID critiques.

The limitations of Frechet Distance (FID) for conditional generation are well known in

(

(

Our diagnostic is in this spirit but specifically targets the

Pose-based SL generation evaluation.

Most directly adjacent,

meta-evaluate

3
Approach

Let
f_{\theta}
be any Sign Language Production (SLP) model, regression
or diffusion-based, that takes a sentence in natural language and
generates a sequence of human poses. Consider

\{(t_{i},m_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
the full test set, where
t_{i}
is the

i
-th text input and
m_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times D}
its
ground-truth motion clip.
Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{T\times D}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a frozen
motion autoencoder MoAE, with latent dimension
d=256
.
We use
T=100
frames, per-frame

D=44\text{ upper-body joints}\times 3=132
pelvis-relative

3
D coordinates and trained it on How2Sign train
motions (see architecture in Appendix
A
). Define

z_{i}:=\phi(f_{\theta}(t_{i};s_{1}))
as the latent representation of SLP model
output, and
z_{i}^{*}:=\phi(m_{i})
, the MoAE encoded GT motion for the
associated text sentence. We compute the following pairwise statistics:

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{inter}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{i\neq j}\,\|z_{i}-z_{j}\|_{2},

(1)

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{intra}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{i}\,\big\|\phi(f_{\theta}(t_{i};s_{1}))-\phi(f_{\theta}(t_{i};s_{2}))\big\|_{2}.

(2)

Here
s_{1}\neq s_{2}

are two independent sampling seeds of a stochastic (diffusion)
generator; a deterministic (regression) model takes no seed, so

f_{\theta}(t_{i};s_{1})=f_{\theta}(t_{i};s_{2})
.

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{inter\text{-}GT}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{i\neq j}\,\|z_{i}^{*}-z_{j}^{*}\|_{2},

(3)

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{pair}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{i}\,\|z_{i}-z_{i}^{*}\|_{2},

(4)

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{pair\text{-}rand}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{i}\,\|z_{i}-z_{\pi(i)}^{*}\|_{2}.

(5)

Here
\pi
is a fixed random
derangement of the test indices, i.e. a random shuffling of indices

\{1,\dots,N\}
; so
z_{\pi(i)}^{*}
is the GT motion of some test
sample other than
i
.

Only
d_{\mathrm{inter}}
and
d_{\mathrm{pair}}
capture how the
output pose is affected by the input sentence and whether it lands
on the correct target;
d_{\mathrm{intra}}
isolates pure
sampling noise, so input-driven variation can be told apart from it;

d_{\mathrm{inter\text{-}GT}}
supplies the natural cross-sentence
diversity scale, a small
d_{\mathrm{inter}}
counts as collapse only
relative to it; and
d_{\mathrm{pair\text{-}rand}}
measures
whether the generated SLP output is closer to another example
other than its own GT.

Three diagnostic ratios.
From these we form three unitless
quantities: a
condition
ratio (input-driven variance vs. sampling noise), a
diversity
ratio (output variance vs. GT
cross-sentence variance), and a
faithfulness
ratio (target
alignment vs. a random-GT baseline):

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{cond}}

\displaystyle=d_{\mathrm{inter}}/d_{\mathrm{intra}},

(6)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{div}}

\displaystyle=d_{\mathrm{inter}}/d_{\mathrm{inter\text{-}GT}},

(7)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}

\displaystyle=d_{\mathrm{pair}}/d_{\mathrm{pair\text{-}rand}}.

(8)

Since
\rho_{\mathrm{cond}}
requires a stochastic sampling, a deterministic model has

d_{\mathrm{intra}}=0
(no seed dependence), making
\rho_{\mathrm{cond}}

undefined. The other two ratios use no resampling and apply unchanged;
we report
\rho_{\mathrm{div}}
and
\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}
in that case.

FID measures a Fréchet distance
between two unconditional Gaussians fit to feature distributions: a
model whose outputs cluster around the GT marginal mean attains small
FID even when individual outputs
f_{\theta}(t_{i})
are not faithful
motion representing their input
t_{i}
.
The guideline of
\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}
approaching
1.0
captures
this failure mode directly. The diagnostic requires no new training
(it reuses one frozen MoAE and
O(N^{2})
pairwise distances on
the full test split) and costs at most one extra forward pass per
sentence (the second seed for
d_{\mathrm{intra}}
; none for
deterministic models).

3.1
Stability of the diagnostic

We compute these diagnostics for a large variety of models and restrictions on train and test datasets.
The numbers in Table
1
use the
full
test split of each dataset,
so the reported values do not depend on which subsample of test
sentences is drawn. Even on the full test split, almost every SLP model we test gives

\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}\approx 1
. A fair concern is that this value reflects a
problem with the metric itself, not with the models: either (i) the
MoAE cannot tell motions apart in its latent space, or (ii)
\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}

always returns
\approx 1
regardless of input. We rule both out with
two checks.

(1) MoAE initialization.
Re-training the MoAE from a
different random initialization (same architecture and data) and
re-running the diagnostic preserves the relative ordering of all
measured main-table checkpoints; the diagnostic is robust to MoAE
training noise and not an artefact of one particular MoAE fit.

(2) GT-vs-GT check: can
\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}
ever move?

To rule out (ii), we feed the diagnostic the strongest possible
“alignment” case: use the GT motion itself in place of the
model output. Setting
z_{i}=z_{i}^{*}=\phi(m_{i})
in
\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}
trivially
returns
0
when each input has a single matched GT, as on How2Sign
(
96.6\%/82.2\%
of train/test sentences are
unique; row 15 of Table
1
).
The more informative case is
ASL3DWord, where multiple GT clips per gloss (
\sim
3.3
/gloss)
admit a non-trivial within- vs across-gloss reference,

\rho_{\mathrm{faith,ref}}=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{g,\,k\neq k^{\prime}}\|z^{*}_{g_{k}}-z^{*}_{g_{k^{\prime}}}\|}{\mathbb{E}_{g\neq g^{\prime},\,k,k^{\prime}}\|z^{*}_{g_{k}}-z^{*}_{g^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}}\|},

(9)

which evaluates to
0.752
on ASL3DWord (App.
A
,
§
5.4
) — a non-zero floor capturing real
across-take variance within a gloss.

4
Three Diagnostic Levels of Conditional Collapse

The diagnostic ratios decompose SLP failure into three independent

levels
, denoted
\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}
,
\tau_{3}
:

\tau_{1}
: Initial-pose level.

Satisfied when the model’s

t=0
pose varies as a function of the input text (
varied
);
failed when every generated initial pose is identical across sentences
(
frozen
). Visually inspectable in rendered output.

\tau_{2}
: Output diversity level.

Satisfied when outputs
vary across different inputs as much as GT motions do across
sentences (
\rho_{\mathrm{div}}\in[0.5,2.0]
); failed when outputs cluster
around a near-identical mean motion (
\rho_{\mathrm{div}}\to 0
).

\tau_{3}
: Faithfulness level.

Satisfied when each output
aligns to its specific target GT and not to a random one
(
\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}
near the dataset’s GT-vs-GT value); failed when
alignment is no better than random (
\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}\approx 1
;
Section
3
).

The central empirical finding of this paper is that these three diagnostic
levels are

independently attainable
: a model can satisfy
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
without attaining
\tau_{3}
, but on How2Sign,
no configuration we
tested attains satisfactory
\tau_{3}

. Every checkpoint we evaluated falls
into one of three cases:

(i) Frozen start + jitter trajectory

(
\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}
,
\tau_{3}
all collapsed). The dominant How2Sign 26K
failure mode is a near-identical canonical start regardless of input,
then small-amplitude noise around the GT marginal mean. Covers
essentially all full-corpus sentence-level checkpoints, including our
prior ablation’s FID champion.

(ii) Varied start + jitter trajectory

(
\tau_{1}
satisfactory,
\tau_{2}
satisfactory,
\tau_{3}
collapsed).
A visibly different start per input, but the trajectory is still
noise, not coherent signing. Appears only under aggressive
input-distribution narrowing: small vocabularies or short
sentences (configurations in §
5
and
Appendix
A
).

(iii) Varied start + coherent motion

(
\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}
,
\tau_{3}
all satisfactory). This case is never
observed on How2Sign and is reached only on the gloss-level
ASL3DWord dataset (§
5.4
).

Note that for the same
dataset configurations that achieve satisfactory
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
,

\tau_{3}
indicates collapse. Table

reports which
configurations fall into which case. Figure

show visibly different starts (

satisfied). Even with

\tau_{1}
fully satisfied (row 4) the trajectory is jitter that does not

6
Discussion

Why FID misleads in SLP.

Beyond the mean-motion mechanism of §
3
, a model
whose outputs match the GT distribution but are paired with the wrong
inputs also gets a low FID; neither case matches what a reader expects
from a small FID number. The blind spot is worse in SLP because GT
motion varies in a small range
anatomical envelope)
and test sets are typically only a few hundred
motions.

Reproducibility note on Neural Sign Actors.

Neural Sign Actors
(
Baltatzis et al. 2024
)
reports the lowest
sentence-level How2Sign FID in the literature but releases neither
code nor weights. Following the published description, our
re-implementation needed several debugging rounds (gradient-norm
clipping, loss-formulation fixes) to converge, and even then our best
run reaches FID
\approx 151
(an order of magnitude above the
reported value), with
\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}\approx 1.00
and no input-specific
motion in rendered samples.
We do not interpret this discrepancy, and it does not
affect our claim: at the How2Sign 26K budget
even an NSA-class FID is consistent with
\tau_{1}
/
\tau_{2}
satisfied
+

\tau_{3}

collapsed, the regime our
14
How2Sign checkpoints occupy. We read prior
FID gains in this line as improvements in
\tau_{1}
and partial
\tau_{2}
,
not
\tau_{3}
.

Recommended evaluation protocol.

We recommend SLP papers report all three ratios alongside FID:

\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}
as a bright-line check (treat
\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}\geq 0.95
as
failure), and
\rho_{\mathrm{div}}
to separate “random anatomical poses”
(low
\rho_{\mathrm{div}}
) from “diverse but mis-aligned outputs” (near-GT

\rho_{\mathrm{div}}
). Computing the diagnostic on a
shared
external motion
MoAE (we release ours, Appendix
A
) also removes the
per-paper-MoAE inconsistency behind FID drift across the literature.
The diagnostic is parameter-free and adds negligible cost on top of
standard generation.

Bidirectional evidence of the data bottleneck.

The bottleneck is independently visible in the sign-to-text direction.

Uthus et al. 2023
report that a translator trained

directly
on How2Sign reaches only BLEU-4
\approx 1.22
,
consistent with output dominated by high-frequency boilerplate
(§
5.3
, Table
2
), with faithful translation
emerging only after
11
K hours of pretraining. Our text-to-motion
experiments reach the symmetric conclusion:
\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}
never leaves the
collapse band on How2Sign 26K and the
13
K
\to
26
K gradient does
not improve it. The bottleneck is the same in both directions:
How2Sign’s
26
K paired sentences are below what is needed to learn
the motion–text correspondence.

What
would
satisfy
\tau_{3}
?

The most concrete candidate is large-scale
text-to-motion

pretraining on an auxiliary corpus: the YouTube-ASL translator becomes
faithful in the sign-to-text direction only after
11
K hours of
pretraining, and a similarly-scaled motion pretrain may transfer to
text-to-motion.

7
Conclusion

We introduced a diagnostic that exposes conditional collapse in
sentence-level Sign Language Production, and used it to establish
that the How2Sign paired-data scale is a major bottleneck on the
faithfulness level (
\tau_{3}
) within the architecture space we
tested, with collapse persisting across data quantity and input
unit length. A best-effort reimplementation of
Neural Sign Actors places it in the same
\tau_{3}
-collapsed regime
as the rest of our checkpoints, while the same
diffusion backbone and loss transferred unchanged to gloss-level ASL3DWord
satisfies
\tau_{3}
(
\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}=0.73
), consistent with sentence-level
paired-data scale being a major bottleneck within the architecture
space we tested. The FID–alignment disconnect we document argues for a field-wide
protocol change: SLP papers should report

(\rho_{\mathrm{cond}},\rho_{\mathrm{div}},\rho_{\mathrm{faith}})
alongside FID and BLEU, and treat

\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}\geq 0.95
as a clear failure. We release all our
code
1
1

1

https://github.com/hongrui16/slp-diagnostic
so that future
papers can compute identical ratios.

8
Limitations

Our
14
How2Sign checkpoints cover two architecture families and
four data axes but do not cover the full design space; in particular,
we do not test large-scale text-to-motion pretraining on an auxiliary
corpus. The diagnostic has the limitations of any MoAE: a poorly-trained
MoAE could in principle push
\rho_{\mathrm{faith}}
artificially close to
1
. We
address this with a single frozen MoAE trained on the target
dataset’s GT train motions, the MoAE-initialization and GT-vs-GT
checks of Section
3.1
, and an
independent check against visual classification, which gives the same
conclusion on every checkpoint where we did it.

Appendix A
Implementation details

MoAE.

The MoAE
\phi
used by
verify-collapse
is a
small transformer autoencoder operating on per-frame

3
-D joint coordinates: each generated SMPL-X axis-angle sequence is
first lifted to
T\times 144\times 3
joints by SMPL-X forward
kinematics, restricted to the
44
upper-body joints (spine, head,
shoulders, elbows, wrists, all
30
finger joints), and made
pelvis-relative, yielding per-frame input dimension
D=132
. A
linear projection lifts each frame to a
d_{\mathrm{model}}=256

token; learnable absolute positional embeddings are added; a
3
-layer
transformer encoder (
4
attention heads, feed-forward dim
512
,
GELU, pre-LN) produces contextualized per-frame features. A masked
mean over valid frames (padding excluded) gives the
256
-dim
bottleneck
z
. The decoder broadcasts
z
across the target
sequence length, adds the same positional embeddings, runs through
another
3
-layer transformer stack with the same hyper-parameters,
and projects back to
D
. Position embeddings are shared between
encoder and decoder; padding is handled with a boolean mask in both
directions.

Training loss.
For each batch we minimize per-frame MSE
between reconstruction and target plus an

\alpha_{\mathrm{vel}}=0.5
MSE on the first-difference (velocity)
of the reconstruction:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MoAE}}\;=\;\mathrm{MSE}(\hat{x},x)\;+\;\alpha_{\mathrm{vel}}\,\mathrm{MSE}(\Delta\hat{x},\Delta x).

The velocity term is essential: without it, the MoAE collapses to
emitting a per-sample mean pose at every frame, which would make
z

insensitive to motion dynamics and defeat the diagnostic; the
encoder must retain information about
how
the body moves, not
only
what
poses it visits. Training: How2Sign train motions
only, sequence length
T=200
frames, batch size
128
,
100

epochs, AdamW (
Loshchilov and Hutter 2019
) at lr

3\times 10^{-4}
, dropout
0.1
(eval at dropout
0
), bf16 mixed
precision. The encoder is never exposed to model-generated motion
during training, so the bottleneck does not learn collapse-specific
features that could bias the diagnostic toward any particular
generator.

The
same
frozen
\phi
checkpoint is used to score every
How2Sign diagnostic in this paper, removing per-paper FID-feature
inconsistency. A separate MoAE of the same architecture is
trained on Phoenix train motions only (
7{,}096
sequences) and is
used for the PT leak audit (Appendix
C
). The MoAE-initialization and GT-vs-GT checks that validate the diagnostic
are reported in Section
3.1
.

Cross-dataset ratios are qualitative, not quantitative.

The two non-How2Sign diagnostics in this paper (gloss-level
ASL3DWord, row 16 of Table

Section

Therefore, within a single dataset, an

The prompt below is sent to the LLM once per sentence, with

reproduced verbatim and spans multiple pages.

You are an ASL (American Sign Language) linguistic expert. Your
task is to extract pseudo-gloss from an English sentence.

Pseudo-gloss is a sequence of words that determine the core meaning
of the sentence. Each word should correspond to a distinct sign
that an ASL signer would produce. The test is: "If I remove this
word, does the core meaning of the sentence change?"

Follow these three steps in order:

## Step 1: Phrase-level chunking
Identify multi-word structures and treat them as a single unit
BEFORE analyzing individual words:
- Auxiliary verb chains: "is going to", "has been doing", "would
have been", "are going to be learning" -> mark the entire chain
as auxiliary, drop it all
- Phrasal verbs: "get out", "pick up", "break down", "set up",
"hold down" -> mark as a single verb unit, keep the particle
- Light verb constructions: "make sure" -> "sure", "take a look"
-> "look", "get rid of" -> "rid" -- the light verb (make/take/
get) is structural, drop it
- Discourse frames: "let me/let’s" (when meaning "allow me to"),
"you know", "I mean" -> drop

This step is critical: without it, "going" inside "is going to"
would be mistakenly kept as "go".

## Step 2: Decide which units determine the core meaning
Keep words that would be produced as an independent sign in ASL:
- Content words: nouns, verbs, adjectives, content adverbs
(always, sometimes, often)
- Pronouns: i, you, we, he, she, they, me, my, your, his, her,
our, their
- Negation: not, no, never, nothing, none, nobody
- Interrogative WH-words: what, when, where, who, why, how, which
(in questions)
- Numbers: one, two, three, second, third, etc.
- Modal verbs that carry meaning: can (ability), might/maybe
(possibility), should/must (obligation)
- Spatial/directional words when they express location or
movement: inside, outside, up, down, around, forward, back,
off, over

Drop words that don’t change the core meaning:
- Articles: a, an, the
- Copulas: is, am, are, was, were (as linking verb)
- Auxiliary chains identified in Step 1
- Conjunctions: and, but, or, so, because, if, that, then
- Structural prepositions: of, to (infinitive marker), at, by,
for, with, from (when purely grammatical)
- Degree modifiers: very, really, pretty (as "fairly"), quite,
extremely, a lot -- these are expressed through sign intensity
in ASL, not as separate signs
- Discourse fillers: okay, well, actually, basically, just,
literally, essentially
- Determiners: this, that, these, those, any, some (when not
carrying meaning)
- Relative pronouns: which/that introducing a clause ("the
button, which opens..." -> drop "which")
- Structural "how": "learn how to play" -> drop "how" (vs
interrogative "How do you play?" -> keep "how")

If the same content repeats for emphasis in the original
sentence, keep it only once (de-duplicate).

## Step 3: Restore to citation form
Lemmatize each kept word to its base/dictionary form:
- Verb tense: gives->give, went->go, tied->tie, learning->learn,
spoken->speak
- Plural: steps->step, hands->hand, feet->foot, children->child
- Comparative/superlative: highest->high, bigger->big,
better->good
- Never produce a non-word fragment (e.g. other->oth is WRONG,
keep "other")

## Output format
Output ONLY the pseudo-gloss as a single line of space-separated
lowercase words. No explanation.

Code-level audit of Progressive Transformers

A prominent Phoenix SLP system, Progressive
Transformers
(
Saunders et al. 2020
)
, reports better BLEU
than our checkpoints. Reading the released code, we find three
inference/evaluation paths that leak ground-truth timing or length
into the reported protocol; the two most important are (L1) the
predicted counter scalar is overwritten by the GT counter at every
decoder step, and (L2) the inference output length is set to the GT
sequence length rather than learned via a stop signal.

632

/

computed on the full test split
(Table

Appendix D
Back-translation evaluator: training details and failure modes

Training setup.

Our back-translation (motion-to-text) evaluator on How2Sign is
trained pose-to-text with translation cross-entropy only, no
gloss-CTC loss, no pretrained CSLR features, no auxiliary
pretraining. Input is axis-angle SMPL-X; decoding at evaluation is
beam-
5
; the test split has
n=2{,}357
paired sentences. In
this regime BT-BLEU-4 plateaus at a ceiling of
\approx 2.5
even on
GT motion, and cross-regime comparisons against
gloss-CTC-supervised evaluators (e.g. Sign Language
Transformers
(
Camgoz et al. 2020
)
) are not directly meaningful.

Failure-mode commentary on the examples.

Section
5.3
(Table
2
) shows

3
representative GT/prediction pairs at our BLEU-4
=1.24

checkpoint, in the same regime as the BLEU-4
\approx 1.22

no-pretraining baseline of
Uthus et al. 2023
. The pattern is
consistent across the test split: (i) input-independent high-frequency
template language — instructional constructions (“you can do it”,
“you want to make sure”, “i’m going to take my right/left hand”)

light, very light, very light,

characteristic of an autoregressive decoder estimating a

signal from the encoded motion.
