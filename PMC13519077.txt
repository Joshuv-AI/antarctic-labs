Claudin 18.2 expression—concordance between primary and metastatic lesions in gastric and gastroesophageal adenocarcinomas: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: NB Dahlgaard, Y Tong, K Shitara, M Fassan, M Mau-Sørensen, K Egebjerg
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundClaudin 18 isoform 2 (CLDN18.2) is a clinically validated therapeutic target in gastric and gastroesophageal adenocarcinomas. Biomarker assessment in pivotal trials has largely relied on primary tumor (PT) tissue, despite growing evidence of spatial heterogeneity between PTs and metastatic lesions (MLs). This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated concordance of CLDN18.2 expression between paired PT and ML samples.Materials and methodsPubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus were searched for studies reporting paired CLDN18.2 immunohistochemistry (IHC) results in PTs and MLs. Random-effects meta-analyses of proportions estimated overall discordance, PT-positive to ML-negative (PT+→ML-) conversion, and PT-negative to ML-positive (PT-→ML+) conversion. Agreement metrics, heterogeneity, subgroup analyses, and sensitivity analyses were conducted.ResultsEleven studies comprising 896 paired PT-ML samples were analyzed. Overall, 20.2% of cases were discordant, with a pooled discordance proportion of 21% [95% confidence interval (CI) 18-24; I2 = 35%]. Concordance beyond chance was moderate (Cohen’s κ = 0.57). Significant asymmetry was observed, with PT+→ML- conversion occurring in 35.0% (95% CI 24.0-48.0) of PT-positive cases, compared with PT-→ML+ conversion in 14.2% (95% CI 9.8-20.3) of PT-negative cases (McNemar P = 0.0047). Discordance was numerically higher in distant metastases and with higher IHC cut-offs. Findings were consistent across sensitivity analyses.ConclusionsFindings indicate that CLDN18.2 expression demonstrates moderate spatial heterogeneity between PTs and MLs. Approximately one in five patients show discordance, most commonly reflecting loss of CLDN18.2 in MLs. These results suggest that single-site PT testing may not fully capture biomarker distribution across disease sites.Key words: biomarker heterogeneity, CLDN18.2, gastric cancer, gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma, meta-analysis, zolbetuximab

Background

Gastric adenocarcinomas and gastroesophageal adenocarcinomas (GEAs) remain among the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Despite therapeutic improvements, most patients present with advanced disease, and long-term survival remains poor.1 Recent molecular insights2, 3, 4, 5 and pivotal clinical trials6,7 have identified claudin 18 isoform 2 (CLDN18.2) as a bona fide therapeutic target.

CLDN18.2 is a tight junction protein normally confined to gastric mucosa involved in the maintenance of epithelial integrity. During malignant transformation, the loss of polarity exposes CLDN18.2 on the tumor surface, rendering the protein accessible for antibody-based therapy.8 A recent meta-analysis has further linked CLDN18.2 expression in tumors with poorer overall survival, suggesting an association with aggressive tumor biology.9

Zolbetuximab—a monoclonal antibody directed against CLDN18.2—mediates antibody- and complement-dependent cytotoxicity.10 The phase III randomized trials, SPOTLIGHT and GLOW, demonstrated that addition of zolbetuximab to chemotherapy significantly improved progression-free and overall survival in patients with HER2-negative, CLDN18.2-positive GEA.6,7 These studies established CLDN18.2 as the first clinically validated molecular target in GEA since HER2.

Eligibility in these trials was based on immunohistochemical (IHC) assessment of CLDN18.2 in tumor samples, defining CLDN18.2 positivity as membranous staining of ≥75% of tumor cells with ≥2+ intensity using the VENTANA CLDN18 (43-14A) assay.6,7 By far the most common site of biopsy for trials is primary tumor (PT). However, the lack of uniform biomarker expression across all tumor sites represents a major challenge for optimal patient selection as shown by growing evidence of intratumoral and interlesional heterogeneity.2,11 Additionally, temporal or treatment-induced changes may also play a role. These observations raise questions about ideal timing and site for biopsy-based biomarker assessment. Such variations are well recognized for multiple biomarkers, including HER2 and programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), and may equally affect CLDN18.2.12, 13, 14, 15 Furthermore, methodological variability, including differences in IHC antibody clones, scoring criteria, and positivity cut-offs, contributes to uncertainty in reported CLDN18.2 prevalence and concordance rates between studies.16

Given the growing clinical relevance of CLDN18.2-targeted therapy, a precise understanding of its expression patterns across disease sites is crucial. Recent studies have reported discordant CLDN18.2 expression between PTs and metastatic lesions (MLs), with both loss and gain of expression described.17, 18, 19

This meta-analysis was conducted to systematically evaluate the consistency of CLDN18.2 expression between PT and MLs in gastric and GEAs. Specifically, the aims were to (i) quantify the degree and direction of discordance between PT and ML, (ii) estimate the pooled proportions of conversion from CLDN18.2 positivity in PTs (PT+) to negativity in MLs (ML−) and from negativity in PTs (PT−) to positivity in ML (ML+), and (iii) explore potential variation by metastatic site or methodological factors. By clarifying the extent of biomarker heterogeneity, this study seeks to inform biopsy strategies, improve diagnostic accuracy, and optimize patient selection for CLDN18.2-targeted therapies in clinical practice.

Materials and methods

This systematic review and meta-analysis followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. The protocol was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO; registration number 1069000). The primary objective was to evaluate the concordance of CLDN18.2 expression between PTs and MLs in GEAs.

Literature search and study selection

A comprehensive search was conducted in PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus with no language or date restrictions (Supplementary Methods S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369). Reference lists of eligible articles and reviews were screened manually. Two reviewers (N.B.D and Y.T.) independently screened all titles, abstracts, and full texts. Discrepancies were resolved by consensus with a third reviewer (K.E.). Inclusion and exclusion criteria are shown in Supplementary Methods S2, (available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369). No restriction on CLDN18.2 positivity cut-off was applied, and studies were included regardless of the threshold used. As this study was based exclusively on previously published data, ethical approval and informed consent were not required.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Data were extracted on study characteristics, patient demographics, tumor histology, IHC methodology, site of PT/ML, and paired CLDN18.2 expression for each site. Any discrepancies in data extraction were resolved by discussion with a third reviewer.

Quality was assessed using the QUADAS-2 tool (Supplementary Methods S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369), across four domains (patient selection, index test, reference standard, and flow/timing).20 Studies rated as high or unclear risk in >2 domains were considered high risk of bias and retained for sensitivity analyses.

Statistical analysis

Concordance estimation

Paired PT-ML CLDN18.2 expression data were summarized in 2 × 2 tables (Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369) for each study. Agreement between PT and ML was quantified using the metrics shown in Supplementary Table S1, (available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369), all reported with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

Additionally, Cohen’s κ was calculated to assess chance-corrected agreement between paired samples. McNemar’s chi-square test was used to evaluate the symmetry of discordant paired observations, comparing the frequency of PT+→ML− versus PT−→ML+ conversion of CLDN18.2 expression.

Discordance and conversion of expression

The pooled proportion of discordance, and the proportion of PT+→ML− and PT−→ML+ were calculated using random-effects meta-analyses of proportions with logit transformation and inverse-variance weighting. Heterogeneity was assessed using Cochran’s Q and I2 statistics.

Correlation analysis

Study-level correlation between PT CLDN18.2 positivity and discordance rate was evaluated using Pearson’s correlation coefficient (r) with 95% CIs.

Subgroup and sensitivity analyses

Predefined subgroup analyses examined metastatic site: regional lymph nodes (LNs) versus other/distant metastases, geographic region, and IHC scoring cut-off. Publication bias was assessed using funnel plot inspection and Egger’s test. Leave-one-out (LOO) sensitivity analyses were carried out for the main outcomes (overall discordance and PT+→ML− conversion). Sensitivity analyses were carried out by excluding low-quality studies or those with extreme effect sizes. All results were expressed with 95% CIs, and forest plots were generated to visually summarize individual and pooled estimates. All analyses were conducted in RStudio (v2025.05.0) with P < 0.05 considered significant.

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. As this study was based exclusively on previously published data, ethical approval and informed consent were not required.

Results

A total of 1070 records were identified through PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus. After duplicate removal, 696 studies were screened, and 26 underwent full-text review. Concordance of CLDN18.2 expression between paired PTs and MLs

Across all 896 paired PT and ML samples, 244 (27.2%) were concordantly positive, 471 (52.6%) concordantly negative, and 181 (20.2%) discordant (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369). Similarly, by random-effects model the pooled proportion of discordant CLDN18.2 expression between PTs and MLs was assessed to be 21% (95% CI 18-24). Heterogeneity was moderate with Cochran’s Q = 15.39 (df = 10, P = 0.12), I2 = 35.0% (95% CI 0.0-68.1), and τ2 = 0.040 (Figure 1).

Positive percent agreement was 0.69 (95% CI 0.64-0.74), negative percent agreement was 0.87 (95% CI 0.84-0.90), overall percent agreement was 0.80 (95% CI 0.77-0.82), and overall percent discordance was 0.20 (95% CI 0.18-0.23). Cohen’s κ was 0.57 (95% CI 0.52-0.62). McNemar’s test indicated significant asymmetry between discordant pairs (χ2(1) = 7.98, P = 0.0047; Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369).

Directional analyses demonstrated a clear asymmetry in CLDN18.2 expression changes between paired PT and MLs (Supplementary Table S3A and SB, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369). Subgroup analyses

Overall PT-ML discordance

Subgroup analyses of overall PT-ML discordance showed numerically higher discordance in distant MLs compared with regional LNs, but no statistically significant differences were observed between metastatic sites (Q = 2.40, P = 0.12). Discordance rates were comparable between geographic regions (Supplementary Figure S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369), with overlapping CIs and no evidence of regional differences (Q = 0.40, P = 0.53). Studies applying a higher CLDN18.2 positivity threshold (≥75%) demonstrated numerically higher discordance than those using a ≥40% cut-off; this difference approached but did not reach statistical significance (Q = 3.62, P = 0.057). Discordance rates were similar across sampling sources (resection, biopsy, or mixed), with no significant subgroup differences (Q = 2.34, P = 0.31). Heterogeneity within subgroups was generally low to moderate (Table 3, Supplementary Figures S2-S5, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369).

PT+→ML– conversion of CLDN18.2 expression

PT+→ML- conversion was numerically more common in distant MLs than in regional LNs, but this difference was not statistically significant (Q = 1.24, P = 0.27). No significant differences were observed across geographic regions (Q = 0.18, P = 0.67), IHC positivity cut-offs (Q = 4.16, P = 0.04), or sampling sources (Q = 0.24, P = 0.89). Subgroup analyses are presented in Supplementary Figures S6-S9, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369). Substantial heterogeneity persisted within several subgroups, suggesting underlying biological variability rather than subgroup-specific effects (Table 3).

Correlation analyses

Pearson correlation analysis showed no significant correlation between the prevalence of CLDN18.2 positivity in PTs and the discordance between PTs and MLs (r = −0.04, 95% CI −0.63 to 0.57, P = 0.90; Supplementary Figure S10, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369).

Similarly, Pearson correlation analysis showed no significant association between the prevalence of PT+ and the rate of ML– among patients with PT+. Across studies, the correlation coefficient was r = –0.36 (95% CI –0.79 to 0.31; P = 0.28; Supplementary Figure S11, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369).

Publication bias and sensitivity analyses

A funnel plot and Egger’s test showed no significant publication bias (P = 0.72; Supplementary Figure S12, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369).

LOO analyses demonstrated robust findings: pooled estimates after omitting one study at a time varied between 18% and 28%, with overlapping CIs, and the pooled probability of CLDN18.2 conversion from PT+→ML– ranged from 24% to 58% versus 20.7%, while heterogeneity slightly decreased (Supplementary Figure S13, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369).

Excluding small studies23,26 did not substantially affect the pooled proportion [0.207 (95% CI 0.18-0.24) to 0.20 (95% CI 0.17-0.24)]. The heterogeneity measures also changed only marginally (I2 from 35% to 39% and τ2 from 0.040 to 0.053).

A QUADAS-2 evaluation is available in Supplementary Table S1, (available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369). Excluding studies with high risk of bias,22,26 the pooled proportion of discordant CLDN18.2 expression remained stable [21.9% (95% CI 18.8-25.4) to 20.7% (95% CI 17.5-24.4)] in the full analysis. The heterogeneity statistic decreased (I2 = 35% to 24%; Supplementary Table S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100369).

Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis provide a comprehensive synthesis of CLDN18.2 concordance between paired PTs and MLs in gastric adenocarcinomas and GEAs. The principal finding is that CLDN18.2 expression shows moderate but clinically meaningful intertumoral spatial heterogeneity: approximately one in five patients demonstrated discordance between PT and ML, with a directional predominance of PT+→ML- conversion.

Agreement beyond chance was moderate (Cohen’s κ = 0.57) with a predominance of PT+→ML- conversion. This supports a non-random pattern of change, suggesting that technical misclassification alone is unlikely to explain the findings and that biological downregulation during metastatic progression may contribute. Clinically, CLDN18.2 positivity in PT would typically qualify patients for treatment, even in the presence of discordant MLs. Therefore, PT+→ML- discordance is unlikely to result in missed treatment opportunities but could potentially instead lead to varied treatment response across disease sites.

Notably, conversion from PT–→ML+ was observed in a clinically relevant minority of patients. Approximately 14% of PT– cases demonstrated positive conversion in MLs, indicating that CLDN18.2 expression may emerge during disease progression. However, as the present analysis is based primarily on spatial comparisons rather than longitudinal sampling, this observation should be interpreted with caution and may also reflect intratumoral heterogeneity or variability around the positivity threshold.

Subgroup analyses showed numerically higher discordance and PT+→ML- conversion rates in distant MLs compared with regional LNs, consistent with increasing biological divergence during progression. However, no subgroup fully explained the observed heterogeneity. Studies using lower positivity thresholds may be more prone to classification variability in tumors with borderline or heterogeneous expression. However, such discordance may be less clinically relevant, as treatment benefit has primarily been demonstrated in patients with ≥75% CLDN18.2 expression. Overall, the findings likely reflect true biological heterogeneity between tumor sites rather than technical variation alone, since paired samples within individual studies were assessed using identical IHC protocols.

Beyond the LN versus distant metastasis distinction used for quantitative synthesis, several individual studies provided descriptive data on specific metastatic organs, most commonly peritoneal and hepatic lesions. Notably, studies focusing on peritoneal metastases frequently reported substantial PT+→ML- conversion, consistent with advanced biological divergence in this metastatic subgroup.18,20,27 When reported separately, liver metastases showed variable patterns of CLDN18.2 concordance and discordance across studies,14,19,23,24,26 whereas data on less frequent metastatic sites such as ovary or lung were sparse and inconsistently reported, typically grouped with other distant sites and unsuitable for pooled organ-specific analysis. These limitations precluded reliable organ-level meta-analysis but nonetheless suggest that CLDN18.2 expression may vary across metastatic organs, warranting further investigation in prospectively designed studies with predefined site-specific sampling.

The influence of oncologic therapy on CLDN18.2 stability could not be directly assessed in the quantitative meta-analysis, as pooled estimates were based on baseline paired samples. Nevertheless, several of the included studies reported separate within-study analyses comparing CLDN18.2 expression before and after systemic therapy, with some suggesting preserved expression14,24,25 and others describing PT+→ML– conversion in post-treatment or metachronous lesions.10,18,28 Although these findings were not incorporated into the pooled analyses, they indicate that therapeutic exposure may modify CLDN18.2 expression in a subset of patients and underscore the need for prospective longitudinal sampling to clarify temporal dynamics.

The spatial heterogeneity observed for CLDN18.2 parallels reported patterns for other predictive biomarkers in gastric cancer, most notably HER2 and PD-L1, for which discordance between PTs and MLs has been consistently documented. A meta-analysis15 of paired gastric cancer samples demonstrated HER2 discordance rates of ∼7%-20%, with bidirectional conversion across disease sites, highlighting the limitations of single-site testing. Even greater variability has been described for PD-L1, with one meta-analysis reporting discordance ranging from 16% to 41%.27 However, CLDN18.2 differs in biological role: HER2 functions as an oncogenic driver whose amplification status evolves between sites, and PD-L1 reflects immune–tumor interactions and adaptive immune escape,13,14,21,29,30 whereas CLDN18.2 is a tight junction protein whose therapeutic accessibility arises from loss of epithelial polarity rather than pathway activation.4 These differences may contribute to the observation that CLDN18.2 discordance appears predominantly directional and spatial, rather than growth- or immune-modulated, reinforcing the need to interpret CLDN18.2 expression as a site-dependent tumor characteristic rather than a fixed molecular trait.1,15,21

The clinical implications are particularly relevant in the context of the two pivotal phase III trials, SPOTLIGHT and GLOW, which demonstrated survival benefit of zolbetuximab in CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative advanced gastric adenocarcinomas or GEAs but relied exclusively on PT tissue for biomarker assessment. In these trials, CLDN18.2 positivity was identified in 38.4% of screened patients. Among CLDN18.2-positive cases, the vast majority were identified based on PT tissue (1498/1730, 86.6%), while only a minority were identified from ML samples (201/1730, 11.6%). Although biopsy site-specific enrollment data were not reported, these figures indicate that patient selection relied predominantly on PT assessment. Based on the present pooled estimates, approximately one-third of the patients that enrolled because of PT+ would be expected to harbor ML– (∼360 out of ∼1100 patients), potentially contributing to heterogenous treatment response across disease sites in a subset of patients. Conversely, a smaller group of patients with PT– may demonstrate CLDN18.2 expression in MLs. This highlights a potential limitation of single-site testing, even though therapeutic benefit may still occur in CLDN18 discordant patients.6,7 Overall, our findings identify spatial heterogeneity of CLDN18.2 as an important consideration in biomarker interpretation and patient selection for CLDN18-targeted therapy. While the direct clinical implications remain to be established, these findings may help contextualize heterogeneous treatment response across disease sites and should be considered in future studies evaluating CLDN18-targeted therapies.

The strengths of this study include its systematic methodology, paired-sample design, and quantitative synthesis of global evidence. Limitations include the modest number of eligible studies, predominantly retrospective designs, and heterogeneity in IHC protocols and cut-offs, and a limited reporting of treatment exposure and sampling intervals. Despite these constraints, sensitivity analyses confirmed the robustness of the main findings.

In conclusion, our findings indicate that CLDN18.2 expression demonstrates moderate but clinically meaningful spatial heterogeneity between paired PT and MLs gastric and GEAs. Discordance affects a substantial proportion of patients (21%), with PT+→ML– conversion being considerably more common than PT–→ML+, although the latter occurs in a clinically relevant minority. These findings highlight variability in CLDN18.2 expression between tumor sites and suggest that reliance on single-site testing may not fully capture biomarker distribution throughout disease sites. The clinical utility of metastatic or longitudinal CLDN18.2 assessment remains to be established in prospective studies. Future prospective studies with standardized testing and predefined sampling timepoints are warranted to further interpret biomarker dynamics under treatment.
