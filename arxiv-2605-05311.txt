Canonical quantization of all minisuperspaces with consistent symmetry reductions
Poula Tadros, Ivan Kolář, Otakar Svítek

Abstract
We present the quantization of all symmetry reductions of the Einstein–Hilbert Lagrangian that correctly reproduce the reduced Einstein’s field equations — i.e., characterized by the infinitesimal group actions obeying the principle of symmetric criticality. These correspond to the spacetime symmetries of spherical/hyperbolic/planar Schwarzschild/Taub–NUT, BI/BII/BIII-metrics, near-horizon extreme Kerr geometry, swirling universe, closed/open/flat FLRW cosmologies, other FLRW-type metrics, and Bianchi type I, II, VIII, and IX spacetimes. We derive the Hamiltonian and the conformal symmetries of the superspace metrics (the conditional symmetries), promote them to operators, and solve the Wheeler–DeWitt equation with and without imposing these symmetries.

The very first step in the quantization procedure is to derive the reduced Lagrangian by substituting the spacetime metric ansatz into the theory’s Lagrangian and contracting with the appropriate
l
-chain (to reduce the volume element on the manifold to the volume element on the reduced space). For minisuperspaces and for the Einstein–Hilbert Lagrangian, the reduced Lagrangian can always be written in the form
{L=\tfrac{1}{2}G_{\alpha\beta}q^{\prime\alpha}q^{\prime\beta}+V(q)}
where
G_{\alpha\beta}
is the metric tensor of the minisuperspace called the
supermetric
,
V(q)
is the
superpotential
, and
q
denotes the coordinates of the minisuperspace. From the reduced Lagrangian, the canonical momenta and Hamiltonian are calculated in the usual manner, we then promote the minisuperspace coordinates and canonical momenta to Hermitian operators and require that the Hamiltonian operator annihilates the physical states in the Hilbert space. The question is whether the symmetry-reduced theory yields the same field equations as the full theory when the spacetime metric is substituted in its field equations — i.e., the variation commutes with the symmetry reduction. If such
equivalence
holds for any theory, the symmetry is said to obey the
principle of symmetric criticality (PSC)

Fels and Torre 2002
;
Anderson and Fels 1997
;
Anderson et al. 2000
;
Anderson et al. 1999
(see also
Palais 1979
;
Torre 2011
;
Frausto et al. 2025
). This is a major limitation of minisuperspace quantization. It can lead to wrong field equations and a non-unique symmetry reduction (when PSC1 is violated) or to an insufficient number of field equations (when PSC2 is violated). The latter means extra spurious solutions, which may imply that, upon quantization, the Hilbert space is larger than the physical Hilbert space. In principle, the field equations of the reduced theory could coincide with the reduced field equations of the full theory only for specific theories (even if PSC is violated), or for metric ansatzes that go beyond those fully captured by symmetries. However, tThe first possibility cannot be analyzed systematically, since no restricted versions of PSC1 and PSC2 are known for subclasses of theories. Nevertheless, the equivalence can always be checked directly, and in GR it does not occur for PSC-violating minisuperspace reductions. The second possibility does not admit a rigorously defined Lagrangian reduction in the first place. For example, ad hoc ansatz reductions do not come with a canonical object playing the role of the
l
-chain. Consequently, in GR, PSC-compatible minisuperspace models seem to be the only ones admitting a consistent and rigorous symmetry reduction of the Lagrangian, and hence a well-defined canonical quantization.

Already at the classical level, the minisuperspace may possess symmetries, called the
conditional symmetries (CSs)
, which correspond to conformal Killing vectors of the supermetric that scale the superpotential consistently
Christodoulakis et al. 2002
;
Paliathanasis 2024
;
Dimakis et al. 2016a
;
Christodoulakis and Papadopoulos 2001
;
Christodoulakis et al. 2018
. They arise in constrained systems as symmetries that leave the solutions of the full system invariant only when the primary constraints are applied; in other words, they do not act as symmetries of the system without the primary constraints, which is why they are called ‘conditional’ symmetries. Like spacetime symmetries, CSs lead to conserved quantities through the first Noether theorem. In minisuperspace quantization, CSs are used to define
constants of motion (COMs)
commuting with the Hamiltonian operator, and the wave function must be an eigenfunction of them (once promoted to operators). This property is used to simplify the WDW equation. As we shall see, imposing all eigenvalue equations from the COMs often leads to a trivial wave function; thus, only subalgebras of the full algebra of COMs are applied, leaving room for non-trivial wave functions
Christodoulakis et al. 2013a
.

A problem associated with this type of quantization is the
nonuniqueness of the measure
in the minisuperspace. There have been attempts to select relevant measures in
Kuchař 2011
;
Halliwell 1988
, but the problem remains open. In this paper, we fix the measure and the probability distribution whenever possible by imposing Hermiticity of the relevant CS operators, i.e., when the number of CS operators required to be Hermitian is greater than or equal to the dimension of the minisuperspace
Christodoulakis et al. 2013a
. Other conditions may also be used to fix the probability distribution:

•

Rescale the supermetric and superpotential so that the superpotential is constant. In this gauge, the CS generators become Killing vectors of the supermetric, turning CSs into Noether symmetries.

•

Demand a normalizable probability distribution and rescale the supermetric and superpotential accordingly.

•

Require that only operators corresponding to a selected subalgebra of CSs are Hermitian.

•

Demand that all operators corresponding to the COMs be Hermitian, even if the wave function is not assumed to be their eigenfunction (this requires more CS generators than the dimension of the superspace).

However, at present there is no concrete way to fix the measure on minisuperspaces, and it remains an open problem.

In this paper, we carry out canonical quantization of all PSC-compatible minisuperspace models, i.e., those admitting a consistent and rigorous symmetry reduction. We write the reduced Lagrangians, derive the algebras of CSs (and their subalgebras) and the corresponding COMs, and solve the WDW equation both for general wave functions and with imposed subalgebras of the COM algebra

II
Symmetry reduction of Lagrangians

PSC guarantees the equivalence of the field equations calculated by variation of the reduced theory with the reduced field equations (obtained from the full theory) simultaneously for all Lagrangians. As shown in
Fels and Torre 2002
(see also
Palais 1979
;
Anderson and Fels 1997
;
Anderson et al. 2000
;
Anderson et al. 1999
for previous works), there exist two conditions, PSC1 and PSC2, on the spacetimes symmetries (given by the Lie algebras of Killing vector fields) that are satisfied if and only if PSC holds. All possible symmetries when PSC holds were identified in
Frausto et al. 2025
by direct checks of PSC1 and PSC2 with respect to the modern mathematical classification of infinitesimal group actions.

Let
M
be a
4
-dimensional manifold and
\Gamma
a
d
-dimensional Lie algebra of vector fields on

generating an infinitesimal action of some Lie group. To distinguish in-equivalent actions (e.g., with the same abstract algebra) one needs to analyze their isotropy subalgebras
\Gamma_{\mathrm{x}}
, which are realized by those vector fields that leave the point
{\mathrm{x}\in M}
unchanged,
{\Gamma_{\mathrm{x}}=\{\,X\in\Gamma\mid X|_{\mathrm{x}}=0\,\}}
. Note that the quotient
\Gamma/\Gamma_{\mathrm{x}}
is the tangent space to the orbit of
\mathrm{x}
. If
{\pounds_{X}g=0}
,
{X\in\Gamma}
, then
X
and
{g}
are called the
Killing vectors
and the
symmetry-invariant metric
. The isotropy subalgebras for the Lorentzian metric correspond to the subalgebra of the Lorentz algebra. Whenever the spacetime locally splits into an isotropy-preserving slice and a group orbit, it can be classified by the abstract
Lorentzian pairs
of algebra-subalgebra [corresponding to
(\Gamma,\Gamma_{\mathrm{x}})

;

[-,3,-]

In contrast to the reduction of the field equations

Note that the symmetry-invariant metrics

III.1.3
Conditional symmetries

CSs are classical symmetries of constrained systems such that they leave symmetry-invariant the solutions of the full system only if the primary constraints are applied
Christodoulakis et al. 2013a
. The generators of the CSs are the conformal Killing vectors
\xi^{\alpha}=(\xi^{1}(q),\xi^{4}(q))
of the supermetric
3
3

3

The CS generators of the full degenerate minisuperspace (the Killing vectors of the supermetric when rescaling
V
to be constant) are those of the reduced minisuperspace plus additional generators:
\xi_{q_{2}}=f_{1}(q)\partial_{q_{2}},\xi_{q_{3}}=f_{2}(q)\partial_{q_{3}}
where
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are arbitrary functions, i.e., one extra family of generators for every degenerate direction.
that scale the superpotential consistently,

\displaystyle\pounds_{\xi}G^{\alpha\beta}=\Phi G^{\alpha\beta},\quad\pounds_{\xi}V=\Phi V,

(17)

where
\Phi=\Phi(q)
. For the symmetry-invariant metrics having the symmetries of s./h. Schwarzschild (
[4,3,\{3,1\}]
), the solutions are

\xi_{1}=\left(-q_{1},q_{4}\right),\quad\xi_{2}=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{q_{4}}},0\right),\quad\xi_{3}=\left(-\frac{q_{1}}{2\sqrt{q_{4}}},\sqrt{q_{4}}\right).

(18)

The COMs on the superspace
Q_{i}=\xi^{\alpha}_{i}p_{\alpha}
read

Q_{1}=-q_{1}p_{1}+q_{4}p_{4},\quad Q_{2}=\frac{p_{1}}{\sqrt{q_{4}}},\quad Q_{3}=-\frac{q_{1}p_{1}}{2\sqrt{q_{4}}}+\sqrt{q_{4}}\,p_{4},

(19)

satisfying the Poisson brackets

\displaystyle\{Q_{1},H\}=0,\quad\{Q_{2},H\}=0,\quad\{Q_{3},H\}=0.

(20)

The Poisson brackets of the COMs are given by

\{Q_{1},Q_{3}\}=Q_{3},\quad\{Q_{2},Q_{1}\}=Q_{2},\quad\{Q_{2},Q_{3}\}=0.

(21)

Thus, the generators form a closed algebra of Bianchi type VI with

III.1.4
Quantization

Here, we quantize the system on the

Here,

p_{1}

where

Q_{i}

\hat{Q}_{i}=-\frac{i}{2\mu}\left(\mu\xi_{i}^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}+\partial_{\alpha}(\xi_{i}^{\alpha}\mu)\right).

The eigenvalue equations arising from the COMs are

\Psi_{z}=q_{1}^{-iz/2}\left(AJ_{iz}\left(4\sqrt{kq_{1}q_{4}}\right)+BY_{iz}\left(4\sqrt{kq_{1}q_{4}}\right)\right),

The above wave function is derived only by solving (

\hat{Q}_{i}\ket{\Psi}=\kappa_{i}\ket{\Psi}

Substituting with the forms for the COMs, we get a system of partial differential equations consisting of the WDW equation and three eigenvalue equations:

\begin{gathered}q_{1}^{2}\partial_{q_{1}}^{2}\Psi-2q_{1}q_{4}\partial_{q_{1}}\partial_{q_{4}}\Psi+q_{1}\partial_{q_{1}}\Psi-4kq_{1}q_{4}\Psi=0,\\
-i\left(-q_{1}\partial_{q_{1}}\Psi+q_{4}\partial_{q_{4}}\Psi\right)=\kappa_{1}\Psi,\quad-i\frac{1}{\sqrt{q_{4}}}\partial_{q_{1}}\Psi=\kappa_{2}\Psi,\quad-i\left(\sqrt{q_{4}}\partial_{q_{4}}\Psi-\frac{q_{1}}{2\sqrt{q_{4}}}\partial_{q_{1}}\Psi\right)=\kappa_{3}\Psi,\end{gathered}

5

Imposing the conditions of the

III.2

III.2.1

The Killing vectors defining the symmetries of p. Schwarzschild are

Conditional symmetries

Writing the generators of the CSs as

\displaystyle\partial_{q_{1}}\xi^{4}=0,\quad\partial_{q_{1}}\xi^{1}+\partial_{q_{4}}\xi^{4}=\Phi,\quad q_{4}\xi^{1}-q_{1}\xi^{4}+2q_{4}^{2}\partial_{q_{4}}\xi^{1}+2q_{1}q_{4}\partial_{q_{4}}\xi^{4}=q_{1}q_{4}\Phi.

III.2.4
Quantization

The system is quantized by promoting the coordinates and canonical momenta to operators as before. The WDW equation in this case is

\partial_{\alpha}(\mu G^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta})\Psi=0.

(46)

Substituting the supermetric, we get

(48)

for any functions

\Psi_{2}

for any functions
\xi^{1}
and
\xi^{4}
of the forms (
41
) and (

and
\xi^{4}

III.3

[4,3,\{4,2\}]

III.3.1

The Killing vectors representing the symmetries of s./h. Taub–NUT are given by

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-k\rho}\left(\cos\varphi\partial_{\rho}-\tfrac{\sin\varphi}{\rho}\partial_{\varphi}\right)-2n\tfrac{1-\sqrt{1-k\rho^{2}}}{k}\tfrac{\sin\varphi}{\rho}\partial_{t},

\displaystyle X_{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-k\rho}\left(\sin\varphi\partial_{\rho}-\tfrac{\cos\varphi}{\rho}\partial_{\varphi}\right)+2n\tfrac{1-\sqrt{1-k\rho^{2}}}{k}\tfrac{\cos\varphi}{\rho}\partial_{t},\quad X_{3}=\partial_{\varphi},\quad X_{4}=\partial_{t},

To find the reduced GR Lagrangian, we calculate the Ricci scalar for the metric ansatz (

\displaystyle R=

III.3.3
Conditional symmetries

Now, we derive the algebra of CSs. Their generators are given by the vector field

\displaystyle\pounds_{\xi}G^{\alpha\beta}=\Phi G^{\alpha\beta},\quad\pounds_{\xi}V=\Phi V.

(61)

Substituting into the first equation, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{q_{1}}\xi^{4}=0,\quad\partial_{q_{1}}\xi^{1}+\partial_{q_{4}}\xi^{4}=-\tfrac{n^{2}}{q_{4}}\tfrac{q_{4}\xi^{1}-q_{1}\xi^{4}}{kq_{4}+n^{2}q_{1}},\quad q_{4}\xi^{1}-q_{1}\xi^{4}+2q_{4}^{2}\partial_{q_{4}}\xi^{1}-2q_{1}q_{4}\partial_{q_{4}}\xi^{1}=n^{2}q_{1}\tfrac{q_{4}\xi^{1}-q_{1}\xi^{4}}{kq_{4}+n^{2}q_{1}}.

(62)

From the first equation, we can write
\xi^{4}=\xi^{4}(q_{4})
. The second and third equations give

q_{4}\xi^{1}-q_{1}\xi^{4}+2q_{4}^{2}\partial_{q_{4}}\xi^{1}-q_{1}q_{4}\partial_{q_{1}}\xi^{1}+q_{1}q_{4}\xi^{4}{}^{\prime}=0.

(63)

Dividing by
q_{1}
and differentiating with respect to
q_{1}
, we get an equation for
\xi^{1}
,

q_{1}\partial_{q_{1}}\xi^{1}-\xi^{1}+2q_{1}q_{4}\partial_{q_{1}}\partial_{q_{4}}\xi^{1}-2q_{4}\partial_{q_{4}}\xi^{1}-q_{1}^{2}\partial_{q_{1}}^{2}\xi^{1}=0.

(64)

The general solution of this equation is

\xi^{1}=q_{1}F(q_{4})+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{q_{4}}}G\left(q_{1}\sqrt{q_{4}}\right),

(65)

where
F
and
G
are arbitrary functions. We first deduce the form of
G
. To do that we begin with the second equation in (
62
) and differentiate it twice with respect to
q_{1}
; the resulting equation reads

(kq_{4}+n^{2}q_{1})\sqrt{q_{4}}G^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(q_{1}\sqrt{q_{4}}\right)=-3n^{2}G^{\prime\prime}\left(q_{1}\sqrt{q_{4}}\right).

(66)

The general solution for
G
is given by

G\left(q_{1}\sqrt{q_{4}}\right)=C_{1}+C_{2}q_{1}\sqrt{q_{4}},

(67)

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are arbitrary constants. Now, substituting this solution into the first derivative of (
63
) with respect to
q_{1}
, we obtain

q_{4}\partial_{q_{4}}\xi^{4}=-\xi^{4}.

(68)

The general solution is

\xi^{4}=\tfrac{A}{q_{4}},

(69)

where
A
is an arbitrary constant. The function
\xi^{1}
can be written as

\xi^{1}=q_{1}F(q_{4})+\tfrac{C_{1}}{q_{1}}+C_{2}\sqrt{q_{4}}.

(70)

Using this form in the second equation of (
62
), we get an algebraic equation in
F
:

\left(\tfrac{kq_{4}}{n^{2}}+q_{1}\right)\left(q_{4}^{2}F(q_{4})-C_{1}-A\right)=-q_{1}q_{4}^{2}F(q_{4})-\tfrac{C_{1}q_{4}^{2}}{q_{1}}-C_{2}q_{4}^{2}\sqrt{q_{4}}+Aq_{1}.

(71)

Since
F
is only a function of
q_{4}
, all orders of
q_{1}
must vanish. This gives a system of algebraic equations:

C_{1}=0,\quad q_{4}^{2}F(q_{4})=A,\quad\tfrac{4kA}{n^{2}}=-C_{2}q_{4}\sqrt{q_{4}}.

(72)

The third equation implies that
C_{2}=A=0
, and the second equation necessitates that
F(q_{4})=0
. Thus, the only solution for the system (
62
) is

\xi^{1}=\xi^{4}=0,

(73)

meaning that there are no CSs.

III.3.4
Quantization

To quantize the system, we promote the generalized coordinates and momenta to operators as in the previous sections.
Since there are no CSs, the wave function is determined only by the solution of the WDW equation. Substituting our supermetric and superpotential into (
25
), after some algebra, we end up with the WDW equation of the form

q_{1}\partial_{q_{1}}\Psi+q_{1}^{2}\partial_{q_{1}}^{2}\Psi-2q_{1}q_{4}\partial_{q_{1}}\partial_{q_{4}}-4kq_{1}q_{4}-4n^{2}q_{1}^{2}=0,

(74)

whose general solution is

\Psi_{z}(q)=\left(AJ_{z}\left(4\sqrt{kq_{1}q_{4}}\right)+BY_{z}\left(4\sqrt{kq_{1}q_{4}}\right)\right)\left(CI_{\frac{z}{2}}\left(2q_{1}n\right)+DK_{\frac{z}{2}}\left(2q_{1}n\right)\right),

(75)

where
A,B,C
and
D
are complex constants.
Since there are no CSs, the measure on the minisuperspace cannot be uniquely determined; hence, neither can the probability distribution. The main difference between this wave function and that of s./h. Schwarzschild is that the absolute value of the wave function for Taub–NUT is unbounded at infinity (
q_{1},q_{4}\to\infty
), indicating that, as a quantum system, it is not confined.

III.4
Planar Taub–NUT —
[4,3,5]

III.4.1
Symmetry-invariant metric and classical solutions

The Killing vectors of the p. Taub–NUT are

X_{1}=\cos\varphi\partial_{\rho}-\tfrac{\sin\varphi}{\rho}\partial_{\varphi}-\tfrac{2n\rho\sin\varphi}{2}\partial_{t},\quad X_{2}=\sin\varphi\partial_{\rho}+\tfrac{\cos\varphi}{\rho}\partial_{\varphi}+\tfrac{2n\rho\cos\varphi}{2}\partial_{t},\quad X_{3}=\partial_{\varphi},\quad X_{4}=\partial_{t}.

(76)

The general symmetry-invariant metric reads

\check{g}=-q_{1}(r)\left(dt+n\rho^{2}d\varphi\right)^{2}+2q_{2}(r)\left(dt+n\rho^{2}d\varphi\right)dr+q_{3}(r)dr^{2}+q_{4}(r)\left(d\rho^{2}+\rho^{2}d\varphi^{2}\right).

(77)

For this class of metrics, there is a classical solution for GR corresponding to
q_{1}(r)=\tfrac{1}{q_{3}(r)}=\tfrac{-2mr}{r^{2}+n^{2}}
,
q_{2}(r)=0
and
q_{4}(r)=r^{2}+n^{2}
.

III.4.2
Reduced Lagrangian and generalized momenta

The formula for the Ricci scalar of the symmetry-invariant metric is given by (
53
) but with
k=0
. We again emphasize that all quantities should be derived from the symmetry-invariant metric (
77
), as not all formulas from the previous section can be directly extended to
{k=0}
.
Nevertheless, the reduced Lagrangian coincides with (
54
) where
{k=0}
.
Also, the reduced Lagrangian can be written in the form (
55
), where the supermetric and the superpotential are (
56
) except that here
k=0
.
Consequently, the canonical momenta and Hamiltonian have the forms of (
57
) and (
59
), respectively, but with
k=0
.

III.4.3
Conditional symmetries

Let us calculate the CSs generated by the vector fields
\xi^{\alpha}=(\xi^{1}(q),\xi^{4}(q))
as solutions of

\displaystyle\pounds_{\xi}G^{\alpha\beta}=\Phi G^{\alpha\beta},\quad\pounds_{\xi}V=\Phi V,

(78)

where
\Phi=\Phi(q)
.
The equations for
\xi^{1}
and
\xi^{4}
are given by

\displaystyle\partial_{q_{1}}\xi^{4}=0,\quad\partial_{q_{1}}\xi^{1}+\partial_{q_{4}}\xi^{4}=-\tfrac{q_{4}\xi^{1}-q_{1}\xi^{4}}{q_{1}q_{4}},\quad q_{4}\partial_{q_{4}}\xi^{1}-q_{1}\partial_{q_{1}}\xi^{1}=0.

(79)

The general solution for these equations is
\xi^{1}(q)=Aq_{1}q_{4}+\tfrac{B}{q_{1}q_{4}}
and
\xi^{4}(q_{4})=-2Bq_{4}^{2}+Cq_{4}
, where
A,B,C
are arbitrary constants. The generators of CSs are then given by

\xi^{\alpha}_{1}=(0,q_{4}),\quad\xi^{\alpha}_{2}=\left(\tfrac{1}{q_{1}q_{4}},0\right),\quad\xi^{\alpha}_{3}=\left(q_{1}q_{4},-2q_{4}^{2}\right).

(80)

Thus, we obtain three COMs
Q_{i}=\xi^{\alpha}_{i}p_{\alpha}
corresponding to

Q_{1}=q_{4}p_{4},\quad Q_{2}=\tfrac{p_{1}}{q_{1}q_{4}},\quad Q_{3}=q_{1}q_{4}p_{1}-2q_{4}^{2}p_{4}.

(81)

These are indeed conserved, since their Poisson brackets with the Hamiltonian satisfy

\{Q_{1},H\}=-H,\quad\{Q_{2},H\}=\tfrac{1}{q_{1}^{2}q_{4}}H,\quad\{Q_{3},H\}=3q_{4}H,

(82)

meaning that they vanish weakly — that is, they are zero on the surfaces where the Hamiltonian constraint holds.
Their Poisson brackets are given by

\{Q_{1},Q_{2}\}=Q_{2},\quad\{Q_{1},Q_{3}\}=-Q_{3},\quad\{Q_{2},Q_{3}\}=0,

(83)

which shows that it is again a closed algebra of Bianchi type VI with
h=-1
.

III.4.4
Quantization

To quantize the system, we promote
q_{1},q_{4},p_{1},p_{4}
to operators as in the previous sections. The WDW equation can be written as

q_{1}\partial_{q_{1}}\Psi+q_{1}^{2}\partial_{q_{1}}^{2}\Psi-2q_{1}q_{4}\partial_{q_{1}}\partial_{q_{4}}\Psi-4n^{2}q_{1}^{2}\Psi=0.

(84)

The solution of this equation is given by

\Psi_{z}(q)=(q_{1}q_{4})^{z/2}\big[AI_{\tfrac{z}{2}}\left(2nq_{1}\right)+BK_{\tfrac{z}{2}}\left(2nq_{1}\right)\big],

(85)

where
A
and
B
are arbitrary complex constants.
This wave function is unbounded at infinity, meaning that the corresponding quantum system is not confined.

Imposing the constraints due to CSs, we demand the wave function to be an eigenfunction of the COMs, i.e.,
\hat{Q}_{i}\ket{\Psi}=\kappa_{i}\ket{\Psi}
, where
\kappa_{i}
are constants. Explicitly, this can be written as

\displaystyle-iq_{4}\partial_{q_{4}}\Psi=\kappa_{1}\Psi,\quad-\tfrac{i}{q_{1}q_{4}}\partial_{q_{1}}\Psi=\kappa_{2}\Psi,\quad-iq_{1}q_{4}\partial_{q_{1}}\Psi+2iq_{4}^{2}\partial_{q_{4}}\Psi=\kappa_{3}\Psi.

(86)

Using these equations, the Hamiltonian constraint takes the form

2iq_{1}^{2}q_{4}\kappa_{2}\Psi-q_{1}^{4}q_{4}\kappa_{2}^{2}\Psi+2q_{1}^{2}q_{4}\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\Psi-4n^{2}q_{1}^{2}\Psi=0,

(87)

which has only the trivial solution
\Psi=0
for non-zero
n
. Notice that when
n=0
, the WDW equation coincides with its p. Schwarzschild counterpart. However, each equation must be solved separately and the limit
n\to 0
of the wave function (
85
) does not simplify directly to the wave function of p. Schwarzschild.

As in the previous cases, the full algebra gives only the trivial solution, so we restrict ourselves to subalgebras. The non-Abelian subalgebras
\langle Q_{1},Q_{2}\rangle
and
\langle Q_{1},Q_{3}\rangle
result in trivial wave functions, while the
2d
Abelian subalgebra
\langle Q_{2},Q_{3}\rangle
as well as
\langle Q_{2}\rangle
leads to

\Psi(q)=Ae^{\tfrac{i\kappa_{2}q_{1}^{2}q_{4}}{2}-\tfrac{2in^{2}}{\kappa_{2}q_{4}}},

(88)

where
A
is an arbitrary complex constant. The subalgebra
\langle Q_{3}\rangle
produces the same wave function with the reparameterization
\kappa_{2}=-\tfrac{4n^{2}}{\kappa_{3}}
. For
\langle Q_{2},Q_{3}\rangle
, we can fix the measure by requiring the quantized CS generators to be Hermitian. The first step is to rescale
G_{\alpha\beta}
by
\tfrac{-2n^{2}q_{1}}{q_{4}}
and
V
by
\tfrac{-q_{4}}{2n^{2}q_{1}}
setting the rescaled superpotential to one, thus, the CS generators are Killing vectors for the rescaled supermetric. Solving the equations
\partial_{\alpha}\left(\mu\xi^{\alpha}\right)=0
with the generators
\xi_{2}
and
\xi_{3}
, we find that the measure is fixed up to a normalization constant:
\mu=B\tfrac{q_{1}}{q_{4}}
, where
B
is a real positive constant. Putting all together, we get the probability distribution

P(q)=\tfrac{|A|^{2}Bq_{1}}{q_{4}}.

(89)

Finally, restricting to
\langle Q_{1}\rangle
gives the wave function

\Psi(q)=(q_{1}q_{4})^{i\kappa_{1}}\big[AI_{i\kappa_{1}}\left(2nq_{1}\right)+BK_{i\kappa_{1}}\left(2nq_{1}\right)\big],

(90)

where
A
and
B
are arbitrary complex constants.

III.5

[4,3,\{8,9,10,11\}]

Colléaux et al. 2025

III.5.1
B-metrics —
[4,3,\{8,11\}]

•

In the case of s. Schwarzschild, performing the double Wick rotations
t=i\mathfrak{q}
and

\check{g}=q_{1}d\mathfrak{q}^{2}+2\tilde{q}_{2}d\mathfrak{q}dr+q_{3}dr^{2}+q_{4}\left(-\tfrac{d\rho^{2}}{\rho^{2}-1}+\rho^{2}d\varphi^{2}\right),

(91)

after complex redefinition of the function
\tilde{q}_{2}=iq_{2}

[4,3,8]

\varepsilon R
and performing the symmetry reduction,

For h. Schwarzschild, after performing the transformations

The reduced Lagrangian from

For the p. Schwarzschild case, applying the double Wick rotations

and

NHEK and swirling —

where
\tilde{q}_{2}=iq_{2}

The reduced Lagrangian derived from the reduction of

\check{g}=q_{1}\left(d\mathfrak{q}+2nxdy\right)^{2}+2\tilde{q}_{2}dr\left(d\mathfrak{q}+2nxdy\right)+q_{3}dr^{2}+q_{4}\left(dx^{2}-dy^{2}\right),

IV
Symmetries of FLRW-type spacetimes —
[6,3,-]

Here, we consider the class of symmetry-invariant metrics possessing the symmetries of FLRW spacetimes (
[6,3,\{1,2,3\}]
), as well as the related symmetries
[6,3,\{4,5,6\}]
where the orbit is
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
,
\mathrm{M}_{3}
, or
\mathrm{dS}_{3}
, so we refer to them as the
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}/\mathrm{M}_{3}/\mathrm{dS}_{3}
-homogeneous spacetimes (
[6,3,\{4,5,6\}]
).

IV.1
Closed, open, and flat FLRW —
[6,3,\{1,3,2\}]

IV.1.1
Symmetry-invariant metric and classical solutions

The Killing vectors generating the isometries of FLRW spacetimes depend on the spatial curvature. For closed FLRW spacetimes, they are

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=\cos\varphi\partial_{\varv}-\cot\varv\sin\varphi\partial_{\varphi},\quad X_{2}=-\sin\varphi\partial_{\varv}-\cos\varphi\cot\varv\partial_{\varphi},\quad X_{3}=\partial_{\varphi},

(97)

\displaystyle X_{4}

\displaystyle=\cos\varphi\sin\varv\partial_{\chi}+\cos\varv\cos\varphi\cot\chi\partial_{\varv}-\cot\chi\csc\varv\sin\varphi\partial_{\varphi},

\displaystyle X_{5}

\displaystyle=\sin\varv\sin\varphi\partial_{\chi}+\cos\varv\cot\chi\sin\varphi\partial_{\varv}+\cos\varphi\cot\chi\csc\varv\partial_{\varphi},

\displaystyle X_{6}

\displaystyle=\cos\varv\partial_{\chi}-\cot\chi\sin\varv\partial_{\varv}.

For open FLRW spacetimes, the Killing vectors read

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=\cos\varphi\partial_{\varv}-\cot\varv\sin\varphi\partial_{\varphi},\quad X_{2}=-\sin\varphi\partial_{\varv}-\cos\varphi\cot\varv\partial_{\varphi},\quad X_{3}=\partial_{\varphi},

(98)

\displaystyle X_{4}

\displaystyle=\cos\varphi\sin\varv\partial_{\chi}+\cos\varv\cos\varphi\operatorname{cth}\chi\partial_{\varv}-\operatorname{cth}\chi\csc\varv\sin\varphi\partial_{\varphi},

\displaystyle X_{5}

\displaystyle=\sin\varv\sin\varphi\partial_{\chi}+\cos\varv\operatorname{cth}\chi\sin\varphi\partial_{\varv}+\cos\varphi\operatorname{cth}\chi\csc\varv\partial_{\varphi},

\displaystyle X_{6}

\displaystyle=\cos\varv\partial_{\chi}-\operatorname{cth}\chi\sin\varv\partial_{\varv}.

Finally, for flat FLRW spacetimes, the Killing vectors take the simpler form

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\varv},\quad X_{2}=\partial_{\varphi},\quad X_{3}=-\varphi\partial_{\varv}+\varv\partial_{\varphi},\quad X_{4}=\varphi\partial_{\chi}-\chi\partial_{\varphi},\quad X_{5}=\varv\partial_{\chi}-\chi\partial_{\varv},\quad X_{6}=-\partial_{\chi}.

(99)

The symmetry-invariant metrics are

\check{g}=-q_{1}(t)^{2}dt^{2}+q_{2}(t)^{2}\begin{cases}d\chi^{2}+\sin^{2}\chi(d\varv^{2}+\sin^{2}\varv d\varphi^{2}),\\
d\chi^{2}+\operatorname{sh}^{2}\chi(d\varv^{2}+\sin^{2}\varv d\varphi^{2}),\\
d\chi^{2}+d\varv^{2}+d\varphi^{2},\\
\end{cases}

(100)

for closed, open, and flat FLRW spacetimes, respectively. Note that the most general symmetry-invariant metric uses
q_{1}(t)
and
q_{2}(t)
without squaring, due to the linearity of
\mathcal{L}_{X_{i}}\check{g}=0
. However, in this form, the supermetric and superpotential become constant, causing the quantization method used in this paper to break down. Hence, we retain the squares in the symmetry-invariant metrics. The only symmetry-invariant metrics that admit a classical vacuum solution for GR correspond to the p. FLRW with
{q_{1}(t)=1}
and
{q_{2}(t)=\textrm{const.}}
or the open FLRW with
{q_{1}(t)=1}
and
{q_{2}(t)=t}
both of which describe (parts of) the Minkowski spacetime. In the following, we construct the reduced Lagrangian for the minisuperspaces corresponding to the symmetry-invariant metrics (
100
), determine their CSs, and subsequently proceed to their quantization.

IV.1.2
Reduced Lagrangian and generalized momenta

To derive the reduced Lagrangian, we substitute the symmetry-invariant metric into
\varepsilon R
. This yields

\varepsilon R=\begin{cases}\frac{6q_{2}\left(-q_{2}\dot{q}_{2}\dot{q}_{1}+q_{1}\left(q_{2}\ddot{q}_{2}+\dot{q}_{2}^{2}\right)+q_{1}^{3}\right)}{q_{1}^{2}}\sin\varv\,\sin^{2}\chi,\\
\frac{6q_{2}\left(-q_{2}\dot{q}_{2}\dot{q}_{1}+q_{1}\left(q_{2}\ddot{q}_{2}+\dot{q}_{2}^{2}\right)-q_{1}^{3}\right)}{q_{1}^{2}}\sin\varv\,\operatorname{sh}^{2}\chi,\\
\frac{6q_{2}\left(-q_{2}\dot{q}_{2}\dot{q}_{1}+q_{1}\left(q_{2}\ddot{q}_{2}+\dot{q}_{2}^{2}\right)\right)}{q_{1}^{2}},\end{cases}

(101)

where
\dot{q}_{1}=\tfrac{dq_{1}}{dt}
and
\dot{q}_{2}=\tfrac{dq_{2}}{dt}
.
The reduced Lagrangians for the three cases can be written in the compact form

\check{L}=6q_{2}\left(kq_{1}-\tfrac{\dot{q}_{2}^{2}}{q_{1}}\right),

(102)

where
k=1,-1,0
represent closed, open, and flat FLRW, respectively.
Writing the reduced Lagrangian in the form
\check{L}=\tfrac{1}{2q_{1}}G_{\alpha\beta}(q_{2})\dot{q}^{\alpha}\dot{q}^{\beta}-q_{1}V(q_{2})
, the supermetric
G_{\alpha\beta}
in this case is a
1\times 1
matrix:
G_{\alpha\beta}(q_{2})=-12q_{2}
, with
\alpha,\beta=2
and
V(q_{2})=-6kq_{2}
. The canonical momentum conjugate to
q_{2}
is derived from
p_{2}=\tfrac{\partial\check{L}}{\partial\dot{q}_{2}}
, resulting in

p_{2}=-12\frac{q_{2}\dot{q}_{2}}{q_{1}},

(103)

thus,
\dot{q}_{2}=-\tfrac{q_{1}p_{2}}{12q_{2}}
. Using these formulae, the Hamiltonian
H=\dot{q}_{2}p_{2}-\check{L}
is given by

H=q_{1}\left(-\tfrac{p^{2}_{2}}{24q_{2}}-6kq_{2}\right).

(104)

IV.1.3
Conditional symmetries

The generators of the CS algebra are easily derivable from

\pounds_{\xi}G^{\alpha\beta}=\Phi G^{\alpha\beta}
and
\pounds_{\xi}V=\Phi V
, where
\Phi=\Phi(q_{2})
,
\alpha,\beta=q_{2}
and
\xi^{\alpha}(q_{2})
is the CS generator and is composed of one component which we will call
\xi
. These two equations converge into
\xi k=q_{2}\Phi k
. If
k=\pm 1
, then
\xi=q_{2}\Phi
. If
k=0
, the equation
\pounds_{\xi}V=\Phi V
is satisfied trivially and from
\pounds_{\xi}G^{\alpha\beta}=\Phi G^{\alpha\beta}
, we also end up with
\xi=q_{2}\Phi
. The COMs are given by
Q=\xi p_{2}=q_{2}\Phi p_{2}
. From the requirement
\{Q,H\}\propto H
, we get
\Phi=\tfrac{1}{q_{2}}
, i.e.,
\xi=1
and
Q=p_{2}
. Thus, the algebra of CSs is trivial.

IV.1.4
Quantization

Moving on to the quantization procedure, we promote the coordinates and momenta to operators similarly to the previous sections. The WDW equation is

\tfrac{1}{24q_{2}}\tfrac{d^{2}\Psi}{dq_{2}^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{48q_{2}^{2}}\tfrac{d\Psi}{dq_{2}}-6kq_{2}\Psi=0.

(105)

For
k=\pm 1
, the general solution to this equation is given by

\Psi(q_{2})=3^{3/8}(-k)^{3/16}q_{2}^{3/4}\Big[AJ_{-\tfrac{3}{8}}\left(6i\sqrt{k}q_{2}^{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\tfrac{5}{8}\right)+BJ_{\tfrac{3}{8}}\left(6i\sqrt{k}q_{2}^{2}\right)\Gamma(\tfrac{11}{8})\Big],

(106)

where
A
and
B
are arbitrary (normalization) complex constants. In this case, the measure can be fixed by requiring the CS generators to be Hermitian by going to a gauge where the superpotential is constant. This is done by rescaling it by
\frac{1}{q_{2}}
:
V=-6k
, while rescaling
G_{\alpha\beta}
by
q_{2}
:
G_{\alpha\beta}=-12q_{2}^{2}
.
The measure is then given by
\mu=C\sqrt{12}q_{2}
, where
C
is a positive real constant. For
k=\pm 1
, the probability distribution is then given by

\displaystyle P(q_{2})=\mu|\Psi(q_{2})|^{2}=\tilde{A}q_{2}^{5/2}\big|AJ_{-\tfrac{3}{8}}\left(6i\sqrt{k}q_{2}^{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\tfrac{5}{8}\right)+BJ_{\tfrac{3}{8}}\left(6i\sqrt{k}q_{2}^{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\tfrac{11}{8}\right)\big|^{2},

(107)

where
\tilde{A}=3^{3/4}k^{3/8}\sqrt{12}C
.
For
k=0
, the general solution is

\Psi(q_{2})=Aq_{2}^{3/2}+B.

(108)

The equation used to fix the measure is given by
\partial_{\nu}(\mu\xi^{\nu})=0
. Noting that
\xi^{q_{2}}=\xi=1
, we can see that the solution is
\mu=C
, where
C
is a positive real constant. Thus, the probability distribution is

P(q_{2})=C|Aq_{2}^{3/2}+B|^{2}.

(109)

IV.2
\mathrm{AdS_{3}}
-homogeneous —
[6,3,4]

In this case, the symmetry-invariant metric given by

\check{g}=q_{1}(t)^{2}dt^{2}+q_{2}(t)^{2}\left(-\operatorname{ch}^{2}y_{2}dy_{1}^{2}+dy_{2}^{2}+\operatorname{sh}^{2}y_{2}dy_{3}^{2}\right).

(110)

The corresponding reduced Lagrangian is derived as usual with the end result being

\check{L}=\tfrac{6q_{2}\dot{q}_{2}^{2}}{q_{1}}-6q_{2}q_{1},

(111)

which is the same as the reduced Lagrangian of
[6,3,1]
up to an overall sign. This implies that the Hamiltonian coincides with that of
[6,3,1]
, namely
H=-\tfrac{p_{2}^{2}}{24q_{2}}-6q_{2}
. Upon quantization, the WDW equation and CSs remain unchanged. Thus, the wave function for
[6,3,4]
is given by (
106
) with
k=1
.

IV.3
\mathrm{dS_{3}}
-homogeneous —
[6,3,6]

In this case, the symmetry-invariant metric is

\check{g}=q_{1}(t)^{2}dt^{2}+q_{2}(t)^{2}\left(-dy_{1}^{2}+\operatorname{ch}^{2}y_{1}(dy_{2}^{2}+\sin^{2}y_{2}dy_{3}^{2})\right).

(112)

The corresponding reduced Lagrangian reads

\check{L}=-6q_{2}\dot{q}_{2}^{2}-6q_{2},

(113)

which matches that of
[6,3,3]
up to an overall sign. Consequently, the Hamiltonian is
H=-\tfrac{p_{2}^{2}}{24q_{2}}+6q_{2}
, and the quantization yields the same WDW equation and CSs as for
[6,3,3]
. The corresponding wave function is therefore given by (
106
) with
k=-1
.

IV.4
\mathrm{M_{3}}
-homogeneous —
[6,3,5]

Here, the symmetry-invariant metric is given by

\check{g}=q_{1}(t)^{2}dt^{2}+q_{2}(t)^{2}\left(-dy_{1}^{2}+dy_{2}^{2}+dy_{3}^{2}\right).

(114)

The reduced Lagrangian is

\check{L}=-6q_{2}\dot{q}_{2}^{2},

(115)

identical to that of
[6,3,2]
. Accordingly, the Hamiltonian coincides with the
[6,3,2]
case,
H=-\tfrac{p_{2}^{2}}{24q_{2}}
, and the WDW equation and CSs are the same. The resulting wave function is therefore given by (
108
).

IV.5
Models with cosmological constant and scalar field

As mentioned in the beginning of the section, only flat or open FLRW admit a vacuum solution in GR and this is just the Minkowski spacetime which belongs to
[10,4,-]
instead (i.e., maximally symmetric spacetime). In order to quantize models with solutions that belong strictly to
[6,3,-]
, we add a cosmological constant and a massless scalar field. The cosmological-constant and matter terms to be added to the Lagrangian can be written as

\check{L}_{\text{matter}}=\epsilon\left(\Lambda+\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi\right),

(116)

where
\Lambda
is the cosmological constant and
\phi=\phi(t)
is a massless scalar field.

IV.5.1
Reduced Lagrangian and generalized momenta

Substituting the symmetry-invariant metrics (
100
) in (
116
) and adding these terms to the gravitational reduced Lagrangian, we get the total reduced Lagrangian

\check{L}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}\left(-6q_{2}\dot{q}_{2}^{2}+q_{2}^{3}\dot{\phi}^{2}\right)+q_{1}\left(6kq_{2}+\Lambda q_{2}^{3}\right).

(117)

This expression can be recast to the form
\check{L}=\tfrac{1}{2q_{1}}G_{\alpha\beta}(q)\dot{q}^{\alpha}\dot{q}^{\beta}-q_{1}V(q)
, where
q=(q_{2},\phi)
and the supermetric and superpotential read

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}-12q_{2}&0\\
0&2q_{2}^{3}\end{pmatrix},\quad V=-6kq_{2}-\Lambda q_{2}^{3}.

(118)

The canonical momenta are given by

p_{2}=\tfrac{\partial\check{L}}{\partial\dot{q}_{2}}=-12\frac{q_{2}\dot{q}_{2}}{q_{1}},\quad p_{\phi}=\tfrac{\partial\check{L}}{\partial\dot{\phi}}=2\frac{q_{2}^{3}\dot{\phi}}{q_{1}}.

(119)

Using

\dot{q}_{2}=-\tfrac{q_{1}p_{2}}{12q_{2}},\quad\dot{\phi}=\tfrac{q_{1}p_{\phi}}{2q_{2}^{3}},

(120)

the Hamiltonian becomes

H=p_{2}\dot{q}_{2}+p_{\phi}\dot{\phi}-\check{L}=q_{1}\left(-\tfrac{p_{2}^{2}}{24q_{2}}+\tfrac{p_{\phi}^{2}}{4q_{2}^{3}}-6kq_{2}-\Lambda q_{2}^{3}\right).

(121)

IV.5.2
Conditional symmetries

The generators of the CS algebra
\xi^{\alpha}=(\xi^{1}(q),\xi^{4}(q))
are calculated from

\pounds_{\xi}G^{\alpha\beta}=\Phi G^{\alpha\beta},\quad\pounds_{\xi}V=\Phi V,

(122)

where
\Phi=\Phi(q)
.
For
k=\pm 1
, the equations reduce to

\displaystyle q_{2}^{2}\partial_{q_{2}}\xi^{4}=6\partial_{\phi}\xi^{1},\quad\xi^{1}+2q_{2}\partial_{q_{2}}\xi^{1}=-\tfrac{\xi^{1}(6k+3\Lambda q_{2}^{2})}{6k+\Lambda q_{2}^{2}},\quad 3\xi^{1}+2q_{2}\partial_{\phi}\xi^{4}=-\tfrac{\xi^{1}(6k+3\Lambda q_{2}^{2})}{6k+\Lambda q_{2}^{2}}.

(123)

The solution is
\xi^{1}=0
and
\xi^{4}=A
, where
A
is a real constant. Thus, the generator for the
1d
CS algebra is
\xi^{\alpha}=(0,1)
, which yields the constant of motion
Q=\xi^{\alpha}p_{\alpha}=p_{\phi}
.

For
k=0
, the generators of the CS algebra are derived by solving

\displaystyle q_{2}^{2}\partial_{q_{2}}\xi^{4}=6\partial_{\phi}\xi^{1},\quad q_{2}\partial_{q_{2}}\xi^{1}=-2\xi^{1},\quad q_{2}^{2}\partial_{\phi}\xi^{4}=-3\xi^{1}.

(124)

The general solution is

\xi^{1}=\tfrac{A}{q_{2}^{2}}e^{\sqrt{3/2}\phi}+\tfrac{B}{q_{2}^{2}}e^{-\sqrt{3/2}\phi},\quad\xi^{4}=-\tfrac{\sqrt{6}A}{q_{2}^{3}}e^{\sqrt{3/2}\phi}+\tfrac{\sqrt{6}B}{q_{2}^{3}}e^{-\sqrt{3/2}\phi}+C,

(125)

where
A
,
B
, and
C
are real constants. Thus, the generators of the corresponding
3d
CS algebra are given by

\xi_{1}=(0,1),\quad\xi_{2}=\left(\tfrac{e^{-\sqrt{3/2}\phi}}{q_{2}^{2}},\sqrt{6}\tfrac{e^{-\sqrt{3/2}\phi}}{q_{2}^{3}}\right),\quad\xi_{3}=\left(\tfrac{e^{\sqrt{3/2}\phi}}{q_{2}^{2}},-\sqrt{6}\tfrac{e^{\sqrt{3/2}\phi}}{q_{2}^{3}}\right).

(126)

The COMs derived from these generators read
Q_{1}=p_{\phi}
,
Q_{2}=\tfrac{e^{-\sqrt{3/2}\phi}}{q_{2}^{2}}p_{2}+\sqrt{6}\tfrac{e^{-\sqrt{3/2}\phi}}{q_{2}^{3}}p_{\phi}
, and
Q_{3}=\tfrac{e^{\sqrt{3/2}\phi}}{q_{2}^{2}}p_{2}-\sqrt{6}\tfrac{e^{\sqrt{3/2}\phi}}{q_{2}^{3}}p_{\phi}
, with Poisson brackets
\{Q_{1},Q_{2}\}=Q_{2}
,
\{Q_{3},Q_{1}\}=Q_{3}
, and
\{Q_{3},Q_{2}\}=0
. Again, the resulting algebra is of Bianchi type VI with
h=-1
.

IV.5.3
Quantization

Apart from the operators
\hat{q}_{2}
and
\hat{p}_{2}
we also introduce
\phi\to\hat{\phi}
such that
\hat{\phi}\ket{\Psi}=\phi\ket{\Psi}
and
\hat{p}_{\phi}\ket{\Psi}=-i\partial_{\phi}\ket{\Psi}
. The WDW equation is

\left(-\tfrac{1}{2\mu}\partial_{\alpha}\left(\mu G^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta}\right)+V\right)\Psi(q)=0.

(127)

Upon inserting the supermetric and superpotential, it takes the form

\tfrac{q_{2}^{2}}{6}\partial_{q_{2}}^{2}\Psi+\tfrac{q_{2}}{6}\partial_{q_{2}}\Psi+\partial_{\phi}^{2}\Psi+(24kq_{2}^{2}+4\Lambda q_{2}^{4})\Psi=0.

(128)

From this we distinguish between two cases:

1.

k=\pm 1

The general solution of (
128
) is

\displaystyle\Psi(q)

\displaystyle=q_{2}^{-1}e^{-\sqrt{6\Lambda}q_{2}^{2}}\big(2\sqrt{6\Lambda}q_{2}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+6\lambda}+\tfrac{1}{2}}\Big[A\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}\phi)+B\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}\phi)\Big]

(129)

\displaystyle\times\Bigg\{C_{1}{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+6\lambda}+\tfrac{18k}{\sqrt{6\Lambda}}+\tfrac{1}{2};\sqrt{1+6\lambda}+1;2\sqrt{6\Lambda}q_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+C_{2}\big(2\sqrt{6\Lambda}q_{2}^{2}\big)^{-\sqrt{1+6\lambda}}{}_{1}F_{1}\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+6\lambda}+\tfrac{18k}{\sqrt{6\Lambda}}+\tfrac{1}{2};1-\sqrt{1+6\lambda};2\sqrt{6\Lambda}q_{2}^{2}\right)\Bigg\},

where
{}_{1}F_{1}(a;b;z)
is the confluent hypergeometric function of the first kind,
A,B,C_{1},C_{2}
and
\lambda
are arbitrary complex constants.

We can simplify the equation using the CSs, which is promoted to an operator
Q\to\hat{Q}
such that
\hat{Q}\ket{\Psi}=\kappa\ket{\Psi}
with
\kappa
being a constant. Since
Q=p_{\phi}
, we have
\hat{Q}\ket{\Psi}=\hat{p}\ket{\Psi}=-i\partial_{\phi}\ket{\Psi}
. This imposes an additional eigenvalue equation
-i\partial_{\phi}\Psi=\kappa\Psi
whose solution is
\Psi(q)=\tilde{\Psi}(q_{2})e^{i\kappa\phi}
. Substituting into the WDW equation, we get

\tfrac{q_{2}^{2}}{6}\partial_{q_{2}}^{2}\tilde{\Psi}+\tfrac{q_{2}}{6}\partial_{a}\tilde{\Psi}+\left(24kq_{2}^{2}+4\Lambda q_{2}^{4}-\kappa^{2}\right)\tilde{\Psi}=0.

(130)

The solution is

\displaystyle\tilde{\Psi}(q_{2})

\displaystyle=C_{1}(6\Lambda)^{\frac{1+\sqrt{1-\frac{3}{2}\kappa^{2}}}{4}}q_{2}^{1+\sqrt{1-\frac{3}{2}\kappa^{2}}}e^{-\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{6\Lambda}q_{2}^{2}}{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\left(1+\sqrt{1-\tfrac{3}{2}\kappa^{2}}\right)+\tfrac{9k}{\sqrt{6\Lambda}};1+\sqrt{1-\tfrac{3}{2}\kappa^{2}};\sqrt{6\Lambda}q_{2}^{2}\right)

(131)

\displaystyle+C_{2}(6\Lambda)^{\frac{1-\sqrt{1-\frac{3}{2}\kappa^{2}}}{4}}q_{2}^{1-\sqrt{1-\frac{3}{2}\kappa^{2}}}e^{-\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{6\Lambda}q_{2}^{2}}{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\left(1-\sqrt{1-\tfrac{3}{2}\kappa^{2}}\right)+\tfrac{9k}{\sqrt{6\Lambda}};1-\sqrt{1-\tfrac{3}{2}\kappa^{2}};\sqrt{6\Lambda}q_{2}^{2}\right),

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are arbitrary complex constants.
Then, the wave function is given by

\Psi(q)=\tilde{\Psi}(q_{2})e^{i\kappa\phi}.

(132)

2.

k=0

The general solution of (
128
) is given by

\displaystyle\Psi(q)

\displaystyle=C_{1}q_{2}^{1+\sqrt{1-\frac{3}{2}\lambda^{2}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{6\Lambda}q_{2}^{2}}{}_{1}F_{1}\Bigg(\tfrac{1+\sqrt{1-\frac{3}{2}\lambda^{2}}}{2};1+\sqrt{1-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda^{2}};\sqrt{6\Lambda}q_{2}^{2}\Bigg)e^{i\lambda\phi}

(133)

\displaystyle+C_{2}q_{2}^{1-\sqrt{1-\frac{3}{2}\lambda^{2}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{6\Lambda}q_{2}^{2}}{}_{1}F_{1}\Bigg(\tfrac{1-\sqrt{1-\frac{3}{2}\lambda^{2}}}{2};1-\sqrt{1-\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda^{2}};\sqrt{6\Lambda}q_{2}^{2}\Bigg)e^{-i\lambda\phi},

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are arbitrary complex constants.
As in the previous case, we can simplify the Hamiltonian constraint using CSs. Upon quantization, the eigenvalue equations for the CSs are

-i\partial_{\phi}\Psi=\kappa_{1}\Psi,\quad-i\left(\tfrac{e^{-\sqrt{3/2}\phi}}{q_{2}^{2}}\partial_{q_{2}}+\sqrt{6}\tfrac{e^{-\sqrt{3/2}\phi}}{q_{2}^{3}}\partial_{\phi}\right)\Psi=\kappa_{2}\Psi,\quad-i\left(\tfrac{e^{\sqrt{3/2}\phi}}{q_{2}^{2}}\partial_{q_{2}}-\sqrt{6}\tfrac{e^{\sqrt{3/2}\phi}}{q_{2}^{3}}\partial_{\phi}\right)\Psi=\kappa_{3}\Psi,

(134)

where
\kappa_{1}
,
\kappa_{2}
, and
\kappa_{3}
are constants. Imposing all equations leads to a vanishing wave function (
\Psi(q)=0
). Restricting ourselves to subalgebras of the CS algebra, we find that all subalgebras lead to trivial wave functions except
\langle Q_{1}\rangle
which results in (
131
) with
k=0
and
\kappa\to\kappa_{1}
.

As explained in the previous section, the CSs and the quantization of the
\mathrm{AdS_{3}}
/
\mathrm{M_{3}}
/
\mathrm{dS_{3}}
-homogeneous spacetimes
[6,3,\{4,5,6\}]
with a cosmological constant and a massless scalar field yield the same results as those of

explored in this section.

Above, we reviewed the quantization of FLRW cosmologies in vacuum and with an added cosmological constant and a minimally coupled massless scalar field. It is worth mentioning that these models were studied extensively with different matter content. The minisuperspace quantization of FLRW was performed with a minimally coupled scalar field
Lin 2024

Dimakis et al. 2016b

The quantization of FLRW with

.

V
Symmetries of Bianchi class A —
[3,3,\{2,3,8,9\}]

Bianchi class A models refers to Bianchi types I, II, VIII, IX, which are all PSC-compatible. In contrast, the Bianchi class B models do not satisfy PSC2, and their minisuperspaces therefore lead to incorrect reduced field equations
Hawking 1969
;
Fels and Torre 2002
. In GR, only the most general Bianchi type I and II models admit vacuum solutions strictly with such symmetries. In contrast, for Bianchi type VIII and IX models, solutions exist only with enhanced symmetries as we will see below.

V.1
Bianchi type I —
[3,3,2]

In this section, following
Christodoulakis et al. 2002
, we review the quantization of symmetry-invariant metrics with Bianchi type I symmetries.

V.1.1
Symmetry-invariant metric and classical solutions

The Killing vectors defining the symmetries of Bianchi type I (
[3,3,2]
) are

X_{1}=\partial_{x^{1}},\quad X_{2}=\partial_{x^{2}},\quad X_{3}=\partial_{x^{3}}.

(135)

The general symmetry-invariant metric reads

\check{g}=(-N(t)+N_{\alpha}(t)N^{\alpha}(t))dt^{2}+2N_{\alpha}(t)\sigma^{\alpha}(x)dt+\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\sigma^{\alpha}(x)\sigma^{\beta},

(136)

with
\sigma^{\alpha}(x)
being the 1-forms satisfying
d\sigma^{\alpha}(x)=0
, i.e.,
\sigma^{\alpha}=dx^{\alpha}
,
and
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}
being the Riemannian metric on the homogeneous
3d
space.
A known vacuum GR solution strictly within this class is the Kasner cosmology
Kasner 1921
,

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+t^{2p_{1}}(dx^{1}){}^{2}+t^{2p_{2}}(dx^{2}){}^{2}+t^{2p_{3}}(dx^{3}){}^{2},\quad\sum_{\mathclap{i=1}}^{3}p_{i}=1.

(137)

V.1.2
Hamiltonian and generalized momenta

Following
Sneddon 1976
, the Hamiltonian can be written as

H=\tfrac{N}{\sqrt{\gamma}}H_{0},

(138)

where
\gamma=\det(\gamma_{\alpha\beta})
. For Bianchi type I, we have

H_{0}=\tfrac{1}{2}L_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\pi^{\alpha\beta}\pi^{\mu\nu},

(139)

where
L_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}=\gamma_{\alpha\mu}\gamma_{\beta\nu}+\gamma_{\alpha\nu}\gamma_{\beta\mu}-\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\gamma_{\mu\nu}
, and
\pi^{\mu\nu}
are the canonical momenta dual to
\gamma_{\mu\nu}
in the sense that they satisfy

\{\gamma_{\alpha\beta},\pi^{\mu\nu}\}=\delta_{\alpha}^{\mu}\delta_{\beta}^{\nu}-\delta_{\beta}^{\mu}\delta_{\alpha}^{\nu}.

(140)

V.1.3
Conditional symmetries

In this formalism, the generators of the CS algebra are given by
Christodoulakis et al. 2002

E_{(I)}{}_{\beta\rho}=\lambda_{(I)\beta}^{\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha\rho},

(141)

where
I=\{1,2,\cdots,9\}
and

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\lambda_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\lambda_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\lambda_{4}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\lambda_{5}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix},

(142)

\displaystyle\lambda_{6}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\lambda_{7}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&-1&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\lambda_{8}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&-1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad\lambda_{9}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

The COMs are then given by

Q_{(I)}=E_{(I)}{}_{\beta\rho}\pi^{\beta\rho},

(143)

which can be shown to satisfy
\{Q_{(I)},H_{0}\}=0
. Notice that the matrices
\lambda_{(I)}
form an algebra

[\lambda_{(I)},\lambda_{(J)}]=C_{IJ}^{M}\lambda_{(M)},

(144)

implying that
\{Q_{(I)},Q_{(J)}\}=-\tfrac{1}{2}C_{IJ}^{M}Q_{(M)}
,
with the non-vanishing structure constants
C_{IJ}^{M}
given by

\displaystyle C_{13}^{2}=C_{24}^{1}=C_{28}^{2}=C_{46}^{5}=C_{25}^{7}=C_{68}^{6}=C_{16}^{7}=C_{35}^{6}=C_{37}^{3}=C_{57}^{5}=1,\quad C_{67}^{6}=C_{38}^{3}=2,\quad C_{48}^{4}=C_{17}^{1}=-2

(145)

\displaystyle C_{15}^{4}=C_{34}^{8}=C_{47}^{4}=C_{25}^{8}=C_{18}^{1}=C_{26}^{3}=C_{27}^{2}=C_{58}^{5}=-1.

V.1.4
Quantization

To quantize this system, we promote
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}
and
\pi^{\alpha\beta}
to operators as follows:
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\to\hat{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}
such that

\hat{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\ket{\Psi}=\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\ket{\Psi}
and
\pi^{\alpha\beta}\to\hat{\pi}^{\alpha\beta}
such that
\hat{\pi}^{\alpha\beta}\ket{\Psi}=-i\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\gamma_{\alpha\beta}}\ket{\Psi}
. The commutators between
\hat{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}
and
\hat{\pi}^{\alpha\beta}
follow from (
140
) by replacing the Poisson bracket with a commutator:

[\hat{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta},\hat{\pi}^{\mu\nu}]=\tfrac{i}{2}(\delta_{\alpha}^{\mu}\delta_{\beta}^{\nu}-\delta_{\beta}^{\mu}\delta_{\alpha}^{\nu}).

(146)

The eigenvalue equations for the COMs are given by

\hat{Q}_{(I)}\hat{\Psi}=-i\lambda_{(I)\alpha}^{\tau}\gamma_{\tau\beta}\tfrac{\partial\ket{\Psi}}{\partial\gamma_{\alpha\beta}}=\kappa_{(I)}\ket{\Psi},

(147)

where
\kappa_{(I)}
are constants. The commutators between
\hat{Q}_{I}
s are given by replacing the Poisson bracket in the classical expression with a commutator, i.e.,
[\hat{Q}_{I},\hat{Q}_{(J)}]=-\tfrac{1}{2}C_{IJ}^{M}\hat{Q}_{(M)}
, i.e., the COM algebra is
9-
dimensional and non-Abelian. Applying these to
\ket{\Psi}
, we get the selection rule
C_{IJ}^{M}\kappa_{(M)}=0
, and given the values of the structure constants, we deduce that
\kappa_{i}=0
where
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,8\}
, but
\kappa_{9}
remains arbitrary. Solving the system of equations (
147
) in light of the values of
\kappa_{(I)}
, we get

\Psi(\gamma)=c\gamma^{i\kappa_{9}/3},

(148)

where
c
is an arbitrary constant, that is, the wave function
\Psi
is only a function of the determinant of the spatial metric
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}
. To fix
\kappa_{9}
, we need the Hamiltonian constraint/WDW equation
\hat{H}_{0}\ket{\Psi}=0
. Promoting
H_{0}
into an operator naively will result in the usual ordering problem where the operator is not Hermitian; instead, we rearrange the terms in
H_{0}
in such a way that its corresponding operator is Hermitian. The correct formula for the Hamiltonian operator is found from the identity (for the full derivation, see appendix A in
Christodoulakis et al. 2002
)

L_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\pi^{\alpha\beta}\pi^{\mu\nu}=\left(L_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\tfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\partial\gamma_{\mu\nu}}+3\gamma_{\kappa\lambda}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\gamma_{\kappa\lambda}}+\tfrac{3}{2}\right).

(149)

The Hamiltonian constraint then reads

-\tfrac{1}{2}\left(L_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\tfrac{\partial^{2}\Psi}{\partial\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\partial\gamma_{\mu\nu}}+3\gamma_{\kappa\lambda}\tfrac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\gamma_{\kappa\lambda}}+\tfrac{3}{2}\Psi\right)=0.

(150)

This equation by itself cannot be solved, however, imposing the CSs, i.e., substituting (
148
), we get
\kappa_{9}=\pm\tfrac{3i}{\sqrt{2}}
. Thus, the wave function for the quantized vacuum Bianchi type I is given by

\Psi(\gamma)=c_{1}\gamma^{1/\sqrt{2}}+c_{2}\gamma^{-1/\sqrt{2}},

(151)

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are arbitrary constants.

In addition to the vacuum case, versions including matter have also been quantized in the literature. For instance, Bianchi type I cosmology with
n
scalar fields and an exponential potential was quantized in
Socorro et al. 2020
, the inclusion of a radiation field was studied in
gen Shen and yi Cheng 1997
, and the model with toroidal spatial sections and a cosmological constant was analyzed in
Hervik 2000
.

V.2
Bianchi type II —
[3,3,3]

Now, we follow
Christodoulakis and Papadopoulos 2002
;
Christodoulakis and Papadopoulos 2001
;
Christodoulakis et al. 1997
reviewing the quantization of the symmetry-invariant metrics possessing the symmetries of Bianchi type II (
[3,3,3]
).

V.2.1
Symmetry-invariant metric and classical solutions

The Killing vectors defining the symmetries of Bianchi type II model are

\displaystyle X_{1}=\partial_{x^{1}},\quad X_{2}=\partial_{x^{4}},\quad X_{3}=x^{4}\partial_{x^{1}}+\partial_{x^{3}}.

(152)

The symmetry-invariant metric is given by (
136
), where now
\sigma^{1}(x)=dx^{2}-x^{1}dx^{3}
,
\sigma^{2}(x)=dx^{3}
, and
\sigma^{3}(x)=dx^{1}
satisfying
d\sigma^{\alpha}(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}C^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}\sigma^{\beta}\wedge\sigma^{\gamma}
with

.
The general Bianchi type II admits, for example, the vacuum GR solution from

(153)

with

f_{2}(\tau)^{2}=\tfrac{e^{2a_{1}\tau+a_{3}}}{2a_{1}}\operatorname{ch}(2a_{1}\tau+a_{2})

,

V.2.2
Hamiltonian and generalized momenta

The Hamiltonian can be written as

H=\tfrac{N}{\sqrt{\gamma}}H_{0}+N^{\alpha}H_{\alpha},

(154)

where

\displaystyle H_{0}=L_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\pi^{\alpha\beta}\pi^{\mu\nu}+\gamma R,\quad H_{\alpha}=C_{\alpha\rho}^{\mu}\gamma_{\beta\mu}\pi^{\beta\rho}

(155)

with
R=C_{\mu\kappa}^{\alpha}C_{\nu\lambda}^{\beta}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\gamma^{\kappa\lambda}
. The generalized momenta are defined similar to the that of Bianchi I.

V.2.3
Conditional symmetries

The CSs are generated by
E_{(I)}{}_{\alpha\beta}=\epsilon^{\mu}_{(I)}{}_{\beta}\gamma_{\alpha\mu}
, where
I=1,2,\cdots,6
, and
\epsilon_{(I)}
are matrices that generate matrices of the form

\begin{pmatrix}\kappa+\mu&0&0\\
0&\kappa&\rho\\
0&\sigma&\mu\end{pmatrix}.

(156)

The COMs are given by
Q_{(I)}=E_{(I)}{}_{\alpha\beta}\pi^{\alpha\beta}
. The algebra satisfied by
Q_{(I)}
is

\{Q_{(I)},Q_{(J)}\}=\tilde{C}_{IJ}^{M}Q_{(M)},\quad\{Q_{(I)},H_{\alpha}\}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda_{(I)}{}_{\alpha}^{\beta}H_{\beta},\quad\{Q_{(I)},H_{0}\}=-2(\kappa+\mu)\gamma R,

(157)

where
\lambda_{(I)}
are the generators of matrices of the form

\begin{pmatrix}\kappa+\mu&x&y\\
0&\kappa&\rho\\
0&\sigma&\mu\end{pmatrix}.

(158)

To obtain true COMs, we have to restrict ourselves to the subalgebra where
{\kappa+\mu=0}
rendering
\{Q_{(I)},H_{0}\}=0
. Let us call the generators of this subalgebra
\tilde{E}_{(I)}{}_{\alpha\beta}
and the COMs
\tilde{Q}_{(I)}=\tilde{E}_{(I)}{}_{\alpha\beta}\pi^{\alpha\beta}
, i.e., the COM algebra is
5-
dimensional and non-Abelian.

V.2.4
Quantization

Quantizing the system in the same way as in the last subsection, we get the eigenvalue equations for the CSs

\epsilon_{(I)}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\gamma_{\alpha\rho}\tfrac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\gamma_{\beta\rho}}=0,

(159)

where the eigenvalues are taken to be zero in order for
\tilde{Q}_{(I)}
to generate symmetries
Christodoulakis and Papadopoulos 2001
.
Solving these equations while recognizing that
\epsilon_{(I)}^{\alpha}{}_{\alpha}=0
, we get

\Psi=\Psi(\gamma,q),

(160)

where
q=C_{\mu\kappa}^{\alpha}C^{\beta}_{\nu\lambda}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\gamma^{\kappa\lambda}
. Using (
149
), the WDW equation is given by

-\tfrac{1}{2}\left(L_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\tfrac{\partial^{2}\Psi}{\partial\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\partial\gamma_{\mu\nu}}+3\gamma_{\kappa\lambda}\tfrac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\gamma_{\kappa\lambda}}+\tfrac{3}{2}\Psi\right)+\gamma R\Psi=0.

(161)

This equation can not be solved in general, but we can impose the CSs to simplify it. Substituting (
160
), we get

5q^{2}\tfrac{\partial^{2}\Psi}{\partial q^{2}}-3\gamma^{2}\tfrac{\partial^{2}\Psi}{\partial\gamma^{2}}+2q\gamma\tfrac{\partial^{2}\Psi}{\partial\gamma\partial q}+5q\tfrac{\partial\Psi}{\partial q}-3\gamma\tfrac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\gamma}-2q\gamma\Psi=0,

(162)

whose solution reads

\Psi(\gamma,q)=c_{1}I_{0}\left(\sqrt{2q\gamma}\right)+c_{2}K_{0}\left(\sqrt{2q\gamma}\right),

(163)

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are constants.

VI
Conclusions

In this paper, we carried out the minisuperspace quantization procedure for all PSC-compatible models classified via the Hicks classification. We reviewed the minisuperspace quantization of the s. Schwarzschild, FLRW, and Bianchi type I, II, VIII, IX spacetimes, and presented for the first time the quantizations of h./p. Schwarzschild, s./h./p. Taub–NUT, BI/BII/BIII metrics, the swirling universe, near-horizon extremal Kerr, and
\mathrm{AdS_{3}}
/
\mathrm{M_{3}}
/
\mathrm{dS_{3}}
homogeneous spacetimes. Note that the regions inside the horizons of the s./h. Taub–NUT spacetimes are special cases of the Bianchi IX and VIII models, respectively, which have been previously derived in the literature. However, here, we derived the wave function that is valid inside and outside the horizon. In all cases, we systematically derived the reduced Lagrangians and the generalized momenta, thus, defining the corresponding minisuperspaces. We then computed the algebras of CSs and the COMs they induce. Finally, we applied the minisuperspace quantization procedure, promoting the coordinates and the generalized momenta to operators, writing and solving their WDW equations. In vacuum, a summary of the results obtained in the paper is displayed in Tab.
1
.

To obtain nontrivial solutions within
[6,3,-]
, we introduced a cosmological constant and a minimally coupled massless scalar field, since otherwise the only symmetry-invariant solutions are Minkowski spacetime (for flat FLRW) or Milne spacetime (for open FLRW). A summary of their CSs and quantization is displayed in Tab.
2
. Similarly, for
[3,3,-]
, the only symmetry-invariant metrics admitting vacuum solutions are for Bianchi type I (
[3,3,2]
) and II (
[3,3,3]
) symmetries. On the other hand, Bianchi types VIII (
[3,3,8]
) and IX (
[3,3,9]
) admit only vacuum solutions with enhanced symmetries, and thus are not strictly within
[3,3,-]
.

A potential concern regarding the results obtained in this paper is that we relied on PSC to ensure that the equations derived from the symmetry-reduced theory coincide with the reduced equations of the full theory. While PSC guarantees this equivalence for any gravitational theory, one might still wonder whether PSC-incompatible models could nevertheless exhibit such an equivalence in a specific theory, e.g. GR. However, we have verified that this is not the case for all PSC-incompatible metrics in GR by directly comparing the reduced Einstein equations with the Euler–Lagrange equations derived from the corresponding reduced Lagrangians, and finding that they differ.

While PSC guarantees the equivalence of the field equations between the full and reduced models at the classical level, this is less clear quantum mechanically, since there is no quantum analogue of PSC. The reason is that we only have access to the symmetry-reduced theories and their associated wave functions. The corresponding quantum description would be given by the full quantum gravity equations, which are infinite-dimensional: the WDW equation is a functional equation that is generally ill-defined (at best, only formally defined as an infinite system of coupled differential equations). Another possible route to derive the full quantum dynamics and test this equivalence would be loop quantum gravity; however, this programme is still incomplete, and the problem remains open.

Our results naturally motivate future work aimed at extracting physical interpretations from the CSs and wave functions of minisuperspace models, in particular probabilistic statements such as the likelihood that a quantum solution admits curvature singularities, is asymptotically flat, possesses a horizon, or has a regular symmetry axis. The main difficulty is that the measure on minisuperspace cannot be fixed by the quantization procedure alone, so the probability distribution remains undetermined, since the scheme does not select a unique supermetric and superpotential but rather a class related by the rescalings discussed in Sec.
III
. One must therefore either fix a specific supermetric and superpotential or impose additional conditions to determine the measure.

A common method to fix the probability distribution is to demand that all constants of motion operators be Hermitian, which only works if there are more COMs than dimensions of the minisuperspace. However, in this paper, we only impose Hermiticity on a COM when using its eigenvalue equation as a constraint in addition to the WDW equation. A geometrically motivated way to select a supermetric and a superpotential is to rescale them such that the superpotential is constant. In this case, the CS generators are Killing vectors of the supermetric (instead of conformal Killing vectors), and the CSs can be identified with Noether symmetries. This fixes the measure, up to a constant, to the square root of the absolute value of the determinant of the supermetric for which the superpotential is constant. This works without conditions on the COM operators, so it is valid for minisuperspaces with no CSs, such as the s./h. Taub–NUT cases. Other conditions may also be imposed; for example, requiring the probability distribution to be Gaussian, or normalizable in general, may be useful when comparing with semiclassical counterparts. However, an agreed-upon rigorous way to fix the probability distribution (up to a constant) is still lacking.

Another application to be considered is the calculation of physical quantities such as mass or angular deficit for the quantum spacetimes in question. This has been done in semiclassical settings, where the quantum spacetime is approximated by a Gaussian function peaked at the classical value. This facilitates the derivation of physical quantities similar to those of the classical spacetime
Davidson and Yellin 2014
; however, constructing a general method to extract physical quantities from full quantum theories remains an open problem.
