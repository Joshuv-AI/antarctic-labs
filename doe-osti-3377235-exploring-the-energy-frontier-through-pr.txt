Summary

This DOE Early Career Award supported a research program using the Compact Muon Solenoid
(CMS) experiment at the CERN Large Hadron Collider (LHC) to probe physics beyond the Stan-
dard Model in the top quark and Higgs boson sectors, alongside detector and trigger developments
for the High-Luminosity LHC (HL-LHC) upgrade. Exploiting the top quark and Higgs boson to
test fundamental interactions with high precision, while developing the fast charged-particle track-
ing needed to extend the physics reach of CMS at the HL-LHC, the objectives were to (i) search
for charged lepton flavor violation (LFV) in the top quark sector; (ii) measure the top quark–Higgs
boson interaction through boosted tt̄H(bb̄) production, including the charge-parity (CP) proper-
ties of the top quark Yukawa coupling; (iii) develop the hardware-based Level-1 (L1) track finding
system for the HL-LHC CMS upgrade; and (iv) develop novel L1 trigger algorithms that exploit
tracking information.
 The first objective produced two searches for charged LFV in top quark production and decay.
The first considered eµ interactions (t → eµq, q = u/c) in the trilepton final state, using the full
CMS Run-2 data set (139 fb−1 at 13 TeV) within an Effective Field Theory framework. Combining
data-driven nonprompt-background estimation with multivariate discrimination, it was released as
a CMS preliminary result [1] and published in Phys. Rev. D [2]. The data were consistent with the
Standard Model and set the strongest limits to date on the LFV branching fractions, improving
on prior constraints by about an order of magnitude; the work also corrected a bug in the signal
generator (SMEFTsim/SMeftFR v2) that benefited related CMS and ATLAS analyses. A second
search, extending to hadronically-decaying τ leptons and considering eµ, eτ , and µτ interactions
simultaneously – the first direct probe of eτ in this sector – is complete and in CMS review.
 The second objective, a measurement of boosted tt̄H(bb̄) production targeting the CP struc-
ture of the top quark Yukawa coupling, was deferred early in the award by the delayed start of
LHC Run-3 (the measurement targets Run-3 data). Within the period of performance the effort
produced preliminary, foundational work – analysis frameworks, modeling of the challenging tt̄ + bb̄
background, and boosted H → bb̄ identification using deep neural network taggers – but did not
reach a public measurement.
 The group made leading contributions to the hardware-based L1 track finding system for the
HL-LHC, which reconstructs charged-particle trajectories at the 40 MHz bunch-crossing rate as an
entirely new handle for the upgraded CMS trigger. Contributions spanned algorithm development
(Kalman Filter track fit optimization, track quality multivariate discriminators, and stub-to-seed
matching to reduce truncation), a new high-level synthesis (HLS) track finding simulation frame-
work, firmware implementation, and hardware integration tests at the CERN Tracker Integration
Facility. The group also developed a novel displaced vertex L1 trigger algorithm – a realistic, full
implementation documented as a CMS Detector Performance Summary [3] – opening new avenues
for triggering on long-lived particles at the HL-LHC.
 The PI held leading roles within CMS, serving as co-coordinator of the L1 Track Finding
(firmware and software) group and subsequently of Tracker Backend Systems. The award supported
three PhD students (one completing the PhD in December 2023) and a postdoctoral researcher, and
group members gained hands-on experience in CMS detector operations through shift and on-call
duties at CERN. Overall, the program advanced DOE HEP priorities by delivering precision tests
of the Standard Model in the top quark sector, contributing critically to the trigger capabilities
underpinning the HL-LHC physics program, and training early-career researchers in experimental
particle physics and scientific computing.

1. Introduction

The Standard Model of particle physics has been remarkably successful in explaining experimental
data, and has repeatedly held up to precision tests. Yet it is known to be incomplete: many key
questions about our universe remain unanswered, including the nature of dark matter, the observed
matter–antimatter asymmetry, and the origin of the large gap between the electroweak and Planck
scales (the hierarchy problem). The Large Hadron Collider (LHC) at CERN provides an extremely
powerful tool to address these questions, colliding protons at unprecedented energies and enabling
detailed tests of the Standard Model. The discovery of the Higgs boson answered one key question
but raised new ones about its nature and interactions, and with no striking discovery of new physics
so far, it is increasingly likely that physics beyond the Standard Model will manifest through small
deviations that only precision measurements can reveal.
 This award probed signs of physics beyond the Standard Model through precision tests in
the top quark and Higgs boson sectors – the heaviest known elementary particles, and therefore
uniquely sensitive probes of the interactions among fundamental particles. The research exploited
proton–proton collision data collected by the Compact Muon Solenoid (CMS) experiment during
LHC Run 2 (2016–2018, 139 fb−1 at 13 TeV) and early Run 3. In parallel, the program pursued
leading developments of fast charged-particle trajectory reconstruction (“tracking”) to maximize
the physics potential of the High-Luminosity LHC (HL-LHC) upgrade, contributing to both the
hardware-based track finding and its use in the downstream trigger system.

2. Background and Objectives

Scientific background
Within the Standard Model, charged lepton flavor is conserved in all interactions, so any sign
of charged lepton flavor violation (LFV) at the LHC would be a clear signature of new physics.
Tantalizing hints of LFV have emerged in B-meson decays, yet the top quark sector remains
poorly constrained – no dedicated CMS study existed at the start of the award. LFV in top
quark production and decay is therefore a promising venue for discovering or constraining beyond-
Standard-Model (BSM) effects, and is favorably interpreted within an Effective Field Theory (EFT)
framework that systematically parametrizes such effects through higher-dimensional operators.
 The top quark is the particle most strongly coupled to the Higgs boson, with a Yukawa coupling
yt of order unity, and therefore plays a central role in the hierarchy problem and in many models
of BSM physics. Because the decay H → tt̄ is kinematically forbidden, yt is accessed through
the associated production of a Higgs boson with a top quark pair (tt̄H). Studying this process
in greater detail probes possible deviations from the Standard Model, including the charge-parity
(CP) properties of the coupling, whose sensitivity is enhanced at high Higgs boson transverse
momentum – motivating the use of the boosted regime, where the H → bb̄ decay is reconstructed
with jet substructure techniques.
 The HL-LHC will deliver an order of magnitude more data (∼3000 fb−1 ) at the cost of ∼200
overlapping interactions per bunch crossing, conditions far beyond the original CMS design. Sus-
taining the trigger under these conditions requires an entirely new handle: the reconstruction of
charged-particle trajectories in the hardware-based Level-1 (L1) trigger at the 40 MHz bunch-
crossing rate. Beyond controlling event rates, L1 tracking can enable altogether new analyses,
notably searches for long-lived particles through displaced tracking.

Project objectives
The research program had four distinct yet connected objectives:

  (i) Probe lepton flavor violation in the top quark sector – a clear signature of new physics, not
 well constrained in this sector (initially eµ interactions, later extended to τ -lepton modes).

 (ii) Measure the top quark–Higgs boson interaction through tt̄H(bb̄) production in the boosted
 regime, including the CP properties of the top quark Yukawa coupling.

(iii) Develop the hardware-based L1 track finding for the CMS HL-LHC upgrade.

(iv) Explore new L1 trigger algorithms that exploit tracking information, in particular displaced
 tracking to target long-lived particles.

3. Accomplishments

3.1 Lepton flavor violation in the top quark sector
The first objective produced a search for charged LFV in top quark production and decay, consid-
ering the coupling tqeµ with quark flavor q = u/c. Both LFV in top quark production (single top
produced together with an e± µ∓ pair) and in top quark decay (in tt̄ events, one top decaying via
an LFV interaction) were considered, and the analysis used the trilepton final state, in which the
SM top decays leptonically. This final state – an opposite-charge eµ pair from the LFV vertex, a
third charged lepton, a b-tagged jet, and missing transverse momentum – was chosen because it
strongly suppresses background relative to the hadronic final state. The measurement used the full
CMS Run 2 data set (139 fb−1 at 13 TeV) and was interpreted within an EFT framework using
dimension-six operators, treating the production and decay scenarios separately.
 The dominant prompt backgrounds (tt̄W/Z and diboson production) were modeled from sim-
ulation validated in data control regions, while the more challenging nonprompt background was
estimated directly from data using a novel three-dimensional data-driven method, leveraging the
PI’s prior expertise in nonprompt background estimation. Signal was separated from background
using a multivariate discriminant. Over the first two years of the award, graduate student J. Li and
postdoc A. Parker built the analysis framework, background estimates, and discriminant, developed
MVA-based lepton identification, and evaluated the systematic uncertainties and limit-setting pro-
cedure. No excess consistent with a signal was observed, and the analysis placed limits improving
on prior constraints by about an order of magnitude. It was released as a CMS preliminary result [1]
– presented at Moriond EW 2023 and LHCP 2023 – and published in Phys. Rev. D [2], forming
a major part of Li’s PhD thesis (defended December 2023). During this work the team identified
and corrected a bug in the signal generator (SMEFTsim/SMeftFR v2), producing corrected signal
models that also benefited related CMS and ATLAS analyses.
 Building on this result, the analysis was extended in the later part of the award to LFV involving
hadronically-decaying τ leptons, searching the eµ, eτ , and µτ flavor-mixing modes simultaneously –
including the first direct probe of eτ interactions in the top sector. Li and graduate student E. Tsai
developed the signal modeling, optimized a DNN-based multi-classification τ -lepton identification,
and estimated the difficult data-driven “fake” τ background, then evaluated BDT- and DNN-based
discriminants and the systematic uncertainties. The analysis is complete and in CMS internal
review.

3.2 Top quark–Higgs boson interaction
The second objective was a measurement of the top quark Yukawa coupling through tt̄H production,
in the final state where the Higgs boson decays to bb̄ and both top quarks decay leptonically. The
analysis targets the boosted regime, where the high-pT Higgs boson decay is reconstructed as a
single large-radius jet and jet substructure techniques help separate the signal from the dominant,
otherwise irreducible tt̄bb̄ background; this regime also carries enhanced sensitivity to non-SM
effects, including the CP properties of the coupling. Deep neural network taggers are used to
identify the boosted H → bb̄ decay.
 This objective was deferred early in the award by the delayed start of LHC Run 3 as the
measurement targeted using the full Run 3 data, with effort redirected to the HL-LHC upgrade
tasks in the interim, as well as the second LFV analysis with hadronically-decaying τ leptons. From
the third year onward, graduate student R. McCarthy (with A. Parker) developed the analysis
framework, studied and modeled the challenging tt̄bb̄ background, and applied the DNN-based
boosted H → bb̄ tagging. Within the period of performance this produced preliminary, foundational
work but did not reach a public measurement.

3.3 L1 track finding for the HL-LHC
The group made leading contributions to the hardware-based L1 track finding system, which re-
constructs charged-particle trajectories (pT > 2 GeV, z0 to ∼1 mm) from outer-tracker stubs at
the 40 MHz bunch-crossing rate. The algorithm forms track seeds (tracklets) from stub pairs,
projects them to build road-based track candidates, removes duplicates, and fits the candidates
with a Kalman Filter, implemented in FPGAs on the ATCA-based Apollo platform with the outer
tracker divided into nine ϕ sectors and a time-multiplex factor of 18.
 Across the award, group contributions spanned algorithm development, performance studies,
software and firmware development, and hardware testing. Specific developments included studies
to minimize truncation, optimization of the Kalman Filter track fit to improve efficiency (e.g. for
electrons), development of multivariate track quality discriminators, and studies of the impact of
detector degradation. The group became a core contributor to a new CMS-wide framework that
integrates HLS algorithm “processing blocks” into the CMS software and simulation chain, writing
the Tracklet Processor producer, Input Router module, and associated analyzer code, and later Stub
Router and Projection Calculator modules with work to reduce truncation. On the hardware side,
the group wrote firmware to implement the algorithm and conducted thermal and link tests of the
Apollo boards at the CERN Tracker Integration Facility. The PI co-coordinated the CMS L1 Track
Finding (firmware and software) group through 2023 and subsequently became co-coordinator of
Tracker Backend Systems, encompassing hardware, firmware, and software for the upgraded inner
and outer tracker readout systems including L1 tracking (>400 boards in total), as well as module
test systems for assembly quality assurance.

3.4 New L1 trigger algorithms
Beyond developing L1 tracking, the group developed algorithms that use the tracks in the down-
stream L1 trigger, building on the PI’s expertise in hadronic object triggers and earlier group work
improving electron identification. As the L1 trigger project matured, the group’s effort concen-
trated within the Global Track Trigger (GTT) on a displaced vertex algorithm designed to identify
exotic long-lived particles – reconstructing displacements transverse to the beamline over a wide

4. Changes / problems

The measurement of boosted tt̄H(bb̄) production (Sec. 3.2) was affected by the delayed start of LHC
Run 3. Because the analysis targets Run 3 data, its start was pushed later than originally planned,
and group effort was redirected to the HL-LHC upgrade tasks in the interim. The measurement is
challenging in its own right – in particular the modeling of the dominant, irreducible tt̄bb̄ background
– and within the period of performance the effort produced preliminary, foundational work (analysis
frameworks, background modeling, and boosted H → bb̄ identification) but did not reach a public
measurement.
 A no-cost extension carried the period of performance from its original end date of August 31,
2025 through December 1, 2025.

5. Personnel and training

The award supported the training of graduate students and a postdoctoral researcher at North-
eastern University, alongside undergraduate researchers funded through other sources. Beyond
their research contributions, group members took on significant leadership roles within the CMS
Collaboration.
 Postdoctoral researcher Dr. Ashley Parker played a leading role in the top quark LFV analysis
and in the operation of the CMS tracker. She was convener for the CMS Top Mass and Properties
group during 2022–2023 (an L3 position), responsible for the coordination and review of all asso-
ciated analyses, having previously been the contact for jet and missing-energy objects within the
CMS Top group (2020–2022). She also served on CMS analysis review committees and initiated
the “Top Student Sessions,” a forum for students and young postdocs to share knowledge through
an informal communication channel. Dr. Parker left the group in 2024 for a career in industry.
 Graduate student Jack Li built much of the LFV analysis and defended his PhD in December
2023. As a temporary postdoc in the group he served as convener for the CMS Top ttX group
(an L3 position) responsible for measurements involving pairs of top quarks, helping to shape and
implement the CMS top physics program; he is now a postdoctoral researcher at Purdue University.
Graduate student Ryan McCarthy carried the boosted tt̄H(bb̄) analysis and the displaced-vertex
trigger development; graduate student Emily Tsai the τ -lepton LFV extension and secondary-vertex
reconstruction studies. McCarthy is expected to graduate in 2026 and Tsai in 2027. Since 2024, Tsai
has been convener for the Performance subgroup within the CMS b-Tagging and Vertexing (BTV)
Physics Object Group (an L3 position), overseeing development, calibration, and commissioning
studies related to secondary vertexing and jet flavor tagging, having previously been the BTV
contact for the CMS Top group; these roles are building both her physics and management skills
and her recognition within the collaboration.

6. Conclusions

This Early Career Award advanced precision tests of the Standard Model in the top quark and Higgs
boson sectors while establishing leading contributions to the trigger developments that underpin
the HL-LHC physics program. The award’s central physics result, a search for charged lepton flavor
violation in top quark production and decay, set the strongest limits to date on these interactions
and was published in Phys. Rev. D; it was subsequently extended to a second search involving
τ leptons, including the first direct probe of eτ interactions in this sector. The measurement of
boosted tt̄H(bb̄) production, delayed by the late start of Run 3, produced the analysis foundations
toward characterizing the top quark Yukawa coupling. On the detector side, the group made
leading contributions to the hardware-based L1 track finding for the CMS HL-LHC upgrade and
developed a novel displaced-vertex L1 trigger algorithm that opens new avenues for long-lived-
particle searches. Together with the training of graduate students and a postdoctoral researcher,
these outcomes advanced key DOE HEP priorities and position the group to exploit the full Run 3
dataset and the HL-LHC to come.
