Public Executive Summary

This project advanced the development of a hybrid electrochemical and catalytic system to
produce and compress hydrogen to very high pressures, up to 700 bar. By combining hydrogen
generation with compression in a single integrated process, the project addressed one of the
major cost and efficiency challenges in the hydrogen economy.

1) Contribution to understanding:
The research deepened understanding of how electrochemical reactions and catalytic
processes can be coupled to achieve both hydrogen generation and compression in one system.
The team demonstrated how new catalyst materials, such as molybdenum carbide, and
innovative system designs can improve durability and efficiency under high-pressure
conditions. These insights contribute to the broader field of hydrogen energy conversion by
showing viable alternatives to traditional mechanical compression.

2) Technical effectiveness and economic feasibility:
Laboratory-scale experiments confirmed that the hybrid approach can operate efficiently at
practical voltages and deliver hydrogen at pressures suitable for storage and transportation.
The system achieved high efficiency in small cells, was scaled up to larger multi-cell stacks, and
successfully demonstrated hydrogen compression to 350 bar, establishing a pathway toward
700 bar. A techno-economic analysis estimated that the combined cost of hydrogen production
and compression could approach $2 per kilogram, aligning with Department of Energy cost
targets for clean hydrogen. This demonstrates that the approach has potential to be both
technically effective and economically competitive.

3) Public benefit:
If deployed, this technology could make clean hydrogen more affordable and widely available,
supporting national goals for decarbonizing transportation, industry, and power systems. By
reducing the need for large, costly mechanical compressors, the technology offers a pathway
toward simpler, lower-cost infrastructure for fueling hydrogen vehicles and storing renewable
energy. More broadly, the project contributes to U.S. leadership in clean energy innovation and
supports the transition to a sustainable, low-carbon economy.

Project Activities
The focus of this project was to develop and validate a hybrid electrochemical and catalytic
system capable of both generating and compressing hydrogen to high pressures, providing a
cost-effective alternative to conventional production and mechanical compression. The
approach combined electrochemical hydrogen generation using vanadium redox couples with
catalytic hydrogen recombination for self-pressurization, supported by the development of
durable molybdenum carbide catalysts and the scale-up of cell hardware from 10 cm2 to 100
cm2 single cells and multi-cell stacks. The underlying hypothesis was that integrating hydrogen
production and compression into a single system would significantly reduce energy
consumption and infrastructure cost while maintaining high efficiency and durability. The
project achieved key results including demonstration of 10 cm2 hybrid cells at <2 V and >97%
efficiency, stable 20-hour operation with no measurable degradation, successful scale-up to
100 cm2 cells and a 3-cell stack, and repeated compression to 350 bar, with techno-economic
analysis projecting a combined cost of ~$2/kg-H2
