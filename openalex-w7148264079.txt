Abstract

42
43
44

Background: The prevalence of heart failure (HF) is increasing worldwide, and

45

rehospitalizations due to exacerbations remain a major clinical and economic burden.

46

Beyond medical triggers, insufficient patient understanding and inadequate self-

47

management often contribute to recurrent admissions. The Kurume-HEARTS program

48

was developed to provide regular planned hospitalizations incorporating structured

49

education, cardiac rehabilitation, and medication adjustment for patients with recurrent

50

HF.

51

Objective: To retrospectively evaluate the clinical and economic impact of the Kurume-

52

HEARTS program.

53

Methods: We enrolled consecutive patients with recurrent HF hospitalizations who

54

underwent the program at Kurume University Hospital between January 2020 and

55

October 2025. Outcomes compared planned versus unplanned hospitalizations within the

56

same patients. Co-primary endpoints were total hospitalization cost and total length of

57

stay per person-year. Secondary endpoints included per-hospitalization cost, length of

58

stay, unplanned and planned admission frequency, and NT-proBNP levels at admission.

59

Results: Of 31 screened patients, 20 with recurrent heart failure were included. During a

61

62

the Kurume-HEARTS program than during unplanned hospitalizations, while length of

63

64

65

Introduction

97
98

Heart failure (HF) affects more than 64 million individuals worldwide, and

99

hospitalizations for decompensated HF contribute substantially to morbidity, mortality,

100

and health care costs.[1, 2] In the United States alone, HF accounts for more than $30

101

billion in annual health care expenditures,[3] with inpatient care representing the largest

102

component, primarily driven by comorbidities, invasive procedures, and readmissions.[4]

103

Despite advances in pharmacologic and non-pharmacologic therapies that

104

improve survival in chronic HF,[5-9] acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) remains

105

one of the leading causes of hospitalization worldwide. Patients admitted with ADHF

106

experience significant in-hospital morbidity and mortality, frequent rehospitalizations,

107

and an elevated risk of subsequent cardiovascular death.[10] In the United States and

108

Europe, there are more than 1 million hospitalizations for HF annually, with

109

rehospitalization rates approaching one quarter of patients within 30 days.[11-14] Beyond

110

their prognostic implications, rehospitalizations impose a disproportionate economic

111

burden, with each recurrent admission adding substantially to cumulative costs.

112

Rehospitalization for cardiovascular disease within 90 days of discharge is also

113

independently associated with increased mortality risk, irrespective of the precise interval

Effective inpatient care requires comprehensive diagnostic evaluation, triage

117

118

adequate decongestion, and structured discharge planning.[2, 17] In addition, patients

119

120

121

122

123

124

125

126

127

128

Statistical Analysis

242

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median and

243

interquartile range (IQR). The primary analysis was a paired, within-patient comparison

244

of outcomes between unplanned hospitalizations and admissions for the Kurume-

245

HEARTS program. All paired comparisons were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-

246

rank test. For the primary endpoints, the observation period for each patient was

247

calculated from their first admission date to their last discharge date. The total cost and

248

total length of stay for each hospitalization type were then annualized by dividing by

249

this observation period (in years) to yield cost per person-year and length of stay per

250

person-year, respectively. The effect size for these primary endpoints is reported as the

251

Hodges-Lehmann median of the within-patient differences (Unplanned hospitalizations

252

− Kurume-HEARTS program) with its 95% confidence interval (CI).

253

For the secondary endpoints, per-hospitalization cost was summarized as the

254

patient-level geometric mean for each hospitalization type, while per-hospitalization

255

length of stay was computed as the patient-level median. The effect sizes for both

256

endpoints are reported as the Hodges-Lehmann median of the within-patient

257

differences. NT-proBNP was summarized as the patient-level geometric mean;

medRxiv preprint doi: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.01.26349992; this version posted April 2, 2026. The copyright holder for this preprint

however, the comparison was performed on the log-transformed values to derive the

geometric mean ratio (GMR = Unplanned hospitalizations / Kurume-HEARTS

260

program) and its 95% CI. The number of admissions was annualized to derive

261

327

as follows. First, the Kurume-HEARTS program was associated with significantly lower

336

337

these findings suggest that a structured planned hospitalization program for patients with

338

advanced HF may improve the efficiency of inpatient care and reduce the clinical and

339

Unplanned hospitalizations are a principal driver of resource use and costs in HF, which

343

is the leading cause of hospitalization among older adults in the United States.[27] Early

344

post-discharge is a particularly vulnerable period; contemporary cohorts report ≈30% risk

345

of rehospitalization or all-cause death within 6 months of an HF admission.[28, 29]

346

Economic evaluations (e.g., REHAB-HF) highlight that most direct costs (>75%) accrue

347

to inpatient care, and that even when interventions improve function and QoL, inpatient

348

expenditures remain substantial.[30-32]. Our findings mirror this clinical reality: patients

349

admitted emergently typically present with advanced congestion and organ dysfunction,

350

necessitating intensive treatment, prolonged monitoring, and complex management, all

351

of which lengthen stays and inflate costs.

352
353

Value of Planned Hospitalizations of Kurume-HEARTS program

354

This study demonstrated that a planned, structured hospitalization strategy may provide

355

an additional framework for optimizing care in patients with recurrent HF.

356

Multidisciplinary HF disease management and cardiac rehabilitation programs have

357

consistently been shown to reduce HF readmissions and improve functional capacity and

358

QoL through comprehensive interventions including medication optimization, patient

359

education, exercise training, and coordinated follow-up. However, many patients with

361

362

approach in which hospitalization itself is used proactively as a platform for clinical

363

stabilization, reassessment of hemodynamics and volume status, and systematic

364

optimization of GDMT.

365

By scheduling hospitalizations proactively, multidisciplinary teams can titrate

366

therapies, reinforce self-care behaviors, and address emerging clinical issues before

367

severe decompensation occurs. In our cohort, planned hospitalizations were consistently

368

associated with lower per-admission costs and shorter lengths of stay compared with

369

370

the number of hospital encounters, their lower intensity and shorter duration appeared to

371

372

373

374

375

376

characteristics. Currently, the program duration is typically set at 5–10 days, but defining

377

Integration Into Heart Failure Programs

385

Planned hospitalizations for HF education and rehabilitation should be considered within

386

broader multidisciplinary programs that combine education, dietary and exercise

387

counseling, medication titration, and transitions-of-care support. Evidence from

388

telerehabilitation (e.g., TELEREH-HF) suggests improvements in functional status and

389

QoL, though durable reductions in hard outcomes may require sustained, structured

390

follow-up beyond short-term programs.[33-36]. At the same time, the TELEREH-HF

391

study demonstrates that the improvements achieved during the 9-week hybrid

392

comprehensive telerehabilitation do not increase the percentage of days alive and out of

393

the hospital or reduce mortality or hospitalization rates on longer-term follow-up (after

394

the intervention was stopped).[33] Education-centered, multidisciplinary strategies—

395

Policy and Resource Allocation

401

From a health policy perspective, recognizing the differential impact of planned and

402

unplanned hospitalizations has implications for resource allocation. Investing in planned

403

hospitalizations and structured programs may prove cost-effective by reducing the need

404

for resource-intensive unplanned hospitalizations. Reimbursement models should

405

consider supporting planned hospitalizations for HF education and rehabilitation as part

406

of standard HF management to optimize long-term cost savings and patient outcomes.

Limitations and Future Directions

417

First, this was a single-center, retrospective study with a small cohort (n=20), which limits

418

generalizability and precludes robust risk-adjusted modeling. Further, differences in

419

reimbursement systems across countries should also be taken into account. Also, selection

420

bias and residual confounding (e.g., socioeconomic factors, outpatient adherence,

421

community resources) cannot be excluded. Second, the content and structure of the

422

Kurume-HEARTS program are not yet standardized and are currently being implemented

423

in an exploratory manner. Third, we did not assess long-term outcomes such as all-cause

424

mortality, cardiovascular mortality, or health-related QoL. To address these limitations,

425

we have initiated prospective, multicenter studies.
