 ABSTRACT
 The Pacific-Ocean Neutrino Experiment (P-ONE) is an upcoming large-volume neutrino telescope
 planned to be built off the coast of Vancouver Island, Canada. It aims to detect high-energy neutrinos
 via the Cherenkov radiation produced in water by secondary particles born from high-energy neutrino
 interactions. Its first set of detectors is planned to be commissioned this year. However, since the
 telescope is still in its early stages of development, many tests and simulations must still be run to
 characterize the efficacy of the detectors. One necessary simulation must be devoted towards examining
 whether the polarization of the incoming Cherenkov light impacts the strength of the signals by the
 PMTs, and it must be supported by experimental data. In this report, I detail what I have completed
 during my SULI internship at the Los Alamos National Lab (LANL); I present the preliminary results
 of a Geant4 polarization simulation as well as preliminary laboratory measurements that will later be
 used to inform the future P-ONE design.
