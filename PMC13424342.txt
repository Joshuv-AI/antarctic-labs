Venetoclax, azacitidine, and G-CSF targeting molecular residual disease in CBFB::MYH11 AML resistant to high-dose cytarabine
Authors: Ting Shi, Ruicong Xue, Jing Jia, Menghan Liu, Yalan Zhou, Chuanying Geng, Yuan Jian, Ying Tian, Yanru Zhang, Wenjing Li, Lefu Huang, Huixing Zhou, Aijun Liu, Wen Gao, Wenming Chen, Yun Leng, Yin Wu, Hong-Hu Zhu
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) with the CBFB::MYH11 rearrangement is generally considered a favorable-risk subtype highly responsive to intensive cytarabine-based chemotherapy [1]. However, up to 30% of patients ultimately relapse, driven largely by persistent measurable residual disease (MRD) [1]. Patients failing to achieve deep molecular responses during early consolidation therapy, typically defined as a >3-log reduction in transcript levels or an absolute threshold below 0.1%, face a significantly elevated risk of relapse [2, 3]. While allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT) can effectively overcome this adverse prognosis [4], its widespread application is frequently precluded by patient comorbidities and donor availability. This underscores an urgent clinical need for effective non-transplant regimens capable of eradicating MRD.

Emerging data suggest that hypomethylating agents (HMAs), particularly when combined with the BCL-2 inhibitor venetoclax, can deepen responses and prolong remission in CBFB::MYH11 AML [5–8], with preclinical evidence of elevated BCL-2 expression in CBFB::MYH11 AML providing further mechanistic rationale for this approach [9, 10]. However, these prior studies were limited by small sample sizes, and the molecular response rates leave considerable room for improvement. We previously developed the VAG regimen by incorporating granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) into the venetoclax-azacitidine backbone, which improved the composite complete remission (CRc) rate from 43.4% with VA to 81.6% in unfit AML patients [11]. Building on these findings, we conducted the present retrospective study to evaluate the efficacy of the VAG regimen in eradicating MRD in patients with CBFB::MYH11 AML who remain refractory to intensive consolidation therapy, with the aim of establishing a robust non-transplant salvage strategy for this population.

We conducted a retrospective analysis of consecutive patients aged over 14 years with CBFB::MYH11 AML treated at Beijing Chaoyang Hospital between January 2023 and October 2025. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Chaoyang Hospital and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Eligible patients had persistent MRD or molecular relapse following consolidation therapy. Patients who developed hematologic relapse prior to VAG therapy, did not receive VAG therapy, or lacked complete baseline medical records were excluded. Regardless of treatment intensity, MRD persistence was defined as a CBFB::MYH11/ABL1 ratio ≥0.01% after two cycles of consolidation therapy; molecular relapse as conversion from MRD negativity to positivity or a ≥1 log increase in transcript levels, confirmed in 2 consecutive samples; and hematologic relapse as bone marrow blasts ≥ 5%, reappearance of circulating blasts in at least two peripheral blood samples at least one week apart, or new extramedullary disease. The VAG regimen consisted of venetoclax (400 mg/day, days 1–21, with appropriate dose adjustments for concurrent CYP3A inhibitors), azacitidine (75 mg/m²/day, days 1–7), and G-CSF (5 μg/kg/day, days 1–7). Cycles were administered every 4–5 weeks. Once a molecular response was achieved, patients then received 2–3 cycles of VAG regimen.

The primary endpoint was the molecular response rate, defined as either a ≥0.5 log reduction in CBFB::MYH11 transcript levels or achievement of MRD negativity (transcript levels below the qPCR detection limit). Secondary endpoints included event-free survival (EFS) and overall survival (OS). EFS was measured from the initiation of VAG therapy to the first occurrence of hematologic relapse, allo-HSCT, or death from any cause. Pre- and post-treatment CBFB::MYH11 transcript levels were compared using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, and survival outcomes were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method. The data cutoff was January 30, 2026.

A total of 33 CBFB::MYH11 AML patients were reviewed, of whom 27 were eligible and included in this study (Fig. S1). Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table S1. The cohort comprised 10 (37.0%) males and 17 (63.0%) females, with a mean age of 39 years (range, 15–61). At diagnosis, the most frequent mutations were NRAS (48.1%), KIT (44.4%), KRAS (25.9%), and FLT3 (25.9%). All patients achieved CRc after one or two cycles of induction chemotherapy. The induction regimen consisted of the “7 + 3” regimen (n = 20, 74.1%), the HAA regimen (homoharringtonine, cytarabine, and aclarubicin; n = 5, 18.5%), or a less intensive regimen for patients with significant comorbidities (n = 2, 7.4%). Of the 25 patients who were fit for intensive consolidation, all received high-dose cytarabine (HiDAC)-based therapy. Prior to VAG therapy, patients had received a median of 5 treatment cycles (range, 2–9) over a median of 7.5 months from initial diagnosis (range, 2.6–27.8). Despite these treatments, 20 patients (74.1%) were enrolled for MRD persistence and 7 (25.9%) for molecular relapse.

Among the 26 evaluable patients, the median CBFB::MYH11 transcript level decreased from 0.120 to 0.0275% (P = 0.0018) after the first cycle of VAG treatment (Fig. 1A), with a median reduction of 0.6 log (Fig. 1C). Following one to three cycles of VAG treatment, 18 of 26 evaluable patients (69.2%) achieved a molecular response, including 13 (50.0%) who attained MRD negativity and 5 (19.2%) who achieved a greater than 0.5 log reduction in CBFB::MYH11 transcript levels. The remaining 8 patients (30.8%) did not respond to VAG and were switched to alternative regimens, including CAG, HAA, and other therapies (Fig. 1B). In the MRD relapse cohort (n = 7), 6 patients (85.7%) achieved a molecular response (MRD negativity, n = 5; >0.5 log reduction, n = 1). At a median follow-up of 7.2 months, no deaths had occurred, yielding an estimated 2-year OS of 100% (Fig. 2A). The median EFS had not yet been reached, with an estimated 2-year EFS of 71.1% (95% CI, 45.4–86.6%) (Fig. 2B). Among the 18 patients who achieved a molecular response, 7 (38.9%) remained in sustained molecular remission at a median remission duration of 7.0 months. Ten patients (55.6%) subsequently experienced molecular relapse after a median MRD-negative interval of 4.6 months, of whom 5 (50.0%) proceeded to HSCT and the remaining 5 patients received alternative salvage regimens (Fig. S2). Hematologic toxicity data were available for 17 patients, as the remainder underwent follow-up monitoring at community-based facilities. The most common grade ≥3 hematologic adverse events were neutropenia (15/17, 88.2%), thrombocytopenia (8/17, 47.1%), and anemia (5/17, 29.4%). Non-hematologic adverse events were recorded only during the inpatient period of azacitidine administration, as patients self-administered venetoclax on an outpatient basis thereafter without systematic toxicity monitoring. Within this observation window, the most frequently recorded non-hematologic events were hypokalemia (8/27, 29.6%), nausea (4/27, 14.8%), and fatigue (2/27, 7.4%). No tumor lysis syndrome was documented.

CBFB::MYH11 AML, despite its favorable-risk classification, is associated with MRD persistence or molecular relapse in approximately 30% of patients following HiDAC-based consolidation, conferring a substantially elevated risk of hematologic relapse [1]. HSCT remains the most established approach for MRD eradication in this setting [4]. However, many patients are ineligible for or decline transplantation, and effective non-transplant salvage strategies remain incompletely defined. Several small-cohort studies have explored this unmet need. Our group previously reported that the CAG regimen induced molecular responses in approximately 52% of CBF-AML patients with MRD persistence [12]. HMAs have also demonstrated activity in this setting, with complete molecular remission rates of approximately 52% reported across several studies [5, 6]. More recently, the combination of venetoclax and azacitidine has been evaluated in small cohorts, with complete molecular remission rates ranging from 46 to 50% [7, 8]. Nonetheless, molecular response rates remain to be further improved. In this study, one to three cycles of the VAG regimen achieved an overall molecular response in 69.2% of patients, with 50% attaining MRD negative. To the best of our knowledge, this represents the largest reported cohort to date specifically evaluating a non-transplant regimen in this molecular subtype. Notably, MRD negativity in our study was defined using a more stringent threshold of <0.001%, compared with the 0.01% cutoff adopted in most prior reports, suggesting that the VAG regimen confers superior MRD clearance relative to previously described treatment approaches.

We hypothesize that the efficacy of the VAG regimen in eradicating MRD in CBFB::MYH11 AML is attributable to multiple synergistic mechanisms. First, the high expression of BCL-2 in CBFB::MYH11 AML confers a heightened sensitivity to venetoclax, a finding consistently validated in both cell lines and patient derived primary blasts [9, 10]. Second, the CBFβ-SMMHC fusion protein induces hypermethylation at the promoters of critical RUNX1 target genes, providing a pharmacological rationale for the incorporation of HMAs [13]. Third, G-CSF potentiates the elimination of leukemic stem cells (LSCs) by priming quiescent LSCs into the cell cycle, thereby enhancing chemo-sensitivity [14]. Notably, our recent work suggests that the integration of G-CSF into the VA backbone can effectively reverse the immunosuppressive bone marrow microenvironment [15]. This modulation drives the expansion and activation of immune effector cells, restoring anti-leukemic immunosurveillance.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. This retrospective, single-center study had a modest sample size, relatively short follow-up, and potential selection bias. Mutational profiling at MRD persistence or molecular relapse was not routinely available. Larger prospective multicenter studies are warranted to validate these findings.

In conclusion, the VAG regimen effectively eradicates MRD and exhibits a survival advantage in CBFB::MYH11 AML patients with persistent or recurrent MRD, offering a viable alternative for transplant-ineligible individuals.
