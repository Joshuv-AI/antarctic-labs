Diastereodivergent
Access to cis,cis- and cis,trans-Angular
Triquinanes: Total Synthesis of (±)-Arnicenone

Chia-Hao Liu, Hung-Tse Huang, Han-Jung Li, Shubham A. Dighe, Deng-Lian Hou, Li-Yun Wu, Yu-Wen Huang

Abstract

Angular triquinanes constitute an important class of
polycyclic
natural products characterized by compact, angularly fused ring systems
and multiple contiguous stereocenters. Herein, we report a diastereodivergent
strategy for stereocontrolled access to both cis,trans- and cis,cis-angular
triquinanes from common allene precursors. The cis,trans-fused triquinanes were constructed through
a Lewis acid-promoted intramolecular [3+2] Danheiser annulation, whereas
the complementary cis,cis frameworks
were accessed via photochemical [2+2] cycloaddition followed by oxidative
ring expansion. Notably, substitution at the R1 position
of the allene precursor reversed the diastereoselectivity of the [3+2]
annulation, providing direct access to a cis,cis-angular triquinane. DFT calculations indicate that the
initial spirocyclization governs both the reactivity and stereochemical
outcome of the [3+2] pathway, with substrate-dependent steric interactions
accounting for the observed diastereodivergence. The synthetic utility
of this strategy was demonstrated through the total synthesis of (±)-arnicenone
via the [2+2] cycloaddition/oxidative ring-expansion pathway, while
an alternative synthesis from the cis,cis-[3+2] Danheiser annulation product further highlights the complementary
nature of the two approaches. Together, these results establish a
unified platform for stereodivergent access to angular triquinanes
and their application to complex natural product synthesis.

Angular triquinanes
−

 (1 and 2) constitute a prominent structural
motif in natural products, particularly within terpenoid and sesquiterpenoid
families ().
Their compact, angularly fused tricyclic frameworkcomprising
three contiguous five-membered ringsfeatures multiple stereogenic
centers and significant conformational constraint. This dense stereochemical
architecture renders angular triquinanes both synthetically challenging
and biologically intriguing. Among these, arnicenone
−

 (3) exemplifies the structural complexity associated
with this class of molecules. Other representative members include
isocomene
,
 (4), pentalenene
,
 (5), retigeranic acid
,
 (6), and waihoensene
,
 (7), which collectively
illustrate the structural diversity of angular triquinane natural
products. These molecules differ in their oxidation states, substitution
patterns, and ring fusion architectures, yet all possess highly congested
polycyclic frameworks featuring multiple contiguous stereogenic centers.
Their remarkable structural complexity has inspired the development
of numerous synthetic strategies and continues to serve as an important
driving force in angular triquinane synthesis. Considerable efforts
have been devoted to the construction of cis,cis-triquinane skeletons (1). Representative
strategies include assembly of the tricyclic core from preformed diquinane
intermediates, the Pauson–Khand
reaction (a),
−

 ring expansion of cyclobutane derivatives (10) (b), and various pericyclic transformations such as Nazarov
reaction (c).
,

More broadly, the construction of highly congested
and stereochemically
constrained molecular architectures remains a longstanding challenge
in synthetic chemistry, as exemplified by trans-fused
5,5 ring systems, strained bridged polycycles,
and atropisomeric systems.
−

 Within this context, angular triquinanes present a distinct synthetic
challenge because their compact polycyclic frameworks require precise
control over multiple stereogenic centers and ring-junction geometries.
While naturally occurring angular triquinanes predominantly exhibit cis,cis ring-junction stereochemistry,
the corresponding cis,trans frameworks
remain largely unexplored. Despite recent advances in the construction
of cis,cis-angular triquinane skeletons,
methods enabling diastereodivergent
−

 access to both stereochemical
manifolds from closely related precursors remain limited. Such strategies
are highly desirable because they would provide access not only to
naturally occurring frameworks but also to complementary stereochemical
space beyond that represented in nature.

In 1981, Danheiser
and co-workers reported the first intermolecular
[3+2] annulation of allenylsilanes with alkenes, which has since emerged
as a useful strategy for the construction of cis-fused
5/5 ring systems.
−

 More than four decades later, Li and co-workers developed the first
intramolecular variant of this transformation, providing an efficient
approach to trans-fused 5/5 ring systems and demonstrating
its synthetic utility in the synthesis of β-funebrene (d). Li and co-workers subsequently extended this strategy to
a Type II intramolecular [3+2] annulation, further broadening the
synthetic scope of this transformation and enabling its application
to the synthesis of strepsesquitriol (e). The synthetic
utility of the Danheiser annulation was further demonstrated by Zhai
and co-workers in their total synthesis of (−)-conidiogenone,
in which the annulation was employed to construct a 5,5,5-cis,cis-angular tricyclic framework. Inspired by these advances, we envisioned that
the intramolecular [3+2] Danheiser annulation could provide stereocontrolled
access to angular triquinanes with distinct ring-junction stereochemistries.
Specifically, cis,trans-angular
triquinanes (19) were targeted via a [3+2] Danheiser
annulation strategy from allenic precursor (18), leveraging
its efficiency in constructing five-membered carbocycles with well-defined
stereochemical outcomes (f). In contrast, cis,cis-angular triquinanes (21) were designed to arise from
a [2+2] cycloaddition followed by oxidative ring expansion,
−

 offering a mechanistically orthogonal pathway to the same carbon
framework. Herein, we report a diastereodivergent strategy for the
synthesis of angular triquinanes that enables stereocontrolled access
to both cis,trans- and cis,cis-fused systems. The synthetic utility of this
approach is further demonstrated through its application to the total
synthesis of (±)-arnicenone (3).

A series
of different allenes were prepared as outlined in . The diketone intermediates 24(a-d) were obtained efficiently via Stork-Danheiser
transposition with Grignard (23) and sequential acidic workup in 71–82% yield. These diketones 24(a-d) then underwent regioselective acetylide
addition and acylation to deliver 25(a-k) in 29–79% yield. The stage was set for subsequent SN2′ allenation proceeding cleanly to afford the desired
allenes 26(a-n) in 57–91% yield,
establishing unified intermediates poised for divergent cyclization.

[3+2] Danheiser Annulation

With the allenes in hand,
we first examined the intramolecular [3+2] Danheiser annulation (). Optimization of the Lewis acid, solvent, and reaction temperature
identified conditions that efficiently furnished the angular triquinane
products with generally high diastereoselectivity. Substrates bearing
R1 = H readily underwent the annulation to afford the corresponding
angular triquinanes 28(a–l), generally
favoring the cis,trans-diastereomer.
The cis,cis/cis,trans ratios ranged from 1:2 to exclusive formation
of the cis,trans-isomer. Notably,
the cyclohexenone-derived substrates 28(j–l) furnished the cis,trans-isomer
exclusively. Variation at R2 was well tolerated, with substrates
bearing R2 = H (28(g–i)) providing the desired products in moderate to good yields (45–82%).
In contrast, increasing the steric demand at R3 resulted
in diminished reactivity. Substrates bearing R3 = Et (28f and 28l) preferentially afforded the cis,trans-isomer, albeit in lower yield
(33% for 28l), whereas further increasing the size of
R3 to n-Bu or i-Pr completely
suppressed the desired [3+2] annulation under the optimized conditions.
The silyl substituent was also varied to examine its influence on
the annulation, with TMS, TES, TBS, TIPS, and DMPS groups being evaluated.
In addition, an H,H-substituted allene lacking a silyl group and an
H,DMPS-substituted allene were examined, but neither underwent the
desired [3+2] annulation under the optimized conditions. The lack
of reactivity of the nonsilylated allene is consistent with the proposed
Sakurai-type cyclization and the stabilizing β-silyl effect,
whereas the failure of the H,DMPS-substituted substrate suggests that
an appropriate allene substitution pattern is also required for efficient
cyclization. The practicality of the [3+2] Danheiser annulation was
further demonstrated by a gram-scale reaction, which furnished 28a in 58% yield (see Supporting Information for experimental details). Interestingly, introduction of a methyl
substituent at R1 markedly altered both the reactivity
and stereochemical outcome of the annulation. Substrates bearing R1 = Me exhibited substantially reduced reactivity. Nevertheless,
the cyclopentenone-derived substrate underwent cyclization to afford 28m in 10% yield with reversed diastereoselectivity, favoring
the cis,cis-isomer (cis,cis/cis,trans = 4:1). Desilylation of the allene precursor to give 29 was observed as the predominant competing pathway for these less
reactive substrates. The substrate-dependent reversal of diastereoselectivity
observed for 28m prompted further investigation of the
stereochemical origin of the [3+2] annulation by DFT calculations
(vide infra). Notably, despite its modest yield, cis,cis-28m could be further elaborated
to (±)-arnicenone (3), demonstrating the synthetic
utility of the [3+2] annulation product (vide infra).

[2+2] Cycloaddition and Ring Expansion

To access the
complementary cis,cis-fused framework,
we next evaluated a [2+2] cycloaddition strategy (). Cycloaddition of the corresponding
allene precursors (27) afforded cyclobutane intermediates 30, which likely exploit the inherent rigidity of the four-membered
ring to control the stereochemical outcome, delivering cis,cis-5,4,5- or cis,cis-6,4,5-tricyclic skeletons. These intermediates (30)
subsequently underwent oxidative ring expansion to generate the cis,cis-angular triquinane core and the
corresponding cis,cis-6,5,5-tricyclic
frameworks, respectively. Mechanistically, the distinct stereochemical
outcome arises from an orthogonal reaction manifold: the [2+2] pathway
establishes an all-cis relationship during cyclobutane
formation and preserves it through stereoselective oxidative expansion.
Thus, selective choice of annulation mode enables predictable control
over ring-junction geometry. After optimization, the sequence proceeded
in good overall yield with exclusive diastereoselectivity. In general,
the [2+2] cycloaddition exhibited broad substrate scope. In contrast
to the [3+2] Danheiser annulation (), which favored substrates bearing R1 = H, the [2+2] cycloaddition pathway tolerated both R1 = H and Me. Substituents at R2 were also well
accommodated (H or Me), while variation at R3 (Me, Et,
and iPr) was likewise compatible. TMS, TES, and TIPS substituents
were all effective under the cycloaddition conditions. Notably, allenes
bearing a terminal geminal diproton (30l, 30m, and 30n) also participated efficiently, affording
the desired products in 62–85% yield. The practicality of the
[2+2] cycloaddition was further demonstrated by a gram-scale reaction,
which furnished 30a in 55% yield (see Supporting Information for experimental details). To further
examine the effect of linker size on the two complementary cyclization
pathways, we also investigated an allene precursor designed to generate
a six-membered bottom ring. Under the optimized [2+2] cycloaddition
conditions, however, the corresponding substrate failed to afford
the desired cycloadduct. In contrast, the same substrate underwent
the intramolecular [3+2] Danheiser annulation to furnish the cis,trans-5,5,6-tricyclic product in 30%
yield (see Supporting Information for substrate
structure and experimental details). These results demonstrate that
extension of the linker to generate a six-membered bottom ring is
tolerated in the [3+2] pathway, albeit with reduced efficiency, whereas
the corresponding [2+2] cycloaddition is unsuccessful under the optimized
conditions.

Total Synthesis of (±)-Arnicenone

With the [2+2]
cycloaddition-derived cis,cis-5,4,5-tricyclic
intermediate 30n in hand, we next investigated its elaboration
toward (±)-arnicenone (3) (). To this end, 30n was subjected
to epoxidation with mCPBA to afford epoxide 31 in 87% yield with an α/β ratio of 1:4. The
α-isomer was unambiguously confirmed by X-ray diffraction (XRD)
analysis. Both diastereomers were then subjected to LiBr-promoted
ring expansion in the presence of HMPA
at elevated temperature (130 °C), furnishing the 1,4-diketone
(33). However, further attempts to convert 33 into the corresponding α-methylated product 34 were unsuccessful. Given the unsuccessful α-methylation of 33, we turned to an alternative strategy involving reduction
of ketone 30n, followed by hydroxyl-directed epoxidation
of the exo-olefin with mCPBA, which
proceeded smoothly to afford epoxide 38 with exclusive
β-selectivity (). Treatment of this intermediate with LiBr in the presence
of HMPA in toluene at 130 °C promoted rearrangement to furnish
the cis,cis-angular triquinane core
in 99% yield, with a diastereomeric ratio of 1:5 favoring the desired
3-hydroxyketone regioisomer (40) over the 4-hydroxyketone
(39). Oxidation of 40 with IBX then delivered the corresponding 1,3-diketone
(41) in 98% yield. Regioselective conversion of the carbonyl
group into the dithiane using 1,2-ethanedithiol and BF3·Et2O proceeded efficiently to afford the protected
intermediate (42) in 86% yield. The identity of 42 was unambiguously confirmed by X-ray diffraction (XRD)
analysis. Wittig olefination employing CH3PPh3Br and tBuOK installed the exocyclic alkene (43), and subsequent TsOH-mediated olefin isomerization furnished
the product (44) in 65% yield over two steps. Removal
of the thioacetal with MeI in the presence of CaCO3 provided
ketone (35) in 80% yield. Formation of the silyl enol
ether followed by Pd­(OAc)2-mediated oxidation (Saegusa–Ito
reaction) delivered the enone intermediate
(36) in 76% yield. Finally, conjugate methyl addition
using MeLi/CuI furnished the target molecule (±)-arnicenone (3) in 78% yield (16 steps, 9.6% overall yield). To further
demonstrate the synthetic utility of the [3+2] Danheiser annulation,
we also established an alternative route to (±)-arnicenone from cis,cis-28m (). Despite its modest yield
(10%) in the annulation, 28m possesses the requisite cis,cis-angular triquinane framework for
direct elaboration toward the natural product. Desilylation of 28m followed by Saegusa–Ito reaction furnished enone 36, which underwent conjugate methylation to afford (±)-arnicenone
(3) (7 steps, 2.1% overall yield). Thus, the cis,cis-angular triquinane generated directly
through the [3+2] annulation provides an alternative entry to the
natural product. In parallel, the synthetic utility of the cis,trans-[3+2] annulation product was
examined through its elaboration toward the corresponding epi-arnicenone analogue. The sequence was successfully advanced
to the late-stage epi-arnicenone precursor enone;
however, the final conjugate methylation could not be achieved under
the conditions examined (see Supporting Information for details).

DFT Calculations of [3+2] Danheiser Annulation

The
identity of R1 (Me vs H) proved crucial in controlling
both the reactivity and stereochemical outcome of the [3+2] annulation.
Because the present study was directed toward angular triquinane frameworks
relevant to natural product synthesis, the investigation of substitution
at R1 was focused on R1 = H and R1 = Me; larger substituents at this position were not examined. Substrates
bearing R1 = H generally favored formation of the cis,trans-isomer, whereas introduction
of R1 = Me resulted in diminished reactivity and reversed
the diastereoselectivity, with 28m favoring the cis,cis-isomer (cis,cis/cis,trans = 4:1).
Notably, preferential formation of a cis,cis-angular tricyclic framework was also observed by Zhai
and co-workers in their Danheiser annulation en route to (−)-conidiogenone.

in our system, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we have developed a diastereodivergent
strategy
for the stereocontrolled synthesis of angular triquinanes through
complementary [3+2] Danheiser annulation and [2+2] cycloaddition pathways
from common allene precursors. The [3+2] annulation generally provides
access to cis,trans-angular triquinanes,
whereas the [2+2] cycloaddition followed by oxidative ring expansion
furnishes the complementary cis,cis-fused tricyclic architectures. DFT calculations revealed that the
initial spirocyclization governs both the reactivity and stereochemical
outcome of the [3+2] annulation and rationalized the R1-dependent reversal of diastereoselectivity. The practicality of
both cyclization pathways was further demonstrated on gram scale.
Finally, the synthetic utility of this strategy was demonstrated through
the total synthesis of (±)-arnicenone via the [2+2] cycloaddition/oxidative
ring-expansion pathway, while an alternative synthesis from the cis,cis-[3+2] Danheiser annulation product 28m further highlighted the complementary nature of the two
approaches. Collectively, these results establish a versatile platform
for stereodivergent access to angular triquinanes and their application
to complex natural product synthesis.
