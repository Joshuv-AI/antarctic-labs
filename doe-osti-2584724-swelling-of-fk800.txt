 1. Introduction

The report summarizes an assessment of the strain experienced by FK800 upon change in humidity and
temperature of its surroundings.

The FK800 samples were obtained from Laural Hargrove in the form of a disk that was 12 mm in diameter
and 0.5 – 1 mm in thickness. The sample had a Lot # 05172301. For TMA testing, the sample was cut into
a small film of dimensions 0.5 mm x 3.38 mm x 6.57 mm and tested under the tensile mode. A single set of
swelling experiments were performed on the sample between November and December 2024.

 2. Polymer Swelling

Change in the length of FK800 in response to moisture sorption was measured using the NETZSCH
Thermo-mechanical Analyzer (TMA). Several measurements were made using the TMA’s tension module
to quantify the strain experienced by FK800 under different humidities at 50 ℃. Data from almost all
experiments showed little correlation between the change in humidity and the observed strain. Two
examples are shown in Figure 1 and Figure 2 from experiments conducted on November 8th and
December 5th, 2024, respectively. The reason behind the lack of correlation is not fully clear. Visually,
several cracks were easily visible which could impact the mechanical response of the material. In other
tests, the sample also exhibited softening behavior at 50 ℃ which could decrease the sample’s Young’s
modulus. The lack of reproducibility of the measured observations and lack of correlation between the
imposed humidity and observed strain response could be due to the observed cracks and softening of
FK800.

Figure 1. Evolution of measured strain (blue) and relative humidity (orange) for FK800 as measured in the
experiment conducted on November 15th, 2024.

Due to the lack of correlation between the measured strain and the imposed humidity, the experimental data
was analyzed to find the maximum swelling observed. This was computed by finding the difference
between the minimum and the maximum strain value measured in the portion of the experiment when the
temperature is held constant, and the humidity is varied. The intention is to come up with bounds for the
maximum swelling one can expect due to change in humidity. The data from the experiment performed on

November 12, 2024, could not be processed due to lack of humidity control in this experiment. Maximum
strain values from the remaining three experiments varied between 5.2 × 10−4 and 2.6 × 10−3 . It should
be noted that given the lack of correlation between the change in strain and the change in humidity, the
observed swelling cannot be attributed to the change in humidity with any confidence. Other factors like
the duration of the experiment may also have an impact on the measure maximum strain.

Figure 2. Evolution of measured strain (blue) and relative humidity (orange) for FK800 as measured in the
experiment conducted on December 5th, 2024.

These observations are in direct contradiction to the observations from the moisture uptake experiments.
The report titled (U) The role of FK-800 in Water Sorption Equilibrium and Dynamics on LX-17-1 shows
that the amount of moisture absorbed by FK800 increases with increases in humidity. Figure 5, 7, and 8 in
the report show a clear correlation between change in RH and change in moisture uptake unlike the lack of
any correlation between the swelling and change in RH seen in Figure 1 and Figure 2. In the absence of
additional observations to quantify the change in strain in response to change in RH, in our assessment, the
hygroscopic swelling of FK800 should be treated as unknown.

 3. Thermal Expansion

Change in the size of TPX in response to temperature change was also measured using the NETZSCH
Thermo-mechanical Analyzer. The observed thermal strain was more consistent with our expectations than
the hygroscopic strain seen in Figure 1 and Figure 2 as the thermal strain consistently increased with
increase in temperature. The two datasets from November 8 and December 5, 2024, could be used to
estimate the coefficient of thermal expansion. The data from November 8 had a spike in strain early and so
the coefficient of thermal expansion was estimated after removing the first 70 data points. The estimated
coefficient of thermal expansion 1.2 × 10−4 ℃-1. The data from December 5 showed slightly non-linear
behavior and the estimated coefficient of thermal expansion was 5.6 × 10−4 C-1.

 4. Summary

The hygroscopic swelling and the thermal expansion behavior of FK800 showed anomalous behavior
relative to typical expectations. The hygroscopic swelling showed no correlation to the change in humidity,
i.e., the material didn’t necessarily always swell when humidity was increased, it sometime shrank as well.
In response to increase in temperature, the material consistently expanded. However, based on two datasets
the rate of expansion with temperature changed between experiments. Hence, it was difficult to extract any
reliable parameter values for this material.
