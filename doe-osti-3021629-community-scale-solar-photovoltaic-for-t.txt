1.0 Executive Summary
The Forest County Potawatomi Community (FCPC), a Federally recognized Indian Tribe in Milwaukee,
WI, installed solar photovoltaic (PV) systems on or adjacent to ten (10) Tribally owned facilities. These
systems are estimated to produce over 1 megawatt (MW) of power to offset grid electricity use, saving
approximately $107,000 annually.

2.0 Background
FCPC has a membership exceeding 1,400 individuals and is located on reservation lands in Forest
County, Wisconsin, and tribal trust lands in Milwaukee County, Wisconsin. The Tribe owns and
operates significant governmental and business operations in both areas. Until relatively recently,
FCPC was one of the poorest tribes in the Midwest. Due to its successful gaming operations, it now has
improved its financial situation and is using its resources to benefit both its members and the public
through environmental and other community efforts, many of which are long delayed because of the
extended period of abject poverty.

The Potawatomi culture and values direct FCPC to take only from Mother Earth what is needed to
survive. This led FCPC to its long-term goal of becoming energy independent. As an interim step,
nearly 55 million kWh of annual renewable energy certificates (“RECs”) were purchased, which offset
more than 100% of the combined electricity use of the Tribe’s facilities.

Since 2010, FCPC has had nine projects with DOE’s Office of Indian Energy that have included:
 • LED lighting upgrade at Potawatomi Bingo Casino.
 • Energy efficiency improvements for Wunder Hall, a 34,000 square foot building which is listed
 on the National Register of Historic Places.
 • Installed over 3.3 MW of solar PV.
 • Completed energy audits for over 90 homes and buildings.

Further details on the above projects and more can be found on the DOE, Office of Indian Energy Policy
and Programs website.

3.0 Project Objectives
The Project proposed the following objectives to offset grid electrical usage for the Tribe:
 1. Deploy 1.2 MW of roof- and ground-mount solar PV across ten sites.
 2. Evaluate, monitor, and verify system functioning over a 12-month period.

The Project would benefit the Tribe by reducing the use of fossil generated electricity from 4.2-99% at
ten tribal buildings across the reservation.

4.0 Description of Activities Performed
FCPC initially broke the project into 15 activities:
 1. Site-Specific Solar PV Systems Specifications and Implementation Plan
 2. Bid Out & Hire an Energy Investor/Contractor
 3. LLC Formation
 4. Power Purchase Agreement & Interconnection Application
 5. Lease Agreements
 6. Final System Design
 7. Structural Review
 8. Obtain Permits
 9. Order/Secure Materials
 10. Install Racking
 11. Install Solar PV Modules
 12. Install Inverters
 13. Connect Inverters to Electric Distribution System
 14. Test/Inspect/Commission
 15. Verification of Energy Use Reduction

Activity numbers three, five, and eight were not performed. Originally, FCPC anticipated creating an
LLC to take advantage of tax credits which would have required a lease for the LLC to operate on tribal
lands as well as permits from the Tribe. Because the State of Wisconsin’s approach to these
arrangements changed there was no benefit in LLC creation for tax purposes.

The project was initially designed to install solar at eight tribal locations. However, land use changes at
several locations rendered the original locations no longer viable. Thus, new sites were identified and
plans changed. The original contractor designed and installed at seven locations. These installations
were active in 2022. The Tribe contracted with a second contractor for design, installation and
activation of three additional sites in 2024. After installation, several months of continued work with
the contractor was required to finalize set-ups, troubleshoot construction issues, and repair items
damaged in an ice storm.

Remote monitoring systems and internet connections were initially considered for a few sites but were
later added to the scope for all sites. An advanced monitoring system was installed at the Community
Center, one of the larger energy-consumption buildings, to accurately track energy generation and use.

Production for the system follows typical Wisconsin weather patterns and is variable with the greatest
amount of production in summer months. Annual energy production from the project’s installed

5.0 Conclusions and Recommendations
The Tribe revised the project at several junctures. FCPC had originally planned to install approximately
1,068 kW. Several locations were removed from consideration during the final planning process. The
Tribe revised its sites and included additional solar at a pre-existing site. Ultimately, the Tribe was able
to install over 1 MW at ten tribal locations.

The changes discussed earlier resulted in an additional 200 kilowatts (20%) of solar being installed
compared to the originally proposed amount. This increase led to solar installations at locations
previously reliant on utility-sourced energy. The project changes reinforce the need for flexibility. For
future projects, FCPC will ensure backup locations are identified.

FCPC originally monitored solar production through a stock web-based system for analyses; however,
while tracking production on the website is helpful, it does not provide sufficient information to fully
measure and plan energy usage for the future. Because the solar production is used “behind-the-
meter” (before reaching the utility meter), offset data is not available. Thus, the Tribe installed a robust
monitoring system in the Community Center, one of the larger energy-consumption buildings, to
accurately track total energy generation and ensure the solar is offsetting usage. Additionally, the
system in the Community Center will be able to monitor different loads within the building. Eventually,
with more data, the Tribe will be able to design energy storage, additional solar, energy efficiency
measures, and similar projects to fully control energy use at the building and watch trends.

In its initial proposal, FCPC did not include internet connectivity, wiring, or similar costs in the planning.
Unfortunately, project changes lead to the installation of solar on several buildings that were not
internet ready. FCPC had to later incorporate internet installation which slowed the project.

6.0 Lessons Learned
FCPC had several personnel changes throughout the course of this project. The changes reinforced the
importance of maintaining a total project record including a motion list, timeline, milestone emails,
and similar documents separately from employee emails. Project files are important to track status of
various items as well as warranty paperwork, payment invoices and other key documents.

Although the Tribe was able to include a building-specific monitoring system in the Community Center,
it was not originally planned. In lower energy-consumption buildings where the solar almost offsets
building use, building-specific monitoring is not necessarily critical but in larger buildings when the
production is not accounted for on a utility bill, it is difficult to ensure the offset is being credited.
Future projects will include system monitoring to better understand a building’s total consumption
without relying wholly on the utility bills.

Although the FCPC Energy Team worked on community engagement at several stages it is also
important to work with other tribal departments. FCPC’s IT team was brought in late in the project
when it was determined that internet was needed at several sites. Because the sites were added later
in the planning, it could not have initially been known that internet would be needed at those
locations. However, for future projects, meetings will be held with outside departments to identify
issues early on.
