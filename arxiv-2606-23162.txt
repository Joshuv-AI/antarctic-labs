Sublinear Time Algorithms for Abelian Group Property Testing
Nader H. Bshouty

Abstract
In this paper, we study the problems of abelian group property testing in two models. In the partially specified model (PS-model), the algorithm does not know the group size but can access randomly chosen elements of the group, along with the Cayley table of these elements, which provides the result of the binary operation for every pair of selected elements. In the stronger fully specified model (FS-model), the algorithm knows the size of the group and has access to all its elements and the Cayley table. In property testing of abelian group property, given a finite set G G and oracle access to a binary operation ∗ : G 2 → G *:G^{2}\to G , we aim to distinguish whether ( G , ∗ ) (G,*) is an abelian group or is ϵ \epsilon -far from any abelian group over G G . Using a novel approach, we present a tester in the PS-model (and consequently in the FS-model) that runs in time O ~ ​ ( | G | + 1 / ϵ ) \tilde{O}(\sqrt{|G|}+1/\epsilon) , improving upon the Goldreich-Tauber tester, which runs in time O ⁡ ( | G | / ϵ ) O(|G|/\epsilon) . Additionally, our tester improves another tester by Goldreich and Tauber that runs in time O ⁡ ( | G | 2 ) O(|G|^{2}) and makes O ~ ​ ( | G | + 1 / ϵ ) \tilde{O}(|G|+1/\epsilon) queries. We further extend our result to testing subclasses of abelian groups 𝒢 {\cal G} that are closed under isomorphism. Specifically, if one can decide in time T T whether an abelian group of the form ℤ m 1 × ⋯ × ℤ m r \mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{r}} belongs to 𝒢 {\cal G} , then there exists a tester for 𝒢 {\cal G} that runs in time T + O ~ ​ ( | G | + 1 / ϵ ) T+\tilde{O}(\sqrt{|G|}+1/\epsilon) and makes O ⁡ ( | G | + 1 / ϵ ) O(\sqrt{|G|}+1/\epsilon) queries. This result gives testers that run in time O ⁡ ( | G | + 1 / ϵ ) O(\sqrt{|G|}+1/\epsilon) for subclasses such as abelian groups of rank at most k k , abelian p p -groups, and vector spaces over ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} . We then present two subclasses, 𝒢 1 {\cal G}_{1} and 𝒢 2 {\cal G}_{2} , of abelian groups that are closed under isomorphism for which any tester for 𝒢 1 {\cal G}_{1} in the FS-model must run in time Ω ⁡ ( | G | 1 / 4 + 1 / ϵ ) \Omega(|G|^{1/4}+1/\epsilon) , and any tester for 𝒢 2 {\cal G}_{2} in the PS-model must run in time Ω ⁡ ( | G | + 1 / ϵ ) \Omega(\sqrt{|G|}+1/\epsilon) , showing that our approach provides tight bounds for certain subclasses of abelian groups.

Our Technique

Our approach is novel and does not rely on testing the associativity, commutativity, and cancellativity of
(G,*)
, as previous methods have done
[
5
,
10
,
15
,
16
]

Given a finite
magma

(G,*)
– a finite set

with a binary operation
*:G\times G\to G

we begin by running the algorithm from
[
3
]
that constructs generators for
(G,*)
with triangular relations
2
2

2

A set of generators
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{t}\}
of a group

triangular relations
if, for each
i\in[t]

. If the algorithm fails, we reject. If it successfully returns a set of generators with triangular relations, denoted as

(\Gamma({\cal R}),\cdot)
that satisfies those relations.

The key results ensuring the correctness of our approach are as follows:

1.

If
(G,*)
is an abelian group, then it is isomorphic to
\Gamma({\cal R})
via an isomorphism
\Psi:\Gamma({\cal R})\to G
.

2.

For every
x\in\Gamma({\cal R})
,
\Psi(x)
can be computed in time
poly(\log|G|)
.

3.

Both multiplication and inversion in
\Gamma({\cal R})
can be computed in time
poly(\log|G|)
.

Before presenting our primary tester, we first introduce a simple tester that runs in time
\tilde{O}(|G|+1/\epsilon)
. The main idea is to construct, in time
\tilde{O}(|G|)
, a table of the isomorphic values
\Psi:\Gamma({\cal R})\to G
. Once this table is constructed, we check whether
\Psi
is a bijective map. If not, then we reject.

If
\Psi
is bijective, we then, choose
O(1/\epsilon)
pairs
(x,y)\in\Gamma({\cal R})^{2}
uniformly at random and verify whether
\Psi(x\cdot y)=\Psi(x)*\Psi(y)
.

We then prove that this algorithm provides a tester that runs in time
\tilde{O}(|G|+1/\epsilon)
.

To achieve an improved time complexity of
\tilde{O}(\sqrt{|G|}+1/\epsilon)
, we avoid constructing the entire table of
\Psi
and, therefore, cannot directly verify whether
\Psi
is bijective. Instead, we first check whether
\Psi
is
\Theta(1)
-close to a bijective map and then verify whether
\Psi(x\cdot y)=\Psi(x)*\Psi(y)
. The first step requires
O(\sqrt{|G|})
queries, and the second step requires
O(1/\epsilon)
queries.

Although this two-step approach may not be universally applicable, we show that it holds for abelian groups due to their specific algebraic structure.

For testing subclasses
{\cal G}
of abelian groups that are closed under isomorphism, we use the same tester and add a verification procedure to check that
\Gamma({\cal R})
belongs to
{\cal G}
.

The lower bound results follow directly from Theorems 7 and 8 in
[
3

A One-Sided Tester for Abelian Group

In this section, we prove the following two theorems in the FS-model.

Why not the PS-model?

The one-sided tester presented in this section is stated only in the FS-model. In the PS-model, the size of the group is unknown and the algorithm only receives uniformly random elements from an oracle. Since a one-sided tester must accept every abelian group with probability
1

However, after any finite sequence of samples, there is always a positive probability that the oracle continues returning only previously seen elements. Therefore, no deterministic procedure can certify that all elements of the group have been observed and hence cannot determine
|G|

Theorem 4.1
.

There exists a one-sided tester for abelian groups that runs in time
\tilde{O}(|G|+1/\epsilon)

Theorem 7.5

{\cal G}

Proof.

{\cal T}
for
{\cal G}

Consider the set of groups
{\cal G}^{\prime}

Given

H_{1}^{\prime}
