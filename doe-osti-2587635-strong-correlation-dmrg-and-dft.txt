Executive Summary/Abstract
This project developed new ways to improve computer simulations of materials where electrons
interact strongly with each other, a challenge for today’s most widely used method, density
functional theory (DFT). We used an exact numerical method, the density matrix renormalization
group (DMRG), to create highly accurate reference results for simple model systems, and used
these to test DFT, prove when it will converge, and even train machine-learned functionals. We
also invented new kinds of localized basis functions (“gausslets” and “multi-sliced gausslets”)
and a “sliced-basis” approach that make high-accuracy simulations faster and more practical.
These methods were applied to extended hydrogen systems, enabling the direct derivation of
accurate low-energy models from rst-principles calculations. We also introduced a new
formalism, Conditional-Probability DFT, which could bypass traditional approximations. The
tools and results from this work, including open-source software releases, will help scientists
design and understand complex quantum materials.

 Full Project Summary
The DOE award DE-SC0008696 supported a sustained program to advance electronic structure
theory for strongly correlated systems, combining formally exact many-body solvers with new
theoretical and computational frameworks. Our long-term goal was to bridge the gap between the
high accuracy of the Density Matrix Renormalization Group (DMRG) and the broad
applicability of Density Functional Theory (DFT), using exact results to guide, test, and inspire
improved approximate methods.

In the early years, in collaboration with Kieron Burke’s group, we used DMRG as an “exact”
continuum solver to generate benchmark data for one-dimensional soft-Coulomb systems. These
exact densities and energies served as testbeds for exchange–correlation functionals and as the
foundation for developing new DFT formalisms and approximations. In later years, we
developed novel basis sets and algorithms—including gausslets and sliced-basis DMRG (sb-
DMRG)—to extend high-accuracy continuum calculations to more complex and higher-
dimensional systems.

This award also played a key role in the creation and development of the ITensor software library
for Tensor Network and DMRG calculations. This library started in our group and is now
supported by the Simons Foundation/Flatiron Institute. The current main developer, Miles
Stoudenmire, received key early support from this award as it was rst being created. The
ITensor paper has received more than 1000 citations, which may make it the most popular
DMRG/Tensor Network software library.

1. Motivation and Hypothesis
DFT is the workhorse of electronic structure, but its accuracy hinges on the exchange–correlation
functional. In strongly correlated regimes—such as stretched bonds, low-dimensional
conductors, and Mott insulators—errors can be severe. Our hypothesis was that exact results
from carefully constructed continuum models would expose both strengths and limitations of
existing functionals, provide exact “targets” for functional development, and guide the creation
of new frameworks that remain accurate under strong correlation.

We pursued three linked efforts:

  1. Construct continuum systems solvable to near-machine precision with DMRG,
 enabling exact DFT benchmarks.

  2. Develop new DFT formalisms and machine-learned functionals trained on exact data.

  3. Create new basis sets and algorithms for extending high-accuracy solvers to larger and
 more complex systems.

2. Approach

2.1 Exact DFT benchmarks with DMRG

We developed one-dimensional continuum models with soft-Coulomb interactions, allowing
DMRG to compute essentially exact ground states and densities. These models enabled the rst
fully self-consistent Kohn–Sham calculations with the exact exchange–correlation functional,
obtained by inverting the DMRG density, and yielded a rigorous proof of Kohn–Sham
convergence conditions, separating numerical issues from intrinsic functional errors
(Stoudenmire et al., 2012; Burke et al., 2016).

2.2 Machine-learned and alternative DFT formalisms

Exact DMRG data allowed the construction of machine-learned functionals that reach chemical
accuracy even in strongly correlated regimes, including stretched H₂ and extended hydrogen
chains (Li et al., 2016). We also introduced Conditional-Probability DFT (CP-DFT), a
formally exact approach that bypasses the traditional energy-functional route by solving for
conditional probability densities (McCarty et al., 2020; Pederson et al., 2022).

2.3 Gausslets and sliced-basis methods

To go beyond 1D, we created gausslets—orthogonal, localized basis functions with near-
diagonal Coulomb matrices—and multi-sliced gausslets for 3D systems. Combined with sliced-
basis DMRG (sb-DMRG), these methods treat one spatial direction explicitly while
representing transverse degrees of freedom with compact local orbitals. This yields continuum-
accurate correlated calculations with favorable scaling (White, 2017; Stoudenmire & White,
2017; White & Stoudenmire, 2019; Qi et al., 2020).

2.4 Applications to extended systems and effective models

Using sb-DMRG and related many-body methods, a large multi-institutional collaboration
obtained benchmark-quality results for extended hydrogen chains—including the equation of
state, dimerization physics, metal–insulator crossover, and competing magnetic orders (Motta et
al., 2017; Motta et al., 2020). In parallel, we developed a downfolding framework using natural-
orbital Wannier functions to derive compact Hubbard-like models from high-accuracy continuum
data, providing a controlled link between ab initio calculations and effective low-energy theories.

2.5 Further applications and extensions of fundamental work

The techniques developed in the previous sections also led to further applications and novel
theoretical questions. The one-dimensional DMRG benchmarks were used to test a novel ML
method (Kalita et al, 2022). The ability to have exact benchmarks also led to some of the
theoretical questions in Cisostomo et al, 2023, and the role of exact conditions in DFT (Pederson

 3. Key Findings
 • Exact DFT benchmarks for atoms, molecules, and periodic systems; rst self-consistent
 Kohn–Sham results with the exact functional and a proof of convergence conditions.

 • Machine-learned functionals capturing strong correlation, and a new exact formalism
 (CP-DFT) that avoids the explicit energy functional.

 • Gausslets, multi-sliced gausslets, and sb-DMRG, enabling continuum-accurate
 correlated calculations in extended systems.

 • Hydrogen chain benchmarks and phase diagrams from multi-method collaborations.

 • Controlled downfolding of continuum results into effective Hamiltonians.

 4. Signi cance

 This project delivered a complete cycle: exact solution → functional insight → improved tools
 → new applications. The benchmarks, proofs, and algorithms are now used by other groups.
 Gausslets and sb-DMRG have broad applicability in quantum chemistry, quantum Monte Carlo,
 and quantum simulation. By anchoring functional development to exact physics and extending
 the reach of high-accuracy solvers, this work strengthens the foundation for predictive materials
 modeling in strongly correlated regimes.
