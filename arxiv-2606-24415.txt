Novel Triple-Based Problems for the Construction of Phylogenetic Networks via Least Common Ancestors
Patricia A. Ebert, Anna Lindeberg, Marc Hellmuth

Abstract
Evolutionary histories are often represented by rooted phylogenetic networks, whose leaves correspond to extant taxa and whose internal vertices represent ancestral lineages. Since such histories must usually be inferred from incomplete data, in particular from genomic sequences of present-day taxa, one often obtains only local information about relative evolutionary proximity. For instance, sequence data may suggest that two taxa x x and y y are more closely related to each other than either is to a third taxon z z . This information is classically encoded by a rooted triple x ​ y | z xy|z . In this paper, we study rooted triples in phylogenetic networks under an ancestor-based interpretation: x ​ y | z xy|z is displayed if the unique least common ancestor (LCA) of x x and y y lies strictly below the unique LCA of x x and z z , respectively of y y and z z , and the latter two LCAs coincide. We also introduce anchored triples x ¯ ​ y | z \underline{x}y|z , which retain only the asymmetric comparison that the LCA of x x and y y lies below the LCA of x x and z z . This relaxation is natural in networks, where different pairwise ancestral relationships need not behave as they do in trees. We consider several variants of consistency problems for ordinary and anchored triples, both with and without forbidden triples. Somewhat surprisingly, these ancestor-based consistency questions for triples in phylogenetic networks do not appear to have been addressed before despite their direct biological interpretation and the fact that such constraints can be inferred naturally from genomic sequence data. By translating these questions into realization problems for required and forbidden LCA-constraints, we show that all resulting problems can be solved in polynomial time. Moreover, whenever a solution exists, a suitable realizing DAG and phylogenetic network can be constructed within the same time bound.



Keywords:
DAG; phylogenetic super-network; rooted, anchored and forbidden triples; triplets;
LCA-constraints; consistency problems; poset; polynomial-time algorithms

Introduction

Rooted triples play a central role in phylogenetics as local constraints describing the relative
evolutionary proximity of three taxa. To be more precise, a rooted triple (also known as a rooted
triplet)
xy|z
expresses that taxa
x
and
y

is to
z

classical BUILD algorithm of Aho et al.
[
1

decides in polynomial time whether there
exists a tree that displays a given set of rooted triples and, if so, constructs such a tree.

However, many evolutionary histories are not adequately described by trees. Processes such as
hybridization or horizontal gene transfer create reticulate patterns of ancestry, in which lineages
may merge as well as split. Phylogenetic networks, and more generally directed acyclic graphs
(DAGs), provide a natural framework for representing such non-tree-like evolution
[
19
]
.
Consequently, it is important to understand when local triple information can be combined into a DAG
or network rather than into a tree.

In contrast, the LCA-based notion

has received comparatively less attention so far,
although it is closer to situations in which triples are inferred from pairwise evolutionary

of phylogenetic trees from best-match relations

In this contribution, we consider several fundamental algorithmic “triple consistency
(
TC

be a set of required triples and, possibly, let

be a set of forbidden triples, where
triples may either be rooted (“ordinary”) or anchored. Now, the task is to decide whether there
exists a phylogenetic network, respectively a DAG, that displays all required triples and displays
none of the forbidden ones. For ordinary triples, “display” is understood in the sense of

(T1)
; for anchored triples, it is understood as

-display. This gives the following
four basic decision problems:

\begin{array}[]{l|l|l}\text{Problem}&\text{Input}&\text{Question: Is there a phylogenetic network (resp., DAG) $G$ that \ldots}\\
\hline\cr\texttt{TC}&\text{triple set }\mathcal{R}&\text{\ldots\ displays all triples in }\mathcal{R}\,?\\[1.99997pt]
\texttt{ATC}&\text{anchored triple set }\mathcal{R}&\text{\ldots\ \text{\tiny\faIconFromMacro{faAnchor}}-displays all anchored triples in }\mathcal{R}\,?\\[1.99997pt]
\texttt{TC-F}&\text{triple sets }\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}&\text{\ldots\ displays all triples in }\mathcal{R}\text{ and none in }\mathcal{F}\,?\\[1.99997pt]
\texttt{ATC-F}&\text{anchored triple sets }\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}&\text{\ldots\ \text{\tiny\faIconFromMacro{faAnchor}}-displays all anchored triples in }\mathcal{R}\text{ and none in }\mathcal{F}\,?\\[1.99997pt]
\end{array}

While every set of rooted triples can be topologically-displayed by some
network
[
20
,
22
]

(T1)

and

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)

requirement globally. We say that a DAG

has the

if

is
well-defined for all leaves

and

of

and

for solutions to

, respectively,
ATC-F
among phylogenetic networks or DAGs

In the following, we show that all of these problems can be solved in polynomial time. To this end,
we build on recent work in which we established results for LCA-based constraints

,

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)

. We sometimes use

]

A

For a given DAG

\preceq_{G}
-minimal common ancestor of

and

in

. In a network

. Moreover, we are
interested in DAGs where

. A DAG

.

For a given DAG

on

, we further define the relation
\trianglelefteq_{G}
on

as

\trianglelefteq_{G}\coloneqq\{(ab,xy)\,:\,\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab),\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\text{ are well-defined and }\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\}.

For later reference, we provide the following simple but useful result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

be a DAG on

such that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
, and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
are well-defined
for three leaves

. If
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
, then

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a DAG on
X
and suppose that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
, and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
are
well-defined for three leaves
x,y,z\in X
. Assume that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
. This
together with

and

implies that the vertex

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
must be a common ancestor of

. By the defining property of LCAs, we

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
.
∎

To recall, a (rooted) triple

is a binary tree on three distinct leaves

,

, and

. Moreover, an anchored

is an ordered tuple defined on three distinct elements

. We
consider here sets of rooted and anchored triples and define the set of leaves occurring in those

Definition 2.2
.

For a rooted triple

, we put

. For

of rooted or anchored triples, we define

X_{\mathcal{R}}\coloneqq\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{R}}L(t)\ \text{and}\ X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{F}}L(t).

We now show that
(T1)
implies

as mentioned in the introduction.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

be a DAG on
X
and
x,y,z\in X
. If

is displayed by
G
, then

is

.

Proof.

Let
G
be a DAG on
X
. Suppose that

is displayed by
G
. Hence,
x
,
y
, and
z
are
pairwise distinct. Moreover,
u\prec_{G}v
for

u\coloneqq\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
and
v\coloneqq\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
. In particular,
u
and
v
are
distinct. Since

, there are directed paths
P_{ux}=u\leadsto x
and
P_{uy}=u\leadsto y
. Since

, there is, by definition of LCAs, no descendant
w\neq u
of

that is an ancestor of both

and

. This immediately implies that
P_{ux}
and
P_{uy}
can
only intersect in

and are, therefore, internally vertex-disjoint. Moreover,

implies that there are directed paths

and
P_{vz}=v\leadsto z
. Note that

P_{vu}
can be extended to a

-path

by joining

and
P_{ux}
. By the same

must be internally vertex-disjoint. Since

is composed

and

and

as well as

and

are internally

and

are not internally vertex-disjoint, then there

and

and

are internally vertex-disjoint and, by similar arguments,

.

Throughout this paper, we often transform a DAG into a network and will use the following simple result.

Lemma 2.4

(From DAGs to networks,
[

, Lem 2.2]
)
.

Let

Additionally, we make frequent use of an
xy
-extension based on two distinct leaves

Let

We provide polynomial-time solutions for the triple-based problems in

forbidden sets of triples into suitable relations encoding LCA-constraints. The resulting relations

on realizable relations and

-realizable pairs of relations introduced

Definition 3.1

((Strict) Realization,
[

)
.

Let

be a relation on
\mathbb{P}_{2}(X)
and

. Then,

, the LCAs
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)

and

(I0)

(ab,cd)\in R
implies that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)
.

Moreover,
R

is realized by

if, for all

, the LCAs

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)
are well-defined and the following implications hold for

and

(I1)

(ab,cd)\in R

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)

(I2)

(ab,cd)\in R

.

In this case, we say that

is

.

Note that a relation

is strictly realized by

if and only if

is realized by

and

is asymmetric, i.e., strict realizability is just a special case of realizability

[

, Lem 5]
.

We further consider pairs
(R,F)
of relations on
\mathbb{P}_{2}(X)

and

are relations

\mathbb{P}_{2}(X)

represents “required

Definition 3.2

((Strict)

-Realization,
[

)

Let

. Then,

is

-realized
(resp.,

-realized
) by

is realized (resp., strictly

and

:

(F)

If
(ab,cd)\in F
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)

then
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\nprec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)
.

In this case, we say that
(R,F)
is
RF
-realizable
(resp.,

RF
-realizable)
.

Central to this theory are the closure operators
\operatorname{cl}
and
\operatorname{cl}_{F}
. To be more precise, let

and

be relations on
\mathbb{P}_{2}(X)
. We define

Definition 3.5

(Canonical DAG,

The following results summarize the realizability criteria that will be used throughout the paper.

Theorem 3.7

4
Consistency Problems for Anchored Triples

We are now able to tackle triple-based problems, starting with consistency problems related to
anchored triples. Note that, by definition, for every anchored triple
\underline{x}y|z

,

, and

are pairwise distinct. Recall that an anchored triple
\underline{x}y|z

-displayed
by a DAG or network

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
and

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)

It is

-displays
\underline{x}y|z

if and only if Condition (ii) holds. In general DAGs or networks, however,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
or

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
may fail to be well-defined; see for instance

in Figure

and the anchored triple
\underline{y}x|z
. We now consider the following two problems.

(

-displays

\mathcal{R}

(

.

Let
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
be a pair of anchored triple sets. Moreover, let

Let
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
be a pair of anchored triple sets and define

Moreover, let

We first show that Statement (1) implies Statement (2). To this end, assume that
G

-agrees

(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})

.

-agrees with

-realizes

(R,F)

-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
. Suppose that
\underline{x}y|z\in\mathcal{R}
and, thus,
(xy,xz)\in R
.
Since
G

RF
-realizes
(R,F)
,
G

and the vertices
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)

In this section, we return to “ordinary” rooted triples, as opposed to anchored triples. Our aim
is to construct a phylogenetic DAG or network that displays all required triples and displays none
of the forbidden ones. To be more precise, we consider the following two problems and show that they
can be solved in polynomial time.

Problem

(
Triples Consistency (TC)
)
.

Input:

A set
\mathcal{R}
of triples.

Question:

Is there a phylogenetic network (resp., DAG) on
X_{\mathcal{R}}
that displays all
triples in
\mathcal{R}
?

Problem

(
Triples Consistency with Forbidden Triples (TC-F)
)
.

Input:

Two sets
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{F}
of triples.

Is there a phylogenetic network (resp., DAG) on
X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}
that displays all
triples in
\mathcal{R}
but none in
\mathcal{F}
?

To recall,
x,y
, and
z
are pairwise distinct for any triple

. Moreover, a triple

is

if

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)

Thus, a triple

is not displayed by a DAG

(i’)
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
, or
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
is not well-defined or

all LCAs
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
, and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
are well-defined and one of the following
conditions holds

(ii’)
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\nprec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
or  (iii’)
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\nprec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
or  (iv’)
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)\neq\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
.

In the anchored triple setting, we considered the
ATC
problem as a special case of

ATC-F
by taking
\mathcal{F}=\emptyset
. In this setting, it was very useful to define the
forbidden relation
F=\{(xy,xz)\,:\,\underline{x}y|z\in\mathcal{F}\}
. This definition works because an
anchored triple
\underline{x}y|z
is not

-displayed by a DAG
G
whenever Condition (ii’)
holds, assuming that both
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
are well-defined. For ordinary rooted
triples, however, the situation is more subtle. In this case, non-displaying is characterized by the
disjunction of Conditions (ii’), (iii’), and (iv’). This “or” structure makes it more difficult to
define a simple relation
F
directly from
\mathcal{F}
.

We therefore first consider the
TC
problem and provide a polynomial-time algorithm for
deciding whether a set of triples
\mathcal{R}
can be displayed by a DAG or network and, if so, for
constructing such a DAG or network. To this end, we will show how a set of rooted triples can be
translated into a “symmetric” set of anchored triples, for which we can reuse the results of
Section
4
in a straightforward manner. For the
TC-F
problem, the connection
to anchored triples is no longer so direct. Therefore, we first translate the set of required
triples
\mathcal{R}
into the relation

R_{\mathcal{R}}\coloneqq\{(xy,xz),(xy,yz)\,:\,xy|z\in\mathcal{R}\},

on
\mathbb{P}_{2}(X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}})
and subsequently saturate it with additional
LCA-constraints induced by
\mathcal{F}
. This saturated version of
R_{\mathcal{R}}
is then used to decide the

TC-F
problem in polynomial time. We note in passing that
R_{\mathcal{R}}

satisfies
(X1)
for all triple sets
\mathcal{R}
.

5.1
Required Rooted Triples

We first provide a simple but useful result
that links the notion of displaying rooted triples and

-displaying anchored triples.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G
be a DAG on
X
and
x,y,z\in X
. Then the following two statements are equivalent

(1)

G
displays
xy|z
.

(2)

G

-displays both
\underline{x}y|z
and
\underline{y}x|z
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a DAG on
X
and
x,y,z\in X
. If
G
displays
xy|z
, then
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
,
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
are well-defined and it holds that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
. By
definition,
G

-displays both
\underline{x}y|z
and
\underline{y}x|z
. For the converse,
assume that
G

-displays both
\underline{x}y|z
and
\underline{y}x|z
. Hence,

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
, and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
are well-defined and it holds that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
. Moreover, by Lemma
2.1
, we
obtain
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
. Consequently,

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
holds. In other words,
G
displays the triple
xy|z
.
∎

The latter result allows us to reduce the problem of displaying rooted triples to the corresponding
problem for anchored triples.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a set of triples and put
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{\underline{x}y|z,\underline{y}x|z\,:\,xy|z\in\mathcal{R}\}
. Then the following statements are equivalent for every DAG
G
on
X_{\mathcal{R}}
.

(1)

G
displays all triples in
\mathcal{R}
.

(2)

G

-displays all anchored triples in
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
.

(3)

G
strictly realizes
R_{\mathcal{R}}
.

(4)

G
realizes
R_{\mathcal{R}}
and
(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\notin\operatorname{tc}(R_{\mathcal{R}})
for all
xy|z\in\mathcal{R}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
, and
G
be as stated and let

be a relation on
\mathbb{P}_{2}(X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}})
. One easily verifies that
R^{\prime}
and

R_{\mathcal{R}}
coincide. By Lemma
5.1
, Statements (1) and (2) are
equivalent. Moreover, by definition,
G

-displays all anchored triples in
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
if and
only if
G

-agrees with
(\mathcal{R}^{\prime},\emptyset)
. Therefore,
Theorem
4.2
implies that Statement (2) is equivalent to
G

strictly
RF
-realizing
(R^{\prime},\emptyset)
which, by definition, is equivalent to Statement (3).
Similarly, Theorem
4.2
states that Statement (3) is equivalent
to
G
realizing
R^{\prime}
and
(xz,xy)\notin\operatorname{tc}(R^{\prime})
for all
\underline{x}y|z\in\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
. Note that
by definition of
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
and
R^{\prime}
, we have
\underline{x}y|z\in\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
if and only if
xy|z\in\mathcal{R}

if and only if
\underline{y}x|z\in\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
. Thus,

and
(xz,xy)\notin\operatorname{tc}(R^{\prime})

and

for all

∎

In particular, if there exists a DAG that displays all required triples in
\mathcal{R}
, there also exists
a phylogenetic network that does so.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a set of triples. Then, the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

There is a DAG on
X_{\mathcal{R}}
that displays all triples in
\mathcal{R}
.

(2)

There is a phylogenetic network on
X_{\mathcal{R}}
that displays all triples in
\mathcal{R}
.

In particular, suppose that there exists a DAG that displays all triples in
\mathcal{R}
. Then the
canonical DAG
\mathscr{G}_{R_{\mathcal{R}}}
as well as the network

obtained from it by

are phylogenetic and display all triples in

.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a set of triples and
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{\underline{x}y|z,\underline{y}x|z\,:\,xy|z\in\mathcal{R}\}
. By Theorem
5.2
, a DAG displays all triples in

if and only if it

-displays all anchored triples in
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
, equivalently, if and only if it

-agrees with

(\mathcal{R}^{\prime},\emptyset)
. By Theorem

, there is a DAG on

that

-agrees with

,

5.2
Required and Forbidden Rooted Triples

We now consider pairs
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
of triple sets and design a polynomial-time algorithm for

Triples Consistency with Forbidden Triples (TC-F)
. Analogously to the notion of a DAG

-agreeing with a pair of anchored triple sets, we use the following terminology.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
be a pair of triple sets. A DAG
G

agrees
with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
if
G
displays
all triples in
\mathcal{R}
but none of the ones in
\mathcal{F}
.

To recall, a triple
xy|z
is not displayed by a DAG
G
if (i’) at least one of
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
, or
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
is not well-defined or, otherwise, (ii’)
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\nprec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
or (iii’)
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\nprec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
or (iv’)
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)\neq\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
. We note
in passing that one can show that if a triple is not displayed by a DAG
G
, then at least one of
the conditions (i’), (ii’), and (iii’) must be satisfied. Indeed, if all three LCAs are well-defined
and neither (ii’) nor (iii’) holds, then
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
and

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
. By applying Lemma
2.1
twice, we obtain

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
and, hence,
xy|z
is displayed.

For us some other observations are more important. Our approach to obtain a DAG
G^{\prime}
that agrees
with a pair
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
of triple sets is based, in essence, on the following ideas. First, recall
that for a given DAG
G
on
X
, we defined the relation
\trianglelefteq_{G}
on
\mathbb{P}_{2}(X)
as

\trianglelefteq_{G}\coloneqq\{(ab,xy)\,:\,\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab),\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\text{ are well-defined and }\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\}.

Now consider a forbidden triple
xy|z\in\mathcal{F}
and let
G
be a DAG that displays all triples in

\mathcal{R}
. Two different situations can occur. First, suppose that not all of the three LCAs

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy),\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz),\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
are forced to be well-defined by the required triples in
\mathcal{R}
.
In other words, there is some
ab\in\{xy,xz,yz\}
such that no triple in
\mathcal{R}
requires
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)

to be well-defined. If
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
is already not well-defined, then
G
does not display
xy|z
, and
there is nothing to do. If, on the other hand,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
is well-defined, then we can apply an

ab
-extension to obtain a DAG
G^{\prime}
in which
\operatorname{lca}_{G^{\prime}}(ab)
is no longer well-defined (cf. Observation
2.5
). By construction, this prevents
G^{\prime}
from displaying the
forbidden triple
xy|z
, while the triples in
\mathcal{R}
remain displayed. This first case is handled in
Definition
5.7
and Proposition
5.8
.

Second, suppose that the required triples force all three LCAs
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
, and

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
to be well-defined. In this case, non-display of
xy|z
cannot be ensured merely by
making one of these LCAs not well-defined, since that would, in turn, prevent
G
from displaying
all required triples. Instead, we have to modify the underlying LCA-constraints. If
xy|z
is
displayed by
G
, then
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
and, thus,
\{(xy,xz),(xy,yz),(xz,yz),(yz,xz)\}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
holds. The idea is then to add the “reverse”
elements
(xz,xy)
and
(yz,xy)
. For any DAG
G^{\prime}
realizing the resulting relation
Q
, this
enforces
\operatorname{lca}_{G^{\prime}}(xy)=\operatorname{lca}_{G^{\prime}}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G^{\prime}}(yz)
. Consequently,
G^{\prime}
does not display
xy|z
.
Provided that the required triples in
\mathcal{R}
are still displayed in
G^{\prime}
, this DAG
G^{\prime}
agrees with

(\mathcal{R},\{xy|z\})
. This second case is handled systematically by the saturation procedure introduced
in Definition
5.11
, which forms the basis for solving the
TC-F
problem in
polynomial time. We now introduce the main notation needed to formalize these ideas and start with
the following

Definition 5.6
.

If
\mathcal{S}
is a set of rooted triples, we define the
triple-support
as

\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\coloneqq\bigcup_{xy|z\in\mathcal{S}}\{xy,xz,yz\}
.

Note that since a triple
xy|z
is defined on three distinct leaves
x,y,z
, every

xy\in\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{S}}
satisfies
x\neq y
. Hence, if all triples in
\mathcal{R}
are displayed by

G
, then
\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{R}}}
contains precisely those elements
ab
for which there is some
triple in
\mathcal{R}
enforcing
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
to be well-defined in
G
. This motivates to define a
restriction
\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}}
of
\mathcal{F}
for a pair
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
of triple sets as follows

\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}}\coloneqq\{xy|z\in\mathcal{F}\ \,:\,xy,xz,yz\in\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{R}}\}.

Thus, the set
\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}}
contains all triples
xy|z\in\mathcal{F}
for which
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
, and

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
must be well-defined in any DAG
G
that displays all triples in
\mathcal{R}
and, in
particular, in any DAG
G
that agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
. Consequently, if
ab\in\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{R}}
and, thus,
a\neq b
, then
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
does not need to be
well-defined in a DAG
G
that agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
. In this case, we can apply an
ab
-extension
to
G
to obtain
G^{\prime}
for which
|\operatorname{LCA}_{G^{\prime}}(ab)|>1
holds (cf. Observation
2.5
). This
observation, in turn, motivates the following definition, that we need in the upcoming results.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
be a pair of triple sets. Given a DAG
G
on
X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}
, the
\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}}
-extension of

G
is obtained from
G
by stepwise application of
xy
-extensions for all
xy\in\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{R}}
.

The definition of the
\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}}
-extension is similar to that of the
F_{|R}
-extension
(cf. Definition
2.6
). In particular, suppose that
R
and
F
are relations on

\mathbb{P}_{2}(X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}})
such that
\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{R}}=\operatorname{supp}_{R}
and
\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{F}}=\operatorname{supp}_{F}
. Since
\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{F}}

contains no elements
xx
, the set
\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{R}}
coincides with

\operatorname{supp}_{F}\setminus\operatorname{supp}_{R}^{+}
. Hence, in this situation, the
\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}}
-extension of a DAG
coincides with its
F_{|R}
-extension.

The next result shows that, for the purpose of deciding whether a DAG exists
that agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
, it suffices to consider the restricted
forbidden set
\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}}
instead of
\mathcal{F}
.

Proposition 5.8
.

For every pair
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
of triple sets and every DAG
G
on
X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}
, the following statements
hold.

(1)

If
G
agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
, then
G
agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}})
.

(2)

If
G
agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}})
, then the
\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}}
-extension of
G
agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
.

Thus, there exists a DAG that agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
if and only if there exists a DAG that agrees
with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}})
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
be a pair of triple sets and
G
be a DAG on
X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}
. Clearly, if
G
agrees with

(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
, then
G
agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}})
, since
\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
, which proves
Statement (1).

Suppose now that
G
agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}})
, and let
H
be the
\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}}
-extension of
G
. This
extension
H
is obtained by applying
pq
-extensions for all pairs

pq\in\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{R}}
. Hence, for every
ab\in\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{R}}
, no
ab
-extension is
applied. By Observation
2.5
, each such extension preserves the set of LCAs of
every pair in
\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{R}}
and preserves all ancestor relations among the vertices of the previous
DAG. Consequently, after all extensions have been applied, we have
\operatorname{LCA}_{H}(ab)=\operatorname{LCA}_{G}(ab)
for all

ab\in\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{R}}
, and the ancestor relations among the vertices of
G
are the same in
G
and in

H
. Hence, every triple in
\mathcal{R}
remains displayed by
H
. Moreover, since every triple in
\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}}

involves only pairs from
\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{R}}
, every triple in
\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}}
remains not displayed by
H
. It
remains to consider a triple
xy|z\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}}
. By definition of
\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}}
, at least one of
the three pairs
xy,xz,yz
lies in
\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{R}}
. For this pair, say
pq
, a

pq
-extension was applied to obtain
H
. Hence, by Observation
2.5
,

\operatorname{lca}_{H}(pq)
is not well-defined. Consequently, at least one of
\operatorname{lca}_{H}(xy)
,
\operatorname{lca}_{H}(xz)
, and

\operatorname{lca}_{H}(yz)
is not well-defined, and so
H
does not display
xy|z
. Thus,
H
agrees with

(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
. This proves Statement (2).

Statement (1) and (2) together imply that there exists a DAG that agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
if and
only if there exists a DAG that agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}})
.
∎

Remark 2
.

From now on, unless explicitly stated otherwise, all relations considered in this section are
relations on
\mathbb{P}_{2}(X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}})
.

To recall, we have defined

R_{\mathcal{R}}\coloneqq\{(xy,xz),(xy,yz)\,:\,xy|z\in\mathcal{R}\}.

Hence, for
\mathcal{R}=\{xy|z\}
, we have

R_{\{xy|z\}}=\{(xy,xz),(xy,yz)\}.

We now consider the following extended relation associated with
xy|z
:

R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}\coloneqq R_{\{xy|z\}}\cup\{(xz,yz),(yz,xz)\}=\{(xy,xz),(xy,yz),(xz,yz),(yz,xz)\}.

The relation
R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}
encodes precisely the LCA-constraints imposed by the triple
xy|z
.
Indeed, if a DAG
G
displays
xy|z
, then
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
. The strict
inequalities
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
are represented by the
pairs
(xy,xz)
and
(xy,yz)
, respectively, while the equality
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
is
represented by the two pairs
(xz,yz)
and
(yz,xz)
in
R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}
.

We define now a particular restriction of a given relation
R
on
\mathbb{P}_{2}(X)
to three elements of

X
.

Definition 5.9
.

Given a relation
R
on
\mathbb{P}_{2}(X)
and three distinct elements
x,y,z

\in X
, we define the

restriction of
R
to
x,y,z
as

R|^{\neq}_{xyz}\coloneqq R\cap\{(xy,xz),(xy,yz),(xz,xy),(xz,yz),(yz,xy),(yz,xz)\}.

Since
x,y,z
are pairwise distinct, the restriction
R|^{\neq}_{xyz}
only records comparisons between the
three distinct pairs
xy
,
xz
, and
yz
. In particular, elements involving repeated pairs, such as

(xy,xy)
, as well as elements involving repeated leaves, such as
(xx,xy)
or
(zz,zx)
, do not
occur in
R|^{\neq}_{xyz}
. We next relate this restricted relation to the relation associated with a single
triple.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
xy|z
be a triple and
G
be a DAG with leaves
x,y
, and
z
. Then,
G
displays
xy|z
if and
only if
\trianglelefteq_{G}\mid^{\neq}_{xyz}=R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a DAG on
X
and
xy|z
a triple with
x,y,z\in X
. Since, by definition,
x,y,z
are
pairwise distinct and
\trianglelefteq_{G}
is a relation on
\mathbb{P}_{2}(X)
, it follows that
\trianglelefteq_{G}\mid^{\neq}_{xyz}
is
well-defined. Suppose
G
displays
xy|z
, then
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
, and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
are
well-defined and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
holds. Thus,

(xy,xz),(xy,yz),(xz,yz),(yz,xz)\in\trianglelefteq_{G}
and
(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\notin\trianglelefteq_{G}
holds. This implies

\trianglelefteq_{G}\mid^{\neq}_{xyz}=\{(xy,xz),(xy,yz),(xz,yz),(yz,xz)\}=R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}
. Now suppose that

\trianglelefteq_{G}\mid^{\neq}_{xyz}=R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}
. Since
(xy,xz),(xy,yz)\in\trianglelefteq_{G}
, we conclude that

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz),
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
are well-defined. Moreover,
(xy,xz)\in\trianglelefteq_{G}
implies

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
and
(xz,xy)\notin\trianglelefteq_{G}
implies
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)\npreceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)

and, therefore,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
. Moreover,
(xz,yz),(yz,xz)\in\trianglelefteq_{G}
imply

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
. In summary,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
holds. Hence,

xy|z
is displayed by
G
.
∎

In what follows, we consider pairs
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
of triple sets and want to determine if there exists
a DAG that agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
. To this end, we will saturate
R_{\mathcal{R}}
using the following
procedure.

Definition 5.11
.

For a pair
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
of triple sets, we define the following procedure:

Saturate
R_{\mathcal{R}}
with
\mathcal{F}
(in short
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
)

Q_{0}\coloneqq\operatorname{cl}(R_{\mathcal{R}})

\mathcal{F}_{0}\coloneqq\left\{xy|z\in\mathcal{F}\,:\,Q_{0}|^{\neq}_{xyz}=R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}\right\}

i\coloneqq 0

while

\mathcal{F}_{i}\neq\emptyset

do

Let
xy|z
be an arbitrary element in
\mathcal{F}_{i}

Q_{i+1}\coloneqq\operatorname{cl}\left(Q_{i}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\}\right)

\mathcal{F}_{i+1}\coloneqq\left\{xy|z\in\mathcal{F}\,:\,Q_{i+1}|^{\neq}_{xyz}=R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}\right\}

i\coloneqq i+1

return

Q_{i}

To provide some intuition behind the greedy-like method
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
, let us consider

Q_{0}=\operatorname{cl}(R_{\mathcal{R}})
. Clearly, if
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\emptyset
, then
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
stops and we
still have
Q_{0}=\operatorname{cl}(R_{\mathcal{R}})
. Suppose now that
\mathcal{F}_{0}\neq\emptyset
. In this case,
Q_{1}
is
obtained from
Q_{0}
by adding the two elements
(xz,xy)
and
(yz,xy)
for some triple
xy|z\in\mathcal{F}

with
Q_{0}|^{\neq}_{xyz}=R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}
. The purpose of adding these pairs is to ensure that
xy|z
is
not displayed by any DAG realizing the updated relation
Q_{1}
. Indeed, if
G
realizes
Q_{1}
, then

(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\in Q_{1}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
(cf.
[
24
, Lem 7]
). Consequently,

\trianglelefteq_{G}\mid^{\neq}_{xyz}\neq R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}
and, hence,
G
does not display
xy|z
by
Lemma
5.10
. This is exactly what is required, since
xy|z\in\mathcal{F}
is a
forbidden triple. After adding the two elements to
Q_{0}
, we compute the closure in order to obtain
all further LCA-constraints implied by the constraints in
Q_{0}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\}
that must be
respected by any DAG realizing
Q_{0}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\}
.

As we shall see, this eventually yields a final relation
Q_{K}
that is returned by

Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
and for which the canonical DAG
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
displays all triples in
\mathcal{R}

precisely if there is some DAG that agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
, see Theorem
5.15
.
In particular, this in turn can be used to prove that the Problem
TC-F
can be solved in
polynomial time, see Theorem
5.17
. The latter ideas are also illustrated in
Figure
5
.

Note that the constructed relations

depend on the chosen arbitrary element of

\mathcal{F}_{i}
. Nevertheless, the following results hold for each order in which those elements are chosen.
Indeed, we now show that

R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
always terminates and provide some additional

.

Lemma 5.12
.

For every pair
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
of triple sets,
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
terminates. Let

Q_{0},\dots,Q_{K}
be the constructed relations during some run of
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
. Then,
it holds that
Q_{i}=\operatorname{tc}(Q_{i})=\operatorname{cl}(Q_{i})
for all
i\in\{0,\dots,K\}
and
Q_{i}\subseteq Q_{i+1}
for
all
i\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}
. Moreover,
Q_{i}
is a realizable relation for all
i\in\{0,\dots,K\}
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
be a pair of triple sets. We first argue that
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)

terminates. By construction and the monotonicity of the closure operator
\operatorname{cl}
, it holds that
Q_{i}\subseteq Q_{i+1}
in each step of
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
. In particular, if
xy|z\in\mathcal{F}_{i}

is the currently considered triple used to compute
Q_{i+1}=\operatorname{cl}\left(Q_{i}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\}\right)
, then
(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\in Q_{i+1}
which together with
(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\notin R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}
implies that
Q_{j}|^{\neq}_{xyz}\neq R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}
for all
j>i
. This, in turn,
implies that
xy|z\notin\mathcal{F}_{j}
for all
j>i
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is finite, and
\mathcal{F}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{F}

for all
i
,
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
terminates after at most
|\mathcal{F}|
iterations of the

while
-loop of
Sat
(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
).

We continue with showing that
Q_{i}\subseteq Q_{i+1}
for each
0\leq i<K
and that
Q_{i}=\operatorname{tc}(Q_{i})=\operatorname{cl}(Q_{i})
for each
0\leq i\leq K
. For
0\leq i<K
, we have, by monotonicity of
\operatorname{cl}
and
definition of
Q_{i+1}
, that
Q_{i}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(Q_{i}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\})=Q_{i+1}
. Moreover,
idempotency of
\operatorname{cl}
implies for
Q_{0}=\operatorname{cl}(R_{\mathcal{R}})
that
\operatorname{cl}(Q_{0})=\operatorname{cl}(\operatorname{cl}(R_{\mathcal{R}}))=\operatorname{cl}(R_{\mathcal{R}})=Q_{0}
and for
Q_{i}=\operatorname{cl}(Q_{i-1}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\})
that
\operatorname{cl}(Q_{i})=\operatorname{cl}(\operatorname{cl}(Q_{i-1}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\}))=\operatorname{cl}(Q_{i-1}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\})=Q_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq K
. This together
with extensivity of
\operatorname{tc}
and transitivity of
\operatorname{cl}(Q_{i})
implies that
Q_{i}\subseteq\operatorname{tc}(Q_{i})=\operatorname{tc}(\operatorname{cl}(Q_{i}))=\operatorname{cl}(Q_{i})=Q_{i}
and, therefore,
Q_{i}=\operatorname{tc}(Q_{i})=\operatorname{cl}(Q_{i})
for all
0\leq i\leq K
.

It remains to show that
Q_{i}
is realizable for all
0\leq i\leq K
. We proceed by induction on

i
and start with
i=0
. By definition,
Q_{0}=\operatorname{cl}(R_{\mathcal{R}})
and, for every triple
xy|z\in\mathcal{R}
,
the leaves
x,y,z
are pairwise distinct. Thus, for all
a,b,x\in X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}
with
ab\neq xx
, it holds
that
(ab,xx)\notin R_{\mathcal{R}}
. By definition,
R_{\mathcal{R}}
satisfies
(X1)
. This together with

[
24
, Prop 28]
implies that
\operatorname{cl}(R_{\mathcal{R}})
and, therefore,
Q_{0}=\operatorname{cl}(R_{\mathcal{R}})
is realizable.
Now assume that there exist some
0\leq k<K
such that
Q_{k}
is realizable. Consider

Q_{k+1}\coloneqq\operatorname{cl}\left(Q_{k}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\}\right)
, which exists since
k<K
. Since

Q_{k}
is realizable, Theorem
3.7
implies that
Q_{k}
satisfies
(X1)
. Hence, the set

Q_{k}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\}
does, by construction, satisfy
(X1)
. This together with

[
24
, Prop 28]
implies that
\operatorname{cl}(Q_{k}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\})=Q_{k+1}
is realizable,
which completes this proof.
∎

Remark 3
.

By Lemma
5.12
,
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
always terminates and,
thus, there is one last call of the
while
-loop in which it is verified that

\mathcal{F}_{i}=\emptyset
and the algorithm stops. Hence, there are
K+1\geq 1
calls of the

while
-loop condition
\mathcal{F}_{i}\neq\emptyset
and we assume, from here on, that

Q_{0},\dots,Q_{K}
are the constructed relations during some run of
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
.
Moreover, we call
Q_{K}
a
final relation
of
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
.

Recall that the choice of the triple
xy|z\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
in each iteration may affect the final relation

Q_{K}
returned by
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
. In particular, for different runs of

Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
, the number of iterations may differ, and it remains open whether the
final relation
Q_{K}
of one run always coincides with the final relation
Q_{L}
of another run.

The following lemma establishes the soundness of the saturation procedure: if a DAG
G
agrees with

(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
, then every relation
Q_{i}
constructed during some run of
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
is
already contained in the LCA-relation
\trianglelefteq_{G}
of
G
.

Lemma 5.13
.

Assume that
G
is a DAG that agrees with the pair
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
of triple sets. Then, it holds that

Q_{i}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
for all relations
Q_{0},\dots,Q_{K}
constructed during some run of

Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a DAG that agrees with the pair
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
of triple sets. Let
Q_{0},\dots,Q_{K}
be the
relations constructed during some run of
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
. We proceed by induction on

i\in\{0,\dots,K\}
. For the base case, let
i=0
. By definition,
Q_{0}\coloneqq\operatorname{cl}(R_{\mathcal{R}})
. Since

G
displays every triple in
\mathcal{R}
, Theorem
5.2
implies that
G
realizes
R_{\mathcal{R}}
.
By
[
24
, Lem 7]
,
R_{\mathcal{R}}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
follows. Moreover,
\operatorname{cl}(\trianglelefteq_{G})=\trianglelefteq_{G}
holds
by
[
24
, Prop 21]
. The latter two arguments together with monotonicity of
\operatorname{cl}
imply
that
Q_{0}=\operatorname{cl}(R_{\mathcal{R}})\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(\trianglelefteq_{G})=\trianglelefteq_{G}
.

Assume now that for some
i\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}
it holds that
Q_{i}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
. To recall,

R|^{\neq}_{xyz}=R\cap\{(xy,xz),(xy,yz),(xz,xy),(xz,yz),(yz,xy),(yz,xz)\}\text{ for any relation }R

while
R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}=\{(xy,xz),(xy,yz),(xz,yz),(yz,xz)\}
. Consider now
Q_{i+1}=\operatorname{cl}\left(Q_{i}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\}\right)
. Since
i<K
, the relation
Q_{i+1}
exists and has, in
particular, been constructed using some triple
xy|z\in\mathcal{F}
for which

Q_{i}|^{\neq}_{xyz}=R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}
. Since
Q_{i}|^{\neq}_{xyz}=R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}
, it holds that

R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}\subseteq Q_{i}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
. In particular, the three LCAs
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
, and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
are well-defined and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)

must hold. However, as
G
agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
, the triple
xy|z\in\mathcal{F}
is not displayed by

G
. Hence, we obtain
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
. Thus,
(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\in\trianglelefteq_{G}

holds. This together with
Q_{i}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
implies that
Q_{i}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
. By
[
24
, Prop 21]
,
\operatorname{cl}(\trianglelefteq_{G})=\trianglelefteq_{G}
holds. The latter two
arguments together with monotonicity of
\operatorname{cl}
imply that

Q_{i+1}=\operatorname{cl}(Q_{i}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\})\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(\trianglelefteq_{G})=\trianglelefteq_{G}
. In summary, we verified that

Q_{i+1}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
. The full statement follows by induction.
∎

We next record a non-trivial consequence of Lemma
5.12
and

5.13
. In particular,
if a DAG agreeing with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
exists,
the canonical DAG associated with
Q_{i}
still realizes the relation
R_{\mathcal{R}}
induced by the
required triple set
\mathcal{R}
.

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
be a pair of triple sets and
Q_{0},\dots,Q_{K}
be the relations constructed during
some run of
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
. If there is a DAG that agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
, then the
canonical DAG
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{i}}
realizes
R_{\mathcal{R}}
for all
i\in\{0,\dots,K\}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a DAG that agrees with the pair
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
of triple sets. Let
Q_{0},\dots,Q_{K}
be the
relations constructed during some run of
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
.

We show that
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{i}}
realizes
R_{\mathcal{R}}
for all
i\in\{0,\dots,K\}
. To this
end, let
i\in\{0,\dots,K\}
be chosen arbitrarily. Observe first that transitivity of
\operatorname{cl}

implies that
\operatorname{tc}(R_{\mathcal{R}})\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(R_{\mathcal{R}})=Q_{0}
. Moreover, by
Lemma
5.12
,
Q_{0}\subseteq Q_{i}=\operatorname{tc}(Q_{i})
holds and, hence,
\operatorname{tc}(R_{\mathcal{R}})\subseteq Q_{i}=\operatorname{tc}(Q_{i})
. To verify that
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{i}}
realizes
R_{\mathcal{R}}
, we show that
(I1)
and

(I2)
hold for
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{i}}
and
R_{\mathcal{R}}
. We start with
(I2)
. Hence, suppose
(ab,cd)\in R_{\mathcal{R}}\subseteq Q_{i}
and
(cd,ab)\in\operatorname{tc}(R_{\mathcal{R}})\subseteq\operatorname{tc}(Q_{i})
.
Lemma
5.12
implies that
Q_{i}
is realizable and by
Theorem
3.7
, the canonical DAG
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{i}}
realizes
Q_{i}
. Hence,
\operatorname{lca}_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{i}}}(ab)=\operatorname{lca}_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{i}}}(cd)
must hold and, in particular,
(I2)
is satisfied for
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{i}}
and

R_{\mathcal{R}}
. For
(I1)
, let
(ab,cd)\in R_{\mathcal{R}}\subseteq Q_{i}
and
(cd,ab)\notin\operatorname{tc}(R_{\mathcal{R}})
. Assume,
for contradiction, that
(cd,ab)\in\operatorname{tc}(Q_{i})
. In this case, Lemma
5.13
implies
that
\operatorname{tc}(Q_{i})=Q_{i}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
and, therefore,
(ab,cd),(cd,ab)\in\trianglelefteq_{G}
. In other words,

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(cd)
, which implies that
G
does not realize
R_{\mathcal{R}}
. However,
G
displays all
triples in
\mathcal{R}
and, by Theorem
5.2
, realizes
R_{\mathcal{R}}
; a contradiction. Hence,

(cd,ab)\notin\operatorname{tc}(Q_{i})
. Since
Q_{i}
is realized by
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{i}}
and since
(ab,cd)\in Q_{i}
and

(cd,ab)\notin\operatorname{tc}(Q_{i})
, it holds that
\operatorname{lca}_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{i}}}(ab)\prec_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{i}}}\operatorname{lca}_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{i}}}(cd)
. Therefore,
(I1)
is satisfied for
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{i}}
and
R_{\mathcal{R}}
. In summary,

\mathscr{G}_{Q_{i}}
realizes
R_{\mathcal{R}}
.
∎

We now combine the preceding ingredients to characterize when a pair
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
of required and
forbidden triple sets agrees with some DAG. The theorem shows, in particular, that the final relation

Q_{K}
produced by the saturation procedure is sufficient to decide whether a DAG agreeing with

(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
exists.

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
be a pair of triple sets and
Q_{K}
be the final relation constructed during some run
of
Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
. Then, the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

There exists a DAG on
X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}
that agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
.

(2)

The canonical DAG
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
displays all triples in
\mathcal{R}
.

(3)

The canonical DAG
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
realizes
R_{\mathcal{R}}
and
(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\notin\operatorname{tc}(R_{\mathcal{R}})
for
all
xy|z\in\mathcal{R}
.

(4)

(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\notin Q_{K}
for all
xy|z\in\mathcal{R}
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
be a pair of triple sets,
Q_{K}
be the final relation constructed during some run of

Sat(
R_{\mathcal{R}},\mathcal{F}
)
and put
\mathscr{G}\coloneqq\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
. We prove this theorem by verifying
the following implications:
(1)\Rightarrow(4)\Rightarrow(3)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(1)
.

Suppose that Statement (1) holds. Hence, there exists a DAG
G
on
X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}
that agrees with

(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
.
By Lemma
5.13
, we have

6
Consistency Problems with Additional LCA-Constraints

In the previous section, a triple
xy|z
was considered not to be displayed by a DAG
G
whenever at
least one of the conditions required for display fails. In particular, if at least one of

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)

This convention allowed us to use

-extensions were used in
Section

to exclude forbidden anchored triples via non-well-defined relevant LCAs.

One may, however, want to impose a stronger interpretation of forbidden anchored or rooted triples:
a forbidden triple should fail to be displayed because the relative positions of the relevant LCAs
do not satisfy the corresponding display condition, and not merely because one of the underlying
LCAs is not well-defined. This motivates variants in which certain LCAs, possibly associated with
forbidden anchored or rooted triples, or even all LCAs, are required to be well-defined.

In the following, we first discuss the problems for anchored triples in Section
6.1
,
before turning to “ordinary” rooted triples in Section
6.2
.

(1)

\mathscr{W}
-

-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})

(2)

There exists a DAG that
\mathscr{W}^{\prime}
-

-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathscr{W}
, and
\mathscr{W}^{\prime}
be as stated. Clearly, Statement (2) implies

\mathscr{W}\subseteq\mathscr{W^{\prime}}

\mathscr{W}
-

-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
. By Theorem

, we

is a phylogenetic network. In particular,
\operatorname{LCA}_{G}(xy)\neq\emptyset

We now construct a DAG

that
\mathscr{W}^{\prime}
-

-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
. For that, we want

, but the

and

\mathscr{W}

We now verify

. Let
(p,q)\in R
and thus,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(p)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(q)
.
If
(q,p)\in\operatorname{tc}(R)
, then there is a chain
q=p_{0}\;R\;p_{1}\;R\;\dots\;R\;p_{k}=p
. Since for all

(p_{i},p_{i+1})\in R
it holds
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(p_{i})\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(p_{i+1})
, transitivity of
\preceq_{G}

implies that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(q)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(p)
and, consequently,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(p)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(q)
, and so

(I2)
holds. Assume that
(q,p)\notin\operatorname{tc}(R)
. Hence,
p\neq q
. This and
(p,q)\in R
implies
that
(p,q)\in R_{\mathcal{R}}
. Hence,
(p,q)
comes from some displayed triple
xy|z\in\mathcal{R}
, i.e.,

(p,q)=(xy,xz)
or
(p,q)=(xy,yz)
. Since
G
displays
xy|z
, we obtain
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(p)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(q)
and
(I1)
holds. In summary,
G
realizes
R
.

It remains to show that
(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\notin\operatorname{tc}(R)
for every
xy|z\in\mathcal{R}
. Suppose, for
contradiction, that
(xz,xy)\in\operatorname{tc}(R)
for some
xy|z\in\mathcal{R}
. By definition, we have
(xy,xz)\in R_{\mathcal{R}}\subseteq R
. As
G
realizes
R
,
(I2)
would imply
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
,
contradicting the fact that
G
displays
xy|z
. Hence
(xz,xy)\notin\operatorname{tc}(R)
. The argument for

(yz,xy)\notin\operatorname{tc}(R)
is analogous. We have therefore shown that Statement (1) implies Statement
(2).

Conversely, assume that
G
realizes
R
and that
(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\notin\operatorname{tc}(R)
for every

xy|z\in\mathcal{R}
. Since
(ab,ab)\in R
for every
ab\in\mathscr{W}
, realizability of
R
implies that

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
is well-defined for all
ab\in\mathscr{W}
.

Now let
xy|z\in\mathcal{R}
. By definition,
(xy,xz),(xy,yz)\in R_{\mathcal{R}}\subseteq R
. Since

(xz,xy)\notin\operatorname{tc}(R)
and
(yz,xy)\notin\operatorname{tc}(R)
,
(I1)
gives
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
and

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
. In particular, the LCAs
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
, and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)

are well-defined. Applying Lemma
2.1
to
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
yields

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
. Similarly, applying Lemma
2.1
to

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
yields
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
. Thus,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
holds. Together with
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
, this shows that
G

displays
xy|z
. Since this holds for every
xy|z\in\mathcal{R}
, Statement (2) implies Statement (1) and
the proof is complete.
∎

We now return to the setting with forbidden triples and provide the
following strengthened notion of agreement.

Definition 6.9
.

Let
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
be a pair of triple sets and let
\mathscr{W}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{2}(X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}})
. A DAG
G

\mathscr{W}
-agrees
with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
if
G
agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})

and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
is well-defined for every
ab\in\mathscr{W}
.

The next result is the analogue of Theorem
5.16
for
\mathscr{W}
-
TC-F
. On a technical note, the auxiliary relation
F
used in this result is introduced
only to specify the
F_{|R}
-extension of the canonical DAG
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
but not to construct

\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
.

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
be a pair of triple sets and let
\mathscr{W}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{2}(X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}})
. Let
R\coloneqq R_{\mathcal{R}}\cup\{(ab,ab)\,:\,ab\in\mathscr{W}\}

and
F=\{(xy,xy),(xz,xz),(yz,yz)\,:\,xy|z\in\mathcal{F}\}

be relations on
\mathbb{P}_{2}(X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}})
.
Let
Q_{K}
be the final relation constructed during some run of
\textsc{Sat}\bigl(R,\mathcal{F}\bigr)
.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a DAG on
X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}
that
\mathscr{W}
-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
.

(2)

The
F_{|R}
-extension of the canonical DAG
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
is phylogenetic and
\mathscr{W}
-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
.

(3)

There exists a phylogenetic network on
X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}
that
\mathscr{W}
-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
.

(4)

The network obtained from the
F_{|R}
-extension of
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
by
Lemma
2.4
is phylogenetic and
\mathscr{W}
-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
.

In particular,
\mathscr{W}
-
TC-F
can be solved in polynomial time in
|X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}|
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathscr{W}
,
R
,
F
, and
Q_{K}
be as stated.
By the same arguments as used in in the proof of Lemma
5.12
,

\textsc{Sat}\bigl(R,\mathcal{F}\bigr)
terminates and thus, the final relation
Q_{K}
is well-defined.
Clearly, Statement (3) implies Statement (1),
since every phylogenetic network is, in particular, a DAG. We now argue that
(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(4)\Rightarrow(3)
. We note in passing that the proof follows, in large parts, the
same ideas as the proof of Theorem
5.15
. The main difference is that we now
also have to account for the additional constraints
(ab,ab)\in R
for all
ab\in\mathscr{W}
where

R
is the relation used as input for the saturation procedure that constructs
Q_{K}
.

Assume first that Statement (1) holds, and let
G
be a DAG on
X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}
that
\mathscr{W}
-agrees with

(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
. By Lemma
6.8
,
G
realizes
R
and, for every
xy|z\in\mathcal{R}
,
we have
(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\notin\operatorname{tc}(R)
. We first show that every relation
Q_{0},\dots,Q_{K}

constructed during the saturation procedure is contained in
\trianglelefteq_{G}
. For
i=0
, we have

R\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
, since
G
realizes
R
and by
[
24
, Lem 7]
. Hence,

Q_{0}=\operatorname{cl}(R)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(\trianglelefteq_{G})=\trianglelefteq_{G}
holds by monotonicity of
\operatorname{cl}
and

[
24
, Prop 21]
. Now assume that
Q_{i}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
and that the saturation procedure
constructs
Q_{i+1}=\operatorname{cl}\bigl(Q_{i}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\}\bigr)
because we have
Q_{i}|^{\neq}_{xyz}=R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}
for some forbidden triple
xy|z\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
. Since
Q_{i}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
,
this implies
R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}=\{(xy,xz),(xy,yz),(xz,yz),(yz,xz)\}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
. Hence, the
LCAs
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
, and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
are well-defined and satisfy
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
. Since
G
does not display the forbidden triple
xy|z
, it cannot hold that

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
. Therefore, we have
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
.
Consequently,
(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\in\trianglelefteq_{G}
and together with
Q_{i}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
, we obtain
Q_{i+1}=\operatorname{cl}\bigl(Q_{i}\cup\{(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\}\bigr)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}(\trianglelefteq_{G})=\trianglelefteq_{G}
. By induction,

Q_{i}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
holds for all
i
,
0\leq i\leq K
.

We now show that
Q_{K}
is realizable. Note that by idempotency of
\operatorname{cl}
, every
Q_{i}
is closed, that
is,
Q_{i}=\operatorname{cl}(Q_{i})
. Additionally,
Q_{i}=\operatorname{tc}(Q_{i})=\operatorname{cl}(Q_{i})
holds for all
0\leq i\leq K
by
extensivity of
\operatorname{tc}
and transitivity of
\operatorname{cl}
. To argue that Condition
(X1)
holds for
Q_{K}
,
suppose, for contradiction, that
(X1)
fails for
Q_{K}
. Then there are
a,b,x\in X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}
with

ab\neq xx
such that
(ab,xx)\in Q_{K}
. Since
Q_{K}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
, this implies
(ab,xx)\in\trianglelefteq_{G}
.
Thus,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xx)
are well-defined and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xx)=x
. Since

x
is a leaf,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(ab)=x
and
a=b=x
and, thus,
ab=xx
holds; contradicting our assumption

ab\neq xx
. Therefore,
(X1)
holds for
Q_{K}
. Since
Q_{K}=\operatorname{cl}(Q_{K})
and
Q_{K}
satisfies

(X1)
, it follows from
[
24
, Cor 32]
that
Q_{K}
is realizable. Consequently, by
Theorem
3.7
, the canonical DAG
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
realizes
Q_{K}
.

We next show that
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
displays all triples in
\mathcal{R}
and that all LCAs required by

\mathscr{W}
are well-defined. Since
(ab,ab)\in R\subseteq Q_{K}
for all
ab\in\mathscr{W}
and

Q_{K}
is realized by
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
, each
\operatorname{lca}_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}}(ab)
for
ab\in\mathscr{W}
is
well-defined. It remains to prove that all triples in
\mathcal{R}
are displayed by
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
. Let

xy|z\in\mathcal{R}
. We first show that
(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\notin\operatorname{tc}(Q_{K})
. Suppose, for contradiction, that

(xz,xy)\in\operatorname{tc}(Q_{K})
. Since
Q_{K}=\operatorname{tc}(Q_{K})
, we have
(xz,xy)\in Q_{K}
. Since
Q_{K}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
,
we obtain
(xz,xy)\in\trianglelefteq_{G}
together with
(xy,xz)\in R\subseteq Q_{K}\subseteq\trianglelefteq_{G}
. Hence,

(xy,xz),(xz,xy)\in\trianglelefteq_{G}
and, by definition of
\trianglelefteq_{G}
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)\preceq_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
must hold, which implies that
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)=\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
. This
contradicts the fact that
G
displays
xy|z
, which requires
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\prec_{G}\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
.
Therefore,
(xz,xy)\notin\operatorname{tc}(Q_{K})
must hold. The proof that
(yz,xy)\notin\operatorname{tc}(Q_{K})
is analogous.
Since
(xy,xz),(xy,yz)\in R_{\mathcal{R}}\subseteq R\subseteq Q_{K}
and
(xz,xy),(yz,xy)\notin\operatorname{tc}(Q_{K})
and
since
Q_{K}
is realized by
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
,
(I1)
implies that

\operatorname{lca}_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}}(xy)\prec_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}}\operatorname{lca}_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}}(xz)
and

\operatorname{lca}_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}}(xy)\prec_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}}\operatorname{lca}_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}}(yz)
. By Lemma
2.1
, applied
twice, we then get
\operatorname{lca}_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}}(xz)=\operatorname{lca}_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}}(yz)
and, hence,
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
displays

xy|z
.

We continue by showing that
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
displays none of the triples in
\mathcal{F}^{\circ}\coloneqq\{xy|z\in\mathcal{F}\,:\,xy,xz,yz\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}\}
. Let
xy|z\in\mathcal{F}^{\circ}
and assume, for contradiction,
that
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
displays
xy|z
. By Lemma
5.10
, we have
\trianglelefteq_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}}\mid^{\neq}_{xyz}=R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}
. Since
Q_{K}
is closed and realized by its canonical DAG

\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
,
[
24
, Thm 47]
implies
Q_{K}=\trianglelefteq_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}}\cap\bigl(\operatorname{supp}_{Q_{K}}^{+}\times\operatorname{supp}_{Q_{K}}^{+}\bigr)
. Since
R\subseteq Q_{K}
, where both
R
and

Q_{K}
are relations on
\mathbb{P}_{2}(X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}})
, it is easily seen that
\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{Q_{K}}^{+}
. Hence, in particular,
xy,xz,yz\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{Q_{K}}^{+}
. Consequently, the previous arguments taken together imply

Q_{K}|^{\neq}_{xyz}=\trianglelefteq_{\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}}\mid^{\neq}_{xyz}=R^{\textup{ext}}_{\{xy|z\}}.
But then
xy|z
would belong to the set of
forbidden triples selected by the final saturation step. This contradicts the fact that
Q_{K}
is the
final relation. Thus
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
does not display
xy|z
. Since
xy|z\in\mathcal{F}^{\circ}
was arbitrary,

\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
displays no triple in
\mathcal{F}^{\circ}
. In summary,
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}

\mathscr{W}
-agrees with

(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}^{\circ})
.

It remains to show that the
F_{|R}
-extension
\tilde{G}
of
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}

\mathscr{W}
-agrees with

(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
. Recall that to obtain
\tilde{G}
, we apply an
ab
-extension to
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
for every

ab\in\operatorname{supp}_{F}\setminus\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
. Thus, for every triple
xy|z\in\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{F}^{\circ}
, no

xy
-,
xz
-, or
yz
-extension was applied to obtain
\tilde{G}
. This together with
Observation
2.5
implies that
\tilde{G}

\mathscr{W}
-agrees with

(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}^{\circ})
. Moreover, for every triple
xy|z\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{\circ}
, at least one
element
xy,xz,yz
is not contained in
\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R}
. Hence, an
xy
-,
xz
-, or
yz
-extension was
applied. By Observation
2.5
, the corresponding LCA is not well-defined and,
thus,
xy|z
is not displayed by
\tilde{G}
. Consequently,
\tilde{G}

\mathscr{W}
-agrees with

(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
. Lastly, by Proposition
3.6
and the fact that
Q_{K}

is realizable,
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
is phylogenetic and by construction,
\tilde{G}
is phylogenetic. Thus,
Statement (2) holds.

We continue by showing that Statement (2) implies Statement (4). Assume that the
F_{|R}
-extension
G

of
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
is phylogenetic and
\mathscr{W}
-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
. By
Observation
2.5
,
G
is a DAG on
X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}
, as
Q_{K}
is a relation on

\mathbb{P}_{2}(X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}})
. Let
N
be the network obtained from
G
by Lemma
2.4
. By this
lemma,
N
is a phylogenetic network on
X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}
, the ancestor relation among the vertices of
G
is
preserved in
N
, and every nonempty LCA set in
G
is preserved in
N
. Since
G
displays all
triples in
\mathcal{R}
, the relevant LCAs are well-defined in
G
and hence remain well-defined and
unchanged in
N
. Therefore, all triples in
\mathcal{R}
are displayed by
N
. Similarly,
\operatorname{lca}_{N}(ab)
is
well-defined for every
ab\in\mathscr{W}
.

It remains to show that no triple in
\mathcal{F}
is displayed by
N
. Reusing the notation
\mathcal{F}^{\circ}
of
a previous paragraph, consider first
xy|z\in\mathcal{F}^{\circ}
. Then
xy,xz,yz\in\operatorname{supp}_{R}^{+}
. Then,
xy\in\operatorname{supp}^{+}_{R_{\mathcal{R}}}
or
xy\in\mathscr{W}
. Since
G

\mathscr{W}
-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
, the LCA

\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
is well-defined in both cases. By a similar argument,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(yz)
are
also well-defined. Thus, since
G
does not display
xy|z
, the display condition must fail in
G
.
In
N
, all relevant LCAs are unchanged and the ancestor relation among vertices of
G
is
preserved, so the display condition still fails in
N
. Thus
N
does not display any triple in

\mathcal{F}^{\circ}
. Now let
xy|z\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{\circ}
. Then at least one pair
ab\in{xy,xz,yz}

belongs to
\operatorname{supp}_{F}\setminus\operatorname{supp}_{R}^{+}
, and hence an
ab
-extension was applied when
constructing
G
. By Observation
2.5
, this gives
|\operatorname{LCA}_{G}(ab)|\geq 2
. Since

\operatorname{LCA}_{G}(ab)\neq\emptyset
, Lemma
2.4
yields
\operatorname{LCA}_{N}(ab)=\operatorname{LCA}_{G}(ab)
. Thus

\operatorname{lca}_{N}(ab)
is still not well-defined, and so
N
does not display
xy|z
. Hence,
N

\mathscr{W}
-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
, and Statement (4) follows.

Finally, Statement (4) immediately implies Statement (3), since the network described in
Statement (4) is a phylogenetic network on
X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}
that
\mathscr{W}
-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
.

In summary, Statements (1), (2), (3), and (4) are equivalent. Moreover, as in the proof of
Theorem
5.17
, the final relation
Q_{K}
and the canonical DAG
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
can be
computed in polynomial time in
|X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}|
. The
F_{|R}
-extension of
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}
can also be constructed
in polynomial time, and checking whether it is phylogenetic and
\mathscr{W}
-agrees with

(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
is polynomial as well. Hence
\mathscr{W}
-
TC-F
can be solved in polynomial
time in
|X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}|
.
∎

Moreover, for
\mathscr{W}=\emptyset
, the
F_{|R}
-extension of a DAG coincides with the
\mathcal{F}_{|\mathcal{R}}
-extension.
Thus, Theorem
6.10
yields the results for
TC-F
as the special case obtained by taking

\mathscr{W}=\emptyset
(cf. Theorem
5.16
).
Now consider the following two problems.

Problem

(
Strong Triples Consistency with Forbidden Triples (strong-TC-F)
)
.

Input:

Two sets
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{F}
of triples.

Question:

Is there a phylogenetic network (resp., DAG)
G
on
X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}}
that agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})

and for which the LCAs
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)
,
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xz)
, and

(

)
.

Input:

\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{F}
of triples.

Question:

These two variants are just special cases of

whereas

Corollary 6.11
.

The problems

and

can be solved in polynomial time.
Moreover, let
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
be a pair of triple sets and let
\mathscr{W}\in\{\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{P}_{2}(X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}})\}
. Define

,

, and

as in Theorem
6.10
. If

(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
is a yes-instance of the corresponding

-

canonical DAG
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}

\mathscr{W}
-agrees with

Proof.

The polynomial-time solvability of

and
LCA-TC-F
follows directly
from Theorem
6.10
by taking
\mathscr{W}=\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mathscr{W}=\mathbb{P}_{2}(X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}})
,
respectively.

It remains to prove the second assertion. Let
\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{F}
,
R
,
F
, and

be as stated. By

, we have
\operatorname{supp}_{F}=\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{F}}
. For both choices

. Moreover, by definition
of

, we have

for every

, and hence
\mathscr{W}\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{R}^{+}
. Consequently,
\operatorname{supp}_{F}\setminus\operatorname{supp}_{R}^{+}=\emptyset
. Thus, the

-extension of
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}

applies no

therefore, coincides with

. Hence, if
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
is a yes-instance of the corresponding

\mathscr{W}
-

problem, Theorem

implies that
\mathscr{G}_{Q_{K}}

\mathscr{W}
-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
.
∎

Similar to the anchored triple case, we can now also prove that the
strong-TC-F
and

LCA-TC-F
problem are equivalent by proving a slightly stronger statement.

Proposition 6.12
.

Let
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
be a pair of triple sets, let
\mathscr{W}=\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mathscr{W}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{2}(X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}})
such that
\mathscr{W}\subseteq\mathscr{W}^{\prime}
. Then the following statements
are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a DAG that
\mathscr{W}
-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
.

(2)

There exists a DAG that
\mathscr{W}^{\prime}
-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathscr{W}
, and
\mathscr{W}^{\prime}
be as stated.
Clearly, Statement (2) implies Statement (1), since
\mathscr{W}\subseteq\mathscr{W}^{\prime}
.

It remains to show that Statement (1) implies Statement (2).
We use here the same ideas as for the proof of Proposition
6.6
.
Suppose that there exists a DAG
G
that

\mathscr{W}
-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
. By Theorem
6.10
, we may assume that this DAG
is a phylogenetic network. In particular,
\operatorname{LCA}_{G}(xy)\neq\emptyset
for every
xy\in\mathbb{P}_{2}(X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}})
.
We now construct a DAG
H
that
\mathscr{W}^{\prime}
-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
. For that, put

\mathscr{Z}\coloneqq\{xy\in\mathscr{W}^{\prime}\,:\,\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)\text{ is not well-defined}\}
.
We then define the DAG
H
by setting

V(H)\coloneqq V(G)\cup\{u_{xy}\,:\,xy\in\mathscr{Z}\}

and

E(H)\coloneqq E(G)\cup\{(u_{xy},x),(u_{xy},y)\,:\,xy\in\mathscr{Z}\}\cup\{(v,u_{xy})\,:\,xy\in\mathscr{Z}\text{ and }v\in\operatorname{LCA}_{G}(xy)\}.

As outlined in the proof of Proposition
6.6
,
H
remains a
DAG and
a\preceq_{G}b
if and only if
a\preceq_{H}b
for all
a,b\in V(G)
. Moreover,
\operatorname{LCA}_{G}(ab)=\operatorname{LCA}_{H}(ab)
holds for all
ab\in\mathbb{P}_{2}(X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}})\setminus\mathscr{Z}
and
\operatorname{lca}_{H}(xy)=u_{xy}
for
each
xy\in\mathscr{Z}
. Since
G

\mathscr{W}
-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
and
\mathscr{W}=\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{F}}
, all LCAs relevant to the triples in
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{F}
are well-defined in
G
and
therefore, in
H
. In particular, the latter two arguments imply that
H

\mathscr{W}
-agrees with

(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
. This together with
\operatorname{lca}_{H}(xy)=u_{xy}
being well-defined for each
xy\in\mathscr{Z}
and
\operatorname{lca}_{G}(xy)=\operatorname{lca}_{H}(xy)
for all
xy\in\mathscr{W}^{\prime}\setminus\mathscr{Z}
implies that

H

\mathscr{W}^{\prime}
-agrees with
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
.
∎

Applying Proposition
6.12
with

\mathscr{W}=\operatorname{t-supp}_{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mathscr{W}^{\prime}=\mathbb{P}_{2}(X_{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F}})
yields the following result.

Corollary 6.13
.

The problems
strong-TC-F
and
LCA-TC-F
are equivalent.

7
Summary and Outlook

In this work, we studied an alternative notion of displaying rooted triples in phylogenetic DAGs and
networks based on least common ancestor constraints. We also introduced anchored triples as a means
of inferring phylogenetic DAGs and networks. We considered several consistency problems involving
required and forbidden rooted or anchored triples and showed that all of them can be solved in
polynomial time. To this end, we constructed suitable relations of required and forbidden
LCA-constraints and analyzed the corresponding realizability problems. We further used

xy
-extensions to ensure that two leaves
x
and
y
have multiple least common ancestors. This
allowed us to guarantee that certain forbidden rooted or anchored triples are not displayed,
respectively not

-displayed. Conversely, we showed that the LCA of
x
and
y
can be forced
to be well-defined by adding the pair
(xy,xy)
to the required relation. This leads to the problem

\mathscr{W}
-
TC-F
, which generalizes the four consistency problems considered for rooted
triples. Analogously,
\mathscr{W}
-
ATC-F
generalizes the corresponding problems for
anchored triples.
Our results are summarized in Table
1
.

It would be interesting to determine whether the problem of constructing a phylogenetic network that
agrees with a pair of required and forbidden triple sets remains solvable in polynomial time when
the solution is restricted to a specific class of networks, such as binary or level-
k

networks for fixed
k
. This question is particularly relevant because constructing a binary network
that topologically-displays all required triples and none of the forbidden ones is NP-hard

[
12
]
.

Biologists may still wish to obtain a phylogenetic network even when no network agrees with the
given pair of required and forbidden triple sets. Suppose that
\mathcal{R}
is a triple set for
which the associated relation
R_{\mathcal{R}}
is not realizable. By construction,
R_{\mathcal{R}}
satisfies

(X1)
, and hence
[
24
, Prop 28]
implies that the canonical DAG
\mathscr{G}_{R_{\mathcal{R}}}
is
well-defined, although it does not realize
R_{\mathcal{R}}
. This naturally raises the question of which
triples in
\mathcal{R}
are nevertheless displayed by
\mathscr{G}_{R_{\mathcal{R}}}
and how many such triples can be
guaranteed to be displayed. Analogous questions arise when a set
\mathcal{F}
of forbidden triples is
prescribed as well.
More generally, this motivates the optimization problem of constructing a network that
maximizes the number of displayed required triples while simultaneously minimizing the number of
displayed forbidden triples. Similarly, suppose that
(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{F})
is an instance for which an
agreeing phylogenetic network exists, but no such network can be required to have all pairwise LCAs
well-defined. One may then ask for an agreeing phylogenetic network that maximizes the number of
leaf pairs having a well-defined LCA. All of these optimization problems can naturally also be
studied for anchored triples.

Another commonly studied question in phylogenetic combinatorics is whether a phylogenetic network is
encoded by the triples it displays. For example, let
\mathscr{T}(N)
denote the set of all triples
topologically-displayed by a network
N
. A class
\mathcal{C}
of networks is encoded by
\mathscr{T}
if, for all
N,N^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
,
\mathscr{T}(N)=\mathscr{T}(N^{\prime})
implies
N\simeq N^{\prime}
. Gambette
and Huber
[
8
]
showed that topologically-displayed triples do not even encode level-1
networks. In the present paper, we considered a different, LCA-based notion of displaying triples.
By Lemma
2.3
, every triple displayed in the LCA sense is also topologically-displayed,
whereas the converse does not hold in general. Consequently, negative encoding results for
topologically-displayed triples do not automatically transfer to LCA-based displayed triples, since

\mathscr{T}(N)=\mathscr{T}(N^{\prime})
does not automatically imply equality of the sets of displayed
triples. Nevertheless, displayed triples still do not encode level-1 networks. Indeed, consider the
network
N_{2}
in Figure
2
and the star tree
T
on
X=\{x,y,z\}
. Both are
level-1 networks, and neither displays any rooted triple in the LCA sense, but they are not
isomorphic. Hence level-1 networks are not encoded by the rooted triples they display. It remains an
interesting open question whether level-1 networks, or more generally which classes of networks, are
encoded by their

-displayed anchored triples. Relatedly, it was recently shown that several
natural classes of networks are encoded by their full LCA relation
\trianglelefteq_{N}
, including regular
level-1 networks, shortcut-free binary level-1 networks, and binary normal networks
[
13
]
.
For several of these classes, even the restricted relation
\trianglelefteq_{N}^{3}
, consisting only of
LCA-constraints of the form
(ab,ac)\in\trianglelefteq_{N}
for pairwise distinct
a,b,c\in X
, suffices to
encode them. Moreover, for a fixed leaf set
X
, the aforementioned classes
are encoded by the strict subrelation
\blacktriangleleft_{N}
consisting of all pairs
(ab,xy)\in\trianglelefteq_{N}
for which

\operatorname{lca}_{N}(ab)\prec_{N}\operatorname{lca}_{N}(xy)
. This relation is closely connected to the anchored-triple setting,
since an anchored triple
\underline{a}b|c
corresponds precisely to the strict LCA-constraint
\operatorname{lca}_{N}(ab)\prec_{N}\operatorname{lca}_{N}(ac)
, and hence to
(ab,ac)\in\blacktriangleleft_{N}
. However,
\blacktriangleleft_{N}
may contain more
information than the set of anchored triples

-displayed by
N
, because it also records
strict LCA-comparisons between arbitrary leaf pairs
ab
and
xy
.
