Inverse scattering for the focusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation with elliptic background and full soliton gas
Tamara Grava, Robert Jenkins, Xiaofan Zhang, Zechuan Zhang

Abstract
In this manuscript we develop the direct and inverse scattering problem for the cubic focusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation and for initial data that are asymptotic to an elliptic travelling wave with distinct phase at ± ∞ \pm\infty . We consider the case in which the spectral bands intersect the real axis. We then show that this class of initial data has non zero intersection with the full soliton gas initial data.



1
Introduction and outline

In this manuscript we study the direct and inverse scattering transforms associated to the Cauchy problem for the cubic focusing nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation

\text{i}u_{t}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}u_{xx}+|u|^{2}u=0\,,\hskip 10.00002ptu(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\hskip 20.00003pt(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

(1.1)

for initial data
u_{0}
which approaches a step-like elliptic background (cf. (
1.8
)).
Here
u=u(x,t)\in{\mathbb{C}}
and subscripts denote partial derivatives. The term ‘background’ here refers to a function
u_{b}(x,0)
which our initial data approaches asymptotically.
Typically one knowns the explicit evolution
u_{b}(x,t)
of the background under evolution by (
1.1
).
The Cauchy problem on the zero background
[
2
,
10
]
and for nonzero constant backgrounds
[
6
,
12
,
16
]
are well-studied.
Much less is known about the behavior of solutions which approach non-constant background states.
The motivation to study such problem stems from the fact that the development of direct and inverse scattering for such initial data enables to study long time behaviour of elliptic travelling waves under a broad range of perturbations.
While the family of linearly and orbitally stable perturbations of elliptic travelling wave are well known, (see, e.g.
[
17
]
[
13
]
), it is not known what happens to families of unstable perturbation of elliptic travelling waves over long time. This is the main motivation of our present study.

The cubic NLS equation (
1.1
) admits a family of quasi-periodic solutions known as finite-gap solutions.
The simplest of these solutions (the genus one case) can be expressed by elliptic functions. The explicit form is given by

\displaystyle u_{e}(x,t;x_{0},\varphi_{0}):=\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\lambda U(\lambda(x-x_{0}-vt);\lambda^{2}t)\mathrm{e}^{\text{i}v\left((x-x_{0})-\frac{v}{2}t\right)+2\text{i}\varphi_{0}}

(1.2)

\displaystyle U(x;t)=\sqrt{\left[b-m\,\mathrm{sn}^{2}(x;m)\right]}\mathrm{e}^{\text{i}\phi(x)-\text{i}\widetilde{\omega_{0}}t}

(1.3)

\displaystyle\phi(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\dfrac{\sqrt{b(1-b)(b-m)}\mathrm{d}y}{\left[b-m\,\mathrm{sn}^{2}(y;m)\right]}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{\omega_{0}}=\frac{1+m-3b}{2}

(1.4)

where
\mathrm{sn}(\,\cdot\,;m)
is the Jacobi elliptic function of modulus
m
. The six parameters
(m,b,v,\lambda,x_{0},\varphi_{0})
satisfy
0<m<b<1
,
\lambda>0
, and
v,x_{0},\varphi_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
The symmetries of (
1.1
):

1.

u(x,t)\mapsto u(x,t)\,\mathrm{e}^{\text{i}\varphi_{0}}
,
\varphi_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}

(Phase invariance);

2.

u(x,t)\mapsto u(x-x_{0},t)
,
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
(Translation
invariance);

3.

u(x,t)\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\text{i}\big(vx-\frac{v^{2}}{2}t\big)}u(x-vt,t)
,
v\in{\mathbb{R}}
(Galilean invariance);

4.

u(x,t)\mapsto\lambda u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t)
,
\lambda>0

(Dilation invariance),

allow one to reduce the six parameter family of solutions (
1.2
) to (
1.3
) depending only on the two parameters
(m,b)
. In what follows, in order to have formulas which directly apply to the general case we will work with general solutions (
1.2
)

The cubic NLS equation is integrable, namely it can be expressed as the compatibility condition of two linear equations, the so-called Zakharov-Shabat (ZS) pair, introduced in 1972
[
23
]
.
This pair takes the form

\displaystyle W_{x}=\mathcal{L}(u,z)\,W\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(u,z)=-\text{i}z\sigma_{3}+U(x,t)\,,

(1.5a)

\displaystyle W_{t}=\mathcal{B}(u,z)\,W\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(u,z)=-\text{i}z^{2}\sigma_{3}+zU-\frac{1}{2}\text{i}\sigma_{3}(U^{2}-U_{x})\,,

(1.5b)

where
W(x,t;z)\in\mbox{Mat}(2\times 2,\mathbb{C})
is the fundamental matrix solution of the above linear system,
z\in\mathbb{C}
is the spectral parameter, and

U(x,t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&u(x,t)\\
-\overline{u(x,t)}&0\end{pmatrix},\hskip 20.00003pt\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}[r]1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(1.6)

In the spectral plane, the finite-gap solution (
1.2
) is parameterized by two distinct points
z_{1},z_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{+}
and the phase constants
x_{0},\varphi_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
There is a one-to-one map between the complex parameters
z_{1},z_{2}
and the four real parameters
m,b,\lambda,v
:

m=1-\left|\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{1}-\overline{z_{2}}}\right|^{2},\hskip 20.00003ptb=\left(\dfrac{\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(z_{1}-\overline{z_{2}})}{|z_{1}-\overline{z_{2}}|}\right)^{2},\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda=|z_{1}-\overline{z_{2}}|,\hskip 20.00003ptv=-\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(z_{1}+z_{2})\,.

(1.7)

The ZS scattering problem (
1.5a
), with potential of the form (
1.2
), generates a continuous spectrum on the real line and two bands, which form the set
\Sigma
consisting of two finite arcs connecting
z_{1},z_{2},\bar{z}_{1},\bar{z}_{2}
.
The precise topology of these arcs depends on the choice of
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
(see Proposition
2.1
).

In this work, we study the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) given initial data that approaches different elliptic travelling waves at either spatial infinity.
These waves have the same spectral parameters
z_{1},z_{2}
, but different phase parameters
x_{0}
and
\varphi_{0}
.
For this reason we indicate the elliptic travelling wave as
u_{e}(x,t,x_{0},\varphi_{0})
. We assume that

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\to\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{e}^{\ell}(x)=u_{e}(x,0,x^{\ell}_{0},\varphi^{\ell}_{0}),&\qquad\mbox{as $x\to-\infty$}\\
u_{e}^{r}(x)=u_{e}(x,0,x^{r}_{0},\varphi^{r}_{0}),&\qquad\mbox{as $x\to\infty$,}\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

where
x_{0}^{\ell},x_{0}^{r}
and
\varphi_{0}^{\ell},\varphi_{0}^{r}
are in general, distinct phase shifts. Both limiting elliptic waves generate a continuous spectrum of the associated ZS operator, denoted by
\Sigma^{\ell}\cup{\mathbb{R}}
and
\Sigma^{r}\cup{\mathbb{R}}
, respectively.
In our previous work
[
21
]
we chose initial data such that: 1) the arcs
\Sigma^{\ell}
and
\Sigma^{r}
were independent with distinct endpoints; 2) neither spectral bands was permitted to intersect the real axis.
In this manuscript we choose initial data such that:

•

\Sigma^{\ell}\equiv\Sigma^{r}=\Sigma
;

•

\Sigma
is permitted to intersects the real line (see Figure
1

For illustration, Figure

Statement of the result.

To state our results we introduce standard Sobolev spaces on the real halt-lines
{\mathbb{R}}^{\pm}
,

\mathcal{W}^{n,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{\pm})=\left\{f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{\pm})\,\mid\,\partial_{x}^{j}f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{\pm}),0\leq j\leq n\right\},

and the weighted spaces

L^{p,k}({\mathbb{R}}^{\pm})=\left\{f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{\pm}),\;\;|x|^{k}f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{\pm})\right\},\hskip 10.00002ptp,k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Assumption 1.1
.

The initial data
u_{0}
satisfies (
1.8
) in the strong sense that
u_{0}-u_{e}^{\ell}\in\mathcal{W}^{4,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{-})

and
u_{0}-u_{e}^{r}\in\mathcal{W}^{4,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{+})
.

Assumption 1.2
.

The initial data
u_{0}
is generic in the sense of
[
2
]
, i.e., it produces a discrete spectrum that is simple, finite, and contains no points in
{\mathbb{R}}
.

We consider initial data satisfying assumptions
1.1
and
1.2
, with the spectral bands
\Sigma
that intersect the real line.
The main results of this manuscript are:

•

the calculation of the direct spectral map between initial data and scattering data;

•

the realization of the scattering data for the inverse problem as a limit of infinite number of solitons that we call the full gas problem.

The soliton gas solutions were introduced in
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
for the Korteweg-de Vries and modified Korteweg de Vries equations and
in
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation. In all these cases solitons are so dense that are no more distinguishable from one another and they form a condensate that has non-zero density for
x\to+\infty
or
x\to-\infty
. The so called
full gas solution
, namely the case in which solitons have non zero density as
x\to+\infty
and
x\to-\infty
was derived in
[
20
]
for the modified Korteweg-de Vries equation and it is derived in the present manuscript for the NLS equation. In particular our result shows that any step-like initial data of the form (
1.8
)
can be obtained from a full soliton gas. Furthermore the primitive potential formally introduced by Dyachenko, Zakharov and Zakharov,
[
14
]
arises naturally from step-like potential and can be seen as a full soliton gas.

Theorem 1.3

(Direct problem)
.

Given initial data
u_{0}
of the type (
1.8
) that satisfies Assumptions
1.1
and
1.2
, the scattering map takes the form

u_{0}\mapsto\left(r_{1},r_{2},\rho,\{(z_{j},c_{j})\}_{k=1}^{N}\right)
where

•

r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are continuous functions supported on
\Sigma^{+}=\Sigma\cap{\mathbb{C}}^{+}
, i.e.,
r_{1},r_{2}\in C(\Sigma^{+})
;

•

\rho\in L^{2,2}({\mathbb{R}})\cap L^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}})
;

•

The finite set of discrete points
z_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{+}\setminus\Sigma
are
L^{2}
eigenvalues of the ZS scattering operator (
1.5a
) with associated norming constant
c_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash\{0\}
,
j=1,\dots,N
.

Given the spectral data, we express the inverse problem at any time
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
as a Riemann-Hilbert problem that encodes the time-evolved solution of the NLS equation.
By Assumption
1.2
the discrete spectrum is finite and does not intersect
\mathbb{R}\cup\Sigma
. To set the Riemann-Hilbert problem we orient
{\mathbb{R}}
left-to-right.
When the spectral bands
\Sigma
cross the real axis (Figure
1
) we orient each connected component upward; when
\Sigma
does not cross the real axis (Figure
A.2
) we orient
\Sigma^{+}
from
z_{1}
to
z_{2}
and
\Sigma^{-}
from
\bar{z}_{2}
to
\bar{z}_{1}
. In either case
\Sigma
is anti-symmetric, i.e.,
\overline{\Sigma}=-\Sigma
.

RHP 1.1
.

Given the data described in Theorem
1.3
,
find a
2\times 2
matrix-valued function
M(z;x,t)
which satisfies the following conditions:

1.

M(z;x,t)
is analytic for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(\mathbb{R}\cup\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2})
with simple poles at the points
\{z_{j},\overline{z_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{N}
;

2.

M(z;x,t)=I+\mathcal{O}(z^{-1})
, as
z\to\infty
;

3.

M(z;x,t)
satisfies Schwarz symmetry:
\overline{M(\overline{z};x,t)}=\sigma_{2}M(z;x,t)\sigma_{2}
,
where
\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}[r]0&-\mathrm{i}\,\\
\mathrm{i}&0\end{pmatrix}
;

4.

M(z;x,t)
satisfies the jump condition
M(z_{+};x,t)=M(z_{-};x,t)V(z;x,t)
for
z\in{\mathbb{R}}\cup\Sigma

where
V(z;x,t)
is given by

V(z;x,t)=\begin{cases}\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{1-r_{1}(z)r_{2}(z)}{1+r_{1}(z)r_{2}(z)}&\dfrac{2\mathrm{i}r_{2}(z)}{1+r_{1}(z)r_{2}(z)}\mathrm{e}^{-2\theta(z;x,t)}\vskip 2.15277pt\\[8.00003pt]
\dfrac{2\mathrm{i}r_{1}(z)}{1+r_{1}(z)r_{2}(z)}\mathrm{e}^{2\theta(z;x,t)}&\dfrac{1-r_{1}(z)r_{2}(z)}{1+r_{1}(z)r_{2}(z)}\end{pmatrix},&z\in\Sigma\cap{\mathbb{C}}^{+},\\
\begin{pmatrix}1+|\rho(z)|^{2}&\rho^{*}(z)\mathrm{e}^{-2\theta(z;x,t)}\vskip 2.15277pt\\
\rho(z)\mathrm{e}^{2\theta(z;x,t)}&1\end{pmatrix},&z\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(1.9)

and for
z\in\Sigma\cap{\mathbb{C}}^{-}
,
V(z;x,t)=\sigma_{2}\overline{V(\bar{z};x,t)}\sigma_{2}
.

\theta(z;x,t)=\mathrm{i}tz^{2}+\mathrm{i}xz\,;

(1.10)

5.

M(z;x,t)
has simple poles at the points
z_{k}
,
\overline{z}_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,N
, with residues

\underset{z=z_{k}}{\mathop{\rm Res}\limits}\,M(z;x,t)=\lim_{z\to z_{k}}M(z;x,t)\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
c_{k}\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}(x-x_{0}^{r})z_{k}+2\mathrm{i}tz_{k}^{2}}&0\end{pmatrix},

(1.11)

and the residue at
\overline{z}_{k}
is obtained by Schwarz symmetry.

Theorem 1.4

(Inverse problem)
.

The potential

, with reflection coefficients

.

Theorem 1.5

(Soliton-gas realization)
.

2
Genus-one Riemann-Hilbert Problem

We consider the genus-one Riemann surface

\mathcal{X}=\left\{(z,R)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:R^{2}=(z-z_{1})(z-\overline{z_{1}})(z-z_{2})(z-\overline{z_{2}})\right\}\,.

(2.1)

We model
\mathcal{X}
as a two sheeted cover of the Riemann sphere.
We fix a canonical homology basis on
\mathcal{X}
by choosing the

\mathbf{a}
-cycle to be a closed loop on the first sheet encircling
the branch points
z_{1}
and
\overline{z}_{1}
counterclockwise, and the

\mathbf{b}
-cycle to be an arc starting from
z_{2}
on the first sheet,
passing to
z_{1}
, and returning to the starting point on the second
sheet. See Figure
3
.
The oriented branch cuts used to define
R(z)
as a single-valued function
on each sheet will be introduced later.

Figure 3
:
The homology basis for the Riemann surface
\mathcal{X}
associated with
R^{2}=(z-z_{1})(z-\overline{z_{1}})(z-z_{2})(z-\overline{z_{2}})
.

Denote by
\mathrm{d}p
and
\mathrm{d}q
the quasi-momentum and quasi-energy differentials, respectively. These are meromorphic differentials of the second kind on
\mathcal{X}

that are analytic away from the pre-images of
\infty
, where they satisfy

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}p=\pm[1+\mathcal{O}(z^{-2})]\mathrm{d}z,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathrm{d}q=\pm[2z+\mathcal{O}(z^{-2})]\mathrm{d}z\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{as $z\to\infty^{\pm}$}\,.

(2.2)

and normalized by the condition that

\displaystyle\oint\limits_{\mathbf{a}}\mathrm{d}p=0\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\oint\limits_{\mathbf{a}}\mathrm{d}q=0\,.

The above conditions uniquely determine the differentials

\begin{gathered}\mathrm{d}p=\frac{z^{2}-\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(z_{1}+z_{2})z+c_{0}}{R}\mathrm{d}z\,,\\
\mathrm{d}q=2\frac{z^{3}-\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(z_{1}+z_{2})z^{2}+(\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(z_{1})\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(z_{2})+\frac{\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(z_{1})^{2}+\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(z_{2})^{2}}{2})z+c_{1}}{R}\mathrm{d}z\,,\end{gathered}

(2.3)

where the constant coefficients, computed in
[
21
]
, are given by

\displaystyle c_{0}=\dfrac{1}{2}(|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2})-\frac{1}{2}|z_{1}-\overline{z_{2}}|^{2}\dfrac{E(m)}{K(m)}\,,

(2.4)

\displaystyle c_{1}=\dfrac{c_{0}}{2}\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(z_{1}+z_{2})-\frac{1}{2}(\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(z_{1})|z_{2}|^{2}+\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(z_{2})|z_{1}|^{2})\,,

where
m
is given in (
1.7
). The
\bf{b}
-periods of these differentials, also computed in
[
21
]
, are given by

\Omega_{1}:=\oint_{\mathbf{b}}\mathrm{d}p=\frac{\pi|z_{1}-\overline{z_{2}}|}{K(m)},\hskip 20.00003pt\Omega_{2}:=\oint_{\mathbf{b}}\mathrm{d}q=-v\Omega_{1}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptv=-\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(z_{1}+z_{2}).

(2.5)

Choosing
z_{2}
as the base point,
we define the Abelian integrals
p(z)
and
q(z)
by

p(z)=\int_{z_{2}}^{z}\mathrm{d}p,\hskip 10.00002ptq(z)=\int_{z_{2}}^{z}\mathrm{d}q.

(2.6)

According to standard finite-gap theory
[
24
]
, expanding these integrals near the point at infinity on the first sheet
z\to\infty^{+}
yields:

p(z)=z+E+o(z^{-1}),\hskip 10.00002ptq(z)=z^{2}+N+o(1),

(2.7)

), the relations (
3.10
) defining the scattering coefficients, and the Schwarz symmetry (

3.2
Discrete spectral data

For a given step-like potential
u_{0}
, consider the zero set

\mathrm{Z}(u_{0})=\mathrm{Z}=\{z\in\overline{\mathbb{C}^{+}}\,:\,a(z)=0\}.

(3.14)

At any point
z_{0}\in\mathrm{Z}
, due to the determinantal formula (
3.10
), the two vector solutions
W_{1}^{\ell}(x;z_{0})
and
W^{r}_{2}(x;z_{0})
are linearly dependent. So, for each
z_{0}\in\mathrm{Z}
there exists a nonzero complex number
\zeta=\zeta(z_{0})
such that

\displaystyle W_{1}^{\ell}(x;z_{0})=\zeta(z_{0})W_{2}^{r}(x;z_{0}),\hskip 20.00003pt\zeta:\mathrm{Z}\to{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\},\hskip 10.00002ptz_{0}\in\mathrm{Z}.

(3.15a)

The Schwarz symmetry (
3.7
) implies that
a^{*}(z_{0})=0
and

\displaystyle W_{2}^{\ell}(x,\overline{z_{0}})=-\overline{\zeta(z_{0})}W_{1}^{r}(x;\overline{z_{0}}),\hskip 20.00003ptz_{0}\in\mathrm{Z}.

(3.15b)

Notice that for
z_{0}\not\in\Sigma\cap{\mathbb{R}}
, that is, not in the continuous spectrum, the two vector solutions
W_{1}^{\ell}(x;z_{0})
and
W_{2}^{r}(x;z_{0})
defined by (
3.2
)-(
3.4
) decay exponentially to zero, respectively, as
x\to-\infty
and
x\to+\infty
. It follows that any
z_{0}\in\mathrm{Z}\setminus(\Sigma\cap{\mathbb{R}})
is a true
L^{2}
eigenvalue of the Zakharov-Shabat scattering operator (
1.5a
). Because the scattering operator is non self-adjoint, the structure of
\mathrm{Z}
can be quite complicated. Generally speaking
\mathrm{Z}
can contain zeros of higher order, can intersect the continuous spectrum, and possess accumulation points in the continuous spectrum. Here we assume that:

(1)

The zeros of
a(z)
are all simple and isolated.

(2)

There are no zeros of
a(z)
on the real line, i.e.,
\mathrm{Z}\cap{\mathbb{R}}=\emptyset
.

These conditions are generic in the sense that they define a dense open subset of the collection of all allowable initial data.
Since the asymptotic condition (
3.11a
) ensures that
\mathrm{Z}
is bounded, one immediate consequence of these assumptions is that
\mathrm{Z}
is finite. So, under these assumptions we can enumerate the discrete spectrum
\mathrm{Z}=\{z_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
.
We allow for the possibility that
\mathrm{Z}\cap\Sigma\neq\emptyset
. However, because
a(z)
is not analytic for
z\in\Sigma
, any point
z_{0}\in\Sigma\cap\mathrm{Z}
must be considered as a boundary value of the cut plane
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma
. Knowing that
\lim_{z\to z_{0}\in\Sigma_{+}}a(z)=0
does not imply that
\lim_{z\to z_{0}\in\Sigma_{-}}a(z)=0
. Here we remind the reader that our convention is that subscripts
\pm
refer to boundary values along the curve; superscripts
\pm
denote the parts of
\Sigma
in the upper/lower complex half-planes.
