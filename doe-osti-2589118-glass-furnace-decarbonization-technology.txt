 1. Executive Summary

This report summarizes the objectives, approach, and outcomes of the Glass Furnace
Decarbonization Technology Stack project undertaken by Owens-Brockway Glass
Container Incorporated under award number DE-CD0000093. The project aimed to
implement a pioneering combination of furnace technologies at the Zanesville, OH
manufacturing site to reduce carbon intensity (CI) by 20–40% compared to baseline
operations. The proposed Furnace Technology Stack included a Gas/Oxy-fuel system,
OPTIMELT™ Heat Recovery System, raw material pre-heating, electric boosting, and
forehearth electrification—designed to demonstrate commercial feasibility and drive
industry-wide decarbonization.

The project also emphasized community engagement, workforce development, and
environmental equity, with goals to improve air quality, support union labor, and expand
glass recycling availability in surrounding communities.

Despite initial planning and collaboration with industry partners, the project was
discontinued before entering the procurement and construction phases due to the
termination of the funding award. As a result, the technology readiness level (TRL)
remained unchanged, and the project did not reach a stage where technical results could
be validated or disseminated. No measurable energy savings or emission reductions were
achieved, and no publications or presentations were produced.

 2. Project Objectives

Owens-Brockway Glass Container Inc. will implement a first-in-market combination of
furnace technologies that reduce carbon intensity (CI) by 20-40%, compared to glass
produced on a baseline furnace, as part of a full retrofit of a furnace at a manufacturing site
in Zanesville, OH. The Furnace Technology Stack includes a Gas/Oxy-fuel system,
OPTIMELT™ Heat Recovery System, raw material pre-heating, 20-30% electric boost, and

electric forehearth conversion. The project demonstrates the commercial feasibility and
functionality of the Furnace Technology Stack’s combination of technologies that will serve
as a foundation to drive industry transformation towards decarbonization by improving
adoption of these innovative technologies. Through this project, O-I will improve air quality
for nearby communities, engage with communities and workers to pursue Project Labor
Agreements and Community Workforce Agreements, and promote workers’ rights and
equity.

 1. Install and commence commercial operations of the Furnace Technology Stack to
 produce commercial quality soda-lime glass containers at the project facility.
 2. Demonstrate, quantify, and verify cradle-to-gate reductions in CI of 20-40% for glass
 produced on the upgraded furnace with the proposed Furnace Technology Stack, as
 compared to glass produced on a baseline furnace.
 3. Through successful deployment of the Furnace Technology Stack, achieve a
 reduction of fuel consumption by at least 50% for the furnace, refiner, and
 forehearth as well as a reduction of at least 40% Scope 1 emissions for the
 manufacturing unit of the upgraded furnace at the project facility.
 4. Source clean or low-carbon electricity to power at least 25% of the furnace
 technologies’ energy demands, thereby reducing Scope 2 emissions.
 5. Evaluate Furnace Technology Stack performance at Zanesville to understand the
 impact of various operation conditions on production output (e.g., container size,
 container type).
 6. Demonstrate economic viability of an optimized decarbonized production process
 to inform future O-I furnace retrofits based on current retrofit/overhaul timing, and
 identify potential increased emissions reductions based on O-I long-term goals.
 7. Prioritize meaningful two-way engagement with the community and O-I’s workforce
 to prioritize the current social needs of the local community, work with existing
 union workforce to create high-quality, high-wage union jobs, provide upskilling to
 support the installation and operation of technologically advanced equipment, and
 improve air quality for nearby communities.
 8. Support establishment of glass recycling availability in all of the communities
 surrounding the project site.

 3. Technical Approach

The O-I team collaborated with multiple established industry partners to support the
integration of the Furnace Technology Stack at the Zanesville facility. The technology stack
began at a Technology Readiness Level (TRL) of 7, with the objective of reaching TRL 9 by
project completion. However, due to the termination of the funding award, the TRL did not
advance beyond its initial level. While concept development efforts were underway with
external collaborators, the project had not yet entered the procurement or construction
phases at the time of award termination.

 4. Results and Discussion

Due to the termination of the award, the project was unable to progress to a stage where
measurable energy savings and emission reductions could be achieved.

 5. Conclusions

The project was initiated with the objective of advancing energy-efficient glass furnace
technology and initial planning and development activities were successfully undertaken.
However, due to the termination of the funding award, the project was discontinued before
reaching critical implementation phases. As a result, it did not progress to a stage where
technical results could be validated, disseminated through publications or presentations,
or where measurable outcomes such as energy savings and emission reductions could be
realized. While the early groundwork may inform future initiatives, the premature
termination limited the project's ability to fulfill its intended impact.

 6. Publications and Presentations

The project did not advance to a stage suitable for disseminating results through
publications, presentations, or public communications.
