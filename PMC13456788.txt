Predicting early postoperative corneal edema after phacoemulsification: A retrospective observational study
Authors: Jing Fu, Yanting Shi, Yi Yang, Tingting Wang, Wenxian Du, Lu Mo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to develop and internally validate a multivariable prediction model for early postoperative corneal edema following phacoemulsification by integrating patient-related and intraoperative parameters. This retrospective cohort study included 912 consecutive eyes from 912 patients who underwent standard phacoemulsification at a tertiary ophthalmic center in Southwest China between January 2021 and January 2023. Early postoperative corneal edema was defined as either a ≥5% increase in central corneal thickness from baseline on postoperative day 1 or the presence of clinically significant stromal haze on slit-lamp examination. Demographic, systemic, preoperative, and intraoperative variables were evaluated using univariate logistic regression, followed by multivariable logistic regression for model development. Model performance was assessed in terms of discrimination and calibration. Discrimination was quantified using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, whereas calibration was evaluated using calibration plots, calibration intercept and slope, and the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test. A nomogram was constructed based on the final multivariable model to estimate individualized risk. Early postoperative corneal edema occurred in 138 of 912 eyes (15.1%). In the multivariable model, older age, diabetes mellitus, lower preoperative endothelial cell density, and greater cataract and intraoperative complexity, reflected by higher lens nucleus hardness, longer operative duration, higher cumulative dissipated energy, and greater irrigation volume, were associated with early postoperative corneal edema. Intraoperative anterior chamber instability and capsular rupture or vitreous loss were also associated with an increased risk. The final model achieved an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.892 (95% confidence interval: 0.865–0.919) and demonstrated acceptable calibration in internal validation. Both systemic and intraoperative factors were associated with early postoperative corneal edema after phacoemulsification. The internally validated prediction model demonstrated good discrimination and calibration and may support preliminary perioperative risk estimation in similar clinical settings. However, external validation is required before the nomogram can be recommended for routine clinical implementation.

1. Introduction

Cataract surgery performed by phacoemulsification is among the most commonly undertaken ophthalmic procedures worldwide and is generally associated with favorable visual outcomes. Nevertheless, early postoperative corneal edema remains a clinically relevant complication, potentially resulting in transient visual impairment and delayed visual rehabilitation.[1,2] Although most cases are self-limited and resolve with conservative management, persistent or pronounced edema may progress to corneal decompensation and, in severe instances, necessitate endothelial keratoplasty.[3,4] Therefore, early identification of patients at elevated risk and a clearer understanding of the factors associated with postoperative edema may help improve perioperative assessment and postoperative monitoring.

Early postoperative corneal edema is closely related to baseline endothelial vulnerability and intraoperative surgical stress. Prior studies have identified lower endothelial cell density (ECD), advanced age, lens nucleus hardness, and phacoemulsification-related parameters, including cumulative dissipated energy (CDE), ultrasound duration, and irrigation volume, as factors associated with postoperative endothelial injury and corneal thickening.[5–9] Nevertheless, a recent narrative review highlighted that, despite extensive investigation, the predictive accuracy of existing risk models remains suboptimal, underscoring the need for more robust multivariable approaches to improve individualized risk stratification.[10]

In clinical practice, early postoperative corneal edema may reflect the combined influence of patient-related susceptibility and procedure-related stress. Diabetes mellitus and intraoperative events, such as anterior chamber instability or capsular rupture, have been associated with poorer endothelial recovery after phacoemulsification.[11,12]

More recently, nomogram-based prediction models have attempted to integrate patient-specific and procedural variables to improve individualized risk estimation.[13,14] For example, Luo et al developed a nomogram for predicting corneal edema after cataract surgery and demonstrated the feasibility of predictive modeling in this setting.[13] However, previous models have generally included a more limited set of quantitative intraoperative parameters. In contrast, the present model was designed to integrate routinely available demographic and systemic factors, preoperative corneal parameters, and objective intraoperative surgical stress indicators, including operation time, CDE, irrigation volume, anterior chamber instability, capsular rupture or vitreous loss, as well as surgical year as a temporal practice-related variable. This approach may provide a more comprehensive summary of perioperative risk-related information in the present single-center cohort, although its transportability requires external validation.

In this context, we conducted a retrospective cohort study including 912 consecutive eyes undergoing phacoemulsification at a tertiary ophthalmic center in China. The study had 2 main objectives. First, we aimed to identify independent predictors of early postoperative corneal edema across demographic, preoperative, and intraoperative domains. Early corneal edema was defined as either a ≥5% increase in central corneal thickness (CCT) or clinically significant stromal haze on postoperative day 1. Second, we developed a multivariable prediction model, presented as a nomogram, to estimate individualized risk. We anticipated that combining patient-related characteristics with intraoperative variables would provide a more comprehensive assessment of edema risk and may help inform perioperative risk stratification and patient counseling, subject to further external validation.

2. Methods

2.1. Study design and setting

This retrospective observational study was conducted at the Department of Ophthalmology, The Affiliated Hospital, Southwest Medical University (Luzhou, China). Medical records of patients who underwent phacoemulsification with intraocular lens implantation between January 2021 and January 2023 were reviewed. Because all cases were derived from a single tertiary center using relatively standardized surgical and perioperative protocols, the model was developed and internally validated within this institutional context.

2.2. Ethical approval

This study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of The Affiliated Hospital, Southwest Medical University (Approval No. KY2025597). Because this was a retrospective observational study based on routinely collected electronic medical records and operative system data, all data were deidentified/anonymized before analysis, and no additional diagnostic or therapeutic interventions were performed for research purposes. The requirement for signed informed consent was waived by the ethics committee because of the retrospective design, minimal risk to participants, and the use of deidentified/anonymized data. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and institutional data confidentiality requirements.

2.3. Participants

Eligible participants were patients aged ≥40 years who underwent phacoemulsification for age-related cataract by surgeons independently qualified to perform phacoemulsification during the study period. This age cutoff was prespecified a priori to restrict the cohort to typical age-related cataract; cataracts in patients aged <40 years often have different etiologies and perioperative characteristics that could increase clinical heterogeneity and affect risk estimation. Exclusion criteria included age <40 years; previous intraocular surgery; concurrent ocular pathology that could affect corneal status (e.g., Fuchs endothelial dystrophy, keratoconus, or uveitis); intraoperative conversion to extracapsular cataract extraction; and missing key outcome, preoperative corneal measurement, or intraoperative parameter data required for model development. Missing key data included unavailable postoperative day 1 CCT or slit-lamp outcome assessment, missing preoperative CCT or ECD measurements, or incomplete intraoperative records for operation time, CDE, or irrigation volume. After rechecking the screening records, no otherwise eligible patients/eyes were excluded solely because of missing key data; all 912 eyes included in the final analytic cohort had complete data for the primary outcome, preoperative CCT and ECD, operation time, CDE, and irrigation volume. Each patient contributed only 1 eye; if both eyes were eligible, 1 eye was selected using a prespecified simple random procedure. Specifically, a random number (uniform distribution) was generated, and the right eye was selected when the number was ≥0.5 and the left eye when it was <0.5. We analyzed 1 eye per patient to preserve the independence assumption in logistic regression and to avoid bias from inter-eye correlation that could lead to underestimated standard errors and overly narrow confidence intervals (CIs).

2.4. Outcome definition and variables

The primary outcome was early postoperative corneal edema, defined as either a ≥5% increase in CCT from baseline on postoperative day 1 or clinically significant stromal haze consistent with corneal edema on slit-lamp examination. Slit-lamp assessments were performed by experienced ophthalmologists according to standardized postoperative examination procedures. Mild transient stromal haze or Descemet’s folds without a ≥5% CCT increase were not classified as outcome events. Secondary postoperative outcomes included best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and intraocular pressure on postoperative day 1 and day 7, and changes in ECD at 1 month in eyes with available follow-up data.

Candidate predictors were grouped into 3 domains. Demographic and systemic variables included age, sex, body mass index, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, coronary artery disease, and smoking status. Diabetes mellitus and hypertension were defined according to a documented physician diagnosis and/or the active use of glucose-lowering or antihypertensive medication at the time of surgery. Detailed indicators of systemic disease control, such as glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), diabetes duration, glycemic variability, diabetes-related microvascular or macrovascular complications, long-term blood pressure control, and hypertension-related end-organ complications, were not consistently available in the retrospective dataset and were therefore not included in the candidate predictor set. Preoperative ocular variables included BCVA (logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution [LogMAR]), baseline CCT, ECD, and lens nucleus hardness according to the Lens Opacities Classification System III (LOCS III). Intraoperative variables included operation time, CDE, irrigation volume, use of a dispersive ophthalmic viscosurgical device (OVD), anterior chamber instability, capsular rupture or vitreous loss, surgeon experience (≤10 vs >10 years of independent phacoemulsification practice), and year of surgery. CCT and ECD were measured using noncontact specular microscopy (SP-3000P, Topcon, Japan) by trained technicians under standardized institutional procedures.

2.5. Data sources and measurements

All demographic, clinical, and surgical data were retrieved from the hospital’s electronic medical record system and operative logs. CCT and slit-lamp findings, including stromal haze, were assessed preoperatively and on postoperative day 1 (±12 hours). All CCT and ECD measurements were obtained using the same noncontact specular microscopy device (SP-3000P, Topcon, Japan) under standardized operating protocols throughout the study period. The device provides automated measurement and averaging functions, internal quality control, and instrument calibration to reduce measurement variability in routine practice. Examinations with poor fixation, low image quality, or unreliable automated outputs were repeated according to routine clinical procedures. Measurements were performed by trained technicians who followed the same institutional operating protocol. ECD was assessed preoperatively and, when available, at 1 month after surgery (±2 weeks) by specular microscopy. Surgical parameters, including operation time, CDE, and irrigation volume, were automatically extracted from the phacoemulsification system (Infinity Vision System, Alcon Laboratories). All surgeries were performed under topical anesthesia using a clear corneal incision and standard coaxial phacoemulsification. Formal interobserver and intraobserver reproducibility metrics, such as intraclass correlation coefficients or coefficients of variation for CCT and ECD, were not available because repeated independent measurements were not routinely recorded in the retrospective clinical dataset. Therefore, formal measurement reproducibility could not be quantified, and this should be considered an important methodological limitation. Nevertheless, measurement variability was reduced in routine practice through standardized equipment, calibration, automated measurement procedures, and trained personnel.

2.6. Missing data handling

A complete-case analytic framework was used, in which patients were included in the final analysis only when the primary outcome, key preoperative corneal measurements, and core intraoperative parameters were available. After rechecking the analytic dataset, all 912 eyes included in the final analytic cohort had complete data for the primary outcome, postoperative day 1 CCT or slit-lamp outcome assessment, preoperative CCT and ECD, operation time, CDE, and irrigation volume. Thus, the extent of missingness for these key outcome-defining and model-defining variables was 0% in the analytic cohort, and no otherwise eligible patients/eyes were excluded solely because of missing key data. Therefore, a comparison between included cases and cases excluded because of missing key data was not applicable. Multiple imputation was not performed because the key variables required for outcome definition and model construction were complete in the analytic cohort. In contrast, several additional systemic disease-control indicators, including HbA1c, diabetes duration, glycemic variability, diabetes-related microvascular or macrovascular complications, long-term blood pressure control, and hypertension-related end-organ complications, were not consistently available in the retrospective electronic medical records and were therefore not included in the candidate predictor set. These variables were considered unavailable retrospective data rather than partially missing model covariates; therefore, imputing them would have required unverifiable assumptions and could have introduced artificial precision.

2.7. Bias control

To reduce potential selection bias, all eligible consecutive cases meeting the inclusion criteria and with complete key outcome and predictor data were included. Measurement bias was reduced by using automated digital data capture from phacoemulsification consoles, standardized ophthalmic assessment procedures, and uniform specular microscopy equipment for corneal measurements. Although no otherwise eligible patients/eyes were excluded solely because of missing key data, the retrospective design may still introduce selection bias through unmeasured or inconsistently recorded clinical variables. Statistical adjustment for confounding was performed using multivariable logistic regression including variables with P < .10 in univariate analyses or variables deemed clinically relevant. Because detailed indicators of systemic disease control, such as HbA1c, diabetes duration, glycemic variability, diabetes-related microvascular or macrovascular complications, long-term blood pressure control, and hypertension-related end-organ complications, were not systematically recorded for all patients, these variables were not entered into the candidate predictor set or sensitivity analyses. Therefore, residual confounding related to the severity, duration, and control of diabetes mellitus and hypertension could not be fully excluded.

2.8. Sample size

The study included all eligible complete cases within the study period. The final analytic sample comprised 912 eyes with 138 outcome events. With 11 predictors included in the final multivariable model, the events-per-variable (EPV) ratio was 12.5, supporting an acceptable sample size for model development, while recognizing that overfitting could not be completely excluded.

2.9. Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were tested for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test and expressed as mean ± standard deviation, while categorical variables were presented as numbers and percentages. All statistical analyses were conducted on the complete-case analytic cohort of 912 eyes with complete key outcome and predictor data. Between-group comparisons were performed using independent-sample t tests or χ2 tests, as appropriate. All regression analyses were performed at the eye level with 1 randomly selected eye per patient to avoid within-subject clustering effects. Univariate logistic regression was first applied to identify variables associated with early postoperative corneal edema. Variables with P < .10 in univariate analysis or variables deemed clinically relevant were considered for multivariable modeling. The final model was specified based on clinical relevance, model parsimony, avoidance of collinearity or redundancy, and stability of coefficient estimates. Multicollinearity was evaluated using the variance inflation factor (VIF). To assess the assumption of linearity in the logit for continuous predictors, we formally evaluated potential deviations from linearity within the logistic regression framework. No evidence of meaningful nonlinearity was detected, and alternative model specifications did not materially change effect estimates or model discrimination. Therefore, continuous predictors were retained as linear terms in the final model to preserve interpretability and facilitate nomogram construction. Given the potential correlation between surgical complexity variables (e.g., lens nucleus hardness, CDE, and intraoperative capsular rupture), we specifically examined collinearity diagnostics to ensure stable coefficient estimates. All VIF values were <2, indicating no concerning multicollinearity. The final multivariable model included 11 predictors, resulting in an EPV ratio of 12.5 (138 events/11 predictors), exceeding the commonly recommended minimum of 10 EPV. Although this EPV ratio suggested an acceptable sample size for multivariable modeling, we recognized that overfitting could not be completely excluded. Therefore, bootstrap internal validation was used to quantify optimism in model performance and to assess the stability of discrimination and calibration. Model discrimination was assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve. Calibration was evaluated using the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test and a bootstrap bias-corrected calibration plot with 1000 resamples. Model stability and potential optimism were further assessed using bootstrap internal validation to estimate optimism-correcteddiscrimination and calibration. Model robustness was explored through prespecified subgroup analyses stratified by clinically relevant factors, including age group, diabetes status, lens nucleus hardness, surgeon experience, and surgical year. These subgroup analyses were performed to examine whether the direction and relative magnitude of the main associations were broadly consistent across clinically relevant strata, rather than to develop separate prediction models within each subgroup. Sensitivity analyses were conducted for surgeon experience by modeling years of independent phacoemulsification practice as a continuous variable and by testing alternative prespecified cutoffs of 5 and 15 years while keeping all other covariates in the final model unchanged. These analyses were considered supportive internal robustness checks to assess the consistency of the main associations within the present cohort and were not intended to replace external validation in independent cohorts. All analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.5.1, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), with P < .05 considered statistically significant.

3. Results

3.1. Baseline characteristics of the study population

As shown in Table 1, the study included a total of 912 patients who underwent phacoemulsification at a tertiary hospital in Southwest China between January 2021 and January 2023, among whom 138 (15.1%) developed early postoperative corneal edema. Patients with corneal edema were significantly older than those without edema (74.1 ± 7.5 years vs 70.1 ± 8.1 years, P < .001). Sex distribution was comparable between groups; the proportion of females was numerically higher in the edema group (58.7%) than in the non-edema group (51.8%), but the difference was not statistically significant (P = .12). Systemic comorbidities showed distinct patterns: hypertension (P = .02) and diabetes mellitus (P < .001) were more prevalent among patients who developed corneal edema, whereas the prevalence of coronary artery disease and current smoking did not differ significantly between groups (P = .25 and P = .19, respectively). Preoperative ocular parameters also demonstrated marked differences. Eyes that developed edema had a thicker baseline central cornea (545 ± 36 µm vs 534 ± 32 µm, P = .002) and a lower ECD (2296 ± 341 cells/mm2 vs 2476 ± 320 cells/mm2, P < .001) compared with those without edema. In addition, the mean lens nucleus hardness was higher in the edema group than in the non-edema group (LOCS III 3.6 ± 0.7 vs 3.1 ± 0.8, P < .001). Surgeon experience also appeared influential, with a lower proportion of cases performed by surgeons with >10 years of experience in the edema group (56.5% vs 68.0%, P = .01). Across the study period, the incidence of corneal edema decreased from 2021 to 2023 (18.2%, 16.8%, and 8.9%, respectively; P = .04). These baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1.

3.2. Intraoperative characteristics

As summarized in Table 2, several intraoperative parameters were significantly associated with early postoperative corneal edema. Eyes that developed corneal edema had a longer mean operation time (19.6 ± 6.1 vs 16.3 ± 5.1 minutes, P < .001) and higher CDE (10.1 ± 3.8 vs 7.3% ± 3.2%·seconds, P < .001). Similarly, the mean irrigation fluid volume was significantly higher among eyes with edema (95.3 ± 28.7 vs 79.9 ± 24.5 mL, P < .001). Intraoperative instability of the anterior chamber occurred more frequently in the edema group (15.2% vs 6.1%, P = .001), as did intraoperative complications such as capsular rupture or vitreous loss (5.8% vs 1.8%, P = .008). The use of dispersive OVDs was more common in patients who developed corneal edema (58.7% vs 44.6%, P = .003). In contrast, the use of balanced salt solution (BSS Plus) and the type of intraocular lens implanted did not differ significantly between groups. Phacoemulsification technique differed significantly between groups (P = .02), with a higher proportion of divide-and-conquer cases among patients who developed edema and a higher proportion of phaco-chop cases among eyes without edema. 3.3. Postoperative outcomes

Postoperative assessments were routinely performed on postoperative day 1 and day 7, and specular microscopy at 1 month was available in a subset of eyes. As presented in Table 3, early postoperative corneal edema occurred in 138 of 912 eyes (15.1%). Eyes with edema showed a markedly greater mean increase in CCT on postoperative day 1 (+67.2 ± 26.3 µm) compared with those without edema (+22.4 ± 17.9 µm, P < .001), corresponding to a mean relative increase of 12.4% versus 4.1% (P < .001). Any stromal haze on slit-lamp examination was documented in 79.0% of eyes with edema and 1.6% of eyes without edema (P < .001); mild transient haze without a ≥5% CCT increase was not classified as early corneal edema. Visual recovery was slower among patients with edema, with a mean BCVA of 0.62 ± 0.35 LogMAR on day 1 and 0.29 ± 0.24 LogMAR on day 7, both significantly worse than that of eyes without edema (0.33 ± 0.26 and 0.17 ± 0.20 LogMAR, respectively; P < .001). Only 63.8% of eyes with edema achieved BCVA ≤ 0.3 LogMAR by day 7, compared with 81.1% in the non-edema group (P < .001). Although intraocular pressure did not differ significantly (P = .09), transient Descemet’s folds were more common in eyes with edema (15.9% vs 3.1%, P < .001). Persistent edema on day 7 occurred in 17.4% of initially edematous eyes, while none of the non-edematous eyes exhibited residual swelling. Among the subset with 1-month follow-up data, eyes that developed early edema showed a greater reduction in ECD at 1 month (−312 ± 122 vs −153 ± 84 cells/mm2, P < .001). 3.4. Univariate analysis

As summarized in Table 4, several systemic and intraoperative factors demonstrated significant associations with the occurrence of early postoperative corneal edema. Among systemic characteristics, older age (odds ratio [OR] 1.07, 95% CI: 1.05–1.10, P < .001), hypertension (OR 1.56, P = .02), and diabetes mellitus (OR 2.79, P < .001) were associated with higher odds of postoperative edema. Preoperative corneal parameters also played an important role – greater baseline corneal thickness (per 10 µm increase: OR 1.22, P = .002) and lower ECD (per 100-cell decrease: OR 1.24, P < .001) were associated with higher odds of edema. Regarding intraoperative variables, higher lens nucleus hardness (OR 1.71, P < .001), prolonged operation time (OR 1.09, P < .001), higher CDE (OR 1.22, P < .001), and larger irrigation fluid volume (OR 1.19, P < .001) were associated with higher odds of corneal edema. Intraoperative anterior chamber instability (OR 2.74, P < .001) and capsular rupture or vitreous loss (OR 3.41, P = .006) were also associated with higher odds of edema. The use of a dispersive OVD was also associated with higher odds of edema (OR 1.77, P = .003). Compared with the phaco-chop technique, divide-and-conquer phacoemulsification was associated with higher odds of edema (OR 2.18, P = .003). Procedures performed in 2023 were associated with lower odds of edema compared with those performed in 2021 (OR 0.44, P = .005). Collectively, these univariate results identified both patient-related and surgery-related factors associated with early postoperative corneal edema and provided a foundation for subsequent multivariable analysis.

3.5. Multivariate analysis and model performance

As summarized in Table 5 and visualized in Figure 1, several factors remained independently associated with early postoperative corneal edema after adjusting for potential confounders. Increasing age was a modest yet consistent predictor of edema (aOR 1.05 per year, P < .001), whereas documented diabetes mellitus was associated with higher odds of edema (aOR 2.12, 95% CI: 1.33–3.38, P = .001). Lower preoperative ECD was associated with higher odds of postoperative edema (aOR 1.18 per 100-cell decrease, P = .002). Indicators of surgical complexity – including higher lens nucleus hardness, prolonged operation time, greater CDE, and larger irrigation fluid volume – were all independently associated with higher odds of early corneal edema (all P ≤ .002). Intraoperative anterior chamber instability (aOR 2.08, P = .03) and capsular rupture or vitreous loss (aOR 2.79, P = .046) were also associated with higher odds of edema. The association of capsular rupture or vitreous loss remained after adjustment for lens nucleus hardness and CDE. Surgeon experience ≤10 years showed a nonsignificant association with higher odds of edema (aOR 1.48, 95% CI: 0.96–2.29, P = .08). Procedures performed in 2023 were associated with lower odds of postoperative edema compared with those performed in 2021 (aOR 0.53, P = .04). The final multivariable logistic regression model demonstrated good discriminative performance (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve = 0.892, 95% CI: 0.865–0.919; Fig. 2) and showed acceptable calibration between predicted and observed probabilities (Fig. 3). Decision curve analysis showed that the model provided a higher net benefit than the treat-all and treat-none strategies across a range of threshold probabilities within the present cohort (Supplementary Figure 1, Supplemental Digital Content 1). Based on the final multivariable model, a nomogram was constructed to estimate the individualized probability of early postoperative corneal edema (Fig. 4). In addition, stratification of patients according to model-predicted risk showed a clear gradient in observed event rates, increasing from 2.76% in the low-risk group to 16.85% in the intermediate-risk group and 49.73% in the high-risk group, further supporting the model’s potential value for preliminary risk stratification within the present cohort (Supplementary Table 1, Supplemental Digital Content 2). Together, these results suggest that both patient-specific and surgery-related factors were associated with early postoperative corneal edema, and that the internally validated model may provide a structured approach for preliminary risk estimation within the present cohort. Additional internal robustness assessments were performed to evaluate whether the direction of the primary associations was broadly consistent across clinically relevant subgroups and alternative definitions of surgeon experience. In prespecified subgroup analyses stratified by age group, diabetes status, lens nucleus hardness, surgeon experience, and surgical year, the direction of the main associations was generally consistent with that observed in the overall cohort. Sensitivity analyses using alternative specifications of surgeon experience, including modeling years of independent phacoemulsification practice as a continuous variable and applying alternative cutoffs of 5 and 15 years, yielded similar effect directions and did not materially change the conclusions of the primary model (Supplementary Table 2, Supplemental Digital Content 3). Bootstrap internal validation showed minimal optimism in model performance, with no substantial deterioration in optimism-corrected discrimination or calibration across resamples. These findings suggest acceptable internal stability of the model and a limited degree of apparent overfitting, although external validation remains necessary.

4. Discussion

This retrospective cohort study examined factors associated with early postoperative corneal edema following phacoemulsification and developed an internally validated multivariable nomogram integrating patient-related and intraoperative variables. Older age, diabetes mellitus, reduced preoperative ECD, greater lens nucleus hardness, longer operative duration, higher CDE, larger irrigation volume, anterior chamber instability, and capsular rupture or vitreous loss were associated with early postoperative corneal edema. These findings support the concept that early postoperative corneal edema may reflect the combined burden of baseline endothelial vulnerability and procedure-related stress while recognizing that the retrospective observational design does not allow for causal inference.

Supportive subgroup and sensitivity analyses showed that the direction of the main associations was generally consistent across clinically relevant strata and alternative definitions of surgeon experience. Clinically, these analyses suggest that the observed associations were not driven solely by a single subgroup or by 1 arbitrary cutoff for surgeon experience. However, because all such analyses were conducted within the same single-center dataset, they should be interpreted only as internal robustness checks rather than as external validation or evidence of model transportability.

Although the EPV ratio was acceptable and bootstrap internal validation suggested limited optimism, overfitting cannot be completely excluded in this single-center model-development study. Penalized regression, formal shrinkage, recalibration, and model updating were not performed in the present development cohort. Therefore, the model should not be considered fully optimized for transportability. Future external validation studies should assess calibration performance and consider penalized modeling, shrinkage, recalibration, or model updating when applying the model to independent populations.

Prior studies have highlighted the multifactorial vulnerability of the corneal endothelium during phacoemulsification, where intrinsic cellular reserve and extrinsic surgical trauma interact dynamically.[15–19] Age-related endothelial senescence,[15] diabetes-related metabolic susceptibility,[16,17] and reduced baseline ECD[18] may be associated with a lower functional reserve available to withstand ultrasonic energy and fluidic turbulence. However, diabetes mellitus was analyzed as a binary comorbidity in the present study. Therefore, we could not determine whether the observed association differed according to glycemic control, disease duration, glycemic variability, or the presence of diabetes-related microvascular complications.

In this context, corneal edema may be viewed as a threshold phenomenon that occurs when cumulative mechanical and metabolic stress exceeds endothelial compensatory capacity. Surgical complexity, reflected by increased CDE, prolonged operative time, and higher irrigation volume, further amplifies endothelial injury.[19] These parameters may represent not only technical difficulty but also the magnitude and duration of intraocular stress exposure, which could be associated with transient impairment of endothelial pump function and barrier integrity in the early postoperative period. Capsular rupture is plausibly linked to greater nucleus hardness and higher energy delivery; therefore, we included lens nucleus hardness (LOCS III) as a preoperative predictor and CDE as an intraoperative stress marker. Notably, lens nucleus hardness remained independently associated with early corneal edema after multivariable adjustment, and collinearity diagnostics (VIF < 2) supported the stability of the estimates despite potential interrelationships among these variables. Importantly, by integrating patient-related vulnerability markers, including age, diabetes, and baseline ECD, with procedure-related stress markers, including CDE, irrigation load, operative duration, and capsular events, the nomogram provides a structured framework for estimating the combined risk profile associated with early postoperative corneal edema. This integrative approach may offer a more comprehensive way to summarize risk-related information than isolated factor analysis, although its clinical usefulness requires confirmation through external validation.

These findings are biologically plausible within the framework of cumulative endothelial stress during phacoemulsification, while recognizing the observational nature of the study. Advanced age and diabetes may reflect diminished endothelial functional reserve, whereas elevated CDE and increased irrigation volume may represent markers of greater intraoperative mechanical and oxidative stress on the corneal endothelium.[10,20] Experimental models have suggested that excessive irrigation pressure and prolonged ultrasound exposure can adversely affect endothelial barrier function and stromal hydration.[11] Intraoperative anterior chamber instability and capsular rupture may also be associated with greater shear stress and fluid turbulence.[21] However, the present study was not designed to establish these mechanisms directly. Rather than proving a causal pathway, our findings suggest that patient-related susceptibility and procedure-related stress markers may jointly help characterize a higher-risk clinical profile.

The present model builds upon prior predictive efforts in this field. Luo et al[13] developed a nomogram using copula entropy-based feature selection, whereas Eleiwa et al[22] and Arnalich-Montiel et al[14] focused mainly on corneal edema or endothelial decompensation in the context of Fuchs endothelial corneal dystrophy. Compared with these earlier studies, our model incorporated both patient-related vulnerability markers and quantitative intraoperative parameters, including CDE and irrigation volume, within a unified multivariable framework. By including objective procedural metrics and temporal changes in surgical practice, the model may better summarize perioperative risk-related information in this single-center cohort. However, its value for individualized risk stratification requires confirmation in external validation studies.

The model was developed in a relatively large consecutive single-center cohort with standardized surgical and perioperative protocols, which supports internal consistency. However, differences in surgeon experience, phacoemulsification platforms, fluidics settings, postoperative protocols, patient demographics, and baseline endothelial characteristics may affect model calibration in other settings. Therefore, the nomogram should be regarded as an internally validated risk-estimation model whose transportability requires prospective external validation.

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting our findings. First, the retrospective, single-center design may introduce selection bias and restrict external validity. Accordingly, the nomogram should be interpreted as an internally validated risk-estimation model rather than a tool ready for broad clinical implementation. Its transportability to other institutions, surgeons, patient populations, and phacoemulsification systems requires prospective external validation. Missing data were addressed using a complete-case framework, and all eyes included in the final analytic cohort had complete key outcome, preoperative corneal measurement, and intraoperative parameter data. No otherwise eligible patients/eyes were excluded solely because of missing key data; therefore, a comparison between included cases and cases excluded for missingness was not applicable. Nevertheless, because of the retrospective design, selection bias related to unmeasured or inconsistently documented clinical variables cannot be fully excluded. Future prospective studies should use standardized data collection procedures and predefined missing-data management strategies to minimize this potential bias. Although all consecutive eligible cases were included to minimize selection bias, residual confounding by unmeasured factors – such as endothelial morphology, intraoperative fluidics settings, or postoperative anti-inflammatory regimens – cannot be excluded. In addition, diabetes mellitus and hypertension were modeled as binary comorbidities. Detailed systemic disease-control indicators, including HbA1c, diabetes duration, glycemicvariability, diabetes-related microvascular or macrovascular complications, long-term blood pressure control, and hypertension-related end-organ complications, were not uniformly captured in the retrospective dataset and therefore could not be incorporated into the primary model. As a result, the severity, duration, and control of systemic disease could not be fully accounted for, which may have led to residual confounding when interpreting the associations of diabetes mellitus and hypertension with early postoperative corneal edema. Future prospective studies should collect these systemic disease-control indicators to determine whether metabolic or vascular disease control modifies the risk of early postoperative corneal edema. Second, measurement bias was reduced by using automated data capture from phacoemulsification consoles, standardized ophthalmic examination procedures, and a uniform noncontact specular microscopy device for CCT and ECD measurements; nevertheless, CCT-based edema diagnosis might not fully capture functional endothelial impairment. Formal interobserver and intraobserver reproducibility metrics for CCT and ECD, such as intraclass correlation coefficients or coefficients of variation, were not available because repeated independent measurements were not routinely recorded in this retrospective dataset. This represents an important methodological limitation and may have introduced measurement variability. Future prospective studies should include repeated measurements and predefined reproducibility assessments for key ocular parameters. Importantly, although the primary outcome incorporated both ≥5% CCT increase and clinically significant stromal haze, CCT change constituted an objective quantitative component of the composite definition. Therefore, the observed associations are unlikely to have been driven solely by subjective haze assessment. Third, the study assessed edema only on postoperative day 1; thus, long-term persistence or recovery patterns were not analyzed. Fourth, although the EPV ratio was acceptable and bootstrap internal validation suggested limited optimism, potential overfitting cannot be completely excluded because the model was developed and internally evaluated within a single-center dataset. Penalized regression, formal shrinkage, recalibration, and model updating were not performed in the present development cohort, which should be considered a limitation of model optimization. These approaches should be considered in future external validation or model-updating studies, especially when applying the model to smaller samples or different clinical settings. Finally, some intraoperative variables (e.g., irrigation volume and chamber instability) might vary with surgeon skill and console calibration, potentially introducing modest imprecision in effect estimates. However, the magnitude and direction of the observed associations should still be interpreted cautiously because residual measurement error and unmeasured confounding cannot be fully excluded.

In summary, this retrospective observational study identified several patient-related and intraoperative factors associated with early postoperative corneal edema after phacoemulsification. The internally validated nomogram demonstrated good discrimination and calibration in this single-center cohort and may provide a structured approach for preliminary risk estimation in similar clinical settings. However, the model should not be considered ready for routine clinical implementation until it has been externally validated in independent, prospective, and multicenter cohorts. Future studies incorporating advanced imaging, detailed systemic disease-control indicators such as HbA1c and blood pressure control, and real-time intraoperative metrics may further refine risk prediction and clarify the clinical utility of the model.
