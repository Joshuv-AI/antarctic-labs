Improving cold snare polypectomy quality: are online educational interventions sufficient?
Authors: Seung Min Hong, Dong Hoon Baek
Published: 2026

Cold snare polypectomy (CSP) is the standard technique for removing colorectal polyps <10 mm in size. Recent guidelines recommend CSP as the preferred method for resecting sessile colorectal polyps <10 mm.1 While CSP may be a technically simple technique, its clinical importance should not be underestimated. Small polyps are not always clinically insignificant. Previous studies have reported advanced histological features, even in diminutive and small colorectal polyps.2 Given that most colonoscopic polyps are <10 mm, CSP is routinely performed by endoscopists, making proper techniques essential for achieving complete resection.

In this issue of Clinical Endoscopy, de Benito Sanz et al.3 report findings from the POLIPEVA study. In this prospective, multicenter study, the effects of an online educational intervention on the incomplete resection rates associated with CSP for colorectal lesions <10 mm were evaluated. The study was conducted from 2021 to 2024 across 14 centers in Spain. After the baseline assessment, the participating endoscopists completed a 1 hour online educational program, while incomplete resection rates were reassessed in the second phase. The educational content included the theoretical basis of CSP, technical instructions, resection edge assessments, practical tips, polypectomy video clips, and live question-and-answer sessions. To assess resection completeness, the mucosal defect was carefully inspected after CSP and two random biopsies were obtained from the margins at both ends of the defect. Complete resection was defined as the presence of only normal mucosa in the margin biopsy specimens.

The overall baseline incomplete resection rate was 4.8%, and the median rate among the individual endoscopists was 4.3%, with values ranging from 0% to 15.6%. Among the 13 endoscopists who completed both the pre- and post-intervention assessments, the incomplete resection rate decreased from 4.8% in phase 1 to 4.0% in phase 2; however, this difference was not statistically significant. The results at the individual endoscopist level were also inconsistent: seven endoscopists showed improvements, while six showed deterioration. Among the 3,087 polypectomies, delayed bleeding was rare and no perforations were observed, further supporting the favorable safety profile of CSP.

The POLIPEVA study showed that online education did not significantly improve the rate of incomplete resection with CSP. By contrast, educational interventions can improve lesion-detection rates, including the adenoma-detection rate (ADR) and serrated polyp-detection rate. In the EQUIP study, the ADR increased from 36% to 47% among endoscopists who participated in a structured endoscopic quality improvement program, while no significant change was observed in the group that received no education.4 Additionally, an educational intervention study targeting serrated polyp detection reported an improved proximal serrated polyp-detection rate after a 45 minutes educational session.5

However, education in polypectomy differs from that for improving the ADR. Complete polyp resection requires technical proficiency, including securing an adequate margin of normal tissue, maintaining proper snare positioning, and performing a continuous close-and-cut maneuver without suction or tenting.6 These skills may not be adequately addressed through didactic lectures alone and may require individualized instruction based on the actual procedural performance.

Studies that have directly evaluated polypectomy education have highlighted the limitations of lecture-based training. In one study, gastroenterology fellows underwent a lecture-based polypectomy training course, and their technical performance was assessed before and after training. However, overall competency scores did not improve significantly.7 By contrast, in the COMPLETE randomized controlled trial, video-based feedback was compared to conventional apprentice-based concurrent feedback for CSP training based on polyps <1 cm. Although achieving CSP competence requires a prolonged learning curve, video-based feedback is more effective than conventional feedback in improving trainee proficiency.8

The findings of the POLIPEVA study should not be interpreted as evidence that online education is ineffective. Rather, the study indicates that a 1 hour online lecture alone may be insufficient to substantially reduce the incomplete resection rate associated with CSP. The relatively low incomplete resection rate of 4.8% in phase 1 may have also limited the detectable effect of the intervention because of a possible floor effect, leaving only a narrow margin for further improvement. In addition, incomplete resection rates were widely distributed, ranging from 0% to 15.6% at baseline and from 0% to 8.0% in phase 1; however, endoscopists with poorer baseline performance did not show greater improvement after the intervention. This finding suggests that baseline performance alone may not reliably identify those who are most likely to benefit from a single online educational course. Nevertheless, this finding should be interpreted cautiously, because only 13 endoscopists completed both phases. Online education may be more effective when combined with procedural video reviews, expert feedback, repeated assessments, technique-specific checklists, and individual performance audits. A recent study demonstrated poor concordance between gastroenterology trainees’ self-assessments and expert assessments of CSP competence, highlighting the importance of external performance assessments and feedback.9 Further studies are needed to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of these educational strategies.

In conclusion, the online CSP lecture evaluated in the POLIPEVA study did not improve the complete resection rate. Future educational strategies for CSP, including online programs, should move beyond lecture-based instruction to individualized performance-based training that incorporates direct assessments and feedback.
