Nickel-Glycerolate/MOF-Based
Electrode Materials for
Energy Storage and Water-Splitting Applications

Iara Schlag Durães Campos, Abelardo Gregório Banze, Tarso Leandro Bastos, Raylander Rodrigues Silva, Niélcio Batista da Silva Filho, Leonardo Morais Da Silva, Josué Martins Gonçalves, Paulo Roberto Martins

Abstract

Herein, binary coordination
compounds based on Ni-Gly/ZIF-67 and
Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67 were employed as electrode materials for energy
storage and water-splitting applications, respectively. The structure
and morphology of the binary electrodes were characterized by XRD,
XPS, and SEM. Electrochemical studies (cyclic voltammetry and electrochemical
impedance spectroscopy) revealed that the binary materials exhibited
distinct electrochemical behavior for energy storage and water splitting.
In fact, whereas Ni-Gly/ZIF-67 was used as an electrode material for
energy storage, Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67 was used as an electrocatalyst
for water splitting. The Ni-Gly/ZIF-67 electrode material achieved
a high specific capacity of 106.9 mA h g–1 at 1.0
A g–1 and retained 71.6% of its capacity after 5000
cycles, combining pseudocapacitive/battery-type behavior. Furthermore,
a symmetric supercapacitor device was assembled, achieving maximum
energy and power densities of 0.765 W h kg–1 and
6076 W kg–1, respectively. The Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67
electrode material exhibited excellent oxygen evolution reaction (OER)
activity, with an overpotential of 430 mV at 10 mA cm–2, a Tafel slope of 108 mV dec–1, and stable operation
for 10 h. These results demonstrate a synergism between MOF- and Ni-Gly-derived
frameworks, providing valuable insights for the rational design of
sustainable multifunctional electrodes for integrated energy storage
and conversion systems.

Introduction

The transition to sustainable
and efficient energy systems requires
the development of energy materials capable of operating in multifunctional
applications. Among the most pressing
global challenges are large-scale electrical energy storage and the
production of clean fuels via renewable routes. Even though these technologies are intended for different
energy demands, both require electrode materials with abundant electroactive
sites, efficient charge transport, and long-term stability. Consequently,
electrode material platforms whose electrochemical properties can
be rationally tailored for distinct energy storage and conversion
applications are of particular interest.

Regarding energy storage,
supercapacitors and battery-type systems
are recognized as complementary technologies, the former offer high-density
power and long cycle life, while the latter exhibit high-density energy.
,
 Additionally, the performance of these devices depends heavily on
the electrode material, particularly its porosity, surface area, and
conductivity. On the other hand, water
splitting has received significant attention as a sustainable approach
for producing the so-called green hydrogen. This process produces hydrogen and oxygen gases as clean products,
with a theoretical potential of 1.23 V. It involves two half-reactions,
where the oxygen evolution reaction (OER) occurs at the anode, and
the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) occurs at the cathode. Due to
slow kinetics, particularly for OER, substantial overpotentials are
required for efficient operation.
,
 Recent research
focuses on the search for efficient, robust, and low-cost electrocatalysts,
since traditional noble-metal catalysts such as platinum (for HER)
and iridium or ruthenium oxides (for OER) are expensive and scarce.
−

 Consequently, compositional tuning of the same coordination compound
platform to satisfy the distinct requirements of electrochemical energy
storage and energy conversion has emerged as a crucial area of research.

In this context, the so-called coordination compounds have been extensively reported as an important class of materials
with great prospects for energy conversion and storage. Among the
most important coordination compounds, metal–organic frameworks
(MOFs) and their derivatives are promising platforms due to their
large surface areas, tunable porosity, and chemical versatility.
,
 Notably, the cobalt-based zeolitic imidazolate framework (ZIF-67)
has been widely investigated. Studies by Wen et al., Yang et al.,
and Catterjee et al. have revealed that introducing a second transition
metal, such as iron (Fe), into the ZIF-67 structure can create synergistic
electronic interactions, significantly enhancing the OER activity
by optimizing the adsorption energies of oxygen-containing intermediates.
,,
 These findings demonstrate that
rational compositional modification of MOFs represents an effective
strategy for tailoring their electrochemical properties toward specific
applications.

Beyond classical MOFs, metal glycerolates (M-Gly)
and their derivatives
have emerged as a promising class of electrode materials, spanning
a wide compositional space from metal oxides and hydroxides to metal
sulfides and phosphides. These materials
have shown great potential as electrode materials (or scaffolds and
precursors) for supercapacitors and batteries, as well as for electrocatalysis
in water splitting. In fact, recent studies have reported promising
results for the use of M-Gly as precursors for the oxygen evolution
reaction (OER), as the in situ formation of metal oxyhydroxide (MOOH) species, during electrochemical
process, on the electrode surface is key to achieving high-performance
catalysts. The superior activity is attributed to the abundant MOOH
active sites generated by electrochemical activation in an alkaline
electrolyte commonly used in OER conditions. Recent insights from Gonçalves et al. challenge traditional synthesis pathways, revealing that
materials long thought to be metal hydroxides (e.g., those synthesized
via the Tower’s method) are, in fact, metal-glycerolates (Ni-Gly).
These layered metal–organic precursors, upon controlled reaction
with aqueous alkali, can be transformed into highly active nickel
oxyhydroxide (NiOOH) phases for electrocatalysis and energy storage,
thereby opening a new avenue for precursor design.
,
 This underscores the continuous evolution in material synthesis,
where understanding the true nature of precursors is key to engineering
advanced electrodes with dual electrochemical functionalities for
both energy storage and conversion.

Furthermore, the strategic
coupling of MOFs with battery-type metal
hydroxides, such as Ni­(OH)2, is a powerful design approach
to create multifunctional electrodes. By combining the porous architecture
of MOFs with the high Faradaic activity of nickel-based compounds,
these hybrid systems seek to overcome the intrinsic limitations of
the individual components while taking advantage of their complementary
electrochemical properties. This approach, as explored in recent works,
leverages the high conductivity and rich redox chemistry of the hierarchical
porous scaffold while integrating the high charge-storage properties
of the metal hydroxide. A notable strategy
demonstrated by Li et al. involved the
synthesis of Ni­(OH)2@Co/C nanocomposites with a core–shell
architecture, in which ZIF-67-derived Co/C served as a conductive
core and ultrathin Ni­(OH)2 nanosheets formed the active
shell. The resulting mixed electrode delivered an impressive specific
capacitance of 952 F g–1 at 0.5 A g–1 and maintained 84% of its capacity after 10,000 cycles, effectively
addressing the intrinsic conductivity and cycling stability limitations
of pristine Ni­(OH)2. These studies clearly demonstrate
the benefits of integrating coordination frameworks with nickel-based
electroactive materials. However, they have predominantly focused
on conventional nickel hydroxides, whereas the electrochemical behavior
of nickel glycerolates coupled with MOFs remains considerably less
explored. Thus, nickel glycerolates possess a distinct coordination
environment that may facilitate integration with ZIF-based frameworks,
and active NiOOH is electrochemically formed in situ. However, integrating
nickel glycerolates directly with monometallic and bimetallic ZIF
frameworks to tailor targeted electrochemical functions remains underexplored.

Herein, we investigate two binary electrodes with distinct application
targets, Ni-Gly/ZIF-67 designed specifically for energy storage, and
Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67 tailored for the oxygen evolution reaction (OER).
This work provides a systematic understanding of how structural characteristics
and chemical composition govern the electrochemical properties and
specific performance of these advanced electrode materials.

Experimental Section

Synthesis

ZIF-67 was synthesized
following a previously reported procedure. Initially, Co­(C2H3O)2·6H2O (0.312 g) was added to methanol (25 mL), and the mixture
was allowed to rest for 10 min. Subsequently, a solution of 2-methylimidazole
(0.410 g) in methanol was added to the mixture, which was then stirred
under magnetic conditions for 12 h. Finally, a purple colloidal solution
of ZIF-67 was obtained. CoFe-ZIF-67 was synthesized by mixing Co­(C2H3O)2·6H2O (0.233 g)
and FeSO4·7H2O (0.087 g) in methanol (25
mL). After the static period, 2-methylimidazole (0.410 g) in methanol
was added to the reaction medium, and the mixture was stirred magnetically
for 12 h. At the end of the reaction, a purple colloidal solution
of CoFe-ZIF-67 was obtained. In order to obtain Ni-Gly, Ni­(C2H3O)2·4H2O (1.394 g) was dissolved
in 15 mL of glycerol (solution A), followed by the addition of a KOH
solution (0.628 g in 10 mL of n-butanol) into solution
A, as reported previously. The resulting
mixture was magnetically stirred for 6 h, yielding a green colloidal
solution.

Characterization

The samples were
characterized through X-ray diffraction (Shimadzu XRD-6000 diffractometer
with Cu Kα radiation of 1.5418 Å). Scanning Electron Microscopy
(SEM-FEG Jeol JSM7100F) with an accelerating voltage of 2.5 kV, and
X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS - Thermo Fisher Scientific,
UK), where the analyses were carried out for Ni-Gly/ZIF-67 and Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67
using Al as the excitation source. In addition, an FTIR spectrophotometer
(PerkinElmer Spectrum 400) was used to record spectra of ZIF-67 and
CoFe-ZIF-67 in the region of 400 – 4000 cm–1.

Preparation of Electrodes and Electrochemical
Measurements

The samples were deposited onto a Fluorine-doped
Tin Oxide (FTO) glass substrate. Prior to deposition, the FTO was
washed with soap and rinsed copiously with water. An area of 1.0 cm2 was defined using adhesive tape, followed by the drop-casting
of 10 μL of either ZIF-67 or CoFe-ZIF-67 colloidal solution.
The substrates were then heated at 80 °C for 2 h in a conventional
oven. After cooling, 10 μL of Ni-Gly colloidal solution was
deposited onto the modified FTO and spin-coated at 2500 rpm for 1
min to ensure film homogeneity. Finally, the substrates were thermally
treated at 240 °C for 30 min to remove residual solvents from
the synthesis, yielding Ni-Gly/ZIF-67 and Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67. For
comparison, ZIF-67, CoFe-ZIF-67, and Ni-Gly films were also deposited
on FTO glass substrates.

All electrochemical experiments were
performed using a Potentiostat/Galvanostat from AUTOLAB (AUT84605)
and a three-electrode cell configuration with an FTO glass substrate
modified as the working electrode, an Ag/AgCl (KCl–saturated) reference electrode, and a platinum wire as the auxiliary electrode.
The supporting electrolyte is a KOH (1.0 mol L–1) solution. The electrochemical behavior of the samples (ZIF-67,
CoFe-ZIF-67, Ni-Gly, Ni-Gly/ZIF-67, and Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67)
was evaluated by Cyclic Voltammetry (CV) and Electrochemical Impedance
Spectroscopy (EIS). CVs were recorded at 20 mV s–1 in the – 0.1 to 0.55 V potential range, and EIS was carried
out applying a potential of 0.50 V using the frequency range of 0.01
Hz to 100 kHz with a sinusoidal AC perturbation with an amplitude
of 10 mV at 25 °C. The galvanostatic charge–discharge
(GCD) curves were performed within a potential window of 0 to 0.43
V using different current densities (e.g., 1 to 12 A g–1). Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) was recorded in the – 0.10
to 0.55 V range at a scan rate of 5 mV s–1, and
Chronopotentiometric measurements were conducted at a constant current
of 10 mA for a continuous period of 10 h.

Results and Discussion

The FTIR analyses
of ZIF-67 and CoFe-ZIF-67 were carried out, and
the spectra are presented in A. It was observed that the band at 425 cm–1, common for both samples, is assigned to the metal–nitrogen
(M-N) stretching vibration, indicating coordination between the imidazolate
ligand and the metal centers. Comparison
of the two spectra further reveals that the characteristic bands located
at 1575 cm–1, corresponding to the CN stretching
vibration of the imidazole ring, 1420 cm–1, attributed
to in-plane deformation of the imidazole ring, 1120 cm–1, assigned to C–N stretching, 750 cm–1,
and 677 cm–1, associated with out-of-plane bending
vibrations, remain essentially unchanged in their vibrational frequencies.
Additional evidence suggesting the possible incorporation of iron
is provided by the appearance of a distinct absorption band centered
at approximately 620 cm–1, which is absent in the
spectrum of pristine ZIF-67. The appearance of this new band indicates
a modification of the coordination environment and may be associated
with changes arising from the presence of iron species in the bimetallic
framework. In addition, the C–H stretching vibrations (2900
– 3100 cm–1) were observed for both samples.

The XRD patterns of Ni-Gly, ZIF-67, CoFe-ZIF-67,
Ni-Gly/ZIF-67,
and Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67 based electrodes are shown in B. ZIF-67 and CoFe-ZIF-67 displayed
identical patterns, with no crystalline diffraction peaks, consistent
with previous reports. Ni-Gly exhibited
a single diffraction peak at 2θ = 10.26°, a typical structural
feature of metal–glycerolates, composed of layered metal–oxygen
frameworks interspaced with coordinated glycerolate ions. As reported by Gonçalves et al., this material is a mixture of Ni2+ complexes coordinated to O-donor ligands (e.g., glycerol, glycerolate,
acetate, OH–), which converts to layered Ni-Gly
upon heat treatment. Notably, Ni-Gly/ZIF-67 and Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67
also show a single diffraction peak in the same 2θ region as
Ni-Gly, as expected for a homogeneous film obtained by spin-coating.

XPS analyses were conducted on Ni-Gly/ZIF-67 and Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67
to determine their chemical composition and the oxidation states of
the constituent elements. The survey spectra (C) showed characteristic peaks corresponding
to Co, Ni, Fe, C, O, and residual K, as byproducts of the Ni-Gly synthesis.

The presence of mixed-valence iron in the modified framework was confirmed
in the fitted high-resolution Fe 2p spectrum. In fact, the Fe 2p spectrum
presented peaks related to Fe2+ species at 712.20 and 722.75

decrease in the electron density around Ni, suggesting that the introduction
of Fe into ZIF-67 induces an electronic interaction with the Ni-Gly
matrix. Such electronic modulation may facilitate the electrochemical
oxidation of Ni2+ into electrocatalytically active NiOOH
species under alkaline conditions, thereby contributing to the enhanced
OER performance.

According to ,
specific capacity can be determined from 1 to 12 A g–1; the values are displayed in Figure S4D. Although the ZIF-67 electrode material is more correctly classified
as pseudocapacitive, with pseudocapacitance typically expressed in
F g–1, the specific capacity of the other materials

Using specific currents of 1 and 12 A g–1, the specific
capacities obtained were 9.9 and 9.6 mA h g–1 (ZIF-67),
36.4 and 26.1 mA h g–1 (Ni-Gly), and 106.9 and 93.6
mA h g–1 (Ni-Gly/ZIF-67). It can be observed that
the capacities of ZIF-67 remained almost unchanged, whereas the capacities
of Ni-Gly and Ni-Gly/ZIF-67 decreased as the specific current increased.
These results support the presence of distinct energy storage mechanisms
in the electrode materials studied here. In fact, the constant specific
capacities at different specific currents, with a capacity retention
of 96.9%, suggest a fast, reversible surface redox reaction for the
ZIF-67 electrode material, consistent with pseudocapacitive behavior.
In contrast, Ni-Gly exhibited a capacity retention of 71.7%, indicating
that the electrode material performance is strongly dependent on current
density, as battery-like materials are governed by solid-state diffusion-controlled
Faradaic processes. For Ni-Gly/ZIF-67, an intermediate capacity retention
of 87.5% was found, revealing characteristics of both pseudocapacitive
and battery-like materials. Given that Ni-Gly/ZIF-67 showed both high
specific capacity and excellent capacity retention, this electrode
material was selected for further evaluation of its long-term stability.
Thus, the binary electrode was subjected to 5,000 consecutive charge/discharge
cycles at 12 A g–1, and it retained 71.6% of its
capacity, as shown in D.

The improved electrochemical performance of Ni-Gly/ZIF-67
may originate
from the complementary electrochemical roles of its two components,
arising from a structural integration between Ni-Gly and ZIF-67. Ni-Gly
provides Faradaic redox-active sites for charge storage, whereas the
ZIF-67 framework improves electrolyte accessibility, facilitating
ion diffusion and promoting a more homogeneous distribution of electrochemically
accessible active sites.

was compared with previously reported materials based on MOF-derived

reasonable electrochemical stability under less favorable ionic conditions.
Overall, these results suggest that Ni-Gly/ZIF-67 offers a competitive
balance between performance and durability, particularly given its

The catalytic activity of the materials toward
the OER was evaluated
using LSV. The electrochemical results are presented in . As shown in A, the overpotential (η)
values for the electrodes modified with CoFe-ZIF-67, Ni-Gly, and the
Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67 binary film determined at a current density of
10 mA cm–2 were 460 mV, 570 mV, and 430 mV, respectively.

Among the investigated materials, the Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67
demonstrated
the lowest overpotential, indicating superior catalytic performance.
In addition to these quantitative results from LSV, the observations
from CV further corroborated the favorable role of Fe incorporation
in facilitating the OER. The introduction of iron ions into the structures,
both in CoFe-ZIF-67 and Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67 electrode materials, clearly
enhanced catalytic activity. In fact, according to S. Anantharaj,
S. Kundu, and S. Noda Fe plays several
roles in the OER activity of Ni- and Co-based catalysts. In this context,
the improved electrocatalytic properties of the binary electrode material
can be attributed to the incorporation of Fe, which is expected to
contribute through several mechanisms, including: (i) during OER,
Ni-Gly turns into catalytically active NiOOH species, and Fe (CoFe-ZIF-67)
modifies the electronic structure of NiOOH, adjusting the adsorption
energy of the *OH, *O and *OOH intermediates, (ii) increases the electronic
conductivity of the metal-oxyhydroxide, although this is not the dominant
factor; (iii) promotes structural reorganization, often leading to
the formation of Layer Double Hydroxide-type phases; (iv) acts as
a strong Lewis acid, polarizing the Ni–O bonds and favoring
the formation of highly oxidizing Ni species. Although the exact role
of Fe in enhancing OER activity remains the subject of debate in the
literature, it is widely accepted that
its incorporation modulates the electronic structure of the catalyst.
Indeed, this modulation is evident by XPS analysis, as evidenced by
a shift in the binding energies of the Ni 2p peaks in Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67,
suggesting an electron-transfer effect between Ni, Co, and Fe. This
contribution was anticipated, given that the OER involves the transfer
of four electrons and, consequently, four protons.

plots in B
enabled a deeper analysis of the reaction kinetics. The
Tafel slope (b) corresponds to the number of millivolts
required to increase the current density by 1 order of magnitude (b/mV dec–1) and is described by the Tafel
equation: (η = a + blog­(j)), where b is obtained by linear fitting
analysis of the overpotential in the linear region (or high-field
approximation) of the plot.
,
 Lower values of b indicate more favorable reaction kinetics, since smaller
increases in overpotential are required to achieve a 10-fold increase
in current density. In this study, the
Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67 exhibited the lowest Tafel slope (108 mV dec–1) compared to CoFe-ZIF-67 (137 mV dec–1) and Ni-Gly (165 mV dec–1), confirming its superior
catalytic activity. These results are consistent with the linear sweep
voltammetry analyses, consolidating the Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67 binary
electrode as the most promising OER catalyst among the investigated
materials.

The long-term electrochemical stability of the Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67
electrode material, which exhibited the best OER performance, was
evaluated by chronopotentiometric measurements at 10 mA cm–2 for 10 h; the resulting curve is shown in C. The electrode demonstrated stable performance,
with only a slight potential increase from 1.644 to 1.655 V vs. RHE
over the testing period. This minor variation can be attributed to
partial leaching of the electroactive material, which requires a small
additional potential to maintain the constant current density.

The oxygen evolution reaction (OER) performance
of the Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67
electrocatalyst was compared with that of several electrocatalysts
reported in the literature ().

For comparison, Ni–Mo-MOF
demonstrates a lower overpotential
of 290 mV with a Tafel slope of 79 mV dec–1, although
its operational stability is limited to 11.1 h. Similarly, Ni–Co-ZIF67/MXene achieves improved activity,
with an overpotential of 270 mV and a Tafel slope of 57.7 mV dec–1, maintaining stability for 16 h. These enhancements are commonly attributed to the incorporation
of highly conductive supports, which facilitate charge transfer and
improve the accessibility of active sites. It is important to note
that direct comparisons of overpotential and Tafel slope values should
consider variations in experimental conditions, including electrolyte
composition, catalyst loading, and substrate materials. For instance,
the incorporation of conductive materials such as MXene can enhance charge transfer and increase the
accessibility of active sites. Moreover, MXene can contribute to the
catalytic behavior through its conductive nature and interfacial interactions
with the metal–organic framework. In this context, the Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67
electrocatalyst demonstrates consistent, reproducible OER activity
and stability for 10 h under the tested conditions. Although its overpotential
and Tafel slope are higher than those of some optimized systems, the
results highlight the viability of this material as a functional OER
electrocatalyst without the need for complex hybridization strategies.

Conclusions

In summary, this study
demonstrates the
dual electrochemical functionality
of the Ni-Gly/MOF binary materials, integrating energy storage and
water-splitting applications on a single material platform. The synthesis
of Ni-Gly/ZIF-67 and Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67 coordination compounds yielded
homogeneous, porous, and multimetallic structures with enhanced charge
transport and redox activity. The functional properties were successfully
tailored by compositional tuning. Thus, for energy storage applications,
the Ni-Gly/ZIF-67 binary electrode exhibited a high specific capacity
and excellent cycling stability, supporting its characteristics as
a battery-type electrode for charge storage in high-energy devices.
In contrast, Fe incorporation into the MOF framework (Ni-Gly/CoFe-ZIF-67)
modulated the electronic environment around Ni sites, facilitating
the in situ formation of active NiOOH species. This
significantly enhanced OER activity, resulting in low overpotential,
favorable kinetics, and long-term durability. These findings highlight
how coupling MOF-derived architectures with Ni-Gly overcomes the intrinsic
limitations of each component, such as poor activity and limited stability,
thereby enabling multifunctional performance. Overall, this work presents
a rational, cost-effective approach to designing sustainable electrode
materials that efficiently bridge energy storage and conversion in
integrated energy systems.
