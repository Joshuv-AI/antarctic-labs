 “Hedge”: Characterizing tariff design options
 • Satchwell et al. (2025) note that “regulators and utilities are exploring tariff designs for
 large-load customers to achieve desired policy and financial outcomes, provide reliable
 power to data centers, and mitigate risks and adverse impacts to other ratepayers.”1
 ◦ Satchwell et al. also note utilities have considerable latitude to design tariffs, which broadly
 cover eligibility, contract duration, and energy sources.1
 • Electricity tariffs have multiple objectives but could affect costs of data center
 development and speed to power.
 ◦ Creative/flexible provisions may improve speed to power. Helpful questions to ask include:
 – Who bears the financial risk of underutilized demand capacity? (Is it the data center developer? Other
 utility customers? Does this change over time?)
 – Under what conditions is capacity not firm (e.g., up-front demand flexibility or curtailable load
 arrangements)?
