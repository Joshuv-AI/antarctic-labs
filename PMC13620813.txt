Phylogenetic analyses and morphological studies reveal two new species of Biscogniauxia (Xylariales, Ascomycota) from Hainan Tropical Rainforest National Park, China

Si-Yu Ma, Shuang Liu, Zhi Qu, Huan Jiang, An-Hong Zhu, Hai-Xia Ma

Abstract

The species diversity of the family Graphostromataceae is poorly understood in tropical regions. The present work concerns a survey of wood-inhabiting macrofungi from the Hainan Tropical Rainforest, which is located in south-central Hainan Province. This study introduces two new species of Biscogniauxia, viz., B.
diaoluoshanensissp. nov. and B.
jianfengensissp. nov., which are herein described based on morphological and molecular evidence. Morphologically, B.
diaoluoshanensis is characterized by applanate stromata, ovoid to pyriform perithecia, and ascospores with a conspicuous straight, almost spore-length germ slit. Biscogniauxia
jianfengensis is distinguished by applanate to slightly pulvinate stromata, tubular perithecia, ascospores with an inconspicuous, unilateral, straight, spore-length germ slit, bearing an opaque cellular appendage on one side. The phylogenetic analysis inferred from ITS, RPB2 and TUB2 sequence data confirmed that the two new species are distinct within the genus Biscogniauxia. Detailed descriptions, illustrations, and phylogenetic analysis results are provided for each new species, and a dichotomous identification key is presented for the genus as well.

It is characterized by corticolous, carbonaceous bipartite stromata developing underneath the bark of the host and breaking when mature, unitunicate asci with an amyloid apical apparatus, and ellipsoid brown ascospores with germ slits and usually lacking a slimy sheath or slimy appendages (; ). The genus Biscogniauxia is the largest genus in the Graphostromataceae, and has been recorded worldwide, including North America (), Africa (; ), Europe (; ; ; ), Asia (; ; ; ; ; ) and Oceania (, ). Species of Biscogniauxia are widely known as saprobes, but some are plant pathogens causing diseases of dicotyledonous angiosperm trees including Acacia, Acer, Alnus, Artocarpus, Carya, Celtis, Coprosma, Cydonia, Eucalyptus, Fagus, Fraxinus, Gluta, Lithocarpus, Padus, Phyllirea, Pisonia, Populus, Prunus, Psidium, Pyrus, Quercus, Rhamnus, Rubus, and Tilia (e.g. ; , ; ; ; ; ; ; ; ; ), and some are endophytes which have the potential to become pathogens when their host plants are old and stressed (). In addition, some species are important producers of novel bioactive compounds and secondary metabolites (). Therefore, Biscogniauxia species are of great interest due to their ecological and economic significance.

One hundred and twenty records of Biscogniauxia are listed in Index Fungorum (http://www.indexfungorum.org/names/Names.asp/, accessed on 5 August 2026), and so far, more than 65 species are widely accepted (; ; ; ; ; ; ; ; ; , ; ; ). However, collections from tropical regions of China are few, and many undescribed novel species probably exist and are expected to be discovered. During the investigations of the diversity of wood-decaying fungi in Hainan Tropical Rainforest National Park, several specimens of Xylariales were collected. Two novel species of Biscogniauxia are identified using morphological evidence and molecular phylogenetic analyses, and a dichotomous identification key to Biscogniauxia taxa in China is proposed in this survey. The study makes a contribution to our understanding of the species diversity of Graphostromataceae in tropical China and reveals the phylogenetic relationships amongst Biscogniauxia species.

Materials and methods

Specimen collection and morphological study

The National Park of Hainan Tropical Rainforest lies in south-central Hainan Province, covering the coordinate range of 18°33'16"– 19°14'16"N and 108°44'32"– 110°04'43"E, with a tropical monsoon climate. The study area harbours 4,367 higher plant species belonging to 1,368 genera and 289 families. The dead branches bearing fungal stromata were collected from the National Park. Fresh specimens were dried, labeled, and transported for morphological and molecular studies. The studied specimens are deposited in the Fungarium of the Institute of Tropical Bioscience and Biotechnology, Chinese Academy of Tropical Agricultural Sciences (FCATAS).

The macro-morphological descriptions were observed from field notes and photos captured in the field. The stromatal surface and perithecia were observed and measured using a VHX-6000 3D microscope of the Keyence Corporation (Osaka, Japan). The micro-morphological data were obtained from dried specimens observed under an Olympus IX73 inverted fluorescence microscope (Tokyo, Japan) with a 10 × 100 oil immersion objective (). Sections mounted in water, Melzer’s reagent, and KOH were used to observe micro-morphological characters, following the method described by . At least 30 asci and 50 ascospores from each specimen were measured randomly. The following abbreviations are used: x̄ = arithmetical average length of all ascospores × arithmetical average width of all ascospores, and n = a number of ascospores measured from a number of specimens. When presenting the variation in ascospore sizes, 5% of measurements were excluded from each end of the range and given in parentheses.

DNA extraction, PCR amplification, and sequencing

Genomic DNA was extracted from the dried specimens using a Plant DNA Rapid Extraction Kit (Magen) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Primer pairs used for amplification were nuclear rDNA region encompassing the internal transcribed spacer (ITS), along with the 5.8S rDNA with ITS5/ITS4 (; ), the portion of the β-tubulin gene (TUB2) with Bt2a/Bt2b (), and the RNA polymerase II subunit (RPB2) with RPB2-5F/RPB2-7cR (). The PCR procedure was as follows: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 2 min, followed by 35 cycles of 95 °C for 30 s, annealing at 52 °C (ITS), 61 °C (TUB2), and 54 °C (RPB2) for 45 s each, extension at 72 °C for 1 min, and a final extension at 72 °C for 10 min. DNA sequencing was performed at Beijing Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd., and all the newly generated sequences were uploaded to NCBI GenBank and listed in Table .

Phylogenetic analyses

Phylogenetic analyses for Biscogniauxia and related genera, including Graphostroma, Camillea, Cryptostroma, and Obolarina, were performed with Bayesian inference (BI) and Maximum likelihood (ML) analyses based on the combined ITS-RPB2-TUB2 dataset (Table ). Jackrogersella
cohaerens (Pers.) L. Wendt, Kuhnert & M. Stadler and Hypoxylon
rubiginosum (Pers.) Fr. were selected as outgroups (; ; ; ). The sequences were aligned using the online analysis website MAFFT (MAFFT alignment and NJ/UPGMA phylogeny). The aligned sequences were trimmed using BioEdit software (), and individual gene datasets were concatenated using MEGA 6.0 (). The maximum likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree was constructed using the RAxML GUI v1.3.1 software, employing the GTRGAMMA model and the ML+rapid bootstrap procedure with 1,000 replicates to assess the confidence of each branch (). The trimmed sequences were converted to NEXUS format, and manual adjustments were made using ClustalX 1.83 (). Bayesian inference (BI) analyses were performed using MrBayes v.3.2.6 (). The best-fit substitution models for each gene were determined using jModelTest 2 (). Six Markov chains were run for 3,000,000 generations, with trees sampled every 100 generations. The first 25% of the sampled trees were discarded as burn-in, and the remaining trees were used to calculate posterior probabilities (). The phylogenetic tree was visually edited using FigTree v1.4.4 (http://tree.bio.ed.ac.uk/software/figtree/).

Results

Phylogenetic inference

The phylogenetic analysis was based on a total of 148 sequences from three DNA loci (56 ITS, 44 RPB2, and 48 TUB2) derived from 61 strains representing 43 species, including two newly described species, 31 known species of Biscogniauxia, 3 species of Camillea, one species of Cryptostroma, 2 species of Graphostroma, 2 species of Obolarina, and the outgroups Jackrogersella
cohaerens (YMJ 310) and Hypoxylon
rubiginosum (YMJ 24).

The dataset contained a total of 3,233 aligned characters, of which 1,683 were constant characters, 1,326 were phylogenetically informative characters, and 224 were variable characters that lacked parsimony information. Phylogenetic trees generated from ML and BI analyses of the combined dataset of ITS-RPB2-TUB2 were highly similar in topology. Only the ML tree is represented in Fig. , with ML bootstrap values ≥ 80% and Bayesian posterior probabilities ≥0.90 labelled along the branches. The phylogram based on the combined ITS-RPB2-TUB2 sequences (Fig. ) showed that our two species were assigned to the genus Biscogniauxia, in which B.
jianfengensis grouped with B.
philippinensis var. microspora Y.M. Ju & J.D. Rogers (YMJ 89041101) with high support value (BS = 100, BPP = 1), and B.
diaoluoshanensis resolved as a basal lineage of the other Biscogniauxia taxa included in this study. Based on phylogenetic and morphological evidence (described below), we propose two new species, namely B.
diaoluoshanensis sp. nov. and B.
jianfengensis sp. nov., to accommodate our specimens.

Remarks.

Biscogniauxia
diaoluoshanensis is characterized by slightly concave stromata with a blackish-brown surface and shiny black papillate ostioles, and nearly equilateral brown ascospores with a conspicuous straight, almost spore-length germ slit. Biscogniauxia
granmoi Lar. N. Vassiljeva resembles the new species in its stromatal morphology and ascospore shape but possesses larger perithecia (0.6–0.8 mm high × 0.3–0.5 mm broad) and larger asci (119–142 μm total length), and occurs on trunks and branches of Prunus (; ). Another similar species, B.
nummularia (Bull.: Fr.) Kuntze, resembles the new species in stromatal appearance, but can be distinguished by its larger obovoid perithecia (0.3–0.5 × 0.5–0.7 mm), longer asci (95–120 μm total length), and wider ascospores (7.5–8.5 μm) (; ). In addition, it occurs on wood of Fagus (; ). Phylogenetically, the new species falls within Biscogniauxia clade 4 yet exhibits no obvious affinity to other taxa in this clade. Clade 4 comprises the type species B.
nummularia, ten additional Biscogniauxia species, and our new collection. Biscogniauxia
nummularia (MUCL 51395, Holotype) differs from B.
diaoluoshanensis by 168 base pairs in TUB2 alignment, representing 11.52% of the total aligned TUB2 region. Strain CBS 969.70 of B.
nummularia shows 95-base-pair divergence in RPB2 and 164-base-pair divergence in TUB2 compared with the new species, corresponding to 9.27% and 11.28% of aligned nucleotide positions of RPB2 and TUB2 regions, respectively. Biscogniauxia
capnodes (YMJ 138) differs from B.
diaoluoshanensis by 83 base pairs in RPB2, accounting for 7.57% of the aligned RPB2 region. For other congeneric species, sequence similarity to the new species is below 91% for both RPB2 and TUB2. All previously known species display ITS sequence similarity below 86% relative to this novel taxon.

Remarks.

Phylogenetically, Biscogniauxia
jianfengensis forms a sister lineage to the tropical variety B.
philippinensis var. microspore, with this clade itself sister to B.
latirima Y.M. Ju & J.D. Rogers. Biscogniauxia
philippinensis var. microspore resembles the new species in stromatal morphology and ascospore shape but differs in having a larger perithecia (1.4–2.4 × 0.2–0.3 mm vs. 450–570 × 210–480 μm) and longer asci with the spore-bearing parts (asci 190–220 μm total length vs. 125–186 μm total length) (). In contrast, B.
latirima shares applanate stromata and similar perithecia shape but diverges in smaller apical apparatus (4–5 × 5–6 μm vs. 2.6–5.7 × 6.6–9.2 μm), and larger ascospores (18–21.5 × 10–12 μm vs. 14.8–18.8 × 8.5–10.6 μm) lacking a visible cellular appendage (). Morphologically, the new species B.
jianfengensis is similar to the tropical taxon B.
philippinensis in stromatal morphology, but the latter has larger ascospores (22–27 × 11.5–13 μm) with a straight, faint spore-length germ slit (). Unfortunately, no molecular sequences are available for B.
philippinensis, so nucleotide difference comparison could not be implemented.

Discussion

In the present study, two new species of Biscogniauxia from Hainan Tropical Rainforest National Park in China are described based on morphological characteristics and phylogenetic analyses. Phylogenetic analyses on the species of Biscogniauxia presented confirmed that the genus Biscogniauxia is polyphyletic, as in previous studies (; ; ; ; ). The species analyzed appeared mainly distributed in six separate clades (except B.
sinensis, B.
atropunctata (Schwein.) Pouzar, B.
uniapiculata, and B.
bartholomaei (Peck) Lar.N. Vassiljeva) in our study. Biscogniauxia
diaoluoshanensis and B.
jianfengensis were clearly separated from other Biscogniauxia species and from each other in Biscogniauxia clade 4 and clade 2 respectively. Biscogniauxia
nummularia, the type species of the genus, was included in clade 4. The phylogenetic tree indicates that the classification status of the genus Biscogniauxia remains ambiguous. In our phylogenetic analyses, Camillea species nested within the Biscogniauxia clade, which is consistent with recent findings reported by . Accordingly, further studies on the diversity, phylogeny, and evolution of Biscogniauxia require expanded sampling and molecular data from broader geographic regions.

A number of macrofungi belonging to Basidiomycota and Ascomycota have been extensively studied in recent years, and numerous new taxa have been described from Hainan Province (; ; , ; ; ; ; ; ; ). Members of Graphostromataceae are widely distributed across tropical regions worldwide; nevertheless, records from tropical Asia remain relatively sparse (; ; ; ). To date, only three species of Graphostromataceae have been documented from Hainan Province (; ). Therefore, the species diversity of Graphostromataceae is still largely underexplored on this tropical island, and further surveys across diverse forest types in Hainan are likely to yield additional new species.
