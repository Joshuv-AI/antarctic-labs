A new 240Pu PFNS evaluation was recently undertaken at LANL as a strategic priority. It truly
is an NCSP end-to-end product. It factors in a new diKerential experiment funded by NCSP,
builds on theoretical work coming out of a previous NCSP nuclear data evaluation
milestone and was validated with an NCERC experiment that was recently evaluated as an
integral benchmark with NCSP funds.

The 240Pu PFNS experiment, shown in Fig. 1, was undertaken by the LANL/ LLNL Chi-Nu
team. The 240Pu parallel plate avalanche counter was manufactured at LLNL by C.-Y. Wu
and R. Henderson. The experiment was subsequently undertaken and analyzed at LANL by
K. Kelly and M. Devlin. It is the ﬁrst 240Pu PFNS experiment for incident neutron energies
above 0.85 MeV, and spans a range of 1.5-20 MeV incident neutron energy with coverage
from 0.8-10 MeV in emitted neutron energy.

 Fig. 1 The Chi-Nu array used to measure the 240Pu PFNS.

These experimental data were merged in the evaluation by D. Neudecker with model values
from an extended Los Alamos model implemented in the code CoH3 by T. Kawano. A
portion of the model parameter systematics were established based on previous NCSP

work by A.E. Lovell and D. Neudecker simultaneously evaluating minor Pu isotope average
prompt ﬁssion neutron multiplicities.

The evaluated 240Pu PFNS resulting from 1.5 MeV incident neutrons agrees well with new
Chi-Nu data in Fig. 2. However, the new PFNS diKers at the peak of the spectrum and at
high outgoing neutron energies from ENDF/B-VIII.1 as the latter evaluation did not include
experimental PFNS data at all. These new evaluated PFNS results were benchmarked with
the high-240Pu Jupiter experiment by J. Bess and D. Shepard.
