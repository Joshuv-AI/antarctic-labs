Étale extensions of unipotent torsors
Gabriel Bassan

Abstract
. In this paper we study extension problems for torsors in positive characteristic. Let F F be a field of characteristic p > 0 p>0 and U / F U/F be a unipotent algebraic group. As our first main result, we prove that every U U -torsor defined over the generic point of a discrete valuation ring 𝒪 K \mathcal{O}_{K} , containing a field F F , extends to the normalization of 𝒪 K \mathcal{O}_{K} in some finite separable extension of its fraction field. We then globalize this result and prove that for X / F X/F a normal integral curve over an algebraically closed field F F , every U U -torsor over an open set X ∘ ⊆ X X^{\circ}\subseteq X extends to some ramified cover of X X which is étale over X ∘ X^{\circ} . As an application, we are able to find isomorphisms between certain unipotent variants of Nori’s fundamental group scheme for curves.



Introduction

Let
X
be a normal integral scheme,
X^{\circ}\subseteq X
a dense open subscheme, and
G
a group scheme over
X
. A basic question that appears in several guises (compactifications of torsors, ramification theory, and “tame/wild” variants of fundamental groups for example) is the following:

Question
.

Given a
G
-torsor
P\to X^{\circ}
, can one extend it across the boundary to all of
X
?

Although on nice situations (
X/F
a normal integral variety over a field and
G/F
a smooth connected algebraic group for example), one can extend torsors by normalizing
X
in the function field of
P
, the resulting
X
-scheme usually cease to be a torsor due to ramification behavior on the boundary.

Example
.

Let
n>1
be an integer. Let
F
be a field such that
\operatorname{char}(F)\nmid n
and
F
contain the
n
-th roots of unity. Consider
X\vcentcolon=\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
X^{\circ}\vcentcolon=X\setminus\{0,\infty\}
and the
\mu_{n}
-torsor

P\vcentcolon=\Spec F[t,t^{-1},T]/(T^{n}-t)\to X^{\circ}\simeq\Spec F[t,t^{-1}].

Taking the normalization of
X
in the function field of
P
, we obtain (up to isomorphism) the
n
-th power morphism
\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
t\mapsto t^{n}
. This map is ramified at
0
and
\infty
and therefore, cannot be a
\mu_{n}
-torsor.

Nonetheless, for a finite étale group
G
, one can bypass the ramification issue by passing to some finite étale cover
V\to X^{\circ}
and show that every
G
-torsor extends to the normalization of
X
in (the fraction field of) some finite étale cover
V
(see Proposition
1.12
). This suggests the following refined question.

Question
.

Given a
G
-torsor
P\to X^{\circ}
, can one extend it to some finite cover of
X
ramified only across the boundary
X\setminus X^{\circ}
?

In characteristic
0
, since every finite group scheme is étale, the answer is always affirmative for finite groups. In characteristic
p>0
, where finite non-étale group schemes can occur naturally, the question becomes more delicate, even for finite group schemes. Our main goal is to show that, nevertheless, torsors under unipotent groups over curves do admit extensions after passing to some cover ramified outside of
X^{\circ}
. Our first result answers a local version of this question for unipotent groups.

Theorem A
Theorem
3.14
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be a DVR, containing a field
F
of characteristic
p>0
, with fraction field
K
and perfect residue field. Let
U/F
be a unipotent group and
P\to\Spec K
be a
U
-torsor. Then, there exists a finite separable extension
L/K
such that the base change
P_{L}\to\Spec L
extends to the normalization of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
in
L
.

We then globalize this result and obtain the following theorem.

Theorem B
Theorem
4.4
.

Let
X/F
be a normal integral curve over an algebraically closed field
F
of characteristic
p>0
and
U/F
be a unipotent group scheme. Let
X^{\circ}\subseteq X
be a nonempty open set and
P\to X^{\circ}
be a
U
-torsor. Then, there exists a finite surjective morphism
Y\to X
which is étale over
X^{\circ}
such that
P
extends to
Y
.

As an application, we explain how to define a natural intermediate fundamental group scheme
\pi^{n}(X^{\circ})
(which has been previously defined in
[
2
]
), sitting between the Nori fundamental group of
X^{\circ}
and
X
, i.e

\pi^{N}(X^{\circ})\twoheadrightarrow\pi^{n}(X^{\circ})\twoheadrightarrow\pi^{N}(X)

and prove that the “gap” between
\pi^{N}(X^{\circ})
and
\pi^{n}(X^{\circ})
has “no unipotent part”. More precisely, we prove the following result.

Theorem C
Theorem
5.12
.

If
X/F
is a normal separated integral curve over an algebraically closed field
F
of characteristic
p>0
and
X^{\circ}\subseteq X
is a nonempty open set, then the morphism

\pi^{N}(X^{\circ})\twoheadrightarrow\pi^{n}(X^{\circ})

becomes an isomorphism after passing to the maximal pro-unipotent quotients.

Organization of the paper

•

In Section
1
we go through some preliminary notions. In
1.1
we give a review on nonabelian cohomology, with an important emphasis on the obstruction to lifting torsors in an exact sequence. This gives the cohomological backbone of our extension arguments in the following sections. In
1.2
we precisely define and obtain some simple results on étale extensions of torsors. In
1.3
we recall the ramification behavior of Artin–Schreier extensions.

•

Section
2
is devoted to proving a local version of our extension result for discrete valuation rings and the group
\alpha_{p}
. This is mostly done by explicit calculations involving uniformizers of Artin–Schreier extensions. This section provides the initial results needed to start the more abstract machinery used in the following sections.

•

In Section
3
we extend the results of Section
2
to unipotent groups, but still over a DVR (
Theorem A
). For this, we make a “dévissage” argument using the machinery of nonabelian cohomology to reduce the theorems to statements of the preceding section.

•

In Section
4
we are able to extend our results to curves and prove
Theorem B
by using the results of Section
3
to find local extensions of a torsor along the boundary of an open set and then use a Riemann–Roch argument to find a suitable cover which allows us to glue the local extensions.

•

In the last section, Section
5
, we show some applications (
Theorem C
) of our results to the Nori fundamental group scheme of open curves.

Notation and conventions

Throughout this paper, a ring
R
will always be unital commutative. A variety over a field
F
means a separated
F
-scheme of finite type. A curve over a field
F
is a variety of dimension
1
. Every group scheme
G/X
is supposed to be fppf over its base scheme
X
. An algebraic group
G
over a field
F
is an
F
-group scheme. An algebraic group
G/F
is said to be unipotent if it admits a composition series over
\overline{F}
with successive quotients isomorphic to subgroups of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
.

All torsors and cohomology groups are taken in the fppf topology. A
G
-torsor
P\to X
means a right
G
-torsor.

Given a field
K
, a valuation on
K
will always be assumed to be discrete (with valuation group a discrete subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
). In this setup, we usually denote the valuation ring of
v
by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and its residue field by
k
. For a finite extension
L/K
we usually denote an extension of
v
to
L
by
v_{L}
and we write
e(v_{L}|v)
for the ramification index and
f(v_{L}|v)
for the residue degree. By a normalized valuation we mean a valuation with image equal to
\mathbb{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. Since every discrete subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
is of the form
\alpha\mathbb{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, we can always normalize (non trivial) valuations in a unique way. We will sometimes say that a normalized valuation
v_{L}
extends some normalized valuation
v
to mean that
v_{L}
is the normalization of a valuation extending
v
. We will usually denote the normalization of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
in
L
by
\mathcal{O}_{L}
.

Given a field
K
of characteristic
p>0
, we will denote the Artin–Schreier polynomial
\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(T)\in K[T]
as
\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(T)\vcentcolon=T^{p}-T\in K[T]
. Given
f\in K
, we denote the splitting field of
\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(T)-a\in K[T]
by
K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},f}/K
. Whenever
\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(T)-f
is irreducible, this is a finite Galois extension of
K
with Galois group
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
called an Artin–Schreier extension.

1.
Preliminaries and auxiliary results

We start by covering some preliminaries. We will not necessarily give full proofs of propositions, but sketches and references will be given.

1.1.
Nonabelian cohomology and twists

Notation 1.1
.

Let
G
be a group scheme over a scheme
X
, we denote by

H^{1}(X,G)

the set of isomorphism classes of (right)
G
-torsors (not necessarily representable). This set has a preferred point given by the trivial torsor. With this, we will usually look at
H^{1}(X,G)
as a pointed set.

Remark 1.2
.

If
G
is commutative, this group agrees with the first cohomology of
G
as an abelian sheaf on the fppf site of
X
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
G
be a group scheme over
X
. Let
P
be a (right)
G
-torsor, and let
Y\to X
be an
X
-scheme equipped with a (left)
G
-action
a\colon G\times_{X}Y\to Y
. Define the twist (or contracted product) of
Y
by
P
to be the fppf sheaf

\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 12.40814pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P,a}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 12.40814pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P,a}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 13.4258pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P,a}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 14.43999pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P,a}}}}\;Y\vcentcolon=P\times^{G,a}Y\vcentcolon=(P\times_{X}Y)/G,

where
G
acts (on the left) on
P\times_{X}Y
by

g\cdot(p,y)\vcentcolon=(p\cdot g^{-1},\;a(g)\cdot y).

When no confusion arises, we will omit the action
a
in the notation and denote the twist simply by
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 6.66771pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.66771pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.23419pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.24838pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}\;Y
or
P\times^{G}Y
.

If
f\colon G\to H
is a morphism of
X
-group schemes, and
P\to X
is a
G
-torsor, the same construction yields an
H
-torsor
P_{H}\vcentcolon=f_{\ast}P\vcentcolon=P\times^{G}H
, where
G
acts on
H
through
f
. We call this torsor the induced torsor. Twisting is compatible with morphisms of
X
-group schemes
H\to G
, functorial with respect to
Y
and compatible with base change
X^{\prime}\to X
by an fppf morphism.

Remark 1.4
.

Observe that given a class
\alpha\in H^{1}(X,G)
, two different representatives
P
and
Q
of
\alpha
, give rise to isomorphic twists, but not in a unique way. In this sense, the construction really depends on the torsor and not only in its cohomology class. Nonetheless, we will sometimes write
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 5.69876pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\alpha}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 5.69876pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\alpha}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 7.55594pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\alpha}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.57013pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\alpha}}}}\;Y
to mean a twist by some representative of
\alpha
.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
G
be a group scheme over
X
and
Y\to X
an affine morphism with a
G
-action. Then
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 6.66771pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.66771pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.23419pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.24838pt\mathopen{\vphantom{\;Y}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}\;Y

For the representability, this follows from effective fppf descent of affine morphisms (see

Proposition 1.6

Let

\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 6.66771pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.66771pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.08376pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.05048pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}A
has a canonical

-group scheme structure such that

\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 6.66771pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.66771pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.08376pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.05048pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}A

Proof.

From the construction of the twist as a gluing of

which is compatible with trivializations. Moreover, since commutativity amounts to an equality between morphisms, it can be checked fppf locally.
∎

Assume we have an exact sequence of
X
-group schemes

1\to A\xrightarrow{u}G\xrightarrow{v}H\to 1

with

comutative. Conjugation gives an action of

on

, and since

is abelian, this factors through an action (the adjoint action) of

on

which we will denote by

or sometimes simply by

. For an

, we can therefore define the twist
\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 6.66771pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.66771pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.08376pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.05048pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}A
using the

-action on

. Under these assumptions, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
P\to X
be an
H
-torsor. Then, there exists an element (the obstruction class)

d(P)\in H^{2}(X,\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 6.66771pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 6.66771pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 8.08376pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 9.05048pt\mathopen{\vphantom{A}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{P}}}}A)

such that

if and only if

is induced from a

-torsor. Moreover, if

, then
(v_{\ast})^{-1}([P])
is a homogeneous space under

Before finishing this section, we prove a lemma relating torsors induced through the absolute Frobenius morphism and the Frobenius twist of a torsor.

Lemma 1.9
.

Let
R
be a ring of characteristic
p>0
,
G/R
a group scheme and
P
a
G
-torsor (not necessarily representable). Let
F^{N}_{G}\colon G\to G^{(p^{N})}
be the

th Frobenius morphism of

. Then
(F^{N}_{G})_{\ast}P\cong P^{(p^{N})}
as
G^{(p^{N})}
-torsors.

Proof.

The proof can be found in
[
5
, Lemma 3.1]
, but we will give the argument here. Consider the morphism
\Psi\colon P\times_{R}G^{(p^{N})}\to P^{(p^{N})}
given on the level of functors by
(p,h)\mapsto F^{N}_{P}(p)h
. This map is clearly
G^{(p^{N})}
-invariant (with
G^{(p^{N})}
(right) acting on the left-hand side by acting on the second coordinate). Moreover, it is
G
-invariant for the diagonal (left) action of

on

and the trivial action on

. Indeed, for points

(resp.

,

) of

(resp.

,

, we have

\Psi(g\cdot(p,h))=\Psi(pg^{-1},F_{G}^{N}(g)h)=F^{N}_{P}(pg^{-1})F^{N}_{G}(g)h.

But since the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P\times_{R}G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{(p^{N})}\times_{R}G^{(p^{N})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{(p^{N})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{P}^{N}\times F_{G}^{N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{P}^{N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a^{(p^{N})}}

commutes, we have that

1.2.
Étale extensions of torsors

We begin by recalling the notion of étale extensions of torsors.

Definition 1.10
.

Let
X
be a normal integral scheme,
X^{\circ}\subseteq X
a dense open subscheme and
G/X
an
X
-group scheme. Given
P\to X^{\circ}
a
G
-torsor, we say that
P
étale extends to
X
(or is étale extendable) if there exists a connected normal scheme
Y
and a finite surjective morphism
f\colon Y\to X
étale over
X^{\circ}
together with a
G_{Y}
-torsor
P_{Y}\to Y
whose restriction to
f^{-1}(X^{\circ})
is (isomorphic to)
f^{\ast}P\to f^{-1}(X^{\circ})
. We say that any such
P_{Y}
is an étale extension of
P
over
Y
.

Remark 1.11
.

Let me point out that if
X
is a normal integral variety over a field, this definition is equivalent to asking the following: there exists a connected étale cover
f\colon V\to X^{\circ}
such that the torsor
f^{\ast}P\to X^{\circ}
extends to the normalization of
X
in
K(X^{\circ})
. This is the definition of a torsor “étale locally extending” given in
[
2
, Definition 3.2.]
. Indeed, this definition implies ours by taking
f\colon Y\to X
as the normalization of
X
in the function field
K(V)
(by
[
7
, Corollary 12.52]

f\colon Y\to X
will be finite) and observing that
f^{-1}(X^{\circ})=V
. On the other hand, given our
f\colon Y\to X
, setting
V=f^{-1}(X^{\circ})
gives us a connected finite étale cover
V\to X^{\circ}
and moreover, since
Y
is normal, it is the normalization of
X
in
K(V)=K(Y)
is
Y
.

From now on, let us fix a field
F
. We will be interested in understanding which torsors admit étale extensions. First of all, recall that given an algebraic group
G/F
, we have an exact sequence

1\to G^{\circ}\to G\to\pi_{0}(G)\to 1\;,

where
\pi_{0}(G)
is the the group of connected components of
G
, which is finite étale, and
G^{\circ}
is the identity component of
G
.

For finite étale groups, it is easy to see that all torsors étale extend.

Proposition 1.12
.

Let
G/F
be a finite étale group scheme,
X/F
a normal integral
F
-scheme and
X^{\circ}\subseteq X
a dense open subscheme. Then every
G
-torsor over
X^{\circ}
étale extends to
X
.

Proof.

Given a fixed nontrivial
G
-torsor
p\colon P\to X^{\circ}
, any connected component of it will be a finite étale cover of
X^{\circ}
. Fix a connected component
T
. Since
P
is trivial after pullback by itself, it will also be trivial after pullback to
T
. Therefore,
P_{T}\cong G_{T}
extends trivially to the normalization of
X
in
K(T)
, proving the claim.
∎

In particular, since every finite group scheme is étale in characteristic
0
, we have the following obvious corollary.

Corollary 1.13
.

If
F
is a field of characteristic
0
, then every torsor for any finite group scheme étale extends.

We now show how to reduce problems of étale extensions to connected groups.

Proposition 1.14
.

Let
G/F
be an algebraic group,
X/F
a normal integral variety over
F
and
X^{\circ}\subseteq X
an open subscheme. Let
P\to X^{\circ}
be a
G
-torsor and
P_{0}\to X^{\circ}
the
\pi_{0}(G)
-torsor induced by
G\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{0}(G)
. Suppose that one of the following conditions hold:

(1)

The cohomology class of
P_{0}\to X^{\circ}
is trivial in
H^{1}(X^{\circ},\pi_{0}(G))
and every
G^{\circ}
-torsor on
X^{\circ}
étale extends to
X
.

(2)

There exists a connected component
T
of
P_{0}
such that every
G^{\circ}
-torsor on
T
étale extends to
Z
, where
Z
is the normalization of
X
in
K(T)
.

Then
P\to X^{\circ}
étale extends to
X
.

Proof.

If the class of

in

is trivial, by the exact sequence in (non-comutative) cohomology associated to

we have that
P\to X^{\circ}
comes from
G^{\circ}
. Therefore, it is clear that
P\to X^{\circ}
étale extends to
X
.

If the class of
\varphi\colon P_{0}\to X^{\circ}
in

is not trivial and the second hypothesis holds, we can pull back

by the connected étale cover

to obtain a

\left.\varphi\right|_{T}^{\ast}P\to T
such that its induced

-torsor has a trivial class in

. As before, this implies that
\left.\varphi\right|_{T}^{\ast}P\to T
comes from a

-torsor

and so by hypothesis we can find a connected étale cover

such that

in

.
If we prove that the normalization

of

in

is the same as the normalization of

in

, then we are done by taking the composition
\psi\circ\left.\varphi\right|_{T}V\to X
as the connected étale cover. This follows from
[
13
,
Tag 035I
]
applied to the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec K(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\;,}

which gives a map

realizing

as the normalization of

in

.
∎

Before finishing this section, let me observe that given two étale extendable torsors, we can always find a finite morphism where both extend at the same time.

Lemma 1.15
.

Let
G/F
be an algebraic group,

a normal integral variety over

(i=1,2)

Indeed, if

in which

.
∎

1.3.
Ramification of Artin–Schreier extensions

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
equipped with a normalized discrete valuation
v
and perfect residue field
k
. In this section, for the convenience of the reader, we briefly go through the ramification behavior of Artin–Schreier extensions
K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},f}
of
K
. The contents of this should be well-known and can also be found in
[
14
, Propositions 3.7.7 and 3.7.8]
. To be more precise with the setup, fix a non-trivial Artin–Schreier extension
L\vcentcolon=K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},f}
and a discrete valuation
v_{L}
extending
v
. Since
L
is Galois of order
p
, we have the equality

p=g\cdot e(v_{L}|v)\cdot f(v_{L}|v),

where
g
is the number of distinct discrete valuations in
L
extending
v
. In particular, only one among
g
,
e(v_{L}|v)
and
f(v_{L}|v)
is equal to
p
while the rest are equal to
1
. The valuation of
f
will determine in which case we are.

Remark 1.16
.

It is not hard to see that
K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},f+\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(g)}\cong K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},f}
as extensions of
K
, and therefore, Artin–Schreier extensions
K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},f}/K
only depend (up to isomorphism) on the class of
f
in the additive group
K/\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(K)
.

Lemma 1.17
.

For any
f\in K
there exists
g\in K
such that
v(f+\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(g))\geqslant 0
or
v(f+\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(g))<0
and
p\nmid v(f+\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(g))
. In particular, any Artin–Schreier extension is of the form
K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},f}
with either
v(f)\geqslant 0
or
v(f)<0
and prime to
p
.

Proof.

Fix
f\in K
. Suppose that
v(f)=-mp
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
(so that
f
is not already of the desired form). Let
t\in K
be a uniformizer and write

f=ut^{-mp}

for some
u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
. Let
\overline{u}\in k^{\times}
be its residue. Since
k
is perfect, there exists
\overline{c}\in k^{\times}
with
\overline{c}^{p}=\overline{u}
. Now, let
c\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
be a lift of
\overline{c}
and
g\vcentcolon=ct^{-m}
. We then have that

f-\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(g)=ut^{-mp}-c^{p}t^{-mp}+ct^{-m}=(u-c^{p})t^{-mp}+ct^{-m}.

Since
\overline{(u-c^{p})}=0\in k
, we have that
v(u-c^{p})>0
and therefore,

v(f-\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(g))>-mp=v(f).

Let
f_{1}=f-\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(g)
. Then
K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},f_{1}}\cong K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},f}
and
v(f_{1})>v(f)
. If either
v(f_{1})<0
and
p\nmid v(f_{1})
or
v(f_{1})\geqslant 0
we are done. Otherwise we can continue this process to eventually (in a finite number of steps) reach some
g
such that
K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},g}\cong K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},f}
satisfying either
v(g)<0
and
p\nmid v(g)
and
v(g)\geqslant 0
.
∎

Lemma 1.18
.

Suppose that
v(f)>0
and
f\not\in\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(K)
. Let
v_{L}
be a valuation on
L\vcentcolon=K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},f}
extending
v
. Then
e(v_{L}|v)=f(v_{L}|v)=1
and
g=p
. In particular,
L/K
is unramified at
v
.

Proof.

Since passing to the henselizations of
K
and
L
does not change
e(v_{L}|v)
and
f(v_{L}|v)
, we can suppose that
K
and
L
are henselian. In this case, since
v(f)>0
, we have that
\overline{f}=0\in\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(k)
. By Hensel’s lemma, we have that
f\in\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(K)
and therefore,
L=K
. We conclude that
e(v_{L}|v)=f(v_{L}|v)=1
.
∎

Lemma 1.19
.

Suppose that
v(f)=0
and
f\not\in\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(K)
. Let
v_{L}
be the (unique) valuation on
L=K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},f}
extending
v
. Then we have that
e(v_{L}|v)=g=1
and
f(v_{L}|v)=p
. In particular,
L/K
is unramified at
v
.

Proof.

Let
x\in L
such that
x^{p}-x=f
. Since
v(f)=0
, an easy calculation shows that
v_{L}(x)=0
. Therefore, we can reduce the preceding equation to the residue field
l
of
v_{L}
to conclude that the polynomial
\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(T)-\overline{f}\in l[T]
is completely reducible. Therefore,
k_{\overline{f}}\subseteq l
and
f(v_{L}|v)=[l\colon k]\geqslant p
. Since
p=g\cdot f(v_{L}|v)\cdot e(v_{L}|v)
, we conclude that
f(v_{L}|v)=p
and
g=e(v_{L}|v)=1
.
∎

Lemma 1.20
.

Suppose that
v(f)<0
and
p\nmid v(f)
. Let
v_{L}
be the (unique) valuation on
L=K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},f}
extending
v
. Then we have that
e(v_{L}|v)=p
and
f(v_{L}|v)=g=1
. In particular,
L/K
is totally and wildly ramified of degree
p
at
v
.

Proof.

Let us start by calculating
v_{L}(x)
. If
v_{L}(x)\geqslant 0
, we would have that

v(f)=v_{L}(f)=v_{L}(x^{p}-x)\geqslant\text{min}\{pv_{L}(x),v_{L}(x)\}\geqslant 0\;.

This is a contradiction since
v(f)<0
. Therefore
v_{L}(x)<0
and we have that

v(f)=v_{L}(f)=v_{L}(x^{p}-x)=\text{min}\{pv_{L}(x),v_{L}(x)\}=pv_{L}(x)\;,

i.e.
v_{L}(x)=v(f)/p
. We conclude that
v_{L}(L)
contains a subgroup of index at least
p
and so,
e(v_{L}|v)\geqslant p
. Since
p=g\cdot f(v_{L}|v)\cdot e(v_{L}|v)
, we must have that
g=f(v_{L}|v)=1
and
e(v_{L}|v)=p
.
∎

Remark 1.21
.

Let me observe that if
v(f)<0
is prime to
p
, then
f\not\in\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$}(K)
. Indeed, if
f=x^{p}-x
, a calculation shows that
v(f)=pv(x)
, i.e. it is divisible by
p
.

2.
Étale extending
\alpha_{p}
-torsors over DVRs

In this section, we will be interested in understanding when
\alpha_{p}
-torsors on the generic point of a discrete valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{K}
étale extends to all of
\Spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
. The main theorem of this section is the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be a DVR of positive characteristic
p>0
with fraction field
K
and perfect residue field. Then every
\alpha_{p}
-torsor on
\Spec K
étale extends to
\Spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

To get to this statement, let me start by recalling some facts regarding cohomology with
\alpha_{p}
coefficients.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring. Then we have the following

•

H^{1}(R,\alpha_{p})
is functorially isomorphic to the quotient group
R/R^{p}
;

•

H^{i}(R,\alpha_{p})=0
for
i>1
.

Proof.

Consider the exact sequence of (fppf-)sheaves

0\rightarrow\alpha_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a}\xrightarrow{F}\mathbb{G}_{a}\rightarrow 0,

where
F
is the frobenius morphism. Observe that
\mathbb{G}_{a,R}=\mathcal{O}_{R}
as sheaves on the fppf-site (see
[
13
,
Tag 03OG
]
). Therefore, by
[
13
,
Tag 03P2
]
, the fppf-cohomology of
\mathbb{G}_{a,R}
is isomorphic to the quasi-coherent cohomology of the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{R}
of
R
. Since
\Spec R
is affine, we have that
H^{i}(R,\mathcal{O}_{R})=0
for all
i>0
.
By looking at the cohomology long exact sequence we have that
H^{i}(R,\alpha_{p})=0
for
i>0
and moreover, we obtain the exact sequence

0\to\alpha_{p}(R)\to R\xrightarrow{F}R\to H^{1}(R,\alpha_{p})\to 0\;;

i.e., we have an isomorphism
R/\text{Im}(F)=R/R^{p}\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{1}(R,\alpha_{p})
. Functoriality follows from that of the long exact sequence itself.
∎

For the rest of this section, let us fix a (not necessarily complete) discrete valuation ring (DVR)
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of positive characteristic
p>0
with fraction field
K
, perfect residue field
k
and normalized valuation
v
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
L/K
be an extension of fields and
v_{L}
a discrete valuation of
L
extending
v
. We define the abelian group

Q_{L}\vcentcolon=H^{1}(L,\alpha_{p})/H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\alpha_{p})

and denote the class of an element

in
Q_{L}
by
[a]_{L}
. Given extensions
M/L/K
of fields and extensions of the valuation
v
, we denote by
Q_{L}\xrightarrow{(\cdot)_{M}}Q_{M}
the map induced by
H^{1}(L,\alpha_{p})\to H^{1}(M,\alpha_{p})
.

The group
Q_{K}
measures which
\alpha_{p}
-torsors over the generic point of
\Spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
extend to
\Spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

The isomorphisms
R/R^{p}\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{1}(R,\alpha_{p})
for
R=L
and
R=\mathcal{O}_{L}
induce an isomorphism
L/(\mathcal{O}_{L}+L^{p})\xrightarrow{\sim}Q_{L}
.

Proof.

This is clear from Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

If
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is complete, then
Q_{L}
has the structure of a
k
-vector space for any extension
L/K
. Moreover, for extensions

the induced map
Q_{L}\to Q_{M}
is
k
-linear.

Proof.

By Cohen’s struture Theorem (see
[
9
, 28.P]
), if
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is complete, there exists a section
k\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{K}
of the reduction map
\mathcal{O}_{K}\to k
, endowing
K
and therefore any extension
L/K
with a
k
-vector space structure. It is clear that
\mathcal{O}_{L}
is a sub
k
-vector space, and since
k^{p}=k
,
L^{p}
is also a sub

-vector space of

. Therefore, the quotient
Q_{L}
is also a
k
-vector space. It is also clear that given extensions

, the map
Q_{L}\to Q_{M}
is
k
-linear.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is complete. If
[a]_{K}\in Q_{K}
is nonzero, then it has a representative
a_{0}\in K
with
v(a_{0})<0
and
p\nmid v(a_{0})
. More precisely, given a uniformizer
t
of
K
, we can arrange so that the power series expansion of
a_{0}
is a finite sum of negative exponent terms all non divisible by
p
.

Proof.

Let
t
be a uniformizer of
K
and

a=\sum_{m\geqslant-N}b_{m}t^{m}

be the expansion of
a
as a power series on
t
with coefficients in
k
. We can then write
a=b+a_{1}
where

is the positive part of the expansion, and

is a finite combination of negative powers of

Moreover, since

p\nmid m\end{subarray}}}^{-1}b_{m}t^{m}.

In conclusion, we have

p\nmid m\end{subarray}}}^{-1}b_{m}t^{m}.

where

and

. Thus, taking

p\nmid m\end{subarray}}}^{-1}b_{m}t^{m},

we have that

∎

Remark 2.7
.

Lemma 2.8

Let

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
t
be a uniformizer of
K
and let

be prime to
p
. Let

the normalized valuation of

\frac{1}{t^{m}}=\frac{1}{\pi^{mp}}+g\in L

Proof.

This is equivalent to proving that
v_{L}\left(\frac{1}{t^{m}}-\frac{1}{\pi^{mp}}\right)=(p-1)s-mp
.
Let us start by calculating
v_{L}(t^{m}-\pi^{mp})
. Observe that

\displaystyle t^{m}-\pi^{mp}

\displaystyle=t^{m}-(x^{p})^{mu}t^{mpv}=t^{m}-\left(\frac{1}{t^{s}}+x\right)^{mu}t^{mpv}\;

\displaystyle=t^{m}-t^{mpv}\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\binom{mu}{n}\frac{x^{n}}{t^{(msu-sn)}}

\displaystyle=t^{m}-t^{mpv}\frac{1}{t^{smu}}-t^{mpv}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\binom{mu}{n}\frac{x^{n}}{t^{(msu-sn)}}

\displaystyle=t^{m}-t^{m(-su+pv)}-t^{mpv}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\binom{mu}{n}\frac{x^{n}}{t^{(msu-sn)}}

\displaystyle=-t^{mpv}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\binom{mu}{n}\frac{x^{n}}{t^{(msu-sn)}}\;.

To calculate

not divisible by

v_{L}\left(\binom{mu}{n}\frac{x^{n}}{t^{(msu-sn)}}\right)=-pmsu+sn(p-1)\;.

Since all of these valuations are different as

\displaystyle v_{L}\left(\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\binom{mu}{n}\frac{x^{n}}{t^{(msu-sn)}}\right)

\displaystyle=\text{min}\{-pmsu+sn(p-1)\}

\displaystyle=-pmsu+s(p-1)\;,

where the minimun is taken among

, and achieved at

\displaystyle v_{L}(t^{m}-\pi^{mp})

\displaystyle=v_{L}(-t^{mpv}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\binom{mu}{n}\frac{x^{n}}{t^{(msu-sn)}})

\displaystyle=mp^{2}v-pmsu+s(p-1)

\displaystyle=mp(-su+pv)+s(p-1)

\displaystyle=mp+s(p-1)\;.

We then obtain that

\displaystyle v_{L}\left(\frac{1}{t^{m}}-\frac{1}{\pi^{mp}}\right)

\displaystyle=v_{L}\left(\frac{\pi^{mp}-t^{m}}{t^{m}\pi^{mp}}\right)

\displaystyle=v_{L}(\pi^{mp}-t^{m})-v_{L}(t^{m}\pi^{mp})

\displaystyle=mp+(p-1)s-2mp

Recall that if

Proposition 2.10

Suppose that

Lemma 2.12

Let

Lemma 2.21
.

Let

be a field. Let

be nonequivalent nontrivial discrete valuations of

3.
Étale extending unipotent torsors over DVRs

In this section we modify slightly our setup. We fix a field
F
of characteristic
p>0
and a (not necessarily complete) DVR containing
F
denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with fraction field
K
, perfect residue field
k
, normalized valuation
v
and uniformizer
t
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
F
be a field and
G/F
be an algebraic group . A subnormal series
S
of length
n
of
G
is a finite sequence of subgroups of
G

S\colon G_{0}\subseteq G_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{n}

such that
G_{0}=1
,
G_{n}=G
and
G_{i}
is normal in
G_{i+1}
for all
i
. A subnormal series is a characteristic series if each
G_{i}
is characteristic in
G
, i.e invariant under automorphisms of
G
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
F
be a field of characteristic
p>0
and
U/F
be a connected unipotent group. Then there exists a characteristic series

S\colon 1=U_{0}\subseteq U_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq U_{n}=U,

of
U
and an integer
0\leqslant n_{0}\leqslant n
such that
U_{i}/U_{i-1}\cong\alpha_{p}^{r}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n_{0}
and
U_{i}/U_{i-1}\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}^{r_{i}}
for
n_{0}<i\leqslant n
.

Proof.

This is part of
[
3
, Exp. XVII, Prop. 4.3.1]
.
∎

Let me observe that these kind of series are not unique and that
n
and
n_{0}
do depend on the series! Indeed, even for
\mathbb{G}_{a}
we can see this. In this case we have two distinct characteristic series as above,

1\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{a}

and

1\subseteq\alpha_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{a}.

Nevertheless, if
n_{0}=n
for some characteristic series
S
as above, then the group is infinitesimal and the same will be true for all such series.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
F
be a field of characteristic
p>0
and
U/F
be a connected unipotent group. We define integers
0\leqslant n_{0}(U)\leqslant n(U)
as

n(U)\vcentcolon=\min\{n\mid S\text{ is a series as in Lemma \ref{serie unipotente corpo} of length $n$}\}.

and

n_{0}(U)\vcentcolon=\min\{n_{0}\mid S\text{ is a series as in Lemma \ref{serie unipotente corpo} with length equal to $n(U)$}\}.

Example 3.4
.

Fix an integer
r>0
. We have a copies of
\alpha_{p^{i}}
for
0\leqslant i\leqslant r
inside
\alpha_{p^{r}}
given by the kernels of the
i
th power of Frobenius. This gives us a characteristic series

0\subseteq\alpha_{p}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\alpha_{p^{r}}

of length
r
with successive quotients given by
\alpha_{p}
.

Example 3.5
.

Let
F
be a nonperfect field and
a\in F\setminus F^{p}
. Let
G\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2}
be the closed subgroup given by the equation
y^{p}=x+ax^{p}
. This is a
1
-dimensional smooth connected subgroup which is a nontrivial twist of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
(see
[
10
, Example 14.20.]
). Over
F^{\prime}\vcentcolon=F(a^{1/p})
we have an isomorphism
\mathbb{G}_{a,F^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{\sim}G_{F^{\prime}}
given by

t\mapsto(t^{p},t+a^{1/p}t^{p}).

Now, consider the map
G\to\mathbb{G}_{a}
given by the projection on the first coordinate. This is an fppf morphism whose kernel is given by the pairs
(0,y)
, which is isomorphic to
\alpha_{p}
. Thus, we have a (non-split) exact sequence

0\to\alpha_{p}\to G\to\mathbb{G}_{a}\to 0,

giving a characteristic series of
G
with successive quotients isomorphic to
\alpha_{p}
or
\mathbb{G}_{a}
.

Our strategy to prove our theorem for unipotent groups is to first deal with the cases where the group is
\alpha_{p}
or
\mathbb{G}_{a}
and then use Lemma
3.2
to deduce a general result. First we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
F
be a field of characteristic
p>0
. We have that

\underline{\text{Aut}}(\alpha_{p,F}^{r})\cong\text{GL}_{r,F}.

as group functors.

Proof.

By
[
3
, Exp. VII
A
, Remarque 7.5.]
, for any ring
R
of characteristic
p>0
, we have an equivalence of categories between affine flat
R
-group schemes of finite presentation with trivial Frobenius morphism and finite dimensional
p
-Lie algebras over
R
. Under this equivalence,
\alpha_{p,R}^{r}
corresponds to the Lie algebra
R^{r}
with zero bracket and zero
p
-map. It is then clear that the
R
-automorphisms of this
p
-Lie algebra is
GL_{r}(R)
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
0\to\alpha_{p}^{r}\xrightarrow{u}G\xrightarrow{v}H\to 0
be an exact sequence of affine flat
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-group schemes of finite presentation. Let
\alpha\in H^{1}(K,G)
and suppose that there exists

(\beta_{H})_{K}=v_{\ast}(\alpha)\in H^{1}(K,H)
. Then for any Artin–Schreier extension
L\vcentcolon=K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},t^{-s}}
with

not divisible by

, we have that
\alpha
étale extend to
\Spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
after passing to

.

Proof.

By Proposition
1.7
, the obstruction class for

to be in the image of

lives in
H^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K},\mathchoice{\mathop{}\kern 11.45807pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(\alpha_{p}^{r})}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\beta_{H}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 11.45807pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(\alpha_{p}^{r})}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\beta_{H}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 17.3176pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(\alpha_{p}^{r})}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\beta_{H}}}}}{\mathop{}\kern 19.7572pt\mathopen{\vphantom{(\alpha_{p}^{r})}}^{\mathmakebox[0pt][l]{\mathmakebox[r]{\beta_{H}}}}}(\alpha_{p}^{r}))

Since

by induction, if we let

we have that the class

étale extends to

after passing to some Artin–Schreier tower

∎

The result follows from Theorem

∎

4.
Étale extending unipotent torsors over curves

We now prove results similar to what we proved in the previous section where the ambient space is now taken to be a curve. Namely, given a torsor under a connected unipotent group on an open set
X^{\circ}
of a curve
X/F
, does it étale extend to all of
X
?

The main idea in this section is to reduce this problem to the case of a DVR by finding a suitable ramified cover of the curve where we can extend the torsor at the local rings of the points where the torsor is not defined. To find this ramified cover, we will need some kind of approximation lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
F
be an algebraically closed field and
X/F
be a normal integral curve with normal compactification of genus
g
. Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\in X
be distinct closed points. Then for any choice of integers
n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\geqslant 1
such that
n_{1}+\cdots n_{r}\geqslant 2g
, there exists a rational function
f\in F(X)
with polar divisor

\text{div}_{\infty}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}[x_{i}].

Proof.

After passing to the normal compactification we can suppose that
X/F
is proper. Consider the divisor

D=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}[x_{i}]\;.

Since
\text{deg}(D)=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}\geqslant 2g
, by Riemann–Roch we have that

l(D)=\text{deg}(D)+1-g>0

and

l(D-[x_{i}])=\text{deg}(D-[x_{i}])+1-g=l(D)-1<l(D)

Now, we want to find a nonzero function
f\in L(D)\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}L(D-[x_{i}])
, i.e., prove that
L(D)\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}L(D-[x_{i}])\neq 0
. But this is clear since a vector space over an infinite field cannot be a finite union of proper subspaces.
Thus, there exists such an
f\in F(X)
and we are done.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

In the preceding lemma we need the poles to be large enough so that
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=0
and we can apply Riemann–Roch to assure the existence of our desired function. Observe that if
X
is affine, for arbitrary integers
n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}
, we could look at the divisor

D=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}[x_{i}]+N[x_{\infty}],

where
x_{\infty}
is a point at infinity in a compactification of
X
. By setting
N
to be very large, we can use the same technique as before to find a rational function in
F(X)
with order
n_{i}
at the points
x_{i}
without any restraints on the
n_{i}s
. This will not be important to us.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
F
be an algebraically closed field,
U/F
be a connected unipotent group scheme of finite type and
X/F
be a normal integral curve. Let
0\to U\xrightarrow{u}G\xrightarrow{v}H\to 0
be an exact sequence of affine flat
X
-group schemes of finite type. Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
be distinct closed points of
X
and denote
X^{\circ}=X\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
. Let
P\to X^{\circ}
be a
G
-torsor and suppose that the induced
H
-torsor
P_{H}\to X^{\circ}
extends to an
H
-torsor over
X
. Then, there exists a normal connected integral curve
Y
and a surjective finite morphism
\varphi\colon Y\to X
étale over
X^{\circ}
and totally and wildly ramified over each
x_{i}
such that the pulled-back torsor
\varphi^{\ast}P\to\varphi^{-1}(X^{\circ})
extends to
Y
.

Proof.

Let
K
be the function field of
X
and
v_{i}
be the valuation in
K
induced by
x_{i}
. Let
P_{K}\to\Spec K
be the induced
G
-torsor over
\Spec K
and let
N\geqslant 0
be the integer (which depends on
P_{K}\to\Spec K
) in Proposition
3.15
. By Lemma
4.1
, we can find
f_{1}\in K
with poles only at the
x_{i}
’s with orders as large as we want and not divisible by
p
. Let
K_{1}\vcentcolon=K_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\mathchar 381$},f_{1}}
and
X_{1}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{1}}X
be the normalization of
X
in the function field
K_{1}
. Let
X_{1}^{\circ}\vcentcolon=\varphi_{1}^{-1}(X^{\circ})
. It is an open subscheme of
X_{1}
with complement
\varphi_{1}^{-1}(\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\})
. By Propositions
1.18
,
1.19
and
1.20
,
\varphi_{1}
is étale over
X^{\circ}
and totally ramified over each
x_{i}
. In particular,
\varphi_{1}^{-1}(x_{i})
consists of a single point of
X_{1}
. We can then iterate this construction to recursively construct a tower
\varphi\colon Y=X_{N}\to X_{N-1}\to\cdots\to X_{0}=X
of finite surjective morphisms with each
X_{i}
normal, connected and integral such that it induces a tower
L=K_{N}/\cdots/K_{0}=K
of Artin–Schreier extensions totally ramified over each
x_{i}
and unramified over
X^{\circ}
. Thus,
\varphi
is étale over
X^{\circ}
. Let
Y^{\circ}=\varphi^{-1}(X^{\circ})
. It remains to prove that
\varphi^{\ast}P\to Y^{\circ}
extends to
Y
. For that, observe that since
\varphi
is totally ramified over each
x_{i}
, we have that
Y=Y^{\circ}\cup\{y_{1},\dots,y_{r}\}
, where
y_{i}\in Y
is the unique point in
\varphi^{-1}(x_{i})
. Moreover, if
v_{L,i}
is the valuation on
L
induced by
y_{i}
, we have that
\mathcal{O}_{Y,y_{i}}
is the valuation ring of
v_{L,i}
. By Proposition
3.15
, the torsor
P_{L}\to\Spec L
extends to
\Spec\mathcal{O}_{Y,y_{i}}
for each
i
. By spreading out, there exists open neighborhoods
W_{i}\subseteq Y
of
y_{i}
not containing any other
y_{i}
and a
G
-torsor
P_{i}\to W_{i}
which restricts to
P_{L}\to\Spec L
at the generic point and such that
P_{i}\to W_{i}
and
P_{Y^{\circ}}\to Y^{\circ}
are isomorphic at
Y^{\circ}\cap W_{i}
. Fix such isomorphisms

\psi_{i}\colon\left.P_{i}\right|_{Y^{\circ}\cap W_{i}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\left.P_{Y^{\circ}}\right|_{Y^{\circ}\cap W_{i}}.

Since
W_{i}\cap W_{j}\subseteq Y^{\circ}
, we have isomorphisms between
P_{i}
and
P_{j}
on
W_{i}\cap W_{j}
given by the composition

\psi_{ij}\colon\left.P_{i}\right|_{W_{i}\cap W_{j}}\xrightarrow{\psi_{i}}\left.P_{Y^{\circ}}\right|_{W_{i}\cap W_{j}}\xrightarrow{\psi_{j}^{-1}}\left.P_{j}\right|_{W_{i}\cap W_{j}}.

Clearly,
\{\psi_{ij}\}_{i,j}
satisfy the cocycle conditions on triple intersections, and therefore by Zariski gluing, we can find a torsor extending
\varphi^{\ast}P\to Y^{\circ}
.
∎

By setting
H=1
in the previous Proposition, we obtain the following.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
X/F
be a normal integral curve over an algebraically closed field

and
U/F
a connected unipotent algebraic group. Let

be distinct closed points of

and denote

. Let

be a
U
-torsor. Then there exists a normal connected integral curve

and a surjective finite morphism
Y\xrightarrow{\varphi}X
étale over
X^{\circ}
and totally (wildly) ramified over each

such that the pullback torsor
P_{\varphi^{-1}(X^{\circ})}\to\varphi^{-1}(X^{\circ})
extends to

. Moreover,
f
can be taken to be the normalization of

in an Artin–Schreier tower
L/F(X)
.

Before finishing this section, let me observe that the exact same argument as in the proof of Theorem
4.4
proves the following.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
X/F
be a normal integral curve over an algebraically closed field
F
and
G/F
an algebraic group. Let

be distinct closed points of

5.
Consequences for the Nori fundamental group

We start by recalling some definitions and properties related to the Nori fundamental group (see
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
). For that, fix
F
an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic and
X
a reduced and connected
F
-scheme. Fix also a rational point
x\in X(F)
.

Definition 5.1
.

We define a category
N(X,x)
as the category having as objects triples
(G,P,p)
where

•

G
is a finite
F
-group scheme,

•

P\to X
is a
G
-torsor,

•

p\in P(F)
over
x
.

A morphism
(G,P,p)\to(G^{\prime},P^{\prime},p^{\prime})
is a pair
(\varphi,\Phi)
consisting of a homomorphism

\varphi:G\to G^{\prime}
and a
\varphi
-equivariant morphism of
X
-schemes
\Phi:P\to P^{\prime}
such that
\Phi(p)=p^{\prime}
.

One can prove that
N(X,x)
is cofiltered. (This is
[
12
, Proposition 2, p.85]
). This leads to the following definition.

Definition 5.2
.

(
[
12
, Chapter II]
)
The Nori fundamental group scheme of
(X,x)
is the profinite
F
-group scheme

\pi^{N}(X,x):=\lim_{(G,P,p)\in N(X,x)}G,

with transition maps induced by the morphisms in
N(X,x)
.

This group is analogous to the étale fundamental group in the sense that it classifies finite torsors.

Proposition 5.3
.

For every (pro-)finite
F
-group scheme
G
, we have a bijection (functorial in
G
)

\textup{Hom}_{F}(\pi^{N}(X,x),\,G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\{\text{Isomorphism classes of pointed $G$-torsors $(P,p)$ on $X$}\}.

Proof.

Let me give a brief overview of the proof. It is enough to prove the bijection for
G
finite. Let
\pi^{N}(X,x)\xrightarrow{\varphi}G
be a morphism. Since
G
is finite,
\varphi
factors through some finite group
H
, for some
(H,Q,q)\in N(X,x)
, i.e.
\varphi=\pi^{N}(X,x)\to H\xrightarrow{\psi}G
. We can then take the induced pointed
G
-torsor
(Q\times^{H}G,[q,1])
. This gives the map

\textup{Hom}_{F}(\pi^{N}(X,x),\,G)\to\{\text{isomorphism classes of pointed $G$-torsors $(P,p)$ on $X$}\}.

On the other hand, given a pointed
G
-torsor
(P,p)
, by the definition of
\pi^{N}(X,x)
there exists a morphism
\pi^{N}(X,x)\to G
. One can then show that these define inverse maps which are functorial in
G
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

If
X
is proper and integral, then
\pi^{N}(X,x)
agrees with Nori’s Tannakian fundamental group scheme attached to the Tannakian category of
essentially finite vector bundles on
X
with fibre functor
x^{*}
. For details, see
[
12
]
.

By taking appropriate subcategories of
N(X,x)
, we can define some variants of
\pi^{N}(X,x)
(in fact quotients of
\pi^{N}(X,x)
) classifying finite torsors satisfying specific properties. For example, in the setting of Definition
1.10
, one can define a variant of
\pi^{N}(X^{\circ},x)
which detects only finite torsors which étale extend to
X
.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
X/F
be a normal integral variety,
X^{\circ}\subseteq X
a dense open subscheme and
x\in X^{\circ}(F)
. Let
N_{ext}(X^{\circ},x)
be the full subcategory of
N(X^{\circ},x)
whose objects are the triples
(G,P,p)
where
P\to X^{\circ}
étale extends to
X
.

Proposition 5.6
.

N_{ext}(X^{\circ},x)
is a cofiltered category.

Proof.

It is enough to show that
N_{ext}(X^{\circ},x)
has fiber products. In
[
12
, Proposition 2, p.85]
Nori shows that, since
X^{\circ}
is reduced and connected,
N(X^{\circ},x)
has fiber products given by

(G_{1},P_{1},p_{1})\times_{(G,P,p)}(G_{2},P_{2},p_{2})=(G_{1}\times_{G}G_{2},P_{1}\times_{P}P_{2},p_{1}\times p_{2}).

It is then enough to show that
N_{ext}(X^{\circ},x)
is closed under fiber products in
N(X^{\circ},x)
. Consider then morphisms
(G_{i},P_{i},p_{i})\to(G,P,p)
in
N_{ext}(X^{\circ},x)

(i=1,2)
. By Proposition
1.15
, we can find a finite morphism
f\colon Y\to X
, with
Y
a connected normal variety over
F
,where both
P
and the
P_{i}
’s extend. Since
Y
is reduced and connected, the fiber product of the extensions exists over
Y
and clearly extends the pullback of
(G_{1}\times_{G}G_{2},P_{1}\times_{P}P_{2},p_{1}\times p_{2})
to
f^{-1}(X^{\circ})
. This shows that
N_{ext}(X^{\circ},x)
is closed under fiber products.
∎

Definition 5.7
.

We define the profinite group scheme
\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x)
as the inverse limit

\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x)\vcentcolon=\lim_{(G,P,p)\in N_{ext}(X,x)}G.

Proposition 5.8
.

For every (pro-)finite
F
-group scheme
G
, we have a functorial bijection

\textup{Hom}_{F}(\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x),\,G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{isomorphism classes of pointed \'{e}tale extendable}\\
\text{$G$-torsors on $X^{\circ}$}\end{array}\right\}.

Proof.

The proof is completely analogous to that of Proposition
5.3
.
∎

Since any finite torsor over
X^{\circ}
coming from
X
trivially étale extends to
X
, from the universal property, one sees that the morphism
\pi^{N}(X^{\circ},x)\to\pi^{N}(X,x)
(which corresponds to pulling back from
X
to
X^{\circ}
) factors through
\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x)
. Moreover, one can easily see that all of the maps

\pi^{N}(X^{\circ},x)\to\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x)\to\pi^{N}(X,x)

are surjective (faithfully flat). One can then think of
\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x)
as being some kind of intermediary group living between the fundamental groups of
X^{\circ}
and
X
.

Remark 5.9
.

Under the preceding setup, in
[
2
]
they define a group scheme
\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x)
by using “formal orbifolds”. They prove that this group satisfies the same universal property as our
\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x)
. Therefore, these two groups are isomorphic.

One can also capture the unipotent part of these groups in a similar vein.

Definition 5.10
.

Let
N_{\mathrm{uni}}(X,x)
be the full subcategory of
N(X,x)
consisting of the triples
(G,P,p)
with
G
finite unipotent. For a dense open
X^{\circ}\subseteq X
and
x\in X^{\circ}
, we also define a full subcategory
N_{\mathrm{uni,ext}}(X,x)
as
N_{uni}(X^{\circ},x)\cap N_{ext}(X^{\circ},x)
. We then define

\pi^{U}(X,x):=\lim_{(G,P,p)\in N_{uni}(X,x)}G

and

\pi^{u}(X^{\circ},x):=\lim_{(G,P,p)\in N_{uni,ext}(X^{\circ},x)}G.

Once again, as expected, we have the following universal properties.

Proposition 5.11
.

For every (pro-)finite unipotent
k
-group scheme
U
, we have functorial bijections

\textup{Hom}_{F}(\pi^{U}(X,x),U)\xrightarrow{\sim}\{\text{isomorphism classes of pointed }U\text{-torsors on }X\}.

and

\textup{Hom}_{F}(\pi^{u}(X^{\circ},x),\,U)\xrightarrow{\sim}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{isomorphism classes of pointed \'{e}tale extendable}\\
\text{$U$-torsors on $X^{\circ}$}\end{array}\right\}.

In particular, the canonical maps

\pi^{N}(X,x)\to\pi^{U}(X,x)

and

\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x)\to\pi^{u}(X^{\circ},x)

exhibits

\pi^{U}(X,x)
(resp.
\pi^{u}(X^{\circ},x)
) as the maximal pro-unipotent quotient of
\pi^{N}(X,x)
(resp.
\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x)
), i.e. the initial morphism from
\pi^{N}(X,x)
(resp.
\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x)
) to a pro-unipotent
k
-group scheme.

Of course, we have natural maps between these groups. For example, we have a surjection
\pi^{N}(X^{\circ},x)\twoheadrightarrow\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x)
induced by the inclusion

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{isomorphism classes of pointed \'{e}tale extendable}\\
\text{$U$-torsors}\end{array}\right\}\hookrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{isomorphism classes}\\
\text{of pointed $U$-torsors}\end{array}\right\}.

The kernel of this map is not known to be trivial or not, but using the results of the last section, we can at least guarantee that “there is no unipotent part” in the kernel for curves.

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
X/F
be a normal separated integral curve over an algebraically closed field
F
of characteristic
p>0
. Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
be distinct closed points of
X
and denote
X^{\circ}=X\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
. Let
x\in X^{\circ}(F)
. Then the morphism
\pi^{U}(X^{\circ},x)\to\pi^{u}(X^{\circ},x)
induced by
\pi^{N}(X^{\circ},x)\twoheadrightarrow\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x)
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
U/F
be a finite unipotent group. Clearly, every
F
-morphism
\pi^{N}(X^{\circ},x)\to U
(resp.
\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x)\to U
) factors trough
\pi^{U}(X^{\circ},x)
(resp.
\pi^{u}(X^{\circ},x)
), and therefore, we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Hom}_{F}(\pi^{u}(X^{\circ},x),U)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Hom}_{F}(\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x),U)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Hom}_{F}(\pi^{U}(X^{\circ},x),U)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Hom}_{F}(\pi^{N}(X^{\circ},x),U)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

with isomorphisms on the horizontal arrows. Thus, it is enough to prove that

\textup{Hom}_{F}(\pi^{n}(X^{\circ},x),U)\to\textup{Hom}_{k}(\pi^{N}(X^{\circ},x),U)

is an isomorphism for all
U/F
finite unipotent. By the universal properties of
\pi^{N}
and
\pi^{n}
, this corresponds to proving that the map

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{isomorphism classes of pointed \'{e}tale extendable}\\
\text{$U$-torsors}\end{array}\right\}\hookrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{isomorphism classes}\\
\text{of pointed $U$-torsors}\end{array}\right\}

is an isomorphism. This follows from Proposition
4.4
.
∎
