Spatial organization of HPV-associated immune programs across tissue compartments in oropharyngeal cancer

Naohiro Wakisaka, Makiko Moriyama-Kita, Satoru Kondo, Yosuke Nakanishi, Kazuhira Endo, Hisashi Sugimoto, Tomokazu Yoshizaki

ABSTRACT
Background: Human papillomavirus (HPV)-positive oropharyngeal cancer (OPC) is characterized by a distinct immune microenvironment; however, the biological significance of immune-related transcriptional programs may vary according to their spatial localization. We investigated the spatial organization of HPV-associated transcriptional programs across anatomically distinct tissue compartments in OPC. Methods: An oropharynx-restricted The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA)-oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (OPSCC) cohort comprising 77 tumors (51 HPV-positive and 26 HPV-negative) was analyzed using single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA). Spatial transcriptomic profiling using GeoMx Digital Spatial Profiling was evaluated in lymphoid follicular regions (LFRs) and tumor tissue regions (TTRs) from six OPC patients (four HPV-positive and two HPV-negative), with the patient treated as the primary experimental unit. Tumor-free surrounding palatine tonsillar tissue (PTT) was analyzed in an independent microarray cohort comprising 23 HPV-positive and four HPV-negative cases. Eight prespecified immune- and metabolism-related transcriptional programs were evaluated, with additional exploratory cell-type deconvolution and overall survival analyses. Results: In TCGA-OPSCC, all five immune-related programs were higher in HPV-positive tumors, and Stem-like, Tpex, B-cell activity, and T-cell activity remained significantly associated with HPV-positive status after adjustment for clinicopathological covariates and FDR correction. In paired GeoMx analyses, all five immune-related programs were higher in LFRs than in TTRs in all six patients, whereas Estrogen Response Early and Estrogen Response Late were higher in TTRs in all six patients (BH-FDR = 0.0357 for each program). Bile Acid Metabolism showed no consistent compartment-specific difference. HPV-associated differences within individual GeoMx compartments and HPV-by-compartment interactions were not statistically significant after FDR correction. In PTT, immune-related and metabolic programs showed directional HPV-associated differences, but none remained significant after FDR correction. Higher scores for several immune-related programs were associated with improved overall survival in univariable TCGA-OPSCC analyses, but these associations were attenuated after accounting for HPV status. Conclusions: HPV-associated immune transcriptional programs identified in bulk OPC show consistent spatial organization across lymphoid follicular and tumor tissue compartments. The most robust spatial feature was within-patient compartmentalization between LFRs and TTRs, whereas evidence for HPV-associated differences within individual spatial compartments was limited. These findings emphasize the importance of anatomical context when interpreting transcriptional programs in OPC.

Introduction Human papillomavirus (HPV)-positive oropharyngeal cancer (OPC) is a distinct subtype of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) characterized by favorable clinical outcomes and enhanced responsiveness to radiotherapy and systemic treatment compared with HPV-negative disease [ 1 – 3 ]. These clinical differences are closely associated with a unique tumor immune microenvironment, including increased infiltration of T cells, B cells, and tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS), which contribute to effective antitumor immunity and improved prognosis [ 4 – 7 ]. As immune-related transcriptional biomarkers are increasingly incorporated into precision oncology, understanding the biological significance of these immune programs has become essential for patient stratification and the development of immunotherapeutic strategies. Recent transcriptomic studies have identified characteristic HPV-associated immune programs, including Stem-like, progenitor-exhausted (Tpex), cytotoxic T-cell, and B-cell signatures, primarily through bulk RNA sequencing and single-cell transcriptomic analyses [ 8 – 13 ]. However, these approaches provide limited information regarding the anatomical localization of these immune programs within tumor-associated tissues. Because bulk transcriptomic profiles represent averaged signals derived from heterogeneous tissue compartments, identical transcriptional signatures may reflect biologically distinct processes depending on where they are expressed. Consequently, interpretation of immune-related biomarkers based solely on bulk transcriptomic data may overlook spatial heterogeneity. Spatially resolved transcriptomic approaches therefore offer an important opportunity to determine not only which immune programs are present, but also where they are organized within the tumor ecosystem, thereby providing a more biologically informative framework for biomarker interpretation [ 6 , 14 – 16 ]. Despite substantial advances in the characterization of HPV-associated immune programs, their spatial organization across anatomically distinct tumor-associated tissues remains poorly understood. Previous transcriptomic and single-cell studies have primarily focused on bulk tumors or dissociated cellular populations, providing limited information regarding the anatomical compartments in which these transcriptional programs are represented [ 8 – 13 ]. This limitation is particularly relevant in OPC because tumor-associated tissues comprise multiple biological compartments with distinct cellular compositions and functional properties, including lymphoid follicular regions (LFRs), tumor tissue regions (TTRs), and tumor-free surrounding palatine tonsillar tissue (PTT). We therefore integrated bulk transcriptomic data from an oropharynx-restricted The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA)- oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (OPSCC) cohort, GeoMx Digital Spatial Profiling (DSP) of LFRs and TTRs, and microarray analyses of tumor-free surrounding PTT. Through this multi-compartment approach, we sought to determine how HPV-associated transcriptional programs identified in bulk OPC are spatially organized across distinct anatomical compartments and whether their spatial distribution differs according to HPV status. Materials and methods Study design and analytical workflow This study was designed to characterize the spatial organization of HPV-associated transcriptional programs across anatomically distinct tissue compartments in OPC. Findings across TCGA-OPSCC, GeoMx DSP, and PTT were integrated to characterize the spatial organization of HPV-associated transcriptional programs. First, RNA-sequencing data from the oropharynx-restricted TCGA-OPSCC cohort were analyzed to define transcriptional programs associated with HPV status at the bulk-tumor level. Second, GeoMx DSP was used to examine the spatial organization of these programs across LFRs and TTRs. Because multiple regions of interest (ROIs) were obtained from each patient, the patient was treated as the primary experimental unit for statistical inference. Spatial organization within the GeoMx cohort was further evaluated by paired within-patient comparisons between LFRs and TTRs and by HPV-by-compartment interaction analyses. Third, microarray data from tumor-free surrounding PTT were analyzed as an exploratory assessment of the transcriptional context of surrounding tonsillar tissue. Transcriptional programs were quantified using single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA). Across analyses, statistical inference was based on the appropriate independent experimental unit for each dataset, and multiple testing was controlled using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (BH-FDR) procedure as described below. Additional exploratory analyses included reference-based cell-type deconvolution of GeoMx LFRs and overall survival analyses in the TCGA-OPSCC cohort. The study was conducted under institutional ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of Kanazawa University (Approval No. 2016–033) as part of a broader research protocol entitled “Human genome and gene analysis of head and neck malignant tumors,” with an approved study period from March 29, 2016, to March 31, 2031. Written informed consent was obtained from all enrolled patients. TCGA-OPSCC dataset and HPV annotation RNA-sequencing data and corresponding clinical information were obtained from TCGA-Head and Neck Squamous Cell Carcinoma (HNSC) dataset [ 17 ]. To provide an anatomically matched bulk-tumor reference for the spatial analyses, the present study was restricted to tumors arising from oropharyngeal subsites. The resulting TCGA-OPSCC cohort comprised 77 tumors, including 40 tonsillar tumors, 25 base-of-tongue tumors, 11 tumors classified as oropharynx not otherwise specified, and one posterior pharyngeal wall tumor. Gene-expression data were converted to gene-symbol-based expression profiles for downstream analyses. HPV status was assigned using the RNA-seq-based HPV annotation reported by Nulton et al. [ 18 ]. Tumors annotated as HPV16-, HPV33-, HPV35-, or HPV56-positive were grouped as HPV-positive for the present analysis. The final TCGA-OPSCC cohort comprised 51 HPV-positive and 26 HPV-negative tumors. Available clinicopathological characteristics were summarized according to HPV status. Age was compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, whereas sex/gender, smoking status, clinical stage, and anatomical subsite were compared using Fisher’s exact test. These comparisons were considered descriptive cohort characterization and were not included in the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (BH-FDR) testing families used for transcriptional-program analyses. Primary comparisons of transcriptional program scores between HPV-positive and HPV-negative tumors were performed in all 77 cases. To assess whether HPV-associated differences were retained after accounting for relevant clinicopathological factors, multivariable analyses were additionally performed using complete cases with available covariate data (n = 74; 50 HPV-positive and 24 HPV-negative). These models included age, sex/gender, smoking status, clinical stage, and anatomical subsite as covariates. Gene signature collection and ssGSEA analysis Eight prespecified transcriptional programs were evaluated across the datasets. Five immune-related programs were included: a Stem-like T-cell-associated program, a precursor-exhausted T-cell-associated (Tpex) program, T-cell activity, B-cell activity, and a terminally exhausted T-cell-associated (Tex-like) program. The Stem-like, Tpex, T-cell activity, and B-cell activity signatures were constructed from established marker-gene sets, whereas the Tex-like signature was adopted from Shi et al. [ 19 ]. Three additional programs—Hallmark Estrogen Response Early, Hallmark Estrogen Response Late, and Hallmark Bile Acid Metabolism—were obtained from the Hallmark collection of the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) [ 20 ]. The complete gene sets and the genes represented in the analyzed expression data are provided in S1 Table . Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was performed using the GSVA package in R [ 21 ]. Gene sets were matched to the available expression features within each dataset before ssGSEA calculation. Enrichment scores were calculated separately for each TCGA-OPSCC tumor, each GeoMx region of interest (ROI), and each PTT sample. For GeoMx DSP, ROI-level ssGSEA scores were subsequently averaged within each patient and anatomical compartment to generate patient-level scores for primary statistical analyses. Because ssGSEA scores are dependent on the underlying expression dataset and platform, absolute enrichment scores were not directly compared across TCGA RNA sequencing, GeoMx DSP, and PTT microarray datasets; instead, each dataset was analyzed separately, and the resulting patterns were integrated qualitatively. Complementary marker-gene analysis As a complementary analysis of the Stem-like T-cell-associated transcriptional program identified by ssGSEA, eight prespecified marker genes (TCF7, IL7R, CCR7, LEF1, BACH2, SELL, SLAMF6, and CD28) were evaluated in the TCGA-OPSCC cohort. Expression levels were compared between HPV-positive and HPV-negative tumors, and P values were adjusted across the eight genes using the BH-FDR procedure. This marker-gene analysis was considered complementary to, rather than an independent validation of, the predefined multigene Stem-like T-cell-associated transcriptional program. GeoMx DSP dataset and compartment definition Spatial transcriptomic analysis was performed using the NanoString GeoMx DSP on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded OPC specimens from six patients, comprising four HPV-positive and two HPV-negative cases. For each patient, six LFR ROIs and six TTR ROIs were analyzed, yielding 12 ROIs per patient and 72 ROIs in total. ROI selection was performed independently by two investigators (M.M.-K. and N.W.) who were blinded to HPV and nodal status. PanCK, CD45, and SMA morphology markers were used to assist identification of epithelial/tumor, immune, and stromal compartments. LFRs were defined histologically within palatine tonsillar tissue as lymphoid follicular regions containing a B-cell follicular component together with the adjacent T-cell zone. Thus, each LFR ROI represented a mixed lymphoid region rather than a purified B-cell or T-cell population. LFRs were selected within the lymphoid architecture of the palatine tonsil and were not classified as tumor-induced tertiary lymphoid structures (TLSs). TTRs were defined as regions containing carcinoma tissue and were selected separately from LFRs. GeoMx DSP data processing and statistical unit GeoMx gene-expression data were processed using the GeoMx DSP Data Analysis Suite with Q3 normalization, as described previously [ 16 ]. All 72 selected ROIs were retained for the present analyses, with no patient samples or ROIs excluded after data processing. ssGSEA scores for the eight prespecified transcriptional programs were calculated separately for each ROI. Because six ROIs from each compartment were obtained from each patient, ROI-level observations within a patient were not treated as independent biological replicates. The patient was therefore defined as the primary experimental unit for statistical inference. For each patient, the six ROI-level ssGSEA scores within each compartment were averaged to generate one patient-level LFR score and one patient-level TTR score for each transcriptional program. HPV-associated comparisons within each compartment were based primarily on these patient-level scores. ROI-level analyses were considered exploratory and were used to characterize within-patient and within-compartment variability rather than for primary statistical inference. Paired compartment and HPV-by-compartment interaction analyses To directly assess transcriptional differences between LFRs and TTRs while accounting for the paired study design, patient-level compartment scores were compared within patients. For each transcriptional program, the within-patient difference was defined as the mean LFR score minus the mean TTR score. These paired differences were evaluated across the six patients using a two-sided exact Wilcoxon signed-rank test. P values were adjusted across the eight prespecified transcriptional programs using the BH-FDR procedure. To determine whether the magnitude of the LFR–TTR difference varied according to HPV status, HPV-by-compartment interactions were evaluated using an exact patient-label permutation test. For each transcriptional program, the patient-level compartment difference was defined as Δ = mean LFR score − mean TTR score, and the interaction statistic was calculated as the difference in mean Δ between HPV-positive and HPV-negative patients. Given the four HPV-positive and two HPV-negative patients, all 15 possible assignments of four of the six patients to the HPV-positive group were enumerated. Two-sided exact permutation P values were calculated as the proportion of the 15 permuted statistics whose absolute values were greater than or equal to the absolute value of the observed statistic. P values were adjusted across the eight prespecified transcriptional programs using the BH-FDR procedure. PTT microarray dataset Microarray gene-expression data from tumor-free surrounding PTT were obtained from the publicly available GSE228432 dataset. The dataset comprised 27 OPC patients, including 23 HPV-positive and four HPV-negative cases. PTT samples represented peritumoral PTT from which the tumor-containing region had been excluded by macrodissection of FFPE tissue sections before RNA extraction, as described previously [ 22 ]. The designation “tumor-free” therefore refers operationally to exclusion of the identified tumor-containing region and does not imply systematic microscopic exclusion of occult epithelial abnormalities, inflammatory infiltrates, or lymphoid structures. The PTT dataset was analyzed separately as an exploratory assessment of the transcriptional context of tumor-free surrounding tonsillar tissue. ssGSEA scores for the same eight prespecified transcriptional programs used in the TCGA-OPSCC and GeoMx analyses were calculated for each PTT sample. Because of the marked imbalance in sample size between the HPV-positive and HPV-negative groups, particularly the small number of HPV-negative cases, findings from this dataset were considered exploratory and were interpreted on the basis of effect direction, effect size, and multiple-testing-adjusted P values. SpatialDecon analysis To explore whether HPV-associated differences in the Stem-like T-cell-associated transcriptional program within LFRs could be related to differences in broad immune-cell composition, reference-based cell-type deconvolution was performed on the 36 LFR ROIs using SpatialDecon (version 1.18.0) [ 23 ] with the ImmuneTumor_safeTME reference matrix. Cell-type abundance estimates were obtained for each ROI and summarized into broad immune-cell categories, including B cells, CD4 T cells, CD8 T cells, and total T cells. For patient-level analyses, estimated abundances were averaged across the six LFR ROIs from each patient. Patient-level cell-type estimates were compared between HPV-positive and HPV-negative patients. Associations between the patient-level Stem-like program score and the estimated abundance of each broad immune-cell category were additionally assessed using Spearman rank correlation. As exploratory sensitivity analyses, linear regression models were used to evaluate the association between HPV status and the Stem-like program score after adjustment separately for estimated CD8 T-cell abundance and total T-cell abundance. Given the small number of patients (n = 6), the deconvolution, correlation, and composition-adjusted analyses were considered exploratory and were not interpreted as establishing a cell-type-specific origin or composition-independent effect of the Stem-like transcriptional program. Overall survival analysis in TCGA-OPSCC Overall survival was explored in the TCGA-OPSCC cohort using Cox proportional hazards regression. Overall survival time was defined from the available TCGA clinical follow-up data, with death from any cause treated as the event and patients alive at last follow-up censored. For each transcriptional program, ssGSEA scores were standardized to a mean of 0 and standard deviation of 1, and hazard ratios (HRs) were estimated per 1-standard-deviation increase in score. Univariable Cox models were fitted separately for each of the eight prespecified transcriptional programs. To assess whether associations between transcriptional program scores and overall survival were retained after accounting for HPV status, additional Cox models including HPV status as a covariate were fitted for each program. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed for the Cox models. Because evidence of non-proportional hazards was observed for HPV status, HPV-stratified Cox analyses were additionally performed as sensitivity analyses. Given the limited number of deaths and the exploratory nature of these analyses, no additional multivariable clinicopathological adjustment was performed. P values were adjusted across the eight transcriptional programs within each survival-analysis family using the BH-FDR procedure. Statistical analysis All statistical tests were two-sided. Comparisons between independent HPV-positive and HPV-negative groups were performed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, with exact P values used where appropriate. Effect sizes for group comparisons were quantified using Cliff’s delta (δ), with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals. Paired comparisons between patient-level LFR and TTR scores were performed using two-sided exact Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, as described above. For GeoMx analyses, the patient rather than the individual ROI was treated as the primary experimental unit. Patient-level compartment scores were calculated by averaging the six ROI-level scores within each compartment for each patient. ROI-level analyses were considered exploratory and were not treated as independent biological replicates for primary statistical inference. HPV-by-compartment interactions were evaluated using the exact patient-label permutation procedure described above. For the TCGA-OPSCC cohort, multivariable linear regression was used to evaluate associations between HPV status and each transcriptional program after adjustment for age, sex/gender, smoking status, clinical stage, and anatomical subsite. Overall survival analyses were performed using Cox proportional hazards regression as described above. Multiple testing was controlled using the BH-FDR procedure. FDR correction was performed separately within each prespecified family of eight transcriptional-program tests for a given dataset and analysis, rather than across different datasets or distinct analytical questions. BH-adjusted P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Results with nominal P < 0.05 but BH-adjusted P ≥ 0.05 were described as nominal associations and were not considered statistically significant. Raw and BH-adjusted P values are reported where applicable. Statistical analyses were performed in R. Given the small number of patients in the GeoMx cohort, HPV-associated comparisons and interaction analyses were interpreted cautiously, with consideration of effect sizes, uncertainty, consistency of direction across patients, and FDR-adjusted statistical evidence. Results HPV-positive tumors exhibit coordinated immune-program enrichment in the TCGA-OPSCC cohort To characterize HPV-associated transcriptional programs in an anatomically matched bulk-tumor cohort, we analyzed 77 tumors from the oropharynx-restricted TCGA-OPSCC cohort, comprising 51 HPV-positive and 26 HPV-negative tumors. Clinicopathological characteristics of the TCGA-OPSCC cohort according to HPV status are summarized in S2 Table . Age, sex/gender, and clinical stage did not differ significantly between HPV-positive and HPV-negative tumors (P = 0.304, P = 0.432, and P = 1.000, respectively). Smoking status showed a nonsignificant difference between groups (P = 0.064). In contrast, anatomical subsite differed significantly according to HPV status (P = 0.005), with tonsillar tumors more frequently represented among HPV-positive cases. In unadjusted comparisons, all five prespecified immune-related programs showed higher ssGSEA scores in HPV-positive tumors ( Fig 2A and S3A Table ). The corresponding effect sizes were largest for B-cell activity (Cliff’s δ = 0.618), followed by T-cell activity (δ = 0.561), the Stem-like T-cell-associated program (δ = 0.534), Tpex (δ = 0.499), and the Tex-like program (δ = 0.407). All five immune-related programs remained statistically significant after BH-FDR correction. In contrast, Estrogen Response Early, Estrogen Response Late, and Bile Acid Metabolism showed no significant HPV-associated differences. 10.1371/journal.pone.0356717.g002 Fig 2 HPV-associated transcriptional programs in the TCGA-OPSCC cohort. Estrogen-response and bile acid metabolism programs were not significantly associated with HPV status after adjustment. We next evaluated whether these associations were retained after adjustment for relevant clinicopathological factors. Multivariable linear regression analyses were performed in 74 complete cases (50 HPV-positive and 24 HPV-negative), with adjustment for age, sex/gender, smoking status, clinical stage, and anatomical subsite ( Fig 2B and S3B Table ). HPV-positive status remained significantly associated with higher scores for the Stem-like program (β = 0.0958, BH-FDR = 0.0130), Tpex (β = 0.0829, BH-FDR = 0.0404), B-cell activity (β = 0.1108, BH-FDR = 0.0130), and T-cell activity (β = 0.1295, BH-FDR = 0.0130). The Tex-like program showed a nominal positive association with HPV-positive status (β = 0.0574, nominal P = 0.0353) but did not remain significant after FDR correction (BH-FDR = 0.0565). The three Hallmark programs were not significantly associated with HPV status after multivariable adjustment. Together, these findings identify a coordinated HPV-associated immune transcriptional phenotype in oropharyngeal tumors, with four immune-related programs remaining associated with HPV-positive status after adjustment for clinicopathological covariates. Complementary marker-gene analysis supports the HPV-associated Stem-like transcriptional program As a complementary analysis of the Stem-like T-cell-associated transcriptional program, we examined eight prespecified marker genes (TCF7, IL7R, CCR7, LEF1, BACH2, SELL, SLAMF6, and CD28) in the TCGA-OPSCC cohort ( S3C Table ). Several of these genes showed higher expression in HPV-positive tumors. After multivariable adjustment for clinicopathological covariates and correction for multiple testing across the eight genes, CCR7, SELL, SLAMF6, and CD28 remained significantly associated with HPV-positive status. TCF7 showed a nominal association that did not remain statistically significant after FDR correction, whereas IL7R was not significantly associated with HPV status. These findings were directionally consistent with the ssGSEA-based Stem-like transcriptional program and provide complementary gene-level context for the multigene signature. Given the overlap between these marker genes and the biological framework underlying the predefined signature, this analysis was not considered an independent validation of the Stem-like program. Reference-based deconvolution does not identify a clear HPV-associated difference in broad LFR immune-cell composition To explore whether the HPV-associated direction of the Stem-like T-cell-associated transcriptional program within LFRs could be related to differences in broad immune-cell composition, we performed reference-based cell-type deconvolution of the 36 LFR ROIs and summarized the resulting estimates at the patient level ( S1A Fig and S5A Table ). Estimated abundances of B cells, CD4 T cells, CD8 T cells, and total T cells did not differ significantly between HPV-positive and HPV-negative patients after BH-FDR correction. We next examined associations between the patient-level Stem-like program score and deconvolution-derived immune-cell estimates. The Stem-like score was not positively associated with estimated CD8 T-cell abundance (Spearman ρ = −0.371, P = 0.468), whereas a positive association was observed with estimated total T-cell abundance (ρ = 0.829, P = 0.042) ( S1B Fig and S5B Table ). Associations with B-cell and CD4 T-cell estimates were not statistically significant. As exploratory sensitivity analyses, we evaluated the HPV-associated direction of the Stem-like program after adjustment separately for estimated CD8 T-cell and total T-cell abundance ( S5C Table ). The HPV-associated coefficient remained positive after adjustment for CD8 T-cell abundance (β = 0.0387, 95% CI, −0.0175 to 0.0950; P = 0.116) and total T-cell abundance (β = 0.0351, 95% CI, −0.0267 to 0.0968; P = 0.169), but neither association was statistically significant. Together, these exploratory analyses did not identify a clear HPV-associated difference in broad immune-cell composition within LFRs. Although broad cell-type composition alone may not fully account for the observed HPV-associated direction of the Stem-like transcriptional program, the small sample size precludes inference regarding composition-independent enrichment or the specific cellular origin of this program. Discussion The principal finding of the present study is that HPV-associated immune transcriptional programs identified in bulk OPC are spatially organized across anatomically distinct tissue compartments. In the oropharynx-restricted TCGA-OPSCC cohort, HPV-positive tumors showed coordinated enrichment of multiple immune-related programs, including Stem-like, Tpex, T-cell activity, and B-cell activity programs. Spatial analysis using GeoMx DSP further demonstrated that the most consistent feature was not an HPV-associated difference within an individual compartment, but rather a marked within-patient segregation between LFRs and TTRs: all five immune-related programs were higher in LFRs, whereas Estrogen Response Early and Estrogen Response Late were higher in TTRs. In contrast, HPV-associated differences within individual GeoMx compartments and HPV-by-compartment interactions were not statistically significant after FDR correction. These findings emphasize that transcriptional programs identified in bulk tissue should be interpreted in relation to their anatomical localization and support a compartment-aware framework for interpreting spatially resolved immune biomarkers in OPC [ 24 – 28 ]. Among the compartment-specific findings, the consistent enrichment of the Stem-like T-cell-associated transcriptional program in LFRs relative to TTRs was a prominent spatial feature. Previous studies have identified stem-like or progenitor-exhausted T-cell populations characterized by TCF7 and related markers that can sustain antitumor immune responses and generate differentiated effector populations during chronic antigen stimulation or immune checkpoint blockade [ 10 – 12 , 29 ]. Likewise, TLSs and other organized lymphoid aggregates can provide specialized microenvironments supporting antigen presentation and coordinated T- and B-cell interactions, and have been associated with favorable clinical outcomes and responses to immunotherapy in several cancer types [ 4 , 14 , 15 , 26 , 30 ]. In the present study, however, the Stem-like score represents a multigene transcriptional program rather than direct quantification of a defined stem-like CD8 T-cell population. Moreover, the LFRs analyzed here were lymphoid follicular regions within the lymphoid architecture of the palatine tonsil, containing a B-cell follicular component and adjacent T-cell zone, and were not classified as tumor-induced TLSs. Thus, the higher Stem-like program scores observed in LFRs should be interpreted as enrichment of a stem-like T-cell-associated transcriptional program within a lymphoid-rich anatomical compartment, rather than as direct evidence of increased numbers of stem-like CD8 T cells or the presence of tumor-induced TLSs. The exploratory deconvolution analysis provides additional context for interpreting the Stem-like transcriptional program. Broad immune-cell composition within LFRs, including estimated B-cell, CD4 T-cell, CD8 T-cell, and total T-cell abundance, did not show significant HPV-associated differences after FDR correction. Moreover, the Stem-like program score was not positively associated with estimated CD8 T-cell abundance, although it was positively associated with estimated total T-cell abundance. In sensitivity analyses adjusting separately for CD8 T-cell or total T-cell abundance, the HPV-associated coefficient for the Stem-like program remained positive but was not statistically significant. These findings suggest that broad differences in estimated immune-cell abundance alone may not fully account for the observed transcriptional pattern. However, given the small number of patients and the reference-based nature of the deconvolution analysis, these results do not establish composition-independent enrichment or identify the specific cellular source of the Stem-like program. Higher-resolution approaches that directly resolve cellular identity and state will be required to determine which lymphoid populations contribute to this transcriptional signal. The paired comparison between LFRs and TTRs further highlights the importance of anatomical compartment in interpreting transcriptional programs in OPC. All five immune-related programs were consistently higher in LFRs than in TTRs within the same patients, whereas Estrogen Response Early and Estrogen Response Late showed the opposite pattern and were consistently higher in TTRs. Bile Acid Metabolism, in contrast, showed no consistent compartment-specific difference. These findings extend previous observations that the head and neck tumor microenvironment contains spatially distinct immune niches and microenvironmental architectures [ 6 , 24 , 27 , 28 ]. Importantly, the higher estrogen-response scores in TTRs should be interpreted as compartment-specific transcriptional enrichment rather than evidence of a specific estrogen-dependent mechanism, because the present analysis does not resolve the cellular source or functional activity underlying these Hallmark signatures. Taken together, the paired findings support spatial segregation of immune-related and estrogen-response transcriptional programs between lymphoid-rich and tumor-containing compartments, emphasizing that the biological interpretation of transcriptomic signatures depends on the anatomical compartment in which they are measured [ 24 , 25 , 28 ]. Tumor-free surrounding PTT provided a complementary view of transcriptional programs outside the tumor-containing compartment. The five immune-related programs showed directional differences toward higher scores in HPV-negative PTT, whereas Estrogen Response Early, Estrogen Response Late, and Bile Acid Metabolism showed directional differences toward higher scores in HPV-positive PTT. However, none of these differences remained statistically significant after FDR correction. Accordingly, these findings do not provide evidence for a persistent HPV-associated immune field in surrounding tonsillar tissue, but suggest that anatomically adjacent tissues may provide complementary context for interpreting spatially heterogeneous transcriptional programs [ 24 , 28 ]. Taken together, these findings indicate that transcriptional programs identified in bulk tissue can exhibit distinct patterns across anatomical compartments. Anatomical context should therefore be considered when interpreting transcriptional signatures and designing future spatial biomarker and tissue-sampling studies [ 24 , 25 , 27 , 28 ]. This study has several limitations. First, the GeoMx DSP cohort comprised only six patients, including four HPV-positive and two HPV-negative cases, which substantially limited statistical power for detecting HPV-associated differences within individual compartments and HPV-by-compartment interactions. We therefore treated the patient as the primary experimental unit, while ROI-level analyses were considered exploratory because multiple ROIs obtained from the same patient are not statistically independent. Second, LFRs represented mixed lymphoid regions containing both B-cell follicular and adjacent T-cell-zone components, and the ssGSEA signatures used in this study reflect multigene transcriptional programs rather than direct measurements of specific immune-cell populations or functional states. Reference-based deconvolution provided additional information on broad immune-cell composition, but this analysis was exploratory and cannot resolve the precise cellular sources of the observed transcriptional programs. Third, the PTT cohort was markedly imbalanced with respect to HPV status, with 23 HPV-positive and only four HPV-negative cases, limiting inference from the observed directional differences. Fourth, the three datasets were generated using different transcriptomic platforms and sampled biologically distinct tissue contexts; accordingly, our integration focused on the direction and spatial organization of predefined transcriptional programs rather than direct comparison of absolute ssGSEA scores across datasets. In addition, HPV classification was not determined using an identical assay across the three datasets, and the TCGA-OPSCC classification was based on published RNA-seq-derived viral annotation that included multiple high-risk HPV types; this methodological heterogeneity should be considered when comparing HPV-associated patterns across datasets. Fifth, the survival analyses were exploratory, with only 21 deaths in the TCGA-OPSCC cohort. Associations between several immune programs and overall survival observed in univariable analyses were attenuated after accounting for HPV status, and the proportional-hazards assumption was not satisfied for HPV status. Thus, these analyses do not establish prognostic value independent of HPV status. Finally, no standardized cohort linking the evaluated transcriptional programs to response to immune checkpoint therapy was available in the datasets analyzed here. Larger independent spatial cohorts, ideally incorporating higher-resolution cellular measurements and treatment-response data, will therefore be required to validate the biological and clinical relevance of the observed compartment-specific transcriptional organization. Future studies using higher-resolution spatial technologies will be important for identifying the cellular populations contributing to the Stem-like and other immune-related programs within LFRs. Larger independent spatial cohorts will also be required to determine the reproducibility and clinical relevance of the observed LFR–TTR compartmentalization. Conclusion In summary, this study demonstrates that HPV-associated immune transcriptional programs identified in bulk OPC are spatially organized across distinct anatomical compartments. In the oropharynx-restricted TCGA-OPSCC cohort, HPV-positive tumors showed coordinated enrichment of multiple immune-related programs, whereas GeoMx DSP revealed consistent within-patient compartmentalization, with immune-related programs enriched in LFRs and estrogen-response programs enriched in TTRs. In contrast, HPV-associated differences within individual GeoMx compartments and HPV-by-compartment interactions were not statistically significant after FDR correction, and findings in tumor-free surrounding PTT remained exploratory. Together, these results identify anatomical compartment as an important context for interpreting transcriptional programs in OPC and support a compartment-aware framework for future spatial biomarker studies.
