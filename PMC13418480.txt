Cost-effectiveness of finotonlimab plus bevacizumab versus sorafenib as first-line therapy in unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma in China
Authors: Huihui Xu, Shengjian Yu, Yan Yang, Haoze Bian, Zimiao Wu, Feng Xuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) imposes a substantial health burden in China. Finotonlimab plus bevacizumab recently prolonged progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) vs. sorafenib, but its economic value remains unknown. Here we evaluated the cost-effectiveness of finotonlimab plus bevacizumab vs. sorafenib from the Chinese healthcare system perspective. Parametric survival models were fitted to extrapolate PFS and OS. Total costs, life-years (LYs), quality-adjusted life-years (QALYs), incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs), incremental net monetary benefit (INMB), and incremental net health benefit (INHB) were estimated at a willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold of $27,906 per QALY. Uncertainty was evaluated by one-way and two-way sensitivity analyses, probabilistic sensitivity analysis (PSA), subgroup analyses, scenario analyses, and price simulations. In the base-case analysis, sorafenib yielded 1.74 LYs and 1.25 QALYs at a total cost of $10,303.10, whereas finotonlimab plus bevacizumab yielded 3.01 LYs and 2.18 QALYs at a total cost of $58,595.49. Compared with sorafenib, the combination increased costs by $48,292.39 and generated gains of 1.27 LYs and 0.93 QALYs, resulting in ICERs of $38,203.46 per LY and $51,899.31 per QALY. INMB (−$22,325.82) and INHB (−0.80 QALYs) were negative. Sensitivity analyses identified PFS utility and bevacizumab cost as key drivers, but all ICERs remained above the WTP threshold. In PSA, the mean ICER was $50561.81 per QALY, and the probability of cost-effectiveness was 0% at the prespecified threshold. Based on the assumptions and inputs used in the present model, finotonlimab plus bevacizumab was unlikely to be cost-effective compared with sorafenib at the prespecified WTP in China.KEYWORDS: Cost-effectiveness analysis, finotonlimab, bevacizumab, sorafenib, unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma, partitioned survival model, quality-adjusted life-year, China

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the dominant histological subtype of primary liver cancer, remains a major global health challenge.1 Chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) and hepatitis C virus (HCV) infections are major causes of HCC.2,3 Both can promote chronic inflammation, fibrosis, and cirrhosis, while HBV may also contribute through direct oncogenic mechanisms such as viral deoxyribonucleic acid integration.4–6 According to Global Cancer Observatory (GLOBOCAN) 2022, there were 866,136 new cases of liver cancer and 758,725 liver cancer deaths worldwide, ranking liver cancer sixth in incidence and third in cancer-related mortality globally.7,8 China bears a disproportionate share of this burden, with 367,657 new cases and 316,544 deaths in 2022 alone, making liver cancer the fourth most commonly diagnosed cancer and the second leading cause of cancer death nationally.8 In other words, China contributed more than 40% of the global liver cancer incidence and mortality burden,8 underscoring the exceptional public health importance of HCC in the Chinese setting.

Despite improvements in surveillance and locoregional management, HCC is still frequently diagnosed beyond the window for curative treatment. Approximately 70% of patients present with intermediate or advanced-stage disease.9 For patients with advanced or unresectable HCC, prognosis remains poor. In the landmark SHARP trial, sorafenib, which long served as the first-line standard of care, achieved a median overall survival (OS) of only 10.7 months,10 underscoring the persistent unmet need for more effective frontline options.

Over the past decade, the therapeutic landscape of unresectable HCC has undergone a profound shift with the emergence of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) and anti-angiogenic therapy.11 Immune checkpoint blockade targeting programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) or programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) has offered a new mechanism for restoring antitumor immune responses, whereas inhibition of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) signaling can suppress tumor angiogenesis and remodel the tumor microenvironment to enhance immunotherapy activity. This biological complementarity has reshaped first-line treatment development in HCC. The IMbrave150 study first demonstrated that atezolizumab plus bevacizumab significantly improved both OS and progression-free survival (PFS) compared with sorafenib, establishing an immunotherapy-based combination as a new first-line standard for unresectable HCC.12 In parallel, the ORIENT-32 trial showed that sintilimab plus a bevacizumab biosimilar improved survival outcomes vs. sorafenib in a predominantly HBV-associated Chinese population,13 further supporting the clinical value of PD-1 blockade combined with anti-VEGF therapy in China. Subsequent studies, including HIMALAYA and CheckMate 9DW, confirmed that immune-based regimens can provide durable survival advantages in the first-line setting.14,15

These developments are particularly important in China, where the high population burden of HCC intersects with a rapidly evolving domestic oncology innovation ecosystem. In recent years, China has moved from a largely generics-oriented market toward a major source of innovative anticancer drug development. Policy-driven reforms, including the “Major New Drug Development” National Science and Technology Project and the “Drug Regulatory Science Action Plan,” have accelerated drug research, clinical development, and regulatory review, while strengthening support for domestically developed anticancer therapies.16 Within this context, finotonlimab is a domestically developed, recombinant humanized anti – PD-1 IgG4 monoclonal antibody.9 It was first approved by the National Medical Products Administration on February 8, 2025, in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy for the first-line treatment of recurrent or metastatic head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, becoming the first domestically developed PD-1 inhibitor approved for this indication in China. On February 25, 2025, its indication was expanded to include finotonlimab plus bevacizumab for patients with previously untreated unresectable or metastatic HCC. Although direct head-to-head evidence has not established superiority over other PD-1 inhibitors available in China, finotonlimab is distinguished in this setting by phase 3 evidence generated entirely in a Chinese population and its combination with a domestically developed bevacizumab biosimilar. In a randomized trial involving 346 patients from 67 Chinese centers, finotonlimab plus bevacizumab improved PFS (7.1 vs. 2.9 months; hazard ratio [HR], 0.50) and OS (22.1 vs. 14.2 months; HR, 0.60) compared with sorafenib.9 Finotonlimab was subsequently included in the 2025 National Reimbursement Drug List, announced on December 7, 2025 and implemented on January 1, 2026, with coverage for its combination with bevacizumab in previously untreated unresectable or metastatic HCC. The regimen was also designated a level I, class 1A first-line recommendation in the 2026 Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology guideline for HCC. Its rapid progression from regulatory approval to reimbursement coverage and guideline endorsement highlights its increasing clinical and policy relevance in China.

However, clinical efficacy alone is insufficient to determine whether a new regimen should be widely adopted in a resource-constrained healthcare system. This issue is especially important in oncology, where innovative therapies frequently deliver meaningful survival gains at high acquisition cost. In China, the economic burden associated with liver cancer is substantial at both the societal and household levels. In a multicenter cross-sectional study spanning 39 hospitals across 13 Chinese provinces, liver cancer patients incurred mean total costs of 53,220 Chinese yuan (CNY), with 77.2% facing catastrophic financial hardship.17 Cost-effectiveness analysis (CEA) therefore provides an essential framework for evaluating the value of innovative cancer therapies beyond efficacy endpoints alone.18,19 By jointly considering costs and health outcomes, typically expressed as quality-adjusted life-years (QALYs), CEA can generate decision-relevant evidence for payers, support reimbursement deliberations, and inform rational drug pricing.18,19 Moreover, pharmacoeconomic evidence has become increasingly important for value appraisal, and the efficient allocation of healthcare resources in the Chinese policy context. The 2025 bilingual update to China’s pharmacoeconomic evaluation framework was explicitly positioned as a tool to support value-based decision-making across the full chain of drug assessment and access.20 In parallel, recent studies in China suggests that effective payment standards for an additional QALY have increasingly clustered around 1.0 to 1.5 times gross domestic product (GDP) per capita for most reimbursed medicines,21,22 with higher benchmarks approaching 1.5 to 2.0 times GDP considered in selected settings.23,24

Against this background, this study aims to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of finotonlimab plus bevacizumab vs. sorafenib as first-line treatment for unresectable HCC from the perspective of the Chinese healthcare system. The analysis will estimate total costs, life-years (LYs), QALY, incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICER), incremental net monetary benefits (INMB), and incremental net health benefits (INHB) and will characterize decision uncertainty through deterministic and probabilistic sensitivity analyses (PSA), subgroup analyses, scenario analyses, and price simulations. The findings are expected to inform value-based pricing strategies and maximize the potential population-level benefit of innovative HCC therapy in China.

Materials and methods

Study overview

We conducted a model-based CEA to compare finotonlimab plus bevacizumab with sorafenib as first-line treatment for patients with unresectable HCC. The analysis was performed from the perspective of the Chinese healthcare system and reported in accordance with the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards 2022 (CHEERS 2022), with the completed checklist provided in Table S1.25 A flow chart summarizing the overall analytic workflow of the study is presented in Figure 1(A).

All clinical efficacy, safety, treatment, and survival inputs were derived from a phase 3, multicenter, open-label, randomized trial conducted in China, which enrolled patients with unresectable advanced HCC (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT04560894; Chinadrugtrials.org.cn: CTR20201976 and CTR20201974).9 A partitioned survival model (PSM) with three mutually exclusive health states was developed: PFS, progressed disease (PD), and death (Figure 1(B)). All patients entered the model in the PFS state. At each cycle, the proportion of patients in the PFS state was estimated directly from the PFS curve, the proportion alive was estimated from the OS curve, and the proportion in the PD state was calculated as the difference between OS and PFS.

The model generated total costs, LYs, QALYs, and the ICER. We also calculated the INHB and INMB according to the following equations: INHBλ=ΔE−ΔCλ, INMBλ=ΔE×λ−ΔC, where ΔC=μC1−μC0 and ΔE=μE1−μE0; μCi and μEi represent total costs and QALYs for treatment i, respectively; i = 1 denotes finotonlimab plus bevacizumab and i = 0 denotes sorafenib; and λ is the willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold.

Model construction

A PSM was used because it is widely applied in oncology economic evaluations and is well suited to time-to-event outcomes. The model cycle length was 21 d, consistent with the dosing interval of finotonlimab plus bevacizumab. A lifetime horizon was adopted, such that more than 99% of the simulated cohort had died by the end of the model horizon. In accordance with the China Guidelines for Pharmacoeconomic Evaluations (2025), both costs and health outcomes were discounted at an annual rate of 4.5%.20

Patients and treatments

The economic model was informed by the modified intention-to-treat population of the phase 3 trial, which included 346 patients with unresectable advanced HCC who were randomly assigned in a 2:1 ratio to receive finotonlimab plus bevacizumab (n = 230) or sorafenib (n = 116).9 The trial was designed to enroll approximately 342 patients and provided 85% statistical power to detect a hazard ratio of 0.67 for each of the dual primary end points, PFS and OS, with the overall type I error controlled at a 2-sided level of 0.05. Because the present study was a model-based economic evaluation using published clinical trial outcomes, no separate sample-size or statistical-power calculation was performed for the economic model. Patients in the intervention group received finotonlimab 200 mg intravenously over 60 min, followed by bevacizumab 15 mg/kg intravenously every 3 weeks (Figure 1(C)), whereas patients in the control group received sorafenib 400 mg orally twice daily. For drug dosing and cost calculations, the base-case analysis assumed a representative patient aged 57 y with a body weight of 60 kg.9

Survival data processing and extrapolation

PFS and OS data were extracted and reconstructed from published Kaplan – Meier (KM) curves of the phase 3 trial using WebPlotDigitizer (version 4.8) and R with the “survHE” and “survival” packages.26,27 To extrapolate survival beyond the trial follow-up period, seven parametric survival distributions were evaluated: Exponential, Gamma, Gompertz, Weibull, Gen-gamma, Log-logistic, and Log-normal. The base-case distributions were selected by jointly considering statistical goodness of fit, visual agreement with the reconstructed KM curves, and the clinical plausibility of the projected survival patterns. Statistical fit was evaluated using the Akaike information criterion (AIC), Bayesian information criterion (BIC). Clinical plausibility was assessed by examining whether the fitted curves reproduced the trial-reported median OS and PFS estimates and treatment HRs, adequately followed the observed survival patterns during trial follow-up, maintained OS probabilities at or above the corresponding PFS probabilities, and generated reasonable long-term survival projections over the model horizon.

Costs and health utilities

All model input parameters are summarized in Table 1. Only direct medical costs were included, comprising drug acquisition, drug administration, routine follow-up, laboratory testing, tumor imaging (enhanced computed tomography), management of treatment-related adverse events (AEs), best supportive care, and end-of-life care. Drug unit prices were extracted on March 21, 2026, from publicly available Chinese bid-winning records.35 When multiple regional prices were available, the median reported price was used in the base-case analysis. Other medical service and disease-management costs were obtained from published Chinese studies. Confidential discounts and patient-assistance programs were not included. Potential variation in drug acquisition costs was examined through deterministic sensitivity analyses and drug-price simulations. All costs were inflated to 2025 Chinese prices using the China Consumer Price Index and converted to United States Dollar (USD) using the 2025 average exchange rate of 1 USD = 7.14 CNY. The base-case WTP threshold was set at USD 27,906 per QALY gained, equivalent to two times the GDP per capita in China, as recommended by the China Guidelines for Pharmacoeconomic Evaluations (2025).20 Because grade 1–2 AEs are generally manageable in routine practice, only grade 3 or higher AEs with an incidence of >5% were included in the model. AE-related costs were applied in the first cycle only. Health-state utility values were used to convert LYs into QALYs. Because preference-based quality-of-life data were not reported in the pivotal phase 3 trial, utilities of 0.76 for PFS and 0.68 for PD were obtained from published model-based evaluations of patients with advanced or unresectable HCC receiving first-line systemic therapy.28,29 One source evaluated previously untreated patients with unresectable HCC receiving atezolizumab plus bevacizumab or sorafenib, consistent with the first-line treatment setting of the present analysis28; the other evaluated cabozantinib plus atezolizumab vs. sorafenib in patients with advanced HCC.29 These source populations were broadly comparable to the finotonlimab trial population with respect to advanced disease and the use of first-line systemic therapy, although differences in geographic composition, underlying liver disease, patient characteristics, and treatment regimens may limit the transferability of the estimates. To address this uncertainty, the PFS and PD utilities were varied from 0.61 to 0.91 and from 0.54 to 0.82, respectively, in the deterministic sensitivity analysis and were assigned beta distributions in the PSA. The utility of death was assumed to be 0.

Sensitivity analyses

To examine model uncertainty and test the robustness of the base-case findings, we performed one-way analysis, two-way analysis, and PSA. In the one-way sensitivity analysis, each parameter was varied by ±20% from its base-case value to reflect plausible real-world uncertainty. The results were presented using tornado diagrams. In the two-way sensitivity analysis, the two parameters with the greatest impact on the ICER were varied simultaneously to further explore their joint effect on cost-effectiveness. In the PSA, 1,000 Monte Carlo simulations were performed by simultaneously sampling all uncertain model parameters from prespecified probability distributions according to parameter type. Beta distributions were assigned to health utilities and AE incidences, and Gamma distributions were assigned to cost parameters. PSA results were presented as incremental cost-effectiveness scatter plots and cost-effectiveness acceptability curve (CEAC). Because the base-case outcomes were deterministic expectations generated by the decision model rather than statistical estimates derived directly from individual patient-level observations, confidence intervals were not calculated. Parameter and decision uncertainty were instead characterized through deterministic and PSA.28,43

Price simulation

To further evaluate the economic value of finotonlimab plus bevacizumab under different pricing conditions, we performed a price simulation analysis. The prices of finotonlimab and bevacizumab were reduced separately by 10% to 90%, and a joint price-reduction scenario was also examined. These reductions were modeled as hypothetical proportional discounts to identify the price combinations required for the regimen to meet the prespecified WTP threshold, rather than as forecasts of future transaction prices. The simulated values were not calibrated to any specific previous national reimbursement negotiation and did not incorporate confidential negotiated discounts. Although price reductions of this magnitude may be difficult to achieve in routine practice, they may become more feasible through national reimbursement negotiations, centralized procurement, market competition, or increased availability of biosimilars. Therefore, the analyses should be interpreted as threshold and policy-scenario assessments that illustrate the pricing conditions under which finotonlimab plus bevacizumab may become cost-effective compared with sorafenib.

Subgroup and scenario analyses

For subgroup analyses, HRs for OS and PFS comparing finotonlimab plus bevacizumab with sorafenib were collected for clinically relevant subgroups, including sex, age, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS), macrovascular invasion and/or extrahepatic spread (MVI and/or EHS), extrahepatic metastasis, macrovascular invasion, Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) stage, and Child – Pugh score, baseline α-fetoprotein (AFP), history of viral hepatitis, and previous local treatment. Because subgroup-specific survival curves for sorafenib were not available, we assumed that the baseline survival function of sorafenib in each subgroup was identical to that of the overall trial population. Subgroup-specific survival for finotonlimab plus bevacizumab was then estimated by applying the subgroup-specific HRs to the sorafenib survival functions according to the standard proportional hazards relationship: SOS,subgroup,FP+Bt=SOS,SORtHROS and SPFS,subgroup,FP+Bt=SPFS,SORtHRPFS, where S(t) denotes the survival probability at time t, FP+B denotes finotonlimab plus bevacizumab, and SOR denotes sorafenib.44–46 Because baseline risks may differ across patient subgroups, this assumption introduces additional uncertainty into the estimated subgroup-specific survival and economic outcomes. Accordingly, the subgroup analyses were considered exploratory and should not be interpreted as definitive evidence of cost-effectiveness within individual subgroups.

We also performed several scenario analyses to test key structural assumptions. Scenario 1 shortened the model time horizon to 10 y, and Scenario 2 shortened it to 8 y. Scenario 3 assumed a bevacizumab dose of 7.5 mg/kg, which is half the dose used in the pivotal trial and has been applied in clinical practice.47,48 Scenario 4 applied an annual discount rate of 0% to both costs and health outcomes. To assess structural uncertainty arising from long-term survival extrapolation, Scenario 5 replaced the base-case survival functions with the corresponding second-best-fitting clinically plausible parametric distributions, while all other model inputs were held constant.

Model validation

Model validity was assessed through technical, internal, and structural checks. Technical validation examined model calculations, health-state occupancy, survival constraints, cost accumulation, and discounting. Internal validation compared the reconstructed and fitted survival outcomes with those reported in the pivotal phase 3 trial. Structural uncertainty was evaluated through scenario analyses, including the use of second-best-fitting survival distributions. Detailed validation procedures and results are provided in the Supplementary Methods, and the major model assumptions are summarized in Table S2.

All data processing, survival modeling, economic analyses, and visualization were performed using Microsoft Excel and R version 4.4.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Parametric survival fitting

The reconstructed KM curves and the selected base-case fitted survival curves are presented in Figure 2, and the fitted curves for the seven candidate parametric distributions are shown in Figure S1 for the finotonlimab plus bevacizumab group and Figure S2 for the sorafenib group. The consistency between the reconstructed and trial-reported outcomes was further evaluated in Table S3. The fitted median OS was 22.06 months for finotonlimab plus bevacizumab and 14.23 months for sorafenib, compared with the corresponding trial-reported values of 22.1 and 14.2 months. The fitted median PFS estimates were 7.14 and 2.88 months, respectively, closely matching the reported values of 7.1 and 2.9 months. The HR derived from the reconstructed data was 0.610 (95% CI, 0.450–0.827) for OS and 0.507 (95% CI, 0.390–0.659) for PFS, which were consistent with the trial-reported HRs of 0.60 (95% CI, 0.44–0.81) and 0.50 (95% CI, 0.38–0.65), respectively. As summarized in Table S4, the gen-gamma distribution provided the best fit for PFS in both treatment groups, with the lowest AIC/BIC values in the finotonlimab plus bevacizumab group (1097.169/1107.483) and the sorafenib group (461.8888/470.1496). For OS in the finotonlimab plus bevacizumab group, the log-normal distribution showed the best fit, with the lowest AIC/BIC values (918.5820/925.4581). For OS in the sorafenib group, the log-normal distribution achieved the lowest AIC (560.6626), whereas the exponential distribution had the lowest BIC (564.2669). Because the BIC difference between the two models was (<2), and because the exponential model imposes a constant hazard that may be overly restrictive for long-term survival extrapolation in advanced HCC, the log-normal distribution was considered more appropriate for the base-case analysis.

Base-case analysis

In the base-case analysis, sorafenib was associated with a total cost of $10,303.10, yielding 1.74 LYs and 1.25 QALYs, whereas finotonlimab plus bevacizumab increased total cost to $58,595.49 and generated 3.01 LYs and 2.18 QALYs (Table 2). Compared with sorafenib, finotonlimab plus bevacizumab resulted in an incremental cost of $48,292.39, with gains of 1.27 LYs and 0.93 QALYs. The corresponding ICERs were $38,203.46 per LY and $51,899.31 per QALY. Although finotonlimab plus bevacizumab improved survival and health outcomes, its ICER per QALY substantially exceeded the prespecified WTP threshold of $27,906 per QALY gained. Consistently, the regimen was associated with a negative INMB (−$22,325.82) and a negative INHB (−0.80 QALYs), indicating that finotonlimab plus bevacizumab is unlikely to be cost-effective as first-line therapy compared with sorafenib for unresectable HCC in China under the current WTP threshold. Base-case values represent deterministic model estimates. Uncertainty surrounding these outcomes was evaluated through PSA rather than conventional confidence intervals.

Sensitivity analyses

Deterministic sensitivity analyses

In the one-way sensitivity analysis, the model was most sensitive to the cost of bevacizumab (100 mg), followed by utility of PFS, and patient weight (Figure 3). Across the tested ranges, the ICER varied from $45,304.36 to $63,694.74 for bevacizumab cost, $45,278.84 to $60,787.38 for PFS utility, and $47,558.22 to $60,581.51 for weight (Figure 3). In contrast, uncertainties in routine follow-up, supportive care, administration, and AE-related parameters had relatively small effects on the ICER. The cost of sorafenib (200 mg) also exerted a relatively small influence, with the ICER remaining within a narrow range of $51,587.59–$52,211.04. Importantly, even under the most favorable value of each parameter, all ICER estimates remained well above the WTP threshold of $27,906 per QALY.

Tornado plot of one-way sensitivity analysis. Abbreviation: PFS = progression-free survival. PD = progressive disease. CT = computed tomography. ICER = incremental cost-effectiveness ratio. QALY = quality-adjusted life year.

The two-way sensitivity analysis simultaneously varied the utility of PFS and the cost of bevacizumab (100 mg), two of the most influential parameters identified in the one-way sensitivity analysis (Table 3). Across all parameter combinations, the ICER varied from $39,525.17 /QALY to $74,602.83/QALY, with lower ICERs observed at higher PFS utility values and lower bevacizumab costs. Conversely, lower PFS utility and higher bevacizumab cost were associated with less favorable economic outcomes. However, even in the most favorable scenario, the ICER remained markedly higher than the prespecified WTP threshold of $27,906 per QALY gained. Similarly, the INMB ranged from −$12,392.48 to −$37,098.19 and remained negative across all scenarios. These findings indicate that simultaneous variation in the two most influential parameters did not alter the conclusion that finotonlimab plus bevacizumab was not cost-effective compared with sorafenib.

Probabilistic sensitivity analysis

The PSA showed that all 1,000 simulations for finotonlimab plus bevacizumab vs. sorafenib were distributed in the northeast quadrant of the cost-effectiveness plane, reflecting consistently higher costs and improved health outcomes for the combination regimen (Figure 4(A)). The incremental cost ranged from $31,346.49 to $80,752.29, and the incremental QALY gain ranged from 0.55 to 1.23, corresponding to a mean ICER of $50,561.81/QALY. Notably, no simulated ICER fell below the prespecified WTP threshold of $27,906/QALY. The CEAC demonstrated that finotonlimab plus bevacizumab had a 0% probability of being cost-effective relative to sorafenib at the prespecified WTP threshold of $27,906/QALY (Figure 4(B)). Sorafenib remained the preferred strategy at lower threshold values, with a cost-effectiveness probability of 100% up to $33,000/QALY. The probability for finotonlimab plus bevacizumab began to rise at approximately $34,000/QALY and reached 50.0% at $54,000/QALY. With further increases in the WTP threshold, the probability of finotonlimab plus bevacizumab being cost-effective increased steadily, exceeding 90% at $68,000/QALY and reaching 100% at $108,000/QALY.

Subgroup analysis

In subgroup analyses, the ICER of finotonlimab plus bevacizumab vs. sorafenib ranged from $27,824.25/QALY to $96,776.30/QALY across the prespecified patient subgroups (Table 4). The lowest ICERs were observed in patients without MVI and/or EHS, those without extrahepatic metastasis, and those with BCLC stage B disease, whereas the highest ICERs were observed in patients aged ≥65 y, those with Child – Pugh score 6, and those with extrahepatic metastasis. Importantly, all subgroup-specific ICERs remained above the prespecified WTP threshold of $27,906/QALY, with the sole exception of patients without MVI and/or EHS.

Scenario analyses

Scenario analyses confirmed the robustness of the base-case findings (Table 2). Across all alternative assumptions, finotonlimab plus bevacizumab consistently yielded greater LYs and QALYs than sorafenib, but at substantially higher total costs. The ICER ranged from $34,534.92/QALY to $55,395.07/QALY, with the lowest value observed when bevacizumab was administered at 7.5 mg/kg and the highest value observed under the 8 y time horizon scenario. Nevertheless, all ICERs remained above the prespecified WTP threshold of $27,906/QALY, while INMB and INHB remained negative in every scenario.

Price analysis

Price simulation analyses further explored the impact of stepwise drug price reductions on the economic attractiveness of the combination regimen. In the two-way price simulation, the unit prices of finotonlimab and bevacizumab were simultaneously varied from 10% to 100% of their respective base-case values in increments of 10%. Across the 100 simulated price combinations, the ICER ranged from $8,930.28/QALY, when both drug prices were reduced to 10% of their base-case values, to $51,899.31/QALY, when both drugs remained at their full base-case prices. Overall, 43 of the 100 price combinations (43.0%) yielded ICERs below the WTP threshold of $27,906/QALY (Table 5 and Figure 5). The cost-effectiveness threshold line intersected the left boundary of the heatmap at a finotonlimab price of $13.94 per 100 mg, corresponding to a maximum bevacizumab price of approximately $81.73 per 100 mg. At the right boundary, where finotonlimab remained at its base-case price of $139.40 per 100 mg, the bevacizumab price would need to decrease to approximately $53.85 per 100 mg for the combination to meet the WTP threshold (Figure 5). A further price simulation under Scenario 3 showed a more favorable range of ICERs, from $7,193.84 to $34,534.92/QALY, with 82 of 100 simulated price combinations (82.0%) meeting the WTP threshold (Table S5 and Figure S3). When bevacizumab remained at $139.54 per 100 mg, finotonlimab would need to be priced at no more than approximately $32.86 per 100 mg. Conversely, when finotonlimab remained at $139.40 per 100 mg, the maximum bevacizumab price compatible with cost-effectiveness was approximately $96.93 per 100 mg (Figure S3).

Discussion

In this study, we evaluated the cost-effectiveness of finotonlimab plus bevacizumab vs. sorafenib as first-line therapy for unresectable HCC from the perspective of the Chinese healthcare system. Our findings showed that finotonlimab plus bevacizumab generated meaningful gains in survival and quality-adjusted survival compared with sorafenib, but at a substantially higher cost. Although the combination regimen provided an additional 1.27 LYs and 0.93 QALYs, it increased total costs by $48,292.39, resulting in an ICER of $51,899.31 per QALY, which was far above the prespecified WTP threshold of $27,906 per QALY. The negative INMB and INHB further indicated that the regimen is unlikely to represent good value for money in China at current prices. The conclusion demonstrated robustness to deterministic sensitivity analysis, PSA, and prespecified subgroup analyses.

HCC remains a major clinical and public health challenge in the Chinese setting. Although the phase 3 trial established the superiority of finotonlimab plus bevacizumab over sorafenib in survival outcomes,9 our analysis shows that improved efficacy does not translate into acceptable economic value under the current Chinese WTP threshold. This result is also consistent with the pricing context of sorafenib. Since 2017, China has implemented national price negotiations between marketing authorization holders and the medical insurance agency to enhance access to innovative medicines. Drugs included in the National Reimbursement Drug List demonstrated mean price reductions of 44.0–60.7%.49 The inclusion of sorafenib in Drugs included in the National Reimbursement Drug List negotiations drove substantial price reductions, plausibly improving its cost-effectiveness in the present comparison.

Our findings are also consistent with several studies which likewise shown that clinically active but expensive immunotherapy-based regimens remain economically unattractive vs. sorafenib. For example, Liu et al. reported that atezolizumab plus bevacizumab generated an additional 0.64 LYs and 0.47 QALYs vs. sorafenib in China, but the incremental cost was sufficiently large that the ICER reached $181,552.71/QALY, with a 0% probability of cost-effectiveness at the Chinese threshold.50 In the United States, the corresponding ICER was $253,247.07/QALY despite 0.72 additional LYs and 0.57 QALYs.50 Similarly, Luo et al. found that anlotinib plus penpulimab provided only 0.21 additional QALYs at an incremental cost of $18,194.86, yielding an ICER of $86,546.80/QALY,41 whereas Zhu et al. showed that cabozantinib plus atezolizumab was also not cost-effective vs. sorafenib from both Chinese and US perspectives.29 Real-world evidence from Japan further supports this pattern. Kuwano and colleagues reported substantially higher mean total medical expenditures with atezolizumab plus bevacizumab vs. lenvatinib in patients with BCLC stage B HCC ($101,480.1 vs. $65,227.9).51 This disparity was driven primarily by elevated drug acquisition costs ($70,000 vs. $33,241.4), thereby conferring an economic advantage to lenvatinib.51 Additionally, although finotonlimab plus chemotherapy confers clinical benefit in recurrent or metastatic head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, its cost-effectiveness remains unfavorable at current pricing in China (ICER, $113,051.4 per QALY).52 In contrast, some regimens have been reported to be cost-effective in China. Zheng et al. found that tislelizumab increased QALYs by 0.18 at an incremental cost of only $1,874.37, resulting in an ICER of $10,413.17/QALY under a WTP threshold of $37,304.34/QALY.53 Zou et al. reported that camrelizumab plus rivoceranib yielded an additional 0.26 QALYs at an incremental cost of $7,150.15, corresponding to an ICER of $27,633.75/QALY, which was below their threshold.54 In addition, a 2024 Singapore study concluded that atezolizumab plus bevacizumab was cost-effective under Singapore’s GDP-per-capita threshold, highlighting that the same broad therapeutic approach can generate different economic conclusions under different reimbursement contexts.55 A simple explanation for these differences is that cost-effectiveness in unresectable HCC is highly regimen-specific and context-dependent. It depends not only on the magnitude of clinical benefit, but also on drug prices, body-weight-related dosing, and the WTP threshold applied in each health system.

The robustness analyses reinforce this interpretation. The one-way sensitivity analysis showed that the cost of bevacizumab had the greatest influence on the ICER, followed by the utility of PFS, patient weight, and the utility of PD. This pattern is clinically and economically plausible because bevacizumab is administered according to body weight, such that higher body weight directly increases drug acquisition costs, whereas health-state utility assumptions determine how survival gains are translated into QALYs. In particular, a higher PFS utility increases the value assigned to additional time without disease progression and therefore improves the estimated cost-effectiveness of the combination. However, preference-based quality-of-life data were not collected in the pivotal trial; consequently, the model relied on literature-derived utilities and applied identical PFS and PD values to both treatment groups. This assumption may not fully capture treatment-specific differences in symptom control, treatment administration, treatment burden, or toxicity and may therefore either overestimate or underestimate the QALY gain associated with finotonlimab plus bevacizumab. Nevertheless, the ICER remained above the WTP threshold throughout the prespecified utility ranges. Similarly, even the most favorable combination of a higher PFS utility and a lower bevacizumab price in the two-way sensitivity analysis did not reduce the ICER below the threshold. In PSA, all 1,000 simulations remained in the northeast quadrant, and none fell below the prespecified threshold. The subgroup and scenario analyses also offer clinically relevant nuance. Numerically lower ICERs were observed in patients without MVI and/or EHS, those without extrahepatic metastasis, and those with BCLC stage B disease. This pattern may indicate that patients with lower disease burden, less aggressive biology, or more favorable prognosis derive greater value from survival-extending systemic therapy. Despite clear heterogeneity in ICER magnitude, none of the subgroups crossed the prespecified WTP threshold except patients without MVI and/or EHS. Similarly, scenario analyses showed that shortening the time horizon, removing discounting, or lowering bevacizumab dosing to 7.5 mg/kg changed the magnitude of the ICER but not the direction of the conclusion. In short, uncertainty modified the magnitude of the ICER, but it did not overturn the central finding of non-cost-effectiveness.

From a policy perspective, our price simulation analyses indicate that pricing is the main actionable lever for improving the value of finotonlimab plus bevacizumab. In the base-case simulation, only 43 of 100 finotonlimab plus bevacizumab price combinations met the WTP threshold. Substantial price reductions in finotonlimab and/or bevacizumab are likely to be required. This implication is consistent with recent HCC pharmacoeconomic studies showing that high-cost ICI-based combinations often remain not cost-effective until major price concessions are introduced.50,56 At the same time, our 7.5 mg/kg bevacizumab scenario lowered the ICER but did not bring it below the WTP threshold, suggesting that dose optimization alone is unlikely to rescue the regimen economically. A more realistic policy pathway may therefore involve a combination of measures, including national price negotiation and facilitate the development of generic drugs and biosimilar.50,57,58 Regional economic heterogeneity should also be acknowledged. According to recent official and statistical compilations, China’s 2024 national per-capita GDP was $13,121.68, but per-capita GDP varied substantially across provinces. For example, approximately $27,610.46 in Beijing, $17,238.51 in Zhejiang, $9817.89 in Jiangxi, and $6598.98 in Gansu.59 This nearly fourfold gap implies that the affordability of high-cost first-line HCC regimens may differ markedly across regions. Additionally, from a broader sustainability perspective, metabolic risk modification, drug repurposing, and mechanism-based drug discovery may complement value-based pricing in reducing the long-term clinical and economic burden of HCC. Recent observational studies have associated glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist use with a lower incidence of HCC among patients with type 2 diabetes.60–62 However, these findings require prospective confirmation before such agents can be recommended specifically for HCC prevention. In parallel, preclinical studies of simplified imidazo[2,1-b] quinazoline topoisomerase I inhibitors and conformer-selective human thymidylate synthase inhibitors illustrate potential avenues for anticancer drug development.63,64

Strengths and limitations

This study has several strengths. First, it provides a comprehensive economic evaluation of finotonlimab plus bevacizumab as first-line treatment for unresectable HCC from the perspective of the Chinese healthcare system, using efficacy and safety data from a randomized phase 3 trial conducted in China. Second, the model incorporated clinically relevant outcomes, including costs, LYs, QALYs, ICER, INMB, and INHB. Third, uncertainty was extensively assessed through one-way and two-way sensitivity analyses, PSA, subgroup analyses, scenario analyses, and price simulations. The price analyses further provide potentially useful evidence for value-based pricing negotiations. Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, individual patient-level data were unavailable. PFS and OS were reconstructed from published KM curves and extrapolated beyond the observed trial follow-up. Although technical and internal validation demonstrated close agreement between the reconstructed and trial-reported survival outcomes, and the fitted distributions were selected using statistical goodness-of-fit measures, visual assessment, and clinical plausibility, the reconstruction process and long-term extrapolation inevitably introduce uncertainty. Second, health-state utility values were obtained from external studies of advanced HCC rather than measured directly in the pivotal trial. These estimates may not fully reflect the preferences of Chinese patients or treatment-specific quality-of-life effects associated with finotonlimab plus bevacizumab. Third, subgroup outcomes were estimated by applying subgroup-specific HRs to the overall sorafenib baseline survival functions because subgroup-specific KM curves were unavailable. This approach may not fully capture differences in baseline prognosis across subgroups and relies on the proportional hazards assumption, thereby introducing additional uncertainty. Accordingly, the subgroup findings should be interpreted as exploratory rather than definitive. Fourth, post-progression care was represented using best supportive care and related costs. Given the rapidly evolving treatment landscape for advanced HCC, actual subsequent therapies and their duration and costs may differ across patients and clinical settings, potentially affecting the estimated ICER. Fifth, as with all model-based economic evaluations, the model could not fully capture real-world heterogeneity in patient characteristics, treatment adherence, dose modifications, treatment sequencing, resource use, and regional pricing. Finally, the analysis was conducted from the perspective of the Chinese healthcare system, and the findings may not be directly generalizable to healthcare systems with different treatment practices, prices, and WTP thresholds.

Conclusion

In conclusion, finotonlimab plus bevacizumab as first-line therapy for unresectable HCC in China appears to provide clinically meaningful gains in survival and QALYs compared with sorafenib, but these benefits are achieved at a cost that is unlikely to be acceptable under the current WTP threshold. Substantial price reduction of finotonlimab plus bevacizumab may yield favorable cost-effectiveness. Future research should prioritize the direct measurement of China-specific preference-based health utilities and incorporate real-world evidence on long-term effectiveness and treatment patterns to more accurately define the conditions under which this regimen may be cost-effective.
