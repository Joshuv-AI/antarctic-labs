. For any bounded subdomain

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\Big[-\Delta u-\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})u\Big]\cdot[(X\cdot\nabla)u]\,dx

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\partial\omega}\Big[-(\nu\cdot\nabla)u\cdot(X\cdot\nabla)u+e_{\varepsilon}(u)\nu\cdot X\Big]\,d\sigma+\int_{\omega}\Big\{\sum_{i,j=1}^{M}\nabla_{i}X_{j}\nabla_{i}u\cdot\nabla_{j}u-e_{\varepsilon}(u)\nabla\cdot X\Big\}\,dx,

where
\nu
is the unit outer normal vector field to

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\Delta u\cdot[(X\cdot\nabla)u]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}\Big\{\nabla_{i}\big(\nabla_{i}u_{k}X_{j}\nabla_{j}u_{k}\big)-\nabla_{i}u_{k}\nabla_{i}X_{j}\nabla_{j}u_{k}-\nabla_{i}u_{k}X_{j}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}u_{k}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}\Big\{\nabla_{i}\big(\nabla_{i}u_{k}X_{j}\nabla_{j}u_{k}\big)-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{j}\big((\nabla_{i}u_{k})^{2}X_{j}\big)-\nabla_{i}u_{k}\nabla_{i}X_{j}\nabla_{j}u_{k}+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{i}u_{k})^{2}\nabla_{j}X_{j}\Big\},

and

-W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})u\cdot[(X\cdot\nabla)u]=\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\nabla[W(1-|u|^{2})].

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\Big[-\Delta u-\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})u\Big]\cdot[(X\cdot\nabla)u]

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\omega}\Big[-(\nu\cdot\nabla)u\cdot(X\cdot\nabla)u+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\nu\cdot X+\frac{1}{2{\varepsilon}^{2}}W(1-|u|^{2})\nu\cdot X\Big]\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\omega}\Big\{\sum_{i,j,k}\nabla_{i}u_{k}\nabla_{i}X_{j}\nabla_{j}u_{k}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\nabla\cdot X-\frac{1}{2{\varepsilon}^{2}}W(1-|u|^{2})\nabla\cdot X\Big\}\,dx.

Recalling the expression for
e_{\varepsilon}(u)
, we obtain the lemma.
∎

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{M-2}}\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{r}}e_{\varepsilon}(u)\,d\sigma-\frac{M-2}{r^{M-1}}\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r}}e_{\varepsilon}(u)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{M-2}}\int_{\Omega\cap\partial B_{r}}|\partial_{r}u|^{2}\,d\sigma+\frac{1}{r^{M-1}}\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r}}\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W(1-|u|^{2})\,dx

Lemma
3.4
.

Let
r_{0}>0
and
\varphi:(0,r_{0})\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a differentiable function satisfying, for some
C>0
,

\varphi^{\prime}(r)\geq-Cr-C\varphi(2r)-C\sqrt{\varphi(2r)},\quad\forall r\in(0,r_{0}/2).

Then, for any
0<r<r_{0}
,

\sup_{(0,r]}\Big(1+\sqrt{\varphi(s)+\frac{1}{2}Cs^{2}}\Big)\leq e^{\frac{1}{4}Cr}\sup_{[r/2,r]}\Big(1+\sqrt{\varphi(s)+\frac{1}{2}Cs^{2}}\Big).

(3.3)

Consequently, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exists
r_{*}>0
such that if

and
\varphi\leq\delta
in
[r/2,r]
, then
\varphi\leq 2\delta
in
(0,r]
.

Proof.

Note that once (
3.3
) is shown, the last assertion is obtained by simply selecting
r_{*}>0
sufficiently small so that

e^{\frac{1}{4}Cr_{*}}\Big[1+\big(\delta+\frac{1}{2}Cr_{*}^{2}\big)^{1/2}\Big]-1\leq\sqrt{2\delta}.

This is possible as the limit as
r_{*}\rightarrow 0
of the left side is equal to
\sqrt{\delta}<\sqrt{2\delta}
.

Fix some
0<r\leq r_{0}
. For
k\geq 0
, let
r_{k}=r2^{-k}
,

t_{k}=\sup_{(r_{k+1},r_{k})}\varphi\quad\text{ and }\quad T_{k}=\sup_{(r_{k+1},r_{k})}\Big[1+\big(\varphi(s)+\frac{1}{2}Cs^{2}\big)^{1/2}\Big].

To prove (
3.3
), we only need to show that

T_{k+1}\leq e^{\frac{1}{2}Cr_{k+2}}T_{k},\qquad k\geq 0.

(3.4)

(Once this is done, a simple induction gives

T_{k+1}\leq e^{\frac{1}{2}C\sum_{k\geq 0}r_{k+2}}T_{0}=e^{\frac{1}{4}Cr}T_{0},\hbox{ for all }k\geq 0,

which yields the conclusion.) To prove (
3.4
), consider for every
k\geq 0
the open set:

J_{k+1}=\{s\in(r_{k+2},r_{k+1}):\varphi(2s)<\varphi(s)\}\subset(r_{k+2},r_{k+1}).

If
J_{k+1}
is empty, we have
\varphi(s)\leq\varphi(2s)
for
s\in[r_{k+2},r_{k+1}]
, which implies that
T_{k+1}\leq T_{k}
and (
3.4
) follows.

Suppose in the rest of the proof that
J_{k+1}
is non-empty. For
s\in[r_{k+2},r_{k+1}]\setminus J_{k+1}
, we have
\varphi(s)\leq\varphi(2s)
and so

\sup_{[r_{k+2},r_{k+1}]\setminus J_{k+1}}\Big[1+\big(\varphi(s)+\frac{1}{2}Cs^{2}\big)^{1/2}\Big]\leq T_{k}.

(3.5)

For the estimate in
J_{k+1}
, we use that
\varphi(2s)<\varphi(s)
for
r\in J_{k+1}
which combined with our hypothesis yields

\varphi^{\prime}(s)\geq-Cs-C\varphi(s)-C\sqrt{\varphi(s)}\quad\textrm{for every }\quad s\in J_{k+1}.

This implies

\frac{d}{ds}\Big\{e^{\frac{1}{2}Cs}\Big[1+\big(\varphi(s)+\frac{1}{2}Cs^{2}\big)^{1/2}\Big]\Big\}\geq 0\quad\textrm{in }\quad J_{k+1}.

(3.6)

Since
J_{k+1}
is open, there exist at most countably many disjoint intervals
(a_{k,j},b_{k,j})\subset(r_{k+2},r_{k+1})
such that
J_{k+1}=\cup_{j}(a_{k,j},b_{k,j})
. For each

\varphi(b_{k,j})=\begin{cases}\varphi(r_{k+1})&\text{ if }b_{k,j}=r_{k+1},\\
\varphi(2b_{k,j})&\text{ if }b_{k,j}<r_{k+1},\end{cases}

which implies

\varphi(b_{k,j})\leq t_{k}.

(3.7)

From (

Hence

\sup_{J_{k+1}}\Big[1+\big(\varphi(s)+\frac{1}{2}Cs^{2}\big)^{1/2}\Big]\leq e^{\frac{1}{2}Cr_{k+2}}T_{k}.

(3.8)

Estimate (

and
\Omega\cap B_{r_{0}}=\{(x^{\prime},x_{M})\in B_{r_{0}}:x_{M}\geq G(x^{\prime})\}

Pick

,

in

, and

in

. Imposing that

and testing the harmonic map equation against

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap B_{8r}}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}\chi^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega\cap B_{8r}}\Big[|\nabla u_{0}||\nabla v_{0}|\chi^{2}+|u_{0}-v_{0}|\Big(2\chi|\nabla u_{0}||\nabla\chi|\,dx+|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}\chi^{2}\Big)\Big]\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\Omega\cap B_{8r}}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}\chi^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{\Omega\cap B_{8r}}|\nabla v_{0}|^{2}\chi^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega\cap B_{8r}}|u_{0}-v_{0}|\Big(|\nabla\chi|^{2}+2|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}\chi^{2}\Big)\,dx.

Using the fact that

\int_{\Omega\cap B_{8r}}|\nabla v_{0}|^{2}\chi^{2}\,dx\leq C(\|u_{bd}\|_{C^{1}(\partial\Omega)})r^{M}.

(4.5)

Next, note that (

|u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(0)|\leq\mu\quad\forall~x\in\bar{\Omega}\cap B_{r_{2}},

which further implies

|v_{0}(x)-u_{0}(0)|=|u_{0}(x^{\prime},G(x^{\prime}))-u_{0}(0)|\leq\mu\quad\forall~x\in\bar{\Omega}\cap B_{r_{2}}

Imposing further that

which implies

Imposing further that

Recalling that

in

For simplicity, we drop the index

in the following. As

for every

\Delta[e(u)]=|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+|\Delta u|^{2}-\frac{2|\nabla u|^{2}}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})+\frac{|\nabla|u|^{2}|^{2}W^{\prime\prime}(1-|u|^{2})}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}\quad\textrm{a.e. in }\,\Omega;

in particular,
\Delta[e(u)]\in C^{0}(\Omega)
as the above RHS is continuous in
\Omega
.
Indeed, we compute firstly a.e. in
\Omega
:

\frac{1}{2}\Delta[|\nabla u|^{2}]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j,k}\nabla_{k}^{2}[(\nabla_{i}u_{j})^{2}]=\sum_{i,j,k}\big[(\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}u_{j})^{2}+(\nabla_{k}^{2}\nabla_{i}u_{j})\nabla_{i}u_{j}\big]=|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\sum_{i,j}(\nabla_{i}\Delta u_{j})\nabla_{i}u_{j}.

To compute the last term above, we use for every

Secondly, as

\Omega

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2{\varepsilon}^{2}}\sum_{k}\nabla_{k}^{2}\big[W(1-|u|^{2})\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2{\varepsilon}^{2}}\sum_{k}\nabla_{k}[-2u\cdot\nabla_{k}uW^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}(u\cdot\Delta u+|\nabla u|^{2})W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})+\frac{|\nabla|u|^{2}|^{2}}{2{\varepsilon}^{2}}W^{\prime\prime}(1-|u|^{2}).

Summing up, we obtain a.e. in
\Omega
:

\displaystyle\Delta[e(u)]

\displaystyle=|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}-\frac{2|\nabla u|^{2}}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})-\frac{u\cdot\Delta u}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})+\frac{|\nabla|u|^{2}|^{2}}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W^{\prime\prime}(1-|u|^{2})

\displaystyle=|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}-\frac{2|\nabla u|^{2}}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})+|\Delta u|^{2}+\frac{|\nabla|u|^{2}|^{2}}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W^{\prime\prime}(1-|u|^{2})

Using that

we conclude

\displaystyle-\Delta[e(u)]+|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2|\nabla u|^{2}}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})-|\Delta u|^{2}-\frac{|\nabla|u|^{2}|^{2}}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W^{\prime\prime}(1-|u|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 2|\nabla u|^{4}-\frac{|\nabla|u|^{2}|^{2}}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W^{\prime\prime}(1-|u|^{2})\leq 2|\nabla u|^{4}\quad\text{ in }\omega.

Above, we used (
1.6
) thanks to

. Estimate (

) follows from the nonnegativity of

in (

).

limit
u_{0}
is continuous.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

By Remark
2.8
and Theorem
1.1
, we know that
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})
and
|u_{\varepsilon}|\leq 1
in
\Omega
. Let
r_{0}
,
\delta
and
\eta_{0}
be as in Proposition
4.11
. By hypothesis, after possibly shrinking
r_{0}
, we may assume that
u_{0}
is continuous in

K^{\prime}=\bar{\Omega}\cap(\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(0)+K)=\{x+y\in\bar{\Omega}\,:\,|x|\leq r_{0},y\in K\}.

Moreover, by Lemma
4.5
, we can find
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0

5
Uniform Laplacian estimate

The main result of this section is the uniform Laplacian estimate for solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
to (
1.3
)
converging as in (
1.2
) to a stationary harmonic map
u_{0}
in the region where
u_{0}
is continuous.

Proposition
5.1
.

Let
W\in C^{2}((-\infty,1])
be such that (
1.4
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) hold,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{M}
be a
C^{2}
bounded domain and

u_{bd}\in C^{1,\beta}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{S}^{N-1})
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
.
Let
(u_{\varepsilon})_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\subset H^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a family of solutions to (
1.3
) that satisfies (
1.2
) for a stationary harmonic map
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{S}^{N-1})
. Then for any compact
K\subset\overline{\Omega}
where
u_{0}
is continuous in a neighborhood of
K
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
M,N,W,\Omega,K,u_{bd}
and
u_{0}
such that

\sup_{K}|\Delta u_{{\varepsilon}}|\leq C\quad\forall~{\varepsilon}>0.

Moreover,

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{1,\beta}(K)}\leq C\quad\forall~{\varepsilon}>0.

Clearly, Proposition
5.1
implies Theorem
1.3
in the introduction.

The ideas of proof of Proposition
5.1
are as follows: Using the non-degeneracy assumption (
1.5
) and the convexity assumption (
1.6
), it is shown that the function
W(1-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})
satisfies a partial differential inequality (see (
5.9
)) of the form:

-{\varepsilon}^{2}\Delta f+af^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha+1}}\leq C,

(5.1)

with
a>0,C>0
.
When
\alpha=1
, (
5.1
) is linear and one can use arguments from
[
6
,
35
]
to obtain a pointwise upper bound for
W(1-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})
. When
\alpha>1
, (
5.1
) is semi-linear and we borrow ideas available in the context of conformal geometry
[
30
]
to prove a pointwise upper bound for
W(1-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})
. Using the non-degeneracy assumption (
1.5
) and the bound for
W(1-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})
, we deduce a bound for
W^{\prime}(1-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})
and hence a bound for
\Delta u_{\varepsilon}
(by (
1.3
)), as desired.

We start with
L^{\infty}
estimates for (
5.1
) in Lemmas
5.2
and
5.3
. In the linear case, we will use the fact that, for
\gamma\geq 0
, the modified Bessel equation

r^{2}y^{\prime\prime}+ry^{\prime}-(r^{2}+\gamma^{2})y=0\quad\textrm{ in }(0,\infty).

has a unique, up to a multiplicative scalar, solution which is bounded as
r\rightarrow 0
, namely the modified Bessel function of the first kind
I_{\gamma}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
. Moreover,
I_{\gamma}(r)\sim r^{\gamma}
as
r\rightarrow 0
and
I_{\gamma}(r)\sim e^{r}\,r^{-1/2}
as
r\rightarrow\infty
. See e.g.
[
1
, §9.6.1, §9.6.7, §9.6.10 and §9.7.1]
. In particular, there exists a constant
d_{\gamma}\geq 1
such that

\frac{1}{d_{\gamma}}\leq\frac{I_{\gamma}(r/2)e^{r/2}}{I_{\gamma}(r)}\leq d_{\gamma}\text{ for all }r\in(0,\infty).

(5.2)

Lemma
5.2
.

Let
M\geq 2
,
\Omega
be a domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{M}
,
x_{0}

\in

\bar{\Omega}
and
R>0
such that
B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega
is a Lipschitz domain. Assume that
f\in H^{1}(B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)
satisfies in the weak sense

-{\varepsilon}^{2}\,\Delta f+a\,f\leq C\text{ in }B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega,

where
{\varepsilon}
,
a
and
C
are positive constants. If
B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\partial\Omega
is non-empty, assume also that

f=0\text{ on }B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\partial\Omega.

With

\gamma=\frac{M-2}{2}\geq 0
, we have

f(x)\leq\frac{C}{a}+\Big[\sup_{\partial B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}\Big(f-\frac{C}{a}\Big)^{+}\Big]\,\frac{I_{\gamma}\left(\frac{\sqrt{a}|x-x_{0}|}{{\varepsilon}}\right)R^{\gamma}}{I_{\gamma}\left(\frac{\sqrt{a}R}{{\varepsilon}}\right)|x-x_{0}|^{\gamma}}\quad\textrm{ for almost all }x\in B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega.

Consequently,

f(x)\leq\frac{C}{a}+2^{\gamma}d_{\gamma}\Big[\sup_{\partial B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}\Big(f-\frac{C}{a}\Big)^{+}\Big]\exp\Big(-\frac{\sqrt{a}R}{2{\varepsilon}}\Big)\quad\text{ for almost all }x\in B_{R/2}(x_{0})\cap\Omega.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
x_{0}=0
and write
B_{R}
in place of
B_{R}(x_{0})
.

We first find a barrier function
\phi
in
B_{R}
that will be used for comparison with
f
. For that, we proceed as in the proof of
[
35
, Lemma
6
]
by imposing that
\phi|_{\partial B_{R}}\equiv 1
,
\phi\geq 0
in
B_{R}
and

-{\varepsilon}^{2}\,\Delta\phi+a\,\phi=0\quad\text{ in }B_{R}.

(5.3)

We look for such
\phi
in the class of radial functions, i.e.,
\phi(x)=\phi(r)
,
r=|x|\in(0,R)
which means that
\phi
solves the ODE

-{\varepsilon}^{2}(\phi^{\prime\prime}+\frac{M-1}{r}\phi^{\prime})+a\phi=0\quad\textrm{in }(0,R).

The change of variables
\phi(r):=r^{-\gamma}y(r)
with
\gamma=\frac{M-2}{2}\geq 0
yields a modified Bessel equation for the function
y
:

r^{2}y^{\prime\prime}+ry^{\prime}-(\frac{a}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}r^{2}+\gamma^{2})y=0\quad\textrm{in }(0,R).

We are thus led to set

y(r)=I_{\gamma}(\frac{\sqrt{a}}{{\varepsilon}}r)

and

\phi(x):=\frac{I_{\gamma}\left(\frac{\sqrt{a}|x|}{{\varepsilon}}\right)R^{\gamma}}{I_{\gamma}\left(\frac{\sqrt{a}R}{{\varepsilon}}\right)|x|^{\gamma}}>0\quad\text{ for every }x\in B_{R}\setminus\{0\}.

(5.4)

As
I_{\gamma}(s)\sim s^{\gamma}
as
s\rightarrow 0
, we have
\phi\in L^{\infty}(B_{R})
. Since
\phi
satisfies the PDE (
5.3
) in
B_{R}\setminus\{0\}
, standard elliptic regularity yields that
\phi

\in

C^{\infty}(B_{R})
and
\phi
satisfies (
5.3
) in all of
B_{R}
.

Now the function

\tilde{f}(x):=f(x)-\frac{C}{a}-\Big[\sup_{\partial B_{R}\cap\Omega}\Big(f-\frac{C}{a}\Big)^{+}\Big]\,\phi(x)

satisfies
-{\varepsilon}^{2}\Delta\tilde{f}+a\tilde{f}\leq 0
in
B_{R}\cap\Omega
and
\tilde{f}\leq 0
on
\partial(B_{R}\cap\Omega)
. By the weak maximum principle,
\tilde{f}\leq 0
in
B_{R}\cap\Omega
, which yields the first conclusion.

Next, note that since
(r^{M-1}\phi^{\prime})^{\prime}=\frac{a}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}r^{M-1}\phi\geq 0
(by (
5.3
)) and
\phi^{\prime}(0)=0
(by smoothness and symmetry of
\phi
in
B_{R}
) we have
\phi^{\prime}\geq 0
. Hence

\phi(x)\leq\phi(R/2)=\frac{2^{\gamma}I_{\gamma}\left(\frac{\sqrt{a}R}{2{\varepsilon}}\right)}{I_{\gamma}\left(\frac{\sqrt{a}R}{{\varepsilon}}\right)}\text{ for }x\in B_{R/2}.

Using (
5.2
) we then have

\phi(x)\leq\phi(R/2)\leq 2^{\gamma}d_{\gamma}\exp\Big(-\frac{\sqrt{a}R}{2{\varepsilon}}\Big)\text{ for }x\in B_{R/2}.

The second conclusion follows from the above estimate and the first conclusion.
∎

We next turn to the semi-linear case.

Lemma
5.3
.

Let
M\geq 2
,
\gamma>1
,
\Omega
be a domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{M}
,
x_{0}

\in

\bar{\Omega}
, and
R>0
such that
B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega
is a Lipschitz domain. Assume that
f\in H^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)
is non-negative and satisfies in the weak sense

-{\varepsilon}^{2}\,\Delta f+a\,f^{\gamma}\leq C\text{ in }B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega,

where
{\varepsilon}
,
a
and
C
are positive constants. If
B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\partial\Omega
is non-empty, assume also that

f=0\text{ on }B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\partial\Omega.

Then there exists
c=c(M,\gamma)>0
such that

f(x)\leq\Big(\frac{C}{a}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}+\Big(\frac{cR^{2}{\varepsilon}^{2}}{a}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}(R^{2}-|x-x_{0}|^{2})^{-\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}\quad\textrm{ for }x\in B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
x_{0}=0
and write
B_{R}
in place of
B_{R}(x_{0})
. Replacing
\Omega
by
\Omega\cup B_{R}
and extend
f
by zero in
B_{R}\setminus\Omega
, we may assume without loss of generality that
B_{R}\subset\Omega
. (Here we have used that
f
is non-negative.)

We start by constructing positive barrier functions
Z
in
B_{R}
:

\begin{cases}-{\varepsilon}^{2}\,\Delta Z+a\,Z^{\gamma}\geq C\text{ in }B_{R},\\
Z(x)\rightarrow\infty\text{ as }|x|\rightarrow R.\end{cases}

(5.5)

This resembles the Loewner-Nirenberg equation in conformal geometry
[
30
]
. Motivating by this work, we let
r=|x|
and use the ansatz

Z(x)=Z_{\lambda,\mu}(x):=\lambda(R^{2}-r^{2})^{-\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}+\mu\quad\textrm{ for }x\in B_{R}

with
\lambda
and
\mu
positive, to be fixed later. Since
\lambda>0
,
Z(x)\rightarrow\infty
as
|x|\rightarrow R
. We compute

\displaystyle\Delta Z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{M-1}}\left(r^{M-1}Z^{\prime}(r)\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\lambda}{(\gamma-1)r^{M-1}}\left(r^{M}(R^{2}-r^{2})^{-\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\lambda}{\gamma-1}(R^{2}-r^{2})^{-\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\left[M(R^{2}-r^{2})+\frac{2(\gamma+1)}{\gamma-1}r^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{4\lambda}{\gamma-1}(R^{2}-r^{2})^{-\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\left[MR^{2}-(M-\frac{2(\gamma+1)}{\gamma-1})r^{2}\right].

Using the inequality
(s_{1}+s_{2})^{\gamma}\geq s_{1}^{\gamma}+s_{2}^{\gamma}
for
s_{1},s_{2}\geq 0
, we also have

\displaystyle Z^{\gamma}=\left[\lambda(R^{2}-r^{2})^{-\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}+\mu\right]^{\gamma}\geq\lambda^{\gamma}(R^{2}-r^{2})^{-\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}+\mu^{\gamma}.

It follows that

\displaystyle-{\varepsilon}^{2}\Delta Z+aZ^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\qquad\geq-\frac{4{\varepsilon}^{2}\lambda}{\gamma-1}(R^{2}-r^{2})^{-\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\left[MR^{2}-(M-\frac{2(\gamma+1)}{\gamma-1})r^{2}\right]+a\left[\lambda^{\gamma}(R^{2}-r^{2})^{-\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}+\mu^{\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\frac{4{\varepsilon}^{2}\lambda}{\gamma-1}(R^{2}-r^{2})^{-\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\underbrace{\left[\frac{a(\gamma-1)}{4{\varepsilon}^{2}}\lambda^{\gamma-1}-MR^{2}+(M-\frac{2(\gamma+1)}{\gamma-1})r^{2}\right]}_{=:I}+a\mu^{\gamma}.

In order for
Z
to satisfy (
5.5
), the right hand side above needs to be greater than
C
. To this end, we pick

\lambda=\Big[\frac{4}{\gamma-1}\Big(M+\frac{2(\gamma+1)}{\gamma-1}\Big)\frac{R^{2}{\varepsilon}^{2}}{a}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\quad(\text{so that }I>0),

and impose that

a\mu^{\gamma}\geq C\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\mu\geq\Big(\frac{C}{a}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}=:\underline{\mu}.

With
\lambda
and
\underline{\mu}
as above, to finish the proof, we only need to show that

f\leq Z_{\lambda,\underline{\mu}}\text{ a.e. in }B_{R}.

(5.6)

Suppose by contradiction that

z:=\inf_{B_{R}}(Z_{\lambda,\underline{\mu}}-f)<0.

(Note that since
f\in L^{\infty}(B_{R})
,
z
is finite.) Let
\mu=\underline{\mu}-z>\underline{\mu}
and
g=Z_{\lambda,\mu}-f
. Then
g\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}\cap L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(B_{R})
,
\inf_{B_{R}}g=0
and
g
satisfies

-{\varepsilon}^{2}\Delta g+a(x)g\geq 0\text{ in }B_{R},

where

a(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{a(Z_{\lambda,\mu}(x)^{\gamma}-f(x)^{\gamma})}{Z_{\lambda,\mu}(x)-f(x)}&\textrm{ if }Z_{\lambda,\mu}(x)>f(x)\\
a\gamma Z_{\lambda,\mu}(x)^{\gamma-1}&\textrm{ if }Z_{\lambda,\mu}(x)=f(x).\end{cases}

Since
Z_{\lambda,\mu}(x)\rightarrow\infty
as
|x|\rightarrow R
and
f\in L^{\infty}(B)
, there exists
0<R^{\prime}<R
such that

\inf_{B_{R}\setminus B_{R^{\prime}}}g\geq 1\text{ and hence }\inf_{B_{R^{\prime}}}g=\inf_{B_{R}}g=0.

By the weak Harnack inequality (see e.g.
[
18
, Theorem 8.18]
), this implies that

\|g\|_{L^{1}(B_{(R+R^{\prime})/2})}\leq C(M,a,\lambda,\mu,R,R^{\prime})\inf_{B_{R^{\prime}}}g=0,

i.e.
g=0
a.e. in
B_{(R+R^{\prime})/2}
. It follows that

0=\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{(R+R^{\prime})/2})}\geq\inf_{B_{(R+R^{\prime})/2}\setminus B_{R^{\prime}}}g\geq\inf_{B_{R}\setminus B_{R^{\prime}}}g\geq 1,

which is absurd. We have thus proved (
5.6
) and concluded the proof.
∎

We now prove a localised version of the uniform Laplacian estimate claimed in Proposition
5.1
:

Lemma

.

Let
W\in C^{2}((-\infty,1])
be such that (
1.4
), (
1.5
) and (

(depending only on

,
N

Proof.
To simplify the presentation, we drop the index
{\varepsilon}
. We denote

Y(x):=\frac{W(1-|u(x)|^{2})}{2}.

Recall that
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,\beta}(\Omega)
(see Remark
2.8
) and

in
\Omega
by Theorem
1.1
.

We start with the choice of
r_{1}
and
\delta_{1}
. We fix
r_{1}>0

In particular,

2^{-\frac{2\alpha+1}{\alpha+1}}c_{0}^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}(\alpha+1)W(t)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}\leq W^{\prime}(t)\leq 2^{\frac{2\alpha+1}{\alpha+1}}c_{0}^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}(\alpha+1)W(t)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}\text{ for }t\in[0,\kappa^{\prime}].

(5.7)

We can take
\delta_{1}=\min\{1/4,\kappa^{\prime}/2\}
.

We now fix a ball
B_{r}(x_{0})
with
x_{0}\in\bar{\Omega}
and

such that

in
B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega
and proceed to bound
\Delta u_{\varepsilon}
in
B_{r/2}(x_{0})\cap\Omega
. In the proof,
C
denotes a generic positive constant which depends only on the dimension

,
N
,
r_{1}
,
\delta_{1}
, and the behaviour of the function
W
in
[0,1]
.

Note that, by (

|\Delta u_{\varepsilon}|\leq\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}\max_{[0,1]}|W^{\prime}|\text{ in }\Omega.

Hence if

, then the conclusion follows. We assume in the rest of the proof that

.

Case 1:

\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega)\geq r
or

is a Lipschitz domain. Without loss of generality, we can take
x_{0}=0
and write
B_{r}
in place of
B_{r}(x_{0})
.

We compute

\displaystyle\Delta Y

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}\nabla_{i}[W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})u\cdot(\nabla_{i}u)]

\displaystyle=2W^{\prime\prime}(1-|u|^{2})u^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}-W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}-W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})u\cdot(\Delta u)

\displaystyle=2W^{\prime\prime}(1-|u|^{2})u^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}-W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}(W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2}))^{2}|u|^{2},

where we have used (

) for the last identity.

Note that

in

. Using this as well as (

) and (
5.7
), we can estimates the terms on the right hand side of (

W^{\prime\prime}(1-|u|^{2})u^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\geq 0\textrm{ in }B_{r}\cap\Omega.

Second, using the second inequality in (

-W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}\geq-C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\cap\Omega)}^{2}Y^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}\textrm{ in }B_{r}\cap\Omega\text{ and for }1\leq i\leq M.

Putting the above estimates into (

-{\varepsilon}^{2}\Delta Y+\frac{1}{\hat{C}}Y^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha+1}}\leq{\varepsilon}^{4}\hat{C}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\cap\Omega)}^{4}\textrm{ in }B_{r}\cap\Omega.

Subcase 1a:

0\leq Y\leq{\varepsilon}^{4}\hat{C}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\cap\Omega)}^{4}+C\exp\Big(-\frac{r}{2\sqrt{\hat{C}}{\varepsilon}}\Big)\leq C{\varepsilon}^{4}(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\cap\Omega)}^{4}+r^{-4})\textrm{ in }B_{r/2}\cap\Omega.

Subcase 1b:

\alpha>1

) and applying Lemma

\displaystyle 0\leq Y

\displaystyle\leq\Big({\varepsilon}^{4}\hat{C}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\cap\Omega)}^{4}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2\alpha}}+\Big(\frac{C\hat{C}{\varepsilon}^{2}}{r^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{\alpha}}\Big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\cap\Omega)}^{\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{\alpha}}+{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{\alpha(\alpha-1)}}r^{-\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{\alpha-1}}\Big)\quad\textrm{ in }B_{r/2}\cap\Omega.

In particular, since

, we have

0\leq Y\leq C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{\alpha}}(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\cap\Omega)}^{\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{\alpha}}+r^{-\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{\alpha}})\quad\textrm{ in }B_{r/2}\cap\Omega.

Recalling (
1.3

|W^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2})|\leq CY^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}
(by (
5.7
)) and using the inequaity
(a+b)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}\leq a^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}+b^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}
for
a,b\geq 0
, we deduce the desired estimate
\|\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/2}\cap\Omega)}\leq C(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\cap\Omega)}^{2}+r^{-2})
.

Case 2:

2r/3<\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega)<r
. In this case
B_{r/2}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
and for every
x\in B_{r/2}(x_{0})
,
B_{r/6}(x)\subset\Omega
. By Case 1, we have

|\Delta u(x)|\leq C(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/6}(x))}^{2}+r^{-2})\leq C(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)}^{2}+r^{-2})\text{ for all }x\in B_{r/2}(x_{0}).

We again obtain
\|\Delta u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/2}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)}\leq C(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega)}^{2}+r^{-2})
.

\square

Now we prove the main result in Proposition
5.1
.

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

By Remark
2.8
and Theorem
1.1
, we know that
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,\beta}(\Omega)
and
|u_{\varepsilon}|\leq 1
in
\Omega
. Let
r_{1},\delta_{1}
be as in Lemma
5.4
. Let
\omega_{1}\subset\omega_{2}\subset\bar{\Omega}
be neighborhoods of
K
where
u_{0}
is continuous such that, after possibly shinking
r_{1}
slightly,
B_{r_{1}}(x)\cap\Omega\subset\omega_{1}\cap\Omega
for every
x\in K
and
B_{r_{1}}(x)\cap\Omega\subset\omega_{2}\cap\Omega
for every
x\in\omega_{1}
. By Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
and the continuity of
u_{0}
on
\omega_{2}
, we know that
|u_{\varepsilon}|\to 1
uniformly in
\omega_{2}
. Hence there exists
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
so that

0\leq 1-|u_{\varepsilon}|\leq\delta_{1}\text{ in }\omega_{2}\text{ for all }{\varepsilon}\in(0,{\varepsilon}_{0}).

In the sequel, the constant
C
denotes a generic constant depending only on
M,N,W,\Omega,K
,
r_{1},\delta_{1},{\varepsilon}_{0},\omega_{1},\omega_{2},u_{bd}
and
u_{0}
.

By Proposition
4.1
, after possibly shrinking
{\varepsilon}_{0}
, we have

|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\text{ in }\omega_{2}\text{ for all }{\varepsilon}\in(0,{\varepsilon}_{0}).

Applying Lemma
5.4
to
u_{\varepsilon}
in
B_{r_{1}}(x)\cap\Omega
for any
x\in\omega_{1}
, we have

|\Delta u_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\text{ in }\omega_{1}\text{ for all }{\varepsilon}\in(0,{\varepsilon}_{0}).

Enlarging
C
if necessary and recalling (
1.3
), we see that this estimate also hold for
{\varepsilon}\geq{\varepsilon}_{0}
. Applying
W^{2,p}
-interior estimate for any

(e.g.

]

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{0,\beta}(B_{r_{1}/4}(x_{0}))}\leq C\quad\forall x_{0}\in K\text{ such that }B_{r_{1}/2}(x_{0})\subset\omega_{1}.

Applying Lemma

A.1
Weak and stationary harmonicity of the limit

Lemma
A.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{M}
be a domain,
W\in C^{1}((-\infty,1])
and
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a solution to (
1.3
) for small
{\varepsilon}>0
. If
u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
H^{1}
to a limit
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{S}^{N-1})
as
{\varepsilon}\to 0
, then
u_{0}
is a weakly
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
-valued harmonic map, i.e,
-\Delta u_{0}=u_{0}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}
in the distribution sense in
\Omega
. If, in addition, we assume that (
1.2
) holds and
W
satisfies (
1.8
), then
u_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow u_{0}
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
,
\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W(1-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})\rightarrow 0
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
and
u_{0}
is a stationary harmonic map, i.e, the stress-energy tensor

S_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}\delta_{ij}-\nabla_{i}u_{0}\cdot\nabla_{j}u_{0},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq M

is divergence-free in the distributional sense in
\Omega
.

Proof.

To simplify the notation, in the following, we write
u
instead of
u_{0}
.
It is known that
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{S}^{N-1})
is a weakly harmonic map in
\Omega
if and only if (see e.g.
[
34
, Lemma 3.7]
):

\nabla\cdot(u\wedge\nabla u)=0\textrm{ in the distribution sense in }\Omega,

where
\cdot
is the scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{M}
and
\wedge
denotes the wedge product in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, that is,

\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k=1}^{M}(u_{i}\nabla_{k}u_{j}-u_{j}\nabla_{k}u_{i})\nabla_{k}\varphi^{ij}\,dx=0,\quad\forall\varphi^{ij}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}),1\leq i<j\leq N.

(A.1)

To prove (
A.1
), by multiplying (
1.3
) by
u_{{\varepsilon},j}\varphi^{ij}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and integrating by parts, we have

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{{\varepsilon},i}\cdot\nabla(u_{{\varepsilon},j}\varphi^{ij})\,dx=\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}W^{\prime}(1-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})u_{{\varepsilon},i}\cdot u_{{\varepsilon},j}\varphi^{ij}\,dx,\quad\forall 1\leq i<j\leq N.

Setting
\tilde{\varphi}^{ji}:=\varphi^{ij}
, by reversing above
i
and
j
and then by subtraction we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k=1}^{M}(u_{{\varepsilon},i}\nabla_{k}u_{{\varepsilon},j}-u_{{\varepsilon},j}\nabla_{k}u_{{\varepsilon},i})\,\nabla_{k}\varphi^{ij}\,dx=0,\quad\forall 1\leq i<j\leq N.

As
u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
H^{1}
and strongly in
L^{2}
, passing to the limit
{\varepsilon}\to 0
, we get (
A.1
).

Assume now that (
1.2
) and (
1.8
) hold. Then

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\leq\liminf_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\leq\limsup_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\leq 2\limsup_{{\varepsilon}\to 0}{\mycal F}_{\varepsilon}[u_{\varepsilon}]=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx.

(A.2)

Hence
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\to\nabla u
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Returning to (
1.2
), we deduce that
\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W(1-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})\rightarrow 0
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.
It remains to show that
S
is divergence-free. Consider the stress-energy tensor
S^{\varepsilon}
of
u_{\varepsilon}
associated to the functional
{\mycal F}_{\varepsilon}
defined for
i,j=1,\dots,M
:

S^{\varepsilon}_{ij}=e_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\delta_{ij}-\nabla_{i}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{j}u_{\varepsilon},

where
e_{\varepsilon}
is defined in (
3.1
).
By (
1.2
),
e_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
and so
S^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow S
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
as
{\varepsilon}\to 0
. Therefore, it is enough to show that
S^{\varepsilon}
is divergence-free. Since
W\in C^{1}
and
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, by elliptic regularity for (
1.3
), we have
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{2,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
and (
1.3
) holds almost everywhere. This also implies that
S^{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
. Using (
1.3
), we compute for
1\leq j\leq N
:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}\nabla_{i}S^{\varepsilon}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{i}u_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{j}u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{i=1}^{M}\big(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{i}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{j}u_{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{i}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}u_{\varepsilon}\big)

\displaystyle=-\Big(\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W^{\prime}(1-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})u_{\varepsilon}\Big)\cdot\nabla_{j}u_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:eps}}}{{=}}0\quad\textrm{a.e. in $\Omega$}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma
A.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{M}
be a
C^{2}
bounded domain,
W\in C^{2}((-\infty,1])
satisfy (
1.4
),
(u_{\varepsilon})_{{\varepsilon}\downarrow 0}
be critical points of
{\mycal F}_{\varepsilon}
in
H^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
satisfying (
1.2
) for some
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. Suppose also that
u_{\varepsilon}|_{\partial\Omega}\in C^{0}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{S}^{N-1})
. Then
u_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow u_{0}
in
H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
,
|u_{0}|=1
a.e. in
\Omega
, and
u_{0}
is a stationary
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
-valued harmonic map.

Proof.

By inspection of (
A.2
) (which does not require that
|u_{0}|=1
in
\Omega
), we have
u_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow u_{0}
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
and

\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W(1-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})\rightarrow 0
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. In particular, for a sequence
{\varepsilon}\to 0
,
u_{\varepsilon}\to u_{0}
a.e. in
\Omega
and
\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}W(1-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})\to 0
a.e. in
\Omega
. Thus,
W(1-|u_{0}|^{2})=0
a.e. in
\Omega
.
Recall that
|u_{\varepsilon}|\leq 1
by Theorem
1.1
; thus,
|u_{0}|\leq 1
a.e. in
\Omega
. As
W>0
in
(0,1]
(by (
1.4
)), this implies
|u_{0}|=1
a.e. in
\Omega
. Finally, by Lemma
A.1
, we conclude that
u_{0}
is a stationary
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
-valued harmonic map.
∎

A.2
An elliptic boundary estimate

In the following we provide an elliptic boundary estimate in the half ball

B_{r}^{+}(0)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{M}\,:\,|x|<r,x_{M}>0\}

which is a slightly changed version of
[
35
, Lemma
11
]
. We denote
x=(x^{\prime},x_{M})\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
with
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{M-1}
.

Lemma
A.3
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
,
(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq M}\in C^{0,\beta}(B_{r}^{+}(0))
be uniformly elliptic,
(b_{i})_{1\leq i\leq M}\in L^{\infty}(B_{r}^{+}(0))
and let
u\in H^{1}(B_{r}^{+}(0))
be a weak solution to

\begin{cases}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{M}\nabla_{i}(a_{ij}\nabla_{j}u)+\sum_{i=1}^{M}b_{i}\nabla_{i}u=f+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\nabla_{i}h_{i}\text{ in }B_{r}^{+}(0),\\
u=g\text{ on }B_{r}(0)\cap\{x_{M}=0\},\end{cases}

where
f\in L^{p}(B_{r}^{+}(0))
with
p>M
,
h_{i}\in C^{0,\beta}(B_{r}^{+}(0))
and
g\in C^{1,\beta}(B_{r}(0)\cap\{x_{M}=0\})
. Then there exists a constant
C
depending only on
M
,
\beta
,
p
, the ellipticity constants of
(a_{ij})
, and an upper bound for
\|a_{ij}\|_{C^{0,\beta}(B_{r}^{+}(0))}
and
\|b_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}^{+}(0))}
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{C^{0}(\bar{B}_{r/2}^{+}(0))}+r^{\beta}[\nabla u]_{C^{0,\beta}(B_{r/2}^{+}(0))}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big[r^{1-\frac{M}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{r}^{+}(0))}+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\Big(\|h_{i}\|_{C^{0}(\bar{B}_{r}^{+}(0))}+r^{\beta}[h_{i}]_{C^{0,\beta}(B_{r}^{+}(0))}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\|\nabla_{\tau}g\|_{C^{0}(\bar{B}_{r}(0)\cap\{x_{M}=0\})}+r^{\beta}[\nabla_{\tau}g]_{C^{0,\beta}(B_{r}(0)\cap\{x_{M}=0\})}

\displaystyle\qquad+r^{-\frac{M}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{r}^{+}(0))}\Big].

Proof.

By scaling, it suffices to consider
r=1
. Also, replacing
u
by
u(x^{\prime},x_{M})-g(x^{\prime},0)
,
f
by
f(x^{\prime},x_{M})-\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}b_{i}(x^{\prime},x_{M})\nabla_{i}g(x^{\prime},0)
and
h_{i}
by
h_{i}(x^{\prime},x_{M})+\sum_{j=1}^{M-1}a_{ij}(x^{\prime},x_{M})\nabla_{j}g(x^{\prime},0)
, we may assume without loss of generality that
g\equiv 0
. Then we have the chain of inequalities:

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{C^{0,\beta}(B_{1/2}^{+}(0))}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big[\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{3/4}^{+}(0))}+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|h_{i}\|_{C^{0,\beta}(B_{3/4}^{+}(0))}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4}^{+}(0))}\Big],

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3/4}^{+}(0))}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big[\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1}^{+}(0))}+\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|h_{i}\|_{C^{0}(\bar{B}_{1}^{+}(0))}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}^{+}(0))}\Big],

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}^{+}(0))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}^{+}(0))}.

where we have used
[
18
, Corollary 8.36]
for the first inequality,
[
18
, Theorem 8.25]
for the second inequality, and Poincaré’s inequality for the third inequality. The conclusion follows.
∎
