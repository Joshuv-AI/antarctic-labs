Duplex Paternò–Büchi
Isobaric
Reagents for One-Step CC-Resolved Lipid Isomer Quantification

Xi Chen, Gopal Reddy Ramidi, Nitya Shree, Dallas Freitas, Syuan-Ting Kuo, Huiying Guo, Luchen Wuyang, Aidan Slagter, Peiyu Cai, Ashok Polu, Mahua Choudhury, Xin Yan

Abstract

Lipid isomeromics, the systematic structural and quantitative
profiling
of lipid isomers, is needed to determine how subtle variations in
lipid structure contribute to metabolic regulation and disease. Carbon–carbon
double-bond (CC) positional isomers can have distinct biological
functions despite sharing identical masses and sum compositions; however,
their quantitative analysis remains challenging. Here, we introduce
duplex Paternò–Büchi (PB)-derived isobaric mass
tags, termed AMPE-tags, that integrate lipid derivatization, CC
positional-isomer identification, and relative quantification in a
single photochemical step. The tags combine a PB-reactive ketone,
an isotope-encoded methylpiperazine reporter, and a mass-balancing
group within one molecular scaffold. Photoirradiation installs the
tag directly at lipid CC bonds to form oxetane-containing
PB products. Higher-energy collisional dissociation of intact PB products
generates reporter ions for total-level quantification, whereas collision-induced
dissociation produces CC position-specific diagnostic ions
that retain the reporter-bearing tag. Subsequent MS3 fragmentation
of these diagnostic ions releases reporter ions directly associated
with individual positional isomers, enabling isomer-level quantification
without chromatographic resolution of CC positional isomers.
The duplex tags provided accurate relative quantification of unsaturated
fatty acids over a 200-fold concentration-ratio range and in structurally
diverse mixtures containing PB regioisomers and minor photochemical
byproducts. Application to visceral adipose tissue from mice fed chow
or high-fat diets identified 18 unsaturated fatty acids and 37 CC
positional isomers, revealing diet-associated isomer remodeling that
was masked at the total-fatty-acid level, including opposing changes
among FA 18:1 positional isomers. These PB-derived isobaric reagents
provide a chemical platform for duplex lipid isomeromics and enable
biologically informative quantification beyond conventional sum-composition
lipid analysis.

Introduction

Lipids are essential biomolecules involved
in energy storage, membrane
organization, and cellular signaling, and perturbations in lipid composition
are associated with numerous physiological and pathological processes.
−

 Beyond changes in bulk lipid abundance, subtle structural variations
among lipid isomers can alter membrane properties, metabolic flux,
and signaling functions. Here, we use the term lipid isomeromics to describe the systematic structural characterization and quantitative
profiling of lipid isomers in biological systems. Among the diverse
forms of lipid structural isomerism, carbon–carbon double-bond
(CC) positional isomers are particularly important because
they share identical elemental compositions and masses while often
exhibiting distinct biological activities. Altered distributions of
lipid CC positional isomers have been associated with cancer,
type 2 diabetes, obesity, and other metabolic disorders.
−

 Therefore, analytical strategies that can identify and quantify
lipid CC positional isomers are needed to advance lipid isomeromics
and clarify the biological roles of structurally resolved lipid species.

Mass spectrometry (MS) has become a central platform for lipid
analysis because of its high sensitivity, selectivity, and throughput.
−

 However, conventional MS-based lipidomics remains poorly equipped
to distinguish and quantify CC positional isomers. These isomers
have nearly identical physicochemical properties, co-ionize readily,
and often produce similar fragmentation patterns. In particular, collision-induced
dissociation (CID), the most widely used tandem MS method, generally
does not cleave CC bonds efficiently, thereby providing limited
positional information.
,
 To address this limitation,
specialized ion activation methods, including ozone-induced dissociation
(OzID),
,
 electron-impact excitation of ions from
organics (EIEIO), and ultraviolet photodissociation
(UVPD),
−

 as well as derivatization-based approaches such as
ozonolysis,
−

 epoxidation,
,−

 aziridination,
−

 methoxylation, methylthiolation, cross-metathesis, peroxidation, and Paternò–Büchi
(PB)-based reactions
,
 have been developed for CC
bond localization.

Among these approaches, PB chemistry offers
a particularly attractive
foundation for lipid isomeromics. The PB reaction is a [2 + 2] photocycloaddition
between a photoexcited carbonyl group and a CC bond that converts
the double bond into an oxetane ring.
,
 Subsequent
fragmentation of the oxetane ring generates CC position-specific
diagnostic ions. PB reactions have been applied to a broad range of
lipid classes, including fatty acids (FAs), glycerophospholipids (GPLs),
cholesteryl esters (CEs), and triacylglycerols (TAGs), and are compatible
with direct-infusion MS, LC–MS/MS, mass spectrometry imaging,
and liquid extraction surface analysis.
,,,−

 Despite these advantages for structural characterization, PB-based
workflows have not yet provided a general strategy for direct reporter-ion
quantification of individual lipid CC positional isomers.

Quantification presents a distinct and more difficult challenge
than structural assignment. In this work, total-level quantification
refers to measurement of the combined abundance of all CC
positional isomers sharing the same lipid composition, whereas isomer-level
quantification refers to measurement of individual CC positional
isomers within that population. Conventional MS signal intensities
do not directly reflect analyte abundance because they are affected
by matrix effects, ionization efficiency, and instrumental variability. Quantification based on diagnostic fragment
ion intensities typically requires authentic standards and isomer-specific
calibration curves, which are unavailable for many endogenous lipid
isomers and can be affected by changes in sample matrix or instrument
conditions.
,
 Internal-standard-based approaches
can improve quantitative performance but often rely on chromatographic
separation, which remains difficult for closely related positional
isomers. Consequently, direct and robust
quantification of lipid CC positional isomers remains an important
unmet need.

Isobaric mass tagging provides an attractive framework
for relative
quantification because samples labeled with chemically equivalent
tags can be combined before MS analysis, thereby minimizing variability
arising from sample handling and ionization. Originally developed
for proteomics, isobaric tags such as tandem mass tags (TMT) and isobaric
tags for relative and absolute quantification (iTRAQ) produce distinct
reporter ions upon tandem MS while retaining identical precursor masses.
−

 Recent studies have adapted isobaric labeling to lipid analysis,
including workflows that combine SUGAR tags with meta-chloroperoxybenzoic
acid epoxidation, aziridination followed by TMT labeling, and diazobutanone-assisted
isobaric labeling for relative quantification of multiple lipid classes.
,−

 These studies have established the potential of isobaric strategies
for lipid quantification. However, current workflows generally require
separate CC derivatization and isobaric-labeling steps. In
many existing workflows, reporter ions are generated from intact tagged
lipids and therefore primarily report the combined abundance of isomeric
lipids at the total level. Quantification of individual isomers consequently
often still relies on chromatographic resolution or indirect estimation
from isomer-diagnostic fragment intensities.

We reasoned that
PB chemistry could overcome these limitations
through rational reagent design. In addition to providing a photochemical
handle for CC derivatization, the carbonyl-containing PB reagent
can be structurally elaborated to incorporate an isotope-encoded reporter
and a mass-balancing group. Critically, PB-derived diagnostic ions
can retain the reporter-bearing tag scaffold after oxetane-ring cleavage.
This feature creates an opportunity to generate reporter ions not
only from intact PB products for total-level quantification but also
from isomer-specific diagnostic ions for direct isomer-level quantification.

Here, we report a new class of duplex PB-derived isobaric mass
tags, termed AMPE-tags, for one-step lipid derivatization, CC
positional-isomer identification, and relative quantification. The
AMPE-tags integrate a PB-reactive ketone, an isotope-encoded methylpiperazine
reporter, and a mass-balancing group within a single reagent. Upon
photoirradiation, the tags react directly with lipid CC bonds
to form PB products. Higher-energy collisional dissociation (HCD)
of intact PB products releases reporter ions that quantify lipids
at the total level, whereas CID generates CC position-specific
diagnostic ions that retain the tag structure. Subsequent HCD of these
diagnostic ions in MS3 releases reporter ions directly
associated with individual CC positional isomers. Using the
duplex AMPE-tag pair, we demonstrate accurate relative quantification
of FAs at both the total and isomeric levels without requiring chromatographic
resolution of CC positional isomers. We further apply this
strategy to profile diet-associated changes in FA composition and
CC positional-isomer distributions in visceral adipose tissue
(VAT) from mice fed with chow or high-fat diets. This work establishes
PB-derived isobaric reagents as a chemical platform for duplex lipid
isomeromics.

Results and Discussion

Rational Design of Duplex PB-Derived Isobaric Reagents

We designed AMPE-tags as a new class of duplex PB-derived isobaric
reagents that integrate lipid derivatization, CC positional-isomer
identification, and relative quantification within a single molecular
scaffold ().
The reagent design was guided by three requirements: efficient photochemical
reactivity toward lipid CC bonds, generation of CC
position-specific diagnostic ions, and retention of an isotope-encoded
reporter on those diagnostic ions to enable direct isomer-level quantification.
Conventional isobaric tags comprise a reactive group, a reporter group,
and a mass-balancing group. In the AMPE-tag design, the PB-reactive
ketone serves not only as the lipid-labeling group but also as the
structural probe that converts a CC bond into a fragmentable
oxetane ring.

PB chemistry was selected because the carbonyl-containing
reagent
can be structurally elaborated without compromising its ability to
undergo [2 + 2] photocycloaddition with lipid CC bonds. Upon
photoirradiation, the reaction of the ketone with a lipid CC
bond forms an oxetane ring. Subsequent CID cleavage of this ring produces
diagnostic ions that encode the original CC bond position.
Importantly, the PB-derived diagnostic ions retain the reporter-bearing
portion of the AMPE-tag. This feature distinguishes the present design
from workflows in which structural derivatization and isobaric labeling
are introduced independently and enables a two-tiered fragmentation
strategy: HCD of intact PB products releases reporter ions for total-level
quantification, whereas HCD of isomer-specific diagnostic ions generated
by CID releases reporter ions directly associated with individual
CC positional isomers.

1-(4-(Aminomethyl)­phenyl)­ethan-1-one
(AMPE), an acetophenone-derived
PB reagent, was selected as the reactive core. Conjugation of the
ketone to the aromatic ring favors formation of the reactive triplet
excited state and has been reported to promote PB reactivity while
suppressing competing photochemical pathways. The aminomethyl substituent provides a synthetically accessible
site for coupling to the isobaric-tagging module. Placement of the
amine on a methylene spacer, rather than directly on the aromatic
ring, was intended to minimize electron-donating effects that could
attenuate the photoreactivity of the acetophenone moiety.

The AMPE-tag incorporates a methylpiperazine-derived
reporter group
connected to the PB-reactive moiety through an amide-containing balancing
region. The reporter group design is intended to provide quantitative
functionality rather than simply enhance the absolute detection sensitivity.
Reporter ion-based quantification requires a specifically designed
structure that undergoes efficient and reproducible cleavage to generate
abundant, stable charged reporter ions, as exemplified by established
isobaric tags such as TMT and iTRAQ. Methylpiperazine was selected
because it readily generates a low-mass reporter ion upon HCD and
resembles reporter architectures used in established peptide isobaric
tags.

Duplex encoding was achieved by placing a single 13C
atom at complementary positions in the reporter and balancing regions.
In AMPE-tag i113, the 13C label is positioned at the amide
carbonyl within the balancing region, whereas in AMPE-tag i114, the 13C label is located within the methylpiperazine-containing
reporter region. The two reagents, therefore, have identical overall
masses but generate reporter ions at m/z 113 and 114, respectively. This isotope-placement strategy establishes
the duplex format while preserving a modular architecture that can
be expanded in future designs through additional stable-isotope encoding
and balancing-group modification.

The AMPE-tags were synthesized
from 1-methylpiperazine and ethyl
2-bromoacetate, followed by hydrolysis to afford 2-(4-methylpiperazin-1-yl)­acetic
acid. Coupling of this intermediate with AMPE yielded the final tag
scaffold, and the duplex isotopic variants were prepared from isotopically
differentiated bromoacetate precursors. Detailed synthetic procedures
and characterization data are provided in the Supporting Information S1 and S2. During route development,
activation of the carboxylic acid with N,N′-disuccinimidyl carbonate provided a cleaner route to the final AMPE-tag
than HATU-mediated coupling by avoiding persistent coupling-reagent
impurities and the need for DMF-based purification.

We next
examined the dissociation behavior of the duplex reagents
to establish their suitability for isobaric quantification. HCD generated
the expected reporter ions at m/z 113 and 114, and the two isotopic tags exhibited comparable reporter-ion
yields under optimized fragmentation conditions (Figure S7). Moreover, reporter-ion intensity ratios closely
tracked the molar ratios of AMPE-tag i113 and i114 across the tested
range (Figure S8), demonstrating that isotope
encoding did not introduce a measurable bias in reporter generation.
These results establish the AMPE-tags as chemically equivalent duplex
reagents designed to connect PB-enabled CC localization with
hierarchical quantification at the total and isomeric levels.

One-Step PB Tagging Enables Lipid CC Positional-Isomer
Identification

We first evaluated whether the AMPE scaffold
could support efficient one-step photochemical labeling of unsaturated
lipids while preserving the characteristic Paternò–Büchi
(PB) fragmentation behavior required for CC positional-isomer
identification. A nonisotope-encoded AMPE-tag was initially used to
simplify characterization of the PB products. PC 18:1(9)/18:1(9) was
selected as a representative substrate because it contains two Δ9
double bonds and produces readily interpretable PB-derived diagnostic
ions. Lipid and tag were combined in acetonitrile and subjected to
UV irradiation in a microflow photoreactor under optimized conditions.
Microflow irradiation was used to promote efficient photochemical
conversion while minimizing reaction time and competing photochemical
pathways.

Before irradiation, the
spectrum contained signals corresponding to the unreacted AMPE-tag,
native PC, and a noncovalent lipid–tag adduct. CID of the latter
generated only underivatized PC fragments, confirming that this species
did not represent a covalent PB product. In contrast, UV irradiation
produced a dominant ion at m/z 1075.8,
consistent with the formation of a mono-PB adduct of PC 18:1(9)/18:1(9)
(a–d).
Under the optimized conditions (see Supporting Information S4), the apparent conversion of PC to the PB product
reached 78% (Figure S14). Although the
substrate contains two CC bonds, mono-PB addition was the
predominant reaction pathway, providing a simplified product distribution
suitable for subsequent structural and quantitative analysis.

CID of the PB product generated the expected phosphocholine
headgroup-loss
ions together with oxetane-cleavage products diagnostic of the Δ9
CC position (e). As expected for PB chemistry, formation of two oxetane
regioisomers produced complementary diagnostic ions. Although headgroup
fragmentation competed with diagnostic-ion formation for PC, the observed
diagnostic ions enabled unambiguous localization of the Δ9 double
bonds. Detailed assignments of low-abundance diagnostic ions, multiply
charged products, and minor photochemical byproducts are provided
in Supporting Information S5.1.

We
next examined the applicability of AMPE-mediated PB tagging
to representative unsaturated lipid classes. The AMPE scaffold also
functions as a charge tag, facilitating positive-ion detection of
lipids that are otherwise weakly ionized under conventional electrospray
conditions. PB products from fatty acids, fatty acid ethyl esters,
cholesteryl esters, glycerophospholipids, and triacylglycerols generated
CC position-specific diagnostic ions upon CID (Figures S17–S18). For lipid classes lacking
dominant headgroup-loss pathways, diagnostic ions constituted among
the most abundant product ions. In addition, lipids containing multiple
CC bonds generated interpretable diagnostic-ion patterns,
allowing unambiguous assignment of multiple double-bond positions.
These results establish that AMPE-tags provide a broadly applicable,
single-step route to PB-enabled CC positional-isomer identification
and provide the structural foundation for the hierarchical quantification
strategy described below.

Hierarchical Quantification at the Total and Isomeric Levels

We next evaluated whether AMPE-tags could support hierarchical
relative quantification at both the total and isomeric levels. These
two readouts provide complementary biological information. Total-level
quantification measures the combined abundance of all CC positional
isomers sharing the same lipid composition and therefore reveals changes
in the overall lipid pool. In contrast, isomer-level quantification
resolves the abundance of individual positional isomers and can reveal
pathway- or enzyme-associated remodeling that may be masked when the
isomers are considered collectively.

HCD of an intact PB product
releases isotope-encoded reporter ions that report the combined abundance
of the corresponding lipid composition. In contrast, CID first generates
CC position-specific diagnostic ions that retain the reporter-bearing
AMPE-tag scaffold; subsequent HCD of an isolated diagnostic ion in
MS3 releases reporter ions directly associated with the
corresponding positional isomer. This tandem fragmentation sequence
therefore enables total- and isomer-level relative quantification
from the same tagged lipid population.

FA 18:1(9) and FA 18:1(6)
were selected as model analytes owing
to their ability to generate abundant diagnostic ions for positional
isomer identification. The dissociation behavior of AMPE-tag-labeled
FAs was investigated and optimized as well (see Supporting Information S6). Sample and reference mixtures
containing the two isomers were independently labeled with AMPE-tag
i113 and AMPE-tag i114, respectively, followed by 1:1 mixing before
MS analysis (a). The resulting PB products were detected at m/z 573.4. CID generated the expected diagnostic
ions for the Δ9 and Δ6 isomers, including ions at m/z 401.3 and 431.3 for FA 18:1(9) and
ions at m/z 389.3 and 443.3 for
FA 18:1(6) (b,c). HCD of the intact PB product at m/z 573.4 generated reporter ions at m/z 113 and 114 for total-level quantification, whereas HCD
of each isomer-specific diagnostic ion generated the corresponding
reporter-ion pair for isomer-level quantification (c).

The total-level response was evaluated using FA
18:1(9)/FA 18:1(6)
mixtures at a fixed 1:1 isomer ratio over sample-to-reference concentration
ratios of 0.02–4. Direct-infusion nanoESI-MS provided the expected
reporter-ion response at moderate and high analyte concentrations;
however, quantification became less accurate at the lowest concentrations
because chemical background and minor PB-derived byproducts contributed
to the intact-product signal. Although a linear calibration curve
could still be obtained, the intercept deviated from 0 as expected
(d). Incorporation
of LC separation resolved the PB products from these interferences
and substantially improved total-level quantification. The resulting
LC–MS calibration curve exhibited a slope of 0.9515, an intercept
of −0.0018, and an R
2 value of
0.9999 across the tested concentration-ratio range (c). These data demonstrate
that the reporter-ion ratio derived from the intact PB product accurately
reflects the combined abundance of FA 18:1 positional isomers after
removal of coeluting chemical interference.

We then examined
isomer-level quantification by isolating the Δ9-
or Δ6-specific diagnostic ions generated by CID and subjecting
them to HCD in MS3. In contrast to the total-level measurement,
direct-infusion nanoESI-MS was sufficient for isomer-level quantification
because isolation of the diagnostic ion provided an additional level
of chemical selectivity before reporter-ion generation (see Supporting Information S8.1). For both FA 18:1(9)
and FA 18:1(6), reporter-ion ratios obtained from their corresponding
diagnostic ions tracked the expected sample-to-reference concentration
ratios with excellent linearity (R
2 >
0.995), slopes close to unity, and intercepts near zero (e). Where two complementary
diagnostic ions were available for a given isomer, the final isomer-level
ratio was calculated from the averaged reporter-ion ratios obtained
from both ions. These results establish that PB-derived diagnostic
ions can serve not only as structural markers for CC localization
but also as reporter-bearing intermediates for direct relative quantification
of individual lipid positional isomers.

The hierarchical nature
of this workflow is therefore important
for lipid isomeromics. Total-level measurements establish whether
the overall abundance of a lipid pool changes, whereas isomer-level
measurements determine whether that change reflects proportional variation
across all isomers or selective remodeling of specific isomeric species.
The AMPE-tag platform links lipid structural assignment and relative
quantification within a single labeling reaction and provides two
complementary levels of quantitative information from the same tagged
analyte population. It should also be noted that purification of the
tags is critical for accurate quantification. Residual impurities
from the synthesis process, such as triethylamine, can alter tag reactivity
and compromise quantification accuracy (see Supporting Information S7). Additional validation using FA 18:1(9)/FA
18:1(6) mixtures at various ratios, polyunsaturated FAs, FA derivatives,
and structurally diverse FA mixtures is described below, as well as
in Supporting Information S8. The limit
of detection (LOD) was determined at 50 nM (see Supporting Information S9).

It should be noted that
although AMPE-mediated PB tagging generated
CC position-specific diagnostic ions from multiple lipid classes,
the most extensive quantitative validation in this study was performed
using fatty acids, which produce abundant PB diagnostic ions with
limited competing fragmentation. Quantitative analysis was also demonstrated
for representative fatty acid ethyl ester and cholesteryl ester species
(Figures S29 and S30), indicating that
the reporter-ion-based strategy is applicable beyond free fatty acids.
In contrast, more structurally complex intact lipids, including glycerophospholipids
and triacylglycerols, were evaluated here primarily for CC
localization. Full quantitative validation for these lipid classes
will require future systematic evaluation because competing headgroup
loss, neutral loss, and acyl-chain fragmentation may influence PB
diagnostic-ion formation and reporter-ion responses.

Quantitative Accuracy and Scope in Structurally Diverse FA Mixtures

We next assessed the robustness of the AMPE-tag strategy in a structurally
diverse FA mixture containing species with different chain lengths,
degrees of unsaturation, and CC positional-isomer compositions.
The mixture comprised FA 16:1(9), FA 18:1(9), FA 18:1(6), FA 18:2­(9,12),
FA 18:3­(9,12,15), and FA 20:4­(5,8,11,14). Each FA was present at 20
μM in the sample mixture, whereas an otherwise identical reference
mixture contained each analyte at 10 μM. The sample and reference
were independently labeled with AMPE-tag i113 and AMPE-tag i114, respectively,
combined at equal volumes after photoirradiation, and analyzed by
LC–MS/MS in triplicate.

LC separation was used to resolve
PB products from excess AMPE-tags and minor photochemical byproducts
before tandem MS analysis (see Supporting Information S10). This separation also revealed the intrinsic product complexity
of PB chemistry. For example, the extracted-ion chromatogram of AMPE-tag-labeled
FA 16:1(9) contained three partially resolved features (a–d). CID analysis indicated
that the latter two peaks corresponded to the expected complementary
PB regioisomers, each yielding a distinct Δ9 diagnostic ion,
whereas the earliest-eluting feature produced few diagnostic ions
and was assigned as a minor photochemical side product. Thus, a single
lipid can generate multiple PB-derived species with distinct chromatographic
and fragmentation behaviors.

Despite this product complexity, reporter-ion ratios
remained consistent
with the expected 2-fold concentration difference between the sample
and reference mixtures. At the total level, measured reporter-ion
ratios for the six FAs ranged from 1.83 to 1.93. Isomer-level measurements,
obtained by HCD of the corresponding diagnostic ions in MS3, yielded ratios from 1.88 to 2.00 (e,f). For FAs that generated multiple complementary
diagnostic ions, the final isomer-level ratio was calculated by averaging
the reporter-ion ratios obtained from the corresponding diagnostic-ion
pair.

These results demonstrate that the duplex AMPE-tags tolerate
the
formation of PB regioisomers and minor side products while maintaining
accurate relative quantification across structurally diverse unsaturated
FAs. More broadly, the data show that the reporter-ion readout is
preserved across lipid species containing multiple CC bonds
and remains compatible with both total- and isomer-level analysis
in a mixed-lipid setting.

Application of Duplex Lipid Isomeromics to Diet-Associated FA
Isomer Remodeling in Murine Visceral Adipose Tissue

We next
applied the duplex AMPE-tag platform to characterize diet-associated
changes in fatty acid (FA) composition and CC positional-isomer
distributions in murine visceral adipose tissue (VAT). VAT is a major
site of triacylglycerol storage and therefore provides a relevant
system for examining remodeling of esterified FA pools in response
to dietary fat intake.
,
 We focused the VAT application
on fatty acids released by saponification. This design was chosen
because VAT is enriched in triacylglycerols, and hydrolysis provides
a direct readout of the fatty acyl-chain composition of this major
lipid storage pool. In addition, fatty acids generate abundant PB
diagnostic ions without strong competition from headgroup fragmentation,
allowing rigorous evaluation of both total-level and isomer-level
quantification in a biological matrix.

Lipids were extracted
from VAT collected from mice fed either a chow diet (CD) or a high-fat
diet (HFD) for 14 weeks (three biological replicates per group), which
is a common model to study obesity and relevant diseases, including
hyperlipidemia, diabetes, liver steatosis, and metabolic syndrome.
−

 Triacylglycerols constitute the predominant lipid class in VAT (Table S2), and the
extracts were subjected to saponification
,,
 to release constituent fatty acyl chains
before AMPE-tag derivatization and LC–MS/MS analysis (Table S3).

To enable relative quantification
across biological samples, VAT
extracts from three chow diet-fed mice (CD1–CD3) and three
high-fat diet-fed mice (HFD1–HFD3) were analyzed. Each biological
extract was labeled with AMPE-tag i114, whereas an aliquot of the
CD1 extract was independently labeled with AMPE-tag i113 and used
as a common reference for all measurements (a). CD1 was selected as the common reference
to enable direct comparison of reporter-ion ratios across the complete
sample set. The i113/i114 ratio obtained from independently labeled
CD1 aliquots was close to unity, supporting comparable labeling efficiency
and reporter-ion generation by the duplex tags in the biological matrix
(Figure S33). CC positional-isomer
assignments were obtained from CID-generated diagnostic ions, whereas
reporter ions released from intact PB products and isomer-specific
diagnostic ions provided total- and isomer-level quantitative readouts,
respectively. Three technical replicates were acquired for each biological
sample.

Across the VAT samples, 18 unsaturated FAs and
37 corresponding
CC positional isomers were identified and relatively quantified
(Table S4). Principal component analysis
showed clear separation of the CD and HFD groups based on the combined
FA and FA-isomer profiles (d), consistent with extensive diet-associated remodeling
of the FA pool.
−

 Volcano-plot analysis further indicated that most
detected unsaturated FAs and positional isomers were decreased in
HFD samples relative to CD controls, with several notable exceptions,
including FA 18:1(9), FA 19:1(11), and FA 20:2­(11,14) (c).

Importantly, isomer-level
analysis revealed changes that were obscured
or attenuated when the same lipids were considered only at the total
level. For example, total FA 18:1 did not differ significantly between
the CD and HFD groups; however, its individual positional isomers
exhibited divergent trends. FA 18:1(9) increased significantly in
HFD VAT, FA 18:1(11) remained comparatively unchanged, and FA 18:1(12)
decreased significantly (b, see Supporting Information S11.5, for additional details on FA 18:1 positional-isomer assignment).
Thus, the aggregate measurement of FA 18:1 masked opposing changes
among its constituent isomers. A related pattern was observed for
FA 18:3: although the total FA abundance differed between diet groups,
FA 18:3­(9,12,15) displayed an even stronger diet-associated change.
In contrast, the total-level and isomer-level responses of FA 18:2
were nearly identical, consistent with FA 18:2­(9,12) representing
the dominant FA 18:2 isomer in these samples.

Polyunsaturated
FAs exhibited some of the largest decreases in
HFD VAT. In particular, FA 20:5, FA 22:5, and FA 22:6 each showed
log2 fold changes below −2, suggesting an overall
reduction in the relative abundance of highly unsaturated FA species
in the HFD group. This trend is consistent with prior observations
of increased FA saturation and reduced polyunsaturated FA content
in tissues from HFD-fed mice.
,
 The analysis also enabled
detection of several unsaturated odd-chain FAs, including FA 17:1,
FA 19:1, and FA 19:2, together with their positional isomers. Although
their diet-associated changes were generally less pronounced than
those of even-chain FAs, these measurements illustrate the additional
compositional detail accessible through isomer-resolved analysis.

These results demonstrate that duplex lipid isomeromics can reveal
diet-associated FA remodeling that is not fully captured by conventional
total-level measurements. The divergent behavior of FA 18:1 positional
isomers illustrates the value of coupling structural resolution with
quantitative analysis and identifies candidate isomer-level signatures
for further investigation in larger biological cohorts.

Conclusion

We developed AMPE-tags as a new class of
duplex PB-derived isobaric
reagents for one-step lipid derivatization, CC positional-isomer
identification, and relative quantification at both sum and isomer
levels. The design integrates a PB-reactive ketone, an isotope-encoded
reporter, and a mass-balancing group within a single reagent. Photoinduced
PB reaction installs the reporter-bearing tag directly at lipid CC
bonds, enabling CID generation of position-specific diagnostic ions
and subsequent HCD release of reporter ions from either intact PB
products or isolated diagnostic ions. This fragmentation hierarchy
provides complementary quantitative information: MS2 reporter
ions measure the combined abundance of lipid positional isomers, whereas
MS3 reporter ions generated from diagnostic ions measure
individual CC positional isomers.

Using the duplex AMPE-tag
pair, we demonstrated accurate relative
quantification of unsaturated FAs across a broad concentration range
and in structurally diverse mixtures containing PB regioisomers and
minor photochemical byproducts. Application to murine visceral adipose
tissue revealed diet-associated remodeling of FA CC positional
isomers, including divergent changes among FA 18:1 isomers that were
not apparent from total-level analysis alone. These results illustrate
how duplex lipid isomeromics can uncover structural changes obscured
in conventional sum-composition lipid measurements.

The current
implementation is optimized most extensively for FAs,
for which PB products generate abundant diagnostic ions. From a practical
perspective, although AMPE-tags are structurally more complex than
simple PB reagents such as 2-acetylpyridine, they can be prepared
from accessible building blocks through a short synthetic route, with
detailed procedures provided in the Supporting Information S1 and S2. The full hierarchical workflow requires
MS3 capability for isomer-level reporter-ion quantification,
and such capability is available on ion trap-Orbitrap and related
hybrid and tribrid platforms commonly used in advanced lipidomics
and proteomics facilities. On instruments without MS3,
AMPE-tags can still support CC positional-isomer identification
and sum-level reporter-ion quantification by MS2.

Future studies will extend this PB-derived isobaric-reagent platform
to intact lipid-class-resolved isomeromics, including glycerophospholipids,
cholesteryl esters, triacylglycerols, and other unsaturated lipid
classes. Such applications will require class-specific optimization
of derivatization, separation, and fragmentation conditions, particularly
for lipids in which headgroup or neutral-loss pathways compete with
PB diagnostic-ion formation. More broadly, this work establishes PB-derived
isobaric tagging as a chemical strategy for converting lipid CC
bonds into sites of both structural assignment and quantitative measurement.
