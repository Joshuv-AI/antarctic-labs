From monodromy to S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) SL(2,\mathbb{R}) : reconstructing the logarithmic sector of chiral TMG from virasoro flow
Yannick Mvondo-She

Abstract
We construct and analyze the logarithmic sector of chiral Topologically Massive Gravity (TMG) at the critical point μ ​ ℓ = 1 \mu\ell=1 from the perspective of Virasoro evolution and radial monodromy in AdS 3 \mathrm{AdS}_{3} . We show that the logarithmic graviton arises naturally as a generalized eigenstate of L 0 L_{0} , with its Jordan structure persisting uniformly across the full S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) L SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L} descendant tower generated by L − 1 L_{-1} . A central result is that the logarithmic mixing of primary and descendant states can be equivalently interpreted as unipotent monodromy under analytic continuation of the radial coordinate r → e 2 ​ π ​ i ​ r r\to e^{2\pi i}r . This establishes a direct identification between the LCFT Jordan cell structure and a geometric monodromy operator acting in the bulk. We demonstrate that requiring monodromy-compatible Virasoro flow uniquely reconstructs the full indecomposable logarithmic module, including all descendant levels, and show explicit equivalence with the logarithmic graviton module previously obtained in the linearized analysis of chiral TMG. This provides a unified representation-theoretic and geometric characterization of logarithmic gravity in AdS 3 \mathrm{AdS}_{3} .

Introduction

Three-dimensional gravity provides a unique setting in which classical geometry, quantum gravity, and conformal field theory intersect in a highly constrained and analytically tractable manner. In particular, Topologically Massive Gravity (TMG)
[
2
,
3
]
introduces a gravitational Chern–Simons deformation of Einstein gravity in three dimensions, yielding a propagating massive graviton while preserving the simplicity of lower-dimensional dynamics.

The action of cosmological TMG is

S_{\mathrm{TMG}}=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{3}x\sqrt{-g}\left(R+\frac{2}{\ell^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{32\pi G\mu}\int d^{3}x\,\varepsilon^{\lambda\mu\nu}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\lambda\sigma}\left(\partial_{\mu}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\rho\nu}+\frac{2}{3}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mu\tau}\Gamma^{\tau}_{\nu\rho}\right).

(1)

Linearized excitations around
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
organize into representations of

SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}\times SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{R}.

(2)

Of particular interest is the chiral point
[
9
]

\mu\ell=1,

(3)

at which the left central charge vanishes,

c_{L}=0,\qquad c_{R}=\frac{3\ell}{G}.

(4)

At this point, the linearized massive graviton degenerates with a left-moving boundary graviton, producing logarithmic solutions
[
6
,
7
]
. These modes are characterized by non-diagonalizable Virasoro action and asymptotic growth of the form

\psi^{\log}\sim\rho\,e^{-2\rho}.

(5)

This structure strongly suggests that the dual field theory is a logarithmic conformal field theory (LCFT)
[
12
]
, where
L_{0}
acts non-diagonalizably:

L_{0}\psi^{\log}=h\psi^{\log}+\psi^{L}.

(6)

Recent work
[
10
,
11
]
has proposed a complementary geometric interpretation: logarithmic mixing arises from monodromy in analytically continued radial evolution. In this picture,

\psi^{\log}\sim\psi^{L}\log r,

(7)

and analytic continuation
r\to e^{2\pi i}r
induces

\psi^{\log}\rightarrow\psi^{\log}+2\pi i\,\psi^{L}.

(8)

The central aim of this paper is to unify

•

LCFT Jordan structure,

•

Virasoro degenerate flow,

•

radial monodromy in
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
,

•

and global
SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}
descendant structure.

We show that the full logarithmic module is uniquely reconstructed from monodromy-compatible Virasoro evolution.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we analyze the emergence of logarithmic modes at the chiral point of TMG from the perspective of degenerate Virasoro evolution, showing how the non-diagonalizable action of
L_{0}
gives rise to a rank-two Jordan structure. Section 3 reformulates this logarithmic sector geometrically in terms of radial analytic continuation and unipotent monodromy in
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
, establishing the correspondence between LCFT indecomposability and bulk monodromy. In Section 4 we demonstrate that requiring compatibility between monodromy and Virasoro flow uniquely reconstructs the full
SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}
logarithmic descendant module. Section 5 presents an explicit low-level analysis of the resulting indecomposable representation and illustrates the uniform propagation of the Jordan structure throughout the descendant tower. In Section 6 we establish the precise equivalence between the reconstructed module and the logarithmic graviton module obtained in the standard linearized analysis of chiral TMG by Grumiller and collaborators. Finally, Section 7 summarizes the main results and discusses possible extensions to higher-rank logarithmic sectors, alternative boundary conditions, and more general non-chiral backgrounds.

2
Logarithmic Modes and Degenerate Virasoro Flow

In this section we analyze the origin of logarithmic modes in critical Topologically Massive Gravity from the perspective of Virasoro representation theory. We emphasize how the degeneracy of the linearized bulk spectrum at the chiral point
\mu\ell=1
leads to a non-diagonalizable action of the global conformal generator
L_{0}
, and how this structure can be reformulated in terms of a nilpotent deformation of Virasoro flow. This provides the basic representation-theoretic mechanism underlying the logarithmic sector, which will later be reinterpreted geometrically in terms of radial evolution and monodromy.

2.1
Chiral Point and Degenerate Linearized Spectrum

We consider perturbations of the form

g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu},

(9)

around global
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
, where
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
denotes the
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
background metric. In Topologically Massive Gravity, such perturbations decompose into representations of the isometry algebra

SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}\times SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{R},

(10)

which act as left- and right-moving global conformal symmetries on the boundary.

At the chiral point
\mu\ell=1
, the linearized operator governing left-moving excitations degenerates
[
9
,
6
]
. This degeneracy implies that the usual separation between massless left-moving modes and massive bulk gravitons breaks down. As a result, a new solution appears which is not an eigenmode of the linearized operator but rather a generalized eigenmode. This logarithmic partner
\psi^{\log}
can be constructed by differentiating the massive graviton solution with respect to the deformation parameter,

\psi^{\log}=\left.\frac{\partial\psi^{M}(\mu\ell)}{\partial(\mu\ell)}\right|_{\mu\ell=1},

(11)

where
\psi^{M}
denotes the massive graviton branch. This construction is standard in logarithmic conformal field theory (LCFT) and produces states that lie in a Jordan cell of the scaling operator
L_{0}
, rather than forming a diagonalizable representation.

2.2
Jordan Structure

The left-moving boundary graviton
\psi^{L}
satisfies the highest-weight condition

L_{0}\psi^{L}=h\psi^{L},

(12)

with conformal weight
h
determined by the
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
representation theory.

By contrast, the logarithmic partner fails to be an eigenstate and instead satisfies

L_{0}\psi^{\log}=h\psi^{\log}+\psi^{L}.

(13)

This relation shows that
L_{0}
is not diagonalizable on the span of
\{\psi^{L},\psi^{\log}\}
but instead admits a rank-two Jordan block structure. It is therefore natural to decompose the operator as

L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N,\qquad N^{2}=0,

(14)

where
N
is a nilpotent operator encoding the logarithmic mixing between primary and logarithmic states. This structure is characteristic of LCFT representations and is responsible for logarithmic terms in correlation functions
[
8
,
12
]
.

2.3
Degenerate Virasoro Flow

Exponentiating the action of
L_{0}
defines a one-parameter scaling flow

U(t)=e^{tL_{0}},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},

(15)

which induces a continuous evolution of states within the representation space. From the perspective of the global conformal algebra, this flow is generated by dilatations and therefore implements scale transformations on boundary operators. In this sense,
U(t)
provides a representation-theoretic realization of conformal scaling, acting consistently on both primary and descendant states.

Using the Jordan decomposition
L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N
, with
N^{2}=0
, the exponential map truncates exactly,

e^{tL_{0}}=e^{ht}(1+tN).

(16)

The parameter
t
is naturally interpreted as the logarithmic scaling parameter. In the state-operator correspondence,
L_{0}
generates dilatations on the boundary CFT, so
t
corresponds to the logarithm of the energy scale. In the bulk
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
description, this flow is equivalently realized as radial evolution in Fefferman–Graham coordinates, where
r=e^{\rho}
, so that

t\sim\rho\sim\log r.

(17)

Applying the flow operator to the logarithmic pair yields

\displaystyle e^{tL_{0}}\psi^{L}

\displaystyle=e^{ht}\psi^{L},

(18)

\displaystyle e^{tL_{0}}\psi^{\log}

\displaystyle=e^{ht}(\psi^{\log}+t\psi^{L}).

(19)

Equation (
18
) shows that the primary state transforms purely by scaling, while Equation (
19
) exhibits an additional linear growth in
t
, characteristic of logarithmic representations. This term reflects the nontrivial action of the nilpotent operator
N
and is directly responsible for the appearance of logarithmic divergences in correlation functions and asymptotic bulk behavior.

Thus, logarithmic mixing is naturally interpreted as a nilpotent deformation of standard conformal scaling, arising from the non-diagonalizable structure of
L_{0}
at the chiral point. This provides a direct link between the degeneracy of the bulk linearized operator and the emergence of LCFT behavior in the dual boundary description.

3
Monodromy and Radial Origin of the Logarithmic Sector

The logarithmic sector of chiral Topologically Massive Gravity admits a natural geometric interpretation in terms of analytic continuation in the radial direction of
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
. In this section we show that the indecomposable Virasoro structure can be understood as a manifestation of unipotent monodromy acting on asymptotic bulk wavefunctions. Closely related ideas appear in the analysis of logarithmic modes in critical TMG and their dual LCFT interpretation
[
6
,
7
,
12
]
.

3.1
Logarithmic Radial Behavior

In Fefferman–Graham coordinates, the asymptotic region of
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
is conveniently described by introducing

r=e^{\rho},

(20)

where
\rho\to\infty
corresponds to approaching the conformal boundary.

At the chiral point
\mu\ell=1
, the left-moving graviton exhibits standard falloff behavior

\psi^{L}\sim r^{-2},

(21)

consistent with a primary operator of conformal weight
(h,\bar{h})=(2,0)
in the dual CFT.

The logarithmic partner arises from a degeneracy in the linearized equations of motion, leading to an additional radial prefactor linear in
\rho

\psi^{\log}\sim\rho\,r^{-2}.

(22)

Using
\rho=\log r
, this may be rewritten as

\psi^{\log}\sim(\log r)\,r^{-2}.

(23)

This is the standard hallmark of logarithmic conformal behavior in bulk gravity duals of LCFTs
[
12
]
. In particular, the appearance of
\log r
reflects the non-diagonalizable action of
L_{0}
in the dual representation. Thus we may write the proportionality relation

\psi^{\log}\sim\psi^{L}\log r,

(24)

emphasizing that the logarithmic mode is generated by differentiation with respect to the scaling parameter.

3.2
Analytic Continuation

We now consider analytic continuation around the origin in the complexified radial coordinate. Under a
2\pi
rotation,

r\to e^{2\pi i}r,

(25)

the logarithm transforms as

\log r\to\log r+2\pi i.

(26)

This monodromy is a direct consequence of the multivalued nature of the logarithm and is independent of the choice of branch cut. Applying this transformation to the logarithmic graviton yields

\displaystyle\psi^{\log}

\displaystyle\to(\log r+2\pi i)\,\psi^{L}

(27)

\displaystyle=\psi^{\log}+2\pi i\psi^{L}.

(28)

This demonstrates that the logarithmic partner does not return to itself under analytic continuation, but instead mixes with the primary mode. This behavior is characteristic of indecomposable representations and is directly analogous to Jordan block structure in LCFT
[
8
]
.

3.3
Monodromy Operator

The above transformation can be encoded in matrix form by introducing the basis vector

\Psi=\begin{pmatrix}\psi^{\log}\\
\psi^{L}\end{pmatrix}.

(29)

The analytic continuation acts as

\Psi\to M\Psi,

(30)

where the monodromy matrix is given by

M=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\pi i\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(31)

Since
M
is unipotent, it admits a nilpotent logarithm

M=e^{2\pi iN},\qquad N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad N^{2}=0.

(32)

This identifies
N
as the generator of logarithmic mixing. In particular,
N
coincides with the nilpotent part of the Jordan decomposition of
L_{0}
acting on the logarithmic module.

We therefore arrive at the central identification

\boxed{\text{Jordan structure of }L_{0}\quad\equiv\quad\text{unipotent monodromy in radial }\mathrm{AdS}_{3}.}

(33)

This equivalence provides a direct geometric interpretation of logarithmic conformal structure: the indecomposability of the dual LCFT is realized as a nontrivial analytic continuation structure in the bulk radial coordinate, rather than as an abstract algebraic artifact.

4
SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}
from Monodromy-Invariant Flow

The logarithmic structure identified in the previous sections is not restricted to the primary level, but extends consistently to the full tower of Virasoro descendants. In this section we show that requiring compatibility between monodromy and Virasoro evolution uniquely fixes the action of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}
on the entire logarithmic module. This provides a representation-theoretic realization of the bulk asymptotic symmetry structure in
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
, consistent with standard analyses of linearized TMG
[
1
,
6
]
. We focus on the global conformal subalgebra generated by
\{L_{-1},L_{0},L_{1}\}
, which acts on asymptotic states and organizes bulk excitations into
SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}
representations.

4.1
Scaling covariance of descendant operators

Using the fundamental commutator

[L_{0},L_{-1}]=L_{-1},

(34)

we see that
L_{-1}
has conformal weight
-1
under dilatations generated by
L_{0}
. This is the infinitesimal statement that
L_{-1}
transforms homogeneously under scaling. Exponentiating this relation gives the adjoint action of the one-parameter dilatation group:

e^{tL_{0}}L_{-1}e^{-tL_{0}}=e^{t}L_{-1}.

(35)

This is the standard representation-theoretic statement that
L_{-1}
carries weight
1
under
L_{0}
, and is crucial for the consistent construction of descendant towers in any highest-weight representation.

4.2
Covariance of descendant towers

Iterating the adjoint action yields

e^{tL_{0}}L_{-1}^{n}e^{-tL_{0}}=e^{nt}L_{-1}^{n}.

(36)

This shows that
L_{-1}^{n}
transforms as a homogeneous operator of weight
n
. In the bulk interpretation, this corresponds to the fact that descendant levels shift the effective scaling dimension by integers, consistent with the radial Hamiltonian interpretation of
L_{0}
in Fefferman–Graham coordinates. Such scaling covariance is essential for ensuring that the logarithmic deformation does not spoil the global conformal structure of the module.

4.3
Action on logarithmic descendant states

Applying these operators to the logarithmic module, we define the descendant towers

\displaystyle\psi_{n}^{L}

\displaystyle=L_{-1}^{n}\psi^{L},

(37)

\displaystyle\psi_{n}^{\log}

\displaystyle=L_{-1}^{n}\psi^{\log}.

(38)

These definitions are consistent with the standard construction of Virasoro modules and coincide with the asymptotic symmetry action on bulk graviton perturbations
[
12
]
.

Because the logarithmic structure originates solely from the
L_{0}
action at level zero, the descendant construction does not introduce additional logarithmic corrections beyond those induced by the primary Jordan cell.

4.4
Propagation of the Jordan structure

We now compute the action of
L_{0}
on the descendant states. Using repeatedly

[L_{0},L_{-1}^{n}]=nL_{-1}^{n},

(39)

we obtain

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{n}^{L}

\displaystyle=(h+n)\psi_{n}^{L},

(40)

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{n}^{\log}

\displaystyle=(h+n)\psi_{n}^{\log}+\psi_{n}^{L}.

(41)

Several important features follow immediately

•

the conformal weight shifts linearly with the level, as in any highest-weight representation.

•

the logarithmic mixing term
\psi_{n}^{L}
is
level-independent in structure
, i.e. it does not acquire additional polynomial corrections in
n
.

•

the indecomposable structure is therefore stable under Virasoro descendant generation.

This stability is a characteristic feature of logarithmic conformal field theories, where Jordan cells persist uniformly across descendant towers
[
8
,
4
]
.

4.5
Monodromy-compatible Virasoro flow

The key observation of this work is that the above structure is not arbitrary, but is uniquely fixed by requiring compatibility with the radial monodromy structure introduced in the previous section. In particular, since the logarithmic mixing at level zero is generated by a unipotent monodromy operator acting on
\psi^{\log}
, consistency requires that this same nilpotent operator commute with the action of descendant generation:

[L_{-1},N]=0.

(42)

This ensures that monodromy and Virasoro evolution are simultaneously diagonalisable up to Jordan structure, and therefore define a consistent indecomposable representation of the full global conformal algebra.

4.6
Reconstruction of the full
SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}
module

We therefore conclude that the combined action of
L_{0}
and
L_{-1}
generates a complete logarithmic representation of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}
, with basis

\{\psi_{n}^{L},\psi_{n}^{\log}\}_{n\geq 0}.

(43)

Each level
n
forms a rank-two Jordan cell

L_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}h+n&1\\
0&h+n\end{pmatrix}.

(44)

Thus monodromy-compatible Virasoro flow reconstructs not only the primary logarithmic pair, but the entire indecomposable descendant tower.

\boxed{\text{Monodromy-invariant flow}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{full }SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}\text{ logarithmic module}.}

(45)

This establishes a direct equivalence between bulk radial analytic structure and boundary representation theory, reinforcing the interpretation of logarithmic gravity as a geometric realization of LCFT indecomposability.

5
Low-Level Structure of the Logarithmic
SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}
Module

In the previous section we showed that monodromy-compatible Virasoro flow reconstructs the full logarithmic descendant tower of critical TMG. The purpose of the present section is to analyze the explicit structure of this module at low descendant levels. Our goal is twofold

1.

to demonstrate concretely how the Jordan structure propagates through the descendant tower;

2.

to make explicit contact with the logarithmic modules familiar from the LCFT interpretation of chiral TMG.

We shall see that every descendant level carries an identical indecomposable structure governed by the same nilpotent extension.

5.1
Primary Logarithmic Pair

We begin with the logarithmic pair

(\psi^{L},\psi^{\log}),

(46)

satisfying

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi^{L}

\displaystyle=h\psi^{L},

(47)

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi^{\log}

\displaystyle=h\psi^{\log}+\psi^{L}.

(48)

The highest-weight conditions are

L_{1}\psi^{L}=0,\qquad L_{1}\psi^{\log}=0.

(49)

Introducing

\Psi_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}\psi^{\log}\\
\psi^{L}\end{pmatrix},

(50)

the action of
L_{0}
takes the Jordan form

L_{0}\Psi_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}h&1\\
0&h\end{pmatrix}\Psi_{0}.

(51)

Thus the primary level already forms a rank-two indecomposable module.

5.2
Level-One Descendants

We now construct the first descendant level.

Define

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{L}

\displaystyle=L_{-1}\psi^{L},

(52)

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{\log}

\displaystyle=L_{-1}\psi^{\log}.

(53)

We first compute the action of
L_{0}
on
\psi_{1}^{L}
. Using the commutator

[L_{0},L_{-1}]=L_{-1},

(54)

we obtain

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{1}^{L}

\displaystyle=L_{0}L_{-1}\psi^{L}

\displaystyle=(L_{-1}L_{0}+[L_{0},L_{-1}])\psi^{L}

\displaystyle=L_{-1}(h\psi^{L})+L_{-1}\psi^{L}

\displaystyle=(h+1)\psi_{1}^{L}.

(55)

Thus
\psi_{1}^{L}
has conformal weight
h+1
.

We next compute the action of
L_{0}
on the logarithmic descendant:

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{1}^{\log}

\displaystyle=L_{0}L_{-1}\psi^{\log}

\displaystyle=(L_{-1}L_{0}+[L_{0},L_{-1}])\psi^{\log}

\displaystyle=L_{-1}(h\psi^{\log}+\psi^{L})+L_{-1}\psi^{\log}

\displaystyle=(h+1)\psi_{1}^{\log}+\psi_{1}^{L}.

(56)

Therefore the level-one descendants form another Jordan pair:

(\psi_{1}^{L},\psi_{1}^{\log}).

(57)

In matrix form,

L_{0}\Psi_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}h+1&1\\
0&h+1\end{pmatrix}\Psi_{1},

(58)

where

\Psi_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}^{\log}\\
\psi_{1}^{L}\end{pmatrix}.

(59)

Thus the Jordan structure propagates unchanged to the first descendant level.

5.3
Level-Two Descendants

We now proceed to level two. Define

\displaystyle\psi_{2}^{L}

\displaystyle=L_{-1}^{2}\psi^{L},

(60)

\displaystyle\psi_{2}^{\log}

\displaystyle=L_{-1}^{2}\psi^{\log}.

(61)

We first compute the ordinary descendant transformation

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{2}^{L}

\displaystyle=L_{0}L_{-1}^{2}\psi^{L}.

(62)

Using

[L_{0},L_{-1}^{2}]=2L_{-1}^{2},

(63)

we obtain

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{2}^{L}

\displaystyle=(L_{-1}^{2}L_{0}+[L_{0},L_{-1}^{2}])\psi^{L}

\displaystyle=L_{-1}^{2}(h\psi^{L})+2L_{-1}^{2}\psi^{L}

\displaystyle=(h+2)\psi_{2}^{L}.

(64)

For the logarithmic descendant,

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{2}^{\log}

\displaystyle=L_{0}L_{-1}^{2}\psi^{\log}

\displaystyle=(L_{-1}^{2}L_{0}+[L_{0},L_{-1}^{2}])\psi^{\log}

\displaystyle=L_{-1}^{2}(h\psi^{\log}+\psi^{L})+2L_{-1}^{2}\psi^{\log}

\displaystyle=(h+2)\psi_{2}^{\log}+\psi_{2}^{L}.

(65)

Again the same indecomposable structure appears

L_{0}\Psi_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}h+2&1\\
0&h+2\end{pmatrix}\Psi_{2}.

(66)

5.4
General Recursive Structure

The low-level computations strongly suggest the general relation

L_{0}\psi_{n}^{\log}=(h+n)\psi_{n}^{\log}+\psi_{n}^{L},

(67)

with

\psi_{n}^{\log}=L_{-1}^{n}\psi^{\log},\qquad\psi_{n}^{L}=L_{-1}^{n}\psi^{L}.

(68)

We now prove this recursively. Suppose

L_{0}\psi_{n}^{\log}=(h+n)\psi_{n}^{\log}+\psi_{n}^{L}.

(69)

Then

\displaystyle L_{0}\psi_{n+1}^{\log}

\displaystyle=L_{0}L_{-1}\psi_{n}^{\log}

\displaystyle=(L_{-1}L_{0}+[L_{0},L_{-1}])\psi_{n}^{\log}

\displaystyle=L_{-1}\left((h+n)\psi_{n}^{\log}+\psi_{n}^{L}\right)+L_{-1}\psi_{n}^{\log}

\displaystyle=(h+n+1)\psi_{n+1}^{\log}+\psi_{n+1}^{L}.

(70)

Thus the Jordan structure propagates to all levels.

5.5
Nilpotent Structure at Each Level

At every descendant level
n
, define

\Psi_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{n}^{\log}\\
\psi_{n}^{L}\end{pmatrix}.

(71)

Then

L_{0}\Psi_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}h+n&1\\
0&h+n\end{pmatrix}\Psi_{n}.

(72)

Equivalently,

L_{0}=(h+n)\mathbf{1}+N,

(73)

with

N^{2}=0.

(74)

The same nilpotent operator therefore governs every descendant level. This is one of the most important structural properties of the logarithmic module

\boxed{\text{The indecomposable structure is stable under descendant generation.}}

(75)

5.6
Monodromy Structure at Low Levels

We now examine the monodromy transformation. At level zero,

\psi^{\log}\rightarrow\psi^{\log}+2\pi i\,\psi^{L}.

(76)

Applying
L_{-1}
gives the level-one transformation:

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{\log}

\displaystyle=L_{-1}\psi^{\log}

\displaystyle\rightarrow L_{-1}(\psi^{\log}+2\pi i\psi^{L})

\displaystyle=\psi_{1}^{\log}+2\pi i\,\psi_{1}^{L}.

(77)

Similarly,

\psi_{2}^{\log}\rightarrow\psi_{2}^{\log}+2\pi i\,\psi_{2}^{L}.

(78)

Hence every level transforms through the same unipotent matrix

M=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\pi i\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(79)

The entire descendant tower therefore carries a uniform monodromy representation.

5.7
Structure of the Indecomposable Module

The logarithmic module reconstructed above differs fundamentally from an ordinary highest-weight representation. For an ordinary irreducible module,
L_{0}
is diagonalizable

L_{0}\psi_{n}=(h+n)\psi_{n}.

(80)

In contrast, the logarithmic module contains generalized eigenvectors

(L_{0}-h-n)\psi_{n}^{\log}=\psi_{n}^{L},

(81)

while

(L_{0}-h-n)^{2}\psi_{n}^{\log}=0.

(82)

Thus each level forms a rank-two Jordan cell. The module is reducible because the states
\psi_{n}^{L}
form an invariant submodule. However, it is not fully decomposable because the logarithmic descendants cannot be separated from the ordinary descendants.
The logarithmic tower therefore constitutes an indecomposable but reducible representation of the global conformal algebra.

5.8
Uniform Indecomposability

Each level
n
satisfies

(L_{0}-h-n)^{2}\psi_{n}^{\log}=0,\qquad(L_{0}-h-n)\psi_{n}^{L}=0.

(83)

Thus every descendant level carries an identical rank-two Jordan cell.

5.9
Summary

We have explicitly constructed the low-level structure of the logarithmic
SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}
module generated by monodromy-compatible Virasoro flow.
The principal results are

1.

every descendant level forms a rank-two Jordan pair;

2.

the same nilpotent structure governs all levels;

3.

the monodromy transformation acts uniformly throughout the module;

4.

and the full descendant tower forms an indecomposable logarithmic representation of the global conformal algebra.

These results establish explicitly that the logarithmic descendant tower reconstructed from monodromy flow coincides structurally with the logarithmic modules appearing in the LCFT interpretation of critical TMG.

In the next section we shall make this correspondence precise by identifying the reconstructed module with the logarithmic graviton module originally obtained by Grumiller and collaborators.

6
Identification with the Grumiller et al. Logarithmic Module

In the previous sections we constructed a logarithmic
SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}
module arising from monodromy-compatible Virasoro flow in critical TMG. We now demonstrate that this module is equivalent, level-by-level, to the logarithmic graviton module originally identified in
[
6
,
7
,
12
]
. The goal of this section is not merely to state agreement, but to establish a precise identification map between

•

the monodromy-derived Jordan module constructed in Sections 2–5, and

•

the standard logarithmic graviton module in cosmological TMG.

We proceed by comparing: (i) primary structure, (ii) descendant organization, (iii) asymptotic behavior, and (iv) action of global generators.

6.1
The Grumiller–Johansson Logarithmic Pair

In the standard analysis of linearized cosmological TMG at the chiral point
[
6
]
, one finds three classes of solutions:

1.

left-moving massless gravitons
\psi^{L}
,

2.

right-moving massless gravitons
\psi^{R}
,

3.

logarithmic gravitons
\psi^{\log}
.

7
Summary and outlook

In this work we have shown that the logarithmic sector of chiral Topologically Massive Gravity admits a unified description in terms of Virasoro representation theory and radial monodromy in
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
. Starting from the degeneracy at the chiral point
\mu\ell=1
, we constructed the logarithmic graviton as a generalized
L_{0}
-eigenstate and demonstrated that its Jordan structure propagates consistently through the entire
SL(2,\mathbb{R})_{L}
descendant tower generated by
L_{-1}
.

A central outcome is the identification of logarithmic mixing with unipotent monodromy under analytic continuation of the radial coordinate. In this picture, the logarithmic partner of a primary state is not an independent deformation but arises from a nontrivial analytic structure of bulk wavefunctions. The nilpotent operator governing the LCFT Jordan cell is thereby realized geometrically as the generator of this monodromy.

Requiring compatibility between Virasoro evolution and radial monodromy uniquely fixes the structure of the full indecomposable module. The resulting representation is shown to be isomorphic, at every descendant level, to the logarithmic graviton module obtained in the standard linearized analysis of cosmological TMG. This establishes a direct equivalence between the LCFT description and a purely bulk geometric formulation.

Several directions naturally follow. First, it would be interesting to extend the present analysis to higher-rank logarithmic sectors, where
L_{0}
develops larger Jordan blocks and monodromy becomes higher order. Second, the role of boundary conditions in selecting or truncating logarithmic modes deserves further clarification, particularly in relation to consistency of holographic duals. Finally, it may be possible to generalize the present monodromy framework beyond the chiral point, where logarithmic structures are expected to emerge in more subtle forms.

Overall, the results suggest that logarithmic gravity in
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
should be understood not only as a degeneracy of the linearized spectrum, but as a manifestation of a deeper analytic and representation-theoretic structure encoded in bulk monodromy.
