 Executive Summary
In this Technical Report, the chemical and radionuclide contaminant results from the April 2026 Semiannual sample of the
Salt Waste Processing Facility (SWPF) Decontaminated Salt Solution (DSS) are presented in tabulated form. The
information from this characterization will be used by Savannah River Mission Completion (SRMC) for the transfer of
aqueous waste from SWPF to the Saltstone Production Facility (SPF) where the waste will be treated and disposed in the
Saltstone Disposal Facility. This Technical Report compares results, where applicable, to SPF Waste Acceptance Criteria
(WAC) LIMITS and TARGETS that were established at the time the SWPF DSS sample was obtained. 1 The April 2026
Semiannual sample of the SWPF DSS is a composite from three months of SWPF processing during the First Quarter Fiscal
Year 2026 (1QFY2026) and three months of SWPF processing during the Second Quarter Fiscal Year 2026 (2QFY2026).

The following facts pertaining to the WAC are drawn from the analytical results provided in this report.

 • WAC TARGETS and LIMITS were met for all analyzed chemical and radioactive contaminants for which the
 detection limits are below the WAC TARGETS and LIMITS.1

 • Nitrosamines were not detected in the SWPF DSS salt solution sample above the instrument detection limits of
 <1 mg/L.

 • Comparison of analyzed concentrations to WAC LIMITS and TARGETS: Free hydroxide, nitrate and nitrite anions
 and total mercury are the highest analytes relative to the WAC LIMITS at 32%, 29%, 15% and 11%, respectively
 (Table 3-1). Aluminum is the highest analyte relative to the WAC TARGET at 91%, with average TOC at a lower
 ratio to WAC TARGET of 12% (Table 3-2). Radionuclides Tc-99 and I-129 are the highest analytes relative to the
 WAC LIMITS at 47% and 76%, respectively (Table 3-3). None of the radionuclides shown in Table 3-4 are higher
 than 7% of the WAC TARGETS. Average sodium concentration of 6.12M (Table 3-7) is 87% of the upper WAC
 LIMIT for processability.

1.0 Introduction
Salt Waste Processing Facility (SWPF) Decontaminated Salt Solution (DSS) aqueous waste is analyzed on a
semiannual basis and the results are compared to the Waste Acceptance Criteria (WAC) of the Z-Area Saltstone
Production Facility (SPF).1 Information from this characterization will be used by SRMC for the transfer of aqueous
waste from the SWPF to the SPF where the waste will be treated and disposed in the Saltstone Disposal Facility.
This Technical Report compares results, where applicable, to SPF WAC LIMITS and TARGETS.1 The chemical and
radionuclide contaminant results from the characterization of the April 2026 semiannual sampling of the SWPF
DSS were originally requested by SRMC personnel via a Task Technical Request (TTR)2 that was issued in 2023.
Details of the testing are presented in the Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) Task Technical and Quality
Assurance Plan (TTQAP)3 that was issued in 2023. This Technical Report is part of Deliverable 2 relating to Task 1
from the SRMC request.2 Data pertaining to the regulatory limits for Resource Conservation and Recovery Act
(RCRA) metals per Task 2 from the SRMC request will be obtained semiannually in October and April for SWPF
composited DSS samples. The semiannual SWPF DSS WAC analysis is to occur in October (for the 3QFY and 4QFY
SWPF DSS samples) and April (for the 1QFY and 2QFY SWPF DSS samples) of each year.

2.0 Experimental
2.1 Technical
The April 2026 semiannual SWPF DSS overall composite sample is comprised of SWPF DSS quarterly composite
sample mixtures from the First Quarter Fiscal Year 2026 (1QFY2026) and the Second Quarter Fiscal Year 2026
(2QFY2026). These quarterly SWPF DSS composites are composited across SWPF micro-batches. The 1QFY2026
SWPF DSS quarterly composite samples (three 500-mL near-zero headspace aliquots in a Teflon® bottle and a
single 60-mL near-zero headspace aliquot in a glass bottle) were received from SWPF at SRNL on January 26,
2026.4 The 2QFY2026 SWPF DSS quarterly composite samples (two 500-mL near-zero headspace aliquots in
Teflon® bottles) were received from SWPF at SRNL on April 30, 2026.4 The 60-mL sample was obtained for volatile
organic analysis (VOA) and semi-volatile organic analysis (SVOA). A duplicate empty 60-mL amber bottle was also
transferred from SWPF to SRNL. This empty bottle was filled with deionized water at SRNL to serve as the
VOA/SVOA control blank. These quarterly SWPF DSS composites and the VOA/SVOA sample from SWPF were
stored upon receipt in a refrigerator to maintain sample temperatures less than 5 °C. The quarterly DSS composite
samples (500 mL of 1QFY26 and 500 mL of 2QFY26) were blended at SRNL in a radiochemical hood to give an
overall 1-L composite sample representing processing during the 1QFY2026 and 2QFY2026 per SRMC guidance. 5
Detailed information obtained from SWPF personnel for each quarterly composite is shown in Table 2-1. The
overall SRNL composite as well as the single VOA/SVOA sample and blank were submitted for analysis on April 30,
2026.

Immediately after the initial 1-L SRNL composite was prepared, 30-mL aliquots and 15-mL aliquots of the
composite sample were poured into Teflon® and glass containers, respectively, with zero headspace. These two
sets of triplicate samples were used for Hg speciation measurements. The zero headspace vials for Hg speciation
testing were put in shrouded containers to minimize exposure to light and transferred to storage in a refrigerator
until removed for analysis. 6 Aliquots of the SRNL 1-L composite were promptly collected with slurry pipettes and
placed into high-density polyethylene (HDPE) bottles for all other analyses.

Unless otherwise stated, all concentrations presented in the tables (except upper limits) are averages based on
analyses of triplicate aliquots of the April 2026 semiannual SWPF DSS 1-L composite sample prepared at SRNL.
The 1-sigma standard deviation of each average is also presented. Several of the contaminants were either not
detected in the samples or detected at values below the method reporting limit (MRL). For contaminants not
detected or detected below the MRL, the result is preceded by a “<”, which indicates the result is an upper limit
based on the sensitivity of the method used to analyze the individual analyte. Standard deviations are indicated
as Not Applicable (NA) for these contaminants not detected or detected at values below the MRL. If only one
value out of the triplicate analysis is above the detection limit, then that single value is reported and noted in the
tables. Also, if only two values out of the triplicate analyses are above the detection limit, then the average of
those two values is reported and noted in the tables.

All VOA, SVOA and nitrosamines analyses were performed on the 60-mL VOA/SVOA sample and blank, and all
other analyses were performed on the 1-L composite sample. The VOA method is performed per SRNL Nuclear &

 5

Chemical Processing (N&CP) Procedure L16.1, ADS-2656. 7 This method is based upon a purge-and-trap, Gas
Chromatographic/Mass Spectrometric (GC/MS) process that involves dilution of 1 mL of SWPF DSS supernate with
4 mL of water derived from a Millipore instrument (Milli-Q® Advantage A10/A10® Total Organic Carbon (TOC)
Monitor). The SVOA method is performed per SRNL N&CP Procedure L16.1, ADS-2657.8 Both of these methods
use discrete standards as detailed in the procedures.7,8 The SVOA method uses organic solvents to extract SVOA
analytes that are subsequently measured by GC/MS. A 3-mL dichloromethane (also known as methylene chloride,
CH2Cl2) volume is used to initially extract 10 mL of SWPF DSS supernate for phenol. The same SWPF DSS supernate
is then extracted with 2 additional 3-mL volumes of dichloromethane. The dichloromethane extracts are all
combined and concentrated to 1 mL before analysis for phenol. Tributyl phosphate is analyzed from a 0.01 mL
hexane (C6H14) extract of 10 mL of SWPF DSS supernate. Isopar La and Norpar 13 are analyzed from a 2.5 mL
hexane extraction of 10 mL of SWPF DSS supernate. Nitrosamines are analyzed by a separate SVOA method that
uses 2 mL of dichloromethane as extractant and 10 mL of SWPF DSS supernate with deuterated N-
nitrosodimethylamine-d6 (NDMA-d6) as a standard along with a separate GC/MS analysis methodology.

Data reported for inductively coupled plasma atomic emission spectroscopy (ICP-ES) and inductively coupled
plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) are derived from the digested SWPF DSS supernate (1 mL supernate diluted
to 50 mL total volume) by the aqua regia method. 9 The aqua regia method heats the SWPF DSS supernate mixed
with a 1:3 mixture of nitric acid/hydrochloric acid for 2 hours in sealed Teflon® containers in an oven at 115 ± 5
ºC. The heating step is performed with an oven under temperature control with a Measurement & Testing
Equipment (M&TE) controller with a defined temperature set point shutdown system. A blank using water derived
from the Millipore instrument described above is also prepared for the aqua regia method. Anion and the
ammonium cation analyses are determined from Ion Chromatography (IC). Total Inorganic Carbon/Total Organic
Carbon (TIC/TOC) analysis was used to measure the TIC (carbonate) and TOC components from filtered supernate
derived using a 0.45-micron nylon filter disposable plastic filter cup. TIC/TOC analyses were also performed on
the SWPF DSS supernate for comparison of results to the filtrate. The tetraphenylborate (TPB) anion and
ethylenediaminetetraacetate (EDTA) were analyzed using High Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC). The
IC, TIC/TOC and HPLC methods all used undiluted samples. All the above analyses, excluding VOA and SVOA, used
approximately 150 mL of the 1-L composite sample. Densities were measured on triplicate samples of the SWPF
DSS using an Anton Parr DMA 35 portable density meter. Total and soluble weight percent solids were determined
on quadruplicate portions of the SWPF DSS sample, without filtration and with filtration (using a 0.45-micron nylon
filter disposable plastic filter cup), respectively, using the “Weight Percent Solids Determination Using a Furnace
or Oven” procedure. 10 Approximately 630 mL of the 1-L composite was used to determine all the measured
radionuclide concentrations in triplicate. Radionuclides reported using the ICP-MS method are converted from a
reported mass per volume basis to activity per volume units using the specific activities (Ci/g) reported from the
Department of Energy 1996 Integrated Data Base Report.11 The Cs-137 and Cs-134 radionuclides are determined
from gamma spectroscopy. Total beta is measured from a radscreen method using Liquid Scintillation Counting
(LSC). The total alpha is measured from the same radscreen method after removal of Cs-137 from the sample
using ammonium phosphomolybdate. No other radiochemical species in the matrix are removed. This
methodology for measuring total alpha concentrations typically results in upper limits rather than minimum
detectable activities when other peaks from competing radionuclides, e.g., Sr-90, in the matrix prevent detection
of the alpha emitters. Plutonium isotopes (Pu-238, Pu-239 and Pu-240) are determined from a Plutonium alpha
Pulse Height Analysis (Pu alpha PHA) method.

a
  Isopar L is a trademark chemical (Isopar™ L) manufactured by ExxonMobil. It is a synthetic isoparaffinic hydrocarbon that
is manufactured from a petroleum based raw material.

Mercury analyses included Total mercury using the Direct Mercury Analyzer (DMA) method12 and monomethyl
mercury and ethyl mercury by Cold Vapor Atomic Fluorescence Spectroscopy (CVAFS).13 Soluble elemental
mercury (Hg(0)) was measured using a Purge & Trap, Thermal Desorption, CVAFS (P&T-TD-CVAFS) method. 14 A
variation of this method was also used to analyze for inorganic mercury (Hg(I/II)). 15 Dimethyl mercury was
analyzed using a semi-quantitative VOA method that involves extraction followed by GC/MS.7 The parent sample
for all mercury analyses performed at SRNL was obtained from the original SWPF DSS sample within two days of
sample receipt. As discussed above, the parent SWPF DSS samples for mercury analyses were obtained in near
zero-headspace containers that were shrouded to minimize exposure to light and immediately refrigerated after
preparation from the 1-L composite on the same day of preparation.6 Total mercury, monomethyl and ethyl
mercury are determined from the SWPF DSS parent sample obtained in the 30-mL Teflon® bottle. All other species
are determined from the 15-mL SWPF DSS parent stored in the glass bottle. Samples of SWPF for mercury
speciation analysis were submitted without dilution. These samples were diluted to meet the targeted calibration
range of either the DMA instrument for total Hg or the CVAFS instrument for other Hg species and for VOA analysis
for dimethyl mercury.

3.0 Results and Discussion
Analyzed nonradionuclide chemical average concentrations, their 1-sigma standard deviations and their
corresponding WAC LIMITS are shown in Table 3-1 that correspond to the Attachment 8.1 LIMITS in the WAC.1
Per the WAC, the LIMITS shown shall not be exceeded accounting for the analytical uncertainty in each measured
concentration.1 Analyzed nonradionuclide chemical average concentrations, their standard deviations and their
corresponding WAC TARGETS are shown in Table 3-2 that correspond to the Attachment 8.2 TARGETS in the WAC.1
Per the WAC, the TARGETS shown shall not be exceeded accounting for the analytical uncertainty in each
measured concentration.1 The LIMITS refer to a type of acceptance criteria that, if not satisfied, will have an
adverse impact on repository requirements, whereas the TARGETS refer to a type of acceptance criteria that is set
as a guideline to protect a LIMIT.1 For the chemical contaminants and the radionuclides given in tables below, an
analytical uncertainty of 2 sigma (2σ) shall be accounted for in sample analyses used to determine the analytical
uncertainty vs. either the LIMIT or TARGET.1 The standard deviations given in tables for this WAC report are taken
as 1 sigma (1σ) values that are calculated from the normal ‘standard deviation’ function for either duplicate or
triplicate values from within Excel® spreadsheets. The 1σ analytical (instrument) uncertainties for the methods
indicated in Table 3-1 and Table 3-2 are 5% for ICP-ES, 10% for IC, ICP-MS and DMA Total Mercury. The 1σ
analytical (instrument) uncertainties are 10% for the elemental mercury, ionic mercury, monomethyl mercury,
ethyl mercury and dimethyl mercury. The 1σ analytical (instrument) uncertainty for VOA/SVOA and free
hydroxide is 10% and 7% for TIC/TOC.

Comparison of the average analyzed detectable values shown in Table 3-1 to the WAC LIMITS indicates that free
hydroxide, nitrate and nitrite anions and total mercury are the highest analytes relative to the WAC LIMITS at 32%,
29%, 15% and 11%, respectively. These measured concentrations relative to the WAC LIMITS are similar to past
semiannual reports for free hydroxide, nitrate and nitrite. Average concentrations reported for the SWPF DSS
filtrate for carbonate and TOC in Table 3-1 are within 5% of the respective values also measured for the SWPF DSS
supernate. The current total mercury average concentration (35.2 mg/L) is similar to the previous two semiannual
SWPF DSS samples in October of 2025 (37.1 mg/L)18 and October of 2024 (48.2 mg/L).19 A lower total mercury
average concentration (17.9 mg/L) was reported for the April 2025 semiannual SWPF DSS sample.20 Summation
of all the average concentrations of detectable mercury species shown in Table 3-1 and Table 3-2 (mg Hg/L)
compared to the average total mercury concentration indicates ~ 108% recovery. Comparison of the average
analyzed values shown in Table 3-2 to the WAC TARGETS indicates that aluminum is the highest analyte relative
to the WAC TARGET at 91%, with average TOC at a lower ratio to WAC TARGET of 12%. No detectable VOA
analytes (butanol, propanol, benzene and toluene) were detected above the indicated method detection limits
from duplicate analyses as shown in Table 3-1 and Table 3-2.

Analyzed radionuclide average concentrations and the respective radiochemical analysis methods, their standard
deviations and their corresponding WAC LIMITS and TARGETS are shown in Table 3-3 and Table 3-4, respectively.
The 1σ analytical (instrument) uncertainties for the methods indicated in and Table 3-3 and Table 3-4 are in the
range of 5% to 20% and are sample specific. Some of the 1σ analytical (instrument) uncertainties were higher for
the U-232 Alpha PHA method (a range for the triplicate samples from 24% to 55%), the Se-79 beta liquid
scintillation method (a range for the triplicate samples from 17% to 22%), the Pu-239/240 Pu Alpha PHA method
(a range of 29% to 35%) and the Am-242 Am/Cm Counting method (43%). Comparison of the average analyzed
detectable values shown in Table 3-3 to the WAC LIMITS indicates that Tc-99 and I-129 are the highest analytes
relative to the WAC LIMITS at 47% and 76%, respectively Comparison of the average analyzed detectable values

4.0 Conclusions
The following conclusions pertaining to the WAC are drawn from the analytical results provided in this report.

 • WAC TARGETS and LIMITS were met for all analyzed chemical and radioactive contaminants for which
 the detection limits are below the WAC TARGETS and LIMITS.1

 • Nitrosamines were not detected in the SWPF DSS salt solution sample above the instrument detection
 limits of <1 mg/L.

 • Comparison of analyzed concentrations to WAC LIMITS and TARGETS: Free hydroxide, nitrate and nitrite
 anions and total mercury are the highest analytes relative to the WAC LIMITS at 32%, 29%, 15% and
 11%, respectively (Table 3-1). Aluminum is the highest analyte relative to the WAC TARGET at 91%, with
 average TOC at a lower ratio to WAC TARGET of 12% (Table 3-2). Radionuclides Tc-99 and I-129 are the
 highest analytes relative to the WAC LIMITS at 47% and 76%, respectively (Table 3-3). None of the
 radionuclides shown in Table 3-4 are higher than 7% of the WAC TARGETS. Average sodium
 concentration of 6.12M (Table 3-7) is 87% of the upper WAC LIMIT for processability.
