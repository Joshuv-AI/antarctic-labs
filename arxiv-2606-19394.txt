On Epimorphisms of Hypergraphic Automata and Input Symbol Semigroups Thanks: Corresponding author. J. Hamoud (hamoud.math@gmail.com)
J. Hamoud

Abstract
. Hypergraphic automata are automata whose state sets and output symbol sets are hypergraphs invariant under the actions of the transition and output functions. Universally attracting objects in the category of such automata are called universal hypergraphic automata; their semigroups of input symbols are algebras of mappings whose properties are tightly linked to the algebraic structure of the automata themselves. This paper establishes a complete characterisation of epimorphisms of universal hypergraphic automata and of their semigroups of input symbols. A central contribution is the introduction of two distinct notions of epimorphism for hypergraphs including weak, strong and the proof that these notions diverge in general but necessarily coincide for the important subclass of p ∗ p^{*} -hypergraphs, which includes automata whose state hypergraphs and output hypergraphs are projective or affine planes. The main results give necessary and sufficient conditions for a triple ( f , ℙ s , g ) (f,\mathbb{P}_{s},g) to be an epimorphism of universal hypergraphic automata, expressed in terms of the component maps on the state and output hypergraphs.

2.1.
Problem Statement

The present paper addresses the following natural problem: describe completely the
structure of epimorphisms of universal hypergraphic automata
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X},H_{Y})

and of their semigroups of input symbols
S(H_{X},H_{Y})
, for effective hypergraphs
with
p
-definable edges. More precisely, given two such automata

\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X},H_{Y})
and
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y_{1}})
and mappings

f:X\to X_{1}
,
g:Y\to Y_{1}
, we seek necessary and sufficient conditions on

f
and
g
under which the induced triple
\gamma=(f,\mathbb{P}_{s},g)
is an
epimorphism of automata, and correspondingly under which
\mathbb{P}_{s}=(f^{2},f\times g)

is an epimorphism of their input symbol semigroups. The problem is non-trivial
because, unlike the monomorphism case, the epimorphism condition on the semigroup
level does not impose a single uniform requirement on the output map
g
: as we
show, the precise condition depends on the geometric structure of the hypergraphs
involved, leading naturally to a distinction between two notions of epimorphism
for hypergraphs that we call weak and strong.
The present paper
continues this programme by studying
epimorphisms
.

A new and distinctive feature of the epimorphism problem is that it
necessitates a careful distinction between two notions of epimorphism for
hypergraphs.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{s}(\varphi,\psi)\,\mathbb{P}_{s}(\varphi_{1},\psi_{1})

\displaystyle=\bigl(f^{2}(\varphi),\,(f\times g)(\psi)\bigr)\bigl(f^{2}(\varphi_{1}),\,(f\times g)(\psi_{1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\bigl(f^{2}(\varphi)\,f^{2}(\varphi_{1}),\;f^{2}(\varphi)\,(f\times g)(\psi_{1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\bigl(f\circ\varphi\varphi_{1}\circ f^{-1},\;g\circ\varphi\psi_{1}\circ f^{-1}\bigr)

For all

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

,
H_{Y}=(Y,L_{Y})
,
H_{X_{1}}=(X_{1},L_{X_{1}})
, and

H_{Y_{1}}=(Y_{1},L_{Y_{1}})
be effective hypergraphs with
p
-definable edges.
Let
f\colon X\to X_{1}
be a surjection such that there exists a set

V=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{p+1}\}\subseteq X
in which all
p
-element
subsets are bounded in
H_{X}
but
f(V)=\{f(v_{1}),\ldots,f(v_{p+1})\}

is an unbounded set in
H_{X_{1}}
. Let
g\colon Y\to Y_{1}
be a mapping. Then
the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mathbb{P}_{s}=(f^{2},f\times g)
is an epimorphism from
S(H_{X},H_{Y})
to

S(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y_{1}})
;

(2)

f
is an isomorphism from
H_{X}
to
H_{X_{1}}
and
g
is a weak
epimorphism from
H_{Y}
to
H_{Y_{1}}
;

(3)

\gamma=(f,\mathbb{P}_{s},g)
is an epimorphism from
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X},H_{Y})
to

\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y_{1}})
.

Proof.

According to Lemma
3.1
, we emphsize that Condition 2 implies both Conditions 1
and 3. Thus, we show that Condition 3 implies Condition 1. It remains
to show that Condition 1 implies Condition 2.
Assume Condition 1 satisfy
\mathbb{P}_{s}=(f^{2},f\times g)
is a surjective semigroup
homomorphism.

Case 1:
g
is surjective (weak epimorphism condition).
In this case, consider any
b_{1}\in Y_{1}
. By Lemma
2.1
, for any

a_{1}\in X_{1}
the pair
(C_{a_{1}},C_{b_{1}})
is a right zero of

S(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y_{1}})
. By surjectivity of
\mathbb{P}_{s}
, there exists

(\varphi,\psi)\in S(H_{X},H_{Y})
with
\mathbb{P}_{s}(\varphi,\psi)=(C_{a_{1}},C_{b_{1}})
.
Then
(f\times g)(\psi)=C_{b_{1}}
, meaning

for
all

. In particular,
b_{1}\in g(Y)
. Since
b_{1}
was arbitrary,

, hence
g
is surjective.

Case 2:
f
is a homomorphism.
In this case, we consider any edge

. Any mapping
\varphi\colon X\to l
is an
endomorphism of
H_{X}
. For any
\psi\in\mathrm{Hom}(H_{X},H_{Y})
, the pair

(\varphi,\psi)\in S(H_{X},H_{Y})
, and
\mathbb{P}_{s}(\varphi,\psi)\in S(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y_{1}})
.
By Condition 1,
\mathbb{P}_{s}(\varphi,\psi)=(f^{2}(\varphi),(f\times g)(\psi))
, so

\chi=f^{2}(\varphi)
is an endomorphism of

. For any

. Thus

is a
bounded set in
H_{X_{1}}
, so there exists
r\in L_{X_{1}}
with

f(l)\subseteq r
. Since

was arbitrary,
f
is a homomorphism.
Since
f
is a surjection by hypothesis,
f
is an epimorphism of hypergraphs. Therefore, by an analogous argument using
(f\times g)(\psi)
,
g
is a homomorphism.

Case 3:
f
is injective.
Assume
f(a)=f(b)
where

. Since
H_{X}
is effective, both

and
C_{b}
are endomorphisms of
H_{X}
. By Lemma

,
for some

(C_{a},C_{d})
and
(C_{b},C_{d})
are right zeros of

. Then:

Since
\mathbb{P}_{s}
is a homomorphism of semigroups, right zeros map to right zeros.
By Lemma
2.1
applied to

, the
image must be a pair of constant maps. Since

implies

, we get
\mathbb{P}_{s}(C_{a},C_{d})=\mathbb{P}_{s}(C_{b},C_{d})
.
But
\mathbb{P}_{s}
sends distinct right zeros to distinct right zeros (it is injective
on this subset, as each right zero
(C_{a},C_{d})
determines
a
uniquely
through the first component). Hence
C_{a}=C_{b}
, so
a=b
. Thus
f
is
injective, and since it is surjective by hypothesis,
f
is a bijection.

f^{-1}
is a homomorphism (so
f
is an isomorphism).

such that
no edge

satisfies

. By Definition
3
,

l
contains at least
p+1
distinct vertices

. Since

f
is a bijection,
H_{X}
contains
p+1
distinct vertices

with

H_{X}
contains
all of them.

By hypothesis, there exists

whose

p
-element subsets are all bounded in
H_{X}
. We consider two cases.

(1)

All

are unbounded in

.

For any edge

, by assumption no

-element subset of
f^{-1}(l)

lies in

, meaning
\varphi(r)
is a
p
-element

. By hypothesis, all

Hence

. Then

(2)

Some

\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{p}\}\subseteq r

.

Let

,
H_{X_{1}}
be projective or affine planes, viewed as

,

is an epimorphism from

to

(2)

to

and

is a strong epimorphism from

to

;

(3)

is a strong epimorphism from
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X},H_{Y})

to
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y_{1}})
.

Proof.

Any projective or affine plane with more than four points is a

(see

). An isomorphism of

4.
Applications

In this section, we derive four consequences of
Theorem
3.3
.
The first result reveals a fundamental rigidity property of universal
hypergraphic automata under epimorphisms. The second and third apply the
characterisation to automata whose hypergraphs are classical geometric
structures. The fourth places our results in the broader categorical
framework of epi-mono factorisations.
Thus, all hypergraphs are assumed to be effective with

p
-definable edges unless otherwise stated.

According to Theorem
3.3
it follows that the state
hypergraph of a universal hypergraphic automaton cannot be non-trivially
compressed by any epimorphism. Thus, the state component of every epimorphism must
be an isomorphism. We call among Proposition
4.1
this property
state rigidity
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
H_{X},H_{Y},H_{X_{1}},H_{Y_{1}}
be effective hypergraphs with
p
-definable
edges, and let
\gamma=(f,\mathbb{P}_{s},g)
be an epimorphism from
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X},H_{Y})

to
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y_{1}})
. Then,

(1)

f\colon H_{X}\to H_{X_{1}}
is an isomorphism; in particular,

|X|=|X_{1}|
and
|L_{X}|=|L_{X_{1}}|
.

(2)

The only compression permitted by any epimorphism occurs on the
output hypergraph where
g\colon H_{Y}\twoheadrightarrow H_{Y_{1}}
is a
surjective hypergraph homomorphism with
|Y_{1}|\leq|Y|
and

|L_{Y_{1}}|\leq|L_{Y}|
.

(3)

The image automaton
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y_{1}})
is completely
determined, up to isomorphism of its state hypergraph, by the kernel
partition of
g
on the output hypergraph.

Proof.

Recall Theorem
3.3
, then Statement 1 follows immediately
Condition 2 as every epimorphism
\gamma=(f,\mathbb{P}_{s},g)
requires
f
to be an
isomorphism
H_{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}H_{X_{1}}
. Then, we noticed that an isomorphism preserves both
the vertex set cardinality and the edge family cardinality, giving

|X|=|X_{1}|
and
|L_{X}|=|L_{X_{1}}|
.

Thus, for Statement 2, by establishing Theorem
3.3
it requires
g
to be a weak epimorphism, hence a surjective hypergraph homomorphism. Then, surjectivity gives

g(Y)=Y_{1}
, and
|Y_{1}|\leq|Y|
. Since
g
is a homomorphism, every edge

l_{1}\in L_{Y_{1}}
satisfies
l_{1}\supseteq g(l)
for some
l\in L_{Y}
,
so
|L_{Y_{1}}|\leq|L_{Y}|
.

Finally, assume two vertices
y,y^{\prime}\in Y
are identified in
H_{Y_{1}}

precisely when
g(y)=g(y^{\prime})
, and two edges
l,l^{\prime}\in L_{Y}
merge when

g(l)=g(l^{\prime})
. Then, the kernel congruences
\ker_{V}(g)
on
Y
and
\ker_{E}(g)

on
L_{Y}
completely determine the combinatorial structure of
H_{Y_{1}}=H_{Y}/(\ker_{V}(g),\ker_{E}(g))
. Since
f
is an isomorphism (Statement 1),
the state component of
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y_{1}})
is determined up to
isomorphism by
H_{X}
alone. Hence
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y_{1}})
is determined,
up to isomorphism of its state hypergraph, by the kernel partition of
g
which Statement 3 holds.
∎

Proposition
4.1
has a natural interpretation: in universal
hypergraphic automata, the
state structure is preserved
by any
surjective morphism, while the
output structure may be compressed
. This
contrasts sharply with the situation for general (non-universal) automata,
where epimorphisms may identify states. The rigidity of the state component is
therefore a distinctive feature of the universality of
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X},H_{Y})
.

We now apply Corollary

to obtain a concrete group-theoretic
description of all epimorphisms between automata whose state hypergraphs are
classical finite planes.

[

]
. Similarly, the

has

.

Let

\mathcal{L}\subseteq Y^{*}

is
recognisable
by
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X},H_{Y})
if there exists a state

x_{0}\in X
and a set
F\subseteq Y
of accepting output symbols such that

\mathcal{L}=\{w\in S^{*}:\lambda^{\prime}(x_{0},w)\in F\}
. A direct consequence
of Theorem
3.3
is the following language-preservation
property: if
\mathcal{L}
is recognisable by
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X},H_{Y})
via the
accepting set
F\subseteq Y
, and
\gamma=(f,\mathbb{P}_{s},g)
is an epimorphism
onto
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y_{1}})
, then the image language
g(\mathcal{L})=\{g(y):y\in\mathcal{L}\}
is recognisable by
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y_{1}})

via the accepting set
g(F)\subseteq Y_{1}
. Since
g
is surjective,
every recognisable language of
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y_{1}})
arises in this way,
so epimorphisms provide a complete and exact transfer of recognisable languages
between universal hypergraphic automata.

We place our results in a categorical context. Let
\mathbf{HAtm}
denote the
category whose objects are universal hypergraphic automata
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X},H_{Y})

over effective hypergraphs with
p
-definable edges, and whose morphisms are
triples
\gamma=(f,\mathbb{P}_{s},g)
as defined in Section
3
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Every morphism
\gamma=(f,\mathbb{P}_{s},g)
in
\mathbf{HAtm}
admits a
factorisation

\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X},H_{Y})\;\xrightarrow{\;\varepsilon\;}\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y^{\prime}})\;\xrightarrow{\;\mu\;}\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y_{1}}),

where
\varepsilon=(f,\mathbb{P}_{s}^{\prime},g^{\prime})
is an epimorphism and

\mu=(\mathrm{id}_{X_{1}},\mathbb{P}_{s}^{\prime\prime},h)
is a monomorphism.

Proof.

Assume that the
image hypergraph

H_{Y^{\prime}}=(Y^{\prime},L_{Y^{\prime}})
where

Y^{\prime}=g(Y)
and
L_{Y^{\prime}}=\{g(l):l\in L_{Y}\}
.
Since
g
is a homomorphism,
H_{Y^{\prime}}
is a well-defined hypergraph with

p
-definable edges.
Consider
g^{\prime}\colon H_{Y}\to H_{Y^{\prime}}
by
g^{\prime}(y)=g(y)
where
g^{\prime}
is surjective on
vertices by construction, and maps every edge
l\in L_{Y}
to
g(l)\in L_{Y^{\prime}}

by definition of
L_{Y^{\prime}}
; hence
g^{\prime}
is a strong epimorphism. According to
Theorem
3.3
, with the induced
\mathbb{P}_{s}^{\prime}
, the triple

\varepsilon=(f,\mathbb{P}_{s}^{\prime},g^{\prime})
is an epimorphism from
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X},H_{Y})
to

\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X_{1}},H_{Y^{\prime}})
.

Thus, assume that
h\colon H_{Y^{\prime}}\to H_{Y_{1}}
by
h(g(y))=g(y)
. Then
h
is injective and maps edges of
H_{Y^{\prime}}
into bounded
sets of
H_{Y_{1}}
, so
h
is a monomorphism of hypergraphs. According to the
monomorphism characterisation
[
13
]
, with
\mathrm{id}_{X_{1}}

an isomorphism and
h
a monomorphism, the triple

\mu=(\mathrm{id}_{X_{1}},\mathbb{P}_{s}^{\prime\prime},h)
is a monomorphism in
\mathbf{HAtm}
.
Since
\mu\circ\varepsilon=\gamma
by construction, and any two such
factorisations are connected by a unique isomorphism of the intermediate
object (by the standard argument for orthogonal epi-mono systems), the
factorisation is unique up to isomorphism.
∎

Actually, based on Proposition
4.4
shows that the category
\mathbf{HAtm}

admits an
orthogonal epi-mono factorisation system
.
The explicit description of the intermediate object as the image hypergraph
automaton
\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X_{1}},g(H_{Y}))
is made possible precisely because

3.3
and
[
13
]
together give a
complete algebraic description of both epimorphisms and monomorphisms in

\mathbf{HAtm}
.

Conclusion

This paper characterises epimorphisms between universal hypergraphic automata
\mathrm{Atm}
and their input symbol semigroups
S(H_{X},H_{Y})
for effective hypergraphs with
p
-definable edges.
The main contribution is the introduction of weak and strong epimorphism for hypergraphs, which are different in general but must coincide for
p
-hypergraphs, projective and affine planes.
The main results
give necessary and sufficient conditions for a triple
\gamma=(f,\mathbb{P}_{s},g)

to be an epimorphism of automata entirely in terms of the component maps
f

and
g
where
f
must always be an isomorphism of the state hypergraph (state
rigidity), while
g
must be a weak or strong epimorphism of the output
hypergraph depending on the geometric structure of the hypergraphs involved.
These findings, together with previous studies on isomorphisms
[
12
]
and monomorphisms
[
13
]
, provide a comprehensive description of structure-preserving mappings between universal hypergraphic automata. The applications in Section
4
show that the results have multiple implications, including the state-rigidity of universal hypergraphic automata, the group-theoretic description of epimorphisms over projective and affine planes (parameterised by
P\Gamma L(3,q)
and
A\Gamma L(2,q)
), the exact transfer of recognisable languages under epimorphisms, and the existence of an orthogonal epi-mono factorisation system in the category
\mathbf{HAtm}
.

Several directions remain open and are natural continuations of the present
work. First, the structure of
congruences
on
S(H_{X},H_{Y})
and the
corresponding quotient automata have not been studied; this would require
understanding which equivalence relations on
\mathrm{End}H_{X}\times\mathrm{Hom}(H_{X},H_{Y})

are compatible with the semigroup multiplication, a problem expected to be
considerably more involved than the epimorphism problem. The endomorphism monoid
\mathrm{End}H
of an effective hypergraph with
p
-definable edges, including Green’s relations, idempotents, and rank function, is mainly unexplored and of independent importance. The functor
(H_{X},H_{Y})\mapsto\mathrm{Atm}(H_{X},H_{Y})
and its preservation of limits or colimits in the category of hypergraphs remains an open categorical subject.
Extending the current conclusions to partial or non-effective hypergraphs would greatly expand the theory’s scope.
