A multi-strategy integrated improved nutcracker optimization algorithm and its applications in water conservancy engineering
Authors: Jin Sun, Guang Yang, Dongmei Ma, Xiheng He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryTo enhance the performance of nutcracker optimization algorithm (NOA) for complex optimization problems in water conservancy engineering, an improvement framework is investigated. The performances of the selected 26 meta-heuristic algorithms are analyzed using benchmark functions. Targeting the limitations of NOA, the improved NOA (INOA) is constructed by incorporating logistic-tent chaotic mapping, tangent flight strategy, dynamic fitness-distance balance selection, criss-cross mutation, Gaussian mutation perturbation, and Tent chaotic perturbation. INOA is adopted to optimize attention mechanism long short-term memory neural network (ATLSTM) for broken wire signal identification of prestressed concrete cylinder pipe (PCCP) and to optimize the hydraulic-seasonal-time (HST) model for dam deformation prediction. The results indicate that the NOA exhibits optimal overall performance among the selected algorithms and that the INOA improves optimization accuracy, convergence speed, stability, and operational efficiency. In both engineering applications, INOA outperforms other baseline optimizers, demonstrating its potential for intelligent monitoring and prediction in water conservancy engineering.Keywords: NOA, multi-strategy integration, performance testing, abnormal acoustic signal identification, dam deformation prediction

Introduction

Meta-heuristic algorithms, by simulating evolution mechanisms, physical phenomena, human activities, and biological group behaviors in nature, offer efficient and feasible methods for solving complex optimization problems,1,2,3,4,5 e.g., ant colony optimization (ACO),6 particle swarm optimization (PSO),7 artificial bee colony (ABC),8 gray wolf optimization (GWO),9 whale optimization algorithm (WOA),10 and sparrow search algorithm (SSA).11 In the field of water conservancy engineering, numerous optimization problems require efficient and robust optimization algorithms with outstanding global search performance, convergence accuracy, and stability, such as dam safety monitoring,12 runoff prediction,13 pattern recognition,14 physical and mechanical parameter inversion,15 model parameter optimization,16,17 and effective feature mining.18 Accordingly, developing scientific meta-heuristic algorithms to provide reliable computational support for complex optimization problems is of great practical significance.19,20

Nutcracker optimization algorithm (NOA),21 proposed by Mohammed Abdel-Basset et al. in 2023, is inspired by the seasonal behaviors of Clark’s nutcrackers. During summer and autumn, nutcrackers engage in active foraging and adopt a decentralized storage strategy to preserve the food. In winter and spring, relying on their spatial memory, nutcrackers take environmental markers as reference points to accurately retrieve the cached food. By simulating the storage-retrieving behavior, the NOA consists of two core optimization strategies, i.e., foraging and storage strategy (phase 1) and cache-search and retrieving strategy (phase 2). During the implementation process, these strategies are further divided into the foraging stage, storage stage, cache-search stage, and retrieving stage. The framework of NOA is illustrated in Figure 1. NOA dynamically selects an adaptive optimization strategy through random number generation mechanism, thus realizing the self-adaptive switching between exploration and exploitation. In phase 1, Lévy flight mechanism and random number generation model are introduced, effectively enhancing the global exploration capability and dynamic adjustment flexibility. In phase 2, by optimizing the population update strategy, local search accuracy and convergence efficiency are improved on the premise of maintaining population diversity.22,23,24 NOA demonstrates outstanding performance in terms of convergence speed, robustness, and optimization accuracy, and it has shown prominent application advantages in several practical scenarios.25,26,27,28,29 However, NOA still encounters issues when dealing with complex optimization problems.30 On the one hand, random generation strategy is adopted to produce initial population, which may result in the inherent inadequacy of population diversity. On the other hand, NOA lacks an effective mutation mechanism. As the iteration proceeds, nutcracker individuals tend to exhibit an aggregation trend, which may cause premature convergence.

Studies on the improvement approaches of meta-heuristic algorithms have primarily focused on four perspectives, i.e., optimizing initial population, enhancing local escape capability, improving convergence speed, and maintaining population diversity. To improve the quality of initial population, strategies such as backward learning,31 sine-plane mapping and reverse learning,32 piecewise compound one-dimensional chaotic mapping,33 Gaussian perturbation optimization,34 and Bernoulli mapping35 have been employed. For the enhancement of local escape capability, various strategies, e.g., group follow strategy,36 lens opposition-based learning,37 osprey global exploration strategy,38 adaptive somersault factor,39 and convergence improvement strategy,40 have been introduced, thereby expanding the search range of the algorithm. In terms of accelerating convergence speed, multiple strategies have been adopted, e.g., differential variance,41 pheromone of the black widow algorithm,42 phagocytosis mechanism,43 and normal cloud model.44 To maintain population diversity, methods such as inverted S-shaped escape energy,45 dual mutation operation,46 and Lévy flight47 can ensure strong global exploration capabilities in the late iterations. Progress has also been attained in the improvement of NOA. Zhu et al. employ the adaptive t-distribution perturbation to accelerate convergence and enhance local exploitation capability.48 Abdel-Basset et al. integrate the S-shaped and V-shaped transfer functions into the NOA to bridge the gap between the continuous nature of the classical NOA and the discrete nature of the binary problems.49 Dahou et al. improve NOA for feature selection by introducing tournament mechanism, enhancing the performance of false information detection.50 Evangeline et al. propose the surrogate-assisted NOA to improve computational efficiency.51

To improve the performance of NOA in addressing complex optimization problems in water conservancy engineering, targeted improvement strategies require in-depth investigation. The research is organized as follows. First, the performances of the selected 26 meta-heuristic algorithms are analyzed using benchmark functions. Second, specific strategies are incorporated into the NOA framework, thereby yielding the improved NOA (INOA). Third, two engineering applications are conducted to verify the feasibility of INOA. Finally, the main conclusions and future research directions are outlined.

Results

Performance analysis of NOA

The 26 typical meta-heuristic algorithms (Table 1) are selected for performance comparison. To ensure a comprehensive and fair comparison, the chosen algorithms cover classic algorithms and newly developed methods. Benchmark functions, serving as standardized test cases, are used to assess the efficiency, accuracy, and robustness of the selected optimization algorithms. The used benchmark functions are categorized into 3 types, i.e., unimodal functions, multimodal functions, and fixed-dimension multimodal functions. Specifically, unimodal functions are used to verify the convergence speed and optimization accuracy, including Sphere (F1), Schwefel 2.22 (F2), Schwefel 1.2 (F3), Schwefel 2.21 (F4), Rosenbrock (F5), Step (F6), and Quartic (F7). Multimodal functions are adopted to assess the global exploration capability and local optima escaping ability, covering Schwefel 2.26 (F8), Rastrigin (F9), Ackley (F10), Griewank (F11), Penalized (F12), and Penalized 2 (F13). Fixed-dimension multimodal functions are utilized to test the performance of meta-heuristic algorithms in identifying the global optimal solution among multiple local optima, consisting of Foxholes (F14), Kowalik (F15), Six Hump Camel (F16), Branin (F17), Goldstein Price (F18), Hartmann 3 (F19), Hartmann 6 (F20), Shekel 5 (F21), Shekel 7 (F22), and Shekel 10 (F23). To ensure the fairness, objectivity, and reproducibility of comparative experiments, all tests on benchmark functions are carried out under a unified hardware platform, software configuration, and operating parameters. After multiple trial runs, the maximum iteration number Tmax and population size N are fixed as 500 and 200, respectively. Each algorithm is set to run 50 times independently on each function to account for stochastic variations in algorithm performance. The optimization performances of the 26 algorithms are comprehensively evaluated by optimal value (Opt), standard deviation (SD), and average value (Ave). Detailed experimental results are given in Tables S1–S9, and the key findings are summarized as follows.

In the tests of unimodal functions, NOA, FLO, and OOA deliver comparable performance on F1–F4 and F6. These three algorithms can find global optimal or near-optimal solutions and exhibit better stability than other competitors. On F5, NOA converges to a local optimum and performs slightly worse than FLO and OOA, yet it outperforms the rest. For F7, the discrepancies across all compared algorithms are negligible, and all can reach global optimal or near-optimal solutions.

In the multimodal function tests, NOA, FLO, OOA, and DBO achieve similar results on F8–F12. These four algorithms obtain global optimal or near-optimal solutions with higher stability than other methods. On F13, NOA shows poorer stability than FLO, and the optimization performance is comparable to that of OOA and is superior to that of the remaining algorithms.

As for fixed-dimension multimodal functions, NOA, FLO, and OOA all locate the global optima on F14, F16–F19, and F21–F23, among which NOA presents the most robust stability. On F15, NOA yields the average value closest to the theoretical optimum along with the smallest standard deviation, demonstrating its superiority over other algorithms. For F20, NOA also outperforms all comparison methods.

Based on the above analysis, among the 26 meta-heuristic algorithms, NOA achieves the best comprehensive performance. However, it still has certain shortcomings, such as the tendency to get trapped in local optima and unsatisfactory stability under specific scenarios.

For the unimodal function tests, on F1–F4, both INOA and NOA successfully converge to the global optimal solution, with the mean values and the standard deviations of 0, while INOA exhibits a more prominent convergence speed. According to the above analysis, the following conclusions can be drawn: INOA significantly outperforms NOA in terms of optimization accuracy, convergence speed, stability, and operational efficiency. The ablation experiment results indicate that the introduced improvement strategies are independently effective and mutually complementary, and the algorithm performance is continuously improved with the progressive superposition of these strategies. Both NOA and INOA have a time complexity of O (T×N×dim) at the same level. The average CPU time of INOA is 10.71 s, which is 7.8% shorter than that of NOA (11.62 s). Meanwhile, under the same termination accuracy, the average iteration numbers of NOA and INOA are 378 and 284, respectively, representing a 24.87% reduction in average iterations. Therefore, the optimization capability of INOA is effectively enhanced compared with NOA.

Engineering application I

Research background

Long-distance water conveyance projects, such as South-to-North Water Diversion Project, are crucial for optimizing water resource allocation and ensuring water security. Prestressed concrete cylinder pipe (PCCP), with its excellent tightness, strength and impermeability, serves as a widely applied pipeline material in these projects, as presented in Figure 4. The bearing capacity is primarily dependent on prestressed steel wires; nevertheless, these wires are prone to fracture under the combined influence of corrosive environment conditions and hydrogen embrittlement. Once the number of broken wires reaches a certain threshold, there exists a high risk of pipe burst. Among various monitoring technologies, distributed optical fiber (DOF) sensing is an effective approach for the real-time monitoring of broken wires.52 During the water conveyance process, multiple excitation factors can induce abnormal vibrations in the sensing optical fibers. In practice, the performance of abnormal acoustic signal identification model is closely related to the hyperparameters. Improper parameter settings can lead to low identification accuracy or high false alarm rates.53,54,55 To effectively capture the broken wire signals, it is necessary to establish scientific hyperparameter estimation methods to enhance the applicability of abnormal acoustic signal identification model under complex operating conditions.

A PCCP water transmission project under construction

Research data

In this case, acoustic signals are obtained by prototype monitoring experiment. The used DOF system is based on phase-sensitive optical time domain reflectometry (Φ-OTDR), as illustrated in Figure 5. The experiment design is shown in Figure 6. The arranged PCCPs consist of five sections, with the lower portions of the pipes embedded underground. Water is injected to simulate actual operation conditions, and the water depth is approximately 1.5 m. The four types of optical fibers, labeled as 1#, 2#, 3#, and 4#, are installed. The length of the optical fibers is approximately 10 km. Knocking tests are conducted to select the best one from the four optical fibers. The results indicate that 3# has the highest sensitivity degree and signal-to-noise ratio and that the average positioning error is approximately ±2 m. Consequently, 3# is used for signal monitoring. On-site operators use a geological hammer to strike the pipe wall to simulate the impact of floating objects in water on the PCCPs. Meanwhile, a gear-cutting machine is employed to cut the prestressed steel wires to simulate the broken wire signals generated under hydrogen embrittlement. Two broken wire working conditions are set, i.e., cutting only one steel wire and cutting two steel wires simultaneously, denoted as broken wire-1 signal and broken wire-2 signal, respectively. 60 broken wire-1 signals, 60 broken wire-2 signals, and 120 knock signals are collected. The signal waveforms are shown in Figure 7.

For the captured vibration signals, the time domain parameters, including maximum value, minimum value, absolute maximum value, peak-to-peak value, mean value, standard deviation, rectified mean value, energy, root-mean-square, crest factor, impulse factor, margin factor, shape factor, kurtosis factor, skewness factor, A factor, and B factor, are extracted. Time domain signals are mapped to the frequency domain via the fast Fourier transform, and the frequency domain parameters, including centroid frequency, mean square frequency, frequency variance, standard deviation 1, third moment 1, fourth moment 1, mean value, variance, third moment, fourth moment, C factor, D factor, E factor, G factor, and J factor, are extracted. Time domain signals are subjected to multi-frequency decomposition, using wavelet analysis. For the high-frequency components, time-frequency domain parameters, including mean value, standard deviation, variance, root-mean-square, absolute maximum, skewness factor, kurtosis factor, rectified mean value, crest factor, margin factor, impulse factor, A factor, B factor, and energy, are extracted. The calculation expressions of the time domain parameters, frequency domain parameters, and time-frequency domain parameters are given in the work by Muñiz Calvente et al.56 The established feature parameter set (FPS) has a dimension of 122, consisting of 17 time domain parameters, 15 frequency domain parameters, and 90 time-frequency domain parameters.

To enhance identification performance, the attention (AT) mechanism is introduced into long short-term memory neural network (LSTM).57 Among the 122 parameters, some are ineffective for identifying broken wire signals. To address this issue, the “relief” filter criterion is employed to screen the effective FPS (EFPS) from FPS,58,59,60 and the detailed principle is not elaborated herein. Specifically, the top 20 ranked parameters are selected as EFPS. With EFPS as the input parameters and acoustic signal category labels as the output parameters, INOA-ATLSTM is constructed. It can be seen from Table 4 that INOA-ATLSTM achieves the highest overall accuracy, outperforming all baseline algorithms and ablation variants. These results collectively confirm that the INOA optimization framework provides consistent improvements over well-established baseline optimizers, thereby more convincingly demonstrating the advantage of INOA in practical hyperparameter optimization tasks.

Discussion

The main conclusions are as follows: first, by simulating the food storage and retrieving behaviors of nutcrackers, the NOA features high flexibility in search strategies and excellent global optimization capability. The comprehensive test results obtained from unimodal, multimodal, and fixed-dimension multimodal benchmark functions indicate that the NOA achieves the optimal performance among the selected 26 meta-heuristic algorithms. Second, logistic-tent chaotic mapping optimizes the distribution range and uniformity of the initial population; tangent flight and dynamic fitness-distance balance selection enhance the global search capability; criss-cross mutation maintains population diversity; and Gaussian mutation perturbation and Tent chaotic perturbation strengthen the local optima escaping capability. Overall, the INOA exhibits a better optimization performance and a higher reliability in solving complex optimization problems than the NOA. Third, in the abnormal acoustic signal identification case, the Acc value of INOA-ATLSTM is 97.63%. For broken wire-1 and broken wire-2 signals, the F1_score values of INOA-ATLSTM are 95.11% and 94.89%, respectively. INOA-ATLSTM exhibits superior identification performance to that of NOA-ATLSTM, PSO-ATLSTM, and GWO-ATLSTM. Finally, in the dam safety monitoring case, for deformation fitting, the values of R and S for INOA-HST are 0.9925 and 0.1421, respectively. For deformation prediction, the values of R and S of INOA-HST are 0.9868 and 0.5349, respectively. The deformation interpretation performance and prediction capability of INOA-HST are better than those of NOA-HST, GWO-HST, and PSO-HST. This study provides an efficient and reliable intelligent optimization method for complex engineering optimization problems and offers practical technical references for the application of meta-heuristic algorithms in water conservancy engineering.

In the benchmark functions and practical engineering cases adopted in this study, the INOA delivers favorable optimization accuracy and convergence performance. Nevertheless, engineering problems vary significantly in terms of constraint settings, design variable properties, and objective function characteristics. For the extended application of INOA to broader engineering optimization scenarios, tailored parameter optimization frameworks and configuration strategies should be formulated in accordance with the inherent features of specific problems. Future research on the NOA should focus on four core directions. First, it is necessary to integrate cutting-edge technologies such as chaos theory, reinforcement learning, and surrogate models to construct an adaptive parameter adjustment mechanism and a multi-strategy hybrid framework. Second, efforts should be devoted to the in-depth exploration of the multi-objective optimization field and to promoting the deep integration of NOA with advanced engineering technologies including numerical simulation and digital twins. Third, there are abundant high-dimensional complex optimization issues in water conservancy engineering, and each issue has unique attributes that require dedicated research. To further demonstrate the practical value of the proposed improvements, subsequent work will extend the INOA to higher dimensional engineering optimization scenarios. Finally, to enhance model interpretability, future research will adopt quantitative techniques to evaluate feature contributions and incorporate physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) to enforce physical consistency.

Limitations of the study

This study focuses on improving the optimization performance of NOA and validating the effectiveness of the proposed INOA through benchmark functions and two representative applications in water conservancy engineering. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the INOA integrates multiple improvement strategies, and the optimal strategy combination may vary across different optimization problems. While ablation experiments verify the effectiveness of the incorporated strategies, the optimal strategy configuration for different practical applications requires further evaluation through problem-oriented ablation analyses. Second, the present study mainly considers benchmark functions and representative water conservancy engineering applications. The applicability of INOA to more complex, large-scale, and high-dimensional optimization problems, such as viscoelastic parameter inversion, warrants further investigation.
