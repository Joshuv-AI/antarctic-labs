Blow-Up Constructions and Applications to Segre Classes and Multidegree formulas
Kai Huang

Abstract
. By comparing simultaneous multigraded blow-ups with suitable iterated blow-up construction, we establish a birational correspondence between the associated exceptional divisors. We further investigate blow-ups arising from rational maps to multiprojective spaces and derive intersection-theoretic formulas for the Chern classes of the resulting exceptional divisors and pullback of tautological line bundles. These constructions have two main applications. First, they yield a proof of the product formula for Segre classes without any pure-dimensionality hypothesis. Second, they lead to a degree formula for arbitrary closed subschemes of multiprojective spaces, recovering and extending the classical formula of van der Waerden.



1.
Introduction

The main goal of this paper is to study two geometric constructions arising from blow-ups and to illustrate their applications in intersection theory.

The first part of the paper is devoted to the comparison of simultaneous multigraded blow-ups with suitable iterated blow-up constructions. We establish a birational correspondence between these models and describe the resulting relations among their exceptional divisors. This comparison provides a geometric mechanism for transferring intersection-theoretic information between the two constructions.

The second part studies blow-ups associated with rational maps to multiprojective spaces. Starting from the identification of a blow-up with the scheme-theoretic closure of the graph of a rational map, we analyze the relations among the pullback of tautological line bundles and exceptional divisors and derive explicit formulas for their Chern classes.

As a first application, we revisit the product formula for Segre classes. Existing proofs generally require the schemes involved to be pure-dimensional, since this hypothesis is needed to apply the standard cycle-theoretic relations in intersection theory (such as
[
1
, Lemma 4.2, Proposition 4.2 and Example 4.2.4]
). Using the birational correspondence constructed above, we show that the product formula is fundamentally a consequence of the geometry of blow-ups. More precisely, an appropriate exceptional divisor serves as a geometric bridge that reduces the problem from arbitrary closed embeddings to the case of Cartier divisors on a suitable resolution. This yields a proof of the formula that avoids any pure-dimensionality assumptions.

A second application concerns degree formulas for closed subschemes of multiprojective spaces.
This result may be viewed as a modern reformulation and extension of the classical degree formula of van der Waerden
[
4
]
. The original argument was formulated in the language of classical algebraic geometry and relied essentially on the assumption that the varieties under consideration were integral (as explained in Remark
3.2.1
). By contrast, our proof is entirely intersection-theoretic, based on the formalism of Chow groups, blow-ups, and refined intersection products developed by Fulton. Besides providing a conceptual and functorial derivation of the formula, this approach naturally extends the result from varieties to arbitrary closed subschemes.

We follow the notation and conventions of
[
1
]
throughout the paper.

\displaystyle=\tau_{*}\left(\sum_{i\geq 0}c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(1))^{i}\cap[E]\right)=\tau_{*}\phi_{*}\left(\sum_{i\geq 0}c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(1))^{i}\cap[E^{\prime}]\right)\quad\text{(Projection formula)}

X\times\mathbb{P}^{m_{1}}_{\mathbb{Z}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{m_{n}}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
As this closure is equal to
\widetilde{X}
, it follows that

\Gamma\cong\widetilde{X}
.
The commutative diagram below induces the claimed isomorphism of invertible sheaves

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{1}\cap...\cap U_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X}_{1}\times_{X}\cdots\times_{X}\widetilde{X}_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X}_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{m_{1}}_{X}\times_{X}\cdots\times_{X}\mathbb{P}^{m_{n}}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\times\mathbb{P}^{m_{i}}_{\mathbb{Z}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{m_{i}}_{\mathbb{Z}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces pr_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{1}\times...\times\iota_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces pr_{i}}

∎

3.2.
Application to Multidegree Formula

Let
k
be a field. Applying Proposition
3.1.2
to

X=\mathbb{P}^{m+n+1}_{k}=\mathrm{Proj}\ k[x_{0},\ldots,x_{m},y_{0},\ldots,y_{n}],\qquad\mathcal{L}_{1}=\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(1),

with sections

x_{0},\ldots,x_{m}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}_{1}),\qquad y_{0},\ldots,y_{n}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}_{2}),

we obtain ideal sheaves
\mathcal{I}_{i}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
, blow-up

\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X
, open imbedding
j:U_{1}\cap U_{2}\to\tilde{X}
, closed imbedding

\iota:\widetilde{X}\hookrightarrow X\times\mathbb{P}^{m}_{k}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
,
and morphisms
\eta_{i}:\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{X}_{i}
. We denote

\displaystyle pr

\displaystyle:X\times\mathbb{P}_{k}^{m}\times\mathbb{P}_{k}^{n}\to\mathbb{P}_{k}^{m}\times\mathbb{P}_{k}^{n},

\displaystyle pr_{1}

\displaystyle:X\times\mathbb{P}_{k}^{m}\times\mathbb{P}_{k}^{n}\to\mathbb{P}_{k}^{m},

\displaystyle pr_{2}

\displaystyle:X\times\mathbb{P}_{k}^{m}\times\mathbb{P}_{k}^{n}\to\mathbb{P}_{k}^{n},

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle:=c_{1}(\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)),

\displaystyle t_{1}

\displaystyle:=c_{1}(\iota^{*}pr_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}_{k}}(1)),

\displaystyle t_{2}

\displaystyle:=c_{1}(\iota^{*}pr_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}}(1)).

Since
\widetilde{X}
is identified with the graph closure, every fiber of

pr\circ\iota:\widetilde{X}\to\mathbb{P}_{k}^{m}\times\mathbb{P}_{k}^{n}

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. By Miracle Flatness, it follows that
pr\circ\iota
is a smooth proper morphism of relative dimension
1
.
Moreover, since
\eta_{i}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}_{i}}(-1)
corresponds to the effective Cartier divisor
\widetilde{X}\times_{X}V(\mathcal{I}_{i})
on
\widetilde{X}
and
V(\mathcal{I}_{1})\cap V(\mathcal{I}_{2})=\varnothing
, it follows that

c_{1}\bigl(\eta_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}_{1}}(-1)\bigr)\cdot c_{1}\bigl(\eta_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}_{2}}(-1)\bigr)=0,

which is equivalent to

\xi^{2}-(t_{1}+t_{2})\xi+t_{1}t_{2}=0.

Hence,

\xi^{\ell}=h_{\ell-1}(t_{1},t_{2})\,\xi-t_{1}t_{2}\,h_{\ell-2}(t_{1},t_{2})\qquad(\ell\geq 1),

where
h_{l}(T_{1},T_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}[T_{1},T_{2}]
is defined by

h_{l}(T_{1},T_{2})=\begin{cases}T_{1}^{l}+T_{1}^{l-1}T_{2}+\cdots+T_{2}^{l},&l\geq 0,\\
0,&l=-1.\end{cases}

Now let
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{m}_{k}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
be a closed subscheme. let
V^{\prime\prime}:=(pr\circ\iota)^{-1}(V)\subset\tilde{X}

be its scheme-theoretic inverse image, and let
V^{\prime}\subset X

be the scheme-theoretic image of

(pr\circ\iota\circ j)^{-1}(V)\to U_{1}\cap U_{2}\to X
. It follows from
[
1
, Lemma 1.7.1]
that

\pi_{*}[V^{\prime\prime}]=[V^{\prime}]\in Z_{*}(X).

Furthermore, if

[V]=\sum_{i}r_{i}[V_{i}]\in Z_{*}(\mathbb{P}^{m}_{k}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})

is the fundamental cycle of
V
, then

[V^{\prime\prime}]=\sum_{i}r_{i}[V_{i}^{\prime\prime}]\in Z_{*}(\widetilde{X}),\qquad[V^{\prime}]=\sum_{i}r_{i}[V_{i}^{\prime}]\in Z_{*}(X).

Consequently,

\pi_{*}[V^{\prime\prime}]_{\ell}=[V^{\prime}]_{\ell}

for every
0\leq\ell\leq m+n+1
, where the subscript
\ell
denotes the
\ell
-dimensional component of a cycle.

Remark 3.2.1
.

Note that if
V
is an integral closed subscheme of dimension
d
, then
V^{\prime}
is integral of dimension
d+1
. Furthermore, the bihomogeneous equations defining
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{m}_{k}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
also define
V^{\prime}
as a closed subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{m+n+1}_{k}
. Hence our construction recovers the situation treated by van der Waerden in
[
4
]
.

For non-integral subschemes, however, the relation between degrees and bidegrees is more subtle. For instance, let
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
be defined by

x_{0}y_{0}=x_{1}y_{1}=0
.
Then the sum of the bidegrees of
V
equals
2
, while the closed subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}
defined by the same equations has degree
4
.

Definition 3.2.2
.

For integers
0\leq a\leq m+n+1
and
0\leq b\leq m+n
, and for closed subschemes

Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{m+n+1}_{k},Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{m}_{k}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k},

we introduce the following notions of degree.

(1)

The codimension-
a
degree of
Y
:

Under the identification

A^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{m+n+1}_{k})\cong\mathbb{Z}[T]/(T^{m+n+2}),

the cycle class
[Y]
is represented by a polynomial
f(T)
of degree at most
m+n+1
. The codimension-
a
degree of
Y
is defined to be the coefficient of
T^{a}
in
f(T)
.

(2)

The codimension-
b
bidegrees of
Z
:

Under the identification

A^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{m}_{k}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})\cong\mathbb{Z}[T_{1},T_{2}]/(T_{1}^{m+1},T_{2}^{n+1}),

the cycle class
[Z]
is represented by a polynomial
g(T_{1},T_{2})
of total degree at most
m+n
. For each pair
(\alpha,\beta)
satisfying
\alpha+\beta=b
, the coefficient of
T_{1}^{\alpha}T_{2}^{\beta}
in
g(T_{1},T_{2})
is called a codimension-
b
bidegree of
Z
.

Proposition 3.2.3
.

Let
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{m}_{k}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
be a closed subscheme, and let
V^{\prime\prime}
and
V^{\prime}
be the closed subschemes associated to
V
by the above construction. Then, for every
0\leq d\leq m+n
, the codimension-
d
degree of
V^{\prime}
is equal to the sum of the codimension-
d
bidegrees of
V
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we denote by
\tau
the composition
pr\circ\iota
.

\displaystyle\text{The codimension-}d\text{ degree of }V^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))^{m+n+1-d}\cap[V^{\prime}]_{m+n+1-d}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\pi_{*}\left(\xi^{m+n+1-d}\cap[V^{\prime\prime}]_{m+n+1-d}\right)\quad\text{(Projection formula)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{X}}h_{m+n-d}(t_{1},t_{2})\xi\cap\left(\tau^{*}[V]_{m+n-d}\right)-t_{1}t_{2}h_{m+n-d-1}(t_{1},t_{2})\cap\left(\tau^{*}[V]_{m+n-d}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{X}}\xi\cap\left[\tau^{*}\left(h_{m+n-d}(c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}_{k}}(1)|),c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}}(1)|))\cap[V]_{m+n-d}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\tau^{*}\left(c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}_{k}}(1)|)\cdot c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}}(1)|)\cdot h_{m+n-d-1}(c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}_{k}}(1)|),c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}}(1)|))\cap[V]_{m+n-d}\right),

where
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}_{k}}(1)|
denotes the pullback of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}_{k}}(1)
along the projection

\mathbb{P}^{m}_{k}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}\to\mathbb{P}^{m}_{k}
, and analogously for
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}}(1)|
. Since all fibers of
\tau
are isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the first term in the last equation is the sum of the codimension-
d
bidegrees of
V
, while the second term vanishes for dimensional reasons.
∎

Remark 3.2.4
.

One can generalize the above proposition to arbitrary multiprojective spaces: the codimension-
d
degree of
V^{\prime}
is equal to the sum of the codimension-
d
multidegrees of
V
.
