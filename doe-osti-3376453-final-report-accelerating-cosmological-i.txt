Final Report DE-SC0023892
Accelerating cosmological inference for LSST and DESI
 with neural networks

 PI: Tim Eifler, University of Arizona

1 Project Goals and Status

The main goal of this proposal to train a suite of neural networks that compute data vectors
and covariance matrices for different analysis setups of cosmic frontier experiments such as
the Dark Energy Survey (DES), the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), and
Rubin Observatory’s Legacy Survey of Space and Time. These neural networks are critically
important to map out science strategies and to deliver the promised science return for DESI
and LSST in the future and to enhance DES analyses after the survey has been completed.

Objective 1: Build emulators from the CosmoLike analysis framework to predict multi-
probe data vectors for the most interesting science cases from DESI and LSST considering

 • a variety of combinations of cosmological probes, e.g., galaxy weak lensing, galaxy
 clustering, baryon acoustic oscillations, galaxy cluster number counts, galaxy cluster
 weak lensing, CMB temperature and polarization, CMB lensing, and cross-correlations
 thereof.

 • time-dependent dark energy, different neutrino physics models, early dark energy, dif-
 ferent modified gravity scenarios, dark matter-dark energy coupling.

Objective 2: Build emulators from UniverseMachine framework to predict galaxy sum-
mary statistics, e.g. stellar mass functions, luminosity functions, galaxy correlation functions
for the galaxy samples that DESI and LSST will use to constrain cosmology. This will dra-
matically accelerate building accurate galaxy mock catalogs to validate cosmology pipelines.

Objective 3: Build emulators for multi-probe covariance matrices as a function of the
underlying cosmological model. We plan to build these covariance emulators for different
survey strategy scenarios of DESI and LSST (varying depth and area of the survey) and for
different probe combinations similar to Objective 1.

 We made significant progress on all objectives, but also realized that some aspects were
open ended ML problems that were beyond the scope of this award. Should more funding
related to ML/AI DOE projects become available we are in an excellent position to continue
building emulators for observables for LSST/DESI and their covariances.

  Below we detail the achievements of this award.

Objective 1: Two papers, Boruah et al. (2022) Accelerating cosmological inference with
Gaussian processes and neural networks - an application to LSST Y1 weak lensing and galaxy
clustering and Boruah et al. (2024) Machine Learning LSST 3x2pt analyses – forecasting the
impact of systematics on cosmological constraints using neural networks have emerged from
this topic. The latter paper was directly enabled by this DOE grant.

 In these papers we build a neural network emulator for one of the main Rubin cos-
mology analyses, the analysis of combined lensing and clustering measurements, so-called
3x2pt. This emulator reduces the computational requirements for the modeling of the 3x2pt
observables by several orders of magnitude, which allows us to run fast inference while main-
taining the full realism of the data analysis pipeline. We use this emulator to run simulated
3×2 (cosmic shear, galaxy-galaxy lensing, and galaxy clustering) analyses for mock LSST-
Y1/Y3/Y6/Y10 surveys, closely following the LSST-DESC Science Requirement document,
in order to study the impact of various systematic effects (galaxy bias, intrinsic alignment,
baryonic physics, shear calibration and photo-z uncertainties).

 One of our main findings is that uncertainties in galaxy bias parameterization contribute
most to the error budget; improvements on this astrophysics systematic are the most promis-
ing avenue to improve the constraining power for LSST. This emulator will be further im-
proved with a transformer based neural network model that can cover a larger parameter
space compared to the multi-layer perceptron design.

interest across the community and is being implemented in the analysis of DES data and
in forecasting LSST. The cosmology group of faculty Vivian Miranda has become a close
collaborator on this topic. Prof Miranda, a former postdoc of PI Eifler and Co-I Krause,

 These papers use attention based neural networks, extending the Resnet-MLP architecture
of the Boruah papers.

 We have further applied the idea of ML accelerated inference to real data from LSST
precursor surveys, specifically Dark Energy Survey (DES) Year 3 and Year 6 multi-probe
analyses. This resulted in one publication, Xu et al. (2025) Constraining baryonic feedback
and cosmology from DES Y3 and Planck PR4 6×2pt data. I. ΛCDM models, and another
one in progress on DES Year 6 that will be continued as part of DOE Comparative Review
funding.

 In this publication, for the first time, we were able to run 1000s of simulated analyses
to understand the DES specific parameter space (physics and systematics including the
impact of priors and scale cuts) properly before using our models on data. The resulting
constraints are more constraining than the DES Key Paper analyses of Year 3 and even
more constraining than the recent Year 6 data. ML accelerated inference will be an integral
part of the LSST and DESI analyses in the future, yet integration into the LSST specific
code bases (in particular into LSST’s Core Cosmological Library) was not part of this
proposal, yet is critical for making full use of LSST data.

  Training data for our neural networks are computed from CosmoLike, which has also
been adopted as the main inference and analysis code for the weak lensing and multi-probe
analyses of the NASA’s next flagship astrophysics mission, the Roman Space Telescope.
Within the Project Infrastructure Teams of Roman, the idea of ML accelerated inference
has become state of the art and our team was recruited to implement it for the corresponding
dark energy analysis.

Objective 2: This project turned out to be more complex than expected. A full integration
of UniverseMachine and CosmoLike frameworks to infuse galaxy formation knowledge into
cosmological analyses was always beyond the scope of this award, hence we identified an
interface across which the additional information from UniverseMachine can be infused
into CosmoLike.

 In Salcedo et al. (2024) One galaxy sample to rule them all: HOD modeling of DES
Y3 source galaxies, we detail how accelerating the UniverseMachine empirical modeling of
galaxy formation observables can significantly enhance cosmological constraints. While we
explore this in the context of a DES Year 3 analysis the extension to LSST is straightforward
and can commence once the corresponding galaxy sample is available.

  Our method leads to significant improvement in cosmological constraining power, which
comes from the fact that our machinery allows us to obtain tight priors on galaxy bias param-

eters, obtained by the galaxy formation knowledge infused from UniverseMachine. Galaxy
bias is one of the largest uncertainties in LSST and DESI cosmological analyses (e.g see the
Boruah et al., 2024, paper for LSST). Usually galaxy bias parameters are left freely varying,
meaning that its uncertainty is self-calibrated through the constraining power of the data.
The so consumed constraining power is hence lacking for the cosmology parameters. Our
new idea allows for stringent priors on galaxy bias uncertainties and hence more constraining
power for cosmology parameters.

 This project is evolving into a new research area, likely beyond the scope of this pro-
posal. We will confine work supported by this proposal to upgrade model precision of
UniverseMachine and to further accelerate its runtime through neural networks. Initial
work on in this direction has been performed by graduate student Haley Bowden resulting in
one publication (Bowden & Behroozi, 2025). In this paper co-I Behroozi and PhD student
Bowden present a novel neural-network based method, which takes advantage of observable
measures of a galaxy and its environment to recover whether it (1) is a central or a satel-
lite, (2) has experienced an interaction with a more massive neighbor, and (3) is currently
orbiting or infalling onto such a neighbor. This method offers a new avenue for studying the
role of dark matter environment in galaxy formation and evolution, ultimately leading to a
better understanding of the galaxy bias systematic that is haunting DES/LSST and DESI.

Objective 3: Graduate Student Joe Adamo published Adamo et al. (2024) Neural network
based emulation of galaxy power spectrum covariances – A reanalysis of BOSS DR12 data
as a major result on this objective. Using a combination of Transformer and Resnet-MLP
architecture this paper demonstrates the feasibility to not only machine learn observables,
but also the more complex covariances. Covariances require more sophisticated neural net-
work architectures since their dimension scales quadratically with the dimension of the data
vector and their calculation requires significantly more complex numerical operations. Their
emulation however is critical, in particular for LSST, in order to model the covariance’s cos-
mology dependence that causes it to change at every step of the MCMC. Mr Adamo’s neural
network architectures can be extended to DESI and LSST covariances as well, however we
could not complete this task as part of this award. It will be continued if future DOE funding
becomes available.

3 Products

Two large software packages were greatly expanded as part of this award and used to generate
training data for the neural network emulators. The now widely used CosmoLike https://
github.com/CosmoLike and CosmoCov https://github.com/CosmoLike/CosmoCov codes
uniquely provide the multi-probe covariances required for the DES 3×2pt key projects in Y1
through Y6. CosmoLike (Krause & Eifler, 2017) is an open-source software framework for
simulated likelihood analyses of cosmological surveys. It is based on an integrated ansatz
of jointly analyzing LSS probes, and it includes unique covariance computation capabilities
that include the (dominant) higher-order terms of the matter density field. The covariance
computations are also available through a separate, open-source and user-friendly software
package. We have further expanded the UniverseMachine software package, which utilizes
galaxy formation observables to calculate the probability of finding galaxies within halos
of given mass. This package can be used to reduce one of the largest uncertainties in
cosmological analyses, so-called galaxy bias.

 This HEP AI/ML seed grant has opened the door to several science prospects, whose full
