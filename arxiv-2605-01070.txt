Stability of p-area minimizing surfaces in the Heisenberg group
Amir Moradifam, Gerardo Orozco-Fernandez

Abstract
We study the stability of minimizers of weighted p p -area functionals associated with prescribed p p -mean curvature surfaces in the Heisenberg group. While existence and uniqueness results are well established, quantitative stability with respect to perturbations of the mean curvature H H remains largely unexplored in the nonzero- H H regime. Using a Rockafellar–Fenchel duality framework, we identify a unique underlying vector field associated with each minimizer and prove its stability under perturbations of H H . This yields quantitative control of the direction field of the horizontal gradient. Building on this structure, we establish L 1 L^{1} stability of admissible minimizers under natural geometric assumptions on level sets. In dimensions two and three, we also derive W 1 , 1 W^{1,1} stability estimates under additional regularity and structural hypotheses, with explicit rates in terms of ‖ H − H ~ ‖ L ∞ \|H-\tilde{H}\|_{L^{\infty}} . Our results provide the first quantitative stability theory for p p -area minimizing graphs with prescribed nonzero p p -mean curvature, even in the unweighted case. Numerical simulations are included to illustrate the robustness of the theoretical results.

Introduction

The study of minimal and constant mean curvature surfaces is a central theme in geometric analysis and the calculus of variations.
In the Euclidean or Riemannian setting, surfaces with zero mean curvature locally minimize area, while those with constant nonzero mean curvature describe equilibrium interfaces under pressure or volume constraints.
A natural question is how these notions extend to
sub-Riemannian
or
pseudohermitian
geometries, where only a horizontal distribution of admissible directions is available and the metric is determined by the Carnot–Carathéodory distance.

The Heisenberg group arises naturally in several areas of physics where geometry is constrained or noncommutative. It provides the geometric framework underlying the position–momentum relations in quantum mechanics, reflecting the fundamental noncommutativity of observables. It also models systems with restricted motion, such as nonholonomic mechanics and anisotropic media, where only certain directions of movement are allowed. In addition, sub-Riemannian structures like the Heisenberg group appear in wave propagation and geometric optics, as well as in energy minimization problems involving interfaces in constrained environments. These connections make it a fundamental model for studying physical phenomena where classical Euclidean geometry is insufficient. The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
provides the simplest and most fundamental example of such a geometry.
It is a step-two nilpotent Lie group endowed with a natural horizontal subbundle and a noncommutative structure that profoundly influences the geometry of curves and surfaces.
In this setting, the appropriate analogue of classical mean curvature is the
p
-mean curvature
, defined as the divergence of a normalized horizontal gradient.
The nonparametric form of the prescribed
p
-mean curvature equation for graphs
u:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n}\to\mathbb{R}
is

\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\nabla u-X^{*}}{|\nabla u-X^{*}|}\right)=H\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(1)

where
X^{*}=(y_{1},-x_{1},\dots,y_{n},-x_{n})
.
This equation arises as the Euler–Lagrange equation for the
p
-area functional

E(u)=\int_{\Omega}\bigl(\,|\nabla u-X^{*}|+H\,u\bigr)\,dx,\qquad a(x)>0,

(2)

whose minimizers represent
surfaces of prescribed
p
-mean curvature

H
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
Here
X^{*}
encodes the intrinsic rotation of horizontal directions in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
The term
\nabla u-X^{*}
corresponds to the horizontal gradient of the defining function
\phi(x,y,t)=t-u(x,y)
, and
|\nabla u-X^{*}|
therefore measures the horizontal slope of the graph.
The divergence in (
1
) expresses that the surface has the prescribed
p
-mean curvature
H
. The equation (
1
) with
X\equiv 0
and
H\equiv 0
have been extensively studied by many authors, including the first author, see
[
20
,
21
,
27
,
28
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
39
,
40
,
41
,
42
]
.

Early progress on this problem was achieved by Pauls
[
37
]
, who studied the
p
-minimal surface case (
H=0
) in
\mathbb{H}^{1}
.
He formulated the associated subelliptic equation, proved existence for certain Plateau-type boundary data, and showed that uniqueness can fail, since distinct
p
-minimal graphs may share the same boundary.
This phenomenon reflects the non-strict convexity of the
p
-area functional and illustrates the delicate analytical nature of the problem.

Cheng, Hwang, Malchiodi, and Yang
[
8
]
developed the geometric and analytic foundations of
p
-minimal surfaces in pseudohermitian manifolds.
They introduced intrinsic definitions of
p
-mean curvature and
p
-area, elucidated the role of characteristic curves, and analyzed the mixed elliptic–hyperbolic character of the governing equation.
Their results show that the
p
-mean curvature encodes fundamental geometric information of pseudohermitian structures and connects naturally to calibration theory and Cauchy–Riemann (CR) geometry (see also
[
6
]
).

Building on this framework, Cheng, Hwang, and Yang
[
9
]
established existence and uniqueness for minimizers of the functional

E(u)=\int_{\Omega}\bigl(|\nabla u-X^{*}|+H\,u\bigr)\,dx,

(3)

under suitable boundary slope and convexity conditions.
Their analysis treats the full prescribed
p
-mean curvature equation with nonzero
H
, addressing the degeneracy of the PDE along the characteristic set

\Sigma(u)=\{x\in\Omega:\nabla u=X^{*}(x)\}

and the coupling introduced by the linear term
H\,u
.
The results in
[
9
]
treat the full prescribed
p
-mean curvature equation with
nonzero
H
, showing that this case requires analytical techniques beyond those used for
the minimal (
H=0
) setting and constitutes an essential part of the general theory.
In the broader sub-Riemannian context, surfaces with constant
p
-mean curvature are closely
connected to the
isoperimetric problem
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, where minimizers of the
horizontal perimeter under fixed volume are expected to have boundaries of constant

p
-mean curvature.

The nonzero-
H
regime thus occupies a central position in pseudohermitian geometry.
Geometrically, it corresponds to sub-Riemannian analogues of constant mean curvature surfaces, while analytically it introduces substantial challenges: the coupling term
H\,u
links geometry and potential, breaks homogeneity, and amplifies the effects of degeneracy where
|\nabla u-X^{*}|
vanishes.
Nonuniqueness and limited regularity can persist, and the dependence of minimizers on the data
H
becomes a subtle and important issue.

In
[
29
]
, the authors studued existence and structure of minimizers of the energy functional
\mathbb{E}
from a different point of view, using the Rockafellar-Fenchel duality, and proved various existence results for the weighted form of the functional

\mathcal{F}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\left(a|\nabla u-X^{*}|+Hu\right)dx,

(4)

where
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is a positive function. Minimizers of this functional will satisfy the Euler-Lagrange equation

\nabla\cdot\left(a\frac{\nabla u-X^{*}}{|\nabla u-X^{*}|}\right)=H,

(5)

which could be viewed as the
p
-mean curvature of the function
(X,u(X))
, with respect to the metric
g=a^{\frac{2}{n-1}}dx
, which is conformal to the Euclidean metric (see also
[
30
]
for a generalization of the results).

Although the existence and uniqueness theory is well developed, the question of
stability
, that is, how minimizers depend quantitatively on perturbations of
H(x)
, remains largely open when
H\neq 0
. Understanding this dependence is essential both for theoretical completeness and for the reliability of numerical schemes based on variational or operator splitting methods. The aim of the present paper is to investigate the stability of minimizers of the functional (
4
) with respect to the mean curvature
H
. Building on the geometric and analytic foundations developed in
[
8
,
9
,
22
,
23
,
29
,
30
,
37
]
, we establish new stability and quantitative continuity results for minimizers of the
p
-area functional with prescribed nonzero
H
.

These results provide a more refined understanding of the structure of pseudohermitian
p
-mean curvature graphs and extend the existing theory beyond existence and uniqueness to include stability. They are new even in the special case
a\equiv 1
. We also carry out numerical simulations to illustrate the robustness of the results.

2
Duality and Stability of the Underlying Vector Field

Let
\Omega
be a connected, open, and bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with Lipschitz boundary. Consider the minimization problem

\inf\limits_{w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(a|Dw+F|+Hw),

(6)

and note that the more general minimization problem on
H^{1}_{f}(\Omega)=\{w\in H^{1}(\Omega):w=f\ \ \text{on}\ \ \partial\Omega\}
can be reduced to (
11
) with a simple change of variable
w=w_{0}+f
, where
w_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
f\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{f}(\Omega)
.

By Rockafellar-Fenchel duality
[
13
]
, the dual problem associated to (
6
) can be written as

(D)\hskip 14.22636pt\sup\{\langle F,b\rangle:b\in\mathcal{D}_{0}\ \ \hbox{and}\ \ |b|\leq a\ \ \hbox{a.e. in}\ \ \Omega\},

(7)

where

\mathcal{D}_{0}:=\left\{b\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{n}:\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot b+Hu=0,\ \ \hbox{for all}\ \ u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right\}.

(8)

Indeed
b\in(L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{n}\cap\mathcal{D}_{0}
if and only if

\nabla\cdot b=H\ \ \hbox{a.e. in}\ \ \Omega.

See section 3.1 in
[
29
]
for more details. The following result shows that there is a unique underlying vector field
J
that determines the direction of
Du+F
in
\Omega
, for any minimizer
u
.

Theorem 2.1

[
29
]

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
F,H\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
a\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be a positive function, and assume (
6
) is bounded below. Then the duality gap is zero and the dual problem
(D)
has a solution, i.e. there exists a vector field
J\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
with
|J|\leq a
,
|Du+F|-a.e.
in
\Omega
, such that

\inf_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\left(a\left|Du+F\right|+Hu\right)dx=\langle F,J\rangle

(9)

Moreover

a\frac{Du+F}{|Du+F|}=J,\ \ \ \ |Du+F|-a.e.\ \ \hbox{in}\ \ \Omega,

(10)

for any minimizer
u
of (
6
).

Remark 2.2

Note that the (
6
) may not have a minimizer, but the dual problem
(D)
always has a solution
J\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{n}
. In addition, the minimization problem (
6
) is not strictly convex, and it may have multiple minimizers (see
[
21
]
), and the equality (
10
) holds for every minimizer of (
6
).

This theorem and the underlying vector field
J
will play an essential role in our stability arguments. Now let
u
be a minimizer of

\inf\limits_{w\in BV_{0}(\Omega)}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(a|Dw+F|+Hw).

(11)

Note that
u
satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation

\nabla\cdot\left(a\frac{Du+F}{|Du+F|}\right)=H,\hskip 7.22743ptu|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

(12)

Then there exits a vector field
J
, independent of the choice of the minimizer
u
, such that

a\frac{Du+F}{|Du+F|}=J,\ \ \ \ |Du+F|-a.e.\ \ \hbox{in}\ \ \Omega,

with
\nabla\cdot J=H
. Moreover

J(x):=\sigma(x)(Du(x)+F(x))\quad\text{and}\quad|J|=a,\ \ \ \ |Du+F|-a.e.\ \ \hbox{in}\ \ \Omega,

for some function
\sigma\geq 0
. In particular,

a=|J|=\sigma|Du+F|=J\cdot\frac{(Du+F)}{|Du+F|},\ \ \ \ |Du+F|-a.e.\ \ \hbox{in}\ \ \Omega.

Similarly, if
\tilde{u}
be a minimizer of

\inf\limits_{w\in BV_{0}(\Omega)}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(a|Dw+F|+\tilde{H}w),

(13)

where
\tilde{H}
is a perturbation of
H
, then one can similarly define
\tilde{J}
and
\tilde{\sigma}
.

Next we aim to show that the structure of the level sets of minimizers of the least gradient problem (
11
) is stable. The arguments are partly inspired by our earlier results in
[
22
]
. Throughout the paper, we will assume that
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and

0<m\leq a(x)\leq M,\,\,x\in\Omega,

(14)

for some positive constants
m,M.

It is well known that (see
[
17
,
25
]
) there exists a constant
C_{\Omega}>0
depending only on
\Omega
such that,
\text{for all }u\in BV_{0}(\Omega),

\|u\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}|Du|.

(15)

We shall need the following Lemmas.

Lemma 2.3

Suppose

\|F\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq k_{1}

for some
k_{1}>0,
and

\|H\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\displaystyle\frac{m}{C_{\Omega}}

(16)

where
C_{\Omega}
is the constant in (
15
). If
u
is a minimizer of (
13
), then

\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C,

(17)

for some constant
C,
depending on
\Omega,\,m,
and
k_{1},
independent of
u
and
\tilde{u}.
Consequently, the optimization problem (
13
) is bounded away from
-\infty
.

Proof.
We have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Omega}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|Du|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Omega}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|Du+F|\,dx+C_{\Omega}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|F|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\Omega}}{m}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a|Du+F|\,dx+k_{1}C_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\Omega}}{m}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a|Du+F|+Hu\,dx-\frac{C_{\Omega}}{m}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}Hu\,dx+k_{1}C_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\Omega}C_{1}}{m}+\frac{C_{\Omega}}{m}\|H\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+k_{1}C_{\Omega}.

where
C_{1}=\left|\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a|Du+F|+Hu\,dx\right|.
Thus

\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C_{\Omega}C_{1}+mk_{1}C_{\Omega}}{m-C_{\Omega}\|H\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}},

and hence
\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
is bounded above by a constant independent of
u
.

\Box

Lemma 2.4

Let
u
and
\tilde{u}
be minimizers of (
11
) and (
13
). Then

\left|\int_{\Omega}a|Du+F|+Hu\,dx-\int_{\Omega}a|D\tilde{u}+F|+\tilde{H}\tilde{u}\,dx\right|\leq C\|H-\tilde{H}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

for some constant
C=C(\Omega,m,k_{1})
independent of
u
and
\tilde{u}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}Hu\,dx-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\tilde{H}u\,dx

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a|Du+F|+Hu\,dx-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a|Du+F|+\tilde{H}u\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a|Du+F|+Hu\,dx-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a|D\tilde{u}+F|+\tilde{H}\tilde{u}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a|D\tilde{u}+F|+H\tilde{u}\,dx-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a|D\tilde{u}+F|+\tilde{H}\tilde{u}\,dx

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}H\tilde{u}\,dx-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\tilde{H}\tilde{u}\,dx.

Thus,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(H-\tilde{H})u\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a|Du+F|+Hu\,dx-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a|D\tilde{u}+F|+\tilde{H}\tilde{u}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(H-\tilde{H})\tilde{u}\,dx.

Hence

\displaystyle-\|H-\tilde{H}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a|Du+F|+Hu\,dx-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a|D\tilde{u}+F|+\tilde{H}\tilde{u}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\|H-\tilde{H}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)},

and therefore

\displaystyle\left|\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a\big(|Du+F|-|D\tilde{u}+F|\big)+Hu-\tilde{H}\tilde{u}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)},\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\right\}\|H-\tilde{H}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Note that similar to Lemma
2.3
,
\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
is bounded above by a constant, and thus, the proof is complete.
\Box

Let
\nu_{\Omega}
denote the outer unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega.
Then for every
T\in\left(L^{\infty}(\Omega)\right)^{n}
with
\nabla\cdot T\in L^{n}(\Omega),
there exists a unique function
[T,\nu_{\Omega}]\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
such that

\int_{\partial\Omega}[T,\nu_{\Omega}]u\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\Omega}u\nabla\cdot T\,dx+\int_{\Omega}T\cdot Du\,dx,\hskip 7.22743ptu\in C^{1}(\bar{\Omega}).

(18)

Moreover, for
u\in BV(\Omega)
and
T\in\left(L^{\infty}(\Omega)\right)^{n}
with
\nabla\cdot T\in L^{n}(\Omega),
the linear functional
u\mapsto(T\cdot Du)
gives rise to a Radon measure on
\Omega
and (
18
) holds for all

(see
[
1
,
3
]
).

Lemma 2.5

Suppose that
0\leq\sigma(x)\leq\sigma_{1}
for some constant
\sigma_{1}>0.
Then

\int_{\Omega}|J||\tilde{J}|-J\cdot\tilde{J}\,dx\leq C\|H-\tilde{H}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

where
C=C(\Omega,m,k_{1},\sigma_{1})
is a constant independent of
u
and
\tilde{u}.

Proof.

Note that by the weak integration by parts formula,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u\nabla\cdot\tilde{J}+\tilde{J}\cdot Du\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}[\tilde{J},\nu_{\Omega}]u\,dx=0=\int_{\partial\Omega}[\tilde{J},\nu_{\Omega}]\tilde{u}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{u}\nabla\cdot\tilde{J}+\tilde{J}\cdot D\tilde{u}\,dx

and thus,

\int_{\Omega}\tilde{H}u+\tilde{J}\cdot Du\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{H}\tilde{u}+\tilde{J}\cdot D\tilde{u}\,dx.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|J||\tilde{J}|-J\cdot\tilde{J}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sigma|\tilde{J}||Du+F|-\sigma\tilde{J}\cdot(Du+F)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{1}\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{J}||Du+F|-\tilde{J}\cdot F-\tilde{J}\cdot Du\,dx

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{J}||Du+F|-\tilde{J}\cdot F-(-\tilde{H}u+\tilde{H}\tilde{u}+\tilde{J}\cdot D\tilde{u})\,dx

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{J}||Du+F|-\tilde{J}\cdot(D\tilde{u}+F)+\tilde{H}u-\tilde{H}\tilde{u}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{J}||Du+F|-|\tilde{J}||D\tilde{u}+F|+\tilde{H}u-\tilde{H}\tilde{u}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}\int_{\Omega}a|Du+F|+Hu-a|D\tilde{u}+F|-\tilde{H}\tilde{u}+\left(\tilde{H}-H\right)u\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{1}(C+\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)})\|H-\tilde{H}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

where
C
is as in Lemma
2.4
. By Lemma
2.3
, the proof is complete.

\Box

Now we are ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.6

Suppose that
0\leq\sigma(x)\leq\sigma_{1}
for some constant
\sigma_{1}>0.
Then

\|J-\tilde{J}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|H-\tilde{H}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
C=C(\Omega,m,k_{1},\sigma_{1})
is a constant independent of
u
and
\tilde{u}.

Proof.
We have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{|J-\tilde{J}|^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{|J|^{2}+|\tilde{J}|^{2}-2J\cdot\tilde{J}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{2a^{2}-2J\cdot\tilde{J}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{|J||\tilde{J}|-J\cdot\tilde{J}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2|\Omega|}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|J||\tilde{J}|-J\cdot\tilde{J}\,dx\right)^{1/2}.

Hence the result follows from Lemma
2.5
.

\Box

3
L^{1}
stability of the minimizers

In this section, we establish the stability of minimizers of the least gradient problem (
11
) in
L^{1}(\Omega).
In general, (
11
) does not have unique minimizers so in order to prove any stability results, further assumptions on
H,\tilde{H}
and on the corresponding minimizers are necessary.

Definition 3.1

Fix positive constants
\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1}.
We say that
u\in C^{1}\left(\bar{\Omega}\right)
is admissible if it solves (
11
) for some
\sigma\in C(\Omega)
with

0<\sigma_{0}<\sigma<\sigma_{1},

and
m\leq|J|=|\sigma(\nabla u+F)|\leq M,
where
m
and
M
are positive constants as in (
14
). We similarly define admissibility for
\tilde{u}.

For the remainder of the paper, we will assume that
F
is a conservative vector field
\big(
i.e.
F=\nabla f
for some function
f\in C^{1}(\Omega)\big).
Furthermore, assume
f\in C(\bar{\Omega})
and on
\bar{\Omega},
define
v:=u+f
and
\tilde{v}:=\tilde{u}+f.
Then
v-\tilde{v}=u-\tilde{u}.

We will prove our results in dimension

and then extend them to dimension

Let
v\in C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})
with
|\nabla v|>0
almost everywhere in
\Omega.
By the regularity result of De Giorgi, (Theorem 4.11 in
[

]
) it follows that almost all level sets of

are
C^{1}
hypersurfaces. For

we will furthermore assume that the length of level sets of

in
\Omega
is uniformly bounded, i.e.

\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\{v=t\}\cap\Omega}\,dl=K<\infty.

(19)

Lemma 3.2

Almost every level set of
v
reaches
\partial\Omega.

Proof.

Almost every level set is a
C^{1}
hypersurface. Suppose that there exists
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\Gamma_{0}:=\{x\in\bar{\Omega}:v(x)=t\}
satisfies
\Gamma_{0}\cap\partial\Omega=\varnothing.
By the Alexander duality theorem (see, e.g., Theorem 27.10 in

[
14
]
),
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is partitioned into a bounded open connected region,
\Omega_{t},
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\bar{\Omega}_{t},
which share the boundary
\Gamma_{0}.
In
\bar{\Omega}_{t},
define
w(x)=v(x)-t.
Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}a|Dw|+Hw\,dx

\displaystyle\geq m\|Dw\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})}-\|H\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|w\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})}

\displaystyle\geq\left(m-\|H\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}C_{\Omega_{t}}\right)\|Dw\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})}

where
C_{\Omega_{t}}
is the constant in (
15
). Extend the definition of
w
to be identically 0 on
\bar{\Omega}\backslash\bar{\Omega}_{t}.
Since
\|w\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})}=\|w\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega}\|Dw\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)},
we may assume that
C_{\Omega_{t}}\leq C_{\Omega}.
Therefore, by (
16
),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}a|Dw|+Hw\,dx\geq\left(m-\|H\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}C_{\Omega}\right)\|Dw\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})}\geq 0.

Equivalently,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}a|Dv|+Hv\,dx\geq t\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}H\,dx,

and the inequality is strict if
v\not\equiv t
. Hence,
v\equiv t
is the only minimizer of
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}a|Dv|+Hv\,dx
in
\Omega_{t}.

Now consider the disjoint collection of all such
\Omega_{t}
and, for each
t,
choose
x_{t}\in\Omega_{t}\cap\mathbb{Q}^{n}.
Then each
x_{t}
is distinct. Thus, the number of level sets that do not reach the boundary is countable.

\Box

Theorem 3.3

Let
n=2,
and suppose
u
and
\tilde{u}
are admissible with
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0=\tilde{u}|_{\partial\Omega}.
If
u
satisfies (
19

\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|H-\tilde{H}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}},

for some constant
C(\Omega,m,M,k_{1},K,\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1})
independent of
\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{\sigma}.

Proof.

Since
u
is admissible,

(20)

Let
\Gamma_{t}
be a connected component of
\{x\in\Omega:v(x)=t\}

\gamma:[0,L]\to\Gamma_{t}
be a path parameterized by the arc length of
\Gamma_{t}
with

by

Then

is constant on

then

on

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\nabla v(\gamma(s))\cdot\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\nabla\tilde{v}(\gamma(s))\cdot\gamma^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma(\gamma(s))}{\tilde{\sigma}(\gamma(s))}\nabla v(\gamma(s))\cdot\gamma^{\prime}(s)-\nabla\tilde{v}(\gamma(s))\cdot\gamma^{\prime}(s)

2.6

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\{v=t\}\cap\Omega}|v-\tilde{v}|\,dl\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K}{\sigma_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\{v=t\}\cap\Omega}|J-\tilde{J}|\,dl\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{KMC}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}\|H-\tilde{H}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{1/2}

for some constant

20

u,

Algorithm 1

Let
\lambda>0.
Let
H\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and initialize
b^{0},d^{0}\in\left(L^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{n}.
For
k\geq 0,

1.

Solve

\Delta u^{k+1}=-\nabla\cdot\left(b^{k}-d^{k}\right)+\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}H,\kern 5.0ptu^{k+1}\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

2.

Compute

d^{k+1}:=\begin{cases}\max\left\{\left|b^{k}+\nabla u^{k+1}+F\right|-\frac{a}{\lambda},0\right\}\frac{b^{k}+\nabla u^{k+1}+F}{\left|b^{k}+\nabla u^{k+1}+F\right|}-F&\text{if }\left|b^{k}+\nabla u^{k+1}+F\right|\neq 0\\
-F&\text{if }\left|b^{k}+\nabla u^{k+1}+F\right|=0\end{cases}.

3.

Let

b^{k+1}:=b^{k}+\nabla u^{k+1}-d^{k+1}.
